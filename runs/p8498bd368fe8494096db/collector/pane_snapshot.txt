## Solution

The identity $P(x,y)P(z,t)=P(xz-yt,xt+yz)$ is precisely multiplicativity under c
omplex multiplication, since $(x+iy)(z+it)=(xz-yt)+i(xt+yz)$.

**Step 1: Reformulate via complex variables.** Define $f(u)=P(\Re u,\Im u)$ for
$u\in\mathbb C$. The identity becomes
$$f(uv)=f(u)f(v)\qquad\text{for all }u,v\in\mathbb C.$$
Introduce $A,B$ (formally $A=z,\ B=\bar z$) and set
$$Q(A,B):=P\!\left(\tfrac{A+B}{2},\,\tfrac{A-B}{2i}\right),$$
a polynomial in $A,B$ with complex coefficients, so that $f(u)=Q(u,\bar u)$. Sin
ce the original identity is a polynomial identity in the four real variables $x,
y,z,t$, it extends to a polynomial identity in four complex variables $A,B,W,V$:
$$Q(AW,BV)=Q(A,B)\,Q(W,V).\tag{$\ast$}$$

**Step 2: Extract coefficients.** Write $Q(A,B)=\sum_{i,j} c_{ij}A^i B^j$. Then
$$Q(AW,BV)=\sum_{i,j} c_{ij}\,A^i W^i B^j V^j,$$
while
$$Q(A,B)Q(W,V)=\sum_{i,j,k,l} c_{ij}c_{kl}\,A^i B^j W^k V^l.$$
Comparing the coefficient of $A^i B^j W^k V^l$ in $(\ast)$:
- the left side contributes $c_{ij}$ when $i=k$ and $j=l$, and $0$ otherwise;
- the right side contributes $c_{ij}c_{kl}$.

Hence
$$c_{ij}c_{kl}=0\ \text{whenever }(i,j)\ne(k,l),\qquad c_{ij}=c_{ij}^2\ \text{wh
en }(i,j)=(k,l).$$
So every $c_{ij}\in\{0,1\}$, and any two *distinct* pairs have product $0$. Ther
efore **at most one** coefficient is nonzero, and that one equals $1$. Thus eith
er $Q\equiv 0$, or $Q(A,B)=A^i B^j$ for a single pair $(i,j)$.

**Step 3: Impose the reality condition.** A real polynomial $P(x,y)$ corresponds
 to $Q$ satisfying $Q(A,B)=Q(B,A)$ (swapping $A\leftrightarrow B$ is complex con
jugation, which fixes real-valued functions). For $Q(A,B)=A^i B^j$ this forces $
i=j$. Setting $i=j=n\ge 0$ gives
$$Q(A,B)=(AB)^n,\qquad P(x,y)=(x^2+y^2)^n.$$

**Step 4: Verification.** For $P(x,y)=(x^2+y^2)^n$,
$$P(xz-yt,xt+yz)=\bigl((xz-yt)^2+(xt+yz)^2\bigr)^n=\bigl((x^2+y^2)(z^2+t^2)\bigr
)^n=P(x,y)P(z,t),$$
using $(xz-yt)^2+(xt+yz)^2=(x^2+y^2)(z^2+t^2)$. The zero polynomial trivially sa
tisfies the identity.

**Conclusion.** The polynomials satisfying the identity are exactly
$$\boxed{P\equiv 0\quad\text{and}\quad P(x,y)=(x^2+y^2)^n\ \text{for }n=0,1,2,\d
ots}$$
