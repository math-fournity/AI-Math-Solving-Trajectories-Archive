}^5$, hence a $\mathbb{P}^2$. Three general $\mathbb{P}^2$'s in $\mathbb{P}^5$ a

**Step 1: Dimension of $I_{L\cup M\cup N}(3)$.** The Hilbert function of $\mathb
b{P}^2$ in degree 3 is $\binom{3+2}{2}=10$. Since $L,M,N$ are pairwise disjoint
and general, the Hilbert function of the union is:
$$H_{L\cup M\cup N}(3) = H_L(3)+H_M(3)+H_N(3) = 3\cdot 10 = 30.$$
Thus $\dim I_{L\cup M\cup N}(3) = 56 - 30 = 26$.

**Step 2: Counting derivative conditions.** Let $f\in I_{L\cup M\cup N}(3)$. At
a point $l_i\in L$, since $f|_L\equiv 0$, the value $f(l_i)=0$ and all partial d
erivatives along tangent directions to $L$ vanish automatically. The condition $
\nabla f(l_i)=0$ thus imposes only the vanishing of the **3 normal derivatives**
 (the conormal directions to $L\subset\mathbb{P}^5$). This gives 3 independent l
inear conditions per point.

By Euler's relation $\sum_j x_j \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_j} = 3f$, at $l_i\i
n L$ (where the tangent partials already vanish and $f(l_i)=0$), the 3 remaining
 normal partials satisfy no further relation (since the normal coordinates of $l
_i$ are zero). So indeed 3 independent conditions per point.

With 9 points total (3 on each subspace), the derivative conditions contribute $
9\times 3 = 27$ linear conditions.

**Step 3: Independence of the 27 conditions.** We verify these 27 conditions hav
e rank 26 on the 26-dimensional space $V = I_{L\cup M\cup N}(3)$.

Consider the **conormal map**. For each subspace, define:
$$\phi_L: V \to H^0(L,\, N^*_{L/\mathbb{P}^5}(3)) \cong H^0(\mathbb{P}^2,\mathca

where $N^*_{L/\mathbb{P}^5}\cong\mathcal{O}_L(-1)^{\oplus 3}$ is the conormal bu

bmod I_L^2$ restricted to $L$). The target has dimension $3\times 6 = 18$. Simil

**Claim:** The combined map $\phi = (\phi_L,\phi_M,\phi_N): V \to H^0(L,N^*_L(3)

*Proof of claim.* The kernel is $V \cap I_L^2(3)\cap I_M^2(3)\cap I_N^2(3) = I_L

 For general $L,M,N$, pairwise intersections satisfy $\dim(I_L^2(3)\cap I_M^2(3)

So $U:=\operatorname{im}(\phi)$ is a 26-dimensional subspace of a $54$-dimension

**Step 4: The derivative conditions as a subspace intersection.** The 27 derivat

$$f\in V \text{ such that } \phi(f)\in W:= W_L\oplus W_M\oplus W_N,\quad \dim W

The solution space is $\phi^{-1}(U\cap W)$, which has dimension $\dim(U\cap W)$

**Step 5: Dimension of $U\cap W$.** We have a 26-dimensional subspace $U$ and a
27-dimensional subspace $W$ in a 54-dimensional space, where:
- $U$ is determined by the general subspaces $L,M,N$,

The condition $\dim(U\cap W)\geq 1$ is a **closed condition** on the product of

$$\dim(U\cap W) = \max(0,\; 26+27-54) = \max(0,\,-1) = 0.$$

To confirm generality: $W_L$ is a general 9-dimensional subspace of $A$ (since t

**Conclusion.** The space of cubics satisfying all conditions has dimension
$$\dim(U\cap W) = 0.$$
