## Proof

We count the number of distinct colorings of the four sides of a rhombus using 6
 colors, where two colorings are identified if one can be **rotated** (in the pl
ane) to match the other. Reflections are explicitly excluded.

### Step 1: Determine the rotation group

A rhombus has all four sides equal, but (unless it is a square) its only rotatio
nal symmetries in the plane are:

- **0° (identity)**, and
- **180° rotation**.

A 90° rotation does **not** map a general rhombus onto itself (the diagonals of
a rhombus are perpendicular but generally of unequal length, so a 90° turn would
 not align the figure). Thus the rotation group acting on the four sides is the
cyclic group $C_2 = \{e, r\}$ of order 2.

Label the four sides $1, 2, 3, 4$ in cyclic order. The 180° rotation $r$ swaps o
pposite sides:
$$r: \quad 1 \leftrightarrow 3, \quad 2 \leftrightarrow 4.$$

### Step 2: Apply Burnside's lemma

The number of distinct colorings is
$$N = \frac{1}{|C_2|}\sum_{g \in C_2} |\operatorname{Fix}(g)|.$$

**Identity $e$:** Every coloring is fixed. There are $6^4 = 1296$ colorings.

**180° rotation $r$:** A coloring $(c_1, c_2, c_3, c_4)$ is fixed by $r$ iff $c_
1 = c_3$ and $c_2 = c_4$. We freely choose $c_1$ and $c_2$ (6 choices each), and
 then $c_3, c_4$ are determined. This gives $6^2 = 36$ fixed colorings.

### Step 3: Compute

$$N = \frac{1296 + 36}{2} = \frac{1332}{2} = 666.$$

### Verification

We can cross-check by direct counting. A coloring is either:
- **Symmetric** under 180° rotation ($c_1=c_3,\; c_2=c_4$): there are $6^2 = 36$
 such colorings, each forming an orbit of size 1.
- **Asymmetric**: the remaining $1296 - 36 = 1260$ colorings pair up under the 1
80° rotation into orbits of size 2, giving $1260/2 = 630$ orbits.

Total distinct colorings: $36 + 630 = 666$. ✓

### Answer

$$\boxed{666}$$
