## Proof

tion, answer, or proof sketch is leaked. Proceeding.

---

We determine the values of $\alpha$ for which
$$I(\alpha)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^2}\frac{dx\,dy}{(1+x^2+xy+y^2)^\alpha}$$
converges.

### Step 1. The quadratic form is positive definite

Write $Q(x,y)=x^2+xy+y^2$. Its associated matrix is
$$A=\begin{pmatrix}1 & 1/2\\ 1/2 & 1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det A = 1-\tfrac14=\tf
rac34>0,\quad \operatorname{tr}A=2>0,$$
so $A$ is positive definite with eigenvalues $\lambda_1=\tfrac12$, $\lambda_2=\t
frac32$. Hence
$$\tfrac12(x^2+y^2)\le Q(x,y)\le \tfrac32(x^2+y^2)\quad\text{for all }(x,y)\in\m
athbb{R}^2.$$

### Step 2. No singularity at the origin

Near $(0,0)$, $Q(x,y)\to 0$, so $1+Q(x,y)\to 1$ and the integrand tends to $1$.
The integrand is continuous and bounded on every bounded region, so there is no
local singularity. Convergence is determined entirely by behavior at infinity.

### Step 3. Reduction to a radial integral at infinity

Using the bounds from Step 1, for $r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\ge 1$:
$$\frac{1}{(1+\tfrac32\,r^2)^\alpha}\le\frac{1}{(1+Q(x,y))^\alpha}\le\frac{1}{(1
+\tfrac12\,r^2)^\alpha}.$$

Passing to polar coordinates $(x,y)=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)$, $dx\,dy=r\,dr\,d
\theta$, the integral over $\{r\ge 1\}$ is comparable (up to positive constants
independent of $r$) to
$$\int_1^\infty\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{r\,d\theta\,dr}{(1+c\,r^2)^\alpha}=2\pi\int_1^
\infty\frac{r\,dr}{(1+c\,r^2)^\alpha}$$
for $c\in\{\tfrac12,\tfrac32\}$. By the comparison test for nonnegative function
s, $I(\alpha)$ converges if and only if
$$\int_1^\infty\frac{r\,dr}{(1+r^2)^\alpha}$$
converges.

### Step 4. Evaluate the comparison integral

Substituting $u=1+r^2$, $du=2r\,dr$:
$$\int_1^\infty\frac{r\,dr}{(1+r^2)^\alpha}=\frac12\int_2^\infty u^{-\alpha}\,du
.$$

This converges if and only if $\alpha>1$ (it diverges for $\alpha\le 1$, giving
$\log$ divergence at $\alpha=1$ and power divergence for $\alpha<1$).

### Step 5. Conclusion

- For $\alpha>1$: both bounding integrals converge, so $I(\alpha)$ converges.
- For $\alpha\le 1$: the lower bound integral diverges (it is $\ge$ a constant m
ultiple of the divergent $\int r^{1-2\alpha}dr$), so $I(\alpha)$ diverges.

$$\boxed{\,\iint_{\mathbb{R}^2}\frac{dx\,dy}{(1+x^2+xy+y^2)^\alpha}\text{ conver
ges if and only if }\alpha>1.\,}$$
