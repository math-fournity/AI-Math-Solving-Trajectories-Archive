## Solution

We compute the contour integral
$$I = \int_C \frac{e^z}{z^3}\,dz,$$
where $C$ is the boundary of the square with vertices $\pm 4 \pm 4i$, traversed
once counterclockwise (positive orientation).

### Step 1. Locate the singularity

The integrand $g(z) = \dfrac{e^z}{z^3}$ has a single singularity at $z = 0$, whi
ch is a pole of order $3$. The square with vertices $\pm 4 \pm 4i$ is the set $\
{z : -4 \le \operatorname{Re} z \le 4,\ -4 \le \operatorname{Im} z \le 4\}$, so
the origin $z=0$ lies strictly in its interior. The integrand is holomorphic eve
rywhere else, so $z=0$ is the only singularity enclosed by $C$.

### Step 2. Apply Cauchy's integral formula for derivatives

Cauchy's integral formula for derivatives states that if $f$ is holomorphic insi
de and on a positively oriented simple closed contour $C$ enclosing $a$, then
$$f^{(n)}(a) = \frac{n!}{2\pi i}\oint_C \frac{f(z)}{(z-a)^{n+1}}\,dz.$$

We rewrite the integrand as
$$\frac{e^z}{z^3} = \frac{e^z}{(z-0)^{3}},$$
so we identify $f(z) = e^z$, $a = 0$, and $n+1 = 3$, i.e. $n = 2$.

### Step 3. Compute the derivative

Since $f(z) = e^z$, every derivative is $f^{(k)}(z) = e^z$. In particular,
$$f''(0) = e^0 = 1.$$

### Step 4. Assemble the result

Substituting into Cauchy's formula:
$$\oint_C \frac{e^z}{z^3}\,dz = \frac{2\pi i}{2!}\,f''(0) = \pi i \cdot 1 = \pi
i.$$

### Verification (residue check)

Equivalently, the residue of $\dfrac{e^z}{z^3}$ at $z=0$ is the coefficient of $
z^{-1}$ in the Laurent expansion
$$\frac{e^z}{z^3} = \frac{1}{z^3}\left(1 + z + \frac{z^2}{2!} + \frac{z^3}{3!} +
 \cdots\right) = \frac{1}{z^3} + \frac{1}{z^2} + \frac{1}{2z} + \frac{1}{6} + \c
dots.$$

The coefficient of $z^{-1}$ is $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so by the residue theorem
$$\oint_C \frac{e^z}{z^3}\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{e^z}{
z^3} = 2\pi i \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \pi i,$$
confirming the result.

### Answer

$$\boxed{\int_C \frac{e^z}{z^3}\,dz = \pi i.}$$
