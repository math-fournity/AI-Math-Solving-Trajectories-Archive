# Ordinary Singularity on a Plane Curve

## Setup

Let $C$ be a plane curve over a field $k$, defined by a non-constant homogeneous
 polynomial $F(X,Y,Z) \in k[X,Y,Z]$ (or, in affine coordinates, by $f(x,y) \in k
[x,y]$). Let $P \in C(k^{\mathrm{al}})$ be a singular point of $C$.

## Multiplicity and the Tangent Cone

Recall that $P$ is singular precisely when all first-order partial derivatives o
f the defining equation vanish at $P$:
$$F(P) = 0, \quad \frac{\partial F}{\partial X}(P) = \frac{\partial F}{\partial
Y}(P) = \frac{\partial F}{\partial Z}(P) = 0.$$

The **multiplicity** $m = \mathrm{mult}_P(C)$ is the degree of the lowest-degree
 non-zero homogeneous part of the defining equation after translating $P$ to the
 origin. Concretely, suppose $P = (0:0:1)$ in projective coordinates (achievable
 by a linear change of coordinates), and write
$$F(X,Y,Z) = F_m(X,Y,Z) + F_{m+1}(X,Y,Z) + \cdots,$$
where each $F_i$ is homogeneous of degree $i$ and $F_m \neq 0$. Then $m = \mathr
m{mult}_P(C)$, and the **tangent cone** of $C$ at $P$ is the degree-$m$ plane cu
rve defined by $F_m(X,Y,Z) = 0$.

Since $P = (0:0:1)$, the tangent cone lives in the tangent directions, i.e. in t
he line at infinity of the local coordinates, and we may dehomogenize by setting
 $Z = 1$ to obtain the lowest-degree homogeneous part $f_m(x,y)$ of the affine e
quation.

## The Condition

**Definition.** The singular point $P$ is an **ordinary singularity** (also call
ed an **ordinary $m$-fold point** or **ordinary multiple point**) of $C$ if and
only if the tangent cone at $P$ splits completely into $m$ distinct linear facto
rs over $k^{\mathrm{al}}$:

$$F_m(X,Y,Z) = \prod_{i=1}^{m} L_i(X,Y,Z),$$

where the $L_i$ are $m$ **distinct** linear forms over $k^{\mathrm{al}}$.

Equivalently, in affine local coordinates with $P$ at the origin, writing the lo
west-degree homogeneous part as
$$f_m(x,y) = \sum_{j=0}^{m} \binom{m}{j} a_j\, x^j y^{m-j},$$
the point $P$ is ordinary if and only if the homogeneous polynomial $f_m(x,y)$ h
as $m$ distinct roots in $\mathbb{P}^1(k^{\mathrm{al}})$, i.e. the polynomial $g
(t) = f_m(1,t) = \sum_{j=0}^{m} \binom{m}{j} a_j\, t^{m-j}$ (or $f_m(t,1)$ if ne
eded) is squarefree over $k^{\mathrm{al}}$.

## Equivalent Formulations

The condition can be stated in several equivalent ways:

1. **(Distinct tangent lines)** $C$ has exactly $m$ distinct tangent lines at $P
$ over $k^{\mathrm{al}}$.

2. **(Squarefree tangent cone)** The tangent cone $F_m = 0$ is a reduced degree-
$m$ divisor on $\mathbb{P}^1_{k^{\mathrm{al}}}$, i.e. $F_m$ is squarefree over $
k^{\mathrm{al}}$.

3. **(Discriminant nonzero)** The discriminant of the binary form $f_m(x,y)$ is
nonzero in $k^{\mathrm{al}}$.

4. **(Transversality of branches)** The $m$ formal (or local) branches of $C$ at
 $P$ have pairwise distinct tangent directions.

## Why "ordinary"

The term "ordinary" signifies that the singularity is the **mildest possible** f
or a point of multiplicity $m$: the only singularity data is the multiplicity, a
nd the tangent directions are all distinct (no two branches share a tangent, so
there is no higher-order tangency among the branches). This is the generic situa
tion among all singularities of multiplicity $m$.

## Summary

> **A singular point $P$ of multiplicity $m$ on a plane curve $C$ is an ordinary
 singularity if and only if the tangent cone of $C$ at $P$ splits into $m$ disti
nct linear factors over $k^{\mathrm{al}}$, equivalently, $C$ has $m$ distinct ta
ngent lines at $P$ over the algebraic closure.**
