## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $S = \sqrt{T^*T}$.

**Step 1: Reformulate the norm condition as a quadratic form identity.**

Since $S \geq 0$, $S$ is self-adjoint, so $S^*S = S^2$. For every $x \in H$:

$$\|Sx\|^2 = \langle Sx, Sx\rangle = \langle S^*S\, x, x\rangle = \langle S^2 x,
 x\rangle,$$

$$\|Tx\|^2 = \langle Tx, Tx\rangle = \langle T^*T\, x, x\rangle.$$

The hypothesis $\|Sx\| = \|Tx\|$ for all $x$ implies $\|Sx\|^2 = \|Tx\|^2$ for a
ll $x$, hence

$$\langle S^2 x, x\rangle = \langle T^*T\, x, x\rangle, \qquad \forall\, x \in H
. \tag{1}$$

**Step 2: Conclude $S^2 = T^*T$ via the polarization identity.**

Set $A := S^2 - T^*T$. Both $S^2$ (square of a positive, hence self-adjoint, ope
rator) and $T^*T$ are self-adjoint, so $A$ is self-adjoint. Equation (1) gives

$$\langle Ax, x\rangle = 0, \qquad \forall\, x \in H. \tag{2}$$

For a self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space, (2) forces $A = 0$. Indeed, by t
he polarization identity (valid over both $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$): for se
lf-adjoint $A$,

$$\langle Ax, y\rangle = \tfrac{1}{4}\bigl(\langle A(x+y),\, x+y\rangle - \langl
e A(x-y),\, x-y\rangle\bigr),$$

so $\langle Ax, x\rangle = 0$ for all $x$ implies $\langle Ax, y\rangle = 0$ for
 all $x, y$, hence $A = 0$. Therefore

$$S^2 = T^*T. \tag{3}$$

**Step 3: Apply uniqueness of the positive square root.**

The operator $T^*T$ is positive ($T^*T \geq 0$). A standard theorem of spectral
theory states that every positive operator $P$ on a Hilbert space admits a **uni
que** positive square root: there is exactly one operator $B \geq 0$ with $B^2 =
 P$, denoted $B = \sqrt{P}$.

By (3), $S^2 = T^*T$ with $S \geq 0$. Thus $S$ is a positive square root of $T^*
T$. By uniqueness,

$$S = \sqrt{T^*T}. \qquad \blacksquare$$
