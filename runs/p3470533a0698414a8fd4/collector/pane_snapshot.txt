## Proof

We evaluate
$$L = \lim_{x \to 1^-} \prod_{n=0}^\infty \left(\frac{1+x^{n+1}}{1+x^n}\right)^{
x^n}.$$

**Step 1: Convergence of the product for fixed $x \in (0,1)$.**

For fixed $x\in(0,1)$, set $a_n = \ln(1+x^n)$. The log of the partial product is
$$S_N(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{N} x^n\,(a_{n+1}-a_n).$$
As $n\to\infty$, $x^n\to 0$ and $a_{n+1}-a_n = \ln\!\left(\frac{1+x^{n+1}}{1+x^n
}\right) \sim x^n(x-1)$, so the general term is $O(x^{2n})$, hence the series co
nverges absolutely. Thus $P(x)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\prod_{n=0}^N\left(\frac{1+x^{n+
1}}{1+x^n}\right)^{x^n}$ is well defined.

**Step 2: Abel (summation-by-parts) reformulation.**

Using $\sum_{n=0}^N x^n(a_{n+1}-a_n) = \sum_{n=1}^{N+1}x^{n-1}a_n - \sum_{n=0}^N
 x^n a_n$, we rearrange:

$$S_N = \frac{1-x}{x}\sum_{n=0}^{N}x^n a_n + x^N a_{N+1} - \frac{a_0}{x}.$$

As $N\to\infty$: $x^N a_{N+1}\to 0$ (since $x^N\to 0$, $a_{N+1}\to 0$), and $a_0
=\ln(1+x^0)=\ln 2$. Therefore

$$\ln P(x) = \frac{1-x}{x}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^n\ln(1+x^n) \;-\; \frac{\ln 2}{x}
. \tag{$\star$}$$

**Step 3: Riemann-sum limit.**

Set $x = e^{-t}$ with $t\to 0^+$. Then $x^n = e^{-nt}$ and

$$\frac{1-x}{x} = e^t - 1 \;\sim\; t, \qquad \frac{\ln 2}{x}=e^t\ln 2 \;\to\; \l
n 2.$$

The sum in $(\star)$ is a left Riemann sum with mesh $t$:

$$t\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} e^{-nt}\ln(1+e^{-nt}) \;\xrightarrow{t\to 0^+}\; \int_0^{
\infty} e^{-u}\ln(1+e^{-u})\,du,$$

since $f(u)=e^{-u}\ln(1+e^{-u})$ is continuous, non-negative, and integrable on
$[0,\infty)$ (decaying as $u\,e^{-u}$ near $0$ and as $e^{-2u}$ at $\infty$). He
nce

$$\frac{1-x}{x}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^n\ln(1+x^n) \;\xrightarrow{t\to 0^+}\; \int_
0^{\infty} e^{-u}\ln(1+e^{-u})\,du.$$

**Step 4: Evaluate the integral.**

Substitute $w = e^{-u}$, $dw = -w\,du$:

$$\int_0^{\infty} e^{-u}\ln(1+e^{-u})\,du = \int_0^1 \ln(1+w)\,dw = \bigl[(1+w)\
ln(1+w)-(1+w)\bigr]_0^1 = (2\ln 2 - 2)-(-1) = 2\ln 2 - 1.$$

**Step 5: Combine.**

Taking the limit in $(\star)$:

$$\ln L = (2\ln 2 - 1) - \ln 2 = \ln 2 - 1.$$

Therefore

$$L = e^{\ln 2 - 1} = \frac{2}{e}.$$

$$\boxed{\dfrac{2}{e}}$$
