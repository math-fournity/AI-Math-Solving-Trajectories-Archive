**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.** Let $S_N(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{N} c_n f_n(x)$ and suppose $S_N(x) \to g(x)
$ a.e. We show $c_n \to 0$.

**Step 1: The terms tend to zero a.e.** Since $S_N \to g$ a.e., we have
$$c_n f_n(x) = S_n(x) - S_{n-1}(x) \to g(x) - g(x) = 0 \quad \text{a.e.}$$

**Step 2: The sequence $\{c_n\}$ is bounded.** Suppose for contradiction that $\
{c_n\}$ is unbounded. Then there is a subsequence $\{n_k\}$ with $|c_{n_k}| \to
\infty$. From Step 1, $c_{n_k} f_{n_k}(x) \to 0$ a.e., so
$$f_{n_k}(x) = \frac{c_{n_k} f_{n_k}(x)}{c_{n_k}} \to 0 \quad \text{a.e.}$$
(since the numerator $\to 0$ a.e. and $|c_{n_k}| \to \infty$). Because $|f_{n_k}
| \le M$, we have $|f_{n_k}|^2 \le M^2 \in L^1(0,1)$, so by the Dominated Conver
gence Theorem,
$$\int_0^1 f_{n_k}(x)^2\,dx \to 0.$$
But orthonormality gives $\int_0^1 f_{n_k}^2 = 1$ for every $k$, a contradiction
. Hence $\{c_n\}$ is bounded: there exists $C$ with $|c_n| \le C$ for all $n$.

**Step 3: Conclude $c_n \to 0$.** From Step 2, $|c_n f_n(x)| \le CM$, so $|c_n f
_n|^2 \le C^2 M^2 \in L^1(0,1)$. Since $c_n f_n \to 0$ a.e. (Step 1), the Domina
ted Convergence Theorem gives
$$\int_0^1 c_n^2 f_n(x)^2\,dx \to 0.$$
By orthonormality, $\int_0^1 c_n^2 f_n^2 = c_n^2 \int_0^1 f_n^2 = c_n^2$. Theref
ore $c_n^2 \to 0$, i.e. $c_n \to 0$. $\square$
