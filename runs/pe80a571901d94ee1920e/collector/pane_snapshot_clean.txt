Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether all order-$p^2$ subgroups o
f $U_n$ are invariant under diagonal conjugation.

**Answer: No.** Not all subgroups of order $p^2$ are invariant. I'll construct a
n explicit counterexample for $p \geq 3$, $n = 3$.

---

## Proof

**Setup.** Take $n = 3$ and $p \geq 3$. Then
$$U_3 = \left\{\begin{pmatrix} 1 & a & b \\ 0 & 1 & c \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}
: a,b,c \in \mathbb{F}_p\right\}, \qquad |U_3| = p^3.$$
Since $p \geq 3 \geq n$, every element of $U_3$ has order $p$ (because $(I+N)^p
= I + N^p = I$ for strictly upper triangular $N$ with $N^3 = 0$ and $p \geq 3$).
 Thus every subgroup of order $p^2$ is elementary abelian $\cong \mathbb{Z}/p \t
imes \mathbb{Z}/p$.

**Diagonal action.** For $D = \operatorname{diag}(d_1, d_2, d_3) \in T$,
$$\varphi_D\!\begin{pmatrix} 1 & a & b \\ 0 & 1 & c \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{pmatrix} =
\begin{pmatrix} 1 & \frac{d_1}{d_2}\,a & \frac{d_1}{d_3}\,b \\ 0 & 1 & \frac{d_2
}{d_3}\,c \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}.$$
So the $(1,2)$-entry scales by $d_1/d_2$, the $(2,3)$-entry by $d_2/d_3$, and th
e $(1,3)$-entry by $d_1/d_3$.

**A non-invariant subgroup.** Define
$$H = \left\{\begin{pmatrix} 1 & t & b \\ 0 & 1 & t \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{pmatrix} :
t, b \in \mathbb{F}_p\right\}.$$

*$H$ is a subgroup:* multiplying two elements,
$$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & t & b \\ 0 & 1 & t \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix
} 1 & s & c \\ 0 & 1 & s \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & t{+}s &
 b + ts + c \\ 0 & 1 & t{+}s \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{pmatrix} \in H.$$
Inverses are clear, so $H \leq U_3$ with $|H| = p^2$.

*$H$ is not invariant:* Take $D = \operatorname{diag}(1, 1, \alpha)$ where $\alp
ha \in \mathbb{F}_p^*$ with $\alpha \neq 1$ (possible since $p \geq 3$ gives $|\
mathbb{F}_p^*| \geq 2$). Consider $M = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\
 0 & 0 & 1\end{pmatrix} \in H$ (with $t=1, b=0$). Then
$$\varphi_D(M) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & \alpha^{-1} \\ 0 & 0 & 1\e
nd{pmatrix}.$$
This has $(1,2)$-entry $= 1$ and $(2,3)$-entry $= \alpha^{-1} \neq 1$, so it is
**not** of the form $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & t & b \\ 0 & 1 & t \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{pma
trix}$ (which would require equal $(1,2)$ and $(2,3)$ entries). Hence $\varphi_D
(M) \notin H$.

**Conclusion.** The subgroup $H$ of order $p^2$ is not invariant under the diago
nal automorphism $\varphi_D$. Therefore, not all subgroups of order $p^2$ of $U_
n$ are invariant under the diagonal automorphisms. $\blacksquare$
