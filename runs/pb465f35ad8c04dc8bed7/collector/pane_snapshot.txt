## Claim

$$H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(M;\mathbb{R}) = 0.$$

## Proof

The argument proceeds in three stages: (1) reduce to the interior of $M$, which
is a manifold without boundary; (2) verify the interior is connected, oriented,
and noncompact; (3) apply Poincaré duality for noncompact manifolds.

### Step 1 — Reduction to the interior

Let $U = \operatorname{int}(M)$. Since $\partial M \neq \varnothing$, the bounda
ry admits a collar neighborhood $C \cong [0,1)\times \partial M \hookrightarrow
M$. Using this collar, one constructs a smooth deformation retraction of $M$ ont
o $U$: points in the collar $[0,\varepsilon)\times\partial M$ are pushed inward
by a smooth map $(s,x)\mapsto (\varphi(s),x)$ where $\varphi:[0,1)\to(0,1)$ is a
 smooth increasing function with $\varphi(s)\to \varepsilon/2$ as $s\to 0$ and $
\varphi(s)=s$ for $s\geq \varepsilon/2$, extended by the identity away from the
collar. This gives a homotopy equivalence $M \simeq U$.

By homotopy invariance of de Rham cohomology:
$$H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(M;\mathbb{R}) \;\cong\; H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(U;\mathbb{R}).$$

### Step 2 — Properties of the interior

The interior $U = \operatorname{int}(M)$ is:

- **A smooth $m$-manifold without boundary** (by definition of interior).
- **Connected:** $M$ is connected and $U$ is dense in $M$ (every point of $M$, i
ncluding boundary points, is a limit of interior points), so $U$ is connected.
- **Oriented:** The orientation of $M$ restricts to an orientation of $U$.
- **Noncompact:** Since $\partial M \neq \varnothing$, removing the boundary fro
m $M$ yields a noncompact space. (Indeed, any point of $\partial M$ is a limit p
oint of $U$ not contained in $U$, so $U$ is not closed in any compactification;
equivalently, a collar $[0,1)\times\partial M$ contributes a noncompact end.) In
 particular, even if $M$ were compact, $U$ would be noncompact; here $M$ is alre
ady noncompact, so $U$ is certainly noncompact.

### Step 3 — Poincaré duality for noncompact manifolds

For a connected, oriented, noncompact $m$-manifold $U$ **without boundary**, Poi
ncaré duality (in the form suitable for noncompact manifolds, using compactly su
pported cohomology) provides an isomorphism:

$$H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(U;\mathbb{R}) \;\cong\; \bigl(H^{m-k}_c(U;\mathbb{R})\bigr)^
*,$$

where $H^{\bullet}_c$ denotes compactly supported de Rham cohomology. This is es
tablished via the pairing
$$H^k_c(U)\times H^{m-k}(U)\longrightarrow \mathbb{R},\qquad ([\alpha],[\beta])\
mapsto \int_U \alpha\wedge\beta,$$
which is nondegenerate for oriented manifolds (the proof uses a good cover and t
he Poincaré lemma for compactly supported forms; see Bott–Tu, *Differential Form
s in Algebraic Topology*, or Madsen–Tornehave).

### Step 4 — Computing $H^0_c(U;\mathbb{R})$

By definition, $H^0_c(U;\mathbb{R})$ is the space of compactly supported, closed
 $0$-forms modulo exact ones. A closed $0$-form is a locally constant function,
and the only exact $0$-form is $0$, so:

$$H^0_c(U;\mathbb{R}) = \bigl\{\text{compactly supported locally constant functi
ons on } U\bigr\}.$$

A locally constant function on a **connected** space is **constant**. A constant
 function on a **noncompact** space with **compact support** must be identically
 zero (a nonzero constant has noncompact support). Since $U$ is both connected a
nd noncompact:

$$H^0_c(U;\mathbb{R}) = 0.$$

### Step 5 — Conclusion

Setting $k = m$ in the Poincaré duality isomorphism of Step 3:

$$H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(U;\mathbb{R}) \;\cong\; \bigl(H^0_c(U;\mathbb{R})\bigr)^* =
0^* = 0.$$

Combining with the isomorphism from Step 1:

$$\boxed{H^m_{\mathrm{dR}}(M;\mathbb{R}) = 0.}$$

### Remark

The key ingredients are: (i) a manifold with nonempty boundary is homotopy equiv
alent to its interior, which is a manifold *without* boundary; (ii) the interior
 of a manifold with nonempty boundary is always noncompact (regardless of whethe
r $M$ itself is compact); (iii) on a connected, oriented, noncompact manifold wi
thout boundary, the top-degree de Rham cohomology vanishes because Poincaré dual
ity identifies it with the dual of compactly supported $H^0$, which is zero. The
 noncompactness of $M$ stated in the hypothesis is, in fact, redundant once $\pa
rtial M\neq\varnothing$ is assumed—the conclusion holds for *any* connected, ori
ented $m$-manifold with nonempty boundary.
