## Proof

**Setup.** Let $H_k = \sum_{j=1}^k 1/j$ denote the $k$-th harmonic number, and d
efine
$$D_k = \log k + \gamma + \frac{1}{2k} - \frac{1}{12k^2} + \frac{1}{60k^4}.$$
We study $q_{a,3}(n) = \prod_{k=1}^n \frac{H_k}{D_k}$.

**Step 1: The Euler–Maclaurin expansion of $H_k$.** The classical asymptotic exp
ansion is
$$H_k = \log k + \gamma + \frac{1}{2k} - \frac{1}{12k^2} + \frac{1}{120k^4} - \f
rac{1}{252k^6} + O(k^{-8}),$$
where the coefficients come from $-\frac{B_{2j}}{2j\,k^{2j}}$ with Bernoulli num
bers $B_2 = 1/6$, $B_4 = -1/30$, $B_6 = 1/42$.

**Step 2: The discrepancy $H_k - D_k$.** Comparing term by term:
$$H_k - D_k = \left(\frac{1}{120} - \frac{1}{60}\right)\frac{1}{k^4} - \frac{1}{
252k^6} + O(k^{-8}) = -\frac{1}{60k^4} + O(k^{-6}).$$

The denominator uses $1/60$ instead of the correct $1/120$ at order $k^{-4}$, cr
eating a discrepancy of order $k^{-4}$.

**Step 3: The ratio $H_k/D_k$ for large $k$.** Since $D_k = \log k\,(1 + \gamma/
\log k + O(1/(k\log k)))$, we have
$$\frac{H_k - D_k}{D_k} = \frac{-\frac{1}{60k^4} + O(k^{-6})}{\log k\,(1 + o(1))
} = -\frac{1}{60\,k^4\log k}\left(1 + o(1)\right).$$

More precisely, expanding the denominator:
$$\frac{H_k - D_k}{D_k} = -\frac{1}{60\,k^4\log k} + O\!\left(\frac{1}{k^5(\log
k)^2}\right) + O\!\left(\frac{1}{k^6\log k}\right).$$

**Step 4: Logarithm of the product.** Using $\log(1+x) = x + O(x^2)$ for $|x|$ s
mall:
$$\log\frac{H_k}{D_k} = -\frac{1}{60\,k^4\log k} + O\!\left(\frac{1}{k^5(\log k)
^2}\right), \qquad k \geq 2.$$

(The $k=1$ term is finite: $D_1 = \gamma + \tfrac{13}{30} \approx 1.0106$ and $H
_1 = 1$, contributing the constant $\log\frac{1}{\gamma + 13/30}$.)

Summing from $k=2$ to $n$:
$$\log q_{a,3}(n) = \log\frac{H_1}{D_1} - \frac{1}{60}\sum_{k=2}^{n}\frac{1}{k^4
\log k} + O\!\left(\sum_{k=2}^{n}\frac{1}{k^5(\log k)^2}\right).$$

**Step 5: Convergence.** Both series $\sum_{k=2}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k^4\log k}$ a
nd $\sum_{k=2}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k^5(\log k)^2}$ converge (by comparison with $\
sum 1/k^{4-\epsilon}$ and $\sum 1/k^{5-\epsilon}$ for any $\epsilon > 0$, or by
the integral test). Therefore $\log q_{a,3}(n)$ converges to a finite limit $C$
as $n \to \infty$, and
$$q_{a,3}(n) \longrightarrow L := e^C > 0.$$

**Step 6: Rate of convergence.** The tail determines the approach to the limit:
$$\log q_{a,3}(n) - C = \frac{1}{60}\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^4\log k} + O
\!\left(\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^5(\log k)^2}\right).$$

By the integral test (Euler–Maclaurin), the dominant tail is:
$$\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^4\log k} \sim \int_n^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x^4\log
 x}.$$

Substituting $u = \log x$:
$$\int_n^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x^4\log x} = \int_{\log n}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-3u}}{u}\
,du = \frac{1}{3n^3\log n}\left(1 + O\!\left(\frac{1}{\log n}\right)\right),$$
where the last step follows from integration by parts (Laplace-type estimate). T
he error term's tail is $O(1/(n^4(\log n)^2))$, which is of lower order.

Therefore:
$$\log q_{a,3}(n) = C + \frac{1}{180\,n^3\log n}\left(1 + o(1)\right).$$

Exponentiating:
$$\boxed{q_{a,3}(n) = L\left(1 + \frac{1}{180\,n^3\log n} + o\!\left(\frac{1}{n^
3\log n}\right)\right) \quad \text{as } n\to\infty,}$$
where $L = \prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_k}{D_k}$ is a positive constant.

**Summary.** The denominator $D_k$ matches the Euler–Maclaurin expansion of $H_k
$ through order $k^{-2}$ but uses $1/60$ instead of the correct $1/120$ at order
 $k^{-4}$. This makes $H_k/D_k = 1 - \frac{1}{60\,k^4\log k}(1+o(1))$, and since
 $\sum \frac{1}{k^4\log k}$ converges, the infinite product converges to a finit
e positive constant $L$. The product approaches $L$ from above (each factor $< 1
$ for large $k$) with leading correction $\frac{L}{180\,n^3\log n}$.
