## Proof

**Step 1: Convert to a polynomial identity.**

Multiplying both sides by $x^2 y^2 z^2$ (valid since $x,y,z \neq 0$):

$$x^2 y^2 z^2\, P(x+y+z) = y^2 z^2\, P(y)P(z) + x^2 z^2\, P(z)P(x) + x^2 y^2\, P
(x)P(y). \tag{$*$}$$

Both sides are polynomials in $x,y,z$ that agree on the dense open set $\{xyz \n
eq 0\}$, so $(*)$ holds as a polynomial identity for all $x,y,z \in \mathbb{R}$.

**Step 2: $P \equiv 0$ is a solution.**

If $P \equiv 0$, both sides of the original equation are $0$. ✓

**Step 3: No nonzero solution exists.**

Suppose $P$ is nonzero of degree $n$ with leading coefficient $\alpha \neq 0$.

**Degree analysis.** The leading homogeneous part (highest total degree part) of
 the LHS of $(*)$ is:
$$\alpha \cdot x^2 y^2 z^2 \cdot (x+y+z)^n, \quad \text{total degree } n+6.$$

The leading homogeneous part of the RHS of $(*)$ is:
$$\alpha^2\!\left(y^{n+2}z^{n+2} + x^{n+2}z^{n+2} + x^{n+2}y^{n+2}\right), \quad
 \text{total degree } 2n+4.$$

Since the three RHS terms involve disjoint variable sets (the first has no $x$,
the second no $y$, the third no $z$), they cannot cancel each other. Likewise, t
he LHS leading part is nonzero since $\alpha \neq 0$.

For the identity to hold, the total degrees must match:
$$n + 6 = 2n + 4 \implies n = 2.$$

(If $n < 2$, the LHS has strictly higher degree, forcing $\alpha = 0$—contradict
ion. If $n > 2$, the RHS has strictly higher degree, forcing $\alpha^2 = 0$—cont
radiction.)

**Step 4: Contradiction for $n = 2$.**

With $n = 2$, equating the leading homogeneous parts of degree $8$:
$$\alpha \cdot x^2 y^2 z^2 (x+y+z)^2 = \alpha^2\!\left(y^4 z^4 + x^4 z^4 + x^4 y
^4\right).$$

Since $\alpha \neq 0$, divide by $\alpha$:
$$x^2 y^2 z^2 (x+y+z)^2 = \alpha\!\left(y^4 z^4 + x^4 z^4 + x^4 y^4\right). \tag
{$**$}$$

Now compare the coefficient of the monomial $x^4 y^2 z^2$ on both sides:

- **LHS:** Expanding $(x+y+z)^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + 2xy + 2xz + 2yz$, the term $
x^2 y^2 z^2 \cdot x^2 = x^4 y^2 z^2$ appears with **coefficient $1$**.

- **RHS:** The three monomials $y^4 z^4$, $x^4 z^4$, $x^4 y^4$ are each missing
one variable. None of them equals $x^4 y^2 z^2$, so the coefficient of $x^4 y^2
z^2$ on the RHS is **$0$**.

Equation $(**)$ requires $1 = 0$, a contradiction.

**Conclusion.**

The assumption that $P$ is nonzero leads to a contradiction. Therefore the only
polynomial satisfying the equation is:

$$\boxed{P(a) \equiv 0}.$$
