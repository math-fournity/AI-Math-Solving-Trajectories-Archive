## Solution

We evaluate $I = \displaystyle\oint_{|z|=1} \frac{1}{1+e^z}\,dz$ via the residue
 theorem.

**Step 1: Locate the poles.**

The integrand $f(z) = \dfrac{1}{1+e^z}$ is meromorphic. Poles occur where the de
nominator vanishes:

$$1 + e^z = 0 \implies e^z = -1 \implies z = (2k+1)\pi i, \quad k \in \mathbb{Z}
.$$

**Step 2: Determine which poles lie inside $|z|=1$.**

For every integer $k$, the pole $z_k = (2k+1)\pi i$ has modulus

$$|z_k| = |2k+1|\,\pi \geq \pi \approx 3.14159 > 1.$$

The closest poles to the origin are $z = \pm\pi i$, both at distance $\pi > 1$.
Hence **no poles** of $f$ lie inside the unit circle $|z|=1$.

**Step 3: Check regularity on the contour.**

The function $f$ is analytic at every point of the closed disk $\overline{D} = \
{z : |z| \leq 1\}$, since:
- The nearest singularity is at $|z| = \pi > 1$, so $f$ is holomorphic on an ope
n set containing $\overline{D}$.
- In particular $f(0) = \frac{1}{1+e^0} = \frac{1}{2}$ is finite, and no singula
rity touches the contour.

**Step 4: Apply the residue theorem.**

By the residue theorem,

$$\oint_{|z|=1} \frac{1}{1+e^z}\,dz = 2\pi i \sum_{\substack{k \in \mathbb{Z} \\
 |z_k|<1}} \operatorname{Res}(f, z_k).$$

Since the sum is empty (no poles inside $|z|=1$),

$$I = 2\pi i \cdot 0 = 0.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\oint_{|z|=1} \frac{1}{1+e^z}\,dz = 0}$$

The integrand $\frac{1}{1+e^z}$ has all its poles at $z=(2k+1)\pi i$ ($k\in\math
bb{Z}$), every one of which lies at distance $\geq \pi > 1$ from the origin. Wit
h no singularities enclosed by $|z|=1$, Cauchy's theorem (the zero-residue case
of the residue theorem) gives the value $0$.
