## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle L = \lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{1}^{1+\delta}\frac{n}{x^{2
}(1+x^{n})}\,dx$, where $\delta>0$ is fixed.

### Step 1. Substitution $t = x^{n}$

Let $t = x^{n}$, so $x = t^{1/n}$ and $dx = \tfrac{1}{n}\,t^{1/n-1}\,dt$. The li
mits become $t=1$ (when $x=1$) and $t=(1+\delta)^{n}$ (when $x=1+\delta$). Then

$$
\int_{1}^{1+\delta}\frac{n}{x^{2}(1+x^{n})}\,dx
= \int_{1}^{(1+\delta)^{n}} \frac{n}{t^{2/n}(1+t)}\cdot\frac{1}{n}\,t^{1/n-1}\,d
t
= \int_{1}^{(1+\delta)^{n}} \frac{t^{-1-1/n}}{1+t}\,dt.
$$

Denote $I_n = \displaystyle\int_{1}^{(1+\delta)^{n}} \frac{t^{-1-1/n}}{1+t}\,dt$
.

### Step 2. Pointwise limit of the integrand

Define $g_n(t) = \dfrac{t^{-1-1/n}}{1+t}\,\mathbf{1}_{[1,\,(1+\delta)^{n}]}(t)$
on $[1,\infty)$.

For every **fixed** $t \ge 1$: since $(1+\delta)^{n}\to\infty$, for all sufficie
ntly large $n$ we have $(1+\delta)^{n} > t$, so the indicator equals $1$; also $
t^{-1/n}\to 1$. Hence

$$
g_n(t) \;\longrightarrow\; \frac{1}{t(1+t)} \qquad (t\ge 1).
$$

### Step 3. Dominating function (applying DCT)

For $t\ge 1$ and every $n\ge 1$: since $t^{-1/n}\le 1$ (because $t\ge 1$),

$$
g_n(t) = \frac{t^{-1}\,t^{-1/n}}{1+t}\,\mathbf{1}_{[1,(1+\delta)^n]}(t) \;\le\;
\frac{1}{t(1+t)} \;\le\; \frac{1}{t^{2}}.
$$

The function $\dfrac{1}{t^{2}}$ is integrable on $[1,\infty)$ (with $\int_1^\inf
ty t^{-2}\,dt = 1$). By **Lebesgue's Dominated Convergence Theorem**,

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty} I_n = \int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{t(1+t)}\,dt.
$$

### Step 4. Evaluate the limiting integral

Using partial fractions $\dfrac{1}{t(1+t)} = \dfrac{1}{t} - \dfrac{1}{1+t}$:

$$
\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t(1+t)}\,dt
= \int_{1}^{\infty}\!\left(\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{1+t}\right)dt
= \Big[\ln t - \ln(1+t)\Big]_{1}^{\infty}
= \left[\ln\frac{t}{1+t}\right]_{1}^{\infty}.
$$

As $t\to\infty$, $\dfrac{t}{1+t}\to 1$, so $\ln\dfrac{t}{1+t}\to 0$. At $t=1$: $
\ln\dfrac{1}{2}=-\ln 2$. Therefore

$$
\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t(1+t)}\,dt = 0 - (-\ln 2) = \ln 2.
$$

### Conclusion

$$
\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{1}^{1+\delta}\frac{n}{x^{2}(1+x^{n})}
\,dx = \ln 2.}
$$
