## Analysis

**Problem.** Determine whether $D'(t)=2D(2t)-D(t)$, $D(0)=0$, admits a closed-fo
rm solution.

### 1. Power-series attempt (shows no analytic closed form)

Set $D(t)=\sum_{n\ge 0}a_n t^n$. Then $D'(t)=\sum (n+1)a_{n+1}t^n$ and $D(2t)=\s
um a_n 2^n t^n$, so
$$
(n+1)a_{n+1}=a_n\bigl(2^{n+1}-1\bigr),\qquad a_0=D(0)=0.
$$
Hence $a_1=0$, and inductively **every** $a_n=0$: the only analytic solution wit
h $D(0)=0$ is $D\equiv 0$.

Even ignoring the initial condition, the formal solution $a_n=a_0\prod_{k=1}^n(2
^k-1)/n!$ has
$$
|a_n|^{1/n}\sim \frac{2^{n/2}e}{n}\to\infty,
$$
so the **radius of convergence is $0$**: the Taylor series diverges for every $t
\neq 0$. This is the hallmark of the *advanced* pantograph ($q=2>1$): no nontriv
ial analytic solution exists at all.

### 2. Laplace transform (gives the actual solutions)

Let $\widehat D(s)=\int_0^\infty e^{-st}D(t)\,dt$. Using $\mathcal L\{D(2t)\}(s)
=\tfrac12\widehat D(s/2)$ and $D(0)=0$:
$$
s\widehat D(s)=\widehat D(s/2)-\widehat D(s)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (s+1)\wide
hat D(s)=\widehat D(s/2).
$$
Iterating,
$$
\widehat D(s)=\frac{\widehat D(s/2^n)}{\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1+s/2^k)}\xrightarrow[n
\to\infty]{}\frac{A}{\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}(1+s/2^k)}=\frac{A}{(-s;\tfrac12)_\inft
y}=A\,e_{1/2}(-s),
$$
where $A=\widehat D(0)=\int_0^\infty D$ and $(-s;\tfrac12)_\infty$ is a $q$-Poch
hammer product (a $q$-exponential). The product converges for all $s$; its zeros
 (poles of $\widehat D$) lie at $s=-2^k$, $k=0,1,2,\dots$

### 3. Inversion by residues

For $t>0$, close the Bromwich contour to the left and pick up residues at $s=-2^
m$. A direct computation of $\operatorname{Res}_{s=-2^m}$ using $\prod_{j\ne m}(
1-2^{m-j})=(-1)^m\bigl(\prod_{i=1}^m(2^i-1)\bigr)(\tfrac12;\tfrac12)_\infty$ giv
es, with $P_m:=\prod_{i=1}^m(2^i-1)$, $P_0:=1$,
$$
\boxed{\;D(t)=C\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^m\,2^m\,e^{-2^m t}}{P_m}\;},\qquad
C=\frac{A}{(\tfrac12;\tfrac12)_\infty}.
$$

### 4. The initial condition is automatic

At $t=0$ the partial sums satisfy the telescoping identity
$$
S_n:=\sum_{m=0}^n\frac{(-1)^m 2^m}{P_m}=\frac{(-1)^n}{P_n}\xrightarrow[n\to\inft
y]{}0,
$$
since $P_m\sim 2^{m(m+1)/2}(\tfrac12;\tfrac12)_\infty\to\infty$ super-exponentia
lly. Hence $D(0)=0$ **for every value of $C$**: there is a genuine one-parameter
 family of solutions, all satisfying $D(0)=0$. (The trivial solution is $C=0$.)

### 5. Verification

Uniform convergence on $[0,\infty)$ follows from the Weierstrass bound $2^m/P_m\
sim 2^{-m(m-1)/2}$ (and $4^m/P_m\sim 2^{-m(m-3)/2}$ for the derivative), so $D\i
n C^\infty$. Differentiating termwise,
$$
D'(t)=C\sum_{m\ge 0}\frac{(-1)^{m+1}4^m}{P_m}e^{-2^m t}.
$$
Using $P_m=(2^m-1)P_{m-1}$ one checks
$$
2D(2t)-D(t)=C\!\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{(-1)^n(2^n-4^n)}{P_n}e^{-2^n t}-Ce^{-t}=C\!\su
m_{n\ge 0}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}4^n}{P_n}e^{-2^n t}=D'(t).\;\checkmark
$$

### 6. Determination

The general solution is the **lacunary exponential series** above (equivalently,
 the inverse Laplace transform of the $q$-exponential $e_{1/2}(-s)=1/(-s;\tfrac1
2)_\infty$). It is **not** a finite combination of elementary or classical speci
al functions:

- the Taylor series diverges (zero radius of convergence), so no analytic closed
 form exists;
- the $q$-Pochhammer product $(-s;\tfrac12)_\infty$ and its inverse Laplace tran
sform are basic-hypergeometric objects with no elementary reduction;
- the residue expansion is an infinite lacunary Dirichlet series $\sum (-1)^m 2^
m e^{-2^m t}/\prod_{i=1}^m(2^i-1)$, which does not sum to any finite closed expr
ession.

**Conclusion.** The initial-value problem $D'(t)=2D(2t)-D(t)$, $D(0)=0$, is *not
 uniquely determined* (a one-parameter family of $C^\infty$ solutions exists, al
l flat at the origin), and **it cannot be solved in elementary closed form**. Th
e best explicit representation is the lacunary series
$$
D(t)=C\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^m\,2^m\,e^{-2^m t}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(2^i-1)},
$$
(or, equivalently, $D=\mathcal L^{-1}\!\bigl[A\,e_{1/2}(-s)\bigr]$), which is a
basic-hypergeometric/$q$-exponential object rather than a finite closed form.
