## Solution

**The space.** Let $X = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^3 : 1 \le |x| \le 2\}$, the closed sp
herical shell. The equivalence relation identifies each point $x$ on the inner s
phere $S_1 = \{|x|=1\}$ with the point $y = 2x$ on the outer sphere $S_2 = \{|x|
=2\}$ (same direction from the origin).

**Homeomorphism with $S^2 \times [1,2]$.** Every point $x \in X$ with $x \ne 0$
has a unique polar decomposition $x = r\,\hat{x}$ where $r = |x| \in [1,2]$ and
$\hat{x} = x/|x| \in S^2$. The map
$$\Phi : X \to S^2 \times [1,2], \qquad \Phi(x) = \bigl(x/|x|,\; |x|\bigr)$$
is a homeomorphism (continuous with continuous inverse $(\hat{x}, r) \mapsto r\h
at{x}$).

**Transporting the relation.** Under $\Phi$, a point $x \in S_1$ corresponds to
$(\hat{x}, 1) \in S^2 \times \{1\}$, and the identified point $y = 2x \in S_2$ c
orresponds to $(2x/|2x|, |2x|) = (\hat{x}, 2) \in S^2 \times \{2\}$. Thus the eq
uivalence relation becomes:
$$(\hat{x}, 1) \sim (\hat{x}, 2) \quad \text{for all } \hat{x} \in S^2.$$
This identifies the two boundary copies of $S^2$ via the identity map on the $S^
2$-factor.

**Quotient is $S^2 \times S^1$.** The quotient $S^2 \times [1,2] \big/ \bigl((\h
at{x},1)\sim(\hat{x},2)\bigr)$ is precisely $S^2 \times \bigl([1,2]/(1\sim 2)\bi
gr) = S^2 \times S^1$, since the relation acts only on the interval factor and g
lues its endpoints. Formally, the map
$$S^2 \times [1,2] \to S^2 \times S^1, \qquad (\hat{x}, r) \mapsto \bigl(\hat{x}
,\, e^{2\pi i r}\bigr)$$
has kernel of equivalence exactly $\{(\hat{x},1)\sim(\hat{x},2)\}$, so it descen
ds to a homeomorphism $X/{\sim} \cong S^2 \times S^1$.

**Fundamental group.** Since $S^2$ is simply connected ($\pi_1(S^2) = 0$) and $\
pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, the product formula gives
$$\pi_1(X/{\sim},\, x_0) \;\cong\; \pi_1(S^2 \times S^1) \;\cong\; \pi_1(S^2) \t
imes \pi_1(S^1) \;\cong\; 0 \times \mathbb{Z} \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}.$$

A generator is the loop that travels once around the $S^1$-factor, i.e. the imag
e in the quotient of any radial segment $\{r\hat{x}_0 : 1 \le r \le 2\}$ travers
ed from $S_1$ to $S_2$ (whose endpoints are identified, closing it into a loop).

$$\boxed{\pi_1(X/{\sim},\, x_0) \cong \mathbb{Z}}$$
