## Solution

**Setup.** Ed arranges 5 green (G) and r red (R) marbles in a row. There are r+4
 adjacent pairs. Each pair is either "same" (SS) or "different" (DD = color chan
ge). The condition is #same = #different, so each equals (r+4)/2, requiring r ev
en.

**Runs.** Let k be the number of runs (maximal monochromatic blocks). The number
 of color changes equals k−1, so #different = k−1 and #same = (r+4)−(k−1) = r+5−
k. Setting them equal:

$$r + 5 - k = k - 1 \implies k = \frac{r+6}{2}, \qquad r = 2k - 6.$$

Let g = #G-runs, h = #R-runs, so g + h = k. Since runs alternate, |g − h| ≤ 1. E
ach G-run needs ≥1 of the 5 green marbles, so **g ≤ 5**.

**Maximizing r.** Since r = 2k − 6, maximize k:
- k even: g = k/2 ≤ 5 → k ≤ 10 → r ≤ 14.
- k odd: the smaller run-count is (k−1)/2; set g = (k−1)/2 ≤ 5 → k ≤ 11 → r ≤ 16
.

For k = 11: g = 5, h = 6 (starts and ends with R). This is valid. For k = 12: g
= 6 > 5, impossible. So **m = 16**.

**Counting N.** With r = 16, k = 11. The only feasible split with g ≤ 5 and |g−h
| ≤ 1 is (g, h) = (5, 6) — the case (6, 5) is impossible since g = 6 > 5.

The arrangement must start and end with R: **R G R G R G R G R G R**. The 5 G-ru
ns each contain exactly 1 green (forced, since 5 greens fill 5 runs). The 6 R-ru
ns contain 16 reds total, each ≥ 1. The number of such distributions is the numb
er of positive integer solutions to

$$x_1 + x_2 + \cdots + x_6 = 16, \quad x_i \ge 1,$$

which is $\binom{16-1}{6-1} = \binom{15}{5}$.

Computing: $\binom{15}{5} = \frac{15 \cdot 14 \cdot 13 \cdot 12 \cdot 11}{120} =
 \frac{360360}{120} = 3003$.

**Verification.** Total marbles = 21, adjacent pairs = 20. Color changes = k−1 =
 10, same pairs = 20−10 = 10. ✓ The condition holds for every such arrangement,
and no other (g,h) is feasible.

$$N = 3003, \qquad N \bmod 1000 = \boxed{3}.$$
