## Proof

**Setup.** Let $A$ and $B$ be finite sets, and let $G$ be a finite group acting
on $A$. The group $G$ acts on $B^A$ (the set of all mappings $f:A\to B$) by
$$(g\cdot f)(a) \;=\; f\bigl(g^{-1}\cdot a\bigr), \qquad g\in G,\; a\in A,\; f\i
n B^A.$$
One verifies this is a genuine left action: $e\cdot f = f$, and $(gh)\cdot f = g
\cdot(h\cdot f)$.

**Goal.** By Burnside's lemma, the number of orbits of $G$ on $B^A$ is
$$\mathcal{N} \;=\; \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\bigl|\operatorname{Fix}(g)\bigr|,
$$
where $\operatorname{Fix}(g)=\{f\in B^A : g\cdot f = f\}$. We compute $|\operato
rname{Fix}(g)|$ in terms of the cycle structure of $g$ on $A$.

**Key observation.** A map $f:A\to B$ is fixed by $g$ iff
$$f(g^{-1}\cdot a)=f(a)\quad\forall\,a\in A \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(g\cd
ot a)=f(a)\quad\forall\,a\in A.$$
Equivalently, $f$ is constant on every orbit of the cyclic subgroup $\langle g\r
angle$ acting on $A$. The orbits of $\langle g\rangle$ on $A$ are precisely the
cycles in the cycle decomposition of the permutation $g$ on $A$.

**Counting fixed maps.** Suppose $g$ has exactly $k=c(g)$ cycles on $A$, namely
$C_1,\dots,C_k$. For $f$ to be fixed by $g$, $f$ must assign a single value of $
B$ to each cycle $C_i$, and these $k$ choices are independent. Hence
$$\bigl|\operatorname{Fix}(g)\bigr| \;=\; |B|^k \;=\; |B|^{c(g)}.$$

**Summing over $G$.** Grouping the elements of $G$ by their number of cycles on
$A$, and using the definition $c_k(G) = \#\{g\in G : c(g)=k\}$, we obtain
$$\sum_{g\in G}\bigl|\operatorname{Fix}(g)\bigr| \;=\; \sum_{g\in G}|B|^{c(g)} \
;=\; \sum_{k} c_k(G)\,|B|^k,$$
where the last sum ranges over all $k$ for which $c_k(G)\neq 0$ (in particular $
1\le k\le |A|$).

**Applying Burnside's lemma.** Dividing by $|G|$,
$$\boxed{\;\mathcal{N} \;=\; \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{k} c_k(G)\,|B|^k\;}.$$

**Sanity checks.**
- *Trivial group* $G=\{e\}$: $c_{|A|}(G)=1$ (identity has $|A|$ cycles) and all
other $c_k=0$, giving $\mathcal{N}=|B|^{|A|}$, i.e. every map is its own orbit.
✓
- *$G$ transitive on $A$, $|B|=1$*: only $c_1$ contributes a nonzero term via $|
B|^k=1$, giving $\mathcal{N}=c_1(G)/|G|$. Since $|B|=1$ there is a single map, h
ence one orbit; indeed the unique element with one cycle need not be all of $G$,
 but the formula still yields $1$ because $\sum_k c_k(G)=|G|$ and $|B|^k=1$ for
all $k$, so $\mathcal{N}=|G|/|G|=1$. ✓
- *$B$-independence on cycle count*: the formula depends on $G$ only through the
 cycle-index statistics $c_k(G)$, which is the content of the **cycle index poly
nomial** $Z_G(x_1,\dots,x_{|A|})=\frac1{|G|}\sum_k c_k(G)x_1^{\,?}\cdots$ specia
lized to $x_i=|B|$. Indeed $\mathcal{N}=Z_G(|B|,\dots,|B|)$ when $G$ acts on $A$
 and we color $A$ with $|B|$ colors. ✓

This completes the derivation.
