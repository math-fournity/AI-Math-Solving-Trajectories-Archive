## Solution

We seek the first two terms of the asymptotic expansion of
$$I(k)=\int_{0}^{5}(t^{2}+2t)^{-1/2}e^{-kt}\,dt,\qquad k\to\infty.$$

### Setup: Watson's lemma

As $k\to\infty$ the factor $e^{-kt}$ localizes the integral to a neighborhood of
 the left endpoint $t=0$, where the integrand is singular. Watson's lemma applie
s: we expand the non-exponential factor in powers of $t$ near $0$ and integrate
term-by-term against $e^{-kt}$ over $[0,\infty)$.

### Expansion of the amplitude near $t=0$

Factor
$$t^{2}+2t=t(t+2)=2t\!\left(1+\tfrac{t}{2}\right),$$
so
$$(t^{2}+2t)^{-1/2}=(2t)^{-1/2}\!\left(1+\tfrac{t}{2}\right)^{-1/2}=\frac{1}{\sq
rt{2}}\,t^{-1/2}\!\left(1+\tfrac{t}{2}\right)^{-1/2}.$$

Using the binomial series $(1+u)^{-1/2}=1-\tfrac12 u+\tfrac38 u^{2}-\cdots$ with
 $u=t/2$:
$$\left(1+\tfrac{t}{2}\right)^{-1/2}=1-\frac{t}{4}+\frac{3t^{2}}{32}-\cdots$$

Hence
$$(t^{2}+2t)^{-1/2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,t^{-1/2}\!\left(1-\frac{t}{4}+\frac{3t^{
2}}{32}-\cdots\right).$$

### Term-by-term integration

Watson's lemma gives, with $\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}e^{-kt}\,dt=\Gamma(s)\,k^{-s
}$:

$$I(k)\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1/2}e^{-kt}\,dt-\frac14\i
nt_{0}^{\infty}t^{1/2}e^{-kt}\,dt+\cdots\right].$$

Using $\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt\pi$ and $\Gamma(3/2)=\tfrac12\sqrt\pi$:

$$\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1/2}e^{-kt}\,dt=\sqrt\pi\,k^{-1/2},\qquad \int_{0}^{\inft
y}t^{1/2}e^{-kt}\,dt=\tfrac12\sqrt\pi\,k^{-3/2}.$$

Therefore
$$I(k)\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[\sqrt\pi\,k^{-1/2}-\frac14\cdot\frac{\sqrt\pi}
{2}\,k^{-3/2}+\cdots\right]
=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,k^{-1/2}-\frac18\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,k^{-3/2}+\cdots.$$

### Justification of the upper-limit truncation

The replacement of $[0,5]$ by $[0,\infty)$ is exponentially accurate: for any $\
delta>0$, the contribution from $[\delta,5]$ is $O(e^{-k\delta})$, which is smal
ler than every power $k^{-N}$. The Watson series is thus an asymptotic expansion
 in integer steps of $k^{-1/2}$, and the two leading terms displayed above are c
orrect to the stated order.

### Result

The first two terms of the asymptotic expansion as $k\to\infty$ are

$$\boxed{\,I(k)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,k^{-1/2}\;-\;\frac18\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}
}\,k^{-3/2}\,}$$

i.e. $I(k)\sim\sqrt{\dfrac{\pi}{2k}}-\dfrac{1}{8}\sqrt{\dfrac{\pi}{2}}\,k^{-3/2}
+\cdots$.
