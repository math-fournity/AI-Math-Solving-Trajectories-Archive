## Proof

The answer is **No**. A counterexample is the symmetric group $G = S_3$.

**$S_3$ is supersolvable.** The series
$$1 \trianglelefteq A_3 \trianglelefteq S_3$$
is a normal series with cyclic factors: $A_3 \cong C_3$ and $S_3/A_3 \cong C_2$,
 both cyclic.

**Prime divisors of $|S_3|$.** We have $|S_3| = 6 = 2 \cdot 3$, so the prime div
isors are $2$ and $3$.

**Minimal normal subgroups of $S_3$.** The normal subgroups of $S_3$ are exactly
 $1$, $A_3$, and $S_3$. Hence the unique minimal normal subgroup is $A_3$, which
 has order $3$.

**Failure for the prime $2$.** The only minimal normal subgroup is $A_3$ of orde
r $3$, and $2 \nmid 3$. The subgroups of order $2$ in $S_3$ are $\langle(12)\ran
gle$, $\langle(13)\rangle$, $\langle(23)\rangle$, none of which is normal in $S_
3$ (they are conjugate to each other). Therefore $S_3$ has **no** minimal normal
 subgroup whose order is divisible by $2$.

**Conclusion.** $S_3$ is a finite supersolvable group, yet it does not possess a
 minimal normal subgroup for the prime divisor $2$ of its order. The statement i
s therefore false.
