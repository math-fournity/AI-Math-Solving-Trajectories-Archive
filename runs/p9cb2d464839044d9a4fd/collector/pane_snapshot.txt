## (a) Verification of the Solutions

**The PDE and boundary conditions.** We consider Laplace's equation $u_{xx}+u_{y
y}=0$ on the upper half-plane $\{(x,y): -\infty<x<\infty,\; y>0\}$, with
$$u(x,0)=0, \qquad u_y(x,0)=\tfrac{1}{n}\sin(nx).$$

**Case 1: $u_y(x,0)=0\;\Rightarrow\; u\equiv 0$.**

The zero function has all derivatives identically zero, so $u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0$, $u
(x,0)=0$, and $u_y(x,0)=0$. All conditions are satisfied. $\checkmark$

**Case 2: $u(x,y)=\frac{1}{n^2}\sin(nx)\sinh(ny)$ for $n>0$.**

Compute the partial derivatives:

$$u_x = \frac{1}{n}\cos(nx)\sinh(ny), \qquad u_{xx} = -\sin(nx)\sinh(ny),$$

$$u_y = \frac{1}{n}\sin(nx)\cosh(ny), \qquad u_{yy} = \sin(nx)\sinh(ny).$$

**Laplace's equation:**
$$u_{xx}+u_{yy} = -\sin(nx)\sinh(ny)+\sin(nx)\sinh(ny)=0.\;\checkmark$$

**Boundary condition on $u$:**
$$u(x,0)=\frac{1}{n^2}\sin(nx)\underbrace{\sinh(0)}_{=0}=0.\;\checkmark$$

**Boundary condition on $u_y$:**
$$u_y(x,0)=\frac{1}{n}\sin(nx)\underbrace{\cosh(0)}_{=1}=\frac{1}{n}\sin(nx).\;\
checkmark$$

All conditions are verified.

## (b) Why the Problem Is Not Well-Posed

A problem is **well-posed in the sense of Hadamard** if it satisfies three condi
tions:

1. **Existence** — a solution exists.
2. **Uniqueness** — the solution is unique.
3. **Continuous dependence on the data** — the solution changes by a small amoun
t when the data change by a small amount.

The failure here is in condition **(3)**.

Consider the family of boundary-value problems indexed by $n=1,2,3,\dots$, each
with data
$$u_y^{(n)}(x,0)=\frac{1}{n}\sin(nx).$$

As $n\to\infty$, the boundary data converge **uniformly to zero**:
$$\|u_y^{(n)}(\cdot,0)-0\|_{\infty}=\frac{1}{n}\longrightarrow 0.$$

The zero boundary data corresponds to the trivial solution $u\equiv 0$. So if th
e problem were well-posed, the solutions $u^{(n)}$ should converge to $u\equiv 0
$.

However, the actual solutions are
$$u^{(n)}(x,y)=\frac{1}{n^2}\sin(nx)\sinh(ny).$$

For any **fixed** $y>0$, using $\sinh(ny)\sim \frac{1}{2}e^{ny}$ as $n\to\infty$
:
$$|u^{(n)}(x,y)|\le \frac{1}{n^2}\sinh(ny)\sim \frac{e^{ny}}{2n^2}\xrightarrow[n
\to\infty]{}\infty.$$

The exponential growth $e^{ny}$ overwhelms the polynomial decay $1/n^2$, so **th
e solutions diverge** even though the boundary data converge to zero.

**Conclusion.** We have a sequence of problems whose boundary data converge unif
ormly to the data of the trivial problem, yet whose solutions **blow up** at eve
ry point with $y>0$. This is a direct violation of continuous dependence on the
data: an arbitrarily small perturbation of the boundary data can produce an arbi
trarily large change in the solution. Therefore the Cauchy problem for Laplace's
 equation on the half-plane is **not well-posed**.
