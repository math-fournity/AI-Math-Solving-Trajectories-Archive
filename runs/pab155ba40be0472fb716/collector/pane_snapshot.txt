## (a) Equilibrium points

At equilibrium, $x(t) \equiv x^*$ is constant, so $x(t-\tau)=x^*$ and $\dot x =
0$:
$$0 = -x^{*3} - x^* = -x^*(x^{*2}+1).$$
For real $x^*$, $x^{*2}+1>0$, hence the **only real equilibrium is**
$$\boxed{x^* = 0.}$$

## (b) Characteristic equation of the linearization

Set $x(t)=x^*+y(t)=y(t)$. Then
$$\dot y(t) = -y(t)^3 - y(t-\tau).$$
Keeping only linear terms (the cubic is higher order): $\dot y(t) = -y(t-\tau)$.
 Seeking $y=e^{\lambda t}$ gives
$$\lambda\,e^{\lambda t} = -e^{\lambda(t-\tau)} \;\Longrightarrow\; \lambda + e^
{-\lambda\tau} = 0.$$
This is the **characteristic (quasi-polynomial) equation**
$$\boxed{\Delta(\lambda)\equiv \lambda + e^{-\lambda\tau}=0.}$$
(For a DDE this is transcendental, not an ordinary polynomial; it is the charact
eristic equation of the linearized delay system.)

## (c) Nyquist criterion

Rewrite $\Delta(\lambda)=0$ in the standard closed-loop form $1+G(s)=0$:
$$1 + \frac{e^{-s\tau}}{s} = 0, \qquad G(s)=\frac{e^{-s\tau}}{s}.$$
The open-loop transfer function is $G(s)=e^{-s\tau}/s$. Its only pole is at $s=0
$ (on the imaginary axis); with the standard Nyquist indentation to the right of
 the origin, the number of open-loop poles in the open RHP is $P=0$.

The Nyquist plot is $G(j\omega)=\dfrac{e^{-j\omega\tau}}{j\omega}=\dfrac{-\sin(\
omega\tau)-j\cos(\omega\tau)}{\omega}$, so
$$\operatorname{Re}G(j\omega)=-\frac{\sin(\omega\tau)}{\omega},\qquad \operatorn
ame{Im}G(j\omega)=-\frac{\cos(\omega\tau)}{\omega}.$$

Key features:
- As $\omega\to 0^+$: $\operatorname{Re}G\to -\tau$, $\operatorname{Im}G\to -\in
fty$ (the plot enters from $-j\infty$ at real part $-\tau$).
- As $\omega\to +\infty$: $G(j\omega)\to 0$, spiraling into the origin.
- Real-axis crossings occur where $\cos(\omega\tau)=0$, i.e. $\omega_k=\dfrac{(2
k+1)\pi}{2\tau}$, $k=0,1,2,\dots$, with
  $$\operatorname{Re}G(j\omega_k)=-\frac{(-1)^k}{\omega_k}=\frac{(-1)^{k+1}\,2\t
au}{(2k+1)\pi}.$$
  The **leftmost** real-axis crossing is at $k=0$:
  $$\operatorname{Re}G\!\left(j\frac{\pi}{2\tau}\right)=-\frac{2\tau}{\pi}.$$

The full Nyquist contour (positive-frequency spiral, its mirror image, and the i
mage of the indentation — a large semicircle centered at $-\tau$ bulging into th

tmost real-axis crossing lies to the left of $-1$:
$$-\frac{2\tau}{\pi} < -1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \tau > \frac{\pi}{2}.$$

$x^*=0$ is **asymptotically stable**.
- **$\tau = \pi/2$:** $-2\tau/\pi = -1$, the plot passes exactly through $-1$. E
