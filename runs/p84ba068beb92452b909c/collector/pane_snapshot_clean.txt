## Proof

The statement is **false**.

**Key observation.** The hypothesis — that $R_{\mathfrak p}$ is a regular local
ring for every prime ideal $\mathfrak p \subseteq R$ — is precisely the definiti
on of $R$ being a **regular ring** (in the sense of commutative algebra). Howeve
r, the conclusion requires $R$ to be a **regular local ring**, which demands tha
t $R$ itself be a *local* ring (i.e., possess a unique maximal ideal). A general
 regular ring need not be local, so the implication fails.

**Counterexample.** Let $R = k[x]$, the polynomial ring in one variable over a f
ield $k$.

- The prime ideals of $R$ are $(0)$ and the ideals $(f)$ generated by irreducibl
e polynomials $f \in k[x]$.
- $R_{(0)} = k(x)$, the fraction field, which is a regular local ring (a field i
s a regular local ring of Krull dimension $0$).
- $R_{(f)}$ is a discrete valuation ring for each irreducible $f$, hence a regul
ar local ring of Krull dimension $1$.

Thus $R_{\mathfrak p}$ is a regular local ring for **every** prime $\mathfrak p$
 of $R$.

Yet $R = k[x]$ is **not** a local ring: it has infinitely many maximal ideals $(
f)$ for the various irreducible polynomials $f$. Since a regular local ring must
, by definition, be local, $R = k[x]$ is not a regular local ring.

Therefore the hypothesis holds while the conclusion fails, and the statement is
**false**. $\blacksquare$
