## Proof

Let \(W(t)\) be a standard Brownian motion. By definition, \(W\) has independent
 increments, and each increment \(W(t) - W(s)\) (for \(t > s\)) is distributed a
s \(N(0, t-s)\), independent of the past.

Define the three independent increments:

\[
X_1 = W(1) - W(0) = W(1), \quad X_2 = W(2) - W(1), \quad X_3 = W(3) - W(2).
\]

By the independent-increments property, \(X_1, X_2, X_3\) are mutually independe
nt, each \(N(0,1)\).

Express the values of \(W\) at times 1, 2, 3 in terms of these increments:

\[
W(1) = X_1, \quad W(2) = X_1 + X_2, \quad W(3) = X_1 + X_2 + X_3.
\]

Now rewrite the event \(\{W(3) > W(2) > W(1)\}\):

\[
W(2) > W(1) \iff X_1 + X_2 > X_1 \iff X_2 > 0,
\]

\[
W(3) > W(2) \iff X_1 + X_2 + X_3 > X_1 + X_2 \iff X_3 > 0.
\]

Notably, \(X_1\) cancels out completely and places no constraint on the event. T
herefore

\[
\{W(3) > W(2) > W(1)\} = \{X_2 > 0\} \cap \{X_3 > 0\}.
\]

Since \(X_2\) and \(X_3\) are independent and each is symmetric about 0 (being c
entered Gaussian),

\[
P(X_2 > 0) = \tfrac{1}{2}, \qquad P(X_3 > 0) = \tfrac{1}{2}.
\]

By independence,

\[
P\bigl(W(3) > W(2) > W(1)\bigr) = P(X_2 > 0)\,P(X_3 > 0) = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\tfr
ac{1}{2} = \tfrac{1}{4}.
\]

\[
\boxed{P\bigl(W(3) > W(2) > W(1)\bigr) = \dfrac{1}{4}}
\]
