**Proof.**

Define
$$g(z) := f(z) - \sin z, \qquad z \in D(0,1).$$

Since $f$ is holomorphic on $D(0,1)$ and $\sin z$ is entire (hence holomorphic o
n $D(0,1)$), the function $g$ is holomorphic on $D(0,1)$.

By hypothesis there exists a sequence $(z_n)$ of **distinct** points in $D(0,1)$
 with $z_n \to 0$ and $f(z_n) = \sin z_n$ for every $n$. Therefore
$$g(z_n) = f(z_n) - \sin z_n = 0 \qquad \text{for all } n.$$

Thus each $z_n$ is a zero of $g$. The sequence $(z_n)$ consists of distinct poin
ts and converges to $0$, so the zeros of $g$ accumulate at the point $0$. Moreov
er $0 \in D(0,1)$, which is an **interior point** of the domain of holomorphy of
 $g$.

We now apply the **identity theorem** for holomorphic functions:

> *If $h$ is holomorphic on a connected open set $\Omega$, and the zeros of $h$
accumulate at a point of $\Omega$, then $h \equiv 0$ on $\Omega$.*

The disk $D(0,1)$ is connected and open, $g$ is holomorphic on it, and the zeros
 of $g$ accumulate at $0 \in D(0,1)$. Hence
$$g \equiv 0 \quad \text{on } D(0,1).$$

Recalling the definition of $g$, this gives
$$f(z) - \sin z = 0 \qquad \text{for all } z \in D(0,1),$$
i.e.
$$f(z) = \sin z \qquad \text{for all } z \in D(0,1). \qquad \blacksquare$$
