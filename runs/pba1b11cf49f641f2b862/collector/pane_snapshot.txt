## Proof

We compute the operator norm of $T:(C[0,\pi],\|\cdot\|_\infty)\to(C[0,\pi],\|\cd
ot\|_\infty)$, where
$$T(f)(x)=\int_0^x \sin(x+y)\,f(y)\,dy.$$

### Upper bound

For any $f\in C[0,\pi]$ with $\|f\|_\infty\le 1$ and any $x\in[0,\pi]$,
$$|T(f)(x)|=\left|\int_0^x \sin(x+y)\,f(y)\,dy\right|\le\int_0^x|\sin(x+y)|\,dy.
$$
Substituting $u=x+y$,
$$\int_0^x|\sin(x+y)|\,dy=\int_x^{2x}|\sin u|\,du=:h(x).$$
Hence $\|T(f)\|_\infty\le\sup_{x\in[0,\pi]}h(x)$, giving $\|T\|\le\sup_x h(x)$.

**Evaluating $h(x)=\int_x^{2x}|\sin u|\,du$.**

- **$x\in[0,\pi/2]$:** then $2x\le\pi$, so $[x,2x]\subseteq[0,\pi]$ and $|\sin u
|=\sin u$:
 $$h(x)=\cos x-\cos 2x.$$
- **$x\in[\pi/2,\pi]$:** then $2x\in[\pi,2\pi]$, so we split at $\pi$:
 $$h(x)=\int_x^\pi\sin u\,du+\int_\pi^{2x}(-\sin u)\,du=(1+\cos x)+(1+\cos 2x)=2
+\cos x+\cos 2x.$$

**Maximizing.** On $[0,\pi/2]$: $h'(x)=\sin x(4\cos x-1)$, critical point $\cos
x=\tfrac14$ gives $h=\tfrac14-(-\tfrac78)=\tfrac98$; endpoints give $h(0)=0$, $h
(\pi/2)=1$. On $[\pi/2,\pi]$: $h'(x)=-\sin x(1+4\cos x)$, with a local minimum $
\tfrac78$ at $\cos x=-\tfrac14$, and endpoints $h(\pi/2)=1$, $h(\pi)=2+\cos\pi+\
cos 2\pi=2-1+1=2$.

Thus $\sup_{x\in[0,\pi]}h(x)=2$, and
$$\|T\|\le 2.$$

### Lower bound (attained)

Take the constant function $f\equiv -1$, which is continuous with $\|f\|_\infty=
1$. Then
$$T(f)(x)=-\int_0^x\sin(x+y)\,dy=-(\cos x-\cos 2x)=\cos 2x-\cos x.$$
Evaluating at $x=\pi$:
$$T(f)(\pi)=\cos(2\pi)-\cos\pi=1-(-1)=2.$$
Therefore $\|T(f)\|_\infty\ge|T(f)(\pi)|=2$, so $\|T\|\ge 2$.

### Conclusion

Combining both bounds,
$$\boxed{\|T\|=2}.$$
