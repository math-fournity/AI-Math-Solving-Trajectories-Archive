**Claim:** Yes. If $C \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is compact, convex, and has non-empt
y interior, then $\partial C$ is homeomorphic to $S^{n-1} = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n
 : \|x\|=1\}$.

---

## Proof

**Setup and a key lemma.** Pick a point $p \in \operatorname{int}(C)$. Since $\o
peratorname{int}(C)$ is open and non-empty, there exists $\varepsilon > 0$ with
$B(p,\varepsilon) \subset C$.

> **Lemma (radial interior).** *If $p \in \operatorname{int}(C)$ and $y \in C$,
then the half-open segment $[p, y) = \{(1-t)p + ty : 0 \le t < 1\}$ lies in $\op
eratorname{int}(C)$.*
>
> *Proof.* Let $z = (1-t)p + ty$ with $0 \le t < 1$. For any $w \in B(z,\,(1-t)\
varepsilon)$, write $w = z + (1-t)\varepsilon\, s$ with $\|s\|\le 1$. Then
> $$w = (1-t)\underbrace{(p + \varepsilon\, s)}_{\in\, B(p,\varepsilon)\,\subset
\, C} + t\,y \;\in\; C$$
> by convexity, since $p+\varepsilon s \in B(p,\varepsilon)\subset C$ and $y\in
C$. Thus $B(z,(1-t)\varepsilon)\subset C$, so $z\in\operatorname{int}(C)$. $\squ
are$

**Defining the radial map.** For each unit vector $u \in S^{n-1}$, consider the
ray $p + t u$, $t \ge 0$. Since $C$ is bounded, the ray eventually leaves $C$; s
ince $C$ is closed, the last point of contact with $C$ is attained. Define
$$\rho(u) \;=\; \sup\{\, t \ge 0 : p + t u \in C \,\}.$$
Then $\rho(u) < \infty$ (boundedness of $C$), $\rho(u) \ge \varepsilon > 0$ (sin
ce $B(p,\varepsilon)\subset C$), and $p + \rho(u)\,u \in C$ (closedness). Moreov
er $p+\rho(u)u \in \partial C$: for any $t > \rho(u)$, $p+tu \notin C$ by defini
tion of the supremum, so every neighborhood of $p+\rho(u)u$ meets both $C$ and i
ts complement.

Define $f : S^{n-1} \to \partial C$ by
$$f(u) = p + \rho(u)\,u.$$

**$f$ is surjective.** Let $x \in \partial C \subset C$ (closedness). Set $u = (
x-p)/\|x-p\| \in S^{n-1}$ and $t_0 = \|x-p\| > 0$ (note $x \neq p$ since $p \in
\operatorname{int}(C)$ but $x \in \partial C$). Since $p, x \in C$ and $C$ is co
nvex, $[p,x] \subset C$, so $\rho(u) \ge t_0$. If $\rho(u) > t_0$, then $y := p
+ \rho(u)\,u \in C$ with $y$ strictly beyond $x$ on the ray, and $x \in [p, y)$,
 so by the Lemma $x \in \operatorname{int}(C)$—contradicting $x \in \partial C$.
 Hence $\rho(u) = t_0$ and $f(u) = p + t_0 u = x$.

**$f$ is injective.** If $f(u) = f(v)$, then $\rho(u)\,u = \rho(v)\,v$. Taking n
orms gives $\rho(u) = \rho(v) > 0$, hence $u = v$.

**$\rho$ (and thus $f$) is continuous.** Let $u_k \to u$ in $S^{n-1}$ and set $t
_k = \rho(u_k)$. The sequence $\{t_k\}$ is bounded (since $C$ is bounded), so ev
ery subsequence has a convergent sub-subsequence.

*Upper semicontinuity ($\limsup t_k \le \rho(u)$):* If $t_{k_j} \to t^*$, then $
p + t_{k_j}\,u_{k_j} \to p + t^* u$. Each point lies in $C$ (closed), so $p + t^
* u \in C$, giving $t^* \le \rho(u)$.

*Lower semicontinuity ($\liminf t_k \ge \rho(u)$):* Suppose for contradiction th
at some subsequence $t_{k_j} \to t^* < \rho(u)$. Then $p + t^* u \in [p,\, p+\rh
o(u)u) \subset \operatorname{int}(C)$ by the Lemma, so there is $\delta > 0$ wit
h $B(p+t^*u,\,\delta) \subset C$. For large $j$,
$$\|p + t_{k_j}u_{k_j} - (p + t^* u)\| < \delta,$$
so $p + t_{k_j}u_{k_j} \in \operatorname{int}(C)$. But $p + t_{k_j}u_{k_j} = f(u
_{k_j}) \in \partial C$, and $\operatorname{int}(C) \cap \partial C = \varnothin
g$—contradiction.

Thus $\rho$ is continuous, and $f(u) = p + \rho(u)\,u$ is continuous.

**$f$ is a homeomorphism.** $S^{n-1}$ is compact, $\partial C \subset \mathbb{R}
^n$ is Hausdorff, and $f : S^{n-1} \to \partial C$ is a continuous bijection. A
continuous bijection from a compact space onto a Hausdorff space is a homeomorph
ism. $\square$

Therefore the boundary of $C$ **is** homeomorphic to $S^{n-1}$.
