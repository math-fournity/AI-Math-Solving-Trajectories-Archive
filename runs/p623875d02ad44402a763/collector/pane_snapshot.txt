## Proof

### Step 1: Kummer's Theorem

By Kummer's theorem, $v_p\!\left(\binom{2n}{n}\right)$ equals the number of carr
ies when adding $n + n$ in base $p$.

### Step 2: A Sufficient Residue Condition

**Claim:** If $n \equiv -1 \pmod{p^{10}}$, then $v_p\!\left(\binom{2n}{n}\right)
 \geq 10$.

*Proof of claim:* The residue $n \equiv -1 \pmod{p^{10}}$ means the last 10 base
-$p$ digits of $n$ are all $p-1$. When computing $n + n$ in base $p$:
- **Position 0:** $2(p-1) = 2p - 2 \geq p$ (since $p \geq 2$), so a carry is pro
duced. Carry out: $c_1 = 1$.
- **Position $j$** for $1 \leq j \leq 9$: $2(p-1) + 1 = 2p - 1 \geq p$, so a car
ry is produced. Carry out: $c_{j+1} = 1$.

Thus at least 10 carries occur among the first 10 positions, giving $v_p\!\left(
\binom{2n}{n}\right) \geq 10$. $\square$

### Step 3: Chinese Remainder Theorem

Set $M = \prod_{i=1}^{2020} p_i^{10}$. Since $p_1, \ldots, p_{2020}$ are distinc
t primes, the moduli $p_1^{10}, \ldots, p_{2020}^{10}$ are pairwise coprime. By
the Chinese Remainder Theorem, there exists a unique residue $r^* \in \{0, 1, \l
dots, M-1\}$ satisfying:
$$r^* \equiv -1 \pmod{p_i^{10}} \quad \text{for all } i = 1, \ldots, 2020.$$

### Step 4: Equidistribution of $a_k \bmod M$

We use **Vinogradov's theorem**: for any irrational $\alpha$, the sequence $\{p_
k \alpha\}_{k=1}^{\infty}$ is equidistributed modulo 1.

Since $\nu$ is irrational and $M$ is a positive integer, $\nu/M$ is irrational.
By Vinogradov's theorem, $\{p_k \cdot (\nu/M)\} = \{p_k \nu / M\}$ is equidistri
buted mod 1, which means $p_k \nu \bmod M$ is equidistributed in $[0, M)$.

Now write $p_k \nu = q_k M + s_k$ where $q_k = \lfloor p_k \nu / M \rfloor$ and
$s_k = p_k \nu \bmod M \in (0, M)$. (We have $s_k \neq 0$ since $p_k \nu = q_k M

imilarly $s_k$ is never an integer.)

Then:
$$a_k = \lfloor p_k \nu \rfloor = q_k M + \lfloor s_k \rfloor, \quad \text{so} \
quad a_k \bmod M = \lfloor s_k \rfloor.$$

Since $s_k$ is equidistributed in $[0, M)$, the sequence $\lfloor s_k \rfloor$ i
s equidistributed in $\{0, 1, \ldots, M-1\}$: each value $r$ is attained when $s
_k \in [r, r+1)$, an interval of measure $1/M$.

### Step 5: Infinitely Many Good $k$

By equidistribution, the set $\{k \geq 1 : a_k \equiv r^* \pmod{M}\}$ is infinit

For every such $k$:
$$a_k \equiv r^* \equiv -1 \pmod{p_i^{10}} \quad \text{for all } i = 1, \ldots,
2020.$$

s, 2020$.
