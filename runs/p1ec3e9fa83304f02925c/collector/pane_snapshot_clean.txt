## Proof

**Answer: No.** The uniform limit of a sequence of Lipschitz functions with unif
ormly bounded derivatives need not be differentiable everywhere.

### Counterexample

Define on $\mathbb{R}$:

$$f_n(x) = \sqrt{x^2 + \tfrac{1}{n^2}}, \qquad n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$$

**Each $f_n$ is smooth (hence Lipschitz) with uniformly bounded derivative.** Si
nce $f_n$ is smooth, its derivative is

$$f_n'(x) = \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1/n^2}}.$$

We have $|f_n'(x)| \leq 1$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$ and every $n$. Hence eac
h $f_n$ is $1$-Lipschitz, and the Lipschitz constants (equivalently, the bounds
on the derivatives) are **uniformly bounded** by $L = 1$.

**The sequence converges uniformly to $f(x) = |x|$.** For every $x$,

$$0 \leq f_n(x) - |x| = \sqrt{x^2 + \tfrac{1}{n^2}} - |x| = \frac{1/n^2}{\sqrt{x
^2 + 1/n^2} + |x|} \leq \frac{1/n^2}{1/n} = \frac{1}{n}.$$

Thus $\|f_n - |\cdot|\,\|_\infty \leq 1/n \to 0$, giving uniform convergence on
all of $\mathbb{R}$.

**The limit $f(x) = |x|$ is not differentiable at $x = 0$.** The left derivative
 at $0$ is $-1$ and the right derivative is $+1$, so $f'(0)$ does not exist.

### Conclusion

We have exhibited a sequence of smooth (hence Lipschitz) functions with derivati
ves uniformly bounded by $1$, converging uniformly to $|x|$, which fails to be d
ifferentiable at the origin. Therefore, the uniform limit of a sequence of Lipsc
hitz functions with bounded derivatives is **not** necessarily differentiable ev
erywhere.
