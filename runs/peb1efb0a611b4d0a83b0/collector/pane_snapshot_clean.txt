## Generalization

**Theorem.** Let $D \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ be an open disc and $f : D \to \mathbb
{C}$ holomorphic. If the image $f(D)$ is contained in the zero locus of a **nonc
onstant real polynomial** $P(x,y)$ in two real variables, i.e.

$$f(D) \subseteq Z(P) := \{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^2 : P(x,y)=0\},$$

then $f$ is constant.

This generalizes the four classical cases, since a line ($ax+by+c=0$), a circle
($x^2+y^2-r^2=0$), a parabola ($y-x^2=0$), and a hyperbola ($x^2/a^2-y^2/b^2-1=0
$) are each the zero set of a nonconstant real polynomial.

---

## Proof

We use the **open mapping theorem**: a nonconstant holomorphic function on a dom
ain maps open sets to open sets.

**Key lemma.** *If $P(x,y)$ is a nonconstant real polynomial, then $Z(P)$ has em
pty interior in $\mathbb{R}^2$.*

*Proof of lemma.* Suppose $Z(P)$ contains a nonempty open set $U$. Then $P$ vani
shes identically on $U$. Since $P$ is a polynomial, it is real-analytic on all o
f $\mathbb{R}^2$. By the identity theorem for real-analytic functions (a real-an
alytic function vanishing on an open set vanishes everywhere), $P \equiv 0$ on $
\mathbb{R}^2$, contradicting that $P$ is nonconstant (hence nonzero). $\square$

**Main argument.** Suppose for contradiction that $f$ is nonconstant. Since $D$
is a domain (open and connected), the open mapping theorem gives that $f(D)$ is
an **open** subset of $\mathbb{C} \cong \mathbb{R}^2$. Moreover $f(D)$ is nonemp
ty since $D$ is nonempty.

By hypothesis $f(D) \subseteq Z(P)$, so $f(D)$ is a nonempty open set contained
in $Z(P)$. But by the lemma, $Z(P)$ has empty interior — meaning it contains no
nonempty open set. This is a contradiction.

Therefore $f$ must be constant. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** The same argument shows more generally that if $f$ is holomorphic on
 any domain $\Omega$ and $f(\Omega)$ is contained in any subset of $\mathbb{C}$
with empty interior, then $f$ is constant. The real-algebraic-curve condition is
 the natural common generalization of line, circle, parabola, and hyperbola, sin
ce all four are zero sets of nonconstant real polynomials, and every such zero s
et has empty interior.
