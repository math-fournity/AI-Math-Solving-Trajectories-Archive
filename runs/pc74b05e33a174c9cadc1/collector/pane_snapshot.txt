## Solution

**Claim:** No such number $K$ exists.

**Proof.** We show that for every positive integer $K$, there exists a natural n
umber $n$ such that none of $n, n+1, \ldots, n+K-1$ is a product of two distinct
 primes.

Recall that a positive integer $m$ is a product of two distinct primes if and on
ly if $m = pq$ for primes $p < q$, equivalently $\Omega(m) = 2$ and $\omega(m) =
 2$, where $\Omega(m)$ counts prime factors with multiplicity and $\omega(m)$ co
unts distinct prime factors. In particular, if $\Omega(m) \ge 3$, then $m$ is **
not** a product of two distinct primes.

**Construction.** Fix an arbitrary positive integer $K$. We will find $n$ such t
hat $\Omega(n+i) \ge 3$ for every $i = 0, 1, \ldots, K-1$.

Select $3K$ **distinct** primes
$$p_0, q_0, r_0,\; p_1, q_1, r_1,\; \ldots,\; p_{K-1}, q_{K-1}, r_{K-1},$$
which is possible since there are infinitely many primes. For each $i \in \{0, 1
, \ldots, K-1\}$, impose the congruence
$$n \equiv -i \pmod{p_i\, q_i\, r_i}.$$

Since all $3K$ primes are distinct, the moduli $p_i q_i r_i$ are pairwise coprim
e. By the **Chinese Remainder Theorem**, there exists an integer $n$ satisfying
all $K$ congruences simultaneously. Moreover, by adding a sufficiently large mul
tiple of the product $M = \prod_{i=0}^{K-1} p_i q_i r_i$, we may take $n$ to be
a (large) natural number.

For each $i$, the congruence gives $p_i q_i r_i \mid (n + i)$, so
$$\Omega(n + i) \;\ge\; \Omega(p_i q_i r_i) \;=\; 3.$$

Therefore $n + i$ has at least three prime factors (counted with multiplicity) a
nd hence **cannot** be a product of two distinct primes.

Since every one of $n, n+1, \ldots, n+K-1$ has $\Omega \ge 3$, none of them is a
 product of two distinct primes. This holds for the **arbitrary** $K$ we fixed a
t the outset.

**Conclusion.** For every candidate $K$, we have exhibited a block of $K$ consec
utive natural numbers containing no product of two distinct primes. Hence no sin
gle $K$ can guarantee that every block of $K$ consecutive natural numbers contai
ns such a number.

$$\boxed{\text{No such } K \text{ exists.}}$$
