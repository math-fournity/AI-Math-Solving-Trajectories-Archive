## Problem

Evaluate $$I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x^6+1}\,dx.$$

## Proof

**Step 1: Set up the contour integral.**

Consider $f(z) = \frac{1}{z^6+1}$ and the standard semicircular contour $C_R$ co
nsisting of $[-R,R]$ on the real axis followed by the upper semicircle $\Gamma_R
$ of radius $R$. Since $|f(z)| = O(R^{-6})$ on $\Gamma_R$ and $\text{length}(\Ga
mma_R)=\pi R$, the integral over $\Gamma_R$ is $O(R^{-5})\to 0$ as $R\to\infty$.
 Hence

$$I = \lim_{R\to\infty}\oint_{C_R} f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i \sum_{\text{Im}(z_k)>0}\ope
ratorname{Res}(f,z_k).$$

**Step 2: Locate the poles in the upper half-plane.**

The poles are the roots of $z^6 = -1 = e^{i\pi}$, namely
$$z_k = e^{i(2k+1)\pi/6},\qquad k=0,1,\dots,5.$$
The arguments are $\frac{\pi}{6},\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{5\pi}{6},\frac{7\pi}{6},\fr
ac{3\pi}{2},\frac{11\pi}{6}$. Those in the upper half-plane ($0<\arg<\pi$) are:
$$z_0 = e^{i\pi/6},\quad z_1 = e^{i\pi/2}=i,\quad z_2 = e^{i5\pi/6}.$$
All are simple poles.

**Step 3: Compute the residues.**

At a simple root $z_k$ of $z^6+1$, with $g(z)=z^6+1$,
$$\operatorname{Res}(f,z_k)=\frac{1}{g'(z_k)}=\frac{1}{6z_k^5}.$$

- $z_0=e^{i\pi/6}$: $z_0^5=e^{i5\pi/6}$, so $\operatorname{Res}=\frac{1}{6}e^{-i
5\pi/6}$.
- $z_1=i=e^{i\pi/2}$: $z_1^5=e^{i5\pi/2}=e^{i\pi/2}=i$, so $\operatorname{Res}=\
frac{1}{6i}=-\frac{i}{6}$.
- $z_2=e^{i5\pi/6}$: $z_2^5=e^{i25\pi/6}=e^{i(25\pi/6-4\pi)}=e^{i\pi/6}$, so $\o
peratorname{Res}=\frac{1}{6}e^{-i\pi/6}$.

**Step 4: Sum the residues.**

$$\sum = \frac{1}{6}\!\left(e^{-i5\pi/6}+e^{-i\pi/6}\right) - \frac{i}{6}.$$

Factor the bracket by writing $e^{-i5\pi/6}=e^{-i\pi/2}e^{-i\pi/3}$ and $e^{-i\p
i/6}=e^{-i\pi/2}e^{i\pi/3}$:
$$e^{-i5\pi/6}+e^{-i\pi/6}=e^{-i\pi/2}\!\left(e^{-i\pi/3}+e^{i\pi/3}\right)=(-i)
\cdot 2\cos\!\tfrac{\pi}{3}=(-i)\cdot 1 = -i.$$

Therefore
$$\sum = \frac{1}{6}(-i) - \frac{i}{6} = -\frac{i}{6}-\frac{i}{6} = -\frac{i}{3}
.$$

**Step 5: Apply the residue theorem.**

$$I = 2\pi i\cdot\!\left(-\frac{i}{3}\right) = 2\pi i\cdot\frac{-i}{3} = \frac{2
\pi}{3}.$$

## Result

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x^6+1}=\frac{2\pi}{3}}.$$
