## Proof

**Assumption.** \(n\) is a positive integer. We are given a segment of length \(
n\) and must construct a segment of length \(n^{n}\) with straightedge and compa
ss.

We use two standard facts about straightedge-and-compass constructions:

1. **Equal division.** A segment can be divided into any prescribed number \(k\)
 of equal parts. (Construction: from one endpoint draw an auxiliary ray; step of
f \(k\) equal arbitrary lengths with the compass; join the last mark to the othe
r endpoint; through each mark construct the parallel to this joining line. The i
ntersections divide the segment into \(k\) equal parts.)
2. **Multiplication via similar triangles.** Given segments of lengths \(1, a, b
\), a segment of length \(ab\) is constructible. (Construction: draw an angle wi
th vertex \(O\); on one ray mark \(A\) with \(OA=1\) and \(C\) with \(OC=a\); on
 the other ray mark \(B\) with \(OB=b\); draw \(AB\); through \(C\) draw the par
allel to \(AB\), meeting the second ray at \(D\). By similarity, \(OD = \frac{OC
\cdot OB}{OA}=ab\).)

Both constructions use only straightedge and compass (constructing a parallel is
 itself a compass-and-straightedge operation, e.g. by copying an angle).

---

**Construction.**

*Step 0 (unit length).* Take the given segment \(S_0\) of length \(n\). Divide i
t into \(n\) equal parts using Fact 1. One part is a segment of length \(1\). Ke
ep this unit segment.

*Step 1 (first power).* We already have a segment of length \(n\): the original
\(S_0\). Call it \(S_1\).

*Step \(k\) (induction).* Suppose we have constructed a segment \(S_{k}\) of len
gth \(n^{k}\) for some \(1\le k < n\). Using Fact 2 with \(a = n^{k}\) (the segm
ent \(S_k\)) and \(b = n\) (the segment \(S_1\), together with the unit segment
from Step 0), construct a segment \(S_{k+1}\) of length \(n^{k}\cdot n = n^{k+1}
\).

*Termination.* After performing Step \(k\) for \(k=1,2,\dots,n-1\), we obtain a
segment \(S_n\) of length \(n^{n}\).

---

**Correctness.** The construction produces, by induction on \(k\), a segment of
length \(n^{k}\) for every \(k=1,\dots,n\):
- Base \(k=1\): \(S_1\) is the given segment of length \(n=n^{1}\).
- Inductive step: if \(S_k\) has length \(n^{k}\), then Fact 2 gives \(S_{k+1}\)
 of length \(n^{k}\cdot n = n^{k+1}\).

At \(k=n\) we have a segment of length \(n^{n}\), as required. Every operation u
sed (equal division, copying an angle / drawing a parallel, laying off a compass
 length) is a legitimate straightedge-and-compass operation, so the entire const
ruction is valid.

**Complexity note.** The construction uses \(n-1\) multiplication steps, each a
constant-size similar-triangles figure, plus one equal-division of the original
segment into \(n\) parts. This is a finite procedure for any fixed positive inte
ger \(n\).
