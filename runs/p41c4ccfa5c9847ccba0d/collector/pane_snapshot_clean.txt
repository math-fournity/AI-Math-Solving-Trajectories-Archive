## Proof

**Claim:** Let $\pi_1(x) = |\{p \le x : p \text{ prime},\; p \equiv 1 \pmod{6}\}
|$ and $\pi_5(x) = |\{p \le x : p \text{ prime},\; p \equiv 5 \pmod{6}\}|$. Then
$$\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi_1(x)}{\pi_5(x)} = 1.$$

**Step 1. Reduction to two residue classes.** Every prime $p > 3$ is coprime to
$6 = 2\cdot 3$. The reduced residue system modulo $6$ is $\{1, 5\}$ (since $\var
phi(6)=2$). Hence every prime $p>3$ satisfies exactly one of $p\equiv 1\pmod 6$
or $p\equiv 5\pmod 6$. The primes $2$ and $3$ contribute only $O(1)$ to either c
ount, so they are negligible for the asymptotic ratio.

**Step 2. Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions.** Dirichlet'
s theorem (in its quantitative form) states: for $\gcd(a,m)=1$,
$$\pi(x;\,m,\,a) := |\{p\le x : p\text{ prime},\; p\equiv a\pmod m\}| \;\sim\; \
frac{1}{\varphi(m)}\,\frac{x}{\ln x}\quad\text{as }x\to\infty.$$

**Step 3. Apply to $m=6$.** Both $a=1$ and $a=5$ are coprime to $6$, and $\varph
i(6)=2$. Therefore
$$\pi_1(x) = \pi(x;6,1) \sim \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{x}{\ln x},\qquad \pi_5(x) = \
pi(x;6,5) \sim \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{x}{\ln x}.$$

**Step 4. Compute the ratio.** Since both quantities are asymptotic to the same
quantity $\tfrac{1}{2}\,x/\ln x$,
$$\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi_1(x)}{\pi_5(x)} = \lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi_1(x)}{
\tfrac{1}{2}x/\ln x}\cdot\frac{\tfrac{1}{2}x/\ln x}{\pi_5(x)} = 1\cdot 1 = 1.$$

More precisely, writing $\pi_1(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{Li}(x) + E_1(x)$ and $
\pi_5(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{Li}(x) + E_5(x)$ where $E_1, E_5 = o(\mathrm{Li
}(x))$, we get
$$\frac{\pi_1(x)}{\pi_5(x)} = \frac{\tfrac12\mathrm{Li}(x)+E_1(x)}{\tfrac12\math
rm{Li}(x)+E_5(x)} = \frac{1 + 2E_1(x)/\mathrm{Li}(x)}{1 + 2E_5(x)/\mathrm{Li}(x)
} \longrightarrow \frac{1+0}{1+0}=1.$$

**Conclusion.** The primes congruent to $1\pmod 6$ and the primes congruent to $
5\pmod 6$ are asymptotically equal: each class contains half of all primes (in d
ensity), so their ratio tends to $1$.
