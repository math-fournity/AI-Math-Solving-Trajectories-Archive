## Proof

We determine the first eigenvalue (bottom of the spectrum) of the Dirichlet Lapl
acian on the half-plane $\Omega = \mathbb{R}^2_+ = \{(x,y) : y > 0\}$.

**Step 1: Non-negativity.** The operator $-\Delta$ with Dirichlet boundary condi
tions is non-negative. For any $u \in H^1_0(\Omega)$,
$$\langle -\Delta u, u\rangle = \int_\Omega |\nabla u|^2 \,dx\,dy \geq 0.$$
Hence $\lambda_1 := \inf \sigma(-\Delta) \geq 0$.

**Step 2: Domain monotonicity.** For Dirichlet eigenvalues, if $\Omega_1 \subset
eq \Omega_2$, then $\lambda_1(\Omega_1) \geq \lambda_1(\Omega_2)$. This follows
from the variational (Rayleigh–Ritz) characterization:
$$\lambda_1(\Omega) = \inf_{\substack{u \in H^1_0(\Omega)\\ u\neq 0}} \frac{\int
_\Omega |\nabla u|^2}{\int_\Omega |u|^2},$$
since $H^1_0(\Omega_1) \hookrightarrow H^1_0(\Omega_2)$ by zero extension.

**Step 3: Disks inside the half-plane.** For each $R > 0$, let $D_R$ be the open
 disk of radius $R$ centered at $(0, R)$. Then $D_R \subset \mathbb{R}^2_+$, sin
ce every point $(x,y) \in D_R$ satisfies $y > 0$ (the lowest point of $\overline
{D_R}$ is $(0,0)$, on the boundary). By domain monotonicity:
$$\lambda_1(\mathbb{R}^2_+) \leq \lambda_1(D_R).$$

**Step 4: Scaling.** The disk $D_R$ is a translate of the disk of radius $R$, so
 by scaling ($u(x) \mapsto u(Rx)$, eigenvalues scale as $1/R^2$):
$$\lambda_1(D_R) = \frac{\lambda_1(D_1)}{R^2},$$
where $D_1$ is the unit disk and $\lambda_1(D_1) > 0$ is the known first eigenva
lue of the unit disk (namely $\lambda_1(D_1) = j_{0,1}^2 \approx 5.783$, with $j
_{0,1}$ the first zero of $J_0$).

**Step 5: Taking $R \to \infty$.** From Steps 3 and 4:
$$\lambda_1(\mathbb{R}^2_+) \leq \frac{\lambda_1(D_1)}{R^2} \xrightarrow{R\to\in
fty} 0.$$
Combined with Step 1 ($\lambda_1 \geq 0$), we conclude:
$$\boxed{\lambda_1(\mathbb{R}^2_+) = 0.}$$

**Remark on the nature of the spectrum.** The value $0$ is the bottom of the *co
ntinuous* spectrum, not a genuine $L^2$-eigenvalue. Indeed, the half-plane is tr
anslation-invariant in $x$; Fourier transforming in $x$ reduces the problem to $
-u''(y) = (\lambda - k^2)u(y)$ on $(0,\infty)$ with $u(0)=0$, whose solutions $\
sin(\sqrt{\lambda-k^2}\,y)$ are not in $L^2(0,\infty)$. Thus $\sigma(-\Delta) =
[0,\infty)$ is purely continuous, and the "first eigenvalue" in the sense of $\i
nf\sigma$ is $0$.
