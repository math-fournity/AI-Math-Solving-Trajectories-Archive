## Solution

We seek the best approximation of $f(x) = x^3 + x^2 + e^{-x}$ in the subspace
$$G = \{g \in C[-1,1] : g(x) = g(-x)\ \forall x\}$$
of even functions, with respect to the $L^2[-1,1]$ inner product $\langle u,v\ra
ngle = \int_{-1}^{1} u(x)v(x)\,dx$.

### Decomposition into even and odd parts

Any function $f \in C[-1,1]$ decomposes uniquely as $f = f_e + f_o$, where
$$f_e(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}(f(x)+f(-x)), \qquad f_o(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}(f(x)-f(-x))$$
are the even and odd parts respectively. Clearly $f_e \in G$ and $f_o$ is odd.

### Orthogonality of even and odd subspaces

For any even function $g \in G$ and any odd function $h$,
$$\langle g, h\rangle = \int_{-1}^{1} g(x)h(x)\,dx = 0,$$
since $g(x)h(x)$ is odd and the integral of an odd function over $[-1,1]$ vanish
es. Hence $G$ and the subspace of odd functions are orthogonal.

### The orthogonal projection onto $G$ is the even part

The best $L^2$-approximation of $f$ in $G$ is the orthogonal projection $P_G f$.
 Since $f = f_e + f_o$ with $f_e \in G$ and $f_o \perp G$, the orthogonal projec
tion is
$$P_G f = f_e.$$
Indeed, for any $g \in G$:
$$\|f - g\|^2 = \|f_e - g + f_o\|^2 = \|f_e - g\|^2 + \|f_o\|^2 \geq \|f_o\|^2 =
 \|f - f_e\|^2,$$
with equality iff $g = f_e$. So $f_e$ is the unique best approximation.

### Computing the even part

$$f(x) = x^3 + x^2 + e^{-x}, \qquad f(-x) = -x^3 + x^2 + e^{x}.$$

Therefore
$$f_e(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(f(x)+f(-x)\bigr) = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(2x^2 + e^{-x}
+ e^{x}\bigr) = x^2 + \frac{e^{x}+e^{-x}}{2} = x^2 + \cosh x.$$

### Verification

- $g^*(x) = x^2 + \cosh x$ is even, so $g^* \in G$. ✓
- The error $f - g^* = (x^3 + x^2 + e^{-x}) - (x^2 + \cosh x) = x^3 + e^{-x} - \
cosh x = x^3 - \sinh x$, which is odd (since $x^3$ and $\sinh x$ are both odd).
✓
- Being odd, the error is orthogonal to every even function, confirming $g^*$ is
 the orthogonal projection. ✓

### Result

The best approximation of $x^3 + x^2 + e^{-x}$ in $G$ is

$$\boxed{g^*(x) = x^2 + \cosh x}.$$
