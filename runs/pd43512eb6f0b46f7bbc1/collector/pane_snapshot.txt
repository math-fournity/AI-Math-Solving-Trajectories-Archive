Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a continuous family of self
-adjoint PSD matrices with constant rank and a spectral gap admits a continuous
unitary block-diagonalization separating the zero and positive parts.

**Answer: Yes, such continuous $U(x,y)$ and $B(x,y)$ exist.**

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $A:[0,1]^2 \to M_n(\mathbb{C})$ be continuous, with each $A(x,y)$
 self-adjoint and positive semi-definite, of constant rank $r$. The spectrum of
$A(x,y)$ consists of $n-r$ zero eigenvalues and $r$ eigenvalues in $[a,b]$ with
$a>0$. Thus there is a spectral gap $(0,a)$ separating $\{0\}$ from $[a,b]$.

**Step 1: Continuous spectral projection onto the range.**

Pick $c$ with $0 < c < a$. Define a continuous function $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{
R}$ with $f(t)=0$ for $t \leq c/2$ and $f(t)=1$ for $t \geq c$. By the continuou
s functional calculus for self-adjoint matrices, define
$$P(x,y) := f(A(x,y)).$$
Since $f=0$ on a neighborhood of $\{0\}$ and $f=1$ on a neighborhood of $[a,b]$,
 the matrix $P(x,y)$ is the spectral projection of $A(x,y)$ onto the eigenspace
associated with eigenvalues in $[a,b]$, i.e., onto $\operatorname{range}(A(x,y))
$. As both $A$ and $f$ are continuous, $P:[0,1]^2 \to M_n(\mathbb{C})$ is contin
uous, and satisfies $P^2 = P = P^*$ with constant rank $r$.

**Step 2: Triviality of the associated vector bundle.**

The continuous projection $P$ defines a rank-$r$ complex vector bundle $E$ over
$X = [0,1]^2$:
$$E = \{(p, v) \in [0,1]^2 \times \mathbb{C}^n : v \in \operatorname{range}(P(p)
)\}.$$
This is a subbundle of the trivial bundle $[0,1]^2 \times \mathbb{C}^n$.

Since $[0,1]^2$ is **contractible** (and paracompact), every complex vector bund
le over it is **trivial**. (This is a standard result: the classifying space $\m
athrm{BU}(r)$ has no nontrivial homotopy classes of maps from a contractible spa
ce.) Therefore $E$ admits $r$ continuous orthonormal sections $s_1, \ldots, s_r
: [0,1]^2 \to \mathbb{C}^n$, meaning $\{s_1(p), \ldots, s_r(p)\}$ is an orthonor
mal basis of $\operatorname{range}(P(p))$ for each $p = (x,y)$.

Likewise, the kernel bundle $\ker(P)$ (rank $n-r$) is trivial over $[0,1]^2$, yi
elding continuous orthonormal sections $s_{r+1}, \ldots, s_n$ with $\{s_{r+1}(p)
, \ldots, s_n(p)\}$ an orthonormal basis of $\ker(A(p)) = \ker(P(p))$.

**Step 3: Construction of $U$ and $B$.**

Define the unitary matrix
$$U(p) = \big[\,s_{r+1}(p) \;\cdots\; s_n(p) \;\big|\; s_1(p) \;\cdots\; s_r(p)\
,\big],$$
which is continuous in $p=(x,y)$ since each column is continuous. Set
$$B(p) := U(p)^* A(p)\, U(p) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & B_2(p) \end{pmatrix}
,$$
where $B_2(p) = \big[s_1(p)\;\cdots\;s_r(p)\big]^* A(p)\,\big[s_1(p)\;\cdots\;s_
r(p)\big]$ is an $r\times r$ continuous matrix.

**Verification of the block structure.** The $(i,j)$-entry of $B$ is $s_i(p)^* A
(p)\, s_j(p)$.
- For $i, j \in \{r+1, \ldots, n\}$: $s_j(p) \in \ker(A(p))$, so $A(p)s_j(p) = 0
$, giving the zero block.
- For $i \in \{r+1,\ldots,n\}$, $j \in \{1,\ldots,r\}$: $s_i(p) \in \ker(A(p))$,
 so $s_i(p)^* A(p) = (A(p)s_i(p))^* = 0$, giving zero. The conjugate case is ide
ntical.

Thus the off-diagonal blocks vanish and $B$ has the claimed form. Equivalently,
$A(p) = U(p)\, B(p)\, U(p)^*$.

**Properties of $B_2$.** Since $B_2(p) = U_2(p)^* A(p) U_2(p)$ where $U_2 = [s_1
, \ldots, s_r]$, it is self-adjoint, positive definite (as $A$ is positive on it
s range with eigenvalues $\geq a > 0$), with eigenvalues in $[a,b]$, and continu
ous in $(x,y)$.

**Conclusion.** The spectral gap $a > 0$ provides a continuous spectral projecti
on (Step 1), and the contractibility of the domain $[0,1]^2$ trivializes the res
ulting vector bundle (Step 2), yielding a continuous unitary $U(x,y)$ that block
-diagonalizes $A(x,y)$ into the desired form (Step 3). $\blacksquare$
