## Solution

We evaluate
$$S = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \sum_{\substack{j=1 \\ j \neq k}}^{\infty} \sum_{\subs
tack{i=1 \\ i \neq j,\, i \neq k}}^{\infty} \frac{1}{4^i 4^j 4^k}.$$

The summand $\frac{1}{4^{i+j+k}}$ is symmetric in $(i,j,k)$, and the distinctnes
s condition $i \neq j,\, i \neq k,\, j \neq k$ is also symmetric. Hence $S$ equa
ls the sum over **all ordered triples** $(i,j,k)$ of distinct positive integers
of $\frac{1}{4^{i+j+k}}$.

### Inclusion–Exclusion

Let $x = \tfrac14$, so $\sum_{n \geq 1} x^n = \frac{x}{1-x} = \frac{1}{3}$.

**Full sum** (all ordered triples, no restriction):
$$A = \left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} x^n\right)^{\!3} = \left(\tfrac{1}{3}\right)^3 =
 \frac{1}{27}.$$

**Pair-equal sums.** For the event $E_{ij}:\, i=j$ (with $k$ free):
$$\sum_{E_{ij}} x^{i+j+k} = \left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} x^{2i}\right)\!\left(\sum_
{k=1}^{\infty} x^k\right) = \frac{x^2}{1-x^2}\cdot\frac{x}{1-x} = \frac{1/16}{15
/16}\cdot\frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{15}\cdot\frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{45}.$$
By symmetry, each of $E_{ij}, E_{jk}, E_{ik}$ contributes $\frac{1}{45}$.

**All-equal sum** ($i=j=k$, the triple intersection of any two pair-events):
$$C = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} x^{3i} = \frac{x^3}{1-x^3} = \frac{1/64}{63/64} = \fra
c{1}{63}.$$

By inclusion–exclusion, the weighted sum over triples with **at least one** pair
 equal is
$$3\cdot\frac{1}{45} - 3\cdot\frac{1}{63} + \frac{1}{63} = \frac{1}{15} - \frac{
2}{63}.$$

### Final computation

$$S = A - \left(\frac{1}{15} - \frac{2}{63}\right) = \frac{1}{27} - \frac{1}{15}
 + \frac{2}{63}.$$

Using $\operatorname{lcm}(27,15,63) = 945$:
$$\frac{1}{27} = \frac{35}{945},\qquad \frac{1}{15} = \frac{63}{945},\qquad \fra
c{2}{63} = \frac{30}{945}.$$

$$S = \frac{35 - 63 + 30}{945} = \frac{2}{945}.$$

### Verification (unordered triples)

Since the summand and distinctness condition are fully symmetric, $S = 6\sum_{1
\le a < b < c} x^{a+b+c}$ with $x=\tfrac14$. Substituting $a=a'+1,\, b=b'+2,\, c
=c'+3$ with $0 \le a' \le b' \le c'$ gives
$$\sum_{a<b<c} x^{a+b+c} = \frac{x^6}{(1-x)(1-x^2)(1-x^3)} = \frac{1/4096}{(3/4)
(15/16)(63/64)} = \frac{1/4096}{2835/4096} = \frac{1}{2835}.$$
Thus $S = 6 \cdot \frac{1}{2835} = \frac{2}{945}$, confirming the result.
