# Problem

In a specialized data-processing facility, an engineer is monitoring a sequence of energy outputs generated by a system. The baseline energy levels follow the Lucas sequence $l_n$, where the initial pulse $l_0$ is 2 units, the second pulse $l_1$ is 1 unit, and every subsequent pulse $l_{n+2}$ is the sum of the previous two pulses ($l_{n+1} + l_n$).

The engineer is calculating the total efficiency of a massive array of $n+1$ processors. The efficiency contribution of the $k$-th processor (where $k$ ranges from $0$ to $n$) is defined as the reciprocal of the sum of the $(2k)$-th energy pulse and a constant stabilizer pulse $l_2$. Specifically, the total efficiency for an array of size $n+1$ is given by the sum:
$$E_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \frac{1}{l_{2k} + l_2}$$

As the technology scales and the number of processors $n$ approaches infinity, the engineer needs to determine the theoretical limit of this total efficiency.

Compute the value of $\lim_{n \to \infty} E_n$.
