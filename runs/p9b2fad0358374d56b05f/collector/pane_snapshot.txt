# Solution

**Claim:** $\mu_n = 1$ for all $n \geq 1$.

## Lower Bound: $\mu(P) \geq 1$ for every monic polynomial of degree $n$

Let $P(z) = z^n + a_{n-1}z^{n-1} + \cdots + a_0$ be monic of degree $n$. The coe
fficient of $z^n$ is $1$, and by Cauchy's integral formula (equivalently, extrac
ting the $n$-th Fourier coefficient of $P$ on the unit circle):

$$1 = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=1} \frac{P(z)}{z^{n+1}}\,dz = \frac{1}{2\pi}\in
t_0^{2\pi} P(e^{i\theta})\,e^{-in\theta}\,d\theta.$$

Taking absolute values and applying the triangle inequality:

$$1 = \left|\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} P(e^{i\theta})\,e^{-in\theta}\,d\theta\r
ight| \leq \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} \left|P(e^{i\theta})\right|\,d\theta \leq
 \sup_{|z|=1}|P(z)| = \mu(P).$$

Hence $\mu(P) \geq 1$ for every monic polynomial of degree $n$, so $\mu_n \geq 1
$.

## Upper Bound: $\mu_n \leq 1$ via an explicit polynomial

Consider $P(z) = z^n$, which is monic of degree $n$. On the unit circle $|z|=1$:

$$|P(z)| = |z^n| = |z|^n = 1.$$

Therefore $\mu(P) = \sup_{|z|=1}|z^n| = 1$, which gives $\mu_n \leq 1$.

## Conclusion

Combining both bounds:

$$\mu_n \geq 1 \quad\text{and}\quad \mu_n \leq 1,$$

so $\mu_n = 1$ for all $n \geq 1$. The infimum is attained (and is in fact a min
imum) by the monomial $P(z) = z^n$.
