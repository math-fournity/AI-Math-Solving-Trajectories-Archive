Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether any subset $A \subseteq \ma
thbb{R}$ can have Lebesgue inner measure strictly greater than its Lebesgue oute
r measure.

**Answer: No.** For every subset $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, the inner measure is
always at most the outer measure: $m_*(A) \leq m^*(A)$.

---

## Proof

We use the standard Lebesgue outer measure $m^*$ and inner measure $m_*$ on $\ma
thbb{R}$.

### Bounded case

Let $A \subseteq [a,b]$ be a bounded set. The Lebesgue inner measure is defined
as:

$$m_*(A) = (b - a) - m^*([a,b] \setminus A).$$

Since $[a,b] = A \cup ([a,b] \setminus A)$, by **countable subadditivity** of ou
ter measure:

$$m^*([a,b]) \leq m^*(A) + m^*([a,b] \setminus A).$$

Because $[a,b]$ is a closed interval, $m^*([a,b]) = b - a$. Substituting:

$$b - a \leq m^*(A) + m^*([a,b] \setminus A),$$

which rearranges to:

$$m^*([a,b] \setminus A) \geq (b - a) - m^*(A).$$

Now plug this back into the definition of inner measure:

$$m_*(A) = (b-a) - m^*([a,b] \setminus A) \leq (b-a) - \bigl((b-a) - m^*(A)\bigr
) = m^*(A).$$

Thus $m_*(A) \leq m^*(A)$ for every bounded $A$.

### Unbounded case

For a general (possibly unbounded) $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, define:

$$m_*(A) = \sup_{n \in \mathbb{N}}\, m_*(A \cap [-n,n]), \qquad m^*(A) = \sup_{n
 \in \mathbb{N}}\, m^*(A \cap [-n,n]).$$

(The supremum form for $m^*$ agrees with the standard definition because outer m
easure is monotone and the intervals $[-n,n]$ exhaust $\mathbb{R}$.)

For each $n$, the set $A \cap [-n,n]$ is bounded, so by the bounded case:

$$m_*(A \cap [-n,n]) \leq m^*(A \cap [-n,n]) \leq m^*(A),$$

where the last inequality uses monotonicity of outer measure ($A \cap [-n,n] \su
bseteq A$). Taking the supremum over $n$:

$$m_*(A) = \sup_n\, m_*(A \cap [-n,n]) \leq m^*(A).$$

### Robustness under alternative definitions

The same conclusion holds under the other common definition of inner measure:

$$m_*(A) = \sup\{m(B) : B \subseteq A,\; B \text{ Lebesgue measurable}\}.$$

For any measurable $B \subseteq A$, we have $m(B) = m^*(B) \leq m^*(A)$ by monot
onicity of outer measure. Taking the supremum yields $m_*(A) \leq m^*(A)$.

### Conclusion

In every standard formulation of Lebesgue measure theory, the inequality $m_*(A)
 \leq m^*(A)$ holds for **all** $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}$. The proof relies only
on:

1. **Countable subadditivity** of outer measure, and
2. **Monotonicity** of outer measure.

Both are foundational properties of $m^*$. Therefore, **there does not exist** a
 subset of the real numbers whose inner measure is larger than its outer measure
.
