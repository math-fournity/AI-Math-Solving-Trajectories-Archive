## Proof

**Claim.** Yes. For every $i \ge 0$, the restriction of $L_i F$ to $\text{mod}(R
)$ is naturally isomorphic to $L_i(F|_{\text{mod}})$ as a functor $\text{mod}(R)
 \to \text{mod}(R)$.

We establish this in three steps: (1) the relevant resolutions exist and coincid
e, (2) the values land in $\text{mod}(R)$, and (3) a universal $\delta$-functor
argument gives naturality.

### Step 1. $\text{mod}(R)$ has enough projectives and admits projective resolut
ions that are simultaneously projective resolutions in $\text{Mod}(R)$.

Since $R$ is commutative Noetherian, every finitely generated $R$-module is fini
tely presented, and $\text{mod}(R)$ is abelian (submodules of finitely generated
 modules over a Noetherian ring are finitely generated). The finitely generated
free modules $R^n$ are projective in $\text{mod}(R)$, so $\text{mod}(R)$ has eno
ugh projectives.

Given $M \in \text{mod}(R)$, choose a surjection $R^{n_0} \twoheadrightarrow M$.
 Its kernel $K_0$ is a submodule of the Noetherian module $R^{n_0}$, hence finit
ely generated. Choose a surjection $R^{n_1} \twoheadrightarrow K_0$, set $K_1 =
\ker(R^{n_1} \to K_0)$, which is again finitely generated, and continue. This pr
oduces a resolution

$$\cdots \to R^{n_2} \to R^{n_1} \to R^{n_0} \to M \to 0$$

in which every term is a finitely generated free (hence projective) $R$-module.
Exactness is a set-theoretic property of the underlying maps, so this sequence i
s exact in $\text{Mod}(R)$ as well. Thus **a projective resolution of $M$ in $\t
ext{mod}(R)$ is automatically a projective resolution of $M$ in $\text{Mod}(R)$*
*.

### Step 2. $L_i F(M) \in \text{mod}(R)$ for $M \in \text{mod}(R)$, and computin
g $L_i F(M)$ via a finitely generated projective resolution yields the same comp
lex as computing $L_i(F|_{\text{mod}})(M)$.

Take a resolution $P_\bullet \to M$ by finitely generated free modules as in Ste
p 1. By hypothesis, $F(P_j) \in \text{mod}(R)$ for every $j$ (since $P_j \in \te
xt{mod}(R)$). Therefore $F(P_\bullet)$ is a complex whose terms are finitely gen
erated $R$-modules. Over a Noetherian ring, the kernel and image of any $R$-line
ar map between finitely generated modules are finitely generated, so the homolog
y

$$L_i F(M) = H_i\big(F(P_\bullet)\big)$$

is finitely generated. Hence $L_i F(M) \in \text{mod}(R)$.

Now, $L_i(F|_{\text{mod}})(M)$ is computed by applying $F|_{\text{mod}}$ to a pr
ojective resolution of $M$ in $\text{mod}(R)$. Using the *same* resolution $P_\b
ullet \to M$, we obtain

$$L_i(F|_{\text{mod}})(M) = H_i\big(F|_{\text{mod}}(P_\bullet)\big) = H_i\big(F(
P_\bullet)\big) = L_i F(M),$$

where the middle equality holds because $F|_{\text{mod}}$ and $F$ agree on finit
ely generated modules, and $P_\bullet$ consists of finitely generated modules. S
ince left derived functors are independent (up to canonical isomorphism) of the
choice of projective resolution, this shows

$$L_i F(M) \cong L_i(F|_{\text{mod}})(M) \quad \text{for all } M \in \text{mod}(
R),\; i \ge 0.$$

### Step 3. The isomorphism is natural (universal $\delta$-functor argument).

Both $L_i F\big|_{\text{mod}(R)}$ and $L_i(F|_{\text{mod}})$ are homological $\d
elta$-functors on $\text{mod}(R)$:

- A short exact sequence $0 \to M' \to M \to M'' \to 0$ in $\text{mod}(R)$ is al
so short exact in $\text{Mod}(R)$, so the long exact sequence for $L_i F$ restri
cts to a long exact sequence in $\text{mod}(R)$. This makes $\{L_i F|_{\text{mod
}(R)}\}$ a $\delta$-functor on $\text{mod}(R)$.
- $\{L_i(F|_{\text{mod}})\}$ is a $\delta$-functor on $\text{mod}(R)$ by constru
ction.

Both are **effaceable** for $i > 0$: for any $M \in \text{mod}(R)$, there is an
epimorphism $P \twoheadrightarrow M$ with $P = R^n$ finitely generated projectiv
e. Since $P$ is projective in both $\text{mod}(R)$ and $\text{Mod}(R)$, we have
$L_i F(P) = 0$ and $L_i(F|_{\text{mod}})(P) = 0$ for $i > 0$. Effaceable $\delta
$-functors are universal.

At $i = 0$: $L_0 F \cong F$ (because $F$ is right exact) and $L_0(F|_{\text{mod}
}) \cong F|_{\text{mod}}$, so both $\delta$-functors have the same $0$-th term,
namely $F|_{\text{mod}}$.

By the uniqueness of universal $\delta$-functors with a fixed $0$-th term, there
 is a **natural isomorphism** of $\delta$-functors

$$L_i F\big|_{\text{mod}(R)} \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; L_i(F|_{\text{mod}}) \qu
ad \text{for all } i \ge 0.$$

enough projectives and that homology of complexes of finitely generated modules
stays finitely generated) and that $F$ maps $\text{mod}(R)$ into itself (ensurin
g the derived complexes land in $\text{mod}(R)$) together guarantee that the lef
t derived functors of $F$ and of $F|_{\text{mod}}$ agree, naturally, on $\text{m
od}(R)$ for every $i \ge 0$.
