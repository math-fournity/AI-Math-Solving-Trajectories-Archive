### Step 1: $f$ is $C^\infty$ with $f^{(n)}(0)=0$ for all $n$.

**(a)** $f^{(n)}(0) = 0$, and

**(b)** For $x \neq 0$, $f^{(n)}(x)$ is a finite sum of terms of the form $C\, x

^{(n)}(x)| \le K_n\, |x|^{-M_n}\, e^{-1/x^2}$ for constants $K_n, M_n$.

**Base case ($n=0$):** $f(0)=0$ by definition, and $|f(x)| \le e^{-1/x^2}$ for $
x\neq 0$. ✓

**Inductive step.** Assume (a) and (b) hold for $n$. Since $|x|^{-M}e^{-1/x^2}\t

$$f^{(n+1)}(0)=\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{f^{(n)}(x)-f^{(n)}(0)}{x}=\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{f
^{(n)}(x)}{x}.$$
By (b), $\left|\frac{f^{(n)}(x)}{x}\right|\le K_n\,|x|^{-M_n-1}e^{-1/x^2}\to 0$,
