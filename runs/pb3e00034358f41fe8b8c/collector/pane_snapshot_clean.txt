# Stone Representation Theorem

## Statement

**Stone Representation Theorem.** Every Boolean algebra $B$ is isomorphic to a f
ield of sets. Concretely, $B$ is isomorphic to the field of all clopen (closed-a
nd-open) subsets of its Stone space $S(B)$, equipped with the operations of unio
n, intersection, and complementation.

Equivalently (and most usefully stated): For every Boolean algebra $B$, there ex
ists a set $X$ and a field of sets $\mathcal{F} \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)$ such t
hat $B \cong \mathcal{F}$, where the isomorphism preserves $\vee, \wedge, \neg,
0, 1$ (mapping to $\cup, \cap, \complement, \varnothing, X$ respectively).

## Setup and Definitions

A **Boolean algebra** is a distributive complemented lattice $(B, \vee, \wedge,
\neg, 0, 1)$: an algebra satisfying the lattice axioms, distributivity, and havi
ng a least element $0$, greatest element $1$, and a complement $\neg b$ for ever
y $b$ with $b \vee \neg b = 1$ and $b \wedge \neg b = 0$.

A **filter** $F \subseteq B$ is a nonempty subset with $1 \in F$, closed upward
($a \in F, a \le b \Rightarrow b \in F$) and under meets ($a, b \in F \Rightarro
w a \wedge b \in F$). A filter is **proper** if $0 \notin F$. An **ultrafilter**
 is a maximal proper filter, equivalently a proper filter such that for every $a
 \in B$, exactly one of $a, \neg a$ belongs to $F$.

A **field of sets** is a family $\mathcal{F}$ of subsets of some set $X$ closed
under finite union, finite intersection, and complementation (relative to $X$).

## Proof

### Step 1: The Stone space

Let $S(B)$ denote the set of all ultrafilters of $B$. For each $b \in B$, define
$$U_b = \{ F \in S(B) : b \in F \}.$$

### Step 2: The $U_b$ form a field of sets

We verify the field-of-sets operations are mirrored by the Boolean operations:

- **$U_0 = \varnothing$ and $U_1 = S(B)$.** No proper (hence no ultra) filter co
ntains $0$; every filter contains $1$.

- **$U_{a \vee b} = U_a \cup U_b$.** For an ultrafilter $F$: $a \vee b \in F \if
f a \in F \text{ or } b \in F$.
  - ($\Leftarrow$): filters are upward closed.
  - ($\Rightarrow$): if neither $a$ nor $b$ is in $F$, then by the ultrafilter p
roperty $\neg a, \neg b \in F$, so $\neg a \wedge \neg b = \neg(a \vee b) \in F$
, contradicting $a \vee b \in F$ (since then $0 = (a\vee b)\wedge\neg(a\vee b)\i
n F$).

- **$U_{a \wedge b} = U_a \cap U_b$.** Immediate: $a \wedge b \in F \iff a \in F
 \text{ and } b \in F$, using filter closure under meets and upward closure.

- **$U_{\neg b} = S(B) \setminus U_b$.** By the ultrafilter property, exactly on
e of $b, \neg b$ lies in $F$.

Thus $\mathcal{F} := \{U_b : b \in B\}$ is a field of sets on $S(B)$.

### Step 3: The map $\varphi: B \to \mathcal{F}$, $\varphi(b) = U_b$, is a homom
orphism

Steps 1–2 show $\varphi$ preserves $\vee, \wedge, \neg, 0, 1$:
$$\varphi(a \vee b) = \varphi(a) \cup \varphi(b),\quad \varphi(a \wedge b) = \va
rphi(a) \cap \varphi(b),\quad \varphi(\neg b) = \overline{\varphi(b)},$$
$$\varphi(0) = \varnothing,\quad \varphi(1) = S(B).$$

### Step 4: $\varphi$ is injective

Suppose $b \neq 0$. We show $U_b \neq \varnothing$, i.e., there exists an ultraf
ilter containing $b$.

Consider the principal filter $\uparrow b = \{c \in B : b \le c\}$. Since $b \ne
q 0$, this is proper ($0 \notin \uparrow b$, for $b \le 0$ would force $b = 0$).
 By **Zorn's Lemma**, every proper filter extends to an ultrafilter:

> *Zorn's argument.* The collection of proper filters containing $\uparrow b$, o
rdered by inclusion, is inductive: the union of any chain of proper filters is p
roper (if $0$ were in the union, it would be in some member of the chain, contra
diction). Hence there is a maximal element, i.e., an ultrafilter $F \supseteq \u
parrow b$, and $b \in F$.

Therefore $b \neq 0 \Rightarrow U_b \neq \varnothing$. Now if $\varphi(a) = \var
phi(b)$, then $\varphi(a \triangle b) = \varphi(a)\triangle\varphi(b) = \varnoth
ing$ (using the homomorphism properties and $a\triangle b = (a\wedge\neg b)\vee(
\neg a\wedge b)$), so $a \triangle b = 0$, giving $a = b$.

(Equivalently and more directly: if $a \neq b$, then either $a \wedge \neg b \ne
q 0$ or $b \wedge \neg a \neq 0$; in the first case some ultrafilter contains $a
 \wedge \neg b$, hence contains $a$ but not $b$, witnessing $U_a \neq U_b$.)

### Step 5: $\varphi$ is surjective onto $\mathcal{F}$

By definition $\mathcal{F} = \{U_b : b \in B\} = \operatorname{im}(\varphi)$, so
 surjectivity is automatic.

### Conclusion

$\varphi: B \to \mathcal{F}$ is a bijective homomorphism of Boolean algebras, he
nce an **isomorphism**. Therefore $B \cong \mathcal{F}$, a field of sets. $\blac
ksquare$

## Remarks on Significance

1. **Duality.** Endowing $S(B)$ with the topology generated by the basis $\{U_b\
}_{b\in B}$ yields the **Stone space** of $B$ — a compact, Hausdorff, totally di
sconnected (i.e., Boolean) space. The theorem then refines to: *every Boolean al
gebra is isomorphic to the field of clopen sets of its Stone space*. This sets u
p the **Stone duality**, a contravariant equivalence between Boolean algebras an
d Boolean (Stone) spaces.

2. **Concrete representation.** The theorem says abstract Boolean algebras are *
exactly* the fields of sets — every identity valid in the two-element Boolean al
gebra $\{0,1\}$ (hence in all fields of sets) holds in every Boolean algebra. Th
is yields the completeness of equational Boolean logic.

3. **Role of the Axiom of Choice.** The extension of a proper filter to an ultra
filter (Step 4) requires Zorn's Lemma / the Ultrafilter Theorem, a weak form of
the Axiom of Choice. Without it, the theorem need not hold in full generality.
