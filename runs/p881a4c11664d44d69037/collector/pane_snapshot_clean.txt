**Reachability preservation.** Removing edges incident to $c$ only affects $c$'s

c$ removed, and let $C_1, \dots, C_m$ be its connected components. Let $t_i$ be

**Key observation:** We can remove edges from $c$ to each $C_i$ as long as at le

## Parity Analysis

**Lemma 1:** The number of odd-degree vertices (among non-capital cities) in com
ponent $C_i$ is congruent to $t_i \pmod{2}$.

*Proof.* In the subgraph $C_i$, the number of odd-degree vertices is even (hands
hake lemma). For each of the $t_i$ neighbors of $c$ in $C_i$, adding the edge to
 $c$ flips its parity. Non-neighbor vertices keep their parity. So the count of
odd whole-graph degrees in $C_i$ equals (odd count in $C_i$) $+\, t_i - 2o_i$, w
here $o_i$ is the number of $c$-neighbors with odd degree within $C_i$. This is
$\equiv t_i \pmod{2}$. $\square$

**Corollary:** If $t_i$ is odd, $C_i$ contributes at least 1 odd-degree vertex.
If $t_i$ is even, $C_i$ contributes an even number ($\geq 0$).
