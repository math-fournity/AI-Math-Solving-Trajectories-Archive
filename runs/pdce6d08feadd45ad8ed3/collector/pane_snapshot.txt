**Answer: Yes.** Every $f \in C_0(X_0)$ extends to a function $\tilde f \in C_0(
X)$ with $\tilde f|_{X_0}=f$.

---

**Proof.**

Let $X^\ast = X\cup\{\infty\}$ be the one-point compactification of $X$. Since $
X$ is locally compact Hausdorff, $X^\ast$ is compact Hausdorff, hence normal.

**Step 1. $X_0\cup\{\infty\}$ is closed in $X^\ast$.**

Its complement is $X^\ast\setminus(X_0\cup\{\infty\})=X\setminus X_0$, which is
open in $X$ and hence open in $X^\ast$ (since $X$ is open in $X^\ast$). Thus $X_
0\cup\{\infty\}$ is closed in $X^\ast$.

**Step 2. The subspace topology on $X_0\cup\{\infty\}$ inherited from $X^\ast$ c
oincides with the one-point compactification topology of $X_0$.**

Neighborhoods of $\infty$ in $X^\ast$ have the form $\{\infty\}\cup(X\setminus K
)$ with $K\subseteq X$ compact. Intersecting with $X_0\cup\{\infty\}$ gives $\{\
infty\}\cup\bigl(X_0\setminus(K\cap X_0)\bigr)$. Since $X_0$ is closed in $X$, t
he set $K\cap X_0$ is a closed subset of the compact set $K$, hence compact in $
X$; being contained in $X_0$ it is compact in $X_0$. So these are neighborhoods
of the form $\{\infty\}\cup(X_0\setminus C)$ with $C$ compact in $X_0$.

Conversely, every $C$ compact in $X_0$ is compact in $X$ (compactness is intrins
ic), and $\{\infty\}\cup(X_0\setminus C)=\bigl(\{\infty\}\cup(X\setminus C)\bigr
)\cap(X_0\cup\{\infty\})$ is open in the subspace topology.

Hence the two topologies agree. (When $X_0$ is compact, $\infty$ is isolated in
both topologies, so the claim still holds.)

**Step 3. Extend $f$ to $X_0\cup\{\infty\}$.**

Define $f^\ast:X_0\cup\{\infty\}\to\mathbb{C}$ (or $\mathbb{R}$) by $f^\ast|_{X_
0}=f$ and $f^\ast(\infty)=0$. Since $f\in C_0(X_0)$, by definition $f$ extends c
ontinuously to the one-point compactification $X_0^\ast$ with value $0$ at the p
oint at infinity. By Step 2 this is exactly continuity of $f^\ast$ on $X_0\cup\{
\infty\}\subseteq X^\ast$.

**Step 4. Apply Tietze's extension theorem.**

$X^\ast$ is compact Hausdorff (hence normal) and $X_0\cup\{\infty\}$ is a closed
 subset. By Tietze's theorem, $f^\ast$ extends to a continuous function $g:X^\as
t\to\mathbb{C}$ (for complex-valued $f$, extend real and imaginary parts separat
ely).

**Step 5. Restrict back to $X$.**

Since $g(\infty)=f^\ast(\infty)=0$, the restriction $\tilde f:=g|_X$ is continuo
us on $X$ and vanishes at infinity, i.e. $\tilde f\in C_0(X)$. Moreover $\tilde
f|_{X_0}=f^\ast|_{X_0}=f$. $\quad\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** This shows the restriction map $r:C_0(X)\to C_0(X_0)$, $g\mapsto g|_
{X_0}$, is surjective; its kernel is the closed ideal $\{g\in C_0(X):g|_{X_0}=0\
}$, giving the standard C\*-algebraic identification $C_0(X_0)\cong C_0(X)/\ker
r$.
