**Problem.** Let $a, b$ be positive integers. Show that if $4ab - 1 \mid (4a^2 -
 1)^2$ then $a = b$.

---

**Proof.**

**Step 1: Reduction to a symmetric divisibility condition.**

Compute $4a^2 - 1$ modulo $4ab - 1$:

$$4a^2 - 1 = 4a(a - b) + (4ab - 1) \equiv 4a(a - b) \pmod{4ab - 1}.$$

Therefore

$$(4a^2 - 1)^2 \equiv 16a^2(a-b)^2 \pmod{4ab - 1}.$$

Since $4ab - 1 = 4a \cdot b - 1$, we have $\gcd(4a,\, 4ab - 1) = \gcd(4a, -1) =
1$, hence $\gcd(16a^2,\, 4ab - 1) = 1$.

The condition $4ab - 1 \mid (4a^2-1)^2$ thus forces

$$\boxed{4ab - 1 \mid (a - b)^2.}$$

This condition is symmetric in $a$ and $b$.

**Step 2: Assume for contradiction that $a \neq b$.**

By symmetry suppose $a > b \geq 1$. Set $c = a - b \geq 1$. Since $(a-b)^2 > 0$
and $4ab - 1 > 0$, there exists a positive integer $k$ with

$$(a - b)^2 = k(4ab - 1). \tag{$\star$}$$

Expanding $(\star)$ as a quadratic in $a$ (with $b, k$ fixed):

$$a^2 - 2b(1 + 2k)\,a + (b^2 + k) = 0. \tag{$\dagger$}$$

**Step 3: Vieta jumping.**

Let $a'$ be the other root of $(\dagger)$. By Vieta's formulas:

$$a + a' = 2b(1 + 2k), \qquad a \cdot a' = b^2 + k.$$

Since $a^2 - 2b(1+2k)a + (b^2+k) = 0$, reducing modulo $a$ gives $a \mid (b^2 +
k)$, so $a' = (b^2+k)/a$ is a **positive integer** (positivity: $b^2(1+2k)^2 - k
(4b^2(1+k)-1) = b^2 + k > 0$ shows $a' > 0$). Since $a'$ is a root of $(\dagger)
$, the pair $(a', b)$ also satisfies $(\star)$.

**Step 4: $a' \neq a$ and $a' \not> a$, so $a' < a$ (descent).**

- **$a' = a$ is impossible:** Then $a = b(1+2k)$ and $a^2 = b^2 + k$, giving $b^
2(1+2k)^2 = b^2 + k$, i.e., $4kb^2(1+k) = k$, i.e., $4b^2(1+k) = 1$. But $b \geq
 1, k \geq 1$ gives $4b^2(1+k) \geq 8$. Contradiction.

- **$a' > a$ is impossible:** Then $a^2 < a \cdot a' = b^2 + k$, so $(a-b)(a+b)
< k$, i.e., $c(a+b) < k$. But from $(\star)$, $c^2 = k(4ab - 1) \geq 7k$ (since
$a > b \geq 1 \Rightarrow 4ab - 1 \geq 7$), so $k \leq c^2/7$. Combining: $c(a+b
) < c^2/7$, giving $a + b < c/7 = (a-b)/7$. Yet $a + b = (a-b) + 2b > a - b = c
> c/7$. **Contradiction.**

Therefore $a' < a$.

**Step 5: The descent strictly decreases $\max(a,b)$ and cannot terminate.**

From $(a', b)$ with $(a'-b)^2 = k(4a'b - 1)$:

- If $a' > b$: new pair $(a', b)$, $\max = a' < a$.
- If $a' = b$: then $0 = k(4b^2 - 1)$, forcing $k = 0$, contradicting $k \geq 1$
.
- If $a' < b$: new pair $(b, a')$ (using symmetry), $\max = b < a$.

In every valid case, $\max$ of the pair strictly decreases. This produces an inf
inite strictly decreasing sequence of positive integers — **impossible** by well
-ordering. The descent can only terminate at a pair $(c, c)$ with $c = c$, but t
hat forces $k = 0$, contradicting $k \geq 1$.

**Conclusion.** The assumption $a > b$ (and by symmetry $a < b$) leads to a cont
radiction. Hence $a = b$. $\blacksquare$
