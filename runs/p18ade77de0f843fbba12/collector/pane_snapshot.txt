## Answer

**Yes.** There exists a basis of $V$ with respect to which every $f \in K$ is an
 orthogonal matrix.

### Proof

The key idea is to construct a $K$-invariant inner product on $V$ by averaging a
n arbitrary inner product over $K$ using the Haar measure, then take an orthonor
mal basis for this invariant inner product.

---

**Step 1: Choose an arbitrary inner product.**

Since $V$ is a finite-dimensional real vector space, pick any inner product $\la
ngle \cdot,\cdot\rangle_0$ on $V$.

**Step 2: Use the Haar measure on $K$.**

Since $K$ is a compact topological group, it admits a (unique) bi-invariant prob
ability Haar measure $\mu$. Compactness guarantees that $\mu$ is finite (normali
zed to $\mu(K)=1$) and both left- and right-invariant (compact groups are unimod
ular).

**Step 3: Define the averaged inner product.**

Set
$$\langle u, v\rangle \;=\; \int_K \langle f(u),\, f(v)\rangle_0 \;d\mu(f), \qqu
ad u,v\in V.$$

**Step 4: Verify this is an inner product.**

- *Bilinearity*: Immediate from bilinearity of $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_0$ and
 linearity of the integral.
- *Symmetry*: $\langle u,v\rangle = \int_K \langle f(u),f(v)\rangle_0\,d\mu = \i
nt_K \langle f(v),f(u)\rangle_0\,d\mu = \langle v,u\rangle$.
- *Positive definiteness*: $\langle u,u\rangle = \int_K \|f(u)\|_0^2\,d\mu \ge 0
$. If $u\neq 0$, then $f(u)\neq 0$ for every $f\in K$ (since each $f\in GL(V)$ i
s invertible), so $\|f(u)\|_0^2 > 0$ for all $f$, and the integral of a strictly
 positive continuous function over a compact set is strictly positive. Hence $\l
angle u,u\rangle > 0$.

**Step 5: Verify $K$-invariance.**

For any $g\in K$ and $u,v\in V$:
$$\langle g(u),\, g(v)\rangle = \int_K \langle f(g(u)),\, f(g(v))\rangle_0\,d\mu
(f) = \int_K \langle (f\circ g)(u),\, (f\circ g)(v)\rangle_0\,d\mu(f).$$

Make the substitution $h = f\circ g$, i.e., $f = h\circ g^{-1}$. The map $f\maps
to f\circ g$ is right-translation by $g$ on $K$. Since $\mu$ is right-invariant
(compact groups are unimodular), $d\mu(f)=d\mu(h)$, giving:
$$\langle g(u),\, g(v)\rangle = \int_K \langle h(u),\, h(v)\rangle_0\,d\mu(h) =
\langle u, v\rangle.$$

Thus every $g\in K$ preserves $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$.

**Step 6: Choose an orthonormal basis.**

Let $\{e_1,\dots,e_n\}$ be an orthonormal basis of $V$ with respect to the $K$-i
nvariant inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$. For any $g\in K$, since $g$
preserves $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$, the set $\{g(e_1),\dots,g(e_n)\}$ is agai
n orthonormal. Therefore the matrix of $g$ in the basis $\{e_1,\dots,e_n\}$ sati
sfies $A^T A = I$, i.e., it is an orthogonal matrix.

---

**Remark on the role of compactness.** Compactness is essential: it guarantees a
 finite Haar measure, enabling the averaging. For non-compact subgroups of $GL(V
)$, such an invariant inner product need not exist (e.g., a non-compact one-para
meter subgroup cannot be conjugated into $O(n)$).

Equivalently, this shows every compact subgroup of $GL(n,\mathbb{R})$ is conjuga
te to a subgroup of the orthogonal group $O(n)$. $\blacksquare$
