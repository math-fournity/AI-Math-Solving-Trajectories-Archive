**Solution.**

**Step 1: Factor the polynomial.**

Let $x = p^2$. Then
$$p^6 - 7p^2 + 6 = x^3 - 7x + 6.$$
Testing $x=1$: $1-7+6=0$, so $(x-1)$ is a factor. Polynomial division gives
$$x^3 - 7x + 6 = (x-1)(x^2+x-6) = (x-1)(x-2)(x+3).$$
Substituting back:
$$p^6 - 7p^2 + 6 = (p^2-1)(p^2-2)(p^2+3) = (p-1)(p+1)(p^2-2)(p^2+3).$$

**Step 2: Compute the GCD of two specific values.**

For $p=11$:
- $p^2-1 = 120 = 2^3 \cdot 3 \cdot 5$
- $p^2-2 = 119 = 7 \cdot 17$
- $p^2+3 = 124 = 2^2 \cdot 31$
- $v_{11} = 2^5 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 \cdot 17 \cdot 31$

For $p=13$:
- $p^2-1 = 168 = 2^3 \cdot 3 \cdot 7$
- $p^2-2 = 167$ (prime)
- $p^2+3 = 172 = 2^2 \cdot 43$
- $v_{13} = 2^5 \cdot 3 \cdot 7 \cdot 43 \cdot 167$

Therefore
$$\gcd(v_{11}, v_{13}) = 2^5 \cdot 3 \cdot 7 = 672.$$

Since the GCD over **all** primes $p \ge 11$ divides $\gcd(v_{11},v_{13}) = 672$
, the answer is **at most** $672$. It remains to show $672$ divides $v_p$ for ev
ery prime $p \ge 11$.

**Step 3: Show $2^5 \mid v_p$ for all primes $p \ge 11$.**

Every prime $p \ge 11$ is odd. Among the consecutive even integers $p-1$ and $p+
1$, one is divisible by $4$ and the other by $2$, so $v_2(p^2-1) \ge 3$. Since $
p$ is odd, $p^2$ is odd, so $p^2+3$ is even; in fact:
- If $p \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$: $p^2+3 \equiv 1+3 = 4 \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$, so $v_2(p^
2+3) \ge 2$.
- If $p \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$: $p^2+3 \equiv 9+3 = 12 \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$, so $v_2(p
^2+3) \ge 2$.

Thus $v_2(v_p) \ge 3 + 2 = 5$, i.e., $2^5 \mid v_p$.

This bound is **sharp**: for $p=11$, $v_2(p^2-1)=v_2(120)=3$ and $v_2(p^2+3)=v_2
(124)=2$, giving exactly $v_2=5$.

**Step 4: Show $3 \mid v_p$ for all primes $p \ge 11$.**

Every prime $p \ge 11$ satisfies $p \not\equiv 0 \pmod{3}$, so $p \equiv 1$ or $
2 \pmod{3}$. In either case, $p^2 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$, so $p^2 - 1 \equiv 0 \pmod
{3}$. Hence $3 \mid v_p$.

This is sharp: for $p=11$, $v_3(120)=1$ and $v_3(119)=v_3(124)=0$, so $v_3(v_{11
})=1$.

**Step 5: Show $7 \mid v_p$ for all primes $p \ge 11$.**

Every prime $p \ge 11$ satisfies $p \not\equiv 0 \pmod{7}$, so $p^2 \pmod{7} \in
 \{1, 2, 4\}$ (computing squares of $1,2,3,4,5,6 \pmod{7}$). We check each case
against the three factors:
- $p^2 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$: $p^2 - 1 \equiv 0 \pmod{7}$. ✓
- $p^2 \equiv 2 \pmod{7}$: $p^2 - 2 \equiv 0 \pmod{7}$. ✓
- $p^2 \equiv 4 \pmod{7}$: $p^2 + 3 \equiv 0 \pmod{7}$. ✓

In every case, $7 \mid v_p$.

This is sharp: for $p=11$, $p^2 \equiv 2 \pmod{7}$, so $7 \mid (p^2-2) = 119 = 7
 \cdot 17$, giving $v_7 = 1$.

**Step 6: Conclusion.**

We have shown:
- $672 = 2^5 \cdot 3 \cdot 7$ divides $v_p$ for every prime $p \ge 11$ (Steps 3–
5).
- The GCD of all values divides $\gcd(v_{11}, v_{13}) = 672$ (Step 2).
- Each prime-power component ($2^5$, $3^1$, $7^1$) is sharp, so no larger diviso
r works.

Therefore the greatest common divisor is:

$$\boxed{672}$$
