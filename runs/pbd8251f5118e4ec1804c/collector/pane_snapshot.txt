## Proof

We evaluate
$$I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\sin^2(\pi R x)}{R(\pi x)^2}\,dx, \qquad R>0
.$$

**Step 1: Rewrite in terms of the sinc function.**

Define $\operatorname{sinc}(t) = \dfrac{\sin(\pi t)}{\pi t}$ (with $\operatornam
e{sinc}(0)=1$ by continuity). Then

$$\frac{\sin^2(\pi R x)}{R(\pi x)^2} = \frac{1}{R}\left(\frac{\sin(\pi R x)}{\pi
 x}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{R}\bigl(\pi R x\bigr)^2\left(\frac{\sin(\pi R x)}{\pi R
x}\right)^2\frac{1}{(\pi x)^2}\cdot\frac{1}{1}.$$

More directly, since $\dfrac{\sin(\pi R x)}{\pi x} = R\,\dfrac{\sin(\pi R x)}{\p
i R x} = R\,\operatorname{sinc}(Rx)$,

$$\frac{\sin^2(\pi R x)}{R(\pi x)^2} = \frac{1}{R}\bigl(R\,\operatorname{sinc}(R
x)\bigr)^2 = R\,\operatorname{sinc}^2(Rx).$$

**Step 2: Substitute $t = Rx$.**

With $t = Rx$, $dt = R\,dx$, so $dx = dt/R$:

$$I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} R\,\operatorname{sinc}^2(Rx)\,dx = \int_{-\infty}^
{\infty} R\,\operatorname{sinc}^2(t)\,\frac{dt}{R} = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \op
eratorname{sinc}^2(t)\,dt.$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the normalized sinc-squared integral.**

Using $\sin^2(\pi t) = \frac{1-\cos(2\pi t)}{2}$,

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{sinc}^2(t)\,dt = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\
frac{\sin^2(\pi t)}{(\pi t)^2}\,dt = \frac{1}{\pi^2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac
{\sin^2(\pi t)}{t^2}\,dt = \frac{1}{2\pi^2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos(2
\pi t)}{t^2}\,dt.$$

Integrate by parts with $u = 1-\cos(2\pi t)$, $dv = t^{-2}dt$ (so $v=-1/t$, $du
= 2\pi\sin(2\pi t)\,dt$):

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos(2\pi t)}{t^2}\,dt = \underbrace{\left[-\fr
ac{1-\cos(2\pi t)}{t}\right]_{-\infty}^{\infty}}_{=\,0} + \int_{-\infty}^{\infty
}\frac{2\pi\sin(2\pi t)}{t}\,dt.$$

With $s = 2\pi t$ ($dt = ds/(2\pi)$):

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{2\pi\sin(2\pi t)}{t}\,dt = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty
}\frac{2\pi\sin s}{s/(2\pi)}\,\frac{ds}{2\pi} = 2\pi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac
{\sin s}{s}\,ds = 2\pi\cdot\pi = 2\pi^2,$$

using the classical Dirichlet integral $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\fra
c{\sin s}{s}\,ds = \pi$.

Therefore

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{sinc}^2(t)\,dt = \frac{1}{2\pi^2}\cdot 2\
pi^2 = 1.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\sin^2(\pi R x)}{R(\pi x)^2}\,dx = 1.
}$$

The value is $1$, independent of $R>0$. (This is the $L^1$-normalization of the
Fejér/sinc kernel: scaling in $x$ by $R$ is exactly compensated by the prefactor
 $1/R$.)
