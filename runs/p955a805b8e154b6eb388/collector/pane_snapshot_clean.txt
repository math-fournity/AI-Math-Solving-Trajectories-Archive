## Proof

Let $p_i$ denote the $i$-th prime, and set $k = k(n) = \lfloor \log n \rfloor$.
We study

$$L_n := \log\log n \prod_{i=1}^{k} \frac{p_i - 1}{p_i} = \log\log n \prod_{i=1}
^{k}\left(1 - \frac{1}{p_i}\right).$$

We will show $L_n \to e^{-\gamma} > 0$, where $\gamma$ is the Euler–Mascheroni c
onstant.

**Step 1: Locate the largest prime in the product.**

By the prime number theorem, the $k$-th prime satisfies $p_k \sim k\log k$. With
 $k = \lfloor \log n\rfloor \to \infty$,

$$p_k \sim (\log n)\cdot \log\log n \qquad (n\to\infty).$$

In particular $p_k \to \infty$, so Mertens' theorem applies to the partial produ
ct $\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-1/p_i) = \prod_{p \le p_k}(1-1/p)$.

**Step 2: Apply Mertens' third theorem.**

Mertens' theorem (1874) states that as $x\to\infty$,

$$\prod_{p \le x}\left(1 - \frac{1}{p}\right) \sim \frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\log x}.$$

Applying this with $x = p_k$,

$$\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(1 - \frac{1}{p_i}\right) \sim \frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\log p_k
}.$$

**Step 3: Estimate $\log p_k$.**

From $p_k \sim (\log n)(\log\log n)$,

$$\log p_k = \log\big((\log n)(\log\log n)\big)(1+o(1)) = \log\log n + \log\log\
log n + o(1).$$

Since $\log\log\log n = o(\log\log n)$,

$$\log p_k \sim \log\log n \qquad (n\to\infty).$$

**Step 4: Combine.**

Substituting into the Mertens asymptotic,

$$\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_i}\right) \sim \frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\log p_k}
\sim \frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\log\log n}.$$

Therefore

$$L_n = \log\log n \cdot \prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_i}\right) \sim \log\l
og n \cdot \frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\log\log n} = e^{-\gamma}.$$

**Step 5: Conclude.**

The Euler–Mascheroni constant satisfies $\gamma \approx 0.5772 < 1$, so

$$e^{-\gamma} > e^{-1} > 0.$$

Hence

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} \log\log n \prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor\log n\rfloor}\frac{p_i-1}{p_
i} = e^{-\gamma} > 0.$$

The limit exists and is strictly greater than zero. $\blacksquare$
