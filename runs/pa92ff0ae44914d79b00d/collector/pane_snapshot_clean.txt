## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. A simple algebraic extension of degree $n$ of $\mathbb{F}$ embed
s into $M_n(\mathbb{F})$ as an $\mathbb{F}$-algebra.

**Setup.** Let $K = \mathbb{F}(\alpha)$ where $\alpha$ is algebraic over $\mathb
b{F}$ with minimal polynomial $p(x) \in \mathbb{F}[x]$ of degree $n$. Then $K$ i
s an $n$-dimensional $\mathbb{F}$-vector space with basis $\{1, \alpha, \alpha^2
, \dots, \alpha^{n-1}\}$.

**Construction (the regular representation).** Define a map
$$\varphi : K \to \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(K) \cong M_n(\mathbb{F})$$
by letting $\varphi(a)$ be the $\mathbb{F}$-linear endomorphism "left multiplica
tion by $a$":
$$\varphi(a)(b) = a \cdot b, \qquad b \in K.$$

**$\varphi$ is an $\mathbb{F}$-algebra homomorphism.** For $a, a' \in K$ and $\l
ambda \in \mathbb{F}$:
- $\varphi(a + a')(b) = (a+a')b = ab + a'b = (\varphi(a)+\varphi(a'))(b)$, so $\
varphi(a+a') = \varphi(a)+\varphi(a')$.
- $\varphi(\lambda a)(b) = (\lambda a)b = \lambda(ab) = \lambda\,\varphi(a)(b)$,
 so $\varphi(\lambda a) = \lambda\,\varphi(a)$. Thus $\varphi$ is $\mathbb{F}$-l
inear.
- $\varphi(aa')(b) = (aa')b = a(a'b) = \varphi(a)\big(\varphi(a')(b)\big) = (\va
rphi(a)\circ\varphi(a'))(b)$, so $\varphi(aa') = \varphi(a)\,\varphi(a')$.
- $\varphi(1)(b) = 1\cdot b = b$, so $\varphi(1) = \operatorname{id}_K$, the ide
ntity endomorphism.

Hence $\varphi$ is a unital $\mathbb{F}$-algebra homomorphism.

**$\varphi$ is injective.** The kernel $\ker\varphi$ is a two-sided ideal of $K$
. Since $K = \mathbb{F}(\alpha)$ is a field (a simple algebraic extension of a f
ield is a field), its only two-sided ideals are $\{0\}$ and $K$. But $\varphi(1)
 = \operatorname{id}_K \neq 0$, so $\ker\varphi \neq K$. Therefore $\ker\varphi
= \{0\}$, and $\varphi$ is injective.

**Conclusion.** The injective $\mathbb{F}$-algebra homomorphism $\varphi : K \ho
okrightarrow M_n(\mathbb{F})$ is the desired embedding.

**Remark on the image of $\alpha$.** With respect to the basis $\{1, \alpha, \do
ts, \alpha^{n-1}\}$, the matrix $\varphi(\alpha)$ is the **companion matrix** of
 the minimal polynomial $p(x)$. Writing $p(x) = x^n + c_{n-1}x^{n-1} + \cdots +
c_1 x + c_0$, the relation $\alpha^n = -c_{n-1}\alpha^{n-1} - \cdots - c_0$ give
s
$$\varphi(\alpha) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & -c_0 \\ 1 & 0 & \cdots
& 0 & -c_1 \\ 0 & 1 & \cdots & 0 & -c_2 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \
vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & 1 & -c_{n-1} \end{pmatrix},$$
whose characteristic (and minimal) polynomial is exactly $p(x)$. This confirms c
oncretely that $\mathbb{F}(\alpha) \cong \mathbb{F}[\varphi(\alpha)] \subseteq M
_n(\mathbb{F})$, a commutative subfield of $M_n(\mathbb{F})$ isomorphic to $K$.
