# Solution

## Setting up the equation

Let $T = \operatorname{Trace}(Q\Sigma Q^\top)$. Since $M$ is the diagonal matrix
 of eigenvalues of $Q\Sigma Q^\top$, we have $T = \sum_i M_i$. The equation beco
mes:

$$8\lambda + 2\sum_i M_i - \sum_i \frac{2M_i}{1 - 2\lambda M_i} = 0.$$

## Simplification

Divide the entire equation by 2:

$$4\lambda + \sum_i M_i - \sum_i \frac{M_i}{1 - 2\lambda M_i} = 0.$$

Combine the two sums term-by-term:

$$\sum_i \left(M_i - \frac{M_i}{1-2\lambda M_i}\right) = \sum_i \frac{M_i(1-2\la
mbda M_i) - M_i}{1-2\lambda M_i} = \sum_i \frac{-2\lambda M_i^2}{1-2\lambda M_i}
.$$

Substituting back:

$$4\lambda - 2\lambda \sum_i \frac{M_i^2}{1-2\lambda M_i} = 0.$$

Factor out $2\lambda$:

$$2\lambda\left(2 - \sum_i \frac{M_i^2}{1-2\lambda M_i}\right) = 0.$$

## Extracting the roots

This gives two cases:

**Case 1:** $\lambda = 0$.

**Verification:** Substituting $\lambda = 0$ into the original equation:
$$8(0) + 2\sum_i M_i - \sum_i \frac{2M_i}{1-0} = 2\sum_i M_i - 2\sum_i M_i = 0.
\quad\checkmark$$

So $\lambda = 0$ is always a root.

**Case 2:** $\displaystyle\sum_i \frac{M_i^2}{1-2\lambda M_i} = 2$.

This is a rational equation in $\lambda$. If $Q\Sigma Q^\top$ has $n$ distinct e
igenvalues $M_1, \dots, M_n$, clearing denominators (multiplying by $\prod_i (1-
2\lambda M_i)$) yields a polynomial of degree $n$ in $\lambda$, giving up to $n$
 additional roots (excluding the poles $\lambda = \frac{1}{2M_i}$, which are not
 in the domain of the original equation).

## Conclusion

The roots of the equation are:

$$\boxed{\lambda = 0 \quad\text{and the solutions of}\quad \sum_i \frac{M_i^2}{1
-2\lambda M_i} = 2.}$$

Equivalently, $\lambda = 0$ together with the $n$ roots of the degree-$n$ polyno
mial obtained from $\sum_i \frac{M_i^2}{1-2\lambda M_i} = 2$ after clearing deno
minators, where $n$ is the number of distinct eigenvalues of $Q\Sigma Q^\top$.
