## Proof

**Claim:** The generators $\{g_1, \ldots, g_m\}$ where $g_i = y_i - \psi_i$ form
 a Gröbner basis for $I = \langle y_1 - \psi_1, \ldots, y_m - \psi_m\rangle$ wit
h respect to the lexicographic order $y_1 > y_2 > \cdots > y_m > x_1 > \cdots >
x_n$.

**Key observation.** Since each $\psi_i \in k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ involves only t
he variables $x_1, \ldots, x_n$, which are all strictly smaller than every $y_j$
 in the given lex order, the leading monomial of $g_i = y_i - \psi_i$ is:

$$\operatorname{LM}(g_i) = y_i, \qquad \operatorname{LC}(g_i) = 1, \qquad i = 1,
 \ldots, m.$$

The leading monomials $y_1, y_2, \ldots, y_m$ are **pairwise coprime** (they are
 distinct indeterminates), so $\gcd(\operatorname{LM}(g_i), \operatorname{LM}(g_
j)) = 1$ for all $i \neq j$.

**Verification via Buchberger's criterion.** By Buchberger's criterion, $\{g_1,
\ldots, g_m\}$ is a Gröbner basis if and only if every S-polynomial $S(g_i, g_j)
$ ($i \neq j$) reduces to $0$ modulo $\{g_1, \ldots, g_m\}$.

Fix $i \neq j$; without loss of generality assume $i < j$ (so $y_i > y_j$). The
S-polynomial is:

$$S(g_i, g_j) = \frac{\operatorname{lcm}(y_i, y_j)}{y_i}\, g_i - \frac{\operator
name{lcm}(y_i, y_j)}{y_j}\, g_j = y_j\, g_i - y_i\, g_j.$$

Since $y_i$ and $y_j$ are distinct variables, $\operatorname{lcm}(y_i, y_j) = y_
i y_j$. Expanding:

$$S(g_i, g_j) = y_j(y_i - \psi_i) - y_i(y_j - \psi_j) = y_i\,\psi_j - y_j\,\psi_
i.$$

**Reduction to zero.** We reduce $S(g_i, g_j)$ step by step.

*Phase 1: Eliminate $y_i$ using $g_i$.* Since $i < j$, every monomial divisible
by $y_i$ is strictly greater than every monomial divisible by $y_j$ (but not $y_
i$) in the lex order. Hence the leading term of $S(g_i, g_j)$ is $y_i \cdot \ope
ratorname{LT}(\psi_j)$, which is divisible by $\operatorname{LM}(g_i) = y_i$. We
 reduce:

$$S(g_i, g_j) - \operatorname{LT}(\psi_j)\cdot g_i = y_i\,\operatorname{red}(\ps
i_j) - y_j\,\psi_i + \operatorname{LT}(\psi_j)\,\psi_i,$$

where $\operatorname{red}(\psi_j) = \psi_j - \operatorname{LT}(\psi_j)$. The new
 leading term is $y_i \cdot \operatorname{LT}(\operatorname{red}(\psi_j))$ (if $
\operatorname{red}(\psi_j) \neq 0$), still divisible by $y_i$. We continue reduc
ing each term of $\psi_j$ against $g_i$. After processing all terms of $\psi_j$,
 every occurrence of $y_i$ has been replaced by $\psi_i$, yielding:

$$S(g_i, g_j) \;\xrightarrow{g_i}\; \psi_i\,\psi_j - y_j\,\psi_i.$$

*Phase 2: Eliminate $y_j$ using $g_j$.* The remaining polynomial is $\psi_i\psi_
j - y_j\psi_i$. Its leading term is $-y_j \cdot \operatorname{LT}(\psi_i)$ (sinc
e $y_j$ exceeds any monomial in $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$), which is divisible by $\op
eratorname{LM}(g_j) = y_j$. We reduce:

$$\psi_i\psi_j - y_j\psi_i + \operatorname{LT}(\psi_i)\cdot g_j = \psi_i\psi_j -
 y_j\,\operatorname{red}(\psi_i) - \operatorname{LT}(\psi_i)\,\psi_j.$$

The leading term is again divisible by $y_j$. Continuing this process, each term
 of $\psi_i$ is reduced against $g_j$, replacing every $y_j$ by $\psi_j$. After
processing all terms of $\psi_i$:

$$\psi_i\psi_j - y_j\,\psi_i \;\xrightarrow{g_j}\; \psi_i\psi_j - \psi_i\psi_j =
 0.$$

**Edge cases.** If $\psi_j = 0$, then $S(g_i, g_j) = -y_j\psi_i$, and reducing $
y_j\psi_i$ against $g_j$ replaces $y_j$ by $\psi_j = 0$, giving $0$. If both $\p
si_i = \psi_j = 0$, then $S(g_i, g_j) = y_i y_j - y_i y_j = 0$ trivially. In all
 cases the S-polynomial reduces to $0$.

**Conclusion.** Every S-polynomial $S(g_i, g_j)$ with $i \neq j$ reduces to $0$
modulo $\{g_1, \ldots, g_m\}$. By Buchberger's criterion, the set $\{y_1 - \psi_
1, \ldots, y_m - \psi_m\}$ is a Gröbner basis for $I$ with respect to the lexico
graphic order $y_1 > y_2 > \cdots > y_m > x_1 > \cdots > x_n$.

This is in fact an instance of **Buchberger's product criterion**: when the lead
ing monomials of the generators are pairwise coprime — as is the case here with
the distinct variables $y_1, \ldots, y_m$ — the generators automatically form a
Gröbner basis. The hypothesis $\psi_i \in k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ is essential: it
guarantees that $\operatorname{LM}(g_i) = y_i$ (since $\psi_i$ involves only var
iables strictly below $y_i$ in the term order), which makes the leading monomial
s pairwise coprime.
