Proof

Claim: Every conformal diffeomorphism of a closed hyperbolic surface of genus $g
\geq 2$ is an isometry.

Answer: Yes.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Setup. Let $\Sigma$ be a closed oriented surface of genus $g \geq 2$ equipped
with a hyperbolic metric $g$ (a Riemannian metric of constant Gaussian curvature
$-1$). The conformal class $[g]$ makes $\Sigma$ into a closed Riemann surface of
genus $\geq 2$. Let $f\colon \Sigma \to \Sigma$ be a conformal diffeomorphism,
i.e., a diffeomorphism with $f^*[g] = [g]$.

Step 1 — Uniqueness of the hyperbolic metric in a conformal class
(Uniformization).

By the Uniformization Theorem, on a closed Riemann surface of genus $g \geq 2$,
there exists a unique Riemannian metric of constant Gaussian curvature $-1$
compatible with the given conformal structure.

Proof of uniqueness. Suppose $g$ and $\tilde g = e^{2u}g$ are both metrics of
constant curvature $-1$ in the same conformal class on the closed surface $
\Sigma$. The curvature transformation law under a conformal change $\tilde g =
e^{2u}g$ reads $$\widetilde K = e^{-2u}\bigl(K - \Delta_g u\bigr),$$ where $
\Delta_g$ is the Laplace–Beltrami operator of $g$. Setting $K = \widetilde K =
-1$: $$-1 = e^{-2u}(-1 - \Delta_g u) \implies \Delta_g u = e^{2u} - 1.$$ Since $
\Sigma$ is closed, $\int_\Sigma \Delta_g u, dA_g = 0$, so $\int_\Sigma
(e^{2u}-1),dA_g = 0$.

Now apply the maximum principle. At a point where $u$ attains its maximum, $
\Delta_g u \leq 0$, so $e^{2u} - 1 \leq 0$, giving $u \leq 0$ at the maximum. At
a point where $u$ attains its minimum, $\Delta_g u \geq 0$, so $e^{2u} - 1 \geq
0$, giving $u \geq 0$ at the minimum. Hence $$\max_\Sigma u \leq 0 \leq
\min_\Sigma u,$$ which forces $u \equiv 0$, i.e., $\tilde g = g$. ∎

Step 2 — Pullback of the hyperbolic metric is hyperbolic and conformally
equivalent.

Since $f$ is a diffeomorphism, the pullback $f^*g$ is a Riemannian metric on $
\Sigma$ with the same constant Gaussian curvature $-1$ (Gaussian curvature is a
diffeomorphism invariant: $K_{f^*g} = (K_g)\circ f$).

Since $f$ is conformal, $f^*[g] = [g]$, meaning $f^*g$ lies in the same
conformal class as $g$.

*Step 3 — Uniqueness forces $f^g = g$.

Both $g$ and $f^*g$ are metrics of constant curvature $-1$ in the conformal
class $[g]$. By the uniqueness established in Step 1, $$f^*g = g.$$

Conclusion. The equality $f^*g = g$ is precisely the statement that $f$ is an
isometry of $(\Sigma, g)$. Therefore every conformal diffeomorphism of a closed
hyperbolic surface of genus $g \geq 2$ is an isometry. $\blacksquare$

Remark. The essential ingredients are: (i) the Uniformization Theorem providing
existence and uniqueness of the curvature $-1$ metric in each conformal class
for genus $\geq 2$, and (ii) the fact that conformal diffeomorphisms preserve
the conformal class while diffeomorphisms preserve curvature. The genus $\geq 2$
hypothesis is crucial: on the sphere (genus $0$) the conformal group $
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ is strictly larger than the isometry group $
\mathrm{SO}(3)$, and on the torus (genus $1$) the flat metric is not uniquely
determined by the conformal structure (the moduli space is nontrivial), so
conformal maps need not be isometries in those cases.
