## Proof

We are given the generating function for the Legendre polynomials

$$\Phi(x,h) = (1 - 2xh + h^2)^{-1/2} = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} P_l(x)\, h^l, \qquad
|h|<1,$$

and Legendre's differential equation

$$(1-x^2)\,P_l''(x) - 2x\,P_l'(x) + l(l+1)\,P_l(x) = 0. \tag{LDE}$$

We must show

$$(1-x^2)\frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x^2} - 2x\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial
x} + h\frac{\partial^2}{\partial h^2}\!\left(h\Phi\right) = 0. \tag{$*$}$$

---

### Step 1. The first two terms

Differentiating the series $\Phi = \sum_{l\ge 0} P_l(x) h^l$ term-by-term with r
espect to $x$ (valid for $|h|<1$):

$$\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial x} = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} P_l'(x)\, h^l, \qquad \
frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x^2} = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} P_l''(x)\, h^l.$$

Hence

$$(1-x^2)\frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x^2} - 2x\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial
x} = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\Big[(1-x^2)P_l''(x) - 2x\,P_l'(x)\Big]\,h^l.$$

By Legendre's equation (LDE), the bracket equals $-l(l+1)\,P_l(x)$. Therefore

$$(1-x^2)\frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x^2} - 2x\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial
x} = -\sum_{l=0}^{\infty} l(l+1)\,P_l(x)\,h^l. \tag{1}$$

---

### Step 2. The third term $h\,\dfrac{\partial^2}{\partial h^2}(h\Phi)$

First,

$$h\Phi = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} P_l(x)\,h^{l+1}.$$

Differentiating with respect to $h$:

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial h}(h\Phi) = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} (l+1)\,P_l(x)\,h^{l},
$$

$$\frac{\partial^2}{\partial h^2}(h\Phi) = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} (l+1)\,l\,P_l(x)\
,h^{l-1}.$$

Multiplying by $h$:

$$h\frac{\partial^2}{\partial h^2}(h\Phi) = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} l(l+1)\,P_l(x)\,
h^{l}. \tag{2}$$

(The $l=0$ term vanishes since $l(l+1)=0$, so the sum is effectively from $l=1$,
 but writing it from $l=0$ is harmless.)

---

### Step 3. Combine

Adding (1) and (2):

$$(1-x^2)\frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x^2} - 2x\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial
x} + h\frac{\partial^2}{\partial h^2}(h\Phi) = -\sum_{l=0}^{\infty} l(l+1)\,P_l(
x)\,h^l + \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} l(l+1)\,P_l(x)\,h^l = 0.$$

This holds for every $|h|<1$ (where the series converges absolutely and term-by-
term differentiation is justified), completing the verification. $\blacksquare$
