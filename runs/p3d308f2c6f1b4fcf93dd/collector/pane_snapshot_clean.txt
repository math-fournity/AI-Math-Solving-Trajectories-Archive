Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether there exists a quasisimple
group $G$ and an odd prime $p$ such that $G$ has cyclic Sylow $p$-subgroups and
a weakly real element of $p$-power order (where "weakly real" means real but not
 strongly real — i.e., conjugate to its inverse, but not conjugate to its invers
e via any involution).

**Answer: Yes.** I exhibit $G = SL_2(5)$ and $p = 3$.

---

## Proof

### Step 1: $G = SL_2(5)$ is quasisimple

$SL_2(5)$ is the binary icosahedral group $2.A_5$, a double cover of $A_5 \cong
PSL_2(5)$. We have $|SL_2(5)| = 5(5^2-1) = 120$ and $Z(G) = \{\pm I\} \cong \mat
hbb{Z}/2$.

$PSL_2(5) \cong A_5$ is non-abelian simple. To confirm $G$ is perfect: $SL_2(5)$
 has a **unique** element of order 2, namely $-I$ (central), whereas $A_5$ has 1
5 involutions. If $G$ had a subgroup isomorphic to $A_5$, it would contain non-c
entral involutions — contradiction. So the extension $1 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to G \
to A_5 \to 1$ is non-split, forcing $[G,G] = G$ (the commutator subgroup surject
s onto $A_5$ and cannot be a complement). Thus $G$ is perfect, and $G/Z(G) \cong
 A_5$ is simple, so $G$ is **quasisimple**.

### Step 2: $p=3$ is odd and Sylow 3-subgroups are cyclic

$|G| = 120 = 2^3 \cdot 3 \cdot 5$, so Sylow 3-subgroups have order 3, hence are
cyclic ($\cong \mathbb{Z}/3$). ✓

### Step 3: A weakly real element of order 3 exists

Consider the **non-split torus** $T$ in $SL_2(5)$, of order $q+1 = 6$, so $T \co
ng \mathbb{Z}/6$. Let $x \in T$ be an element of order 3 (existing since $3 \mid
 6$). Then $P := \langle x \rangle$ is a Sylow 3-subgroup.

**$x$ is real.** The normalizer $N_G(T) = T \rtimes \langle w \rangle$, where $w
$ acts by inversion on $T$ and $w^2 = -I$ (the central involution). Since $w x w
^{-1} = x^{-1}$, the element $x$ is real.

**$N_G(\langle x \rangle) = N_G(T)$.** Since $T$ is cyclic of order 6, $\langle
x \rangle$ is its unique subgroup of order 3. The centralizer $C_G(x) = T$ (as $
x$ is semisimple with eigenvalues in $\mathbb{F}_{25} \setminus \mathbb{F}_5$, s
o its centralizer is the non-split torus). Any $g \in N_G(\langle x \rangle$ nor
malizes $C_G(\langle x \rangle) = T$, giving $N_G(\langle x \rangle) \leq N_G(T)
$; conversely $N_G(T)$ normalizes the unique order-3 subgroup of $T$. So they ar
e equal.

**No involution inverts $x$.** Any element inverting $x$ lies in $N_G(\langle x
\rangle) = N_G(T) = T \cup Tw$. We examine all involutions in $N_G(T)$:

- **In $T \cong \mathbb{Z}/6$:** the unique involution is the element of order 2
, which is $-I$ (central). A central element cannot invert a non-central element
.
- **In the coset $Tw$:** for any $t \in T$, $(tw)^2 = t \cdot (wtw^{-1}) \cdot w
^2 = t \cdot t^{-1} \cdot (-I) = -I \neq I$. So **no** element of $Tw$ is an inv
olution.

Therefore the only involution in $N_G(T)$ is $-I$, which is central and does not
 invert $x$. Since every element inverting $x$ must lie in $N_G(T)$, **no involu
tion inverts $x$**.

### Conclusion

$x$ is real (conjugate to $x^{-1}$ via $w$) but **not** strongly real (no involu
tion conjugates $x$ to $x^{-1}$). Hence $x$ is **weakly real**, of order 3 (a 3-
power), in the quasisimple group $SL_2(5)$, which has cyclic Sylow 3-subgroups.

The answer is **yes**. $\blacksquare$
