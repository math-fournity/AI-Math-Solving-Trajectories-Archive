## Solution

**Stability Index** of a chain of length $n$ is $\left\lfloor \dfrac{n^2}{7} \ri
ght\rfloor$.

A chain is **Level-Alpha** if $\left\lfloor \dfrac{n^2}{7} \right\rfloor = p$ fo
r some prime $p < 300$.

### Step 1: Determine the range of $n$

We need $\left\lfloor \dfrac{n^2}{7} \right\rfloor < 300$, which gives $n^2 < 21
00$, so $n \le 45$ (since $45^2 = 2025 < 2100$ and $46^2 = 2116 > 2100$).

We also need $\left\lfloor \dfrac{n^2}{7} \right\rfloor \ge 2$ (smallest prime),
 so $n^2 \ge 14$, giving $n \ge 4$.

Thus $n \in \{4, 5, \ldots, 45\}$.

### Step 2: Distinctness of floor values

For $n \ge 4$, the gap between consecutive squares is $2n+1 \ge 9 > 7$. Since th
e floor function partitions integers into intervals of length 7, two consecutive
 squares (differing by $\ge 9$) must land in different intervals. Hence $\left\l
floor n^2/7 \right\rfloor$ is **strictly increasing** for $n \ge 4$, so each val
id $n$ yields a distinct prime.
