## Answer

**Yes.** For every smooth projective algebraic curve $X$ over $\mathbb{C}$ and e
very positive integer $d$, there exists a smooth projective curve $Y$ and a fini
te morphism $f: Y \to X$ with $\deg(f) = d$.

---

## Proof

**Case $d = 1$.** Take $Y = X$ and $f = \mathrm{id}_X$, which is finite of degre
e $1$.

**Case $d \geq 2$.** Let $K = \mathbb{C}(X)$ be the function field of $X$.

**Step 1: Constructing an irreducible degree-$d$ extension of $K$.**

Pick any point $P \in X$. Since $X$ is smooth, the local ring $\mathcal{O}_{X,P}
$ is a discrete valuation ring; let $\pi_P \in K^*$ be a uniformizer, so $v_P(\p
i_P) = 1$.

Consider the polynomial $T^d - \pi_P \in K[T]$. We claim it is irreducible over
$K$. Indeed, over the DVR $\mathcal{O}_{X,P}$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}_P
 = (\pi_P)$, the polynomial $T^d - \pi_P$ is **Eisenstein**: the leading coeffic
ient is $1$ (a unit), all intermediate coefficients are $0 \in \mathfrak{m}_P$,
and the constant term $-\pi_P$ lies in $\mathfrak{m}_P \setminus \mathfrak{m}_P^
2$ (since $v_P(\pi_P) = 1$). By the Eisenstein irreducibility criterion, $T^d -
\pi_P$ is irreducible over $K$.

Set $L = K(\alpha)$ where $\alpha^d = \pi_P$. Then $[L : K] = d$, and since $\ma
thrm{char}\,\mathbb{C} = 0$, the extension $L/K$ is separable.

**Step 2: Normalization gives a smooth projective curve $Y$.**

Let $Y$ be the normalization of $X$ in $L$. Concretely, $Y$ is the unique normal
 projective curve with function field $\mathbb{C}(Y) = L$ equipped with a finite
 morphism $f: Y \to X$ inducing the inclusion $K \hookrightarrow L$ on function
fields.

- **$Y$ is smooth over $\mathbb{C}$:** Since $X$ is a smooth curve and $L/K$ is
a finite separable extension, the normalization $Y$ is a normal curve. Over the
perfect field $\mathbb{C}$, normality, regularity, and smoothness are equivalent
 for curves. Hence $Y$ is smooth.

- **$Y$ is projective over $\mathbb{C}$:** The morphism $f: Y \to X$ is finite (
normalization of a curve is finite), hence projective. Since $X$ is projective o
ver $\mathbb{C}$ and projective morphisms are stable under composition, $Y$ is p
rojective over $\mathbb{C}$.

- **$\deg(f) = d$:** The degree of the finite morphism $f$ between smooth projec
tive curves equals the degree of the associated function field extension:
$$\deg(f) = [\mathbb{C}(Y) : \mathbb{C}(X)] = [L : K] = d.$$

**Conclusion.** For every positive integer $d$, we have produced a smooth projec
tive curve $Y$ and a finite morphism $f: Y \to X$ of degree $d$. $\blacksquare$
