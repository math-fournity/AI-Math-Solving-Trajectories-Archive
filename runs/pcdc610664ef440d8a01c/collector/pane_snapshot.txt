## Setting Up the Constraint

Let $u = x(2-x) = 2x - x^2$. For $x \in (0,2)$, we have $u \in (0,1]$ with maxim
um $u=1$ at $x=1$.

From the constraint:
$$\frac{1}{3-y} = 10 - \frac{8}{u} = \frac{10u - 8}{u}$$

For $3 - y > 0$ (i.e., $y < 3$), we need $10u - 8 > 0$, so $u > \frac{4}{5}$.

Then:
$$3 - y = \frac{u}{10u - 8}, \qquad y = 3 - \frac{u}{10u-8} = \frac{29u - 24}{10
u - 8}$$

For $y > 0$: $29u - 24 > 0$, i.e., $u > \frac{24}{29} \approx 0.8276$.

So the feasible region requires $u \in \left(\frac{24}{29},\, 1\right]$, giving
$y \in (0, \frac{5}{2}]$.

## Parametrization

Set $t = x - 1 \in (-1, 1)$, so $x = 1 + t$ and $u = 1 - t^2$. To minimize $\fra
c{12}{x}$, we want $x$ large, so we focus on $t \in (0, 1)$ (i.e., $x > 1$). The
 other branch $x < 1$ gives strictly larger $\frac{12}{x}$ for the same $u$, hen

With $t \in (0, \sqrt{5/29})$ (the upper bound ensures $y > 0$):

2 = \frac{12}{1+t} - \frac{(5-29t^2)^2}{16(1-5t^2)^2}$$

## Lagrange Multipliers

$$\frac{12}{x^2} = \frac{8y(3-y)^2(x-1)}{u^2}$$

Since $u = x(2-x)$, we have $u^2/x^2 = (2-x)^2$, giving:

$$\frac{3(2-x)^2}{2(x-1)} = y(3-y)^2$$

The left side is positive only when $x > 1$, confirming $x > 1$ at any interior
critical point.

## Substituting the Constraint

Using $y = \frac{29u-24}{10u-8}$ and $(3-y)^2 = \frac{u^2}{(10u-8)^2}$, with $u

$$\frac{3(1-t)^2}{2t} = \frac{(5-29t^2)(1-t^2)^2}{8(1-5t^2)^3}$$

Factoring $(1-t^2)^2 = (1-t)^2(1+t)^2$ and canceling $(1-t)^2 \neq 0$:

$$\frac{3}{2t} = \frac{(5-29t^2)(1+t)^2}{8(1-5t^2)^3}$$

Cross-multiplying:

$$12(1-5t^2)^3 = t(5-29t^2)(1+t)^2$$
