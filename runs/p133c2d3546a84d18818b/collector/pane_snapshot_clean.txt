## Proof

**Claim.** For any conclusion $C$ (from a fixed finite set of premises $\Gamma$)
 that is derivable in natural deduction, there exists a derivation of $C$ from $
\Gamma$ with the fewest possible number of inference steps.

**Proof.**

A natural deduction derivation is a finite object: a finite tree (or, in systems
 permitting DAG-sharing, a finite DAG) whose nodes are inference-rule applicatio
ns, whose leaves are either assumptions or axioms, and whose root is the conclus
ion. Every such derivation has a well-defined **step count** — the number of inf
erence-rule applications (interior nodes) — which is a non-negative integer.

Fix a conclusion $C$ and premises $\Gamma$, and suppose $C$ is derivable from $\
Gamma$ (i.e., $\Gamma \vdash C$). Consider the set

$$S = \{\, n \in \mathbb{N} : \text{there exists a derivation of } C \text{ from
 } \Gamma \text{ using exactly } n \text{ inference steps}\,\}.$$

By the assumption of derivability, $S$ is **non-empty**: there is at least one d
erivation, hence at least one $n \in S$.

Now $S \subseteq \mathbb{N}$, and $\mathbb{N}$ is **well-ordered**: every non-em
pty subset of $\mathbb{N}$ has a least element. Therefore $S$ has a minimum elem
ent, call it $n^* = \min S$.

By definition of $S$, there exists a derivation $D^*$ of $C$ from $\Gamma$ using
 exactly $n^*$ inference steps. Since $n^*$ is the minimum of $S$, no derivation
 of $C$ from $\Gamma$ uses fewer than $n^*$ steps. Hence $D^*$ is a **shortest**
 derivation: a derivation in which the number of steps is minimized. $\blacksqua
re$

**Remark on why finiteness is essential.** The argument rests on two facts: (1)
each individual derivation is finite (so its step count is a well-defined natura
l number), and (2) the natural numbers are well-ordered (so any non-empty set of
 candidate step-counts has a minimum). Neither fact requires the *set of all der
ivations* to be finite — it may well be infinite, since one can always pad a der
ivation with redundant steps. Well-ordering guarantees a minimum regardless. The
 conclusion fails only for *underivable* sequents, where $S = \emptyset$ and no
derivation exists at all.
