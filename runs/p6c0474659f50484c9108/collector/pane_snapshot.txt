## Solution

**Setting up the recurrence.** We have $a_1 = 1$, $a_2 = 0$, $S_k = \sum_{i=1}^k
 a_i$, and for $n \geq 3$:
$$a_n = S_{n-2}\,(S_{n-1}+1).$$

**Computing initial values.**

| $n$ | $a_n$ | $S_n$ |
|-----|-------|-------|
| 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 0 | 1 |
| 3 | $1\cdot(1+1)=2$ | 3 |
| 4 | $1\cdot(3+1)=4$ | 7 |
| 5 | $3\cdot(7+1)=24$ | 31 |
| 6 | $7\cdot(31+1)=224$ | 255 |
| 7 | $31\cdot(255+1)=7936$ | 8191 |

**Identifying the pattern.** The partial sums are $S_n = 1, 1, 3, 7, 31, 255, 81
91, \ldots$ which match $2^{F_n}-1$ where $F_n$ is the Fibonacci sequence ($F_1=
F_2=1$, $F_{n}=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}$):

$$S_1=2^1-1,\; S_2=2^1-1,\; S_3=2^2-1,\; S_4=2^3-1,\; S_5=2^5-1,\; S_6=2^8-1,\;
S_7=2^{13}-1.$$

**Proof by induction.** Suppose $S_k = 2^{F_k}-1$ for all $k < n$. Then:
$$a_n = S_{n-2}(S_{n-1}+1) = (2^{F_{n-2}}-1)\cdot 2^{F_{n-1}},$$
$$S_n = S_{n-1}+a_n = (2^{F_{n-1}}-1) + 2^{F_{n-1}}(2^{F_{n-2}}-1) = 2^{F_{n-1}+
F_{n-2}}-1 = 2^{F_n}-1. \quad\checkmark$$

**Divisibility condition.** Since $a_n = (2^{F_{n-2}}-1)\cdot 2^{F_{n-1}}$ and $
\gcd(127, 2^{F_{n-1}})=1$, we need:
$$127 \mid (2^{F_{n-2}}-1) \iff 2^{F_{n-2}} \equiv 1 \pmod{127}.$$

**Order of 2 modulo 127.** Since $127 = 2^7 - 1$ is a Mersenne prime, $2^7 \equi
v 1 \pmod{127}$. As 7 is prime and $2^1 \not\equiv 1$, the multiplicative order
of 2 mod 127 is exactly **7**. Therefore:
$$2^k \equiv 1 \pmod{127} \iff 7 \mid k.$$

**Finding the first Fibonacci number divisible by 7.** We need $7 \mid F_{n-2}$.
 Computing $F_k \pmod{7}$:

| $k$ | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|-----|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $F_k$ | 1 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 5 | 8 | 13 | 21 |
| $F_k \bmod 7$ | 1 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 5 | 1 | 6 | **0** |

The first $k$ with $7 \mid F_k$ is $k = 8$ (since $F_8 = 21 = 3\times 7$).

**Conclusion.** Setting $n-2 = 8$ gives $n = 10$. Checking all $m \in \{3,4,\ldo
ts,9\}$: the corresponding $F_{m-2} \in \{1,1,2,3,5,8,13\}$ are all not divisibl
e by 7, confirming none of $a_3, \ldots, a_9$ is divisible by 127.

**Verification:** $a_{10} = (2^{21}-1)\cdot 2^{34}$. Since $7 \mid 21$, we have
$127 \mid (2^{21}-1) = 2097151$ (indeed $127 \times 16513 = 2097151$), so $127 \
mid a_{10}$.

$$\boxed{m = 10}$$
