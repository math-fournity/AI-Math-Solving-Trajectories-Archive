## Proof

**Setup.** For each permutation $(p,q,r)$ of $(a,b,c)$, the quadratic $px^2+qx+r
=0$ has discriminant $q^2 - 4pr$. The six permutations group into three pairs ac
cording to which element occupies the middle position $q$:

| Pair | Permutations | Shared discriminant |
|------|-------------|-------------------|
| $q=a$ | $(b,a,c),\;(c,a,b)$ | $D_a = a^2-4bc$ |
| $q=b$ | $(a,b,c),\;(c,b,a)$ | $D_b = b^2-4ac$ |
| $q=c$ | $(a,c,b),\;(b,c,a)$ | $D_c = c^2-4ab$ |

A pair contributes rational roots only when its discriminant is a non-negative p
erfect square. Each permutation in a contributing pair yields at most 2 rational
 roots, so each pair contributes **at most 4**.

**Upper bound: $|S|\le 8$.**

WLOG assume $a \le b \le c$. Since $a,b,c$ are positive integers with $a\le b$ a
nd $a\le c$, we have $a^2 \le bc < 4bc$, hence
$$D_a = a^2 - 4bc < 0.$$
So the $q=a$ pair contributes **no real roots**. At most the remaining two pairs
 ($q=b$ and $q=c$) contribute, each giving at most 4 roots. Therefore $|S(a,b,c)
| \le 8$.

**Construction achieving 8.** Take $(a,b,c)=(2,27,55)$.

- $D_b = 27^2 - 4\cdot2\cdot55 = 729-440 = 289 = 17^2$. ✓
- $D_c = 55^2 - 4\cdot2\cdot27 = 3025-216 = 2809 = 53^2$. ✓
- $D_a = 4 - 4\cdot27\cdot55 < 0$. (No contribution.)

**$q=b$ pair** ($s=\sqrt{D_b}=17$):

- $(2,27,55)$: $2x^2+27x+55=0$, roots $\frac{-27\pm17}{4} = -\tfrac{5}{2},\;-11$
.
- $(55,27,2)$: $55x^2+27x+2=0$, roots $\frac{-27\pm17}{110} = -\tfrac{1}{11},\;-
\tfrac{2}{5}$.

**$q=c$ pair** ($s=\sqrt{D_c}=53$):

- $(2,55,27)$: $2x^2+55x+27=0$, roots $\frac{-55\pm53}{4} = -\tfrac{1}{2},\;-27$
.
- $(27,55,2)$: $27x^2+55x+2=0$, roots $\frac{-55\pm53}{54} = -\tfrac{1}{27},\;-2
$.

Collecting all roots:
$$S(2,27,55)=\left\{-\tfrac{5}{2},\;-11,\;-\tfrac{1}{11},\;-\tfrac{2}{5},\;-\tfr
ac{1}{2},\;-27,\;-\tfrac{1}{27},\;-2\right\}.$$

These **8 values are all distinct**, so $|S(2,27,55)|=8$.

**Verification that no coincidences occur.** Within each pair, the four roots ar
e $\frac{-q\pm s}{2p_1}$ and $\frac{-q\pm s}{2p_2}$ (where $p_1,p_2$ are the two
 non-$q$ elements). A same-sign coincidence requires $p_1=p_2$ (impossible) or $
-q\pm s=0$ (impossible for positive integers). A cross-sign coincidence $\frac{-
q+s}{2p_1}=\frac{-q-s}{2p_2}$ forces $s=\frac{q|p_2-p_1|}{p_1+p_2}$, which combi
ned with $s^2=q^2-4p_1p_2$ yields $q=p_1+p_2$. For our example: $27\ne 2+55$ and
 $55\ne 2+27$, so no within-pair coincidences. Cross-pair coincidences are ruled
 out by direct inspection of the 8 distinct values listed above.

**Conclusion.** The upper bound is $8$ and it is achieved, so the maximum number
 of elements in $S(a,b,c)$ is $\boxed{8}$.
