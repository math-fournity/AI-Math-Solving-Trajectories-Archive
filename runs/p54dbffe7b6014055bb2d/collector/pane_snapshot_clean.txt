## Solution

**Step 1: Rewrite as an Euler product.**

The primorial $n\# = \prod_{p \le n} p$. Every divisor $d \mid n\#$ is squarefre
e and of the form $d = \prod_{p \in S} p$ for some subset $S$ of primes $\le n$.
 For such $d$, $\omega(d) = |S|$, so $(-1)^{\omega(d)} 2^{\omega(d)} = (-2)^{\om
ega(d)}$. Therefore

$$g(n) = \sum_{\substack{d \mid n\# \\ d \text{ sqfree}}} \frac{(-2)^{\omega(d)}
}{d} = \prod_{p \le n} \left(1 + \frac{(-2)}{p}\right) = \prod_{p \le n}\left(1
- \frac{2}{p}\right).$$

**Step 2: The factor at $p=2$.**

The factor for $p=2$ is $1 - 2/2 = 0$, so **as literally stated, $g(n) = 0$ for
all $n \ge 2$**. The nontrivial content of the problem is the product over **odd
** primes, which is the standard sieve-theoretic object (arising in the Hardy–Li
ttlewood twin prime heuristic). We therefore analyze

$$G(n) := \prod_{2 < p \le n}\left(1 - \frac{2}{p}\right),$$

which is the meaningful asymptotic target.

**Step 3: Relate to Mertens' product.**

Write $1 - 2/p = (1-1/p)^2 \cdot \frac{p(p-2)}{(p-1)^2}$. Hence

$$G(n) = \left[\prod_{2<p\le n}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\right]^2 \cdot \prod_{
2<p\le n}\frac{p(p-2)}{(p-1)^2}.$$

By **Mertens' theorem**, $\prod_{p \le n}(1-1/p) \sim \frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\log n}
$, so

$$\prod_{2<p\le n}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right) = \frac{\prod_{p\le n}(1-1/p)}{1-1/
2} = 2\prod_{p\le n}(1-1/p) \sim \frac{2e^{-\gamma}}{\log n}.$$

**Step 4: The convergent product.**

Since $\frac{p(p-2)}{(p-1)^2} = 1 - \frac{1}{(p-1)^2} = 1 + O(1/p^2)$, the infin
ite product converges absolutely:

$$C_2 := \prod_{p>2}\frac{p(p-2)}{(p-1)^2} \approx 0.66016\ldots$$

This is the **twin prime constant**. The tail $\prod_{p>n} \frac{p(p-2)}{(p-1)^2
} = 1 + O(1/n)$, so the finite product tends to $C_2$.

**Step 5: Combine.**

$$G(n) = \left[\frac{2e^{-\gamma}}{\log n}(1+o(1))\right]^2 \cdot C_2(1+o(1)) =
\frac{4\,C_2\,e^{-2\gamma}}{(\log n)^2}\,(1+o(1)).$$

**Verification via logarithmic summation.** Taking logs directly:

$$\log G(n) = \sum_{2<p\le n}\log\!\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right) = -2\sum_{2<p\le n
}\frac{1}{p} + \sum_{2<p\le n}\!\left[\log\!\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right)+\frac{2}{
p}\right].$$

Using $\sum_{p\le n}1/p = \log\log n + B_1 + o(1)$ (Meissel–Mertens constant $B_
1$), the first sum is $\log\log n + B_1 - \tfrac12 + o(1)$. The bracketed term i
s $O(1/p^2)$, so the second sum converges to a constant $C_0$. Thus

$$\log G(n) = -2\log\log n - 2B_1 + 1 + C_0 + o(1),$$

giving $G(n) \sim e^{1+C_0-2B_1}/(\log n)^2$. One verifies $e^{1+C_0-2B_1} = 4C_
2 e^{-2\gamma}$, confirming consistency.

**Numerical value of the constant:**

$$4\,C_2\,e^{-2\gamma} \approx 4 \times 0.66016 \times 0.31523 \approx 0.8325.$$

## Result

As literally stated, $g(n) = 0$ for all $n \ge 2$ (the $p=2$ factor vanishes). T
he nontrivial asymptotic — for the product over odd primes, which is the standar
d sieve-theoretic interpretation — is:

$$\boxed{\prod_{2<p\le n}\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right) \;\sim\; \frac{4\,C_2\,e^{-2
\gamma}}{(\log n)^2}}$$

where $C_2 = \prod_{p>2}\frac{p(p-2)}{(p-1)^2} \approx 0.66016$ is the twin prim
e constant and $\gamma \approx 0.5772$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant. Equival
ently, $g(n) \asymp (\log n)^{-2}$ (up to the vanishing $p=2$ factor), with the
precise leading constant $4C_2 e^{-2\gamma} \approx 0.8325$.
