## Proof

### Step 1: $|\cdot|$ is a non-archimedean absolute value on $R$

**(i) Positive definiteness.** $|f| = 0 \iff e^{-v(f)} = 0 \iff v(f) = +\infty \
iff f = 0$. For $f \neq 0$, $v(f) \in \mathbb{Z}$, so $|f| = e^{-v(f)} > 0$.

**(ii) Multiplicativity.** For $f = \sum_{k=n}^{m} a_k t^k$, $g = \sum_{\ell=p}^
{q} b_\ell t^\ell$ with $a_n, b_p \neq 0$, the product $fg = \sum_{j=n+p}^{m+q}
c_j t^j$ has lowest-degree term $c_{n+p} = a_n b_p$. Since $A$ is a field (hence
 an integral domain), $a_n b_p \neq 0$, so $v(fg) = n + p = v(f) + v(g)$, giving
 $|fg| = |f|\,|g|$.

**(iii) Ultrametric inequality.** $v(f+g) \geq \min(v(f), v(g))$ (the lowest non
zero term of $f+g$ cannot be lower than both lowest terms), so $|f+g| = e^{-v(f+
g)} \leq e^{-\min(v(f),v(g))} = \max(|f|,|g|) \leq |f|+|g|$.

### Step 4: $R$ is dense in $L$

### Step 5: The natural isomorphism $\widehat{R} \cong L$
