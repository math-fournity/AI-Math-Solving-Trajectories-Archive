**Problem.** Let $B_t$ be standard Brownian motion. Compute the distribution of
$L = \sup\{t \le 1 : B_t = 0\}$.

---

## Proof

Define $g(t) = \sup\{s \le t : B_s = 0\}$, the last zero of Brownian motion befo
re time $t$. We seek the distribution of $L = g(1)$.

**Step 1: Reduce to a hitting-time probability.**

The event $\{g(1) \le t\}$ is the event that $B$ has no zero in $(t, 1]$. Since
$P(B_t = 0) = 0$, we may write
$$
P(g(1) \le t) = P\bigl(B_s \ne 0 \text{ for all } s \in (t,1]\bigr).
$$
By the Markov property at the deterministic time $t$, conditioning on $B_t$:
$$
P(g(1) \le t) = \mathbb{E}\Bigl[\,P_{B_t}\bigl(\tau_0 > 1-t\bigr)\Bigr],
$$
where $\tau_0 = \inf\{s \ge 0 : B_s = 0\}$ and $P_x$ denotes Brownian motion sta
rted at $x$.

**Step 2: Hitting-time probability via the reflection principle.**

For $x \ne 0$, by symmetry it suffices to take $x > 0$. Then
$$
P_x(\tau_0 > u) = P\Bigl(\min_{0 \le s \le u}(x + B_s) > 0\Bigr) = 1 - P\Bigl(\m
in_{0 \le s \le u} B_s \le -x\Bigr).
$$
By the reflection principle, $P(\min_{0\le s\le u} B_s \le -x) = 2P(B_u \ge x) =
 2\bigl(1 - \Phi(x/\sqrt{u})\bigr)$. Hence
$$
P_x(\tau_0 > u) = 2\,\Phi\!\Bigl(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{u}}\Bigr) - 1.
$$

**Step 3: Average over $B_t$.**

Setting $u = 1-t$ and $x = B_t \sim N(0,t)$:
$$
P(g(1) \le t) = \mathbb{E}\Bigl[2\,\Phi\!\Bigl(\frac{|B_t|}{\sqrt{1-t}}\Bigr) -
1\Bigr] = 2\,\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Phi\!\Bigl(a\,|Z|\Bigr)\Bigr] - 1,
$$
where $Z \sim N(0,1)$ and $a = \sqrt{t/(1-t)}$.

**Step 4: Evaluate $\mathbb{E}[\Phi(a|Z|)]$.**

Let $U = |Z|$ with density $\sqrt{2/\pi}\,e^{-u^2/2}$ on $[0,\infty)$. Define
$$
F(a) = \mathbb{E}[\Phi(aU)] = \int_0^\infty \Phi(au)\,\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,e^{-
u^2/2}\,du.
$$
Differentiating under the integral:
$$
F'(a) = \int_0^\infty u\,\phi(au)\,\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,e^{-u^2/2}\,du = \frac{
1}{\pi}\int_0^\infty u\,e^{-(1+a^2)u^2/2}\,du = \frac{1}{\pi(1+a^2)}.
$$
Integrating and using $F(0) = \mathbb{E}[\Phi(0)] = \tfrac{1}{2}$:
$$
F(a) = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{\pi}\arctan(a).
$$

**Step 5: Conclude.**

$$
P(g(1) \le t) = 2\Bigl(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{\pi}\arctan\!\sqrt{\frac{t}{1-t}}\
Bigr) - 1 = \frac{2}{\pi}\arctan\!\sqrt{\frac{t}{1-t}}.
$$
Using the identity $\arctan\!\sqrt{t/(1-t)} = \arcsin(\sqrt{t})$ (which follows
from $\sin\theta = \tan\theta/\sqrt{1+\tan^2\theta}$ with $\theta = \arctan\sqrt
{t/(1-t)}$), we obtain

$$
\boxed{\,P(L \le t) = \frac{2}{\pi}\arcsin(\sqrt{t}\,), \qquad 0 \le t \le 1.\,}
$$

This is the **arcsine distribution** on $[0,1]$. Differentiating gives the densi
ty
$$
f_L(t) = \frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{t(1-t)}}, \qquad 0 < t < 1,
$$
which is the classical **Lévy arcsine law** for the last zero of Brownian motion
.
