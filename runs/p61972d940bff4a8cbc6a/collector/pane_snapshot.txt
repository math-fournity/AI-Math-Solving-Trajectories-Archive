**Proof.**

 $\mathbb{C}$). The key observation is that $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ is itself an

**Step 1: The group algebra $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}[G]$ splits completely.**

Since $\operatorname{char}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}) = 0$ and $|G| < \infty$, Masch
ke's theorem gives that $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}[G]$ is semisimple. By the Artin–W
edderburn theorem:
$$\overline{\mathbb{Q}}[G] \cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^{r} M_{d_i}(\overline{\mathbb{Q
}})$$
for some integers $d_i$, where $r$ equals the number of conjugacy classes of $G$
. Here each simple component is a full matrix algebra over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}
}$ (not over a nontrivial division algebra), because any finite-dimensional divi
sion algebra over an algebraically closed field $F$ must equal $F$ itself: if $D
$ is such a division algebra and $x \in D$, then $F(x)$ is a finite algebraic ex
tension of $F$ inside $D$, so $x \in F$ since $F$ is algebraically closed.

**Step 2: Irreducible $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$-representations give all irreducib

erline{\mathbb{Q}}^{d_i}$ (column vectors, with $M_{d_i}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$
 acting by left multiplication). These are representations $G \to \mathrm{GL}_{d
_i}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$ with all matrix entries in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$.

Now observe that
$$\mathbb{C} \otimes_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}} \overline{\mathbb{Q}}[G] \cong \mat
hbb{C} \otimes_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}} (\overline{\mathbb{Q}} \otimes_{\mathbb{Q

$$\mathbb{C}[G] \cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^{r} M_{d_i}(\mathbb{C}).$$
The simple $\mathbb{C}[G]$-modules are $\mathbb{C}^{d_i} = \mathbb{C} \otimes_{\
overline{\mathbb{Q}}} \overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{d_i}$, obtained from the simple $\o

educible $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$-representations ($= r$) equals the number of ir
reducible $\mathbb{C}$-representations (also $r$, the number of conjugacy classe

Since each entry is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$ and only finitely many are adjoi
ned, $K_i$ is a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}$, i.e., a **number field**. By c
onstruction, $\rho_i$ is a representation $G \to \mathrm{GL}_{d_i}(K_i)$ with en
tries in $K_i$.

**Conclusion.**

Every irreducible complex representation $V$ of $G$ is isomorphic (over $\mathbb
{C}$) to $\mathbb{C} \otimes_{K_i} \rho_i$ for some representation $\rho_i$ defi
ned over a number field $K_i$. In other words, $V$ is isomorphic over $\mathbb{C

Therefore, **yes**: every irreducible representation of $G$ over $\mathbb{C}$ is
