# Solution

**Answer: No.** Such a countable family does not exist.

## Proof

We show that for any countable family $(f_k)_{k \geq 1}$ of bounded continuous f
unctions on $\mathbb{R}^d$, one can construct a sequence of Borel probability me
asures $(\mu_n)$ such that $\lim_{n\to\infty} \int f_k\,d\mu_n$ exists for every
 $k$, yet $(\mu_n)$ does not converge weakly to any probability measure.

### Construction via diagonal subsequence

Let $(f_k)_{k \geq 1}$ be an arbitrary countable family of bounded continuous re
al-valued functions on $\mathbb{R}^d$. We work in $\mathbb{R}^d$ with $d \geq 1$
.

**Step 1 (Initial sequence).** Set $x^{(0)}_n = (n, 0, \dots, 0) \in \mathbb{R}^
d$, so $\|x^{(0)}_n\| \to \infty$.

**Step 2 (Iterative subsequence extraction).** Since each $f_k$ is bounded, the
sequence $\bigl(f_1(x^{(0)}_n)\bigr)_{n \geq 1}$ is bounded in $\mathbb{R}$, hen
ce admits a convergent subsequence (Bolzano–Weierstrass). Let $x^{(1)} = (x^{(1)
}_1, x^{(1)}_2, \dots)$ be the corresponding subsequence of $x^{(0)}$, so that $
f_1(x^{(1)}_n) \to L_1$ for some $L_1 \in \mathbb{R}$.

Inductively, given $x^{(k-1)}$, the sequence $\bigl(f_k(x^{(k-1)}_n)\bigr)$ is b
ounded, so we extract a subsequence $x^{(k)}$ of $x^{(k-1)}$ such that $f_k(x^{(
k)}_n) \to L_k$ for some $L_k \in \mathbb{R}$.

**Step 3 (Diagonal sequence).** Define $y_n = x^{(n)}_n$, the $n$-th element of
the $n$-th subsequence. We claim:

1. **$\|y_n\| \to \infty$:** Since each $x^{(k)}$ is a subsequence of $x^{(0)}$,
 every $y_n$ is some element of $x^{(0)}$, and the indices in $x^{(0)}$ are stri
ctly increasing (as we keep taking subsequences and moving along the diagonal),
so $\|y_n\| \to \infty$.

2. **$f_k(y_n) \to L_k$ for every fixed $k$:** For $n \geq k$, the sequence $x^{
(n)}$ is a subsequence of $x^{(k)}$ (by transitivity of the subsequence relation
). Therefore $y_n = x^{(n)}_n$ is an element of $x^{(k)}$ at some index $m_n \ge
q n$, and $(y_n)_{n \geq k}$ is a subsequence of $x^{(k)}$. Since $f_k(x^{(k)}_j
) \to L_k$, we get $f_k(y_n) \to L_k$.

### The counterexample

Define $\mu_n = \delta_{y_n}$, the Dirac mass at $y_n$. Then:

- **All limits exist:** $\int f_k\,d\mu_n = f_k(y_n) \to L_k \in \mathbb{R}$ for
 every $k$.

- **$(\mu_n)$ is not tight:** Since $\|y_n\| \to \infty$, for any compact $K \su
bset \mathbb{R}^d$ (which is bounded), $\mu_n(K) = 0$ for all sufficiently large
 $n$. Hence $\limsup_{n\to\infty} \mu_n(K) = 0 < 1$, so the sequence is not tigh
t.

- **No weak limit to a probability measure:** By Prokhorov's theorem, if a seque
nce of probability measures on $\mathbb{R}^d$ converges weakly to a probability
measure, it must be tight. Since $(\mu_n)$ is not tight, it cannot converge weak
ly to any Borel probability measure. (Indeed, every subsequential weak limit wou
ld have to put mass 0 on every compact set, which is impossible for a probabilit
y measure on $\mathbb{R}^d$.)

### Conclusion

For any countable family $(f_k)$ of bounded continuous functions, we have produc
ed a sequence $(\mu_n)$ of probability measures for which $\lim_n \int f_k\,d\mu
_n$ exists for every $k$, but $(\mu_n)$ does not converge weakly to any probabil
ity measure. Therefore, no countable family with the stated property exists. $\b
lacksquare$
