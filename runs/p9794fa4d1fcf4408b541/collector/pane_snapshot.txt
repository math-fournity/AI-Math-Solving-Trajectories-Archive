## Solution

Decompose the Brownian motion into independent increments. Define:

- $X_1 = W(1)$, with $X_1 \sim N(0, 1)$
- $X_2 = W(2) - W(1)$, with $X_2 \sim N(0, 1)$
- $X_3 = W(4) - W(2)$, with $X_3 \sim N(0, 2)$

By the independent increments property of Brownian motion, $X_1, X_2, X_3$ are m
utually independent.

The conditioning $\sigma$-algebra $\sigma(W(1), W(2))$ is the same as $\sigma(X_
1, X_2)$, since $W(1) = X_1$ and $W(2) = X_1 + X_2$ is an invertible linear tran
sformation. Therefore $X_3$ is independent of the conditioning information.

Now write $W(4)$ in terms of the conditioning variables and the independent rema
inder:

$$W(4) = W(2) + X_3.$$

Squaring:

$$W^2(4) = W^2(2) + 2\,W(2)\,X_3 + X_3^2.$$

Taking conditional expectation and using that $W(2)$ is measurable with respect
to $\sigma(W(1),W(2))$ while $X_3$ is independent of it:

$$\mathbb{E}[W^2(4) \mid W(1), W(2)] = W^2(2) + 2\,W(2)\,\mathbb{E}[X_3] + \math
bb{E}[X_3^2].$$

Since $X_3 \sim N(0, 2)$, we have $\mathbb{E}[X_3] = 0$ and $\mathbb{E}[X_3^2] =
 \operatorname{Var}(X_3) = 2$. Hence

$$\mathbb{E}[W^2(4) \mid W(1), W(2)] = W^2(2) + 2.$$

**Answer:**

$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}[W^{2}(4) \mid W(1), W(2)] = W^{2}(2) + 2.}$$
