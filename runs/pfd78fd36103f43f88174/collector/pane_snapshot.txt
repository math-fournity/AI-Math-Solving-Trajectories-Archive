## Proof

**Setup.** For $x \in \mathbb{R}$, the tangent line $\ell_x$ to $y = P(x)$ at $(
x, P(x))$ has equation
$$Y = P(x) + P'(x)(X - x).$$
A point $(a, b) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ lies on $\ell_x$ if and only if
$$b = P(x) + P'(x)(a - x).$$

Fix $a \in \mathbb{R}$ and define
$$f_a(x) := P(x) + (a - x)\,P'(x) = P(x) - x\,P'(x) + a\,P'(x).$$
Then $(a, b) \in \bigcup_{x \in \mathbb{R}} \ell_x$ if and only if $b \in f_a(\m
athbb{R})$. Therefore
$$\bigcup_{x \in \mathbb{R}} \ell_x = \mathbb{R}^2 \iff \forall\, a \in \mathbb{
R},\ f_a(\mathbb{R}) = \mathbb{R},$$
i.e., the union equals $\mathbb{R}^2$ exactly when $f_a$ is surjective for every
 $a$.

**Degree of $f_a$.** Let $n = \deg P \geq 2$ and write $P(x) = c_n x^n + \text{(
lower terms)}$, $c_n \neq 0$. Then $P'(x) = n\,c_n\,x^{n-1} + \cdots$, so
$$x\,P'(x) = n\,c_n\,x^n + \text{(lower terms)}, \qquad a\,P'(x) = \text{degree
} \leq n-1.$$
Hence
$$f_a(x) = c_n x^n - n\,c_n\,x^n + \text{(lower terms)} = (1 - n)\,c_n\,x^n + \t
ext{(lower terms)}.$$
Since $n \geq 2$, we have $1 - n \neq 0$, so $\deg f_a = n$ with leading coeffic
ient $(1-n)\,c_n \neq 0$. Crucially, **$\deg f_a = \deg P$ for every $a$**, and
the leading coefficient $(1-n)c_n$ is independent of $a$.

---

### (a) $\deg P$ is odd

Here $n \geq 3$ is odd. For every $a \in \mathbb{R}$, $f_a$ is a polynomial of *
*odd** degree $n$ with nonzero leading coefficient $(1-n)c_n$. Any real polynomi
al of odd degree is surjective onto $\mathbb{R}$ (its limits at $+\infty$ and $-
\infty$ have opposite signs). Therefore $f_a(\mathbb{R}) = \mathbb{R}$ for every
 $a$, which gives
$$\bigcup_{x \in \mathbb{R}} \ell_x = \mathbb{R}^2. \qquad \blacksquare$$

---

### (b) Even degree

Here $n \geq 2$ is even. For every $a \in \mathbb{R}$, $f_a$ is a polynomial of
**even** degree $n$ with nonzero leading coefficient $(1-n)c_n$. Since $n \geq 2
$, we have $1 - n < 0$, so:

- If $c_n > 0$: leading coefficient $(1-n)c_n < 0$, so $f_a(x) \to -\infty$ as $
x \to \pm\infty$; $f_a$ is **bounded above**.
- If $c_n < 0$: leading coefficient $(1-n)c_n > 0$, so $f_a(x) \to +\infty$ as $
x \to \pm\infty$; $f_a$ is **bounded below**.

In either case $f_a(\mathbb{R})$ is a proper subset of $\mathbb{R}$ (a half-line
 $(-\infty, M_a]$ or $[m_a, +\infty)$). Consequently, for **every** $a$ there ex
ists some $b \in \mathbb{R}$ with $b \notin f_a(\mathbb{R})$, i.e., $(a, b) \not
in \bigcup_x \ell_x$.

Thus no even-degree polynomial can satisfy $\bigcup_x \ell_x = \mathbb{R}^2$.

**Answer to (b): No**, there does not exist a polynomial of even degree for whic
h the equality holds. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** The key observation is that the "envelope coordinate" $f_a(x) = P(x)
 + (a-x)P'(x)$ is itself a polynomial of the same degree $n$ as $P$ (with leadin
g coefficient $(1-n)c_n \neq 0$ for $n \geq 2$), so surjectivity of $f_a$ for al
l $a$ is equivalent to $n$ being odd.
