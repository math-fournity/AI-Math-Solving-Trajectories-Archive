## Proof

**Step 1: Change of variables.** Let $t = e^y$, so $dt = e^y\,dy$ and $\log t =
y$. The integral becomes

$$f(x) = \int_1^\infty \exp\!\Big(y - \frac{x\,e^y}{y}\Big)\,dy = \int_1^\infty
e^{\phi(y)}\,dy, \qquad \phi(y) = y - \frac{x\,e^y}{y}.$$

**Step 2: Locate the critical point.** Setting $\phi'(y) = 0$:

$$\phi'(y) = 1 - x\,\frac{e^y(y-1)}{y^2} = 0 \implies x = \frac{y^2}{e^y(y-1)}.$
$

Let $y^*$ be the unique solution. As $x \to 0^+$, $y^* \to \infty$. From the equ
ation, $x\,e^{y^*} = \frac{(y^*)^2}{y^*-1}$, so $\frac{x\,e^{y^*}}{y^*} = \frac{
y^*}{y^*-1} \to 1$. Taking logarithms: $\log x + y^* - \log y^* = \log\!\big(\fr
ac{y^*}{y^*-1}\big) \to 0$, giving

$$y^* = \log(1/x) + \log y^* + o(1).$$

Denoting $L = \log(1/x)$, we get $y^* = L + \log L + o(\log L)$, so **$y^* \sim
L$**.

**Step 3: Laplace approximation.** Compute the second derivative at $y^*$:

$$\phi''(y) = -x\,\frac{d}{dy}\!\left[\frac{e^y(y-1)}{y^2}\right] = -x\,e^y\,\fr
ac{y^2 - 2y + 2}{y^3}.$$

At $y = y^*$, using $x\,e^{y^*} = \frac{(y^*)^2}{y^*-1}$:

$$|\phi''(y^*)| = \frac{(y^*)^2}{y^*-1}\cdot\frac{(y^*)^2 - 2y^* + 2}{(y^*)^3} =
 \frac{(y^*)^2 - 2y^* + 2}{y^*(y^*-1)} \to 1 \quad\text{as } y^*\to\infty.$$

The value at the peak: $\phi(y^*) = y^* - \frac{x\,e^{y^*}}{y^*} = y^* - \frac{y
^*}{y^*-1} = y^* - 1 + o(1)$.

By Laplace's method (the integrand is exponentially concentrated near $y^*$):

$$f(x) \sim e^{\phi(y^*)}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{|\phi''(y^*)|}} \sim \sqrt{2\pi}\;e^{
y^*-1}.$$

**Step 4: Express in terms of $x$.** From $e^{y^*} = \frac{(y^*)^2}{x(y^*-1)} \s
im \frac{y^*}{x}$:

$$f(x) \sim \sqrt{2\pi}\cdot\frac{y^*}{e\,x} \sim \frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{e}\cdot\fra
c{L + \log L}{x} = \frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{e}\cdot\frac{\log(1/x) + \log\log(1/x)}{x}
.$$

**Step 5: Extract the power.** Since $\log\log(1/x) = o(\log(1/x))$, the leading
 asymptotic behavior is

$$f(x) \sim \frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{e}\cdot\frac{\log(1/x)}{x}.$$

**Sanity check (original variable):** The integrand $e^{-xt/\log t} \approx 1$ f
or $t \ll t^*$ and decays rapidly for $t \gg t^*$, where $t^*$ satisfies $x\,t^*
/\log t^* \approx 1$, i.e., $t^* \approx \log(1/x)/x$. The integral is dominated
 by the region $[e, t^*]$, giving $f(x) \approx t^* \sim \log(1/x)/x$, independe
ntly confirming the result.

## Conclusion

$$\boxed{p = 1}$$

The asymptotic growth rate is $f(x) \sim \dfrac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{e}\cdot\dfrac{\log(
1/x)}{x}$ as $x \to 0^+$, so $p = 1$.
