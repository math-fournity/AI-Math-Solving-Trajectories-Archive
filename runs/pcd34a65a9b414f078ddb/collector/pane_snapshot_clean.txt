## Proof

**Claim.** Let $A$ be a finitely generated ring (a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z
}$-algebra) and $\mathfrak{m} \subset A$ a maximal ideal. Then $A/\mathfrak{m}$
is a finite field.

**Proof.**

Write $A = \mathbb{Z}[x_1, \dots, x_n]/I$ for some ideal $I$. A maximal ideal $\
mathfrak{m}$ of $A$ lifts to a maximal ideal $\mathfrak{M} \subset \mathbb{Z}[x_
1,\dots,x_n]$ containing $I$, and $A/\mathfrak{m} \cong \mathbb{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_n
]/\mathfrak{M}$. So it suffices to show:

> **Key Lemma (Zariski's lemma for $\mathbb{Z}$).** If $\mathfrak{M}$ is a maxim
al ideal of $\mathbb{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$, then $\mathbb{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_n]/\mathfr
ak{M}$ is a finite field.

We prove the lemma by induction on $n$.

**Base case $n = 0$.** Here $\mathbb{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_n] = \mathbb{Z}$, and the ma
ximal ideals are $(p)$ for primes $p$. The quotient $\mathbb{Z}/(p) = \mathbb{F}
_p$ is finite. ✓

**Inductive step.** Assume the result for $n - 1$ variables; prove it for $n$. L
et $\mathfrak{M} \subset \mathbb{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ be maximal and set $K := \ma
thbb{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_n]/\mathfrak{M}$, a field. Let $\bar{x}_i$ denote the image
of $x_i$ in $K$, and let $\bar{\mathbb{Z}}$ be the image of $\mathbb{Z}$.

**Step 1. The image of $\mathbb{Z}$ in $K$ is a finite field, hence $K$ has posi
tive characteristic $p$.**

The kernel of $\mathbb{Z} \to K$ is a prime ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$, so it is eith
er $(0)$ or $(p)$ for a prime $p$.

- If the kernel is $(p)$: the image is $\mathbb{F}_p$, a finite field. ✓
- If the kernel is $(0)$: then $\mathbb{Z}$ injects into $K$, so $K$ has charact
eristic $0$ and contains $\mathbb{Q}$ (since $K$ is a field, every nonzero integ
er is invertible, giving an embedding $\mathbb{Q} \hookrightarrow K$). We will d
erive a contradiction in this case (Step 2 below), showing this cannot occur.

**Step 2. The characteristic-$0$ case is impossible (contradiction argument).**

Suppose for contradiction that $\mathbb{Q} \hookrightarrow K$, i.e., $\operatorn
ame{char} K = 0$. Then $K = \mathbb{Q}[\bar{x}_1, \dots, \bar{x}_n]$ is a field
that is finitely generated as a $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra. By **Zariski's lemma** (th
e classical form: a field that is finitely generated as an algebra over a field
is a finite algebraic extension), $K$ is a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}$, i.e
., a number field.

Now consider the composite map
$$\varphi: \mathbb{Z}[x_1, \dots, x_n] \twoheadrightarrow K.$$
Each $\bar{x}_i \in K$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$, so it satisfies a monic p
olynomial $f_i(t) \in \mathbb{Q}[t]$. Clearing denominators, there exist nonzero
 integers $d_i$ such that $d_i \bar{x}_i$ is integral over $\mathbb{Z}$, i.e., $
\bar{x}_i$ is integral over $\mathbb{Z}[1/d]$ where $d = d_1 d_2 \cdots d_n$.

Since $K = \mathbb{Z}[\bar{x}_1, \dots, \bar{x}_n]$ and each $\bar{x}_i$ is inte
gral over $\mathbb{Z}[1/d]$, the whole ring $K$ is integral over $R := \mathbb{Z
}[1/d]$. In particular, every element of $K$ is integral over $R$.

Now pick any prime $\ell$ not dividing $d$. The element $1/\ell \in \mathbb{Q} \
subset K$ must be integral over $R = \mathbb{Z}[1/d]$. Being integral over $R$ a
nd lying in $\mathbb{Q}$, it is integral over $\mathbb{Z}[1/d] \cap \mathbb{Q}$.
 But $\mathbb{Z}[1/d] \cap \mathbb{Q} = \mathbb{Z}[1/d]$ (as a subring of $\math
bb{Q}$). So $1/\ell$ is integral over $\mathbb{Z}[1/d]$.

This means $1/\ell$ satisfies a monic polynomial with coefficients in $\mathbb{Z
}[1/d]$:
$$t^m + a_1 t^{m-1} + \cdots + a_m = 0, \quad a_j \in \mathbb{Z}[1/d].$$
Multiplying through by a common denominator (a power of $d$) and substituting $t
 = 1/\ell$, we get an integrality relation of $1/\ell$ over $\mathbb{Z}$. But $1
/\ell$ is integral over $\mathbb{Z}$ only if $1/\ell \in \mathbb{Z}$ (since $\ma
thbb{Z}$ is integrally closed), which is false for $\ell > 1$. **Contradiction.*
*

Therefore $\operatorname{char} K \neq 0$, and the kernel of $\mathbb{Z} \to K$ i
s $(p)$ for some prime $p$.

**Step 3. $K$ is a finite field.**

We have established $\operatorname{char} K = p$ and the image of $\mathbb{Z}$ is
 $\mathbb{F}_p$. So $K$ is a field finitely generated as an $\mathbb{F}_p$-algeb
ra: $K = \mathbb{F}_p[\bar{x}_1, \dots, \bar{x}_n]$. By Zariski's lemma (classic
al form over the field $\mathbb{F}_p$), $K$ is a finite algebraic extension of $
\mathbb{F}_p$, hence a finite field. ✓

This completes the induction and proves the Key Lemma. Applying it to $A = \math
bb{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_n]/I$ with $\mathfrak{m}$ lifting to $\mathfrak{M}$, we conclu
de $A/\mathfrak{m} \cong \mathbb{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_n]/\mathfrak{M}$ is a finite fie
ld. $\blacksquare$

**Answer: Yes, $A/\mathfrak{m}$ is necessarily a finite field.**
