Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether
$$\left(\int f_1\,d\mu\right)^2+\cdots+\left(\int f_n\,d\mu\right)^2 \leq \left(
\int \sqrt{f_1^2+\cdots+f_n^2}\,d\mu\right)^2$$
holds for integrable $f_1,\dots,f_n$ on a (possibly non-$\sigma$-finite) measure
 space.

**Answer: Yes, the inequality holds.**

## Proof

Set $F = (f_1,\dots,f_n) : X \to \mathbb{R}^n$ and $\|F\| = \sqrt{f_1^2+\cdots+f
_n^2}$. The desired inequality is equivalent (taking square roots of non-negativ
e quantities) to:

$$\left\|\int F\,d\mu\right\| \leq \int \|F\|\,d\mu, \tag{$\star$}$$

where $\int F\,d\mu := \left(\int f_1\,d\mu,\dots,\int f_n\,d\mu\right) \in \mat
hbb{R}^n$ and $\|\cdot\|$ is the Euclidean norm.

**Integrability of $\|F\|$.** Since each $f_i$ is integrable, $|f_i|$ is integra
ble. By the triangle inequality in $\mathbb{R}^n$:
$$\|F\| = \sqrt{f_1^2+\cdots+f_n^2} \leq |f_1|+\cdots+|f_n|,$$
so $\|F\|$ is integrable by comparison. Thus the right-hand side of $(\star)$ is
 a finite non-negative real number.

**Proof of $(\star)$.** Let $v = \int F\,d\mu \in \mathbb{R}^n$. If $v = 0$, the
n $(\star)$ is immediate since the right side is $\geq 0$. Otherwise, set $u = v
/\|v\|$, a unit vector in $\mathbb{R}^n$. Using the linearity of the integral (w
hich requires no $\sigma$-finiteness):

$$\|v\| = u \cdot v = \sum_{i=1}^n u_i \int f_i\,d\mu = \int \left(\sum_{i=1}^n
u_i f_i\right) d\mu = \int (u \cdot F)\,d\mu.$$

The function $u \cdot F = \sum_i u_i f_i$ is integrable (linear combination of i
ntegrable functions). By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality in $\mathbb{R}^n$, applie
d pointwise:

$$u \cdot F(x) \leq \|u\|\,\|F(x)\| = \|F(x)\| \quad \text{for all } x \in X.$$

Since $u \cdot F \leq \|F\|$ pointwise and both functions are integrable, the **
monotonicity of the Lebesgue integral** (which holds for any measure space, $\si
gma$-finite or not) gives:

$$\int (u \cdot F)\,d\mu \leq \int \|F\|\,d\mu.$$

Combining:
$$\|v\| = \int (u \cdot F)\,d\mu \leq \int \|F\|\,d\mu.$$

Squaring both sides (both are non-negative) yields:

$$\left(\int f_1\,d\mu\right)^2 + \cdots + \left(\int f_n\,d\mu\right)^2 = \|v\|
^2 \leq \left(\int \sqrt{f_1^2+\cdots+f_n^2}\,d\mu\right)^2. \qquad \blacksquare
$$

**Remark on the hypotheses.** The proof uses only: (1) linearity of the integral
, (2) monotonicity of the integral, (3) the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality in $\mathb
b{R}^n$, and (4) integrability of $\|F\|$ (which follows from that of each $f_i$
). None of these require $\sigma$-finiteness, so the inequality is valid on **an
y** measure space. Equality holds if and only if $F(x)$ is, $\mu$-a.e., a non-ne
gative scalar multiple of a fixed unit vector.
