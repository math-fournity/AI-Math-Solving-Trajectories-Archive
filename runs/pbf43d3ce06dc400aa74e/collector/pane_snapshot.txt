**Proof.**

Since the supports of $\mu_n$ and $\mu$ are uniformly bounded, there exists a co
mpact set $K \subset \mathbb{C}^d$ (a closed ball) such that $\operatorname{supp
}(\mu_n) \subseteq K$ for all $n$ and $\operatorname{supp}(\mu) \subseteq K$.

Let $\mathcal{A}$ be the algebra of polynomials in $z_1,\ldots,z_d,\overline{z_1
},\ldots,\overline{z_d}$, restricted to $K$. We verify the hypotheses of the Sto
ne–Weierstrass theorem:

1. **$\mathcal{A}$ is a unital subalgebra of $C(K)$**: It is closed under additi
on, multiplication, and scalar multiplication, and contains the constant functio
n $1$.

2. **$\mathcal{A}$ separates points**: For any two distinct points $w \neq w'$ i
n $K \subseteq \mathbb{C}^d$, some coordinate $w_j \neq w'_j$. The polynomial $f
(z) = z_j$ satisfies $f(w) \neq f(w')$.

3. **$\mathcal{A}$ is self-adjoint (closed under complex conjugation)**: The con
jugate of a polynomial in $z_j$ and $\overline{z_j}$ is again a polynomial in $z
_j$ and $\overline{z_j}$ (since $\overline{z_j} = \overline{z_j}$ and $\overline
{\overline{z_j}} = z_j$).

By the Stone–Weierstrass theorem, $\mathcal{A}$ is dense in $C(K)$ with respect
to the uniform norm.

Now let $g \in C(K)$ be arbitrary. For every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists a p
olynomial $p \in \mathcal{A}$ with $\sup_{z \in K}|g(z) - p(z)| < \varepsilon$.
Since all measures are supported in $K$,

$$\left|\int g\,d\mu_n - \int g\,d\mu\right| \leq \left|\int (g-p)\,d\mu_n\right
| + \left|\int p\,d\mu_n - \int p\,d\mu\right| + \left|\int (p-g)\,d\mu\right|.$
$

The first and third terms are each $< \varepsilon$ (as $\mu_n,\mu$ are probabili
ty measures). The middle term $\to 0$ as $n\to\infty$ by hypothesis. Hence

$$\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|\int g\,d\mu_n - \int g\,d\mu\right| \leq 2\varepsil
on.$$

Since $\varepsilon > 0$ is arbitrary, $\int g\,d\mu_n \to \int g\,d\mu$ for ever

\mathbb{R}^{2d}$. This is precisely weak convergence $\mu_n \to \mu$. $\blacksqu
