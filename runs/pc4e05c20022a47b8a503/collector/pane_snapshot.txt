## Proof

**Claim.** Yes, there exists a unique bounded linear operator $S_1 \otimes S_2:
H_1 \otimes H_2 \to K_1 \otimes K_2$ satisfying $(S_1 \otimes S_2)(\xi_1 \otimes
 \xi_2) = (S_1 \xi_1)\otimes(S_2 \xi_2)$, and moreover $\|S_1 \otimes S_2\| = \|
S_1\|\,\|S_2\|$.

---

### Step 1. Definition on the algebraic tensor product

Let $H_1 \odot H_2$ denote the algebraic tensor product. Define a map $T_0: H_1
\odot H_2 \to K_1 \otimes K_2$ on elementary tensors by
$$T_0(\xi_1 \otimes \xi_2) = (S_1 \xi_1)\otimes(S_2 \xi_2),$$
and extend by linearity. To see that this is well-defined, note that for each fi
xed $\xi_2 \in H_2$, the map $\xi_1 \mapsto (S_1 \xi_1)\otimes(S_2 \xi_2)$ is li
near, and for each fixed $\xi_1$, the map $\xi_2 \mapsto (S_1 \xi_1)\otimes(S_2
\xi_2)$ is linear. By the universal property of the algebraic tensor product, $T
_0$ is a well-defined linear map.

### Step 2. Boundedness on the algebraic tensor product

Equip $H_1 \otimes H_2$ with the Hilbert-space inner product (the completion inn
er product). For an elementary tensor $\xi_1 \otimes \xi_2$,
$$\|T_0(\xi_1 \otimes \xi_2)\| = \|(S_1 \xi_1)\otimes(S_2 \xi_2)\| = \|S_1 \xi_1
\|\,\|S_2 \xi_2\| \le \|S_1\|\,\|S_2\|\,\|\xi_1\|\,\|\xi_2\| = \|S_1\|\,\|S_2\|\
,\|\xi_1 \otimes \xi_2\|.$$

For a general element $u = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \xi_i^{(1)} \otimes \xi_i^{(2)} \in H_
1 \odot H_2$, we must show $\|T_0 u\| \le \|S_1\|\,\|S_2\|\,\|u\|$.

Pick orthonormal bases $\{e_\alpha\}$ of $H_1$ and $\{f_\beta\}$ of $H_2$. Then
$\{e_\alpha \otimes f_\beta\}$ is an orthonormal basis of $H_1 \otimes H_2$, and
$$\|u\|^2 = \sum_{\alpha,\beta} \left|\sum_{i} \langle \xi_i^{(1)}, e_\alpha\ran

### Step 3. Clean argument via finite-rank approximation

Since $T_0$ is linear and the algebraic tensor product is dense in $H_1\otimes H
_2$, it suffices to show $\|T_0 u\| \le \|S_1\|\|S_2\|\|u\|$ for all $u$ in a de
nse subspace. We may assume $u$ lies in $E_1 \otimes E_2$ where $E_1 \subseteq H
_1$, $E_2 \subseteq H_2$ are finite-dimensional subspaces (such elements are den

On the finite-dimensional space $E_1 \otimes E_2$, choose orthonormal bases $\{e
_1,\dots,e_m\}$ of $E_1$ and $\{f_1,\dots,f_n\}$ of $E_2$. Write $u = \sum_{j,k}

$$T_0 u = \sum_{j,k} c_{jk}\, (S_1 e_j)\otimes(S_2 f_k).$$

Since the elementary tensors $(S_1 e_j)\otimes(S_2 f_k)$ are **orthogonal** in $

$$\|T_0 u\|^2 = \sum_{j,k}|c_{jk}|^2\,\|S_1 e_j\|^2\,\|S_2 f_k\|^2 \le \sum_{j,k

use the Gram matrix. Let $G^{(1)}_{jj'} = \langle S_1 e_j, S_1 e_{j'}\rangle = \

$$\|T_0 u\|^2 = c^*\,(G^{(1)}\otimes G^{(2)})\,c,$$

2\|^2\, I_{mn},$$
we conclude
$$\|T_0 u\|^2 \le \|S_1\|^2\|S_2\|^2\,\|c\|^2 = \|S_1\|^2\|S_2\|^2\,\|u\|^2.$$

Thus $\|T_0 u\| \le \|S_1\|\|S_2\|\|u\|$ for all $u$ in the dense subspace of fi

### Step 4. Extension to the completion

Since $T_0$ is bounded (with norm $\le \|S_1\|\|S_2\|$) on a dense subspace of $
H_1\otimes H_2$, it extends uniquely to a bounded linear operator $S_1\otimes S_
2: H_1\otimes H_2 \to K_1\otimes K_2$ with $\|S_1\otimes S_2\| \le \|S_1\|\|S_2\
|$. By construction, $(S_1\otimes S_2)(\xi_1\otimes\xi_2) = (S_1\xi_1)\otimes(S_
2\xi_2)$.

### Step 5. Norm equality

For the reverse inequality, take any unit vectors $\xi_1\in H_1$, $\xi_2\in H_2$
 with $\|S_1\xi_1\|$ and $\|S_2\xi_2\|$ approximating $\|S_1\|$ and $\|S_2\|$ re
spectively. Then $\xi_1\otimes\xi_2$ is a unit vector and
$$\|S_1\otimes S_2\| \ge \|(S_1\otimes S_2)(\xi_1\otimes\xi_2)\| = \|S_1\xi_1\|\
,\|S_2\xi_2\|.$$
Taking suprema gives $\|S_1\otimes S_2\| \ge \|S_1\|\|S_2\|$. Combined with Step
 4,
$$\boxed{\|S_1\otimes S_2\| = \|S_1\|\,\|S_2\|}.$$

### Conclusion

Such a bounded linear operator $S_1\otimes S_2$ exists (and is unique), defined
by continuous linear extension from the algebraic tensor product, with operator
norm $\|S_1\otimes S_2\| = \|S_1\|\,\|S_2\|$.
