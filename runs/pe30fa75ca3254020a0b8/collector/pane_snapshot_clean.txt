Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a semisimple Lie algebra ov
er $\mathbb{C}$ always satisfies $\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak{g}) = \operatornam
e{Der}(\mathfrak{g})$, i.e., whether every derivation is inner.

**Answer: The statement is TRUE.**

## Proof

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a semisimple Lie algebra over $\mathbb{C}$. We show that e
very derivation of $\mathfrak{g}$ is inner, i.e., $\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{
g}) = \operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak{g})$.

### Setup

Recall that a derivation $\delta: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$ satisfies $\del
ta([x,y]) = [\delta(x), y] + [x, \delta(y)]$. The inner derivations are those of
 the form $\operatorname{ad}_v$ for some $v \in \mathfrak{g}$, i.e., $\operatorn
ame{ad}_v(x) = [v, x]$.

Observe that a derivation is precisely a **1-cocycle** in the Chevalley–Eilenber
g complex $Z^1(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{ad}})$, where $\mathfrak{g}_{
\mathrm{ad}}$ denotes $\mathfrak{g}$ equipped with the adjoint representation. I
ndeed, the 1-cocycle condition for a linear map $\delta: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathf
rak{g}_{\mathrm{ad}}$ is:
$$\delta([x,y]) = x \cdot \delta(y) - y \cdot \delta(x) = [x, \delta(y)] - [y, \
delta(x)] = [x, \delta(y)] + [\delta(x), y],$$
which is exactly the derivation condition. A 1-coboundary is $\delta(x) = x \cdo
t v = [x, v] = -\operatorname{ad}_v(x)$ for some fixed $v$, which is an inner de
rivation. Thus:
$$H^1(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{ad}}) = \operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{g
}) / \operatorname{Inn}(\mathfrak{g}).$$

The statement $\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{g}) = \operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak{g}
)$ is equivalent to $H^1(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{ad}}) = 0$.

### Whitehead's First Lemma

**Lemma (Whitehead, 1936).** *Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a semisimple Lie algebra ove
r a field of characteristic $0$ and $V$ a finite-dimensional $\mathfrak{g}$-modu
le. Then $H^1(\mathfrak{g}, V) = 0$.*

**Proof of the Lemma.** Let $\delta: \mathfrak{g} \to V$ be a 1-cocycle, so $\de
lta([x,y]) = x\cdot\delta(y) - y\cdot\delta(x)$. We seek $v_0 \in V$ with $\delt
a(x) = x \cdot v_0$ for all $x$.

**Step 1 (Reduction to simple).** Write $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{g}_1 \oplus \c
dots \oplus \mathfrak{g}_k$ as a direct sum of simple ideals. The restriction $\
delta_i = \delta|_{\mathfrak{g}_i}$ is a cocycle for $\mathfrak{g}_i$ (since for
 $x, y \in \mathfrak{g}_i$ and $z \in \mathfrak{g}_j$ with $j \neq i$, the cocyc
le condition gives $\delta([x,z]) = x\cdot\delta(z) - z\cdot\delta(x)$; but $[x,
z]=0$, so $x \cdot \delta(z) = z \cdot \delta(x)$, and the cross-terms are contr
olled). It suffices to solve the problem for each simple factor independently.

**Step 2 (Casimir element).** Assume $\mathfrak{g}$ is simple. The Killing form
$\kappa$ is nondegenerate. Choose dual bases $\{e_i\}, \{e^i\}$ with $\kappa(e_i
, e^j) = \delta_{ij}$. The **Casimir element** $C = \sum_i e_i \cdot e^i \in U(\
mathfrak{g})$ acts on any $\mathfrak{g}$-module $W$ as a $\mathfrak{g}$-endomorp
hism (it is central in $U(\mathfrak{g})$).

**Step 3 (The Casimir trick).** Define:
$$v := \sum_i e_i \cdot \delta(e^i) \in V.$$
Compute $x \cdot v$ for $x \in \mathfrak{g}$:
$$x \cdot v = \sum_i [x, e_i] \cdot \delta(e^i) + \sum_i e_i \cdot (x \cdot \del
ta(e^i)).$$
By the cocycle condition, $x \cdot \delta(e^i) = \delta([x, e^i]) + e^i \cdot \d
elta(x)$, so:
$$x \cdot v = \underbrace{\sum_i [x,e_i]\cdot\delta(e^i) + \sum_i e_i \cdot \del
ta([x,e^i])}_{(*)} + \sum_i e_i \cdot (e^i \cdot \delta(x)).$$

Write $[x, e_i] = \sum_j c_{ji} e_j$. By $\kappa$-duality, $[x, e^i] = -\sum_j c
_{ij} e^j$. Then:
$$(*) = \sum_{i,j} c_{ji}\, e_j \cdot \delta(e^i) - \sum_{i,j} c_{ij}\, e_i \cdo
t \delta(e^j) = \sum_{i,j} c_{ji}\, e_j \cdot \delta(e^i) - \sum_{i,j} c_{ji}\,
e_j \cdot \delta(e^i) = 0.$$
Therefore:
$$x \cdot v = \left(\sum_i e_i \cdot e^i\right) \cdot \delta(x) = C \cdot \delta
(x).$$

**Step 4 (Invertibility on nontrivial modules).** The Casimir $C$ acts as a $\ma
thfrak{g}$-endomorphism, so $\ker(C|_V)$ and $\operatorname{im}(C|_V)$ are submo
dules. Decompose $V = V_0 \oplus V_1$ where $V_0 = \ker(C|_V)$ and $V_1$ is a co
mplementary submodule. On any **nontrivial** irreducible module $W$, $C$ acts as
 a nonzero scalar: indeed, $\operatorname{tr}_W(C) = \sum_i \operatorname{tr}_W(
\rho(e_i)\rho(e^i)) = \frac{\dim \mathfrak{g}}{\dim W} \cdot \frac{\operatorname
{tr}_W(\kappa_W)}{\dim \mathfrak{g}} \cdot \dim W > 0$ (where $\kappa_W$ is the
trace form of $W$, which is a nonzero multiple of $\kappa$ since $\mathfrak{g}$
is simple and $W$ is nontrivial). By Schur's lemma and complete reducibility, $C
$ is invertible on $V_1$.

**Step 5 (Trivial part).** On the trivial submodule $V_0$, the cocycle condition
 gives $\delta([x,y]) = 0$ for all $x, y$. Since $\mathfrak{g}$ is semisimple, $
\mathfrak{g} = [\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}]$, so $\delta|_{V_0} = 0$.

**Step 6 (Conclusion).** On $V_1$, $C$ is invertible. Set $v_0 := C^{-1} v$. The
n:
$$x \cdot v_0 = C^{-1}(x \cdot v) = C^{-1}(C \cdot \delta(x)) = \delta(x) \quad
\text{on } V_1,$$
and $\delta(x) = 0 = x \cdot 0$ on $V_0$. Thus $\delta(x) = x \cdot v_0$ for all
 $x$, proving $\delta$ is a coboundary. $\square$

### Applying the Lemma

Since $\mathfrak{g}$ is semisimple over $\mathbb{C}$ (characteristic $0$) and $\
mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{ad}}$ is a finite-dimensional $\mathfrak{g}$-module, Whiteh
ead's first lemma gives:
$$H^1(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{ad}}) = 0.$$
This means every 1-cocycle (derivation) is a 1-coboundary (inner derivation). Th
erefore:
$$\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{g}) = \operatorname{Inn}(\mathfrak{g}) = \operato
rname{ad}(\mathfrak{g}).$$

### Remark on the adjoint representation

Note that $\operatorname{ad}: \mathfrak{g} \to \operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{g})$
 is injective because $Z(\mathfrak{g}) = 0$ for semisimple $\mathfrak{g}$ (the r
adical contains the center, and semisimple means radical $= 0$). Hence $\operato
rname{ad}(\mathfrak{g}) \cong \mathfrak{g}$, and the equality $\operatorname{Der
}(\mathfrak{g}) = \operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak{g})$ identifies the derivation alg
ebra with $\mathfrak{g}$ itself.

### Conclusion

The statement is **true**: every semisimple Lie algebra over $\mathbb{C}$ has th
e property that its adjoint representation equals its derivation algebra, i.e.,
every derivation is inner.
