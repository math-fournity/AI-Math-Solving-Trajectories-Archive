**Problem.** Determine the values of $n$ for which $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ contain
s $i$.

---

## Answer

$$i\in\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 4\mid n.$$

---

## Proof

We use two standard facts about cyclotomic fields (see e.g. Washington, *Introdu
ction to Cyclotomic Fields*, or any treatment of the Kronecker–Weber theorem wit
h conductors).

**Fact 1 (Conductor).** Define $f(n)$ by
$$f(n)=\begin{cases} n & n\not\equiv 2\pmod 4,\\[2pt] n/2 & n\equiv 2\pmod 4.\en
d{cases}$$
Then $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{f(n)})$, and $f(n)$ is the *conducto
r* of the abelian extension $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}$, i.e. the smallest
$N$ with $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_N)$.

*Justification of the equality.* The only nontrivial case is $n\equiv 2\pmod 4$,
 so $n=2m$ with $m$ odd. Then $\zeta_n^2=\zeta_m$, giving $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m)\s
ubseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$. Conversely, since $m$ is odd,
$$\zeta_n=\zeta_{2m}=e^{\pi i/m}=-\,e^{\pi i(m+1)/m}=-\zeta_m^{(m+1)/2}\in\mathb
b{Q}(\zeta_m),$$
so $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m)=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n/2})$. $\square$

Note that $f(n)$ is always either **odd** or **divisible by $4$**: if $n$ is odd
, $f(n)=n$ is odd; if $4\mid n$, $f(n)=n$ is divisible by $4$; if $n\equiv 2\pmo
d 4$, $f(n)=n/2$ is odd.

**Fact 2 (Inclusion via conductors).** For abelian subfields of cyclotomic field
s, $K\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ if and only if the conductor of $K$ divides $
f(n)$. In particular
$$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m)\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)\iff f(m)\mid f(n).$$

This is the conductor-theoretic form of the Kronecker–Weber theorem: the conduct
or of an abelian extension $K/\mathbb{Q}$ is the minimal cyclotomic level carryi
ng it, and $K\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ exactly when that minimal level divid
es $f(n)$.

---

### The forward direction ($4\mid n\Rightarrow i\in\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$)

If $4\mid n$, then $n/4$ is an integer and
$$\zeta_n^{\,n/4}=e^{2\pi i\,(n/4)/n}=e^{2\pi i/4}=e^{\pi i/2}=i.$$
Hence $i\in\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$.

### The reverse direction ($i\in\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)\Rightarrow 4\mid n$)

The field $\mathbb{Q}(i)=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_4)$ is abelian over $\mathbb{Q}$ with
conductor $4$: it is not contained in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_1)=\mathbb{Q}$, nor in $
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_2)=\mathbb{Q}$, nor in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3
})$, and the smallest cyclotomic field containing it is $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_4)$ it
self.

By Fact 2,
$$i\in\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)\iff\mathbb{Q}(i)\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)\iff 4\mid
 f(n).$$

It remains to determine when $4\mid f(n)$:

- **$n$ odd:** $f(n)=n$ is odd, so $4\nmid f(n)$.
- **$n\equiv 2\pmod 4$:** $f(n)=n/2$ is odd, so $4\nmid f(n)$.
- **$4\mid n$:** $f(n)=n$, and $4\mid n$, so $4\mid f(n)$.

Thus $4\mid f(n)$ if and only if $4\mid n$.

---

### Conclusion

Combining both directions,
$$\boxed{\,i\in\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)\iff 4\mid n\,}.$$

Equivalently, $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ contains the imaginary unit $i$ exactly for
those $n$ divisible by $4$.
