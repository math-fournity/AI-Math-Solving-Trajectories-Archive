## Proof

Let $V$ be a complex finite-dimensional inner product space, and let $T: V \to V
$ be a linear operator. Let $A = TT^* - T^*T$.

**Step 1: $A$ is self-adjoint.**

We compute:
$$A^* = (TT^* - T^*T)^* = (TT^*)^* - (T^*T)^* = TT^* - T^*T = A.$$

So $A$ is self-adjoint. (This holds for any $T$.)

**Step 2: $\operatorname{tr}(A) = 0$.**

Using the cyclic property of the trace,
$$\operatorname{tr}(TT^*) = \operatorname{tr}(T^*T),$$
so
$$\operatorname{tr}(A) = \operatorname{tr}(TT^*) - \operatorname{tr}(T^*T) = 0.$
$

**Step 3: A positive operator with zero trace is the zero operator.**

By hypothesis, $A$ is a positive operator. Since $V$ is a complex finite-dimensi
onal inner product space and $A$ is self-adjoint, the Spectral Theorem applies:
$A$ is diagonalizable with real eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$. Posit
ivity means every eigenvalue satisfies $\lambda_i \geq 0$.

The trace equals the sum of eigenvalues:
$$\operatorname{tr}(A) = \lambda_1 + \cdots + \lambda_n = 0.$$

Since each $\lambda_i \geq 0$ and their sum is $0$, we must have $\lambda_i = 0$
 for all $i$. Hence $A$ is diagonalizable with all eigenvalues equal to $0$, whi
ch means
$$A = 0.$$

**Conclusion.**

Therefore $TT^* - T^*T = 0$, i.e., $T$ is normal. The answer to the question is
**yes**: $TT^* - T^*T$ is necessarily the zero operator on $V$.
