## Solution

**Step 1: Compute the divergence of F.**

$$\vec{F} = (xy^2 + \cos(yz))\,\hat{i} - (x^2 + \sin(zx))\,\hat{j} + (z + \cos(x
y))\,\hat{k}$$

$$\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(xy^2 + \cos(yz)) + \frac{\p
artial}{\partial y}(-(x^2+\sin(zx))) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(z+\cos(xy))$$

$$= y^2 + 0 + 1 = y^2 + 1.$$

**Step 2: Apply the Divergence Theorem.**

Since $\partial E$ is a closed surface oriented outward and $E$ is a solid regio
n,

$$\iint_{\partial E} \vec{F}\cdot d\vec{S} = \iiint_E (\nabla\cdot\vec{F})\,dV =
 \iiint_E (y^2+1)\,dV.$$

**Step 3: Describe the region $E$.**

$E$ is the Steinmetz solid defined by $x^2+y^2\le 1$ and $y^2+z^2\le 1$. For a f
ixed value of $y\in[-1,1]$, the constraints become $x^2\le 1-y^2$ and $z^2\le 1-
y^2$, which are independent. The cross-section is a square with side length $2\s
qrt{1-y^2}$, hence area $4(1-y^2)$.

**Step 4: Evaluate the integral.**

$$\iiint_E (y^2+1)\,dV = \int_{-1}^{1}(y^2+1)\cdot 4(1-y^2)\,dy = 4\int_{-1}^{1}
(1-y^4)\,dy,$$

since $(y^2+1)(1-y^2) = 1 - y^4$.

$$= 4\left[y - \frac{y^5}{5}\right]_{-1}^{1} = 4\left(\left(1-\tfrac{1}{5}\right
)-\left(-1+\tfrac{1}{5}\right)\right) = 4\cdot\frac{8}{5} = \frac{32}{5}.$$

**Answer:**

$$\boxed{\dfrac{32}{5}}$$
