## Proof

**Key ingredients.** We work with $u \in H^{s_k}_0(\Omega) \subset H^{s_k}(\math
bb{R}^N)$ (extended by zero), $[u]_{s_k}^2=1$, and $0<s_1<\cdots<s_k<1$, $\sum a
_i=1$.

**Step 1: The sum is strictly positive, so $0$ is not an upper bound.**

Since $[u]_{s_k}^2=1>0$ and $a_k>0$, the term $a_k[u]_{s_k}^2 = a_k > 0$. All ot
her terms are non-negative. Hence
$$\sum_{i=1}^k a_i [u]_{s_i}^2 \geq a_k > 0,$$
so $0$ is **not** an upper bound.

**Step 2: The sum is bounded for any fixed $\Omega$, but the bound depends on $\
Omega$.**

We use two standard facts:

*(a) Fractional Poincaré inequality.* For $u \in H^{s_k}_0(\Omega)$ with $\Omega
$ bounded and Lipschitz:
$$\|u\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^N)}^2 \leq C_P\, [u]_{s_k}^2,$$
where $C_P = C_P(\Omega, N, s_k) > 0$.

*(b) Interpolation of Gagliardo seminorms.* For $0 < s_i < s_k < 1$, using the F
ourier representation $[u]_s^2 \asymp \int_{\mathbb{R}^N} |\xi|^{2s}|\hat u(\xi)
|^2\,d\xi$ and Hölder's inequality with exponent $s_k/s_i$ and its conjugate $s_
k/(s_k-s_i)$:
$$[u]_{s_i}^2 \leq C\, [u]_{s_k}^{2s_i/s_k}\, \|u\|_{L^2}^{2(1-s_i/s_k)}.$$

Combining (a) and (b) with $[u]_{s_k}^2 = 1$:
$$[u]_{s_i}^2 \leq C\, C_P^{\,1 - s_i/s_k}\, [u]_{s_k}^2 = C_i,$$
where $C_i = C\, C_P^{\,1-s_i/s_k}$ is a finite constant depending on $\Omega, N
, s_i, s_k$.

Therefore:
$$\sum_{i=1}^k a_i [u]_{s_i}^2 \leq \sum_{i=1}^k a_i\, C_i =: M(\Omega, N, s_1,\
ldots,s_k, a_1,\ldots,a_k) < \infty.$$

**Step 3: The bound is not $k$ in general (and not universal).**

We show that no universal constant — in particular not $k$ — can bound the sum i
ndependent of $\Omega$.

Take $\Omega = B_R(0) \subset \mathbb{R}^N$ and $u_R(x) = \phi(x/R)$, where $\ph
i \in C_c^\infty(B_1)$ is a fixed nonzero bump with $\phi \equiv 1$ on $B_{1/2}$
. Under the scaling $x = RX, y = RY$:

$$[u_R]_s^2 = R^{N-2s}\, [\phi]_s^2.$$

Hence:
$$\frac{[u_R]_{s_i}^2}{[u_R]_{s_k}^2} = R^{2(s_k - s_i)}\, \frac{[\phi]_{s_i}^2}
{[\phi]_{s_k}^2} \xrightarrow{R\to\infty} \infty \quad (\text{since } s_k > s_i)
.$$

Normalizing so that $[u_R]_{s_k}^2 = 1$ (set $\tilde u_R = u_R / [u_R]_{s_k}$),
we get:
$$\sum_{i=1}^k a_i [\tilde u_R]_{s_i}^2 = a_k + \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} a_i \cdot R^{2(
s_k-s_i)} \frac{[\phi]_{s_i}^2}{[\phi]_{s_k}^2} \xrightarrow{R\to\infty} \infty.
$$

So by choosing a sufficiently large bounded domain $\Omega = B_R$, the quantity
$\sum a_i [u]_{s_i}^2$ (with $[u]_{s_k}^2=1$) **exceeds any prescribed constant*
*, in particular it exceeds $k$.

**Conclusion.**

- $0$ is not an upper bound (the sum is strictly positive).
- $k$ is not a valid upper bound in general (the sum can be made arbitrarily lar
ge by choosing a large domain; and even for a fixed domain, the actual bound $M$
 depends on $\Omega$ and the exponents, with no reason to equal $k$).
- The correct answer is **none**.
