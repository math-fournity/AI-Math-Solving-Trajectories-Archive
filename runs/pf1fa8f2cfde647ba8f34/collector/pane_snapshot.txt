## Proof

**Yes**, there exists an open neighborhood of $a$ on which $f$ is injective.

**Setup.** Since $(Df)_a : A \to B$ is injective, its rank equals $\dim(A)$. The
 hypothesis states this rank is $m$, so $\dim(A) = m$. The condition $m < \dim(B
)$ gives $n := \dim(B) > m$.

Choose coordinates so that $a = 0 \in A \cong \mathbb{R}^m$ and $B \cong \mathbb
{R}^n$. The Jacobian $Df(0)$ is an $n \times m$ matrix of rank $m$.

**Key observation.** Since $Df(0)$ has rank $m$, there exist $m$ linearly indepe
ndent rows. After reordering the coordinates of $B$, assume the first $m$ rows a
re linearly independent. Let

$$\pi : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m, \quad \pi(y_1, \dots, y_n) = (y_1, \dots,
 y_m)$$

be the projection onto the first $m$ coordinates, and set $g := \pi \circ f : A
\to \mathbb{R}^m$.

The differential $Dg(0) = \pi \circ Df(0)$ is exactly the $m \times m$ matrix fo
rmed by the first $m$ rows of $Df(0)$, which is invertible by construction.

**Applying the inverse function theorem.** Since $f$ is $C^\infty$, so is $g$, a
nd $Dg(0)$ is invertible. By the inverse function theorem, there exists an open
neighborhood $U$ of $a$ such that $g|_U : U \to g(U)$ is a diffeomorphism. In pa
rticular, $g$ is injective on $U$.

**Concluding $f$ is injective on $U$.** For any $x, y \in U$, if $f(x) = f(y)$,
then applying $\pi$:

$$g(x) = \pi(f(x)) = \pi(f(y)) = g(y).$$

Since $g$ is injective on $U$, this implies $x = y$. Therefore $f$ is injective
on $U$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The inverse function theorem alone does not apply directly (it requi
res $\dim(A) = \dim(B)$). The essential idea is that injectivity of $Df_a$ guara
ntees some $m$-dimensional coordinate projection of $f$ has invertible different
ial; that projection is a local diffeomorphism by the inverse function theorem,
and its injectivity forces $f$ itself to be locally injective.
