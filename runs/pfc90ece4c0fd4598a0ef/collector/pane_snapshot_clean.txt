## Proof

**Setup.** Let $X = \mathbb{R}$ and $B = \{U \subseteq \mathbb{R} : |U| = 5\}$,
the collection of all 5-element subsets of $\mathbb{R}$. We determine $\sigma(B)
$ and then count its members $V$ satisfying $|V|\le\aleph_0$ or $|V^c|\le\aleph_
0$.

**Step 1: Every singleton $\{x\}$ belongs to $\sigma(B)$.**

Take two distinct 5-element sets sharing exactly 4 elements, e.g. $\{x,a,b,c,d\}
$ and $\{x,a,b,c,e\}$ (with $d\ne e$, both different from $x,a,b,c$). Both lie i
n $B\subseteq\sigma(B)$, so their intersection $\{x,a,b,c\}\in\sigma(B)$. Repeat
ing this "intersect two sets sharing $k$ elements to get a $(k{-}1)$-element set
" argument, we obtain all 4-, 3-, 2-element sets, and finally all singletons $\{
x\}\in\sigma(B)$.

**Step 2: $\sigma(B)$ contains all countable and all co-countable subsets of $\m
athbb{R}$.**

Since $\sigma(B)$ is closed under countable unions and contains every singleton,
 every countable subset $A=\bigcup_{i}\{x_i\}$ lies in $\sigma(B)$. By closure u
nder complements, every co-countable set $A^c$ (with $A$ countable) also lies in
 $\sigma(B)$.

**Step 3: $\sigma(B)$ equals the countable/co-countable sigma-algebra.**

Let $\mathcal{C} = \{A\subseteq\mathbb{R}: |A|\le\aleph_0 \text{ or } |A^c|\le\a
leph_0\}$. This is a sigma-algebra: it contains $\emptyset,\mathbb{R}$; it is cl
osed under complements by symmetry; and it is closed under countable unions (a c
ountable union of countable sets is countable; if any member is co-countable, th
e union is co-countable). Since every 5-element set is countable, $B\subseteq\ma
thcal{C}$, hence $\sigma(B)\subseteq\mathcal{C}$. Combined with Step 2, $\sigma(
B)=\mathcal{C}$.

**Step 4: Compute $f(5)$.**

Since $\sigma(B)=\mathcal{C}$, *every* $V\in\sigma(B)$ satisfies $|V|\le\aleph_0
$ or $|V^c|\le\aleph_0$ by definition. Thus
$$f(5)=|\sigma(B)|=|\mathcal{C}|.$$

Now $\mathcal{C}$ is the disjoint union of the countable sets and the co-countab
le sets (a set cannot be both countable and co-countable, since that would force
 $|\mathbb{R}|\le\aleph_0$).

- **Number of countable subsets of $\mathbb{R}$:** Each countable subset is the
range of a sequence in $\mathbb{R}$, so the count is at most $|\mathbb{R}|^{\ale
ph_0}=\mathfrak{c}^{\aleph_0}=(2^{\aleph_0})^{\aleph_0}=2^{\aleph_0\cdot\aleph_0
}=2^{\aleph_0}=\mathfrak{c}$. It is at least $\mathfrak{c}$ (there are $\mathfra
k{c}$ singletons). Hence exactly $\mathfrak{c}=2^{\aleph_0}$.
- **Number of co-countable subsets:** equal to the number of countable subsets (
via complementation), so also $2^{\aleph_0}$.

Therefore
$$f(5)=2^{\aleph_0}+2^{\aleph_0}=2^{\aleph_0}.$$

**Remark.** The value is independent of $n$: for any $n\ge 1$, the same argument
 shows $\sigma(\{U:|U|=n\})=\mathcal{C}$, giving $f(n)=2^{\aleph_0}$.

$$\boxed{f(5)=2^{\aleph_0}}$$
