## Proof

**Claim.** The quadratic variation of $Y_t = \int_0^t \operatorname{sign}(X_s)\,
ds$ is zero: $\langle Y\rangle_t = 0$ for all $t\ge 0$.

**Key observation.** The process $Y$ is defined as an integral against Lebesgue
measure $ds$, not against the Brownian motion $dX_s$. Specifically, the integran
d $\operatorname{sign}(X_s)$ is a bounded (deterministic given the path) process
 with $|\operatorname{sign}(X_s)|\le 1$, and it is integrated against $ds$. Ther
efore $Y$ is a process of **finite variation** on every compact time interval $[
0,t]$.

**Finite variation implies zero quadratic variation.** We recall the standard fa
ct:

> If $A = (A_t)_{t\ge 0}$ is a continuous adapted process whose sample paths are
 of finite variation on every compact interval $[0,t]$, then its quadratic varia
tion is identically zero.

*Proof of the fact.* Fix $t>0$ and a partition $\pi = \{0=t_0 < t_1 < \cdots < t
_n = t\}$ with mesh $\|\pi\|$. Then

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n}(A_{t_i}-A_{t_{i-1}})^2 \;\le\; \Big(\max_i |A_{t_i}-A_{t_{i-1}}
|\Big)\sum_{i=1}^{n}|A_{t_i}-A_{t_{i-1}}| \;\le\; \Big(\max_i |A_{t_i}-A_{t_{i-1
}}|\Big)\cdot V_A[0,t],$$

where $V_A[0,t]$ is the total variation of $A$ on $[0,t]$, which is finite by hy
pothesis. Since $A$ is continuous on the compact set $[0,t]$, it is uniformly co
ntinuous, so $\max_i |A_{t_i}-A_{t_{i-1}}|\to 0$ as $\|\pi\|\to 0$. Hence

$$\lim_{\|\pi\|\to 0}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(A_{t_i}-A_{t_{i-1}})^2 = 0,$$

i.e., $\langle A\rangle_t = 0$.

**Applying the fact to $Y$.** The sample paths of $Y$ are

$$Y_t(\omega) = \int_0^t \operatorname{sign}(X_s(\omega))\,ds.$$

The integrand $s\mapsto \operatorname{sign}(X_s(\omega))$ is bounded (by $1$) an
d measurable, so the integral is well-defined for every path. Moreover, $Y$ has
continuous paths (as an integral of an $L^1$ function against $ds$), and its tot
al variation on $[0,t]$ satisfies

$$V_Y[0,t] \;\le\; \int_0^t |\operatorname{sign}(X_s)|\,ds \;\le\; t < \infty.$$

Thus $Y$ is a continuous process of finite variation on $[0,t]$. By the fact abo
ve,

$$\boxed{\langle Y\rangle_t = 0 \quad\text{for all } t\ge 0.}$$

**Remark.** The quadratic variation is a property that captures the "roughness"
of a process. Brownian motion $X$ itself has $\langle X\rangle_t = t$ because it
s paths are of infinite variation. But $Y$ is built by *smoothing* — integrating
 a bounded signal against Lebesgue time — which produces a finite-variation (hen
ce smooth) path, regardless of how irregular $X$ is. The sign function only flip
s the direction of accumulation but never introduces a stochastic (Itô) differen
tial, so no quadratic variation is generated.
