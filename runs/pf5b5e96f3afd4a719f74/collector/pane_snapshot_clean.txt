## Proof

**Yes**, $\gamma$ can be chosen to be weakly simple.

Let $R$ be a simply-connected compact region in the plane whose boundary $\parti
al R$ is a continuous image of $[0,1]$ (i.e., $\partial R$ is a Peano continuum)
. We construct a parameterization $\gamma$ of $\partial R$ that is weakly simple
.

### Step 1: $\partial R$ is locally connected

Since $\partial R$ is the continuous image of $[0,1]$, it is a Peano continuum.
By the Hahn–Mazurkiewicz theorem, every Peano continuum is locally connected. Th
us $\partial R$ is locally connected.

### Step 2: The interior $D = \operatorname{int}(R)$ is a simply-connected domai
n

Since $R$ is a simply-connected compact region (the closure of a bounded simply-
connected open set), $D = \operatorname{int}(R)$ is a bounded, simply-connected
open subset of $\mathbb{R}^2$.

### Step 3: Conformal parametrization via the Riemann Mapping Theorem

By the Riemann Mapping Theorem, there exists a conformal map $f: \mathbb{D} \to
D$, where $\mathbb{D} = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| < 1\}$ is the open unit disk.

### Step 4: Continuous extension via Carathéodory's Theorem

Since $\partial R = \partial D$ is locally connected (Step 1), Carathéodory's ex
tension theorem guarantees that $f$ extends to a continuous map $\bar{f}: \overl
ine{\mathbb{D}} \to R$ on the closed disk $\overline{\mathbb{D}} = \{z : |z| \le
 1\}$. The restriction $\bar{f}\big|_{S^1}$ is a continuous surjection from the
unit circle $S^1 = \partial\mathbb{D}$ onto $\partial R$.

### Step 5: Define the candidate parameterization $\gamma$

Define $\gamma: [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}^2$ by
$$\gamma(t) = \bar{f}(e^{2\pi i t}).$$
This is a continuous closed curve with $\gamma(0) = \gamma(1) = \bar{f}(1)$, and
 its image is $\partial R$.

### Step 6: Construct approximating simple closed curves

For each $0 < r < 1$, define $\gamma_r: [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}^2$ by
$$\gamma_r(t) = \bar{f}(r \cdot e^{2\pi i t}).$$

Since $f$ is conformal (hence injective) on $\mathbb{D}$, and the circle $\{z :
|z| = r\}$ lies entirely in $\mathbb{D}$ for $r < 1$, the map $t \mapsto f(r\,e^
{2\pi i t})$ is injective on $[0,1)$. Therefore **$\gamma_r$ is a simple closed
curve** for every $r \in (0,1)$.

### Step 7: Fréchet convergence $\gamma_r \to \gamma$

Since $\bar{f}$ is continuous on the compact set $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$, it is
**uniformly continuous**. Given any $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists $\delta > 0$
 such that $|z - w| < \delta \implies \|\bar{f}(z) - \bar{f}(w)\| < \varepsilon$
.

For any $r$ with $1 - r < \delta$, and for all $t \in [0,1]$:
$$|r\,e^{2\pi i t} - e^{2\pi i t}| = 1 - r < \delta,$$
so
$$\|\gamma_r(t) - \gamma(t)\| = \|\bar{f}(r\,e^{2\pi i t}) - \bar{f}(e^{2\pi i t
})\| < \varepsilon.$$

This holds for all $t$ simultaneously, so $\gamma_r \to \gamma$ **uniformly** as
 $r \to 1^-$. The Fréchet distance satisfies
$$d_F(\gamma_r, \gamma) \le \max_{t \in [0,1]} \|\gamma_r(t) - \gamma(t)\| \to 0
 \quad \text{as } r \to 1^-,$$
since the identity reparameterization is admissible. Thus $\gamma_r \to \gamma$
in Fréchet distance.

### Step 8: Conclusion

Taking any sequence $r_n \to 1^-$ (e.g., $r_n = 1 - 1/n$), the curves $\{\gamma_
{r_n}\}$ are simple closed curves converging to $\gamma$ in Fréchet distance. Th
erefore $\gamma$ is **weakly simple**.

The key ingredients are:
1. $\partial R$ being a curve $\Rightarrow$ $\partial R$ is a Peano continuum $\
Rightarrow$ locally connected.
2. $R$ simply-connected $\Rightarrow$ $\operatorname{int}(R)$ is a simply-connec
ted domain.
3. **Riemann Mapping Theorem**: conformal map $f: \mathbb{D} \to \operatorname{i
nt}(R)$.
4. **Carathéodory's Theorem**: local connectivity of $\partial R$ ensures $f$ ex
tends continuously to $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$.
5. **Level curves** $\gamma_r = \bar{f}(r\,e^{2\pi i t})$ are simple closed curv
es (by injectivity of $f$ on $\mathbb{D}$) converging uniformly—hence in Fréchet
 distance—to $\gamma = \bar{f}(e^{2\pi i t})$.

This gives the desired weakly simple parameterization of $\partial R$. $\blacksq
