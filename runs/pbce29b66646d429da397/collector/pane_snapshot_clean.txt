Looking at this problem, I need to compute the line integral of the vortex field
 centered at $(-1,0)$ over a combined curve $C$ (square CCW + circle CW). Let me
 work through it using the definition for the square and Green's theorem for the
 circle, as specified.

## Solution

**The vector field and its curl.**

$$F(x,y)=\left(\frac{-y}{(x+1)^2+y^2},\;\frac{x+1}{(x+1)^2+y^2}\right)$$

This is the standard vortex field centered at $(-1,0)$. Computing the curl:

$$\frac{\partial F_2}{\partial x}=\frac{y^2-(x+1)^2}{\big((x+1)^2+y^2\big)^2},\q
quad \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial y}=\frac{y^2-(x+1)^2}{\big((x+1)^2+y^2\big)^2}
$$

So $\frac{\partial F_2}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial F_1}{\partial y}=0$ everywher
e except at the singularity $(-1,0)$.

### Part 1: Square integral by definition (direct parametrization)

The square has vertices $(4,4),(-4,4),(-4,-4),(4,-4)$, oriented CCW. I parametri
ze each side.

**Side 1:** $(4,4)\to(-4,4)$: $y=4$, $x$ goes from $4$ to $-4$, $dy=0$.
$$I_1=\int_4^{-4}\frac{-4}{(x+1)^2+16}\,dx=4\int_{-4}^{4}\frac{dx}{(x+1)^2+16}=4
\cdot\frac{1}{4}\arctan\frac{x+1}{4}\Bigg|_{-4}^{4}=\arctan\frac{5}{4}+\arctan\f
rac{3}{4}$$

**Side 2:** $(-4,4)\to(-4,-4)$: $x=-4$, $y$ goes from $4$ to $-4$, $dx=0$.
$$I_2=\int_4^{-4}\frac{-3}{9+y^2}\,dy=3\int_{-4}^{4}\frac{dy}{9+y^2}=3\cdot\frac
{1}{3}\arctan\frac{y}{3}\Bigg|_{-4}^{4}=2\arctan\frac{4}{3}$$

**Side 3:** $(-4,-4)\to(4,-4)$: $y=-4$, $x$ goes from $-4$ to $4$, $dy=0$.
$$I_3=\int_{-4}^{4}\frac{4}{(x+1)^2+16}\,dx=\arctan\frac{5}{4}+\arctan\frac{3}{4
}$$

**Side 4:** $(4,-4)\to(4,4)$: $x=4$, $y$ goes from $-4$ to $4$, $dx=0$.
$$I_4=\int_{-4}^{4}\frac{5}{25+y^2}\,dy=5\cdot\frac{1}{5}\arctan\frac{y}{5}\Bigg
|_{-4}^{4}=2\arctan\frac{4}{5}$$

**Summing all four sides:**
$$\oint_{\text{square}}F\cdot d\vec r=2\arctan\frac{5}{4}+2\arctan\frac{3}{4}+2\
arctan\frac{4}{3}+2\arctan\frac{4}{5}$$

Using the complementary angle identity $\arctan a+\arctan\frac{1}{a}=\frac{\pi}{
2}$ (for $a>0$):

- $\arctan\frac{3}{4}+\arctan\frac{4}{3}=\frac{\pi}{2}$
- $\arctan\frac{5}{4}+\arctan\frac{4}{5}=\frac{\pi}{2}$

Therefore:
$$\oint_{\text{square}}F\cdot d\vec r=2\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}+2\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}=\p
i+\pi=\boxed{2\pi}$$

### Part 2: Circle integral by Green's Theorem

The circle of radius $1$ centered at $(-1,0)$ is oriented **clockwise**. The sin
gularity $(-1,0)$ lies at the center of this circle, so we cannot apply Green's
theorem to the disk alone.

Instead, let $D$ be the region **inside the square but outside the circle**. The
 boundary of $D$ is:
$$\partial D = \text{square (CCW)} \cup \text{circle (CW)} = C$$

This is exactly our curve $C$. The singularity $(-1,0)$ lies **inside the circle
**, hence **outside** $D$. Therefore $F$ is smooth on all of $D$, and the curl i
s zero throughout $D$.

By Green's Theorem:
$$\oint_C F\cdot d\vec r = \iint_D \left(\frac{\partial F_2}{\partial x}-\frac{\
partial F_1}{\partial y}\right)dA = \iint_D 0\,dA = 0$$

This gives us the total integral directly. Since we already computed the square
part:
$$\oint_{\text{square}}F\cdot d\vec r + \oint_{\text{circle}}F\cdot d\vec r = 0$
$
$$2\pi + \oint_{\text{circle}}F\cdot d\vec r = 0 \implies \oint_{\text{circle}}F
\cdot d\vec r = -2\pi$$

**Verification of the circle integral by direct parametrization:** Parametrize t
he clockwise circle as $x=-1+\cos t$, $y=-\sin t$, $t\in[0,2\pi]$. Then $(x+1)^2
+y^2=1$, $F_1=\sin t$, $F_2=\cos t$, $dx=-\sin t\,dt$, $dy=-\cos t\,dt$, giving
$F\cdot d\vec r = -1\,dt$, so the integral is $\int_0^{2\pi}(-1)\,dt=-2\pi$. ✓

---

### Final Result

$$\oint_C F\cdot d\vec r = \underbrace{2\pi}_{\text{square (CCW)}} + \underbrace
{(-2\pi)}_{\text{circle (CW)}} = \boxed{0}$$

The line integral is **$0$**. Intuitively, the vortex field's circulation around
 the singularity (captured by the square, $+2\pi$) is exactly canceled by the cl
ockwise traversal of the circle enclosing that same singularity ($-2\pi$), yield
ing zero net circulation over the combined curve bounding the singularity-free r
egion $D$.
