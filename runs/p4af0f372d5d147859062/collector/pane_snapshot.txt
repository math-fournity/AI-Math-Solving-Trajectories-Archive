Looking at this problem, I need to determine the minimum number of punches where
 each punch removes all points at irrational distance from its center, and the f
irst two punches are at rational distance from each other.

**Answer: 3**

## Proof

**Setup.** A punch centered at $P$ removes all points $Q$ with $d(P,Q) \notin \m
athbb{Q}$. A point $Q$ survives a collection of punches at $P_1, \dots, P_n$ iff
 $d(P_i, Q) \in \mathbb{Q}$ for **every** $i$. We want no survivors.

---

### Two punches do not suffice

Place $P_1$ at the origin and $P_2$ at $(1,0)$ (distance $1 \in \mathbb{Q}$, sat
isfying the constraint). The point $P_1$ itself has $d(P_1, P_1) = 0 \in \mathbb
{Q}$ and $d(P_2, P_1) = 1 \in \mathbb{Q}$, so $P_1$ survives both punches. Thus
at least one point remains, and **2 punches are insufficient**.

---

### The surviving set after two punches is countable

After punches at $P_1 = (0,0)$ and $P_2 = (1,0)$, a point $Q = (x,y)$ survives i
ff there exist $a, b \in \mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}$ with:
$$x^2 + y^2 = a^2, \qquad (x-1)^2 + y^2 = b^2.$$

Subtracting gives $2x - 1 = a^2 - b^2$, so $x = \frac{a^2 - b^2 + 1}{2} \in \mat
hbb{Q}$, and then $y^2 = a^2 - x^2 \in \mathbb{Q}$.

For each pair $(a, b) \in \mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}^2$, the two circle equations yield
 **at most 2 intersection points**. Since $\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}^2$ is countable,
the surviving set
$$S = \{Q \in \mathbb{R}^2 : d(P_1, Q) \in \mathbb{Q} \text{ and } d(P_2, Q) \in
 \mathbb{Q}\}$$
is **countable**. Moreover, every $Q = (x,y) \in S$ satisfies $x \in \mathbb{Q}$
 and $y^2 \in \mathbb{Q}$.

---

### Three punches suffice (constructive)

Place the third punch at $P_3 = (\alpha, 0)$ where $\alpha$ is any **transcenden
tal** number (e.g., $\alpha = \pi$).

For any $Q = (x, y) \in S$ with $x \in \mathbb{Q}$ and $y^2 \in \mathbb{Q}$:
$$d(P_3, Q)^2 = (x - \alpha)^2 + y^2 = \alpha^2 - 2x\,\alpha + (x^2 + y^2).$$

This is a polynomial in $\alpha$ with **rational coefficients** (since $x \in \m
athbb{Q}$ and $x^2 + y^2 \in \mathbb{Q}$):

- **If $x \neq 0$:** this is a non-constant polynomial in $\alpha$ with rational
 coefficients. Evaluated at a transcendental $\alpha$, the result is transcenden
tal. Hence $d(P_3,Q)^2$ is transcendental, so it cannot be a rational square, an
d $d(P_3, Q) \notin \mathbb{Q}$.

- **If $x = 0$:** $d(P_3,Q)^2 = \alpha^2 + y^2$. Since $\alpha$ is transcendenta
l, $\alpha^2$ is transcendental (otherwise $\alpha$ would be a root of $t^2 - \a
lpha^2$ with algebraic coefficients). Adding the rational number $y^2$ preserves
 transcendence. So again $d(P_3, Q) \notin \mathbb{Q}$.

Therefore **every** point $Q \in S$ is at irrational distance from $P_3$, and th
e third punch removes all of them.

After three punches, any survivor would need rational distance from all of $P_1,
 P_2, P_3$—i.e., it would lie in $S$ **and** be at rational distance from $P_3$.
 But no point in $S$ is at rational distance from $P_3$. So **no point survives*
*.

---

### Conclusion

- 2 punches at rational distance leave at least $P_1$ surviving. ✗
- 3 punches (first two at rational distance, third at a transcendental offset) r
emove every point. ✓

The minimum number of punches required is $\boxed{3}$.
