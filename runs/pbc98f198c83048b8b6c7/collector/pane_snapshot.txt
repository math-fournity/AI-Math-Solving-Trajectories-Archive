## Solution

We solve the initial value problem

$$y'' + y = f(x), \qquad y(0)=0,\; y'(0)=0,$$

by constructing the Green's function $G(x,\xi)$ satisfying

$$\left(\frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2}+1\right)G(x,\xi)=\delta(x-\xi), \qquad G
(0,\xi)=0,\quad G_x(0,\xi)=0.$$

### Constructing $G(x,\xi)$

The homogeneous equation $u''+u=0$ has the fundamental pair $\{\cos x,\sin x\}$.

**Region $x<\xi$.** Here $G$ satisfies the homogeneous equation with zero initia
l data at $x=0$. The unique solution of a homogeneous IVP with zero data is iden
tically zero, so

$$G(x,\xi)=0, \qquad x<\xi.$$

This is the *causal* (retarded) form forced by the initial conditions being impo
sed at the left endpoint.

**Region $x>\xi$.** Here $G$ again satisfies the homogeneous equation, so

$$G(x,\xi)=A(\xi)\cos x+B(\xi)\sin x.$$

**Matching at $x=\xi$.** Integrating the defining equation across an infinitesim
al interval around $x=\xi$ gives the jump conditions

$$G(\xi^+,\xi)-G(\xi^-,\xi)=0 \quad\text{(continuity)},\qquad G_x(\xi^+,\xi)-G_x
(\xi^-,\xi)=1 \quad\text{(unit jump in derivative)}.$$

Since $G(\xi^-,\xi)=0$ and $G_x(\xi^-,\xi)=0$, these become

$$A\cos\xi+B\sin\xi=0,\qquad -A\sin\xi+B\cos\xi=1.$$

The coefficient matrix $\begin{pmatrix}\cos\xi&\sin\xi\\-\sin\xi&\cos\xi\end{pma
trix}$ has determinant $\cos^2\xi+\sin^2\xi=1$, so

$$A=-\sin\xi,\qquad B=\cos\xi.$$

Hence, for $x>\xi$,

$$G(x,\xi)=-\sin\xi\cos x+\cos\xi\sin x=\sin(x-\xi).$$

Combining both regions with the Heaviside step function $H$,

$$\boxed{G(x,\xi)=\sin(x-\xi)\,H(x-\xi).}$$

### The solution

By superposition,

$$y(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}G(x,\xi)\,f(\xi)\,d\xi=\int_{0}^{x}\sin(x-\xi)\,f(\xi)\,
d\xi.$$

### Verification

Let $y(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\sin(x-\xi)\,f(\xi)\,d\xi$.

- **Initial conditions.** $y(0)=\int_{0}^{0}\cdots=0$. ✓
- **First derivative** (Leibniz rule; the boundary term carries $\sin 0=0$):
  $$y'(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\cos(x-\xi)\,f(\xi)\,d\xi,\qquad y'(0)=0.\;\checkmark$$
- **Second derivative** (Leibniz rule; boundary term $\cos 0\,f(x)=f(x)$):
  $$y''(x)=f(x)+\int_{0}^{x}(-\sin(x-\xi))\,f(\xi)\,d\xi=f(x)-y(x).$$

Therefore

$$y''(x)+y(x)=f(x),$$

and both initial conditions $y(0)=0$, $y'(0)=0$ hold. The construction is comple
te.

### Final answer

$$\boxed{\,y(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\sin(x-\xi)\,f(\xi)\,d\xi\,}$$

with Green's function $G(x,\xi)=\sin(x-\xi)\,H(x-\xi)$.
