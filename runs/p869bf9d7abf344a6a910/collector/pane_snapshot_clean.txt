## Answer

**Yes**, such a sequence exists. The key tool is the Open Mapping Theorem.

### Proof

Since $T:X\to Y$ is a surjective continuous linear map between Banach spaces, th
e **Open Mapping Theorem** applies: $T$ is an open map. A standard equivalent fo
rmulation is that there exists a constant $M>0$ such that

$$\forall\, y\in Y,\ \exists\, x\in X:\quad T(x)=y\ \text{and}\ \|x\|\le M\|y\|.
 \tag{$\ast$}$$

(This says $T(B_X)$ contains a ball around $0$ in $Y$; rescaling gives $(\ast)$.
)

**Step 1: Lift the limit.** Since $T$ is surjective, pick any $x\in X$ with $T(x
)=y$.

**Step 2: Lift the errors.** Set $z_n := y_n - y$. Then $z_n\to 0$ in $Y$. Apply
ing $(\ast)$ to each $z_n$, there exists $w_n\in X$ with
$$T(w_n)=z_n\quad\text{and}\quad \|w_n\|\le M\|z_n\|.$$
Since $z_n\to 0$, we have $\|w_n\|\le M\|z_n\|\to 0$, hence $w_n\to 0$ in $X$.

**Step 3: Combine.** Define $x_n := x + w_n$. Then
$$T(x_n)=T(x)+T(w_n)=y+z_n=y+(y_n-y)=y_n,$$
so $T(x_n)=y_n$ for all $n$, and $T(x)=y$. Moreover,
$$\|x_n - x\|=\|w_n\|\to 0,$$
so $x_n\to x$ in $X$.

Thus the desired sequence $\{x_n\}$ converging to $x$ with $T(x_n)=y_n$ and $T(x
)=y$ exists. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The surjectivity hypothesis cannot be dropped: if $T$ is not surject
ive, some $y_n$ may lie outside the range of $T$, making the lift impossible. Th
e completeness of both spaces (Banach) is essential for the Open Mapping Theorem
, which is the engine of the argument.
