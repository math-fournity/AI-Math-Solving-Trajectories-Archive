## Proof

Let $\mathcal{T}_p$ denote the transition operator (defined on some ambient spac
e), and let $T_p$ and $Q_p$ be its matrix representations when restricted to the
 invariant subspaces $V_{2N+1}$ and $V_{2N-1}$, respectively.

**Key observation.** Since the transition operator can be *restricted* to both $
V_{2N+1}$ and $V_{2N-1}$, both spaces are $\mathcal{T}_p$-invariant. Moreover, $
V_{2N-1} \subset V_{2N+1}$ (as polynomial spaces of lower degree are contained i
n those of higher degree). Therefore, $Q_p$ is the restriction of $\mathcal{T}_p
|_{V_{2N+1}}$ to the invariant subspace $V_{2N-1}$.

**Dimensions.** Taking $V_n$ as the space of polynomials of degree $\leq n$:
$$\dim V_{2N+1} = 2N+2, \qquad \dim V_{2N-1} = 2N,$$
so the quotient $V_{2N+1}/V_{2N-1}$ is $2$-dimensional.

**Block triangular structure.** Choose a basis $\{e_1, \dots, e_{2N}\}$ for $V_{
2N-1}$ and extend it to a basis $\{e_1, \dots, e_{2N}, e_{2N+1}, e_{2N+2}\}$ for
 $V_{2N+1}$. Since $V_{2N-1}$ is $\mathcal{T}_p$-invariant, the matrix of $\math
cal{T}_p|_{V_{2N+1}}$ in this basis is block upper triangular:

$$T_p = \begin{pmatrix} Q_p & B \\ 0 & C \end{pmatrix},$$

where $Q_p$ is the $2N \times 2N$ matrix of $\mathcal{T}_p|_{V_{2N-1}}$, $B$ is
$2N \times 2$, and $C$ is the $2 \times 2$ matrix representing the induced actio
n on the quotient $V_{2N+1}/V_{2N-1}$.

**Characteristic polynomial.** By the block triangular structure:

$$\det(T_p - \lambda I) = \det(Q_p - \lambda I)\cdot\det(C - \lambda I).$$

Hence the eigenvalues of $T_p$ are precisely the eigenvalues of $Q_p$ together w
ith the two eigenvalues of $C$ (counted with multiplicity).

**Conclusion.** The eigenvalues of $Q_p$ are a **subset** of the eigenvalues of
$T_p$. Equivalently, the spectrum of $T_p$ consists of all eigenvalues of $Q_p$
plus two additional eigenvalues (with multiplicity) arising from the action of t
he transition operator on the $2$-dimensional quotient space $V_{2N+1}/V_{2N-1}$
:

$$\sigma(T_p) = \sigma(Q_p) \cup \sigma(C), \qquad |\sigma(C)| = 2 \text{ (with
multiplicity)}.$$

$\square$
