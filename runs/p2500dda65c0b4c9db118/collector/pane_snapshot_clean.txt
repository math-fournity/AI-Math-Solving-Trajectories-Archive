Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether mutual injective lattice ho
momorphisms between the topologies of two Hausdorff spaces force a homeomorphism
.

**Answer: No.** I'll construct an explicit counterexample.

## Counterexample

Let $X = \mathbb{R}$ with the usual topology $\tau$, and let $Y = \mathbb{R} \sq
cup \mathbb{R}$ (the disjoint union of two copies of $\mathbb{R}$) with the disj
oint union topology $\sigma$.

**Both spaces are Hausdorff.** The usual topology on $\mathbb{R}$ is Hausdorff,
and a disjoint union of Hausdorff spaces is Hausdorff.

**The spaces are not homeomorphic.** $X = \mathbb{R}$ is connected, while $Y = \
mathbb{R} \sqcup \mathbb{R}$ has two connected components (the two copies of $\m
athbb{R}$). Since connectedness is a topological invariant, $X \not\cong Y$.

**The topology of $Y$** consists of all sets $U_1 \sqcup U_2$ where $U_1, U_2 \i
n \tau$.

---

### Injective lattice homomorphism $f: \tau \to \sigma$

Define $f(U) = U \sqcup \emptyset$.

- **Preserves $\cap$:** $f(U \cap V) = (U \cap V) \sqcup \emptyset = (U \sqcup \
emptyset) \cap (V \sqcup \emptyset) = f(U) \cap f(V)$. ✓
- **Preserves $\cup$:** $f(U \cup V) = (U \cup V) \sqcup \emptyset = (U \sqcup \
emptyset) \cup (V \sqcup \emptyset) = f(U) \cup f(V)$. ✓
- **Injective:** If $f(U) = f(V)$, then $U \sqcup \emptyset = V \sqcup \emptyset
$, so $U = V$. ✓

---

### Injective lattice homomorphism $g: \sigma \to \tau$

Choose homeomorphisms $h_1: \mathbb{R} \to (-\infty, 0)$ and $h_2: \mathbb{R} \t
o (0, \infty)$ (e.g., $h_1(x) = x - e^x$ type maps; concretely, $h_1(x) = -e^x$
and $h_2(x) = e^x$ work, or simply note both $(-\infty,0)$ and $(0,\infty)$ are
homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}$). Define:
$$g(U_1 \sqcup U_2) = h_1(U_1) \cup h_2(U_2).$$

Note that $h_1(U_1) \subseteq (-\infty, 0)$ and $h_2(U_2) \subseteq (0, \infty)$
, so the two parts lie in **disjoint** regions of $\mathbb{R}$.

- **Preserves $\cap$:** Since $h_1$ is a homeomorphism, $h_1(U_1 \cap V_1) = h_1
(U_1) \cap h_1(V_1)$, and similarly for $h_2$. Then:
$$g\bigl((U_1 \sqcup U_2) \cap (V_1 \sqcup V_2)\bigr) = g\bigl((U_1 \cap V_1) \s
qcup (U_2 \cap V_2)\bigr) = h_1(U_1 \cap V_1) \cup h_2(U_2 \cap V_2).$$
On the other hand:
$$g(U_1 \sqcup U_2) \cap g(V_1 \sqcup V_2) = \bigl(h_1(U_1) \cup h_2(U_2)\bigr)
\cap \bigl(h_1(V_1) \cup h_2(V_2)\bigr).$$
Because $h_1(\cdot) \subseteq (-\infty,0)$ and $h_2(\cdot) \subseteq (0,\infty)$
 are disjoint, the intersection distributes:
$$= \bigl(h_1(U_1) \cap h_1(V_1)\bigr) \cup \bigl(h_2(U_2) \cap h_2(V_2)\bigr) =
 h_1(U_1 \cap V_1) \cup h_2(U_2 \cap V_2).$$
These are equal. ✓

- **Preserves $\cup$:**
$$g\bigl((U_1 \sqcup U_2) \cup (V_1 \sqcup V_2)\bigr) = g\bigl((U_1 \cup V_1) \s
qcup (U_2 \cup V_2)\bigr) = h_1(U_1 \cup V_1) \cup h_2(U_2 \cup V_2).$$
$$= h_1(U_1) \cup h_1(V_1) \cup h_2(U_2) \cup h_2(V_2) = g(U_1 \sqcup U_2) \cup
g(V_1 \sqcup V_2). \checkmark$$

- **Injective:** If $g(U_1 \sqcup U_2) = g(V_1 \sqcup V_2)$, then $h_1(U_1) \cup
 h_2(U_2) = h_1(V_1) \cup h_2(V_2)$. Since the $h_1$-parts lie in $(-\infty, 0)$
 and the $h_2$-parts lie in $(0, \infty)$:
$$h_1(U_1) = h_1(V_1) \quad \text{and} \quad h_2(U_2) = h_2(V_2).$$
Since $h_1, h_2$ are bijections, $U_1 = V_1$ and $U_2 = V_2$, so $U_1 \sqcup U_2
 = V_1 \sqcup V_2$. ✓

### Conclusion

We have exhibited two Hausdorff spaces $X = \mathbb{R}$ and $Y = \mathbb{R} \sqc
up \mathbb{R}$ that are **not homeomorphic** (one is connected, the other is not
), yet there exist injective lattice homomorphisms $f: \tau \to \sigma$ and $g:
\sigma \to \tau$ in both directions.

Therefore, the existence of mutual injective lattice homomorphisms between the t
opologies of two $T_2$-spaces does **not** imply that the spaces are homeomorphi
c.
