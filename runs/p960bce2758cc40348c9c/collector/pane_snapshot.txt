Looking at this problem, I need to prove that $B, D, E, F$ are concyclic where $
BB_1$ is the angle bisector, $I$ is the incenter, $D, E$ are intersections of th
e perpendicular bisector of $AC$ with circle $(AIC)$, and $F$ is on $B_1C$ with
$AB_1 = CF$.

**Proof.**

**Setup.** Place coordinates so that $A = (-d, 0)$, $C = (d, 0)$ (so the perpend
icular bisector of $AC$ is the $y$-axis), and $B = (p, q)$ with $q > 0$. Let $a
= BC$, $b = CA = 2d$, $c = AB$. Set $s = a + c$ and $t = c - a$.

**Key identity 1 (locus of $F$).** By the angle bisector theorem, $B_1 = (e, 0)$
 where $\frac{e+d}{d-e} = \frac{c}{a}$, giving $e = \frac{dt}{s}$. Since $CF = A
B_1 = e + d$ and $F$ lies on line $B_1C$ (the $x$-axis), we get $F = (d - (e+d),
 0) = (-e, 0)$. Thus **$F$ is the reflection of $B_1$ over the midpoint of $AC$*
*.

**Key identity 2 ($DE$ is a diameter).** The circumcenter of $\triangle AIC$ lie
s on the perpendicular bisector of side $AC$, i.e., on the $y$-axis. Since $D$ a
nd $E$ are the intersections of this $y$-axis with circle $(AIC)$, the line $DE$
 passes through the center, so **$DE$ is a diameter** of circle $(AIC)$.

**Algebraic preliminaries.** From $c^2 = (p+d)^2 + q^2$ and $a^2 = (p-d)^2 + q^2
$:
- $c^2 - a^2 = 4pd \implies st = 4pd \implies p = \frac{st}{4d}$.
- $c^2 + a^2 = 2(p^2 + d^2 + q^2) \implies p^2 + q^2 = \frac{s^2 + t^2}{4} - d^2
$.

**Center of circle $(AIC)$.** The incenter is $I = \frac{a \cdot A + b \cdot B +
 c \cdot C}{a+b+c}$, giving:
$$I_x = \frac{d(2p + t)}{s + 2d}, \qquad I_y = \frac{2dq}{s + 2d}.$$

The center of circle $(AIC)$ is at $(0, k)$ where $k = \frac{I_x^2 + I_y^2 - d^2
}{2I_y}$. Computing:
$$k = \frac{d\bigl[(2p+t)^2 + 4q^2 - (s+2d)^2\bigr]}{4q(s+2d)}.$$

**Simplification of the bracket.** Using $4(p^2+q^2) = s^2 + t^2 - 4d^2$ and $4p
t = \frac{t^2 s}{d}$:
$$(2p+t)^2 + 4q^2 - (s+2d)^2 = 4(p^2+q^2) + 4pt + t^2 - s^2 - 4sd - 4d^2$$
$$= (s^2 + t^2 - 4d^2) + \frac{t^2 s}{d} + t^2 - s^2 - 4sd - 4d^2 = 2t^2 - 8d^2
+ \frac{s(t^2 - 4d^2)}{d} = \frac{(t^2 - 4d^2)(s + 2d)}{d}.$$

Therefore:
$$\boxed{k = \frac{t^2 - 4d^2}{4q}.}$$

**Circle through $D$, $E$, $F$.** Since $D = (0, k+R)$ and $E = (0, k-R)$ (where
 $R$ is the radius of circle $(AIC)$), the midpoint of $DE$ is $(0, k)$. The cen
ter of circle $(DEF)$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $DE$, i.e., on the l
ine $y = k$, say at $(h, k)$. From $|{(h,k) - F}| = |{(h,k) - D}|$:
$$(h + e)^2 + k^2 = h^2 + R^2 \implies 2eh = R^2 - k^2 - e^2 = d^2 - e^2 \implie
s h = \frac{d^2 - e^2}{2e}.$$

**Concyclicity condition.** Point $B = (p, q)$ lies on circle $(DEF)$ iff $(p-h)
^2 + (q-k)^2 = h^2 + R^2$, which simplifies to:
$$p^2 + q^2 - d^2 = 2ph + 2qk. \tag{$\star$}$$

**Verification of $(\star)$.**

*Left side:* $p^2 + q^2 - d^2 = \frac{s^2 + t^2}{4} - 2d^2$.

*First term on right:* $2ph = \frac{p(d^2 - e^2)}{e} = \frac{p \cdot d^2\bigl(1
- \frac{t^2}{s^2}\bigr)}{\frac{dt}{s}} = \frac{pd(s^2 - t^2)}{st} = \frac{pd \cd
ot 4ac}{st} = \frac{4pd \cdot ac}{st} = ac = \frac{s^2 - t^2}{4}$, where we used
 $4pd = st$.

*Second term on right:* $2qk = 2q \cdot \frac{t^2 - 4d^2}{4q} = \frac{t^2 - 4d^2
}{2}$.

*Right side total:* $\frac{s^2 - t^2}{4} + \frac{t^2 - 4d^2}{2} = \frac{s^2 - t^
2 + 2t^2 - 8d^2}{4} = \frac{s^2 + t^2}{4} - 2d^2$.

This equals the left side. $\quad(\star)$ is verified. $\blacksquare$

Since $B$ satisfies the circle equation of $(DEF)$, the four points $B, D, E, F$
 are concyclic.
