## Solution

We use generating functions. Define
$$P(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} P(n)\, x^n, \qquad G(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} G(n)\
, x^n = e^{-\lambda}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda x)^n}{n!} = e^{\lambda(x-1
)}.$$

The desired expectation is $E[N] = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} n\,P_n = P'(1)$.

**Converting the recurrence to an ODE.** Multiply the recurrence
$$\sum_{j=0}^{n} G(j)\,P(n-j) + (n+1)\bigl[P(n+1)-P(n)\bigr] = 0$$
by $x^n$ and sum over $n \ge 0$.

- **First term** (convolution): $\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge 0}\sum_{j=0}^{n}G(j)\,P
(n-j)\,x^n = G(x)\,P(x).$

- **Second term**: $\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge 0}(n+1)\,P(n+1)\,x^n = P'(x).$

- **Third term**: $\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge 0}(n+1)\,P(n)\,x^n = x\,P'(x) + P(x).
$

Combining:
$$G(x)\,P(x) + P'(x) - x\,P'(x) - P(x) = 0,$$
which gives the first-order ODE
$$(1-x)\,P'(x) = P(x)\bigl(1 - G(x)\bigr) = P(x)\bigl(1 - e^{\lambda(x-1)}\bigr)
. \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Extracting $P'(1)$.** Substituting $x=1$ into $(\ast)$ yields $0=0$ (both side
s vanish), so we take the limit. Set $h = 1-x \to 0$:
$$h\,P'(1-h) = P(1-h)\bigl(1 - e^{-\lambda h}\bigr).$$

Dividing by $h$:
$$P'(1-h) = P(1-h)\cdot\frac{1-e^{-\lambda h}}{h}.$$

Since $\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{1-e^{-\lambda h}}{h} = \lambda$ and $P(1)
 = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} P_n = 1$, we obtain
$$P'(1) = P(1)\cdot\lambda = \lambda.$$

**Conclusion.** The expectation is
$$\boxed{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} n\,P_n = P'(1) = \lambda.}$$
