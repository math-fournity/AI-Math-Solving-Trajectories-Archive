Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $G = \langle x, y \mid x^p
= y^q = (xy)^r \rangle$ is finite for all integers $p, q, r$ with $\{|p|, |q|, |
r|\} = \{2, 3, 5\}$.

aked answers. Proceeding.

## Proof

**Step 1: The common value is central.**

Let $z = x^p = y^q = (xy)^r$. Since $z = x^p$, we have $xz = x \cdot x^p = x^{p+
1} = x^p \cdot x = zx$, so $z$ commutes with $x$. Since $z = y^q$, similarly $z$
 commutes with $y$. As $z$ commutes with both generators, **$z$ is central** in
$G$.

**Step 2: The quotient $G/\langle z \rangle$ is $A_5$.**

Setting $z = 1$ yields $G/\langle z \rangle \cong \langle x, y \mid x^{|p|} = y^
{|q|} = (xy)^{|r|} = 1 \rangle = \Delta(|p|, |q|, |r|)$, the triangle group. Sin
ce $\{|p|, |q|, |r|\} = \{2, 3, 5\}$ and $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{5
} = \frac{31}{30} > 1$, this is the spherical case: $\Delta(2, 3, 5) \cong A_5$,
 **finite of order 60**.

Since $z$ is central, the kernel of $G \twoheadrightarrow A_5$ is exactly $\lang
le z \rangle$ (the normal closure of a central element is the cyclic subgroup it
 generates).

**Step 3: The abelianization $G^{\text{ab}}$ is always finite.**

In additive notation, the abelianization is $\mathbb{Z}^2 / \langle (p, -q),\; (
p - r, -r) \rangle$, with order
$$|D| = \left|\det\begin{pmatrix} p & -q \\ p-r & -r \end{pmatrix}\right| = |pq
- pr - qr|.$$

A systematic check over all $6 \times 8 = 48$ combinations (permutations of $\{2
,3,5\}$ assigned to $|p|,|q|,|r|$, times independent sign choices) shows $|D|$ a
lways takes values in $\{1, 11, 19, 31\}$, which are **all nonzero**. For instan
ce, with $|p|=2, |q|=3, |r|=5$: $|pq| = 6$ and $|r(p+q)| \in \{5, 25\}$, giving
$|D| \in \{|6 \pm 5|, |6 \pm 25|\} = \{1, 11, 19, 31\}$; the other two assignmen
ts yield the same set by symmetry.

Thus $G^{\text{ab}}$ is **always finite**.

**Step 4: $z$ must have finite order (by contradiction).**

Suppose $z$ has infinite order. Then $\langle z \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}$, and
$$1 \to \mathbb{Z} \to G \to A_5 \to 1$$
is a **central extension of $A_5$ by $\mathbb{Z}$**.

Central extensions of a group $F$ by an abelian group $C$ (with trivial action)
are classified by $H^2(F, C)$. By the universal coefficient theorem:
$$H^2(A_5, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(H_2(A_5),\, \mathbb{Z}) \;\oplus
\; \operatorname{Ext}^1(H_1(A_5),\, \mathbb{Z}).$$

Now:
- $A_5$ is simple non-abelian, hence **perfect**: $H_1(A_5) = A_5^{\text{ab}} =
0$, so $\operatorname{Ext}^1(0, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$.
- The **Schur multiplier** of $A_5$ is $H_2(A_5) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$,
so $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/2,\, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$ (no nontrivial homomorph
ism from a finite group to $\mathbb{Z}$).

Therefore $H^2(A_5, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$, meaning **every central extension of $A_5$
 by $\mathbb{Z}$ splits**:
$$G \cong A_5 \times \mathbb{Z}.$$

But then $G^{\text{ab}} \cong A_5^{\text{ab}} \times \mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}
$, which is **infinite**—contradicting Step 3, which established that $G^{\text{
ab}}$ is finite.

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

The contradiction forces $z$ to have **finite order** in $G$. Since $G/\langle z
 \rangle \cong A_5$ is finite and $\langle z \rangle$ is finite, $G$ is a finite
 group (of order $60 \cdot \operatorname{ord}(z)$).

This holds for **every** choice of $p, q, r$ with $\{|p|, |q|, |r|\} = \{2, 3, 5
\}$, as the argument uses only:
- the finiteness of $\Delta(2,3,5) \cong A_5$ (independent of signs/order),
- the nonvanishing of $|pq - pr - qr|$ (verified for all 48 combinations),
- the perfectness of $A_5$ and $H_2(A_5) = \mathbb{Z}/2$ (structural properties
of $A_5$).

**The group is finite for all such $p, q, r$.** $\blacksquare$
