## Solution

We solve the heat equation in a finite cylinder with homogeneous Dirichlet bound
ary conditions using separation of variables.

### Setting up separation

Seek a separated solution $u(\rho,z,t) = R(\rho)\,Z(z)\,T(t)$. Substituting into

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \kappa\!\left(\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial \rh
o^2} + \frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial u}{\partial \rho} + \frac{\partial^2 u}{\par
tial z^2}\right)$$

and dividing by $\kappa RZT$ gives

$$\frac{T'}{\kappa T} = \frac{R'' + \frac{1}{\rho}R'}{R} + \frac{Z''}{Z} = -\lam
bda^2,$$

for a separation constant $-\lambda^2 < 0$ (negative to ensure decay in time). S
plitting further,

$$\frac{Z''}{Z} = -\mu^2, \qquad \frac{R''+\frac{1}{\rho}R'}{R} = -(\lambda^2-\m
u^2) = -\nu^2.$$

### Axial eigenfunctions ($z$-problem)

$Z'' + \mu^2 Z = 0$ with $Z(0)=Z(1)=0$ gives

$$Z_n(z) = \sin(n\pi z), \qquad \mu_n = n\pi, \quad n=1,2,3,\dots$$

### Radial eigenfunctions ($\rho$-problem)

$R'' + \frac{1}{\rho}R' + \nu^2 R = 0$, i.e. Bessel's equation of order zero. Th
e bounded solution at $\rho=0$ is $J_0(\nu\rho)$. The boundary condition $R(1)=0
$ requires

$$J_0(\nu) = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \nu = \alpha_m, \quad m=1,2,3,\dots$$

where $\alpha_m$ denotes the $m$-th positive zero of $J_0$. Thus $R_m(\rho) = J_
0(\alpha_m \rho)$.

### Time factor

With $\lambda^2 = \mu_n^2 + \nu_m^2 = (n\pi)^2 + \alpha_m^2$,

$$T_{nm}(t) = \exp\!\bigl[-\kappa\bigl((n\pi)^2 + \alpha_m^2\bigr)t\bigr].$$

### General solution

Superposing all separated solutions,

$$u(\rho,z,t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty} A_{nm}\, J_0(\alpha_m\rho
)\,\sin(n\pi z)\, \exp\!\bigl[-\kappa\bigl((n\pi)^2+\alpha_m^2\bigr)t\bigr].$$

### Determining the coefficients

At $t=0$ the initial condition requires

$$f(\rho,z) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty} A_{nm}\, J_0(\alpha_m\rho)\
,\sin(n\pi z).$$

This is a double expansion in the orthogonal systems $\{\sin(n\pi z)\}$ on $[0,1
]$ and $\{J_0(\alpha_m\rho)\}$ on $[0,1]$ with weight $\rho$.

**Axial orthogonality:**

$$\int_0^1 \sin(n\pi z)\sin(n'\pi z)\,dz = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\delta_{nn'}.$$

**Radial orthogonality** (with weight $\rho$):

$$\int_0^1 \rho\, J_0(\alpha_m\rho)\,J_0(\alpha_{m'}\rho)\,d\rho = \tfrac{1}{2}\
bigl[J_1(\alpha_m)\bigr]^2\,\delta_{mm'},$$

using the standard identity $\int_0^1 \rho J_0(\alpha_m\rho)^2\,d\rho = \frac{1}
{2}[J_0'(\alpha_m)^2 + J_0(\alpha_m)^2] = \frac{1}{2}J_1(\alpha_m)^2$ since $J_0
(\alpha_m)=0$ and $J_0'=-J_1$.

Multiplying both sides of the initial expansion by $\rho\, J_0(\alpha_{m'}\rho)\
,\sin(n'\pi z)$ and integrating over $\rho\in[0,1]$, $z\in[0,1]$, orthogonality
isolates $A_{nm}$:

$$A_{nm} = \frac{\displaystyle\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^1 f(\rho,z)\, J_0(\alpha_m\rho)
\,\sin(n\pi z)\,\rho\,d\rho\,dz}{\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}J_1(\alpha_m)^2 \;\cdo
t\; \tfrac{1}{2}}.$$

Hence

$$\boxed{\;A_{nm} = \frac{4}{\bigl[J_1(\alpha_m)\bigr]^2}\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^1 f(
\rho,z)\, J_0(\alpha_m\rho)\,\sin(n\pi z)\,\rho\,d\rho\,dz\;}$$

### Final answer

The temperature at any point of the cylinder at any subsequent time is

$$\boxed{\;u(\rho,z,t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty} A_{nm}\, J_0(\al
pha_m\rho)\,\sin(n\pi z)\, \exp\!\bigl[-\kappa\bigl((n\pi)^2+\alpha_m^2\bigr)t\b
igr]\;}$$

where $\alpha_m$ ($m=1,2,3,\dots$) are the positive zeros of the Bessel function
 $J_0$, and

$$A_{nm} = \frac{4}{\bigl[J_1(\alpha_m)\bigr]^2}\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^1 f(\rho,z)\,
 J_0(\alpha_m\rho)\,\sin(n\pi z)\,\rho\,d\rho\,dz.$$

### Verification of boundary and initial conditions

- **$z=0$ and $z=1$:** $\sin(n\pi\cdot 0)=\sin(n\pi\cdot 1)=0$, so $u(\rho,0,t)=
u(\rho,1,t)=0$. ✓
- **$\rho=1$:** $J_0(\alpha_m\cdot 1)=J_0(\alpha_m)=0$ by definition of $\alpha_
m$, so $u(1,z,t)=0$. ✓
- **Boundedness at $\rho=0$:** $J_0$ is bounded at the origin, so $|u|<M$. ✓
- **Initial condition:** The double series reduces to $f(\rho,z)$ at $t=0$ by th
e completeness/orthogonality of the eigenfunction systems, with coefficients $A_
{nm}$ as derived. ✓
- **PDE:** Each term satisfies the heat equation since the separation constant r
elation $(n\pi)^2+\alpha_m^2$ couples the spatial and temporal eigenvalues corre
ctly. ✓
