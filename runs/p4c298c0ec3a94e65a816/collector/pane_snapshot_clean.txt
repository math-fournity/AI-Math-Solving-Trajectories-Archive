## Proof

The answer is **No**: not every right $R$-module need be finitely cogenerated. W
e provide an explicit counterexample.

**Counterexample.** Let $R = k$ be a field.

### Step 1: The hypothesis is satisfied.

Every right $k$-module is a vector space over $k$. Let $V$ be a nonzero right $k
$-module.

- If $\dim_k V = 1$, then $V$ has no proper nonzero submodules, so $V$ is **simp
le**.
- If $\dim_k V \geq 2$, choose a basis and write $V = ke_1 \oplus W$ where $W =
\operatorname{span}(e_2, e_3, \ldots) \neq 0$. Both summands are nonzero, so $V$
 is **decomposable**.

Thus every nonzero right $k$-module is simple or decomposable, satisfying the hy
pothesis. (The zero module is trivially finitely cogenerated and does not affect
 the conclusion either way.)

### Step 2: A non-finitely-cogenerated module exists over $k$.

Consider $V = k^{(\mathbb{N})}$, the countably infinite-dimensional vector space
 over $k$ (the direct sum of countably many copies of $k$), with basis $\{e_1, e
_2, e_3, \ldots\}$.

Recall that a module $M$ is **finitely cogenerated** if for every family $\{N_i\
}_{i \in I}$ of submodules with $\bigcap_{i \in I} N_i = 0$, there exists a fini
te subset $J \subseteq I$ such that $\bigcap_{i \in J} N_i = 0$.

For each $i \geq 1$, define the subspace:
$$N_i = \bigoplus_{j \neq i} k\, e_j = \{v \in V : \text{the } i\text{-th coordi
nate of } v \text{ is } 0\}.$$

**The full intersection is zero:** If $v \in \bigcap_{i=1}^{\infty} N_i$, then e
very coordinate of $v$ is $0$, so $v = 0$. Hence $\bigcap_{i=1}^{\infty} N_i = 0
$.

**No finite sub-intersection is zero:** For any finite set $\{i_1, \ldots, i_n\}
$,
$$N_{i_1} \cap \cdots \cap N_{i_n} = \bigoplus_{j \notin \{i_1, \ldots, i_n\}} k
\, e_j,$$
which is an infinite-dimensional (hence nonzero) subspace of $V$.

Therefore, there exists a family of submodules with zero intersection but no fin
ite subfamily with zero intersection. By definition, $V = k^{(\mathbb{N})}$ is *
*not finitely cogenerated**.

### Step 3: Verification via the socle characterization.

We can also verify this using the standard characterization: $M$ is finitely cog
enerated if and only if $\operatorname{Soc}(M)$ is finitely generated and essent
ial in $M$.

Since $k$ is a field, $V = k^{(\mathbb{N})}$ is semisimple — it is a direct sum
of simple modules $ke_i$. Therefore $\operatorname{Soc}(V) = V$. This socle is e
ssential in $V$ (trivially, as it equals $V$), but it is **not finitely generate
d**: any finite set of vectors spans only a finite-dimensional subspace, whereas
 $V$ is infinite-dimensional. Hence $V$ is not finitely cogenerated, confirming
the result.

### Conclusion.

The field $k$ satisfies the hypothesis that every (nonzero) right $R$-module is
simple or decomposable. However, the right $k$-module $k^{(\mathbb{N})}$ is not
finitely cogenerated. Therefore, the condition does **not** imply that every rig
ht $R$-module is finitely cogenerated.
