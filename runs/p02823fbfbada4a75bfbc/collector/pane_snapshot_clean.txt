Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest $N$ that can be written as
$(x^2-1)(y^2-1) = N$ in at least two distinct ways with $1 < x \leq y$ (integers
).

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $a = x^2 - 1$ and $b = y^2 - 1$. The constraint $1 < x \leq y$ gi
ves $3 \leq a \leq b$. The values of $n^2 - 1$ for $n = 2, 3, 4, \ldots$ are:
$$3,\; 8,\; 15,\; 24,\; 35,\; 48,\; 63,\; 80,\; 99,\; 120,\; 143,\; 168,\; \ldot
s$$

We seek the smallest $N = a \cdot b$ (with $a, b$ from this list, $a \leq b$) th
at admits **two distinct** representations.

**Bounding the search.** For $N < 360$, since $a \leq b$ we have $a^2 \leq N < 3
60$, so $a < 19$. The only values $x^2 - 1 < 19$ are $a \in \{3, 8, 15\}$ (from
$x = 2, 3, 4$). I enumerate all products $a \cdot b < 360$ with $a \leq b$ and $
a, b$ in the list:

| $a$ | Valid $b$ (with $ab < 360$) | Products |
|-----|---------------------------|----------|
| 3 | 3, 8, 15, 24, 35, 48, 63, 80, 99 | 9, 24, 45, 72, 105, 144, 189, 240, 297
|
| 8 | 8, 15, 24, 35 | 64, 120, 192, 280 |
| 15 | 15 | 225 |

Collecting all products below 360:
$$9,\; 24,\; 45,\; 64,\; 72,\; 105,\; 120,\; 144,\; 189,\; 192,\; 225,\; 240,\;
280,\; 297$$

These are **all distinct** — no value appears twice. Hence no $N < 360$ has two
representations.

**Exhibiting $N = 360$.** Two distinct pairs achieve $N = 360$:

1. $(x, y) = (2, 11)$: $(2^2 - 1)(11^2 - 1) = 3 \times 120 = 360$, with $1 < 2 \
leq 11$. ✓
2. $(x, y) = (4, 5)$: $(4^2 - 1)(5^2 - 1) = 15 \times 24 = 360$, with $1 < 4 \le
q 5$. ✓

These pairs are distinct, and both satisfy all constraints.

**Conclusion.** Since no $N < 360$ admits two representations (verified by exhau
stive enumeration over all feasible $a$ values), and $N = 360$ admits two repres
entations, the smallest such positive integer is:

$$\boxed{N = 360}$$
