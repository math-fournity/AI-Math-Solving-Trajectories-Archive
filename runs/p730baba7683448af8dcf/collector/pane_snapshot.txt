## Proof

**Claim.** If $f_n \to f$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, then $\Lambda_{
f_n} \to \Lambda_f$ in $\mathcal{D}'(\mathbb{R}^n)$.

**Setup.** Recall that every $g \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ defines a
distribution $\Lambda_g \in \mathcal{D}'(\mathbb{R}^n)$ by
$$
\Lambda_g(\varphi) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} g(x)\,\varphi(x)\,dx, \qquad \varphi \i
n C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n).
$$
This is well-defined because $\varphi$ has compact support $K = \operatorname{su
pp}\varphi$, so the integral reduces to $\int_K g\,\varphi$, which is finite sin
ce $g|_K \in L^1(K)$ and $\varphi$ is bounded.

Convergence $f_n \to f$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ means: for every compact set $K
\subset \mathbb{R}^n$,
$$
\int_K |f_n(x) - f(x)|\,dx \xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{} 0.
$$

Convergence $\Lambda_{f_n} \to \Lambda_f$ in $\mathcal{D}'(\mathbb{R}^n)$ (weak-
* convergence) means: for every test function $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}
^n)$,
$$
\Lambda_{f_n}(\varphi) \xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{} \Lambda_f(\varphi).
$$

**Argument.** Fix an arbitrary $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ and let $K
 = \operatorname{supp}\varphi$, which is compact. Then
$$
\bigl|\Lambda_{f_n}(\varphi) - \Lambda_f(\varphi)\bigr|
= \left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \bigl(f_n(x) - f(x)\bigr)\,\varphi(x)\,dx\right|
= \left|\int_K \bigl(f_n(x) - f(x)\bigr)\,\varphi(x)\,dx\right|.
$$
Since $\varphi$ is continuous on the compact set $K$, it is bounded there; set $
M = \sup_{x \in K}|\varphi(x)| < \infty$. By the triangle inequality for integra
ls,
$$
\left|\int_K \bigl(f_n - f\bigr)\varphi\,dx\right|
\le \int_K |f_n - f|\,|\varphi|\,dx
\le M \int_K |f_n(x) - f(x)|\,dx.
$$
Because $K$ is compact and $f_n \to f$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$, the right-hand s
ide tends to $0$ as $n \to \infty$. Therefore
$$
\Lambda_{f_n}(\varphi) \to \Lambda_f(\varphi).
$$
Since $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ was arbitrary, this holds for every
 test function, which is precisely the definition of $\Lambda_{f_n} \to \Lambda_
f$ in $\mathcal{D}'(\mathbb{R}^n)$. $\blacksquare$
