igenvalue, so is $\bar{\lambda} = a - bi$, and their product is
$$\lambda\bar{\lambda} = a^2 + b^2 > 0.$$
Every real eigenvalue $\lambda$ satisfies $\lambda > 0$. The determinant is the
product of all eigenvalues (with algebraic multiplicity), so it is a product of
positive real numbers and positive conjugate-pair products. Hence
$$\det(M) > 0.$$

**Step 2: The set $GL_n^+(\mathbb{R}) = \{M \in M_n(\mathbb{R}) : \det(M) > 0\}$

0,\infty))$ is the preimage of an open set under a continuous map, hence open in

*Connectedness:* Every $M \in GL_n^+(\mathbb{R})$ admits a polar decomposition $
M = PQ$, where $P = (MM^T)^{1/2}$ is symmetric positive definite and $Q = P^{-1}
M \in SO(n)$ (since $\det(Q) = \det(M)/\det(P) = 1$). The set of symmetric posit
ive definite matrices is convex (hence path-connected): the segment $(1-t)P + tI

**Step 3: Any open connected subset of $\mathbb{R}^m$ is polygonally (piecewise-

$$S = \{y \in U : \text{there exists a piecewise linear path in } U \text{ from

Since $U$ is connected and $S$ is both open and closed in $U$ (and non-empty, as
