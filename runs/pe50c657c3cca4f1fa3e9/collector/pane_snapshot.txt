## Proof

We seek a continuous random variable $X$ supported on $(0, \infty)$ with prescri
bed moments $E(X) = \alpha_1$ and $E(X^2) = \alpha_2$ that maximizes the differe
ntial entropy
$$h(X) = -\int_0^\infty f(x)\log f(x)\,dx.$$

### Setup via Lagrange multipliers

We maximize the entropy functional subject to three linear constraints:
$$\int_0^\infty f(x)\,dx = 1, \quad \int_0^\infty x\,f(x)\,dx = \alpha_1, \quad
\int_0^\infty x^2\,f(x)\,dx = \alpha_2.$$

Introducing Lagrange multipliers $\lambda_0, \lambda_1, \lambda_2$, the first-or
der variation of
$$-\int_0^\infty f\log f\,dx + \lambda_0\!\left(\int f\,dx - 1\right) + \lambda_
1\!\left(\int x f\,dx - \alpha_1\right) + \lambda_2\!\left(\int x^2 f\,dx - \alp
ha_2\right)$$
yields the pointwise condition
$$-\log f(x) - 1 + \lambda_0 + \lambda_1 x + \lambda_2 x^2 = 0,$$
so the extremizing density has the **exponential-family form**
$$\boxed{f(x) = \exp\!\big(\lambda_0 + \lambda_1 x + \lambda_2 x^2\big), \qquad
x > 0.}$$

### Integrability and identification as a truncated normal

For $f$ to be integrable on $(0,\infty)$, the quadratic coefficient must satisfy
 $\lambda_2 < 0$. Completing the square,
$$\lambda_0 + \lambda_1 x + \lambda_2 x^2 = \lambda_2\!\left(x + \frac{\lambda_1
}{2\lambda_2}\right)^{\!2} + \lambda_0 - \frac{\lambda_1^2}{4\lambda_2}.$$

Setting $\mu = -\lambda_1/(2\lambda_2)$ and $\sigma^2 = -1/(2\lambda_2) > 0$, th
e density becomes
$$f(x) = \frac{1}{Z}\exp\!\left(-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right), \qquad x >
0,$$
where $Z = \sigma\sqrt{2\pi}\,\Phi(\mu/\sigma)$ is the normalizing constant (wit
h $\Phi$ the standard normal CDF). This is precisely the **normal distribution $
N(\mu, \sigma^2)$ truncated to $(0,\infty)$**.

### The multipliers are determined by the moment constraints

The parameters $\mu \in \mathbb{R}$ and $\sigma^2 > 0$ are uniquely determined (
when a feasible distribution exists) by the two equations
$$E[X] = \mu + \sigma\,\frac{\varphi(\mu/\sigma)}{\Phi(\mu/\sigma)} = \alpha_1,
\qquad E[X^2] = \mu^2 + \sigma^2 + \sigma\mu\,\frac{\varphi(\mu/\sigma)}{\Phi(\m
u/\sigma)} = \alpha_2,$$
where $\varphi$ is the standard normal density. (These are the standard moment f
ormulas for a truncated normal.)

### This is the global maximum

The entropy functional $f \mapsto -\int f\log f$ is **strictly concave** in $f$,
 and the constraints are affine (linear) in $f$. Therefore the critical point fo
und via Lagrange multipliers is the **unique global maximizer**, provided the fe
asible set is nonempty.

### Feasibility condition

A distribution on $(0,\infty)$ with $E(X)=\alpha_1$ and $E(X^2)=\alpha_2$ exists
 if and only if
$$\alpha_1 > 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \alpha_2 > \alpha_1^2.$$
The first condition is immediate since $X>0$. The second is Jensen's inequality
$\text{Var}(X) = \alpha_2 - \alpha_1^2 > 0$ (strict, since a point mass is not a
 continuous density and has entropy $-\infty$). Under these conditions the trunc
ated normal above is well-defined and achieves the maximum entropy.

### Conclusion

The maximum-entropy distribution on the positive real axis with $E(X)=\alpha_1$
and $E(X^2)=\alpha_2$ is the **truncated normal distribution**: $X \sim N(\mu,\s
igma^2)$ conditioned on $X > 0$, with density
$$f(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}\,\Phi(\mu/\sigma)}\exp\!\left(-\frac{(x-\mu)
^2}{2\sigma^2}\right), \qquad x > 0,$$
where $\mu$ and $\sigma^2$ are the unique real parameters satisfying the two mom
ent constraints above.
