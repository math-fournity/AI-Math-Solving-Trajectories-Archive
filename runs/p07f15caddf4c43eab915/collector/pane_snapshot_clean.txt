## Proof

**Yes**, the homomorphism $k \to k'$ factors through one of the $A_i$.

**Setup.** Let $k \xrightarrow{\alpha} \bigoplus_{i=1}^{n} A_i \xrightarrow{\bet
a} k'$ be the given factorization, with each $A_i$ artinian local and $k, k'$ fi
elds. The composition $\beta \circ \alpha$ is non-zero by hypothesis, so $\beta$
 is non-zero. Since $k'$ is a field, $\beta$ is surjective.

**Idempotents.** For each $i$, let $e_i \in \bigoplus A_i$ be the element with t
he identity of $A_i$ in the $i$-th component and $0$ elsewhere. The $e_i$ are ce
ntral idempotents satisfying $e_i e_j = 0$ for $i \neq j$ and $\sum_{i=1}^n e_i
= 1$.

**Key step.** Since $\beta$ is a ring homomorphism, each $\beta(e_i)$ is an idem
potent in the field $k'$. The only idempotents in a field are $0$ and $1$. Moreo
ver,
$$\sum_{i=1}^n \beta(e_i) = \beta\!\left(\sum e_i\right) = \beta(1) = 1.$$
Since each $\beta(e_i) \in \{0,1\}$ and they sum to $1$, **exactly one** of them
 equals $1$. Say $\beta(e_j) = 1$ and $\beta(e_i) = 0$ for $i \neq j$.

**Factorization.** Let $\iota_j: A_j \hookrightarrow \bigoplus A_i$ be the natur
al inclusion and $\pi_j: \bigoplus A_i \to A_j$ the projection. Define:
$$\tilde{\alpha} := \pi_j \circ \alpha : k \to A_j, \qquad \tilde{\beta} := \bet
a \circ \iota_j : A_j \to k'.$$
Both are ring homomorphisms. We verify $\tilde{\beta} \circ \tilde{\alpha} = \be
ta \circ \alpha$.

For any $x \in k$, writing $\alpha(x) = \sum_i \alpha(x)\, e_i$ (since $\sum e_i
 = 1$):
$$\beta(\alpha(x)) = \sum_i \beta(\alpha(x)\, e_i) = \sum_i \beta(\alpha(x))\,\b
eta(e_i) = \beta(\alpha(x))\cdot\beta(e_j) = \beta(\alpha(x)\, e_j).$$
But $\alpha(x)\, e_j = \iota_j(\pi_j(\alpha(x))) = \iota_j(\tilde\alpha(x))$, so
$$\beta(\alpha(x)) = \beta(\iota_j(\tilde\alpha(x))) = \tilde\beta(\tilde\alpha(
x)).$$

Thus $k \xrightarrow{\tilde\alpha} A_j \xrightarrow{\tilde\beta} k'$ is a factor
ization of $k \to k'$ through $A_j$. $\blacksquare$
