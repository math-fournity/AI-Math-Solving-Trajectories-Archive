## Proof

Let $x = 10^{31}$. We want $\left\lfloor \dfrac{x^3}{x+3} \right\rfloor$.

**Polynomial division.** Dividing $x^3$ by $x+3$:

$$x^3 = (x+3)(x^2 - 3x + 9) - 27$$

so

$$\frac{x^3}{x+3} = x^2 - 3x + 9 - \frac{27}{x+3}.$$

**Bounding the remainder.** Since $x = 10^{31} \ge 27$,

$$0 < \frac{27}{x+3} < 1.$$

Therefore $\dfrac{x^3}{x+3}$ is strictly between $x^2 - 3x + 8$ and $x^2 - 3x +
9$, giving

$$\left\lfloor \frac{x^3}{x+3} \right\rfloor = x^2 - 3x + 8 = 10^{62} - 3\cdot 1
0^{31} + 8.$$

**Last two digits.** Both $10^{62}$ and $3\cdot 10^{31}$ are multiples of $100$,
 so they contribute $00$ to the last two digits. The only contribution to the la
st two digits comes from $+8$:

$$10^{62} - 3\cdot 10^{31} + 8 \equiv 8 \pmod{100}.$$

Thus the tens digit is $\mathbf{0}$ and the units digit is $\mathbf{8}$.
