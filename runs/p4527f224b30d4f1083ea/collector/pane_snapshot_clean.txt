## Solution

**Answer leak check:** The problem statement contains no leaked answers or solut

**Claim:** For $3 < a < 1+\sqrt{6}$ and $y_0 \in (0,1)\setminus\{(a-1)/a\}$,
$$\lambda(y_0) = \tfrac{1}{2}\ln\bigl|\,4 + 2a - a^{2}\,\bigr|.$$

---

### Step 1. Dynamics in the regime $3 < a < 1+\sqrt{6}$

The fixed points of $F(x)=ax(1-x)$ are $x=0$ and $x^*=(a-1)/a$. The multiplier a
t $x^*$ is
$$F'(x^*) = a(1-2x^*) = a - 2(a-1) = 2 - a,$$
so $|F'(x^*)| = a-2 > 1$ for $a>3$: **$x^*$ is unstable**. Hence the excluded po
int $(a-1)/a$ is precisely the (unstable) fixed point, and every other orbit in
$(0,1)$ is repelled from it.

At $a=3$ the fixed point undergoes a period-doubling bifurcation, giving birth t
o a stable 2-cycle which persists (and remains stable) until the next doubling a
t $a = 1+\sqrt{6}$. Thus for $3<a<1+\sqrt{6}$ the **2-cycle is the attractor** o
f $(0,1)\setminus\{x^*\}$.

### Step 2. Locating the 2-cycle

The period-2 points satisfy $F^{2}(x)=x$ with $F(x)\neq x$. Solving $F^{2}(x)-x=
0$ and factoring out the fixed-point roots $x=0$ and $x=(a-1)/a$ leaves the quad
ratic
$$a^{2}x^{2} - a(a+1)x + (a+1) = 0,$$
with roots
$$x_{1,2} = \frac{(a+1) \pm \sqrt{(a+1)(a-3)}}{2a}.$$
The discriminant $(a+1)(a-3)$ is positive for $a>3$ and equals $2>0$ at $a=1+\sq
rt{6}$, so the 2-cycle is real throughout the regime. By Vieta:
$$x_1+x_2 = \frac{a+1}{a}, \qquad x_1 x_2 = \frac{a+1}{a^{2}}.$$

### Step 3. Multiplier of the 2-cycle

With $F'(x)=a(1-2x)$,
$$F'(x_1)\,F'(x_2) = a^{2}(1-2x_1)(1-2x_2) = a^{2}\bigl[1 - 2(x_1+x_2) + 4x_1 x_
2\bigr].$$
Substituting the symmetric functions:
$$= a^{2}\!\left[1 - \frac{2(a+1)}{a} + \frac{4(a+1)}{a^{2}}\right] = a^{2} - 2a
(a+1) + 4(a+1) = -a^{2}+2a+4.$$
So
$$F'(x_1)\,F'(x_2) = 4 + 2a - a^{2}.$$

**Stability check.** At $a=3$: $4+6-9=1$ (birth, multiplier $+1$). At $a=1+\sqrt
6$: $4+2(1+\sqrt6)-(1+\sqrt6)^2 = 4+2+2\sqrt6-7-2\sqrt6 = -1$ (next doubling, mu
ltiplier $-1$). In between, $|4+2a-a^{2}|<1$, confirming the 2-cycle is the stab
le attractor.

### Step 4. Lyapunov exponent

For any $y_0\in(0,1)\setminus\{x^*\}$, the orbit converges to the attracting 2-c
ycle $\{x_1,x_2\}$. The Lyapunov exponent is the time-average of $\ln|F'|$ along
 the orbit, which equals the average over one period of the cycle:
$$\lambda(y_0) = \frac{1}{2}\Bigl[\ln|F'(x_1)| + \ln|F'(x_2)|\Bigr] = \frac{1}{2
}\ln\bigl|F'(x_1)\,F'(x_2)\bigr| = \frac{1}{2}\ln\bigl|4+2a-a^{2}\bigr|.$$

Since $|4+2a-a^{2}|<1$ in the interior of the regime, $\lambda(y_0)<0$, consiste
nt with stable periodic motion. The value is independent of $y_0$ (as long as $y
_0\neq (a-1)/a$), because all such orbits share the same attractor.

### Result

$$\boxed{\lambda(y_0) = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\ln\bigl|\,4 + 2a - a^{2}\,\bigr|}$$
