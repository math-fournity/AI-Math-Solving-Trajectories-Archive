## Proof

**Claim.** A continuous image of a Polish space can carry the indiscrete topolog
y, and hence need not be Hausdorff.

**Key definitions.** A *Polish space* is a separable, completely metrizable topo
logical space. A topology on a set $Y$ is *indiscrete* (trivial) if the only ope
n sets are $\varnothing$ and $Y$.

**Step 1. The indiscrete topology on a set with $\geq 2$ points is not Hausdorff
.**

If $|Y| \geq 2$ and $Y$ carries the indiscrete topology, then the only nonempty
open set is $Y$ itself. Given any two distinct points $a, b \in Y$, every open n
eighborhood of $a$ is $Y$ and every open neighborhood of $b$ is $Y$, so no pair
of disjoint open sets can separate $a$ and $b$. Thus $Y$ is not Hausdorff.

**Step 2. Construction of an indiscrete continuous image of a Polish space.**

Let $X = \mathbb{R}$ with its usual topology. This is a Polish space (it is sepa
rable and completely metrizable via the standard metric $d(x,y)=|x-y|$).

Let $Y = \{0, 1\}$ equipped with the indiscrete topology $\{\varnothing, Y\}$.

Define $f \colon \mathbb{R} \to Y$ by
$$f(x) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } x < 0, \\ 1 & \text{if } x \geq 0. \end{ca
ses}$$

**$f$ is surjective:** $f(-1) = 0$ and $f(0) = 1$, so both elements of $Y$ are a
ttained.

**$f$ is continuous:** The only open subsets of $Y$ are $\varnothing$ and $Y$. T
heir preimages are
$$f^{-1}(\varnothing) = \varnothing, \qquad f^{-1}(Y) = \mathbb{R},$$
both of which are open in $\mathbb{R}$. Since the preimage of every open set is
open, $f$ is continuous.

**Step 3. Conclusion.**

We have exhibited a Polish space $X = \mathbb{R}$ and a continuous surjection $f
 \colon X \to Y$ onto a two-point space $Y$ with the indiscrete topology. By Ste
p 1, $Y$ is not Hausdorff. Therefore a continuous image of a Polish space can ha
ve an indiscrete topology, violating the Hausdorff property.

**Remark.** The essential reason is that continuity only constrains the preimage
s of open sets. When the target topology is indiscrete, the only nontrivial prei
mage is that of the whole space, which is always open. Hence *every* function in
to an indiscrete space is continuous, and any surjection from a Polish space ont
o a set of equal or smaller cardinality yields an indiscrete continuous image. I
n particular, the class of continuous images of Polish spaces is far larger than
 the class of Polish (or even Hausdorff) spaces; it includes all spaces of cardi
nality $\leq |\mathbb{R}|$ carrying the indiscrete topology.
