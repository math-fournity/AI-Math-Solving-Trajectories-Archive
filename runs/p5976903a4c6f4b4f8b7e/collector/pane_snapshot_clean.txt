## Proof

**Setup.** Let $(E, \overrightarrow{E})$ be a normed affine space with origin $O
$, and let $\Omega \subseteq E$ be open. We equip $\Omega$ with the **canonical
flat (affine) connection** $\nabla$, defined as follows. A vector field $Z$ on $
\Omega$ is a smooth map $Z:\Omega\to\overrightarrow{E}$. For a vector field $X$
and a vector field $Y$, the covariant derivative is

$$
\nabla_X Y \;=\; D_X Y \;:=\; dY\cdot X,
$$

i.e. the directional (Fréchet) derivative of the map $Y:\Omega\to\overrightarrow
{E}$ in the direction $X$. This is well-defined because $\overrightarrow{E}$ is
a vector space, so the derivative of $Y$ at a point is a linear map $\overrighta
rrow{E}\to\overrightarrow{E}$, and evaluating it on $X(p)$ yields another vector
 field.

---

### 1. The Lie bracket via covariant derivatives

The **Lie bracket** of two vector fields is defined intrinsically as the commuta
tor of derivations on $C^\infty(\Omega)$:

$$
[X,Y](f) \;=\; X(Y(f)) - Y(X(f)), \qquad f\in C^\infty(\Omega).
$$

We claim:

$$
\boxed{\,[X,Y] \;=\; D_X Y \;-\; D_Y X\,}
$$

**Proof of the claim.** Choose an affine frame $(O,(u_1,\dots,u_n))$ and let $x^
i$ be the associated affine coordinates, so a point $p\in E$ has coordinates $(x
^1,\dots,x^n)$ with $\overrightarrow{OP}=\sum_i x^i u_i$. Write

$$
X \;=\; \sum_i X^i\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}, \qquad Y \;=\; \sum_j Y^j\,\f
rac{\partial}{\partial x^j}.
$$

Since the frame $(u_i)$ is constant (the $u_i$ do not depend on $p$), the covari
ant derivative reduces to componentwise differentiation:

$$
D_X Y \;=\; \sum_j \bigl(dY^j\cdot X\bigr)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^j}
\;=\; \sum_{i,j} X^i\,\frac{\partial Y^j}{\partial x^i}\,\frac{\partial}{\partia
l x^j}.
$$

Likewise

$$
D_Y X \;=\; \sum_{i,j} Y^i\,\frac{\partial X^j}{\partial x^i}\,\frac{\partial}{\
partial x^j}.
$$

On the other hand, the Lie bracket in coordinates is the standard commutator:

$$
[X,Y] \;=\; \sum_j\Bigl(\sum_i X^i\,\frac{\partial Y^j}{\partial x^i} - Y^i\,\fr
ac{\partial X^j}{\partial x^i}\Bigr)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^j}.
$$

This is obtained by direct computation: $X(Y(f))=\sum_{i,j}X^i\,\partial_i Y^j\,
\partial_j f + \sum_j Y^j\,X(\partial_j f)$, and the second-order terms $\sum_{i
,j}X^i\,Y^j\,\partial_i\partial_j f$ cancel with the corresponding terms from $Y
(X(f))$ by symmetry of mixed partials, leaving exactly the first-order expressio
n above.

Comparing, we obtain

$$
[X,Y] \;=\; D_X Y \;-\; D_Y X. \qquad\blacksquare
$$

**Remark (torsion).** The quantity $T(X,Y):=D_X Y - D_Y X - [X,Y]$ is the **tors
ion** of the connection. The identity above says precisely that the canonical fl
at affine connection is **torsion-free**. This is the coordinate-free way of sta
ting the symmetry of the connection coefficients ($\Gamma^k_{ij}=\Gamma^k_{ji}$)
, which here are all zero because the affine frame is constant.

---

### 2. Coordinate representation

With the affine frame $(O,(u_1,\dots,u_n))$ and coordinates $x^1,\dots,x^n$, wri
ting $X=\sum_i X^i\,\partial_i$ and $Y=\sum_j Y^j\,\partial_j$, the $k$-th compo
nent of the Lie bracket is:

$$
\boxed{\;[X,Y]^k \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{n}\Bigl(X^i\,\frac{\partial Y^k}{\partial x^i
} \;-\; Y^i\,\frac{\partial X^k}{\partial x^i}\Bigr), \qquad k=1,\dots,n.\;}
$$

Equivalently, using the Jacobian matrices $JY=(\partial_i Y^k)_{i,k}$ and $JX=(\
partial_i X^k)_{i,k}$,

$$
[X,Y]^k \;=\; (JY\cdot X)^k \;-\; (JX\cdot Y)^k,
$$

i.e. in vector notation $\,[X,Y] = JY\,X - JX\,Y$, where $X=(X^1,\dots,X^n)^\top
$ and $Y=(Y^1,\dots,Y^n)^\top$ are the column vectors of components, and $JY$, $
JX$ are the Jacobian matrices of $Y$ and $X$ respectively.

---

### Summary

- **Coordinate-free form:** $\;[X,Y] = D_X Y - D_Y X$, reflecting the torsion-fr
eeness of the canonical flat connection on an affine space.
- **Coordinate form (affine frame $(O,(u_1,\dots,u_n))$):** $\;[X,Y]^k = \sum_i\
bigl(X^i\,\partial_i Y^k - Y^i\,\partial_i X^k\bigr)$, or compactly $[X,Y]=JY\,X
 - JX\,Y$.
