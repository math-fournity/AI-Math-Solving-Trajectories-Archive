Looking at this problem, I need to determine which Galois groups can occur for a
n irreducible quartic over Q with negative discriminant.

## Proof

**Step 1: The candidates.** The Galois group $G$ of an irreducible quartic over
$\mathbb{Q}$ is a transitive subgroup of $S_4$. The transitive subgroups of $S_4
$ are:
$$S_4,\quad A_4,\quad D_4,\quad V_4,\quad C_4.$$

**Step 2: Negative discriminant eliminates $A_4$ and $V_4$.** The discriminant $
\Delta$ of $f$ is a perfect square in $\mathbb{Q}$ if and only if $G \subseteq A
_4$. Since $\Delta < 0$, it cannot be a perfect square of any rational number (s
quares of rationals are non-negative), so $G \not\subseteq A_4$. This eliminates
 $A_4$ and $V_4$ (both are subgroups of $A_4$). The remaining candidates are $S_
4$, $D_4$, and $C_4$.

**Step 3: $C_4$ is impossible when $\Delta < 0$.** Let the roots of $f$ be $r_1,
 r_2, r_3, r_4$. Complex conjugation acts on the roots as an element $c \in G$ w
ith $c^2 = 1$. The sign of the discriminant is $(-1)^{r_2}$, where $r_2$ is the
number of pairs of complex-conjugate (non-real) roots. In particular, $\Delta <
0$ if and only if $r_2 = 1$ (exactly 2 real roots and 2 complex-conjugate roots)
, which requires $c$ to be a permutation that fixes exactly 2 roots and swaps th
e other 2 — i.e., a **transposition**.

Now suppose $G = C_4 = \langle (1\,2\,3\,4) \rangle$. The elements of order $\le
q 2$ in $C_4$ are:
$$\{1,\; (1\,3)(2\,4)\}.$$
- If $c = 1$: all roots are real, so $r_2 = 0$ and $\Delta > 0$.
- If $c = (1\,3)(2\,4)$: all four roots pair into two complex-conjugate pairs, s
o $r_2 = 2$ and $\Delta > 0$.

In neither case is $r_2 = 1$, so $\Delta > 0$ always when $G = C_4$. The key poi
nt is that $C_4$ contains no transposition — its only nontrivial element of orde
r 2 is the double transposition $(1\,3)(2\,4)$, which pairs all roots into compl
ex conjugate pairs rather than fixing two. Therefore $C_4$ **cannot** occur when
 $\Delta < 0$.

**Step 4: $S_4$ and $D_4$ can occur with $\Delta < 0$.** It remains to show both
 are realized.

- **$S_4$:** Consider $f(x) = x^4 + x - 1$. This is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}
$ (no rational roots; it cannot factor into two quadratics over $\mathbb{Q}$ sin
ce the system $bd = -1$, $a(d-b)=1$, $b+d-a^2=0$ has no rational solution). Its
discriminant (using the formula $\Delta = 256q^3 - 27p^4$ for $x^4+px+q$ with $p
=1, q=-1$) is $\Delta = 256(-1)^3 - 27(1)^4 = -283 < 0$. The resolvent cubic is
$y^3 + 4y - 1$, which is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ (no rational roots). Sinc
e the resolvent is irreducible and $\Delta$ is not a square, $G = S_4$.

- **$D_4$:** Consider $f(x) = x^4 - 2$, irreducible by Eisenstein's criterion at
 $p=2$. Its roots are $\alpha, -\alpha, i\alpha, -i\alpha$ where $\alpha = 2^{1/
4}$. Computing the discriminant directly:
$$\Delta = \prod_{i<j}(r_i - r_j)^2 = -256\alpha^{12} = -256 \cdot 8 = -2048 < 0
.$$
The resolvent cubic is $y^3 + 8y = y(y^2+8)$, which has exactly one rational roo
t ($y=0$) with $y^2+8$ irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$. Since the resolvent has ex
actly one rational root and $\Delta$ is not a square, $G = D_4$. (Concretely, th
e splitting field is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2}, i)$, which has degree 8 over $\mat
hbb{Q}$, confirming $|G| = 8$ and $G = D_4$.)

**Conclusion.** The possible Galois groups over $\mathbb{Q}$ of an irreducible q
uartic with negative discriminant are exactly:
$$\boxed{S_4 \text{ and } D_4.}$$
