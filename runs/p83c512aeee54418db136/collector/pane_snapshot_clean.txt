## Proof

Let $C\subset\mathbb{P}^2_\mathbb{C}$ be a plane curve and $p\in C$ a point of m
ultiplicity $m$. Choose affine coordinates $(x,y)$ centered at $p$ (i.e. $p=(0,0
)$). The curve is locally the zero locus of a single equation $f(x,y)=0$, where
$f\in\mathbb{C}\{x,y\}$ (or $\mathbb{C}[x,y]_{(x,y)}$) has order $\operatorname{
ord}_p(f)=m$. Concretely

$$f = f_m + f_{m+1} + f_{m+2} + \cdots, \qquad f_m\neq 0,\quad f_j\in\mathbb{C}[
x,y]_j,$$

so the initial form $\operatorname{in}(f)=f_m$ is a nonzero homogeneous polynomi
al of degree $m$.

The local ring of the plane at $p$ is the regular local ring $R=\mathbb{C}[x,y]_
{(x,y)}$ of dimension $2$, with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{n}=(x,y)$. Since $C$ is
 a hypersurface in $\mathbb{P}^2$,

$$\mathcal{O}_p(C) \cong R/(f),\qquad \mathfrak{m} = \mathfrak{n}/(f).$$

**Associated graded ring.** The associated graded ring of $R$ is

$$\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{n}} R \;\cong\; \mathbb{C}[x,y],$$

a polynomial ring in two variables (hence a domain). A standard theorem on assoc
iated graded rings of quotients by a non-zerodivisor (applicable here because $f
$ is $R$-regular, $R$ being regular local, and $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{n}}
R$ is a domain) gives

$$\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{O}_p(C) \;\cong\; \operatorname{gr}_{
\mathfrak{n}} R \big/ (\operatorname{in}(f)) \;\cong\; \mathbb{C}[x,y]/(f_m).$$

Indeed, since $\mathbb{C}[x,y]$ is a domain and $f_m\neq 0$, the initial form $f
_m$ is a non-zerodivisor in $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{n}}R$, so the ideal of
 initial forms of $(f)$ is exactly the principal ideal $(f_m)$.

**Identifying the graded pieces.** By definition of the associated graded ring,

$$\mathfrak{m}^k/\mathfrak{m}^{k+1} \;\cong\; \big(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{
m}}\mathcal{O}_p(C)\big)_k \;\cong\; \big(\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(f_m)\big)_k.$$

The degree-$k$ piece of the quotient is

$$\big(\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(f_m)\big)_k \;=\; \mathbb{C}[x,y]_k \big/ \big(f_m\cdot
\mathbb{C}[x,y]_{k-m}\big),$$

where $\mathbb{C}[x,y]_j=0$ for $j<0$. Since $f_m$ is homogeneous of degree $m$
and $\mathbb{C}[x,y]$ is a domain, multiplication by $f_m$ gives an injective li
near map

$$\mathbb{C}[x,y]_{k-m}\xrightarrow{\;\cdot f_m\;}\mathbb{C}[x,y]_k.$$

Using $\dim_\mathbb{C}\mathbb{C}[x,y]_j = j+1$ for $j\ge 0$:

- **For $0\le k < m$:** $\mathbb{C}[x,y]_{k-m}=0$, so no relation is imposed, an
d
  $$\dim_\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{m}^k/\mathfrak{m}^{k+1} = \dim_\mathbb{C}\mathbb{C}
[x,y]_k = k+1.$$

- **For $k\ge m$:** the map $\cdot f_m$ is injective, so
  $$\dim_\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{m}^k/\mathfrak{m}^{k+1} = \dim_\mathbb{C}\mathbb{C}
[x,y]_k - \dim_\mathbb{C}\mathbb{C}[x,y]_{k-m} = (k+1)-(k-m+1)=m.$$

**Consistency checks.** For $k=0$: $\mathfrak{m}^0/\mathfrak{m}^1=\mathcal{O}_p(
C)/\mathfrak{m}\cong\mathbb{C}$, dimension $1=0+1$. ✓
For $k=m-1$: dimension $m$. For $k=m$: dimension $m$. The two formulas agree at
the transition, giving a continuous Hilbert function that stabilizes at $m$ — ex
actly the Hilbert–Samuel function of a plane curve singularity of multiplicity $
m$, with multiplicity (length $R/\mathfrak{m}^{m}$ minus…) equal to $m$. ✓

The result is independent of the finer analytic type of the singularity (e.g. of
 whether the tangent cone $f_m=0$ is reduced): it depends only on the multiplici
ty $m$, because the associated graded ring is determined solely by the initial f
orm $f_m$, and the dimension count uses only that $f_m$ is a nonzero homogeneous
 form of degree $m$ in two variables.

## Conclusion

$$\boxed{\dim_\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{m}^k/\mathfrak{m}^{k+1} \;=\; \begin{cases} k+
1, & 0\le k \le m-1,\\[4pt] m, & k\ge m. \end{cases}}$$

Equivalently, $\dim_\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{m}^k/\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}=\min(k+1,\,m)$ f
or all $k\ge 0$.
