## Riemann Surface for $w = \sqrt{z^2+1}$: Continuity Along a Path

### Setup and branch structure

The function $w = \sqrt{z^2+1}$ has branch points where $z^2+1=0$, i.e. at $z =
\pm i$. Writing
$$z^2 + 1 = (z-i)(z+i),$$
we introduce a branch of the square root by writing
$$w(z) = \sqrt{z-i}\,\sqrt{z+i},$$
where each square root is defined with a branch cut. A standard choice: cut from
 $i$ to $+i\infty$ along the imaginary axis, and from $-i$ to $-i\infty$ along t
he imaginary axis. Equivalently, the two branch points $\pm i$ are connected to
infinity.

The Riemann surface is two-sheeted (since the square root is degree 2). Crossing
 a branch cut once swaps the two sheets (sign of $w$ flips); crossing twice retu
rns to the original sheet.

### Choosing the path: a circle enclosing $+i$ only

Let $\gamma(t) = i + r e^{it}$, $t\in[0,2\pi]$, with $0 < r < 2$ (so the circle
of radius $r$ centered at $i$ encloses $+i$ but not $-i$, since $-i$ is at dista
nce $2$ from $i$).

On this path, $z-i = r e^{it}$, so $\arg(z-i)$ increases continuously from $0$ t
o $2\pi$ as $t$ goes from $0$ to $2\pi$. Meanwhile $z+i = 2i + r e^{it}$, which
traces a circle of radius $r$ centered at $2i$; since $r < 2$, this circle does
**not** enclose the origin, so $\arg(z+i)$ returns to its starting value (net ch
ange $0$).

### Tracking $w$ continuously

Define the continuous branch on the starting sheet with $w(\gamma(0)) = \sqrt{r}
\sqrt{2i}$ (principal values). Along $\gamma$:

$$w(\gamma(t)) = \sqrt{r}\, e^{it/2}\cdot \sqrt{2i + r e^{it}}.$$

- The factor $\sqrt{r}\,e^{it/2}$ is continuous in $t$ and at $t=2\pi$ equals $\
sqrt{r}\,e^{i\pi} = -\sqrt{r}$, i.e. it picks up a factor $e^{i\pi}=-1$.
- The factor $\sqrt{2i+re^{it}}$ is continuous for all $t\in[0,2\pi]$ because $2
i+re^{it}$ never crosses the branch cut of $\sqrt{\cdot}$ (it stays in a neighbo
rhood of $2i$, away from the origin and the cut), and returns to its original va
lue at $t=2\pi$.

Therefore:
$$w(\gamma(2\pi)) = -\sqrt{r}\,\sqrt{2i} = -w(\gamma(0)).$$

**Continuity verification:** Every factor above is a continuous function of $t$
on $[0,2\pi]$:
- $re^{it}$ is continuous, so $\sqrt{r}e^{it/2}$ is continuous (the square root
branch is continuous along the circle since it never hits the cut).
- $2i+re^{it}$ is continuous and stays in a simply-connected region avoiding the
 branch cut, so its square root (chosen continuously) is continuous.
- The product of continuous functions is continuous.

Hence $w(\gamma(t))$ varies **continuously** along the entire closed path. The s
ign flip $w\mapsto -w$ at $t=2\pi$ is **not** a discontinuity — it reflects that
 we have moved to the **second sheet** of the Riemann surface. On the surface it
self (where the two sheets are glued along the cuts), the point $\gamma(2\pi)$ o
n sheet 2 is a *different point* from $\gamma(0)$ on sheet 1, so there is no con
tradiction with continuity.

### Geometric interpretation

The monodromy around $+i$ alone is a single transposition of the two sheets (sig
n change), because encircling one branch point of $\sqrt{(z-i)(z+i)}$ crosses on
e branch cut. Encircling **both** $\pm i$ (e.g. a large circle $|z|=R>1$) would
cross two cuts, giving monodromy $(-1)^2 = +1$: the function returns to its orig
inal value, consistent with $\sqrt{z^2+1}\sim z$ at infinity being single-valued
 there.

### Summary

Along $\gamma(t)=i+re^{it}$ ($0<r<2$), the value
$$w(\gamma(t))=\sqrt{r}\,e^{it/2}\sqrt{2i+re^{it}}$$
is a product of continuous functions of $t$, hence varies continuously. After on
e full loop the value becomes $-w(\gamma(0))$, which on the two-sheeted Riemann
surface corresponds to arriving at the antipodal sheet — a continuous transport,
 not a jump. This confirms that $\sqrt{z^2+1}$ is continuous (indeed holomorphic
) along paths on its Riemann surface, with the sign change encoding the sheet tr
ansition across the branch cut emanating from $+i$.
