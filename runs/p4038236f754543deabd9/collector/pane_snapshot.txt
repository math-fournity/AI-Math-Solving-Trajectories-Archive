Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest $n = k$ such that every 2-c
oloring of a $3 \times n$ board contains a rectangle (formed by 2 rows and 2 col
umns) whose four corners are all the same color.

## Proof

**Setup.** Encode colors as $0$ (black) and $1$ (white). Each column of the $3 \
times n$ board is a vector in $\{0,1\}^3$. A *monochromatic rectangle* is determ
ined by choosing 2 rows $i, j$ and 2 columns $c, c'$ such that all four cells $(
i,c), (j,c), (i,c'), (j,c')$ share the same color.

**Key definition.** Define six **slots**: for each pair of rows $p \in \{(1,2),
(1,3), (2,3)\}$ and each color $a \in \{0, 1\}$, the slot $(p, a)$. We say a col
umn **occupies** slot $(p, a)$ if both cells in rows $p$ of that column have col
or $a$.

**Observation 1 (monochromatic rectangle criterion).** Two columns form a monoch
romatic rectangle if and only if they occupy a common slot. Indeed, if columns $
c, c'$ both occupy slot $((i,j), a)$, then cells $(i,c), (j,c), (i,c'), (j,c')$
are all color $a$.

**Observation 2 (every column occupies at least one slot).** Each column has 3 c
ells colored with 2 colors. By the pigeonhole principle, at least two of the thr
ee cells share the same color $a$, corresponding to some pair of rows $p$. Hence
 the column occupies slot $(p, a)$.

**Upper bound: $n = 7$ forces a monochromatic rectangle.**

Suppose for contradiction that a $3 \times 7$ coloring has no monochromatic rect
angle. By Observation 1, no two columns occupy a common slot. By Observation 2,
each of the 7 columns occupies a non-empty set of slots. Since no two columns sh
are a slot, these 7 non-empty sets are pairwise disjoint subsets of a 6-element
set (the six slots). But 7 pairwise disjoint non-empty subsets of a 6-element se
t is impossible — they would require at least 7 distinct elements. Contradiction
.

Therefore every coloring of a $3 \times 7$ board contains a monochromatic rectan
gle.

**Lower bound: $n = 6$ does not force a monochromatic rectangle.**

We exhibit a coloring of a $3 \times 6$ board with no monochromatic rectangle. U
se the six column vectors that have exactly one cell of one color and two of the
 other (i.e., all non-constant vectors in $\{0,1\}^3$):

| Column | Row 1 | Row 2 | Row 3 | Slot occupied |
|--------|-------|-------|-------|---------------|
| 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 | $((1,2), 0)$ |
| 2 | 0 | 1 | 0 | $((1,3), 0)$ |
| 3 | 1 | 0 | 0 | $((2,3), 0)$ |
| 4 | 0 | 1 | 1 | $((2,3), 1)$ |
| 5 | 1 | 0 | 1 | $((1,3), 1)$ |
| 6 | 1 | 1 | 0 | $((1,2), 1)$ |

Each column occupies **exactly one** slot, and the six columns occupy the six di
stinct slots, one each. Since no two columns share a slot, by Observation 1 ther
e is no monochromatic rectangle. (One may verify directly: for each pair of rows
, the pattern $00$ appears in exactly one column and $11$ in exactly one column,
 so no monochromatic rectangle arises on any pair of rows.)

**Conclusion.** The value $n = 6$ admits a coloring with no monochromatic rectan
gle, while $n = 7$ forces one in every coloring. Therefore the smallest such $k$
 is:

$$\boxed{k = 7}$$
