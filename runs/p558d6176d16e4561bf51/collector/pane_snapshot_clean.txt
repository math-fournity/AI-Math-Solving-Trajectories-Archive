### Setup and definitions

In gravitational lensing, the lensing potential $\psi(\vec\theta)$ is a 2D scala
r field on the image plane. The deflection angle and the convergence are defined
 in terms of it by

$$\vec\alpha(\vec\theta) = \nabla_\theta\,\psi(\vec\theta), \qquad \kappa(\vec\t
heta) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\nabla^2_\theta\,\psi(\vec\theta),$$

where $\nabla^2_\theta = \partial^2/\partial\theta_1^2 + \partial^2/\partial\the
ta_2^2$ is the two-dimensional Laplacian. The second relation is the trace of th
e Hessian of $\psi$ (the lensing Jacobian), since $\kappa = \tfrac12(\psi_{,11}+
\psi_{,22})$.

Hence $\psi$ satisfies the **two-dimensional Poisson equation**

$$\boxed{\;\nabla^2_\theta\,\psi(\vec\theta) = 2\,\kappa(\vec\theta).\;}$$

### Green's function of the 2D Laplacian

The fundamental solution of $\nabla^2$ in two dimensions is

$$G(\vec\theta) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|\vec\theta|, \qquad \nabla^2 G(\vec\theta) =
 \delta^{(2)}(\vec\theta),$$

because $\nabla^2\ln|\vec\theta| = 2\pi\,\delta^{(2)}(\vec\theta)$ (verified by
applying the divergence theorem to a small disk of radius $\varepsilon$: $\int_{
|\vec\theta|<\varepsilon}\nabla^2\ln|\vec\theta|\,d^2\theta = \oint \partial_r\l
n r\,r\,d\varphi = \oint d\varphi = 2\pi$).

### Convolution solution

Convolving the Poisson equation with $G$ and using $\nabla^2(G*\psi) = (\nabla^2
 G)*\psi = \delta*\psi = \psi$,

$$\psi(\vec\theta) = \int 2\,\kappa(\vec\theta')\,G(\vec\theta-\vec\theta')\,d^2
\theta' = \int 2\,\kappa(\vec\theta')\,\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|\vec\theta-\vec\theta'|
\,d^2\theta'.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{\;\psi(\vec\theta) = \frac{1}{\pi}\int \kappa(\vec\theta')\,\ln\!\big|\
vec\theta-\vec\theta'\big|\,d^2\theta'.\;}$$
