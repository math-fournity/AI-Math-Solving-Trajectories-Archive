# Solution

We seek $c \in \mathbb{R}$ and $L > 0$ with $\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty} \fra
c{r^c \int_0^{\pi/2} x^r \sin x\,dx}{\int_0^{\pi/2} x^r \cos x\,dx} = L$.

**Step 1: Integration by parts relation.**

Let $I_s(r) = \int_0^{\pi/2} x^r \sin x\,dx$ and $I_c(r) = \int_0^{\pi/2} x^r \c
os x\,dx$.

Integrating $I_s(r)$ by parts ($u = x^r$, $dv = \sin x\,dx$):
$$I_s(r) = \bigl[-x^r \cos x\bigr]_0^{\pi/2} + r\int_0^{\pi/2} x^{r-1}\cos x\,dx
 = r\, I_c(r-1), \tag{1}$$
since $\cos(\pi/2)=0$ and $0^r\cos 0 = 0$ for $r>0$.

Integrating $I_c(r)$ by parts ($u = x^r$, $dv = \cos x\,dx$):
$$I_c(r) = \bigl[x^r \sin x\bigr]_0^{\pi/2} - r\int_0^{\pi/2} x^{r-1}\sin x\,dx
= \left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)^r - r\, I_s(r-1). \tag{2}$$

**Step 2: Laplace-type asymptotic for $I_c(r)$.**

The integrand $x^r \cos x = e^{r\ln x}\cos x$ is dominated near the maximum $x =
 \pi/2$ of $\ln x$ on $[0,\pi/2]$. Set $a = \pi/2$ and substitute $x = a - t$, $
t = \frac{a\,s}{r}$ ($dt = \frac{a}{r}ds$):

$$I_c(r) = a^r \frac{a}{r}\int_0^{r}\!\left(1-\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\!r}\!\sin\!\l
eft(\frac{as}{r}\right)ds.$$

For fixed $s$, $(1-s/r)^r \to e^{-s}$ and $\sin(as/r) \sim \frac{as}{r}$. By dom
inated convergence (the integrand is controlled by $e^{-s/2}\cdot \frac{as}{r}$
for large $r$):

$$I_c(r) \sim a^r \frac{a}{r}\cdot\frac{a}{r}\int_0^\infty s\,e^{-s}\,ds = a^r\f
rac{a^2}{r^2}\cdot 1 = \left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)^r\frac{\pi^2}{4r^2}. \tag{3}$
$

**Step 3: The ratio.**

From (1) and (3) (with $r\to r-1$, noting $I_c(r-1)\sim (\pi/2)^{r-1}\frac{\pi^2
}{4(r-1)^2}\sim (\pi/2)^{r-1}\frac{\pi^2}{4r^2}$):

$$\frac{I_s(r)}{I_c(r)} = \frac{r\, I_c(r-1)}{I_c(r)} \sim \frac{r\cdot (\pi/2)^
{r-1}\frac{\pi^2}{4r^2}}{(\pi/2)^r\frac{\pi^2}{4r^2}} = \frac{r}{\pi/2} = \frac{
2r}{\pi}. \tag{4}$$

**Step 4: Determining $c$ and $L$.**

$$\frac{r^c\, I_s(r)}{I_c(r)} \sim r^c\cdot\frac{2r}{\pi} = \frac{2}{\pi}\,r^{c+
1}.$$

For a finite positive limit, we need $c+1=0$, i.e. $\boxed{c = -1}$, giving

$$L = \frac{2}{\pi}.$$

**Verification of the limit (confirming no lower-order correction shifts the val
ue).** Expanding to next order: $I_s(r) \sim (\pi/2)^r\frac{\pi}{2r}\bigl(1 - \f
rac{1}{r}+O(r^{-2})\bigr)$ and $I_c(r)\sim (\pi/2)^r\frac{\pi^2}{4r^2}\bigl(1-\f
rac{3}{r}+O(r^{-2})\bigr)$, so

$$\frac{r^{-1}I_s(r)}{I_c(r)} \sim \frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\frac{1-1/r}{1-3/r} \to \fr
ac{2}{\pi}.$$

The $O(1/r)$ corrections vanish, confirming the limit.

$$c = -1, \qquad L = \frac{2}{\pi}.$$
