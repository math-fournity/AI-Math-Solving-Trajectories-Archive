## Proof

The binomial expansion of $(a+b)^n$ is

$$
(a+b)^n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k} a^{n-k} b^k.
$$

Denote the $(k+1)$-th monomial by $T_k = \binom{n}{k} a^{n-k} b^k$, for $k = 0,
1, \dots, n$. We seek the $k$ maximizing $T_k$.

**Ratio of consecutive monomials.** For $0 \le k \le n-1$,

$$
\frac{T_{k+1}}{T_k} = \frac{\binom{n}{k+1}}{\binom{n}{k}} \cdot \frac{b}{a} = \f
rac{n-k}{k+1} \cdot \frac{b}{a}.
$$

Therefore

$$
T_{k+1} \ge T_k \iff \frac{(n-k)\,b}{(k+1)\,a} \ge 1 \iff (n-k)b \ge (k+1)a \iff
 k \le \frac{nb - a}{a+b}.
$$

Set

$$
m := \frac{nb - a}{a+b}.
$$

Then:
- $T_{k+1} > T_k$ when $k < m$ (the sequence is strictly increasing),
- $T_{k+1} = T_k$ when $k = m$ (a tie, possible only if $m$ is a non-negative in
teger),
- $T_{k+1} < T_k$ when $k > m$ (the sequence is strictly decreasing).

**Range of $m$.** Since $a, b > 0$ and $n \ge 0$:
- $m \ge -1$: because $nb - a \ge -(a+b) \iff (n+1)b \ge 0$, which holds.
- $m < n$: because $nb - a < n(a+b) \iff -a < na$, which holds since $a > 0$.

Hence $-1 \le m < n$, so $\lfloor m \rfloor + 1 \in \{0, 1, \dots, n\}$, which i
s a valid index.

**Locating the maximum.** The sequence $T_0, T_1, \dots, T_n$ increases (non-str
ictly) for $k \le \lfloor m \rfloor$ and decreases (strictly) for $k > \lfloor m
 \rfloor$ (once past the plateau). Therefore the maximum is attained at

$$
\boxed{k^* = \left\lfloor \frac{nb - a}{a+b} \right\rfloor + 1,}
$$

giving the greatest monomial

$$
T_{k^*} = \binom{n}{\,k^*\,}\, a^{\,n - k^*}\, b^{\,k^*}.
$$

**Tie case.** If $m = \frac{nb-a}{a+b}$ is a non-negative integer, then $T_m = T
_{m+1}$ (equality at $k = m$), and both are greatest. In this case $\lfloor m \r
floor + 1 = m + 1$, and the equally greatest monomials are $T_m$ and $T_{m+1}$.

**Verification.**
- $a = b = 1,\ n = 4$: $m = 3/2$, $k^* = 2$; monomials $1, 4, 6, 4, 1$ — max is
$6 = \binom{4}{2}$. ✓
- $a = 1,\ b = 2,\ n = 3$: $m = 5/3$, $k^* = 2$; monomials $1, 6, 12, 8$ — max i
s $12 = \binom{3}{2}\cdot 4$. ✓
- $a = b = 1,\ n = 3$: $m = 1$ (integer); monomials $1, 3, 3, 1$ — both $T_1 = T
_2 = 3$ are greatest. ✓
