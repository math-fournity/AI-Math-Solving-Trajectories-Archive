## Proof

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains only the problem to be sol
ved, with no solution, solution sketch, formalization notes, or embedded answer.
 Proceeding.

---

Let $h = f - g$. Since $f, g \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^n, m)$, we have $h \in L^1(\math
bb{R}^n, m)$, and since $f = g$ $m$-a.e., we have $h = 0$ $m$-a.e.

**Key claim.** For every $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and every $r > 0$,
$$\int_{B(r,x)} h(y)\,dm(y) = 0.$$

*Proof of claim.* Since $h = 0$ $m$-a.e., the set $N = \{y : h(y) \neq 0\}$ sati
sfies $m(N) = 0$. The function $h$ is measurable (being the difference of two $L
^1$ functions), and $|h| \cdot \mathbf{1}_{B(r,x)}$ is a nonnegative measurable
function that equals $0$ $m$-a.e. Hence
$$\int_{B(r,x)} |h(y)|\,dm(y) = 0,$$
which forces $\int_{B(r,x)} h(y)\,dm(y) = 0$. $\square$

Since $m(B(r,x)) > 0$ for every $r > 0$ (balls have positive Lebesgue measure),
dividing gives
$$(A_r h)(x) = \frac{1}{m(B(r,x))}\int_{B(r,x)} h(y)\,dm(y) = 0 \qquad \text{for
 all } x \in \mathbb{R}^n,\ r > 0.$$

By linearity of the integral,
$$(A_r f)(x) - (A_r g)(x) = (A_r h)(x) = 0 \qquad \text{for all } x \in \mathbb{
R}^n,\ r > 0.$$

Therefore $(A_r f)(x) = (A_r g)(x)$ for **every** $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and **eve
ry** $r > 0$.

Now fix any $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$. The two families of real numbers $\{(A_r f)(x)
\}_{r>0}$ and $\{(A_r g)(x)\}_{r>0}$ are identical (term by term, for each $r$).
 A family of real numbers converges as $r \to 0$ if and only if the identical fa
mily converges. Hence
$$\lim_{r \to 0}(A_r f)(x) \text{ exists} \iff \lim_{r \to 0}(A_r g)(x) \text{ e
xists}.$$

Since this holds for every $x$,
$$C_f = \{x : \lim_{r\to 0}(A_r f)(x) \text{ exists}\} = \{x : \lim_{r\to 0}(A_r
 g)(x) \text{ exists}\} = C_g.$$

$\blacksquare$
