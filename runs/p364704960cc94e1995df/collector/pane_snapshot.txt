**Answer: No.** The condition $P_1\circ X_t^{-1}=P_2\circ Y_t^{-1}$ only equates
 the *one-dimensional marginals* of the two processes at each time $t$; it says
nothing about joint distributions. A process can share all one-time marginals wi
th a Markov process yet fail to be Markov. Here is an explicit counterexample.

---

**Construction.** Let $(Y_t)_{t\in\{0,1,2\}}$ be defined on any probability spac
e as three i.i.d. Bernoulli$(1/2)$ random variables taking values in $\{0,1\}\su
bset\mathbb{R}$. Being independent and identically distributed, $(Y_t)$ is a Mar
kov process: for every $t$, the conditional law of $Y_{t+1}$ given the past depe
nds only on $Y_t$ (in fact it is constant, equal to Bernoulli$(1/2)$, since the
variables are independent).

Now construct $(X_t)_{t\in\{0,1,2\}}$ on a probability space supporting two i.i.
d. Bernoulli$(1/2)$ variables $Z_0,Z_1$ by
$$X_0 := Z_0,\qquad X_1 := Z_1,\qquad X_2 := X_0\oplus X_1,$$
where $\oplus$ denotes addition modulo $2$ (XOR).

**Marginals match.** Each $X_t$ is Bernoulli$(1/2)$:
- $X_0=Z_0\sim\text{Bernoulli}(1/2)$,
- $X_1=Z_1\sim\text{Bernoulli}(1/2)$,
- $X_2=Z_0\oplus Z_1\sim\text{Bernoulli}(1/2)$ (XOR of two independent fair bits
 is a fair bit).

Since each $Y_t\sim\text{Bernoulli}(1/2)$ as well, we have
$$P_1\circ X_t^{-1}=P_2\circ Y_t^{-1}=\tfrac12\delta_0+\tfrac12\delta_1\qquad\te
xt{for every }t\in\{0,1,2\}.$$

**$(X_t)$ is not Markov.** Compare the conditional distributions of $X_2$:
$$P(X_2=1\mid X_1=0)=P(Z_0=1)=\tfrac12,$$
but
$$P(X_2=1\mid X_1=0,\,X_0=1)=P(Z_0\oplus Z_1=1\mid Z_1=0,\,Z_0=1)=P(1\oplus0=1)=
1.$$
Thus
$$P(X_2=1\mid X_1=0)\;=\;\tfrac12\;\neq\;1\;=\;P(X_2=1\mid X_1=0,\,X_0=1),$$
so the conditional law of the future state $X_2$ given the present $X_1$ is *not
* independent of the past $X_0$. The Markov property fails.

**Conclusion.** We have exhibited a Markov process $(Y_t)$ and a non-Markov proc
ess $(X_t)$ with identical one-dimensional marginals at every time. Therefore th
e equality $P_1\circ X_t^{-1}=P_2\circ Y_t^{-1}$ (as measures on $\mathbb{R}$, f
or each $t$) does **not** force $(X_t)$ to be Markov.
