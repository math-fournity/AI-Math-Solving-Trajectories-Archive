## Solution

We evaluate the limit
$$L = \lim_{(x,y)\to (0,0)} \frac{x^2y^2}{x^2+y^2}\left(1-\cos\!\left(\tfrac{1}{
x}\right)\cos\!\left(\tfrac{1}{y}\right)\right).$$

**Step 1: Bound the oscillatory factor.**

Since $|\cos(\cdot)|\le 1$, we have
$$\cos\!\left(\tfrac{1}{x}\right)\cos\!\left(\tfrac{1}{y}\right)\in[-1,1],$$
so
$$0\le 1-\cos\!\left(\tfrac{1}{x}\right)\cos\!\left(\tfrac{1}{y}\right)\le 2.$$

**Step 2: Bound the rational factor.**

By AM–GM, $x^2y^2\le \frac{(x^2+y^2)^2}{4}$, hence for $(x,y)\neq(0,0)$,
$$0\le \frac{x^2y^2}{x^2+y^2}\le \frac{x^2+y^2}{4}.$$

**Step 3: Apply the squeeze theorem.**

Combining the two bounds, the whole expression $E(x,y)$ satisfies
$$0\le E(x,y)=\frac{x^2y^2}{x^2+y^2}\left(1-\cos\!\left(\tfrac{1}{x}\right)\cos\
!\left(\tfrac{1}{y}\right)\right)\le \frac{x^2+y^2}{4}\cdot 2=\frac{x^2+y^2}{2}.
$$

As $(x,y)\to(0,0)$, the upper bound $\frac{x^2+y^2}{2}\to 0$. By the squeeze the
orem,
$$L = 0.$$

The limit exists and equals $\boxed{0}$.
