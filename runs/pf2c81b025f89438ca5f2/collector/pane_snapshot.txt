## Solution

We work over an algebraically closed field $k$. Recall that $k[[Z_1]]$ is a comp
lete DVR, hence in particular an **integral domain** of Krull dimension $1$. Con
sequently, any subring of $k[[Z_1]]$ is also an integral domain of dimension $\l
eq 1$. This gives us two obstructions to the existence of an injective homomorph
ism $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\hookrightarrow k[[Z_1]]$:

1. **Domain obstruction:** $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ must be an integral domain.
2. **Dimension obstruction:** $\dim \mathcal{O}_{X,x}\leq 1$ (automatically sati
sfied for a curve at a closed point).

We exploit the domain obstruction for the negative example and exhibit a singula
r-but-irreducible curve for the positive example.

---

### Positive example: the cusp

Let
$$X = \operatorname{Spec} k[x,y]/(y^2 - x^3), \qquad x_0 = (x,y) = (0,0).$$

The point $x_0$ is **singular**: the Jacobian matrix is $(\partial f/\partial x,
\; \partial f/\partial y) = (-3x^2,\; 2y)$, which vanishes at the origin.

The normalization of $X$ is $\mathbb{A}^1 = \operatorname{Spec} k[t]$, with the
map
$$\nu: \mathbb{A}^1 \to X, \qquad t \mapsto (t^2, t^3),$$
i.e. $x = t^2,\; y = t^3$. This identifies $k[x,y]/(y^2-x^3) \cong k[t^2, t^3] \
subset k[t]$.

The local ring at the singular point is
$$\mathcal{O}_{X,x_0} \;=\; k[t^2, t^3]_{(t^2,\, t^3)}.$$

Since $k[t^2,t^3]\subset k[t]$ and $k[t]$ is a domain, $\mathcal{O}_{X,x_0}$ is
an integral domain. We construct the injection as a composition:

**Step 1.** The inclusion $k[t^2, t^3] \hookrightarrow k[t]$ induces, after loca
lizing at the multiplicative set $S = k[t^2,t^3]\setminus (t^2,t^3)$, an injecti
ve map
$$\mathcal{O}_{X,x_0} = S^{-1}k[t^2,t^3] \;\hookrightarrow\; S^{-1}k[t].$$
(Injectivity is preserved because $k[t^2,t^3]\to k[t]$ is injective and localiza
tion is exact.) Note that $S^{-1}k[t] = k[t]_{(t)}$ since every element of $k[t^
2,t^3]\setminus(t^2,t^3)$ is a unit in $k[t]_{(t)}$.

**Step 2.** The natural map $k[t]_{(t)} \hookrightarrow k[[t]]$ from the local r
ing to its $(t)$-adic completion is injective (the local ring is a domain, and c
ompletion of a Noetherian local domain is faithful).

**Composition.** Setting $Z_1 = t$, we obtain
$$\mathcal{O}_{X,x_0} \;\hookrightarrow\; k[t]_{(t)} \;\hookrightarrow\; k[[t]]
= k[[Z_1]],$$
an injective $k$-algebra homomorphism from the local ring of a **singular** poin
t into a one-variable formal power series ring. $\checkmark$

### Negative example: the node (reducible singularity)

Let
$$X = \operatorname{Spec} k[x,y]/(xy), \qquad x_0 = (x,y) = (0,0).$$

The point $x_0$ is **singular** (the Jacobian $(y, x)$ vanishes at the origin).
The curve $X$ is the union of the two coordinate axes, hence is **reducible** at
 $x_0$.

The local ring is
$$\mathcal{O}_{X,x_0} \;=\; \bigl(k[x,y]/(xy)\bigr)_{(x,y)}.$$

In this ring, the images $\bar{x}$ and $\bar{y}$ satisfy $\bar{x}\cdot\bar{y} =
0$, yet $\bar{x}\neq 0$ and $\bar{y}\neq 0$ (neither lies in the maximal ideal's
 radical in a way that makes it zero — concretely, $\bar{x}$ maps to a nonzero e
lement in the residue field of the branch $y=0$, and similarly for $\bar{y}$). T
hus $\mathcal{O}_{X,x_0}$ **has zero divisors** and is not an integral domain.

Now suppose, for contradiction, that there exists an injective $k$-algebra homom
orphism
$$\varphi: \mathcal{O}_{X,x_0} \hookrightarrow k[[Z_1]].$$

Since $k[[Z_1]]$ is an integral domain, any subring of it is also an integral do
main. But $\varphi$ injective would make $\mathcal{O}_{X,x_0}$ isomorphic to a s
ubring of $k[[Z_1]]$, forcing $\mathcal{O}_{X,x_0}$ to be a domain — contradicti
ng the existence of zero divisors $\bar{x}\bar{y}=0$ with $\bar{x},\bar{y}\neq 0
$.

Therefore **no injective homomorphism** $\mathcal{O}_{X,x_0}\to k[[Z_1]]$ exists
. $\checkmark$

### Summary

| | Curve $X$ | Point $x$ | Singular? | $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ a domain? | Injectio
n into $k[[Z_1]]$? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Positive | $y^2 = x^3$ (cusp) | origin | yes | yes (irreducible) | **yes** |
| Negative | $xy = 0$ (two lines) | origin | yes | no (reducible) | **no** |

The key distinction is **irreducibility of the singularity**: the cusp is a unib
ranch (irreducible) singularity whose local ring is a domain and embeds into its
 normalization $k[[t]]$; the node is a multibranch (reducible) singularity whose
 local ring has zero divisors and therefore cannot embed into the domain $k[[Z_1
]]$.
