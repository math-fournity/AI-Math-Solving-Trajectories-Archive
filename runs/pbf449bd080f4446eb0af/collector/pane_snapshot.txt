Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $C(X)$ embeds densely into
$C(\mathcal{M}(X))$ under the natural embedding, where $X$ is compact and $\math
cal{M}(X)$ is the space of probability measures on $X$ with the weak* topology.

**Answer: No.** Here is the proof.

---

## Setup and the natural embedding

Let $X$ be a compact Hausdorff space, and let $\mathcal{M}(X)$ denote the space
of Borel probability measures on $X$, equipped with the weak* topology (i.e., $\
mu_\alpha \to \mu$ iff $\int f\,d\mu_\alpha \to \int f\,d\mu$ for all $f \in C(X
)$). By the Banach–Alaoglu theorem, $\mathcal{M}(X)$ is a compact Hausdorff spac
e (it is a weak*-closed subset of the unit ball of $C(X)^*$).

The natural embedding $\Phi: C(X) \hookrightarrow C(\mathcal{M}(X))$ is defined
by
$$\Phi(f)(\mu) = \hat{f}(\mu) := \int_X f\,d\mu, \qquad \mu \in \mathcal{M}(X).$
$
This is well-defined and continuous: $\hat{f}$ is continuous on $\mathcal{M}(X)$
 by definition of the weak* topology, and $\|\hat{f}\|_\infty = \|f\|_\infty$ (s
ince $|\int f\,d\mu| \le \|f\|_\infty$ and equality is attained at Dirac measure
s).

The question is whether $\Phi(C(X))$ is dense in $C(\mathcal{M}(X))$ with respec
t to the sup norm.

## Key observation: the image consists of affine functions

For each $f \in C(X)$, the function $\hat{f}: \mathcal{M}(X) \to \mathbb{R}$ is
**affine**: for $\mu, \nu \in \mathcal{M}(X)$ and $t \in [0,1]$,
$$\hat{f}(t\mu + (1-t)\nu) = \int f\,d(t\mu+(1-t)\nu) = t\int f\,d\mu + (1-t)\in
t f\,d\nu = t\hat{f}(\mu) + (1-t)\hat{f}(\nu).$$

Let $A(\mathcal{M}(X)) \subset C(\mathcal{M}(X))$ denote the closed subspace of
continuous affine functions. The space of affine functions is closed under unifo
rm limits (a uniform limit of affine functions is affine, since affinity is char
acterized by the closed condition $g(t\mu+(1-t)\nu) = tg(\mu)+(1-t)g(\nu)$). The
refore:
$$\overline{\Phi(C(X))} \subseteq A(\mathcal{M}(X)).$$

## The image equals all continuous affine functions

We show $\Phi(C(X)) = A(\mathcal{M}(X))$, so the closure is exactly $A(\mathcal{
M}(X))$.

The Dirac map $\delta: X \to \mathcal{M}(X)$, $x \mapsto \delta_x$, is continuou
s (since $\delta_x(f) = f(x)$). Given $g \in A(\mathcal{M}(X))$, define $f(x) :=
 g(\delta_x)$. Then $f \in C(X)$.

Every $\mu \in \mathcal{M}(X)$ is the barycenter of the Dirac measures: $\mu = \
int_X \delta_x\,d\mu(x)$, meaning for every $h \in C(X)$, $\int h\,d\mu = \int h
(x)\,d\mu(x) = \int \delta_x(h)\,d\mu(x)$.

Since $g$ is continuous and affine, by approximating the integral with finite co
nvex combinations (valid since $\mathcal{M}(X)$ is compact and $g$ is continuous
) and using affinity:
$$g(\mu) = g\!\left(\int_X \delta_x\,d\mu(x)\right) = \int_X g(\delta_x)\,d\mu(x
) = \int_X f\,d\mu = \hat{f}(\mu).$$

Thus $g = \hat{f} = \Phi(f)$, proving $A(\mathcal{M}(X)) \subseteq \Phi(C(X))$.
Combined with the earlier inclusion, $\Phi(C(X)) = A(\mathcal{M}(X))$, and so:
$$\overline{\Phi(C(X))} = A(\mathcal{M}(X)).$$

## $A(\mathcal{M}(X))$ is a proper subspace whenever $|X| \geq 2$

It remains to show that $A(\mathcal{M}(X)) \subsetneq C(\mathcal{M}(X))$ in gene
ral.

**Counterexample.** Take $X = \{0, 1\}$ (a two-point discrete space, which is co
mpact). Then every probability measure on $X$ is of the form $\mu = (1-t)\delta_
0 + t\delta_1$ for a unique $t \in [0,1]$. The map $\mu \mapsto t = \mu(\{1\})$
is a homeomorphism $\mathcal{M}(X) \cong [0,1]$.

Under this identification, for $f \in C(X) = \mathbb{R}^2$ (say $f = (a, b)$ mea
ning $f(0)=a, f(1)=b$):
$$\hat{f}(\mu) = (1-t)a + tb = a + (b-a)t.$$
So $\Phi(C(X))$ is the 2-dimensional space of affine (linear) functions $\{a + (
b-a)t : a, b \in \mathbb{R}\}$ on $[0,1]$.

This is a **finite-dimensional** (hence closed) **proper** subspace of $C([0,1])
$. For instance, $h(t) = t^2 \in C([0,1])$ is not affine. The distance from $h$
to the affine functions is positive: the best uniform approximation of $t^2$ by
a linear function $at + b$ on $[0,1]$ has error $\geq 1/4 > 0$ (the minimax erro
r for degree-1 approximation of $t^2$ on $[0,1]$ is $1/8$ by Chebyshev approxima
tion, but in any case it is strictly positive).

Therefore $\overline{\Phi(C(X))} = A(\mathcal{M}(X)) \subsetneq C(\mathcal{M}(X)
)$, and $C(X)$ is **not** dense in $C(\mathcal{M}(X))$.

## Conclusion

The closure of $C(X)$ in $C(\mathcal{M}(X))$ under the natural embedding $f \map
sto \hat{f}$ is precisely the space of continuous affine functions $A(\mathcal{M
}(X))$. This is a proper closed subspace of $C(\mathcal{M}(X))$ whenever $X$ has
 at least two points (as demonstrated by $X = \{0,1\}$, where $A(\mathcal{M}(X))
$ is the 2-dimensional space of linear functions on $[0,1]$, a proper subspace o
f $C([0,1])$).

**No, $C(X)$ is not dense in $C(\mathcal{M}(X))$ in general.**
