Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest $p > 15$ such that no matte
r how qualifications are arranged (each company qualifies exactly $p$ of 300 app
licants, every applicant qualified by ≥ 1 company), a valid assignment always ex
ists.

## Proof

**Setup.** We have 20 companies each needing 15 applicants, and 300 applicants e
ach assigned to exactly one company. This is a bipartite assignment problem. By
Hall's marriage theorem (generalized), a perfect assignment exists if and only i
f for every subset $S$ of $k$ companies, the set $N(S)$ of applicants qualified
by at least one company in $S$ satisfies $|N(S)| \geq 15k$.

**Goal.** Find the smallest $p$ such that Hall's condition is *always* satisfied
, regardless of the qualification configuration.

### Part 1: $p = 285$ always works

For any subset $S$ of $k$ companies:

- **If $k \leq 19$:** Any single company in $S$ qualifies 285 applicants, all be
longing to $N(S)$, so $|N(S)| \geq 285$. Since $k \leq 19$, we have $15k \leq 28
5$, thus $|N(S)| \geq 285 \geq 15k$. ✓

- **If $k = 20$:** Every applicant is qualified by at least one company (given),
 and all companies are in $S$, so $N(S)$ is the entire set of 300 applicants: $|
N(S)| = 300 \geq 15 \cdot 20 = 300$. ✓

Hall's condition holds for all subsets, so a valid assignment always exists when
 $p = 285$.

### Part 2: $p = 284$ can fail

**Counterexample.** Construct the following configuration with $p = 284$:

- **Companies $C_1, \ldots, C_{19}$:** Each qualifies the same set $\{a_1, a_2,
\ldots, a_{284}\}$.
- **Company $C_{20}$:** Qualifies $\{a_{285}, \ldots, a_{300}\} \cup \{a_1, \ldo
ts, a_{268}\}$, which is $16 + 268 = 284$ applicants.

**Verification of constraints:**
- Each company qualifies exactly 284 applicants. ✓
- Each applicant is qualified by at least one company: $a_1, \ldots, a_{284}$ by
 $C_1,\ldots,C_{19}$; $a_{285}, \ldots, a_{300}$ by $C_{20}$. ✓

**Hall violation:** Take $S = \{C_1, \ldots, C_{19}\}$ ($k = 19$). Then $N(S) =
\{a_1, \ldots, a_{284}\}$, so $|N(S)| = 284$. But we need $|N(S)| \geq 15 \cdot
19 = 285$. Since $284 < 285$, Hall's condition fails. The 19 companies require $
19 \times 15 = 285$ distinct applicants but only have access to 284. No valid as
signment exists.

### Part 3: $p = 284$ is the largest failing value

For a Hall violation with subset size $k$ ($1 \leq k \leq 19$), we need a union
size $U$ with:
1. $U \geq p$ (each of the $k$ companies has $p$ qualified applicants in $N(S)$)
,
2. $U \leq 15k - 1$ (Hall violation),
3. $(20 - k)p \geq 300 - U$ (remaining $20-k$ companies can cover the $300 - U$
remaining applicants).

Combining (2) and (3): $(20-k)p \geq 301 - 15k$, i.e., $p \geq \frac{301 - 15k}{
20 - k}$.

Combining (1) and (2): $p \leq 15k - 1$.

So a Hall violation is possible for a given $p$ iff there exists $k \in \{1, \ld
ots, 19\}$ with:
$$\frac{301 - 15k}{20 - k} \leq p \leq 15k - 1.$$

For $k = 19$: the range is $\left[\frac{301 - 285}{1}, 284\right] = [16, 284]$,
which already covers all integers from 16 to 284. For smaller $k$, the ranges ar
e subintervals within $[16, 284]$. Thus every $p$ with $16 \leq p \leq 284$ admi
ts a Hall violation, while $p \geq 285$ does not.

### Conclusion

The smallest value of $p$ for which a valid assignment is always possible is $\b
oxed{285}$.
