## Proof

Let $(B_t)_{t\ge 0}$ be a standard Brownian motion with $B_0=0$, and let $a<0<b$
. Define
$$T_{a,b}=\inf\{t>0:B_t\notin(a,b)\}.$$
We claim
$$\mathbf{E}[T_{a,b}]=-ab.$$

**Step 1. A useful martingale.** The process
$$M_t:=B_t^2-t$$
is a martingale with respect to the natural filtration of $B$. Indeed, $\mathbf{
E}[B_t^2]=t$ and the increments $B_t^2-t$ have zero conditional mean, which is v
erified by the standard computation $\mathbf{E}[B_t^2\mid\mathcal F_s]=B_s^2+(t-
s)$ for $s\le t$.

**Step 2. Apply optional stopping to the bounded process $B_{t\wedge T_{a,b}}$.*
* Since $t\wedge T_{a,b}$ is a bounded stopping time and $M_t$ is a martingale,
the optional stopping theorem gives
$$\mathbf{E}\!\left[B_{t\wedge T_{a,b}}^{\,2}-(t\wedge T_{a,b})\right]=\mathbf{E
}[M_0]=0,$$
i.e.
$$\mathbf{E}[t\wedge T_{a,b}]=\mathbf{E}\!\left[B_{t\wedge T_{a,b}}^{\,2}\right]
.\tag{1}$$

**Step 3. Take $t\to\infty$ on both sides.**

- *Left-hand side.* Since $t\wedge T_{a,b}\nearrow T_{a,b}$ monotonically as $t\
to\infty$, the monotone convergence theorem yields
$$\mathbf{E}[t\wedge T_{a,b}]\;\nearrow\;\mathbf{E}[T_{a,b}].$$

- *Right-hand side.* For every $t\ge 0$, the stopped process satisfies $a\le B_{
t\wedge T_{a,b}}\le b$, so $B_{t\wedge T_{a,b}}^{\,2}\le \max(a^2,b^2)$, a unifo
rm integrable bound. Moreover $B_{t\wedge T_{a,b}}\to B_{T_{a,b}}$ a.s. (since $
T_{a,b}<\infty$ a.s., a standard fact for Brownian motion in a bounded interval)
. By dominated convergence,
$$\mathbf{E}\!\left[B_{t\wedge T_{a,b}}^{\,2}\right]\;\to\;\mathbf{E}\!\left[B_{
T_{a,b}}^{\,2}\right].$$

Combining with (1),
$$\mathbf{E}[T_{a,b}]=\mathbf{E}\!\left[B_{T_{a,b}}^{\,2}\right].\tag{2}$$

**Step 4. Distribution of $B_{T_{a,b}}$.** The exit value is $B_{T_{a,b}}\in\{a,
b\}$. Applying the optional stopping theorem to the bounded martingale $B_{t\wed
ge T_{a,b}}$ (with $|B_{t\wedge T_{a,b}}|\le\max(|a|,|b|)$) and letting $t\to\in
fty$ by dominated convergence gives
$$0=\mathbf{E}[B_0]=\mathbf{E}[B_{T_{a,b}}]=a\,\mathbf P(B_{T_{a,b}}=a)+b\,\math
bf P(B_{T_{a,b}}=b).$$
Together with $\mathbf P(B_{T_{a,b}}=a)+\mathbf P(B_{T_{a,b}}=b)=1$, this solves
 to
$$\mathbf P(B_{T_{a,b}}=a)=\frac{b}{b-a},\qquad \mathbf P(B_{T_{a,b}}=b)=\frac{-
a}{b-a}.$$

**Step 5. Compute $\mathbf{E}[B_{T_{a,b}}^{\,2}]$.** Using the distribution abov
e,
$$\mathbf{E}\!\left[B_{T_{a,b}}^{\,2}\right]=a^{2}\cdot\frac{b}{b-a}+b^{2}\cdot\
frac{-a}{b-a}=\frac{a^{2}b-ab^{2}}{b-a}=\frac{ab\,(a-b)}{b-a}=-ab.$$

**Step 6. Conclusion.** Substituting into (2),
$$\boxed{\mathbf{E}[T_{a,b}]=-ab.}$$

Since $a<0<b$, we have $-ab>0$, consistent with $T_{a,b}$ being a positive rando
