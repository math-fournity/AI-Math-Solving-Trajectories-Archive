Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether there exists a sequence **a
** such that all iterated partial sums $T^p(\mathbf{a})$ are bounded.

**Answer:** The only such sequence is $\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{0}$. No nontrivial s
equence exists.

---

## Proof

**Setup and formula.** Define $b^{(p)}_n := T^p(\mathbf{a})_n$. We first establi
sh by induction that

$$b^{(p)}_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n-k+p-1}{n-k}\, a_k. \tag{$\star$}$$

*Base case* ($p=0$): The only surviving term is $k=n$, giving $a_n = b^{(0)}_n$.
 ✓

*Inductive step:* Assuming $(\star)$ for $p$,
$$b^{(p+1)}_n = \sum_{j=0}^{n} b^{(p)}_j = \sum_{j=0}^{n} \sum_{k=0}^{j} \binom{
j-k+p-1}{j-k} a_k = \sum_{k=0}^{n} a_k \sum_{i=0}^{n-k} \binom{i+p-1}{i},$$
where $i = j-k$. By the hockey-stick identity, $\sum_{i=0}^{N} \binom{i+p-1}{i}
= \binom{N+p}{N}$, so
$$b^{(p+1)}_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n-k+p}{n-k} a_k,$$
which is $(\star)$ with $p$ replaced by $p+1$. ✓

**The zero sequence works.** If $\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{0}$, then $T^p(\mathbf{0})
 = \mathbf{0}$ for every $p \geq 0$, which is bounded.

**No nontrivial sequence works.** Suppose for contradiction that $\mathbf{a} \ne
q \mathbf{0}$ and $T^p(\mathbf{a})$ is bounded for all $p \geq 0$. Let

$$m := \min\{\, k \geq 0 : a_k \neq 0 \,\}.$$

Apply formula $(\star)$ with $n = m+1$. Since $a_k = 0$ for all $k < m$, only th
e terms $k = m$ and $k = m+1$ survive:

$$b^{(p)}_{m+1} = a_m \binom{(m+1)-m+p-1}{(m+1)-m} + a_{m+1}\binom{p-1}{0} = a_m
 \binom{p}{1} + a_{m+1} = a_m \cdot p + a_{m+1}.$$

Since $a_m \neq 0$, this is a **linear function of $p$ with nonzero slope**. The
refore

$$\left|b^{(p)}_{m+1}\right| = |a_m \cdot p + a_{m+1}| \xrightarrow{p \to \infty
} \infty.$$

But boundedness of $T^p(\mathbf{a})$ requires $\sup_n |b^{(p)}_n| < \infty$ for
each $p$, and in particular

$$\sup_n |b^{(p)}_n| \geq |b^{(p)}_{m+1}| \xrightarrow{p \to \infty} \infty.$$

This contradicts the assumption that $T^p(\mathbf{a})$ is bounded for all $p \ge
q 0$.

**Conclusion.** The assumption $\mathbf{a} \neq \mathbf{0}$ leads to a contradic
tion. Therefore $\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{0}$ is the **unique** sequence for which $
T^p(\mathbf{a})$ is bounded for every $p \geq 0$. $\blacksquare$
