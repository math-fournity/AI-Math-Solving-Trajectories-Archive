## Solution

### Setup

Consider a rod (Euler–Bernoulli beam) of length $l$, hinged at both ends, with f
lexural rigidity $EI$ and mass per unit length $\mu = \rho A$. A constant transv
erse force $P$ moves along the rod from $x=0$ to $x=l$ with constant velocity $v
_0$, starting at $t=0$.

The equation of transverse vibrations is:

$$EI\,\frac{\partial^4 w}{\partial x^4} + \mu\,\frac{\partial^2 w}{\partial t^2}
 = P\,\delta(x - v_0 t), \qquad 0 \le t \le \frac{l}{v_0}$$

**Boundary conditions** (hinged ends):

$$w(0,t)=0,\quad w''(0,t)=0,\quad w(l,t)=0,\quad w''(l,t)=0$$

**Initial conditions:**

$$w(x,0)=0,\quad \dot w(x,0)=0$$

### Eigenfunction expansion

For a simply-supported beam, the eigenfunctions and natural frequencies are:

$$\phi_n(x) = \sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}, \qquad \omega_n = \frac{n^2\pi^2}{l^2}\sqrt{
\frac{EI}{\mu}}, \qquad n=1,2,3,\dots$$

Set $\alpha = \sqrt{EI/\mu}$, so $\omega_n = \frac{n^2\pi^2\alpha}{l^2}$.

Expand the solution:

$$w(x,t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} T_n(t)\,\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}$$

Substituting into the PDE, multiplying by $\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}$, and integratin
g over $[0,l]$ using orthogonality $\left(\int_0^l \sin^2\frac{n\pi x}{l}\,dx =
\frac{l}{2}\right)$ and the sifting property of the delta function:

$$\ddot T_n + \omega_n^2\, T_n = \frac{2P}{\mu l}\,\sin\frac{n\pi v_0 t}{l}$$

Define the **forcing frequency** in the $n$-th mode:

$$\Omega_n = \frac{n\pi v_0}{l}$$

The ODE becomes:

$$\ddot T_n + \omega_n^2\, T_n = \frac{2P}{\mu l}\,\sin(\Omega_n t), \qquad T_n(
0)=0,\;\dot T_n(0)=0$$

### Phase I: Forced vibrations ($0 \le t \le l/v_0$)

**Non-resonant case** ($\Omega_n \ne \omega_n$, i.e., $v_0 \ne \frac{n\pi\alpha}
{l}$):

$$\boxed{T_n(t) = \frac{2P}{\mu l\,(\omega_n^2 - \Omega_n^2)}\left[\sin(\Omega_n
 t) - \frac{\Omega_n}{\omega_n}\sin(\omega_n t)\right]}$$

where

$$\omega_n^2 - \Omega_n^2 = \frac{n^2\pi^2}{l^2}\!\left(\frac{n^2\pi^2\alpha^2}{
l^2} - v_0^2\right), \qquad \frac{\Omega_n}{\omega_n} = \frac{v_0\, l}{n\pi\alph
a}$$

**Resonant case** ($\Omega_n = \omega_n$, i.e., $v_0 = \frac{n\pi\alpha}{l}$ — t
he **critical velocity** for mode $n$):

$$T_n(t) = \frac{P}{\mu l\,\omega_n}\left[\frac{\sin(\omega_n t)}{\omega_n} - t\
cos(\omega_n t)\right]$$

The deflection during Phase I is:

$$\boxed{w(x,t) = \frac{2P}{\mu l}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}}
{\omega_n^2 - \Omega_n^2}\left[\sin\frac{n\pi v_0 t}{l} - \frac{v_0 l}{n\pi\alph
a}\sin\!\left(\frac{n^2\pi^2\alpha}{l^2}\,t\right)\right]}$$

### Phase II: Free vibrations ($t > l/v_0$)

After the force leaves the rod at $t = l/v_0$, the rod vibrates freely. We compu
te the state at $t_0 = l/v_0$:

Since $\sin(\Omega_n \cdot l/v_0) = \sin(n\pi) = 0$ and $\cos(\Omega_n \cdot l/v
_0) = (-1)^n$:

$$T_n(t_0) = -\frac{2P\,\Omega_n}{\mu l\,\omega_n\,(\omega_n^2 - \Omega_n^2)}\,\
sin\!\left(\omega_n\frac{l}{v_0}\right)$$

$$\dot T_n(t_0) = \frac{2P\,\Omega_n}{\mu l\,(\omega_n^2 - \Omega_n^2)}\left[(-1
)^n - \cos\!\left(\omega_n\frac{l}{v_0}\right)\right]$$

For $t > l/v_0$, with $\tau = t - l/v_0$, each mode undergoes free oscillation:

$$T_n(t) = T_n(t_0)\cos(\omega_n\tau) + \frac{\dot T_n(t_0)}{\omega_n}\sin(\omeg
a_n\tau)$$

Substituting and using the identity $-\sin A\cos B - \cos A\sin B = -\sin(A+B)$
with $A = \omega_n l/v_0$, $B = \omega_n\tau$, so $A+B = \omega_n t$:

$$\boxed{T_n(t) = \frac{2P\,\Omega_n}{\mu l\,\omega_n\,(\omega_n^2 - \Omega_n^2)
}\left[(-1)^n\sin\!\left(\omega_n\!\left(t - \frac{l}{v_0}\right)\right) - \sin(
\omega_n t)\right], \qquad t > \frac{l}{v_0}}$$

The free-vibration deflection is:

$$\boxed{w(x,t) = \frac{2P\,v_0}{\mu\,\alpha\,l^2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n\
,(\omega_n^2 - \Omega_n^2)}\left[(-1)^n\sin\!\left(\omega_n\!\left(t-\frac{l}{v_
0}\right)\right) - \sin(\omega_n t)\right]\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}}$$

where I used $\Omega_n/\omega_n = v_0 l/(n\pi\alpha)$.

### Physical interpretation

- **Phase I** ($0 \le t \le l/v_0$): Each mode is a forced oscillator driven at
frequency $\Omega_n = n\pi v_0/l$. The response is a superposition of the forced
 oscillation (at frequency $\Omega_n$) and the free oscillation (at the natural
frequency $\omega_n$) needed to satisfy zero initial conditions.

- **Phase II** ($t > l/v_0$): The force has left the rod. Each mode oscillates f
reely at its natural frequency $\omega_n$, with amplitude determined by the ener
gy imparted during Phase I.

- **Resonance**: When the travel velocity equals a **critical velocity** $v_{0,\
text{cr}}^{(n)} = \frac{n\pi}{l}\sqrt{\frac{EI}{\mu}}$, the forcing frequency $\
Omega_n$ coincides with the natural frequency $\omega_n$, and the $n$-th mode gr
ows linearly in amplitude (secular term $t\cos\omega_n t$), leading to large def
lections.
