**Answer: Yes, every subset of $X$ is compact.**

**Proof.**

**Step 1: $X$ is compact.**

The empty set $\emptyset$ is compact in any topological space. Applying the hypo
thesis with $A = \emptyset$, we get $X \setminus \emptyset = X$ is compact. ∎

**Step 2: Every closed subset of $X$ is compact.**

This is the standard fact that closed subsets of a compact space are compact. Fo
r completeness: if $C \subseteq X$ is closed and $\{U_i\}_{i \in I}$ is a cover
of $C$ by open sets in $X$, then $\{U_i\}_{i \in I} \cup \{X \setminus C\}$ is a
n open cover of $X$. Since $X$ is compact, it has a finite subcover; discarding
$X \setminus C$ if it appears yields a finite subcover of $C$. ∎

**Step 3: Every open subset of $X$ is compact.**

Let $U \subseteq X$ be open. Then $C = X \setminus U$ is closed, hence compact b
y Step 2. By the hypothesis (applied to $A = C$), $X \setminus C = U$ is compact
. ∎

**Step 4: Every subset of $X$ is compact.**

Let $B \subseteq X$ be arbitrary, and let $\{U_i\}_{i \in I}$ be any cover of $B
$ by open sets in $X$. Set
$$W = \bigcup_{i \in I} U_i.$$
Then $W$ is open, so $W$ is compact by Step 3. Since $B \subseteq W$, the family
 $\{U_i\}_{i \in I}$ is an open cover of $W$ (every point of $W$ lies in some $U
_i$, and each $U_i \subseteq W$). By compactness of $W$, there exist $i_1, \dots
, i_n \in I$ such that
$$W \subseteq U_{i_1} \cup \cdots \cup U_{i_n}.$$
Since $B \subseteq W$, we have $B \subseteq U_{i_1} \cup \cdots \cup U_{i_n}$, g
iving a finite subcover of $B$.

Since $B$ was an arbitrary subset and $\{U_i\}$ was an arbitrary open cover, eve
ry subset of $X$ is compact. ∎

**Remark.** The key insight is that the hypothesis forces a "duality" between co
mpact and co-compact sets: $A$ is compact $\iff$ $X \setminus A$ is compact. Com
bined with the fact that closed subsets of the compact space $X$ are always comp
act, this forces all *open* sets to be compact as well. Once every open set is c
ompact, any subset $B$ covered by open sets is automatically covered inside the
compact open set $W = \bigcup U_i$, which admits a finite subcover.

### PROOF COMPLETE
