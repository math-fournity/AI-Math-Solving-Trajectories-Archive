Looking at this problem, I need to prove that line NC passes through the midpoin
t of AX, given the arc-trisection and the isosceles condition on N.

**Proof.**

Let $R$ be the circumradius. Since $X,Y$ lie on the minor arc $\widehat{BC}$ wit
h $\widehat{BX}=\widehat{XY}=\widehat{YC}$, each sub-arc has measure $2A/3$ (the
 minor arc $\widehat{BC}$ has measure $2A$). The inscribed angles from $A$ give
$$\angle BAX = \angle XAY = \angle YAC = \frac{A}{3},$$
so rays $AX$ and $AY$ trisect $\angle BAC$, with $X$ adjacent to $B$ and $Y$ adj
acent to $C$.

**Lengths.** By the inscribed angle theorem:
- $|AB|=2R\sin C$, $|AC|=2R\sin B$.
- $\angle ABX = \tfrac{1}{2}(\text{arc } AX \text{ not containing } B) = \tfrac{
2A}{3}+B$, so $|AX|=2R\sin(\tfrac{2A}{3}+B)=2R\sin(\tfrac{A}{3}+C)$.
- $|AY|=2R\sin(\tfrac{A}{3}+B)=2R\sin(\tfrac{2A}{3}+C)$.

**Deriving the key condition.** Place $A$ at the origin with ray $AB$ along the
positive $x$-axis, so ray $AC$ makes angle $A$ and ray $AY$ makes angle $2A/3$ w
ith the $x$-axis. Since $N\in\overline{AY}$ with $|AN|=|AB|=2R\sin C$:
$$N = 2R\sin C\,(\cos\tfrac{2A}{3},\,\sin\tfrac{2A}{3}).$$
Now $|NC|=|AB|=2R\sin C$, $|AC|=2R\sin B$, and $\angle NAC = \angle YAC = A/3$.
The law of cosines in $\triangle ANC$ gives
$$(2R\sin C)^2 = (2R\sin C)^2 + (2R\sin B)^2 - 2(2R\sin C)(2R\sin B)\cos\tfrac{A
}{3},$$
hence $\sin B = 2\sin C\cos\tfrac{A}{3}$. Since $B=\pi-A-C$:
$$\boxed{\sin(A+C) = 2\sin C\cos\tfrac{A}{3}.}\tag{$\star$}$$

**Coordinates for collinearity.** Set $p = A/3$ (so $A=3p$) and normalize $R=1$.
 With $A$ at the origin:
- $M$ (midpoint of $AX$) $= \sin(p+C)(\cos p,\sin p)$, since $X = 2\sin(p+C)(\co
s p,\sin p)$.
- $N = 2\sin C\,(\cos 2p,\sin 2p)$.
- $\mathcal{C} = 2\sin B\,(\cos 3p,\sin 3p) = 2\sin(3p+C)(\cos 3p,\sin 3p) = 4\s
in C\cos p\,(\cos 3p,\sin 3p)$, using $(\star)$.

**Computing the cross product.** We evaluate $(M-N)\times(\mathcal{C}-N) = \det(
M,\mathcal{C})-\det(M,N)-\det(N,\mathcal{C})$:
- $\det(M,\mathcal{C}) = \sin(p+C)\cdot 4\sin C\cos p\cdot\sin(3p-p) = 4\sin C\c
os p\sin 2p\sin(p+C)$.
- $\det(M,N) = \sin(p+C)\cdot 2\sin C\cdot\sin(2p-p) = 2\sin C\sin p\sin(p+C)$.
- $\det(N,\mathcal{C}) = 2\sin C\cdot 4\sin C\cos p\cdot\sin(3p-2p) = 8\sin^2 C\
cos p\sin p$.

Therefore
$$(M-N)\times(\mathcal{C}-N) = 2\sin C\sin p\bigl[\sin(p+C)(4\cos^2 p - 1) - 4\s
in C\cos p\bigr].$$
Using $4\cos^2 p - 1 = \sin 3p/\sin p$ and then $(\star)$ in the form $4\sin C\c
os p = 2\sin(3p+C)$:
$$= 2\sin C\bigl[\sin(p+C)\sin 3p - 2\sin(3p+C)\sin p\bigr].$$

**Showing the bracket vanishes.** By product-to-sum:
$$\sin(p+C)\sin 3p = \tfrac12[\cos(C-2p)-\cos(C+4p)],\quad 2\sin(3p+C)\sin p = \
cos(2p+C)-\cos(4p+C).$$
So the bracket equals $\tfrac12\cos(2p-C)+\tfrac12\cos(4p+C)-\cos(2p+C) = \cos 3
p\cos(p+C)-\cos(2p+C)$ (sum-to-product on the first two terms). We show this is
zero.

Expanding $\cos(2p+C)=\cos 3p\cos(C-p)-\sin 3p\sin(C-p)$:
$$\cos 3p\cos(p+C)-\cos(2p+C) = \cos 3p[\cos(p+C)-\cos(C-p)]+\sin 3p\sin(C-p).$$
Using $\cos(p+C)-\cos(C-p)=-2\sin C\sin p$ and $\sin(C-p)=\sin C\cos p - \cos C\
sin p$:
$$= -2\sin C\sin p\cos 3p + \sin 3p(\sin C\cos p - \cos C\sin p)$$
$$= \sin C(\sin 3p\cos p - 2\sin p\cos 3p) - \sin p\sin 3p\cos C.$$
Since $\sin 3p\cos p - 2\sin p\cos 3p = \tfrac12(\sin 4p+\sin 2p)-(\sin 4p-\sin
2p)=\tfrac12(3\sin 2p-\sin 4p)=\sin p\cos p(3-2\cos 2p)$, this becomes
$$= \sin p\bigl[\sin C\cos p(3-2\cos 2p) - \sin 3p\cos C\bigr].$$
This vanishes iff $\tan C = \dfrac{\sin 3p}{\cos p(3-2\cos 2p)}$. $\quad(\dagger
)$

From $(\star)$: $\sin 3p\cos C + \cos 3p\sin C = 2\sin C\cos p$, so $\sin 3p\cos
 C = \sin C(2\cos p - \cos 3p)$, giving
$$\tan C = \frac{\sin 3p}{2\cos p - \cos 3p}.\quad(\ddagger)$$
Now $2\cos p - \cos 3p = 2\cos p - (4\cos^3 p - 3\cos p) = 5\cos p - 4\cos^3 p =
 \cos p(5-4\cos^2 p) = \cos p(3-2\cos 2p)$, so $(\dagger)=(\ddagger)$. The brack
et is zero.

Since $\sin C\neq 0$ and $\sin p\neq 0$, the cross product $(M-N)\times(\mathcal
{C}-N)=0$, proving $M,N,\mathcal{C}$ are collinear.

Therefore the line $NC$ passes through the midpoint of $\overline{AX}$. $\blacks
quare$
