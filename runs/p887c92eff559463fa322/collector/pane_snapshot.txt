# Evaluation of $\int_{0}^{\infty} x \exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2+a^2}{2}\right) I_0(a
x)\, dx$

## Setup: a probabilistic interpretation

Let $X_1, X_2$ be independent standard normal random variables, $X_1, X_2 \sim N
(0,1)$, with joint density
$$
f(x_1,x_2)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\,e^{-(x_1^2+x_2^2)/2}.
$$

Consider the **shifted** random vector $(X_1+a,\; X_2)$. Its joint density is
$$
g(x_1,x_2)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\exp\!\Big(-\tfrac{(x_1-a)^2+x_2^2}{2}\Big).
$$

Define the radial variable
$$
R=\sqrt{(X_1+a)^2+X_2^2}.
$$

Since $g$ is a probability density on $\mathbb{R}^2$, the distribution of $R$ is
 a probability density on $[0,\infty)$, and consequently
$$
\int_{0}^{\infty} f_R(r)\,dr = 1.
$$

We now compute $f_R(r)$ explicitly.

## Computing the radial density

Pass to polar coordinates $x_1 = r\cos\theta$, $x_2 = r\sin\theta$, with Jacobia
n $r$:
$$
f_R(r)=\int_{0}^{2\pi} g(r\cos\theta,\, r\sin\theta)\,r\,d\theta
= \frac{r}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\exp\!\Big(-\tfrac{(r\cos\theta-a)^2+r^2\sin^2\th
eta}{2}\Big)\,d\theta.
$$

Expand the exponent:
$$
(r\cos\theta-a)^2+r^2\sin^2\theta = r^2\cos^2\theta - 2ar\cos\theta + a^2 + r^2\
sin^2\theta = r^2 - 2ar\cos\theta + a^2.
$$

Hence
$$
f_R(r)=\frac{r}{2\pi}\,e^{-(r^2+a^2)/2}\int_{0}^{2\pi} e^{ar\cos\theta}\,d\theta
.
$$

## Using the integral representation of $I_0$

The modified Bessel function of the first kind of order zero has the standard re
presentation
$$
I_0(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi} e^{z\cos\theta}\,d\theta.
$$

Applying this with $z = ar$:
$$
f_R(r)=r\,\exp\!\Big(-\frac{r^2+a^2}{2}\Big)\,I_0(ar).
$$

## Conclusion

Since $f_R$ is a genuine probability density (it is the radial part of the bivar
iate Gaussian density shifted by $(a,0)$), it must integrate to $1$:
$$
\int_{0}^{\infty} r\,\exp\!\Big(-\frac{r^2+a^2}{2}\Big)\,I_0(ar)\,dr = 1.
$$

Renaming the dummy variable $r\to x$ gives the desired result:

$$
\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty} x\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2+a^2}{2}\right) I
_0(ax)\, dx \;=\; 1.}
$$

**Remark.** This integral is the normalization constant of the **Rice (Rician) d
istribution** with parameters $\nu = a$ and $\sigma = 1$, whose density is preci
sely $f_R(r)=r\,e^{-(r^2+a^2)/2}\,I_0(ar)$. The result holds for all $a\in\mathb
b{R}$ (and trivially also $a\ge 0$ since $I_0$ is even, $I_0(ax)=I_0(|a|x)$).
