# Asymptotic Error Expansions for Richardson Extrapolation

## 1. Setup and the base expansion

Let $A(h)$ be a numerical approximation to an exact quantity $L$, depending on a
 step size $h>0$. We assume $A(h)$ admits an asymptotic expansion in powers of $
h$ as $h\to 0$:

$$
A(h) \;=\; L \;+\; \sum_{j=1}^{\infty} c_j\, h^{k_j},
\qquad 0 < k_1 < k_2 < k_3 < \cdots,
$$

where the $c_j$ are constants independent of $h$ (they may depend on $L$ and on
the method), and the exponents $k_j$ are real and strictly increasing. The **err
or** is

$$
E(h) \;:=\; A(h) - L \;=\; c_1 h^{k_1} + c_2 h^{k_2} + c_3 h^{k_3} + \cdots,
$$

so the leading error is $O(h^{k_1})$, as assumed in the problem.

The expansion is to be understood asymptotically: for any fixed $N$,
$$
A(h) = L + \sum_{j=1}^{N} c_j h^{k_j} + O(h^{k_{N+1}}), \qquad h\to 0.
$$

## 2. The one-step extrapolation (eliminating the leading term)

Fix a **refinement ratio** $q>1$ (in practice $q=2$). Evaluate the method at two
 step sizes, $h$ and $h/q$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A(h)   &= L + c_1 h^{k_1} + c_2 h^{k_2} + c_3 h^{k_3} + \cdots,\\
A(h/q) &= L + c_1 (h/q)^{k_1} + c_2 (h/q)^{k_2} + c_3 (h/q)^{k_3} + \cdots\\
       &= L + c_1 q^{-k_1} h^{k_1} + c_2 q^{-k_2} h^{k_2} + c_3 q^{-k_3} h^{k_3}
 + \cdots.
\end{aligned}
$$

Form the linear combination that cancels the $h^{k_1}$ term. Multiply the second
 equation by $q^{k_1}$ and subtract the first:

$$
q^{k_1} A(h/q) - A(h) = (q^{k_1}-1)\,L \;+\; \sum_{j\ge 1} c_j\bigl(q^{k_1-k_j}-
1\bigr)\,h^{k_j}.
$$

The $j=1$ summand vanishes because $q^{k_1-k_1}-1 = 0$. Dividing by $(q^{k_1}-1)
\neq 0$ defines the **first Richardson extrapolant**:

$$
\boxed{\;A_1(h) \;:=\; \frac{q^{k_1}\,A(h/q) - A(h)}{q^{k_1}-1}\;}
$$

and its expansion is

$$
A_1(h) = L + \sum_{j\ge 2} c_j^{(1)}\, h^{k_j},
\qquad
c_j^{(1)} = c_j\,\frac{q^{k_1-k_j}-1}{q^{k_1}-1},\quad j\ge 2.
$$

The leading error of $A_1$ is now $O(h^{k_2})$: one full order of the expansion
has been removed. The new error expansion is

$$
E_1(h) := A_1(h)-L = c_2^{(1)} h^{k_2} + c_3^{(1)} h^{k_3} + \cdots.
$$

## 3. Iterated extrapolation (the Romberg/Bulirsch tableau)

The same algebra applies to $A_1$, since $A_1$ has the *same structural form* —
an exact value plus a series in $h^{k_2}, h^{k_3},\ldots$ — only with the leadin
g exponent shifted from $k_1$ to $k_2$. Define the recursion

$$
A_0(h) := A(h),\qquad
\boxed{\;A_{m+1}(h) \;:=\; \frac{q^{k_{m+1}}\,A_m(h/q) - A_m(h)}{q^{k_{m+1}}-1}\
;},\qquad m\ge 0.
$$

**Inductive claim.** For each $m\ge 0$,
$$
A_m(h) = L + \sum_{j\ge m+1} c_j^{(m)}\, h^{k_j},
\qquad
c_j^{(m)} = c_j\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{q^{k_i-k_j}-1}{q^{k_i}-1},
\qquad c_j^{(0)}=c_j,
$$
with the empty product (when $m=0$) equal to $1$.

**Proof by induction.** The base case $m=0$ is the original expansion. Assume th
e form holds for $m$. Then

$$
A_m(h/q) = L + \sum_{j\ge m+1} c_j^{(m)}\, q^{-k_j} h^{k_j}.
$$

Multiply by $q^{k_{m+1}}$ and subtract $A_m(h)$:

$$
q^{k_{m+1}} A_m(h/q) - A_m(h) = (q^{k_{m+1}}-1)L + \sum_{j\ge m+1} c_j^{(m)}\big
l(q^{k_{m+1}-k_j}-1\bigr) h^{k_j}.
$$

The $j=m+1$ term vanishes ($q^{k_{m+1}-k_{m+1}}-1=0$). Dividing by $q^{k_{m+1}}-
1$ gives $A_{m+1}(h)=L+\sum_{j\ge m+2}c_j^{(m+1)}h^{k_j}$ with

$$
c_j^{(m+1)} = c_j^{(m)}\,\frac{q^{k_{m+1}-k_j}-1}{q^{k_{m+1}}-1}
= c_j\prod_{i=1}^{m+1}\frac{q^{k_i-k_j}-1}{q^{k_i}-1},
$$

using the induction hypothesis. This closes the induction. $\square$

## 4. The asymptotic error expansion of the $m$-th extrapolant

Setting $E_m(h):=A_m(h)-L$, the **asymptotic error expansion of the $m$-th Richa
rdson extrapolant** is

$$
\boxed{\;
E_m(h) \;=\; \sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}\, c_j^{(m)}\, h^{k_j}
\;=\; \sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}\left[c_j\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{q^{k_i-k_j}-1}{q^{k_i}-
1}\right] h^{k_j},
\;}
$$

with leading term

$$
E_m(h) \;=\; c_{m+1}\!\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{q^{k_i-k_{m+1}}-1}{q^{k_i}-1}\r
ight] h^{k_{m+1}} \;+\; O(h^{k_{m+2}}).
$$

Since $k_i < k_{m+1}$ for $i\le m$, each factor $q^{k_i-k_{m+1}}-1<0$ while $q^{
k_i}-1>0$, so the product is a definite nonzero constant; in particular the lead
ing coefficient does **not** vanish, and

$$
E_m(h) = O(h^{k_{m+1}}).
$$

Thus each extrapolation step raises the order of accuracy from $O(h^{k_m})$ to $
O(h^{k_{m+1}})$, eliminating exactly one term of the original expansion.

## 6. Summary

Starting from $A(h)=L+\sum_{j\ge1}c_j h^{k_j}$ with $0<k_1<k_2<\cdots$, the Rich

$$
A_{m+1}(h)=\frac{q^{k_{m+1}}A_m(h/q)-A_m(h)}{q^{k_{m+1}}-1},\qquad A_0(h)=A(h),
$$

satisfy the asymptotic error expansion

$$
\boxed{\;E_m(h)=A_m(h)-L=\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}c_j\!\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{q^
{k_i-k_j}-1}{q^{k_i}-1}\right]h^{k_j}=O(h^{k_{m+1}})\;}

so that each extrapolation eliminates the current leading error term and raises

_{m+1}$, giving the classical Romberg orders $h^{(m+1)p}$ with rapidly decaying
leading constants. This completes the derivation of the asymptotic error expansi
