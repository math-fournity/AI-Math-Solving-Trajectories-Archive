- $A(n)$ counts non-increasing sequences summing to $n$ with each $a_i + 1$ a po

- $B(n)$ counts non-increasing sequences summing to $n$ with $b_j \geq 2b_{j+1}$

I'll establish a bijection via a change of variables.

**Proof.**

**Step 1: Reformulating $A(n)$.** Since each $a_i \geq 1$ and $a_i + 1$ is a pow
er of two, we have $a_i \in \{2^j - 1 : j \geq 1\} = \{1, 3, 7, 15, \ldots\}$. A
 non-increasing sequence of such values summing to $n$ is determined by the mult
iplicities $c_1, c_2, c_3, \ldots$ (finitely many nonzero, each $c_j \geq 0$), w
here $c_j$ counts how many times $2^j - 1$ appears, satisfying:
$$n = \sum_{j \geq 1} c_j\,(2^j - 1).$$

**Step 2: Reformulating $B(n)$ via a change of variables.** Let $b_1 \geq b_2 \g
eq \cdots \geq b_m \geq 1$ be a sequence with $b_j \geq 2b_{j+1}$ for $j = 1, \l
dots, m-1$. Define
$$d_j = b_j - 2b_{j+1} \quad (j = 1, \ldots, m-1), \qquad d_m = b_m.$$
Then $d_j \geq 0$ for $j < m$ and $d_m \geq 1$. This transformation is invertibl
e: given $(d_1, \ldots, d_m)$, we recover
$$b_j = \sum_{i=j}^{m} 2^{i-j}\, d_i, \qquad j = 1, \ldots, m.$$
One verifies directly that $b_j \geq 2b_{j+1}$ (since $b_j = d_j + 2b_{j+1} \geq
 2b_{j+1}$) and $b_m = d_m \geq 1$, so this indeed produces a valid $B$-sequence
.

**Step 3: The sum transforms correctly.** Computing the total:
$$\sum_{j=1}^{m} b_j = \sum_{j=1}^{m} \sum_{i=j}^{m} 2^{i-j}\, d_i = \sum_{i=1}^
{m} d_i \sum_{j=1}^{i} 2^{i-j} = \sum_{i=1}^{m} d_i\,(2^i - 1).$$
The inner sum is $\sum_{j=1}^{i} 2^{i-j} = 2^{i-1} + 2^{i-2} + \cdots + 1 = 2^i
- 1$.

Thus $n = \sum_{i=1}^{m} d_i\,(2^i - 1)$ with $d_1, \ldots, d_{m-1} \geq 0$ and
$d_m \geq 1$.

**Step 4: The bijection.** The map $(b_1, \ldots, b_m) \mapsto (d_1, \ldots, d_m
)$ is a bijection from $B$-sequences summing to $n$ onto tuples $(d_1, \ldots, d
_m)$ with $d_j \geq 0$ for $j < m$, $d_m \geq 1$, and $\sum_{j} d_j(2^j - 1) = n
$. (The inverse is given by the formula in Step 2.)

Setting $c_j = d_j$, the $A$-sequences summing to $n$ are in bijection with exac
tly the same set of tuples: non-negative integers $(c_1, c_2, c_3, \ldots)$, fin
itely many nonzero, satisfying $\sum_{j \geq 1} c_j(2^j - 1) = n$. (The conditio
n "$d_m \geq 1$ where $m$ is the last nonzero index" is automatic, since a nonze

**Explicit bijection.** Given a $B$-sequence $(b_1, \ldots, b_m)$, compute $d_j
= b_j - 2b_{j+1}$ (with $b_{m+1} := 0$), then form the $A$-sequence consisting o

ersely, given an $A$-sequence, let $c_j$ be the multiplicity of $2^j - 1$, set $
m = \max\{j : c_j > 0\}$, and recover $b_m = c_m$, $b_j = c_j + 2b_{j+1}$ for $j

Both directions are well-defined and mutually inverse, establishing $A(n) = B(n)
$ for every positive integer $n$. $\blacksquare$
