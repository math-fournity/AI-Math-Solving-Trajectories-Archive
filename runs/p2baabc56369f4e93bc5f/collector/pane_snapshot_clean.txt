## Proof

**Definitions.** A function $f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}$ has a *jump discontinuity* at
 $x_0$ if both one-sided limits $f(x_0^-)$ and $f(x_0^+)$ exist as finite real n
umbers and $f(x_0^-)\neq f(x_0^+)$. A function is *piecewise continuous* on $[0,
1]$ if there exists a **finite** partition $0=x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_n=1$ such that $f
$ is continuous on each open subinterval $(x_{i-1},x_i)$ and the one-sided limit
s at every partition point exist and are finite. In particular, a piecewise cont
inuous function has at most finitely many discontinuities.

**Construction.** Let $\{q_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ be an enumeration of the rational
 numbers in $(0,1)$. Define
$$
f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^n}\,\mathbf{1}_{[q_n,1]}(x)=\sum_{\{n\,:\,q_n
\le x\}}\frac{1}{2^n},\qquad x\in[0,1].
$$
The series converges absolutely (it is dominated by $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}=1
$), so $f$ is well defined and bounded. Since each summand is non-decreasing, $f
$ is **monotone non-decreasing**.

**Claim 1: $f$ is continuous at every irrational point.**

Let $x_0\in[0,1]$ be irrational. Since $q_n\neq x_0$ for all $n$,
$$
f(x_0)=\sum_{\{n:\,q_n<x_0\}}\frac{1}{2^n}.
$$
Given $\varepsilon>0$, pick $N$ with $\sum_{n>N}2^{-n}<\varepsilon$. Among $q_1,
\dots,q_N$ that are $<x_0$, let $q_{k}$ be the largest; set $\delta=x_0-q_k>0$ (
if none are $<x_0$, take $\delta=x_0$). For any $x\in(x_0-\delta,x_0)$, every $q
_n\le x$ with $n\le N$ satisfies $q_n<x_0$, so
$$
f(x_0)-f(x)\le\sum_{n>N}2^{-n}<\varepsilon.
$$
Thus $f(x_0^-)=f(x_0)$. A symmetric argument gives $f(x_0^+)=f(x_0)$. Hence $f$
is continuous at $x_0$.

**Claim 2: $f$ has a jump discontinuity at every rational point.**

Let $x_0=q_k$. Then
$$
f(x_0^-)=\sum_{\{n:\,q_n<x_0\}}\frac{1}{2^n},\qquad f(x_0^+)=\sum_{\{n:\,q_n\le
x_0\}}\frac{1}{2^n}=f(x_0^-)+\frac{1}{2^k}.
$$
Both one-sided limits exist and are finite, and $f(x_0^+)-f(x_0^-)=2^{-k}>0$. He
nce $f$ has a jump discontinuity at $q_k$.

**Conclusion of discontinuity structure.** The discontinuities of $f$ are exactl
y the rational points of $(0,1)$, and every one of them is a jump discontinuity.
 Thus $f$ has **only** jump discontinuities.

**Claim 3: $f$ is not piecewise continuous.**

The rationals in $(0,1)$ are dense and infinite. Any finite partition $0=x_0<x_1
<\cdots<x_m=1$ has only finitely many subintervals, and piecewise continuity wou
ld require $f$ to be continuous on each open subinterval $(x_{i-1},x_i)$. But ev
ery nonempty open subinterval contains a rational, at which $f$ is discontinuous
. Therefore no finite partition can make $f$ continuous on all open subintervals
. Hence $f$ is **not** piecewise continuous.

**Answer.** Yes. The function $f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}\mathbf{1}_{[q_n,1]
}(x)$, where $\{q_n\}$ enumerates $\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1)$, has only jump discontin
uities (at every rational, countably many in total) yet is not piecewise continu
ous, since it has infinitely many discontinuities and no finite partition of $[0
,1]$ can accommodate them all.
