## Proof

**Claim.** The fractional $p$-Laplace operator $(-\Delta_p)^s$ is rotationally i
nvariant under any orthogonal map $R \in O(n)$, i.e., for every sufficiently reg
ular $u$ and every $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$,

$$(-\Delta_p)^s(u \circ R)(x) = \big((-\Delta_p)^s u\big)(Rx).$$

**Setup.** Let $R \in O(n)$ be an orthogonal map and define $v(x) := u(Rx)$. We
compute $(-\Delta_p)^s v(x)$ directly from the definition and show it equals $(-
\Delta_p)^s u(Rx)$.

**Three properties of orthogonal maps.** We use the following standard facts:

1. **Isometry:** $|Rx - Ry| = |x - y|$ for all $x, y$, since $|Rw|^2 = w^\top R^
\top R w = |w|^2$.
2. **Unit Jacobian:** $|\det R| = 1$, so the change of variables $z = Ry$ has $d
y = dz$.
3. **Balls map to balls:** Since $R$ is an isometry, $y \in B_\varepsilon(x) \if
f |y - x| < \varepsilon \iff |Ry - Rx| < \varepsilon \iff z \in B_\varepsilon(Rx
)$.

**Computation.** Starting from the definition applied to $v$:

$$(-\Delta_p)^s v(x) = \lim_{\varepsilon \to 0} \int_{\mathbb{R}^n \setminus B_\
varepsilon(x)} \frac{|v(x) - v(y)|^{p-2}\big(v(x) - v(y)\big)}{|x - y|^{n+ps}}\,
 dy.$$

Substituting $v(y) = u(Ry)$:

$$= \lim_{\varepsilon \to 0} \int_{\mathbb{R}^n \setminus B_\varepsilon(x)} \fra
c{|u(Rx) - u(Ry)|^{p-2}\big(u(Rx) - u(Ry)\big)}{|x - y|^{n+ps}}\, dy.$$

Perform the change of variables $z = Ry$ (so $y = R^{-1}z$). By properties (1)–(
3) above:

- The domain $\mathbb{R}^n \setminus B_\varepsilon(x)$ becomes $\mathbb{R}^n \se
tminus B_\varepsilon(Rx)$.
- $|x - y| = |Rx - Ry| = |Rx - z|$.
- $dy = dz$.

Therefore:

$$= \lim_{\varepsilon \to 0} \int_{\mathbb{R}^n \setminus B_\varepsilon(Rx)} \fr
ac{|u(Rx) - u(z)|^{p-2}\big(u(Rx) - u(z)\big)}{|Rx - z|^{n+ps}}\, dz.$$

The right-hand side is precisely the definition of $(-\Delta_p)^s u$ evaluated a
t the point $Rx$:

$$= \big((-\Delta_p)^s u\big)(Rx).$$

**Conclusion.** We have shown

$$(-\Delta_p)^s(u \circ R)(x) = \big((-\Delta_p)^s u\big)(Rx) \quad \text{for al
l } x \in \mathbb{R}^n,$$

which is exactly the statement that $(-\Delta_p)^s$ is rotationally invariant un
der the orthogonal map $R$. The argument uses only that $R$ is an isometry (pres
erving the singular kernel $|x-y|^{-(n+ps)}$), has unit determinant (preserving
the Lebesgue measure $dy$), and maps balls to balls (preserving the principal-va
lue exclusion region). All three hold for every $R \in O(n)$, including reflecti
ons ($\det R = -1$). $\blacksquare$
