## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, such a holomorphic function exists.

**Construction.** Let $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ be the given integer. Define
$$
f(z) = \frac{\sin(\pi z)}{z - n}, \qquad z \neq n,
$$
and extend $f$ to $z = n$ by continuity. We show that $f$ extends to an entire f
unction with the required properties.

**Step 1: $f$ is entire.**

The function $\sin(\pi z)$ is entire, and $z - n$ is entire with a simple zero a
t $z = n$. The only potential singularity of the quotient is at $z = n$. Since $
\sin(\pi z)$ has a simple zero at $z = n$ (as $\sin(\pi n) = 0$ and $\frac{d}{dz
}\sin(\pi z)\big|_{z=n} = \pi\cos(\pi n) = \pi(-1)^n \neq 0$), the zero of $\sin
(\pi z)$ at $z = n$ exactly cancels the simple zero of $z - n$. Therefore the si
ngularity at $z = n$ is removable, and the limit
$$
\lim_{z \to n} \frac{\sin(\pi z)}{z - n} = \pi \cos(\pi n) = \pi(-1)^n
$$
is finite and nonzero. Defining $f(n) = \pi(-1)^n$ yields a function holomorphic
 on all of $\mathbb{C}$, i.e., $f$ is entire.

**Step 2: $f(m) = 0$ for every integer $m \neq n$.**

For any integer $m \neq n$, we have $\sin(\pi m) = 0$ and $m - n \neq 0$, so
$$
f(m) = \frac{\sin(\pi m)}{m - n} = \frac{0}{m - n} = 0.
$$

**Step 3: $f(n) \neq 0$.**

By the removable singularity extension,
$$
f(n) = \lim_{z \to n} \frac{\sin(\pi z)}{z - n} = \pi \cos(\pi n) = \pi (-1)^n \
neq 0.
$$

**Conclusion.** The function $f(z) = \frac{\sin(\pi z)}{z - n}$ (with the remova
ble singularity at $z = n$ filled in) is an entire function satisfying $f(n) \ne
q 0$ and $f(m) = 0$ for all integers $m \neq n$. Therefore, such a holomorphic f
unction exists. $\blacksquare$
