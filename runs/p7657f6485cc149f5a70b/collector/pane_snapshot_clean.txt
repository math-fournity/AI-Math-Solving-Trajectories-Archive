## Proof

We show that $L = -\frac{d^2}{dx^2} + V(x)$, with domain $D(L) = H^2(\mathbb{R})
$, is self-adjoint on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ whenever $V \in L^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ is
 real-valued. The argument has three parts: (i) the free operator is self-adjoin
t on $H^2$; (ii) $V$ is a bounded self-adjoint operator; (iii) the Kato–Rellich
theorem lifts self-adjointness to the sum.

---

### 1. The free operator $H_0 = -\tfrac{d^2}{dx^2}$ is self-adjoint on $H^2(\mat
hbb{R})$

Let $\mathcal{F}: L^2(\mathbb{R}) \to L^2(\mathbb{R})$ be the unitary Fourier tr
ansform. For $u \in H^2(\mathbb{R})$ one has $\widehat{u''}(\xi) = -\xi^2 \wideh
at{u}(\xi)$, so

$$
\widehat{H_0 u}(\xi) = \xi^2 \widehat{u}(\xi).
$$

Under $\mathcal{F}$, $H_0$ is therefore unitarily equivalent to the multiplicati
on operator

$$
(M_{\xi^2} f)(\xi) = \xi^2 f(\xi), \qquad D(M_{\xi^2}) = \{f \in L^2 : \xi^2 f \
in L^2\}.
$$

A multiplication operator $M_\varphi$ by a real-valued measurable function $\var
phi$ is self-adjoint on its maximal domain $\{f : \varphi f \in L^2\}$: symmetry
 is immediate from $\langle M_\varphi f, g\rangle = \int \varphi f \bar g = \lan
gle f, M_\varphi g\rangle$, and the domain of the adjoint equals the maximal dom
ain because $M_\varphi^* = M_{\bar\varphi} = M_\varphi$ with the same maximal do
main. Hence $M_{\xi^2}$ is self-adjoint.

It remains to identify the maximal domain with $H^2(\mathbb{R})$. By definition

$$
H^2(\mathbb{R}) = \{u \in L^2 : u', u'' \in L^2\} = \{u \in L^2 : (1+\xi^2)\wide
hat u \in L^2\},
$$

the last equality being the standard Fourier characterization of Sobolev spaces.
 Since $(1+\xi^2)\widehat u \in L^2 \iff \widehat u \in L^2 \text{ and } \xi^2 \
widehat u \in L^2$, we have $D(M_{\xi^2}) = \mathcal{F}(H^2(\mathbb{R}))$. Thus
$H_0$ is self-adjoint on $H^2(\mathbb{R})$.

---

### 2. $V$ is a bounded self-adjoint operator on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$

Since $V$ is real-valued and bounded, the multiplication operator $(M_V u)(x) =
V(x)u(x)$ is bounded on $L^2$ with $\|M_V\| \leq \|V\|_{L^\infty}$, and

$$
\langle M_V u, v\rangle = \int V u \bar v = \int u \overline{V v} = \langle u, M
_V v\rangle,
$$

because $V = \bar V$. Hence $M_V$ is a bounded self-adjoint operator defined on
all of $L^2(\mathbb{R})$.

---

### 3. Kato–Rellich theorem

We use the following standard theorem.

> **Kato–Rellich theorem.** Let $A$ be self-adjoint on $D(A)$ and let $B$ be sym
metric with $D(A) \subseteq D(B)$. Suppose $B$ is $A$-bounded with relative boun
d $< \tfrac12$, i.e. there exist $a < \tfrac12$ and $b \geq 0$ with
> $$\|B u\| \leq a \|A u\| + b \|u\|, \qquad u \in D(A).$$
> Then $A + B$ is self-adjoint on $D(A)$, and essentially self-adjoint on any co
re of $A$.

Apply this with $A = H_0$ (self-adjoint on $H^2$, by step 1) and $B = M_V$ (symm
etric, bounded, by step 2). Since $M_V$ is bounded,

$$
\|M_V u\|_{L^2} \leq \|V\|_{L^\infty} \|u\|_{L^2} \leq a \|H_0 u\|_{L^2} + b \|u
\|_{L^2}
$$

holds for **any** $a > 0$ (with $b = \|V\|_{L^\infty}$). In particular we may ta
ke $a < \tfrac12$. Thus the relative bound is $0 < \tfrac12$, and the hypotheses
 of Kato–Rellich are satisfied.

Consequently

$$
L = H_0 + M_V = -\tfrac{d^2}{dx^2} + V(x)
$$

is self-adjoint on $D(H_0) = H^2(\mathbb{R})$.

---

### 4. Direct sanity check: symmetry

For completeness, we verify symmetry directly. For $u, v \in H^2(\mathbb{R})$,

$$
\langle L u, v\rangle = \int_{\mathbb{R}} (-u'' + V u)\,\bar v\, dx = -\int u''
\bar v\, dx + \int V u \bar v\, dx.
$$

By density of $C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ in $H^2(\mathbb{R})$ and continuity of bo
th sides, it suffices to integrate by parts for $u \in C_c^\infty$ (no boundary
terms appear):

$$
-\int u'' \bar v\, dx = -\int u \bar v''\, dx = \int u\, \overline{(-v'')}\, dx.
$$

Since $V$ is real, $\int V u \bar v\, dx = \int u \overline{V v}\, dx$. Hence

$$
\langle L u, v\rangle = \int u\, \overline{(-v'' + V v)}\, dx = \langle u, L v\r
angle,
$$

so $L$ is symmetric on $H^2(\mathbb{R})$. Combined with Kato–Rellich, which give
s $D(L^*) = D(L) = H^2(\mathbb{R})$, we conclude $L = L^*$, i.e. $L$ is self-adj
oint.

---

### Conclusion

The operator $L = -\tfrac{d^2}{dx^2} + V(x)$ with domain $H^2(\mathbb{R})$ is **
self-adjoint** on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ for every real-valued bounded potential $V \
in L^\infty(\mathbb{R})$. The key ingredients are: the free kinetic operator is
self-adjoint on $H^2$ (via the Fourier transform), the bounded real multiplicati
on $V$ is a bounded self-adjoint perturbation, and the Kato–Rellich theorem (app
licable because $V$ has $H_0$-relative bound $0 < \tfrac12$) preserves self-adjo
intness of the sum on the original domain $H^2(\mathbb{R})$.
