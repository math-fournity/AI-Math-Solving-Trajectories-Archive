Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a bounded complementary com
ponent $C$ of a domain $U$ remains a complementary component when we intersect $
U$ with an open disk $D$ containing $C$.

## Proof

**The statement is TRUE.**

Let $U$ be a domain (open, connected) in $\mathbb{R}^2$, and let $C$ be a bounde
d complementary component of $U$, i.e., $C$ is a connected component of $\mathbb
{R}^2 \setminus U$. Let $D$ be an open disk with $C \subset D$.

We must show that $C$ is a connected component of
$$X := \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus (U \cap D).$$

**Key observation.** Since $U \cap D = U \cap D$, by De Morgan's law:
$$X = (\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus U) \cup (\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus D).$$

**Step 1: $C$ is closed in $\mathbb{R}^2$.**

Since $U$ is open, $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus U$ is closed. The set $C$ is a connec
ted component of $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus U$. In any topological space, connected
 components are closed (the closure of a connected set is connected, so a maxima
l connected set equals its own closure). Hence $C$ is closed in $\mathbb{R}^2 \s
etminus U$, and since $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus U$ is closed in $\mathbb{R}^2$, th
e set $C$ is closed in $\mathbb{R}^2$.

**Step 2: $C$ is closed in $X$.**

Since $C \subset \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus U \subset X$ and $C$ is closed in $\math
bb{R}^2$, it follows that $C$ is closed in $X$ (subspace topology).

**Step 3: $X \setminus C$ is closed in $\mathbb{R}^2$, hence $C$ is open in $X$.
**

We compute:
$$X \setminus C = \big((\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus U) \setminus C\big) \cup (\mathbb
{R}^2 \setminus D).$$

- $(\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus U) \setminus C = (\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus U) \cap (\ma
thbb{R}^2 \setminus C)$. Both $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus U$ (complement of open $U$
) and $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus C$ (complement of closed $C$, by Step 1) are close
d in $\mathbb{R}^2$. Their intersection is closed.

- $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus D$ is closed in $\mathbb{R}^2$ (since $D$ is open).

Therefore $X \setminus C$ is a finite union of closed sets, hence closed in $\ma
thbb{R}^2$. Since $X \setminus C \subset X$, it is closed in $X$, which means $C
$ is **open** in $X$.

**Step 4: $C$ is a connected component of $X$.**

$C$ is connected (being a component of $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus U$), and we have
shown $C$ is both open and closed in $X$. Let $K$ be the connected component of
$X$ containing any point $x \in C$. Since $C$ is open and closed in $X$, the set
 $K \cap C$ is both open and closed in $K$. It is nonempty (contains $x$), and $
K$ is connected, so $K \cap C = K$, giving $K \subseteq C$. Conversely, $C$ is a
 connected subset of $X$ containing $x$, so $C \subseteq K$ (by maximality of $K
$). Therefore $K = C$.

**Conclusion.** $C$ is a connected component of $X = \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus (U \
cap D)$, i.e., $C$ is a complementary component of $U \cap D$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The argument uses only that $D$ is open and contains $C$; the disk s
tructure is unnecessary. The essential ingredients are: (1) components of a clos
ed set are closed, and (2) $C \cap (\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus D) = \emptyset$ so th
at $X \setminus C$ decomposes as a union of two closed sets.
