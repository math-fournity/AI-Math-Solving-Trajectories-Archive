**Answer: Yes, $f_{b_n} \to f_b$ uniformly on $A$.**

**Proof.** Suppose, for contradiction, that the convergence is not uniform. Then
 there exists some $\varepsilon > 0$ such that for infinitely many $n$,

$$\sup_{a \in A} d\big(f(a, b_n),\, f(a, b)\big) \geq \varepsilon.$$

Passing to a subsequence (still denoted $b_n$), we may assume that for **every**
 $n$ there exists a point $a_n \in A$ with

$$d\big(f(a_n, b_n),\, f(a_n, b)\big) \geq \varepsilon. \tag{$\ast$}$$

Since $A$ is compact, the sequence $(a_n)$ admits a convergent subsequence. Rela
beling, we may assume $a_n \to a \in A$.

Now consider the sequence of pairs $(a_n, b_n)$ in $A \times B$. Since $a_n \to
a$ and $b_n \to b$, by the definition of the product topology we have

$$(a_n, b_n) \to (a, b) \quad \text{in } A \times B.$$

By the continuity of $f$,

$$f(a_n, b_n) \to f(a, b) \quad \text{in } C.$$

Likewise, since $a_n \to a$ and $b$ is fixed, $(a_n, b) \to (a, b)$, so again by
 continuity

$$f(a_n, b) \to f(a, b) \quad \text{in } C.$$

Combining these two limits (using the triangle inequality for the metric $d$ on
$C$),

$$d\big(f(a_n, b_n),\, f(a_n, b)\big) \leq d\big(f(a_n, b_n), f(a, b)\big) + d\b
ig(f(a, b), f(a_n, b)\big) \to 0.$$

This contradicts $(\ast)$, which asserts that this distance is bounded below by
$\varepsilon > 0$ for all $n$.

Therefore our assumption was false, and we conclude

$$\sup_{a \in A} d\big(f(a, b_n),\, f(a, b)\big) \to 0 \quad \text{as } n \to \i
nfty,$$

i.e., $f_{b_n} \to f_b$ **uniformly** on $A$. $\blacksquare$
