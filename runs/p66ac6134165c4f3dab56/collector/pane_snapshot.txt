## Proof

**Setup.** For a positive integer $n$ with $k$ digits in base 3 (i.e., $3^{k-1}
\le n \le 3^k - 1$), the leading digit is 1 or 2, and the remaining $k-1$ digits
 are each in $\{0,1,2\}$. The value $a(n)$ counts the zero digits among these $k
-1$ positions (the leading digit is never zero).

**Counting weighted digit strings.** For $k$-digit base-3 numbers, define
$$N_k(x) := \sum_{\substack{n:\, k\text{ digits}}} x^{a(n)}.$$
If exactly $j$ of the $k-1$ trailing digits are zero, there are $\binom{k-1}{j}$
 ways to choose their positions, $2^{k-1-j}$ ways to fill the remaining position
s with 1's and 2's, and $2$ choices for the leading digit. Hence the count is $2
^{k-j}\binom{k-1}{j}$, and
$$N_k(x) = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} 2^{k-j}\binom{k-1}{j}\,x^j = 2^k\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\bin
om{k-1}{j}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^j = 2^k\left(1+\frac{x}{2}\right)^{k-1} = 2\,
(2+x)^{k-1},$$
by the binomial theorem.

**Bounding the block sum.** Let $S_k := \sum_{\substack{n:\, k\text{ digits}}} \
frac{x^{a(n)}}{n^3}$. Since $3^{k-1} \le n \le 3^k - 1 < 3^k$ for $k$-digit $n$:
$$\frac{N_k(x)}{3^{3k}} \;\le\; S_k \;\le\; \frac{N_k(x)}{3^{3(k-1)}}.$$
Substituting $N_k(x) = 2(2+x)^{k-1}$:
$$\frac{2}{27}\left(\frac{2+x}{27}\right)^{k-1} \;\le\; S_k \;\le\; 2\left(\frac
{2+x}{27}\right)^{k-1}.$$

**Convergence criterion.** The full series is $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} S_k$. Both bo
unds are constant multiples of $r^{k-1}$ where $r = \frac{2+x}{27}$.

- **If $r < 1$ (i.e., $x < 25$):** The upper bound $\sum_{k} 2\,r^{k-1}$ is a co
nvergent geometric series, so $\sum S_k$ converges by comparison.

- **If $r \ge 1$ (i.e., $x \ge 25$):** The lower bound $\sum_{k} \frac{2}{27}\,r
^{k-1}$ diverges (it is a constant times the harmonic-type sum $\sum 1$ when $r=
1$, or a divergent geometric series when $r>1$). Hence $\sum S_k$ diverges.

**Conclusion.** The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^{a(n)}}{n^3
}$ converges if and only if $\boxed{0 < x < 25}$.
