## Solution

We construct the Dirac operator on flat Euclidean space $\mathbb{E}^n$ step by s
tep: the Clifford algebra, the spinor bundle, the Levi-Civita (here trivial) con
nection, and finally the Dirac operator as the composition of Clifford multiplic
ation with the spin connection.

### 1. Clifford algebra

Let $V = \mathbb{R}^n$ with the standard positive-definite inner product $g = \d
elta_{ij}$. The **Clifford algebra** $\mathrm{Cl}(V,g) = \mathrm{Cl}_n$ is the a
ssociative algebra generated by $V$ subject to the relation
$$
v \cdot w + w \cdot v = -2\,g(v,w), \qquad v,w \in V.
$$
Equivalently, for the standard basis $e_1,\dots,e_n$,
$$
e_i \cdot e_j + e_j \cdot e_i = -2\delta_{ij}.
$$

### 2. Spin representation and spinor bundle

The spin group $\mathrm{Spin}(n) \subset \mathrm{Cl}_n^{\mathrm{even}}$ acts on
a complex vector space $\Sigma$ (the **spinor space**) of dimension $\dim_{\math
bb{C}}\Sigma = 2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$. Concretely, $\mathrm{Cl}_n \otimes \math
bb{C} \cong \mathrm{End}(\Sigma)$ for $n$ even, and $\cong \mathrm{End}(\Sigma)
\oplus \mathrm{End}(\Sigma)$ for $n$ odd; in either case we fix an irreducible c
omplex representation
$$
\rho : \mathrm{Cl}_n \otimes \mathbb{C} \longrightarrow \mathrm{End}(\Sigma).
$$
We write $\rho(v)(\psi) = v \cdot \psi$ for the **Clifford multiplication** of a
 vector $v$ on a spinor $\psi$.

On flat $\mathbb{E}^n$ the frame bundle is trivial, so the **spinor bundle** is
the trivial bundle
$$
\mathbb{S} = \mathbb{E}^n \times \Sigma \longrightarrow \mathbb{E}^n.
$$
Its sections are simply maps $\psi : \mathbb{E}^n \to \Sigma$, i.e. $\Gamma(\mat
hbb{S}) \cong C^\infty(\mathbb{E}^n,\Sigma)$.

### 3. Spin connection

Because $\mathbb{E}^n$ is flat and the frame bundle is trivial, the Levi-Civita
connection is the trivial connection: the Christoffel symbols vanish, $\Gamma^k_
{ij} = 0$, and the spin connection reduces to
$$
\nabla^{\mathbb{S}} = d,
$$
i.e. componentwise differentiation. In the global orthonormal frame $\{\partial/
\partial x^i\}$,
$$
(\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}_i \psi)(x) = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x^i}(x) \in \Sig
ma.
$$

### 4. Definition of the Dirac operator

The **Dirac operator** is defined as the composition
$$
D := \rho \circ \nabla^{\mathbb{S}} : \Gamma(\mathbb{S}) \xrightarrow{\nabla^{\m
athbb{S}}} \Gamma(T^*\mathbb{E}^n \otimes \mathbb{S}) \xrightarrow{\rho} \Gamma(
\mathbb{S}),
$$
where in the second step we use the metric to identify $T^*\mathbb{E}^n \cong T\
mathbb{E}^n$ and then Clifford-multiply.

Concretely, using the global orthonormal frame $\{e_i\} = \{\partial/\partial x^
i\}$ and Clifford multiplication $e_i \cdot$:

$$
\boxed{\,D \psi \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{n} e_i \cdot \nabla^{\mathbb{S}}_{e_i}\psi \;=
\; \sum_{i=1}^{n} e_i \cdot \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x^i}\,}
$$

or, writing $\gamma_i := \rho(e_i) \in \mathrm{End}(\Sigma)$ for the **Dirac gam
ma matrices** satisfying
$$
\gamma_i \gamma_j + \gamma_j \gamma_i = -2\delta_{ij}\,\mathrm{Id}_\Sigma,
$$

$$
\boxed{\,D \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{n} \gamma_i\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\,}
$$

This is the Dirac operator on flat Euclidean $\mathbb{E}^n$.

### 5. Verification of the key properties

**(a) Square gives the Laplacian.** Using the Clifford relations,
$$
D^2 \psi = \sum_{i,j} \gamma_i\gamma_j\,\partial_i\partial_j \psi.
$$
Split into $i=j$ and $i\neq j$:
$$
\gamma_i\gamma_i = -\mathrm{Id}_\Sigma \quad\text{(from } \gamma_i^2 = -\mathrm{
Id}\text{)},
$$
and for $i\neq j$, $\gamma_i\gamma_j + \gamma_j\gamma_i = 0$, while $\partial_i\
partial_j = \partial_j\partial_i$ is symmetric. Hence the antisymmetric part can
cels:
$$
\sum_{i\neq j}\gamma_i\gamma_j\,\partial_i\partial_j = \tfrac12\sum_{i\neq j}(\g
amma_i\gamma_j+\gamma_j\gamma_i)\partial_i\partial_j = 0.
$$
Therefore
$$
D^2 \psi = \sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_i^2\,\partial_i^2\psi = -\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_
i^2\psi = -\Delta\psi,
$$
i.e.
$$
\boxed{\,D^2 = -\Delta\,}
$$
where $\Delta = \sum_i \partial_i^2$ is the (positive) Laplace–Beltrami operator
. This is the **Lichnerowicz formula** $D^2 = \nabla^*\nabla + \tfrac14\,\mathrm
{scal}$, which on flat space reduces to $D^2 = -\Delta$ since the scalar curvatu
re $\mathrm{scal} \equiv 0$.

**(b) First-order, elliptic.** The principal symbol of $D$ at a cotangent vector
 $\xi = (\xi_i)$ is
$$
\sigma_D(\xi) = \sum_i \gamma_i\,\xi_i \in \mathrm{End}(\Sigma).
$$
Its square is
$$
\sigma_D(\xi)^2 = \sum_{i,j}\gamma_i\gamma_j\,\xi_i\xi_j = \sum_i \gamma_i^2 \xi
_i^2 = -|\xi|^2\,\mathrm{Id}_\Sigma,
$$
so $\sigma_D(\xi)$ is invertible for $\xi \neq 0$. Hence $D$ is elliptic of orde
r $1$.

**(c) Formal self-adjointness.** With respect to the $L^2$ inner product on spin
ors (using the Hermitian structure on $\Sigma$ and the volume form on $\mathbb{E
}^n$), each $\gamma_i$ is skew-Hermitian and $\partial_i$ is skew-adjoint (after
 integration by parts), so $D = \sum_i \gamma_i\partial_i$ is formally self-adjo
int:
$$
D^* = D.
$$

### 6. Summary

On flat Euclidean space $\mathbb{E}^n$ with global coordinates $x^1,\dots,x^n$ a
nd the trivial spin structure, the spinor bundle is $\mathbb{E}^n\times\Sigma$ a
nd the spin connection is ordinary differentiation. The Dirac operator is

$$
\boxed{\,D \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_i\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\,}
$$

where $\{\gamma_i\}_{i=1}^n$ are Dirac gamma matrices satisfying $\gamma_i\gamma
_j + \gamma_j\gamma_i = -2\delta_{ij}\,\mathrm{Id}$. It is a first-order ellipti
c, formally self-adjoint operator whose square is the negative Laplacian, $D^2 =
 -\Delta$.
