## Proof

Let $P = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : Ax \le b\}$ be a rational polyhedron, where $A \
in \mathbb{Q}^{m \times n}$ and $b \in \mathbb{Q}^m$. Denote the $i$-th row of $
A$ by $a_i^T$.

**Claim:** Every face $F$ of $P$ can be written as $F = \{x \in P : c^T x = d\}$
 for some $c \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ and $d \in \mathbb{Z}$.

### Case 1: $F = P$ (the improper face)

Take $c = \mathbf{0} \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ and $d = 0 \in \mathbb{Z}$. Then $\{x \in
 P : \mathbf{0}^T x = 0\} = P = F$. ✓

### Case 2: $F$ is a proper, non-empty face

**Step 1: Characterize $F$ via its active constraint set.**

Define $I(F) = \{i \in \{1,\dots,m\} : a_i^T x = b_i \text{ for all } x \in F\}$
. A standard result in polyhedral theory gives:

$$F = \{x \in P : a_i^T x = b_i \text{ for all } i \in I(F)\}.$$

Since $F$ is a **proper** face, $I(F) \neq \emptyset$. Moreover, there exists $j
 \in I(F)$ with $a_j \neq 0$. Indeed, if $a_i = 0$ for all $i \in I(F)$, then ea
ch constraint $a_i^T x = b_i$ is the condition $0 = b_i$, which is either satisf
ied everywhere (if $b_i \geq 0$, given the constraint $0 \le b_i$) or satisfied
nowhere (if $b_i < 0$, making $P = \emptyset$). In the former case these constra
ints impose no restriction, giving $F = P$, contradicting properness; in the lat
ter, $P = \emptyset$, contradicting $F \neq \emptyset$.

**Step 2: Construct a rational supporting functional defining exactly $F$.**

Choose coefficients $\lambda_i > 0$ for all $i \in I(F)$ as follows: set $\lambd
a_j = 1$ for the index $j$ with $a_j \neq 0$ identified above, and $\lambda_i =
\varepsilon$ for $i \in I(F) \setminus \{j\}$, where $\varepsilon > 0$ is a smal
l rational number.

Define:
$$c = \sum_{i \in I(F)} \lambda_i\, a_i, \qquad d = \sum_{i \in I(F)} \lambda_i\
, b_i.$$

Since all $a_i, b_i, \lambda_i$ are rational, both $c$ and $d$ are rational. Sin
ce $a_j \neq 0$ and $c = a_j + \varepsilon \sum_{i \neq j} a_i$, we have $c \neq
 0$ for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon > 0$ (the map $\varepsilon \mapsto c
$ is continuous and non-zero at $\varepsilon = 0$). Choose such an $\varepsilon$
.

**Step 3: Verify the supporting property and exactness.**

- **Supporting inequality:** For any $x \in P$, since $a_i^T x \le b_i$ for all
$i$ and $\lambda_i > 0$:
$$c^T x = \sum_{i \in I(F)} \lambda_i\, a_i^T x \le \sum_{i \in I(F)} \lambda_i\
, b_i = d.$$

- **Exactness:** $c^T x = d$ holds iff $\sum \lambda_i(a_i^T x - b_i) = 0$. Sinc
e each term $\lambda_i(a_i^T x - b_i) \le 0$ (with $\lambda_i > 0$), the sum is
zero iff each term is zero, i.e., $a_i^T x = b_i$ for all $i \in I(F)$. Therefor
e:
$$\{x \in P : c^T x = d\} = \{x \in P : a_i^T x = b_i,\; i \in I(F)\} = F.$$

**Step 4: Clear denominators to obtain integral data.**

Since $c \in \mathbb{Q}^n$ and $d \in \mathbb{Q}$, let $L$ be a positive common
multiple of all denominators of the components of $c$ and $d$. Set:
$$c' = L \cdot c \in \mathbb{Z}^n, \qquad d' = L \cdot d \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

Since $L > 0$, the inequality $c'^T x \le d'$ on $P$ and the equality condition
are preserved. Thus:
$$F = \{x \in P : c'^T x = d'\}, \qquad c' \in \mathbb{Z}^n,\; d' \in \mathbb{Z}
. \quad \checkmark$$

### Conclusion

Both cases are covered: the improper face $P$ (trivially, with $c = 0, d = 0$) a
nd every proper non-empty face (via the construction above). Therefore, **yes**:
 every face $F$ of a rational polyhedron $P = \{x : Ax \le b\}$ can be expressed
 as $F = \{x \in P : c^T x = d\}$ with $c \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ and $d \in \mathbb{Z
}$.

The essential reason is that the normal cone of any face is generated by rationa
l vectors (the rows of $A$ corresponding to active constraints), so a strictly p
ositive rational combination yields a rational supporting functional defining ex
actly that face; clearing denominators then produces integral data without chang
ing the face. $\blacksquare$
