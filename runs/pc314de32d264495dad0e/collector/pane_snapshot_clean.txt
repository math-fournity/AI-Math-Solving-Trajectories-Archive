# Solution

**Answer: Yes, $L/K$ must be unramified.**

## Proof

Let $K_n$ denote the unramified extension of $K$ of degree $n$. The hypothesis i
s:
$$N_{K_n/K}(K_n^*) \subseteq N_{L/K}(L^*).$$

**Step 1: Compute the norm group of the unramified extension.**

Since $K_n/K$ is unramified of degree $n$, a uniformizer $\pi$ of $K$ is also a
uniformizer of $K_n$. The norm of $\pi \in K \subset K_n$ is $N_{K_n/K}(\pi) = \
pi^n$. On the unit groups, the norm map $N_{K_n/K}: \mathcal{O}_{K_n}^* \to \mat
hcal{O}_K^*$ is surjective (the residue field extension $\mathbb{F}_{q^n}/\mathb
b{F}_q$ is cyclic, so the norm on residue fields is surjective, and Hensel's lem
ma lifts this to units). Therefore:
$$N_{K_n/K}(K_n^*) = \pi^{n\mathbb{Z}} \cdot \mathcal{O}_K^*.$$

**Step 2: Apply the Galois correspondence of local class field theory.**

By local class field theory, the reciprocity map establishes an **order-reversin
g** bijection between finite abelian extensions of $K$ and open subgroups of $K^
*$ of finite index, via $E \mapsto N_{E/K}(E^*)$. Concretely, for finite abelian
 extensions $E_1/K$ and $E_2/K$:
$$N_{E_1/K}(E_1^*) \subseteq N_{E_2/K}(E_2^*) \iff E_2 \subseteq E_1.$$

Applying this to the hypothesis $N_{K_n/K}(K_n^*) \subseteq N_{L/K}(L^*)$ with $
E_1 = K_n$ and $E_2 = L$, we obtain:
$$L \subseteq K_n.$$

**Step 3: Conclude.**

Since $K_n/K$ is unramified, every intermediate extension $L$ with $K \subseteq
L \subseteq K_n$ is also unramified over $K$ (unramifiedness is preserved under
taking subextensions: the ramification index and residue degree are multiplicati
ve in towers, and $e(K_n/K)=1$ forces $e(L/K)=1$).

Therefore $L/K$ is unramified. $\blacksquare$
