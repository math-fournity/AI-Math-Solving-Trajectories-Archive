## Proof

Let $\mathbb{R}_c$ denote the set of all computable real numbers, and let $\math
bb{R}_c \cap (0,1)$ denote the computable numbers in the open interval $(0,1)$.

**Claim:** $|\mathbb{R}_c \cap (0,1)| = |\mathbb{R}_c|$.

**Step 1: A computability-preserving bijection between $(0,1)$ and $\mathbb{R}$.
**

Consider the map
$$\varphi : (0,1) \to \mathbb{R}, \qquad \varphi(x) = \tan\!\big(\pi(x - \tfrac{
1}{2})\big).$$
As $x$ ranges over $(0,1)$, the argument $\pi(x - \tfrac{1}{2})$ ranges over $(-
\tfrac{\pi}{2}, \tfrac{\pi}{2})$, on which $\tan$ is a strictly increasing bijec
tion onto $\mathbb{R}$. Hence $\varphi$ is a bijection between $(0,1)$ and $\mat
hbb{R}$, with inverse
$$\varphi^{-1}(y) = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{\pi}\arctan(y).$$

**Step 2: $\varphi$ restricts to a bijection between the computable elements.**

By hypothesis, the tangent function preserves computability in both directions:
$x$ is computable iff $\tan(x)$ is computable (whenever both are defined). The f
unctions involved in $\varphi$ and $\varphi^{-1}$ are:
- $x \mapsto \pi(x - \tfrac{1}{2})$: a composition of computable operations (the
 constant $\pi$ is computable, addition and multiplication by computable constan
ts preserve computability),
- $\tan$: preserves computability in both directions by hypothesis,
- $y \mapsto \tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{\pi}\arctan(y)$: $\arctan$ is the inverse
of $\tan$ on $(-\tfrac{\pi}{2},\tfrac{\pi}{2})$, so it preserves computability i
n both directions by the same hypothesis; the constants $\tfrac{1}{2}$ and $\tfr
ac{1}{\pi}$ are computable; addition and multiplication by computable constants
preserve computability.

Therefore:
- If $x \in \mathbb{R}_c \cap (0,1)$, then $\varphi(x) \in \mathbb{R}_c$.
- If $y \in \mathbb{R}_c$, then $\varphi^{-1}(y) \in \mathbb{R}_c \cap (0,1)$.

Since $\varphi$ and $\varphi^{-1}$ are mutual inverses and both preserve computa
bility, the restriction
$$\varphi\big|_{\mathbb{R}_c \cap (0,1)} : \mathbb{R}_c \cap (0,1) \to \mathbb{R
}_c$$
is a bijection.

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

A bijection between two sets exists if and only if they have the same cardinalit
y. We have exhibited an explicit bijection $\varphi$ between $\mathbb{R}_c \cap
(0,1)$ and $\mathbb{R}_c$. Hence
$$|\mathbb{R}_c \cap (0,1)| = |\mathbb{R}_c|.$$

(Both sets are in fact countably infinite — $\mathbb{R}_c$ is countable since co
mputable reals are indexed by Turing machines, and $\mathbb{R}_c \cap (0,1)$ is
infinite since it contains all rationals in $(0,1)$ — but the bijection above es
tablishes the equality of cardinalities directly and constructively, using exact
ly the hypothesis provided.)
