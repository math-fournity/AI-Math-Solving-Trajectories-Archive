## Solution

We seek the smallest positive integer $k$ with $10^k \equiv 1 \pmod{11^{10}}$, i
.e., the multiplicative order of $10$ modulo $11^{10}$.

**Step 1: Order of 10 mod 11.**

Since $10 \equiv -1 \pmod{11}$, we have $10^2 \equiv 1 \pmod{11}$ and $10 \not\e
quiv 1 \pmod{11}$. Thus $\operatorname{ord}_{11}(10) = 2$.

**Step 2: Check the lifting condition.**

Compute $10^2 = 100 = 1 + 99 = 1 + 9 \cdot 11$. Since $11 \nmid 9$, we have
$$10^2 \not\equiv 1 \pmod{11^2}.$$

**Step 3: Apply the order-lifting lemma.**

> **Lemma.** Let $p$ be an odd prime, $\gcd(a,p)=1$, and let $d = \operatorname{
ord}_p(a)$. If $a^d \not\equiv 1 \pmod{p^2}$, then for every $n \geq 1$,
> $$\operatorname{ord}_{p^n}(a) = d \cdot p^{n-1}.$$

*Proof of lemma.* We show by induction on $n$ that $a^{d \cdot p^{n-1}} = 1 + c_
n\, p^n$ with $p \nmid c_n$. The base case $n=1$ holds by hypothesis: $a^d = 1 +
 c_1\, p$ with $p \nmid c_1$. For the inductive step, write $a^{d\cdot p^{n-1}}
= 1 + c_n\, p^n$ with $p \nmid c_n$. Then
$$a^{d\cdot p^n} = (1 + c_n\, p^n)^p = 1 + c_n\, p^{n+1} + \binom{p}{2}c_n^2\, p
^{2n} + \cdots$$
For $p$ odd, every term beyond the first two is divisible by $p^{n+2}$ (the $j$-
th term has $p$-adic valuation $\geq n+1 + (j-1)(n-1) \geq n+2$ for $j \geq 2$,
using $\nu_p(\binom{p}{j}) \geq 1$ for $1 \leq j \leq p-1$). Hence
$$a^{d\cdot p^n} = 1 + p^{n+1}\!\left(c_n + (\text{multiple of }p)\right),$$
so $c_{n+1} \equiv c_n \pmod{p}$, giving $p \nmid c_{n+1}$. This proves $a^{d\cd
ot p^{n-1}} \equiv 1 \pmod{p^n}$ but $\not\equiv 1 \pmod{p^{n+1}}$, establishing
 the order as $d\cdot p^{n-1}$. $\square$

**Step 4: Compute the answer.**

With $a=10$, $p=11$, $d=2$, and the lifting condition verified in Step 2, the le
mma gives
$$\operatorname{ord}_{11^{10}}(10) = 2 \cdot 11^{9}.$$

Computing $11^9$:
$$11^9 = 2357947691,$$
so
$$k = 2 \times 2357947691 = 4715895382.$$

**Verification of minimality.** The order $2 \cdot 11^9$ must divide $\varphi(11
^{10}) = 10 \cdot 11^9$. Indeed $2 \cdot 11^9 \mid 10 \cdot 11^9 = 2 \cdot 5 \cd
ot 11^9$. ✓ The lemma guarantees no smaller exponent works, since the order incr
eases by a factor of $11$ at each lifting step from $11^1$ to $11^{10}$, startin
g from $2$.

$$\boxed{k = 2 \cdot 11^9 = 4{,}715{,}895{,}382}$$
