Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum number of consecutive intege
rs $k$ such that $k \mid P_k$, where $P_k = p_1 p_2 \cdots p_k$ is the product o
f the first $k$ primes.

## Key Observation: Reduction to Squarefree

**$P_k$ is squarefree.** Since $P_k = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 \cdots p_k$ is a
 product of *distinct* primes, every prime appears with exponent exactly 1.

**Forward direction:** If $k \mid P_k$, then $k$ is a divisor of a squarefree nu
mber, so $k$ itself must be squarefree (any prime squared dividing $k$ would hav
e to divide $P_k$, impossible).

**Backward direction:** Suppose $k$ is squarefree, so $k = q_1 q_2 \cdots q_m$ w
ith distinct primes $q_i$. Each $q_i \leq k$. For $k \geq 2$, we have $k < p_k$
(since $p_k \geq k+1$ by induction: $p_1=2, p_2=3, \ldots$). So each $q_i < p_k$
, meaning $q_i$ is one of the primes $p_1, \ldots, p_{k-1} \subset \{p_1, \ldots
, p_k\}$. Thus each $q_i \mid P_k$, and since they're distinct, $k \mid P_k$. Fo
r $k=1$: $1 \mid P_1 = 2$ trivially.

**Conclusion:** $k$ is efficient $\iff$ $k$ is squarefree.

## Maximum Consecutive Squarefree Integers

**Upper bound — at most 3:** Among any 4 consecutive integers $n, n+1, n+2, n+3$
, exactly one is divisible by $4 = 2^2$. That integer is not squarefree. So 4 co
nsecutive squarefree integers are impossible.

**Lower bound — 3 is achievable:** Consider $k = 1, 2, 3$:
- $k=1$: $P_1 = 2$, and $1 \mid 2$. ✓
- $k=2$: $P_2 = 2 \cdot 3 = 6$, and $2 \mid 6$. ✓
- $k=3$: $P_3 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 = 30$, and $3 \mid 30$. ✓

All three are squarefree (1 is vacuously squarefree, 2 and 3 are prime). Meanwhi
le $k=4 = 2^2$ is not squarefree, and indeed $4 \nmid P_4 = 210$.

(Other examples of 3 consecutive efficient values exist, e.g., $k=5,6,7$: $5\mid
 2310$, $6\mid 2310$, $7\mid 2310$, but $8=2^3$ fails.)

## Answer

The maximum possible number of consecutive efficient batch sizes is $\boxed{3}$.
