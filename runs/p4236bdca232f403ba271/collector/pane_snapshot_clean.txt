## Proof

**Step 1: Reduction via the Universal Coefficient Theorem.**

Let $S$ be a non-abelian finite simple group and let $A = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
$ with trivial $S$-action. The Universal Coefficient Theorem for group cohomolog
y gives the short exact sequence:

$$0 \to \operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(H_1(S, \mathbb{Z}),\, A) \to H^2(S, A)
 \to \operatorname{Hom}(H_2(S, \mathbb{Z}),\, A) \to 0.$$

Since $S$ is non-abelian simple, $S$ is perfect: $[S, S] = S$, so the abelianiza
tion $S_{\mathrm{ab}} = S/[S,S] = 0$. Therefore:

$$H_1(S, \mathbb{Z}) = S_{\mathrm{ab}} = 0,$$

which forces $\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(0, A) = 0$, and we obtain an iso
morphism:

$$H^2(S, \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(H_2(S, \mathbb{Z}),\,
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}).$$

**Step 2: Identifying $H_2(S, \mathbb{Z})$ with the Schur multiplier.**

By definition, the Schur multiplier of $S$ is $M(S) = H_2(S, \mathbb{Z})$. Thus:

$$H^2(S, \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(M(S),\, \mathbb{Z}/p\m
athbb{Z}).$$

Since $M(S)$ is a finite abelian group, $\operatorname{Hom}(M(S), \mathbb{Z}/p\m
athbb{Z})$ is non-trivial if and only if $M(S)$ has an element of order $p$, equ
ivalently, $p \mid |M(S)|$.

**Step 3: Exhibiting a group with odd prime dividing its Schur multiplier.**

Take $S = A_6$, the alternating group on 6 letters. Then:

- $A_6$ is non-abelian (since $n \geq 5$) and simple (since $n \geq 5$).
- The Schur multiplier of $A_6$ is $M(A_6) \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$.

This is a classical result. It can be seen via the exceptional isomorphism $A_6
\cong \operatorname{PSL}(2, 9)$. The Schur multiplier of $\operatorname{PSL}(2,
q)$ is generically $\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(2, q-1)$, but $q = 9$ is an exceptional case
 carrying an additional factor of $\mathbb{Z}/3$, yielding:

$$M(\operatorname{PSL}(2, 9)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/3 \cong \math
bb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}.$$

The extra $\mathbb{Z}/3$ factor corresponds to the existence of the triple cover
 $3.A_6$, a well-documented group in the ATLAS of Finite Groups. Concretely, $\o
peratorname{SL}(2,9) \to \operatorname{PSL}(2,9)$ provides the double cover (the
 $\mathbb{Z}/2$ part), while the triple cover arises from the exceptional outer
automorphism structure of $A_6$ (which has outer automorphism group $\mathbb{Z}/
2 \times \mathbb{Z}/2$, reflecting the multiple non-equivalent descriptions of $
A_6$ as $\operatorname{PSL}(2,9)$, $\operatorname{Sp}(4,2)'$, etc.).

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Set $p = 3$. Since $3 \mid |M(A_6)| = 6$:

$$H^2(A_6,\, \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/6\mathb
b{Z},\, \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \neq 0.$$

Therefore, **yes**, such a group exists: $S = A_6$ with $p = 3$ satisfies $H^2(S
, \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \neq 0$.
