## Proof

**Setup and notation.** Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a separable Hilbert space. The **st
rong (operator) topology** on $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})$ is generated by seminor
ms $p_\xi(A) = \|A\xi\|$. The **strong\* topology** is generated by seminorms $p
_\xi(A) = \|A\xi\|$ and $q_\xi(A) = \|A^*\xi\|$. Since the strong* topology has
all the seminorms of the strong topology and more, it is **finer**: $\text{stron
g} \subseteq \text{strong*}$.

**Step 1: $S \subseteq T$.** If $f$ is strong-continuous, then $f^{-1}(U)$ is st
rong-open for every open $U \subseteq \mathbb{C}$. Since strong-open sets are st
rong*-open (the strong* topology is finer), $f^{-1}(U)$ is strong*-open. Hence $
f$ is strong*-continuous. So $S \subseteq T$ and $S \cap T = S$.

**Step 2: Characterization of $S$.** A linear functional $f$ is strong-continuou
s iff there exist $\xi_1,\dots,\xi_n \in \mathcal{H}$ such that $f$ factors thro
ugh $A \mapsto (A\xi_1,\dots,A\xi_n) \in \mathcal{H}^n$. By the Riesz representa
tion theorem applied to $\mathcal{H}^n$, there exist $\eta_1,\dots,\eta_n \in \m
athcal{H}$ with:
$$f(A) = \sum_{i=1}^n \langle A\xi_i, \eta_i\rangle.$$
These are exactly the **finite-rank normal functionals** (equivalently, the WOT-
continuous functionals). This is the classical result that WOT, SOT, and strong*
 all share the same continuous linear functionals (see Takesaki, *Theory of Oper
ator Algebras I*, or Kadison–Ringrose, Ch. 7).

**Step 3: $T \subseteq S$ (the key step).** Suppose $f$ is strong*-continuous an
d **linear** (over $\mathbb{C}$). Then $f$ factors through the map:
$$\Phi: A \mapsto (A\xi_1,\dots,A\xi_n,\, A^*\xi_1',\dots,A^*\xi_m') \in \mathca
l{H}^{n+m}$$
for some finite collection of vectors, giving:
$$f(A) = \sum_{i=1}^n \langle A\xi_i, \eta_i\rangle + \sum_{j=1}^m \langle A^*\x
i_j', \eta_j'\rangle.$$

The first sum is **linear** in $A$. The second sum is **conjugate-linear** in $A
$, since $(\lambda A)^* = \bar{\lambda}A^*$ implies $\langle (\lambda A)^*\xi_j'
, \eta_j'\rangle = \bar{\lambda}\langle A^*\xi_j',\eta_j'\rangle$.

Since $f$ is given to be linear, the conjugate-linear part must vanish identical
ly: $\sum_{j=1}^m \langle A^*\xi_j', \eta_j'\rangle = 0$ for all $A$. This means
 $\sum_j \langle A\eta_j', \xi_j'\rangle = 0$ for all $A$, i.e., the finite-rank
 operator $R = \sum_j |\xi_j'\rangle\langle \eta_j'|$ satisfies $\operatorname{t
r}(RA) = 0$ for all $A$, forcing $R = 0$.

Therefore $f(A) = \sum_{i=1}^n \langle A\xi_i, \eta_i\rangle$, which is in $S$.
Hence $T \subseteq S$.

**Step 4: Conclusion $S = T$.** From Steps 1 and 3, $S \subseteq T$ and $T \subs
eteq S$, so:
$$S = T = S \cap T = \left\{A \mapsto \sum_{i=1}^n \langle A\xi_i, \eta_i\rangle
 : n \in \mathbb{N},\ \xi_i, \eta_i \in \mathcal{H}\right\}.$$

**Step 5: Cardinality for separable $\mathcal{H}$.** Each such functional corres
ponds to a **finite-rank operator** $R = \sum_i |\eta_i\rangle\langle\xi_i|$ via
 $f(A) = \operatorname{tr}(RA)$. The set of finite-rank operators on a separable
 (infinite-dimensional) Hilbert space is:
$$\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} \{R \in \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}) : \operatorname{rank}(
R) \leq n\}.$$
- Each $\mathcal{H}$ has cardinality $\mathfrak{c} = 2^{\aleph_0}$ (separable Hi
lbert space $\cong \ell^2$).
- Rank-$n$ operators are parametrized by $2n$ vectors, giving cardinality $\math
frak{c}^{2n} = \mathfrak{c}$.
- Countable union: $\aleph_0 \cdot \mathfrak{c} = \mathfrak{c}$.
- Lower bound: rank-1 operators alone already give $\mathfrak{c}$ distinct funct
ionals.

Therefore:
$$|S \cap T| = |S| = |T| = \mathfrak{c} = 2^{\aleph_0}.$$

## Answer

The strong and strong* topologies on $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})$ yield **identica
l** sets of continuous linear functionals — both equal the set of finite-rank no
rmal functionals. Hence $S = T = S \cap T$, and for a separable Hilbert space, t
he number of distinct elements is:

$$\boxed{2^{\aleph_0}}$$

(uncountably infinitely many — the cardinality of the continuum).
