Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the product of $k$ pairwise
 distinct Galois radii of an integer $n$ is always a $k$-quasi-Galois radius of
$n$.

## Definitions (Natural Interpretation)

A **Galois radius** of a non-negative integer $n$ is a non-negative integer $r$
such that $n - r$ is a perfect square, i.e., $r = n - a^2$ for some integer $a$
with $0 \le a \le \lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor$.

A **$k$-quasi-Galois radius** of $n$ is a non-negative integer $r$ such that $n
- r$ is a perfect $k$-th power.

Note that for $k=2$, the $2$-quasi-Galois radii coincide with the Galois radii.

## Answer: No

**Counterexample:** $n = 6$, $k = 2$.

**Step 1: Find the Galois radii of 6.**

We compute $r = 6 - a^2$ for $a = 0, 1, 2$:

| $a$ | $a^2$ | $r = 6 - a^2$ | $n - r = a^2$ | Perfect square? |
|-----|-------|---------------|----------------|------------------|
| 0   | 0     | 6             | 0 = $0^2$      | ✓                |
| 1   | 1     | 5             | 1 = $1^2$      | ✓                |
| 2   | 4     | 2             | 4 = $2^2$      | ✓                |

The Galois radii of $6$ are $\{6, 5, 2\}$.

**Step 2: Select two pairwise distinct Galois radii.**

Take $r_1 = 5$ and $r_2 = 2$. These are distinct Galois radii since:
- $6 - 5 = 1 = 1^2$ ✓
- $6 - 2 = 4 = 2^2$ ✓
- $5 \neq 2$ ✓

**Step 3: Compute the product and test whether it is a $2$-quasi-Galois radius.*
*

$$r_1 \cdot r_2 = 5 \times 2 = 10$$

For $10$ to be a $2$-quasi-Galois radius of $6$, we need $6 - 10 = -4$ to be a p
erfect square.

Since $-4 < 0$, it is **not** a perfect square (perfect squares are non-negative
).

**Conclusion:** The product $r_1 \cdot r_2 = 10$ is not a $2$-quasi-Galois radiu
s of $6$.

Therefore, the product of $k$ pairwise distinct Galois radii of an integer $n$ i
s **not** always a $k$-quasi-Galois radius of $n$.
