# Brachistochrone: Variational Derivation

## Setup and convention

Place the origin at the starting point $(0,0)$. Take the $y$-axis pointing **dow
nward** (in the direction of gravity), so the bead descends to the endpoint $(a,
b)$ with $b>0$. The bead starts from rest, so by conservation of mechanical ener
gy the speed at depth $y$ is

$$v(y)=\sqrt{2gy}.$$

Let the wire be the graph $y=y(x)$, $x\in[0,a]$, with $y(0)=0$, $y(a)=b$. The tr
avel time is

$$T[y]=\int_{0}^{a}\frac{ds}{v}=\int_{0}^{a}\frac{\sqrt{1+y'^{2}}}{\sqrt{2gy}}\,
dx.$$

## The functional and its first integral

The Lagrangian (dropping the constant $1/\sqrt{2g}$, which does not affect the m
inimizer) is

$$L(y,y')=\frac{\sqrt{1+y'^{2}}}{\sqrt{y}}.$$

It has no explicit $x$-dependence, so the Beltrami identity applies:

$$L-y'\frac{\partial L}{\partial y'}=\text{const}.$$

Compute

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial y'}=\frac{y'}{\sqrt{y}\sqrt{1+y'^{2}}},$$

hence

$$L-y'\frac{\partial L}{\partial y'}=\frac{\sqrt{1+y'^{2}}}{\sqrt{y}}-\frac{y'^{
2}}{\sqrt{y}\sqrt{1+y'^{2}}}
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{y}\sqrt{1+y'^{2}}}.$$

Setting this equal to a constant $1/k$ (with $k>0$) gives the **Euler–Lagrange f
irst integral**

$$\boxed{\,y\,(1+y'^{2})=k^{2}\,}.\tag{1}$$

## Solving the first integral

From (1),

$$y'^{2}=\frac{k^{2}-y}{y},\qquad \frac{dx}{dy}=\sqrt{\frac{y}{k^{2}-y}}.$$

Substitute $y=k^{2}\sin^{2}\phi$ (so $\sqrt{y}=k\sin\phi$, $\sqrt{k^{2}-y}=k\cos
\phi$, $dy=2k^{2}\sin\phi\cos\phi\,d\phi$):

$$dx=\frac{k\sin\phi}{k\cos\phi}\,2k^{2}\sin\phi\cos\phi\,d\phi=2k^{2}\sin^{2}\p
hi\,d\phi
=k^{2}(1-\cos 2\phi)\,d\phi.$$

Integrating,

$$x=k^{2}\!\left(\phi-\tfrac{1}{2}\sin 2\phi\right)+C,\qquad y=k^{2}\sin^{2}\phi
.$$

The starting condition $x=y=0$ at $\phi=0$ gives $C=0$. Now set $\theta=2\phi$ a
nd $R=k^{2}/2$:

$$\boxed{\;x=R(\theta-\sin\theta),\qquad y=R(1-\cos\theta),\qquad \theta\in[0,\t
heta_{1}]\;}\tag{2}$$

with $R>0$. This is a **cycloid**: the curve traced by a point on the rim of a c
ircle of radius $R$ rolling along the line $y=0$ (the horizontal through the rel
ease point).

## Verification

From (2), $\dot x=R(1-\cos\theta)$, $\dot y=R\sin\theta$, so

$$y'=\frac{\dot y}{\dot x}=\frac{\sin\theta}{1-\cos\theta}=\cot\frac{\theta}{2},
\qquad
1+y'^{2}=\csc^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}.$$

Then

$$y(1+y'^{2})=R(1-\cos\theta)\csc^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}=R\cdot 2\sin^{2}\frac{\the
ta}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}}=2R=k^{2},$$

which reproduces (1) with $k^{2}=2R$. The first integral is satisfied identicall
y. ✓

## Determining the constants

The two parameters $R$ and $\theta_{1}$ are fixed by the endpoint $(a,b)$:

$$a=R(\theta_{1}-\sin\theta_{1}),\qquad b=R(1-\cos\theta_{1}).$$

Dividing eliminates $R$:

$$\frac{b}{a}=\frac{1-\cos\theta_{1}}{\theta_{1}-\sin\theta_{1}}.\tag{3}$$

The function on the right increases from $0$ (at $\theta_{1}\to 0$) through $2/\
pi\approx 0.637$ (at $\theta_{1}=\pi$) and grows without bound as $\theta_{1}\to
 2\pi$, so for every physically relevant ratio $b/a>0$ there is a unique $\theta
_{1}\in(0,2\pi)$ solving (3). Then

$$R=\frac{b}{1-\cos\theta_{1}}.$$

## Why it is a minimum (not merely stationary)

The cycloid (2) is the unique smooth extremal of the time functional. The second
-variation / Jacobi analysis for the brachistochrone functional shows that the f
irst conjugate point of the origin occurs at $\theta=2\pi$ (the next cusp of the
 cycloid). Since the physical endpoint always has $\theta_{1}<2\pi$, no conjugat
e point is reached before $(a,b)$, so the Jacobi (Legendre–Clebsch) sufficient c
ondition is met: the cycloid gives a **strict local minimum** of $T$. Combined w
ith the Weierstrass condition (the integrand is convex in $y'$ for $y>0$), this
establishes the cycloid as the global minimizer among admissible curves.

## Physical character of the solution

- At the release point $\theta=0$ the cycloid has a **cusp**: $\dot x=\dot y=0$
and the tangent is vertical, so the bead immediately acquires speed along a stee
p (vertical) tangent — this is precisely what makes the cycloid faster than the
straight chord, which wastes the initial drop on a shallow slope.
- The travel time along the cycloid is remarkably

$$T=\sqrt{\frac{R}{g}}\,\theta_{1},$$

independent of the bead's mass, and proportional to $\theta_{1}$ (hence to the a
rc parameter, not to the chord length).

## Conclusion

The wire of fastest descent — the **brachistochrone** — is an arc of a cycloid,
parametrized by

$$\boxed{\,x(\theta)=R(\theta-\sin\theta),\quad y(\theta)=R(1-\cos\theta),\quad
0\le\theta\le\theta_{1}\,}$$

with $R$ and $\theta_{1}\in(0,2\pi)$ uniquely determined by the endpoint conditi
ons

$$a=R(\theta_{1}-\sin\theta_{1}),\qquad b=R(1-\cos\theta_{1}).$$

Equivalently, it is the curve generated by a point on the rim of a circle of rad
ius $R$ rolling along the horizontal through the release point, starting from a
cusp at the origin and ending at $(a,b)$.
