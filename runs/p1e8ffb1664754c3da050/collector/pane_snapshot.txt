The set $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$ with the product topology is a compact abelian gr
oup (the Cantor group, isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ raised to a counta
ble product). Its Haar measure $\lambda$ is the product measure obtained by plac
ing the uniform probability measure (mass $1/2$ on each of $\{0\}$ and $\{1\}$)
on every coordinate factor.

Consider the singleton $\{(0,0,\ldots)\}$. For each $n\geq 1$, the cylinder set
$$C_n := \{x \in \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}} : x_1 = x_2 = \cdots = x_n = 0\}$$
contains $\{(0,0,\ldots)\}$, and by the product structure of Haar measure,
$$\lambda(C_n) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} \lambda_i(\{0\}) = \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^n.
$$

The singleton is the intersection of the decreasing sequence of cylinder sets:
$$\{(0,0,\ldots)\} = \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} C_n.$$

Since $\lambda$ is a finite (probability) measure and $C_1 \supset C_2 \supset \
cdots$ with $\lambda(C_1) = 1/2 < \infty$, continuity from above gives
$$\lambda\!\left(\{(0,0,\ldots)\}\right) = \lambda\!\left(\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}
 C_n\right) = \lim_{n\to\infty} \lambda(C_n) = \lim_{n\to\infty} \left(\tfrac{1}
{2}\right)^n = 0.$$

Therefore,
$$\boxed{\lambda\!\left(\{(0,0,\ldots)\}\right) = 0.}$$
