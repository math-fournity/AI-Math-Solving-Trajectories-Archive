## Proof

**Definition.** $(A^k)_{k \in \mathbb{N}}$ is *eventually periodic* if $\exists
N, p > 0$ such that $A^{k+p} = A^k$ for all $k \geq N$.

### Step 1: Characterize eventual periodicity via the minimal polynomial

**Claim.** $(A^k)$ is eventually periodic if and only if the minimal polynomial
$m_A(x) \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ of $A$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ has the form
$$m_A(x) = x^a \cdot g(x), \quad a \geq 0,$$
where $g \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ is monic, $g(0) \neq 0$, $g$ is squarefree, and ever
y irreducible factor of $g$ is a cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_d$.

**Proof of $(\Rightarrow)$.** Suppose $A^{k+p} = A^k$ for all $k \geq N$. Over $
\mathbb{C}$, write $A = PJP^{-1}$ with $J$ in Jordan normal form. Then $J^{k+p}
= J^k$ for all $k \geq N$, so each Jordan block $J_{\lambda, m}$ (eigenvalue $\l
ambda$, size $m$) satisfies $J_{\lambda,m}^{k+p} = J_{\lambda,m}^k$ for all $k \
geq N$.

The $(i,j)$-entry of $J_{\lambda,m}^k$ is $\binom{k}{j-i}\lambda^{k-(j-i)}$ for
$j \geq i$.

- **Diagonal** ($j = i$): $\lambda^{k+p} = \lambda^k$ for all $k \geq N$. If $\l
ambda \neq 0$, this forces $\lambda^p = 1$, so $\lambda$ is a root of unity.

- **Off-diagonal** ($j > i$, $\lambda \neq 0$): Using $\lambda^p = 1$, the condi
tion reduces to $\binom{k+p}{j-i} = \binom{k}{j-i}$ for all $k \geq N$. For $j -
 i = 1$, this gives $(k+p) - k = p = 0$, a contradiction. So $m = 1$ for every n
onzero eigenvalue.

- **$\lambda = 0$**: $J_{0,m}^k = 0$ for $k \geq m$, so the condition is automat
ically satisfied.

Since the minimal polynomial is $m_A(x) = \prod_\lambda (x-\lambda)^{e_\lambda}$
 where $e_\lambda$ is the largest Jordan block size for $\lambda$, we get $e_\la
mbda = 1$ for all $\lambda \neq 0$ and $e_0$ arbitrary. Over $\mathbb{Q}$, the n
onzero eigenvalues (roots of unity) have minimal polynomials that are cyclotomic
, each appearing with multiplicity 1. Hence $m_A(x) = x^{e_0} \cdot g(x)$ with $
g$ squarefree and all irreducible factors of $g$ cyclotomic. $\checkmark$

**Proof of $(\Leftarrow)$.** If $m_A(x) = x^a \cdot g(x)$ with $g$ squarefree an
d all irreducible factors of $g$ cyclotomic, then over $\mathbb{C}$: eigenvalue
$0$ has Jordan blocks of size $\leq a$, and every nonzero eigenvalue is a root o
f unity with Jordan block size $1$. The nilpotent part vanishes for $k \geq a$.
The semisimple part has eigenvalues that are roots of unity; let $p = \operatorn
ame{lcm}$ of their orders. Then $A^{k+p} = A^k$ for all $k \geq a$. $\checkmark$

### Step 2: The characterization is decidable

Given $A \in \operatorname{Mat}(n \times n, \mathbb{Z})$:

1. **Compute $m_A(x)$.** Find the smallest $d$ such that $\{I, A, \ldots, A^d\}$
 are linearly dependent over $\mathbb{Q}$, via Gaussian elimination with exact r
ational arithmetic. This yields $m_A(x) \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ monic.

2. **Factor $m_A(x)$ over $\mathbb{Z}$:** Write $m_A(x) = x^a \cdot g_1(x)^{b_1}
 \cdots g_r(x)^{b_r}$ with $g_i$ distinct monic irreducible, $g_i(0) \neq 0$. Po
lynomial factorization over $\mathbb{Z}$ is algorithmic (e.g., LLL-based factori
zation).

3. **Check squarefreeness:** Verify each $b_i = 1$.

4. **Check each $g_i$ is cyclotomic:** For each $g_i$ of degree $d_i$, enumerate
 all positive integers $n$ with $\varphi(n) = d_i$. This set is **finite** becau
se $\varphi(n) \geq \sqrt{n/2}$ for $n \geq 3$ (a classical bound), so $n \leq 2
d_i^2$. For each such $n$, compute $\Phi_n(x)$ and check whether $g_i = \Phi_n$.

All four steps are algorithmic with exact arithmetic, so membership in $B_n$ is
decidable.

### Conclusion

For every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, the set $B_n$ is computable. The decision procedur
e is: compute the minimal polynomial of $A$, factor it, verify that the non-$x$
part is squarefree and that each irreducible factor is cyclotomic (by matching a
gainst finitely many cyclotomic polynomials of the appropriate degree).
