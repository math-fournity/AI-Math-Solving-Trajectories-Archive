## Proof

We work in $R = \mathbb{C}[x,y]$ and $I = (y - x^2,\; y^2)$. We claim
$$\sqrt{I} = (x, y).$$

**Step 1: $I \subseteq (x,y)$, so $\sqrt{I} \subseteq (x,y)$.**

The ideal $(x,y)$ is maximal, since $R/(x,y) \cong \mathbb{C}$ is a field; hence
 it is prime and therefore radical. Both generators of $I$ lie in $(x,y)$:
- $y - x^2 \in (x,y)$, since both $y$ and $x^2$ are in $(x,y)$;
- $y^2 \in (x,y)$, since $y \in (x,y)$.

Thus $I \subseteq (x,y)$. Because $(x,y)$ is a radical ideal containing $I$, eve
ry element of $\sqrt{I}$ must also lie in $(x,y)$. Hence
$$\sqrt{I} \subseteq (x,y).$$

**Step 2: $y \in \sqrt{I}$.**

The generator $y^2 \in I$, so $y^2 \in I$ with exponent $n = 2$. By definition o
f the radical, $y \in \sqrt{I}$.

**Step 3: $x \in \sqrt{I}$.**

Since $y - x^2 \in I$, we have the congruence $x^2 \equiv y \pmod{I}$. Squaring
both sides,
$$x^4 \equiv y^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{I},$$
where the last congruence uses $y^2 \in I$. Hence $x^4 \in I$, so $x \in \sqrt{I
}$ (with $n = 4$).

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

From Steps 2 and 3, both $x, y \in \sqrt{I}$, so the ideal they generate satisfi
es
$$(x, y) \subseteq \sqrt{I}.$$
Combining with Step 1,
$$\sqrt{I} = (x, y).$$

**Consistency check via the Nullstellensatz.** The variety $V(I)$ is obtained fr
om $y - x^2 = 0$ and $y^2 = 0$. From $y^2 = 0$ we get $y = 0$; substituting into
 $y - x^2 = 0$ gives $x^2 = 0$, hence $x = 0$. So $V(I) = \{(0,0)\}$, and $I(V(I
)) = (x,y)$. Since $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraically closed, the Nullstellensatz giv
es $\sqrt{I} = I(V(I)) = (x,y)$, confirming the direct computation above.
