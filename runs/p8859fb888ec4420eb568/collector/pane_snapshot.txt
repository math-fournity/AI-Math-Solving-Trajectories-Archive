## Proof

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains no solutions, sketches, or
 formalization hints. Proceeding.

**Setup.** Place the crate on the left pan. Each of the four weights $w_1,w_2,w_
3,w_4$ is assigned a coefficient $c_i\in\{-1,0,+1\}$: $+1$ means the weight is o
n the right pan (opposite the crate), $-1$ means it is on the left pan (with the
 crate), and $0$ means it is unused. Equilibrium gives the crate mass
$$m = c_1 w_1 + c_2 w_2 + c_3 w_3 + c_4 w_4, \qquad c_i\in\{-1,0,+1\}.$$
We need every integer $1,2,\dots,n$ to be **uniquely** representable in this for
m.

---

### Upper bound: $n\le 40$

There are $3^4=81$ coefficient vectors $(c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4)$. Negating all coeffic
ients negates the represented value, so the nonzero represented values come in $
\pm$-pairs. Hence the number of **distinct positive** values representable is at
 most
$$\frac{3^4-1}{2}=40.$$
Since we need $n$ distinct positive integers ($1$ through $n$) each representabl
e, $n\le 40$.

---

### Construction achieving $n=40$: weights $\{1,3,9,27\}$

Take $w_i = 3^{i-1}$, i.e. $\{1,3,9,27\}$. We prove every integer in $[-40,40]$
is representable, and uniquely.

**Uniqueness.** Suppose $\sum_{i=1}^{4} c_i\,3^{i-1}=\sum_{i=1}^{4} d_i\,3^{i-1}
$ with $c_i,d_i\in\{-1,0,1\}$. Then $\sum (c_i-d_i)\,3^{i-1}=0$ with $c_i-d_i\in
\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}$. Reducing mod $3$: the only multiple of $3$ in $\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}
$ is $0$, so $c_1=d_1$. Dividing the remaining equation by $3$ and repeating ind
uctively gives $c_i=d_i$ for all $i$. Representation is unique.

**Coverage (by induction).** Claim: with weights $\{1,3,\dots,3^{k-1}\}$, every
integer in $\bigl[-\tfrac{3^k-1}{2},\,\tfrac{3^k-1}{2}\bigr]$ is representable.

*Base* ($k=1$): weight $\{1\}$ represents $\{-1,0,1\}=[-1,1]$. ✓

*Inductive step:* Assume coverage for $k$ weights, range $I_k=\bigl[-\tfrac{3^k-
1}{2},\,\tfrac{3^k-1}{2}\bigr]$. Add weight $3^k$. For any $m\in I_{k+1}=\bigl[-
\tfrac{3^{k+1}-1}{2},\,\tfrac{3^{k+1}-1}{2}\bigr]$:

- If $m\in I_k$: set coefficient of $3^k$ to $0$; represent $m$ with the first $
k$ weights.
- If $m\in \bigl[\tfrac{3^k+1}{2},\,\tfrac{3^{k+1}-1}{2}\bigr]$: then $m-3^k\in
I_k$; set coefficient of $3^k$ to $+1$; represent $m-3^k$ with the first $k$ wei
ghts.
- If $m\in\bigl[-\tfrac{3^{k+1}-1}{2},\,-\tfrac{3^k+1}{2}\bigr]$: then $m+3^k\in
 I_k$; set coefficient of $3^k$ to $-1$; represent $m+3^k$ with the first $k$ we
ights.

These three intervals partition $I_{k+1}$ (their endpoints meet since $\tfrac{3^
k-1}{2}+1=\tfrac{3^k+1}{2}$ and similarly on the negative side). So every $m\in
I_{k+1}$ is representable. ✓

For $k=4$: $I_4=\bigl[-\tfrac{81-1}{2},\,\tfrac{81-1}{2}\bigr]=[-40,40]$. Thus e
very integer $1,2,\dots,40$ is representable — and by the uniqueness argument, e
ach has exactly one representation, so the crate's mass is **uniquely identified
**.

---

### Conclusion

The upper bound gives $n\le 40$, and the weights $\{1,3,9,27\}$ achieve $n=40$ w
ith unique identification of every integer mass from $1$ to $40$.

$$\boxed{n=40}$$
