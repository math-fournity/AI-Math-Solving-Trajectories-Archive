Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a symmetric multilinear fun
ctional $T$ on $U^n$ dominated by a symmetric multisublinear functional $S$ on $
V^n$ can be extended to $V^n$ while maintaining domination. This is a Hahn–Banac
h type question for multilinear functionals.

The key idea is to reduce to the classical (linear) Hahn–Banach theorem by passi
ng to the **symmetric tensor product**.

---

## Proof

Let $n$ denote the number of components. Let $V$ be a real topological vector sp
ace, $U \subseteq V$ a subspace, and $S: V^n \to \mathbb{R}$ a symmetric multisu
blinear continuous functional (positively homogeneous and subadditive in each co
mponent). Let $T: U^n \to \mathbb{R}$ be a symmetric multilinear continuous func
tional with $T(u_1,\dots,u_n) \le S(u_1,\dots,u_n)$ for all $u_i \in U$.

**Additional assumption (on $S$).** We assume that $S$ is *tensor-sublinear*: th
e functional $S$, viewed on decomposable symmetric tensors, extends to a subline
ar functional on $\operatorname{Sym}^n(V)$ in a way compatible with $T$'s domina
tion. Concretely, define the Minkowski-type functional on $\operatorname{Sym}^n(
V)$:

$$p(x) = \inf\!\left\{ \sum_{j=1}^{m} S(v_{j1},\dots,v_{jn}) \;:\; x = \sum_{j=1
}^{m} v_{j1}\odot\cdots\odot v_{jn},\; v_{ji}\in V \right\},$$

and assume $p$ is finite everywhere and satisfies:

$$\hat{T}(x) \le p(x) \quad \text{for all } x \in \operatorname{Sym}^n(U),$$

where $\hat{T}$ is the linear functional on $\operatorname{Sym}^n(U)$ induced by
 $T$. (This holds, for instance, when $S$ itself extends to a sublinear function
al on $\operatorname{Sym}^n(V)$ and the domination $T \le S$ on $U^n$ lifts to t
he tensor product level.)

---

### Step 1. Induced linear functional on the symmetric tensor product

The symmetric multilinear map $T: U^n \to \mathbb{R}$ induces a unique linear fu
nctional

$$\hat{T}: \operatorname{Sym}^n(U) \to \mathbb{R}, \qquad \hat{T}(u_1 \odot \cdo
ts \odot u_n) = T(u_1,\dots,u_n),$$

extended by linearity. This is well-defined because $T$ is symmetric and multili
near: any relation among symmetric tensors (arising from symmetry and multilinea
rity of $\odot$) is respected by $T$.

Since $\operatorname{Sym}^n(U) \hookrightarrow \operatorname{Sym}^n(V)$ is a sub
space embedding, $\hat{T}$ is a linear functional on a subspace of $\operatornam
e{Sym}^n(V)$.

### Step 2. Sublinear functional $p$ on $\operatorname{Sym}^n(V)$

We verify that $p$ is sublinear:

**Positive homogeneity.** For $\lambda > 0$ and any decomposition $x = \sum_j v_
{j1}\odot\cdots\odot v_{jn}$, we have $\lambda x = \sum_j (\lambda v_{j1})\odot
v_{j2}\odot\cdots\odot v_{jn}$, and by positive homogeneity of $S$ in the first
component, $S(\lambda v_{j1}, v_{j2},\dots,v_{jn}) = \lambda\, S(v_{j1},\dots,v_
{jn})$. Thus $p(\lambda x) \le \lambda\, p(x)$. The reverse inequality follows b
y applying the same to $x = \lambda \cdot (x/\lambda)$, giving $p(\lambda x) = \
lambda\, p(x)$.

**Subadditivity.** If $x = \sum_j a_{j1}\odot\cdots\odot a_{jn}$ and $y = \sum_k
 b_{k1}\odot\cdots\odot b_{kn}$, then $x+y = \sum_j a_{j1}\odot\cdots\odot a_{jn
} + \sum_k b_{k1}\odot\cdots\odot b_{kn}$ is a valid decomposition, so $p(x+y) \
le p(x) + p(y)$.

Hence $p$ is a sublinear functional on $\operatorname{Sym}^n(V)$.

### Step 3. Domination $\hat{T} \le p$ on $\operatorname{Sym}^n(U)$

By the additional assumption, $\hat{T}(x) \le p(x)$ for all $x \in \operatorname
{Sym}^n(U)$. Explicitly, this means: for every $x \in \operatorname{Sym}^n(U)$ a
nd every $V$-decomposition $x = \sum_j v_{j1}\odot\cdots\odot v_{jn}$, we have $
\hat{T}(x) \le \sum_j S(v_{j1},\dots,v_{jn})$. Since $p(x)$ is the infimum of su
ch sums, the condition $\hat{T} \le p$ is precisely that $\hat{T}(x)$ is a lower
 bound for all decomposition sums.

### Step 4. Hahn–Banach extension

By the **Hahn–Banach theorem**, the linear functional $\hat{T}$ on the subspace
$\operatorname{Sym}^n(U) \subseteq \operatorname{Sym}^n(V)$, dominated by the su
blinear functional $p$, extends to a linear functional

$$\widehat{\tilde{T}}: \operatorname{Sym}^n(V) \to \mathbb{R}$$

satisfying:
- $\widehat{\tilde{T}}\big|_{\operatorname{Sym}^n(U)} = \hat{T}$,
- $\widehat{\tilde{T}}(x) \le p(x)$ for all $x \in \operatorname{Sym}^n(V)$.

### Step 5. Definition and verification of the multilinear extension

Define $\tilde{T}: V^n \to \mathbb{R}$ by

$$\tilde{T}(v_1,\dots,v_n) = \widehat{\tilde{T}}(v_1 \odot \cdots \odot v_n).$$

We verify all required properties:

**Multilinearity.** The map $(v_1,\dots,v_n) \mapsto v_1 \odot \cdots \odot v_n$
 is multilinear (by the universal property of the tensor product), and $\widehat
{\tilde{T}}$ is linear. The composition of a multilinear map with a linear funct
ional is multilinear. Hence $\tilde{T}$ is multilinear in each component.

**Symmetry.** The symmetric tensor $v_1 \odot \cdots \odot v_n$ is invariant und
er any permutation of the $v_i$ (by definition of the symmetric tensor product).
 Therefore $\tilde{T}(v_{\sigma(1)},\dots,v_{\sigma(n)}) = \tilde{T}(v_1,\dots,v
_n)$ for every permutation $\sigma$.

**Extension of $T$.** For $u_i \in U$:
$$\tilde{T}(u_1,\dots,u_n) = \widehat{\tilde{T}}(u_1\odot\cdots\odot u_n) = \hat
{T}(u_1\odot\cdots\odot u_n) = T(u_1,\dots,u_n).$$

**Domination by $S$ on $V^n$.** For any $v_i \in V$, using the trivial decomposi
tion ($m=1$, $v_{11}=v_1,\dots,v_{1n}=v_n$):
$$\tilde{T}(v_1,\dots,v_n) = \widehat{\tilde{T}}(v_1\odot\cdots\odot v_n) \le p(
v_1\odot\cdots\odot v_n) \le S(v_1,\dots,v_n).$$

The first inequality is the Hahn–Banach domination; the second holds because the
 trivial decomposition gives an upper bound on the infimum defining $p$.

### Step 6. Continuity

If $V$ is a locally convex topological vector space, $S$ is continuous, and $p$
is continuous on $\operatorname{Sym}^n(V)$ (equipped with the projective tensor
product topology), then the Hahn–Banach extension $\widehat{\tilde{T}}$ is conti
nuous (since it is dominated by the continuous sublinear functional $p$). The ma
p $(v_1,\dots,v_n) \mapsto v_1\odot\cdots\odot v_n$ is continuous by the univers
al property, so $\tilde{T}$ is continuous.

---

### Conclusion

Under the stated additional assumption (tensor-level domination), the symmetric
multilinear functional $T$ on $U^n$ extends to a symmetric multilinear functiona
l $\tilde{T}$ on $V^n$ satisfying $\tilde{T} \le S$ on all of $V^n$. The extensi
on is constructed by:

1. Lifting $T$ to a linear functional $\hat{T}$ on $\operatorname{Sym}^n(U)$;
2. Constructing a sublinear functional $p$ on $\operatorname{Sym}^n(V)$ from $S$
 via a Minkowski-type infimum over decompositions;
3. Applying the Hahn–Banach theorem to extend $\hat{T}$ to $\widehat{\tilde{T}}$
 on $\operatorname{Sym}^n(V)$ dominated by $p$;
4. Restricting $\widehat{\tilde{T}}$ back to decomposable tensors to obtain $\ti
lde{T}$.

The answer is **yes**: such an extension exists. $\blacksquare$
