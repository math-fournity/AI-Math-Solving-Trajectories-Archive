## Proof

**Claim:** Every square traceless matrix is unitarily similar to a zero-diagonal
 matrix.

**Proof by induction on n (the size of the matrix).**

**Base case (n = 1):** A 1×1 traceless matrix is [0], already zero-diagonal.

**Inductive step:** Assume the result holds for all (n−1)×(n−1) traceless matric
es. Let A be an n×n matrix with tr(A) = 0.

**Key lemma:** There exists a unit vector v such that ⟨v, Av⟩ = 0.

*Proof of lemma.* The **numerical range** (field of values) of A is
$$W(A) = \{\langle v, Av\rangle : \|v\| = 1\}.$$
Two standard facts:
1. **W(A) is convex** (Toeplitz–Hausdorff theorem).
2. **Every eigenvalue of A lies in W(A)**: if Av = λv with ‖v‖ = 1, then ⟨v, Av⟩
 = λ.

Let λ₁, …, λₙ be the eigenvalues of A. Since tr(A) = 0, we have Σλᵢ = 0, hence
$$0 = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \tfrac{1}{n}\,\lambda_i,$$
which expresses 0 as a convex combination of the eigenvalues (weights 1/n > 0, s
umming to 1). Therefore 0 lies in the convex hull of {λ₁, …, λₙ}. Since each λᵢ
∈ W(A) and W(A) is convex, the convex hull of the eigenvalues is contained in W(
A). Thus **0 ∈ W(A)**, giving a unit vector v with ⟨v, Av⟩ = 0. ∎

**Completing the induction.** Take the unit vector v with ⟨v, Av⟩ = 0 and extend
 it to an orthonormal basis {v, w₁, …, w_{n−1}} of ℂⁿ. Let Q = [v | w₁ | … | w_{
n−1}] be the unitary matrix with these columns. Then

$$Q^* A Q = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & * \\ * & B \end{pmatrix},$$

where B is (n−1)×(n−1). Since unitary similarity preserves trace,
$$\operatorname{tr}(B) = \operatorname{tr}(Q^*AQ) - 0 = \operatorname{tr}(A) = 0
.$$

By the inductive hypothesis, there exists an (n−1)×(n−1) unitary V such that V*B
V has zero diagonal. Define the n×n unitary

$$U = Q \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & V \end{pmatrix}.$$

Then

$$U^* A U = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & V^* \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 0 & *
 \\ * & B \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & V \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pm
atrix} 0 & * \\ * & V^*BV \end{pmatrix},$$

which has zero diagonal (the (1,1) entry is 0, and the diagonal of V*BV is zero
by construction).

By induction, the result holds for all n. ∎

**Answer: Yes.** Every square traceless matrix is unitarily similar to a zero-di
agonal matrix. The proof rests on two pillars: (1) the Toeplitz–Hausdorff convex
ity of the numerical range, which guarantees that 0 (a convex combination of eig
envalues, since they sum to zero) belongs to W(A), providing a unit vector kille
d by the quadratic form; and (2) induction, which peels off this zero diagonal e
ntry and reduces the problem by one dimension while preserving tracelessness.
