## Proof

**Answer: Yes, $A: H \supset \mathcal{D}(A) \to H$ is a closed operator.**

We must show: if $\{u_n\} \subset \mathcal{D}(A)$ with $u_n \to u$ in $H$ and $A
u_n \to f$ in $H$, then $u \in \mathcal{D}(A)$ and $Au = f$.

---

**Step 1: $\{u_n\}$ is bounded in $V$, hence $u \in V$.**

Since $u_n \in \mathcal{D}(A)$, we have $Au_n \in H$ and the duality pairing red
uces to the $H$-inner product:
$$a(u_n, u_n) = \langle Au_n, u_n\rangle_{V',V} = (Au_n, u_n)_H.$$
By the Gårding inequality,
$$\alpha\,|u_n|_V^2 \le a(u_n,u_n) + \lambda(u_n,u_n)_H = (Au_n, u_n)_H + \lambd
a\|u_n\|_H^2.$$
Since $Au_n \to f$ in $H$ and $u_n \to u$ in $H$,
$$(Au_n, u_n)_H \to (f, u)_H, \qquad \|u_n\|_H^2 \to \|u\|_H^2.$$
Therefore $\{|u_n|_V^2\}$ is bounded, i.e. $\{u_n\}$ is bounded in $V$.

Since $V$ is a Hilbert space, every bounded sequence has a weakly convergent sub
sequence. Extract $u_{n_k} \rightharpoonup w$ weakly in $V$ for some $w \in V$.
The continuous embedding $V \hookrightarrow H$ implies $u_{n_k} \rightharpoonup
w$ weakly in $H$. But $u_{n_k} \to u$ strongly in $H$, so weak uniqueness gives
$w = u$. Hence $u \in V$.

---

**Step 2: $Au = f$ in $V'$, hence $u \in \mathcal{D}(A)$ and $Au = f$ in $H$.**

Since $A: V \to V'$ is bounded and linear, it is weakly sequentially continuous,
 so
$$Au_{n_k} \rightharpoonup Au \quad \text{weakly in } V'.$$
For any $v \in V$,
$$\langle Au_{n_k}, v\rangle_{V',V} \to \langle Au, v\rangle_{V',V}.$$
On the other hand, $Au_{n_k} \in H$ and $Au_{n_k} \to f$ in $H$, so
$$\langle Au_{n_k}, v\rangle_{V',V} = (Au_{n_k}, v)_H \to (f, v)_H.$$
Combining,
$$\langle Au, v\rangle_{V',V} = (f, v)_H \quad \text{for all } v \in V.$$
Identifying $f \in H \subset V'$, the right-hand side is $\langle f, v\rangle_{V
',V}$. Since $V$ is dense in $H$ (hence separates points of $V'$),
$$Au = f \quad \text{in } V'.$$
Because $f \in H$, this means $Au \in H$, so $u \in \mathcal{D}(A)$, and $Au = f
$ in $H$.

---

**Conclusion.** The graph of $A$ is closed: whenever $u_n \to u$ in $H$ and $Au_
n \to f$ in $H$, we have $u \in \mathcal{D}(A)$ and $Au = f$. Therefore $A: H \s
upset \mathcal{D}(A) \to H$ is a closed operator. $\blacksquare$
