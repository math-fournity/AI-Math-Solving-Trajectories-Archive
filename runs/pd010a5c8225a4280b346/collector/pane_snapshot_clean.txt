## Proof

**Setup.** Let $L : X \to Y$ be a bounded linear operator between Banach spaces.
 Assume $L$ is injective and that its range $\mathcal{R}(L) := L(X)$ is closed i
n $Y$.

**Goal.** Show that $L$ is boundedly invertible on its range, i.e., that the inv
erse map
$$L^{-1} : \mathcal{R}(L) \to X$$
is a well-defined bounded linear operator.

---

**Step 1 — The range is a Banach space.**

Since $Y$ is a Banach space and $\mathcal{R}(L)$ is a closed subspace of $Y$, th
e range $\mathcal{R}(L)$ equipped with the restricted norm $\|\cdot\|_Y$ is itse
lf a Banach space.

**Step 2 — $L$ is a bounded bijection $X \to \mathcal{R}(L)$.**

View $L$ as the map
$$L : X \longrightarrow \mathcal{R}(L), \qquad x \longmapsto Lx.$$

- **Linearity** is inherited from the original $L$.
- **Boundedness** holds because $\|Lx\|_{\mathcal{R}(L)} = \|Lx\|_Y \le \|L\|\,\
|x\|_X$, so the operator norm of $L : X \to \mathcal{R}(L)$ is at most $\|L\|$.
- **Injectivity** is given.
- **Surjectivity** onto $\mathcal{R}(L)$ holds by the very definition of the ran
ge.

Hence $L : X \to \mathcal{R}(L)$ is a continuous linear bijection between two Ba
nach spaces.

**Step 3 — Apply the Bounded Inverse Theorem.**

The Bounded Inverse Theorem (a direct corollary of the Open Mapping Theorem) sta
tes: *a continuous linear bijection between Banach spaces has a continuous linea
r inverse.*

Applying it to $L : X \to \mathcal{R}(L)$, we conclude that
$$L^{-1} : \mathcal{R}(L) \to X$$
is a bounded linear operator. Equivalently, there exists a constant $c > 0$ such
 that
$$\|x\|_X \le c\,\|Lx\|_Y \qquad \text{for all } x \in X,$$
which is the quantitative form of bounded invertibility on the range (a lower bo
und / "bounded below" estimate).

---

**Conclusion.** Yes. If $L$ is an injective bounded linear operator between Bana
ch spaces with closed range, then $L$ is boundedly invertible on its range. The
key ingredients are:

1. Closedness of the range makes $\mathcal{R}(L)$ a Banach space.
2. Injectivity + surjectivity onto the range makes $L : X \to \mathcal{R}(L)$ a
continuous bijection between Banach spaces.
3. The Open Mapping Theorem / Bounded Inverse Theorem then guarantees that $L^{-
1} : \mathcal{R}(L) \to X$ is bounded.

**Remark (closed unbounded operators).** The same conclusion holds if $L$ is mer
ely a *closed* (possibly unbounded) linear operator: the graph of $L^{-1}$ is ob
tained from the graph of $L$ by swapping coordinates, so $L^{-1}$ is closed; sin
ce it is defined on all of the Banach space $\mathcal{R}(L)$, the Closed Graph T
heorem forces $L^{-1}$ to be bounded. Thus the answer remains **yes** in that br
oader setting as well. $\blacksquare$
