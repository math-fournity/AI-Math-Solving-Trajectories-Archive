## Proof

Let $f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}$ be defined by $f(x)=x^{\top}Ax-r$. Then $M=f^{
-1}(0)$.

Since $A$ is symmetric, by the spectral theorem there exists an orthogonal matri
x $Q\in O(n)$ such that
$$A = Q^{\top}DQ,\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_k,0,\dots,
0),$$
where $k=\operatorname{rank}(A)$ and $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_k>0$ (positivity f
ollows from $A\succeq 0$).

Set $y=Qx$. Since $Q$ is orthogonal, $x^{\top}Ax = y^{\top}Dy = \sum_{i=1}^{k}\l
ambda_i y_i^2$. Hence $M$ is the image under the orthogonal change of coordinate
s $x\mapsto Q^{\top}y$ of
$$\widetilde M = \Big\{y\in\mathbb{R}^n:\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_i y_i^2 = r\Big\}.
$$
It suffices to analyze $\widetilde M$.

**Nonemptiness.** Since $r>0$ and $\lambda_1>0$, the point $y=(\sqrt{r/\lambda_1
},0,\dots,0)\in\widetilde M$, so $M\neq\varnothing$.

**Regularity.** Define $g(y)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_i y_i^2 - r$. Then $g$ is a p
olynomial, hence $C^{\infty}$ (in particular $C^l$ for every $l$). Its gradient
is
$$\nabla g(y) = (2\lambda_1 y_1,\;\dots,\;2\lambda_k y_k,\;0,\;\dots,\;0).$$
Suppose $\nabla g(y)=0$ for some $y\in\widetilde M$. Then $y_1=\dots=y_k=0$, whi
ch forces $g(y)=\sum_{i=1}^k\lambda_i\cdot 0 - r = -r \neq 0$, contradicting $y\
in\widetilde M=g^{-1}(0)$. Therefore
$$\nabla g(y)\neq 0\quad\text{for every }y\in\widetilde M,$$
i.e., $g$ is a submersion at every point of $\widetilde M$.

**Conclusion by the Regular Level Set Theorem.** Since $g:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb
{R}$ is a $C^{\infty}$ submersion at every point of the level set $\widetilde M=
g^{-1}(0)$, the set $\widetilde M$ is a $C^{\infty}$ embedded submanifold of $\m
athbb{R}^n$ of codimension $1$, i.e., of dimension $n-1$. Pulling back through t
he orthogonal (hence $C^{\infty}$ diffeomorphism) map $x\mapsto Qx$, the origina
l set $M$ is likewise a $C^{\infty}$ embedded submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^n$ of d
imension $n-1$.

Since $M$ is $C^{\infty}$, it is $C^l$ for every $l\ge 0$.

**Answer.** $M$ is a $C^{\infty}$ (hence $C^l$ for all $l$) embedded submanifold
 of $\mathbb{R}^n$, and
$$\dim M = n-1.$$
