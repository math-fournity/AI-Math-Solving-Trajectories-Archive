**Step 1: Reduction to an integral via weak convergence.**

Let $\mu_n = \frac1n\sum_{k=1}^n \delta_{x_{kn}}$ be the empirical measure of th
e zeros of $P_n$. A classical result in the theory of orthogonal polynomials sta
tes that for Jacobi (in particular Legendre) polynomials, $\mu_n$ converges weak
ly to the **arcsine (Chebyshev) equilibrium measure** on $[-1,1]$:
$$
\mu_n \;\Rightarrow\; \mu(dx) = \frac{dx}{\pi\sqrt{1-x^2}}, \qquad x\in(-1,1).
$$
Since $\lambda>1$, the function $f(x)=\log\!\bigl(1+\tfrac{x}{\lambda}\bigr)$ is
 continuous on $[-1,1]$ (note $1+x/\lambda \ge 1-1/\lambda>0$). Hence by weak co
nvergence,
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\sum_{k=1}^n \log\!\Bigl(1+\frac{x_{kn}}{\lambda}\Bigr)
= \int_{-1}^{1}\log\!\Bigl(1+\frac{x}{\lambda}\Bigr)\,\frac{dx}{\pi\sqrt{1-x^2}}
.
$$

**Step 2: Evaluate the integral.**

Substitute $x=\cos\theta$, $\theta\in(0,\pi)$: $dx=-\sin\theta\,d\theta$, $\sqrt
{1-x^2}=\sin\theta$, so
$$
I \;=\; \frac1\pi\int_0^\pi \log\!\Bigl(1+\frac{\cos\theta}{\lambda}\Bigr)\,d\th
eta
\;=\; \frac1\pi\int_0^\pi \log(\lambda+\cos\theta)\,d\theta \;-\; \log\lambda.
$$

We use the standard identity (valid for $a>|b|\ge 0$):
$$
\int_0^\pi \log(a+b\cos\theta)\,d\theta \;=\; \pi\log\!\frac{a+\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}{2
}.
$$
*Brief justification:* differentiating in $a$ gives $\int_0^\pi \frac{d\theta}{a
+b\cos\theta}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}$; integrating back in $a$ yields $\pi\l
og(a+\sqrt{a^2-b^2})+C(b)$, and the constant $C(b)=-\pi\log 2$ is fixed by evalu
ating at $a=b$ using $\int_0^\pi\log(1+\cos\theta)\,d\theta=-\pi\log 2$.

Applying this with $a=\lambda$, $b=1$ (legitimate since $\lambda>1$):
$$
\int_0^\pi \log(\lambda+\cos\theta)\,d\theta \;=\; \pi\log\!\frac{\lambda+\sqrt{
\lambda^2-1}}{2}.
$$

Therefore
$$
I \;=\; \log\!\frac{\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^2-1}}{2} \;-\; \log\lambda
\;=\; \log\!\frac{\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^2-1}}{2\lambda}.
$$

**Step 3: Consistency checks.**
- As $\lambda\to\infty$: $\frac{\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^2-1}}{2\lambda}\to 1$, so
$I\to 0$, matching $\frac1n\sum x_{kn}\to 0$ by symmetry.
- As $\lambda\to 1^+$: $I\to\log\frac12=-\log 2$, matching $\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi\
log(1+\cos\theta)\,d\theta=-\log 2$.

Both confirm the formula.

$$
\boxed{\displaystyle \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\sum_{k=1}^n\log\!\Bigl(1+\frac{x_{
kn}}{\lambda}\Bigr)=\log\!\frac{\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^2-1}}{2\lambda}}
$$
