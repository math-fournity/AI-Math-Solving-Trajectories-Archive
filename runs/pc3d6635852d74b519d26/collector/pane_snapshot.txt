## Proof

We show that the closure of $N = \{f \in C[0,1] \mid f(1)=0\}$ in $L^2[0,1]$ equ
als $L^2[0,1]$.

**Key idea.** Membership in $L^2[0,1]$ is up to equality a.e., so the value of a
 function at a single point (here $x=1$) is irrelevant. We exploit this by showi
ng that *every* continuous function on $[0,1]$ can be approximated in $L^2$-norm
 by elements of $N$; since $C[0,1]$ is dense in $L^2[0,1]$, this yields the resu
lt.

**Step 1: Continuous functions vanishing at $1$ approximate arbitrary continuous
 functions.**

Let $g \in C[0,1]$. For each $n \ge 1$, define
$$
g_n(x) = \begin{cases}
g(x), & 0 \le x \le 1 - \tfrac{1}{n},\\[4pt]
g\!\left(1-\tfrac{1}{n}\right)\cdot n(1-x), & 1-\tfrac{1}{n} \le x \le 1.
\end{cases}
$$
This is continuous (the two pieces agree at $x = 1-1/n$), and $g_n(1) = 0$, so $
g_n \in N$.

The functions $g$ and $g_n$ differ only on $[1-1/n,\, 1]$, which has measure $1/
n$. Since $g$ is continuous on the compact interval $[0,1]$, it is bounded: $|g(
x)| \le M$. Also $|g_n(x)| \le M$ on $[1-1/n,1]$ (it is a convex combination of
values of $g$ bounded by $M$). Hence
$$
\|g_n - g\|_2^2 = \int_{1-1/n}^{1} |g_n(x)-g(x)|^2\,dx \le (2M)^2 \cdot \frac{1}
{n} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.
$$
So $g \in \overline{N}^{\,L^2}$.

**Step 2: Density of $C[0,1]$ in $L^2[0,1]$.**

It is a standard result that $C[0,1]$ is dense in $L^2[0,1]$ (e.g., via approxim
ation of simple functions by continuous functions, or via the Weierstrass approx
imation theorem combined with the density of simple functions).

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

From Step 1, $C[0,1] \subseteq \overline{N}^{\,L^2}$. From Step 2, $L^2[0,1] = \
overline{C[0,1]}^{\,L^2}$. Therefore
$$
L^2[0,1] = \overline{C[0,1]}^{\,L^2} \subseteq \overline{\overline{N}^{\,L^2}}^{
\,L^2} = \overline{N}^{\,L^2} \subseteq L^2[0,1].
$$
Hence $\overline{N}^{\,L^2} = L^2[0,1]$.

**Answer:** Yes, the closure of $N$ under the $L^2[0,1]$ norm equals $L^2[0,1]$.
