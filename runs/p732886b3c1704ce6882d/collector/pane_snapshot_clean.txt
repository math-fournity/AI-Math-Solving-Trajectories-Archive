## Proof

### Setup and Definitions

Let $K$ be a simplicial complex. Its **barycentric subdivision** $\text{sd}\, K$
 has:
- **Vertices**: the barycenters $b(\sigma)$ of all simplices $\sigma \in K$.
- **$n$-simplices**: ordered chains $b(\sigma_0), b(\sigma_1), \dots, b(\sigma_n
)$ where $\sigma_0 \subsetneq \sigma_1 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq \sigma_n$ is
 a strict chain of simplices of $K$.

The **standard chain map** $\lambda: C_n(\text{sd}\, K) \to C_n(K)$ is defined o
n a generator $[b(\sigma_0), \dots, b(\sigma_n)]$ (with $\sigma_0 \subsetneq \cd
ots \subsetneq \sigma_n$) by:
$$\lambda\big([b(\sigma_0), \dots, b(\sigma_n)]\big) = \begin{cases} \pm\,\sigma
_n & \text{if } \dim \sigma_i = i \text{ for all } i \text{ (full chain)},\\ 0 &
 \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$
The sign $\pm$ is determined by whether the vertex ordering $[b(\sigma_0), \dots
, b(\sigma_n)]$ agrees with the chosen orientation of $\sigma_n$. One verifies $
\lambda$ is a chain map (see Munkres, §17).

### Step 1: The Subdivision Operator $\text{Sd}: C_n(K) \to C_n(\text{sd}\, K)$

We define $\text{Sd}$ inductively using the **cone construction**. For a vertex
$p$ and an $(n{-}1)$-chain $c = \sum a_i\, \tau_i$ in $C_{n-1}(\text{sd}\, K)$,
define the **cone**:
$$p \cdot c \;=\; \sum a_i\, [p, \tau_i] \;\in\; C_n(\text{sd}\, K).$$

**Definition (inductive):**
- **$n = 0$:** For a vertex $v$ of $K$, $b(v) = v$ is a vertex of $\text{sd}\, K
$. Set $\text{Sd}(v) = v$.
- **$n \geq 1$:** For an $n$-simplex $\sigma$ of $K$:
$$\boxed{\text{Sd}(\sigma) \;=\; b(\sigma) \cdot \text{Sd}(\partial \sigma).}$$

Extend linearly to all of $C_n(K)$.

---

### Step 2: $\text{Sd}$ Commutes with the Boundary Operator

We prove $\partial \circ \text{Sd} = \text{Sd} \circ \partial$ by induction on $
n$.

**Base case ($n=0$):** $\partial(\text{Sd}(v)) = \partial(v) = 0 = \text{Sd}(0)
= \text{Sd}(\partial v)$. ✓

**Inductive step ($n \geq 1$):** We use the **cone identity**: for any vertex $p
$ and chain $c$,
$$\partial(p \cdot c) = c - p \cdot \partial(c).$$

Applying this with $p = b(\sigma)$ and $c = \text{Sd}(\partial \sigma)$:
$$\partial(\text{Sd}(\sigma)) = \partial\big(b(\sigma) \cdot \text{Sd}(\partial\
sigma)\big) = \text{Sd}(\partial\sigma) - b(\sigma) \cdot \partial\big(\text{Sd}
(\partial\sigma)\big).$$

By the induction hypothesis applied to the $(n{-}1)$-chain $\partial\sigma$:
$$\partial\big(\text{Sd}(\partial\sigma)\big) = \text{Sd}\big(\partial(\partial\
sigma)\big) = \text{Sd}(0) = 0.$$

Therefore:
$$\partial(\text{Sd}(\sigma)) = \text{Sd}(\partial\sigma). \qquad \checkmark$$

So $\text{Sd}$ is a **chain map**, hence induces a homomorphism $\text{Sd}_*: H_
n(K) \to H_n(\text{sd}\, K)$.

### Step 3: $\text{Sd}_*$ Is the Inverse of $\lambda_*$

We show $\lambda \circ \text{Sd} \simeq \text{id}_{C_*(K)}$ and $\text{Sd} \circ
 \lambda \simeq \text{id}_{C_*(\text{sd}\, K)}$ (chain homotopic) via the **meth
od of acyclic models**.

**Framework.** Consider the category of simplicial complexes and simplicial maps
. The functors $C_*(-)$ and $C_*(\text{sd}(-))$ are **free** with models $\{\Del
ta^n\}$ and $\{\text{sd}\,\Delta^n\}$ respectively:
- $C_n(K)$ is free on the $n$-simplices of $K$ (images of $\Delta^n$ under simpl

- $C_n(\text{sd}\, K)$ is free on the $n$-simplices of $\text{sd}\, K$ (images o

Both model complexes are **acyclic**: $\Delta^n$ is contractible, and $\text{sd}
\,\Delta^n$ is also contractible (barycentric subdivision of a simplex is a cone
, hence contractible). Thus $H_q(\Delta^n) = 0$ and $H_q(\text{sd}\,\Delta^n) =
0$ for $q > 0$.

**(a) $\lambda \circ \text{Sd} \simeq \text{id}_{C_*(K)}$:**

Both $\lambda \circ \text{Sd}$ and $\text{id}$ are chain maps $C_*(K) \to C_*(K)
$. They agree on $H_0$: for a vertex $v$,
$$(\lambda \circ \text{Sd})(v) = \lambda(v) = v = \text{id}(v).$$
So $(\lambda \circ \text{Sd})_* = \text{id}_* = (\text{id})_*$ on $H_0$.

Since $C_*(K)$ is free with models $\{\Delta^n\}$ and $C_*(K)$ is acyclic on the
se models, the **acyclic models theorem** guarantees a chain homotopy $D: C_n(K)

$$\partial D + D\partial = \lambda \circ \text{Sd} - \text{id}.$$

**(b) $\text{Sd} \circ \lambda \simeq \text{id}_{C_*(\text{sd}\, K)}$:**

Both $\text{Sd} \circ \lambda$ and $\text{id}$ are chain maps $C_*(\text{sd}\, K
) \to C_*(\text{sd}\, K)$. They agree on $H_0$:

A vertex of $\text{sd}\, K$ is $b(\sigma)$ for some simplex $\sigma$ of $K$.
- If $\dim \sigma = 0$, say $\sigma = \{v\}$: $\lambda(b(v)) = v$, $\text{Sd}(v)

- If $\dim \sigma > 0$: $\lambda(b(\sigma)) = 0$, so $(\text{Sd} \circ \lambda)(

$$(\text{Sd} \circ \lambda)_*\big([b(\sigma)]\big) = [b(v)] = [b(\sigma)] = \tex
t{id}_*\big([b(\sigma)]\big).$$

So $(\text{Sd} \circ \lambda)_* = \text{id}_*$ on $H_0$.

Since $C_*(\text{sd}\, K)$ is free with models $\{\text{sd}\,\Delta^n\}$ and $C_
*(\text{sd}\, K)$ is acyclic on these models, the acyclic models theorem gives a
 chain homotopy $D': C_n(\text{sd}\, K) \to C_{n+1}(\text{sd}\, K)$ with:
$$\partial D' + D'\partial = \text{Sd} \circ \lambda - \text{id}.$$

**Conclusion.** Since $\lambda \circ \text{Sd} \simeq \text{id}$ and $\text{Sd}
\circ \lambda \simeq \text{id}$, passing to homology:
$$\lambda_* \circ \text{Sd}_* = \text{id}_{H_n(K)}, \qquad \text{Sd}_* \circ \la

Therefore:
$$\boxed{\text{Sd}_* = (\lambda_*)^{-1}.}$$
