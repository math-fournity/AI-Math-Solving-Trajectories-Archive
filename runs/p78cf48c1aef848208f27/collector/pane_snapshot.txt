# Proof: Uniqueness (Coordinate-Independence) of the Absolute Derivative Along a
 Curve

## Setup

Let $M$ be a smooth $n$-dimensional manifold equipped with an affine connection
$\nabla$. Let $\gamma: I \to M$ be a smooth curve, and let $T$ be a tensor field
 of type $(r,s)$ defined (at least) along $\gamma$. The **absolute derivative**
of $T$ along $\gamma$, denoted $\frac{DT}{dt}$, is defined in local coordinates
$x^i$ by applying the covariant derivative along the tangent $\dot\gamma$:

$$\frac{DT}{dt} = \nabla_{\dot\gamma} T.$$

We show this is a well-defined tensor of type $(r,s)$ along $\gamma$, independen
t of the coordinate chart used to compute it. We give two complementary argument
s: (A) a coordinate-free argument establishing uniqueness by construction, and (
B) an explicit verification of the tensor transformation law for a vector field,
 followed by the extension to general tensors.

---

## Argument A: Coordinate-Free Definition

The covariant derivative $\nabla$ is specified axiomatically as a map

$$\nabla: \mathfrak{X}(M) \times \mathcal{T}^{(r,s)}(M) \to \mathcal{T}^{(r,s)}(
M), \quad (X, T) \mapsto \nabla_X T,$$

satisfying, for all smooth function $f$, vector fields $X, Y$, and tensor fields
 $S, T$ of type $(r,s)$:

1. **$\mathbb{R}$-linearity in $X$:** $\nabla_{fX+Y} T = f\,\nabla_X T + \nabla_
Y T$.
2. **Leibniz (additivity) in $T$:** $\nabla_X(S+T) = \nabla_X S + \nabla_X T$.
3. **Leibniz (product) rule:** $\nabla_X(fT) = (Xf)\,T + f\,\nabla_X T$, and ana
logously for contractions.

These axioms are stated entirely in terms of smooth functions, vector fields, an
d tensor algebra — **no coordinate system is invoked**. A standard theorem (see,
 e.g., the existence/uniqueness theorem for connections) asserts that on a smoot
h manifold, such an operator exists and is uniquely determined by its action on
functions ($\nabla_X f = Xf$) and vector fields, the latter being encoded by the
 Christoffel symbols in any chart. Crucially, the *operator* $\nabla$ is a geome
tric object: its value $\nabla_X T$ at a point $p$ depends only on $X_p$ and the
 germ of $T$ along any curve through $p$ with tangent $X_p$.

Now the tangent vector $\dot\gamma(t) \in T_{\gamma(t)}M$ is a coordinate-indepe
ndent geometric object (it is the velocity of the curve). Since $\nabla_{\dot\ga
mma(t)} T$ is defined pointwise along $\gamma$ using only the connection axioms
and the geometric data $(\dot\gamma, T)$, the result

$$\frac{DT}{dt}\bigg|_t := \nabla_{\dot\gamma(t)} T \in T^{(r,s)}_{\gamma(t)} M$
$

is a tensor of type $(r,s)$ at $\gamma(t)$, constructed without reference to any
 chart. Therefore the absolute derivative is **intrinsically defined** and hence
 unique: any two coordinate computations must yield the same geometric tensor. $
\square$

## Argument B: Explicit Verification of the Transformation Law

To make the abstract argument concrete and to confirm that the *coordinate formu
la* indeed produces a tensor, we verify the transformation law explicitly. We tr
eat the case of a vector field $V = V^i \partial_i$ along $\gamma$ in full detai

### B.1 The coordinate formula

In a chart $x^i$, the absolute derivative of $V$ along $\gamma$ has components

$$\frac{DV^i}{dt} = \frac{dV^i}{dt} + \Gamma^i{}_{jk}\, V^j\, \frac{dx^k}{dt}, \
tag{1}$$

where $\Gamma^i{}_{jk}$ are the Christoffel symbols of $\nabla$ in the chart $x^
i$.

### B.2 Transformation laws

Under a smooth change of coordinates $x^i \leftrightarrow x^{i'}$, the relevant
quantities transform as:

- **Vector components:** $V^{i'} = \frac{\partial x^{i'}}{\partial x^i}\, V^i. \
tag{2}$
- **Christoffel symbols (inhomogeneous law):**
$$\Gamma^{i'}{}_{j'k'} = \frac{\partial x^{i'}}{\partial x^i}\frac{\partial x^j}
{\partial x^{j'}}\frac{\partial x^k}{\partial x^{k'}}\,\Gamma^i{}_{jk} + \frac{\
partial x^{i'}}{\partial x^i}\,\frac{\partial^2 x^i}{\partial x^{j'}\partial x^{
k'}}. \tag{3}$$
- **Curve tangent:** $\frac{dx^{k'}}{dt} = \frac{\partial x^{k'}}{\partial x^k}\
,\frac{dx^k}{dt}. \tag{4}$

A key auxiliary identity, obtained by differentiating $\frac{\partial x^{i'}}{\p
artial x^i}\frac{\partial x^i}{\partial x^{j'}} = \delta^{i'}_{j'}$ with respect

$$\frac{\partial x^{i'}}{\partial x^i}\,\frac{\partial^2 x^i}{\partial x^{j'}\pa
rtial x^{k'}} = -\frac{\partial^2 x^{i'}}{\partial x^m \partial x^n}\,\frac{\par
tial x^m}{\partial x^{j'}}\,\frac{\partial x^n}{\partial x^{k'}}. \tag{5}$$

Also, differentiating (2) along $\gamma$ via the chain rule:

$$\frac{dV^{i'}}{dt} = \frac{\partial x^{i'}}{\partial x^i}\,\frac{dV^i}{dt} + \
frac{\partial^2 x^{i'}}{\partial x^i \partial x^k}\,\frac{dx^k}{dt}\,V^i. \tag{6
}$$

### B.3 Computing the connection term in primed coordinates

Substitute (2), (3), (4) into the primed version of (1):

$$\Gamma^{i'}{}_{j'k'}\, V^{j'}\,\frac{dx^{k'}}{dt} = \underbrace{\frac{\partial

eneous part}} + \underbrace{\frac{\partial x^{i'}}{\partial x^i}\,\frac{\partial

^m\,\frac{\partial x^{k'}}{\partial x^k}\,\frac{dx^k}{dt}}_{\text{(II): inhomoge

For part (I), the Jacobian factors $\frac{\partial x^j}{\partial x^{j'}}\frac{\p

$$\text{(I)} = \frac{\partial x^{i'}}{\partial x^i}\,\Gamma^i{}_{jk}\,V^j\,\frac

$$\text{(II)} = \left[-\frac{\partial^2 x^{i'}}{\partial x^a \partial x^b}\,\fra

Relabeling $a\to i,\, b\to k$:

$$\text{(II)} = -\frac{\partial^2 x^{i'}}{\partial x^i \partial x^k}\,V^i\,\frac

### B.4 Cancellation and conclusion

Sum the ordinary derivative (6) and the connection term:

$$\frac{DV^{i'}}{dt} = \frac{dV^{i'}}{dt} + \Gamma^{i'}{}_{j'k'}\,V^{j'}\,\frac{
dx^{k'}}{dt}$$

$$= \left[\frac{\partial x^{i'}}{\partial x^i}\,\frac{dV^i}{dt} + \frac{\partial

{\partial x^{i'}}{\partial x^i}\,\Gamma^i{}_{jk}\,V^j\,\frac{dx^k}{dt} - \frac{\

The two inhomogeneous terms (the second-derivative pieces) **cancel exactly**, l

$$\frac{DV^{i'}}{dt} = \frac{\partial x^{i'}}{\partial x^i}\left(\frac{dV^i}{dt}
