Looking at this problem, I need to find the exponent (LCM of all element orders)
 of the Rubik's cube group.

## Proof

**Setup.** The Rubik's cube group $G$ consists of all reachable configurations.
A configuration is determined by:
- Corner permutation $\sigma \in S_8$, edge permutation $\tau \in S_{12}$
- Corner orientations $c \in (\mathbb{Z}/3)^8$, edge orientations $e \in (\mathb
b{Z}/2)^{12}$

subject to three constraints: $\text{sgn}(\sigma) = \text{sgn}(\tau)$, $\sum c_i
 \equiv 0 \pmod{3}$, $\sum e_i \equiv 0 \pmod{2}$.

Every move sequence corresponds to an element of $G$, and the "order" of a move
sequence is the order of that group element. The LCM of all orders is the **expo
nent** of $G$.

**Group order.** $|G| = \frac{8!\cdot 12!}{2}\cdot 3^7 \cdot 2^{11} = 2^{27}\cdo
t 3^{14}\cdot 5^3\cdot 7^2\cdot 11$.

**Order of an element.** The order of $(\sigma, \tau, c, e)$ is $\text{lcm}(\tex
t{ord}(\sigma,c),\, \text{ord}(\tau,e))$, where:

- **Corner part** $(\mathbb{Z}/3)^8 \rtimes S_8$: If $\sigma$ has a cycle of len
gth $\ell$ with orientation-sum $s \pmod{3}$ along that cycle, the contribution
to the order is $\ell$ if $s=0$, and $3\ell$ if $s\neq 0$.
- **Edge part** $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{12} \rtimes S_{12}$: If $\tau$ has a cycle of l
ength $m$ with orientation-sum $u \pmod{2}$, the contribution is $m$ if $u=0$, a
nd $2m$ if $u=1$.

The overall order is the LCM of all cycle contributions from both parts.

**Finding the exponent.** I determine the maximum power of each prime $p \mid |G
|$ achievable in some element's order.

**$p=2$:** The maximum 2-power from a single cycle contribution:
- Corners: $8$-cycle gives $8 = 2^3$ (twist gives $24 = 2^3\cdot 3$, still $2^3$
).
- Edges: $8$-cycle with twist gives $16 = 2^4$.

To achieve $2^4$: use an $8$-cycle with twist in edges (odd parity) and a transp
osition in corners (odd parity). Parities match. The edge orientation-sum along
the 8-cycle is $1\pmod{2}$, with the remaining 4 edges summing to $1\pmod{2}$ (t
otal $0$). Order $= \text{lcm}(16, 2) = 16$. ✓

No cycle contribution is divisible by $32$ (max cycle length is $12$, and $2\cdo
t 12=24<32$), so $2^5$ is impossible. **Max: $2^4$.**

**$p=3$:**
- Corners: $3$-cycle with twist gives $9 = 3^2$; $6$-cycle with twist gives $18
= 2\cdot 3^2$.
- Edges: $9$-cycle with twist gives $18 = 2\cdot 3^2$.

To achieve $3^2$: $3$-cycle with twist in corners (even parity) + identity in ed
ges (even parity). The 3 corner orientations sum to $1\pmod{3}$, remaining 5 sum
 to $2\pmod{3}$. Order $= 9$. ✓

No contribution is divisible by $27$ (max is $3\cdot 8=24$ or $2\cdot 9=18$), so
 $3^3$ is impossible. **Max: $3^2$.**

**$p=5$:** A $5$-cycle in corners (even parity) + identity in edges gives order
$5$. No cycle of length $\geq 25$ exists. **Max: $5^1$.**

**$p=7$:** A $7$-cycle in corners (even parity) + identity in edges gives order
$7$. No cycle of length $\geq 49$ exists. **Max: $7^1$.**

**$p=11$:** An $11$-cycle in edges (even parity) + identity in corners (even par
ity) gives order $11$. No cycle of length $\geq 121$ exists. **Max: $11^1$.**

**No other primes** appear in $|G|$.

**Verification of achievability.** Each prime power is achieved by a valid eleme
nt satisfying all three constraints (parity, corner twist, edge flip), as shown
above. Since the exponent is the LCM over *all* element orders, and each prime p
ower is achieved by *some* element, the exponent is:

$$\text{lcm} = 2^4 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 \cdot 11 = 16 \cdot 9 \cdot 5 \cdot
 7 \cdot 11 = 55440.$$

**Conclusion.** The least common multiple of the orders of all possible move seq
uences in a Rubik's cube is:

$$\boxed{55440}$$
