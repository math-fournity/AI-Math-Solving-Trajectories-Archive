## Proof

We evaluate $\displaystyle L = \lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1 \ln^m x\,\ln^n(1+x)\,dx
$, where $m\in\mathbb{N}^+$ is fixed and $n\to\infty$.

**Key observation.** For $x\in[0,1]$, the function $\ln(1+x)$ is increasing, so
$$0 \le \ln(1+x) \le \ln 2 \approx 0.6931 < 1,$$
with $\ln(1+x)=0$ at $x=0$ and $\ln(1+x)=\ln 2$ at $x=1$.

**Bounding the integrand.** Since $\ln(1+x)\ge 0$ on $[0,1]$, we have $|\ln(1+x)
|^n = \ln^n(1+x) \le (\ln 2)^n$ for all $x\in[0,1]$. Therefore
$$\left|\ln^m x\,\ln^n(1+x)\right| \le (\ln 2)^n\,|\ln x|^m.$$

**Integrating.** The function $|\ln x|^m$ is integrable on $(0,1)$; indeed, subs
tituting $x=e^{-t}$ ($t\in[0,\infty)$, $dx = -e^{-t}\,dt$):
$$\int_0^1 |\ln x|^m\,dx = \int_0^\infty t^m e^{-t}\,dt = \Gamma(m+1) = m!.$$

Hence, for every $n$,
$$\left|\int_0^1 \ln^m x\,\ln^n(1+x)\,dx\right| \le (\ln 2)^n \int_0^1 |\ln x|^m
\,dx = m!\,(\ln 2)^n.$$

**Taking the limit.** Since $0 < \ln 2 < 1$, we have $(\ln 2)^n \to 0$ as $n\to\
infty$. By the squeeze theorem,
$$0 \le \left|\int_0^1 \ln^m x\,\ln^n(1+x)\,dx\right| \le m!\,(\ln 2)^n \xrighta
rrow{n\to\infty} 0.$$

Therefore,
$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1 \ln^m x\,\ln^n(1+x)\,dx = 0.}$$
