**Step 2: Classify the singularity.**

Since $\cos^2 z$ is entire and $\cos^2 0 = 1 \neq 0$, the function $f(z)$ has a
pole of order $2$ at $z=0$.

**Step 3: Compute the residue.**

For a pole of order $2$, the residue is

$$
\operatorname{Res}(f,0)=\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{d}{dz}\!\left[z^2 f(z)\right]=\lim_{z
\to 0}\frac{d}{dz}\!\left[\cos^2 z\right].

Differentiating:

$$
\frac{d}{dz}\cos^2 z = 2\cos z\cdot(-\sin z)=-\sin 2z.

Therefore

$$
\operatorname{Res}(f,0)=\lim_{z\to 0}(-\sin 2z)=0.
$$

By the residue theorem,

$$
\oint_L \frac{\cos^2 z}{z^2}\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}(f,0)=2\pi i\c
dot 0=0.
$$

**Conclusion.**

$$
\boxed{\displaystyle \oint_L \frac{\cos^2 z}{z^2}\,dz = 0}
$$
