## Solution

**Setting up the residue computation.** The contour $|z|=1$ encloses the point $
z = \tfrac{1}{2}$ (since $|\tfrac{1}{2}| < 1$), which is the unique singularity
of the integrand $\dfrac{P(z)}{(2z-1)^{n+1}}$. Writing

$$2z - 1 = 2\!\left(z - \tfrac{1}{2}\right),$$

we have

$$\frac{P(z)}{(2z-1)^{n+1}} = \frac{P(z)}{2^{\,n+1}\left(z-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{
n+1}}.$$

This has a pole of order $n+1$ at $z = \tfrac{1}{2}$. By the residue theorem,

$$\oint_{|z|=1} \frac{P(z)}{(2z-1)^{n+1}}\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}_
{z=1/2} \frac{P(z)}{2^{\,n+1}\left(z-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{n+1}}.$$

The residue at a pole of order $n+1$ is

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=1/2} = \frac{1}{n!}\left[\frac{d^n}{dz^n}\frac{P(z)}{2^{
\,n+1}}\right]_{z=1/2} = \frac{P^{(n)}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)}{n!\cdot 2^{\,
n+1}}.$$

**Translating the conditions.** The hypothesis $\displaystyle\oint_{|z|=1} \frac
{P(z)}{(2z-1)^{n+1}}\,dz = 0$ for every $n = 0, 1, \dots, d$ is therefore equiva
lent to

$$P^{(n)}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right) = 0 \quad \text{for all } n = 0, 1, \dots,
d.$$

**Concluding $P \equiv 0$.** The conditions $P^{(n)}(\tfrac{1}{2}) = 0$ for $n =
 0, \dots, d$ say that $z = \tfrac{1}{2}$ is a zero of $P$ of multiplicity at le
ast $d+1$. Hence

$$P(z) = \left(z - \tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{d+1} Q(z)$$

for some polynomial $Q$. But $\deg P \le d$, while $\deg\!\left[\left(z-\tfrac{1
}{2}\right)^{d+1}Q\right] \ge d+1$ whenever $Q \neq 0$. This forces $Q \equiv 0$
, and therefore

$$\boxed{P(z) = 0.}$$
