Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether there's an isometric embedd
ing $L^1(a,b;H^*) \hookrightarrow (L^{\infty}(a,b;H))^*$, with $H$ a separable H
ilbert space.

**Answer: Yes, such an isometric embedding exists.**

## Proof

### The natural map

Define $T: L^1(a,b;H^*) \to (L^{\infty}(a,b;H))^*$ by
$$T(f)(g) = \int_a^b \langle f(t),\, g(t)\rangle_{H^*,H}\, dt, \qquad f \in L^1(
a,b;H^*),\; g \in L^{\infty}(a,b;H).$$

**Well-definedness of the integrand.** Since $f$ is strongly measurable and $g$
is strongly measurable, and the duality pairing $H^* \times H \to \mathbb{R}$ (o
r $\mathbb{C}$) is jointly continuous, the scalar function $t \mapsto \langle f(
t), g(t)\rangle$ is measurable. Moreover,
$$|\langle f(t),g(t)\rangle| \le \|f(t)\|_{H^*}\,\|g(t)\|_H \le \|f(t)\|_{H^*},$
$
which is integrable since $f \in L^1$. So the integral is well-defined.

**Boundedness (upper bound).** For $\|g\|_{L^\infty} \le 1$:
$$|T(f)(g)| \le \int_a^b \|f(t)\|_{H^*}\,\|g(t)\|_H\, dt \le \|g\|_{L^\infty}\in
t_a^b \|f(t)\|_{H^*}\,dt = \|g\|_{L^\infty}\,\|f\|_{L^1}.$$
Hence $\|T(f)\|_{(L^\infty)^*} \le \|f\|_{L^1}$, so $T(f)$ is a bounded linear f
unctional and $T$ is a bounded linear map with $\|T\| \le 1$.

### The lower bound via a measurable selection

It remains to show $\|T(f)\| \ge \|f\|_{L^1}$ for every $f \in L^1(a,b;H^*)$.

**Using the Hilbert space structure.** Since $H$ is a Hilbert space, the Riesz m
ap $R: H \to H^*$, $h \mapsto (\,\cdot\,, h)_H$, is an isometric isomorphism. Le
t $\tilde{f}(t) := R^{-1}(f(t)) \in H$. Then:
- $\tilde{f}$ is strongly measurable (composition of a strongly measurable funct
ion with the continuous linear map $R^{-1}$),
- $\|\tilde{f}(t)\|_H = \|f(t)\|_{H^*}$ for all $t$,
- $\langle f(t), h\rangle_{H^*,H} = (\tilde{f}(t), h)_H$ for all $h \in H$.

**Constructing the test function $g$.** Define
$$g(t) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{\tilde{f}(t)}{\|\tilde{f}(t)\|_H} & \text{if } \ti
lde{f}(t) \neq 0, \\[6pt] 0 & \text{if } \tilde{f}(t) = 0. \end{cases}$$

**Measurability of $g$.** The set $A = \{t : \tilde{f}(t) \neq 0\} = \{t : \|\ti
lde{f}(t)\|_H > 0\}$ is measurable (since $t \mapsto \|\tilde{f}(t)\|_H$ is meas
urable). On $A$, $g$ is the composition of $\tilde{f}|_A$ with the continuous ma
p $x \mapsto x/\|x\|$ on $H\setminus\{0\}$, hence strongly measurable on $A$. On
 $A^c$, $g \equiv 0$ is strongly measurable. Since $H$ is separable, by Pettis's
 theorem, weak measurability suffices; gluing on the two measurable pieces gives
 that $g$ is strongly measurable on all of $(a,b)$.

**Bound on $g$.** Clearly $\|g(t)\|_H \le 1$ for all $t$, so $g \in L^{\infty}(a
,b;H)$ with $\|g\|_{L^\infty} \le 1$.

**Key computation.** For $t \in A$:
$$\langle f(t), g(t)\rangle = (\tilde{f}(t), g(t))_H = \left(\tilde{f}(t),\, \fr
ac{\tilde{f}(t)}{\|\tilde{f}(t)\|_H}\right)_H = \frac{\|\tilde{f}(t)\|_H^2}{\|\t
ilde{f}(t)\|_H} = \|\tilde{f}(t)\|_H = \|f(t)\|_{H^*}.$$

For $t \in A^c$: both sides are $0$. Therefore $\langle f(t), g(t)\rangle = \|f(
t)\|_{H^*}$ a.e., and:
$$T(f)(g) = \int_a^b \langle f(t), g(t)\rangle\, dt = \int_a^b \|f(t)\|_{H^*}\,
dt = \|f\|_{L^1(a,b;H^*)}.$$

Since $\|g\|_{L^\infty} \le 1$:
$$\|T(f)\|_{(L^\infty)^*} \ge |T(f)(g)| = \|f\|_{L^1}.$$

### Conclusion

Combining both inequalities:
$$\|T(f)\|_{(L^{\infty}(a,b;H))^*} = \|f\|_{L^1(a,b;H^*)} \quad \text{for all }
f \in L^1(a,b;H^*).$$

The map $T$ is linear, norm-preserving, and hence injective. Therefore $T$ is an
 isometric embedding of $L^1(a,b;H^*)$ into $(L^{\infty}(a,b;H))^*$.

**Remark on the role of separability.** The separability of $H$ ensures that str
ong measurability is equivalent to weak measurability (Pettis's theorem), which
guarantees that the pointwise-normalized function $g(t) = \tilde{f}(t)/\|\tilde{
f}(t)\|$ is strongly measurable and hence belongs to $L^{\infty}(a,b;H)$. The Hi
lbert space structure (via the Riesz map) provides the canonical isometry $H^* \
cong H$ that makes the explicit construction of the norming function $g$ possibl
e.
