## Proof

Let $M \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ denote the Mandelbrot set:
$$M = \bigl\{ c \in \mathbb{C} : \text{the orbit } z_0=0,\; z_{n+1}=z_n^2+c \tex
t{ is bounded}\bigr\}.$$

We show $|M| = 2^{\aleph_0}$ by establishing two inequalities.

### Upper bound: $|M| \le 2^{\aleph_0}$

$M \subseteq \mathbb{C}$, and $|\mathbb{C}| = |\mathbb{R}^2| = 2^{\aleph_0}$. He
nce $|M| \le 2^{\aleph_0}$.

### Lower bound: $|M| \ge 2^{\aleph_0}$

It suffices to exhibit a subset of $M$ with cardinality $2^{\aleph_0}$. We show
$M$ contains a nonempty open subset of $\mathbb{C}$; any nonempty open subset of
 $\mathbb{C}$ contains an open disk, hence a continuum-many family of points, so
 has cardinality $2^{\aleph_0}$.

*Proof of claim.* Fix $c$ with $|c| < \tfrac14$. Consider the fixed points of $f
_c(z) = z^2 + c$, i.e. solutions of $z = z^2 + c$, equivalently $z^2 - z + c = 0

$$\alpha = \tfrac12 - \sqrt{\tfrac14 - c}, \qquad \beta = \tfrac12 + \sqrt{\tfra
c14 - c}.$$
The multiplier of $f_c$ at a fixed point $p$ is $f_c'(p) = 2p$. At $\alpha$:
$$|f_c'(\alpha)| = |2\alpha| = |1 - 2\sqrt{\tfrac14 - c}|.$$
For $|c|<\tfrac14$, the value $\tfrac14 - c$ lies in the disk of radius $\tfrac1
4$ centered at $\tfrac14$, which is contained in the open right half-plane and a
voids the ray $(-\infty,0]$; its principal square root satisfies $|\sqrt{\tfrac1
4-c}| < \tfrac12$ (since $|\tfrac14-c|<\tfrac12$ and the square root of a number
 of modulus $<\tfrac12$ has modulus $<\sqrt{\tfrac12}<1$; more sharply, $|\tfrac
14-c|\le \tfrac14+|c|<\tfrac12$, so $|\sqrt{\tfrac14-c}|<\sqrt{\tfrac12}$). In p
articular $|2\sqrt{\tfrac14-c}|<1$, so
$$|f_c'(\alpha)| = |1 - 2\sqrt{\tfrac14 - c}| \le 1 + |2\sqrt{\tfrac14-c}|.$$
A sharper route: the main cardioid is exactly the set of $c$ for which $f_c$ has

cardioid is parametrized by $c = \frac{w}{2} - \frac{w^2}{4}$ with $|w|<1$ (attr
acting when $|w|<1$), and its image under $w\mapsto \tfrac{w}{2}-\tfrac{w^2}{4}$

Concretely, since $w \mapsto \tfrac{w}{2}-\tfrac{w^2}{4}$ is holomorphic with no
nzero derivative $\tfrac12$ at $w=0$, by the open mapping theorem its image on a

An attracting fixed point attracts the critical orbit: since $0$ is the critical

is an attracting cycle (the critical orbit converges to the attracting cycle), t

Therefore $M$ contains a nonempty open disk $\{c:|c|<\varepsilon\}$, which has c
ardinality $2^{\aleph_0}$.

### Conclusion

We have $2^{\aleph_0} \le |M| \le 2^{\aleph_0}$, so by Cantor–Schröder–Bernstein
,
$$|M| = 2^{\aleph_0}.$$

The Mandelbrot set is **not countable**; it has the cardinality of the continuum
, $2^{\aleph_0}$.
