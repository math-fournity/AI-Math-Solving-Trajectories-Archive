## Proof

**Setup.** Define $\operatorname{sinc}(t) = \frac{\sin t}{t}$ (with $\operatorna
me{sinc}(0)=1$). Set $a_0 = 1,\; a_1 = \tfrac{1}{3},\; a_2 = \tfrac{1}{5}$, so

$$I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \prod_{k=0}^{2} \operatorname{sinc}(a_k x)\, dx.$$

**Fourier transform convention.** Use $\hat{f}(\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
 f(x)\,e^{-i\omega x}\,dx$, with inverse $f(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int \hat{f}(\ome
ga)\,e^{i\omega x}\,d\omega$.

**Key identity.** For $a > 0$:

$$\widehat{\operatorname{sinc}(a\,\cdot)}(\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \fra
c{\sin(ax)}{ax}\,e^{-i\omega x}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{a}\,\mathbf{1}_{[-a,\,a]}(\omeg
a),$$

where $\mathbf{1}_{[-a,a]}$ is the indicator of $[-a,a]$. (This follows from the
 classical Dirichlet integral $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(ax)}{x}e^{-i\om
ega x}dx = \pi\,\mathbf{1}_{[-a,a]}(\omega)$, divided by $a$.)

**Product-to-convolution.** Under this convention, $\widehat{fg} = \frac{1}{2\pi
}(\hat{f}*\hat{g})$. Iterating for three factors:

$$\widehat{f_0 f_1 f_2}(\omega) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}\,(\hat{f}_0 * \hat{f}_1 * \
hat{f}_2)(\omega).$$

Since $I = \int f_0 f_1 f_2\,dx = \widehat{f_0 f_1 f_2}(0)$:

$$I = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}\,(\hat{f}_0 * \hat{f}_1 * \hat{f}_2)(0) = \frac{1}{4\pi
^2}\cdot\frac{\pi^3}{a_0 a_1 a_2}\cdot\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{[-a_0,a_0]}*\mathbf{1}_{
[-a_1,a_1]}*\mathbf{1}_{[-a_2,a_2]}\bigr)(0).$$

**Evaluating the convolution of indicators.** First compute the two-fold convolu
tion $g(t) = (\mathbf{1}_{[-a_0,a_0]}*\mathbf{1}_{[-a_1,a_1]})(t)$, which equals
 the length of $[-a_0,a_0]\cap[t-a_1,\,t+a_1]$. Assuming $a_0 \geq a_1$:

$$g(t) = \begin{cases} 2a_1, & |t| \le a_0 - a_1,\\ a_0 + a_1 - |t|, & a_0 - a_1
 \le |t| \le a_0 + a_1,\\ 0, & |t| \ge a_0 + a_1. \end{cases}$$

With $a_0=1,\,a_1=\tfrac13$: the flat region is $|t|\le \tfrac{2}{3}$, where $g(
t)=\tfrac{2}{3}$.

**Triple convolution at 0.** Using evenness of the indicators:

$$\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{[-a_0,a_0]}*\mathbf{1}_{[-a_1,a_1]}*\mathbf{1}_{[-a_2,a_2]}\
bigr)(0) = \int_{-a_2}^{a_2} g(t)\,dt.$$

**Critical condition.** Since $a_2 = \tfrac{1}{5} < \tfrac{2}{3} = a_0 - a_1$, t
he interval $[-a_2, a_2]$ lies entirely within the flat region of $g$. Therefore
 $g(t) = \tfrac{2}{3}$ throughout:

$$\int_{-1/5}^{1/5} g(t)\,dt = \frac{2}{5}\cdot\frac{2}{3} = \frac{4}{15}.$$

**Assembling the result.**

$$I = \frac{1}{4\pi^2}\cdot\frac{\pi^3}{1\cdot\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{5}}\cdot\
frac{4}{15} = \frac{1}{4\pi^2}\cdot 15\pi^3 \cdot \frac{4}{15} = \frac{15\pi^3\c
dot 4}{4\pi^2\cdot 15} = \pi.$$

**Verification of the condition.** The computation relied on $a_2 \le a_0 - a_1$
, i.e., $\tfrac{1}{5} \le 1 - \tfrac{1}{3} = \tfrac{2}{3}$. Equivalently, $a_1 +
 a_2 = \tfrac{1}{3}+\tfrac{1}{5} = \tfrac{8}{15} \le 1 = a_0$. This is the Borwe
in condition guaranteeing the integral equals $\frac{\pi}{a_0} = \pi$.

**Sanity check ($n=0$).** With a single factor, $I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\fra
c{\sin x}{x}\,dx = \pi$ (Dirichlet integral), matching $\frac{\pi}{a_0}=\pi$. ✓
