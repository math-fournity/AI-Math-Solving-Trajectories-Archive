## Solution

We count ordered pairs $(a,b)$ with $1 \le a,b \le 1000$ and $1000 \mid a^2+b^2$
.

Since $1000 = 2^3 \cdot 5^3 = 8 \cdot 125$ with $\gcd(8,125)=1$, by CRT the numb
er of solutions mod $1000$ equals (solutions mod $8$) $\times$ (solutions mod $1
25$). The range $1,\dots,1000$ is a complete residue system mod $1000$, so this
gives the answer directly.

### Count mod 8

Squares mod 8: $a$ odd $\Rightarrow a^2\equiv 1$; $a\equiv 2,6\Rightarrow a^2\eq
uiv 4$; $a\equiv 0,4\Rightarrow a^2\equiv 0$. So $a^2\in\{0,1,4\}$ with multipli
cities $2,4,2$.

$a^2+b^2\equiv 0\pmod 8$ requires $(a^2,b^2)\in\{(0,0),(4,4)\}$ (since $1+7\not\
equiv 0$ and $7$ is not a square mod 8).

- $(0,0)$: $2\times 2 = 4$ pairs.
- $(4,4)$: $2\times 2 = 4$ pairs.

Total mod 8: $\boxed{8}$.

### Count mod 125

Since $5\equiv 1\pmod 4$, $-1$ is a quadratic residue mod $5$, and by Hensel's l
emma there exists $i$ with $i^2\equiv -1\pmod{5^k}$ for all $k\ge 1$.

The change of variables $u = x+iy,\ v = x-iy$ (with inverse $x=(u+v)/2,\ y=(u-v)
/(2i)$) is a bijection on $(\mathbb Z/5^k\mathbb Z)^2$ since $2$ and $i$ are uni
ts mod $5^k$. Thus
$$x^2+y^2\equiv 0\pmod{5^k} \iff (x+iy)(x-iy)\equiv 0 \iff uv\equiv 0\pmod{5^k}.
$$

**Counting $(u,v)$ with $uv\equiv 0\pmod{5^k}$:** Write $u=5^j u'$ with $u'$ a u
nit (or $u=0$, i.e. $j=k$).

- $j=k$ ($u=0$): $1$ choice for $u$, $v$ arbitrary $\Rightarrow 5^k$ pairs.
- $0\le j\le k-1$: $u'$ ranges over $\varphi(5^{k-j})=4\cdot 5^{k-j-1}$ units; $
v$ must satisfy $v_5(v)\ge k-j$, giving $5^j$ choices. Contribution: $4\cdot 5^{
k-j-1}\cdot 5^j = 4\cdot 5^{k-1}$ per $j$.

Summing: $N(k) = k\cdot 4\cdot 5^{k-1} + 5^k = 5^{k-1}(4k+5)$.

Check $k=1$: $5^0(4+5)=9$. Direct count mod 5: squares $\{0,1,4\}$; $x^2+y^2\equ
iv 0$ gives $(0,0)$, $(\pm1,\pm2)$, $(\pm2,\pm1)$: $1+4+4=9$. ✓

For $k=3$: $N(3) = 5^2(12+5) = 25\cdot 17 = \boxed{425}$.

### Final count

$$8 \times 425 = \boxed{3400}.$$
