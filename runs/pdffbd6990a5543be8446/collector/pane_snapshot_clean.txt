## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $V_{\bar{k}}(I)$ is necessarily finite.

Let $A = k[x_1,\dots,x_n]/I$. Since $I$ is maximal, $A$ is a field. By **Zariski
's Lemma** (a form of the Nullstellensatz valid over arbitrary fields), the cano
nical map $k \to A$ makes $A$ a **finite** field extension of $k$. Set $K := A$
and $d := :k] < \infty$.

**Step 1: Bijection between $V_{\bar{k}}(I)$ and $k$-algebra homomorphisms $K \t
o \bar{k}$.**

Let $\bar x_i$ denote the image of $x_i$ in $K$.

- *(Point $\Rightarrow$ homomorphism.)* Given $a = (a_1,\dots,a_n) \in V_{\bar{k
}}(I)$, the evaluation map $\mathrm{ev}_a : k[x_1,\dots,x_n] \to \bar{k}$, $f \m
apsto f(a)$, is a $k$-algebra homomorphism. Since $f(a)=0$ for every $f \in I$,
it descends to a $k$-algebra homomorphism $\psi_a : K \to \bar{k}$ with $\psi_a(
\bar x_i) = a_i$.

- *(Homomorphism $\Rightarrow$ point.)* Given a $k$-algebra homomorphism $\psi :
 K \to \bar{k}$, set $a_i := \psi(\bar x_i) \in \bar{k}$. For any $f \in I$, wri
ting $f = \sum c_\alpha x^\alpha$, we have $f(a) = \sum c_\alpha a^\alpha = \psi
\!\big(\sum c_\alpha \bar x^\alpha\big) = \psi(0) = 0$, since $\sum c_\alpha \ba
r x^\alpha = 0$ in $K$. Hence $a = (a_1,\dots,a_n) \in V_{\bar{k}}(I)$.

These two constructions are mutually inverse: $\psi_a$ is recovered from $a$ via
 $\psi_a(\bar x_i)=a_i$, and $a$ is recovered from $\psi$ via $a_i = \psi(\bar x
_i)$. Thus
$$V_{\bar{k}}(I) \;\longleftrightarrow\; \mathrm{Hom}_{k\text{-alg}}(K,\, \bar{k
}).$$

**Step 2: $\mathrm{Hom}_{k\text{-alg}}(K, \bar{k})$ is finite.**

Since $K/k$ is finite, $K = k(\alpha)$ for some $\alpha$ (primitive element theo
rem applies to the separable part; in general $K$ is generated over $k$ by finit
ely many elements, and we may pick a primitive element for the separable closure
 of $k$ in $K$). A $k$-algebra homomorphism $\psi : K \to \bar{k}$ is completely
 determined by $\psi(\alpha)$, which must be a root of the minimal polynomial $m
_\alpha \in k[t]$ of $\alpha$ over $k$. Since $\deg m_\alpha \leq :k] = d$, th
ere are at most $d$ such roots in $\bar{k}$.

More precisely, the number of $k$-embeddings $K \hookrightarrow \bar{k}$ equals
the **separable degree** $:k]_s$, which satisfies $:k]_s \leq :k] = d < \i
nfty$ (with equality iff $K/k$ is separable).

**Conclusion.**

$$\bigl|V_{\bar{k}}(I)\bigr| \;=\; \bigl|\mathrm{Hom}_{k\text{-alg}}(K,\,\bar{k}
)\bigr| \;=\; :k]_s \;\leq\; :k] \;<\; \infty.$$

Therefore $V_{\bar{k}}(I)$ is finite. (The points form a single orbit under $\ma
thrm{Gal}(\bar{k}/k)$ when $K/k$ is separable, and the orbit size is $:k]$; in
 the inseparable case the orbit is smaller, corresponding to the separable degre
e.)
