Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $K = F(a_1,\ldots,a_n) \cap
 F^{\mathrm{alg}}$ (the algebraic closure of $F$ in the finitely generated exten

**Answer: Yes, $K$ is always finitely generated (in fact, finite) over $F$, even
 when $F$ is imperfect.**

---

## Proof

Let $L = F(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$ and $K = L \cap F^{\mathrm{alg}}$, the set of elemen
ts of $L$ algebraic over $F$. Since $K \subseteq F^{\mathrm{alg}}$, the extensio
n $K/F$ is algebraic, so "finitely generated" is equivalent to "finite." We prov
e $:F] < \infty$.

### Step 1: Transcendence basis reduction

Choose a transcendence basis $t_1,\ldots,t_r$ of $L/F$ and set $F(\mathbf{t}) =
F(t_1,\ldots,t_r)$. Then $L$ is algebraic over $F(\mathbf{t})$. Since $L$ is fin
itely generated over $F(\mathbf{t})$ and algebraic, $L$ is **finite** over $F(\m
athbf{t})$. Set $d = [L : F(\mathbf{t})]$.

### Step 2: Degree preservation for separable elements

**Claim:** If $\alpha \in K$ is separable over $F$, then $[F(\mathbf{t},\alpha)
: F(\mathbf{t})] = [F(\alpha) : F]$.

The extension $F(\mathbf{t})/F$ is purely transcendental, hence **regular**: $F$
 is algebraically closed in $F(\mathbf{t})$, and $F(\mathbf{t})/F$ is separable.
 Regular extensions are linearly disjoint from separable algebraic extensions. S
ince $F(\alpha)/F$ is finite separable, $F(\mathbf{t})$ and $F(\alpha)$ are line
arly disjoint over $F$, giving the equality. $\checkmark$

### Step 3: Degree preservation for purely inseparable elements

**Claim:** If $\alpha \in K$ is purely inseparable over $F$ (with $\mathrm{char}
(F) = p > 0$), then $[F(\mathbf{t},\alpha) : F(\mathbf{t})] = [F(\alpha) : F]$.

Let $e$ be minimal with $\alpha^{p^e} \in F$, so $[F(\alpha):F] = p^e$. Let $e'$
 be minimal with $\alpha^{p^{e'}}} \in F(\mathbf{t})$, so $[F(\mathbf{t},\alpha)
:F(\mathbf{t})] = p^{e'}}$. Clearly $e' \leq e$.

Set $\beta = \alpha^{p^{e'}}} \in F(\mathbf{t})$ and $m = e - e'$. Then $\beta^{
p^m} = \alpha^{p^e} \in F$. Write $\beta = f/g$ with $f, g \in F[t_1,\ldots,t_r]
$ coprime. Then $f^{p^m}/g^{p^m} \in F$, so $f^{p^m} = c \cdot g^{p^m}$ for some
 $c \in F^\times$. By unique factorization in $F[t_1,\ldots,t_r]$, comparing exp
onents of each irreducible polynomial in the $t_i$: the exponent of any irreduci
ble factor $\pi$ in $f^{p^m}$ is $p^m \cdot v_\pi(f)$ and in $c \cdot g^{p^m}$ i
s $p^m \cdot v_\pi(g)$ (since $c \in F^\times$ is a unit). So $v_\pi(f) = v_\pi(
g)$ for all $\pi$, meaning $f$ and $g$ are associates, hence $\beta \in F$.

Thus $\alpha^{p^{e'}}} \in F$, giving $e' \geq e$, so $e' = e$. $\checkmark$

### Step 4: Bounding the separable part $_s : F]$

Let $K_s$ be the **separable closure** of $F$ in $L$ (the maximal separable sube
xtension of $K/F$). For any $\alpha \in K_s$, by Step 2:
$$[F(\alpha) : F] = [F(\mathbf{t}, \alpha) : F(\mathbf{t})] \leq [L : F(\mathbf{
t})] = d.$$

Since $K_s/F$ is separable algebraic with every element of degree $\leq d$: if $
_s : F] > d$, then some finite subextension $E/F$ with $[E:F] > d$ exists, and
 by the primitive element theorem $E = F(\alpha)$ for some $\alpha$ with $[F(\al
pha):F] > d$, contradiction. Therefore:
$$_s : F] \leq d.$$

### Step 5: $K_s$ is algebraically closed in $K_s(\mathbf{t})$

Since $K_s(\mathbf{t})/K_s$ is purely transcendental, $K_s$ is algebraically clo
sed in $K_s(\mathbf{t})$: any element of $K_s(\mathbf{t})$ algebraic over $K_s$
must lie in $K_s$ (a standard property of purely transcendental extensions).

### Step 6: Bounding $ : K_s]$

Since $K$ is algebraic over $F$ and $K_s$ is the separable closure of $F$ in $K$
, the extension $K/K_s$ is **purely inseparable** (hence algebraic). Therefore:
$$K \cap K_s(\mathbf{t}) = \{x \in K_s(\mathbf{t}) : x \text{ is algebraic over
} K_s\} = K_s,$$
where the first equality uses that $K$ is algebraic over $K_s$.

By the compositum degree formula (with $K(\mathbf{t}) = K \cdot K_s(\mathbf{t})$
 inside $L$):
$$(\mathbf{t}) : K_s(\mathbf{t})] =  : K \cap K_s(\mathbf{t})] =  : K_s].$
$

By linear disjointness ($K_s/F$ separable, $F(\mathbf{t})/F$ regular):
$$_s(\mathbf{t}) : F(\mathbf{t})] = _s : F].$$

Since $K(\mathbf{t}) \subseteq L$:
$$(\mathbf{t}) : F(\mathbf{t})] \leq [L : F(\mathbf{t})] = d.$$

By the tower $F(\mathbf{t}) \subseteq K_s(\mathbf{t}) \subseteq K(\mathbf{t})$:
$$d \geq (\mathbf{t}) : F(\mathbf{t})] = (\mathbf{t}) : K_s(\mathbf{t})] \cd
ot _s(\mathbf{t}) : F(\mathbf{t})] =  : K_s] \cdot _s : F].$$

### Step 7: Conclusion

Combining everything:
$$ : F] =  : K_s] \cdot _s : F] \leq d = [L : F(\mathbf{t})] < \infty.$$

Therefore $K$ is a **finite** (hence finitely generated) extension of $F$.

**Key points of the argument:**
- **Separable case** (Step 2): Separable irreducible polynomials over $F$ remain
 irreducible over $F(\mathbf{t})$ by regularity of purely transcendental extensi
ons.
- **Purely inseparable case** (Step 3): The UFD argument shows $F(\mathbf{t})^{p
^m} \cap F = F^{p^m}$, so purely inseparable element degrees are also preserved—
this is where the "possibly imperfect" hypothesis matters, and the result still
holds.
- **Algebraic closedness** (Step 5): $K_s$ algebraically closed in $K_s(\mathbf{
t})$ gives $K \cap K_s(\mathbf{t}) = K_s$, which yields $(\mathbf{t}):K_s(\mat
hbf{t})] = :K_s]$.

The imperfection of $F$ is handled by the separate treatment of the purely insep
arable part (Step 3), where the UFD structure of $F[t_1,\ldots,t_r]$ ensures tha
t $p$-th power roots cannot "appear" in a purely transcendental extension unless
 they already exist in $F$.
