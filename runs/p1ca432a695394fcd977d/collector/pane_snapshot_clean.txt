## Solution

We seek the Green's function on the first quadrant $0 < x, y < \infty$ with Diri
chlet data on both axes and decay at infinity. Because the domain is semi-infini
te in each variable with homogeneous Dirichlet conditions at the origin, the **F
ourier sine transform** is the natural tool in both $x$ and $y$.

### Step 1 — Apply the Fourier sine transform in $x$

Define
$$
G(k, y \mid \xi, \eta) = \int_0^\infty g(x, y \mid \xi, \eta)\,\sin(kx)\,dx .
$$

Using integration by parts twice, and using $g(0,y)=0$ together with $g,g_x \to
0$ as $x\to\infty$, the sine transform of $\partial^2 g/\partial x^2$ is $-k^2 G
$. The sine transform of $\delta(x-\xi)$ is $\sin(k\xi)$. Hence the PDE becomes
$$
-k^2\,G(k,y\mid\xi,\eta) + \frac{\partial^2 G}{\partial y^2} = -\sin(k\xi)\,\del
ta(y-\eta).
$$

### Step 2 — Apply the Fourier sine transform in $y$

Define the double transform
$$
\widehat{G}(k,\ell\mid\xi,\eta) = \int_0^\infty G(k,y\mid\xi,\eta)\,\sin(\ell y)
\,dy .
$$

The same boundary reasoning in $y$ (using $g(x,0)=0$ and decay) gives the transf
orm of $\partial^2 G/\partial y^2$ as $-\ell^2\widehat{G}$, and the transform of
 $\delta(y-\eta)$ is $\sin(\ell\eta)$. Therefore
$$
-(k^2+\ell^2)\,\widehat{G}(k,\ell\mid\xi,\eta) = -\sin(k\xi)\,\sin(\ell\eta),
$$
so
$$
\widehat{G}(k,\ell\mid\xi,\eta) = \frac{\sin(k\xi)\,\sin(\ell\eta)}{k^2+\ell^2}.
$$

### Step 3 — Invert in $y$

Using the inverse sine transform $G(k,y) = \tfrac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\infty \widehat{
G}(k,\ell)\sin(\ell y)\,d\ell$:
$$
G(k,y\mid\xi,\eta) = \frac{2}{\pi}\sin(k\xi)\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin(\ell\eta)\s
in(\ell y)}{k^2+\ell^2}\,d\ell .
$$

Write $\sin(\ell\eta)\sin(\ell y)=\tfrac12[\cos\ell(\eta-y)-\cos\ell(\eta+y)]$ a
nd use the standard integral
$$
\int_0^\infty \frac{\cos(\ell\alpha)}{k^2+\ell^2}\,d\ell = \frac{\pi}{2k}\,e^{-k
|\alpha|},\qquad k>0,
$$
to obtain
$$
\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin(\ell\eta)\sin(\ell y)}{k^2+\ell^2}\,d\ell
= \frac{\pi}{4k}\Big[e^{-k|\eta-y|}-e^{-k(\eta+y)}\Big].
$$

Thus
$$
G(k,y\mid\xi,\eta) = \frac{\sin(k\xi)}{2k}\Big[e^{-k|\eta-y|}-e^{-k(\eta+y)}\Big
].
$$

### Step 4 — Invert in $x$

Using $g(x,y) = \tfrac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\infty G(k,y)\sin(kx)\,dk$:
$$
g(x,y\mid\xi,\eta) = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin(k\xi)\sin(kx)}{k}\Big
[e^{-k|\eta-y|}-e^{-k(\eta+y)}\Big]dk .
$$

Set $A=\xi-x,\; B=\xi+x,\; c_1=|\eta-y|,\; c_2=\eta+y$, and use $\sin(k\xi)\sin(
kx)=\tfrac12[\cos(kA)-\cos(kB)]$:
$$
g = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^\infty \frac{\cos(kA)-\cos(kB)}{k}\Big[e^{-kc_1}-e^{-kc
_2}\Big]dk .
$$

Expanding the product of differences and applying the Frullani-type identity (ob
tained by differentiating w.r.t. $a$ and integrating back, using $\int_0^\infty
e^{-kc}\sin(ka)\,dk = a/(c^2+a^2)$):
$$
\int_0^\infty \frac{\cos(ka)-\cos(kb)}{k}\,e^{-kc}\,dk = \frac12\ln\!\frac{c^2+b
^2}{c^2+a^2},
$$
we get
$$
g = \frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot\frac12\left[\ln\frac{c_1^2+B^2}{c_1^2+A^2} - \ln\frac{c_
2^2+B^2}{c_2^2+A^2}\right]
= \frac{1}{4\pi}\ln\!\left\{\frac{(c_1^2+B^2)(c_2^2+A^2)}{(c_1^2+A^2)(c_2^2+B^2)
}\right\}.
$$

### Step 5 — Substitute back

With $A^2=(\xi-x)^2=(x-\xi)^2$, $B^2=(\xi+x)^2=(x+\xi)^2$, $c_1^2=(\eta-y)^2=(y-
\eta)^2$, $c_2^2=(\eta+y)^2=(y+\eta)^2$:

$$
\boxed{\;g(x,y\mid\xi,\eta) = \frac{1}{4\pi}\ln\!\left\{\frac{\big[(x+\xi)^2+(y-
\eta)^2\big]\big[(x-\xi)^2+(y+\eta)^2\big]}{\big[(x-\xi)^2+(y-\eta)^2\big]\big[(
x+\xi)^2+(y+\eta)^2\big]}\right\}\;}
$$

### Verification

**PDE.** Away from $(\xi,\eta)$ each logarithmic term is harmonic (it is $\ln$ o
f a distance, the 2D free-space Green's function), so $\nabla^2 g = 0$ there. Ne
ar $(x,y)=(\xi,\eta)$, only the term $-\tfrac{1}{4\pi}\ln[(x-\xi)^2+(y-\eta)^2]
= -\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\ln r_1$ is singular, and since $\nabla^2\big(-\tfrac{1}{2\pi}
\ln r\big)=-\delta$, the source $-\delta(x-\xi)\delta(y-\eta)$ is reproduced.

**Boundary $x=0$.** Setting $x=0$: numerator factors become $[\xi^2+(y-\eta)^2][
\xi^2+(y+\eta)^2]$, identical to the denominator factors — ratio $=1$, $\ln 1 =
0$. So $g(0,y)=0$. ✓

**Boundary $y=0$.** Setting $y=0$: numerator $=[(x+\xi)^2+\eta^2][(x-\xi)^2+\eta
^2]$, denominator $=[(x-\xi)^2+\eta^2][(x+\xi)^2+\eta^2]$ — ratio $=1$, $\ln 1=0
$. So $g(x,0)=0$. ✓

**Decay at infinity.** As $x,y\to\infty$ the four squared distances become asymp
totically equal, the ratio inside the logarithm tends to $1$, and $g\to 0$. ✓

**Method-of-images consistency.** The result equals $\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\ln\!\tfrac{
r_2 r_3}{r_1 r_4}$ with $r_1=\sqrt{(x-\xi)^2+(y-\eta)^2}$, $r_2=\sqrt{(x+\xi)^2+
(y-\eta)^2}$, $r_3=\sqrt{(x-\xi)^2+(y+\eta)^2}$, $r_4=\sqrt{(x+\xi)^2+(y+\eta)^2
}$, i.e. a unit positive source at $(\xi,\eta)$, negative images at $(-\xi,\eta)
$ and $(\xi,-\eta)$, and a positive image at $(-\xi,-\eta)$ — exactly the image
system enforcing Dirichlet data on both axes.
