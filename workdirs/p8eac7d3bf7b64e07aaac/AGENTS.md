# Problem

A specialized architectural firm is designing a triangular pavilion. From each corner of the triangular footprint, a structural support beam is extended to the exact midpoint of the opposite exterior wall. These three interior beams (the medians) intersect at a central pillar, and in doing so, they divide each of the pavilion’s three primary corner angles into two smaller sub-angles, creating a total of six sub-angles distributed across the three corners.

An interior designer wants to install wide-angle decorative lighting in as many of these six sub-angles as possible, but the lighting fixtures can only be installed in a sub-angle if that angle is strictly greater than $30^\circ$. 

What is the maximum possible number $k$ of these six sub-angles that can be greater than $30^\circ$ simultaneously?
