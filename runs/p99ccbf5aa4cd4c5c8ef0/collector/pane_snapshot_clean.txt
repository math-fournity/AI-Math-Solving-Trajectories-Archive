## Proof

We construct an explicit infinite family of such integers $n$.

**Key tool: the Pell equation.** The Pell equation
$$x^2 - 2y^2 = 1$$
has infinitely many solutions in positive integers. The fundamental solution is
$(x,y)=(3,2)$, and all positive solutions $(x_j,y_j)$ are generated by
$$x_j + y_j\sqrt{2} = (3+2\sqrt{2})^{j}, \qquad j=1,2,3,\dots$$
In particular the $y_j$ form a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers
: $2, 12, 70, 408, \dots$

**Construction.** For each solution $(x_j, y_j)$ with $j\ge 1$, set
$$n = 4\,y_j^2.$$
We claim that $n$, $n+1$, $n+2$ are each a sum of two squares.

1. **$n = 4y_j^2 = (2y_j)^2 + 0^2$.** ✓

2. **$n+1 = 4y_j^2 + 1 = (2y_j)^2 + 1^2$.** ✓

3. **$n+2 = 4y_j^2 + 2 = 2(2y_j^2 + 1)$.** Since $x_j^2 - 2y_j^2 = 1$, we have $
2y_j^2 + 1 = x_j^2$. Therefore
$$n+2 = 2\,x_j^2 = x_j^2 + x_j^2.$$
✓

**Infinitely many distinct $n$.** Since the $y_j$ are strictly increasing positi
ve integers, the values $n = 4y_j^2$ are all distinct. Hence there are infinitel
y many such $n$.

**Verification of the Pell equation claim.** The Pell equation $x^2 - 2y^2 = 1$
has infinitely many positive solutions: starting from the fundamental solution $
(3,2)$, the recurrence
$$x_{j+1} = 3x_j + 4y_j, \qquad y_{j+1} = 2x_j + 3y_j$$
produces an infinite strictly increasing sequence of solutions, since $(3+2\sqrt
2)^j(3+2\sqrt2) = (3+2\sqrt2)^{j+1}$ and $N(3+2\sqrt2)=9-8=1$, so every $(x_j,y_
j)$ satisfies $x_j^2 - 2y_j^2 = 1$.

**Concrete examples.**
- $j=1$: $(x,y)=(3,2)$, $n=16$: $16=4^2+0^2$, $17=4^2+1^2$, $18=3^2+3^2$. ✓
- $j=2$: $(x,y)=(17,12)$, $n=576$: $576=24^2+0^2$, $577=24^2+1^2$, $578=17^2+17^
2$. ✓
- $j=3$: $(x,y)=(99,70)$, $n=19600$: $19600=140^2+0^2$, $19601=140^2+1^2$, $1960
2=99^2+99^2$. ✓

This gives infinitely many integers $n$ such that $n$, $n+1$, and $n+2$ are each
 the sum of two squares of integers. $\blacksquare$
