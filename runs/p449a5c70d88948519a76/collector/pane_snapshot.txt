Looking at this problem, I need to find the relevant power of 10 that divides so
me Fibonacci number among the first 100,000,001 terms.

## Problem Interpretation

The probe transmits $F(0), F(1), \ldots, F(10^8)$ — that's $10^8 + 1 = 100{,}000
{,}001$ signals. We seek the largest $k$ such that $10^k \mid F(n)$ for some $1
\le n \le 10^8$ (excluding $F(0)=0$, which is trivially divisible by everything)
.

## Key Tool: Rank of Apparition

For positive integer $m$, define $z(m) = \min\{n \ge 1 : m \mid F(n)\}$. A class
ical theorem of Fibonacci divisibility states:

$$m \mid F(n) \iff z(m) \mid n$$

**Consequence:** There exists $n$ with $1 \le n \le 10^8$ and $10^k \mid F(n)$ i
f and only if $z(10^k) \le 10^8$.

## Computing $z(10^k)$

Since $10^k = 2^k \cdot 5^k$ with $\gcd(2^k, 5^k)=1$:
$$z(10^k) = \operatorname{lcm}\!\big(z(2^k),\, z(5^k)\big)$$

**For $5^k$:** $z(5) = 5$ (since $F(5)=5$). Since $5 \nmid F(5)/5 = 1$, the lift
ing-the-exponent property gives:
$$z(5^k) = 5^{k-1} \cdot z(5) = 5^k \quad \text{for all } k \ge 1$$

**For $2^k$:** Computing directly:
- $z(2) = 3$ ($F(3)=2$), $z(4) = 6$ ($F(6)=8$), $z(8) = 6$ ($F(6)=8$, and $8\mid
 8$)

At $z(8)=6$: $F(6)/8 = 1$ and $2 \nmid 1$, so $16 \nmid F(6)$. The lifting prope
rty then gives doubling:
- $z(16) = 12$, $z(32) = 24$, $z(64) = 48$, $\ldots$
- For $k \ge 3$: $z(2^k) = 3 \cdot 2^{k-2}$

(Verified: $z(8)=3\cdot2=6$ ✓, $z(16)=3\cdot4=12$ ✓, $z(32)=3\cdot8=24$ ✓)

**Combining for $k \ge 3$:**
$$z(10^k) = \operatorname{lcm}\!\big(3\cdot 2^{k-2},\; 5^k\big)$$

Since $\gcd(3\cdot 2^{k-2},\, 5^k) = 1$ (one has prime factors $\{2,3\}$, the ot
her $\{5\}$):
$$z(10^k) = 3 \cdot 2^{k-2} \cdot 5^k = 3 \cdot 25 \cdot (2^{k-2}\cdot 5^{k-2})
= 75 \cdot 10^{k-2}$$

## Finding the Critical $k$

| $k$ | $z(10^k)$ | $\le 10^8$? |
|-----|-----------|-------------|
| 8 | $75 \cdot 10^6 = 75{,}000{,}000$ | ✓ |
| 9 | $75 \cdot 10^7 = 750{,}000{,}$ | ✗ |

- **$k=8$:** $z(10^8) = 75{,}000{,}000 \le 100{,}000{,}000$, so $10^8 \mid F(75{
,}000{,}000)$. ✓
- **$k=9$:** $z(10^9) = 750{,}000{,}000 > 100{,}000{,}000$, so no $F(n)$ with $1
 \le n \le 10^8$ is divisible by $10^9$. ✗

Moreover, $F(75{,}000{,}000)$ is divisible by $10^8$ but **not** $10^9$, since $
z(10^9) = 750{,}000{,}000 \nmid 75{,}000{,}000$.

## Verification of Small Cases

- $k=1$: $z(10) = \operatorname{lcm}(3,5) = 15$; $F(15) = 610 = 10 \times 61$ ✓
- $k=2$: $z(100) = \operatorname{lcm}(6, 25) = 150 \le 10^8$ ✓
- $k=3$: $z(1000) = \operatorname{lcm}(6, 125) = 750 = 75 \cdot 10$ ✓ (formula h
olds)

## Conclusion

The largest $k$ such that at least one of the $100{,}000{,}001$ Fibonacci number
s $F(0), F(1), \ldots, F(10^8)$ (with $n \ge 1$) is divisible by $10^k$ is:

$$\boxed{k = 8}$$

This is achieved at $n = 75{,}000{,}000$, where $10^8 \mid F(75{,}000{,}000)$ bu
t $10^9 \nmid F(75{,}000{,}000)$.
