## Proof

Let $Q(y) = P(y - 1)$. Setting $x = y + 1$, we have $x^2 - 2x = y^2 - 1$ and $x
- 2 = y - 1$. The identity becomes:

$$Q(y^2) \equiv (Q(y))^2 \quad \text{for all real } y. \tag{$\star$}$$

Since both sides are polynomials and agree on all reals, ($\star$) holds as a po
lynomial identity.

**Step 2: Determine the leading coefficient.**

Let $Q(y) = a_n y^n + \cdots$ with $a_n \neq 0$. Then $Q(y^2)$ has leading term
$a_n y^{2n}$, while $(Q(y))^2$ has leading term $a_n^2 y^{2n}$. Equating: $a_n =
 a_n^2$, so $a_n = 1$ (since $a_n \neq 0$). **$Q$ is monic.**

**Step 3: Multiplicity relation for roots.**

Let $m(\alpha)$ denote the multiplicity of $\alpha$ as a root of $Q$ (over $\mat
hbb{C}$), with $m(\alpha) = 0$ if $\alpha$ is not a root. Comparing multipliciti
es in ($\star$):

- **For $\alpha \neq 0$:** The multiplicity of $\alpha$ in $Q(y^2)$ equals $m(\a
lpha^2)$ (since $y^2 - \beta$ has simple roots when $\beta \neq 0$). The multipl
icity of $\alpha$ in $(Q(y))^2$ is $2\,m(\alpha)$. Thus:
$$m(\alpha^2) = 2\,m(\alpha) \quad \text{for all } \alpha \neq 0. \tag{$\dagger$
}$$

- **For $\alpha = 0$:** The multiplicity of $0$ in $Q(y^2)$ is $2\,m(0)$ (each f
actor $y^2$ contributes multiplicity $2$), which equals the multiplicity $2\,m(0
)$ in $(Q(y))^2$. This is **automatically satisfied**, so $m(0)$ is unconstraine

$$m\!\left(\alpha^{2^k}\right) = 2^k \cdot m(\alpha) > 0 \quad \text{for all } k
 \geq 0.$$

So $\alpha, \alpha^2, \alpha^4, \alpha^8, \ldots$ are all roots of $Q$.

**Case A: $|\alpha| \neq 1$.** Then $|\alpha^{2^k}| = |\alpha|^{2^k} \to 0$ or $
\to \infty$, so all $\alpha^{2^k}$ are distinct — infinitely many roots. Contrad

**Case B: $|\alpha| = 1$, $\alpha$ not a root of unity.** If $\alpha^{2^j} = \al
pha^{2^k}$ for some $j < k$, then $\alpha^{2^j(2^{k-j}-1)} = 1$, making $\alpha$
 a root of unity. So all $\alpha^{2^k}$ are distinct — infinitely many roots. Co

**Case C: $\alpha$ is a root of unity.** Let $\alpha$ be a primitive $n$-th root
 of unity, with $n = 2^s \cdot m$, $m$ odd. Then $\beta := \alpha^{2^s}$ is a pr
imitive $m$-th root of unity ($m$ odd), and $m(\beta) = 2^s \cdot m(\alpha) > 0$

$$m(\beta) = m\!\left(\beta^{2^p}\right) = 2^p \cdot m(\beta),$$

so $(2^p - 1)\,m(\beta) = 0$, giving $m(\beta) = 0$ — contradiction.

All cases yield contradictions, so **$Q$ has no nonzero root**.
