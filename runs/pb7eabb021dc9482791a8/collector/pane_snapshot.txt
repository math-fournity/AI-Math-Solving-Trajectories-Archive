## Proof

**Theorem (Lie's Third Theorem).** Every finite-dimensional real Lie algebra $\m
athfrak{h}$ is isomorphic to the Lie algebra of some Lie group.

**Proof.**

Let $\mathfrak{h}$ be a finite-dimensional real Lie algebra. We construct a Lie
group $G$ with $\operatorname{Lie}(G)\cong\mathfrak{h}$ in two steps.

**Step 1: Embed $\mathfrak{h}$ into a matrix Lie algebra (Ado's Theorem).**

By **Ado's Theorem**, every finite-dimensional Lie algebra over a field of chara
cteristic zero admits a faithful finite-dimensional representation. Since $\math
bb{R}$ has characteristic zero, there exists a finite-dimensional real vector sp
ace $V$ and an injective Lie algebra homomorphism
$$
\rho:\mathfrak{h}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)=\operatorname{Lie}(GL(V)).
$$
Thus $\rho(\mathfrak{h})$ is a Lie subalgebra of $\mathfrak{gl}(V)$, isomorphic
to $\mathfrak{h}$.

**Step 2: Realize the subalgebra as the Lie algebra of a Lie subgroup.**

We use the **Lie subgroup–subalgebra theorem**, which is a consequence of Froben
ius's theorem on integrable distributions:

> *Let $G$ be a Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$. For every Lie subalge
bra $\mathfrak{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}$, there exists a unique connected immerse
d Lie subgroup $K\hookrightarrow G$ such that $\operatorname{Lie}(K)=\mathfrak{k
}$.*

Apply this theorem with $G=GL(V)$, $\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}(V)$, and $\mathfr
ak{k}=\rho(\mathfrak{h})$. We obtain a connected immersed Lie subgroup
$$
H\hookrightarrow GL(V)
$$
satisfying
$$
\operatorname{Lie}(H)=\rho(\mathfrak{h})\cong\mathfrak{h}.
$$

**Conclusion.** The immersed Lie subgroup $H\subseteq GL(V)$ is a Lie group (it
is a manifold with a smooth group operation, being an immersed submanifold of $G
L(V)$ that is closed under the group operations). Its Lie algebra is $\rho(\math
frak{h})\cong\mathfrak{h}$. Setting $G=H$, we have $\operatorname{Lie}(G)\cong\m
athfrak{h}$.

**Remark (simply connected version).** If one additionally requires $G$ to be si
mply connected, take the universal covering group $\widetilde{H}\to H$. The univ
ersal cover of a connected Lie group is again a Lie group with the same Lie alge
bra, so $\operatorname{Lie}(\widetilde{H})\cong\mathfrak{h}$, and $\widetilde{H}
$ is simply connected. By the uniqueness of the simply connected Lie group with
a given Lie algebra, this $\widetilde{H}$ is the unique such group up to isomorp
hism.

Therefore, for any finite-dimensional real Lie algebra $\mathfrak{h}$, there nec
essarily exists a Lie group $G$ with $\operatorname{Lie}(G)\cong\mathfrak{h}$. $
\blacksquare$
