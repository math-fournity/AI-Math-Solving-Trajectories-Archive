## Proof

**Yes**, any symmetric bidifferential can be expressed in this form.

**Setup.** Let $C$ be a compact Riemann surface of genus $g$, and let $V = \math
rm{H}^0(C, \mathrm{K}_C)$ be the $g$-dimensional vector space of holomorphic 1-f
orms with basis $\eta_1, \dots, \eta_g$. A bidifferential on $C^{\times 2} = C \
times C$ is a holomorphic section of the line bundle
$$\mathrm{K}_C \boxtimes \mathrm{K}_C := p_1^*\mathrm{K}_C \otimes p_2^*\mathrm{
K}_C$$
on $C \times C$, where $p_1, p_2 : C \times C \to C$ are the two projections. Th
e space of bidifferentials is $\mathrm{H}^0(C \times C,\, p_1^*\mathrm{K}_C \oti
mes p_2^*\mathrm{K}_C)$.

**Step 1: Künneth decomposition.** By the Künneth formula for coherent cohomolog
y on a product of curves, applied at degree $0$:
$$\mathrm{H}^0(C \times C,\, p_1^*\mathrm{K}_C \otimes p_2^*\mathrm{K}_C) \;\con
g\; \mathrm{H}^0(C, \mathrm{K}_C) \otimes \mathrm{H}^0(C, \mathrm{K}_C) = V \oti
mes V.$$
Under this isomorphism, a pure tensor $\alpha \otimes \beta \in V \otimes V$ map
s to the bidifferential $\alpha \boxtimes \beta = p_1^*\alpha \otimes p_2^*\beta
$. Consequently, the collection $\{\eta_i \boxtimes \eta_j\}_{1 \le i,j \le g}$
is a basis of the space of all bidifferentials, which has dimension $g^2$.

**Step 2: Action of the swap involution.** Let $\sigma : C \times C \to C \times
 C$ be the swap map $\sigma(z_1, z_2) = (z_2, z_1)$. Pullback by $\sigma$ acts o
n bidifferentials. On basis elements:
$$\sigma^*(\eta_i \boxtimes \eta_j) = \sigma^*(p_1^*\eta_i \otimes p_2^*\eta_j)
= p_2^*\eta_i \otimes p_1^*\eta_j = \eta_j \boxtimes \eta_i.$$
A bidifferential $\omega$ is **symmetric** by definition iff $\sigma^*\omega = \
omega$.

**Step 3: Characterizing symmetric bidifferentials.** Any bidifferential $\omega
$ can be uniquely written (by Step 1) as
$$\omega = \sum_{i,j=1}^g A_{ij}\, \eta_i \boxtimes \eta_j, \qquad A_{ij} \in \m
athbb{C}.$$
Applying $\sigma^*$ and using Step 2:
$$\sigma^*\omega = \sum_{i,j=1}^g A_{ij}\, \eta_j \boxtimes \eta_i = \sum_{i,j=1
}^g A_{ji}\, \eta_i \boxtimes \eta_j,$$
where in the last equality we relabeled the dummy indices $i \leftrightarrow j$.
 Since $\{\eta_i \boxtimes \eta_j\}$ is a basis (linearly independent), the cond
ition $\sigma^*\omega = \omega$ holds if and only if the coefficients match:
$$A_{ij} = A_{ji} \quad \text{for all } 1 \le i, j \le g.$$

**Conclusion.** The symmetric bidifferentials are precisely the elements of $\ma
thrm{H}^0(C \times C, \mathrm{K}_C \boxtimes \mathrm{K}_C)$ fixed by $\sigma^*$,
 which by the computation above are exactly those of the form $\sum_{i,j=1}^g A_
{ij}\, \eta_i \boxtimes \eta_j$ with $A_{ij} = A_{ji}$. Equivalently, the symmet
ric bidifferentials form the subspace $\mathrm{Sym}^2(V) \subset V \otimes V$, o
f dimension $\binom{g+1}{2} = \frac{g(g+1)}{2}$, spanned by $\{\eta_i \boxtimes
\eta_i\}_{1 \le i \le g}$ together with $\{\eta_i \boxtimes \eta_j + \eta_j \box
times \eta_i\}_{1 \le i < j \le g}$.

Therefore, **yes**: every symmetric bidifferential on $C^{\times 2}$ can be expr
essed as $\sum_{i,j=1}^g A_{ij}\, \eta_i \boxtimes \eta_j$ with $A_{ij} = A_{ji}
$, and conversely every such expression is symmetric.
