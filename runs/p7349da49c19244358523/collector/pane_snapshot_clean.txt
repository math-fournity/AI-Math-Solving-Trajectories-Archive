## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $p_n(x_n) \to a(x)$.

**Key fact (uniform convergence on compact subsets).** Since $p_m$ is the $m$-th
 Taylor polynomial of $a$ and $p_m \to a$, the function $a$ is real-analytic. A
standard property of Taylor series of analytic functions is that the Taylor poly
nomials converge to the function **uniformly on every compact subset** of the (o
pen) interval/disk of convergence. In particular, for any point $x$ in the inter
ior of the domain of convergence and any $\delta > 0$ small enough that $[x-\del
ta, x+\delta]$ lies inside the domain, we have
$$\sup_{z \in [x-\delta, x+\delta]} |p_m(z) - a(z)| \longrightarrow 0 \quad \tex
t{as } m \to \infty.$$

**Continuity of $a$.** Each $p_m$ is a polynomial and hence continuous. Since $p
_m \to a$ uniformly on compact sets, the limit $a$ is continuous (uniform limit
of continuous functions is continuous).

**Setting up the diagonal estimate.** Since $x_n \to x$, choose $\delta > 0$ suc
h that $K := [x - \delta,\, x + \delta]$ is a compact subset of the domain of co
nvergence. There exists $N_0$ such that for all $n \geq N_0$, $x_n \in K$.

We decompose the error into two parts:
$$p_n(x_n) - a(x) = \underbrace{\bigl(p_n(x_n) - a(x_n)\bigr)}_{\text{(I)}} + \u
nderbrace{\bigl(a(x_n) - a(x)\bigr)}_{\text{(II)}}.$$

**Term (II) vanishes:** Since $a$ is continuous (established above) and $x_n \to
 x$,
$$|a(x_n) - a(x)| \longrightarrow 0.$$

**Term (I) vanishes:** For every $n \geq N_0$, the point $x_n$ lies in $K$, so
$$|p_n(x_n) - a(x_n)| \leq \sup_{z \in K} |p_n(z) - a(z)| \longrightarrow 0$$
by the uniform convergence of $p_n$ to $a$ on the compact set $K$.

**Combining:** By the triangle inequality,
$$|p_n(x_n) - a(x)| \leq \sup_{z \in K}|p_n(z) - a(z)| + |a(x_n) - a(x)| \longri
ghtarrow 0 + 0 = 0.$$

Therefore $p_n(x_n) \to a(x)$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on the necessity of uniform convergence.** The argument relies cruciall
y on $p_m \to a$ being **uniform** on a neighborhood of $x$ (not merely pointwis
e). For Taylor polynomials of an analytic function, this uniform-on-compact-subs
ets convergence is automatic within the radius of convergence, so the conclusion
 holds. Under mere pointwise convergence (which does not arise for Taylor series
 of analytic functions inside the radius of convergence), the conclusion can fai
l in general—uniform convergence on a neighborhood of $x$ is the essential hypot
hesis.
