**Claim.** The unique surjective solution is $f(x)=x$.

**Verification.** With $f(x)=x$ we have $f(f(x))=x$, and $2x\cdot x = x(x+x)=2x^
{2}$. ✓ The identity is surjective on $\mathbb R_{>0}$.

### Step 1. Reformulation via $g(x)=f(x)/x$

Set $g(x)=\dfrac{f(x)}{x}>0$. Then $f(x)=x\,g(x)$ and
$$f(f(x))=f(x)\,g(f(x))=x\,g(x)\,g(f(x)).$$
The equation $2x\,f(f(x))=f(x)\bigl(x+f(f(x))\bigr)$ becomes
$$2x\cdot x\,g(x)\,g(f(x))=x\,g(x)\bigl(x+x\,g(x)\,g(f(x))\bigr).$$
Dividing by $x\,g(x)>0$ and then by $x>0$:
$$2\,g(f(x))=1+g(x)\,g(f(x)),$$
hence
$$g(f(x))=\frac{1}{2-g(x)}.\tag{1}$$
Since $g(f(x))>0$, we need $g(x)<2$, i.e. $g:\mathbb R_{>0}\to(0,2)$.

### Step 2. Reduction to a one-dimensional dynamical relation

Let $h(x)=g(x)-1$, so $h:\mathbb R_{>0}\to(-1,1)$. From (1):
$$1+h(f(x))=\frac{1}{2-(1+h(x))}=\frac{1}{1-h(x)},$$
giving
$$h(f(x))=\frac{h(x)}{1-h(x)}.\tag{2}$$
Define $T(t)=\dfrac{t}{1-t}$. Then (2) reads $h(f(x))=T(h(x))$, and by induction
 on $n\ge 0$:
$$h\bigl(f^{(n)}(x)\bigr)=T^{\,n}(h(x)),\qquad T^{\,n}(t)=\frac{t}{1-nt}.\tag{3}
$$
(Proof of (3): $T^{n+1}(t)=\dfrac{t/(1-nt)}{1-t/(1-nt)}=\dfrac{t}{1-(n+1)t}$.)

Since every $h(f^{(n)}(x))\in(-1,1)$, we require $T^{n}(t)\in(-1,1)$ for all $n\
ge0$, where $t=h(x)$.

### Step 3. $h(x)\le 0$ for all $x$ (i.e. $f(x)\le x$)

Suppose $t=h(x)>0$ for some $x$.

- If $1/t\notin\mathbb Z$, let $N=\lfloor 1/t\rfloor$. Then $1-Nt=t\{1/t\}>0$ wi
th $\{1/t\}\in(0,1)$, so
  $$T^{N}(t)=\frac{t}{1-Nt}=\frac{1}{\{1/t\}}>1,$$
  contradicting $T^{N}(t)<1$.
- If $1/t=k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}$, then
  $$T^{k-1}(t)=\frac{t}{1-(k-1)t}=\frac{t}{1/k}=kt=1,$$
  again contradicting $T^{k-1}(t)<1$.

So $h(x)\le0$ for every $x>0$; equivalently $f(x)\le x$ for all $x>0$.

### Step 4. Surjectivity forces $h\equiv 0$

Since $f$ is surjective, the range of $h\circ f$ equals the range of $h$. Using
(2):
$$S:=h(\mathbb R_{>0})=h(f(\mathbb R_{>0}))=T\bigl(h(\mathbb R_{>0})\bigr)=T(S).
$$
From Step 3, $S\subseteq(-1,0]$. Now $T$ maps $(-1,0]$ into $\bigl(-\tfrac12,0\b
igr]$ because
$$\lim_{t\to-1^{+}}T(t)=-\tfrac12,\quad T(0)=0,\quad T'<0\text{ on }(-1,0).$$
Hence $S=T(S)\subseteq(-\tfrac12,0]$. Reapplying $T$: $S\subseteq T\bigl((-\tfra
c12,0]\bigr)=(-\tfrac13,0]$. By induction,
$$S\subseteq\Bigl(-\frac{1}{n+1},\,0\Bigr]\quad\text{for every }n\ge1.$$
Letting $n\to\infty$ gives $S\subseteq\{0\}$, so $S=\{0\}$, i.e. $h\equiv0$, i.e
. $g\equiv1$, i.e.
$$f(x)=x\quad\text{for all }x>0.$$

### Step 5. Conclusion

The identity $f(x)=x$ satisfies the equation and is surjective, and Steps 1–4 sh
ow it is the only surjective solution.
