## Proof

**Claim:** For any continuous $f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}$, $\mathcal{H}^1(\mathrm{gra
ph}\,f)\ge 1$.

**Key tool.** Let $\pi:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$ be the projection onto the fir
st coordinate, $\pi(x,y)=x$. This map is $1$-Lipschitz, since
$$|\pi(p)-\pi(q)|=|x_p-x_q|\le \|(x_p,y_p)-(x_q,y_q)\|.$$

A standard property of Hausdorff measure is that a $1$-Lipschitz map does not in
crease $\mathcal{H}^1$-measure: for every set $A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^2$,
$$\mathcal{H}^1(\pi(A))\le \mathcal{H}^1(A).$$
(Indeed, any cover of $A$ by sets of diameter $\le\delta$ maps to a cover of $\p
i(A)$ by sets of diameter $\le\delta$, so the infimum defining $\mathcal{H}^1_\d
elta$ of the image is bounded by that of $A$; taking $\delta\to0$ gives the ineq
uality.)

**Applying it.** Take $A=\mathrm{graph}\,f=\{(x,f(x)):x\in[0,1]\}$. Since $f$ is
 defined on all of $[0,1]$, every $x\in[0,1]$ gives a point $(x,f(x))\in A$, so
$$\pi(A)=\{x:\exists\,y,\,(x,y)\in A\}=[0,1].$$

Therefore
$$\mathcal{H}^1(\mathrm{graph}\,f)\ge \mathcal{H}^1(\pi(\mathrm{graph}\,f))=\mat
hcal{H}^1([0,1])=1.$$

**Conclusion.** The answer is **yes**: $\mathcal{H}^1(\mathrm{graph}\,f)\ge 1$ f
or every continuous $f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}$.

**Remark.** The bound is sharp: for the constant function $f\equiv 0$, the graph
 is the segment $\{(x,0):x\in[0,1]\}$, which is a line segment of length $1$, so
 $\mathcal{H}^1(\mathrm{graph}\,f)=1$. For non-constant continuous $f$ the measu
re is in fact strictly larger (it equals the total variation length of the curve
, which exceeds $1$ whenever $f$ is non-constant), but the question only asks fo
r the lower bound, which holds with equality in the constant case.
