## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. For any algebraic curve $X$ over an algebraically closed field $
k$, there exists $f \in k[x,y]$ such that $X$ is birational to $\{f(x,y)=0\}$.

Let $X$ be an algebraic curve over $k$ (an irreducible algebraic variety of dime
nsion 1), and let $K = k(X)$ be its function field. Then $K$ is a finitely gener
ated field extension of $k$ with $\operatorname{tr.deg}_k K = 1$.

### Step 1: Separating transcendence basis

Since $k$ is algebraically closed, $k$ is perfect. By the theorem on separably g
enerated extensions, every finitely generated extension of a perfect field is se
parably generated. Therefore there exists an element $t \in K$, transcendental o
ver $k$, such that $K / k(t)$ is a **finite separable** extension.

### Step 2: Primitive element theorem

The field $k(t)$ is infinite (since $k$ is infinite, as every algebraically clos
ed field is infinite). The extension $K/k(t)$ is finite and separable. By the pr
imitive element theorem, there exists $y \in K$ such that

$$K = k(t, y).$$

### Step 3: Construct the polynomial

Since $y$ is algebraic over $k(t)$, let $g(Y) \in k(t)[Y]$ be the minimal polyno
mial of $y$ over $k(t)$. Clearing denominators, we obtain a polynomial $f \in k[
t, Y]$. Renaming $t \to x$ and $Y \to y$, we have $f \in k[x,y]$ with $f(t, y) =
 0$ in $K$.

By Gauss's lemma, since $g$ is irreducible in $k(t)[Y]$ and $f$ is a primitive p
olynomial (after dividing out the content), $f$ is **irreducible in $k[x,y]$**.

### Step 4: Verify birationality

Consider the affine plane curve $C = \{f(x,y) = 0\} \subset \mathbb{A}^2_k$. Sin
ce $f$ is irreducible, $C$ is an irreducible curve with coordinate ring $k[C] =
k[x,y]/(f)$ and function field

$$k(C) = \operatorname{Frac}\!\big(k[x,y]/(f)\big) = k(\bar{x}, \bar{y}),$$

where $\bar{x}, \bar{y}$ are the images of $x, y$ satisfying $f(\bar{x}, \bar{y}
) = 0$.

The assignment $\bar{x} \mapsto t,\; \bar{y} \mapsto y$ defines a $k$-algebra ho
momorphism $k[x,y]/(f) \to K$. Since $f$ is the (cleared) minimal polynomial of
$y$ over $k(t)$, this map is injective and induces an isomorphism on fraction fi
elds:

$$k(C) = k(\bar{x}, \bar{y}) \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; k(t, y) = K = k(X).$$

Two algebraic curves over $k$ are birational if and only if their function field
s are isomorphic as $k$-extensions. Since $k(C) \cong k(X)$, the curve $X$ is bi
rational to the plane curve $C = \{f(x,y) = 0\}$.

### Conclusion

For every algebraic curve $X$ over an algebraically closed field $k$, there exis
ts a polynomial $f \in k[x,y]$ such that $X$ is birational to $\{f(x,y) = 0\}$.
The key ingredients are:

1. **$k$ perfect** (algebraically closed $\Rightarrow$ perfect) $\Rightarrow$ $k
(X)$ is separably generated over $k$, giving a separating transcendence basis el
ement $t$ with $k(X)/k(t)$ finite separable.
2. **Primitive element theorem** (applicable since $k(t)$ is infinite and the ex
tension is finite separable) $\Rightarrow$ $k(X) = k(t, y)$ for some $y$.
3. The minimal polynomial of $y$ over $k(t)$, cleared of denominators, gives the
 desired $f \in k[x,y]$, and the function field of $\{f=0\}$ is isomorphic to $k
(X)$.
