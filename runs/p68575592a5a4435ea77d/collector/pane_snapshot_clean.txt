# Proof

**Claim.** Yes. The field $\mathbb{F}(a)$ embeds into $M_n(\mathbb{F})$.

**Setup.** Since $a$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{F}$ of degree $n$, the extension
 $\mathbb{F}(a)$ is an $n$-dimensional $\mathbb{F}$-vector space with basis $\{1
, a, a^2, \dots, a^{n-1}\}$. Let $p(x) = x^n + c_{n-1}x^{n-1} + \cdots + c_1 x +
 c_0 \in \mathbb{F}[x]$ be the minimal polynomial of $a$ over $\mathbb{F}$ (moni
c, degree $n$).

**Construction of the map.** For each $\beta \in \mathbb{F}(a)$, define the left
-multiplication map
$$
L_\beta : \mathbb{F}(a) \to \mathbb{F}(a), \qquad L_\beta(v) = \beta v.
$$
This is $\mathbb{F}$-linear: for $\lambda, \mu \in \mathbb{F}$ and $u, v \in \ma
thbb{F}(a)$,
$$
L_\beta(\lambda u + \mu v) = \beta(\lambda u + \mu v) = \lambda(\beta u) + \mu(\
beta v) = \lambda L_\beta(u) + \mu L_\beta(v),
$$
using commutativity of $\mathbb{F}(a)$ and that $\lambda, \mu$ are scalars (cent
ral in $\mathbb{F}(a)$). Hence $L_\beta \in \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mat
hbb{F}(a))$.

Fixing the basis $\{1, a, \dots, a^{n-1}\}$ identifies $\operatorname{End}_{\mat
hbb{F}}(\mathbb{F}(a)) \cong M_n(\mathbb{F})$, so each $L_\beta$ is represented
by a matrix $[\beta] \in M_n(\mathbb{F})$. Define
$$
\varphi : \mathbb{F}(a) \to M_n(\mathbb{F}), \qquad \varphi(\beta) = [\beta].
$$

**$\varphi$ is an $\mathbb{F}$-algebra homomorphism.** For $\beta, \gamma \in \m
athbb{F}(a)$ and $\lambda \in \mathbb{F}$:
- $\varphi(\beta + \gamma) = [\beta + \gamma] = [\beta] + [\gamma] = \varphi(\be
ta) + \varphi(\gamma)$, since $L_{\beta+\gamma} = L_\beta + L_\gamma$.
- $\varphi(\beta\gamma) = [\beta\gamma] = [\beta][\gamma] = \varphi(\beta)\varph
i(\gamma)$, since $L_{\beta\gamma} = L_\beta \circ L_\gamma$.
- $\varphi(\lambda) = \lambda I_n$ for $\lambda \in \mathbb{F}$, since $L_\lambd
a = \lambda \cdot \mathrm{id}$, so $\varphi$ is $\mathbb{F}$-linear and unital (
$\varphi(1) = I_n$).

Thus $\varphi$ is a unital $\mathbb{F}$-algebra homomorphism.

**$\varphi$ is injective.** The kernel is a two-sided ideal of the field $\mathb
b{F}(a)$. Since $\mathbb{F}(a)$ is a field, its only ideals are $\{0\}$ and $\ma
thbb{F}(a)$ itself. But $\varphi(1) = I_n \neq 0$, so $\ker\varphi \neq \mathbb{
F}(a)$, hence $\ker\varphi = \{0\}$. Therefore $\varphi$ is injective.

(Equivalently and more directly: if $\varphi(\beta) = 0$, then $L_\beta = 0$, so
 $\beta \cdot 1 = 0$, giving $\beta = 0$.)

**Conclusion.** $\varphi : \mathbb{F}(a) \hookrightarrow M_n(\mathbb{F})$ is an
injective $\mathbb{F}$-algebra homomorphism, i.e., an embedding of $\mathbb{F}(a
)$ into the matrix ring $M_n(\mathbb{F})$.

**Remark (the matrix of $a$).** Concretely, $\varphi(a)$ is the companion matrix
 of $p$:
$$
\varphi(a) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & -c_0 \\ 1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & -
c_1 \\ 0 & 1 & \cdots & 0 & -c_2 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots \
\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & 1 & -c_{n-1} \end{pmatrix},
$$
obtained by expressing $a \cdot a^{i}$ in the basis $\{1, a, \dots, a^{n-1}\}$,
using $a^n = -c_{n-1}a^{n-1} - \cdots - c_0$. Its characteristic polynomial is e
xactly $p(x)$, so $\varphi(a)$ has minimal polynomial $p$ over $\mathbb{F}$, con
firming the embedding realizes $\mathbb{F}(a) \cong \mathbb{F}[\varphi(a)] \subs
eteq M_n(\mathbb{F})$.
