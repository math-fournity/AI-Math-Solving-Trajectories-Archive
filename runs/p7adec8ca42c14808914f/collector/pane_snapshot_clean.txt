## Solution

**Setup.** Since the three face angles at $P$ are all $90^\circ$, place $P$ at t
he origin with the three edges along coordinate axes:
$$A=(a,0,0),\quad B=(0,b,0),\quad C=(0,0,c),\qquad a=AP,\;b=BP,\;c=CP.$$

**Edge lengths.**
$$AB=\sqrt{a^2+b^2},\quad AC=\sqrt{a^2+c^2},\quad BC=\sqrt{b^2+c^2}.$$

**Area of $\triangle ABC$.** With $\vec{AB}=(-a,b,0)$ and $\vec{AC}=(-a,0,c)$,
$$\vec{AB}\times\vec{AC}=(bc,\,ac,\,ab),\qquad |\vec{AB}\times\vec{AC}|=\sqrt{a^
2b^2+b^2c^2+c^2a^2}.$$
So $\,[ABC]=\tfrac12\sqrt{a^2b^2+b^2c^2+c^2a^2}$.

**Using $AP^2=[ABC]$:**
$$a^2=\tfrac12\sqrt{a^2b^2+b^2c^2+c^2a^2}\;\Longrightarrow\;4a^4=a^2b^2+b^2c^2+c
^2a^2.\tag{1}$$

**Using $\angle ABC=30^\circ$.** At $B$: $\vec{BA}=(a,-b,0)$, $\vec{BC}=(0,-b,c)
$, so
$$\cos\angle ABC=\frac{b^2}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\,\sqrt{b^2+c^2}}=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}.$$
Squaring: $\;4b^4=3(a^2+b^2)(b^2+c^2).\tag{2}$

**Target: $\tan\angle ACB$.** At $C$: $\vec{CA}=(a,0,-c)$, $\vec{CB}=(0,b,-c)$,
and $\vec{CA}\times\vec{CB}=(bc,ac,ab)$, whose magnitude is $2a^2$ by (1). Thus
$$\cos\angle ACB=\frac{c^2}{\sqrt{a^2+c^2}\,\sqrt{b^2+c^2}},\qquad \sin\angle AC
B=\frac{2a^2}{\sqrt{a^2+c^2}\,\sqrt{b^2+c^2}},$$
$$\tan\angle ACB=\frac{2a^2}{c^2}.\tag{3}$$

**Normalize.** Let $x=b^2/a^2$, $y=c^2/a^2$. Then (1) and (2) become
$$x+xy+y=4,\qquad 4x^2=3(1+x)(x+y).$$
From the first, $x=\dfrac{4-y}{1+y}$. Substituting into the second:
$$1+x=\frac{5}{1+y},\qquad x+y=\frac{4+y^2}{1+y},$$
$$4\cdot\frac{(4-y)^2}{(1+y)^2}=3\cdot\frac{5}{1+y}\cdot\frac{4+y^2}{(1+y)^2}\cd
ot(1+y)=\frac{15(4+y^2)}{(1+y)^2}.$$
So $4(4-y)^2=15(4+y^2)$, i.e.
$$64-32y+4y^2=60+15y^2\;\Longrightarrow\;11y^2+32y-4=0.$$
$$y=\frac{-32\pm\sqrt{1024+176}}{22}=\frac{-16\pm 10\sqrt3}{11}.$$
Since $y=c^2/a^2>0$, we take $y=\dfrac{10\sqrt3-16}{11}$.

**Final computation.**
$$\tan\angle ACB=\frac{2}{y}=\frac{22}{10\sqrt3-16}\cdot\frac{10\sqrt3+16}{10\sq
rt3+16}=\frac{22(10\sqrt3+16)}{300-256}=\frac{22(10\sqrt3+16)}{44}=5\sqrt3+8.$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{8+5\sqrt3}$
