## Proof

Let

$$f(q) = \sum_{p \in \mathbb{P}} q^p, \qquad 0 < q < 1.$$

We determine its value and whether a closed form exists.

### 1. Convergence

For $0<q<1$, since every prime satisfies $p\ge 2$,

$$0 \le f(q) = \sum_{p\in\mathbb{P}} q^p \le \sum_{n=2}^{\infty} q^n = \frac{q^2
}{1-q} < \infty.$$

So the series converges absolutely for every $0<q<1$. The radius of convergence
of the power series $f(q)=\sum_{n\ge 1} a_n q^n$ (with $a_n = \mathbf{1}_{\mathb
b{P}}(n)\in\{0,1\}$) is

$$R = \frac{1}{\limsup_{n\to\infty} a_n^{1/n}} = 1,$$

since $a_n=1$ for infinitely many $n$ (there are infinitely many primes) and $a_
n\in\{0,1\}$.

### 2. The unit circle is a natural boundary

The coefficients $a_n = \mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n)$ are **integers**, and the ra
dius of convergence is $1$. We invoke:

> **Pólya–Carlson Theorem.** *If a power series $\sum a_n z^n$ has integer coeff
icients and radius of convergence $1$, then either it represents a rational func
tion, or the unit circle $|z|=1$ is a natural boundary (i.e., $f$ cannot be anal
ytically continued across any arc of $|z|=1$).*

To apply it we must rule out the rational case. A rational generating function w
ith bounded integer coefficients yields a coefficient sequence that is **eventua
lly periodic** (it satisfies a linear recurrence with integer coefficients, and
bounded integer solutions of such recurrences are eventually periodic). The prim
e indicator sequence $\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n)$ is *not* eventually periodic:
if it were periodic with period $T$ beyond some $N_0$, then either primes would
eventually have density $k/T$ for some $k$, or be eventually absent. But the pri
mes have natural density $0$ while occurring infinitely often, and more concrete
ly, periodicity would force every residue class mod $T$ that ever contains a pri
me to contain primes at *every* sufficiently large representative — in particula
r the class $0 \pmod T$ would have to contain infinitely many primes, which is i
mpossible once $T$ has any prime factor (take $T$ itself: no prime $>T$ is divis
ible by $T$). Hence the sequence is not eventually periodic, so $f$ is **not rat
ional**.

By Pólya–Carlson, the unit circle $|q|=1$ is a **natural boundary** of $f$.

### 3. No closed-form expression exists

Every function expressible in closed form via the usual operations (arithmetic,
composition, exponentiation, logarithms, trigonometric functions) and the standa
rd special functions (gamma, zeta, polylogarithms, hypergeometric functions, etc
.) has only **isolated singularities** and **branch cuts** — that is, its singul
ar set in the finite plane is a closed set with empty interior, and in particula
r it can be analytically continued across some arc of any circle on which it is
defined. Such a function **cannot** have a natural boundary.

Since $f(q)=\sum_{p\in\mathbb{P}} q^p$ has the unit circle as a natural boundary
, it cannot coincide, on the open disk $|q|<1$, with any finite combination of e
lementary functions or standard special functions. Therefore:

> **The series $\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\mathbb{P}} q^p$ converges for every $0<q
<1$ (to a finite value bounded by $q^2/(1-q)$), but it does not admit a closed-f
orm expression in terms of elementary functions or standard special functions.**

### 4. Best available representation (for completeness)

The value can be represented, but not in closed form, via Möbius inversion. Writ
ing $-\log(1-q^n)=\sum_{m\ge 1} q^{nm}/m$ and inverting the divisor relation $\m
athbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(N) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{d\mid N} d\,a_d$ yields

$$f(q) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n\bigl(-\log(1-q^n)\bigr), \qquad a_n = \frac{1}{
n}\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)\,\frac{n}{d}\,\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}\!\left(\tfrac{n}{d}
\right),$$

an infinite sum of logarithms with non-elementary coefficients — confirming that
 no finite closed form is available.

---

**Conclusion.** For $0<q<1$ the series $\sum_{p\in\mathbb{P}} q^p$ converges abs
olutely, but by the Pólya–Carlson theorem its unit circle is a natural boundary
(the coefficient sequence $\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}$ is integer-valued, of radius
 $1$, and not eventually periodic), which precludes any representation as an ele
mentary or standard-special-function closed form. **No closed-form expression ex
ists.**
