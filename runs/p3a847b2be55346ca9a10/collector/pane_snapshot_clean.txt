## Proof

We work on the circle $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$, since $f$ has period $1$. Zeros a
re counted on $[0,1)$ with multiplicity.

### Preliminaries

Since $a_N \neq 0$, each $f^{(k)}$ is a nonzero trigonometric polynomial of degr
ee $N$:
$$f^{(k)}(t) = \sum_{j=1}^N a_j (2\pi j)^k \sin\!\left(2\pi j t + \tfrac{k\pi}{2
}\right),$$
with leading term $a_N(2\pi N)^k \sin(2\pi N t + k\pi/2) \not\equiv 0$. A trigon
ometric polynomial of degree $N$ has at most $2N$ zeros on the circle (counting
multiplicity), so $N_k \leq 2N$ for all $k$.

### Part 1: $N_0 \leq N_1 \leq N_2 \leq \cdots$

**Claim.** If $g: \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{R}$ is smooth and not identi
cally zero, with $m$ zeros (counting multiplicity) on the circle, then $g'$ has
at least $m$ zeros (counting multiplicity) on the circle.

*Proof of Claim.* Let $t_1 < t_2 < \cdots < t_r$ be the distinct zeros of $g$ in
 $[0,1)$ with multiplicities $m_1, \ldots, m_r$, so $\sum m_i = m$.

**(a) Zeros from multiplicity.** If $t_i$ is a zero of $g$ of multiplicity $m_i
\geq 1$, then $g(t_i)=g'(t_i)=\cdots=g^{(m_i-1)}(t_i)=0$ and $g^{(m_i)}(t_i)\neq
 0$. Hence $t_i$ is a zero of $g'$ of multiplicity $\geq m_i - 1$.

**(b) Zeros from Rolle's theorem.** On each open interval $(t_i, t_{i+1})$ for $
i=1,\ldots,r-1$, and on the "wrap-around" interval $(t_r, t_1+1)$, the function
$g$ vanishes at both endpoints and is differentiable, so by Rolle's theorem $g'$
 has at least one zero in each such interval. This gives at least $r$ additional
 zeros (one per interval), lying in open intervals disjoint from the points $t_i
$.

**(c) Total count.** The zeros from (a) and (b) lie in disjoint sets (the points
 $t_i$ vs. the open intervals between them), so:
$$N(g') \geq \sum_{i=1}^r (m_i - 1) + r = \sum_{i=1}^r m_i = m. \qquad \square$$

Applying the claim with $g = f^{(k)}$ gives $N_{k+1} \geq N_k$ for all $k \geq 0
$.

### Part 2: $\lim_{k\to\infty} N_k = 2N$

$$R_k(t) = \sum_{j=1}^{N-1} \frac{a_j}{a_N}\left(\frac{j}{N}\right)^k \sin\!\lef
t(2\pi j t + \tfrac{k\pi}{2}\right).$$

Since $j/N < 1$ for $j < N$, we have $R_k \to 0$ and $R_k' \to 0$ uniformly as $

**The model function.** Let $\phi_k(t) = \sin(2\pi N t + k\pi/2)$. Its zeros on

and each is **simple** since $|\phi_k'(t_m^{(k)})| = |2\pi N \cos(2\pi N t_m^{(k

**Perturbation argument.** Choose $\delta < \frac{1}{4N}$ and let $I_m = (t_m^{(

- At the center: $|\phi_k(t_m^{(k)})| = 0$ and $|\phi_k'(t_m^{(k)})| = 2\pi N$.
- At the endpoints $t_m^{(k)} \pm \delta$: $|\phi_k| = |\sin(2\pi N\delta)| \geq

Now choose $K$ large enough that $\|R_k\|_\infty < c_0/2$ and $\|R_k'\|_\infty <

2. **Inside each $I_m$:** At the endpoints, $g_k$ has the same sign as $\phi_k$
