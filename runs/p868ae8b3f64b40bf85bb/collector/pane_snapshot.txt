## Proof

**Claim:** $Y^2 \cdot 1_{|Y| \leq n} \to Y^2$ almost surely as $n \to \infty$.

**Key fact.** A random variable is, by definition, finite almost surely. That is
,
$$\mathbb{P}(|Y| < \infty) = 1.$$

**Pointwise argument.** Fix any $\omega$ in the event $\{|Y| < \infty\}$, which
has probability $1$. Then $Y(\omega)$ is a fixed finite real number, so $|Y(\ome
ga)| =: M(\omega) < \infty$. For every integer $n \geq M(\omega)$, we have $|Y(\
omega)| \leq n$, hence
$$1_{\{|Y| \leq n\}}(\omega) = 1,$$
and therefore
$$Y^2(\omega) \cdot 1_{\{|Y| \leq n\}}(\omega) = Y^2(\omega).$$

Thus for every $\omega \in \{|Y| < \infty\}$, the sequence $Y^2(\omega) \cdot 1_
{\{|Y| \leq n\}}(\omega)$ is eventually constant (equal to $Y^2(\omega)$ for all
 $n \geq \lceil M(\omega) \rceil$), and in particular converges to $Y^2(\omega)$
.

Since $\mathbb{P}(|Y| < \infty) = 1$, this convergence holds almost surely:
$$Y^2 \cdot 1_{\{|Y| \leq n\}} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} Y^2.$$

**On the role of the hypothesis $\mathbb{E}(Y^2) = \infty$.** This condition pla
ys no role in the almost-sure convergence. It is relevant only to convergence in
 mean: by the Monotone Convergence Theorem,
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[Y^2 \cdot 1_{\{|Y| \leq n\}}\right] \uparrow \mathbb{E}[Y^2]
 = \infty,$$
so the sequence does **not** converge to $Y^2$ in $L^1$. The truncation $Y^2 \cd
ot 1_{\{|Y| \leq n\}}$ is an increasing sequence bounded above by $Y^2$, converg
ing pointwise to $Y^2$ everywhere on $\{|Y|<\infty\}$, but its expectation diver
ges — exactly the situation where a.s. convergence holds without $L^1$ convergen
ce.

**Conclusion.** Yes, $Y^2 \cdot 1_{\{|Y| \leq n\}}$ converges almost surely to $
Y^2$ as $n \to \infty$, regardless of whether $\mathbb{E}(Y^2)$ is finite or inf
inite.
