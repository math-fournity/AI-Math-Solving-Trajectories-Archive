# The Trefoil Knot is Isotopic to the Unknot in ℝ⁴

## Setup

Let $K \subset \mathbb{R}^3 \subset \mathbb{R}^4$ be the trefoil knot, realized
as a smooth (tame) embedding $f: S^1 \to \mathbb{R}^3$. We write coordinates on
$\mathbb{R}^4$ as $(x,y,z,w)$, so $\mathbb{R}^3 = \{w=0\}$.

We show $K$ is ambient isotopic to the standard unknot $U \subset \mathbb{R}^3$
via an isotopy supported in $\mathbb{R}^4$.

## Key Lemma: Crossing changes are realizable in ℝ⁴

**Lemma.** Let $D$ be a knot diagram of $K$ with finitely many crossings. For an
y single crossing $c$ of $D$, there exists an ambient isotopy $H_t: \mathbb{R}^4
 \to \mathbb{R}^4$, $t \in [0,1]$, fixed outside a small neighborhood of $c$, su
ch that $H_1(K)$ is a knot whose diagram is $D$ with the crossing $c$ switched (
an over-crossing becomes an under-crossing or vice versa).

*Proof of Lemma.* Near the crossing $c$, the diagram shows two arcs of $K$ proje
cted onto the $(x,y)$-plane, crossing at a point, with one arc passing "over" th
e other in the $z$-direction. Concretely, in a small ball $B$ around the crossin
g, the over-strand has $z > 0$ and the under-strand has $z < 0$ (after a small p
erturbation), both with $w = 0$.

Define a smooth bump function $\varphi: \mathbb{R}^4 \to [0,1]$ supported in $B$
 with $\varphi = 1$ near the crossing point. Define the isotopy by translating t
he over-strand in the $w$-direction:

$$H_t(x,y,z,w) = (x,\, y,\, z,\, w + t\,\varphi(x,y,z,w)\cdot h),$$

where $h > 0$ is a small constant. This is a smooth ambient isotopy (a diffeomor
phism for each $t$, since it is a shear in $w$ depending smoothly on position, w
ith inverse given by $-t\varphi h$). It is the identity outside $B$.

Under $H_1$, the over-strand near $c$ is displaced to $w = h > 0$ while the unde
r-strand (where $\varphi$ may also be nonzero) is displaced by a smaller amount;
 choosing $h$ and the support appropriately, the over-strand moves to a strictly
 larger $w$-value than the under-strand throughout the crossing region. Projecti
ng back to $\mathbb{R}^3$ (i.e., to $w=0$) after the isotopy, the roles of over
and under are effectively interchanged: the strand that was on top in $z$ is now
 "above" in $w$, and the crossing is switched in the diagram.

More directly: after lifting the over-strand to $w = h$, we can slide it in the
$(x,y)$-plane past the under-strand (they no longer intersect in $\mathbb{R}^4$
because they have different $w$-coordinates), and then lower it back to $w=0$ on
 the other side. The composite is an ambient isotopy in $\mathbb{R}^4$ that swit
ches the crossing. $\square$

## Applying to the trefoil

The trefoil has a standard diagram with $3$ crossings. Its **unknotting number i
s $1$**: changing a single appropriate crossing converts the trefoil diagram int
o a diagram of the unknot. (This is verified, for instance, by the standard 3-cr
ossing diagram where switching one crossing produces a diagram that admits a Rei
demeister-I simplification to a circle.)

By the Lemma, this single crossing change is realized by an ambient isotopy of $
\mathbb{R}^4$. Therefore:

$$K \;\underset{\mathbb{R}^4}{\sim}\; K' \;\underset{\mathbb{R}^3}{\sim}\; U,$$

where $K'$ is the knot obtained by switching the crossing (which is the unknot i
n $\mathbb{R}^3$) and $\sim$ denotes ambient isotopy. Composing, $K$ is ambient
isotopic to $U$ in $\mathbb{R}^4$.

## General remark (why ℝ⁴ is special)

The argument above does not use any special property of the trefoil beyond havin
g a finite diagram. Indeed, **every** tame knot in $\mathbb{R}^3$ becomes unknot
ted in $\mathbb{R}^4$, because every knot has a finite diagram and finite unknot
ting number, and each crossing change is realizable using the extra $w$-dimensio
n as in the Lemma. The obstruction to unknotting in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is precisely
that crossing changes are *not* achievable by ambient isotopy there—strands cann
ot pass through each other without the extra dimension. In $\mathbb{R}^4$ this o
bstruction vanishes.

## Conclusion

The trefoil knot, embedded in $\mathbb{R}^3 \subset \mathbb{R}^4$, is ambient is
otopic to the unknot in $\mathbb{R}^4$. The extra fourth coordinate provides roo
m to lift a strand over a crossing, switch it, and lower it back, and a single s
uch crossing change (the trefoil's unknotting number is $1$) suffices to reduce
the trefoil to the unknot.
