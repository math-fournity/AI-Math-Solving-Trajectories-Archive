## Solution

**Problem.** Given $\omega \ast u - u'' = f$ with $\omega \in L^1(\mathbb{R})$,
$\omega(x)>0$, $\omega$ even, and for $x>0$: $\omega\in C^2[0,\infty)$, $\omega'
(x)<0$, $\omega''(x)>0$. Find the solution operator as a convolution operator.

---

### Step 1. Preliminaries: $\omega$ vanishes at infinity

Since $\omega'(x)<0$ for $x>0$ and $\omega(x)>0$, the function $\omega$ is stric
tly decreasing on $(0,\infty)$ and bounded below by $0$. Hence $\omega(x)\to L\g
e 0$ as $x\to+\infty$. But $\omega\in L^1(\mathbb{R})$ and $\omega>0$, so $L=0$.
 By evenness, $\omega(x)\to 0$ as $|x|\to\infty$.

### Step 2. Pólya's criterion: $\widehat{\omega}(\xi)\ge 0$

We use the Fourier transform convention $\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}} f(x)
\,e^{-ix\xi}\,dx$.

**Pólya's criterion.** *If $h$ is an even, continuous function with $h(x)\to 0$
as $|x|\to\infty$ and $h$ is convex on $(0,\infty)$, then $\widehat{h}(\xi)\ge 0
$ for all $\xi$.*

Our $\omega$ satisfies all hypotheses: it is even, continuous (in fact $C^2$ on
$[0,\infty)$), $\omega(x)\to 0$ as $|x|\to\infty$ (Step 1), and $\omega''(x)>0$
for $x>0$ means $\omega$ is convex on $(0,\infty)$. Therefore

$$\widehat{\omega}(\xi)\ge 0 \quad\text{for all } \xi\in\mathbb{R}.$$

Moreover $\widehat{\omega}(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega(x)\,dx = \|\omega\|_{L^1}>
0$.

### Step 3. The symbol $\widehat{\omega}(\xi)+\xi^2$ is strictly positive

From Step 2, $\widehat{\omega}(\xi)\ge 0$, so

$$\widehat{\omega}(\xi)+\xi^2 \ge \xi^2 \ge 0.$$

At $\xi=0$: $\widehat{\omega}(0)+0=\|\omega\|_{L^1}>0$. For $\xi\ne 0$: $\wideha
t{\omega}(\xi)+\xi^2\ge \xi^2>0$. Hence

$$\widehat{\omega}(\xi)+\xi^2 > 0 \quad\text{for all } \xi\in\mathbb{R}.$$

### Step 4. Definition of the Green's function $g$

Define

$$\widehat{g}(\xi) := \frac{1}{\widehat{\omega}(\xi)+\xi^2}, \qquad \xi\in\mathb
b{R}.$$

This is well-defined and continuous by Step 3. We establish its integrability:

- **Upper bound near $0$:** $\widehat{g}(\xi)\le \frac{1}{\widehat{\omega}(0)} =
 \frac{1}{\|\omega\|_{L^1}}$ for all $\xi$.
- **Decay at infinity:** For $|\xi|\ge 1$, $\widehat{g}(\xi)\le \frac{1}{\xi^2}$
.

Therefore $\widehat{g}\in L^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ and $\widehat{g}(\xi)=O(\xi^{-2}
)$ as $|\xi|\to\infty$, which gives $\widehat{g}\in L^1(\mathbb{R})\cap L^2(\mat
hbb{R})$.

Since $\widehat{g}\in L^1$, the inverse Fourier transform yields a bounded conti
nuous function vanishing at infinity:

$$g(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}} \frac{e^{ix\xi}}{\widehat{\omega}(\xi)+\x
i^2}\,d\xi \;\in\; C_0(\mathbb{R})\cap L^\infty(\mathbb{R})\cap L^2(\mathbb{R}).
$$

### Step 5. The solution operator is $u = g\ast f$

**Claim.** For $f\in L^1(\mathbb{R})\cap L^2(\mathbb{R})$ (or more generally for
 $f$ such that $g\ast f$ is defined), the function

$$u := g\ast f$$

solves $\omega\ast u - u'' = f$ in the distributional sense.

**Proof of claim.** Since $g\in L^2$ and $f\in L^2$, the convolution $u=g\ast f$
 is well-defined (by Cauchy–Schwarz, $g\ast f\in C_0\cap L^\infty$; in fact $g\a
st f\in C_0$ by Young's inequality since $g\in L^2, f\in L^2$ gives $u\in L^\inf
ty$, and more precisely $g\in L^1$ would give $u\in C_0$; here we use the Fourie
r-side argument).

Taking the Fourier transform (in the distributional sense):

$$\widehat{u}(\xi) = \widehat{g}(\xi)\,\widehat{f}(\xi) = \frac{\widehat{f}(\xi)
}{\widehat{\omega}(\xi)+\xi^2}.$$

Now compute the Fourier transform of the left-hand side of the equation:

$$\widehat{\omega\ast u - u''}(\xi) = \widehat{\omega}(\xi)\,\widehat{u}(\xi) -
\widehat{u''}(\xi) = \widehat{\omega}(\xi)\,\widehat{u}(\xi) + \xi^2\,\widehat{u
}(\xi) = \bigl(\widehat{\omega}(\xi)+\xi^2\bigr)\,\widehat{u}(\xi).$$

Substituting $\widehat{u}=\widehat{g}\,\widehat{f}=\frac{\widehat{f}}{\widehat{\
omega}+\xi^2}$:

$$\widehat{\omega\ast u - u''}(\xi) = \bigl(\widehat{\omega}(\xi)+\xi^2\bigr)\cd
ot\frac{\widehat{f}(\xi)}{\widehat{\omega}(\xi)+\xi^2} = \widehat{f}(\xi).$$

By injectivity of the Fourier transform on tempered distributions, $\omega\ast u
 - u'' = f$. $\quad\blacksquare$

### Step 6. Summary

The solution operator of the equation $\omega\ast u - u'' = f$ is the **convolut
ion operator**

$$\boxed{u = g\ast f, \qquad g(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{e^{ix\xi}
}{\widehat{\omega}(\xi)+\xi^2}\,d\xi,}$$

i.e., $g$ is the inverse Fourier transform of $\bigl(\widehat{\omega}(\xi)+\xi^2
\bigr)^{-1}$.

The hypotheses on $\omega$ (positivity, evenness, monotone decrease, and convexi
ty on $(0,\infty)$) serve two purposes:

1. **Pólya's criterion** guarantees $\widehat{\omega}(\xi)\ge 0$, ensuring the s
ymbol $\widehat{\omega}(\xi)+\xi^2$ is **strictly positive** and hence invertibl
e.
2. The resulting Green's function $g$ satisfies $g\in C_0(\mathbb{R})\cap L^\inf
ty(\mathbb{R})\cap L^2(\mathbb{R})$, so the convolution $g\ast f$ is well-define
d for $f\in L^1\cap L^2$ and yields the unique distributional solution.
