## Proof

We use the **Regular Value Theorem** (preimage theorem): if $w$ is a regular val
ue of a smooth map $g:\mathbb{R}^3\to\mathbb{R}$, then $g^{-1}(w)$ is a smooth e
mbedded submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^3$ of codimension $1$ (hence dimension $2$).
A value $w$ is a *critical value* if there exists a point $p\in g^{-1}(w)$ with
$dg_p = 0$ (the zero linear map, since the target is $\mathbb{R}$).

**Step 1: Compute the critical points.**

The derivative of $g(x,y,z)=x^3-2y^2+z^4$ is
$$dg_{(x,y,z)} = \bigl(3x^2,\;-4y,\;4z^3\bigr).$$
This vanishes identically iff
$$3x^2=0,\quad -4y=0,\quad 4z^3=0 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; x=0,\;y=0,\;z=0.$$
So the **unique critical point** is the origin $p_0=(0,0,0)$.

**Step 2: Identify the critical value.**

$$g(0,0,0)=0-0+0=0.$$
Hence $w=0$ is the **only critical value**. Every $w\neq 0$ is a regular value.

**Step 3: Regular values give submanifolds.**

For every $w\neq 0$, by the Regular Value Theorem, $g^{-1}(w)=\{(x,y,z):x^3-2y^2
+z^4=w\}$ is a smooth $2$-dimensional embedded submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^3$.

**Step 4: The critical value $w=0$ does NOT yield a submanifold.**

We show $S:=g^{-1}(0)=\{(x,y,z):x^3=2y^2-z^4\}$ is not a smooth submanifold at t
he origin.

Since the real cube root is defined for every real number, we may solve for $x$
uniquely:
$$S = \bigl\{\bigl(h(y,z),\,y,\,z\bigr): (y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^2\bigr\},\qquad h(y,
z)=\bigl(2y^2-z^4\bigr)^{1/3}.$$
Thus $S$ is the **global graph** of $h$ over the $(y,z)$-plane, and the projecti
on $\pi:S\to\mathbb{R}^2$, $(x,y,z)\mapsto(y,z)$, is a homeomorphism.

We check whether $S$ could be a local graph over any other coordinate plane near
 the origin:

- Over $(x,z)$: solving for $y$ gives $y=\pm\sqrt{(x^3+z^4)/2}$, which is real o
nly when $x^3+z^4\ge 0$. This is **not** a full neighborhood of $(0,0)$ (e.g. $x
<0,z=0$ is excluded), so $S$ is not a local graph over $(x,z)$.
- Over $(x,y)$: solving for $z$ gives $z=\bigl(2y^2-x^3\bigr)^{1/4}$, real only
when $2y^2-x^3\ge 0$, again **not** a full neighborhood of $(0,0)$.

Hence the **only** coordinate plane over which $S$ is a local graph near the ori
gin is the $(y,z)$-plane. Consequently, if $S$ were a smooth $2$-submanifold nea
r the origin, it would have to be locally the graph $x=h(y,z)$ with $h$ **smooth
** near $(0,0)$.

But $h$ is not even differentiable at the origin: restricting to $z=0$,
$$h(y,0)=\bigl(2y^2\bigr)^{1/3}=2^{1/3}\,|y|^{2/3},$$
and
$$\frac{d}{dy}\Big|_{y=0}\,2^{1/3}|y|^{2/3}=2^{1/3}\cdot\tfrac{2}{3}\,|y|^{-1/3}
\operatorname{sgn}(y)\;\xrightarrow[y\to 0]{}\;\pm\infty,$$
which diverges. So $h\notin C^1$ at $(0,0)$, contradicting the requirement that
$h$ be smooth.

Therefore $g^{-1}(0)$ is **not** a smooth submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^3$.

**Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 3 and 4:

$$\boxed{\,g^{-1}(w)\text{ is a (smooth) submanifold of }\mathbb{R}^3 \iff w\neq
 0\,}.$$
