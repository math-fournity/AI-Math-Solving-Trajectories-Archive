## Solution

**Setup.** Place the isosceles triangle with $A=(0,h)$, $B=(-c,0)$, $C=(c,0)$, s
o $AB=AC=b=\sqrt{c^2+h^2}$ and $BC=2c$. The incenter is $O=(0, r_{\text{in}})$ w
here $r_{\text{in}}=\frac{ch}{c+b}$.

**Feet of perpendiculars.** Computing projections of $O$ onto each side:
- $K=(0,0)$ on $BC$,
- $L=\!\left(\frac{ch^2}{(c+b)b},\,\frac{ch}{b}\right)$ on $CA$,
- $M=\!\left(-\frac{ch^2}{(c+b)b},\,\frac{ch}{b}\right)$ on $AB$ (by symmetry).

**Finding $N$.** Parametrize line $KM$: $(-dt,et)$ with $d=\frac{ch^2}{(c+b)b}$,
 $e=\frac{ch}{b}$. Parametrize line $OL$: $(ds,\,r_{\text{in}}+(e-r_{\text{in}})
s)$. Setting equal gives $s=-t$ and $t=\frac{r_{\text{in}}}{2e-r_{\text{in}}}=\f
rac{b}{2c+b}$. Hence
$$N=\!\left(-\frac{ch^2}{(c+b)(2c+b)},\,\frac{ch}{2c+b}\right).$$

**Direction of $BQ$.** The vector $N-B$ simplifies (using $h^2=b^2-c^2$):
$$N_x+c = \frac{3c^2}{2c+b},\qquad N_y=\frac{ch}{2c+b},$$
so $BQ$ has direction $(3c,\,h)$.

**Finding $Q$.** Line $BQ$: $(-c+3ct,\,ht)$. Intersecting with $CA: hx+cy=ch$:
$$h(-c+3ct)+c(ht)=ch \;\Longrightarrow\; -ch+4cht=ch \;\Longrightarrow\; t=\tfra
c12.$$
So $Q=(c/2,\,h/2)$.

**Finding $P$ and distances.** Project $A-B=(c,h)$ onto direction $(3c,h)$:
$$\text{proj coeff}=\frac{3c^2+h^2}{9c^2+h^2}=\frac{2c^2+b^2}{8c^2+b^2}.$$
With $|(3c,h)|=\sqrt{8c^2+b^2}$:
$$BP=\frac{2c^2+b^2}{\sqrt{8c^2+b^2}},\qquad AP=\frac{2ch}{\sqrt{8c^2+b^2}},\qqu
ad PQ=\frac{|b^2-4c^2|}{2\sqrt{8c^2+b^2}}.$$

**Applying the condition $BP=AP+2\,PQ$.** Multiplying through by $\sqrt{8c^2+b^2
}$:
$$2c^2+b^2 = 2ch + |b^2-4c^2|.$$

**Substituting $r=b/(2c)$.** Then $b=2cr$, $h=c\sqrt{4r^2-1}$ (requiring $r>1/2$
). Dividing by $2c^2$:
$$1+2r^2 = \sqrt{4r^2-1}+2|r^2-1|.$$

**Case 1: $r\ge 1$.** $|r^2-1|=r^2-1$:
$$1+2r^2=\sqrt{4r^2-1}+2r^2-2 \;\Longrightarrow\; 3=\sqrt{4r^2-1} \;\Longrightar
row\; 9=4r^2-1 \;\Longrightarrow\; r^2=\frac52.$$
Valid since $\sqrt{5/2}\ge 1$. ✓

**Case 2: $1/2<r<1$.** $|r^2-1|=1-r^2$:
$$1+2r^2=\sqrt{4r^2-1}+2-2r^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; 4r^2-1=\sqrt{4r^2-1}.$$
Setting $u=\sqrt{4r^2-1}\ge 0$: $u^2=u$, so $u=0$ (gives $r=1/2$, degenerate) or
 $u=1$, giving $4r^2-1=1$, i.e. $r^2=\frac12$. Valid since $1/\sqrt2\in(1/2,1)$.
 ✓

**Result.** The two values are $r_1^2=\frac52$ and $r_2^2=\frac12$, therefore
$$r_1^2+r_2^2=\frac52+\frac12=\boxed{3}.$$
