## Proof

Let $g(u) = \dfrac{1-e^{-u^4}}{u^2}$ for $u>0$, extended by $g(0)=0$.

**Step 1: $g$ is continuous on $[0,\infty)$ with $g(0)=0$ and $g(u)\to 0$ as $u\
to\infty$.**

As $u\to 0^+$: $1-e^{-u^4}=u^4-\tfrac{u^8}{2}+O(u^{12})$, so $g(u)=u^2-\tfrac{u^
6}{2}+O(u^{10})\to 0$. Setting $g(0)=0$ makes $g$ continuous.

As $u\to\infty$: $g(u)\to u^{-2}\to 0$.

**Step 2: First integration by parts.**

Set $I(x)=\int_0^\infty g(u)\sin(xu)\,du$. With $dv=\sin(xu)\,du$, $v=-\cos(xu)/
x$:

$$I(x)=\Bigl[-\tfrac{g(u)\cos(xu)}{x}\Bigr]_0^\infty+\frac{1}{x}\int_0^\infty g'
(u)\cos(xu)\,du.$$

The boundary term vanishes since $g(0)=0$ and $g(u)\to 0$. Thus

$$I(x)=\frac{1}{x}\int_0^\infty g'(u)\cos(xu)\,du.$$

**Step 3: $g'(0)=0$ and $g'(u)\to 0$ as $u\to\infty$.**

For $u>0$: $g'(u)=4u\,e^{-u^4}-\dfrac{2(1-e^{-u^4})}{u^3}$.

As $u\to 0^+$: $4u\,e^{-u^4}\to 0$ and $\frac{2(1-e^{-u^4})}{u^3}=\frac{2(u^4+O(
u^8))}{u^3}=2u+O(u^5)\to 0$, so $g'(0)=0$.

As $u\to\infty$: both terms $\to 0$.

**Step 4: Second integration by parts.**

With $dw=\cos(xu)\,du$, $w=\sin(xu)/x$:

$$I(x)=\frac{1}{x}\Bigl[\tfrac{g'(u)\sin(xu)}{x}\Bigr]_0^\infty-\frac{1}{x^2}\in
t_0^\infty g''(u)\sin(xu)\,du.$$

The boundary term vanishes since $g'(0)=0$ and $g'(u)\to 0$. Thus

$$I(x)=-\frac{1}{x^2}\int_0^\infty g''(u)\sin(xu)\,du.$$

**Step 5: $g''\in L^1([0,\infty))$.**

For $u>0$:

$$g''(u)=\bigl(4e^{-u^4}-16u^4 e^{-u^4}\bigr)-\Bigl(8e^{-u^4}-\frac{6(1-e^{-u^4}
)}{u^4}\Bigr)=-4e^{-u^4}-16u^4 e^{-u^4}+\frac{6(1-e^{-u^4})}{u^4}.$$

- As $u\to 0^+$: $-4-0+\frac{6u^4+O(u^8)}{u^4}\to -4+6=2$, so $g''$ extends cont
inuously with $g''(0)=2$.
- As $u\to\infty$: $-4e^{-u^4}\to 0$, $-16u^4 e^{-u^4}\to 0$, $\frac{6(1-e^{-u^4
})}{u^4}\sim\frac{6}{u^4}\to 0$.

Hence $g''$ is continuous on $[0,\infty)$ and decays like $O(u^{-4})$, so $g''\i
n L^1([0,\infty))$.

**Step 6: Bound and conclude.**

$$|I(x)|=\frac{1}{x^2}\left|\int_0^\infty g''(u)\sin(xu)\,du\right|\le\frac{1}{x
^2}\int_0^\infty|g''(u)|\,du=\frac{\|g''\|_1}{x^2}\xrightarrow{x\to\infty}0.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{\lim_{x\to\infty}\int_0^{\infty}\frac{1-e^{-u^4}}{u^2}\sin(xu)\,du=0.}$
$

This proof uses only integration by parts and the triangle inequality — no Riema
nn-Lebesgue lemma.
