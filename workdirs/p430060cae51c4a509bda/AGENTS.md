# Problem

Consider pairs of four-digit positive integers $(M, N)$ where both $M$ and $N$ are perfect squares. These pairs must satisfy the following properties:
1. The decimal representations of $M$ and $N$ have equal digits in exactly two positions.
2. In the remaining two positions, the digit in $M$ is exactly one greater than the corresponding digit in $N$.
For example, $(3600, 2500)$ is one such pair because the last two digits are the same ($0, 0$) and the first two digits of $M$ ($3, 6$) are each one greater than the first two digits of $N$ ($2, 5$).
Find all such pairs $(M, N)$ and calculate the sum of all $M$ and $N$ values across all distinct pairs.
