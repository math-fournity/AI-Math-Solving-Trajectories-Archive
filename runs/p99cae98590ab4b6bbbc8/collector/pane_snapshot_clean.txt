## Solution

We solve the Dirichlet problem for Laplace's equation in the first quadrant $\{(
x,y): x>0,\, y>0\}$:

$$u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0,\qquad u(x,0)=0,\qquad u(0,y)=f(y)=\begin{cases}1,&0<y<1,\\0,&
y>1.\end{cases}$$

### Step 1: Fourier sine transform in $y$

Since $u(x,0)=0$, the **Fourier sine transform in $y$** is the natural tool. Def
ine

$$\widehat{u}(x,\eta)=\int_0^\infty u(x,y)\sin(\eta y)\,dy,\qquad \eta>0.$$

Integrating by parts (using $u(x,0)=0$ and decay as $y\to\infty$), the Laplace e
quation transforms to

$$\frac{\partial^2 \widehat{u}}{\partial x^2}-\eta^2\,\widehat{u}=0.$$

The general solution is $\widehat{u}(x,\eta)=C(\eta)\,e^{-\eta x}+D(\eta)\,e^{\e
ta x}$. Boundedness as $x\to\infty$ forces $D(\eta)=0$, so

$$\widehat{u}(x,\eta)=C(\eta)\,e^{-\eta x}.$$

### Step 2: Apply the boundary condition at $x=0$

Setting $x=0$ gives $\widehat{u}(0,\eta)=C(\eta)$. But $\widehat{u}(0,\eta)=\int
_0^\infty f(y)\sin(\eta y)\,dy$, hence

$$C(\eta)=\int_0^1\sin(\eta y)\,dy=\frac{1-\cos\eta}{\eta}.$$

### Step 3: Invert the transform

By the inversion formula for the sine transform,

$$u(x,y)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\infty \frac{1-\cos\eta}{\eta}\,e^{-\eta x}\sin(\et
a y)\,d\eta.$$

### Step 4: Evaluate the integral in closed form

Use the identity $\dfrac{1-\cos\eta}{\eta}=\displaystyle\int_0^1\sin(\eta t)\,dt
$ to write

$$u(x,y)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^1\!\int_0^\infty e^{-\eta x}\sin(\eta t)\sin(\eta y
)\,d\eta\,dt.$$

Using $\sin(\eta t)\sin(\eta y)=\tfrac12[\cos\eta(t-y)-\cos\eta(t+y)]$ and the s
tandard integral $\int_0^\infty e^{-a\eta}\cos(b\eta)\,d\eta=\frac{a}{a^2+b^2}$,

$$\int_0^\infty e^{-\eta x}\sin(\eta t)\sin(\eta y)\,d\eta=\frac{1}{2}\left[\fra
c{x}{x^2+(t-y)^2}-\frac{x}{x^2+(t+y)^2}\right].$$

Therefore

$$u(x,y)=\frac{x}{\pi}\int_0^1\left[\frac{1}{x^2+(t-y)^2}-\frac{1}{x^2+(t+y)^2}\
right]dt.$$

Integrating via $\displaystyle\int\frac{dt}{x^2+(t\mp y)^2}=\frac{1}{x}\arctan\!
\frac{t\mp y}{x}$:

$$u(x,y)=\frac{1}{\pi}\left[\arctan\frac{t-y}{x}-\arctan\frac{t+y}{x}\right]_{t=
0}^{t=1}.$$

Since $\arctan(-y/x)=-\arctan(y/x)$, the lower limit contributes $+\,2\arctan(y/
x)$, giving

$$\boxed{\,u(x,y)=\frac{1}{\pi}\left[\arctan\frac{1-y}{x}-\arctan\frac{1+y}{x}+2
\arctan\frac{y}{x}\right]\,},\qquad x>0,\;y>0.$$

### Step 5: Verification of boundary conditions

**Harmonicity.** Each integrand $e^{-\eta x}\sin(\eta y)$ satisfies Laplace's eq
uation, and the integral converges absolutely for $x>0$; differentiating under t
he integral is justified, so $u$ is harmonic in the open first quadrant.

**Boundary $y=0$:** $u(x,0)=\frac{1}{\pi}\bigl[\arctan\frac{1}{x}-\arctan\frac{1
}{x}+0\bigr]=0.$ ✓

**Boundary $x=0^+$:** As $x\to0^+$, using $\arctan(a/x)\to\frac{\pi}{2}\operator
name{sgn}(a)$:

- For $0<y<1$: $\arctan\frac{1-y}{x}\to\frac\pi2$, $\;\arctan\frac{1+y}{x}\to\fr
ac\pi2$, $\;2\arctan\frac{y}{x}\to\pi$, so $u\to\frac{1}{\pi}(\frac\pi2-\frac\pi
2+\pi)=1.$ ✓
- For $y>1$: $\arctan\frac{1-y}{x}\to-\frac\pi2$, $\;\arctan\frac{1+y}{x}\to\fra
c\pi2$, $\;2\arctan\frac{y}{x}\to\pi$, so $u\to\frac{1}{\pi}(-\frac\pi2-\frac\pi
2+\pi)=0.$ ✓

Both boundary conditions are recovered, confirming the solution.
