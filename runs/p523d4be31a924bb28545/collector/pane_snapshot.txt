## Proof

**Setup.** The region is
$$V = \{(x,y,z) : 0 \le x \le 1,\; x^2 \le y \le \sqrt{x},\; 0 \le z \le 1\}.$$

The integrand $\frac{\sin x - \sin y}{xy+1}$ is independent of $z$, so the $z$-i
ntegral contributes a factor of $(1-0)=1$:
$$\iiint_V \frac{\sin x - \sin y}{xy+1}\,dx\,dy\,dz = \iint_D \frac{\sin x - \si
n y}{xy+1}\,dA,$$
where $D = \{(x,y): 0\le x\le 1,\; x^2 \le y \le \sqrt{x}\}$ is the projection o
nto the $xy$-plane.

**Symmetry of the region.** The curves bounding $D$ are $y = x^2$ and $y = \sqrt
{x}$, i.e., $x = y^2$. Thus $D$ is the region enclosed between $y=x^2$ and $x=y^
2$ in the unit square. This region is invariant under the swap $(x,y)\mapsto(y,x
)$: if $(x,y)\in D$, then $0\le y\le 1$ and $y^2 \le x \le \sqrt{y}$, which is e
xactly $D$ described with $y$ as the outer variable. Hence $D$ is symmetric unde
r $x\leftrightarrow y$.

**Antisymmetry of the integrand.** Let $f(x,y) = \frac{\sin x - \sin y}{xy+1}$.
Under the swap,
$$f(y,x) = \frac{\sin y - \sin x}{yx+1} = -\frac{\sin x - \sin y}{xy+1} = -f(x,y
).$$
So $f$ is antisymmetric under $x\leftrightarrow y$.

**Applying the symmetry.** By the change of variables $(x,y)\mapsto(y,x)$ (which
 has Jacobian $-1$, i.e., $|J|=1$, and maps $D$ onto itself):
$$\iint_D f(x,y)\,dA = \iint_D f(y,x)\,dA = \iint_D \bigl(-f(x,y)\bigr)\,dA = -\
iint_D f(x,y)\,dA.$$

Therefore $2\iint_D f(x,y)\,dA = 0$, giving
$$\iint_D \frac{\sin x - \sin y}{xy+1}\,dA = 0.$$

Multiplying by the $z$-factor of $1$:
$$\iiint_V \frac{\sin x - \sin y}{xy+1}\,dx\,dy\,dz = 0.$$
