## Proof

We prove both directions. Throughout, $W^{1,1}([a,b])$ denotes the set of $L^1$
functions on $[a,b]$ whose weak derivative (in the distributional sense) also be
longs to $L^1([a,b])$.

### Direction 1: Absolutely continuous $\Rightarrow$ $W^{1,1}$

Assume $f$ is absolutely continuous (AC) on $[a,b]$. A classical theorem of Lebe
sgue states that an AC function is differentiable almost everywhere, its derivat
ive $f'$ belongs to $L^1([a,b])$, and the Newton–Leibniz formula holds:
$$f(x)-f(a)=\int_a^x f'(t)\,dt,\qquad x\in[a,b].$$

It remains to verify that $f'$ is the **weak** derivative of $f$. Let $\varphi\i
n C_c^\infty(a,b)$. Using the integration-by-parts formula valid for AC function
s (which is justified by approximating $\varphi$ and using the Newton–Leibniz re
presentation, or directly via the Stieltjes integral of an AC integrator),
$$\int_a^b f(x)\varphi'(x)\,dx = \big[f(x)\varphi(x)\big]_a^b - \int_a^b f'(x)\v
arphi(x)\,dx = -\int_a^b f'(x)\varphi(x)\,dx,$$
since $\varphi$ vanishes near $a$ and $b$. This is precisely the definition of $
f'$ being the weak derivative of $f$. Hence $f\in W^{1,1}([a,b])$.

### Direction 2: $W^{1,1}$ $\Rightarrow$ absolutely continuous

Assume $f\in W^{1,1}([a,b])$, and let $g\in L^1([a,b])$ be its weak derivative,
i.e.
$$\int_a^b f\,\varphi' = -\int_a^b g\,\varphi,\qquad \forall\,\varphi\in C_c^\in
fty(a,b).$$

Define
$$F(x)=\int_a^x g(t)\,dt,\qquad x\in[a,b].$$
Since $g\in L^1$, $F$ is absolutely continuous on $[a,b]$, and $F'=g$ a.e. By Di
rection 1, $g$ is the weak derivative of $F$, so
$$\int_a^b F\,\varphi' = -\int_a^b g\,\varphi,\qquad \forall\,\varphi\in C_c^\in
fty(a,b).$$

Subtracting the two weak-derivative identities gives
$$\int_a^b (f-F)\,\varphi' = 0,\qquad \forall\,\varphi\in C_c^\infty(a,b).$$

A standard lemma (Du Bois–Reymond / Lagrange identity) states: if $h\in L^1_{\te
xt{loc}}(a,b)$ satisfies $\int h\,\varphi'=0$ for all $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(a,b
)$, then $h$ is constant a.e. on $(a,b)$. (Proof sketch: let $\rho_\varepsilon$
be a standard mollifier and set $\psi_\varepsilon = \rho_\varepsilon * h$; then
$\psi_\varepsilon'=0$ on compact subintervals for small $\varepsilon$, so $\psi_
\varepsilon$ is constant, and $\psi_\varepsilon\to h$ in $L^1_{\text{loc}}$, for
cing $h$ to be a.e. constant.)

Thus $f-F = C$ a.e. on $[a,b]$ for some constant $C$. Since $F$ is absolutely co
ntinuous and constants are absolutely continuous, the representative
$$\tilde f(x) := F(x) + C = C + \int_a^x g(t)\,dt$$
is absolutely continuous on $[a,b]$ and equals $f$ a.e. In the Sobolev conventio
n, $f$ is identified with this AC representative, so $f$ is absolutely continuou
s.

### Conclusion

Both implications hold, therefore a function $f$ on $[a,b]$ belongs to $W^{1,1}(
[a,b])$ **if and only if** $f$ is absolutely continuous on $[a,b]$.
