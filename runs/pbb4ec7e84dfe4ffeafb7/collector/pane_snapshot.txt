## Proof

**Claim.** For every holomorphic vector bundle $E$ on $X=\mathbb{C}$, the first
sheaf cohomology group vanishes: $H^1(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{O}(E))=0$.

**Step 1. $\mathbb{C}$ is a Stein manifold.**

A complex manifold is Stein if it is holomorphically convex, holomorphically sep
arable, and admits holomorphic charts. Equivalently, by the theorem of Stein dom
ains, every open Riemann surface (non-compact connected one-dimensional complex
manifold) is Stein. Since $\mathbb{C}$ is a non-compact connected Riemann surfac
e, it is Stein. (Concretely: $\mathbb{C}$ is a domain of holomorphy in $\mathbb{
C}^1$, hence Stein.)

**Step 2. The sheaf of a holomorphic vector bundle is coherent.**

A holomorphic vector bundle $E\to\mathbb{C}$ of rank $r$ determines the sheaf $\
mathcal{O}(E)$ of holomorphic sections. Locally, after trivialization, $\mathcal
{O}(E)\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\oplus r}$, so $\mathcal{O}(E)$ is a locall
y free $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}$-module of finite rank. Locally free sheaves of
 finite rank over the structure sheaf are coherent analytic sheaves. Hence $\mat
hcal{O}(E)$ is coherent.

**Step 3. Apply Cartan's Theorem B.**

Cartan's Theorem B states: *If $X$ is a Stein manifold and $\mathcal{F}$ is a co
herent analytic sheaf on $X$, then $H^q(X,\mathcal{F})=0$ for all $q\geq 1$.*

By Step 1, $X=\mathbb{C}$ is Stein. By Step 2, $\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{O}(E)$ is c
oherent. Therefore, for every $q\geq 1$,
$$H^q(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{O}(E))=0.$$
In particular,
$$\boxed{H^1(\mathbb{C},E)=0.}$$

**Conclusion.** Yes, $H^1(X,E)=0$ for every holomorphic vector bundle $E$ on $X=
\mathbb{C}$, because $\mathbb{C}$ is a Stein manifold and the sheaf of holomorph
ic sections of $E$ is coherent, so Cartan's Theorem B forces all higher cohomolo
gy to vanish.
