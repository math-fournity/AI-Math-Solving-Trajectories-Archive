Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $R$ must be finitely genera
ted as an $\mathbb{R}$-algebra, given that $A = R \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{C
}$ is finitely generated as a $\mathbb{C}$-algebra.

**Answer: Yes, $R$ is necessarily a finitely generated $\mathbb{R}$-algebra.**

**Proof.**

We apply the **Artin–Tate lemma**, which states:

> Let $k$ be a Noetherian ring and $k \subseteq A \subseteq B$ be rings. If $B$
is a finitely generated $k$-algebra and $B$ is finitely generated as an $A$-modu
le, then $A$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra.

We set $k = \mathbb{R}$, $A = R$, and $B = R \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{C}$, w
here $R$ embeds into $B$ via $r \mapsto r \otimes 1$. We verify the two hypothes
es.

**Step 1: $B$ is a finitely generated $\mathbb{R}$-algebra.**

By hypothesis, $B = R \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{C}$ is a finitely generated $
\mathbb{C}$-algebra, so there exist elements $b_1, \ldots, b_n \in B$ such that
$B = \mathbb{C}[b_1, \ldots, b_n]$. Since $\mathbb{C} = \mathbb{R}[i]/(i^2+1)$ i
s itself a finitely generated $\mathbb{R}$-algebra (generated by $i$), we have
$$B = \mathbb{R}[i, b_1, \ldots, b_n],$$
so $B$ is a finitely generated $\mathbb{R}$-algebra.

**Step 2: $B$ is a finitely generated $R$-module.**

As an $\mathbb{R}$-vector space, $\mathbb{C} = \mathbb{R} \cdot 1 \oplus \mathbb
{R} \cdot i$. Therefore, as an $R$-module (via the embedding $r \mapsto r \otime
s 1$),
$$B = R \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{C} \cong R \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} (\mathbb{R}
 \oplus \mathbb{R}\cdot i) \cong (R \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{R}) \oplus (R \
otimes_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{R}) \cong R \oplus R.$$
Concretely, every element of $B$ can be written uniquely as $r_1 \otimes 1 + r_2
 \otimes i$ with $r_1, r_2 \in R$, so $B$ is a free $R$-module of rank $2$, gene
rated by $\{1 \otimes 1,\; 1 \otimes i\}$. In particular, $B$ is finitely genera
ted as an $R$-module.

**Step 3: Apply Artin–Tate.**

The field $\mathbb{R}$ is Noetherian. We have the inclusions $\mathbb{R} \subset
eq R \subseteq B$ (where $R \subseteq B$ is via $r \mapsto r \otimes 1$). By Ste
ps 1 and 2, $B$ is a finitely generated $\mathbb{R}$-algebra and a finitely gene
rated $R$-module. The Artin–Tate lemma then yields that $R$ is a finitely genera
ted $\mathbb{R}$-algebra. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The key structural fact is that $\mathbb{C}$ is a *finite* (degree 2
) extension of $\mathbb{R}$, which makes $B$ a finite $R$-module. The same argum
ent works for any finite Galois (or even finite separable) field extension in pl
ace of $\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}$.
