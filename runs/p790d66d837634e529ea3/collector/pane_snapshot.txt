**Answer: Yes, $V$ is semisimple.**

**Proof.** We prove the contrapositive-free direction directly: assuming $V_{k^a
}$ is semisimple, we show $V$ is semisimple.

Recall that a finite-dimensional representation is semisimple iff every subrepre
sentation admits a complementary subrepresentation, equivalently every short exa
ct sequence of representations
$$0 \to W \to V \to U \to 0$$
splits.

Let $W \subseteq V$ be any $kG$-submodule and set $U = V/W$. We must show that t
he exact sequence
$$0 \to W \to V \xrightarrow{\pi} U \to 0 \tag{$\ast$}$$
splits over $k$.

**Step 1 (base change splits).** Tensoring with $k^a$ yields
$$0 \to W_{k^a} \to V_{k^a} \to U_{k^a} \to 0.$$
Since $V_{k^a}$ is semisimple by hypothesis, the submodule $W_{k^a} \subseteq V_
{k^a}$ admits a $k^a G$-complement. Equivalently, there exists a $k^a$-linear $G
$-equivariant section $s : U_{k^a} \to V_{k^a}$ with $\pi \circ s = \mathrm{id}$
. Thus ($\ast$) splits over $k^a$.

**Step 2 (the splitting condition is a linear system over $k$).** Consider the f
inite-dimensional $k$-vector space $E = \mathrm{Hom}_k(U, V)$. The set of $k$-li
near splittings of ($\ast$) that are $G$-equivariant is
$$S_k = \bigl\{ s \in E : \pi \circ s = \mathrm{id}_U,\;\; s\circ \rho_U(g) = \r
ho_V(g)\circ s \;\;\forall\, g \in G \bigr\},$$
where $\rho_U, \rho_V$ are the $G$-actions (given by matrices over $k$). The con
dition $\pi \circ s = \mathrm{id}_U$ is affine-linear in the coordinates of $s$,
 and each intertwining condition $s\circ\rho_U(g) = \rho_V(g)\circ s$ is $k$-lin
ear in $s$. Hence $S_k$ is the solution set of a (possibly inhomogeneous) system
 of linear equations **with coefficients in $k$**.

After base change, $S_{k^a}$ is exactly the set of $k^a G$-equivariant sections
of the base-changed sequence, and
$$S_{k^a} = S_k \otimes_k k^a$$
as the solution set of the same linear system over $k^a$.

**Step 3 (linear descent of solvability).** A system of linear equations $Ax = b
$ with coefficients in $k$ is solvable over $k$ iff it is solvable over any exte
nsion field $K \supseteq k$. Indeed, solvability over a field $F$ is equivalent
to $\mathrm{rank}(A) = \mathrm{rank}(A \mid b)$, and rank is preserved under fie
ld extension (a minor vanishes over $k$ iff it vanishes over $K$, since $k \hook
rightarrow K$).

**Step 4 (conclusion).** By Step 1, $S_{k^a} \neq \varnothing$. By Step 3 (appli
ed to the linear system defining $S_k$), $S_k \neq \varnothing$. Therefore ($\as
t$) splits over $k$: $W$ admits a $kG$-complement in $V$.

Since $W \subseteq V$ was an arbitrary $kG$-submodule, every subrepresentation o
f $V$ has a complement, so $V$ is semisimple. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The argument uses only linear-algebraic descent of solvability of li
near systems, so the implication "$V_{k^a}$ semisimple $\Rightarrow$ $V$ semisim
ple" holds for **any** field $k$ (the characteristic-$0$ hypothesis is not neede
d for this direction; it is relevant for the converse, which relies on Maschke-t
ype semisimplicity of the group algebra).
