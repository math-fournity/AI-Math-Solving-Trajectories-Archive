## Proof

Let $C \subseteq [0,1]$ be the standard middle-thirds Cantor set, i.e.

$$C = \Bigl\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n\,3^{-n} : a_n \in \{0,2\}\Bigr\}.$$

We prove $C+C+C = [0,3]$ in two steps: first $C+C=[0,2]$, then $[0,2]+C=[0,3]$.

---

### Step 1. $C+C = [0,2]$.

By definition,

$$C+C = \Bigl\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(a_n+b_n)\,3^{-n} : a_n,b_n\in\{0,2\}\Bigr\}.$
$

Since $a_n+b_n\in\{0,2,4\}$, and conversely every value in $\{0,2,4\}$ is realiz
ed as $a_n+b_n$ with $a_n,b_n\in\{0,2\}$ (namely $0=0+0$, $2=0+2$, $4=2+2$), we
have

$$C+C = \Bigl\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} d_n\,3^{-n} : d_n\in\{0,2,4\}\Bigr\}.$$

Write $d_n = 2e_n$ with $e_n\in\{0,1,2\}$. Then

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} d_n\,3^{-n} = 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} e_n\,3^{-n}.$$

The inner sum $\sum e_n\,3^{-n}$ with $e_n\in\{0,1,2\}$ is exactly the set of ba
se-$3$ (ternary) expansions of numbers in $[0,1]$, since $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2\c
dot 3^{-n}=1$. Every real number in $[0,1]$ admits such an expansion. Hence

$$\Bigl\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} e_n\,3^{-n}: e_n\in\{0,1,2\}\Bigr\} = [0,1],$$

and therefore

$$C+C = 2\cdot[0,1] = [0,2].$$

---

### Step 2. $[0,2]+C = [0,3]$.

The inclusion $[0,2]+C\subseteq [0,3]$ is immediate since $C\subseteq[0,1]$.

For the reverse inclusion, let $z\in[0,3]$.

- If $z\in[0,2]$: take $c=0\in C$ and $w=z\in[0,2]$. Then $z=w+c$.
- If $z\in[2,3]$: take $c=1\in C$ (note $1=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2\cdot 3^{-n}\in C
$) and $w=z-1\in[1,2]\subseteq[0,2]$. Then $z=w+c$.

Thus every $z\in[0,3]$ lies in $[0,2]+C$, giving $[0,3]\subseteq[0,2]+C$.

Combining both inclusions, $[0,2]+C=[0,3]$.

---

### Conclusion

$$C+C+C = (C+C)+C = [0,2]+C = [0,3].$$

The sum of three copies of the Cantor set is the closed interval $[0,3]$.
