## Proof

Label the four columns of rooms $1,2,3,4$ along the corridor. In column $i$, let
 $T_i$ be the room on the top side and $B_i$ the room on the bottom side, so $T_
i$ and $B_i$ are opposite rooms ($i=1,2,3,4$).

**Adjacency structure.** Two rooms are *adjacent* exactly when they are consecut
ive rooms on the same side of the corridor: $T_i\sim T_{i+1}$ and $B_i\sim B_{i+
1}$ for $i=1,2,3$. Two rooms are *opposite* exactly when they share a column: $T
_i\sim B_i$.

**Key observation.** Since opposite rooms are forbidden, we may choose **at most
 one** room from each of the 4 columns. We must place 4 guests into 4 rooms, so
we must choose **exactly one** room from each column.

Thus a valid selection is determined by a side-choice string $(s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4)$
 with $s_i\in\{T,B\}$, giving $2^4=16$ candidates before applying the adjacency
constraint.

**Applying adjacency.** Two consecutive columns $i,i+1$ both chosen on the same
side would put two guests in adjacent rooms ($T_i,T_{i+1}$ or $B_i,B_{i+1}$), wh
ich is forbidden. Hence consecutive entries must differ:
$$s_1\neq s_2,\quad s_2\neq s_3,\quad s_3\neq s_4.$$
This forces the string to alternate, giving exactly two possibilities:
- $TBTB$: rooms $\{T_1,B_2,T_3,B_4\}=\{1,6,3,8\}$,
- $BTBT$: rooms $\{B_1,T_2,B_3,T_4\}=\{5,2,7,4\}$.

**Verification.** In $\{1,3,6,8\}$: top-side rooms $1,3$ are separated by room $
2$ (not adjacent); bottom-side rooms $6,8$ are separated by room $7$ (not adjace
nt); all four columns are distinct (no opposites). Same check holds for $\{2,4,5
,7\}$. Both are valid, and no other side-choice string satisfies the alternation
 requirement.

So there are exactly **2** valid sets of 4 rooms.

**Counting accommodations.** The four guests are distinct, and any bijection fro
m the 4 guests to the 4 chosen rooms is a different accommodation. For each vali
d room-set there are $4!$ assignments, so the total is
$$2\times 4! = 2\times 24 = 48.$$
