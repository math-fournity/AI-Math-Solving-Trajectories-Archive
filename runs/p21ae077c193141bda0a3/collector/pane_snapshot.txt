## Proof

**Claim.** Yes, $A'$ and $B'$ also fill $S_{0,4}$.

**Definition (filling).** Two (isotopy classes of) essential simple closed curve
s on a surface $S$ **fill** $S$ if the complement $S \setminus (A \cup B)$ is a
disjoint union of topological disks (together with any annular neighborhoods of
the boundary components of $S$, which are not considered part of the "interestin
g" complement). Equivalently, $A$ and $B$ are in minimal position (transverse, n
o bigons) and every component of $S \setminus (A \cup B)$ is a disk or a periphe
ral annulus.

**Key observation.** Filling is a topological property: it depends only on the h
omeomorphism type of the complement $S \setminus (A \cup B)$, not on any particu
lar geometric realization of the curves.

**Argument.** Let $\phi \in MCG(S_{0,4})$. By definition, $\phi$ is the isotopy
class of an orientation-preserving homeomorphism $f : S_{0,4} \to S_{0,4}$. Choo
se a representative $f$ of the class $\phi$, and choose representatives of the i
sotopy classes $A$, $B$ (also denoted $A$, $B$) so that $A' = f(A)$ and $B' = f(
B)$ are transverse and in minimal position — this can always be achieved by an a
rbitrarily small isotopy of $f$ within its class, since transversality and minim
al position are generic and preserved under small perturbation.

Since $f$ is a homeomorphism, it restricts to a homeomorphism of pairs:
$$f : \bigl(S_{0,4},\, A \cup B\bigr) \;\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\; \bigl(S_{0,4},
\, A' \cup B'\bigr).$$
Consequently $f$ induces a homeomorphism of complements:
$$S_{0,4} \setminus (A \cup B) \;\cong\; S_{0,4} \setminus (A' \cup B').$$

By hypothesis, $A$ and $B$ fill $S_{0,4}$, so every component of $S_{0,4} \setmi
nus (A \cup B)$ is a disk (or a peripheral annulus parallel to a boundary compon
ent). Since the two complements are homeomorphic, every component of $S_{0,4} \s
etminus (A' \cup B')$ is also a disk (or a peripheral annulus). Therefore $A'$ a
nd $B'$ fill $S_{0,4}$.

**Why isotopy does not matter.** The argument above uses a specific representati
ve $f$ and specific curve representatives. If we replace $f$ by an isotopic home
omorphism, or replace $A$, $B$ by isotopic curves, the complement $S_{0,4}\setmi
nus(A\cup B)$ changes only by a homeomorphism (isotopy extends to a homeomorphis
m of the surface by the isotopy extension theorem). Hence the homeomorphism type
 of the complement — and thus the filling property — is an invariant of the isot
opy classes. The conclusion therefore holds for the mapping class $\phi$ and the
 isotopy classes $A'$, $B'$.

**Conclusion.** The mapping class group acts by homeomorphisms, and filling is p
reserved by homeomorphisms. Hence for every $\phi \in MCG(S_{0,4})$, the images
$A' = \phi(A)$ and $B' = \phi(B)$ fill $S_{0,4}$. $\blacksquare$
