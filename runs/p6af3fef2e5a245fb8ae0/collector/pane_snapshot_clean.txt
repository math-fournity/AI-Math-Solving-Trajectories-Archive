Looking at this problem, I need to find the number of natural numbers $n < m = 2
007^{2008}$ such that $m \mid n(2n+1)(5n+2)$.

## Proof

**Factorization.** $2007 = 3^2 \times 223$, where $223$ is prime (checked agains
t all primes up to $\sqrt{223} \approx 14.9$). Thus $m = 3^{4016} \times 223^{20
08}$.

**Pairwise coprimality of the three factors.** For any integer $n$:
- $\gcd(n,\, 2n+1) = \gcd(n, 1) = 1$
- $\gcd(n,\, 5n+2) = \gcd(n, 2) \mid 2$
- $\gcd(2n+1,\, 5n+2) = \gcd(2n+1,\, 5(2n+1)-2(5n+2)) = \gcd(2n+1, 1) = 1$

Since $m$ is odd, the only possible common factor (which is 2) is irrelevant. So
 for every odd prime $p \mid m$, at most one of $n$, $2n+1$, $5n+2$ is divisible
 by $p$.

**Reduction to prime powers.** Since $\gcd(3^{4016}, 223^{2008}) = 1$, by CRT th
e condition $m \mid n(2n+1)(5n+2)$ is equivalent to:
$$3^{4016} \mid n(2n+1)(5n+2) \quad \text{AND} \quad 223^{2008} \mid n(2n+1)(5n+
2).$$

The number of solutions mod $m$ equals $N_3 \times N_{223}$, where $N_p$ counts
solutions mod $p^{e_p}$.

**Counting solutions mod $p^e$ for $p \in \{3, 223\}$.** Since the three factors
 are pairwise coprime mod $p$, the condition $p^e \mid n(2n+1)(5n+2)$ holds iff
$p^e$ divides exactly one of the three factors. Each gives one residue class:

1. $p^e \mid n$: the class $n \equiv 0 \pmod{p^e}$.
2. $p^e \mid (2n+1)$: since $\gcd(2,p)=1$, this gives $n \equiv -2^{-1} \pmod{p^
e}$.
3. $p^e \mid (5n+2)$: since $\gcd(5,p)=1$ (as $p \in \{3,223\}$), this gives $n
\equiv -2 \cdot 5^{-1} \pmod{p^e}$.

**The three classes are distinct.** Checking mod $p$:
- For $p = 3$: $n \equiv 0$, $2n+1 \equiv 0 \Rightarrow n \equiv 1$, $5n+2 \equi
v 0 \Rightarrow 2n \equiv 1 \Rightarrow n \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$. Three distinct res
idues.
- For $p = 223$: $n \equiv 0$; $2n+1 \equiv 0 \Rightarrow n \equiv 111$; $5n+2 \
equiv 0 \Rightarrow n \equiv 178 \pmod{223}$. Three distinct residues.

So $N_3 = 3$ and $N_{223} = 3$.

**Combining via CRT.** The total number of solutions modulo $m$ is:
$$N_3 \times N_{223} = 3 \times 3 = 9.$$

Each solution is a distinct residue in $\{0, 1, \ldots, m-1\}$. The solution $n
\equiv 0 \pmod{m}$ (i.e., $n = 0$) is included, and $0 \cdot 1 \cdot 2 = 0$ is i
ndeed divisible by $m$. Since $n < m$ and natural numbers (自然数) include $0$,
all 9 solutions are valid.

**Verification with small case.** Taking $m = 21 = 3 \times 7$ (both odd primes
not dividing 2 or 5), the same argument predicts $3 \times 3 = 9$ solutions. Bru
te-force checking $n = 0, \ldots, 20$ confirms exactly 9 solutions: $n \in \{0,
1, 3, 7, 8, 10, 14, 15, 17\}$, matching the 9 CRT combinations.

The number of natural numbers $n < m$ with $m \mid n(2n+1)(5n+2)$ is $\boxed{9}$
