**Proof.**

Let $R$ be the circumradius, and let $M$ be the midpoint of the arc $BC$ not con
taining $A$.

**Step 1. $M$ is the midpoint of $II_a$, with $MI=MI_a=2R\sin\frac A2$.**

Since $M$ is the midpoint of arc $BC$, the line $AM$ is the internal bisector of
 $\angle A$, so $I,M,I_a$ are collinear. The standard fact that $M$ is the circu
mcenter of $\triangle BIC$ gives $MI=MB$; indeed $\angle MBC=\angle MCB=\frac A2
$ and $\angle IBC=\frac B2,\ \angle ICB=\frac C2$ yield $\angle MBI=\angle MIB=\
frac{A+B}{2}$, hence $MI=MB$. As arc $MB$ has measure $A$, $MB=2R\sin\frac A2$,
so $MI=2R\sin\frac A2$. For the excenter, $\angle BI_aC=\frac\pi2-\frac A2$, so
the circumradius of $\triangle BI_aC$ is
$$\frac{BC}{2\sin\angle BI_aC}=\frac{2R\sin A}{2\cos(A/2)}=2R\sin\frac A2=MB.$$
Since $M$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $BC$ on the same side as $I_a$,
$M$ is the circumcenter of $\triangle BI_aC$, giving $MI_a=2R\sin\frac A2$. Thus
 $MI=MI_a$ and $M$ is the midpoint of $II_a$.

**Step 2. A convenient coordinate system.**

Place the origin at $M$, with the $x$-axis along $MI_a$ (the bisector of $\angle
 A$) and the $y$-axis perpendicular. Set
$$p=\frac{C-B}{2},\qquad s=\sin\frac A2,\qquad k=2\cos A-1=1-4s^2.$$

In the circumcircle frame ($O$ at the origin, $M=(R,0)$, $B=R(\cos A,\sin A)$, $
C=R(\cos A,-\sin A)$, $A=R(-\cos(C-B),-\sin(C-B))$), the direction $M\to A$ is $
-(\cos p,\sin p)$ (since $A-M=-2R\cos p\,(\cos p,\sin p)$), so $M\to I_a$ has di
rection $(\cos p,\sin p)$, with perpendicular $(-\sin p,\cos p)$.

- $I=(-2Rs,0)$, $\ I_a=(2Rs,0)$ (from Step 1).
- $O-M=(-R,0)$, hence
$$O=\bigl((-R,0)\cdot(\cos p,\sin p),\ (-R,0)\cdot(-\sin p,\cos p)\bigr)=R(-\cos
 p,\ \sin p).$$
- Reflecting $A$ over $BC$ (the line $x=R\cos A$) gives $A'=R(2\cos A+\cos(C-B),
-\sin(C-B))$. Projecting $A'-M=R(2\cos A-1+\cos 2p,\,-\sin 2p)$ onto our frame:
$$A'=R\bigl(k\cos p+\cos 3p,\ -k\sin p-\sin 3p\bigr).$$

**Step 3. $\tan\angle IOI_a$.**

$$OI=R(\cos p-2s,-\sin p),\qquad OI_a=R(\cos p+2s,-\sin p).$$
$$\text{dot}=R^2\bigl[(\cos p-2s)(\cos p+2s)+\sin^2 p\bigr]=R^2(1-4s^2)=R^2 k,$$
$$|\text{cross}|=R^2\bigl|(\cos p-2s)(-\sin p)-(-\sin p)(\cos p+2s)\bigr|=4R^2 s
\,|\sin p|.$$
So
$$\tan\angle IOI_a=\frac{4s\,|\sin p|}{k}.\tag{1}$$

**Step 4. $\tan\angle IA'I_a$.**

Let $X=k\cos p+\cos 3p$, $Y=k\sin p+\sin 3p$, so $A'=R(X,-Y)$ and
$$A'I=R(-2s-X,\,Y),\qquad A'I_a=R(2s-X,\,Y).$$
$$\text{dot}=R^2\bigl[(-2s-X)(2s-X)+Y^2\bigr]=R^2(X^2-4s^2+Y^2).$$
Using $X^2+Y^2=k^2+1+2k\cos 2p$,
$$\text{dot}=R^2\bigl(k^2+1+2k\cos 2p-4s^2\bigr)=R^2\,k\,(k+1+2\cos 2p).$$
For the cross product,
$$|\text{cross}|=R^2\bigl|(-2s-X)Y-Y(2s-X)\bigr|=4R^2 s\,|Y|.$$
Now use $\sin 3p=\sin p(1+2\cos 2p)$, whence
$$Y=k\sin p+\sin 3p=\sin p\,(k+1+2\cos 2p).$$
Therefore
$$|\text{cross}|=4R^2 s\,|\sin p|\,(k+1+2\cos 2p),$$
and
$$\tan\angle IA'I_a=\frac{4s\,|\sin p|\,|\,k+1+2\cos 2p\,|}{k\,(k+1+2\cos 2p)}.\
tag{2}$$

**Step 5. The common factor is positive.**

$$k+1+2\cos 2p=2\cos A+2\cos(C-B)=2(\cos A+\cos(C-B)).$$
By sum-to-product,
$$\cos A+\cos(C-B)=2\cos\frac{A+C-B}{2}\cos\frac{A-C+B}{2}=2\cos\!\Bigl(\frac\pi
2-B\Bigr)\cos\!\Bigl(\frac\pi2-C\Bigr)=2\sin B\sin C.$$
So $k+1+2\cos 2p=4\sin B\sin C>0$ (the triangle is acute). Substituting into (2)
,
$$\tan\angle IA'I_a=\frac{4s\,|\sin p|}{k}=\tan\angle IOI_a.$$

Both angles lie in $[0,\pi]$, and their cosines share the sign of $k$ (each dot
product is $R^2 k$ times a positive factor), so equal tangents force equal angle
s. The boundary cases are consistent: when $k=0$ (i.e. $A=60^\circ$) both dot pr
oducts vanish and both angles equal $\frac\pi2$; when $\sin p=0$ (i.e. $B=C$) al
l relevant points lie on the axis of symmetry and both angles are simultaneously
 $0$ or $\pi$.

Hence $\angle IOI_a=\angle IA'I_a$. $\blacksquare$
