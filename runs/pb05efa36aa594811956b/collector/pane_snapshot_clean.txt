## Proof

 the $n \times m$ integer matrix representing $f$ in the standard bases. Denote
the columns of $F$ by $c_1, \ldots, c_m \in \mathbb{Z}^n \subset \mathbb{R}^n$,
with Euclidean lengths $\|c_j\| = \bigl(\sum_{i=1}^n f_{ij}^2\bigr)^{1/2}$. Let
$r = \operatorname{rank}(F)$.

**Step 1: Order of the torsion subgroup via Smith normal form.**

By the Smith normal form theorem, there exist unimodular matrices $U \in \mathrm
{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$, $V \in \mathrm{GL}_m(\mathbb{Z})$ such that $UFV = D$, wher
e $D$ is diagonal with entries $d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_r > 0$ followed
by zeros. The torsion subgroup of $N/\operatorname{image}(f)$ is

$$\operatorname{tors}\bigl(N/\operatorname{image}(f)\bigr) \cong \bigoplus_{i=1}
^{r} \mathbb{Z}/d_i\mathbb{Z},$$

so its order is $d_1 d_2 \cdots d_r$.

**Step 2: Determinant divisors.**

Let $\Delta_k(F)$ denote the greatest common divisor of all $k \times k$ minors
of $F$ (with $\Delta_0 = 1$). A standard result in the theory of Smith normal fo
rm gives $d_k = \Delta_k(F)/\Delta_{k-1}(F)$, hence

$$|\operatorname{tors}(N/\operatorname{image}(f))| = d_1 \cdots d_r = \Delta_r(F
).$$

**Step 3: Hadamard's inequality bounds each minor.**

Since $\operatorname{rank}(F) = r$, at least one $r \times r$ minor of $F$ is no
nzero. Let $A$ be any $r \times r$ submatrix of $F$, obtained by selecting a set
 $R$ of $r$ rows and a set $S$ of $r$ columns. By **Hadamard's determinant inequ
ality**,

$$|\det(A)| \leq \prod_{j \in S} \|a_j\|,$$

where $a_j$ is the $j$-th column of $A$. Since $a_j$ is obtained from $c_j$ by s
electing only $r$ of its $n$ entries, we have $\|a_j\| \leq \|c_j\|$. Therefore

$$|\det(A)| \leq \prod_{j \in S} \|c_j\|.$$

If any column $c_j$ with $j \in S$ is the zero vector, then $\det(A) = 0$, and t
he inequality is trivial. Otherwise, every $j \in S$ is among the nonzero column
s, so $S$ is a subset of the index set of nonzero columns, giving

$$|\det(A)| \leq \prod_{j \,:\, c_j \neq 0} \|c_j\|.$$

**Step 4: Combining the bounds.**

The quantity $\Delta_r(F)$ is the gcd of all $r \times r$ minors. Since at least
 one such minor is nonzero, $\Delta_r(F)$ is at most the absolute value of any s
ingle nonzero $r \times r$ minor:

$$\Delta_r(F) \leq |\det(A)| \quad \text{for any nonzero } r \times r \text{ min
or } A.$$

Applying the bound from Step 3:

$$|\operatorname{tors}(N/\operatorname{image}(f))| = \Delta_r(F) \leq \prod_{j \
,:\, c_j \neq 0} \|c_j\|.$$

**Edge case $r = 0$:** If $F = 0$, there are no nonzero columns (empty product $
= 1$), and $N/\operatorname{image}(f) = N$ is torsion-free, so the order is $1 \
leq 1$. ✓

**Conclusion.** The order of the torsion subgroup of $N/\operatorname{image}(f)$
 is bounded above by the product of the Euclidean lengths of the nonzero columns
 of $F$. $\blacksquare$
