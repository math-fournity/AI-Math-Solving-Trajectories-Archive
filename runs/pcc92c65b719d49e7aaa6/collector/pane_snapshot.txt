## Proof

The statement is **true**.

**Setup.** Let $E$ be Hausdorff, $(K_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ a decreasing sequence
of compact subsets ($K_1 \supseteq K_2 \supseteq \cdots$), and $U \subset E$ ope
n with $\bigcap_{n} K_n \subset U$.

**Key facts in a Hausdorff space.** Every compact subset of a Hausdorff space is
 closed. Hence each $K_n$ is closed, and $K_1$ is compact.

**Step 1: Reduce to a sequence of closed subsets of $K_1$ with empty intersectio
n.**

Define $F_n := K_n \setminus U = K_n \cap U^c$. Since $K_n$ is closed (compact i
n Hausdorff) and $U^c$ is closed, $F_n$ is closed in $E$. Since $F_n \subseteq K
_n \subseteq K_1$, each $F_n$ is a closed subset of the compact set $K_1$, hence
 compact. The sequence $(F_n)$ is decreasing because $(K_n)$ is.

Compute the intersection:
$$\bigcap_{n} F_n = \bigcap_{n} (K_n \cap U^c) = \left(\bigcap_{n} K_n\right) \c
ap U^c \subset U \cap U^c = \emptyset.$$

So $\bigcap_n F_n = \emptyset$.

**Step 2: Apply the finite intersection property.**

A topological space is compact iff every family of closed subsets with the finit
e intersection property (every finite subfamily has nonempty intersection) has n
onempty total intersection. Contrapositively: in a compact space, any family of
closed subsets with **empty** total intersection has a finite subfamily with emp
ty intersection.

Apply this to the compact set $K_1$ and the decreasing family $\{F_n\}$ of close
d subsets of $K_1$. Since $\bigcap_n F_n = \emptyset$, there exist $n_1 < \cdots
 < n_k$ with $F_{n_1} \cap \cdots \cap F_{n_k} = \emptyset$. Because the family
is decreasing, $F_{n_1} \supseteq F_{n_2} \supseteq \cdots \supseteq F_{n_k}$, s
o
$$F_{n_1} \cap \cdots \cap F_{n_k} = F_{n_k} = \emptyset.$$

**Step 3: Conclude.**

Set $n_0 := n_k$. Then $F_{n_0} = K_{n_0} \setminus U = \emptyset$, i.e. $K_{n_0
} \subset U$. For every $n \geq n_0$, since the sequence is decreasing, $K_n \su
bseteq K_{n_0} \subset U$. $\blacksquare$
