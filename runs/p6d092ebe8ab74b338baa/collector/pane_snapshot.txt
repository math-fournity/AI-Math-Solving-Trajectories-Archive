## Conditional Covariance of Brownian Motion given $B_1$

**Setup.** Let $B$ be a standard one-dimensional Brownian motion, and $s,t \in [
0,1]$. We compute $\operatorname{Cov}(B_s, B_t \mid B_1)$.

**Key decomposition (Brownian bridge).** Define
$$X_t := B_t - t\,B_1, \qquad t \in [0,1].$$

**Claim 1: $X$ is independent of $B_1$.**

For any $t \in [0,1]$, the pair $(B_t, B_1)$ is jointly Gaussian, and
$$\operatorname{Cov}(B_t - tB_1,\; B_1) = \operatorname{Cov}(B_t, B_1) - t\,\ope
ratorname{Var}(B_1) = t - t\cdot 1 = 0.$$
Since $(X_t, B_1)$ is jointly Gaussian and uncorrelated, they are independent. M
ore generally, the entire process $(X_t)_{t\in[0,1]}$ is jointly Gaussian and ev
ery $X_t$ is uncorrelated with $B_1$, so $X$ is independent of $B_1$.

**Claim 2: Conditional means.** Since $B_t = X_t + t\,B_1$ and $X_t \perp B_1$,
$$\mathbb{E}[B_t \mid B_1] = \mathbb{E}[X_t] + t\,B_1 = t\,B_1.$$

**Claim 3: Conditional covariance.** Using $B_s = X_s + sB_1$ and $B_t = X_t + t
B_1$, and the independence of $X$ from $B_1$ (so conditioning on $B_1$ only remo
ves the deterministic $B_1$-terms):
$$\operatorname{Cov}(B_s, B_t \mid B_1) = \operatorname{Cov}(X_s, X_t \mid B_1)
= \operatorname{Cov}(X_s, X_t).$$

Now compute $\operatorname{Cov}(X_s, X_t) = \mathbb{E}[X_s X_t]$ (since $\mathbb
{E}[X_s]=\mathbb{E}[X_t]=0$):
$$\mathbb{E}[X_s X_t] = \mathbb{E}\big[(B_s - sB_1)(B_t - tB_1)\big].$$

Expanding and using $\mathbb{E}[B_u B_v] = \min(u,v)$:
$$= \mathbb{E}[B_s B_t] - t\,\mathbb{E}[B_s B_1] - s\,\mathbb{E}[B_1 B_t] + st\,
\mathbb{E}[B_1^2]$$
$$= \min(s,t) - t\cdot\min(s,1) - s\cdot\min(1,t) + st\cdot 1.$$

Since $s,t \in [0,1]$, we have $\min(s,1)=s$ and $\min(1,t)=t$, giving
$$= \min(s,t) - ts - st + st = \min(s,t) - st.$$

**Result.**
$$\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}(B_s, B_t \mid B_1) = \min(s,t) - st.}$$

**Sanity checks.**
- At $s=t$: $\operatorname{Var}(B_t \mid B_1) = t - t^2 = t(1-t)$, the well-know
n Brownian bridge variance. ✓
- At $t=1$: $\operatorname{Cov}(B_s, B_1 \mid B_1) = \min(s,1)-s = s - s = 0$, c
onsistent with $B_1$ being deterministic given itself. ✓
- At $s=0$ or $t=0$: gives $0$, consistent with $B_0=0$ a.s. ✓
