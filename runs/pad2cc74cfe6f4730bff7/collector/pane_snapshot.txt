## Proof

The ellipse is $E: \frac{x^2}{4} + y^2 = 1$, with upper branch parameterized as
$z(x) = (x,\, y(x))$ where $y(x) = \sqrt{1 - \tfrac{x^2}{4}}$ for $x \in (0, 2)$
. Let $z_0 = (x_0, y_0)$ be a fixed point on $E$ (so $y_0 = \sqrt{1 - \tfrac{x_0
^2}{4}}$).

**Claim.** $C = 1$ satisfies $\delta(z, z_0) \le C\,|z - z_0|$ for all $z$ with
$x > 0$, and this is the optimal (smallest) universal constant.

---

### Step 1: $\delta(z, z_0) \le |x - x_0|$

Since $y(t) = \sqrt{1 - t^2/4} \in [0, 1]$ for all $t \in [0, 2]$, we bound the
integrand:

$$\delta(z, z_0) = \left|\int_x^{x_0} y(t)\, dt\right| \le \left|\int_x^{x_0} 1\
, dt\right| = |x - x_0|.$$

### Step 2: $|x - x_0| \le |z - z_0|$

The Euclidean distance satisfies

$$|z - z_0|^2 = (x - x_0)^2 + (y(x) - y_0)^2 \ge (x - x_0)^2,$$

so $|z - z_0| \ge |x - x_0|$.

### Step 3: Combining

$$\delta(z, z_0) \le |x - x_0| \le |z - z_0| = 1 \cdot |z - z_0|.$$

Hence $C = 1$ is a valid constant.

---

### Step 4: Optimality — no $C < 1$ works universally

Take $z_0$ with $x_0 \to 0^+$ (so $y_0 \to 1$) and $z$ with $x \to 0^+$, $x \ne
x_0$. As $x, x_0 \to 0^+$:

- $y(t) \to 1$ uniformly on the interval between $x$ and $x_0$, so $\delta(z, z_
0) = \left|\int_x^{x_0} y(t)\,dt\right| \to |x - x_0|$.
- $y(x) - y_0 \to 0$ (both tend to $1$), so $|z - z_0| = \sqrt{(x-x_0)^2 + (y(x)
-y_0)^2} \to |x - x_0|$.

Therefore

$$\frac{\delta(z, z_0)}{|z - z_0|} \to 1 \quad \text{as } x, x_0 \to 0^+.$$

This shows the ratio can be made arbitrarily close to $1$, so no constant $C < 1
$ can serve as a universal bound. Since we proved the ratio is always $\le 1$, t
he optimal constant is exactly

$$\boxed{C = 1}.$$
