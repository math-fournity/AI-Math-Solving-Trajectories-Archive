## Problem

Solve
$$f(x) = \lambda \int_{x}^{\infty} (y-x)^{a-1} f(y)\,dy,\qquad a>0.$$

## Solution

**Step 1 — Recognize the operator.** The right-sided Riemann–Liouville fractiona
l integral of order $a$ is
$$(I_{-}^{a} f)(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(a)}\int_{x}^{\infty}(y-x)^{a-1}f(y)\,dy.$$
Hence the equation reads
$$f(x)=\lambda\,\Gamma(a)\,(I_{-}^{a}f)(x). \tag{1}$$

**Step 2 — Eigenfunctions of $I_{-}^{a}$.** Try $f(x)=e^{-sx}$ with $\operatorna
me{Re}s>0$ (so the integral converges at $+\infty$). Substitute $t=y-x$:
$$\int_{x}^{\infty}(y-x)^{a-1}e^{-sy}\,dy = e^{-sx}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{a-1}e^{-s
t}\,dt = e^{-sx}\,\frac{\Gamma(a)}{s^{a}}.$$
Therefore
$$(I_{-}^{a}e^{-s\,\cdot})(x)=\frac{e^{-sx}}{s^{a}},$$
i.e. $e^{-sx}$ is an eigenfunction of $I_{-}^{a}$ with eigenvalue $1/s^{a}$.

**Step 3 — Determine $s$.** Plugging into (1):
$$e^{-sx}=\lambda\,\Gamma(a)\,\frac{e^{-sx}}{s^{a}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad s^{
a}=\lambda\,\Gamma(a).$$
Thus
$$s=\bigl(\lambda\,\Gamma(a)\bigr)^{1/a}.$$

**Step 4 — General solution.** The equation is linear and homogeneous, so any co
nstant multiple of an eigenfunction is again a solution. The full set of (decayi
ng, integrable) solutions is obtained from all roots $s$ of $s^{a}=\lambda\Gamma
(a)$ with $\operatorname{Re}s>0$; each such root contributes a term $C_{k}\,e^{-
s_{k}x}$. For real $\lambda>0$ the principal (real, positive) root is
$$s_{0}=\bigl(\lambda\,\Gamma(a)\bigr)^{1/a}>0,$$
giving the real-valued decaying solution
$$\boxed{\,f(x)=C\,\exp\!\Bigl(-\bigl(\lambda\,\Gamma(a)\bigr)^{1/a}\,x\Bigr),\q
quad C\in\mathbb{R}.\,}$$

**Verification.** With $s=(\lambda\Gamma(a))^{1/a}$,
$$\lambda\int_{x}^{\infty}(y-x)^{a-1}Ce^{-sy}\,dy = C\,\lambda\,e^{-sx}\frac{\Ga
mma(a)}{s^{a}} = C\,e^{-sx}\cdot\frac{\lambda\Gamma(a)}{\lambda\Gamma(a)}=Ce^{-s
x}=f(x).\;\checkmark$$

The integral converges since $a>0$ and $\operatorname{Re}s>0$, and $f\to0$ as $x
\to\infty$, consistent with the equation.
