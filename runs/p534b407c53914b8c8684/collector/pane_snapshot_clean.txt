## Solution

**Claim:** $\limsup_{k\to\infty} \dfrac{c_k}{k} = 1$.

**Upper bound.** Every $k$-regular graph has maximum degree $k$, so by the greed
y coloring algorithm its chromatic number is at most $k+1$. Hence $c_k \le k+1$
for all $k \ge 1$.

**Lower bound.** The complete graph $K_{k+1}$ is $k$-regular (each vertex has de
gree $k$) and has chromatic number $k+1$. Therefore $c_k \ge k+1$.

**Combining.** From both bounds, $c_k = k+1$ for every $k \ge 1$. Consequently

$$\frac{c_k}{k} = \frac{k+1}{k} = 1 + \frac{1}{k} \xrightarrow{k\to\infty} 1.$$

Since the limit exists and equals $1$, the limsup equals the same value:

$$\limsup_{k\to\infty} \frac{c_k}{k} = \lim_{k\to\infty} \frac{k+1}{k} = 1.$$
