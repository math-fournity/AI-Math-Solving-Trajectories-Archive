## Proof

**Step 1: Identify the recurrence and its closed form.**

The matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix}\alpha & -1 \\ 1 & 0\end{bmatrix}$ generates the
linear recurrence
$$\binom{x_{k+1}}{x_k} = A\binom{x_k}{x_{k-1}}, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad x_{k+1}
= \alpha\, x_k - x_{k-1},$$
with $\det A = 1$ and $\operatorname{tr} A = \alpha$.

The characteristic equation of the recurrence is $r^2 - \alpha r + 1 = 0$, with
roots
$$\mu_{\pm} = \frac{\alpha \pm \sqrt{\alpha^2 - 4}}{2}, \qquad \mu_+\mu_- = 1.$$

Writing the product in the given formula as
$$\prod_{i=m}^{2m-1}(k-i) = \frac{(k-m)!}{(k-2m)!},$$
the coefficient becomes $\frac{1}{m!}\frac{(k-m)!}{(k-2m)!} = \binom{k-m}{m}$, s
o the first sum is
$$\sum_{m=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}(-1)^m \binom{k-m}{m}\alpha^{k-2m} =: U_k(\alph
a),$$
the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind (up to the substitution $\alpha = 2\
cos\theta$), satisfying $U_0=1,\;U_1=\alpha,\;U_{n}=\alpha U_{n-1}-U_{n-2}$. Hen
ce the given expression is exactly
$$x_{k+1} = x_1\, U_k(\alpha) - x_0\, U_{k-1}(\alpha),$$
the general solution of $x_{k+1}=\alpha x_k - x_{k-1}$, equivalently
$$x_{k+1} = C_+\,\mu_+^{\,k+1} + C_-\,\mu_-^{\,k+1}.$$
So the long-time behavior of $\{x_k\}$ is governed entirely by the eigenvalues $
\mu_\pm$ of $A$.

**Step 2: Eigenvalue analysis of $A$.**

Since $\mu_+\mu_- = 1$, the two eigenvalues are reciprocals. There are three reg

*Case (a): $|\alpha| > 2$ (i.e. $\alpha^2-4 > 0$).* The eigenvalues are real and

- If $\alpha > 2$: both positive, with $\mu_+ > 1 > \mu_- > 0$.
- If $\alpha < -2$: both negative, with $|\mu_+| > 1 > |\mu_-|$.

In either subcase one eigenvalue has modulus $> 1$. For generic initial data ($C

*Case (b): $|\alpha| = 2$ (i.e. $\alpha = \pm 2$).* The eigenvalue $\mu = \alpha

$$A - \mu I = \begin{bmatrix}\alpha-\mu & -1 \\ 1 & -\mu\end{bmatrix} = \begin{b

has rank $1$ (its two rows are proportional), the geometric multiplicity is $1 <

$$x_k = (C_1 + C_2\, k)\,\mu^k.$$

*Case (c): $|\alpha| < 2$ (i.e. $\alpha^2 - 4 < 0$).* The eigenvalues are comple

$$x_{k+1} = x_1\,\frac{\sin((k+1)\theta)}{\sin\theta} - x_0\,\frac{\sin(k\theta)
