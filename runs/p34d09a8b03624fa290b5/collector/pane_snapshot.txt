**Identify all singularities of the integrand** $f(z) = \frac{\tan z}{z}\, e^{-1
/(z+2)}$:

1. **$z = 0$** (from the factor $1/z$): Since $\tan z = z + \frac{z^3}{3} + \cdo
ts$, we have $\frac{\tan z}{z} = 1 + \frac{z^2}{3} + \cdots$, which is analytic
at $z=0$ (removable singularity, value $1$). The factor $e^{-1/(z+2)}$ is analyt
ic at $z=0$ (since $0+2=2\neq 0$), with value $e^{-1/2}$. Thus $z=0$ is a **remo
vable singularity** of $f$.

2. **Poles of $\tan z$** at $z = \frac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$, $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ (zero
s of $\cos z$): The closest to the origin are $z = \pm\frac{\pi}{2} \approx \pm
1.5708$. Since $\left|\pm\frac{\pi}{2}\right| = \frac{\pi}{2} > 1$, **all poles
of $\tan z$ lie strictly outside the unit disk**. The factor $e^{-1/(z+2)}$ neve
r vanishes (exponential is never zero), so it does not cancel any of these poles
.

3. **Essential singularity of $e^{-1/(z+2)}$** at $z = -2$: Since $|-2| = 2 > 1$
, this point lies **outside the unit disk**.

**Conclusion on singularities inside $|z| \leq 1$:** The only singularity in the
 closed unit disk is the removable singularity at $z=0$. After removing it, $f$
is analytic on an open set containing $\{|z| \leq 1\}$ (in particular, no singul
arities lie on the boundary $|z|=1$, since $\pi/2 > 1$ and $|-2|=2$).

**Apply Cauchy's Integral Theorem:** Since $f$ is analytic on and inside the sim
ple closed contour $|z|=1$, by Cauchy's theorem:

$$\boxed{I = \int_{|z|=1} \frac{\tan(z)}{z\, e^{1/(z+2)}}\, dz = 0.}$$
