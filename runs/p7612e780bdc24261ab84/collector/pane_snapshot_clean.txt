**Claim:** $E[\Delta M_T]$ is **not** necessarily $0$. We provide a counterexamp

**Setup.** Let $(N_t)_{t\ge 0}$ be a Poisson process with intensity $\lambda>0$
on a filtered space $(\Omega,\mathcal F,(\mathcal F_t),P)$, where $(\mathcal F_t
)$ is the (usual conditions) natural filtration of $N$. Let
$$T=\inf\{t>0:N_t=1\}$$
be the first jump time of $N$. It is classical that $T$ is a **totally inaccessi
ble** stopping time: its conditional hazard rate is the constant $\lambda$, so i
t cannot be foretold by any predictable stopping time; equivalently $P(T=S<\inft
y)=0$ for every predictable stopping time $S$.

**The martingale.** Define the compensated Poisson martingale, stopped at the de
terministic time $1$:
$$M_t = N_{t\wedge 1}-\lambda(t\wedge 1),\qquad t\ge 0.$$
Then $M_0=0$. For $t\le 1$, by independent increments,
$$E[N_1-\lambda\mid\mathcal F_t]=N_t+\lambda(1-t)-\lambda=N_t-\lambda t=M_t,$$
and for $t>1$, $M_t=N_1-\lambda=M_1=E[M_1\mid\mathcal F_t]$ (since $\mathcal F_t
=\mathcal F_1$). Hence $M_t=E[M_1\mid\mathcal F_t]$ for all $t\ge 0$, with $M_1=
N_1-\lambda\in L^1$. Therefore $M$ is a **uniformly integrable** martingale, nul
l at $0$.

**Computing $\Delta M_T$.** The compensator $t\mapsto\lambda(t\wedge 1)$ is cont
inuous, so the jumps of $M$ coincide exactly with the jumps of $N$ (on $[0,1]$).

- **If $T\le 1$:** $M_T=N_T-\lambda T=1-\lambda T$ and $M_{T-}=N_{T-}-\lambda T=
0-\lambda T=-\lambda T$, so
$$\Delta M_T = M_T-M_{T-}=1.$$

- **If $T>1$:** then $N$ has no jump on $[0,1]$, so $N_1=0$ and $M_t=N_1-\lambda
=-\lambda$ is constant on $[1,\infty)$. In particular $M_T=M_{T-}=-\lambda$, so
$$\Delta M_T=0.$$

$$E[\Delta M_T]=E[\mathbf 1_{\{T\le 1\}}\cdot 1+\mathbf 1_{\{T>1\}}\cdot 0]=P(T\
le 1)=1-e^{-\lambda}.$$

Since $\lambda>0$, we have $1-e^{-\lambda}>0$, and thus
$$\boxed{E[\Delta M_T]=1-e^{-\lambda}\neq 0.}$$

artingale $M$ null at $0$ such that $E[\Delta M_T]\neq 0$. Hence the answer to t
