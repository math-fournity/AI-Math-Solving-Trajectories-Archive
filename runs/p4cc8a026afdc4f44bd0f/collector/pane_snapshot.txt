## Proof

### Step 1: The Standard Rubik's Cube Group

A Rubik's Cube has 8 corner pieces and 12 edge pieces (centers are fixed to the
core).

**Corners:** 8 corner pieces can be permuted in $8!$ ways, and each corner has 3
 possible orientations, giving $3^8$ orientation states. However, the corner ori
entation constraint requires the sum of all corner twists $\equiv 0 \pmod{3}$, r
educing this to $3^7$.

**Edges:** 12 edge pieces can be permuted in $12!$ ways, and each edge has 2 pos
sible orientations, giving $2^{12}$ orientation states. However, the edge orient
ation constraint requires the sum of all edge flips $\equiv 0 \pmod{2}$, reducin
g this to $2^{10}$.

**Parity constraint:** The permutation parity of corners must equal the permutat
ion parity of edges, introducing a further division by 2.

Combining these, the standard Rubik's Cube group order is:

$$|G| = \frac{8! \times 3^8 \times 12! \times 2^{12}}{3 \times 2 \times 2} = 8!
\times 3^7 \times 12! \times 2^{10}$$

Computing:
- $8! = 40{,}320$
- $3^7 = 2{,}187$
- $12! = 479{,}001{,}600$
- $2^{10} = 1{,}024$

$$|G| = 40{,}320 \times 2{,}187 \times 479{,}001{,}600 \times 1{,}024 = 43{,}252
{,}003{,}274{,}489{,}856{,}000$$

### Step 2: Color Scheme Factor

A standard Rubik's Cube has a fixed color scheme: 6 distinct colors assigned to
6 faces, with 3 fixed pairs of opposite colors. The problem states that **colors
 are allowed to be adjacent or opposite** — meaning we lift the standard opposit
e-pair restriction and allow **any** assignment of 6 colors to 6 faces.

The number of ways to assign 6 distinct colors to 6 faces is $6! = 720$.

Since a "color pattern" on the cube should not depend on the cube's orientation
in space, we identify color schemes that differ only by a rotation of the entire
 cube. The rotation group of the cube has **24 elements**.

By **Burnside's lemma**, the number of distinct color schemes up to rotation is:

$$\text{Distinct schemes} = \frac{1}{|R|} \sum_{g \in R} |\text{Fix}(g)|$$

where $R$ is the rotation group (24 elements). Since all 6 colors are **distinct
**, any non-identity rotation (which permutes at least some faces non-trivially)
 cannot fix any coloring. Therefore:

- Identity: $|\text{Fix}(e)| = 6! = 720$
- All 23 non-identity rotations: $|\text{Fix}(g)| = 0$

$$\text{Distinct color schemes} = \frac{720}{24} = 30$$

### Step 3: Combining Both Factors

Each distinct color scheme defines a different assignment of colors to the 6 cen
ter positions. For each fixed color scheme, the corner and edge pieces can reach
 exactly $|G|$ configurations through legal moves (twisting corners, flipping ed
ges, permuting pieces).

Since different color schemes produce genuinely different color patterns (the ce
nter colors differ), the total number of unique color patterns is:

$$\text{Total} = 30 \times |G| = 30 \times 43{,}252{,}003{,}274{,}489{,}856{,}00
0$$
