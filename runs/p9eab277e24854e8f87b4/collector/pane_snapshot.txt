## Proof

### 1. Setup and notation

In the simple linear regression $y_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x_i + \varepsilon_i$, t
he design matrix is

$$
\mathbf{X} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & x_1 \\ 1 & x_2 \\ \vdots & \vdots \\ 1 & x_n \e
nd{pmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times 2},
$$

so

$$
\hat Q_{xx} := \frac{\mathbf{X}^T\mathbf{X}}{n} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \bar x \\
\bar x & \overline{x^2} \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\bar x := \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n x_i,\quad \overline{x^2} := \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n x_
i^2.
$$

Assume the standard regularity conditions for cross-sectional regression:

- **(R1) i.i.d. sampling**: $\{(x_i,\varepsilon_i)\}_{i=1}^n$ are i.i.d. with $E
[\varepsilon_i\mid x_i]=0$.
- **(R2) finite second moments**: $E[x_i^2]<\infty$ (so $E[x_i^4]<\infty$ is *no
t* needed for the rate of $\hat Q_{xx}$ itself, but $E[x_i^4]<\infty$ gives the
CLT for $\overline{x^2}$; we assume $E[x_i^4]<\infty$).
- **(R3) non-degeneracy**: $Q_{xx}:=E[\mathbf{x}_i\mathbf{x}_i^T]$ is positive d
efinite, where $\mathbf{x}_i=(1,x_i)^T$. Equivalently, $\operatorname{Var}(x_i)>
0$, which makes $\det Q_{xx}=\operatorname{Var}(x_i)>0$.

Under (R1)–(R3),

$$
Q_{xx} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \mu_x \\ \mu_x & E[x_i^2] \end{pmatrix},
\qquad \mu_x := E[x_i].
$$

### 2. Rate of $\hat Q_{xx} - Q_{xx}$

By the Law of Large Numbers (LLN), $\bar x \stackrel{p}{\to} \mu_x$ and $\overli
ne{x^2}\stackrel{p}{\to} E[x_i^2]$, giving $\hat Q_{xx}\stackrel{p}{\to} Q_{xx}$
.

By the Central Limit Theorem (CLT), since $E[x_i^2]<\infty$ and $E[x_i^4]<\infty

$$
\sqrt n\,(\bar x - \mu_x) \stackrel{d}{\to} N\!\bigl(0,\operatorname{Var}(x_i)\b

\qquad
\sqrt n\,\bigl(\overline{x^2}-E[x_i^2]\bigr) \stackrel{d}{\to} N\!\bigl(0,\opera
torname{Var}(x_i^2)\bigr).
$$

Convergence in distribution to a non-degenerate limit implies boundedness in pro
bability, so

$$
\bar x - \mu_x = O_p(n^{-1/2}), \qquad \overline{x^2}-E[x_i^2]=O_p(n^{-1/2}).
$$

The constant entry "$1$" equals its limit exactly (rate $0$). Therefore each ent

$$
\boxed{\;\hat Q_{xx}-Q_{xx}=O_p(n^{-1/2}).\;} \tag{1}
$$

### 3. Linearization of the inverse map

The map $A\mapsto A^{-1}$ is continuously differentiable on the open set of inve
rtible matrices, with Fréchet derivative at $A$ given by the linear map

$$
D(A^{-1})[H] = -A^{-1} H A^{-1}.
$$

Since $\hat Q_{xx}\stackrel{p}{\to} Q_{xx}$ and $Q_{xx}$ is invertible (by R3, $
\det Q_{xx}=\operatorname{Var}(x_i)>0$), $\hat Q_{xx}$ is invertible with probab
ility tending to 1. Applying the first-order (mean-value / Neumann-series) expan
sion of the inverse around $Q_{xx}$:

$$
\hat Q_{xx}^{-1} - Q_{xx}^{-1}
= -Q_{xx}^{-1}\bigl(\hat Q_{xx}-Q_{xx}\bigr)Q_{xx}^{-1}
+ R_n, \tag{2}
$$

where the remainder satisfies, for some constant $C$ depending only on $Q_{xx}$,

$$
\|R_n\| \le C\,\|\hat Q_{xx}-Q_{xx}\|^2.
$$
