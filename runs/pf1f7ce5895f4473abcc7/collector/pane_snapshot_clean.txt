## Proof

We seek a first-order differential operator $D$ (with Clifford-algebra-valued co
efficients) such that $D^2 = \Box$.

**The Clifford algebra relations.** The elements $\gamma_0, \gamma_1, \gamma_2,
\gamma_3$ satisfy
$$\gamma_0^2 = 1, \qquad \gamma_1^2 = \gamma_2^2 = \gamma_3^2 = -1,$$
and for $\mu \neq \nu$ they anticommute: $\gamma_\mu \gamma_\nu = -\gamma_\nu \g
amma_\mu$, i.e. $\{\gamma_\mu, \gamma_\nu\} = 0$. Equivalently $\{\gamma_\mu, \g
amma_\nu\} = 2\eta_{\mu\nu}$ with $\eta = \mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$.

**Define the candidate operator (the Dirac operator).** Let
$$\boxed{D \;=\; \gamma_0\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t} \;+\; \gamma_1\,\frac{\pa
rtial}{\partial x} \;+\; \gamma_2\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y} \;+\; \gamma_3\,\
frac{\partial}{\partial z}.}$$

**Compute $D^2$.** Expanding the square, the partial derivatives commute among t
hemselves, so the only nontrivial algebra comes from the $\gamma_\mu$:

$$D^2 = \sum_{\mu,\nu} \gamma_\mu \gamma_\nu\, \partial_\mu \partial_\nu = \sum_
{\mu} \gamma_\mu^2\, \partial_\mu^2 \;+\; \sum_{\mu < \nu} \{\gamma_\mu, \gamma_
\nu\}\,\partial_\mu \partial_\nu.$$

By the anticommutation relations, every cross term $\{\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu\} =
0$ for $\mu \neq \nu$, so all mixed partials vanish. The diagonal terms give, us
ing $(\partial_0, \partial_1, \partial_2, \partial_3) = (\partial_t, \partial_x,
 \partial_y, \partial_z)$:

$$D^2 = \gamma_0^2\,\partial_t^2 + \gamma_1^2\,\partial_x^2 + \gamma_2^2\,\parti
al_y^2 + \gamma_3^2\,\partial_z^2.$$

Substituting the squares of the $\gamma_\mu$:

$$D^2 = (1)\,\partial_t^2 + (-1)\,\partial_x^2 + (-1)\,\partial_y^2 + (-1)\,\par
tial_z^2 = \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)^2 - \nabla^2 = \Box.$$

**Conclusion.** The operator
$$D = \gamma_0\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t} + \gamma_1\,\frac{\partial}{\partial
 x} + \gamma_2\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y} + \gamma_3\,\frac{\partial}{\partial
 z}$$
is a square root of the wave operator: $D^2 = \Box$. This is precisely the **Dir
ac operator**, and its construction is the essential step in passing from the se
cond-order Klein–Gordon/wave equation to the first-order Dirac equation — the Cl
ifford algebra's anticommutation relations are exactly what is needed to make th
e cross terms cancel and reproduce the Lorentzian signature $(+,-,-,-)$ of $\Box
$.
