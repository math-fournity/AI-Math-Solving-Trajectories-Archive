# Cauchy Integral Formula for Several Complex Variables

## Setup

Let $f(z)$ be analytic in a domain $D \subset \mathbb{C}^n$, and let $z^0 = (z_1
^0, \ldots, z_n^0) \in D$. The **polycylinder** (polydisc) centered at $z^0$ wit
h polyradius $r = (r_1, \ldots, r_n)$, where each $r_j > 0$, is

$$C(r, z^0) = \{ z \in \mathbb{C}^n : |z_j - z_j^0| < r_j,\ j = 1, \ldots, n \}.
$$

Its **distinguished boundary** (the Shilov boundary) is the $n$-fold product of
the boundary circles:

$$\Gamma(r, z^0) = \{ z \in \mathbb{C}^n : |z_j - z_j^0| = r_j,\ j = 1, \ldots,
n \}.$$

We assume $C(r, z^0) \subset D$ (shrinking the radii if necessary so the closed
polydisc lies in $D$).

## Derivation

Since $f$ is analytic in $D$, it is separately analytic in each variable $z_j$ w
hen the others are held fixed (this is the definition of analyticity in several
complex variables: $f$ is continuous and separately holomorphic, which by Hartog
s's theorem is equivalent to joint holomorphy).

Fix a point $\zeta = (\zeta_1, \ldots, \zeta_n) \in C(r, z^0)$, so $|\zeta_j - z
_j^0| < r_j$ for every $j$.

**Step 1 — Apply the one-variable formula in $z_1$.** Holding $z_2, \ldots, z_n$
 fixed at values on their respective boundary circles $|z_j - z_j^0| = r_j$, the
 function $z_1 \mapsto f(z_1, z_2, \ldots, z_n)$ is holomorphic in $|z_1 - z_1^0
| < r_1$. The one-variable Cauchy formula gives

$$f(\zeta_1, z_2, \ldots, z_n) = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_{|z_1 - z_1^0| = r_1} \f
rac{f(z_1, z_2, \ldots, z_n)}{z_1 - \zeta_1}\, dz_1.$$

**Step 2 — Apply the one-variable formula in $z_2$.** Now $f(\zeta_1, z_2, \ldot
s, z_n)$ is holomorphic in $z_2$ (with the remaining variables on their boundary
 circles). Applying the one-variable formula in $z_2$:

$$f(\zeta_1, \zeta_2, z_3, \ldots, z_n) = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_{|z_2 - z_2^0|
= r_2} \frac{f(\zeta_1, z_2, \ldots, z_n)}{z_2 - \zeta_2}\, dz_2.$$

Substituting the expression from Step 1:

$$f(\zeta_1, \zeta_2, z_3, \ldots, z_n) = \frac{1}{(2\pi i)^2} \oint_{|z_2 - z_2
^0| = r_2} \oint_{|z_1 - z_1^0| = r_1} \frac{f(z_1, z_2, \ldots, z_n)}{(z_1 - \z
eta_1)(z_2 - \zeta_2)}\, dz_1\, dz_2.$$

**Step 3 — Iterate over all $n$ variables.** Repeating this procedure for $z_3,
z_4, \ldots, z_n$ in succession, each time applying the one-variable Cauchy form
ula to the function obtained by fixing the already-resolved variables at $\zeta_
j$ and the not-yet-resolved variables on their boundary circles, we obtain:

$$\boxed{f(\zeta) = \frac{1}{(2\pi i)^n} \int_{|z_n - z_n^0| = r_n} \cdots \int_
{|z_1 - z_1^0| = r_1} \frac{f(z_1, \ldots, z_n)}{(z_1 - \zeta_1)\cdots(z_n - \ze
ta_n)}\, dz_1 \cdots dz_n.}$$

Equivalently, writing the iterated integral as an integral over the distinguishe
d boundary $\Gamma(r, z^0)$ with the product measure:

$$f(\zeta) = \frac{1}{(2\pi i)^n} \int_{\Gamma(r, z^0)} \frac{f(z)}{\prod_{j=1}^
{n}(z_j - \zeta_j)}\, dz_1 \cdots dz_n.$$

## Justification of the iteration (Fubini / continuity)

At each step, the integrand is continuous on the product of the boundary circles
 (since $f$ is continuous on the closed polydisc and the denominators $z_j - \ze
ta_j$ are bounded away from zero because $|\zeta_j - z_j^0| < r_j = |z_j - z_j^0
|$). Therefore Fubini's theorem applies and the iterated integrals may be interc
hanged freely; the order of integration is immaterial. The continuity of $f$ on
the closed polydisc—which is guaranteed because $f$ is analytic (hence continuou
s) on the open set $D \supset \overline{C(r,z^0)}$—is exactly what makes the one
-variable Cauchy formula applicable at each stage.

## Differentiation under the integral: derivatives formula

Because the kernel $\prod_j (z_j - \zeta_j)^{-1}$ is smooth in $\zeta$ inside th
e polydisc and the integration is over a compact set bounded away from $\zeta$,
we may differentiate under the integral sign arbitrarily. For any multi-index $\
alpha = (\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n) \in \mathbb{N}^n$:

$$\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|} f}{\partial z_1^{\alpha_1} \cdots \partial z_n^{\alp
ha_n}}(\zeta) = \frac{\alpha_1! \cdots \alpha_n!}{(2\pi i)^n} \int_{\Gamma(r, z^
0)} \frac{f(z)}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z_j - \zeta_j)^{\alpha_j + 1}}\, dz_1 \cdots dz_
n.$$

## Consequences

1. **Analyticity implies joint holomorphy.** The formula shows $f$ is represente
d by a convergent power series (expand each factor $(z_j - \zeta_j)^{-1}$ as a g
eometric series in $(z_j^0 - \zeta_j)/(z_j - z_j^0)$, which converges uniformly
since $|\zeta_j - z_j^0| < r_j = |z_j - z_j^0|$). This yields the Taylor expansi
on

$$f(z) = \sum_{\alpha \in \mathbb{N}^n} \frac{\partial^{|\alpha|} f(z^0)}{\parti
al z_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots\partial z_n^{\alpha_n}} \frac{(z_1 - z_1^0)^{\alpha_1}\c
dots(z_n - z_n^0)^{\alpha_n}}{\alpha_1!\cdots\alpha_n!},$$

converging uniformly on compact sub-polydiscs. Hence separate analyticity + cont
inuity (Hartogs) gives full joint analyticity, and the Cauchy formula is consist
ent.

2. **Maximum principle.** From the integral representation, $|f(\zeta)| \leq \ma
x_{\Gamma}|f|$, with equality only when $f$ is constant—giving the maximum modul
us principle on the polydisc.

3. **Mean-value property.** Setting all $\zeta_j = z_j^0$ recovers the mean-valu
e property over the torus $\Gamma$.

This completes the derivation of the Cauchy integral formula for analytic functi
ons of several complex variables on a polycylinder.
