Looking at this problem, I need to find the number of ways to place chips $1, 2,
 \ldots, 2018$ at the vertices of a regular 2018-gon such that for every pair of
 adjacent ports, the sum of their chips equals the sum at the antipodal pair.

## Proof

Label the ports $0, 1, 2, \ldots, 2017$ cyclically. Since $2018 = 2 \times 1009$
, the antipode of port $i$ is port $i + 1009 \pmod{2018}$. Let $a_i$ denote the
chip at port $i$, so $(a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_{2017})$ is a permutation of $\{1, 2,
 \ldots, 2018\}$.

**The balance condition.** For every $i$ (indices mod 2018), ports $i$ and $i+1$
 are adjacent, and their antipodal ports are $i+1009$ and $i+1010$. The conditio
n requires:
$$a_i + a_{i+1} = a_{i+1009} + a_{i+1010} \quad \text{for all } i. \tag{1}$$

**Introducing antipodal differences.** Define $d_i = a_i - a_{i+1009}$ for $i =
0, 1, \ldots, 1008$. Rewriting (1):
$$a_i - a_{i+1009} = a_{i+1010} - a_{i+1}.$$

The right side is $a_{(i+1)+1009} - a_{i+1} = -(a_{i+1} - a_{(i+1)+1009}) = -d_{
i+1}$. Therefore:
$$d_i = -d_{i+1} \quad \text{for all } i, \text{ with indices mod } 1009. \tag{2
}$$

**The key parity argument.** Applying (2) repeatedly:
$$d_0 = -d_1 = d_2 = -d_3 = \cdots = (-1)^k\, d_k.$$

Since $1009$ is **odd**, going all the way around the cycle ($k = 1009$) gives:
$$d_0 = (-1)^{1009}\, d_{1009} = -d_0,$$
where $d_{1009} = d_0$ (indices mod 1009). This forces $d_0 = 0$, and consequent
ly $d_i = 0$ for all $i$.

**Contradiction.** $d_i = 0$ means $a_i = a_{i+1009}$ for every $i = 0, 1, \ldot
s, 1008$. But the $a_i$ form a permutation of $\{1, 2, \ldots, 2018\}$ — all val
ues are distinct. Having $a_i = a_{i+1009}$ means each of the 1009 values $a_0,
\ldots, a_{1008}$ appears at least twice, which is impossible for a permutation
of 2018 distinct numbers.

**Conclusion.** No arrangement satisfies the balance condition. Since there are
zero valid arrangements, the count up to rotation is also zero.
