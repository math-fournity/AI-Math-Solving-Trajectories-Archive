## Solution

We evaluate $\int_{\gamma} \tanh(z)\,dz$ where $\gamma(t)=e^{it}$, $0\le t\le 2\
pi$, is the unit circle $|z|=1$ traversed once counterclockwise.

**Singularities of $\tanh(z)$.** Since $\tanh(z)=\dfrac{\sinh z}{\cosh z}$ and $
\sinh, \cosh$ are entire, the only singularities are poles at the zeros of $\cos
h(z)$. Now
$$\cosh z = 0 \iff e^{z}+e^{-z}=0 \iff e^{2z}=-1 \iff 2z = i\pi(2k+1),\quad k\in
\mathbb{Z},$$
so the poles are at
$$z_k = i\pi\!\left(k+\tfrac{1}{2}\right),\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}.$$

**Locating poles relative to the contour.** The moduli are
$$|z_k| = \pi\left|k+\tfrac{1}{2}\right|.$$
The smallest value is attained at $k=0$ and $k=-1$, giving $|z_0|=|z_{-1}|=\pi/2
\approx 1.5708$. Since
$$\frac{\pi}{2} > 1,$$
**every** pole of $\tanh(z)$ lies strictly outside the unit circle $|z|=1$. In p
articular, $\tanh(z)$ is holomorphic on an open neighborhood of the closed disk
$\overline{D}=\{z:|z|\le 1\}$ (e.g. on $|z|<\pi/2$).

**Applying the residue theorem.** The residue theorem states
$$\int_{\gamma} \tanh(z)\,dz = 2\pi i \sum_{\substack{k\in\mathbb{Z}\\ |z_k|<1}}
 \operatorname{Res}(\tanh, z_k).$$
The sum on the right is empty, since no pole satisfies $|z_k|<1$. Hence
$$\int_{\gamma} \tanh(z)\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot 0 = 0.$$

**Verification via Cauchy's theorem.** Equivalently, because $\tanh(z)$ is holom
orphic on the simply connected domain $|z|<\pi/2$ which contains $\gamma$ and it
s interior, Cauchy's theorem directly gives $\int_{\gamma}\tanh(z)\,dz = 0$, con
firming the residue-theorem computation.

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \int_{\gamma} \tanh(z)\,dz = 0}$$
