## Proof

**Interpretation.** In the context of a normed vector space, "functionals" means
 linear functionals $E \to \mathbb{R}$. The answer is **yes**: $f$ must be a con
vex combination of $g_1, \ldots, g_n$.

---

**Theorem.** Let $E$ be a normed vector space and let $f, g_1, \ldots, g_n$ be l
inear functionals on $E$. If for every $x \in E$,
$$f(x) \in \operatorname{conv}\{g_1(x), \ldots, g_n(x)\},$$
then there exist $\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_n \geq 0$ with $\sum_{i=1}^n \lambd
a_i = 1$ such that $f = \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i g_i$.

---

**Proof.**

**Step 1 (Reduction to finite dimensions).** Define the linear map
$$T: E \to \mathbb{R}^{n+1}, \qquad T(x) = \bigl(f(x),\, g_1(x),\, \ldots,\, g_n
(x)\bigr).$$
Since $f, g_1, \ldots, g_n$ are linear, $T$ is linear, and $S := T(E)$ is a line
ar subspace of $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$, hence **finite-dimensional**.

For $i = 0, 1, \ldots, n$, let $\pi_i : S \to \mathbb{R}$ denote the $(i{+}1)$-s
t coordinate projection. The hypothesis becomes:
$$\pi_0(s) \in \operatorname{conv}\{\pi_1(s), \ldots, \pi_n(s)\} \quad \text{for
 all } s \in S. \tag{$*$}$$

Since the convex hull of finitely many real numbers is the interval $[\min, \max
]$, condition $(*)$ is equivalent to:
$$\min_{1 \le i \le n} \pi_i(s) \;\le\; \pi_0(s) \;\le\; \max_{1 \le i \le n} \p
i_i(s) \quad \text{for all } s \in S. \tag{$**$}$$

**Step 2 (Separation argument).** Consider the set
$$C := \operatorname{conv}\{\pi_1, \ldots, \pi_n\} \subseteq S^*.$$
This is the convex hull of finitely many points in the dual space $S^*$, so $C$
is a **compact** convex subset of the finite-dimensional vector space $S^*$.

**Claim:** $\pi_0 \in C$.

Suppose for contradiction that $\pi_0 \notin C$. Since $C$ is nonempty, compact,
 and convex, and $\pi_0$ is a point outside $C$ in the finite-dimensional space
$S^*$, the **strict separating hyperplane theorem** provides a linear functional
 $\ell \in S^{**}$ such that
$$\ell(\pi_0) \;>\; \max_{\varphi \in C}\, \ell(\varphi) \;=\; \max_{1 \le i \le
 n}\, \ell(\pi_i).$$

Since $S$ is finite-dimensional, it is **reflexive**: $S^{**} \cong S$. Concrete
ly, every $\ell \in S^{**}$ is evaluation at some point $s_0 \in S$, i.e., $\ell
(\varphi) = \varphi(s_0)$ for all $\varphi \in S^*$. Substituting:
$$\pi_0(s_0) \;>\; \max_{1 \le i \le n}\, \pi_i(s_0).$$

This directly contradicts $(**)$. Hence $\pi_0 \in C$.

**Step 3 (Conclusion).** Since $\pi_0 \in C = \operatorname{conv}\{\pi_1, \ldots
, \pi_n\}$, there exist $\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_n \ge 0$ with $\sum \lambda_
i = 1$ such that
$$\pi_0 = \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i\, \pi_i \quad \text{as linear functionals on }
S.$$

Now for any $x \in E$, we have $T(x) \in S$, so:
$$f(x) = \pi_0\bigl(T(x)\bigr) = \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i\, \pi_i\bigl(T(x)\bigr)
= \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i\, g_i(x).$$

Since this holds for all $x \in E$, we conclude
$$f = \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i\, g_i,$$
a convex combination of $g_1, \ldots, g_n$. $\blacksquare$

---

**Key ideas.** The proof hinges on two reductions:
1. **Finite-dimensional reduction:** The map $T$ collapses the (possibly infinit
e-dimensional) space $E$ into a finite-dimensional subspace $S \subseteq \mathbb
{R}^{n+1}$, where all the action happens.
2. **Separation:** If $f$ were *not* a convex combination of the $g_i$, the sepa
rating hyperplane theorem would produce a single point $s_0 \in S$ (i.e., a sing
le $x \in E$) at which $f$ exceeds all $g_i$ simultaneously — contradicting the
pointwise convex hull condition.

The norm on $E$ plays no role; the result holds for any vector space with linear
 functionals.
