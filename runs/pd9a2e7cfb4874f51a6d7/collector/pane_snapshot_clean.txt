## Proof

Let $R = k[x]/\langle x^2 \rangle$. This is a local commutative algebra with max
imal ideal $\mathfrak m = (x)$, residue field $R/\mathfrak m \cong k$, and $\mat
hfrak m^2 = 0$. We compute $\operatorname{Ext}_R(k,k)$ via a projective resoluti
on of the simple module $k = R/(x)$.

### Step 1. A free resolution of $k$

Consider the infinite complex
$$
\cdots \xrightarrow{\;\cdot x\;} R \xrightarrow{\;\cdot x\;} R \xrightarrow{\;\c
dot x\;} R \xrightarrow{\;\pi\;} k \longrightarrow 0,
$$
where $\pi: R \to k$ is the quotient map and every differential in the free part
 is multiplication by $x$.

**Exactness.** Since $x^2 = 0$ in $R$, the composition of two consecutive multip
lication-by-$x$ maps is zero, so $\operatorname{im}(\cdot x) \subseteq \ker(\cdo
t x)$. For the reverse inclusion, write $r = a + bx \in R$ with $a,b\in k$. Then
 $xr = ax$, so $\ker(\cdot x) = \{r : a = 0\} = k\cdot x = \operatorname{im}(\cd
ot x)$. Likewise $\ker\pi = (x) = \operatorname{im}(\cdot x)$. Hence the complex
 is exact everywhere, giving a free (hence projective) resolution $P_\bullet \to
 k$.

### Step 2. Apply $\operatorname{Hom}_R(-,k)$

Since $R$ is free of rank 1 over itself, $\operatorname{Hom}_R(R, k) \cong k$ vi
a $f \mapsto f(1)$. The differential $d_n: P_n \to P_{n-1}$ is multiplication by
 $x$, so the induced differential
$$
d_n^*: \operatorname{Hom}_R(P_{n-1}, k) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}_R(P_n
, k)
$$
sends $f$ to $f \circ (\cdot x)$. For $f$ determined by $f(1) = a \in k$,
$$
(f \circ (\cdot x))(1) = f(x) = f(x\cdot 1) = x\cdot f(1) = x\cdot a = 0,
$$
because $x$ acts trivially on $k = R/(x)$. Thus **every induced differential is
the zero map**, and the cochain complex is
$$
0 \longrightarrow k \xrightarrow{\;0\;} k \xrightarrow{\;0\;} k \xrightarrow{\;0
\;} k \xrightarrow{\;0\;} \cdots
$$

### Step 3. Cohomology

Since all differentials vanish,
$$
\operatorname{Ext}^n_R(k,k) = \ker d_n^* / \operatorname{im} d_{n-1}^* = k / 0 \
cong k \qquad \text{for all } n \geq 0.
$$

### Step 4. Ring structure (Yoneda product)

The resolution is *1-periodic*: every differential is the same map $\cdot x$. Th
e degree-1 generator $\varepsilon \in \operatorname{Ext}^1_R(k,k)$ is represente
d by the short exact sequence
$$
0 \longrightarrow k \xrightarrow{\;\cdot x\;} R \xrightarrow{\;\pi\;} k \longrig
htarrow 0
$$
(where $k \hookrightarrow R$ sends $1 \mapsto x$, landing in the socle $\operato
rname{ann}_R(x) = (x)$). Splicing this extension with itself $n$ times reproduce
s $n$ consecutive segments of the periodic resolution, so the Yoneda product $\v
arepsilon^n$ is the generator of $\operatorname{Ext}^n_R(k,k)$. There are no rel
ations (the resolution never terminates and no splicing becomes trivial), hence
$$
\operatorname{Ext}_R(k,k) \cong k[\varepsilon], \qquad |\varepsilon| = 1,
$$
a **polynomial algebra on one generator of degree $1$**.

### Conclusion

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Ext}_{k[x]/\langle x^2\rangle}(k,k) \;\cong\; k[\varepsilon
]\ \text{with }|\varepsilon|=1, \quad\text{i.e. } \operatorname{Ext}^n \cong k \
text{ for every } n\geq 0.}$$
