# Solution

## Setup

Let $\mathcal{D}$ be the unit disc. Player A picks $p_0 \in \mathcal{D}$. At ste
p $n$, B picks a line $\ell_n$ through $p_{n-1}$ (direction $\alpha_n$ uniform o
n $[0,\pi)$), and A picks $p_n$ uniformly on the chord $\mathcal{D}\cap\ell_n$.
B wins iff $(p_n)$ converges.

We prove $P(\text{B wins}) = 0$.

## Key estimate: step sizes stay large

Let $\mathcal{F}_n = \sigma(p_0,\dots,p_n)$ and $A_n = \{|p_n - p_{n-1}| \geq 1/
2\}$.

**Claim.** There exists $p_0 > 0$ such that $P(A_n \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1}) \geq
p_0$ for all $n$, deterministically (uniformly over all positions $p_{n-1}$).

*Proof of claim.* Fix $r = |p_{n-1}| \in [0,1]$. Let $\alpha$ be the angle betwe
en $\ell_n$ and the radial direction. Parametrize $\ell_n$ as $p_{n-1} + t\,u$ (
$u$ unit). The chord is $t \in [t_1, t_2]$ where
$$t_{1,2} = -r\cos\alpha \mp \sqrt{1 - r^2\sin^2\alpha},$$
and the step size is $|p_n - p_{n-1}| = |t|$ with $t \sim \mathrm{Uniform}[t_1,t
_2]$.

Restrict to $\alpha \in [0, \pi/6]$ (chosen by B with probability $1/6$):
- $\sin\alpha \leq 1/2 \Rightarrow 1 - r^2\sin^2\alpha \geq 3/4 \Rightarrow \sqr
t{1-r^2\sin^2\alpha} \geq \sqrt{3}/2$.
- $t_1 = -r\cos\alpha - \sqrt{1-r^2\sin^2\alpha} \leq -\sqrt{3}/2 < -1/2$.
- The interval $[t_1,\, -1/2] \subset [t_1, t_2]$ has length $\geq \sqrt{3}/2 -
1/2 = (\sqrt{3}-1)/2$.
- Chord length $t_2 - t_1 = 2\sqrt{1-r^2\sin^2\alpha} \leq 2$.

So $P(|t| \geq 1/2 \mid r,\alpha) \geq P(t \leq -1/2) \geq \frac{(\sqrt{3}-1)/2}
{2} = \frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{4}$.

Averaging over B's direction:
$$P(A_n \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1}) \geq \frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{4} = \fra
c{\sqrt{3}-1}{24} =: p_0 > 0,$$
uniformly over all $r \in [0,1]$. $\square$

## Applying conditional Borel–Cantelli

By Lévy's conditional Borel–Cantelli lemma: since
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P(A_n \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1}) \geq \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} p_0
 = \infty \quad \text{surely},$$
the events $A_n$ occur **infinitely often** almost surely.

## Conclusion

Therefore $|p_n - p_{n-1}| \geq 1/2$ for infinitely many $n$, a.s., so $|p_n - p
_{n-1}| \not\to 0$. Since any convergent sequence satisfies $|p_n - p_{n-1}| \to
 0$, the sequence $(p_n)$ does not converge almost surely.

$$\boxed{P(\mathcal{B}\text{ wins}) = 0}$$
