## Proof

Assume $X$ is Hausdorff and $Y\subseteq X$ with both $Y$ and $X\setminus Y$ dens

$$y \in U \subseteq K,$$

and $K$ is compact in $Y$.

Since $U$ is open in $Y$, there exists an open set $V$ in $X$ with $U = V\cap Y$
 and $y\in V$.

**Step 1: $K$ is closed in $X$.** The inclusion map $\iota:Y\hookrightarrow X$ i
s continuous, so the image $\iota(K)=K$ of the compact set $K$ is compact in $X$

$ is closed in $X$.

**Step 2: The closure of $V\cap Y$ in $X$ is contained in $K$.** We have $V\cap
Y = U \subseteq K$, and $K$ is closed in $X$, so

$$\overline{V\cap Y}^{\,X} \subseteq K.$$

**Step 3: $V\subseteq \overline{V\cap Y}^{\,X}$.** Because $Y$ is dense in $X$,
every nonempty open subset of $X$ meets $Y$. In particular, every nonempty open
subset $W$ of $V$ (which is also open in $X$) satisfies $W\cap Y\neq\emptyset$,
i.e. $W\cap(V\cap Y)\neq\emptyset$. Thus $V\cap Y$ is dense in $V$, which gives
$V\subseteq \overline{V\cap Y}^{\,X}$.

**Step 4: Contradiction.** Combining Steps 2 and 3,

$$V \subseteq \overline{V\cap Y}^{\,X} \subseteq K \subseteq Y.$$

So $V\subseteq Y$, meaning $V\cap (X\setminus Y)=\emptyset$. But $V$ is a nonemp

every nonempty open subset of $X$ must intersect $X\setminus Y$. This is a contr
adiction.

Therefore no point of $Y$ can have a compact neighborhood in $Y$. $\blacksquare$
