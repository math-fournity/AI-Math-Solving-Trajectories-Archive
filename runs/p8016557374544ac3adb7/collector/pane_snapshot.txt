# Proof

**Theorem.** Every uncountable totally ordered set can be partitioned into two u
ncountable subsets (without assuming completeness of the order).

**Proof.** Let $(L,{<})$ be an uncountable totally ordered set.

---

**Case 1.** There exists $c\in L$ such that both $L_{<c}:=\{x\in L:x<c\}$ and $L
_{>c}:=\{x\in L:x>c\}$ are uncountable.

Set $A=L_{<c}$ and $B=L\setminus A=\{x\in L:x\geq c\}$. Then $A$ is uncountable
by assumption, and $B\supseteq L_{>c}$ is uncountable. Done.

---

**Case 2.** For every $c\in L$, at least one of $L_{<c}$, $L_{>c}$ is countable.

Define:
$$L^{-}=\{c\in L:|L_{<c}|\le\aleph_{0}\},\qquad L^{+}=\{c\in L:|L_{>c}|\le\aleph
_{0}\}.$$

By the case assumption, $L=L^{-}\cup L^{+}$. Since $L$ is uncountable, at least
one of $L^{-},L^{+}$ is uncountable (a union of two countable sets is countable)
.

**Subcase 2a: $L^{-}$ is uncountable.**

We build a strictly increasing sequence $\langle a_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\ra
ngle$ in $L^{-}$ by transfinite recursion:

- Pick any $a_{0}\in L^{-}$.
- **Successor step:** given $a_{\alpha}$, pick $a_{\alpha+1}\in L^{-}$ with $a_{
\alpha+1}>a_{\alpha}$.
- **Limit step:** for a countable limit ordinal $\lambda$, given $\langle a_{\be
ta}:\beta<\lambda\rangle$, pick $a_{\lambda}\in L^{-}$ with $a_{\lambda}>a_{\bet
a}$ for all $\beta<\lambda$.

*The construction never gets stuck.* At stage $\alpha<\omega_{1}$, the set $S_{\
alpha}=\{a_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}$ is countable (since $\alpha$ is a countable o
rdinal). We need an element of $L^{-}$ above every element of $S_{\alpha}$.

Suppose for contradiction that no such element exists, i.e., $S_{\alpha}$ is cof
inal in $L^{-}$. Then:
$$L^{-}=\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}\{x\in L^{-}:x\le a_{\beta}\}.$$
For each $\beta<\alpha$, we have $\{x\in L^{-}:x\le a_{\beta}\}\subseteq L_{<a_{
\beta}}\cup\{a_{\beta}\}$, which is countable because $a_{\beta}\in L^{-}$. Sinc
e $\alpha<\omega_{1}$ is countable, this is a countable union of countable sets—
hence countable, contradicting $L^{-}$ being uncountable.

Therefore the construction succeeds for all $\alpha<\omega_{1}$, yielding a subs
et $W=\{a_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}\subseteq L^{-}$ order-isomorphic to $\ome
ga_{1}$.

**Partition $W$.** Let
$$W_{\mathrm{lim}}=\{a_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1},\ \alpha\text{ is a limit ordi
nal}\},\qquad W_{\mathrm{succ}}=\{a_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1},\ \alpha\text{ is
 a successor ordinal or }0\}.$$

Both are uncountable:
- The map $\alpha\mapsto\alpha+1$ injects $\omega_{1}$ into the successor ordina
ls below $\omega_{1}$, so $|W_{\mathrm{succ}}|=\aleph_{1}$.
- The limit ordinals are cofinal in $\omega_{1}$ (for any $\alpha<\omega_{1}$, $
\alpha+\omega<\omega_{1}$ is a limit ordinal above $\alpha$), so $W_{\mathrm{lim
}}$ is uncountable (a countable cofinal subset of $\omega_{1}$ would have counta
ble supremum, contradicting $\operatorname{cf}(\omega_{1})=\aleph_{1}$).

Set $A=W_{\mathrm{lim}}$ and $B=L\setminus A$. Then $A$ is uncountable, $B\supse
teq W_{\mathrm{succ}}$ is uncountable, and $A\cup B=L$, $A\cap B=\emptyset$. Don
e.

**Subcase 2b: $L^{-}$ is countable, so $L^{+}$ is uncountable.**

Apply Subcase 2a to the **reverse order** ${>}$. In the reversed order, the role
 of $L^{-}$ is played by $\{c\in L:|L_{>c}|\le\aleph_{0}\}=L^{+}$, which is unco
untable. The argument of Subcase 2a produces a partition of $L$ into two uncount
able subsets. $\square$

---

In every case, $L$ admits a partition into two uncountable subsets. The argument
 uses only the total order structure and the Axiom of Choice (for the transfinit
e recursion and the fact that countable unions of countable sets are countable)—
**no completeness assumption** on the order is needed. $\blacksquare$
