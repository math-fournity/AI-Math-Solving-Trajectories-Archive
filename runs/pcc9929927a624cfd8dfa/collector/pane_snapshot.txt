## Solution

The series in question is

$$S = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} 6^{2k} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} 36^k.$$

A series $\sum a_k$ converges in $\mathbb{Q}_p$ if and only if $a_k \to 0$ in th
e $p$-adic norm. Here $a_k = 36^k$. Since

$$36 = 2^2 \cdot 9, \qquad |36|_2 = 2^{-2} = \tfrac{1}{4},$$

we have $|36^k|_2 = (1/4)^k \to 0$ as $k \to \infty$. Hence the series converges

### Step 2: Apply the geometric series formula

In $\mathbb{Q}_p$, the identity $\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} r^k = \frac{1}{1-r}$ holds
whenever $|r|_p < 1$. Since $|36|_2 = 1/4 < 1$, we may apply it with $r = 36$:

$$S = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} 36^k = \frac{1}{1 - 36} = \frac{1}{-35} = -\frac{1}{35

We must check that $35$ is invertible in $\mathbb{Z}_2$. Since $35 = 5 \cdot 7$

### Step 4: Cross-check via partial sums

The $N$-th partial sum is

$$S_N = \sum_{k=0}^{N} 36^k = \frac{36^{N+1} - 1}{36 - 1} = \frac{36^{N+1} - 1}{
35}.$$

As $N \to \infty$, $36^{N+1} \to 0$ in $\mathbb{Q}_2$ (because $|36|_2 < 1$). Th
erefore

$$\lim_{N\to\infty} S_N = \frac{0 - 1}{35} = -\frac{1}{35},$$

The limit of the sequence $1 + 6^2 + 6^4 + \cdots$ in $\mathbb{Q}_2$ is

$$\boxed{-\dfrac{1}{35}}.$$
