## Answer

**False.**

**Counterexample.** Let $G = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, the cyclic group of order
$2$, so $n = |G| = 2$.

The alternating group $A_2$ consists of the even permutations on $\{1,2\}$. The
only permutations of $\{1,2\}$ are the identity and the transposition $(1\;2)$.
The identity is even; the transposition $(1\;2)$ is odd. Hence

$$A_2 = \{e\},$$

which is the trivial group of order $1$.

Since $|G| = 2 > 1 = |A_2|$, the group $G$ cannot be isomorphic to a subgroup of
 $A_2$ (a subgroup of a group of order $1$ has order at most $1$, but $G$ has or
der $2$).

Therefore it is **not** the case that every finite group $G$ of order $n$ is iso
morphic to a subgroup of $A_n$. $\blacksquare$

### PROOF COMPLETE
