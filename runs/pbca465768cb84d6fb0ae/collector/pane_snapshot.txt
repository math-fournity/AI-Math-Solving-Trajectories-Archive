## Solution

We solve Laplace's equation $\nabla^2 V = 0$ on the interior and exterior of the
 unit sphere, with boundary data
$$V(1,\theta,\phi) = v_0\,\sin^2\theta\cos 2\phi.$$

**Step 1 — Identify the spherical harmonic content of the boundary data.**

The real spherical harmonics of degree $l=2$ include
$$Y_2^2 = \sqrt{\tfrac{15}{32\pi}}\,\sin^2\theta\,e^{2i\phi},\qquad Y_2^{-2} = \
sqrt{\tfrac{15}{32\pi}}\,\sin^2\theta\,e^{-2i\phi}.$$
Hence
$$\sin^2\theta\cos 2\phi = \tfrac{1}{2}\sin^2\theta\bigl(e^{2i\phi}+e^{-2i\phi}\
bigr) = \sqrt{\tfrac{8\pi}{15}}\,\bigl(Y_2^2 + Y_2^{-2}\bigr).$$
So the boundary data is a pure $l=2$, $m=\pm 2$ mode.

**Step 2 — General separated solutions.**

The general harmonic solution regular at the origin is
$$V_{\text{in}} = \sum_{l,m} A_{lm}\,r^l\,Y_l^m(\theta,\phi),$$
and the general harmonic solution decaying at infinity is
$$V_{\text{out}} = \sum_{l,m} B_{lm}\,r^{-(l+1)}\,Y_l^m(\theta,\phi).$$

**Step 3 — Match the boundary at $r=1$.**

Inside: at $r=1$,
$$\sum_{l,m} A_{lm}Y_l^m = v_0\sqrt{\tfrac{8\pi}{15}}\,(Y_2^2+Y_2^{-2}).$$
By orthogonality, the only nonzero coefficients are
$$A_{2,2}=A_{2,-2}=v_0\sqrt{\tfrac{8\pi}{15}},\quad\text{all others }0.$$

Outside: at $r=1$,
$$\sum_{l,m} B_{lm}Y_l^m = v_0\sqrt{\tfrac{8\pi}{15}}\,(Y_2^2+Y_2^{-2}),$$
giving
$$B_{2,2}=B_{2,-2}=v_0\sqrt{\tfrac{8\pi}{15}},\quad\text{all others }0.$$

**Step 4 — Assemble the potentials.**

Inside ($0\le r<1$):
$$V_{\text{in}} = v_0\sqrt{\tfrac{8\pi}{15}}\,r^2\,(Y_2^2+Y_2^{-2}) = v_0\,r^2\,
\sin^2\theta\cos 2\phi.$$

Outside ($r>1$):
$$V_{\text{out}} = v_0\sqrt{\tfrac{8\pi}{15}}\,r^{-3}\,(Y_2^2+Y_2^{-2}) = v_0\,\
frac{\sin^2\theta\cos 2\phi}{r^3}.$$

**Verification.** Both are harmonic (each term $r^l Y_l^m$ and $r^{-(l+1)}Y_l^m$
 satisfies Laplace's equation). At $r=1$ both reduce to $v_0\sin^2\theta\cos 2\p
hi$. The interior solution is regular at $r=0$; the exterior solution decays as
$r\to\infty$. Uniqueness of the Dirichlet problem confirms these are the solutio
ns.

### Answers

**(a)** Inside the sphere ($0\le r<1$):
$$\boxed{\,V = v_0\,r^2\,\sin^2\theta\,\cos 2\phi\,}$$

**(b)** Outside the sphere ($r>1$):
$$\boxed{\,V = v_0\,\dfrac{\sin^2\theta\,\cos 2\phi}{r^{3}}\,}$$
