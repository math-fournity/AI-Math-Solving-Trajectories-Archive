Looking at this problem, I need to find the supremum of the total volume of the
six "edge-zones" created by four planes through an interior point P parallel to
the faces of a regular tetrahedron of volume 1.

## Proof

**Setup.** Let the tetrahedron $T$ have vertices $A, B, C, D$ and volume 1. Let
$P$ have barycentric coordinates $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta)$ with $\alpha+
\beta+\gamma+\delta=1$, all positive. The four partition planes are $\{a = \alph
a\}$, $\{b = \beta\}$, $\{c = \gamma\}$, $\{d = \delta\}$ (parallel to the faces
 opposite $A, B, C, D$ respectively).

**Identifying the 14 regions.** Since $\alpha+\beta+\gamma+\delta=1$, the four s
ign conditions $(a-\alpha, b-\beta, c-\gamma, d-\delta)$ must sum to zero, so no
t all signs can agree. The possible sign patterns are:
- **One positive, three negative** (4 regions): these are **parallelepipeds** at
 the vertices.
- **Three positive, one negative** (4 regions): these are **tetrahedra** at the
faces.
- **Two positive, two negative** (6 regions): these are the **edge-zones**.

This gives $4+4+6 = 14$ regions. ✓

**Volume computation.** Use $(b,c,d)$ as coordinates with $a = 1-b-c-d$. The ful
l simplex $\{b,c,d \geq 0,\; b+c+d \leq 1\}$ has volume $1/6$ in these coordinat
es, corresponding to actual volume 1, so the conversion factor is **6**.

*Vertex parallelepipeds.* The region at $A$ (sign $+,-,-,-$) is $b \in [0,\beta]
,\; c \in [0,\gamma],\; d \in [0,\delta]$, a box of volume $\beta\gamma\delta$ i
n $(b,c,d)$-space. Since $\beta+\gamma+\delta = 1-\alpha < 1$, the constraint $b
+c+d \leq 1$ is automatic. Actual volume $= 6\beta\gamma\delta$. By symmetry, th
e four parallelepiped volumes are $6\beta\gamma\delta,\; 6\alpha\gamma\delta,\;
6\alpha\beta\delta,\; 6\alpha\beta\gamma$.

*Face tetrahedra.* The region at face $BCD$ (sign $-,+,+,+$) requires $b > \beta
, c > \gamma, d > \delta$ and $b+c+d \leq 1$. Substituting $u=b-\beta, v=c-\gamm
a, w=d-\delta$ gives $\{u,v,w \geq 0,\; u+v+w \leq \alpha\}$, a tetrahedron of v
olume $\alpha^3/6$. Actual volume $= \alpha^3$. By symmetry, the four tetrahedro
n volumes are $\alpha^3, \beta^3, \gamma^3, \delta^3$.

*Edge-zones.* The region at edge $AB$ (sign $+,+,-,-$) requires $a > \alpha, b >
 \beta, c \in [0,\gamma], d \in [0,\delta]$, with $b < \beta+\gamma+\delta-c-d$
(from $a > \alpha$). Computing:
$$V_{AB}^{(b,c,d)} = \int_0^{\gamma}\!\int_0^{\delta} (\gamma+\delta-c-d)\, dd\,
 dc = \frac{\gamma\delta(\gamma+\delta)}{2}.$$
Actual volume $= 3\gamma\delta(\gamma+\delta)$. By symmetry, the six edge-zone v
olumes are $3x_i x_j(x_i + x_j)$ for each pair $\{i,j\} \subset \{\alpha,\beta,\
gamma,\delta\}$.

**Summing the edge-zones.**
$$f(P) = 3\sum_{i<j} x_i x_j(x_i+x_j) = 3\sum_{i \neq j} x_i^2 x_j = 3\sum_i x_i
^2\!\left(\sum_{j\neq i} x_j\right) = 3\sum_i x_i^2(1-x_i) = 3(S_2 - S_3),$$
where $S_k = \sum x_i^k$ and $x_i \in \{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\}$.

**Verification.** Total volume $= (\alpha^3+\beta^3+\gamma^3+\delta^3) + 6(\alph
a\beta\gamma+\alpha\beta\delta+\alpha\gamma\delta+\beta\gamma\delta) + 3(S_2-S_3
)$. Using Newton's identities with $e_1=1$: $S_2 = 1-2e_2$, $S_3 = 1-3e_2+3e_3$,
 this equals $3(1-2e_2) - 2(1-3e_2+3e_3) + 6e_3 = 1$. ✓

**Maximizing $F = S_2 - S_3 = \sum x_i^2(1-x_i)$.** We maximize over the closed
simplex $\{x_i \geq 0,\; \sum x_i = 1\}$ and then take the supremum over the int
erior.

*Interior critical points* (Lagrange multipliers): $2x_i - 3x_i^2 = \lambda$ for
 all $i$, so each $x_i$ is a root of $3t^2 - 2t + \lambda = 0$. At most two dist
inct values $p, q$ with $p+q = 2/3$ (Vieta's). Checking $k$ values equal to $p$
and $4-k$ equal to $q$:
- All equal ($k=4$): $x_i = 1/4$, $F = 3/16$.
- $k=1$ or $k=3$: $(p,q) = (1/2, 1/6)$, $F = 7/36$.
- $k=2$: requires $p+q = 1/2 \neq 2/3$; no solution.

Maximum interior value: $7/36 \approx 0.194$.

*Face critical points* (one $x_i = 0$): Same analysis with 3 variables. The only
 critical point is $x_i = 1/3$, giving $F = 2/9 \approx 0.222$.

*Edge critical points* (two $x_i = 0$): $F = \alpha\beta$ with $\alpha+\beta=1$.
 Maximum at $\alpha = \beta = 1/2$: $F = 1/4$.

*Vertices* (three $x_i = 0$): $F = 0$.

Since $F$ is continuous on the compact closed simplex, the global maximum is $\b
oxed{1/4}$, attained at $(1/2, 1/2, 0, 0)$ and permutations — points on the boun
dary (edges of $T$), not in the interior.

**Approaching the supremum from the interior.** Set $\alpha = \beta = \frac{1-2\
varepsilon}{2}$, $\gamma = \delta = \varepsilon$ for small $\varepsilon > 0$. Th
en:
$$F = \frac{1}{4} - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \varepsilon^2 \;\longrightarrow\; \f
rac{1}{4} \quad\text{as } \varepsilon \to 0^+.$$
So $F$ approaches $1/4$ from below, confirming the supremum (not attained) is $1
/4$.

**Conclusion.**
$$\sup_{P \in \operatorname{int}(T)} f(P) = 3 \times \frac{1}{4} = \boxed{\dfrac
{3}{4}}.$$
