= k[[x,y,z,w]]/(x^2y + y^{n-1} + z^2 + w^2)$ is not a UFD, where $k$ is algebrai
cally closed of characteristic 0.

## Proof

Let $S = k[[x,y,z,w]]$ and $f = x^2y + y^{n-1} + z^2 + w^2$. Since $k$ is algebr
aically closed of characteristic 0, $i = \sqrt{-1} \in k$, so $z^2 + w^2 = (z+iw
)(z-iw)$.

### Step 1: $R_n$ is a domain

The polynomial $f$ is weighted homogeneous with weights $\mathrm{wt}(x)=n-2$, $\
mathrm{wt}(y)=2$, $\mathrm{wt}(z)=\mathrm{wt}(w)=n-1$, and degree $2(n-1)$. The
partial derivatives are $2xy$, $x^2+(n-1)y^{n-2}$, $2z$, $2w$. The singular locu
s requires $xy=0$, $x^2+(n-1)y^{n-2}=0$, $z=w=0$. If $x=0$ then $y=0$; if $y=0$
then $x=0$. So the singular locus is the origin — an isolated singularity. If $f
 = gh$ with $g,h$ non-units, then $V(g) \cap V(h)$ has dimension $\geq 2$, contr
adicting the isolated singularity. Thus $f$ is irreducible and $R_n$ is a domain
.

### Step 2: The key relation

In $R_n$:
$$y\cdot(x^2 + y^{n-2}) = -(z+iw)(z-iw). \quad (\star)$$

### Step 3: Weighted-degree irreducibility criterion

The minimal positive weight is $2$ (from $y$). Units have weight $0$; non-units
have weight $\geq 2$.

**Lemma.** Let $g \in R_n$ have initial weighted form $g_0$ of weight $d < 2(n-1
)$. If $g_0$ is irreducible as a weighted-homogeneous element (i.e., cannot be w
ritten as a product of two positive-weight weighted-homogeneous elements), then
$g$ is irreducible in $R_n$.

*Proof.* If $g = ab$ in $R_n$, then $g - ab = cf$ in $S$. Since $\mathrm{wt}(cf)
 \geq 2(n-1) > d$, the initial form of $ab$ equals $g_0$, so $a_0 b_0 = g_0$ wit
h $\mathrm{wt}(a_0)+\mathrm{wt}(b_0)=d$. If both $a,b$ are non-units, both $a_0,
 b_0$ have weight $\geq 2$, giving a nontrivial factorization of $g_0$, contradi
ction. $\square$

### Step 4: $y$, $z+iw$, $z-iw$ are irreducible and pairwise non-associate

- **$y$** (weight $2$): $2 < 2+2=4$, so no factorization into positive-weight pa
rts. Irreducible.
- **$z \pm iw$** (weight $n-1 \geq 3$): If $a_0 b_0 = z \pm iw$ and either facto
r involves $x$ or $y$, the product involves $x$ or $y$ — contradiction. So both
are in $k[z,w]$, with weights that are multiples of $n-1$. Then $(p+q)(n-1) = n-
1$ forces $p+q=1$, making one factor a unit. Irreducible.
- **Non-associate:** $y$ has weight $2$ and $z \pm iw$ have weight $n-1 \geq 3$.
 Different weights $\Rightarrow$ non-associate (units have weight $0$). Also $z+
iw$ and $z-iw$ are non-associate (reducing $(z+iw) = u(z-iw)$ mod the maximal id
eal gives $1 = u_0$ and $1 = -u_0$, contradiction).

### Step 5: $y \nmid (z+iw)$ and $y \nmid (z-iw)$

$R_n/(y) \cong k[[x,z,w]]/(z^2+w^2) = k[[x,z,w]]/((z+iw)(z-iw))$. In this ring,
$z+iw \neq 0$ (it is a nonzero zero-divisor). So $y \nmid (z+iw)$, and similarly
 $y \nmid (z-iw)$.

### Step 6: Odd $n \geq 5$ — $x^2 + y^{n-2}$ is irreducible

Weight $2(n-2) = 2n-4 < 2n-2 = 2(n-1)$. Since $n-2$ is odd, $-y^{n-2}$ is not a
square in $k((y))$, so by Weierstrass preparation, $x^2 + y^{n-2}$ is irreducibl
e in $k[[x,y]]$, hence in $S$, and its initial form cannot be factored. By the L
emma, $x^2 + y^{n-2}$ is irreducible in $R_n$.

Its weight $2n-4 \neq 2$ and $\neq n-1$ (for $n \geq 5$), so it is non-associate
 to $y$, $z+iw$, $z-iw$.

Relation $(\star)$ gives two factorizations of the same element into irreducible
s:
$$y \cdot (x^2+y^{n-2}) = -(z+iw)\cdot(z-iw),$$
with four pairwise non-associate irreducible factors. This is impossible in a UF
D. **So $R_n$ is not a UFD for odd $n \geq 5$.**

### Step 7: Even $n \geq 4$ — factoring $x^2 + y^{n-2}$

Since $n-2$ is even: $x^2 + y^{n-2} = (x + iy^{(n-2)/2})(x - iy^{(n-2)/2})$ in $
S$ (hence in $R_n$).

Each factor has weight $n-2 < 2(n-1)$. The initial form $x \pm iy^{(n-2)/2}$ is
degree $1$ in $x$, hence irreducible in $k[[x,y]]$ (and in $S$). By the Lemma, b
oth $x \pm iy^{(n-2)/2}$ are irreducible in $R_n$.

**Non-associateness:** Their weight is $n-2 \neq n-1$ (weight of $z \pm iw$), so
 non-associate to $z \pm iw$. For $n \geq 6$: weight $n-2 \neq 2$, so non-associ
ate to $y$. For $n = 4$: weight $= 2$, same as $y$, but $x+iy \notin (y)$ since
$x \neq 0$ in $R_4/(y)$, so non-associate to $y$. Also $x+iy$ and $x-iy$ are non
-associate: if $x+iy = u(x-iy)$, then $(1-u)x + i(1+u)y = 0$; reducing mod $\mat
hfrak{m}$ gives $u_0 = 1$ and $2i = 0$, contradicting $\mathrm{char}(k) = 0$.

Relation $(\star)$ becomes:
$$y \cdot (x+iy^{(n-2)/2}) \cdot (x-iy^{(n-2)/2}) = -(z+iw)(z-iw),$$
with **five** pairwise non-associate irreducible factors — $3$ on the left, $2$
on the right. This is impossible in a UFD. **So $R_n$ is not a UFD for even $n \
geq 4$.**

### Conclusion

$R_n$ is **not a UFD for all $n \geq 4$**.

The essential reason: over an algebraically closed field, $z^2 + w^2$ splits as
$(z+iw)(z-iw)$, while $y(x^2+y^{n-2})$ provides a genuinely different factorizat
ion (into $2$ irreducibles when $n$ is odd, into $3$ when $n$ is even). The weig
hted-degree structure guarantees all factors are irreducible and pairwise non-as
sociate, yielding a non-unique factorization for every $n \geq 4$.
