## Proof

We interpret the question as asking: what is the minimal Heegaard genus among cl
osed orientable 3-manifolds that are neither $S^3$ nor a lens space? We show the
 answer is **2**: such manifolds have genus $\geq 2$, and the bound is sharp.

### Background

A **Heegaard splitting** of a closed orientable 3-manifold $M$ is a decompositio
n $M = H_1 \cup_\Sigma H_2$ where $H_1, H_2$ are handlebodies sharing a common b
oundary surface $\Sigma$. The **Heegaard genus** $g(M)$ is the minimal genus of
$\Sigma$ over all Heegaard splittings.

### Step 1: Genus 0 ⟺ $S^3$

A genus-0 Heegaard splitting uses a surface $\Sigma$ of genus 0, i.e., $\Sigma =
 S^2$. Each handlebody of genus 0 is a 3-ball $B^3$. Thus $M = B^3 \cup_{S^2} B^
3 = S^3$. Conversely $S^3$ admits the standard genus-0 splitting. Hence

$$g(M) = 0 \iff M = S^3.$$

### Step 2: Genus 1 ⟺ lens space (including $S^3$ and $S^1\times S^2$)

A genus-1 Heegaard splitting uses $\Sigma = T^2$ (the torus). Each handlebody of
 genus 1 is a solid torus $V = S^1 \times D^2$. Thus $M$ is obtained by gluing t
wo solid tori $V_1, V_2$ along their boundary tori via some homeomorphism $\varp
hi: \partial V_1 \to \partial V_2$.

Let $\mu_i, \lambda_i$ be a meridian–longitude pair on $\partial V_i$. The gluin
g is determined (up to homeomorphism of the result) by the image of the meridian
: $\varphi_*(\mu_1) = p\,\mu_2 + q\,\lambda_2$ with $\gcd(p,q)=1$. The resulting
 manifold is the **lens space** $L(p,q)$, with the conventions:
- $L(0,1) = S^1 \times S^2$ (meridian maps to longitude),
- $L(1,0) = S^3$ (meridian maps to meridian),
- $L(p,q)$ for $p \geq 2$ are the usual lens spaces.

Conversely, every lens space admits a genus-1 Heegaard splitting (its standard d
escription as the union of two solid tori). Therefore

$$g(M) = 1 \iff M \text{ is a lens space (in the generalized sense, including }
S^3 \text{ and } S^1\times S^2).$$

### Step 3: Manifolds that are neither $S^3$ nor lens spaces have genus $\geq 2$

Combining Steps 1 and 2:

- $g(M) = 0 \Rightarrow M = S^3$ (excluded).
- $g(M) = 1 \Rightarrow M$ is a lens space (excluded).

Hence for any closed orientable $M$ that is neither $S^3$ nor a lens space,

$$g(M) \geq 2.$$

### Step 4: The bound 2 is sharp

Consider $M = L(p,q) \# L(p',q')$ where $p, p' \geq 2$, so each summand is a non
trivial lens space. By the prime decomposition theorem, $M$ is reducible and hen
ce **not** a lens space (lens spaces with $p\geq 2$ are irreducible, and $S^3$ i
s irreducible), and clearly $M \neq S^3$.

By the additivity of Heegaard genus under connected sum (Haken's lemma: $g(M_1 \
# M_2) = g(M_1) + g(M_2)$ for nontrivial summands),

$$g(M) = g(L(p,q)) + g(L(p',q')) = 1 + 1 = 2.$$

Thus $M$ is neither $S^3$ nor a lens space, yet has Heegaard genus exactly 2.

### Conclusion

Closed orientable 3-manifolds that are neither the 3-sphere nor lens spaces have
 **Heegaard genus at least 2**, and this lower bound is attained (e.g., by the c
onnected sum of two nontrivial lens spaces). The minimal Heegaard genus of such
manifolds is therefore

$$\boxed{2}.$$
