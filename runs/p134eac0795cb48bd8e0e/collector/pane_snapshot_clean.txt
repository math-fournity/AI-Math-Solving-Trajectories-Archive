## Solution

**Setting up the problem.** We have $f, g: \{1,\dots,2000\}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$, a
nd there exist subsets $X, Y \subseteq \{1,\dots,2000\}$ with $|X| = |Y| = 1000$
 such that:
$$x \notin X \text{ and } y \notin Y \implies f(x,y) = g(x,y).$$

We seek the maximum number of pairs $(x,y)$ where $f(x,y) \neq g(x,y)$.

**Upper bound.** The condition forces $f(x,y) = g(x,y)$ on the "complement recta
ngle":
$$\{(x,y) : x \notin X,\; y \notin Y\} = \{1,\dots,2000\}\setminus X \;\times\;
\{1,\dots,2000\}\setminus Y.$$
This rectangle has size $(2000 - 1000)\times(2000 - 1000) = 1000 \times 1000 = 1
{,}000{,}000$.

Therefore, the pairs where $f \neq g$ can occur **only** where $x \in X$ **or**
$y \in Y$. The number of such pairs is, by inclusion-exclusion:
$$|X|\cdot 2000 + |Y|\cdot 2000 - |X|\cdot|Y| = 1000\cdot 2000 + 1000\cdot 2000
- 1000\cdot 1000 = 2{,}000{,}000 + 2{,}000{,}000 - 1{,}000{,}000 = 3{,}000{,}000
.$$

So the number of disagreeing pairs is at most $\boxed{3{,}000{,}000}$.

**Achievability.** Choose any $X, Y$ with $|X|=|Y|=1000$. Define:
$$f(x,y) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } x \in X \text{ or } y \in Y, \\ 0 & \tex
t{otherwise}, \end{cases} \qquad g(x,y) = 0 \text{ for all } x,y.$$

Then $f(x,y) \neq g(x,y)$ exactly on $\{(x,y) : x \in X \text{ or } y \in Y\}$,
which has $3{,}000{,}000$ pairs, and $f = g = 0$ on the complement rectangle whe
re $x \notin X, y \notin Y$. The condition is satisfied, and exactly $3{,}000{,}
000$ pairs disagree.

**Conclusion.** The maximum number of couples $(x,y)$ with $f(x,y) \neq g(x,y)$
is $\mathbf{3{,}000{,}000}$.
