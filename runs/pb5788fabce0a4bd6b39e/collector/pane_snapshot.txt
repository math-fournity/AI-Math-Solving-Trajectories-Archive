## Proof

**Counterexample.** Let $G = \bar{K}_7 \vee (K_3 \sqcup K_3)$, the join of the e
mpty graph on 7 vertices with the disjoint union of two triangles. Denote $A = V
(\bar{K}_7)$ (7 vertices, no internal edges) and $B = V(K_3 \sqcup K_3) = \{b_1,
b_2,b_3\} \cup \{c_1,c_2,c_3\}$ (two triangles, no edges between them). The join
 adds all edges between $A$ and $B$. So $|V(G)| = 13$.

### $G$ is connected

Every vertex of $A$ is adjacent to every vertex of $B$. Any two vertices in $A$
share a common neighbor in $B$ (path of length 2). Any two vertices in $B$ are c
onnected either directly (if in the same triangle) or via any vertex of $A$ (pat
h of length 2). So $G$ is connected. ✓

### Degree sequence

- Each $a \in A$: adjacent to all 6 vertices of $B$, none in $A$ → degree **6**.
 (7 such vertices)
- Each $b_i$ or $c_j$: adjacent to 2 in its triangle + all 7 in $A$ → degree **9
**. (6 such vertices)

Degree sequence (non-increasing): $(\underbrace{9,\ldots,9}_{6},\underbrace{6,\l
dots,6}_{7})$, sum $= 54+42 = 96$ (even, consistent with $|E|=48$ edges: $3+3$ w
ithin triangles $+ 42$ cross edges).

### Havel–Hakimi residue $= 2$

Iteratively remove the largest entry $d$ and subtract 1 from the next $d$ entrie
s, re-sorting each time:

Each step's sum decreases by $2d$ (removing $d$ and subtracting 1 from $d$ entri

The process terminates with **two zeros**, so $\operatorname{residue}(G) = 2$.

### Independence number $\alpha(G) = 7$

An independent set cannot contain vertices from both $A$ and $B$ (the join makes
 every $a$–$b$ pair adjacent). Within $A$: all 7 vertices are mutually non-adjac
ent → independent set of size 7. Within $B$: an independent set contains at most
 one vertex per triangle → size $\leq 2$. Therefore $\alpha(G) = 7$.

### Largest induced bipartite subgraph $b(G) = 9$

Let $S \subseteq V(G)$ with $G[S]$ bipartite, so $S = X \sqcup Y$ with $X, Y$ in
dependent. Since every $a \in A$ is adjacent to every $b \in B$, an independent
set is contained entirely in $A$ or entirely in $B$ (with $|S \cap B| \leq 2$, a
t most one per triangle). Thus each of $X, Y$ is either a subset of $A$ (size $\
leq 7$) or an independent subset of $B$ (size $\leq 2$).

- **Both in $A$:** $|S| \leq 7$.
- **Both in $B$:** $|S| \leq 4$.
- **One in $A$, one in $B$:** $|S| \leq 7 + 2 = 9$.

The bound 9 is **achieved**: take $S = A \cup \{b_1, c_1\}$ (one vertex from eac
h triangle). Then $G[S] \cong K_{7,2}$ (complete bipartite, since $A$ is one par
t, $\{b_1,c_1\}$ the other, with no internal edges in either part). This is bipa
rtite with 9 vertices. No larger induced bipartite subgraph exists, so $b(G) = 9
$.

### The conjecture fails

$$\left\lfloor \frac{\operatorname{residue}(G) + 2\,b(G)}{3} \right\rfloor = \le

But $\alpha(G) = 7 > 6$. The inequality
$$\alpha(G) \le \left\lfloor \frac{\operatorname{residue}(G) + 2\,b(G)}{3} \righ
t\rfloor$$
is **violated** by $G$.

### Conclusion

The connected graph $G = \bar{K}_7 \vee (K_3 \sqcup K_3)$ on 13 vertices satisfi
es $\alpha(G) = 7$, $\operatorname{residue}(G) = 2$, and $b(G) = 9$, giving a co
njectured right-hand side of $6 < 7 = \alpha(G)$. This refutes the universal sta
tement, establishing that the conjecture is false — i.e., `answer(False)` holds.
