## Proof

**Setup and parity.** Let the $m$ distinct positive even numbers be $e_1<\cdots<
e_m$ and the $n$ distinct positive odd numbers be $o_1<\cdots<o_n$. The sum of $
m$ even numbers is even; the sum of $n$ odd numbers has the parity of $n$. Since
 the total is $1987$ (odd), **$n$ must be odd**.

**Minimum-sum bound.** The smallest $m$ distinct positive even numbers are $2,4,
\ldots,2m$, with sum $m(m+1)$. The smallest $n$ distinct positive odd numbers ar
e $1,3,\ldots,2n-1$, with sum $n^2$. Therefore every valid configuration satisfi
es
$$m(m+1)+n^2 \le 1987. \tag{1}$$

**Achievability of equality up to slack.** Conversely, for any $m\ge 0$ and odd
$n\ge 1$ satisfying (1), the slack $S = 1987 - m(m+1) - n^2$ is nonnegative and
even (since $m(m+1)$ is even, $n^2$ is odd, and $1987$ is odd). Starting from th
e minimal sets $\{2,4,\ldots,2m\}$ and $\{1,3,\ldots,2n-1\}$, increase the large
st element $2m$ by $S$; this keeps it even, keeps it larger than all other evens
 (so distinct), and raises the total to exactly $1987$. Hence **every** pair $(m
,n)$ with $n$ odd and $m(m+1)+n^2\le 1987$ is attainable. (When $m=0$, increase
the largest odd instead — irrelevant here since the optimum has $m\ge 1$.)

Thus the problem reduces to:
$$\text{maximize } 3m+4n \quad\text{subject to}\quad m(m+1)+n^2\le 1987,\; n\ge
1 \text{ odd}.$$

For each odd $n$, the best $m$ is $m(n)=\max\{k: k(k+1)\le 1987-n^2\}$.

**Evaluating the candidates.** Since $n^2\le 1987$ gives $n\le 44$, the odd cand
idates are $n=1,3,\ldots,43$. Computing $m(n)$ and $3m+4n$ at the relevant value
s:

| $n$ | $1987-n^2$ | $m(n)$ | $m(m+1)$ | $3m+4n$ |
|-----|-----------|--------|----------|---------|
| 43 | 138 | 11 | 132 | 205 |
| 41 | 306 | 17 | 306 | 215 |
| 39 | 466 | 21 | 462 | 219 |
| 37 | 618 | 24 | 600 | 220 |
| **35** | **762** | **27** | **756** | **221** |
| 33 | 898 | 29 | 870 | 219 |
| 31 | 1026 | 31 | 992 | 217 |
| 29 | 1146 | 33 | 1122 | 215 |
| 27 | 1258 | 34 | 1190 | 210 |
| 25 | 1362 | 36 | 1332 | 208 |

**Bounding the remaining values.**

- **$n\ge 37$:** From the table, the values are $205, 215, 219, 220$ — all $<221
$.
- **$n\le 25$:** Here $4n\le 100$ and $n^2\ge 1$, so $m(m+1)\le 1986$, giving $m
\le 44$ and $3m\le 132$; but the binding case is $n=25$ (largest $n$ in this ran
ge, hence largest $4n$), which already yields $208$. For smaller $n$, $4n$ drops
 by $8$ per step while $3m$ rises by at most $6$ (since $m$ grows by $\le 2$), s
o the value only decreases. Hence all values here are $\le 208 < 221$.
- **$27\le n\le 33$:** From the table: $210, 215, 217, 219$ — all $<221$.
- **$n=35$:** gives exactly $221$.

**Construction attaining $221$.** Take $m=27$, $n=35$:
- Even set: $\{2,4,6,\ldots,52, 60\}$ (replace $54$ by $60$), sum $= 756 - 54 +
60 = 762$.
- Odd set: $\{1,3,5,\ldots,69\}$, sum $= 35^2 = 1225$.
- Total: $762 + 1225 = 1987$. ✓

All $27$ evens are distinct positive evens; all $35$ odds are distinct positive
odds. The value $3\cdot 27 + 4\cdot 35 = 81 + 140 = \boxed{221}$.
