## Proof

Let $P_1,\dots,P_{15}$ be points in $\mathbb{R}^3$ with $|P_i|^2 = x_i^2+y_i^2+z
_i^2 \le 1$. We seek the maximum of
$$S = \sum_{1\le i<j\le 15} |P_iP_j|^2.$$

**Key identity.** For any $n$ points,
$$\sum_{i<j}|P_i-P_j|^2 = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}|P_i-P_j|^2 = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,
j}\bigl(|P_i|^2+|P_j|^2-2\,P_i\cdot P_j\bigr).$$
The first two terms give $\frac{1}{2}(2n\sum_i|P_i|^2)=n\sum_i|P_i|^2$, and the
cross term gives $-\frac{1}{2}\cdot 2\,|\sum_i P_i|^2 = -|\sum_i P_i|^2$. Hence
$$S = n\sum_{i=1}^{n}|P_i|^2 \;-\; \Bigl|\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_i\Bigr|^2.$$

**Upper bound.** With $n=15$ and $|P_i|^2\le 1$:
$$S = 15\sum_{i=1}^{15}|P_i|^2 - \Bigl|\sum_{i=1}^{15}P_i\Bigr|^2 \le 15\cdot 15
 - 0 = 225,$$
since $\sum|P_i|^2\le 15$ and $|\sum P_i|^2\ge 0$.

**Achievability.** It remains to exhibit 15 points on the unit sphere whose cent
roid is the origin, so that both $\sum|P_i|^2=15$ and $\sum P_i=0$.

Place the 15 points equally spaced on the equator of the unit sphere:
$$P_k = \bigl(\cos\tfrac{2\pi k}{15},\;\sin\tfrac{2\pi k}{15},\;0\bigr),\qquad k
=0,1,\dots,14.$$
Each satisfies $|P_k|^2=1$, and
$$\sum_{k=0}^{14}P_k = \Bigl(\sum_{k=0}^{14}\cos\tfrac{2\pi k}{15},\;\sum_{k=0}^
{14}\sin\tfrac{2\pi k}{15},\;0\Bigr) = (0,0,0),$$
since these are the 15th roots of unity (real and imaginary parts), whose sum is
 $0$.

Therefore $S = 15\cdot 15 - 0 = 225$ is attained.

## Conclusion

The maximum possible value of $\displaystyle\sum_{1\le i<j\le 15}|P_iP_j|^2$ is

$$\boxed{225}.$$
