# Problem

A high-security data center is designed in the shape of a rare polyhedral facility with 30 independent fiber-optic corridors (edges) connecting 20 server hubs (vertices). The facility is structured as a regular dodecahedron, meaning the corridors form 12 distinct pentagonal security sectors (faces), where each hub serves as a junction for exactly three corridors.

The chief network engineer needs to activate a subset of these corridors to carry high-frequency data. However, due to the specific load-balancing protocols of the facility, the activation must follow one strict rule: within each of the 12 pentagonal sectors, the number of activated corridors must be an even number (0, 2, or 4).

The corridors are all physically distinct and mapped to specific locations in the grid. Two activation configurations are considered different if the set of activated corridors is not identical. 

In how many distinct ways can the engineer choose which corridors to activate so that the load-balancing rule is satisfied for all 12 sectors?
