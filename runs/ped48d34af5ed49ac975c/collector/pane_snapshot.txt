## Proof

Let $W$ be a standard Wiener process and fix $\epsilon > 0$. Define the process
$$X(s) = W_{s+\epsilon} - W_s, \qquad s \ge 0.$$
We compute the quadratic variation $[X]_t$ on $[0,t]$, defined as the limit in p
robability
$$[X]_t = \lim_{\|\pi\|\to 0} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \bigl(X(t_i)-X(t_{i-1})\bigr)^2,$$
where $\pi = \{0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_n=t\}$ and $\|\pi\|=\max_i \Delta_i$ with $\De
lta_i = t_i - t_{i-1}$.

**Step 1. Decompose the increments.** For each $i$,
$$X(t_i) - X(t_{i-1}) = \bigl(W_{t_i+\epsilon}-W_{t_{i-1}+\epsilon}\bigr) - \big
l(W_{t_i}-W_{t_{i-1}}\bigr).$$
Set
$$A_i = W_{t_i+\epsilon}-W_{t_{i-1}+\epsilon}, \qquad B_i = W_{t_i}-W_{t_{i-1}}.
$$
Then $X(t_i)-X(t_{i-1}) = A_i - B_i$.

**Step 2. Independence for fine partitions.** The increment $A_i$ is taken over
the interval $[t_{i-1}+\epsilon,\, t_i+\epsilon]$ of length $\Delta_i$, and $B_i
$ over $[t_{i-1},\, t_i]$ of length $\Delta_i$. Once $\|\pi\| < \epsilon$, these
 two intervals are disjoint (in fact separated by a gap of $\epsilon - \Delta_i
> 0$). By the independent-increments property of Brownian motion, $A_i$ and $B_i
$ are then independent, each $\mathcal N(0,\Delta_i)$.

Hence $A_i - B_i \sim \mathcal N(0,\, 2\Delta_i)$, so
$$\mathbb E\bigl[(A_i-B_i)^2\bigr] = 2\Delta_i, \qquad \operatorname{Var}\bigl[(
A_i-B_i)^2\bigr] = 2(2\Delta_i)^2 = 8\Delta_i^2,$$
using $(A_i-B_i)^2/(2\Delta_i)\sim \chi^2_1$.

**Step 3. Convergence of the mean.** Let $S_\pi = \sum_i (A_i-B_i)^2$. Then
$$\mathbb E[S_\pi] = \sum_i 2\Delta_i = 2t.$$

**Step 4. Vanishing of the variance.**
$$\operatorname{Var}(S_\pi) = \sum_i \operatorname{Var}\bigl[(A_i-B_i)^2\bigr] =
 \sum_i 8\Delta_i^2 \le 8\,\|\pi\|\sum_i \Delta_i = 8\,\|\pi\|\, t \xrightarrow{
\|\pi\|\to 0} 0.$$
(Independence across $i$ holds because the relevant Brownian increments are over
 disjoint intervals once $\|\pi\|<\epsilon$.)

**Step 5. Conclusion.** Since $\mathbb E[S_\pi]=2t$ and $\operatorname{Var}(S_\p
i)\to 0$, we have $S_\pi \to 2t$ in $L^2$, hence in probability. Therefore the q
uadratic variation exists and equals
$$[X]_t = 2t.$$

Equivalently, in differential form, $d[X]_s = 2\,ds$.
