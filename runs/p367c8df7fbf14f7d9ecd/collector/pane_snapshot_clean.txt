## Proof

**Claim.** Every affine $k$-variety — i.e. an affine scheme $X$ over $k$ that is
 reduced and of finite type over $k$ — is of the form $X = \operatorname{Spec} A
$ for a finitely generated, reduced $k$-algebra $A$. Conversely, every such $\op
eratorname{Spec} A$ is an affine $k$-variety. Hence the answer is **yes**.

---

**Setup.** Let $X$ be an affine $k$-variety. Being an affine scheme means $X = \
operatorname{Spec} A$ for some commutative ring $A$. Being a scheme *over* $k$ m
eans there is a fixed morphism $f \colon X \to \operatorname{Spec} k$, i.e. a mo
rphism of schemes over $\operatorname{Spec} k$. Since $X = \operatorname{Spec} A
$ and the base is $\operatorname{Spec} k$, the global-sections functor gives an
equivalence

$$
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Sch}}(\operatorname{Spec} A,\, \operatorname{Spec} k
) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Ring}}(k,\, A),
$$

so the structure morphism $f$ corresponds to a ring homomorphism $\varphi \colon
 k \to A$. This endows $A$ with the structure of a $k$-algebra (via $\varphi$).
The morphism $f$ being a morphism *of schemes over $\operatorname{Spec} k$* is e
xactly the condition that $\varphi$ is the $k$-algebra structure map, so $A$ is
a $k$-algebra and $f$ is the canonical morphism $\operatorname{Spec} A \to \oper
atorname{Spec} k$ induced by $\varphi$.

---

**Step 1: Finite type $\Longrightarrow$ $A$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra.
**

Recall that a morphism $f \colon X \to S$ is *of finite type* if $S$ admits an o
pen cover by affine opens $U_i = \operatorname{Spec} R_i$ such that each $f^{-1}
(U_i)$ is covered by finitely many affine opens $V_{ij} = \operatorname{Spec} B_
{ij}$ with each $B_{ij}$ a finitely generated $R_i$-algebra.

Here $S = \operatorname{Spec} k$ is a single affine open, so we may take the cov
er $\{S\}$. Then $f^{-1}(S) = X = \operatorname{Spec} A$ must be covered by fini
tely many affine opens $V_j = \operatorname{Spec} B_j$ with each $B_j$ a finitel
y generated $k$-algebra. But $X$ itself is affine, $\operatorname{Spec} A$, so w
e may take the single affine open $V = X = \operatorname{Spec} A$ covering $f^{-
1}(S)$. The finite-type condition then demands that $A$ be a finitely generated
$k$-algebra.

(More directly: for an affine morphism $\operatorname{Spec} A \to \operatorname{
Spec} R$, being of finite type is *equivalent* to $A$ being a finitely generated
 $R$-algebra; this is the standard equivalence between the scheme-theoretic and
algebraic notions of finite type for affine morphisms. With $R = k$, we get that
 $A$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra.)

---

**Step 2: Reduced $\Longrightarrow$ $A$ is reduced.**

A scheme $X$ is *reduced* if for every open $U \subseteq X$ the ring $\mathcal{O
}_X(U)$ is reduced (has no nilpotent elements), equivalently if all the local ri
ngs $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ are reduced, equivalently if $\mathcal{O}_X(U)$ is reduc
ed for affine opens $U$. For $X = \operatorname{Spec} A$, the global sections ar
e $\mathcal{O}_X(X) = A$, so reducedness of $X$ implies $A$ is a reduced ring.

---

**Step 3: Conclusion of the forward direction.**

Combining Steps 1 and 2: $X = \operatorname{Spec} A$ where $A$ is a $k$-algebra
(from the structure morphism), finitely generated as a $k$-algebra (from finite
type), and reduced (from reducedness of $X$). Thus every affine $k$-variety is t
he Spec of a finitely generated reduced $k$-algebra.

---

**Step 4: Converse (completeness of the correspondence).**

Conversely, let $A$ be any finitely generated reduced $k$-algebra. Then $X = \op
eratorname{Spec} A$ is an affine scheme. The $k$-algebra structure $k \to A$ giv
es a morphism $\operatorname{Spec} A \to \operatorname{Spec} k$ making $X$ a sch
eme over $k$. Since $A$ is finitely generated over $k$, this morphism is of fini
te type (by the same affine finite-type equivalence used in Step 1). Since $A$ i
s reduced, $X$ is reduced. Hence $\operatorname{Spec} A$ is an affine $k$-variet
y.

---

**Summary.** The two directions together establish a bijection (up to isomorphis
m)

$$
\left\{\text{affine } k\text{-varieties}\right\} \;\longleftrightarrow\; \left\{
\text{finitely generated reduced } k\text{-algebras}\right\},
$$

given by $X \mapsto \mathcal{O}_X(X)$ and $A \mapsto \operatorname{Spec} A$. The
refore every affine $k$-variety, given by an affine scheme that is reduced and o
f finite type over $k$, does correspond to the $\operatorname{Spec}$ of a finite
ly generated $k$-algebra (which is necessarily reduced). $\blacksquare$
