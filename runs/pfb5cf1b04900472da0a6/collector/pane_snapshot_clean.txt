## Proof

Let $N = 4444^{4444}$. Let $A$ be the sum of the digits of $N$, $B$ the sum of t
he digits of $A$, and let $C$ be the sum of the digits of $B$. We seek $C$.

**Key fact.** For any positive integer $n$, the sum of its digits is congruent t
o $n$ modulo $9$. Hence
$$N \equiv A \equiv B \equiv C \pmod{9}.$$

**Step 1: Bound $A$.** Since $4444 < 10000 = 10^4$,
$$N = 4444^{4444} < (10^4)^{4444} = 10^{17776}.$$
So $N$ has at most $17776$ digits, giving
$$A \le 9 \times 17776 = 159984.$$

**Step 2: Bound $B$.** Since $A \le 159984$ (a 6‑digit number), the largest poss
ible digit sum of any integer $\le 159984$ is attained at $99999$, namely $45$.
Thus
$$B \le 45.$$

**Step 3: Bound $C$.** Since $B \le 45$, the largest digit sum of any integer $\
le 45$ is attained at $39$, namely $12$. Thus
$$1 \le C \le 12.$$

**Step 4: Compute $N \bmod 9$.** We have
$$4444 \equiv 4+4+4+4 = 16 \equiv 7 \pmod{9}.$$
The powers of $7$ modulo $9$ cycle with period $3$:
$$7^1 \equiv 7,\quad 7^2 = 49 \equiv 4,\quad 7^3 \equiv 28 \equiv 1 \pmod{9}.$$
Since $4444 = 3 \times 1481 + 1$,
$$N = 4444^{4444} \equiv 7^{4444} \equiv 7^{1} \equiv 7 \pmod{9}.$$

**Step 5: Conclude.** By the key fact,
$$C \equiv N \equiv 7 \pmod{9}.$$
The only integer in the range $[1, 12]$ that is congruent to $7$ modulo $9$ is $
7$ itself (the next candidate, $16$, exceeds $12$).

Therefore the sum of the digits of $B$ is $\boxed{7}$.
