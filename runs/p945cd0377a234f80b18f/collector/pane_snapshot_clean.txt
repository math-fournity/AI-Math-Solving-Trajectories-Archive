Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether there exists a finite local
 ring $R$ (not a field) with a prime $p > 2$ dividing $|R^\times|$, and a primit
ive $p$-th root of unity $u \in R^\times$ such that $u - 1$ is also a unit.

**Answer: Yes, such a ring exists.**

**Proof.**

We construct an explicit example. Take $R = \mathbb{Z}/49\mathbb{Z}$ and $p = 3$
.

**$R$ is a finite local ring, not a field.** The ring $\mathbb{Z}/49\mathbb{Z}$
has unique maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m} = (7)/(49)$, with residue field $R/\mathf
rak{m} \cong \mathbb{F}_7$. Since $\mathfrak{m} \neq 0$, $R$ is not a field.

**$p = 3 > 2$ divides $|R^\times|$.** We have $|R^\times| = |\mathbb{Z}/49\mathb
b{Z}| - |(7)/(49)| = 49 - 7 = 42 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 7$, so $3 \mid 42$. ✓

**Constructing a primitive 3rd root of unity.** In $\mathbb{F}_7$, the element $
\bar{a} = 2$ satisfies $2^3 = 8 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$ and $2 \not\equiv 1 \pmod{7}$
, so $\bar{a}$ is a primitive 3rd root of unity in $\mathbb{F}_7^\times$.

Since $f(x) = x^3 - 1$ has $f(2) \equiv 0 \pmod{7}$ and $f'(2) = 3 \cdot 4 = 12
\equiv 5 \not\equiv 0 \pmod{7}$, Hensel's Lemma applies (the residue characteris
tic $7$ is coprime to $p = 3$). There exists a unique lift $u \in \mathbb{Z}/49\
mathbb{Z}$ with $u \equiv 2 \pmod{7}$ and $u^3 \equiv 1 \pmod{49}$.

Computing explicitly: write $u = 2 + 7t$. Then
$$u^3 = (2+7t)^3 \equiv 8 + 84t \pmod{49}.$$
Setting $u^3 \equiv 1 \pmod{49}$: $84t \equiv -7 \pmod{49}$, i.e., $35t \equiv 4
2 \pmod{49}$, i.e., $5t \equiv 6 \pmod{7}$, giving $t \equiv 4 \pmod{7}$. So $u
= 2 + 28 = 30$.

**Verification:** $30^3 = 27000 = 551 \cdot 49 + 1$, so $30^3 \equiv 1 \pmod{49}
$. Since $30 \equiv 2 \pmod{7} \not\equiv 1 \pmod{7}$, we have $u \neq 1$ in $R$
, and since $3$ is prime, $u$ has order exactly $3$ in $R^\times$. Thus $u = 30$
 is a primitive 3rd root of unity. ✓

**$u - 1$ is a unit.** We have $u - 1 = 29$. Since $\gcd(29, 49) = 1$ (as $29$ i
s prime and $29 \neq 7$), the element $29$ is a unit in $\mathbb{Z}/49\mathbb{Z}
$.

Equivalently, $u - 1 \equiv 2 - 1 = 1 \pmod{7}$, so $u - 1 \notin \mathfrak{m}$,
 hence $u - 1 \in R^\times$. ✓

**General principle.** The construction works whenever we choose a prime $p_0 \n
eq p$ with $p \mid (p_0 - 1)$ and take $R = \mathbb{Z}/p_0^2\mathbb{Z}$. The key
 points are:
- The residue characteristic $p_0 \neq p$ ensures Hensel's Lemma lifts primitive
 $p$-th roots of unity from $\mathbb{F}_{p_0}$ to $R$.
- The lifted root $u$ satisfies $\bar{u} \neq \bar{1}$ in $\mathbb{F}_{p_0}$, so
 $u - 1 \notin \mathfrak{m}$, making $u - 1$ a unit.

This shows that the obstruction $u - 1 \in \mathfrak{m}$ (which always occurs wh
en $p$ equals the residue characteristic, since then $u \equiv 1 \pmod{\mathfrak
{m}}$) can be avoided precisely when $p$ is coprime to the residue characteristi
c.
