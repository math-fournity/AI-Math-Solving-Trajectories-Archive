# Solution

**Answer: No.** The image of a nonarchimedean absolute value on a locally compac
t field cannot contain a nonempty interval.

## Proof

Let $(k, |\cdot|)$ be a nonarchimedean valued field that is locally compact (for
 the topology induced by $|\cdot|$). Set
$$|k^\times| = \{|x| : x \in k^\times\} \subseteq \mathbb{R}_{>0}, \quad \mathca
l{O} = \{x : |x|\le 1\}, \quad \mathfrak{m} = \{x : |x|<1\}, \quad \mathcal{O}^\
times = \{x : |x|=1\}.$$
We prove that $|k^\times|$ is **discrete** in $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$, which immediate
ly rules out containing any interval.

---

### Step 1. The valuation ring $\mathcal{O}$ is compact.

The absolute value gives a genuine metric $d(x,y)=|x-y|$, so $k$ is Hausdorff. B
y local compactness there is a compact neighborhood $C$ of $0$ with $B(0,r)=\{x:
|x|<r\}\subseteq C$ for some $r>0$.

If $|\cdot|$ is trivial, then $k$ is discrete; local compactness forces $k$ fini
te, so $\mathcal{O}=k$ is compact and $|k^\times|=\{1\}$ is discrete — done. Oth
erwise $|\cdot|$ is nontrivial, so values are unbounded: some $b\in k^\times$ ha
s $|b|\ne 1$, and $|b|^n\to\infty$ along one sign of $n$. Pick $a\in k^\times$ w
ith $|a|>1/r$. Then $a\cdot C$ is compact (homeomorphic image of $C$) and contai
ns
$$a\cdot B(0,r) = B(0,|a|r) \supseteq \{x:|x|\le 1\} = \mathcal{O}$$
since $|a|r>1$. As $\mathcal{O} = |\cdot|^{-1}([0,1])$ is closed and lies inside
 the compact set $a\cdot C$, it is compact. $\square$

---

### Step 2. A non-discrete value group would be dense.

$\log|k^\times|$ is a subgroup of $(\mathbb{R},+)$. A subgroup of $(\mathbb{R},+
)$ is either discrete (of the form $a\mathbb{Z}$) or dense. Hence $|k^\times|$ i
s either discrete or dense in $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$.

---

### Step 3. Density of $|k^\times|$ contradicts compactness of $\mathcal{O}$.

Suppose for contradiction that $|k^\times|$ is dense. Then
$$\mathfrak{m} = \bigcup_{\rho\in|k^\times|,\,\rho<1} B(0,\rho),$$
because every $x\in\mathfrak{m}$ (with $|x|<1$) admits, by density, some $\rho\i
n|k^\times|$ with $|x|<\rho<1$.

The unit group $\mathcal{O}^\times = \mathcal{O}\setminus\mathfrak{m}$ is closed
 in $\mathcal{O}$, hence compact. For each $u\in\mathcal{O}^\times$ the open bal
l $B(u,1)=\{x:|x-u|<1\}$ satisfies $B(u,1)\subseteq\mathcal{O}^\times$: indeed $
|x-u|<1=|u|$ forces $|x|=|u|=1$ by the nonarchimedean isosceles principle. Thus
$\{B(u,1):u\in\mathcal{O}^\times\}$ is an open cover of $\mathcal{O}^\times$; by
 compactness, finitely many $B(u_1,1),\dots,B(u_N,1)$ cover $\mathcal{O}^\times$
.

Now
$$\{B(u_1,1),\dots,B(u_N,1)\}\;\cup\;\{B(0,\rho):\rho\in|k^\times|,\rho<1\}$$
is an open cover of $\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}^\times\cup\mathfrak{m}$. By compact
ness it admits a finite subcover. The balls $B(0,\rho)$ are nested, so their fin
ite union equals $B(0,\rho^\*)$ for $\rho^\*=\max$ of the finitely many $\rho$'s
. Therefore
$$\mathcal{O}\;\subseteq\; B(0,\rho^\*)\;\cup\; B(u_1,1)\cup\cdots\cup B(u_N,1)\
;\subseteq\;\{x:|x|<\rho^\*\}\;\cup\;\{x:|x|=1\}.$$
Consequently there is **no** $x\in k$ with $\rho^\*\le |x|<1$, i.e. $|k^\times|\
cap[\rho^\*,1)=\varnothing$. This contradicts the density of $|k^\times|$. $\squ
are$

---

### Conclusion

Steps 2 and 3 force $|k^\times|$ to be discrete. A discrete subgroup of $\mathbb
{R}_{>0}^\times$ is of the form $\{c^n:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}$ for some $c>0$ — counta
ble and totally disconnected — so it **cannot** contain a nonempty interval $(a,
b)\subset\mathbb{R}$.

Therefore the image of $|\cdot|$ cannot contain a nonempty interval. $\blacksqua
re$
