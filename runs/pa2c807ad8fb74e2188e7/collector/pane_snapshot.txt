## Proof

**Step 1: Reformulate as a total variation distance.**

Let $p = 1-x \in (1/2, 1)$. Define two binomial PMFs:
$$P(k) = \binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k} = \binom{n}{k}(1-x)^k x^{n-k}, \qquad Q(k) =
 \binom{n}{k}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^k\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{n-k} = \frac{
\binom{n}{k}}{2^n}.$$

Then the sum is $S_n = \sum_{k=0}^n |P(k) - Q(k)|$, where $P = \mathrm{Bin}(n, 1
-x)$ and $Q = \mathrm{Bin}(n, 1/2)$.

Since $\sum P(k) = \sum Q(k) = 1$, we have $\sum(P(k)-Q(k))=0$, so:
$$S_n = 2\sum_{k:\, P(k)>Q(k)}(P(k)-Q(k)) = 2\,d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q).$$

**Step 2: Find the crossover point.**

$P(k) > Q(k)$ iff $\frac{P(k)}{Q(k)} > 1$, i.e.:
$$\frac{p^k(1-p)^{n-k}}{(1/2)^n} > 1 \iff 2^n p^k (1-p)^{n-k} > 1.$$

Taking logarithms (with $p=1-x$, $1-p=x$):
$$n\ln 2 + k\ln(1-x) + (n-k)\ln x > 0.$$

Solving for $k$:
$$k\left[\ln(1-x)-\ln x\right] > -n\left[\ln 2 + \ln x\right] = -n\ln(2x).$$

Since $x \in (0,1/2)$, we have $\ln\frac{1-x}{x} > 0$ and $\ln(2x) < 0$, so:
$$k > k^* := n\cdot\alpha, \qquad \alpha := \frac{\ln\frac{1}{2x}}{\ln\frac{1-x}
{x}}.$$

**Step 3: Show $\alpha \in (1/2,\, 1-x)$.**

*$\alpha > 1/2$:* This is equivalent to $\ln\frac{1}{2x} > \frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{1
-x}{x}$, i.e., $\frac{1}{2x} > \sqrt{\frac{1-x}{x}}$, i.e., $1 > 4x(1-x)$. The f
unction $f(x)=4x(1-x)$ satisfies $f(x) < f(1/2) = 1$ for all $x \in (0,1/2)$. ✓

*$\alpha < 1-x$:* This is equivalent to $\ln\frac{1}{2x} < (1-x)\ln\frac{1-x}{x}
$, i.e.:
$$-\ln 2 < (1-x)\ln(1-x) + x\ln x = -H(x),$$
where $H(x) = -(1-x)\ln(1-x) - x\ln x$ is the binary entropy. This requires $H(x
) < \ln 2 = H(1/2)$, which holds for all $x \in (0,1/2)$ since $H$ is strictly i
ncreasing on $(0,1/2)$. ✓

**Step 4: Evaluate the limit.**

Since $P(k) > Q(k)$ exactly when $k > k^* = \alpha n$:
$$S_n = 2\bigl(P(K > \alpha n) - Q(K > \alpha n)\bigr),$$
where $K \sim \mathrm{Bin}(n, 1-x)$ under $P$ and $K \sim \mathrm{Bin}(n, 1/2)$
under $Q$.

- **Under $P$:** $K/n \xrightarrow{p} 1-x > \alpha$, so $P(K > \alpha n) \to 1$.
- **Under $Q$:** $K/n \xrightarrow{p} 1/2 < \alpha$, so $Q(K > \alpha n) \to 0$.

(Both follow from the Weak Law of Large Numbers, since the threshold $\alpha n$
is at distance $\Theta(n)$ from each mean while the standard deviations are $O(\
sqrt{n})$.)

Therefore:
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} S_n = 2(1 - 0) = \boxed{2}.$$
