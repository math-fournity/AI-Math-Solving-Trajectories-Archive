\operatorname{diag}(ZZ^T\mathbf{1}) - ZZ^T.$$ Since $I_m$ is deterministic,
expectation distributes over the Kronecker product: $$\mathbb{E}\bigl[I_m
\otimes L\bigr] = I_m \otimes \mathbb{E}[L].$$ So it suffices to compute $
\mathbb{E}[L]$.

Step 1: $\mathbb{E}[ZZ^T]$. Since the entries $Z_1,\dots,Z_n$ are independent
with $\mathbb{E}[Z_i]=0$ and $\operatorname{Var}(Z_i)=\sigma^2$, $$(\mathbb{E}
[ZZ^T])_{ij} = \mathbb{E}[Z_i Z_j] = \begin{cases} \sigma^2, & i=j,\ 0, & i\neq
j,\end{cases}$$ hence $\mathbb{E}[ZZ^T] = \sigma^2 I_n$.

Step 2: $\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{diag}(ZZ^T\mathbf{1})]$. The $i$-th component
of $ZZ^T\mathbf{1}$ is $$(ZZ^T\mathbf{1})i = \sum{j=1}^n Z_i Z_j.$$ Taking
expectation, $$\mathbb{E}\bigl[(ZZ^T\mathbf{1})i\bigr] = \sum{j=1}^n \mathbb{E}
[Z_i Z_j] = \mathbb{E}[Z_i^2] + \sum_{j\neq i}\underbrace{\mathbb{E}
[Z_i]\mathbb{E}[Z_j]}_{=0} = \sigma^2.$$ Thus every diagonal entry of $
\operatorname{diag}(ZZ^T\mathbf{1})$ has expectation $\sigma^2$, giving $$
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\operatorname{diag}(ZZ^T\mathbf{1})\bigr] = \sigma^2 I_n.$$

Step 3: $\mathbb{E}[L]$. Combining Steps 1 and 2, $$\mathbb{E}[L] =
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\operatorname{diag}(ZZ^T\mathbf{1})\bigr] - \mathbb{E}[ZZ^T] =
\sigma^2 I_n - \sigma^2 I_n = \mathbf{0}_{n\times n}.$$

Conclusion. $$\mathbb{E}\bigl[I_m \otimes (\operatorname{diag}(ZZ^T\mathbf{1}) -
ZZ^T)\bigr] = I_m \otimes \mathbf{0}{n\times n} = \mathbf{0}{mn \times mn}.$$
