## Proof

**Step 1: The canonical topology on $Set_G$.**

$Set_G$ is a Grothendieck topos (equivalent to $\mathrm{Set}^{BG}$). In any topo
s, every epimorphism is a universal effective epimorphism. The canonical topolog
y — the largest topology for which all representables are sheaves — therefore ha
s as covering families the jointly epimorphic families. In $Set_G$, jointly epim
orphic = jointly surjective (on underlying sets). So **coverings are jointly sur
jective families of $G$-maps**.

**Step 2: The covering $q: G \to G/H$.**

Let $G$ denote the regular left $G$-set. The quotient map $q(g) = gH$ is a surje
ctive $G$-map, hence a covering.

**Step 3: The fiber product $G \times_{G/H} G$.**

$$G \times_{G/H} G = \{(g_1,g_2) : g_1H = g_2H\}, \quad k\cdot(g_1,g_2)=(kg_1,kg
_2).$$

Define $\phi: G\times H \to G\times_{G/H}G$ by $(g,h)\mapsto(g,gh)$. This is a b
ijection (inverse: $(g_1,g_2)\mapsto(g_1,\, g_1^{-1}g_2)$, with $g_1^{-1}g_2\in
H$). The $G$-action transports to $k\cdot(g,h)=(kg,h)$, so $H$ is a trivial inde
xing set. Therefore
$$G\times_{G/H}G \;\cong\; G\times H \;\cong\; \coprod_{h\in H} G \quad\text{(as
 $G$-sets).}$$

**Step 4: Applying the sheaf condition.**

Since $S$ is a sheaf, $S(G/H)$ is the equalizer of
$$S(G) \rightrightarrows S(G\times_{G/H}G).$$
Using $S(\coprod_{h} G)=\prod_h S(G)=S(G)^H$ (coproducts are covered by their su
mmands), and identifying the two projections under $\phi$:

- $\pi_1\circ\phi(g,h)=g$: on the $h$-th copy this is $\mathrm{id}_G$, so $S(\pi
_1)(a) = (a)_{h\in H}$ (constant tuple).
- $\pi_2\circ\phi(g,h)=gh = R_h(g)$: on the $h$-th copy this is **right multipli
cation** $R_h:G\to G$.

Note $R_h$ is a $G$-map since left and right multiplication commute: $R_h(k\cdot
 g)=kgh=k\cdot R_h(g)$. Hence
$$S(\pi_2)(a)=\bigl(S(R_h)(a)\bigr)_{h\in H}.$$

The equalizer condition $S(\pi_1)(a)=S(\pi_2)(a)$ becomes $a = S(R_h)(a)$ for ev
ery $h\in H$. Therefore:

$$\boxed{\,S(G/H)\;\cong\;\{\,a\in S(G) : S(R_h)(a)=a\ \text{for all }h\in H\,\}
\;=:\;S(G)^H.\,}$$

**Step 5: The natural $G$-action on $S(G)$.**

The $G$-endomorphisms of the regular $G$-set are exactly the right multiplicatio
ns: a $G$-map $f:G\to G$ satisfies $f(k)=f(k\cdot e)=k\cdot f(e)=kf(e)$, so $f=R
_{f(e)}$. Since $R_g\circ R_h = R_{hg}$, we get $S(R_h)\circ S(R_g)=S(R_{hg})$.
Defining
$$g\cdot a := S(R_g)(a)$$
gives a **left $G$-action** on $S(G)$:
$$h\cdot(g\cdot a)=S(R_h)\bigl(S(R_g)(a)\bigr)=S(R_{hg})(a)=(hg)\cdot a.$$
Under this action, $S(G)^H=\{a\in S(G): h\cdot a = a\ \forall\, h\in H\}$ is the
 subgroup of $H$-invariants.

**Step 6: Why normality matters — the $G/H$-module structure.**

For $a\in S(G)^H$, $g\in G$, $h\in H$:
$$h\cdot(g\cdot a)=(hg)\cdot a = \bigl(g\cdot g^{-1}hg\bigr)\cdot a = g\cdot\und
erbrace{(g^{-1}hg\cdot a)}_{=\,a,\ \text{since }g^{-1}hg\in H\trianglelefteq G}
= g\cdot a.$$
So $g\cdot a\in S(G)^H$: the $G$-action **preserves** $S(G)^H$. Since $H$ acts t
rivially on $S(G)^H$ by definition, the action **descends to $G/H$**. Thus:

> **$S(G/H)\cong S(G)^H$ is naturally a $\mathbb{Z}[G/H]$-module.**

**Remark (conceptual framing).** Sheaves of abelian groups on $Set_G$ (canonical
 topology) are equivalent to $\mathbb{Z}[G]$-modules $M$, via $S(X)=\mathrm{Hom}
_G(X,M)$ with $M=S(G)$ and $g\cdot m = S(R_g)(m)$. Under this equivalence, $S(G/
H)=M^H$ (the $H$-invariants), and for $H\trianglelefteq G$ this carries a natura
l $\mathbb{Z}[G/H]$-module structure.
