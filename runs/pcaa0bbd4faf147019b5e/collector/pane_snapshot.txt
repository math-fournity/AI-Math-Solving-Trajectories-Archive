## Solution

We want to evaluate
$$L = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\ln\!\left(3^{p_{n+2}} - 1\right)}{\ln\!\left(3^
{p_{n+1}} - 1\right)},$$
where $p_k$ denotes the $k$-th prime.

**Step 1. Reduce the logarithms to $p_k \ln 3$.**

For any index $k$, factor out the dominant term:
$$\ln\!\left(3^{p_k} - 1\right) = \ln\!\left(3^{p_k}\bigl(1 - 3^{-p_k}\bigr)\rig
ht) = p_k \ln 3 + \ln\!\left(1 - 3^{-p_k}\right).$$

Since $p_k \to \infty$, we have $3^{-p_k} \to 0$, and therefore $\ln(1 - 3^{-p_k
}) \to 0$. Hence
$$\ln\!\left(3^{p_k} - 1\right) \sim p_k \ln 3 \quad (k \to \infty).$$

Applying this with $k = n+2$ and $k = n+1$,
$$L = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{p_{n+2}\ln 3 + o(1)}{p_{n+1}\ln 3 + o(1)} = \lim_{
n\to\infty} \frac{p_{n+2}}{p_{n+1}}.$$

**Step 2. Apply the Prime Number Theorem.**

The Prime Number Theorem gives the asymptotic
$$p_k \sim k \ln k \quad (k \to \infty).$$

Therefore
$$\frac{p_{n+2}}{p_{n+1}} \sim \frac{(n+2)\ln(n+2)}{(n+1)\ln(n+1)}.$$

Since $\dfrac{n+2}{n+1} \to 1$ and $\dfrac{\ln(n+2)}{\ln(n+1)} \to 1$ (as $\ln(n
+2) - \ln(n+1) = \ln\!\tfrac{n+2}{n+1} \to 0$), we obtain
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{p_{n+2}}{p_{n+1}} = 1.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{L = 1}.$$
