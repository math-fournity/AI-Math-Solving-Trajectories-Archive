Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the stated condition (for e
very point and every countable decreasing local base, some base element's closur

**Answer: Yes, it implies $X$ is regular.**

**Proof.**

We interpret the hypothesis as: for every point $x \in X$ and every countable de
creasing local base $(U_n)_{n\ge 1}$ of open neighborhoods of $x$, there exists
an integer $n$ with $\overline{U_n} \subseteq U_1$.

We show $X$ is regular, i.e., for every $x \in X$ and every open neighborhood $V
$ of $x$, there exists an open neighborhood $W$ of $x$ with $\overline{W} \subse
teq V$.

Let $x \in X$ and let $V$ be an open neighborhood of $x$. Since $X$ is first cou
ntable, $x$ admits a countable local base $\{B_n : n \ge 1\}$. Define
$$U_n \;=\; V \cap B_1 \cap B_2 \cap \cdots \cap B_n, \qquad n \ge 1.$$

**Claim:** $(U_n)_{n\ge 1}$ is a countable decreasing local base at $x$ with $U_
1 \subseteq V$.

- *Open neighborhoods of $x$:* Each $U_n$ is a finite intersection of open sets,
 hence open; and $x \in V$ and $x \in B_k$ for all $k$, so $x \in U_n$.
- *Decreasing:* $U_{n+1} = U_n \cap B_{n+1} \subseteq U_n$.
- *Local base:* For any open set $O$ containing $x$, there exists $k$ with $B_k
\subseteq O$ (since $\{B_n\}$ is a local base), so $U_k \subseteq B_k \subseteq
O$.
- *Containment:* $U_1 = V \cap B_1 \subseteq V$.

Applying the hypothesis to this base, there exists $n$ such that
$$\overline{U_n} \subseteq U_1 \subseteq V.$$

Setting $W = U_n$, we have found an open neighborhood $W$ of $x$ with $\overline
{W} \subseteq V$.

Since $x$ and $V$ were arbitrary, $X$ is regular at every point. (The Hausdorff
condition ensures $X$ is $T_1$, so combined with the regularity property, $X$ is
 $T_3$-regular.) $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The key insight is that first countability lets us *build* a decreas
ing local base whose first element $U_1$ sits inside any prescribed open neighbo
rhood $V$. The hypothesis—applicable to *every* such base—then yields a smaller
base element whose closure lands inside $U_1 \subseteq V$. Thus the apparently w
eak condition "$\overline{U_n} \subseteq U_1$" (only about the *first* element o
f the base) is in fact equivalent to full regularity, because we can always re-i
ndex any target neighborhood into the $U_1$ position.
