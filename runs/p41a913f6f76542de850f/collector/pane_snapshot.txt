## Proof

**Answer: Yes.**

Let $T$ be a closed symmetric operator on a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$. Suppose
 there exists a non-real complex number $\lambda \in \rho(T)$ (the resolvent set
) such that both $T - \lambda I$ and $T - \bar{\lambda} I$ map onto $\mathcal{H}
$.

**Key facts for closed symmetric operators:**

1. **Injectivity at non-real points:** For any non-real $\mu$, the operator $T -
 \mu I$ is injective. Indeed, if $Tx = \mu x$ with $x \neq 0$, then $\mu\langle
x, x\rangle = \langle Tx, x\rangle = \langle x, Tx\rangle = \bar{\mu}\langle x,
x\rangle$, giving $(\mu - \bar{\mu})\|x\|^2 = 0$, a contradiction since $\mu$ is
 non-real.

2. **Closed range and orthogonal complement:** For non-real $\mu$, $\operatornam
e{Ran}(T - \mu I)$ is closed, and
$$\operatorname{Ran}(T - \mu I)^\perp = \ker(T^* - \bar{\mu} I).$$
This follows because $y \in \operatorname{Ran}(T-\mu I)^\perp$ iff $\langle (T-\
mu I)x, y\rangle = 0$ for all $x \in \mathcal{D}(T)$, which is equivalent to $y
\in \mathcal{D}(T^*)$ and $T^*y = \bar{\mu}y$.

3. **Constancy of deficiency indices on half-planes:** The dimension $n_+ := \di
m\ker(T^* - \mu I)$ is constant for all $\mu$ with $\operatorname{Im}(\mu) > 0$,
 and $n_- := \dim\ker(T^* - \mu I)$ is constant for all $\mu$ with $\operatornam
e{Im}(\mu) < 0$. (This is a standard result: the deficiency subspaces at differe
nt points in the same half-plane are isomorphic via bounded invertible maps cons
tructed from the resolvent.)

4. **Resolvent criterion:** For a closed operator, $\mu \in \rho(T)$ iff $T - \m
u I$ is bijective (the boundedness of the inverse then follows from the closed g
raph theorem).

5. **Self-adjointness criterion:** A closed symmetric operator $T$ is self-adjoi
nt if and only if $n_+ = n_- = 0$.

**Applying the hypotheses:**

Since $\lambda \in \rho(T)$ and $\lambda$ is non-real, $T - \lambda I$ is biject
ive, hence surjective.

**Case 1: $\operatorname{Im}(\lambda) > 0$.** Then $\bar{\lambda}$ has $\operato
rname{Im}(\bar{\lambda}) < 0$.
- From $\lambda \in \rho(T)$: $\operatorname{Ran}(T - \lambda I) = \mathcal{H}$,
 so $\ker(T^* - \bar{\lambda} I) = \{0\}$. Since $\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\lambda
}) < 0$, this gives $n_- = 0$.
- From $T - \bar{\lambda} I$ surjective: $\operatorname{Ran}(T - \bar{\lambda} I
) = \mathcal{H}$, so $\ker(T^* - \lambda I) = \{0\}$. Since $\operatorname{Im}(\
lambda) > 0$, this gives $n_+ = 0$.

**Case 2: $\operatorname{Im}(\lambda) < 0$.** Then $\bar{\lambda}$ has $\operato
rname{Im}(\bar{\lambda}) > 0$.
- From $\lambda \in \rho(T)$: surjectivity of $T - \lambda I$ gives $\ker(T^* -
\bar{\lambda} I) = \{0\}$. Since $\operatorname{Im}(\bar{\lambda}) > 0$, this gi
ves $n_+ = 0$.
- From $T - \bar{\lambda} I$ surjective: $\ker(T^* - \lambda I) = \{0\}$. Since
$\operatorname{Im}(\lambda) < 0$, this gives $n_- = 0$.

In both cases, $n_+ = n_- = 0$. By the self-adjointness criterion (fact 5), $T$
is self-adjoint. $\blacksquare$
