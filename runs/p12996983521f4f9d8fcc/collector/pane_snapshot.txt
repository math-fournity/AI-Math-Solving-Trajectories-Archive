## Proof

We work on the torus $\mathbb{T}$, expanding $u_0 \in L^2(\mathbb{T})$ in Fourie
r series: $u_0 = \sum_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} \hat{u}_0(k) e^{ikx}$.

The Schrödinger propagator acts diagonally:
$$e^{it\partial_x^2} u_0 = \sum_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} e^{-itk^2}\hat{u}_0(k)\,e^{ik
x}.$$

Therefore:
$$u_\varepsilon = \frac{e^{i\varepsilon\partial_x^2}u_0 - u_0}{\varepsilon} = \s
um_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} \frac{e^{-i\varepsilon k^2}-1}{\varepsilon}\,\hat{u}_0(k)\
,e^{ikx}.$$

The $H^{-2}$ norm is:
$$\|u_\varepsilon\|_{H^{-2}}^2 = \sum_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} (1+k^2)^{-2}\left|\frac
{e^{-i\varepsilon k^2}-1}{\varepsilon}\right|^2 |\hat{u}_0(k)|^2.$$

**Key estimate on the multiplier.** Using $|e^{-i\theta}-1| = 2|\sin(\theta/2)|
\leq |\theta|$ (from $|\sin x| \leq |x|$):

$$\left|\frac{e^{-i\varepsilon k^2}-1}{\varepsilon}\right| = \frac{2|\sin(\varep
silon k^2/2)|}{\varepsilon} \leq \frac{2 \cdot \varepsilon k^2/2}{\varepsilon} =
 k^2.$$

**Combining with the $H^{-2}$ weight.** Since $(1+k^2)^2 = 1 + 2k^2 + k^4 \geq k
^4$, we have $(1+k^2)^{-2}\,k^4 \leq 1$. Therefore:

$$\|u_\varepsilon\|_{H^{-2}}^2 \leq \sum_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} (1+k^2)^{-2}\,k^4\,|
\hat{u}_0(k)|^2 \leq \sum_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} |\hat{u}_0(k)|^2 = \|u_0\|_{L^2}^2.
$$

This bound is independent of $\varepsilon > 0$. Since $u_0 \in L^2(\mathbb{T})$,
 the right-hand side is finite. Hence:

$$\sup_{\varepsilon > 0} \|u_\varepsilon\|_{H^{-2}(\mathbb{T})} \leq \|u_0\|_{L^
2(\mathbb{T})} < \infty.$$

The family $\{u_\varepsilon\}_{\varepsilon > 0}$ is uniformly bounded in $H^{-2}
(\mathbb{T})$.

**Answer: Yes.**
