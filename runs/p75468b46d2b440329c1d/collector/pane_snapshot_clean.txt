## Proof

ypothesis states $|T_n| \le 1$ for all $n \ge 1$ and all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$.

**Step 1: Bounding individual terms.**

For $n \ge 1$, since $T_n - T_{n-1} = 3^n\bigl(g(x+ny)-g(x-ny)\bigr)$ (with $T_0

$$\bigl|3^n\bigl(g(x+ny)-g(x-ny)\bigr)\bigr| = |T_n - T_{n-1}| \le |T_n| + |T_{n
-1}| \le 1 + 1 = 2.$$

Therefore, for all $n \ge 1$:
$$\bigl|g(x+ny) - g(x-ny)\bigr| \le \frac{2}{3^n}. \tag{$\star$}$$

(For $n=1$, using $T_0=0$ gives the sharper $|a_1| \le 1/3$, but the bound $2/3^
n$ suffices for all $n$.)

**Step 2: g is constant.**

$$x = \frac{u+v}{2}, \qquad y = \frac{u-v}{2n}.$$

Then $x + ny = \frac{u+v}{2} + n\cdot\frac{u-v}{2n} = u$ and $x - ny = v$. Apply
ing $(\star)$:

$$|g(u) - g(v)| \le \frac{2}{3^n} \quad \text{for all } n \ge 1.$$

Since $\frac{2}{3^n} \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$, we conclude $|g(u) - g(v)| = 0$,

$$\boxed{g(2019) = 17}$$
