## Proof

**Claim**: Yes, such a matrix $R$ exists. In fact, $R = -2I$ satisfies $P \prec
RPR^T$ for any symmetric positive definite $P$, while $R \prec -I$.

---

### Part 1: A matrix $R$ with $R \prec -I$ and $P \prec RPR^T$ exists

Let $P$ be any symmetric positive definite matrix. Set $R = -2I$.

- **$R \prec -I$**: $R + I = -2I + I = -I \prec 0$. ✓
- **$P \prec RPR^T$**: $RPR^T = (-2I)\,P\,(-2I)^T = 4P$, so
$$RPR^T - P = 4P - P = 3P \succ 0,$$
since $P \succ 0$. Hence $P \prec RPR^T$. ✓

More generally, $R = cI$ for any scalar $c$ with $|c| > 1$ gives $RPR^T = c^2 P
\succ P$, and $c < -1$ ensures $R \prec -I$.

---

### Part 2: The inequality does NOT hold for all $R \prec -I$ (counterexample)

Take
$$P = \begin{pmatrix} 100 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \succ 0, \qquad R = \begin{
pmatrix} -2 & 0.5 \\ 0.5 & -2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

**Check $R \prec -I$**: $R + I = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0.5 \\ 0.5 & -1 \end{pmatr
ix}$ has eigenvalues $-1 \pm 0.5 \in \{-0.5,\, -1.5\}$, both negative, so $R + I
 \prec 0$. ✓

**Compute $RPR^T$**:
$$RPR^T = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 0.5 \\ 0.5 & -2 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 100
& 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} -2 & 0.5 \\ 0.5 & -2 \end{pmatrix} = \
begin{pmatrix} 400.25 & -101 \\ -101 & 29 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Then
$$RPR^T - P = \begin{pmatrix} 300.25 & -101 \\ -101 & 28 \end{pmatrix}.$$

The determinant is
$$\det(RPR^T - P) = 300.25 \times 28 - 101^2 = 8407 - 10201 = -1794 < 0.$$

Since the $(1,1)$-entry $300.25 > 0$ but $\det < 0$, the matrix $RPR^T - P$ is *
*indefinite**, so $P \not\prec RPR^T$.

---

### Conclusion

The inequality $P \prec RPR^T$ fails for some $R \prec -I$ (Part 2), yet it succ
eeds for $R = -2I \prec -I$ (Part 1). Therefore, **yes**, there exists a matrix
$R$ (with $R \prec -I$) such that $P \prec RPR^T$ holds, even though the inequal
ity is not universally valid over all $R \prec -I$.
