## Solution

The region is defined by $(x^2+y^2+z^2)^2 = x$. Setting $r^2 = x^2+y^2+z^2$, the
 boundary becomes $r^4 = x$. Since $r \ge 0$, we need $x \ge 0$. Also $x \le r$
(as $x$ is one coordinate), so $r^4 \le r$, giving $r^3 \le 1$, i.e. $r \le 1$.
The region is bounded.

**Spherical coordinates.** Let $x = r\sin\phi\cos\theta$, $y = r\sin\phi\sin\the
ta$, $z = r\cos\phi$, with $r\ge 0$, $\phi\in[0,\pi]$, $\theta\in[0,2\pi)$. The
boundary equation $r^4 = x$ becomes

$$r^3 = \sin\phi\cos\theta, \qquad r = (\sin\phi\cos\theta)^{1/3}.$$

Since $\sin\phi \ge 0$ on $[0,\pi]$, we need $\cos\theta \ge 0$, i.e. $\theta \i
n [-\tfrac{\pi}{2}, \tfrac{\pi}{2}]$.

**Volume integral.** The region is $0 \le r \le (\sin\phi\cos\theta)^{1/3}$, so

$$V = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\!\int_{0}^{\pi}\!\int_{0}^{(\sin\phi\cos\theta)^{1/3
}} r^2\sin\phi\;dr\,d\phi\,d\theta.$$

**Inner integral** (in $r$):

$$\int_{0}^{(\sin\phi\cos\theta)^{1/3}} r^2\,dr = \frac{1}{3}(\sin\phi\cos\theta
).$$

**Middle integral** (in $\phi$):

$$\int_{0}^{\pi} \frac{1}{3}\sin\phi\cos\theta\cdot\sin\phi\;d\phi = \frac{\cos\
theta}{3}\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^2\phi\,d\phi = \frac{\cos\theta}{3}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2
} = \frac{\pi\cos\theta}{6}.$$

**Outer integral** (in $\theta$):

$$V = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\frac{\pi\cos\theta}{6}\,d\theta = \frac{\pi}{6}\cdot
\Big[\sin\theta\Big]_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} = \frac{\pi}{6}\cdot 2 = \frac{\pi}{3}.$$

**Verification of boundedness and closure.** The surface $r^4 = x$ with $x\ge 0$
 encloses a compact region: $r \le 1$ as shown, and $r=0$ is included (the origi
n satisfies the equation). The Jacobian $r^2\sin\phi$ is non-negative throughout
, and the parametrization covers the full region since every point with $x\ge 0$
 inside the surface has a unique $(r,\phi,\theta)$ with $\cos\theta\ge 0$.

$$\boxed{V = \dfrac{\pi}{3}}$$
