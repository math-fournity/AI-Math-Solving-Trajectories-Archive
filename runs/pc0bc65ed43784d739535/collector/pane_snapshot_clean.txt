Looking at this problem, I need to find the number of pairs $(x,y) \in \mathbb{N

## Step 1: Establish that $x, y$ must be perfect squares

Rewrite the equation as:
$$\frac{\sqrt{y} - \sqrt{x}}{\sqrt{xy}} = \frac{1}{2016} \implies 2016(\sqrt{y}
- \sqrt{x}) = \sqrt{xy}$$

Squaring both sides:
$$2016^2(y - 2\sqrt{xy} + x) = xy \implies 2016^2(x+y) - xy = 2 \cdot 2016^2 \sq
rt{xy}$$

The left side is rational, so $\sqrt{xy}$ must be rational, meaning $xy$ is a pe
rfect square. Write $x = d\,a^2$, $y = d\,b^2$ where $d$ is squarefree. Then $\s
qrt{xy} = abd$ and the equation $2016(b-a)\sqrt{d} = abd$ gives $2016(b-a) = ab\
sqrt{d}$. Since the left side is an integer and $d$ is squarefree, we need $d =
1$.

**Therefore $x = a^2$ and $y = b^2$ are perfect squares**, with $a < b$ (since t
he left side is positive).

## Step 2: Reduce to a divisor counting problem

The equation becomes:
$$\frac{1}{a} - \frac{1}{b} = \frac{1}{2016} \implies 2016(b-a) = ab$$

Rearranging: $ab - 2016b + 2016a = 0$, so:
$$(a - 2016)(b + 2016) = -2016^2$$

Set $u = a - 2016$, $v = b + 2016$. Then $uv = -2016^2$.

**Constraints:**
- $a \geq 1 \implies u \geq -2015$, i.e., $u \geq -2015$
- $b > a \geq 1 \implies v = b + 2016 \geq 2018 > 0$
- Since $uv = -2016^2 < 0$ and $v > 0$, we need $u < 0$.

Set $s = -u > 0$, so $sv = 2016^2$ with $s \leq 2015$ (from $u \geq -2015$) and
$v = 2016^2/s$.

**Checking $b > a$ is automatic:** $b - a = (v - 2016) - (2016 - s) = v + s - 40
32 = \frac{2016^2}{s} + s - 4032$. By AM-GM, $\frac{2016^2}{s} + s \geq 2 \cdot
2016 = 4032$, with equality iff $s = 2016$. Since $s \leq 2015 < 2016$, the ineq
uality is strict, so $b > a$ always holds. ✓

**Integrality:** $v = 2016^2/s$ must be a positive integer, so $s \mid 2016^2$.

## Step 3: Count the divisors

The number of solutions equals the number of positive divisors $s$ of $2016^2$ w
ith $s \leq 2015$ (equivalently $s < 2016$, since $2015 = 5 \cdot 13 \cdot 31$ i

**Factorization:** $2016 = 2^5 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 7$, so $2016^2 = 2^{10} \cdot 3^4

**Total divisors:** $(10+1)(4+1)(2+1) = 11 \cdot 5 \cdot 3 = 165$.

**Pairing argument:** Divisors of $2016^2$ pair as $(s,\, 2016^2/s)$. The unique

Therefore, the number of divisors $s < 2016$ is $\frac{165 - 1}{2} = 82$.
