## Proof

**Notation.** Recall $(I:r^n) = \{x \in R : xr^n \in I\}$. This is an ascending
chain:
$$I \subseteq (I:r) \subseteq (I:r^2) \subseteq \cdots \subseteq R,$$
since if $xr^n \in I$, then $xr^{n+1} = (xr^n)\cdot r \in I$ (as $I$ is an ideal
).

**Key equivalence.** We first observe:
$$(I:r^n) = R \iff r^n \in I.$$
Indeed, if $r^n \in I$, then for every $x \in R$, $xr^n \in I$ (since $I$ is an
ideal), giving $(I:r^n) = R$. Conversely, if $(I:r^n) = R$, then $1 \in (I:r^n)$
, so $1 \cdot r^n = r^n \in I$.

**Step 1: The chain stabilizes.** Since $\{(I:r^n)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ is finite a
s a set and the chain is ascending, it stabilizes: there exists $N \in \mathbb{N
}$ such that
$$(I:r^N) = (I:r^{N+1}) = (I:r^{N+2}) = \cdots.$$

**Step 2: An intersection identity at stabilization.** We claim:
$$I = (I:r^N) \cap \bigl(I + (r^N)\bigr).$$

*Proof of the identity.* The inclusion $I \subseteq (I:r^N) \cap (I + (r^N))$ is
 clear: if $a \in I$, then $ar^N \in I$ (so $a \in (I:r^N)$), and $a \in I \subs
eteq I + (r^N)$.

For the reverse inclusion, let $x \in (I:r^N) \cap (I + (r^N))$. Then $xr^N \in
I$ and $x = a + b\,r^N$ for some $a \in I$, $b \in R$. Computing:
$$xr^N = a\,r^N + b\,r^{2N}.$$
Since $a \in I$, we have $ar^N \in I$, so $br^{2N} = xr^N - ar^N \in I$, i.e., $
b \in (I:r^{2N})$. By stabilization, $(I:r^{2N}) = (I:r^N)$, so $b \in (I:r^N)$,
 meaning $br^N \in I$. Therefore:
$$x = a + br^N \in I. \qquad \square$$

**Step 3: Applying irreducibility.** Since $I$ is irreducible and
$$I = (I:r^N) \cap \bigl(I + (r^N)\bigr),$$
we must have either $(I:r^N) = I$ or $I + (r^N) = I$.

Now, $(I:r) \neq I$ by hypothesis, and the chain is ascending, so:
$$(I:r^N) \supseteq (I:r) \supsetneq I.$$
Thus $(I:r^N) \neq I$, forcing the second alternative:
$$I + (r^N) = I,$$
which means $r^N \in I$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.** Since $r^N \in I$, by the key equivalence, $(I:r^N) = R$
. Therefore the set
$$\{n \in \mathbb{N},\; n \geq 1 : r^n \in I\}$$
is nonempty (it contains $N$). The smallest $n$ such that $(I:r^n) = R$ is:
$$\boxed{n = \min\{\,m \in \mathbb{N},\; m \geq 1 : r^m \in I\,\}.}$$

This minimum exists and is finite by the argument above. Specifically, it equals
 the stabilization index of the chain $\{(I:r^n)\}$, which is the first index at
 which the chain reaches $R$.
