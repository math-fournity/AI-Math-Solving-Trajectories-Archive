## Proof

**Claim:** The statement is **true**. If $A$ is a positive operator on a complex
 Hilbert space $H$ with an ONB of eigenvectors $\{e_j\}$ (eigenvalues $c_j$), th
en $\sqrt{A}\, e_j = \sqrt{c_j}\, e_j$ for every $j$.

---

### Setup and the spectral representation

Since $A$ is positive, every eigenvalue satisfies $c_j \ge 0$ (real). Because $\
{e_j\}$ is an ONB of eigenvectors, $A$ admits the spectral representation

$$A x \;=\; \sum_j c_j\,\langle x, e_j\rangle\, e_j,$$

with domain $D(A) = \bigl\{x \in H : \sum_j c_j^{\,2}\,|\langle x,e_j\rangle|^2
< \infty\bigr\}$ (this matters only when $A$ is unbounded; when $A$ is bounded t
he series converges for all $x$).

### The candidate square root

Define the operator $B$ by

$$B x \;=\; \sum_j \sqrt{c_j}\,\langle x, e_j\rangle\, e_j, \qquad D(B) = \Bigl\
{x \in H : \sum_j c_j\,|\langle x,e_j\rangle|^2 < \infty\Bigr\}.$$

Since $c_j \ge 0$, $\sqrt{c_j}$ is a well-defined non-negative real number. Note
 $D(A) \subseteq D(B)$ because $c_j^2 \ge c_j$ is false in general — actually we
 need $c_j \le c_j^2$ when $c_j \ge 1$ and $c_j \ge c_j^2$ when $0 \le c_j \le 1
$. The correct inclusion is: $D(A) \subseteq D(B)$ holds because $c_j \le \max(c
_j, c_j^2)$, so $\sum c_j^2 |\langle x,e_j\rangle|^2 < \infty \Rightarrow \sum c
_j |\langle x,e_j\rangle|^2 < \infty$ (using $c_j \le 1 + c_j^2$). In any case e
ach $e_j \in D(B)$ since the defining sum is a single finite term.

### $B$ is positive

For $x \in D(B)$,

$$\langle Bx, x\rangle \;=\; \sum_j \sqrt{c_j}\,|\langle x,e_j\rangle|^2 \;\ge\;
 0,$$

so $B$ is positive. Also $B$ is self-adjoint (it is diagonal in an ONB with real
 entries), hence $B$ is a positive self-adjoint operator.

### $B^2 = A$

For $x \in D(A) \subseteq D(B)$, we have $Bx \in D(B)$ (one checks $Bx$ has coef
ficients $\sqrt{c_j}\langle x,e_j\rangle$, and $\sum c_j |\sqrt{c_j}\langle x,e_
j\rangle|^2 = \sum c_j^2 |\langle x,e_j\rangle|^2 < \infty$ since $x \in D(A)$).
 Therefore

$$B^2 x \;=\; B(Bx) \;=\; \sum_j \sqrt{c_j}\,\langle Bx, e_j\rangle\, e_j \;=\;
\sum_j \sqrt{c_j}\cdot\sqrt{c_j}\,\langle x,e_j\rangle\, e_j \;=\; \sum_j c_j\,\
langle x,e_j\rangle\, e_j \;=\; A x.$$

Thus $B^2 = A$ on $D(A)$, and $D(B^2) = D(A)$ (a direct computation of the domai
n of $B^2$ gives exactly $\sum c_j^2 |\langle x,e_j\rangle|^2 < \infty$). So $B^
2 = A$ as operators.

### Uniqueness of the positive square root

By the spectral theorem (functional calculus for self-adjoint operators), the po
sitive square root of a positive operator is unique. Since $B$ is positive and $
B^2 = A$, we conclude $B = \sqrt{A}$.

### Eigenvalues of $\sqrt{A}$

For each $j$, since $e_j \in D(B) = D(\sqrt{A})$,

$$\sqrt{A}\, e_j \;=\; B\, e_j \;=\; \sum_k \sqrt{c_k}\,\langle e_j, e_k\rangle\
, e_k \;=\; \sqrt{c_j}\, e_j.$$

Hence $e_j$ is an eigenvector of $\sqrt{A}$ with eigenvalue $\sqrt{c_j}$.

### Conclusion

The statement is **true**: $\sqrt{c_j}$ are eigenvalues of $\sqrt{A}$ with eigen
vectors $\{e_j\}$.
