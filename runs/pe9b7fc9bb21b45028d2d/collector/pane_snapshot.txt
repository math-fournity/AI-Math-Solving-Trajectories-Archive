## Proof

Let $M = \begin{bmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{bmatrix}$ with each block $n \times
 n$, and $J = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & I \\ -I & 0 \end{bmatrix}$. Over $\mathbb{R}$,
 $M^* = M^\top$. We establish the equivalence of

**(I)** $M^* J M = J$, and

**(II)** $A^* C$ and $B^* D$ are symmetric, and $A^* D - C^* B = I$.

### Computing $M^* J M$

Since $M^* = \begin{bmatrix} A^* & C^* \\ B^* & D^* \end{bmatrix}$, first multip
ly:

$$JM = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & I \\ -I & 0 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} A & B \\ C &
 D \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} C & D \\ -A & -B \end{bmatrix}.$$

Then

$$M^* J M = \begin{bmatrix} A^* & C^* \\ B^* & D^* \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
C & D \\ -A & -B \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} A^* C - C^* A & A^* D - C^* B \
\ B^* C - D^* A & B^* D - D^* B \end{bmatrix}.$$

### (I) $\Rightarrow$ (II)

Assume $M^* J M = J = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & I \\ -I & 0 \end{bmatrix}$. Equating b
locks:

- **Top-left:** $A^* C - C^* A = 0$, i.e. $A^* C = C^* A = (A^* C)^*$. Hence $A^
* C$ is symmetric.
- **Top-right:** $A^* D - C^* B = I$.
- **Bottom-right:** $B^* D - D^* B = 0$, i.e. $B^* D = D^* B = (B^* D)^*$. Hence
 $B^* D$ is symmetric.

(The bottom-left block gives $B^* C - D^* A = -I$, which is the adjoint of the t
op-right condition and is automatically satisfied once $A^* D - C^* B = I$ holds
, since $(A^* D - C^* B)^* = D^* A - B^* C = I^* = I$.)

Thus (II) holds.

### (II) $\Rightarrow$ (I)

Assume $A^* C$ is symmetric, $B^* D$ is symmetric, and $A^* D - C^* B = I$. Then
:

- **Top-left:** $A^* C - C^* A = A^* C - (A^* C)^* = 0$, using symmetry of $A^*
C$.
- **Top-right:** $A^* D - C^* B = I$ by hypothesis.
- **Bottom-left:** $B^* C - D^* A = -(D^* A - B^* C) = -(A^* D - C^* B)^* = -I^*
 = -I$, using the hypothesis and the fact that $I^* = I$.
- **Bottom-right:** $B^* D - D^* B = B^* D - (B^* D)^* = 0$, using symmetry of $
B^* D$.

Therefore

$$M^* J M = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & I \\ -I & 0 \end{bmatrix} = J,$$

so (I) holds.

### Conclusion

Conditions (I) and (II) are equivalent. The four block equations of $M^* J M = J
$ reduce to three independent conditions — symmetry of $A^* C$, symmetry of $B^*
 D$, and $A^* D - C^* B = I$ — because the bottom-left block equation is the adj
oint of the top-right one and carries no independent information.
