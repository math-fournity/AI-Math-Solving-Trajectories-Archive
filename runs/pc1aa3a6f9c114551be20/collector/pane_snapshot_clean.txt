## Proof

**The action.** Define $g\colon S^1\times S^1 \to S^1\times S^1$ by $g(w,z) = (i
w, iz)$. Since $i^4 = 1$, we have $g^4 = \mathrm{id}$, and
$$g^2(w,z) = (-w,-z), \qquad g^3(w,z) = (-iw,-iz).$$
So $g$ generates an action of $G = \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ on the torus $T = S^1
\times S^1$.

**The action is free.** Suppose $g^k(w,z) = (w,z)$ for some $0 \le k \le 3$. The
n $i^k w = w$ and $i^k z = z$. For $k = 1,2,3$ we have $i^k \neq 1$, so $(i^k -
1)w = 0$ forces $w = 0$, contradicting $|w| = 1$. Hence the action is free, and
the quotient map $T \to T/G$ is a 4-fold covering.

**Euler characteristic.** The torus has $\chi(T) = 0$. For a free action of a fi
nite group of order $|G| = 4$, the quotient map is a $|G|$-sheeted covering, so
$$\chi(T) = |G|\cdot \chi(T/G) \implies 0 = 4\cdot\chi(T/G) \implies \chi(T/G) =
 0.$$

**Orientability.** The map $g$ is a rotation in each factor (multiplication by $
i$), hence orientation-preserving. The quotient of an orientable surface by an o
rientation-preserving free action is orientable. So $T/G$ is a compact, connecte
d, orientable surface.

**Conclusion via classification.** A compact, connected, orientable surface is d
etermined up to homeomorphism by its Euler characteristic. The unique such surfa
ce with $\chi = 0$ is the torus $S^1\times S^1$.

**Verification via the universal cover (independent check).** Lift the action to
 the universal cover $\mathbb{R}^2 \xrightarrow{p} T$, $p(x,y) = (e^{2\pi i x},
e^{2\pi i y})$. The deck group is $\Lambda_0 = \langle (1,0),\, (0,1)\rangle$, a
nd the lift of $g$ is $\tilde g(x,y) = (x + \tfrac14,\, y + \tfrac14)$. The full
 group acting on $\mathbb{R}^2$ is
$$\Lambda = \big\langle (1,0),\ (0,1),\ (\tfrac14,\tfrac14)\big\rangle.$$
Since $(\tfrac14,\tfrac14)\cdot 4 - (1,0) = (0,1)$, this lattice equals $\langle
 (\tfrac14,\tfrac14),\, (1,0)\rangle$, which has determinant $\tfrac14 \neq 0$ —
 a rank-2 lattice. Therefore
$$T/G \;\cong\; \mathbb{R}^2/\Lambda \;\cong\; S^1\times S^1,$$
a torus, confirming the classification argument.

---

The quotient space of the torus $S^1\times S^1$ under $(w,z)\sim(iw,iz)$ is **a
torus**, homeomorphic to $S^1\times S^1$.
