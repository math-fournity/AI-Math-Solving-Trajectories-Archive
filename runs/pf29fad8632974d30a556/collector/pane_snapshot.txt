## Proof

**Claim:** Every normal number contains each finite sequence of digits infinitel
y often.

**Definition.** A real number $x$ is *normal in base $b$* if, for every finite b
lock $w$ of $k$ digits (in base $b$), the asymptotic frequency of occurrences of
 $w$ among the first $N$ digits of the base-$b$ expansion of $x$ equals $b^{-k}$
; that is,

$$\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{A_w(N)}{N-k+1}=b^{-k},$$

where $A_w(N)$ counts the number of occurrences of the block $w$ as a consecutiv
e substring among the first $N$ digits.

**Argument.** Fix any finite block $w$ of length $k$. Since $x$ is normal in bas
e $b$, the limit above holds and equals $b^{-k}>0$.

Suppose, for contradiction, that $w$ occurs only finitely many times—say at most
 $M$ times total. Then $A_w(N)\le M$ for all $N$, so

$$\frac{A_w(N)}{N-k+1}\le\frac{M}{N-k+1}\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}0.$$

This contradicts the normality condition, which requires the limit to be $b^{-k}
>0$.

Therefore $w$ must occur infinitely often. Since $w$ was an arbitrary finite blo
ck, **every** finite sequence of digits occurs infinitely often in a normal numb
er. $\blacksquare$
