**Answer: Yes.**

**Construction.** Let $\mathcal{H} = L^2(\mathbb{R})$ and let $A = -i\tfrac{d}{d
x}$ with domain $D(A) = H^1(\mathbb{R})$. This is the momentum operator, a stand
ard self-adjoint operator on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$.

Let $S = L^2([0,\infty)) \hookrightarrow L^2(\mathbb{R})$, i.e. the closed subsp
ace of functions supported (a.e.) on $[0,\infty)$. Let $P$ be the orthogonal pro
jection onto $S$, so $P$ is multiplication by $\chi_{[0,\infty)}$.

The restricted operator $\hat{A}$ on $S$ has domain
$$D(\hat{A}) = S \cap D(A) = \{f \in H^1(\mathbb{R}) : f|_{(-\infty,0)} = 0\}.$$
A function in $H^1(\mathbb{R})$ that vanishes on $(-\infty,0)$ is continuous, he
nce satisfies $f(0) = 0$. Conversely every $f \in H^1([0,\infty))$ with $f(0)=0$
 extends by zero to an element of $H^1(\mathbb{R})$. Thus
$$D(\hat{A}) = \{f \in H^1([0,\infty)) : f(0) = 0\}, \qquad \hat{A}f = -if'.$$
This domain is dense in $S = L^2([0,\infty))$ (it contains $C_c^\infty((0,\infty
))$).

**$\hat{A}$ is symmetric.** For $f, g \in D(\hat{A})$, integration by parts give
s
$$\langle \hat{A}f, g\rangle = \int_0^\infty (-if')\bar g\, dx = -i[f\bar g]_0^\
infty + \int_0^\infty f\,\overline{(-ig')}\, dx = \langle f, \hat{A}g\rangle,$$
since the boundary term vanishes (at $0$ because $f(0)=0$; at $\infty$ because $
H^1$ functions on the half-line decay appropriately).

**$\hat{A}$ is not self-adjoint.** We compute $\hat{A}^*$. A function $g \in L^2
([0,\infty))$ belongs to $D(\hat{A}^*)$ iff there exists $h \in L^2([0,\infty))$
 with $\langle \hat{A}f, g\rangle = \langle f, h\rangle$ for all $f \in D(\hat{A
})$. Repeating the integration by parts,
$$\langle \hat{A}f, g\rangle = i\,f(0)\bar g(0) + \int_0^\infty f\,\overline{(-i
g')}\, dx.$$
Since every $f \in D(\hat{A})$ satisfies $f(0)=0$, the boundary term $i f(0)\bar
 g(0)$ vanishes **regardless of the value of $g(0)$**. Hence the only requiremen
t is $g \in H^1([0,\infty))$ (so that $-ig' \in L^2$), with **no boundary condit
ion** at $0$. Therefore
$$D(\hat{A}^*) = H^1([0,\infty)), \qquad \hat{A}^*g = -ig'.$$
This is a **proper extension** of $D(\hat{A}) = \{f \in H^1([0,\infty)) : f(0) =
 0\}$, e.g. $g(x) = e^{-x}$ belongs to $D(\hat{A}^*) \setminus D(\hat{A})$.

Since $D(\hat{A}) \subsetneq D(\hat{A}^*)$, the operator $\hat{A}$ is symmetric
but **not self-adjoint** on $S$, even though its domain is dense in $S$.

**Conclusion.** Yes, it is possible for $\hat{A}$ to be non-self-adjoint on $S$.
