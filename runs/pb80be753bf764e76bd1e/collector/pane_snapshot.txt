## Proof

Set coordinates with $M$ at the origin, $B = (-1, 0)$, $C = (1, 0)$, and $A = (u
, v)$ with $v \neq 0$.

**Power of a point.** Since $BM = CM = 1$ and $BC = 2$:

- Power of $C$ w.r.t. $\odot(ABM)$: $CA \cdot CB_1 = CB \cdot CM = 2$.
- Power of $B$ w.r.t. $\odot(AMC)$: $BA \cdot BC_1 = BC \cdot BM = 2$.

Let $b^2 = (u-1)^2 + v^2 = CA^2$ and $c^2 = (u+1)^2 + v^2 = BA^2$. Then $CB_1 =
2/b$, $BC_1 = 2/c$, and placing $B_1$ on $\overrightarrow{CA}$, $C_1$ on $\overr
ightarrow{BA}$:

$$B_1 = \left(\frac{u^2 + v^2 - 1}{b^2},\; \frac{2v}{b^2}\right), \qquad C_1 = \
left(-\frac{u^2 + v^2 - 1}{c^2},\; \frac{2v}{c^2}\right).$$

**Setting up the circumcenter condition.** Let $P = u^2 + v^2$ and $s = P + 1$,
so $b^2 = s - 2u$, $c^2 = s + 2u$, and $b^2 + c^2 = 2s$. The circumcenter $O = (
h, k)$ of $\triangle AC_1 B_1$ satisfies $OA = OB_1 = OC_1$. We show $h = 0$.

With $h = 0$, the equations $OA^2 = OB_1^2$ and $OA^2 = OC_1^2$ give:

$$2k(y_{B_1} - v) = x_{B_1}^2 + y_{B_1}^2 - P, \qquad 2k(y_{C_1} - v) = x_{C_1}^
2 + y_{C_1}^2 - P.$$

A consistent $k$ exists iff $\dfrac{x_{B_1}^2 + y_{B_1}^2 - P}{y_{B_1} - v} = \d
frac{x_{C_1}^2 + y_{C_1}^2 - P}{y_{C_1} - v}$. Using $y_{B_1} - v = \tfrac{v(2 -
 b^2)}{b^2}$ and $y_{C_1} - v = \tfrac{v(2 - c^2)}{c^2}$, and setting $T = (P-1)
^2 + 4v^2$ (so that $x_{B_1}^2 + y_{B_1}^2 = T/b^4$ and $x_{C_1}^2 + y_{C_1}^2 =
 T/c^4$), this becomes after clearing denominators:

$$\left(\frac{T}{b^2} - P \cdot b^2\right)(2 - c^2) = \left(\frac{T}{c^2} - P \c
dot c^2\right)(2 - b^2). \tag{$\star$}$$

**Key identity:** $T = b^2 c^2$.

*Proof.* $b^2 c^2 = (s - 2u)(s + 2u) = s^2 - 4u^2 = (P+1)^2 - 4u^2$, while $T =
(P-1)^2 + 4v^2$. Expanding:
$$(P+1)^2 - 4u^2 = P^2 + 2P + 1 - 4u^2, \qquad (P-1)^2 + 4v^2 = P^2 - 2P + 1 + 4
v^2.$$
These are equal since $2P - 4u^2 = 2(u^2+v^2) - 4u^2 = -2u^2 + 2v^2$ equals $-2P
 + 4v^2 = -2(u^2+v^2) + 4v^2 = -2u^2 + 2v^2$. $\checkmark$

**Completing the proof of $(\star)$.** Using $T = b^2 c^2$ and $P = s - 1$:

$$\frac{T}{b^2} - P \cdot b^2 = c^2 - (s-1)b^2 = (b^2 + c^2) - s\cdot b^2 = 2s -
 sb^2 = s(2 - b^2),$$

$$\frac{T}{c^2} - P \cdot c^2 = b^2 - (s-1)c^2 = (b^2 + c^2) - s\cdot c^2 = 2s -
 sc^2 = s(2 - c^2).$$

Substituting into $(\star)$:

$$s(2 - b^2)(2 - c^2) = s(2 - c^2)(2 - b^2). \quad \checkmark$$

So condition $(\star)$ holds, a consistent $k$ exists, and the circumcenter sati
sfies $h = 0$.

**Conclusion.** Since $O = (0, k)$ lies on the $y$-axis — the perpendicular bise
ctor of $BC$ — we conclude $OB = OC$. $\blacksquare$
