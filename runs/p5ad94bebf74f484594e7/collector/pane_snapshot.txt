# de Rham's Theorem

## Statement

Let $M$ be a smooth (i.e. $C^\infty$) manifold that is **smoothly homotopy equiv
alent to a finite CW-complex** (in particular, any closed smooth manifold, or mo
re generally any smooth manifold admitting a finite good cover). Let $H^*_{\math
rm{dR}}(M;\mathbb{R})$ denote the de Rham cohomology ring of $M$, whose product
is the **wedge product** $\wedge$ of differential forms, and let $H^*(M;\mathbb{
R})$ denote the singular cohomology of $M$ with real coefficients, equipped with
 the **cup product** $\smile$.

**de Rham's Theorem.** There is a canonical ring isomorphism
$$H^*_{\mathrm{dR}}(M;\mathbb{R}) \;\cong\; H^*(M;\mathbb{R}),$$
i.e. a graded-$\mathbb{R}$-algebra isomorphism that is natural in $M$ and, in ea
ch degree $k$, sends the class $[\omega]\in H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)$ of a closed $k
$-form $\omega$ to the singular cohomology class obtained by **integration of $\
omega$ over smooth $k$-simplices**:
$$[\omega]\;\longmapsto\;\Big(\sigma\mapsto\int_\sigma \omega\Big).$$

Concretely, the map is
$$\Phi: H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\to H^k(M;\mathbb{R}),\qquad \Phi([\omega])(\sigma)=
\int_{\Delta^k}\sigma^*\omega,$$
for every smooth singular $k$-simplex $\sigma:\Delta^k\to M$. The theorem assert
s that $\Phi$ is well-defined, an isomorphism of graded vector spaces in every d
egree, and a **ring homomorphism** respecting the products:
$$\Phi([\omega]\wedge[\eta])=\Phi([\omega])\smile\Phi([\eta]).$$

---

## Explanation

### 1. The de Rham complex

On a smooth manifold $M$ one has the **de Rham complex**
$$0\to\Omega^0(M)\xrightarrow{d}\Omega^1(M)\xrightarrow{d}\Omega^2(M)\xrightarro
w{d}\cdots,$$
where $\Omega^k(M)$ is the space of smooth $k$-forms and $d$ is the exterior der
ivative, satisfying $d\circ d=0$. The de Rham cohomology is
$$H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)=\frac{\ker(d:\Omega^k\to\Omega^{k+1})}{\operatorname{im}(
d:\Omega^{k-1}\to\Omega^k)}=\frac{\{\text{closed }k\text{-forms}\}}{\{\text{exac
t }k\text{-forms}\}}.$$
The wedge product $\wedge$ makes $\bigoplus_k H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)$ into a grade
d-commutative $\mathbb{R}$-algebra, since $d(\omega\wedge\eta)=d\omega\wedge\eta
+(-1)^{|\omega|}\omega\wedge d\eta$ shows the product descends to cohomology.

### 2. The integration map and Stokes' theorem

Define, for a closed $k$-form $\omega$ and a smooth singular $k$-chain $c=\sum n
_i\sigma_i$,
$$\Phi(\omega)(c)=\sum_i n_i\int_{\Delta^k}\sigma_i^*\omega.$$
**Stokes' theorem** gives $\int_{\partial c}\omega=\int_c d\omega$. Two conseque
nces:

- If $\omega=d\alpha$ is exact, then $\Phi(\omega)(c)=\int_c d\alpha=\int_{\part
ial c}\alpha=0$ on every cycle $c$, so $\Phi$ kills exact forms — it **descends
to cohomology**.
- If $c=\partial b$ is a boundary, then $\Phi(\omega)(c)=\int_{\partial b}\omega
=\int_b d\omega=0$ for closed $\omega$, so $\Phi(\omega)$ **vanishes on boundari
es** and hence defines a singular cohomology class.

Thus $\Phi:H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\to H^k(M;\mathbb{R})$ is a well-defined linear m
ap, natural in $M$ (commuting with pullbacks by smooth maps).

### 3. Compatibility with products (the ring structure)

The key identity making $\Phi$ a **ring** homomorphism is the relation between t
he wedge product of forms and the cup product of singular cochains. Recall the c
up product of a singular $p$-cochain $c$ and a $q$-cochain $d$ is
$$(c\smile d)(\sigma)=c\big(\sigma|_{[v_0,\dots,v_p]}\big)\cdot d\big(\sigma|_{[
v_p,\dots,v_{p+q}]}\big).$$
For closed forms $\omega\in\Omega^p$, $\eta\in\Omega^q$ and a smooth $(p+q)$-sim
plex $\sigma$,
$$\int_\sigma \omega\wedge\eta=\int_{\sigma|_{[v_0,\dots,v_p]}}\omega\;\cdot\;\i
nt_{\sigma|_{[v_p,\dots,v_{p+q}]}}\eta,$$
which is exactly $\Phi(\omega\wedge\eta)(\sigma)=\big(\Phi(\omega)\smile\Phi(\et
a)\big)(\sigma)$. (This follows from the definition of $\wedge$ and Fubini's the
orem applied to the simplex, using that $\sigma^*(\omega\wedge\eta)=\sigma^*\ome
ga\wedge\sigma^*\eta$ splits over the front and back faces.) Therefore
$$\Phi([\omega]\wedge[\eta])=\Phi([\omega])\smile\Phi([\eta]),$$
so $\Phi$ is a homomorphism of graded $\mathbb{R}$-algebras.

### 4. $\Phi$ is an isomorphism

The proof that $\Phi$ is an isomorphism proceeds by the **Mayer–Vietoris / good-
cover argument** (the standard sheaf-theoretic proof due to Weil):

- **Sheaf of forms is fine/acyclic.** The sheaf $\Omega^k$ of smooth $k$-forms i
s a *fine* sheaf (admits partitions of unity), hence acyclic: $H^p(M;\Omega^k)=0
$ for $p>0$. The Poincaré lemma says the sequence of sheaves
  $$0\to\mathbb{R}\to\Omega^0\xrightarrow{d}\Omega^1\xrightarrow{d}\Omega^2\to\c
dots$$
  is **exact** (locally, every closed form is exact). This is a **resolution of
the constant sheaf $\mathbb{R}$** by acyclic sheaves.

- **Abstract de Rham theorem.** The de Rham complex computes the sheaf cohomolog
y of $\mathbb{R}$:
  $$H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\cong H^k(M;\mathbb{R}_{\text{sheaf}}).$$
  Sheaf cohomology of the constant sheaf $\mathbb{R}$ agrees with singular cohom
ology $H^k(M;\mathbb{R})$ (this is a standard theorem: singular cohomology is th
e sheaf cohomology of the constant sheaf on a locally contractible space). The i
somorphism induced by this identification is exactly the integration map $\Phi$.

- **Finite good cover / Mayer–Vietoris induction.** Equivalently and more concre
tely: cover $M$ by finitely many **good covers** (every finite intersection is d
iffeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^n$, hence contractible, so de Rham and singular coho
mology of each intersection and their intersections agree — both vanish above de
gree $0$ and are $\mathbb{R}$ in degree $0$). The Mayer–Vietoris sequences for d
e Rham cohomology (built from the short exact sequence of forms on a two-set cov
er) and for singular cohomology are **naturally identified by $\Phi$** at the le
vel of the open sets and their intersections. The **five lemma** then propagates
 the isomorphism from the contractible pieces (where it is trivially true: both
sides are $\mathbb{R}$ in degree $0$ and $0$ elsewhere) up through the Mayer–Vie
toris induction to all of $M$. Finiteness of the good cover ensures the inductio
n terminates.

Both routes give the same conclusion: $\Phi$ is an isomorphism of graded vector
spaces in every degree, and combined with the product compatibility of §3, an is
omorphism of graded $\mathbb{R}$-algebras.

### 5. Summary of what the theorem says

de Rham's theorem identifies two a priori different cohomology theories:

| | de Rham | singular |
|---|---|---|
| **coefficients** | $\mathbb{R}$ (intrinsic to $M$) | $\mathbb{R}$ (constant sh
eaf) |
| **chains** | differential forms $\Omega^k(M)$ | singular cochains $\operatorna
me{Hom}(C_k,\mathbb{R})$ |
| **differential** | exterior derivative $d$ | coboundary $\delta$ |
| **product** | wedge $\wedge$ | cup $\smile$ |

The integration map $\Phi([\omega])(\sigma)=\int_\sigma\omega$ is the canonical,
 natural isomorphism identifying them as graded-commutative $\mathbb{R}$-algebra
s. The theorem thereby shows that **the topological information encoded by singu
lar cohomology is completely captured by the calculus of differential forms**, a
nd that the wedge product and cup product are the same product under this identi
fication. This is the foundational result connecting **differential geometry** (
forms, integration) with **algebraic topology** (singular cohomology, cup produc
t), and it is the prototype of the more general de Rham theorem in sheaf theory.
