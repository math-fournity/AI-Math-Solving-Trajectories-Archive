## Solution

We seek $a_{20}$ in the Laurent expansion of $f(z) = \dfrac{1}{\sin z}$ about $z
=0$, where

$$a_k = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma} \frac{f(z)}{z^{k+1}}\,dz.$$

For $k=20$:

$$a_{20} = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma} \frac{1}{z^{21}\sin z}\,dz.$$

### Step 1. Parity of the integrand

Let $g(z) = \dfrac{1}{z^{21}\sin z}$. Using $\sin(-z) = -\sin z$ and $(-z)^{21}
= -z^{21}$:

$$g(-z) = \frac{1}{(-z)^{21}\sin(-z)} = \frac{1}{(-z^{21})(-\sin z)} = \frac{1}{
z^{21}\sin z} = g(z).$$

So $g$ is an **even** function.

### Step 2. Laurent expansion of an even function contains only even powers

An even function satisfies $g(z) = g(-z)$. If its Laurent series is $g(z) = \sum
_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n z^n$, then

$$\sum c_n z^n = \sum c_n (-z)^n = \sum (-1)^n c_n z^n,$$

so $c_n = (-1)^n c_n$, which forces $c_n = 0$ for all odd $n$. Hence **every odd
-indexed Laurent coefficient of $g$ vanishes**, in particular $c_{-1}$ (the coef
ficient of $z^{-1}$).

### Step 3. The integral equals the residue

By the residue theorem (with $\Gamma$ a small positively-oriented circle around
$0$ lying inside the punctured neighborhood where $g$ is analytic except at $0$)
:

$$a_{20} = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma} g(z)\,dz = \operatorname{Res}_{z=0} g(z
) = c_{-1}.$$

Since $-1$ is odd and $g$ is even, $c_{-1} = 0$.

### Step 4. Consistency check via the Laurent series of $1/\sin z$

Since $\sin z = z\left(1 - \dfrac{z^2}{6} + \dfrac{z^4}{120} - \cdots\right)$ is
 $z$ times an even function,

$$\frac{1}{\sin z} = \frac{1}{z}\cdot\frac{1}{1 - z^2/6 + z^4/120 - \cdots},$$

which is $\dfrac{1}{z}$ (an odd power) times an even function. Therefore $1/\sin
 z$ contains **only odd powers** of $z$: $z^{-1}, z^{1}, z^{3}, z^{5}, \dots$. T
he coefficient of $z^{20}$ (an even power) is necessarily $0$, confirming the co
ntour-integral result.

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{a_{20} = 0}$$
