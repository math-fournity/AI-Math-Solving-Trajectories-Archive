# Geodesics on a Sphere of Radius $r$

## Setup

The metric is $ds^2 = r^2 d\varphi^2 + r^2\sin^2\varphi\, d\theta^2$, with coord
inates $x^1=\varphi$, $x^2=\theta$. The metric components are
$$g_{11}=r^2,\qquad g_{22}=r^2\sin^2\varphi,\qquad g_{12}=g_{21}=0.$$

## Christoffel Symbols

The inverse metric is $g^{11}=1/r^2$, $g^{22}=1/(r^2\sin^2\varphi)$. The nonzero
 Christoffel symbols are:

$$\Gamma^1_{22}=-\sin\varphi\cos\varphi,\qquad \Gamma^2_{12}=\Gamma^2_{21}=\cot\
varphi.$$

(All others vanish: $\Gamma^1_{11}=\Gamma^2_{22}=0$ since $g_{ij}$ is independen
t of $\theta$ and $g_{11}$ is constant.)

## Geodesic Equations

Using an affine parameter $s$ (arc length, so $g_{ij}\dot x^i\dot x^j = r^2$):

**Equation 1** ($\varphi$):
$$\ddot\varphi - \sin\varphi\cos\varphi\,\dot\theta^2 = 0. \tag{1}$$

**Equation 2** ($\theta$):
$$\ddot\theta + 2\cot\varphi\,\dot\varphi\,\dot\theta = 0. \tag{2}$$

## First Integrals

**Energy integral** (from $g_{ij}\dot x^i\dot x^j = r^2$):
$$r^2\dot\varphi^2 + r^2\sin^2\varphi\,\dot\theta^2 = r^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; \d
ot\varphi^2 + \sin^2\varphi\,\dot\theta^2 = 1. \tag{3}$$

**Angular momentum integral**: Since $\theta$ is a cyclic coordinate ($\partial
g/\partial\theta=0$), equation (2) integrates to
$$\sin^2\varphi\,\dot\theta = c \quad (c = \text{const}). \tag{4}$$

(Indeed, $\frac{d}{ds}(\sin^2\varphi\,\dot\theta) = 2\sin\varphi\cos\varphi\,\do
t\varphi\,\dot\theta + \sin^2\varphi\,\ddot\theta = \sin^2\varphi\bigl(\ddot\the
ta + 2\cot\varphi\,\dot\varphi\,\dot\theta\bigr)=0$.)

## Deriving the Orbit

Substituting (4) into (3):
$$\dot\varphi^2 = 1 - \frac{c^2}{\sin^2\varphi}. \tag{5}$$

From (4) and (5), $\frac{d\theta}{d\varphi} = \frac{\dot\theta}{\dot\varphi} = \
frac{c/\sin^2\varphi}{\pm\sqrt{1-c^2/\sin^2\varphi}}$, giving
$$\frac{d\theta}{d\varphi} = \frac{c}{\sin\varphi\sqrt{\sin^2\varphi - c^2}}. \t
ag{6}$$

Integrating: let $u=\cos\varphi$, $du=-\sin\varphi\,d\varphi$. Then $\sin^2\varp
hi=1-u^2$ and
$$\theta = \int \frac{c\,d\varphi}{\sin\varphi\sqrt{\sin^2\varphi-c^2}} = -\int
\frac{c\,du}{(1-u^2)\sqrt{1-c^2-u^2}}.$$

Writing $1-c^2 = \alpha^2$ (with $\alpha>0$ for nontrivial geodesics) and using
the standard integral, the result is:

$$\boxed{\cos\varphi = A\cos\theta + B\sin\theta} \tag{7}$$

where $A,B$ are constants (with $A^2+B^2 = 1-c^2 \le 1$).

## Geometric Interpretation

Equation (7) is the equation of a **great circle**. To see this, embed the spher
e in $\mathbb{R}^3$:
$$(X,Y,Z) = (r\sin\varphi\cos\theta,\; r\sin\varphi\sin\theta,\; r\cos\varphi).$
$

Then $Z = r\cos\varphi = r(A\cos\theta + B\sin\theta) = A\,X + B\,Y$, i.e.,
$$A\,X + B\,Y - Z = 0. \tag{8}$$

This is the equation of a plane through the origin. The intersection of a plane
through the center of the sphere with the sphere is a **great circle**.

## Verification

**Great circles are geodesics**: A great circle is the intersection of the spher
e with a plane through the origin. By rotational symmetry of the sphere, any gre
at circle can be rotated to the equator $\varphi = \pi/2$. On the equator, $\dot
\varphi=0$, $\ddot\varphi=0$, $\dot\theta=\text{const}$, and both geodesic equat
ions (1) and (2) are satisfied (since $\cos(\pi/2)=0$, $\cot(\pi/2)=0$). Hence g
reat circles satisfy the geodesic equations.

**All geodesics are great circles**: The general solution (7)–(8) is a plane thr
ough the origin, i.e., a great circle. The special cases $c=0$ (meridians, $\the
ta=\text{const}$, which are great circles through the poles) and $c=\pm 1$ (the
equator) are included.

## Conclusion

The geodesics on a sphere of radius $r$ with metric $ds^2 = r^2\,d\varphi^2 + r^
2\sin^2\varphi\,d\theta^2$ are precisely the **great circles** — the intersectio
ns of the sphere with planes passing through its center. Equivalently, they are
the curves satisfying $\cos\varphi = A\cos\theta + B\sin\theta$ for constants $A
, B$, which in Cartesian embedding coordinates is the linear relation $AX + BY -
 Z = 0$.
