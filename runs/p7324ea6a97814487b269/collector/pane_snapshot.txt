**Answer: Yes.**

Let $R = \mathbb{F}_2[x]/(x^2)$, the ring of dual numbers over $\mathbb{F}_2$. I
ts elements are $\{0,\, 1,\, x,\, 1+x\}$ with $x^2 = 0$ and all arithmetic mod $
2$.

---

**Rank of $R$ as a ring is $1$.**

The element $1+x$ alone generates $R$ as a ring. Indeed, starting from $1+x$ and
 closing under $+$ and $\cdot$:
- $(1+x) + (1+x) = 0$,
- $(1+x)^2 = 1 + 2x + x^2 = 1$,
- $1 + (1+x) = x$,

so we obtain $\{0, 1, x, 1+x\} = R$. Hence $\operatorname{rank}(R) = 1$.

---

**Rank of the additive group $(R,+)$ is $2$.**

As an abelian group $(R,+) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^2$, which requires two
 generators (e.g. $\{1, x\}$). A single element generates at most a cyclic subgr
oup of order $2$, i.e. $\{0, a\}$, never all four elements. Thus $\operatorname{
rank}(R,+) = 2$.

---

**Rank of the multiplicative monoid $(R,\cdot)$ is $2$.**

The monoid $(R,\cdot)$ has identity $1$ and zero $0$. The multiplication table (
besides $0$ and $1$) is:
$$x^2 = 0,\qquad x(1+x) = x,\qquad (1+x)^2 = 1.$$

A single generator $a$ produces the submonoid $\langle a\rangle = \{1,\, a,\, a^
2,\, a^3,\dots\}$:
- $a = x$: $\{1, x, 0\}$ — missing $1+x$.
- $a = 1+x$: $\{1, 1+x\}$ (since $(1+x)^2=1$) — missing $0$ and $x$.
- $a = 0$: $\{1, 0\}$ — missing $x$ and $1+x$.

So no single element generates the whole monoid. But $\{x,\, 1+x\}$ does: from t
hese we get $1 = (1+x)^2$, $0 = x^2$, $x$, and $1+x$ — all four elements. Hence
$\operatorname{rank}(R,\cdot) = 2$.

---

**Conclusion.** For $R = \mathbb{F}_2[x]/(x^2)$:
$$\operatorname{rank}(R) = 1 \;<\; 2 = \operatorname{rank}(R,+) \quad\text{and}\
quad \operatorname{rank}(R) = 1 \;<\; 2 = \operatorname{rank}(R,\cdot).$$

So such a finite ring exists.
