Looking at this problem, I need to find which $n \in \{3,4,\ldots,50\}$ do **not
** admit a tournament where every player is "not out-performed" by any other.

## Setting Up the Equivalence

Let $W(A)$ denote the set of players defeated by $A$. Player $A$ is "not out-per
formed" by $B$ means $W(A) \not\subseteq W(B)$, i.e., there exists $C$ with $A \
to C$ and $C \to B$ (since every pair plays, "$B$ didn't defeat $C$" means $C \t
o B$).

**Key observation:** If $A \to B$, then $B \in W(A) \setminus W(B)$, so $W(A) \n
ot\subseteq W(B)$ automatically. The only nontrivial case is when $B \to A$: we
need some $C$ with $A \to C$ and $C \to B$, forming a directed 3-cycle $B \to A
\to C \to B$.

**Equivalence:** The condition holds for all pairs iff **every directed edge lie
s in a directed 3-cycle**.

## Odd $n \geq 3$: All Work

A **regular tournament** on odd $n$ (each vertex has out-degree $(n-1)/2$) exist
s for all odd $n \geq 3$ (e.g., the cyclic tournament). In such a tournament, al
l winning sets have equal size $(n-1)/2$. For distinct $A, B$ with $B \to A$: si
nce $|W(A)| = |W(B)|$ and $A \in W(B) \setminus W(A)$, we have $W(A) \neq W(B)$,
 so $W(A) \not\subseteq W(B)$ (equal-size distinct sets can't contain each other
). ✓

So **every odd $n \geq 3$ belongs to $S$**.

## $n = 4$: Impossible

No vertex can have out-degree $0$ (empty winning set ⊆ everything) or $3$ (then
any other vertex $A$ has $W(A) \subseteq V\setminus\{A,B\} \subseteq V\setminus\
{B\} = W(B)$). So out-degrees must be $\{2,2,1,1\}$.

Let $v$ have out-degree $1$, beating only $u$. For edge $a \to v$ (where $a$ bea
ts $v$), we need $v \to C$ and $C \to a$, forcing $C = u$ and $u \to a$. Similar
ly $u \to b$ for the other in-neighbor $b$ of $v$. So $u$ beats both $a$ and $b$
 (out-degree $2$).

Now between $a$ and $b$: say $a \to b$ (out-degrees: $a$ has $2$, $b$ has $1$).
Edge $a \to b$ needs $b \to C$ and $C \to a$. But $b$ beats only $v$, so $C = v$
 requires $v \to a$, contradicting $a \to v$. The case $b \to a$ is symmetric. *
*Contradiction in both cases.** ✗

So **$4 \notin S$**.

## Even $n \geq 6$: All Work

**Construction:** Take the regular (cyclic) tournament on $m = n-1$ vertices (od
d $m \geq 5$), where $i \to j$ iff $j - i \pmod{m} \in \{1, 2, \ldots, (m-1)/2\}
$. Add vertex $m$ with out-neighbors $S = \{\text{even vertices}\}$ and in-neigh
bors $T = \{\text{odd vertices}\}$.

**Original edges** remain in 3-cycles (regular tournament property). ✓

**New edges** $m \to s$ ($s \in S$): need $s \to t$ for some $t \in T$. Every ev
en vertex $2j$ beats $2j+1 \in T$ (for $j < (m-1)/2$); vertex $m-1$ beats $1 \in
 T$ since $(m-3)/2 \geq 1$ for $m \geq 5$. ✓

**New edges** $t \to m$ ($t \in T$): need $m \to s$ and $s \to t$ for some $s \i
n S$. Every odd vertex $2j+1$ is beaten by even vertex $2j \in S$ (since $(2j+1)
-2j = 1 \leq (m-1)/2$). ✓

So the bipartite graph from $S$ to $T$ has no isolated vertices on either side,
and all new edges lie in 3-cycles. **Every even $n \geq 6$ belongs to $S$.**

## Conclusion

$$S \supseteq \{3, 5, 6, 7, 8, \ldots, 50\}, \quad 4 \notin S$$

$$\{3, 4, 5, \ldots, 50\} \setminus S = \{4\}$$

The sum of all elements is $\boxed{4}$.
