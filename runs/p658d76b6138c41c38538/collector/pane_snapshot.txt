## Proof

Yes, such groups exist. Here is an explicit construction.

**Construction.** Let $G = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. Set
$$H = \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}, \qquad I = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z
}/2\mathbb{Z}.$$

Define $h: G \to H$ by $h(\bar{1}) = \bar{2}$ (the unique element of order $2$ i
n $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$), and define $i: G \to I$ by $i(\bar{1}) = (\bar{1},
\bar{0})$.

**Verification.**

1. **$h$ and $i$ are injective.** In both cases the image has order $2 = |G|$, s
o the maps are injective.

2. **$\operatorname{img}(h) \trianglelefteq H$ and $\operatorname{img}(i) \trian
glelefteq I$.** Both $H$ and $I$ are abelian, so every subgroup is normal. In pa
rticular $\operatorname{img}(h) = \{\bar{0}, \bar{2}\} \trianglelefteq \mathbb{Z
}/4\mathbb{Z}$ and $\operatorname{img}(i) = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \{\bar
{0}\} \trianglelefteq \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.

3. **Isomorphic quotients.** We have
$$H/\operatorname{img}(h) = (\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z})/\{\bar{0},\bar{2}\} \cong \
mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},$$
and
$$I/\operatorname{img}(i) = (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z
})/(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \{\bar{0}\}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.$$
Hence $H/\operatorname{img}(h) \cong I/\operatorname{img}(i)$.

4. **$H \not\cong I$.** The group $H = \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ is cyclic of orde
r $4$, while $I = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ has no e
lement of order $4$ (every non-identity element has order $2$), so $I$ is not cy
clic. Therefore $H \not\cong I$.

**Conclusion.** The groups $G = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, $H = \mathbb{Z}/4\mathb
b{Z}$, $I = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ together with
the homomorphisms $h$ and $i$ defined above satisfy all the required conditions,
 yet $H \not\cong I$. Thus such groups do exist. $\blacksquare$
