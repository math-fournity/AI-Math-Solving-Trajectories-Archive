## Answer

**Yes**, the Calderón–Zygmund inequality extends to higher-order Sobolev spaces
— **provided** one stays in the natural range $1<p<\infty$ and imposes appropria
te boundary conditions (e.g. Dirichlet) on a sufficiently smooth bounded domain.
 It **fails** for $p=1$ and $p=\infty$ already at order $k=0$.

Below is the precise statement and proof.

---

### Theorem

Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ be a bounded open set with $C^{k+2}$ boundary, l
et $1<p<\infty$, and let $u\in W^{k+2,p}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_0(\Omega)$. Then th
ere exists $C=C(\Omega,k,p,n)$ such that

$$
\|u\|_{W^{k+2,p}(\Omega)}\;\le\;C\,\|\Delta u\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}.
$$

In particular

$$
\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}\;\le\;C\,\|\Delta u\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}.
$$

---

### Proof

The argument has three stages: (1) the whole-space estimate via Riesz transforms
, (2) localization to a bounded domain, (3) absorption of the lower-order term.

#### Stage 1 — The estimate on $\mathbb{R}^n$

Let $v\in \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^n)$. Taking the Fourier transform of $\partial_
{ij}v$ and of $\Delta v$ gives

$$
\widehat{\partial_{ij}v}(\xi)=-\xi_i\xi_j\widehat v(\xi),\qquad
\widehat{\Delta v}(\xi)=-|\xi|^{2}\widehat v(\xi),
$$

so that, in the sense of tempered distributions,

$$
\partial_{ij}v \;=\; R_i R_j(\Delta v),
$$

where $R_i$ is the $i$-th Riesz transform, the Fourier multiplier $-i\xi_i/|\xi|
$. The Riesz transforms are bounded on $L^p(\mathbb{R}^n)$ for $1<p<\infty$ (the
 classical Calderón–Zygmund theorem), with operator norm depending only on $p,n$
. Hence

$$
\|\partial_{ij}v\|_{L^p}\le C\,\|\Delta v\|_{L^p}. \tag{1}
$$

This is the $k=0$ Calderón–Zygmund inequality on $\mathbb{R}^n$.

Now let $v\in W^{k+2,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)$. For every multi-index $|\alpha|\le k$ th
e derivative $D^\alpha v$ lies in $W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ and, because constant-
coefficient differential operators commute,

$$
\Delta(D^\alpha v)=D^\alpha(\Delta v).
$$

Applying (1) to $D^\alpha v$ and summing over $|\alpha|\le k$ and $i,j$,

$$
\sum_{|\alpha|\le k}\sum_{i,j}\|\partial_{ij}D^\alpha v\|_{L^p}
\;\le\; C\sum_{|\alpha|\le k}\|D^\alpha\Delta v\|_{L^p}.
$$

The left-hand side is exactly $\|\nabla^2 v\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)}$ and the r
ight-hand side is $C\|\Delta v\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)}$. Thus

$$
\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)}\le C\,\|\Delta v\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^n
)}. \tag{2}
$$

The key point — and the reason the extension to $k\ge1$ is essentially free on $
\mathbb{R}^n$ — is that **differentiation commutes with the Laplacian**, so the
$k=0$ inequality applied to each $D^\alpha v$ yields the $k$-th order inequality
 with the *same* constant. No new singular-integral theory is needed; only the $
L^p$-boundedness of the Riesz transforms is used.

#### Stage 2 — Localization to a bounded domain

Let $\{\varphi_\ell\}$ be a finite smooth partition of unity subordinate to a co
ver of $\overline\Omega$ by coordinate patches, each patch either lying in the i
nterior of $\Omega$ or being a boundary patch on which $\partial\Omega$ is flatt
ened to a hyperplane by a $C^{k+2}$ diffeomorphism $\Phi_\ell$.

Write $u=\sum_\ell \varphi_\ell u$. For an **interior** patch, $\varphi_\ell u$
is compactly supported in $\mathbb{R}^n$; extending by zero and applying (2) giv
es

$$
\|\nabla^2(\varphi_\ell u)\|_{W^{k,p}}\le C\bigl(\|\Delta(\varphi_\ell u)\|_{W^{
k,p}}\bigr)
\le C\bigl(\|\varphi_\ell\Delta u\|_{W^{k,p}}+\text{lower-order terms in }\varph
i_\ell u\bigr). \tag{3}
$$

The "lower-order terms" arise from the product rule $\Delta(\varphi_\ell u)=\var
phi_\ell\Delta u+2\nabla\varphi_\ell\cdot\nabla u+(\Delta\varphi_\ell)u$, and si
milarly at higher derivatives; they involve derivatives of $u$ of order $\le k+1
$ and are controlled by $\|u\|_{W^{k+1,p}}$.

For a **boundary** patch, flatten $\partial\Omega$ to $\{x_n=0\}$ with $\Omega$
locally $\{x_n>0\}$. The Dirichlet condition $u|_{\partial\Omega}=0$ becomes $w|
_{\{x_n=0\}}=0$ for $w=\varphi_\ell u\circ\Phi_\ell^{-1}$. Perform the **odd ref
lection** across $\{x_n=0\}$:

$$
\tilde w(x',x_n)=
\begin{cases}
w(x',x_n),&x_n\ge0,\\
-w(x',-x_n),&x_n<0.
\end{cases}
$$

Because $w$ vanishes on $\{x_n=0\}$, $\tilde w\in W^{k+2,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, and
the reflected Laplacian $\widetilde{\Delta w}$ (with the sign flipped on the low
er half-space by the odd reflection) satisfies $\widetilde{\Delta w}=\Delta\tild
e w$ in the distributional sense. Applying (2) to $\tilde w$ and restricting bac
k to the upper half-space yields an estimate of the same shape as (3). (The chan
ge of variables $\Phi_\ell$ is $C^{k+2}$, so it distorts the $W^{k,p}$ norms onl
y by bounded factors, and the principal part of the transformed operator is stil
l the Laplacian up to lower-order perturbations.)

Summing (3) and its boundary analogue over the finite cover,

$$
\|\nabla^2 u\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}
\;\le\; C\,\|\Delta u\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}+C\,\|u\|_{W^{k+1,p}(\Omega)}. \tag{4}
$$

A standard interpolation/Young estimate (using that $W^{k,p}\hookrightarrow W^{k
+1,p}$ insertion is controlled by $\varepsilon\|u\|_{W^{k+2,p}}+\varepsilon^{-1}
\|u\|_{L^p}$) lets one replace the $W^{k+1,p}$ remainder by $\varepsilon\|u\|_{W
^{k+2,p}}+C_\varepsilon\|u\|_{L^p}$. Absorbing $\varepsilon\|u\|_{W^{k+2,p}}$ in
to the left side gives the **a priori estimate with a lower-order remainder**:

$$
\|u\|_{W^{k+2,p}(\Omega)}\le C\bigl(\|\Delta u\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^p(\O
mega)}\bigr). \tag{5}
$$

#### Stage 3 — Absorbing the lower-order term

It remains to remove $\|u\|_{L^p}$ from (5). Consider the Dirichlet problem

$$
-\Delta u=f\ \text{in }\Omega,\qquad u|_{\partial\Omega}=0. \tag{6}
$$

The operator $-\Delta:W^{k+2,p}\cap W^{1,p}_0\to W^{k,p}$ is injective: if $\Del
ta u=0$ with $u|_{\partial\Omega}=0$, then $u\equiv0$ by the weak maximum princi
ple (or by the energy identity $\int|\nabla u|^2=0$).

Suppose (5) held *without* the $\|u\|_{L^p}$ term were false. Then there would e
xist a sequence $\{u_m\}\subset W^{k+2,p}\cap W^{1,p}_0$ with

$$
\|u_m\|_{W^{k+2,p}}=1,\qquad \|\Delta u_m\|_{W^{k,p}}\to0. \tag{7}
$$

By (5), $\|u_m\|_{L^p}\ge c>0$. The embedding $W^{k+2,p}\hookrightarrow\hookrigh
tarrow L^p$ is **compact** (Rellich–Kondrachov, since $\Omega$ is bounded and $k
+2>0$), so a subsequence (still denoted $u_m$) converges strongly in $L^p$ to so
me $u_*\in L^p$ with $\|u_*\|_{L^p}\ge c$. On the other hand $\{\Delta u_m\}$ is
 bounded in $W^{k,p}$ and tends to $0$; elliptic regularity (5) gives boundednes
s of $\{u_m\}$ in $W^{k+2,p}$, hence a further subsequence converges weakly in $
W^{k+2,p}$ and strongly in $L^p$ to $u_*$. Passing to the limit in $\Delta u_m\t
o0$ in the distributional sense gives $\Delta u_*=0$ with $u_*|_{\partial\Omega}
=0$, so $u_*=0$ — contradicting $\|u_*\|_{L^p}\ge c>0$.

Therefore (7) is impossible and there is $C$ with

$$
\|u\|_{W^{k+2,p}(\Omega)}\le C\,\|\Delta u\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)},\qquad u\in W^{k+
2,p}\cap W^{1,p}_0. \tag{8}
$$

Restricting (8) to the second-derivative part of the $W^{k+2,p}$ norm yields the
 claimed

$$
\boxed{\;\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}\le C\,\|\Delta u\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}\
;}. \qquad\blacksquare
$$

---

### Sharpness — the cases $p=1$ and $p=\infty$

The restriction $1<p<\infty$ is **necessary**. The Riesz transforms $R_i$ are *n
ot* bounded on $L^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$ nor on $L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ (their kernel
s decay like $|x|^{-n}$, so they map $L^\infty$ into BMO, not $L^\infty$, and du
ally they are not of weak type $(1,1)$ into $L^1$). Consequently the very first
step (1) already fails for $p=1,\infty$, and no higher-order extension can rescu
e it. For instance on $\mathbb{R}^n$ one can construct $u\in W^{2,\infty}$ with
$\Delta u\in L^\infty$ but $\partial_{12}u\notin L^\infty$, by taking $u$ whose
second derivatives reproduce the unbounded output of a Riesz transform applied t
o a bounded input. The same obstruction persists on bounded domains (it is local
).

So the complete answer to "does the Calderón–Zygmund inequality extend to higher
-order Sobolev spaces?" is:

> **Yes for $1<p<\infty$ on a bounded $C^{k+2}$ domain with Dirichlet boundary c
onditions (and similarly for other well-posed elliptic boundary value problems w
ith trivial kernel); no for $p=1$ and $p=\infty$.** The extension is essentially
 automatic because differentiation commutes with the Laplacian, so the $k=0$ Rie
sz-transform argument lifts verbatim to every $k\ge1$.
