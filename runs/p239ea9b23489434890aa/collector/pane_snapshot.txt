## Solution

**Change of variables (spherical-type).** Set
$$x = a\sin\theta\cos\phi,\qquad y = a\sin\theta\sin\phi,$$
with $\theta\in[0,\pi/2]$, $\phi\in[0,2\pi]$. This is **not** the polar substitu
tion $(r,\phi)$; it maps the disk onto the parameter domain of a hemisphere, int
roducing the new variable $\theta$ in place of $r$.

**Domain mapping.** When $(x,y)$ ranges over the disk $x^2+y^2\le a^2$, we have
$a^2\sin^2\theta\le a^2$, i.e. $\sin\theta\in[0,1]$, giving $\theta\in[0,\pi/2]$
, and $\phi$ ranges over $[0,2\pi]$. The boundary $x^2+y^2=a^2$ corresponds to $
\theta=\pi/2$; the origin to $\theta=0$.

**Jacobian.**
$$\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(\theta,\phi)}=\begin{vmatrix}a\cos\theta\cos\phi
 & -a\sin\theta\sin\phi\\ a\cos\theta\sin\phi & a\sin\theta\cos\phi\end{vmatrix}
=a^2\sin\theta\cos\theta(\cos^2\phi+\sin^2\phi)=a^2\sin\theta\cos\theta.$$

Since $\theta\in[0,\pi/2]$, this is non-negative, so $dx\,dy = a^2\sin\theta\cos
\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi$.

**Transformed integrand.**
$$\sqrt{x^2+y^2}=a\sin\theta,\qquad \sqrt{a^2-x^2-y^2}=\sqrt{a^2-a^2\sin^2\theta
}=a\cos\theta.$$

So the integrand becomes $a(\sin\theta-\cos\theta)$.

**The integral.**
$$I = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{\pi/2} a(\sin\theta-\cos\theta)\cdot a^2\sin\thet
a\cos\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi = a^3\int_0^{2\pi}d\phi\int_0^{\pi/2}(\sin\theta-\co
s\theta)\sin\theta\cos\theta\,d\theta.$$

The $\phi$-integral gives $2\pi$. For the $\theta$-integral, split:
$$\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^2\theta\cos\theta\,d\theta = \left[\tfrac{\sin^3\theta}{3}\
right]_0^{\pi/2}=\tfrac{1}{3},$$
$$\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos^2\theta\sin\theta\,d\theta = \left[-\tfrac{\cos^3\theta}{3}
\right]_0^{\pi/2}=\tfrac{1}{3}.$$

Therefore
$$\int_0^{\pi/2}(\sin\theta-\cos\theta)\sin\theta\cos\theta\,d\theta = \tfrac{1}
{3}-\tfrac{1}{3}=0,$$

and
$$I = a^3\cdot 2\pi\cdot 0 = \boxed{0}.$$

---

**Verification (independent check via symmetry).** The two terms $\sqrt{x^2+y^2}
$ and $\sqrt{a^2-x^2-y^2}$ are exchanged under the involution $r\mapsto\sqrt{a^2
-r^2}$ (with the appropriate Jacobian adjustment). Concretely, substituting $r=\
sqrt{x^2+y^2}$ and then $u=a^2-r^2$ in the second term shows each term contribut
es $2\pi a^3/3$, so they cancel exactly. This confirms $I=0$.
