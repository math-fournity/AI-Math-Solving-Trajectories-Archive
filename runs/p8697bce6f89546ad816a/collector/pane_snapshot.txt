Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum number of one-way (directed)
 edges in a complete mixed graph on 50 vertices such that every 10-vertex subset
 contains a Hamiltonian cycle.

## Proof

**Setup and Key Equivalence.** We have a complete mixed graph on $n=50$ vertices
: each pair is connected by either a one-way edge (Restricted Line) or a two-way
 edge (High-Capacity Corridor). We need every $k=10$ subset to contain a Hamilto
nian cycle.

Define the **bidirectional closure** as the semicomplete digraph where $i \to j$
 exists iff the edge $ij$ is one-way $i \to j$ or two-way. A Hamiltonian cycle i
n the mixed graph exists iff we can orient the two-way edges to obtain a strongl
y connected tournament, which by Camion's theorem has a Hamiltonian cycle.

**Claim:** A 10-subset $S$ has a Hamiltonian cycle iff the bidirectional closure
 restricted to $S$ is strongly connected.

*Proof of Claim:* If a Hamiltonian cycle exists, the closure is strongly connect
ed (the cycle provides directed paths between all pairs). Conversely, if the clo
sure on $S$ is strongly connected, it is a strongly connected semicomplete digra
ph, which has a Hamiltonian cycle (extension of Camion's theorem to semicomplete
 digraphs). Each edge of this cycle is either one-way (traversed in its directio
n) or bidirectional (corresponding to a two-way edge traversed in the needed dir
ection). $\square$

**Upper Bound: At most 400 directed edges.**

Suppose vertex $v$ has $\geq 9$ one-way out-edges to vertices $u_1, \ldots, u_9$
. Consider the 10-subset $S = \{v, u_1, \ldots, u_9\}$ with partition $X = \{v\}
$, $Y = \{u_1, \ldots, u_9\}$. Every cross-edge is one-way $v \to u_i$, so in th
e bidirectional closure, there is **no edge from $Y$ to $X$**. Thus $S$ is not s
trongly connected, violating the requirement.

Therefore every vertex has **at most 8 one-way out-edges**. By the symmetric arg
ument (reversing all directions), every vertex has at most 8 one-way in-edges.

Since each directed edge contributes exactly 1 to the total out-degree count:
$$\text{directed edges} = \sum_{v} \text{out-deg}(v) \leq 50 \times 8 = 400.$$

**Construction achieving 400 directed edges.**

Label vertices $0, 1, \ldots, 49$ around a cycle. Define:
- **One-way edge** $i \to j$ if $(j - i) \bmod 50 \in \{1, 2, \ldots, 8\}$.
- **Two-way edge** between $i$ and $j$ if $(j - i) \bmod 50 \in \{9, 10, \ldots,
 41\}$.

Each vertex has exactly 8 one-way out-edges and 8 one-way in-edges, giving $50 \
times 8 = 400$ directed edges and $1225 - 400 = 825$ two-way edges.

**Verification that every 10-subset is strongly connected.**

In the bidirectional closure:
- $i \to j$ exists iff $(j-i) \bmod 50 \in \{1, \ldots, 41\}$.
- $j \to i$ exists iff $(j-i) \bmod 50 \in \{9, \ldots, 49\}$.

Vertex $x$ **dominates** vertex $y$ (i.e., $x \to y$ exists but $y \to x$ does n
ot) iff $(y - x) \bmod 50 \in \{1, \ldots, 8\}$. So $x$'s domination set is the
arc $\{x+1, x+2, \ldots, x+8\} \pmod{50}$, which has exactly **8 vertices**.

A 10-subset $S$ with partition $(X, Y)$, $|X| = a$, $|Y| = 10 - a$, is **not** s
trongly connected iff $X$ dominates $Y$, meaning $Y \subseteq \bigcap_{x \in X}
\{x+1, \ldots, x+8\} \pmod{50}$.

For $a$ distinct vertices, the maximum intersection of their domination arcs (ea
ch of length 8) is achieved when the vertices are consecutive: $x, x+1, \ldots,
x+a-1$. The intersection is then:
$$\{x+a, x+a+1, \ldots, x+8\} \pmod{50}, \quad \text{size} = 8 - a + 1 = 9 - a.$
$

We need this to be $\geq 10 - a$ for a dominating partition to exist. But $9 - a
 < 10 - a$ for all $a \geq 1$.

| $a = |X|$ | Max intersection | Required $|Y| = 10-a$ | Feasible? |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------------|-----------|
| 1 | 8 | 9 | No ($8 < 9$) |
| 2 | 7 | 8 | No ($7 < 8$) |
| 3 | 6 | 7 | No ($6 < 7$) |
| $\vdots$ | $\vdots$ | $\vdots$ | $\vdots$ |
| 8 | 1 | 2 | No ($1 < 2$) |
| 9 | 0 | 1 | No ($0 < 1$) |

No dominating partition exists for any 10-subset, so every 10-subset is strongly
 connected in the bidirectional closure. By the Claim, every 10-subset has a Ham
iltonian cycle.

**Conclusion.** The upper bound is 400 and the construction achieves 400. The ma
ximum possible number of Restricted Lines is:

$$\boxed{400}$$
