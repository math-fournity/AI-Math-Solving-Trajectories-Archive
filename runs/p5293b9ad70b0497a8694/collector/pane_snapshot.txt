## Proof

Define the entire function $g(z) = \cos z + e^z$. Note that for real $t \in [0,1
]$, $g(t) = \cos t + e^t = f(t)$.

Set $h(z) = f(z) - g(z)$ for $z \in \bar{U}$. Then:
- $h$ is continuous on $\bar{U}$,
- $h$ is holomorphic on $U$,
- $h(t) = 0$ for all $t \in [0,1]$ (the bottom edge of the square, lying on the
real axis).

The square $U$ has vertices $0, 1, i, 1+i$, so $U \subset \{z : \operatorname{Im
} z > 0\}$ with the segment $[0,1] \subset \partial U \cap \mathbb{R}$.

**Applying the Schwarz Reflection Principle.** Let $U^* = \{\bar{z} : z \in U\}$
 be the reflection of $U$ across the real axis, and set $D = U \cup (0,1) \cup U
^*$, which is a domain symmetric about $\mathbb{R}$. Since $h$ is holomorphic on
 $U$, continuous on $U \cup [0,1]$, and **real-valued** (in fact, identically ze
ro) on $[0,1]$, the Schwarz reflection principle gives a holomorphic extension o
f $h$ to all of $D$, defined by
$$h(\bar{z}) = \overline{h(z)}, \quad z \in U \cup [0,1] \cup U^*.$$

**Applying the Identity Theorem.** The extended $h$ is holomorphic on $D$ and va
nishes on the segment $[0,1] \subset D$, which has a limit point in $D$ (every p
oint of $(0,1)$ is a limit point). By the identity theorem, $h \equiv 0$ on $D$,
 and in particular $h \equiv 0$ on $U$.

**Conclusion.** Since $h = f - g \equiv 0$ on $U$ and both $f$ and $g$ are conti
nuous on $\bar{U}$, we have $f = g$ on $\bar{U}$ by continuity. Therefore

$$\boxed{f(z) = \cos z + e^z \quad \text{for all } z \in \bar{U}.}$$
