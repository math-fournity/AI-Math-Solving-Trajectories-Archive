Proof

Theorem. The separable C*-algebras form a set; that is, there exists a set $
\mathcal{S}$ of C*-algebras such that every separable C*-algebra is *-isomorphic
to some member of $\mathcal{S}$.

Key idea. Every separable C*-algebra admits a faithful representation on a
separable Hilbert space, hence embeds into $\mathcal{B}(\ell^2(\mathbb{N}))$.
Since $\mathcal{B}(\ell^2)$ is a set, its C*-subalgebras form a set (a
subcollection of the power set), and this set contains a representative of every
isomorphism class.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 1. Every separable C*-algebra has a faithful representation on a separable
Hilbert space.

Let $A$ be a separable C*-algebra and let $S(A)$ denote its state space equipped
with the weak* topology. Since $A$ is separable, $S(A)$ is weak*-compact and
metrizable, hence separable. Choose a countable weak*-dense sequence
${\varphi_n}_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq S(A)$.

For each $n$, let $(H_n, \pi_n, \xi_n)$ be the GNS triple of $\varphi_n$: $H_n$
is the completion of $A / N_{\varphi_n}$ where $N_{\varphi_n} = {a \in A :
\varphi_n(a^*a) = 0}$, $\pi_n$ is the induced representation, and $\xi_n$ is the
canonical cyclic vector. Since $A$ is separable, each $H_n$ is a separable
Hilbert space.

Form the direct-sum representation $$\pi := \bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \pi_n :
A \longrightarrow \mathcal{B}!\left(\bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{N}} H_n\right).$$

The Hilbert space $\bigoplus_n H_n$ is a countable direct sum of separable
spaces, hence separable.

Claim: $\pi$ is faithful (injective).

Suppose $a \in A$ and $\pi(a) = 0$. Then $\pi_n(a) = 0$ for every $n$, so $$
\varphi_n(a^*a) = |\pi_n(a)\xi_n|^2 = 0 \quad \text{for all } n.$$ Since
${\varphi_n}$ is weak*-dense in $S(A)$ and the map $\varphi \mapsto
\varphi(a^*a)$ is weak*-continuous, we get $\varphi(a^*a) = 0$ for every state $
\varphi \in S(A)$.

Now $a^*a \geq 0$. A standard fact: states separate positive elements, meaning
if $b \geq 0$ and $\varphi(b) = 0$ for all $\varphi \in S(A)$, then $b = 0$.
(Proof: if $b \neq 0$, then $|b| > 0$ and there is a state $\psi$ with $\psi(b)
= |b| > 0$, obtained from a character on the commutative C*-subalgebra generated
by $b$.) Therefore $a^*a = 0$, hence $a = 0$.

So $\pi$ is faithful and $A \cong \pi(A)$ as C*-algebras. $\square$

Step 2. Every separable Hilbert space embeds into $\ell^2(\mathbb{N})$.

Let $K = \bigoplus_n H_n$ be the separable Hilbert space from Step 1. If $K$ is
finite-dimensional, say $\dim K = d < \infty$, then $K \cong \mathbb{C}^d$,
which embeds isometrically as a closed subspace of $\ell^2(\mathbb{N})$ (via the
first $d$ coordinates). If $K$ is infinite-dimensional and separable, then $K
\cong \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$ (all infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert spaces are
isomorphic).

In either case, there is an isometric embedding $U : K \hookrightarrow
\ell^2(\mathbb{N})$, and conjugation by $U$ gives an injective *-homomorphism $$
\mathrm{Ad}_U : \mathcal{B}(K) \hookrightarrow \mathcal{B}(\ell^2(\mathbb{N})),
\qquad T \mapsto U,T,U^*,$$ whose image consists of those operators on $
\ell^2(\mathbb{N})$ that preserve the subspace $U(K)$ and vanish on its
orthogonal complement (extended by zero).

Composing, $\mathrm{Ad}_U \circ \pi : A \to \mathcal{B}(\ell^2(\mathbb{N}))$ is
a faithful representation, so $$A ;\cong; (\mathrm{Ad}_U \circ \pi)(A) ;
\subseteq; \mathcal{B}(\ell^2(\mathbb{N})).$$

Every separable C*-algebra is *-isomorphic to a C*-subalgebra of $\mathcal{B}
(\ell^2(\mathbb{N}))$. $\square$

Step 3. The C*-subalgebras of $\mathcal{B}(\ell^2(\mathbb{N}))$ form a set.

The Hilbert space $\ell^2(\mathbb{N})$ is a set (its elements are square-
summable sequences, i.e., functions $\mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{C}$, forming a
subset of $\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}$). The set of all bounded linear operators $
\mathcal{B}(\ell^2(\mathbb{N}))$ is a set: a bounded linear operator on a
Hilbert space is determined by its values on an orthonormal basis, so $
\mathcal{B}(\ell^2)$ injects into $(\ell^2)^{\mathbb{N}}$, which is a set.

Define $$\mathcal{S} := \bigl{, B \in \mathcal{P}\bigl(\mathcal{B}
(\ell^2(\mathbb{N}))\bigr) : B \text{ is a C*-subalgebra of } \mathcal{B}
(\ell^2(\mathbb{N})) ,\bigr}.$$

This is a definable subcollection of the power set $\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{B}
(\ell^2(\mathbb{N})))$, hence a set by Separation.

Step 4. Conclusion.

By Step 2, every separable C*-algebra $A$ is *-isomorphic to some $B \in
\mathcal{S}$. By Step 3, $\mathcal{S}$ is a set. Therefore the separable C*-
algebras form a set (up to *-isomorphism): $\mathcal{S}$ is a set of
representatives for all isomorphism classes. $\blacksquare$

Remark. The separability hypothesis is essential. Without it, a C*-algebra may
require a non-separable Hilbert space for a faithful representation, and as the
cardinality of the underlying Hilbert space is unbounded, the collection of all
C*-algebras (up to isomorphism) is a proper class. Separability forces every
faithful representation to live on a fixed separable Hilbert space $
\ell^2(\mathbb{N})$, collapsing the collection to a set.
