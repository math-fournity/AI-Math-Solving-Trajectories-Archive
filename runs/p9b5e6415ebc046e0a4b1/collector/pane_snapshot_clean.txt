## Proof

**Answer: Yes, such a measurable function exists.**

**Setup.** Let $X = Y = [0,1]$, both equipped with the Lebesgue $\sigma$-algebra
 $\mathcal{L}$. Consider the identity function
$$f: X \to Y, \quad f(x) = x.$$

**Step 1: $f$ is measurable.** By definition, $f$ is measurable if for every $B
\in \mathcal{L}$ (Lebesgue-measurable subset of $Y$), we have $f^{-1}(B) \in \ma
thcal{L}$. For the identity function, $f^{-1}(B) = B$ for every $B \subset Y$. I
n particular, for every $B \in \mathcal{L}$, we have $f^{-1}(B) = B \in \mathcal
{L}$. Hence $f$ is measurable.

**Step 2: There exists $B \subset Y$ with $f^{-1}(B) \notin \mathcal{L}$.** Let
$V \subset [0,1]$ be a Vitali set. By the classical construction (choosing one r
epresentative from each coset of $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}$ in $[0,1]$), $V$ is **n
ot** Lebesgue-measurable: $V \notin \mathcal{L}$.

Set $B = V \subset Y$. Then
$$f^{-1}(B) = f^{-1}(V) = V \notin \mathcal{L}.$$

So $f^{-1}(B)$ is not a measurable set.

**Conclusion.** The identity function $f: ([0,1], \mathcal{L}) \to ([0,1], \math
cal{L})$ is measurable, yet for the (non-measurable) Vitali set $B = V \subset Y
$, the preimage $f^{-1}(B) = V$ is not Lebesgue-measurable. This shows that meas
urability of $f$ only guarantees that preimages of **measurable** sets are measu
rable; preimages of arbitrary subsets of $Y$ need not be measurable.
