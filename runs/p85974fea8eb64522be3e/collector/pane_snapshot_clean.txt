## Solution

We evaluate
$$I_n = \int_{2}^{n} \frac{\sin(nx)(x-2)}{\sqrt{\log^5(x)(2+x^3)}}\,dx = \int_2^
n \sin(nx)\,f(x)\,dx,$$
where $f(x) = \dfrac{x-2}{\log^{5/2}(x)\,\sqrt{2+x^3}}$.

**Key observations.** Note $f(2)=0$. For large $x$,
$$f(x) \sim \frac{x}{\log^{5/2}(x)\,x^{3/2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}\,\log^{5/2}(x)}
,$$
so $f\notin L^1([2,\infty))$ (the integral $\int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}\log^{5/2}x}$
 diverges). Thus we cannot apply Riemann–Lebesgue directly to $f$. Instead we in
tegrate by parts to reduce to $f'$.

**Integration by parts.** On $[2,n]$, $f$ is $C^1$ (since $\log x>0$ for $x\ge 2
$). Setting $u=f(x)$, $dv=\sin(nx)\,dx$:
$$I_n = \left[-\frac{\cos(nx)}{n}\,f(x)\right]_2^n + \frac{1}{n}\int_2^n \cos(nx
)\,f'(x)\,dx.$$

**Boundary term.** Since $f(2)=0$:
$$\left[-\frac{\cos(nx)}{n}\,f(x)\right]_2^n = -\frac{\cos(n^2)}{n}\,f(n).$$
For large $n$,
$$|f(n)| = \frac{n-2}{\log^{5/2}(n)\sqrt{2+n^3}} \le \frac{n}{\log^{5/2}(n)\,n^{
3/2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\,\log^{5/2}(n)},$$
so
$$\left|\frac{\cos(n^2)}{n}\,f(n)\right| \le \frac{1}{n^{3/2}\,\log^{5/2}(n)} \l
ongrightarrow 0.$$

**The $f'$ integral.** We claim $f'\in L^1([2,\infty))$. Writing $g(x)=\log^{5/2
}(x)(2+x^3)^{1/2}$, we have $f=(x-2)/g$ and
$$f'(x)=\frac{1}{g(x)}-\frac{(x-2)\,g'(x)}{g(x)^2}.$$
For large $x$, logarithmic differentiation gives $\frac{g'(x)}{g(x)} = \frac{5}{
2x\log x}+\frac{3x^2}{2(2+x^3)} \sim \frac{3}{2x}$, hence
$$f'(x) \sim \frac{1}{x^{3/2}\log^{5/2}(x)} - \frac{3}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{x^{3/2}\l
og^{5/2}(x)} = -\frac{1}{2\,x^{3/2}\log^{5/2}(x)}.$$
Since $\int_2^\infty \frac{dx}{x^{3/2}\log^{5/2}(x)}<\infty$ (substitute $u=\log
 x$: $\int \frac{e^{-u/2}}{u^{5/2}}\,du$ converges), we conclude $f'\in L^1([2,\
infty))$.

Now write
$$\int_2^n \cos(nx)\,f'(x)\,dx = \int_2^\infty \cos(nx)\,f'(x)\,dx - \int_n^\inf
ty \cos(nx)\,f'(x)\,dx.$$
- By the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma ($f'\in L^1$), $\int_2^\infty \cos(nx)\,f'(x)\,d
x \to 0$.
- $\left|\int_n^\infty \cos(nx)\,f'(x)\,dx\right| \le \int_n^\infty |f'(x)|\,dx
\to 0$ (tail of an $L^1$ function).

Hence $\int_2^n \cos(nx)\,f'(x)\,dx \to 0$, and therefore
$$\frac{1}{n}\int_2^n \cos(nx)\,f'(x)\,dx \longrightarrow 0.$$

**Conclusion.** Both the boundary term and the remainder integral tend to $0$, s
o
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} I_n = 0.$$
