Looking at this problem, I need to find the least $n$ such that for any circular
 arrangement of 2014 balls (106 colors, 19 each), some $n$ consecutive balls con
tain at least 53 distinct colors.

## Solution

**Answer: $n = 971$.**

### Lower Bound ($n \geq 971$): A counterexample for $n = 970$

Arrange the balls in 106 contiguous blocks of 19, one block per color, around th
e circle. Any window of 970 consecutive balls starting at offset $r$ within a bl
ock covers $20 - r$ balls of the current block, then $\lfloor(950+r)/19\rfloor$
full blocks, plus at most one partial block. One checks that for every $r \in \{
1,\dots,19\}$, the total number of blocks (colors) touched is exactly **52**. Th
us no window of 970 has 53 colors, so $n \geq 971$.

### Upper Bound ($n = 971$ suffices)

We prove that **every** arrangement has a window of 971 consecutive balls with $
\geq 53$ colors.

**Setup.** There are 2014 cyclic starting positions; window $i$ covers positions
 $i, i+1, \dots, i+970 \pmod{2014}$. For color $c$, let $G_c$ be the largest gap
 between consecutive balls of color $c$ (cyclically, with 19 gaps summing to 201
4).

**Counting windows containing a color.** Window $i$ *avoids* color $c$ iff all 1
9 balls of $c$ lie in the complementary arc of length $2014 - 971 = 1043$. The m
inimal spanning arc of color $c$ has length $L_c = 2014 - G_c$. The number of 10
43-arcs containing all 19 balls is $\max(0,\, G_c - 970)$ (valid when $G_c \geq
971$; otherwise 0). Hence:

$$\text{windows containing } c \;=\; 2014 - \max(0,\, G_c - 970).$$

**Double counting.** Let $S = \sum_{\text{windows}} (\text{number of colors in w
indow}) = \sum_{c} (\text{windows containing } c)$.

Since each $G_c \leq 2014 - 18 = 1996$ (18 gaps are $\geq 1$), we get $\max(0, G
_c - 970) \leq 1026$, so:

$$S = 106 \cdot 2014 - \sum_c \max(0, G_c - 970) \;\geq\; 106 \cdot 2014 - 106 \
cdot 1026 = 106 \cdot 988 = 104728.$$

**Suppose for contradiction** every window of 971 has $\leq 52$ colors. Then $S
\leq 2014 \cdot 52 = 104728$. Combined with $S \geq 104728$, we get $S = 104728$
, forcing:

1. **Every window has exactly 52 colors** (since each $\leq 52$ and the sum equa
ls $2014 \times 52$).
2. **$G_c = 1996$ for every color $c$** (equality in the bound).

Condition 2 means: for every color, the 19 balls occupy **19 consecutive positio
ns** (18 gaps of 1, one gap of 1996). This is precisely the **block arrangement*
*.

**Contradiction.** In the block arrangement, consider the window of 971 starting
 at the **last** ball of any block. It contains: 1 ball of the current block, th
en 51 full blocks ($51 \times 19 = 969$ balls), then 1 ball of the next block —
totaling $1 + 969 + 1 = 971$ balls across $\mathbf{53}$ colors. This contradicts
 condition 1 (every window has exactly 52 colors).

Therefore the assumption is false: some window of 971 consecutive balls always c
ontains $\geq 53$ colors. $\blacksquare$
