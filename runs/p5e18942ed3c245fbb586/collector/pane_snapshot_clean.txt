# Solution

**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

We construct an explicit example. Let $R$ denote the hyperfinite $\mathrm{II}_1$
 factor.

**Step 1: $R \cong R \mathbin{\bar\otimes} R$.**

This is standard. For instance, $R$ is characterized (Connes, 1976) as the uniqu
e separable injective $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor. The tensor product $R \mathbin{\ba
r\otimes} R$ is again a separable $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor (tensor product of fini
te factors with the product trace), and it is injective (a conditional expectati
on $B(H_1\otimes H_2) \to R\bar\otimes R$ is obtained by tensoring the expectati
ons onto each factor). Hence $R \mathbin{\bar\otimes} R \cong R$ by Connes' clas
sification. (One may also see this directly from the CAR-algebra model of $R$.)

**Step 2: The subalgebra $R \mathbin{\bar\otimes} 1 \subset R \mathbin{\bar\otim
es} R$.**

Inside $R \mathbin{\bar\otimes} R$, consider
$$R \mathbin{\bar\otimes} 1 := \{x \otimes 1 : x \in R\}.$$
This is a von Neumann subalgebra, isomorphic to $R$ via $x \mapsto x\otimes 1$.
Since $R$ is a separable $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor, so is $R \mathbin{\bar\otimes}
1$.

**Step 3: The inclusion is strict.**

Since $R$ is a $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor, it contains a non-scalar element — for in
stance a projection $p$ with $0 < \tau(p) < 1$. Then $1 \otimes p \in R \mathbin
{\bar\otimes} R$. If $1\otimes p = x \otimes 1$ for some $x\in R$, then applying
 the slice map (partial trace) in the second variable gives $p = \tau(p)\cdot 1$
 on the left-hand side's second slot... more directly: the subalgebras $R\bar\ot
imes 1$ and $1\bar\otimes R$ intersect exactly in the scalars $\mathbb{C}\cdot 1
$ (their relative commutant structure forces this: $(R\bar\otimes 1)' \cap (R\ba
r\otimes R) = 1\bar\otimes R$, and $R\bar\otimes 1 \cap 1\bar\otimes R = \mathbb
{C}\cdot 1$ since $R$ is a factor). As $p$ is non-scalar, $1\otimes p \notin \ma
thbb{C}\cdot 1$, hence $1\otimes p \notin R\bar\otimes 1$. Therefore
$$R \mathbin{\bar\otimes} 1 \subsetneq R \mathbin{\bar\otimes} R.$$

**Step 4: Transfer into $R$.**

Let $\varphi: R \mathbin{\bar\otimes} R \xrightarrow{\;\cong\;} R$ be the isomor
phism from Step 1. Then
$$M := \varphi\!\left(R \mathbin{\bar\otimes} 1\right) \subset R$$
is a von Neumann subalgebra of $R$, isomorphic to $R\bar\otimes 1 \cong R$, henc
e a **separable $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor**. Since $\varphi$ is an isomorphism and
$R\bar\otimes 1 \subsetneq R\bar\otimes R$, we have
$$M \subsetneq R.$$

Thus $M$ is a separable type $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor that is a strict subalgebra
of the hyperfinite $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** In fact these are essentially the *only* examples: if $N \subset R$
is any inclusion of $\mathrm{II}_1$ factors, the trace-preserving conditional ex
pectation $E: R \to N$ (which exists for any inclusion of finite von Neumann alg
ebras) composed with a conditional expectation $B(H) \to R$ (injectivity of $R$)
 yields a conditional expectation $B(H) \to N$, so $N$ is injective, hence $N \c
ong R$ by Connes. So every separable $\mathrm{II}_1$ subfactor of $R$ is isomorp
hic to $R$ itself — but it can still be *strictly* contained, as shown above.
