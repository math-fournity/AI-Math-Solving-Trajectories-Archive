## Solution

The representing object is the category $[n]$, i.e. the category generated by th
e linearly ordered set $\{0, 1, \ldots, n\}$: it has $n+1$ objects and a unique
morphism $i \to j$ whenever $i \le j$.

---

### Proof

**The candidate.** Let $[n]$ denote the category whose objects are $0, 1, \ldots
, n$ and whose hom-sets are
$$\mathrm{Hom}_{[n]}(i,j) = \begin{cases} \{\,i \to j\,\} & \text{if } i \le j,
\\ \emptyset & \text{if } i > j. \end{cases}$$
Composition is forced: $(j \to k) \circ (i \to j) = (i \to k)$. This is the free
 category on the quiver $0 \to 1 \to 2 \to \cdots \to n$; equivalently it is the
 category associated to the poset $(\{0,\ldots,n\}, \le)$.

**The bijection.** For any category $\mathcal{C}$, define
$$\Phi_{\mathcal{C}} : \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{Cat}}([n],\, \mathcal{C}) \;\longri
ghtarrow\; D_n(\mathcal{C})$$
as follows. A functor $F : [n] \to \mathcal{C}$ is completely determined by its
values on the *generating* morphisms $\alpha_i : (i{-}1) \to i$ for $i = 1, \ldo
ts, n$, since every morphism $i \to j$ (with $i < j$) in $[n]$ admits the unique
 factorisation
$$i \to j \;=\; \alpha_{i+1} \circ \alpha_{i+2} \circ \cdots \circ \alpha_j.$$
Setting $A_k = F(k)$ and $f_i = F(\alpha_i) : A_{i-1} \to A_i$, the pair $(A_\bu
llet, (f_1,\ldots,f_n))$ is precisely a composable $n$-tuple in $\mathcal{C}$, s
ince $\mathrm{cod}(f_i) = A_i = \mathrm{dom}(f_{i+1})$. We define
$$\Phi_{\mathcal{C}}(F) = (f_1, \ldots, f_n).$$

Conversely, given a composable $n$-tuple $(f_1, \ldots, f_n)$ with $f_i : A_{i-1
} \to A_i$, define a functor $\Psi_{\mathcal{C}}(f_1,\ldots,f_n) : [n] \to \math
cal{C}$ by
$$F(k) = A_k, \qquad F(i \to j) = f_{i+1} \circ f_{i+2} \circ \cdots \circ f_j \
quad (i < j),$$
and $F(i \to i) = \mathrm{id}_{A_i}$. Functoriality holds because factorisation
in $[n]$ is unique: if $i \le j \le k$, then
$$F(k \text{ arrow from } i) = f_{i+1} \circ \cdots \circ f_k = (f_{j+1} \circ \
cdots \circ f_k) \circ (f_{i+1} \circ \cdots \circ f_j) = F(j \to k) \circ F(i \
to j).$$
The identities are preserved by construction. Clearly $\Phi_{\mathcal{C}}$ and $
\Psi_{\mathcal{C}}$ are mutually inverse, so $\Phi_{\mathcal{C}}$ is a bijection
.

**Naturality.** Let $G : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}$ be a functor. By definitio
n $D_n(G)$ acts on a composable tuple by post-composition:
$$D_n(G)(f_1,\ldots,f_n) = (G f_1, \ldots, G f_n).$$
On the other hand, $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{Cat}}([n], -)$ acts by post-compositio
n of functors: $G \circ (-)$. Unwinding the bijection,
$$\Phi_{\mathcal{D}}\!\bigl(G \circ F\bigr) = \bigl(G(F\alpha_1), \ldots, G(F\al
pha_n)\bigr) = \bigl(G f_1, \ldots, G f_n\bigr) = D_n(G)\bigl(\Phi_{\mathcal{C}}
(F)\bigr).$$
Hence the square
$$\require{AMScd}
\begin{CD}
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{Cat}}([n],\mathcal{C}) @>{\Phi_{\mathcal{C}}}>> D_n(\mathc
al{C})\\
@V{G \circ (-)}VV @VV{D_n(G)}V\\
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{Cat}}([n],\mathcal{D}) @>>{\Phi_{\mathcal{D}}}> D_n(\mathc
al{D})
\end{CD}$$
commutes, establishing naturality in $\mathcal{C}$.

**Conclusion.** The bijections $\Phi_{\mathcal{C}}$ assemble into a natural isom
orphism
$$D_n \;\cong\; \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{Cat}}([n],\, -),$$
so $D_n$ is representable and its representing object is the category $[n]$, the
 free category on the linear quiver $0 \to 1 \to \cdots \to n$ (equivalently, th
e category associated to the ordinal $\{0,1,\ldots,n\}$). This is the same $[n]$
 that appears as the $n$-simplex indexing category in the nerve construction $N(
\mathcal{C})_n = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{Cat}}([n],\mathcal{C})$.
