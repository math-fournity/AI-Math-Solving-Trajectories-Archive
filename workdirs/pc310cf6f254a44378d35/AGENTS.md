# Problem

In a futuristic city, an architect is designing a modular garden within a $6 \times 6$ square plaza. The plaza is composed of $36$ individual unit squares. The architect has been provided with exactly $9$ rectangular solar benches, each of which occupies exactly two adjacent unit squares (a $1 \times 2$ or $2 \times 1$ footprint).

The city council has imposed a strict "Open Air" regulation: in every possible $2 \times 2$ quadrant of the $6 \times 6$ plaza, there must be at least two unoccupied unit squares that share the same row or share the same column.

Let $f(3)$ represent the total number of distinct ways the architect can arrange these $9$ benches on the $6 \times 6$ grid such that the regulation is satisfied for every $2 \times 2$ subgrid. Calculate $f(3)$.
