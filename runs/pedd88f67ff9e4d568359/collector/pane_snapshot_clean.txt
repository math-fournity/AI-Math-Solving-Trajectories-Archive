**Answer: Yes, it is possible.** We provide an explicit counterexample.

---

**Setup.** Let $X = Y = \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$, the Banach space of square-summable
 real sequences, with standard orthonormal basis $\{e_n\}_{n \geq 1}$. Let $D =
X$ (which is trivially dense in itself).

**Definition of the operators.** For each $n \geq 1$, define $T_n \in \mathcal{L
}(\ell^2, \ell^2)$ by
$$T_n x = \langle x, e_n\rangle\, e_1 = x_n\, e_1, \qquad x = (x_1, x_2, \dots)
\in \ell^2.$$

**$T_n$ is bounded with $\|T_n\| = 1$.** Linearity is clear. For the norm:
$$\|T_n x\|_{\ell^2} = |x_n|\,\|e_1\| = |x_n| \leq \|x\|_{\ell^2},$$
so $\|T_n\| \leq 1$. On the other hand, $T_n e_n = e_1$, so $\|T_n e_n\| = 1 = \
|e_n\|$, giving $\|T_n\| \geq 1$. Hence $\|T_n\| = 1$ for every $n$.

**The sequence $\{T_n\}$ is bounded.** $\sup_{n} \|T_n\| = 1 < \infty$. ✓

**Pointwise convergence to $T = 0$.** For any $x = (x_1, x_2, \dots) \in \ell^2$
, we have $\sum_{k=1}^\infty x_k^2 < \infty$, which forces $x_k \to 0$ as $k \to
 \infty$. Therefore
$$\|T_n x\| = |x_n| \xrightarrow{n \to \infty} 0,$$
so $T_n x \to 0$ for every $x \in X$. The limit operator is $T = 0$, with $\|T\|
 = 0$. In particular, $T_n z$ converges for all $z \in D = X$. ✓

**The norms do not converge.** We have
$$\|T_n\|_{\mathcal{L}} = 1 \quad \text{for all } n, \qquad \|T\|_{\mathcal{L}}
= 0.$$
Thus $\|T_n\|_{\mathcal{L}} \to 1 \neq 0 = \|T\|_{\mathcal{L}}$, i.e.,
$$\|T_n\|_{\mathcal{L}} \not\to \|T\|_{\mathcal{L}}.$$

---

**Remark.** This illustrates the general principle that pointwise (strong) opera
tor convergence $T_n \xrightarrow{s} T$ guarantees only the lower semicontinuity
 of the norm:
$$\|T\| \leq \liminf_{n\to\infty} \|T_n\|,$$
but **not** equality. The "mass" of $T_n$ can escape to parts of the space where
 every fixed vector sees it vanishing (here, the coordinate $x_n \to 0$ for each
 fixed $x$), while the operator norm—being a supremum over the unit ball—detects
 this escaping mass and stays bounded away from $\|T\|$.
