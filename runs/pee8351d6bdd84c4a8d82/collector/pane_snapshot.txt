## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $v = u'$, i.e. $v$ is the weak (distributional) time derivative
of $u$.

**Setup and definitions.** Recall that for a function $w \in L^2(0,T;H)$, an ele
ment $z \in L^2(0,T;H^*)$ is the *weak derivative* of $w$ (written $z = w'$) iff
 for every test function $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(0,T)$ and every $h \in H$,

$$\int_0^T \langle z(t), h\rangle_{H^*,H}\,\varphi(t)\,dt \;=\; -\int_0^T (w(t),
 h)_H\,\varphi'(t)\,dt. \tag{def}$$

By hypothesis, for each $m$ the pair $(u_m, u_m')$ satisfies (def) with $w = u_m
$, $z = u_m'$:

$$\int_0^T \langle u_m'(t), h\rangle\,\varphi(t)\,dt \;=\; -\int_0^T (u_m(t), h)
_H\,\varphi'(t)\,dt. \tag{$*$}$$

**Passing to the limit on the left-hand side.** Define the functional
$$\Phi_1 : L^2(0,T;H^*) \to \mathbb{R}, \qquad \Phi_1(z) := \int_0^T \langle z(t
), h\rangle\,\varphi(t)\,dt.$$
Since $h \in H$ and $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(0,T) \subset L^2(0,T)$, the map $t \
mapsto \varphi(t)h$ belongs to $L^2(0,T;H)$, so $\Phi_1$ is a continuous linear
functional on $L^2(0,T;H^*)$. The hypothesis $u_m' \rightharpoonup v$ in $L^2(0,
T;H^*)$ therefore gives
$$\Phi_1(u_m') \to \Phi_1(v), \quad\text{i.e.}\quad \int_0^T \langle u_m'(t), h\
rangle\,\varphi(t)\,dt \;\longrightarrow\; \int_0^T \langle v(t), h\rangle\,\var
phi(t)\,dt. \tag{LHS}$$

**Passing to the limit on the right-hand side.** Define
$$\Phi_2 : L^2(0,T;H) \to \mathbb{R}, \qquad \Phi_2(w) := \int_0^T (w(t), h)_H\,
\varphi'(t)\,dt.$$
Since $t \mapsto \varphi'(t)h \in L^2(0,T;H)$, $\Phi_2$ is a continuous linear f
unctional on $L^2(0,T;H)$. The hypothesis $u_m \rightharpoonup u$ in $L^2(0,T;H)
$ gives
$$\Phi_2(u_m) \to \Phi_2(u), \quad\text{i.e.}\quad \int_0^T (u_m(t), h)_H\,\varp
hi'(t)\,dt \;\longrightarrow\; \int_0^T (u(t), h)_H\,\varphi'(t)\,dt. \tag{RHS}$
$

**Combining.** Taking $m \to \infty$ in ($*$) and using (LHS) and (RHS),

$$\int_0^T \langle v(t), h\rangle\,\varphi(t)\,dt \;=\; -\int_0^T (u(t), h)_H\,\
varphi'(t)\,dt.$$

This holds for every $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(0,T)$ and every $h \in H$. By the d
efinition (def) of the weak derivative, this is precisely the statement that $v
= u'$ (as an element of $L^2(0,T;H^*)$).

**Conclusion.** The weak limit of the derivatives equals the derivative of the w
eak limit: $v = u'$ in $L^2(0,T;H^*)$.
