## Proof

**Theorem (Steinhaus).** Let $A, B \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ be Lebesgue measurable
with $m(A), m(B) > 0$. Then $A + B$ contains a nonempty open interval.

**Step 1: Reduction to finite measure.** Since $m(A) > 0$ and $\mathbb{R} = \big
cup_{n} [-n,n]$, there exists $n$ with $m(A \cap [-n,n]) > 0$; set $A' = A \cap
[-n,n]$. Likewise choose $B' = B \cap [-m,m]$ with $m(B') > 0$. Then $A', B'$ ha
ve finite, positive measure, and $A' + B' \subseteq A + B$, so it suffices to sh
ow $A' + B'$ contains an open interval.

**Step 2: Define the convolution.** Let $f = \mathbf{1}_{A'} * \mathbf{1}_{B'}$,
 i.e.
$$f(x) = \int_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbf{1}_{A'}(t)\,\mathbf{1}_{B'}(x - t)\,dt = m\bi
gl(A' \cap (x - B')\bigr).$$

**Step 3: $f$ is continuous.** For $h \in \mathbb{R}$,
$$|f(x+h) - f(x)| \le \int_{A'} |\mathbf{1}_{B'}(x+h-t) - \mathbf{1}_{B'}(x-t)|\
,dt \le \int_{\mathbb{R}} |\mathbf{1}_{B'}(s+h) - \mathbf{1}_{B'}(s)|\,ds.$$
The last expression is $\|\mathbf{1}_{B'}(\cdot + h) - \mathbf{1}_{B'}(\cdot)\|_
{L^1}$, which tends to $0$ as $h \to 0$ by continuity of translation in $L^1$ (v
alid since $\mathbf{1}_{B'} \in L^1$). Hence $f$ is (uniformly) continuous.

**Step 4: $f$ is not identically zero.** By Fubini/Tonelli,
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} f(x)\,dx = \int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbf{1}_{A'
}(t)\,\mathbf{1}_{B'}(x-t)\,dt\,dx = m(A')\,m(B') > 0.$$
Therefore $f$ is not identically zero, so there exists $x_0$ with $f(x_0) > 0$.

**Step 5: Positivity of $f$ gives an interval in $A+B$.** Since $f(x_0) = m(A' \
cap (x_0 - B')) > 0$, the set $A' \cap (x_0 - B')$ is nonempty: there exist $a \
in A'$, $b \in B'$ with $a = x_0 - b$, i.e. $x_0 = a + b \in A' + B'$. More impo
rtantly, $f(x) > 0$ implies $x \in A' + B'$ (the same argument: $A' \cap (x - B'
) \neq \emptyset$ means $x = a + b$ for some $a \in A'$, $b \in B'$).

By continuity of $f$ and $f(x_0) > 0$, there exists $\varepsilon > 0$ such that
$f(x) > 0$ for all $x \in (x_0 - \varepsilon,\, x_0 + \varepsilon)$. Hence
$$(x_0 - \varepsilon,\, x_0 + \varepsilon) \subseteq A' + B' \subseteq A + B.$$

Thus $A + B$ contains a nonempty open interval. $\blacksquare$
