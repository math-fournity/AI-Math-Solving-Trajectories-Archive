## Proof

**Model.** The country is the complete graph $K_n$ (cities = vertices, railroads
 = edges). The worker must traverse every edge exactly once by train. A flight i
s a "jump" between two cities that does not consume a railroad edge, effectively
 connecting two separate train-trails.

If the worker uses $f$ flights, the journey consists of $f + 1$ train-trails (ma
ximal consecutive train-edge sequences) that together partition all $\binom{n}{2
}$ edges of $K_n$.

**Parity observation.** In any trail, each internal vertex contributes an even n
umber of incident edges (entries and exits pair up), while each endpoint contrib
utes an odd number. Since every vertex of $K_n$ has degree $n - 1$:

- **$n$ odd:** $n - 1$ is even, so each vertex is an endpoint of an *even* numbe
r of trails (possibly zero).
- **$n$ even:** $n - 1$ is odd, so each vertex is an endpoint of an *odd* number
 of trails (at least one).

---

**Case 1: $n$ is odd.** Every vertex has even degree, so $K_n$ is Eulerian: a cl
osed Eulerian circuit traverses all edges exactly once by train alone. The minim
um number of flights is $\boxed{0}$.

---

**Case 2: $n$ is even.** Every vertex has odd degree $n-1$, so each of the $n$ v
ertices must be an endpoint of at least one of the $f+1$ trails. Since each trai
l has at most 2 endpoints:

$$2(f+1) \geq n \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f \geq \frac{n}{2} - 1.$$

**Achievability.** Pair the $n$ vertices into $n/2$ pairs and add one dummy edge
 per pair. Every vertex now has degree $n - 1 + 1 = n$ (even), so the augmented
graph admits an Eulerian circuit. Removing the $n/2$ dummy edges from this circu
it breaks it into exactly $n/2$ edge-disjoint trails covering all original edges
 of $K_n$. The worker traverses these $n/2$ trails by train, using $n/2 - 1$ fli
ghts to jump between consecutive trails. This achieves the lower bound.

**Verification on small cases:**
- $n = 2$: $K_2$ has 1 edge; one trail, 0 flights. Formula: $2/2 - 1 = 0$. ✓
- $n = 4$: $K_4$ has 6 edges, all vertices degree 3 (odd). Decompose into trails
: $1\to2\to3\to1\to4$ (edges 12, 23, 13, 14) and $2\to4\to3$ (edges 24, 34). One
 flight (city 4 → city 2). Formula: $4/2 - 1 = 1$. ✓
- $n = 6$: 6 odd vertices → 3 trails → 2 flights. Formula: $6/2 - 1 = 2$. ✓

---

**Conclusion.** The minimal number of flights is:

$$f_{\min} = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } n \text{ is odd}, \\[6pt] \dfrac{n}{2}
 - 1 & \text{if } n \text{ is even}. \end{cases}$$
