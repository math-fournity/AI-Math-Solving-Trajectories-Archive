Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a finite-dimensional $k$-al
gebra $A$ with $A/J(A) \cong k^n$ must contain a 1-dimensional two-sided ideal.

**Answer: Yes.** Here is the proof.

---

**Proof.**

Let $J = J(A)$. Since $A$ is finite-dimensional over $k$, $J$ is nilpotent. Let
$m \geq 0$ be the nilpotency index: $J^m \neq 0$ and $J^{m+1} = 0$ (where $J^0 =
 A$ by convention, covering the case $J=0$).

Since $A/J \cong k^n$ is semisimple and $J$ is nilpotent, the idempotent lifting
 theorem applies: there exist pairwise orthogonal idempotents $e_1, \dots, e_n \
in A$ with $e_1 + \cdots + e_n = 1$ whose images in $A/J$ are the standard basis
 idempotents of $k^n$. In particular $e_i \notin J$ for all $i$, and as a $k$-ve
ctor space:

$$A = \bigoplus_{i=1}^n k\, e_i \;\oplus\; J.$$

**Key observation.** The nonzero ideal $J^m$ admits the Peirce decomposition:

$$J^m = \bigoplus_{i,j=1}^n e_i\, J^m\, e_j.$$

Since $J^m \neq 0$, at least one component $e_i\, J^m\, e_j$ is nonzero. Choose
any nonzero $v \in e_i\, J^m\, e_j$.

**Claim.** $kv$ is a two-sided ideal of $A$ with $\dim_k(kv) = 1$.

*Left ideal.* For any $a \in A$, write $a = \sum_{\ell} \alpha_\ell\, e_\ell + j
$ with $\alpha_\ell \in k$ and $j \in J$. Then:

$$a\,v = \sum_\ell \alpha_\ell\, e_\ell\, v \;+\; j\,v.$$

Since $v \in e_i\, J^m\, e_j$, orthogonality gives $e_\ell\, v = \delta_{\ell i}
\, v$, so the first term equals $\alpha_i\, v \in kv$. Since $v \in J^m$ and $j
\in J$, we have $j\,v \in J \cdot J^m = J^{m+1} = 0$. Thus $av = \alpha_i\, v \i
n kv$.

*Right ideal.* Similarly:

$$v\,a = \sum_\ell \alpha_\ell\, v\, e_\ell \;+\; v\,j.$$

Since $v \in e_i\, J^m\, e_j$, we have $v\, e_\ell = \delta_{j\ell}\, v$, so the
 first term equals $\alpha_j\, v \in kv$. Since $v \in J^m$ and $j \in J$, we ha
ve $v\,j \in J^m \cdot J = J^{m+1} = 0$. Thus $va = \alpha_j\, v \in kv$.

Therefore $kv$ is closed under both left and right multiplication by $A$, hence
is a two-sided ideal. Since $v \neq 0$, we have $\dim_k(kv) = 1$. $\square$

---

**Remark.** The argument uses two ingredients: (1) nilpotency of $J$ guarantees
a nonzero "last power" $J^m$ annihilated by $J$ on both sides, and (2) the Peirc
e decomposition relative to the lifted idempotents isolates elements that are si
multaneously eigenvectors for all left and right multiplications by the $e_\ell$
, reducing the $A$-action to scalar multiplication. The hypothesis $A/J(A) \cong
 k^n$ (rather than a product of matrix algebras or division algebras) is essenti
al: it ensures the idempotents $e_i$ act by *scalars* on each Peirce component,
which is what makes the 1-dimensional subspace stable.
