**Interpretation:** With $z = x + iy$ and the integral over $d|x|$ from 0 to 1,
the request to express the answer purely in terms of $\pi$ (with no free paramet
er) forces $y = 0$. Then $|z| = |x| = x$ on $[0,1]$, and $d|x| = dx$. The integr
al reduces to:

$$I = \int_0^1 \frac{\sin(\ln x)}{\ln x}\,dx.$$

## Proof

**Key identity.** For any real $u$,
$$\frac{\sin u}{u} = \int_0^1 \cos(tu)\,dt,$$
since $\int_0^1 \cos(tu)\,dt = \frac{\sin u}{u}$.

Applying this with $u = \ln x$:

$$I = \int_0^1 \frac{\sin(\ln x)}{\ln x}\,dx = \int_0^1 \left(\int_0^1 \cos(t\ln
 x)\,dt\right) dx.$$

**Swap the order of integration** (justified by absolute integrability — see ver
ification below):

$$I = \int_0^1 \left(\int_0^1 \cos(t\ln x)\,dx\right) dt.$$

**Evaluate the inner integral.** Write $\cos(t\ln x) = \operatorname{Re}(x^{it})
$:

$$\int_0^1 \cos(t\ln x)\,dx = \operatorname{Re}\int_0^1 x^{it}\,dx = \operatorna
me{Re}\left[\frac{x^{1+it}}{1+it}\right]_0^1 = \operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{1}{1
+it}\right).$$

Rationalizing:
$$\frac{1}{1+it} = \frac{1-it}{1+t^2}, \qquad \operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{1}{1+
it}\right) = \frac{1}{1+t^2}.$$

(The boundary at $x=0$: $|x^{1+it}| = x^1 \to 0$, so the lower limit contributes
 $0$.)

**Finish the outer integral:**

$$I = \int_0^1 \frac{dt}{1+t^2} = \arctan(t)\Big|_0^1 = \arctan(1) - \arctan(0)
= \frac{\pi}{4}.$$

**Justification of the swap (Fubini).** We check absolute integrability:
$$\int_0^1\int_0^1 |\cos(t\ln x)|\,dt\,dx \le \int_0^1\int_0^1 1\,dt\,dx = 1 < \
infty,$$
so Fubini's theorem applies and the swap is valid.

---

## Result

$$\boxed{I = \dfrac{\pi}{4}}$$
