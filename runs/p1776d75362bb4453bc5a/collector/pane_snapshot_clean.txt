## Solution

**Setup and conditional structure.** Condition on $U_1,\dots,U_n$. Then
$$X_n = \sum_{k=1}^n U_k a_k,\qquad Y_n = \sum_{k=1}^n (U_k^2-\tfrac12)a_k$$
is a sum of i.i.d. (conditional on $U$'s) 2D random vectors $a_k\,(U_k,\,U_k^2-\
tfrac12)$, each taking values $\pm(U_k,\,U_k^2-\tfrac12)$ with probability $1/2$
. By the multivariate CLT, $\bigl(X_n/\sqrt n,\,Y_n/\sqrt n\bigr)$ converges (co
nditionally) to a centered bivariate normal with covariance matrix
$$\Sigma_n=\frac1n\sum_{k=1}^n\begin{pmatrix}U_k^2 & U_k(U_k^2-\tfrac12)\\ U_k(U
_k^2-\tfrac12) & (U_k^2-\tfrac12)^2\end{pmatrix}.$$

**Deterministic limit of $\Sigma_n$.** By the LLN (the summands are i.i.d. since
 $U_k$ are):
- $\frac1n\sum U_k^2 \to \mathbb E[U^2]=\tfrac13$,
- $\frac1n\sum (U_k^2-\tfrac12)^2 \to \mathbb E[(U^2-\tfrac12)^2]=\mathbb E[U^4]
-\mathbb E[U^2]+\tfrac14=\tfrac15-\tfrac13+\tfrac14=\tfrac{7}{60}$,
- $\frac1n\sum U_k(U_k^2-\tfrac12)\to \mathbb E[U^3]-\tfrac12\mathbb E[U]=\tfrac
14-\tfrac14=0$.

Since $\Sigma_n\to\Sigma:=\mathrm{diag}\bigl(\tfrac13,\tfrac{7}{60}\bigr)$ deter
ministically, the conditional limit is a fixed bivariate normal, hence the **unc
onditional** limit also holds:
$$\bigl(X_n/\sqrt n,\,Y_n/\sqrt n\bigr)\xrightarrow{d}(Z_1,Z_2),\qquad (Z_1,Z_2)
\sim N\!\left(0,\,\mathrm{diag}\bigl(\tfrac13,\tfrac{7}{60}\bigr)\right).$$
Zero covariance + joint normality $\Rightarrow$ $Z_1,Z_2$ **independent**.

**Reducing the event.** Let $A=Z_1\sqrt 3$ and $B=Z_2\sqrt{60/7}$; then $A,B$ ar
e i.i.d. $N(0,1)$. The boundary of the event is
$$|X_n|>Y_n\sqrt{20/7}\iff |Z_1|>Z_2\sqrt{20/7}.$$
Compute the right-hand scaling:
$$Z_2\sqrt{20/7}=B\sqrt{7/60}\cdot\sqrt{20/7}=B\sqrt{20/60}=B/\sqrt 3.$$
Also $Z_1=A/\sqrt 3$. Thus
$$|Z_1|>Z_2\sqrt{20/7}\iff |A|/\sqrt 3>B/\sqrt 3\iff |A|>B.$$

**Computing $\mathbb P(|A|>B)$.** Split on the sign of $B$:
$$\mathbb P(|A|>B)=\mathbb P(B<0)+\mathbb P(|A|>B,\,B\ge 0)=\tfrac12+\mathbb P(|
A|>B,\,B\ge 0).$$
(The first term uses $|A|\ge 0>B$ whenever $B<0$.) For the second term, by symme
try of $A$,
$$\mathbb P(|A|>B,\,B\ge 0)=\mathbb P(A>B\ge 0)+\mathbb P(-A>B\ge 0)=2\,\mathbb
P(A>B\ge 0).$$
The joint density of $(A,B)$ is rotationally invariant (isotropic). The region $
\{A>B\ge 0\}$ is the sector of the first quadrant lying below the line $A=B$, i.
e. the angular sector from $0^\circ$ to $45^\circ$ — exactly half of the first q
uadrant. Hence
$$\mathbb P(A>B\ge 0)=\tfrac12\cdot\mathbb P(\text{first quadrant})=\tfrac12\cdo
t\tfrac14=\tfrac18.$$
Therefore
$$\mathbb P(|A|>B,\,B\ge 0)=2\cdot\tfrac18=\tfrac14,\qquad \mathbb P(|A|>B)=\tfr
ac12+\tfrac14=\tfrac34.$$

**Conclusion.**
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb P\!\left(|X_n|>Y_n\sqrt{\tfrac{20}{7}}\right)=\mathbb
 P(|A|>B)=\boxed{\tfrac34}.$$
