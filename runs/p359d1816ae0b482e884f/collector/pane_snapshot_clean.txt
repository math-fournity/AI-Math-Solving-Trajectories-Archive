## Proof

Place the 4-cube as $C = [-\tfrac12, \tfrac12]^4 \subset \mathbb{R}^4$ (side len
gth 1, centered at the origin). A *circle* is a 2-dimensional object: it lies in
 some affine 2-plane and consists of all points at a fixed distance (the radius)
 from a center.

**Symmetry reduction.** The cube is centrally symmetric and invariant under coor
dinate permutations and sign flips. The largest inscribed circle may be taken ce
ntered at the origin and lying in a linear 2-plane $P$ through the origin. (Tran
slating the center off the origin only reduces the available room in at least on
e direction; a standard symmetrization argument confirms the optimum is at the c
enter.)

**Cross-section inradius.** For a unit vector $u \in P$, the ray from the origin
 in direction $u$ hits the boundary of $C$ at distance
$$t(u) = \frac{1/2}{\max_i |u_i|} = \frac{1}{2\,\|u\|_\infty}.$$
The intersection $P \cap C$ is a centrally symmetric polygon whose inradius (abo
ut the origin) is
$$r(P) = \min_{\substack{u \in P \\ \|u\|=1}} t(u) = \frac{1}{2\,\displaystyle\m
ax_{\substack{u \in P\\\|u\|=1}} \|u\|_\infty}.$$
So maximizing $r(P)$ is equivalent to minimizing $M(P) := \max_{u\in P,\,\|u\|=1
}\|u\|_\infty$.

**Expressing $M(P)$.** Let $P$ be spanned by orthonormal vectors $a, b \in \math
bb{R}^4$. Every unit $u\in P$ is $u = \cos\theta\, a + \sin\theta\, b$. Then
$$\max_{\theta}\max_i |\cos\theta\, a_i + \sin\theta\, b_i| = \max_i \max_\theta
 |\cos\theta\, a_i + \sin\theta\, b_i| = \max_i \sqrt{a_i^2 + b_i^2},$$
since $\max_\theta |A\cos\theta + B\sin\theta| = \sqrt{A^2+B^2}$. Thus
$$M(P) = \max_{i=1,\dots,4} \sqrt{a_i^2 + b_i^2}.$$

**Lower bound on $M(P)$.** Set $c_i := \sqrt{a_i^2+b_i^2}$ (the norm of the $i$-
th row of the $4\times 2$ matrix with orthonormal columns $a,b$). Then
$$\sum_{i=1}^4 c_i^2 = \sum_i (a_i^2+b_i^2) = \|a\|^2 + \|b\|^2 = 2.$$
With four nonnegative numbers $c_i^2$ summing to $2$, the largest satisfies $\ma
x c_i^2 \ge 2/4 = 1/2$, hence
$$M(P) = \max_i c_i \ge \frac{1}{\sqrt2}.$$
Therefore $r(P) \le \dfrac{1}{2\cdot(1/\sqrt2)} = \dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$ for every p
lane $P$.

**Achievability.** Take
$$a = \tfrac12(1,1,1,1), \qquad b = \tfrac12(1,1,-1,-1).$$
Then $\|a\|=\|b\|=1$ and $a\cdot b = 0$, so they span a 2-plane. Each row norm i
s
$$\sqrt{a_i^2+b_i^2} = \sqrt{\tfrac14+\tfrac14} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2},$$
so $M(P)=1/\sqrt2$ and $r(P) = \sqrt2/2$.

Concretely, the circle of radius $r=\sqrt2/2$ in this plane is
$$\gamma(\theta) = r\bigl(\tfrac{\cos\theta+\sin\theta}{2},\;\tfrac{\cos\theta+\
sin\theta}{2},\;\tfrac{\cos\theta-\sin\theta}{2},\;\tfrac{\cos\theta-\sin\theta}
{2}\bigr).$$
Each coordinate has absolute value $\le r\cdot\tfrac{|\cos\theta|+|\sin\theta|}{
2} \le \tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2} = \tfrac12$, with equality achiev
ed (e.g. at $\theta=0$ the point is $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,0,0)\cdot\sqrt2 = (\tfra
c{\sqrt2}{2},\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2},0,0)$... checking: at $\theta=0$, $\gamma = \tfra
c{\sqrt2}{2}(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12) = (\tfrac{\sqrt2}{4},\tfrac{\s
qrt2}{4},\tfrac{\sqrt2}{4},\tfrac{\sqrt2}{4}) \approx (0.354,0.354,0.354,0.354)$
, inside). At $\theta=\pi/4$: $\cos=\sin=\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$, giving $\gamma = \t
frac{\sqrt2}{2}(\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2},0,0) = (\tfrac12,\tfrac12,0,
0)$, on the boundary. So the circle touches the boundary and stays inside, confi
rming radius $\sqrt2/2$ is attained.

**Conclusion.** The largest possible radius is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}}.$$
