# Solution

We analyze the planar system
$$\dot x = 4-4x^2-y^2, \qquad \dot y = 3xy.$$

The standard task for such a system is to locate all equilibrium (critical) poin
ts and classify each by type. We do this completely.

## 1. Equilibrium points

Equilibria satisfy $\dot x = \dot y = 0$.

From $\dot y = 3xy = 0$, either $x=0$ or $y=0$.

- **If $x=0$**: $\dot x = 4 - y^2 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; y = \pm 2$. Points: $(0,2)
$, $(0,-2)$.
- **If $y=0$**: $\dot x = 4 - 4x^2 = 4(1-x^2) = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x = \pm 1$. Po
ints: $(1,0)$, $(-1,0)$.

So there are **four equilibria**: $(0,2),\,(0,-2),\,(1,0),\,(-1,0)$.

## 2. Linearization

The Jacobian is
$$J(x,y) = \begin{pmatrix} \partial_x \dot x & \partial_y \dot x \\ \partial_x \
dot y & \partial_y \dot y \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -8x & -2y \\ 3y & 3x \
end{pmatrix}.$$

**At $(1,0)$:** $J = \begin{pmatrix} -8 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$, eigenvalues
 $\lambda_1 = -8$, $\lambda_2 = 3$. Real, opposite signs $\Rightarrow$ **saddle*
*. Stable direction $(1,0)$, unstable direction $(0,1)$.

**At $(-1,0)$:** $J = \begin{pmatrix} 8 & 0 \\ 0 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$, eigenvalue
s $\lambda_1 = 8$, $\lambda_2 = -3$. Real, opposite signs $\Rightarrow$ **saddle
**. Unstable direction $(1,0)$, stable direction $(0,1)$.

**At $(0,2)$:** $J = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -4 \\ 6 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, characteris
tic polynomial $\lambda^2 + 24 = 0$, eigenvalues $\lambda = \pm 2i\sqrt{6}$. Pur
ely imaginary, nonzero. Linearization says **center**, but the nonlinear type (c
enter vs. spiral) must be determined.

**At $(0,-2)$:** $J = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 4 \\ -6 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, eigenvalue
s $\lambda = \pm 2i\sqrt{6}$. Same situation.

## 3. The points $(0,\pm 2)$ are genuine centers (reversibility argument)

We show the system is **reversible**, which forces these to be nonlinear centers
.

Define the reflection $R(x,y) = (-x,\,y)$ (across the $y$-axis). Its derivative
is $DR = \mathrm{diag}(-1,1)$. Let $F(x,y) = (\dot x, \dot y) = (4-4x^2-y^2,\; 3
xy)$. Compute $F$ at the reflected point:
$$F(R(x,y)) = F(-x,y) = \bigl(4-4x^2-y^2,\; 3(-x)y\bigr) = \bigl(f(x,y),\; -g(x,
y)\bigr),$$
since $f(-x,y)=f(x,y)$ (even in $x$) and $g(-x,y)=-g(x,y)$ (odd in $x$). On the
other hand,
$$-DR\,F(x,y) = -\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}f\\g\end{pm
atrix} = \begin{pmatrix}f\\-g\end{pmatrix}.$$
Thus $F\circ R = -DR\circ F$, which is precisely the **reversibility condition**
 $F(Rp) = -DR\,F(p)$.

The fixed set of $R$ is the $y$-axis $\{x=0\}$, and both equilibria $(0,2)$ and
$(0,-2)$ lie on it. A standard theorem of planar reversible systems states:

> *If $\dot z = F(z)$ is reversible under an involution $R$ whose fixed set is a
 line, and $p\in\mathrm{Fix}(R)$ is an equilibrium whose linearization has purel
y imaginary, nonzero eigenvalues, then $p$ is a **center** (it is surrounded by
a punctured neighborhood of closed orbits).*

(Intuition: reversibility forces the Poincaré first-return map on a transverse s
ection to be an involution, so every Lyapunov focal value vanishes; no spiral ca
n form.)

Both $(0,2)$ and $(0,-2)$ satisfy the hypotheses (on $\mathrm{Fix}(R)$, eigenval
ues $\pm 2i\sqrt6$), hence both are **centers**.

*Direct local check at $(0,2)$:* set $u=x,\;v=y-2$. Then
$$\dot u = -4v - 4u^2 - v^2,\qquad \dot v = 6u + 3uv,$$
which is reversible under $(u,v)\mapsto(-u,v)$: $\dot u(-u,v)=\dot u(u,v)$ and $
\dot v(-u,v)=-\dot v(u,v)$, confirming the same structure. The argument at $(0,-
2)$ is identical.

## 4. Summary of the phase portrait

| Equilibrium | Eigenvalues of $J$ | Type |
|---|---|---|
| $(1,0)$ | $-8,\;3$ | saddle |
| $(-1,0)$ | $8,\;-3$ | saddle |
| $(0,2)$ | $\pm 2i\sqrt6$ | center |
| $(0,-2)$ | $\pm 2i\sqrt6$ | center |

The $x$-axis ($y=0$) is invariant (since $\dot y = 3xy = 0$ there), with $\dot x
 = 4(1-x^2)$: flow moves right for $|x|<1$ and left for $|x|>1$, consistent with
 $(\pm1,0)$ being saddles and $(1,0)$ having its stable manifold along the $x$-a
xis. Around each of $(0,\pm2)$ there is a family of closed orbits (centers), and
 the two saddles at $(\pm1,0)$ organize the global separatrices.

**Conclusion.** The system has exactly four equilibria: $(1,0)$ and $(-1,0)$ are
 **saddles**, while $(0,2)$ and $(0,-2)$ are **centers** (the latter established
 by the reversibility of the system under $x\mapsto -x$).
