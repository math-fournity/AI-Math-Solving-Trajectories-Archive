# Solution: Instantaneous Cylindrical Heat Source

## Problem Setup

We seek the temperature $T(r,t)$ in an infinite 2D region due to an instantaneou
s release of $Q$ units of heat per unit length, uniformly distributed on the cir
cle $r = r'$ at $t = 0$.

The heat equation in radial coordinates (azimuthal symmetry, no $z$-dependence)
is:

$$\frac{\partial T}{\partial t} = a^2\left(\frac{\partial^2 T}{\partial r^2} + \
frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial T}{\partial r}\right), \qquad r > 0,\; t > 0,$$

where $a^2$ is the thermal diffusivity and $c\rho$ is the volumetric heat capaci
ty.

## Initial Condition

The total heat per unit length is $Q$, concentrated on the circle $r = r'$. The
initial temperature is a radially symmetric distribution:

$$T(r,0) = \frac{Q}{2\pi r'\, c\rho}\,\delta(r - r').$$

**Verification of normalization:** The total heat is

$$\int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_0^\infty T(r,0)\,c\rho\; r\,dr\,d\theta = \frac{Q}{2\pi r'
\,c\rho}\cdot c\rho \cdot 2\pi \cdot r' = Q. \quad\checkmark$$

## Solution via the 2D Heat Kernel

The fundamental solution (Green's function) of the 2D heat equation $\partial_t
u = a^2 \nabla^2 u$ is

$$G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}_0,t) = \frac{1}{4\pi a^2 t}\exp\!\left(-\frac{|\mathbf
{r}-\mathbf{r}_0|^2}{4a^2 t}\right).$$

The solution is the convolution $T(\mathbf{r},t) = \int G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}_
0,t)\,T(\mathbf{r}_0,0)\,d^2\mathbf{r}_0$.

Writing $\mathbf{r}_0 = (\rho\cos\phi,\,\rho\sin\phi)$ and using $|\mathbf{r}-\m
athbf{r}_0|^2 = r^2 + \rho^2 - 2r\rho\cos(\theta-\phi)$, the initial condition $
T(\mathbf{r}_0,0) = \frac{Q}{2\pi r'\,c\rho}\delta(\rho - r')$ gives:

$$T(r,t) = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_0^\infty \frac{1}{4\pi a^2 t}\exp\!\left(-\frac{r
^2+\rho^2-2r\rho\cos(\theta-\phi)}{4a^2 t}\right)\frac{Q}{2\pi r'\,c\rho}\,\delt
a(\rho-r')\;\rho\,d\rho\,d\phi.$$

The delta function selects $\rho = r'$ (contributing a factor $r'$ from $\rho\,d
\rho$), yielding:

$$T(r,t) = \frac{Q}{2\pi\,c\rho}\cdot\frac{1}{4\pi a^2 t}\int_0^{2\pi}\exp\!\lef
t(-\frac{r^2 + r'^2 - 2rr'\cos\phi}{4a^2 t}\right)d\phi,$$

where we used periodicity to replace $\theta-\phi$ by $\phi$.

## Evaluating the Angular Integral

Factor out the $\phi$-independent part:

$$\int_0^{2\pi}\exp\!\left(-\frac{r^2+r'^2}{4a^2 t}\right)\exp\!\left(\frac{rr'\
cos\phi}{2a^2 t}\right)d\phi = e^{-(r^2+r'^2)/(4a^2 t)}\int_0^{2\pi} e^{z\cos\ph
i}\,d\phi,$$

with $z = \frac{rr'}{2a^2 t}$. The standard integral representation of the modif
ied Bessel function gives:

$$\int_0^{2\pi} e^{z\cos\phi}\,d\phi = 2\pi\, I_0(z),$$

where $I_0$ is the modified Bessel function of the first kind of order zero. The
refore:

$$T(r,t) = \frac{Q}{2\pi\,c\rho}\cdot\frac{1}{4\pi a^2 t}\cdot 2\pi\,\exp\!\left
(-\frac{r^2+r'^2}{4a^2 t}\right) I_0\!\left(\frac{rr'}{2a^2 t}\right).$$

## Result

$$\boxed{T(r,t) = \frac{Q}{4\pi\, a^2 t\, c\rho}\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{r^2 + r'^2}
{4a^2 t}\right)\,I_0\!\left(\frac{r\,r'}{2a^2 t}\right)}$$

where:
- $a^2$ is the thermal diffusivity,
- $c\rho$ is the volumetric heat capacity,
- $I_0$ is the modified Bessel function of the first kind of order zero,
- $r'$ is the radius of the cylindrical source,
- $Q$ is the heat liberated per unit length.

## Verification

**Conservation of heat.** Using the identity $\int_0^\infty e^{-\alpha r^2} I_0(
\beta r)\,r\,dr = \frac{1}{2\alpha}\,e^{\beta^2/(4\alpha)}$ with $\alpha = \frac
{1}{4a^2 t}$, $\beta = \frac{r'}{2a^2 t}$:

$$\int_0^\infty T(r,t)\,c\rho\cdot 2\pi r\,dr = Q\cdot\frac{1}{4\pi a^2 t}\cdot
2\pi\cdot e^{-(r'^2)/(4a^2 t)}\cdot 2a^2 t\cdot e^{r'^2/(4a^2 t)} = Q.\quad\chec
kmark$$

**Limiting case $r'\to 0$ (line source).** Since $I_0(0)=1$ and $e^{-r'^2/(4a^2
t)}\to 1$:

$$T(r,t)\to \frac{Q}{4\pi a^2 t\, c\rho}\,e^{-r^2/(4a^2 t)},$$

which is the well-known source function of an instantaneous line source in 2D. $
\checkmark$

**Satisfaction of the heat equation.** The solution is a superposition (convolut
ion) of the 2D heat kernel with the initial data, so it satisfies $\partial_t T
= a^2\nabla^2 T$ for $t>0$ by linearity. $\checkmark$

**Initial condition.** As $t\to 0^+$, the kernel $\frac{1}{4\pi a^2 t}e^{-|\math
bf{r}-\mathbf{r}_0|^2/(4a^2 t)}\to \delta(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_0)$, so $T(r,t)\
to \frac{Q}{2\pi r'\,c\rho}\delta(r-r')$. $\checkmark$
