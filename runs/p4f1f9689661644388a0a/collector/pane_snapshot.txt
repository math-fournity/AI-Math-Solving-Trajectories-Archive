**Theorem.** For every $\mathbb{C}$-linear map $f: M_n(\mathbb{C}) \to M_n(\math
bb{C})$, there exist matrices $A_1, \dots, A_d, B_1, \dots, B_d \in M_n(\mathbb{
C})$ such that
$$f(X) = \sum_{j=1}^d A_j X B_j \quad \text{for all } X \in M_n(\mathbb{C}).$$

**Proof.**

Let $\{E_{ij}\}_{1 \le i,j \le n}$ be the standard basis of $M_n(\mathbb{C})$, w
here $E_{ij}$ has entry $1$ in position $(i,j)$ and $0$ elsewhere. Recall the mu
ltiplication rule $E_{ij} E_{kl} = \delta_{jk} E_{il}$.

For each pair of indices $(i,j)$ and $(k,l)$, define the linear map
$$T_{ij,kl}: M_n(\mathbb{C}) \to M_n(\mathbb{C}), \qquad T_{ij,kl}(X) = E_{ij}\,
 X\, E_{kl}.$$

Each $T_{ij,kl}$ is of the desired form (a single term $AXB$). We claim that the
 collection $\{T_{ij,kl}\}$, indexed by all $1 \le i,j,k,l \le n$, forms a basis
 of $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(M_n(\mathbb{C}))$.

**Counting.** The space $M_n(\mathbb{C})$ has dimension $n^2$ over $\mathbb{C}$,
 so $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(M_n(\mathbb{C}))$ has dimension $(n^2)^2 =
n^4$. The collection $\{T_{ij,kl}\}$ has exactly $n^4$ elements.

**Linear independence.** Suppose $\sum_{i,j,k,l} c_{ij,kl}\, T_{ij,kl} = 0$ for
some scalars $c_{ij,kl} \in \mathbb{C}$. Evaluate on a basis element $E_{ab}$:
$$0 = \sum_{i,j,k,l} c_{ij,kl}\, E_{ij}\, E_{ab}\, E_{kl} = \sum_{i,j,k,l} c_{ij
,kl}\, \delta_{ja}\, \delta_{bk}\, E_{il} = \sum_{i,l} c_{i,a,b,l}\, E_{il}.$$
Since $\{E_{il}\}$ is linearly independent, we conclude $c_{i,a,b,l} = 0$ for al
l $i, l$. Since $a, b$ were arbitrary, every coefficient $c_{ij,kl} = 0$. Hence
the collection is linearly independent.

**Conclusion.** The $n^4$ linearly independent maps $\{T_{ij,kl}\}$ form a basis
 of the $n^4$-dimensional space $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(M_n(\mathbb{C})
)$. Therefore any $\mathbb{C}$-linear map $f: M_n(\mathbb{C}) \to M_n(\mathbb{C}
)$ is a linear combination of the $T_{ij,kl}$:
$$f = \sum_{i,j,k,l} \alpha_{ij,kl}\, T_{ij,kl}.$$

nd $B_1, \dots, B_d$ (each an $E_{ij}$ or $E_{kl}$ scaled by its coefficient) su
