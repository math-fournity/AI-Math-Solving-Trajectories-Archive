**Claim:** The Lebesgue measure of $\left\{\frac{x}{y} : x, y \in I_\alpha^*\rig
ht\}$ is $+\infty$.

## Proof

Let $R = \left\{\frac{x}{y} : x,\, y \in I_\alpha^*\right\}$.

**Step 1: $I_\alpha$ is a perfect set containing $0$, and $0$ is a limit point.*
*

An $\alpha$-Cantor set is constructed by starting with $[0,1]$ and iteratively r
emoving open middle intervals from each remaining closed interval, removing a to
tal measure of $1-\alpha$. The point $0$ is the left endpoint of the leftmost cl
osed interval at every stage, so it is never removed; hence $0 \in I_\alpha$. Si
nce $I_\alpha$ is a Cantor-type set, it is perfect (closed with no isolated poin
ts). Therefore $0$ is a limit point of $I_\alpha$, meaning $I_\alpha^* = I_\alph
a \setminus \{0\}$ contains a sequence $\{y_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ with $y_n \in I_\a
lpha^*$ and $y_n \to 0^+$.

**Step 2: For each fixed $y \in I_\alpha^*$, the slice $S_y = \{x/y : x \in I_\a
lpha^*\}$ has measure $\alpha/y$.**

Since $I_\alpha^* = I_\alpha \setminus \{0\}$ and removing a single point does n
ot change Lebesgue measure, $m(I_\alpha^*) = m(I_\alpha) = \alpha$. The set $S_y
 = \frac{1}{y} \cdot I_\alpha^*$ is the image of $I_\alpha^*$ under the linear s
caling map $x \mapsto x/y$, which scales Lebesgue measure by the factor $1/y$. T
herefore:
$$m(S_y) = \frac{1}{y}\, m(I_\alpha^*) = \frac{\alpha}{y}.$$

**Step 3: $R$ contains every slice $S_y$, so its measure is at least $\alpha/y$
for every $y \in I_\alpha^*$.**

By definition:
$$R = \bigcup_{y \,\in\, I_\alpha^*} S_y \supseteq S_y \quad \text{for every } y
 \in I_\alpha^*.$$
Hence $m(R) \geq m(S_y) = \alpha/y$ for every $y \in I_\alpha^*$.

**Step 4: The measure is infinite.**

Applying the above to the sequence $y_n \to 0^+$ from Step 1:
$$m(R) \geq m(S_{y_n}) = \frac{\alpha}{y_n} \xrightarrow{n \to \infty} +\infty,$
$
since $\alpha > 0$ and $y_n \to 0^+$. Therefore:
$$m(R) = +\infty. \qquad \blacksquare$$
