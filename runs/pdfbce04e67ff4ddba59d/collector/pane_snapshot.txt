## Solution

**Problem:** Find the number of distinct Hamiltonian cycles in $K_9$ such that n
o two cycles share an edge (i.e., the maximum number of edge-disjoint Hamiltonia
n cycles in $K_9$).

### Counting edges

The complete graph $K_9$ has
$$\binom{9}{2} = 36 \text{ edges.}$$

Each Hamiltonian cycle on 9 vertices uses exactly 9 edges (one cycle through all
 9 vertices). Therefore, if we have $k$ edge-disjoint Hamiltonian cycles, they c
onsume $9k$ edges, giving the upper bound:
$$9k \le 36 \implies k \le 4.$$

### Achieving the bound — Walecki's Theorem

A classical result due to Walecki states:

> **Theorem (Walecki).** For odd $n$, the complete graph $K_n$ admits a **Hamilt
onian decomposition** — its edge set can be partitioned into $\frac{n-1}{2}$ edg
e-disjoint Hamiltonian cycles. For even $n$, $K_n$ can be decomposed into $\frac
{n-2}{2}$ Hamiltonian cycles plus one perfect matching.

Since $n = 9$ is odd, Walecki's theorem guarantees that $K_9$ decomposes into
$$\frac{9-1}{2} = 4$$
edge-disjoint Hamiltonian cycles, using all $4 \times 9 = 36$ edges.

### Explicit construction (verification)

Label the vertices $\{0, 1, 2, \ldots, 8\}$. The standard Walecki construction g
ives the following 4 Hamiltonian cycles (each is a cyclic ordering of all 9 vert
ices):

- **Cycle $C_0$:** $0\text{-}1\text{-}8\text{-}2\text{-}7\text{-}3\text{-}6\text
{-}4\text{-}5\text{-}0$
- **Cycle $C_1$:** $0\text{-}2\text{-}1\text{-}3\text{-}8\text{-}4\text{-}6\text
{-}5\text{-}7\text{-}0$
- **Cycle $C_2$:** $0\text{-}3\text{-}2\text{-}4\text{-}1\text{-}5\text{-}8\text
{-}6\text{-}7\text{-}0$
- **Cycle $C_3$:** $0\text{-}4\text{-}3\text{-}5\text{-}2\text{-}6\text{-}1\text
{-}7\text{-}8\text{-}0$

One can verify that the 36 edges across these four cycles are all distinct, part
itioning the edge set of $K_9$.

### Conclusion

The maximum number of edge-disjoint Hamiltonian cycles in $K_9$ is:

$$\boxed{4}$$
