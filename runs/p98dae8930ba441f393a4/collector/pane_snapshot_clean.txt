## Proof

Let $K_\mathfrak{p}$ denote the completion of $K$ at the finite prime $\mathfrak
{p}$, with $\mathfrak{p} \mid p$. Then $K_\mathfrak{p}$ is a finite extension of
 $\mathbb{Q}_p$; write $e$ for the ramification index, $f$ for the residue degre
e, and $q = p^f$ for the cardinality of the residue field $\mathbb{F}_q = \mathc
al{O}_\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{p}$. Let $d = _\mathfrak{p} : \mathbb{Q}_p] = ef
\geq 1$.

### Step 1. Structure of the local unit group

The unit group of the ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ decomposes as

$$\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}^{\times} \cong \mu_{q-1} \times (1 + \mathfrak{p}),$$

where $\mu_{q-1}$ is the group of $(q-1)$-th roots of unity (the Teichmüller lif
t of $\mathbb{F}_q^{\times}$) and $1 + \mathfrak{p}$ is the group of principal u
nits. This is the standard decomposition coming from Hensel's lemma: every unit
has a unique Teichmüller representative times a principal unit.

### Step 2. The $p$-completion of $\mu_{q-1}$ is trivial

Since $q = p^f$, we have $\gcd(q-1, p) = 1$. Thus $\mu_{q-1}$ is a finite group
of order $q - 1$, which is coprime to $p$. The $p$-completion (pro-$p$ completio
n) of a finite group of order coprime to $p$ is trivial, because there are no no
rmal subgroups of $p$-power index. Equivalently, $\varprojlim \mu_{q-1}/\mu_{q-1
}^{p^n} = \{1\}$ since the $p$-th power map is an automorphism of $\mu_{q-1}$.

### Step 3. $1 + \mathfrak{p}$ is already a pro-$p$ group

The principal unit group admits the filtration

$$1 + \mathfrak{p} \supset 1 + \mathfrak{p}^2 \supset 1 + \mathfrak{p}^3 \supset
 \cdots$$

with $1 + \mathfrak{p} = \varprojlim_n (1+\mathfrak{p})/(1+\mathfrak{p}^n)$. Eac
h successive quotient satisfies

$$(1 + \mathfrak{p}^n)/(1 + \mathfrak{p}^{n+1}) \cong \mathbb{F}_q^+ \cong (\mat
hbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^f,$$

so the order of $(1+\mathfrak{p})/(1+\mathfrak{p}^n)$ is $q^{n-1} = p^{f(n-1)}$,
 a power of $p$. Hence each finite quotient is a $p$-group, and $1 + \mathfrak{p
}$ is a pro-$p$ group. A pro-$p$ group is equal to its own pro-$p$ completion, s
o the $p$-completion of $1 + \mathfrak{p}$ is $1 + \mathfrak{p}$ itself.

### Step 4. $1 + \mathfrak{p}$ is infinite

The $p$-adic logarithm $\log : 1 + \mathfrak{p}^n \to \mathfrak{p}^n$ is an isom
orphism of $\mathbb{Z}_p$-modules for all $n > e/(p-1)$ (for $p$ odd; for $p = 2
$ one takes $n$ sufficiently large as well). Since $\mathfrak{p}^n \cong \mathca
l{O}_\mathfrak{p} \cong \mathbb{Z}_p^d$ as a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module, the subgroup
 $1 + \mathfrak{p}^n$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_p^d$ with $d = _\mathfrak{p
}:\mathbb{Q}_p] \geq 1$.

The quotient $(1+\mathfrak{p})/(1+\mathfrak{p}^n)$ is finite, so $1 + \mathfrak{
p}$ is a finite extension (as a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module) of $\mathbb{Z}_p^d$. In p
articular, $1 + \mathfrak{p}$ is a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module of $
\mathbb{Z}_p$-rank $d \geq 1$, hence is uncountably infinite. (Concretely, $1 +
\mathfrak{p}$ contains the uncountable subgroup $1 + \mathfrak{p}^n \cong \mathb
b{Z}_p^d$.)

### Step 5. Conclusion

The $p$-completion of $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}^{\times}$ is

$$\widehat{(\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}^{\times})}_p \;\cong\; \widehat{(\mu_{q-1})
}_p \times \widehat{(1+\mathfrak{p})}_p \;=\; \{1\} \times (1+\mathfrak{p}) \;\c
ong\; 1 + \mathfrak{p},$$

which is infinite (indeed uncountable, being a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module of rank $
_\mathfrak{p}:\mathbb{Q}_p] \geq 1$).

**The $p$-completion of the unit group $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}^{\times}$ is in
finite.** $\blacksquare$
