# USAMO 1998, Problem 1 — Proof

The set $\{1,2,\dots,1998\}$ contains exactly $999$ odd integers and $999$ even
integers.

Let $k$ denote the number of pairs $\{a_i,b_i\}$ with $|a_i-b_i|=1$, so that $99
9-k$ pairs have $|a_i-b_i|=6$.

**Key observation on parity.** A pair with difference $1$ consists of two consec
utive integers, hence one odd and one even. A pair with difference $6$ consists
of two integers of the *same* parity (since $6$ is even).

The $k$ pairs of difference $1$ therefore consume $k$ odd and $k$ even integers.
 The remaining $999-k$ odd integers and $999-k$ even integers must be covered en
tirely by the difference-$6$ pairs, each of which uses two integers of the same
parity. Consequently both $999-k$ (the number of leftover odd integers) and $999
-k$ (the number of leftover even integers) must be even:

$$999 - k \equiv 0 \pmod{2}.$$

Since $999$ is odd, this forces

$$k \equiv 1 \pmod{2},$$

i.e. **$k$ is odd**.

Now compute the sum modulo $2$. Each term $|a_i-b_i|$ is either $1$ (odd) or $6$
 (even), so

$$S = \sum_{i=1}^{999}|a_i-b_i| \equiv k \pmod{2}.$$

Since $k$ is odd, $S$ is odd. $\blacksquare$
