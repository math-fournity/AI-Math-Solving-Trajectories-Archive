## Proof

**Claim.** Yes. Any two splitting fields $E, E' \subseteq \overline{F}$ for $f \
in F[x]$ are equal as sets.

**Setup.** Fix an algebraic closure $\overline{F}$ of $F$. Let $f(x) \in F[x]$.
In $\overline{F}[x]$, the polynomial $f$ factors completely as

$$
f(x) = c\,(x - \alpha_1)(x - \alpha_2)\cdots(x - \alpha_n),
$$

where $c \in F^\times$ and $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n \in \overline{F}$ are the
roots of $f$ in $\overline{F}$ (listed with multiplicity). The set of roots

$$
R := \{\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n\} \subseteq \overline{F}
$$

is **uniquely determined** by $f$ and $\overline{F}$: it is simply the set of al
l $\alpha \in \overline{F}$ satisfying $f(\alpha) = 0$. There is no choice invol
ved.

**Definition of splitting field inside $\overline{F}$.** A subfield $E \subseteq
 \overline{F}$ is a splitting field of $f$ over $F$ (within $\overline{F}$) if:

1. $F \subseteq E$,
2. $f$ splits completely in $E[x]$, i.e., all roots $R \subseteq E$,
3. $E$ is generated over $F$ by the roots: $E = F(R)$.

Condition (3) says $E$ is the **smallest** subfield of $\overline{F}$ containing
 $F$ and all of $R$. Equivalently,

$$
E = \bigcap \{ K \subseteq \overline{F} : K \text{ is a subfield}, \, F \cup R \
subseteq K \}.
$$

This is an intersection of subfields of the fixed ambient field $\overline{F}$,
hence a subfield of $\overline{F}$, and it is uniquely determined by $F$ and $R$
 alone — with no further choices.

**Conclusion.** Let $E, E' \subseteq \overline{F}$ both be splitting fields of $
f$ over $F$. By condition (2), both contain $R$. By condition (3),

$$
E = F(R) = E'.
$$

Both are exactly the smallest subfield of $\overline{F}$ containing $F$ and $R$,
 so they are the same subset of $\overline{F}$.

**Remark on the distinction between "equal as sets" and "isomorphic".** The gene
ral uniqueness theorem for splitting fields states that any two splitting fields
 of $f$ (not necessarily inside a common algebraic closure) are $F$-isomorphic.
That theorem requires a choice of embedding and is proved via extension of embed
dings. However, once we fix a single ambient algebraic closure $\overline{F}$ an
d require both splitting fields to be subfields of it, the roots are pinned down
 as specific elements of $\overline{F}$, and the splitting field becomes the fie
ld $F(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)$ generated by those specific elements. There is n
o ambiguity left, so the two splitting fields coincide as sets — a strictly stro
nger conclusion than mere isomorphism.
