## Proof

**Claim:** $\int_Q G\,dx = 0$.

**Setup.** Let $Q = [-\tfrac12, \tfrac12]^N$ be the unit cube centered at the or
igin. "$Q$-periodic" means each $g_i$ satisfies $g_i(x + e_j) = g_i(x)$ for ever
y $j = 1, \dots, N$ and every $x \in \mathbb{R}^N$, where $e_j$ is the $j$-th st
andard basis vector (the cube has side length $1$, so the period lattice is $\ma
thbb{Z}^N$). Each $g_i \in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^N)$, hence is locally Lipschi
tz.

**Step 1: Reduce by Fubini.** Since $G = \sum_{i=1}^N \partial_{x_i} g_i$ and ea
ch $\partial_{x_i} g_i \in L^\infty(Q) \subset L^1(Q)$, by linearity and Fubini'
s theorem,
$$
\int_Q G\,dx = \sum_{i=1}^N \int_Q \partial_{x_i} g_i\,dx = \sum_{i=1}^N \int_{Q
_{-i}} \left(\int_{-1/2}^{1/2} \partial_{x_i} g_i(x_{-i}, t)\,dt\right) dx_{-i},
$$
where $Q_{-i} = [-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]^{N-1}$ is the projection of $Q$ onto the co
ordinates other than $x_i$.

**Step 2: Apply the fundamental theorem of calculus on lines.** A standard chara
cterization of $W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^N)$ (the ACL property, valid for all $W^
{1,p}$, $p \ge 1$) states: for a.e. fixed $x_{-i} \in \mathbb{R}^{N-1}$, the map
 $t \mapsto g_i(x_{-i}, t)$ is absolutely continuous (in fact Lipschitz) on ever
y compact interval, and
$$
\int_{-1/2}^{1/2} \partial_{x_i} g_i(x_{-i}, t)\,dt = g_i(x_{-i}, \tfrac12) - g_
i(x_{-i}, -\tfrac12)
$$
for a.e. $x_{-i}$.

**Step 3: Boundary cancellation via periodicity.** By $Q$-periodicity (period $1
$ in the $x_i$-direction),
$$
g_i(x_{-i}, \tfrac12) = g_i(x_{-i}, -\tfrac12 + 1) = g_i(x_{-i}, -\tfrac12)
$$
for every $x_{-i}$. Therefore, for a.e. $x_{-i}$,
$$
\int_{-1/2}^{1/2} \partial_{x_i} g_i(x_{-i}, t)\,dt = g_i(x_{-i}, \tfrac12) - g_
i(x_{-i}, -\tfrac12) = 0.
$$

**Step 4: Conclude.** Substituting back,
$$
\int_Q \partial_{x_i} g_i\,dx = \int_{Q_{-i}} 0\,dx_{-i} = 0 \quad \text{for eac
h } i = 1, \dots, N,
$$
and hence
$$
\int_Q G\,dx = \sum_{i=1}^N 0 = 0.
$$

**Remark on regularity.** The argument requires only that the fundamental theore
m of calculus holds along coordinate lines and that the boundary values are well
-defined and match by periodicity. Both are guaranteed by $g_i \in W^{1,\infty}(
\mathbb{R}^N)$: such functions are locally Lipschitz, hence absolutely continuou
s on lines (ACL), so the one-dimensional FTC applies for a.e. transverse paramet
er, and the pointwise periodicity identity $g_i(x+e_j)=g_i(x)$ holds everywhere
(as an element of $W^{1,\infty}$, $g_i$ has a Lipschitz representative, and peri
odicity is preserved in this representative).

**Conclusion.** The statement is **true**: for every $Q$-periodic vector field $
g \in (W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^N))^N$,
$$
\int_Q \operatorname{div} g\,dx = 0.
$$
