**Yes, it is possible.** Below is the construction and proof.

## Construction

We use the **Smith–Volterra–Cantor (SVC) set**, a "fat Cantor set" of positive m
easure, and take its complement.

**Inductive construction of the SVC set $C$.** Set $C_0 = [0,1]$. For $n = 1, 2,
 3, \dots$, obtain $C_n$ from $C_{n-1}$ by deleting from each connected componen
t of $C_{n-1}$ its **middle open interval of length $1/4^n$**. Then $C_n$ is a u
nion of $2^n$ closed intervals, and

$$C = \bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty} C_n$$

is the Smith–Volterra–Cantor set.

**Total length removed.** At step $n$ we delete $2^{n-1}$ open intervals, each o
f length $1/4^n$, so the total length removed is

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2^{n-1}}{4^n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^{n+1}
} = \frac{1/4}{1 - 1/2} = \frac{1}{2}.$$

Hence the Lebesgue measure of $C$ is

$$m(C) = 1 - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2}.$$

## Verification of the required properties

Let $U = [0,1] \setminus C$.

1. **$U$ is open.** Each $C_n$ is closed (a finite union of closed intervals), s
o $C = \bigcap C_n$ is closed. Therefore $U = [0,1]\setminus C$ is open in $[0,1
]$.

2. **$U$ is dense in $[0,1]$.** The set $C$ is a Cantor-type set: at every stage
 every remaining interval is split, so $C$ contains no non-degenerate interval,
i.e. $\operatorname{int}(C) = \varnothing$. A closed set with empty interior is
**nowhere dense**, and the complement of a nowhere dense closed set is dense. He
nce $U$ is dense in $[0,1]$.

3. **$0 < m(U) < 1$.** Since $U$ is the complement of $C$ in $[0,1]$,

$$m(U) = m([0,1]) - m(C) = 1 - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2},$$

which satisfies $0 < \tfrac{1}{2} < 1$.

## Conclusion

The set $U = [0,1] \setminus C$, where $C$ is the Smith–Volterra–Cantor set, is
a **dense open subset of $[0,1]$ with measure $\tfrac{1}{2}$**, strictly between
 $0$ and $1$. Therefore such a set **can** be constructed.

(More generally, by removing intervals of total length $\alpha$ for any prescrib
ed $\alpha \in (0,1)$, one obtains a dense open subset of $[0,1]$ with measure e
xactly $1 - \alpha \in (0,1)$.)
