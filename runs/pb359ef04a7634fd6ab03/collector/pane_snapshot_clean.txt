## Proof

Let $X \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(p, \delta)$ with shape $p > 0$ and rate $\delta > 0$.
 Its density is
$$
f(x) = \frac{\delta^{p}}{\Gamma(p)}\, x^{p-1} e^{-\delta x}, \qquad x > 0.
$$
The CDF is $B(x) = \frac{\gamma(p,\delta x)}{\Gamma(p)}$ (lower incomplete gamma
 ratio), so the tail (survival function) is
$$
\bar B(x) = 1 - B(x) = \frac{\Gamma(p,\delta x)}{\Gamma(p)},
\qquad
\Gamma(p,z) := \int_{z}^{\infty} t^{p-1} e^{-t}\,dt.
$$
We seek the asymptotic form of $\bar B(x)$ as $x \to \infty$, equivalently $z =
\delta x \to \infty$.

### Derivation by repeated integration by parts

Set $u = t^{p-1}$, $dv = e^{-t}dt$, so $du = (p-1)t^{p-2}dt$, $v = -e^{-t}$. The
n
$$
\Gamma(p,z) = \Big[-t^{p-1}e^{-t}\Big]_{z}^{\infty} + (p-1)\int_{z}^{\infty} t^{
p-2} e^{-t}\,dt
            = z^{p-1} e^{-z} + (p-1)\,\Gamma(p-1,z).
$$
Iterating this recurrence $N$ times (for any fixed $N \ge 1$),
$$
\Gamma(p,z) = e^{-z}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \frac{(p-1)(p-2)\cdots(p-k)}{z^{k}}\, z^{p-
1}
            + (p-1)(p-2)\cdots(p-N)\,\Gamma(p-N,z),
$$
i.e.
$$
\Gamma(p,z) = z^{p-1} e^{-z}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k}(p-j)}{z^{k}}
            + \prod_{j=1}^{N}(p-j)\,\Gamma(p-N,z).
$$

### Estimating the remainder

For $z \to \infty$ and fixed $N$, the remainder term is bounded by
$$
\big|\Gamma(p-N,z)\big| \le \int_{z}^{\infty} t^{\,\mathrm{Re}(p)-N-1} e^{-t}\,d
t
   \le z^{\,\mathrm{Re}(p)-N-1} e^{-z}\,(1 + o(1)),
$$
since $e^{-t} \le e^{-z} e^{-(t-z)}$ and $\int_{z}^{\infty} e^{-(t-z)}dt = 1$, w
ith the polynomial factor $t^{\mathrm{Re}(p)-N-1} \le C z^{\mathrm{Re}(p)-N-1}$
up to lower order. Hence the remainder is $O\!\big(z^{p-1-N} e^{-z}\big)$, which
 is $o\!\big(z^{p-1} e^{-z} z^{-(N-1)}\big)$. Therefore, for every fixed $N$,
$$
\Gamma(p,z) \sim z^{p-1} e^{-z}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(p-1)(p-2)\cdots(p-k)}{
z^{k}},
\qquad z \to \infty,
$$
as an **asymptotic series** (the series is generally divergent, but truncating a
t any fixed $N$ gives an approximation with error $O(z^{p-1-N}e^{-z})$). Writing
 the first few terms explicitly:
$$
\Gamma(p,z) \sim z^{p-1} e^{-z}\left[1 + \frac{p-1}{z} + \frac{(p-1)(p-2)}{z^{2}
} + \frac{(p-1)(p-2)(p-3)}{z^{3}} + \cdots\right].
$$

### Tail of the Gamma distribution

Substituting $z = \delta x$ and dividing by $\Gamma(p)$:
$$
\boxed{\;
\bar B(x) = 1 - B(x) \;\sim\; \frac{(\delta x)^{p-1}\, e^{-\delta x}}{\Gamma(p)}
\left[1 + \frac{p-1}{\delta x} + \frac{(p-1)(p-2)}{(\delta x)^{2}} + \frac{(p-1)
(p-2)(p-3)}{(\delta x)^{3}} + \cdots\right],
\quad x \to \infty.
\;}
$$

The **leading-order** asymptotic form is
$$
\bar B(x) \sim \frac{\delta^{p}\, x^{p-1}\, e^{-\delta x}}{\delta\,\Gamma(p)} =
\frac{(\delta x)^{p-1} e^{-\delta x}}{\Gamma(p)}, \qquad x \to \infty,
$$
which is precisely the density $f(x)$ divided by the rate $\delta$ — a direct co
nsequence of the integration-by-parts identity $\bar B(x) = \frac{f(x)}{\delta}
+ \frac{p-1}{\delta}\bar B_{p-1}(x)$, reflecting the well-known fact that for a
Gamma variable the tail is asymptotically $\frac{1}{\delta} f(x)$.
