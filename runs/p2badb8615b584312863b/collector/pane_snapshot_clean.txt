## Decomposition of the Diffusion-Transport-Reaction Operator

**Operator.** Consider the one-dimensional diffusion-transport-reaction operator

$$Lu = -\mu\, u'' + b\, u' + \sigma\, u,$$

defined on a suitable domain (e.g. $H^2_0(\Omega)$ or with periodic boundary con
ditions on $\Omega=(0,1)$), so that all boundary terms arising from integration
by parts vanish. We assume $\mu>0$, and $b,\sigma\in\mathbb{R}$.

---

### Step 1. Compute the adjoint $L^*$

For smooth $u, v$ vanishing (or periodic) at the boundary, compute $\langle Lu,
v\rangle = \int_\Omega (Lu)\, v\, dx$ term by term.

**Diffusion term.** Integrating by parts twice (boundary terms vanish):
$$\int_\Omega (-\mu u'')\, v\, dx = -\mu\big[u'v\big]_{\partial\Omega} + \mu\int
_\Omega u'\, v'\, dx = \mu\big[uv'\big]_{\partial\Omega} - \mu\int_\Omega u\, v'
'\, dx = \int_\Omega u\,(-\mu v'')\, dx.$$

**Transport term.** Integrating by parts once:
$$\int_\Omega (b\, u')\, v\, dx = b\big[uv\big]_{\partial\Omega} - b\int_\Omega
u\, v'\, dx = \int_\Omega u\,(-b\, v')\, dx.$$

**Reaction term.** Multiplication is self-adjoint:
$$\int_\Omega (\sigma u)\, v\, dx = \int_\Omega u\,(\sigma v)\, dx.$$

Collecting,
$$\langle Lu, v\rangle = \int_\Omega u\,\big(-\mu v'' - b\, v' + \sigma v\big)\,
 dx = \langle u, L^* v\rangle,$$

so the **adjoint operator** is
$$\boxed{L^* v = -\mu\, v'' - b\, v' + \sigma\, v.}$$

Compared with $L$, the sign of the first-order (transport) coefficient is flippe
d, while the second-order (diffusion) and zero-order (reaction) terms are unchan
ged — exactly as expected: even-order derivatives are formally self-adjoint, odd
-order derivatives are formally skew-adjoint.

---

### Step 2. Symmetric and skew-symmetric parts

Every bounded (or closed densely-defined) operator decomposes uniquely as
$$L = L_s + L_a, \qquad L_s := \tfrac{1}{2}(L + L^*), \qquad L_a := \tfrac{1}{2}
(L - L^*),$$
with $L_s^* = L_s$ (symmetric / self-adjoint) and $L_a^* = -L_a$ (skew-symmetric
 / skew-adjoint).

**Symmetric part.**
$$L_s u = \tfrac{1}{2}\big[( -\mu u'' + b u' + \sigma u) + (-\mu u'' - b u' + \s
igma u)\big] = -\mu\, u'' + \sigma\, u.$$

**Skew-symmetric part.**
$$L_a u = \tfrac{1}{2}\big[( -\mu u'' + b u' + \sigma u) - (-\mu u'' - b u' + \s
igma u)\big] = b\, u'.$$

---

### Step 3. Verification

- **$L_s$ is symmetric.** $L_s = -\mu\, \tfrac{d^2}{dx^2} + \sigma$ is a sum of
two self-adjoint operators (the second-derivative with Dirichlet/periodic condit
ions is self-adjoint; multiplication by a real constant $\sigma$ is self-adjoint
). Hence $L_s^* = L_s$. ✓

- **$L_a$ is skew-symmetric.** $L_a = b\, \tfrac{d}{dx}$, and from Step 1, $(b\,
 \tfrac{d}{dx})^* = -b\, \tfrac{d}{dx} = -L_a$. Hence $L_a^* = -L_a$. ✓

- **Reconstruction.** $L_s u + L_a u = (-\mu u'' + \sigma u) + (b u') = -\mu u''
 + b u' + \sigma u = Lu$. ✓

---

### Result

The one-dimensional diffusion-transport-reaction operator

$$Lu = -\mu\, u'' + b\, u' + \sigma\, u$$

decomposes into

$$\boxed{L = L_s + L_a,}$$

where

$$\boxed{L_s\, u = -\mu\, u'' + \sigma\, u \quad\text{(symmetric / self-adjoint
part)}}$$

combines the **diffusion** ($-\mu\, u''$) and **reaction** ($\sigma\, u$) terms,
 and

$$\boxed{L_a\, u = b\, u' \quad\text{(skew-symmetric / skew-adjoint part)}}$$

is the **transport** (advection) term. The split reflects the parity of the deri
vatives: even-order terms ($u''$, $u$) are self-adjoint and go to $L_s$; the odd
-order term ($u'$) is skew-adjoint and goes to $L_a$.
