# Solution

## Problem Restatement

Given 10 points in space with no four coplanar, find the maximum number of lines
 (edges) that can be drawn between pairs of points such that no triangle or para
llelogram is formed.

## Key Observation: The Parallelogram Condition is Vacuous

**Claim:** Under the hypothesis that no four points are coplanar, no parallelogr
am can ever be formed, regardless of which lines are drawn.

**Proof of Claim:** A parallelogram with vertices $A, B, C, D$ requires $\vec{AB
} = \vec{DC}$ (equivalently, the diagonals bisect each other: $A + C = B + D$).
This is a planar figure — all four vertices lie in a single plane. Since no four
 of the given 10 points are coplanar, it is impossible for any four points to fo
rm a parallelogram. The same applies to parallel lines: if lines $AB$ and $CD$ w
ere parallel, they would be coplanar, forcing $A, B, C, D$ into one plane — agai
n impossible.

Therefore, the parallelogram constraint is automatically satisfied, and the prob
lem reduces to:

> What is the maximum number of edges in a **triangle-free** graph on 10 vertice
s?

## Reduction to Turán's Theorem

We model the configuration as a graph $G$ on 10 vertices (the points), where edg
es represent drawn lines. The condition "no triangle is formed" means $G$ contai
ns no $K_3$ (triangle-free).

By **Turán's theorem**, the maximum number of edges in a $K_{r+1}$-free graph on
 $n$ vertices is achieved by the **Turán graph** $T(n, r)$ — the complete $r$-pa
rtite graph with part sizes as equal as possible.

For triangle-free graphs, $r = 2$ (we forbid $K_3$). The Turán graph $T(10, 2)$
is the complete bipartite graph $K_{5,5}$ with parts of size 5 and 5.

The number of edges is:
$$e(T(10,2)) = 5 \times 5 = 25$$

Equivalently, by Mantel's theorem (the special case of Turán's theorem for trian
gle-free graphs):
$$\max e(G) = \left\lfloor \frac{n^2}{4} \right\rfloor = \left\lfloor \frac{100}
{4} \right\rfloor = 25$$

## Construction Achieving 25

Partition the 10 points into two groups of 5: $\{A_1, \ldots, A_5\}$ and $\{B_1,
 \ldots, B_5\}$. Draw all 25 lines $A_iB_j$ ($1 \le i \le 5$, $1 \le j \le 5$) a
nd no lines within either group.

- **Triangle-free:** Any triangle requires three mutually adjacent vertices. But
 edges only cross between the two groups, so no two vertices in the same group a
re adjacent. A triangle would need at least two vertices in the same group to be
 adjacent — impossible. Hence the graph is triangle-free.
- **No parallelogram:** Guaranteed by the no-four-coplanar condition (proven abo
ve).

Such a point configuration exists: 10 points in general position in $\mathbb{R}^
3$ (no four coplanar) is easily achieved, e.g., points on the moment curve $(t,
t^2, t^3)$ for distinct values of $t$.

## Upper Bound: 25 is Optimal

By Turán's theorem, any triangle-free graph on 10 vertices has at most 25 edges.
 Any configuration of lines avoiding triangles corresponds to such a graph, so 2
5 is an upper bound. Combined with the construction above, the maximum is exactl
y 25.

## Answer

$$\boxed{25}$$
