## Solution

We evaluate the contour integral $\displaystyle \int_{\partial B(0,\pi/4)} \frac
{1}{z\tan z}\,dz$ using the residue theorem.

**Step 1: Identify the integrand and its singularities.**

Since $\tan z = \frac{\sin z}{\cos z}$, we rewrite

$$\frac{1}{z\tan z} = \frac{\cos z}{z\sin z}.$$

Singularities occur where $z\sin z = 0$, i.e., at $z = 0$ and at $z = n\pi$ ($n
\in \mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$).

**Step 2: Determine which singularities lie inside $|z| = \pi/4$.**

The nonzero zeros of $\sin z$ are at $z = \pm\pi, \pm 2\pi, \ldots$, all of whic
h satisfy $|z| \geq \pi > \pi/4$. Hence the **only** singularity inside the cont
our $|z|=\pi/4$ is at $z = 0$.

**Step 3: Classify the singularity at $z=0$ and compute the residue.**

Near $z=0$, using the Taylor expansions $\sin z = z - \frac{z^3}{6} + \cdots$ an
d $\cos z = 1 - \frac{z^2}{2} + \cdots$:

$$z\sin z = z^2 - \frac{z^4}{6} + \cdots = z^2\!\left(1 - \frac{z^2}{6}+\cdots\r
ight),$$

so

$$\frac{\cos z}{z\sin z} = \frac{1}{z^2}\cdot\frac{1 - z^2/2 + \cdots}{1 - z^2/6
 + \cdots} = \frac{1}{z^2}\left(1 - \frac{z^2}{2}+\cdots\right)\!\left(1+\frac{z
^2}{6}+\cdots\right) = \frac{1}{z^2} - \frac{1}{3} + \cdots$$

This is a **pole of order 2** at $z=0$. The residue is the coefficient of $z^{-1
}$, which is **0**.

Equivalently, using the residue formula for a pole of order 2:

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{\cos z}{z\sin z} = \lim_{z\to 0}\frac{d}{dz}\!\l
eft[z^2\cdot\frac{\cos z}{z\sin z}\right] = \lim_{z\to 0}\frac{d}{dz}\bigl[z\cot
 z\bigr].$$

The well-known expansion $z\cot z = 1 - \frac{z^2}{3} - \cdots$ gives $\frac{d}{
dz}[z\cot z] = -\frac{2z}{3} - \cdots$, which vanishes at $z=0$. Thus

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{\cos z}{z\sin z} = 0.$$

**Step 4: Apply the residue theorem.**

By the residue theorem,

$$\int_{\partial B(0,\pi/4)} \frac{1}{z\tan z}\,dz = 2\pi i \sum_{\text{inside}}
 \operatorname{Res} = 2\pi i \cdot 0 = 0.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \int_{B(0,\pi/4)} \frac{1}{z\tan z}\,dz = 0}$$
