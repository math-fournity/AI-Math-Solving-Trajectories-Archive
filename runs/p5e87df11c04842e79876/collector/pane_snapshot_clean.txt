Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether, for any Lebesgue measurabl
e set $E$ with $0 < m(E) < \infty$, any complex $L^\infty$ function $f$, and any
 $0 < t < 1$, there exists a measurable subset $A \subseteq E$ with $m(A) = t \c
dot m(E)$ and the average of $f$ over $A$ equal to the average over $E$.

**Answer: Yes, such a subset $A$ always exists.**

**Proof.**

**Setup.** Let $L = m(E) > 0$. Work on the measure space $(E, \mathcal{L}|_E, m|
_E)$, where $m|_E$ is Lebesgue measure restricted to $E$. This measure is finite
 (since $m(E) < \infty$) and non-atomic (since Lebesgue measure is non-atomic).

Since $f \in L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ and $m(E) < \infty$, the restriction $f|_E$
 lies in $L^1(E, m)$. Write $f = u + iv$ where $u = \operatorname{Re} f$ and $v
= \operatorname{Im} f$ are real-valued $L^\infty$ (hence $L^1$ on $E$) functions
.

**Defining the vector measure.** Define three finite signed measures on $(E, \ma
thcal{L}|_E)$:
$$\mu_0(B) = m(B), \quad \mu_1(B) = \int_B u\, dm, \quad \mu_2(B) = \int_B v\, d
m$$
for measurable $B \subseteq E$.

**Each $\mu_i$ is non-atomic.** For $\mu_0 = m|_E$, this is immediate since Lebe
sgue measure is non-atomic. For $\mu_1$ (and similarly $\mu_2$): suppose $|\mu_1
|(A) = \int_A |u|\, dm > 0$. Then the set $A^+ = \{x \in A : u(x) \neq 0\}$ has
positive $m$-measure. Since $m$ is non-atomic, there exists $B \subseteq A^+$ wi
th $0 < m(B) < m(A^+)$. Since $|u| > 0$ on $B$ (with $m(B) > 0$), we get $|\mu_1
|(B) > 0$; and since $|u| > 0$ on $A^+ \setminus B$ (with $m(A^+ \setminus B) >
0$), we get $|\mu_1|(B) < |\mu_1|(A^+) \leq |\mu_1|(A)$. Hence $\mu_1$ is non-at
omic. The same argument applies to $\mu_2$.

**Applying Lyapunov's Theorem.** *Lyapunov's theorem* states: if $\mu_0, \mu_1,
\ldots, \mu_k$ are finite non-atomic signed measures on a measurable space, then
 the range
$$\mathcal{R} = \{(\mu_0(B), \mu_1(B), \ldots, \mu_k(B)) : B \text{ measurable}\
}$$
is a **compact convex** subset of $\mathbb{R}^{k+1}$.

Applying this to $(\mu_0, \mu_1, \mu_2)$ on $(E, \mathcal{L}|_E)$, the range
$$\mathcal{R} = \{(m(B),\, \textstyle\int_B u\, dm,\, \int_B v\, dm) : B \subset
eq E \text{ measurable}\} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^3$$
is compact and convex.

**Convexity argument.** Taking $B = \emptyset$ gives $(0, 0, 0) \in \mathcal{R}$
. Taking $B = E$ gives
$$\left(m(E),\, \textstyle\int_E u\, dm,\, \int_E v\, dm\right) \in \mathcal{R}.
$$

Since $\mathcal{R}$ is convex, for any $t \in [0,1]$, the convex combination
$$t \cdot \left(m(E),\, \textstyle\int_E u\, dm,\, \int_E v\, dm\right) + (1-t)
\cdot (0,0,0) = \left(t\, m(E),\; t \textstyle\int_E u\, dm,\; t \int_E v\, dm\r
ight)$$
belongs to $\mathcal{R}$.

Therefore, there exists a measurable set $A \subseteq E$ such that:
$$m(A) = t\, m(E), \quad \int_A u\, dm = t \int_E u\, dm, \quad \int_A v\, dm =
t \int_E v\, dm.$$

Combining the last two equalities: $\displaystyle\int_A f\, dm = t \int_E f\, dm
$.

**Verifying the average condition.** Since $t > 0$ and $m(E) > 0$, we have $m(A)
 = t\, m(E) > 0$, so:
$$\frac{1}{m(A)} \int_A f\, dm = \frac{t \int_E f\, dm}{t\, m(E)} = \frac{1}{m(E
)} \int_E f\, dm.$$

Thus $A \subseteq E$ is measurable with $m(A) = t \cdot m(E)$ and $\frac{1}{m(A)
}\int_A f\, dm = \frac{1}{m(E)}\int_E f\, dm$, as required. $\blacksquare$
