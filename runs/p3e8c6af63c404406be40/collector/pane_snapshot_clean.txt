### Defining the function and its Dirichlet series

We have $\delta(1)=1$ and for $n>1$, $\delta(n)=\sum_{d\mid n,\, d<n}\delta(d)$.
 For $n>1$, the full divisor sum satisfies $\sum_{d\mid n}\delta(d) = \delta(n)+
\delta(n)=2\delta(n)$, while $\sum_{d\mid 1}\delta(d)=1$.

Let $f(s)=\sum_{n\ge 1}\delta(n)n^{-s}$. Then $f(s)\zeta(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}\bigl(\s
um_{d\mid n}\delta(d)\bigr)n^{-s}=1+2\sum_{n\ge2}\delta(n)n^{-s}=2f(s)-1$, givin
g
$$f(s)=\frac{1}{2-\zeta(s)}.$$

### Two-variable generating function for $\delta(3^i 5^j)$

Set $b_{i,j}=\delta(3^i 5^j)$ and $G(x,y)=\sum_{i,j\ge0}b_{i,j}x^i y^j$. The par
tial sums $S(i,j)=\sum_{i'\le i,\,j'\le j}b_{i',j'}$ satisfy $S(i,j)=2b_{i,j}$ f
or $(i,j)\ne(0,0)$ and $S(0,0)=1$. Since $\sum S(i,j)x^i y^j = G(x,y)/((1-x)(1-y
))$, we get
$$\frac{G(x,y)}{(1-x)(1-y)}=2G(x,y)-1 \implies G(x,y)=\frac{(1-x)(1-y)}{1-2x-2y+
2xy}.$$

### Extracting the diagonal

We need $\mathcal{S}=\sum_{k\ge0}b_{k,k}/15^k = D(1/15)$ where $D(t)=\sum_{k\ge0
}b_{k,k}t^k$ is the diagonal of $G$. Substituting $y=t/x$:
$$\frac{G(x,t/x)}{x}=\frac{-(1-x)(x-t)}{x\bigl(2x^2-(1+2t)x+2t\bigr)}.$$
The roots of $2x^2-(1+2t)x+2t=0$ are $r_1,r_2$ with $r_1+r_2=\tfrac{1+2t}{2}$, $
r_1r_2=t$. As $t\to0$, $r_1\to0$, $r_2\to\tfrac12$. Computing residues at $x=0$
and $x=r_1$:

- $\operatorname{Res}_{x=0}=\tfrac{1}{2}$ (since near $x=0$, integrand $\sim t/(
2t\,x)=1/(2x)$).
- $\operatorname{Res}_{x=r_1}=-\frac{(1-r_1)(1-r_2)}{2(r_1-r_2)}$. Using $(1-r_1
)(1-r_2)=1-(r_1+r_2)+r_1r_2=1-\tfrac{1+2t}{2}+t=\tfrac12$, and $r_1-r_2=-\tfrac1
2\sqrt{1-12t+4t^2}$:

$$D(t)=\frac12+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{1-12t+4t^2}}.$$

**Verification:** $D(0)=1$, $D'(0)=3$, $D''(0)\cdot\tfrac12=26$, matching $\delt
a(1)=1,\ \delta(15)=3,\ \delta(225)=26$. ✓

### Evaluating at $t=1/15$

$$1-12\cdot\tfrac{1}{15}+4\cdot\tfrac{1}{225}=1-\tfrac45+\tfrac{4}{225}=\tfrac{4
5+4}{225}=\tfrac{49}{225},\qquad \sqrt{\tfrac{49}{225}}=\tfrac{7}{15}.$$

$$\mathcal{S}=D\!\left(\tfrac{1}{15}\right)=\frac12+\frac{1}{2\cdot 7/15}=\frac1
2+\frac{15}{14}=\frac{7+15}{14}=\frac{11}{7}.$$

Since $\gcd(11,7)=1$, we have $p=11$, $q=7$.

$$1000p+q = 11000+7 = \boxed{11007}.$$
