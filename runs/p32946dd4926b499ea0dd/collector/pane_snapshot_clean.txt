## Solution

We use the **Cauchy estimate** for derivatives of an analytic function.

**Cauchy integral formula for derivatives.** If $g$ is analytic on and inside th
e circle $|z|=R$, then

$$g^{(n)}(0) = \frac{n!}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=R} \frac{g(z)}{z^{n+1}}\,dz.$$

**Cauchy's estimate.** Taking absolute values and using $|g(z)|\leq M$ on $|z|=R
$:

$$|g^{(n)}(0)| \le \frac{n!}{2\pi}\cdot 2\pi R \cdot \frac{M}{R^{n+1}} = \frac{n
!\,M}{R^{n}}.$$

**Applying to our problem.** We have:
- $g$ analytic on $\bar D$, so on $|z|=1$ (with $R=1$),
- $M = \sup_{|z|\le 1}|g(z)| \le e^2$,
- $n=3$ (third derivative), $R=1$.

Therefore:

$$|g'''(0)| \le \frac{3!\,e^{2}}{1^{3}} = 6e^{2}.$$

**Sharpness check.** The bound is attained (up to the constant) by $g(z)=e^{2}z^
{3}$... but wait, that has $|g(z)|\le e^2$ on $|z|=1$ and $g'''(0)=6e^{2}$, so t
he bound is in fact **sharp**.

$$\boxed{6e^{2}}$$
