## Proof

Let $A = k[x,y]$, $I = (x^2, xy, y^2)$, and $B = A/I$. We compute $\operatorname
{Der}_k(B, B)$.

**Step 1: Structure of $B$.**

The ideal $I$ contains all monomials of degree $\geq 2$, so $B$ has $k$-basis $\
{1, \bar{x}, \bar{y}\}$ where $\bar{x}, \bar{y}$ denote the images of $x, y$. Th
e maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m} = (\bar{x}, \bar{y}) \subset B$ satisfies $\mathfr
ak{m}^2 = 0$, and $B/\mathfrak{m} \cong k$.

**Step 2: Lifting derivations.**

A $k$-derivation $D: B \to B$ corresponds to a $k$-derivation $\delta: A \to B$
(composing with the projection $A \to B$) that vanishes on $I$, i.e. $\delta(I)
= 0$. Since $A = k[x,y]$ is a polynomial algebra, $\delta$ is freely determined
by $\delta(x) = a$ and $\delta(y) = b$ for arbitrary $a, b \in B$. The constrain
t is:

$$\delta(x^2) = 0, \quad \delta(xy) = 0, \quad \delta(y^2) = 0 \quad \text{in }
B.$$

By the Leibniz rule (computing in $B$):

- $\delta(x^2) = 2\bar{x}\,a = 0$,
- $\delta(xy) = \bar{y}\,a + \bar{x}\,b = 0$,
- $\delta(y^2) = 2\bar{y}\,b = 0$.

**Step 3: Solving the constraints.**

Write $a = \alpha_0 + \alpha_1\bar{x} + \alpha_2\bar{y}$ and $b = \beta_0 + \bet
a_1\bar{x} + \beta_2\bar{y}$ with $\alpha_i, \beta_i \in k$. Using $\bar{x}^2 =
\bar{x}\bar{y} = \bar{y}^2 = 0$:

- $2\bar{x}\,a = 2\alpha_0\,\bar{x} = 0 \implies 2\alpha_0 = 0$,
- $\bar{y}\,a + \bar{x}\,b = \alpha_0\,\bar{y} + \beta_0\,\bar{x} = 0 \implies \
alpha_0 = 0$ and $\beta_0 = 0$,
- $2\bar{y}\,b = 2\beta_0\,\bar{y} = 0 \implies 2\beta_0 = 0$.

The middle equation forces $\alpha_0 = \beta_0 = 0$ (in any characteristic, sinc
e $\bar{x}, \bar{y}$ are $k$-linearly independent), which automatically satisfie
s the first and third equations. Thus:

$$a = \alpha_1\bar{x} + \alpha_2\bar{y}, \qquad b = \beta_1\bar{x} + \beta_2\bar
{y},$$

with $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \beta_1, \beta_2 \in k$ free.

**Step 4: The $B$-module structure.**

The four basis derivations are:

| $D$ | $D(\bar{x})$ | $D(\bar{y})$ |
|-----|---------------|---------------|
| $D_1$ | $\bar{x}$ | $0$ |
| $D_2$ | $\bar{y}$ | $0$ |
| $D_3$ | $0$ | $\bar{x}$ |
| $D_4$ | $0$ | $\bar{y}$ |

For any $c \in \mathfrak{m}$ and any $D_i$, we have $(c \cdot D_i)(\bar{x}) = c
\cdot D_i(\bar{x}) \in \mathfrak{m} \cdot \mathfrak{m} = \mathfrak{m}^2 = 0$, an
d similarly $(c \cdot D_i)(\bar{y}) = 0$. Hence $\mathfrak{m}$ annihilates $\ope
ratorname{Der}_k(B,B)$, so the $B$-module structure factors through $B/\mathfrak
{m} \cong k$.

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Der}_k(B, B) \cong k^4}$$

as a $k$-vector space (equivalently, as a $B$-module with $\mathfrak{m}$ acting
trivially), freely generated by the four derivations $D_1, D_2, D_3, D_4$ above.
 Equivalently, $\operatorname{Der}_k(B,B) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_k(\mathfrak{m
}, \mathfrak{m}) \cong \mathfrak{m}^{\oplus 2}$, since a derivation is determine
d by its (arbitrary) values on the two generators $\bar{x}, \bar{y}$ of $\mathfr
ak{m}$, both of which must land in $\mathfrak{m}$.
