# Solution

**Problem.** Determine the values of the parameter $\alpha$ for which the series
 $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl(n^{\,n^\alpha}-1\bigr)$ converges.

---

## Step 1. Necessary condition: $a_n\to 0$

The general term is
$$a_n = n^{n^\alpha}-1 = e^{\,n^\alpha\ln n}-1.$$

For the series to have any chance of converging we need $a_n\to 0$, i.e. $e^{\,n
^\alpha\ln n}\to 1$, i.e.
$$n^\alpha\ln n\to 0.\tag{$\ast$}$$

- **$\alpha>0$:** $n^\alpha\to\infty$ and $\ln n\to\infty$, so $n^\alpha\ln n\to
+\infty$; $a_n\to+\infty$. **Diverges.**
- **$\alpha=0$:** $n^\alpha=1$, so $a_n=n-1\to+\infty$. **Diverges.**
- **$\alpha<0$:** write $\alpha=-\beta$, $\beta>0$. Then $n^\alpha\ln n=\dfrac{\
ln n}{n^\beta}\to 0$ for every $\beta>0$. Condition $(\ast)$ holds.

So convergence is possible only for $\alpha<0$.

---

## Step 2. Asymptotics for $\alpha<0$

Set $x_n:=n^\alpha\ln n$. For $\alpha<0$ we have $x_n\to 0$, and using $e^x-1\si
m x$ as $x\to 0$:
$$a_n = e^{x_n}-1 \sim x_n = n^\alpha\ln n \qquad (n\to\infty).$$

Since $\dfrac{a_n}{n^\alpha\ln n}\to 1>0$, the **limit comparison test** gives:
$$\sum a_n \text{ converges} \iff \sum n^\alpha\ln n \text{ converges}.$$

---

## Step 3. Convergence of $\sum n^\alpha\ln n$

- **$\alpha<-1$:** Choose $\varepsilon>0$ with $\alpha+\varepsilon<-1$. Since $\
ln n=o(n^\varepsilon)$, we get $n^\alpha\ln n = o\!\left(n^{\alpha+\varepsilon}\
right)$. The series $\sum n^{\alpha+\varepsilon}$ converges (exponent $<-1$), he
nce $\sum n^\alpha\ln n$ **converges** by comparison.

- **$\alpha=-1$:** $\displaystyle\sum\frac{\ln n}{n}$ **diverges** by the integr
al test:
  $$\int_2^{\infty}\frac{\ln x}{x}\,dx = \frac{(\ln x)^2}{2}\Big|_2^{\infty}=+\i
nfty.$$

- **$-1<\alpha<0$:** For $n\ge 3$, $\ln n\ge 1$, so $n^\alpha\ln n\ge n^\alpha>0
$, and $\sum n^\alpha$ diverges (exponent $>-1$). Hence $\sum n^\alpha\ln n$ **d
iverges**.

---

## Conclusion

Combining Steps 1–3:

$$\boxed{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl(n^{\,n^\alpha}-1\bigr)\ \text{converges}\iff \
alpha<-1.}$$

The series converges exactly for $\alpha\in(-\infty,\,-1)$.
