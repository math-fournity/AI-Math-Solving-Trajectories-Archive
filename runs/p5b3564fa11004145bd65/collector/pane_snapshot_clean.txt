## Disproof

The statement is **false** in general. We exhibit an explicit counterexample.

### Counterexample

Let $k$ be a field and set $R = k[[t^2, t^3]] \subset k[[t]]$, the ring of forma
l power series in $t^2$ and $t^3$. This is the completion of the local ring of t
he cusp $y^2 = x^3$ at the origin.

**$R$ is a 1-dimensional noetherian local domain.** It is a complete local domai
n (a subring of the domain $k[[t]]$), hence noetherian by the Cohen structure th
eorem / since it is a finitely generated module-analog; more directly, $R = k \o
plus t^2 k[[t]]$ is a complete local noetherian domain of Krull dimension 1 (its
 only nonzero prime is $\mathfrak m = (t^2, t^3)$, since $R/(t^2,t^3) \cong k$ a
nd $R$ is integral over... ). Concretely, $\mathfrak m = (t^2, t^3)$ is the uniq
ue maximal ideal, and $\dim R = 1$ because $R \subset k[[t]]$ is integral (every
 element of $k[[t]]$ is integral over $R$: $t$ satisfies $X^2 - t^2 = 0$ with $t
^2 \in R$, and $k[[t]] = R[t]$) and $k[[t]]$ is 1-dimensional.

**Integral closure.** $R$ is a complete local noetherian domain, hence excellent
, hence Japanese (N-2). Its integral closure in $\operatorname{Frac}(R) = k((t))
$ is
$$S = k[[t]],$$
the DVR. Indeed $k[[t]]$ is integrally closed and integral over $R$ (as noted, $
t$ is integral over $R$, and $k[[t]] = R[t]$), and any element of $k((t))$ integ
ral over $R \subset k[[t]]$ is integral over $k[[t]]$, hence lies in $k[[t]]$.

**Computing $J(S)$.** $S = k[[t]]$ is a DVR, hence local with unique maximal ide
al $(t)$. Therefore
$$J(S) = (t).$$

**Computing $\mathfrak m S$.** We have $\mathfrak m = (t^2, t^3)$, so
$$\mathfrak m S = (t^2, t^3)\, k[[t]] = t^2(1, t)\, k[[t]] = t^2 \cdot k[[t]] =
(t^2),$$
since $(1, t) = k[[t]]$ (the element $1$ is a unit).

**Comparison.**
$$\mathfrak m S = (t^2) \subsetneq (t) = J(S),$$
because $t \in (t) \setminus (t^2)$ (a uniformizer is not twice-divisible in a D
VR).

### The inclusion that always holds

For completeness, note that the inclusion $\mathfrak m S \subseteq J(S)$ is alwa
ys true: $R \hookrightarrow S$ is integral, so by lying-over every maximal ideal
 $\mathfrak M$ of $S$ contracts to a maximal ideal of $R$, which must be $\mathf
rak m$ (as $R$ is local). Hence $\mathfrak m \subseteq \mathfrak M \cap R$, givi
ng $\mathfrak m S \subseteq \mathfrak M$ for every maximal $\mathfrak M$, and th
us $\mathfrak m S \subseteq \bigcap_{\mathfrak M} \mathfrak M = J(S)$.

The counterexample above shows the reverse inclusion $J(S) \subseteq \mathfrak m
 S$ fails: $t \in J(S) = (t)$ but $t \notin (t^2) = \mathfrak m S$.

### Conclusion

The equality $\mathfrak m S = J(S)$ does **not** hold in general. The cusp $R =
k[[t^2, t^3]]$ with $S = k[[t]]$ provides a counterexample in which $\mathfrak m
 S = (t^2) \subsetneq (t) = J(S)$.
