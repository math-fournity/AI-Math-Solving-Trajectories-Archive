# Solution

**Claim:** The number of countable partitions of $\mathbb{N}$ into countable set
s is $2^{\aleph_0}$ (the cardinality of the continuum).

## Setup and Interpretation

A *countable partition of $\mathbb{N}$ into countable sets* is a partition $\mat
hcal{P} = \{A_i\}_{i \in I}$ where $I$ is countable and each $A_i$ is a countabl
e (i.e., countably infinite) subset of $\mathbb{N}$, with $\bigsqcup_{i \in I} A
_i = \mathbb{N}$.

We establish the cardinality by proving matching upper and lower bounds of $2^{\
aleph_0}$.

## Upper Bound: At most $2^{\aleph_0}$

Every partition of $\mathbb{N}$ determines (and is determined by) an equivalence
 relation $\sim$ on $\mathbb{N}$, where $a \sim b$ iff $a$ and $b$ lie in the sa
me part. An equivalence relation is a subset of $\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}$.
Since $|\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}| = \aleph_0$, the power set $\mathcal{P}(\m
athbb{N} \times \mathbb{N})$ has cardinality $2^{\aleph_0}$. Therefore the set o
f *all* partitions of $\mathbb{N}$ has cardinality at most $2^{\aleph_0}$, and t
he set of countable partitions into countable sets — being a subset — also has c
ardinality at most $2^{\aleph_0}$.

## Lower Bound: At least $2^{\aleph_0}$

We construct an injection from $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})$ (which has cardinality
$2^{\aleph_0}$) into the set of countable partitions of $\mathbb{N}$ into counta
ble sets.

**Identification.** Since $\mathbb{N}$, $\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}$, and $\ma
thbb{N} \times \mathbb{N} \times \{0,1\}$ are all countably infinite, fix a bije
ction $\varphi: \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N} \times \{0,1\}$. It
suffices to construct the partitions on $\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N} \times \{0
,1\}$ and transport them to $\mathbb{N}$ via $\varphi$.

**Construction.** For each subset $S \subseteq \mathbb{N}$, define a partition $
\mathcal{P}_S$ of $\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N} \times \{0,1\}$ as follows. For
each $n \in \mathbb{N}$:

- If $n \in S$: the part is $A_n = \{(n, m, 0) : m \in \mathbb{N}\} \cup \{(n, m
, 1) : m \in \mathbb{N}\}$ (merging the two "columns" over $n$).
- If $n \notin S$: the two parts are $B_n^0 = \{(n, m, 0) : m \in \mathbb{N}\}$
and $B_n^1 = \{(n, m, 1) : m \in \mathbb{N}\}$ (keeping the columns separate).

The partition $\mathcal{P}_S$ is the collection of all such $A_n$ (for $n \in S$
) and $B_n^0, B_n^1$ (for $n \notin S$).

**Each part is countably infinite.** Every part is either $\{(n,m,j): m \in \mat
hbb{N}\}$ for fixed $n, j$ (a copy of $\mathbb{N}$) or the union of two such cop
ies, both countably infinite.

**The number of parts is countably infinite.** Each $n \in \mathbb{N}$ contribut
es either 1 or 2 parts, so the total number of parts is between $|\mathbb{N}|$ a
nd $2|\mathbb{N}| = |\mathbb{N}|$, hence countably infinite. (It is infinite bec
ause $\mathbb{N}$ is infinite and each $n$ contributes at least one part.)

**Injectivity.** Let $S \neq T$ be distinct subsets of $\mathbb{N}$. Pick $n \in
 S \bigtriangleup T$; without loss of generality, $n \in S \setminus T$. In $\ma
thcal{P}_S$, the elements $(n, 0, 0)$ and $(n, 0, 1)$ belong to the same part $A
_n$. In $\mathcal{P}_T$, they belong to different parts $B_n^0$ and $B_n^1$. Hen
ce $\mathcal{P}_S \neq \mathcal{P}_T$, proving injectivity.

This injection shows the set of such partitions has cardinality at least $|\math
cal{P}(\mathbb{N})| = 2^{\aleph_0}$.

## Conclusion

The upper and lower bounds match:

$$\left|\{\text{countable partitions of } \mathbb{N} \text{ into countable sets}
\}\right| = 2^{\aleph_0}.$$
