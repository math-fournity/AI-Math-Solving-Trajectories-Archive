## Proof

Let the 7 people be vertices of a simple graph (no loops, no multiple edges), wh
ere an edge represents a handshake. The degree of a vertex = number of people th
at person shook hands with.

**Setting up the degree sequence.** We are given:
- 1 person of degree 1,
- 2 people of degree 2,
- 3 people of degree 3.

This accounts for 1 + 2 + 3 = 6 people. Let the 7th person have degree $d$. Sinc
e there are only 6 other people, $0 \le d \le 6$.

**Parity constraint.** The sum of all degrees equals twice the number of edges (
handshake lemma):
$$1\cdot1 + 2\cdot2 + 3\cdot3 + d = 1 + 4 + 9 + d = 14 + d.$$
This must be even, so $d$ must be even. The candidates are $d \in \{0, 2, 4, 6\}
$, giving total handshakes
$$\frac{14+d}{2} = 7 + \frac{d}{2} \in \{7, 8, 9, 10\}.$$

It remains to show each candidate degree sequence is graphical (realizable as a
simple graph). We verify via the Havel–Hakimi algorithm.

**$d = 0$: sequence $(3,3,3,2,2,1,0)$.**
$$3,3,3,2,2,1,0 \to 2,2,1,2,1,0 \to 2,2,2,1,1,0 \to 1,1,1,1,0 \to 1,1,0,0 \to 0,
0,0.\ \checkmark$$

**$d = 2$: sequence $(3,3,3,2,2,2,1)$.**
$$3,3,3,2,2,2,1 \to 2,2,2,2,1,1 \to 1,1,2,1,1 \to 2,1,1,1,1 \to 0,1,1,1 \to 1,1,
1,0 \to 0,1,0 \to 0,0.\ \checkmark$$

**$d = 4$: sequence $(4,3,3,3,2,2,1)$.**
$$4,3,3,3,2,2,1 \to 2,2,2,2,2,1 \to 1,1,2,2,1 \to 2,1,1,1,1 \to 0,1,1,1 \to 1,1,
1,0 \to 0,1,0 \to 0,0.\ \checkmark$$

**$d = 6$: sequence $(6,3,3,3,2,2,1)$.** The degree-6 vertex connects to all oth
ers; removing it and subtracting 1 from each remaining degree gives $(2,2,2,1,1,
0)$:
$$2,2,2,1,1,0 \to 1,1,1,1,0 \to 0,1,1,0 \to 1,0,0 \to 0,0.\ \checkmark$$

All four sequences are graphical, so the total number of handshakes can be $7, 8
, 9,$ or $10$ — **four** different values.
