## Proof

We compute $\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^4 B_t^2\, dB_t\right]$.

**Key fact (Itô isometry / martingale property).** If the integrand $H_t$ is pro
gressively measurable and satisfies
$$
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^T H_t^2\, dt\right] < \infty,
$$
then the Itô integral $M_T := \int_0^T H_t\, dB_t$ is a martingale with $M_0 = 0
$. In particular,
$$
\mathbb{E}[M_T] = \mathbb{E}[M_0] = 0.
$$

**Verify the square-integrability condition.** Here $H_t = B_t^2$, which is clea
rly progressively measurable (it is a continuous adapted process). We compute
$$
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^4 B_t^2\, dt\right] = \int_0^4 \mathbb{E}[B_t^2]\, dt =
 \int_0^4 t\, dt = \frac{4^2}{2} = 8 < \infty.
$$

(For the sharper Itô isometry condition we also note $\mathbb{E}[B_t^4] = 3t^2$,
 so $\int_0^4 3t^2\,dt = 64 < \infty$, confirming $H \in L^2([0,4]\times\Omega)$
.)

**Conclusion.** Since $H_t = B_t^2$ satisfies the square-integrability hypothesi
s, the process
$$
M_t = \int_0^t B_s^2\, dB_s, \qquad 0 \le t \le 4,
$$
is a martingale with $M_0 = 0$. Therefore
$$
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^4 B_t^2\, dB_t\right] = \mathbb{E}[M_4] = \mathbb{E}[M_
0] = 0.
$$

$$
\boxed{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^4 B_t^2\, dB_t\right] = 0}
$$
