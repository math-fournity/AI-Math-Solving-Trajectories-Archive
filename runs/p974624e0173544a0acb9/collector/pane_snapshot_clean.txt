## Proof

Define
$$S(n) = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{2n+1}{2k+1}\, 2^{3k} = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{2n+1
}{2k+1}\, 8^{k}.$$

We prove $5 \nmid S(n)$ for every integer $n \ge 0$.

**Step 1. A closed form via conjugates.**

Set $\alpha = 1 + 2\sqrt{2}$ and $\beta = 1 - 2\sqrt{2}$. By the binomial theore
m,
$$(1 \pm 2\sqrt{2})^{2n+1} = \sum_{j=0}^{2n+1} \binom{2n+1}{j} (\pm 2\sqrt{2})^{
j}.$$
Subtracting the two expansions kills the even-$j$ terms and doubles the odd-$j$
terms. For $j = 2k+1$,
$$(2\sqrt{2})^{2k+1} = (2\sqrt{2})\cdot (2\sqrt{2})^{2k} = 2\sqrt{2}\cdot 8^{k}.
$$
Hence
$$\alpha^{2n+1} - \beta^{2n+1} = 2\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{2n+1}{2k+1}(2\sqrt{2})\,8
^{k} = 4\sqrt{2}\, S(n),$$
so
$$S(n) = \frac{\alpha^{2n+1} - \beta^{2n+1}}{4\sqrt{2}}. \tag{1}$$

Note $\alpha\beta = 1 - 8 = -7$ and $\alpha+\beta = 2$.

**Step 2. Reduction modulo 5.**

Since $2$ is a quadratic non-residue mod $5$ (the squares mod $5$ are $0,1,4$),
we adjoin a square root $\omega$ of $2$ and work in $\mathbb{F}_{25} = \mathbb{F
}_5(\omega)$, where $\omega^2 = 2$. The identity (1) descends to $\mathbb{F}_{25
}$:
$$S(n) \equiv \frac{\alpha^{2n+1} - \beta^{2n+1}}{4\omega} \pmod{5}, \qquad \alp
ha = 1+2\omega,\ \beta = 1-2\omega \in \mathbb{F}_{25}.$$
Since $4\omega \neq 0$, we have $S(n) \equiv 0 \pmod 5$ if and only if
$$\alpha^{2n+1} = \beta^{2n+1} \quad\text{in } \mathbb{F}_{25}. \tag{2}$$

**Step 3. Translating to a multiplicative order condition.**

In $\mathbb{F}_{25}$ we have $\alpha\beta = -7 \equiv 3 \pmod 5$, so $\beta \neq
 0$ and $\alpha/\beta$ is well-defined. Condition (2) is equivalent to
$$\gamma^{2n+1} = 1, \qquad \gamma := \alpha/\beta = \alpha^{2}/(\alpha\beta) =
\alpha^{2}/3.$$
Compute $\alpha^{2} = (1+2\omega)^2 = 1 + 4\omega + 4\omega^{2} = 1 + 4\omega +
8 \equiv 4 + 4\omega \pmod 5$. Since $3^{-1} \equiv 2 \pmod 5$,
$$\gamma = \frac{4+4\omega}{3} = (4+4\omega)\cdot 2 = 8+8\omega \equiv 3 + 3\ome
ga \pmod 5.$$

Thus $S(n) \equiv 0 \pmod 5$ iff $\gamma^{2n+1} = 1$ in $\mathbb{F}_{25}$, i.e.
iff $\operatorname{ord}(\gamma) \mid (2n+1)$.

**Step 4. The order of $\gamma$ is even.**

The group $\mathbb{F}_{25}^{\times}$ has order $24$, so $\operatorname{ord}(\gam
ma) \mid 24$. The odd divisors of $24$ are $1$ and $3$. We rule both out:

- $\gamma = 1$ would mean $3+3\omega = 1$, i.e. $3\omega = 3$, i.e. $\omega = 1$
, contradicting $\omega^2 = 2 \neq 1$. So $\operatorname{ord}(\gamma) \neq 1$.
- $\gamma^{3} = 1$? Compute directly:
$$\gamma^{2} = (3+3\omega)^{2} = 9 + 18\omega + 9\omega^{2} = 9 + 18\omega + 18
= 27 + 18\omega \equiv 2 + 3\omega \pmod 5,$$
$$\gamma^{3} = \gamma^{2}\cdot\gamma = (2+3\omega)(3+3\omega) = 6 + 6\omega + 9\
omega + 9\omega^{2} = 6 + 15\omega + 18 = 24 + 15\omega \equiv 4 \pmod 5.$$
So $\gamma^{3} = 4 \neq 1$, hence $\operatorname{ord}(\gamma) \neq 3$.

Having excluded the only two odd divisors of $24$, we conclude $\operatorname{or
d}(\gamma)$ is **even**.

**Step 5. Conclusion.**

Since $\operatorname{ord}(\gamma)$ is even, it cannot divide any odd integer $2n
+1$ (with $n \ge 0$). Therefore $\gamma^{2n+1} \neq 1$ for all $n \ge 0$, which
by Step 3 means $\alpha^{2n+1} \neq \beta^{2n+1}$, which by Step 2 means
$$S(n) \not\equiv 0 \pmod{5}.$$

Hence $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{2n+1}{2k+1}\,2^{3k}$ is not divisible b
y $5$ for any integer $n \ge 0$. $\blacksquare$
