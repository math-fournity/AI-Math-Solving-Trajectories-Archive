## Proof

Let $C$ be the curve
$$C = \{X^4+Y^4+Z^4=0\}\cap\{X^3+Y^3+(Z-tW)^3=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^3.$$

Denote the two defining surfaces by
- $S_1 = \{F_1=0\}$, $\deg F_1 = 4$,
- $S_2 = \{F_2=0\}$, $\deg F_2 = 3$.

For generic $t$, $S_1$ and $S_2$ are smooth and meet transversely, so $C = S_1\c
ap S_2$ is a **smooth complete intersection of type $(4,3)$** in $\mathbb{P}^3$.
 In particular $C$ is a Cartier divisor on the smooth surface $S_1$, cut out by
the restriction of $F_2$ (a section of $\mathcal{O}_{S_1}(3)$).

### Step 1. Canonical bundle of $S_1$

By the adjunction formula applied to the smooth degree-$4$ hypersurface $S_1\sub
set\mathbb{P}^3$,
$$K_{S_1} = \bigl(K_{\mathbb{P}^3}\otimes \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^3}(S_1)\bigr)\
big|_{S_1} = \bigl(\mathcal{O}(-4)\otimes\mathcal{O}(4)\bigr)\big|_{S_1} = \math
cal{O}_{S_1}(0).$$

(Equivalently, $K_{S_1}=\mathcal{O}_{S_1}(-4+4)=\mathcal{O}_{S_1}$.)

### Step 2. Canonical bundle of $C$

The curve $C$ is the divisor on $S_1$ associated to the section $F_2|_{S_1}\in H
^0(S_1,\mathcal{O}_{S_1}(3))$, so its associated line bundle on $S_1$ is
$$\mathcal{O}_{S_1}(C) = \mathcal{O}_{S_1}(3).$$

Applying adjunction again on $S_1$:
$$K_C = \bigl(K_{S_1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{S_1}(C)\bigr)\big|_C = \bigl(\mathcal{O
}_{S_1}(0)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{S_1}(3)\bigr)\big|_C = \mathcal{O}_C(3).$$

Equivalently, combining both adjunctions into the standard complete-intersection
 formula,
$$K_C = \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^3}(-4+4+3)\big|_C = \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^3}(3
)\big|_C.$$

### Step 3. Degree of $C$

A complete intersection of surfaces of degrees $a,b$ in $\mathbb{P}^3$ has degre
e $ab$ (Bézout). Hence
$$\deg C = 4\cdot 3 = 12.$$

### Step 4. Genus via Riemann–Hurwitz / degree of $K_C$

Since $K_C = \mathcal{O}_C(3)$,
$$\deg K_C = 3\cdot \deg C = 3\cdot 12 = 36.$$

By Riemann–Roch for curves, $\deg K_C = 2g-2$, so
$$2g - 2 = 36 \implies g = 19.$$

### General formula check

For a smooth complete intersection of type $(a,b)$ in $\mathbb{P}^3$,
$$g = \tfrac{1}{2}\,ab\,(a+b-4)+1.$$
With $(a,b)=(4,3)$:
$$g = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 4\cdot 3\cdot(4+3-4)+1 = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 12\cdot 3 + 1
 = 18+1 = 19.\quad\checkmark$$

---

**The genus of the curve is $\boxed{19}$.**
