## Solution

**Claim:** Every nontrivial solution of $y'' + \frac{m}{x}y = 0$ has infinitely
many positive zeros if and only if $m > 0$.

---

### Case 1: $m > 0$ — every nontrivial solution oscillates

We use the **Sturm Comparison Theorem**: if $u'' + f(x)\,u = 0$ and $v'' + g(x)\
,v = 0$ with $f(x) \geq g(x)$ on $[a,b]$, and $f \not\equiv g$ on any subinterva
l, then between two consecutive zeros of $v$ there is a zero of $u$.

**Comparison equation (Euler):** Fix any $c > \tfrac{1}{4}$ (say $c = 1$) and co
nsider
$$z'' + \frac{c}{x^2}\,z = 0, \qquad x > 0.$$
This is an Euler equation with indicial roots $r = \tfrac{1}{2} \pm i\omega$, $\
omega = \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{4c - 1}$. Its real-valued solutions are
$$z(x) = x^{1/2}\bigl(A\cos(\omega \ln x) + B\sin(\omega \ln x)\bigr).$$
The zeros satisfy $\omega \ln x = n\pi + \text{const}$, i.e. $x_n = e^{(n\pi + \
text{const})/\omega} \to \infty$. Hence **every nontrivial solution $z$ has infi
nitely many zeros tending to $+\infty$**.

**Applying Sturm comparison:** Set $f(x) = \frac{m}{x}$ (the coefficient of our
equation) and $g(x) = \frac{c}{x^2}$ (the Euler coefficient). For $x \geq \frac{
c}{m}$:
$$\frac{m}{x} \geq \frac{c}{x^2} \iff mx \geq c \iff x \geq \frac{c}{m}.$$
Moreover $f \not\equiv g$ on any interval (they agree only at the single point $
x = c/m$).

Take any nontrivial solution $z$ of the Euler equation. Its zeros $x_n \to \inft
y$, so for all sufficiently large $n$, both $x_n$ and $x_{n+1}$ lie in $[\tfrac{
c}{m}, \infty)$. By Sturm comparison, **every** nontrivial solution $y$ of $y''
+ \frac{m}{x}y = 0$ has at least one zero in $(x_n, x_{n+1}]$ for each such $n$.
 Since there are infinitely many consecutive pairs, $y$ has **infinitely many po
sitive zeros**. $\checkmark$

---

### Case 2: $m < 0$ — no solution has two positive zeros

Suppose a nontrivial solution $y$ has two positive zeros $a < b$. Multiply the e
quation $y'' + \frac{m}{x}y = 0$ by $y$ and integrate over $[a,b]$:

$$\int_a^b y\,y''\,dx + \int_a^b \frac{m}{x}\,y^2\,dx = 0.$$

Integrating by parts (with $y(a) = y(b) = 0$):

$$\bigl[y\,y'\bigr]_a^b - \int_a^b (y')^2\,dx + \int_a^b \frac{m}{x}\,y^2\,dx =
0.$$

The boundary term vanishes, giving:

$$\int_a^b (y')^2\,dx = \int_a^b \frac{m}{x}\,y^2\,dx.$$

The left-hand side is $\geq 0$. When $m < 0$, the right-hand side is $< 0$ (sinc
e $m/x < 0$ on $(a,b) \subset (0,\infty)$ and $y^2 \not\equiv 0$). **Contradicti
on.** Hence no nontrivial solution can have two positive zeros — at most one — s
o certainly not infinitely many.

---

### Case 3: $m = 0$ — at most one zero

The equation reduces to $y'' = 0$, with general solution $y = Ax + B$. A nontriv
ial solution has at most one real zero, hence not infinitely many.

---

### Conclusion

The condition is:

$$\boxed{m > 0}.$$

For $m > 0$, Sturm comparison with an oscillatory Euler equation ($c > 1/4$) for
ces every nontrivial solution to have infinitely many positive zeros. For $m \le
q 0$, an energy/integral argument (or direct solution) shows no nontrivial solut
ion can have more than one positive zero.
