## Proof

Let $p = a+b+c$, $q = ab+bc+ca$, $r = abc$. The constraint gives $q = p-1$, and
the inequality becomes:
$$(p-2)^2 \ge k \cdot r \cdot (p-3).$$

### Step 1: Reformulating the constraint

Observe that
$$abc - (a-1)(b-1)(c-1) = ab+bc+ca - a-b-c + 1 = q - p + 1 = 0,$$
so the constraint is equivalent to $abc = (a-1)(b-1)(c-1)$.

### Step 2: Identifying the binding region

When $r(p-3) \le 0$, the RHS is $\le 0$ (for $k > 0$) while LHS $= (p-2)^2 \ge 0
$, so the inequality holds automatically. The binding case is $r(p-3) > 0$.

### Step 3: Discriminant analysis

Since $a, b, c$ are real roots of $f(t) = t^3 - pt^2 + (p-1)t - r = 0$, the disc
riminant satisfies $\Delta \ge 0$. Computing with $A=-p, B=p-1, C=-r$:

$$\Delta = (p-1)^2(p-2)^2 - 2p(2p-3)(p-3)\,r - 27r^2 \ge 0.$$

For fixed $p$, this is a quadratic in $r$ with leading coefficient $-27 < 0$, so
 the feasible $r$ form an interval $[r_-(p), r_+(p)]$. The product of roots sati
sfies $r_- \cdot r_+ = -(p-1)^2(p-2)^2/27 \le 0$, so $r_- \le 0 \le r_+$ (with e
quality only when $p=1$ or $p=2$).

### Step 4: Reduction to $\Delta = 0$ (two variables equal)

For fixed $p \neq 3$, the ratio $g = (p-2)^2/(r(p-3)) = C/r$ where $C = (p-2)^2/
(p-3)$ is constant, so $g$ is **monotonic** in $r$:

- **$p > 3$:** $C > 0$, so $g$ decreases in $r$. With $r > 0$, the minimum is at
 $r = r_+(p)$.
- **$p < 3$, $p \neq 2$:** $C < 0$, so $g$ increases in $r$. With $r < 0$, the m
inimum is at $r = r_-(p)$.

In both cases, the minimum over feasible $r$ is at an endpoint where $\Delta = 0
$, i.e., **two of $a, b, c$ are equal**. (At $p=2$: the cubic $t(t-1)^2 = r$ req
uires $r \in [0, 4/27]$, so $r \ge 0$ and $r(p-3) \le 0$—automatic case.)

### Step 5: Computing the minimum on $b = c$

Set $b = c$. The constraint $ab^2 = (a-1)(b-1)^2$ gives $a = -(b-1)^2/(2b-1)$. T
hen:

$$p = \frac{3b^2-1}{2b-1}, \quad r = \frac{-b^2(b-1)^2}{2b-1}, \quad p-2 = \frac
{(3b-1)(b-1)}{2b-1}, \quad p-3 = \frac{3b^2-6b+2}{2b-1}.$$

The ratio becomes:
$$g(b) = \frac{(p-2)^2}{r(p-3)} = \frac{(3b-1)^2}{b^2(6b - 3b^2 - 2)}.$$

The condition $r(p-3) > 0$ requires $3b^2 - 6b + 2 < 0$, i.e., $b \in \left(1 -
\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}},\; 1 + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)$, excluding $b = 0, \tfrac
{1}{2}, 1$.

### Step 6: Minimizing $g(b)$

Setting $g'(b) = 0$ (equivalently $u'v = uv'$ with $u = (3b-1)^2$, $v = b^2(6b-3
b^2-2)$) yields:
$$9b^3 - 15b^2 + 9b - 2 = 0 \implies (3b-2)(3b^2 - 3b + 1) = 0.$$

Since $3b^2 - 3b + 1$ has discriminant $9 - 12 = -3 < 0$, the **unique critical
point** is $b = 2/3$.

**Evaluating:** At $b = 2/3$:
- $a = -(1/9)/(1/3) = -1/3$, giving $(a,b,c) = (-1/3, 2/3, 2/3)$.
- $g(2/3) = \dfrac{(2-1)^2}{(4/9)(6 \cdot 2/3 - 3 \cdot 4/9 - 2)} = \dfrac{1}{(4
/9)(4 - 4/3 - 2)} = \dfrac{1}{(4/9)(2/3)} = \dfrac{27}{8}.$

**Boundary behavior:** $g \to +\infty$ as $b \to 1 \pm 1/\sqrt{3}$, and $g(1) =
g(1/2^+) = 4 > 27/8$. So the global minimum is $\boxed{27/8}$.

### Step 7: Verification of equality

At $(a,b,c) = (-1/3, 2/3, 2/3)$:
- **Constraint:** $ab+bc+ca = -2/9 + 4/9 - 2/9 = 0$, and $a+b+c = 1$, so $q + 1
= 1 = p$. ✓
- **LHS:** $(q-1)^2 = (0-1)^2 = 1$.
- **RHS:** $\frac{27}{8} \cdot abc \cdot (p-3) = \frac{27}{8} \cdot (-4/27) \cdo
t (-2) = \frac{27}{8} \cdot \frac{8}{27} = 1$.

Equality holds. ✓

### Conclusion

The best constant is $k = \dfrac{27}{8}$.

- **Sharpness:** Equality at $(a,b,c) = (-1/3, 2/3, 2/3)$ and permutations.
- **Validity:** For $r(p-3) \le 0$ the inequality is automatic. For $r(p-3) > 0$
, the ratio $(p-2)^2/(r(p-3))$ is minimized (over feasible $(p,r)$) on the discr
iminant-zero boundary where two variables are equal, and there its minimum is $2
