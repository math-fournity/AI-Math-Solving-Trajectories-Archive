# Solution

**Answer: No, not in general.** A non-constant smooth map from a Hilbert manifol
d $M$ into a Hilbert space $H$ that sends every geodesic to a straight line exis
ts if and only if $M$ admits a non-zero parallel 1-form (equivalently, the Levi-
Civita connection has non-transitive holonomy, i.e., $M$ has a flat factor in it
s de Rham decomposition). In particular, for such a map to be an immersion, $M$
must be flat. For a generic Hilbert manifold with non-vanishing curvature and ir
reducible holonomy, no such non-constant map exists.

## Proof

Let $(M, g)$ be a Hilbert manifold with its Levi-Civita connection $\nabla$, and
 let $f: M \to H$ be a smooth map into a Hilbert space $H$ (equipped with its ca
nonical flat affine connection). We say $f$ **takes geodesics to straight lines*
* if for every geodesic $\gamma: I \to M$ (i.e., $\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma
= 0$), the curve $f \circ \gamma: I \to H$ is a straight line, meaning $\frac{d^
2}{dt^2}(f \circ \gamma) = 0$.

### Step 1: The condition is $\nabla df = 0$.

By the standard identity for the second derivative of a composed map:

$$\frac{d^2}{dt^2}(f \circ \gamma) = d f\!\left(\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma\ri
ght) + (\nabla df)(\dot\gamma, \dot\gamma).$$

Since $\gamma$ is a geodesic, $\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma = 0$, so:

$$\frac{d^2}{dt^2}(f \circ \gamma) = (\nabla df)(\dot\gamma, \dot\gamma).$$

Here $\nabla df$ is the **covariant Hessian** (second fundamental form) of $f$,
a symmetric section of $T^*M \otimes T^*M \otimes H$.

The condition that $f \circ \gamma$ is a straight line for every geodesic $\gamm
a$ is therefore:

$$(\nabla df)(\dot\gamma, \dot\gamma) = 0 \quad \text{for every geodesic } \gamm
a.$$

At any point $p \in M$ and for any tangent vector $v \in T_p M$, there exists a
geodesic $\gamma$ with $\dot\gamma(0) = v$ (by the geodesic existence theorem, v
alid for strong Riemannian metrics on Hilbert manifolds). Hence:

$$(\nabla df)_p(v, v) = 0 \quad \text{for all } v \in T_p M, \text{ for all } p
\in M.$$

### Step 2: Polarization gives $\nabla df = 0$.

Since $\nabla df$ is a symmetric bilinear form (valued in $H$), the **polarizati
on identity** applies over $\mathbb{R}$:

$$(\nabla df)(v, w) = \tfrac{1}{2}\big[(\nabla df)(v+w, v+w) - (\nabla df)(v,v)
- (\nabla df)(w,w)\big] = 0$$

for all $v, w \in T_p M$. Therefore:

$$\boxed{\nabla df = 0 \text{ everywhere on } M.}$$

This means $df$ is a **parallel section** of the bundle $T^*M \otimes H$ (where
$H$ carries the pulled-back flat connection).

### Step 3: Consequences of $\nabla df = 0$.

**(a) Constant rank.** Since $df$ is parallel, parallel transport along any path
 preserves $df$. Therefore the rank of $df$ is constant on each connected compon
ent of $M$.

**(b) Curvature constraint.** A parallel section is annihilated by the curvature
 of the bundle. The curvature of $T^*M \otimes H$ is $R^* \otimes \mathrm{id}_H$
 (since $H$ is flat). Thus for all vector fields $X, Y$:

$$R^*(X,Y) \cdot df = 0,$$

i.e., $df(R(X,Y)Z) = 0$ for all $X, Y, Z$. This means the image of the curvature
 endomorphism $R(X,Y): T_pM \to T_pM$ lies in $\ker(df_p)$ at every point.

**(c) Non-constant $f$ requires a parallel 1-form.** If $f$ is non-constant, the
n $df \neq 0$ at some (hence every, by parallelism) point. Each component $f^\al
pha$ (in a basis of $H$) gives a 1-form $\alpha = df^\alpha$ satisfying $\nabla
\alpha = 0$, i.e., a **parallel 1-form**. A non-zero parallel 1-form exists if a
nd only if the holonomy group $\mathrm{Hol}(\nabla)$ fixes some vector in $T_p^*
M$ (equivalently, in $T_p M$ via the metric).

### Step 4: When does a parallel 1-form exist?

A parallel 1-form $\alpha$ ($\nabla \alpha = 0$) is automatically closed ($d\alp
ha = 0$, since $d\alpha(X,Y) = (\nabla_X\alpha)(Y) - (\nabla_Y\alpha)(X) = 0$) a
nd has constant norm. Its dual vector field $\alpha^\sharp$ (via $g$) is a paral
lel vector field, hence a Killing field of constant length.

By the **de Rham decomposition theorem** (which extends to Hilbert manifolds und
er appropriate completeness hypotheses), a complete Riemannian manifold splits a
s a Riemannian product $M = M_0 \times M_1 \times \cdots$ where $M_0$ is flat (t
he flat factor, possibly zero-dimensional) and the $M_i$ have irreducible holono
my. Parallel 1-forms exist precisely corresponding to the flat factor $M_0$: the
 space of parallel 1-forms has dimension $\dim M_0$.

Therefore:
- If $M$ has **no flat factor** (e.g., $M$ has irreducible holonomy and non-zero
 curvature), then there are **no non-zero parallel 1-forms**, and the only solut
ion is $f = \text{constant}$.
- If $M$ has a flat factor of dimension $k$, then there are $k$ linearly indepen
dent parallel 1-forms, and one can construct a non-constant $f$ (locally, an aff
ine projection onto the flat factor composed with a linear map into $H$).

### Step 5: For $f$ to be an immersion, $M$ must be flat.

If $f$ is an immersion, then $\mathrm{rank}(df) = \dim M$, so $\ker(df) = 0$. Fr
om Step 3(b), $R(X,Y)Z \in \ker(df) = 0$ for all $X, Y, Z$, hence $R = 0$: the m
anifold is **flat**.

Conversely, if $M$ is flat and simply connected, then $M$ is globally isometric
to an open subset of a Hilbert space, and the inclusion (or the developing map)
is a smooth map taking geodesics to straight lines.

### Conclusion

For a **general** Hilbert manifold $M$ with non-vanishing curvature and irreduci
ble holonomy (e.g., any infinite-dimensional analogue of a space form with non-z
ero curvature), **no non-constant smooth map** $f: M \to H$ into any Hilbert spa
ce $H$ can take all geodesics to straight lines. The obstruction is the curvatur
e: the condition forces $\nabla df = 0$, which forces the curvature to annihilat
e the image of $df$, and with irreducible holonomy and $R \neq 0$, this leaves o
nly $df = 0$.

The answer to the question is therefore **No**: such a map does not exist in gen
eral. It exists (non-trivially) if and only if $M$ has a flat factor in its de R
ham decomposition, and it can be an immersion only when $M$ is flat.
