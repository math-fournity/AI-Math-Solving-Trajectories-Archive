# Problem

In the competitive world of professional logistics, a massive warehouse contains a square grid of storage bays, arranged in 20 rows and 20 columns. A shipment of 400 distinct crates, labeled with unique serial numbers from 1 to 400, arrives at the facility. 

The warehouse manager allows the floor staff to distribute these 400 crates into the 400 available bays in any configuration they choose, provided that every bay contains exactly one crate. Once the crates are placed, the manager evaluates the "distribution spread" of each individual row and each individual column. The spread of a row or column is defined as the difference between the highest and lowest serial numbers found within that specific line.

The manager wants to determine the "Guaranteed Minimum Spread." What is the largest positive integer $N$ such that, regardless of how the crates are arranged in the $20 \times 20$ grid, there will always be at least one row or at least one column that contains two crates whose serial numbers differ by at least $N$?
