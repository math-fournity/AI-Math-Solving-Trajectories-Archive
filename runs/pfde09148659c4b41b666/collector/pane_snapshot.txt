## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $\{\mu(e_i)\}_{i=1}^m$ is also an integral basis of $\mathcal{O}
_K$.

**Step 1: $\mu$ maps algebraic integers to algebraic integers.**

Let $\alpha \in \mathcal{O}_K$, so $\alpha$ is an algebraic integer. Let $f(x) \
in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ be its (monic) minimal polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}$. Since $\m
u \in \text{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ fixes $\mathbb{Q}$ pointwise, applying $\mu$ to
the equation $f(\alpha) = 0$ gives

$$f(\mu(\alpha)) = \mu(f(\alpha)) = \mu(0) = 0.$$

Thus $\mu(\alpha)$ is also a root of the same monic polynomial $f \in \mathbb{Z}
[x]$, hence $\mu(\alpha)$ is an algebraic integer. Since $\mu(\alpha) \in K$ and
 is an algebraic integer, we have $\mu(\alpha) \in \mathcal{O}_K$. Therefore

$$\mu(\mathcal{O}_K) \subseteq \mathcal{O}_K.$$

**Step 2: $\mu$ restricts to a $\mathbb{Z}$-module automorphism of $\mathcal{O}_
K$.**

The same argument applied to $\mu^{-1} \in \text{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ gives $\mu^
{-1}(\mathcal{O}_K) \subseteq \mathcal{O}_K$, which implies $\mathcal{O}_K \subs
eteq \mu(\mathcal{O}_K)$. Combining with Step 1:

$$\mu(\mathcal{O}_K) = \mathcal{O}_K.$$

Since $\mu$ is a field automorphism, it is in particular additive, so its restri
ction to $\mathcal{O}_K$ is a $\mathbb{Z}$-module homomorphism. As $\mu(\mathcal
{O}_K) = \mathcal{O}_K$ and $\mu$ is injective, this restriction is a $\mathbb{Z
}$-module **automorphism** of $\mathcal{O}_K$.

**Step 3: The image of a basis under an automorphism is a basis.**

By hypothesis, $\{e_i\}_{i=1}^m$ is a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis of $\mathcal{O}_K$, mea
ning every element of $\mathcal{O}_K$ is a **unique** $\mathbb{Z}$-linear combin
ation of the $e_i$. Let $M: \mathcal{O}_K \to \mathcal{O}_K$ denote the $\mathbb
{Z}$-module automorphism $\alpha \mapsto \mu(\alpha)$.

- **Spanning:** For any $\beta \in \mathcal{O}_K$, since $\mu$ is surjective on
$\mathcal{O}_K$, there exists $\alpha \in \mathcal{O}_K$ with $\mu(\alpha) = \be
ta$. Write $\alpha = \sum_i a_i e_i$ with $a_i \in \mathbb{Z}$. Then
$$\beta = \mu(\alpha) = \sum_i a_i\, \mu(e_i),$$
so $\{\mu(e_i)\}$ spans $\mathcal{O}_K$ over $\mathbb{Z}$.

- **Linear independence:** Suppose $\sum_i a_i\, \mu(e_i) = 0$ with $a_i \in \ma
thbb{Z}$. Applying $\mu^{-1}$:
$$\sum_i a_i\, e_i = \mu^{-1}(0) = 0.$$
Since $\{e_i\}$ is $\mathbb{Z}$-linearly independent, $a_i = 0$ for all $i$.

**Conclusion:** $\{\mu(e_i)\}_{i=1}^m$ is a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis of $\mathcal{O}_K
$, i.e., an integral basis of $\mathcal{O}_K$. $\blacksquare$
