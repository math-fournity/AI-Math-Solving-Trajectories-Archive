## Proof

**Definition.** A *box function* $f:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ is piecewise constant: there
exist breakpoints $0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_n=1$ and values $v_1,\dots,v_n\in[0,1]$ wi
th $f(x)=v_i$ for $x\in(t_{i-1},t_i)$.

**Step 1: Characterize $Of$ for a box function.**

$f$ is constant (hence continuous) on each open interval $(t_{i-1},t_i)$, so $Of
(x)=0$ for every interior point $x\in(t_{i-1},t_i)$. At the endpoints $0$ and $1
$, $f$ is locally constant, so $Of(0)=Of(1)=0$.

At a breakpoint $t_i$ ($1\le i\le n-1$): for small $d>0$, the ball $B_d(t_i)$ co
ntains points where $f=v_i$ (left side) and points where $f=v_{i+1}$ (right side
), so
$$Of(t_i)=\lim_{d\to 0}\sup_{y,z\in B_d(t_i)}|f(y)-f(z)|=|v_{i+1}-v_i|.$$

Therefore $Of$ is **nonzero only at finitely many isolated points** — namely tho
se breakpoints $t_i$ where $v_i\ne v_{i+1}$ — and $Of(x)=0$ everywhere else.

**Step 2: A general lemma.**

> **Lemma.** If $g:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}$ is nonzero only at finitely many isolated
 points $p_1,\dots,p_m$ (with $g(p_i)=a_i>0$ and $g(x)=0$ for $x\notin\{p_1,\dot
s,p_m\}$), then $Og=g$.

*Proof of Lemma.* Since the points are isolated and finite, there exists $\delta
>0$ such that the balls $B_\delta(p_i)$ are pairwise disjoint and contain no oth
er $p_j$.

- **At $p_i$:** For any $d<\delta$, on $B_d(p_i)$ the function $g$ takes the val
ue $a_i$ at $p_i$ and $0$ at all other points. Hence $\sup_{y,z\in B_d(p_i)}|g(y
)-g(z)|=a_i$, giving $Og(p_i)=a_i=g(p_i)$.

- **At $x\notin\{p_1,\dots,p_m\}$:** Choose $d<\delta$ small enough that $B_d(x)
$ contains no $p_i$. Then $g\equiv 0$ on $B_d(x)$, so $Og(x)=0=g(x)$.

Thus $Og=g$ pointwise. $\square$
