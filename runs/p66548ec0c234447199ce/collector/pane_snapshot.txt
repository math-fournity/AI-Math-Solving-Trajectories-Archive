Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the existence of a unit vec
tor $x$ with $\text{dist}(x,Y)=1$ forces the proper subspace $Y$ to be finite-di
mensional.

**Key observation.** Since $Y$ is a subspace, $0 \in Y$, so $\text{dist}(x,Y) \l
eq \|x-0\| = \|x\| = 1$. The condition $\text{dist}(x,Y)=1$ therefore means the
infimum is achieved at $y=0$, i.e., $\|x-y\|\geq 1 = \|x\|$ for all $y\in Y$.

This is a form of "orthogonality" of $x$ to $Y$ in the normed-space sense. The q
uestion is whether this can happen when $Y$ is infinite-dimensional.

---

**Proof.**

The answer is **No**: $Y$ is not necessarily finite-dimensional.

**Counterexample.** Let $X = \ell^2(\mathbb{R})$, the Hilbert space of square-su
mmable real sequences with norm $\|a\|_2 = \bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} a_i^2\bigr)
^{1/2}$. Define

$$Y = \bigl\{a = (a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots) \in \ell^2 : a_1 = 0\bigr\}.$$

**$Y$ is a proper subspace.** It is clearly a linear subspace, and it is proper
since $e_1 = (1,0,0,\dots)\notin Y$.

**$Y$ is infinite-dimensional.** The vectors $e_2, e_3, e_4, \dots$ all belong t
o $Y$ and are linearly independent, so $\dim Y = \infty$.

**The distance condition holds.** Set $x = e_1 = (1, 0, 0, \dots)$. Then $\|x\|_
2 = 1$. For any $y = (0, a_2, a_3, \dots) \in Y$,

$$\|x - y\|_2^2 = |1 - 0|^2 + \sum_{i=2}^{\infty}|0 - a_i|^2 = 1 + \sum_{i=2}^{\
infty} a_i^2 \geq 1.$$

Hence $\|x - y\|_2 \geq 1$ for every $y \in Y$, with equality at $y = 0 \in Y$.
Therefore

$$\operatorname{dist}(x, Y) = \inf_{y \in Y} \|x - y\|_2 = 1 = \|x\|.$$

All the hypotheses of the problem are satisfied, yet $Y$ is infinite-dimensional
. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** The same construction works in $\ell^p(\mathbb{R})$ for any $1 \leq
p \leq \infty$: taking $Y = \{a : a_1 = 0\}$ and $x = e_1$ gives $\|x\|_p = 1$ a
nd $\|x - y\|_p^p = 1 + \sum_{i\geq 2}|a_i|^p \geq 1$ for all $y \in Y$, so $\op
eratorname{dist}(x,Y)=1$ with $Y$ infinite-dimensional. More abstractly, wheneve
r a norm-one functional $f \in X^*$ attains its norm at a unit vector $x$ (i.e.,
 $f(x)=1=\|f\|$), the kernel $Y = \ker(f)$ satisfies $\operatorname{dist}(x, Y)
= 1$, and such kernels are typically infinite-dimensional (e.g., the first coord
inate functional on any $\ell^p$).
