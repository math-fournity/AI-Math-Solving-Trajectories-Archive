**Lemma.** Let $R$ be an integral domain with field of fractions $K$, and let $\

$$\operatorname{Frac}(R[\alpha]) = K(\alpha).$$

*Proof of Lemma.* Since $\alpha$ is algebraic over $K$, the ring $K[\alpha]$ is
a field (equal to $K(\alpha)$). As $R[\alpha] \subseteq K[\alpha] = K(\alpha)$,
the ring $R[\alpha]$ is a subring of a field, hence an integral domain.

We prove $\operatorname{Frac}(R[\alpha]) = K(\alpha)$ by mutual inclusion.

*($\subseteq$)*: $K(\alpha)$ is a field containing both $R$ (since $R \subseteq
K \subseteq K(\alpha)$) and $\alpha$, so it contains $R[\alpha]$. Being a field,
