# Solution

**Yes.** Every continuous image of $[0,1]$ (into a Hausdorff space) is locally c
onnected.

## Proof

We use three facts:

1. $[0,1]$ is **compact** and **locally connected**.
2. A continuous surjection from a compact space onto a Hausdorff space is a **cl
osed map**, hence a **quotient map**.
3. A **quotient image of a locally connected space is locally connected**.

Only fact (3) requires detailed justification; we prove it as a lemma.

### Lemma

*Let $q : X \to Y$ be a quotient map with $X$ locally connected. Then $Y$ is loc
ally connected.*

**Proof of Lemma.** Recall the characterization: a space is locally connected if
f for every open set $U \subseteq Y$, every connected component of $U$ is open i
n $Y$.

Let $U$ be open in $Y$ and let $C$ be a connected component of $U$. To show $C$
is open in $Y$, it suffices (since $q$ is a quotient map) to show that $q^{-1}(C
)$ is open in $X$.

Since $U$ is open in $Y$, $q^{-1}(U)$ is open in $X$. Because $X$ is locally con
nected, every connected component of the open set $q^{-1}(U)$ is open in $X$.

**Claim.** $q^{-1}(C)$ is a union of connected components of $q^{-1}(U)$.

Let $D$ be any connected component of $q^{-1}(U)$. Then $q(D)$ is connected (bei
ng the continuous image of a connected set) and $q(D) \subseteq U$, so $q(D)$ li
es within a single connected component of $U$. If $D \cap q^{-1}(C) \neq \varnot
hing$, then $q(D) \cap C \neq \varnothing$, which forces $q(D) \subseteq C$, hen
ce $D \subseteq q^{-1}(C)$. Otherwise $D \cap q^{-1}(C) = \varnothing$. Thus eac
h component $D$ is either wholly contained in $q^{-1}(C)$ or disjoint from it, p
roving the claim.

Therefore $q^{-1}(C)$ is a union of (open) connected components of $q^{-1}(U)$,
hence $q^{-1}(C)$ is open in $X$. By the quotient property, $C$ is open in $Y$.
$\square$

### Main argument

Let $f : [0,1] \to Y$ be continuous and surjective, with $Y$ Hausdorff.

- **$f$ is a closed map:** $[0,1]$ is compact, so every closed subset of $[0,1]$
 is compact; its image under $f$ is compact; and compact subsets of a Hausdorff
space are closed.
- **$f$ is a quotient map:** A continuous surjection that is closed is a quotien
t map. (Indeed, if $f^{-1}(A)$ is open in $[0,1]$, then $f^{-1}(Y \setminus A) =
 [0,1] \setminus f^{-1}(A)$ is closed, hence compact; its image $Y \setminus A$
is compact, hence closed in $Y$; so $A$ is open.)
- **$[0,1]$ is locally connected:** every point has a neighborhood basis of open
 intervals, which are connected.
- Applying the Lemma with $q = f$ and $X = [0,1]$, we conclude that $Y$ is local
ly connected. $\blacksquare$

### Remark on the Hausdorff hypothesis

The Hausdorff (or at least $T_1$) assumption on the codomain is the standard set
ting for this result — it is what guarantees that the continuous surjection from
 the compact domain is a closed (hence quotient) map. Under this standard assump
tion, the Hahn–Mazurkiewicz theorem further characterizes such images precisely
as the **Peano continua**: compact, connected, locally connected, second-countab
le Hausdorff spaces. Local connectivity is therefore an intrinsic, guaranteed fe
ature of every continuous image of $[0,1]$.
