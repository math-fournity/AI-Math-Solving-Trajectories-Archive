## Proof

Place the unit circle at the origin with $A = (-1, 0)$ and $B = (1, 0)$, so that
 $AB$ is a diameter of length $2$.

**Step 1: Locate $C$.** Since $AB$ is a diameter, $\angle ACB = 90°$ (Thales). W
rite $C = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ on the unit circle. Then

$$|AC|^2 = 2 + 2\cos\theta, \qquad |CB|^2 = 2 - 2\cos\theta.$$

The ratio $|AC|/|CB| = 3/4$ gives

$$\frac{2+2\cos\theta}{2-2\cos\theta} = \frac{9}{16} \implies 16(1+\cos\theta) =
 9(1-\cos\theta) \implies 25\cos\theta = -7,$$

so $\cos\theta = -7/25$ and $\sin\theta = 24/25$. Thus $C = (-7/25,\; 24/25)$, w
ith $|AC| = 6/5$ and $|CB| = 8/5$.

**Step 2: Direction of the angle bisector from $B$.** The unit vectors from $B$
toward $A$ and $C$ are

$$\widehat{BA} = (-1, 0), \qquad \widehat{BC} = \frac{C - B}{|BC|} = \frac{(-32/
25,\, 24/25)}{8/5} = \left(-\tfrac{4}{5},\, \tfrac{3}{5}\right).$$

Their sum gives the bisector direction:

$$\widehat{BA} + \widehat{BC} = \left(-\tfrac{9}{5},\, \tfrac{3}{5}\right) \prop
to (-3,\, 1).$$

**Step 3: Find $D$.** The bisector ray from $B = (1,0)$ is $(1 - 3t,\; t)$ for $
t \ge 0$. Substituting into $x^2 + y^2 = 1$:

$$(1-3t)^2 + t^2 = 1 \implies 10t^2 - 6t = 0 \implies t = 0 \text{ (point } B\te
xt{)} \;\text{or}\; t = \tfrac{3}{5}.$$

Hence $D = \bigl(1 - \tfrac{9}{5},\; \tfrac{3}{5}\bigr) = \bigl(-\tfrac{4}{5},\;
 \tfrac{3}{5}\bigr)$, which lies on the unit circle since $\tfrac{16}{25} + \tfr
ac{9}{25} = 1$. ✓

**Step 4: Compute $|AD|$.**

$$|AD| = \sqrt{\left(-\tfrac{4}{5} + 1\right)^2 + \left(\tfrac{3}{5}\right)^2} =
 \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{25} + \tfrac{9}{25}} = \sqrt{\tfrac{10}{25}} = \frac{\sqrt{10}}
{5}.$$

**Verification.** $D$ is the midpoint of arc $AC$ not containing $B$, so $|AD| =
 |DC|$ should hold:

$$|DC| = \sqrt{\left(-\tfrac{7}{25}+\tfrac{4}{5}\right)^2 + \left(\tfrac{24}{25}
-\tfrac{3}{5}\right)^2} = \sqrt{\left(\tfrac{13}{25}\right)^2 + \left(\tfrac{9}{
25}\right)^2} = \sqrt{\tfrac{250}{625}} = \frac{\sqrt{10}}{5}. \;\checkmark$$

$$\boxed{|AD| = \dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{5}}$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
