**Proof.**

**Yes**, such a set exists. We construct a Cantor-type set $E \subset [0,1]$ wit
h Lebesgue measure $0$ and Hausdorff dimension $1$.

**Construction.** Set $\delta_n = \frac{1}{n+1}$ and $r_n = \frac{1-\delta_n}{2}
 = \frac{n}{2(n+1)}$ for $n \geq 1$. Begin with $E_0 = [0,1]$. At stage $n \geq
1$, replace each closed interval $I$ of length $\ell$ in $E_{n-1}$ by two closed
 subintervals of length $\ell \cdot r_n$, placed at the left and right ends of $
I$ (removing the open middle portion of length $\ell \cdot \delta_n$). Let $E =
\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty} E_n$.

After stage $n$, $E_n$ consists of $2^n$ disjoint closed intervals, each of leng
th
$$L_n = \prod_{k=1}^{n} r_k = \prod_{k=1}^{n} \frac{k}{2(k+1)} = \frac{1}{(n+1)\
,2^n}.$$

**Lebesgue measure is zero.** The total length of $E_n$ is
$$2^n L_n = \prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-\delta_k) = \prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{k}{k+1} = \frac{1
}{n+1} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.$$
Since $E \subseteq E_n$ for every $n$, $\lambda(E) \leq \frac{1}{n+1}$ for all $
n$, hence $\lambda(E)=0$.

**Hausdorff dimension is 1.** The upper bound $\dim_H(E)\leq 1$ is immediate sin
ce $E\subseteq\mathbb{R}$.

For the lower bound, we use the **mass distribution principle**: if there exists
 a probability measure $\mu$ on $E$ and a constant $C_s$ with $\mu(B(x,r))\leq C
_s\,r^s$ for all $x\in E$, $r>0$, then $\mathcal{H}^s(E)\geq 1/C_s>0$ and hence
$\dim_H(E)\geq s$.

Define the natural Cantor measure $\mu$: each of the $2^n$ level-$n$ intervals r
eceives mass $1/2^n$. This gives a consistent sequence of measures supported on
$E_n$; by compactness of $E$, the weak limit $\mu$ is a probability measure supp
orted on $E$.

Fix $s\in(0,1)$ and $r>0$. For $r\geq 1$, $\mu(B(x,r))\leq 1\leq r^s$, so the bo
und holds trivially. For $0<r<1$, choose $n\geq 1$ with $L_n\leq r < L_{n-1}$.

**Counting bound.** The level-$n$ intervals are disjoint, each of length $L_n$.
The number of them intersecting $[x-r,x+r]$ (length $2r$) is at most $2r/L_n+2$,
 since $k$ disjoint intervals of length $L_n$ intersecting $[x-r,x+r]$ have tota
l length $kL_n\leq 2r+2L_n$.

Therefore
$$\mu(B(x,r))\leq\left(\frac{2r}{L_n}+2\right)\frac{1}{2^n}=2r(n+1)+\frac{2}{2^n
}.$$

Using $r<L_{n-1}=\frac{1}{n\cdot 2^{n-1}}$:
$$2r(n+1)<\frac{2(n+1)}{n\cdot 2^{n-1}}=\frac{4(n+1)}{n\cdot 2^n}\leq\frac{8}{2^
n},$$
so $\mu(B(x,r))\leq\frac{10}{2^n}$.

From $r\geq L_n=\frac{1}{(n+1)2^n}$, we get $\frac{1}{2^n}\leq(n+1)r$, hence
$$\mu(B(x,r))\leq 10(n+1)\,r.$$

**Absorbing the factor $(n+1)$.** Since $s-1<0$ and $r<L_{n-1}=\frac{1}{n\cdot 2
^{n-1}}$:
$$r^{s-1}>L_{n-1}^{s-1}=\left(\frac{1}{n\cdot 2^{n-1}}\right)^{s-1}=n^{1-s}\,2^{
(n-1)(1-s)}.$$
Thus
$$\frac{(n+1)}{r^{s-1}}<\frac{(n+1)}{n^{1-s}\,2^{(n-1)(1-s)}}=\frac{(n+1)}{n^{1-
s}}\cdot 2^{-(n-1)(1-s)}\sim n^s\cdot 2^{-(n-1)(1-s)}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0,$
$
since the exponential decay $2^{-(n-1)(1-s)}$ dominates the polynomial growth $n
^s$. Hence there exists a constant $C_s$ (depending only on $s$) such that $(n+1
)\leq C_s\,r^{s-1}$ for all $n\geq 1$.

Combining:
$$\mu(B(x,r))\leq 10(n+1)\,r\leq 10\,C_s\,r^{s-1}\cdot r=10\,C_s\,r^s.$$

By the mass distribution principle, $\mathcal{H}^s(E)>0$ and $\dim_H(E)\geq s$.
Since $s<1$ was arbitrary, $\dim_H(E)\geq 1$.

Combining with the upper bound, $\dim_H(E)=1$.

**Conclusion.** The set $E\subset[0,1]$ has Lebesgue measure $0$ and Hausdorff d
imension $1$. $\blacksquare$
