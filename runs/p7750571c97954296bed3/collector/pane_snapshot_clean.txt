## Proof

**Construction.** Let $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$ denote the Cantor space (the produc
t of countably many copies of the discrete space $\{0,1\}$). Define the standard
 homeomorphism
$$\psi : \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}} \to C, \qquad \psi\big((b_n)_{n\ge 1}\big) = \sum_
{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2b_n}{3^n}.$$
This is well-defined, continuous, and bijective: its image consists of exactly t
hose numbers in $[0,1]$ with a ternary expansion using only digits $0$ and $2$,
which is $C$. Injectivity holds because a ternary expansion with digits in $\{0,
2\}$ is unique (the only ternary ambiguities are of the form $\ldots d\,0\,0\,0\
ldots = \ldots (d{-}1)\,2\,2\,2\ldots$, which would require a digit $d-1 = 1 \no
tin \{0,2\}$). Since $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$ is compact and $C$ is Hausdorff, $\p
si$ is a homeomorphism.

Now define two maps $F, G : \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}} \to [0,1]$:
$$F\big((b_n)\big) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{b_n}{2^n}, \qquad G\big((b_n)\big
) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{b_n}{3^n}.$$
Both are continuous (uniformly convergent series of continuous functions). Defin
e
$$h : C \to [0,1]\times[0,1], \qquad h(c) = \big(F(\psi^{-1}(c)),\; G(\psi^{-1}(
c))\big).$$

**$h$ is continuous.** It is the composition of the continuous map $\psi^{-1}$ w
ith the continuous pair $(F, G)$. $\checkmark$

**$h$ is injective.** Suppose $h(c) = h(c')$. Write $\psi^{-1}(c) = (b_n)$ and $
\psi^{-1}(c') = (b'_n)$. Then $G((b_n)) = G((b'_n))$, i.e.,
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{b_n}{3^n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{b'_n}{3^n}.$$
This is a ternary expansion with digits in $\{0,1\}$. Such an expansion is uniqu
e: the only ternary ambiguities require a repeating digit $2$ (i.e., $\ldots d\,
0\,0\,0\ldots = \ldots(d{-}1)\,2\,2\,2\ldots$), but the digit $2$ never appears
in a $\{0,1\}$-valued ternary expansion. Therefore $b_n = b'_n$ for all $n$, so
$c = c'$. $\checkmark$

**$h$ is a homeomorphism onto its image.** $C$ is compact, $[0,1]^2$ is Hausdorf
f, and $h$ is a continuous injection, so $h$ is a homeomorphism from $C$ onto $h
(C)$. $\checkmark$

**Every vertical line $\{x\}\times[0,1]$ intersects $h(C)$.** Let $x \in [0,1]$.
 Choose a binary expansion $x = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n/2^n$ with $b_n \in \{0,1
\}$ (every real in $[0,1]$ has such an expansion). Set $c = \psi((b_n)) \in C$.
Then
$$h(c) = \left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{b_n}{2^n},\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{
b_n}{3^n}\right) = \left(x,\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{b_n}{3^n}\right) \in \{x
\}\times[0,1].$$
Thus $\{x\}\times[0,1] \cap h(C) \neq \varnothing$ for every $x \in [0,1]$. $\ch
eckmark$

**Summary.** The map $h$ sends a Cantor-set point $c = \sum 2b_n/3^n$ to the pai
r $\big(\sum b_n/2^n,\; \sum b_n/3^n\big)$. The first coordinate is the Cantor f
unction (continuous surjection onto $[0,1]$, guaranteeing every vertical line is
 hit), and the second coordinate is a continuous injection into $[0,1]$ (using t
he uniqueness of $\{0,1\}$-digit ternary expansions, guaranteeing $h$ is injecti
ve). Compactness of $C$ then ensures $h$ is a homeomorphism onto its image.
