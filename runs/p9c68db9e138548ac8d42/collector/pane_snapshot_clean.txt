## Proof

**Claim:** $P(x,x)$ cannot have odd degree.

Write $P(x,y)$ in the binomial-coefficient basis:
$$P(x,y) = \sum_{i,j \ge 0} b_{i,j}\binom{x}{i}\binom{y}{j},$$
where the sum is finite (only finitely many $b_{i,j} \neq 0$).

**Step 1: First condition forces $b_{i,j}=0$ for $i < j$.**

Fix $n \ge 0$. Then
$$P(n,y) = \sum_{j \ge 0}\Bigl(\sum_{i \ge 0} b_{i,j}\binom{n}{i}\Bigr)\binom{y}
{j}.$$
Since $\binom{n}{i}=0$ for $i > n$ (non-negative integer $n$), the coefficient o
f $\binom{y}{j}$ is $f_j(n) = \sum_{i=0}^{n} b_{i,j}\binom{n}{i}$.

The hypothesis says: for $j > n$, $f_j(n) = 0$. Taking $n = 0, 1, \dots, j-1$:

- $n=0$: $f_j(0) = b_{0,j} = 0$.
- $n=1$: $f_j(1) = b_{0,j}+b_{1,j} = 0 \Rightarrow b_{1,j}=0$.
- $n=k$: knowing $b_{0,j}=\dots=b_{k-1,j}=0$, we get $f_j(k)=b_{k,j}=0$.

By induction up to $n=j-1$: $b_{i,j}=0$ for all $i < j$.

**Step 2: Second condition forces $b_{i,j}=0$ for $j < i$.**

By the symmetric argument (swapping the roles of $x$ and $y$), applying the cond
ition on $P(x,n)$ to the coefficient of $\binom{x}{i}$, we obtain $b_{i,j}=0$ fo
r all $j < i$.

**Step 3: Only diagonal terms survive.**

Combining Steps 1 and 2: $b_{i,j} \neq 0$ only when $i = j$. Hence
$$P(x,y) = \sum_{i \ge 0} b_{i,i}\binom{x}{i}\binom{y}{i}.$$

**Step 4: $P(x,x)$ has even degree.**

Setting $y = x$:
$$P(x,x) = \sum_{i \ge 0} b_{i,i}\binom{x}{i}^2.$$

Each $\binom{x}{i} = \frac{x(x-1)\cdots(x-i+1)}{i!}$ is a polynomial of degree $
i$ with leading coefficient $\frac{1}{i!}$, so $\binom{x}{i}^2$ has degree $2i$
with leading coefficient $\frac{1}{(i!)^2} \neq 0$.

Since different values of $i$ produce different degrees $2i$, the terms of highe
st degree cannot cancel. If $I = \max\{i : b_{i,i} \neq 0\}$, then
$$\deg P(x,x) = 2I,$$
which is even. (If all $b_{i,i}=0$, then $P \equiv 0$.)

**Conclusion:** $P(x,x)$ always has even degree (or is identically zero). It **c
annot** have odd degree.
