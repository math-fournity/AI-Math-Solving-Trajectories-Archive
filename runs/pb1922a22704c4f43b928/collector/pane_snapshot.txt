## Part I — Lower Bound

**Claim.** $\exists\, c_1 \in (0,1]$ such that $c_1\sqrt{n/\log n} \le f(n)$ for
 all sufficiently large $n$.

### Key ingredient

**Theorem (Kim 1995, building on Ajtai–Komlós–Szemerédi 1980).** $R(3,t) = \Thet
a(t^2/\log t)$. In particular, $\exists\, c_0 > 0$ such that for all sufficientl
y large $t$:
$$R(3,t) \ge c_0\,\frac{t^2}{\log t}.$$

By definition of the Ramsey number, this means: for every sufficiently large $t$
, there exists a triangle-free graph on $n' = \lfloor c_0\, t^2/\log t\rfloor -
1$ vertices with **no independent set of size $t$**, i.e., $\alpha < t$.
