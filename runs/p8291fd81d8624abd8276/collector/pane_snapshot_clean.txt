No. Here is the proof.

**Theorem.** Let $M$ be a connected smooth manifold. If $U\subset M$ is open and
 homeomorphic to an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^m$, and $V\subset M$ is open and
homeomorphic to an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$, then $m=n$. In particular, the
 situation described in the problem (with $n\neq m$) cannot occur.

**Key tool — Dimension Theorem.** *If an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^a$ is homeom
orphic to an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^b$, then $a=b$.*

*Proof of the Dimension Theorem.* Suppose $A\subset\mathbb{R}^a$ and $B\subset\m
athbb{R}^b$ are open and $h:A\to B$ is a homeomorphism. Assume for contradiction
 that $a<b$ (the case $b<a$ is symmetric). Pick $x\in A$ and set $y=h(x)\in B$.
Restricting $h$, we get a homeomorphism
$$A\setminus\{x\}\;\cong\;B\setminus\{y\}.$$
Since $A$ is open, a small closed ball $\overline{B_r(x)}\subset A$ deformation-
retracts onto $S^{a-1}$, and $A\setminus\{x\}$ contains a copy of $S^{a-1}$ as a
 deformation retract of a neighborhood; in particular
$$H_{a-1}(A\setminus\{x\};\,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}\neq 0.$$
On the other hand, $B\setminus\{y\}$ (an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^b$ with one
point removed) is homotopy equivalent to $S^{b-1}$, hence is $(b-2)$-connected.
Since $a<b$, we have $a-1\le b-2$, so
$$H_{a-1}(B\setminus\{y\};\,\mathbb{Z})=0.$$
This contradicts $A\setminus\{x\}\cong B\setminus\{y\}$. Hence $a=b$. $\square$

*(Equivalently, one may cite Brouwer's Invariance of Domain: a continuous inject
ive map $W\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^k$ from open $W\subset\mathbb{R}^k$ has open
 image; this forces the dimension to be a topological invariant.)*

**Proof of the Theorem.** Since $M$ is a smooth manifold, every point $p\in M$ h
as an open neighborhood $W_p$ homeomorphic to an open subset of some $\mathbb{R}
^{k(p)}$. Define a function
$$d:M\longrightarrow\mathbb{N},\qquad d(p)=k(p).$$

*Well-definedness.* Suppose $p$ has two neighborhoods $W_p\cong A\subset\mathbb{
R}^a$ (open) and $W'_p\cong B\subset\mathbb{R}^b$ (open). Then $W_p\cap W'_p$ is
 an open neighborhood of $p$ homeomorphic (via the first chart) to an open subse
t of $\mathbb{R}^a$ and (via the second chart) to an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^
b$. By the Dimension Theorem, $a=b$. So $d(p)$ is independent of the choice of c
hart.

*Local constancy.* If $d(p)=k$, then $p$ has a neighborhood $W_p$ homeomorphic t
o an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^k$. For every $q\in W_p$, the same $W_p$ witness
es that $q$ has a neighborhood homeomorphic to an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^k$,
 so $d(q)=k$ by well-definedness. Thus $d$ is constant on $W_p$; it is locally c
onstant.

*Conclusion.* A locally constant function on a connected space is constant. Henc
e $d\equiv N$ for some fixed $N\in\mathbb{N}$. Now:
- $U$ is open and homeomorphic to an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^m$. For any $p\i
n U$, the homeomorphism exhibits a neighborhood of $p$ as an open subset of $\ma
thbb{R}^m$, so $d(p)=m$, hence $m=N$.
- Likewise $V$ gives $n=N$.

Therefore $m=n$, contradicting the hypothesis $n\neq m$. $\square$

**Remark on the disconnected case.** A *smooth manifold* is by definition equipp
ed with a single fixed dimension: its atlas consists of charts into $\mathbb{R}^
N$ for one common $N$, with smooth (hence continuous) transition maps. Thus the
scenario in the problem is excluded already by the definition. The content of th
e proof above is that, even at the purely topological level, *connectedness alon
e* forces a single dimension — the dimension of a (connected) manifold is a topo
logical invariant, not a convention. For a disconnected space one could in princ
iple declare different dimensions on different components, but such an object is
 not a smooth manifold in the standard sense (which requires a globally fixed $N
$).
