## Proof

We prove that for every set $X = \{x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n\}$ of $n$ real numbers,
there exists a non-empty subset $S \subseteq X$ and an integer $m$ such that $\l
eft|m + \sum_{s \in S} s\right| \le \dfrac{1}{n+1}$.

It suffices to show that some non-empty subset sum is within $\dfrac{1}{n+1}$ of
 an integer (then we take $m$ to be the negative of that nearest integer).

**Setup.** Fix any ordering of the elements and define the partial sums
$$
P_0 = 0, \qquad P_k = x_1 + x_2 + \cdots + x_k \quad (1 \le k \le n).
$$
Consider the $n+1$ fractional parts $\{P_0\}, \{P_1\}, \dots, \{P_n\} \in [0,1)$
, where $\{P_0\} = \{0\} = 0$. View $[0,1)$ as a circle by identifying $0$ and $
1$.

**Pigeonhole on the circle.** Place the $n+1$ points $\{P_0\}, \{P_1\}, \dots, \
{P_n\}$ on this circle (of circumference $1$). These points cut the circle into
$n+1$ arcs whose lengths sum to $1$. Hence the shortest arc has length at most $
\dfrac{1}{n+1}$. (If two points coincide, the shortest arc has length $0 \le \df
rac{1}{n+1}$, and the argument below applies a fortiori.)

**Extracting a close pair.** The endpoints of this shortest arc are two of our p
oints, say $\{P_i\}$ and $\{P_j\}$ with $i \neq j$. Being endpoints of an arc of
 length $\le \dfrac{1}{n+1}$ on the unit circle means
$$
\{P_i\} - \{P_j\ \} \equiv \delta \pmod 1 \quad \text{for some } |\delta| \le \t
frac{1}{n+1}.
$$
Since $P_i - P_j = (\{P_i\} - \{P_j\}) + \ell$ for some integer $\ell$, we obtai
n
$$
\left|(P_i - P_j) - (\ell + \delta)\right| = 0 \le \tfrac{1}{n+1},
$$
i.e., $P_i - P_j$ is within $\dfrac{1}{n+1}$ of the integer $\ell + \delta$'s ne
arest integer. More directly: there exists an integer $m'$ with $|P_i - P_j - m'
| \le \dfrac{1}{n+1}$.

**The difference is a non-empty subset sum.** Assume WLOG $i > j$ (the case $i <
 j$ is symmetric; the case where one index is $0$ is handled below). Then
$$
P_i - P_j = x_{j+1} + x_{j+2} + \cdots + x_i,
$$
which is the sum of the non-empty subset $S = \{x_{j+1}, \dots, x_i\}$. If one o
f the two indices is $0$, say $j = 0$, then $P_i - P_0 = P_i = x_1 + \cdots + x_
i$, the sum of the non-empty subset $\{x_1, \dots, x_i\}$. In every case the dif
ference is a non-empty subset sum.

**Conclusion.** We have found a non-empty subset $S \subseteq X$ and an integer
$m'$ with
$$
\left|\sum_{s \in S} s - m'\right| \le \frac{1}{n+1}.
$$
Setting $m = -m'$ yields
$$
\left|m + \sum_{s \in S} s\right| \le \frac{1}{n+1}.
$$

This completes the proof. $\blacksquare$
