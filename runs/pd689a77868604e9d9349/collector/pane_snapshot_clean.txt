## Proof

**Claim:** The degree of $\nu_{n,d}(C)$ is $ad$, **not** $ad^n$.

**Setup.** Let $\nu_{n,d}\colon \mathbb{P}^n \to \mathbb{P}^N$ be the Veronese e
mbedding of degree $d$, where $N = \binom{n+d}{d}-1$. It is the map sending $[x_
0:\cdots:x_n]$ to the vector of all monomials of degree $d$ in $x_0,\dots,x_n$.
This is a closed embedding, hence $\nu_{n,d}(C)$ is a curve isomorphic to $C$.

**Degree via hyperplane sections.** By definition, the degree of a curve $D \sub
set \mathbb{P}^N$ is the number of points (counted with multiplicity) in $D \cap
 H$, where $H$ is a general hyperplane in $\mathbb{P}^N$.

**Pullback of a hyperplane.** A hyperplane $H \subset \mathbb{P}^N$ is cut out b
y a single linear form in the coordinates of $\mathbb{P}^N$. Each coordinate of
$\mathbb{P}^N$ is a degree-$d$ monomial in $x_0,\dots,x_n$. Therefore a linear f
orm in the $\mathbb{P}^N$-coordinates pulls back under $\nu_{n,d}$ to a homogene
ous polynomial of degree $d$ in $x_0,\dots,x_n$. In other words,
$$\nu_{n,d}^{-1}(H) = V(F) \subset \mathbb{P}^n$$
for some homogeneous polynomial $F$ of degree $d$. Conversely, every degree-$d$
hypersurface in $\mathbb{P}^n$ arises this way (its defining polynomial is a lin
ear combination of degree-$d$ monomials, i.e., a linear form in the Veronese coo
rdinates).

**Counting intersection points.** Since $\nu_{n,d}$ is an embedding,
$$|\nu_{n,d}(C) \cap H| = |C \cap \nu_{n,d}^{-1}(H)| = |C \cap V(F)|.$$
For a **general** hyperplane $H$, the corresponding $F$ is a general degree-$d$
form, so $V(F)$ is a general degree-$d$ hypersurface. By Bézout's theorem, a cur
ve of degree $a$ and a hypersurface of degree $d$ in $\mathbb{P}^n$ (with $n \ge
q 2$) intersect in exactly
$$a \cdot d$$
points, counted with multiplicity, provided the intersection is proper (which ho
lds for general $F$). Therefore
$$\deg\bigl(\nu_{n,d}(C)\bigr) = ad.$$

**Why not $ad^n$?** The factor $d^n$ would arise if one were intersecting with $
n$ independent degree-$d$ hypersurfaces simultaneously (giving $d^n$ points on $
\mathbb{P}^n$ itself, which is indeed the degree of the Veronese variety $\nu_{n
,d}(\mathbb{P}^n) \subset \mathbb{P}^N$). But for a **curve**, a single hyperpla
ne section suffices to define degree, and that single hyperplane pulls back to a
 **single** degree-$d$ hypersurface, contributing only a single factor of $d$. T
he extra factors of $d$ come from the codimension-$n$ intersection that defines
the degree of the $n$-dimensional Veronese variety, not from a curve inside it.

**Sanity check ($n=2$, $d=2$).** Take $C$ a line ($a=1$) in $\mathbb{P}^2$. The
Veronese $\nu_{2,2}$ sends $\mathbb{P}^2 \to \mathbb{P}^5$. A line in $\mathbb{P
}^2$ maps to a conic in $\mathbb{P}^5$, which has degree $2 = a\cdot d$. The for
mula $ad^n = 1\cdot 4 = 4$ is clearly wrong here. Likewise, a conic ($a=2$) in $
\mathbb{P}^2$ maps to a degree-$4$ curve under $\nu_{2,2}$, matching $ad = 4$, w
hile $ad^n = 8$ fails.

**Conclusion.** The degree of the image of a degree-$a$ curve under the degree-$
d$ Veronese map is
$$\boxed{\deg\bigl(\nu_{n,d}(C)\bigr) = ad.}$$
It is **not** $ad^n$; the latter is the degree of the Veronese variety $\nu_{n,d
}(\mathbb{P}^n)$ itself, not of a curve inside it.
