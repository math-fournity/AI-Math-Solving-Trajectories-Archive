Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every dense subset $S \subs
et \mathbb{R}^3$ can host a combinatorially equivalent copy of every convex poly
hedron.

**Answer: No.**

## Proof

We construct a dense subset $S \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ in **general position** (no
 four points of $S$ are coplanar), then show that any convex polyhedron with a n
on-triangular face—such as the cube—cannot be realized with vertices in $S$.

### Construction of $S$

Let $\{B_k\}_{k=1}^\infty$ be an enumeration of all open balls in $\mathbb{R}^3$
 with rational centers and rational radii (a countable basis for the topology).
We build $S = \{s_1, s_2, \dots\}$ inductively.

**Inductive step.** Suppose $s_1, \dots, s_{k-1}$ have been chosen, with no four
 coplanar among them. Define the *bad set*:
$$\mathcal{B}_k = \bigcup_{1 \le i < j < \ell \le k-1} \operatorname{Plane}(s_i,
 s_j, s_\ell),$$
where $\operatorname{Plane}(s_i, s_j, s_\ell)$ is the unique plane through $s_i,
 s_j, s_\ell$ (when they are non-collinear; collinear triples impose no constrai
nt). This is a union of at most $\binom{k-1}{3}$ planes.

Each plane is closed and nowhere-dense in $\mathbb{R}^3$. By the Baire category
theorem (applied to the complete metric space $\mathbb{R}^3$), a **finite** unio
n of closed nowhere-dense sets is closed and nowhere-dense (it has empty interio
r). Therefore $\mathcal{B}_k$ is closed and nowhere-dense, so $B_k \setminus \ma
thcal{B}_k$ is non-empty (a non-empty open set minus a closed nowhere-dense set
is non-empty). Choose:
$$s_k \in B_k \setminus \mathcal{B}_k.$$

**Properties of $S$:**
1. **Dense:** $S$ intersects every basis element $B_k$ (since $s_k \in B_k$), so
 $S$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}^3$.
2. **General position:** By construction, $s_k$ avoids the plane through any thr
ee previously chosen points, so no four points of $S$ are coplanar.

### The Counterexample

Take $P$ to be the **cube**, a convex polyhedron with 8 vertices and 6 quadrilat
eral faces.

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a convex polyhedron $P'$ combinato
rially equivalent to $P$ with all vertices in $S$. By combinatorial equivalence,
 $P'$ has 6 quadrilateral faces, each with exactly 4 vertices. Each face of $P'$
 is supported by a plane, so its 4 vertices are **coplanar**.

But these 4 vertices are distinct points of $S$, and $S$ has no four coplanar po
ints. **Contradiction.**

Therefore, no polyhedron combinatorially equivalent to the cube can have all ver
tices in $S$.

### Conclusion

There exists a dense subset $S \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ (any dense set in general p
osition) and a convex polyhedron $P$ (the cube, or any polyhedron with a non-tri
angular face) such that no combinatorially equivalent polyhedron has all vertice
s in $S$.

**Remark.** For *simplicial* polyhedra (all faces triangular), the realization s
pace is open in $\mathbb{R}^{3n}$ (no coplanarity constraints beyond non-degener
acy, which is an open condition), so the density argument *does* work and the an
swer would be yes. The obstruction arises precisely from non-triangular faces, w
hich impose coplanarity constraints that a general-position dense set can defeat
.
