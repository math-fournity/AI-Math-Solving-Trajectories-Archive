# Problem

In the competitive world of high-tech logistics, a specialized distribution hub operates using three different types of automated processing units: Type-M, Type-N, and Type-K.

The energy consumption of these units is measured in "Power-Cells" according to the following strict operational protocols:
- A Type-M unit requires a base of 2 units of energy raised to the power of its operational level $m$, where $m$ is a non-negative integer.
- A Type-N unit requires a base of 3 units of energy raised to the power of its operational level $n$, where $n$ is a non-negative integer.
- A single auxiliary stabilizer always consumes exactly 1 Power-Cell.
- The total energy output of the hub is generated by a Type-K unit, which produces a base of 6 units of energy raised to the power of its efficiency rating $k$, where $k$ is a non-negative integer.

For the system to remain stable, the sum of the energy consumed by the Type-M unit, the Type-N unit, and the stabilizer must exactly equal the total energy produced by the Type-K unit. This stability is defined by the equilibrium equation:
$2^m + 3^n + 1 = 6^k$

Determine the sum of all possible values of the aggregate system metric $(k + m + n)$ across all valid integer configurations that satisfy this equilibrium.
