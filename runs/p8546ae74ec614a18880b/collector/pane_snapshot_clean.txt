## Solution

**Setup.** Place the $k$ substations at the $k$-th roots of unity on the unit ci
rcle:
$$S_m = e^{2\pi i m/k}, \quad m = 0, 1, \dots, k-1.$$

**Step 1: Express the distance.** For two points at angles $\frac{2\pi m}{k}$ an
d $\frac{2\pi n}{k}$, the chord length is
$$|S_m - S_n| = 2\left|\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi(m-n)}{k}\right)\right|.$$

**Step 2: Reduce to a single sum.** The sum over all ordered pairs $i \neq j$ is
$$\sum_{\substack{i,j=0 \\ i \neq j}}^{k-1} \frac{1}{(S_i S_j)^2} = \sum_{\subst
ack{i,j=0 \\ i \neq j}}^{k-1} \frac{1}{4\sin^2\!\bigl(\pi(i-j)/k\bigr)}.$$

Setting $d = (i - j) \bmod k$, for each $d \in \{1, 2, \dots, k-1\}$ there are e
xactly $k$ ordered pairs $(i, j)$. Therefore
$$= \frac{k}{4} \sum_{d=1}^{k-1} \csc^2\!\left(\frac{\pi d}{k}\right).$$

**Step 3: The key trigonometric identity.** We prove
$$\sum_{d=1}^{k-1} \csc^2\!\left(\frac{\pi d}{k}\right) = \frac{k^2 - 1}{3}.$$

Starting from the partial fraction / product identity for cotangent:
$$\sum_{d=0}^{k-1} \cot\!\left(x + \frac{d\pi}{k}\right) = k\cot(kx).$$

Differentiating both sides with respect to $x$:
$$-\sum_{d=0}^{k-1} \csc^2\!\left(x + \frac{d\pi}{k}\right) = -k^2 \csc^2(kx),$$
hence
$$\sum_{d=0}^{k-1} \csc^2\!\left(x + \frac{d\pi}{k}\right) = k^2 \csc^2(kx).$$

Now take $x \to 0$. The $d = 0$ term $\csc^2(x)$ and the right-hand side $k^2\cs
c^2(kx)$ both diverge, but their divergences cancel. Using the Laurent expansion
 $\csc^2(x) = \frac{1}{x^2} + \frac{1}{3} + O(x^2)$:

$$k^2 \csc^2(kx) = \frac{1}{x^2} + \frac{k^2}{3} + O(x^2), \qquad \csc^2(x) = \f
rac{1}{x^2} + \frac{1}{3} + O(x^2).$$

Subtracting:
$$\sum_{d=1}^{k-1} \csc^2\!\left(\frac{d\pi}{k}\right) = \lim_{x \to 0}\Bigl[k^2
\csc^2(kx) - \csc^2(x)\Bigr] = \frac{k^2 - 1}{3}.$$

**Step 4: Combine.**
$$\sum_{i \neq j}^{k} \frac{1}{(S_i S_j)^2} = \frac{k}{4} \cdot \frac{k^2 - 1}{3
} = \boxed{\dfrac{k(k^2 - 1)}{12}}.$$

**Verification with small cases:**
- **$k = 2$:** Two antipodal points, distance $2$. Sum $= 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{4} =
 \tfrac{1}{2}$. Formula: $\tfrac{2 \cdot 3}{12} = \tfrac{1}{2}$. ✓
- **$k = 3$:** Equilateral triangle, side $\sqrt{3}$. Six ordered pairs, each $\
tfrac{1}{3}$. Sum $= 2$. Formula: $\tfrac{3 \cdot 8}{12} = 2$. ✓
- **$k = 4$:** Square, side $\sqrt{2}$, diagonal $2$. Sum $= 8 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2
} + 4 \cdot \tfrac{1}{4} = 5$. Formula: $\tfrac{4 \cdot 15}{12} = 5$. ✓
