## Setup

Triangle ABC is inscribed in a circle with ∠A = 60°, ∠B = 80°, ∠C = 40°. By the
inscribed angle theorem, the arcs (not containing the opposite vertex) are:
- arc BC = 2·∠A = 120°
- arc AC = 2·∠B = 160°
- arc AB = 2·∠C = 80°

(Check: 120° + 160° + 80° = 360° ✓)

Place the circle with center O at the origin, radius R. Going counterclockwise,
place:
- A at central angle 0°
- B at central angle 80°
- C at central angle 200°

This gives arc AB = 80°, arc BC = 120°, arc CA = 160°. ✓

## Key Geometric Fact

B is the farthest vertex from X means |XB| > |XA| **and** |XB| > |XC|.

- |XB| > |XA| ⟺ X is on **A's side** of the perpendicular bisector of AB.
- |XB| > |XC| ⟺ X is on **C's side** of the perpendicular bisector of BC.

**Crucially**, the perpendicular bisector of any chord of a circle passes throug
h the center O. So both perpendicular bisectors are lines through O, and the des
ired region is the intersection of two half-planes (each bounded by a line throu
gh O) with the disk — which is a **sector** of the disk.

## Finding the Perpendicular Bisectors

The perpendicular bisector of chord AB passes through O and the midpoint of arc
AB:
- Midpoint of arc AB (from 0° to 80°) = **40°** (and 220°).

The perpendicular bisector of chord BC passes through O and the midpoint of arc
BC:
- Midpoint of arc BC (from 80° to 200°) = **140°** (and 320°).

## Determining the Correct Sides

**A's side** of the perpendicular bisector of AB (the line at 40°/220°): A is at
 0°, which lies in the semicircle (220°, 360°) ∪ (0°, 40°). So A's side = semici
rcle centered at 320°.

**C's side** of the perpendicular bisector of BC (the line at 140°/320°): C is a
t 200°, which lies in the semicircle (140°, 320°). So C's side = semicircle cent
ered at 230°.

## Computing the Sector

The two lines (at 40°/220° and 140°/320°) create four sectors at O: 100°, 80°, 1
00°, 80°.

The intersection of A's side and C's side:
- A's side: (220°, 360°) ∪ (0°, 40°) — contains sectors (220°, 320°) and (320°,
40°)
- C's side: (140°, 320°) — contains sectors (140°, 220°) and (220°, 320°)

**Intersection = sector (220°, 320°), with central angle 100°.**

## Computing the Probability

The region where B is farthest is a sector of the disk with central angle 100°.
Its area is:

$$\frac{100°}{360°} \times \pi R^2$$

The total area is πR², so the probability is:

$$\frac{100}{360} = \frac{5}{18}$$

## Verification

**Sanity check (equilateral triangle):** If all angles are 60°, the formula give

**General formula:** The probability that vertex B is farthest = (180° − ∠B)/360
°. Here: (180° − 80°)/360° = 100°/360° = 5/18. ✓
