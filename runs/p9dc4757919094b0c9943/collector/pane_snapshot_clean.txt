Looking at this problem, I need to find all positive integers $x$ such that $|x^
2 - x - 6|$ has exactly 4 positive divisors.

## Setup

Factor: $x^2 - x - 6 = (x-3)(x+2)$, so $|x^2-x-6| = |x-3| \cdot (x+2)$ (since $x
+2 > 0$ for positive $x$).

A positive integer has exactly 4 divisors iff it is $p^3$ (prime $p$) or $p \cdo
t q$ (distinct primes $p, q$).

## Small cases

- **$x = 1$:** $|1-1-6| = 6 = 2 \cdot 3$. Divisors: $\{1,2,3,6\}$ → **4 divisors
 ✓**
- **$x = 2$:** $|4-2-6| = 4 = 2^2$. Divisors: $\{1,2,4\}$ → 3 divisors ✗
- **$x = 3$:** value is $0$ → not valid ✗

## Case $x \geq 4$: both factors positive

Set $a = x-3 \geq 1$ and $b = x+2 = a+5$. We need $a(a+5)$ to have exactly 4 div
isors.

**GCD analysis:** $\gcd(a, a+5) = \gcd(a, 5)$, giving two subcases.

### Subcase A: $5 \nmid a$ (coprime factors)

Then $\tau(a(a+5)) = \tau(a)\cdot\tau(a+5) = 4$, so $(\tau(a), \tau(a+5)) \in \{
(1,4), (2,2), (4,1)\}$.

- **$\tau(a)=1$:** $a=1$, so $x=4$, $a+5=6=2\cdot3$, $\tau(6)=4$. **✓** (gives $
x=4$)
- **$\tau(a)=2, \tau(a+5)=2$:** both $a$ and $a+5$ prime. Two primes differing b
y 5 must include 2 (the only even prime). If $a=2$: $a+5=7$ (prime) ✓ → $x=5$. I
f $a+5=2$: $a=-3$ (invalid). So only **$x=5$ ✓**.
- **$\tau(a)=4, \tau(a+5)=1$:** requires $a+5=1$, impossible.

### Subcase B: $5 \mid a$

Write $a = 5k$, so $a(a+5) = 25k(k+1) = 5^2 \cdot k(k+1)$.

For exactly 4 divisors, $25k(k+1)$ must be $p^3$ or $pq$ (distinct primes):

- **$= p^3$:** $5^2 \mid p^3 \Rightarrow p=5$, so $25k(k+1)=125 \Rightarrow k(k+
1)=5$, no integer solution.
- **$= pq$:** $5^2 \mid pq$ requires both $p,q$ divisible by 5, but they're dist
inct primes — at most one is 5, so $5^2 \nmid pq$. Contradiction.

**No solutions** in this subcase.

## Verification of found solutions

| $x$ | $\|x^2-x-6\|$ | factorization | # divisors |
|-----|---------------|---------------|------------|
| 1 | 6 | $2 \cdot 3$ | 4 ✓ |
| 4 | 6 | $2 \cdot 3$ | 4 ✓ |
| 5 | 14 | $2 \cdot 7$ | 4 ✓ |

Spot-checking larger values confirms no others: $x=6 \to 24$ (8 divisors), $x=7
\to 36$ (9 divisors), $x=8 \to 50$ (6 divisors), $x=9 \to 66$ (8 divisors), $x=1
0 \to 84$ (12 divisors) — and the divisor count only grows thereafter.

## Conclusion

The valid positive integers are $x \in \{1, 4, 5\}$.
