## Solution

We work in the Poincaré disk model of the hyperbolic plane, where the boundary i
s the unit circle and the hyperbolic distance from the origin to a point at Eucl
idean radius $r$ is

$$R = 2\,\operatorname{arctanh}(r) = \ln\frac{1+r}{1-r}.$$

This $R$ is the **circumradius** of the regular octagon (hyperbolic distance fro
m the center to any vertex).

### Step 1: Hyperbolic right-triangle relation

Consider the right triangle formed by:
- the center $O$ of the octagon,
- a vertex $V$,
- the midpoint $M$ of an adjacent side.

This is a hyperbolic right triangle with the right angle at $M$. The angle at $O
$ is $\pi/n = \pi/8$ (half the central angle $2\pi/8$), and the angle at $V$ is
$\alpha/2 = \pi/8$ (half the interior angle $\alpha = \pi/4$). The hypotenuse (s
ide opposite the right angle) is the circumradius $R$.

Applying the **hyperbolic law of cosines for angles** with $C = \pi/2$:

$$\cos C = -\cos A\,\cos B + \sin A\,\sin B\,\cosh c,$$

where $A = \pi/8$, $B = \pi/8$, $c = R$. Since $\cos C = 0$:

$$\cosh R = \cot A\,\cot B = \cot^2\!\left(\frac{\pi}{8}\right).$$

### Step 2: Evaluate $\cot(\pi/8)$

Using the half-angle identity, $\tan(\pi/8) = \sqrt{2}-1$, so

$$\cot\!\left(\frac{\pi}{8}\right) = \sqrt{2}+1,$$

and therefore

$$\cosh R = (\sqrt{2}+1)^2 = 3 + 2\sqrt{2}.$$

### Step 3: Convert to Euclidean radius

Using $\cosh(2\operatorname{arctanh} r) = \dfrac{1+r^2}{1-r^2}$ (which follows f
rom $\cosh(2x)=1+2\sinh^2 x$ and $\sinh(\operatorname{arctanh} r)=\frac{r}{\sqrt
{1-r^2}}$):

$$\frac{1+r^2}{1-r^2} = 3 + 2\sqrt{2}.$$

Let $u = r^2$:

$$1 + u = (3+2\sqrt{2})(1-u),$$
$$u\,(4+2\sqrt{2}) = 2+2\sqrt{2},$$
$$u = \frac{2+2\sqrt{2}}{4+2\sqrt{2}} = \frac{1+\sqrt{2}}{2+\sqrt{2}}.$$

Rationalizing by multiplying by $\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{2-\sqrt{2}}$:

$$u = \frac{(1+\sqrt{2})(2-\sqrt{2})}{(2+\sqrt{2})(2-\sqrt{2})} = \frac{2-\sqrt{
2}+2\sqrt{2}-2}{4-2} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.$$

### Step 4: Solve for $r$

$$r^2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = 2^{-1/2}, \qquad r = 2^{-1/4} = \frac{1}{\sqrt[4]{2
}}.$$

### Verification

With $r = 2^{-1/4}$: $r^2 = 1/\sqrt{2}$, and

$$\frac{1+r^2}{1-r^2} = \frac{1+1/\sqrt{2}}{1-1/\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}+1}{\s
qrt{2}-1} = \frac{(\sqrt{2}+1)^2}{1} = 3+2\sqrt{2}. \checkmark$$

This confirms $\cosh R = 3 + 2\sqrt{2} = \cot^2(\pi/8)$, which gives interior an
gle $\alpha = 2\cdot\pi/8 = \pi/4 = 45°$.

### Result

$$\boxed{r = 2^{-1/4} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt[4]{2}}}$$
