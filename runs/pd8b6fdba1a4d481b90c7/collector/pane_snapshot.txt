## Proof that $\pi_4(S^3) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$

### Step 1: Reduction via the Hopf fibration $S^1 \hookrightarrow S^3 \to S^2$

The Hopf fibration $S^1 \hookrightarrow S^3 \xrightarrow{p} S^2$ yields a long e
xact sequence of homotopy groups:
$$\cdots \to \pi_4(S^1) \to \pi_4(S^3) \to \pi_4(S^2) \to \pi_3(S^1) \to \cdots$
$

Since $S^1$ is a $K(\mathbb{Z},1)$, we have $\pi_k(S^1) = 0$ for all $k \geq 2$.
 In particular $\pi_4(S^1) = 0$ and $\pi_3(S^1) = 0$, so the exact sequence give
s:
$$0 \to \pi_4(S^3) \to \pi_4(S^2) \to 0$$
hence $\pi_4(S^3) \cong \pi_4(S^2)$.

### Step 2: The EHP sequence

The EHP sequence (arising from the fibration $\Omega S^{2n+1} \to S^n \to \Omega
 S^{n+1}$ due to James–Toda) gives, for each $n \geq 1$, a long exact sequence:
$$\cdots \to \pi_{k+1}(S^{2n+1}) \xrightarrow{P} \pi_{k-1}(S^n) \xrightarrow{E}
\pi_k(S^{n+1}) \xrightarrow{H} \pi_k(S^{2n+1}) \xrightarrow{P} \pi_{k-2}(S^n) \t
o \cdots$$
where $E$ is suspension, $H$ is the Hopf invariant, and $P$ is the Whitehead-pro
duct homomorphism.

### Step 3: Apply the EHP sequence with $n=2$, $k=4$

Setting $n=2$ (so $S^{2n+1}=S^5$, $S^n = S^2$, $S^{n+1}=S^3$) and $k=4$:
$$\cdots \to \pi_5(S^5) \xrightarrow{P} \pi_3(S^2) \xrightarrow{E} \pi_4(S^3) \x
rightarrow{H} \pi_4(S^5) \xrightarrow{P} \pi_2(S^2) \to \cdots$$

We use the following standard facts:
- $\pi_5(S^5) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by $\mathrm{id}_{S^5}$.
- $\pi_3(S^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by the Hopf map $\eta: S^3 \to S^2$.
- $\pi_4(S^5) = 0$, since $4 < 5$ (by cellular approximation, as $S^5$ has no ce
lls in dimensions $1$–$4$).
- $\pi_2(S^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by $\mathrm{id}_{S^2}$.

Substituting:
$$\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{P} \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{E} \pi_4(S^3) \to 0 \to \ma
thbb{Z} \to \cdots$$

### Step 4: Determine the $P$ map

The $P$ homomorphism $P: \pi_{k+1}(S^{2n+1}) \to \pi_{k-1}(S^n)$ sends the ident
ity $\mathrm{id} \in \pi_{2n+1}(S^{2n+1})$ to the **Whitehead square** $[i_n, i_
n] \in \pi_{2n-1}(S^n)$, where $i_n = \mathrm{id}_{S^n}$.

In our case, $P: \pi_5(S^5) \to \pi_3(S^2)$ sends $\mathrm{id}_{S^5} \mapsto [i_
2, i_2] \in \pi_3(S^2)$.

The Whitehead square $[i_2, i_2]$ is classical. Its **Hopf invariant is $\pm 2$*
* (for $n$ even, $[i_n,i_n]$ has Hopf invariant $\pm 2$; for $n$ odd, it has Hop
f invariant $0$). Since the Hopf invariant $H: \pi_3(S^2) \to \mathbb{Z}$ is an
isomorphism sending $\eta \mapsto 1$, we conclude:
$$[i_2, i_2] = \pm 2\,\eta$$

Therefore $P: \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}$ is **multiplication by $\pm 2$**, so $\
mathrm{im}(P) = 2\mathbb{Z}$.

### Step 5: Conclude

From the exact sequence
$$\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\cdot(\pm 2)} \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{E} \pi_4(S^3) \t
o 0,$$
exactness at the second $\mathbb{Z}$ gives $\ker(E) = \mathrm{im}(P) = 2\mathbb{
Z}$, and exactness at $\pi_4(S^3)$ gives that $E$ is surjective. Therefore:
$$\pi_4(S^3) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\ker(E) = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.$$

### Step 6: Identify the generator

The generator is $\Sigma\eta: S^4 \to S^3$, the **suspension of the Hopf map**,
since $E(\eta) = \Sigma\eta$ and $E$ is surjective with kernel $2\mathbb{Z}$.

That $2\,\Sigma\eta = 0$ also follows directly from the general fact that **the
suspension of any Whitehead product is null-homotopic**: $E([i_2,i_2]) = E(\pm 2
\eta) = \pm 2\,\Sigma\eta = 0$.

### Cross-check (Freudenthal suspension)

The Freudenthal suspension theorem states $E: \pi_k(S^n) \to \pi_{k+1}(S^{n+1})$
 is an isomorphism for $k \leq 2n-2$.

- $E: \pi_4(S^3) \to \pi_5(S^4)$: $4 \leq 2(3)-2 = 4$ ✓ — isomorphism.
- $E: \pi_5(S^4) \to \pi_6(S^5)$: $5 \leq 2(4)-2 = 6$ ✓ — isomorphism.
- $E: \pi_6(S^5) \to \pi_7(S^6)$: $6 \leq 2(5)-2 = 8$ ✓ — isomorphism.

In the stable range ($n \geq 3$), $\pi_{n+1}(S^n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ (the first
 stable stem $\pi_1^S$). In particular $\pi_7(S^6) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$. Tracing
back:
$$\pi_4(S^3) \cong \pi_5(S^4) \cong \pi_6(S^5) \cong \pi_7(S^6) \cong \mathbb{Z}
/2,$$
confirming the result.

---

$$\boxed{\pi_4(S^3) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}$$

The group is generated by the suspended Hopf map $\Sigma\eta: S^4 \to S^3$, whic
h has order $2$.
