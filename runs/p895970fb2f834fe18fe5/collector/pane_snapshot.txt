## Proof

$W(s)$ is a Wiener process, so $W(s) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, s)$, i.e. a Gaussian wi
th mean $\mu = 0$ and variance $\sigma^2 = s$.

For a Gaussian random variable $X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$, the moment g
enerating function is

$$E(e^{tX}) = \exp\!\left(\mu t + \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2 t^2\right).$$

Apply this with $X = W(s)$, $\mu = 0$, $\sigma^2 = s$, and $t = -2\sqrt{s}$:

$$E\!\left(e^{-2\sqrt{s}\,W(s)}\right) = \exp\!\left(0\cdot(-2\sqrt{s}) + \tfrac
{1}{2}\,s\,(-2\sqrt{s})^2\right).$$

Compute the exponent:

$$(-2\sqrt{s})^2 = 4s, \qquad \tfrac{1}{2}\,s\cdot 4s = 2s^2.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{E\!\left(e^{-2\sqrt{s}\,W(s)}\right) = e^{2s^2}.}$$
