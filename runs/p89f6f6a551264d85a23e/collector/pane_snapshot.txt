## Proof

**Theorem.** A Reidemeister III move can be performed on a minimal crossing diag
ram of a knot if and only if the diagram is non-alternating. Specifically:
- No RIII move can be performed on any reduced alternating diagram (which is a m
inimal diagram).
- There exist non-alternating minimal diagrams on which RIII moves can be perfor

### Part 0: RIII preserves crossing number

A Reidemeister III move replaces one triangular configuration of three crossings
 with another triangular configuration of three crossings. The total number of c
rossings is unchanged. Therefore, applying RIII to a minimal diagram yields anot
her diagram with the same (minimal) crossing number. The question reduces to: do
es the geometric configuration required for RIII exist on a minimal diagram?

### Part 1: No RIII on reduced alternating diagrams

Let $D$ be a reduced alternating diagram. By the Kauffman–Murasugi–Thistlethwait
e theorem, $D$ is a minimal diagram. We show $D$ contains no RIII-eligible confi
guration.

**RIII configuration requirement.** A RIII move requires a triangular region bou
nded by three arcs $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$, where each pair of arcs crosses at a
 crossing, and one arc (say $\alpha$) passes **over** both other arcs at its two
 crossing points (or **under** both). Crucially, $\alpha$ must go over (or under
) at two consecutive crossings along itself, with no intermediate crossing on $\
alpha$ between them.

**Alternating property.** In an alternating diagram, as one traverses the knot,
the over/under type alternates at each successive crossing: over, under, over, u
nder, and so on. Each arc of the triangle connects two crossings with no interme
diate crossings (since the triangle is a local region). By the alternating prope
rty, as the knot traverses an arc from one crossing to the next consecutive cros
sing, the over/under type switches. Therefore, each arc goes **over** at one end
point and **under** at the other.

**Conclusion.** Since no arc goes over at both endpoints (or under at both), the
 configuration required for RIII does not exist. Therefore, **no RIII move can b
e performed on a reduced alternating diagram**, and hence no RIII move can be pe
rformed on this minimal diagram.

### Part 2: RIII on non-alternating minimal diagrams

We exhibit non-alternating knots with minimal diagrams on which RIII can be perf
ormed.

**Non-alternating pretzel knots.** Consider the pretzel knot $P(a, b, -c)$ where
 $a, b, c \geq 3$ are all odd. This is a knot (all parameters odd) with a standa
rd pretzel diagram having $a + b + c$ crossings. The diagram is non-alternating
because the third twist region has opposite sign to the first two.

**Crossing number.** For pretzel knots $P(a,b,c)$ with all parameters odd and $|
a|,|b|,|c| \geq 2$, the crossing number equals $|a|+|b|+|c|$ (the standard pretz
el diagram is minimal; this follows from the span of the Kauffman bracket polyno
mial, which equals $|a|+|b|+|c|$ for these knots). Thus the standard diagram is
a minimal diagram.

**RIII-eligible configuration.** At the junction between the second twist region
 ($b$ positive twists) and the third twist region ($c$ negative twists), the alt
ernation breaks. In a positive twist region, the strands alternate over–under in
 one rotational direction; in a negative twist region, they alternate in the opp
osite rotational direction. At the junction, a strand that goes **over** at the
last crossing of the second region continues to go **over** at the first crossin
g of the third region, instead of switching to under. This creates two consecuti
ve over-crossings on the same strand.

At this junction, three strands meet and form a triangular region. The strand wi
th two consecutive over-crossings passes **over** both other strands. The other
two strands cross each other at a crossing within the junction, completing the t
riangle with three crossings. This is precisely the RIII-eligible configuration:
 one strand over both others, with the other two strands crossing at the third v
ertex.

Therefore, **a RIII move can be performed on the minimal diagram of $P(a, b, -c)
$**.

### Conclusion

A Reidemeister III move **cannot** be performed on a reduced alternating minimal
 diagram, because the alternating property forces each arc to go over at one end
 and under at the other, preventing the RIII configuration. A Reidemeister III m
ove **can** be performed on a non-alternating minimal diagram, as demonstrated b
y non-alternating pretzel knots where the break in alternation at twist-region j
unctions creates the required triangular configuration with one strand passing o
ver both others.

**Answer:** A Reidemeister III move can be performed on a knot with a minimal nu

eir minimal diagrams.
