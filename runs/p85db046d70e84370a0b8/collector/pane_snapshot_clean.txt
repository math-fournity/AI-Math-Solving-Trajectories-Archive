## Proof

**Claim.** Let $X$ be a set with two topologies $\tau_1 \subseteq \tau_2$ (i.e.,
 $\tau_2$ is finer than $\tau_1$, and $\tau_1$ is coarser than $\tau_2$). If $(X
, \tau_2)$ is compact, then $(X, \tau_1)$ is compact.

**Proof.**

Suppose $(X, \tau_2)$ is compact. We show that $(X, \tau_1)$ is compact.

Let $\{U_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in A}$ be an arbitrary open cover of $X$ in the topol
ogy $\tau_1$. That is, each $U_\alpha \in \tau_1$ and
$$X = \bigcup_{\alpha \in A} U_\alpha.$$

Since $\tau_1 \subseteq \tau_2$, every $U_\alpha \in \tau_1$ also satisfies $U_\
alpha \in \tau_2$. Therefore $\{U_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in A}$ is also an open cover
 of $X$ in the finer topology $\tau_2$.

By the compactness of $(X, \tau_2)$, there exists a finite subset $F \subseteq A
$ such that
$$X = \bigcup_{\alpha \in F} U_\alpha.$$

This finite subcover $\{U_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in F}$ consists entirely of sets fro
m $\tau_1$ (since each $U_\alpha \in \tau_1$ by assumption). Hence it is a finit
e subcover of the original cover in $\tau_1$.

Since every open cover of $(X, \tau_1)$ admits a finite subcover, $(X, \tau_1)$
is compact. $\blacksquare$

**Remark (converse fails).** The converse is false in general: compactness under
 a coarser topology does not imply compactness under a finer topology. For examp
le, let $X = [0,1]$. The trivial (indiscrete) topology $\tau_1 = \{\emptyset, X\
}$ is compact (every open cover contains $X$ itself), but the discrete topology
$\tau_2 = \mathcal{P}(X)$ on $[0,1]$ is finer and not compact (the cover $\{\{x\
} : x \in [0,1]\}$ has no finite subcover).

Thus the implication holds in exactly one direction: **finer-compact $\Rightarro
w$ coarser-compact**, but not conversely.

### PROOF COMPLETE
