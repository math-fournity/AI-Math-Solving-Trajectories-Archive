## Proof

**Setup.** A permutation decomposes uniquely as a set of disjoint cycles. We see
k the EGF
$$F(x) = \sum_{n \ge 0} a_n \frac{x^n}{n!}$$
where $a_n$ counts permutations of $[n]$ in which all cycle lengths are distinct
.

**Component EGF.** A single cycle of length $k$ on $k$ labeled elements: there a
re $(k-1)!$ such cycles, so its EGF contribution is
$$\frac{(k-1)!}{k!} x^k = \frac{x^k}{k}.$$

**The distinct-lengths constraint as a labeled product.** Ordinarily, the class
of all permutations is a SET of cycles, giving $\exp\!\bigl(\sum_{k\ge1} x^k/k\b
igr) = \frac{1}{1-x}$. The SET construction (exponential) permits any number of
cycles of each length.

The distinct-lengths condition changes the construction: for each length $k \ge
1$, we may include **at most one** cycle of length $k$. Since cycles of differen
t lengths are distinguishable by their length, the appropriate labeled construct
ion is a **labeled product** over the "sorts" $k = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$, where sort
$k$ contributes either nothing (EGF $1$) or exactly one cycle of length $k$ (EGF
 $x^k/k$).

In labeled combinatorics, the labeled product of structures from distinct sorts
multiplies their EGFs (the labels are distributed among the components). Therefo
re:

$$F(x) = \prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(1 + \frac{x^k}{k}\right).$$

**Verification for small $n$.**

- **$n=1$:** Only $(1)$, cycle lengths $\{1\}$ — distinct. $a_1 = 1$. The produc
t gives $x$ from the $k{=}1$ factor, so $[x^1/1!] = 1$. ✓

- **$n=2$:** $(1)(2)$ has lengths $\{1,1\}$ — not distinct; $(12)$ has length $\
{2\}$ — distinct. $a_2 = 1$. The $x^2$ coefficient in $(1+x)(1+x^2/2)\cdots$ is
$1/2$ (from the $k{=}2$ factor alone), giving $a_2 = 2!\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}=1$. ✓

- **$n=3$:** Valid: $(12)(3),(13)(2),(23)(1),(123),(132)$, so $a_3=5$. The $x^3$
 coefficient: from $k{=}1\times k{=}2$ gives $x\cdot x^2/2 = x^3/2$, and from $k
{=}3$ gives $x^3/3$. Total $x^3$ coeff $= 1/2+1/3=5/6$, so $a_3 = 6\cdot 5/6 = 5
$. ✓

- **$n=4$:** Valid cycle types with distinct parts: $\{4\}$ giving $(4{-}1)!=6$
permutations, and $\{3,1\}$ giving $\binom{4}{3}(3{-}1)!(1{-}1)!=8$ permutations
. Total $a_4=14$. The $x^4$ coefficient: from $k{=}4$ alone: $1/4$; from $k{=}1\
times k{=}3$: $1/3$. Total $= 1/4+1/3=7/12$, so $a_4=24\cdot 7/12=14$. ✓

**Convergence.** For $|x|<1$, the series $\sum_{k\ge1} x^k/k = -\log(1-x)$ conve
rges, so $\sum |x^k/k|<\infty$, which guarantees absolute convergence of the inf
inite product $\prod(1+x^k/k)$.

**Conclusion.** The exponential generating function of permutations with cycles
of distinct lengths is

$$\boxed{F(x) = \prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{x^k}{k}\right)}.$$
