## Solution

Let $\alpha = \sqrt{29}+\sqrt{21}$ and $\beta = \sqrt{29}-\sqrt{21}$.

**Step 1: Conjugate setup.** We have
$$\alpha\beta = 29-21 = 8, \qquad \alpha^2+\beta^2 = 2(29+21)=100, \qquad \alpha
^2\beta^2 = 64.$$
So $\alpha^2$ and $\beta^2$ are roots of
$$x^2 - 100x + 64 = 0.$$

**Step 2: Integer sequence.** Define $T_n = \alpha^{2n}+\beta^{2n} = (\alpha^2)^
n+(\beta^2)^n$. Then $T_n$ satisfies
$$T_n = 100\,T_{n-1} - 64\,T_{n-2},\qquad T_0=2,\; T_1=100.$$
Since $2012 = 2\cdot 1006$, we have $\alpha^{2012}+\beta^{2012} = T_{1006}\in\ma
thbb{Z}$.

**Step 3: Floor reduction.** Since $\beta = \sqrt{29}-\sqrt{21}\approx 0.802 \in

$$\lfloor \alpha^{2012}\rfloor = T_{1006}-1.$$

$$T_n \equiv -64\,T_{n-2} \equiv 36\,T_{n-2}\pmod{100}.$$

$$T_{2k}\equiv 2\cdot 36^k \pmod{100}.$$

and
$$\lfloor\alpha^{2012}\rfloor = T_{1006}-1 \equiv 12-1 = 11\pmod{100}.$$
