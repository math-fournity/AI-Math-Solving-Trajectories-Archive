## Proof that $K_0(k[x]) \cong \mathbb{Z}$

**Setup.** Let $k$ be a field and $R = k[x]$. The Grothendieck group $K_0(R)$ is
 defined as the Grothendieck completion of the monoid of isomorphism classes of
finitely generated projective $R$-modules under direct sum. Concretely, $K_0(R)$
 is the free abelian group on isomorphism classes $[P]$ of finitely generated pr
ojective $R$-modules, modulo the relations $[P \oplus Q] = [P] + [Q]$.

**Step 1: The rank map.** Define a map
$$\operatorname{rk}: K_0(k[x]) \to \mathbb{Z}$$
as follows. For any finitely generated projective $k[x]$-module $P$, set $\opera
torname{rk}([P]) = \dim_k(P \otimes_{k[x]} k)$, where $k$ is viewed as a $k[x]$-
module via the evaluation homomorphism $k[x] \to k$, $x \mapsto 0$ (i.e., $k \co
ng k[x]/(x)$).

This is well-defined: tensor product is right-exact and preserves direct sums, s
o $\operatorname{rk}([P \oplus Q]) = \operatorname{rk}([P]) + \operatorname{rk}(
[Q])$, which means the relation $[P \oplus Q] = [P] + [Q]$ is respected. Thus $\
operatorname{rk}$ descends to a well-defined group homomorphism $K_0(k[x]) \to \
mathbb{Z}$.

**Step 2: Surjectivity.** The free module $k[x]^n$ has $\operatorname{rk}([k[x]^
n]) = \dim_k(k^n) = n$. In particular, $\operatorname{rk}([k[x]]) = 1$, so $\ope
ratorname{rk}$ is surjective.

**Step 3: Every finitely generated projective $k[x]$-module is free (Quillen–Sus
lin).** The **Quillen–Suslin theorem** (the Serre conjecture, proved independent
ly by Quillen and Suslin in 1976) states:

> *Every finitely generated projective module over a polynomial ring $k[x_1, \do
ts, x_n]$ (over a field $k$, or more generally over a PID) is free.*

Applying this to the one-variable case $R = k[x]$: every finitely generated proj
ective $k[x]$-module $P$ is isomorphic to $k[x]^n$ for some $n \geq 0$.

**Step 4: Injectivity.** Since every finitely generated projective $k[x]$-module
 is free, every element of $K_0(k[x])$ is of the form $[k[x]^n] - [k[x]^m]$ for
some $n, m \geq 0$. Suppose $\operatorname{rk}([k[x]^n] - [k[x]^m]) = 0$, i.e.,
$n - m = 0$, i.e., $n = m$. Then $[k[x]^n] - [k[x]^m] = 0$ in $K_0(k[x])$. Hence
 $\ker(\operatorname{rk}) = 0$, and $\operatorname{rk}$ is injective.

**Conclusion.** The rank map $\operatorname{rk}: K_0(k[x]) \to \mathbb{Z}$ is a
group isomorphism. Therefore
$$K_0(k[x]) \cong \mathbb{Z}.$$

**Remark.** The key input is the Quillen–Suslin theorem, which collapses the cla
ssification of finitely generated projectives over $k[x]$ to the single integer
invariant of rank. Without it, one would only know that $K_0(k[x])$ surjects ont
o $\mathbb{Z}$ (via rank) but could not rule out the existence of non-free stabl
y-free or non-trivial projective modules contributing additional classes. The Qu
illen–Suslin theorem guarantees that no such modules exist, making the rank map
an isomorphism.
