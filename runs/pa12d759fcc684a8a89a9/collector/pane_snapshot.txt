## Step 2: Show $n = 2$ works

Let $\zeta = e^{2\pi i/7}$ (primitive 7th root of unity). Set $z_1 = \zeta$, $z_
2 = \zeta^2$.

Since $7$ is prime and $1 \leq k \leq 5$, we have $\gcd(k, 7) = 1$, so $z_1^k, z
_2^k \neq 1$ and are non-trivial 7th roots of unity.

By conjugate symmetry $|1 - \zeta^j| = |1 - \zeta^{7-j}|$, the distinct values a
re:
$$a = |1-\zeta| = 2\sin(\pi/7), \quad b = |1-\zeta^2| = 2\sin(2\pi/7), \quad c =
 |1-\zeta^3| = 2\sin(3\pi/7)$$

Computing the products for each $k$:

| $k$ | $z_1^k$ | $z_2^k$ | $\|1-z_1^k\| \cdot \|1-z_2^k\|$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $\zeta$ | $\zeta^2$ | $ab$ |
| 2 | $\zeta^2$ | $\zeta^4$ | $b \cdot c$ (since $\|1-\zeta^4\| = \|1-\zeta^3\|
= c$) |
| 3 | $\zeta^3$ | $\zeta^6$ | $c \cdot a$ (since $\|1-\zeta^6\| = \|1-\zeta\| =
a$) |
| 4 | $\zeta^4$ | $\zeta^1$ | $c \cdot a$ (since $\zeta^8 = \zeta$, $\|1-\zeta^4

| 5 | $\zeta^5$ | $\zeta^3$ | $b \cdot c$ (since $\|1-\zeta^5\| = \|1-\zeta^2\|
= b$, $\zeta^{10} = \zeta^3$) |

So we need $ab > 1$, $bc > 1$, and $ca > 1$.

**Rigorous verification:**

Using $\sin(\pi/7) > \sin(\pi/8)$, $\sin(2\pi/7) > \sin(\pi/4) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}
{2}$, and $\sin(3\pi/7) > \sin(\pi/3) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$:

All products exceed 1, so $n = 2$ suffices.
