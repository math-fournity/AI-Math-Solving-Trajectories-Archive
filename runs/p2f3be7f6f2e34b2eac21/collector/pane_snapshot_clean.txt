## Solution

**Claim.** Let $R$ be a ring and let $M_1 \subseteq M_2 \subseteq M_3 \subseteq
\cdots$ be an ascending chain of $R$-submodules of some module. Let $M = \bigcup
_{i=1}^{\infty} M_i$. Then $M$ is finitely generated **if and only if** the chai
n stabilizes at a finitely generated stage, i.e., there exists $N \geq 1$ such t
hat $M_N = M_{N+1} = \cdots = M$ and $M_N$ is finitely generated.

In particular, **if $M$ is finitely generated, the chain must stabilize** — a st
rictly ascending chain can never have a finitely generated union.

---

### Proof

**($\Rightarrow$) Finite generation forces stabilization.**

Suppose $M$ is finitely generated, with generators $x_1, \dots, x_n$. Since $M =
 \bigcup_i M_i$, each generator $x_j$ belongs to some $M_{i_j}$ (the chain is ex
haustive, so every element of $M$ lands in some term). Set

$$N = \max\{i_1, \dots, i_n\}.$$

Because the chain is ascending, $M_{i_j} \subseteq M_N$ for every $j$, so every
generator $x_j \in M_N$. Therefore

$$M = \langle x_1, \dots, x_n \rangle \subseteq M_N \subseteq M.$$

The inclusion $M_N \subseteq M$ is automatic (each $M_i \subseteq M$). Hence $M
= M_N$, and consequently $M_N = M_{N+k}$ for all $k \geq 0$: the chain stabilize
s at index $N$. Moreover $M_N = M$ is finitely generated by hypothesis. $\checkm
ark$

**($\Leftarrow$) Stabilization at a finitely generated stage gives a finitely ge
nerated union.**

Suppose there exists $N$ with $M_N = M_{N+1} = \cdots$, so that $M = M_N$, and $
M_N$ is finitely generated (say by $y_1, \dots, y_m$). Then $M = M_N = \langle y
_1, \dots, y_m \rangle$ is finitely generated. $\checkmark$

---

### Consequences and sharpness

1. **A strictly ascending chain never has a finitely generated union.** If the c
hain does not stabilize — i.e., for every $i$ there exists $j > i$ with $M_i \su
bsetneq M_j$ — then by the forward direction $M$ cannot be finitely generated.

2. **Both outcomes are possible:**
   - *Finitely generated union:* Take $M_i = R^n$ for all $i$ (constant chain).
The union is $R^n$, finitely generated.
   - *Non-finitely generated union (chain stabilizes at a non-f.g. stage):* Over
 a non-Noetherian ring $R$, let $I \subseteq R$ be an ideal that is not finitely
 generated, and set $M_i = I$ for all $i$. The union is $I$, not finitely genera
ted.
   - *Non-finitely generated union (chain does not stabilize):* Over $R = \mathb
b{Z}$, let $M_i = \langle \tfrac{1}{p_1}, \dots, \tfrac{1}{p_i} \rangle \subsete
q \mathbb{Q}$ where $p_1, p_2, \dots$ are the primes. This is a strictly ascendi
ng chain in the $\mathbb{Z}$-module $\mathbb{Q}$, and the union $\bigcup_i M_i$
is not finitely generated (it is not even cyclic, since no single fraction gener
ates all $\frac{1}{p}$).

3. **Noetherian connection.** Over a Noetherian ring, every submodule of a finit
ely generated module is finitely generated, and every ascending chain stabilizes
. Thus for chains inside a finitely generated module over a Noetherian ring, the
 union is always finitely generated (and the chain always stabilizes). The forwa
rd direction proved above is, in fact, the key lemma underlying the equivalence
"Noetherian $\iff$ every ascending chain of ideals stabilizes": if an ideal $I =
 \bigcup I_i$ were generated by finitely many elements, those generators would a
ll lie in some $I_N$, forcing $I = I_N$.

---

**Summary.** The union of an ascending chain $M_1 \subseteq M_2 \subseteq \cdots
$ is finitely generated if and only if the chain stabilizes at a finitely genera
ted term. The essential mechanism: finitely many generators can each be "caught"
 at some finite stage, and the largest such stage then contains all generators,
hence equals the entire union — forcing stabilization.
