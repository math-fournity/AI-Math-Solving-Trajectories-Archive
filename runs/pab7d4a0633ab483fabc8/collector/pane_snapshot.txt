## Proof

**Coordinate setup.** Place $O=(0,0)$, $B=(a,0)$, $A=(a,b)$ with $a,b>0$, so the
 right angle is at $B$. Let $D=a^{2}+b^{2}$. The line $OB$ is the $x$-axis, and
the line $OA$ has equation $bx-ay=0$.

Let the circle have center $C=(c,0)$ on $OB$ (take $c>0$). Since it is tangent t
o $OA$, its radius is
$$r=\frac{|bc|}{\sqrt{D}}=\frac{bc}{\sqrt{D}},\qquad r^{2}=\frac{b^{2}c^{2}}{D}.
$$

**The two tangents from $A$.** A line through $A=(a,b)$ of slope $m$ has equatio
n $mx-y+(b-ma)=0$. Its distance from $C=(c,0)$ equals $r$ iff
$$\bigl(m(c-a)+b\bigr)^{2}=r^{2}(m^{2}+1).$$
Expanding and using $r^{2}=b^{2}c^{2}/D$ gives the quadratic in $m$:
$$m^{2}Q+2mb(c-a)+P=0,\qquad Q=(c-a)^{2}-r^{2},\;\; P=b^{2}-r^{2}. \tag{1}$$
One root is $m_{1}=b/a$ (the slope of $OA$); let $m_{2}$ be the other root — the
 slope of $AT$. Set $m:=m_{2}$ and
$$V=m^{2}+1,\qquad W=am+b,\qquad S=m(c-a)+b,\qquad H=D-ac.$$
Note $S^{2}=r^{2}V$ (this is exactly the tangency condition). The line $AT$ is $
y=mx+(b-ma)$.

**The median and point $M$.** The midpoint of $OA$ is $(a/2,b/2)$, so the median
 from $B$ has equation $bx+ay=ab$. Intersecting with $AT$:
$$M=\left(\frac{a^{2}m}{W},\;\frac{b^{2}}{W}\right).$$
Then
$$|MB|^{2}=\left(\frac{a^{2}m}{W}-a\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{b^{2}}{W}\right)^{2}=
\frac{a^{2}b^{2}+b^{4}}{W^{2}}=\frac{b^{2}D}{W^{2}}. \tag{2}$$

**The point $T$.** Since $T$ is the foot of the perpendicular from $C$ to $AT$,
$$T=\left(c-\frac{mS}{V},\;\frac{S}{V}\right).$$
Both $M$ and $T$ lie on $AT$, where $y=mx+(b-ma)$, so the distance between them
is $|MT|^{2}=V\,(x_{M}-x_{T})^{2}$. Now
$$x_{M}-x_{T}=\frac{a^{2}m}{W}-c+\frac{mS}{V}=\frac{(ma(a-c)-bc)V+mSW}{WV}.$$
Expanding the numerator (using $u:=c-a$, so $S=mu+b$, $W=am+b$):
\begin{align*}
E&:=\bigl(ma(a-c)-bc\bigr)V+mSW\\
&=(-mau-bc)(m^{2}+1)+m(mu+b)(am+b)\\
&=m^{2}\underbrace{(-bc+ub+ab)}_{=\,0}+m(b^{2}-au)-bc\\
&=m\bigl(b^{2}-a(c-a)\bigr)-bc=m(D-ac)-bc=mH-bc,
\end{align*}
since $-bc+ub+ab=-bc+(c-a)b+ab=0$. Thus
$$|MT|^{2}=\frac{E^{2}}{W^{2}V},\qquad E=mH-bc. \tag{3}$$

**Key identity: $E^{2}=b^{2}DV$.** Compute
$$E^{2}-b^{2}DV=m^{2}\bigl(H^{2}-b^{2}D\bigr)-2mbcH+b^{2}(c^{2}-D). \tag{4}$$
Two substitutions (both verified by direct expansion):
- $H^{2}-b^{2}D=(D-ac)^{2}-b^{2}D=QD$,
- $b^{2}(c^{2}-D)=-b^{2}(D-c^{2})=-PD$ (since $P=b^{2}(D-c^{2})/D$).

So (4) becomes $E^{2}-b^{2}DV=D(m^{2}Q-P)-2mbcH$. From the quadratic (1), $m^{2}
Q=-2mb(c-a)-P$, hence $m^{2}Q-P=-2mb(c-a)-2P$, and
$$E^{2}-b^{2}DV=-2mb\bigl[D(c-a)+cH\bigr]-2PD.$$
Using $m=b/a\cdot m_{1}$ is not needed directly; instead use $m_{1}m_{2}=P/Q$ wi
th $m_{1}=b/a$, giving $mb=aP/Q$. Also $D(c-a)+cH=D(c-a)+c(D-ac)=2cD-aD-ac^{2}$,
 and
$$a(2cD-aD-ac^{2})+DQ=\bigl(2acD-a^{2}D-a^{2}c^{2}\bigr)+\bigl(a^{2}c^{2}-2acD+a
^{2}D\bigr)=0,$$
where $DQ=a^{2}c^{2}-2acD+a^{2}D$ is checked by expanding $D\bigl((c-a)^{2}-r^{2
}\bigr)$. Therefore $a(2cD-aD-ac^{2})=-DQ$, and
$$E^{2}-b^{2}DV=-2\cdot\frac{aP}{Q}\cdot\frac{-DQ}{a}-2PD=2PD-2PD=0.$$
Hence $E^{2}=b^{2}DV$.

**Conclusion.** From (2) and (3),
$$|MT|^{2}=\frac{E^{2}}{W^{2}V}=\frac{b^{2}DV}{W^{2}V}=\frac{b^{2}D}{W^{2}}=|MB|
^{2}.$$
Therefore $MB=MT$, as was to be proved. $\blacksquare$
