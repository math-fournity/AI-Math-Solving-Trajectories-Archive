## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. The order of every element of an affine algebraic group over $k
= \overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$ is finite.

**Key fact.** The field $k = \overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$ is the directed union of i
ts finite subfields:
$$k = \bigcup_{m \geq 1} \mathbb{F}_{p^m}.$$
Consequently, any finite tuple of elements of $k$ lies in a single finite subfie
ld $\mathbb{F}_{p^m}$.

**Setup.** Let $G$ be an affine algebraic group over $k$. Since $G$ is affine, i
t admits a faithful representation: there exists $n \geq 1$ and a closed embeddi
ng of algebraic groups $G \hookrightarrow \mathrm{GL}_{n,k}$ over $k$ (every aff
ine algebraic group over a field embeds into some $\mathrm{GL}_n$; this is a sta
ndard theorem, e.g. via the coordinate algebra being a Hopf algebra of finite ty
pe, yielding a faithful comodule on a finite-dimensional vector space).

**Argument.** Let $g \in G(k)$ be any element. Under the embedding $G \hookright
arrow \mathrm{GL}_{n,k}$, the element $g$ is an $n \times n$ invertible matrix w
ith entries in $k$. This matrix has finitely many entries (at most $n^2$), and e
ach entry lies in $k = \overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$. By the key fact above, there ex
ists $m \geq 1$ such that all entries of $g$ lie in $\mathbb{F}_{p^m} \subset k$
.

Therefore $g \in G(\mathbb{F}_{p^m})$, where we identify
$$G(\mathbb{F}_{p^m}) = G(k) \cap \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_{p^m})$$
via the embedding. Now:

- $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_{p^m})$ is a finite group (its order is $\prod_{i=0}
^{n-1}(p^{mn} - p^{mi})$).
- $G(\mathbb{F}_{p^m})$ is a subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_{p^m})$, henc
e is itself finite.

Since $g \in G(\mathbb{F}_{p^m})$ and $G(\mathbb{F}_{p^m})$ is a finite group, t
he cyclic subgroup $\langle g \rangle$ is finite. In particular, $g$ has finite
order (dividing $|G(\mathbb{F}_{p^m})|$, which in turn divides $|\mathrm{GL}_n(\
mathbb{F}_{p^m})|$).

**Conclusion.** Every element of $G(k)$ has finite order. $\blacksquare$
