## Proof

**Setup.** There are $N = 1{,}000{,}000$ piles, each containing $1996$ coins. Ex
actly one pile consists entirely of fake coins (weight $9$ g each); all other pi
les contain only real coins (weight $10$ g each). We have a one-pan scale that r
eports total weight with $1$-gram accuracy. We wish to find the minimum number o
f weighings needed to identify the fake pile.

**Key observation.** In any single weighing, suppose we take $c_i$ coins from pi
le $i$ (where $0 \le c_i \le 1996$, since each pile has only $1996$ coins). If p
ile $j$ is the fake pile, the total weight is

$$W = \sum_{i} 10\,c_i \;-\; c_j,$$

because every coin from pile $j$ is $1$ g lighter than a real coin. The "expecte
d" weight (if all coins were real) is $E = \sum_i 10\,c_i$, which we know exactl
y since we chose the $c_i$. Therefore the **deficit** $E - W = c_j$ is determine
d by the single weighing. To identify the fake pile from this deficit alone, the
 values $c_1, c_2, \dots, c_N$ must be **pairwise distinct**.

**One weighing is not enough.** Each $c_i$ is an integer in $\{0, 1, \dots, 1996
\}$, giving at most $1997$ distinct values. But we have $N = 1{,}000{,}000$ pile
s, and $1997 < 1{,}000{,}000$. By the pigeonhole principle, at least two piles m
ust share the same $c_i$ value, so a single weighing cannot distinguish all pile
s. Hence at least **two** weighings are required.

**Two weighings suffice.** Assign to each pile $i$ a pair $(a_i, b_i)$ with $0 \
le a_i \le 1996$ and $0 \le b_i \le 1996$, choosing the pairs to be **pairwise d
istinct** across all $N$ piles. This is possible because the number of available
 pairs is

$$1997 \times 1997 = 3{,}988{,}009 \;\ge\; 1{,}000{,}000 = N.$$

- **Weighing 1:** Take $a_i$ coins from pile $i$ for every $i$. The deficit equa
ls $a_j$, revealing the first coordinate of the fake pile.
- **Weighing 2:** Take $b_i$ coins from pile $i$ for every $i$. The deficit equa
ls $b_j$, revealing the second coordinate.

Since all pairs $(a_i, b_i)$ are distinct, the pair $(a_j, b_j)$ uniquely identi
fies pile $j$.

**Conclusion.** One weighing cannot suffice (only $1997$ distinct signals for $1
{,}000{,}000$ piles), while two weighings suffice (providing $1997^2 = 3{,}988{,
}009 > 1{,}000{,}000$ distinct signals). Therefore the minimum number of weighin
gs is:

$$\boxed{2}$$
