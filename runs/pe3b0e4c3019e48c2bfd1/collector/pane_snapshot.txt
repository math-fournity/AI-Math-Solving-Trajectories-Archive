**Claim:** Yes, $co^{-1}(V)$ is open in $\mathcal{F}$.

**Proof.**

We show that the convex-hull map $co\colon \mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{C}$ is continu
ous (in fact $1$-Lipschitz) with respect to the Hausdorff metric. The result the
n follows immediately, since the preimage of an open set under a continuous map
is open.

Let $d_H$ denote the Hausdorff metric on compact subsets of $\mathbb{R}^n$, and
let $B$ denote the closed Euclidean unit ball. Recall that for compact sets $A_1
,A_2\subset\mathbb{R}^n$,

$$d_H(A_1,A_2)\le \varepsilon \iff A_1\subset A_2+\varepsilon B \;\text{ and }\;
 A_2\subset A_1+\varepsilon B.$$

**Key inequality.** For any compact sets $A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb{R}^n$,

$$d_H\!\bigl(\operatorname{co}(A_1),\,\operatorname{co}(A_2)\bigr)\;\le\; d_H(A_
1,A_2).$$

*Proof of the inequality.* Set $\varepsilon = d_H(A_1,A_2)$. Then $A_1\subset A_
2+\varepsilon B$. Taking convex hulls of both sides and using the fact that $B$
is convex:

$$\operatorname{co}(A_1)\;\subset\;\operatorname{co}(A_2+\varepsilon B).$$

Now every point of $\operatorname{co}(A_2+\varepsilon B)$ is a convex combinatio
n $\sum_i \lambda_i(a_i+\varepsilon b_i)$ with $a_i\in A_2$, $b_i\in B$, $\lambd
a_i\ge 0$, $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$. Rewriting,

$$\sum_i \lambda_i a_i \;+\; \varepsilon\sum_i \lambda_i b_i.$$

The first term lies in $\operatorname{co}(A_2)$ and the second lies in $\varepsi
lon B$ (since $B$ is convex). Hence

$$\operatorname{co}(A_1)\;\subset\;\operatorname{co}(A_2)+\varepsilon B.$$

By symmetry (exchanging $A_1,A_2$),

$$\operatorname{co}(A_2)\;\subset\;\operatorname{co}(A_1)+\varepsilon B.$$

Therefore $d_H(\operatorname{co}(A_1),\operatorname{co}(A_2))\le\varepsilon = d_
H(A_1,A_2)$. $\square$

**Application.** Every $F\in\mathcal{F}$ is a finite (hence compact) subset of $
\mathbb{R}^n$, and $\operatorname{co}(F)$ is a compact convex polytope, hence an
 element of $\mathcal{C}$. The Hausdorff metric on $\mathcal{F}$ (resp.\ $\mathc
al{C}$) is the restriction of $d_H$ to finite sets (resp.\ convex hulls of finit
e sets). The key inequality applies to all compact sets, so in particular to ele
ments of $\mathcal{F}$, giving

$$d_H\!\bigl(co(F_1),\,co(F_2)\bigr)\;\le\;d_H(F_1,F_2)\qquad\forall\,F_1,F_2\in
\mathcal{F}.$$

Thus $co\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{C}$ is $1$-Lipschitz, hence continuous.

**Conclusion.** Let $V\subset\mathcal{C}$ be open. Since $co$ is continuous, $co
^{-1}(V)=\{F\in\mathcal{F}:co(F)\in V\}$ is open in $\mathcal{F}$. $\blacksquare
$
