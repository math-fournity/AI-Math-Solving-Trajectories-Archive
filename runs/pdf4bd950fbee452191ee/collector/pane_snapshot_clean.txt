## Solution

I'll work in the complex plane, identifying $(x,y)$ with $z = x + iy$. Let $\ome
ga = e^{i\theta} = e^{2\pi i/n}$, a primitive $n$-th root of unity.

**Rotation formula.** A counterclockwise rotation by angle $\theta$ about a poin
t $p$ acts as
$$R_p(z) = p + \omega(z - p) = \omega z + (1-\omega)p.$$

Since $P_k = (k,0)$ corresponds to the complex number $k$, we have
$$R_k(z) = \omega z + (1-\omega)\,k.$$

**Composing the rotations.** Let $z_0 = z$ and $z_k = R_k(z_{k-1})$ for $k = 1,\
dots,n$, so $R(z) = z_n$. A simple induction gives
$$z_k = \omega^k z + (1-\omega)\sum_{j=1}^{k} j\,\omega^{k-j}.$$

For $k = n$, since $\omega^n = 1$:
$$R(z) = z + (1-\omega)\underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^{n} j\,\omega^{n-j}}_{S}.$$

**Evaluating the sum.** Substitute $m = n-j$ (so $j = n-m$, $m$ runs $0$ to $n-1
$):
$$S = \sum_{m=0}^{n-1}(n-m)\,\omega^m = n\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\omega^m - \sum_{m=0}^{
n-1} m\,\omega^m.$$

Since $n \ge 2$ and $\omega$ is a primitive $n$-th root of unity, $\sum_{m=0}^{n
-1}\omega^m = 0$, so
$$S = -\sum_{m=0}^{n-1} m\,\omega^m.$$

To evaluate $T = \sum_{m=0}^{n-1} m\,\omega^m$, differentiate the geometric iden
tity $\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}x^m = \frac{1-x^n}{1-x}$ and multiply by $x$:
$$\sum_{m=0}^{n-1} m\,x^m = \frac{-n\,x^n(1-x) + x(1-x^n)}{(1-x)^2}.$$
Setting $x = \omega$ (so $\omega^n = 1$):
$$T = \frac{-n\,\omega^n(1-\omega)}{(1-\omega)^2} = \frac{-n}{1-\omega}.$$

Therefore $S = -T = \dfrac{n}{1-\omega}$, and
$$R(z) = z + (1-\omega)\cdot\frac{n}{1-\omega} = z + n.$$

**Conclusion.** The translation $z \mapsto z + n$ corresponds to
$$\boxed{R(x,y) = (x+n,\; y)}.$$

**Verification ($n=2$).** Here $\theta = \pi$, $\omega = -1$. $R_1(x,y) = (2-x,-
y)$, then $R_2(2-x,-y) = (4-(2-x),\, -(-y)) = (x+2,\, y) = (x+n,\,y)$. ✓

The composition of all $n$ rotations is simply a translation by $n$ units in the
 $x$-direction.
