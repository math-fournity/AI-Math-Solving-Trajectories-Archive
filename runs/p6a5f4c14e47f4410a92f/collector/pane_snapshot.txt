## Proof

**Claim.** The necessary and sufficient condition is that **$x_0$ be an accumula
tion point of $A \cap B$**.

---

### Sufficiency

Suppose $x_0$ is an accumulation point of $A \cap B$. We use the standard fact:

> **Lemma.** If $\lim_{x \to x_0,\, x \in A} f(x) = L$ exists and $E \subseteq A
$ is a set for which $x_0$ is an accumulation point, then $\lim_{x \to x_0,\, x
\in E} f(x) = L$.

*Proof of Lemma.* For every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is $\delta > 0$ with $|f(x)
- L| < \varepsilon$ for all $x \in A$ satisfying $0 < |x - x_0| < \delta$. Since
 $E \subseteq A$, the same $\delta$ works for $x \in E$. ∎

Now set $L_f = \lim_{x \to x_0} f(x)$ and $L_g = \lim_{x \to x_0} g(x)$. Since $
x_0$ is an accumulation point of $A \cap B \subseteq A$, the Lemma gives

$$\lim_{x \to x_0,\, x \in A \cap B} f(x) = L_f.$$

Since $x_0$ is an accumulation point of $A \cap B \subseteq B$, the Lemma gives

$$\lim_{x \to x_0,\, x \in A \cap B} g(x) = L_g.$$

But $f = g$ on $A \cap B$, so the two limits on the left are limits of the *same
* function restricted to the *same* set, hence equal. Therefore $L_f = L_g$. ∎

---

### Necessity

Suppose $x_0$ is **not** an accumulation point of $A \cap B$. We exhibit a count
erexample.

Take $x_0 = 0$ and
$$A = \left\{\tfrac{1}{2n} : n \ge 1\right\}, \qquad B = \left\{\tfrac{1}{2n+1}
: n \ge 1\right\}.$$

Then $0$ is an accumulation point of both $A$ and $B$ (each contains points arbi
trarily close to $0$), yet $A \cap B = \varnothing$, so $0$ is not an accumulati
on point of $A \cap B$.

Define $f(x) = 0$ for $x \in A$ and $g(x) = 1$ for $x \in B$. The agreement cond
ition holds vacuously on $A \cap B = \varnothing$. Both limits exist:

$$\lim_{x \to 0} f(x) = 0, \qquad \lim_{x \to 0} g(x) = 1,$$

yet $0 \neq 1$. Hence whenever the desired conclusion holds for *all* such $f, g
$, it must be that $x_0$ is an accumulation point of $A \cap B$. ∎

---

### Conclusion

The condition on $A$ and $B$ that is both necessary and sufficient is:

> **$x_0$ is an accumulation point of $A \cap B$.**

Under this hypothesis (together with the given hypotheses that $x_0$ is an accum
ulation point of each of $A$ and $B$, that $f$ and $g$ agree on $A \cap B$, and
that both limits exist), the two limits are forced to be equal; and without this
 hypothesis one can construct examples where the two limits exist but differ.
