**Proof.**

Recall that for $z = x + iy$ with $x = \Re z$, $y = \Im z$, we have $z^2 = (x^2
- y^2) + 2ixy$, so
$$\Re(z^2) = (\Re z)^2 - (\Im z)^2.$$

The given bound is therefore
$$|f(z)| \le e^{\Re(z^2)} \quad \text{for all } z \in \mathbb{C}.$$

Since the bound holds for all $z \in \mathbb{C}$, $f$ is entire. Define
$$g(z) = f(z)\, e^{-z^2}.$$
As a product of entire functions, $g$ is entire. Its modulus satisfies
$$|g(z)| = |f(z)|\cdot |e^{-z^2}| = |f(z)|\, e^{-\Re(z^2)} \le e^{\Re(z^2)} \cdo
t e^{-\Re(z^2)} = 1$$
for every $z \in \mathbb{C}$.

Thus $g$ is a bounded entire function. By Liouville's theorem, $g$ is constant.
Evaluating at the origin,
$$g(0) = f(0)\, e^{0} = 0 \cdot 1 = 0.$$
Hence $g \equiv 0$, and since $e^{-z^2}$ never vanishes, we conclude
$$f(z) = 0 \quad \text{for all } z \in \mathbb{C}. \qquad \blacksquare$$
