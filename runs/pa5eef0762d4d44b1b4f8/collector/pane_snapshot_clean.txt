## Solution

We solve the wave equation on the disk $r \le a$ in polar coordinates,
$$u_{tt}=c^{2}\nabla^{2}u,\qquad \nabla^{2}u=u_{rr}+\tfrac{1}{r}u_{r}+\tfrac{1}{
r^{2}}u_{\theta\theta},$$
with $u(a,\theta,t)=0$, $u(r,\theta,0)=0$, $u_{t}(r,\theta,0)=\alpha(r)\sin 3\th
eta$.

### Separation of variables

Set $u=R(r)\Theta(\theta)T(t)$. The angular equation $\Theta''+n^{2}\Theta=0$ wi
th $2\pi$-periodicity forces $n\in\mathbb Z$. Since both initial conditions are
proportional to $\sin 3\theta$, only the $n=3$ mode is excited, so we take $\The
ta(\theta)=\sin 3\theta$ throughout.

The radial equation (finite at $r=0$) is Bessel's equation of order $3$:
$$R''+\frac{1}{r}R'+\Big(\lambda-\frac{9}{r^{2}}\Big)R=0,\qquad R(r)=J_{3}(\sqrt
{\lambda}\,r).$$
The boundary condition $R(a)=0$ requires $J_{3}(\sqrt{\lambda}\,a)=0$, i.e. $\sq
rt{\lambda}\,a=j_{3,k}$, the $k$-th positive zero of $J_{3}$. Thus
$$\lambda_{k}=\frac{j_{3,k}^{2}}{a^{2}},\qquad \omega_{k}=c\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}=\f
rac{c\,j_{3,k}}{a},\qquad k=1,2,\dots$$

The time equation $T''+\omega_{k}^{2}T=0$ gives $T_{k}(t)=A_{k}\cos\omega_{k}t+B
_{k}\sin\omega_{k}t$.

### General form of the solution

$$u(r,\theta,t)=\sin 3\theta\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigl[A_{k}\cos\omega_{k}t+B_{k}\
sin\omega_{k}t\bigr]J_{3}\!\Big(\frac{j_{3,k}}{a}r\Big).$$

### Applying the initial conditions

**Displacement:** $u(r,\theta,0)=0$ gives $\sum_k A_k J_3(j_{3,k}r/a)=0$ for all
 $r$, hence $A_k=0$ for every $k$.

**Velocity:** $u_t(r,\theta,0)=\alpha(r)\sin 3\theta$ gives
$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}B_{k}\,\omega_{k}\,J_{3}\!\Big(\frac{j_{3,k}}{a}r\Big)=\alp
ha(r).$$
This is a Fourier–Bessel expansion of $\alpha(r)$ in the basis $\{J_3(j_{3,k}r/a
)\}_{k\ge1}$, which is orthogonal on $[0,a]$ with weight $r$. The coefficients a
re
$$C_k:=B_k\omega_k=\frac{2}{a^{2}\,J_{4}(j_{3,k})^{2}}\int_{0}^{a}\alpha(r)\,J_{
3}\!\Big(\frac{j_{3,k}}{a}r\Big)\,r\,dr,$$
using the standard identity $\int_0^a J_3(j_{3,k}r/a)^2\,r\,dr=\tfrac{a^{2}}{2}J
_4(j_{3,k})^2$ (consequence of $\int_0^1 x J_n(j_{n,k}x)^2 dx=\tfrac12 J_{n+1}(j
_{n,k})^2$).

Therefore $B_k=C_k/\omega_k=\dfrac{a\,C_k}{c\,j_{3,k}}$.

### Final answer

$$\boxed{\;u(r,\theta,t)=\sin 3\theta\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{a}{c\,j_{3,k}}\,C_
k\,\sin\!\Big(\frac{c\,j_{3,k}}{a}t\Big)\,J_{3}\!\Big(\frac{j_{3,k}}{a}r\Big)\;}
$$

where $j_{3,k}$ is the $k$-th positive zero of the Bessel function $J_3$, and

$$C_k=\frac{2}{a^{2}\,J_{4}(j_{3,k})^{2}}\int_{0}^{a}\alpha(r)\,J_{3}\!\Big(\fra
c{j_{3,k}}{a}r\Big)\,r\,dr.$$

**Verification.** Each term satisfies the wave equation (product of eigenfunctio
ns of the Laplacian with the corresponding time harmonic), the boundary conditio
n $u(a,\theta,t)=0$ holds because $J_3(j_{3,k})=0$, the initial displacement van
ishes because every term carries a factor $\sin(\omega_k\cdot 0)=0$, and the ini
tial velocity reduces to the Fourier–Bessel series of $\alpha(r)$ times $\sin 3\
theta$ by construction.
