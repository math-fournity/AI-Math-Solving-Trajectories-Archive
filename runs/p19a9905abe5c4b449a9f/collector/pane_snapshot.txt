## Proof

Let $p(x) = x^3 + x^2 + x + 1$. Observe the factorization
$$p(x) = (x+1)(x^2+1) = (x+1)(x-i)(x+i),$$
so the zero set of $p$ in $\mathbb{C}$ is $Z(p) = \{-1,\, i,\, -i\}$.

**Step 1: $\sigma(T) \subseteq Z(p)$.**

Since $T$ is bounded, $p(T) = T^3 + T^2 + T + I = 0$. By the spectral mapping th
eorem for polynomials,
$$p(\sigma(T)) = \sigma(p(T)) = \sigma(0) = \{0\}.$$
Hence for every $\lambda \in \sigma(T)$, we have $p(\lambda) = 0$, i.e. $\lambda
 \in Z(p) = \{-1, i, -i\}$.

**Step 2: $\sigma(T) \subseteq \mathbb{R}$.**

This is the standard fact that the spectrum of a bounded self-adjoint operator o
n a complex Hilbert space is real. (Briefly: if $\lambda = a + ib \in \sigma(T)$
 with $b \neq 0$, then $(T - \lambda I)^*(T-\lambda I) = (T-aI)^2 + b^2 I$ is bo
unded below by $b^2 I > 0$, forcing $T - \lambda I$ to be invertible—a contradic
tion.)

**Step 3: Combine.**

From Steps 1 and 2,
$$\sigma(T) \subseteq Z(p) \cap \mathbb{R} = \{-1,\, i,\, -i\} \cap \mathbb{R} =
 \{-1\}.$$

**Step 4: $\sigma(T) \neq \emptyset$.**

The spectrum of any bounded operator on a nonzero complex Hilbert space is nonem
pty (a consequence of the resolvent being a bounded entire function that vanishe
s at infinity, hence identically zero if the spectrum were empty—contradicting $
(\lambda I - T)^{-1} \to 0$ as $|\lambda|\to\infty$). If $H = \{0\}$ is the zero
 space, the spectrum is empty by convention, but the problem is trivial in that
degenerate case; for the nontrivial case $H \neq \{0\}$, the spectrum is nonempt
y.

Therefore $\sigma(T) = \{-1\}$.

**Consistency check.** With $\sigma(T) = \{-1\}$, the spectral theorem for bound
ed self-adjoint operators gives $T = -I$. Indeed $(-I)^3 + (-I)^2 + (-I) + I = -
I + I - I + I = 0$, confirming consistency.

$$\boxed{\sigma(T) = \{-1\}}$$
