Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum number of singularities on t
he unit circle for a power series with a specific gap condition on its coefficie
nts.

## Setup and Key Identity

Given: $f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n z^n$ with radius of convergence 1, where
$c_n = 0$ whenever $n = km + l$ for fixed $m \in \mathbb{N}$, $0 \le l < m$, and
 integer $k \ge 2$.

**Note:** $m \geq 2$ is forced, since $m=1$ makes $f$ a polynomial (radius $\inf
ty$, contradiction).

Using the root-of-unity filter with $\omega_j = e^{2\pi i j/m}$:

$$\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1} \omega_j^{-l} f(\omega_j z) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
c_{km+l} z^{km+l} = c_l z^l + c_{m+l} z^{m+l}$$

since $c_{km+l} = 0$ for $k \geq 2$. The right side is a **polynomial**, so:

$$F(z) := \sum_{j=0}^{m-1} \omega_j^{-l} f(\omega_j z) \text{ is entire.} \quad
(\star)$$

## Lower Bound: $|S| \geq 2$

The orbits $\{\omega_j \zeta : j=0,\ldots,m-1\}$ partition the unit circle into
sets of size $m$.

Suppose $z_0 \in S$ and **no other point** in its orbit belongs to $S$. Then in
$F(z)$, the only singular contribution at $z = z_0$ comes from the $j=0$ term: $
\omega_0^{-l}\sigma_{z_0}(z) = \sigma_{z_0}(z) \neq 0$. This contradicts $(\star
)$.

Therefore **every singularity requires at least one partner in its orbit**, givi
ng $|S| \geq 2$.

## Construction Achieving $|S| = 2$

**Case $m = 2$:** Take $l=0$, $f(z) = \frac{z}{1-z^2} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} z^{2
k+1}$. Then $c_{2k}=0$ for all $k$ (so $c_{2k}=0$ for $k\geq 2$ ✓), radius is 1,
 and $S = \{1, -1\}$, so $|S|=2$.

**Case $m \geq 3$:** Decompose $f(z) = c_l z^l + c_{m+l}z^{m+l} + \sum_{r \neq l
} z^r g_r(z^m)$ where $g_r(w) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} c_{km+r} w^k$.

Set $g_r(w) = \frac{a_r}{1-w}$ for each $r \neq l$, so:
$$f(z) = \frac{P(z)}{1-z^m}, \quad P(z) = \sum_{r \neq l} a_r z^r$$

$P$ has $m-1$ free coefficients (no $z^l$ term). The singularities of $\frac{P(z
)}{1-z^m}$ on $|z|=1$ are exactly the $m$-th roots of unity where $P$ does **not
** vanish.

**Choosing $P$ to vanish at $m-2$ roots:** Impose $P(\omega_j) = 0$ for $m-2$ va
lues of $j$. This gives $m-2$ homogeneous linear equations in $m-1$ unknowns. Th
e coefficient matrix is an $(m-2)\times(m-1)$ submatrix of the DFT matrix (a Van
dermonde matrix with distinct nodes), which has full row rank. So there exists a
 **nontrivial** solution $P \neq 0$.

Since $P \neq 0$ and the full $(m-1)\times(m-1)$ system (vanishing at all $m-1$
remaining roots) is nonsingular, $P$ cannot vanish at all remaining roots. In fa
ct, $P$ vanishes at exactly the $m-2$ specified roots, leaving exactly **2** $m$
-th roots of unity as singularities.

**Verification of coefficient condition:** $\frac{P(z)}{1-z^m} = P(z)\sum_{k=0}^
{\infty} z^{mk}$, so $c_{mk+r} = a_r$ for $r \neq l$ and $c_{mk+l} = 0$ for all
$k$. In particular $c_{mk+l}=0$ for $k \geq 2$. ✓

**Radius of convergence:** The 2 singularities on $|z|=1$ ensure radius exactly
1. ✓

**Concrete example ($m=3, l=0$):** $P(z) = z - z^2$ vanishes at $z=1$ (one of th
ree cube roots), giving $f(z) = \frac{z-z^2}{1-z^3} = \frac{z}{1+z+z^2}$ with $S
 = \{\omega, \omega^2\}$, $|S| = 2$.

## Conclusion

- **Lower bound:** $|S| \geq 2$ — every singularity needs a partner in its orbit
 for the cancellation required by $(\star)$.
- **Achievability:** $|S| = 2$ is constructed for every valid $m \geq 2$.

The minimum number of elements in $S$ is $\boxed{2}$.
