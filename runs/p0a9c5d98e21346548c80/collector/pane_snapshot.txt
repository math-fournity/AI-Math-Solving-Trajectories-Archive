## Solution

### 1. Setting up the classification

The given PDE is

$$(\ell+x)\,u_{xx}+2xy\,u_{xy}-y^{2}\,u_{yy}=0,$$

where I write the parameter as $\ell$. The principal-part coefficients are

$$A=\ell+x,\qquad B=xy\quad(\text{since }2B=2xy),\qquad C=-y^{2}.$$

The type is determined by the discriminant

$$\Delta = B^{2}-AC = x^{2}y^{2}-(\ell+x)(-y^{2})=y^{2}\!\bigl(x^{2}+x+\ell\bigr
).$$

Set

$$Q(x)=x^{2}+x+\ell ,\qquad \text{so}\quad \Delta = y^{2}\,Q(x).$$

Since $y^{2}\ge 0$ always, the sign of $\Delta$ is governed by $y^{2}$ and $Q(x)
$:

| Sign of $\Delta$ | Type |
|---|---|
| $\Delta>0$ | **hyperbolic** |
| $\Delta=0$ | **parabolic** |
| $\Delta<0$ | **elliptic** |

---

### 2. Dependence on the parameter $\ell$

The quadratic $Q(x)=x^{2}+x+\ell$ has its own discriminant $D_{Q}=1-4\ell$.

#### Case I: $\ell<\tfrac14$ (i.e. $D_Q>0$)

$Q$ has two real roots

$$x_{1}=\frac{-1-\sqrt{1-4\ell}}{2},\qquad x_{2}=\frac{-1+\sqrt{1-4\ell}}{2},\qq
uad x_{1}<x_{2}.$$

$Q>0$ for $x<x_{1}$ or $x>x_{2}$; $\;Q<0$ for $x_{1}<x<x_{2}$; $\;Q=0$ at $x=x_{
1},x_{2}$.

- **Hyperbolic:** $y\neq 0$ and $\bigl(x<x_{1}\;\text{or}\;x>x_{2}\bigr)$.
- **Elliptic:** $y\neq 0$ and $x_{1}<x<x_{2}$.
- **Parabolic:** $y=0$ (the entire $x$-axis), or $x=x_{1}$, or $x=x_{2}$ (vertic
al lines, any $y$).

#### Case II: $\ell=\tfrac14$ (i.e. $D_Q=0$)

$Q(x)=\bigl(x+\tfrac12\bigr)^{2}\ge 0$, vanishing only at $x=-\tfrac12$.

- **Hyperbolic:** $y\neq 0$ and $x\neq -\tfrac12$.
- **Parabolic:** $y=0$, or $x=-\tfrac12$.
- **Elliptic:** none (the elliptic strip has shrunk to zero width — this is the
**transition value** of $\ell$).

#### Case III: $\ell>\tfrac14$ (i.e. $D_Q<0$)

$Q(x)>0$ for all $x$.

- **Hyperbolic:** $y\neq 0$ (the entire upper and lower half-planes).
- **Parabolic:** $y=0$ (the $x$-axis).
- **Elliptic:** none.

**Summary of the $\ell$-dependence.** As $\ell$ decreases from $+\infty$, the eq
uation is everywhere hyperbolic (off the $x$-axis). At the **critical value** $\
ell=\tfrac14$ an elliptic region is born (a degenerate line $x=-\tfrac12$). For
$\ell<\tfrac14$ a genuine elliptic strip $x_{1}<x<x_{2}$ opens up; it widens wit
hout bound as $\ell\to-\infty$ (since $x_{1}\to-\infty$, $x_{2}\to+\infty$). The
 parabolic set is always the $x$-axis plus the zero set of $Q$.

---

### 3. Characteristic (characteristic-curve) equation

The characteristic equation is

$$A\,(dy)^{2}-2B\,dx\,dy+C\,(dx)^{2}=0\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;(\ell+x)(y')^{2}-2x
y\,y'-y^{2}=0,$$

giving

$$\boxed{y'=\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{y\bigl(x\pm\sqrt{Q(x)}\bigr)}{\ell+x}}.$$

Separating variables:

$$\frac{dy}{y}=\frac{x\pm\sqrt{Q}}{\ell+x}\,dx=\Bigl(1-\frac{\ell}{\ell+x}\Bigr)
dx\pm\frac{\sqrt{Q}}{\ell+x}\,dx.$$

Integrating:

$$\ln|y|=x-\ell\ln|\ell+x|\pm J(x)+\text{const},\qquad J(x):=\int\!\frac{\sqrt{x
^{2}+x+\ell}}{\ell+x}\,dx.$$

---

### 4. Canonical form — Hyperbolic region ($\Delta>0$)

Define the two characteristic coordinates

$$\xi=\ln|y|-x+\ell\ln|\ell+x|-J(x),\qquad \eta=\ln|y|-x+\ell\ln|\ell+x|+J(x),$$

so that $\xi=\text{const}$ and $\eta=\text{const}$ are the two characteristic fa
milies. Their partial derivatives are

$$\xi_{x}=-\frac{x+\sqrt{Q}}{\ell+x},\;\;\xi_{y}=\frac1y,\qquad \eta_{x}=-\frac{
x-\sqrt{Q}}{\ell+x},\;\;\eta_{y}=\frac1y.$$

**Principal part.** A direct computation gives

$$\bar A=A\xi_{x}^{2}+2B\xi_{x}\xi_{y}+C\xi_{y}^{2}=0,\qquad \bar C=A\eta_{x}^{2
}+2B\eta_{x}\eta_{y}+C\eta_{y}^{2}=0,$$

$$\bar B=A\xi_{x}\eta_{x}+B(\xi_{x}\eta_{y}+\xi_{y}\eta_{x})+C\xi_{y}\eta_{y}=-\
frac{2Q}{\ell+x}\neq 0.$$

(The vanishing of $\bar A,\bar C$ confirms $\xi,\eta$ are indeed characteristic
coordinates.)

**Lower-order terms.** Since the original equation has $D=E=F=0$, the only first
-order terms come from second derivatives of $\xi,\eta$:

$$\bar D=(\ell+x)\xi_{xx}+2xy\,\xi_{xy}-y^{2}\xi_{yy}=\frac{2x\sqrt{Q}-(2\ell-1)
x+\ell}{2\sqrt{Q}\,(\ell+x)},$$

$$\bar E=(\ell+x)\eta_{xx}+2xy\,\eta_{xy}-y^{2}\eta_{yy}=\frac{2x\sqrt{Q}+(2\ell
-1)x-\ell}{2\sqrt{Q}\,(\ell+x)}.$$

Dividing the transformed equation $2\bar B\,u_{\xi\eta}+\bar D\,u_{\xi}+\bar E\,
u_{\eta}=0$ by $2\bar B=-4Q/(\ell+x)$:

$$\boxed{u_{\xi\eta}\;-\;\frac{2x\sqrt{Q}-(2\ell-1)x+\ell}{8\,Q^{3/2}}\;u_{\xi}\
;-\;\frac{2x\sqrt{Q}+(2\ell-1)x-\ell}{8\,Q^{3/2}}\;u_{\eta}=0,}$$

where $Q=x^{2}+x+\ell>0$ and $x$ is to be expressed through $\xi,\eta$ via $\eta
-\xi=2J(x)$ (then $y$ from $\xi+\eta$). This is the **canonical hyperbolic form*
* $u_{\xi\eta}=\Phi(\xi,\eta,u,u_{\xi},u_{\eta})$.

---

### 5. Canonical form — Elliptic region ($\Delta<0$; requires $\ell<\tfrac14$, $
x_{1}<x<x_{2}$, $y\neq0$)

Here $Q<0$; write $|Q|=-Q=-(x^{2}+x+\ell)>0$. The characteristic equation has **
complex** roots $y'=\frac{y(x\pm i\sqrt{|Q|})}{\ell+x}$. Integrating,

$$\ln|y|=x-\ell\ln|\ell+x|\pm i\!\int\!\frac{\sqrt{|Q|}}{\ell+x}\,dx+\text{const
}.$$

Take the real and imaginary parts as new coordinates:

$$\xi=\ln|y|-x+\ell\ln|\ell+x|,\qquad \eta=\int\!\frac{\sqrt{|Q|}}{\ell+x}\,dx=:
J_{0}(x).$$

Their derivatives:

$$\xi_{x}=-\frac{x}{\ell+x},\;\;\xi_{y}=\frac1y,\qquad \eta_{x}=\frac{\sqrt{|Q|}
}{\ell+x},\;\;\eta_{y}=0.$$

**Principal part:**

$$\bar A=(\ell+x)\xi_{x}^{2}+2xy\,\xi_{x}\xi_{y}-y^{2}\xi_{y}^{2}=-\frac{Q}{\ell
+x}=\frac{|Q|}{\ell+x},$$

$$\bar C=(\ell+x)\eta_{x}^{2}+2xy\,\eta_{x}\eta_{y}-y^{2}\eta_{y}^{2}=\frac{|Q|}
{\ell+x},$$

$$\bar B=(\ell+x)\xi_{x}\eta_{x}+xy(\xi_{x}\eta_{y}+\xi_{y}\eta_{x})-y^{2}\xi_{y
}\eta_{y}=0.$$

So $\bar A=\bar C=|Q|/(\ell+x)>0$ and $\bar B=0$ — the principal part is a multi
ple of the Laplacian, as expected.

**Lower-order terms:**

$$\bar D=(\ell+x)\xi_{xx}+2xy\,\xi_{xy}-y^{2}\xi_{yy}=\frac{x}{\ell+x},$$

$$\bar E=(\ell+x)\eta_{xx}+2xy\,\eta_{xy}-y^{2}\eta_{yy}=\frac{\ell(1-2x)+x}{2\s
qrt{|Q|}\,(\ell+x)}.$$

Dividing by $\bar A=|Q|/(\ell+x)$:

$$\boxed{u_{\xi\xi}+u_{\eta\eta}+\frac{x}{|Q|}\;u_{\xi}\;-\;\frac{\ell(1-2x)+x}{
2\,|Q|^{3/2}}\;u_{\eta}=0,}$$

where $|Q|=-(x^{2}+x+\ell)>0$ and $x$ is recovered from $\eta=J_{0}(x)$. This is
 the **canonical elliptic form** $u_{\xi\xi}+u_{\eta\eta}=\Psi(\xi,\eta,u,u_{\xi
},u_{\eta})$.

---

### 6. Canonical form — Parabolic set ($\Delta=0$)

The parabolic set is the union of curves $\{y=0\}\cup\{x=x_{1}\}\cup\{x=x_{2}\}$
 (for $\ell<\tfrac14$; for $\ell=\tfrac14$ the two vertical lines merge into $x=
-\tfrac12$; for $\ell>\tfrac14$ only $y=0$ remains). On each parabolic curve the
 equation degenerates to a single second derivative.

#### (a) Along $y=0$ (the $x$-axis)

Here $A=\ell+x$, $B=0$, $C=0$. The (double) characteristic is $dy=0$, i.e. $y=\t
ext{const}$. Choose

$$\xi=y,\qquad \eta=x.$$

The transformed equation reads

$$(\ell+\eta)\,u_{\eta\eta}+2\eta\xi\,u_{\xi\eta}-\xi^{2}\,u_{\xi\xi}=0.$$

On the parabolic curve $\xi=0$ (i.e. $y=0$), provided $\ell+\eta\neq0$:

$$\boxed{u_{\eta\eta}=0\qquad(y=0,\;x\neq-\ell).}$$

If $\ell=0$ and $x=0$ (so $A=0$ too), the equation becomes $-y^{2}u_{yy}=0$, i.e
. $u_{yy}=0$ for $y\neq0$ — also parabolic canonical form.

#### (b) Along $x=x_{i}$ ($Q(x_{i})=0$, $y\neq0$, $\ell+x_{i}\neq0$)

Here $B^{2}=AC$ with $A=\ell+x_{i}\neq0$. The single characteristic family satis
fies

$$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{B}{A}=\frac{x_{i}\,y}{\ell+x_{i}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 y\,e^{-x_{i}x/(\ell+x_{i})}=\text{const}.$$

Choose

$$\xi=y\,e^{-x_{i}\eta/(\ell+x_{i})},\qquad \eta=x.$$

One verifies $\bar A=0$ on $x=x_{i}$ (as $Q(x_{i})=0$) and $\bar C=\ell+x_{i}\ne
q0$. On the parabolic curve $\eta=x_{i}$:

$$\boxed{(\ell+x_{i})\,u_{\eta\eta}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad u_{\eta\eta}=0\qq
uad(x=x_{i},\;\ell+x_{i}\neq0).}$$

For the transition case $\ell=\tfrac14$, $x_{i}=-\tfrac12$, $\ell+x_{i}=-\tfrac1
4\neq0$, so the same formula applies.

---

### 7. Consolidated answer

| $\ell$ | Hyperbolic | Elliptic | Parabolic |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\ell>\tfrac14$ | $y\neq0$ (all $x$) | — | $y=0$ |
| $\ell=\tfrac14$ | $y\neq0,\;x\neq-\tfrac12$ | — | $y=0$ or $x=-\tfrac12$ |
| $\ell<\tfrac14$ | $y\neq0,\;x<x_{1}$ or $x>x_{2}$ | $y\neq0,\;x_{1}<x<x_{2}$ |
 $y=0$, $x=x_{1}$, $x=x_{2}$ |

where $x_{1,2}=\frac{-1\mp\sqrt{1-4\ell}}{2}$.

**Canonical forms:**

- **Hyperbolic:** $u_{\xi\eta}-\dfrac{2x\sqrt{Q}-(2\ell-1)x+\ell}{8Q^{3/2}}\,u_{
\xi}-\dfrac{2x\sqrt{Q}+(2\ell-1)x-\ell}{8Q^{3/2}}\,u_{\eta}=0$, with $\xi,\eta$
the two characteristic coordinates built from $J(x)=\int\frac{\sqrt{Q}}{\ell+x}d
x$.

- **Elliptic:** $u_{\xi\xi}+u_{\eta\eta}+\dfrac{x}{|Q|}\,u_{\xi}-\dfrac{\ell(1-2
x)+x}{2|Q|^{3/2}}\,u_{\eta}=0$, with $\xi=\ln|y|-x+\ell\ln|\ell+x|$, $\eta=\int\
frac{\sqrt{|Q|}}{\ell+x}dx$.

- **Parabolic:** On each parabolic curve the equation degenerates to $u_{\eta\et
a}=0$ (in coordinates where $\eta$ is transverse to the curve and $\xi$ is along
 the characteristic direction).

The critical value is $\ell=\tfrac14$: for $\ell\ge\frac14$ there is no elliptic
 region; for $\ell<\frac14$ an elliptic strip $\{x_{1}<x<x_{2},\,y\neq0\}$ appea
rs and widens as $\ell$ decreases.
