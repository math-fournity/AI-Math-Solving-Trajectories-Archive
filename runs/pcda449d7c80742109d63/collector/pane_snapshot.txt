oints are also concyclic, with side lengths $1, 3, \sqrt{2}, 2\sqrt{2}$.

## Proof

**Step 1: The midpoint condition forces perpendicular diagonals.**

The midpoints of any quadrilateral form the Varignon parallelogram. A parallelog
ram is concyclic iff it is a rectangle. The Varignon parallelogram has sides par
allel to the diagonals of $ABCD$, so it is a rectangle iff the diagonals $AC \pe
rp BD$.

**Step 2: Coordinate setup.**

Place the diagonal intersection $Q$ at the origin, with $AC$ along the $x$-axis
and $BD$ along the $y$-axis:
$$A = (a, 0),\quad B = (0, b),\quad C = (-c, 0),\quad D = (0, -d), \quad a, b, c
, d > 0.$$

The side lengths are:
$$AB = \sqrt{a^2+b^2},\quad BC = \sqrt{b^2+c^2},\quad CD = \sqrt{c^2+d^2},\quad
DA = \sqrt{a^2+d^2}.$$

**Step 3: Cyclic condition.**

Substituting the four points into the general circle equation $x^2+y^2+Dx+Ey+F=0
$ yields $F = -ac$ from the $x$-axis points and $F = -bd$ from the $y$-axis poin
ts. Consistency requires:
$$ac = bd. \tag{$\star$}$$

**Step 4: Determining $a^2, b^2, c^2, d^2$.**

Let $p=a^2, q=b^2, r=c^2, s=d^2$. The side-length squares $\{p+q,\, q+r,\, r+s,\
, p+s\} = \{1, 2, 8, 9\}$. Since $(p+q)+(r+s) = (q+r)+(p+s) = p+q+r+s$, opposite
 sides must pair to equal sums. The only partition of $\{1,2,8,9\}$ into two pai
rs summing to $10$ is $\{1,9\}$ and $\{2,8\}$.

Solving each assignment with condition $(\star)$ (i.e., $pr = qs$), one finds in
 every case:
$$\{p, q, r, s\} = \left\{\tfrac{1}{5},\, \tfrac{4}{5},\, \tfrac{9}{5},\, \tfrac
{36}{5}\right\}, \quad \text{with } pr = qs = \tfrac{36}{25}, \text{ so } ac = b
d = \tfrac{6}{5}.$$

Also, $p+q+r+s = \frac{50}{5} = 10$, confirming $a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2 = 10$.

**Step 5: Key geometric fact — $\angle MPN = 90°$.**

We have:
$$M = \left(\tfrac{a}{2}, \tfrac{b}{2}\right),\quad N = \left(-\tfrac{c}{2}, -\t
frac{d}{2}\right),\quad P = \left(\frac{-b^2 c}{b^2+c^2},\, \frac{bc^2}{b^2+c^2}
\right),$$
where $P$ is the foot of the perpendicular from $Q=(0,0)$ to line $BC$.

Computing $\vec{PM} \cdot \vec{PN}$:

$$\vec{PM} \cdot \vec{PN} = \frac{(b^2 - c^2)}{4(b^2+c^2)}\,(ac - bd).$$

By condition $(\star)$, $ac = bd$, so $\vec{PM} \cdot \vec{PN} = 0$, hence $\ang
le MPN = 90°$.

**Step 6: Circumradius of $\triangle MNP$.**

Since $\angle MPN = 90°$, the circumcircle of $\triangle MNP$ has $MN$ as its di
ameter. Thus:
$$\rho = \frac{|MN|}{2}, \qquad \rho^2 = \frac{|MN|^2}{4}.$$

Now:
$$|MN|^2 = \frac{(a+c)^2 + (b+d)^2}{4} = \frac{(a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2) + 2(ac+bd)}{4}
= \frac{10 + 2\cdot\frac{6}{5} + 2\cdot\frac{6}{5}}{4} = \frac{10 + \frac{24}{5}
}{4} = \frac{\frac{74}{5}}{4} = \frac{37}{10}.$$

Therefore:
$$\rho^2 = \frac{37}{40}.$$

**Step 7: All configurations give the same value.**

Since $a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2 = 10$ and $ac = bd = 6/5$ are **invariant** across all va
lid side-length assignments, $|MN|^2 = 37/10$ in every case. The circumcircle ar
ea is always:
$$\pi\rho^2 = \frac{37\pi}{40}.$$

The greatest possible area of the circumcircle of triangle $MNP$ is:

$$\boxed{\dfrac{37\pi}{40}}$$
