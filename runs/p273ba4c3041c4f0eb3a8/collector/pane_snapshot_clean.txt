Let $X$ be a random variable with density $w(x)=1-|x|$ on $[-1,1]$, and set $Y =

$$\mathbb{E}[Y] = r, \qquad \mathbb{E}[Y^k] = r^{k+1}.$$

Since $X$ has a continuous, strictly increasing CDF $F_X$, the transform $U = F_

 reduces to: *does there exist a non-negative random variable $Y$ with $\mathbb{
E}[Y]=r$ and $\mathbb{E}[Y^k]=r^{k+1}$?*

**Step 2: Consistency check.**

By Jensen's inequality ($t \mapsto t^k$ is convex on $[0,\infty)$ for $k>1$):
$$\mathbb{E}[Y^k] \geq (\mathbb{E}[Y])^k = r^k.$$
Since $r > 1$, we have $r^{k+1} > r^k$, so the required value exceeds the Jensen
 lower bound — the constraints are consistent. Moreover, $\mathbb{E}[Y^k]$ can b
e made arbitrarily large (by concentrating mass at a large value with small prob
ability), so $r^{k+1}$ lies in the achievable range $(r^k, \infty)$.

**Step 3: Explicit construction (two-point distribution).**

Set
$$p = r^{-1/(k-1)} \in (0,1), \qquad c = r^{k/(k-1)} > 0,$$
and let $Y$ take value $c$ with probability $p$ and value $0$ with probability $
1-p$.

**First moment:**
$$\mathbb{E}[Y] = p \cdot c = r^{-1/(k-1)} \cdot r^{k/(k-1)} = r^{(k-1)/(k-1)} =
 r. \quad \checkmark$$

**$k$-th moment:**
$$\mathbb{E}[Y^k] = p \cdot c^k = r^{-1/(k-1)} \cdot \left(r^{k/(k-1)}\right)^k
= r^{-1/(k-1)} \cdot r^{k^2/(k-1)} = r^{(k^2-1)/(k-1)} = r^{(k+1)(k-1)/(k-1)} =
r^{k+1}. \quad \checkmark$$

**Step 4: Realization as $f(X)$.**

Define $f:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}_+$ by
$$f(x) = \begin{cases} c & \text{if } F_X(x) > 1 - p, \\ 0 & \text{if } F_X(x) \
leq 1 - p. \end{cases}$$

Since $F_X$ is continuous and strictly increasing, the set $\{x : F_X(x) > 1-p\}
$ is a Borel measurable interval, so $f$ is a measurable step function with $f \
geq 0$. Then $f(X) = c$ iff $F_X(X) > 1-p$, which occurs with probability $p$, a
nd $f(X) = 0$ otherwise. Thus $f(X)$ has exactly the distribution constructed in
 Step 3.

**Step 5: Verification of the integrals.**

$$\int_{-1}^{1} f(x)(1-|x|)\,dx = \mathbb{E}[f(X)] = \mathbb{E}[Y] = r,$$
$$\int_{-1}^{1} f(x)^k(1-|x|)\,dx = \mathbb{E}[f(X)^k] = \mathbb{E}[Y^k] = r^{k+
1}.$$

Both conditions are satisfied. $\blacksquare$
