# Spectrum of the Weighted Right Shift on ℓ∞(ℕ)

## Setup

Let $T: \ell^\infty(\mathbb{N}) \to \ell^\infty(\mathbb{N})$ be defined by
$$T(a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots) = \left(0, \frac{a_1}{1}, \frac{a_2}{2}, \frac{a_3}{3}
, \dots\right).$$

Equivalently, $(Ta)_1 = 0$ and $(Ta)_{j} = a_{j-1}/(j-1)$ for $j \geq 2$. The we
ights are $w_n = 1/n$.

## Step 1: $T$ is bounded with $\|T\| = 1$

For any $a \in \ell^\infty$,
$$\|Ta\|_\infty = \sup_{j \geq 2} \frac{|a_{j-1}|}{j-1} \leq \sup_{n \geq 1} |a_
n| \cdot \sup_{n \geq 1}\frac{1}{n} = \|a\|_\infty,$$
so $\|T\| \leq 1$. Equality holds since $T(e_1) = e_2$ and $\|e_2\|_\infty = 1 =
 \|e_1\|_\infty$. Thus $\|T\| = 1$.

## Step 2: Compute $\|T^n\| = 1/n!$

By induction, $T^n$ shifts right by $n$ positions with cumulative weights:
$$(T^n a)_{n+k} = \frac{a_k}{k(k+1)(k+2)\cdots(k+n-1)} = \frac{a_k \cdot (k-1)!}
{(k+n-1)!}, \qquad k \geq 1,$$
and $(T^n a)_j = 0$ for $1 \leq j \leq n$.

Therefore
$$\|T^n\| = \sup_{k \geq 1} \frac{1}{k(k+1)\cdots(k+n-1)} = \sup_{k \geq 1} \fra
c{(k-1)!}{(k+n-1)!}.$$

The denominator $k(k+1)\cdots(k+n-1)$ is minimized at $k=1$, giving $1 \cdot 2 \
cdots n = n!$. Hence
$$\|T^n\| = \frac{1}{n!}.$$

## Step 3: Spectral radius $r(T) = 0$

By the spectral radius formula (Gelfand),
$$r(T) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \|T^n\|^{1/n} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left(\frac{1}{
n!}\right)^{1/n} = 0,$$
since $n!$ grows super-exponentially (by Stirling, $(n!)^{1/n} \sim n/e \to \inf
ty$).

## Step 4: $\sigma(T) \subseteq \{0\}$

For a bounded linear operator on a complex Banach space, the spectrum $\sigma(T)
$ is a non-empty compact subset of the closed disk $\{|\lambda| \leq r(T)\}$. Si
nce $r(T) = 0$,
$$\sigma(T) \subseteq \{0\}.$$

## Step 5: $0 \in \sigma(T)$

The operator $T$ is not surjective: every vector in the range of $T$ has first c
oordinate $0$, so no sequence with nonzero first component lies in the range. In
 particular, $e_1 = (1,0,0,\dots) \notin \operatorname{ran}(T)$. Hence $T = T -
0 \cdot I$ is not invertible, and $0 \in \sigma(T)$.

## Step 6: Classification of $0$ within the spectrum

- **Not an eigenvalue (point spectrum):** If $Ta = 0$, then $a_n/n = 0$ for all
$n$, so $a = 0$. More generally, $Ta = \lambda a$ with $\lambda \neq 0$ forces $
a_1 = 0$ (from $0 = \lambda a_1$), then $a_2 = a_1/\lambda = 0$, and inductively
 $a = 0$. So $T$ has **no eigenvalues at all**.

- **Range is not dense (residual spectrum):** The range of $T$ is
$$\operatorname{ran}(T) = \{b \in \ell^\infty : b_1 = 0\},$$
a closed proper subspace (codimension 1). It is not dense in $\ell^\infty$ (e.g.
, $e_1$ is at distance $1$ from it). Since $T$ is injective but $\operatorname{r
an}(T)$ is not dense, $0$ belongs to the **residual spectrum** $\sigma_r(T)$.

## Conclusion

$$\boxed{\sigma(T) = \{0\}}.$$

The sole spectral value $0$ lies in the **residual spectrum**: $T$ is injective,
