## Proof

Let $R = A[x,y,z]$ and $I = \langle xy, xz\rangle$. We claim

$$\dim B = \dim A + 2.$$

**Step 1. Decompose $I$ as an intersection.**

We show $I = \langle x\rangle \cap \langle y,z\rangle$ in $R$. The inclusion $\l
angle x\rangle\cap\langle y,z\rangle \supseteq \langle xy, xz\rangle$ is clear s
ince $xy, xz \in \langle x\rangle$ and $xy, xz \in \langle y,z\rangle$. For the
reverse, write $f \in \langle x\rangle \cap \langle y,z\rangle$ as $f = \sum a_{
ijk}x^i y^j z^k$. Being in $\langle x\rangle$ forces $i\geq 1$ for every monomia
l with nonzero coefficient; being in $\langle y,z\rangle$ forces $j+k\geq 1$ for
 every such monomial (pure powers $x^i$ are killed). Hence every monomial of $f$
 is divisible by $x$ and by $y$ or $z$, i.e. by $xy$ or $xz$, so $f \in \langle
xy, xz\rangle$. Thus

$$I = \langle x\rangle \cap \langle y,z\rangle.$$

**Step 2. Identify the minimal primes over $I$.**

Since $I = \mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}$ with $\mathfrak{a}=\langle x\rangle$, $
\mathfrak{b}=\langle y,z\rangle$, a prime $P\supseteq I$ is minimal over $I$ iff
 it is minimal over $\mathfrak{a}$ or minimal over $\mathfrak{b}$. Concretely:

- Minimal primes over $\langle x\rangle$ correspond to minimal primes of $R/\lan
gle x\rangle \cong A[y,z]$. Since $A$ is Noetherian, its minimal primes $\mathfr
ak{p}_1,\dots,\mathfrak{p}_r$ are finite, and the minimal primes of $A[y,z]$ are
 $\mathfrak{p}_\alpha[y,z]$. Lifting back, the minimal primes over $\langle x\ra
ngle$ are
$$P_\alpha^{(1)} = \mathfrak{p}_\alpha[x,y,z] + \langle x\rangle.$$

- Likewise, minimal primes over $\langle y,z\rangle$ are
$$P_\alpha^{(2)} = \mathfrak{p}_\alpha[x,y,z] + \langle y,z\rangle.$$

These are all the minimal primes of $B$.

**Step 3. Compute the dimension of each irreducible component.**

For a Noetherian domain $D$, the standard dimension formula gives $\dim D[t_1,\d
ots,t_n] = \dim D + n$. Applying this:

- $R/P_\alpha^{(1)} \cong (A/\mathfrak{p}_\alpha)[y,z]$, so
$$\dim R/P_\alpha^{(1)} = \dim(A/\mathfrak{p}_\alpha) + 2.$$

- $R/P_\alpha^{(2)} \cong (A/\mathfrak{p}_\alpha)[x]$, so
$$\dim R/P_\alpha^{(2)} = \dim(A/\mathfrak{p}_\alpha) + 1.$$

**Step 4. Conclude.**

The Krull dimension of $B$ is the supremum of the dimensions of its irreducible
components:

$$\dim B = \max\Bigl\{\max_\alpha\bigl(\dim(A/\mathfrak{p}_\alpha)+2\bigr),\;\ma
x_\alpha\bigl(\dim(A/\mathfrak{p}_\alpha)+1\bigr)\Bigr\} = \max_\alpha \dim(A/\m
athfrak{p}_\alpha) + 2.$$

For a Noetherian ring, every prime contains a minimal prime, so the longest chai
n of primes in $A$ is realized inside some $A/\mathfrak{p}_\alpha$; hence

$$\dim A = \max_\alpha \dim(A/\mathfrak{p}_\alpha).$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{\dim B = \dim A + 2}.$$
