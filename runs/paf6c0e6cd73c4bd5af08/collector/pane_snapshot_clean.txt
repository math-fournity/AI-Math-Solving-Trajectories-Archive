## Solution: Periodic Strip Problem with Dirichlet Boundary Conditions

**Problem setup.** We seek a harmonic function $\phi(x,y)$ satisfying Laplace's
equation
$$\nabla^2 \phi = \phi_{xx} + \phi_{yy} = 0$$
on the strip $\{(x,y) : x \in \mathbb{R},\; 0 \le y \le 1\}$, with $2\pi$-period
icity in $x$ and Dirichlet boundary conditions
$$\phi(x,0) = f(x), \qquad \phi(x,1) = g(x),$$
where $f$ and $g$ are $2\pi$-periodic.

---

### Step 1. Fourier expansion of the boundary data

Since $f$ and $g$ are $2\pi$-periodic, expand them in Fourier series:
$$f(x) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} f_n\, e^{inx}, \qquad g(x) = \sum_{n=-\infty}
^{\infty} g_n\, e^{inx},$$
with coefficients
$$f_n = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(x)\,e^{-inx}\,dx, \qquad g_n = \frac{1}{2\
pi}\int_0^{2\pi} g(x)\,e^{-inx}\,dx.$$

---

### Step 2. Separation of variables

By periodicity in $x$, we seek solutions of the form $\phi(x,y) = e^{inx}Y_n(y)$
. Substituting into Laplace's equation gives the ODE
$$Y_n'' - n^2 Y_n = 0.$$

- **For $n = 0$:** $Y_0'' = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; Y_0(y) = A_0 + B_0\, y$.
- **For $n \neq 0$:** $Y_n(y) = A_n \cosh(ny) + B_n \sinh(ny)$.

The general solution is therefore
$$\phi(x,y) = A_0 + B_0\, y + \sum_{n \neq 0}\bigl[A_n \cosh(ny) + B_n \sinh(ny)
\bigr]\, e^{inx}.$$

---

### Step 3. Applying the boundary conditions

**At $y = 0$:** $\phi(x,0) = f(x)$ gives
$$A_0 + \sum_{n\neq 0} A_n\, e^{inx} = \sum_{n} f_n\, e^{inx},$$
so
$$A_0 = f_0, \qquad A_n = f_n \quad (n \neq 0).$$

**At $y = 1$:** $\phi(x,1) = g(x)$ gives
$$A_0 + B_0 + \sum_{n\neq 0}\bigl[A_n \cosh n + B_n \sinh n\bigr]\, e^{inx} = \s
um_{n} g_n\, e^{inx}.$$

Matching the constant term ($n=0$):
$$A_0 + B_0 = g_0 \;\Longrightarrow\; B_0 = g_0 - f_0.$$

Matching each $n \neq 0$ mode:
$$f_n \cosh n + B_n \sinh n = g_n \;\Longrightarrow\; B_n = \frac{g_n - f_n \cos
h n}{\sinh n}.$$

---

### Step 4. General solution in standard (Poisson kernel) form

Substituting back and using the hyperbolic identity
$$\sinh\bigl(n(1-y)\bigr) = \sinh n\,\cosh(ny) - \cosh n\,\sinh(ny),$$
the $n \neq 0$ contribution simplifies:
$$f_n \cosh(ny) + \frac{g_n - f_n \cosh n}{\sinh n}\,\sinh(ny) = \frac{f_n\,\sin
h\bigl(n(1-y)\bigr) + g_n\,\sinh(ny)}{\sinh n}.$$

Hence the **general solution** is:

$$\boxed{\phi(x,y) = f_0 + (g_0 - f_0)\,y + \sum_{n \neq 0}\left[\frac{f_n\,\sin
h\bigl(n(1-y)\bigr) + g_n\,\sinh(ny)}{\sinh n}\right] e^{inx}.}$$

---

### Step 5. How the coefficients are determined

The coefficients $f_n$ and $g_n$ are the **Fourier coefficients of the prescribe
d boundary data**:

$$f_n = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(x)\,e^{-inx}\,dx, \qquad g_n = \frac{1}{2\
pi}\int_0^{2\pi} g(x)\,e^{-inx}\,dx.$$

- The **zero mode** ($n=0$) gives the linear interpolation $f_0 + (g_0 - f_0)y$
between the mean values of $f$ and $g$.
- Each **nonzero mode** ($n \neq 0$) is a harmonic function that interpolates be
tween the $n$-th Fourier mode of $f$ at $y=0$ and the $n$-th Fourier mode of $g$
 at $y=1$, weighted by the hyperbolic sine kernels $\sinh(ny)/\sinh n$ and $\sin
h(n(1-y))/\sinh n$.

These kernels are the **Poisson kernels for the strip**: $\sinh(ny)/\sinh n$ is
$1$ at $y=1$ and $0$ at $y=0$, while $\sinh(n(1-y))/\sinh n$ is $1$ at $y=0$ and
 $0$ at $y=1$. This guarantees that the boundary conditions are satisfied exactl
y, and uniqueness follows from the maximum principle for harmonic functions on a
 bounded domain.
