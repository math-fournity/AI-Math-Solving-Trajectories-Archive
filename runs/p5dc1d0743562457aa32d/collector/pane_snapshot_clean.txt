## Proof

**Claim:** The solvable radical of $G'$ is normal in $G$.

**Definitions.** For a group $H$, the *solvable radical* $\operatorname{Rad}(H)$
 is the largest solvable normal subgroup of $H$, equivalently the join of all so
lvable normal subgroups of $H$.

**Step 1: $G'$ is characteristic in $G$.**

The commutator subgroup $G' = [G,G]$ is generated by all commutators $[x,y] = x^
{-1}y^{-1}xy$ with $x,y \in G$. Any automorphism $\varphi \in \operatorname{Aut}
(G)$ satisfies $\varphi([x,y]) = [\varphi(x),\varphi(y)]$, so $\varphi(G') = G'$
. Hence $G'$ is characteristic (and therefore normal) in $G$.

**Step 2: $\operatorname{Rad}(H)$ is characteristic in $H$ for any group $H$.**

Let $\varphi \in \operatorname{Aut}(H)$. If $N \trianglelefteq H$ is solvable, t
hen $\varphi(N) \trianglelefteq H$ (automorphisms preserve normality) and $\varp
hi(N)$ is solvable (automorphisms preserve subgroup chains and abelian quotients
). Thus $\varphi$ permutes the set of solvable normal subgroups of $H$. Since $\
operatorname{Rad}(H)$ is the join of this entire set,

$$\varphi(\operatorname{Rad}(H)) = \varphi\!\left(\bigvee_{N} N\right) = \bigvee
_{N} \varphi(N) = \bigvee_{N} N = \operatorname{Rad}(H),$$

where the join ranges over all solvable normal subgroups $N$ of $H$, and the las
t equality uses that $\varphi$ merely permutes this collection. Hence $\operator
name{Rad}(H)$ is characteristic in $H$.

**Step 3: Characteristic is transitive.**

If $A$ is characteristic in $B$ and $B$ is characteristic in $C$, then $A$ is ch
aracteristic in $C$: every automorphism of $C$ restricts to an automorphism of $
B$ (since $B$ is characteristic in $C$), and that automorphism of $B$ fixes $A$
(since $A$ is characteristic in $B$).

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Applying Step 2 with $H = G'$, the subgroup $\operatorname{Rad}(G')$ is characte
ristic in $G'$. By Step 1, $G'$ is characteristic in $G$. By Step 3 (transitivit
y of characteristicity),

$$\operatorname{Rad}(G') \text{ is characteristic in } G.$$

Every characteristic subgroup is normal, so

$$\operatorname{Rad}(G') \trianglelefteq G. \qquad \blacksquare$$
