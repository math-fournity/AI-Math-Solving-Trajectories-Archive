The central conjecture is `green_14_polynomial`:

t $d \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $W(k, r) = O(r^d)$ as $r \to \infty$, for all $k

This is tagged `@[category research open]` — it is a **genuinely open problem**

### What is known (the "solved" sub-theorems)

The surrounding theorems catalog the known results for the $k = 3$ case:

1. **`green_14_polynomial_k_eq_3`**: $W(3, r)$ does **not** have polynomial grow
th [Gr21]. Green proved a superpolynomial lower bound.
2. **`green_14_quadratic`**: $W(3, r) \not\ll r^2$ (answer: `False`) [Gr21].
3. **`green_14_lower_bound_green`**: $W(3, r) \gg \exp(c(\log r)^{4/3 - o(1)})$

4. **`green_14_lower_bound_hunter`**: Improved to $W(3, r) \gg \exp(c(\log r)^{2

5. **`green_14_lower_bound_brown_landman_robertson`**: $W(3, r) \gg r^{2 - 1/\lo

7. **`green_14_upper_bound_schoen`**: $W(3, r) < \exp(r^{1-c})$ for some $c > 0$

### Why the main problem is open

For $k = 3$, the situation is well-understood: $W(3, r)$ is **superpolynomial**
but **subexponential** in $r$.

For $k \geq 4$, the behavior of $W(k, r)$ as $r \to \infty$ is **not known**. Th
e key difficulties are:

- **Lower bounds**: For $k \geq 4$, the best known lower bounds for $W(k, r)$ co
me from probabilistic constructions, giving roughly $W(k, r) \geq r^{c \cdot k /
 \log k}$ type bounds (via the Lovász local lemma / random colorings), but these
 are far from tight.
- **Upper bounds**: The general van der Waerden upper bounds (Gowers's quantitat
ive bounds) give tower-type or worse growth, which is vastly superpolynomial.
- **The gap**: For $k \geq 4$, there is no known construction or argument that p
ins down whether the growth is polynomial, quasipolynomial, or worse. The $k = 3
$ case is special because the Kelley–Meka breakthrough eMe23] and Green's refi
nement [Gr21] exploit specific structure of 3-term arithmetic progressions that
does not generalize to $k \geq 4$.

The transition from $k = 3$ (superpolynomial, settled) to $k \geq 4$ (unknown) r
epresents a fundamental barrier in our understanding. The techniques that work f
or 3-APs (cap set bounds, Fourier-analytic methods, the Kelley–Meka density incr
ement) do not extend in any known way to longer progressions.
