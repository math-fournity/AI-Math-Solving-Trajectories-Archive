## Proof

Let the plane curve be $\gamma(x) = [X(x), Y(x)]$ parameterized by arc length $x
$, so that $\|\gamma'(x)\| = 1$. Define the unit tangent $T = \gamma'$ and the u
nit normal $N = R(\pi/2)\,T$, where

$$
R(\phi) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\phi & -\sin\phi \\ \sin\phi & \cos\phi \end{pmatr
ix}.
$$

The Frenet frame is the $2\times 2$ matrix $\Phi = [T, N]$, i.e. the matrix whos
e columns are $T$ and $N$.

### Step 1. Orthonormality of the frame

Since $x$ is arc length, $\|T\|^2 = T\cdot T = 1$. Differentiating,

$$
0 = \frac{d}{dx}(T\cdot T) = 2\,T\cdot T', \quad\text{so}\quad T\cdot T' = 0. \t
ag{1}
$$

Also $N = R(\pi/2)T$ is a $90^\circ$ rotation of $T$, so $\|N\|=1$ and $T\cdot N
 = 0$. Differentiating $T\cdot N = 0$,

$$
T'\cdot N + T\cdot N' = 0. \tag{2}
$$

### Step 2. The curvature

Because $T'$ is orthogonal to $T$ (by (1)) and the plane is two-dimensional, $T'
$ must be parallel to $N$. We define the (signed) curvature $\kappa$ by

$$
T' = \kappa\, N. \tag{3}
$$

### Step 3. Deriving $N'$

From (2) and (3),

$$
T\cdot N' = -\,T'\cdot N = -\kappa\,(N\cdot N) = -\kappa. \tag{4}
$$

Differentiating $N\cdot N = 1$ gives $N\cdot N' = 0$, so $N'$ is orthogonal to $
N$, hence parallel to $T$. Combining with (4),

$$
N' = -\kappa\, T. \tag{5}
$$

(The sign is forced: $N'\cdot T = -\kappa$ and $\|T\|=1$.)

### Step 4. Assembling $\Phi' = \Phi L$

Writing $\Phi = [T, N]$ as a column-pair matrix and differentiating column-wise
using (3) and (5):

$$
\Phi' = [T', N'] = [\kappa N,\; -\kappa T].
$$

We want to write this as $\Phi L = [T, N]\,L$, i.e. the columns of $\Phi L$ are
linear combinations of the columns $T, N$ of $\Phi$ with coefficients given by t
he columns of $L$:

- First column of $\Phi L$: $L_{11}T + L_{21}N = T' = \kappa N$, so $L_{11}=0$,
$L_{21}=\kappa$.
- Second column of $\Phi L$: $L_{12}T + L_{22}N = N' = -\kappa T$, so $L_{12}=-\
kappa$, $L_{22}=0$.

Therefore

$$
L = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -\kappa \\[2pt] \kappa & 0 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

### Conclusion

The Frenet formula for a plane curve parameterized by arc length is

$$
\boxed{\;\Phi' = \Phi\,L,\qquad L = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -\kappa \\ \kappa & 0 \e
nd{pmatrix}\;}
$$

or equivalently, written as the system of Frenet–Serret equations,

$$
T' = \kappa\, N, \qquad N' = -\kappa\, T.
$$

The matrix $L$ is skew-symmetric, with the curvature $\kappa$ as its single inde
pendent entry — reflecting the fact that the Frenet frame evolves by an infinite
simal rotation at angular rate $\kappa$.
