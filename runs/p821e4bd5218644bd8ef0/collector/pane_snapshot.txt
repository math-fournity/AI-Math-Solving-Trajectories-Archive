## Proof

**Yes**, such a uniform bound $M$ exists, depending only on $I$, $m$, and $c$.

**Setup.** Let $V = \mathcal{P}_m$ be the vector space of real polynomials of de
gree $\leq m$, which has dimension $m+1$. The condition $p(I) \subset [0,c]$ mea
ns $0 \leq p(x) \leq c$ for all $x \in [a,b]$, in particular $\|p\|_\infty := \s
up_{x \in [a,b]} |p(x)| \leq c$.

**Key tool: Lagrange interpolation.** Choose $m+1$ distinct nodes $x_0, x_1, \do
ts, x_m \in [a,b]$ (for instance, $x_j = a + \frac{j}{m}(b-a)$). Every $p \in \m
athcal{P}_m$ is uniquely determined by its values at these nodes, and

$$p(x) = \sum_{j=0}^{m} p(x_j)\, L_j(x), \qquad L_j(x) = \prod_{\substack{i=0 \\
 i \neq j}}^{m} \frac{x - x_i}{x_j - x_i}.$$

Each Lagrange basis polynomial $L_j$ has degree $m$, so write $L_j(x) = \sum_{k=
0}^{m} \ell_{j,k}\, x^k$. The coefficients $\ell_{j,k}$ are **fixed real numbers
** determined entirely by the nodes $\{x_0,\dots,x_m\}$, hence by $I$ and $m$ al
one.

**Bounding the coefficients of $p$.** Writing $p(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{m} a_k x^k$ an
d matching coefficients in the interpolation formula gives

$$a_k = \sum_{j=0}^{m} p(x_j)\, \ell_{j,k}.$$

Since $x_j \in [a,b]$, the hypothesis $p(I) \subset [0,c]$ gives $|p(x_j)| \leq
c$. Therefore

$$|a_k| \leq \sum_{j=0}^{m} |p(x_j)|\, |\ell_{j,k}| \leq c \sum_{j=0}^{m} |\ell_
{j,k}|.$$

**Defining $M$.** Set

$$\boxed{M \;=\; c \cdot \max_{0 \leq k \leq m} \sum_{j=0}^{m} |\ell_{j,k}|.}$$

The inner sums $\sum_{j=0}^{m} |\ell_{j,k}|$ are finite constants depending only
 on the nodes $\{x_0,\dots,x_m\} \subset I$, hence only on $I$ and $m$. Thus $M$
 depends only on $I$, $m$, and $c$, and for every $p \in \mathcal{P}_m$ with $p(
I) \subset [0,c]$ and every coefficient $a_k$ of $p$,

$$|a_k| \leq M. \qquad \blacksquare$$

**Remark.** The same conclusion follows abstractly: on the finite-dimensional sp
ace $\mathcal{P}_m$ all norms are equivalent, so the coefficient functionals $a_
k : \mathcal{P}_m \to \mathbb{R}$ (which are linear) are continuous with respect
 to $\|\cdot\|_\infty$. Hence $|a_k(p)| \leq C_k \|p\|_\infty \leq C_k c$. The L
agrange construction above simply makes the constants $C_k$ explicit.
