## Proof

**Yes.** We assume the standard setting where $\Omega$ is a bounded open set (so
 that $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ is meaningful). We prove that $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ is
 dense in $C_0(\bar\Omega) := \{f \in C(\bar\Omega) : f|_{\partial\Omega} = 0\}$
 under the sup norm.

---

### Step 1 — Extension to a compactly supported continuous function on $\mathbb{
R}^n$

Define $F: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ by
$$F(x) = \begin{cases} f(x), & x \in \bar\Omega, \\ 0, & x \in \mathbb{R}^n \set
minus \bar\Omega. \end{cases}$$

**Claim:** $F \in C_c(\mathbb{R}^n)$.

- *Compact support:* $\operatorname{supp} F \subset \bar\Omega$, which is compac
t since $\Omega$ is bounded.
- *Continuity:* Clear at interior points of $\Omega$ and at exterior points of $
\bar\Omega$. At a boundary point $x_0 \in \partial\Omega$: $F(x_0) = f(x_0) = 0$
. For any sequence $x_k \to x_0$:
  - If $x_k \in \bar\Omega$: $F(x_k) = f(x_k) \to f(x_0) = 0$ by continuity of $
f$.
  - If $x_k \notin \bar\Omega$: $F(x_k) = 0 \to 0$.

  Hence $F(x_k) \to 0 = F(x_0)$, so $F$ is continuous at $x_0$. ∎

---

### Step 2 — Smooth cutoff: reduce to compactly supported functions *inside* $\O
mega$

For $\delta > 0$, define the inner parallel set
$$\Omega_\delta := \{x \in \Omega : \operatorname{dist}(x, \partial\Omega) > \de
lta\}.$$

By the standard smooth Urysohn lemma (mollifying a continuous cutoff between $\O
mega_{2\delta}$ and $\mathbb{R}^n \setminus \Omega_\delta$), there exists
$$\eta_\delta \in C_c^\infty(\Omega), \quad 0 \le \eta_\delta \le 1, \quad \eta_
\delta \equiv 1 \text{ on } \Omega_{2\delta}, \quad \operatorname{supp} \eta_\de
lta \subset \Omega_\delta.$$

Define $g_\delta := f \cdot \eta_\delta$ on $\bar\Omega$ (with $g_\delta = 0$ on
 $\partial\Omega$ since $\operatorname{supp}\eta_\delta \subset \Omega$). Then $
g_\delta \in C_c(\Omega)$ (continuous on $\bar\Omega$, compactly supported in $\
Omega$).

**Claim:** $\|g_\delta - f\|_{C(\bar\Omega)} \to 0$ as $\delta \to 0$.

- On $\Omega_{2\delta}$: $\eta_\delta = 1$, so $g_\delta = f$; the difference is
 $0$.
- On $\Omega \setminus \Omega_{2\delta} = \{x \in \Omega : \operatorname{dist}(x
,\partial\Omega) \le 2\delta\}$: $|g_\delta(x) - f(x)| = |f(x)|\cdot|\eta_\delta
(x)-1| \le |f(x)|$.
- On $\partial\Omega$: $g_\delta = 0 = f$.

Since $f \in C(\bar\Omega)$ and $f|_{\partial\Omega} = 0$, for every $\varepsilo
n > 0$ there exists $\delta_0 > 0$ such that $|f(x)| < \varepsilon$ whenever $\o
peratorname{dist}(x, \partial\Omega) < 2\delta_0$. Thus for all $\delta < \delta
_0$:
$$\|g_\delta - f\|_{C(\bar\Omega)} = \sup_{x \in \Omega \setminus \Omega_{2\delt
a}} |f(x)| \cdot |\eta_\delta(x) - 1| \le \sup_{\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\O
mega) \le 2\delta} |f(x)| < \varepsilon. \quad\checkmark$$

### Step 3 — Mollification

Let $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(B(0,1))$ be the standard mollifier ($\varphi \ge 0$,
 $\int \varphi = 1$) and $\varphi_\varepsilon(x) = \varepsilon^{-n}\varphi(x/\va
repsilon)$.

Since $g_\delta \in C_c(\Omega)$ with $\operatorname{supp} g_\delta \subset \Ome
ga_\delta$, choose $\varepsilon < \delta/2$ and set
$$h_{\delta,\varepsilon} := g_\delta * \varphi_\varepsilon.$$

Then $h_{\delta,\varepsilon} \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ with
$$\operatorname{supp} h_{\delta,\varepsilon} \subset \operatorname{supp} g_\delt
a + B(0,\varepsilon) \subset \Omega_\delta + B(0,\varepsilon) \subset \Omega_{\d
elta/2} \subset \Omega.$$

Hence $h_{\delta,\varepsilon}|_{\bar\Omega} \in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$. By the stan
dard property of mollification, since $g_\delta$ is uniformly continuous:
$$\|h_{\delta,\varepsilon} - g_\delta\|_{C(\bar\Omega)} \to 0 \quad \text{as } \
varepsilon \to 0.$$

---

### Step 4 — Combining the two approximations

Given $\varepsilon > 0$:

1. Choose $\delta > 0$ so that $\|g_\delta - f\|_{C(\bar\Omega)} < \varepsilon/2
$ (Step 2).
2. Choose $\varepsilon_0 < \delta/2$ so that $\|h_{\delta,\varepsilon_0} - g_\de
lta\|_{C(\bar\Omega)} < \varepsilon/2$ (Step 3).

Then $f_n := h_{\delta,\varepsilon_0} \in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ and
$$\|f_n - f\|_{C(\bar\Omega)} \le \|h_{\delta,\varepsilon_0} - g_\delta\|_\infty
 + \|g_\delta - f\|_\infty < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \va
repsilon.$$

Selecting a sequence $(\delta_k, \varepsilon_k) \to (0,0)$ with $\varepsilon_k <
 \delta_k/2$ and $\|h_{\delta_k,\varepsilon_k} - f\|_\infty < 1/k$ yields $\{f_k
\} \subset C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ with $\|f_k - f\|_{C(\bar\Omega)} \to 0$.

---

### Conclusion

**Yes**, such a sequence exists. The key ingredients are: (1) the boundary condi
tion $f|_{\partial\Omega}=0$ allows extending $f$ to a compactly supported conti
nuous function on $\mathbb{R}^n$; (2) a smooth cutoff $\eta_\delta$ forces the s
upport strictly inside $\Omega$ with controllable error (using that $f$ vanishes
 near $\partial\Omega$); (3) mollification then produces a $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$
function, with support kept inside $\Omega$ by choosing the mollification radius
 smaller than the cutoff margin. $\blacksquare$
