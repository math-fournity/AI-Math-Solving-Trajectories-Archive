Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a $P$-primary ideal $Q$ con
taining $P^n$ must also contain the $n$th symbolic power $P^{(n)}$.

**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

Recall that the $n$th symbolic power of $P$ is defined as
$$P^{(n)} = P^n R_P \cap R,$$
where $R_P$ is the localization of $R$ at the prime ideal $P$.

The key ingredient is the following standard fact:

**Claim.** If $Q$ is a $P$-primary ideal, then $QR_P \cap R = Q$.

*Proof of Claim.* The inclusion $Q \subseteq QR_P \cap R$ is immediate (take $s=
1$). For the reverse inclusion, recall that
$$QR_P \cap R = \{x \in R : \exists\, s \in R \setminus P \text{ such that } sx
\in Q\}.$$
Suppose $x \in QR_P \cap R$, so there exists $s \notin P$ with $sx \in Q$. Since
 $Q$ is $P$-primary, $\sqrt{Q} = P$, so $s \notin \sqrt{Q}$. By the definition o
f a primary ideal, $sx \in Q$ and $s \notin \sqrt{Q}$ implies $x \in Q$. Hence $
QR_P \cap R \subseteq Q$, proving the claim. $\square$

Now we chain the inclusions. Since $P^n \subseteq Q$, localizing at $P$ gives
$$P^n R_P \subseteq QR_P.$$
Intersecting with $R$:
$$P^{(n)} = P^n R_P \cap R \subseteq QR_P \cap R = Q,$$
where the last equality uses the Claim.

Therefore $P^{(n)} \subseteq Q$. $\blacksquare$
