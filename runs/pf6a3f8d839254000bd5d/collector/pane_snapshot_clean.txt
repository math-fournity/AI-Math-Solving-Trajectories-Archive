## Solution

We find all $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ satisfying
$$f(x-f(y))=f(f(y))+x\,f(y)+f(x)-1 \qquad (\star)$$
for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$.

Let $P(x,y)$ denote assertion $(\star)$, let $S=\operatorname{Im}(f)$, and let $
a=f(0)$.

**Step 1. $f$ is not constant.** If $f\equiv c$, then $(\star)$ gives $c=2c-1+xc
$ for all $x$, forcing $c=0$ and $0=-1$, a contradiction. Hence $|S|\ge 2$.

**Step 2. A key identity on the image.** For $t\in S$ (say $t=f(y)$), equation $
(\star)$ rewrites as
$$f(x-t)=f(t)+xt+f(x)-1, \qquad \forall\,x\in\mathbb{R},\ t\in S. \tag{E}$$
Setting $x=t$ in (E): $f(0)=2f(t)+t^{2}-1$, so
$$f(t)=\frac{a+1-t^{2}}{2}, \qquad \forall\,t\in S. \tag{F}$$
Substituting (F) back into (E):
$$f(x-t)=f(x)+xt+\frac{a-1-t^{2}}{2}, \qquad \forall\,x\in\mathbb{R},\ t\in S. \
tag{G}$$

**Step 3. The difference set $S-S$ equals $\mathbb{R}$.** For $t\in S$, define $
\phi_t(x)=f(x-t)$. Since $x\mapsto x-t$ is a bijection of $\mathbb{R}$, $\phi_t(
\mathbb{R})=\operatorname{Im}(f)=S$. By (G), $\phi_t(x)=f(x)+xt+\tfrac{a-1-t^{2}
}{2}$.

Pick two distinct elements $t_1,t_2\in S$ (possible since $|S|\ge2$). Then for e
very $x$,
$$\phi_{t_1}(x)-\phi_{t_2}(x)=(t_1-t_2)\,x+\frac{t_2^{2}-t_1^{2}}{2}=(t_1-t_2)\B
igl(x-\frac{t_1+t_2}{2}\Bigr).$$
Both $\phi_{t_1}(x),\phi_{t_2}(x)\in S$, and since $t_1-t_2\ne0$, the right-hand
 side ranges over all of $\mathbb{R}$ as $x$ varies. Therefore
$$S-S=\mathbb{R}. \tag{D}$$

**Step 4. $f$ is quadratic.** Subtracting two instances of (G) for $t_1,t_2\in S
$:
$$f(x-t_1)-f(x-t_2)=(t_1-t_2)\Bigl(x-\frac{t_1+t_2}{2}\Bigr).$$
Set $u=x-t_2$ and $d=t_2-t_1$; then $d\in S-S=\mathbb{R}$ by (D), and
$$f(u+d)-f(u)=-du-\frac{d^{2}}{2}, \qquad \forall\,u,d\in\mathbb{R}. \tag{H}$$
Setting $u=0$ in (H): $f(d)=a-\dfrac{d^{2}}{2}$ for all $d\in\mathbb{R}$, i.e.
$$f(x)=a-\frac{x^{2}}{2}. \tag{Q}$$

**Step 5. Determining $a$.** Substitute (Q) into $(\star)$. We have $f(y)=a-\tfr
ac{y^{2}}{2}$, $f(f(y))=a-\tfrac{(a-y^{2}/2)^{2}}{2}$, and
$$\text{LHS}=a-\frac{(x-a+y^{2}/2)^{2}}{2}=a-\frac{x^{2}}{2}-\frac{(a-y^{2}/2)^{
2}}{2}+x\Bigl(a-\frac{y^{2}}{2}\Bigr),$$
$$\text{RHS}=a-\frac{(a-y^{2}/2)^{2}}{2}+x\Bigl(a-\frac{y^{2}}{2}\Bigr)+\Bigl(a-
\frac{x^{2}}{2}\Bigr)-1=2a-1-\frac{(a-y^{2}/2)^{2}}{2}+x\Bigl(a-\frac{y^{2}}{2}\
Bigr)-\frac{x^{2}}{2}.$$
All terms cancel except $a$ on the left and $2a-1$ on the right, giving $a=1$.

**Step 6. Verification.** With $a=1$, $f(x)=1-\dfrac{x^{2}}{2}$. The computation
 above shows LHS$=$RHS identically, so $(\star)$ is satisfied.

**Conclusion.** The unique solution is
$$\boxed{\,f(x)=1-\dfrac{x^{2}}{2}\,}.$$
