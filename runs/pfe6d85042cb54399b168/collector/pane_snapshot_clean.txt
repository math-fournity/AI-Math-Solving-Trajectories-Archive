## Solution

We evaluate the line integral using **Stokes' theorem**:

$$\oint_C \vec{F}\cdot d\vec{r} = \iint_S (\nabla \times \vec{F})\cdot \hat{n}\,
 dS$$

where $\vec{F} = (xy^2,\; x^2y,\; z^3)$ and $S$ is any surface bounded by $C$ (w
e may take the portion of the plane $x+y+z=1$ inside the cylinder $x^2+y^2=4$).

### Computing the curl

$$\nabla \times \vec{F} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ \partia
l_x & \partial_y & \partial_z \\ xy^2 & x^2y & z^3 \end{vmatrix}$$

- $\hat{i}$-component: $\dfrac{\partial(z^3)}{\partial y} - \dfrac{\partial(x^2y
)}{\partial z} = 0 - 0 = 0$
- $\hat{j}$-component: $\dfrac{\partial(xy^2)}{\partial z} - \dfrac{\partial(z^3
)}{\partial x} = 0 - 0 = 0$
- $\hat{k}$-component: $\dfrac{\partial(x^2y)}{\partial x} - \dfrac{\partial(xy^
2)}{\partial y} = 2xy - 2xy = 0$

Hence

$$\nabla \times \vec{F} = (0,\,0,\,0).$$

### Applying Stokes' theorem

Since the curl vanishes identically, for **any** surface $S$ bounded by $C$,

$$\oint_C xy^2\,dx + x^2y\,dy + z^3\,dz = \iint_S (0,0,0)\cdot \hat{n}\, dS = 0.
$$

### Verification (conservative field observation)

Note that $\vec{F}$ is in fact conservative: a scalar potential is

$$\phi(x,y,z) = \tfrac{1}{2}x^2y^2 + \tfrac{1}{4}z^4,$$

since $\nabla\phi = (xy^2,\, x^2y,\, z^3) = \vec{F}$. A conservative field has z
ero curl, and its line integral over any closed curve is $0$. This independently
 confirms the result, regardless of the particular choice of $C$.

### Answer

$$\boxed{\oint_C xy^2\,dx + x^2y\,dy + z^3\,dz = 0}$$
