## Proof

Let $S$ be a closed oriented surface of genus $g$, and let $X = \{p_1, \dots, p_
n\}$ be a finite set of $n$ points on $S$.

**Step 1: Replace $S \setminus X$ by a compact surface with boundary.**

Choose pairwise disjoint closed disk neighborhoods $D_1, \dots, D_n$ around each
 $p_i$. Set $\bar{S} = S \setminus \bigcup_i \operatorname{int}(D_i)$. This is a
 compact oriented surface of genus $g$ with $n$ boundary circles. Since each pun
ctured disk $D_i \setminus \{p_i\}$ deformation retracts onto $\partial D_i$, th
e inclusion $\bar{S} \hookrightarrow S \setminus X$ is a deformation retract. He
nce
$$H^1(S \setminus X;\, \mathbb{C}) \cong H^1(\bar{S};\, \mathbb{C}).$$

**Step 2: Compute $\dim H^1(S \setminus X;\, \mathbb{C})$.**

The surface $\bar{S}$ has Euler characteristic $\chi(\bar{S}) = 2 - 2g - n$. Its
 fundamental group is free of rank $1 - \chi(\bar{S}) = 2g + n - 1$, so $\bar{S}
$ deformation retracts onto a wedge of $2g + n - 1$ circles. Thus
$$H_1(\bar{S};\, \mathbb{C}) \cong \mathbb{C}^{2g+n-1}, \qquad H^1(\bar{S};\, \m
athbb{C}) \cong \mathbb{C}^{2g+n-1}.$$

**Step 3: Relate $H^1(S, X;\, \mathbb{C})$ to $H^1(\bar{S}, \partial \bar{S};\,
\mathbb{C})$ via excision.**

Each $D_i$ is a contractible neighborhood of $p_i$, so the inclusion $X \hookrig
htarrow \bigcup_i D_i$ is a homotopy equivalence of pairs. By excision of the in
teriors $\bigcup_i \operatorname{int}(D_i)$:
$$H^1(S,\, X;\, \mathbb{C}) \cong H^1\!\left(S,\, \textstyle\bigcup_i D_i;\, \ma
thbb{C}\right) \cong H^1(\bar{S},\, \partial \bar{S};\, \mathbb{C}).$$

**Step 4: Apply Poincaré–Lefschetz duality.**

The surface $\bar{S}$ is a compact oriented $2$-manifold with boundary $\partial
 \bar{S}$. Poincaré–Lefschetz duality gives a canonical isomorphism
$$H^1(\bar{S},\, \partial \bar{S};\, \mathbb{C}) \;\cong\; H_{2-1}(\bar{S};\, \m
athbb{C}) = H_1(\bar{S};\, \mathbb{C}) \cong \mathbb{C}^{2g+n-1}.$$

**Step 5: Conclude.**

Combining Steps 2–4:
$$H^1(S \setminus X;\, \mathbb{C}) \cong \mathbb{C}^{2g+n-1} \cong H^1(S,\, X;\,
 \mathbb{C}).$$

Both cohomology groups are complex vector spaces of the same dimension $2g + n -
 1$, and therefore are isomorphic. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The isomorphism can be made explicit and natural: composing the defo
rmation retraction $H^1(S \setminus X) \xrightarrow{\sim} H^1(\bar{S})$ with Poi
ncaré–Lefschetz duality $H^1(\bar{S}, \partial \bar{S}) \xrightarrow{\sim} H_1(\
bar{S})$ and the de Rham/universal-coefficient identification $H^1(\bar{S}) \con
g H_1(\bar{S})^*$ (using the intersection pairing on $\bar{S}$, which is nondege
nerate modulo boundary) yields a canonical isomorphism $H^1(S \setminus X; \math
bb{C}) \simeq H^1(S, X; \mathbb{C})$.
