Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every non-zero Lie algebra
over an algebraically closed field has an ad-nilpotent element (a non-zero eleme
nt $x$ with $\text{ad}(x)$ nilpotent).

**Answer: Yes** (for finite-dimensional Lie algebras, the standard setting).

## Proof

**Theorem.** Every non-zero finite-dimensional Lie algebra $L$ over an algebraic
ally closed field $k$ contains a non-zero ad-nilpotent element.

**Proof.** Let $R$ be the solvable radical of $L$. We split into two cases.

---

### Case 1: $R \neq 0$ (the solvable case)

Since $R$ is solvable, its derived series $R^{(0)}=R \supset R^{(1)}=[R,R] \sups
et R^{(2)} \supset \cdots$ terminates. Let $A = R^{(m)}$ be the last non-zero te
rm, so $A \neq 0$ and $[A,A]=0$; thus $A$ is a non-zero **abelian** Lie algebra.

**$A$ is an ideal of $L$:** Each $R^{(i)}$ is a characteristic ideal of $R$ (pre
served by every derivation of $R$). Since $R \trianglelefteq L$, each $\text{ad}
(x)$ for $x \in L$ restricts to a derivation of $R$, hence preserves $A$. So $A
\trianglelefteq L$.

**Finding the ad-nilpotent element:** Take any non-zero $a \in A$. Since $A$ is
an ideal:
$$\text{ad}(a)(L) = [a, L] \subseteq A.$$
Since $A$ is abelian:
$$\text{ad}(a)(A) = [a, A] = 0.$$
Therefore $\text{ad}(a)^2(L) \subseteq \text{ad}(a)(A) = 0$, giving $\text{ad}(a
)^2 = 0$. So $a$ is a **non-zero ad-nilpotent element**. ✓

---

### Case 2: $R = 0$ (the semisimple case)

$L$ is semisimple. Since $L \neq 0$ and $L$ is semisimple, $L$ is **not nilpoten
t** (nilpotent ⟹ solvable ⟹ $L \subseteq R = 0$, contradiction).

**Cartan subalgebra and weight decomposition.** Let $H$ be a Cartan subalgebra o
f $L$ (which exists for any finite-dimensional Lie algebra over any field). Sinc
e $H$ is nilpotent and $L$ is not, $H \neq L$.

Over the algebraically closed field $k$, $L$ admits the **generalized weight spa
ce decomposition** with respect to $H$:
$$L = L_0 \oplus \bigoplus_{\alpha \in \Phi} L_\alpha,$$
where $L_0 = H$ (a defining property of Cartan subalgebras), and $\Phi = \{\alph
a \in H^* \setminus \{0\} : L_\alpha \neq 0\}$ is the set of non-zero weights. S
ince $H \neq L$, we have $\Phi \neq \emptyset$.

**Key property:** The weight spaces satisfy $[L_\alpha, L_\beta] \subseteq L_{\a
lpha+\beta}$ (with $L_\gamma = 0$ if $\gamma \notin \Phi \cup \{0\}$). This foll
ows from the Jacobi identity and the derivation property of $\text{ad}(h)$.

**Finding the ad-nilpotent element:** Pick any $\alpha \in \Phi$ and any non-zer
o $x \in L_\alpha$. Then:
- $\text{ad}(x)(L_0) = \text{ad}(x)(H) \subseteq L_\alpha$
- $\text{ad}(x)(L_\beta) \subseteq L_{\alpha+\beta}$ for each weight $\beta$

Iterating: $\text{ad}(x)^k(L_\beta) \subseteq L_{k\alpha + \beta}$ for all $k \g
eq 0$ and all $\beta \in \Phi \cup \{0\}$.

Since $\Phi \cup \{0\}$ is a **finite** set (as $L$ is finite-dimensional), for
sufficiently large $k$, we have $k\alpha + \beta \notin \Phi \cup \{0\}$ for eve
ry $\beta \in \Phi \cup \{0\}$. Hence $L_{k\alpha+\beta} = 0$ for all such $\bet
a$, giving $\text{ad}(x)^k = 0$.

So $x$ is a **non-zero ad-nilpotent element**. ✓

---

### Conclusion

In both cases ($R \neq 0$ and $R = 0$), the non-zero Lie algebra $L$ contains a
non-zero ad-nilpotent element. The algebraically closed hypothesis is essential:
 it guarantees the existence of the generalized weight space decomposition (eige
nvalues of $\text{ad}(h)$ lie in $k$). $\blacksquare$

**Remark on the abelian case:** If $L$ is abelian and non-zero, then $\text{ad}(
x) = 0$ for all $x$, so every non-zero element is ad-nilpotent — this is subsume
d by Case 1 (an abelian Lie algebra is its own radical, and $A = R = L$).
