The answer is **yes**, the equality holds.

**Proof.**

Recall that a bounded set $E \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is *Jordan measurable* precis
ely when its characteristic function $\chi_E$ is Riemann integrable, and the Jor
dan content is $c(E) = \int \chi_E$ (Riemann integral). A classical characteriza
tion states:

> A bounded set $E$ is Jordan measurable if and only if its boundary $\partial E
$ has Lebesgue measure zero, i.e., $m(\partial E) = 0$.

Moreover, when $E$ is Jordan measurable, the Riemann integral of $\chi_E$ agrees
 with its Lebesgue integral, so
$$c(E) = \int \chi_E \, dR = \int \chi_E \, dm = m(E),$$
where $m$ denotes Lebesgue measure. In other words, **Jordan content coincides w
ith Lebesgue measure on the class of Jordan measurable sets.**

Now apply this to the given data:

- Each $A_n \in \mathcal{D}_I$ is Jordan measurable, so $c(A_n) = m(A_n)$ for ev
ery $n$.
- The union $A := \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n$ is in $\mathcal{D}_I$, hence Jorda
n measurable, so $c(A) = m(A)$.
- The sets $A_n$ are mutually disjoint.

By countable additivity of Lebesgue measure for disjoint measurable sets,
$$m(A) = m\!\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n\right) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} m(A_n)
.$$

Substituting the equalities $c(A) = m(A)$ and $c(A_n) = m(A_n)$, we obtain
$$c\!\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n\right) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} c(A_n).$$

Thus Jordan content is countably additive on any disjoint family of Jordan measu
rable sets whose union is itself Jordan measurable. $\blacksquare$
