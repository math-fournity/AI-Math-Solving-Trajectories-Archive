## Solution

**Setting up the ratio.** Let $r = x/y$ (valid since $y \neq 0$). Substituting $
x = ry$ into the constraint:

$$y^2(r^2y^2 - ry^2 + y^2) + r^3y^3(ry - y) = 0$$
$$y^4(r^2 - r + 1) + r^3y^4(r-1) = 0$$

Dividing by $y^4 \neq 0$:

$$r^2 - r + 1 + r^3(r-1) = 0 \implies r^4 - r^3 + r^2 - r + 1 = 0.$$

**Identifying the root of unity.** Multiplying by $(r+1)$:

$$(r+1)(r^4 - r^3 + r^2 - r + 1) = r^5 + 1 = 0,$$

so $r^5 = -1$, i.e., $r^{10} = 1$ and $r^5 \neq 1$. Since $r = -1$ gives $r^4 -
r^3 + r^2 - r + 1 = 5 \neq 0$, we have $r \neq -1$. Thus $r$ is a **primitive 10
th root of unity** (order exactly 10).

**Rewriting the sum.** The target expression is

$$\sum_{m=0}^{29}\sum_{n=0}^{29} \left(\frac{x}{y}\right)^{18mn} = \sum_{m=0}^{2
9}\sum_{n=0}^{29} r^{18mn}.$$

Since $r^{10} = 1$ and $18 \equiv 8 \pmod{10}$, we have $r^{18mn} = r^{8mn}$. Le
t $\omega = r^8$. Because $\gcd(8,10) = 2$, the order of $\omega$ is $10/2 = 5$,
 so $\omega$ is a **primitive 5th root of unity**. The sum becomes

$$S = \sum_{m=0}^{29}\sum_{n=0}^{29} \omega^{mn}.$$

**Evaluating the inner sum.** For fixed $m$:

- If $5 \mid m$: then $\omega^{mn} = (\omega^5)^n = 1$ for all $n$, so $\sum_{n=
0}^{29} \omega^{mn} = 30$.
- If $5 \nmid m$: then $\omega^m$ is a primitive 5th root of unity, and the 30 t
erms form 6 complete cycles of period 5:
  $$\sum_{n=0}^{29} (\omega^m)^n = 6\sum_{n=0}^{4}(\omega^m)^n = 6 \cdot \frac{1
 - (\omega^m)^5}{1 - \omega^m} = 6 \cdot 0 = 0.$$

**Counting.** Among $m = 0, 1, \dots, 29$, the multiples of 5 are $0, 5, 10, 15,
 20, 25$ — exactly **6 values**, each contributing 30. All other values of $m$ c
ontribute 0.

Therefore:

$$S = 6 \times 30 = \boxed{180}.$$
