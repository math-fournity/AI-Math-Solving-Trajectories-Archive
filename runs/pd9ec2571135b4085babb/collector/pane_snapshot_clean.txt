## Proof

Let $A = \{(a^2+1,\, a^3+a) \mid a \in \mathbb{R}\} \subset \mathbb{R}^2$. Write
 a point of $A$ as $(x,y) = (a^2+1,\, a^3+a)$.

**Step 1: Find the algebraic relation.** Since $y = a^3+a = a(a^2+1) = ax$, we h
ave $a = y/x$ (whenever $x \neq 0$, and indeed $x = a^2+1 \geq 1 > 0$ always). S
ubstituting back into $x = a^2+1$:

$$x = \frac{y^2}{x^2} + 1 \implies x^3 = y^2 + x^2 \implies y^2 = x^2(x-1).$$

Equivalently, every point of $A$ satisfies

$$f(x,y) := x^3 - x^2 - y^2 = 0.$$

Hence $A \subseteq V(f) := \{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^2 : f(x,y)=0\}$.

**Step 2: $f$ is irreducible in $\mathbb{R}[x,y]$, so $V(f)$ is an irreducible c
urve.** View $f = -(y^2 - x^2(x-1))$ as a quadratic in $y$ over the field $\math
bb{R}(x)$. It is reducible over $\mathbb{R}(x)$ iff $x^2(x-1)$ is a square in $\
mathbb{R}(x)$, i.e. iff $x-1$ is a square in $\mathbb{R}(x)$. But $x-1$ has a si
mple zero at $x=1$, so it is not a square. Therefore $y^2 - x^2(x-1)$ is irreduc
ible over $\mathbb{R}(x)$, and by Gauss's lemma irreducible in $\mathbb{R}[x,y]$
. Thus $\langle f\rangle$ is a prime ideal and $V(f)$ is an irreducible algebrai
c set of dimension $1$.

**Step 3: The Zariski closure of $A$ equals $V(f)$.** The Zariski closure $\over
line{A}$ is the smallest algebraic set containing $A$. Since $V(f)$ is algebraic
 and contains $A$, we have $\overline{A} \subseteq V(f)$. The set $A$ is infinit
e (the parametrization $a \mapsto (a^2+1, a^3+a)$ is injective, since $a$ is rec
overed from $y/x$). Because $V(f)$ is irreducible of dimension $1$, every proper
 algebraic subset of $V(f)$ is finite. Since $\overline{A}$ is an algebraic subs
et of $V(f)$ containing the infinite set $A$, it cannot be proper; hence $\overl
ine{A} = V(f)$.

**Conclusion.** The Zariski closure of $A$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ is

$$\boxed{\overline{A} = V(x^3 - x^2 - y^2) = \{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^2 : y^2 = x^2(
x-1)\}}.$$
