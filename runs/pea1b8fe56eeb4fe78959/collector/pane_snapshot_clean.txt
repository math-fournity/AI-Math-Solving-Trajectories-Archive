## Proof

**Claim.** The function $f(z) = z^{-3}$ is analytic on $\mathbb{C} \setminus \ma
thbb{D}$ and belongs to $L^p(\mathbb{C} \setminus \mathbb{D})$ for every $p$ wit
h $1 \leq p < \infty$.

**Analyticity.** The function $f(z) = 1/z^3$ is holomorphic on $\mathbb{C} \setm
inus \{0\}$. Since $\{0\} \subset \mathbb{D}$ (the closed unit disk), we have $\
mathbb{C} \setminus \mathbb{D} \subset \mathbb{C} \setminus \{0\}$, so $f$ is an
alytic on $\mathbb{C} \setminus \mathbb{D} = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| > 1\}$. Cl

**$L^p$ integrability.** We compute the $L^p$ norm of $f$ over $\mathbb{C} \setm
inus \mathbb{D}$ with respect to two-dimensional Lebesgue (area) measure. Passin
g to polar coordinates $z = re^{i\theta}$, where $dA(z) = r\,dr\,d\theta$:

$$\int_{\mathbb{C} \setminus \mathbb{D}} |f(z)|^p \, dA(z) = \int_1^{\infty} \in
t_0^{2\pi} |z^{-3}|^p \, r\,d\theta\,dr = \int_1^{\infty} \int_0^{2\pi} r^{-3p}
\cdot r \, d\theta\,dr$$

$$= 2\pi \int_1^{\infty} r^{1-3p}\,dr.$$

This improper integral converges at $r \to \infty$ if and only if the exponent $
1 - 3p < -1$, i.e., $3p > 2$, i.e., $p > 2/3$.

Since the problem assumes $p \geq 1 > 2/3$, the integral converges. Explicitly,
for $p \geq 1$:

$$\int_{\mathbb{C} \setminus \mathbb{D}} |f(z)|^p \, dA(z) = 2\pi \cdot \frac{1}
{3p - 2} < \infty.$$

Therefore $f(z) = z^{-3} \in L^p(\mathbb{C} \setminus \mathbb{D})$ for all $1 \l
eq p < \infty$, and $f$ is nonzero and analytic on $\mathbb{C} \setminus \mathbb
{D}$.

**Remark.** More generally, $f(z) = z^{-n}$ belongs to $L^p(\mathbb{C}\setminus\
mathbb{D})$ if and only if $np > 2$. For $p = 1$, one needs $n \geq 3$; for $p >
 1$, $n \geq 2$ suffices. The choice $n = 3$ works uniformly for all $p \geq 1$.
