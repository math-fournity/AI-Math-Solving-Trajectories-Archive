## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. For any fixed $g \in G$, the left-translation map $L_g \colon G
\to G$, $L_g(h) = g \cdot h$, is an isometry of $G$ with respect to any bi-invar
iant Riemannian metric.

**Proof.**

Recall that a Riemannian metric $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$ on a Lie group $G
$ is called **bi-invariant** if it is invariant under both left translations and
 right translations. Concretely, for all $a, x \in G$ and all tangent vectors $u
, v \in T_x G$:

$$
\langle (dL_a)_x u,\; (dL_a)_x v \rangle_{ax} = \langle u, v \rangle_x \quad \te
xt{(left-invariance)},
$$

and

$$
\langle (dR_a)_x u,\; (dR_a)_x v \rangle_{xa} = \langle u, v \rangle_x \quad \te
xt{(right-invariance)}.
$$

Bi-invariance therefore **includes** left-invariance as part of its definition.
In particular, for the fixed element $g \in G$, taking $a = g$ in the left-invar
iance condition gives, for every $h \in G$ and every $u, v \in T_h G$:

$$
\langle (dL_g)_h u,\; (dL_g)_h v \rangle_{gh} = \langle u, v \rangle_h.
$$

This is precisely the statement that the differential of $L_g$ preserves the inn
er product on every tangent space. Since $L_g$ is a diffeomorphism (its inverse
is $L_{g^{-1}}$) and its differential is a linear isometry $T_h G \to T_{gh} G$
at every point $h$, the map $L_g$ is a Riemannian isometry of $(G, \langle \cdot
, \cdot \rangle)$.

**Remark on the role of semi-simplicity.** The hypothesis that $G$ is semi-simpl
e is not needed for this conclusion. What matters is the existence of a bi-invar
iant metric. Every compact Lie group (hence every compact semi-simple Lie group)
 admits a bi-invariant metric via averaging any inner product over the group (us
ing the Haar measure). A non-compact semi-simple Lie group need not admit a bi-i
nvariant Riemannian metric at all; but the problem *assumes* such a metric is gi
ven, and once it is given, left-invariance is part of what "bi-invariant" means.
 Thus the conclusion holds under the stated hypotheses regardless of compactness
.

**Conclusion.** Under a bi-invariant metric, every left translation $L_g$ (and,
by the symmetric part of bi-invariance, every right translation $R_g$) is an iso
metry. Hence the answer is **yes**.
