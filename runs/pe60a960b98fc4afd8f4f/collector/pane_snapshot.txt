# Solution

## Setup and Simplification

Let $g: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be the Heaviside step function:
$$g(t) = \begin{cases} 1 & t \geq 0 \\ 0 & t < 0 \end{cases}$$

Let $x_i^T$ denote the $i$-th row of $X$, so $(X\beta)_i = x_i^T \beta$. Define
$$f(\beta) = \|g(X\beta)\|_2^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{n} g\!\left(x_i^T \beta\right)^2.$$

**Key observation.** Since $g(t) \in \{0, 1\}$ for all $t$, we have $g(t)^2 = g(
t)$. Therefore
$$f(\beta) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} g\!\left(x_i^T \beta\right). \tag{1}$$

This is a sum of step functions of linear forms in $\beta$, hence $f$ is **piece
wise constant** on $\mathbb{R}^d$.

---

## Classical Derivative

**Claim.** $\nabla_\beta f(\beta) = \mathbf{0}$ for every $\beta \in \mathbb{R}^
d$ such that $x_i^T \beta \neq 0$ for all $i = 1, \dots, n$.

*Proof.* Fix such a $\beta$. For each $i$, since $x_i^T \beta \neq 0$, there exi
sts an open neighborhood $U_i \ni \beta$ on which $\operatorname{sgn}(x_i^T \bet
a')$ is constant (by continuity of the linear map $\beta' \mapsto x_i^T \beta'$)
. On $U_i$, the value $g(x_i^T \beta')$ is constant. Let $U = \bigcap_i U_i$; on
 $U$, every summand in (1) is constant, so $f$ is constant on $U$. Hence $\nabla
_\beta f(\beta) = \mathbf{0}$. $\square$

At any $\beta$ where $x_i^T \beta = 0$ for some $i$, the function $f$ has a **ju
mp discontinuity** along the hyperplane $\{\beta : x_i^T \beta = 0\}$, so the cl
assical gradient does not exist there.

---

## Distributional Derivative

In the sense of distributions, the derivative of the Heaviside function is the D
irac delta: $g'(t) = \delta(t)$. Differentiating (1) componentwise:

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial \beta_j} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} g'\!\left(x_i^T \beta\rig
ht) \cdot x_{ij} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \delta\!\left(x_i^T \beta\right)\, x_{ij}.$$

Stacking into a vector:

$$\boxed{\nabla_\beta \,\|g(X\beta)\|_2^2 \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{n} \delta\!\left(x_i
^T \beta\right)\, x_i \;=\; X^\top \,\delta(X\beta),} \tag{2}$$

where $\delta(X\beta) := \bigl(\delta(x_1^T\beta),\, \dots,\, \delta(x_n^T\beta)
\bigr)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the componentwise Dirac delta.

**Derivation detail.** Since $g(t)^2 = g(t)$, we differentiate $g(x_i^T\beta)$ d
irectly (no chain-rule factor of $2g$ needed):
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial \beta_j} g(x_i^T \beta) = \delta(x_i^T \beta)\, x_{ij
},$$
and summing over $i$ gives (2). The result is a distribution-valued vector conce
ntrated on the union of hyperplanes $\bigcup_i \{\beta : x_i^T \beta = 0\}$.

## Summary

| Setting | Result |
|---|---|
| Classical (a.e.) | $\nabla_\beta \|g(X\beta)\|_2^2 = \mathbf{0}$, valid wherev
er $x_i^T\beta \neq 0\ \forall i$ |
| Classical (boundary) | Does not exist (jump discontinuities on $\bigcup_i\{x_i
^T\beta=0\}$) |
| Distributional | $\nabla_\beta \|g(X\beta)\|_2^2 = X^\top\,\delta(X\beta)$ |

The gradient vanishes classically almost everywhere because the step function re
nders $\|g(X\beta)\|_2^2$ piecewise constant; the distributional gradient is a s
um of Dirac masses supported on the hyperplanes where $X\beta$ has a zero compon
ent, weighted by the corresponding rows of $X$.
