## Solution

### Setup

The Hamilton–Jacobi equation for a time-independent Hamiltonian is obtained by r
eplacing each momentum $p_i$ with $\partial S/\partial q_i$ and setting $H = E$:

$$\frac{1}{2m}\left[\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial r}\right)^2 + \frac{1}{r^{2
}}\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial \theta}\right)^2 + \frac{1}{r^{2}\sin^{2}\the
ta}\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial \phi}\right)^2\right] + V(r) = E. \tag{1}$$

We seek a **complete integral** $S(E, L, L_z, r, \theta, \phi)$: a solution depe
nding on three independent constants (the system has three degrees of freedom),
from which the general solution of the equations of motion is obtained by differ
entiating with respect to those constants.

### Separation of variables

We try the additive ansatz

$$S = S_r(r) + S_\theta(\theta) + S_\phi(\phi). \tag{2}$$

**Azimuthal part.** The coordinate $\phi$ does not appear in $H$ (it is cyclic),
 so (1) depends on $\phi$ only through $(\partial S/\partial\phi)^2$. For (1) to
 hold for all $\phi$, $\partial S/\partial\phi$ must be a constant, which we cal
l $L_z$:

$$\frac{dS_\phi}{d\phi} = L_z \quad\Longrightarrow\quad S_\phi(\phi) = L_z\,\phi
. \tag{3}$$

Substituting back into (1):

$$\frac{1}{2m}\left[\left(\frac{dS_r}{dr}\right)^2 + \frac{1}{r^{2}}\left(\frac{
dS_\theta}{d\theta}\right)^2 + \frac{L_z^{2}}{r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\right] + V(r)
 = E. \tag{4}$$

**Polar part.** Multiply (4) by $2m$ and isolate the $r$-dependent and $\theta$-
dependent pieces:

$$\left(\frac{dS_r}{dr}\right)^2 + \frac{1}{r^{2}}\underbrace{\left[\left(\frac{
dS_\theta}{d\theta}\right)^2 + \frac{L_z^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta}\right]}_{\displays
tyle =:\;L^{2}} = 2m\bigl(E - V(r)\bigr). \tag{5}$$

The bracketed quantity depends only on $\theta$, while the rest depends on $r$ a
nd $1/r^2$. The only way a purely $\theta$-dependent function equals (through th
e factor $1/r^2$) an $r$-dependent expression for all $(r,\theta)$ is for the br
acket to be a **constant**, which we denote $L^2$:

$$\left(\frac{dS_\theta}{d\theta}\right)^2 + \frac{L_z^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta} = L^
{2}. \tag{6}$$

Hence

$$\frac{dS_\theta}{d\theta} = \pm\sqrt{L^{2} - \frac{L_z^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta}}\,
, \tag{7}$$

and

$$S_\theta(\theta) = \pm\int^{\theta}\sqrt{L^{2} - \frac{L_z^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta
'}}\,d\theta'. \tag{8}$$

**Radial part.** With (6), equation (5) reduces to a purely radial equation:

$$\left(\frac{dS_r}{dr}\right)^2 + \frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}} = 2m\bigl(E - V(r)\bigr),
 \tag{9}$$

so

$$\frac{dS_r}{dr} = \pm\sqrt{2m\bigl(E - V(r)\bigr) - \frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}}\,, \t
ag{10}$$

and

$$S_r(r) = \pm\int^{r}\sqrt{2m\bigl(E - V(r')\bigr) - \frac{L^{2}}{r'^{2}}}\,dr'
. \tag{11}$$

### The complete integral

Assembling (2), (3), (8), and (11):

$$\boxed{\;S(E, L, L_z;\, r, \theta, \phi) \;=\; \pm\int^{r}\sqrt{\,2m\bigl(E -
V(r')\bigr) - \frac{L^{2}}{r'^{2}}\,}\,dr' \;\pm\; \int^{\theta}\sqrt{\,L^{2} -
\frac{L_z^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta'}\,}\,d\theta' \;+\; L_z\,\phi.\;} \tag{12}$$

### Verification that this is a complete integral

1. **It solves the HJ equation.** By construction, $\partial S/\partial r$, $\pa
rtial S/\partial\theta$, $\partial S/\partial\phi$ reproduce exactly the momenta
 that appear in (1), and the separation constants were chosen so that (1) is ide
ntically satisfied.

2. **It depends on three independent constants** $(E, L, L_z)$ — one per degree
of freedom — as required for a complete integral of a 3-DOF system.

3. **Non-degeneracy.** The Jacobian $\det\!\bigl(\partial^2 S/\partial q_i\,\par
tial\alpha_j\bigr)$ (with $q_i \in \{r,\theta,\phi\}$, $\alpha_j \in \{E, L, L_z
\}$) is generically non-vanishing:
   - $\partial^2 S/\partial\phi\,\partial L_z = 1$,
   - $\partial^2 S/\partial\theta\,\partial L = \pm L/\sqrt{L^2 - L_z^2/\sin^2\t
heta}$ (generically $\neq 0$),
   - $\partial^2 S/\partial r\,\partial E = \pm m/\sqrt{2m(E-V)-L^2/r^2}$ (gener
ically $\neq 0$),

   so the three constants are functionally independent and the usual HJ construc
tion of the trajectory (set $\partial S/\partial\alpha_j = \beta_j$, solve for $
q_i$) goes through.

### Physical interpretation of the constants

- **$E$** — total energy.
- **$L_z$** — $z$-component of angular momentum (conjugate to the cyclic coordin
ate $\phi$).
- **$L^2$** — squared magnitude of the total angular momentum; equation (6) is p
recisely the statement $|\mathbf{L}|^2 = L_\theta^2 + L_z^2/\sin^2\theta$, i.e.
the decomposition of $\mathbf{L}^2$ in spherical coordinates. The separation suc
ceeds because the central potential makes $\mathbf{L}^2$ and $L_z$ conserved, gi
ving the three mutually commuting integrals of motion $(H, \mathbf{L}^2, L_z)$.
