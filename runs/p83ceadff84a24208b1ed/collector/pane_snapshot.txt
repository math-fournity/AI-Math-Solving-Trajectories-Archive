## Proof

We seek distinct positive integers $a < b$ such that all three means are positiv
e integers, minimizing $b - a$.

**Reduction via gcd.** Let $g = \gcd(a,b)$, with $a = g x$, $b = g y$, $\gcd(x,y
)=1$.

The geometric mean condition $\sqrt{ab} \in \mathbb{Z}$ gives $\sqrt{g^2 xy} = g
\sqrt{xy} \in \mathbb{Z}$, so $xy$ is a perfect square. Since $\gcd(x,y)=1$, bot
h $x$ and $y$ must be perfect squares: $x = m^2$, $y = n^2$ with $\gcd(m,n)=1$,
$m < n$. Thus
$$a = g m^2, \qquad b = g n^2.$$

**Arithmetic mean.** $\frac{a+b}{2} = \frac{g(m^2+n^2)}{2} \in \mathbb{Z}$.

**Harmonic mean.** $\frac{2ab}{a+b} = \frac{2g^2 m^2 n^2}{g(m^2+n^2)} = \frac{2g
\, m^2 n^2}{m^2+n^2} \in \mathbb{Z}$.

Let $S = m^2 + n^2$. Since $\gcd(m,n)=1$, we have $\gcd(m^2 n^2, S)=1$ (any prim
e dividing both $m^2 n^2$ and $m^2+n^2$ would divide one of $m,n$ and hence both
 — contradicting coprimality). Therefore $S \mid 2g$.

Write $2g = kS$ for a positive integer $k$, so $g = kS/2$ (requiring $kS$ even).

**Rechecking the arithmetic mean** with $g = kS/2$:
$$\frac{a+b}{2} = \frac{gS}{2} = \frac{kS^2}{4} \in \mathbb{Z} \implies 4 \mid k
S^2.$$

**The harmonic mean** becomes $\frac{2g\,m^2 n^2}{S} = k\, m^2 n^2$, which is au
tomatically an integer. Good — no further constraint.

**Minimizing the difference.** We have
$$b - a = g(n^2 - m^2) = \frac{kS}{2}(n^2 - m^2).$$

We minimize over coprime $1 \le m < n$ and valid $k$.

**Case A: $S$ even.** Then $kS$ is automatically even, and $4 \mid kS^2$ automat
ically (since $S^2$ divisible by $4$). Minimum $k=1$, giving $g = S/2$ and
$$b - a = \tfrac{S}{2}(n^2 - m^2).$$

**Case B: $S$ odd.** Then $S^2$ is odd, so $4 \mid kS^2$ forces $4 \mid k$. Also
 $kS$ even forces $k$ even (consistent). Minimum $k=4$, giving $g = 2S$ and
$$b - a = 2S(n^2 - m^2).$$

**Enumerating small coprime pairs $(m,n)$:**

| $(m,n)$ | $S=m^2+n^2$ | parity | min $g$ | $n^2-m^2$ | $b-a$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $(1,2)$ | $5$ | odd | $10$ | $3$ | **30** |
| $(1,3)$ | $10$ | even | $5$ | $8$ | $40$ |
| $(2,3)$ | $13$ | odd | $26$ | $5$ | $130$ |
| $(3,4)$ | $25$ | odd | $50$ | $7$ | $350$ |
| $(1,4)$ | $17$ | odd | $34$ | $15$ | $510$ |
| $(3,5)$ | $34$ | even | $17$ | $16$ | $272$ |
| $(1,5)$ | $26$ | even | $13$ | $24$ | $312$ |
| $(4,5)$ | $41$ | odd | $82$ | $9$ | $738$ |
| $(5,6)$ | $61$ | odd | $122$ | $11$ | $1342$ |
| $(1,6)$ | $37$ | odd | $74$ | $35$ | $2590$ |

For all larger pairs, $S \ge 50$ and $n^2 - m^2 \ge 9$, so $b-a \ge \tfrac{50}{2
}\cdot 9 = 225 > 30$ (even case) or $b-a \ge 2\cdot 50\cdot 9 = 900$ (odd case).
 Hence no further pair can beat $30$.

**Verification of the minimizer.** Take $(m,n)=(1,2)$, $k=4$, $g=10$:
$$a = 10\cdot 1 = 10, \qquad b = 10\cdot 4 = 40.$$
- Arithmetic mean: $\frac{10+40}{2} = 25$ ✓
- Geometric mean: $\sqrt{10\cdot 40} = \sqrt{400} = 20$ ✓
- Harmonic mean: $\frac{2\cdot 10\cdot 40}{10+40} = \frac{800}{50} = 16$ ✓

All three are positive integers, and $|a-b| = 30$.

**Conclusion.** The smallest possible absolute difference between the two pressu
re levels is

$$\boxed{30}.$$
