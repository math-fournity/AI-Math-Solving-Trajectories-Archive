# Problem

In a futuristic city, an urban planning zone is defined by a perfect square lot $U$ with a side length of 1 kilometer. A telecommunications company is required to place exactly $4n$ signal towers (where $n$ is a specific positive integer) at distinct locations within the interior of this square lot.

The city council mandates that there must be at least one rectangular "exclusive coverage zone" $V$ located entirely within the lot $U$ that satisfies three strict zoning regulations:
1. The boundaries of the rectangular zone $V$ must be perfectly parallel to the boundaries of the square lot $U$.
2. The zone $V$ must contain exactly one signal tower from the $4n$ towers placed.
3. The area of this rectangular zone $V$ must be no less than a specific value $\mu$ (measured in square kilometers).

Given that the towers can be placed in any configuration of $4n$ points within the open square, determine the largest possible value of $\mu$ that the city council can guarantee will always be achievable regardless of how the $4n$ towers are arranged.
