## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. Every Galois extension $K/F$ with $\operatorname{Gal}(K/F) \cong
 A_3$ is the splitting field of an irreducible cubic polynomial over $F$.

**Proof.**

Since $A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ is cyclic of order $3$, we have $:F]
= 3$, and $K/F$ is separable (being Galois).

**Step 1: $K = F(\alpha)$ for some $\alpha \in K$.**

Since $K/F$ is a finite separable extension, it has only finitely many intermedi
ate fields (a standard theorem: embed $K$ into its Galois closure $L/F$; interme
diate fields of $K/F$ are finite in number). By the primitive element theorem, a
 finite extension is simple if and only if it has finitely many intermediate fie
lds. Therefore $K = F(\alpha)$ for some $\alpha \in K$. This holds over any base
 field $F$ (finite or infinite, any characteristic).

**Step 2: The minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ is an irreducible cubic.**

Since $:F] = 3$ and $K = F(\alpha)$, the minimal polynomial $m(x)$ of $\alpha$
 over $F$ has degree $[F(\alpha):F] = 3$. Hence $m(x)$ is an irreducible cubic p
olynomial in $F[x]$.

**Step 3: $m(x)$ splits completely in $K$.**

Since $K/F$ is Galois, it is normal. The polynomial $m(x)$ is irreducible over $
F$ and has the root $\alpha \in K$. By normality, $m(x)$ splits completely in $K
[x]$, so all three roots of $m(x)$ lie in $K$.

**Step 4: $K$ is the splitting field of $m(x)$.**

Let $E$ be the splitting field of $m(x)$ over $F$, i.e., the subfield of $K$ gen
erated over $F$ by all roots of $m(x)$. Since $\alpha$ is one of the roots and $
K = F(\alpha)$, we have $K \subseteq E$. Conversely, since all roots of $m(x)$ l
ie in $K$, we have $E \subseteq K$. Therefore $K = E$, and $K$ is the splitting
field of the irreducible cubic $m(x)$ over $F$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on characteristic $3$.** In characteristic $3$, cyclic cubic extensions
 are described by Artin–Schreier theory: $K = F(\alpha)$ with $\alpha^3 - \alpha
 = a$ for some $a \in F$, and the minimal polynomial $x^3 - x - a$ is an irreduc
ible cubic that splits in $K$. This is consistent with the general argument abov
e—the primitive element theorem and normality handle all characteristics uniform
ly.
