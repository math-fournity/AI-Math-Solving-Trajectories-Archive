Looking at this problem, I need to show that for an acute triangle ABC with orth
ocenter H, a point D on BC, and P such that ADPH is a parallelogram, the angle ∠
BPC > ∠BAC.

**Proof.**

Set coordinates with $B=(0,0)$, $C=(1,0)$, and $A=(a,b)$ with $b>0$. Since $\tri
angle ABC$ is acute:
- Angle at $B$ acute: $a>0$. Angle at $C$ acute: $a<1$.
- Angle at $A$ acute: $\vec{AB}\cdot\vec{AC}=a^2+b^2-a>0$.

**Step 1: Locate $H$ and $P$.**

The altitude from $A$ is $x=a$; the altitude from $B$ (perpendicular to $AC$) gi
ves $H=\bigl(a,\;\frac{a(1-a)}{b}\bigr)$.

In parallelogram $ADPH$ (vertices in order), diagonals bisect: $A+P=D+H$, so $P=
D+H-A$. With $D=(d,0)$, $d\in(0,1)$:
$$P=\left(d,\;\frac{a-a^2-b^2}{b}\right)=(d,\,-p),\qquad p:=\frac{a^2+b^2-a}{b}>
0.$$
So $P$ lies on the opposite side of $BC$ from $A$, at perpendicular distance $p=
|AH|$ from $BC$, with foot $D$.

**Step 2: Circumradii via the extended law of sines.**

$$R_{ABC}=\frac{|AB|\cdot|AC|}{2b},\qquad R_{BPC}=\frac{|PB|\cdot|PC|}{2p},$$
using $\text{Area}(ABC)=\frac{b}{2}$ and $\text{Area}(BPC)=\frac{p}{2}$.

**Step 3: Key identity.**

$$|AB|^2\cdot|AC|^2=(a^2+b^2)\bigl((1-a)^2+b^2\bigr)=(a^2+b^2-a)^2+b^2=p^2b^2+b^
2=b^2(p^2+1).$$
(Both sides expand to $(a^2{+}b^2)^2-2a(a^2{+}b^2)+(a^2{+}b^2)$.)

Hence $|AB|\cdot|AC|=b\sqrt{p^2+1}$ and $R_{ABC}=\frac{\sqrt{p^2+1}}{2}$.

**Step 4: Compare $R_{BPC}$ and $R_{ABC}$.**

With $|PB|^2=d^2+p^2$ and $|PC|^2=(1-d)^2+p^2$:
$$R_{BPC}<R_{ABC}\iff (d^2+p^2)\bigl((1-d)^2+p^2\bigr)<p^2(p^2+1).$$
Expanding the left side using $d^2+(1-d)^2=1-2d(1-d)$:
$$d^2(1-d)^2+p^2\bigl(1-2d(1-d)\bigr)+p^4 < p^4+p^2,$$
$$d^2(1-d)^2-2p^2\,d(1-d)<0,$$
$$d(1-d)\bigl[d(1-d)-2p^2\bigr]<0.$$
For $d\in(0,1)$, $d(1-d)>0$, so this is equivalent to:
$$d(1-d)<2p^2.\qquad(\star)$$

**Step 5: Two cases.**

Compute $\vec{PB}\cdot\vec{PC}=(-d)(1-d)+p^2=p^2-d(1-d)$, so $\cos\angle BPC$ ha
s the sign of $p^2-d(1-d)$.

**Case 1:** $d(1-d)\ge p^2$. Then $\cos\angle BPC\le 0$, so $\angle BPC\ge 90°$.
 Since $\triangle ABC$ is acute, $\angle BAC<90°$, giving $\angle BPC\ge 90°>\an
gle BAC$. ✓

**Case 2:** $d(1-d)<p^2$. Then $\angle BPC<90°$ (acute). By the extended law of
sines applied to both triangles sharing side $BC$:
$$\sin\angle BPC=\frac{BC}{2R_{BPC}},\quad \sin\angle BAC=\frac{BC}{2R_{ABC}}.$$
Both angles being acute, $\angle BPC>\angle BAC\iff\sin\angle BPC>\sin\angle BAC
\iff R_{BPC}<R_{ABC}$, which by Step 4 is equivalent to $(\star)$. In this case
$d(1-d)<p^2<2p^2$ (since $p>0$), so $(\star)$ holds. ✓

**Conclusion.** In both cases $\angle BPC>\angle BAC$ for every $D$ in the inter
ior of $[BC]$. (At the endpoints $D=B$ or $D=C$, one checks $d(1-d)=0$ gives $R_
{BPC}=R_{ABC}$, hence equality.) $\blacksquare$
