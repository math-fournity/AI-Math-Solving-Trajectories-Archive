## Proof

**Setup.** Let $\mathbf{Group}$ be the category of groups and $\mathbf{Ab}$ the
full subcategory of abelian groups. Define:

- $I: \mathbf{Ab} \to \mathbf{Group}$, the inclusion functor: on objects it view
s an abelian group as a group; on morphisms it is the identity on the underlying
 homomorphism.
- $A: \mathbf{Group} \to \mathbf{Ab}$, the abelianizer functor: on objects $A(G)
 = G/[G,G]$, the abelianization; on morphisms $A(f: G \to H)$ is the unique homo
morphism $G/[G,G] \to H/[H,H]$ satisfying $A(f)(g[G,G]) = f(g)[H,H]$.

The composite $IA: \mathbf{Group} \to \mathbf{Group}$ sends $G \mapsto G/[G,G]$
(viewed as a group) and $f \mapsto A(f)$ (viewed as a group homomorphism). The i
dentity functor $1_{\mathbf{Group}}$ sends $G \mapsto G$, $f \mapsto f$.

For each group $G$, let $\pi_G: G \to IA(G) = G/[G,G]$ be the canonical quotient
 homomorphism $g \mapsto g[G,G]$.

**Claim.** The family $\pi = \{\pi_G\}_{G \in \mathbf{Group}}$ is a natural tran
sformation $1_{\mathbf{Group}} \Longrightarrow IA$.

**Verification of naturality.** We must show that for every group homomorphism $
f: G \to H$, the following square commutes:

$$
\require{AMScd}
\begin{CD}
G @>{\pi_G}>> IA(G) = G/[G,G]\\
@V{f}VV @VV{IA(f)}V\\
H @>{\pi_H}>> IA(H) = H/[H,H]
\end{CD}
$$

i.e., $IA(f) \circ \pi_G = \pi_H \circ f$ as maps $G \to H/[H,H]$.

**Step 1: $A(f)$ is well-defined.** Since $f$ is a homomorphism, $f([G,G]) \subs
eteq [H,H]$ (commutators map to commutators: $f([g,h]) = [f(g),f(h)]$). Hence th
e map $\bar f: G \to H/[H,H]$ defined by $\bar f(g) = f(g)[H,H]$ vanishes on $[G
,G]$, so it factors uniquely through $G/[G,G]$. This factorization is precisely
$A(f): G/[G,G] \to H/[H,H]$, characterized by
$$A(f)(g[G,G]) = f(g)[H,H].$$

**Step 2: The square commutes.** For every $g \in G$,
$$(IA(f) \circ \pi_G)(g) = IA(f)(g[G,G]) = A(f)(g[G,G]) = f(g)[H,H],$$
while
$$(\pi_H \circ f)(g) = \pi_H(f(g)) = f(g)[H,H].$$
The two expressions agree for all $g \in G$, so $IA(f) \circ \pi_G = \pi_H \circ
 f$.

**Step 3: Component-wise check.** Each $\pi_G: G \to IA(G)$ is a group homomorph
ism (the canonical quotient), so $\pi_G$ is a valid morphism in $\mathbf{Group}$
 from $1_{\mathbf{Group}}(G) = G$ to $IA(G) = G/[G,G]$.

**Conclusion.** The family $\pi_G: G \to G/[G,G]$ assigns to each group a homomo
rphism $1_{\mathbf{Group}}(G) \to IA(G)$, and the naturality square commutes for
 every homomorphism $f: G \to H$. Therefore $\pi: 1_{\mathbf{Group}} \Longrighta
rrow IA$ is a natural transformation. $\blacksquare$
