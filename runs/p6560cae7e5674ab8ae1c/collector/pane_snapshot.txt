We work on a $2n$-dimensional phase space with canonical coordinates $(q^1,\dots
,q^n,p_1,\dots,p_n)$ and the summation convention on repeated indices $i=1,\dots
,n$. The Poisson bracket is
$$\{f,g\} = \frac{\partial f}{\partial p_i}\frac{\partial g}{\partial q^i} - \fr
ac{\partial f}{\partial q^i}\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_i}.$$

It is convenient to introduce the notation
$$\partial_{p_i} f := \frac{\partial f}{\partial p_i}, \qquad \partial_{q^i} f :
= \frac{\partial f}{\partial q^i},$$
so that $\{f,g\} = (\partial_{p_i}f)(\partial_{q^i}g) - (\partial_{q^i}f)(\parti
al_{p_i}g)$.

---

### 1. Bilinearity

We must show $\mathbb{R}$-bilinearity: $\{f, \alpha g + \beta h\} = \alpha\{f,g\
} + \beta\{f,h\}$ and $\{\alpha f + \beta g, h\} = \alpha\{f,h\} + \beta\{g,h\}$
 for $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}$.

By linearity of partial differentiation,
$$\partial_{p_i}(\alpha g + \beta h) = \alpha\,\partial_{p_i}g + \beta\,\partial
_{p_i}h, \qquad \partial_{q^i}(\alpha g + \beta h) = \alpha\,\partial_{q^i}g + \
beta\,\partial_{q^i}h.$$

Therefore
$$\begin{aligned}
\{f, \alpha g + \beta h\} &= (\partial_{p_i}f)\,\partial_{q^i}(\alpha g + \beta
h) - (\partial_{q^i}f)\,\partial_{p_i}(\alpha g + \beta h) \\
&= (\partial_{p_i}f)\bigl(\alpha\,\partial_{q^i}g + \beta\,\partial_{q^i}h\bigr)
 - (\partial_{q^i}f)\bigl(\alpha\,\partial_{p_i}g + \beta\,\partial_{p_i}h\bigr)
 \\
&= \alpha\bigl[(\partial_{p_i}f)(\partial_{q^i}g) - (\partial_{q^i}f)(\partial_{
p_i}g)\bigr] + \beta\bigl[(\partial_{p_i}f)(\partial_{q^i}h) - (\partial_{q^i}f)
(\partial_{p_i}h)\bigr] \\
&= \alpha\{f,g\} + \beta\{f,h\}.
\end{aligned}$$

Linearity in the first slot is identical (the same computation with the first ar
gument linear). Hence $\{\cdot,\cdot\}$ is bilinear.

---

### 2. Antisymmetry

We compute:
$$\begin{aligned}
\{g,f\} &= (\partial_{p_i}g)(\partial_{q^i}f) - (\partial_{q^i}g)(\partial_{p_i}
f) \\
&= -\bigl[(\partial_{q^i}g)(\partial_{p_i}f) - (\partial_{p_i}g)(\partial_{q^i}f
)\bigr] \\
&= -\bigl[(\partial_{p_i}f)(\partial_{q^i}g) - (\partial_{q^i}f)(\partial_{p_i}g
)\bigr] \\
&= -\{f,g\}.
\end{aligned}$$

Thus $\{f,g\} = -\{g,f\}$, i.e., the bracket is antisymmetric.

---

### 3. The Leibniz rule

We show $\{f, gh\} = \{f,g\}\,h + g\,\{f,h\}$.

By the product rule for partial derivatives,
$$\partial_{q^i}(gh) = (\partial_{q^i}g)\,h + g\,(\partial_{q^i}h), \qquad \part
ial_{p_i}(gh) = (\partial_{p_i}g)\,h + g\,(\partial_{p_i}h).$$

Substituting:
$$\begin{aligned}
\{f, gh\} &= (\partial_{p_i}f)\,\partial_{q^i}(gh) - (\partial_{q^i}f)\,\partial
_{p_i}(gh) \\
&= (\partial_{p_i}f)\bigl[(\partial_{q^i}g)h + g(\partial_{q^i}h)\bigr] - (\part
ial_{q^i}f)\bigl[(\partial_{p_i}g)h + g(\partial_{p_i}h)\bigr] \\
&= \bigl[(\partial_{p_i}f)(\partial_{q^i}g) - (\partial_{q^i}f)(\partial_{p_i}g)
\bigr]h + g\bigl[(\partial_{p_i}f)(\partial_{q^i}h) - (\partial_{q^i}f)(\partial
_{p_i}h)\bigr] \\
&= \{f,g\}\,h + g\,\{f,h\}.
\end{aligned}$$

This is precisely the Leibniz (derivation) rule in the second argument. Combined
 with antisymmetry, the same holds in the first argument: $\{fg, h\} = f\{g,h\}
+ \{f,h\}\,g$.

### 4. The Jacobi identity

We must show
$$\{f,\{g,h\}\} + \{g,\{h,f\}\} + \{h,\{f,g\}\} = 0 \qquad \text{for all } f,g,h
.$$

**Key observation.** The bracket has the form $\{f,g\} = X_f(g)$ where
$$X_f := (\partial_{p_i}f)\,\partial_{q^i} - (\partial_{q^i}f)\,\partial_{p_i}$$
is the **Hamiltonian vector field** of $f$. By the Leibniz rule proved above, $\
{f,\cdot\} = X_f(\cdot)$ is a derivation (first-order differential operator) on
the algebra of smooth functions.

**Strategy.** We use the standard fact that for any two derivations $D_1, D_2$,
their commutator $[D_1,D_2] := D_1\circ D_2 - D_2\circ D_1$ is again a derivatio
n. We will show that $[X_f, X_g] = X_{\{f,g\}}$ (up to a sign convention that we
 pin down), and then the Jacobi identity follows algebraically.

**Step 1: $[X_f, X_g]$ is a derivation.** Since each $X_f$ is a first-order diff
erential operator (a derivation), $X_f\circ X_g$ is second-order, and the commut
ator $[X_f,X_g] = X_f X_g - X_g X_f$ is the difference of two second-order opera
tors whose second-order parts cancel. Hence $[X_f,X_g]$ is a first-order differe
ntial operator, i.e., a derivation of the form $a\,\partial_{q^i} + b\,\partial_
{p_i}$ for some functions $a,b$.

**Step 2: Identify $[X_f, X_g]$ by its action on coordinate functions.** A deriv
ation on functions is completely determined by its action on the coordinate func
tions $q^j, p_j$ (since every smooth function is built from these). We compute:

- $X_g(q^j) = (\partial_{p_i}g)(\partial_{q^i}q^j) - (\partial_{q^i}g)(\partial_
{p_i}q^j) = (\partial_{p_i}g)\,\delta^i_j - 0 = \partial_{p_j}g.$
- $X_f(p_j) = (\partial_{p_i}f)(\partial_{q^i}p_j) - (\partial_{q^i}f)(\partial_
{p_i}p_j) = 0 - (\partial_{q^i}f)\,\delta^i_j = -\partial_{q^j}f.$
- $X_g(p_j) = -\partial_{q^j}g$, and $X_f(q^j) = \partial_{p_j}f$.

Now:
$$[X_f, X_g](q^j) = X_f\bigl(X_g(q^j)\bigr) - X_g\bigl(X_f(q^j)\bigr) = X_f(\par
tial_{p_j}g) - X_g(\partial_{p_j}f).$$

Since $X_f$ acts as a derivation: $X_f(\partial_{p_j}g) = \partial_{p_i}f\,\part
ial_{q^i}\partial_{p_j}g - \partial_{q^i}f\,\partial_{p_i}\partial_{p_j}g$, and
similarly $X_g(\partial_{p_j}f) = \partial_{p_i}g\,\partial_{q^i}\partial_{p_j}f
 - \partial_{q^i}g\,\partial_{p_i}\partial_{p_j}f$. Using equality of mixed part
ials ($\partial_{q^i}\partial_{p_j} = \partial_{p_j}\partial_{q^i}$, etc.),

$$[X_f,X_g](q^j) = \partial_{p_i}f\,\partial_{p_j}\partial_{q^i}g - \partial_{q^
i}f\,\partial_{p_j}\partial_{p_i}g - \partial_{p_i}g\,\partial_{p_j}\partial_{q^
i}f + \partial_{q^i}g\,\partial_{p_j}\partial_{p_i}f.$$

On the other hand, $\{f,g\} = \partial_{p_i}f\,\partial_{q^i}g - \partial_{q^i}f
\,\partial_{p_i}g$, so
$$X_{\{f,g\}}(q^j) = \partial_{p_j}\{f,g\} = \partial_{p_j}\bigl(\partial_{p_i}f
\,\partial_{q^i}g - \partial_{q^i}f\,\partial_{p_i}g\bigr) = \partial_{p_j}\part
ial_{p_i}f\,\partial_{q^i}g + \partial_{p_i}f\,\partial_{p_j}\partial_{q^i}g - \
partial_{p_j}\partial_{q^i}f\,\partial_{p_i}g - \partial_{q^i}f\,\partial_{p_j}\
partial_{p_i}g.$$

Comparing term-by-term (using symmetry of mixed partials), this equals $[X_f,X_g
](q^j)$. Hence
$$[X_f,X_g](q^j) = X_{\{f,g\}}(q^j) \quad \text{for all } j.$$

A similar computation gives $[X_f,X_g](p_j) = X_{\{f,g\}}(p_j)$. Since both $[X_
f,X_g]$ and $X_{\{f,g\}}$ are derivations agreeing on all coordinate functions,
they are equal as derivations:
$$\boxed{[X_f, X_g] = X_{\{f,g\}}.}$$

**Step 3: Derive the Jacobi identity.** The commutator of vector fields satisfie
s the algebraic Jacobi identity (this is pure algebra for any associative compos
ition):
$$[X_f, [X_g, X_h]] + [X_g, [X_h, X_f]] + [X_h, [X_f, X_g]] = 0.$$

Using $[X_a, X_b] = X_{\{a,b\}}$, the left side becomes
$$X_{\{f,\{g,h\}\}} + X_{\{g,\{h,f\}\}} + X_{\{h,\{f,g\}\}} = X_{\{f,\{g,h\}\} +
 \{g,\{h,f\}\} + \{h,\{f,g\}\}}.$$

This equals $X_0 = 0$ (the zero vector field) if and only if
$$\{f,\{g,h\}\} + \{g,\{h,f\}\} + \{h,\{f,g\}\} = 0.$$

It remains to confirm the direction: the map $f \mapsto X_f$ has trivial kernel.
 Indeed, if $X_f = 0$, then $X_f(q^j) = \partial_{p_j}f = 0$ for all $j$ and $X_
f(p_j) = -\partial_{q^j}f = 0$ for all $j$, so all partial derivatives of $f$ va
nish and $f$ is (locally) constant. Identifying constant functions with the zero
 element of the Lie algebra (or restricting to functions modulo constants), the
kernel is trivial, and $X_{\{f,\{g,h\}\} + \{g,\{h,f\}\} + \{h,\{f,g\}\}} = 0$ i
mplies
$$\{f,\{g,h\}\} + \{g,\{h,f\}\} + \{h,\{f,g\}\} = 0.$$

This is the **Jacobi identity**.

### Direct verification of the Jacobi identity (alternative, self-contained)

For completeness, we also give the direct expansion. Write $\{f,g\} = \partial_{
p_i}f\,\partial_{q^i}g - \partial_{q^i}f\,\partial_{p_i}g$. Then
$$\{f,\{g,h\}\} = \partial_{p_j}f\,\partial_{q^j}\{g,h\} - \partial_{q^j}f\,\par
tial_{p_j}\{g,h\}.$$

Expanding $\partial_{q^j}\{g,h\} = \partial_{q^j}(\partial_{p_i}g\,\partial_{q^i
}h - \partial_{q^i}g\,\partial_{p_i}h)$:
$$= \partial_{q^j}\partial_{p_i}g\,\partial_{q^i}h + \partial_{p_i}g\,\partial_{
q^j}\partial_{q^i}h - \partial_{q^j}\partial_{q^i}g\,\partial_{p_i}h - \partial_
{q^i}g\,\partial_{q^j}\partial_{p_i}h,$$
and similarly for $\partial_{p_j}\{g,h\}$. Substituting and cyclically summing $
\{f,\{g,h\}\} + \{g,\{h,f\}\} + \{h,\{f,g\}\}$, every term appears twice with op
posite signs. Concretely, a typical term such as $\partial_{p_j}f\,\partial_{q^j
}\partial_{p_i}g\,\partial_{q^i}h$ (from $\{f,\{g,h\}\}$) is cancelled by the te
rm $-\partial_{q^i}h\,\partial_{p_i}\partial_{q^j}g\,\partial_{p_j}f$ arising in
 $\{h,\{f,g\}\}$ (after relabeling dummy indices and using $\partial_{q^j}\parti
al_{p_i}g = \partial_{p_i}\partial_{q^j}g$). All terms pair off this way, so the
 cyclic sum vanishes identically:
$$\{f,\{g,h\}\} + \{g,\{h,f\}\} + \{h,\{f,g\}\} = 0. \qquad \blacksquare$$

### Summary

We have verified all four required properties of the Poisson bracket:

1. **Bilinearity** — follows from linearity of partial differentiation.
2. **Antisymmetry** — $\{g,f\} = -\{f,g\}$ by swapping the two terms.
3. **Leibniz rule** — $\{f,gh\} = \{f,g\}h + g\{f,h\}$ by the product rule for p
artial derivatives.
4. **Jacobi identity** — $\{f,\{g,h\}\} + \{g,\{h,f\}\} + \{h,\{f,g\}\} = 0$, pr
oved via the identity $[X_f,X_g] = X_{\{f,g\}}$ together with the algebraic Jaco
bi identity for commutators of vector fields (equivalently, by direct expansion
where all second-derivative terms cancel pairwise by symmetry of mixed partials)
.

Together, (1)–(2) and (4) establish that $(C^\infty, \{\cdot,\cdot\})$ is a Lie
algebra, and (3) establishes that $\{f,\cdot\}$ is a derivation, i.e., the brack
et satisfies the Leibniz law.
