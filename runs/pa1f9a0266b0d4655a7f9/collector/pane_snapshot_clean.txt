**Theorem.** There exists a finite topological space whose fundamental group is
isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}$ (the infinite cyclic group).

**Proof.**

The key tool is McCord's theorem (1966):

> **McCord's Theorem.** *Let $K$ be a finite simplicial complex and let $\mathca
l{F}(K)$ be its face poset (the set of nonempty simplices of $K$, ordered by inc
lusion), equipped with the Alexandrov topology (in which the open sets are the u
pward-closed subsets). Then there exists a weak homotopy equivalence*
> $$f : K \to \mathcal{F}(K),$$
> *where $K$ carries the usual topology of its geometric realization. In particu
lar, $\pi_n(\mathcal{F}(K)) \cong \pi_n(K)$ for all $n \geq 0$.*

More generally, McCord showed that for every finite $T_0$ space $X$, the order c
omplex $\Delta(X)$ (the simplicial complex of all chains in the poset $(X, \leq)
$) admits a weak homotopy equivalence $|\Delta(X)| \to X$, so $X$ and $|\Delta(X
)|$ share all homotopy groups.

**Construction.** Take $K$ to be the boundary of a $2$-simplex, i.e., $S^1$ tria
ngulated as a triangle with three vertices $v_0, v_1, v_2$ and three edges $e_{0
1}, e_{12}, e_{20}$. This is a finite simplicial complex with geometric realizat
ion homeomorphic to $S^1$, so
$$\pi_1(K) \cong \pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}.$$

The face poset $\mathcal{F}(K)$ has $6$ elements (three vertices and three edges
, with $v_i < e_{ij}$ whenever $v_i$ is a face of $e_{ij}$). Equipped with the A
lexandrov topology, this is a **finite topological space** (6 points).

By McCord's theorem, there is a weak homotopy equivalence $K \to \mathcal{F}(K)$
, which induces an isomorphism on all homotopy groups. In particular:
$$\pi_1(\mathcal{F}(K)) \cong \pi_1(K) \cong \mathbb{Z}.$$

Therefore $\mathcal{F}(K)$ is a finite topological space (with 6 points) whose f
undamental group is isomorphic to the infinite cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}$. $\blac
ksquare$

**Remark on minimality.** The minimal finite model of $S^1$ (due to Barmak–Minia

ision of $S^1$, hence homeomorphic to $S^1$, giving $\pi_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}$. No
 finite space with fewer than 4 points has fundamental group $\mathbb{Z}$.
