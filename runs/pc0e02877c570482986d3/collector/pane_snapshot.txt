## Proof

Let $T(n)$ denote the tower of $n$ twos, defined right-associatively:
$$T(1)=2, \qquad T(n)=2^{T(n-1)} \text{ for } n\ge 2.$$

We seek
$$L=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2^{n^k}}{T(n)}.$$

**Step 1: Rewrite the ratio.** Since $T(n)=2^{T(n-1)}$,
$$\frac{2^{n^k}}{T(n)}=\frac{2^{n^k}}{2^{T(n-1)}}=2^{\,n^k-T(n-1)}.$$
Thus $L=\lim_{n\to\infty}2^{\,n^k-T(n-1)}$, and it suffices to determine the beh
avior of the exponent $n^k-T(n-1)$.

**Step 2: Compare $T(n-1)$ with $2^n$.** We claim $T(n-1)\ge 2^{n-1}$ for all $n
\ge 2$, with strict inequality for $n\ge 3$, and in fact $T(n-1)/2^n\to\infty$.

Proof by induction that $T(n-1)\ge 2^{n-1}$: For $n=2$, $T(1)=2=2^1$. Assume $T(
n-1)\ge 2^{n-1}$. Then
$$T(n)=2^{T(n-1)}\ge 2^{2^{n-1}}\ge 2^n$$
since $2^{n-1}\ge n$ for $n\ge 2$. Hence $T(n)\ge 2^n$, i.e. $T((n+1)-1)\ge 2^{(
n+1)-1}$, completing the induction.

Moreover, for $n\ge 3$ the inequality is strict and grows: $T(n-1)\ge 2^{2^{n-2}
}$ (using $T(n-2)\ge 2^{n-2}$, so $T(n-1)=2^{T(n-2)}\ge 2^{2^{n-2}}$). Since $2^
{n-2}\ge n$ for $n\ge 4$, we get $T(n-1)\ge 2^n$ for $n\ge 4$, and the gap widen
s super-exponentially. In particular
$$\frac{2^n}{T(n-1)}\to 0,\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \frac{T(n-1)}{2^n}\to\
infty.$$

**Step 3: Use the hypothesis $n^k/2^n\to 0$.** We are given that for every fixed
 $k$,
$$\frac{n^k}{2^n}\to 0.$$
Combining with Step 2,
$$\frac{n^k}{T(n-1)}=\frac{n^k}{2^n}\cdot\frac{2^n}{T(n-1)}\longrightarrow 0\cdo
t 0 = 0.$$
Hence $n^k = o\!\left(T(n-1)\right)$, i.e. $n^k/T(n-1)\to 0$.

**Step 4: Conclude the exponent diverges to $-\infty$.** Since $n^k/T(n-1)\to 0$
 and $T(n-1)\to\infty$,
$$n^k - T(n-1)=T(n-1)\!\left(\frac{n^k}{T(n-1)}-1\right)\longrightarrow +\infty\
cdot(-1)=-\infty.$$

**Step 5: Take the limit.** Since $2^x\to 0$ as $x\to-\infty$,
$$L=\lim_{n\to\infty}2^{\,n^k-T(n-1)}=0.$$

**Relation to the given fact.** The hypothesis $n^k/2^n\to 0$ tells us that any
polynomial $n^k$ is negligible compared to the single exponential $2^n$. The tow
er $T(n-1)$ dwarfs even $2^n$ (since $T(n-1)\ge 2^{2^{n-2}}$ for $n\ge 3$), so $
n^k$ is negligible compared to $T(n-1)$ a fortiori:
$$\frac{n^k}{T(n-1)}=\underbrace{\frac{n^k}{2^n}}_{\to\,0}\cdot\underbrace{\frac
{2^n}{T(n-1)}}_{\to\,0}\longrightarrow 0.$$
This forces the exponent $n^k-T(n-1)\to-\infty$, and therefore the whole express
ion tends to $0$.

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2^{n^k}}{2^{2^{\iddots^2}}}=0}$$
