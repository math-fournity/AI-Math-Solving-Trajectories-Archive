## Solution

Let $F = \mathbb{Q}(t)$, the field of rational functions in one indeterminate ov
er $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Claim.** $\operatorname{Aut}(F) \cong \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q})$, which is bo
th infinite and countable.

---

### Step 1. Every automorphism fixes $\mathbb{Q}$.

Since $F$ has characteristic $0$, its prime subfield is $\mathbb{Q}$. Any field
automorphism fixes $1$, hence fixes every element of the prime subfield. So ever
y automorphism of $F$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-automorphism:
$$\operatorname{Aut}(F) = \operatorname{Aut}(F/\mathbb{Q}).$$

### Step 2. Description of $\operatorname{Aut}(F/\mathbb{Q})$.

Let $\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(F/\mathbb{Q})$. Then $\sigma$ is determined b
y $\sigma(t)$, since $F = \mathbb{Q}(t)$. The element $\sigma(t)$ must generate
$F$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, i.e. $\mathbb{Q}(\sigma(t)) = \mathbb{Q}(t)$.

Write $\sigma(t) = \dfrac{f(t)}{g(t)}$ with $f,g \in \mathbb{Q}[t]$ coprime and
$g \neq 0$. The condition $\mathbb{Q}(f(t)/g(t)) = \mathbb{Q}(t)$ forces $\max(\
deg f, \deg g) = 1$ (otherwise $t$ would be algebraic of degree $\geqslant 2$ ov
er $\mathbb{Q}(f(t)/g(t))$ by a degree count, contradicting equality). Hence
$$\sigma(t) = \frac{at+b}{ct+d}, \qquad a,b,c,d \in \mathbb{Q},\quad ad - bc \ne
q 0.$$

Conversely, every such Möbius transformation $t \mapsto (at+b)/(ct+d)$ with $ad
- bc \neq 0$ defines a $\mathbb{Q}$-automorphism of $F$, with inverse given by t
he inverse matrix. Two such transformations give the same automorphism exactly w
hen the matrices $\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}$ differ by a nonzero scal
ar. Therefore
$$\operatorname{Aut}(F) \cong \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q}) = \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb
{Q})/\mathbb{Q}^{\times}.$$

### Step 3. $\operatorname{Aut}(F)$ is countable.

$\mathbb{Q}$ is countable, so $\mathbb{Q}^4$ is countable, hence $\mathrm{GL}_2(
\mathbb{Q}) \subseteq \mathbb{Q}^4$ is countable. Quotienting by the countable s
ubgroup $\mathbb{Q}^{\times}$ (in fact by the scalar matrices) keeps it countabl
e. Thus $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q})$ is countable.

### Step 4. $\operatorname{Aut}(F)$ is infinite.

For each $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, the translation $t \mapsto t + n$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$
-automorphism of $F$ (here $a=d=1$, $b=n$, $c=0$). These are pairwise distinct,
giving infinitely many elements. (Equivalently, $t \mapsto t+1$ has infinite ord
er.) Hence $\operatorname{Aut}(F)$ is infinite.

---

### Conclusion

The field $F = \mathbb{Q}(t)$ has
$$\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Q}(t)) \cong \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q}),$$
which is **infinite** (it contains all translations $t \mapsto t+n$, $n \in \mat
hbb{Z}$) and **countable** (it is a quotient of the countable group $\mathrm{GL}
_2(\mathbb{Q})$). This is the desired field.
