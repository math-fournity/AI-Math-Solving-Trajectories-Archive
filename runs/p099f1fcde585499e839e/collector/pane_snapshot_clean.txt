## Solution

We study the forced wave equation
$$y_{tt} = c^2 y_{xx} + \cos\omega t, \qquad y(0,t)=y(L,t)=0.$$

**Step 1: Eigenfunction expansion.**

The eigenfunctions of $-\frac{d^2}{dx^2}$ with Dirichlet boundary conditions on
$(0,L)$ are
$$\phi_n(x) = \sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right), \qquad \lambda_n = \left(\fra
c{n\pi}{L}\right)^2, \qquad n=1,2,3,\dots$$
with natural frequencies $\omega_n = c\sqrt{\lambda_n} = \frac{n\pi c}{L}$.

Expand $y(x,t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} T_n(t)\,\phi_n(x)$. The spatially-constant
forcing $\cos\omega t$ must be expanded in a sine series:
$$1 = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n \sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right), \qquad b_n =
\frac{2}{L}\int_0^L \sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)dx = \frac{2}{n\pi}\bigl(
1-(-1)^n\bigr).$$
Hence
$$b_n = \begin{cases} \dfrac{4}{n\pi}, & n \text{ odd},\\[4pt] 0, & n \text{ eve
n}. \end{cases}$$

**Step 2: Modal ODEs.**

Substituting and using orthogonality gives, for each $n$:
$$T_n''(t) + \omega_n^2\, T_n(t) = b_n \cos\omega t, \qquad \omega_n = \frac{n\p
i c}{L}.$$

This is a forced harmonic oscillator. Its general solution is
$$T_n(t) = \underbrace{A_n\cos\omega_n t + B_n\sin\omega_n t}_{\text{homogeneous
 (bounded)}} + \underbrace{(\text{particular})}_{\text{depends on }\omega\text{
vs. }\omega_n}.$$

**Step 3: Boundedness when $\omega \neq \omega_n$ for all $n$.**

For every mode, $\omega \neq \omega_n$, so the particular solution is
$$T_n^{(p)}(t) = \frac{b_n}{\omega_n^2 - \omega^2}\cos\omega t,$$
which is bounded. The homogeneous part is bounded. Therefore every $T_n$ is boun
ded, and (with reasonable regularity) $y(x,t)$ is bounded. No solution can be un
bounded.

**Step 4: Resonance when $\omega = \omega_n$ for some $n$.**

When $\omega = \omega_n$, the modal equation becomes
$$T_n'' + \omega_n^2 T_n = b_n \cos\omega_n t.$$

- **If $b_n \neq 0$ (i.e., $n$ odd):** This is classical resonance. The particul
ar solution is
$$T_n^{(p)}(t) = \frac{b_n}{2\omega_n}\,t\sin\omega_n t,$$
which grows linearly in amplitude: $|T_n^{(p)}(t)| \sim \frac{b_n}{2\omega_n}t \
to \infty$. Choosing initial conditions so that the homogeneous part of this mod
e vanishes (e.g. $y(x,0)=0$, $y_t(x,0)=0$), the solution contains this resonant
term, and since $\sin(n\pi x/L)$ is nonzero on $(0,L)$, we get
$$\max_{0<x<L}|y(x,t)| \to \infty \quad\text{as } t\to\infty.$$

- **If $b_n = 0$ (i.e., $n$ even):** The modal equation reduces to the *homogene
ous* equation $T_n'' + \omega_n^2 T_n = 0$, whose solutions are bounded. All oth
er modes have $\omega \neq \omega_m$ and are bounded by Step 3. Hence no solutio
n can be unbounded in this case.

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

A solution can have $\max_{0<x<L}|y(x,t)|\to\infty$ as $t\to\infty$ **if and onl
y if** the forcing frequency coincides with a natural frequency that is actually
 excited by the (spatially constant) forcing. Since the sine-series of a constan
t vanishes on even modes, only odd modes are excited. Therefore:

$$\boxed{\omega = \omega_n = \frac{n\pi c}{L} \quad\text{for } n = 1,3,5,\dots \
text{ (positive odd integers)},}$$

equivalently $\omega = \dfrac{(2k-1)\pi c}{L}$ for $k=1,2,3,\dots$. At these val
ues, resonance produces a secular (linearly growing) term, making the solution u
nbounded; at all other values of $\omega$, every modal response is bounded.
