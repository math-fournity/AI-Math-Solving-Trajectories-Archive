Looking at this problem, I need to find point $P$ inside acute triangle $ABC$ su
ch that $P$ is the orthocenter of the triangle formed by reflecting $P$ over the
 three sides, then determine the largest angle of the triangle with sides $PA, P
B, PC$.

## Setup

Place $P$ at the origin. Let $P_A, P_B, P_C$ be reflections of $P$ over sides $B
C, CA, AB$ respectively. Since reflection over a line gives a perpendicular bise
ctor relationship:

- $PP_A \perp BC$ with midpoint of $PP_A$ on $BC$, so $|PP_A| = 2h_a$ where $h_a
 = d(P, BC)$
- Similarly $|PP_B| = 2h_b$, $|PP_C| = 2h_c$

Express the reflections using inward unit normals: $P_A = 2h_a\hat{n}_a$, $P_B =
 2h_b\hat{n}_b$, $P_C = 2h_c\hat{n}_c$.

## Orthocenter Conditions

$P$ is the orthocenter of $\triangle P_AP_BP_C$ requires:
$$\vec{P_A}\cdot(\vec{P_C}-\vec{P_B})=0,\quad \vec{P_B}\cdot(\vec{P_C}-\vec{P_A}
)=0,\quad \vec{P_C}\cdot(\vec{P_B}-\vec{P_A})=0$$

These reduce to: $\vec{P_A}\cdot\vec{P_B} = \vec{P_B}\cdot\vec{P_C} = \vec{P_C}\
cdot\vec{P_A}$.

## Computing the Dot Products

The angle between inward normals $\hat{n}_a$ and $\hat{n}_b$ (to sides $BC$ and
$CA$) is $\pi - C$, so $\hat{n}_a\cdot\hat{n}_b = -\cos C$. Similarly $\hat{n}_b
\cdot\hat{n}_c = -\cos A$ and $\hat{n}_c\cdot\hat{n}_a = -\cos B$.

The equal-dot-product condition becomes:
$$h_a h_b(-\cos C) = h_b h_c(-\cos A) = h_c h_a(-\cos B)$$

Dividing by $h_a h_b h_c > 0$:
$$\frac{\cos C}{h_c} = \frac{\cos A}{h_a} = \frac{\cos B}{h_b}$$

Therefore: $h_a : h_b : h_c = \cos A : \cos B : \cos C$.

## Identifying $P$ as the Circumcenter

In areal coordinates, $h_a = u\cdot H_a$ where $H_a = 2\Delta/a$ is the altitude
 from $A$. So $h_a \propto u/a$, giving:
$$u : v : w = a\cos A : b\cos B : c\cos C$$

By the Law of Sines ($a = 2R\sin A$), we get $a\cos A = R\sin 2A$, so:
$$u : v : w = \sin 2A : \sin 2B : \sin 2C$$

These are exactly the **areal coordinates of the circumcenter $O$**. Since $\tri
angle ABC$ is acute, $O$ lies in the interior. ✓

**Verification:** For $P = O$, the distance from $O$ to side $BC$ is $R\cos A$ (
standard result), so $h_a : h_b : h_c = \cos A : \cos B : \cos C$. ✓ The dot pro
ducts all equal $-4R^2\cos A\cos B\cos C$, confirming the orthocenter condition.
 ✓

## Computing the Answer

Since $P = O$ is the circumcenter:
$$PA = PB = PC = R$$

The triangle formed by segments $PA, PB, PC$ has all three sides equal to $R$ —
it is **equilateral**, with every angle equal to $60°$.

$$x = 60$$
