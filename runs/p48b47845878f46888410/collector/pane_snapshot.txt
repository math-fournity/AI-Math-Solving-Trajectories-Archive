## Proof

**Setup.** Let $F$ be a field with $\operatorname{char}(F) \neq 2$, let $x$ be t
ranscendental over $F$, and set
$$y = \frac{x^8+1}{x^4} = x^4 + x^{-4} \in F(x).$$
Define $g(s) = s^8 - y\,s^4 + 1 \in F(y)[s]$. Since
$$g(x) = x^8 - \frac{x^8+1}{x^4}\cdot x^4 + 1 = x^8 - (x^8+1) + 1 = 0,$$
$x$ is a root of $g(s)$ over $F(y)$. We determine the minimal polynomial of $x$
over $F(y)$ by computing the extension degree $[F(x):F(y)]$.

**Step 1: Intermediate field $F(x^4)$.** Set $t = x^4$, so $y = t + t^{-1} = \fr
ac{t^2+1}{t}$. Then $t$ satisfies
$$T^2 - y\,T + 1 = 0 \quad \text{over } F(y).$$
This polynomial is irreducible over $F(y)$: if it were reducible, then $t \in F(
y) = F(t+t^{-1})$, which would force $F(t) = F(t+t^{-1})$. But for transcendenta
l $t$, the extension $F(t)/F(t+t^{-1})$ has degree $2$ (the automorphism $t \map
sto t^{-1}$ of $F(t)$ fixes $t+t^{-1}$ and is nontrivial), a contradiction. Henc
e
$$[F(x^4) : F(y)] = [F(t):F(t+t^{-1})] = 2.$$

**Step 2: Extension $F(x)/F(x^4)$.** The element $x$ satisfies $S^4 - x^4 = 0$ o
ver $F(x^4) = F(t)$. Since $t$ is transcendental over $F$, the binomial $S^4 - t
$ is irreducible over $F(t)$: $t$ is not a $p$-th power in $F(t)$ for any prime
$p \mid 4$ (a transcendental element is not a proper power in the rational funct
ion field), and $\operatorname{char}(F) \nmid 4$. Therefore
$$[F(x) : F(x^4)] = 4.$$

**Step 3: Tower degree.** By the tower formula,
$$[F(x):F(y)] = [F(x):F(x^4)]\cdot [F(x^4):F(y)] = 4 \times 2 = 8.$$

**Step 4: Conclusion.** The minimal polynomial of $x$ over $F(y)$ has degree $[F
(x):F(y)] = 8$. The polynomial $g(s) = s^8 - y\,s^4 + 1$ lies in $F(y)[s]$, has
degree $8$, and has $x$ as a root. Since the minimal polynomial divides any anni
hilating polynomial and has the same degree, $g(s)$ **is** the minimal polynomia
l.

**Verification of irreducibility via root structure.** The roots of $g(s)=0$ are

ight roots $\{\zeta\, x,\ \zeta\, x^{-1} : \zeta^4 = 1\}$. None of these lie in
$F(y)$: $x \notin F(y)$ since $[F(x):F(y)]=8>1$, and similarly for the others (t
hey generate the same degree-$8$ extension). No proper subset of these roots is
stable under the full symmetry group fixing $y$ (generated by $x \mapsto \zeta x
$ and $x \mapsto x^{-1}$), confirming that $g$ cannot factor over $F(y)$.
