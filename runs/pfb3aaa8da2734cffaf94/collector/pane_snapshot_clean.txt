## Derivation of the Navier–Stokes Equation for a Viscous Incompressible Fluid

### Starting equations

**1. Dynamic equation of fluid flow (Cauchy's momentum equation).**

For a continuum, conservation of linear momentum gives

$$\rho\left(\frac{\partial \mathbf{v}}{\partial t}+(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathb
f{v}\right)=\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}+\rho\mathbf{f},$$

where $\rho$ is the density, $\mathbf{v}$ the velocity field, $\boldsymbol{\sigm
a}$ the Cauchy stress tensor, and $\mathbf{f}$ the body force per unit mass.

**2. Incompressibility condition.**

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0,\qquad \rho=\text{const}.$$

**3. Constitutive relation for a Newtonian viscous fluid.**

The stress tensor decomposes into an isotropic pressure part and a viscous (devi
atoric) part:

$$\sigma_{ij}=-p\,\delta_{ij}+\tau_{ij},$$

with the Newtonian viscous stress

$$\tau_{ij}=\mu\left(\frac{\partial v_i}{\partial x_j}+\frac{\partial v_j}{\part
ial x_i}\right)+\lambda\,(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v})\,\delta_{ij},$$

where $\mu$ is the dynamic (shear) viscosity and $\lambda$ the second (bulk) vis
cosity coefficient.

---

### Step 1 — Apply incompressibility to the constitutive relation

Since $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0$, the bulk-viscosity term vanishes identically:

$$\tau_{ij}=\mu\left(\frac{\partial v_i}{\partial x_j}+\frac{\partial v_j}{\part
ial x_i}\right).$$

Thus the stress tensor for an incompressible Newtonian fluid is

$$\sigma_{ij}=-p\,\delta_{ij}+\mu\left(\frac{\partial v_i}{\partial x_j}+\frac{\
partial v_j}{\partial x_i}\right).$$

---

### Step 2 — Compute the divergence of the stress tensor

The force term in the momentum equation is $(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma})_i=\
dfrac{\partial\sigma_{ij}}{\partial x_j}$. Differentiating:

$$\frac{\partial\sigma_{ij}}{\partial x_j}=-\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_j}\delt
a_{ij}+\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\!\left(\frac{\partial v_i}{\partial x_j}
+\frac{\partial v_j}{\partial x_i}\right).$$

The first term gives $-\partial p/\partial x_i$. Expanding the viscous part:

$$\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\!\left(\frac{\partial v_i}{\partial x_j}\righ
t)+\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\!\left(\frac{\partial v_j}{\partial x_i}\rig
ht)=\mu\,\nabla^2 v_i+\mu\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\!\left(\frac{\partial v
_j}{\partial x_j}\right).$$

The second piece is $\mu\,\partial_i(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v})$, which vanishes by
incompressibility. Therefore

$$\frac{\partial\sigma_{ij}}{\partial x_j}=-\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_i}+\mu\
,\nabla^2 v_i.$$

In vector form:

$$\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}=-\nabla p+\mu\,\nabla^2\mathbf{v}.$$

---

### Step 3 — Substitute into the dynamic equation

Inserting the result of Step 2 into Cauchy's momentum equation:

$$\rho\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}+(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf
{v}\right)=-\nabla p+\mu\,\nabla^2\mathbf{v}+\rho\mathbf{f}.$$

---

### Step 4 — Final form

Since $\rho$ is constant, divide through by $\rho$ and define the **kinematic vi
scosity** $\nu=\mu/\rho$:

$$\boxed{\;\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}+(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{
v}=-\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla p+\nu\,\nabla^2\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{f}\;}$$

together with the constraints

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0,\qquad \rho=\text{const}.$$

This is the **Navier–Stokes equation for a viscous, incompressible fluid**.
