## Proof

Let $A = \{f \in K[x,y] : f(x,y) = f(y,x)\}$ be the subring of symmetric polynom
ials. The elementary symmetric polynomials
$$e_1 = x + y, \qquad e_2 = xy$$
belong to $A$, and by the fundamental theorem on symmetric polynomials in two va
riables, $A = K[e_1, e_2]$.

**Claim.** $K[x,y]$ is generated as an $A$-module by $\{1,\, x\}$.

**Step 1: $K[x,y] = A[x]$.**

Since $e_1 = x+y \in A$, we have $y = e_1 - x \in A[x]$. Hence every polynomial
$f(x,y) \in K[x,y]$ can be rewritten by substituting $y = e_1 - x$:
$$f(x,y) = f(x,\, e_1 - x) \in A[x].$$
Conversely $A[x] \subseteq K[x,y]$ since $A \subseteq K[x,y]$ and $x \in K[x,y]$
. Therefore $K[x,y] = A[x]$.

**Step 2: $x$ is integral over $A$ of degree $2$.**

Both $x$ and $y$ are roots of the monic polynomial
$$T^2 - e_1\, T + e_2 = T^2 - (x+y)\,T + xy \in A[T].$$
In particular,
$$x^2 = e_1\, x - e_2, \qquad \text{with } e_1, e_2 \in A.$$

**Step 3: Reduction to $A$-linear combination of $\{1, x\}$.**

Any element of $A[x]$ is a polynomial $g(x) = a_0 + a_1 x + a_2 x^2 + \cdots + a
_n x^n$ with $a_i \in A$. Using the relation $x^2 = e_1 x - e_2$ repeatedly, eve
ry power $x^k$ for $k \geq 2$ reduces to an $A$-linear combination of $1$ and $x
$:

- $x^2 = e_1 x - e_2$,
- $x^3 = x \cdot x^2 = e_1 x^2 - e_2 x = e_1(e_1 x - e_2) - e_2 x = (e_1^2 - e_2
)\,x - e_1 e_2$,

and by induction, $x^k = \alpha_k x + \beta_k$ with $\alpha_k, \beta_k \in A$ fo
r all $k \geq 0$.

Therefore
$$g(x) = \underbrace{\left(a_0 + a_2\beta_2 + a_3\beta_3 + \cdots\right)}_{\in\,
 A}\cdot 1 \;+\; \underbrace{\left(a_1 + a_2\alpha_2 + a_3\alpha_3 + \cdots\righ
t)}_{\in\, A}\cdot x.$$

Thus every element of $K[x,y] = A[x]$ is an $A$-linear combination of $1$ and $x
$.

**Step 4: $\{1, x\}$ is $A$-linearly independent.**

Suppose $a \cdot 1 + b \cdot x = 0$ with $a, b \in A$. Viewing this in $K[x,y]$,
 if $b \neq 0$ then $x = -a/b$ would be a ratio of symmetric polynomials, hence
symmetric: $x = y$, which is false in $K[x,y]$. More directly: $a + bx$ is symme
tric only if $b = 0$ (since $a + bx$ evaluated at $(x,y)$ vs. $(y,x)$ gives $a +
 bx$ vs. $a + by$; equality forces $b(x-y) = 0$, so $b = 0$), and then $a = 0$.
Hence $\{1, x\}$ is $A$-linearly independent.

**Conclusion.** $K[x,y]$ is a **free $A$-module of rank $2$** with generating se
t
$$\boxed{\{1,\; x\}}.$$

Equivalently, $\{1, y\}$ is also a generating set (by the same argument with the
 roles of $x$ and $y$ exchanged).

### PROOF COMPLETE
