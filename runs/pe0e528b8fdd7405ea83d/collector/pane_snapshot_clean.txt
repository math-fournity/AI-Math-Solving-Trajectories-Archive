Looking at this problem, I need to analyze the quotient $P(K)/P^+(K)$ for a quad
ratic extension $K/\mathbb{Q}$ under the assumption that $\mathcal{O}_K$ has no
unit of norm $-1$.

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $K/\mathbb{Q}$ be a quadratic extension. Recall:
- $P(K) = \{(\alpha) : \alpha \in K^*\}$ is the group of principal fractional id
eals.
- $P^+(K) = \{(\alpha) \in P(K) : N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha) > 0\}$ is the subgrou
p with positively-normed generators.

**Step 1: Define the norm-sign map.**

Define $\phi: P(K) \to \{+1, -1\}$ by
$$\phi((\alpha)) = \operatorname{sgn}\!\big(N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)\big).$$

**Step 2: Well-definedness.**

Suppose $(\alpha) = (\beta)$, so $\alpha = u\beta$ for some unit $u \in \mathcal
{O}_K^*$. Then
$$N(\alpha) = N(u)\, N(\beta).$$
Since $u$ is a unit, $N(u) = \pm 1$. The map $\phi$ is well-defined if and only
if $N(u) = +1$ for **every** unit $u$, i.e., $\mathcal{O}_K$ contains no unit of
 norm $-1$.

**By the hypothesis of the problem**, $\mathcal{O}_K$ has no unit of norm $-1$,
so **all units have norm $+1$**, and $\phi$ is well-defined.

**Step 3: $\phi$ is a surjective group homomorphism with kernel $P^+(K)$.**

- **Homomorphism:** $\phi((\alpha)(\beta)) = \operatorname{sgn}(N(\alpha\beta))
= \operatorname{sgn}(N(\alpha)N(\beta)) = \phi((\alpha))\,\phi((\beta))$.
- **Kernel:** $\ker\phi = \{(\alpha) : N(\alpha) > 0\} = P^+(K)$.
- **Surjectivity:** We need an element of $K^*$ with negative norm. Since the hy
pothesis (no unit of norm $-1$) is **non-vacuous** only for **real quadratic** f
ields $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ with $d > 0$, consider $\alpha = \sqrt{d}$. The
n
$$N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d}) = (\sqrt{d})(-\sqrt{d}) = -d < 0.$$
So $\phi$ is surjective. (For imaginary quadratic fields, all norms are positive
, $P(K)=P^+(K)$ trivially, and the assumption is vacuous—the problem's hypothesi
s points to the real quadratic case.)

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

By the First Isomorphism Theorem,
$$P(K)/P^+(K) = P(K)/\ker\phi \cong \operatorname{im}(\phi) = \{+1, -1\} \cong \
mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.$$

**The order of $P(K)/P^+(K)$ is $2$.**
