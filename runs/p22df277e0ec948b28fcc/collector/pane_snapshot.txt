# Proof

**Yes, the length of $U$ is always at least 1.**

## Setup

$X \subseteq [0,1]$ with $|X|=1$ (Lebesgue measure). For each $x\in X$, $\vareps
ilon_x>0$, and $I_x=(x,x+\varepsilon_x)$. Let
$$U=\bigcup_{x\in X} I_x.$$
Since $U$ is a union of open intervals, $U$ is open and therefore measurable. Be
ing an open subset of $\mathbb{R}$, $U$ decomposes uniquely as a countable disjo
int union of open intervals (its connected components):
$$U=\bigsqcup_{n} (a_n,b_n), \qquad |U|=\sum_n (b_n-a_n).$$

## Key observation

**Claim.** $X\subseteq\bigcup_n [a_n,b_n)$.

*Proof of claim.* Fix $x\in X$. The interval $I_x=(x,x+\varepsilon_x)$ is nonemp
ty (since $\varepsilon_x>0$), open, and connected, with $I_x\subseteq U$. Since
$U$ is the disjoint union of the components $(a_n,b_n)$ and $I_x$ is connected,
$I_x$ must lie entirely inside a single component: there exists $n$ with
$$(x,x+\varepsilon_x)\subseteq (a_n,b_n).$$
From this inclusion:
- $a_n\le x$: otherwise $a_n>x$, and then $(x,\min(a_n,x+\varepsilon_x))$ is a n
onempty subinterval of $I_x$ lying outside $(a_n,b_n)$, contradicting $I_x\subse
teq(a_n,b_n)$.
- $x+\varepsilon_x\le b_n$, hence $x<b_n$.

Therefore $x\in[a_n,b_n)\subseteq\bigcup_n[a_n,b_n)$. ∎

## Conclusion

The intervals $[a_n,b_n)$ are pairwise disjoint up to endpoints (the components
$(a_n,b_n)$ are disjoint, and adjoining left endpoints only adds a countable, me
asure-zero set). Hence
$$\left|\bigcup_n [a_n,b_n)\right|=\sum_n(b_n-a_n)=|U|.$$
By the claim, $X\subseteq\bigcup_n[a_n,b_n)$, so
$$1=|X|\le\left|\bigcup_n[a_n,b_n)\right|=|U|.$$

Thus $|U|\ge 1$ always. $\blacksquare$
