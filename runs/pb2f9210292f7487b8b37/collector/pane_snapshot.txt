## Solution

The kernel $K(x,t) = x\cos t + t^2\sin x + \cos x\sin t$ is **degenerate** (sepa
rable), being a sum of three products of a function of $x$ times a function of $
t$. Set

$$c_1=\!\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\!\cos t\,\varphi(t)\,dt,\quad c_2=\!\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\
!t^2\varphi(t)\,dt,\quad c_3=\!\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\!\sin t\,\varphi(t)\,dt.$$

Then the equation gives

$$\varphi(x)=x+\lambda\bigl(x\,c_1+\sin x\,c_2+\cos x\,c_3\bigr).\tag{1}$$

Substituting (1) into the definitions of $c_i$ and using parity:

- $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}t\cos t\,dt=0$ (odd), $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin t\cos t\,dt=0$
(odd), $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cos^2 t\,dt=\pi$;
- $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}t^3\,dt=0$ (odd), $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}t^2\sin t\,dt=0$ (odd),
$\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}t^2\cos t\,dt=-4\pi$;
- $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}t\sin t\,dt=2\pi$, $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin^2 t\,dt=\pi$.

This yields the linear system

$$\begin{aligned}
c_1 &= \lambda\pi\,c_3,\\
c_2 &= -4\lambda\pi\,c_3,\\
c_3 &= 2\pi + 2\pi\lambda\,c_1 + \pi\lambda\,c_2.
\end{aligned}$$

Inserting the first two into the third:

$$c_3 = 2\pi + 2\pi\lambda(\lambda\pi c_3) + \pi\lambda(-4\lambda\pi c_3)=2\pi-2
\pi^2\lambda^2 c_3,$$

so $(1+2\pi^2\lambda^2)c_3=2\pi$, hence (for real $\lambda$, the denominator is
always positive)

$$c_3=\frac{2\pi}{1+2\pi^2\lambda^2},\quad c_1=\frac{2\lambda\pi^2}{1+2\pi^2\lam
bda^2},\quad c_2=\frac{-8\lambda\pi^2}{1+2\pi^2\lambda^2}.$$

Plugging into (1):

$$\varphi(x)=x+\frac{\lambda}{1+2\pi^2\lambda^2}\bigl(2\lambda\pi^2 x-8\lambda\p
i^2\sin x+2\pi\cos x\bigr).$$

Collecting the coefficient of $x$:

$$1+\frac{2\lambda^2\pi^2}{1+2\pi^2\lambda^2}=\frac{1+4\pi^2\lambda^2}{1+2\pi^2\
lambda^2}.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{\;\varphi(x)=\frac{(1+4\pi^2\lambda^2)\,x+2\lambda\pi\cos x-8\lambda^2\
pi^2\sin x}{1+2\pi^2\lambda^2}\;}$$

(valid for all real $\lambda$, since $1+2\pi^2\lambda^2\neq 0$).
