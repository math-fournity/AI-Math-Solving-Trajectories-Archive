**Answer: No.**

**Counterexample.** Let $X = [0,1]$ equipped with the usual Euclidean metric. Th
en $X$ is a compact metric space. Since every closed subset of a compact space i
s compact, *every* closed subset of $X$ is compact; in particular every closed a
nd bounded subset of $X$ is compact. Thus $X$ satisfies the hypothesis.

However, $X = [0,1]$ is **not** a vector space (it is not closed under addition
or scalar multiplication — e.g. $0.7 + 0.7 = 1.4 \notin [0,1]$), hence it cannot
 be a normed space at all, let alone a finite-dimensional one.

Therefore the Heine–Borel-type property "every closed and bounded subset is comp
act" does **not** imply that $X$ is a finite-dimensional normed space.
