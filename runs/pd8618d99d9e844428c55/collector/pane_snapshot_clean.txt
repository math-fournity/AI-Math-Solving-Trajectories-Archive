## Solution

We determine the completion of the metric space $(\ell^1(\mathbb{N}), d_*)$, whe
re $d_*(a,b) = \sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}} |a_n - b_n|$.

**Key observation.** The metric $d_*$ is precisely the metric induced by the sup
-norm $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ on $\ell^\infty(\mathbb{N})$. Since every absolutely su
mmable sequence is bounded, $\ell^1(\mathbb{N}) \subseteq \ell^\infty(\mathbb{N}
)$, and $d_*$ is the restriction of the $\ell^\infty$-norm metric to $\ell^1(\ma
thbb{N})$.

**Claim.** The completion of $(\ell^1(\mathbb{N}), d_*)$ is $(c_0(\mathbb{N}), \
|\cdot\|_\infty)$, where $c_0(\mathbb{N}) = \{a \in \ell^\infty : a_n \to 0\}$.

**Proof.** We establish three facts.

*Fact 1: $\ell^1(\mathbb{N}) \subseteq c_0(\mathbb{N})$.* If $\sum_{n}|a_n| < \i
nfty$, then $|a_n| \to 0$, so $a_n \to 0$. Hence $a \in c_0$.

*Fact 2: $c_0(\mathbb{N})$ is a closed subspace of $\ell^\infty(\mathbb{N})$.* L
et $(a^{(k)})_{k\geq 1}$ be a sequence in $c_0$ converging to $a$ in $\ell^\inft

$$|a_n| \leq |a_n - a^{(k)}_n| + |a^{(k)}_n| < \varepsilon/2 + \varepsilon/2 = \
varepsilon.$$
So $a_n \to 0$, i.e., $a \in c_0$. Thus $c_0$ is closed in $\ell^\infty$.

*Fact 3: $\ell^1(\mathbb{N})$ is dense in $c_0(\mathbb{N})$ with respect to $\|\
cdot\|_\infty$.* Let $a \in c_0$ and $\varepsilon > 0$. Since $a_n \to 0$, there

1$ by $b_n = a_n$ for $n \leq N$ and $b_n = 0$ for $n > N$. Then $b$ has finite
support, so $b \in \ell^1$, and
$$\|a - b\|_\infty = \sup_{n > N} |a_n| \leq \varepsilon.$$
Hence $\ell^1$ is dense in $c_0$ under the sup-norm.

**Conclusion.** Since $\ell^\infty(\mathbb{N})$ is complete (it is a Banach spac
e) and $c_0(\mathbb{N})$ is a closed subspace of $\ell^\infty(\mathbb{N})$, the
space $c_0(\mathbb{N})$ is itself complete. By Facts 1 and 3, $\ell^1(\mathbb{N}

By uniqueness of the completion up to isometric isomorphism, the completion of $

$$\boxed{(c_0(\mathbb{N}),\, \|\cdot\|_\infty)}.$$
