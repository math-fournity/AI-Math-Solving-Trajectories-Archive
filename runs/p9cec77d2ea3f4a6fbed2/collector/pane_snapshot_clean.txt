**Step 1: Edges only connect vertices of equal 2-adic valuation.**

Let $v(x)$ denote the 2-adic valuation of $x$ (the largest power of 2 dividing $
x$). Suppose $a + b = 2^k$ with $v(a) < v(b)$. Write $a = 2^{v(a)} m$, $b = 2^{v
(b)} n$ with $m, n$ odd. Then:
$$a + b = 2^{v(a)}\bigl(m + 2^{v(b)-v(a)} n\bigr)$$
Since $v(b) > v(a)$, the term $2^{v(b)-v(a)} n$ is even, so $m + (\text{even})$
is odd. Thus $v(a+b) = v(a)$. For $a+b = 2^k$, we need $v(a) = k$ and $m + 2^{v(
b)-v(a)}n = 1$. But $m \geq 1$ and $2^{v(b)-v(a)}n \geq 2$, giving a sum $\geq 3
$, contradiction. By symmetry, $v(a) = v(b)$ for every edge.

**Step 2: The graph decomposes into 11 connected components by valuation.**

The 2-adic valuations present in $\{1, \ldots, 2012\}$ are $v = 0, 1, \ldots, 10

floor 2012/2^v \rfloor\}$, and two vertices $2^v m, 2^v n$ are adjacent iff $m +

**Claim:** The odd-sum graph on odd numbers $\{1, 3, 5, \ldots\}$ up to any $N \

*Proof of claim:* For any odd $m \geq 3$ with $m \leq N$, let $2^j$ be the small

So each valuation class $v = 0, 1, \ldots, 9$ (where $N_v \geq 3$) forms one con
