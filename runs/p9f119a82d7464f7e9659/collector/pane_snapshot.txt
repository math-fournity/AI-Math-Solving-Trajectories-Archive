## Solution

**Yes**, it is always possible to find a valuation ring of $k(x,y)$ with Krull d
imension $2$, for any field $k$.

### Construction

Define a map $v: k[x,y]\setminus\{0\} \to \mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}$ as follows
. The target is equipped with the **lexicographic order** (first coordinate comp
ared first). For a nonzero polynomial $f = \sum a_{ij}\, x^i y^j$, set

$$v(f) = \min_{\text{lex}}\bigl\{(i,j) : a_{ij} \neq 0\bigr\},$$

i.e., $v(f) = (i_0, j_0)$ where $i_0$ is the smallest power of $x$ appearing in
$f$, and $j_0$ is the smallest power of $y$ appearing among the terms $a_{i_0, j
}\, x^{i_0} y^j$.

### Verification that $v$ is a valuation

**Multiplicativity ($v(fg) = v(f) + v(g)$).** Write $f = a\, x^{i_1}y^{j_1} + (\
text{terms lex-greater than } (i_1,j_1))$ and $g = b\, x^{i_2}y^{j_2} + (\text{t
erms lex-greater than } (i_2,j_2))$ with $a,b \neq 0$. Then the lex-smallest ter
m of $fg$ is $ab\, x^{i_1+i_2}y^{j_1+j_2}$ with $ab \neq 0$, since no other pair
 of terms can produce the same exponent $(i_1+i_2,\, j_1+j_2)$ with a lex-smalle
r or equal first coordinate. Hence $v(fg) = (i_1+i_2,\, j_1+j_2) = v(f)+v(g)$.

**Triangle inequality ($v(f+g) \geq \min(v(f), v(g))$).** Immediate: the lex-sma
llest term of $f+g$ is at least as large as the lex-min of the two leading terms
.

Since $k[x,y]$ is an integral domain and $v$ satisfies $v(fg)=v(f)+v(g)$ and $v(
f+g)\geq\min(v(f),v(g))$, it extends uniquely to a valuation on the fraction fie
ld $k(x,y)$ by $v(f/g) = v(f) - v(g)$.

### The valuation ring and its dimension

Define the valuation ring:

$$V = \bigl\{\, \alpha \in k(x,y) : v(\alpha) \geq (0,0) \,\bigr\} \cup \{0\}.$$

This is a valuation ring of $k(x,y)$: for every $\alpha \in k(x,y)^*$, either $v
(\alpha) \geq (0,0)$ (so $\alpha \in V$) or $v(\alpha) < (0,0)$, in which case $
v(\alpha^{-1}) = -v(\alpha) > (0,0)$, so $\alpha^{-1} \in V$.

**Value group.** Since $v(x) = (1,0)$ and $v(y) = (0,1)$, the value group contai
ns $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$, hence equals all of $\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}$ (lex).

**Krull dimension.** The Krull dimension of a valuation ring equals the **rank**
 of its value group (the number of proper convex subgroups). For $\mathbb{Z}\tim
es\mathbb{Z}$ with lexicographic order, the convex subgroups are:

$$\{(0,0)\} \;\subset\; \{0\}\times\mathbb{Z} \;\subset\; \mathbb{Z}\times\mathb
b{Z},$$

giving exactly **2 proper convex subgroups**, so the rank is $2$. Therefore $\di
m V = 2$.

### Consistency check via Abhyankar's inequality

The valuation is trivial on $k$ (constants have value $(0,0)$), so it is a $k$-v
aluation. Abhyankar's inequality states:

$$\operatorname{tr.deg}(k(x,y)/k) \;\geq\; \operatorname{rat.rk}(v) + \operatorn
ame{tr.deg}(\kappa(v)/k).$$

Here $\operatorname{tr.deg}(k(x,y)/k) = 2$. The value group $\mathbb{Z}\times\ma
thbb{Z}$ has rational rank $2$. The residue field $\kappa(v)$ consists of the le
ading coefficients of elements with value $(0,0)$, which is just $k$, so $\opera
torname{tr.deg}(\kappa(v)/k)=0$. The inequality reads $2 \geq 2 + 0$, which is *
*tight** (equality holds). This confirms the construction is at the extremal cas
e and is consistent.

### Conclusion

For **any** field $k$, the lexicographic monomial valuation on $k(x,y)$ defined
above produces a valuation ring with value group $\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}$ (l
exicographic), which has rank $2$ and hence Krull dimension $2$. The constructio
n depends only on the variables $x, y$ and not on any special property of $k$.
