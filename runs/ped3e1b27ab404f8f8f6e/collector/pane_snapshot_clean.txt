## Solution

The intersection of the 4D unit hypercube $[0,1]^4$ with the hyperplane $x+y+z+w
=2$ is a 3-dimensional polytope. I determine its volume by two independent metho
ds.

### Method 1: Identifying the polytope as a regular octahedron

**Vertices of the intersection.** A vertex of the slice arises either from a hyp
ercube vertex lying on the hyperplane, or from the hyperplane meeting an edge of
 the hypercube in its interior. Hypercube edges connect vertices whose coordinat
e-sums differ by exactly $1$. The hyperplane sits at sum $=2$, so:
- Edges between sum-$1$ and sum-$2$ vertices meet the hyperplane **at** the sum-
$2$ endpoint.
- Edges between sum-$2$ and sum-$3$ vertices meet the hyperplane **at** the sum-
$2$ endpoint.
- Edges between sum-$0$/sum-$1$ (both $<2$) or sum-$3$/sum-$4$ (both $>2$) never
 reach sum $=2$.

Hence no edge is cut in its interior; the slice's vertices are exactly the $6$ h
ypercube vertices with sum $=2$, namely all permutations of $(1,1,0,0)$.

**It is a regular octahedron.** The center of these $6$ points is $(\tfrac12,\tf
rac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$, the hypercube center. The distance from the center to
 any vertex, e.g. $(1,1,0,0)$, is
$$\sqrt{(\tfrac12)^2+(\tfrac12)^2+(\tfrac12)^2+(\tfrac12)^2} = 1.$$
The edge length between two adjacent vertices, e.g. $(1,1,0,0)$ and $(1,0,1,0)$,
 is $\sqrt{0+1+1+0}=\sqrt{2}=1\cdot\sqrt{2}$, confirming a regular octahedron wi
th circumradius $r=1$ (the inherited Euclidean metric from $\mathbb{R}^4$ is use
d).

**Volume of a regular octahedron with $r=1$.** Such an octahedron is $\{|u|+|v|+
|s|\le 1\}\subset\mathbb{R}^3$. In the first octant this is the tetrahedron $u+v
+s\le 1$, $u,v,s\ge0$, of volume $\tfrac16$. By symmetry over $8$ octants:
$$\text{Vol} = 8\cdot\tfrac16 = \tfrac43.$$

### Method 2: Coarea / slicing formula (verification)

Let $V(t)=\operatorname{Vol}\bigl(\{x\in[0,1]^4:\sum x_i\le t\}\bigr)$. By inclu
sion–exclusion,
$$V(t)=\frac{1}{24}\sum_{k=0}^{4}(-1)^k\binom{4}{k}(t-k)_+^4.$$
At $t=2$: $V(2)=\tfrac{1}{24}(16-4)=\tfrac12$, consistent with the hyperplane pa
ssing through the center.

Differentiating, $V'(t)=\tfrac16\sum_{k=0}^{4}(-1)^k\binom{4}{k}(t-k)_+^3$, so
$$V'(2)=\tfrac16(8-4)=\tfrac23.$$

By the coarea formula with $f=x+y+z+w$ and $|\nabla f|=|(1,1,1,1)|=2$:
$$V'(t)=\frac{1}{|\nabla f|}\cdot\operatorname{Vol}_3(\text{slice})=\frac{1}{2}\
operatorname{Vol}_3(\text{slice}).$$
Thus $\operatorname{Vol}_3(\text{slice})=2\cdot\tfrac23=\tfrac43$, agreeing with
 Method 1.

### Result

$$\boxed{\dfrac{4}{3}}$$
