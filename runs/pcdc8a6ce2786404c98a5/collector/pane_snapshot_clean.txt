# Proof

**Claim:** The finite-rank operators are dense in $B(H)$ in the weak operator to
pology (WOT).

Recall that a basic WOT-neighborhood of $T \in B(H)$ is of the form
$$
\mathcal{N}(T; x_1,\dots,x_n, y_1,\dots,y_n, \varepsilon)
= \bigl\{ S \in B(H) : |\langle (S-T)x_k, y_k\rangle| < \varepsilon,\ k=1,\dots,
n \bigr\},
$$
where $x_1,\dots,x_n, y_1,\dots,y_n \in H$ and $\varepsilon > 0$. Thus it suffic
es to show: for every $T \in B(H)$, every finite collection $\{x_k\}_{k=1}^n, \{
y_k\}_{k=1}^n \subset H$, and every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists a finite-ran
k operator $F$ with $|\langle (F-T)x_k, y_k\rangle| < \varepsilon$ for all $k$.

**Construction.** Let
$$
M := \operatorname{span}\{x_1, \dots, x_n\} \subseteq H,
$$
a finite-dimensional (hence closed) subspace, and let $P_M : H \to H$ be the ort
hogonal projection onto $M$. Define
$$
F := T\,P_M \;\in B(H).
$$

**$F$ has finite rank.** The range of $F$ is
$$
\operatorname{ran}(F) = T(\operatorname{ran} P_M) = T(M).
$$
Since $M$ is finite-dimensional and $T$ is linear, $T(M)$ is finite-dimensional.
 Hence $\operatorname{rank}(F) = \dim T(M) \le \dim M < \infty$.

**$F$ lies in the prescribed WOT-neighborhood.** For each $k = 1,\dots,n$ we hav
e $x_k \in M$, so $P_M x_k = x_k$, and therefore
$$
F x_k = T P_M x_k = T x_k.
$$
Consequently $(F - T)x_k = 0$, which gives
$$
|\langle (F - T)x_k, y_k\rangle| = 0 < \varepsilon, \qquad k = 1,\dots,n.
$$

So $F$ belongs to $\mathcal{N}(T; x_1,\dots,x_n, y_1,\dots,y_n, \varepsilon)$.

**Conclusion.** Every WOT-neighborhood of every $T \in B(H)$ contains a finite-r
ank operator. Therefore the finite-rank operators are dense in $B(H)$ in the wea
k operator topology. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The argument actually shows something stronger: since $(F-T)x_k = 0$
 exactly (not merely small), the same construction places $F$ in any *strong* op
erator topology neighborhood $\{S : \|(S-T)x_k\| < \varepsilon\}$ as well. Hence
 finite-rank operators are dense in $B(H)$ in the strong operator topology, and
*a fortiori* in the weaker weak operator topology.
