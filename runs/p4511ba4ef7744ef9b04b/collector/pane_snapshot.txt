## Argument Principle for Meromorphic Functions

**Setting.** Let $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ be a simply-connected open set, l
et $f$ be a meromorphic function on $\Omega$ that is not identically zero, and l
et $\gamma : [0,1] \to \Omega$ be a closed, piecewise-$C^1$ curve whose image do
es not pass through any zero or pole of $f$ and which is null-homotopic in $\Ome
ga$ (automatic when $\Omega$ is simply connected and $\gamma$ is a simple closed
 curve, or more generally whenever $\gamma$ bounds a region inside $\Omega$). De
note by $Z(f) = \{a \in \Omega : f(a) = 0\}$ the set of zeros and by $P(f) = \{a
 \in \Omega : f \text{ has a pole at } a\}$ the set of poles. For $a \in Z(f)$ l
et $\operatorname{ord}_a(f) \geq 1$ be the order (multiplicity) of the zero, and
 for $b \in P(f)$ let $\operatorname{ord}_b(f) \geq 1$ be the order of the pole.

---

### Statement (Argument Principle)

$$\boxed{\;\operatorname{Ind}_{f\circ\gamma}(0) \;=\; \sum_{a \in Z(f)} \operato
rname{ord}_a(f)\,\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(a) \;-\; \sum_{b \in P(f)} \operatorn
ame{ord}_b(f)\,\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(b)\;}$$

where $\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(a) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma \frac{dz}{z-a
}$ is the winding number (index) of $\gamma$ around $a$, and $\operatorname{Ind}
_{f\circ\gamma}(0) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{f\circ\gamma}\frac{dw}{w} = \frac{1}
{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma \frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}\,dz$ is the winding number of the image
curve $f\circ\gamma$ around the origin.

Equivalently, writing $N(\gamma) = \sum_{a\in Z(f)}\operatorname{ord}_a(f)\opera
torname{Ind}_\gamma(a)$ for the total zero-count and $P(\gamma) = \sum_{b\in P(f
)}\operatorname{ord}_b(f)\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(b)$ for the total pole-count
(both counted with multiplicity and weighted by the winding number of $\gamma$),

$$\operatorname{Ind}_{f\circ\gamma}(0) \;=\; N(\gamma) - P(\gamma).$$

The integer $\operatorname{Ind}_{f\circ\gamma}(0)$ is precisely the net change i
n $\arg f(z)$ as $z$ traverses $\gamma$ once, divided by $2\pi$; this is the ori
gin of the name "Argument Principle."

---

### Proof

**Step 1 — Local form of a meromorphic function.** Let $c \in \Omega$ be either
a zero or a pole of $f$ of order $m = \operatorname{ord}_c(f) \geq 1$. Then in a
 neighborhood $U_c$ of $c$ we may write

$$f(z) = (z-c)^{m}\, g(z), \qquad g \text{ holomorphic and nowhere vanishing on
} U_c,$$

where $m > 0$ if $c$ is a zero and $m < 0$ (with $|m|$ the pole order) if $c$ is
 a pole. Taking the logarithmic derivative,

$$\frac{f'(z)}{f(z)} = \frac{m}{z-c} + \frac{g'(z)}{g(z)}.$$

The term $g'/g$ is holomorphic on $U_c$ (since $g$ is holomorphic and zero-free
there), so it contributes no residue at $c$. Hence

$$\operatorname{Res}\!\left(\frac{f'}{f},\, c\right) = m = \operatorname{ord}_c(
f),$$

positive for a zero and negative for a pole.

**Step 2 — The logarithmic derivative is meromorphic with controlled poles.** Si
nce $f$ is meromorphic and not identically zero, $f'/f$ is meromorphic on $\Omeg
a$. Its only possible poles are at the zeros and poles of $f$, and by Step 1 eac
h such point $c$ is a simple pole of $f'/f$ with residue $\operatorname{ord}_c(f
)$. At every other point of $\Omega$, the function $f'/f$ is holomorphic.

**Step 3 — Apply the Residue Theorem to $f'/f$.** The curve $\gamma$ is null-hom
otopic in the simply-connected domain $\Omega$, so it bounds (in the sense of ho
mology) a $2$-chain in $\Omega$. By hypothesis $\gamma$ avoids every zero and po
le of $f$, so all the poles of $f'/f$ that are "enclosed" by $\gamma$ (i.e., tho
se $c$ with $\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(c) \neq 0$) lie in the interior region an
d are finitely many — finiteness follows because the zeros and poles of a meromo
rphic function are discrete in $\Omega$ and the set $\{c : \operatorname{Ind}_\g
amma(c) \neq 0\}$ is compactly contained in $\Omega$.

The (homological) Residue Theorem gives

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma \frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}\,dz \;=\; \sum_{c \,:\, \opera
torname{Ind}_\gamma(c)\neq 0} \operatorname{Res}\!\left(\frac{f'}{f},\,c\right)\
,\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(c).$$

Substituting the residue from Step 1,

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma \frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}\,dz \;=\; \sum_{c \in Z(f)\cup
 P(f)} \operatorname{ord}_c(f)\,\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(c).$$

Separating zeros (positive order) from poles (negative order, with $\operatornam
e{ord}_b(f) = -\,\text{pole order}$, or equivalently writing the pole order as a
 positive integer and subtracting):

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma \frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}\,dz \;=\; \sum_{a \in Z(f)} \o
peratorname{ord}_a(f)\,\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(a) \;-\; \sum_{b \in P(f)} \ope
ratorname{ord}_b(f)\,\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(b).$$

**Step 4 — Identify the left-hand side as a winding number.** By the change of v
ariables $w = f(z)$,

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma \frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}\,dz \;=\; \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oin
t_{f\circ\gamma}\frac{dw}{w} \;=\; \operatorname{Ind}_{f\circ\gamma}(0),$$

which is exactly the winding number of the closed curve $f\circ\gamma$ about $0$
. (The hypothesis that $\gamma$ avoids zeros and poles of $f$ guarantees $f\circ
\gamma$ never hits $0$, so this winding number is well-defined.)

**Step 5 — Conclusion.** Combining Steps 3 and 4,

$$\operatorname{Ind}_{f\circ\gamma}(0) \;=\; \sum_{a \in Z(f)} \operatorname{ord
}_a(f)\,\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(a) \;-\; \sum_{b \in P(f)} \operatorname{ord}_
b(f)\,\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(b). \qquad\blacksquare$$

### Explanation of the terms

- **$\operatorname{Ind}_{f\circ\gamma}(0)$** — the winding number of the image c
urve $f(\gamma)$ around the origin. Geometrically, as $z$ travels once around $\
gamma$, the point $f(z)$ travels around $0$ a net number of times; this counts t
he total change of $\arg f$ divided by $2\pi$.

- **$\operatorname{ord}_a(f)\,\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(a)$** — each zero $a$ of
 order $m$ contributes $m$ times the winding number of $\gamma$ around $a$. Loca
lly $f(z) \approx (z-a)^m\cdot(\text{unit})$, so the argument of $f$ rotates $m$
 times as fast as the argument of $(z-a)$; the factor $\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma
(a)$ counts how many times $\gamma$ winds around $a$.

- **$-\operatorname{ord}_b(f)\,\operatorname{Ind}_\gamma(b)$** — each pole $b$ o
f order $n$ contributes $-n$ times the winding number of $\gamma$ around $b$. Lo
cally $f(z) \approx (z-b)^{-n}\cdot(\text{unit})$, so the argument of $f$ rotate
s $n$ times in the *opposite* direction, hence the minus sign.

- **Simply-connected hypothesis** — guarantees that $\gamma$ is null-homotopic i
n $\Omega$, so the Residue Theorem applies to the region bounded by $\gamma$ wit
hout needing to track non-trivial homology. (The theorem in fact holds on any do
main for any cycle $\gamma$ homologous to zero in $\Omega$ and avoiding the zero
s and poles of $f$; simple connectivity is a convenient sufficient condition ens
uring every closed curve is null-homologous.)
