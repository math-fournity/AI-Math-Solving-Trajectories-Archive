## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. The preimage of an algebraic set under $f$ is an algebraic set.
In fact, this holds for *any* morphism $\mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{C}} \to \mathbb{P}
^n_{\mathbb{C}}$; the hypotheses of surjectivity and finite fibers are not neede
d for this conclusion.

---

### Setup

A morphism $f : \mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{C}} \to \mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{C}}$ is give
n by $n+1$ homogeneous polynomials $F_0, \ldots, F_n \in \mathbb{C}[X_0, \ldots,
 X_n]$ of the same degree $d$, with no common zero on $\mathbb{P}^n$ (i.e., $\gc
d(F_0,\ldots,F_n)=1$ as homogeneous polynomials, or equivalently $V(F_0,\ldots,F
_n) = \varnothing$ in $\mathbb{P}^n$). Concretely, $f([X_0:\cdots:X_n]) = [F_0(X
):\cdots:F_n(X)]$.

### Algebraic argument

Let $Y \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ be an algebraic set. Then $Y = V(g_1, \ldots, g_m
)$ for some homogeneous polynomials $g_1, \ldots, g_m \in \mathbb{C}[Y_0, \ldots
, Y_n]$.

A point $p = [X_0:\cdots:X_n] \in \mathbb{P}^n$ lies in $f^{-1}(Y)$ if and only
if $f(p) \in Y$, i.e.,

$$g_i\big(F_0(X), \ldots, F_n(X)\big) = 0 \quad \text{for all } i = 1, \ldots, m
.$$

Define $h_i(X_0,\ldots,X_n) := g_i(F_0(X), \ldots, F_n(X))$. Since each $g_i$ is
 homogeneous (say of degree $e_i$) and each $F_j$ is homogeneous of degree $d$,
each $h_i$ is homogeneous of degree $e_i \cdot d$. Therefore:

$$f^{-1}(Y) = V(h_1, \ldots, h_m) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n,$$

the common zero locus of homogeneous polynomials, which is an algebraic set. $\s
quare$

### Topological argument (alternative)

A morphism of varieties is continuous with respect to the Zariski topology. Alge
braic sets in $\mathbb{P}^n$ are precisely the Zariski-closed subsets. The preim
age of a closed set under a continuous map is closed. Hence $f^{-1}(Y)$ is Zaris
ki-closed, i.e., algebraic.

### Remark on the hypotheses

The conditions that $f$ is surjective and has finite fibers imply (since $\mathb
b{P}^n$ is proper, so a quasi-finite morphism from a proper variety is finite) t
hat $f$ is a **finite morphism**. Finite morphisms are closed, so under these ad
ditional hypotheses $f$ also maps algebraic sets *forward* to algebraic sets (i.
e., $f$ is a closed map). But for the *preimage* direction asked in the problem,
 continuity alone suffices, and the surjectivity/finiteness conditions play no r
ole.
