## Counterexample

Define the sequence by:
$$a_{2m-1} = m^2, \qquad a_{2m} = m(m+1), \qquad m = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$$

This gives: $1,\ 2,\ 4,\ 6,\ 9,\ 12,\ 16,\ 20,\ 25,\ 30,\ 36,\ \ldots$

## Verification

**Strictly increasing and natural-valued.** For each $m \geq 1$:
$$a_{2m-1} = m^2 < m(m+1) = a_{2m} < (m+1)^2 = a_{2m+1},$$
since $m < m+1$. All terms are positive integers. ✓

**Each interior term is AM or GM of its neighbors.** There are two cases:

*Even positions* ($n = 2m$, $m \geq 1$): The neighbors are $a_{2m-1} = m^2$ and
$a_{2m+1} = (m+1)^2$, so:
$$\sqrt{a_{2m-1} \cdot a_{2m+1}} = \sqrt{m^2 \cdot (m+1)^2} = m(m+1) = a_{2m}.$$
Thus $a_{2m}$ is the **geometric mean** of its neighbors. ✓

*Odd positions* ($n = 2m+1$, $m \geq 1$): The neighbors are $a_{2m} = m(m+1)$ an
d $a_{2m+2} = (m+1)(m+2)$, so:
$$\frac{a_{2m} + a_{2m+2}}{2} = \frac{m(m+1) + (m+1)(m+2)}{2} = \frac{(m+1)(2m+2
)}{2} = (m+1)^2 = a_{2m+1}.$$
Thus $a_{2m+1}$ is the **arithmetic mean** of its neighbors. ✓

The types alternate **G, A, G, A, G, A, …** forever.

**Not eventually an arithmetic progression.** The successive differences are:
$$a_{n+1} - a_n = 1,\ 2,\ 2,\ 3,\ 3,\ 4,\ 4,\ 5,\ 5,\ \ldots$$
These are not eventually constant, so the sequence is not eventually an AP.

These are not eventually constant, so the sequence is not eventually a GP.

The sequence $(a_n)$ is a strictly increasing infinite sequence of natural numbe
