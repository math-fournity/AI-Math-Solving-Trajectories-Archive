**Answer: No.** The implication $cA=\{0\}\Rightarrow c=0$ does **not** hold in g
eneral. Below is an explicit counterexample.

### Counterexample

Let $B$ be the Banach algebra of upper-triangular $2\times 2$ complex matrices:
$$B=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\0&z\end{pmatrix}:x,y,z\in\mathbb{C}\right\},$$
equipped with (say) the operator norm. This is a closed subalgebra of $M_2(\math
bb{C})$, hence a Banach algebra.

Let
$$A=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&0\end{pmatrix}:a,b\in\mathbb{C}\right\}.$$

**$A$ is a bi-ideal of $B$.** For $\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\0&z\end{pmatrix}\in B$ an
d $\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\in A$:
$$\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\0&z\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&0\end{pmatrix}=\begi
n{pmatrix}xa&xb\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\in A,\qquad \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&0\end{pmatri
x}\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\0&z\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}ax&ay+bz\\0&0\end{pmatrix}
\in A.$$
So $A$ is a two-sided (closed) ideal.

**The hypothesis $Ab=\{0\}\Rightarrow b=0$ holds.** Take $b=\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\
0&z\end{pmatrix}\in B$ and suppose $Ab=\{0\}$. Then for **all** $a_0,b_0\in\math
bb{C}$:
$$\begin{pmatrix}a_0&b_0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\0&z\end{pmatrix}=\
begin{pmatrix}a_0x&\,a_0y+b_0z\\0&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&0\end{pma
trix}.$$
- $a_0x=0$ for all $a_0\Rightarrow x=0$.
- $a_0y+b_0z=0$ for all $a_0,b_0\Rightarrow y=0$ and $z=0$.

Hence $b=0$. ✓

**The conclusion $cA=\{0\}\Rightarrow c=0$ fails.** Set
$$c=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\neq 0.$$
For every $\begin{pmatrix}a_0&b_0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\in A$:
$$c\begin{pmatrix}a_0&b_0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}a_0&b_0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.$$
Thus $cA=\{0\}$, yet $c\neq 0$.

---

### Why the asymmetry

The hypothesis "$Ab=\{0\}\Rightarrow b=0$" says that $A$ is **right-faithful**:
left-multiplication by elements of $A$ detects every nonzero element of $B$. The
 desired conclusion is the **left-faithful** analogue. These are genuinely diffe
rent conditions: right-faithfulness constrains the *right* action of $A$ (hence
the bottom-right entry $z$ and the off-diagonal $y$ of $b$, which $A$ can "reach
" from the left), while left-faithfulness constrains the *left* action (the top-
left entry $x$ of $c$, which in the upper-triangular setting $A$ cannot "see" fr
om the right when $c$ has $x=0$). The upper-triangular structure breaks the symm
etry between the two sides, and no Banach-algebra axiom restores it.
