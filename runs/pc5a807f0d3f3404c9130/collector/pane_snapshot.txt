## Derivation of the Dirac Equation for a Free Spin-1/2 Field

### 1. Setup: Dirac spinors and gamma matrices

Let $\psi(x)$ be a Dirac spinor field, a 4-component complex spinor living in sp
acetime. Its Dirac-conjugate is defined as

$$\bar{\psi}(x) = \psi^\dagger(x)\,\gamma^0,$$

where $\gamma^\mu$ ($\mu = 0,1,2,3$) are the $4\times 4$ Dirac matrices satisfyi
ng the Clifford algebra

$$\{\gamma^\mu, \gamma^\nu\} = 2\,\eta^{\mu\nu}\,\mathbf{1}_4,$$

with metric signature $\eta^{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(+1,-1,-1,-1)$. We also defi
ne

$$\gamma^5 = i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3, \qquad \sigma^{\mu\nu} = \tfrac{
i}{2}[\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu].$$

### 2. The free Dirac Lagrangian density

The Lorentz-invariant, Hermitian Lagrangian density for a free massive spin-1/2
field is

$$\boxed{\mathcal{L} = \bar{\psi}\,(i\gamma^\mu \partial_\mu - m)\,\psi.}$$

Equivalently, since $\bar{\psi}\,i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi$ and its hermitian
conjugate combine to give the symmetric form,

$$\mathcal{L} = \tfrac{i}{2}\Big[\bar{\psi}\,\gamma^\mu\,(\partial_\mu\psi) - (\
partial_\mu\bar{\psi})\,\gamma^\mu\psi\Big] - m\,\bar{\psi}\psi,$$

which differs from the first form only by a total derivative $\tfrac{i}{2}\parti
al_\mu(\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu\psi)$ and therefore yields the same equations of mot
ion. We use the first (asymmetric) form for the derivation; treating $\psi$ and
$\bar{\psi}$ as independent fields.

### 3. Euler–Lagrange equations for fields

For a generic field $\phi_a$ the Euler–Lagrange equation is

$$\partial_\mu\!\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi_a)}\r
ight) - \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\phi_a} = 0.$$

We apply it twice: once with $\phi_a = \psi$ (a column spinor) and once with $\p
hi_a = \bar{\psi}$ (a row spinor). The two are treated as independent dynamical
variables.

### 4. Variation with respect to $\bar{\psi}$ — the Dirac equation

With $\mathcal{L} = \bar{\psi}\,i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi - m\,\bar{\psi}\psi$
:

- **Derivative w.r.t. $\bar{\psi}$ (no derivatives of $\bar{\psi}$ appear):**

$$\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\bar{\psi}} = i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi -
 m\,\psi.$$

- **Derivative w.r.t. $\partial_\mu\bar{\psi}$:**

$$\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\bar{\psi})} = 0 \quad\Longrig
htarrow\quad \partial_\mu\!\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\m
u\bar{\psi})}\right) = 0.$$

Substituting into the Euler–Lagrange equation:

$$0 - \big(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi - m\psi\big) = 0,$$

so

$$\boxed{(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - m)\,\psi = 0.}$$

This is the **Dirac equation**.

### 5. Variation with respect to $\psi$ — the adjoint Dirac equation

Now vary w.r.t. $\psi$. Since $\psi$ appears both undifferentiated (in $-m\bar{\
psi}\psi$) and differentiated (in $\bar{\psi}\,i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi$):

- **Derivative w.r.t. $\psi$:**

$$\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\psi} = -m\,\bar{\psi}.$$

- **Derivative w.r.t. $\partial_\mu\psi$:**

$$\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\psi)} = \bar{\psi}\,i\gamma^\
mu.$$

Euler–Lagrange:

$$\partial_\mu\!\big(\bar{\psi}\,i\gamma^\mu\big) - (-m\bar{\psi}) = 0 \;\Longri
ghtarrow\; i\,(\partial_\mu\bar{\psi})\,\gamma^\mu + m\,\bar{\psi} = 0.$$

Taking the hermitian conjugate and using $(\gamma^\mu)^\dagger = \gamma^0\gamma^
\mu\gamma^0$ together with $\bar{\psi}=\psi^\dagger\gamma^0$, this is more clean
ly written as

$$\boxed{\bar{\psi}\,(-i\!\overset{\leftarrow}{\partial}_\mu\gamma^\mu - m) = 0,
}$$

or equivalently, after hermitian conjugation,

$$\boxed{(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu + m)\,\psi = 0 \;\;\text{is NOT obtained; rath
er}\;\; i\partial_\mu\bar{\psi}\,\gamma^\mu + m\bar{\psi} = 0,}$$

i.e. the **adjoint Dirac equation**

$$i\,\partial_\mu\bar{\psi}\,\gamma^\mu + m\,\bar{\psi} = 0.$$

### 6. Consistency check — recovering the Klein–Gordon equation

Act on the Dirac equation from the left with $(i\gamma^\nu\partial_\nu + m)$:

$$(i\gamma^\nu\partial_\nu + m)(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - m)\psi = \big(-\gamma^
\nu\gamma^\mu\partial_\nu\partial_\mu - m^2\big)\psi.$$

Using $\gamma^\nu\gamma^\mu\partial_\nu\partial_\mu = \tfrac{1}{2}\{\gamma^\nu,\
gamma^\mu\}\partial_\nu\partial_\mu = \eta^{\nu\mu}\partial_\nu\partial_\mu = \B
ox$ (the antisymmetric part vanishes because $\partial_\nu\partial_\mu$ is symme
tric),

$$(-\Box - m^2)\psi = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; (\Box + m^2)\psi = 0.$$

Thus each component of $\psi$ satisfies the Klein–Gordon equation, confirming th
at the Dirac field describes a particle of mass $m$, while the first-order form
encodes spin-1/2 (the $\gamma^\mu$ structure yields the correct $J=1/2$ represen
tation of the Lorentz group).

### 7. Explicit covariant form

Writing $\partial_\mu = (\partial/\partial t,\, \nabla)$ with $\gamma^\mu = (\ga
mma^0, \gamma^i)$ and using $\partial_\mu\gamma^\mu = \gamma^0\partial_0 + \gamm
a^i\partial_i = \gamma^0\partial_t - \vec{\gamma}\cdot\nabla$ (since $\partial_i
 = -\partial^i$), the Dirac equation becomes

$$\boxed{i\gamma^0\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} = \big(-i\vec{\gamma}\cdot\nab
la + m\big)\psi,}$$

or, multiplying by $\gamma^0$ and using $\alpha^i = \gamma^0\gamma^i$, $\beta =
\gamma^0$,

$$\boxed{i\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} = \big(-i\vec{\alpha}\cdot\nabla + \be
ta\,m\big)\psi,}$$

the standard Hamiltonian form $i\partial_t\psi = H_D\psi$ with Dirac Hamiltonian
 $H_D = \vec{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{p} + \beta m$ (with $\mathbf{p}=-i\nabla$).

### Summary

Starting from the Lorentz-scalar, Hermitian Lagrangian density

$$\mathcal{L} = \bar{\psi}(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - m)\psi,$$

and treating $\psi$ and $\bar{\psi}=\psi^\dagger\gamma^0$ as independent fields,
 the Euler–Lagrange equations yield:

- **Dirac equation** (from varying $\bar{\psi}$):

$$\boxed{(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - m)\psi = 0.}$$

- **Adjoint Dirac equation** (from varying $\psi$):

$$\boxed{i\,\partial_\mu\bar{\psi}\,\gamma^\mu + m\,\bar{\psi} = 0.}$$

The gamma matrices obey $\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=2\eta^{\mu\nu}\mathbf{1}_4$,
and squaring the Dirac operator reproduces the Klein–Gordon equation $(\Box+m^2)
\psi=0$, confirming the field describes a free relativistic spin-1/2 particle of
 mass $m$.
