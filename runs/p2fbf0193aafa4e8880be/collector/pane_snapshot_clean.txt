## Proof

We evaluate $I = \oint_C \sqrt{z^2-1}\,dz$, where $C$ is the circle $|z|=R$ (tra
versed counterclockwise) with $R>1$, and $f(z)=\sqrt{z^2-1}$ is the holomorphic
branch in $1<|z|<\infty$ satisfying $f(z)\sim z$ as $z\to\infty$.

**Step 1: Laurent expansion of $f$ for $|z|>1$.**

For $|z|>1$ we factor out $z$:
$$f(z)=\sqrt{z^2-1}=z\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{z^2}},$$
where the branch of $\sqrt{1-1/z^2}$ is chosen so that $\sqrt{1-1/z^2}\to 1$ as
$z\to\infty$ (consistent with $f(z)\sim z$).

Since $|1/z^2|<1$, we apply the binomial series
$$(1-w)^{1/2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{1/2}{n}(-1)^n w^n = 1-\frac{w}{2}-\frac{
w^2}{8}-\frac{w^3}{16}-\cdots, \qquad |w|<1,$$
with $w=1/z^2$:
$$\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{z^2}}=1-\frac{1}{2z^2}-\frac{1}{8z^4}-\frac{1}{16z^6}-\cdots$
$

Multiplying by $z$:
$$f(z)=z-\frac{1}{2z}-\frac{1}{8z^3}-\frac{1}{16z^5}-\cdots$$

This Laurent series converges for $|z|>1$ and represents the holomorphic branch
of $f$ in that region.

**Step 2: Extract the residue.**

The coefficient of $z^{-1}$ in the Laurent expansion is
$$c_{-1}=-\frac{1}{2}.$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the integral.**

The contour $C: |z|=R$ (counterclockwise) lies entirely in the annulus of holomo
rphy $1<|z|<\infty$. By the standard result for Laurent series (or the residue t
heorem applied to the exterior domain), integrating term-by-term:
$$\oint_C z^n\,dz = \begin{cases} 2\pi i & n=-1,\\ 0 & n\neq -1, \end{cases}$$
so only the $z^{-1}$ term contributes:
$$I = \oint_C f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i\cdot c_{-1} = 2\pi i\cdot\left(-\frac{1}{2}\righ
t) = -\pi i.$$

**Verification via residue at infinity.** The function $f$ is holomorphic in $|z
|>1$ (including a neighborhood of $\infty$ after the change $w=1/z$). The residu
e at infinity is
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=\infty} f(z) = -c_{-1} = \frac{1}{2}.$$
The integral over a counterclockwise circle enclosing all finite singularities (
the branch points $\pm1$) equals
$$I = -2\pi i\,\operatorname{Res}_{z=\infty} f(z) = -2\pi i\cdot\frac{1}{2} = -\
pi i,$$
confirming the result.

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{I = \oint_C \sqrt{z^2-1}\,dz = -\pi i.}$$
