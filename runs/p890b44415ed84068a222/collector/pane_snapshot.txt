## Proof

**Euler characteristic.** For a finite CW complex, the Euler characteristic is t
he alternating sum of the numbers of cells:

$$\chi(X) = c_0 - c_1 + c_2 = 2 - 3 + 3 = 2.$$

**Determining $H_2(X)$.** Consider the cellular chain complex

$$0 \longrightarrow C_2 \xrightarrow{\ d_2\ } C_1 \xrightarrow{\ d_1\ } C_0 \lon
grightarrow 0,$$

where $C_0 \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$, $C_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}^3$, $C_2 \cong \mathbb{Z}^
3$, and there are no cells in dimensions $\geq 3$, so $C_i = 0$ for $i \geq 3$.

Since $X$ is connected, $H_0(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, so $\beta_0 = 1$. The hypothe
sis $H_1(X) \cong \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3$ gives $\beta_1 = 1$ (the rank
of the free part).

For a finite CW complex, the Euler characteristic equals the alternating sum of
the Betti numbers (ranks of the free parts of the homology groups):

$$\chi(X) = \beta_0 - \beta_1 + \beta_2.$$

Substituting:

$$2 = 1 - 1 + \beta_2 \implies \beta_2 = 2.$$

So the free part of $H_2(X)$ has rank $2$.

It remains to show $H_2(X)$ is torsion-free. Since there are no $3$-cells, $d_3
= 0$, and therefore

$$H_2(X) = \ker(d_2)/\operatorname{im}(d_3) = \ker(d_2) \subseteq C_2 \cong \mat
hbb{Z}^3.$$

A subgroup of a free abelian group is free abelian (by the structure theorem for
 finitely generated abelian groups, or equivalently since $\mathbb{Z}^3$ is a PI
D-module and submodules of free modules over a PID are free). Hence $\ker(d_2)$
is free abelian, and so $H_2(X)$ has no torsion.

Combining $\beta_2 = 2$ with torsion-freeness:

$$H_2(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2.$$

**Realizability check.** The data are consistent: take $d_1: \mathbb{Z}^3 \to \m
athbb{Z}^2$ of rank $1$ (so $\ker d_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$, giving $H_0 \cong \ma
thbb{Z}$), and let $\operatorname{im}(d_2) = \langle (3,0) \rangle \subseteq \ke
r(d_1) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$. Then

$$H_1 = \ker(d_1)/\operatorname{im}(d_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2/\langle(3,0)\rangle
\cong \mathbb{Z}/3 \oplus \mathbb{Z},$$

and $\ker(d_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$ since $d_2$ has rank $1$. Such boundary maps
are realized by a CW complex with the prescribed cells (e.g., via a presentation
 complex construction), confirming the hypotheses are satisfiable.

**Conclusion.** The Euler characteristic is $\chi(X) = 2$, and the only possibil
ity is

$$\boxed{H_2(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2}.$$
