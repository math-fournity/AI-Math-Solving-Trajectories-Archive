## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** Under the standard assumption that the underlying measure space
 has positive total measure, the condition implies that $f$ is not almost everyw
here equal to zero. In fact, it implies the much stronger statement that $|f(t)|
 = \infty$ almost everywhere.

**Step 1: The condition forces $|f| \geq c$ a.e. for every $c > 0$.**

For each $c > 0$, the hypothesis gives
$$m\big(\{t : |f(t)| < c\}\big) = 0.$$
Taking complements, the set $\{t : |f(t)| \geq c\}$ has full measure, i.e.,
$$m\big(\{t : |f(t)| \geq c\}^c\big) = 0.$$

**Step 2: $|f| \geq c$ a.e. for every $c > 0$ implies $f$ is not a.e. zero.**

Suppose, for contradiction, that $f$ is a.e. equal to zero. Then the set
$$A = \{t : f(t) = 0\}$$
has full measure: $m(A^c) = 0$.

For any $c > 0$, we have $A \subseteq \{t : |f(t)| < c\}$ (since $0 < c$). There
fore
$$m\big(\{t : |f(t)| < c\}\big) \geq m(A) = m(\Omega),$$
where $\Omega$ is the whole space. If $m(\Omega) > 0$, this contradicts the hypo
thesis that $m(\{t : |f(t)| < c\}) = 0$.

Hence $f$ cannot be a.e. equal to zero. $\square$

**Remark (stronger conclusion).** Taking $c = 1/n$ for $n = 1, 2, \dots$, each s
et $B_n = \{t : |f(t)| \geq 1/n\}$ has full measure. The countable intersection
$B = \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n$ also has full measure. On $B$, we have $|f(t)|
\geq 1/n$ for every $n$, which forces $|f(t)| = \infty$. Thus the hypothesis act
ually implies $|f(t)| = \infty$ a.e. — a far stronger conclusion than merely "no
t a.e. zero." In particular, if $f$ is required to be finite-valued (real-valued
 measurable), the hypothesis cannot be satisfied on any space of positive measur
e at all.
