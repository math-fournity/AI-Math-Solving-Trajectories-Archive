## Proof

Let $Z = f^{-1}(0)$. Since $f$ is continuous, $Z$ is closed. The hypothesis $f(x
)f(y)<0$ means (relabeling if necessary) $f(x)>0$ and $f(y)<0$, so the sets

$$U=\{p:f(p)>0\},\qquad V=\{p:f(p)<0\}$$

are nonempty, disjoint, open subsets of $\mathbb{R}^n\setminus Z$. Hence $\mathb
b{R}^n\setminus Z$ is disconnected.

We will use the following lemma.

**Lemma.** If $E\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ is closed and $\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E)=0$, the
n $\mathbb{R}^n\setminus E$ is path-connected (hence connected).

*Proof of Lemma.* Let $a,b\notin E$. Since $E$ is closed and $a,b\notin E$, ther
e is $\delta>0$ with $B(a,\delta)\cup B(b,\delta)\subset \mathbb{R}^n\setminus E
$. Choose an $(n-1)$-sphere $S$ centered at the midpoint of $a,b$, of radius $R>
\max(|a-b|/2,\delta)$, so that $a,b$ lie in the interior of the ball bounded by
$S$.

For each $m\in S$ consider the polygonal path $\gamma_m=[a,m]\cup[m,b]$. This pa
th meets $E$ only if $[a,m]\cap E\neq\varnothing$ or $[b,m]\cap E\neq\varnothing
$.

Consider the radial projection $\pi_a:\mathbb{R}^n\setminus\{a\}\to S$ sending $
p\mapsto a+R\frac{p-a}{|p-a|}$. Since $E\cap B(a,\delta)=\varnothing$, the map $
\pi_a$ is Lipschitz on $E$ (with constant $R/\delta$). Lipschitz maps do not inc
rease Hausdorff measure, so

$$\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\pi_a(E))\le (R/\delta)^{n-1}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E)=0.$$

If $[a,m]\cap E\neq\varnothing$ for some $m\in S$, then $m\in\pi_a(E)$. Thus $\{
m\in S:[a,m]\cap E\neq\varnothing\}\subset\pi_a(E)\cap S$, which has $(n-1)$-dim
ensional measure zero on $S$. The same argument with $b$ in place of $a$ shows $
\{m\in S:[b,m]\cap E\neq\varnothing\}$ has measure zero on $S$.

Therefore for $\mathcal{H}^{n-1}$-a.e. $m\in S$ (in particular for some $m$), th
e path $\gamma_m$ avoids $E$ entirely, giving a path in $\mathbb{R}^n\setminus E
$ from $a$ to $b$. ∎

**Conclusion.** Applying the contrapositive of the Lemma to $E=Z$: since $\mathb
b{R}^n\setminus Z$ is disconnected, we must have $\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(Z)>0$. A sta
ndard fact in fractal geometry states that if $\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)>0$ then $\dim_
H(E)\ge s$. Consequently

$$\dim_H(Z)\ge n-1.$$

Thus the statement is **true**: under the given hypotheses, the Hausdorff dimens
ion of the zero set of $f$ is at least $n-1$.
