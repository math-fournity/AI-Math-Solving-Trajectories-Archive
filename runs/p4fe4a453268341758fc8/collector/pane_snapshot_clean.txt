## Proof

with lattice $\Lambda_p = \mathbb{Z} + \mathbb{Z}p$, where we take $\operatornam
e{Im} p > 0$ (so the parallelogram with vertices $0,1,1+p,p$ is positively orien

### 1. The equivalences generate the modular identification

The three listed maps identify values of $p$ yielding *isomorphic* tori:
- $p \mapsto p+1$: $\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}(p+1) = \mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}p$ (change
 of basis $\{1,p\}\to\{1,p+1\}$).
- $p \mapsto -p$: $\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}(-p)=\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}p$ (basis $\{1
,-p\}$).
- $p \mapsto 1/p$: $\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}(1/p) = (1/p)(\mathbb{Z}p+\mathbb{Z}) =
 (1/p)\Lambda_p$, a homothetic lattice, hence the same torus.

Composing $p\mapsto -p$ with $p\mapsto 1/p$ gives $S:p\mapsto -1/p$; together wi
th $T:p\mapsto p+1$ these generate the modular group $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
=\langle S,T\rangle$ (with the reflection $p\mapsto -p$ extending it to the full
 identification group). So the listed equivalences are exactly the modular actio
n, and "special values with additional discrete symmetries" are the **elliptic p
oints** of this action — points with nontrivial stabilizer.

### 2. Automorphisms fixing the origin

Every automorphism of $E_p$ as a Riemann surface is of the form $z\mapsto \alpha
 z + \beta$ with $\alpha\Lambda_p=\Lambda_p$ and $\beta\in\mathbb{C}/\Lambda_p$.
 Those fixing the origin are multiplications by
$$\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Lambda_p):=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^*:\alpha\Lambda_
p=\Lambda_p\}.$$
Writing $\alpha\cdot 1 = m+np$ and $\alpha\cdot p = r+sp$ ($m,n,r,s\in\mathbb{Z}
$), the matrix $M=\begin{pmatrix}m&r\\ n&s\end{pmatrix}$ satisfies $\det M=\pm1$
 (invertibility of $\alpha$), and
$$\alpha = m+np,\qquad np^2+(m-s)p-r=0.$$
Thus $\operatorname{End}(E_p):=\{\alpha:\alpha\Lambda_p\subseteq\Lambda_p\}$ is
either $\mathbb{Z}$ (when $n=0$ generically) or an order in an imaginary quadrat
ic field (when $n\neq0$, forcing $p$ to be a quadratic irrationality). The autom
orphisms are precisely the **units** of this endomorphism ring, and since units
of a lattice in $\mathbb{C}$ must lie on the unit circle ($|\alpha|=1$, as $\alp
ha$ permutes the finite set of lattice vectors of minimal length), they are root
s of unity. The only imaginary quadratic orders whose unit groups exceed $\{\pm1

- $\mathbb{Z}[i]$: units $\{\pm1,\pm i\}$, order $4$;
- $\mathbb{Z}[\rho]$ with $\rho=e^{2\pi i/3}$: units $\{\pm1,\pm\rho,\pm\rho^2\}

Hence the generic torus has automorphism group $\{\pm1\}$ (order $2$), and the o
nly tori with *additional* symmetries are the **square** and **hexagonal** latti

### 3. Locating the special values via the equivalences

A nontrivial stabilizer means some non‑identity composition of the given maps fi

**Square lattice — $p=i$.** The composition $p\mapsto -p$ then $p\mapsto 1/p$, i

By the modular equivalence $T$, $\rho$ is identified with $e^{i\pi/3}=\tfrac12+\
