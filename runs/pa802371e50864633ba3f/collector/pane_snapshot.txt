## Proof

**Construction.** Let $f \colon \mathbb{R} \to (0,\infty)$ be the standard Gauss
ian density
\[
f(x) \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,e^{-x^{2}/2},
\]
which is strictly positive everywhere and satisfies $\int_{\mathbb{R}} f\,d\lamb
da = 1$. Define a set function $\mu$ on the Lebesgue $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{
L}$ by
\[
\mu(A) \;=\; \int_{A} f\,d\lambda, \qquad A \in \mathcal{L}.
\]

**$\mu$ is a measure.** Since $f \geq 0$ is measurable, $\mu$ is the indefinite
integral of a non-negative measurable function, hence a measure on $(\mathbb{R},
 \mathcal{L})$. Explicitly: $\mu(\emptyset) = 0$, and for disjoint $(A_n)$,
\[
\mu\!\Big(\bigcup_n A_n\Big) = \int_{\bigcup_n A_n} f\,d\lambda = \sum_n \int_{A
_n} f\,d\lambda = \sum_n \mu(A_n),
\]
by the monotone convergence theorem applied to $f \cdot \mathbb{1}_{\bigcup_{n=1
}^N A_n} \uparrow f \cdot \mathbb{1}_{\bigcup_n A_n}$.

**Total mass.** $\mu(\mathbb{R}) = \int_{\mathbb{R}} f\,d\lambda = 1$, so $\mu$
is a probability measure.

**Null sets coincide.** We prove $\mu(A)=0 \iff \lambda(A)=0$.

*($\Leftarrow$)* If $\lambda(A) = 0$, then $\mu(A) = \int_A f\,d\lambda = 0$, si
nce the Lebesgue integral of any measurable function over a null set is $0$.

*($\Rightarrow$)* Suppose $\mu(A) = 0$, i.e. $\int_A f\,d\lambda = 0$. The integ
rand $f \cdot \mathbb{1}_A$ is non-negative and measurable, so $\int f \cdot \ma
thbb{1}_A\,d\lambda = 0$ implies $f \cdot \mathbb{1}_A = 0$ $\lambda$-a.e. Since
 $f(x) > 0$ for **every** $x \in \mathbb{R}$, the set $\{x : f(x) = 0\} = \empty
set$, so $\mathbb{1}_A = 0$ $\lambda$-a.e., which gives $\lambda(A) = 0$.

**Handling uncountable null sets (e.g. the Cantor set).** The Cantor set $C$ is
uncountable yet $\lambda(C) = 0$. By the ($\Leftarrow$) direction, $\mu(C) = \in
t_C f\,d\lambda = 0$: the integral of any integrable function over a $\lambda$-n
ull set vanishes, regardless of the cardinality of that set. Conversely, any unc
ountable set $S$ with $\mu(S) = 0$ must satisfy $\lambda(S) = 0$ by the ($\Right
arrow$) direction. Thus the equivalence $\mu(A)=0 \iff \lambda(A)=0$ holds for *
*all** Lebesgue-measurable sets, countable or uncountable, with no distinction b
ased on cardinality.

**Remark on why a constant multiple fails.** One might try $\mu = c\,\lambda$ fo
r a constant $c > 0$, which trivially preserves null sets. But then $\mu(\mathbb
{R}) = c\,\lambda(\mathbb{R}) = c \cdot \infty = \infty \neq 1$. The Gaussian de
nsity $f$ resolves this: it is everywhere positive (so no $\lambda$-positive set
 is "missed"), yet integrable (so the total mass is finite and normalized to $1$
). The strict positivity of $f$ is the essential property ensuring the reverse i
mplication $\mu(A)=0 \Rightarrow \lambda(A)=0$; integrability ensures $\mu(\math
bb{R})<\infty$.
