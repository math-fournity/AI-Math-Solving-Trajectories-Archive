## Proof

**Setup.** Consider a 1-dimensional unbiased random walk $S_n = X_1 + X_2 + \cdo
ts + X_n$ starting at $S_0 = 0$, where the $X_i$ are i.i.d. with $\mathbb{P}(X_i
 = +1) = \mathbb{P}(X_i = -1) = \tfrac{1}{2}$. For a point $c \in \mathbb{Z}^*$,
 let
$$T_c = \inf\{n \ge 1 : S_n = c\}$$
denote the first hitting time of $c$.

**Key facts.**

*Fact 1 (Recurrence).* The unbiased walk on $\mathbb{Z}$ is recurrent: $\mathbb{
P}(T_c < \infty) = 1$ for every $c \in \mathbb{Z}$. This follows since $\sum_n \
mathbb{P}(S_n = 0) = \infty$, so by the Borel–Cantelli / recurrence criterion fo
r irreducible Markov chains, every state is recurrent. Hence $S_{T_c} = c$ almos
t surely.

*Fact 2 (Martingale).* $\{S_n\}$ is a martingale with respect to its natural fil
tration, with $\mathbb{E}[S_0]=0$ and bounded increments $|X_i| \le 1$.

**Main argument.** We claim $\mathbb{E}[T_b] = \infty$ for every $b \in \mathbb{
Z}^*$.

Suppose for contradiction that $\mathbb{E}[T_b] < \infty$ for some $b \in \mathb
b{Z}^*$. Since:
- $T_b$ is a stopping time with $\mathbb{P}(T_b < \infty) = 1$ (Fact 1),
- $\mathbb{E}[T_b] < \infty$ (assumption),
- the increments $X_i$ are bounded ($|X_i| \le 1$),

the **Optional Stopping Theorem** applies, giving
$$\mathbb{E}[S_{T_b}] = \mathbb{E}[S_0] = 0.$$

But by Fact 1, $S_{T_b} = b$ almost surely, so $\mathbb{E}[S_{T_b}] = b$. Combin
ing:
$$b = 0.$$

This contradicts $b \in \mathbb{Z}^*$ (i.e., $b \neq 0$). Therefore the assumpti
on $\mathbb{E}[T_b] < \infty$ is false, and
$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}[T_b] = \infty.}$$

**Remark on the hypothesis.** The given condition — that $\mathbb{E}[T_a] = \inf
ty$ for some $a \in \mathbb{Z}^*$ — is in fact *automatically satisfied* for the
 unbiased walk: the argument above (with $b$ replaced by $a$) shows $\mathbb{E}[
T_a] = \infty$ for **every** nonzero $a$. Thus the hypothesis merely confirms we
 are dealing with the standard unbiased random walk, and the same reasoning yiel
ds the conclusion for $b$. The expected hitting time of any nonzero point of a 1
-dimensional unbiased random walk is infinite.
