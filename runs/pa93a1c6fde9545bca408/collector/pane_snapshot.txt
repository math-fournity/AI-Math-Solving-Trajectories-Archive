Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether for any ray from the origin
 and any charge configuration, there exists a rational $\alpha$ making $\lim_{x\
to\infty}\|F(R_x)\|x^\alpha$ a nonzero constant.

## Analysis

The field is $\vec{F}(R) = \sum_{i=1}^n q_i \frac{\vec{R}-\vec{P_i}}{|\vec{R}-\v
ec{P_i}|^3}$, which is the Coulomb field of the charge configuration.

**When the field is not identically zero along the ray**, the multipole expansio
n gives an asymptotic series in integer powers of $1/x$:

$$\vec{F}(R_x) = Q\,x^{-2}\hat{u} + x^{-3}\bigl(3(\hat{u}\cdot\vec{p})\hat{u}-\v
ec{p}\bigr) + x^{-4}\vec{G} + \cdots$$

where $Q = \sum q_i$ (monopole), $\vec{p} = \sum q_i \vec{P_i}$ (dipole), etc. E
ach coefficient is at an integer power $x^{-k}$, so the leading nonzero term yie
lds $\alpha = k$ (an integer, hence rational) with a nonzero limit. One can show
 not all coefficients vanish simultaneously (the potential $\phi = \sum q_i/|R-P
_i|$ is harmonic outside the charges with non-removable singularities, so it can
not decay faster than every power).

**However**, the field can vanish *identically* along a ray, making every coeffi
cient zero for that particular direction.

## Counterexample

Place four charges in the $z=0$ plane:

$$q_1=+1,\ P_1=(1,0,0);\quad q_2=+1,\ P_2=(-1,0,0);\quad q_3=-1,\ P_3=(0,1,0);\q
uad q_4=-1,\ P_4=(0,-1,0).$$

Choose the ray $\ell$ along the positive $z$-axis, so $R_x = (0,0,x)$.

**Key observation**: All four points lie on the circle of radius 1 in the $z=0$
plane, centered at the origin. For any point $R_x = (0,0,x)$ on the $z$-axis, ev
ery $P_i$ is at the same distance:

$$D(P_i, R_x) = \sqrt{x^2 + 1} \quad \text{for all } i.$$

Since all distances are equal, the scalar factor $1/D(P_i,R_x)^2 = 1/(x^2+1)$ fa
ctors out, and the unit vectors are $\vec{r_i} = (R_x - P_i)/\sqrt{x^2+1}$. Thus
:

$$\vec{F}(R_x) = \frac{1}{(x^2+1)^{3/2}} \sum_{i=1}^4 q_i\,(R_x - P_i) = \frac{1
}{(x^2+1)^{3/2}}\left(R_x\sum q_i - \sum q_i P_i\right).$$

Compute the two sums:

$$\sum_{i=1}^4 q_i = 1+1-1-1 = 0,$$

$$\sum_{i=1}^4 q_i P_i = 1\cdot(1,0,0)+1\cdot(-1,0,0)+(-1)\cdot(0,1,0)+(-1)\cdot
(0,-1,0) = (0,0,0).$$

Therefore:

$$\vec{F}(R_x) = \frac{1}{(x^2+1)^{3/2}}\bigl(0\cdot R_x - \vec{0}\bigr) = \vec{
0} \quad \text{for all } x > 0.$$

## Conclusion

For this configuration and ray, $\|F(R_x)\| = 0$ for every $x > 0$. Hence for **
any** rational number $\alpha$:

$$\lim_{x\to\infty}\|F(R_x)\|\,x^\alpha = \lim_{x\to\infty} 0 = 0,$$

which is not a nonzero constant. No rational $\alpha$ satisfies the required con
dition.

The answer is **No**: it is not true that for any configuration of points and ch
arges (and any ray), there exists a rational $\alpha$ such that the limit conver
ges to a nonzero constant. The counterexample above—four charges forming a symme
tric quadrupole with vanishing total charge and vanishing dipole moment, viewed
along the symmetry axis—produces a field that is identically zero along the ray,
 precluding any such $\alpha$.
