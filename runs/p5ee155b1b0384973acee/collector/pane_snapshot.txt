## Proof

Let $A=\{1,2,\dots,n\}$. For a uniformly random $k$-element subset $\{X_1<\cdots
<X_k\}\subseteq A$ (chosen uniformly among all $\binom nk$ subsets), define the
sum
$$
S_n \;=\; X_1+\cdots+X_k .
$$
We determine the asymptotic distribution of $S_n$ as $n\to\infty$, for every adm
issible $k=k(n)\in\{1,\dots,n\}$.

---

### 1. Mean, variance, and symmetry

**Symmetry.** The involution $x\mapsto n+1-x$ is a bijection of $A$ sending a $k
$-subset with sum $s$ to a $k$-subset with sum $k(n+1)-s$. Hence the law of $S_n
$ is symmetric about
$$
\boxed{\mu_n \;=\; \frac{k(n+1)}{2}} .
$$

**Mean.** Writing $S_n=\sum_{j=1}^{n} j\,\mathbf 1_{\{j\text{ selected}\}}$ with
 $\Pr(j\text{ selected})=k/n$ gives $\mathbb E S_n = \frac{k}{n}\sum_{j=1}^n j =
 \frac{k(n+1)}{2}=\mu_n$. ✓

**Variance.** Let $I_j=\mathbf 1_{\{j\text{ selected}\}}$. Then $(I_1,\dots,I_n)
$ is uniform over $\{0,1\}^n$ with exactly $k$ ones, so
$$
\operatorname{Var}(I_j)=\frac{k(n-k)}{n^2},\qquad \operatorname{Cov}(I_i,I_j)=-\
frac{k(n-k)}{n^2(n-1)}\ (i\neq j),
$$
(using $\mathbb E[I_iI_j]=k(k-1)/(n(n-1))$). Since $\operatorname{Cov}(I_i,I_j)=
-\operatorname{Var}(I_1)/(n-1)$,
$$
\operatorname{Var}(S_n)=\operatorname{Var}(I_1)\cdot\frac{n\sum j^2-(\sum j)^2}{
n-1}.
$$
Using $\sum_{j=1}^n j^2=\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}$ and $(\sum j)^2=\frac{n^2(n+1)^2
}{4}$,
$$
n\sum j^2-(\sum j)^2 \;=\; \frac{n^2(n+1)(n-1)}{12}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{\sigma_n^2 \;=\; \operatorname{Var}(S_n)\;=\;\frac{k(n-k)(n+1)}{12}} .
$$

---

### 2. Bulk regime: $k\to\infty$ and $n-k\to\infty$ — asymptotic normality

**Permutation representation.** Let $\pi=(\pi(1),\dots,\pi(n))$ be a uniform ran
dom permutation of $\{1,\dots,n\}$. Then $\{\pi(1),\dots,\pi(k)\}$ is a uniform
$k$-subset, so
$$
S_n \;\stackrel{d}{=}\; \sum_{i=1}^{k}\pi(i) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{n} c_i\, a_{\pi(i
)},\qquad c_i=\mathbf 1_{\{i\le k\}},\quad a_i=i .
$$

**Wald–Wolfowitz / Noether CLT for random permutations.** *Theorem (Wald–Wolfowi
tz 1944; Noether 1949).* For arrays $(c_i),(a_i)$ with means $\bar c,\bar a$, th
e statistic $T=\sum_i c_i a_{\pi(i)}$ over a uniform permutation satisfies $\mat
hbb E T=n\bar c\,\bar a$ and $\operatorname{Var}T=\frac{1}{n-1}\bigl(\sum(c_i-\b
ar c)^2\bigr)\bigl(\sum(a_i-\bar a)^2\bigr)$, and
$$
\frac{T-\mathbb ET}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}T}}\;\xrightarrow{d}\;\mathcal N(0,1
)
$$
provided **Noether's condition** holds:
$$
\frac{\max_i(c_i-\bar c)^2}{\sum_i(c_i-\bar c)^2}\to 0
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{\max_i(a_i-\bar a)^2}{\sum_i(a_i-\bar a)^2}\to 0 .
$$

**Verification of the conditions.**

- For $(a_i)=(1,2,\dots,n)$: $\bar a=(n+1)/2$, $\sum(a_i-\bar a)^2=n(n^2-1)/12$,
 $\max(a_i-\bar a)^2=((n-1)/2)^2$, so the ratio equals $\frac{3(n-1)}{n(n+1)}\to
 0$. ✓

- For $(c_i)=(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{k},\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{n-k})$: $\bar c
=k/n$, $\sum(c_i-\bar c)^2=k(n-k)/n$, $\max(c_i-\bar c)^2=\max(k,n-k)^2/n^2$, so
 the ratio equals
$$
\frac{\max(k,n-k)^2}{n\,k\,(n-k)} .
$$
By symmetry of $S_n$ under $k\leftrightarrow n-k$ (complementation: $S_n^{(k)}=n
(n+1)/2-S_n^{(n-k)}$), assume $k\le n/2$. Then $\max(k,n-k)=n-k$ and the ratio i
s $\frac{n-k}{nk}\le\frac{1}{k}\to 0$ **iff $k\to\infty$**, and simultaneously w
e need $n-k\to\infty$ (so that the complementary side also diverges). Thus Noeth
er's condition holds **exactly when $k\to\infty$ and $n-k\to\infty$**.

**Conclusion of the bulk regime.** Under $k\to\infty$ and $n-k\to\infty$,
$$
\boxed{\displaystyle\frac{S_n-\frac{k(n+1)}{2}}{\sqrt{\frac{k(n-k)(n+1)}{12}}}\;
\xrightarrow{d}\;\mathcal N(0,1)} .
$$
Equivalently, the number of $k$-subsets whose sum equals $s$ is, uniformly for $
s=\mu_n+O(\sigma_n)$,
$$
\binom{n}{k}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_n^2}}\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{(s-\mu_n)^2}{
2\sigma_n^2}\Bigr)\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr).
$$
(This is also the asymptotic-normality statement for the coefficients of the Gau
ssian binomial coefficient $\binom{n}{k}_q$, whose coefficient of $q^{s-k(k+1)/2
}$ counts $k$-subsets of sum $s$.)

### 3. Edge regime: $k$ fixed (or $n-k$ fixed) — the Irwin–Hall limit

Suppose $k$ is fixed and $n\to\infty$ (the case $n-k$ fixed reduces to this by c
omplementation).

Generate the subset as $k$ i.i.d. draws $Y_1,\dots,Y_k$ from $\{1,\dots,n\}$ *wi
th replacement*, conditioned on being distinct. For fixed $k$,
$$
\Pr(\text{all distinct})=\frac{(n)_k}{n^k}\to 1,
$$
so the conditional law has the same limiting distribution as the unconditional o
rder statistics of $Y_1,\dots,Y_k$. Since $Y_i/n\Rightarrow \mathrm{Uniform}(0,1
)$ i.i.d., by the continuous-mapping theorem
$$
\frac{S_n}{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^k Y_{(i)}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^k Y_i \;\xri
ghtarrow{d}\; \sum_{i=1}^k U_i \;=:\; \mathrm{IH}_k,
$$
where $U_1,\dots,U_k\stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)$ and $\m
athrm{IH}_k$ is the **Irwin–Hall distribution** of order $k$ (mean $k/2$, varian
ce $k/12$).

Because $\sigma_n^2=\frac{k(n-k)(n+1)}{12}\sim\frac{kn^2}{12}$ when $k$ is fixed
, standardizing gives
$$
\frac{S_n-\mu_n}{\sigma_n}\;\xrightarrow{d}\;\frac{\mathrm{IH}_k-k/2}{\sqrt{k/12
}} .
$$
For $k=1$ this is the uniform law on $[-\sqrt3,\sqrt3]$; for $k=2$ the triangula
r law; in general it is the standardized Irwin–Hall law, which is **not** Gaussi
an for fixed $k$.

**Complement edge ($n-k=j$ fixed).** Since $S_n^{(k)}=\frac{n(n+1)}2-S_n^{(j)}$
with $\mu_n^{(k)}=\frac{(n-j)(n+1)}2$ and $\sigma_n^{2,(k)}=\frac{(n-j)j(n+1)}{1
2}\sim\frac{jn^2}{12}=\sigma_n^{2,(j)}$,
$$
\frac{S_n^{(k)}-\mu_n^{(k)}}{\sigma_n^{(k)}}=-\,\frac{S_n^{(j)}-\mu_n^{(j)}}{\si
gma_n^{(j)}}\;\xrightarrow{d}\;-\,\frac{\mathrm{IH}_j-j/2}{\sqrt{j/12}}\;=\;\fra
c{\mathrm{IH}_j-j/2}{\sqrt{j/12}},
$$
the last equality using the symmetry of $\mathrm{IH}_j$ about $j/2$. So the same
 Irwin–Hall limit of order $j=n-k$ is obtained.

---

### 4. Unified statement

Put $m_n:=\min(k,n-k)$. The distribution of $S_n$ is symmetric about $\mu_n=\fra
c{k(n+1)}2$ with variance $\sigma_n^2=\frac{k(n-k)(n+1)}{12}$, and:

- **If $m_n\to\infty$** (both $k$ and $n-k$ diverge):
$$
\frac{S_n-\mu_n}{\sigma_n}\;\xrightarrow{d}\;\mathcal N(0,1).
$$
The sums are **asymptotically normal**.

- **If $m_n$ is fixed** (one of $k,n-k$ stays bounded while the other diverges):
$$
\frac{S_n-\mu_n}{\sigma_n}\;\xrightarrow{d}\;\frac{\mathrm{IH}_{m_n}-m_n/2}{\sqr
t{m_n/12}},
$$
the **standardized Irwin–Hall distribution** of order $m_n$.

These two regimes are consistent: as $m_n\to\infty$ the Irwin–Hall law tends to
$\mathcal N(0,1)$ by the classical CLT, so the edge formula smoothly bridges int
o the bulk normal law. In particular, the **generic asymptotic distribution is G
aussian** $\mathcal N\!\bigl(\frac{k(n+1)}2,\frac{k(n-k)(n+1)}{12}\bigr)$, with
the Gaussian approximation becoming exact in the limit whenever $\min(k,n-k)\to\
infty$.
