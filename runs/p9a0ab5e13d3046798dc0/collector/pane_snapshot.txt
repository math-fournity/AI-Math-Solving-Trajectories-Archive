Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum degree $d$ such that $\deg(A
^{101} - B^{103}) \leq d$ forces $A^{101} = B^{103}$, where $A, B$ are non-const
ant polynomials over $\mathbb{C}$ with at least one of degree $> 1$.

This equals $\left(\min \deg(A^{101} - B^{103})\right) - 1$, where the minimum i
s over all valid $(A, B)$ with $A^{101} \neq B^{103}$.

Let $a = \deg(A)$, $b = \deg(B)$, with $a, b \geq 1$ and $\max(a,b) > 1$.

## Case 1: $101a \neq 103b$

The leading terms have different degrees, so no cancellation occurs:
$$\deg(A^{101} - B^{103}) = \max(101a,\, 103b)$$

The minimum over valid pairs with $101a \neq 103b$ is at $(a,b) = (2,1)$:
$$\max(202, 103) = 202$$
(achievable, e.g., $A = x^2, B = x$ gives $P = x^{202} - x^{103}$, degree 202).

## Case 2: $101a = 103b$ (leading terms may cancel)

Since $\gcd(101, 103) = 1$, we need $a = 103k, b = 101k$. Take $k=1$ (smallest):
 $a = 103, b = 101$, so $\deg(A^{101}) = \deg(B^{103}) = 10403$.

Let $D = \gcd(A^{101}, B^{103})$, $F = A^{101}/D$, $G = B^{103}/D$, with $\gcd(F
, G) = 1$. Then $P = D(F - G)$, and $\deg(F) = \deg(G) = 10403 - \deg(D)$.

**Applying the Mason–Stothers theorem** to $F + (-G) + (-(F-G)) = 0$:

Since $\gcd(F, G) = 1$, we have $\gcd(F, G, F-G) = 1$, so:
$$\max(\deg F, \deg G, \deg(F{-}G)) \leq r(F \cdot G \cdot (F{-}G)) - 1$$

**Bounding the radical:** Since $F \mid A^{101}$, the roots of $F$ are among the
 roots of $A$, so $r(F) \leq r(A) \leq \deg(A) = 103$. Similarly $r(G) \leq r(B)
 \leq 101$. Also $r(F{-}G) \leq \deg(F{-}G)$. Therefore:
$$r(F \cdot G \cdot (F{-}G)) \leq 103 + 101 + \deg(F{-}G) = 204 + \deg(F{-}G)$$

Since $\deg(F) = \deg(G) = 10403 - \deg(D) \geq \deg(F{-}G)$:
$$10403 - \deg(D) \leq 204 + \deg(F{-}G) - 1 = 203 + \deg(F{-}G)$$

$$\deg(F{-}G) \geq 10200 - \deg(D)$$

$$\deg(P) = \deg(D) + \deg(F{-}G) \geq 10200$$

For $k \geq 2$, degrees scale up, giving $\deg(P) \geq 10200k \geq 20400$.

## Conclusion

| Case | Minimum degree of nonzero $P$ |
|------|------|
| $101a \neq 103b$ | $202$ (at $a=2, b=1$) |
| $101a = 103b$ | $\geq 10200$ |

The overall minimum degree of a nonzero $A^{101} - B^{103}$ is $\min(202, 10200)
 = 202$.

Therefore, the maximum degree $d$ such that $\deg(A^{101} - B^{103}) \leq d$ nec
essarily implies $A^{101} = B^{103}$ is:

$$\boxed{d = 202 - 1 = 201}$$
