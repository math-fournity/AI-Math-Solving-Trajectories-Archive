Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a non-abelian Lie group can
 have the property that every cyclic subgroup ⟨g⟩ is compact.

**Answer: Yes, it is possible.** A non-abelian finite group provides an example.
 Moreover, no positive-dimensional Lie group can have this property.

---

## Proof

### Part 1: A positive-dimensional Lie group cannot have this property

Let $G$ be a Lie group with $\dim G > 0$. We show there exists $g \in G$ with $\
langle g \rangle$ not compact.

Since $\dim G > 0$, pick any nonzero $X \in \mathfrak{g}$ and consider the one-p
arameter subgroup $\gamma(t) = \exp(tX)$.

**Case 1: $\gamma$ is injective** (kernel $= \{0\}$). Set $g = \exp(X)$. Then $\
langle g \rangle = \{\exp(nX) : n \in \mathbb{Z}\} \cong \mathbb{Z}$, an infinit
e discrete subgroup, which is not compact.

**Case 2: $\gamma$ is periodic** with minimal period $T > 0$ (kernel $= T\mathbb
{Z}$). Then $\gamma(\mathbb{R}) \cong S^1$. Choose $\lambda = T/\sqrt{2}$ and se
t $g = \exp(\lambda X)$. The cyclic subgroup $\langle g \rangle = \{\exp(n\lambd
a X) : n \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ corresponds to $\{n\lambda \bmod T : n \in \mathbb{Z}
\} = \{n/\sqrt{2} \bmod 1 : n \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ in $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z} \cong
S^1$. Since $1/\sqrt{2}$ is irrational, this set is dense in $S^1$ but not equal
 to $S^1$, hence **not closed**. Since compact subsets of a Hausdorff space are
closed, $\langle g \rangle$ is not compact.

In both cases we produced an element whose cyclic subgroup is not compact. $\squ
are$

**Corollary:** If every $\langle g \rangle$ is compact, then $\mathfrak{g} = \{0
\}$, i.e., $G$ is zero-dimensional (discrete).

### Part 2: A non-abelian example exists among discrete Lie groups

Consider $S_3$, the symmetric group on 3 elements. This is a non-abelian group (
e.g., $(12)(13) = (132) \neq (123) = (13)(12)$).

As a finite group, $S_3$ is a **0-dimensional compact Lie group**: the underlyin
g manifold is a finite discrete set (automatically a smooth 0-dimensional manifo
ld), and all group operations are trivially smooth.

Every element of $S_3$ has finite order:
- $e$: order 1,
- $(12), (13), (23)$: order 2,
- $(123), (132)$: order 3.

Therefore, for each $g \in S_3$, the cyclic subgroup $\langle g \rangle$ is **fi
nite**, hence **compact**.

Thus $S_3$ is a non-abelian Lie group in which the subgroup generated by each el
ement is compact. $\square$

### Summary

- **Yes, it is possible.** Any non-abelian finite group (e.g., $S_3$) is a 0-dim
ensional non-abelian Lie group where every cyclic subgroup is finite and hence c
ompact.
- **No positive-dimensional Lie group** can have this property: for any nonzero
$X$ in the Lie algebra, one can always find an element $\exp(\lambda X)$ whose c
yclic subgroup is either infinite discrete (if the one-parameter subgroup is inj
ective) or dense-but-not-closed in a circle (if the one-parameter subgroup is pe
riodic with irrational relative period), and in either case not compact.
