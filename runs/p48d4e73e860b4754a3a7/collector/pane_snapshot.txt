## Answer

**Not necessarily.** The hypotheses do not force the union to be countably gener
ated, nor do they force it to be non‑countably‑generated. The union can be eithe
r. The nontrivial part is refuting the natural conjecture "the union of a chain
of non‑countably‑generated ideals is again non‑countably‑generated": I give a co
unterexample in which the union **is** countably generated.

(The opposite direction is trivial: a one‑element chain $\{J\}$ with $J$ non‑cou
ntably‑generated has union $J$, which is not countably generated.)

### Counterexample: a chain of non‑countably‑generated ideals with countably gen
erated union

Let
\[
\mathfrak A=\mathcal P(\omega\times\omega_{1}),
\]
and write $C_{n}=\{n\}\times\omega_{1}$ for the $n$‑th "column" ($n\in\omega$).
Each $C_{n}$ is in bijection with $\omega_{1}$.

**The candidate union.** Define
\[
I=\big\langle C_{n}:n\in\omega\big\rangle_{\mathrm{id}}
   =\big\{X\subseteq\omega\times\omega_{1}:\pi_{1}[X]\text{ is finite}\big\}.
\]
This is the ideal of sets supported on finitely many columns. It is generated by
 the countable family $\{C_{n}:n\in\omega\}$, so **$I$ is countably generated**.

**The chain.** For $n\in\omega$ set
\[
J_{n}=\Big\{X\in I:\text{ for every }m\ge n,\; X\cap C_{m}\text{ is countable}\B
ig\}.
\]
In words: $J_{n}$ consists of those elements of $I$ which, on columns $C_{m}$ wi
th $m\ge n$, meet each column only countably (columns $C_{0},\dots,C_{n-1}$ may
be met arbitrarily).

*$J_{n}$ is an ideal.* $\emptyset\in J_{n}$; if $X\in J_{n}$ and $Y\subseteq X$
then $Y\cap C_{m}\subseteq X\cap C_{m}$ is countable for $m\ge n$ and $\pi_{1}[Y
]\subseteq\pi_{1}[X]$ is finite, so $Y\in J_{n}$; and if $X,Y\in J_{n}$ then $(X
\cup Y)\cap C_{m}=(X\cap C_{m})\cup(Y\cap C_{m})$ is countable for $m\ge n$ and
$\pi_{1}[X\cup Y]$ is finite, so $X\cup Y\in J_{n}$.

*The chain is increasing.* If $X\in J_{n}$ then $X\cap C_{m}$ is countable for a
ll $m\ge n$, hence certainly for all $m\ge n+1$; so $X\in J_{n+1}$. Thus $J_{0}\
subseteq J_{1}\subseteq\cdots$.

*The union equals $I$.* The inclusion $J_{n}\subseteq I$ is immediate from the d
efinition. Conversely, take $X\in I$ and let $\pi_{1}[X]=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}$,
 finite. Put $N=\max\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}+1$. For every $m\ge N$ we have $m>\max
 i_{j}$, so $X\cap C_{m}=\emptyset$, which is countable. Hence $X\in J_{N}$. The
refore
\[
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}J_{n}=I.
\]

*Each $J_{n}$ is **not** countably generated.* Suppose, for a contradiction, tha
t $J_{n}=\langle \{G_{p}:p\in\omega\}\rangle_{\mathrm{id}}$ for some countable f
amily $\{G_{p}\}\subseteq J_{n}$. Since each $G_{p}\in J_{n}$ and $n\ge n$, the
intersection $G_{p}\cap C_{n}$ is countable. Set
\[
B=\bigcup_{p\in\omega}(G_{p}\cap C_{n})\subseteq C_{n}.
\]
Then $B$ is countable (a countable union of countable sets), while $C_{n}\cong\o
mega_{1}$ is uncountable, so there exists $d\in C_{n}\setminus B$. Now $\{d\}\in
 J_{n}$: indeed $\pi_{1}[\{d\}]=\{n\}$ is finite, and $\{d\}\cap C_{m}$ is $\{d\
}$ (countable) when $m=n$ and $\emptyset$ when $m\neq n$, hence countable for ev
ery $m\ge n$. On the other hand, $d\notin B\supseteq G_{p}\cap C_{n}$ and $d\in
C_{n}$, so $d\notin G_{p}$ for every $p$; consequently $d\notin\bigcup_{p}G_{p}$
, and therefore $\{d\}$ is not contained in any finite union $G_{p_{1}}\cup\cdot
s\cup G_{p_{k}}$. But every element of the ideal generated by $\{G_{p}\}$ is con
tained in such a finite union. Hence $\{d\}\notin\langle\{G_{p}\}\rangle$, contr
adicting $\{d\}\in J_{n}$. So $J_{n}$ is not countably generated.

### Conclusion

We have produced a Boolean algebra $\mathfrak A=\mathcal P(\omega\times\omega_{1
})$ and an increasing chain $\mathfrak J=\{J_{n}:n\in\omega\}$ of ideals, **none
 of them countably generated**, whose union $\bigcup\mathfrak J=I$ **is countabl
y generated** (by $\{C_{n}:n\in\omega\}$). Together with the trivial observation
 that a singleton chain $\{J\}$ with $J$ non‑countably‑generated yields a non‑co
untably‑generated union, this shows:

**The union $\bigcup\mathfrak J$ is not determined to be countably generated or
not by the stated hypotheses; it can be either.** In particular, the answer to t
he question "Is the union countably generated?" is **not necessarily**.
