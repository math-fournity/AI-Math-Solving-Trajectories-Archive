## Proof

We evaluate $I(t) = \int_{0}^{i\infty} e^{-x}\, x^{t-1}\, dx$ by rotating the co
ntour of the standard Gamma integral onto the positive imaginary axis.

**Contour setup.** Take the principal branch of $z^{t-1}$ (branch cut along the
negative real axis), so $z^{t-1}$ is analytic in the open first quadrant and con
tinuous on its closure. Consider the closed, positively-oriented contour $\mathc
al{C}$ consisting of:

1. the segment $[\varepsilon, R]$ on the positive real axis;
2. the circular arc $\Gamma_R: z = Re^{i\theta}$, $0 \le \theta \le \pi/2$;
3. the segment from $iR$ down to $i\varepsilon$ on the positive imaginary axis;
4. the circular arc $\gamma_\varepsilon: z = \varepsilon e^{i\theta}$, $\pi/2 \g
e \theta \ge 0$.

Since $e^{-z}z^{t-1}$ is analytic inside and on $\mathcal{C}$, Cauchy's theorem
gives

$$\int_{\varepsilon}^{R} e^{-x}x^{t-1}\,dx \;+\; \int_{\Gamma_R} e^{-z}z^{t-1}\,
dz \;-\; \int_{i\varepsilon}^{iR} e^{-z}z^{t-1}\,dz \;+\; \int_{\gamma_\varepsil
on} e^{-z}z^{t-1}\,dz \;=\; 0.$$

**Vanishing of the large arc.** On $\Gamma_R$, $z = Re^{i\theta}$, so $|e^{-z}|
= e^{-R\cos\theta}$ and $|z^{t-1}| = R^{\operatorname{Re}(t)-1}e^{-\operatorname
{Im}(t)\theta}$. Thus

$$\left|\int_{\Gamma_R}\right| \le C\, R^{\operatorname{Re}(t)}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}
e^{-R\cos\theta}\,d\theta.$$

Substituting $u = \pi/2 - \theta$ near $\theta = \pi/2$ shows $\int_{0}^{\pi/2}
e^{-R\cos\theta}\,d\theta = O(1/R)$, hence $\left|\int_{\Gamma_R}\right| = O(R^{
\operatorname{Re}(t)-1}) \to 0$ as $R \to \infty$ provided $\operatorname{Re}(t)
 < 1$.

**Vanishing of the small arc.** On $\gamma_\varepsilon$, $|e^{-z}z^{t-1}| = O(\v
arepsilon^{\operatorname{Re}(t)-1})$ and the arc length is $O(\varepsilon)$, so
$\left|\int_{\gamma_\varepsilon}\right| = O(\varepsilon^{\operatorname{Re}(t)})
\to 0$ as $\varepsilon \to 0$ provided $\operatorname{Re}(t) > 0$.

**Conclusion for $0 < \operatorname{Re}(t) < 1$.** Letting $\varepsilon \to 0$ a
nd $R \to \infty$,

$$\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-x}x^{t-1}\,dx \;=\; \int_{0}^{i\infty} e^{-z}z^{t-1}\,dz
.$$

The left-hand side is the defining integral of the Gamma function, so

$$\boxed{\int_{0}^{i\infty} e^{-x}\, x^{t-1}\, dx \;=\; \Gamma(t), \qquad 0 < \o
peratorname{Re}(t) < 1.}$$

**Analytic continuation.** Both sides are meromorphic functions of $t$: the righ
t-hand side $\Gamma(t)$ is meromorphic with simple poles at $t = 0, -1, -2, \dot
s$; the left-hand side, viewed as a function of $t$ through $x^{t-1} = e^{(t-1)\
log x}$ (principal branch), is analytic in $t$ on the same domain. The identity
theorem extends the equality to all $t$ with $\operatorname{Re}(t) > 0$ by conti
nuation within the half-plane (the contour rotation argument can be repeated wit
h the real-axis integral providing the analytic continuation), and then to all $
t \in \mathbb{C} \setminus \{0,-1,-2,\dots\}$ where $\Gamma(t)$ is defined, with
 the integral understood as the analytic continuation of the contour integral.
