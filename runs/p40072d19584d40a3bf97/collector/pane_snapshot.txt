## Solution

Let $n = 2013$ and $S_n$ be the set of all permutations of $\{1, 2, \ldots, n\}$
. We compute
$$\sum_{\sigma \in S_n} f(\sigma)^4 = |S_n| \cdot \mathbb{E}[f^4] = n!\cdot \mat
hbb{E}[f^4].$$

**Indicator decomposition.** For each $i \in \{1,\ldots,n\}$, let $X_i = \mathbf
{1}[\sigma(i)=i]$, so $f(\sigma) = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$. Then
$$\mathbb{E}[f^4] = \sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{n} \mathbb{E}[X_i X_j X_k X_l].$$

**Key fact.** If the ordered tuple $(i,j,k,l)$ uses exactly $m$ distinct values,
 then $X_i X_j X_k X_l = 1$ iff all $m$ corresponding elements are fixed, which
happens with probability
$$\frac{(n-m)!}{n!} = \frac{1}{n(n-1)\cdots(n-m+1)}.$$

So we group the $n^4$ ordered tuples by the number $m \in \{1,2,3,4\}$ of distin
ct indices and count each group.

**Counting by multiplicity pattern.**

- **$m=1$** (all four equal, pattern $4$): count $n$. Contribution: $n \cdot \tf
rac{1}{n} = 1$.

- **$m=2$**, two sub-patterns:
  - Pattern $(3,1)$: choose the 3 positions sharing a value ($\binom{4}{3}=4$ wa
ys), then pick 2 distinct ordered values: $n(n-1)$. Count $4\,n(n-1)$. Contribut
ion: $4\,n(n-1)\cdot\tfrac{1}{n(n-1)}=4$.
  - Pattern $(2,2)$: partition 4 positions into 2 pairs (3 ways), pick 2 distinc
t ordered values: $n(n-1)$. Count $3\,n(n-1)$. Contribution: $3$.

- **$m=3$** (pattern $(2,1,1)$): choose the 2 positions for the doubled value ($
\binom{4}{2}=6$ ways), assign 3 distinct ordered values: $n(n-1)(n-2)$. Count $6
\,n(n-1)(n-2)$. Contribution: $6\,n(n-1)(n-2)\cdot\tfrac{1}{n(n-1)(n-2)}=6$.

- **$m=4$** (all distinct): count $n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)$. Contribution: $1$.

**Summing.**
$$\mathbb{E}[f^4] = 1 + 4 + 3 + 6 + 1 = 15.$$

(As a sanity check, this equals the Bell number $B_4 = 15$, matching the known f
act that the fixed-point count of a random permutation of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ conve
rges in distribution to $\mathrm{Poisson}(1)$, whose 4th moment is exactly $B_4
= 15$; the computation above shows the equality is *exact* for all $n \ge 4$.)

**Final answer.**
$$\sum_{\sigma \in S} f(\sigma)^4 = n!\cdot \mathbb{E}[f^4] = 2013!\cdot 15 = \b
oxed{15\cdot 2013!}.$$
