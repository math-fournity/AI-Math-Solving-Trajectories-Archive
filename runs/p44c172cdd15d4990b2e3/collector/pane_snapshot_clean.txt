Looking at this problem, I need to find the least $n$ such that every $t \in (0,
1)$ is a sum of $n$ distinct "interesting" irrationals (irrationals in $(0,1)$ w
hose first four decimal digits are identical).

## Setup

For each digit $d \in \{0,1,\dots,9\}$, the interesting numbers with leading dig
it-pattern $dddd$ lie in:
$$I_d = \left[\frac{1111d}{10000},\; \frac{1111d}{10000} + \frac{1}{10000}\right
) = [a_d,\; a_d + \varepsilon)$$
where $a_d = \frac{1111d}{10000}$ and $\varepsilon = \frac{1}{10000}$.

Since $a_d$ and $a_d+\varepsilon$ are **rational**, the irrational elements of $
I_d$ lie in the **open** interval $(a_d, a_d+\varepsilon)$.

## Achievable sums for a fixed digit-combination

Choose digits $d_1,\dots,d_n$ (repetition allowed) with $m = d_1+\cdots+d_n$. Ev
ery integer $m \in \{0,1,\dots,9n\}$ is attainable. Setting $x_i = a_{d_i} + \de
lta_i$ with $\delta_i \in (0,\varepsilon)$ irrational, the sum is:
$$T = S_m + \delta, \quad S_m = \frac{1111m}{10000}, \quad \delta = \sum \delta_
i \in (0,\, n\varepsilon).$$

**Claim:** For $n \geq 2$, every $T \in (S_m,\, S_m + n\varepsilon)$ is achievab
le with distinct irrational $x_i$.

*Proof:* Given $\delta \in (0, n\varepsilon)$, we need irrational $\delta_i \in
(0,\varepsilon)$ with $\sum \delta_i = \delta$ and all $x_i$ distinct. When $d_i
 \neq d_j$, distinctness is automatic since $|a_{d_i}-a_{d_j}| \geq \frac{1111}{
10000} > \varepsilon$. When $d_i = d_j$, we need $\delta_i \neq \delta_j$. The v
alid set is an open subset of the hyperplane $\sum \delta_i = \delta$ in $(0,\va
repsilon)^n$, minus countably many hyperplanes (rationality + equality constrain
ts). For $n \geq 2$ this is non-empty (indeed dense). $\checkmark$

The endpoints $S_m$ and $S_m + n\varepsilon$ are **not** achievable (they force
all $\delta_i$ to rational boundary values). So the achievable set for fixed $m$
 is the **open** interval $(S_m,\, S_m + n\varepsilon)$.

## Coverage condition

The total achievable set is:
$$\bigcup_{m=0}^{9n} \left(\frac{1111m}{10000},\; \frac{1111m+n}{10000}\right).$
$

Consecutive intervals $m$ and $m+1$ **overlap** (as open intervals) iff:
$$\frac{1111m+n}{10000} > \frac{1111(m+1)}{10000} \iff n > 1111 \iff n \geq 1112
.$$

- **First interval** ($m=0$): $(0,\, n/10000)$ — starts at $0$, covering all sma
ll $t>0$. ✓
- **Last interval** ($m=9n$): ends at $\frac{9999n+n}{10000} = n > 1$. ✓

## $n = 1111$ fails

With $n = 1111$, consecutive open intervals are **adjacent but disjoint**:
$$\left(\frac{1111m}{10000},\, \frac{1111(m+1)}{10000}\right) \cup \left(\frac{1
111(m+1)}{10000},\, \frac{1111(m+2)}{10000}\right)$$
The point $t = \frac{1111(m+1)}{10000}$ belongs to **neither** interval. For $m=
0,\dots,8$, these gap points $\frac{1111k}{10000}$ ($k=1,\dots,9$) all lie in $(
0,1)$ (since $\frac{1111\cdot 9}{10000} = 0.9999 < 1$). So 9 values of $t$ are u
ncovered. ✗

## $n = 1112$ works

With $n = 1112 > 1111$, every pair of consecutive open intervals **strictly over
laps**. The union is the single open interval $(0,\, 1112)$, which contains all
of $(0,1)$. Every $t \in (0,1)$ falls in some $(S_m, S_m + n\varepsilon)$ and is
 achievable as a sum of $n = 1112$ distinct interesting irrationals. ✓

## Answer

$$\boxed{n = 1112}$$
