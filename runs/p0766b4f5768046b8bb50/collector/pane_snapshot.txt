## Proof

**Claim:** $J$ admits a minimum on $K$.

We apply the **direct method of the calculus of variations**.

**Step 1. Reformulate the problem.** Since $K = u_0 + H^1_0(\Omega)$, every $v \
in K$ can be written uniquely as $v = u_0 + w$ with $w \in H^1_0(\Omega)$. Defin
e
$$\widetilde{J}(w) := J(u_0 + w) = \int_\Omega \langle A(x)\nabla(u_0+w),\, \nab
la(u_0+w)\rangle\, dx, \qquad w \in H^1_0(\Omega).$$
Finding $\min_K J$ is equivalent to finding $\min_{H^1_0} \widetilde{J}$.

**Step 2. Coercivity of $\widetilde{J}$ on $H^1_0(\Omega)$.** By the ellipticity
 condition,
$$\widetilde{J}(w) \ge \lambda_1 \int_\Omega |\nabla(u_0+w)|^2\, dx \ge \lambda_
1\Big(\tfrac12\|\nabla w\|_{L^2}^2 - \|\nabla u_0\|_{L^2}^2\Big),$$
using $|a+b|^2 \ge \tfrac12|a|^2 - |b|^2$. By the **Poincaré inequality** (valid
 on $H^1_0(\Omega)$ for bounded $\Omega$), $\|w\|_{H^1} \le C\|\nabla w\|_{L^2}$
. Hence
$$\widetilde{J}(w) \ge \tfrac{\lambda_1}{2}\|\nabla w\|_{L^2}^2 - \lambda_1\|\na
bla u_0\|_{L^2}^2 \ge c\,\|w\|_{H^1}^2 - C,$$
with $c, C > 0$. Thus $\widetilde{J}$ is coercive on $H^1_0(\Omega)$.

**Step 3. Weak lower semicontinuity.** Expand:
$$\widetilde{J}(w) = \int_\Omega \langle A\nabla w,\nabla w\rangle\, dx + 2\int_
\Omega \langle A\nabla u_0,\nabla w\rangle\, dx + \int_\Omega \langle A\nabla u_
0,\nabla u_0\rangle\, dx.$$
The first term is a continuous quadratic form on $L^2(\Omega)^n$; since $A$ is u
niformly elliptic, the bilinear form $a(w_1,w_2)=\int\langle A\nabla w_1,\nabla
w_2\rangle$ is continuous and **convex** on $H^1_0$, hence weakly lower semicont
inuous. The second term is a continuous linear functional of $w$ on $H^1_0$, hen
ce weakly continuous. The third term is constant. Therefore $\widetilde{J}$ is *
*weakly lower semicontinuous** on $H^1_0(\Omega)$.

**Step 4. Existence of a minimizer.** Let $m = \inf_{H^1_0}\widetilde{J}$ (finit
e, since $\widetilde{J}(0)<\infty$). Take a minimizing sequence $\{w_k\}\subset
H^1_0$ with $\widetilde{J}(w_k)\to m$. By coercivity, $\{w_k\}$ is bounded in $H
^1_0(\Omega)$. Since $H^1_0(\Omega)$ is a **reflexive** Banach space (it is a cl
osed subspace of the Hilbert space $H^1(\Omega)$), there exists a subsequence (s
till denoted $w_k$) and $w^*\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ with $w_k \rightharpoonup w^*$ we
akly in $H^1_0$. By weak lower semicontinuity,
$$\widetilde{J}(w^*) \le \liminf_{k\to\infty}\widetilde{J}(w_k) = m.$$
Since $m$ is the infimum, $\widetilde{J}(w^*) = m$, so $w^*$ is a minimizer.

**Step 5. Conclusion.** Setting $u^* = u_0 + w^* \in K$, we have $J(u^*) = \wide
tilde{J}(w^*) = m = \min_K J$. Moreover, since $A$ is uniformly elliptic, $\wide
tilde{J}$ is **strictly convex** on $H^1_0$ (the quadratic part $\int\langle A\n
abla w,\nabla w\rangle \ge \lambda_1\|\nabla w\|^2$ is strictly convex on $H^1_0
$ by Poincaré), so the minimizer is in fact **unique**.

Therefore, $J$ admits a (unique) minimum on $K$.
