## Proof

Let the digits be $M,A,Y$ with $M,Y\in\{1,\dots,9\}$ and $A\in\{0,\dots,9\}$. Th
e two codes are

$$\text{MAYA}=1000M+101A+10Y,\qquad \text{YAM}=100Y+10A+M.$$

We seek the smallest natural number $k$ with $\text{MAYA}=k\cdot\text{YAM}$.

### Setting up the equation

Expanding $k(100Y+10A+M)=1000M+101A+10Y$ and collecting terms gives

$$(1000-k)M+(101-10k)A=10(10k-1)Y. \tag{1}$$

A useful observation: comparing units digits, the units digit of $\text{MAYA}$ i
s $A$, while the units digit of $k\cdot\text{YAM}$ is $(kM)\bmod 10$. Hence

$$A\equiv kM\pmod{10}. \tag{2}$$

### $k=10$ works

For $k=10$, equation (1) becomes $990M+A=990Y$, i.e. $A=990(Y-M)$. Since $0\le A
\le 9$, we need $Y=M$ and $A=0$. This is admissible for every $M=Y\in\{1,\dots,9
\}$. For example, $M=Y=1,\ A=0$ gives $\text{MAYA}=1010$ and $\text{YAM}=101$, a
nd indeed $1010=10\cdot 101$. So $k=10$ is achievable.

### Ruling out $k=1,\dots,9$

For $k\le 9$ both coefficients $1000-k$ and $101-10k$ are positive. Since $Y\le
9$, equation (1) yields the bound

$$(1000-k)M+(101-10k)A\le 90(10k-1). \tag{3}$$

This severely restricts $M$. For each $k=1,\dots,9$ we list every $M$ allowed by
 (3), the unique $A\in\{0,\dots,9\}$ satisfying (2), and the resulting $Y=\dfrac
{(1000-k)M+(101-10k)A}{10(10k-1)}$:

| $k$ | bound on $M$ | candidates $(M,A)$ | $Y$ |
|-----|--------------|--------------------|-----|
| 1 | $999M\le 810$ → none | — | — |
| 2 | $M=1,\ A=2$ | $(1,2)$ | $1160/190$ ✗ |
| 3 | $M\le2$ | $(1,3),(2,6)$ | $1210/290,\ 2420/290$ ✗ |
| 4 | $M\le3$ | $(1,4),(2,8),(3,2)$ | $1240/390,\ 2480/390,\ 3110/390$ ✗ |
| 5 | $M\le4$ | $(1,5),(2,0),(3,5),(4,0)$ | $1250/490,\ 1990/490,\ 3240/490,\ 39
80/490$ ✗ |
| 6 | $M\le5$ | $(1,6),(2,2),(3,8),(4,4),(5,0)$ | all non-integer ✗ |
| 7 | $M\le6$ | $(1,7),(2,4),(3,1),(4,8),(5,5),(6,2)$ | all non-integer ✗ |
| 8 | $M\le7$ | $(1,8),(2,6),(3,4),(4,2),(5,0),(6,8),(7,6)$ | all non-integer ✗
|
| 9 | $M\le8$ | $(1,9),(2,8),\dots,(8,2)$ | all non-integer ✗ |

(For $k=1$, (3) gives $999M+91A\le 810$, impossible since $M\ge1$.)

In every case $k=1,\dots,9$, either no $(M,A)$ satisfies the bound, or the uniqu
e candidate forced by the units-digit condition (2) produces a value of $Y$ that
 is not an integer in $\{1,\dots,9\}$. Hence no digit configuration realizes any
 $k\le 9$.

### Conclusion

The smallest natural number $k$ for which a valid digit configuration exists is

$$\boxed{k=10}.$$
