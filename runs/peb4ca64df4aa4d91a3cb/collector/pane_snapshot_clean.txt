## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. For every orthogonal matrix $A \in O(n)$ and every $\varepsilon
> 0$, there exists a rational orthogonal matrix $B$ with $\|A - B\| < \varepsilo
n$ (where $\|\cdot\|$ is the operator norm).

The argument proceeds in four steps.

---

**Step 1 — Reduction to $SO(n)$.**

Let $A \in O(n)$.

- If $\det A = 1$, set $R := A$ and $D := I_n$.
- If $\det A = -1$, set $D := \operatorname{diag}(-1, 1, \dots, 1)$ and $R := A
D$. Then $\det R = (\det A)(\det D) = (-1)(-1) = 1$, so $R \in SO(n)$, and $A =
R D$.

In both cases $D$ is a fixed **rational** orthogonal matrix and $R \in SO(n)$ wi
th $A = R D$. If we find a rational orthogonal $R' \in SO(n)$ with $\|R - R'\| <
 \varepsilon$, then $B := R' D$ is rational orthogonal (product of rational matr
ices), and since $D$ is orthogonal (so $\|D\| = 1$ and right-multiplication by $
D$ is an isometry for the operator norm),

$$\|A - B\| = \|R D - R' D\| = \|(R - R') D\| = \|R - R'\| < \varepsilon.$$

Hence it suffices to approximate elements of $SO(n)$ by **rational** elements of
 $SO(n)$.

---

**Step 2 — $SO(n)$ is generated by Givens rotations.**

For $1 \le i < j \le n$ and $\theta \in \mathbb{R}$, the *Givens rotation* $G_{i
j}(\theta)$ is the identity matrix with the $(i,i),(i,j),(j,i),(j,j)$ entries re
placed by $\cos\theta, -\sin\theta, \sin\theta, \cos\theta$. It is a standard fa
ct (Givens-based QR / Euler-angle decomposition) that every $R \in SO(n)$ is a f
inite product

$$R = G_{i_1 j_1}(\theta_1)\, G_{i_2 j_2}(\theta_2) \cdots G_{i_m j_m}(\theta_m)
, \qquad m \le \tfrac{n(n-1)}{2}.$$

(For $n = 1$ this is trivial since $SO(1) = \{1\}$, already rational.)

---

**Step 3 — Rational rotations are dense in $SO(2)$.**

A $2\times 2$ rotation is $R(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta
\\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}$. Rational points on the unit circle ar
e dense: the parametrization

$$c(t) = \frac{1 - t^2}{1 + t^2}, \qquad s(t) = \frac{2t}{1 + t^2}$$

satisfies $c(t)^2 + s(t)^2 = 1$ for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$, and $(c(t), s(t)) \t
o (\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ as $t \to \tan(\theta/2)$ (for $\theta \neq \pi$; fo
r $\theta = \pi$ the point $(-1, 0)$ is already rational). Since $\mathbb{Q}$ is
 dense in $\mathbb{R}$, choosing rational $t_k \to \tan(\theta/2)$ gives rationa
l $c(t_k), s(t_k)$ with $(c(t_k), s(t_k)) \to (\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$.

Therefore, for every Givens rotation $G_{ij}(\theta)$ and every $\delta > 0$, th
ere is a **rational orthogonal** Givens matrix $\widetilde G_{ij}$ (same embeddi
ng, rational $\cos/\sin$) with $\|G_{ij}(\theta) - \widetilde G_{ij}\| < \delta$
.

---

**Step 4 — Product approximation.**

Write $R = \prod_{k=1}^{m} G_k$ as in Step 2. For each $k$ choose a rational ort
hogonal $\widetilde G_k$ with $\|G_k - \widetilde G_k\| < \delta$, and set $R' :
= \prod_{k=1}^{m} \widetilde G_k$. Then $R'$ is rational orthogonal (a product o
f rational matrices) and lies in $SO(n)$.

All factors $G_k, \widetilde G_k$ are orthogonal, hence have operator norm $1$.
By the telescoping inequality (insert one difference at a time, using submultipl
icativity and $\|G_k\| = \|\widetilde G_k\| = 1$),

$$\|R - R'\| = \left\|\prod_{k=1}^{m} G_k - \prod_{k=1}^{m} \widetilde G_k\right
\| \le \sum_{k=1}^{m} \|G_k - \widetilde G_k\| < m\,\delta.$$

Choosing $\delta = \varepsilon / m$ yields $\|R - R'\| < \varepsilon$.

---

**Conclusion.**

By Step 1, $B = R' D$ is a rational orthogonal matrix with $\|A - B\| = \|R - R'
\| < \varepsilon$. Since $A \in O(n)$ and $\varepsilon > 0$ were arbitrary, **ra
tional orthogonal matrices are dense in $O(n)$**, and the answer to the question
 is

$$\boxed{\text{Yes.}}$$
