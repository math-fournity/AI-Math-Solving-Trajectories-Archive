## Proof

**Yes, such a group exists.** Let $G = S_5$, $F = \operatorname{Stab}_{S_5}(5) \
cong S_4$, and $H = \langle (1\;2\;3\;4\;5)\rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}$
.

Here "injection $i:F\to H$" means an injective group homomorphism (embedding), a
s is standard when mapping between algebraic structures.

---

**Condition 1: No embedding $F\hookrightarrow H$ or $H\hookrightarrow F$.**

$F\cong S_4$ is non-abelian of order $24$; $H\cong\mathbb{Z}/5$ is abelian of or
der $5$.

- An embedding $F\to H$ would place a non-abelian group inside an abelian one —
impossible.
- An embedding $H\to F$ would require $|H|=5$ to divide $|F|=24$ (Lagrange), but
 $5\nmid 24$ — impossible.

So neither embeds into the other. ✓

---

**Condition 2: $N_G(H)/H$ and $N_G(F)/F$ are both abelian.**

*$Normalizer of $F$:* $F=\operatorname{Stab}(5)$ acts transitively on $\{1,2,3,4
\}$, so the unique point fixed by all of $F$ is $5$. Any $\sigma\in N_G(F)$ perm
utes the set of common fixed points of $F$, hence $\sigma(5)=5$, so $\sigma\in F
$. Thus $N_G(F)=F$ and
$$N_G(F)/F \cong \{e\}, \quad\text{abelian.}\;\checkmark$$

*Normalizer of $H$:* $H$ is a Sylow $5$-subgroup of $S_5$. A standard computatio
n gives $N_{S_5}(H)=\operatorname{AGL}(1,5)\cong \mathbb{Z}/5\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/4
$, of order $20$. Concretely, $\sigma$ normalizes $H$ iff $\sigma(1\;2\;3\;4\;5)
\sigma^{-1}=(1\;2\;3\;4\;5)^k$ for some $k\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}$, i.e., $\sigma$ is a
n affine map $x\mapsto ax+b$ on $\mathbb{F}_5$. Therefore
$$N_G(H)/H \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}, \quad\text{abelian.}\;\checkmark$$

---

**Condition 3: $N_G(F\cap H)/(F\cap H)$ is not abelian.**

Every non-identity element of $H=\langle(1\;2\;3\;4\;5)\rangle$ is a $5$-cycle,
hence moves the point $5$. Every element of $F=\operatorname{Stab}(5)$ fixes $5$
. Therefore
$$F\cap H = \{e\}.$$
Consequently
$$N_G(F\cap H)/(F\cap H) = N_{S_5}(\{e\})/\{e\} = S_5,$$
which is **non-abelian** (e.g., $(1\;2)(1\;3)\neq(1\;3)(1\;2)$). ✓

---

All three conditions are satisfied by $G=S_5$, $F\cong S_4$, $H\cong\mathbb{Z}/5
$, so such a group **does exist**.
