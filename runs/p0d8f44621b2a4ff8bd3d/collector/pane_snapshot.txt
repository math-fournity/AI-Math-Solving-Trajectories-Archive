Proof

Claim: A Riemannian manifold with constant scalar curvature also has constant
sectional curvature if and only if the dimension is $n = 2$.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Dimension 2: Constant scalar curvature ⟹ constant sectional curvature

In dimension $n = 2$, the Riemann curvature tensor has a single independent
component. At each point, the curvature tensor takes the form

$$R_{ijkl} = K,(g_{ik},g_{jl} - g_{il},g_{jk}),$$

where $K$ is the Gaussian curvature — which is also the unique sectional
curvature (there is only one 2-plane in each tangent space up to the action on
the unit tangent bundle, and all sectional curvatures at a point coincide). The
scalar curvature is

$$R = g^{ik}g^{jl}R_{ijkl} = 2K.$$

Therefore $R \equiv \text{const}$ implies $K = R/2 \equiv \text{const}$. Since
$K$ is the sectional curvature at every point, the manifold has constant
sectional curvature. ∎

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Dimensions $n \geq 3$: Constant scalar curvature does not imply constant
sectional curvature

It suffices to give a counterexample valid in every dimension $n \geq 3$.

Consider the product manifold

$$M = S^2(r) \times \mathbb{R}^{,n-2}$$

with the product metric, where $S^2(r)$ is the round sphere of radius $r$.

Scalar curvature. Scalar curvature is additive under Riemannian products:

$$R_M = R_{S^2(r)} + R_{\mathbb{R}^{n-2}} = \frac{2}{r^2} + 0 = \frac{2}{r^2},$$

which is a constant.

Sectional curvature. Take two types of 2-planes at any point:

  • A 2-plane tangent to the $S^2(r)$ factor has sectional curvature $1/r^2$.
  • A 2-plane spanned by one direction in $S^2(r)$ and one direction in $
  \mathbb{R}^{n-2}$ has sectional curvature $0$ (mixed planes of a product have
  zero sectional curvature).

Thus the sectional curvature takes two distinct values ($1/r^2$ and $0$), so it
is not constant, even though the scalar curvature is constant.

This counterexample works for every $n \geq 3$ (for $n = 3$ one may use
$S^2(r)\times S^1$ with the product metric, giving the same conclusion). Hence
in no dimension $n \geq 3$ does constant scalar curvature force constant
sectional curvature.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Conclusion

The only dimension in which constant scalar curvature guarantees constant
sectional curvature is

$$\boxed{n = 2}.$$

In dimension 2 the curvature tensor is determined by a single function (the
Gaussian/sectional curvature $K$), and the scalar curvature is simply $R = 2K$,
so constancy of $R$ is equivalent to constancy of $K$. In all dimensions $n \geq
3$, the scalar curvature (the trace of the Ricci tensor) does not determine the
full curvature tensor, and explicit product examples show that constant scalar
curvature is compatible with non-constant sectional curvature.
