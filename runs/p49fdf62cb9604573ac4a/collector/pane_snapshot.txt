## Answer: No.

**Claim.** Weak convergence to $0$ (i.e. $f(x_n)\to 0$ for every $f\in X^*$) doe
s **not** imply norm convergence $x_n\to 0$ in general.

**Counterexample.** Let $X=\ell^2$, the Banach (Hilbert) space of square-summabl
e sequences, and let $x_n=e_n=(0,\dots,0,1,0,\dots)$ be the $n$-th standard unit
 vector.

*Norm.* We have $\|e_n\|_2=1$ for every $n$, so $x_n$ does **not** converge to $
0$ in norm.

*Weak convergence.* By the Riesz representation theorem, every continuous linear
 functional $f\in(\ell^2)^*$ is of the form $f(x)=\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^
\infty x_k\overline{y_k}$ for some unique $y=(y_k)\in\ell^2$. Hence

$$f(e_n)=\langle e_n,y\rangle=\overline{y_n}.$$

Since $y\in\ell^2$, we have $\sum_k|y_k|^2<\infty$, which forces $|y_k|^2\to 0$,
 i.e. $y_n\to 0$. Therefore $f(e_n)=\overline{y_n}\to 0$ for every $f\in(\ell^2)
^*$.

Thus $\{e_n\}$ satisfies $f(x_n)\to 0$ for all $f\in X^*$, yet $\|x_n\|=1\not\to
 0$.

**Conclusion.** The implication "$f(x_n)\to 0\ \forall f\in X^* \implies x_n\to
0$ in norm" is **false** in general. (It holds only under additional hypotheses,
 e.g. when $\{x_n\}$ is known to converge in norm to *some* limit, or in finite-
dimensional spaces where weak and norm topologies coincide.)
