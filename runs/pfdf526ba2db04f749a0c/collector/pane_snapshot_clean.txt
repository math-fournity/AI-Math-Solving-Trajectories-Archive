## Proof

Let $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ be distinct positive integers, and let $b_1 < b_2 < \
dots < b_n$ be the same $n$ values arranged in strictly increasing order. Since
the $a_k$ are distinct positive integers, the sorted sequence satisfies

$$
b_k \ge k \qquad (k = 1, 2, \dots, n),
$$

because the $k$-th smallest distinct positive integer is at least $k$.

**Step 1: Reduction to the sorted order (Rearrangement Inequality).**

The weights $w_k = \dfrac{1}{k^2}$ are strictly decreasing in $k$:
$$
w_1 > w_2 > \dots > w_n \ge 0.
$$

The sum $S = \sum_{k=1}^n a_k\, w_k$ is a sum of products of the multiset $\{a_1
,\dots,a_n\}$ with the multiset $\{w_1,\dots,w_n\}$, paired according to the giv
en order of the $a_k$. By the Rearrangement Inequality, among all pairings of th
e two multisets, the sum $\sum a_k w_k$ is **minimized** when the two sequences
are ordered oppositely — i.e., when the smallest $a$ is paired with the largest
$w$, the second smallest $a$ with the second largest $w$, and so on. Concretely,
 since $w_1 > w_2 > \dots > w_n$, the minimum is attained when $a_1 \le a_2 \le
\dots \le a_n$, giving

$$
\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{a_k}{k^2} \;\ge\; \sum_{k=1}^n \frac{b_k}{k^2}.
$$

**Step 2: Lower bound for the sorted sum.**

Using $b_k \ge k$ for every $k$,

$$
\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{b_k}{k^2} \;\ge\; \sum_{k=1}^n \frac{k}{k^2} \;=\; \sum_{k=1}
^n \frac{1}{k}.
$$

**Step 3: Combine.**

Chaining the two inequalities,

$$
\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{a_k}{k^2} \;\ge\; \sum_{k=1}^n \frac{b_k}{k^2} \;\ge\; \sum_{
k=1}^n \frac{1}{k}.
$$

**Equality case.** Equality holds throughout iff (i) the $a_k$ are already in in
creasing order (so the rearrangement step is tight) and (ii) $b_k = k$ for all $
k$, i.e. $\{a_1,\dots,a_n\} = \{1,2,\dots,n\}$ in increasing order, meaning $a_k
 = k$. One readily checks that then $\sum a_k/k^2 = \sum k/k^2 = \sum 1/k$, conf
irming sharpness.

Therefore, for every $n$ and every choice of distinct positive integers $a_1, \d
ots, a_n$,

$$
\boxed{\;\sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{a_k}{k^2} \;\ge\; \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{1}{k}\;}
$$

as was to be proved. $\blacksquare$
