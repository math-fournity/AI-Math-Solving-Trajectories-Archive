## Proof

**Yes.** Given an isomorphism $W \cong U \otimes V$, one can transport the canon
ical bilinear map of the tensor product along the isomorphism to obtain a biline
ar map $B': U \times V \to W$ satisfying the universal property.

**Setup.** Let $\otimes: U \times V \to U \otimes V$ denote the canonical biline
ar map of the tensor product, which satisfies the universal property: for every
bilinear map $f: U \times V \to Z$ (into any vector space $Z$), there exists a u
nique linear map $\bar{f}_0: U \otimes V \to Z$ such that
$$f = \bar{f}_0 \circ \otimes.$$

Since $W \cong U \otimes V$, fix any (linear) isomorphism $\phi: U \otimes V \xr
ightarrow{\;\sim\;} W$.

**Construction of $B'$.** Define
$$B' := \phi \circ \otimes : U \times V \to W, \qquad B'(u,v) = \phi(u \otimes v
).$$

**$B'$ is bilinear.** The map $\otimes$ is bilinear and $\phi$ is linear, so the
ir composite $B'$ is bilinear.

**$B'$ has the universal property.** Let $f: U \times V \to Z$ be any bilinear m
ap. By the universal property of $\otimes$, there is a unique linear $\bar{f}_0:
 U \otimes V \to Z$ with $f = \bar{f}_0 \circ \otimes$. Set
$$\bar{f} := \bar{f}_0 \circ \phi^{-1} : W \to Z.$$
This is linear (composite of linear maps). Then
$$\bar{f} \circ B' = \bar{f}_0 \circ \phi^{-1} \circ \phi \circ \otimes = \bar{f
}_0 \circ \otimes = f.$$

**Uniqueness.** Suppose $\bar{g}: W \to Z$ is any linear map with $\bar{g} \circ
 B' = f$. Then $\bar{g} \circ \phi \circ \otimes = f$, so $\bar{g} \circ \phi$ i
s a linear map $U \otimes V \to Z$ factorizing $f$ through $\otimes$. By the uni
queness part of the universal property of $\otimes$, we must have $\bar{g} \circ
 \phi = \bar{f}_0$. Composing on the right with $\phi^{-1}$ gives $\bar{g} = \ba
r{f}_0 \circ \phi^{-1} = \bar{f}$. Hence $\bar{f}$ is unique.

**Conclusion.** For any isomorphism $\phi: U \otimes V \to W$, the bilinear map
$B' = \phi \circ \otimes$ satisfies the universal property of the tensor product
. Therefore, whenever $W \cong U \otimes V$, such a bilinear map $B'$ necessaril
y exists. $\blacksquare$
