# Answer: No

Convergence in distribution plus uniform integrability of $\{X_n^2\}$ does **not
** imply $L^2$ convergence.

## Counterexample

Let $X \sim N(0,1)$ on a probability space, and set $X_n = -X$ for every $n$.

**Convergence in distribution.** Since $X$ is symmetric ($N(0,1)$), the random v
ariable $-X$ has the same distribution as $X$. Hence $X_n \stackrel{d}{=} X$ for
 all $n$, and trivially $X_n \xrightarrow{d} X$.

**Uniform integrability of $\{X_n^2\}$.** We have $X_n^2 = X^2$ for all $n$, so
$\{X_n^2\}$ is a singleton family $\{X^2\}$. A single integrable random variable
 is uniformly integrable (since $E[X^2 \mathbf{1}_{X^2 > M}] \to 0$ as $M \to \i
nfty$ by dominated convergence, and $\sup_n E[X_n^2] = E[X^2] = 1 < \infty$). ✓

**Failure of $L^2$ convergence.**
$$E\bigl[|X_n - X|^2\bigr] = E\bigl[|-X - X|^2\bigr] = E\bigl[4X^2\bigr] = 4 \qu
ad \text{for all } n.$$
This does not tend to $0$, so $X_n \not\to X$ in $L^2$.

## Why this happens

Convergence in distribution controls only the **marginal** laws of $X_n$ and $X$
, not their **joint** coupling. $L^2$ convergence depends on the joint law throu
gh $E[|X_n-X|^2]$. Uniform integrability of $\{X_n^2\}$ guarantees $E[X_n^2]\to
E[X^2]$ (by the standard theorem: convergence in distribution + UI of $\{|X_n|^p
\}$ $\Rightarrow$ convergence of $p$-th moments), and via Skorokhod representati
on one can always *re-couple* the variables to obtain $L^2$ convergence on a dif
ferent space. But on the original probability space, with an adversarial couplin
g (here $X_n = -X$), $L^2$ convergence can fail.

The correct sufficient condition for $L^p$ convergence is **convergence in proba
bility** (not merely in distribution) together with uniform integrability of $\{
|X_n|^p\}$.
