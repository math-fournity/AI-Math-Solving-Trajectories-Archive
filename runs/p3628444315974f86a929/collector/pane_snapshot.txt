Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the equation $F(X) = X^{\ci
rc n} - \sum_{i=0}^n C_i \circ X^{\circ i} = 0$ always has exactly one symmetric
 positive definite (SPD) solution, given SPD matrices $C_i$.

**Key observation:** Since the Schur product is component-wise, the matrix equat
ion decouples into independent scalar polynomial equations — one per entry $(j,k
)$:
$$x_{jk}^n - \sum_{i=0}^n c^{(i)}_{jk}\, x_{jk}^i = 0.$$

The SPD constraint on $X$ is global, but the equation itself does not couple ent
ries. This means off-diagonal entries can have multiple real roots, and the SPD
constraint may not select a unique one.

---

## Counterexample ($n=2$, $2\times 2$)

Set
$$C_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -\tfrac{1}{10} \\[2pt] -\tfrac{1}{10} & 1 \end{pmatr
ix},\quad C_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix},\quad C_2 = \begin{
pmatrix} \tfrac{1}{2} & 0 \\ 0 & \tfrac{1}{2} \end{pmatrix}.$$

**All are SPD:** $C_0$ has eigenvalues $\tfrac{9}{10},\tfrac{11}{10}$; $C_1$ has
 eigenvalues $1,3$; $C_2$ has eigenvalue $\tfrac{1}{2}$ (double).

The equation $X^{\circ 2} - C_0 - C_1\circ X - C_2\circ X^{\circ 2}=0$ becomes $
(J-C_2)\circ X^{\circ 2} - C_1\circ X - C_0 = 0$, where $J$ is the all-ones matr
ix. Writing $X=\begin{pmatrix} a&b\\ b&d\end{pmatrix}$, the entry-wise equations
 are:

**Diagonal $(1,1)$ and $(2,2)$:** $\;\tfrac{1}{2}\,t^2 - 2t - 1 = 0 \;\Longright
arrow\; t = 2\pm\sqrt{6}$. The positive root is $a = d = 2+\sqrt{6}$ (unique pos
itive root by Descartes' rule: one sign change).

**Off-diagonal $(1,2)$:** $\;b^2 - b + \tfrac{1}{10} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; b =
 \dfrac{1\pm\sqrt{3/5}}{2}$.

Both roots are real, positive, and distinct:
$$b_1 = \frac{1+\sqrt{3/5}}{2} \approx 0.887, \qquad b_2 = \frac{1-\sqrt{3/5}}{2
} \approx 0.113.$$

**Both yield SPD matrices.** Since $a=d=2+\sqrt6\approx 4.449$ and $b_1, b_2 < 1
 < a$, each matrix
$$X_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 2+\sqrt6 & b_1 \\ b_1 & 2+\sqrt6 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad X
_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 2+\sqrt6 & b_2 \\ b_2 & 2+\sqrt6 \end{pmatrix}$$
has positive diagonal entries and positive determinant:
$$\det X_k = (2+\sqrt6)^2 - b_k^2 > (2+\sqrt6)^2 - 1 = 10 + 4\sqrt6 - 1 > 0.$$

Both $X_1$ and $X_2$ are SPD, $X_1 \neq X_2$, and both satisfy $F(X)=0$.

---

## Conclusion

There exist symmetric positive definite matrices $C_0, C_1, C_2$ for which the e
quation $F(X)=0$ has **two distinct** symmetric positive definite solutions. The
refore, it is **not true** that there is exactly one symmetric positive definite
 matrix $X$ satisfying the equation.
