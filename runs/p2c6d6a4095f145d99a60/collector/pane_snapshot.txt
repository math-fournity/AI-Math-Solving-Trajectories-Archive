## Proof

**Claim:** A non-compact self-adjoint bounded linear operator $T: H \to H$ on an
 infinite-dimensional Hilbert space $H$ **cannot** have a discrete spectrum.

**Definitions.** The *discrete spectrum* $\sigma_{\mathrm{disc}}(T)$ of a self-a
djoint operator is the set of isolated eigenvalues of finite (geometric) multipl
icity. To say "$T$ has a discrete spectrum" means $\sigma(T) = \sigma_{\mathrm{d
isc}}(T)$, i.e., every spectral value is an isolated eigenvalue of finite multip
licity, and the essential spectrum $\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(T) = \sigma(T)\setminu
s\sigma_{\mathrm{disc}}(T)$ is empty.

**Argument.** Suppose for contradiction that $T$ has a discrete spectrum.

**Step 1: The spectrum is finite.** Since $T$ is bounded, $\sigma(T)$ is a compa
ct subset of $\mathbb{R}$. By the discrete spectrum assumption, every point of $
\sigma(T)$ is isolated. A compact subset of $\mathbb{R}$ in which every point is
 isolated must be finite (otherwise, an infinite compact set would have an accum
ulation point by the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem, contradicting isolation). Henc
e
$$\sigma(T) = \{\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \ldots, \lambda_n\}$$
for some finite $n$, where each $\lambda_i$ is an eigenvalue of finite multiplic
ity.

**Step 2: Apply the spectral theorem.** By the spectral theorem for bounded self
-adjoint operators (valid over real Hilbert spaces by complexification), since t
he spectrum is finite,
$$T = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \lambda_i\, P_i,$$
where $P_i$ is the orthogonal projection onto the eigenspace $E_i = \ker(T - \la
mbda_i I)$. Each $E_i$ is finite-dimensional by the finite-multiplicity assumpti
on.

**Step 3: $T$ has finite rank.** The range of $T$ satisfies
$$\operatorname{ran}(T) \subseteq \bigoplus_{i=1}^{n} E_i.$$
Since each $E_i$ is finite-dimensional, the direct sum $\bigoplus_{i=1}^n E_i$ i
s finite-dimensional. Therefore $T$ has finite rank.

**Step 4: Contradiction.** Every finite-rank operator is compact. (Indeed, the i
mage of the unit ball under a finite-rank operator is a bounded subset of a fini
te-dimensional space, hence precompact.) This contradicts the hypothesis that $T
$ is **not** compact.

**Remark.** The argument shows something stronger: *no* bounded self-adjoint ope
rator on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space can have a purely discrete spectr
um. Even a compact self-adjoint operator has $0 \in \sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(T)$ (s
ince $0$ is an accumulation point of eigenvalues, or an eigenvalue of infinite m
ultiplicity), so its spectrum is not purely discrete either. The essential spect
rum of any bounded self-adjoint operator on infinite-dimensional $H$ is non-empt
y.

**Conclusion.** A non-compact self-adjoint bounded linear operator on an infinit
e-dimensional real separable Hilbert space **cannot** have a discrete spectrum.
$\blacksquare$
