**Yes, $f_*\omega_{C/S}$ is locally free.**

**Step 1: The relative dualizing sheaf is invertible.**

The geometric fibers of $f$ are stable curves in the sense of Deligne–Mumford, h
ence nodal curves: their only singularities are ordinary double points (nodes).
A node is locally of the form $\operatorname{Spec} k[[x,y]]/(xy)$, a hypersurfac

geometric fiber is a Gorenstein curve. As $f$ is flat (given) with Gorenstein fi
bers, $f$ is a Gorenstein morphism. For a Gorenstein morphism, the relative dual
izing sheaf $\omega_{C/S}$ is an invertible sheaf on $C$.

**Step 2: $\omega_{C/S}$ is flat over $S$.**

Since $\omega_{C/S}$ is invertible on $C$, it is locally free, hence flat over $
C$. Since $f$ is flat, $C$ is flat over $S$. By transitivity of flatness, $\omeg
a_{C/S}$ is flat over $S$.

**Step 3: The function $s \mapsto h^0(C_s, \omega_{C_s})$ is constant on $S$.**

For a flat proper family, the Euler characteristic $\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C_s})$ is
locally constant on the base. Since $S$ is connected, the arithmetic genus $g :=
 1 - \chi(\mathcal{O}_{C_s})$ is constant on $S$.

The geometric fibers $C_{\bar{s}}$ are stable curves, hence connected and reduce
d. For a connected, reduced, proper curve over an algebraically closed field, $h
^0(C_{\bar{s}}, \mathcal{O}_{C_{\bar{s}}}) = 1$.

Since the fibers are Gorenstein, Serre duality holds on each fiber with dualizin

$$h^0(C_{\bar{s}}, \omega_{C_{\bar{s}}}) = h^1(C_{\bar{s}}, \mathcal{O}_{C_{\bar
{s}}}).$$

From $\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C_{\bar{s}}}) = h^0 - h^1 = 1 - g$ and $h^0 = 1$, we obt
