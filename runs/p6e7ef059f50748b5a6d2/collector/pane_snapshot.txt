## Proof

**Setup and notation.** Let $(M,g)$ be a Riemannian manifold with Levi-Civita co
nnection $\nabla$. We use the musical isomorphism: a vector field $v$ correspond
s to the 1-form $\omega = v^\flat$, with $g(v, X) = \omega(X)$. The codifferenti
al on 1-forms satisfies $\delta\omega = -\operatorname{div} v$. The Hodge Laplac
ian is $\Delta = d\delta + \delta d$, and the connection (rough) Laplacian is $\
nabla^*\nabla = -\operatorname{Tr}(\nabla^2)$. We work at a point $p$ in a norma
l orthonormal frame $\{e_i\}$ (so $\nabla_{e_i}e_j|_p = 0$).

**Goal.** Show that substituting the given identity into the computation of $\de
lta d\omega$ yields the Weitzenböck formula
$$\Delta\omega = \nabla^*\nabla\,\omega - \operatorname{Ric}(\cdot, v),$$
which (up to the curvature sign convention) is Proposition 28.2: $\Delta\omega =
 \nabla^*\nabla\omega + \operatorname{Ric}(\omega)$.

---

**Step 1. Compute $\delta d\omega(u)$.**

For a 2-form $\alpha$, $(\delta\alpha)(u) = -\sum_i (\nabla_{e_i}\alpha)(e_i, u)
$. With $\alpha = d\omega$ and the normal frame:
$$(\delta d\omega)(u) = -\sum_i e_i\big((d\omega)(e_i,u)\big) + \sum_i (d\omega)
(e_i, \nabla_{e_i}u).$$

Using $(d\omega)(e_i,u) = g(\nabla_{e_i}v, u) - g(\nabla_u v, e_i)$:
$$e_i\big((d\omega)(e_i,u)\big) = g(\nabla_{e_i}\nabla_{e_i}v, u) + g(\nabla_{e_
i}v, \nabla_{e_i}u) - g(\nabla_{e_i}\nabla_u v, e_i),$$
so summing over $i$:
$$\sum_i e_i\big((d\omega)(e_i,u)\big) = g\!\Big(\sum_i \nabla_{e_i}\nabla_{e_i}
v,\, u\Big) + \sum_i g(\nabla_{e_i}v, \nabla_{e_i}u) - \operatorname{div}(\nabla
_u v).$$

Also
$$\sum_i (d\omega)(e_i, \nabla_{e_i}u) = \sum_i g(\nabla_{e_i}v, \nabla_{e_i}u)
- \sum_i g(\nabla_{\nabla_{e_i}u}v, e_i) = \sum_i g(\nabla_{e_i}v, \nabla_{e_i}u
) - \operatorname{Tr}\big((\nabla u)(\nabla v)\big).$$

Combining (the $\sum_i g(\nabla_{e_i}v,\nabla_{e_i}u)$ terms **cancel**):
$$(\delta d\omega)(u) = -g\!\Big(\sum_i \nabla_{e_i}\nabla_{e_i}v,\, u\Big) + \o
peratorname{div}(\nabla_u v) - \operatorname{Tr}\big((\nabla u)(\nabla v)\big).$
$

**Step 2. Substitute the given identity.**

The given formula $\operatorname{div}(\nabla_u v) = \nabla_u(\operatorname{div}
v) + \operatorname{Tr}((\nabla u)(\nabla v)) - \operatorname{Ric}(u,v)$ gives:
$$(\delta d\omega)(u) = -g\!\Big(\sum_i \nabla_{e_i}\nabla_{e_i}v,\, u\Big) + \n
abla_u(\operatorname{div} v) + \underbrace{\operatorname{Tr}((\nabla u)(\nabla v
)) - \operatorname{Tr}((\nabla u)(\nabla v))}_{=\,0} - \operatorname{Ric}(u,v).$
$

The trace terms **cancel exactly** — this is the crux of the argument — leaving:
$$(\delta d\omega)(u) = -g\!\Big(\sum_i \nabla_{e_i}\nabla_{e_i}v,\, u\Big) + \n
abla_u(\operatorname{div} v) - \operatorname{Ric}(u,v).$$

**Step 3. Identify each term.**

- **Rough Laplacian.** A direct computation (using metric-compatibility) shows $
(\nabla_{e_i}\omega)(u) = g(\nabla_{e_i}v, u)$ and $(\nabla_{e_i}\nabla_{e_i}\om
ega)(u) = g(\nabla_{e_i}\nabla_{e_i}v, u)$, hence
$$(\nabla^*\nabla\omega)(u) = -\sum_i (\nabla_{e_i}\nabla_{e_i}\omega)(u) = -g\!
\Big(\sum_i \nabla_{e_i}\nabla_{e_i}v,\, u\Big).$$

- **$d\delta$ term.** Since $\delta\omega = -\operatorname{div} v$,
$$(d\delta\omega)(u) = u(\delta\omega) = -\nabla_u(\operatorname{div} v), \quad\
text{i.e.}\quad \nabla_u(\operatorname{div} v) = -(d\delta\omega)(u).$$

Substituting:
$$(\delta d\omega)(u) = (\nabla^*\nabla\omega)(u) - (d\delta\omega)(u) - \operat
orname{Ric}(u,v).$$

**Step 4. Rearrange to the Weitzenböck formula.**

Moving $d\delta\omega$ to the left:
$$(\delta d\omega + d\delta\omega)(u) = (\nabla^*\nabla\omega)(u) - \operatornam
e{Ric}(u,v),$$
i.e.,
$$\boxed{\;\Delta\omega = \nabla^*\nabla\omega - \operatorname{Ric}(\cdot,\, v)\
;}$$
where $\operatorname{Ric}(\cdot, v)$ denotes the 1-form $u \mapsto \operatorname
{Ric}(u, v)$.

---

**Interpretation.** The given identity is the commutator $[\operatorname{div}, \
nabla_u]v = \operatorname{Tr}((\nabla u)(\nabla v)) - \operatorname{Ric}(u,v)$.
It records exactly how divergence fails to commute with covariant differentiatio
n — the failure being a first-order term $\operatorname{Tr}((\nabla u)(\nabla v)
)$ plus a zeroth-order curvature term $-\operatorname{Ric}(u,v)$.

When one computes the Hodge Laplacian $\Delta = \delta d + d\delta$ on a 1-form,
 the $\delta d$ piece naturally produces both a rough-Laplacian part $\nabla^*\n
abla\omega$ and a "cross term" $\operatorname{Tr}((\nabla u)(\nabla v))$ coming
from differentiating the coefficients of $d\omega$. Substituting the divergence
identity causes this cross term to cancel against the identical trace term appea
ring in $\operatorname{div}(\nabla_u v)$, and what survives is precisely the Ric
ci curvature as the zeroth-order correction. Thus:

> The Weitzenböck formula $\Delta\omega = \nabla^*\nabla\omega \pm \operatorname
{Ric}(\omega)$ is nothing but the divergence-commutator identity, reorganized: t
he cancellation of the first-order trace terms isolates the Ricci curvature as t
he sole difference between the Hodge (topological) Laplacian and the connection
(analytic) Laplacian on 1-forms.

This is Proposition 28.2 (with the sign of $\operatorname{Ric}$ determined by th
e curvature convention in the given identity; under the convention $\operatornam
e{Ric}(X,Y) = \sum_i g(R(X,e_i)e_i, Y)$ with the opposite sign of $R$, the formu
la reads $\Delta\omega = \nabla^*\nabla\omega + \operatorname{Ric}(\omega)$).
