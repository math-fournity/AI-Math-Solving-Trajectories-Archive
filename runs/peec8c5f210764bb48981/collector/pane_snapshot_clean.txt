## Proof

**Yes.** In fact, *every* subspace (closed or not) of a separable Hilbert space
admits a countable orthonormal basis as an inner product space. Here is the just
ification.

Let $H$ be a separable Hilbert space and let $M \subseteq H$ be a linear subspac
e, not assumed closed.

**Step 1 — $M$ is separable and has a countable dense orthonormal set.**
As a subset of the separable metric space $H$, the subspace $M$ (with the induce
d metric) is separable. Let $\{x_n\}_{n\ge 1}$ be a countable dense subset of $M
$. Applying the Gram–Schmidt process to $\{x_n\}$ — discarding zero vectors and
linearly dependent ones — produces a countable (or finite) orthonormal set $\{e_
k\}$ contained in $M$ whose linear span is dense in $M$.

**Step 2 — $\{e_k\}$ is an orthonormal basis of the inner product space $M$.**
We show that every $v \in M$ satisfies
$$v = \sum_{k} \langle v, e_k\rangle\, e_k,$$
with convergence in the norm of $M$ (equivalently, of $H$).

By Bessel's inequality, $\sum_k |\langle v, e_k\rangle|^2 \le \|v\|^2 < \infty$,
 so the partial sums $s_N = \sum_{k=1}^N \langle v, e_k\rangle e_k$ form a Cauch
y sequence. Let $\overline{M}$ (the closure of $M$ in $H$) be the completion of
$M$; then $s_N$ converges in $\overline{M}$ to some $w$. For each fixed $j$ and
all $N \ge j$,
$$\langle v - s_N,\, e_j\rangle = 0.$$
Passing to the limit gives $\langle w, e_j\rangle = \langle v, e_j\rangle$, henc
e $w - v \perp e_j$ for every $j$. Since $\operatorname{span}\{e_k\}$ is dense i
n $M$ and therefore in $\overline{M}$, we get $w - v \perp \overline{M}$, so $w
= v$. Thus $s_N \to v$ in norm. Because $v \in M$, the series converges *inside*
 $M$. Hence $\{e_k\}$ is an orthonormal basis of $M$ in the strong (Fourier-expa
nsion) sense. It is also maximal orthonormal: any unit vector orthogonal to all
$e_k$ would be orthogonal to the dense span, hence to $M$, hence zero — a contra
diction.

**Step 3 — $M$ need not be a Hilbert space.**
Take the concrete example $H = \ell^2(\mathbb N)$ and $M = c_{00}$, the subspace
 of finitely supported sequences. Then:
- $M$ is a linear subspace of the separable Hilbert space $\ell^2$.
- $M$ is **not closed**: its closure in $\ell^2$ is all of $\ell^2 \neq c_{00}$.
 Consequently $M$ is not complete, hence not a Hilbert space.
- The standard unit vectors $\{e_n\}_{n\ge 1}$ form a **countable orthonormal ba
sis** of $c_{00}$: every finitely supported sequence is a *finite* linear combin
ation of the $e_n$, and $\{e_n\}$ is maximal orthonormal since a finitely suppor
ted vector orthogonal to every $e_n$ must be the zero vector.

**Conclusion.** A non-closed subspace of a separable Hilbert space can indeed po
ssess a countable orthonormal basis while failing to be a Hilbert space. The exa
mple $c_{00} \subset \ell^2$ exhibits this, and the general argument shows that
*every* subspace of a separable Hilbert space — closed or not — carries a counta
ble orthonormal basis as an inner product space.
