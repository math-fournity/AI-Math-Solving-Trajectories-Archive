## Proof

Let $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ be a quadratic field with (fundamental) discrimin
ant $D$, and let $G = (\mathbb{Z}/D\mathbb{Z})^*$. Let $\chi_D = \left(\frac{D}{
\cdot}\right)$ denote the Kronecker symbol, which is the primitive quadratic Dir
ichlet character modulo $|D|$ associated to $K/\mathbb{Q}$.

**Claim:** $H^* = \ker(\chi_D)$, and therefore $(G : H^*) = 2$.

**Step 1: $H^* \subseteq \ker(\chi_D)$.**

Let $\mathfrak{a}$ be an integral ideal of $\mathcal{O}_K$ with $\gcd(N(\mathfra
k{a}), D) = 1$. We show $\chi_D(N(\mathfrak{a})) = 1$.

The ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ factors into prime ideals $\mathfrak{a} = \prod \mathfr
ak{p}_i^{e_i}$, and $N(\mathfrak{a}) = \prod N(\mathfrak{p}_i)^{e_i}$. For each
prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}_i$ with $N(\mathfrak{p}_i) = p \nmid D$:

- If $p$ **splits** in $K$: $p\mathcal{O}_K = \mathfrak{p}\bar{\mathfrak{p}}$, $
N(\mathfrak{p}) = p$, and $\chi_D(p) = \left(\frac{D}{p}\right) = +1$ (by defini
tion of splitting).
- If $p$ is **inert** in $K$: $p\mathcal{O}_K$ is prime with $N(p\mathcal{O}_K)
= p^2$, and $\chi_D(p^2) = \chi_D(p)^2 = 1$.

Since $\chi_D$ is completely multiplicative and $N(\mathfrak{a})$ is a product o
f such norms, we get $\chi_D(N(\mathfrak{a})) = 1$. Hence every norm residue lie
s in $\ker(\chi_D)$, so $H^* \subseteq \ker(\chi_D)$.

**Step 2: $\ker(\chi_D) \subseteq H^*$.**

Let $a \in \ker(\chi_D)$, so $a \in G$ and $\chi_D(a) = \left(\frac{D}{a}\right)
 = 1$. By Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions, there exists
 a rational prime $p$ with $p \equiv a \pmod{D}$ and $\gcd(p, D) = 1$.

Then $\chi_D(p) = \chi_D(a) = 1$, which means $\left(\frac{D}{p}\right) = 1$, so
 $p$ **splits** in $K$: $p\mathcal{O}_K = \mathfrak{p}\bar{\mathfrak{p}}$ with $
N(\mathfrak{p}) = p$.

Since $N(\mathfrak{p}) = p \equiv a \pmod{D}$, the residue class $a \bmod D$ con
tains the norm of the integral ideal $\mathfrak{p}$. Therefore $a \in H^*$.

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

From Steps 1 and 2, $H^* = \ker(\chi_D)$. The Kronecker symbol $\chi_D = \left(\
frac{D}{\cdot}\right)$ is a non-trivial quadratic character on $G$ (non-trivial
because $D \neq 1$ is a fundamental discriminant of a non-trivial quadratic exte
nsion). A non-trivial quadratic character is surjective onto $\{\pm 1\}$, so its
 kernel has index exactly 2.

Therefore:
$$\boxed{(G : H^*) = 2}$$
