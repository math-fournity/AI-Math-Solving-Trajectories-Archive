## Solution

We work in the Gaussian integers $\mathbb{Z}[i]$, which form a Euclidean domain
(hence a PID) with norm $N(a+bi) = a^2 + b^2$. In a PID, the ideal generated by
two elements is principal, generated by their gcd.

**Step 1: Compute norms.**

- $N(3+4i) = 9 + 16 = 25$
- $N(4+7i) = 16 + 49 = 65$

If $d = \gcd(3+4i,\, 4+7i)$, then $N(d) \mid \gcd(25, 65) = 5$. So $N(d) \in \{1
, 5\}$.

**Step 2: Test a candidate of norm 5.**

The elements of norm 5 in $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ are $\pm 1 \pm 2i$ and $\pm 2 \pm i$.
Test $d = 1 - 2i$:

$$\frac{3+4i}{1-2i} = \frac{(3+4i)(1+2i)}{5} = \frac{3 + 6i + 4i + 8i^2}{5} = \f
rac{-5 + 10i}{5} = -1 + 2i \in \mathbb{Z}[i]. \checkmark$$

$$\frac{4+7i}{1-2i} = \frac{(4+7i)(1+2i)}{5} = \frac{4 + 8i + 7i + 14i^2}{5} = \
frac{-10 + 15i}{5} = -2 + 3i \in \mathbb{Z}[i]. \checkmark$$

So $1 - 2i$ divides both $3+4i$ and $4+7i$.

**Step 3: Verify the divisions explicitly.**

$$(1-2i)(-1+2i) = -1 + 2i + 2i - 4i^2 = -1 + 4 + 4i = 3 + 4i. \checkmark$$

$$(1-2i)(-2+3i) = -2 + 3i + 4i - 6i^2 = -2 + 6 + 7i = 4 + 7i. \checkmark$$

**Step 4: Confirm it is the gcd (not a unit).**

Since $N(1-2i) = 5 > 1$, it is not a unit, and since $N(d) \mid 5$ with $d \neq$
 unit, we must have $N(d) = 5$. Thus $1-2i$ (up to multiplication by a unit $\{\
pm 1, \pm i\}$) is the gcd.

**Conclusion.** Since $1-2i$ divides both generators and both generators divide
any common divisor of norm 5, the ideal is principal:

$$(3+4i,\; 4+7i) = (1-2i).$$

The generator of the ideal is $\boxed{1-2i}$ (up to a unit).
