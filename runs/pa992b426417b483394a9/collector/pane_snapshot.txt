## Solution

We evaluate
$$I = \iiint_E \frac{dx\,dy\,dz}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2+(z-2)^2}}, \qquad E = \{(x,y,z):
x^2+y^2+z^2 \le 1\}.$$

**Interpretation.** The denominator is $|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{a}|$ with $\mathbf{r
}=(x,y,z)$ and $\mathbf{a}=(0,0,2)$. Thus $I$ is the Newtonian potential at the
point $\mathbf{a}$ (with $|\mathbf{a}|=d=2>1$, i.e. **outside** the unit ball) p
roduced by a body of unit density filling the unit ball.

**Spherical coordinates.** Center the coordinates at the origin (center of $E$)
and align the polar axis with $\mathbf{a}$. Write $x^2+y^2 = r^2\sin^2\theta$, $
z = r\cos\theta$, $d=|\mathbf{a}|=2$. Then
$$|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{a}| = \sqrt{r^2 + d^2 - 2rd\cos\theta}, \qquad dV = r^2\si
n\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\phi.$$

**Angular integration.** For fixed $r\in[0,1]$,
$$A(r) = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!d\phi \int_0^{\pi}\frac{\sin\theta\,d\theta}{\sqrt{r^2
+d^2-2rd\cos\theta}}.$$
Set $u=\cos\theta$, $du=-\sin\theta\,d\theta$:
$$A(r) = 2\pi\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{du}{\sqrt{r^2+d^2-2rdu}}.$$
Using $\displaystyle\int\frac{du}{\sqrt{\alpha-\beta u}} = -\tfrac{2}{\beta}\sqr
t{\alpha-\beta u}$ with $\alpha=r^2+d^2$, $\beta=2rd$:
$$A(r) = 2\pi\!\left[-\frac{1}{rd}\sqrt{r^2+d^2-2rdu}\right]_{u=-1}^{u=1} = -\fr
ac{2\pi}{rd}\Big(\sqrt{(r-d)^2}-\sqrt{(r+d)^2}\Big).$$
Since $d=2>1\ge r$, we have $|r-d|=d-r$ and $\sqrt{(r+d)^2}=r+d$, so
$$A(r) = -\frac{2\pi}{rd}\big[(d-r)-(r+d)\big] = -\frac{2\pi}{rd}(-2r) = \frac{4
\pi}{d}.$$
Note this is **independent of $r$** — a manifestation of the shell theorem.

**Radial integration.**
$$I = \int_0^1 r^2 A(r)\,dr = \int_0^1 r^2\cdot\frac{4\pi}{d}\,dr = \frac{4\pi}{
d}\cdot\frac{1}{3} = \frac{4\pi}{3d}.$$

**Substitute $d=2$:**
$$\boxed{I = \frac{4\pi}{3\cdot 2} = \frac{2\pi}{3}.}$$

**Sanity check.** This agrees with Newton's shell theorem: a uniform ball of rad
ius $R=1$ and unit density acts, at any exterior point distance $d$, like a poin
t mass $M=\tfrac{4\pi}{3}$ at the center, giving potential $M/d = \tfrac{4\pi}{3
d}$. With $d=2$ this is $\tfrac{2\pi}{3}$. ✓
