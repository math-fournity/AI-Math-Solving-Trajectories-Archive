eigenfunctions in $x$ are determined by the homogeneous boundary conditions at $

## Part (a): $u_x(0,y) = g_1(y)$, $u_x(L,y) = g_2(y)$, $u(x,0) = f(x)$

**Eigenfunctions:** Neumann–Neumann in $x$ gives $X'(0)=0$, $X'(L)=0$, yielding
$\cos(n\pi x/L)$, $n = 0,1,2,\ldots$, with $\lambda_n = (n\pi/L)^2$.

**General solution (bounded as $y\to -\infty$):**
$$u(x,y) = a_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n \cos\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) e^{
n\pi y/L}$$

(The $n=0$ mode gives $Y''=0 \Rightarrow Y = A + By$; boundedness forces $B=0$,
so $Y = a_0$.)

**Checking the side conditions:** Since $\frac{d}{dx}\cos(n\pi x/L)\big|_{x=0} =
 0$ and $\frac{d}{dx}\cos(n\pi x/L)\big|_{x=L} = -\frac{n\pi}{L}\sin(n\pi) = 0$,
 every eigenfunction automatically satisfies $u_x(0,y) = 0$ and $u_x(L,y) = 0$.
Therefore:

$$\boxed{g_1(y) = 0 \text{ and } g_2(y) = 0 \quad \text{(solvability condition)}
}$$

When this holds, the top condition $u(x,0) = f(x)$ determines the coefficients v
ia the Fourier cosine series:
$$a_0 = \frac{1}{L}\int_0^L f(x)\,dx, \qquad a_n = \frac{2}{L}\int_0^L f(x)\cos\
!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)dx, \quad n \ge 1.$$

**Physical explanation:** The Neumann conditions specify the horizontal flux thr
ough the sides. The cosine eigenfunctions (which are the only admissible modes f
or NN in $x$) have zero derivative at both endpoints, so they cannot represent a

## Part (b): $u(0,y) = g_1(y)$, $u_x(L,y) = 0$, $u_y(x,0) = 0$

**Eigenfunctions:** Dirichlet at $x=0$, Neumann at $x=L$: $X(0)=0$, $X'(L)=0$, y

$$u(x,y) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n \sin(k_n x)\, e^{k_n y}$$

**At $x=0$:** $u(0,y) = 0$ (since $\sin(0)=0$). This requires:

$$\boxed{g_1(y) = 0 \quad \text{(solvability condition)}}$$

**At $y=0$:** $u_y(x,0) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n k_n \sin(k_n x) = 0$. Since th

**Solution:** $u \equiv 0$ (when $g_1 = 0$).

**Physical explanation:** The Neumann condition $u_y(x,0)=0$ means zero vertical

flux through the right side. The solution decays at $-\infty$. With no flux ente
ring through the top or right side, and the solution vanishing at $-\infty$, the
 only consistent harmonic function is $u = 0$. Any non-zero Dirichlet data $g_1$
 at $x=0$ would specify a non-zero boundary value that is incompatible with the
zero-flux constraints everywhere else.

## Part (c): $u_x(0,y) = 0$, $u_x(L,y) = 0$, $u_y(x,0) = f(x)$

**Eigenfunctions:** Neumann–Neumann in $x$: $\cos(n\pi x/L)$, $n = 0,1,2,\ldots$

**General solution:**
$$u(x,y) = a_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n \cos\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) e^{
n\pi y/L}$$

**At $y=0$:**
$$u_y(x,0) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n \frac{n\pi}{L} \cos\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}
\right) = f(x)$$

Note the $n=0$ term (constant $a_0$) does **not** contribute to $u_y$. Expanding
 $f$ in a cosine series, the $n=0$ coefficient is $\frac{1}{L}\int_0^L f(x)\,dx$
. Since this cannot be matched by any term in $u_y$:

$$\boxed{\int_0^L f(x)\,dx = 0 \quad \text{(solvability condition)}}$$

When satisfied, the coefficients are:
$$a_n = \frac{2}{n\pi}\int_0^L f(x)\cos\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)dx, \quad
n \ge 1$$

and $a_0$ is **arbitrary** (the solution is unique only up to an additive consta
nt — the standard Neumann non-uniqueness).

**Physical explanation:** This is the classic Neumann problem solvability condit
ion. All three boundaries have Neumann (flux) conditions: the sides have zero fl
ux, and the top has flux $f(x)$. By the divergence theorem applied to $\nabla^2
u = 0$ over the strip (truncated at $y = -R$ and letting $R \to \infty$, where t
he contribution vanishes due to decay):

$$\int_0^L u_y(x,0)\,dx = \int_0^L f(x)\,dx = 0$$

The net flux through the top must be zero because the sides are insulated and th
e solution vanishes at $-\infty$. A non-zero net flux would mean energy is being
 injected with nowhere to go, so no steady-state (harmonic) solution exists. The
 arbitrary constant $a_0$ reflects the fact that specifying only fluxes determin
es $u$ up to a constant (the "ground state" or reference level is undetermined).
