**Problem.** Find all ordered triples $(p, a, b)$ with $p$ prime, $a, b \geq 0$
integers, such that $p^a + p^b$ is a perfect power (i.e. $= n^k$ for some $n \ge
q 1$, $k \geq 2$).

---

## Proof

By symmetry in $a, b$ we may assume $a \leq b$, then double back at the end.

### Case A: $a = b$

$p^a + p^a = 2p^a$. For this to be a $k$-th power ($k \geq 2$), every prime in i
ts factorization $2 \cdot p^a$ must have exponent divisible by $k$.

- **$p = 2$:** $2^{a+1}$ is a perfect power iff $a + 1 \geq 2$, i.e. $a \geq 1$.
 Gives $(2, a, a)$, $a \geq 1$.
- **$p$ odd:** the exponent of $2$ is $1$, not divisible by any $k \geq 2$. No s
olution.

### Case B: $a < b$

Write $p^a + p^b = p^a(1 + p^{b-a})$ with $c := b - a \geq 1$.

**Sub-case B1: $a \geq 1$.** Since $c \geq 1$, we have $p \nmid (1 + p^c)$, so $
\gcd(p^a,\, 1+p^c) = 1$. If the product is a $k$-th power ($k \geq 2$), both cop
rime factors are $k$-th powers. Thus $k \mid a$ and
$$1 + p^c = m^k, \qquad m \geq 2,\ k \geq 2. \tag{$\star$}$$

**Sub-case B2: $a = 0$.** Then $1 + p^b$ itself must be a perfect power, which i
s again ($\star$) with $c = b$.

So in **all** cases with $a < b$, the problem reduces to solving ($\star$): $m^k
 - p^c = 1$ with $p$ prime, $c \geq 1$, $m \geq 2$, $k \geq 2$, together with th
e divisibility condition $k \mid a$ (vacuous when $a = 0$).

---

### Solving $m^k - p^c = 1$

**When $c \geq 2$:** both exponents satisfy $k, c \geq 2$, so Mihailescu's theor
em (Catalan's conjecture) applies: the only solution to $x^r - y^s = 1$ with $x,
 y, r, s \geq 2$ is $3^2 - 2^3 = 1$. Hence
$$m = 3,\ k = 2,\ p = 2,\ c = 3.$$
This forces $k = 2 \mid a$, and $b = a + 3$. Together with the $a = 0$ instance
(where $2 \mid 0$), we get
$$(2,\, a,\, a+3) \quad\text{with } a \geq 0 \text{ even.}$$

**When $c = 1$:** $m^k - 1 = p$ is prime. Factor $m^k - 1 = (m-1)(m^{k-1} + \cdo
ts + 1)$. Primality forces $m - 1 = 1$, i.e. $m = 2$, giving Mersenne primes
$$p = 2^k - 1 \quad (k \geq 2,\ 2^k - 1 \text{ prime}).$$
The condition $k \mid a$ (with $a \geq 0$; for $a = 0$ it is automatic) yields
$$(2^k - 1,\, a,\, a+1) \quad\text{with } k \mid a,\ a \geq 0.$$

---

### Verification

- $(2, a, a)$, $a \geq 1$: $2^{a+1}$, a perfect power. ✓
- $(2, a, a+3)$, $a$ even $\geq 0$: $2^a(1 + 8) = 2^a \cdot 9 = 2^a \cdot 3^2$.
With $a = 2t$: $= 2^{2t} \cdot 3^2 = (2^t \cdot 3)^2$. ✓
- $(2^k-1, a, a+1)$, $k \mid a$: write $a = kq$. Then $p^a(1+p) = p^{kq}\cdot 2^
k = (2p^q)^k$. ✓

### Symmetry

For $a \neq b$, swapping $a, b$ gives the same value, so each asymmetric family
contributes both orderings.

---

## Final Answer

The complete set of ordered triples $(p, a, b)$ is:

1. **$(2,\, n,\, n)$** for every integer $n \geq 1$.

2. **$(2,\, n,\, n+3)$** and **$(2,\, n+3,\, n)$** for every **even** integer $n
 \geq 0$.

3. **$(2^k - 1,\, n,\, n+1)$** and **$(2^k - 1,\, n+1,\, n)$** for every integer
 $k \geq 2$ such that $2^k - 1$ is prime (a Mersenne prime), and every integer $
n \geq 0$ with $k \mid n$.

### The final answer is: All ordered triples $(p,a,b)$ are: $(2,n,n)$ for $n\geq

1,n+1,n)$ for $k\geq 2$ with $2^k-1$ Mersenne prime and $k\mid n$, $n\geq 0$.
