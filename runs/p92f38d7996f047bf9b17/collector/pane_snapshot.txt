**Setup.** Let $x = e^t$ (so $t = \ln x$, and $x \to \infty \iff t \to \infty$).
 Define $Y(t) = y(e^t)$. Then

$$y' = \frac{dY}{dt}\cdot\frac{dt}{dx} = Y'\,e^{-t}, \qquad y'' = \frac{d}{dx}\!
\left(Y' e^{-t}\right) = \left(Y'' - Y'\right)e^{-2t}.$$

Substituting into $x^2 y'' - 3x\,y' - \lambda\, y = 0$:

$$e^{2t}\cdot(Y''-Y')e^{-2t} - 3\,e^t\cdot Y'e^{-t} - \lambda Y = 0 \;\;\Longrig
htarrow\;\; Y'' - 4Y' - \lambda Y = 0.$$

This is a constant-coefficient ODE with characteristic equation $r^2 - 4r - \lam
bda = 0$, giving

$$r = 2 \pm \sqrt{4 + \lambda}.$$

We seek $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$ for which a **nontrivial** solution satisfies $
\lim_{t\to\infty} Y(t) = 0$.

---

**Case 1: $\lambda > -4$ (two distinct real roots).** Set $r_1 = 2 + \sqrt{4+\la
mbda} > 2 > 0$ and $r_2 = 2 - \sqrt{4+\lambda}$.

- $r_1 > 0$ always, so $e^{r_1 t} \to \infty$.
- $r_2 < 0 \iff \sqrt{4+\lambda} > 2 \iff \lambda > 0$.

The general solution is $Y(t) = C_1 e^{r_1 t} + C_2 e^{r_2 t}$. A nontrivial sol
ution with $Y \to 0$ exists iff some root is negative, i.e., **iff $\lambda > 0$
** (take $C_1 = 0$, $C_2 \neq 0$).

For $-4 < \lambda \le 0$: $r_2 \ge 0$, so both roots are non-negative; every non
trivial solution either grows or is constant — none tends to $0$.

**Case 2: $\lambda = -4$ (repeated root $r = 2$).** $Y(t) = (C_1 + C_2 t)e^{2t}
\to \infty$ for every nontrivial choice. No solution tends to $0$.

**Case 3: $\lambda < -4$ (complex roots $r = 2 \pm i\omega$, $\omega = \sqrt{-4-
\lambda}$).** $Y(t) = e^{2t}\bigl(C_1\cos\omega t + C_2\sin\omega t\bigr)$. The
factor $e^{2t}\to\infty$ with bounded oscillation, so no nontrivial solution ten
ds to $0$.

---

**Conclusion.** The set of admissible $\lambda$ is exactly $(0, \infty)$. Its in
fimum is

$$\boxed{0}.$$
