## Proof

Let $a = 2015$. We wish to evaluate
$$I = \int_0^{\infty} e^{-x} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k (ax)^{2k}}{((2k)!)
^2} \, dx.$$

**Step 1: Justify interchanging the sum and integral.**

Consider the sum of absolute values of the integrand:
$$\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(ax)^{2k}}{((2k)!)^2}.$$
For each fixed $x \ge 0$, this series converges (it is dominated by $\sum_{k=0}^
{\infty} \frac{(ax)^{2k}}{(2k)!} = \cosh(ax)$, since $((2k)!)^2 \ge (2k)!$). To
apply Fubini–Tonelli, we verify that
$$\int_0^{\infty} e^{-x} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(ax)^{2k}}{((2k)!)^2}\,dx \;=
\; \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{a^{2k}}{((2k)!)^2}\int_0^{\infty} e^{-x}x^{2k}\,dx
\;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{a^{2k}}{(2k)!} \;=\; \cosh(a) < \infty.$$
Since this is finite, Tonelli's theorem (applied to the non-negative absolute-va
lue integrand) justifies the interchange. Hence
$$I = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k\, a^{2k}}{((2k)!)^2}\int_0^{\infty} e^{-x
}\, x^{2k}\, dx.$$

**Step 2: Evaluate the inner integral.**

For every non-negative integer $k$,
$$\int_0^{\infty} e^{-x}\, x^{2k}\, dx = \Gamma(2k+1) = (2k)!.$$

**Step 3: Substitute and simplify.**

$$I = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k\, a^{2k}}{((2k)!)^2}\cdot (2k)! = \sum_{k
=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k\, a^{2k}}{(2k)!}.$$

**Step 4: Recognize the Taylor series of cosine.**

The Taylor expansion of $\cos(a)$ about $0$ is
$$\cos(a) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k\, a^{2k}}{(2k)!},$$
which converges for all real $a$.

Therefore,
$$I = \cos(a) = \cos(2015).$$

$$\boxed{\int_0^{\infty} e^{-x} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k (2015x)^{2k}}{(
(2k)!)^2} \, dx = \cos(2015).}$$
