# Proof

Let $R = \mathbb{C}[X,Y]/(X^2+Y^2-1)$. We identify the quotient field of $R$.

## Step 1: A convenient change of variables

Over $\mathbb{C}$, introduce new variables
$$U = X + iY, \qquad V = X - iY.$$
These are invertible linear forms (with inverse $X = \tfrac{U+V}{2}$, $Y = \tfra
c{U-V}{2i}$), so the map $\mathbb{C}[X,Y] \to \mathbb{C}[U,V]$ is a $\mathbb{C}$
-algebra automorphism. A direct computation gives
$$X^2 + Y^2 = (X+iY)(X-iY) = UV.$$
Therefore the defining relation $X^2+Y^2-1=0$ becomes
$$UV - 1 = 0.$$

## Step 2: The ring is a Laurent polynomial ring

Under the above change of variables,
$$R = \mathbb{C}[X,Y]/(X^2+Y^2-1) \cong \mathbb{C}[U,V]/(UV-1).$$
The relation $UV=1$ says precisely that $V = U^{-1}$. Hence
$$\mathbb{C}[U,V]/(UV-1) \cong \mathbb{C}[U, U^{-1}] = \mathbb{C}[t, t^{-1}],$$
the ring of Laurent polynomials in one variable (setting $t = U$).

## Step 3: The quotient field

The ring $\mathbb{C}[t, t^{-1}]$ embeds into its fraction field. Every element o
f $\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]$ is a finite sum $\sum_{k=-m}^{n} a_k t^k$, i.e., a rati
o $\frac{P(t)}{t^m}$ with $P \in \mathbb{C}[t]$. Its fraction field therefore co
nsists of all ratios $\frac{P(t)}{Q(t)}$ with $P, Q \in \mathbb{C}[t]$, $Q \neq
0$ (since $t^{-m}$ is already a unit in the ring, multiplying numerator and deno
minator by a suitable power of $t$ clears all negative powers). This is exactly
the rational function field
$$\operatorname{Frac}\bigl(\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\bigr) = \mathbb{C}(t).$$

## Conclusion

The quotient field of $\mathbb{C}[X,Y]/(X^2+Y^2-1)$ is the field of rational fun
ctions in one variable over $\mathbb{C}$:

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Frac}\bigl(\mathbb{C}[X,Y]/(X^2+Y^2-1)\bigr) \;\cong\; \m
athbb{C}(t).}$$
