## Proof

We use the Fourier transform convention
$$\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,dx,\qquad x\cdot\
xi=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\xi_{j}.$$
With this convention the Gaussian identity is
$$\widehat{e^{-t|x|^{2}}}(\xi)=\Big(\frac{\pi}{t}\Big)^{n/2}e^{-|\xi|^{2}/(4t)},
\qquad t>0.\tag{1}$$

The constant prescribed in the problem is
$$\gamma(\alpha,n)=\frac{2^{\,n-\alpha}\,\Gamma\!\big((n-\alpha)/2\big)}{(4\pi)^
{n/2}\,\Gamma(\alpha/2)}=\frac{\Gamma\!\big((n-\alpha)/2\big)}{2^{\alpha}\,\pi^{
n/2}\,\Gamma(\alpha/2)},\tag{2}$$
since $(4\pi)^{n/2}=2^{n}\pi^{n/2}$.

---

### Step 1. A Laplace representation of $|x|^{-\beta}$

For $\beta>0$ and $x\neq 0$,
$$|x|^{-\beta}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\beta/2-1}e^{-t|x|^{
2}}\,dt,\tag{3}$$
because the substitution $u=t|x|^{2}$ gives $\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\beta/2-1}e^{-t
|x|^{2}}dt=\Gamma(\beta/2)\,|x|^{-\beta}$.

---

### Step 2. Fourier transform of $|x|^{-\beta}$ for $0<\beta<n$

Set $\beta=n-\alpha\in(0,n)$, so that $|x|^{\alpha-n}=|x|^{-\beta}$. Insert (3)
and use (1), interchanging the integrals (justified below):
$$\widehat{|x|^{-\beta}}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\beta
/2-1}\Big(\frac{\pi}{t}\Big)^{n/2}e^{-|\xi|^{2}/(4t)}\,dt=\frac{\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamm
a(\beta/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{(\beta-n)/2-1}e^{-|\xi|^{2}/(4t)}\,dt.\tag{4}$$

Substitute $u=|\xi|^{2}/(4t)$, i.e. $t=|\xi|^{2}/(4u)$, $dt=-|\xi|^{2}(4u^{2})^{
-1}du$:
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{(\beta-n)/2-1}e^{-|\xi|^{2}/(4t)}dt=\Big(\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{
4}\Big)^{(\beta-n)/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{(n-\beta)/2-1}e^{-u}\,du=4^{(n-\beta)/2
}\,|\xi|^{\beta-n}\,\Gamma\!\big((n-\beta)/2\big).$$

Hence
$$\widehat{|x|^{-\beta}}(\xi)=\frac{\pi^{n/2}\,2^{\,n-\beta}\,\Gamma\!\big((n-\b
eta)/2\big)}{\Gamma(\beta/2)}\,|\xi|^{\beta-n}.\tag{5}$$

---

### Step 3. Specialize to $\beta=n-\alpha$

With $\beta=n-\alpha$ we have $(n-\beta)/2=\alpha/2$, $\beta/2=(n-\alpha)/2$, an
d $\beta-n=-\alpha$, so (5) becomes
$$\widehat{|x|^{\alpha-n}}(\xi)=\frac{\pi^{n/2}\,2^{\alpha}\,\Gamma(\alpha/2)}{\
Gamma\!\big((n-\alpha)/2\big)}\,|\xi|^{-\alpha}.\tag{6}$$

---

### Step 4. Apply the normalization $\gamma(\alpha,n)$

Multiplying (6) by (2):
$$\gamma(\alpha,n)\,\widehat{|x|^{\alpha-n}}(\xi)=\frac{\Gamma\!\big((n-\alpha)/
2\big)}{2^{\alpha}\,\pi^{n/2}\,\Gamma(\alpha/2)}\;\cdot\;\frac{\pi^{n/2}\,2^{\al
pha}\,\Gamma(\alpha/2)}{\Gamma\!\big((n-\alpha)/2\big)}\,|\xi|^{-\alpha}=|\xi|^{
-\alpha}.\tag{7}$$

---

### Justification of the interchange and distributional validity

The function $|x|^{\alpha-n}=|x|^{-\beta}$ is locally integrable because $\beta<
n$ (the singularity at the origin is of order $<n$), and it decays at infinity s
ince $\beta>0$; hence it defines a tempered distribution. The interchange in (4)
 is legitimate for $\xi\neq 0$ by Tonelli's theorem applied to the positive kern
el after the change of variables, and the resulting integral converges absolutel
y there. Both sides of (5) are tempered distributions: the right-hand side $|\xi
|^{-\alpha}=|\xi|^{\beta-n}$ is locally integrable near $\xi=0$ because $\alpha<
n$, and decays at infinity. They agree on the dense open set $\{\xi\neq 0\}$ and
 are homogeneous of the same degree ($\beta-n$ on the left, $-(\beta-n)-n=-\beta
=\alpha-n$ on the right — consistent with the Fourier transform mapping homogene
ous distributions of degree $d$ to degree $-d-n$), so equality extends to all of
 $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ as tempered distributions. (Equivalently, one may regularize b
y replacing $|x|^{-\beta}$ with $|x|^{-\beta}e^{-\varepsilon|x|^{2}}$, perform t
he absolutely convergent computation, and let $\varepsilon\downarrow 0$.)

---

### Conclusion

With the convention $\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,
dx$, the Fourier transform of the tempered distribution $\gamma(\alpha,n)\,|x|^{
\alpha-n}$ is

$$\boxed{\,\widehat{\gamma(\alpha,n)\,|x|^{\alpha-n}}(\xi)=|\xi|^{-\alpha}\,}.$$

This is precisely the Riesz kernel identity: $\gamma(\alpha,n)|x|^{\alpha-n}$ is
 the convolution kernel whose Fourier multiplier is $|\xi|^{-\alpha}$.
