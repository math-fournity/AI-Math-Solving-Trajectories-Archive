## Proof

Let $E = \{x \in X : f(x) < 1\}$. We claim
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_E e^{-f^n}\,d\mu = \mu(E).$$

**Pointwise limit on $E$.** For every $x \in E$, we have $0 \le f(x) < 1$, hence
 $f(x)^n \to 0$ as $n\to\infty$. Therefore
$$e^{-f(x)^n} \longrightarrow e^{0} = 1 \quad\text{for all } x\in E.$$

**Domination on $E$.** Since $0 \le f(x) < 1$ on $E$, for every $n\ge 1$ we have
 $0 \le f(x)^n \le 1$, so
$$e^{-1} \le e^{-f(x)^n} \le 1 \quad\text{for all } x\in E,\ n\ge 1.$$

**Case 1: $\mu(E) < \infty$.** The constant function $1$ is integrable over $E$,
 and $|e^{-f^n}|\le 1$ on $E$. By the Dominated Convergence Theorem,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_E e^{-f^n}\,d\mu = \int_E \lim_{n\to\infty} e^{-f^n}\,d\
mu = \int_E 1\,d\mu = \mu(E).$$

**Case 2: $\mu(E) = \infty$.** From the upper bound $e^{-f^n}\le 1$,
$$\int_E e^{-f^n}\,d\mu \le \int_E 1\,d\mu = \mu(E) = \infty.$$
From the lower bound $e^{-f^n}\ge e^{-1} > 0$ on $E$,
$$\int_E e^{-f^n}\,d\mu \ge \int_E e^{-1}\,d\mu = e^{-1}\,\mu(E) = \infty.$$
Hence $\int_E e^{-f^n}\,d\mu = \infty = \mu(E)$ for every $n$, and the limit is
$\mu(E)$.

(Equivalently, one may apply Fatou's lemma: $\mu(E)=\int_E\liminf e^{-f^n}\,d\mu
\le\liminf\int_E e^{-f^n}\,d\mu\le\mu(E)$, which also covers the infinite case.)

**Conclusion.** In both cases,
$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_E e^{-f^n}\,d\mu = \mu(E).}$$
