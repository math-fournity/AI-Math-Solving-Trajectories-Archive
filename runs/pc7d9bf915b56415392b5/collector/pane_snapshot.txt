## Solution

**Factorization:** $2016 = 2^5 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 7$.

The squared distance between $(x_1,y_1)$ and $(x_2,y_2)$ is $\Delta x^2 + \Delta
 y^2$ where $\Delta x = x_1-x_2$, $\Delta y = y_1-y_2$. This depends only on res
idues mod $2016$, so the problem reduces to finding the maximum independent set
in the graph $G$ on $(\mathbb{Z}/2016\mathbb{Z})^2$ where two vertices are adjac
ent iff $\Delta x^2+\Delta y^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{2016}$. The answer is $\alpha(G)+1
$.

By CRT, $(\mathbb{Z}/2016\mathbb{Z})^2 \cong (\mathbb{Z}/32\mathbb{Z})^2 \times
(\mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z})^2 \times (\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z})^2$, and the adjacency
 condition splits via AND over the three prime-power components. I analyze each.

### Component mod 7

Since $7 \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$, $-1$ is not a quadratic residue mod 7. If $\Delta y
 \not\equiv 0 \pmod{7}$, then $\Delta x^2 \equiv -\Delta y^2 \pmod{7}$ requires
$-1$ to be a QR — impossible. So $\Delta y \equiv 0$, and symmetrically $\Delta
x \equiv 0 \pmod{7}$.

**Condition:** $\Delta x^2+\Delta y^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{7}$ iff $7 \mid \Delta x$ a
nd $7 \mid \Delta y$.

$G_7$ is a union of $7^2 = 49$ cliques of size 1. So $\alpha(G_7) = 49$.

### Component mod 9

Since $3 \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$, $-1$ is not a QR mod 3. If $\Delta y \not\equiv 0 \
pmod{3}$, then $\Delta x^2 \equiv -\Delta y^2 \pmod{3}$ requires $-1$ to be a QR
 mod 3 — impossible. So $3 \mid \Delta x$ and $3 \mid \Delta y$. Then $\Delta x=
3a, \Delta y=3b$ gives $9(a^2+b^2) \equiv 0 \pmod{9}$, always true.

**Condition:** $\Delta x^2+\Delta y^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{9}$ iff $3 \mid \Delta x$ a
nd $3 \mid \Delta y$.

$G_9$ is a union of $3^2 = 9$ cliques of size 9. So $\alpha(G_9) = 9$.

### Component mod 32

This requires careful 2-adic analysis. I determine when $\Delta x^2 + \Delta y^2
 \equiv 0 \pmod{32}$.

- **Both odd:** $\Delta x^2+\Delta y^2 \equiv 2 \pmod{4}$. ✗
- **One odd, one even:** Sum is odd. ✗
- **Both $\equiv 2 \pmod{4}$:** $\Delta x=2a, \Delta y=2b$ with $a,b$ odd. Then
$4(a^2+b^2) \equiv 4 \cdot 2 = 8 \pmod{32}$. ✗
- **$v_2(\Delta x)=2, v_2(\Delta y)\geq 3$** (or vice versa): $16a^2 + 64b'^2 =
16(a^2+4b'^2)$ with $a$ odd gives $\equiv 16 \pmod{32}$. ✗
- **Both divisible by 4:** $\Delta x = 4a, \Delta y = 4b$, giving $16(a^2+b^2)$.
 Need $a^2+b^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{2}$, i.e., $a \equiv b \pmod{2}$ (same parity). ✓

**Condition:** $\Delta x^2+\Delta y^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{32}$ iff $4 \mid \Delta x$,
 $4 \mid \Delta y$, and $\Delta x/4 \equiv \Delta y/4 \pmod{2}$.

Define the invariant $f_{32}(x,y) = \big(x \bmod 4,\; y \bmod 4,\; (\lfloor x/4
\rfloor - \lfloor y/4 \rfloor) \bmod 2\big)$. Two points are adjacent iff they s
hare the same $f_{32}$-value. There are $4 \cdot 4 \cdot 2 = 32$ cliques, each o
f size $1024/32 = 32$. So $\alpha(G_{32}) = 32$.

### Combining via CRT

Each component graph $G_{p^k}$ is a **disjoint union of cliques**. The AND produ
ct (tensor product) of disjoint unions of cliques is again a disjoint union of c
liques: vertex $(v_{32}, v_9, v_7)$ is adjacent to $(w_{32}, w_9, w_7)$ iff they
 lie in the same clique in **all three** components, i.e., they share the same c
ombined invariant $(f_{32}, f_9, f_7)$.

The number of cliques in $G$ is:
$$32 \times 9 \times 49 = 14112$$

Each clique has size $32 \times 9 \times 1 = 288$, and $14112 \times 288 = 2016^
2 = 4064256$. ✓

For a disjoint union of cliques, the independence number equals the number of cl
iques (pick one vertex from each). Therefore:
$$\alpha(G) = 14112$$

### Conclusion

- **Lower bound (construction):** Select one lattice point from each of the 1411
2 cliques (fibers of the invariant map $F$). No two share an invariant, so no pa
ir has $\Delta x^2+\Delta y^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{2016}$. This gives 14112 points wit
h no valid pair.

- **Upper bound (pigeonhole):** Among any 14113 lattice points, two must share t
he same invariant $F$ by pigeonhole, forcing $\Delta x^2+\Delta y^2 \equiv 0 \pm
od{2016}$.

$$\boxed{n = 14112 + 1 = 14113}$$
