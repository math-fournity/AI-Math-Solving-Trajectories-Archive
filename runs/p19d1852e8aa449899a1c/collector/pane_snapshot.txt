## Answer: Yes

A separable Hilbert space can have a finite orthonormal basis.

### Proof

Recall the relevant definitions:

- A Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ is **separable** if it contains a countable dens
e subset.
- An **orthonormal basis** of $\mathcal{H}$ is a maximal orthonormal set (equiva
lently, an orthonormal set whose closed linear span is all of $\mathcal{H}$).

**Construction.** Consider $\mathcal{H} = \mathbb{C}^n$ equipped with the standa
rd inner product
$$\langle x, y \rangle = \sum_{k=1}^{n} x_k \overline{y_k}.$$
This is a Hilbert space (it is complete, being a finite-dimensional normed space
 over $\mathbb{C}$).

**Finite orthonormal basis.** The standard unit vectors $e_1, \dots, e_n$, where
$$e_k = (0, \dots, 0, \underbrace{1}_{k\text{th}}, 0, \dots, 0),$$
form an orthonormal set, and their linear span is all of $\mathbb{C}^n$. Hence $
\{e_1, \dots, e_n\}$ is an orthonormal basis of $\mathcal{H}$ consisting of exac
tly $n$ elements — a **finite** orthonormal basis.

**Separability.** The set $\mathbb{Q}^n + i\mathbb{Q}^n$ (vectors with rational
real and imaginary parts) is countable and dense in $\mathbb{C}^n$. Therefore $\
mathcal{H} = \mathbb{C}^n$ is separable.

**Conclusion.** We have exhibited a Hilbert space ($\mathbb{C}^n$) that is simul
taneously separable and possesses a finite orthonormal basis. Therefore, a separ
able Hilbert space **can** have a finite orthonormal basis.

**Remark.** In general, every separable Hilbert space has a *countable* orthonor
mal basis. A finite orthonormal basis is simply the special case where the Hilbe
rt space is finite-dimensional. The infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space
s (such as $\ell^2$) have countably infinite orthonormal bases, but this does no
t preclude the finite case. The answer is therefore **yes**.
