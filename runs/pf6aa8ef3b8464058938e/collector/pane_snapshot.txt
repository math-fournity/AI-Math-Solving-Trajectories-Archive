## Solution

We evaluate

$$I = \int_{|z|=1} \frac{1-\cos z}{(e^z-1)\sin z}\,dz$$

by the residue theorem, with the contour $|z|=1$ positively oriented.

### Step 1. Locate the singularities inside $|z|<1$.

The integrand $f(z)=\dfrac{1-\cos z}{(e^z-1)\sin z}$ is singular where the denom
inator vanishes:

- $e^z-1=0 \iff z=2\pi i k,\; k\in\mathbb Z$. Inside $|z|<1$: only $z=0$ (from $
k=0$).
- $\sin z=0 \iff z=\pi k,\; k\in\mathbb Z$. Inside $|z|<1$: only $z=0$ (from $k=
0$).

So **$z=0$ is the only singularity inside the contour**.

### Step 2. Determine the nature of the singularity at $z=0$.

Expand each factor in a Taylor series about $z=0$:

- **Numerator:** $1-\cos z = \dfrac{z^2}{2}-\dfrac{z^4}{24}+O(z^6)=\dfrac{z^2}{2
}\!\left(1-\dfrac{z^2}{12}+O(z^4)\right)$, a **zero of order 2**.
- **Factor 1 of denominator:** $e^z-1 = z+\dfrac{z^2}{2}+\cdots = z\!\left(1+\df
rac{z}{2}+\cdots\right)$, a **zero of order 1**.
- **Factor 2 of denominator:** $\sin z = z-\dfrac{z^3}{6}+\cdots = z\!\left(1-\d
frac{z^2}{6}+\cdots\right)$, a **zero of order 1**.

Hence the denominator $(e^z-1)\sin z$ has a **zero of order $1+1=2$** at $z=0$.

Since numerator and denominator both vanish to order exactly 2, the quotient $f(
z)$ has a **removable singularity** at $z=0$. Indeed,

$$\lim_{z\to 0} f(z) = \frac{1/2}{1\cdot 1} = \frac{1}{2}.$$

### Step 3. Compute the residue.

A removable singularity has no principal part in its Laurent expansion, so the c
oefficient of $z^{-1}$ is zero:

$$\operatorname{Res}(f,\,0)=0.$$

### Step 4. Apply the residue theorem.

By the Cauchy residue theorem,

$$I = 2\pi i \sum_{\substack{\text{poles inside}\\|z|<1}} \operatorname{Res}(f,\
,a) = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}(f,0) = 2\pi i \cdot 0 = 0.$$

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{|z|=1} \frac{1-\cos z}{(e^z-1)\sin z}\,dz = 0.}$$
