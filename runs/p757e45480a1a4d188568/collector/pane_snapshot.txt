## Setup

Place the center $O$ at the origin. The 12 vertices form a regular dodecagon wit
h side length 1, so the circumradius is:
$$R = \frac{1}{2\sin(\pi/12)} = \frac{1}{2 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{6}-\sqrt{2}}{4}} =
\frac{\sqrt{6}+\sqrt{2}}{2}$$

## Finding each shield's radius

Each shield passes through $O$ and two adjacent vertices $A_{2i-1}, A_{2i}$ (sep
arated by $30°$ at $O$). By symmetry, the center of shield $i$ lies on the perpe
ndicular bisector of the chord $A_{2i-1}A_{2i}$, at distance $c$ from $O$ along
the midpoint direction. Setting the distance from the shield's center to $O$ equ
al to its radius $r = c$, and to $A_{2i}$ equal to $c$:

$$c = \frac{R}{2\cos(\pi/12)} = \frac{(\sqrt{6}+\sqrt{2})/2}{2 \cdot (\sqrt{6}+\
sqrt{2})/4} = 1$$

**Each shield has radius 1, with its center at distance 1 from $O$.**

## Configuration of the six shields

The six shield centers are at distance 1 from $O$, at midpoint angles $15°, 75°,
 135°, 195°, 255°, 315°$ — spaced exactly $60°$ apart. Adjacent centers are dist
ance $2\sin(30°) = 1$ apart. All six circles pass through $O$ (since each center
 is at distance 1 = radius from $O$).

This is the classic configuration: **six unit circles with centers forming a reg
ular hexagon of side 1, all passing through $O$**.

## Identifying the boundary

Adjacent circles $C_i, C_{i+1}$ (centers 1 apart, both radius 1) intersect at $O
$ and at a point $P_i$ at distance $\sqrt{3}$ from $O$ (forming equilateral tria
ngles with the centers). The six points $P_1, \ldots, P_6$ lie at distance $\sqr
t{3}$ from $O$, spaced $60°$ apart — a regular hexagon.

**Key verification:** The outer arc of each circle (from $P_{i-1}$ to $P_i$ thro
ugh the farthest point at distance 2 from $O$) is entirely uncovered by other ci
rcles. I verified this by checking that for points on $C_1$'s outer arc (paramet
rized by angle $t \in [-\pi/3, \pi/3]$ from $C_1$'s center), the distance to eve
ry other circle's center exceeds 1. For example, the distance squared to $C_2$ i
s $2 + \cos t - \sqrt{3}\sin t = 2 + 2\cos(t+\pi/3)$, which is $\geq 1$ for $t \
in [0, \pi/3]$ (equality only at $t = \pi/3$). Non-adjacent circles are even far
ther.

The union is simply connected (every direction from $O$ is covered up to at leas
t $r = \sqrt{3}$), with boundary consisting of **6 circular arcs**, each subtend
ing $120°$ at its center.

## Computing the area

Using Green's theorem for a region bounded by circular arcs:

$$A = \frac{1}{2}\sum_i r_i^2(\Delta t_i - \sin\Delta t_i) + \frac{1}{2}\sum_i (
P_i \times P_{i+1})$$

The first sum is the total circular segment area; the second is the polygon area
 of the vertices.

**Circular segments:** 6 arcs, each with $r = 1$ and $\Delta t = 2\pi/3$:
$$\frac{1}{2} \cdot 6 \cdot 1^2 \cdot \left(\frac{2\pi}{3} - \sin\frac{2\pi}{3}\
right) = 3\left(\frac{2\pi}{3} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) = 2\pi - \frac{3\sqrt
{3}}{2}$$

**Polygon (regular hexagon):** Vertices at distance $\sqrt{3}$ from $O$, side le
ngth $\sqrt{3}$:
$$\text{Area} = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}(\sqrt{3})^2 = \frac{9\sqrt{3}}{2}$$

**Total area:**
$$A = \left(2\pi - \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) + \frac{9\sqrt{3}}{2} = 2\pi + \fr
ac{6\sqrt{3}}{2} = 2\pi + 3\sqrt{3}$$
