## Proof

We use the **Lebesgue density theorem**: for any measurable set $A \subseteq \ma
thbb{R}$, for almost every $x \in \mathbb{R}$,
$$\lim_{r \to 0^+} \frac{m\bigl(A \cap (x-r,\, x+r)\bigr)}{2r} = \mathbf{1}_A(x)
.$$
In particular, the set
$$D \;=\; \Bigl\{x \in A : \lim_{r\to 0^+} \tfrac{m(A \cap (x-r,x+r))}{2r} = 1\B
igr\}$$
satisfies $m(A \setminus D) = 0$, i.e. **$m(A) = m(D)$** (almost every point of
$A$ is a Lebesgue point of density $1$).

---

**Applying the hypothesis.** Let $x \in (0,1)$. For all sufficiently small $r >
0$ we have $[x-r,\, x+r] \subseteq [0,1]$, so the hypothesis gives
$$m\bigl(A \cap [x-r,\, x+r]\bigr) \;\le\; \frac{2r}{2} \;=\; r.$$
Hence for every $x \in (0,1)$,
$$\limsup_{r\to 0^+} \frac{m(A \cap (x-r,x+r))}{2r} \;\le\; \frac{1}{2}.$$
The same bound holds at the endpoints by a one-sided argument: e.g. at $0$, the
hypothesis applied to $[0,r]$ gives $m(A\cap[0,r])\le r/2$, and similarly at $1$
. Thus **at every point $x \in [0,1]$ the upper density of $A$ is at most $1/2$*
*.

---

**Conclusion.** A point $x \in D$ must have density exactly $1$, yet every point
 of $[0,1]$ has upper density $\le 1/2 < 1$. Therefore
$$D \;\subseteq\; \{x : \text{density of } A \text{ at } x = 1\} \;\cap\; \{x :
\text{upper density} \le \tfrac12\} \;=\; \varnothing.$$
So $D = \varnothing$ and consequently
$$m(A) \;=\; m(D) \;=\; 0. \qquad\blacksquare$$
