## Proof

**Claim:** In general, $H(\mu) \neq H(f_\#\mu)$. The equality does not hold for
arbitrary measurable maps $f$.

**Counterexample.** Let $d = 1$. Take $\mu$ to be the standard Gaussian measure
$\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ on $\mathbb{R}$, with density
$$\mu(x) = \varphi(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,e^{-x^2/2}.$$

Take the measurable (in fact, linear) map $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ defined by
 $f(x) = 2x$.

**Entropy of $\mu$.** The differential entropy (using the problem's convention $
H(\mu) = \int \mu\log\mu\,dx$) of a Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(m,\sigma^2)$ is
$$H(\mathcal{N}(m,\sigma^2)) = -\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e\,\sigma^2).$$
For $\mu = \mathcal{N}(0,1)$:
$$H(\mu) = -\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e).$$

**Push-forward measure.** The push-forward $f_\#\mu$ is the distribution of $f(X
) = 2X$ where $X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$. Since scaling a Gaussian by $2$ gives $\m
athcal{N}(0,4)$, we have $f_\#\mu = \mathcal{N}(0,4)$, with density
$$(f_\#\mu)(y) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{8\pi}}\,e^{-y^2/8}.$$

**Entropy of $f_\#\mu$.** Applying the Gaussian entropy formula with $\sigma^2 =
 4$:
$$H(f_\#\mu) = -\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e \cdot 4) = -\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e) - \lo
g 2.$$

**Comparison.** Therefore
$$H(f_\#\mu) = H(\mu) - \log 2 \neq H(\mu),$$
since $\log 2 \neq 0$.

**General principle.** More generally, for any invertible linear map $f(x) = Ax$
 on $\mathbb{R}^d$ with $A\in GL(d)$, the push-forward density exists and the en
tropy transforms as
$$H(f_\#\mu) = H(\mu) - \log|\det A|.$$
This equals $H(\mu)$ only when $|\det A| = 1$, i.e., when $f$ is volume-preservi
ng. For a general measurable map $f$ (which may be non-injective, non-surjective
, or singular), the push-forward may not even admit a density, so $H(f_\#\mu)$ m
ay be undefined or $-\infty$, while $H(\mu)$ is finite.

**Conclusion.** The entropy of a probability measure is **not** in general equal
 to the entropy of its push-forward. Equality holds only under special condition
s (e.g., $f$ measure-preserving / volume-preserving), not for arbitrary measurab
le $f$.
