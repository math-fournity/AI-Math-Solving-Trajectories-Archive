## Proof

**Setup and notation.** A "regular triangular pyramid" (正三棱锥) has an equilat
eral triangular base of side length $s$ and an apex directly above the centroid,
 with all three lateral edges equal. Let $h$ be the height and $\ell$ the latera
l edge length, so $\ell^2 = h^2 + s^2/3$ (since the centroid-to-vertex distance
in an equilateral triangle of side $s$ is $s/\sqrt{3}$).

**Volume.** The base area is $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}s^2$, so
$$V = \tfrac{1}{3}\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}s^2\cdot h = \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{12}s^2 h
 = 36\sqrt{2},$$
giving
$$s^2 h = \frac{36\sqrt{2}\cdot 12}{\sqrt{3}} = 144\sqrt{6}. \tag{1}$$

**Dihedral angle along a lateral edge.** Place the centroid at the origin with t
he apex at $(0,0,h)$ and base vertices
$$A=\bigl(\tfrac{s}{\sqrt 3},0,0\bigr),\quad B=\bigl(-\tfrac{s}{2\sqrt 3},\tfrac
{s}{2},0\bigr),\quad C=\bigl(-\tfrac{s}{2\sqrt 3},-\tfrac{s}{2},0\bigr).$$
The two lateral faces meeting at edge $PA$ are $\triangle PAB$ and $\triangle PA
C$. Computing the outward normals to these faces and taking the angle between th
em, the interior dihedral angle $\theta$ along $PA$ satisfies
$$\cos\theta = \frac{6h^2 - s^2}{12h^2 + s^2}. \tag{2}$$

**Geometric constraint from sharing the edge.** The three identical pyramids all
 meet along one common lateral edge (from a shared ground vertex to the shared a
pex). Around this common edge, each pyramid occupies the dihedral wedge angle $\
theta$. For the three bunkers to fit around the shared edge without overlapping,
$$3\theta \le 360^\circ \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \theta \le 120^\circ \quad\Lon
grightarrow\quad \cos\theta \ge -\tfrac{1}{2}.$$
Using (2):
$$\frac{6h^2-s^2}{12h^2+s^2} \ge -\frac{1}{2} \;\Longrightarrow\; 12h^2-2s^2 \ge
 -12h^2-s^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; 24h^2 \ge s^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; s \le 2\sqrt{6
}\,h. \tag{3}$$

**Maximizing $s$.** To maximize $s$ we take equality in (3): $s = 2\sqrt{6}\,h$.
 Substituting into the volume constraint (1):
$$s^2 h = (2\sqrt{6}\,h)^2\cdot h = 24h^3 = 144\sqrt{6} \;\Longrightarrow\; h^3
= 6\sqrt{6} = 6^{3/2} \;\Longrightarrow\; h = \sqrt{6}.$$
Therefore
$$s = 2\sqrt{6}\cdot\sqrt{6} = 12.$$

**Verification.** With $s=12$, $h=\sqrt{6}$:
- Volume $= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{12}\cdot 144\cdot\sqrt{6} = 12\sqrt{18} = 36\sqrt{2}
$. ✓
- $\cos\theta = \frac{6\cdot 6 - 144}{12\cdot 6 + 144} = \frac{-108}{216} = -\fr
ac{1}{2}$, so $\theta = 120^\circ$ and $3\theta = 360^\circ$. ✓

The three bunkers exactly fill the space around the common edge, and increasing
$s$ further would force $\theta > 120^\circ$, causing overlap. Hence the maximum
 is attained.

$$\boxed{s = 12}$$
