**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

Let $f \colon \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be continuous. We construct a sequence
of polynomials $(p_n)_{n=1}^{\infty}$ converging to $f$ pointwise on $\mathbb{R}
$.

For each positive integer $n$, consider the compact interval $K_n = [-n, n]$. Si
nce $f$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$, its restriction to $K_n$ is continuous. B
y the **Weierstrass approximation theorem**, there exists a polynomial $p_n$ suc
h that

$$
\sup_{x \in K_n} |p_n(x) - f(x)| < \frac{1}{n}.
$$

We claim $p_n \to f$ pointwise on $\mathbb{R}$.

Fix any $x \in \mathbb{R}$. Choose $N \in \mathbb{N}$ with $N > |x|$, so that $x
 \in K_n = [-n,n]$ for all $n \ge N$. Then for every $n \ge N$,

$$
|p_n(x) - f(x)| < \frac{1}{n}.
$$

Letting $n \to \infty$, we obtain $p_n(x) \to f(x)$. Since $x$ was arbitrary, $p
_n \to f$ pointwise on all of $\mathbb{R}$.

**Remark.** The convergence is generally not uniform on $\mathbb{R}$ (uniform po
lynomial approximation on all of $\mathbb{R}$ is possible only when $f$ itself i
s a polynomial, since a uniform limit of polynomials on $\mathbb{R}$ that is con
tinuous must be a polynomial — a uniform limit of bounded-on-compact functions n
eed not be, but uniform convergence on $\mathbb{R}$ of polynomials forces the li
mit to be a polynomial). The pointwise result, however, holds for every continuo
us $f$.
