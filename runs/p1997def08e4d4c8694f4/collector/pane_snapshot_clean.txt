# Proof (IMO 1966, Problem 4)

**Key identity.** For any real $y$ with $\sin y \neq 0$ and $\sin 2y \neq 0$,
$$\cot y - \cot 2y = \frac{1}{\sin 2y}.$$

*Proof of the identity.* Using $\sin 2y = 2\sin y\cos y$:
$$\cot y - \cot 2y = \frac{\cos y}{\sin y} - \frac{\cos 2y}{\sin 2y} = \frac{\co
s y\sin 2y - \cos 2y\sin y}{\sin y\sin 2y} = \frac{\sin(2y - y)}{\sin y\sin 2y}
= \frac{\sin y}{\sin y\sin 2y} = \frac{1}{\sin 2y}. \quad\square$$

**Well-definedness.** The hypothesis is that $x \neq k\pi/2^{t}$ for every integ
er $k$ and every non-negative integer $t$. We must check that every term appeari
ng is defined.

- For each $j \in \{1,\dots,n\}$, the term $1/\sin(2^{j}x)$ requires $\sin(2^{j}
x)\neq 0$, i.e. $2^{j}x \neq m\pi$, i.e. $x \neq m\pi/2^{j} = m\pi/2^{t}$ with $
t=j\geq 0$. This is excluded by hypothesis.
- $\cot x$ requires $\sin x\neq 0$, i.e. $x\neq m\pi = m\pi/2^{0}$, excluded (ca
se $t=0$).
- $\cot(2^{n}x)$ requires $\sin(2^{n}x)\neq 0$, i.e. $x\neq m\pi/2^{n}$, exclude
d (case $t=n$).

Hence both sides are well-defined.

**Telescoping.** Apply the key identity with $y = 2^{j-1}x$ for $j = 1, 2, \dots
, n$:
$$\frac{1}{\sin(2^{j}x)} = \cot(2^{j-1}x) - \cot(2^{j}x), \qquad j=1,\dots,n.$$

(Here $y = 2^{j-1}x$, so $2y = 2^{j}x$; the requirements $\sin y\neq 0$ and $\si
n 2y\neq 0$ become $\sin(2^{j-1}x)\neq 0$ and $\sin(2^{j}x)\neq 0$, both guarant
eed by the hypothesis since $j-1, j \in \{0,\dots,n\}$ are non-negative integers
.)

Summing over $j = 1, \dots, n$:
$$\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\sin(2^{j}x)} = \sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigl(\cot(2^{j-1}x) - \c
ot(2^{j}x)\bigr).$$

The right-hand side is a telescoping sum: writing out the terms,
$$\bigl(\cot x - \cot 2x\bigr) + \bigl(\cot 2x - \cot 4x\bigr) + \cdots + \bigl(
\cot(2^{n-1}x) - \cot(2^{n}x)\bigr).$$

All intermediate terms cancel, leaving
$$\cot x - \cot(2^{n}x).$$

Therefore
$$\frac{1}{\sin 2x} + \frac{1}{\sin 4x} + \cdots + \frac{1}{\sin(2^{n}x)} = \cot
 x - \cot(2^{n}x),$$
as required. $\blacksquare$
