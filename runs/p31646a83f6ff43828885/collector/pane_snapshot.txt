## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $h \in L^2$.

**Setup.** Let $\Omega$ be the underlying domain. Recall that $W^{-1,2}(\Omega)
= (W^{1,2}_0(\Omega))^*$, and that every element of $W^{-1,2}(\Omega)$ is a dist
ribution on $\Omega$ (since $C_c^\infty(\Omega) \hookrightarrow W^{1,2}_0(\Omega
)$, the action of $h \in W^{-1,2}$ on test functions is well-defined and distrib
utional). Likewise, every $u \in L^2(\Omega)$ defines a distribution via
$$\langle u, \varphi\rangle = \int_\Omega u\,\varphi\,dx, \qquad \varphi \in C_c
^\infty(\Omega).$$

The **distributional equation** $u = h$ means
$$\langle u, \varphi\rangle = \langle h, \varphi\rangle \quad \text{for all } \v
arphi \in C_c^\infty(\Omega),$$
i.e., $u$ and $h$ are the **same distribution**.

**Key fact (injectivity of $L^2 \hookrightarrow \mathcal{D}'$).** The natural ma
p $J: L^2(\Omega) \to \mathcal{D}'(\Omega)$, $f \mapsto \big(\varphi \mapsto \in
t f\varphi\big)$, is **injective**. Indeed, if $J(f) = 0$, then $\int_\Omega f\,
\varphi\,dx = 0$ for all $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$. By the fundamental le
mma of the calculus of variations (du Bois-Reymond / testing against smooth comp
actly supported functions), $f = 0$ a.e. Hence $\ker J = \{0\}$.

**Conclusion.** Since $u = h$ as distributions and $u \in L^2(\Omega)$, the dist
ribution $h$ is represented by the $L^2$ function $u$. By injectivity of $J$, th
is representative is unique (up to a.e. equality). Therefore $h$, as an element
of $W^{-1,2}$, **lies in (the image of) $L^2$**, with $h = u$ a.e.

In particular, $\|h\|_{L^2} = \|u\|_{L^2} < \infty$, so $h \in L^2(\Omega)$. $\b
lacksquare$
