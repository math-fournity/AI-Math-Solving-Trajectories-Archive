**Problem restatement.** For a prime $p$, $p$ people sit around a round table on
 multiple nights so that no two people are neighbors on more than one night. Fin
d the maximum number of nights.

**Answer.** For an odd prime $p$, the maximum is $\dfrac{p-1}{2}$. (The degenera
te case $p=2$ gives $1$ night.)

---

**Upper bound.** Label the people $0,1,\dots,p-1$. A seating on a given night is
 a Hamiltonian cycle in the complete graph $K_p$, using exactly $p$ edges (the $
p$ adjacencies around the table). The condition "each person has different neigh
bors every night" means no adjacency (edge) $\{x,y\}$ is reused on two different
 nights — otherwise $x$ and $y$ would be neighbors on two nights. Hence the nigh
ts give **edge-disjoint** Hamiltonian cycles. Since $K_p$ has $\binom{p}{2}=\dfr
ac{p(p-1)}{2}$ edges and each night consumes $p$ of them,

$$\#\text{nights} \;\le\; \frac{p(p-1)/2}{p} \;=\; \frac{p-1}{2}.$$

**Construction achieving the bound (odd prime $p$).** Identify the people with $
\mathbb{Z}_p=\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}$. For each $a\in\{1,2,\dots,(p-1)/2\}$, define th
e cyclic ordering

$$C_a:\quad 0,\; a,\; 2a,\; 3a,\; \dots,\; (p-1)a \pmod p,\quad \text{back to }0
.$$

Since $p$ is prime, $\gcd(a,p)=1$, so multiplication by $a$ permutes $\mathbb{Z}
_p$; the sequence $0,a,2a,\dots,(p-1)a$ runs through all $p$ elements before ret
urning to $0$. Thus $C_a$ is a genuine Hamiltonian cycle.

The edges of $C_a$ are $\{ka,\,(k+1)a\}$ for $k=0,\dots,p-1$ (indices mod $p$).
Writing $x=ka$, these are exactly the edges $\{x,\,x+a\}$ as $x$ ranges over $\m
athbb{Z}_p$ — i.e., **all edges of difference $a$**. But an edge of difference $
a$ is the same as an edge of difference $p-a$ (since $\{x,x+a\}=\{x+a,x\}=\{y,\,
y+(p-a)\}$). Therefore the edge sets of $C_a$ and $C_{p-a}$ coincide, and the **
distinct** edge classes are indexed by $a=1,2,\dots,(p-1)/2$.

Every edge $\{x,y\}$ of $K_p$ has a unique difference $d\in\{1,2,\dots,(p-1)/2\}
$ (namely $d=\min(y-x,\,x-y)\bmod p$), so the cycles $C_1,C_2,\dots,C_{(p-1)/2}$
 partition the edge set of $K_p$ into $(p-1)/2$ edge-disjoint Hamiltonian cycles
. Each cycle gives one night's seating, and no edge is shared between two nights
, so every pair of people are neighbors on **at most one** night — exactly the r
equired condition.

This achieves $(p-1)/2$ nights, matching the upper bound.

**The case $p=2$.** With two people there is only one possible adjacency, so at
most one night is possible (and one is achievable). This is the trivial degenera
te case; the interesting answer for odd primes is $\dfrac{p-1}{2}$.

---

**Conclusion.** For an odd prime $p$, the maximum number of nights is $\boxed{\d
frac{p-1}{2}}$.
