$$\lim_{\omega \to \infty} \int_{\mathbb{R}} f(x), e^{-ix\omega}, dx = 0.$$

This is the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma. We prove it in three steps.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 1: Indicator function of a bounded interval

Let $f = \mathbf{1}_{[a,b]}$ with $a < b$. Then

$$\hat{f}(\omega) = \int_a^b e^{-ix\omega},dx = \frac{e^{-ia\omega} - e^{-
ib\omega}}{i\omega}, \qquad \omega \neq 0.$$

Hence

$$|\hat{f}(\omega)| = \frac{|e^{-ia\omega} - e^{-ib\omega}|}{|\omega|} \leq
\frac{2}{|\omega|} \xrightarrow{|\omega|\to\infty} 0.$$

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 2: Step functions (finite linear combinations of interval indicators)

Let $g = \sum_{k=1}^{n} c_k, \mathbf{1}_{[a_k, b_k]}$ be a step function. By
linearity of the Fourier transform,

$$\hat{g}(\omega) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} c_k, \widehat{\mathbf{1}_{[a_k,b_k]}}
(\omega).$$

Each term tends to $0$ as $|\omega| \to \infty$ by Step 1, so $\hat{g}(\omega)
\to 0$.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Density fact. Step functions are dense in $L^1(\mathbb{R})$: for every $f \in
L^1(\mathbb{R})$ and every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists a step function $g$
such that

$$|f - g|{L^1} = \int{\mathbb{R}} |f(x) - g(x)|, dx < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.$$
