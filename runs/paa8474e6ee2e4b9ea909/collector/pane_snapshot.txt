$$a_n = \sqrt{p_n}, \qquad A = \{\sqrt{p_n} \mid n \in \mathbb{N}\} = \{\sqrt{2}

**Claim 1: $A$ is linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}$** (so $A$ itself is an
infinite linearly independent subset).

*Proof of Claim 1.* Fix any finite collection of distinct primes $p_1,\ldots,p_k
$ and suppose
$$q_1\sqrt{p_1} + q_2\sqrt{p_2} + \cdots + q_k\sqrt{p_k} = 0, \qquad q_i \in \ma
thbb{Q}. \tag{$\ast$}$$

Consider the field $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p_1},\ldots,\sqrt{p_k})$. Since the $p_
i$ are distinct primes, no product of a subcollection of them is a perfect squar
e; by induction this gives $[K:\mathbb{Q}] = 2^k$. As $K$ is the splitting field
 of $\prod_{i=1}^k (x^2 - p_i)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, the extension $K/\mathbb{Q}$
is Galois with $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^
k$. In particular, for each $i$ there is an automorphism $\sigma_i \in \operator
name{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ sending $\sqrt{p_i} \mapsto -\sqrt{p_i}$ and fixing $\s
qrt{p_j}$ for all $j \neq i$.

Applying $\sigma_i$ to $(\ast)$:
$$-q_i\sqrt{p_i} + \sum_{j \neq i} q_j \sqrt{p_j} = 0.$$

Subtracting this from $(\ast)$ yields $2q_i\sqrt{p_i} = 0$, hence $q_i = 0$. Sin
ce $i$ was arbitrary, all coefficients vanish. Therefore $\{\sqrt{p_1},\ldots,\s
qrt{p_k}\}$ is linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}$ for every $k$, and $A$ is
an infinite linearly independent set over $\mathbb{Q}$. $\square$

**Claim 2: $A$ is not dense in $\mathbb{R}$.**

*Proof of Claim 2.* Every element of $A$ is of the form $\sqrt{p}$ for a prime $
p \geq 2$, so $A \subset [\sqrt{2},\, \infty)$. The open interval $(0,\,1)$ cont
ains no point of $A$. Hence $A$ is not dense in $\mathbb{R}$. $\square$

**Conclusion.** The set $A = \{\sqrt{p_n} \mid n \in \mathbb{N}\}$ has an infini
