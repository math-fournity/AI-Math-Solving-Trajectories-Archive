## Solution

We evaluate $\oint_{|z|=2} \tan z\,dz$ using the residue theorem.

**Step 1: Identify the singularities of $\tan z$.**

Since $\tan z = \dfrac{\sin z}{\cos z}$, the poles occur where $\cos z = 0$, i.e
., at
$$z_k = \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi, \quad k \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

**Step 2: Determine which poles lie inside $|z|=2$.**

We have $\frac{\pi}{2} \approx 1.5708 < 2$ and $\frac{3\pi}{2} \approx 4.7124 >
2$. By symmetry, $-\frac{\pi}{2} \approx -1.5708$, so $|-\frac{\pi}{2}| = \frac{
\pi}{2} < 2$. Thus the poles inside the contour are:
$$z = \frac{\pi}{2} \quad \text{and} \quad z = -\frac{\pi}{2}.$$

All other poles $z_k$ with $|k| \geq 1$ satisfy $|z_k| \geq \frac{3\pi}{2} > 2$
and lie outside.

**Step 3: Compute the residue at each pole.**

Each pole is simple (since $\cos' z = -\sin z \neq 0$ at these points). For a qu
otient $f(z)/g(z)$ with a simple zero of $g$ at $z_0$, the residue is $f(z_0)/g'
(z_0)$. Here:
$$\operatorname{Res}(\tan z,\, z_0) = \frac{\sin z_0}{(\cos z)'\big|_{z_0}} = \f
rac{\sin z_0}{-\sin z_0} = -1.$$

This holds at every pole of $\tan z$, so:
- $\operatorname{Res}\!\left(\tan z,\, \tfrac{\pi}{2}\right) = -1$,
- $\operatorname{Res}\!\left(\tan z,\, -\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right) = -1$.

**Step 4: Apply the residue theorem.**

$$\oint_{|z|=2} \tan z\,dz = 2\pi i \sum \operatorname{Res} = 2\pi i \bigl((-1)
+ (-1)\bigr) = 2\pi i \cdot (-2) = -4\pi i.$$

### Answer

$$\boxed{\oint_{|z|=2} \tan z\,dz = -4\pi i}$$
