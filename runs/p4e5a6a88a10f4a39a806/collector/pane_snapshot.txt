## Exploration

Computing small cases:

- $P_0 = 9$, digit sum $= 9$
- $P_1 = 9 \times 99 = 891$, digit sum $= 18$
- $P_2 = 891 \times 9999 = 8909109$, digit sum $= 36$
- $P_3 = 8909109 \times 99999999 = 890910891090891$, digit sum $= 72$

The pattern is $9, 18, 36, 72 = 9\cdot 2^0, 9\cdot 2^1, 9\cdot 2^2, 9\cdot 2^3$.

**Conjecture:** The digit sum of $P_n$ is $9 \cdot 2^n$.

## Key Lemma

**Lemma.** If $a + b = 10^m - 1$ (a number with $m$ nines) and $0 \le a, b < 10^
m$, then $\operatorname{digsum}(a) + \operatorname{digsum}(b) = 9m$.

*Proof.* Write $a = \sum a_i 10^i$ and $b = \sum b_i 10^i$ with $0 \le a_i, b_i
\le 9$. Adding column by column from the units digit: in column $i$, we have $a_
i + b_i + c_i = 9 + 10 c_{i+1}$, where $c_i$ is the carry in and $c_{i+1}$ the c
arry out, with $c_0 = 0$ (no initial carry) and $c_m = 0$ (no final carry, since
 the sum is $< 10^m$).

For $i=0$: $c_0 = 0$, so $a_0 + b_0 = 9 + 10 c_1$. Since $a_0 + b_0 \le 18$, we
get $c_1 = 0$ and $a_0 + b_0 = 9$.

By induction: if $c_i = 0$, then $a_i + b_i = 9 + 10 c_{i+1}$, and $a_i + b_i \l
e 18$ forces $c_{i+1} = 0$ and $a_i + b_i = 9$.

So every column satisfies $a_i + b_i = 9$ with no carries, giving $\operatorname
{digsum}(a) + \operatorname{digsum}(b) = \sum(a_i + b_i) = 9m$. $\square$

## Induction

**Base case:** $P_0 = 9$, $\operatorname{digsum}(P_0) = 9 = 9 \cdot 2^0$. ✓

**Inductive step.** Assume $\operatorname{digsum}(P_n) = 9 \cdot 2^n$. Set $m =
2^{n+1}$, so the next factor is $10^m - 1$ and

$$P_{n+1} = P_n \cdot (10^m - 1) = P_n \cdot 10^m - P_n.$$

**Digit count of $P_n$:** $P_n$ is a product of factors with $1, 2, 4, \ldots, 2
^n$ digits, so $P_n$ has exactly $1 + 2 + 4 + \cdots + 2^n = 2^{n+1} - 1 = m - 1
$ digits. Thus $10^{m-2} \le P_n < 10^{m-1} < 10^m$.

**Splitting the product:** Since $P_n < 10^m$,

$$P_{n+1} = P_n \cdot 10^m - P_n = (P_n - 1)\cdot 10^m + (10^m - P_n).$$

**No overlap:** $P_n$ is a product of odd numbers, hence odd, so $P_n$ does not
end in $0$. Therefore $P_n - 1$ has the same $m-1$ digits as $P_n$. Also $10^m -
 P_n > 10^m - 10^{m-1} = 9\cdot 10^{m-1} \ge 10^{m-1}$, so $10^m - P_n$ has exac
tly $m$ digits. The two blocks $(P_n - 1)$ and $(10^m - P_n)$ occupy disjoint di
git positions, so $P_{n+1}$ is the concatenation:

$$P_{n+1} = \underbrace{(P_n - 1)}_{m-1 \text{ digits}}\;\underbrace{(10^m - P_n
)}_{m \text{ digits}},$$

and

$$\operatorname{digsum}(P_{n+1}) = \operatorname{digsum}(P_n - 1) + \operatornam
e{digsum}(10^m - P_n).$$

**Applying the Lemma:** Set $a = P_n - 1$ and $b = 10^m - P_n$. Then $a + b = 10
^m - 1$, and both satisfy $0 \le a, b < 10^m$. By the Lemma:

$$\operatorname{digsum}(P_n - 1) + \operatorname{digsum}(10^m - P_n) = 9m = 9 \c
dot 2^{n+1}.$$

Therefore $\operatorname{digsum}(P_{n+1}) = 9 \cdot 2^{n+1}$. ✓
