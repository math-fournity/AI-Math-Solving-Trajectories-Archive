## Solution

Let
$$f(x) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{x^n}{(n!)^2}.$$

The radius of convergence is infinite (ratio test: $\left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\ri
ght| = \frac{|x|}{(n+1)^2} \to 0$), so $f$ is entire and we may differentiate te
rm-by-term freely.

### Step 1. Derive a differential equation for $f$

Differentiating once:
$$f'(x) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{n\,x^{n-1}}{(n!)^2} = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{
x^{n-1}}{n\,((n-1)!)^2},$$
since $\frac{n}{(n!)^2} = \frac{1}{n\,((n-1)!)^2}$.

Multiply by $x$:
$$x\,f'(x) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{x^{n}}{n\,((n-1)!)^2}.$$

Differentiate this:
$$\frac{d}{dx}\bigl[x\,f'(x)\bigr] = f'(x) + x\,f''(x) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac
{n\,x^{n-1}}{n\,((n-1)!)^2} = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{x^{n-1}}{((n-1)!)^2} = \su
m_{m=0}^\infty \frac{x^{m}}{(m!)^2} = f(x).$$

Hence $f$ satisfies the **second-order ODE**
$$\boxed{x\,f''(x) + f'(x) - f(x) = 0}, \qquad f(0)=1,\; f'(0)=1.$$

### Step 2. Identify the equation as the modified Bessel equation of order $0$

The modified Bessel function of the first kind of order $0$ is defined by
$$I_0(t) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(t/2)^{2n}}{(n!)^2},$$
and it satisfies the modified Bessel equation of order $0$:
$$t^2\,I_0''(t) + t\,I_0'(t) - t^2\,I_0(t) = 0,$$
with $I_0(0)=1$, $I_0'(0)=0$.

### Step 3. Reduce our equation to the modified Bessel equation via substitution

Set $t = 2\sqrt{x}$ (so $x = t^2/4$) and let $g(t) = f(t^2/4)$. Then
$$g'(t) = f'(x)\cdot\frac{t}{2}, \qquad g''(t) = f''(x)\cdot\frac{t^2}{4} + f'(x
)\cdot\frac{1}{2}.$$

Compute:
$$t\,g''(t) + g'(t) - t\,g(t) = t\!\left(f''\frac{t^2}{4}+f'\frac{1}{2}\right) +
 f'\frac{t}{2} - t\,f = f''\frac{t^3}{4} + f'\,t - t\,f.$$

Using $x = t^2/4$:
$$f''\frac{t^3}{4} + f'\,t - t\,f = 2\sqrt{x}\bigl(x\,f'' + f' - f\bigr) = 0,$$
by the ODE from Step 1. Thus $g(t)$ satisfies
$$t^2\,g'' + t\,g' - t^2\,g = 0,$$
the modified Bessel equation of order $0$.

### Step 4. Match initial conditions

The general solution of the modified Bessel equation of order $0$ is
$$g(t) = A\,I_0(t) + B\,K_0(t),$$
where $K_0$ is the modified Bessel function of the second kind. Since $K_0(t) \t
o \infty$ as $t \to 0^+$ while $g(0) = f(0) = 1$ is finite, we must have $B = 0$
.

Then $g(0) = A\,I_0(0) = A = 1$, so $g(t) = I_0(t)$.

### Step 5. Conclude

Since $g(t) = f(t^2/4) = I_0(t)$, substituting back $t = 2\sqrt{x}$ gives

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{x^n}{(n!)^2} = I_0\!\left(2\sqrt{
x}\right)},$$

where $I_0$ is the **modified Bessel function of the first kind of order $0$**.

### Verification (direct series check)

$$I_0(2\sqrt{x}) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{\left(\frac{2\sqrt{x}}{2}\right)^{2n}
}{(n!)^2} = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(\sqrt{x})^{2n}}{(n!)^2} = \sum_{n=0}^\infty
 \frac{x^n}{(n!)^2}. \quad\checkmark$$

The series expansion confirms the result directly: the substitution $t = 2\sqrt{
x}$ in the defining series of $I_0$ reproduces exactly the given power series.
