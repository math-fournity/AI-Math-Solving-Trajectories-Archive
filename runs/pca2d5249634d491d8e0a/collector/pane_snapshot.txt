## Solution

We seek a polynomial $P(x)$ of degree $2020$ with $P(n)=2^n$ for every $n\in\{0,
1,\dots,2020\}$.

**Claim.**
$$P(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{2020}\binom{x}{k},\qquad \binom{x}{k}:=\frac{x(x-1)\cdots(x-k
+1)}{k!},$$
is such a polynomial.

**Proof.**

*Polynomial nature and degree.* For each $k\ge 0$, $\binom{x}{k}$ is a polynomia
l in $x$ of degree exactly $k$ (its leading term is $x^k/k!$). In particular $\b
inom{x}{2020}$ has degree $2020$, and every other summand has degree $\le 2019$.
 Hence $P(x)$ is a polynomial of degree exactly $2020$.

*Agreement with $2^x$ at the prescribed points.* Let $n\in\{0,1,\dots,2020\}$. T
he classical binomial identity (a direct consequence of the binomial theorem app
lied to $(1+1)^n$) gives
$$2^n=(1+1)^n=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}.$$
For integer $n$ and $k>n$ we have $\binom{n}{k}=0$ (the falling factorial $n(n-1
)\cdots(n-k+1)$ contains the factor $0$). Therefore
$$\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{2020}\binom{n}{k}=P(n).$$
Combining the two displayed equations yields $P(n)=2^n$ for every $n=0,1,\dots,2
020$.

*Uniqueness (optional, confirming the construction is forced).* A polynomial of
degree $\le 2020$ is uniquely determined by its values at $2021$ distinct points
. Since $P$ has degree $2020\le 2020$ and matches $2^n$ at the $2021$ distinct p
oints $0,1,\dots,2020$, it is the unique such polynomial. ∎
