## Proof

Define $f(x) = x(4-x) = 4x - x^2$. The system is $q = f(p),\ r = f(q),\ p = f(r)
$, so $p$ is a periodic point of $f$ of period dividing $3$, and $p,q,r$ are req
uired to be **distinct**, so they form a genuine $3$-cycle.

**Conjugation to the Chebyshev map.** Substitute $x = 2 - y$. Then
$$f(2-y) = 4(2-y) - (2-y)^2 = 8 - 4y - (4 - 4y + y^2) = 4 - y^2.$$
Writing $y' = 2 - f(x)$, we get $y' = 2 - (4 - y^2) = y^2 - 2$. So the map $g(y)
 = y^2 - 2$ is conjugate to $f$ via $x = 2 - y$. Setting
$$p = 2 - a,\quad q = 2 - b,\quad r = 2 - c,$$
the system becomes $b = g(a),\ c = g(b),\ a = g(c)$, a $3$-cycle of $g$, and
$$p + q + r = 6 - (a+b+c).$$

**Real $3$-cycles lie in $[-2,2]$.** If $|y| > 2$, then $g(y) = y^2 - 2 > |y|$,
and iterating makes $|g^n(y)|$ grow without bound, so no periodic orbit can leav
e $[-2,2]$. The endpoints $y = \pm 2$ are fixed/pre-fixed to the fixed point $2$
. Hence every real periodic point satisfies $|y| \le 2$ and can be written as $y
 = 2\cos\theta$.

**Chebyshev identity.** $g(2\cos\theta) = 4\cos^2\theta - 2 = 2\cos(2\theta)$. T
herefore $g^n(2\cos\theta) = 2\cos(2^n\theta)$. The period-$3$ condition $g^3(y)
 = y$ becomes
$$\cos(8\theta) = \cos\theta \iff 8\theta = \pm\theta + 2k\pi,$$
giving $\theta = \tfrac{2k\pi}{7}$ or $\theta = \tfrac{2k\pi}{9}$.

**The $\tfrac{2k\pi}{7}$ family.** The orbit of $2\cos(2\pi/7)$ is
$$\{2\cos(2\pi/7),\ 2\cos(4\pi/7),\ 2\cos(8\pi/7)\},$$
and $g$ cycles them (since $2\cos(16\pi/7) = 2\cos(2\pi/7)$). The remaining valu
es $k=3,5,6$ give $2\cos(6\pi/7), 2\cos(10\pi/7), 2\cos(12\pi/7)$, which equal $
2\cos(8\pi/7), 2\cos(4\pi/7), 2\cos(2\pi/7)$ respectively (using $\cos(6\pi/7) =
 \cos(8\pi/7) = -\cos(\pi/7)$, etc.), so they form the **same** cycle. Thus ther
e is exactly one genuine $3$-cycle here. Its sum:
$$a+b+c = 2\bigl[\cos(2\pi/7)+\cos(4\pi/7)+\cos(8\pi/7)\bigr].$$
Using $\cos(8\pi/7) = \cos(6\pi/7)$ and the roots-of-unity identity $1 + 2\cos(2
\pi/7) + 2\cos(4\pi/7) + 2\cos(6\pi/7) = 0$,
$$a+b+c = -1, \qquad p+q+r = 6 - (-1) = \boxed{7}.$$

**The $\tfrac{2k\pi}{9}$ family.** The orbit of $2\cos(2\pi/9)$ is
$$\{2\cos(2\pi/9),\ 2\cos(4\pi/9),\ 2\cos(8\pi/9)\},$$
cycled by $g$ (since $2\cos(16\pi/9) = 2\cos(2\pi/9)$). The values $k=3,6$ give
$\theta = 2\pi/3, 4\pi/3$, i.e. $y = -1$, which is the **fixed point** $g(-1) =
-1$ — excluded. The other $k$ give the same single $3$-cycle. Its sum, using the
 $9$th-roots identity
$$1 + 2\cos(2\pi/9) + 2\cos(4\pi/9) + 2\cos(6\pi/9) + 2\cos(8\pi/9) = 0$$
with $\cos(6\pi/9) = \cos(2\pi/3) = -1/2$:
$$2\cos(2\pi/9) + 2\cos(4\pi/9) + 2\cos(8\pi/9) = 0,$$
so $a+b+c = 0$ and $p+q+r = 6 - 0 = \boxed{6}$.

**Distinctness.** In the first cycle, $2\cos(2\pi/7) \approx 1.247$, $2\cos(4\pi
/7) \approx -0.445$, $2\cos(8\pi/7) \approx -1.802$ are distinct. In the second,
 $2\cos(2\pi/9) \approx 1.532$, $2\cos(4\pi/9) \approx 0.347$, $2\cos(8\pi/9) \a
pprox -1.879$ are distinct. Hence $p,q,r$ are distinct in both cases.

**Exhaustion.** Every real $3$-cycle of $g$ corresponds to a solution of $\cos(8
\theta) = \cos\theta$ with $y = 2\cos\theta \in [-2,2]$, and we have enumerated
all such $\theta$ up to the cycle equivalence. The fixed points ($y = 2$ and $y
= -1$) are the only non-genuine period-$3$ points and were excluded. No other re
al $3$-cycles exist.

Therefore the possible values of $p + q + r$ are $\boxed{6}$ and $\boxed{7}$.
