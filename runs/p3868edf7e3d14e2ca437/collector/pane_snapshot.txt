## Proof

Let $f(x) = \mathbf{E}_x[T_{a,b}]$ denote the expected exit time when the Browni
an motion starts at $x \in [a,b]$, so that the desired quantity is $f(0)$.

**Step 1: Boundary conditions.** If $B_0 \in \{a,b\}$, the process is already ou
tside $(a,b)$, so $T_{a,b}=0$. Hence
$$f(a)=0, \qquad f(b)=0.$$

**Step 2: Differential equation via the infinitesimal generator.** The generator
 of standard Brownian motion is $\mathcal{L} = \tfrac{1}{2}\frac{d^2}{dx^2}$. By
 the Dynkin formula applied to the bounded stopping time $T_{a,b}$ (the exit tim
e from a bounded interval, which has finite expectation), for $x\in(a,b)$,
$$\mathbf{E}_x[B_{T_{a,b}}^2] - x^2 = \mathbf{E}_x\!\left[\int_0^{T_{a,b}} \math
cal{L}(B_t^2)\,dt\right] = \mathbf{E}_x\!\left[\int_0^{T_{a,b}} 1\,dt\right] = \
mathbf{E}_x[T_{a,b}]=f(x).$$

At the exit time, $B_{T_{a,b}}\in\{a,b\}$, so $B_{T_{a,b}}^2 \in \{a^2,b^2\}$. R
ather than computing the exit probabilities, it is cleaner to use the standard O
DE characterization: $f$ is $C^2$ on $(a,b)$ and satisfies
$$\tfrac{1}{2}f''(x) = -1, \qquad x\in(a,b),$$
with $f(a)=f(b)=0$. (The right-hand side $-1$ comes from $\mathcal{L}f = -1$, wh
ich is the standard Poisson equation $\mathcal{L}f = -1$ for the mean exit time.
)

**Step 3: Solve the ODE.** From $\tfrac{1}{2}f''(x)=-1$ we get $f''(x)=-2$, henc
e
$$f(x) = -x^2 + cx + d$$
for constants $c,d$.

Applying the boundary conditions:
- $f(a)=0:\quad -a^2 + ca + d = 0$,
- $f(b)=0:\quad -b^2 + cb + d = 0$.

Subtracting: $-(a^2-b^2) + c(a-b) = 0$, i.e. $-(a-b)(a+b) + c(a-b)=0$, so $c = a
+b$.

Substituting back: $d = a^2 - ca = a^2 - a(a+b) = -ab$.

Therefore
$$f(x) = -x^2 + (a+b)x - ab = -(x-a)(x-b).$$

**Step 4: Evaluate at the origin.** Since $B_0 = 0$,
$$\mathbf{E}[T_{a,b}] = f(0) = -ab.$$

Given $a<0<b$, we have $-ab>0$, confirming the expectation is positive, as requi
red.

$$\boxed{\mathbf{E}[T_{a,b}] = -ab}$$
