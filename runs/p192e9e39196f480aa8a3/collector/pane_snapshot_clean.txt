**Claim.** Yes. Every finite ring $R$ with identity satisfying $a+a=0$ for all $
a\in R$ is (isomorphic to) a subring of $\mathrm{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{F}_2)$
 for some $n$ (with the same identity).

**Proof.**

Let $R$ be a finite ring with $1$ such that $2a=0$ for every $a\in R$.

**Step 1: $R$ is an $\mathbb{F}_2$-algebra.**

Since $1+1=0$, the characteristic of $R$ is $2$. Hence the canonical map
$$\iota:\mathbb{F}_2\longrightarrow R,\qquad \bar 0\mapsto 0,\ \bar 1\mapsto 1$$
is a unital ring homomorphism (the prime subring of $R$ is $\{0,1\}\cong\mathbb{
F}_2$). This gives $R$ the structure of an $\mathbb{F}_2$-algebra: the scalar mu
ltiplication is $\bar\lambda\cdot a=\iota(\bar\lambda)\,a$.

**Step 2: $R$ is finite-dimensional over $\mathbb{F}_2$.**

The additive group $(R,+)$ satisfies $2a=0$ for all $a$, so it is an elementary
abelian $2$-group, i.e. a vector space over $\mathbb{F}_2$. Since $R$ is finite,
 $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_2}R=n<\infty$, and $|R|=2^n$.

**Step 3: The (left) regular representation embeds $R$ into $\mathrm{End}_{\math
bb{F}_2}(R)$.**

For each $a\in R$, define the left-multiplication map
$$L_a:R\longrightarrow R,\qquad L_a(x)=ax.$$
Because multiplication in $R$ is $\mathbb{F}_2$-bilinear (it is $\mathbb{Z}$-bil
inear and the $\mathbb{F}_2$-structure comes from the prime subring), each $L_a$
 is $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear, so $L_a\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}_2}(R)$.

Define
$$\varphi:R\longrightarrow \mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}_2}(R),\qquad \varphi(a)=L_a.
$$

*Ring homomorphism.* For $a,b\in R$ and $x\in R$:
- $L_{a+b}(x)=(a+b)x=ax+bx=L_a(x)+L_b(x)=(L_a+L_b)(x)$, so $\varphi(a+b)=\varphi
(a)+\varphi(b)$.
- $L_{ab}(x)=(ab)x=a(bx)=L_a(L_b(x))=(L_a\circ L_b)(x)$, so $\varphi(ab)=\varphi
(a)\varphi(b)$.
- $L_1(x)=1\cdot x=x=\mathrm{id}_R(x)$, so $\varphi(1)=\mathrm{id}_{R}$, the ide
ntity endomorphism.

Thus $\varphi$ is a unital ring homomorphism.

*Injectivity.* If $\varphi(a)=0$, then $L_a=0$, so $ax=0$ for all $x\in R$. Taki
ng $x=1$ gives $a\cdot 1=a=0$. Hence $\ker\varphi=\{0\}$.

**Step 4: Conclude.**

Choosing an $\mathbb{F}_2$-basis of $R$ identifies $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}_2}(
R)$ with $\mathrm{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{F}_2)$, where $n=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_2}R
$. Under this identification, $\varphi$ is an injective unital ring homomorphism
$$R\hookrightarrow \mathrm{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{F}_2),$$
i.e. $R$ is isomorphic to a subring of $\mathrm{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{F}_2)$
containing the identity matrix (the image of $\varphi(1)=\mathrm{id}_R$).

Therefore every finite ring with identity of characteristic $2$ embeds as a unit
al subring of a matrix ring over $\mathbb{F}_2$. $\blacksquare$
