## Proof

**Setup and definitions.** Let $G = \mathbb{Z}$ equipped with the discrete topol
ogy. The character group $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ consists of all continuous group
 homomorphisms $\chi: \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{T}$, where $\mathbb{T} = \{z \in \m
athbb{C} : |z| = 1\}$ is the circle group. The topology on $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
$ is the **compact-open topology**: a neighborhood subbasis at the trivial chara
cter $\mathbf{1}$ is given by
$$N(K, \varepsilon) = \{\chi \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}} : |\chi(n) - 1| < \varepsi
lon \text{ for all } n \in K\},$$
where $K \subseteq \mathbb{Z}$ is compact and $\varepsilon > 0$. Neighborhoods a
t any other $\chi_0$ are obtained by translation: $\chi_0 \cdot N(K,\varepsilon)
$.

---

**Step 1 — Identify the characters as a group.** Since $\mathbb{Z}$ is the free
abelian group on one generator $1$, any group homomorphism $\chi: \mathbb{Z} \to
 \mathbb{T}$ is completely determined by $\chi(1) = z \in \mathbb{T}$, via $\chi
(n) = z^n$. Conversely, every $z \in \mathbb{T}$ defines a character $\chi_z(n)
= z^n$. The map
$$\Phi: \widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \to \mathbb{T}, \qquad \Phi(\chi) = \chi(1)$$
is a group isomorphism: it is a homomorphism since $(\chi\psi)(1) = \chi(1)\psi(
1)$, injective since $\chi(1)$ determines $\chi$, and surjective by the observat
ion above.

---

**Step 2 — Compact subsets of $\mathbb{Z}$.** Since $\mathbb{Z}$ carries the dis
crete topology, every singleton $\{n\}$ is open. Any compact subset $K$ must be
finite: if $K$ were infinite, the open cover $\{\{n\} : n \in K\}$ admits no fin
ite subcover. Conversely, every finite subset is compact. Hence **compact = fini
te** in $\mathbb{Z}$.

---

**Step 3 — The compact-open topology coincides with the usual topology on $\math
bb{T}$.** Under the identification $\Phi$, a subbasic neighborhood of $1 \in \ma
thbb{T}$ is
$$N(K, \varepsilon) = \{z \in \mathbb{T} : |z^n - 1| < \varepsilon \text{ for al
l } n \in K\},$$
with $K$ finite and $\varepsilon > 0$.

*The compact-open topology is at least as fine as the usual topology.* Taking $K
 = \{1\}$ gives
$$N(\{1\}, \varepsilon) = \{z \in \mathbb{T} : |z - 1| < \varepsilon\},$$
which are exactly the usual Euclidean open arcs around $1$ in $\mathbb{T}$. Thes
e generate the usual topology.

*The compact-open topology is at most as fine as the usual topology.* For any fi
nite $K = \{n_1, \dots, n_k\}$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, the map $z \mapsto z^{n_i}
$ is continuous (in the usual topology) for each $i$. Hence the preimage $\{z \i
n \mathbb{T} : |z^{n_i} - 1| < \varepsilon\}$ is open in the usual topology, and
$$N(K, \varepsilon) = \bigcap_{i=1}^{k} \{z \in \mathbb{T} : |z^{n_i} - 1| < \va
repsilon\}$$
is a finite intersection of usual-open sets, hence usual-open. So every compact-
open subbasic set is open in the usual topology.

Combining both inclusions, the compact-open topology on $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ *
*equals** the usual (Euclidean subspace) topology on $\mathbb{T}$.

---

**Step 4 — Consistency check via Pontryagin duality.** A fundamental theorem of
Pontryagin duality states:

- The dual of a **discrete** LCA group is **compact**, and the dual of a **compa
ct** LCA group is **discrete**.

Since $\mathbb{Z}$ is discrete (and infinite, hence not compact), $\widehat{\mat
hbb{Z}}$ must be compact. The circle $\mathbb{T}$ with its usual topology is com
pact (closed and bounded in $\mathbb{C}$), confirming consistency. Moreover, $\w
idehat{\mathbb{T}} \cong \mathbb{Z}$ (discrete), since every character of $\math
bb{T}$ is of the form $z \mapsto z^n$ for a unique $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, and the c
ompact-open topology on $\widehat{\mathbb{T}}$ (with $\mathbb{T}$ compact) is di
screte — again consistent.

---

**Conclusion.** The character group $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, equipped with the co
mpact-open topology, is isomorphic as a topological group to the circle group $\
mathbb{T}$ with its usual compact topology. Explicitly, the isomorphism is $\chi
 \mapsto \chi(1)$, and the topology is the standard Euclidean subspace topology
inherited from $\mathbb{C}$.
