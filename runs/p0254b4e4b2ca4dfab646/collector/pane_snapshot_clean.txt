Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a real linear combination o
f polynomials with real, distinct, totally interlacing zeros must also have all
real zeros.

**Answer: No.** I'll prove this by explicit counterexample.

## Counterexample

Consider three quadratic polynomials:

$$p_1(z) = z(z-3), \quad p_2(z) = (z-1)(z-4), \quad p_3(z) = (z-2)(z-5).$$

**Each has real, distinct zeros:**
- $p_1$: zeros $0, 3$
- $p_2$: zeros $1, 4$
- $p_3$: zeros $2, 5$

**Total interlacing (every pair interlaces):**

| Pair | Zeros | Interlacing check |
|------|-------|-------------------|
| $p_1, p_2$ | $0,3$ and $1,4$ | $0 < 1 < 3 < 4$ ✓ |
| $p_1, p_3$ | $0,3$ and $2,5$ | $0 < 2 < 3 < 5$ ✓ |
| $p_2, p_3$ | $1,4$ and $2,5$ | $1 < 2 < 4 < 5$ ✓ |

Indeed, all six zeros form a single interlaced chain $0 < 1 < 2 < 3 < 4 < 5$, so
 the sequence is totally interlacing in the strongest possible sense.

**A linear combination with complex zeros:**

Take $c_1 = 1,\; c_2 = -\tfrac{3}{2},\; c_3 = 1$:

$$q(z) = p_1(z) - \tfrac{3}{2}\,p_2(z) + p_3(z).$$

Expanding:

$$q(z) = (z^2 - 3z) - \tfrac{3}{2}(z^2 - 5z + 4) + (z^2 - 7z + 10)$$

$$= \left(1 - \tfrac{3}{2} + 1\right)z^2 + \left(-3 + \tfrac{15}{2} - 7\right)z
+ (-6 + 10)$$

$$= \tfrac{1}{2}\,z^2 - \tfrac{5}{2}\,z + 4 = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(z^2 - 5z + 8\rig
ht).$$

The discriminant is:

$$\Delta = \left(-\tfrac{5}{2}\right)^2 - 4 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 4 = \tfrac{
25}{4} - 8 = -\tfrac{7}{4} < 0.$$

Since $\Delta < 0$, the polynomial $q$ has a **conjugate pair of complex zeros**
:

$$z = \frac{5 \pm i\sqrt{7}}{2},$$

which are **not real**.

## Why this happens

The classical theorem guarantees that for **two** polynomials $p, q$ with real,
simple, interlacing zeros, every combination $\alpha p + \beta q$ has real simpl
e zeros (Hermite–Biehler / Obreschkoff). However, this property does **not** ext
end to three or more polynomials. Total (pairwise) interlacing constrains each p
air, but a linear combination of three or more interlacing polynomials can "esca
pe" the real axis because the pairwise interlacing conditions are not jointly su
fficient to force real-rootedness of all combinations. The space spanned by the
polynomials is not, in general, a real-rooted subspace.
