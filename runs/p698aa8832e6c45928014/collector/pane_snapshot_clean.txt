## Solution

**Setup.** Let circle $\omega$ have center $O$ and radius $r$. Place $T$ (the ta
ngent point of line $\ell$) at $(r, 0)$, so $\ell$ is the vertical line $x = r$.
 Let $A = (r\cos\alpha,\, r\sin\alpha)$ and $B = (r\cos\beta,\, r\sin\beta)$ on
the circle.

**Tangent intersections.** The tangent to $\omega$ at angle $\theta$ has equatio
n $x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta = r$. Its intersection with $\ell: x = r$ gives $y
= r\tan(\theta/2)$. Setting $u = \tan(\alpha/2)$, $v = \tan(\beta/2)$:

$$D = (r,\, ru), \quad C = (r,\, rv), \quad T = (r, 0).$$

Since tangent lengths from an external point are equal: $DA = DT = r|u| = 3$ and
 $CB = CT = r|v| = 2$.

**Hub location.** The line through $A$ and $B$ meets $\ell$ at $P = (r, y_P)$. U
sing the half-angle parametrization $A = \bigl(r\tfrac{1-u^2}{1+u^2},\, \tfrac{2
ru}{1+u^2}\bigr)$, $B = \bigl(r\tfrac{1-v^2}{1+u^2},\, \tfrac{2rv}{1+v^2}\bigr)$
, a direct computation yields:

$$\boxed{y_P = \frac{2ruv}{u+v}}.$$

**Chord length.** $AB^2 = 4r^2\sin^2\!\tfrac{\alpha-\beta}{2} = \frac{4r^2(u-v)^
2}{(1+u^2)(1+v^2)} = 49.$

**Sign analysis.** If $u, v$ have the **same sign** ($u=3/r,\; v=2/r$): then $(u
-v)^2 = 1/r^2$ and $AB^2 = \frac{4}{(1+9/r^2)(1+4/r^2)} \le 4 < 49$, which is **
impossible**.

Therefore $u$ and $v$ have **opposite signs**: $u = 3/r,\; v = -2/r$ (or vice ve
rsa). Then:

$$uv = -\frac{6}{r^2}, \quad u+v = \frac{1}{r}, \quad y_P = \frac{2r \cdot (-6/r
^2)}{1/r} = -12.$$

So $PT = |y_P| = 12$.

**Consistency check.** With $u = 3/r,\, v = -2/r$: $(u-v)^2 = 25/r^2$, and $AB^2
 = \frac{100}{(1+9/r^2)(1+4/r^2)} = 49$ gives $(1+9/r^2)(1+4/r^2) = 100/49$. Set
ting $s = 1/r^2$: $36s^2 + 13s + 1 = 100/49$, i.e., $1764s^2 + 637s - 51 = 0$, y
ielding $s = 17/252 > 0$. Valid. ✓

**Power of a point.** Since $P$ lies on secant $AB$ beyond $B$ (order $A, B, P$)
, and $\ell$ is tangent at $T$:

$$PT^2 = PA \cdot PB.$$

With $BP = x$, we have $PA = x + 7$ and $PT = 12$:

$$x(x+7) = 144 \implies x^2 + 7x - 144 = 0 \implies (x+16)(x-9) = 0.$$

Since $x > 0$, we get $x = 9$.

**Verification.** $PA \cdot PB = 16 \times 9 = 144 = 12^2 = PT^2$. ✓  The parame
tric check confirms $P = A + \tfrac{272}{119}(B - A)$ with $t > 1$ (beyond $B$)
and $BP = \tfrac{153}{119} \times 7 = 9$. ✓

$$\boxed{BP = 9 \text{ kilometers}}$$
