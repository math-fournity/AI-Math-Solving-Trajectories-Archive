Looking at this problem, I need to find the probability that after 4 people each
 independently choose a block of 5 consecutive numbers from a circle of 10, some
 number is left uncovered.

**Setup.** Label the 10 blocks as $B_i = \{i, i+1, i+2, i+3, i+4\} \pmod{10}$ fo
r $i = 1, \ldots, 10$. Each person picks one block uniformly at random, so there
 are $10^4 = 10000$ equally likely outcomes. Let $A_k$ be the event that number
$k$ is uncovered (not in any chosen block).

**Key observation.** Number $k$ belongs to exactly 5 of the 10 blocks (those sta
rting at $k, k-1, k-2, k-3, k-4$), so $P(A_k) = (5/10)^4 = (1/2)^4 = 1/16$.

**Inclusion-Exclusion.** I need $P\!\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{10} A_k\right) = \sum_{
k=1}^{5} (-1)^{k+1} \sum_{|S|=k} \left(\frac{f(S)}{10}\right)^4$, where $f(S)$ =
 number of blocks avoiding all numbers in $S$ = number of length-5 circular arcs
 containing $S$ in their complement. This is nonzero only when $|S| \le 5$.

**Computing $f(S)$.** If $S$ has gaps $g_1, \ldots, g_{|S|}$ between consecutive
 elements on the circle (summing to $10 - |S|$), then $f(S) = \max(0, \max_i g_i
 - 4)$.

**$k=1$:** 10 subsets, each $f=5$: $\quad 10 \cdot (5/10)^4 = 10/16 = 625/1000$.

**$k=2$:** Pairs at circular distance $d$ have $f = 5-d$:
- $d=1$: 10 pairs, $f=4$; $d=2$: 10, $f=3$; $d=3$: 10, $f=2$; $d=4$: 10, $f=1$;
$d=5$: 5, $f=0$.

$$\frac{10 \cdot 4^4 + 10 \cdot 3^4 + 10 \cdot 2^4 + 10 \cdot 1^4}{10^4} = \frac
{2560+810+160+10}{10000} = \frac{354}{1000}$$

**$k=3$:** Gaps sum to 7. Counting triples by max gap:
- Max gap 7 (gaps $7,0,0$ — three consecutive): 10 triples, $f=3$
- Max gap 6 (gaps $6,1,0$): 20 triples, $f=2$
- Max gap 5 (gaps $5,2,0$ or $5,1,1$): 30 triples, $f=1$
- Max gap $\le 4$: 60 triples, $f=0$

$$\frac{10 \cdot 3^4 + 20 \cdot 2^4 + 30 \cdot 1^4}{10^4} = \frac{810+320+30}{10
000} = \frac{116}{1000}$$

**$k=4$:** Gaps sum to 6. Counting quadruples by max gap:
- Max gap 6 (gaps $6,0,0,0$ — four consecutive): 10, $f=2$
- Max gap 5 (gaps $5,1,0,0$): 30, $f=1$
- Max gap $\le 4$: 170, $f=0$

$$\frac{10 \cdot 2^4 + 30 \cdot 1^4}{10^4} = \frac{160+30}{10000} = \frac{19}{10
00}$$

**$k=5$:** Gaps sum to 5. Only max gap 5 (gaps $5,0,0,0,0$ — five consecutive):
10, $f=1$.

$$\frac{10 \cdot 1^4}{10^4} = \frac{1}{1000}$$

**Combining:**

$$p = \frac{625 - 354 + 116 - 19 + 1}{1000} = \frac{369}{1000}$$

**Verification of triple/quadruple counts.** For $k=3$: $10+20+30+60 = 120 = \bi
nom{10}{3}$ ✓. For $k=4$: $10+30+170 = 210 = \binom{10}{4}$ ✓. The gap-multiset
counting uses cyclic necklace enumeration: $\{6,1,0\}$ gives 2 cyclic classes ×
10 = 20; $\{5,2,0\}$ gives 2 × 10 = 20, $\{5,1,1\}$ gives 1 × 10 = 10; $\{5,1,0,
0\}$ gives 3 cyclic classes × 10 = 30.

**Sanity check.** Each number is uncovered with probability $1/16 = 0.0625$; if
independent, the union would be $\approx 0.625$. The events are positively corre
lated (uncovered numbers cluster in the complement arc), so the true probability
 $0.369 < 0.625$ is consistent.

$$10000p = 10000 \cdot \frac{369}{1000} = \boxed{3690}$$
