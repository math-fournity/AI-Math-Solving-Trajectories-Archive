## Analysis

The question asks whether $D \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{R}[x]$ is always a div
ision algebra, where $D$ is a finite-dimensional division algebra over $\mathbb{
R}$.

### Step 1: Classify the division algebras

By the **Frobenius theorem**, the only finite-dimensional division algebras over
 $\mathbb{R}$ are:
- $\mathbb{R}$ (the reals),
- $\mathbb{C}$ (the complex numbers),
- $\mathbb{H}$ (the quaternions).

### Step 2: Simplify the tensor product

Since $\mathbb{R}[x]$ is a free $\mathbb{R}$-module (with basis $\{1, x, x^2, \d
ots\}$), tensoring with $\mathbb{R}[x]$ over $\mathbb{R}$ yields the polynomial
ring:

$$D \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{R}[x] \cong D[x].$$

This isomorphism holds for any $\mathbb{R}$-algebra $D$: the map $d \otimes f(x)
 \mapsto d \cdot f(x)$ is an isomorphism of $\mathbb{R}$-algebras.

### Step 3: Is $D[x]$ a division algebra?

A **division algebra** requires every nonzero element to have a multiplicative i
nverse.

In $D[x]$, consider the element $x$. This is a nonzero element. Suppose there ex
isted $p(x) \in D[x]$ with $x \cdot p(x) = 1$. Writing $p(x) = a_0 + a_1 x + \cd
ots + a_n x^n$, we get:

$$x \cdot p(x) = a_0 x + a_1 x^2 + \cdots + a_n x^{n+1} = 1.$$

Comparing the constant term on both sides: the left side has constant term $0$,
while the right side has constant term $1$. This is a contradiction.

Therefore $x$ has no inverse in $D[x]$, so $D[x]$ is **not** a division algebra.

### Step 4: Concrete counterexample

Take $D = \mathbb{R}$ itself (which is certainly a finite-dimensional division a
lgebra over $\mathbb{R}$):

$$\mathbb{R} \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{R}[x] \cong \mathbb{R}[x].$$

$\mathbb{R}[x]$ is an integral domain but **not a field** (hence not a division
algebra), since $x$ is not invertible.

### Conclusion

The statement is **false**. For every finite-dimensional division algebra $D$ ov
er $\mathbb{R}$, the tensor product $D \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{R}[x] \cong
D[x]$ fails to be a division algebra because the indeterminate $x$ is a nonzero
non-unit.
