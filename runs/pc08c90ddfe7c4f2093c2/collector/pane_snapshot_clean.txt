## Solution

**Setup.** Consider
$$u_t - k^2 u_{xx} = g(x,t), \qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\; t>0, \qquad u(x,0)=\phi(x)
.$$

We assume $\phi$ and $g$ are sufficiently regular (e.g. $\phi$ bounded continuou
s, $g$ continuous with suitable growth) so that all integrals below converge and
 differentiation under the integral sign is justified.

**Step 1 — The heat kernel.** The fundamental solution (heat kernel) of the homo
geneous equation $u_t - k^2 u_{xx}=0$ is
$$\Phi(x,t) = \frac{1}{2k\sqrt{\pi t}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{4k^2 t}\right), \q
quad t>0.$$
It satisfies $\Phi_t - k^2 \Phi_{xx}=0$ for $t>0$ and $\Phi(\cdot,t)\to \delta_0
$ as $t\downarrow 0$ in the sense of distributions, i.e. $\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(
x-\xi,t)\,d\xi = 1$ for every $t>0$ and $\int \Phi(x-\xi,t)\psi(\xi)\,d\xi \to \
psi(x)$ as $t\downarrow 0$ for every bounded continuous $\psi$.

**Step 2 — Homogeneous part (initial data).** Define
$$u_1(x,t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \Phi(x-\xi,t)\,\phi(\xi)\,d\xi = \int_{-\in
fty}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2k\sqrt{\pi t}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{(x-\xi)^2}{4k^2 t}\righ
t)\phi(\xi)\,d\xi.$$
Since $\Phi$ solves the homogeneous heat equation in $(x,t)$ and the convolution
 preserves this, $u_1$ satisfies
$$(u_1)_t - k^2 (u_1)_{xx} = 0, \qquad u_1(x,0)=\phi(x).$$

**Step 3 — Nonhomogeneous part (Duhamel's principle).** Define
$$u_2(x,t) = \int_0^t \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \Phi(x-\xi,\,t-\tau)\,g(\xi,\tau)\
,d\xi\,d\tau.$$
We verify $u_2$ satisfies the nonhomogeneous equation with zero initial data.

- *Initial condition.* As $t\downarrow 0$, the outer integral collapses to a poi
nt, so $u_2(x,0)=0$.

- *PDE.* Differentiate under the integral sign. Using the Leibniz rule,
$$\frac{\partial u_2}{\partial t}(x,t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi(x-\xi,0)\,g
(\xi,t)\,d\xi + \int_0^t\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi_t(x-\xi,t-\tau)\,g(\xi,\tau)
\,d\xi\,d\tau.$$
The boundary term is $\int \Phi(x-\xi,0)g(\xi,t)\,d\xi = g(x,t)$ (since $\Phi(\c
dot,0)=\delta_0$). For the integral term, use $\Phi_t = k^2\Phi_{xx}$:
$$\int_0^t\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi_t(x-\xi,t-\tau)\,g(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi\,d\tau
= k^2\int_0^t\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi_{xx}(x-\xi,t-\tau)\,g(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi\,
d\tau = k^2 (u_2)_{xx}.$$
Therefore
$$(u_2)_t - k^2 (u_2)_{xx} = g(x,t), \qquad u_2(x,0)=0.$$

**Step 4 — Superposition.** By linearity, $u = u_1 + u_2$ satisfies both the PDE
 and the initial condition:
$$u_t - k^2 u_{xx} = 0 + g = g(x,t), \qquad u(x,0) = \phi(x) + 0 = \phi(x).$$

**Conclusion.** The solution is

$$\boxed{\displaystyle u(x,t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2k\sqrt{\pi t}}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{(x-\xi)^2}{4k^2 t}\right)\phi(\xi)\,d\xi \;+\; \int_0^t\!\int

4k^2(t-\tau)}\right)g(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi\,d\tau.}$$

Equivalently, writing $\Phi(x,t)=\frac{1}{2k\sqrt{\pi t}}e^{-x^2/(4k^2 t)}$,

$$u(x,t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi(x-\xi,t)\,\phi(\xi)\,d\xi + \int_0^t\!\in

The first term is the contribution of the initial data $\phi$ propagated by the
heat kernel; the second term is the Duhamel convolution accumulating the source

sumptions, and uniqueness follows from the maximum principle (or energy estimate
