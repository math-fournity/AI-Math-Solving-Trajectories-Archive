# Solution

We solve $u_t = k(u_{xx}+u_{yy}+u_{zz})$ on $0<x<L,\,0<y<H,\,0<z<W$ with $u(x,y,
z,0)=\alpha(x,y,z)$.

Using the hint, product solutions $u=\phi(x,y,z)h(t)$ satisfy $\dot h/h=-\lambda
 k$ and $\nabla^2\phi+\lambda\phi=0$. Hence $h(t)=e^{-\lambda kt}$, and separati
ng $\phi=X(x)Y(y)Z(z)$ with $X''/X=-\mu$, $Y''/Y=-\nu$, $Z''/Z=-\omega$, $\lambd
a=\mu+\nu+\omega$. The boundary conditions decouple into three one-dimensional e
igenvalue problems. The general solution is a superposition of the resulting eig
enfunctions, and the coefficients are fixed by the initial condition via orthogo
nality.

---

## Part (a)

Boundary conditions:
- $x$: $u(0,y,z,t)=0$, $u(L,y,z,t)=0$ $\Rightarrow$ $X(0)=X(L)=0$.
- $y$: $u_y(x,0,z,t)=0$, $u_y(x,H,z,t)=0$ $\Rightarrow$ $Y'(0)=Y'(H)=0$.
- $z$: $u_z(x,y,0,t)=0$, $u(x,y,W,t)=0$ $\Rightarrow$ $Z'(0)=0$, $Z(W)=0$.

**Eigenfunctions and eigenvalues.**

- $X_n(x)=\sin\!\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}$, $\mu_n=\left(\dfrac{n\pi}{L}\right)^2$, $n=1
,2,\dots$
- $Y_m(y)=\cos\!\dfrac{m\pi y}{H}$, $\nu_m=\left(\dfrac{m\pi}{H}\right)^2$, $m=0
,1,2,\dots$
- $Z_p(z)=\cos\!\dfrac{(2p+1)\pi z}{2W}$, $\omega_p=\left(\dfrac{(2p+1)\pi}{2W}\
right)^2$, $p=0,1,2,\dots$

(The $z$-problem: $Z=\cos(\sqrt\omega\,z)$ satisfies $Z'(0)=0$ automatically; $Z
(W)=0$ forces $\sqrt\omega\,W=(2p+1)\pi/2$.)

The 3D eigenvalues are
$$\lambda_{nmp}=\left(\frac{n\pi}{L}\right)^2+\left(\frac{m\pi}{H}\right)^2+\lef
t(\frac{(2p+1)\pi}{2W}\right)^2.$$

**Solution.**
$$u(x,y,z,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty} A_{nmp}\,
\sin\!\frac{n\pi x}{L}\cos\!\frac{m\pi y}{H}\cos\!\frac{(2p+1)\pi z}{2W}\,e^{-k\
lambda_{nmp}t},$$

with coefficients determined by $\alpha$:
$$A_{nmp}=\frac{4\,\varepsilon_m}{LHW}\int_0^W\!\!\int_0^H\!\!\int_0^L \alpha(x,
y,z)\,\sin\!\frac{n\pi x}{L}\cos\!\frac{m\pi y}{H}\cos\!\frac{(2p+1)\pi z}{2W}\,
dx\,dy\,dz,$$
where $\varepsilon_0=1$ and $\varepsilon_m=2$ for $m\ge 1$ (from $\int_0^H\cos^2
(m\pi y/H)\,dy=H/2$ for $m\ge1$, $=H$ for $m=0$; the $x$- and $z$-integrals each
 give $L/2$ and $W/2$).

**Long-time behavior.** Every eigenvalue is strictly positive: $\lambda_{nmp}\ge
 (n\pi/L)^2\ge(\pi/L)^2>0$ since $n\ge1$. Therefore every exponential factor $e^
{-k\lambda_{nmp}t}\to0$, and
$$\boxed{\lim_{t\to\infty}u(x,y,z,t)=0.}$$
Physically: three faces (or pairs of faces) are held at $0$ temperature (Dirichl
et on $x=0,x=L,z=W$), so all heat eventually drains out.

## Part (b)

All boundary conditions are Neumann (insulated):
- $X'(0)=X'(L)=0$,
- $Y'(0)=Y'(H)=0$,
- $Z'(0)=Z'(W)=0$.

**Eigenfunctions and eigenvalues.**
- $X_n(x)=\cos\!\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}$, $\mu_n=\left(\dfrac{n\pi}{L}\right)^2$, $n=0
,1,2,\dots$
- $Y_m(y)=\cos\!\dfrac{m\pi y}{H}$, $\nu_m=\left(\dfrac{m\pi}{H}\right)^2$, $m=0
,1,2,\dots$
- $Z_p(z)=\cos\!\dfrac{p\pi z}{W}$, $\omega_p=\left(\dfrac{p\pi}{W}\right)^2$, $
p=0,1,2,\dots$

$$\lambda_{nmp}=\left(\frac{n\pi}{L}\right)^2+\left(\frac{m\pi}{H}\right)^2+\lef
t(\frac{p\pi}{W}\right)^2.$$

**Solution.**
$$u(x,y,z,t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty} A_{nmp}\,
\cos\!\frac{n\pi x}{L}\cos\!\frac{m\pi y}{H}\cos\!\frac{p\pi z}{W}\,e^{-k\lambda
_{nmp}t},$$

with
$$A_{nmp}=\frac{\varepsilon_n\,\varepsilon_m\,\varepsilon_p}{LHW}\int_0^W\!\!\in
t_0^H\!\!\int_0^L \alpha(x,y,z)\,\cos\!\frac{n\pi x}{L}\cos\!\frac{m\pi y}{H}\co
s\!\frac{p\pi z}{W}\,dx\,dy\,dz,$$
where $\varepsilon_0=1$ and $\varepsilon_j=2$ for $j\ge1$.

**Long-time behavior.** The eigenvalue $\lambda_{000}=0$ (corresponding to the c
onstant eigenfunction $1$) does not decay; every other eigenvalue has $\lambda_{
nmp}>0$ and decays. Hence
$$\lim_{t\to\infty}u(x,y,z,t)=A_{000}=\frac{1}{LHW}\int_0^W\!\!\int_0^H\!\!\int_
0^L \alpha(x,y,z)\,dx\,dy\,dz.$$
$$\boxed{\displaystyle \lim_{t\to\infty}u(x,y,z,t)=\frac{1}{LWH}\iiint_{\Omega}\
alpha\,dV \quad\text{(the spatial average of the initial temperature).}}$$
Physically: with all six faces insulated, no heat escapes, so the temperature eq
uilibrates to the conserved mean of the initial distribution.
