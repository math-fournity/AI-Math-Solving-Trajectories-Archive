## Separation of Laplace's Equation in Spherical Coordinates

We start from

$$0 = \frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left(r^2 \frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\right
) + \frac{1}{\sin\phi}\frac{\partial}{\partial \phi}\!\left(\sin\phi \frac{\part
ial u}{\partial \phi}\right) + \frac{1}{\sin^2\phi}\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial
\theta^2}.$$

### Step 1 — Assume a product solution

Let

$$u(r,\phi,\theta) = R(r)\,\Phi(\phi)\,\Theta(\theta),$$

with none of $R,\Phi,\Theta$ identically zero. Substituting and dividing through
 by $R\,\Phi\,\Theta$ gives

$$0 = \underbrace{\frac{1}{R}\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(r^2\frac{dR}{dr}\right)}_{\text
{function of }r\text{ only}} \;+\; \underbrace{\frac{1}{\Phi\,\sin\phi}\frac{d}{
d\phi}\!\left(\sin\phi\frac{d\Phi}{d\phi}\right) + \frac{1}{\Theta\,\sin^2\phi}\
frac{d^2\Theta}{d\theta^2}}_{\text{function of }\phi,\theta\text{ only}}.$$

Since $r$ and $(\phi,\theta)$ are independent variables, each bracketed group mu
st be constant. By the standard convention we set the radial part equal to $+\el
l(\ell+1)$ (the sign/parametrization is chosen so the angular equations yield th
e familiar Legendre/trigonometric solutions):

$$\frac{1}{R}\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(r^2\frac{dR}{dr}\right) = \ell(\ell+1). \tag{1}
$$

The remaining two terms must then satisfy

$$\frac{1}{\Phi\,\sin\phi}\frac{d}{d\phi}\!\left(\sin\phi\frac{d\Phi}{d\phi}\rig
ht) + \frac{1}{\Theta\,\sin^2\phi}\frac{d^2\Theta}{d\theta^2} = -\ell(\ell+1).$$

### Step 2 — Separate the angular part

Multiply through by $\sin^2\phi$ and rearrange so that all $\phi$-dependence sit
s on one side and all $\theta$-dependence on the other:

$$\frac{\sin\phi}{\Phi}\frac{d}{d\phi}\!\left(\sin\phi\frac{d\Phi}{d\phi}\right)
 + \ell(\ell+1)\sin^2\phi \;=\; -\frac{1}{\Theta}\frac{d^2\Theta}{d\theta^2}.$$

The left side depends only on $\phi$, the right only on $\theta$; both equal a c
ommon constant, which we denote $m^2$:

$$-\frac{1}{\Theta}\frac{d^2\Theta}{d\theta^2} = m^2. \tag{2}$$

### Step 3 — The three ordinary differential equations

**Radial equation.** From (1),

$$\boxed{\,r^2\frac{d^2R}{dr^2} + 2r\frac{dR}{dr} - \ell(\ell+1)\,R = 0\,}. \tag
{I}$$

This is a Cauchy–Euler equation with general solution $R(r) = A\,r^{\ell} + B\,r
^{-(\ell+1)}$.

**Azimuthal equation.** From (2),

$$\boxed{\,\frac{d^2\Theta}{d\theta^2} + m^2\,\Theta = 0\,}. \tag{II}$$

With periodic boundary conditions $\Theta(\theta+2\pi)=\Theta(\theta)$, the cons
tant $m$ is forced to be an integer and $\Theta(\theta)=C\cos(m\theta)+D\sin(m\t
heta)$.

**Polar (associated Legendre) equation.** Substituting (2) back into the $\phi$-
equation gives

$$\frac{\sin\phi}{\Phi}\frac{d}{d\phi}\!\left(\sin\phi\frac{d\Phi}{d\phi}\right)
 + \ell(\ell+1)\sin^2\phi = m^2,$$

i.e.

$$\frac{1}{\sin\phi}\frac{d}{d\phi}\!\left(\sin\phi\frac{d\Phi}{d\phi}\right) +
\left[\ell(\ell+1) - \frac{m^2}{\sin^2\phi}\right]\Phi = 0.$$

Equivalently, with $x=\cos\phi$,

$$\boxed{\,\frac{d}{dx}\!\left[(1-x^2)\frac{d\Phi}{dx}\right] + \left[\ell(\ell+
1) - \frac{m^2}{1-x^2}\right]\Phi = 0\,}. \tag{III}$$

This is the **associated Legendre equation**; its regular solutions are the asso
ciated Legendre functions $P_\ell^m(\cos\phi)$, requiring $\ell=0,1,2,\dots$ and
 $|m|\le \ell$ for boundedness at the poles.

### Summary

The three ODEs produced by separation of variables $u=R(r)\Phi(\phi)\Theta(\thet
a)$ are:

| Equation | ODE |
|---|---|
| **(I) Radial** | $\displaystyle r^2 R'' + 2r R' - \ell(\ell+1)R = 0$ |
| **(II) Azimuthal** | $\displaystyle \Theta'' + m^2\Theta = 0$ |
| **(III) Polar** | $\displaystyle \frac{1}{\sin\phi}\frac{d}{d\phi}\!\bigl(\sin
\phi\,\Phi'\bigr) + \Bigl[\ell(\ell+1)-\frac{m^2}{\sin^2\phi}\Bigr]\Phi = 0$ |

with separation constants $\ell(\ell+1)$ and $m^2$. The full separated solutions
 are the **spherical harmonics** $Y_\ell^m(\phi,\theta)=P_\ell^m(\cos\phi)\,e^{i
m\theta}$ coupled with the radial powers $r^\ell$ and $r^{-(\ell+1)}$.
