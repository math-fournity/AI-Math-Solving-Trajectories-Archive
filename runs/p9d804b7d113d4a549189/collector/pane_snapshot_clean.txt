## Proof

Let $A$ be a commutative Banach algebra over $\mathbb{R}$ (or $\mathbb{C}$), wit
h norm $\|\cdot\|$ satisfying $\|ab\| \le \|a\|\,\|b\|$ for all $a,b \in A$. Def
ine

$$F : A \to A, \qquad F[u] = u^{2}.$$

We claim that $F$ is Fréchet differentiable at every $u \in A$, and that its Fré
chet derivative at $u$ is the bounded linear operator

$$DF[u] : A \to A, \qquad DF[u](h) = 2\,u\,h,$$

where the product is the algebra product of $A$.

---

### Step 1. Candidate for the derivative

Fix $u \in A$. Define $L_u : A \to A$ by $L_u(h) = 2uh$. We verify it is a bound
ed linear operator.

- **Linearity.** For $h_1, h_2 \in A$ and $\alpha, \beta$ scalars,
  $$L_u(\alpha h_1 + \beta h_2) = 2u(\alpha h_1 + \beta h_2) = \alpha\,(2uh_1) +
 \beta\,(2uh_2) = \alpha L_u(h_1) + \beta L_u(h_2),$$
  using bilinearity (distributivity + scalar compatibility) of the algebra produ
ct.

- **Boundedness.** Using submultiplicativity of the norm,
  $$\|L_u(h)\| = \|2uh\| = |2|\,\|uh\| \le 2\,\|u\|\,\|h\|.$$
  Hence $L_u$ is bounded with operator norm $\|L_u\| \le 2\|u\|$.

So $L_u \in \mathcal{L}(A,A)$, the space of bounded linear maps $A \to A$, which
 is the correct codomain for a Fréchet derivative.

---

### Step 2. Verification of the Fréchet derivative definition

We must show

$$\lim_{\|h\|\to 0} \frac{\big\|F[u+h] - F[u] - L_u(h)\big\|}{\|h\|} = 0.$$

Compute the numerator. Using the algebra product (bilinearity) and **commutativi
ty** ($uh = hu$):

$$F[u+h] = (u+h)^{2} = (u+h)(u+h) = u^{2} + uh + hu + h^{2}.$$

Since $A$ is commutative, $uh = hu$, so

$$(u+h)^{2} = u^{2} + 2uh + h^{2}.$$

Therefore

$$F[u+h] - F[u] - L_u(h) = \big(u^{2} + 2uh + h^{2}\big) - u^{2} - 2uh = h^{2}.$
$

Hence the Fréchet quotient is

$$\frac{\big\|F[u+h] - F[u] - L_u(h)\big\|}{\|h\|} = \frac{\|h^{2}\|}{\|h\|} \le
 \frac{\|h\|^{2}}{\|h\|} = \|h\| \xrightarrow{\|h\|\to 0} 0,$$

where we used submultiplicativity $\|h^{2}\| = \|h\cdot h\| \le \|h\|^{2}$.

---

### Step 3. Conclusion

The limit is $0$, so by definition $F$ is Fréchet differentiable at $u$, and

$$\boxed{\,DF[u](h) = 2\,u\,h\,}.$$

Equivalently, $DF[u] = 2\,M_u$, where $M_u : A \to A$ is the multiplication oper
ator $M_u(h) = uh$.

---

### Remark on commutativity

The commutativity hypothesis is exactly what collapses the two cross terms $uh +
 hu$ into $2uh$. In a *non*-commutative Banach algebra the same computation give
s the derivative $DF[u](h) = uh + hu$ (the symmetric product), and the term $2uh
$ would be wrong. Thus commutativity is used precisely once, in Step 2, to ident
ify $uh + hu = 2uh$.
