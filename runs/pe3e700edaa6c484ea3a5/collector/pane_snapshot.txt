## Solution

We seek a natural number $n$ with $|\sin(in)| > 10{,}000$.

**Key identity.** For real $x$, $\sin(ix) = i\sinh(x)$. Hence
$$|\sin(in)| = |i\sinh(n)| = \sinh(n) = \frac{e^{n}-e^{-n}}{2}.$$

So we need $\sinh(n) > 10{,}000$, i.e. (since $e^{-n}>0$) it suffices to find $n
$ with $e^{n} > 20{,}001$.

**Claim.** $n = 10$ works.

**Proof of claim.** We show $e^{10} > 20{,}001$ using only elementary bounds.

*Step 1: $e > 2.7$.* From the series $e = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}$,
$$e > 1 + 1 + \tfrac12 + \tfrac16 + \tfrac1{24} + \tfrac1{120}
= \frac{120+120+60+20+5+1}{120} = \frac{326}{120} = 2.71\overline{6} > 2.7.$$

*Step 2: $e^{10} > 2.7^{10}$.* Since $e > 2.7 > 0$, raising to the 10th power pr
eserves the inequality.

*Step 3: bound $2.7^{10}$ from below.* Compute
$$2.7^5 = 2.7^2 \cdot 2.7^2 \cdot 2.7 = 7.29 \cdot 7.29 \cdot 2.7 = 53.1441 \cdo
t 2.7 = 143.48907.$$
Since $143.48907 > 143$,
$$2.7^{10} = (2.7^5)^2 > 143^2 = 20\,449.$$

Combining,
$$e^{10} > 2.7^{10} > 20\,449 > 20\,001.$$

*Step 4: conclude.* Since $0 < e^{-10} < 1$,
$$\sinh(10) = \frac{e^{10}-e^{-10}}{2} > \frac{20\,449 - 1}{2} = \frac{20\,448}{
2} = 10\,224 > 10\,000.$$

Therefore
$$|\sin(10i)| = \sinh(10) > 10\,000.$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{n = 10}$.
