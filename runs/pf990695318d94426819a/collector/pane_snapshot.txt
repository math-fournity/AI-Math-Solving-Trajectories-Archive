## Solution

**The statement is TRUE.** For almost all $x \in [0,1]$, $f(x) \in [\liminf f_n(
x),\, \limsup f_n(x)]$.

### Proof

Set $\varphi(x) = \liminf_{n\to\infty} f_n(x)$ and $\psi(x) = \limsup_{n\to\inft
y} f_n(x)$. Since $|f_n|_{L_\infty} \le M$, we have $-M \le \varphi \le \psi \le
 M$, so $\varphi, \psi \in L_\infty([0,1]) \subset L_2([0,1])$.

**Step 1: $f \in L_\infty$ with $\|f\|_\infty \le M$.** For any measurable $E \s
ubset [0,1]$, taking $g = \mathbf{1}_E \in L_2$ in the weak convergence gives $\
int_E f = \lim_n \int_E f_n \le M|E|$. Taking $E = \{f > M\}$ yields $\int_E f \
le M|E|$ while $f > M$ on $E$ forces $\int_E f > M|E|$ if $|E|>0$; hence $|E|=0$
. Similarly $f \ge -M$ a.e. Thus $\|f\|_\infty \le M$, so $f \in L_2$.

**Step 2: Fatou with a non-negative weight.** Let $g \in L_2([0,1])$, $g \ge 0$.
 Since $f_n \ge -M$ a.e., we have $g\,f_n \ge -Mg$, and $Mg \in L_1([0,1])$ (as
$g \in L_2 \subset L_1$ on a finite-measure space). Fatou's lemma applies:

$$\int_0^1 \liminf_{n} \big(g(x)\,f_n(x)\big)\,dx \;\le\; \liminf_{n} \int_0^1 g
(x)\,f_n(x)\,dx.$$

Since $g \ge 0$, $\liminf(g\,f_n) = g\,\liminf f_n = g\,\varphi$. By weak conver
gence in $L_2$ (with test function $g \in L_2$), $\int g\,f_n \to \int g\,f$. Th
erefore:

$$\int_0^1 g\,\varphi \;\le\; \int_0^1 g\,f \qquad \text{for all } g \ge 0,\; g
\in L_2. \tag{$\ast$}$$

Applying the same argument to $-f_n$ (using $\liminf(-f_n) = -\limsup f_n = -\ps
i$):

$$\int_0^1 g\,(-\psi) \;\le\; \liminf_n \int_0^1 g\,(-f_n) = -\int_0^1 g\,f,$$

hence

$$\int_0^1 g\,\psi \;\ge\; \int_0^1 g\,f \qquad \text{for all } g \ge 0,\; g \in
 L_2. \tag{$\ast\ast$}$$

**Step 3: Conclude $\varphi \le f \le \psi$ a.e.** From $(\ast)$: $\int g\,(\var
phi - f) \le 0$ for all $g \ge 0$, $g \in L_2$. Since $\varphi - f \in L_\infty
\subset L_2$, choose $g = \max(\varphi - f,\,0) \ge 0$, which belongs to $L_2$.
Then

$$\int_0^1 \max(\varphi - f,\,0)^2\,dx \;\le\; 0,$$

so $\max(\varphi - f,\,0) = 0$ a.e., i.e., $\varphi \le f$ a.e.

From $(\ast\ast)$: $\int g\,(\psi - f) \ge 0$ for all $g \ge 0$, $g \in L_2$. Ch
oose $g = \max(f - \psi,\,0) \ge 0 \in L_2$. Then

$$\int_0^1 \max(f - \psi,\,0)\,(\psi - f)\,dx \;=\; -\int_0^1 \max(f-\psi,\,0)^2
\,dx \;\ge\; 0,$$

so $\max(f - \psi,\,0) = 0$ a.e., i.e., $f \le \psi$ a.e.

Combining: $\varphi(x) \le f(x) \le \psi(x)$ for almost every $x \in [0,1]$, whi
ch is exactly

$$f(x) \in \big[\liminf_{n\to\infty} f_n(x),\;\limsup_{n\to\infty} f_n(x)\big] \
quad \text{for a.e. } x \in [0,1].$$

**Key ingredients:** The uniform $L_\infty$ bound provides the integrable lower
bound $-Mg$ needed to apply Fatou's lemma to the weighted sequence $g\,f_n$; the
 weak $L_2$ convergence identifies the limit of $\int g\,f_n$ as $\int g\,f$ for
 every $g \in L_2$; testing the resulting integral inequalities against $g = (\t
ext{positive part})^+$ forces the pointwise ordering a.e.

### PROOF COMPLETE
