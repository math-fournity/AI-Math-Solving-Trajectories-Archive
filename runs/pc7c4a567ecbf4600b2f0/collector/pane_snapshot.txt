## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, such a polynomial exists.

**Construction:** Consider the polynomial
$$p(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 1 \in \mathbb{R}[x, y].$$

**Verification:** Evaluating at $(x, y) = (\sin 1, \cos 1)$:
$$p(\sin 1, \cos 1) = \sin^2(1) + \cos^2(1) - 1.$$

By the Pythagorean identity, which holds for every real argument,
$$\sin^2(\theta) + \cos^2(\theta) = 1 \quad \text{for all } \theta \in \mathbb{R
},$$

we obtain, setting $\theta = 1$ (in radians),
$$\sin^2(1) + \cos^2(1) = 1.$$

Therefore,
$$p(\sin 1, \cos 1) = 1 - 1 = 0.$$

**Conclusion:** The polynomial $p(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 1 \in \mathbb{R}[x, y]$ sa
tisfies $p(\sin 1, \cos 1) = 0$. Hence such a polynomial exists. $\blacksquare$
