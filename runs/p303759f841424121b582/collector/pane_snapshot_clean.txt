## Proof

Let $G = SL(2, F_q)$ and let $B$ denote the **Borel subgroup** (standard upper t
riangular subgroup) of $G$:

$$B = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & a^{-1} \end{pmatrix} \;\middle|\; a \
in F_q^*,\; b \in F_q \right\}.$$

Every element of $B$ has determinant $a \cdot a^{-1} - b \cdot 0 = 1$, so $B \su
bseteq SL(2, F_q)$. One verifies $B$ is closed under multiplication and inversio
n, hence a subgroup.

### The standard Borel has a common eigenvector

Let $e_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \in F_q^2$. For any $M = \begin{
pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & a^{-1} \end{pmatrix} \in B$:

$$M\, e_1 = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & a^{-1} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1
\\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} a \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = a \begin{pmatrix} 1
\\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = a\, e_1.$$

Thus $e_1$ is an eigenvector of $M$ with eigenvalue $a$. Since this holds for **
every** $M \in B$, the vector $e_1$ is a **common eigenvector** for the entire s
ubgroup $B$. (Equivalently, $B$ fixes the line $\langle e_1 \rangle$, the standa
rd flag $0 \subset \langle e_1 \rangle \subset F_q^2$.)

### Any conjugate of $B$ has a common eigenvector

Let $g \in SL(2, F_q)$ and consider the conjugate subgroup $B' = gBg^{-1}$. Any
element of $B'$ has the form $gNg^{-1}$ for some $N \in B$. Set $v = g\, e_1$. T
hen for any $M' = gNg^{-1} \in B'$:

$$M'\, v = gNg^{-1}(g\,e_1) = gN e_1 = g(\lambda_N\, e_1) = \lambda_N\, (g\,e_1)
 = \lambda_N\, v,$$

where $\lambda_N$ is the eigenvalue of $N$ corresponding to $e_1$ (which exists
by the argument above). Therefore $v = ge_1$ is a **common eigenvector** for the
 conjugate subgroup $gBg^{-1}$.

### Conclusion

**Yes**, all matrices of the upper triangular subgroup $B$ of $SL(2, F_q)$ share
 a common eigenvector (namely $e_1$), and likewise every conjugate $gBg^{-1}$ sh
ares a common eigenvector (namely $ge_1$). This holds for every finite field $F_
q$ regardless of characteristic, since the argument uses only the upper-triangul
ar structure and conjugation, with no dependence on the field's properties beyon
d it being a field.

The geometric content: the Borel subgroup is precisely the stabilizer of a compl
ete flag (a 1-dimensional subspace), and conjugating corresponds to moving that
flag. The common eigenvector is the generator of the 1-dimensional subspace in t
he flag.
