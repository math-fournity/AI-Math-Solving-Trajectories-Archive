## Solution

**Claim:** The chromatic number of $G$ is $n$.

**Lower bound ($\chi(G) \geq n$).**

Consider the family of singletons $\mathcal{F} = \big\{\{1\}, \{2\}, \ldots, \{n
\}\big\}$. For any distinct $i, j \in [n]$, we have $\{i\} \cap \{j\} = \emptyse
t$, so every pair of vertices in $\mathcal{F}$ is adjacent. Thus $\mathcal{F}$ i
s a clique of size $n$, giving
$$\chi(G) \geq \omega(G) \geq n.$$

**Upper bound ($\chi(G) \leq n$).**

Define a coloring $c : 2^{[n]}\setminus\emptyset \to [n]$ by
$$c(A) = \min(A).$$
This uses at most $n$ colors (the values $1, 2, \ldots, n$).

To verify it is proper: suppose $A$ and $B$ are adjacent, i.e., $A \cap B = \emp
tyset$. Then $\min(A) \in A$ and $\min(B) \in B$. Since $A \cap B = \emptyset$,
no element belongs to both, so in particular $\min(A) \neq \min(B)$. Hence $c(A)
 \neq c(B)$, and the coloring is proper.

Therefore $\chi(G) \leq n$.

**Conclusion.** Combining both bounds,
$$\chi(G) = n.$$
