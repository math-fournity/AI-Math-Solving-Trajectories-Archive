## Graph Reformulation

Define a directed graph $G_n$ on vertices $\{1, \ldots, n-1\}$ with edge $i \to
j$ iff $j \equiv -2i \pmod{n}$, $j \neq 0$, and $j \neq i$ (equivalently $n \nmi
d 2i$ and $n \nmid 3i$).

The existence of valid $x_1, \ldots, x_{n-1}$ is equivalent to $G_n$ being a **D
AG**:
- **Cycle $\Rightarrow$ impossible:** A cycle $i_1 \to \cdots \to i_k \to i_1$ f
orces $x_{i_1} < \cdots < x_{i_k} < x_{i_1}$, contradiction.
- **DAG $\Rightarrow$ possible:** Topologically sort and set $x_v = $ position o
f $v$.

## Characterizing Acyclicity

The map $f(i) = (-2i) \bmod n$ makes $G_n$ a **functional graph** (out-degree $\
leq 1$). The orbit of $i$ is $i, f(i), f^2(i), \ldots$ in $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z
}$, which must eventually cycle.

**Key analysis.** Fix $i$ with $d = \gcd(i,n)$, and write $n/d = 2^t \cdot m$ wi
th $m$ odd. By CRT, $\mathbb{Z}/(n/d)\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2^t\mathbb{Z} \
times \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$:

- **Mod $2^t$:** After $t$ steps, $(-2)^{t+k} i' \equiv 0 \pmod{2^t}$ for all $k
 \geq 0$.
- **Mod $m$:** Since $\gcd(-2, m) = 1$, the values $(-2)^k i' \bmod m$ cycle wit
h period $\operatorname{ord}_m(-2)$.

**Three cases:**

1. **$m = 1$** (i.e., $n/d$ is a power of 2): The orbit reaches $0 \pmod{n}$. No
 cycle among nonzero vertices.

2. **$m = 3$:** Since $-2 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$, $\operatorname{ord}_3(-2) = 1$. Th
e orbit reaches a **fixed point** $j$ with $f(j)=j$ (i.e., $3j \equiv 0 \pmod{n}
$, $j \neq 0$). This self-loop is **excluded** from $G_n$, so no cycle.

3. **$m > 3$ (odd):** $\operatorname{ord}_m(-2) \geq 2$, producing a genuine cyc
le of length $\geq 2$. All cycle elements are nonzero (orbit never hits 0 since
$m \nmid 2^k$) and distinct (distinct mod $m$ by CRT), with no fixed points (cyc
le length $\geq 2$). **This creates a cycle in $G_n$.**

**Crucial observation:** As $i$ ranges over $\{1, \ldots, n-1\}$, the value $n/\
gcd(i,n)$ ranges over **all divisors $e > 1$ of $n$** (for any such $e$, take $i
 = n/e$; then $\gcd(i,n) = n/e$).

Therefore: $G_n$ is acyclic **iff every divisor $e > 1$ of $n$ has odd part in $
\{1, 3\}$**.

This is equivalent to: **the odd part of $n$ is $1$ or $3$**, i.e.,
$$n = 2^s \quad \text{or} \quad n = 3 \cdot 2^s \quad \text{for some } s \geq 0,
 \quad n \geq 2.$$

**Verification of small cases:**
- $n=2$: vertex $\{1\}$, $f(1)=0$, no edges. ✓
- $n=3$: $f(1)=f(2)$ are self-loops (excluded). No edges. ✓
- $n=5$: cycle $1\to3\to4\to2\to1$. ✗
- $n=6=3\cdot2$: edges $1\to4$ (self-loop, stop), $5\to2$ (self-loop, stop), $3\
to0$. No cycle. ✓
- $n=9=3^2$: cycle $1\to7\to4\to1$. ✗
- $n=12=3\cdot4$: all orbits reach self-loops or 0. ✓

## Computing the Sum

$$S = \{2^s : s \geq 1\} \cup \{3 \cdot 2^s : s \geq 0\}$$

Since $2^s < 3 \cdot 2^{s-1} < 2^{s+1}$ (because $2 < 3 < 4$), the sorted sequen
ce interleaves:

$$2,\ 3,\ 4,\ 6,\ 8,\ 12,\ 16,\ 24,\ 32,\ 48,\ 64,\ 96,\ \ldots$$

The **smallest 10 elements** are: $2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48$.

$$\text{Sum} = 2+3+4+6+8+12+16+24+32+48 = 155$$
