## Solution

We compute the surface integral $\iint_S \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$, where $S
= \partial E$ is the closed boundary of the solid $E$ enclosed by the two parabo
loids, with outward orientation. Since $S$ is a closed surface bounding a solid
$E$ and $\mathbf{F}$ has smooth components, the **Divergence Theorem** applies:

$$\iint_S \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iiint_E (\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F})\, dV.$$

### Step 1. Compute the divergence

With $\mathbf{F}(x,y,z) = \langle x^3+y^2,\; 2yz+e^z,\; y^2-z^2\rangle$:

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x^3+y^2) + \frac{\partial}
{\partial y}(2yz+e^z) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(y^2-z^2) = 3x^2 + 2z - 2z =
3x^2.$$

The $z$-terms cancel, leaving simply $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F} = 3x^2$.

### Step 2. Describe the region $E$

The two paraboloids are $z = x^2+y^2$ (lower) and $z = 12-2x^2-2y^2$ (upper). Th
eir intersection satisfies

$$x^2+y^2 = 12-2x^2-2y^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; 3(x^2+y^2)=12 \;\Longrightarrow\; x
^2+y^2=4.$$

So the projection of $E$ onto the $xy$-plane is the disk $D = \{(x,y): x^2+y^2\l
e 4\}$, and for each $(x,y)\in D$,

$$x^2+y^2 \;\le\; z \;\le\; 12-2x^2-2y^2.$$

### Step 3. Set up the triple integral in cylindrical coordinates

Let $x = r\cos\theta$, $y = r\sin\theta$, $x^2 = r^2\cos^2\theta$, $dV = r\,dz\,
dr\,d\theta$. The bounds are

$$0\le \theta\le 2\pi,\quad 0\le r\le 2,\quad r^2\le z\le 12-2r^2.$$

Thus

$$\iiint_E 3x^2\,dV = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^2\!\!\int_{r^2}^{12-2r^2} 3r^2\cos
^2\theta\cdot r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta.$$

### Step 4. Evaluate (the integral separates)

Integrating in $z$ first:

$$\int_{r^2}^{12-2r^2} dz = (12-2r^2)-r^2 = 12-3r^2.$$

So the integral factors as

$$\left(\int_0^{2\pi} 3\cos^2\theta\,d\theta\right)\!\left(\int_0^2 r^3(12-3r^2)
\,dr\right).$$

**Angular factor:**

$$\int_0^{2\pi} 3\cos^2\theta\,d\theta = 3\cdot\pi = 3\pi,$$

using $\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^2\theta\,d\theta = \pi$.

**Radial factor:**

$$\int_0^2 r^3(12-3r^2)\,dr = \int_0^2 (12r^3-3r^5)\,dr = \left[3r^4-\frac{r^6}{
2}\right]_0^2 = 3\cdot 16 - \frac{64}{2} = 48-32 = 16.$$

### Step 5. Combine

$$\iint_S \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = (3\pi)\cdot(16) = 48\pi.$$

### Final Answer

$$\boxed{\iint_S \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = 48\pi}$$
