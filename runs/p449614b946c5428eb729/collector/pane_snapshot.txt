**Answer: No.** A smooth manifold cannot have two open sets homeomorphic to open
 subsets of Euclidean spaces of different dimensions. The dimension of a smooth
manifold is a topological invariant, enforced by Brouwer's *Invariance of Domain
*.

---

### Proof

Let $M$ be a smooth manifold. By definition, $M$ is a topological manifold of so
me fixed dimension $m$: it is second-countable, Hausdorff, and every point has a
n open neighborhood homeomorphic to an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^m$.

**Claim.** Every open subset $W \subseteq M$ is itself an $m$-dimensional topolo
gical manifold.

*Proof of claim.* $W$ is open in $M$, so it is second-countable and Hausdorff (t
hese properties are inherited by subspaces). For any $p \in W$, take a coordinat
e chart $(O, \varphi)$ of $M$ around $p$ with $O \subseteq W$ (intersect a coord
inate neighborhood of $p$ in $M$ with $W$ if necessary). Then $(O, \varphi)$ is
also a chart for $W$, mapping $O$ homeomorphically onto an open subset of $\math
bb{R}^m$. Hence $W$ is an $m$-manifold. $\square$

Now suppose, for contradiction, that $M$ admits open sets $U, V \subseteq M$ wit
h
$$U \cong A \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m \text{ (open)}, \qquad V \cong B \subseteq \m
athbb{R}^n \text{ (open)}, \qquad n \neq m.$$

Since $V$ is open in $M$, the Claim shows $V$ is an $m$-dimensional manifold. Th
e homeomorphism $V \cong B$ transports this local structure to $B$, so $B$ is al
so an $m$-dimensional topological manifold.

On the other hand, $B$ is an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$, so $B$ is an $n$-dim
ensional topological manifold: every point of $B$ has a small open ball (in $\ma
thbb{R}^n$) contained in $B$, and such a ball is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^n$.

Thus $B$ is simultaneously an $m$-manifold and an $n$-manifold. Pick any $b \in
B$. There exist open neighborhoods $O_1, O_2$ of $b$ in $B$ with
$$O_1 \cong \mathbb{R}^m \qquad \text{and} \qquad O_2 \cong \mathbb{R}^n.$$
Shrinking to $O = O_1 \cap O_2$ (an open neighborhood of $b$), and choosing a sm
all open ball $D \subseteq O$ around $b$ (open in $\mathbb{R}^n$, hence $D \cong
 \mathbb{R}^n$), we find that $D$ is an open subset of $O_1 \cong \mathbb{R}^m$,
 so $D$ is homeomorphic to an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^m$, while also $D \cong
 \mathbb{R}^n$ (being an open ball in $\mathbb{R}^n$).

This contradicts **Invariance of Dimension** (Brouwer, 1912), the standard corol
lary of Invariance of Domain:

> **Theorem (Invariance of Domain / Dimension).** *If $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$
 and $V \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ are nonempty open subsets that are homeomorphic,
 then $m = n$.*

Equivalently, no open subset of $\mathbb{R}^m$ is homeomorphic to an open subset
 of $\mathbb{R}^n$ when $m \neq n$. (The theorem follows from Invariance of Doma
in — that a continuous injective map on an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^k$ into $\
mathbb{R}^k$ is an open map — together with the local-homology computation $H_k(
\mathbb{R}^k, \mathbb{R}^k \setminus \{0\}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, which distinguish
es the local structure of $\mathbb{R}^k$ at a point for different $k$.)

The contradiction $m = n$ versus the hypothesis $n \neq m$ shows that no such pa
ir $U, V$ can exist.

---

### Conclusion

A smooth manifold $M$ cannot have open sets $U, V$ with $U$ homeomorphic to an o
pen subset of $\mathbb{R}^m$ and $V$ homeomorphic to an open subset of $\mathbb{
R}^n$ for $n \neq m$. The dimension of a smooth (indeed, topological) manifold i
s uniquely determined — it is a topological invariant, guaranteed by Brouwer's I
nvariance of Domain.
