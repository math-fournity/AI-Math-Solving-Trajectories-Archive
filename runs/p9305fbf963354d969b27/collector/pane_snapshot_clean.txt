# Proof: Two Non-Isometric 3-Manifolds in ℝ⁴ with the Same Volume and Surface Ar
ea

**Answer: Yes.** Two non-isometric 3-manifolds in ℝ⁴ can have the same volume an
d surface area.

## Construction

We work in ℝ³ ⊂ ℝ⁴ and consider **spheroids** (ellipsoids of revolution)—smooth
compact 3-manifolds with boundary, equipped with the flat metric induced from ℝ⁴
.

A spheroid with semi-axes $(a, a, c)$ is the set $E(a,c) = \{(x,y,z) \in \mathbb
{R}^3 : x^2/a^2 + y^2/a^2 + z^2/c^2 \le 1\}$.

- It is **prolate** when $c > a$ (elongated along the $z$-axis).
- It is **oblate** when $c < a$ (flattened along the $z$-axis).

Its **volume** and **surface area** (boundary 2-area) are:

$$V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi\, a^2 c, \qquad S = S(a,c).$$

## Normalizing the Volume

Set $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi$ by requiring $a^2 c = 1$, i.e., $c = 1/a^2$. This reduc
es us to a one-parameter family $E(a) := E(a,\, 1/a^2)$ with:

- $a < 1$: prolate (since $c = 1/a^2 > a$).
- $a > 1$: oblate (since $c = 1/a^2 < a$).
- $a = 1$: the unit sphere, $S = 4\pi$.

## Surface Area Formulas

**Prolate** ($a < 1$): With eccentricity $e = \sqrt{1 - a^2/c^2} = \sqrt{1 - a^6
}$,

$$S_{\mathrm{pro}}(a) = 2\pi a^2 + \frac{2\pi}{a}\cdot\frac{\arcsin\!\bigl(\sqrt
{1-a^6}\bigr)}{\sqrt{1-a^6}}.$$

**Oblate** ($a > 1$): With eccentricity $e = \sqrt{1 - c^2/a^2} = \sqrt{1 - 1/a^
6}$,

$$S_{\mathrm{obl}}(a) = 2\pi a^2 + \frac{\pi}{a^4}\cdot\frac{\ln\!\bigl(\frac{1+
e}{1-e}\bigr)}{e}.$$

Both are continuous on their respective domains $(0,1)$ and $(1,\infty)$, and bo
th satisfy $S \to 4\pi$ as $a \to 1$.

## Asymptotic Behavior

**Prolate, $a \to 0^+$:** We have $\sqrt{1-a^6} \to 1$, $\arcsin(1) = \pi/2$, an
d $2\pi a^2 \to 0$, so

$$S_{\mathrm{pro}}(a) \;\to\; \frac{2\pi}{a}\cdot\frac{\pi/2}{1} = \frac{\pi^2}{
a} \;\to\; \infty.$$

**Oblate, $a \to \infty$:** We have $2\pi a^2 \to \infty$ while the second term
$\to 0$ (since $a^{-4}\ln(\cdots) \to 0$), so

$$S_{\mathrm{obl}}(a) \;\to\; \infty.$$

## Applying the Intermediate Value Theorem

Both $S_{\mathrm{pro}}$ on $(0,1)$ and $S_{\mathrm{obl}}$ on $(1,\infty)$ are co
ntinuous, equal $4\pi$ at $a=1$, and tend to $+\infty$ at their respective extre
mes. Therefore, for any target value $S_0 > 4\pi$:

- There exists $a_1 \in (0,1)$ with $S_{\mathrm{pro}}(a_1) = S_0$ (by IVT on $[a
_1, 1]$).
- There exists $a_2 \in (1,\infty)$ with $S_{\mathrm{obl}}(a_2) = S_0$ (by IVT o
n $[1, a_2]$).

The spheroids $E(a_1, 1/a_1^2)$ (prolate) and $E(a_2, 1/a_2^2)$ (oblate) both ha
ve:

$$V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi, \qquad S = S_0.$$

## Non-Isometry

Both spheroids are compact domains in $\mathbb{R}^3$ carrying the **flat Euclide
an metric** (induced from $\mathbb{R}^3 \subset \mathbb{R}^4$). A classical resu
lt states that any isometry between connected open subsets of $\mathbb{R}^n$ wit
h the flat metric extends to a rigid motion of $\mathbb{R}^n$. Therefore, two su
ch spheroids are isometric as Riemannian 3-manifolds with boundary **if and only
 if** they are congruent (related by a rigid motion of $\mathbb{R}^3$), which fo
r spheroids means their semi-axes agree up to permutation.

The prolate spheroid $E(a_1, a_1, c_1)$ with $c_1 = 1/a_1^2 > a_1$ has its **lon
gest** semi-axis unique. The oblate spheroid $E(a_2, a_2, c_2)$ with $c_2 = 1/a_
2^2 < a_2$ has its **shortest** semi-axis unique. Since $c_1/a_1 > 1$ but $c_2/a
_2 < 1$, no permutation of semi-axes or rigid motion can map one to the other. H
ence they are **non-congruent**, and therefore **non-isometric**.

## Conclusion

We have exhibited, for every $S_0 > 4\pi$, a pair of smooth compact 3-manifolds
with boundary in $\mathbb{R}^4$—one prolate spheroid and one oblate spheroid—tha
t share the same volume $\frac{4}{3}\pi$ and the same surface area $S_0$, yet ar
e non-isometric. This proves that **two non-isometric 3-manifolds in $\mathbb{R}
^4$ can indeed have the same volume and surface area**.
