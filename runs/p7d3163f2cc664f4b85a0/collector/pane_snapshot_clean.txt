## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. The condition $x \in M + U$ for every open neighborhood $U$ of $
0$ implies (indeed, is equivalent to) $x \in \overline{M}$.

**Key observation.** In a topological vector space, the scalar-multiplication ma
p $\mu_{-1}\colon X \to X$, $\mu_{-1}(y) = -y$, is a homeomorphism: it is contin
uous (being the restriction of the continuous scalar multiplication $X\times\mat
hbb{K}\to X$ to $\{(-1)\}\times X$) and equals its own inverse. Since $\mu_{-1}(
0)=0$, it permutes the open neighborhoods of $0$:
$$\{U : U \text{ open},\; 0\in U\} \;=\; \{-U : U \text{ open},\; 0\in U\}.$$

**Rewriting the two conditions.** Recall that in a TVS the open neighborhoods of
 a point $x$ are exactly the sets $x + U$ with $U$ an open neighborhood of $0$ (
translation by $x$ is a homeomorphism). Hence

$$x \in \overline{M} \;\iff\; \forall\,\text{open }U\ni 0:\;(x+U)\cap M\neq\varn
othing \;\iff\; \forall\,\text{open }U\ni 0:\;\exists\,m\in M:\;m-x\in U. \tag{$
\star$}$$

On the other hand, the given condition is
$$\forall\,\text{open }U\ni 0:\;x\in M+U \;\iff\; \forall\,\text{open }U\ni 0:\;
\exists\,m\in M:\;x-m\in U. \tag{$\dagger$}$$

**Equivalence of ($\star$) and ($\dagger$).** Starting from ($\dagger$), replace
 $U$ by $-U$. Since negation permutes the open neighborhoods of $0$, this ranges
 over the same family, giving
$$\forall\,\text{open }U\ni 0:\;\exists\,m\in M:\;x-m\in -U \;\iff\; \forall\,\t
ext{open }U\ni 0:\;\exists\,m\in M:\;-(x-m)\in U \;\iff\; \forall\,\text{open }U
\ni 0:\;\exists\,m\in M:\;m-x\in U,$$
which is precisely ($\star$). The reverse implication is identical (apply the sa
me substitution to ($\star$)).

**Conclusion.** The condition $x\in M+U$ for every open neighborhood $U$ of $0$
is equivalent to $x\in\overline{M}$. In particular, it implies $x\in\overline{M}
$. $\blacksquare$
