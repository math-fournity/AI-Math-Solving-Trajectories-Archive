## Proof

We compute the dimension of $R = \mathbb{C}[[x,y]]/I$ where $I = (f_1, f_2)$ wit
h $f_1 = 2xy - y^2 = y(2x-y)$ and $f_2 = x^2 - 2xy = x(x-2y)$.

**Step 1: The variety is supported only at the origin.**

The common zeros of $f_1$ and $f_2$: $f_1 = 0$ gives $y = 0$ or $y = 2x$; $f_2 =
 0$ gives $x = 0$ or $x = 2y$. Checking all four combinations, each forces $x =
y = 0$ over $\mathbb{C}$. So $I$ is zero-dimensional and $R$ is a finite-dimensi
onal $\mathbb{C}$-vector space.

**Step 2: Compute a Gröbner basis (lex order, $x > y$).**

Leading terms: $\text{LT}(f_1) = xy$, $\text{LT}(f_2) = x^2$.

**S-polynomial of $f_1, f_2$:**
$$S = y \cdot f_2 - x \cdot f_1 = y(x^2 - 2xy) - x(2xy - y^2) = -x^2 y - xy^2.$$
Reducing $-x^2 y$ via $f_2$: $-x^2 y = -y(x^2 - 2xy) - 2xy^2 = -y f_2 - 2xy^2$,
so the remainder is $-3xy^2$. Thus $f_3 = xy^2 \in I$.

**S-polynomial of $f_1, f_3$:** lcm of $xy, xy^2$ is $xy^2$.
$$S = y \cdot f_1 - f_3 = y(2xy - y^2) - xy^2 = xy^2 - y^3.$$
Reducing by $f_3$: remainder $-y^3$. Thus $f_4 = y^3 \in I$.

**All remaining S-polynomials reduce to 0:**
- $S(f_2, f_3) = y^2 f_2 - x f_3 = -2xy^3$, reduced by $f_4$ to $0$.
- $S(f_2, f_4) = y^3 f_2 - x^2 f_4 = -2xy^4$, reduced by $f_4$ to $0$.
- $S(f_1, f_4) = y^2 f_1 - x f_4 = 2xy^3 - y^4 - xy^4$, each term reduced by $f_
4$ to $0$.
- $S(f_3, f_4) = y \cdot f_3 - x \cdot f_4 = 0$.

The Gröbner basis is $\{x^2 - 2xy,\; 2xy - y^2,\; xy^2,\; y^3\}$, with leading t
erm ideal $\langle x^2,\; xy,\; y^3 \rangle$.

**Step 3: Count standard monomials.**

The monomials not in $\langle x^2, xy, y^3 \rangle$ are those with $x$-degree $\
leq 1$ and, if $x$-degree $= 1$, then $y$-degree $= 0$, and $y$-degree $\leq 2$:

$$\{1,\; x,\; y,\; y^2\}.$$

These form a $\mathbb{C}$-basis of $R$, so $\dim_\mathbb{C} R = 4$.

**Verification via Bézout:** $f_1$ and $f_2$ are degree-2 curves meeting only at
 the origin, so the intersection multiplicity is $\deg(f_1)\cdot\deg(f_2) = 2 \c
dot 2 = 4$, confirming the result.
