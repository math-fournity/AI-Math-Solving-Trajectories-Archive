## Analysis

This is **Erdős Problem 567**, which asks whether three specific graphs — $Q_3$
(the 3-cube), $K_{3,3}$, and $H_5$ ($C_5$ with two vertex-disjoint chords, equiv
alently $K_4$ with one edge subdivided) — are **Ramsey size linear**. A graph $G
$ is Ramsey size linear if there exists a constant $c_G$ such that for every gra
ph $H$ with $m$ edges and no isolated vertices, the **size Ramsey number** $\hat
{r}(G, H) \leq c_G \cdot m$.

### What is known

**EFRS93** (Erdős–Faudree–Rousseau–Schelp, 1993) introduced the concept and prov
ed:
- Every graph with **at most 4 vertices** is Ramsey size linear.
- Every graph with **at most 4 edges** is Ramsey size linear.
- **Trees** are Ramsey size linear.
- **Closure under subgraphs**: if $G'$ is RSL and $G \subseteq G'$, then $G$ is
RSL.
- They posed the **general conjecture** (Problem 566): *all* graphs are Ramsey s
ize linear.

The three graphs in Problem 567 were specifically chosen as test cases just beyo
nd the reach of their methods:
- $Q_3$: 8 vertices, 12 edges (3-regular)
- $K_{3,3}$: 6 vertices, 9 edges (3-regular)
- $H_5$: 5 vertices, 7 edges (max degree 3)

All three exceed the "≤ 4 vertices" and "≤ 4 edges" thresholds.

### Why the standard tools fall short

**Bounded-degree Ramsey theorem** (Chvátal–Rödl–Szemerédi–Trotter, 1983): For ev
ery $\Delta$, there exists $c(\Delta)$ such that $r(G, H) \leq c(\Delta) \cdot v
(H)$ for all $G$ with max degree $\Delta$. Since $H$ has no isolated vertices, $
v(H) \leq 2e(H)$, giving $r(G,H) = O(e(H))$.

However, the size Ramsey number satisfies only $\hat{r}(G, H) \leq \binom{r(G,H)
}{2}$, yielding a **quadratic** bound $\hat{r}(G, H) = O(e(H)^2)$. The gap betwe
en this quadratic bound and the conjectured linear bound is precisely the conten
t of the open problem. All three graphs have maximum degree 3, so the bounded-de
gree theorem applies — but it gives quadratic, not linear.

**Subgraph closure does not help directly**: We would need a *supergraph* of $Q_
3$, $K_{3,3}$, or $H_5$ that is known to be RSL. But natural supergraphs (e.g.,
$K_8 \supseteq Q_3$, $K_6 \supseteq K_{3,3}$, $K_5 \supseteq H_5$) are complete
graphs, which are **not** RSL for $n \geq 4$ (since $r(K_n, K_m)$ grows superlin
early in $e(K_m)$).

**Subdivision idea for $H_5$**: $H_5 = K_4^*$ is $K_4$ with one edge subdivided.
 $K_4$ has 4 vertices, so it **is** RSL by EFRS93. However, $H_5$ is not a subgr
aph of $K_4$ (it has 5 vertices), and $K_4$ is not a subgraph of $H_5$ (it has e
dge density $K_4$ doesn't share). The subgraph closure property cannot bridge th
is gap, and no known result states that subdividing an edge preserves Ramsey siz
e linearity.

**Bounded treewidth**: All three graphs have treewidth 3. If bounded-treewidth g
raphs were known to be RSL, all three cases would follow. But this is not a know
n result — if it were, Problem 567 would be resolved.

**Probabilistic constructions**: For any fixed $G$, one can show $\hat{r}(G, H)
\leq c_G \cdot e(H) \cdot (\log e(H))^{O(1)}$ using random graph methods. Removi
ng the logarithmic factor to achieve a linear bound is the core difficulty and r
emains open.
