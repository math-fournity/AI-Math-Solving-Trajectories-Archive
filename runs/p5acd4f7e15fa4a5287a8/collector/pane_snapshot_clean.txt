## Solution

**Domain.** We need $2x+1 \ge 0$ (so $x \ge -\tfrac{1}{2}$) and the denominator
nonzero, i.e. $1-\sqrt{2x+1}\ne 0$, i.e. $x\ne 0$.

**Substitution.** Let $t=\sqrt{2x+1}$, so $t\ge 0$, $t\ne 1$, and $x=\tfrac{t^{2
}-1}{2}$.

Then
$$4x^{2}=4\cdot\frac{(t^{2}-1)^{2}}{4}=(t^{2}-1)^{2}=(t-1)^{2}(t+1)^{2},$$
and the denominator is $(1-t)^{2}=(t-1)^{2}$. Since $t\ne 1$, we may cancel:
$$\frac{4x^{2}}{(1-\sqrt{2x+1})^{2}}=\frac{(t-1)^{2}(t+1)^{2}}{(t-1)^{2}}=(t+1)^
{2}.$$

The right-hand side becomes $2x+9=(t^{2}-1)+9=t^{2}+8$.

**Inequality.** The inequality reduces to
$$(t+1)^{2}<t^{2}+8 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; t^{2}+2t+1<t^{2}+8 \;\Longleftrighta
rrow\; 2t<7 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; t<\tfrac{7}{2}.$$

**Translating back.** $t<\tfrac{7}{2}$ with $t=\sqrt{2x+1}\ge 0$ gives
$$\sqrt{2x+1}<\frac{7}{2}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;2x+1<\frac{49}{4}\;\Longleftrigh
tarrow\;x<\frac{45}{8}.$$

Combining with the domain constraints $x\ge -\tfrac{1}{2}$ and $x\ne 0$:

$$\boxed{-\frac{1}{2}\le x<0 \quad\text{or}\quad 0<x<\frac{45}{8}}.$$

**Check.** At $x=-\tfrac12$: LHS $=1$, RHS $=8$ ✓. At $x\to 0^{\pm}$ the LHS ten
ds to a finite limit (the singularity is removable, equal to $4$), and $4<9$ ✓.
At $x=\tfrac{45}{8}$ equality holds (excluded); for $x>\tfrac{45}{8}$ the inequa
lity fails.
