This is the famous **Sum of Three Cubes** conjecture. The problem asks to prove
the full equivalence:

> An integer `n` is a sum of three cubes **if and only if** `n` is not congruent
 to 4 or 5 mod 9.

**Analysis of the two directions:**

**Necessity (only-if direction):** If `n = x³ + y³ + z³`, then `n ≢ 4, 5 (mod 9)
`. This is the *easy* direction and is already proven in the problem text (`mod_
9_of_isSumOfThreeCubes`). The proof works because every cube is congruent to 0,
±1 (mod 9), so a sum of three cubes can only be congruent to {0,1,2,3,6,7,8} mod
 9 — never 4 or 5.

**Sufficiency (if direction):** If `n ≢ 4, 5 (mod 9)`, then there exist integers
 `x, y, z` with `n = x³ + y³ + z³`. This is the **open** direction. It is explic
itly tagged `@[category research open]` in the problem.

**Why this is genuinely open:**

- For any *fixed* `n`, finding a representation as a sum of three cubes is a com
putational search problem (and solutions have been found for all small `n` not ≡
 4,5 mod 9, including famously `n = 33` and `n = 42` in 2019).
- However, **proving that such a representation exists for every admissible `n`*
* (i.e., a uniform existence proof covering all integers) is an open problem in
number theory. No proof of the sufficiency direction is known.
- The problem is listed as an open research conjecture by the Formal Conjectures
 project, and the sufficiency direction has resisted attack despite significant
computational evidence and deep heuristic arguments.
