Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[n]{p_1},
 \ldots, \sqrt[n]{p_k}):\mathbb{Q}] = n^k$ for distinct primes $p_1, \ldots, p_k
$ and $n > 1$.

**Answer: Yes, the equality holds.**

**Proof.**

The key idea is to use Kummer theory over $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ (which contains
the $n$-th roots of unity) and then deduce the result over $\mathbb{Q}$ by a deg
ree comparison.

**Step 1: Independence of the primes in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)^*/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_
n)^{*n}$.**

Let $F = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$, where $\zeta_n$ is a primitive $n$-th root of uni
ty. We claim that the classes of $p_1, \ldots, p_k$ are linearly independent in
$F^*/F^{*n}$ (over $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$).

Suppose $p_1^{e_1} \cdots p_k^{e_k} = b^n$ for some $b \in F^*$ and integers $0
\leq e_i < n$. For each $i$, consider the $p_i$-adic valuation $v_{p_i}$ on $\ma
thbb{Q}$ (with $v_{p_i}(p_i) = 1$), extended to $F$. Since $p_j$ is a $p_i$-adic
 unit for $j \neq i$, we have $v_{p_i}(p_j) = \delta_{ij}$. Applying $v_{p_i}$ t
o both sides:

$$e_i = v_{p_i}(p_1^{e_1} \cdots p_k^{e_k}) = v_{p_i}(b^n) = n \cdot v_{p_i}(b).
$$

Thus $n \mid e_i$. Since $0 \leq e_i < n$, we conclude $e_i = 0$ for all $i$. Th
is proves independence. $\checkmark$

**Step 2: Apply Kummer theory over $F = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$.**

Since $F$ contains the $n$-th roots of unity and $\operatorname{char}(F) = 0 \nm
id n$, Kummer theory applies. The independence established in Step 1 implies:

$$[F(\sqrt[n]{p_1}, \ldots, \sqrt[n]{p_k}) : F] = n^k.$$

That is, $[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n, \sqrt[n]{p_1}, \ldots, \sqrt[n]{p_k}) : \mathbb{Q
}(\zeta_n)] = n^k$.

**Step 3: Degree comparison over $\mathbb{Q}$.**

Let $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[n]{p_1}, \ldots, \sqrt[n]{p_k})$ and $L = \mathbb{Q}(\
zeta_n, \sqrt[n]{p_1}, \ldots, \sqrt[n]{p_k}) = K(\zeta_n)$.

By the tower law applied in two ways:

$$[L : \mathbb{Q}] = [L : F] \cdot [F : \mathbb{Q}] = n^k \cdot [\mathbb{Q}(\zet
a_n) : \mathbb{Q}],$$

and also:

$$[L : \mathbb{Q}] = [L : K] \cdot [K : \mathbb{Q}].$$

Since $L = K(\zeta_n)$, the degree $[L : K]$ equals the degree of the minimal po
lynomial of $\zeta_n$ over $K$, which divides the cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_n$
. Therefore:

$$[L : K] \leq [\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n) : \mathbb{Q}].$$

Combining:

$$n^k \cdot [\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n) : \mathbb{Q}] = [L : K] \cdot [K : \mathbb{Q}]
\leq [\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n) : \mathbb{Q}] \cdot [K : \mathbb{Q}],$$

which gives:

$$[K : \mathbb{Q}] \geq n^k.$$

**Step 4: Upper bound.**

On the other hand, by the tower inequality, since each $\sqrt[n]{p_i}$ satisfies
 the polynomial $x^n - p_i$ of degree $n$:

$$[K : \mathbb{Q}] \leq n \cdot n \cdots n = n^k.$$

**Conclusion.**

From Steps 3 and 4:

$$[K : \mathbb{Q}] = n^k,$$

that is,

$$[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[n]{p_1}, \ldots, \sqrt[n]{p_k}) : \mathbb{Q}] = n^k. \qquad
\blacksquare$$
