## Solution

**Answer: No.** Log-supermodularity combined with monotonicity in *x* does **not
** imply supermodularity. A counterexample suffices.

### Counterexample

Let $X = Y = \{0, 1\}$ with the natural total order (both are lattices). Define
$f : X \times Y \to \mathbb{R}$ by

$$f(0,0) = 1, \quad f(0,1) = \tfrac{1}{2}, \quad f(1,0) = 3, \quad f(1,1) = 2.$$

All values are positive, so log-supermodularity is well-defined.

**Checking monotonicity in $x$:** For every $y \in Y$, we need $f(1,y) \geq f(0,
y)$:
- $y = 0$: $f(1,0) = 3 \geq 1 = f(0,0)$. ✓
- $y = 1$: $f(1,1) = 2 \geq \tfrac{1}{2} = f(0,1)$. ✓

**Checking log-supermodularity:** The only pair with $x' > x$ and $y' > y$ is $x
'=1, x=0, y'=1, y=0$. We need
$$f(1,1)\,f(0,0) \geq f(0,1)\,f(1,0).$$
$$2 \cdot 1 = 2 \geq \tfrac{3}{2} = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 3. \quad \checkmark$$

**Checking supermodularity (which must fail):** For the same pair, supermodulari
ty requires
$$f(1,1) + f(0,0) \geq f(0,1) + f(1,0),$$
$$2 + 1 = 3 \geq \tfrac{1}{2} + 3 = \tfrac{7}{2}. \quad \text{This is } 3 \geq 3
.5, \text{ which is } \textbf{false}. \quad \boldsymbol{\times}$$

### Why the implication fails (intuition)

Log-supermodularity is a **multiplicative** (ratio) condition: it says the ratio
 $f(x',y')/f(x',y)$ is at least $f(x,y')/f(x,y)$, i.e., the "relative" increase
in $f$ when $y$ rises is larger at higher $x$. Supermodularity is an **additive*
* (difference) condition: it says the *absolute* increase $f(\cdot, y') - f(\cdo
t, y)$ is larger at higher $x$.

Monotonicity in $x$ constrains the *levels* but does not reconcile the multiplic
ative and additive conditions. In the counterexample, the values $(f(1,1), f(0,0
)) = (2, 1)$ are *unbalanced* (large ratio, modest sum), while $(f(0,1), f(1,0))
 = (\tfrac{1}{2}, 3)$ are *balanced in the opposite direction* (small ratio, lar
ge sum). This makes the product inequality hold while the sum inequality fails —
 exactly the situation $(B-1)(C-1) < 0$ that monotonicity alone cannot exclude.

### Conclusion

Log-supermodularity and monotonicity in $x$ are independent of (and do not joint
ly imply) supermodularity. The function above satisfies both hypotheses but viol
ates the supermodularity inequality, providing an explicit counterexample.
