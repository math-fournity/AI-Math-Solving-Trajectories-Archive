## Proof

We work in a finite-dimensional normed vector space $(V, \|\cdot\|)$ with $\dim
V = n$, and $\|B\| = \sup_{\|x\|=1} \|Bx\|$ denotes the operator norm induced by
 the vector norm. We ask: does there exist $y \in V$ such that $\|By\| \geq \|B\
|\,\|y\|$ for **every** matrix (linear operator) $B$?

---

### The trivial case: $y = 0$

If $y = 0$, then for every $B$:
$$\|By\| = \|B\cdot 0\| = 0 = \|B\|\cdot 0 = \|B\|\,\|y\|.$$
Hence $\|By\| \geq \|B\|\,\|y\|$ holds (with equality). So $y = 0$ **always** sa
tisfies the condition, in every dimension.

---

### Nonzero $y$ is impossible when $n \geq 2$

Suppose $y \neq 0$ and $\|By\| \geq \|B\|\,\|y\|$ for all $B$. By the defining p
roperty of the operator norm, $\|B\| = \sup_{\|x\|=1}\|Bx\| \geq \|By\|/\|y\|$ (
taking $x = y/\|y\|$), i.e., $\|By\| \leq \|B\|\,\|y\|$ always. Combining with t
he assumed inequality:

$$\|By\| = \|B\|\,\|y\| \quad \text{for every } B. \tag{$\ast$}$$

So $y$ must **attain the operator norm of every matrix $B$**. We show this is im
possible.

Since $n \geq 2$ and $y \neq 0$, extend $y$ to a basis $\{y, e_2, \dots, e_n\}$
of $V$. Define a linear operator $B$ by:
$$B(y) = 0, \qquad B(e_i) = e_i \;\;(i = 2, \dots, n).$$
Then $B \neq 0$ (e.g., $B(e_2) = e_2 \neq 0$), so $\|B\| > 0$. But $By = 0$, giv
ing $\|By\| = 0$. Since $\|y\| > 0$, condition $(\ast)$ would require $0 = \|B\|
\,\|y\| > 0$, a **contradiction**. $\square$

---

### The case $n = 1$

When $n = 1$, every matrix is a scalar $B = b$, with $\|B\| = |b|$. Then $\|By\|
 = |b|\,|y| = \|B\|\,\|y\|$ for every $y$, so the inequality holds with equality
 for **every** vector $y$.

---

### Conclusion

- **In dimension $n \geq 2$:** The only vector satisfying $\|By\| \geq \|B\|\,\|
y\|$ for all matrices $B$ is $y = 0$. No nonzero vector works.
- **In dimension $n = 1$:** Every vector $y$ works (equality holds identically).

In particular, a **nonzero** vector $y$ with the desired universal property **do
es not exist** in any dimension $n \geq 2$; the trivial vector $y = 0$ is the un
ique solution in the general (multidimensional) setting.

### PROOF COMPLETE
