## Proof

We evaluate

$$I(\xi) \;=\; \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-\pi(x+i\xi)^2}\,dx, \qquad \xi\in\mat
hbb{R}.$$

**Key tool.** The function $f(z)=e^{-\pi z^{2}}$ is entire, so its contour integ
ral over any closed piecewise-$C^{1}$ loop vanishes (Cauchy's theorem).

**Contour.** Fix $R>0$ and consider the rectangle $\Gamma_R$ with vertices, trav
ersed counterclockwise,

$$-R,\quad R,\quad R+i\xi,\quad -R+i\xi.$$

Denote its sides:
- $\gamma_1$: bottom, $z=x$, $x\in[-R,R]$;
- $\gamma_2$: right, $z=R+it$, $t\in[0,\xi]$ (or $[\xi,0]$ if $\xi<0$);
- $\gamma_3$: top, $z=x+i\xi$, $x$ from $R$ to $-R$;
- $\gamma_4$: left, $z=-R+it$, $t\in[\xi,0]$ (or $[0,\xi]$).

By Cauchy's theorem, $\oint_{\Gamma_R} f(z)\,dz=0$, i.e.

$$\int_{\gamma_1}f+\int_{\gamma_2}f+\int_{\gamma_3}f+\int_{\gamma_4}f=0.$$

**The horizontal sides.** Parametrizing,

$$\int_{\gamma_1}f\,dz=\int_{-R}^{R}e^{-\pi x^{2}}\,dx,\qquad \int_{\gamma_3}f\,
dz=\int_{R}^{-R}e^{-\pi(x+i\xi)^{2}}\,dx=-\int_{-R}^{R}e^{-\pi(x+i\xi)^{2}}\,dx.
$$

**The vertical sides vanish as $R\to\infty$.** For $\gamma_2$ with $z=R+it$, $t$
 between $0$ and $\xi$:

$$|e^{-\pi z^{2}}|=\bigl|e^{-\pi(R+it)^{2}}\bigr|=e^{-\pi(R^{2}-t^{2})}=e^{-\pi
R^{2}}\,e^{\pi t^{2}}\le e^{-\pi R^{2}}\,e^{\pi\xi^{2}},$$

since $|t|\le|\xi|$. Hence

$$\left|\int_{\gamma_2}f\,dz\right|\le |\xi|\,e^{-\pi R^{2}}\,e^{\pi\xi^{2}}\xri
ghtarrow[R\to\infty]{}0.$$

The identical bound holds for $\gamma_4$ (with $z=-R+it$, $|e^{-\pi z^{2}}|=e^{-
\pi(R^{2}-t^{2})}$). Thus both vertical contributions tend to $0$.

**Take the limit $R\to\infty$.** From the vanishing contour integral,

$$\int_{-R}^{R}e^{-\pi x^{2}}\,dx-\int_{-R}^{R}e^{-\pi(x+i\xi)^{2}}\,dx+\underbr
ace{\int_{\gamma_2}f+\int_{\gamma_4}f}_{\to\,0}=0.$$

Letting $R\to\infty$,

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\pi x^{2}}\,dx=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\pi(x+i\x
i)^{2}}\,dx=I(\xi).$$

**The base Gaussian.** It remains to compute $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\pi x^{
2}}\,dx$. With $u=\sqrt{\pi}\,x$,

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\pi x^{2}}\,dx=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{
\infty}e^{-u^{2}}\,du=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\cdot\sqrt{\pi}=1,$$

using the classical value $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-u^{2}}\,du=\sqrt{\pi}$.

**Conclusion.** For every real $\xi$,

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-\pi(x+i\xi)^{2}}\,dx \;=\; 1.}
$$

**Remark (consistency check).** Expanding the integrand gives $e^{-\pi(x+i\xi)^2
}=e^{\pi\xi^2}\,e^{-\pi x^2}\,e^{-2\pi i x\xi}$. The integral is then $e^{\pi\xi
^2}\,\widehat{g}(\xi)$ where $g(x)=e^{-\pi x^2}$. Since $g$ is a self-Fourier fu
nction under the convention $\widehat{g}(\xi)=\int e^{-2\pi i x\xi}g(x)\,dx$, we
 have $\widehat{g}(\xi)=e^{-\pi\xi^2}$, and the product $e^{\pi\xi^2}\cdot e^{-\
pi\xi^2}=1$ — agreeing with the contour result above.
