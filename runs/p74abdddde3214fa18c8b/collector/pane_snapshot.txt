## Solution

We take 100 consecutive natural numbers $a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_{100}$. Since they
are consecutive, they form a **complete residue system modulo 100**. Therefore:

$$a_1^8 + a_2^8 + \cdots + a_{100}^8 \equiv \sum_{k=0}^{99} k^8 \pmod{100}.$$

We compute this sum modulo 4 and modulo 25 separately, then combine via the Chin
ese Remainder Theorem.

### Modulo 4

For even $k$: $k^8 \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$.
For odd $k$: $k \equiv 1$ or $3 \pmod{4}$, and $1^8 \equiv 3^8 = (3^2)^4 = 9^4 \
equiv 1^4 = 1 \pmod{4}$, so $k^8 \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$.

Among $\{0, 1, \ldots, 99\}$ there are exactly 50 odd numbers, so:

$$\sum_{k=0}^{99} k^8 \equiv 50 \cdot 1 \equiv 2 \pmod{4}.$$

### Modulo 25

Since $100 = 4 \times 25$, the residues mod 25 cycle exactly 4 times:

$$\sum_{k=0}^{99} k^8 \equiv 4 \sum_{k=0}^{24} k^8 \pmod{25}.$$

Now compute $S = \sum_{k=0}^{24} k^8 \pmod{25}$.

**Non-units:** The $k$ divisible by 5 are $k = 0, 5, 10, 15, 20$. Since $5^2 = 2
5$, we have $k^8 \equiv 0 \pmod{25}$ for all these. Contribution: $0$.

**Units:** There are $\varphi(25) = 20$ units mod 25, forming a cyclic group of
order 20. Let $g$ be a generator. Then:

$$\sum_{k \in (\mathbb{Z}/25\mathbb{Z})^*} k^8 = \sum_{j=0}^{19} g^{8j} = \frac{
g^{160} - 1}{g^8 - 1}.$$

Since $g^{20} \equiv 1$, we get $g^{160} = (g^{20})^8 = 1$. Also $g^8 \neq 1$ be
cause the order of $g$ is 20 and $20 \nmid 8$. Therefore:

$$\sum_{k \in (\mathbb{Z}/25\mathbb{Z})^*} k^8 = \frac{1 - 1}{g^8 - 1} = 0 \pmod
{25}.$$

So $S \equiv 0 \pmod{25}$, and thus:

$$\sum_{k=0}^{99} k^8 \equiv 4 \cdot 0 = 0 \pmod{25}.$$

### Chinese Remainder Theorem

We need $x$ with:
- $x \equiv 2 \pmod{4}$
- $x \equiv 0 \pmod{25}$

The values $\equiv 0 \pmod{25}$ in $\{0, \ldots, 99\}$ are $\{0, 25, 50, 75\}$.
Checking mod 4:
- $0 \equiv 0$
- $25 \equiv 1$
- $50 \equiv 2$ ✓
- $75 \equiv 3$

Therefore $x \equiv 50 \pmod{100}$.

### Conclusion

The last two digits of $a_1^8 + a_2^8 + \cdots + a_{100}^8$ are $\boxed{50}$.
