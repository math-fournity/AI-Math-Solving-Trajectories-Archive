## Proof

Let $Y_i = X_i / \sigma_i$. Since $X_i \sim N(0,\sigma_i^2)$ independently, the
$Y_i$ are independent standard normal: $Y_i \sim N(0,1)$.

Set $a_i = 1/\sigma_i^2$ and $b_i = 1/\sigma_i$, so that $a_i = b_i^2$. Rewrite
the key quantities in terms of $Y_i$:

$$Z = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n a_i X_i}{\sum_{i=1}^n a_i} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n b_i Y_i
}{\sum_{i=1}^n b_i^2}, \qquad \xi = \sum_{i=1}^n a_i (X_i - Z)^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n
a_i X_i^2 - \Big(\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\Big) Z^2.$$

The last identity is the standard weighted-variance decomposition $\sum a_i(X_i-
Z)^2 = \sum a_i X_i^2 - (\sum a_i)Z^2$, obtained by expanding the square and usi
ng $\sum a_i X_i = (\sum a_i) Z$.

Now substitute:
- $\sum a_i X_i^2 = \sum (X_i/\sigma_i)^2 = \sum Y_i^2 \sim \chi^2_n$.
- $\big(\sum a_i\big) Z^2 = \big(\sum b_i^2\big)\cdot \dfrac{(\sum b_i Y_i)^2}{(
\sum b_i^2)^2} = \dfrac{(\sum b_i Y_i)^2}{\sum b_i^2}$.

Define the unit vector $e = (b_1,\dots,b_n)/\sqrt{\sum b_j^2}$ and set
$$U = e \cdot Y = \frac{\sum b_i Y_i}{\sqrt{\sum b_i^2}}.$$
Since the $Y_i$ are i.i.d. $N(0,1)$ and $\|e\|=1$, we have $U \sim N(0,1)$, so $
U^2 \sim \chi^2_1$. Moreover $\big(\sum a_i\big)Z^2 = U^2$.

Thus
$$\xi = \sum_{i=1}^n Y_i^2 - U^2 = \|Y\|^2 - (e\cdot Y)^2.$$

Geometrically, $\|Y\|^2 = (e\cdot Y)^2 + \|Y_\perp\|^2$, where $Y_\perp$ is the
projection of $Y$ onto the $(n-1)$-dimensional subspace $e^\perp$. Hence
$$\xi = \|Y_\perp\|^2.$$

By the rotational invariance of the standard multivariate normal, the component
$e\cdot Y$ along $e$ is independent of the orthogonal component $Y_\perp$, and $
Y_\perp$ is distributed as $n-1$ independent standard normals in $e^\perp$. Ther
efore
$$\xi = \sum_{j=1}^{n-1} (Y'_j)^2 \sim \chi^2_{n-1},$$
where $Y'_1,\dots,Y'_{n-1}$ are i.i.d. $N(0,1)$.

**Conclusion.** The random variable $\xi$ follows a chi-squared distribution wit
h $n-1$ degrees of freedom:
$$\boxed{\xi \sim \chi^2_{n-1}}.$$
Moreover, $\xi$ is independent of $Z$ (equivalently, of $U$), since the orthogon
al components of a standard Gaussian are independent.
