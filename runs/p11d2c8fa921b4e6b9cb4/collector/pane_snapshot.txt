## Proof

**Step 1: Find the intersection geometrically.**

The circle is $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ (in 3-space, this is a cylinder). The hyperboloid
is $x^2 + y^2 - z^2 = 1$. Substituting $x^2 + y^2 = 4$:

$$4 - z^2 = 1 \implies z^2 = 3 \implies z = \pm\sqrt{3}.$$

The given parameterization $x(u,v) = (\sinh u\sin v,\, \sinh u\cos v,\, \cosh u)
$ has $z = \cosh u \geq 1$, so it covers only the **upper sheet**. The intersect
ion with this sheet is the single circle at $z = \sqrt{3}$ with radius $2$. Its
length is $2\pi \cdot 2 = 4\pi$.

**Step 2: Verify via the first fundamental form.**

With $\sinh u = 2$ (constant, so $u$ is fixed and $du = 0$), we have $\cosh u =
\sqrt{1+\sinh^2 u} = \sqrt{5}$, hence $\cosh^2 u = 5$, $\sinh^2 u = 4$.

Computing the coefficients (using $\sin^2 v + \cos^2 v = 1$):

$$E = \cosh^2 u(\sin^2 v + \cos^2 v) + \sinh^2 u = \cosh^2 u + \sinh^2 u = 5 + 4
 = 9,$$

$$F = 0, \qquad G = \sinh^2 u(\cos^2 v + \sin^2 v) = \sinh^2 u = 4.$$

**Step 3: Parameterize the intersection curve on the surface.**

On the surface, $x = \sinh u\sin v = 2\sin v$ and $y = \sinh u\cos v = 2\cos v$.
 The intersection circle at $z = \sqrt{3}$ is $(x,y) = (2\cos t, 2\sin t)$. Matc
hing: $\sin v = \cos t$, $\cos v = \sin t$, i.e. $v = \tfrac{\pi}{2} - t$. Cruci
ally, **$u$ is constant** along the curve, so $du = 0$ and $dv = -dt$.

**Step 4: Compute the arc length.**

$$ds = \sqrt{E\,du^2 + 2F\,du\,dv + G\,dv^2} = \sqrt{G}\,|dv| = \sqrt{4}\,|dt| =
 2\,|dt|.$$

Therefore:

$$\int_0^{2\pi} ds = \int_0^{2\pi} 2\,dt = 4\pi.$$

**Remark on the problem's $ds$ expression.** The formula printed in the problem,
 $\sqrt{E\sin^2 t + G\cos^2 t}$, corresponds to $du/dt = \sin t$ and $dv/dt = \c
os t$, which would make $u$ vary with $t$. This is inconsistent with the conditi
on $\sinh u = 2$ (constant $u$). With $u$ fixed, $du \equiv 0$, and only the $G\
,dv^2$ term survives, yielding the clean result above. (The expression $E\sin^2
t + G\cos^2 t = 9\sin^2 t + 4\cos^2 t$ would produce an elliptic integral with n
o closed form, which cannot be the length of a Euclidean circle.)

**Conclusion.** The length of the intersection is:

$$\boxed{4\pi}$$
