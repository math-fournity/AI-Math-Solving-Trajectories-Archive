## Proof

**Trivial case \(k \le 0\):** Take \(G = K_{1}\) (a single vertex), which is bip
artite with \(\chi_{\ell}(G) = 1 > k\).

**Main case \(k \ge 1\):** Let \(S\) be a set with \(|S| = 2k\), and let \(m = \
binom{2k}{k}\) be the number of \(k\)-subsets of \(S\). Construct the complete b
ipartite graph
\[
G = K_{m,m}
\]
with parts \(A = \{a_1, \dots, a_m\}\) and \(B = \{b_1, \dots, b_m\}\). Let \(T_
1, \dots, T_m\) be an enumeration of **all** \(k\)-subsets of \(S\), and let \(U
_1, \dots, U_m\) be another enumeration of **all** \(k\)-subsets of \(S\). Defin
e a list assignment \(L\) by
\[
L(a_i) = T_i, \qquad L(b_j) = U_j.
\]
Every list has size exactly \(k\), so this is a valid witness for \(k\)-choosabi
lity.

**Claim.** \(G\) admits no proper \(L\)-coloring, hence \(\chi_{\ell}(G) > k\).

Suppose for contradiction that a proper \(L\)-coloring \(c\) exists. Since \(G =
 K_{m,m}\) is complete bipartite, every vertex of \(A\) is adjacent to every ver
tex of \(B\). Therefore the set of colors used on \(A\) and the set of colors us
ed on \(B\) must be disjoint:
\[
X := \{c(a_i) : 1 \le i \le m\}, \quad Y := \{c(b_j) : 1 \le j \le m\}, \quad X
\cap Y = \emptyset,
\]
with \(X, Y \subseteq S\).

For each \(i\), the color \(c(a_i)\) lies in \(L(a_i) = T_i\), so \(c(a_i) \in X
 \cap T_i\), hence \(T_i \cap X \neq \emptyset\). Since \(\{T_1, \dots, T_m\}\)
ranges over **every** \(k\)-subset of \(S\), we conclude:

> Every \(k\)-subset of \(S\) intersects \(X\).

This forces \(|X| \ge k+1\). Indeed, if \(|X| \le k\), then \(|S \setminus X| \g
e 2k - k = k\), so there exists a \(k\)-subset of \(S\) contained entirely in \(
S \setminus X\) — contradicting the fact that every \(k\)-subset meets \(X\).

By the identical argument applied to the \(B\)-side (using that \(\{U_1, \dots,
U_m\}\) also ranges over all \(k\)-subsets of \(S\)), every \(k\)-subset of \(S\
) intersects \(Y\), forcing
\[
|Y| \ge k+1.
\]

But \(X\) and \(Y\) are disjoint subsets of \(S\), so
\[
|X| + |Y| \le |S| = 2k,
\]
while the two lower bounds give
\[
|X| + |Y| \ge (k+1) + (k+1) = 2k + 2 > 2k.
\]
This is a contradiction.

Therefore no proper \(L\)-coloring of \(G\) exists, even though every list has s
ize \(k\). Consequently \(\chi_{\ell}(G) > k\).

Since \(G = K_{m,m}\) is bipartite, this gives the desired construction for ever
y integer \(k \ge 1\) (and the trivial construction above handles \(k \le 0\)).
$\blacksquare$
