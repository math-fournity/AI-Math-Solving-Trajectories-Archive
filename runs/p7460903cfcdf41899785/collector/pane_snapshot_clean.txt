# Derivation of the Electromagnetic Stress-Energy Tensor

## Setup and Conventions

I work in flat spacetime with metric signature $(-,+,+,+)$, so $\eta_{\mu\nu} =
\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1)$, and set $c=1$. The electromagnetic field tensor is

$$F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu,$$

with components (in terms of the electric field $\vec{E}$ and magnetic field $\v
ec{B}$):

$$F_{0i} = -E_i, \qquad F_{ij} = -\epsilon_{ijk}\,B_k,$$

and raised indices $F^{0i} = +E_i$, $F^{ij} = -\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$ (since $F^{0i}
=\eta^{00}\eta^{ii}F_{0i}=(-1)(+1)(-E_i)=E_i$ and $F^{ij}=\eta^{ii}\eta^{jj}F_{i
j}=F_{ij}$).

---

## Step 1 — Deriving the Tensor Formula from the Action

The electromagnetic action is

$$S_{\rm EM} = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\;\mathcal{L}_{\rm EM}, \qquad \mathcal{L}_{\
rm EM} = -\frac{1}{4\mu_0}\,F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta} = -\frac{1}{4\mu_0}\,
g^{\alpha\gamma}g^{\beta\delta}\,F_{\alpha\beta}\,F_{\gamma\delta}.$$

Crucially, $F_{\alpha\beta}=\partial_\alpha A_\beta - \partial_\beta A_\alpha$ i
s **independent of the metric**; all metric dependence enters through $\sqrt{-g}
$ and the two inverse metrics $g^{\alpha\gamma}, g^{\beta\delta}$.

The (Hilbert) stress-energy tensor is defined by

$$T_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\frac{\delta S_{\rm EM}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}
.$$

**Varying the action:**

$$\delta S_{\rm EM} = \int d^4x\left[\delta\sqrt{-g}\left(-\frac{F^2}{4\mu_0}\ri
ght) + \sqrt{-g}\left(-\frac{1}{4\mu_0}\right)\delta\!\left(g^{\alpha\gamma}g^{\
beta\delta}\right)F_{\alpha\beta}F_{\gamma\delta}\right],$$

where $F^2 \equiv F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}$. Using $\delta\sqrt{-g} = -\tf
rac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g}\,g_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}$:

$$\delta S_{\rm EM} = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{g_{\mu\nu}\,F^2}{8\mu_0}\,
\delta g^{\mu\nu} \;-\; \frac{1}{4\mu_0}\left(\delta g^{\alpha\gamma}\right)g^{\
beta\delta}F_{\alpha\beta}F_{\gamma\delta} \;-\; \frac{1}{4\mu_0}\,g^{\alpha\gam
ma}\!\left(\delta g^{\beta\delta}\right)F_{\alpha\beta}F_{\gamma\delta}\right].$
$

**Second term:** $\delta g^{\alpha\gamma}\,g^{\beta\delta}F_{\alpha\beta}F_{\gam
ma\delta} = \delta g^{\alpha\gamma}\,F_{\alpha\beta}\,F_\gamma{}^\beta = \delta
g^{\mu\nu}\,F_{\mu\alpha}\,F_\nu{}^\alpha.$

**Third term:** Relabeling $\beta\!\to\!\mu,\;\delta\!\to\!\nu$ and using $F_{\a
lpha\mu}F^\alpha{}_\nu = F_{\mu\alpha}F_\nu{}^\alpha$ (verified via $g^{\alpha\g
amma}F_{\alpha\mu}F_{\gamma\nu} = g^{\alpha\gamma}F_{\mu\alpha}F_{\nu\gamma}$, w
hich follows from $F_{\alpha\mu}=-F_{\mu\alpha}$ and $F_{\gamma\nu}=-F_{\nu\gamm
a}$), the third term equals $\delta g^{\mu\nu}\,F_{\mu\alpha}\,F_\nu{}^\alpha$ a
s well.

Combining:

$$\delta S_{\rm EM} = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{g_{\mu\nu}\,F^2}{8\mu_0} -
 \frac{F_{\mu\alpha}\,F_\nu{}^\alpha}{2\mu_0}\right]\delta g^{\mu\nu}.$$

Applying the definition $T_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S}{\delta
 g^{\mu\nu}}$:

$$\boxed{T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm EM} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\left(F_{\mu\alpha}\,F_{\nu}{}^{\a
lpha} \;-\; \frac{1}{4}\,g_{\mu\nu}\,F_{\alpha\beta}\,F^{\alpha\beta}\right)}$$

This is the general covariant expression, valid in curved spacetime as well (wit
h $g_{\mu\nu}\to g_{\mu\nu}(x)$).

---

## Step 2 — The Lorentz Invariant $F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}$

$$F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta} = 2\,F_{0i}\,F^{0i} + F_{ij}\,F^{ij} = 2(-E_i)(
E_i) + (-\epsilon_{ijk}B_k)(-\epsilon_{ij\ell}B_\ell)$$

$$= -2\vec{E}^2 + \epsilon_{ijk}\epsilon_{ij\ell}\,B_k B_\ell = -2\vec{E}^2 + 2\
,\delta_{k\ell}\,B_k B_\ell = 2\!\left(\vec{B}^2 - \vec{E}^2\right).$$

---

## Step 3 — Component $T_{00}$ (Energy Density)

$$F_{0\alpha}\,F_0{}^\alpha = F_{0i}\,F_0{}^i, \qquad F_0{}^i = \eta^{ij}F_{0j}
= \delta^{ij}(-E_j) = -E_i.$$

$$F_{0\alpha}\,F_0{}^\alpha = (-E_i)(-E_i) = \vec{E}^2.$$

$$T_{00} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\left(\vec{E}^2 - \frac{1}{4}\underbrace{(-1)}_{g_{00}
}\cdot 2(\vec{B}^2-\vec{E}^2)\right) = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\left(\vec{E}^2 + \frac{\v
ec{B}^2-\vec{E}^2}{2}\right) = \boxed{\frac{1}{2\mu_0}\!\left(\vec{E}^2 + \vec{B
}^2\right)}.$$

This is the electromagnetic energy density $u_{\rm EM}$, as expected.

---

## Step 4 — Components $T_{0i} = T_{i0}$ (Energy Flux / Momentum Density)

Since $g_{0i}=0$, the second term vanishes:

$$T_{0i} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\,F_{0\alpha}\,F_i{}^\alpha.$$

$$F_{0\alpha}\,F_i{}^\alpha = \underbrace{F_{00}\,F_i{}^0}_{=\,0} + F_{0j}\,F_i{
}^j, \qquad F_i{}^j = \eta^{jk}F_{ik} = \delta^{jk}F_{ik} = F_{ij}.$$

$$F_{0j}\,F_{ij} = (-E_j)(-\epsilon_{ijk}B_k) = \epsilon_{ijk}\,E_j\,B_k = (\vec
{E}\times\vec{B})_i.$$

$$\boxed{T_{0i} = T_{i0} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\,(\vec{E}\times\vec{B})_i = \frac{1}{
\mu_0}\,(\vec{E}\times\vec{B})_i.}$$

This is the Poynting vector $\vec{S}/\mu_0$... more precisely, $T_{0i} = S_i/\mu
_0$ where $\vec{S} = \vec{E}\times\vec{B}/\mu_0$ is the energy flux density. The
 equality $T_{0i}=T_{i0}$ reflects the symmetry of $T_{\mu\nu}$, encoding the eq
uivalence of energy flux and momentum density.

---

## Step 5 — Components $T_{ij}$ (Maxwell Stress Tensor)

$$T_{ij} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\left(F_{i\alpha}\,F_j{}^\alpha - \frac{1}{4}\,\delta_
{ij}\cdot 2(\vec{B}^2-\vec{E}^2)\right).$$

**Computing $F_{i\alpha}\,F_j{}^\alpha$:**

$$F_{i\alpha}\,F_j{}^\alpha = F_{i0}\,F_j{}^0 + F_{ik}\,F_j{}^k.$$

- $F_{i0} = -F_{0i} = E_i$, and $F_j{}^0 = \eta^{00}F_{j0} = (-1)(-F_{0j}) = F_{
0j} = -E_j$. So $F_{i0}\,F_j{}^0 = E_i(-E_j) = -E_i E_j$.

- $F_j{}^k = \delta^{k\ell}F_{j\ell} = F_{jk}$, so $F_{ik}\,F_{jk} = (-\epsilon_
{ikm}B_m)(-\epsilon_{jkn}B_n) = \epsilon_{ikm}\epsilon_{jkn}\,B_m B_n$.

Using the identity $\epsilon_{ikm}\epsilon_{jkn} = \delta_{ij}\delta_{mn} - \del
ta_{in}\delta_{mj}$:

$$F_{ik}\,F_{jk} = (\delta_{ij}\delta_{mn} - \delta_{in}\delta_{mj})\,B_m B_n =
\delta_{ij}\,\vec{B}^2 - B_i B_j.$$

Therefore:

$$F_{i\alpha}\,F_j{}^\alpha = -E_i E_j + \delta_{ij}\,\vec{B}^2 - B_i B_j.$$

**Assembling $T_{ij}$:**

$$T_{ij} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\left(-E_i E_j - B_i B_j + \delta_{ij}\vec{B}^2 - \fra
c{1}{2}\delta_{ij}(\vec{B}^2 - \vec{E}^2)\right)$$

$$= \frac{1}{\mu_0}\left(-E_i E_j - B_i B_j + \frac{1}{2}\delta_{ij}(\vec{E}^2 +
 \vec{B}^2)\right).$$

$$\boxed{T_{ij} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\left(-E_i E_j - B_i B_j + \frac{1}{2}\,\delta_
{ij}\!\left(\vec{E}^2+\vec{B}^2\right)\right).}$$

The Maxwell stress tensor is $\sigma_{ij} = -T_{ij} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\!\left(E_i
 E_j + B_i B_j - \frac{1}{2}\delta_{ij}(E^2+B^2)\right)$.

---

## Step 6 — Matrix Form

Collecting all components:

$$T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm EM} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\begin{pmatrix} \dfrac{\vec{E}^2+\vec{B}^
2}{2} & (\vec{E}\times\vec{B})_1 & (\vec{E}\times\vec{B})_2 & (\vec{E}\times\vec
{B})_3 \\[6pt] (\vec{E}\times\vec{B})_1 & -E_1^2 - B_1^2 + \frac{E^2+B^2}{2} & -
E_1 E_2 - B_1 B_2 & -E_1 E_3 - B_1 B_3 \\[4pt] (\vec{E}\times\vec{B})_2 & -E_2 E
_1 - B_2 B_1 & -E_2^2 - B_2^2 + \frac{E^2+B^2}{2} & -E_2 E_3 - B_2 B_3 \\[4pt] (
\vec{E}\times\vec{B})_3 & -E_3 E_1 - B_3 B_1 & -E_3 E_2 - B_3 B_2 & -E_3^2 - B_3
^2 + \frac{E^2+B^2}{2} \end{pmatrix}$$

---

## Step 7 — Verification of Key Properties

**Symmetry:** $T_{\mu\nu} = T_{\nu\mu}$. This follows because $F_{\mu\alpha}F_\n
u{}^\alpha$ is symmetric in $\mu\leftrightarrow\nu$ (proven by swapping dummy in
dices and using $g^{\alpha\beta}=g^{\beta\alpha}$), and $g_{\mu\nu}$ is symmetri
c.

**Tracelessness:** $T^\mu{}_\mu = g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu} = -T_{00} + \sum_i T_{ii}
$.

$$\sum_i T_{ii} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\left(-\vec{E}^2 - \vec{B}^2 + \frac{3}{2}(\vec
{E}^2+\vec{B}^2)\right) = \frac{1}{2\mu_0}(\vec{E}^2+\vec{B}^2).$$

$$T^\mu{}_\mu = -\frac{1}{2\mu_0}(\vec{E}^2+\vec{B}^2) + \frac{1}{2\mu_0}(\vec{E
}^2+\vec{B}^2) = 0. \quad\checkmark$$

This reflects the scale invariance (conformal invariance) of Maxwell theory in 4
D.

**Conservation:** $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm EM} = -F_{\nu\alpha}J^\alpha$ (wit
h sources) or $=0$ (in vacuum). This follows from Maxwell's equations $\nabla_\m
u F^{\mu\nu} = \mu_0 J^\nu$ and the Bianchi identity $\nabla_{[\lambda}F_{\mu\nu
]}=0$:

$$\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm EM} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\left[(\nabla^\mu F_{\mu\alpha
})F_\nu{}^\alpha + F_{\mu\alpha}\nabla^\mu F_\nu{}^\alpha - \frac{1}{4}\nabla_\n
u(F^2)\right].$$

The first term gives $J_\alpha F_\nu{}^\alpha$. The remaining terms cancel by th
e Bianchi identity (after index manipulation), yielding $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} =
 -F_{\nu\alpha}J^\alpha$, which is the Lorentz force density — confirming energy
-momentum conservation of the combined field + matter system.

---

## Summary

The electromagnetic stress-energy tensor, derived from the metric variation of t
he electromagnetic action, is:

$$\boxed{T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm EM} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\left(F_{\mu\alpha}\,F_{\nu}{}^{\a
lpha} - \frac{1}{4}\,g_{\mu\nu}\,F_{\alpha\beta}\,F^{\alpha\beta}\right)}$$

with components:

| Component | Expression | Physical meaning |
|---|---|---|
| $T_{00}$ | $\frac{1}{2\mu_0}(E^2+B^2)$ | Energy density |
| $T_{0i}=T_{i0}$ | $\frac{1}{\mu_0}(\vec{E}\times\vec{B})_i$ | Poynting vector
/ momentum density |
| $T_{ij}$ | $\frac{1}{\mu_0}\!\left(-E_iE_j - B_iB_j + \frac{1}{2}\delta_{ij}(E
^2+B^2)\right)$ | Maxwell stress (negative) |

The tensor is symmetric, traceless ($T^\mu{}_\mu=0$), and conserved ($\nabla^\mu
 T_{\mu\nu} = -F_{\nu\alpha}J^\alpha$).
