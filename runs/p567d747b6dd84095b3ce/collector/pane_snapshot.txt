**Claim.** $C[0,1]/M$ is isometrically isomorphic to the scalar field $\mathbb{K
}$ ($\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$), i.e. to the one-dimensional Banach space $\ma
thbb{K}$ with the absolute-value norm.

---

**Proof.**

Equip $C[0,1]$ with the supremum norm $\|f\|_\infty = \sup_{t\in[0,1]}|f(t)|$. T
he quotient norm on $C[0,1]/M$ is

$$
\|[f]\| \;=\; \inf_{g\in M}\|f-g\|_\infty .
$$

**Step 1 — Compute the quotient norm.** For $g\in M$ we have $g(0)=0$, hence $(f
-g)(0)=f(0)$, so

$$
\|f-g\|_\infty \;\ge\; |(f-g)(0)| \;=\; |f(0)|.
$$

Taking the infimum gives $\|[f]\|\ge |f(0)|$.

For the reverse inequality, set $g_0(t)=f(t)-f(0)$. Then $g_0\in C[0,1]$ and $g_
0(0)=f(0)-f(0)=0$, so $g_0\in M$. Moreover $f-g_0\equiv f(0)$ is constant, hence

$$
\|f-g_0\|_\infty = |f(0)|.
$$

Therefore $\|[f]\|\le |f(0)|$. Combining,

$$
\boxed{\;\|[f]\| = |f(0)|\;}.
$$

**Step 2 — Define the candidate isometry.** Define

$$
\Phi : C[0,1]/M \longrightarrow \mathbb{K},\qquad \Phi([f]) = f(0).
$$

*Well-defined:* if $[f_1]=[f_2]$, then $f_1-f_2\in M$, so $(f_1-f_2)(0)=0$, i.e.
 $f_1(0)=f_2(0)$.

*Linearity:* $\Phi(\alpha[f]+\beta[h]) = \Phi([\alpha f+\beta h]) = (\alpha f+\b
eta h)(0) = \alpha f(0)+\beta h(0) = \alpha\Phi([f])+\beta\Phi([h])$.

*Isometry:* by Step 1,
$$
|\Phi([f])| = |f(0)| = \|[f]\|.
$$

*Surjectivity:* for any $c\in\mathbb{K}$, the constant function $f\equiv c$ sati
sfies $\Phi([f])=c$.

**Step 3 — Conclusion.** $\Phi$ is a linear, surjective, norm-preserving bijecti
on between $C[0,1]/M$ and $\mathbb{K}$. Hence

$$
C[0,1]/M \;\cong_{\mathrm{iso}}\; \mathbb{K},
$$

the one-dimensional Banach space over the scalar field (equivalently, $\mathbb{R
}$ with $|\cdot|$ when the scalars are real, or $\mathbb{C}$ with $|\cdot|$ when
 the scalars are complex).

Intuitively, $M$ is the kernel of the evaluation functional $\delta_0:f\mapsto f
(0)$, so $C[0,1]/M$ is the image of $\delta_0$, which is all of $\mathbb{K}$, an
d the quotient norm coincides with $|f(0)|$ as shown above.
