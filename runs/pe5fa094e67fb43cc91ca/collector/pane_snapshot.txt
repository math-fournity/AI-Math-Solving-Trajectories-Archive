Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether inequality on a generating
π-system extends to all Borel sets. Let me reason through this carefully.

**Answer: No.** The comparison does not necessarily extend. Here is a counterexa
mple.

---

## Counterexample

Take $[a,b] = [0,1]$. Let
$$\mu = \delta_1 \quad\text{(Dirac mass at 1)}, \qquad \nu = \delta_0 \quad\text
{(Dirac mass at 0)}.$$
Both are finite measures on $\bigl([0,1], \mathcal{B}([0,1])\bigr)$ with total m
ass $1$.

**The π-system.** Define
$$\mathcal{P} = \bigl\{\, [0,x] : x \in [0,1] \,\bigr\}.$$

*π-system check:* For $x, y \in [0,1]$, $[0,x] \cap [0,y] = [0, \min(x,y)] \in \
mathcal{P}$. ✓

*Generates $\mathcal{B}([0,1])$:* For $a < b$ in $[0,1]$, $[0,b] \setminus [0,a]
 = (a,b]$, so all half-open intervals $(a,b]$ lie in $\sigma(\mathcal{P})$. Thes
e generate $\mathcal{B}([0,1])$. Also $\{0\} = \bigcap_{n} [0, 1/n] \in \sigma(\
mathcal{P})$. ✓

*Contains $[0,1]$:* $[0,1] = [0,1] \in \mathcal{P}$ (take $x=1$). ✓

**The hypothesis holds.** For every $B = [0,x] \in \mathcal{P}$:
- If $x < 1$: then $1 \notin [0,x]$ so $\mu([0,x]) = 0$, while $0 \in [0,x]$ so
$\nu([0,x]) = 1$. Thus $\mu([0,x]) = 0 \le 1 = \nu([0,x])$. ✓
- If $x = 1$: $\mu([0,1]) = 1 \le 1 = \nu([0,1])$. ✓

So $\mu(B) \le \nu(B)$ for every $B \in \mathcal{P}$.

**The conclusion fails.** Consider the Borel set $\{1\}$:
$$\mu(\{1\}) = 1 > 0 = \nu(\{1\}).$$
Hence $\mu \not\le \nu$.

---

## Why the π-λ argument breaks down

One might try to apply Dynkin's π-λ theorem to the class $\mathcal{D} = \{B : \m
u(B) \le \nu(B)\}$. While $[0,1] \in \mathcal{D}$ and $\mathcal{D}$ is closed un
der countable disjoint unions (by finite additivity and monotone convergence of
the partial sums), **$\mathcal{D}$ is not closed under complements**: for $A \in
 \mathcal{D}$,
$$\mu(A^c) \le \nu(A^c) \iff \mu(\Omega) - \mu(A) \le \nu(\Omega) - \nu(A) \iff
\mu(\Omega)-\nu(\Omega) \le \mu(A) - \nu(A),$$
which requires the *global* deficit $\mu(\Omega)-\nu(\Omega)$ to be no larger th
an the *local* deficit $\mu(A)-\nu(A)$—a condition that fails in the counterexam
ple (at $\Omega$ the deficit is $0$, but at $A=\{1\}$ it would be $+1$). Thus $\
mathcal{D}$ is not a λ-system, and the π-λ theorem cannot be applied to extend t
he inequality.

In contrast, the class $\{B : \mu(B) = \nu(B)\}$ *is* a λ-system (complement clo
sure uses $\mu(\Omega)=\nu(\Omega)$), which is why **equality** on a generating
π-system extends, but **inequality** does not.

---

**Conclusion:** The implication does **not** hold in general. The inequality $\m
u \le \nu$ on a generating π-system (even one containing $[a,b]$) does not force
 $\mu \le \nu$ on all of $\mathcal{B}([a,b])$.
