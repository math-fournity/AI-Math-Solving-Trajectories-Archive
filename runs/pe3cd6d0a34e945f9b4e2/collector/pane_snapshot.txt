Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether proper homotopy between pro
per maps implies equality of induced maps on compact support cohomology.

## Answer

**Yes.** If $f, g: X \to Y$ are proper maps between locally compact spaces that
are properly homotopic, then $f^\star = g^\star$ on $H_c^q(Y, M) \to H_c^q(X, M)
$ for all $q$.

## Proof

We compute $H_c^q(X, M)$ using the complex $C_c^\bullet(X, M)$ of compactly supp
orted singular cochains: a cochain $\varphi \in C^q(X, M)$ is compactly supporte
d if there exists a compact $K \subset X$ such that $\varphi(\sigma) = 0$ for ev
ery singular $q$-simplex $\sigma: \Delta^q \to X$ with $\operatorname{im}(\sigma
) \subset X \setminus K$.

**Step 1: Proper maps induce maps on compactly supported cochains.** A proper ma
p $h: X \to Y$ induces $h^\#: C_c^\bullet(Y, M) \to C_c^\bullet(X, M)$ via $(h^\
#\varphi)(\sigma) = \varphi(h \circ \sigma)$. If $\varphi$ is supported in compa
ct $L \subset Y$, then $h^\#\varphi$ is supported in $h^{-1}(L)$, which is compa
ct since $h$ is proper.

**Step 2: Setup.** Let $H: X \times [0,1] \to Y$ be a proper homotopy with $H(x,
0) = f(x)$, $H(x,1) = g(x)$. Since $X$ is locally compact and $[0,1]$ is compact
, $X \times [0,1]$ is locally compact, so the properness of $H$ is well-defined.
 Let $\pi: X \times [0,1] \to X$ be the projection and $i_t: X \to X \times [0,1
]$, $x \mapsto (x,t)$ the inclusions.

**Step 3: The prism operator and dual homotopy operator.** The standard prism op
erator $P: C_q(X) \to C_{q+1}(X \times [0,1])$ decomposes $\Delta^q \times [0,1]
$ into $(q+1)$-simplices and satisfies the chain homotopy relation:
$$\partial P - P\partial = i_{1\#} - i_{0\#}.$$

Dualizing and composing with $H_\#: C_\bullet(X \times [0,1]) \to C_\bullet(Y)$,
 define for $\varphi \in C^{q+1}(Y, M)$:
$$(K\varphi)(\sigma) = \varphi\big(H_\#(P(\sigma))\big), \quad \sigma \in C_q(X)
.$$

The standard computation yields the cochain homotopy relation:
$$\delta(K\varphi) + K(\delta\varphi) = g^\#\varphi - f^\#\varphi, \quad \text{i
.e.,} \quad \delta K + K\delta = g^\# - f^\#.$$

**Step 4: $K$ preserves compact supports (the key step).** Let $\varphi \in C_c^
{q+1}(Y, M)$ be supported in compact $L \subset Y$. Since $H$ is **proper**, $H^
{-1}(L)$ is compact in $X \times [0,1]$. Set
$$K_0 = \pi\big(H^{-1}(L)\big) \subset X.$$
Since $\pi$ is continuous, $K_0$ is compact.

Now take any singular $q$-simplex $\sigma: \Delta^q \to X$ with $\operatorname{i
m}(\sigma) \subset X \setminus K_0$. For every $t \in [0,1]$, the point $(\sigma
(s), t)$ satisfies $\pi(\sigma(s), t) = \sigma(s) \notin K_0 = \pi(H^{-1}(L))$,
so $(\sigma(s), t) \notin H^{-1}(L)$, i.e., $H(\sigma(s), t) \notin L$.

Every $(q+1)$-simplex appearing in $H_\#(P(\sigma))$ is of the form $H \circ (\s
igma \times \mathrm{id}) \circ \tau$ for some affine simplex $\tau$ into $\Delta
^q \times [0,1]$. Its image lies in $H(\operatorname{im}(\sigma) \times [0,1]) \
subset Y \setminus L$. Since $\varphi$ is supported in $L$, we have $\varphi = 0
$ on each such simplex, hence
$$(K\varphi)(\sigma) = 0.$$

This shows $K\varphi$ is supported in the compact set $K_0$, so $K\varphi \in C_
c^q(X, M)$. Thus $K: C_c^\bullet(Y, M) \to C_c^{\bullet-1}(X, M)$ is a well-defi
ned cochain homotopy.

**Step 5: Conclusion.** The relation $\delta K + K\delta = g^\# - f^\#$ holds on
 the compactly supported cochain complex $C_c^\bullet$. Therefore $f^\#$ and $g^
\#$ are chain homotopic as maps $C_c^\bullet(Y, M) \to C_c^\bullet(X, M)$, and c
onsequently they induce the same map on cohomology:
$$f^\star = g^\star: H_c^q(Y, M) \longrightarrow H_c^q(X, M) \quad \text{for all
 } q. \qquad \blacksquare$$

**Remark on the role of properness.** The properness of $H$ is essential: it ens
ures $H^{-1}(L)$ is compact, which makes $K_0 = \pi(H^{-1}(L))$ compact, which i
n turn ensures the homotopy operator $K$ sends compactly supported cochains to c
ompactly supported cochains. Without $H$ being proper, $K_0$ could be non-compac
t and $K\varphi$ would not be compactly supported, breaking the argument. Likewi
se, the properness of $f$ and $g$ is needed so that $f^\#$ and $g^\#$ map $C_c^\
bullet(Y)$ to $C_c^\bullet(X)$ at all.
