## Setup

Let there be $n$ roasters. The intended format is a complete round-robin ($\bino
m{n}{2}$ duels), but some duels were not played. Let roaster $A$ be the one with
 the highest points who also played the fewest duels. Let $d_A$ be the number of
 duels $A$ played. Every other roaster played $\ge d_A+1$ duels, and every roast
er plays at most $n-1$ duels.

Each duel distributes exactly 2 points total (2+0 or 1+1). $A$'s maximum possibl
e score is $2d_A$. For $A$ to be **strictly** highest, every other roaster must
score $\le 2d_A-1$.

## Key constraint: $A$ must miss at least 2 duels

Suppose $A$ misses a duel against roaster $X$. Then $X$ also misses that duel, s
o $X$ plays at most $n-2$ duels. For $X$ to play $\ge d_A+1$ duels we need $n-2
\ge d_A+1$, i.e. $d_A \le n-3$. Since $d_A = (n-1)-k$ where $k$ is the number of
 duels $A$ missed, this gives $k \ge 2$. So **$A$ must miss at least 2 duels**,
hence $d_A \le n-3$.

## $n=2,3$ impossible

- $n=2$: only one duel; both play 1 — no one plays fewer.
- $n=3$: $d_A \le 0$, so $A$ plays 0 duels and scores 0 — cannot be highest.

## $n=4$ impossible

With $n=4$ we need $k\ge 2$, so $d_A \le 1$.

**Case $d_A=1$ ($k=2$):** $A$ misses two roasters, say $C,D$, and plays only $AB

ut the three duels $BC,BD,CD$ distribute $3\times 2 = 6$ points among $B,C,D$, r

**Case $d_A=0$ ($k=3$):** $A$ plays nothing, scores 0 — cannot be highest.

So $n=4$ is impossible.
