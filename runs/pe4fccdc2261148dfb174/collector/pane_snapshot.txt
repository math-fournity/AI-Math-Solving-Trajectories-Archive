Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the comparison functor $J_T
: \operatorname{Kl}(T) \to \operatorname{EM}(T)$ always has a right adjoint, and
 characterize when it does.

## Setup

Let $(T, m, e)$ be a monad on $\mathcal{A}$. The **comparison functor** $J_T: \o
peratorname{Kl}(T) \to \operatorname{EM}(T)$ is:
- **Objects**: $X \mapsto (TX,\, m_X)$, the free $T$-algebra on $X$.
- **Morphisms**: A Kleisli arrow $f: X \to TY$ (morphism $X \to Y$ in $\operator
name{Kl}(T)$) maps to the algebra homomorphism $m_Y \circ Tf : (TX, m_X) \to (TY
, m_Y)$.

**Key facts:**
1. $J_T$ is **fully faithful**: $\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{EM}}((TX,m_X)
,(TY,m_Y)) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X, TY) = \operatorname{Hom}_{\
operatorname{Kl}}(X,Y)$ via $\bar{f} \mapsto \bar{f}\circ e_X$, with inverse $f
\mapsto m_Y \circ Tf$.
2. The essential image of $J_T$ is the full subcategory of **free algebras**.
3. $J_T \circ F_T = F^T$, where $F_T \dashv U_T$ (Kleisli) and $F^T \dashv U^T$
(EM).

## Theorem

**$J_T$ has a right adjoint if and only if every $T$-algebra is free (equivalent
ly, $J_T$ is an equivalence of categories). In general, the answer is no.**

## Proof

### $(\Leftarrow)$: Every algebra free $\Rightarrow$ right adjoint exists

If every $T$-algebra is free, $J_T$ is essentially surjective. Being also fully
faithful, $J_T$ is an **equivalence of categories**. Any equivalence admits a ri
ght adjoint (its quasi-inverse). $\checkmark$

### $(\Rightarrow)$: Right adjoint exists $\Rightarrow$ every algebra is free

Suppose $J_T \dashv R$ for some $R: \operatorname{EM}(T) \to \operatorname{Kl}(T
)$.

**Step 1: Derive $U_T \circ R \cong U^T$.**

Since $J_T \circ F_T = F^T$ and composition of adjoints gives $J_T \circ F_T \da
shv U_T \circ R$, while $F^T \dashv U^T$, uniqueness of right adjoints yields:
$$U_T \circ R \cong U^T.$$

**Step 2: Set up the adjunction isomorphism.**

For any algebra $(A, a)$, set $Y = R(A,a) \in \operatorname{Ob}(\operatorname{Kl
}(T)) = \operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal{A})$. The adjunction gives a natural isomorph
ism (natural in $X \in \operatorname{Kl}(T)$):
$$\Phi_X : \operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{EM}}(J_T(X),\, (A,a)) \xrightarrow{
\;\sim\;} \operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Kl}}(X,\, Y).$$

By the universal property of free algebras, the left side is $\operatorname{Hom}
_{\mathcal{A}}(X, A)$ (via $\bar{g} \mapsto \bar{g} \circ e_X$). The right side
is $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X, TY)$. So $\Phi_X : \operatorname{Hom}_{\
mathcal{A}}(X, A) \to \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X, TY)$, natural in $X$ *
*with respect to Kleisli morphisms**.

**Step 3: Compute the action explicitly.**

Set $\delta = \Phi_A(\operatorname{id}_A) : A \to TY$.

**Claim:** $\Phi_X(g) = \delta \circ g$ for all $g: X \to A$.

*Proof of claim.* Any $g: X \to A$ can be written as $g = a \circ (e_A \circ g)$
 since $a \circ e_A = \operatorname{id}_A$. Set $f = e_A \circ g : X \to TA$ (a
Kleisli morphism $X \to A$) and $g_0 = \operatorname{id}_A$. Then $a \circ T(g_0
) \circ f = a \circ e_A \circ g = g$.

Naturality of $\Phi$ with respect to the Kleisli morphism $f: X \to A$ gives:
$$\Phi_X\bigl(a \circ T(g_0) \circ f\bigr) = m_Y \circ T\bigl(\Phi_A(g_0)\bigr)
\circ f.$$

The left side is $\Phi_X(g)$. The right side is $m_Y \circ T\delta \circ e_A \ci
rc g$. By naturality of $e$, $T\delta \circ e_A = e_{TY} \circ \delta$, and by t
he monad law $m_Y \circ e_{TY} = \operatorname{id}_{TY}$:
$$\Phi_X(g) = \delta \circ g. \qquad \checkmark$$

**Step 4: $\delta$ is an isomorphism in $\mathcal{A}$.**

Since $\Phi_X$ is a bijection for every $X$, the map $g \mapsto \delta \circ g$
is a bijection $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X, A) \to \operatorname{Hom}_{\
mathcal{A}}(X, TY)$ for all $X$. By the **Yoneda lemma** (in $\mathcal{A}$), $\d
elta: A \to TY$ is an isomorphism.

**Step 5: $\delta$ is an algebra homomorphism.**

Set $X = TA$, $g = a: TA \to A$, and use the Kleisli morphism $f = \operatorname
{id}_{TA}: TA \to TA$ (identity morphism $A \to A$ in $\operatorname{Kl}(T)$) wi
th $g_0 = \operatorname{id}_A$ in the naturality condition:
$$\Phi_{TA}(a) = m_Y \circ T\delta \circ \operatorname{id}_{TA} = m_Y \circ T\de
lta.$$

By the claim, $\Phi_{TA}(a) = \delta \circ a$. Therefore:
$$\delta \circ a = m_Y \circ T\delta.$$

This is precisely the **algebra homomorphism condition** for $\delta: (A, a) \to
 (TY, m_Y)$.

**Step 6: Conclude.**

$\delta: (A, a) \to (TY, m_Y)$ is an algebra homomorphism that is an isomorphism
 on underlying objects, hence an **isomorphism in $\operatorname{EM}(T)$**. Sinc
e $(TY, m_Y)$ is a free algebra, $(A, a)$ is free. Since $(A, a)$ was arbitrary,
 **every $T$-algebra is free**. $\blacksquare$

## Counterexample (answer is "no" in general)

Let $T$ be the **free monoid (list) monad** on $\mathbf{Set}$: $T(X) = X^*$, $e_
X(x) = [x]$, $m_X(\text{list of lists}) = \text{flatten}$. The Eilenberg–Moore a
lgebras are **monoids**. The free algebra on $X$ is the free monoid $(X^*, \text
{concat})$.

The monoid $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is **not a free monoid** (any free monoid is
 either trivial or infinite, being isomorphic to $X^*$ for some set $X$). Hence
not every $T$-algebra is free, and by the theorem, $J_T$ does **not** have a rig
ht adjoint.

## Summary

The embedding functor $J_T: \operatorname{Kl}(T) \to \operatorname{EM}(T)$ has a
 right adjoint **if and only if every $T$-algebra is free** (equivalently, $J_T$
 is an equivalence). This condition fails in general — for instance, for the fre
e monoid monad on $\mathbf{Set}$, where non-free monoids exist. Therefore, **$J_
T$ does not have a right adjoint in general**.
