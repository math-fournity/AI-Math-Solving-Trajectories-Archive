Looking at the problem: given $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n \frac{1-e^{-n^2 t}}{n} =
0$ for all $t > 0$, must $a_n = 0$ for all $n$?

**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

Define $S(t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{a_n}{n}(1 - e^{-n^2 t})$, which equals
$0$ for every $t > 0$ by hypothesis.

**Step 1: Boundedness of $\frac{a_n}{n}$.**

Since $S(t)$ converges for each fixed $t > 0$, its terms tend to zero. As $n \to
 \infty$, $1 - e^{-n^2 t} \to 1$, so $\frac{a_n}{n} \to 0$. In particular, $\lef
t|\frac{a_n}{n}\right| \leq M$ for some constant $M$ and all $n$.

**Step 2: Form a Dirichlet-type series that converges absolutely.**

For any $t > 0$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, compute $S(t) - S(t + \varepsilon) = 0 -
0 = 0$:

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{a_n}{n}\bigl[(1 - e^{-n^2 t}) - (1 - e^{-n^2(t+\vare
psilon)})\bigr] = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{a_n}{n}\, e^{-n^2 t}\bigl(e^{-n^2 \v
arepsilon} - 1\bigr) = 0.$$

Setting $b_n := \frac{a_n}{n}(1 - e^{-n^2 \varepsilon})$, this becomes:

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n\, e^{-n^2 t} = 0 \quad \text{for all } t > 0. \tag{$\a
st$}$$

This series converges **absolutely**: $|b_n\, e^{-n^2 t}| \leq M \cdot e^{-n^2 t
}$, and $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} e^{-n^2 t} < \infty$ for every $t > 0$.

**Step 3: Uniqueness of the Dirichlet series.**

We claim: if $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n\, e^{-\lambda_n t} = 0$ for all $t > 0$, w
ith $\lambda_n = n^2$ (strictly increasing, $\lambda_n \to \infty$) and absolute
 convergence for all $t > 0$, then $b_n = 0$ for all $n$.

Suppose for contradiction that not all $b_n$ vanish. Let $n_0$ be the **smallest
** index with $b_{n_0} \neq 0$. From ($\ast$):

$$b_{n_0}\, e^{-\lambda_{n_0} t} = -\sum_{n > n_0} b_n\, e^{-\lambda_n t},$$

hence

$$|b_{n_0}| \leq \sum_{n > n_0} |b_n|\, e^{-(\lambda_n - \lambda_{n_0}) t}. \tag
{$\ast\ast$}$$

For any fixed $t_0 > 0$ and all $t \geq t_0$, the right-hand side of ($\ast\ast$
) is dominated by $\sum_{n > n_0} |b_n|\, e^{-(\lambda_n - \lambda_{n_0}) t_0}$,
 which is finite (by absolute convergence of ($\ast$) at $t_0$). Each summand $|
b_n| e^{-(\lambda_n - \lambda_{n_0}) t} \to 0$ as $t \to \infty$ (since $\lambda
_n > \lambda_{n_0}$). By the **Dominated Convergence Theorem** (applied to the c
ounting measure), the entire sum $\to 0$ as $t \to \infty$.

Taking $t \to \infty$ in ($\ast\ast$) yields $|b_{n_0}| = 0$, i.e., $b_{n_0} = 0
$—contradicting the choice of $n_0$.

Therefore $b_n = 0$ for all $n$.

**Step 4: Conclude $a_n = 0$.**

From $b_n = \frac{a_n}{n}(1 - e^{-n^2 \varepsilon}) = 0$ with $\varepsilon > 0$
fixed: since $1 - e^{-n^2 \varepsilon} \neq 0$ for every $n \geq 1$, we obtain $
\frac{a_n}{n} = 0$, hence

$$a_n = 0 \quad \text{for all } n \in \mathbb{N}. \qquad \blacksquare$$
