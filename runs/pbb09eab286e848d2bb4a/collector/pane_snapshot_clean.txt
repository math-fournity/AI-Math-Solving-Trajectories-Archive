## Proof

**Step 1: Establish strict diagonal dominance and positive diagonal.**

For each column $j$, the column sum is strictly positive:
$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_{ij} = a_{jj} + \sum_{i \neq j} a_{ij} > 0.$$

Since every off-diagonal entry satisfies $a_{ij} \leq 0$ (for $i \neq j$), we ha
ve $\sum_{i\neq j} a_{ij} = -\sum_{i\neq j}|a_{ij}|$. Hence
$$a_{jj} - \sum_{i \neq j}|a_{ij}| > 0 \quad \text{for every } j. \tag{1}$$

In particular $a_{jj} > \sum_{i\neq j}|a_{ij}| \geq 0$, so **every diagonal entr
y is strictly positive**, and $A$ is **strictly (row) diagonally dominant** (usi
ng symmetry, row and column dominance coincide).

**Step 2: Apply the Gershgorin circle theorem.**

Let $\lambda$ be any eigenvalue of $A$ (over $\mathbb{C}$). By Gershgorin's theo
rem, $\lambda$ lies in the union of the discs
$$\overline{D}\!\left(a_{ii},\, R_i\right), \qquad R_i := \sum_{j \neq i}|a_{ij}
|.$$

By (1), each center satisfies $a_{ii} > R_i \geq 0$. Therefore every disc $\over
line{D}(a_{ii}, R_i)$ is contained in the open right half-plane:
$$\overline{D}(a_{ii}, R_i) \subseteq \{z \in \mathbb{C} : \operatorname{Re}(z)
\geq a_{ii} - R_i > 0\}.$$

Consequently every eigenvalue of $A$ has **strictly positive real part**:
$$\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) > 0. \tag{2}$$

**Step 3: Use symmetry to conclude all eigenvalues are real and positive.**

Since $A$ is real symmetric, all of its eigenvalues are real. Combining with (2)
, every eigenvalue $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$ of $A$ satisfies
$$\lambda_k > 0 \quad \text{for } k = 1, \dots, n.$$

**Step 4: Conclude the determinant is positive.**

The determinant equals the product of the eigenvalues:
$$\det(A) = \prod_{k=1}^{n} \lambda_k > 0,$$
since it is a product of $n$ strictly positive real numbers.

$\blacksquare$
