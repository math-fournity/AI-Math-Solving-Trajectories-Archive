Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest $n$ such that there exist r
eal $n \times n$ matrices $A, B$ with $A+iB$ invertible, but $A, B, A+B, A-B, A+
2B, A-2B$ all singular.

## Proof

**Key tool.** Define $f(t) = \det(A + tB)$, a polynomial in $t$ of degree $\leq
n$ with real coefficients (since $A, B$ are real). The leading coefficient (of $
t^n$) is $\det(B)$, because the $t^n$ term in the Leibniz expansion selects $tB_
{i\sigma(i)}$ from every row, summing to $\det(B)$.

**Lower bound: $n \geq 6$.**

The six singularity conditions translate to:
- $\det(A) = 0 \Rightarrow f(0) = 0$,
- $\det(A+B) = 0 \Rightarrow f(1) = 0$,
- $\det(A-B) = 0 \Rightarrow f(-1) = 0$,
- $\det(A+2B) = 0 \Rightarrow f(2) = 0$,
- $\det(A-2B) = 0 \Rightarrow f(-2) = 0$,
- $\det(B) = 0 \Rightarrow$ leading coefficient of $f$ is $0$, so $\deg f \leq n
-1$.

Since $P = A + iB \in GL_n(\mathbb{C})$, we have $f(i) = \det(A+iB) \neq 0$, so
$f$ is not identically zero.

Thus $f$ is a **nonzero** real polynomial of degree $\leq n-1$ with **five disti
nct roots** $0, 1, -1, 2, -2$. A nonzero polynomial of degree $d$ has at most $d
$ roots, so $n - 1 \geq 5$, giving:

$$\boxed{n \geq 6.}$$

**Upper bound: $n = 6$ is achievable.**

Take $A$ and $B$ to be diagonal:
$$A = \operatorname{diag}(0,\ -1,\ 1,\ -2,\ 2,\ 1), \qquad B = \operatorname{dia
g}(1,\ 1,\ 1,\ 1,\ 1,\ 0).$$

Then
$$f(t) = \det(A + tB) = t \cdot (t-1) \cdot (t+1) \cdot (t-2) \cdot (t+2) \cdot
1 = t(t^2-1)(t^2-4),$$
a degree-5 polynomial (consistent with $\deg f \leq 5 = n-1$). Verification of a
ll conditions:

| Matrix | Diagonal entries | Determinant |
|--------|-----------------|-------------|
| $A$ | $(0, -1, 1, -2, 2, 1)$ | $0$ ✓ (entry $a_1=0$) |
| $B$ | $(1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 0)$ | $0$ ✓ (entry $b_6=0$) |
| $A+B$ | $(1, 0, 2, -1, 3, 1)$ | $0$ ✓ (entry $a_2+b_2=0$) |
| $A-B$ | $(-1, -2, 0, -3, 1, 1)$ | $0$ ✓ (entry $a_3-b_3=0$) |
| $A+2B$ | $(2, 1, 3, 0, 4, 1)$ | $0$ ✓ (entry $a_4+2b_4=0$) |
| $A-2B$ | $(-2, -3, -1, -4, 0, 1)$ | $0$ ✓ (entry $a_5-2b_5=0$) |

For invertibility of $P = A + iB$:
$$A + iB = \operatorname{diag}(i,\ -1+i,\ 1+i,\ -2+i,\ 2+i,\ 1),$$
and every diagonal entry is a nonzero complex number ($i \neq 0$, $-1+i \neq 0$,
 $1+i \neq 0$, $-2+i \neq 0$, $2+i \neq 0$, $1 \neq 0$). Hence
$$\det(A + iB) = i(-1+i)(1+i)(-2+i)(2+i) \cdot 1 \neq 0,$$
confirming $P \in GL_6(\mathbb{C})$.

**Conclusion.** The lower bound forces $n \geq 6$, and the explicit construction
 above realizes $n = 6$. Therefore the smallest possible value is

$$n = 6.$$
