## Proof

The function is given by the power series

$$f(z) = \sum_{p\text{ prime}} z^p = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n z^n, \qquad a_n = \
begin{cases} 1 & n \text{ prime},\\ 0 & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$

**Step 1: Radius of convergence.** By the Cauchy–Hadamard formula, the radius of
 convergence is

$$R = \frac{1}{\limsup_{n\to\infty} |a_n|^{1/n}}.$$

For every prime $n$, $|a_n|^{1/n} = 1^{1/n} = 1$. Since there are infinitely man
y primes, the value $1$ is attained along an unbounded subsequence, so

$$\limsup_{n\to\infty} |a_n|^{1/n} = 1, \qquad R = 1.$$

**Step 2: Convergence inside the unit disk.** For $|z|<1$,

$$\sum_{p\text{ prime}} |z|^p \le \sum_{n=2}^{\infty} |z|^n = \frac{|z|^2}{1-|z|
} < \infty,$$

so the series converges absolutely (and uniformly on compact subsets of $B_1(0)$
), hence defines an analytic function on $B_1(0)$.

**Step 3: Divergence outside the unit disk.** For $|z|>1$, the general term $|z|
^p \to \infty$ along primes, so in particular $z^p \not\to 0$; the series diverg
es at every point with $|z|>1$. Consequently the series cannot define a function
 — analytic or otherwise — on any ball $B_r(0)$ with $r>1$.

**Step 4: Maximality.** The series converges exactly on $B_1(0)$ and fails to co
nverge at every point outside the closed unit disk. Therefore the largest radius
 $r$ for which $f(z)=\sum_{p\text{ prime}} z^p$ defines an analytic function on
$B_r(0)$ is

$$\boxed{r = 1}.$$
