Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum number of distinct encryptio
n keys such that 10 distinct teams, each holding exactly 3 keys, are pairwise in
tersecting (any two teams share at least one key).

This is a problem in extremal set theory: find the minimum ground set size $n$ f
or which there exist 10 distinct 3-element subsets that are pairwise intersectin
g.

## Proof

**Notation.** Let $[n] = \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}$. We seek the minimum $n$ such that
 there exist 10 distinct 3-element subsets of $[n]$, any two of which share at l
east one element.

**Lower bound: $n \geq 5$.**

With $n = 4$, the total number of distinct 3-element subsets is $\binom{4}{3} =
4 < 10$. Since we need 10 *distinct* key sets, $n = 4$ is impossible. Hence $n \
geq 5$.

**Upper bound: $n = 5$ suffices.**

Take the ground set $[5] = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$. The collection of all 3-element s
ubsets is:
$$\binom{[5]}{3} = \big\{\{1,2,3\},\; \{1,2,4\},\; \{1,2,5\},\; \{1,3,4\},\; \{1
,3,5\},\; \{1,4,5\},\; \{2,3,4\},\; \{2,3,5\},\; \{2,4,5\},\; \{3,4,5\}\big\}.$$

There are exactly $\binom{5}{3} = 10$ such subsets.

**Pairwise intersection property.** Any two 3-element subsets $A, B$ of a 5-elem
ent set satisfy $|A \cap B| \geq 1$. This follows from the pigeonhole principle:
$$|A \cap B| = |A| + |B| - |A \cup B| \geq 3 + 3 - 5 = 1.$$
Since $|A \cup B| \leq 5$, we get $|A \cap B| \geq 1$.

Therefore, all 10 triples are pairwise intersecting, giving us exactly 10 distin
ct teams with pairwise shared keys, using only 5 keys.

**Conclusion.** The lower bound $n \geq 5$ and the construction with $n = 5$ tog
ether establish that the minimum possible number of distinct encryption keys is:

$$\boxed{5}$$
