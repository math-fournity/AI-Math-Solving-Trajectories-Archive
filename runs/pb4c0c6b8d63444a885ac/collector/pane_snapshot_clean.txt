## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, such a set $A$ exists.

**Strategy.** It suffices to construct two *disjoint* measurable sets $F, G \sub
seteq \mathbb{R}$ such that both have strictly positive measure in every non-deg
enerate interval. Then setting $A := F$ gives
$$0 < m(F \cap I) \leq m(A \cap I), \qquad m(A^c \cap I) \geq m(G \cap I) > 0,$$
so $0 < m(A\cap I) < m(I)$ for every interval $I$ with $m(I)>0$.

**Setup.** Let $\{J_1, J_2, J_3, \dots\}$ be an enumeration of all open interval
s with rational endpoints. Every non-degenerate interval $I$ contains some $J_n$
, so it is enough to ensure $m(F\cap J_n)>0$ and $m(G\cap J_n)>0$ for every $n$.

**Inductive construction.** We build finite collections of closed, nowhere-dense
, pairwise-disjoint sets
$$F_1,\dots,F_n,\; G_1,\dots,G_n$$
with $F_i,G_i \subseteq J_i$ and $m(F_i)>0,\; m(G_i)>0$.

*Base case ($n=1$).* Inside $J_1$ pick two disjoint open subintervals $U,V$. In
each, place a fat Cantor set (Smith–Volterra–Cantor type) of positive measure; c
all them $F_1\subseteq U$ and $G_1\subseteq V$. These are closed, nowhere dense,
 disjoint, and of positive measure.

*Inductive step.* Suppose $F_1,\dots,F_n,G_1,\dots,G_n$ have been constructed, a
ll closed, nowhere dense, and pairwise disjoint. Their union
$$K_n \;=\; \bigcup_{i=1}^{n}(F_i\cup G_i)$$
is a *finite* union of closed nowhere-dense sets, hence is itself closed and now
here dense. Therefore $K_n$ contains no interval, so $J_{n+1}\setminus K_n$ is a
 non-empty open set. Choose two disjoint open subintervals $U',V'\subseteq J_{n+
1}\setminus K_n$. Place fat Cantor sets $F_{n+1}\subseteq U'$ and $G_{n+1}\subse
teq V'$, each of positive measure. Then the extended collection remains pairwise
 disjoint, closed, nowhere dense, with $F_{n+1},G_{n+1}\subseteq J_{n+1}$ and bo
th of positive measure.

**Definition of $A$.** Set
$$F \;=\; \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} F_n, \qquad G \;=\; \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} G_n,
 \qquad A \;=\; F.$$
Since each $F_n$ is closed, $F$ is an $F_\sigma$ set, hence measurable; likewise
 $G$ is measurable. By construction every $F_n$ is disjoint from every $G_m$, so
 $F\cap G=\varnothing$, i.e. $G\subseteq A^c$.

**Verification.** Let $I$ be any interval with $m(I)>0$. Pick $J_n\subseteq I$ (
possible because rational-endpoint intervals form a basis). Then:
- $m(A\cap I)\;\geq\; m(F\cap J_n)\;\geq\; m(F_n)\;>\;0.$
- $m(I)-m(A\cap I)\;=\;m(A^c\cap I)\;\geq\;m(G\cap I)\;\geq\;m(G\cap J_n)\;\geq\
;m(G_n)\;>\;0.$

Combining,
$$0 \;<\; m(A\cap I) \;<\; m(I).$$

Since $I$ was arbitrary, the set $A$ satisfies the required property for every i
nterval. $\blacksquare$
