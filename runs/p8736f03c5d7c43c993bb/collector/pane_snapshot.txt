# Solution

**Answer: No.** A non-amenable connected Lie group cannot have a faithful finite
-dimensional unitary representation.

## Proof

Suppose, for contradiction, that $G$ is a non-amenable connected Lie group admit
ting a faithful finite-dimensional unitary representation $\rho: G \to U(n)$.

### Step 1. The Lie algebra of $G$ is a compact Lie algebra.

Since $\rho$ is faithful, it induces an injective Lie algebra homomorphism $d\rh
o: \mathfrak{g} \hookrightarrow \mathfrak{u}(n)$. The Lie algebra $\mathfrak{u}(
n)$ is a **compact Lie algebra**: it carries the $\mathrm{Ad}$-invariant positiv
e-definite symmetric bilinear form
$$\langle X, Y\rangle = -\mathrm{tr}(XY),$$
which is invariant because $\mathrm{Ad}_g$ (for $g \in U(n)$) is conjugation by
a unitary matrix, preserving $-\mathrm{tr}(XY)$.

A key fact: **any Lie subalgebra of a compact Lie algebra is itself compact.** I
ndeed, if $\mathfrak{h} \subseteq \mathfrak{g}$ and $B$ is an $\mathrm{ad}$-inva
riant positive-definite form on $\mathfrak{g}$, then the restriction $B|_{\mathf
rak{h}}$ is an $\mathrm{ad}$-invariant positive-definite form on $\mathfrak{h}$
(invariance under $\mathfrak{h}$ follows from invariance under all of $\mathfrak
{g}$). Therefore $\mathfrak{g}$, being a subalgebra of the compact algebra $\mat
hfrak{u}(n)$, is a compact Lie algebra.

### Step 2. Structure of a compact Lie algebra.

A compact Lie algebra decomposes as
$$\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}) \oplus [\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}],
$$
where $\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})$ is the center and $[\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g
}]$ is **semisimple and compact**. The radical (maximal solvable ideal) of $\mat
hfrak{g}$ is exactly $\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})$, since a semisimple Lie algebr
a has no nonzero solvable ideals. In particular $\mathrm{rad}(\mathfrak{g}) = \m
athfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})$ is **abelian**.

### Step 3. The semisimple quotient $G/\mathrm{Rad}(G)$ is compact.

For a connected Lie group $G$, the radical $\mathrm{Rad}(G)$ is the connected Li
e subgroup with Lie algebra $\mathrm{rad}(\mathfrak{g}) = \mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak
{g})$. Since $\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})$ is abelian, $\mathrm{Rad}(G)$ is abeli
an (hence solvable, hence amenable).

The quotient $G/\mathrm{Rad}(G)$ is a connected Lie group with Lie algebra $\mat
hfrak{g}/\mathrm{rad}(\mathfrak{g}) \cong [\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}]$, which i
s **compact semisimple**. A classical theorem states:

> *A connected semisimple Lie group with compact Lie algebra is compact.*

(Proof: the simply connected Lie group with a compact semisimple Lie algebra is
compact — this follows because the Killing form is negative definite, so the gro
up is a closed subgroup of its adjoint form times a torus, all compact; and any
quotient of a compact group is compact.)

Therefore $G/\mathrm{Rad}(G)$ is **compact**.

### Step 4. $G$ is amenable — contradiction.

Amenability of connected Lie groups is characterized by:

> *A connected Lie group $G$ is amenable if and only if $G/\mathrm{Rad}(G)$ is c
ompact.*

(This is the standard characterization: a connected Lie group is amenable iff it
s semisimple part is compact; equivalently, the quotient by the solvable radical
 is compact.)

We have shown:
- $\mathrm{Rad}(G)$ is abelian, hence amenable.
- $G/\mathrm{Rad}(G)$ is compact, hence amenable.

Amenability is preserved under **group extensions**: if $N \trianglelefteq G$ wi
th $N$ and $G/N$ both amenable, then $G$ is amenable. Applying this with $N = \m
athrm{Rad}(G)$:

$$G \text{ is amenable.}$$

This contradicts the assumption that $G$ is non-amenable.

### Conclusion

The assumption that a non-amenable connected Lie group admits a faithful finite-
dimensional unitary representation leads to a contradiction. The crucial mechani
sm is:

1. A faithful unitary representation embeds $\mathfrak{g}$ into the compact Lie
algebra $\mathfrak{u}(n)$.
2. Subalgebras of compact Lie algebras are compact.
3. A connected Lie group with compact Lie algebra has compact semisimple quotien
t, hence is amenable.

Therefore, **no non-amenable connected Lie group can have a faithful finite-dime
nsional unitary representation**.
