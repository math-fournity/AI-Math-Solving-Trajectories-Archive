## Proof

**Criterion 1 (Universal Unity):** We need a positive integer $n$ such that for
every positive integer $m$, $n \equiv 1 \pmod{m}$.

This is equivalent to: $m \mid (n - 1)$ for every positive integer $m$.

If $n - 1 \neq 0$, then $n - 1$ is a fixed positive integer, which has only fini
tely many divisors. But the condition requires $n - 1$ to be divisible by *every
* positive integer $m$, including $m = n$ (if $n > 1$) and indeed $m$ arbitraril
y large. A nonzero integer cannot be divisible by integers larger than its absol
ute value. Therefore $n - 1 = 0$, giving $n = 1$.

**Verification of $n = 1$:** For every positive integer $m$, $1 \equiv 1 \pmod{m
}$, since $m \mid (1 - 1) = 0$. ✓

**Criterion 2 (Complexity Preservation):** For $n = 1$:
- $n^2 = 1^2 = 1$
- Digit sum of $n = 1$
- Digit sum of $n^2 = 1$
- $1 \geq 1$ ✓

**Conclusion:** The only positive integer satisfying both criteria is $n = 1$. T
he number of unique positive integers qualifying as "Stable" Core Indices is $\b
oxed{1}$.
