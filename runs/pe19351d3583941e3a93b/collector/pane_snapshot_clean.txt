## Proof

**Claim:** Such a real number exists. Take $r = \sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}$.

**$r$ is not transcendental.** Both $\sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{3}$ are algebraic over
 $\mathbb{Q}$, so their sum $r$ is algebraic. Hence $r$ is not transcendental (a
nd $r$ is clearly real).

**$r^n$ is irrational for every integer $n\ge 1$.** Consider the field $K=\mathb
b{Q}(\sqrt{2},\sqrt{3})$, which is the splitting field of $(x^2-2)(x^2-3)$ over
$\mathbb{Q}$, hence Galois over $\mathbb{Q}$. Its Galois group is
$$\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})\cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^2,$$
generated by the automorphisms
$$\sigma_1:\sqrt{2}\mapsto -\sqrt{2},\ \sqrt{3}\mapsto \sqrt{3},\qquad \sigma_2:
\sqrt{2}\mapsto \sqrt{2},\ \sqrt{3}\mapsto -\sqrt{3}.$$
Since $K/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois, an element $\alpha\in K$ lies in $\mathbb{Q}$ if
 and only if $\tau(\alpha)=\alpha$ for every $\tau\in\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$
.

Suppose, for contradiction, that $r^n\in\mathbb{Q}$ for some $n\ge 1$. Then $\si
gma_1(r^n)=r^n$, i.e.
$$(\sigma_1(r))^n = r^n.$$
Now $\sigma_1(r)=-\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}=\sqrt{3}-\sqrt{2}$, while $r=\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{
3}$. Taking absolute values,
$$|\sigma_1(r)|=\sqrt{3}-\sqrt{2}\approx 0.318,\qquad |r|=\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}\appr
ox 3.146.$$
Both are positive, and $\sqrt{3}-\sqrt{2}\ne \sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}$ (indeed $\sqrt{3
}-\sqrt{2}<1<\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}$). Therefore
$$|\sigma_1(r)|^n = (\sqrt{3}-\sqrt{2})^n < 1 < (\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3})^n = |r|^n\qq
uad (n\ge 1),$$

Hence $r^n\notin\mathbb{Q}$ for every $n\ge 1$; i.e., $r^n$ is irrational for al
