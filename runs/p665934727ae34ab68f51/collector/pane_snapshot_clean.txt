## Proof

We start from the first (standard heat-kernel convolution) form and perform a li
near change of variables to obtain the second form.

**First form.** The temperature at point $(x,y,z)$ at time $t$ is

$$u(x,y,z,t)=\frac{1}{8a^{3}(\pi t)^{3/2}}\iiint_{\mathbb R^{3}} \exp\!\left(-\f
rac{(\lambda-x)^{2}+(\mu-y)^{2}+(\nu-z)^{2}}{4a^{2}t}\right) f(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\
,d\lambda\,d\mu\,d\nu.$$

**Change of variables.** Introduce dimensionless variables centered at the obser
vation point:

$$\beta=\frac{\lambda-x}{2a\sqrt t},\qquad \gamma=\frac{\mu-y}{2a\sqrt t},\qquad
 \sigma=\frac{\nu-z}{2a\sqrt t}.$$

Equivalently,

$$\lambda=x+2a\sqrt t\,\beta,\quad \mu=y+2a\sqrt t\,\gamma,\quad \nu=z+2a\sqrt t
\,\sigma.$$

The Jacobian of this (diagonal, linear) transformation is

$$d\lambda\,d\mu\,d\nu=(2a\sqrt t)^{3}\,d\beta\,d\gamma\,d\sigma=8a^{3}t^{3/2}\,
d\beta\,d\gamma\,d\sigma.$$

**Exponential.** The quadratic form in the exponent becomes a sum of squares:

$$\frac{(\lambda-x)^{2}+(\mu-y)^{2}+(\nu-z)^{2}}{4a^{2}t}=\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2}+\
sigma^{2},$$

so

$$\exp\!\left(-\frac{(\lambda-x)^{2}+(\mu-y)^{2}+(\nu-z)^{2}}{4a^{2}t}\right)=e^
{-\beta^{2}}e^{-\gamma^{2}}e^{-\sigma^{2}}.$$

**Prefactor.** The constant in front of the integral transforms as

$$\frac{1}{8a^{3}(\pi t)^{3/2}}\cdot(2a\sqrt t)^{3}
=\frac{8a^{3}t^{3/2}}{8a^{3}\,\pi^{3/2}\,t^{3/2}}
=\frac{1}{\pi^{3/2}}.$$

**Assembling the second form.** Substituting everything back,

$$u(x,y,z,t)=\frac{1}{\pi^{3/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\beta^{2}}d\beta\int_
{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\gamma^{2}}d\gamma\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\sigma^{2}} f
\!\left(x+2a\sqrt t\,\beta,\;y+2a\sqrt t\,\gamma,\;z+2a\sqrt t\,\sigma\right)d\s
igma.$$

**Remark on the prefactor printed in the problem.** The statement writes $\frac{
1}{\pi^{3}}$ in the second form. This is a typographical error: it should read $
\frac{1}{\pi^{3/2}}$. The consistency check is immediate — setting $f\equiv 1$ (
constant initial temperature $=1$), the solution must remain $u\equiv 1$ for all
 $t$. With the correct prefactor,

$$\frac{1}{\pi^{3/2}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\beta^{2}}d\beta\right)^{3
}=\frac{1}{\pi^{3/2}}\cdot(\sqrt\pi)^{3}=\frac{\pi^{3/2}}{\pi^{3/2}}=1,$$

as required. With $\frac{1}{\pi^{3}}$ one would instead obtain $\pi^{3/2}/\pi^{3
}=\pi^{-3/2}\neq 1$, violating conservation of a constant initial condition. Hen
ce the correct normalization is $\frac{1}{\pi^{3/2}}$.

**Conclusion.** By the linear substitution $\lambda=x+2a\sqrt t\,\beta$, $\mu=y+
2a\sqrt t\,\gamma$, $\nu=z+2a\sqrt t\,\sigma$, the heat-kernel convolution reduc
es to the dimensionless Gaussian-weighted average

$$u=\frac{1}{\pi^{3/2}}\iiint_{\mathbb R^{3}} e^{-(\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2}+\sigma^{
2})}\, f\!\left(x+2a\sqrt t\,\beta,\,y+2a\sqrt t\,\gamma,\,z+2a\sqrt t\,\sigma\r
ight)d\beta\,d\gamma\,d\sigma,$$

which is the desired second representation (with the prefactor corrected to $\pi
^{-3/2}$).
