# Proof

**Claim.** Yes. The image (range) of a compact linear operator between Banach sp
aces cannot contain a closed subspace of infinite dimension.

**Proof.** Let $T:X\to Y$ be compact, and suppose, for a contradiction, that $T(
X)$ contains a closed subspace $M\subseteq Y$ with $\dim M=\infty$.

**Step 1. A bounded right-inverse on $M$.** Set $E:=T^{-1}(M)=\{x\in X:Tx\in M\}
$. Since $M$ is closed and $T$ is continuous, $E$ is a closed subspace of $X$, h
ence a Banach space. The restriction
$$T\big|_E : E \longrightarrow M$$
is bounded, linear, and surjective (surjectivity uses $M\subseteq T(X)$). By the
 **Open Mapping Theorem**, $T\big|_E$ is open, so there exists a constant $c>0$
such that for every $y\in M$ there is an $x\in E$ with
$$Tx = y \quad\text{and}\quad \|x\|\le c\,\|y\|. \tag{1}$$

**Step 2. A separated sequence in $M$.** Since $M$ is an infinite-dimensional no
rmed space, its closed unit ball is not compact. By **Riesz's lemma**, we may co
nstruct a sequence $(y_n)_{n\ge 1}$ in $M$ with
$$\|y_n\|=1 \quad\text{and}\quad \|y_n-y_m\|\ge \tfrac12 \quad\text{for all } n\
neq m. \tag{2}$$
Indeed: pick $y_1$ with $\|y_1\|=1$; having chosen $y_1,\dots,y_n$, the span $Y_
n=\operatorname{span}\{y_1,\dots,y_n\}$ is a proper closed subspace of $M$ (prop
er because $M$ is infinite dimensional), so Riesz's lemma gives $y_{n+1}\in M$ w
ith $\|y_{n+1}\|=1$ and $\operatorname{dist}(y_{n+1},Y_n)\ge \tfrac12$, which yi
elds (2). In particular $(y_n)$ has **no norm-convergent subsequence**, since it
 is not Cauchy.

**Step 3. Contradiction via compactness of $T$.** For each $n$, use (1) to choos
e $x_n\in E$ with
$$Tx_n = y_n, \qquad \|x_n\|\le c\,\|y_n\|=c.$$
Thus $(x_n)$ is a bounded sequence in $X$. Since $T$ is **compact**, the sequenc
e $(Tx_n)=(y_n)$ must have a norm-convergent subsequence in $Y$.

But by (2) the sequence $(y_n)$ is $\tfrac12$-separated, so no subsequence of $(
y_n)$ is Cauchy, and hence no subsequence converges. This is a contradiction.

**Conclusion.** The assumption that $T(X)$ contains a closed infinite-dimensiona
l subspace is impossible. Therefore the image of a compact operator contains no
closed subspace of infinite dimension. $\blacksquare$
