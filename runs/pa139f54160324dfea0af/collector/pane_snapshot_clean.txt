## Solution

Let $d = \sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}$ be the distance from the origin to the center of th
e sphere. The average value of $S(r)=1/r$ over the sphere is

$$\langle S\rangle = \frac{1}{4\pi R^2}\iint_{\text{sphere}} \frac{1}{r}\,dA.$$

**Reduction by symmetry.** The integral depends only on $d$ and $R$, not on the
direction of $(a,b,c)$. Rotate coordinates so the center lies on the $z$-axis at
 $(0,0,d)$. Parametrize the sphere by
$$(x,y,z) = (R\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\; R\sin\theta\sin\varphi,\; d+R\cos\theta),
$$
with $dA = R^2\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\varphi$. Then
$$r^2 = R^2\sin^2\theta + (d+R\cos\theta)^2 = R^2+d^2+2dR\cos\theta.$$

**Compute the integral.**
$$\langle S\rangle = \frac{1}{4\pi R^2}\int_0^{2\pi}\!d\varphi\int_0^{\pi}\frac{
R^2\sin\theta}{\sqrt{R^2+d^2+2dR\cos\theta}}\,d\theta = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\pi}\
frac{\sin\theta\,d\theta}{\sqrt{R^2+d^2+2dR\cos\theta}}.$$

Substitute $u=\cos\theta$, $du=-\sin\theta\,d\theta$:
$$\langle S\rangle = \frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{du}{\sqrt{R^2+d^2+2dRu}} = \f
rac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{2dR}\Big[\sqrt{R^2+d^2+2dRu}\Big]_{-1}^{1}$$
$$= \frac{1}{2dR}\Big(\sqrt{(R+d)^2}-\sqrt{(R-d)^2}\Big) = \frac{1}{2dR}\big(|R+
d|-|R-d|\big).$$

Since $R>0$ and $d\ge 0$, $|R+d|=R+d$.

- **If $d\le R$** (origin inside or on the sphere): $|R-d|=R-d$, so
$$\langle S\rangle = \frac{1}{2dR}\big((R+d)-(R-d)\big) = \frac{1}{2dR}\cdot 2d
= \frac{1}{R}.$$

- **If $d>R$** (origin outside the sphere): $|R-d|=d-R$, so
$$\langle S\rangle = \frac{1}{2dR}\big((R+d)-(d-R)\big) = \frac{1}{2dR}\cdot 2R
= \frac{1}{d}.$$

- **If $d=0$** (origin at the center): every point has $r=R$, so $\langle S\rang
le = 1/R$, agreeing with the $d\le R$ branch by continuity.

**Result.** With $d=\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}$,

$$\boxed{\langle S\rangle = \frac{1}{\max(R,\,d)} = \begin{cases} \dfrac{1}{R},
& d\le R,\\[6pt] \dfrac{1}{d}, & d>R. \end{cases}}$$

In words: the mean signal strength equals the reciprocal of the larger of the or
bital radius $R$ and the distance $d$ from the planet's core to the orbit's cent
er. When the core lies inside (or on) the orbit, the average is $1/R$; when the
core lies outside the orbit, the average is $1/d$.
