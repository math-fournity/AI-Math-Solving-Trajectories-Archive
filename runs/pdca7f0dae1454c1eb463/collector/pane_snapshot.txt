**Answer: No.** A compact connected subset of a Hilbert space need not be path-c
onnected. Below is a counterexample in the Hilbert space $H=\mathbb{R}^2$ (with
its usual inner product).

---

### Counterexample (the closed topologist's sine curve)

Define
$$
X \;=\; \bigl\{(x,\,\sin(1/x)) : 0 < x \le 1\bigr\}\;\cup\;\bigl(\{0\}\times[-1,
1]\bigr)\;\subset\;\mathbb{R}^2.
$$

We show: (i) $X$ is compact, (ii) $X$ is connected, (iii) $X$ is **not** path-co
nnected.

---

**(i) $X$ is compact.**

$X\subset [0,1]\times[-1,1]$, so $X$ is bounded. Let $G=\{(x,\sin(1/x)):0<x\le 1
\}$. The map $x\mapsto (x,\sin(1/x))$ is continuous on $(0,1]$, so $G$ is the im
age of the connected set $(0,1]$; in particular $G$ is connected. The closure $\
overline{G}$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ contains every point $(0,y)$ with $y\in[-1,1]$, b
ecause $\sin(1/x)$ attains every value in $[-1,1]$ infinitely often as $x\to 0^+
$ (take $x_n\to 0$ with $\sin(1/x_n)=y$). Conversely every limit point of $G$ ei
ther lies in $G$ or on $\{0\}\times[-1,1]$. Hence $\overline{G}=X$, so $X$ is cl
osed. Being closed and bounded in $\mathbb{R}^2$, $X$ is compact by Heine–Borel.

---

**(ii) $X$ is connected.**

$G$ is connected (continuous image of the connected interval $(0,1]$). The closu
re of a connected set is connected, so $X=\overline{G}$ is connected.

---

**(iii) $X$ is not path-connected.**

Suppose, for contradiction, that there is a continuous path $\gamma=(\gamma_1,\g
amma_2):[0,1]\to X$ with
$$
\gamma(0)=(0,0),\qquad \gamma(1)=(1,\sin 1).
$$

Set
$$
t_0 \;=\; \sup\{\,t\in[0,1] : \gamma_1(t)=0\,\}.
$$

The set $\{t:\gamma_1(t)=0\}$ is closed (preimage of $\{0\}$ under the continuou
s $\gamma_1$), so $t_0$ belongs to it: $\gamma_1(t_0)=0$. By definition of the s
upremum, for every $t\in(t_0,1]$ we have $\gamma_1(t)\neq 0$. Since $\gamma_1(1)
=1>0$ and $\gamma_1$ is continuous and nonzero on the connected interval $(t_0,1
]$, we get $\gamma_1(t)>0$ for all $t\in(t_0,1]$. Therefore, for $t\in(t_0,1]$,
$$
\gamma(t)\in G,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \gamma_2(t)=\sin\!\bigl(1/\gamma_1(t)\big
r).
$$

By continuity, $\gamma_1(t)\to 0^+$ as $t\to t_0^+$, and $\gamma_2(t)\to \gamma_
2(t_0)=:y_0\in[-1,1]$.

Now construct two sequences converging to $t_0$ from the right. For each large $
n$, the values
$$
a_n=\frac{1}{2\pi n+\pi/2},\qquad b_n=\frac{1}{2\pi n+3\pi/2}
$$
both lie in $(0,1)$ and tend to $0$. Since $\gamma_1$ is continuous on $(t_0,1]$
 with $\gamma_1(t)\to 0$ as $t\to t_0^+$ and $\gamma_1(1)=1$, the intermediate v
alue theorem gives $t_n,s_n\in(t_0,1)$ with
$$
\gamma_1(t_n)=a_n,\qquad \gamma_1(s_n)=b_n,
$$
and necessarily $t_n\to t_0^+$, $s_n\to t_0^+$. Then
$$
\gamma_2(t_n)=\sin(1/a_n)=\sin(2\pi n+\pi/2)=1,
$$
$$
\gamma_2(s_n)=\sin(1/b_n)=\sin(2\pi n+3\pi/2)=-1.
$$

By continuity of $\gamma_2$,
$$
1=\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma_2(t_n)=y_0=\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma_2(s_n)=-1,
$$
a contradiction. Hence no such path $\gamma$ exists, so $X$ is not path-connecte
d.

---

### Conclusion

$H=\mathbb{R}^2$ is a Hilbert space, and $X\subset H$ is both compact and connec
ted, yet $X$ is not path-connected. Therefore a connected compact subset of a Hi
lbert space is **not** necessarily path-connected.
