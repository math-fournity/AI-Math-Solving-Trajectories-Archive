Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum number of moves to cross out
 all natural numbers, where each move (choosing center $x$) crosses out all $y$
with $|x-y|$ composite.

## Solution

**The answer is $\boxed{2}$.**

### One move is not enough

A single move centered at $x$ crosses out $y$ only when $|x-y|$ is composite. Th
e numbers that *survive* are those with $|x-y| \in \{0, 1\} \cup \{\text{primes}
\}$, i.e., $y \in \{x,\ x\pm1,\ x\pm2,\ x\pm3,\ x\pm5,\ x\pm7,\ x\pm11,\ \ldots\
}$. This is an infinite set, so one move always leaves infinitely many numbers u
ncrossed.

### Two moves suffice

**Choice of centers:** $x_1 = 1$, $x_2 = 94$, so $d = |x_1 - x_2| = 93$.

**Key property of 93:** It is odd, and the three consecutive odd numbers $d-2,\
d,\ d+2$ are all composite:
$$91 = 7 \times 13, \quad 93 = 3 \times 31, \quad 95 = 5 \times 19.$$

**Claim:** For every integer $n$, at least one of $|n-1|$ and $|n-94|$ is compos
ite.

*Proof of claim.* Set $u = n - 1$, so $|n-1| = |u|$ and $|n-94| = |u - 93|$. Let
 $S = \{0, 1\} \cup \{\text{primes}\}$. We show there is no integer $u$ with $|u
| \in S$ and $|u - 93| \in S$.

The set $\{u : |u| \in S\}$ consists of $0, \pm1, \pm2$, and $\pm p$ for each pr
ime $p$. We check all cases:

| $u$ | $\|u\|$ | $\|u - 93\|$ | Composite? |
|---|---|---|---|
| $0$ | $0 \in S$ | $93 = 3{\times}31$ | ✓ |
| $\pm1$ | $1 \in S$ | $92$ or $94$ (even $\geq 4$) | ✓ |
| $\pm2$ | $2 \in S$ | $91 = 7{\times}13$ or $95 = 5{\times}19$ | ✓ |
| $p$ odd prime, $p < 93$ | $p \in S$ | $93 - p$, even. Since $p \neq 93$ (compo
site) and $p \neq 91$ (composite), we get $93-p \neq 0, 2$, so $93-p \geq 4$, ev
en → composite | ✓ |
| $p$ odd prime, $p > 93$ | $p \in S$ | $p - 93$, even. Since $p \neq 95$ (compo
site), $p-93 \neq 2$, so $p-93 \geq 4$, even → composite | ✓ |
| $-p$, $p$ odd prime | $p \in S$ | $p + 93$, even and $\geq 96$ → composite | ✓
 |

Every element of $\{u : |u| \in S\}$ is covered. In each case $|u - 93| \notin S
$, i.e., $|u-93|$ is composite. Therefore no integer $u$ has both $|u|$ and $|u-
93|$ in $S$. $\square$

Since every natural number $n$ has $|n-1|$ or $|n-94|$ composite, the two moves
with centers $1$ and $94$ cross out all natural numbers. (Note: $x$ may be chose
n from already crossed-out numbers, so there is no restriction on our centers.)

### Conclusion

One move leaves infinitely many survivors, and two moves suffice. The minimum nu
mber of moves is $\boxed{2}$.
