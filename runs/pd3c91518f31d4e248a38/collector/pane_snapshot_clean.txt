## Proof

**Claim.** If $f \in \mathbb{C}[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$ satisfies $f(P)=0$ for every $P
\in\mathbb{R}^n$, then $f$ is the zero polynomial.

**Proof by induction on $n$.**

**Base case ($n=1$).** Let $f\in\mathbb{C}[X_1]$ with $f(x)=0$ for all $x\in\mat
hbb{R}$. A nonzero univariate polynomial of degree $d$ over a field has at most
$d$ roots. Since $\mathbb{R}$ is infinite, $f$ has infinitely many roots, so $f$
 must be the zero polynomial.

**Inductive step.** Assume the claim holds for $n-1$ variables. Write $f$ as a p
olynomial in $X_n$ with coefficients in $\mathbb{C}[X_1,\ldots,X_{n-1}]$:

$$f = \sum_{k=0}^{d} g_k(X_1,\ldots,X_{n-1})\, X_n^{k},$$

where $g_k \in \mathbb{C}[X_1,\ldots,X_{n-1}]$.

Fix any point $(a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}$. Consider the univariate
 polynomial

$$h(t) := f(a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1}, t) = \sum_{k=0}^{d} g_k(a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1})\, t^
{k} \in \mathbb{C}[t].$$

By hypothesis, $f$ vanishes on all of $\mathbb{R}^n$, so $h(t)=0$ for every $t\i
n\mathbb{R}$. By the base case ($n=1$), $h$ is the zero polynomial, hence every
coefficient vanishes:

$$g_k(a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1}) = 0 \quad \text{for all } k = 0,\ldots,d.$$

Since $(a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}$ was arbitrary, each $g_k$ vanish
es on all of $\mathbb{R}^{n-1}$. By the inductive hypothesis, each $g_k$ is the
zero polynomial. Therefore

$$f = \sum_{k=0}^{d} 0 \cdot X_n^{k} = 0.$$

This completes the induction. $\blacksquare$

**Conclusion:** Yes, $f$ must necessarily be the zero polynomial.
