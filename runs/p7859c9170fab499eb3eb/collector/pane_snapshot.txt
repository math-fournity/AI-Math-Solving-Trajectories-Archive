## Solution

**Setup.** We have 2016 sensors numbered 1–2016. A random subset of 1007 is chos
en; let $p$ be its minimum. The remaining 1009 sensors are drawn one by one unti
l some $q < p$ is found. If none exists (only when $p=1$), $S=1$; otherwise $S =
 p \cdot q$.

**Key observation.** Since $p$ is the minimum of the sample, all sensors with va
lues $\{1, 2, \ldots, p-1\}$ must be in the remaining pile. When $p > 1$, drawin
g sensors one by one from the remaining pile, the first "small" sensor encounter
ed is uniformly distributed over $\{1, \ldots, p-1\}$ (by symmetry of random per
mutations). Thus:

$$E[q \mid p] = \frac{1 + (p-1)}{2} = \frac{p}{2}, \qquad E[S \mid p] = p \cdot
\frac{p}{2} = \frac{p^2}{2} \quad (p \geq 2).$$

For $p = 1$: $E[S \mid p=1] = 1$.

**Distribution of $p$.** The minimum of a random $k$-subset of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$
(here $n=2016$, $k=1007$) follows:

$$P(p = j) = \frac{\binom{n-j}{k-1}}{\binom{n}{k}}, \quad j = 1, \ldots, n-k+1 =
 1010.$$

This is the negative hypergeometric distribution (position of first "success" wh
en drawing without replacement from $n$ items, $k$ marked). Standard formulas gi
ve:

$$E[p] = \frac{n+1}{k+1} = \frac{2017}{1008},$$

$$\text{Var}(p) = \frac{k(n-k)(n+1)}{(k+1)^2(k+2)} = \frac{1007 \cdot 1009 \cdot
 2017}{1008^2 \cdot 1009} = \frac{1007 \cdot 2017}{1008^2}.$$

**Computing $E[p^2]$:**

$$E[p^2] = \text{Var}(p) + (E[p])^2 = \frac{1007 \cdot 2017}{1008^2} + \frac{201
7^2}{1008^2} = \frac{2017(1007 + 2017)}{1008^2} = \frac{2017 \cdot 3024}{1008^2}
.$$

Since $3024 = 3 \times 1008$:

$$E[p^2] = \frac{2017 \cdot 3}{1008} = \frac{6051}{1008} = \frac{2017}{336}.$$

**Computing $P(p=1)$:**

$$P(p=1) = \frac{\binom{2015}{1006}}{\binom{2016}{1007}} = \frac{1007}{2016}.$$

**Assembling $E[S]$:**

$$E[S] = P(p=1)\cdot 1 + \sum_{k \geq 2} P(p=k)\cdot\frac{k^2}{2} = P(p=1) + \fr
ac{1}{2}\left(E[p^2] - P(p=1)\right) = \frac{1}{2}E[p^2] + \frac{1}{2}P(p=1).$$

$$E[S] = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2017}{336} + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1007}{2016} =
\frac{2017}{672} + \frac{1007}{4032}.$$

Since $4032 = 6 \times 672$:

$$E[S] = \frac{2017 \times 6}{4032} + \frac{1007}{4032} = \frac{12102 + 1007}{40
32} = \frac{13109}{4032}.$$

**Simplification check.** $4032 = 2^6 \times 3^2 \times 7$. Testing $13109$: odd
 (not div. by 2), digit sum $14$ (not div. by 3), $13109 = 7 \times 1872 + 5$ (n
ot div. by 7). So $\gcd(13109, 4032) = 1$, and the fraction is already in simple
st form.

**Verification with small case** ($n=4, k=2$): Direct computation gives $E[S] =
23/12$; the formula yields $\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{10}{3} + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac
{1}{2} = \frac{5}{3}+\frac{1}{4} = \frac{23}{12}$. ✓

**Final answer.** With $m = 13109$ and $n = 4032$:

$$m + n = 13109 + 4032 = \boxed{17141}$$
