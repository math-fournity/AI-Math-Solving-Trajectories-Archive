## Proof

**Setup.** In simply isotropic space I³ with coordinates (x, y, z), the metric i
s degenerate: ds² = dx² + dy². The z-direction is isotropic. The projection onto
 the xy-plane is the *Grundriss*. The fundamental property of I³ is:

> **Isotropic distance = Euclidean Grundriss distance.** For any two points P₁ =
 (x₁, y₁, z₁), P₂ = (x₂, y₂, z₂):
> d_iso(P₁, P₂) = √((x₁−x₂)² + (y₁−y₂)²) = d_Eucl(P̄₁, P̄₂).

This extends to arc length: for any curve γ(t), the isotropic arc length equals
the Euclidean arc length of its Grundriss γ̄(t):
L_iso(γ) = ∫ √(ẋ² + ẏ²) dt = L_Eucl(γ̄).

**Ruled surface.** A closed skew ruled surface in I³ is parameterized as
R(u, v) = p(u) + v·d(u),  u ∈ [0, 2π],
where p(u) is a closed directrix, d(u) is the ruling direction, and the surface
is *skew* (non-developable: rulings are neither parallel nor concurrent). Normal
ize so |d̄(u)| = 1 in the Grundriss.

**Striction curve.** The striction curve s(u) = p(u) + v_s(u)·d(u) is the locus
of striction points—the point on each ruling closest (in the isotropic metric) t
o the neighboring ruling. Since the isotropic metric involves only Grundriss coo
rdinates, the striction is determined entirely by the Grundriss geometry:
v_s(u) = −⟨p̄′, d̄′⟩ / |d̄′|²,
where bars denote xy-projections and ⟨·,·⟩ is the Euclidean inner product in the
 plane. The striction curve's Grundriss is s̄(u) = p̄(u) + v_s(u)·d̄(u).

**Offnungsstrecke.** The Offnungsstrecke (opening distance) O of the ruled surfa
ce is the isotropic arc length of the striction curve—the total "opening" of the
 surface measured along the curve of striction points:
O = L_iso(s) = ∫₀²π √(s_x′² + s_y′²) du.

**Euclidean length of the Grundriss of the striction curve.** This is:
L = L_Eucl(s̄) = ∫₀²π √(s_x′² + s_y′²) du.

**The equality.** The integrands are identical: both depend only on (s_x′, s_y′)
, the Grundriss components of the striction curve's derivative. The z-component
s_z′ does not enter the isotropic arc length (because ds² = dx² + dy²), nor does
 it enter the Grundriss Euclidean length (because the Grundriss discards z). The
refore:

O = L_iso(s) = L_Eucl(s̄) = L.

**Verification by example.** Take p(u) = (a cos u, a sin u, 0), d(u) = (cos(u+φ)
, sin(u+φ), h) with φ ≠ 0 (skew condition). Then:
- v_s = −a cos φ (constant),
- s̄(u) = a sin φ · (sin(u+φ), −cos(u+φ)) — a circle of radius a|sin φ|,
- L = 2πa|sin φ|,
- O = ∫₀²π a|sin φ| du = 2πa|sin φ| = L. ✓

**Conclusion.** The Offnungsstrecke of a closed skew ruled surface in I³ **equal
s** the Euclidean length of the Grundriss of its striction curve. This is a dire
ct consequence of the degenerate nature of the simply isotropic metric: since ds
² = dx² + dy², the isotropic length of any curve—including the striction curve—i
s determined entirely by its xy-projection and coincides with the Euclidean leng
th of that projection. The "opening" of the ruled surface, as captured by the st
riction curve, is thus completely measured by the Grundriss geometry.
