Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a regular Lindelöf space $X
$ with a $G_\delta$-diagonal, which is the continuous bijective image of the Mic
hael line, must be submetrizable.

**Answer: Yes, $X$ is submetrizable.**

In fact, the conclusion follows from the conditions that $X$ is regular, Lindelö
f, and has a $G_\delta$-diagonal alone — the continuous bijection from the Micha
el line is not needed for the result.

## Proof

**Step 1: $X$ is normal.**

A regular Lindelöf space is normal. (Given disjoint closed sets $A, B \subseteq
X$, for each $a \in A$ use regularity to find open $U_a$ with $a \in U_a \subset
eq \overline{U_a} \subseteq X \setminus B$. The cover $\{U_a\}_{a \in A}$ of $A$
 has a countable subcover $\{U_{a_k}\}$ by Lindelöf. Similarly obtain $\{V_{b_k}
\}$ covering $B$ with $\overline{V_{b_k}} \subseteq X \setminus A$. Then the sta
ndard construction $U = \bigcup_k (U_{a_k} \setminus \bigcup_{j \leq k} \overlin
e{V_{b_j}})$ and $V = \bigcup_k (V_{b_k} \setminus \bigcup_{j \leq k} \overline{
U_{a_j}})$ gives disjoint open sets separating $A$ and $B$.)

**Step 2: Every open set in $X$ is a cozero set.**

Let $U \subseteq X$ be open. Since $X$ is regular, for each $x \in U$ there exis
ts an open set $V_x$ with $x \in V_x \subseteq \overline{V_x} \subseteq U$. The
collection $\{V_x : x \in U\}$ is an open cover of $U$. Since $X$ is Lindelöf, i
t has a countable subcover $\{V_{x_k}\}_{k \in \omega}$. Then:
$$U = \bigcup_{k} V_{x_k} \subseteq \bigcup_{k} \overline{V_{x_k}} \subseteq U,$
$
so $U = \bigcup_k \overline{V_{x_k}}$, which is $F_\sigma$.

Since $X$ is normal and $U = \bigcup_k F_k$ (with $F_k = \overline{V_{x_k}}$ clo
sed), apply Urysohn's lemma to each pair of disjoint closed sets $F_k$ and $X \s
etminus U$: there exists continuous $g_k: X \to [0,1]$ with $g_k|_{F_k} = 1$ and
 $g_k|_{X \setminus U} = 0$. Define $f = \sum_{k} 2^{-k} g_k$. This sum converge
s uniformly, so $f$ is continuous. For $x \in U$, we have $x \in F_k$ for some $
k$, so $g_k(x) = 1$ and $f(x) \geq 2^{-k} > 0$. For $x \in X \setminus U$, every
 $g_k(x) = 0$, so $f(x) = 0$. Thus $f^{-1}((0,1]) = U$, proving $U$ is a cozero
set.

**Step 3: Construct a countable separating family of continuous functions.**

Since $X$ has a $G_\delta$-diagonal, there exists a sequence of open covers $\{\
mathcal{U}_n\}_{n \in \omega}$ such that for every $x \in X$:
$$\{x\} = \bigcap_{n} \operatorname{st}(x, \mathcal{U}_n).$$

Since $X$ is Lindelöf, each open cover $\mathcal{U}_n$ admits a countable subcov
er. Without loss of generality, write $\mathcal{U}_n = \{U_{n,k}\}_{k \in \omega
}$.

By Step 2, each $U_{n,k}$ is a cozero set, so there exists a continuous function
 $f_{n,k}: X \to [0,1]$ with $f_{n,k}^{-1}((0,1]) = U_{n,k}$.

**Step 4: Define a continuous injection into a metrizable space.**

Define $F: X \to [0,1]^{\omega \times \omega}$ by $F(x) = (f_{n,k}(x))_{n,k \in
\omega}$. Since $\omega \times \omega$ is countable, the target is homeomorphic
to the Hilbert cube $[0,1]^\omega$, which is compact and metrizable. The map $F$
 is continuous because each coordinate $f_{n,k}$ is continuous.

**$F$ is injective.** Suppose $F(x) = F(y)$, i.e., $f_{n,k}(x) = f_{n,k}(y)$ for
 all $n, k$. Fix $n$. Since $\mathcal{U}_n$ covers $X$, there exists $k$ with $x
 \in U_{n,k}$, so $f_{n,k}(x) > 0$. Then $f_{n,k}(y) = f_{n,k}(x) > 0$, which im
plies $y \in U_{n,k}$. Since both $x$ and $y$ belong to $U_{n,k} \in \mathcal{U}
_n$, we have $y \in \operatorname{st}(x, \mathcal{U}_n)$. This holds for every $
n$, so:
$$y \in \bigcap_{n} \operatorname{st}(x, \mathcal{U}_n) = \{x\},$$
hence $y = x$.

**Step 5: Conclude submetrizability.**

The map $F: X \to [0,1]^\omega$ is a continuous injection into a metrizable spac
e. The initial topology $\tau_F = \{F^{-1}(O) : O \text{ open in } [0,1]^\omega\
}$ on $X$ is:
- **Coarser than $\tau_X$**, since $F$ is continuous (each $F^{-1}(O)$ is open i
n $X$).
- **Metrizable**, since $F$ is injective: $\tau_F$ is homeomorphic to the subspa
ce topology on $F(X) \subseteq [0,1]^\omega$, and a subspace of a metrizable spa
ce is metrizable.

Therefore $X$ admits a coarser metrizable topology, i.e., $X$ is **submetrizable
**. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on the Michael line condition.** The Michael line $M$ is itself submetr
izable (the Euclidean topology is a coarser metrizable topology), and the contin
uous bijection $f: M \to X$ shows that $\tau_X$ is coarser than the transported
Michael line topology. However, this alone does not yield submetrizability of $X
$, since the Euclidean (metrizable) topology on $M$ need not be coarser than $\t
au_X$. The essential ingredients for the proof are the $G_\delta$-diagonal (prov
iding a point-separating cover sequence) and the Lindelöf property (making the c
overs countable and ensuring every open set is a cozero set via normality).
