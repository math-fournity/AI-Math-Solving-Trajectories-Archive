Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the "approximately uniform"
 ball condition forces absolute continuity with respect to Lebesgue measure.

**Answer: Yes, the condition implies absolute continuity.**

**Proof.**

**Step 1. The hypothesis implies a uniform linear upper bound on ball measures.*
*

We claim there exists $C_0 > 0$ such that
$$\mu(B_r(x)) \leq C_0\, r \qquad \text{for all } x \in \mathbb{T},\; r > 0. \ta
g{$\star$}$$

Fix $0 < r \leq 1/4$. On the torus (circumference $1$), place centers at $0, 2r,
 4r, \ldots$ to obtain $M \geq \lfloor 1/(2r)\rfloor \geq \frac{1}{4r}$ pairwise
 disjoint balls $B_r(z_1), \dots, B_r(z_M)$. Since these are disjoint and $\mu(\
mathbb{T})=1$:
$$\sum_{j=1}^{M} \mu(B_r(z_j)) \leq 1, \qquad\text{so}\qquad \min_{1\le j\le M}\
mu(B_r(z_j)) \leq \frac{1}{M} \leq 4r.$$

Applying the hypothesis at radius $r$: for every $x \in \mathbb{T}$,
$$\mu(B_r(x)) \leq C\cdot \min_{1\le j\le M}\mu(B_r(z_j)) \leq 4Cr.$$

For $r > 1/4$, we have $\mu(B_r(x)) \leq 1 < 4r$. Setting $C_0 = \max(4C,\, 4)$
yields $(\star)$ for all $r>0$.

**Step 2. The bound $(\star)$ forces absolute continuity.**

Let $A \subset \mathbb{T}$ be Borel with $\lambda(A)=0$, and let $\varepsilon >
0$. Since $\lambda(A)=0$, there exists an open set $U \supset A$ with $\lambda(U
) < \varepsilon$. On the torus, $U$ decomposes as a countable union of disjoint
open arcs: $U = \bigcup_k I_k$.

Each arc $I_k$ of length $|I_k| < 1$ is itself a ball: $I_k = B_{|I_k|/2}(m_k)$,
 where $m_k$ is the midpoint. (If $|I_k|=1$ then $I_k=\mathbb{T}$ and $\mu(I_k)=
1\le C_0/2$ since $C_0\ge 4$.) Applying $(\star)$:
$$\mu(I_k) = \mu\!\Big(B_{|I_k|/2}(m_k)\Big) \leq C_0\cdot\frac{|I_k|}{2} = \fra
c{C_0}{2}\,|I_k|.$$

Summing over all arcs:
$$\mu(A) \leq \mu(U) = \sum_k \mu(I_k) \leq \frac{C_0}{2}\sum_k |I_k| = \frac{C_
0}{2}\,\lambda(U) < \frac{C_0}{2}\,\varepsilon.$$

Since $\varepsilon > 0$ was arbitrary, $\mu(A) = 0$.

Therefore every $\lambda$-null Borel set is $\mu$-null, which is precisely $\mu
\ll \lambda$; that is, **$\mu$ is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue
 measure**.

**Key idea:** The hypothesis that all balls of the same radius have uniformly co
mparable measure, combined with a packing argument on the compact torus, yields
the uniform estimate $\mu(B_r(x)) \lesssim r$. Since open arcs on $\mathbb{T}$ *
are* balls, this estimate passes directly to all open sets, giving $\mu(U) \less
sim \lambda(U)$ for every open $U$—which is exactly absolute continuity. No cove
ring lemma or differentiation theorem is needed. $\blacksquare$
