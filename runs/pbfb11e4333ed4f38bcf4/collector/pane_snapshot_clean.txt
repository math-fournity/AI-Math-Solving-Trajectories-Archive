## Proof

**Setup.** For each $0 < r < R$, let $\phi_r \in C_c^\infty(B(0,R))$ be the test
 function guaranteed by the smooth Urysohn lemma, with $0 \le \phi_r \le 1$ and
$\phi_r \equiv 1$ on $B(0,r)$. We analyze

$$I(r) := \int_{B(0,R)\setminus B(0,r)} \Delta\phi_r(x)\,dx.$$

**Step 1: $\Delta\phi_r = 0$ on $B(0,r)$.**

Since $\phi_r \equiv 1$ on the open ball $B(0,r)$, all second partial derivative
s of $\phi_r$ vanish identically on $B(0,r)$. Hence $\Delta\phi_r = 0$ on $B(0,r
)$, and therefore

$$I(r) = \int_{B(0,R)\setminus B(0,r)} \Delta\phi_r\,dx = \int_{B(0,R)} \Delta\p
hi_r\,dx.$$

**Step 2: Apply the divergence theorem.**

The domain $B(0,R)$ is bounded with smooth boundary $\partial B(0,R) = S^{n-1}(R
)$, and $\phi_r$ is smooth. By the divergence theorem (Green's identity),

$$\int_{B(0,R)} \Delta\phi_r\,dx = \int_{B(0,R)} \nabla \cdot (\nabla\phi_r)\,dx
 = \int_{\partial B(0,R)} \nabla\phi_r \cdot \mathbf{n}\,dS = \int_{\partial B(0
,R)} \frac{\partial \phi_r}{\partial n}\,dS,$$

where $\mathbf{n}$ is the outward unit normal.

**Step 3: The boundary integral vanishes.**

Since $\phi_r$ has **compact support** in $B(0,R)$, there exists $\varepsilon >
0$ (depending on $r$) such that

$$\operatorname{supp}(\phi_r) \subset B(0, R - \varepsilon).$$

Consequently $\phi_r \equiv 0$ on the annular region $\{x : R - \varepsilon \le
|x| < R\}$, which includes a neighborhood of $\partial B(0,R)$. In particular, $
\nabla \phi_r = 0$ on $\partial B(0,R)$, so

$$\frac{\partial \phi_r}{\partial n}\bigg|_{\partial B(0,R)} = 0.$$

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 1–3:

$$I(r) = \int_{B(0,R)} \Delta\phi_r\,dx = \int_{\partial B(0,R)} \frac{\partial
\phi_r}{\partial n}\,dS = 0 \quad \text{for every } 0 < r < R.$$

Since $I(r) = 0$ for **every** $r \in (0, R)$, the limit

$$\lim_{r \to R} I(r) = \lim_{r \to R} 0 = 0$$

exists trivially and equals $0$.

**Remark.** The key points are: (i) $\phi_r \equiv 1$ on $B(0,r)$ forces $\Delta
\phi_r = 0$ there, so the integral over the annulus equals the integral over the
 full ball; and (ii) the compact support condition forces $\phi_r$ (and all its
derivatives) to vanish on $\partial B(0,R)$, making the full-ball integral of $\
Delta\phi_r$ exactly zero by the divergence theorem. The transition region where
 $\Delta\phi_r \neq 0$ lies entirely in the interior annulus $\{r < |x| < R-\var
epsilon\}$, and its positive and negative contributions cancel exactly—a consequ
ence of the fact that $\phi_r$ goes from the constant value $1$ to the constant
value $0$, so the total flux of $\nabla\phi_r$ through any closed surface enclos
ing the transition region is zero.

### PROOF COMPLETE
