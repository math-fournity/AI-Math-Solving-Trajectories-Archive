# Laplacian in General Coordinates

## Setup

Let $x^i$ ($i=1,2,\dots,n$) be general coordinates with metric tensor $g_{ij}$,
its inverse $g^{ij}$, and determinant $g = \det(g_{ij})$. The Laplacian of a sca
lar field $\phi$ is the divergence of its gradient:

$$\nabla^2\phi = \nabla_i \nabla^i \phi = \frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\partial_i\!\left(\s
qrt{g}\, g^{ij}\,\partial_j \phi\right).$$

We derive this in two steps.

## Step 1 — Gradient

For a scalar field, the covariant derivative coincides with the partial derivati
ve, and raising the index with the metric gives the contravariant (vector) gradi
ent:

$$\nabla^i\phi = g^{ij}\,\partial_j\phi.$$

## Step 2 — Divergence of a vector field

For a contravariant vector $V^i$, the covariant divergence is

$$\nabla_i V^i = \partial_i V^i + \Gamma^i_{ik}V^k.$$

We use the standard identity for the contracted Christoffel symbol:

$$\Gamma^i_{ik} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\,\partial_k\sqrt{g}.$$

**Proof of the identity.** Since $g = \det(g_{ij})$, Jacobi's formula gives $\pa
rtial_k g = g\,g^{ij}\partial_k g_{ij}$, hence

$$\partial_k \ln\sqrt{g} = \tfrac{1}{2}\partial_k\ln g = \tfrac{1}{2}g^{ij}\part
ial_k g_{ij}.$$

On the other hand, by definition

$$\Gamma^i_{ik} = \tfrac{1}{2}g^{i\ell}\!\left(\partial_i g_{k\ell} + \partial_k
 g_{i\ell} - \partial_\ell g_{ik}\right).$$

The first and third terms cancel after relabeling dummy indices ($g^{i\ell}\part
ial_i g_{k\ell} = g^{i\ell}\partial_\ell g_{ik}$ by symmetry of $g$ and $g^{i\el
l}$), leaving

$$\Gamma^i_{ik} = \tfrac{1}{2}g^{i\ell}\partial_k g_{i\ell} = \tfrac{1}{2}g^{ij}
\partial_k g_{ij} = \partial_k\ln\sqrt{g} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\partial_k\sqrt{g}
.$$

Substituting back,

$$\nabla_i V^i = \partial_i V^i + \frac{V^k}{\sqrt{g}}\partial_k\sqrt{g} = \frac
{1}{\sqrt{g}}\partial_i\!\left(\sqrt{g}\,V^i\right),$$

where the last equality follows from the product rule $\partial_i(\sqrt{g}\,V^i)
 = \sqrt{g}\,\partial_i V^i + V^i\partial_i\sqrt{g}$.

## Step 3 — Combine

Setting $V^i = \nabla^i\phi = g^{ij}\partial_j\phi$ in the divergence formula:

$$\boxed{\;\nabla^2\phi \;=\; \nabla_i\nabla^i\phi \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\,\pa
rtial_i\!\left(\sqrt{g}\,g^{ij}\,\partial_j\phi\right)\;}$$

(summation over $i,j=1,\dots,n$ implied).

## Verification in special cases

- **Cartesian**: $g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}$, $g=1$, $g^{ij}=\delta^{ij}$ → $\nabla^2\p
hi = \partial_i\partial_i\phi = \sum_i \partial_i^2\phi$. ✓
- **Orthogonal** ($g_{ij}=0$ for $i\neq j$): $g = \prod_i h_i^2$ with $g_{ii}=h_
i^2$, $g^{ii}=1/h_i^2$, giving the familiar orthogonal-coordinate form $\nabla^2
\phi = \frac{1}{h_1\cdots h_n}\partial_i\!\left(\frac{h_1\cdots h_n}{h_i^2}\part
ial_i\phi\right)$. ✓

The formula is coordinate-covariant (a scalar) and reduces correctly in all stan
dard limits.
