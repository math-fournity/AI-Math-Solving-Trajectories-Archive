## Proof

We use the classical infinite product identity:

$$\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\cos\!\left(\frac{x}{2^{n}}\right)=\frac{\sin x}{x}.$$

**Derivation of the identity.** From the double-angle formula $\sin x = 2\sin(x/
2)\cos(x/2)$, i.e. $\cos(x/2)=\dfrac{\sin x}{2\sin(x/2)}$, we obtain by telescop
ing:

$$\prod_{n=1}^{N}\cos\!\left(\frac{x}{2^{n}}\right)=\frac{\sin x}{2^{N}\sin(x/2^
{N})}.$$

Since $\lim_{N\to\infty}2^{N}\sin(x/2^{N})=x$ for every $x\neq 0$, taking $N\to\
infty$ gives the identity (valid for all $x\neq 0$).

**Applying it to the problem.** Set $x=\pi/k$ (with $k\neq 0$). Then

$$\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{k\,2^{n}}\right)=\frac{\sin(\pi/k)}
{\pi/k}=\frac{k\,\sin(\pi/k)}{\pi}.$$

Substituting into the given expression:

$$\frac{\pi\displaystyle\prod_{n\geq 1}\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{k\,2^{n}}\right)}{
\sin(\pi/k)}=\frac{\pi\cdot\dfrac{k\,\sin(\pi/k)}{\pi}}{\sin(\pi/k)}=k,$$

provided $\sin(\pi/k)\neq 0$.

**Domain check.** The expression is well-defined precisely when $\sin(\pi/k)\neq
 0$, i.e. when $\pi/k\notin\pi\mathbb{Z}$, i.e. when $k\notin\{-1,0,1\}$.

- For $k=0$: $\pi/k$ is undefined.
- For $k=\pm 1$: $\sin(\pi/k)=\sin(\pm\pi)=0$, and the product also vanishes (it
s first factor $\cos(\pi/2)=0$), giving an indeterminate $0/0$ form.

For every integer $k$ with $|k|\geq 2$, the expression is well-defined and equal
s $k$, which is an integer.

**Conclusion.** Whenever the expression is defined (for all integers $k$ with $|
k|\geq 2$), it equals $k$ exactly, and is therefore always an integer.
