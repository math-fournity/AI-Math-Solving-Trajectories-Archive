## Solution

We compute $\operatorname{Var}(X) = \mathbb{E}[X^2] - (\mathbb{E}[X])^2$ for $X
= \int_0^1 W^2(t)\,dt$.

### Step 1: Mean

$$\mathbb{E}[X] = \int_0^1 \mathbb{E}[W^2(t)]\,dt = \int_0^1 t\,dt = \frac{1}{2}
.$$

### Step 2: Second moment via Isserlis' theorem

$$\mathbb{E}[X^2] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^1 W^2(s)W^2(t)\,ds\,dt\
right] = \int_0^1\!\!\int_0^1 \mathbb{E}[W^2(s)W^2(t)]\,ds\,dt.$$

Since $(W(s), W(t))$ are jointly Gaussian with mean zero and $\mathbb{E}[W(s)W(t
)] = \min(s,t)$, Isserlis' (Wick's) formula gives

$$\mathbb{E}[W^2(s)W^2(t)] = \mathbb{E}[W^2(s)]\,\mathbb{E}[W^2(t)] + 2\bigl(\ma
thbb{E}[W(s)W(t)]\bigr)^2 = st + 2\min(s,t)^2.$$

Hence

$$\mathbb{E}[X^2] = \underbrace{\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^1 st\,ds\,dt}_{=\,1/4} + 2\un
derbrace{\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^1 \min(s,t)^2\,ds\,dt}_{J}.$$

The first term equals $\bigl(\int_0^1 s\,ds\bigr)^2 = 1/4$.

For $J$, split the unit square by the diagonal $s=t$ and use symmetry:

$$J = 2\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^t s^2\,ds\,dt = 2\int_0^1 \frac{t^3}{3}\,dt = \frac{2}
{12} = \frac{1}{6}.$$

Therefore

$$\mathbb{E}[X^2] = \frac{1}{4} + 2\cdot\frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{3}
= \frac{7}{12}.$$

### Step 3: Variance

$$\operatorname{Var}(X) = \mathbb{E}[X^2] - (\mathbb{E}[X])^2 = \frac{7}{12} - \
frac{1}{4} = \frac{7}{12} - \frac{3}{12} = \frac{4}{12} = \frac{1}{3}.$$

### Result

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(X) = \dfrac{1}{3}}.$$
