## Proof

**Claim.** The Gauss–Bonnet Theorem applies to a smooth two‑dimensional surface
embedded in any Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^n$ with $n\ge 3$ (in particular $\ma
thbb{R}^4$), provided the surface is given the Riemannian metric induced by the
embedding.

---

### 1. Precise statement of the theorem

Let $(M,g)$ be a compact, oriented, two‑dimensional Riemannian manifold, possibl
y with smooth boundary $\partial M$. The **Gauss–Bonnet theorem** asserts

$$
\int_M K\,dA \;+\; \int_{\partial M} k_g\,ds \;=\; 2\pi\,\chi(M),
$$

where $K$ is the Gaussian curvature of $(M,g)$, $dA$ the Riemannian area form, $
k_g$ the geodesic curvature of $\partial M$, and $\chi(M)$ the Euler characteris
tic. When $\partial M=\varnothing$ this reduces to $\int_M K\,dA = 2\pi\chi(M)$.

Every quantity appearing here — $K$, $dA$, $k_g$, $\chi(M)$ — is defined in term
s of the Riemannian metric $g$ and the topology of $M$ alone. **No ambient space
 is referenced.**

---

### 2. The theorem is intrinsic

The Gaussian curvature $K$ of a surface is, by Gauss's *Theorema Egregium*, an i
nvariant of the metric $g$. Concretely, for a 2‑manifold the Riemann curvature t
ensor has a single independent component $R_{1212}$, and

$$
K \;=\; \frac{R_{1212}}{\det g}.
$$

The components $R_{ijkl}$ are obtained from $g$ and its derivatives via the stan
dard formula

$$
R^l{}_{ijk} = \partial_i \Gamma^l_{jk} - \partial_j \Gamma^l_{ik} + \Gamma^l_{im
}\Gamma^m_{jk} - \Gamma^l_{jm}\Gamma^m_{ik},
\qquad
\Gamma^k_{ij} = \tfrac12 g^{kl}(\partial_i g_{jl}+\partial_j g_{il}-\partial_l g
_{ij}).
$$

Thus $K$ is computable from $g$ without any reference to an embedding. The area
form $dA = \sqrt{\det g}\,dx^1\wedge dx^2$ and the geodesic curvature $k_g$ (def
ined from the covariant derivative of $\nabla$ associated to $g$) are likewise i
ntrinsic. The Euler characteristic $\chi(M)$ is a topological invariant. Hence *
*the entire left–hand side and right–hand side of Gauss–Bonnet are intrinsic obj
ects.**

---

### 3. What an embedding into $\mathbb{R}^n$ contributes

A smooth embedding $f:M\to\mathbb{R}^n$ pulls back the Euclidean metric to give
the induced metric $g = f^*(\delta)$. This turns $M$ into a Riemannian 2‑manifol
d $(M,g)$. Once $g$ is fixed, the hypotheses of Gauss–Bonnet are satisfied, and
the theorem applies — **the dimension $n$ of the ambient space plays no role in
the statement or the proof.**

The only place the ambient dimension enters is in *how one computes* $K$ from ex
trinsic data, via the **Gauss equation**. Decompose the second fundamental form
along an orthonormal frame $\{\nu_\alpha\}_{\alpha=1}^{n-2}$ of the normal bundl
e:

$$
\mathrm{I\!I}_{ij} \;=\; \sum_{\alpha=1}^{n-2} h^\alpha_{ij}\,\nu_\alpha.
$$

The Gauss equation reads

$$
R_{1212} \;=\; \langle \mathrm{I\!I}_{11},\mathrm{I\!I}_{22}\rangle - \langle \m
athrm{I\!I}_{12},\mathrm{I\!I}_{12}\rangle
\;=\; \sum_{\alpha=1}^{n-2}\bigl(h^\alpha_{11}h^\alpha_{22} - (h^\alpha_{12})^2\
bigr).
$$

Therefore

$$
K \;=\; \frac{1}{\det g}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n-2} K_\alpha,
\qquad
K_\alpha := h^\alpha_{11}h^\alpha_{22} - (h^\alpha_{12})^2.
$$

- For $n=3$ the normal bundle is 1‑dimensional, the sum has one term, and $K = K
_1 = \kappa_1\kappa_2$, the familiar product of principal curvatures.
- For $n=4$ the normal bundle is 2‑dimensional, and $K = (K_1+K_2)/\det g$ is a
**sum** of two normal‑direction contributions rather than a single product. Ther
e is no longer a pair of principal curvatures whose product equals $K$.

This is purely a change in the *extrinsic representation* of $K$. The intrinsic
quantity $K = R_{1212}/\det g$ — and hence the Gauss–Bonnet integral — is unchan
ged in nature.

---

### 4. Conclusion

The Gauss–Bonnet theorem is a theorem about the intrinsic Riemannian geometry an
d topology of a 2‑manifold. An embedding $M\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^n$ for any
$n\ge 3$ endows $M$ with an induced metric, and with respect to that metric ever
y ingredient of the theorem ($K$, $dA$, $k_g$, $\chi(M)$) is well defined and in
trinsic. The ambient dimension affects only the extrinsic formula by which $K$ i
s computed from the second fundamental form (a single product $\kappa_1\kappa_2$
 in $\mathbb{R}^3$, a sum $\sum_\alpha K_\alpha$ over the $(n-2)$‑dimensional no
rmal bundle in $\mathbb{R}^n$), not the validity of the theorem.

Therefore **the Gauss–Bonnet theorem does apply to surfaces embedded in $\mathbb
{R}^4$ (and more generally in any $\mathbb{R}^n$)**, in the identical form

$$
\int_M K\,dA \;+\; \int_{\partial M} k_g\,ds \;=\; 2\pi\,\chi(M),
$$

with $K$ the intrinsic Gaussian curvature of the induced metric.
