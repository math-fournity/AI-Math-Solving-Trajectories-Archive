# Exterior Derivative of the Exterior Product

## Setup

Let $\rho = R\, dx^i \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^r$ be a $p$-form (with $p$ factors
of $dx$) and $\sigma = S\, dx^j \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^s$ be a $q$-form (with $
q$ factors of $dx$), where $R$ and $S$ are smooth functions.

Their exterior product is:
$$\rho \wedge \sigma = (RS)\, dx^i \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^r \wedge dx^j \wedge
\cdots \wedge dx^s.$$

## Computation

Apply the exterior derivative. Since $dx^i \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^s$ is a const
ant-coefficient form, only the coefficient $RS$ is differentiated:

$$d(\rho \wedge \sigma) = d(RS) \wedge dx^i \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^r \wedge dx^
j \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^s.$$

By the ordinary Leibniz rule for functions, $d(RS) = S\, dR + R\, dS$, so:

$$d(\rho \wedge \sigma) = S\, dR \wedge dx^i \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^r \wedge dx
^j \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^s + R\, dS \wedge dx^i \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^r \wed
ge dx^j \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^s.$$

**First term.** Group the factors belonging to $\rho$ and $\sigma$:

$$S\, dR \wedge dx^i \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^r \wedge dx^j \wedge \cdots \wedge
dx^s = \bigl(dR \wedge dx^i \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^r\bigr) \wedge \bigl(S\, dx^
j \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^s\bigr) = d\rho \wedge \sigma.$$

**Second term.** Here $dS$ (a $1$-form) appears to the **left** of the $p$ one-f
orms $dx^i \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^r$. To regroup it with the factors of $\sigma
$, we must commute $dS$ past each of these $p$ one-forms. Each swap of two $1$-f
orms introduces a factor of $-1$ (antisymmetry of $\wedge$), so passing through
$p$ factors gives $(-1)^p$:

$$R\, dS \wedge dx^i \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^r \wedge dx^j \wedge \cdots \wedge
dx^s = (-1)^p\, R\, dx^i \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^r \wedge dS \wedge dx^j \wedge
\cdots \wedge dx^s.$$

Now regroup:

$$= (-1)^p \bigl(R\, dx^i \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^r\bigr) \wedge \bigl(dS \wedge
 dx^j \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^s\bigr) = (-1)^p\, \rho \wedge d\sigma.$$

## Result

Combining both terms:

$$\boxed{d(\rho \wedge \sigma) = d\rho \wedge \sigma + (-1)^{p}\, \rho \wedge d\
sigma,}$$

where $p = \deg \rho$ is the degree of $\rho$. This is the **graded Leibniz rule
** for the exterior derivative: the sign $(-1)^p$ arises because $d$ (a degree-$
1$ operation) must anticommute past the $p$-form $\rho$ to reach $\sigma$.
