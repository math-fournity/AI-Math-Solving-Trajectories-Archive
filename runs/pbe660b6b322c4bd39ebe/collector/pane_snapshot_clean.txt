## Proof

**Definition.** A domain $\Omega$ satisfies the *interior ball condition* at $x_
0 \in \partial\Omega$ if there exists a ball $B \subset \Omega$ with $x_0 \in \p
artial B$. We say $\Omega$ satisfies the interior ball condition if this holds a
t every $x_0 \in \partial\Omega$.

We prove: **A bounded convex domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ satisfies the
interior ball condition at all boundary points if and only if $\partial\Omega$ i
s of class $C^{1,1}$.**

---

### Part I (Sufficiency): $C^{1,1}$ boundary $\Longrightarrow$ interior ball con
dition

Assume $\partial\Omega$ is $C^{1,1}$ and $\Omega$ is convex. Since $C^{1,1} \sup
set C^1$, the outer unit normal $\nu:\partial\Omega \to S^{n-1}$ is well-defined
 and Lipschitz continuous. The Lipschitz constant $L$ of $\nu$ bounds the princi
pal curvatures: $\kappa_i \leq L$ a.e. on $\partial\Omega$. Since $\Omega$ is co
nvex, all principal curvatures are non-negative, so $0 \leq \kappa_i \leq L$.

Set $r = 1/L$ (if $L=0$, any $r>0$ works). At each $x_0 \in \partial\Omega$, def
ine the center $c = x_0 - r\,\nu(x_0)$ (displaced inward by distance $r$). The b
all $B(c,r)$ touches $\partial\Omega$ at $x_0$.

**Claim:** $B(c,r) \subset \Omega$.

Near $x_0$, represent $\partial\Omega$ as the graph $x_n = \varphi(x')$ with $x_
0$ at the origin, $\nu(x_0) = e_n$, $\varphi(0)=0$, $\nabla\varphi(0)=0$, and $\
Omega = \{x_n < \varphi(x')\}$. The $C^{1,1}$ regularity gives $|D^2\varphi| \le
q L$ a.e., and convexity gives $D^2\varphi \geq 0$. The sphere of radius $r$ tan
gent at the origin from inside is the graph $x_n = r - \sqrt{r^2 - |x'|^2}$, who
se second derivative in any direction is exactly $1/r = L$ at the origin and dec
reases away from it.

Since $\varphi$ is convex with $D^2\varphi \leq L = 1/r$ while the sphere has $D
^2(\text{sphere}) \geq 1/r$ near the contact point (the sphere's curvature decre
ases as $|x'|$ grows), the comparison principle for convex functions gives:

$$\varphi(x') \geq r - \sqrt{r^2 - |x'|^2} \quad \text{for } |x'| < r,$$

which means the graph of $\varphi$ lies *above* the sphere, i.e., the sphere lie
s inside $\Omega$ locally.

Globally, since $\Omega$ is convex, the entire domain lies on one side of the su
pporting hyperplane at $x_0$, and $B(c,r) \subset \{x : (x-x_0)\cdot\nu(x_0) \le
q 0\}$ (the interior half-space). Any point of $\partial\Omega \setminus \{x_0\}
$ is at distance $\geq r$ from $c$ (by the curvature bound and convexity), so $B
(c,r) \cap \partial\Omega = \{x_0\}$, hence $B(c,r) \subset \Omega$. $\checkmark
$

### Part II (Necessity): Interior ball condition $\Longrightarrow$ $C^{1,1}$ bou
ndary

Assume $\Omega$ is bounded, convex, and satisfies the interior ball condition at
 every $x_0 \in \partial\Omega$.

**Step 1: Uniformity of the radius.**

At each $x_0$, let $R(x_0) = \sup\{r : \exists\, B(c,r) \subset \Omega,\, x_0 \i
n \partial B(c,r)\}$. The function $R$ is lower semicontinuous: if $B(c,r) \subs
et \Omega$ touches $x_0$, then for $x_1$ near $x_0$, a slightly smaller ball cen
tered near $c$ touches $x_1$ from inside (by openness of $\Omega$ and continuity
). Since $\partial\Omega$ is compact and $R(x_0) > 0$ everywhere, $R$ attains a
positive minimum $r_0 > 0$. So the interior ball condition holds with **uniform*
* radius $r_0$.

**Step 2: $\partial\Omega$ is $C^1$ (unique normal at every point).**

At $x_0 \in \partial\Omega$, let $B(c_0, r_0) \subset \Omega$ touch $x_0$. The b
all has a unique supporting hyperplane at $x_0$, with outer normal $\nu_0 = (x_0
 - c_0)/r_0$. Since $\Omega$ is convex, it has a supporting hyperplane at $x_0$.
 Because $B(c_0,r_0) \subset \Omega$ and $x_0 \in \partial B(c_0,r_0) \cap \part
ial\Omega$, any supporting hyperplane to $\Omega$ at $x_0$ must also support $B(
c_0,r_0)$ at $x_0$, hence must coincide with the ball's tangent plane. Therefore
 the supporting hyperplane is **unique**, the outer normal is $\nu_0$, and $\par
tial\Omega$ is differentiable at $x_0$.

**Step 3: Local graph representation.**

Fix $x_0 \in \partial\Omega$ with outer normal $\nu_0$. Set coordinates so $x_0
= 0$, $\nu_0 = e_n$, and $\Omega \subset \{x_n \leq 0\}$. Locally, $\partial\Ome
ga$ is the graph $x_n = -\varphi(x')$ where $x' = (x_1,\dots,x_{n-1})$, $\varphi
:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}$ is **convex** (since $\Omega$ is convex), $\varp
hi(0) = 0$, $\nabla\varphi(0) = 0$, and $\varphi \geq 0$.

The interior ball $B(c_0, r_0)$ with $c_0 = -r_0 e_n$ and radius $r_0$ satisfies
 $B(c_0,r_0) \subset \Omega$. The upper boundary of the ball is $x_n = -r_0 + \s
qrt{r_0^2 - |x'|^2}$, and since the ball lies inside $\Omega = \{x_n < -\varphi(
x')\}$:

$$-r_0 + \sqrt{r_0^2 - |x'|^2} \leq -\varphi(x') \quad \text{for } |x'| < r_0,$$

which gives:

$$\varphi(x') \leq r_0 - \sqrt{r_0^2 - |x'|^2} = \frac{|x'|^2}{r_0 + \sqrt{r_0^2
 - |x'|^2}} \leq \frac{|x'|^2}{r_0}. \tag{$\star$}$$

**Step 4: $D^2\varphi$ is bounded $\Longrightarrow$ $\varphi \in C^{1,1}$.**

Since $\varphi$ is convex, its Hessian $D^2\varphi$ (in the distributional sense
) is a positive semidefinite matrix-valued measure: $D^2\varphi \geq 0$.

We show $D^2\varphi \leq \frac{2}{r_0} I$. For any unit vector $e \in \mathbb{R}
^{n-1}$, define $g(t) = \varphi(te)$. Then $g$ is convex, $g(0)=0$, $g'(0)=0$, $
g(t) \geq 0$, and by $(\star)$:

$$g(t) \leq \frac{t^2}{r_0} \quad \text{for } |t| < r_0.$$

The second derivative $\mu = g''$ is a non-negative measure on $(-r_0, r_0)$, an
d:

$$g(t) = \int_0^t (t-s)\,d\mu(s) \quad \text{for } t > 0.$$

The quadratic bound gives $g(t) \leq t^2/r_0 = \int_0^t (t-s)\cdot\frac{2}{r_0}\
,ds$. Since $(t-s) > 0$ on $(0,t)$ and this inequality holds for all $t \in (0,
r_0)$, a standard measure-theoretic comparison yields:

$$\mu \leq \frac{2}{r_0}\,ds \quad \text{on } [0, r_0),$$

i.e., $g''(t) \leq 2/r_0$ a.e. The same argument for $t < 0$ gives $g''(t) \leq
2/r_0$ a.e. on $(-r_0, 0)$.

Since this holds for every direction $e$:

$$0 \leq D^2\varphi \leq \frac{2}{r_0} I \quad \text{(as measures)}.$$

This means $\nabla\varphi$ is Lipschitz continuous with constant $2/r_0$, so $\v
arphi \in C^{1,1}$.

Since $x_0$ was arbitrary and the argument is local with uniform constants, $\pa
rtial\Omega$ is globally $C^{1,1}$. $\checkmark$

### Conclusion

Parts I and II establish the equivalence:

$$\boxed{\partial\Omega \text{ is } C^{1,1} \iff \Omega \text{ satisfies the int
erior ball condition at all boundary points.}}$$

**$C^{1,1}$ is the minimum regularity.** It cannot be weakened:
- $C^{1,\alpha}$ for $\alpha < 1$ is **not sufficient**: a convex domain with bo
undary $x_n = -|x'|^{1+\alpha}$ (for $\alpha \in (0,1)$) has a cusp-like behavio
r where the curvature blows up at the origin, and the interior ball condition fa
ils there (the boundary curves faster than any sphere near the cusp).
- $C^{1,1}$ is **necessary** because the interior ball condition forces the Hess
ian of the local graph function to be bounded (Step 4), which is exactly the $C^
{1,1}$ condition.

The convexity hypothesis is essential for the necessity direction: it provides t
he one-sided curvature bound ($D^2\varphi \geq 0$, i.e., semiconvexity) for free
, while the interior ball condition provides the other side ($D^2\varphi \leq 2/
r_0\, I$, i.e., semiconcavity). Together they yield $C^{1,1}$.
