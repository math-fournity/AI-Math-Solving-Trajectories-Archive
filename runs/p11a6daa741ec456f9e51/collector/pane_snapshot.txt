## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, it follows that $\langle X, N \rangle_t = 0$.

**Setup and notation.** We interpret $\langle X, N \rangle_t$ as the quadratic c
ovariation of the two processes, defined pathwise as the limit in probability (o
r, since the processes are continuous, the uniform limit along refining partitio
ns):

$$\langle X, N \rangle_t = \lim_{|\Pi|\to 0} \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\bigl(X_{t_{i+1}}-X
_{t_i}\bigr)\bigl(N_{t_{i+1}}-N_{t_i}\bigr),$$

where $\Pi = \{0=t_0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_n = t\}$ is a partition of $[0,t]$ and $
|\Pi| = \max_i (t_{i+1}-t_i)$ is its mesh.

**Key hypotheses.** By assumption, $X$ has continuous sample paths and is of bou
nded variation on $[0,t]$ (pathwise), and $N$ has continuous sample paths (and i
s also of bounded variation, though we will only need continuity of $N$ and boun
ded variation of $X$).

**Estimate.** Fix a sample path. Since $N$ is continuous on the compact interval
 $[0,t]$, it is uniformly continuous. Define its modulus of continuity

$$\omega_N(\delta) = \sup\bigl\{|N_s - N_u| : s,u \in [0,t],\ |s-u|\le \delta\bi
gr\},$$

which satisfies $\omega_N(\delta) \to 0$ as $\delta \to 0$. For any partition $\
Pi$ with mesh $|\Pi|$,

$$\begin{aligned}
\left|\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\bigl(X_{t_{i+1}}-X_{t_i}\bigr)\bigl(N_{t_{i+1}}-N_{t_i}\b
igr)\right|
&\le \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\bigl|X_{t_{i+1}}-X_{t_i}\bigr|\cdot\bigl|N_{t_{i+1}}-N_{t_
i}\bigr| \\
&\le \omega_N(|\Pi|)\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\bigl|X_{t_{i+1}}-X_{t_i}\bigr| \\
&\le \omega_N(|\Pi|)\cdot V_X(t),
\end{aligned}$$

where $V_X(t) < \infty$ is the total variation of $X$ on $[0,t]$, finite by hypo
thesis.

**Taking the limit.** As $|\Pi| \to 0$, we have $\omega_N(|\Pi|) \to 0$ while $V
_X(t)$ remains a fixed finite constant. Therefore

$$\lim_{|\Pi|\to 0}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\bigl(X_{t_{i+1}}-X_{t_i}\bigr)\bigl(N_{t_{i+
1}}-N_{t_i}\bigr) = 0.$$

This holds for every sample path (with $V_X(t)$ and $\omega_N$ depending on the
path), so the convergence is in fact pathwise, hence also in probability.

**Conclusion.** The quadratic covariation vanishes:

$$\boxed{\langle X, N \rangle_t = 0.}$$

**Remark on the roles of the hypotheses.** The argument used only two ingredient
s: (i) $X$ is of bounded variation on $[0,t]$ (giving the finite factor $V_X(t)$
), and (ii) $N$ is continuous (giving the factor $\omega_N(|\Pi|)\to 0$). The bo
unded variation of $N$ was not needed. Symmetrically, since $N$ is also of bound
ed variation and $X$ is continuous, one could swap their roles and obtain the sa
me conclusion. Thus the conclusion holds under the given (in fact slightly stron
ger-than-necessary) hypotheses. $\blacksquare$
