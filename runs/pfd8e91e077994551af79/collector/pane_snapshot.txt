# Galois Group of $f(x) = x^5 - x + 1$ over $\mathbb{F}_7$

## Step 1: No roots in $\mathbb{F}_7$

Evaluate $f$ at each element of $\mathbb{F}_7 = \{0,1,2,3,4,5,6\}$:

| $a$ | $a^5$ | $f(a)=a^5-a+1$ | mod 7 |
|-----|-------|----------------|-------|
| 0 | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 32 | 31 | 3 |
| 3 | 243 | 241 | 3 |
| 4 | 1024 | 1021 | 6 |
| 5 | 3125 | 3121 | 6 |
| 6 | 7776 | 7771 | 1 |

No root exists, so $f$ has **no linear factor** over $\mathbb{F}_7$.

## Step 2: No irreducible quadratic factor

The polynomial $x^{49}-x$ is the product of all monic irreducibles over $\mathbb
{F}_7$ of degree dividing 2. Thus $f$ has a quadratic factor iff $\gcd(f,\, x^{4
9}-x)\neq 1$.

**Computing $x^{49} \bmod f$.** Since $f=0$ gives $x^5 \equiv x-1$, we reduce po
wers of $x$ modulo $f$:

$$x^7 = x^2\cdot x^5 = x^2(x-1)=x^3-x^2.$$

By the Frobenius (freshman's dream mod 7):

$$x^{49}=(x^7)^7 \equiv (x^3-x^2)^7 = x^{21}-x^{14} \pmod{f}.$$

Iteratively reducing using $x^5\equiv x-1$:

$$x^{14}\equiv x^4+x^2+4x+2, \qquad x^{21}\equiv 3x^4+6x^3+3x^2+2x+6 \pmod{f}.$$

Therefore

$$x^{49}\equiv x^{21}-x^{14}\equiv 2x^4+6x^3+2x^2+5x+4 \pmod{f},$$

and

$$x^{49}-x \equiv 2x^4+6x^3+2x^2+4x+4 \pmod{f}.$$

**Euclidean algorithm** for $g = \gcd(f,\; 2x^4+6x^3+2x^2+4x+4)$ (working mod 7,
 making remainders monic):

| Step | Division | Monic remainder |
|------|----------|-----------------|
| 1 | $f \div g'$ | $r_1'=x^4+5x^3+3x^2+x+2$ |
| 2 | $g' \div r_1'$ | $r_2'=x^3+x^2+3x$ |
| 3 | $r_1' \div r_2'$ | $r_3'=x^3+2x+4$ |
| 4 | $r_2' \div r_3'$ | $r_4'=x^2+x$ |
| 5 | $r_3' \div r_4'$ | $r_5'=x^2+5x+3$ |
| 6 | $r_4' \div r_5'$ | $r_6'=x+6$ |
| 7 | $r_5' \div r_6'$ | $\boxed{2}$ (nonzero constant) |

The last nonzero remainder is the constant $2\neq 0$, so

$$\gcd(f,\, x^{49}-x)=1.$$

Hence $f$ has **no irreducible factor of degree 1 or 2** over $\mathbb{F}_7$.

## Step 3: Irreducibility

Since $\deg f = 5$ is prime, the only partitions of 5 into irreducible-factor de
grees are:

$$5,\quad 4{+}1,\quad 3{+}2,\quad 3{+}1{+}1,\quad 2{+}2{+}1,\quad 2{+}1{+}1{+}1,
\quad 1{+}1{+}1{+}1{+}1.$$

Every partition other than $\{5\}$ contains a part of size 1 or 2. Since $f$ has
 no factor of degree 1 or 2, **all such factorizations are impossible**. Therefo
re $f$ is **irreducible** over $\mathbb{F}_7$.

## Step 4: The Galois group

Since $f$ is irreducible of degree 5 over $\mathbb{F}_7$, adjoining any root $\a
lpha$ gives $\mathbb{F}_7(\alpha)\cong\mathbb{F}_{7^5}$. Over a finite field, ev
ery finite extension is normal (and separable), so $\mathbb{F}_{7^5}$ is the spl
itting field of $f$.

The Galois group of $\mathbb{F}_{7^5}/\mathbb{F}_7$ is cyclic of order 5, genera
ted by the Frobenius automorphism

$$\varphi:\alpha\longmapsto \alpha^7.$$

## Conclusion

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(f/\mathbb{F}_7)\;\cong\;\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}}$$

the cyclic group of order $5$, generated by the Frobenius automorphism $\varphi:
\alpha\mapsto\alpha^7$.
