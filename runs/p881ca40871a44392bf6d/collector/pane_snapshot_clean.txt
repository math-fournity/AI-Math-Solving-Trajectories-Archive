## Proof

We compute the monoid $\operatorname{Nat}(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{Ab}}, \mathrm{I
d}_{\mathfrak{Ab}})$ under composition.

**Construction of a family indexed by $\mathbb{Z}$.** For each integer $n \in \m
athbb{Z}$, define a natural transformation $\tau^{(n)} : \mathrm{Id} \Rightarrow
 \mathrm{Id}$ by setting, for every abelian group $A$,
$$\tau^{(n)}_A : A \to A, \qquad \tau^{(n)}_A(a) = n\cdot a,$$
where $n\cdot a$ denotes $a$ added to itself $n$ times (with the usual sign conv
ention for $n < 0$). Each $\tau^{(n)}_A$ is a group homomorphism since $n\cdot(a
+b) = n\cdot a + n\cdot b$. Naturality holds: for any homomorphism $f : A \to B$
,
$$f\bigl(\tau^{(n)}_A(a)\bigr) = f(n\cdot a) = n\cdot f(a) = \tau^{(n)}_B(f(a)),
$$
so $f \circ \tau^{(n)}_A = \tau^{(n)}_B \circ f$. Hence $\tau^{(n)}$ is a genuin
e natural transformation.

**Every natural transformation is of this form.** Let $\tau : \mathrm{Id} \Right
arrow \mathrm{Id}$ be any natural transformation. Consider the free abelian grou
p $\mathbb{Z}$ (on one generator, written multiplicatively-free with generator $
1$). The component $\tau_{\mathbb{Z}} : \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}$ is a group ho
momorphism, hence determined by the image of $1$; write
$$\tau_{\mathbb{Z}}(1) = m \qquad \text{for some } m \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

Now let $A$ be an arbitrary abelian group and $a \in A$ an arbitrary element. Th
ere is a unique homomorphism $f_a : \mathbb{Z} \to A$ with $f_a(1) = a$. Natural
ity of $\tau$ applied to $f_a$ gives the commutative square
$$f_a \circ \tau_{\mathbb{Z}} = \tau_A \circ f_a.$$
Evaluating both sides at $1 \in \mathbb{Z}$:
$$f_a\bigl(\tau_{\mathbb{Z}}(1)\bigr) = \tau_A\bigl(f_a(1)\bigr) \implies f_a(m)
 = \tau_A(a) \implies m\cdot a = \tau_A(a).$$
Thus $\tau_A(a) = m\cdot a$ for every $a \in A$ and every abelian group $A$, i.e
. $\tau = \tau^{(m)}$.

**The assignment is a bijection.** The map $n \mapsto \tau^{(n)}$ is injective,
since $\tau^{(n)}_{\mathbb{Z}}(1) = n$, and surjective by the previous paragraph
. Hence
$$\operatorname{Nat}(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{Ab}}, \mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{Ab}}) \
;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}$$
as a set, with $n$ corresponding to "multiplication by $n$."

**Monoid structure.** Under composition of natural transformations,
$$\bigl(\tau^{(m)} \circ \tau^{(n)}\bigr)_A(a) = \tau^{(m)}_A\bigl(n\cdot a\bigr
) = m\cdot(n\cdot a) = (mn)\cdot a = \tau^{(mn)}_A(a),$$
so $\tau^{(m)} \circ \tau^{(n)} = \tau^{(mn)}$. The identity natural transformat
ion is $\tau^{(1)}$ (multiplication by $1$).

Therefore the monoid of natural transformations $\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{Ab}} \Ri
ghtarrow \mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{Ab}}$ is isomorphic to the **multiplicative mono
id of the integers** $(\mathbb{Z}, \cdot, 1)$:
$$\operatorname{Nat}(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{Ab}}, \mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{Ab}}) \
;\cong\; (\mathbb{Z},\, \cdot).$$

Concretely, every such natural transformation is "multiplication by a fixed inte
ger $n$," composition corresponds to multiplication of the corresponding integer
s, and the identity transformation corresponds to $n = 1$.
