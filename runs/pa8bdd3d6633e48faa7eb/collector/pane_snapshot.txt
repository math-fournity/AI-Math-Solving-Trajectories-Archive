\mathfrak{m}$.

Localization $S^{-1}(-)$ is exact (it is tensor product with $S^{-1}R$), and ten
sor product distributes over arbitrary direct sums. Hence
$$M_{\mathfrak{m}} \cong \bigoplus_{\lambda \in k} \bigl(R/(x-\lambda)\bigr)_{\m
athfrak{m}}.$$

**Step 2: The summand at $\lambda = \lambda_0$.**

The module $R/(x - \lambda_0) \cong k$ is simple, annihilated by $\mathfrak{m} =
 (x-\lambda_0)$. For any $s \in S$, the class $\bar s$ acts on $R/(x-\lambda_0)$
 as multiplication by $s(\lambda_0)$. Since $s \notin (x-\lambda_0)$, we have $s
(\lambda_0) \neq 0$, which is a unit in $k$. Thus every element of $S$ already a
cts invertibly on $R/(x-\lambda_0)$, so localization changes nothing:
$$\bigl(R/(x-\lambda_0)\bigr)_{\mathfrak{m}} \cong R/(x-\lambda_0) \cong k.$$

**Step 3: The summands at $\lambda \neq \lambda_0$.**

The annihilator of $R/(x-\lambda)$ is the ideal $(x - \lambda)$. Since $\lambda
\neq \lambda_0$, the element $x - \lambda$ does **not** vanish at $\lambda_0$, s
o $x - \lambda \notin (x - \lambda_0)$, i.e. $x - \lambda \in S$. But $x - \lamb
da$ annihilates the entire module $R/(x-\lambda)$. In the localized module, an e
lement $n/s$ is zero iff there exists $t \in S$ with $t n = 0$. Taking $t = x -
\lambda \in S$, we have $t \cdot n = 0$ for **every** $n \in R/(x-\lambda)$. The
refore every element of $\bigl(R/(x-\lambda)\bigr)_{\mathfrak{m}}$ is zero:
$$\bigl(R/(x-\lambda)\bigr)_{\mathfrak{m}} = 0 \qquad (\lambda \neq \lambda_0).$
$

**Step 4: Combine.**

All summands with $\lambda \neq \lambda_0$ vanish under localization; only the s
ummand at $\lambda_0$ survives. Therefore
$$M_{(x-\lambda_0)} \;\cong\; \bigoplus_{\lambda \in k}\bigl(R/(x-\lambda)\bigr)
_{(x-\lambda_0)} \;\cong\; \bigl(R/(x-\lambda_0)\bigr)_{(x-\lambda_0)} \;\cong\;

**Conclusion.** The localization of $M$ at $(x - \lambda_0)$ is isomorphic to $k
