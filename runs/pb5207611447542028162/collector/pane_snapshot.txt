## Proof

= x^2 + y^2 - 1$ and $f_2 = 4x^2y^2 - 1$, so $\text{LT}(f_1) = x^2$ and $\text{L
T}(f_2) = x^2y^2$.

**Step 1: Compute the S-polynomial $S(f_1, f_2)$.**

$$S(f_1,f_2) = y^2\,f_1 - f_2 = y^2(x^2+y^2-1) - (4x^2y^2-1) = -3x^2y^2 + y^4 -
y^2 + 1.$$

Reduce the leading term $-3x^2y^2$ using $f_2$ (quotient $-3/4$):

$$(-3x^2y^2 + y^4 - y^2 + 1) - \left(-\tfrac{3}{4}\right)(4x^2y^2 - 1) = y^4 - y
^2 + \tfrac{1}{4}.$$

The leading term $y^4$ is not divisible by $x^2$ or $x^2y^2$, so reduction stops
. We obtain:

$$f_3 = y^4 - y^2 + \tfrac{1}{4}, \qquad \text{LT}(f_3) = y^4.$$

**Step 2: Verify Buchberger's criterion for $\{f_1, f_2, f_3\}$.**

*$S(f_1, f_3)$:* $\text{lcm}(x^2, y^4) = x^2y^4$.

$$S = y^4 f_1 - x^2 f_3 = (x^2y^4 + y^6 - y^4) - (x^2y^4 - x^2y^2 + \tfrac{x^2}{
4}) = y^6 - y^4 + x^2y^2 - \tfrac{x^2}{4}.$$

Reduce by $f_2$ (quotient $1/4$ on $x^2y^2$): gives $y^6 - y^4 - x^2/4 + 1/4$. R
educe by $f_1$ (quotient $-1/4$ on $x^2$): gives $y^6 - y^4 + y^2/4$. Reduce by
$f_3$ (quotient $y^2$ on $y^4$):

$$y^6 - y^4 + \tfrac{y^2}{4} - y^2(y^4 - y^2 + \tfrac{1}{4}) = 0. \quad\checkmar

*$S(f_2, f_3)$:* $\text{lcm}(x^2y^2, y^4) = x^2y^4$.

$$S = y^2 f_2 - x^2 f_3 = (4x^2y^4 - y^2) - (x^2y^4 - x^2y^2 + \tfrac{x^2}{4}) =
 3x^2y^4 + x^2y^2 - \tfrac{x^2}{4} - y^2.$$

Reduce by $f_2$ (quotient $3y^2$): gives $-9x^2y^4 + x^2y^2 - x^2/4 + 2y^2$. Red
uce by $f_2$ again (quotient $-9y^2/4$): gives $x^2y^2 - x^2/4 - y^2/4$. Reduce
by $f_2$ (quotient $1/4$): gives $-x^2/4 - y^2/4 + 1/4$. Reduce by $f_1$ (quotie

$$(-\tfrac{x^2}{4} - \tfrac{y^2}{4} + \tfrac{1}{4}) - (-\tfrac{1}{4})(x^2+y^2-1)
 = 0. \quad\checkmark$$

All S-polynomials reduce to zero, so $G = \{f_1, f_2, f_3\}$ is a Gröbner basis.

**Step 3: Reduce to a minimal basis.**

Observe that $f_2$ is redundant: reduce $4x^2y^2 - 1$ by $f_1$ (quotient $4y^2$)
:

$$4x^2y^2 - 1 - 4y^2(x^2+y^2-1) = -4y^4 + 4y^2 - 1.$$

Reduce by $f_3$ (quotient $-4$):

$$-4y^4 + 4y^2 - 1 - (-4)(y^4 - y^2 + \tfrac{1}{4}) = 0.$$

So $f_2 \in \langle f_1, f_3\rangle$, hence $J_2 = \langle f_1, f_3\rangle$. The
 set $\{x^2 + y^2 - 1,\; y^4 - y^2 + \tfrac{1}{4}\}$ is the **reduced Gröbner ba
sis** (both generators are monic, and no non-leading monomial of either is divis
ible by the other's leading term).

**Step 4: Extract standard monomials.**

The leading terms are $x^2$ and $y^4$. A monomial $x^a y^b$ is a **standard mono
mial** iff it is divisible by neither $x^2$ nor $y^4$, i.e., $a \leq 1$ and $b \
leq 3$:

$$B = \{1,\; y,\; y^2,\; y^3,\; x,\; xy,\; xy^2,\; xy^3\}.$$

By the standard theorem on Gröbner bases, the residue classes of the standard mo
nomials form a $\mathbb{Q}$-basis of $\mathbb{Q}[x,y]/J_2$.

**Step 5: Consistency check.** The variety $V(J_2)$ satisfies $x^2+y^2=1$ and $x
^2y^2=1/4$. Setting $u=x^2, v=y^2$ gives $u+v=1, uv=1/4$, so $u,v$ are roots of
$t^2 - t + 1/4 = (t-1/2)^2 = 0$, yielding $u=v=1/2$. Over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}

dim_\mathbb{Q} \mathbb{Q}[x,y]/J_2$. This matches $|B| = 8$. $\quad\checkmark$

**Conclusion.** A $\mathbb{Q}$-basis of $\mathbb{Q}[x,y]/J_2$ is

$$\boxed{B = \{1,\; y,\; y^2,\; y^3,\; x,\; xy,\; xy^2,\; xy^3\}},$$

obtained as the standard monomials of the reduced Gröbner basis $\{x^2+y^2-1,\;
y^4-y^2+\tfrac{1}{4}\}$ (lex order $x>y$). The dimension is $\dim_\mathbb{Q}(\ma
