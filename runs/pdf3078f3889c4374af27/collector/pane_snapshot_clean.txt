## Solution

Let
$$I = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \frac{\sin^{2012}{x}}{\left(1+ \alpha^x\right)\left(
\sin^{2012}{x}+\cos^{2012}{x}\right)}\;{dx}, \qquad \alpha \in \mathbb{R}^+.$$

**Step 1: Isolate the $\alpha^x$ dependence.**

Define
$$f(x) = \frac{\sin^{2012}{x}}{\sin^{2012}{x}+\cos^{2012}{x}}.$$
Since $2012$ is even, $\sin^{2012}(-x)=\sin^{2012}{x}$ and $\cos^{2012}(-x)=\cos
^{2012}{x}$, so $f(-x)=f(x)$; $f$ is even. The integral becomes
$$I = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \frac{f(x)}{1+\alpha^x}\,dx.$$

**Step 2: Apply the substitution $x \mapsto -x$.**

$$I = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \frac{f(-x)}{1+\alpha^{-x}}\,dx = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi
/2} \frac{f(x)}{1+\alpha^{-x}}\,dx.$$

Adding the two expressions for $I$:
$$2I = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} f(x)\left(\frac{1}{1+\alpha^x}+\frac{1}{1+\alpha^{-
x}}\right)dx.$$

The bracket simplifies:
$$\frac{1}{1+\alpha^x}+\frac{1}{1+\alpha^{-x}} = \frac{1}{1+\alpha^x}+\frac{\alp
ha^x}{\alpha^x+1} = \frac{1+\alpha^x}{1+\alpha^x}=1.$$

Hence
$$2I = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} f(x)\,dx.$$

Since $f$ is even,
$$I = \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \frac{\sin^{2012}{x}}{\sin^{2012}{x}+\cos^{2012}{x}}\,dx.
$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the remaining integral via $x \mapsto \frac{\pi}{2}-x$.**

Let
$$J = \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \frac{\sin^{2012}{x}}{\sin^{2012}{x}+\cos^{2012}{x}}\,dx.
$$
Under $x \mapsto \frac{\pi}{2}-x$, $\sin x \leftrightarrow \cos x$, so
$$J = \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \frac{\cos^{2012}{x}}{\cos^{2012}{x}+\sin^{2012}{x}}\,dx.
$$
Adding:
$$2J = \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \frac{\sin^{2012}{x}+\cos^{2012}{x}}{\sin^{2012}{x}+\cos
^{2012}{x}}\,dx = \int_{0}^{\pi/2} 1\,dx = \frac{\pi}{2}.$$
Thus $J = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{I = \frac{\pi}{4}}$$

This value is independent of $\alpha$, valid for all $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}^+$.
