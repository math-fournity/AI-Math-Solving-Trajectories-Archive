## Proof

**Claim:** The product of infinitely many rational numbers can be irrational.

**Construction.** Consider the infinite product

$$P \;=\; \prod_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{4n^{2}}{4n^{2}-1}.$$

Each factor $a_n = \dfrac{4n^{2}}{4n^{2}-1} = \dfrac{4n^{2}}{(2n-1)(2n+1)}$ is a
 ratio of positive integers, hence **rational**. We show that the product conver
ges to an irrational number.

---

**Step 1: The product converges and equals $\pi/2$ (Wallis product).**

Define the Wallis integrals $I_k = \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin^{k} x\, dx$ for $k \ge
0$. Integration by parts gives the recurrence

$$I_k = \frac{k-1}{k}\, I_{k-2}, \qquad k \ge 2,$$

with $I_0 = \pi/2$ and $I_1 = 1$. Iterating yields

$$I_{2n} = \frac{1\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdots(2n-1)}{2\cdot 4\cdot 6\cdots(2n)}\cdot\fr
ac{\pi}{2}, \qquad I_{2n+1} = \frac{2\cdot 4\cdot 6\cdots(2n)}{1\cdot 3\cdot 5\c
dots(2n+1)}.$$

Since $0 < \sin x < 1$ on $(0,\pi/2)$, we have the strict squeeze

$$I_{2n+1} \;<\; I_{2n} \;<\; I_{2n-1} \qquad \text{for all } n \ge 1.$$

Dividing through by $I_{2n+1}$ and using $I_{2n-1} = \frac{2n}{2n-1}\,I_{2n+1}$
(from the recurrence), we get

$$1 \;<\; \frac{I_{2n}}{I_{2n+1}} \;<\; \frac{2n}{2n-1} \;\xrightarrow{n\to\inft
y}\; 1.$$

Hence $\dfrac{I_{2n}}{I_{2n+1}} \to 1$. Now computing the ratio explicitly:

$$\frac{I_{2n}}{I_{2n+1}} = \frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{\bigl(1\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdots(
2n-1)\bigr)^{2}}{\bigl(2\cdot 4\cdot 6\cdots(2n)\bigr)^{2}}\cdot(2n+1) = \frac{\
pi}{2}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(2k-1)^{2}(2k+1)}{(2k)^{2}\cdot 1}\cdot\frac{1}{
2n+1}\cdot(2n+1).$$

A cleaner way to see it: the partial product

$$P_N = \prod_{n=1}^{N}\frac{4n^{2}}{4n^{2}-1} = \prod_{n=1}^{N}\frac{(2n)(2n)}{
(2n-1)(2n+1)} = \frac{2}{1}\cdot\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{4}{3}\cdot\frac{4}{5}\cdot
\frac{6}{5}\cdot\frac{6}{7}\cdots\frac{2N}{2N-1}\cdot\frac{2N}{2N+1}$$

satisfies

$$P_N = \frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{I_{2N+1}}{I_{2N}} \;\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}\; \
frac{\pi}{2}\cdot 1 = \frac{\pi}{2}.$$

This is the **Wallis product**:

$$\boxed{\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{4n^{2}}{4n^{2}-1} = \frac{\pi}{2}.}$$

---

**Step 2: $\pi/2$ is irrational.**

The irrationality of $\pi$ was established by Lambert (1761) and can be proved b
y elementary means (Niven, 1947). Since $\pi$ is irrational and $1/2$ is rationa
l, $\pi/2$ is irrational (a rational times an irrational is irrational).

---

**Conclusion.** The infinite product $\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{4n^{2}}{4n^{2}-1
}$ is a product of infinitely many rational numbers, and it converges to $\pi/2$
, which is irrational. Therefore, **the product of infinitely many rational numb
ers can indeed be irrational.** $\blacksquare$
