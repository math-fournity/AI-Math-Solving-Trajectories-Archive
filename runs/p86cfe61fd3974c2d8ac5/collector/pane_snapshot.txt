## Proof

Let $D$ be a disk with two holes — topologically, a sphere with three open disks
 removed (a **pair of pants**). Denote its three boundary circles $C_1, C_2, C_3
$.

### Step 1: The three boundary circles are fully symmetric

The pair of pants is homeomorphic to $S^2 \setminus (D_1 \cup D_2 \cup D_3)$, wh
ere $D_1, D_2, D_3$ are three disjoint open disks. Place these disks so their ce
nters sit at the vertices of an equilateral triangle inscribed in a great circle
 of $S^2$.

The orientation-preserving homeomorphism group of $S^2$ contains $\mathrm{SO}(3)
$, and within $\mathrm{SO}(3)$:
- A $120°$ rotation about the axis through the center of the triangle realizes t
he 3-cycle $(C_1\, C_2\, C_3)$.
- A $180°$ rotation about an axis through one vertex and the midpoint of the opp
osite side realizes a transposition, e.g. $(C_1\, C_2)$.

A 3-cycle and a transposition generate the full symmetric group $S_3$. Each rota
tion restricts to a self-homeomorphism of the pair of pants. Therefore:

> **Any permutation of $\{C_1, C_2, C_3\}$ is realized by a homeomorphism of the
 pair of pants.**

The three boundary circles are indistinguishable up to self-homeomorphism.

### Step 2: An identification is a partition

To "identify" boundary circles means to glue them together. An identification sc
heme is a **partition** of $\{C_1, C_2, C_3\}$ into groups, where all circles wi
thin a group are identified to one another. For the scheme to actually identify
something, **at least one group must have size $\geq 2$** (otherwise no two circ
les are glued, and nothing has been identified).

### Step 3: Enumerate all partitions with at least one group of size $\geq 2$

The partitions of a 3-element set satisfying this condition are:

| Partition | Type |
|---|---|
| $\{C_1, C_2, C_3\}$ | All three together |
| $\{C_1, C_2\},\; \{C_3\}$ | One pair + one singleton |
| $\{C_1, C_3\},\; \{C_2\}$ | One pair + one singleton |
| $\{C_2, C_3\},\; \{C_1\}$ | One pair + one singleton |

The partition $\{C_1\},\{C_2\},\{C_3\}$ (all singletons) is excluded because no
circle is identified with any other.

### Step 4: Quotient by $S_3$ symmetry

By Step 1, two identification schemes are equivalent if they differ by a permuta
tion of the boundary circles. Under the $S_3$ action:

- The three partitions of "one pair + one singleton" type are all in a **single
orbit**: for instance, the transposition $(C_1\, C_3)$ sends $\{C_1, C_2\},\{C_3
\}$ to $\{C_2, C_3\},\{C_1\}$, and the 3-cycle sends it to $\{C_2, C_3\},\{C_1\}
$, etc.
- The partition $\{C_1, C_2, C_3\}$ (all three together) forms a **separate orbi
t** by itself: no permutation can transform a single group of size 3 into two gr
oups.

Thus there are exactly **two orbits**:

- **Type A:** $\{C_1, C_2, C_3\}$ — all three boundary circles identified togeth
er.
- **Type B:** $\{C_i, C_j\},\; \{C_k\}$ — two boundary circles identified togeth
er, the third left un-identified.

### Step 5: The two types are genuinely distinct

Type A and Type B cannot be equivalent under any self-homeomorphism of the pair
of pants, because:

1. **Combinatorially:** Type A has one group; Type B has two groups. No permutat
ion of three elements changes the number of parts in a partition.

2. **Topologically:** After performing the identification, Type A produces a spa
ce with **no remaining boundary circle** (all three have been glued away), where
as Type B produces a space that **still has one boundary circle** (the un-identi
fied singleton $C_k$). A space with a boundary circle is not homeomorphic to one
 without, so the two identifications yield non-homeomorphic results.

### Conclusion

The pair of pants admits exactly **two** distinct identification schemes for its
 three boundary circles, up to the natural $S_3$ symmetry of the surface:

1. **All three identified together** (one group of three).
2. **Two identified together, one separate** (one pair + one singleton).

No other identification scheme exists, because every partition of three circles
with at least one nontrivial group falls into one of these two $S_3$-orbits, and
 the two orbits are distinguished by the number of groups (equivalently, by whet
her a boundary circle remains after identification). $\blacksquare$
