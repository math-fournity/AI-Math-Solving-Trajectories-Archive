Proof

Let $\bar{F}$ be an algebraic closure of $F$ containing both $E$ and $K$ (embed
both into a common algebraic closure). The compositum $EK$ is the smallest
subfield of $\bar{F}$ containing both $E$ and $K$; it is algebraic over $F$
since both $E/F$ and $K/F$ are algebraic.

We use the following standard characterization of normality:

│ Lemma. An algebraic extension $L/F$ is normal if and only if every $F$-
│ embedding $\sigma : L \to \bar{F}$ satisfies $\sigma(L) = L$.

Let $\sigma : EK \to \bar{F}$ be an $F$-embedding. Since $EK/F$ is algebraic and
$\bar{F}$ is algebraically closed, $\sigma$ extends to an $F$-automorphism $
\tilde{\sigma} \in \operatorname{Gal}(\bar{F}/F)$.

Now restrict $\tilde{\sigma}$ to $E$ and to $K$:

  • $\tilde{\sigma}|_E : E \to \bar{F}$ is an $F$-embedding of $E$. Since $E/F$
  is normal, the Lemma gives $\tilde{\sigma}(E) = E$.
  • $\tilde{\sigma}|_K : K \to \bar{F}$ is an $F$-embedding of $K$. Since $K/F$
  is normal, the Lemma gives $\tilde{\sigma}(K) = K$.

Therefore

$$\tilde{\sigma}(EK) = \tilde{\sigma}(E),\tilde{\sigma}(K) = E \cdot K = EK.$$

Since $\sigma = \tilde{\sigma}\big|_{EK}$, we conclude $\sigma(EK) = EK$.

This holds for every $F$-embedding $\sigma : EK \to \bar{F}$, so by the Lemma,
$EK/F$ is normal. $\blacksquare$
