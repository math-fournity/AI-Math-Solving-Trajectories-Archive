## Proof

Let $\mathbf{D}$ denote the open unit disk. We are given that $f:\mathbf{D}\to\m
athbf{D}$ is holomorphic and that the restriction $f|_{\mathbf{D}\setminus\{0\}}
:\mathbf{D}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbf{D}\setminus\{0\}$ is a bijection.

**Step 1: The inverse is holomorphic on $\mathbf{D}\setminus\{0\}$.**

Since $f$ is injective on $\mathbf{D}\setminus\{0\}$, we have $f'(z)\neq 0$ for
all $z\in\mathbf{D}\setminus\{0\}$. Let $g:\mathbf{D}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbf{D}
\setminus\{0\}$ be the inverse of $f|_{\mathbf{D}\setminus\{0\}}$. By the invers
e function theorem, $g$ is holomorphic on $\mathbf{D}\setminus\{0\}$.

**Step 2: $g$ extends holomorphically to $\mathbf{D}$.**

Since $|g(z)|<1$ for all $z\in\mathbf{D}\setminus\{0\}$, the function $g$ is bou
nded near $0$. By Riemann's removable singularity theorem, $0$ is a removable si
ngularity, so $g$ extends to a holomorphic function $\tilde{g}:\mathbf{D}\to\mat
hbf{C}$.

By the maximum modulus principle, $|\tilde{g}(z)|<1$ for all $z\in\mathbf{D}$ (i
f $|\tilde{g}(0)|=1$, then $\tilde{g}$ would be constant, contradicting injectiv
ity). Hence $\tilde{g}:\mathbf{D}\to\mathbf{D}$.

**Step 3: $f$ and $\tilde{g}$ are mutual inverses on all of $\mathbf{D}$.**

On $\mathbf{D}\setminus\{0\}$, we have $f\circ g = \mathrm{id}$ and $g\circ f =
\mathrm{id}$. The compositions $f\circ\tilde{g}$ and $\tilde{g}\circ f$ are holo
morphic on $\mathbf{D}$ and agree with $\mathrm{id}$ on $\mathbf{D}\setminus\{0\
}$, which has a limit point in $\mathbf{D}$. By the identity theorem:
$$f\circ\tilde{g} = \mathrm{id}_{\mathbf{D}}, \qquad \tilde{g}\circ f = \mathrm{
id}_{\mathbf{D}}.$$

Therefore $f:\mathbf{D}\to\mathbf{D}$ is bijective, i.e., $f$ is a holomorphic a
utomorphism of $\mathbf{D}$.

**Step 4: $f(0)=0$.**

Since $f$ is a bijection $\mathbf{D}\to\mathbf{D}$ and $f(\mathbf{D}\setminus\{0
\})=\mathbf{D}\setminus\{0\}$, the remaining point must map to the remaining poi
nt:
$$f(\{0\}) = \mathbf{D}\setminus f(\mathbf{D}\setminus\{0\}) = \mathbf{D}\setmin
us(\mathbf{D}\setminus\{0\}) = \{0\}.$$

Hence $f(0)=0$. $\blacksquare$
