## Proof

Since the $G_K$-action on $M$ is trivial, the cocycle condition simplifies: a $1
$-cocycle is a continuous map $c: G_K \to M$ satisfying $c(\sigma\tau) = c(\sigm
a) + c(\tau)$, i.e. a continuous group homomorphism. Coboundaries are trivial (t
he action is trivial, so $c_\sigma = \sigma m - m = 0$). Hence

$$H^1(K, M) = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(G_K, M).$$

The localization map $H^1(K,M) \to H^1(K_v, M)$ sends a continuous homomorphism
$\chi: G_K \to M$ to its restriction $\chi|_{G_v}: G_v \to M$, where $G_v \subse
t G_K$ is a decomposition group at $v$ (defined up to conjugation; the kernel of
 the product is independent of the choices). Thus

$$F^1(K, M) = \{\chi \in \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(G_K, M) : \chi|_{G_v} = 0
\text{ for every place } v \text{ of } K\}.$$

We claim $F^1(K,M) = 0$.

Let $\chi \in F^1(K,M)$, so $\chi$ vanishes on $G_v$ for every place $v$. For ea
ch finite place $v$, the decomposition group $G_v$ contains the inertia group $I
_v$, and the fixed field of $I_v$ inside $\bar K$ is the maximal unramified exte
nsion of $K_v$. Crucially, for every finite place $v$ unramified in the extensio
n cut out by $\chi$, the Frobenius element $\mathrm{Frob}_v \in G_v$ lies in the
 kernel of $\chi$.

More precisely: $\chi$ is a continuous homomorphism with values in the *discrete
* group $M$, so its kernel is an open normal subgroup $U \trianglelefteq G_K$, c
orresponding to a finite Galois extension $L/K$. The condition $\chi|_{G_v} = 0$
 means $G_v \subset U$, i.e. $L$ is contained in the fixed field of $G_v$; in pa
rticular $L/K_v$ is trivial for every place $v$, meaning $L \otimes_K K_v = \pro
d K_v$ (the extension $L/K$ splits completely at every place $v$).

Now apply the Chebotarev density theorem. Since $L/K$ is finite Galois, there ex
ists a place $v$ of $K$ that is unramified in $L$ and whose Frobenius conjugacy
class in $\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$ is the identity if and only if $v$ splits completel
y. Chebotarev guarantees that places with Frobenius equal to any prescribed conj
ugacy class have positive density. In particular, if $L \neq K$, there exist pla
ces whose Frobenius is a *non-identity* element of $\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$, and such
 places do **not** split completely in $L$.

But we established that **every** place $v$ splits completely in $L$ (since $\ch
i|_{G_v}=0$ for all $v$). This forces $\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$ to be trivial, i.e. $L
 = K$, i.e. $U = G_K$, i.e. $\chi = 0$.

Therefore the only element of $F^1(K,M)$ is the zero homomorphism:

$$\boxed{F^1(K, M) = 0.}$$
