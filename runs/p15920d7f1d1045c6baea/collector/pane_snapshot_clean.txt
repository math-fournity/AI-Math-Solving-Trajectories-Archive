## Proof

We prove that **there exists a unique** $f \in C([0,1])$ satisfying
$$f(x) = \int_0^x K(x,y)\,f(y)\,dy + g(x), \qquad x \in [0,1].$$

**Setup.** Define the operator $T: C([0,1]) \to C([0,1])$ by
$$(Tf)(x) = \int_0^x K(x,y)\,f(y)\,dy + g(x).$$

*$T$ is well-defined.* Since $K$ is continuous on the compact set $[0,1]^2$, it
is bounded: set $M = \sup_{[0,1]^2}|K|$. For $f \in C([0,1])$, $f$ is bounded an
d the map $x \mapsto \int_0^x K(x,y)f(y)\,dy$ is continuous (uniform continuity
of $K$ on the compact square gives joint continuity of the integrand and hence c
ontinuity of the integral in its upper limit and parameter). Since $g \in C([0,1
])$, we have $Tf \in C([0,1])$.

**Weighted norm.** For $\lambda > 0$ define
$$\|f\|_\lambda = \sup_{x \in [0,1]} |f(x)|\,e^{-\lambda x}.$$
This is a norm on $C([0,1])$, equivalent to the sup-norm $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ (sin
ce $e^{-\lambda} \le e^{-\lambda x} \le 1$ on $[0,1]$). Hence $(C([0,1]), \|\cdo
t\|_\lambda)$ is a Banach space.

**$T$ is a contraction in $\|\cdot\|_\lambda$ for large $\lambda$.** For $f, h \
in C([0,1])$ and $x \in [0,1]$,
$$|(Tf)(x) - (Th)(x)| \le \int_0^x |K(x,y)|\,|f(y)-h(y)|\,dy \le M \int_0^x |f(y
)-h(y)|\,dy.$$
Writing $|f(y)-h(y)| = \|f-h\|_\lambda\, e^{\lambda y}$,
$$|(Tf)(x)-(Th)(x)| \le M\,\|f-h\|_\lambda \int_0^x e^{\lambda y}\,dy = M\,\|f-h
\|_\lambda\,\frac{e^{\lambda x}-1}{\lambda}.$$
Multiplying by $e^{-\lambda x}$,
$$e^{-\lambda x}|(Tf)(x)-(Th)(x)| \le \frac{M}{\lambda}\,\|f-h\|_\lambda\,(1 - e
^{-\lambda x}) \le \frac{M}{\lambda}\,\|f-h\|_\lambda.$$
Taking the supremum over $x \in [0,1]$,
$$\|Tf - Th\|_\lambda \le \frac{M}{\lambda}\,\|f-h\|_\lambda.$$

Choose $\lambda > M$ (possible since $M < \infty$). Then $q := M/\lambda < 1$, s
o $T$ is a contraction with constant $q$.

**Conclusion.** By the Banach fixed-point theorem, $T$ has a unique fixed point
$f \in C([0,1])$, i.e. a unique continuous function satisfying $f(x) = \int_0^x
K(x,y)f(y)\,dy + g(x)$ for all $x \in [0,1]$.

Therefore, **yes**, such a function exists and is unique. $\blacksquare$
