**Problem.** Determine the Assouad dimension of a complete unit ball in $\mathbb
{R}^D$.

**Answer.** The Assouad dimension equals $D$.

---

### Proof

Let $E = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^D : \|x\| \le 1\}$ be the closed (hence complete) un
it ball, equipped with the Euclidean metric. We use the standard definition: the
 Assouad dimension $\dim_A(X)$ is the infimum of all $\alpha \ge 0$ for which th
ere exists a constant $C \ge 0$ such that every ball $B(x,R)$ can be covered by
at most $C\,(R/r)^\alpha$ balls of radius $r$, for all $0 < r < R$.

**Upper bound: $\dim_A(E) \le D$.**

The Assouad dimension of $\mathbb{R}^D$ itself is $D$: a Euclidean ball of radiu
s $R$ is contained in a cube of side $2R$, which is covered by $(\lceil 2R/(2r)\
rceil + 1)^D \le (R/r + 2)^D \le C_D (R/r)^D$ cubes of side $2r$, each inscribed
 in a ball of radius $r$. Hence $\dim_A(\mathbb{R}^D) \le D$ (and in fact $=D$).
 Since $E \subseteq \mathbb{R}^D$ and the Assouad dimension is monotone under su
bsets (any cover of a ball in the ambient space restricts to a cover of its inte
rsection with $E$), we obtain
$$\dim_A(E) \le \dim_A(\mathbb{R}^D) = D.$$

**Lower bound: $\dim_A(E) \ge D$.**

The origin $0$ is an interior point of $E$. For any $0 < r < R < \tfrac12$, the
metric ball in $E$ centered at $0$ of radius $R$ coincides with the full Euclide
an ball:
$$B_E(0,R) = E \cap B(0,R) = B(0,R),$$
since $R < \tfrac12 < 1$.

Suppose $B(0,R) \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^{N} B(y_i, r)$ is a cover by Euclidean $
r$-balls. By a volume argument,
$$\operatorname{vol}\bigl(B(0,R)\bigr) \le \sum_{i=1}^{N} \operatorname{vol}\big
l(B(y_i,r)\bigr) = N \cdot \operatorname{vol}\bigl(B(0,r)\bigr),$$
so, using $\operatorname{vol}(B(0,\rho)) = \omega_D \rho^D$,
$$N \ge \frac{R^D}{r^D} = \left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^D.$$

Thus any cover of $B_E(0,R)$ by $r$-balls requires at least $(R/r)^D$ balls.

Now suppose $\dim_A(E) = \alpha$ with covering constant $C$. Then for all $0 < r
 < R < \tfrac12$,
$$C\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^\alpha \ge \left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^D.$$
Fixing $R$ (say $R = \tfrac14$) and letting $r \to 0^+$, the ratio $(R/r) \to \i
nfty$, forcing $\alpha \ge D$.

**Conclusion.** Combining both bounds,
$$\boxed{\dim_A(E) = D}.$$
