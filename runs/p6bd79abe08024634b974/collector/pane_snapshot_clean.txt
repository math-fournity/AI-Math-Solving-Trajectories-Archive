Looking at this problem, I need to determine when a line $l$ on a smooth quadric
 surface $Q \subset \mathbb{P}^3_k$ can appear as a double component of a plane
section, then compute the degree of the resulting zero cycle on the Fano variety
 $F(Q)$.

## Setup and Key Facts

A smooth quadric surface $Q \subset \mathbb{P}^3$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1
 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ via the Segre embedding. Its Fano variety of lines is:
$$F(Q) \cong \mathbb{P}^1 \sqcup \mathbb{P}^1$$
—one copy of $\mathbb{P}^1$ for each ruling. Every line on $Q$ belongs to exactl
y one ruling, and lines from the same ruling are disjoint, while lines from diff
erent rulings meet in exactly one point.

## Analyzing the Condition $2l \subseteq P \cap Q$

The condition $2l \subseteq P \cap Q$ means: there exists a plane $P \supseteq l
$ such that the conic $C = P \cap Q$ contains $l$ with multiplicity $\geq 2$. In
 scheme-theoretic terms, if $l \subset P \cong \mathbb{P}^2$ has ideal $(f)$ for
 a linear form $f$, then $2l$ has ideal $(f^2)$, and the condition requires $I_C
 \subseteq (f^2)$, i.e., $C = 2l$ is a **double line**.

## Direct Computation

Take the standard smooth quadric $Q: x_0 x_3 - x_1 x_2 = 0$ with Segre coordinat
es $(u_0:u_1)\times(v_0:v_1) \mapsto (u_0 v_0 : u_0 v_1 : u_1 v_0 : u_1 v_1)$.

**A line from the first ruling.** Let $l = \{x_2 = 0,\, x_3 = 0\}$, correspondin
g to $(u_0:u_1) = (1:0)$, with points $(v_0 : v_1 : 0 : 0)$.

**Any plane containing $l$** has equation $\alpha x_2 + \beta x_3 = 0$ for some
$(\alpha:\beta) \in \mathbb{P}^1$.

**The plane section.** For $\beta \neq 0$, substitute $x_3 = -\alpha x_2/\beta$
into $Q$:
$$x_0\!\left(-\frac{\alpha x_2}{\beta}\right) - x_1 x_2 = -\frac{x_2(\alpha x_0
+ \beta x_1)}{\beta} = 0.$$
So $P \cap Q$ is cut out by $x_2(\alpha x_0 + \beta x_1) = 0$ in $P$, giving:
- **Component 1:** $\{x_2 = 0\} \cap P = l$ (since $\beta \neq 0$ forces $x_3 =
0$ too). This is our line, from the **first ruling** $(1:0)$.
- **Component 2:** $\{\alpha x_0 + \beta x_1 = 0,\; \alpha x_2 + \beta x_3 = 0\}
$. Points have the form $(\beta t : -\alpha t : \beta s : -\alpha s)$, which in
Segre coordinates gives $(v_0:v_1) = (\beta:-\alpha)$. This is a line from the *
*second ruling**.

The case $\beta = 0$ gives $P: x_2 = 0$, and the conic becomes $x_0 x_3 = 0$, yi
elding $l$ (first ruling $(1:0)$) union the second-ruling line $(0:1)$.

**Conclusion for every plane $P \supseteq l$:** The conic $P \cap Q = l \cup l'$
 where $l'$ is always a line from the **opposite ruling** — a line distinct from
 $l$.

## Why $2l$ Never Occurs

A double line $2l$ would require $l' = l$, i.e., the two components coincide. Bu
t:
- $l$ is from the first ruling and $l'$ is from the second ruling.
- On a smooth quadric $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$, the two rulings are **
disjoint families**: no line belongs to both rulings.
- Equivalently, a line from the first ruling is $\{s\} \times \mathbb{P}^1$ and
from the second is $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \{t\}$; these are equal only if $\mathbb
{P}^1$ is a point, which is absurd.

Therefore $l' \neq l$ for every plane $P$ containing $l$, and $P \cap Q$ is **ne
ver** a double line.

## Symmetry Across Both Rulings

The same argument applies verbatim to a line $l$ from the second ruling: any pla
ne through $l$ cuts $Q$ in $l$ union a line from the first ruling, always two di
stinct lines.

## Conclusion

The locus $F_{osc}$ is **empty**:
$$F_{osc} = \{[l] \in F(Q) : \exists\, P \text{ plane with } 2l \subseteq P \cap
 Q\} = \emptyset.$$

For a smooth quadric surface, every plane section is either a smooth conic or a
pair of distinct lines (from opposite rulings, meeting at one point)—never a dou
ble line. The associated zero cycle on $F(Q) \cong \mathbb{P}^1 \sqcup \mathbb{P
}^1$ is the zero cycle, and its degree is:

$$\deg(F_{osc}) = 0$$
