# Solution

**Claim.** The supremum of all ordinals that admit an order-preserving injection
 into $[0,1]$ is $\omega_1$, the first uncountable ordinal. It is *not* the init
ial ordinal of $2^{\aleph_0}$ (those two coincide only under the Continuum Hypot
hesis).

---

## Part 1. Every well-ordered subset of $[0,1]$ is countable.

Let $W \subseteq [0,1]$ be well-ordered by the usual order on $\mathbb{R}$. For
every $w \in W$ that is not the maximum of $W$, the set $\{x \in W : x > w\}$ is
 nonempty, so by well-ordering it has a least element $s(w)$. Call $s(w)$ the **
successor** of $w$ in $W$.

The open intervals $(w, s(w))$ are pairwise disjoint: if $w_1 < w_2$ in $W$ and
both have successors, then $s(w_1) \leq w_2$ (since $w_2 \in \{x \in W : x > w_1
\}$ and $s(w_1)$ is the least such), so $(w_1, s(w_1))$ and $(w_2, s(w_2))$ are
disjoint.

Each interval $(w, s(w))$ is nonempty (since $w < s(w)$ in $\mathbb{R}$), so it
contains a rational number. Choosing one rational $q_w \in (w, s(w)) \cap \mathb
b{Q}$ for each non-maximum $w \in W$ gives an injection
$$W \setminus \{\max W\} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Q},$$
since the intervals are disjoint and hence the chosen rationals are distinct. Th
erefore $W$ is countable. $\square$

**Corollary.** If an ordinal $\alpha$ admits an order-preserving injection $f :
\alpha \to [0,1]$, then $f[\alpha] \subseteq [0,1]$ is a well-ordered subset of
$[0,1]$ (well-ordered because $\alpha$ is well-ordered and $f$ is an order-embed
ding), hence countable, so $\alpha$ is a countable ordinal.

---

## Part 2. Every countable ordinal embeds into $[0,1]$.

We use the classical theorem of Cantor: **every countable linear order embeds in
to $\mathbb{Q}$.**

*Proof of the theorem (sketch).* Let $(L, <_L)$ be a countable linear order, enu
merated as $L = \{l_0, l_1, l_2, \ldots\}$. Build an embedding $f : L \to \mathb
b{Q}$ inductively. At stage $n$, we have embedded $l_0, \ldots, l_{n-1}$ into $\
mathbb{Q}$ preserving order. To place $l_n$: the set $\{f(l_i) : i < n,\; l_i <_
L l_n\}$ has a supremum $a \in \mathbb{R} \cup \{-\infty\}$ and the set $\{f(l_i
) : i < n,\; l_n <_L l_i\}$ has an infimum $b \in \mathbb{R} \cup \{+\infty\}$,
with $a \leq b$. Since $\mathbb{Q}$ is dense and without endpoints, we can pick
a rational $f(l_n) \in (a, b)$. This preserves the order at every stage. $\squar
e$

Since $\mathbb{Q} \cap (0,1)$ is itself a countable dense linear order without e
ndpoints, it is order-isomorphic to $\mathbb{Q}$ (again by Cantor's back-and-for
th theorem), so every countable linear order embeds into $\mathbb{Q} \cap (0,1)
\subset [0,1]$.

Every countable ordinal is a countable linear order, so every countable ordinal
admits an order-preserving injection into $[0,1]$. $\square$

---

## Part 3. The supremum is $\omega_1$.

Combining Parts 1 and 2:

$$\{\alpha \in \mathrm{Ord} : \alpha \text{ embeds into } [0,1]\} = \{\alpha \in
 \mathrm{Ord} : \alpha \text{ is countable}\} = \omega_1.$$

The set of all countable ordinals *is* $\omega_1$ itself (as a set). The supremu
m of all countable ordinals is $\omega_1$, the first uncountable ordinal.

---

## Part 4. It is not the initial ordinal of $2^{\aleph_0}$ (in general).

The initial ordinal of a cardinal $\kappa$ is the least ordinal of cardinality $
\kappa$. The initial ordinal of $2^{\aleph_0}$ is the least ordinal of cardinali
ty $2^{\aleph_0}$.

- Under CH, $2^{\aleph_0} = \aleph_1$, so the initial ordinal of $2^{\aleph_0}$
is $\omega_1$. In this case the two candidates coincide.
- Under $\neg$CH, $2^{\aleph_0} \geq \aleph_2$, so the initial ordinal of $2^{\a
leph_0}$ is some $\omega_\beta$ with $\beta \geq 2$, strictly larger than $\omeg
a_1$.

Since the answer $\omega_1$ holds in **ZFC regardless** of whether CH is true, w
hile the "initial ordinal of $2^{\aleph_0}$" answer depends on CH and is general
ly strictly larger, the correct answer is:

$$\boxed{\omega_1}$$

the first uncountable ordinal — **not** the initial ordinal of $2^{\aleph_0}$.
