## Solution

We seek a polynomial $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ satisfying
$$f(f(x)-2y)=2x-3y+f(f(y)-x)\quad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}.$$

### Step 1: Determine the degree of $f$

Let $\deg f = n$.

- **LHS:** $f(x)-2y$ has degree $n$ in $x$ and degree $1$ in $y$. Applying $f$ g
ives $f(f(x)-2y)$ with degree $n\cdot n = n^2$ in $x$ and degree $n\cdot 1 = n$
in $y$.
- **RHS:** $f(y)-x$ has degree $n$ in $y$ and degree $1$ in $x$. Applying $f$ gi
ves $f(f(y)-x)$ with degree $n^2$ in $y$ and degree $n$ in $x$. Adding $2x-3y$ d
oesn't change the leading degree.

Comparing degrees **in $x$**: LHS has degree $n^2$, RHS has degree $\max(1,n)=n$
 (for $n\ge 1$). Hence $n^2=n$, so $n=1$ (the case $n=0$ gives $f$ constant, whi
ch makes LHS constant while RHS $=2x-3y+c$ is not — impossible).

### Step 2: Solve for the linear polynomial

Write $f(x)=ax+b$. Then:

$$f(f(x)-2y)=f(ax+b-2y)=a(ax+b-2y)+b=a^2x+ab-2ay+b.$$

$$f(f(y)-x)=f(ay+b-x)=a(ay+b-x)+b=a^2y+ab-ax+b.$$

$$\text{RHS}=2x-3y+a^2y+ab-ax+b=(2-a)x+(a^2-3)y+ab+b.$$

Equating coefficients with LHS $=a^2x+(-2a)y+ab+b$:

- **$x$-coefficient:** $a^2=2-a\;\Rightarrow\;a^2+a-2=0\;\Rightarrow\;(a+2)(a-1)
=0$, so $a\in\{-2,1\}$.
- **$y$-coefficient:** $-2a=a^2-3\;\Rightarrow\;a^2+2a-3=0\;\Rightarrow\;(a+3)(a
-1)=0$, so $a\in\{-3,1\}$.

The common solution is $a=1$. (The constant term $ab+b=ab+b$ is automatically sa
tisfied.)

### Step 3: Verify

With $f(x)=x+b$:
- LHS: $f(x+b-2y)=x+2b-2y$.
- RHS: $2x-3y+f(y+b-x)=2x-3y+y+b-x+b=x-2y+2b$. ✓

So $f(x)=x+b$ for arbitrary $b\in\mathbb{R}$.

### Step 4: Compute the answer

$$f(21)-f(14)=(21+b)-(14+b)=7.$$
