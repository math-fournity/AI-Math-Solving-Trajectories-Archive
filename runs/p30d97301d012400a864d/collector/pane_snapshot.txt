Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the closed upper hemisphere
 of S² is diffeomorphic to the closed unit disk in R² (as manifolds with boundar
y).

**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

Let $M = \{(x,y,z)\in S^2 : z \ge 0\}$ (closed upper hemisphere) and $D = \{(u,v
)\in\mathbb{R}^2 : u^2+v^2 \le 1\}$ (closed unit disk). Both are smooth 2-manifo
lds with boundary; $\partial M$ is the equator ($z=0$) and $\partial D$ is the u
nit circle.

**Key observation.** The "obvious" map — orthogonal projection $\pi(x,y,z)=(x,y)
$ with inverse $(u,v)\mapsto(u,v,\sqrt{1-u^2-v^2})$ — is a homeomorphism but **n
ot** a diffeomorphism: near the boundary, $z=\sqrt{s}$ where $s=1-u^2-v^2$, and
$\sqrt{s}$ is not smooth at $s=0$. The failure is that $z$ vanishes like the squ
are root of the boundary distance. We fix this by **reparametrizing the radial c
oordinate** so that $z$ vanishes linearly.

**Construction of $F:D\to M$.** Write points of $D$ in polar form $(r\cos\theta,
 r\sin\theta)$, $r\in[0,1]$, and points of $M$ in spherical form $(\sin\varphi\c
os\theta,\,\sin\varphi\sin\theta,\,\cos\varphi)$, $\varphi\in[0,\pi/2]$. Define
$$
F(r\cos\theta,\, r\sin\theta) \;=\; \bigl(\sin\varphi(r)\cos\theta,\;\sin\varphi
(r)\sin\theta,\;\cos\varphi(r)\bigr), \qquad \varphi(r)=\tfrac{\pi}{2}\,r.
$$
In Cartesian coordinates on $D$, with $\rho = u^2+v^2$:
$$
F(u,v) = \Bigl(h(\rho)\,u,\;\; h(\rho)\,v,\;\; k(\rho)\Bigr),
$$
where $h(\rho) = \frac{\sin\!\bigl(\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\rho}\bigr)}{\sqrt{\rho}}$
 (with $h(0)=\pi/2$) and $k(\rho)=\cos\!\bigl(\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\rho}\bigr)$.

**$F$ is a bijection.** The map $r\mapsto\varphi=\frac{\pi}{2}r$ is a bijection
$[0,1]\to[0,\pi/2]$, and $\theta$ is preserved, so $F$ is a bijection $D\to M$ (
center maps to north pole, boundary circle maps to equator).

**$F$ is smooth.** We check three regions:

*Interior of $D$ ($0<r<1$):* $F$ is a composition of smooth functions. ✓

*Center ($r=0$):* Expand $h(\rho) = \frac{\pi}{2}\Bigl(1 - \frac{\pi^2}{24}\rho
+ \cdots\Bigr)$ and $k(\rho)=1-\frac{\pi^2}{8}\rho+\cdots$. Both are **smooth fu
nctions of $\rho=u^2+v^2$** (the Taylor series involve only integer powers of $\
rho$). So $F(u,v)=(h(\rho)u,\, h(\rho)v,\, k(\rho))$ is smooth at the origin. ✓

*Boundary ($r=1$, i.e.\ $s=1-u^2-v^2=0$):* Use the boundary chart $\phi(u,v)=(s,
t)=(1-u^2-v^2,\,v)$ for $D$ near $(1,0)$, and $\psi(x,y,z)=(c,d)=(z,\,y)$ for $M
$ near $(1,0,0)$. In these coordinates:
$$
c = \cos\!\bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{1-s}\bigr), \qquad d = \frac{\sin\!\bigl(\fr
ac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{1-s}\bigr)}{\sqrt{1-s}}\;t.
$$
At $s=0$: $c=0$, $d=t$, and $\frac{\partial c}{\partial s}\big|_{s=0} = \frac{\p
i}{4}\neq 0$, $\frac{\partial d}{\partial t}\big|_{(0,0)}=1$. The map $(s,t)\map
sto(c,d)$ is smooth with invertible Jacobian at the boundary. ✓

**$F^{-1}$ is smooth.** The inverse is $F^{-1}(x,y,z) = \bigl(\alpha(z)\,x,\;\al
pha(z)\,y\bigr)$ where $\alpha(z)=\frac{2}{\pi}\frac{\arccos z}{\sqrt{1-z^2}}$ (
with $\alpha(1)=1$). The function $\alpha$ is smooth on $[0,1]$: near $z=1$, $\a
rccos z \sim \sqrt{2(1-z)}$ and $\sqrt{1-z^2}\sim\sqrt{2(1-z)}$, so the ratio ex
tends smoothly to $1$; near $z=0$, both $\arccos z$ and $\sqrt{1-z^2}$ are smoot
h and nonzero.

*North pole ($z=1$, $x=y=0$):* $F^{-1}(x,y,z)=(\alpha(z)\,x,\,\alpha(z)\,y)$ wit
h $\alpha$ smooth near $z=1$, so this is smooth. ✓

*Boundary ($z=0$):* Using the same boundary charts as above, $F^{-1}$ in coordin
ates $(c,d)\mapsto(s,t)$ gives
$$
s = 1 - \bigl(\tfrac{2}{\pi}\arccos c\bigr)^2, \qquad t = \frac{\frac{2}{\pi}\ar
ccos c}{\sqrt{1-c^2}}\;d.
$$
At $c=0$: $s=0$, $t=d$, $\frac{\partial s}{\partial c}\big|_{c=0}=\frac{4}{\pi}\
neq 0$, $\frac{\partial t}{\partial d}\big|_{(0,0)}=1$. Smooth with invertible J
acobian. ✓

**Conclusion.** $F:D\to M$ is a smooth bijection with smooth inverse, hence a di
ffeomorphism of manifolds with boundary. The closed upper hemisphere of $S^2$ is
 diffeomorphic to the closed disk in $\mathbb{R}^2$.

The essential point is that while the *naive* projection fails to be a diffeomor
phism (its inverse has a $\sqrt{s}$ singularity at the boundary), a radial repar
ametrization $\varphi = \frac{\pi}{2}r$ converts the square-root vanishing of $z
$ at the equator into a linear vanishing, yielding a smooth map in boundary char
ts.
