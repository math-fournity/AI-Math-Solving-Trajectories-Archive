## Proof

**Claim.** The only solutions are the constant polynomials $p(x) = c$ where $c$
is a palindrome number.

Constant polynomials clearly work: if $c$ is a palindrome, then $p(n) = c$ is a
palindrome for all $n$. We prove no non-constant polynomial works.

Suppose for contradiction that $p(x) \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ is non-constant of degre
e $k \geq 1$ with leading coefficient $a_k \neq 0$, and $p(n)$ is a palindrome f
or every positive integer $n$.

### Lemma

For every positive integer $m$, there exist arbitrarily large positive integers
$n$ such that $|p(n)|, |p(n+1)|, \ldots, |p(n+10^m-1)|$ all have the same first
$m$ digits in decimal.

*Proof of Lemma.* WLOG $a_k > 0$ (if $a_k < 0$, work with $|p(n)| = -p(n)$ for l
arge $n$). For large $n$, $p$ is positive and strictly increasing. For $n$ in a
block of $10^m$ consecutive large integers, all $p(n+i)$ share the same digit co
unt $D$, since $p(n+10^m)-p(n) = O(n^{k-1} \cdot 10^m) = o(p(n))$.

The first $m$ digits of $p(n+i)$ are $\lfloor p(n+i)/10^{D-m}\rfloor$. These are
 constant across the block unless some $p(n+i)$ crosses a multiple of $10^{D-m}$
. Over $n \in [N, 2N]$ for large $N$: the range $p(2N)-p(N) = O(N^k)$ contains $
O(N^k / (N^k/10^m)) = O(10^m)$ multiples of $10^{D-m}$. Each crossing creates a
"bad" zone of width $O(10^m)$ in $n$-space. Total bad measure is $O(10^{2m}) = o
(N)$, so good blocks exist for $N$ large enough. $\square$

### Main argument

Fix $m \geq 1$. By the Lemma, choose $n$ large enough that $p(n), \ldots, p(n+10
^m-1)$ all have the same first $m$ digits (and at least $2m$ digits each, so fir
st and last $m$ digits don't overlap).

Since each $p(n+i)$ is a palindrome, its last $m$ digits are the digit-reversal
of its first $m$ digits. Since the first $m$ digits are identical across the blo
ck, the last $m$ digits are also identical:

$$p(n) \equiv p(n+1) \equiv \cdots \equiv p(n+10^m-1) \pmod{10^m}.$$

Since $p$ has integer coefficients, $p(r) \bmod 10^m$ depends only on $r \bmod 1
0^m$. The $10^m$ consecutive integers $n, n+1, \ldots, n+10^m-1$ cover all resid
ue classes modulo $10^m$, so:

$$p(r) \equiv p(0) \pmod{10^m} \quad \text{for all } r \in \{0, 1, \ldots, 10^m-
1\}.$$

By periodicity modulo $10^m$, this extends to all integers:

$$p(n) \equiv p(0) \pmod{10^m} \quad \text{for all } n \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

Since this holds for **every** $m \geq 1$, we have $10^m \mid (p(n) - p(0))$ for
 all $m$ and all $n$. The only integer divisible by all powers of $10$ is $0$, s
o:

$$p(n) = p(0) \quad \text{for all } n \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

A polynomial constant on infinitely many points is constant — contradicting $k \
geq 1$.

### Conclusion

The only polynomials $p(x) \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ with $p(n)$ a palindrome for every
 positive integer $n$ are the **constant polynomials** $p(x) = c$, where $c$ is
any palindrome number (including $0$, positive palindromes like $1, 11, 121, \ld
ots$, and negative palindromes like $-1, -11, -121, \ldots$). $\blacksquare$
