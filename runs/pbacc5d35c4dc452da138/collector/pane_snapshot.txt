## Theorem (`omega_three`)

$$\mathfrak{c} \to (\omega, 3)^3_2$$

That is: for any 2-coloring (red/blue) of all 3-element subsets of the ordinal $
\mathfrak{c}$ (the initial ordinal of cardinality $2^{\aleph_0}$), either there
exists a red-monochromatic subset of order type $\omega$, or there exists a blue
-monochromatic subset of cardinality $3$.

## Proof

Let $\alpha = \mathfrak{c}.\text{ord}$, and let $\text{isRed} : \alpha \to \alph
a \to \alpha \to \text{Prop}$ be any permutation-invariant coloring of ordered t
riples of distinct elements, defining a 2-coloring of all 3-element subsets of $
\alpha$ into red and blue. We must show:

$$\bigl(\exists\, s \subseteq \alpha,\; \operatorname{typeLT}(s) = \omega \;\wed
ge\; s \text{ is red-monochromatic}\bigr) \;\vee\; \bigl(\exists\, s \subseteq \
alpha,\; \#s = 3 \;\wedge\; s \text{ is blue-monochromatic}\bigr).$$

We split into two cases.

### Case 1: There exists a blue triple.

Suppose there exist three distinct elements $a, b, c \in \alpha$ such that the 3
-element subset $\{a, b, c\}$ is colored blue, i.e., $\neg\,\text{isRed}(a, b, c
)$ (equivalently for any ordering, by permutation invariance).

Set $s = \{a, b, c\}$. Then $\#s = 3$. The only 3-element subset of $s$ is $s$ i
tself, and it is blue. Therefore $s$ is blue-monochromatic of cardinality $3$. T
his gives the second disjunct. ✓

### Case 2: No blue triple exists.

Suppose every 3-element subset of $\alpha$ is colored red. That is, for all dist
inct $x, y, z \in \alpha$, $\text{isRed}(x, y, z)$ holds.

Since $\mathfrak{c} = 2^{\aleph_0} \geq \aleph_0 > \aleph_0$ in particular $\mat
hfrak{c} \geq \aleph_0$, so $\omega \leq \mathfrak{c}$ as ordinals. Therefore th
ere exists a subset $s \subseteq \alpha$ of order type $\omega$ — for instance,
take the first $\omega$ elements of $\alpha$ (which exist since $\omega \leq \al
pha$).

Now every 3-element subset of $s$ is a 3-element subset of $\alpha$, hence is co
lored red. Therefore $s$ is red-monochromatic with $\operatorname{typeLT}(s) = \
omega$. This gives the first disjunct. ✓

### Conclusion

In both cases, one of the two alternatives holds. Therefore:

$$\mathfrak{c} \to (\omega, 3)^3_2. \qquad \blacksquare$$
