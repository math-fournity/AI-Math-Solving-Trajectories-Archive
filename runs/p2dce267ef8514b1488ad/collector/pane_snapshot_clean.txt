# Derivation of the Volume Element in Orthogonal Coordinate Systems

## Setup: General Coordinates in $\mathbb{R}^n$

Let an $n$-dimensional (Riemannian) space be parametrized by general curvilinear
 coordinates $(q^1, q^2, \dots, q^n)$. Let the position vector of a point be $\m
athbf{r}(q^1,\dots,q^n)$. The **covariant basis vectors** (tangent vectors along
 each coordinate curve) are

$$\mathbf{e}_i = \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial q^i}, \qquad i = 1,\dots,n.
$$

The **metric tensor** is

$$g_{ij} = \mathbf{e}_i \cdot \mathbf{e}_j = \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial
 q^i}\cdot\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial q^j}.$$

## Volume Element in General Coordinates

The infinitesimal parallelepiped spanned by the coordinate differentials $dq^1,\
dots,dq^n$ has edges $dq^i\,\mathbf{e}_i$. Its (signed) volume is the scalar tri
ple product generalized to $n$ dimensions:

$$dV = \left|\det\big[\mathbf{e}_1 \;\mathbf{e}_2\;\cdots\;\mathbf{e}_n\big]\rig
ht|\,dq^1\,dq^2\cdots dq^n.$$

Squaring and using the Gram determinant identity,

$$\big(\det[\mathbf{e}_1\cdots\mathbf{e}_n]\big)^2 = \det(g_{ij}) = g,$$

so the **general volume element** is

$$\boxed{dV = \sqrt{g}\;dq^1\,dq^2\cdots dq^n, \qquad g = \det(g_{ij}).} \tag{1}
$$

This is the standard expression for the volume element in arbitrary curvilinear
coordinates.

## Specialization to Orthogonal Coordinates

A coordinate system is **orthogonal** when the coordinate curves meet at right a
ngles everywhere, i.e.,

$$\mathbf{e}_i \cdot \mathbf{e}_j = 0 \quad \text{for all } i \neq j.$$

Hence the metric tensor is **diagonal**:

$$g_{ij} = 0\;(i\neq j), \qquad g_{ii} = \mathbf{e}_i\cdot\mathbf{e}_i = \left|\
frac{\partial\mathbf{r}}{\partial q^i}\right|^2.$$

Define the **scale factors** (Lamé coefficients)

$$h_i = \sqrt{g_{ii}} = \left|\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}}{\partial q^i}\right| \ge
 0, \qquad i=1,\dots,n.$$

Then $g_{ii} = h_i^2$, and the metric tensor is

$$(g_{ij}) = \operatorname{diag}(h_1^2,\;h_2^2,\;\dots,\;h_n^2).$$

## Computing the Determinant

Since the matrix is diagonal, its determinant is the product of the diagonal ent
ries:

$$g = \det(g_{ij}) = h_1^2\,h_2^2\cdots h_n^2 = \prod_{i=1}^{n} h_i^2.$$

Taking the square root (and choosing the positive root since volume is non-negat
ive):

$$\sqrt{g} = \sqrt{\prod_{i=1}^{n} h_i^2} = \prod_{i=1}^{n} h_i = h_1\,h_2\cdots
 h_n. \tag{2}$$

## Final Result

Substituting (2) into the general formula (1):

$$\boxed{dV = h_1\,h_2\cdots h_n\;dq^1\,dq^2\cdots dq^n = \left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}
h_i\right)\,dq^1\,dq^2\cdots dq^n.} \tag{3}$$

This is the **volume element in an orthogonal coordinate system**: the product o
f the scale factors times the product of the coordinate differentials.

## Verification Against Known Cases

- **Cartesian coordinates** $(x,y,z)$: $h_x=h_y=h_z=1$, so $dV = dx\,dy\,dz$. ✓
- **Cylindrical coordinates** $(\rho,\varphi,z)$: $h_\rho=1,\;h_\varphi=\rho,\;h
_z=1$, so $dV = \rho\,d\rho\,d\varphi\,dz$. ✓
- **Spherical coordinates** $(r,\theta,\varphi)$: $h_r=1,\;h_\theta=r,\;h_\varph
i=r\sin\theta$, so $dV = r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\varphi$. ✓

All classical cases are recovered, confirming the derivation.

## Summary

Starting from the general-coordinate volume element $dV = \sqrt{\det(g_{ij})}\,d
q^1\cdots dq^n$, the orthogonality condition forces the metric tensor to be diag
onal with entries $g_{ii}=h_i^2$. The determinant then factorizes as the product
 $\prod h_i^2$, whose square root is $\prod h_i$, yielding the orthogonal-coordi
nate volume element $\displaystyle dV = \prod_{i=1}^{n} h_i\;dq^1\cdots dq^n$.
