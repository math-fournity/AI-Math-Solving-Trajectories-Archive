## Solution

Use the substitution $u = x^2 - y^2$, $v = xy$, so the region becomes the rectan
gle $1 \le u \le 9$, $2 \le v \le 4$.

**Jacobian.**
$$\frac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(x,y)} = \begin{vmatrix} 2x & -2y \\ y & x \end{v
matrix} = 2x^2 + 2y^2 = 2(x^2+y^2).$$

Hence $du\,dv = 2(x^2+y^2)\,dx\,dy$, i.e. $dx\,dy = \dfrac{du\,dv}{2(x^2+y^2)}$.

**One-to-one check.** For $v > 0$ (which holds since $2 \le v \le 4$), we have $
x, y > 0$. Given $u, v$,
$$(x^2+y^2)^2 = (x^2-y^2)^2 + 4x^2y^2 = u^2 + 4v^2,$$
so $x^2+y^2 = \sqrt{u^2+4v^2}$, and
$$x^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}(\sqrt{u^2+4v^2}+u),\quad y^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}(\sqrt{u^2+4v^2}
-u).$$
Both are non-negative, and with $x,y \ge 0$ the values are uniquely determined.
Thus the map is a bijection on $M$.

**Integral.** The factor $(x^2+y^2)$ cancels against the Jacobian:
$$\int_M (x^2+y^2)\,dV = \int_1^9\!\!\int_2^4 (x^2+y^2)\cdot\frac{du\,dv}{2(x^2+
y^2)} = \int_1^9\!\!\int_2^4 \frac{1}{2}\,dv\,du = \frac{1}{2}\cdot 8 \cdot 2 =
\boxed{8}.$$
