## Theorem

Let $M$ and $N$ be Riemannian manifolds with boundary, and let $f: M \to N$ be a
 local isometry. If $M$ is complete and $N$ is connected, then $f$ is a covering
 map.

## Proof

**The theorem holds.** The classical proof for manifolds without boundary extend
s to manifolds with boundary. We verify the three key ingredients: (1) $f$ is a
local diffeomorphism preserving boundary, (2) $f$ has the path-lifting property
(using completeness of $M$), and (3) geodesic uniqueness in small balls (using t
he exponential map at boundary points).

### Step 1: $f$ is a local diffeomorphism and preserves boundary/interior

By definition, a local isometry $f: M \to N$ is a smooth map such that for each
$p \in M$, there exist neighborhoods $U \ni p$ and $V \ni f(p)$ with $f|_U: U \t
o V$ an isometry, hence a diffeomorphism of manifolds with boundary. In particul
ar, $f$ is a local diffeomorphism.

The boundary is a topological invariant: a point $p$ lies on $\partial M$ if and
 only if $p$ has no neighborhood in $M$ homeomorphic to an open ball in $\mathbb
{R}^n$ (every neighborhood is instead homeomorphic to a half-ball). Since $f$ is
 a local homeomorphism, it preserves this property, giving
$$f(\partial M) \subseteq \partial N, \qquad f(\operatorname{int} M) \subseteq \
operatorname{int} N.$$

$$T = \sup\{t \in [0,1] : \gamma \text{ can be lifted on } [0,t)\}.$$
We show $T = 1$ by contradiction. Suppose $T < 1$.

The lift $\tilde{\gamma}: [0,T) \to M$ is piecewise smooth. Since $f$ is a local

$$\operatorname{Length}(\tilde{\gamma}|_{[t_1,t_2]}) = \operatorname{Length}(\ga
mma|_{[t_1,t_2]})$$

$$d_M\!\bigl(\tilde{\gamma}(t_1),\, \tilde{\gamma}(t_2)\bigr) \le \operatorname{

So $\tilde{\gamma}(t)$ is Cauchy as $t \to T^-$. Since $M$ is **metrically compl
