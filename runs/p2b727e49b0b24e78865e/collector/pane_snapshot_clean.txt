## Setting up the structure

The distance between lattice points $(x_1,y_1)$ and $(x_2,y_2)$ is $\sqrt{(x_1-x
_2)^2+(y_1-y_2)^2}$, which is an integer iff $(\Delta x)^2+(\Delta y)^2$ is a pe
rfect square.

With $|\Delta x|,|\Delta y| \in \{0,1,2,3,4\}$, I enumerate all pairs $(a,b)$ wi
th $a \leq b$ where $a^2+b^2$ is a perfect square:

| $(a,b)$ | $a^2+b^2$ | Perfect square? |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| (0,1) | 1 | Yes (=1²) |
| (0,2) | 4 | Yes (=2²) |
| (0,3) | 9 | Yes (=3²) |
| (0,4) | 16 | Yes (=4²) |
| (1,1)–(4,4) | 2,5,10,17,8,13,20,18,**25**,32 | Only **25** = 3²+4² |

**Key observation:** Integer distance occurs iff two points share a row ($\Delta
 x=0$), share a column ($\Delta y=0$), **or** $\{|\Delta x|,|\Delta y|\}=\{3,4\}
$ (distance 5).

## Reducing to permutations

Since we choose 5 points from 5 rows and 5 columns with no two sharing a row or
column, by pigeonhole each row and each column contains exactly one point. This
is a **permutation** $\sigma$ of $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$, where the point in row $y$ is
$(\sigma(y), y)$.

For a permutation, $\Delta x \neq 0$ and $\Delta y \neq 0$ for any distinct pair
, so the row/column conditions are automatic. The only remaining bad case is $\{
|\Delta x|,|\Delta y|\}=\{3,4\}$.

Pairs with $|\Delta y|=|a-b|=4$: only $\{a,b\}=\{1,5\}$ → need $|\sigma(1)-\sigm
a(5)|\neq 3$.

Pairs with $|\Delta y|=|a-b|=3$: $\{a,b\}\in\{\{1,4\},\{2,5\}\}$ → need $|\sigma
(1)-\sigma(4)|\neq 4$ and $|\sigma(2)-\sigma(5)|\neq 4$.

**Three constraints:**
1. $|\sigma(1)-\sigma(5)|\neq 3$
2. $|\sigma(1)-\sigma(4)|\neq 4$
3. $|\sigma(2)-\sigma(5)|\neq 4$

## Counting via inclusion-exclusion

Let $A,B,C$ be the sets violating constraints 1,2,3 respectively. Total permutat
ions: $5!=120$.

**$|A|=24$:** $|\sigma(1)-\sigma(5)|=3$ → pairs $\{1,4\},\{2,5\}$: 4 ordered cho
ices × $3!=6$ = 24.

**$|B|=12$:** $|\sigma(1)-\sigma(4)|=4$ → only $\{1,5\}$: 2 × 6 = 12.

**$|C|=12$:** $|\sigma(2)-\sigma(5)|=4$ → only $\{1,5\}$: 2 × 6 = 12.

**$|A\cap B|=4$:** From $B$: $\sigma(1)\in\{1,5\}$.
- $\sigma(1)=1,\sigma(4)=5$: $|1-\sigma(5)|=3\Rightarrow\sigma(5)=4$; remaining
$\{2,3\}$→pos 2,3: 2 ways.
- $\sigma(1)=5,\sigma(4)=1$: $|5-\sigma(5)|=3\Rightarrow\sigma(5)=2$; remaining
$\{3,4\}$→pos 2,3: 2 ways.
Total: 4.

**$|A\cap C|=4$:** From $C$: $\{\sigma(2),\sigma(5)\}=\{1,5\}$.
- $\sigma(2)=1,\sigma(5)=5$: $|\sigma(1)-5|=3\Rightarrow\sigma(1)=2$; remaining
$\{3,4\}$→pos 3,4: 2 ways.
- $\sigma(2)=5,\sigma(5)=1$: $|\sigma(1)-1|=3\Rightarrow\sigma(1)=4$; remaining
$\{2,3\}$→pos 3,4: 2 ways.
Total: 4.

**$|B\cap C|=0$:** Both require $\{1,5\}$ for different position pairs — impossi
ble in a permutation.

**$|A\cap B\cap C|=0$:** Since $B\cap C=\emptyset$.

$$|A\cup B\cup C|=24+12+12-4-4-0+0=40$$

$$\text{Answer}=120-40=\boxed{80}$$
