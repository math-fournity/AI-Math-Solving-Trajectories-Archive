## Proof

We show that no such continuous function exists. The answer is **false**.

**Key facts used:**
- $\mathbb{Q}$ is countable; $\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}$ is uncountable.
- The continuous image of a connected set is connected. Since $\mathbb{R}$ is co
nnected, $f(\mathbb{R})$ is a connected subset of $\mathbb{R}$, i.e., an interva
l (possibly a single point).

**Step 1: $f(\mathbb{R})$ is at most countable.**

By hypothesis:
$$f(\mathbb{R}) = f(\mathbb{Q}) \cup f(\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}) \subseteq
(\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}) \cup \mathbb{Q} = \mathbb{R}.$$

More precisely, $f(\mathbb{Q}) \subseteq \mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}$ and $f(\
mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}) \subseteq \mathbb{Q}$.

Since $\mathbb{Q}$ is countable, its image $f(\mathbb{Q})$ is at most countable.
Since $\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}$ maps into $\mathbb{Q}$, the set $f(\mathbb
{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}) \subseteq \mathbb{Q}$ is also at most countable.

Therefore
$$f(\mathbb{R}) = f(\mathbb{Q}) \cup f(\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q})$$
is a union of two at-most-countable sets, hence **at most countable**.

**Step 2: $f(\mathbb{R})$ is an interval, so it must be a single point.**

Since $f$ is continuous and $\mathbb{R}$ is connected, $f(\mathbb{R})$ is a conn
ected subset of $\mathbb{R}$, i.e., an interval. The only at-most-countable inte
rvals in $\mathbb{R}$ are singletons (every interval containing two distinct poi
nts contains uncountably many points). Hence $f(\mathbb{R}) = \{c\}$ for some co
nstant $c$, meaning $f$ is constant: $f(x) = c$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

**Step 3: A constant function cannot satisfy both inclusions.**

If $f \equiv c$, then:
- $f(\mathbb{Q}) = \{c\} \subseteq \mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}$ requires $c$ t
o be **irrational**.
- $f(\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}) = \{c\} \subseteq \mathbb{Q}$ requires $c$ t
o be **rational**.

No real number is both rational and irrational. Contradiction.

**Conclusion.** Any continuous $f$ satisfying the two inclusions must be constan
t (Step 1–2), yet no constant can satisfy both inclusions (Step 3). Therefore no
 such continuous function exists.
