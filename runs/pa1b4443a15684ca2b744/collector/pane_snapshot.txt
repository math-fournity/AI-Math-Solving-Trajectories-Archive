**Answer: Yes.**

**Construction (typewriter sequence).** For each level $k \geq 0$, partition $[0
,1]$ into $2^k$ dyadic subintervals
$$I_{k,j} = \bigl[\,j\,2^{-k},\,(j+1)\,2^{-k}\,\bigr], \qquad j = 0,1,\dots,2^{k
}-1.$$
Enumerate these intervals in order of increasing $k$, and within each level from
 left to right, assigning one interval to each $n$:
$$f_1 = \mathbf{1}_{I_{0,0}},\quad f_2 = \mathbf{1}_{I_{1,0}},\quad f_3 = \mathb
f{1}_{I_{1,1}},\quad f_4 = \mathbf{1}_{I_{2,0}},\quad \dots$$
Formally, if $n = 2^{k}+j$ with $0 \leq j < 2^{k}$ (using $k=0,j=0$ for $n=1$),
set $f_n = \mathbf{1}_{I_{k,j}}$. Each $f_n$ is a step function, hence in $L^{1}
[0,1]$.

**Integral tends to 0.** The $n$-th function is the indicator of an interval of
length $2^{-k}$, where $k = \lfloor \log_2 n \rfloor$. Thus
$$\int_0^1 |f_n| = |I_{k,j}| = 2^{-k} \xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{} 0.$$

**Limsup equals 1 everywhere.** Fix any $x \in [0,1]$. At every level $k \geq 0$
, the point $x$ belongs to at least one of the $2^{k}$ intervals $I_{k,0},\dots,
I_{k,2^{k}-1}$ (dyadic intervals cover $[0,1]$ at each level). Hence for each $k
$ there exists an index $n$ (corresponding to that level) with $f_n(x) = 1$. Sin
ce there are infinitely many levels, $f_n(x) = 1$ for infinitely many $n$, so
$$\limsup_{n\to\infty} f_n(x) \geq 1.$$
On the other hand $f_n \leq 1$ for all $n$, so $\limsup_{n\to\infty} f_n(x) \leq
 1$. Therefore
$$\limsup_{n\to\infty} f_n(x) = 1 \qquad \text{for every } x \in [0,1].$$

**Remark.** This also shows the failure of the "pointwise" dominated convergence
 theorem without a dominating $L^1$ function: $f_n \to 0$ in $L^1$-norm, yet $\l
imsup f_n = 1$ pointwise (and in fact $\liminf f_n = 0$ pointwise too, since at
each level $x$ is outside $2^{k}-1$ of the $2^{k}$ intervals, giving $f_n(x)=0$
infinitely often).
