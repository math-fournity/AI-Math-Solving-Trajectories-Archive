## Proof

Let $f(x) = \sin x - x\cos x$. The zeros of $f$ are precisely the solutions of $
\tan x = x$ (since $\sin x - x\cos x = 0 \iff \tan x = x$ when $\cos x \neq 0$,
and when $\cos x = 0$ we have $\sin x = \pm 1 \neq 0$, so no extra zeros).

**Zero at the origin.** Expanding:
$$\sin x = x - \tfrac{x^3}{6} + \tfrac{x^5}{120} - \cdots, \qquad x\cos x = x -
\tfrac{x^3}{2} + \tfrac{x^5}{24} - \cdots$$
$$f(x) = \sin x - x\cos x = \tfrac{x^3}{3} - \tfrac{x^5}{30} + O(x^7).$$
So $x=0$ is a triple zero, with leading coefficient $\tfrac{1}{3}$.

**Nonzero zeros.** Since $f$ is odd, its nonzero zeros come in pairs $\pm r_k$,
where $r_k > 0$ are the successive positive roots of $\tan x = x$. These lie in
the intervals $\bigl((k+\tfrac12)\pi,\, (k+1)\pi\bigr)$ for $k=1,2,3,\dots$ (one
 root per branch of $\tan$ where it runs from $0$ to $+\infty$), and $f$ is an e
ntire function of order $1$, so the Hadamard product converges:
$$f(x) = \frac{x^3}{3}\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(1 - \frac{x^2}{r_k^2}\right).$$

**Extracting the sum.** Expanding the product to second order:
$$f(x) = \frac{x^3}{3}\left(1 - x^2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r_k^2} + O(x^4)\r
ight) = \frac{x^3}{3} - \frac{x^5}{3}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r_k^2} + O(x^7)
.$$

Comparing the $x^5$ coefficient with the Taylor expansion $f(x) = \tfrac{x^3}{3}
 - \tfrac{x^5}{30} + O(x^7)$:
$$-\frac{1}{3}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r_k^2} = -\frac{1}{30},$$
$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r_k^2} = \frac{1}{10}.$$
