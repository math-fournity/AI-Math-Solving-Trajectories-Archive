## Evaluation of $\Gamma(-\tfrac{1}{2})$ from the integral definition

**Setup.** The integral $\Gamma(s)=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}t^{s-1}\,dt$ converges on
ly for $\operatorname{Re}s>0$. At $s=-\tfrac12$ the integrand $e^{-t}t^{-3/2}\si
m t^{-3/2}$ near $0$, so the integral diverges there. We evaluate $\Gamma(-\tfra
c12)$ as the **Hadamard finite part** of the truncated integral — which equals t
he analytic continuation (justified at the end) — and we derive every ingredient
 from the integral itself, neither citing $\Gamma(\tfrac12)=\sqrt\pi$ nor the re
cursion $\Gamma(s+1)=s\Gamma(s)$.

---

**Step 1 — Truncate and substitute.** For $\varepsilon>0$ set
$$I(\varepsilon)=\int_\varepsilon^\infty e^{-t}\,t^{-3/2}\,dt.$$
Put $t=u^2$ ($dt=2u\,du$, $t^{-3/2}=u^{-3}$) and $\delta=\sqrt\varepsilon$:
$$I(\varepsilon)=2\int_\delta^\infty \frac{e^{-u^2}}{u^2}\,du.$$

**Step 2 — Integrate by parts once.** With $f=e^{-u^2}$, $g' = u^{-2}$ (so $g=-u
^{-1}$, $f'=-2u\,e^{-u^2}$):
$$2\int_\delta^\infty \frac{e^{-u^2}}{u^2}\,du
=2\Bigl[-\frac{e^{-u^2}}{u}\Bigr]_\delta^\infty-2\int_\delta^\infty \frac{1}{u}\
,2u\,e^{-u^2}\,du
=\frac{2e^{-\delta^2}}{\delta}-4\int_\delta^\infty e^{-u^2}\,du.$$

**Step 3 — Expand as $\delta\to0$.**
$$\frac{2e^{-\delta^2}}{\delta}=\frac{2}{\delta}-2\delta+O(\delta^3),\qquad
4\int_\delta^\infty e^{-u^2}\,du=4\int_0^\infty e^{-u^2}\,du-4\delta+O(\delta^3)
.$$
Hence
$$I(\varepsilon)=\frac{2}{\delta}+2\delta-4\int_0^\infty e^{-u^2}\,du+O(\delta^3
).$$
The term $\frac{2}{\delta}=2\varepsilon^{-1/2}$ is the divergent part; $2\delta\
to0$. The **finite part** (constant term) is
$$\operatorname{FP} I(\varepsilon)=-4\int_0^\infty e^{-u^2}\,du.$$

**Step 4 — Evaluate the Gaussian integral from scratch.**
$$\left(\int_0^\infty e^{-u^2}\,du\right)^2
=\int_0^\infty\!\!\int_0^\infty e^{-(u^2+v^2)}\,du\,dv
=\int_0^{\pi/2}\!\!\int_0^\infty e^{-r^2}\,r\,dr\,d\theta
=\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac12=\frac{\pi}{4},$$
so $\displaystyle\int_0^\infty e^{-u^2}\,du=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}$.

**Step 5 — Conclude.**
$$\Gamma\!\left(-\tfrac12\right)=\operatorname{FP} I(\varepsilon)=-4\cdot\frac{\
sqrt\pi}{2}=-2\sqrt\pi.$$

---

**Why the finite part is the analytic continuation.** For $\operatorname{Re}s>0$
, $I_\varepsilon(s):=\int_\varepsilon^\infty e^{-t}t^{s-1}\,dt\to\Gamma(s)$ with
 no singular term. The divergent term $\frac{2}{\delta}=2\varepsilon^{-1/2}$ is
exactly the boundary contribution $-e^{-\varepsilon}\varepsilon^{s}/s\big|_{s=-1
/2}=2e^{-\varepsilon}\varepsilon^{-1/2}$ produced by the integration by parts; i
t vanishes when $\operatorname{Re}s>0$ and becomes singular when $\operatorname{
Re}s<0$. Removing it leaves an expression analytic on $-1<\operatorname{Re}s<0$
that agrees with $\Gamma(s)$ on $\operatorname{Re}s>0$; by the identity theorem
it is the analytic continuation. Thus the finite part equals $\Gamma(-\tfrac12)$
.

$$\boxed{\,\Gamma\!\left(-\tfrac12\right)=-2\sqrt{\pi}\,}$$
