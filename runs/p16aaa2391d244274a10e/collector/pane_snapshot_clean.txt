**Analysis of Erdős Problem 386**

The problem asks whether there exists some $k$ (with $2 \le k \le n-2$) such tha
t $\binom{n}{k}$ equals a product of consecutive primes for infinitely many $n$.
 Three variants are given: existential over $k$, universal over $k$, and the spe

Let me work through the $k=2$ case, which is the most tractable variant.

**The $k=2$ case:** $\binom{n}{2} = \frac{n(n-1)}{2}$.

For this to be a product of consecutive primes $p_a \cdot p_{a+1} \cdots p_b$, i
t must be **squarefree** (each prime appears exactly once). Since $\gcd(n, n-1)
= 1$, the consecutive primes must partition into two disjoint sets: those dividi
ng $n$ and those dividing $n-1$ (with the factor of 2 handled by the division).

Concretely, we need a partition of $\{p_a, \ldots, p_b\}$ into sets $A$ and $B$
such that:
$$\prod_{p \in A} p - \prod_{p \in B} p = \pm 1 \text{ or } \pm 2$$

(depending on parity handling).

**Why this is genuinely open:**
