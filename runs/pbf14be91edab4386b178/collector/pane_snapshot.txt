## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. The random transposition shuffle on a standard 52-card deck has
a uniform stationary distribution over all $52!$ permutations.

**Setup.** The state space is the symmetric group $S_{52}$, with $|S_{52}| = 52!
$ states (one per deck ordering). A single step of the random transposition shuf
fle works as follows: from the current permutation $\sigma$, choose two position
s $i, j \in \{1,\dots,52\}$ independently and uniformly at random (so $i = j$ is
 allowed), and compose $\sigma$ with the transposition $(i\,j)$. If $i = j$, the
 transposition is the identity and the state is unchanged.

**Step 1 — Irreducibility.** The transpositions $\{(i\,j) : i \neq j\}$ generate
 the entire symmetric group $S_{52}$. Since every transposition is reachable in
one step (pick the corresponding pair $(i,j)$), the chain can reach any permutat
ion from any other via a sequence of transpositions. Hence the chain is **irredu
cible**.

**Step 2 — Aperiodicity.** Choosing $i = j$ produces the identity transposition,
 so every state has a self-loop: $P(\sigma \to \sigma) \geq 1/52 > 0$. A self-lo
op at every state makes every state aperiodic (period 1). Hence the chain is **a
periodic**.

**Step 3 — Symmetry of transitions (doubly stochastic).** For any two states $\s
igma, \tau \in S_{52}$, the one-step transition probability is
$$P(\sigma \to \tau) = \frac{\#\{(i,j) : (i\,j)\,\sigma = \tau\}}{52^2}.$$
Since transpositions are their own inverses, $(i\,j)\,\sigma = \tau \iff \sigma
= (i\,j)\,\tau \iff (i\,j)\,\tau = \sigma$. Therefore the set $\{(i,j) : (i\,j)\
sigma = \tau\}$ is in bijection with $\{(i,j) : (i\,j)\tau = \sigma\}$, giving
$$P(\sigma \to \tau) = P(\tau \to \sigma) \quad \text{for all } \sigma, \tau.$$
The transition matrix is **symmetric**, hence doubly stochastic (every column al
so sums to 1).

**Step 4 — Uniform stationary distribution.** A doubly stochastic transition mat
rix on a finite state space of size $N$ has the uniform distribution $\pi = (1/N
, \dots, 1/N)$ as a stationary distribution, since
$$\sum_{\sigma} \pi(\sigma)\, P(\sigma \to \tau) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{\sigma} P(\s
igma \to \tau) = \frac{1}{N}\cdot 1 = \frac{1}{N} = \pi(\tau).$$
Here $N = 52!$, so $\pi(\sigma) = 1/52!$ for every permutation $\sigma$.

**Step 5 — Uniqueness.** By Steps 1 and 2, the chain is irreducible and aperiodi
c. For a finite, irreducible, aperiodic Markov chain, the stationary distributio
n is **unique**. Therefore the uniform distribution is the unique stationary dis
tribution, and the chain converges to it from every initial state.

**Conclusion.** The random transposition shuffle on a 52-card deck is an irreduc
ible, aperiodic, symmetric (doubly stochastic) Markov chain on $S_{52}$. Its uni
que stationary distribution is the uniform distribution over all $52!$ permutati
ons.
