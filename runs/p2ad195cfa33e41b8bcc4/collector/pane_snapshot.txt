Looking at the problem: I need to determine whether 1990 pairwise coprime positi
ve integers exist such that every sum of two or more of them is composite.

**Answer: Yes.** Here is the proof.

---

## Proof

We prove the result for any $n \geq 2$ (in particular $n = 1990$).

### Setup

Let $\mathcal{S} = \{S \subseteq \{1,\dots,n\} : |S| \geq 2\}$, a finite collect
ion of $2^n - n - 1$ subsets. Choose distinct primes:
- $p_1, p_2, \dots, p_n$ (one per index), and
- for each $S \in \mathcal{S}$, a prime $p_S > n$, all distinct from each other
and from every $p_i$.

This is possible since we need only finitely many primes and there are infinitel
y many. Let
$$M = \Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^n p_i\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{S \in \mathcal{S}} p_S\Bigr).$$

### Defining residues via CRT

For each $i \in \{1,\dots,n\}$, we specify $r_i$ modulo every prime factor of $M
$:

1. **Coprimality constraints:** $r_i \equiv 0 \pmod{p_i}$ and $r_i \equiv 1 \pmo
d{p_k}$ for all $k \neq i$.

2. **Subset-sum constraints:** For each $S \in \mathcal{S}$, designate $m(S) = \
min(S)$. Set
$$r_i \equiv -(|S|-1) \pmod{p_S} \quad\text{if } i = m(S),$$
$$r_i \equiv 1 \pmod{p_S} \quad\text{if } i \in S \setminus \{m(S)\} \text{ or }
 i \notin S.$$

Since all moduli are **distinct primes**, the Chinese Remainder Theorem guarante
es a unique solution $r_i \in \{0, 1, \dots, M-1\}$.

### Key properties of $r_i$

- $p_i \mid r_i$ (from constraint 1).
- $p_k \nmid r_i$ for $k \neq i$ (since $r_i \equiv 1 \pmod{p_k}$).
- $p_S \nmid r_i$ for every $S$: we have $r_i \equiv 1$ or $r_i \equiv -(|S|-1)
\pmod{p_S}$. Since $p_S > n \geq |S| > |S|-1 \geq 1$, neither $1$ nor $|S|-1$ is
 divisible by $p_S$.

Therefore the **only** prime factor of $M$ dividing $r_i$ is $p_i$, so $\gcd(r_i
, M) = p_i$. Also $r_i > 0$ (since $r_i \equiv 1 \pmod{p_k}$ for $k \neq i$).

### Constructing $a_i$ sequentially

Set $a_i = r_i + M\,t_i$ for non-negative integers $t_i$, chosen sequentially fo
r $i = 1, 2, \dots, n$. At step $i$, the values $a_1, \dots, a_{i-1}$ are alread
y fixed. We need:

**(a)** $\gcd(a_i, a_j) = 1$ for all $j < i$, and **(b)** $a_i > \max_{S \in \ma
thcal{S}} p_S$.

**No prime factor of $M$ divides both $a_i$ and $a_j$ ($j < i$):**
- $p_i \mid a_i$ but $p_i \nmid a_j$ (since $a_j \equiv r_j \equiv 1 \pmod{p_i}$
).
- For $k \neq i$: $p_k \nmid a_i$ (since $a_i \equiv r_i \equiv 1 \pmod{p_k}$).
- For any $S$: $p_S \nmid a_i$ and $p_S \nmid a_j$ (as established above).

So only primes $q \nmid M$ could divide both. The set of such primes dividing $a
_1 \cdots a_{i-1}$ is **finite**. For each such $q$, the condition $q \mid a_i$
is equivalent to $t_i \equiv -r_i \cdot M^{-1} \pmod{q}$ (valid since $\gcd(M, q
) = 1$), excluding **one residue class** of $t_i$ modulo $q$.

By CRT, we can find $t_i$ avoiding all these finitely many excluded classes whil
e also being large enough for condition (b). Such $t_i$ exists because infinitel
y many integers satisfy any finite set of admissible residue conditions.

### Verification

**Pairwise coprime:** By the sequential construction, $\gcd(a_i, a_j) = 1$ for a
ll $i \neq j$. Moreover, the $a_i$ are distinct (each $a_i$ is divisible by $p_i
$ but no $a_j$ with $j \neq i$ is) and $a_i \geq p_i \geq 2$.

**All sums of $\geq 2$ elements are composite:** For any $S \in \mathcal{S}$ (so
 $|S| \geq 2$):
$$\sum_{i \in S} a_i = \sum_{i \in S}(r_i + Mt_i) \equiv \sum_{i \in S} r_i \pmo
d{p_S},$$
since $p_S \mid M$. Now:
$$\sum_{i \in S} r_i \equiv \underbrace{-(|S|-1)}_{i = m(S)} + \underbrace{1 + \
cdots + 1}_{|S|-1 \text{ terms}} = 0 \pmod{p_S}.$$

So $p_S \mid \sum_{i \in S} a_i$. Since every $a_i > \max_{S} p_S$ and $|S| \geq
 2$:
$$\sum_{i \in S} a_i > 2\max_S p_S > p_S.$$

Thus $\sum_{i \in S} a_i$ is a positive multiple of $p_S$ strictly exceeding $p_
S$, hence **composite**.

### Conclusion

The $n = 1990$ integers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_{1990}$ are pairwise coprime, and ev
ery sum of two or more of them is composite. Therefore, such a collection exists
. $\blacksquare$
