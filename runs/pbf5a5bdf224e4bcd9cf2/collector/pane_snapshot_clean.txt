## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. A commutative ring with unity whose spectrum is $\{(0)\}$ must b
e a field.

**Setup.** Let $R$ be a commutative ring with unity such that $\operatorname{Spe
c}(R) = \{(0)\}$, i.e., the only prime ideal of $R$ is the zero ideal.

**Step 1: $(0)$ is prime, so $R$ is an integral domain.**

Since $(0) \in \operatorname{Spec}(R)$, the ideal $(0)$ is prime. By definition,
 this means $R/(0) \cong R$ is an integral domain, and in particular $R \neq 0$
(the zero ring is excluded since an integral domain requires $1 \neq 0$).

**Step 2: $(0)$ is the unique maximal ideal.**

Every maximal ideal is prime. Since the only prime ideal is $(0)$, any maximal i
deal of $R$ must equal $(0)$. It remains to confirm a maximal ideal exists. Sinc
e $R$ is a nonzero commutative ring with unity, Zorn's lemma guarantees that eve
ry proper ideal is contained in a maximal ideal; in particular, maximal ideals e
xist. The only candidate is $(0)$, so $(0)$ is the unique maximal ideal of $R$.

**Step 3: $R$ is a field.**

Since $(0)$ is maximal, the quotient $R/(0) \cong R$ is a field.

**Conclusion.** The conditions force $R$ to be an integral domain in which $(0)$
 is maximal, hence $R$ is a field. Conversely, every field has $\operatorname{Sp
ec}(R) = \{(0)\}$, so the condition characterizes fields exactly.
