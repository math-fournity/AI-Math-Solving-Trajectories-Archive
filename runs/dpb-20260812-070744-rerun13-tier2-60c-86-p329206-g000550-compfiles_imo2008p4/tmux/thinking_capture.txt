IMO 2008 Problem 4 — Solution

Problem. Find all $f:\mathbb{R}{>0}\to\mathbb{R}{>0}$ such that $$
\frac{f(w)^2+f(x)^2}{f(y)^2+f(z)^2}=\frac{w^2+x^2}
{y^2+z^2}\qquad\text{whenever }wx=yz.$$

Answer. $;f(t)=\sqrt{\alpha t^2+\beta/t^2};$ for constants $\alpha,\beta\ge 0$
with $\alpha+\beta>0$.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Reduction to a functional equation for $g=f^2$

Set $g(t)=f(t)^2>0$. The condition reads $$\frac{g(w)+g(x)}{g(y)+g(z)}
=\frac{w^2+x^2}{y^2+z^2}\qquad(wx=yz).$$

Fix $P>0$. For any two pairs $(w,x),(y,z)$ with $wx=yz=P$, cross‑multiplying
gives $$\frac{g(w)+g(x)}{w^2+x^2}=\frac{g(y)+g(z)}{y^2+z^2}.$$ So this ratio
depends only on the product $P=wx$; call it $h(P)$. Hence $$g(w)+g(x)=h(wx)
(w^2+x^2)\qquad\forall,w,x>0.\tag{1}$$

Set $x=1$ and let $A=g(1)>0$: $g(w)+A=h(w)(w^2+1)$, so $h(w)=\dfrac{g(w)+A}
{w^2+1}$. Substituting back into (1), $
$(g(w)+g(x))\bigl((wx)^2+1\bigr)=(g(wx)+A)(w^2+x^2).\tag{E}$$

Isolating the "additive" part

Define $\varphi(t)=g(t)-At^2$. Taking $x=1/w$ in (E) (so $wx=1$): $$g(w)+g(1/
w)=A!\left(w^2+\tfrac1{w^2}\right),$$ i.e. $\varphi(w)+\varphi(1/w)=0$
(antisymmetry; in particular $\varphi(1)=0$). Substituting $g=At^2+\varphi$ into
(E), the $A$‑terms cancel and leave $$(\varphi(w)+\varphi(x))
(w^2x^2+1)=\varphi(wx)(w^2+x^2).\tag{$\Phi$}$$

Logarithmic form

Write $w=e^a,\ x=e^b,\ \psi(a)=\varphi(e^a)$. Then $\psi$ is odd ($\psi(-a)=-
\psi(a)$, $\psi(0)=0$), and using $$w^2x^2+1=2e^{a+b}\cosh(a+b),\qquad
w^2+x^2=2e^{a+b}\cosh(a-b),$$ equation $(\Phi)$ becomes $$(\psi(a)+
\psi(b))\cosh(a+b)=\psi(a+b)\cosh(a-b).\tag{$\Psi$}$$

For $a\ne 0$ set $\psi(a)=\sinh(2a),\chi(a)$. Since $\psi$ is odd and $
\sinh(2a)$ is odd, $\chi$ is even. Using $$\sinh 2a+\sinh 2b=2\sinh(a+b)\cosh(a-
b),\quad \sinh(2(a+b))=2\sinh(a+b)\cosh(a+b),$$ equation $(\Psi)$ reduces
(divide by $\cosh(a+b)$) to $$\sinh(2a),\chi(a)+\sinh(2b),\chi(b)=(\sinh 2a+
\sinh 2b),\chi(a+b).\tag{$\star$}$$

$\chi$ is constant

Take any $s,t>0$ and put $S=\sinh 2s,\ T=\sinh 2t,\ U=\sinh(2(s+t))$.

  • From $(\star)$ with $(a,b)=(s,t)$: $;\chi(s+t)=\dfrac{S,\chi(s)+T,\chi(t)}
  {S+T}.$
  • From $(\star)$ with $(a,b)=(s+t,-t)$ (using $\chi(-t)=\chi(t)$ and $
  \sinh(-2t)=-T$): $;\chi(s)=\dfrac{U,\chi(s+t)-T,\chi(t)}{U-T}.$

Substituting the first into the second and simplifying: $$\chi(s)\cdot T,(U-S-
T)=T,(U-S-T),\chi(t).$$

Now $T=\sinh 2t>0$, and $$U-S-T=\sinh 2s(\cosh 2t-1)+\sinh 2t(\cosh
2s-1)>0\qquad(s,t>0),$$ since every factor is positive. Therefore $
\chi(s)=\chi(t)$ for all $s,t>0$. By evenness $\chi$ is constant on $
\mathbb{R}\setminus{0}$; call the constant $c$.

Determining $g$ and $f$

Thus $\psi(a)=c\sinh(2a)$, i.e. $$\varphi(t)=c\sinh(2\ln t)=\tfrac{c}{2}(t^2-
t^{-2}).$$ Renaming the constant, $\varphi(t)=c,(t^2-t^{-2})$, and $
$g(t)=At^2+c(t^2-t^{-2})=(A+c)t^2-c,t^{-2}.$$ Set $\alpha=A+c$ and $\beta=-c$;
then $\alpha+\beta=A=g(1)>0$ and $$g(t)=\alpha t^2+\frac{\beta}{t^2}.$$

Positivity forces $\alpha,\beta\ge 0$: if $\alpha<0$ then $g(t)\to-\infty$ as
$t\to\infty$; if $\beta<0$ then $g(t)\to-\infty$ as $t\to0^+$. Conversely, $
\alpha,\beta\ge0$ with $\alpha+\beta>0$ gives $g(t)>0$ for every $t>0$. So $
$f(t)=\sqrt{\alpha t^2+\beta/t^2},\qquad \alpha,\beta\ge0,\ \alpha+\beta>0.$$

Verification

For $f(t)=\sqrt{\alpha t^2+\beta/t^2}$, $$f(w)^2+f(x)^2=\alpha(w^2+x^2)+\beta!
\left(\tfrac1{w^2}+\tfrac1{x^2}\right)=(w^2+x^2)!\left(\alpha+\frac{\beta}
{(wx)^2}\right),$$ and similarly $f(y)^2+f(z)^2=(y^2+z^2)\bigl(\alpha+\beta/
(yz)^2\bigr)$. Since $wx=yz$, $$\frac{f(w)^2+f(x)^2}{f(y)^2+f(z)^2}
=\frac{w^2+x^2}{y^2+z^2}.\quad\checkmark$$

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Conclusion. The solutions are exactly $$\boxed{,f(x)=\sqrt{\alpha x^2+
\dfrac{\beta}{x^2}};,\qquad \alpha,\beta\ge 0,\ \alpha+\beta>0,}$$ (including
the two pure families $f(x)=\sqrt{\alpha},x$ when $\beta=0$ and
$f(x)=\sqrt{\beta}/x$ when $\alpha=0$).
