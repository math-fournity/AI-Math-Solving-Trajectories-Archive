## Proof

**Question (literal reading).** Can the circle $S^1$ be retracted onto the plane
 $\\mathbb{R}^2$? That is, does there exist a retraction $r:S^1\to\mathbb{R}^2$?

**Answer.** No.

---

**Definition.** A *retraction* of a topological space $X$ onto a subspace $A$ is
 a continuous map $r:X\to A$ such that $r|_A=\mathrm{id}_A$. In particular, the
definition **requires** $A\subseteq X$ (as a subspace). Without this inclusion t
he very phrase "retraction of $X$ onto $A$" has no meaning.

So for "$S^1$ to be retracted onto $\mathbb{R}^2$" to be a well‑posed question,
we would need $\mathbb{R}^2\subseteq S^1$ as a subspace. We show this is impossi
ble.

---

**Key obstruction: dimension.** Both $S^1$ and $\mathbb{R}^2$ are separable metr
ic spaces, so the covering (Lebesgue) dimension $\dim$ is well defined and, for
separable metric spaces, is **monotone under subspaces**:
$$A\subseteq X \;\Longrightarrow\; \dim A\le \dim X.$$
This is the standard subspace theorem for covering dimension in separable metriz
able spaces (see e.g. Hurewicz–Wallman, *Dimension Theory*).

Now:
- $S^1$ is a compact $1$‑manifold, so $\dim S^1=1$.
- $\mathbb{R}^2$ is a $2$‑manifold, so $\dim\mathbb{R}^2=2$.

If $\mathbb{R}^2\subseteq S^1$ were possible, monotonicity would give
$$2=\dim\mathbb{R}^2\le\dim S^1=1,$$
a contradiction. Hence $\mathbb{R}^2$ **cannot be a subspace of $S^1$**.

---

**Alternative obstruction: invariance of domain.** Suppose, for contradiction, t
hat $\mathbb{R}^2$ embedded as a subspace of $S^1$. Pick $p\in\mathbb{R}^2$ and
a small open disk $D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^2$ around $p$; $D$ is homeomorphic to $\
mathbb{R}^2$. Since $S^1$ is a $1$‑manifold, $p$ lies in some chart $U\cong\math
bb{R}$ of $S^1$, and after shrinking $D$ we may assume $D\subseteq U\cong\mathbb
{R}$. Thus $\mathbb{R}^2$ (an open subset of itself) embeds into $\mathbb{R}$. B
y **invariance of domain**, an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$ maps homeomorphical
ly onto an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$ only when the dimensions agree; in part
icular $\mathbb{R}^2$ cannot embed into $\mathbb{R}$. Contradiction.

---

**Conclusion.** Since a retraction $r:S^1\to\mathbb{R}^2$ requires $\mathbb{R}^2
\subseteq S^1$, and $\mathbb{R}^2$ cannot be a subspace of $S^1$ (their covering
 dimensions are $2$ and $1$ respectively, and dimension is monotone on subspaces
 of separable metric spaces), **no such retraction exists**. The circle $S^1$ ca
nnot be retracted onto the plane $\mathbb{R}^2$.
