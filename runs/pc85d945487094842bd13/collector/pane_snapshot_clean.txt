**Why $1006$ is the right number (Hall's theorem).** By Hall's theorem, $K_{n,n}
\setminus E$ has no perfect matching iff there exist $S\subseteq L$, $|S|=s$, wi
th $|N_{K\setminus E}(S)|<s$. A right vertex $r$ is *absent* from $N_{K\setminus
 E}(S)$ exactly when every edge from $S$ to $r$ lies in $E$, i.e. $S\times\{r\}\

$$\frac{s(n-s+1)}{\min(s,n-s+1)}=\max(s,\,n-s+1)$$
matchings. Minimizing over $s$ gives $s=\lceil(n+1)/2\rceil$, i.e. $\max(s,n-s+1
