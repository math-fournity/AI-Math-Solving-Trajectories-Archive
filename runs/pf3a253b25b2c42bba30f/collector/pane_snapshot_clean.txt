## Solution

**Key observation — quadratic residue constraint.**

Since $n = p_1 p_ p_3$ and $n - 1 = k^2$, we have $k^2 \equiv -1 \pmod{p_i}$ for
 each $i \in \{1,2,3\}$. The congruence $x^2 \equiv -1 \pmod{p}$ is solvable if
and only if $p = 2$ or $p \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$.

Since $p_1$ is a two-digit prime ($p_1 \geq 11$), $p_1$ is odd, so $p_1 \equiv 1
 \pmod{4}$ is required. Likewise $p_2 > p_1 \geq 13$ and $p_3 > p_2$, so both ar
e odd primes $> 2$, requiring $p_2 \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$ and $p_3 \equiv 1 \pmod{4}
$.

**All three primes must be $\equiv 1 \pmod{4}$.**

**Finding the smallest $p_1$.** The two-digit primes $\equiv 1 \pmod{4}$ are $13
, 17, 29, 37, \ldots$ The smallest is $p_1 = 13$, which minimizes the product.

**Searching with $p_1 = 13$.** We need primes $p_2, p_3 \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$ with
$13 < p_2 < p_3$, and $13 \cdot p_2 \cdot p_3 - 1$ a perfect square.

Primes $\equiv 1 \pmod{4}$ greater than 13: $17, 29, 37, 41, 53, 61, 73, 89, 97,
