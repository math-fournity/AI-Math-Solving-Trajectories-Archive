## Analysis

Let $g = f + \epsilon \rho Q$. We must determine whether, for sufficiently small
 $\epsilon$, (a) $g$ remains Morse, and (b) $g$ has the **same** critical points
 as $f$. These are two separate claims, and they have different answers.

### Part (a): $g$ remains Morse for small $\epsilon$ — TRUE

Since $f$ is Morse on the compact manifold $X$, its critical set $\operatorname{
Crit}(f) = \{p_1, \dots, p_N\}$ is finite, and each $p_i$ is non-degenerate: $\d
et D^2 f(p_i) \neq 0$.

**Step 1 — Persistence of critical points.** At each $p_i$, the Hessian $D^2 f(p
_i)$ is invertible. By the implicit function theorem applied to $F(x,\epsilon) =
 \nabla f(x) + \epsilon \nabla(\rho Q)(x) = 0$ near $(p_i, 0)$, there exist $\ep
silon_i > 0$, a neighborhood $U_i$ of $p_i$, and a smooth map $p_i(\epsilon)$ wi
th $p_i(0) = p_i$ such that for $|\epsilon| < \epsilon_i$, the unique critical p
oint of $g$ in $U_i$ is $p_i(\epsilon)$. Moreover $D^2 g(p_i(\epsilon)) \to D^2
f(p_i)$ as $\epsilon \to 0$, so by continuity of the determinant and $\det D^2 f
(p_i) \neq 0$, each $p_i(\epsilon)$ is non-degenerate for $|\epsilon|$ small eno
ugh.

**Step 2 — No spurious critical points elsewhere.** Let $K = X \setminus \bigcup
_i U_i$ (compact, since $X$ is compact). On $K$, $\nabla f \neq 0$, and by compa
ctness $\min_K \|\nabla f\| = m > 0$. Since $\nabla(\rho Q)$ is bounded on $X$,
say $\|\nabla(\rho Q)\| \leq M$, we get $\|\nabla g\| \geq m - |\epsilon| M > 0$
 whenever $|\epsilon| < m/M$. Hence $g$ has no critical points on $K$.

Taking $|\epsilon| < \min\bigl(\min_i \epsilon_i,\, m/M\bigr)$, the critical set
 of $g$ is exactly $\{p_1(\epsilon), \dots, p_N(\epsilon)\}$, all non-degenerate
. So **$g$ is Morse**.

### Part (b): $g$ has the *same* critical points as $f$ — FALSE in general

"Same critical points" means $\operatorname{Crit}(g) = \operatorname{Crit}(f)$ a
s sets, i.e. each $p_i(\epsilon) = p_i$. This requires $\nabla g(p_i) = 0$, i.e.
$$\nabla f(p_i) + \epsilon\, \nabla(\rho Q)(p_i) = \epsilon\, \nabla Q(p_i) = 0,
$$
since $\rho \equiv 1$ near $x_0$ (so $\nabla(\rho Q)(p_i) = \nabla Q(p_i)$ for $
p_i$ in that neighborhood, in particular for $p_i = x_0$). Thus $x_0$ remains a
critical point of $g$ **only if** $\nabla Q(x_0) = 0$.

For a generic quadratic $Q$, $\nabla Q(x_0) \neq 0$, so $x_0$ is **not** a criti
cal point of $g$. Instead, by the implicit function theorem, the critical point
moves to
$$x_0(\epsilon) = x_0 - \epsilon\, \bigl(D^2 f(x_0)\bigr)^{-1} \nabla Q(x_0) + O
(\epsilon^2),$$
which is a different point whenever $\nabla Q(x_0) \neq 0$.

**Explicit counterexample.** Take $X = S^1$ (compact), $f(\theta) = \cos\theta$
(Morse, with saddle-type critical point at $\theta = \pi$; on $S^1$ a "saddle" i
s a non-degenerate critical point of index between $0$ and $\dim$). Let $x_0 = \
pi$, $\rho \equiv 1$ near $\pi$, and $Q(\theta) = \theta$ (linear, hence a quadr
atic polynomial of degree $\leq 2$) in a local chart. Then
$$g(\theta) = \cos\theta + \epsilon\,\theta \quad\text{near } \pi,$$
$$g'(\theta) = -\sin\theta + \epsilon.$$
At $\theta = \pi$: $g'(\pi) = -\sin\pi + \epsilon = \epsilon \neq 0$ for $\epsil
on \neq 0$. So $x_0 = \pi$ is **no longer critical** for $g$. The critical point
 shifts to $\theta = \pi - \epsilon + O(\epsilon^3)$. The perturbation is still
Morse for small $\epsilon$ (the shifted critical point remains non-degenerate si
nce $g'' = -\cos\theta$ and $g''(\pi-\epsilon) \to 1 \neq 0$), but the critical
point has **moved**.

### Conclusion

The combined statement "$g$ remains Morse **with the same critical points** as $
f$" is **false** in general. The correct statement is:

> For sufficiently small $\epsilon$, the perturbed function $g = f + \epsilon\rh
o Q$ remains a Morse function, and its critical points are in bijection with tho
se of $f$ (same number, same indices, each persisting nearby). However, the crit
ical points generally **move** — they coincide with those of $f$ only if $\nabla
 Q(p_i) = 0$ at every critical point $p_i$ lying in the region where $\rho \equi
v 1$ (and $\nabla(\rho Q)(p_i)=0$ at critical points in the transition region of
 $\rho$). For a generic quadratic $Q$ with $\nabla Q(x_0) \neq 0$, the saddle po
int $x_0$ shifts to $x_0 - \epsilon (D^2 f(x_0))^{-1}\nabla Q(x_0) + O(\epsilon^
2) \neq x_0$.

The Morse property is structurally stable (open condition on non-degenerate crit
ical points + compactness argument excluding spurious critical points), but **po
sitional coincidence of critical points is not stable** — it is destroyed by any
 perturbation whose gradient at the original critical point is nonzero.
