Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether there exist subsets X (open
, not closed) and Y (bounded, not open) of Euclidean space that are homeomorphic
.

**Answer: No, such subsets do not exist.**

**Proof.**

Suppose for contradiction that there exist subsets $X, Y \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ (
for some $n$) such that:
- $X$ is open (and not closed) in $\mathbb{R}^n$,
- $Y$ is bounded (and not open) in $\mathbb{R}^n$,
- $X$ and $Y$ are homeomorphic.

Let $h: X \to Y$ be a homeomorphism (where $X$ and $Y$ carry the subspace topolo
gies inherited from $\mathbb{R}^n$).

**Key step: Apply Invariance of Domain.**

Consider the map $h$ composed with the inclusion $\iota: Y \hookrightarrow \math
bb{R}^n$, giving a map
$$f = \iota \circ h : X \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}^n.$$

I verify the hypotheses of the **Invariance of Domain Theorem** (Brouwer, 1911):

> **Theorem (Invariance of Domain).** If $U \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is open and $f
: U \to \mathbb{R}^n$ is continuous and injective, then $f(U)$ is open in $\math
bb{R}^n$.

- **$X$ is open in $\mathbb{R}^n$**: by assumption.
- **$f$ is continuous**: $h: X \to Y$ is continuous (homeomorphism), and $\iota:
 Y \to \mathbb{R}^n$ is continuous (inclusion of a subspace). The composition $f
 = \iota \circ h$ is continuous.
- **$f$ is injective**: $h$ is a bijection, hence injective, and $\iota$ is inje
ctive. So $f$ is injective.

All hypotheses are satisfied. By Invariance of Domain, $f(X)$ is open in $\mathb
b{R}^n$.

But $f(X) = \iota(h(X)) = \iota(Y) = Y$ (as a subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$).

**Contradiction:** $Y$ is open in $\mathbb{R}^n$, which contradicts the assumpti
on that $Y$ is **not open**.

Therefore, no such pair $(X, Y)$ exists. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The contradiction arises purely from the combination "$X$ is open in
 $\mathbb{R}^n$" + "$X \cong Y$" + "$Y \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ (same dimension)" —
 Invariance of Domain forces $Y$ to be open. The conditions "not closed" on $X$
and "bounded" on $Y$ are irrelevant to the impossibility; the openness of $X$ al
one, combined with the homeomorphism into the same $\mathbb{R}^n$, already force
s $Y$ to be open. The deep topological content is that dimension and openness ar
e preserved under embeddings into the same Euclidean space — a hallmark of Brouw
er's theorem.
