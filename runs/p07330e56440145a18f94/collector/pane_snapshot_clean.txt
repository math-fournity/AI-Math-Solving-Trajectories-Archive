## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. On a simply connected open domain $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ wit

thonormal frame $\{E_1, E_2\}$ whose dual coframe $\{E^1, E^2\}$ consists of clo

---

### Step 1: A global orthonormal coframe exists

Since $U$ is an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^2$, the tangent bundle $TU$ is trivia
l (it is the restriction of the trivial bundle $T\mathbb{R}^2$). Choose any smoo
th global frame $\{X_1, X_2\}$ and apply the Gram–Schmidt process with respect t
o $g$ to obtain a smooth global orthonormal frame $\{F_1, F_2\}$. Let $\{\omega^
1, \omega^2\}$ be the dual orthonormal coframe, so $g = (\omega^1)^2 + (\omega^2
)^2$.

### Step 2: Cartan structure equations and the connection form

Define the Levi-Civita connection 1-form $\omega_{12}$ (with $\omega_{21} = -\om
ega_{12}$) via the first structure equations:
$$d\omega^1 = -\omega_{12} \wedge \omega^2, \qquad d\omega^2 = \omega_{12} \wedg
e \omega^1.$$

The curvature 2-form is $\Omega_{12} = d\omega_{12} = -K\, \omega^1 \wedge \omeg
a^2$, where $K$ is the Gaussian curvature. By hypothesis $K = 0$, so:
$$d\omega_{12} = 0.$$

### Step 3: Trivialize the connection via Poincaré's lemma

Since $U$ is simply connected and $\omega_{12}$ is closed, by the Poincaré lemma
 there exists a smooth function $\theta: U \to \mathbb{R}$ such that:
$$\omega_{12} = d\theta.$$

### Step 4: Rotate the coframe to kill the connection

Define a new orthonormal coframe by rotating $\{\omega^1, \omega^2\}$ through an
gle $\theta$:
$$E^1 = \cos\theta\, \omega^1 + \sin\theta\, \omega^2,$$
$$E^2 = -\sin\theta\, \omega^1 + \cos\theta\, \omega^2.$$

This is a pointwise rotation, so $\{E^1, E^2\}$ remains orthonormal: $g = (E^1)^
2 + (E^2)^2$.

**Compute $dE^1$:**
$$dE^1 = d(\cos\theta) \wedge \omega^1 + \cos\theta\, d\omega^1 + d(\sin\theta)
\wedge \omega^2 + \sin\theta\, d\omega^2.$$

Substituting $d(\cos\theta) = -\sin\theta\, d\theta$, $d(\sin\theta) = \cos\thet
a\, d\theta$, and the structure equations:
$$dE^1 = -\sin\theta\, d\theta \wedge \omega^1 - \cos\theta\, \omega_{12} \wedge
 \omega^2 + \cos\theta\, d\theta \wedge \omega^2 + \sin\theta\, \omega_{12} \wed
ge \omega^1.$$

Since $\omega_{12} = d\theta$:
$$dE^1 = -\sin\theta\, d\theta \wedge \omega^1 + \sin\theta\, d\theta \wedge \om
ega^1 + \cos\theta\, d\theta \wedge \omega^2 - \cos\theta\, d\theta \wedge \omeg
a^2 = 0.$$

**Compute $dE^2$:**
$$dE^2 = -d(\sin\theta)\wedge\omega^1 - \sin\theta\, d\omega^1 + d(\cos\theta)\w
edge\omega^2 + \cos\theta\, d\omega^2$$
$$= -\cos\theta\, d\theta \wedge \omega^1 + \sin\theta\, \omega_{12}\wedge\omega
^2 - \sin\theta\, d\theta \wedge \omega^2 + \cos\theta\, \omega_{12}\wedge\omega
^1.$$

Again using $\omega_{12} = d\theta$:
$$dE^2 = -\cos\theta\, d\theta \wedge \omega^1 + \cos\theta\, d\theta \wedge \om
ega^1 + \sin\theta\, d\theta \wedge \omega^2 - \sin\theta\, d\theta \wedge \omeg
a^2 = 0.$$

### Step 5: Conclusion

We have constructed a smooth orthonormal coframe $\{E^1, E^2\}$ with $dE^1 = 0$
and $dE^2 = 0$, i.e., both forms are **closed**. Let $\{E_1, E_2\}$ be the dual
vector fields (the frame dual to the coframe). Then $\{E_1, E_2\}$ is the desire
d smooth orthonormal frame.

Moreover, since $U$ is simply connected, each closed $E^i$ is exact: $E^i = df^i
$ for some smooth $f^i: U \to \mathbb{R}$, and the map $F = (f^1, f^2): U \to \m
athbb{R}^2$ is a local isometry to Euclidean space — consistent with the classic
al fact that a flat simply connected Riemannian surface is globally isometric to
 a domain in $(\mathbb{R}^2, g_{\text{Euc}})$.

**Therefore, the answer is yes: such a smooth orthonormal frame with closed dual
 coframe always exists.**
