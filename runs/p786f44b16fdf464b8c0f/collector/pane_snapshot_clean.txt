## Proof

**Coordinate setup.** Place the circumcenter $O$ at the origin with circumradius
 $R$. By a rotation, let $AB$ be the vertical chord $x = R\cos\theta$, with
$$A = (R\cos\theta,\; R\sin\theta), \qquad B = (R\cos\theta,\; -R\sin\theta), \q
quad 0 < \theta < \tfrac{\pi}{2}.$$
The minor arc $AB$ is parametrized by $X = (R\cos t,\, R\sin t)$ with $t \in (-\
theta, \theta)$. Since $\triangle ABC$ is acute, $C = (R\cos\varphi,\, R\sin\var
phi)$ lies on the major arc with $\theta < \varphi < 2\pi - \theta$, and acutene
ss gives:
- $\angle A < 90° \Rightarrow \varphi + \theta > \pi$,
- $\angle B < 90° \Rightarrow \varphi < \pi + \theta$,
- $\angle C < 90° \Rightarrow \theta < \pi/2$.

Let $D = CX \cap AB = (R\cos\theta,\, y_D)$.

**Circumcenters $O_1, O_2$.** Since $A, D, B$ are collinear on the vertical line
 $x = R\cos\theta$, the perpendicular bisectors of $AD$ and $BD$ are horizontal.
 Setting $y_A = R\sin\theta$, $y_B = -R\sin\theta$, $x_{AB} = R\cos\theta$:
$$O_1 = \Big(x_1,\; \tfrac{y_A + y_D}{2}\Big), \qquad O_2 = \Big(x_2,\; \tfrac{y
_B + y_D}{2}\Big).$$

The condition $|O_1 D| = |O_1 X|$ (equidistance from $D$ and $X$) gives, after e
xpanding:
$$(x_X - x_{AB})(2x_1 - x_{AB} - x_X) = (y_D - y_X)(y_A - y_X),$$
so
$$x_1 = \frac{x_{AB}+x_X}{2} + \frac{(y_D - y_X)(y_A - y_X)}{2(x_X - x_{AB})}.$$
Similarly (replacing $y_A$ by $y_B$):
$$x_2 = \frac{x_{AB}+x_X}{2} + \frac{(y_D - y_X)(y_B - y_X)}{2(x_X - x_{AB})}.$$

**Area of $\triangle OO_1O_2$.** With $O = (0,0)$ and $y_1 = \frac{y_A+y_D}{2}$,
 $y_2 = \frac{y_B+y_D}{2}$:
$$\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2}|x_1 y_2 - x_2 y_1|.$$

A direct computation (using $y_A + y_B = 0$) yields:
$$x_1 y_2 - x_2 y_1 = \frac{y_A - y_B}{4}\cdot\frac{y_D^2 + x_{AB}^2 - R^2}{x_X
- x_{AB}}.$$

Since $A$ lies on $\omega$: $x_{AB}^2 = R^2 - y_A^2$, so $y_D^2 + x_{AB}^2 - R^2
 = y_D^2 - y_A^2$. Therefore:
$$\text{Area} = \frac{|y_A - y_B|}{8}\cdot\frac{|y_D^2 - y_A^2|}{|x_X - x_{AB}|}
 = \frac{R\sin\theta}{4}\cdot\frac{|R^2\sin^2\theta - y_D^2|}{R\,|\cos t - \cos\
theta|} = \frac{\sin\theta}{4}\cdot\frac{|R^2\sin^2\theta - y_D^2|}{|\cos t - \c
os\theta|}.$$

**Expressing $y_D$ via the chord equation.** The chord through $C(\varphi)$ and
$X(t)$ has equation
$$x\cos\tfrac{\varphi+t}{2} + y\sin\tfrac{\varphi+t}{2} = R\cos\tfrac{\varphi-t}
{2}.$$
Setting $u = \frac{\varphi+t}{2}$, $v = \frac{\varphi-t}{2}$, and $x = R\cos\the
ta$:
$$y_D = \frac{R(\cos v - \cos\theta\cos u)}{\sin u}.$$

**Key identity.** We compute:
$$R^2\sin^2\theta - y_D^2 = \frac{R^2}{\sin^2 u}\Big[\sin^2\theta\sin^2 u - (\co
s v - \cos\theta\cos u)^2\Big].$$

The bracket factors as:
$$\big[\cos(\theta - u) - \cos v\big]\big[\cos v - \cos(\theta + u)\big].$$

Using the product-to-sum formula on each factor:
$$\cos(\theta-u)-\cos v = -2\sin\tfrac{\theta-t}{2}\sin\tfrac{\theta-\varphi}{2}
, \qquad \cos v - \cos(\theta+u) = 2\sin\tfrac{\theta+\varphi}{2}\sin\tfrac{\the
ta+t}{2}.$$

Their product, via $\sin\alpha\sin\beta = \tfrac{1}{2}(\cos(\alpha-\beta)-\cos(\
alpha+\beta))$:
$$= -4\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}(\cos t - \cos\theta)\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}(\cos\varphi - \cos
\theta) = -(\cos t - \cos\theta)(\cos\varphi - \cos\theta).$$

Therefore:
$$R^2\sin^2\theta - y_D^2 = \frac{-R^2(\cos t - \cos\theta)(\cos\varphi - \cos\t
heta)}{\sin^2 u}.$$

**Substituting back**, the factor $|\cos t - \cos\theta|$ cancels:
$$\boxed{\text{Area} = \frac{R^2\sin\theta\,|\cos\varphi - \cos\theta|}{4\,\sin^
2\!\big(\frac{\varphi+t}{2}\big)}}.$$

**Minimization.** Here $R$, $\theta$, $\varphi$ are fixed by $\triangle ABC$; on
ly $t$ varies. The area is minimized when $\sin^2\!\big(\frac{\varphi+t}{2}\big)
$ is maximized, i.e., equals $1$:
$$\frac{\varphi + t}{2} = \frac{\pi}{2} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t = \pi - \var
phi.$$

The acuteness conditions ($\varphi + \theta > \pi$ and $\varphi < \pi + \theta$)
 guarantee $\pi - \varphi \in (-\theta, \theta)$, so this point lies on the mino
r arc $AB$. The minimum area is $\frac{R^2\sin\theta\,|\cos\varphi-\cos\theta|}{
4}$.

**Geometric interpretation.** At $t = \pi - \varphi$:
$$X = \big(R\cos(\pi-\varphi),\; R\sin(\pi-\varphi)\big) = (-R\cos\varphi,\; R\s
in\varphi).$$
The direction $\overrightarrow{CX} = (-2R\cos\varphi,\, 0)$ is horizontal, i.e.,
 parallel to the perpendicular from $O$ to $AB$. Hence $CX \perp AB$, and $D = C
X \cap AB$ is the foot of the altitude from $C$ to $AB$.

**Conclusion.** The area of $\triangle OO_1O_2$ is minimized at the unique point
 $X$ on the minor arc $AB$ such that $CX \perp AB$ — equivalently, $X$ is the se
cond intersection of the altitude from $C$ to $AB$ with the circumcircle $\omega
$.
