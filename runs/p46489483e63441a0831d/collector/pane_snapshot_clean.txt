## Solution

Let $x$ be a common real root of $P(x)=x^2+ax+b=0$ and $Q(x)=x^3+bx+a=0$.

From $P(x)=0$: $b = -x^2 - ax$. Substituting into $Q(x)=0$:

$$x^3 + (-x^2-ax)x + a = x^3 - x^3 - ax^2 + a = a(1-x^2) = 0.$$

Hence **either $a=0$ or $x^2=1$** (i.e., $x=1$ or $x=-1$). These three cases are
 exhaustive.

---

### Case 1: $a = 0$

$P(x) = x^2+b$, $Q(x) = x^3+bx = x(x^2+b)$. Any root of $P$ automatically satisf
ies $Q$. A real root exists iff $b \le 0$. With $a=0$, $b\in\{-5,-4,-3,-2,-1,0\}
$: **6 configurations**.

### Case 2: $x = 1$

$P(1) = 1+a+b = 0$ and $Q(1) = 1+b+a = 0$, both giving $a+b=-1$, i.e. $b = -1-a$
. Need $a,b\in C$:
- $a\in\{-5,-4,\dots,4\}$ (since $a=5 \Rightarrow b=-6\notin C$): **10 configura
tions**.

### Case 3: $x = -1$

$P(-1) = 1-a+b = 0$ and $Q(-1) = -1-b+a = 0$, both giving $b = a-1$. Need $a,b\i
n C$:
- $a\in\{-4,-3,\dots,5\}$ (since $a=-5 \Rightarrow b=-6\notin C$): **10 configur
ations**.

### Counting the union (inclusion–exclusion)

The three sets:
- $C_1$: $(0,-5),(0,-4),(0,-3),(0,-2),(0,-1),(0,0)$
- $C_2$: $(-5,4),(-4,3),(-3,2),(-2,1),(-1,0),(0,-1),(1,-2),(2,-3),(3,-4),(4,-5)$
- $C_3$: $(-4,-5),(-3,-4),(-2,-3),(-1,-2),(0,-1),(1,0),(2,1),(3,2),(4,3),(5,4)$

**Pairwise intersections** (all reduce to $a=0,\, b=-1$):
- $C_1\cap C_2$: $a=0,\ a+b=-1 \Rightarrow (0,-1)$.
- $C_1\cap C_3$: $a=0,\ b=a-1 \Rightarrow (0,-1)$.
- $C_2\cap C_3$: $-1-a = a-1 \Rightarrow a=0 \Rightarrow (0,-1)$.

So $|C_1\cap C_2|=|C_1\cap C_3|=|C_2\cap C_3|=1$, and the triple intersection is
 $\{(0,-1)\}$, size $1$.

$$|C_1\cup C_2\cup C_3| = 6 + 10 + 10 - 1 - 1 - 1 + 1 = \boxed{24}.$$

**Verification by direct enumeration:** $6$ (from $C_1$) $+\ 9$ (from $C_2\setmi
nus C_1$) $+\ 9$ (from $C_3\setminus(C_1\cup C_2)$) $= 24$. ✓

The number of distinct Synchronized configurations is $\boxed{24}$.
