## Part (a): General solution

We solve the heat equation

$$u_t = a^2\, u_{xx}, \qquad 0 < x < l,\; t>0,$$

with boundary conditions $u(0,t)=u(l,t)=0$ and initial condition $u(x,0)=f(x)$.

**Separation of variables.** Setting $u=X(x)T(t)$ gives $X''+\lambda X=0$, $X(0)
=X(l)=0$, yielding eigenvalues $\lambda_n=(n\pi/l)^2$ and eigenfunctions $X_n(x)
=\sin(n\pi x/l)$, $n=1,2,\dots$. The time part gives $T_n(t)=e^{-(n\pi a/l)^2 t}
$.

Expanding $f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n\sin(n\pi x/l)$ with

$$b_n=\frac{2}{l}\int_0^l f(x)\sin\!\frac{n\pi x}{l}\,dx,$$

the solution is

$$\boxed{\,u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n\,\sin\!\frac{n\pi x}{l}\;\exp\!\Big(\!
-\Big(\frac{n\pi a}{l}\Big)^{\!2}t\Big),\qquad b_n=\frac{2}{l}\int_0^l f(\xi)\si
n\frac{n\pi\xi}{l}\,d\xi.\,}$$

## Part (b): Constant initial temperature $f(x)\equiv U_0$

**Fourier coefficients.**

$$b_n=\frac{2U_0}{l}\int_0^l\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}\,dx=\frac{2U_0}{n\pi}\big(1-\co
s n\pi\big)=\frac{2U_0}{n\pi}\big(1-(-1)^n\big).$$

Hence $b_n=0$ for $n$ even and $b_n=\dfrac{4U_0}{n\pi}$ for $n$ odd. Writing $n=
2m+1$ ($m=0,1,2,\dots$):

$$u(x,t)=\frac{4U_0}{\pi}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2m+1}\sin\frac{(2m+1)\pi x}
{l}\;\exp\!\Big(\!-\Big(\frac{(2m+1)\pi a}{l}\Big)^{\!2}t\Big).$$

**At $x=l/2$:** $\sin\frac{(2m+1)\pi}{2}=(-1)^m$, so

$$u\!\Big(\frac{l}{2},t\Big)=\frac{4U_0}{\pi}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^m}{2m
+1}\exp\!\big(\!-\mu_{2m+1}\,t\big),\qquad \mu_n=\Big(\frac{n\pi a}{l}\Big)^{\!2
}.$$

The first term ($m=0$, $n=1$) is

$$S_1=\frac{4U_0}{\pi}\,e^{-\mu_1 t},\qquad \mu_1=\Big(\frac{\pi a}{l}\Big)^{\!2
}.$$

### Error estimate for the partial sum

Let $S_N=\frac{4U_0}{\pi}\sum_{m=0}^{N}\frac{(-1)^m}{2m+1}e^{-\mu_{2m+1}t}$ be t
he partial sum and $R_N=u-S_N$ the remainder. Since the series is alternating in
 sign with monotonically decreasing magnitudes (for fixed $t>0$), the **Leibniz
estimate** applies:

$$|R_N|\le \frac{4U_0}{\pi}\cdot\frac{1}{2N+3}\,e^{-\mu_{2N+3}\,t}.$$

In particular, replacing the full sum by the **first term alone** ($N=0$):

$$\Big|u\Big(\frac{l}{2},t\Big)-\frac{4U_0}{\pi}e^{-\mu_1 t}\Big|\le \frac{4U_0}
{3\pi}\,e^{-9\mu_1 t}.$$

A cruder but simpler bound (summing all remainders geometrically): since $\mu_{2
m+1}=(2m+1)^2\mu_1$ and for $m\ge1$, $(2m+1)^2-1\ge 8$,

$$\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2m+1}e^{-\mu_{2m+1}t}\le \frac{1}{3}e^{-9\mu_1 t}\
sum_{m=1}^{\infty}e^{-((2m+1)^2-9)\mu_1 t}\le \frac{1}{3}e^{-9\mu_1 t}\cdot\frac
{1}{1-e^{-16\mu_1 t}}.$$

For $\mu_1 t\gtrsim 1$ this is $\approx \tfrac{1}{3}e^{-9\mu_1 t}$, confirming t
he Leibniz bound is sharp.

### Time for the ratio (tail from the 2nd term)/(1st term) $<\varepsilon$

The ratio of the absolute value of the tail (all terms from the 2nd onward) to t
he first term is

$$\rho(t)=\frac{\Big|\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^m}{2m+1}e^{-\mu_{2m+1}t}\Big|
}{\frac{1}{1}\,e^{-\mu_1 t}}\le \sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2m+1}\,e^{-((2m+1)^2
-1)\mu_1 t}.$$

Using $(2m+1)^2-1\ge 8m$ (in fact $\ge 8$ for $m\ge1$) and bounding the sum:

$$\rho(t)\le \frac{1}{3}e^{-8\mu_1 t}\Big(1+\frac{3}{5}e^{-16\mu_1 t}+\cdots\Big
)\le \frac{1}{3}\,e^{-8\mu_1 t}\cdot\frac{1}{1-e^{-16\mu_1 t}}.$$

A **sufficient condition** (dropping the geometric factor, which only makes the
bound tighter) is

$$\frac{1}{3}\,e^{-8\mu_1 t}<\varepsilon \quad\Longrightarrow\quad e^{-8\mu_1 t}
<3\varepsilon,$$

giving

$$\boxed{\;t>\frac{1}{8\mu_1}\ln\frac{1}{3\varepsilon}=\frac{l^2}{8\pi^2 a^2}\ln
\frac{1}{3\varepsilon}\;}$$

(when $3\varepsilon<1$, i.e. $\varepsilon<1/3$). For $\varepsilon\ge 1/3$ the co
ndition is satisfied for all $t>0$ since $\rho(t)\to 0$ as $t\to\infty$ and $\rh
o(0)=\pi/4-1<1/3$ is already $<1/3$... more precisely at $t=0$, $\rho(0)=\frac{\
pi/4 - 1}{1}=\frac{\pi-4}{4}\approx 0.285<1/3$, so for $\varepsilon\ge 1/3$ any
$t\ge 0$ works.

**Summary of results:**

- **(a)** $u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n\sin\!\frac{n\pi x}{l}\,e^{-(n\pi a/l)^2
t}$, $\;b_n=\frac{2}{l}\int_0^l f(\xi)\sin\frac{n\pi\xi}{l}\,d\xi$.

- **(b)** For $f\equiv U_0$: $u(x,t)=\frac{4U_0}{\pi}\sum_{m=0}^\infty\frac{1}{2
m+1}\sin\frac{(2m+1)\pi x}{l}\,e^{-((2m+1)\pi a/l)^2 t}$.

  At $x=l/2$: error of the one-term approximation $\le \frac{4U_0}{3\pi}e^{-9\pi
^2 a^2 t/l^2}$ (Leibniz bound).

  The ratio of the tail (from the 2nd term) to the 1st term is $<\varepsilon$ fo
r all

  $$t>\frac{l^2}{8\pi^2 a^2}\ln\frac{1}{3\varepsilon}\qquad(\varepsilon<1/3).$$
