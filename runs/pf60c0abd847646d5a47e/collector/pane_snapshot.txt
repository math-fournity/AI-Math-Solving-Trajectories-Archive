**Yes.** Every compact subgroup of $K \subseteq GL_n(\mathbb{R})$ is conjugate t
o a subgroup of $O(n)$.

**Proof.** Let $K$ be a compact subgroup of $GL_n(\mathbb{R})$, and let $\langle
\cdot,\cdot\rangle_0$ be the standard Euclidean inner product on $\mathbb{R}^n$.
 Since $K$ is compact, it admits a bi-invariant Haar measure $d\mu$ of total mas
s $1$. Define a new inner product by averaging over $K$:

$$\langle v, w\rangle \;=\; \int_K \langle gv,\, gw\rangle_0\, d\mu(g).$$

This is a positive-definite symmetric bilinear form (positivity follows from $\l
angle v,v\rangle \ge 0$ with equality iff $v=0$, since the integrand is non-nega
tive and equals $\|v\|_0^2>0$ for $v\neq 0$). It is $K$-invariant: for any $h\in
 K$,

$$\langle hv, hw\rangle = \int_K \langle ghv,\, ghw\rangle_0\, d\mu(g) = \int_K
\langle g'v,\, g'w\rangle_0\, d\mu(g') = \langle v,w\rangle,$$

using left-invariance of Haar measure ($g \mapsto gh$ is a measure-preserving ch
ange of variable $g' = gh$).

Now choose a basis $\mathcal{B}$ of $\mathbb{R}^n$ that is orthonormal with resp
ect to $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$. Let $P$ be the change-of-basis matrix from t
he standard basis to $\mathcal{B}$. In the basis $\mathcal{B}$, every $k\in K$ p
reserves the inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$, hence is represented by
an orthogonal matrix. Equivalently,

$$P^{-1} k P \in O(n) \quad\text{for all } k\in K,$$

so $K \subseteq P\, O(n)\, P^{-1}$. Thus $K$ is subconjugate to $O(n)$. $\square
