## Proof

Let $L(n) = \operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\dots,n)$. We compute $\log L(n)$ by collecti
ng, for every prime $p \le n$, the largest power $p^k \le n$; this is exactly th
e exponent of $p$ in $L(n)$. Hence

$$
\log L(n) = \sum_{p \le n} \lfloor \log_p n \rfloor \log p = \sum_{\substack{p^k
 \le n \\ k \ge 1}} \log p.
$$

The right-hand side is precisely the **second Chebyshev function**

$$
\psi(n) := \sum_{m \le n} \Lambda(m) = \sum_{\substack{p^k \le n \\ k\ge 1}} \lo
g p,
$$

where $\Lambda$ is the von Mangoldt function. Thus

$$
\log L(n) = \psi(n).
$$

**Claim.** $\displaystyle \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{\psi(n)}{n} = 1$.

This is the standard equivalent form of the **Prime Number Theorem**. For comple
teness, here is the argument reducing it to $\pi(x) \sim x/\log x$.

Using $\Lambda(m) = \log p$ when $m = p^k$ and $0$ otherwise, and writing $\pi(x
) = \#\{p \le x\}$, summation by parts gives

$$
\psi(x) = \sum_{p \le x} \log p \cdot \lfloor \tfrac{\log x}{\log p}\rfloor.
$$

The contribution of prime powers $p^k$ with $k \ge 2$ is bounded by

$$
\sum_{\substack{p^k \le x \\ k\ge 2}} \log p \le \sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor \log_2 x\rf
loor} \sum_{p \le x^{1/k}} \log p \le \sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor \log_2 x\rfloor} x^{1/
k}\log x = O\!\left(\sqrt{x}\log x\right) = o(x).
$$

So it suffices to handle $\theta(x) := \sum_{p \le x} \log p$. By PNT, $\pi(x) \
sim x/\log x$, and partial summation (Abel summation) yields

$$
\theta(x) = \pi(x)\log x - \int_2^x \frac{\pi(t)}{t}\,dt.
$$

Substituting $\pi(t) \sim t/\log t$:

$$
\theta(x) \sim \frac{x}{\log x}\cdot \log x - \int_2^x \frac{t/\log t}{t}\,dt =
x - \int_2^x \frac{dt}{\log t}.
$$

The logarithmic integral satisfies $\int_2^x \frac{dt}{\log t} \sim \frac{x}{\lo
g x} = o(x)$, so

$$
\theta(x) \sim x.
$$

Combining, $\psi(x) = \theta(x) + O(\sqrt{x}\log x) \sim x$, i.e.

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{\psi(n)}{n} = 1.
$$

**Conclusion.** Since $\log L(n) = \psi(n)$ and $\psi(n)/n \to 1$, the ordinary
limit exists and equals $1$; therefore the limsup equals the same value:

$$
\boxed{\limsup_{n\to\infty} \frac{\log\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\dots,n)}{n} = 1.}
$$
