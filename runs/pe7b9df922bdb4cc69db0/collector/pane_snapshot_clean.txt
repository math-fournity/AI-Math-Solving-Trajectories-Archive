Let $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})$ with $D \in \mathbb{Q}^\times$ a nonsquare (so $[
F:\mathbb{Q}]=2$). Since every quadratic extension is Galois, $F/\mathbb{Q}$ has
 Galois group $G = \{\mathrm{id}, \sigma\}$ where $\sigma(\sqrt{D}) = -\sqrt{D}$
.

**Claim.** $F \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} F \cong F \times F$ as $\mathbb{Q}$-algebras
(equivalently, as $F$-algebras).

**Proof.** Define the map
$$
\varphi : F \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} F \longrightarrow F \times F, \qquad a \otimes
b \longmapsto \bigl(a\,b,\; a\,\sigma(b)\bigr).
$$

*Well-defined and a ring homomorphism.* The assignment is $\mathbb{Q}$-bilinear
in the two factors (since $\sigma$ fixes $\mathbb{Q}$), so it descends to a $\ma
thbb{Q}$-linear map on the tensor product. It respects multiplication:
$$
\varphi\bigl((a\otimes b)(a'\otimes b')\bigr) = \varphi(aa'\otimes bb') = \bigl(
aa'bb',\; aa'\sigma(bb')\bigr) = \bigl(ab,\; a\sigma(b)\bigr)\bigl(a'b',\; a'\si
gma(b')\bigr) = \varphi(a\otimes b)\,\varphi(a'\otimes b').
$$
The unit maps to the unit: $\varphi(1\otimes 1) = (1,1)$. Hence $\varphi$ is a $
\mathbb{Q}$-algebra homomorphism.

*Surjectivity.* It suffices to exhibit preimages of the idempotents $(1,0)$ and
$(0,1)$. Compute:
$$
\varphi(1\otimes 1) = (1,1), \qquad \varphi(\sqrt{D}\otimes\sqrt{D}) = \bigl(D,\
; \sqrt{D}\,\sigma(\sqrt{D})\bigr) = (D, -D).
$$
Therefore
$$
\varphi\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigl(1\otimes 1 + \tfrac{1}{D}\sqrt{D}\otimes\sqrt{D
}\Bigr)\right) = \left(\tfrac{1+1}{2},\,\tfrac{1-1}{2}\right) = (1,0),
$$
$$
\varphi\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigl(1\otimes 1 - \tfrac{1}{D}\sqrt{D}\otimes\sqrt{D
}\Bigr)\right) = \left(\tfrac{1-1}{2},\,\tfrac{1+1}{2}\right) = (0,1).
$$
(These elements lie in $F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}F$ since $\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{D
}\in\mathbb{Q}^\times$.) Hence $\varphi$ is surjective.

*Dimension count.* As $\mathbb{Q}$-vector spaces,
$$
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} F) = [F:\mathbb{Q}]^2 = 4 = 2\cdot\dim_{
\mathbb{Q}} F = \dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(F\times F).
$$
A surjective linear map between spaces of equal finite dimension is an isomorphi
sm. Thus $\varphi$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra isomorphism.

**Conclusion.**
$$
\boxed{\,F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} F \;\cong\; F\times F\,}
$$
i.e. the tensor product splits as the direct product of two copies of $F$. This
is the special case of the general theorem that for a finite Galois extension $L
/K$ with group $G$, one has $L\otimes_K L \cong \prod_{g\in G} L$ via $a\otimes
b\mapsto (a\,g(b))_{g\in G}$; here $|G|=2$.
