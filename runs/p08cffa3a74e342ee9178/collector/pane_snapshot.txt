Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the reduction map $SL_2(\ma
thcal{O}_K) \to SL_2(\mathcal{O}_K/I)$ is surjective for every ring of integers
$\mathcal{O}_K$ and every ideal $I$.

**Answer: Yes, the map is always surjective.**

## Proof

**Edge cases.** If $I = 0$, the map is the identity. If $I = \mathcal{O}_K$, the
n $\mathcal{O}_K/I = 0$ and $SL_2(0)$ is trivial. Both are trivially surjective.
 Henceforth assume $I \neq 0$ is a proper nonzero ideal.

**Step 1: $\mathcal{O}_K/I$ is semilocal.**

Since $\mathcal{O}_K$ is a Dedekind domain, the ideal $I$ factors as $I = \prod_
{i=1}^m \mathfrak{p}_i^{e_i}$ into prime powers. By the Chinese Remainder Theore
m:
$$\mathcal{O}_K/I \cong \prod_{i=1}^m \mathcal{O}_K/\mathfrak{p}_i^{e_i}.$$
Each $\mathcal{O}_K/\mathfrak{p}_i^{e_i}$ is a local ring (its unique maximal id
eal is $\mathfrak{p}_i/\mathfrak{p}_i^{e_i}$). So $R := \mathcal{O}_K/I$ is a fi
nite product of local rings, hence **semilocal**.

**Step 2 (Lemma): Semilocal rings have stable range 1.**

*Proof.* Let $R$ be semilocal with maximal ideals $\mathfrak{m}_1, \dots, \mathf
rak{m}_n$. Suppose $(a, b) = R$. We seek $t$ such that $a + tb$ is a unit, i.e.,
 $a + tb \notin \mathfrak{m}_i$ for all $i$.

For each $i$: If $b \in \mathfrak{m}_i$, then $(a,b)=R$ forces $a \notin \mathfr
ak{m}_i$, so $a+tb \equiv a \notin \mathfrak{m}_i$ for any $t$. If $b \notin \ma
thfrak{m}_i$, then $b$ is a unit mod $\mathfrak{m}_i$, so we can pick $t_i$ with
 $a + t_i b \equiv 1 \pmod{\mathfrak{m}_i}$.

By CRT (the $\mathfrak{m}_i$ are pairwise comaximal), choose $t \equiv t_i \pmod
{\mathfrak{m}_i}$ for all $i$ with $b \notin \mathfrak{m}_i$. Then $a + tb \noti
n \mathfrak{m}_i$ for every $i$, so $a+tb$ is a unit. $\square$

**Step 3 (Lemma): If $R$ has stable range 1, then $SL_2(R) = E_2(R)$.**

*Proof.* Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \in SL_2(R)$, so
$ad - bc = 1$.

**Claim:** $(a, c) = R$. Indeed, if $a, c \in \mathfrak{m}$ for some maximal ide
al $\mathfrak{m}$, then $ad - bc \in \mathfrak{m}$, contradicting $ad - bc = 1 \
notin \mathfrak{m}$.

By stable range 1, there exists $t \in R$ such that $u := a + tc$ is a unit. Com
pute:

$$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & t \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} A = \begin{pmatrix} u & b+td \\ c
 & d \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -u^{-1}c & 1 \end{pmatrix}\b
egin{pmatrix} u & b+td \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} u & b+td \\ 0 &
u^{-1} \end{pmatrix},$$

where the bottom-right entry is $u^{-1}$ because $d - u^{-1}c(b+td) = u^{-1}(ud
- c(b+td)) = u^{-1}((a+tc)d - cb - tcd) = u^{-1}(ad-bc) = u^{-1}$.

Finally:
$$\begin{pmatrix} u & b+td \\ 0 & u^{-1} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} u & 0 \
\ 0 & u^{-1} \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & u^{-1}(b+td) \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix
}.$$

The second factor is elementary. The diagonal factor $\begin{pmatrix} u & 0 \\ 0
 & u^{-1} \end{pmatrix}$ is a product of elementary matrices via the standard id
entities (valid over any commutative ring):

$$\begin{pmatrix} u & 0 \\ 0 & u^{-1} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & u \\ 0
 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -u^{-1} & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatr
ix} 1 & u \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix},$$

$$\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 &
1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -
1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

So $A$ is a product of elementary matrices. $\square$

**Step 4: Surjectivity.**

By Steps 1–3, $SL_2(\mathcal{O}_K/I) = E_2(\mathcal{O}_K/I)$: every element is a
 product of elementary matrices $E_{ij}(\bar{a})$ with $\bar{a} \in \mathcal{O}_
K/I$.

Each such elementary matrix lifts: choose any $a \in \mathcal{O}_K$ with $a \equ
iv \bar{a} \pmod{I}$, and $E_{ij}(a) \in SL_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ maps to $E_{ij}(\b
ar{a})$ under reduction.

Since the image of $SL_2(\mathcal{O}_K) \to SL_2(\mathcal{O}_K/I)$ contains all
elementary matrices, and these generate $SL_2(\mathcal{O}_K/I)$, the map is **su
rjective**. $\square$

**Key point:** The crucial fact is that $\mathcal{O}_K/I$ is *semilocal* (a prod
uct of local rings by CRT), which gives stable range 1, which forces $SL_2 = E_2
$. The elementary matrices then lift trivially. Note that we do **not** need $SL
_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ itself to be generated by elementary matrices (which fails fo
r non-Euclidean $\mathcal{O}_K$) — we only need this for the quotient $\mathcal{
O}_K/I$.
