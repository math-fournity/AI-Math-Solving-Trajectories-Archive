**Answer: The statement is FALSE.**

**Counterexample.**

Let $R = \mathbb{Z}$, let the family consist of the single ideal $I_1 = (2) = 2\
mathbb{Z}$, and let $J = (2, x)$ be the ideal in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ generated by $2
$ and $x$.

**Step 1: Verify the hypothesis $J \cap R \subseteq \bigcup I_i$.**

We identify $R = \mathbb{Z}$ with the constant polynomials in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$. A
n integer $n$ lies in $J = (2,x)$ if and only if $n = 2f(x) + x\,g(x)$ for some
$f, g \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$. Evaluating at $x = 0$ gives $n = 2f(0)$, so $n$ must b
e even. Conversely, every even integer $n = 2 \cdot (n/2)$ lies in $(2) \subsete
q (2,x) = J$. Hence
$$J \cap R = 2\mathbb{Z} = (2) = I_1 = \bigcup_i I_i.$$
Thus $J \cap R \subseteq \bigcup_i I_i$ holds (in fact, with equality).

**Step 2: Show the conclusion $J \subseteq \bigcup I_i[x]$ fails.**

The extension $I_1[x] = (2)\mathbb{Z}[x]$ is the ideal of all polynomials in $\m
athbb{Z}[x]$ whose coefficients are all even. Since the family has a single memb
er,
$$\bigcup_i I_i[x] = I_1[x] = (2) \subseteq \mathbb{Z}[x].$$
Now consider the element $x \in J = (2,x)$. The polynomial $x$ has coefficient $
1$ (which is odd) on the $x^1$-term, so $x \notin (2) = \bigcup_i I_i[x]$. There
fore
$$J \not\subseteq \bigcup_i I_i[x].$$

**Conclusion.** The hypothesis $J \cap R \subseteq \bigcup I_i$ is satisfied, bu
t the conclusion $J \subseteq \bigcup I_i[x]$ fails. The statement is **false**.

**Remark on the underlying reason.** The hypothesis $J \cap R \subseteq \bigcup
I_i$ constrains only the *constant terms* of elements of $J$ (i.e., the elements
 of $J$ that lie in $R$). The conclusion $J \subseteq \bigcup I_i[x]$ requires t
hat *every coefficient* of every element of $J$ lie in some $I_i$. These are vas
tly different conditions: controlling constant terms says nothing about the high
er-degree coefficients. The ideal $J = (2, x)$ is designed so that its constant
elements are all even, yet it contains $x$, whose leading coefficient $1 \notin
(2)$.
