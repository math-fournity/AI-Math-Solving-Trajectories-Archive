Looking at this problem, I need to find all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $g(x) =
 100|f(x)| - \sum_{k=1}^n |f(x^k)|$ is differentiable everywhere, where $f(x) =
e^{x+1} - 1$.

## Locating Non-Differentiable Points

$f(x) = 0 \iff e^{x+1} = 1 \iff x = -1$, and $f'(-1) = e^0 = 1 \neq 0$. So $|f(x
)|$ has a cusp (non-differentiable point) only at $x = -1$.

For $|f(x^k)|$: $f(x^k) = 0 \iff x^k = -1$.
- **Even $k$**: $x^k \geq 0$ always, so $x^k + 1 \geq 1 > 0$, meaning $f(x^k) >
0$ everywhere. Thus $|f(x^k)| = f(x^k)$ is differentiable everywhere.
- **Odd $k$**: $x^k = -1$ has unique solution $x = -1$. The derivative $\frac{d}
{dx}f(x^k)\big|_{x=-1} = e^0 \cdot k \cdot (-1)^{k-1} = k \neq 0$ (since $k-1$ i
s even). So $|f(x^k)|$ has a cusp only at $x = -1$.

**The only point to check is $x = -1$.**

## Computing Left/Right Derivatives at $x = -1$

**For $|f(x)|$**: Near $x=-1$, $f(x) \approx (x+1)$, so $|f(x)| \approx |x+1|$.
- Right derivative: $+1$ (since $f(x) > 0$ for $x > -1$)
- Left derivative: $-1$ (since $f(x) < 0$ for $x < -1$)

**For $|f(x^k)|$, odd $k$**: Near $x=-1$, $f(x^k) \approx x^k + 1 \approx k(x+1)
$ (since $x^k$ is increasing for odd $k$, with $(-1)^{k-1}=1$).
- Right derivative: $+k$
- Left derivative: $-k$

**For $|f(x^k)|$, even $k$**: Differentiable at $x=-1$ with derivative $e^{(-1)^
k+1}\cdot k\cdot(-1)^{k-1} = e^2 \cdot k \cdot (-1) = -e^2 k$ (same from both si
des).

## Setting Up the Differentiability Condition

The right-left derivative jump of $g$ at $x=-1$:

$$\Delta g = 100\cdot(1-(-1)) - \sum_{\substack{k \leq n \\ k \text{ odd}}} (k-(
-k)) - \sum_{\substack{k \leq n \\ k \text{ even}}} 0$$

$$\Delta g = 200 - 2\sum_{\substack{k \leq n \\ k \text{ odd}}} k$$

For differentiability, $\Delta g = 0$:

$$\sum_{\substack{k \leq n \\ k \text{ odd}}} k = 100$$

## Solving

The sum of the first $m$ odd positive integers is $m^2$. So we need $m^2 = 100$,
 giving $m = 10$ odd numbers.

- If $n$ is **odd**: the count of odd $k \in \{1,\ldots,n\}$ is $\frac{n+1}{2}$.
 Setting $= 10$ gives $n = 19$.
- If $n$ is **even**: the count of odd $k \in \{1,\ldots,n\}$ is $\frac{n}{2}$.
Setting $= 10$ gives $n = 20$.

**Verification:**
- $n = 19$: odd $k$ are $1, 3, 5, \ldots, 19$ — ten terms, sum $= 10^2 = 100$ ✓
- $n = 20$: odd $k$ are $1, 3, 5, \ldots, 19$ — ten terms, sum $= 100$ ✓

Both values make $g$ differentiable at $x=-1$ (the only critical point), hence d
ifferentiable on all of $\mathbb{R}$.

## Answer

The possible integer values are $n = 19$ and $n = 20$. Their sum is:

$$19 + 20 = \boxed{39}$$
