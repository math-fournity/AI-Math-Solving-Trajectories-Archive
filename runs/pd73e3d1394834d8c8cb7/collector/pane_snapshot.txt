## Solution

**Convention.** I use the Fourier transform
$$\widehat f(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,e^{-i\omega x}\,dx,\qquad f(x
)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat f(\omega)\,e^{i\omega x}\,d\omeg
a.$$

### Step 1. Simplify the integrand

Observe
$$e^x+e^{-x}+2=(e^{x/2}+e^{-x/2})^2=4\cosh^2\!\tfrac{x}{2}.$$
Hence
$$f(x)=\frac{1}{4\cosh^2(x/2)}=\frac14\,\operatorname{sech}^2\!\tfrac{x}{2}.$$

### Step 2. A key integral via contour integration

We first establish, for real $\xi$,
$$I(\xi):=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{sech}^2 u\;e^{-i\xi u}\,du=\frac{
\pi\xi}{\sinh(\pi\xi/2)}.\tag{$\ast$}$$

Consider $g(z)=\operatorname{sech}^2 z\,e^{-i\xi z}$ and the rectangle with vert
ices $-R,\;R,\;R+i\pi,\;-R+i\pi$. Since $\cosh(z+i\pi)=-\cosh z$, we have $\oper
atorname{sech}^2(z+i\pi)=\operatorname{sech}^2 z$, so $g$ is $i\pi$-periodic in
the imaginary direction.

**Pole inside the strip $0<\operatorname{Im}z<\pi$.** $\cosh z=0$ at $z=i\pi/2$.
 Writing $w=z-i\pi/2$,
$$\cosh z=\cosh(i\pi/2+w)=i\sinh w=iw\bigl(1+\tfrac{w^2}{6}+\cdots\bigr),$$
so $\cosh^2 z=-w^2(1+\tfrac{w^2}{3}+\cdots)$ and
$$\operatorname{sech}^2 z=-\frac{1}{w^2}+\frac13+\cdots.$$
Also $e^{-i\xi z}=e^{-i\xi(i\pi/2)}e^{-i\xi w}=e^{\xi\pi/2}\bigl(1-i\xi w-\tfrac
{\xi^2 w^2}{2}+\cdots\bigr)$. Thus
$$g(z)=e^{\xi\pi/2}\Bigl[-\frac{1}{w^2}+\frac{i\xi}{w}+\Bigl(\frac{\xi^2}{2}+\fr
ac13\Bigr)+\cdots\Bigr],$$
giving the residue
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=i\pi/2}g(z)=i\xi\,e^{\xi\pi/2}.$$

**Boundary contributions.** The vertical sides vanish as $R\to\infty$ since $\op
eratorname{sech}^2 z$ decays like $e^{-2|z|}$. The bottom side gives $I(\xi)$. T
he top side (traversed right-to-left, $z=u+i\pi$, $u:R\to -R$) gives, using peri
odicity and $e^{-i\xi(u+i\pi)}=e^{\xi\pi}e^{-i\xi u}$,
$$\int_{R}^{-R}\operatorname{sech}^2 u\;e^{\xi\pi}e^{-i\xi u}\,du=-e^{\xi\pi}I(\
xi).$$

By the residue theorem,
$$I(\xi)-e^{\xi\pi}I(\xi)=2\pi i\cdot i\xi\,e^{\xi\pi/2}=-2\pi\xi\,e^{\xi\pi/2},
$$
so
$$I(\xi)\bigl(1-e^{\xi\pi}\bigr)=-2\pi\xi\,e^{\xi\pi/2},\qquad
I(\xi)=\frac{2\pi\xi\,e^{\xi\pi/2}}{e^{\xi\pi}-1}=\frac{2\pi\xi}{e^{\xi\pi/2}-e^
{-\xi\pi/2}}=\frac{\pi\xi}{\sinh(\pi\xi/2)}.$$
This proves $(\ast)$. (Consistency check: as $\xi\to0$, $\pi\xi/\sinh(\pi\xi/2)\
to2=\int\operatorname{sech}^2u\,du$. ✓)

### Step 3. Rescale to $\operatorname{sech}^2(x/2)$

With $u=x/2$, $dx=2\,du$:
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{sech}^2\!\tfrac{x}{2}\;e^{-i\omega x}\,dx
=2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{sech}^2 u\;e^{-i(2\omega)u}\,du
=2\cdot\frac{\pi(2\omega)}{\sinh(\pi\omega)}
=\frac{4\pi\omega}{\sinh(\pi\omega)}.$$

### Step 4. Assemble the answer

$$\widehat f(\omega)=\frac14\cdot\frac{4\pi\omega}{\sinh(\pi\omega)}=\frac{\pi\o
mega}{\sinh(\pi\omega)}.$$

**Consistency check at $\omega=0$:** $\displaystyle\lim_{\omega\to0}\frac{\pi\om
ega}{\sinh(\pi\omega)}=1$, and $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\,dx=\fr
ac14\cdot2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{sech}^2u\,du=\frac14\cdot2\cdot2=
1$. ✓

## Result

With the convention $\widehat f(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)e^{-i\omega x
}\,dx$,

$$\boxed{\;\widehat f(\omega)=\dfrac{\pi\omega}{\sinh(\pi\omega)}=\pi\omega\,\op
eratorname{csch}(\pi\omega)\;}$$

(For the symmetric convention $\widehat f(\omega)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int f(x)e^
{-i\omega x}dx$, divide by $\sqrt{2\pi}$; for the convention $\int f(x)e^{-2\pi
i\xi x}dx$, substitute $\omega=2\pi\xi$ to obtain $\frac{2\pi^2\xi}{\sinh(2\pi^2
\xi)}$.)
