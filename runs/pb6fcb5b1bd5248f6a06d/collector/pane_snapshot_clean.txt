## Solution

**Setup.** A best-of-five series ends when one firm reaches 3 wins. Alpha starts
 in the Primary Bay. After each assessment, the **loser** of that assessment is
sent to the Primary Bay for the next round. The firm in the Primary Bay wins tha
t assessment with probability $2/3$; the firm in the Secondary Bay wins with pro
bability $1/3$.

Let $X$ = total number of assessments (across the whole series) won by whichever
 firm occupied the Primary Bay during that assessment.

**Key observation.** Conditional on any particular assessment being played, the
Primary-Bay firm wins it with probability $2/3$, regardless of state. By lineari
ty of expectation,

$$X = \sum_{k} \mathbf{1}[\text{game } k \text{ is played}] \cdot \mathbf{1}[\te
xt{primary firm wins game } k],$$

so

$$\mathbb{E}[X] = \tfrac{2}{3}\,\mathbb{E}[N],$$

where $N$ is the total number of assessments played. It suffices to compute $\ma
thbb{E}[N]$.

**Recursion for $\mathbb{E}[N]$.** Let $E_A(a,b)$ and $E_B(a,b)$ be the expected
 number of *future* assessments from score $(a,b)$ with Alpha / Beta respectivel
y in the Primary Bay. Terminal: $E=0$ when $a=3$ or $b=3$.

At state $(a,b,A)$: the next game is played (count $1$). With prob $2/3$, Alpha
(primary) wins $\to$ score $(a+1,b)$, loser Beta goes to primary $\to$ state $(a
+1,b,B)$. With prob $1/3$, Beta (secondary) wins $\to$ score $(a,b+1)$, loser Al
pha goes to primary $\to$ state $(a,b+1,A)$. Hence

$$E_A(a,b)=1+\tfrac{2}{3}\,\mathbf{1}_{a+1<3}\,E_B(a+1,b)+\tfrac{1}{3}\,\mathbf{
1}_{b+1<3}\,E_A(a,b+1),$$

$$E_B(a,b)=1+\tfrac{2}{3}\,\mathbf{1}_{b+1<3}\,E_A(a,b+1)+\tfrac{1}{3}\,\mathbf{
1}_{a+1<3}\,E_B(a+1,b).$$

**Base level $a+b=4$:** $E_A(2,2)=E_B(2,2)=1$.

**Level $a+b=3$:**
- $E_A(2,1)=1+\tfrac13 E_A(2,2)=1+\tfrac13=\tfrac43$; $\;E_B(2,1)=1+\tfrac23 E_A
(2,2)=\tfrac53$.
- $E_A(1,2)=1+\tfrac23 E_B(2,2)=\tfrac53$; $\;E_B(1,2)=1+\tfrac13 E_B(2,2)=\tfra
c43$.

**Level $a+b=2$:**
- $E_A(2,0)=1+\tfrac13 E_A(2,1)=1+\tfrac49=\tfrac{13}{9}$; $\;E_B(2,0)=1+\tfrac2
3 E_A(2,1)=1+\tfrac89=\tfrac{17}{9}$.
- $E_A(1,1)=E_B(1,1)=1+\tfrac23\cdot\tfrac53+\tfrac13\cdot\tfrac53=1+\tfrac53=\t
frac83$.
- $E_A(0,2)=1+\tfrac23 E_B(1,2)=\tfrac{17}{9}$; $\;E_B(0,2)=1+\tfrac13 E_B(1,2)=
\tfrac{13}{9}$.

**Level $a+b=1$:**
- $E_A(1,0)=1+\tfrac23 E_B(2,0)+\tfrac13 E_A(1,1)=1+\tfrac{34}{27}+\tfrac89=1+\t
frac{58}{27}=\tfrac{85}{27}$.
- $E_B(1,0)=1+\tfrac23 E_A(1,1)+\tfrac13 E_B(2,0)=1+\tfrac{16}{9}+\tfrac{17}{27}
=\tfrac{92}{27}$.
- $E_A(0,1)=1+\tfrac23 E_B(1,1)+\tfrac13 E_A(0,2)=\tfrac{92}{27}$; $\;E_B(0,1)=\
tfrac{85}{27}$.

**Level $a+b=0$:**
$$E_A(0,0)=1+\tfrac23 E_B(1,0)+\tfrac13 E_A(0,1)=1+\tfrac23\cdot\tfrac{92}{27}+\
tfrac13\cdot\tfrac{92}{27}=1+\tfrac{92}{27}=\tfrac{119}{27}.$$

So $\mathbb{E}[N]=\dfrac{119}{27}$, and

$$X=\mathbb{E}[X]=\tfrac23\cdot\tfrac{119}{27}=\dfrac{238}{81}.$$

**Irreducibility.** $\gcd(238,81)$: $238=2\cdot7\cdot17$, $81=3^4$, so $\gcd=1$.
 Thus $\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{238}{81}$ and

$$a+b=238+81=\boxed{319}.$$
