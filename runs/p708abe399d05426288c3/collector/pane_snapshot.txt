## Proof

We work with oriented surfaces $M^2$ embedded in $\mathbb{R}^n$.

### Construction

At each point $p \in M$, let $T_pM$ (dimension 2) be the tangent space with orie
nted orthonormal frame $(e_1, e_2)$, and $N_pM$ (dimension $n-2$) the normal spa
ce with orthonormal frame $(e_3, \dots, e_n)$.

The **second fundamental form** is $h_{ij}^\alpha = \langle \nabla_{e_i} e_j, e_
\alpha\rangle$, and the **shape operator** in normal direction $e_\alpha$ is the
 $2\times 2$ symmetric matrix $A_\alpha = (h_{ij}^\alpha)$.

The **curvature of the normal connection** (Ricci curvature) is given by the Ric
ci equation:
$$R^\perp(e_1, e_2) = \sum_{\alpha < \beta} R^\perp_{\alpha\beta}\, e_\alpha \we
dge e_\beta \;\in\; \textstyle\bigwedge^2 N_pM$$
where $R^\perp_{\alpha\beta} = \langle [A_\alpha, A_\beta]\, e_1,\, e_2\rangle =
 (A_\alpha A_\beta - A_\beta A_\alpha)_{12}$.

**Define the quadvector:**
$$\boxed{Q \;=\; R^\perp(e_1, e_2) \wedge (e_1 \wedge e_2) \;\in\; \textstyle\bi
gwedge^4 \mathbb{R}^n}$$

### Verification of the three required properties

**1. Invariant of embedded manifolds.**

*Frame independence on $T_pM$:* Under an orientation-preserving orthonormal chan
ge $(e_1,e_2)\to(e_1',e_2')$, the area element $e_1\wedge e_2$ is unchanged, and
 $R^\perp(e_1',e_2') = R^\perp(e_1,e_2)$ since $R^\perp$ is a 2-form and $\bigwe
dge^2 T_pM$ is 1-dimensional. Under orientation reversal, both $R^\perp(e_1,e_2)
$ and $e_1\wedge e_2$ pick up the same sign, which cancels in the wedge product.
 Hence $Q$ is invariant under tangent frame changes.

*Frame independence on $N_pM$:* $R^\perp(e_1,e_2)$ transforms as an element of $
\bigwedge^2 N_pM$ under $SO(n-2)$ rotations of the normal frame; the wedge with
$e_1\wedge e_2$ is unaffected. Hence $Q$ is invariant under normal frame changes
.

*Reparametrization and ambient isometry:* $Q$ is constructed from the first and
second fundamental forms, which are invariants of the embedding. Under an ambien
t isometry $\phi$, we have $\phi_* Q = \bigwedge^4\phi\cdot Q$, the correct equi
variance for a geometric tensor. $\checkmark$

**2. Same units as Gaussian curvature $K$.**

The shape operator $A_\alpha = d(e_\alpha)/ds$ has units $1/L$ (derivative of a
unit vector with respect to arc length). Therefore $R^\perp_{\alpha\beta} = [A_\
alpha, A_\beta]_{12}$ has units $1/L^2$. The unit area element $e_1\wedge e_2$ i
s dimensionless. Thus:
$$[Q] = [R^\perp]\cdot[e_1\wedge e_2] = \frac{1}{L^2}\cdot 1 = \frac{1}{L^2} = [
K]. \quad\checkmark$$

**3. Always $0$ in 3D.**

When $n=3$, the normal space $N_pM$ is 1-dimensional, so $\bigwedge^2 N_pM = 0$
and $R^\perp = 0$. Moreover, $\bigwedge^4\mathbb{R}^3 = 0$ since $\binom{3}{4}=0
$. Both reasons independently force $Q=0$ for every surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$. $
\checkmark$

### Non-triviality: $Q\neq 0$ in higher dimensions

We exhibit a surface in $\mathbb{R}^4$ with $Q\neq 0$. Consider the graph
$$\mathbf{x}(u,v) = (u,\; v,\; u^2,\; uv)$$
at the origin $(u,v)=(0,0)$. There, $e_1=(1,0,0,0)$, $e_2=(0,1,0,0)$, $e_3=(0,0,
1,0)$, $e_4=(0,0,0,1)$, and:

$$\mathbf{x}_{uu}=(0,0,2,0),\quad \mathbf{x}_{uv}=(0,0,0,1),\quad \mathbf{x}_{vv
}=(0,0,0,0)$$

$$A_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix},\qquad A_4 = \begin{pmatrix
} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$

$$[A_3, A_4] = A_3A_4 - A_4A_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} -
\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ -2 & 0 \
end{pmatrix}$$

$$R^\perp_{34} = ([A_3,A_4])_{12} = 2$$

$$Q = 2\; e_3\wedge e_4 \wedge e_1\wedge e_2 = 2\; e_1\wedge e_2\wedge e_3\wedge
 e_4 \neq 0$$

### Conclusion

The quadvector $Q = R^\perp(e_1,e_2)\wedge(e_1\wedge e_2)$ is:

- **An invariant** of the embedded surface (well-defined, independent of all fra
me and coordinate choices),
- **Same units as $K$**: both are $1/L^2$,
- **Identically zero in 3D** for two independent reasons (no normal curvature in
 codimension 1, and no grade-4 elements in $\mathbb{R}^3$),
- **Non-trivial** in general, as demonstrated by an explicit surface in $\mathbb
{R}^4$.

Note also that $Q$ is genuinely distinct from $K$: the Gaussian curvature $K = \
sum_\alpha \det(A_\alpha)$ captures the *intrinsic* curvature (symmetric combina
tions of shape operators), while $Q$ captures the *extrinsic normal* curvature (
antisymmetric commutators $[A_\alpha, A_\beta]$). They are complementary invaria
nts of the embedding.

**Yes, such a quadvector quantity exists.**
