## Proof

**Claim:** There exists a pair of consecutive positive integers, both super expo
nential.

**Construction via Chinese Remainder Theorem.**

Let $p = 2$ and $q = 3$. Consider the system of congruences
$$
n \equiv 0 \pmod{2^{1000}}, \qquad n \equiv -1 \pmod{3^{1000}}.
$$
Since $\gcd(2^{1000}, 3^{1000}) = 1$, the Chinese Remainder Theorem guarantees a
 unique solution modulo $M = 2^{1000}\cdot 3^{1000}$.

**The solution is nonzero.** If the solution were $n \equiv 0 \pmod{M}$, then $n
 \equiv 0 \pmod{3^{1000}}$, contradicting $n \equiv -1 \pmod{3^{1000}}$ (since $
3^{1000} \nmid 1$). Hence the least nonnegative representative $r$ of the soluti
on class satisfies $1 \le r \le M-1$.

Set $n = r$. Then:
- $n \ge 1$ and $n+1 \ge 2$, so both are positive integers, and they are consecu
tive.
- $2^{1000} \mid n$, so $n$'s prime factorization contains the prime $2$ raised
to the power $1000$. Thus $n$ is super exponential.
- $3^{1000} \mid (n+1)$, so $n+1$'s prime factorization contains the prime $3$ r
aised to the power $1000$. Thus $n+1$ is super exponential.

Therefore $n$ and $n+1$ are consecutive positive integers, both super exponentia
l. $\blacksquare$
