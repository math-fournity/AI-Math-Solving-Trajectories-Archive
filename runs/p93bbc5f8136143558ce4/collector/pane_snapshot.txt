## Proof

**Claim:** The maximum modulus principle holds for antiholomorphic functions.

**Recall the classical result.** The maximum modulus principle for holomorphic f
unctions states:

> (MMP-hol) Let $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ be a connected open set and $g : \
Omega \to \mathbb{C}$ holomorphic. If $|g|$ attains a local maximum at some poin
t $z_0 \in \Omega$, then $g$ is constant on $\Omega$.

**Definitions.** A function $f : \Omega \to \mathbb{C}$ is *antiholomorphic* if
$\frac{\partial f}{\partial z} = 0$ on $\Omega$, equivalently $f$ is locally exp
ressible as a convergent power series in $\bar{z}$. A basic and immediate equiva
lence is:

> $f$ is antiholomorphic on $\Omega$ $\iff$ $\overline{f}$ is holomorphic on $\O
mega$.

Indeed, writing $f = u + iv$, the condition $\frac{\partial f}{\partial z} = 0$
is equivalent to $\frac{\partial \overline{f}}{\partial \bar{z}} = 0$, which is
the Cauchy–Riemann equation for $\overline{f} = u - iv$.

**Proof of the claim.** Let $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ be connected and open,
 and let $f : \Omega \to \mathbb{C}$ be antiholomorphic. Suppose $|f|$ attains a
 local maximum at $z_0 \in \Omega$; we must show $f$ is constant.

Set $g := \overline{f}$. By the equivalence above, $g$ is holomorphic on $\Omega
$. Moreover,
$$|g(z)| = |\overline{f(z)}| = |f(z)| \quad \text{for all } z \in \Omega.$$
Hence $|g|$ attains a local maximum at $z_0$. By the maximum modulus principle (
MMP-hol) applied to the holomorphic function $g$, we conclude $g$ is constant on
 $\Omega$.

Since $g = \overline{f}$ is constant, $f = \overline{g}$ is constant on $\Omega$
. $\square$

**Remark (converse direction / boundary version).** The same conjugation argumen
t transports every standard form of the maximum modulus principle from the holom
orphic to the antiholomorphic setting:

- *Boundary maximum:* If $\Omega$ is bounded with $f$ antiholomorphic on $\Omega
$ and continuous on $\overline{\Omega}$, then $\max_{\overline{\Omega}} |f| = \m
ax_{\partial\Omega} |f|$.
- *Strict maximum:* A nonconstant antiholomorphic function cannot attain a local
 maximum of $|f|$ in the interior.

Both follow by applying the corresponding holomorphic statement to $\overline{f}
$, using $|\overline{f}| = |f|$.

**Conclusion.** The maximum modulus principle holds verbatim for antiholomorphic
 functions: a nonconstant antiholomorphic function on a connected open set canno
t attain a local maximum of its modulus in the interior. The proof reduces to th
e holomorphic case via complex conjugation, which preserves the modulus.
