## Proof

We prove a slightly stronger lemma by induction, then apply it.

**Lemma.** For every integer $k \ge 0$, among any $2^{k+1}-1$ integers one can f
ind $2^k$ whose sum is divisible by $2^k$.

**Proof of Lemma.** By induction on $k$.

*Base case* $k=0$: We have $2^{1}-1 = 1$ integer and must find $2^{0}=1$ integer
 whose sum (the integer itself) is divisible by $2^{0}=1$. Every integer is divi
sible by $1$, so this holds.

*Inductive step.* Suppose the lemma holds for some $k \ge 0$; we prove it for $k
+1$. Given $2^{k+2}-1$ integers, we must find $2^{k+1}$ of them whose sum is div
isible by $2^{k+1}$.

We extract three pairwise-disjoint blocks, each of size $2^k$ and each with sum
divisible by $2^k$, by applying the inductive hypothesis three times:

1. From the $2^{k+2}-1$ integers (which is $\ge 2^{k+1}-1$), the inductive hypot
hesis yields a block $B_1$ of $2^k$ integers with $\sum_{B_1}\equiv 0 \pmod{2^k}
$. Removing $B_1$ leaves $2^{k+2}-1-2^k = 3\cdot 2^k - 1$ integers.

2. Since $3\cdot 2^k - 1 \ge 2^{k+1}-1$, applying the inductive hypothesis again
 yields a block $B_2$ of $2^k$ integers with $\sum_{B_2}\equiv 0 \pmod{2^k}$, di
sjoint from $B_1$. Removing $B_2$ leaves $2^{k+1}-1$ integers.

3. Applying the inductive hypothesis a third time to the remaining $2^{k+1}-1$ i
ntegers yields a block $B_3$ of $2^k$ integers with $\sum_{B_3}\equiv 0 \pmod{2^
k}$, disjoint from $B_1$ and $B_2$.

Write $\sum_{B_i} = 2^k\, m_i$ for integers $m_1, m_2, m_3$. Among three integer
s $m_1, m_2, m_3$, by the pigeonhole principle at least two have the same parity
; relabel so that $m_1 \equiv m_2 \pmod{2}$. Then $B_1 \cup B_2$ contains $2^k +
 2^k = 2^{k+1}$ integers, and its sum is

$$\sum_{B_1 \cup B_2} = 2^k(m_1 + m_2).$$

Since $m_1 + m_2$ is even, $2^k(m_1+m_2)$ is divisible by $2^{k+1}$. This comple
tes the inductive step, proving the Lemma. $\square$

**Applying the Lemma to the problem.** Set $k = 10$. The Lemma states that among
 any $2^{11}-1 = 2047$ integers, there exist $2^{10} = 1024$ whose sum is divisi
ble by $2^{10} = 1024$.

We are given $2048$ integers. Since $2048 \ge 2047$, select any $2047$ of them a
nd apply the Lemma: it produces $1024$ integers (among those $2047$, hence among
 the original $2048$) whose sum is divisible by $1024$.

Therefore, among any $2048$ integers, one can always find $1024$ whose sum is di
visible by $1024$. $\blacksquare$
