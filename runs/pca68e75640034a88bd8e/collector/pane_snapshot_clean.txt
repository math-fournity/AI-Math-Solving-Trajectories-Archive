## Proof

**Claim.** Yes. There exist constants $0 < A \le B$ such that
$$A\|f\|^{2}\le \langle Sf,f\rangle \le B\|f\|^{2},\qquad \forall f\in H.$$

Concretely one may take $B=\|S\|$ and $A=1/\|S^{-1}\|$.

---

**Step 1. $S$ is self-adjoint.**

For $f,g\in H$, using the definition $Sf=\sum_{j\in J}\langle f,e_j\rangle e_j$
(the series converges since $S$ is bounded, hence the partial sums define $Sf$)
and continuity of the inner product,
$$
\langle Sf,g\rangle=\sum_{j\in J}\langle f,e_j\rangle\langle e_j,g\rangle.
$$
Likewise
$$
\langle f,Sg\rangle=\sum_{j\in J}\langle g,e_j\rangle\langle f,e_j\rangle.
$$
Since the summands are scalars and $\langle e_j,g\rangle=\overline{\langle g,e_j
\rangle}$, we have $\langle f,e_j\rangle\langle e_j,g\rangle=\langle g,e_j\rangl
e\langle f,e_j\rangle$. Hence $\langle Sf,g\rangle=\langle f,Sg\rangle$ for all
$f,g$, so $S=S^{*}$.

**Step 2. $S$ is positive.**

For every $f\in H$,
$$
\langle Sf,f\rangle=\sum_{j\in J}\langle f,e_j\rangle\langle e_j,f\rangle=\sum_{
j\in J}\bigl|\langle f,e_j\rangle\bigr|^{2}\ge 0.
$$
Thus $S\ge 0$.

**Step 3. Upper bound.**

By Cauchy–Schwarz and $\|Sf\|\le\|S\|\,\|f\|$,
$$
\langle Sf,f\rangle\le \|Sf\|\,\|f\|\le \|S\|\,\|f\|^{2}.
$$
So $B:=\|S\|<\infty$ works.

**Step 4. Lower bound.**

Since $S$ is a positive self-adjoint bounded operator, it admits a unique positi
ve self-adjoint square root $S^{1/2}$, and because $S$ is invertible with bounde
d inverse (bounded by the bounded inverse theorem, $S$ being a bounded bijection
 of $H$), $S^{-1}$ is bounded; moreover $S^{-1}$ is positive self-adjoint, so $S
^{-1/2}$ is a well-defined bounded positive self-adjoint operator with $\|S^{-1/
2}\|^{2}=\|S^{-1}\|$.

Write
$$
\langle Sf,f\rangle=\langle S^{1/2}f,S^{1/2}f\rangle=\bigl\|S^{1/2}f\bigr\|^{2}.
$$
Since $f=S^{-1/2}(S^{1/2}f)$,
$$
\|f\|=\bigl\|S^{-1/2}(S^{1/2}f)\bigr\|\le \bigl\|S^{-1/2}\bigr\|\,\bigl\|S^{1/2}
f\bigr\|.
$$
Therefore
$$
\bigl\|S^{1/2}f\bigr\|\ge \frac{\|f\|}{\|S^{-1/2}\|},
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
\langle Sf,f\rangle=\bigl\|S^{1/2}f\bigr\|^{2}\ge \frac{\|f\|^{2}}{\|S^{-1/2}\|^
{2}}=\frac{1}{\|S^{-1}\|}\|f\|^{2}.
$$
Set $A:=1/\|S^{-1}\|>0$.

**Step 5. $A\le B$.**

For a positive self-adjoint operator, $\|S\|=\sup_{\|f\|=1}\langle Sf,f\rangle$
and $\|S^{-1}\|^{-1}=\inf_{\|f\|=1}\langle Sf,f\rangle$, so $A\le B$ automatical
ly. (Equivalently, $\|S\|\,\|S^{-1}\|\ge 1$.)

---

Combining Steps 3–5, for all $f\in H$,
$$
\frac{1}{\|S^{-1}\|}\|f\|^{2}\le \langle Sf,f\rangle\le \|S\|\,\|f\|^{2},
$$
with $0<1/\|S^{-1}\|\le \|S\|<\infty$. Hence such constants $0<A\le B$ do exist.

**Remark.** The crux is that the *form* of $S$ — a sum of rank-one terms $\langl
e\,\cdot\,,e_j\rangle e_j$ — forces $S$ to be self-adjoint and positive *automat
ically*, independent of any frame hypothesis. Boundedness gives the upper bound;
 bounded invertibility (which, for a positive self-adjoint operator, prevents th
e spectrum from accumulating at $0$) gives the strictly positive lower bound. No
 additional assumption (such as $E$ already being a frame) is needed.
