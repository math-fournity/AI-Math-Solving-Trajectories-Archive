## Substitution

Let $y_i = x_i + (i-1)$. Then:
- $\sum y_i = \sum x_i + \sum_{i=1}^{8}(i-1) = 32 + 28 = 60$.
- The constraint $x_i \ge 0$ gives $y_i \ge i-1$.
- The constraint $x_{i+1} \ge x_i$ gives $y_{i+1} - i \ge y_i - (i-1)$, i.e., $y
_{i+1} \ge y_i + 1$ (strict increase).

So I need to **maximize** $\sum y_i^2$ subject to $\sum y_i = 60$, $y_1 \ge 0$,
and $y_{i+1} \ge y_i + 1$ (integers).

Note: $y_i \ge i-1$ is redundant since it follows from $y_1 \ge 0$ and the stric

## Convexity and the Exchange Argument

The function $f(y) = \sum y_i^2$ is **convex**. The feasible set (integer points
 in a polytope defined by linear constraints) has its maximum of a convex object
ive at a **vertex**.

**Exchange argument (direct):** Suppose in a feasible solution, some $y_j$ with
$j < 8$ has slack (i.e., $y_j > y_{j-1}+1$ or $j=1$ with $y_1 > 0$). Decrease $y
_j$ by 1 and increase $y_8$ by 1. Feasibility is maintained (strict increase pre
served since we only decrease a non-tight value, and $y_8$ only grows). The chan
ge in objective is:
$$(y_8+1)^2 + (y_j-1)^2 - y_8^2 - y_j^2 = 2(y_8 - y_j) + 2 > 0$$
since $y_8 > y_j$. So pushing mass to $y_8$ always increases the objective. Repe
ating until $y_1, \ldots, y_7$ are at their minimum forces the optimum to the mo
st concentrated configuration.
