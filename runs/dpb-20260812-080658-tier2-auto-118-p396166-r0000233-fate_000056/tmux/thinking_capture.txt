Problem. Let $n \geq 2$ be fixed, and let $\sigma \in S_n$ be a fixed odd
permutation. Show that every odd permutation in $S_n$ is a product of $\sigma$
and some permutation in $A_n$.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Proof.

Let $\tau$ be any odd permutation in $S_n$. We must show that $\tau = \sigma
\alpha$ for some $\alpha \in A_n$.

Since $\sigma$ is odd, $\sigma^{-1}$ is also odd (the sign homomorphism $
\operatorname{sgn} : S_n \to {\pm 1}$ satisfies $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma^{-1})
= \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)^{-1} = \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma) = -1$).

Set $$\alpha := \sigma^{-1} \tau.$$

Then: $$\sigma \alpha = \sigma(\sigma^{-1}\tau) = (\sigma\sigma^{-1})\tau =
\tau.$$

It remains only to verify that $\alpha \in A_n$, i.e., that $\alpha$ is even.
Using the sign homomorphism: $$\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha) = \operatorname{sgn}
(\sigma^{-1}\tau) = \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma^{-1})\cdot\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)
= (-1)\cdot(-1) = +1.$$

Hence $\alpha$ is even, so $\alpha \in A_n$.

Since $\tau$ was an arbitrary odd permutation, we conclude that every odd
permutation in $S_n$ can be written as $\sigma$ times an element of $A_n$. $
\blacksquare$

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Remark (uniqueness). The element $\alpha \in A_n$ is in fact uniquely determined
by $\tau$: if $\sigma\alpha_1 = \sigma\alpha_2 = \tau$ with $\alpha_1, \alpha_2
\in A_n$, then left-cancelling $\sigma$ gives $\alpha_1 = \alpha_2$. Thus the
map $\alpha \mapsto \sigma\alpha$ is a bijection from $A_n$ onto the set of odd
permutations of $S_n$. This also recovers the well-known fact that $|S_n| = 2|
A_n|$, since the odd permutations form a coset $\sigma A_n$ of the index-2
subgroup $A_n$.
