## Proof

We need the least positive integer $A$ such that the triangle with sides $S=12$,
 $W=9$, and $A$ is acute (every internal angle strictly less than $90°$).

**Acute triangle condition.** A triangle with sides $a, b, c$ (where $c$ is the
largest) is acute if and only if
$$c^2 < a^2 + b^2.$$
(This is the Law of Cosines: the angle opposite $c$ is acute iff $c^2 < a^2+b^2$
; and if the largest angle is acute, all angles are acute.)

**Triangle inequality.** We also need a valid triangle:
$$A + 9 > 12 \implies A > 3 \implies A \ge 4,$$
$$A + 12 > 9 \;\text{(always true for } A>0\text{)}, \qquad 9 + 12 > A \implies
A < 21.$$

**Applying the acute condition.** The largest side is $12$ (we will verify this
holds for the candidate). The acute condition requires
$$12^2 < 9^2 + A^2 \implies 144 < 81 + A^2 \implies A^2 > 63 \implies A > \sqrt{
63} \approx 7.937.$$

Since $A$ is a positive integer, this gives $A \ge 8$.

**Verification that $A=8$ works.** Sides are $12, 9, 8$. The largest side is $12
$.
- Triangle inequality: $8 + 9 = 17 > 12$ ✓, $8+12 > 9$ ✓, $9+12 > 8$ ✓.
- Acute check (largest side): $12^2 = 144 < 9^2 + 8^2 = 81 + 64 = 145$ ✓.
- Other angles: $9^2 = 81 < 12^2 + 8^2 = 208$ ✓; $8^2 = 64 < 12^2 + 9^2 = 225$ ✓
.

All three angles are strictly acute.

**Minimality.** For $A \le 7$, we have $A^2 \le 49 < 63$, so $12^2 = 144 > 81 +
A^2$, making the angle opposite side $12$ obtuse. Hence no $A \le 7$ yields an a
cute triangle.

Therefore the least positive integer $A$ is $\boxed{8}$.

### PROOF COMPLETE
