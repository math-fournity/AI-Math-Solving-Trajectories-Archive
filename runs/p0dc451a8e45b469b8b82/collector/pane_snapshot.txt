Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a bounded complementary com
ponent $C$ of a domain $U$ remains a complementary component of $U \cap D$, wher
e $D$ is an open disk containing $C$.

**Answer: The statement is TRUE.**

**Proof.**

Let $U \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ be a domain (open and connected) and let $C$ be a b
ounded complementary component of $U$, i.e., a bounded connected component of $\
mathbb{R}^2 \setminus U$. Let $D$ be an open disk containing $C$, and set $V = U
 \cap D$. We must show that $C$ is a connected component of $\mathbb{R}^2 \setmi
nus V$.

**Step 1: Setup and key observations.**

Set $A = \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus U$ and $B = \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus D$. Since $U$
 and $D$ are open, both $A$ and $B$ are closed in $\mathbb{R}^2$. By De Morgan's
 law:

$$\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus V = \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus (U \cap D) = (\mathbb{R}^2 \
setminus U) \cup (\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus D) = A \cup B.$$

Since $C$ is a component of the closed set $A$, it is closed in $A$, hence close
d in $\mathbb{R}^2$ (as $A$ is closed). Being closed and bounded, $C$ is **compa
ct**.

Since $C \subset D$ and $D$ is open, we have $C \cap B = \emptyset$. As $C$ is c
ompact and $B$ is closed with $C \cap B = \emptyset$, the distance between them
is positive:

$$\varepsilon := \operatorname{dist}(C, B) > 0.$$

**Step 2: Construct a separating function.**

Define $f: \mathbb{R}^2 \to [0,1]$ by $f(x) = \min\!\bigl(1,\; \operatorname{dis
t}(x, C)/\varepsilon\bigr)$. This is continuous, with $f|_C = 0$ and $f|_B = 1$.

**Step 3: Build a clopen set in $A \cup B$ that isolates $C$ within $A$.**

Let $O = f^{-1}\bigl([0, \tfrac{1}{3})\bigr) \cap (A \cup B)$. Since $f^{-1}\big
l([0,\tfrac{1}{3})\bigr) = \{x : \operatorname{dist}(x, C) < \varepsilon/3\}$ is
 open in $\mathbb{R}^2$, the set $O$ is open in $A \cup B$.

Since $f|_B = 1 \geq \tfrac{1}{3}$, we have $B \cap f^{-1}\bigl([0,\tfrac{1}{3})
\bigr) = \emptyset$, so $O \subset A$.

The complement $(A \cup B) \setminus O = (A \cup B) \cap f^{-1}\bigl([\tfrac{1}{
3}, 1]\bigr)$ is closed in $A \cup B$ (as $f^{-1}([\tfrac{1}{3},1])$ is closed i
n $\mathbb{R}^2$). Hence $O$ is also closed in $A \cup B$.

Therefore $O$ is **clopen** in $A \cup B$, with $C \subset O \subset A$.

**Step 4: Conclude that $C$ is a component of $A \cup B$.**

Let $K$ be the connected component of $A \cup B$ containing any point of $C$. Si
nce $C$ is connected (it is a component of $A$) and $C \subset A \cup B$, we hav
e $C \subset K$.

Since $O$ is clopen in $A \cup B$ and $K$ is connected with $K \cap O \supset C
\neq \emptyset$, we must have $K \subset O$.

Since $O \subset A$, the set $K$ lies entirely in $A$. Thus $K$ is a connected s
ubset of $A$ containing $C$. But $C$ is a **component** (maximal connected subse
t) of $A$, so $K \subset C$.

Combining $C \subset K$ and $K \subset C$, we obtain $K = C$.

**Conclusion.** $C$ is a connected component of $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus V = \mat
hbb{R}^2 \setminus (U \cap D)$, i.e., $C$ is a complementary component of $U \ca
p D$. $\blacksquare$
