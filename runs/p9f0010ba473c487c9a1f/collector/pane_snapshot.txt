## Proof

**Answer: Yes**, such a fixed $\epsilon > 0$ exists.

**Setup.** Parametrize $S^1 = \mathbb{R}/L\mathbb{Z}$ with arc-length parameter,
 so the geodesic distance $|s - t|$ on $S^1$ ranges over $[0, L/2]$. Since $\gam
ma$ is $C^1$, regular ($\gamma'(t) \neq 0$), and defined on the compact set $S^1
$:

- There exist constants $0 < m \leq M$ with $m \leq |\gamma'(t)| \leq M$ for all
 $t$.
- $\gamma'$ is uniformly continuous.

**Step 1: Local monotonicity of distance from $\gamma(s_0)$.**

By uniform continuity of $\gamma'$, choose $\delta \in (0, L/2)$ small enough th
at for all $u, t$ with $|u - t| < \delta$, the angle between $\gamma'(u)$ and $\
gamma'(t)$ is less than $\pi/2$, i.e., $\gamma'(u) \cdot \gamma'(t) > 0$.

Fix $s_0 \in S^1$. For $t \in (s_0, s_0 + \delta)$:

$$\frac{d}{dt}|\gamma(t) - \gamma(s_0)|^2 = 2\,(\gamma(t) - \gamma(s_0))\cdot \g
amma'(t) = 2\int_{s_0}^{t} \gamma'(u)\cdot \gamma'(t)\,du.$$

For every $u \in [s_0, t]$, we have $|u - t| \leq |t - s_0| < \delta$, so $\gamm
a'(u)\cdot\gamma'(t) > 0$. Hence the integral is positive, and $|\gamma(t) - \ga
mma(s_0)|^2$ is **strictly increasing** on $(s_0,\, s_0+\delta)$. By the symmetr
ic argument, it is strictly decreasing on $(s_0-\delta,\, s_0)$.

Therefore $|\gamma(t) - \gamma(s_0)|$ is **strictly increasing in $|t - s_0|$**
for $|t - s_0| < \delta$.

**Step 2: Positive separation of distant points.**

The set $K = \{(s,t) \in S^1 \times S^1 : |s - t| \geq \delta\}$ is compact (clo
sed in $S^1\times S^1$). The function $(s,t) \mapsto |\gamma(s) - \gamma(t)|$ is
 continuous and strictly positive on $K$: if $|\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)|=0$ then $\ga
mma(s)=\gamma(t)$, and injectivity of $\gamma$ forces $s=t$, contradicting $|s-t
|\geq\delta>0$. Hence it attains a positive minimum:

$$D := \min_{(s,t)\in K} |\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)| > 0.$$

**Step 3: Choice of $\epsilon$ and conclusion.**

Set $\epsilon = D/2 > 0$. Fix any $p = \gamma(s_0) \in \Gamma$ and consider the
preimage:

$$\gamma^{-1}(B_\epsilon(p)) = \{t \in S^1 : |\gamma(t) - \gamma(s_0)| < \epsilo
n\}.$$

- **It lies within $\{t : |t - s_0| < \delta\}$:** If $|t - s_0| \geq \delta$, t
hen $(s_0, t) \in K$, so $|\gamma(t)-\gamma(s_0)| \geq D > \epsilon$, excluding
$t$.

- **Within $\{t : |t - s_0| < \delta\}$, it is a single interval:** By Step 1, $
|\gamma(t)-\gamma(s_0)|$ is strictly increasing in $|t-s_0|$ on this region. Sin
ce $|\gamma(s_0\pm\delta)-\gamma(s_0)| \geq D > \epsilon$, the sublevel set $\{t
 : |\gamma(t)-\gamma(s_0)|<\epsilon\}$ is a single open interval $(s_0 - a,\; s_
0 + b)$ with $0 < a, b < \delta$.

Therefore $\gamma^{-1}(B_\epsilon(p))$ is a single connected arc in $S^1$, and

$$B_\epsilon(p)\cap\Gamma = \gamma\bigl(\gamma^{-1}(B_\epsilon(p))\bigr)$$

is the continuous image of a connected set, hence **connected**.

Since $s_0$ was arbitrary, this holds for every $p \in \Gamma$ with the single f
ixed $\epsilon = D/2 > 0$. $\blacksquare$
