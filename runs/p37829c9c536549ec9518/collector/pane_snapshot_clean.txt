## Proof

**Setup.** Let $ABC$ be an acute triangle with $P$ in its interior. Let $P_A, P_
B, P_C$ be the reflections of $P$ over sides $BC, CA, AB$ respectively. We are g
iven that $P$ is the orthocenter of $\triangle P_A P_B P_C$.

**Key observation.** Since $P_A$ is the reflection of $P$ over $BC$, the segment
 $PP_A$ is perpendicular to $BC$ (and bisected by it). Likewise $PP_B \perp CA$
and $PP_C \perp AB$.

For $P$ to be the orthocenter of $\triangle P_A P_B P_C$, the altitude from $P_A
$ must pass through $P$ and be perpendicular to $P_B P_C$. Since $PP_A \perp BC$
, this requires $P_B P_C \parallel BC$. Similarly, $P_A P_C \parallel CA$ and $P
_A P_B \parallel AB$.

**Coordinate computation.** Place $B = (0,0)$, $C = (a,0)$, $A = (u,v)$ with $v
> 0$, and let $P = (p,q)$. Set $s = a - u$.

- $P_A = (p, -q)$ (reflection over the $x$-axis $= BC$).
- Line $CA$: $vx + (a-u)y - va = 0$, i.e. $vx + sy - va = 0$.
- Line $AB$: $vx - uy = 0$.

Using the reflection formula, one computes:
$$P_B = \bigl(p - 2d_1 v,\; q - 2d_1 s\bigr), \quad d_1 = \frac{vp + sq - va}{v^
2 + s^2},$$
$$P_C = \bigl(p - 2d_2 v,\; q + 2d_2 u\bigr), \quad d_2 = \frac{vp - uq}{v^2 + u
^2}.$$

**Condition $P_B P_C \parallel BC$ (horizontal):** equal $y$-coordinates:
$$q - 2d_1 s = q + 2d_2 u \implies -d_1 s = d_2 u.$$

**Condition $P_A P_C \parallel CA$:** direction $P_C - P_A = (-2d_2 v,\; 2q + 2d
_2 u)$ must be proportional to $(-s, v)$:
$$\frac{d_2 v}{s} = \frac{q + d_2 u}{v} \implies d_2(v^2 - su) = sq.$$

Substituting $d_2 = \frac{vp - uq}{v^2 + u^2}$ into $d_2(v^2 - su) = sq$ and sim
plifying:
$$(vp - uq)(v^2 - su) = sq(v^2 + u^2).$$

Expanding the left side and collecting terms in $p$ and $q$:
$$vp(v^2 - su) = q\bigl[u(v^2 - su) + s(v^2 + u^2)\bigr] = q \cdot v^2(u+s) = qv
^2 a,$$
so (dividing by $v$):
$$\boxed{p(v^2 - su) = qva.} \quad (\star)$$

From the first condition $-d_1 s = d_2 u$, after similar algebra (or directly su
bstituting $q = \frac{p(v^2-su)}{va}$ from $(\star)$), one obtains:
$$p(v^2 + su) - qv(u-s) = s(v^2 + u^2). \quad (\star\star)$$

Substituting $q = \frac{p(v^2 - su)}{va}$ into $(\star\star)$:
$$p(v^2 + su) - \frac{p(v^2-su)}{va}\cdot v(u-s) = s(v^2+u^2),$$
$$p\left[(v^2+su) - \frac{(v^2-su)(u-s)}{a}\right] = s(v^2+u^2).$$

The bracket simplifies (using $a = u+s$):
$$\frac{(v^2+su)(u+s) - (v^2-su)(u-s)}{a} = \frac{2s(v^2+u^2)}{a}.$$

Therefore $p \cdot \frac{2s(v^2+u^2)}{a} = s(v^2+u^2)$, giving $p = \frac{a}{2}$
, and then $q = \frac{v^2 - su}{2v}$.

**This is exactly the circumcenter.** Indeed, the circumcenter of $B=(0,0)$, $C=
(a,0)$, $A=(u,v)$ lies on $x = a/2$ (perpendicular bisector of $BC$), and equati
ng $OA = OB$ gives $y_O = \frac{u^2+v^2-au}{2v} = \frac{v^2-su}{2v}$. ✓

Since the system $(\star), (\star\star)$ is linear in $(p,q)$ with a unique solu
tion, **$P$ must be the circumcenter of $\triangle ABC$**.

**Conclusion.** The circumcenter is equidistant from all three vertices:
$$PA = PB = PC = R,$$
where $R$ is the circumradius. Three equal segments always satisfy the triangle
inequality and form an **equilateral triangle**, in which every interior angle e
quals $60°$.

The largest interior angle is therefore $60°$, so:

$$\boxed{x = 60}.$$
