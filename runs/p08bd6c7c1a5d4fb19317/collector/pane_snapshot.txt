Yes. The intersection of a countable collection of dense open sets in ℝ can have
 non-zero (indeed infinite) Lebesgue measure.

**Construction.** Let the countable collection be \(\{U_q : q \in \mathbb{Q}\}\)
, where
\[
U_q \;=\; \mathbb{R}\setminus\{q\}.
\]

**Each \(U_q\) is open.** The singleton \(\{q\}\) is closed, so its complement \
(U_q\) is open.

**Each \(U_q\) is dense.** Removing a single point from \(\mathbb{R}\) leaves a
set whose closure is all of \(\mathbb{R}\); equivalently, every non-empty open i
nterval still contains points of \(U_q\).

**The collection is countable** because \(\mathbb{Q}\) is countable.

**Compute the intersection.**
\[
\bigcap_{q\in\mathbb{Q}} U_q \;=\; \bigcap_{q\in\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\
{q\}) \;=\; \mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}.
\]

**Measure of the intersection.** The rationals \(\mathbb{Q}\) are countable, hen
ce a countable union of singletons, each of Lebesgue measure \(0\). By countable
 subadditivity,
\[
m(\mathbb{Q}) \;=\; 0.
\]
Therefore
\[
m(\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}) \;=\; m(\mathbb{R}) - m(\mathbb{Q}) \;=\; \inft
y - 0 \;=\; \infty.
\]
In particular, on every bounded interval \([a,b]\),
\[
m\big((\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q})\cap[a,b]\big) \;=\; b-a \;>\; 0.
\]

So \(\bigcap_{q\in\mathbb{Q}} U_q = \mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}\) is a countab
le intersection of dense open sets whose Lebesgue measure is non-zero (in fact,
full measure).

**Remark (contrast).** This shows a comeager set (dense \(G_\delta\)) can have f
ull measure. The complementary phenomenon is also true: there exist meager sets
of full measure (e.g. a countable union of fat Cantor sets whose complements shr
ink in measure), whose complements are dense \(G_\delta\) sets of measure zero.
Thus a countable intersection of dense open sets can have measure \(0\), full me
asure, or — by mixing constructions — any prescribed measure behavior. The answe
r to the question is therefore **yes**.
