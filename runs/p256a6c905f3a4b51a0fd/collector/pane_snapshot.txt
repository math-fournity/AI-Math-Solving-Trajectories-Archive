## Answer

**Yes** — in the standard sense of "unique up to isomorphism and reordering of t
he summands." More precisely, the *multiset of isomorphism classes* of irreducib
le summands (with multiplicities) is uniquely determined. The actual subspaces a
re *not* unique when some irreducible occurs with multiplicity ≥ 2, but this non
-uniqueness is exactly the kind absorbed by "up to isomorphism and ordering."

Below is the proof.

---

### Setup

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebra over an algebr
aically closed field $k$ of characteristic $0$, and let $V$ be a finite-dimensio
nal $\mathfrak{g}$-module.

**Weyl's theorem (complete reducibility).** Every finite-dimensional $\mathfrak{
g}$-module is completely reducible, i.e.
$$V \cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^{n} V_i$$
where each $V_i$ is irreducible. (This uses char $0$ + alg. closed + semisimplic
ity of $\mathfrak{g}$: the trace form gives a non-degenerate invariant bilinear
form, Casimir elements act, and the unitarian trick / Casimir-splitting argument
 shows every submodule has a complementary submodule.)

So *existence* of such a decomposition is guaranteed. The question is *uniquenes
s*.

---

### Schur's lemma (in our setting)

Since $k$ is algebraically closed and each $V_i$ is finite-dimensional irreducib
le:

- **Schur's lemma:** $\operatorname{End}_{\mathfrak{g}}(V_i)$ is a finite-dimens
ional division algebra over $k$, hence (alg. closed) $\operatorname{End}_{\mathf
rak{g}}(V_i) \cong k$.
- Consequently $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g}}(V_i, V_j) = 0$ whenever $V_i \
not\cong V_j$ (as $\mathfrak{g}$-modules), and $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g}
}(V_i,V_i) \cong k$.

---

### Uniqueness of multiplicities

Let $\{S_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in A}$ be a set of representatives of the isomorphism
 classes of irreducible $\mathfrak{g}$-modules (only finitely many can occur in
a given finite-dimensional $V$). Write the isotypic decomposition
$$V = \bigoplus_{\alpha} V^{(\alpha)}, \qquad V^{(\alpha)} \cong S_\alpha^{\oplu
s m_\alpha}.$$

**Claim.** $m_\alpha = \dim_k \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g}}(S_\alpha, V)$, w
hich depends only on $V$ and $S_\alpha$, not on any chosen decomposition.

*Proof of claim.* By additivity of Hom in the second variable and Schur's lemma,
$$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g}}(S_\alpha, V) \cong \bigoplus_{\beta} \operat
orname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g}}(S_\alpha, V^{(\beta)}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_{\mat
hfrak{g}}(S_\alpha, S_\alpha^{\oplus m_\alpha}) \cong \operatorname{End}_{\mathf
rak{g}}(S_\alpha)^{\oplus m_\alpha} \cong k^{m_\alpha}.$$
Hence $\dim_k \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g}}(S_\alpha, V) = m_\alpha$. ∎

Since the right-hand side is defined intrinsically from $V$, the integer $m_\alp
ha$ is an invariant of $V$. Therefore:

> **The multiset $\{S_\alpha \text{ with multiplicity } m_\alpha\}$ is uniquely
determined by $V$.**

Equivalently, given any two decompositions
$$V = V_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus V_n = V'_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus V'_m$$
into irreducibles, we have $n = m$ and there is a permutation $\sigma$ of $\{1,\
dots,n\}$ with $V_i \cong V'_{\sigma(i)}$ as $\mathfrak{g}$-modules for all $i$.

This is exactly the statement: **the decomposition is unique up to isomorphism a
nd reordering of the summands.**

---

### Remark on what is *not* unique

The actual subspaces $V_i \subseteq V$ are generally not unique. For instance, i
f $V \cong S \oplus S$ for an irreducible $S$ with $\dim S > 0$, then $V \cong k
^2 \otimes_k S$ as a $\mathfrak{g}$-module (with $\mathfrak{g}$ acting trivially
 on $k^2$), and *any* decomposition $k^2 = \ell_1 \oplus \ell_2$ into two lines
gives a decomposition $V = (\ell_1 \otimes S) \oplus (\ell_2 \otimes S)$ into tw
o copies of $S$. There is a $\mathrm{PGL}_2(k)$-family of such splittings, none
canonical. So the literal subrepresentations are not unique; only their isomorph
ism classes and multiplicities are.

This is the same phenomenon as in the Krull–Schmidt theorem: uniqueness is *up t
o isomorphism and permutation*, not as concrete subobjects.

---

### Conclusion

For a finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebra over an algebraically closed fie
ld of characteristic $0$:

1. **Existence** of a decomposition into irreducibles holds (Weyl's theorem).
2. **Uniqueness up to isomorphism and ordering** holds: the multiplicities $m_\a
lpha = \dim \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g}}(S_\alpha, V)$ are intrinsic invari
ants of $V$, so any two irreducible decompositions have the same summands with t
he same multiplicities, up to permutation and isomorphism.

Hence the answer to the question (in the standard "up to isomorphism and reorder
ing" sense) is **yes**.
