## Proof

Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be an elliptic curve with conductor $N$. The **Hasse–Weil con
jecture** (now the **Modularity Theorem**, proved by Wiles, Taylor–Wiles, and Br
euil–Conrad–Diamond–Taylor) asserts that $E$ is modular. Concretely, it predicts
 an equality of $L$-functions between $L(E,s)$ and the $L$-function of a suitabl
e cusp form of level $\Gamma_0(N)$.

### Setup

The Hasse–Weil $L$-function of $E$ is the Euler product

$$
L(E,s) \;=\; \prod_{p\mid N}\bigl(1 - a_p p^{-s}\bigr)^{-1}\prod_{p\nmid N}\bigl
(1 - a_p p^{-s} + p^{1-2s}\bigr)^{-1},
$$

where for $p\nmid N$ (good reduction) $a_p = p+1-\#\widetilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_p)$
is the trace of Frobenius, and for $p\mid N$ the local factor is determined by t
he reduction type (multiplicative: $a_p=\pm1$; additive: $a_p=0$). This is the $
L$-function attached to the $\ell$-adic Galois representation $\rho_{E,\ell}:G_{
\mathbb{Q}}\to \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ on $H^1_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(E_{\
bar{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$.

### The modular form

Let $\mathcal{M}_1(\Gamma_0(N))^{0}$ denote the space of **cusp forms** (the sup
erscript $0$) of weight $2$ and level $\Gamma_0(N)$ with trivial nebentypus (the
 subscript $1$ indexes the $H^1$-type, i.e. weight $2$, motive). Inside it sits
the subspace of **newforms** $S_2^{\mathrm{new}}(\Gamma_0(N))$. A normalized new
form

$$
f(q)=\sum_{n\ge 1} a_n q^n\in S_2^{\mathrm{new}}(\Gamma_0(N)),\qquad a_1=1,
$$

is a simultaneous eigenform for all Hecke operators $T_p$ ($p\nmid N$) and $U_p$
 ($p\mid N$). Its $L$-function is

$$
L(f,s)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{a_n}{n^s}=\prod_{p\mid N}(1-a_p p^{-s})^{-1}\prod_{p\nm
id N}(1-a_p p^{-s}+p^{1-2s})^{-1}.
$$

### The relationship

The Hasse–Weil conjecture states that **there exists a normalized newform** $f\i
n\mathcal{M}_1(\Gamma_0(N))^{0}$ (i.e. a weight-$2$ newform of level $\Gamma_0(N
)$, trivial character) such that

$$
\boxed{\,L(E,s)=L(f,s)\,}
$$

as complex functions (indeed as entire functions, both admitting analytic contin
uation and satisfying the same functional equation $s\leftrightarrow 2-s$).

Equivalently, identifying the Fourier coefficients of $f$ with the Frobenius tra
ces of $E$:

$$
a_n(f)=a_n(E)\quad\text{for all }n\ge1,
$$

so that the Dirichlet series and Euler products coincide term-by-term. In partic
ular, for every prime $p\nmid N$,

$$
a_p(f)=p+1-\#\widetilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_p),
$$

and for $p\mid N$ the coefficient $a_p(f)$ matches the local Euler factor prescr
ibed by the reduction type of $E$ at $p$.

### Why this is the precise statement

The equality $L(E,s)=L(f,s)$ is equivalent (via the Eichler–Shimura–Deligne cons
truction) to an isomorphism of two-dimensional $\ell$-adic Galois representation
s

$$
\rho_{E,\ell}\;\cong\;\rho_{f,\ell},
$$

where $\rho_{f,\ell}$ is the Galois representation attached to $f$. The conducto
r of $\rho_{E,\ell}$ is exactly $N$ (by the definition of the conductor of $E$),
 which forces the level of $f$ to be $\Gamma_0(N)$; the determinant of $\rho_{E,
\ell}$ is the cyclotomic character, which forces the nebentypus to be trivial an
d the weight to be $2$; and the cuspidality (the $0$ in $\mathcal{M}_1(\Gamma_0(
N))^0$) corresponds to $E$ having no CM-split Eisenstein part, i.e. $L(E,s)$ bei
ng a genuine cusp-form $L$-function rather than an Eisenstein one.

Thus the Hasse–Weil conjecture asserts precisely:

> $L(E,s)$ is the $L$-function of a normalized newform $f\in\mathcal{M}_1(\Gamma
_0(N))^{0}$ of weight $2$, level $\Gamma_0(N)$, and trivial character; equivalen
tly $L(E,s)=L(f,s)$ with $a_n(f)=a_n(E)$ for all $n$.
