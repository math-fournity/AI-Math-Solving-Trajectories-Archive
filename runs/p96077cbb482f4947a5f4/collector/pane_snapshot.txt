We evaluate

$$\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{C_R}\frac{e^{iz^{2}}}{1+z^{4}}\,dz,$$

where $C_R$ is the arc $\{z=Re^{i\theta}:0\le\theta\le\pi/2\}$.

**Parametrization.** With $z=Re^{i\theta}$, $dz=iRe^{i\theta}\,d\theta$, and

$$z^{2}=R^{2}e^{2i\theta}=R^{2}(\cos 2\theta+i\sin 2\theta),$$

so

$$e^{iz^{2}}=e^{iR^{2}\cos 2\theta}\,e^{-R^{2}\sin 2\theta},\qquad |e^{iz^{2}}|=
e^{-R^{2}\sin 2\theta}.$$

Since $\theta\in[0,\pi/2]$, we have $2\theta\in[0,\pi]$, hence $\sin 2\theta\ge
0$ and $|e^{iz^{2}}|\le 1$.

**Bound on the denominator.** For $R>1$,

$$|1+z^{4}|=|1+R^{4}e^{4i\theta}|\ge R^{4}-1.$$

**Estimate of the integral.** Therefore

$$\left|\int_{C_R}\frac{e^{iz^{2}}}{1+z^{4}}\,dz\right|
\le \frac{R}{R^{4}-1}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}e^{-R^{2}\sin 2\theta}\,d\theta.$$

It remains to bound the exponential integral. Using Jordan's inequality $\sin x\
ge \frac{2x}{\pi}$ for $x\in[0,\pi/2]$:

- For $\theta\in[0,\pi/4]$: $2\theta\in[0,\pi/2]$, so $\sin 2\theta\ge \frac{4\t
heta}{\pi}$.
- For $\theta\in[\pi/4,\pi/2]$: writing $\phi=\pi/2-\theta\in[0,\pi/4]$, $\sin 2
\theta=\sin 2\phi\ge \frac{4\phi}{\pi}=\frac{4(\pi/2-\theta)}{\pi}$.

By symmetry of $\sin 2\theta$ about $\theta=\pi/4$,

$$\int_{0}^{\pi/2}e^{-R^{2}\sin 2\theta}\,d\theta
=2\int_{0}^{\pi/4}e^{-R^{2}\sin 2\theta}\,d\theta
\le 2\int_{0}^{\pi/4}e^{-4R^{2}\theta/\pi}\,d\theta
=2\cdot\frac{\pi}{4R^{2}}\Bigl(1-e^{-R^{2}}\Bigr)
\le \frac{\pi}{2R^{2}}.$$

**Combining.**

$$\left|\int_{C_R}\frac{e^{iz^{2}}}{1+z^{4}}\,dz\right|
\le \frac{R}{R^{4}-1}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2R^{2}}
=\frac{\pi R}{2R^{2}(R^{4}-1)}
=\frac{\pi}{2R(R^{4}-1)}\;\xrightarrow[R\to\infty]{}\;0.$$

Hence

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{C_R}\frac{e^{iz^{2}}}{1+z^{4}}\,dz=
0.}$$
