## Proof

**Claim.** The Cantor cube $C = \{0,1\}^I$ is a closed subset of the Hilbert cub
e $H = [0,1]^I$ (equipped with the product topology), for **any** index set $I$.

**Proof.**

Endow $H = [0,1]^I$ with the product topology, and let $\pi_i : H \to [0,1]$ den
ote the projection onto the $i$-th coordinate, for each $i \in I$. Each $\pi_i$
is continuous (projections are continuous in the product topology).

**Step 1. $\{0,1\}$ is closed in $[0,1]$.**

The set $\{0,1\} \subseteq [0,1]$ is finite, and $[0,1]$ is Hausdorff (hence $T_
1$); every finite subset of a $T_1$ space is closed. Equivalently, its complemen
t $(0,1)$ is open in $[0,1]$.

**Step 2. Each cylinder $\pi_i^{-1}(\{0,1\})$ is closed in $H$.**

Since $\pi_i$ is continuous and $\{0,1\}$ is closed in $[0,1]$, the preimage
$$\pi_i^{-1}(\{0,1\}) = \{x \in H : x_i \in \{0,1\}\}$$
is closed in $H$.

**Step 3. $C$ is an intersection of closed sets, hence closed.**

A point $x \in H$ belongs to $C = \{0,1\}^I$ if and only if $x_i \in \{0,1\}$ fo
r **every** $i \in I$. Therefore
$$C = \{0,1\}^I = \bigcap_{i \in I} \pi_i^{-1}(\{0,1\}).$$
Each $\pi_i^{-1}(\{0,1\})$ is closed by Step 2, and an **arbitrary** intersectio
n of closed sets is closed (this holds in any topological space, regardless of w
hether $I$ is finite, countable, or uncountable). Hence $C$ is closed in $H$.

**Step 4 (alternative, via the complement).** For completeness, one may also ver
ify directly that $H \setminus C$ is open. If $x \notin C$, then there exists $i
_0 \in I$ with $x_{i_0} \in (0,1)$. The subbasic open set $\pi_{i_0}^{-1}\big((0
,1)\big)$ contains $x$ and is entirely contained in $H \setminus C$ (every point
 in it has its $i_0$-th coordinate in $(0,1)$, hence is not in $\{0,1\}^I$). Thu
s every point of $H \setminus C$ is an interior point, so $H \setminus C$ is ope
n, confirming $C$ is closed.

**Conclusion.** The Cantor cube $C = \{0,1\}^I$ is a closed subset of the Hilber
t cube $H = [0,1]^I$ for every index set $I$. $\blacksquare$
