## Why $R^i_{\ jlm}$ Is "Mixed" and How Its Transformation Law Establishes Tenso
r-Valuedness

### 1. Setup: the affine connection and its non-tensorial transformation

In affine geometry we are given a connection $\nabla$ on the tangent bundle, wit
h components $\Gamma^i_{\ jk}$ in a coordinate chart $\{x^i\}$, but **no metric*
* $g_{ij}$. Under a smooth change of coordinates $x^i \mapsto x'^i$ the connecti
on coefficients transform as

$$\Gamma'^{i}_{\ jk} \;=\; \underbrace{\frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^a}\,\frac
{\partial x^b}{\partial x'^j}\,\frac{\partial x^c}{\partial x'^k}\,\Gamma^a_{\ b
c}}_{\text{tensorial part}} \;+\; \underbrace{\frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^a}
\,\frac{\partial^2 x^a}{\partial x'^j\,\partial x'^k}}_{\text{inhomogeneous part
 } K^i_{\ jk}}.$$

The inhomogeneous term $K^i_{\ jk}$ is symmetric in $j,k$ (mixed partials commut
e) and is **not** a tensor. Hence $\Gamma^i_{\ jk}$ itself is **not** a tensor —
 it is an affine connection coefficient.

### 2. Definition of the curvature components

The curvature of $\nabla$ is the $(1,3)$-tensor field $R(X,Y)Z = \nabla_X\nabla_
Y Z - \nabla_Y\nabla_X Z - \nabla_{[X,Y]}Z$. In coordinates its components are

$$R^i_{\ jlm} \;=\; \partial_l\,\Gamma^i_{\ jm} - \partial_m\,\Gamma^i_{\ jl} +
\Gamma^i_{\ al}\,\Gamma^a_{\ jm} - \Gamma^i_{\ am}\,\Gamma^a_{\ jl}.$$

It carries **one upper (contravariant) index** $i$ and **three lower (covariant)
 indices** $j,l,m$. A tensor possessing indices of both kinds is called a **mixe
d tensor**; $R^i_{\ jlm}$ is of type $(1,3)$, i.e. mixed of valence $+1-3$.

### 3. Why "mixed" is irreducible in affine geometry

In Riemannian geometry one uses the metric $g_{ij}$ to lower the contravariant i
ndex and form the fully covariant $(0,4)$ tensor $R_{ijlm} = g_{ik}\,R^k_{\ jlm}
$. **In affine geometry there is no metric**, so there is no canonical way to ra
ise or lower indices. The upper index $i$ and the lower indices $j,l,m$ live in
genuinely different spaces (the tangent bundle $TM$ and its dual $T^*M$), and no
 structure is available to identify them. Therefore the mixed $(1,3)$ character
of $R^i_{\ jlm}$ is **irreducible**: it cannot be converted into a purely covari
ant or purely contravariant form. This is the precise sense in which the curvatu
re is "mixed" in the affine setting.

### 4. The transformation law: inhomogeneous terms cancel

Substituting the transformation law of $\Gamma$ into the definition of $R'^i_{\
jlm}$, every term splits into a "tensorial × tensorial" piece, a "tensorial × $K
$" piece, and a "$K$-only" piece. The cross terms and the $K$-only terms combine
 into

$$\partial'_l\,K^i_{\ jm} - \partial'_m\,K^i_{\ jl} + K^i_{\ al}\,K^a_{\ jm} - K
^i_{\ am}\,K^a_{\ jl}.$$

**This expression vanishes identically.** To see this, write $J^i_{\ a} = \parti
al x'^i/\partial x^a$ and $(J^{-1})^a_{\ j} = \partial x^a/\partial x'^j$, so th
at $K^i_{\ jk} = J^i_{\ a}\,\partial'_j(J^{-1})^a_{\ k}$. Define the matrix-valu
ed 1-form $\theta = J\,d(J^{-1})$, i.e. $\theta^i_{\ k} = K^i_{\ jk}\,dx'^j$. Fr
om $J\cdot J^{-1}=I$ we get $\theta = -\,dJ\cdot J^{-1}$, and then

$$d\theta = -d(dJ\cdot J^{-1}) = dJ\wedge dJ^{-1} = -\,dJ\,J^{-1}\wedge dJ\,J^{-
1} = -\,\theta\wedge\theta,$$

which is the **Maurer–Cartan equation** $d\theta + \theta\wedge\theta = 0$. In c
omponents this is exactly the vanishing of the $K$-combination above. (Equivalen
tly, it follows from $d^2(J^{-1})=0$ together with the Leibniz rule.) Thus **all
 inhomogeneous contributions cancel**, and only the tensorial part survives:

$$\boxed{R'^{i}_{\ jlm} \;=\; \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^a}\,\frac{\partial
 x^b}{\partial x'^j}\,\frac{\partial x^c}{\partial x'^l}\,\frac{\partial x^d}{\p
artial x'^m}\,R^a_{\ bcd}.}$$

### 5. How the transformation law establishes tensor-valuedness

The surviving transformation law is **homogeneous** (no additive term): it is pr
ecisely the transformation rule of a **mixed tensor of type $(1,3)$**. Two facts
 are thereby established simultaneously:

1. **Tensor-valuedness.** A quantity is a tensor if and only if it transforms *h
omogeneously* under coordinate changes, i.e. as a multilinear map on vectors and
 covectors. The cancellation of every inhomogeneous term — guaranteed by the Mau
rer–Cartan identity — is what promotes $R^i_{\ jlm}$ from "a combination of non-
tensorial connection coefficients" to "a genuine tensor." The curvature is a ten
sor *because* its transformation is purely homogeneous, even though it is built
from $\Gamma^i_{\ jk}$, which is not.

2. **Mixed character.** The *form* of the homogeneous law — one factor $\partial
 x'/\partial x$ (contravariant, for the index $i$) and three factors $\partial x
/\partial x'$ (covariant, for $j,l,m$) — is exactly what "mixed" means. The tran
sformation law does not merely confirm tensoriality in the abstract; it specifie
s the *type* $(1,3)$, recording that the curvature eats three covectors and one
vector (or equivalently, produces one vector from three covectors and one vector
).

### 6. Summary

- **"Mixed"** denotes that $R^i_{\ jlm}$ has one contravariant and three covaria
nt indices — type $(1,3)$. In affine geometry, lacking a metric, this mixed char
acter is **irreducible**: the upper and lower indices cannot be interconverted,
so the tensor is intrinsically "mixed" rather than purely covariant or contravar
iant.

- **Transformation properties ↔ tensor-valuedness.** The connection $\Gamma$ tra
nsforms inhomogeneously and is not a tensor. The specific antisymmetric combinat
ion defining $R^i_{\ jlm}$ causes every inhomogeneous term to cancel (via the Ma
urer–Cartan equation $d\theta+\theta\wedge\theta=0$), leaving a **purely homogen
eous** transformation law. Homogeneity of the transformation law *is* the criter
ion for tensor-valuedness, and the particular pattern of Jacobian factors (one $
\partial x'/\partial x$, three $\partial x/\partial x'$) *is* the criterion for
the mixed type $(1,3)$. Thus the transformation law simultaneously proves that $
R^i_{\ jlm}$ is a tensor and characterizes it as mixed — the two properties are
two facets of the same homogeneous transformation formula.
