## Part 1: M = maximum of S_p

**Coefficient analysis.** For a permutation, write $S_p = \sum_v c_v \cdot v$, w
here each edge $|a_i - a_{i+1}|$ contributes $+1$ to the larger endpoint and $-1
$ to the smaller. Then:
- Interior local maximum: $c = +2$
- Interior local minimum: $c = -2$
- Interior monotone-through point: $c = 0$
- Endpoint larger than neighbor: $c = +1$; endpoint smaller: $c = -1$

The coefficients satisfy $\sum c_v = 0$, with exactly 2 endpoints ($|c|=1$) and
10 interior vertices ($|c| \in \{0, 2\}$).

**Optimal assignment.** To maximize $S = \sum c_v \cdot v$, assign $+2$ to the l
argest values and $-2$ to the smallest. The unique optimum (verified by checking
 all feasible coefficient patterns):

$$c = +2 \text{ on } \{8,9,10,11,12\},\quad c = -2 \text{ on } \{1,2,3,4,5\},\qu
ad c = +1 \text{ on } 7,\quad c = -1 \text{ on } 6.$$

$$M = 2(8+9+10+11+12) + 7 - 2(1+2+3+4+5) - 6 = 100 + 7 - 30 - 6 = \boxed{71}.$$

All other feasible patterns yield $\leq 69$ (e.g., allowing one $c=0$ interior p
oint gives at most 69), so 71 is the unique maximum, achieved only by **zigzag**
 (alternating) permutations with this exact value assignment.

## Part 2: N = number of permutations achieving M = 71

The structure requires a zigzag with:
- 5 interior peaks $\in \{8,9,10,11,12\}$ (any order): $5!$ ways
- 5 interior valleys $\in \{1,2,3,4,5\}$ (any order): $5!$ ways
- Endpoint peak $= 7$, endpoint valley $= 6$

**Case A:** Valley at position 1 ($=6$), peak at position 12 ($=7$): $5! \times
5! = 14400$.
**Case B:** Peak at position 1 ($=7$), valley at position 12 ($=6$): $5! \times
5! = 14400$.

$$N = 2 \times 5! \times 5! = \boxed{28800}.$$

## Part 3: K = number of optimistic permutations

The condition $a_i > \min(a_{i-1}, a_{i+1})$ for all interior $i$ means **no int
erior local minimum**.

**Characterization.** A permutation has no interior local minimum if and only if
 it is **unimodal** (first increasing, then decreasing — a single peak).

*Proof:* In a unimodal permutation, every interior position is either in the inc
reasing part ($a_i > a_{i-1} \geq \min$), the decreasing part ($a_i > a_{i+1} \g
eq \min$), or the peak ($a_i >$ both neighbors). Conversely, any non-unimodal pe
rmutation has a "down-then-up" pattern creating an interior valley (local minimu
m).

**Counting.** A unimodal permutation of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ has peak value $n$ at s
ome position. Each of the remaining $n-1$ elements independently goes left (plac
ed in increasing order) or right (placed in decreasing order):

$$K = 2^{11} = \boxed{2048}.$$

## Part 4: M_opt = maximum S_p for optimistic permutations

For a unimodal permutation with peak value 12 at position $k$, the left part is
increasing and the right part is decreasing, so the sum telescopes:

$$S_p = \underbrace{(a_k - a_1)}_{\text{increasing part}} + \underbrace{(a_k - a
_{12})}_{\text{decreasing part}} = 2 \cdot 12 - a_1 - a_{12} = 24 - a_1 - a_{12}
.$$

To maximize, minimize $a_1 + a_{12}$. The minimum is $1 + 2 = 3$ (achievable, e.
g., $1, 3, 4, \ldots, 11, 12, 2$):

$$M_{\text{opt}} = 24 - 3 = \boxed{21}.$$

## Part 5: N_opt = number of optimistic permutations achieving M_opt = 21

We need unimodal permutations with $\{a_1, a_{12}\} = \{1, 2\}$.

**Case 1:** $a_1 = 1$, $a_{12} = 2$. Peak (value 12) at position $k$, $2 \leq k
\leq 11$. The 9 elements $\{3,\ldots,11\}$ are split: $k-2$ go left, $9-(k-2)$ g
o right. Count: $\sum_{k=2}^{11} \binom{9}{k-2} = \sum_{j=0}^{9} \binom{9}{j} =
2^9 = 512$.

**Case 2:** $a_1 = 2$, $a_{12} = 1$. By symmetry: $512$.

$$N_{\text{opt}} = 512 + 512 = \boxed{1024}.$$
