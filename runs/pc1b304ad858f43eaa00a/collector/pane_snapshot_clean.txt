## Proof

We compute the infinite product
$$P = \prod_{p\in\mathbb{P}} \frac{p^4+1}{p^4-1}.$$

**Step 1: Rewrite each factor.** For each prime $p$, divide numerator and denomi
nator by $p^4$:
$$\frac{p^4+1}{p^4-1} = \frac{1+p^{-4}}{1-p^{-4}}.$$
Using the identity $(1+x) = \dfrac{1-x^2}{1-x}$ with $x = p^{-4}$,
$$\frac{1+p^{-4}}{1-p^{-4}} = \frac{1-p^{-8}}{(1-p^{-4})^2}.$$

**Step 2: Convert to Euler products.** Taking the product over all primes,
$$P = \prod_{p\in\mathbb{P}} \frac{1-p^{-8}}{(1-p^{-4})^2} = \left(\prod_{p}(1-p
^{-8})\right)\left(\prod_{p}(1-p^{-4})^{-1}\right)^2.$$

By the Euler product formula for the Riemann zeta function,
$$\zeta(s) = \prod_{p}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad \prod_{p}(1-p^{-s})
 = \frac{1}{\zeta(s)}.$$

Applying this with $s=8$ and $s=4$:
$$P = \frac{1}{\zeta(8)}\cdot \zeta(4)^2 = \frac{\zeta(4)^2}{\zeta(8)}.$$

**Step 3: Insert the known values.** The standard evaluations are
$$\zeta(4) = \frac{\pi^4}{90}, \qquad \zeta(8) = \frac{\pi^8}{9450}.$$

(Both follow from the formula $\zeta(2n) = \dfrac{(-1)^{n+1}B_{2n}(2\pi)^{2n}}{2
(2n)!}$, with $B_4=-\tfrac{1}{30}$ giving $\zeta(4)=\pi^4/90$, and $B_8=-\tfrac{
1}{30}$ giving $\zeta(8)=\pi^8/9450$.)

Therefore
$$P = \frac{(\pi^4/90)^2}{\pi^8/9450} = \frac{\pi^8/8100}{\pi^8/9450} = \frac{94
50}{8100} = \frac{7}{6}.$$

**Step 4: Convergence.** Each factor equals $1 + O(p^{-4})$, and $\sum_p p^{-4}
< \sum_{n\ge 2} n^{-4} < \infty$, so the product converges absolutely to a finit
e positive limit, justifying all rearrangements above.

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\prod_{p\in\mathbb{P}} \frac{p^4+1}{p^4-1} = \frac{7}{6}}.
$$
