## Solution

The statement is **false**. We give an explicit counterexample.

### Counterexample

Let
- $X = S^2$ (the 2-sphere),
- $Y = S^1 \vee S^2$ (the wedge of a circle and a 2-sphere).

Define
- $f \colon S^2 \to S^1 \vee S^2$ to be the inclusion of the $S^2$ summand,
- $g \colon S^1 \vee S^2 \to S^2$ to be the map that collapses the $S^1$ summand
 to the wedge point and is the identity on the $S^2$ summand.

Then $g \circ f = \mathrm{id}_{S^2}$, so in particular $g \circ f \simeq 1_X$.

### $X \not\simeq Y$

The two spaces are not homotopy equivalent: $\pi_1(S^2) = 0$ while $\pi_1(S^1 \v
ee S^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ (by the Seifert–van Kampen theorem, or simply because
the $S^1$ summand carries a free fundamental group and the $S^2$ summand is simp
ly connected). Since the fundamental group is a homotopy invariant, $X \not\sime
q Y$.

### Homotopy dimensions are equal, not strictly ordered

Recall the *homotopy dimension* $\operatorname{hdim}(Z)$ is the minimal dimensio
n of a CW complex homotopy equivalent to $Z$.

- $\operatorname{hdim}(S^2) = 2$, since $H_2(S^2;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z} \n
eq 0$, and a CW complex of dimension $\le 1$ has trivial $H_2$ (cellular homolog
y: no 2-cells $\Rightarrow$ $H_2 = 0$).
- $\operatorname{hdim}(S^1 \vee S^2) = 2$: it admits a CW structure with one 0-c
ell, one 1-cell, and one 2-cell, so $\operatorname{hdim} \le 2$; and $H_2(S^1 \v
ee S^2;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z} \neq 0$ rules out dimension $\le 1$.

Hence
$$\operatorname{hdim}(X) = \operatorname{hdim}(Y) = 2,$$
which is **not** a strict inequality.

### Conclusion

The hypothesis "$g \circ f \simeq 1_X$ with $X \not\simeq Y$" only forces $X$ to
 be a homotopy retract of $Y$; it does not force the homotopy dimension to drop.
 The example $X = S^2$, $Y = S^1 \vee S^2$ satisfies all the hypotheses yet has
$\operatorname{hdim}(X) = \operatorname{hdim}(Y)$. Therefore the claimed strict
inequality is **not true in general**.
