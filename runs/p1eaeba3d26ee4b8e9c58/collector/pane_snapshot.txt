# The Hasse–Weil Conjecture for the L-function of an Elliptic Curve

## Setup

Let $\mathcal{W}$ be an elliptic curve defined over $\mathbb{Q}$. For each prime
 $p$, let $a_p = p + 1 - \#\mathcal{W}(\mathbb{F}_p)$ be the trace of Frobenius
at $p$ (for primes of good reduction). The **Hasse–Weil L-function** of $\mathca
l{W}$ is defined by the Euler product

$$
L(s) \;=\; \prod_{p\,\nmid\, N} \frac{1}{1 - a_p\, p^{-s} + p^{1-2s}} \;\prod_{p
\,\mid\, N} \frac{1}{1 - a_p\, p^{-s}},
$$

where $N$ is the **conductor** of $\mathcal{W}$. The conductor is a positive int
eger $N = \prod_p p^{f_p}$ that encodes the primes of bad reduction: $f_p = 1$ f
or multiplicative reduction, $f_p \in \{2,3,\dots\}$ (determined by the wild par
t of the inertia action) for additive reduction, and $f_p = 0$ for good reductio
n. The local factor at a bad prime $p \mid N$ is modified (the $p^{1-2s}$ term d
rops out) to reflect the reduction type.

The Euler product converges absolutely in the half-plane $\operatorname{Re}(s) >
 \tfrac{3}{2}$ (by Hasse's bound $|a_p| \le 2\sqrt{p}$).

## The Conjecture (HW-Conjecture)

**The Hasse–Weil Conjecture asserts two properties of $L(s)$:**

**(i) Analytic continuation.** The function $L(s)$, initially defined only for $
\operatorname{Re}(s) > \tfrac{3}{2}$ by the convergent Euler product, **extends
to a holomorphic function on the entire complex plane** $\mathbb{C}$.

**(ii) Functional equation.** Defining the **completed L-function**

$$
\Lambda(s) \;=\; N^{s/2}\,(2\pi)^{-s}\,\Gamma(s)\,L(s),
$$

the function $\Lambda(s)$ satisfies the **functional equation**

$$
\boxed{\;\Lambda(s) \;=\; \varepsilon\,\Lambda(2-s)\;}
$$

where $\varepsilon \in \{+1, -1\}$ is a constant (the **root number**, or sign o
f the functional equation) depending on $\mathcal{W}$.

## Role of the Ingredients

- **Conductor $N$.** The factor $N^{s/2}$ is the **archimedean-normalized conduc
tor factor**; it compensates for the bad primes so that the functional equation
is symmetric about the central point $s = 1$. Equivalently, $N$ measures the "de
fect" of $\mathcal{W}$ from having everywhere good reduction, and the power $N^{
s/2}$ is what makes $\Lambda(s)$ and $\Lambda(2-s)$ have matching Euler factors
at every prime.

- **Gamma function $\Gamma(s)$.** The factor $(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)$ is the **arc
himedean (Gamma) factor**. It accounts for the contribution of the archimedean p
lace $p = \infty$ to the L-function. For an elliptic curve (a weight-2 object, w
ith Hodge type $(1,0)+(0,1)$), the correct archimedean factor is $\Gamma_{\mathb
b{C}}(s) = 2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)$; up to the harmless constant $2$, this is exac
tly $(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)$. The Gamma factor ensures that the completed function
 $\Lambda(s)$ has the right growth and symmetry properties to admit the clean fu
nctional equation relating $s$ and $2 - s$.

- **Center of symmetry.** The functional equation is symmetric about $s = 1$, wh
ich is the **central point** of the L-function for an elliptic curve (weight $2$
, so the center is $s = k/2 = 1$). This is the point whose vanishing behavior go
verns the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture.

## Reformulation via the Modularity Theorem

The Hasse–Weil Conjecture for elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$ is now a **theor
em**: it is a consequence of the Modularity Theorem (Wiles–Taylor–Breuil–Conrad–
Diamond), which attaches to $\mathcal{W}$ a weight-2 newform $f$ of level $N$ an
d trivial character such that $L(s, \mathcal{W}) = L(s, f)$. Since $L(s, f)$ is
the L-function of a cuspidal automorphic representation of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathb
b{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})$, it is entire and satisfies the standard automorphic functio
nal equation — which is exactly the equation stated above, with the conductor $N
$ being the level of $f$ and the archimedean factor being $\Gamma(s)$ (weight 2)
. Thus the HW-Conjecture is the special, motivic instance of the general princip
le that **every L-function of an algebraic variety should be automorphic** — the
 central instance of the Langlands program.
