**Answer: Yes, $n = m$ must hold.**

## Proof

**Setup and key property.** A *closed domain* in $\mathbb{R}^n$ is the closure $
\overline{\Omega}$ of a nonempty connected open set $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{R}
^n$. Since $\Omega$ is open and $\Omega \subseteq \overline{\Omega} = U$, we hav
e $\Omega \subseteq \operatorname{int}(U)$, so $\operatorname{int}(U) \neq \empt
yset$. Likewise $\operatorname{int}(V) \neq \emptyset$.

**Theorem (Brouwer's Invariance of Domain, 1912).** *If $W \subseteq \mathbb{R}^
a$ is open and $f: W \to \mathbb{R}^a$ is continuous and injective, then $f(W)$
is open in $\mathbb{R}^a$.*

**Corollary (Invariance of Dimension).** *If $a > b$, there is no continuous inj
ective map from a nonempty open subset of $\mathbb{R}^a$ into $\mathbb{R}^b$.*

*Proof of Corollary.* Suppose $f: W \to \mathbb{R}^b$ is continuous and injectiv
e, with $W$ a nonempty open subset of $\mathbb{R}^a$ and $a > b$. Embed $\mathbb
{R}^b \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}^a$ as $\mathbb{R}^b \times \{0\}^{a-b}$. Then $
f: W \to \mathbb{R}^a$ is continuous and injective, so by invariance of domain,
$f(W)$ is open in $\mathbb{R}^a$. But $f(W) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^b \times \{0\}^
{a-b}$, which has empty interior in $\mathbb{R}^a$ since $a > b$. A nonempty ope
n subset of $\mathbb{R}^a$ cannot be contained in a set with empty interior — co
ntradiction. $\square$

**Main argument.** Let $h: U \to V$ be a homeomorphism.

*Step 1: $n \leq m$.* Since $\operatorname{int}(U) \neq \emptyset$, pick $p \in
\operatorname{int}(U)$. There exists $r > 0$ with the open ball $B_n(p, r) \subs
eteq U$. The restriction
$$h\big|_{B_n(p,r)} : B_n(p,r) \to \mathbb{R}^m$$
is continuous and injective, and $B_n(p,r)$ is a nonempty open subset of $\mathb
b{R}^n$. By the Corollary (with $a = n$, $b = m$), we cannot have $n > m$. There
fore $n \leq m$.

*Step 2: $m \leq n$.* Since $\operatorname{int}(V) \neq \emptyset$, pick $q \in
\operatorname{int}(V)$. There exists $s > 0$ with the open ball $B_m(q, s) \subs
eteq V$. The restriction
$$h^{-1}\big|_{B_m(q,s)} : B_m(q,s) \to \mathbb{R}^n$$
is continuous and injective, and $B_m(q,s)$ is a nonempty open subset of $\mathb
b{R}^m$. By the Corollary (with $a = m$, $b = n$), we cannot have $m > n$. There
fore $m \leq n$.

**Conclusion.** Combining both inequalities, $n = m$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on the necessity of "closed domain."** The hypothesis that $U$ and $V$
are *closed domains* (closures of nonempty open sets) is essential: it guarantee
s nonempty interior, providing an open Euclidean ball on which to apply invarian
ce of dimension. Without this, the conclusion fails — for instance, a singleton
$\{0\}$ is a nonempty closed subset of both $\mathbb{R}^1$ and $\mathbb{R}^2$, a
nd these are homeomorphic with $n \neq m$.
