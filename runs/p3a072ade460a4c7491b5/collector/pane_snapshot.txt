## Proof

**Setup.** Let $X_i \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\lambda/n)$ and $Y_i \sim \mathrm{Po
isson}(\lambda/n)$ be independent, with $n \ge \lambda \ge 0$. Define
$$T_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n^2} X_i, \qquad S_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n^2} Y_i.$$
We seek the limiting distribution of $T_n/S_n$ as $n \to \infty$.

**Means and variances.** Since the $X_i$ are i.i.d. and the $Y_i$ are i.i.d., an
d $\{X_i\},\{Y_i\}$ are independent:

- $T_n \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(n^2, \lambda/n)$, so
$$\mathbb{E}[T_n] = n^2 \cdot \tfrac{\lambda}{n} = n\lambda, \qquad \mathrm{Var}
(T_n) = n^2 \cdot \tfrac{\lambda}{n}\bigl(1-\tfrac{\lambda}{n}\bigr) = n\lambda
- \lambda^2.$$

- $S_n \sim \mathrm{Poisson}(n^2 \cdot \lambda/n) = \mathrm{Poisson}(n\lambda)$
(sum of independent Poissons), so
$$\mathbb{E}[S_n] = n\lambda, \qquad \mathrm{Var}(S_n) = n\lambda.$$

**Normalization by the common mean.** Consider $T_n/(n\lambda)$ and $S_n/(n\lamb
da)$. Their variances are
$$\mathrm{Var}\!\Bigl(\frac{T_n}{n\lambda}\Bigr) = \frac{n\lambda - \lambda^2}{n
^2\lambda^2} = \frac{1-\lambda/n}{n\lambda} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0,$$
$$\mathrm{Var}\!\Bigl(\frac{S_n}{n\lambda}\Bigr) = \frac{n\lambda}{n^2\lambda^2}
 = \frac{1}{n\lambda} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.$$
Since both random variables have mean $1$ and variance tending to $0$, Chebyshev
's inequality gives
$$\frac{T_n}{n\lambda} \xrightarrow{P} 1, \qquad \frac{S_n}{n\lambda} \xrightarr
ow{P} 1.$$

**The ratio.** Write
$$\frac{T_n}{S_n} = \frac{T_n/(n\lambda)}{S_n/(n\lambda)}.$$
The numerator converges in probability to $1$; the denominator converges in prob
ability to $1 \neq 0$. By the continuous mapping theorem (the function $g(u,v) =
 u/v$ is continuous at $(1,1)$) and Slutsky's theorem,
$$\frac{T_n}{S_n} \xrightarrow{P} \frac{1}{1} = 1.$$
Convergence in probability implies convergence in distribution, so $T_n/S_n$ con
verges in distribution to the **degenerate distribution $\delta_1$** (point mass
 at $1$).

**CLT refinement (non-degenerate fluctuation).** For completeness, the first-ord
er fluctuation is non-trivial. Let $Z_i = X_i - Y_i$; these are i.i.d. with
$$\mathbb{E}[Z_i] = 0, \qquad \mathrm{Var}(Z_i) = \tfrac{\lambda}{n}\bigl(1-\tfr
ac{\lambda}{n}\bigr) + \tfrac{\lambda}{n} = \tfrac{\lambda}{n}\bigl(2-\tfrac{\la
mbda}{n}\bigr).$$
Then $T_n - S_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n^2} Z_i$ with $\mathrm{Var}(T_n - S_n) = n\lambda
(2 - \lambda/n) = 2n\lambda - \lambda^2$. By the Lindeberg–Lévy CLT,
$$\frac{T_n - S_n}{\sqrt{2n\lambda - \lambda^2}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0,1
).$$
Writing
$$\sqrt{n\lambda}\Bigl(\frac{T_n}{S_n}-1\Bigr) = \frac{T_n - S_n}{\sqrt{2n\lambd
a-\lambda^2}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{n\lambda(2n\lambda-\lambda^2)}}{S_n},$$
and using $S_n/(n\lambda) \xrightarrow{P} 1$ so that $\sqrt{n\lambda(2n\lambda-\
lambda^2)}/S_n \xrightarrow{P} \sqrt{2}$, Slutsky's theorem yields
$$\sqrt{n\lambda}\Bigl(\frac{T_n}{S_n}-1\Bigr) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0,2).
$$

**Conclusion.** The limiting distribution of $T_n/S_n$ is the point mass at $1$:
$$\frac{T_n}{S_n} \xrightarrow{d} \delta_1 \quad \text{as } n\to\infty.$$
Equivalently, $T_n/S_n \xrightarrow{P} 1$, with the centered-and-scaled fluctuat
ion $\sqrt{n\lambda}\,(T_n/S_n - 1) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0,2)$.
