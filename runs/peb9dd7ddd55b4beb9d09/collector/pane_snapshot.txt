## Proof

We determine the asymptotic behavior of

$$R(x) := \frac{\sum_{p \le x} \log p}{\pi(x)},$$

where the sum runs over primes $p \le x$ and $\pi(x) = \#\{p \le x\}$.

**Claim.** $R(x) \sim \log x$, i.e.

$$\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\sum_{p \le x} \log p}{\pi(x) \log x} = 1.$$

---

### Step 1. Two standard forms of the Prime Number Theorem

We use two equivalent reformulations of the PNT (each is a classical consequence
 of the non-vanishing of $\zeta(s)$ on $\Re s = 1$, due to Hadamard and de la Va
llée Poussin):

**(PNT-a)** $\displaystyle \pi(x) \sim \frac{x}{\log x}$.

**(PNT-b)** $\displaystyle \theta(x) := \sum_{p \le x} \log p \sim x$.

The equivalence of (PNT-a) and (PNT-b) is itself elementary; we recall the argum
ent because it is exactly the mechanism we need.

---

### Step 2. Equivalence of (PNT-a) and (PNT-b) via partial summation

Define $\theta(x) = \sum_{p \le x} \log p$. By Abel/partial summation with $a_p
= \log p$ and $f(t) = 1$,

$$\theta(x) = \pi(x)\log x - \int_2^x \frac{\pi(t)}{t}\,dt.$$

**(PNT-a) $\Rightarrow$ (PNT-b).** Substituting $\pi(t) \sim t/\log t$,

$$\int_2^x \frac{\pi(t)}{t}\,dt \sim \int_2^x \frac{dt}{\log t} \sim \frac{x}{\l
og x} \sim \pi(x),$$

where the last asymptotic for the logarithmic integral $\operatorname{li}(x)$ fo
llows by a single integration by parts:
$\operatorname{li}(x) = \frac{x}{\log x} + \int_2^x \frac{dt}{(\log t)^2} + O(1)
$, and the remaining integral is $o(x/\log x)$. Hence

$$\theta(x) = \pi(x)\log x - \pi(x)(1+o(1)) = \pi(x)\log x\bigl(1 - \tfrac{1+o(1
)}{\log x}\bigr) \sim \pi(x)\log x \sim x.$$

**(PNT-b) $\Rightarrow$ (PNT-a).** Conversely, from $\theta(x)\sim x$ and the sa
me identity solved for $\pi(x)\log x = \theta(x) + \int_2^x \pi(t)/t\,dt$, one r
ecovers $\pi(x)\log x \sim x$, i.e. (PNT-a). Thus the two statements are equival
ent, and in particular

$$\theta(x) \sim \pi(x)\log x. \tag{$\star$}$$

---

### Step 3. Direct derivation of the claim from ($\star$)

Dividing both sides of ($\star$) by $\pi(x)\log x$,

$$\frac{\theta(x)}{\pi(x)\log x} = \frac{\sum_{p\le x}\log p}{\pi(x)\log x} \lon
grightarrow 1.$$

Equivalently,

$$\frac{\sum_{p\le x}\log p}{\pi(x)} \sim \log x.$$

---

### Step 4. Interpretation

The quantity $R(x)$ is the **average value of $\log p$ over primes $p \le x$**.
The result $R(x)\sim\log x$ says that, asymptotically, the "typical" prime below
 $x$ has logarithm close to $\log x$ — i.e. the primes up to $x$ are, on a logar
ithmic scale, concentrated near $x$. This is consistent with (and equivalent to)
 the PNT: the density $\sim 1/\log t$ is heaviest near $t=x$, so the $\log p$-we
ighted average is dominated by primes close to $x$.

A quantitative form follows from the standard error term in the PNT: with $\pi(x
) = \operatorname{li}(x) + O(x e^{-c\sqrt{\log x}})$ and $\theta(x) = x + O(x e^
{-c'\sqrt{\log x}})$, one obtains

$$R(x) = \log x + O\!\bigl(e^{-c''\sqrt{\log x}}\bigr) \cdot \log x + O(1) = \lo
g x\bigl(1 + O(e^{-c''\sqrt{\log x}})\bigr),$$

so in fact $R(x) - \log x \to 0$ on the relative scale, and even $R(x) - \log x
= O(1)$.

---

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \frac{\sum_{p \le x} \log p}{\pi(x)} \sim \log x \quad (x
 \to \infty).}$$
