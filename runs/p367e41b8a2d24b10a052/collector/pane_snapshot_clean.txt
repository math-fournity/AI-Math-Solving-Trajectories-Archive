## Solution

**Claim.** The minimal polynomial of $\alpha^m$ over $K$ is $x^{\,n/m}-a$.

Set $d = n/m \in \mathbb{N}$, so $n = md$. Since $\alpha^n = a$ and $n=md$,

$$(\alpha^m)^d = \alpha^{md} = \alpha^n = a.$$

Hence $\alpha^m$ is a root of $g(x) = x^d - a \in K[x]$, and so the minimal poly
nomial of $\alpha^m$ over $K$ divides $g(x)$. In particular $(\alpha^m):K] \le
 d$.

Now consider the tower of extensions
$$K \;\subseteq\; K(\alpha^m) \;\subseteq\; K(\alpha).$$

Since $f(x)=x^n-a$ is irreducible over $K$ and $\alpha$ is a root, $f$ is the mi
nimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $K$, giving
$$(\alpha):K] = n = md.$$

Over $K(\alpha^m)$, the element $\alpha$ is a root of the polynomial $h(x) = x^m
 - \alpha^m \in K(\alpha^m)[x]$, since $h(\alpha) = \alpha^m - \alpha^m = 0$. Th
is polynomial has degree $m$, so
$$(\alpha):K(\alpha^m)] \le m.$$

Applying the tower law,
$$(\alpha):K] = (\alpha):K(\alpha^m)]\cdot(\alpha^m):K] \le m \cdot d = n.
$$

But $(\alpha):K] = n$, so equality must hold throughout:
$$(\alpha):K(\alpha^m)] = m, \qquad (\alpha^m):K] = d.$$

Therefore $(\alpha^m):K] = d = \deg g$. Since $g(x) = x^d - a$ is a monic poly
nomial in $K[x]$ of degree $d$ having $\alpha^m$ as a root, and the minimal poly
nomial of $\alpha^m$ over $K$ has degree $(\alpha^m):K] = d = \deg g$, the min
imal polynomial must equal $g$ itself. Consequently $x^d - a$ is irreducible ove
r $K$.

---

**Conclusion.** With $d = n/m$, the minimal polynomial of $\alpha^m$ over $K$ is

$$\boxed{\,x^{\,n/m} - a\,}.$$
