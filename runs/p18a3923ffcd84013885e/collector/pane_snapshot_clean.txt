# Minkowski's Fundamental Theorem (Geometry of Numbers)

**Theorem (Minkowski, 1896).** Let $L \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ be a full-rank latti
ce with determinant $\det(L) > 0$ (i.e. the volume of a fundamental parallelepip
ed of $L$). Let $K \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ be a **convex** body that is **symmetri
c about the origin** (meaning $K = -K$, i.e. $x \in K \iff -x \in K$). If

$$\operatorname{vol}(K) > 2^n \det(L),$$

then $K$ contains a **non-zero** point of the lattice $L$; that is, there exists
 $v \in L \cap K$ with $v \neq 0$.

---

## Remarks and refinements

**Sharpness of the constant.** The constant $2^n$ is best possible. The open uni
t cube $K = (-1,1)^n$ is convex, centrally symmetric, has volume $2^n$, and with
 respect to the standard lattice $L = \mathbb{Z}^n$ (so $\det(L)=1$) satisfies $
\operatorname{vol}(K) = 2^n \det(L)$, yet $K \cap \mathbb{Z}^n = \{0\}$ — no non
-zero lattice point lies in the *open* cube. Thus the strict inequality $\operat
orname{vol}(K) > 2^n\det(L)$ cannot in general be relaxed to $\geq$ for open bod
ies.

**Closed-body variant.** If $K$ is additionally **compact** (closed and bounded)
, the conclusion holds under the non-strict condition

$$\operatorname{vol}(K) \geq 2^n \det(L).$$

The proof of this sharper form uses a compactness/limiting argument applied to t
he dilates $(1+\varepsilon)K$.

**Standard normalization.** Equivalently, writing $\operatorname{covol}(L) = \de
t(L)$, the theorem says: a centrally symmetric convex body of volume strictly ex
ceeding $2^n \operatorname{covol}(L)$ must contain a non-trivial lattice point.
The factor $2^n$ reflects the "Blichfeldt slack": each coset of $L$ contributes
at most one representative in a half-sized translate of $K$.

---

## Proof sketch (for completeness)

The argument rests on **Blichfeldt's principle**, which itself is a volume-pigeo
nhole argument:

**Blichfeldt's Principle.** If $S \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is measurable with $\ope
ratorname{vol}(S) > \det(L)$, then there exist two distinct points $x, y \in S$
with $x - y \in L$.

*Blichfeldt proof.* Tile $\mathbb{R}^n$ by a fundamental domain $\mathcal{F}$ of
 $L$. The translates $S \cap (\mathcal{F} + v)$, $v \in L$, all project into $\m
athcal{F}$ by translation. Since $\sum_{v} \operatorname{vol}(S \cap (\mathcal{F
}+v)) = \operatorname{vol}(S) > \operatorname{vol}(\mathcal{F}) = \det(L)$, two
of these projected pieces must overlap, yielding $x, y \in S$ with $x - y \in L
\setminus \{0\}$.

*Minkowski from Blichfeldt.* Set $S = \tfrac{1}{2}K = \{\tfrac{1}{2}x : x \in K\
}$. Then $\operatorname{vol}(S) = 2^{-n}\operatorname{vol}(K) > \det(L)$, so by
Blichfeldt there exist $x, y \in \tfrac{1}{2}K$, $x \neq y$, with $x - y \in L \
setminus \{0\}$. By central symmetry $-y \in \tfrac{1}{2}K$, and by convexity $\
tfrac{1}{2}(x) + \tfrac{1}{2}(-y) = \tfrac{1}{2}(x - y) \in \tfrac{1}{2}K$. Henc
e $x - y \in K \cap L \setminus \{0\}$. $\blacksquare$

---

## Significance

Minkowski's theorem is the cornerstone of the **geometry of numbers**. It conver
ts a *geometric volume* condition into an *arithmetic existence* statement, and
it underlies classical results such as:

- **Minkowski's linear forms theorem:** if $\alpha_{ij}$ are real with $\det(\al
pha_{ij}) \neq 0$ and $\prod_i A_i > |\det(\alpha_{ij})|$, there is a non-zero i
nteger vector $m$ with $|\sum_j \alpha_{ij}m_j| \leq A_i$ for all $i$.
- **Finiteness of class numbers** and bounds on discriminants of number fields (
Minkowski's bound $\sqrt{|\Delta_K|}\left(\tfrac{4}{\pi}\right)^{r_2}\tfrac{n!}{
n^n}$).
- The **Minkowski–Hlawka theorem** (a converse in average, due to Siegel–Hlawka)
: there exist lattices of determinant $1$ whose symmetric convex bodies of volum
e up to $\zeta(n)$ avoid all non-zero lattice points — showing $2^n$ is far from
 optimal for *generic* bodies, even though it is sharp for the cube.
