We claim that $\nu = \delta_0$, the Dirac measure concentrated at $0$, satisfies
 the required property.

### Setup

Each $\nu_n$ is defined by its density $n\cdot 1_{(1/n,\,2/n)}$ with respect to
Lebesgue measure. Its total mass is
$$
\nu_n(\mathbb{R}) = n\cdot\left(\frac{2}{n}-\frac{1}{n}\right) = n\cdot\frac{1}{
n} = 1,
$$
so every $\nu_n$ is a probability measure. The support of $\nu_n$ is the interva
l $(1/n,\,2/n)$, which shrinks to the singleton $\{0\}$ as $n\to\infty$.

### Proof of weak convergence

Let $\psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ be continuous. We must show
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi\,d\nu_n = \psi(0) = \int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi
\,d\delta_0.
$$

By definition of $\nu_n$,
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi\,d\nu_n = n\int_{1/n}^{2/n}\psi(x)\,dx.
$$

**Step 1: Reduction to values near 0.** Since $\psi$ is continuous at $0$, for e
very $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $\delta>0$ such that $|x|<\delta \Rightarrow |
\psi(x)-\psi(0)|<\varepsilon$. Choose $N$ so that $2/N<\delta$. Then for all $n\
geq N$, the entire interval $(1/n,2/n)\subset(-\delta,\delta)$, hence $|\psi(x)-
\psi(0)|<\varepsilon$ for all $x\in(1/n,2/n)$.

**Step 2: Estimate.** For $n\geq N$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi\,d\nu_n - \psi(0)\right|
&= \left|n\int_{1/n}^{2/n}\psi(x)\,dx - \psi(0)\right| \\
&= \left|n\int_{1/n}^{2/n}\bigl(\psi(x)-\psi(0)\bigr)\,dx\right| \qquad\bigl(\te
xt{since } n\!\int_{1/n}^{2/n}\psi(0)\,dx = \psi(0)\bigr)\\
&\leq n\int_{1/n}^{2/n}\bigl|\psi(x)-\psi(0)\bigr|\,dx \\
&< n\int_{1/n}^{2/n}\varepsilon\,dx \;=\; n\cdot\varepsilon\cdot\frac{1}{n} \;=\
; \varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 3: Conclusion.** Since $\varepsilon>0$ was arbitrary,
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi\,d\nu_n = \psi(0) = \int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi
\,d\delta_0.
$$

This holds for every continuous $\psi$, so $\nu_n\to\delta_0$ weakly.

### The measure

$$
\boxed{\nu = \delta_0}
$$

the Dirac (point mass) measure at the origin: $\delta_0(E)=1$ if $0\in E$, and $
\delta_0(E)=0$ otherwise.
