## Proof

We show that the Krull dimension of $R$ is finite. In fact, the ascending chain
condition on radical ideals alone suffices; the remaining hypotheses (d.c.c. on
primes, locality, domain, non-Noetherian) are not needed for the conclusion.

### Step 1: ACC on radical ideals $\Longleftrightarrow$ $\operatorname{Spec}(R)$
 is a Noetherian topological space.

The closed subsets of $\operatorname{Spec}(R)$ are precisely the sets $V(I) = \{
P \in \operatorname{Spec}(R) : I \subseteq P\}$ for ideals $I$ of $R$. Since $V(
I) = V(\sqrt{I})$, and $V(I) = V(J)$ if and only if $\sqrt{I} = \sqrt{J}$, there
 is an order-reversing bijection between radical ideals of $R$ and closed subset
s of $\operatorname{Spec}(R)$:

$$I \longmapsto V(I), \qquad V \longmapsto \bigcap_{P \in V} P.$$

Under this bijection, an ascending chain $I_1 \subseteq I_2 \subseteq \cdots$ of
 radical ideals corresponds to a descending chain $V(I_1) \supseteq V(I_2) \sups
eteq \cdots$ of closed sets, and vice versa. Therefore, the a.c.c. on radical id
eals is equivalent to the descending chain condition on closed subsets of $\oper
atorname{Spec}(R)$, which is precisely the definition of $\operatorname{Spec}(R)
$ being a **Noetherian topological space**.

### Step 2: A Noetherian topological space has finite Krull dimension.

Let $X$ be a Noetherian topological space, and suppose for contradiction that $\
dim(X) = \infty$. Define

$$\mathcal{S} = \{F \subseteq X : F \text{ is closed and } \dim(F) = \infty\},$$

where $\dim(F)$ is the Krull dimension of the subspace $F$ (the supremum of leng
ths of chains of irreducible closed subsets of $F$). Since $X \in \mathcal{S}$,
this family is nonempty. By the Noetherian condition (d.c.c. on closed sets), $\
mathcal{S}$ admits a **minimal** element $Y$ with respect to inclusion.

**Claim:** $Y$ is irreducible.

Suppose $Y = A \cup B$ with $A, B$ proper closed subsets of $Y$. Any irreducible
 closed subset of $Y$ is contained in $A$ or in $B$ (an irreducible set containe
d in a finite union must lie in one summand). Hence any chain of irreducible clo
sed subsets of $Y$ is entirely contained in $A$ or in $B$, giving

$$\dim(Y) = \max(\dim(A),\, \dim(B)).$$

Since $\dim(Y) = \infty$, at least one of $\dim(A), \dim(B)$ is $\infty$, so one
 of $A, B$ belongs to $\mathcal{S}$. But $A \subsetneq Y$ and $B \subsetneq Y$,
contradicting the minimality of $Y$. $\square$

Since $\dim(Y) = \infty$, for every positive integer $n$ there exists a strict c
hain

$$Y \supsetneq Z_1 \supsetneq Z_2 \supsetneq \cdots \supsetneq Z_n$$

of irreducible closed subsets of $Y$. In particular, $Z_1$ is a proper closed su
bset of $Y$ that contains chains of irreducible closed sets of length $n - 1$ fo
r every $n$, so $\dim(Z_1) = \infty$. Thus $Z_1 \in \mathcal{S}$, but $Z_1 \subs
etneq Y$, contradicting the minimality of $Y$.

This contradiction establishes $\dim(X) < \infty$.

### Step 3: Conclusion.

The Krull dimension of $R$ equals the Krull dimension of $\operatorname{Spec}(R)
$ as a topological space: chains of prime ideals $P_0 \subsetneq P_1 \subsetneq
\cdots \subsetneq P_n$ in $R$ correspond to chains of irreducible closed sets $V
(P_n) \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq V(P_1) \subsetneq V(P_0)$ in $\operatorname{S
pec}(R)$, and every irreducible closed subset of $\operatorname{Spec}(R)$ is of
the form $V(P)$ for some prime $P$.

By Step 1, the a.c.c. on radical ideals makes $\operatorname{Spec}(R)$ a Noether
ian topological space. By Step 2, $\operatorname{Spec}(R)$ has finite Krull dime
nsion. Therefore:

$$\boxed{\dim(R) < \infty.}$$

### Remark

The d.c.c. on prime ideals, the locality of $R$, and the domain hypothesis are n
ot needed—the a.c.c. on radical ideals alone forces $\operatorname{Spec}(R)$ to
be Noetherian, hence finite-dimensional. The non-Noetherian hypothesis is consis
tent with the result: for example, a non-discrete rank-one valuation domain has
spectrum $\{(0), \mathfrak{m}\}$ (Noetherian, dimension $1$) yet is not Noetheri
an as a ring.
