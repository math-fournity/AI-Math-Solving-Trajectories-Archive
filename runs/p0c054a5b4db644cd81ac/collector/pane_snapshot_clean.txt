## Proof

**Claim:** The two definitions are equivalent.

Let $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$ be open, and set $\bar{\Omega} = \Omega \cup
 \partial\Omega$ (the closure, which is closed in $\mathbb{R}^d$).

---

### (2) $\Rightarrow$ (1)

Suppose $u$ admits a continuous extension $\tilde u : \bar{\Omega} \to \mathbb{R
}$. Let $B \subseteq \Omega$ be bounded. The closure $\overline{B}^{\,\mathbb{R}
^d}$ is closed and bounded in $\mathbb{R}^d$, hence compact. Since $B \subseteq
\Omega \subseteq \bar{\Omega}$ and $\bar{\Omega}$ is closed, we have $\overline{
B}^{\,\mathbb{R}^d} \subseteq \bar{\Omega}$. A continuous function on a compact
set is uniformly continuous, so $\tilde u$ is uniformly continuous on $\overline
{B}^{\,\mathbb{R}^d}$, hence on $B$. Since $u|_B = \tilde u|_B$, $u$ is uniforml
y continuous on $B$. $\checkmark$

---

### (1) $\Rightarrow$ (2)

Suppose $u$ is uniformly continuous on every bounded subset of $\Omega$.

**Definition of the extension.** For $x_0 \in \partial\Omega$, set
$$\tilde u(x_0) := \lim_{\substack{x \to x_0 \\ x \in \Omega}} u(x),$$
and $\tilde u := u$ on $\Omega$. We must show the limit exists and that $\tilde
u$ is continuous on $\bar\Omega$.

**The limit exists and is well-defined.** Let $(x_n) \subseteq \Omega$ with $x_n
 \to x_0$. Then $\{x_n\}$ is bounded, so it lies in some bounded subset $B \subs
eteq \Omega$. By hypothesis, $u$ is uniformly continuous on $B$. Given $\varepsi
lon > 0$, pick $\delta > 0$ from uniform continuity. Since $(x_n)$ is Cauchy, th
ere exists $N$ with $|x_m - x_n| < \delta$ for $m,n \ge N$, giving $|u(x_m)-u(x_
n)| < \varepsilon$. So $(u(x_n))$ is Cauchy in $\mathbb{R}$, hence convergent.

To see the limit is independent of the sequence: if $x_n \to x_0$ and $y_n \to x
_0$ with both in $\Omega$, the interleaved sequence $z_{2n}=x_n,\; z_{2n+1}=y_n$
 also converges to $x_0$ and lies in a bounded subset of $\Omega$, so $(u(z_n))$
 converges; thus $\lim u(x_n) = \lim u(y_n)$.

**Continuity of $\tilde u$ on $\bar\Omega$.** Let $x_0 \in \bar\Omega$ and $(x_n
) \subseteq \bar\Omega$ with $x_n \to x_0$. The set $K = \{x_0\} \cup \{x_n\}$ i
s compact. Choose a bounded open ball $B \supseteq K$. Then $\Omega \cap B$ is a
 bounded subset of $\Omega$, so $u$ is uniformly continuous on $\Omega \cap B$.

Given $\varepsilon > 0$, let $\delta > 0$ be such that $|u(y)-u(z)| < \varepsilo
n/3$ whenever $y,z \in \Omega\cap B$ with $|y-z|<\delta$. Choose $N$ so that $|x
_n - x_0| < \delta/3$ for $n \ge N$.

For each $n \ge N$ and for $x_0$ itself, pick a "witness" point $y_n, y_0 \in \O
mega \cap B$ as follows:
- If $x_n \in \Omega$: take $y_n = x_n$, so $|u(y_n) - \tilde u(x_n)| = 0$.
- If $x_n \in \partial\Omega$: since $x_n$ is a limit point of $\Omega$ (as $\Om
ega$ is open) and $B$ is open with $x_n \in B$, choose $y_n \in \Omega \cap B$ w
ith $|y_n - x_n| < \delta/3$ and $|u(y_n) - \tilde u(x_n)| < \varepsilon/3$ (pos
sible by definition of $\tilde u(x_n)$ as the limit).

Likewise pick $y_0 \in \Omega \cap B$ with $|y_0 - x_0| < \delta/3$ and $|u(y_0)
 - \tilde u(x_0)| < \varepsilon/3$ (or $y_0 = x_0$ if $x_0 \in \Omega$).

Then $|y_n - y_0| \le |y_n - x_n| + |x_n - x_0| + |x_0 - y_0| < \delta$, so $|u(
y_n) - u(y_0)| < \varepsilon/3$. Therefore
$$|\tilde u(x_n) - \tilde u(x_0)| \le |\tilde u(x_n) - u(y_n)| + |u(y_n) - u(y_0
)| + |u(y_0) - \tilde u(x_0)| < \frac{\varepsilon}{3} + \frac{\varepsilon}{3} +
\frac{\varepsilon}{3} = \varepsilon.$$

Hence $\tilde u(x_n) \to \tilde u(x_0)$, proving $\tilde u$ is continuous at $x_
0$. Since $x_0 \in \bar\Omega$ was arbitrary, $\tilde u \in C(\bar\Omega)$. $\ch
eckmark$

Both inclusions hold, so the two definitions of $C(\bar\Omega)$ are **equivalent
