## Proof

**Answer: No.** $\mathbb{R}$-linearity alone does not force $(T\sigma)_p$ to dep
end only on the values of $\sigma$ in an arbitrarily small neighborhood of $p$.

### Counterexample

Take $M = \mathbb{R}$, and let $E_1 = E_2 = \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}$ be the
 trivial real line bundle over $\mathbb{R}$. Sections are just smooth functions
$\sigma : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$, so $\Gamma(E_1) = \Gamma(E_2) = C^\infty(\
mathbb{R})$.

Fix the point $0 \in \mathbb{R}$. Define
$$
(T\sigma)(p) := \sigma(0) \qquad \text{for all } p \in \mathbb{R}.
$$

**$T$ is $\mathbb{R}$-linear.** For $\sigma, \tau \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ and
$a, b \in \mathbb{R}$:
$$
(T(a\sigma + b\tau))(p) = (a\sigma + b\tau)(0) = a\,\sigma(0) + b\,\tau(0) = a(T
\sigma)(p) + b(T\tau)(p),
$$
so $T(a\sigma + b\tau) = a\,T\sigma + b\,T\tau$. The output $T\sigma$ is the con
stant function with value $\sigma(0)$, which is smooth, so $T : \Gamma(E_1) \to
\Gamma(E_2)$.

**Locality fails.** Take $p = 1$. Then $(T\sigma)_1 = \sigma(0)$. This value dep
ends on $\sigma$ at the point $0$, which is a positive distance away from $p = 1
$. In particular, for any neighborhood $U$ of $p = 1$ with $0 \notin U$ (e.g. $U
 = (1/2, 3/2)$), we can find two sections that agree on $U$ but differ at $0$:

- $\sigma_1 \equiv 0$,
- $\sigma_2$ a bump function with $\sigma_2(0) = 1$ and $\sigma_2|_{(1/2,\,3/2)}
 \equiv 0$.

Then $\sigma_1 = \sigma_2$ on $U$, yet
$$
(T\sigma_1)_1 = 0 \neq 1 = (T\sigma_2)_1.
$$
So $(T\sigma)_p$ does **not** depend only on the values of $\sigma$ in an arbitr
arily small neighborhood of $p$.

### Why this is consistent with the theory

The property that $(T\sigma)_p$ depends only on $\sigma$ restricted to an arbitr
arily small neighborhood of $p$ is **locality** (or the *support-decreasing* pro
perty). A standard theorem (Peetre's theorem) states that locality **plus** $\ma
thbb{R}$-linearity forces $T$ to be a differential operator locally. The counter
example above shows that $\mathbb{R}$-linearity by itself is strictly weaker: it
 permits "nonlocal" operators such as evaluation at a distant point, projection
onto a global functional, integration against a kernel, etc.

Concretely, the failure mechanism is that $\mathbb{R}$-linearity only controls h
ow $T$ interacts with **finite** $\mathbb{R}$-linear combinations of sections, w
hile locality is a statement about **infinite** families of sections (it must ho
ld for every pair of sections agreeing near $p$). Nothing in $\mathbb{R}$-linear
ity rules out dependence on values of $\sigma$ far from $p$, as the evaluation-a
t-$0$ operator demonstrates.

### Conclusion

$\mathbb{R}$-linearity of $T : \Gamma(E_1) \to \Gamma(E_2)$ does **not** imply t
hat $(T\sigma)_p$ depends only on the values of $\sigma$ in an arbitrarily small
 neighborhood of $p$. The operator $T\sigma = \sigma(0)\cdot\mathbf{1}$ on the t
rivial line bundle over $\mathbb{R}$ is an explicit $\mathbb{R}$-linear countere
xample.
