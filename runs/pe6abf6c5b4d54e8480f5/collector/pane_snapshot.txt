## Proof

We seek the number of distinct real solutions of $R^3(c)=c$, where $R(c)=2c^2+c-
1$.

### Step 1. Conjugacy to the standard quadratic

Set $u = 2c+\tfrac12$ (equivalently $c = \tfrac{u}{2}-\tfrac14$). Then
$$
R(c) = 2c^2+c-1 = \tfrac{u^2}{2}-\tfrac98,
$$
and the image coordinate is $u' = 2R(c)+\tfrac12 = u^2-\tfrac74$. Thus $R$ is co
njugate (via an invertible affine change) to
$$
f(u) = u^2 - \tfrac74.
$$
Conjugacy preserves the number of real periodic points, so we count real solutio
ns of $f^3(u)=u$.

### Step 2. Compute $f^3(u)-u$

$$
f^2(u)=\bigl(u^2-\tfrac74\bigr)^2-\tfrac74 = u^4-\tfrac72 u^2+\tfrac{21}{16},
$$
$$
f^3(u)=\bigl(f^2(u)\bigr)^2-\tfrac74 = u^8-7u^6+\tfrac{119}{8}u^4-\tfrac{147}{16
}u^2-\tfrac{7}{256}.
$$
Hence
$$
f^3(u)-u = u^8-7u^6+\tfrac{119}{8}u^4-\tfrac{147}{16}u^2-u-\tfrac{7}{256}.
$$

### Step 3. Factor out the fixed-point factor

Fixed points satisfy $f(u)-u = u^2-u-\tfrac74=0$. Polynomial division gives
$$
f^3(u)-u = \bigl(u^2-u-\tfrac74\bigr)\,Q(u),
$$
where
$$
Q(u)=u^6+u^5-\tfrac{17}{4}u^4-\tfrac52 u^3+\tfrac{79}{16}u^2+\tfrac{9}{16}u+\tfr
ac{1}{64}.
$$

### Step 4. The sextic is a perfect square

A direct check shows
$$
Q(u)=\Bigl(u^3+\tfrac12 u^2-\tfrac94 u-\tfrac18\Bigr)^2.
$$
Indeed, expanding $\bigl(u^3+\tfrac12 u^2-\tfrac94 u-\tfrac18\bigr)^2$ reproduce
s every coefficient of $Q$ (the $u^5$ coefficient $2\cdot\tfrac12=1$; $u^4$: $\t
frac14+2(-\tfrac94)=-\tfrac{17}{4}$; $u^3$: $2\cdot\tfrac12\cdot(-\tfrac94)+2(-\
tfrac18)=-\tfrac52$; $u^2$: $\tfrac{81}{16}+2\cdot\tfrac12\cdot(-\tfrac18)=\tfra
c{79}{16}$; $u$: $2(-\tfrac94)(-\tfrac18)=\tfrac{9}{16}$; constant: $\tfrac{1}{6
4}$).

This is the signature of the **period-3 saddle-node (tangent) bifurcation** at p
arameter $-\tfrac74$: the six period-3 points coalesce pairwise into three doubl
e points.

### Step 5. Count real roots

**Fixed points:** $u^2-u-\tfrac74=0$ has discriminant $1+7=8>0$, giving **2 dist
inct real roots**.

**Period-3 points:** the cubic $p(u)=u^3+\tfrac12 u^2-\tfrac94 u-\tfrac18$, or e
quivalently $8u^3+4u^2-18u-1=0$. Its derivative $24u^2+8u-18$ has two real zeros
 $u=\tfrac{-1\pm2\sqrt7}{6}$. Evaluating:
- at the local max $u\approx-1.049$: $g(u)\approx +13.05>0$,
- at the local min $u\approx 0.715$: $g(u)\approx -8.90<0$.

Since the local maximum is positive and the local minimum is negative, the cubic
 has **3 distinct real roots**. (Numerically they lie near $-1.31$, $-0.055$, an
d $1.36$, none of which coincide with the fixed points $\tfrac{1\pm2\sqrt2}{2}\a
pprox 1.914,-0.914$.)

Each cubic root is a double root of $Q$, but for counting *distinct* real values
 only the number of distinct roots matters.

### Step 6. Total

$$
\text{distinct real Stability Points} = 2\ (\text{fixed points}) + 3\ (\text{per
iod-3 tangent points}) = \boxed{5}.
$$
