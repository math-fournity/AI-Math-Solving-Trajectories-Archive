### Restating the integral

The integrand is

$$
\frac{1}{x\cdot \ln(x)\cdot \ln^{2}(x)\cdot \ln^{3}(x)\cdots \ln^{k}(x)^{s}},
$$

where $\ln^{j}(x)$ denotes the $j$-fold iterated logarithm and the final factor
is $(\ln^{k} x)^{s}$. Thus the integral is

$$
I=\int_{e_{k}}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x\,\ln(x)\,\ln^{2}(x)\,\ln^{3}(x)\cdots \ln^{k-
1}(x)\,(\ln^{k} x)^{s}}.
$$

### Key substitution

Set $u=\ln^{k}(x)$. By the chain rule,

$$
\frac{du}{dx}=\frac{1}{x\,\ln(x)\,\ln^{2}(x)\cdots \ln^{k-1}(x)},
$$

since each differentiation $\frac{d}{dx}\ln^{j}(x)=\frac{1}{\ln^{j-1}(x)}\cdot\f
rac{d}{dx}\ln^{j-1}(x)$ peels off one factor. Hence

$$
du=\frac{dx}{x\,\ln(x)\,\ln^{2}(x)\cdots \ln^{k-1}(x)}.
$$

### Changing variables

The integral collapses to

$$
I=\int_{u(e_{k})}^{u(\infty)}\frac{du}{u^{s}}.
$$

**Lower limit.** With $e_{1}=e$, $e_{2}=e^{e}$, …, $e_{k}=e^{e_{k-1}}$, we have
$\ln(e_{k})=e_{k-1}$, $\ln^{2}(e_{k})=e_{k-2}$, …, $\ln^{k}(e_{k})=e_{0}=1$. So
$u(e_{k})=1$.

**Upper limit.** As $x\to\infty$, every iterated logarithm $\ln^{k}(x)\to\infty$
, so $u(\infty)=\infty$.

Therefore

$$
I=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{du}{u^{s}}.
$$

### Convergence of the reduced integral

The $p$-integral $\int_{1}^{\infty}u^{-s}\,du$ converges if and only if $s>1$:

- For $s>1$: $\int_{1}^{\infty}u^{-s}du=\frac{u^{1-s}}{1-s}\Big|_{1}^{\infty}=\f
rac{1}{s-1}<\infty$.
- For $s=1$: $\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{du}{u}=\ln u\big|_{1}^{\infty}=\infty$.
- For $s<1$: $\frac{u^{1-s}}{1-s}\big|_{1}^{\infty}=\infty$.

The substitution is a $C^{1}$-diffeomorphism on $[e_{k},\infty)$ onto $[1,\infty
)$ (since $\ln^{k}$ is strictly increasing and unbounded there), so convergence
is preserved exactly.

### Conclusion

The integral converges if and only if

$$
\boxed{s>1}.
$$
