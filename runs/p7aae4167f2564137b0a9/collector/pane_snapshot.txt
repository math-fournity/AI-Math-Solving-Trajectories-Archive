## Proof

We determine which line bundle the associated-bundle construction
$$E \;=\; \mathbb{C}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}\;\times_{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}\;\mathbb{
C},\qquad t\cdot(z,\lambda)=(tz,\,t^{m}\lambda)$$
produces over $\mathbb{P}^{n}$, where the quotient identifies $(z,\lambda)\sim(t
z,t^{m}\lambda)$.

**Step 1 — The principal bundle and its transitions.**
The projection $\pi:\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}$, $z\mapsto[
z]$, is a principal $\mathbb{C}^{\times}$-bundle for the action $z\mapsto tz$. O
n the standard open cover $U_{i}=\{z_{i}\neq0\}$, a section of $\pi$ is
$$\sigma_{i}([z])=\Big(\tfrac{z_{0}}{z_{i}},\dots,\underbrace{1}_{i},\dots,\tfra
c{z_{n}}{z_{i}}\Big).$$
Since $\sigma_{j}=\frac{z_{i}}{z_{j}}\,\sigma_{i}$, the transition function is $
g_{ij}=\frac{z_{i}}{z_{j}}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}$.

**Step 2 — Transition functions of the associated bundle.**
The associated bundle $E=P\times_{\rho}\mathbb{C}$ for the character $\rho(t)=t^
{m}$ has local frame $e_{i}=[\sigma_{i},1]$ over $U_{i}$. Using the quotient rel
ation $(\sigma_{i}\,g_{ij},\,1)\sim(\sigma_{i},\,\rho(g_{ij}^{-1})\,1)= (\sigma_
{i},\,g_{ij}^{-m})$,
$$e_{j}=[\sigma_{j},1]=\big[\sigma_{i}\,g_{ij},\,1\big]=g_{ij}^{-m}\,[\sigma_{i}
,1]=\Big(\frac{z_{j}}{z_{i}}\Big)^{m} e_{i}.$$

**Step 3 — Identify with $\mathcal{O}(k)$.**
The tautological bundle $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ has frame $\tau_{i}([z])=z/z_{i}$ over
 $U_{i}$, so $\tau_{j}=(z_{i}/z_{j})\,\tau_{i}=(z_{j}/z_{i})^{-1}\tau_{i}$. By c
onvention $\mathcal{O}(k)$ is the $k$-th tensor power, with transition $(z_{j}/z
_{i})^{k}$; in particular $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ has exponent $-1$, confirming the co
nvention. Comparing with Step 2,
$$e_{j}=\Big(\frac{z_{j}}{z_{i}}\Big)^{m}e_{i}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad E\co
ng\mathcal{O}(m).$$

**Step 4 — Direct verification via the tautological map (sanity check).**
For $m=-1$, the map $\phi(z,\lambda)=\lambda\,z\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$ lands in the
 tautological line (the line $\mathbb{C}z$ through $[z]$). It is well-defined on
 the quotient since
$$\phi(tz,\,t^{-1}\lambda)=t^{-1}\lambda\cdot tz=\lambda z=\phi(z,\lambda).$$
Thus weight $-1$ gives $\mathcal{O}(-1)$, i.e. weight $m$ gives $\mathcal{O}(m)$
. ✓

**Conclusion.** The character $\rho(t)=t^{m}$ (action $t\cdot\lambda=t^{m}\lambd
a$) yields the line bundle $\mathcal{O}(m)$ on $\mathbb{P}^{n}$. The sign is det

mathbb{C}^{\times}$ on $\mathbb{C}$ gives $\mathcal{O}(+1)$, the hyperplane bund

-1$.

$$\boxed{E\;\cong\;\mathcal{O}(m)}$$
