## Answer

**Yes.** Algebraic $k$-groups of multiplicative type are always linear (for any
field $k$; the hypothesis that $k$ is a number field of characteristic $0$ is no
t needed).

---

## Proof

Let $G$ be an algebraic $k$-group of multiplicative type. We show $G$ admits a c
losed embedding into $\mathrm{GL}(V)$ for some finite-dimensional $k$-vector spa
ce $V$.

### Step 1. $G$ is affine.

By definition of multiplicative type, there exists a finite Galois extension $L/
k$ and a finitely generated abelian group $M$ such that

$$G_L \;\cong\; D(M) := \operatorname{Spec}\bigl(L[M]\bigr),$$

where $L[M]$ is the group algebra of $M$ over $L$. Since $M$ is finitely generat
ed, $L[M]$ is a finitely generated (commutative) $L$-algebra, so $D(M)$ is an **
affine** scheme of finite type over $L$.

Affineness descends along faithfully flat morphisms (fpqc descent; see EGA IV₂,
2.7.1). Since $L/k$ is faithfully flat and $G_L$ is affine, $G$ is **affine** ov
er $k$. Likewise, finite type descends, so $G$ is an affine group scheme of fini
te type over $k$.

### Step 2. Every affine group scheme of finite type over a field is linear.

This is the classical theorem (see Waterhouse, *Introduction to Affine Group Sch
emes*, §3.4, or Milne, *Algebraic Groups*, Ch. 2). We recall the proof for compl
eteness.

Let $A = k[G]$ be the coordinate (Hopf) algebra. Since $G$ is of finite type, $A
$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra; write $A = k[f_1, \dots, f_m]$.

As a coalgebra, $A$ is the directed union of its finite-dimensional subcoalgebra
s (fundamental theorem of coalgebras). Each $f_i$ lies in some finite-dimensiona
l subcoalgebra $V_i \subset A$; set $V = V_1 + \cdots + V_m$, a finite-dimension
al subcoalgebra containing all generators $f_1, \dots, f_m$.

The comultiplication restricts to a map

$$\Delta\big|_V : V \longrightarrow V \otimes_k A,$$

since $V$ is a subcoalgebra. This coaction defines a representation (the left re
gular representation restricted to $V$):

$$\rho_V : G \longrightarrow \mathrm{GL}(V).$$

The kernel of $\rho_V$ is the closed subgroup scheme $H = \operatorname{Spec}(A/
I)$, where $I$ is the ideal generated by $\{v - \varepsilon(v)\cdot 1 : v \in V\
}$ ($\varepsilon$ = counit). A point $g \in G$ lies in $H(R)$ iff $g$ acts trivi
ally on $V \otimes_k R$.

If $g$ acts trivially on $V$, then in particular it acts trivially on $f_1, \dot
s, f_m$. Since the $f_i$ generate $A$ as a $k$-algebra and the $G$-action on $A$
 is by algebra automorphisms, $g$ acts trivially on all of $A$. The left regular
 representation of $G$ on $A$ is faithful (only the identity acts trivially on e
very function), so $g = e$. Therefore

$$\ker(\rho_V) = \{e\},$$

and $\rho_V : G \hookrightarrow \mathrm{GL}(V)$ is a **closed embedding**.

### Conclusion

$G$ is affine (Step 1) and hence linear (Step 2): there exists a faithful finite
-dimensional representation $G \hookrightarrow \mathrm{GL}(V)$ over $k$. $\qquad
\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The argument uses only that $k$ is a field. The number-field / chara
cteristic-$0$ hypothesis is superfluous; it merely ensures (via Cartier's theore
m) that every group scheme of finite type over $k$ is smooth, so "algebraic $k$-
group" and "smooth affine $k$-group" coincide — but neither smoothness nor chara
cteristic $0$ enters the proof above.
