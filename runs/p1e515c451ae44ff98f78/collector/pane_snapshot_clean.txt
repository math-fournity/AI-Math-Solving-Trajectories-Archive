## Proof

We must show that the average of $n\sin(n\pi/180)$ over $n\in\{2,4,6,\dots,180\}
$ equals $\cot(\pi/180)=1/\tan(\pi/180)$.

There are 90 terms. Setting $n=2k$ with $k=1,2,\dots,90$, the average is
$$A=\frac{1}{90}\sum_{k=1}^{90}2k\,\sin\!\left(\frac{2k\pi}{180}\right)=\frac{1}
{90}\sum_{k=1}^{90}2k\,\sin\!\left(\frac{k\pi}{90}\right).$$

So it suffices to prove
$$\sum_{k=1}^{90}2k\,\sin\!\left(\frac{k\pi}{90}\right)=90\cot\!\left(\frac{\pi}
{180}\right).\tag{$\star$}$$

### A summation identity

**Claim.** For positive integer $N$ and real $\theta\notin 2\pi\mathbb{Z}$,
$$\sum_{k=1}^{N}k\sin(k\theta)=\frac{(N+1)\sin(N\theta)-N\sin\big((N+1)\theta\bi
g)}{4\sin^{2}(\theta/2)}.$$

*Proof of claim.* Let $z=e^{i\theta}$. The standard geometric-sum differentiatio
n gives
$$\sum_{k=1}^{N}kz^{k}=z\frac{d}{dz}\frac{z-z^{N+1}}{1-z}=\frac{z\big(1-(N+1)z^{
N}+Nz^{N+1}\big)}{(1-z)^{2}}.$$
Since $1-z=-2i\,e^{i\theta/2}\sin(\theta/2)$, we get $(1-z)^{2}=-4e^{i\theta}\si
n^{2}(\theta/2)$, hence
$$\frac{z}{(1-z)^{2}}=\frac{-1}{4\sin^{2}(\theta/2)}\in\mathbb{R}.$$
Therefore
$$\sum_{k=1}^{N}k\sin(k\theta)=\operatorname{Im}\!\left[\sum_{k=1}^{N}kz^{k}\rig
ht]=\frac{-\operatorname{Im}\!\big[1-(N+1)e^{iN\theta}+Ne^{i(N+1)\theta}\big]}{4
\sin^{2}(\theta/2)}.$$
The imaginary part of the bracket is $-(N+1)\sin(N\theta)+N\sin((N+1)\theta)$, y
ielding the claim. $\square$

### Applying the identity

Take $N=90$ and $\theta=\pi/90$. Then $N\theta=\pi$ and $(N+1)\theta=91\pi/90=\p
i+\pi/90$, so
$$\sin(N\theta)=\sin\pi=0,\qquad \sin\big((N+1)\theta\big)=\sin\!\left(\pi+\frac
{\pi}{90}\right)=-\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{90}\right).$$

Substituting into the claim:
$$\sum_{k=1}^{90}k\sin\!\left(\frac{k\pi}{90}\right)=\frac{91\cdot 0-90\big(-\si
n(\pi/90)\big)}{4\sin^{2}(\pi/180)}=\frac{90\sin(\pi/90)}{4\sin^{2}(\pi/180)}.$$

Using the double-angle identity $\sin(\pi/90)=\sin(2\cdot\pi/180)=2\sin(\pi/180)
\cos(\pi/180)$:
$$\sum_{k=1}^{90}k\sin\!\left(\frac{k\pi}{90}\right)=\frac{90\cdot 2\sin(\pi/180
)\cos(\pi/180)}{4\sin^{2}(\pi/180)}=\frac{90\cos(\pi/180)}{2\sin(\pi/180)}=45\co
t\!\left(\frac{\pi}{180}\right).$$

Multiplying by 2 gives the sum in $(\star)$:
$$\sum_{k=1}^{90}2k\,\sin\!\left(\frac{k\pi}{90}\right)=2\cdot 45\cot\!\left(\fr
ac{\pi}{180}\right)=90\cot\!\left(\frac{\pi}{180}\right).$$

### Conclusion

The average is therefore
$$A=\frac{1}{90}\cdot 90\cot\!\left(\frac{\pi}{180}\right)=\cot\!\left(\frac{\pi
}{180}\right)=\frac{1}{\tan(\pi/180)}.$$

This is exactly the desired quantity, completing the proof. $\blacksquare$
