## Proof

Let $R = k[x]/\langle x^2\rangle$, and let $\bar x$ denote the image of $x$ in $
R$ (so $\bar x^2 = 0$). The field $k$ is the simple $R$-module $k \cong R/\langl
e \bar x\rangle$, where $\bar x$ acts on $k$ as $0$.

### Step 1. A projective resolution of $k$ over $R$

$R$ is a local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m = \langle \bar x\rangle$ and
 residue field $k = R/\mathfrak m$. Since $R$ is self-injective (Frobenius), eve
ry free module is projective, and we build the minimal free resolution.

The augmentation $\varepsilon : R \to k$ has kernel $\ker \varepsilon = \mathfra
k m = \bar x R$. Define $d_1 : R \to R$ by $d_1(r) = \bar x r$ (multiplication b
y $\bar x$). Then $\operatorname{im} d_1 = \bar x R = \ker \varepsilon$. Now
$$\ker d_1 = \{r \in R : \bar x r = 0\} = \langle \bar x\rangle = \bar x R,$$
because $\bar x \cdot 1 = \bar x \neq 0$ while $\bar x \cdot \bar x = 0$. Hence
$\ker d_1 = \bar x R = \operatorname{im}(\bar x\cdot)$. Setting every $d_n : R \
to R$ to be multiplication by $\bar x$, we obtain a free resolution
$$\cdots \xrightarrow{\;\bar x\;} R \xrightarrow{\;\bar x\;} R \xrightarrow{\;\b
ar x\;} R \xrightarrow{\;\varepsilon\;} k \longrightarrow 0.$$
This resolution is $1$-periodic (every differential is multiplication by $\bar x
$).

### Step 2. Apply $\operatorname{Hom}_R(-,k)$

For each copy of $R$ we have $\operatorname{Hom}_R(R, k) \cong k$ via $f \mapsto
 f(1)$. Indeed an $R$-linear map $f : R \to k$ is determined by $a = f(1) \in k$
, and the $R$-linearity condition $f(\bar x\cdot 1) = \bar x\cdot f(1) = 0$ is a
utomatic since $\bar x$ acts as $0$ on $k$.

The induced differential $d_n^* : \operatorname{Hom}_R(R,k) \to \operatorname{Ho
m}_R(R,k)$ is precomposition with $d_n = (\bar x\cdot)$. For $f$ with $f(1) = a$
,
$$d_n^*(f)(1) = f(d_n(1)) = f(\bar x) = f(\bar x\cdot 1) = \bar x\cdot f(1) = \b
ar x\cdot a = 0 \in k.$$
Thus **$d_n^* = 0$ for every $n \geq 1$**.

### Step 3. Compute the Ext groups

By definition
$$\operatorname{Ext}_R^n(k,k) = \ker d_n^* / \operatorname{im} d_{n+1}^*.$$
Since every $d_n^* = 0$, we get $\ker d_n^* = k$ and $\operatorname{im} d_{n+1}^
* = 0$, hence
$$\boxed{\operatorname{Ext}_{k[x]/\langle x^2\rangle}^n(k,k) \;\cong\; k \quad\t
ext{for every } n \geq 0.}$$

### Step 4. The Ext-algebra structure (Yoneda product)

The resolution is $1$-periodic, so the identity map of the resolution shifted by
 one degree gives a degree-$1$ element $\eta \in \operatorname{Ext}_R^1(k,k) \co
ng k$ (a generator). Composing shifts repeatedly yields $\eta^n \in \operatornam
e{Ext}_R^n(k,k)$, and since each $\operatorname{Ext}^n$ is one-dimensional over
$k$, these powers generate the whole algebra. There are no relations (the resolu
tion never "wraps around" with a nontrivial scalar — the period is realized by t
he identity shift, not by a higher-period map). Therefore
$$\operatorname{Ext}_{k[x]/\langle x^2\rangle}^{*}(k,k) \;\cong\; k[\eta], \qqua
d |\eta| = 1,$$
a polynomial algebra on one generator of degree $1$.

### Summary

As a graded $k$-vector space, $\operatorname{Ext}_{k[x]/\langle x^2\rangle}^n(k,
k) \cong k$ for all $n \geq 0$ (one copy of $k$ in each non-negative degree). As
 a graded $k$-algebra under Yoneda composition, it is the polynomial ring $k[\et
a]$ with $\eta$ in degree $1$.
