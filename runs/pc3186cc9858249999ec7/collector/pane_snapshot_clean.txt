Looking at this problem, I need to find where the inscribed circle of an isoscel
es right triangle intersects segment AP, where P is the intersection of the insc
ribed circle with the altitude CD to the hypotenuse.

## Setup

Let the right angle be at C, with legs CA = CB = a. Place coordinates: C = (0,0)
, A = (a,0), B = (0,a). The hypotenuse AB lies on x + y = a.

**Altitude foot D:** The foot of the perpendicular from C to line x + y = a is D
 = (a/2, a/2).

**Inscribed circle:** The inradius is r = (2a − a√2)/2 = a(2−√2)/2, with incente
r at (r, r).

## Finding P

The altitude CD is the line y = x. Substituting into the circle (x−r)² + (y−r)²
= r²:

$$2(x - r)^2 = r^2 \implies x = r\left(1 \pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)$$

**Far intersection:** x = r(1 + 1/√2) = a(2−√2)/2 · (2+√2)/2 = a(4−2)/4 = **a/2
= D**.

So D itself lies on the inscribed circle — a beautiful property of the isosceles
 right triangle. Therefore P is the **near** intersection (closer to C):

$$P = (p, p), \quad p = r\!\left(1 - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right) = \frac{a(3 - 2\
sqrt{2})}{2}$$

(Verified: p − r = (1−√2)/2 = −r/√2, so (p−r)² = r²/2 ✓)

## Where the circle crosses segment AP

Parametrize AP: $(a, 0) + t(p - a,\; p)$ for $t \in [0,1]$, with A at t=0 and P
at t=1.

Substituting into the circle equation and simplifying (set a=1, u = 1−r = √2/2):

$$t^2\!\left[(1-p)^2 + p^2\right] - t\!\left[2u(1-p) + 2pr\right] + u^2 = 0$$

Computing the coefficients exactly:
- $(1-p)^2 + p^2 = \frac{13 - 8\sqrt{2}}{2}$
- $u^2 = \frac{1}{2}$

One root is t = 1 (since P is on the circle). By Vieta's formulas, the product o
f roots is:

$$t_1 \cdot t_2 = \frac{u^2}{(1-p)^2 + p^2} = \frac{1/2}{(13-8\sqrt{2})/2} = \fr
ac{1}{13 - 8\sqrt{2}}$$

So the other root is:

$$t_2 = \frac{1}{13 - 8\sqrt{2}} = \frac{13 + 8\sqrt{2}}{41}$$

## The ratio

The circle divides AP at the point t = t₂ (between A and P). The two parts are p
roportional to t₂ and (1 − t₂):

$$t_2 : (1 - t_2) = (13 + 8\sqrt{2}) : (28 - 8\sqrt{2})$$

Simplifying: since $13 + 8\sqrt{2} = (3+2\sqrt{2})(7-2\sqrt{2})$ and $28 - 8\sqr
t{2} = 4(7-2\sqrt{2})$:

$$\frac{13 + 8\sqrt{2}}{28 - 8\sqrt{2}} = \frac{(3+2\sqrt{2})(7-2\sqrt{2})}{4(7-
2\sqrt{2})} = \frac{3 + 2\sqrt{2}}{4}$$

**Numerical check:** t₂ ≈ 0.5929, ratio ≈ 0.5929/0.4071 ≈ 1.457, and (3+2√2)/4 ≈
 5.828/4 ≈ 1.457 ✓

## Answer

The circle divides segment AP in the ratio

$$\boxed{(3 + 2\sqrt{2}) : 4}$$

where the first part (from A to the circle) is the longer one.
