## Proof

Let $v \colon K^{\times} \to \mathbb{Z}$ be the normalized discrete valuation as
sociated to the DVR $R$, so that
$$R = \{x \in K : v(x) \ge 0\} \cup \{0\}.$$
Since $R \ne K$ (a DVR is a proper subring of its fraction field), there exists
a uniformizer $\pi \in R$ with $v(\pi)=1$ and $v(\pi^{-1})=-1$, so $\pi^{-1}\not
in R$.

Let $\phi \in \operatorname{Aut}(K)$. Define $w \colon K^{\times}\to\mathbb{Z}$
by $w(x)=v(\phi^{-1}(x))$. This is again a discrete valuation of $K$ (compositio
n of a valuation with a field automorphism), and its valuation ring is
$$\mathcal{O}_w=\{x\in K:w(x)\ge 0\}\cup\{0\}=\{x\in K:v(\phi^{-1}(x))\ge 0\}\cu
p\{0\}=\phi(R).$$
So $\phi(R)$ is a valuation ring of $K$.

**Key fact.** If $V_1\subseteq V_2$ are two valuation rings of the same field $K
$, then the valuation defining $V_2$ is a coarsening of the valuation defining $
V_1$. A rank‑$1$ valuation (such as the one defining the DVR $R$) has only two c
oarsenings: itself and the trivial valuation, whose valuation ring is all of $K$
.

Suppose, for contradiction, that $R \subsetneq \phi(R)$. Then $R$ and $\phi(R)$
are comparable valuation rings of $K$ with $R\subsetneq\phi(R)$. Since $R$ is a
DVR (rank $1$), the only valuation ring of $K$ strictly containing $R$ is $K$ it
self. Hence $\phi(R)=K$.

But $\phi$ is a bijection $K\to K$, so $\phi(R)=K$ implies $R=\phi^{-1}(K)=K$, c
ontradicting the fact that a DVR is a proper subring of its fraction field.

Therefore $R$ cannot be a proper subset of $\phi(R)$. $\blacksquare$
