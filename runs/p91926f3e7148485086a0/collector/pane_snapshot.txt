## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, the two integrals are equal for all $B \in \mathcal{G}$.

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains only the question, no solu
tion, sketch, or answer. Proceeding.

---

Let $f: \Omega \to [0,+\infty)$ be $\mathcal{G}$-measurable and let $B \in \math
cal{G}$. We compare

$$I_1 := \int_B f\,d\mu \quad\text{(in }(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)\text{)}, \qquad
 I_2 := \int_B f\,d\mu|_\mathcal{G} \quad\text{(in }(\Omega,\mathcal{G},\mu|_\ma
thcal{G})\text{)}.$$

Recall the standard construction of the Lebesgue integral of a non-negative meas
urable function: it is the supremum of integrals of non-negative simple function
s dominated by $f$:

$$\int_B f\,d\nu \;=\; \sup\left\{\int_B s\,d\nu \;:\; 0 \le s \le f,\; s \text{
 simple and measurable w.r.t.\ the relevant }\sigma\text{-algebra}\right\}.$$

**Step 1 — Simple functions coincide.** A function $s:\Omega\to[0,+\infty)$ is a
 $\mathcal{G}$-measurable simple function if and only if it has the form $s = \s
um_{i=1}^{n} a_i\,\mathbf{1}_{A_i}$ with $a_i \ge 0$ and $A_i \in \mathcal{G}$.
This characterization depends **only** on $\mathcal{G}$, not on the ambient $\si
gma$-algebra $\mathcal{F}$. Hence the class of admissible simple functions $0 \l
e s \le f$ is **identical** in both measure spaces:

$$\{s : 0\le s\le f,\; s\text{ simple, }\mathcal{G}\text{-measurable}\} \;=\; \{
s : 0\le s\le f,\; s\text{ simple, }\mathcal{F}\text{-measurable with }s\text{ a
ctually }\mathcal{G}\text{-measurable}\}.$$

(The right-hand set is no larger: if $s\le f$ and $s$ is $\mathcal{F}$-simple bu
t not $\mathcal{G}$-measurable, it is not admissible for $I_2$; but it is also *
*not** admissible for $I_1$ in the sense that it does not help, since the suprem
um for $I_1$ over *all* $\mathcal{F}$-simple $0\le s\le f$ equals the supremum o
ver $\mathcal{G}$-simple ones — see Step 3.)

**Step 2 — Integrals of simple functions coincide.** For $s = \sum_{i=1}^n a_i\,
\mathbf{1}_{A_i}$ with $A_i \in \mathcal{G}$ and $B \in \mathcal{G}$, the set $B
\cap A_i \in \mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{F}$, so

$$\int_B s\,d\mu \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^n a_i\,\mu(B\cap A_i) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^n a_i\,
\mu|_\mathcal{G}(B\cap A_i) \;=\; \int_B s\,d\mu|_\mathcal{G},$$

where the middle equality uses the definition $\mu|_\mathcal{G}(E)=\mu(E)$ for $
E\in\mathcal{G}$.

**Step 3 — Suprema coincide.** The integral of $f$ is the supremum of integrals
of simple functions below it. We must confirm that taking the supremum over *all
* $\mathcal{F}$-measurable simple $0\le s\le f$ (for $I_1$) gives the same value
 as taking it over only $\mathcal{G}$-measurable simple functions.

This follows from the standard approximation theorem: for any non-negative $\mat
hcal{G}$-measurable $f$, the canonical approximating sequence

$$s_n \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{n2^n} \frac{k}{2^n}\,\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\frac{k}{2^n}\le
 f < \frac{k+1}{2^n}\right\}} \;+\; n\,\mathbf{1}_{\{f\ge n\}}$$

consists entirely of $\mathcal{G}$-measurable simple functions (the level sets $
\{f < c\}$ lie in $\mathcal{G}$ by $\mathcal{G}$-measurability of $f$), with $s_
n \uparrow f$ pointwise. By the Monotone Convergence Theorem,

$$\int_B f\,d\mu \;=\; \lim_{n\to\infty}\int_B s_n\,d\mu \;=\; \lim_{n\to\infty}
\int_B s_n\,d\mu|_\mathcal{G} \;=\; \int_B f\,d\mu|_\mathcal{G},$$

where the middle equality is Step 2 (applied to each $s_n$, which is $\mathcal{G
}$-simple), and the two outer equalities are MCT applied in the respective measu
re spaces. Since $s_n\uparrow f$ holds pointwise in **both** spaces, MCT is vali
d in both.

**Conclusion.** For every $B\in\mathcal{G}$ and every non-negative $\mathcal{G}$
-measurable $f$,

$$\boxed{\int_B f\,d\mu \;=\; \int_B f\,d\mu|_\mathcal{G}.}$$

The two integrals are equal. The essential reason is that the integral of a $\ma
thcal{G}$-measurable function depends only on the values of the measure on $\mat
hcal{G}$-sets, and $\mu$ and $\mu|_\mathcal{G}$ agree on $\mathcal{G}$ by defini
tion.
