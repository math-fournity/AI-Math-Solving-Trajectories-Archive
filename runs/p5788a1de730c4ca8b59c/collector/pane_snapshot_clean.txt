# Feasible Potentials and Negative Circuits

## Definitions

Let $G$ be a digraph with edge weights $c : E(G) \to \mathbb{R}$. A **feasible p
otential** for $(G, c)$ is a function $p : V(G) \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfying, for
every edge $e = uv \in E(G)$,

$$
p(v) - p(u) \;\le\; c(uv),
$$

or equivalently the **reduced cost** $\bar c(uv) := c(uv) + p(u) - p(v) \ge 0$.

A **negative circuit** is a directed circuit $C$ whose total weight $\sum_{e \in
 C} c(e) < 0$.

## Main Theorem

> **A feasible potential for $(G, c)$ exists if and only if $G$ contains no nega
tive circuit.**

### Proof

**($\Rightarrow$) Feasible potential $\Rightarrow$ no negative circuit.**

Suppose $p$ is feasible and let $C = v_0 v_1 \cdots v_{k-1} v_0$ be any directed
 circuit. Summing the feasibility inequalities $p(v_{i+1}) - p(v_i) \le c(v_i v_
{i+1})$ (indices mod $k$) over all edges of $C$, the left-hand side telescopes:

$$
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\bigl[p(v_{i+1}) - p(v_i)\bigr] = 0 \;\le\; \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} c(v
_i v_{i+1}) = \sum_{e \in C} c(e).
$$

Hence every circuit has non-negative total weight; in particular no negative cir
cuit exists. $\checkmark$

**($\Leftarrow$) No negative circuit $\Rightarrow$ feasible potential exists.**

Augment $G$ by adding a new vertex $s$ and edges $s \to v$ of weight $0$ for eve
ry $v \in V(G)$; call the result $G'$. Any circuit of $G'$ is either a circuit o
f $G$ (hence non-negative by hypothesis) or passes through $s$ — but $s$ has in-
degree $0$, so no circuit passes through $s$. Thus $G'$ has no negative circuit.

Since $G'$ has no negative circuit, shortest-path distances $d(s, v)$ from $s$ t
o every $v$ are well defined and finite. Define

$$
p(v) := d(s, v), \qquad v \in V(G).
$$

For any edge $uv \in E(G)$, the shortest path to $v$ cannot be longer than the s
hortest path to $u$ followed by the edge $uv$:

$$
p(v) = d(s, v) \;\le\; d(s, u) + c(uv) = p(u) + c(uv),
$$

so $p(v) - p(u) \le c(uv)$. This is exactly feasibility. $\checkmark$

Combining both directions, the theorem is proved. $\blacksquare$

## Algorithm: Bellman–Ford

**Input:** Digraph $G = (V, E)$, weights $c : E \to \mathbb{R}$.

**Step 1 (Augmentation).** Add an auxiliary vertex $s$ with a $0$-weight edge $s
 \to v$ to every $v \in V$. Set $n = |V| + 1$.

**Step 2 (Initialization).** Set $d(s) = 0$ and $d(v) = 0$ for all $v \in V$ (eq
uivalently $+\infty$; the $0$-init via the $s$-edges makes every vertex reachabl
e from $s$).

**Step 3 (Relaxation).** Repeat $n - 1 = |V|$ times: for every edge $uv \in E(G'
)$, if $d(u) + c(uv) < d(v)$, set $d(v) := d(u) + c(uv)$.

**Step 4 (Negative-circuit detection).** Perform one more pass over all edges of
 $G'$. If any edge $uv$ still satisfies $d(u) + c(uv) < d(v)$, then $G'$ (and he
nce $G$) contains a negative circuit — **report it**.

**Step 5 (Output potential).** If no edge can be relaxed in Step 4, then $p(v) :
= d(v)$ for $v \in V$ is a feasible potential. Output $p$.

### Correctness

- If $G$ has no negative circuit, then after $|V|$ relaxation passes all shortes
t-path distances from $s$ in $G'$ have stabilized (shortest paths use at most $|
V|$ edges), so Step 4 finds no relaxable edge, and the argument of the theorem's
 ($\Leftarrow$) direction shows $p = d$ is feasible.
- If $G$ has a negative circuit $C$, then some vertex $w$ on $C$ is reachable fr
om $s$, and traversing $C$ repeatedly makes $d(w)$ arbitrarily negative. Hence a
fter $|V|$ passes at least one edge of $C$ is still relaxable, and Step 4 detect
s the negative circuit. (The relaxable edges lie on or lead into a negative circ
uit, which can be extracted by tracing predecessor pointers.)

### Time Complexity

- Augmentation: $O(|V|)$ new edges.
- Each relaxation pass scans all $|E| + |V|$ edges once: $O(|E| + |V|)$.
- There are $|V| + 1$ passes total ($|V|$ relaxation passes $+$ $1$ detection pa
ss).
- **Total: $O\!\bigl(|V|\cdot|E|\bigr)$.**

Space complexity is $O(|V| + |E|)$ for storing the graph and the distance/predec
essor arrays.
