## Proof

Since $A$ is a finite-dimensional commutative $k$-algebra, $A$ is Artinian. Ther
efore $A$ decomposes as a finite product of local Artinian rings:
$$A \cong A_1 \times \cdots \times A_n$$
corresponding to primitive orthogonal idempotents $e_1, \ldots, e_n$ with $e_1 +
 \cdots + e_n = 1$. The spectrum $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ consists of exactly $n
$ points — the maximal ideals $\mathfrak{m}_1, \ldots, \mathfrak{m}_n$, where $\
mathfrak{m}_i$ is the kernel of the projection $A \to A_i$.

**$G$ permutes the primitive idempotents.** For $g \in G$, the element $g(e_i)$
is an idempotent since $g(e_i)^2 = g(e_i^2) = g(e_i)$. It is primitive: if $g(e_
i) = f_1 + f_2$ with $f_1, f_2$ nonzero orthogonal idempotents, then applying $g
^{-1}$ would decompose $e_i$ nontrivially, contradicting primitivity. So $g(e_i)
 = e_{\sigma_g(i)}$ for a permutation $\sigma_g$ of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$. This permu
tation action is exactly the action of $G$ on $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$, since $g
(\mathfrak{m}_i) = \mathfrak{m}_{\sigma_g(i)}$.

**Constructing invariant idempotents from orbits.** Let $O_1, \ldots, O_r$ be th
e orbits of $G$ on $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ (equivalently, on $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$).
 For each orbit $O_j$, define:
$$\varepsilon_j = \sum_{i \in O_j} e_i.$$

Each $\varepsilon_j$ is $G$-invariant: for any $g \in G$,
$$g(\varepsilon_j) = \sum_{i \in O_j} g(e_i) = \sum_{i \in O_j} e_{\sigma_g(i)}
= \sum_{i \in O_j} e_i = \varepsilon_j,$$
since $\sigma_g$ permutes the orbit $O_j$ as a set. Therefore $\varepsilon_j \in
 A^G$.

The idempotents $\varepsilon_1, \ldots, \varepsilon_r$ satisfy:
- **Nonzero:** each $O_j$ is nonempty, so $\varepsilon_j \neq 0$.
- **Orthogonal:** $\varepsilon_j \varepsilon_l = 0$ for $j \neq l$ since the orb
its are disjoint (so $e_i e_{i'} = 0$ for $i \in O_j, i' \in O_l$).
- **Sum to 1:** $\varepsilon_1 + \cdots + \varepsilon_r = e_1 + \cdots + e_n = 1
$.

**Conclusion.** Suppose for contradiction that $r \geq 2$. Then $\varepsilon_1$
is a nontrivial idempotent in $A^G$ (i.e., $\varepsilon_1 \neq 0$ and $\varepsil
on_1 \neq 1$). A ring with a nontrivial idempotent is not local, hence not a fie
ld. But $A^G \cong k$ and $k$ is a field — contradiction.

Therefore $r = 1$, which means $G$ acts transitively on $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$
.

**Remark.** This argument is characteristic-independent — it relies only on the
combinatorial structure of idempotents and orbits, not on any Reynolds operator
or invertibility of $|G|$. The modular case (where $\operatorname{char}(k) \mid
|G|$) presents no obstruction here, because the orbit-to-idempotent corresponden
ce is purely set-theoretic.
