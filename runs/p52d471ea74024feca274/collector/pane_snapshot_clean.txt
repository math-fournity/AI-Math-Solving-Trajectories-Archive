(0,1) ∩ ℚ] ⊆ (a, b) ⊆ [a, b].

By the classical theorem that **every countable linear order embeds into ℚ** (pr

Thus no countable ordinal can serve as the desired α.

**Part 2: ω₁ does not embed into any bounded interval of ℝ.**

Suppose for contradiction that f: ω₁ → [a, b] ⊆ ℝ is an order-embedding. Then W

**Key Lemma:** Every well-ordered subset of ℝ is countable.

*Proof of Lemma.* Let W ⊆ ℝ be well-ordered (in the usual order on ℝ). For each
w ∈ W that is not the maximum of W, let w⁺ denote its immediate successor in W.
Then w < w⁺, so the open interval (w, w⁺) is nonempty and contains a rational nu
mber q_w. The intervals {(w, w⁺) : w ∈ W \ {max W}} are pairwise disjoint (if w₁

ne rational from each interval gives an injection W \ {max W} ↪ ℚ, proving W is
countable. □

Since ω₁ is uncountable, W = f[ω₁] would be an uncountable well-ordered subset o

**Conclusion.**

- Every countable ordinal embeds into *every* bounded interval of *every* ordere

- ω₁ fails to embed into any bounded interval of ℝ, which is an ordered field (P
