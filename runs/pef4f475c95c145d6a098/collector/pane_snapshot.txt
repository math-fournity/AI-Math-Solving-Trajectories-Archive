## Proof

We evaluate $\displaystyle\oint_{|z|=2} \tan z\,dz$ using the residue theorem.

**Step 1: Identify the singularities of $\tan z$ inside $|z|=2$.**

Since $\tan z = \dfrac{\sin z}{\cos z}$, the singularities occur at the zeros of
 $\cos z$, namely
$$z = \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi,\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}.$$

We need those with $|z|<2$. Since $\pi/2 \approx 1.5708 < 2$:
- $k=0$: $z=\pi/2$, $|\pi/2|<2$ ✓
- $k=-1$: $z=-\pi/2$, $|-\pi/2|<2$ ✓
- $k=1$: $z=3\pi/2\approx 4.712$, $|3\pi/2|>2$ ✗
- $k=-2$: $z=-3\pi/2$, $|{-3\pi/2}|>2$ ✗

All other values of $k$ give even larger magnitudes. Hence exactly **two** poles
 lie inside the contour: $z=\pi/2$ and $z=-\pi/2$.

**Step 2: Compute the residue at each pole.**

Each zero of $\cos z$ is simple (since $\cos' z = -\sin z \neq 0$ at these point
s). For a quotient $f/g$ with a simple zero of $g$ at $z_0$,
$$\operatorname{Res}\!\left(\frac{f}{g},\,z_0\right)=\frac{f(z_0)}{g'(z_0)}.$$

Here $f(z)=\sin z$, $g(z)=\cos z$, $g'(z)=-\sin z$, so
$$\operatorname{Res}(\tan z,\,z_0)=\frac{\sin z_0}{-\sin z_0}=-1.$$

- At $z_0=\pi/2$: $\sin(\pi/2)=1\neq 0$, so $\operatorname{Res}(\tan z,\,\pi/2)=
-1$.
- At $z_0=-\pi/2$: $\sin(-\pi/2)=-1\neq 0$, so $\operatorname{Res}(\tan z,\,-\pi
/2)=-1$.

**Step 3: Apply the residue theorem.**

$$\oint_{|z|=2}\tan z\,dz = 2\pi i\sum \operatorname{Res} = 2\pi i\bigl((-1)+(-1
)\bigr)=2\pi i\cdot(-2).$$

$$\boxed{\oint_{|z|=2}\tan z\,dz = -4\pi i.}$$
