## Part (a): Solution via Duhamel's Principle

We solve the inhomogeneous wave equation
$$u_{tt} = c^2 u_{xx} + Q(x,t), \qquad u(x,0)=0,\quad u_t(x,0)=0.$$

**Duhamel's principle.** For each fixed $\tau \ge 0$, let $w(x,t;\tau)$ solve th
e *homogeneous* wave equation
$$w_{tt} = c^2 w_{xx}$$
with initial data at time $t=\tau$:
$$w(x,\tau;\tau) = 0, \qquad w_t(x,\tau;\tau) = Q(x,\tau).$$

Then
$$u(x,t) = \int_0^t w(x,t;\tau)\,d\tau$$
solves the inhomogeneous problem. Verification: $u(x,0)=0$ since the integral co
llapses. By Leibniz,
$$u_t(x,t) = w(x,t;t) + \int_0^t w_t(x,t;\tau)\,d\tau = 0 + \int_0^t w_t\,d\tau,
$$
since $w(x,t;t)=0$. Hence $u_t(x,0)=0$. Differentiating again,
$$u_{tt} = w_t(x,t;t) + \int_0^t w_{tt}\,d\tau = Q(x,t) + c^2\int_0^t w_{xx}\,d\
tau = Q(x,t) + c^2 u_{xx}. \checkmark$$

**d'Alembert's formula for $w$.** The homogeneous wave equation with initial dis
placement $f$ and initial velocity $g$ at time $t=\tau$ has solution
$$w(x,t;\tau) = \tfrac{1}{2}[f(x-c(t-\tau))+f(x+c(t-\tau))] + \frac{1}{2c}\int_{
x-c(t-\tau)}^{x+c(t-\tau)} g(s)\,ds.$$
With $f=0$ and $g(s)=Q(s,\tau)$:
$$w(x,t;\tau) = \frac{1}{2c}\int_{x-c(t-\tau)}^{x+c(t-\tau)} Q(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi.$
$

**Final solution.**
$$\boxed{\displaystyle u(x,t) = \frac{1}{2c}\int_0^t\!\int_{x-c(t-\tau)}^{x+c(t-
\tau)} Q(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi\,d\tau.}$$

## Part (b): Domain of Dependence of $u(x_1,t_1)$

From the formula, $u(x_1,t_1)$ depends on $Q(\xi,\tau)$ exactly for those $(\xi,
\tau)$ satisfying
$$0 \le \tau \le t_1 \qquad\text{and}\qquad x_1 - c(t_1-\tau) \le \xi \le x_1 +
c(t_1-\tau).$$

Equivalently, $|\xi - x_1| \le c(t_1 - \tau)$ with $0\le \tau\le t_1$. This is t
he **backward light cone** (past cone) with apex at the space-time point $(x_1,
t_1)$:

$$\boxed{\;D(x_1,t_1) = \bigl\{(\xi,\tau) : 0\le \tau \le t_1,\; |\xi - x_1| \le
 c(t_1-\tau)\bigr\}.\;}$$

Geometrically, this is the isosceles triangle in the $(x,t)$-plane with:
- **Apex** at $(x_1, t_1)$,
- **Base vertices** at $(x_1 - ct_1,\, 0)$ and $(x_1 + ct_1,\, 0)$ on the $t=0$
axis.

**Interpretation.** A source $Q(\xi,\tau)$ located at position $\xi$ and time $\
tau$ influences the field at $(x_1,t_1)$ if and only if the signal traveling at
speed $c$ emitted from $\xi$ at time $\tau$ reaches $x_1$ by time $t_1$, i.e., $
|\xi-x_1|/c \le t_1 - \tau$, and the source acts at a non-negative time $\tau\ge
 0$. Sources outside this cone (either too far away, or too late) have no effect
 on $u(x_1,t_1)$ — this is the **finite propagation speed** property of the wave
 equation.
