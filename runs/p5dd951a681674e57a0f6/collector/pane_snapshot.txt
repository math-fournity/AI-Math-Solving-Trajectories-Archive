Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether 1968 line segments can be a
rranged so that every endpoint of every segment lies strictly in the interior of
 some other segment.

**Answer: No, it is not possible.**

**Proof.**

Suppose for contradiction that such an arrangement of segments $S_1, S_2, \ldots
, S_{1968}$ exists. Let $P$ be the set of all distinct points that serve as an e
ndpoint of at least one of these segments.

**Key observation.** Every point of $P$ must lie strictly in the interior of som
e segment $S_j$. Indeed, each point $p \in P$ is an endpoint of some segment $S_
i$; by hypothesis both endpoints of $S_i$ lie strictly inside *other* segments,
so $p$ lies strictly in the interior of some $S_j$ ($j \neq i$). This means $p$
lies strictly between the two endpoints of $S_j$ on the line containing $S_j$ —
and both of those endpoints belong to $P$.

**Convex hull argument.** Consider the convex hull $H = \operatorname{conv}(P)$.
 Since $P$ is finite and nonempty, $H$ is a convex polygon (or a segment/point)
whose vertices are elements of $P$. Let $v$ be a vertex of $H$.

**Claim.** $v$ cannot lie strictly between two points of $P$ on any line.

*Proof of claim.* Let $L$ be any line through $v$ and another point $w \in P$. T
he intersection $L \cap H$ is a (non-degenerate) segment $[a,b]$, since it conta
ins both $v$ and $w$. Because $v$ is a **vertex** (extreme point) of $H$, it can
not be expressed as a convex combination of two distinct points of $H$; in parti
cular $v$ cannot lie in the open segment $(a,b)$. Hence $v \in \{a, b\}$, i.e.,
$v$ is an endpoint of $[a,b]$. Since every point of $P$ lying on $L$ is containe
d in $[a,b]$, and $v$ is an endpoint of $[a,b]$, the point $v$ is **not** strict
ly between any two points of $P$ on $L$. $\square$

**Contradiction.** By the key observation, $v$ must lie strictly in the interior
 of some segment $S_j$, hence strictly between the two endpoints of $S_j$ (both
in $P$) on the line of $S_j$. This directly contradicts the Claim.

Therefore no such arrangement exists — for 1968 segments, or indeed for any posi
tive number of segments. $\blacksquare$
