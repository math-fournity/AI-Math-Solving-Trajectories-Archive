## Proof

Let $ABCD$ be a cyclic quadrilateral (vertices in order on the circle) with circ
umradius $R$. Define the half central angles subtending each side:
$$\angle AOB = 2\alpha,\quad \angle BOC = 2\beta,\quad \angle COD = 2\gamma,\qua
d \angle DOA = 2\delta,$$
so that
$$\alpha+\beta+\gamma+\delta = \pi.$$

By the chord–central angle relation, with $AB=AD=a$, $BC=b$, $CD=c$:
$$a=2R\sin\alpha,\quad b=2R\sin\beta,\quad c=2R\sin\gamma,\quad a=2R\sin\delta.$
$

**Using $AB=AD$.** Since $\sin\alpha=\sin\delta$ and all half-angles lie in $(0,
\pi)$, either $\delta=\alpha$ or $\delta=\pi-\alpha$. The latter gives $\beta+\g
amma=0$, impossible. Hence
$$\delta=\alpha,\qquad 2\alpha+\beta+\gamma=\pi,\qquad \gamma=\pi-2\alpha-\beta.
$$

**Using $AB+BC=CD$.** The condition $a+b=c$ becomes
$$\sin\alpha+\sin\beta=\sin\gamma=\sin(\pi-2\alpha-\beta)=\sin(2\alpha+\beta).$$

Expanding $\sin(2\alpha+\beta)=\sin 2\alpha\cos\beta+\cos 2\alpha\sin\beta$:
$$\sin\alpha+\sin\beta = 2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\cos\beta+(1-2\sin^2\alpha)\sin\be
ta.$$
Rearranging,
$$\sin\alpha = 2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\cos\beta-2\sin^2\alpha\sin\beta.$$
Dividing by $\sin\alpha\neq 0$:
$$1 = 2(\cos\alpha\cos\beta-\sin\alpha\sin\beta)=2\cos(\alpha+\beta).$$
Thus
$$\cos(\alpha+\beta)=\tfrac12 \implies \alpha+\beta=\tfrac{\pi}{3}$$
(the only value in $(0,\pi)$ compatible with the geometry).

**Computing $\angle CDA$.** The inscribed angle $\angle CDA$ subtends the arc $\
widehat{CA}$ not containing $D$, which is $\widehat{CB}+\widehat{BA}=2\beta+2\al
pha$. Therefore
$$\angle CDA = \tfrac12(2\alpha+2\beta)=\alpha+\beta=\frac{\pi}{3}.$$

$$\boxed{\measuredangle CDA = 60^\circ}$$
