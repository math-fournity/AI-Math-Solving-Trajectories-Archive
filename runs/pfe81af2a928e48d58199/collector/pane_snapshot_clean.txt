Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the axiom of replacement al
one guarantees that the domain and range of a relation (a collection of ordered
pairs) are sets.

**Answer: No.** The axiom of replacement is not sufficient.

---

## Proof

**Definitions.** In ZF set theory, a relation $R$ is a class of ordered pairs. I
ts domain is $\operatorname{dom}(R) = \{a : \exists b,\; (a,b) \in R\}$ and its
range is $\operatorname{ran}(R) = \{b : \exists a,\; (a,b) \in R\}$. These are a
lways *classes* (definable collections), but they need not be *sets*.

**What replacement actually does.** The axiom of replacement states: if $F$ is a
 class function (a definable functional relation) and $A$ is a **set**, then the
 image $F[A] = \{F(x) : x \in A\}$ is a set. The critical constraint is that rep
lacement requires the **source** $A$ to already be a set. It produces a set as t
he image of a set; it provides no mechanism to conclude that a class is a set, n
or to "compress" a proper class into a set.

**Counterexample.** Consider the identity relation on the universe:
$$R = \{(x, x) : x \text{ is a set}\}.$$
This is a proper class: if $R$ were a set, then $\operatorname{dom}(R) = V$ (the
 class of all sets) would be a set, contradicting the fact that $V$ is a proper
class (by Russell's paradox / the axiom of foundation). Its domain and range are
:
$$\operatorname{dom}(R) = V, \qquad \operatorname{ran}(R) = V,$$
both proper classes, not sets.

The axiom of replacement cannot make $\operatorname{dom}(R)$ or $\operatorname{r
an}(R)$ into sets. Replacement only operates on sets as inputs — it says "small
input → small output." Since $V$ is a proper class, there is no set $A$ and clas
s function $F$ such that $F[A] = V$ (replacement would force $F[A]$ to be a set,
 yielding a contradiction). Replacement thus provides no way to conclude that th
ese classes are sets.

**Why replacement fails in general.** The domain and range of a class relation a
re classes. Whether they are sets depends on whether the relation is "bounded" —
 i.e., whether there exists a set containing all the first (resp. second) compon
ents. Replacement cannot establish such boundedness for an arbitrary relation. I
t is designed to produce sets *from* sets, not to certify that classes are sets.

**When replacement does suffice (but is not the point).** If $R$ is already know
n to be a **set** of ordered pairs, then replacement *does* suffice to show $\op
eratorname{dom}(R)$ and $\operatorname{ran}(R)$ are sets: the first projection $
\pi_1 : (a,b) \mapsto a$ and second projection $\pi_2 : (a,b) \mapsto b$ are cla
ss functions, and by replacement, $\pi_1[R] = \operatorname{dom}(R)$ and $\pi_2[
R] = \operatorname{ran}(R)$ are sets. (In fact, union + separation also suffice
here, so replacement is sufficient but not necessary for set relations.) However
, the question asks about a relation "defined as a collection of ordered pairs"
— a collection that may be a proper class — and for such relations, replacement
is insufficient.

**Conclusion.** The axiom of replacement is not sufficient to ensure that the do
main and range of a relation are sets. Replacement only guarantees that the imag
e of a **set** under a class function is a set. For a relation that is a proper
class, the domain and range can be — and in the case of the identity relation on
 $V$, *are* — proper classes. Replacement provides no mechanism to conclude they
 are sets.
