# Problem

In the city of Modulo, there is a massive central warehouse containing 2016 storage bays, numbered strictly from $0$ to $2015$. A local logistics firm is looking to rent a non-empty collection of these bays, which they call a "Network."

To ensure efficient operations, the firm’s automated transport system has a specific protocol: if a robot is at bay $a$ and another robot (or the same one) is at bay $b$, where $a$ and $b$ are both part of the rented Network, the system must be able to perform a "jump." This jump is calculated by doubling the number of the first bay, adding the number of the second bay, and then finding the remainder of that sum when divided by $2016$. For the Network to be functional, the resulting bay number from this calculation must also be one of the bays rented by the firm.

Based on these operational constraints, how many different non-empty Networks (sets of bay numbers) can the firm choose to rent?
