## Solution

**Model.** Label the slots as rows $R=\{1,\dots,50\}$ and the keys as columns $C
=\{1,\dots,50\}$. A configuration (the manager's or a technician's) is a bijecti
on from slots to keys, i.e. a **perfect matching** in the complete bipartite gra
ph $K_{50,50}$. Two configurations "produce a recognition event" iff their match
ings **share at least one edge**. So the technician needs perfect matchings $M_1
,\dots,M_k$ such that **every** perfect matching $M$ of $K_{50,50}$ shares an ed
ge with at least one $M_j$.

Equivalently, letting $E'=\bigcup_{j=1}^k M_j$, we need: **$K_{50,50}-E'$ has no
 perfect matching** (no base configuration avoids all technician boards).

### Lower bound: $k\ge 26$

Suppose $E'=M_1\cup\cdots\cup M_k$ destroys all perfect matchings. By **Hall's t
heorem**, there exists a set of rows $S\subseteq R$ whose neighborhood in $K_{50
,50}-E'$ is some $T\subseteq C$ with $|T|<|S|$. Hence **every** edge from $S$ to
 $C\setminus T$ must lie in $E'$. The number of such edges is
$$|S|\cdot(50-|T|).$$

Each perfect matching $M_j$ contributes at most $\min(|S|,\,50-|T|)$ edges from
$S$ to $C\setminus T$ (it uses distinct columns, and only $50-|T|$ of them lie i
n $C\setminus T$). Therefore
$$|S|(50-|T|)\;\le\;k\cdot\min(|S|,\,50-|T|),$$
so
$$k\;\ge\;\max\bigl(|S|,\;50-|T|\bigr).$$

Since $|T|\le |S|-1$, we have $50-|T|\ge 50-|S|+1$, giving
$$k\;\ge\;\max\bigl(|S|,\;50-|S|+1\bigr).$$
For integer $|S|\in[1,50]$, this is minimized at $|S|=25$ or $|S|=26$, yielding
$$k\;\ge\;\max(26,25)=26.$$

The subgraph $K_{26,25}$ (rows $\{1,\dots,26\}$ vs. columns $\{26,\dots,50\}$) i
