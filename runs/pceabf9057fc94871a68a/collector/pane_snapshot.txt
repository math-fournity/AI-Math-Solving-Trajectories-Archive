## Solution

**Setup.** Label ports $0,1,\dots,9$ cyclically. Captain $j$ picks start $s_j\in
\{0,\dots,9\}$ uniformly; the zone is the length-5 arc $Z(s_j)=\{s_j,s_j+1,\dots
,s_j+4\}$ (mod 10). Port $i$ is **uncovered** iff no captain covers it, i.e. $i\
notin Z(s_j)$ for all $j$, equivalently every $s_j\notin\{i-4,\dots,i\}$, i.e. e
very $s_j\in S_i:=\{i+1,\dots,i+5\}$ (the length-5 arc just clockwise of $i$).

Let $A_i$ be the event "port $i$ is uncovered." We want $p=P\!\left(\bigcup_i A_
i\right)$, with $|\Omega|=10^4=10000$.

**Key reduction.** For a 4-tuple of starts, let $D$ be the set of distinct start
 positions and let $G(D)$ be the largest gap (number of empty ports) between con
secutive elements of $D$ around the cycle. Port $i$ is uncovered iff all starts
lie in $S_i$, i.e. iff the length-5 arc $S_i$ contains $D$. The number of length
-5 arcs containing $D$ equals $6-L$ where $L$ is the minimal covering-arc length
 of $D$ (provided $L\le 5$, else $0$). Since $L=10-G$, the number of uncovered p
orts is
$$u(D)=\max(0,\,G(D)-4).$$
In particular $u\ge 1\iff G\ge 5\iff$ the four starts fit inside some length-5 a
rc. So
$$p=P(G\ge 5)=P(\text{all four starts lie in some length-5 arc}).$$

**Counting tuples with $G\ge 5$.** Count by $d=|D|$. The number of 4-tuples whos
e distinct-value set is a *fixed* $d$-set is the onto-count
$$\mathrm{onto}(1)=1,\quad \mathrm{onto}(2)=14,\quad \mathrm{onto}(3)=36,\quad \
mathrm{onto}(4)=24.$$
Let $N_d$ = number of $d$-subsets of the 10-cycle with largest gap $\ge 5$.

- **$d=1$:** every singleton has gap $9$. $N_1=10$.
- **$d=2$:** gaps sum to $8$; max gap $\ge 5$ iff the two points are at circular
 distance $t\in\{1,2,3,4\}$ (not $5$). Each such $t$ gives $10$ pairs, so $N_2=4
0$.
- **$d=3$:** gaps $(g_1,g_2,g_3)\ge 0$, sum $7$. Ordered triples with all $g_i\l
e 4$: $36-3\binom{4}{2}=18$. Rooted configs $=10\cdot 18=180$; divide by $3$ (no
 periodic gap sequence): $60$ subsets with all gaps $\le 4$. Total $3$-subsets $
=\binom{10}{3}=120$, so $N_3=120-60=60$.
- **$d=4$:** gaps sum $6$. Ordered 4-tuples with all $\le 4$: $\binom{9}{3}-4\bi
nom{4}{3}=84-16=68$. Rooted $=680$; divide by $4$: $170$ with all gaps $\le 4$.
Total $=\binom{10}{4}=210$, so $N_4=210-170=40$.

Therefore the number of 4-tuples with $G\ge 5$ is
$$1\cdot10+14\cdot40+36\cdot60+24\cdot40=10+560+2160+960=3690.$$

**Verification via inclusion–exclusion (dual).** One can also compute directly:
$$\#\{\text{tuples with some uncovered port}\}=\sum_{\varnothing\ne T\subseteq[1
0]}(-1)^{|T|+1}\,u(T)^4,$$
where $u(T)=\max(0,G_T-4)$. Grouping subsets $T$ by minimal covering length $L_T
\in\{1,\dots,5\}$ and using $u(T)=6-L_T$: a subset with $L_T=l\le 5$ has a uniqu
e minimal arc and must contain both endpoints, giving $10\cdot 2^{l-2}$ subsets
for $l\ge 2$. The signed count per arc is $\sum_{s=0}^{l-2}\binom{l-2}{s}(-1)^{s
+1}=0$ for $l\ge 3$ and $=-1$ for $l=2$, while $l=1$ contributes $+10$. Thus $F(
1)=10$, $F(2)=\cdots=F(5)=0$, and the sum collapses to
$$(5^4-4^4)\cdot 10 = (625-256)\cdot 10 = 3690,$$
confirming the count.

**Result.** $p=\dfrac{3690}{10000}$, so
$$10000p = \boxed{3690}.$$
