## Proof

### Setup

We have the Lagrangian $L = f(x_1, x_2) + \lambda[c - g(x_1, x_2)]$ with the fir
st-order conditions $L_1 = L_2 = 0$, i.e., $f_i = \lambda g_i$ for $i = 1, 2$. T
he second differential of $L$ is:

$$d^2U = L_{11}\,dx_1^2 + 2L_{12}\,dx_1\,dx_2 + L_{22}\,dx_2^2, \qquad L_{ij} =
f_{ij} - \lambda\, g_{ij}.$$

The constraint $g(x_1, x_2) = c$ restricts admissible differentials to satisfy t
he **tangent condition**

$$g_1\,dx_1 + g_2\,dx_2 = 0, \qquad g_i = \frac{\partial g}{\partial x_i}.$$

We must determine the sign of $d^2U$ **on this tangent subspace**, not on all of
 $\mathbb{R}^2$.

### The Bordered Hessian

The **bordered Hessian** for one constraint ($m=1$) and two variables ($n=2$) is

$$\bar{H} = \begin{vmatrix} 0 & g_1 & g_2 \\ g_1 & L_{11} & L_{12} \\ g_2 & L_{1
2} & L_{22} \end{vmatrix}.$$

Expanding along the first row:

$$\bar{H} = 0\cdot(L_{11}L_{22}-L_{12}^2) - g_1(g_1 L_{22} - g_2 L_{12}) + g_2(g
_1 L_{12} - g_2 L_{11})$$

$$= -g_1^2 L_{22} + 2\,g_1 g_2\, L_{12} - g_2^2\, L_{11}.$$

### Relating $\bar{H}$ to $d^2U$ on the constraint

From the tangent condition $g_1\,dx_1 + g_2\,dx_2 = 0$, assuming $g_2 \neq 0$ (t
he case $g_1 \neq 0$ is symmetric), we get $dx_2 = -\dfrac{g_1}{g_2}\,dx_1$. Sub
stituting into $d^2U$:

$$d^2U = L_{11}\,dx_1^2 + 2L_{12}\,dx_1\!\left(-\frac{g_1}{g_2}dx_1\right) + L_{
22}\!\left(-\frac{g_1}{g_2}dx_1\right)^2$$

$$= \frac{dx_1^2}{g_2^2}\Big(g_2^2\, L_{11} - 2\,g_1 g_2\, L_{12} + g_1^2\, L_{2
2}\Big).$$

Since $dx_1^2/g_2^2 \geq 0$ (and $>0$ for nonzero displacements), the sign of $d
^2U$ on the constraint is governed by

$$g_2^2\, L_{11} - 2\,g_1 g_2\, L_{12} + g_1^2\, L_{22} = -\bar{H}.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{d^2U\big|_{g\,=\,c} \;\text{has sign}\; -\bar{H}.}$$

### Sign-definiteness criterion (bordered Hessian test)

With $n=2$ variables and $m=1$ constraint, the only relevant bordered principal
minor is $\bar{H}$ itself (of order $2m+1 = 3$). The standard sufficient second-
order conditions are:

| Condition on $\bar{H}$ | Sign of $d^2U$ on constraint | Type of extremum |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| $\bar{H} > 0$ (sign $(-1)^{m+1}=(-1)^2=+1$) | $d^2U < 0$ (negative definite) |
 **Constrained maximum** |
| $\bar{H} < 0$ (sign $(-1)^{m}=(-1)^1=-1$) | $d^2U > 0$ (positive definite) | *
*Constrained minimum** |
| $\bar{H} = 0$ | $d^2U$ sign-indeterminate | Test inconclusive |

### Conclusion

The sign definiteness of $d^2U$ on the constraint surface $g(x_1,x_2)=c$ is dete
rmined by the bordered Hessian determinant

$$\bar{H} = \begin{vmatrix} 0 & g_1 & g_2 \\ g_1 & L_{11} & L_{12} \\ g_2 & L_{1
2} & L_{22} \end{vmatrix} = -\big(g_1^2\, L_{22} - 2\,g_1 g_2\, L_{12} + g_2^2\,
 L_{11}\big).$$

Specifically, $d^2U$ restricted to admissible $(dx_1, dx_2)$ satisfying $g_1\,dx
_1 + g_2\,dx_2 = 0$ satisfies

$$d^2U = -\frac{\bar{H}}{g_2^2}\,dx_1^2 \quad\Big(\text{equivalently } = -\frac{
\bar{H}}{g_1^2}\,dx_2^2\Big),$$

so:

- **$\bar{H} > 0 \Longrightarrow d^2U$ is negative definite** on the constraint,
 giving a **constrained maximum** of $f$.
- **$\bar{H} < 0 \Longrightarrow d^2U$ is positive definite** on the constraint,
 giving a **constrained minimum** of $f$.

This is the bordered-Hessian second-order sufficient condition for constrained e
xtrema with a single equality constraint.
