## Answer: **Yes.**

**Interpretation.** The canonical way to compare iterated duals is via the isome
tric embedding $J_Y : Y \to Y^{**}$, so the natural chain is
$$X \;\subset\; X^{**} \;\subset\; X^{****} \;\subset\; \cdots$$
Reindexing $Y_n := X^{(2n)}$, the question asks whether $Y_n \subsetneq Y_{n+1}$
 for all $n$. Equivalently: **does there exist $X$ such that no iterated dual of
 $X$ is reflexive?**

---

### Key Theorem
*A Banach space $X$ is reflexive if and only if $X^*$ is reflexive.*

**Proof.**

**$(\Rightarrow)$** Suppose $J_X : X \to X^{**}$ is surjective. Given $\Phi \in
X^{***}$, define $f \in X^*$ by $f(x) := \Phi(J_X(x))$. For any $\varphi \in X^{
**}$, write $\varphi = J_X(x)$ (surjectivity). Then
$$J_{X^*}(f)(\varphi) = \varphi(f) = J_X(x)(f) = f(x) = \Phi(J_X(x)) = \Phi(\var
phi).$$
Hence $J_{X^*}(f) = \Phi$, so $J_{X^*}$ is surjective and $X^*$ is reflexive.

**$(\Leftarrow)$** Suppose $X^*$ is reflexive, so $J_{X^*}$ is surjective. Since
 $X$ is Banach and $J_X$ is an isometry, $J_X(X) \subseteq X^{**}$ is **closed**
. If $J_X(X) \neq X^{**}$, by Hahn–Banach there exists $\Phi \in X^{***}\setminu
s\{0\}$ with $\Phi|_{J_X(X)} = 0$. By surjectivity of $J_{X^*}$, write $\Phi = J
_{X^*}(g)$ for some $g \in X^*$. Then for all $x \in X$:
$$g(x) = J_X(x)(g) = J_{X^*}(g)(J_X(x)) = \Phi(J_X(x)) = 0.$$
So $g = 0$, hence $\Phi = 0$—contradiction. Thus $J_X(X) = X^{**}$. $\square$

### Main Proof

**Concrete example.** Let $X = c_0$, the Banach space of null-convergent sequenc
es with $\|\cdot\|_\infty$. Then
$$X^* \cong \ell^1, \qquad X^{**} \cong \ell^\infty.$$
The canonical embedding $J_X : c_0 \hookrightarrow \ell^\infty$ is the natural i
nclusion, which is **not surjective**: the constant sequence $(1,1,1,\ldots) \in
 \ell^\infty \setminus c_0$. Therefore $X$ is **not reflexive**:
$$X \subsetneq X^{**}.$$

**Inductive propagation.** Applying the Key Theorem repeatedly:
- $X$ not reflexive $\;\Longrightarrow\;$ $X^*$ not reflexive
- $X^*$ not reflexive $\;\Longrightarrow\;$ $X^{**}$ not reflexive
- $X^{**}$ not reflexive $\;\Longrightarrow\;$ $X^{***}$ not reflexive
- $\;\vdots$

By induction, **$X^{(n)}$ is not reflexive for every $n \geq 0$.**

**Strictness of the chain.** Since $X^{(n)}$ is not reflexive, the canonical emb
edding $J_{X^{(n)}} : X^{(n)} \to X^{(n+2)}$ is not surjective. Identifying each
 space with its canonical image in the next, we obtain **two** infinite strictly
 increasing chains:

$$X^{(0)} \subsetneq X^{(2)} \subsetneq X^{(4)} \subsetneq X^{(6)} \subsetneq \c
dots$$

$$X^{(1)} \subsetneq X^{(3)} \subsetneq X^{(5)} \subsetneq X^{(7)} \subsetneq \c
dots$$

Setting $Y_n := X^{(2n)}$, this gives $Y_n \subsetneq Y_{n+1}$ for all $n \in \m
athbb{N}$, as required. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** The argument shows something stronger: *every* non-reflexive Banach
space (e.g., $c_0$, $\ell^1$, $\ell^\infty$, $C[0,1]$, $L^1[0,1]$) produces an i
nfinite strictly increasing chain of iterated duals. Non-reflexivity of $X$ is e
quivalent to the chain never stabilizing.
