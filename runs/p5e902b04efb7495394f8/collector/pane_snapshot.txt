## Solution

**Answer:** $g(t) = f(t) + f'(t)$.

### Setup

Rewrite the integral as a convolution. Let $h(s) = e^{-s}\mathbf{1}_{s \geq 0}$.
 Then

$$\int_{-\infty}^{t} e^{u-t}g(u)\,du = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(t-u)\,g(u)\,du
= (h*g)(t),$$

so the equation reads

$$g = h*g + f'. \tag{1}$$

### Fourier transform

Use the convention $\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)=\int \varphi(t)\,e^{-2\pi i\xi t}\,dt
$. Since $f,f'\in L^1$, all transforms below exist.

- $\widehat{h}(\xi)=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}e^{-2\pi i\xi t}\,dt = \dfrac{1}{1+2\pi
i\xi}.$
- $\widehat{f'}(\xi)=2\pi i\xi\,\widehat{f}(\xi)$ (valid because $f\in L^1$, $f'
$ continuous and in $L^1$ gives $f$ absolutely continuous with $f(t)\to 0$ as $t
\to\pm\infty$).

Taking the Fourier transform of (1):

$$\widehat{g} = \frac{1}{1+2\pi i\xi}\,\widehat{g} + 2\pi i\xi\,\widehat{f}.$$

Rearranging:

$$\widehat{g}\left(1-\frac{1}{1+2\pi i\xi}\right) = 2\pi i\xi\,\widehat{f} \;\;\
Longrightarrow\;\; \widehat{g}\cdot\frac{2\pi i\xi}{1+2\pi i\xi} = 2\pi i\xi\,\w
idehat{f}.$$

For $\xi\neq 0$, divide by $2\pi i\xi$:

$$\widehat{g}(\xi) = (1+2\pi i\xi)\,\widehat{f}(\xi). \tag{2}$$

At $\xi=0$ the equation degenerates to $0=0$, but $\widehat{g}$ must be continuo
us (since $g\in L^1$), and the right side of (2) is continuous with value $\wide
hat{f}(0)$ at $\xi=0$. Hence (2) holds for all $\xi$.

Now $(1+2\pi i\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi) = \widehat{f}(\xi)+\widehat{f'}(\xi)$, so by
uniqueness of the Fourier transform on $L^1$:

$$\boxed{g(t) = f(t) + f'(t).}$$

### Verification

Assume $g=f+f'$. Then

$$\int_{-\infty}^{t} e^{u-t}g(u)\,du = \underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{t} e^{u-t}f(u
)\,du}_{I_1} + \underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{t} e^{u-t}f'(u)\,du}_{I_2}.$$

Integrate $I_2$ by parts:

$$I_2 = \Big[e^{u-t}f(u)\Big]_{-\infty}^{t} - \int_{-\infty}^{t} e^{u-t}f(u)\,du
 = f(t) - I_1,$$

where the boundary term at $-\infty$ vanishes because $f\in L^1$ with $f'$ conti
nuous and in $L^1$ implies $f(t)\to 0$ as $t\to-\infty$, killed by the factor $e
^{u-t}\to 0$.

Therefore

$$\int_{-\infty}^{t} e^{u-t}g(u)\,du = I_1 + (f(t)-I_1) = f(t),$$

and

$$\int_{-\infty}^{t} e^{u-t}g(u)\,du + f'(t) = f(t)+f'(t) = g(t). \checkmark$$

### $L^1$ membership

Since $f\in L^1(\mathbb{R})$ and $f'\in L^1(\mathbb{R})$, their sum $g=f+f'\in L
^1(\mathbb{R})$. $\checkmark$
