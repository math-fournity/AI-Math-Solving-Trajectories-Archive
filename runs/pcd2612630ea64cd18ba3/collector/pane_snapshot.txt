## Proof

Let $\omega$ denote the circumcircle of $\triangle ABC$. Since $AI$, $BI$, $CI$
are the internal angle bisectors, their second intersections $L,M,N$ with $\omeg
a$ are the midpoints of arcs $BC$, $CA$, $AB$ (the arcs not containing $A,B,C$ r
espectively).

---

**Lemma 1 (Power via dot product).** *For any point $X$, the power of $X$ with r
espect to the circle having diameter $IP$ equals $(X-I)\cdot(X-P)$ (Euclidean do
t product).*

*Proof.* The circle with diameter $IP$ is $\{Y:(Y-I)\cdot(Y-P)=0\}$ (Thales). Su
bstituting $X$ gives the signed power. $\square$

---

**Lemma 2 (Three perpendicularities).** $AI\perp MN,\quad BI\perp LN,\quad CI\pe
rp LM.$

*Proof.* Place $\omega$ as the unit circle with
$$A\text{ at angle }0,\qquad B\text{ at angle }2C,\qquad C\text{ at angle }-2B,$
$
so the arcs $AB,BC,CA$ (not containing the opposite vertex) have measures $2C,2A
,2B$. Then
$$L\text{ at }\pi+C-B,\qquad M\text{ at }-B,\qquad N\text{ at }C.$$

A chord joining points at angles $\theta_1,\theta_2$ has direction $(\theta_1+\t
heta_2)/2+\pi/2$ (perpendicular to the radius through the arc-midpoint). Hence
$$\operatorname{dir}(MN)=\tfrac{(-B)+C}{2}+\tfrac{\pi}{2}=\tfrac{C-B}{2}+\tfrac{
\pi}{2}.$$

For $AI$: since $I\in AL$ with $A$ at angle $0$ and $L$ at angle $\pi+C-B$,
$$\vec{AL}=\bigl(\cos(\pi+C-B)-1,\;\sin(\pi+C-B)\bigr)=-2\cos\tfrac{C-B}{2}\Bigl
(\cos\tfrac{C-B}{2},\;\sin\tfrac{C-B}{2}\Bigr),$$
so $\operatorname{dir}(AI)\equiv \tfrac{C-B}{2}\pmod{\pi}$. Therefore
$$\operatorname{dir}(AI)-\operatorname{dir}(MN)=-\tfrac{\pi}{2},$$
giving $AI\perp MN$. By cyclic symmetry ($A\to B\to C\to A$ sends $L\to M\to N\t
o L$ and fixes $I$), we obtain $BI\perp LN$ and $CI\perp LM$. $\square$

---

**Lemma 3 (Power equalities).** With directed lengths,
$$AH\cdot AJ=AF\cdot AG,\qquad BD\cdot BE=BH\cdot BJ,\qquad CD\cdot CE=CF\cdot C
G.$$

*Proof.* By Lemma 1,
$$\operatorname{Pow}_A(\odot IN)-\operatorname{Pow}_A(\odot IM)=(A-I)\cdot(A-N)-
(A-I)\cdot(A-M)=(A-I)\cdot(M-N)=0$$
by Lemma 2 ($AI\perp MN$). Since $H,J\in AB\cap\odot IN$ and $F,G\in CA\cap\odot
 IM$, this reads $AH\cdot AJ=AF\cdot AG$. The other two equalities follow identi
cally from $BI\perp LN$ and $CI\perp LM$. $\square$

---

**Conclusion.** Let $\Omega$ be the circle through $D,E,F$ (well defined for a n
ondegenerate triangle, since $D,E\in BC$ and $F\in CA\setminus\{C\}$ are non-col
linear).

*Step 1: $G\in\Omega$.* The power of $C$ w.r.t.\ $\Omega$ is $\operatorname{Pow}
_C(\Omega)=CD\cdot CE$ (using line $BC$). By Lemma 3, $CD\cdot CE=CF\cdot CG$. S
ince $F\in\Omega\cap CA$ and $G\in CA$, the equality $CF\cdot CG=\operatorname{P
ow}_C(\Omega)$ forces the second intersection of $CA$ with $\Omega$ to be $G$. T
hus $G\in\Omega$.

*Step 2: $H,J\in\Omega$.* Now $\operatorname{Pow}_A(\Omega)=AF\cdot AG=AH\cdot A
J$ and $\operatorname{Pow}_B(\Omega)=BD\cdot BE=BH\cdot BJ$ (both by Lemma 3). I
ntroduce a coordinate $t$ on line $AB$ with $A=0$, $B=c$. Let $H,J$ have coordin
ates $h,j$, and let $P,Q$ be the intersections of $\Omega$ with line $AB$, with
coordinates $p,q$. Then
$$pq=\operatorname{Pow}_A(\Omega)=hj,\qquad (p-c)(q-c)=\operatorname{Pow}_B(\Ome
ga)=(h-c)(j-c).$$
Expanding the second and using $pq=hj$ yields $p+q=h+j$. Equal sum and product g
ive $\{p,q\}=\{h,j\}$, so $H,J\in\Omega$.

Therefore $D,E,F,G,H,J$ all lie on $\Omega$; they are concyclic. $\blacksquare$
