## Proof

We evaluate $I_n = \int_0^\infty \left(\frac{\sin x}{x}\right)^n dx$ for $n=5,6,
7$ using a Fourier-analytic method.

**Key identity.** For $f(t) = \mathbf{1}_{[-1,1]}(t)$, its Fourier transform (co
nvention $\hat f(x)=\int f(t)e^{ixt}\,dt$) is
$$\hat f(x) = \int_{-1}^{1} e^{ixt}\,dt = \frac{2\sin x}{x}, \qquad\text{so}\qqu
ad \frac{\sin x}{x} = \tfrac{1}{2}\hat f(x).$$

**Convolution.** Since $\hat f(x)^n = \widehat{f^{*n}}(x)$ (Fourier transform of
 the $n$-fold convolution), Fourier inversion $\int_{\mathbb{R}} \widehat{g}(x)\
,dx = 2\pi\, g(0)$ gives (the integrand is in $L^1$ for $n\ge 2$; $n=1$ is the c
lassical Dirichlet integral $\pi/2$):
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\sin x}{x}\right)^n dx = \frac{1}{2^n}\int_
{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{f^{*n}}(x)\,dx = \frac{2\pi}{2^n}\,f^{*n}(0) = \frac{
\pi}{2^{n-1}}\,f^{*n}(0).$$
By evenness of the integrand, $I_n = \frac{\pi}{2^n}\,f^{*n}(0)$.

**Evaluating $f^{*n}(0)$.** The convolution $f^{*n}$ is the density (up to norma
lization) of $S = T_1+\cdots+T_n$ with $T_j\sim\text{Uniform}[-1,1]$. Writing $T
_j = 2U_j-1$ with $U_j\sim\text{Uniform}[0,1]$, we get $S = 2W - n$ where $W = U
_1+\cdots+U_n$ follows the **Irwin–Hall distribution** with density
$$p_W(w) = \frac{1}{(n-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor w\rfloor}(-1)^k\binom{n}{k}(w-k)^
{n-1}, \qquad 0\le w\le n.$$
Since $p_S(s) = \frac{1}{2}p_W\!\bigl(\tfrac{s+n}{2}\bigr)$ and $f^{*n} = 2^n\,
p_S$ (because $f$ is the unnormalized indicator, not a probability density), we
obtain
$$f^{*n}(0) = 2^n\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\,p_W(n/2) = \frac{2^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^
{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(-1)^k\binom{n}{k}\Bigl(\frac{n}{2}-k\Bigr)^{n-1} = \frac{1}
{(n-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(-1)^k\binom{n}{k}(n-2k)^{n-1}.$$

**Closed form.** Therefore
$$\boxed{I_n = \frac{\pi}{2^n\,(n-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(-1)^k\bino
m{n}{k}(n-2k)^{n-1}.}$$

**Verification for small $n$.** $n=1$: $\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot 1 = \frac{\pi}{2}$ ✓.
 $n=2$: $\frac{\pi}{4}(2-0)=\frac{\pi}{2}$ ✓. $n=3$: $\frac{\pi}{8\cdot 2}(9-3)=
\frac{6\pi}{16}=\frac{3\pi}{8}$ ✓.

---

### $n=5$

$$I_5 = \frac{\pi}{2^5\cdot 4!}\sum_{k=0}^{2}(-1)^k\binom{5}{k}(5-2k)^4.$$

| $k$ | $(-1)^k\binom{5}{k}(5-2k)^4$ |
|---|---|
| 0 | $1\cdot 1\cdot 5^4 = 625$ |
| 1 | $(-1)\cdot 5\cdot 3^4 = -405$ |
| 2 | $1\cdot 10\cdot 1^4 = 10$ |

Sum $= 625 - 405 + 10 = 230$.
$$I_5 = \frac{230\pi}{32\cdot 24} = \frac{230\pi}{768} = \frac{115\pi}{384}.$$

---

### $n=6$

$$I_6 = \frac{\pi}{2^6\cdot 5!}\sum_{k=0}^{3}(-1)^k\binom{6}{k}(6-2k)^5.$$

| $k$ | $(-1)^k\binom{6}{k}(6-2k)^5$ |
|---|---|
| 0 | $1\cdot 1\cdot 6^5 = 7776$ |
| 1 | $(-1)\cdot 6\cdot 4^5 = -6144$ |
| 2 | $1\cdot 15\cdot 2^5 = 480$ |
| 3 | $(-1)\cdot 20\cdot 0^5 = 0$ |

Sum $= 7776 - 6144 + 480 = 2112$.
$$I_6 = \frac{2112\pi}{64\cdot 120} = \frac{2112\pi}{7680} = \frac{11\pi}{40}.$$

---

### $n=7$

$$I_7 = \frac{\pi}{2^7\cdot 6!}\sum_{k=0}^{3}(-1)^k\binom{7}{k}(7-2k)^6.$$

| $k$ | $(-1)^k\binom{7}{k}(7-2k)^6$ |
|---|---|
| 0 | $1\cdot 1\cdot 7^6 = 117649$ |
| 1 | $(-1)\cdot 7\cdot 5^6 = -109375$ |
| 2 | $1\cdot 21\cdot 3^6 = 15309$ |
| 3 | $(-1)\cdot 35\cdot 1^6 = -35$ |

Sum $= 117649 - 109375 + 15309 - 35 = 23548$.
$$I_7 = \frac{23548\pi}{128\cdot 720} = \frac{23548\pi}{92160} = \frac{5887\pi}{
23040},$$
where $23548 = 4\cdot 5887$ with $5887 = 7\cdot 29^2$ sharing no factor with $23
040 = 2^9\cdot 3^2\cdot 5$.

---

### Results

$$\int_0^\infty \left(\frac{\sin x}{x}\right)^5 dx = \frac{115\pi}{384}, \qquad
\int_0^\infty \left(\frac{\sin x}{x}\right)^6 dx = \frac{11\pi}{40}, \qquad \int
_0^\infty \left(\frac{\sin x}{x}\right)^7 dx = \frac{5887\pi}{23040}.$$
