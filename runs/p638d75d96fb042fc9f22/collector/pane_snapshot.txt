# Ricci Curvature of a Bi-invariant Metric on a Lie Group

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains no solution, hint, or answ
er value. Proceeding.

---

## Setup

Let $G$ be a Lie group with a bi-invariant Riemannian metric $\langle\cdot,\cdot
\rangle$, and let $\mathfrak{g}=T_eG$ be its Lie algebra. Bi-invariance implies
the metric is $\operatorname{Ad}(G)$-invariant, hence **ad-invariant**:

$$\langle [X,Y],Z\rangle = \langle X,[Y,Z]\rangle, \qquad X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{g}.$
$

We identify left-invariant vector fields with elements of $\mathfrak{g}$ and fix
 an orthonormal basis $\{e_i\}$ of $\mathfrak{g}$.

---

## Step 1. The Levi-Civita connection

For left-invariant fields $X,Y,Z$ the functions $\langle Y,Z\rangle,\langle X,Z\
rangle,\langle X,Y\rangle$ are constant, so the Koszul formula reduces to

$$2\langle\nabla_X Y,Z\rangle = -\langle X,[Y,Z]\rangle+\langle Y,[Z,X]\rangle+\
langle Z,[X,Y]\rangle.$$

By ad-invariance and symmetry of the inner product, each of the last two terms e
quals $\langle X,[Y,Z]\rangle$:

- $\langle Y,[Z,X]\rangle=\langle [Y,Z],X\rangle=\langle X,[Y,Z]\rangle$,
- $\langle Z,[X,Y]\rangle=\langle [Z,X],Y\rangle=\langle X,[Y,Z]\rangle$.

Hence

$$2\langle\nabla_X Y,Z\rangle = \langle X,[Y,Z]\rangle = \langle [X,Y],Z\rangle,
$$

and since $Z$ is arbitrary,

$$\boxed{\nabla_X Y = \tfrac12 [X,Y].}$$

---

## Step 2. The curvature tensor

Using $R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_X\nabla_Y Z-\nabla_Y\nabla_X Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z$:

$$\nabla_X\nabla_Y Z = \tfrac12\nabla_X[Y,Z]=\tfrac14[X,[Y,Z]],\qquad \nabla_Y\n
abla_X Z=\tfrac14[Y,[X,Z]],\qquad \nabla_{[X,Y]}Z=\tfrac12[[X,Y],Z].$$

The **Jacobi identity** gives $[X,[Y,Z]]-[Y,[X,Z]]=[[X,Y],Z]$, so

$$R(X,Y)Z=\tfrac14[[X,Y],Z]-\tfrac12[[X,Y],Z]=-\tfrac14[[X,Y],Z].$$

---

## Step 3. The Ricci curvature

With the convention $\operatorname{Ric}(X,Y)=\sum_i\langle R(X,e_i)e_i,Y\rangle$
,

$$\operatorname{Ric}(X,Y)=-\tfrac14\sum_i\big\langle[[X,e_i],e_i],\,Y\big\rangle
.$$

Apply ad-invariance twice:

$$\big\langle[[X,e_i],e_i],Y\big\rangle=\big\langle[X,e_i],[e_i,Y]\big\rangle=-\
big\langle[X,e_i],[Y,e_i]\big\rangle.$$

Therefore

$$\operatorname{Ric}(X,Y)=\tfrac14\sum_i\big\langle[X,e_i],[Y,e_i]\big\rangle.$$

---

## Step 4. Relation to the Killing form

The Killing form is $B(X,Y)=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{ad}X\circ\operatorna
me{ad}Y)=\sum_i\langle[X,[Y,e_i]],e_i\rangle$. By ad-invariance,

$$\langle[X,[Y,e_i]],e_i\rangle=\langle[X,e_i],[e_i,Y]\rangle=-\langle[X,e_i],[Y
,e_i]\rangle,$$

so $B(X,Y)=-\sum_i\langle[X,e_i],[Y,e_i]\rangle$. Substituting above:

$$\operatorname{Ric}(X,Y)=-\tfrac14\,B(X,Y)=-\tfrac14\operatorname{tr}(\operator
name{ad}X\circ\operatorname{ad}Y).$$

---

## Final formula

For a bi-invariant metric on a Lie group $G$, identifying tangent vectors with e
lements of the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ via left translation, the Ricci curvat
ure is

$$\boxed{\;\operatorname{Ric}(X,Y)=\frac14\sum_i\big\langle[X,e_i],[Y,e_i]\big\r
angle=-\frac14\,B(X,Y)=-\frac14\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{ad}X\circ\operato
rname{ad}Y)\;}$$

where $\{e_i\}$ is any $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$-orthonormal basis of $\mathfr
ak{g}$ and $B$ is the Killing form.

**Corollary (sign).** For $X=Y$,

$$\operatorname{Ric}(X,X)=\frac14\sum_i\|[X,e_i]\|^2\ge 0.$$

In particular, when $G$ is compact semisimple, $B$ is negative definite, so $\op
eratorname{Ric}=-\tfrac14 B$ is **positive definite** — compact Lie groups with
bi-invariant metrics have strictly positive Ricci curvature (vanishing only on t
he abelian center, where $[X,e_i]=0$).
