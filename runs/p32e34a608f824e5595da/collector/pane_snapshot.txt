Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $|u_1| = |u_2|$ for two wav
e equation solutions forces $u_1 = \pm u_2$.

**Answer: No.** Here is a counterexample for $d = 1$.

**Key reduction.** The condition $|u_1| = |u_2|$ is equivalent to $u_1^2 = u_2^2
$, i.e., $(u_1 - u_2)(u_1 + u_2) = 0$ pointwise. Setting $v = u_1 - u_2$ and $w
= u_1 + u_2$, both $v$ and $w$ are $C^2$ solutions of the wave equation (by line
arity), and the condition becomes $v \cdot w \equiv 0$. The conclusion $u_1 = u_
2$ or $u_1 = -u_2$ is equivalent to $v \equiv 0$ or $w \equiv 0$. So the questio
n reduces to: **if $v, w$ are $C^2$ wave-equation solutions with $vw \equiv 0$,
must one vanish identically?**

**Construction.** For $d = 1$, the wave equation is $\partial_t^2 u = \partial_x
^2 u$. Any $C^2$ function of $\xi = x + t$ alone is a solution (since $\partial_
t^2 \phi(x+t) = \phi''(x+t) = \partial_x^2 \phi(x+t)$).

Define two $C^2$ functions on $\mathbb{R}$:
$$f(s) = \begin{cases} 0 & s \le 0, \\ s^4 & s > 0, \end{cases} \qquad F(s) = \b
egin{cases} s^4 & s \le 0, \\ 0 & s > 0. \end{cases}$$

**$f$ and $F$ are $C^2$:** For $s < 0$: $f = f' = f'' = 0$. For $s > 0$: $f = s^
4$, $f' = 4s^3$, $f'' = 12s^2$. At $s = 0$: $f(0) = 0$, $f'(0^-) = 0 = f'(0^+)$,
 $f''(0^-) = 0 = f''(0^+)$. So $f \in C^2(\mathbb{R})$. By symmetry, $F \in C^2(
\mathbb{R})$ as well.

**$f \cdot F \equiv 0$:** For $s \le 0$, $f(s) = 0$; for $s > 0$, $F(s) = 0$. So
 $f(s)F(s) = 0$ for all $s$.

**Neither vanishes identically:** $f(1) = 1 \ne 0$ and $F(-1) = 1 \ne 0$.

Now set:
$$u_1(t,x) = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(f(x+t) + F(x+t)\bigr), \qquad u_2(t,x) = \tfrac{1
}{2}\bigl(F(x+t) - f(x+t)\bigr).$$

- **Both are $C^2$ solutions of the wave equation**, since they are $C^2$ functi
ons of $x + t$.
- **$|u_1| = |u_2|$:** We have $u_1 - u_2 = f(x+t)$ and $u_1 + u_2 = F(x+t)$, so
$$u_1^2 - u_2^2 = (u_1 - u_2)(u_1 + u_2) = f(x+t)\,F(x+t) = 0$$
for all $(t,x)$. Hence $u_1^2 = u_2^2$, i.e., $|u_1| = |u_2|$.
- **$u_1 \ne u_2$:** $u_1 - u_2 = f(x+t) \not\equiv 0$ (e.g., at $x+t=1$, $f(1)=
1$).
- **$u_1 \ne -u_2$:** $u_1 + u_2 = F(x+t) \not\equiv 0$ (e.g., at $x+t=-1$, $F(-
1)=1$).

**Remark on $d = 0$.** When $d = 0$, the wave equation reduces to $u''(t) = 0$,
so $u(t) = at + b$. Then $|u_1| = |u_2|$ forces $(u_1 - u_2)(u_1 + u_2) = 0$ as
a polynomial in $t$, so one factor is identically zero, giving $u_1 = \pm u_2$.
Thus the conclusion *does* hold for $d = 0$. However, for $d \ge 1$ the countere
xample above (which extends to any $d \ge 1$ by taking $u_1, u_2$ independent of
 $x_2, \dots, x_d$) shows it fails.

**Conclusion.** The implication does **not** hold in general. For $d \ge 1$, the
re exist $C^2$ real-valued solutions $u_1, u_2$ of the wave equation with $|u_1|
 = |u_2|$ everywhere, yet neither $u_1 = u_2$ nor $u_1 = -u_2$.
