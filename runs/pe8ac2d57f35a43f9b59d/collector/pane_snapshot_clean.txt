## Solution

This is the **Ackermann function**. I evaluate it level by level.

**Level 0.** \(f(0,y)=y+1\).

**Level 1.** \(f(1,0)=f(0,1)=2\), and \(f(1,y+1)=f(0,f(1,y))=f(1,y)+1\). Hence
\[
f(1,y)=y+2.
\]

**Level 2.** \(f(2,0)=f(1,1)=3\), and \(f(2,y+1)=f(1,f(2,y))=f(2,y)+2\). Hence
\[
f(2,y)=2y+3.
\]

**Level 3.** \(f(3,0)=f(2,1)=5\), and \(f(3,y+1)=f(2,f(3,y))=2f(3,y)+3\). Let \(
a_y=f(3,y)\); then \(a_0=5\), \(a_{y+1}=2a_y+3\). Setting \(b_y=a_y+3\) gives \(
b_{y+1}=2b_y\), \(b_0=8\), so \(b_y=2^{y+3}\). Therefore
\[
f(3,y)=2^{y+3}-3.
\]

**Level 4.** \(f(4,0)=f(3,1)=2^{4}-3=13\), and
\[
f(4,y+1)=f(3,f(4,y))=2^{f(4,y)+3}-3.
\]
Let \(c_y=f(4,y)\) and \(d_y=c_y+3\). Then \(d_0=16=2^{4}\) and
\[
d_{y+1}=c_{y+1}+3=2^{c_y+3}=2^{d_y}.
\]
So \(d_0=2^{4}\), \(d_1=2^{2^{4}}\), \(d_2=2^{2^{2^{4}}}\), and in general \(d_y
\) is a tower of \(2\)'s of height \(y+3\):
\[
d_y = \underbrace{2^{2^{\cdot^{\cdot^{2}}}}}_{y+3\text{ twos}} =: 2\uparrow\upar
row(y+3).
\]
Thus
\[
f(4,y)=2\uparrow\uparrow(y+3)-3.
\]

**Verification.** \(f(4,0)=2\uparrow\uparrow 3-3=16-3=13=f(3,1)\). ✓
\(f(4,1)=2\uparrow\uparrow 4-3=65536-3=65533\), and directly \(f(4,1)=f(3,13)=2^
{16}-3=65533\). ✓

**Final answer.** Setting \(y=2008\):
\[
\boxed{f(4,2008)=2\uparrow\uparrow 2011-3},
\]
i.e. a power tower of \(2\)'s of height \(2011\), minus \(3\).
