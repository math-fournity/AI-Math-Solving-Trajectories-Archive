## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** Every connected Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ is com
pact.

Let $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ be a positive-definite inner product on $\mathfr
ak g$ that is invariant, meaning
$$\langle [X,Y],Z\rangle+\langle Y,[X,Z]\rangle=0\quad\text{for all }X,Y,Z\in\ma
thfrak g,$$
or equivalently $\operatorname{ad}_X$ is skew-symmetric with respect to $\langle
\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ for every $X$.

---

**Step 1. The Killing form is negative definite, so $\mathfrak g$ is of compact
type.**

Since $\operatorname{ad}_X$ is skew-symmetric, it is diagonalizable over $\mathb
b R$ with purely imaginary eigenvalues $i\lambda_1,\dots,i\lambda_n$ ($\lambda_j
\in\mathbb R$). Hence every eigenvalue of $\operatorname{ad}_X^2$ is $-\lambda_j
^2\le 0$, and
$$\kappa(X,X)=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{ad}_X^2)=-\sum_j\lambda_j^2\le 0,$
$
with equality iff all $\lambda_j=0$, i.e. $\operatorname{ad}_X=0$, i.e. $X\in\ma
thfrak z(\mathfrak g)$. Because $\mathfrak g$ is semisimple, $\mathfrak z(\mathf
rak g)=0$, so $\kappa(X,X)=0\iff X=0$. Thus $\kappa$ is **negative definite**: $
\mathfrak g$ is a real semisimple Lie algebra of compact type.

---

**Step 2. The adjoint group $\operatorname{Int}(\mathfrak g)$ is compact.**

Because each $\operatorname{ad}_X$ is skew-symmetric, $\exp(\operatorname{ad}_X)
$ is orthogonal with respect to $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$. The adjoint group
$$\operatorname{Int}(\mathfrak g)=\langle \exp(\operatorname{ad}_X):X\in\mathfra
k g\rangle\subseteq \mathrm O(\mathfrak g,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)$$
is a connected subgroup of the compact orthogonal group, hence **compact**. Its
Lie algebra is $\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak g)\cong\mathfrak g$ (the isomorphism
 uses $\mathfrak z(\mathfrak g)=0$), so $\operatorname{Int}(\mathfrak g)$ is com
pact and semisimple.

---

**Step 3. The simply connected group $\widetilde G$ with Lie algebra $\mathfrak
g$ is compact (Weyl's theorem).**

Let $\widetilde G$ be the simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak
 g$. The adjoint representation gives a covering homomorphism
$$\operatorname{Ad}:\widetilde G\longrightarrow \operatorname{Int}(\mathfrak g),
\qquad \ker(\operatorname{Ad})=Z(\widetilde G).$$
Since $\widetilde G$ is simply connected, it is the universal cover of $\operato
rname{Int}(\mathfrak g)$. By **Weyl's theorem** — *the universal covering group
of a compact semisimple Lie group is compact* — $\widetilde G$ is compact.

(For completeness, the standard proof of Weyl's theorem: equip $\operatorname{In
t}(\mathfrak g)$ with the bi-invariant Riemannian metric induced by $-\kappa$; t
he universal cover inherits a complete bi-invariant metric of the same Ricci cur
vature, and a volume-comparison/Myers-type argument shows the universal cover ha
s finite volume, hence is compact. This uses only the compactness and semisimpli
city of $\operatorname{Int}(\mathfrak g)$, not the conclusion.)

---

**Step 4. Every connected Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ is compact.**

Any connected Lie group $G$ with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ is a quotient
$$G\cong \widetilde G/D,$$
where $D\subseteq Z(\widetilde G)$ is a discrete (hence closed) central subgroup
. The quotient of a compact group by a closed subgroup is compact. Since $\widet
ilde G$ is compact (Step 3), $G$ is compact.

---

**Conclusion.** The existence of a positive-definite invariant inner product for
ces $\mathfrak g$ to be of compact type; by Weyl's theorem the simply connected
group is compact; and every connected group with this Lie algebra is a quotient
of it, hence compact. Therefore **yes**, every connected Lie group with Lie alge
bra $\mathfrak g$ is compact.
