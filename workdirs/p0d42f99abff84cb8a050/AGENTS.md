# Problem

Deep in a subterranean mining facility, a specialized laser drill is positioned on a flat, 2D surveying floor. The facility is mapped with several concentric circular safety boundaries, all centered at a single central power core (the origin). These safety boundaries are established at every integer radius $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ meters.

The drill is currently located at an arbitrary starting point that does not lie on any of these boundary lines. The operator fires the laser in a fixed linear direction. The laser beam travels exactly $\frac{\sqrt{5}}{5}$ meters before it intersects its first circular boundary. Continuing along the same straight path, it travels an additional $\sqrt{5}$ meters before intersecting a second circular boundary. Still maintaining its straight trajectory, it travels another $\frac{3\sqrt{5}}{5}$ meters before hitting a third circular boundary.

What is the distance the laser beam will travel from the third boundary until it crosses a fourth circular boundary?
