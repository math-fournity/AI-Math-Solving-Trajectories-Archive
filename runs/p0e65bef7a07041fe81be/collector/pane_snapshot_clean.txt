## Proof that the statement is **FALSE**

**Preliminary observations.** If $A^* = -A$ (skew-adjoint), then for every real
$t$, the matrix $e^{-tA}$ is unitary:
$$
(e^{-tA})^* = e^{(-tA)^*} = e^{tA} = (e^{-tA})^{-1},
$$
since $(-tA)^* = -tA^* = tA$ and $e^{-tA}e^{tA} = I$. Likewise $e^{-tA'}$ is uni
tary. Hence for the operator norm $\|\cdot\|$,
$$
\|e^{-tA} - e^{-tA'}\| \le \|e^{-tA}\| + \|e^{-tA'}\| = 2,
$$
so $\sup_t \|e^{-tA} - e^{-tA'}\| \le 2$ always. When $A = A'$ the sup is $0 = 2
(1-1)$, consistent. The substantive claim is that the sup equals **exactly $2$**
 whenever $A \ne A'$.

**Counterexample.** We exhibit skew-adjoint $A \ne A'$ with $\sup_t \|e^{-tA} -
e^{-tA'}\| < 2$.

Work in $M_2(\mathbb{C})$. Set
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} i & 0 \\ 0 & -i \end{pmatrix}, \qquad A' = R(\theta)\, A\, R
(-\theta),
$$
where $R(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos
\theta \end{pmatrix}$ is a real rotation and $0 < |\theta| < \pi/2$.

- **Skew-adjointness:** $A^* = -A$ is immediate. Since $R(\theta)$ is real ortho
gonal (hence unitary), $A'^* = R(\theta)\,A^*\,R(-\theta) = -R(\theta)\,A\,R(-\t
heta) = -A'$. ✓
- **$A \ne A'$:** For $\theta \ne 0$, the rotation changes the eigenvectors, so
$A' \ne A$. ✓

**Computing the sup.** Using conjugation invariance of the exponential,
$$
e^{-tA'} = e^{-t\,R(\theta)A\,R(-\theta)} = R(\theta)\, e^{-tA}\, R(-\theta).
$$
Let $D = e^{-tA} = \operatorname{diag}(e^{-it}, e^{it})$, with $c = \cos\theta$,
 $s = \sin\theta$. A direct computation gives
$$
D - R(\theta)\,D\,R(-\theta) = (e^{-it} - e^{it})\begin{pmatrix} s^2 & -cs \\ -c
s & -s^2 \end{pmatrix}.
$$
The matrix $M := \begin{pmatrix} s^2 & -cs \\ -cs & -s^2 \end{pmatrix}$ has trac
e $0$ and determinant $-s^2(s^2+c^2) = -s^2$, so its eigenvalues are $\pm s = \p
m\sin\theta$, giving $\|M\| = |\sin\theta|$. Since $|e^{-it} - e^{it}| = 2|\sin
t|$,
$$
\|e^{-tA} - e^{-tA'}\| = 2\,|\sin t|\,|\sin\theta|.
$$
Taking the supremum over $t \in \mathbb{R}$ (achieved at $t = \pi/2 + k\pi$):
$$
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}} \|e^{-tA} - e^{-tA'}\| = 2\,|\sin\theta|.
$$

**Conclusion.** For any $0 < |\theta| < \pi/2$ we have $A \ne A'$, so $2(1 - \de
lta_{A,A'}) = 2$, yet
$$
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}} \|e^{-tA} - e^{-tA'}\| = 2|\sin\theta| < 2.
$$
For instance, at $\theta = \pi/6$: the sup equals $1$, while $2(1-\delta_{A,A'})
 = 2$.

The equality $\sup_{t \in \mathbb{R}} \| e^{-tA}-e^{-tA'} \| = 2(1-\delta_{A,A'}
)$ therefore **fails**. The statement is **false**. $\blacksquare$
