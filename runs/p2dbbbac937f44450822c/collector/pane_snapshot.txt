## Proof

**Definition.** A *chord* of $(M,g)$ is a geodesic segment $\gamma:[0,L]\to M$ s
uch that $\gamma(0),\gamma(L)\in\partial M$, $\gamma(t)\in\operatorname{int}(M)$
 for $t\in(0,L)$, and $\gamma'(0)\perp T_{\gamma(0)}\partial M$ (the geodesic me
ets the boundary perpendicularly at its starting point, heading inward).

**Claim.** $\ell(M,g)>0$. In particular, it is **not possible** for $\ell(M,g)=0
$.

**Step 1: Construction of a uniform collar neighborhood via the inward normal ex
ponential map.**

Let $\nu(p)$ denote the inward-pointing unit normal to $\partial M$ at $p$. Defi
ne the *inward normal exponential map*:
$$\Phi:\partial M\times[0,\infty)\to M,\qquad \Phi(p,t)=\exp_p\!\bigl(t\,\nu(p)\
bigr).$$

At $t=0$, the differential $d\Phi_{(p,0)}:T_p(\partial M)\oplus\mathbb{R}\to T_p
M$ sends $(v,s)\mapsto v+s\,\nu(p)$. Since $T_pM = T_p(\partial M)\oplus\mathbb{
R}\,\nu(p)$, this is a linear isomorphism. By the inverse function theorem, $\Ph
i$ is a local diffeomorphism near each $(p,0)$.

Since $\partial M$ is **compact** and $\Phi$ is smooth, there exists a uniform $
\varepsilon>0$ such that $\Phi$ is a diffeomorphism from $\partial M\times[0,\va
repsilon)$ onto its image
$$U_\varepsilon = \Phi\bigl(\partial M\times[0,\varepsilon)\bigr),$$
which is a collar neighborhood of $\partial M$ in $M$.

In these *boundary normal coordinates* $(p,t)\in\partial M\times[0,\varepsilon)$
:
- The boundary $\partial M$ is the slice $\{t=0\}$.
- The interior $\operatorname{int}(M)\cap U_\varepsilon$ is $\{t>0\}$.
- A normal geodesic from $p\in\partial M$ is the curve $t\mapsto(p,t)$, along wh
ich $t$ is **strictly increasing**.

**Step 2: Normal geodesics cannot return to the boundary within the collar.**

The curve $t\mapsto\Phi(p,t)=(p,t)$ has $t$-coordinate equal to $t$, which is ze
ro only at $t=0$. Therefore, within $U_\varepsilon$, a normal geodesic from the
boundary intersects $\partial M=\{t=0\}$ **only at its starting point**.

**Step 3: No chord of length less than $\varepsilon$ exists.**

Let $\gamma:[0,L]\to M$ be a chord. Since $\gamma(0)\in\partial M$, $\gamma'(0)\
perp T_{\gamma(0)}\partial M$, and $\gamma(t)\in\operatorname{int}(M)$ for small
 $t>0$, the initial velocity $\gamma'(0)$ is a positive multiple of $\nu(\gamma(
0))$. Since $\gamma$ is parameterized by arc length, $\gamma'(0)=\nu(\gamma(0))$
, and thus:
$$\gamma(t)=\exp_{\gamma(0)}\!\bigl(t\,\nu(\gamma(0))\bigr)=\Phi\bigl(\gamma(0),
\,t\bigr)\quad\text{for all }t\text{ in the domain of definition.}$$

Suppose for contradiction that $L<\varepsilon$. Then for all $t\in[0,L]$, the po
int $\gamma(t)=\Phi(\gamma(0),t)$ lies in $U_\varepsilon$ with $t$-coordinate eq
ual to $t$. In particular:
$$\gamma(L)=\Phi\bigl(\gamma(0),\,L\bigr),\quad\text{with } L\text{-coordinate }
=L>0.$$

But $\{t=0\}=\partial M$ in the collar, so $\gamma(L)$ has $t$-coordinate $L>0$,
 meaning $\gamma(L)\in\operatorname{int}(M)$. This **contradicts** the requireme
nt that $\gamma(L)\in\partial M$.

**Conclusion.**

Every chord has length $L\geq\varepsilon>0$. Therefore:
$$\ell(M,g)=\inf\{L>0:\text{there exists a chord of length }L\}\geq\varepsilon>0
.$$

It is **not possible** for $\ell(M,g)$ to be zero.

**Remark.** The argument uses only the compactness of $\partial M$ and the smoot
hness of the metric and boundary (which concavity presupposes). The concavity co
ndition itself is not needed for the positivity of $\ell(M,g)$; the result holds
 for any compact Riemannian manifold with smooth boundary. The concavity may pla
y a role in sharper quantitative estimates or in existence results for chords, b
ut the qualitative positivity of $\ell(M,g)$ follows from the collar neighborhoo
d structure alone.
