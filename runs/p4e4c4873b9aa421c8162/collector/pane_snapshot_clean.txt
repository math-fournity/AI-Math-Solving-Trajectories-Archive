## Proof

**Theorem.** Let $\mathbb{F}$ be any field and let $S_n \hookrightarrow \operato
rname{GL}(n,\mathbb{F})$ be the standard embedding sending each permutation to i
ts permutation matrix. If permutation matrices $P_\alpha, P_\beta$ are conjugate
 (similar) in $\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{F})$, then $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are c
onjugate in $S_n$.

Since two permutations are conjugate in $S_n$ iff they have the same cycle type,
 it suffices to show that the similarity class of a permutation matrix uniquely
determines the cycle type of the corresponding permutation.

---

### Step 1. Block decomposition

Let $\sigma \in S_n$ have cycle type $(k_1, \dots, k_r)$ with $\sum k_i = n$. Up
 to simultaneous reordering of the basis (conjugation by a permutation matrix),
$P_\sigma$ is block diagonal with blocks $C(k_1), \dots, C(k_r)$, where $C(k)$ i
s the $k \times k$ permutation matrix of a $k$-cycle. The matrix $C(k)$ is the *
*companion matrix** of $f_k(x) = x^k - 1$.

### Step 2. Elementary divisors determine similarity

By the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over the PID $\mathbb{F}
[x]$ (equivalently, the theory of rational canonical forms), two matrices over $
\mathbb{F}$ are similar iff they have the same multiset of **elementary divisors
** — the prime-power factors of the invariant factors. For a block diagonal matr
ix with companion blocks of $f_1, \dots, f_r$, the elementary divisors are the p
rime-power factors of all $f_i$ in $\mathbb{F}[x]$.

Thus the elementary divisors of $P_\sigma$ are:
$$\mathcal{E}(\sigma) = \bigcup_{i=1}^{r} \bigl\{\text{prime-power factors of }
x^{k_i}-1 \text{ in } \mathbb{F}[x]\bigr\}.$$

### Step 3. Factorization of $x^k - 1$

**Case $\operatorname{char} \mathbb{F} = p > 0$.** Write $k = p^a m$ with $\gcd(
m,p)=1$. By the freshman's dream, $x^k - 1 = (x^m - 1)^{p^a}$. Since $\gcd(m,p)=
1$, the polynomial $x^m - 1$ is separable, so $x^m - 1 = \prod_j q_j(x)$ (produc
t of **distinct** irreducibles). The prime-power factors of $x^k - 1$ are $\{q_j
^{p^a}\}_j$.

**Case $\operatorname{char} \mathbb{F} = 0$.** Then $x^k - 1$ is separable for e
very $k$, so the prime-power factors are just the irreducible factors $\{q_j\}_j
$, each to the first power. This is the same as the positive-characteristic case
 with $a = 0$.

### Step 4. Cyclotomic labeling of irreducibles

In both cases, we use the factorization $x^m - 1 = \prod_{d \mid m,\, \gcd(d,p)=
1} \Phi_d(x)$, where $\Phi_d$ is the $d$-th cyclotomic polynomial (reduced mod $
p$ when $\operatorname{char}\mathbb{F}=p$).

**Key fact:** The $\Phi_d$ (for $d$ coprime to $p$) are pairwise coprime. Indeed
, over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}$, the roots of $\Phi_d$ are primitive $d$-th roots of u
nity. A primitive $d$-th root $\zeta$ satisfies $\zeta^d=1$ and $\zeta^j \neq 1$
 for $0 < j < d$; if $\zeta$ were also a primitive $d'$-th root with $d' \neq d$
, then $d \mid d'$ and $d' \mid d$, contradiction. Since each $\Phi_d$ is separa
ble (as $\gcd(d,p)=1$), coprimality of the $\Phi_d$ follows.

Therefore each irreducible factor $q$ of $x^m - 1$ divides a **unique** $\Phi_d$
. We define the **label** $\ell(q) = d$, which equals the multiplicative order o
f any root of $q$ in $\bar{\mathbb{F}}^\times$, and is thus determined by $q$ al
one.

### Step 5. Extracting data from elementary divisors

Each elementary divisor has the form $q^{p^a}$ (or $q^1$ in characteristic $0$).
 From it we read off:
- the **exponent** $e = p^a$ (or $1$), and
- the **label** $d = \ell(q)$.

For a cycle of length $k = p^a m$, the elementary divisors all carry exponent $p
^a$, and their labels form the multiset
$$\mathcal{D}(m) = \bigl\{ d^{\,r_d} : d \mid m,\; \gcd(d,p)=1 \bigr\},$$
where $r_d \geq 1$ is the number of irreducible factors of $\Phi_d$ over $\mathb
b{F}$ (a quantity depending only on $\mathbb{F}$ and $d$, not on $m$).

### Step 6. Uniqueness: the greedy algorithm

**Claim.** For a fixed exponent $e = p^a$, the combined label multiset $\biguplu
s_i \mathcal{D}(m_i)$ uniquely determines the multiset $\{m_i\}$.

*Proof of claim.* We proceed by induction on the size of the multiset. Let $d_{\
max}$ be the largest label appearing, with multiplicity $\mu$.

In any valid decomposition, $d_{\max}$ must appear in some $\mathcal{D}(m_i)$, w
hich requires $d_{\max} \mid m_i$. But $m_i$ itself appears as a label in $\math
cal{D}(m_i)$ (with multiplicity $r_{m_i} \geq 1$), so $m_i$ is in the multiset,
hence $m_i \leq d_{\max}$. Together with $d_{\max} \mid m_i$, this forces $m_i =
 d_{\max}$.

Moreover, $d_{\max} \mid m_j$ implies $m_j \geq d_{\max}$, hence $m_j = d_{\max}
$. So the multiplicity of $d_{\max}$ in the combined multiset is exactly $r_{d_{
\max}} \cdot \#\{i : m_i = d_{\max}\}$, giving
$$\#\{i : m_i = d_{\max}\} = \frac{\mu}{r_{d_{\max}}}.$$
This is uniquely determined. We then remove that many copies of $\mathcal{D}(d_{
\max})$ from the multiset and recurse on the remainder (whose maximum label is s
trictly smaller). By induction, the full decomposition is unique. $\square$

### Step 7. Conclusion

Applying the claim to each exponent group separately, the multiset of elementary

- for each exponent $p^a$, the multiset of $m_i$ values, hence the multiset of c
ycle lengths $\{k_i = p^a m_i\}$.

Therefore the similarity class of $P_\sigma$ determines the cycle type of $\sigm
a$. Since two permutations are conjugate in $S_n$ iff they have the same cycle t

$$P_\alpha \sim P_\beta \text{ in } \operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}) \implies \al
pha \sim \beta \text{ in } S_n.$$

**Remark (converse).** The converse is immediate: if $\alpha = \gamma \beta \gam
