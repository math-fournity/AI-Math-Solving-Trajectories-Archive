## Proof

**Setup.** A regular $n$-gon inscribed in a circle of radius $R$ has $n$ sides,
each of length $2R\sin(\pi/n)$. Hence the sum of the squares of all its side len
gths is

$$S_n \;=\; n\bigl(2R\sin(\pi/n)\bigr)^2 \;=\; 4R^{2}\,n\sin^{2}(\pi/n).$$

Therefore

$$\frac{S_5}{S_{20}} \;=\; \frac{5\sin^{2}(\pi/5)}{20\sin^{2}(\pi/20)} \;=\; \fr
ac{\sin^{2}(\pi/5)}{4\sin^{2}(\pi/20)}. \tag{1}$$

**Relating the two sines.** Since $\pi/5 = 4\cdot(\pi/20)$, set $\theta=\pi/20$
and use the double-angle identity twice:

$$\sin(4\theta)=2\sin(2\theta)\cos(2\theta)=4\sin\theta\cos\theta\cos(2\theta).$
$

With $\theta=\pi/20$ this gives

$$\sin(\pi/5)=4\sin(\pi/20)\cos(\pi/20)\cos(\pi/10).$$

Squaring,

$$\sin^{2}(\pi/5)=16\sin^{2}(\pi/20)\cos^{2}(\pi/20)\cos^{2}(\pi/10).$$

**Substituting into (1):**

$$\frac{S_5}{S_{20}}=\frac{16\sin^{2}(\pi/20)\cos^{2}(\pi/20)\cos^{2}(\pi/10)}{4
\sin^{2}(\pi/20)}=4\cos^{2}(\pi/20)\cos^{2}(\pi/10). \tag{2}$$

**Evaluating in surds.** Using $\cos^{2}x=(1+\cos 2x)/2$:

- $\cos^{2}(\pi/10)=\dfrac{1+\cos(\pi/5)}{2}=\dfrac{1+\frac{\sqrt5+1}{4}}{2}=\df
rac{5+\sqrt5}{8}$.
- $\cos^{2}(\pi/20)=\dfrac{1+\cos(\pi/10)}{2}=\dfrac{1+\frac{\sqrt{10+2\sqrt5}}{
4}}{2}=\dfrac{4+\sqrt{10+2\sqrt5}}{8}$.

Hence

$$\frac{S_5}{S_{20}}=4\cdot\frac{5+\sqrt5}{8}\cdot\frac{4+\sqrt{10+2\sqrt5}}{8}=
\frac{(5+\sqrt5)\bigl(4+\sqrt{10+2\sqrt5}\bigr)}{16}.$$

**Numerical check.** $\sin^{2}36^\circ\approx 0.34549$, $\sin^{2}9^\circ\approx
0.024472$, giving $0.34549/(4\cdot0.024472)\approx 3.5295$; and $(5+\sqrt5)(4+\s
qrt{10+2\sqrt5})/16\approx 7.2361\cdot 7.8042/16\approx 3.5295$. ✓
