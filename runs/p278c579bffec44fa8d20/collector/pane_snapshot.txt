## Proof

**Step 1: Identify the domain.** The "right half-plane minus the y-axis" is $H =
 \{z \in \mathbb{C} : \operatorname{Re}(z) > 0\}$, the open right half-plane (th
e open half-plane already excludes the y-axis $\operatorname{Re}(z)=0$).

**Step 2: Conformal map between $H$ and the unit disk.** The Cayley transform
$$\varphi(w) = \frac{1+w}{1-w}$$
maps the unit disk $\mathbb{D}$ biholomorphically onto $H$, with $\varphi(0) = 1
$. Its inverse is
$$\psi(z) = \frac{z-1}{z+1}, \qquad \psi(1) = 0.$$
We compute $\varphi'(w) = \frac{2}{(1-w)^2}$, so $\varphi'(0) = 2$.

**Step 3: Reduce to Schwarz's lemma.** Define $h = g \circ \varphi : \mathbb{D}
\to \mathbb{D}$. This is a holomorphic self-map of the unit disk (since $g : H \
to \mathbb{D}$ and $\varphi : \mathbb{D} \to H$), and
$$h(0) = g(\varphi(0)) = g(1) = 0.$$
By the Schwarz lemma, $|h'(0)| \le 1$.

**Step 4: Relate $h'(0)$ to $g'(1)$.** By the chain rule,
$$h'(0) = g'(\varphi(0)) \cdot \varphi'(0) = g'(1) \cdot 2.$$
Therefore
$$|g'(1)| \cdot 2 = |h'(0)| \le 1,$$
which gives
$$|g'(1)| \le \frac{1}{2}.$$

**Step 5: Sharpness.** The bound is attained by $g(z) = \frac{z-1}{z+1}$, which
maps $H$ into $\mathbb{D}$ with $g(1) = 0$ and
$$g'(z) = \frac{2}{(z+1)^2}, \qquad g'(1) = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}.$$
Hence the bound is sharp.

$$\boxed{|g'(1)| \le \dfrac{1}{2}}$$
