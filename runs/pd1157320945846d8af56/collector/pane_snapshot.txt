Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether: if the generic point of an
 irreducible closed subset Y of a variety X (over an algebraically closed field)
 is regular, then Y contains a closed point regular on X.

**Answer: The statement is TRUE.**

## Proof

Let $X$ be a variety over an algebraically closed field $k$, and let $Y \subsete
q X$ be an irreducible closed subset with generic point $\eta$. Assume $\eta$ is
 a regular point of $X$, i.e., the local ring $\mathcal{O}_{X,\eta}$ is a regula
r local ring.

The proof uses three standard facts:

**Fact 1 (Openness of the regular locus).** For a scheme of finite type over a p
erfect field (in particular, over an algebraically closed field), the regular lo
cus
$$\operatorname{Reg}(X) = \{x \in X : \mathcal{O}_{X,x} \text{ is a regular loca
l ring}\}$$
is an open subset of $X$. This holds because schemes of finite type over a field
 are excellent, and the regular locus of an excellent scheme is open.

**Fact 2 (The intersection is non-empty and open in $Y$).** Since $\eta \in \ope
ratorname{Reg}(X)$ by hypothesis and $\operatorname{Reg}(X)$ is open in $X$, the
 intersection
$$Y \cap \operatorname{Reg}(X)$$
is open in $Y$ (in the subspace topology). It is non-empty because it contains $
\eta$.

**Fact 3 (Jacobson property / density of closed points).** A scheme of finite ty
pe over a field is a *Jacobson scheme*: the set of closed points is dense in eve
ry closed subset. Equivalently, every non-empty open subset of any closed subset
 contains a closed point. (This follows from the fact that finitely generated al
gebras over a field are Jacobson rings, which in the case of an algebraically cl
osed field is a consequence of the Nullstellensatz.)

**Conclusion.** Since $Y$ is a closed subset of $X$ and $X$ is Jacobson, the clo
sed points of $X$ lying in $Y$ are dense in $Y$. The set $Y \cap \operatorname{R
eg}(X)$ is a non-empty open subset of $Y$, so it must contain a closed point $x$
 of $X$ (which, since $Y$ is closed in $X$, is also a closed point of $Y$).

This point $x$ satisfies:
- $x \in Y$ (so $x$ is a closed point contained in $Y$),
- $x \in \operatorname{Reg}(X)$ (so $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ is a regular local ring,
 i.e., $x$ is regular on $X$).

Therefore $Y$ contains a closed point that is regular on $X$. $\blacksquare$
