## Proof

We wish to find

$$\min_{\|x\|=\|y\|=1} \frac{\|x^{T}Ay\|}{\|x\|\,\|y\|} = \min_{\|x\|=\|y\|=1} |
x^{T}Ay|.$$

Let $A$ be an $m\times n$ real matrix, $x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}$, $y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
$.

**Key observation.** For any *fixed* unit vector $y$, the product $Ay$ is a sing
le vector in $\mathbb{R}^{m}$. The quantity $x^{T}Ay$ is the inner product of $x
$ with $Ay$. As long as the ambient space of $x$ has dimension at least $2$, we
can always choose a unit vector $x$ orthogonal to $Ay$.

**Case 1: $m\ge 2$.** Fix any unit $y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$ (such a $y$ exists since
 $n\ge 1$). The vector $Ay\in\mathbb{R}^{m}$ spans a subspace of dimension at mo
st $1$. Its orthogonal complement in $\mathbb{R}^{m}$ has dimension at least $m-
1\ge 1$, so it contains a unit vector $x$. Then

$$x^{T}Ay = 0.$$

Hence the minimum is $0$.

**Case 2: $m=1$, $n\ge 2$.** Now $A$ is a single row vector $a^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^
{1\times n}$ and $x=\pm 1$, so $|x^{T}Ay|=|a^{T}y|$. Since $n\ge 2$, the orthogo
nal complement of $a$ in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ is nontrivial; pick a unit $y\perp a$.
 Then $a^{T}y=0$, so the minimum is $0$.

**Case 3: $m=n=1$.** Here $A=[a]$ is a scalar, $x,y\in\{\pm1\}$, and $|x^{T}Ay|=
|a|$. The minimum is $|a|$.

**Conclusion.** For every matrix of size at least $2$ in one of its dimensions,
the minimum equals $0$. The only exceptional case is a $1\times 1$ matrix (a sca
lar), where the minimum is $|A|$.

In particular, for any nontrivial matrix ($m\ge 2$ or $n\ge 2$), the minimum val
ue is

$$\boxed{0}.$$

The intuition: $x^{T}Ay$ is a *single* bilinear form value, and with two free un
it vectors we always retain enough degrees of freedom to force this one scalar t
o vanish — by aligning $x$ perpendicular to $Ay$ (when $m\ge2$) or $y$ perpendic
ular to $a$ (when $m=1,n\ge2$). This contrasts sharply with the *maximum* $\max_
{\|x\|=\|y\|=1}|x^{T}Ay|$, which equals the largest singular value $\sigma_{\max
}(A)$ and is generally positive.
