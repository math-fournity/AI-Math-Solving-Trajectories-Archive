## Proof

The target function is continuous and piecewise linear with breakpoints at $x=-1
$ and $x=0$. The slopes are $0$, $3$, and $-2$ on the three regions, so the slop
e changes are $+3$ at $x=-1$ and $-5$ at $x=0$.

**Key idea.** If $p(x)=a(x-r)$ is linear, then $|p(x)|=|a|\cdot|x-r|$ has a kink
 at $x=r$ with slope change $+2|a|$ (slope $-|a|$ left of $r$, slope $+|a|$ righ
t of $r$). We place one kink at each breakpoint.

**Construction.** Let $f(x)=\tfrac{3}{2}(x+1)$ (zero at $x=-1$) and $g(x)=\tfrac
{5}{2}x$ (zero at $x=0$). Then:
- $|f(x)|$ contributes slope change $+2\cdot\tfrac{3}{2}=+3$ at $x=-1$. ✓
- $-|g(x)|$ contributes slope change $-2\cdot\tfrac{5}{2}=-5$ at $x=0$. ✓

Let $h(x)=mx+b$ be linear (no kinks). We determine $m,b$ from the region $x<-1$.

**Region $x<-1$:** Here $f(x)<0$ and $g(x)<0$, so $|f(x)|=-\tfrac{3}{2}(x+1)$ an
d $|g(x)|=-\tfrac{5}{2}x$. Thus
$$|f|-|g|+h = -\tfrac{3}{2}(x+1)+\tfrac{5}{2}x+mx+b = (1+m)x+\bigl(b-\tfrac{3}{2
}\bigr).$$
Setting this equal to $-1$ gives $m=-1$ and $b=\tfrac{1}{2}$, so $h(x)=-x+\tfrac
{1}{2}$.

**Verification.**

*Region $-1\le x\le 0$:* $f(x)\ge0$, $g(x)\le0$, so $|f|=\tfrac{3}{2}(x+1)$, $|g
|=-\tfrac{5}{2}x$:
$$\tfrac{3}{2}(x+1)+\tfrac{5}{2}x-x+\tfrac{1}{2} = \bigl(\tfrac{3}{2}+\tfrac{5}{
2}-1\bigr)x+\bigl(\tfrac{3}{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)=3x+2.\ \checkmark$$

*Region $x>0$:* $f(x)>0$, $g(x)>0$, so $|f|=\tfrac{3}{2}(x+1)$, $|g|=\tfrac{5}{2
}x$:
$$\tfrac{3}{2}(x+1)-\tfrac{5}{2}x-x+\tfrac{1}{2}=\bigl(\tfrac{3}{2}-\tfrac{5}{2}
-1\bigr)x+\bigl(\tfrac{3}{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)=-2x+2.\ \checkmark$$

All three regions match. The polynomials are:

$$\boxed{f(x)=\tfrac{3}{2}(x+1),\quad g(x)=\tfrac{5}{2}x,\quad h(x)=-x+\tfrac{1}
{2}.}$$
