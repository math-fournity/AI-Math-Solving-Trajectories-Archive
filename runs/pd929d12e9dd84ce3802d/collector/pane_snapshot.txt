## Solution

**Step 1: Monotonicity of $f$.**

$$f'(x) = 3x^2 - 24x + 69 = 3(x^2 - 8x + 23).$$

The discriminant of $x^2 - 8x + 23$ is $64 - 92 = -28 < 0$, and the leading coef
ficient is positive, so $f'(x) > 0$ for all $x$. Hence $f$ is strictly increasin
g and $f^{-1}$ exists globally.

**Step 2: Locate the integration limits.**

Compute $f$ at small integer points:

- $f(1) = 1 - 12 + 69 + 6 = 64$
- $f(2) = 8 - 48 + 138 + 6 = 104$

Since $f$ is strictly increasing, $f^{-1}(64) = 1$ and $f^{-1}(104) = 2$.

**Step 3: Apply the inverse-function integral formula.**

Using integration by parts with $u = f^{-1}(x)$, $dw = dx$, and then substitutin
g $x = f(t)$:

$$\int_{64}^{104} f^{-1}(x)\,dx = \Big[x\,f^{-1}(x)\Big]_{64}^{104} - \int_{f^{-
1}(64)}^{f^{-1}(104)} f(t)\,dt.$$

The boundary term:

$$\Big[x\,f^{-1}(x)\Big]_{64}^{104} = 104\cdot 2 - 64\cdot 1 = 208 - 64 = 144.$$

**Step 4: Compute $\int_1^2 f(t)\,dt$.**

$$\int_1^2 (t^3 - 12t^2 + 69t + 6)\,dt = \left[\frac{t^4}{4} - 4t^3 + \frac{69t^
2}{2} + 6t\right]_1^2.$$

At $t = 2$: $\frac{16}{4} - 4\cdot 8 + \frac{69\cdot 4}{2} + 12 = 4 - 32 + 138 +
 12 = 122$.

At $t = 1$: $\frac{1}{4} - 4 + \frac{69}{2} + 6 = \frac{1}{4} - 4 + \frac{69}{2}
 + 6 = \frac{1 - 16 + 138 + 24}{4} = \frac{147}{4} = 36.75$.

Difference: $122 - \frac{147}{4} = \frac{488 - 147}{4} = \frac{341}{4}$.

**Step 5: Combine.**

$$\int_{64}^{104} f^{-1}(x)\,dx = 144 - \frac{341}{4} = \frac{576 - 341}{4} = \f
rac{235}{4}.$$

**Verification (geometric check).** The integral represents the area between $y
= f^{-1}(x)$, the $x$-axis, $x = 64$, $x = 104$. Equivalently it equals the area
 of the rectangle $[64,104]\times[1,2]$ minus the area under $f$ on $[1,2]$ mapp
ed appropriately — which is exactly the formula used. The arithmetic $208 - 64 -
 85.25 = 58.75 = \frac{235}{4}$ is consistent.

$$\boxed{\int_{64}^{104} f^{-1}(x)\,dx = \dfrac{235}{4}}$$
