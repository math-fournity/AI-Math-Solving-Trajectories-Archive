## Proof

We evaluate
$$I = \int_0^{\infty}\frac{e^{-x}-e^{-2x}}{x}\,dx.$$

**Convergence.** Near $x=0$, using Taylor expansions,
$$e^{-x}-e^{-2x}=\bigl(1-x+O(x^2)\bigr)-\bigl(1-2x+O(x^2)\bigr)=x+O(x^2),$$
so $\frac{e^{-x}-e^{-2x}}{x}\to 1$, which is integrable near $0$. For large $x$,
$$\left|\frac{e^{-x}-e^{-2x}}{x}\right|\le \frac{e^{-x}}{x},$$
which is integrable on $[1,\infty)$. Hence the improper integral converges.

**Differentiation under the integral sign.** Define, for $b>0$,
$$F(b)=\int_0^{\infty}\frac{e^{-x}-e^{-bx}}{x}\,dx.$$
Fix any interval $[c,d]\subset(0,\infty)$ with $c>0$. For $b\in[c,d]$, the parti
al derivative of the integrand with respect to $b$ is
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial b}\frac{e^{-x}-e^{-bx}}{x}=e^{-bx},$$
and $e^{-bx}\le e^{-cx}$, which is integrable on $[0,\infty)$. Thus differentiat
ion under the integral is justified, giving
$$F'(b)=\int_0^{\infty}e^{-bx}\,dx=\frac{1}{b}.$$

**Integrate.** Therefore $F(b)=\ln b+C$ for some constant $C$. Setting $b=1$:
$$F(1)=\int_0^{\infty}\frac{e^{-x}-e^{-x}}{x}\,dx=0=\ln 1+C=C,$$
so $C=0$ and $F(b)=\ln b$.

**Conclusion.** The requested integral corresponds to $b=2$:
$$I=F(2)=\ln 2.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_0^{\infty}\frac{e^{-x}-e^{-2x}}{x}\,dx=\ln 2}$$
