## Proof

**Setup.** Let $f(X) = X^7 - 3 \in \mathbb{Q}[X]$, let $\alpha = 3^{1/7}$ (the r
eal seventh root), and let $\zeta = e^{2\pi i/7}$ (a primitive seventh root of u
nity). The roots of $f$ in $\mathbb{C}$ are $\alpha\zeta^k$ for $k = 0, 1, \dots
, 6$, so the splitting field is
$$Z = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha, \zeta).$$
We compute $[Z:\mathbb{Q}]$ via the tower
$$[Z:\mathbb{Q}] = [\mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\zeta):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)]\cdot[\mathbb{Q}(
\zeta):\mathbb{Q}] = [\mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\zeta):\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)]\cdot[\mathbb{
Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}].$$

**Step 1: $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}] = 7$.**

The polynomial $X^7 - 3$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ by Eisenstein's criter
ion at the prime $p = 3$. Since $\alpha$ is a root, it is the minimal polynomial
 of $\alpha$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, so $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}] = 7$.

**Step 2: $[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta):\mathbb{Q}] = 6$.**

The minimal polynomial of $\zeta$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is the seventh cyclotomic po
lynomial
$$\Phi_7(X) = X^6 + X^5 + X^4 + X^3 + X^2 + X + 1,$$
which has degree $\varphi(7) = 6$. Hence $[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta):\mathbb{Q}] = 6$.

**Step 3: $X^7 - 3$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$, so $[\mathbb{Q}(\al
pha,\zeta):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)] = 7$.**

Since $7$ is prime, by the binomial irreducibility criterion (Capelli's theorem)
, $X^7 - 3$ is irreducible over a field $K$ (with $\operatorname{char} K \neq 7$
) if and only if $3 \notin K^7$ (i.e., $3$ is not a seventh power in $K$). We ap
ply this with $K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$.

Suppose for contradiction that $3 = \beta^7$ for some $\beta \in \mathbb{Q}(\zet
a)$. Then $\beta$ is a root of $X^7 - 3$, which is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$
 (Step 1), so $[\mathbb{Q}(\beta):\mathbb{Q}] = 7$. But $\beta \in \mathbb{Q}(\z
eta)$ implies $\mathbb{Q}(\beta) \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$, so by the tower l
aw $[\mathbb{Q}(\beta):\mathbb{Q}]$ divides $[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta):\mathbb{Q}] = 6$
. This gives $7 \mid 6$, a contradiction.

Therefore $3 \notin \mathbb{Q}(\zeta)^7$, so $X^7 - 3$ is irreducible over $\mat
hbb{Q}(\zeta)$, and
$$[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\zeta):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)] = 7.$$

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 2 and 3:
$$[Z:\mathbb{Q}] = [\mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\zeta):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)]\cdot[\mathbb{Q}(
\zeta):\mathbb{Q}] = 7 \cdot 6 = \boxed{42}.$$

*Consistency check.* From the other factorization: $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\zeta):\m
athbb{Q}(\alpha)] = 42/7 = 6 = [\mathbb{Q}(\zeta):\mathbb{Q}]$, confirming that
$\Phi_7$ remains irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$, as expected since $\gcd(
7,6)=1$ forces the two extensions to be linearly disjoint.
