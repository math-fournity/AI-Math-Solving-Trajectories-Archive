## Solution

We seek a Möbius transformation mapping the annulus $\{z : r < |z| < 1\}$ onto t
he region between the circles $|z - \tfrac14| = \tfrac14$ and $|z| = 1$.

### Setup

The circle $|z-\tfrac14|=\tfrac14$ is centered at $\tfrac14$ with radius $\tfrac
14$, passing through $0$ and $\tfrac12$. The target region lies between this sma
ll circle and the unit circle.

A Möbius transformation maps circles/lines to circles/lines and is conformal. We
 seek a transformation that:
- maps $|z|=1$ (the outer boundary) to $|w|=1$,
- maps $|z|=r$ (the inner boundary) to $|w-\tfrac14|=\tfrac14$.

### Choosing the transformation

Automorphisms of the unit disk have the form $w = e^{i\theta}\frac{z-a}{1-\bar a
\, z}$ with $|a|<1$, and these map $|z|=1$ to $|w|=1$. By symmetry (both target
circles are symmetric about the real axis), we take $a \in \mathbb{R}$ and $\the
ta=0$:

$$w = \frac{z-a}{1-az}, \qquad a \in \mathbb{R},\ |a|<1.$$

### Determining $a$ from the inner-circle condition

For $z = re^{i\theta}$ on $|z|=r$, we require $|w-\tfrac14|=\tfrac14$, equivalen
tly $|w|^2 = \tfrac12\,\mathrm{Re}(w)$.

Computing with $z=re^{i\theta}$:

$$|w|^2 = \frac{r^2 - 2ar\cos\theta + a^2}{1 - 2ar\cos\theta + a^2 r^2}, \qquad
\mathrm{Re}(w) = \frac{r(1+a^2)\cos\theta - a(1+r^2)}{1 - 2ar\cos\theta + a^2 r^
2}.$$

Setting $|w|^2 = \tfrac12\,\mathrm{Re}(w)$ and clearing the (common, nonzero) de
nominator:

$$2(r^2 - 2ar\cos\theta + a^2) = r(1+a^2)\cos\theta - a(1+r^2).$$

This must hold for **all** $\theta$, so we equate coefficients of $\cos\theta$ a
nd the constant term:

- **$\cos\theta$ coefficient:** $-4ar = r(1+a^2)$, giving $a^2 + 4a + 1 = 0$, so
 $a = -2 \pm \sqrt{3}$. Only $a = \sqrt{3}-2 \approx -0.268$ satisfies $|a|<1$.

- **Constant term:** $2r^2 + 2a^2 = -a(1+r^2)$, i.e. $r^2(2+a) + a(2a+1) = 0$, g
iving

$$r^2 = \frac{-a(2a+1)}{2+a}.$$

### Computing $r$

With $a = \sqrt{3}-2$:
- $2+a = \sqrt{3}$,
- $2a+1 = 2\sqrt{3}-3$,
- $(\sqrt{3}-2)(2\sqrt{3}-3) = 6 - 3\sqrt{3} - 4\sqrt{3} + 6 = 12 - 7\sqrt{3}$,

so $-a(2a+1) = 7\sqrt{3}-12$, and

$$r^2 = \frac{7\sqrt{3}-12}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{21 - 12\sqrt{3}}{3} = 7 - 4\sqrt{3
} = (2-\sqrt{3})^2.$$

Since $0 < r < 1$, we take $r = 2 - \sqrt{3}$.

### Verification

Note $a = \sqrt{3}-2 = -(2-\sqrt{3}) = -r$, so $w = \frac{z+r}{1+rz}$.

**Check $z = r$ (real point on inner circle):** $w = \frac{2r}{1+r^2}$. Since $r
^2 = 7-4\sqrt{3}$, we get $1+r^2 = 8-4\sqrt{3} = 4(2-\sqrt{3}) = 4r$, so $w = \f
rac{2r}{4r} = \tfrac12$. Indeed $|\tfrac12 - \tfrac14| = \tfrac14$. ✓

**Check $z = -r$:** $w = \frac{0}{1-r^2} = 0$. Indeed $|0 - \tfrac14| = \tfrac14
$. ✓

**Check $z=1$ (on unit circle):** $w = \frac{1+r}{1+r} = 1$, and $|1|=1$. ✓

**Region orientation:** $z=0$ (inside $|z|=r$, hence outside the annulus) maps t
o $w = r = 2-\sqrt{3} \approx 0.268$, which satisfies $|w-\tfrac14| \approx 0.01
8 < \tfrac14$ (inside the small circle). Thus the interior of $|z|=r$ maps to th
e interior of $|w-\tfrac14|=\tfrac14$, confirming the annulus $\{r<|z|<1\}$ maps
 to the region **between** $|w-\tfrac14|=\tfrac14$ and $|w|=1$. ✓

### Answer

$$\boxed{r = 2 - \sqrt{3}}$$
