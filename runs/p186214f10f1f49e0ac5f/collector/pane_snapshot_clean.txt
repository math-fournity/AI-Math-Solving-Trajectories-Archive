No. A finite Fourier series is a trigonometric polynomial, and there exist real-
analytic $2\pi$-periodic functions whose Fourier series have infinitely many non
zero coefficients.

**Counterexample.** Consider
$$f(x)=\frac{1}{2-\cos x}.$$

*Analyticity and periodicity.* The denominator satisfies $2-\cos x\ge 1>0$ for a
ll $x\in\mathbb{R}$, so $f$ is real-analytic on all of $\mathbb{R}$ (it is the q
uotient of an analytic function by a nonvanishing analytic function). Since $\co
s x$ is $2\pi$-periodic, $f$ is $2\pi$-periodic.

*Fourier coefficients.* We use the Poisson-kernel identity: for $|r|<1$,
$$\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} r^{|n|}e^{inx}=\frac{1-r^{2}}{1-2r\cos x+r^{2}}.$$

We match the denominator $2-\cos x$ to $1-2r\cos x+r^{2}$ up to a constant facto
r. This requires $-2r$ proportional to $-1$ and $1+r^{2}$ proportional to $2$ wi
th the same constant, i.e.
$$1+r^{2}=2(2r)\;\Longrightarrow\;r^{2}-4r+1=0\;\Longrightarrow\;r=2\pm\sqrt{3}.
$$
Taking the root inside the unit disk, $r=2-\sqrt{3}\in(0,1)$. With $1+r^{2}=2r(2
-\cos x)$, we get
$$\frac{1}{2-\cos x}=\frac{2r}{1-r^{2}}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}r^{|n|}e^{inx}.$
$
A short simplification using $r^{2}=7-4\sqrt{3}$ and $2\sqrt{3}-3=\sqrt{3}\,r$ g
ives $\frac{2r}{1-r^{2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$. Hence
$$f(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left(1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r^{n}\cos(nx)\right),\qquad
 r=2-\sqrt{3}.$$

*Infinitely many nonzero coefficients.* Since $0<r<1$, every coefficient $\wideh
at{f}(n)=\frac{r^{|n|}}{\sqrt{3}}$ is strictly positive, hence nonzero for all $
n\in\mathbb{Z}$. In particular the Fourier series of $f$ has infinitely many non
zero terms and is therefore not a finite Fourier series.

**Conclusion.** The function $f(x)=\frac{1}{2-\cos x}$ is real-analytic and $2\p
i$-periodic, yet its Fourier series is infinite. Thus not every analytic $2\pi$-
periodic function has a finite Fourier series; the answer is **no**.
