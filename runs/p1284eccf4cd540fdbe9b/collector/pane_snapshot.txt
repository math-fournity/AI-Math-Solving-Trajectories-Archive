## Proof

**Answer.** For every prime $p$ and $n\geq 2$ with $(p,n)\neq(2,2)$, set
$$
g(x) \;=\; \Bigl(\prod_{a=0}^{p-1}(x-a)\Bigr)^{n} \;=\; \bigl(x(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(
x-(p-1))\bigr)^{n}.
$$

We verify the three required properties.

**Monic.** The product $\prod_{a=0}^{p-1}(x-a)$ is monic of degree $p$. Raising
a monic polynomial to the $n$-th power yields a monic polynomial, so $g$ is moni
c.

**Degree.** $\deg g = pn$. We claim $pn < p^{n}$, i.e. $n < p^{\,n-1}$, for ever
y $(p,n)\neq(2,2)$ in range:
- $p=2,\; n\geq 3$: $2^{n-1}\geq n$ for all $n\geq 1$, with strict inequality $2
^{n-1}>n$ for $n\geq 3$.
- $p\geq 3,\; n=2$: $p^{1}=p\geq 3 > 2 = n$.
- $p\geq 3,\; n\geq 3$: $p^{\,n-1}\geq 3^{\,n-1} > n$ (since $3^{n-1}$ grows fas
ter than $n$; base case $n=3$: $9>3$).

The unique failure is $p=2,n=2$, where $pn = 4 = p^{n}$, which is exactly the ex
cluded case. Hence $\deg g = pn < p^{n}$ in every allowed case.

**Vanishing modulo $p^{n}$.** Let $y\in\mathbb{Z}$. The $p$ integers $y, y-1, \d
ots, y-(p-1)$ form a complete residue system modulo $p$, so exactly one of them
is divisible by $p$. Therefore
$$
p \;\Big|\; \prod_{a=0}^{p-1}(y-a),
$$
i.e. $\prod_{a=0}^{p-1}(y-a) = p\,m$ for some $m\in\mathbb{Z}$. Consequently
$$
g(y) = \Bigl(\prod_{a=0}^{p-1}(y-a)\Bigr)^{n} = (p\,m)^{n} = p^{n}\,m^{n} \equiv
 0 \pmod{p^{n}}.
$$

Since this holds for every integer $y$, it holds for every residue class $y\in\m
athbb{Z}_{p^{n}}$.

**Conclusion.** The polynomial
$$
\boxed{\,g(x)=\Bigl(x(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-(p-1))\Bigr)^{n}\,}
$$
is monic, has degree $pn<p^{n}$ (for all $(p,n)\neq(2,2)$ with $p$ prime and $n\
geq 2$), and satisfies $g(y)\equiv 0\pmod{p^{n}}$ for every $y\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{
n}}$. $\blacksquare$
