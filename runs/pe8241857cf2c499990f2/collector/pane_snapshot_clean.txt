### 1. Finding the critical points

Set $x' = 0$ and $y' = 0$:

$$x' = x\!\left(\frac{a}{1+y} - 1\right) = 0, \qquad y' = \frac{x}{1+y} - y + \b
eta = 0.$$

From $x' = 0$, either $x = 0$ or $\frac{a}{1+y} = 1$, i.e. $y = a - 1$.

**Case 1: $x = 0$.** Then $y' = -y + \beta = 0 \Rightarrow y = \beta$, giving th
e boundary critical point $(0, \beta)$ on the $y$-axis (not in the open first qu
adrant $x > 0, y > 0$).

**Case 2: $y = a - 1$.** Since $a > 1$, we have $y = a-1 > 0$. Substituting into
 $y' = 0$ with $1+y = a$:

$$\frac{x}{a} - (a-1) + \beta = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; x = a(a - 1 - \beta).$$

For this point to lie in the open first quadrant we need $x > 0$, i.e.

$$a - 1 - \beta > 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \beta < a - 1.$$

**Conclusion on uniqueness.** The point $(0,\beta)$ lies on the boundary, not in
 the open first quadrant. Therefore the system has a (unique) critical point in
the first quadrant **if and only if**

$$\boxed{\,\beta < a - 1\,}$$

(in addition to the given $a > 1$, $\beta > 0$), and that critical point is

$$(\bar x, \bar y) = \bigl(a(a-1-\beta),\; a - 1\bigr).$$

### 2. Linearization and stability

The Jacobian of the system

$$f(x,y) = x\!\left(\tfrac{a}{1+y}-1\right), \qquad g(x,y) = \tfrac{x}{1+y} - y
+ \beta$$

is

$$J(x,y) = \begin{pmatrix} \dfrac{a}{1+y} - 1 & -\dfrac{ax}{(1+y)^2} \\[6pt] \df
rac{1}{1+y} & -\dfrac{x}{(1+y)^2} - 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Evaluating at $(\bar x, \bar y)$ with $1 + \bar y = a$ and $\bar x = a(a-1-\beta
)$:

- $\dfrac{a}{1+\bar y} - 1 = \dfrac{a}{a} - 1 = 0,$
- $-\dfrac{a\bar x}{(1+\bar y)^2} = -\dfrac{a \cdot a(a-1-\beta)}{a^2} = -(a-1-\
beta) = \beta - a + 1,$
- $\dfrac{1}{1+\bar y} = \dfrac{1}{a},$
- $-\dfrac{\bar x}{(1+\bar y)^2} - 1 = -\dfrac{a(a-1-\beta)}{a^2} - 1 = -\dfrac{
a-1-\beta}{a} - 1 = -\dfrac{2a - 1 - \beta}{a}.$

Hence

$$J(\bar x, \bar y) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \beta - a + 1 \\[4pt] \dfrac{1}{a} & -
\dfrac{2a - 1 - \beta}{a} \end{pmatrix}.$$

**Trace:**

$$T = -\frac{2a - 1 - \beta}{a} = \frac{\beta - 2a + 1}{a}.$$

**Determinant:**

$$D = 0 \cdot \!\left(-\tfrac{2a-1-\beta}{a}\right) - (\beta - a + 1)\cdot \tfra
c{1}{a} = -\frac{\beta - a + 1}{a} = \frac{a - 1 - \beta}{a}.$$

Since $\beta < a - 1$, we have $a - 1 - \beta > 0$, so

$$D > 0.$$

For the trace, using $\beta < a - 1 < 2a - 1$ (as $a > 1$):

$$\beta - 2a + 1 < (a-1) - 2a + 1 = -a < 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad T < 0.$$

**Discriminant:**

$$T^2 - 4D = \frac{(\beta - 2a + 1)^2}{a^2} - \frac{4(a-1-\beta)}{a} = \frac{(\b
eta - 2a + 1)^2 - 4a(a-1-\beta)}{a^2}.$$

Expanding the numerator:

$$(\beta - 2a + 1)^2 - 4a(a-1-\beta) = \beta^2 + 2\beta + 1 = (\beta + 1)^2.$$

Therefore

$$T^2 - 4D = \frac{(\beta+1)^2}{a^2} > 0 \qquad (\text{since } \beta > 0).$$

---

### 3. Conclusion

With $D > 0$, $T < 0$, and $T^2 - 4D > 0$, both eigenvalues of $J(\bar x, \bar y
)$ are **real, distinct, and negative**. By the Hartman–Grobman theorem, the cri
tical point $(\bar x, \bar y) = (a(a-1-\beta),\, a-1)$ is an **asymptotically st
able node** (a stable improper node).

**Summary.** The system has a single critical point in the (open) first quadrant
 if and only if

$$a > 1 \quad\text{and}\quad 0 < \beta < a - 1.$$

Under these conditions the unique critical point is $(\bar x, \bar y) = \bigl(a(
a-1-\beta),\, a-1\bigr)$, and it is an **asymptotically stable node**.
