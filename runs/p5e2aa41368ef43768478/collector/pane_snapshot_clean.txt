## Proof

ors. Under the standard convention, $g = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pma
trix}$ acts by
$$g \cdot x = x + y, \qquad g \cdot y = y.$$
(Using the contragredient convention $g \cdot x = x - y$ yields the same invaria
nt ring since $\langle g \rangle = \langle g^{-1} \rangle$.)

**Order of $g$.** Since $\operatorname{char} F = p$ (an odd prime), we have $g^n
 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & n \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, so $g^p = \begin{pmatrix} 1 &
p \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = I$. For $0 < n < p$, $g^n \neq I$ since $n \neq 0$ in
 $F$. Thus $g$ has order $p$, and $\langle g \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z
}$.

**Identifying invariants.** Clearly $y$ is invariant since $g \cdot y = y$. Defi

$$u := x^p - x\,y^{p-1}.$$
We verify $u$ is invariant. Using $(x+y)^p = x^p + y^p$ (the Frobenius, valid in
 characteristic $p$):
$$g \cdot u = (x+y)^p - (x+y)\,y^{p-1} = x^p + y^p - x\,y^{p-1} - y^p = x^p - x\
,y^{p-1} = u. \quad \checkmark$$

**Algebraic independence.** The elements $y$ and $u$ are algebraically independe
nt over $F$. Indeed, suppose $P(y, u) = 0$ for some nonzero $P \in F[T_1, T_2]$.
 Write $P = \sum_{i=0}^{d} a_i(T_1)\, T_2^i$ with $a_d \neq 0$. Then
$$0 = P(y, u) = \sum_{i=0}^{d} a_i(y)\,(x^p - x\,y^{p-1})^i.$$
The term $(x^p - x\,y^{p-1})^i$ has $x$-degree $pi$ (the leading term is $x^{pi}
$). Since the degrees $0, p, 2p, \ldots, dp$ are distinct, the $x$-degree $pd$ t
erm is $a_d(y)\,x^{pd}$, which must vanish, forcing $a_d(y) = 0$—a contradiction
. Hence $F[y, u]$ is a polynomial ring in two variables.

**Every invariant lies in $F[y, u]$.** We now show $F[x,y]^{\langle g \rangle} =
 F[y, u]$.

*Step 1: Field-level invariants via Galois theory.* Consider the fraction fields
. Set $K = F(y)$, and view $K[x]$ as an extension of $K(u)$. The element $x$ sat
isfies
$$x^p - y^{p-1}x - u = 0,$$
i.e., $x$ is a root of $\Phi(T) = T^p - y^{p-1}T - u \in K(u)[T]$. The roots of
$\Phi$ are $x, x+y, x+2y, \ldots, x+(p-1)y$, since for $i \in \mathbb{F}_p$:
$$(x+iy)^p - y^{p-1}(x+iy) - u = x^p + i^p y^p - x\,y^{p-1} - iy^p - (x^p - x\,y
^{p-1}) = (i^p - i)\,y^p = 0,$$
using $i^p = i$ for $i \in \mathbb{F}_p \subseteq F$ (since $F$ is algebraically
 closed, hence contains $\mathbb{F}_p$). Thus
$$\Phi(T) = \prod_{i \in \mathbb{F}_p} \bigl(T - (x+iy)\bigr),$$
which is separable (the roots $x+iy$ are distinct since $y \neq 0$ in $K$). The
extension $K(x)/K(u)$ is therefore Galois of degree $p$, with Galois group $\mat
hbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ acting by $x \mapsto x + iy$. By Galois theory:
$$K(x)^{\langle g \rangle} = K(u) = F(y)(u).$$

*Step 2: Integrality / module basis.* The ring $F[y][x]$ is a free $F[y][u]$-mod

follows from the irreducibility of $\Phi$ over $K(u)$ (it is the minimal polynom

*Step 3: Conclusion.* Let $f \in F[x,y]^{\langle g \rangle} \subseteq F[y][x]$.

$$f = \sum_{i=0}^{p-1} b_i(y, u)\, x^i, \qquad b_i \in F[y][u].$$
This same expression holds in $K(x) = F(y)[x]$. Since $f$ is invariant, by Step

This shows $F[x,y]^{\langle g \rangle} \subseteq F[y, u]$, and the reverse inclu
