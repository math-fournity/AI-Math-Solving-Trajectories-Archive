## Proof

An integer $N$ belongs to $S$ if and only if $N \equiv a \pmod{p}$. We are given
 $x^{41} \equiv a \pmod p$ and $y^{49} \equiv a \pmod p$ for positive integers $
x,y$, and we must decide whether some positive integer $z$ satisfies $z^{2009} \
equiv a \pmod p$.

Note the key factorization
$$2009 = 41 \times 49, \qquad \gcd(41,49)=1.$$

We split into two cases.

---

**Case 1: $p \mid a$.**

Then $a \equiv 0 \pmod p$, so membership in $S$ means simply being divisible by
$p$. The hypothesis $x^{41}\in S$ gives $p\mid x^{41}$, hence $p\mid x$ (consist
ent). Take $z = p$. Then $z^{2009} = p^{2009} \equiv 0 \equiv a \pmod p$, so $z^
{2009}\in S$.

---

**Case 2: $p \nmid a$.**

Then $x^{41}\equiv a\not\equiv 0\pmod p$ forces $p\nmid x$, and likewise $p\nmid
 y$. All relevant elements lie in the cyclic group $\mathbb F_p^{\times}$ of ord
er $p-1$. Fix a generator $g$ and write
$$x = g^{u},\quad y = g^{v},\quad a = g^{w} \pmod p$$
for integers $u,v,w$ (with $w$ well-defined modulo $p-1$). The hypotheses become
$$41\,u \equiv w \pmod{p-1}, \qquad 49\,v \equiv w \pmod{p-1}.$$

A linear congruence $k\,t \equiv w \pmod{p-1}$ is solvable iff $\gcd(k,\,p-1)\mi
d w$. Hence the two hypotheses give
$$d_{41}:=\gcd(41,p-1)\mid w, \qquad d_{49}:=\gcd(49,p-1)\mid w,$$
so $\operatorname{lcm}(d_{41},d_{49})\mid w$.

Now $41$ is prime, so $d_{41}\in\{1,41\}$ is a power of $41$; and $49=7^{2}$, so
 $d_{49}\in\{1,7,49\}$ is a power of $7$. Since $\gcd(41,49)=1$, the two divisor
s $d_{41},d_{49}$ are coprime, whence
$$\operatorname{lcm}(d_{41},d_{49}) = d_{41}\,d_{49}.$$

Writing $p-1 = 41^{\alpha}\,7^{\beta}\,m$ with $\gcd(m,287)=1$, $\alpha\in\{0,1\
}$, $\beta\ge 0$, we compute directly
$$\gcd(2009,\,p-1)=\gcd(41\cdot 49,\,p-1)=41^{\alpha}\,7^{\min(\beta,2)} = d_{41
}\,d_{49}.$$

Therefore
$$\gcd(2009,\,p-1)=\operatorname{lcm}(d_{41},d_{49})\mid w.$$

This is precisely the solvability condition for the congruence
$$2009\,t \equiv w \pmod{p-1}.$$

Pick any solution $t$ and set $z \equiv g^{t}\pmod p$ (with $z$ chosen as the po
sitive representative in $\{1,\dots,p-1\}$, so $z$ is a positive integer and $p\
nmid z$). Then
$$z^{2009} \equiv g^{2009\,t} \equiv g^{w} \equiv a \pmod p,$$
so $z^{2009}\in S$.

---

Both cases yield a positive integer $z$ with $z^{2009}\in S$. The essential poin
t is that since $2009 = 41\cdot 49$ with coprime factors, the solvability of the
 two separate congruences $x^{41}\equiv a$ and $y^{49}\equiv a$ in $\mathbb F_p^
{\times}$ forces $a$ to lie in the image of the $2009$-power map, which is exact
ly the subgroup whose exponent divides $\gcd(2009,p-1)=\operatorname{lcm}(\gcd(4
1,p-1),\gcd(49,p-1))$.
