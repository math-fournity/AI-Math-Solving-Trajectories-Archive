## Proof

We evaluate $\displaystyle L = \lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\gamma(x+1,\,x)}{\Gamma(x+
1)}$.

**Step 1: Probabilistic interpretation.**

Recall $\gamma(s,t) = \int_0^t u^{s-1}e^{-u}\,du$ and $\Gamma(s)=\int_0^\infty u
^{s-1}e^{-u}\,du$. Hence

$$\frac{\gamma(x+1,x)}{\Gamma(x+1)} = \frac{\int_0^x u^x e^{-u}\,du}{\int_0^\inf
ty u^x e^{-u}\,du}.$$

Let $Y_x \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(\alpha,1)$ with shape $\alpha = x+1$ and rate $1$,
whose density is $\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}u^{\alpha-1}e^{-u}$ on $(0,\infty)$. T
hen

$$\frac{\gamma(x+1,x)}{\Gamma(x+1)} = \Pr(Y_x \le x).$$

The mean and variance of $Y_x$ are $\mathbb{E}[Y_x]=x+1$ and $\operatorname{Var}
(Y_x)=x+1$.

**Step 2: Normal approximation to the Gamma distribution.**

Set $Z_x = \frac{Y_x - (x+1)}{\sqrt{x+1}}$. We show $Z_x \xrightarrow{d} \mathca
l{N}(0,1)$ as $x\to\infty$.

The moment generating function of $Y_x$ is $M_{Y_x}(t) = (1-t)^{-(x+1)}$ for $t<
1$. Thus

$$M_{Z_x}(t) = e^{-t\sqrt{x+1}}\left(1 - \tfrac{t}{\sqrt{x+1}}\right)^{-(x+1)}.$
$

Taking logarithms and using $\log(1-u) = -u - \frac{u^2}{2} - O(u^3)$ for $u\to
0$:

$$\log M_{Z_x}(t) = -t\sqrt{x+1} - (x+1)\log\!\left(1-\tfrac{t}{\sqrt{x+1}}\righ
t) = -t\sqrt{x+1} + (x+1)\left(\tfrac{t}{\sqrt{x+1}} + \tfrac{t^2}{2(x+1)} + O(x
^{-3/2})\right).$$

$$= -t\sqrt{x+1} + t\sqrt{x+1} + \tfrac{t^2}{2} + O(x^{-1/2}) \;\longrightarrow\
; \tfrac{t^2}{2}.$$

So $M_{Z_x}(t)\to e^{t^2/2}$, the MGF of $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$. By the continuity t
heorem for MGFs, $Z_x \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0,1)$.

**Step 3: Evaluate the limit.**

Since $Z_x \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ and the standard normal CDF $\Phi$
is continuous everywhere, Pólya's theorem gives **uniform** convergence $F_{Z_x}
\to\Phi$ on $\mathbb{R}$. Now

$$\Pr(Y_x \le x) = \Pr\!\left(Z_x \le \tfrac{x-(x+1)}{\sqrt{x+1}}\right) = \Pr\!
\left(Z_x \le \tfrac{-1}{\sqrt{x+1}}\right) = F_{Z_x}\!\left(\tfrac{-1}{\sqrt{x+
1}}\right).$$

Let $a_x = \frac{-1}{\sqrt{x+1}}\to 0$. Then

$$\left|F_{Z_x}(a_x) - \Phi(0)\right| \le \underbrace{\left|F_{Z_x}(a_x)-\Phi(a_
x)\right|}_{\to\,0\text{ (uniform convergence)}} + \underbrace{\left|\Phi(a_x)-\
Phi(0)\right|}_{\to\,0\text{ (continuity of }\Phi\text{)}} \;\longrightarrow\; 0
.$$

Since $\Phi(0) = \frac{1}{2}$, we conclude

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\gamma(x+1,\,x)}{\Gamma(x+1)} = \fr
ac{1}{2}.}$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
