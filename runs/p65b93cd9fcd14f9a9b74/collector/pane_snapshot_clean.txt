## Proof

The Riemann zeta function $\zeta(s)$ has exactly one pole: a simple pole at $s =
 1$ with residue $1$. We compute the residue of $f(s) = \zeta'(s)/\zeta(s)$ (the
 logarithmic derivative of $\zeta$) at this point.

**Laurent expansion of $\zeta(s)$ near $s = 1$.** Setting $t = s - 1$, the well-
known expansion is

$$\zeta(s) = \frac{1}{t} + \gamma_0 - \gamma_1\, t + \gamma_2\, t^2 - \cdots,$$

where $\gamma_0$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant and $\gamma_n$ are the Stieltj
es constants. In particular,

$$\zeta(s) = \frac{1}{t}\bigl(1 + \gamma_0\, t - \gamma_1\, t^2 + \cdots\bigr).$
$

**Differentiating:**

$$\zeta'(s) = -\frac{1}{t^2} - \gamma_1 + 2\gamma_2\, t - \cdots = -\frac{1}{t^2
}\bigl(1 + \gamma_1\, t^2 - 2\gamma_2\, t^3 + \cdots\bigr).$$

**Forming the quotient:**

$$\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)} = \frac{-\dfrac{1}{t^2}\bigl(1 + \gamma_1\, t^2 + \
cdots\bigr)}{\dfrac{1}{t}\bigl(1 + \gamma_0\, t - \gamma_1\, t^2 + \cdots\bigr)}
 = -\frac{1}{t}\cdot\frac{1 + \gamma_1\, t^2 + \cdots}{1 + \gamma_0\, t + \cdots
}.$$

Expanding the denominator via $\frac{1}{1+u} = 1 - u + u^2 - \cdots$ with $u = \
gamma_0\, t + \cdots$:

$$\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)} = -\frac{1}{t}\bigl(1 - \gamma_0\, t + \cdots\bigr)
\bigl(1 + \gamma_1\, t^2 + \cdots\bigr) = -\frac{1}{t} + \gamma_0 + O(t).$$

**Reading off the residue.** The coefficient of $\frac{1}{t} = \frac{1}{s-1}$ in
 the Laurent expansion of $f(s)$ about $s = 1$ is

$$\boxed{-1}.$$

**General principle (sanity check).** If a meromorphic function $g$ has a simple
 pole at $s_0$ with $g(s) = \frac{a_{-1}}{s-s_0} + a_0 + \cdots$, then $g'(s) =
-\frac{a_{-1}}{(s-s_0)^2} + \cdots$, and

$$\frac{g'(s)}{g(s)} = \frac{-a_{-1}/(s-s_0)^2 + \cdots}{a_{-1}/(s-s_0) + \cdots
} = -\frac{1}{s-s_0} + \cdots,$$

so the logarithmic derivative of a function at a **simple pole** always has resi
due $-1$. Since $\zeta(s)$ has a simple pole at $s=1$, this confirms the result
directly.

Conversely, at a **zero** of $\zeta$ of order $m$ (the trivial zeros $s = -2, -4
, \ldots$, each simple, and the non-trivial zeros), the logarithmic derivative h
as residue $+m$. But the question asks specifically about the **poles** of $\zet
a(s)$, and the unique pole is $s = 1$.
