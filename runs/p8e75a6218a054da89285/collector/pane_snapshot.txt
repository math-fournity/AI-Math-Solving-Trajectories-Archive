## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle I = \int_0^\infty x\,I_0(2x)\,e^{-x^2}\,dx$.

**Step 1: Series expansion of $I_0$.**

The modified Bessel function has the series representation
$$I_0(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(z/2)^{2n}}{(n!)^2}.$$
Setting $z = 2x$ gives
$$I_0(2x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^{2n}}{(n!)^2}.$$

**Step 2: Interchange sum and integral.**

For $x \ge 0$, every term $x^{2n}/(n!)^2$ is non-negative, $x \ge 0$, and $e^{-x
^2} > 0$, so the integrand $x\,I_0(2x)\,e^{-x^2}$ is a non-negative measurable f
unction. By Tonelli's theorem (monotone convergence), we may interchange the sum
 and integral:
$$I = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(n!)^2}\int_0^\infty x^{2n+1}\,e^{-x^2}\,dx.$
$

**Step 3: Evaluate the inner integral.**

Substitute $t = x^2$, $dt = 2x\,dx$:
$$\int_0^\infty x^{2n+1}\,e^{-x^2}\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^\infty t^n\,e^{-t}\,d
t = \frac{1}{2}\,\Gamma(n+1) = \frac{n!}{2}.$$

**Step 4: Sum the series.**

$$I = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(n!)^2}\cdot\frac{n!}{2} = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{n
=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!} = \frac{e}{2}.$$

**Verification via the integral representation (consistency check).**

Using $I_0(2x) = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi e^{2x\cos\theta}\,d\theta$ and completi
ng the square $-x^2+2x\cos\theta = -(x-\cos\theta)^2+\cos^2\theta$, one recovers
 the same value after integrating; the series approach above is rigorous and avo
ids the resulting error-function terms.

**Answer:**
$$\boxed{\int_0^\infty x\,I_0(2x)\,e^{-x^2}\,dx = \dfrac{e}{2}}$$
