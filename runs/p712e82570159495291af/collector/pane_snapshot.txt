## Solution

**Step 1: Find a closed form for $a_n$.**

We claim that $k!(k^2+k+1) = (k+1)(k+1)! - k\cdot k!$.

**Verification.** $(k+1)(k+1)! - k\cdot k! = k!\big[(k+1)^2 - k\big] = k!(k^2+2k
+1-k) = k!(k^2+k+1)$. ✓

Summing from $k=1$ to $n$, with $h(k)=k\cdot k!$, the sum telescopes:

$$a_n = \sum_{k=1}^{n}\big[h(k+1)-h(k)\big] = h(n+1)-h(1) = (n+1)(n+1)! - 1.$$

Therefore $\boxed{1+a_n = (n+1)(n+1)!}$.

**Step 2: Simplify the numerator.**

$$2+4+6+\ldots+2n = 2(1+2+\cdots+n) = n(n+1).$$

**Step 3: Simplify each term of the series.**

$$\frac{2+4+\cdots+2n}{1+a_n} = \frac{n(n+1)}{(n+1)(n+1)!} = \frac{n}{(n+1)!}.$$

**Step 4: Telescope the series.**

$$\frac{n}{(n+1)!} = \frac{(n+1)-1}{(n+1)!} = \frac{1}{n!} - \frac{1}{(n+1)!}.$$

Therefore

$$\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{n}{(n+1)!} = \sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{n!}-\f
rac{1}{(n+1)!}\right] = \frac{1}{2!} - \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{(N+1)!} = \frac
{1}{2} - 0 = \frac{1}{2}.$$

**Conclusion.** The value of the series is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
