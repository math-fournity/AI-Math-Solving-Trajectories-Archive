Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a polynomial $f \in \mathbb
{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_k]$ that always takes integer values on root-tuples of monic int
eger polynomials must be symmetric.

## Proof

The answer is **yes**: $f$ must be symmetric.

**Setup.** Let $f \in \mathbb{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_k]$ satisfy: for every monic $p(t)=
t^k+a_{k-1}t^{k-1}+\cdots+a_0\in\mathbb{Z}[t]$ and every ordering of its roots $
r_1,\dots,r_k$, we have $f(r_1,\dots,r_k)\in\mathbb{Z}$. We show $f(x_{\sigma(1)
},\dots,x_{\sigma(k)})=f(x_1,\dots,x_k)$ for every $\sigma\in S_k$.

Fix $\sigma\in S_k$ and define $g(x_1,\dots,x_k)=f(x_1,\dots,x_k)-f(x_{\sigma(1)
},\dots,x_{\sigma(k)})\in\mathbb{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_k]$. We prove $g\equiv 0$.

**Step 1: $g$ vanishes at roots of any polynomial whose Galois group contains $\
sigma$.**

Let $p\in\mathbb{Z}[t]$ be monic of degree $k$ with roots $r_1,\dots,r_k$ and Ga
lois group $G\leq S_k$. Since the $r_i$ are algebraic integers and $f$ has integ
er coefficients, $f(r_1,\dots,r_k)$ is an algebraic integer. By hypothesis it li
es in $\mathbb{Z}$, so it is fixed by every element of $G$. For $\tau\in G$, ide
ntifying $\tau$ with its permutation action on the roots:
$$f(r_{\tau(1)},\dots,r_{\tau(k)})=\tau\cdot f(r_1,\dots,r_k)=f(r_1,\dots,r_k).$
$
If $\sigma\in G$, taking $\tau=\sigma$ gives $g(r_1,\dots,r_k)=0$.

**Step 2: Symmetrize $g$ via the product over all conjugates.**

Define $\mathcal{G}(x_1,\dots,x_k)=\prod_{\tau\in S_k} g(x_{\tau(1)},\dots,x_{\t
au(k)})$. This is symmetric (permuting the $x_i$ permutes the factors). By the f
undamental theorem of symmetric polynomials, $\mathcal{G}=\Phi(e_1,\dots,e_k)$ f
or some $\Phi\in\mathbb{Z}[y_1,\dots,y_k]$, where $e_j$ is the $j$-th elementary
 symmetric polynomial.

**Step 3: $\Phi$ vanishes on a Zariski-dense set, hence $\Phi\equiv 0$.**

By Hilbert's irreducibility theorem, the set $\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^k$
of $(a_0,\dots,a_{k-1})$ for which $t^k+a_{k-1}t^{k-1}+\cdots+a_0$ has Galois gr
oup $S_k$ is Zariski-dense in $\mathbb{A}^k_{\mathbb{Q}}$. (The generic polynomi
al has Galois group $S_k$ over $\mathbb{Q}(c_0,\dots,c_{k-1})$; Hilbert's theore
m ensures that integer specializations preserving the full Galois group — i.e.,
preserving irreducibility of the polynomial and all its resolvents — form a Hilb
ert set, which is Zariski-dense.)

For $(a_0,\dots,a_{k-1})\in\mathcal{H}$, the Galois group is $S_k\ni\sigma$, so
by Step 1, $g(r_1,\dots,r_k)=0$, hence $\mathcal{G}(r_1,\dots,r_k)=0$. Since $e_
j(r_1,\dots,r_k)=(-1)^j a_{k-j}$, we get:
$$\Phi\big((-1)^1 a_{k-1},\,(-1)^2 a_{k-2},\,\dots,\,(-1)^k a_0\big)=0 \quad\tex
t{for all } (a_0,\dots,a_{k-1})\in\mathcal{H}.$$
Since $\mathcal{H}$ is Zariski-dense and $\Phi$ is a polynomial, $\Phi\equiv 0$.

**Step 4: Conclude $g\equiv 0$.**

$\Phi\equiv 0$ implies $\mathcal{G}=\Phi(e_1,\dots,e_k)\equiv 0$ as a polynomial
. Since $\mathbb{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_k]$ is an integral domain and $\mathcal{G}=\prod
_{\tau} g^\tau = 0$, at least one factor $g^\tau$ is zero. But $g^\tau\equiv 0$
implies $g = (g^\tau)^{\tau^{-1}}\equiv 0$.

**Conclusion.** Since $\sigma\in S_k$ was arbitrary, $f=f^\sigma$ for all $\sigm
a\in S_k$; that is, $f$ is a symmetric function of its arguments. $\blacksquare$
