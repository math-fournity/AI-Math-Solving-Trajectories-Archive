## Counterexample (1D suffices since $B(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}$ is admissible)

Define on $[-1,1]$:
$$u(x) = \begin{cases} x\,\sin(\log|x|), & x\neq 0,\\ 0, & x=0. \end{cases}$$

**Step 1: $u$ is Lipschitz with $u(0)=0$.**

Clearly $u(0)=0$. For $x\neq 0$,
$$u'(x)=\sin(\log|x|)+\cos(\log|x|),\qquad |u'(x)|\le \sqrt{2}.$$
At the origin, $\frac{|u(x)-u(0)|}{|x|}=|\sin(\log|x|)|\le 1$. Hence $u\in C^{0,
1}(\overline{B(0,1)})$ with $[u]_{C^{0,1}}\le \sqrt{2}=:M$ and $u(0)=0$.

**Step 2: Compute the blow-up family.**

For $0<r\le 1$ and $x\neq 0$:
$$u^r(x)=r^{-1}u(rx)=r^{-1}\cdot rx\cdot\sin(\log|rx|)=x\,\sin\!\bigl(\log|x|+\l
og r\bigr),$$
and $u^r(0)=0$. As expected $[u^r]_{C^{0,1}}\le M$ and $u^r(0)=0$.

**Step 3: Two subsequences give two different limits.**

Choose $r_j=e^{-2\pi j}$. Then $\log r_j=-2\pi j$, so
$$u^{r_j}(x)=x\,\sin\!\bigl(\log|x|-2\pi j\bigr)=x\,\sin(\log|x|)\;\;=:\;u^0_1(x
),$$
for every $j$; the sequence is constant and converges uniformly to $u^0_1(x)=x\s
in(\log|x|)$.

Choose $s_j=e^{-2\pi j+\pi/2}$. Then $\log s_j=-2\pi j+\pi/2$, so
$$u^{s_j}(x)=x\,\sin\!\bigl(\log|x|+\pi/2\bigr)=x\,\cos(\log|x|)\;\;=:\;u^0_2(x)
,$$
again constant in $j$, converging uniformly to $u^0_2(x)=x\cos(\log|x|)$.

**Step 4: The two limits are distinct.**

Evaluate at $x=1$:
$$u^0_1(1)=1\cdot\sin(0)=0,\qquad u^0_2(1)=1\cdot\cos(0)=1.$$
Hence $u^0_1\neq u^0_2$.

Both limits are Lipschitz with constant $\le\sqrt{2}$ and vanish at the origin,
so both are admissible blow-up limits produced by Ascoli–Arzelà along legitimate
 subsequences $r_j\to 0$, $s_j\to 0$.

## Conclusion

The compactness furnished by Ascoli–Arzelà guarantees *existence* of a convergen
t subsequence, but **not uniqueness** of the limit. The blow-up limit $u^0$ depe
nds on the subsequence chosen; different sequences $r_j\to 0$ can yield differen
t limits. Therefore,

$$\boxed{\text{The limit } u^0 \text{ is not unique in general.}}$$
