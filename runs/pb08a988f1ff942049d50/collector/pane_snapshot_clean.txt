**Proof.**

For any integers $m > n \ge 0$ and any $t \in [0,T]$, by the telescoping sum and
 the triangle inequality,
$$
|f_m(t) - f_n(t)| = \left|\sum_{k=n}^{m-1}\bigl(f_{k+1}(t)-f_k(t)\bigr)\right| \
le \sum_{k=n}^{m-1}|f_{k+1}(t)-f_k(t)| \le \sum_{k=n}^{m-1} a_k.
$$
Taking the supremum over $t \in [0,T]$,
$$
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|f_m(t)-f_n(t)| \le \sum_{k=n}^{m-1} a_k \le \sum_{k=n}^{\infty}
 a_k.
$$
Since the tail of a convergent series tends to zero, $\sum_{k=n}^{\infty} a_k \t
o 0$ as $n\to\infty$. Hence for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $N$ such th

$$
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|f_m(t)-f_n(t)| < \varepsilon.
$$
Thus $(f_n)$ is uniformly Cauchy on $[0,T]$.

**Step 2: Pointwise limit exists.**

For each fixed $t \in [0,T]$, the inequality $|f_m(t)-f_n(t)| \le \sum_{k=n}^{m-
1}a_k$ shows $(f_n(t))$ is a Cauchy sequence in $\mathbb{R}$, hence convergent.
Define
$$
f(t) := \lim_{n\to\infty} f_n(t), \qquad t \in [0,T].
$$

Letting $m \to \infty$ in $\sup_{t}|f_m(t)-f_n(t)| \le \sum_{k=n}^{\infty}a_k$ (
the pointwise limit preserves the inequality for each $t$, then supremum is pres

$$
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|f(t) - f_n(t)| \le \sum_{k=n}^{\infty} a_k \to 0 \quad\text{as
} n\to\infty.
$$
Therefore $f_n \to f$ uniformly on $[0,T]$.
