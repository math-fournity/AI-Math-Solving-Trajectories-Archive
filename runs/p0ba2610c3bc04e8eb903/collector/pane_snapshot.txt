**Answer: Yes, such a function exists.**

**Proof.**

Since $I \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ is open and $x_0 \in I$, there exists $\delta > 0
$ such that the open interval $(x_0 - \delta,\, x_0 + \delta)$ is contained in $
I$. It suffices to construct $f \in C_0^p(\mathbb{R})$ with $\operatorname{supp}
(f) \subseteq [x_0-\delta,\, x_0+\delta] \subseteq I$, $f(x_0)=1$, and $|f|\le 1
$.

**Step 1: A smooth cutoff.** Define
$$
\phi(t) = \begin{cases} e^{-1/t}, & t > 0,\\ 0, & t \le 0. \end{cases}
$$
It is standard (and verified by induction on the order of the derivative) that $
\phi \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R})$, with $\phi^{(k)}(0)=0$ for every $k \ge 0$. In p
articular $\phi(t)>0$ for $t>0$ and $\phi(t)=0$ for $t\le 0$.

**Step 2: A smooth step function.** Set
$$
\psi(t) = \frac{\phi(t)}{\phi(t)+\phi(1-t)}.
$$
The denominator is strictly positive for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$ (at every $t$, a
t least one of $t>0$ or $1-t>0$ holds, so at least one term is positive), hence
$\psi \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R})$. Moreover:
- For $t \le 0$: $\phi(t)=0$ and $\phi(1-t)>0$, so $\psi(t)=0$.
- For $t \ge 1$: $\phi(1-t)=0$ and $\phi(t)>0$, so $\psi(t)=1$.
- For $0 < t < 1$: both terms are positive, so $0 < \psi(t) < 1$.

Thus $0 \le \psi(t) \le 1$ for all $t$, with $\psi \equiv 0$ on $(-\infty,0]$ an
d $\psi \equiv 1$ on $[1,+\infty)$.

**Step 3: The bump function.** Define
$$
f(x) = \psi\!\left(1 - \frac{(x-x_0)^2}{\delta^2}\right).
$$

*Smoothness.* The map $x \mapsto 1 - (x-x_0)^2/\delta^2$ is a polynomial (hence
$C^\infty$), and $\psi$ is $C^\infty$, so $f \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R}) \subseteq
C^p(\mathbb{R})$.

*Support.* If $|x - x_0| \ge \delta$, then $(x-x_0)^2/\delta^2 \ge 1$, so the ar
gument of $\psi$ is $\le 0$, giving $f(x)=0$. Hence
$$
\operatorname{supp}(f) \subseteq [\,x_0-\delta,\, x_0+\delta\,] \subseteq (x_0-\
delta,\, x_0+\delta) \subseteq I,
$$
and the support is compact, so $f \in C_0^p(\mathbb{R})$.

*Value at $x_0$.* At $x=x_0$ the argument is $1$, so $f(x_0)=\psi(1)=1$.

*Uniform bound.* Since $0 \le \psi \le 1$ everywhere, $0 \le f(x) \le 1$ for all
 $x$, hence
$$
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}} |f(x)| \le 1.
$$

All the required properties are satisfied, so such a function $f$ exists. $\blac
ksquare$
