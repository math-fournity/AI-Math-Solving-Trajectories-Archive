## Solution

**Setup.** Place the unit circle at the origin. Let the five points $A, B, C, D,
 E$ appear at cumulative angles $0,\; a,\; a+b,\; a+b+c,\; a+b+c+d$, where $a,b,
c,d,e > 0$ are the arc-gaps (central angles for $AB, BC, CD, DE, EA$) with $a+b+
c+d+e = 2\pi$.

**Area formula.** For a cyclic polygon on the unit circle (vertices in circular
order), the area is
$$\mathcal{A} = \tfrac12\bigl(\sin a + \sin b + \sin c + \sin d + \sin e\bigr).$
$

**Deriving the perpendicularity constraint.** The chord from angle $\alpha$ to $
\beta$ has direction angle $\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}+\frac{\pi}{2}$ (since $e^{i\b
eta}-e^{i\alpha}=2i\,e^{i(\alpha+\beta)/2}\sin\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}$).

- Chord $AC$: angles $0$ and $a+b$ → direction $\frac{a+b}{2}+\frac{\pi}{2}$.
- Chord $BD$: angles $a$ and $a+b+c$ → direction $\frac{2a+b+c}{2}+\frac{\pi}{2}
=a+\frac{b+c}{2}+\frac{\pi}{2}$.

Perpendicularity requires the direction difference to be $\frac{\pi}{2}\pmod{\pi
}$:
$$\Bigl(a+\tfrac{b+c}{2}+\tfrac{\pi}{2}\Bigr)-\Bigl(\tfrac{a+b}{2}+\tfrac{\pi}{2
}\Bigr)=\tfrac{a+c}{2}\equiv\tfrac{\pi}{2}\pmod{\pi}.$$
Since $0 < a+c < 2\pi$, this gives
$$a + c = \pi.$$

**Reducing the objective.** With $c = \pi - a$, we have $\sin c = \sin a$, so $\
sin a + \sin c = 2\sin a$. Also $b+d+e = 2\pi - (a+c) = \pi$. Thus
$$\mathcal{A} = \tfrac12\bigl(2\sin a + \sin b + \sin d + \sin e\bigr),\qquad a\
in(0,\pi),\;\; b,d,e>0,\;\; b+d+e=\pi.$$

The variables split into two independent groups.

**Group 1: maximize $2\sin a$ on $(0,\pi)$.** Maximum at $a = \pi/2$, value $2$.

**Group 2: maximize $\sin b+\sin d+\sin e$ subject to $b+d+e=\pi$, all positive.
** Since $\sin$ is strictly concave on $(0,\pi)$, Jensen's inequality gives the
maximum at $b=d=e=\pi/3$:
$$\sin\tfrac{\pi}{3}+\sin\tfrac{\pi}{3}+\sin\tfrac{\pi}{3} = \tfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2
}.$$

**Combining.** Both maxima are simultaneously attainable: $a=c=\pi/2$, $b=d=e=\p
i/3$, and the sum is $\pi/2+\pi/3+\pi/2+\pi/3+\pi/3 = 2\pi$. ✓

Since the objective is a sum of concave functions (hence concave) over a convex
domain, this interior critical point is the **global maximum**:
$$\mathcal{A}_{\max} = \tfrac12\Bigl(2 + \tfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}\Bigr) = \frac{4+3\
sqrt{3}}{4}.$$

**Sanity check.** A regular pentagon (no constraint) has area $\frac{5}{2}\sin\f
rac{2\pi}{5}\approx 2.378$. Our value $\frac{4+3\sqrt{3}}{4}\approx 2.299$ is sm
aller, as expected under an extra constraint.
