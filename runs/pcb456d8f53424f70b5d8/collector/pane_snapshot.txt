# Evaluation of $\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{ix}}{x}\,dx$

The integrand $\frac{e^{ix}}{x}$ has a singularity at $x=0$, so the integral is
understood as a **Cauchy principal value**:

$$I = \text{PV}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{ix}}{x}\,dx = \lim_{\substack{R\
to\infty\\ \varepsilon\to 0^+}} \left(\int_{-R}^{-\varepsilon} \frac{e^{ix}}{x}\
,dx + \int_{\varepsilon}^{R} \frac{e^{ix}}{x}\,dx\right).$$

## Contour setup

Consider $f(z) = \dfrac{e^{iz}}{z}$ and the closed contour $\Gamma$ in the upper
 half-plane consisting of:
- the segment $[-R, -\varepsilon]$ on the real axis,
- the small semicircle $C_\varepsilon: z = \varepsilon e^{i\theta}$, $\theta\in[
\pi,0]$ (traversed from $-\varepsilon$ to $+\varepsilon$ above the pole),
- the segment $[\varepsilon, R]$ on the real axis,
- the large semicircle $C_R: z = Re^{i\theta}$, $\theta\in[0,\pi]$.

The pole at $z=0$ lies **outside** $\Gamma$, so $f$ is holomorphic on and inside
 $\Gamma$. By Cauchy's theorem:

$$\int_{-R}^{-\varepsilon} \frac{e^{ix}}{x}\,dx + \int_{C_\varepsilon} \frac{e^{
iz}}{z}\,dz + \int_{\varepsilon}^{R} \frac{e^{ix}}{x}\,dx + \int_{C_R} \frac{e^{
iz}}{z}\,dz = 0. \tag{1}$$

## The large arc $C_R$ vanishes (Jordan's lemma)

On $C_R$, $z = Re^{i\theta}$, $dz = iRe^{i\theta}\,d\theta$, so

$$\int_{C_R} \frac{e^{iz}}{z}\,dz = i\int_0^{\pi} e^{iR\cos\theta - R\sin\theta}
\,d\theta.$$

Since $|e^{iR\cos\theta}|=1$ and $\sin\theta \geq \frac{2\theta}{\pi}$ for $\the
ta\in[0,\pi/2]$ (Jordan's inequality),

$$\left|\int_{C_R} \frac{e^{iz}}{z}\,dz\right| \leq \int_0^{\pi} e^{-R\sin\theta
}\,d\theta = 2\int_0^{\pi/2} e^{-R\sin\theta}\,d\theta \leq 2\int_0^{\pi/2} e^{-
2R\theta/\pi}\,d\theta = \frac{\pi}{R}\bigl(1-e^{-R}\bigr) \xrightarrow{R\to\inf
ty} 0. \tag{2}$$

## The small arc $C_\varepsilon$ contributes $-i\pi$

On $C_\varepsilon$, $z = \varepsilon e^{i\theta}$, $\theta\in[\pi,0]$:

$$\int_{C_\varepsilon} \frac{e^{iz}}{z}\,dz = i\int_{\pi}^{0} e^{i\varepsilon e^
{i\theta}}\,d\theta.$$

As $\varepsilon\to 0^+$, $e^{i\varepsilon e^{i\theta}}\to 1$ uniformly, hence

$$\int_{C_\varepsilon} \frac{e^{iz}}{z}\,dz \;\longrightarrow\; i\int_{\pi}^{0}
1\,d\theta = i(0-\pi) = -i\pi. \tag{3}$$

## Taking the limits

Letting $R\to\infty$ and $\varepsilon\to 0^+$ in (1) and using (2) and (3):

$$\text{PV}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{ix}}{x}\,dx \;-\; i\pi \;+\; 0 \;=\;
 0,$$

so

$$\boxed{\text{PV}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{ix}}{x}\,dx = i\pi.}$$

## Consistency check

Writing $e^{ix} = \cos x + i\sin x$:

$$I = \underbrace{\text{PV}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\cos x}{x}\,dx}_{=\,0\t
ext{ (odd integrand)}} \;+\; i\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\sin x}{
x}\,dx}_{=\,\pi\text{ (Dirichlet integral)}} = 0 + i\pi = i\pi,$$

confirming the result.
