## Proof

We evaluate
$$L = \lim_{x\to\infty}\left[x\log(1-e^x)+\operatorname{Li}_2(e^x)-\tfrac{1}{2}x
^2\right].$$

**Step 1: Analytic continuation identities (principal branch).**

For $z>1$, the standard inversion formula for the dilogarithm gives
$$\operatorname{Li}_2(z)+\operatorname{Li}_2(1/z)=-\frac{\pi^2}{6}-\frac{1}{2}\l
og^2(-z).$$

Since $z>0$, $\log(-z)=\log z+i\pi$, so $\log^2(-z)=\log^2 z+2i\pi\log z-\pi^2$.
 Substituting:

$$\operatorname{Li}_2(z)+\operatorname{Li}_2(1/z)=\frac{\pi^2}{3}-\frac{1}{2}\lo
g^2 z-i\pi\log z. \tag{1}$$

Also, for $z>1$, $1-z<0$, so (principal branch):
$$\log(1-z)=\log(z-1)+i\pi. \tag{2}$$

**Step 2: Simplify the expression.**

Set $z=e^x$ (with $x>0$, so $z>1$ and $\log z = x$). Using (1):

$$\operatorname{Li}_2(e^x)=\frac{\pi^2}{3}-\frac{1}{2}x^2-i\pi x-\operatorname{L
i}_2(e^{-x}).$$

Using (2):

$$x\log(1-e^x)=x\log(e^x-1)+i\pi x.$$

Adding these and subtracting $\frac{1}{2}x^2$:

$$E(x):=x\log(1-e^x)+\operatorname{Li}_2(e^x)-\tfrac{1}{2}x^2$$
$$=x\log(e^x-1)+i\pi x+\frac{\pi^2}{3}-\frac{1}{2}x^2-i\pi x-\operatorname{Li}_2
(e^{-x})-\frac{1}{2}x^2$$

The imaginary parts $i\pi x$ cancel, confirming $E(x)$ is real:

$$E(x)=x\log(e^x-1)-x^2+\frac{\pi^2}{3}-\operatorname{Li}_2(e^{-x}). \tag{3}$$

**Step 3: Reduce to a vanishing remainder.**

Factor $e^x$ out of the logarithm:

$$x\log(e^x-1)=x\log\!\big(e^x(1-e^{-x})\big)=x^2+x\log(1-e^{-x}).$$

Substituting into (3):

$$E(x)=x\log(1-e^{-x})-\operatorname{Li}_2(e^{-x})+\frac{\pi^2}{3}. \tag{4}$$

**Step 4: Take the limit.**

As $x\to\infty$, set $t=e^{-x}\to 0^+$. Using the series $\operatorname{Li}_2(t)
=t+O(t^2)$ and $\log(1-t)=-t+O(t^2)$:

- $\log(1-e^{-x})=-e^{-x}+O(e^{-2x})\to 0$,
- $x\log(1-e^{-x})=-xe^{-x}+O(xe^{-2x})\to 0$,
- $\operatorname{Li}_2(e^{-x})=e^{-x}+O(e^{-2x})\to 0$.

Therefore the first two terms in (4) vanish, and

$$L=\lim_{x\to\infty}E(x)=0-0+\frac{\pi^2}{3}=\boxed{\dfrac{\pi^2}{3}}.$$
