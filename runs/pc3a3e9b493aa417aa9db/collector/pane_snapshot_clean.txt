**Setup.** The parabola has coefficient $1$ at the quadratic term, so its equati
on is
$$y = x^2 + px + q$$
for some constants $p, q$. Completing the square,
$$y = \left(x + \tfrac{p}{2}\right)^2 + \left(q - \tfrac{p^2}{4}\right),$$
which is a translate of the standard parabola $y = x^2$. A rigid translation pre
serves all distances and areas, so the area of triangle $ABC$ is the same as if
the points lay on $y = x^2$. Hence, **without loss of generality, take the parab
ola to be $y = x^2$.**

**Choosing coordinates.** Let the $x$-coordinate of $B$ be $t$. Since the differ
ence of the $x$-coordinates of $A$ and $B$ is $d$, and that of $B$ and $C$ is al
so $d$, we may write
$$x_A = t + d, \qquad x_B = t, \qquad x_C = t - d.$$
(The sign convention does not matter; reversing the roles of $A$ and $C$ reflect
s the triangle, leaving the area unchanged.)

Thus the three points are
$$A = (t+d,\; (t+d)^2), \qquad B = (t,\; t^2), \qquad C = (t-d,\; (t-d)^2).$$

**Computing the area.** Form the vectors from $B$:
$$\vec{BA} = A - B = \bigl(d,\; (t+d)^2 - t^2\bigr) = \bigl(d,\; 2td + d^2\bigr)
,$$
$$\vec{BC} = C - B = \bigl(-d,\; (t-d)^2 - t^2\bigr) = \bigl(-d,\; -2td + d^2\bi
gr).$$

The (signed) area of the triangle is half the absolute value of the determinant
of these two vectors:
$$\det(\vec{BA}, \vec{BC}) = d\cdot(-2td + d^2) - (2td + d^2)\cdot(-d).$$

Expanding:
$$= -2td^2 + d^3 + 2td^2 + d^3 = 2d^3.$$

Therefore
$$\text{Area}(\triangle ABC) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,|2d^3| = d^3.$$

**Verification of independence.** The parameter $t$ (the location of $B$ along t
he parabola) and the linear/quadratic coefficients $p, q$ of the original parabo
la have all cancelled: the determinant equals $2d^3$ regardless of $t$, and the
translation argument removed dependence on $p, q$. The area depends only on $d$.

**Conclusion.** The area of triangle $ABC$ is $\boxed{d^3}$.
