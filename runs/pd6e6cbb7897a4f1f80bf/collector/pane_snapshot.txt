## Solution

Let $\beta = \alpha^2 + 3$, so $\alpha^2 = \beta - 3$. The goal is to find $[\ma
thbb{Q}(\alpha) : \mathbb{Q}(\beta)]$.

**Step 1: Find the minimal polynomial of $\beta$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.**

Since $P(\alpha) = \alpha^4 + 9\alpha^2 + 15 = 0$, substituting $\alpha^2 = \bet
a - 3$:

$$(\beta - 3)^2 + 9(\beta - 3) + 15 = 0$$
$$\beta^2 - 6\beta + 9 + 9\beta - 27 + 15 = 0$$
$$\beta^2 + 3\beta - 3 = 0$$

So $\beta$ satisfies $Q(y) = y^2 + 3y - 3$. The discriminant is $9 + 12 = 21$, w
hich is not a perfect square, so $Q$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$. Thus:

$$[\mathbb{Q}(\beta) : \mathbb{Q}] = 2$$

**Step 2: Show $P(x) = x^4 + 9x^2 + 15$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$.**

Apply **Eisenstein's criterion** with $p = 3$:
- The leading coefficient is $1$, not divisible by $3$. ✓
- The non-leading coefficients are $0, 9, 0, 15$ — all divisible by $3$. ✓
- The constant term $15$ is not divisible by $9 = 3^2$. ✓

So $P$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, giving:

$$[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) : \mathbb{Q}] = 4$$

**Step 3: Apply the tower law.**

Since $\beta = \alpha^2 + 3 \in \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$, we have the tower $\mathbb{
Q} \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\beta) \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$. By the tower law:

$$[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) : \mathbb{Q}] = [\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) : \mathbb{Q}(\beta)] \
cdot [\mathbb{Q}(\beta) : \mathbb{Q}]$$
$$4 = [\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) : \mathbb{Q}(\beta)] \cdot 2$$

Therefore:

$$[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) : \mathbb{Q}(\beta)] = 2$$

**Verification that the degree is exactly 2 (not 1):** If $\alpha \in \mathbb{Q}
(\beta)$, then $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) = \mathbb{Q}(\beta)$, which would force $[\ma
thbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}] = 2$, contradicting $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}]
 = 4$. Hence $\alpha \notin \mathbb{Q}(\beta)$ and the degree is indeed $2$.

$$\boxed{[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}(\alpha^2+3)] = 2}$$
