We claim the polynomials are exactly

$$f(x)=\pm x^{k},\qquad k=0,1,2,\dots$$

(where $x^{0}=1$, giving the constant polynomials $\pm 1$).

---

## Verification that $\pm x^{k}$ work

For distinct primes $p,q$ and $f(x)=\pm x^{k}$, we have $f(p)=\pm p^{k}$ and $f(
q)=\pm q^{k}$. Since $p\neq q$ are distinct primes, $\gcd(p^{k},q^{k})=1$, hence
 $\gcd(f(p),f(q))=1$. ✓

---

## Proof that these are the only solutions

Let $f\in\mathbb Z[x]$ satisfy the condition.

**Step 0 — Reduction to $f(p)\neq 0$ for all primes $p$ (unless $f$ is constant
$\pm1$).**

If $f$ is constant, say $f\equiv c$, then $\gcd(f(p),f(q))=\gcd(c,c)=|c|=1$, so
$c=\pm1$, which is $\pm x^{0}$. Henceforth assume $f$ is non-constant.

Suppose $f(p_{0})=0$ for some prime $p_{0}$. Then for every other prime $q$,
$$\gcd(f(p_{0}),f(q))=\gcd(0,f(q))=|f(q)|=1,$$
so $f(q)=\pm1$ for all primes $q\neq p_{0}$. There are infinitely many such $q$,
 so the non-constant polynomial $f(x)^{2}-1$ has infinitely many roots — impossi
ble. Thus **$f(p)\neq0$ for every prime $p$**.

**Step 1 — A root constraint modulo every prime.**

The condition $\gcd(f(p),f(q))=1$ for all distinct primes $p,q$ is equivalent to
:

> $(\star)$ For every prime $\ell$, **at most one** prime $p$ satisfies $\ell\mi
d f(p)$.

Suppose for some prime $\ell$ the polynomial $f$ has a root $r\bmod \ell$ with $
r\not\equiv0\pmod\ell$. By **Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progres
sions**, there are infinitely many primes $p\equiv r\pmod\ell$, and for each, $f
(p)\equiv f(r)\equiv0\pmod\ell$. This gives at least two distinct primes $p$ wit
h $\ell\mid f(p)$, contradicting $(\star)$.

Therefore:

> $(\dagger)$ For every prime $\ell$, the only root of $f\bmod\ell$ is $r\equiv0
$ (if it is a root at all).

**Step 2 — Factoring out the power of $x$.**

Write $f(x)=x^{m}\,g(x)$ with $m\ge0$ and $g(0)\neq0$. For $r\not\equiv0\pmod\el
l$, $f(r)\equiv0\pmod\ell\iff g(r)\equiv0\pmod\ell$ (since $r^{m}\not\equiv0$).
By $(\dagger)$, $f$ has no nonzero root mod $\ell$, so:

> $(\ddagger)$ **$g$ has no nonzero root modulo any prime $\ell$**, and $g(0)\ne
q0$.

**Step 3 — Prime factors of $g(n)$ divide $n$.**

Let $n\ge1$ be an integer with $g(n)\neq0$, and let $\ell$ be a prime divisor of
 $g(n)$. Then $g(n)\equiv0\pmod\ell$, so $n\bmod\ell$ is a root of $g\bmod\ell$.
 By $(\ddagger)$, this root must be $0$, i.e., $\ell\mid n$. Hence

> **Every prime factor of $g(n)$ divides $n$** (for all $n\ge1$ with $g(n)\neq0$
).

**Step 4 — Evaluating at primes.**

Take $n=\ell$ a prime. Every prime factor of $g(\ell)$ divides $\ell$, so the on
ly possible prime factor is $\ell$ itself. Also $g(\ell)\neq0$ (since $f(\ell)=\
ell^{m}g(\ell)\neq0$ by Step 0). Therefore

$$g(\ell)=\pm\,\ell^{a(\ell)}\quad\text{for some integer }a(\ell)\ge0,$$

for every prime $\ell$.

**Step 5 — Growth forces $g(x)=\pm x^{d}$, then $g(0)\neq0$ forces $d=0$.**

Let $d=\deg g\ge1$ (assuming for contradiction that $g$ is non-constant) and let
 $c\neq0$ be the leading coefficient, so $g(x)\sim c\,x^{d}$ as $x\to\infty$. Th
en

$$\ell^{a(\ell)}=|g(\ell)|\sim|c|\,\ell^{d}\qquad(\ell\to\infty\text{ over prime
s}).$$

Since $a(\ell)$ is a non-negative integer and $\ell^{a(\ell)-d}\to|c|>0$ is a fi
nite positive constant, we must have $a(\ell)=d$ for all sufficiently large prim
es $\ell$, and $|c|=1$. Consequently $g(\ell)=\pm\ell^{d}$ for all sufficiently
large primes $\ell$.

Choose the sign $s\in\{+1,-1\}$ that occurs infinitely often (pigeonhole). Then
$g(\ell)-s\,\ell^{d}=0$ for infinitely many primes $\ell$. The polynomial $g(x)-
s\,x^{d}$ has infinitely many roots, so it is identically zero:

$$g(x)=s\,x^{d}.$$

But $d\ge1$ gives $g(0)=0$, **contradicting** $g(0)\neq0$ from Step 2.

Therefore $g$ is **constant**: $g\equiv c$. Since $g(0)\neq0$, $c\neq0$. If $|c|
>1$, take a prime $\ell\mid c$; then $g(a)=c\equiv0\pmod\ell$ for *every* $a$, i
n particular every nonzero $a\bmod\ell$ is a root of $g\bmod\ell$, contradicting
 $(\ddagger)$. Hence $|c|=1$, i.e., $g\equiv\pm1$.

**Step 6 — Conclusion.**

We have $f(x)=x^{m}\,g(x)=\pm x^{m}$ with $m\ge0$. Including the constant case (
$m=0$), the complete list of solutions is

$$\boxed{\,f(x)=\pm x^{k},\quad k=0,1,2,\dots\,}.$$
