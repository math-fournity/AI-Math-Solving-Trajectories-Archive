# Colimit in the Comma Category $(L, R)$

## Setup and Notation

Let $L: \mathbf{A} \to \mathbf{C}$ and $R: \mathbf{B} \to \mathbf{C}$ be functor
s, with $L$ cocontinuous and $\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}$ cocomplete. Let $\Delta: \
mathbf{J} \to (L, R)$ be a diagram in the comma category.

Recall that an object of $(L,R)$ is a triple $(A, B, f)$ with $A \in \mathbf{A}$
, $B \in \mathbf{B}$, and $f: L(A) \to R(B)$ in $\mathbf{C}$. A morphism $(\alph
a, \beta): (A,B,f) \to (A',B',f')$ requires $R(\beta) \circ f = f' \circ L(\alph
a)$.

There are projection functors $P: (L,R) \to \mathbf{A}$ and $Q: (L,R) \to \mathb
f{B}$ sending $(A,B,f) \mapsto A$ and $(A,B,f) \mapsto B$ respectively.

## Construction

**Step 1: Componentwise colimits.** Since $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are coco
mplete, set:
$$A^* = \operatorname{colim}(P \circ \Delta) \in \mathbf{A}, \qquad B^* = \opera
torname{colim}(Q \circ \Delta) \in \mathbf{B}$$
with colimit cocone legs $\lambda_j: P(\Delta(j)) \to A^*$ and $\mu_j: Q(\Delta(
j)) \to B^*$ for each $j \in \mathbf{J}$.

**Step 2: The structure morphism.** Write $\Delta(j) = (A_j, B_j, f_j)$ where $f
_j: L(A_j) \to R(B_j)$. The compatibility condition for morphisms in $(L,R)$ ens
ures that the $f_j$ assemble into a natural transformation
$$f: L \circ P \circ \Delta \;\Longrightarrow\; R \circ Q \circ \Delta$$
as functors $\mathbf{J} \to \mathbf{C}$.

Since $L$ is cocontinuous, applying $L$ to the colimit cocone gives a colimit co
cone in $\mathbf{C}$:
$$L(\lambda_j): L(A_j) \to L(A^*) \quad \text{is } \operatorname{colim}(L \circ
P \circ \Delta).$$

Applying $R$ to the colimit cocone for $Q \circ \Delta$ gives a cocone (not nece
ssarily colimit) in $\mathbf{C}$:
$$R(\mu_j): R(B_j) \to R(B^*).$$

Composing, the family $R(\mu_j) \circ f_j: L(A_j) \to R(B^*)$ forms a **cocone**
 over $L \circ P \circ \Delta$ with vertex $R(B^*)$. (Verification: for $u: i \t
o j$ in $\mathbf{J}$, naturality of $f$ gives $f_j \circ L(P(\Delta(u))) = R(Q(\
Delta(u))) \circ f_i$, and the cocone property of $\mu$ gives $R(\mu_j) \circ R(
Q(\Delta(u))) = R(\mu_i)$; combining yields $R(\mu_j) \circ f_j \circ L(P(\Delta
(u))) = R(\mu_i) \circ f_i$.)

By the universal property of the colimit $L(A^*) = \operatorname{colim}(L \circ
P \circ \Delta)$, there exists a **unique** morphism
$$f^*: L(A^*) \to R(B^*)$$
such that for all $j \in \mathbf{J}$:
$$f^* \circ L(\lambda_j) = R(\mu_j) \circ f_j. \tag{$\star$}$$

**The colimit of $\Delta$ in $(L,R)$ is the object $(A^*, B^*, f^*)$.**

## Verification of the Universal Property

**Cocone.** The legs $(\lambda_j, \mu_j): \Delta(j) \to (A^*, B^*, f^*)$ are mor
phisms in $(L,R)$ precisely because equation $(\star)$ is exactly the commutativ
ity condition $f^* \circ L(\lambda_j) = R(\mu_j) \circ f_j$.

**Universality.** Let $(A, B, f)$ be any cocone over $\Delta$ with legs $(\alpha
_j, \beta_j): \Delta(j) \to (A, B, f)$, so $f \circ L(\alpha_j) = R(\beta_j) \ci
rc f_j$ for all $j$. Then:
- $\{\alpha_j\}$ is a cocone over $P \circ \Delta$, inducing a unique $\bar\alph
a: A^* \to A$ with $\bar\alpha \circ \lambda_j = \alpha_j$.
- $\{\beta_j\}$ is a cocone over $Q \circ \Delta$, inducing a unique $\bar\beta:
 B^* \to B$ with $\bar\beta \circ \mu_j = \beta_j$.

We must show $(\bar\alpha, \bar\beta)$ is a morphism in $(L,R)$, i.e., $f \circ
L(\bar\alpha) = R(\bar\beta) \circ f^*$. For each $j$:
$$f \circ L(\bar\alpha) \circ L(\lambda_j) = f \circ L(\alpha_j) = R(\beta_j) \c
irc f_j = R(\bar\beta) \circ R(\mu_j) \circ f_j = R(\bar\beta) \circ f^* \circ L
(\lambda_j).$$

The four equalities use: (1) $\bar\alpha \circ \lambda_j = \alpha_j$, (2) the co
cone condition for $(A,B,f)$, (3) $\bar\beta \circ \mu_j = \beta_j$, (4) equatio
n $(\star)$.

Since $\{L(\lambda_j)\}$ is a colimit cocone (hence jointly epimorphic), we conc
lude $f \circ L(\bar\alpha) = R(\bar\beta) \circ f^*$, so $(\bar\alpha, \bar\bet
a)$ is a morphism in $(L,R)$. Uniqueness of $\bar\alpha$ and $\bar\beta$ guarant
ees uniqueness of $(\bar\alpha, \bar\beta)$.

## Summary

The colimit of $\Delta$ in $(L, R)$ is computed **componentwise**:
$$\operatorname{colim} \Delta = \bigl(\operatorname{colim}(P\circ\Delta),\; \ope
ratorname{colim}(Q\circ\Delta),\; f^*\bigr)$$
where $f^*: L(\operatorname{colim}(P\circ\Delta)) \to R(\operatorname{colim}(Q\c
irc\Delta))$ is the unique morphism induced by the universal property of the col
imit $\operatorname{colim}(L \circ P \circ \Delta) \cong L(\operatorname{colim}(
P\circ\Delta))$ (the isomorphism being a consequence of $L$'s cocontinuity), app
lied to the cocone $\{R(\mu_j) \circ f_j\}$.

The essential point is that **cocontinuity of $L$** is what makes this work: it
ensures $L$ maps the colimit of the $\mathbf{A}$-components to the colimit of th
e $L \circ P \circ \Delta$ diagram in $\mathbf{C}$, providing the universal prop
erty needed to uniquely determine the structure morphism $f^*$. No cocontinuity
assumption on $R$ is required—$R$ only needs to be a functor so that $R(\operato
rname{colim}(Q\circ\Delta))$ is a well-defined object of $\mathbf{C}$ serving as
 the vertex of the cocone $\{R(\mu_j) \circ f_j\}$.
