Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every left ideal in a finit
e-dimensional C\*-algebra is of the form $Ap$ for some projection $p$.

**Answer: Yes.** Every left ideal $I$ in a finite-dimensional C\*-algebra $A$ sa
tisfies $I = Ap$ for some projection $p \in A$.

---

**Proof.**

Let $I$ be a left ideal in $A$. Consider the set $I \cap I^*$, where $I^* = \{a^
* : a \in I\}$.

**Step 1: $I \cap I^*$ is a C\*-subalgebra of $A$.**

Since $I$ is a left ideal ($AI \subseteq I$), taking adjoints gives $I^*A \subse
teq I^*$, so $I^*$ is a right ideal. Now $I \cap I^*$ is:
- A subspace (intersection of subspaces).
- Closed under multiplication: if $a, b \in I \cap I^*$, then $ab \in I$ (since
$a \in I$ and $I$ is a left ideal) and $ab \in I^*$ (since $a \in I^*$ and $I^*$
 is a right ideal), so $ab \in I \cap I^*$.
- Closed under involution: if $a \in I \cap I^*$, then $a^* \in I^* \cap I = I \
cap I^*$.

Since $A$ is finite-dimensional, $I \cap I^*$ is closed in norm, hence a C\*-sub
algebra.

**Step 2: $I \cap I^*$ has a unit $p$, which is a projection.**

Every finite-dimensional C\*-algebra is unital (it is isomorphic to a direct sum
 of matrix algebras). Let $p$ be the unit of $I \cap I^*$. Then $p = p^* = p^2$,
 so $p$ is a projection in $A$, and $p \in I \cap I^* \subseteq I$.

**Step 3: $Ap \subseteq I$.**

Since $p \in I$ and $I$ is a left ideal, $Ap \subseteq AI \subseteq I$.

**Step 4: $I \subseteq Ap$.**

Let $x \in I$. Then $x^* \in I^*$, and $x^*x \in I^*I$. We claim $I^*I \subseteq
 I \cap I^*$:
- $I^*I \subseteq I$: for $a \in I^* \subseteq A$ and $b \in I$, $ab \in I$ sinc
e $I$ is a left ideal.
- $I^*I \subseteq I^*$: for $a \in I^*$ and $b \in I \subseteq A$, $ab \in I^*$
since $I^*$ is a right ideal.

So $x^*x \in I \cap I^*$. Since $p$ is the unit of $I \cap I^*$, we have $x^*x \
cdot p = x^*x$, hence:
$$x^*x(1-p) = 0.$$

Multiplying on the left by $(1-p)$:
$$(1-p)x^*x(1-p) = 0.$$

But $(1-p)x^*x(1-p) = (x(1-p))^*(x(1-p))$, so by the C\*-identity:
$$\|x(1-p)\|^2 = \|(x(1-p))^*(x(1-p))\| = 0.$$

Therefore $x(1-p) = 0$, i.e., $x = xp \in Ap$.

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

From Steps 3 and 4, $I = Ap$ where $p$ is the unit of $I \cap I^*$, a projection
 in $A$. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** The finite-dimensionality is used precisely in Step 2, to guarantee
that $I \cap I^*$ is unital. In general C\*-algebras, closed left ideals corresp
ond to projections in the enveloping von Neumann algebra $A^{**}$; the finite-di
mensional case is special because $A = A^{**}$, so the projection lies in $A$ it
self.
