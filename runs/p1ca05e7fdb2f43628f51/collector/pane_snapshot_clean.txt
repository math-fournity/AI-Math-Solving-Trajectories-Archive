## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, such a compact set $K$ exists.

**Setup.** Let $\mu$ be a finite Borel measure on a complete separable metric sp
ace $X$, equipped with a reference Lebesgue measure $\lambda$. By the Lebesgue d
ecomposition theorem, $\mu$ decomposes uniquely as
$$\mu = \mu_{\mathrm{ac}} + \mu_{\mathrm{s}},$$
where $\mu_{\mathrm{ac}} \ll \lambda$ (absolutely continuous part) and $\mu_{\ma
thrm{s}} \perp \lambda$ (singular part). The hypothesis is that the singular par
t is **positive**, i.e., $\mu_{\mathrm{s}} \neq 0$ (equivalently, $\mu_{\mathrm{
s}}(X) > 0$).

**Step 1: Singularity gives a null set carrying all the mass of $\mu_{\mathrm{s}
}$.**

Since $\mu_{\mathrm{s}} \perp \lambda$, there exists a Borel set $A \subseteq X$
 such that
$$\lambda(A) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \mu_{\mathrm{s}}(X \setminus A) = 0.$$
Equivalently, $\mu_{\mathrm{s}}$ is concentrated on $A$, so $\mu_{\mathrm{s}}(A)
 = \mu_{\mathrm{s}}(X) > 0$.

**Step 2: Inner regularity extracts a compact subset of positive measure.**

Since $X$ is a complete separable metric space, every finite Borel measure on $X
$ is **Radon** (in particular, inner regular). Applying inner regularity to $\mu
_{\mathrm{s}}$ and the Borel set $A$:
$$\mu_{\mathrm{s}}(A) = \sup\bigl\{\mu_{\mathrm{s}}(K) : K \subseteq A,\ K \text
{ compact}\bigr\}.$$
Since $\mu_{\mathrm{s}}(A) > 0$, the supremum is strictly positive, so there exi
sts a compact set $K \subseteq A$ with
$$\mu_{\mathrm{s}}(K) > 0.$$

**Step 3: Verify the two required properties of $K$.**

- *Lebesgue measure of $K$ is zero:* Since $K \subseteq A$ and $\lambda(A) = 0$,
 monotonicity of $\lambda$ gives $\lambda(K) \leq \lambda(A) = 0$, hence $\lambd
a(K) = 0$.

- *$\mu(K) > 0$:* Since $\mu = \mu_{\mathrm{ac}} + \mu_{\mathrm{s}}$ with both p
arts non-negative,
$$\mu(K) = \mu_{\mathrm{ac}}(K) + \mu_{\mathrm{s}}(K) \geq \mu_{\mathrm{s}}(K) >
 0.$$

**Conclusion.** The compact set $K \subseteq A$ satisfies $\lambda(K) = 0$ and $
\mu(K) > 0$. Therefore, **yes**, such a compact set exists. In fact, the existen
ce of such a $K$ is equivalent to the singular part being nontrivial: a positive
 singular part forces the measure to charge some Lebesgue-null set, and inner re
gularity (guaranteed by the complete separable structure) lets us extract a comp
act such set of positive $\mu$-measure.
