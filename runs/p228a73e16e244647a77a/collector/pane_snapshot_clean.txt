**Step 2: Convergence in $\mathcal{S}'$ and termwise Fourier transform.**

The partial sums $\sigma_N = \sum_{k=0}^{N} a^k \cos(2\pi b^k t)$ converge to $\
sigma$ uniformly, and since all are bounded by $\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} a^k = \frac{
1}{1-a}$, by dominated convergence we have $\sigma_N \to \sigma$ in $\mathcal{S}
'(\mathbb{R})$. The Fourier transform $\mathcal{F}: \mathcal{S}' \to \mathcal{S}

**Step 3: Fourier transform of each term.**

Using the convention $\hat{f}(\xi) = \int_{\mathbb{R}} f(t)\, e^{-2\pi i \xi t}\

$$\widehat{\cos(2\pi b^k t)} = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\delta(\xi - b^k) + \delta(\xi
+ b^k)\bigr].$$

**Step 4: The Fourier transform of $\sigma$.**

Summing term by term (justified by convergence in $\mathcal{S}'$):

$$\hat{\sigma}(\xi) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{a^k}{2}\bigl[\delta(\xi - b^k) +

This is a locally finite Borel measure of total mass $\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} a^k =
\frac{1}{1-a} < \infty$, confirming it is a well-defined tempered distribution.
The series of measures converges in total variation norm (hence in $\mathcal{S}'

$$\operatorname{supp}(\hat{\sigma}) = \{\pm b^k : k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots\} = \{\pm
