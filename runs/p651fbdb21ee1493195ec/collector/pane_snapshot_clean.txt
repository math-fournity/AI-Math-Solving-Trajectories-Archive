## Proof

### Step 1: The identity component $G_0$ is divisible

Let $G$ be a compact Lie group and let $G_0$ denote the identity component (the
connected component containing the identity). Then $G_0$ is a **compact connecte
d Lie group**.

A classical result states that for a compact connected Lie group, the exponentia

te $g = \exp(X)$ for some $X \in \mathfrak{g}$. For any positive integer $n$, se

$$h^n = \exp(X/n)^n = \exp(X) = g.$$

Therefore $G_0$ is **divisible**: every element has an $n$-th root for every $n
\geq 1$.

### Step 2: Any homomorphism from $G_0$ to $\mathbb{Z}$ is trivial

The image $f(G_0)$ is a divisible subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}$ (since the homomorphi
c image of a divisible group is divisible). But $\mathbb{Z}$ has **no non-trivia

no integer $k$ with $2k = d$ unless $d$ is even, and even then the process fails

Therefore $f|_{G_0} = 0$, i.e., $G_0 \subseteq \ker(f)$.

### Step 3: The component group $G/G_0$ is finite

For a compact Lie group, the group of connected components $\pi_0(G) = G/G_0$ is
 **finite**. This follows because $G$ is a compact manifold: it has finite volum
e, and connected components are disjoint open sets, so there can be only finitel

### Step 4: $f$ factors through a finite group, hence is trivial

Since $G_0 \subseteq \ker(f)$, the homomorphism $f$ factors through the quotient

$$f: G \longrightarrow G/G_0 \xrightarrow{\;\bar{f}\;} \mathbb{Z}$$

where $G/G_0$ is a **finite group**. But any homomorphism from a finite group to
 $\mathbb{Z}$ is trivial, because $\mathbb{Z}$ is **torsion-free**: if $\bar{f}(
\bar{g}) = m \neq 0$ for some $\bar{g}$ of finite order $k$, then $k \cdot m = \
bar{f}(\bar{g}^k) = \bar{f}(e) = 0$, forcing $m = 0$, a contradiction.

Therefore $\bar{f} = 0$, and hence $f = 0$.

Every group homomorphism $f: G \to \mathbb{Z}$ from a compact Lie group $G$ to $

1. $G_0$ is divisible (surjectivity of exp for compact connected Lie groups) $\R

Since no non-trivial homomorphism exists, the conditional premise "if $f$ is non
