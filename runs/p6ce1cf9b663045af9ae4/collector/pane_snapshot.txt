# Solution

**Answer: No, the equality $[K^H:F] = (G:H)$ need not hold in general.**

## Counterexample

Let $F = \mathbb{Q}$ and $K = \mathbb{Q}\bigl(\sqrt{p} : p \text{ prime}\bigr)$,
 the field obtained by adjoining the square root of every prime. This is an infi
nite Galois extension of $\mathbb{Q}$: it is separable (character $0$) and norma
l (every $x^2 - p$ with a root $\sqrt{p}\in K$ also has $-\sqrt{p}\in K$).

**The Galois group.** Each $\sigma\in G=\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ independentl
y sends $\sqrt{p}\mapsto \pm\sqrt{p}$ for each prime $p$, so
$$G \;\cong\; \prod_{p}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},$$
a profinite group (compact, Hausdorff) carrying the Krull (= product) topology.
As an $\mathbb{F}_2$-vector space, $G$ has dimension $2^{\aleph_0}$.

**Constructing a non-closed index-$2$ subgroup.** View $G$ as an $\mathbb{F}_2$-
vector space and choose a Hamel basis (using the Axiom of Choice). Define a line
ar functional $\varphi:G\to\mathbb{F}_2$ that depends on *infinitely many* basis
 elements — equivalently, $\varphi$ is **not** continuous. (A continuous homomor
phism $G\to\mathbb{F}_2$ must factor through a finite subproduct, i.e., depend o
n only finitely many coordinates; our $\varphi$ does not.) Set
$$H := \ker\varphi \;\leq\; G.$$
Then $H$ is a subgroup of index $(G:H)=2$, but $H$ is **not closed**: if it were
 closed of finite index, it would be open, making $\varphi$ continuous — a contr
adiction.

**Key fact: fixed fields depend only on the closure.** For any subgroup $H\leq G
$ with closure $\overline{H}$,
$$K^H = K^{\overline{H}}.$$
*Proof of the nontrivial inclusion $K^H\subseteq K^{\overline{H}}$:* Take $x\in
K^H$. Since $x$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$, it lies in some finite Galois su
bextension $L/\mathbb{Q}$, $L\subseteq K$. The stabilizer $\mathrm{Stab}_G(x)=\{
\sigma\in G:\sigma(x)=x\}$ is the preimage of a subgroup of the finite group $\m
athrm{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})$ under the continuous restriction map, hence is **open*
* (and therefore closed). Since $H\subseteq\mathrm{Stab}_G(x)$ and the stabilize
r is closed, $\overline{H}\subseteq\mathrm{Stab}_G(x)$, giving $x\in K^{\overlin
e{H}}$. $\square$

**The closure of $H$ is all of $G$.** Since $H\subseteq\overline{H}\subseteq G$
and $(G:H)=2$, the index $(G:\overline{H})\in\{1,2\}$. If $(G:\overline{H})=2$,
then $\overline{H}$ is a closed subgroup of finite index, hence open (in a profi
nite group, closed + finite index $\Rightarrow$ open). But $H\subseteq\overline{
H}$ with both of index $2$ forces $H=\overline{H}$, contradicting $H$ not being
closed. Therefore $(G:\overline{H})=1$, i.e.,
$$\overline{H}=G.$$

**Conclusion.** Combining the two facts:
$$K^H = K^{\overline{H}} = K^G = \mathbb{Q}.$$
Hence
$$[K^H:\mathbb{Q}] = 1 \;\neq\; 2 = (G:H).$$

The equality $[K^H:F]=(G:H)$ **fails**: the left side is $1$ while the right sid
e is $2$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The equality *does* hold when $H$ is **closed** in the Krull topolog
y (this is the content of the fundamental theorem of infinite Galois theory). Th
e failure above is precisely due to $H$ being a finite-index subgroup that is no
t closed — such subgroups exist because $G$, as an abstract group, is an infinit
e-dimensional $\mathbb{F}_2$-vector space admitting discontinuous linear functio
nals.
