## Solution

We evaluate
$$L = \lim_{n\to\infty} \sum_{r=0}^{\infty} \frac{\binom{n}{r}}{n^r(r+4)}.$$

Since $\binom{n}{r}=0$ for $r>n$, the sum is effectively $\sum_{r=0}^{n}$.

**Step 1: Pointwise limit of the summand.**

For fixed $r\ge 0$,
$$\frac{\binom{n}{r}}{n^r} = \frac{n(n-1)\cdots(n-r+1)}{r!\,n^r} = \frac{1}{r!}\
prod_{k=0}^{r-1}\frac{n-k}{n} \;\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\; \frac{1}{r!}.$$

**Step 2: Interchange limit and sum (Dominated Convergence).**

For all $n$ and $r\le n$,
$$0 \le \frac{\binom{n}{r}}{n^r} = \frac{1}{r!}\prod_{k=0}^{r-1}\frac{n-k}{n} \l
e \frac{1}{r!},$$
so
$$0 \le \frac{\binom{n}{r}}{n^r(r+4)} \le \frac{1}{r!(r+4)}.$$

The dominating series $\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r!(r+4)}$ converges (since $\
frac{1}{r!(r+4)}\le \frac{1}{r!}$ and $\sum \frac{1}{r!}=e<\infty$). By the domi
nated convergence theorem for series,

$$L = \sum_{r=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{r!(r+4)}.$$

**Step 3: Convert to an integral.**

Using $\displaystyle\frac{1}{r+4}=\int_0^1 x^{r+3}\,dx$,

$$L = \sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r!}\int_0^1 x^{r+3}\,dx = \int_0^1 x^3\sum_{r=
0}^{\infty}\frac{x^r}{r!}\,dx = \int_0^1 x^3 e^x\,dx,$$

where the interchange of sum and integral is justified by absolute convergence (
all terms nonneg, Tonelli/monotone convergence).

**Step 4: Evaluate the integral.**

Repeated integration by parts gives
$$\int x^3 e^x\,dx = e^x(x^3-3x^2+6x-6)+C.$$

Therefore
$$\int_0^1 x^3 e^x\,dx = \Big[e^x(x^3-3x^2+6x-6)\Big]_0^1 = e(1-3+6-6) - 1\cdot(
0-0+0-6) = -2e+6.$$

**Result:**

$$\boxed{L = 6 - 2e}$$
