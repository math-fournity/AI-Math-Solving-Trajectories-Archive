## Proof

We claim that the inclusion map $i: (C^{1}_{2\pi}, \|\cdot\|_1) \to (C_{2\pi}, \
|\cdot\|_0)$ is **compact**.

Recall that a linear operator $T: X \to Y$ between Banach spaces is compact if a
nd only if the image $T(B)$ of the closed unit ball $B$ of $X$ is precompact in
$Y$ (equivalently, every bounded sequence in $X$ has a subsequence whose image c
onverges in $Y$).

We verify compactness of $i$ by showing that every $\|\cdot\|_1$-bounded subset
of $C^{1}_{2\pi}$ is precompact in $(C_{2\pi}, \|\cdot\|_0)$, using the **Arzelà
–Ascoli theorem**.

---

### Step 1. Identify the relevant norms and the unit ball.

The norm on $C^{1}_{2\pi}$ is
$$\|u\|_1 = \|u\|_0 + \|u'\|_0, \qquad \|u\|_0 = \max_{s\in[0,2\pi]}|u(s)|.$$

Let $B = \{u \in C^{1}_{2\pi} : \|u\|_1 \le 1\}$ be the closed unit ball. For ev
ery $u \in B$,
$$\|u\|_0 \le 1 \quad\text{and}\quad \|u'\|_0 \le 1.$$

### Step 2. Uniform boundedness.

For all $u \in B$ and all $s \in [0, 2\pi]$,
$$|u(s)| \le \|u\|_0 \le 1.$$
Hence the family $i(B) = B$ (viewed in $C_{2\pi}$) is **uniformly bounded** by t
he constant $1$.

### Step 3. Equicontinuity.

For $u \in B$ and $s, t \in [0, 2\pi]$, by the fundamental theorem of calculus,
$$|u(s) - u(t)| = \left|\int_t^s u'(\tau)\,d\tau\right| \le \|u'\|_0\,|s - t| \l
e |s - t|.$$

Thus the family $B$ is **equicontinuous**; in fact it is uniformly Lipschitz wit
h constant $1$. Given $\varepsilon > 0$, choose $\delta = \varepsilon$; then $|s
 - t| < \delta$ implies $|u(s) - u(t)| < \varepsilon$ for every $u \in B$, indep
endently of $u$.

### Step 4. Apply Arzelà–Ascoli.

The space $C_{2\pi}$ is the space of continuous $\mathbb{R}^n$-valued functions
on the compact interval $[0, 2\pi]$ with $u(0) = u(2\pi)$, equipped with the sup
-norm $\|\cdot\|_0$. This is a closed subspace of $C([0,2\pi], \mathbb{R}^n)$, h
ence complete.

The family $i(B) \subset C_{2\pi}$ is uniformly bounded (Step 2) and equicontinu
ous (Step 3). By the **Arzelà–Ascoli theorem**, $i(B)$ is precompact in $C([0,2\
pi], \mathbb{R}^n)$ with the sup-norm. Since $C_{2\pi}$ is a closed subspace of
$C([0,2\pi], \mathbb{R}^n)$ and $i(B) \subset C_{2\pi}$, the closure of $i(B)$ i
n $C_{2\pi}$ coincides with its closure in $C([0,2\pi], \mathbb{R}^n)$. Therefor
e $i(B)$ is precompact in $(C_{2\pi}, \|\cdot\|_0)$.

### Step 5. Conclusion.

Since the image of the closed unit ball $B \subset C^{1}_{2\pi}$ under $i$ is pr
ecompact in $C_{2\pi}$, the inclusion map
$$i: (C^{1}_{2\pi}, \|\cdot\|_1) \longrightarrow (C_{2\pi}, \|\cdot\|_0)$$
is a **compact operator**. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** The key mechanism is that the stronger norm $\|\cdot\|_1$ controls t
he derivative, which yields a uniform Lipschitz bound and hence equicontinuity.
This is the prototypical example of a **Sobolev-type compact embedding**: contro
l of one additional derivative on a compact domain forces compactness of the emb
edding into the space of continuous functions.
