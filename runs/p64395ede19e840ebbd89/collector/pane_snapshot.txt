Proof

Claim: Yes, there exists a surface $S$ of degree $d \geq 4$ in $\mathbb{P}
^3_{\mathbb{C}}$ containing no lines. In fact, a general surface of degree $d
\geq 4$ contains no lines.

Setup. Let $N = \binom{d+3}{3} - 1$, so that the space of degree-$d$ surfaces in
$\mathbb{P}^3$ is the projective space $\mathbb{P}^N = \mathbb{P}(H^0(\mathbb{P}
^3, \mathcal{O}(d)))$. The Grassmannian $\mathrm{Gr}(1,3)$ of lines in $
\mathbb{P}^3$ has dimension $4$.

Consider the incidence correspondence $$\Sigma = {(S, L) \in \mathbb{P}^N \times
\mathrm{Gr}(1,3) : L \subset S},$$ with projections $\pi_1: \Sigma \to
\mathbb{P}^N$ and $\pi_2: \Sigma \to \mathrm{Gr}(1,3)$.

Fiber dimension over a fixed line. Fix a line $L \cong \mathbb{P}^1 \subset
\mathbb{P}^3$. A surface $S = V(F)$ of degree $d$ contains $L$ if and only if
$F|_L \equiv 0$. The restriction map $$\rho: H^0(\mathbb{P}^3, \mathcal{O}(d))
\longrightarrow H^0(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(d))$$ is surjective (every degree-
$d$ binary form extends to a degree-$d$ form on $\mathbb{P}^3$, e.g. by choosing
coordinates so $L = V(x_2, x_3)$ and extending a form $g(x_0, x_1)$ to $F =
g(x_0, x_1)$). Since $h^0(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(d)) = d+1$, the kernel of $
\rho$ has codimension exactly $d+1$ in $H^0(\mathbb{P}^3, \mathcal{O}(d))$.

Therefore the fiber $\pi_2^{-1}(L) \cong \mathbb{P}^{N - (d+1)}$ is a projective
space of dimension $N - d - 1$. This holds for every $L \in \mathrm{Gr}(1,3)$,
so $\Sigma$ is irreducible (being a projective bundle over the irreducible
Grassmannian) of dimension $$\dim \Sigma = \dim \mathrm{Gr}(1,3) + (N - d - 1) =
4 + N - d - 1 = N + 3 - d.$$

Conclusion. The image $\pi_1(\Sigma) \subset \mathbb{P}^N$ is the locus of
degree-$d$ surfaces containing at least one line. By the above, $$\dim
\pi_1(\Sigma) \leq \dim \Sigma = N + 3 - d.$$ For $d \geq 4$, we have $3 - d
\leq -1 < 0$, so $\dim \pi_1(\Sigma) < N = \dim \mathbb{P}^N$.

Hence $\pi_1(\Sigma)$ is a proper closed subset of $\mathbb{P}^N$. Its
complement is a nonempty (indeed dense) Zariski-open set: surfaces of degree $d
\geq 4$ containing no line exist in abundance, and a general surface of degree
$d \geq 4$ contains no lines.

Explicitly, one may even write down examples: a smooth surface of degree $d \geq
4$ in general position works, since the locus of surfaces containing a line has
positive codimension and in particular does not cover the smooth locus.

Therefore, there exists a surface of degree $d \geq 4$ in $\mathbb{P}
^3_{\mathbb{C}}$ containing no lines. $\blacksquare$
