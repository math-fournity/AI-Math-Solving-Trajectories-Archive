## Proof

**Problem.** Place the maximum number of boosters in a 10×10 grid so that no fou
r boosters form the corners of an axis-parallel rectangle.

---

### Upper Bound: N ≤ 34

Let $N$ be the total number of boosters, and let $r_i$ be the number of boosters
 in row $i$ ($i = 1, \ldots, 10$).

**Key constraint.** If two rows both have boosters in the same two columns, thos
e four boosters form a forbidden rectangle. Equivalently, every pair of columns
$\{a, b\}$ can appear together in **at most one** row. Since there are $\binom{1
0}{2} = 45$ column-pairs total:

$$\sum_{i=1}^{10} \binom{r_i}{2} \le 45 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \sum r_i^2 \
le N + 90.$$

By Cauchy–Schwarz, $\sum r_i^2 \ge N^2/10$, so $N^2/10 \le N + 90$, giving $N^2
- 10N - 900 \le 0$. The positive root is $5 + 5\sqrt{37} \approx 35.41$, so $N \
le 35$.

**Ruling out $N = 35$.** If $N = 35$, then $\sum r_i^2 \ge 122.5$ and $\sum r_i^
2 \le 125$, so $\sum r_i^2 \in \{123, 124, 125\}$. Since $\sum \binom{r_i}{2} =
\frac{\sum r_i^2 - N}{2}$ must be an integer, $\sum r_i^2$ must have the same pa
rity as $N = 35$ (odd), leaving $\sum r_i^2 \in \{123, 125\}$.

- If $\sum r_i^2 = 123$: then $\sum (r_i - 3.5)^2 = 123 - 245 + 122.5 = 0.5$, bu
t each $(r_i - 3.5)^2 \ge 0.25$ (since $r_i$ is an integer), giving $\sum \ge 2.
5$. Contradiction.
- If $\sum r_i^2 = 125$: then $\sum \binom{r_i}{2} = 45 = \binom{10}{2}$, so **e
very** pair of columns is covered exactly once. Also $\sum (r_i - 3.5)^2 = 2.5 =
 10 \times 0.25$, forcing all $r_i \in \{3, 4\}$ with five $r_i = 4$ and five $r
_i = 3$.

We analyze the case $\sum r_i^2 = 125$. The rows form a **pairwise balanced desi
gn (PBD)** on 10 points (columns) with five blocks of size 4 and five blocks of
size 3, where every pair of points lies in exactly one block.

By the symmetric column argument, the column sums must also be five 4's and five
 5's... five 4's and five 3's. Let $A$ = columns with sum 4 (5 columns), $B$ = c
olumns with sum 3 (5 columns).

**Replication analysis.** For a column $c$ appearing in rows of sizes $s_1, \ldo
ts, s_{r_c}$, the other 9 columns are partitioned: $\sum (s_j - 1) = 9$.

- **Column in $B$** (sum 3): $\sum s_j = 12$ with $s_j \in \{3,4\}$, so $4+4+4 =
 12$. Such a column appears in **three size-4 rows** and **zero size-3 rows**.
- **Column in $A$** (sum 4): $\sum s_j = 13$, so $4+3+3+3 = 13$. Such a column a
ppears in **one size-4 row** and **three size-3 rows**.

**Contradiction.** The five size-3 rows contain only columns from $A$ (since col
umns in $B$ never appear in size-3 rows). Each size-3 row is a 3-subset of the 5
-element set $A$, covering $\binom{3}{2} = 3$ pairs within $A$. Across 5 such ro
ws, this gives $5 \times 3 = 15$ pair-incidences within $A$. But $A$ has only $\
binom{5}{2} = 10$ pairs, and the PBD condition requires each pair to appear at m
ost once. Since $15 > 10$, this is impossible.

Therefore $N = 35$ is unachievable, and $N \le 34$.

### Construction Achieving N = 34

Define 10 rows as subsets of columns $\{0, 1, \ldots, 9\}$:

| Row | Columns | Size |
|-----|---------|------|
| 1 | $\{0,1,2,3\}$ | 4 |
| 2 | $\{0,4,5,6\}$ | 4 |
| 3 | $\{1,4,7,8\}$ | 4 |
| 4 | $\{2,5,7,9\}$ | 4 |
| 5 | $\{0,8,9\}$ | 3 |
| 6 | $\{3,6,7\}$ | 3 |
| 7 | $\{3,4,9\}$ | 3 |
| 8 | $\{3,5,8\}$ | 3 |
| 9 | $\{1,6,9\}$ | 3 |
| 10 | $\{2,6,8\}$ | 3 |

**Total:** $4 \times 4 + 6 \times 3 = 34$ boosters.

**Verification of no rectangle.** One checks that every pair of rows shares **at
 most one** column. For example:
- Rows 1 & 2 share $\{0\}$; Rows 1 & 3 share $\{1\}$; Rows 1 & 4 share $\{2\}$;
Rows 2 & 3 share $\{4\}$; Rows 2 & 4 share $\{5\}$; Rows 3 & 4 share $\{7\}$.
- Rows 5 & 6 share $\{\}$ (empty); Rows 7 & 10 share $\{\}$; Rows 8 & 9 share $\
{\}$.
- All other pairs share exactly one column.

Since no two rows share two or more columns, no four boosters form the corners o
f an axis-parallel rectangle. ✓

**Column sums:** Columns 3, 6, 8, 9 each have sum 4; all others have sum 3. Tota
l $= 4 \times 4 + 6 \times 3 = 34$. ✓

### Conclusion

The upper bound is $N \le 34$ (since $N = 35$ leads to a combinatorial contradic
tion), and an explicit construction achieves $N = 34$. Therefore, the maximum nu
mber of signal boosters is:

$$\boxed{34}$$
