## Proof

**Claim.** Let $(M, g)$ be a Riemannian manifold and $TM$ its tangent bundle, eq
uipped with the canonical smooth structure. The norm map
$$\|\cdot\| : TM \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}, \qquad v \in T_pM \longmapsto \|v\|
_g = \sqrt{g_p(v,v)}$$
is continuous (in fact, smooth on $TM \setminus Z$, where $Z$ is the zero sectio
n, and continuous on all of $TM$).

**Proof.** We work in local coordinates and use the local trivialization of $TM$
.

**Step 1: Local expression.** Let $(U, x^1, \dots, x^n)$ be a coordinate chart o
n $M$. Over $U$ the tangent bundle trivializes as
$$TM|_U \cong U \times \mathbb{R}^n, \qquad v = \sum_i v^i \tfrac{\partial}{\par
tial x^i}\Big|_p \;\longleftrightarrow\; (p, (v^1,\dots,v^n)).$$
This trivialization is a diffeomorphism (it is a smooth bundle chart), so contin
uity of $\|\cdot\|$ can be checked in these coordinates.

In this chart the metric is represented by the matrix $g_{ij}(p) = g_p\!\left(\t
frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}, \tfrac{\partial}{\partial x^j}\right)$, and the sq
uared norm is
$$\|v\|_g^2 = g_p(v, v) = \sum_{i,j=1}^n g_{ij}(p)\, v^i v^j.$$

**Step 2: Smoothness of the squared norm.** Each $g_{ij} : U \to \mathbb{R}$ is
a smooth function (the metric $g$ is a smooth section of $\mathrm{Sym}^2(T^*M)$
by definition of a Riemannian manifold). The component functions $v^i$ are smoot
h coordinates on the total space $TM|_U \cong U \times \mathbb{R}^n$. Therefore
$$\|v\|_g^2 = \sum_{i,j} g_{ij}(p)\, v^i v^j$$
is a finite sum of products of smooth functions of $(p, v^1, \dots, v^n)$, hence
 is **smooth** on $TM|_U$.

**Step 3: From squared norm to norm.** The square-root function $\sqrt{\,\cdot\,
} : [0,\infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ is continuous, and smooth on $(0,\infty)$. Since
$\|v\|_g^2 \geq 0$ for all $v$ (positive-definiteness of $g$), the norm is the c
omposition
$$\|v\|_g = \sqrt{\|v\|_g^2},$$
i.e. $\|\cdot\| = \sqrt{\,\cdot\,} \circ \|\cdot\|_g^2$. As a composition of con
tinuous maps, $\|\cdot\|$ is **continuous** on $TM|_U$. Moreover, where $\|v\|_g
^2 > 0$ (i.e. away from the zero section), both maps are smooth, so $\|\cdot\|$
is smooth on $TM \setminus Z$.

**Step 4: Global continuity.** Continuity is a local property. The charts $\{TM|
_U\}$ cover $TM$, and on each chart the norm is continuous by Step 3. Hence $\|\
cdot\| : TM \to \mathbb{R}$ is continuous on all of $TM$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The only subtlety is at the zero section $Z = \{v : \|v\| = 0\}$, wh
ere $\sqrt{\,\cdot\,}$ is not differentiable. This is why the norm is continuous
 everywhere but smooth only off the zero section. The squared norm $\|v\|_g^2$,
being a polynomial in local coordinates, is smooth on the entire tangent bundle.
