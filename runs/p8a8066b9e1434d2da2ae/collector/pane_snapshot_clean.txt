## Solution

**The answer is no.** The implication does not hold. We provide an explicit coun
terexample.

---

**Counterexample.** Take $n = 2$ and let

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B = \begin{pmatrix}
-2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Both $A$ and $B$ are Hermitian (in fact real symmetric). Their sum is

$$A + B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 - 2 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 + 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix
} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} = -I,$$

which has eigenvalues $-1, -1$, both nonpositive. Thus the hypothesis is satisfi
ed.

Now compute the eigenvalues (in the standard decreasing order $\lambda_1 \ge \la
mbda_2$):

- $A$ has eigenvalues $1$ and $-1$, so $\lambda_1(A) = 1$, $\lambda_2(A) = -1$.
- $B$ has eigenvalues $0$ and $-2$, so $\lambda_1(B) = 0$, $\lambda_2(B) = -2$.

For $i = 1$:

$$\lambda_1(A) = 1 > 0 = \lambda_1(B).$$

Thus $\lambda_1(A) \le \lambda_1(B)$ **fails**, even though $A + B$ has only non
positive eigenvalues.

---

**Remark on why the implication breaks down.** The condition $A + B \le 0$ (nega
tive semidefinite) is equivalent to $A \le -B$ in the Loewner order, which guara
ntees $\lambda_i(A) \le \lambda_i(-B) = -\lambda_{n-i+1}(B)$ for each $i$. But t
he claimed inequality $\lambda_i(A) \le \lambda_i(B)$ compares $\lambda_i(A)$ wi
th $\lambda_i(B)$ at the **same** index, whereas the Loewner comparison with $-B
$ relates $\lambda_i(A)$ to $-\lambda_{n-i+1}(B)$ — the eigenvalue of $B$ at the
 **opposite** end of the spectrum. These are generally unrelated, so no componen
twise inequality $\lambda_i(A) \le \lambda_i(B)$ can be inferred from $A + B \le
 0$ alone. The counterexample above exploits exactly this gap: $A$ has a large p
ositive eigenvalue at the top of its spectrum while $B$'s top eigenvalue is only
 $0$, yet their sum is negative definite because $B$'s large negative eigenvalue
 ($-2$) cancels $A$'s positive eigenvalue ($1$) and $A$'s negative eigenvalue ($
-1$) keeps the other diagonal entry nonpositive.
