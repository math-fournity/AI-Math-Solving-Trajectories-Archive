## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** Such a sequence exists.

**Step 1: Reduction to the torus.** Since $f$ is $\mathbb{Z}^n$-periodic and loc
ally in $L^p$, it descends to a well-defined element $\tilde f \in L^p(\mathbb{T
}^n)$ of the torus $\mathbb{T}^n = \mathbb{R}^n/\mathbb{Z}^n$, identified with $
[0,1)^n$. The norm $\|f\|_{L^p((0,1)^n)}$ is exactly $\|\tilde f\|_{L^p(\mathbb{
T}^n)}$.

**Step 2: Construct a smooth periodic mollifier.** Let $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\
mathbb{R}^n)$ be a standard nonnegative mollifier supported in $B(0,1)$ with $\i
nt_{\mathbb{R}^n}\varphi = 1$. For $\varepsilon > 0$ define the **periodized ker
nel**

$$K_\varepsilon(x) \;=\; \sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^n} \varepsilon^{-n}\,\varphi\!\Big
(\tfrac{x+k}{\varepsilon}\Big).$$

The sum is locally finite (only finitely many $k$ contribute for each $x$, since
 $\varphi$ is supported in $B(0,\varepsilon)$ after scaling), so $K_\varepsilon
\in C^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$. It is $\mathbb{Z}^n$-periodic by construction, and
$\int_{[0,1)^n} K_\varepsilon = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \varepsilon^{-n}\varphi(\cdo
t/\varepsilon) = 1$.

**Step 3: Define the approximants.** Set

$$f_\varepsilon(x) \;=\; \int_{[0,1)^n} f(y)\, K_\varepsilon(x-y)\,dy.$$

*Periodicity:* For $m \in \mathbb{Z}^n$, using periodicity of $K_\varepsilon$,

$$f_\varepsilon(x+m) = \int_{[0,1)^n} f(y)\,K_\varepsilon(x+m-y)\,dy = \int_{[0,
1)^n} f(y)\,K_\varepsilon(x-y)\,dy = f_\varepsilon(x).$$

*Smoothness:* Since $K_\varepsilon$ is smooth and $\partial_x^\alpha K_\varepsil
on$ is bounded for each multi-index $\alpha$, and $f \in L^1([0,1)^n)$ (as $L^p$
 on a bounded domain, $p\ge 1$), we may differentiate under the integral:

$$\partial^\alpha f_\varepsilon(x) = \int_{[0,1)^n} f(y)\,\partial^\alpha K_\var
epsilon(x-y)\,dy,$$

so $f_\varepsilon \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$, and it is periodic, hence smooth
on $\mathbb{T}^n$.

**Step 4: Identify with standard mollification.** A change of variables shows

$$f_\varepsilon(x) = \int_{[0,1)^n} f(y)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^n}\varepsilon^{-n}\
varphi\!\Big(\tfrac{x-y+k}{\varepsilon}\Big)dy = \sum_k \int_{[0,1)^n} f(y)\,\va
repsilon^{-n}\varphi\!\Big(\tfrac{x+k-y}{\varepsilon}\Big)dy.$$

Substituting $z = y - k$ and using $f(z+k)=f(z)$,

$$f_\varepsilon(x) = \sum_k \int_{[0,1)^n+k} f(z)\,\varepsilon^{-n}\varphi\!\Big
(\tfrac{x-z}{\varepsilon}\Big)dz = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} f(z)\,\varepsilon^{-n}\va
rphi\!\Big(\tfrac{x-z}{\varepsilon}\Big)dz = (f * \varphi_\varepsilon)(x).$$

Thus $f_\varepsilon$ is exactly the standard mollification of $f$ on $\mathbb{R}
^n$.

**Step 5: Convergence.** By the classical approximation theorem for mollifiers,
since $f \in L^p_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^n)$,

$$\|f * \varphi_\varepsilon - f\|_{L^p((0,1)^n)} \longrightarrow 0 \quad\text{as
 }\varepsilon\to 0.$$

(For $1\le p<\infty$ this is standard; for $p=\infty$ it holds at every Lebesgue
 point, giving a.e. convergence, and $L^\infty$-convergence holds when $f$ is un
iformly continuous. For the stated $L^p$ with $p<\infty$ the convergence in norm
 is immediate.)

**Conclusion.** Taking $\varepsilon = 1/n$ yields a sequence $f_n = f_{1/n}$ of
smooth $\mathbb{Z}^n$-periodic functions with

$$\|f - f_n\|_{L^p((0,1)^n)} \to 0.$$
