## Solution

**Problem.** Prove there are exactly three right triangles (up to orientation an
d translation) with integer side lengths and area equal to twice their perimeter
.

oof sketch. Proceeding.

### Setup

Let the right triangle have legs $a, b$ and hypotenuse $c$, all positive integer
s, with
$$a^2 + b^2 = c^2.$$
Its area is $\tfrac12 ab$ and its perimeter is $a+b+c$. The condition is
$$\tfrac12 ab = 2(a+b+c), \qquad\text{i.e.,}\qquad ab = 4(a+b+c). \tag{1}$$

### Parametrization

Every integer right triangle is, up to swapping the legs, a multiple of a primit
ive Pythagorean triple. Concretely, there exist integers $k\ge 1$, $m>n\ge 1$, w
ith $\gcd(m,n)=1$ and $m\not\equiv n\pmod 2$, such that
$$a = k(m^2-n^2),\qquad b = 2kmn,\qquad c = k(m^2+n^2).$$
This parametrization lists each triangle (up to leg-swap = orientation) exactly
once.

Substituting into (1):
- $ab = k(m^2-n^2)\cdot 2kmn = 2k^2 mn(m^2-n^2) = 2k^2 mn(m-n)(m+n)$.
- $a+b+c = k(m^2-n^2)+2kmn+k(m^2+n^2) = 2km^2+2kmn = 2km(m+n)$.

So (1) becomes
$$2k^2 mn(m-n)(m+n) = 4\cdot 2km(m+n) = 8km(m+n).$$

Since $m+n>0$ and $k,m>0$, divide both sides by $2km(m+n)$:
$$\boxed{\,k\,n\,(m-n) = 4\,}. \tag{2}$$

### Solving the Diophantine equation

Equation (2) requires three **positive** integers $k,\, n,\,(m-n)$ whose product
 is $4$. The ordered factorizations of $4$ are:

| $k$ | $n$ | $m-n$ | $m$ | $\gcd(m,n)$ | parity $m,n$ | valid? |
|-----|-----|-------|-----|-------------|--------------|--------|
| 1 | 1 | 4 | 5 | 1 | odd, odd (same) | ✗ |
| 1 | 2 | 2 | 4 | 2 | — | ✗ |
| 1 | 4 | 1 | 5 | 1 | odd, even (diff) | ✓ |
| 2 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 1 | odd, odd (same) | ✗ |
| 2 | 2 | 1 | 3 | 1 | odd, even (diff) | ✓ |
| 4 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 1 | even, odd (diff) | ✓ |

The three valid cases:

**Case A:** $(k,n,m)=(1,4,5)$.
$$a=25-16=9,\quad b=2\cdot5\cdot4=40,\quad c=25+16=41.$$
Check: $9^2+40^2=81+1600=1681=41^2$. Area $=\tfrac12\cdot9\cdot40=180$; perimete
r $=9+40+41=90$; $2\cdot90=180$. ✓

**Case B:** $(k,n,m)=(2,2,3)$.
$$a=2(9-4)=10,\quad b=2\cdot2\cdot2\cdot3=24,\quad c=2(9+4)=26.$$
Check: $10^2+24^2=100+576=676=26^2$. Area $=\tfrac12\cdot10\cdot24=120$; perimet
er $=10+24+26=60$; $2\cdot60=120$. ✓

**Case C:** $(k,n,m)=(4,1,2)$.
$$a=4(4-1)=12,\quad b=4\cdot2\cdot2\cdot1=16,\quad c=4(4+1)=20.$$
Check: $12^2+16^2=144+256=400=20^2$. Area $=\tfrac12\cdot12\cdot16=96$; perimete
r $=12+16+20=48$; $2\cdot48=96$. ✓

### Exactly three

The parametrization with the coprimality and opposite-parity conditions enumerat
es every integer right triangle exactly once (up to orientation, i.e. leg-swap).
 Equation (2) is a necessary and sufficient consequence of the area–perimeter co
ndition, and its complete set of positive-integer solutions is the six ordered t
riples above. Only three of them satisfy the primitive-triple constraints, yield
ing exactly the three triangles
$$(9,40,41),\qquad (10,24,26),\qquad (12,16,20).$$
These are pairwise distinct (different side lengths), so no two coincide under o
rientation or translation. Hence there are **exactly three** such right triangle
s. $\blacksquare$
