## Solution

**Setup.** Let $G = (x^2y,\, y^2z,\, z^2x)$. The surface $Y$ is the lateral tube
 $(x+y)^2+(y+z)^2 = 4+z^2$, $0\le z\le 2$, with outward normal. We close it with
 caps $D_0$ ($z=0$) and $D_2$ ($z=2$) and apply the divergence theorem, then sub
tract the cap fluxes.

**Change of variables.** Set
$$u = x+y,\quad v = y+z,\quad z = z.$$
Then $y = v-z$, $x = u-v+z$, and
$$\frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(u,v,z)} = \det\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&1\\0&1&-1\\0
&0&1\end{pmatrix}=1,$$
so the map is volume- and area-preserving. The enclosed region $V$ becomes the s
olid of revolution
$$u^2+v^2 \le 4+z^2,\quad 0\le z\le 2,$$
i.e. a disk of radius $r(z)=\sqrt{4+z^2}$ at each height $z$.

**Divergence.**
$$\nabla\cdot G = 2xy+2yz+2zx = 2(xy+yz+zx).$$
With $x=u-v+z$, $y=v-z$:
$$xy+yz+zx = (u-v+z)(v-z)+(v-z)z+z(u-v+z) = uv - v^2 + 2vz - z^2.$$

**Volume integral.** Integrating over the disk $u^2+v^2\le r^2$ (radius $r^2=4+z
^2$), the odd terms $uv$ and $2vz$ vanish, and $\iint v^2\,du\,dv = \pi r^4/4$,
$\iint z^2\,du\,dv = z^2\pi r^2$:
$$\iint_{u^2+v^2\le r^2}(xy+yz+zx)\,du\,dv = -\frac{\pi r^4}{4}+z^2\pi r^2 = \fr
ac{\pi(4+z^2)(3z^2-4)}{4}.$$
Therefore
$$\iiint_V \nabla\cdot G\,dV = 2\int_0^2 \frac{\pi(4+z^2)(3z^2-4)}{4}\,dz = \fra
c{\pi}{2}\int_0^2(3z^4+8z^2-16)\,dz.$$
$$\int_0^2(3z^4+8z^2-16)\,dz = \frac{96}{5}+\frac{64}{3}-32 = \frac{288+320-480}
{15}=\frac{128}{15}.$$
$$\iiint_V \nabla\cdot G\,dV = \frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{128}{15} = \frac{64\pi}{1
5}.$$

**Cap fluxes.**
- **Top cap** $D_2$ ($z=2$, outward normal $+\mathbf{k}$): $G\cdot\mathbf{k}=z^2
x=4x=4(u-v+2)$. Over $u^2+v^2\le 8$, the $u,v$ terms vanish:
$$\iint_{D_2}G\cdot\mathbf{k}\,dS = 8\cdot(\pi\cdot 8)=64\pi.$$
- **Bottom cap** $D_0$ ($z=0$, outward normal $-\mathbf{k}$): $G\cdot(-\mathbf{k
})=-z^2x=0$, so the flux is $0$.

**Lateral flux.** By the divergence theorem,
$$\iint_Y G\cdot N\,dS = \iiint_V \nabla\cdot G\,dV - \iint_{D_2}G\cdot\mathbf{k
}\,dS - \iint_{D_0}G\cdot(-\mathbf{k})\,dS$$
$$= \frac{64\pi}{15} - 64\pi - 0 = \frac{64\pi - 960\pi}{15} = -\frac{896\pi}{15
}.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\iint_Y G\cdot N\,dS = -\frac{896\pi}{15}}$$
