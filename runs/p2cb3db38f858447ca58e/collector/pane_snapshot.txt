## Example of a densely defined, non-closable linear operator

Let $\mathcal{H} = \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$ with standard orthonormal basis $\{e_n\}_
{n \geq 1}$.

**Domain.** Set $\mathcal{D}(A) = c_{00}$, the linear span of $\{e_n\}$, i.e. th
e finitely supported sequences. This is dense in $\ell^2$.

**Definition of $A$.** Fix $u = (1/n)_{n \geq 1} \in \ell^2$ (note $\sum 1/n^2 <
 \infty$). For $x = (x_n) \in c_{00}$ define the linear functional
$$\phi(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} x_n,$$
which is a finite sum hence well-defined on $c_{00}$, and set
$$Ax = \phi(x)\, u = \Big(\sum_{n} x_n\Big) \cdot u.$$

Then $A : \mathcal{D}(A) \to \mathcal{H}$ is linear (since $\phi$ is linear and
multiplication by $u$ is linear), and $\mathcal{D}(A) = c_{00}$ is dense in $\ma
thcal{H}$.

**$A$ is not closable.** Let $H_N = \sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{1}{n}$ (the $N$-th harm
onic number) and consider
$$x^{(N)} = \frac{1}{H_N}\sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{1}{n}\, e_n \in c_{00} = \mathcal{
D}(A).$$

*Convergence to $0$:*
$$\|x^{(N)}\|^2 = \frac{1}{H_N^2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n^2} \leq \frac{1}{H_N^2
}\cdot\frac{\pi^2}{6} \xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{} 0,$$
since $H_N \to \infty$. Hence $x^{(N)} \to 0$ in $\mathcal{H}$.

*Image stays away from $0$:*
$$\phi(x^{(N)}) = \frac{1}{H_N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n} = \frac{H_N}{H_N} = 1,$
$
so
$$Ax^{(N)} = 1 \cdot u = u \quad \text{for all } N.$$
Thus $Ax^{(N)} \to u \neq 0$ (since $u \neq 0$ in $\ell^2$).

**Conclusion.** The graph $\Gamma(A) = \{(x, Ax) : x \in \mathcal{D}(A)\}$ conta
ins the sequence $(x^{(N)}, Ax^{(N)}) = (x^{(N)}, u)$ with $x^{(N)} \to 0$ and $
Ax^{(N)} \to u \neq 0$. Therefore the closure $\overline{\Gamma(A)}$ contains th
e point $(0, u)$ with $u \neq 0$. If $A$ were closable, $\overline{\Gamma(A)}$ w
ould be the graph of an operator, forcing $u = 0$ — a contradiction. Hence $A$ i
s **not closable**.

This gives a densely defined linear operator on $\ell^2$ that is not closable. ∎
