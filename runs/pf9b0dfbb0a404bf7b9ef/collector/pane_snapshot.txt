## Green's Function for the 3D Laplacian

We seek $g(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')$ satisfying

$$-\nabla^2 g(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}') = \delta^3(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}').$$

### Step 1. Reduction by translation invariance

The operator $-\nabla^2$ has constant coefficients, so the Green's function depe
nds only on the separation $\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'$. Writing $g(\math
bf{r},\mathbf{r}')=G(\mathbf{R})$ with $R=|\mathbf{R}|$, the equation becomes

$$-\nabla_{\mathbf{R}}^2 G(\mathbf{R}) = \delta^3(\mathbf{R}).$$

### Step 2. Solve away from the origin

For $\mathbf{R}\neq 0$, the equation is Laplace's equation $\nabla^2 G = 0$. By
spherical symmetry we take $G=G(R)$, giving

$$\nabla^2 G = \frac{1}{R^2}\frac{d}{dR}\!\left(R^2\frac{dG}{dR}\right)=0.$$

Integrating: $R^2\,G'(R)=C_1$, so $G'(R)=C_1/R^2$, hence

$$G(R) = -\frac{C_1}{R} + C_2.$$

We demand $G\to 0$ as $R\to\infty$ (the standard free-space boundary condition),
 so $C_2=0$. Thus $G(R) = A/R$ for some constant $A=-C_1$.

### Step 3. Fix the constant via the delta function

Integrate the defining equation over a ball $B_\varepsilon$ of radius $\varepsil
on$ centered at the origin:

$$-\int_{B_\varepsilon}\nabla^2 G\,d^3R = \int_{B_\varepsilon}\delta^3(\mathbf{R
})\,d^3R = 1.$$

By the divergence theorem,

$$-\int_{B_\varepsilon}\nabla^2 G\,d^3R = -\oint_{\partial B_\varepsilon}\nabla
G\cdot d\mathbf{S}.$$

With $G=A/R$:

$$\nabla G = \frac{dG}{dR}\hat{\mathbf{R}} = -\frac{A}{R^2}\hat{\mathbf{R}},\qqu
ad d\mathbf{S}=\hat{\mathbf{R}}\,R^2\,d\Omega.$$

Therefore

$$-\oint_{\partial B_\varepsilon}\!\left(-\frac{A}{R^2}\hat{\mathbf{R}}\right)\c
dot\hat{\mathbf{R}}\,R^2\,d\Omega = A\oint d\Omega = 4\pi A.$$

Setting $4\pi A = 1$ gives $A = \dfrac{1}{4\pi}$.

### Step 4. Result

$$\boxed{\,g(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}') = \frac{1}{4\pi\,|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|}\
,}$$

### Verification

For $\mathbf{r}\neq\mathbf{r}'$, direct computation confirms $\nabla^2(1/R)=0$.
At $\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}'$, the flux calculation above shows $-\int\nabla^2 g\,
d^3r = 1$ for any enclosing volume, which is precisely the defining distribution
al property $-\nabla^2 g = \delta^3(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}')$. The boundary condi
tion $g\to 0$ at infinity is satisfied. $\blacksquare$
