## Solution

We evaluate
$$I_n := \int_0^{1}\exp\!\left(-\frac{2n^{2}x^{2}}{1+x/n}\right)dx,\qquad L:=\li
m_{n\to\infty}I_n.$$

**Substitution.** Set $x=t/n$, so $dx=dt/n$. When $x=0$, $t=0$; when $x=1$, $t=n
$. Also $n^{2}x^{2}=n^{2}\cdot(t/n)^{2}=t^{2}$ and $x/n=t/n^{2}$. Hence
$$I_n=\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}\exp\!\left(-\frac{2t^{2}}{1+t/n^{2}}\right)dt. \ta
g{1}$$

**Bounding the integrand.** For $t\in[0,n]$ we have $0\le t/n^{2}\le 1/n\le 1$,
so
$$1\;\le\;1+\frac{t}{n^{2}}\;\le\;1+\frac{1}{n}\;\le\;2.$$
Consequently
$$\frac{2t^{2}}{1+t/n^{2}}\;\ge\;\frac{2t^{2}}{1+1/n}\;\ge\;t^{2},$$
where the last inequality uses $2/(1+1/n)\ge 1$ (valid since $1+1/n\le 2$). Ther
efore
$$\exp\!\left(-\frac{2t^{2}}{1+t/n^{2}}\right)\;\le\;e^{-t^{2}}. \tag{2}$$

**Squeeze.** Combining (1) and (2) with the trivial lower bound $I_n\ge 0$,
$$0\;\le\;I_n\;\le\;\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}e^{-t^{2}}\,dt\;\le\;\frac{1}{n}\int_
{0}^{\infty}e^{-t^{2}}\,dt\;=\;\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2n}.$$

Since $\dfrac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0$, the squeeze theorem g
ives
$$\boxed{\,\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-\frac{2n^{2}x^{2}}{1+x/n}}\,dx=0\,}.
$$

**Remark on the rate (sanity check).** For fixed $t$, $1+t/n^{2}\to 1$, so the i
ntegrand in (1) converges pointwise to $e^{-2t^{2}}$, and dominated convergence
(dominator $e^{-t^{2}}$) yields
$$\int_{0}^{n}e^{-\frac{2t^{2}}{1+t/n^{2}}}\,dt\;\longrightarrow\;\int_{0}^{\inf
ty}e^{-2t^{2}}\,dt=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{4},$$
a finite constant. Thus $I_n\sim \dfrac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{4\,n}\to 0$, confirming the
 limit is $0$ (and not merely bounded away from a nonzero value). The dominant c
ontribution comes from the boundary layer $x=O(1/n)$ of width $O(1/n)$, where th
e integrand is $O(1)$; everywhere else the factor $n^{2}x^{2}$ drives the integr
and to $0$.

The limit exists and equals $0$.
