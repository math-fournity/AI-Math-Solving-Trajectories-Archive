## Proof

**Goal:** Count $n \in \{1, 2, \ldots, 1330\}$ such that $11 \nmid \binom{2n}{n}
$.

### Step 1: Apply Kummer's Theorem

By Kummer's theorem, the exponent of a prime $p$ in $\binom{2n}{n}$ equals the n
umber of carries when computing $n + n$ in base $p$.

Therefore:
$$11 \nmid \binom{2n}{n} \iff \text{there are no carries when adding } n + n \te
xt{ in base } 11.$$

### Step 2: Characterize carry-free doubling in base 11

Writing $n$ in base 11 as $n = (d_k \, d_{k-1} \cdots d_0)_{11}$, the addition $
n + n$ produces no carry if and only if every digit $d_i$ satisfies $2d_i < 11$,
 i.e., $d_i \le 5$ (since base-11 digits range from $0$ to $10$).

So $n$ is **stable** $\iff$ every base-11 digit of $n$ lies in $\{0, 1, 2, 3, 4,
 5\}$.

### Step 3: Express $1330$ in base 11

Compute powers of 11:
$$11^1 = 11, \quad 11^2 = 121, \quad 11^3 = 1331.$$

Since $1330 = 1331 - 1 = 11^3 - 1$, we get:
$$1330 = (10\;\;10\;\;10)_{11}.$$

Verification: $10 \cdot 121 + 10 \cdot 11 + 10 = 1210 + 110 + 10 = 1330$. ✓

### Step 4: Count stable $n$ in $\{1, \ldots, 1330\}$

Every integer from $0$ to $11^3 - 1 = 1330$ has a unique 3-digit base-11 represe
ntation $(d_2\, d_1\, d_0)_{11}$ (allowing leading zeros).

The condition "all digits $\le 5$" restricts each of the 3 digit positions to 6
choices $\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}$, giving:
$$6 \times 6 \times 6 = 6^3 = 216 \text{ numbers in } \{0, 1, \ldots, 1330\}.$$

The largest such number is $(5\;\;5\;\;5)_{11} = 5 \cdot 121 + 5 \cdot 11 + 5 =
665 < 1330$, confirming all 216 numbers lie within the range $\{0, \ldots, 1330\
}$, and no number with a digit $\ge 6$ can qualify.

Since we need $n \ge 1$, we exclude $n = 0$:
$$216 - 1 = 215.$$

### Step 5: Sanity check with small cases

- $n = 1$: $\binom{2}{1} = 2$, not divisible by $11$. Base-11 digit: $1 \le 5$.
✓ stable.
- $n = 5$: $\binom{10}{5} = 252 = 11 \times 22 + 10$, not divisible by $11$. Dig
it: $5 \le 5$. ✓ stable.
- $n = 6$: $\binom{12}{6} = 924 = 11 \times 84$, divisible by $11$. Digit: $6 >
5$. ✓ unstable.

All consistent with the digit criterion.

### Answer

The number of stable drones is $\boxed{215}$.
