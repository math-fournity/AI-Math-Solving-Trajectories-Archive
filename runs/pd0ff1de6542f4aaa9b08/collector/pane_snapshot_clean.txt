**Proof.**

Place the circumcenter $O$ at the origin. Let the circumradius be $R$, and let $
\vec a, \vec b, \vec c$ be the position vectors of $A, B, C$, so $|\vec a|=|\vec
 b|=|\vec c|=R$.

Let the circle $\omega$ through $B$ and $C$ have equation $x^2+y^2+ux+vy+w=0$.

**Step 1: Locate $D$ and $E$.**

Since $B,O,D$ are collinear, $D$ lies on line $BO$, so $\vec d = t_D\,\hat b$ wh
ere $\hat b = \vec b/R$. Substituting into the equation of $\omega$:
$$t^2 + t(u\cos\beta + v\sin\beta) + w = 0.$$
The point $B$ (i.e., $t=R$) is one root; the other is $t_D$. By Vieta's formulas
, $R\cdot t_D = w$, hence $t_D = w/R$.

Likewise, $E$ lies on line $CO$, and substituting $\vec e = t\,\hat c$ gives a q
uadratic with roots $t=R$ and $t_E$, so $R\cdot t_E = w$ and $t_E = w/R$.

Setting $\rho = w/R^2$, we obtain
$$\vec d = \rho\,\vec b, \qquad \vec e = \rho\,\vec c.$$
Thus $D$ and $E$ are the images of $B$ and $C$ under the homothety centered at $
O$ with ratio $\rho$.

**Step 2: Show $M$ lies on line $AO$.**

The point $M$ is the intersection of the line through $D$ perpendicular to $AB$
and the line through $E$ perpendicular to $AC$. We test the candidate $M = \lamb
da\,\vec a$ (a point on line $AO$).

The perpendicularity conditions are:
$$(\lambda\vec a - \rho\vec b)\cdot(\vec b - \vec a) = 0, \qquad (\lambda\vec a
- \rho\vec c)\cdot(\vec c - \vec a) = 0.$$

Expanding the first:
$$\lambda(\vec a\cdot\vec b - \vec a\cdot\vec a) - \rho(\vec b\cdot\vec b - \vec
 b\cdot\vec a) = \lambda(\vec a\cdot\vec b - R^2) - \rho(R^2 - \vec a\cdot\vec b
) = (\vec a\cdot\vec b - R^2)(\lambda + \rho) = 0.$$

Since $A\ne B$, we have $\vec a\cdot\vec b \ne R^2$, so $\lambda = -\rho$.

Expanding the second condition identically:
$$(\vec a\cdot\vec c - R^2)(\lambda + \rho) = 0,$$
and since $A\ne C$, again $\lambda = -\rho$.

Both conditions are satisfied by the **same** value $\lambda = -\rho$. Therefore
 $M = -\rho\,\vec a$, which is a point on line $AO$.

Hence $A$, $M$, and $O$ are collinear. $\blacksquare$
