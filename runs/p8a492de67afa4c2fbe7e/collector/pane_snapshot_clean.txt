## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. For any open set $O \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$, there exist a real-
valued function $f$ on $O$ and an open subset $V \subseteq O$ such that $f$ is n
ot locally bounded at any point of $V$.

**Case 1: $O = \emptyset$.** Take $V = \emptyset$ (an open subset of $O$) and $f
$ the empty function. The condition "$f$ is not locally bounded at any point of
$V$" holds vacuously.

**Case 2: $O \neq \emptyset$.** We construct such $f$ and $V$ explicitly.

Set $V = O$, which is an open subset of itself. Since $O$ is a nonempty open sub
set of $\mathbb{R}^m$, the set
$$Q := O \cap \mathbb{Q}^m$$
is countable and dense in $O$: every open ball contained in $O$ contains a point
 with all rational coordinates (by density of $\mathbb{Q}$ in $\mathbb{R}$ and o
penness of $O$). Enumumerate $Q$ as a sequence of distinct points
$$Q = \{q_1, q_2, q_3, \dots\}.$$

Define $f : O \to \mathbb{R}$ by
$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}
n, & \text{if } x = q_n \text{ for some } n \in \mathbb{N},\\
0, & \text{if } x \notin Q.
\end{cases}
$$
This is well-defined because the points $q_n$ are pairwise distinct, so each $x
\in Q$ corresponds to a unique index $n$.

**Verification that $f$ is not locally bounded at any point of $V = O$.** Let $x
 \in O$ and let $U$ be any open neighborhood of $x$ in $O$. Since $O$ is open in
 $\mathbb{R}^m$, there exists $r > 0$ such that the open ball $B(x, r) \subseteq
 U \cap O$. Because $Q$ is dense in $O$, the ball $B(x, r)$ contains infinitely
many points of $Q$. Hence, for every $M > 0$, there exists an index $n > M$ with
 $q_n \in B(x, r) \subseteq U$, and
$$f(q_n) = n > M.$$
Thus $f$ is unbounded on $U$. Since $U$ was an arbitrary neighborhood of $x$, $f
$ is not locally bounded at $x$. Since $x \in O$ was arbitrary, $f$ is not local
ly bounded at **every** point of $V = O$.

**Conclusion.** In both cases, such a function $f$ and open subset $V \subseteq
O$ exist. $\blacksquare$
