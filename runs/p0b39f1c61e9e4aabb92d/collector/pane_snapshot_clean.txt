Looking at this problem, I need to find all $m > 0$ such that $(mx - 3 + \sqrt{2
})^2 - \sqrt{x+m} = 0$ has exactly two distinct real roots in $[0,1]$.

## Setup

Let $g(x) = mx - 3 + \sqrt{2}$ (linear, increasing) and $h(x) = (x+m)^{1/4}$ (in
creasing, concave). The equation becomes $g(x)^2 = \sqrt{x+m}$, i.e., $|g(x)| =
h(x)$.

The zero of $g$ is at $x_0 = \frac{3-\sqrt{2}}{m}$. Note $g(x_0)^2 = 0 \neq \sqr
t{x_0+m} > 0$, so $x_0$ is never a root. The equation splits into two cases on d
isjoint regions:

- **Equation A** ($g \leq 0$, i.e., $x \leq x_0$): $p(x) := 3-\sqrt{2}-mx = h(x)
$
- **Equation B** ($g \geq 0$, i.e., $x \geq x_0$): $q(x) := mx-3+\sqrt{2} = h(x)
$

## Equation A Analysis

$\phi(x) = p(x) - h(x) = (3-\sqrt{2}-mx) - (x+m)^{1/4}$. Since $p$ is decreasing
 and $-h$ is decreasing, $\phi$ is **strictly decreasing** → at most one root.

- $\phi(x_0) = 0 - (x_0+m)^{1/4} < 0$.
- $\phi(0) = (3-\sqrt{2}) - m^{1/4}$.

A root exists in $[0, x_0]$ iff $\phi(0) \geq 0$, i.e., $m \leq (3-\sqrt{2})^4$.
 When $m \geq 3-\sqrt{2}$, we have $x_0 \leq 1$, so the root lies in $[0,1]$ aut
omatically.

## Equation B Analysis

$\psi(x) = q(x) - h(x) = (mx - 3 + \sqrt{2}) - (x+m)^{1/4}$. Since $q$ is linear
 and $h$ is concave, $\psi$ is **convex** → at most two roots, but since $\psi(x
_0) = 0 - (x_0+m)^{1/4} < 0$, a convex function starting negative can cross zero
 **at most once** in $(x_0, \infty)$. So at most one root in $[x_0, 1]$.

A root exists iff $\psi(1) \geq 0$:
$$m - 3 + \sqrt{2} \geq (1+m)^{1/4}$$

This requires $m \geq 3-\sqrt{2}$ (so $x_0 \leq 1$ and $q(1) \geq 0$).

## Combining the Conditions

Each equation contributes at most 1 root, and the regions are disjoint, so **exa
ctly 2 roots** requires exactly 1 from each:

- **From A:** $m \leq (3-\sqrt{2})^4$
- **From B:** $m \geq 3-\sqrt{2}$ and $m - 3 + \sqrt{2} \geq (1+m)^{1/4}$

## Solving Condition B

Set $t = m - 3 + \sqrt{2} \geq 0$, so $m = t + 3 - \sqrt{2}$ and $1+m = t + 4 -
\sqrt{2}$. The condition $t \geq (t+4-\sqrt{2})^{1/4}$ (with $t \geq 0$) becomes
:

$$t^4 \geq t + 4 - \sqrt{2} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F(t) := t^4 - t - (4-\
sqrt{2}) \geq 0$$

**Key observation:** $t = \sqrt{2}$ gives $F(\sqrt{2}) = 4 - \sqrt{2} - (4-\sqrt
{2}) = 0$. Factoring:

$$F(t) = (t-\sqrt{2})\underbrace{(t^3 + \sqrt{2}\,t^2 + 2t + 2\sqrt{2}-1)}_{G(t)
}$$

Since $G'(t) = 3t^2 + 2\sqrt{2}\,t + 2$ has discriminant $8 - 24 < 0$, we have $
G'(t) > 0$ for all $t$, and $G(0) = 2\sqrt{2}-1 > 0$, so **$G(t) > 0$ for all $t
 \geq 0$**.

Therefore $F(t) \geq 0 \iff t \geq \sqrt{2}$, i.e., $m \geq 3$.

## Final Range

Combining $m \geq 3$ (from B) with $m \leq (3-\sqrt{2})^4$ (from A):

$$(3-\sqrt{2})^4 = (11-6\sqrt{2})^2 = 193 - 132\sqrt{2}$$

## Boundary Verification

- **$m = 3$:** Equation B gives root at $x=1$ (since $q(1)=\sqrt{2}=(1+3)^{1/4}=
\sqrt{2}$ ✓). Equation A: $p(0)=3-\sqrt{2} \approx 1.586 > 3^{1/4} \approx 1.316
$, so root exists in $(0, x_0)$ with $x_0 = (3-\sqrt{2})/3 \approx 0.529$. Two d
istinct roots. ✓

- **$m = 193-132\sqrt{2}$:** Equation A gives root at $x=0$ (since $p(0)=3-\sqrt
{2}=m^{1/4}$ ✓). Equation B: $t = m-3+\sqrt{2} \approx 4.74 > \sqrt{2}$, so root
 exists in $(x_0, 1)$. Two distinct roots. ✓

- **$m$ slightly below 3:** Condition B fails → only 1 root. ✓
- **$m$ slightly above $(3-\sqrt{2})^4$:** Condition A fails → only 1 root. ✓

## Answer

The range of values of $m$ is:

$$\boxed{3 \leq m \leq 193 - 132\sqrt{2}}$$
