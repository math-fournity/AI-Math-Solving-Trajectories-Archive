**Problem.** Let $K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m})$ with discriminant $D$, ring of integer
s $R$ on $\mathbb{Z}$-basis $\{1,\delta\}$, and nontrivial automorphism $\sigma$
. For an odd prime $p$ with $D$ a square mod $p$, we have the splitting $(p)R=(p
,x+\delta)(p,x+\sigma(\delta))$. Determine when the two factors coincide.

---

### Proof

Set $\mathfrak{p}_1=(p,x+\delta)$ and $\mathfrak{p}_2=(p,x+\sigma(\delta))$, and
 let $\Delta:=\delta-\sigma(\delta)$.

**Step 1 — Reduction to a membership test.** Since both $\mathfrak{p}_1,\mathfra
k{p}_2$ are prime ideals of $R$ (they are the prime factors of $(p)R$), they are
 maximal. Hence $\mathfrak{p}_1=\mathfrak{p}_2$ iff either one contains the othe
r.

Now $x+\sigma(\delta)=(x+\delta)-\Delta$, so
$$\Delta\in\mathfrak{p}_1 \;\Longrightarrow\; x+\sigma(\delta)\in\mathfrak{p}_1
\;\Longrightarrow\; \mathfrak{p}_2\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_1 \;\Longrightarrow\; \m
athfrak{p}_1=\mathfrak{p}_2.$$
Conversely, if $\mathfrak{p}_1=\mathfrak{p}_2$, then both $x+\delta$ and $x+\sig
ma(\delta)$ lie in $\mathfrak{p}_1$, so $\Delta\in\mathfrak{p}_1$. Therefore
$$\boxed{\mathfrak{p}_1=\mathfrak{p}_2 \iff \Delta=\delta-\sigma(\delta)\in\math
frak{p}_1.}$$

**Step 2 — Identify $\Delta$ with $\sqrt{D}$.** The discriminant of the basis $\
{1,\delta\}$ is
$$D=\operatorname{disc}(1,\delta)=\bigl(\delta-\sigma(\delta)\bigr)^2=\Delta^2.$
$
Thus $\Delta$ is an element of $R$ whose square is $D$; concretely $\Delta=\pm\s
qrt{D}\in K$ (and $K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})$ since $D=m$ or $D=4m$).

**Step 3 — Translate membership of $\Delta$ into divisibility of $D$.** Because
$\mathfrak{p}_1$ is prime,
$$\Delta\in\mathfrak{p}_1 \iff \Delta^2=D\in\mathfrak{p}_1.$$
(The forward direction is closure under multiplication; the reverse uses primali
ty: $\Delta^2\in\mathfrak{p}_1\Rightarrow\Delta\in\mathfrak{p}_1$.)

Since $\mathfrak{p}_1$ lies above $p$, we have $\mathfrak{p}_1\cap\mathbb{Z}=p\m
athbb{Z}$. As $D\in\mathbb{Z}$,
$$D\in\mathfrak{p}_1 \iff D\in p\mathbb{Z} \iff p\mid D.$$

**Step 4 — Conclusion.** Chaining the equivalences,
$$\mathfrak{p}_1=\mathfrak{p}_2 \iff \Delta\in\mathfrak{p}_1 \iff D\in\mathfrak{
p}_1 \iff p\mid D.$$

he same ideal **if and only if $p$ divides the discriminant $D$**, i.e. iff $p$
ramifies in $K$ (equivalently $D\equiv 0\pmod p$).

*Remark.* This is consistent with the splitting theory of quadratic fields: $D$
a nonzero square mod $p$ gives two *distinct* primes (split, unramified); $D\equ

elta-\sigma(\delta)=\sqrt{D}$ falling into the prime, which happens exactly when
