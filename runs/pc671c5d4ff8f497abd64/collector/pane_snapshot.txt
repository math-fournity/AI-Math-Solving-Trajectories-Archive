R}^n$, hence $P(Y \in U) = 0$, so the elementary conditional distribution is und
efined. We regularize by tubular neighborhoods and show the limit is the standar

**Step 1: Orthogonal decomposition.** Write $\mathbb{R}^n = U \oplus U^\perp$ an
d let $\Pi_U, \Pi_{U^\perp}$ be the orthogonal projections. Since $Y \sim \mathc
al{N}(0, I_n)$ is isotropic, choosing an orthonormal basis adapted to the splitt

$$\Pi_U Y \perp\!\!\!\perp \Pi_{U^\perp} Y,$$
with $\Pi_U Y$ being standard normal on $U$ (its coordinates in any orthonormal
basis of $U$ are i.i.d. $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$) and $\Pi_{U^\perp} Y$ being standard

**Step 2: Regularizing event.** For $\epsilon > 0$ define the closed $\epsilon$-

$$U_\epsilon = \{y \in \mathbb{R}^n : \operatorname{dist}(y, U) \le \epsilon\} =
 \{y : \|\Pi_{U^\perp} y\| \le \epsilon\}.$$
Then $P(Y \in U_\epsilon) = P(\|\Pi_{U^\perp} Y\| \le \epsilon) > 0$, so conditi

$$\mathcal{L}(\Pi_U Y \mid Y \in U_\epsilon) = \mathcal{L}(\Pi_U Y) = \text{stan

Meanwhile, $\mathcal{L}(\Pi_{U^\perp} Y \mid Y \in U_\epsilon)$ is the standard
normal on $U^\perp$ truncated to the ball of radius $\epsilon$, which concentrat

**Step 4: Limit.** Since $\Pi_U Y$ and $\Pi_{U^\perp} Y$ remain independent unde

- the standard normal on $U$ (unchanged), and
- the truncated standard normal on $U^\perp$ in the $\epsilon$-ball.
