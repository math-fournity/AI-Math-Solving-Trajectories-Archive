## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle I = \int_{|z|=\pi/4}\left(1+z+\frac{1}{\tan z}\right)
dz$ using the residue theorem.

### Step 1: Identify the singularities inside the contour

The integrand is $f(z) = 1 + z + \dfrac{1}{\tan z} = 1 + z + \dfrac{\cos z}{\sin
 z}$.

- The term $1 + z$ is entire (no singularities).
- The term $\dfrac{\cos z}{\sin z}$ has singularities where $\sin z = 0$, i.e.,
at $z = n\pi,\ n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

The contour is $|z| = \pi/4$. Since $\pi/4 < \pi$, the only singularity of $\sin
 z$ inside the contour is $z = 0$ (as $|\pm\pi| = \pi > \pi/4$, etc.).

### Step 2: Compute the residue at $z = 0$

At $z = 0$: $\sin z$ has a simple zero ($\sin z \sim z$ near $0$), and $\cos 0 =
 1 \neq 0$, so $\dfrac{\cos z}{\sin z}$ has a **simple pole** at $z = 0$.

For a quotient $\dfrac{g(z)}{h(z)}$ where $h$ has a simple zero at $z_0$ and $g(
z_0) \neq 0$:

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_0}\frac{g(z)}{h(z)} = \frac{g(z_0)}{h'(z_0)}$$

Here $g(z) = \cos z$, $h(z) = \sin z$, $h'(z) = \cos z$. Thus:

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{\cos z}{\sin z} = \frac{\cos 0}{\cos 0} = \frac{
1}{1} = 1$$

Since $1 + z$ is holomorphic, it contributes $0$ to the residue. Therefore:

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z) = 1$$

### Step 3: Apply the residue theorem

The contour $|z| = \pi/4$ is traversed counterclockwise, enclosing exactly one s
ingularity at $z = 0$ with residue $1$:

$$I = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z) = 2\pi i \cdot 1 = \boxed{2\pi
i}$$
