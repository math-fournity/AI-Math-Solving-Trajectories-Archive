## Proof

**Claim:** Such a sequence exists.

**Notation.** Let $S_k = a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_k$ (with $S_0 = 0$). At step $k$
, define $r_k \equiv -S_{k-1} \pmod{k}$, so the divisibility condition $k \mid S
_k$ is equivalent to $a_k \equiv r_k \pmod{k}$.

### Construction

We build $a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots$ inductively. At each step $k \geq 1$:

1. Let $n$ be the smallest natural number not yet used.
2. **If $n \equiv r_k \pmod{k}$:** Set $a_k = n$.
3. **If $n \not\equiv r_k \pmod{k}$:** Choose $a_k$ to be any unused natural num
ber satisfying the simultaneous congruences
$$a_k \equiv r_k \pmod{k}, \qquad a_k \equiv -(S_{k-1} + n) \pmod{k+1}.$$

**Feasibility of Step 3.** Since $\gcd(k, k+1) = 1$, the Chinese Remainder Theor
em guarantees a unique residue class modulo $k(k+1)$ satisfying both congruences
. This class contains infinitely many positive integers, while only $k-1$ number
s have been used. Hence infinitely many unused candidates exist.

### Verification

**Divisibility.** In both cases, $a_k \equiv r_k \equiv -S_{k-1} \pmod{k}$, so $
S_k = S_{k-1} + a_k \equiv 0 \pmod{k}$. ✓

**Injectivity.** We always select $a_k$ from the unused numbers. ✓

**Key inequality in Case 3: $a_k > n$.** Since $n$ is the smallest unused number
 and $a_k$ is unused, $a_k \geq n$. But $a_k \equiv r_k \pmod{k}$ while $n \not\
equiv r_k \pmod{k}$, so $a_k \neq n$, giving $a_k > n$.

**Surjectivity.** We prove by strong induction on $m$ that every natural number
$m$ is eventually placed.

*Base case ($m = 1$):* At $k = 1$, every integer satisfies $n \equiv 0 \pmod{1}$
, so $r_1 = 0$ and the smallest unused number $n = 1$ satisfies $n \equiv r_1 \p
mod{1}$. Thus $a_1 = 1$.

*Inductive step:* Assume all natural numbers less than $m$ are placed by some fi
nite step $K$. Then after step $K$, $m$ is the smallest unused natural number. A
t the next step $k$ where $m$ is the smallest unused:

- **If $m \equiv r_k \pmod{k}$:** Then $a_k = m$ by Case 2. Done.

- **If $m \not\equiv r_k \pmod{k}$:** By Case 3, we choose $a_k$ satisfying $a_k
 \equiv -(S_{k-1} + m) \pmod{k+1}$. Since $a_k > m$ (shown above), $m$ remains t
he smallest unused number at step $k+1$. The residue at step $k+1$ is:
$$r_{k+1} \equiv -S_k = -(S_{k-1} + a_k) \equiv -S_{k-1} + (S_{k-1} + m) = m \pm
od{k+1}.$$
So $m \equiv r_{k+1} \pmod{k+1}$, and $m$ is placed at step $k+1$ by Case 2.

In either case, $m$ is placed within at most 2 steps of becoming the smallest un
used number. By induction, every natural number is eventually placed. ✓

### Conclusion

The constructed sequence is a permutation of the natural numbers (injective and
surjective) satisfying $k \mid S_k$ for all $k \geq 1$. Therefore, such a sequen
ce **exists**. $\blacksquare$
