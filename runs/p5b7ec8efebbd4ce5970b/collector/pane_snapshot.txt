## Solution

Let me transform the equation. Dividing the ODE by $x$ (for $x \neq 0$):

$$f''(x) + 3[f'(x)]^2 = \frac{1 - e^{-x}}{x}.$$

**Key substitution.** Define $F(x) = e^{3f(x)}$. Then
- $F(0) = e^{3f(0)} = 1$,
- $F'(x) = 3f'(x)\,e^{3f(x)} = 3f'(x)F(x)$, so $F'(0) = 3f'(0)F(0) = 0$,
- $F''(x) = 3f''(x)F(x) + 3f'(x)F'(x) = 3F(x)\bigl(f''(x) + 3[f'(x)]^2\bigr)$.

Using the rewritten ODE, this gives the linear equation

$$F''(x) = p(x)\,F(x), \qquad p(x) := \frac{3(1 - e^{-x})}{x}, \qquad F(0)=1,\;
F'(0)=0.$$

The value at $x=0$ is understood by continuity: $\lim_{x\to 0} p(x) = 3$.

**Sign of $p$.** For $x > 0$: $1 - e^{-x} > 0$ and $x > 0$, so $p(x) > 0$. For $
x < 0$: $1 - e^{-x} < 0$ (since $e^{-x} > 1$) and $x < 0$, so $p(x) > 0$. Hence

$$p(x) > 0 \quad \text{for all } x \neq 0, \qquad p(0)=3.$$

**$F$ stays positive and satisfies $F \geq 1$.** Let $(a,b)$ be the maximal open
 interval containing $0$ on which $F > 0$. On $(a,b)$, we have $F'' = pF > 0$, s
o $F$ is strictly convex. Since $F'(0) = 0$, the point $x=0$ is the unique globa
l minimum of $F$ on $(a,b)$, giving $F(x) \geq F(0) = 1 > 0$ throughout $(a,b)$.
 Therefore $F$ cannot approach $0$ at any finite boundary point of $(a,b)$, whic
h forces $(a,b) = (-\infty, +\infty)$. Consequently

$$F(x) \geq 1 \quad \text{for all } x \in \mathbb{R}, \qquad F(0) = 1.$$

**Recovering $f$.** Since $F = e^{3f} > 0$ everywhere,

$$f(x) = \tfrac{1}{3}\ln F(x) \geq \tfrac{1}{3}\ln 1 = 0,$$

with equality exactly at $x = 0$ (where $f(0) = 0$).

**Verification of the minimum.** At $x=0$: $f(0)=0$ and, from $f''(0) + 3[f'(0)]
^2 = \lim_{x\to 0}\frac{1-e^{-x}}{x} = 1$, we get $f''(0) = 1 > 0$, confirming $
x=0$ is a local minimum. The global argument above shows $f(x) \geq 0$ everywher
e, so this local minimum is in fact the global minimum.

$$\boxed{\min_{x} f(x) = 0}$$
