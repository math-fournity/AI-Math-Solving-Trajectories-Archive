## Derivation of the Discrete-Time Tangency (Invariance) Condition

### Setup

Consider the discrete-time map $F:\mathbb{R}^n\times\mathbb{R}^m\to\mathbb{R}^n\
times\mathbb{R}^m$ defined by
$$F(x,y) = \bigl(f(x,y),\; g(x,y)\bigr),$$
and the candidate invariant manifold given as the graph
$$M = \bigl\{(x, h(x)) : x \in \mathbb{R}^n\bigr\}, \qquad h:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mat
hbb{R}^m.$$

### Step 1 — Definition of Invariance

The manifold $M$ is **invariant** under $F$ if $F(M)\subseteq M$; that is, every
 point of $M$ is mapped back into $M$.

Take an arbitrary point $(x, y)\in M$, so $y = h(x)$. Its image under one iterat
ion of the map is
$$F(x, h(x)) = \bigl(f(x, h(x)),\; g(x, h(x))\bigr).$$

For this image to lie in $M$, the second component must equal $h$ evaluated at t
he first component. This gives the **discrete-time tangency (invariance) conditi
on**:

$$\boxed{\;g\bigl(x,\, h(x)\bigr) \;=\; h\!\left(f\bigl(x,\, h(x)\bigr)\right) \
quad \text{for all } x\in\mathbb{R}^n.\;}$$

This is the exact discrete-time analogue of the continuous-time tangency conditi
on $Dh(x)\,F(x,h(x)) = G(x,h(x))$. In continuous time, tangency means the vector
 field lies in the tangent space of the graph; in discrete time, the map sends p
oints to points, so the analogue is the **functional equation** requiring that t
he image of every graph point is again a graph point.

### Step 2 — Geometric Interpretation

The tangent space to the graph $M$ at $(x, h(x))$ is
$$T_{(x,h(x))}M = \bigl\{(v,\, Dh(x)\,v) : v\in\mathbb{R}^n\bigr\}.$$

In continuous time, the vector field $(F, G)$ must lie in this tangent space, yi
elding $G = Dh\, F$. In discrete time there is no vector field to constrain; ins
tead, the map $F$ must send the entire graph into itself, which is precisely the
 functional equation above. The "tangency" is therefore encoded not as a pointwi
se vector condition but as a **global functional relation** between $f$, $g$, an
d $h$.

### Step 3 — Linearized (Differentiated) Form

When $f$, $g$, and $h$ are $C^1$, we may differentiate the invariance condition
with respect to $x$. Applying the chain rule to both sides of $g(x, h(x)) = h(f(
x, h(x)))$:

$$\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}\bigl(x,h(x)\bigr) \;+\; \frac{\partial g}{\parti
al y}\bigl(x,h(x)\bigr)\,Dh(x) \;=\; Dh\!\left(f(x,h(x))\right)\left[\frac{\part
ial f}{\partial x}\bigl(x,h(x)\bigr) \;+\; \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\bigl(x,
h(x)\bigr)\,Dh(x)\right].$$

This is the **linearized invariance equation**. It governs how the Jacobian $Dh$
 is transported under the dynamics and is the direct discrete-time counterpart o
f the continuous-time tangency condition $Dh\,F = G$. It states that the derivat
ive of the map, restricted to the tangent bundle of $M$, maps tangent vectors of
 $M$ to tangent vectors of $M$—i.e., the linearized map preserves the tangent bu
ndle of the graph.

### Step 4 — Differentiability Requirements

The differentiability requirements depend on how far one wishes to push the anal
ysis:

| Requirement | Minimum regularity needed |
|---|---|
| **Functional equation** $g(x,h(x))=h(f(x,h(x)))$ well-defined | $f, g$ at leas
t $C^0$ (continuous); $h$ at least $C^0$ |
| **Linearized invariance equation** (differentiated form) valid | $f, g \in C^1
$ and $h \in C^1$ |
| **Persistence / normally hyperbolic invariant manifold theory** (Hadamard grap
h transform, Fenichel-type results for maps) | $f, g \in C^k$ with $k \geq 1$; $
h$ inherits $C^k$ regularity under normal hyperbolicity |

**Summary of the minimal requirement:** For the tangency condition in its differ
entiated (linearized) form to hold—that is, for the derivative $Dh$ to satisfy t
he transported relation—the map $(f,g)$ and the function $h$ must each be **at l
east $C^1$** (continuously differentiable). The functional equation itself requi
res only continuity, but the tangency interpretation involving tangent spaces an
d the linearized dynamics on the manifold requires first derivatives to exist an
d be continuous.

For higher-order analysis—such as computing normal forms restricted to the invar
iant manifold, or establishing persistence of the manifold under perturbations v
ia the graph transform—one requires $f, g \in C^k$ with $k\geq 1$ (commonly $k \
geq 2$ for persistence theorems), and the invariant manifold function $h$ is the
n guaranteed to be of the same class $C^k$ provided the manifold is normally hyp
erbolic.
