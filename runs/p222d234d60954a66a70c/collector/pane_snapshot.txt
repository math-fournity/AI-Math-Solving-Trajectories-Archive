1. $\mathcal{A}$ is pairwise intersecting (each $S_i \cap S_j \neq \varnothing$
for $1 \le i,j \le k$, so each member is nonempty).
2. Every $B \in \mathcal{B}$ intersects every $A \in \mathcal{A}$ (i.e., $\mathc

4. There is **no** intersection condition within $\mathcal{B}$.

$$k = |\mathcal{B}| \le |H(\mathcal{A})| - |\mathcal{A}| = |H(\mathcal{A})| - k

### Determining $c$

The maximum achievable $k$ for each $n$ is exactly $\lfloor 2^n/3 \rfloor$, so w
e need:
$$c \ge \sup_{n \ge 1} \frac{1000\left\lfloor 2^n/3\right\rfloor}{2^n}.$$

Since $2^n \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$ when $n$ is even and $2^n \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$ when
$n$ is odd:

- **Even $n$:** $\frac{1000\lfloor 2^n/3\rfloor}{2^n} = \frac{1000(2^n - 1)}{3 \

- **Odd $n$:** $\frac{1000\lfloor 2^n/3\rfloor}{2^n} = \frac{1000(2^n - 2)}{3 \c

The supremum is $\frac{1000}{3}$, not attained.
