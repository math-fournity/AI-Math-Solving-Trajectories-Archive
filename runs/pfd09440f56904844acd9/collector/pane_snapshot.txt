Let
$$L = \lim_{x\to\infty} x e^{x^2}\!\left(\int_0^x e^{-t^2}\,dt - \int_0^\infty e
^{-t^2}\,dt\right).$$

Recall the Gaussian integral $\displaystyle\int_0^\infty e^{-t^2}\,dt = \frac{\s
qrt\pi}{2}$. Hence the parenthesized expression is the tail of the integral:
$$\int_0^x e^{-t^2}\,dt - \frac{\sqrt\pi}{2} = -\int_x^\infty e^{-t^2}\,dt.$$

Define $R(x) = \int_x^\infty e^{-t^2}\,dt$. Then
$$L = -\lim_{x\to\infty} x e^{x^2} R(x).$$

**Evaluating $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty} x e^{x^2} R(x)$.**

As $x\to\infty$, both $R(x)\to 0$ and $\dfrac{e^{-x^2}}{x}\to 0$, so we may appl
y L'Hôpital to the quotient $\dfrac{R(x)}{e^{-x^2}/x}$:

$$\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{R(x)}{e^{-x^2}/x} = \lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{R'(x)}{\dfra
c{d}{dx}\!\left(e^{-x^2}/x\right)}.$$

Compute the derivatives:
- $R'(x) = -e^{-x^2}$.
- $\dfrac{d}{dx}\!\left(\dfrac{e^{-x^2}}{x}\right) = \dfrac{-2x\,e^{-x^2}\cdot x
 - e^{-x^2}}{x^2} = -e^{-x^2}\,\dfrac{2x^2+1}{x^2}$.

Therefore
$$\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{R(x)}{e^{-x^2}/x} = \lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{-e^{-x^2}}{-
e^{-x^2}\,\dfrac{2x^2+1}{x^2}} = \lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{x^2}{2x^2+1} = \frac{1}{
2}.$$

Equivalently,
$$\lim_{x\to\infty} x e^{x^2} R(x) = \frac{1}{2}.$$

**Conclusion.** Substituting back,
$$L = -\lim_{x\to\infty} x e^{x^2} R(x) = -\frac{1}{2}.$$

$$\boxed{L = -\dfrac{1}{2}}$$
