## Proof

We determine all total orders on $\mathbb{Q}$ compatible with the field operatio
ns, i.e., orders satisfying:

1. **Translation invariance:** $a \le b \implies a+c \le b+c$ for all $c \in \ma
thbb{Q}$.
2. **Multiplication by non-negatives:** $a \le b,\; 0 \le c \implies ac \le bc$.

Such an order is determined by its **positive cone** $P = \{a \in \mathbb{Q} : a
 > 0\}$, which must satisfy:
- $P$ is closed under $+$ and $\cdot$,
- for every $a \ne 0$, exactly one of $a \in P$ or $-a \in P$,
- $0 \notin P$.

**Key Lemma.** In any field order, every nonzero square is positive.

*Proof.* Let $a \ne 0$. Either $a > 0$ or $-a > 0$ (exactly one). If $a > 0$, th
en $a^2 = a \cdot a > 0$ by closure of $P$ under multiplication. If $-a > 0$, th
en $a^2 = (-a)(-a) > 0$ by the same closure. In either case $a^2 > 0$. $\square$

**Step 1: $1 > 0$ in every field order.**

Since $1 = 1^2$ and $1 \ne 0$, the lemma gives $1 > 0$.

**Step 2: Every positive integer is $> 0$.**

By induction: $1 > 0$, and if $n > 0$ then $n+1 > 0$ (closure of $P$ under addit
ion). Hence $n > 0$ for all $n \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$.

**Step 3: Reciprocals of positive elements are positive.**

Suppose $m > 0$ but $1/m \le 0$. If $1/m < 0$, then $-1/m > 0$, so $m \cdot (-1/
m) = -1 > 0$, contradicting $1 > 0$ (which gives $-1 < 0$). If $1/m = 0$, then $
m \cdot (1/m) = 1 = 0$, contradiction. So $1/m > 0$.

**Step 4: Every positive rational is $> 0$.**

For $n, m \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$: $n > 0$ (Step 2) and $1/m > 0$ (Step 3), so $n/m
 = n \cdot (1/m) > 0$ (closure under multiplication).

**Step 5: The order is forced to be the standard one.**

Let $P$ be the positive cone of any field order on $\mathbb{Q}$. By Step 4, ever
y positive rational (in the usual sense) lies in $P$. Conversely, if some $a \in
 P$ were negative in the usual sense, then $-a$ would be a positive rational, he
nce $-a \in P$ by Step 4 — but then both $a$ and $-a$ are in $P$, contradicting
the trichotomy property of the positive cone. Therefore:

$$P = \{q \in \mathbb{Q} : q > 0 \text{ in the usual sense}\}.$$

Since the positive cone uniquely determines the order, there is **exactly one**
order on $\mathbb{Q}$ compatible with the field operations.

**Conclusion.** The number of orders on $\mathbb{Q}$ compatible with the standar
d field operations is **finite** — in fact, exactly **1**.
