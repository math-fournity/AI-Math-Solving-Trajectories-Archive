We analyze the convergence of
$$I(a) = \int_0^\infty \frac{\sin x}{x^a + x^{2a}}\,dx, \qquad a > 0,$$
by splitting at $x = 1$ and examining the two improper endpoints.

### 1. Behavior near $x = 0$

Factor the denominator:
$$x^a + x^{2a} = x^a(1 + x^a).$$
As $x \to 0^+$, since $a > 0$ we have $x^a \to 0$, so $1 + x^a \to 1$. Using $\s
in x \sim x$,
$$\frac{\sin x}{x^a + x^{2a}} = \frac{\sin x}{x}\cdot \frac{1}{x^{a-1}(1+x^a)} \
;\sim\; \frac{1}{x^{a-1}} = x^{1-a}.$$
The comparison integral $\int_0^1 x^{1-a}\,dx$ converges if and only if $1 - a >
 -1$, i.e. **$a < 2$**.

More rigorously: the function $\frac{\sin x / x}{1 + x^a}$ is continuous and pos
itive on $(0,1]$ with limit $1$ at $0$, so it is bounded between two positive co
nstants on $(0,1]$. Hence $\int_0^1 \frac{\sin x}{x^a+x^{2a}}\,dx$ converges $\i
ff$ $\int_0^1 x^{1-a}\,dx$ converges $\iff$ $a < 2$.

### 2. Behavior near $x = \infty$

Set $h(x) = \dfrac{1}{x^a + x^{2a}}$. For $a > 0$:

- $h(x) > 0$ and $h(x) \to 0$ as $x \to \infty$ (since $x^{2a} \to \infty$).
- $h$ is **monotonically decreasing**: the denominator $g(x) = x^a + x^{2a}$ has
 $g'(x) = a x^{a-1} + 2a x^{2a-1} > 0$ for all $x > 0$, so $g$ is increasing and
 $h = 1/g$ is decreasing.
- The partial integrals $\int_1^N \sin x\,dx = \cos 1 - \cos N$ are bounded (by
$2$).

By **Dirichlet's test** for improper integrals, $\int_1^\infty h(x)\sin x\,dx$ c
onverges for **every** $a > 0$.

### 3. Combining the two conditions

- Near $0$: convergence requires $a < 2$.
- Near $\infty$: convergence holds for all $a > 0$.

Both conditions are satisfied simultaneously precisely when
$$0 < a < 2.$$

### 4. Checking the boundary $a = 2$

At $a = 2$: near $0$, $\dfrac{\sin x}{x^2 + x^4} \sim \dfrac{x}{x^2} = \dfrac{1}
{x}$, and $\int_0^1 \dfrac{dx}{x} = +\infty$. Divergent. ✓ (excluded)

For $a > 2$: near $0$, $\dfrac{\sin x}{x^a+x^{2a}} \sim x^{1-a}$ with $1-a < -1$
, so $\int_0^1 x^{1-a}\,dx$ diverges. ✓ (excluded)

---

**Conclusion.** The integral $\displaystyle\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin x}{x^a + x^{
2a}}\,dx$ converges if and only if

$$\boxed{0 < a < 2}.$$
