## Problem

Let $A$ be a non-unital $C^*$-algebra. Does there exist $a \in A$ and a scalar $
\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$ such that $ab = \lambda b$ for every $b \in A$?

## Claim

The only pair $(a,\lambda)$ satisfying $ab = \lambda b$ for all $b \in A$ is the
 trivial pair $a = 0$, $\lambda = 0$. In particular, **no nonzero element** $a \
in A$ can act as scalar left-multiplication on $A$.

## Proof

We split into two cases.

### Case 1: $\lambda \neq 0$

Define $e := \lambda^{-1} a \in A$. The identity $ab = \lambda b$ becomes

$$
eb = b \qquad \text{for all } b \in A,
$$

so $e$ is a **left identity** for $A$. We show that in a $C^*$-algebra every lef
t identity is automatically a two-sided unit, contradicting the hypothesis that
$A$ is non-unital.

Taking adjoints of $eb = b$ (valid for all $b$) gives

$$
b^* e^* = b^* \qquad \text{for all } b \in A.
$$

Since the map $b \mapsto b^*$ is a bijection of $A$, writing $c = b^*$ yields

$$
c\, e^* = c \qquad \text{for all } c \in A,
$$

so $e^*$ is a **right identity**. Now use both facts on the single element $e^*$
:

- $e$ is a left identity, applied to $e^*$: $\;e\,e^* = e^*$.
- $e^*$ is a right identity, applied to $e$: $\;e\,e^* = e$.

Hence $e = e^*$, i.e. $e$ is self-adjoint. Being a left identity and self-adjoin
t, $e$ is also a right identity (since $be = b$ follows from $b^* e = b^*$, i.e.
 taking adjoint of "$e$ is left identity applied to $b^*$"). Thus $e$ is a two-s
ided unit for $A$.

This contradicts the assumption that $A$ is **non-unital**. Therefore $\lambda \
neq 0$ is impossible.

### Case 2: $\lambda = 0$

The condition becomes

$$
ab = 0 \qquad \text{for all } b \in A,
$$

i.e. $a$ is a left annihilator of $A$. Specializing to $b = a^* \in A$ gives

$$
a\,a^* = 0.
$$

By the $C^*$-identity $\|x\|^2 = \|x^*x\|$ (equivalently $\|xx^*\| = \|x\|^2$),
we obtain

$$
\|a\|^2 = \|a\,a^*\| = \|0\| = 0,
$$

so $\|a\| = 0$ and consequently $a = 0$.

### Conclusion

Combining both cases:

- $\lambda \neq 0$ forces $A$ to be unital — contradiction.
- $\lambda = 0$ forces $a = 0$.

Hence the **unique** solution is $a = 0$ with $\lambda = 0$. No nonzero element
of a non-unital $C^*$-algebra can act as scalar left-multiplication on the whole
 algebra. $\blacksquare$
