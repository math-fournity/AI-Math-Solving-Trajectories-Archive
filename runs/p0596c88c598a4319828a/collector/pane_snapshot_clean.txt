## Setup

Since AB = AC, the triangle is isosceles with axis of symmetry through A and the
 circumcenter O. Place O at the origin with the axis of symmetry along the y-axi
s, and A = (0, 2) on Γ (radius 2).

Then B = (−s, t) and C = (s, t) with s² + t² = 4.

## Locating circle Ω

The center of Ω lies on the angle bisector of ∠A (the y-axis). Since Ω has radiu
s 1 and is internally tangent to Γ (radius 2), its center is at distance 2 − 1 =
 1 from O, so it's at (0, 1) or (0, −1).

**Trying (0, 1):** The distance from (0, 1) to line AB equals 1 gives s/√((t−2)²
 + s²) = 1, which forces (t−2)² = 0, i.e., t = 2, making the triangle degenerate
. Rejected.

**Trying (0, −1):** The line through A = (0,2) and B = (−s, t) has equation (t−2
)x + s(y−2) = 0. The distance from (0, −1) is:

$$\frac{|s(-1-2)|}{\sqrt{(t-2)^2+s^2}} = \frac{3s}{\sqrt{(t-2)^2+s^2}} = 1$$

This gives 9s² = (t−2)² + s², so **8s² = (t−2)²**.

## Solving the system

Substituting s² = 4 − t²:

$$8(4 - t^2) = t^2 - 4t + 4$$
$$32 - 8t^2 = t^2 - 4t + 4$$
$$9t^2 + 4t - 28 = 0$$

By the quadratic formula: t = (−4 ± 32)/18, giving t = 14/9 (the other root t =
−2 is degenerate).

Then s² = 4 − 196/81 = 128/81, so s = 8√2/9.

## Verifying tangency to segments

## Computing the area

- Base BC = 16√2/9

$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{16\sqrt{2}}{9} \cdot \frac{4}{9} = \frac
