## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. Such an extension exists.

**Setup.** Since $X$ is compact Hausdorff, $X$ is normal. The set $\overline{A}$
 is closed in $X$, and $X \setminus U$ is closed in $X$ (as $U$ is open). Since
$A \subseteq U$, we have $X \setminus U \subseteq X \setminus A$, hence

$$\overline{A} \cap (X \setminus U) \;\subseteq\; \overline{A} \cap (X \setminus
 A) \;=\; \overline{A} \setminus A.$$

By hypothesis $f = 0$ on $\overline{A} \setminus A$, so

$$f\big|_{\overline{A} \cap (X \setminus U)} = 0. \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Step 1: Define a continuous function on the closed set $F = \overline{A} \cup
(X \setminus U)$.**

The set $F$ is closed in $X$ (finite union of closed sets). Define $h: F \to [0,
1]$ by

$$h(x) = \begin{cases} f(x), & x \in \overline{A}, \\ 0, & x \in X \setminus U.
\end{cases}$$

This is well-defined by $(\ast)$: the two definitions agree on the overlap $\ove
rline{A} \cap (X \setminus U)$.

Both $\overline{A}$ and $X \setminus U$ are closed in $X$, hence closed in the s
ubspace $F$. The restriction $h|_{\overline{A}} = f$ is continuous (given), and
$h|_{X \setminus U} \equiv 0$ is continuous. By the **pasting (gluing) lemma**,
$h: F \to [0,1]$ is continuous.

**Step 2: Apply the Tietze extension theorem.**

$X$ is normal, $F \subseteq X$ is closed, and $h: F \to [0,1]$ is continuous. By
 the Tietze extension theorem, there exists a continuous function $\tilde{f}: X
\to [0,1]$ with

$$\tilde{f}\big|_{F} = h.$$

**Step 3: Verify the required properties.**

- *Extends $f$:* For $x \in \overline{A} \subseteq F$, $\tilde{f}(x) = h(x) = f(
x)$.
- *Vanishes outside $U$:* For $x \in X \setminus U \subseteq F$, $\tilde{f}(x) =
 h(x) = 0$.
- *Continuous on $X$:* Guaranteed by Tietze; moreover $\tilde{f}$ takes values i
n $[0,1]$.

Therefore $f$ can be extended to a continuous function $\tilde{f}: X \to [0,1]$
that agrees with $f$ on $\overline{A}$ and vanishes on $X \setminus U$. $\blacks
quare$
