## $\mathbb{Z}_2$-Grading of the Clifford Algebra $\mathcal{C}(n,m)$

### Setup

Let $V$ be a real vector space of dimension $n+m$ equipped with a non-degenerate
 symmetric bilinear form $g$ of signature $(n,m)$, meaning $g$ has $n$ positive
and $m$ negative eigenvalues. The **Clifford algebra** $\mathcal{C}(n,m) = \math
rm{Cl}(V,g)$ is the associative algebra generated over $\mathbb{R}$ by $V$ subje
ct to the defining relation
$$
vw + wv = 2\,g(v,w)\cdot 1, \qquad \forall\, v,w \in V.
$$
Equivalently, for an orthonormal basis $\{e_1,\dots,e_{n+m}\}$ with $g(e_i,e_j)=
\eta_{ij}$ (where $\eta_{ii}=+1$ for $i\le n$ and $\eta_{ii}=-1$ for $i>n$),
$$
e_i e_j + e_j e_i = 2\,\eta_{ij}\,\mathbf{1}.
$$
In particular $e_i^2 = \eta_{ii}$. A general basis of $\mathcal{C}(n,m)$ as a ve
ctor space is given by the ordered products
$$
e_{i_1}e_{i_2}\cdots e_{i_k}, \qquad 1\le i_1 < i_2 < \cdots < i_k \le n+m,\quad
 0\le k\le n+m,
$$
so $\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{C}(n,m) = 2^{n+m}$.

### Definition of the $\mathbb{Z}_2$-Grading

Define the **parity map** (also called the *main involution* or *grade involutio
n*)
$$
\alpha : \mathcal{C}(n,m) \longrightarrow \mathcal{C}(n,m)
$$
as the unique algebra automorphism determined by
$$
\alpha(v) = -v, \qquad \forall\, v \in V.
$$
Since $\alpha$ is an automorphism and every element of $\mathcal{C}(n,m)$ is a l
inear combination of products of elements of $V$, on a basis monomial $e_{i_1}\c
dots e_{i_k}$ it acts as
$$
\alpha(e_{i_1}\cdots e_{i_k}) = (-1)^k\, e_{i_1}\cdots e_{i_k}.
$$
One verifies $\alpha^2 = \mathrm{id}$, so $\alpha$ has eigenvalues $\pm 1$. This
 gives the eigenspace decomposition
$$
\mathcal{C}(n,m) = \mathcal{C}^{\bar 0}(n,m) \oplus \mathcal{C}^{\bar 1}(n,m),
$$
where $\bar 0, \bar 1$ are the two elements of $\mathbb{Z}_2 = \{0,1\}/\!\sim$,
and
$$
\mathcal{C}^{\bar 0}(n,m) = \{x \in \mathcal{C}(n,m) : \alpha(x)=x\}, \qquad
\mathcal{C}^{\bar 1}(n,m) = \{x \in \mathcal{C}(n,m) : \alpha(x)=-x\}.
$$

**Explicit description in terms of the basis.** Let $\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(n,m)$ den
ote the linear span of all $k$-fold products $e_{i_1}\cdots e_{i_k}$ with $i_1<\
cdots<i_k$ (the *grade-$k$ subspace*, $\dim \mathcal{C}^{(k)} = \binom{n+m}{k}$)
. Then:
$$
\mathcal{C}^{\bar 0}(n,m) = \bigoplus_{\substack{k=0\\k\text{ even}}}^{n+m} \mat
hcal{C}^{(k)}(n,m), \qquad
\mathcal{C}^{\bar 1}(n,m) = \bigoplus_{\substack{k=1\\k\text{ odd}}}^{n+m} \math
cal{C}^{(k)}(n,m).
$$
That is, $\mathcal{C}^{\bar 0}$ is the span of all **even-grade** monomials (inc
luding the scalar $\mathbf{1}=e_{i_1}\cdots e_{i_0}$ at grade $0$), and $\mathca
l{C}^{\bar 1}$ is the span of all **odd-grade** monomials.

### Verification of the Grading Axiom

To confirm this is a genuine $\mathbb{Z}_2$-grading of the algebra, we must show
$$
\mathcal{C}^{\bar i}\cdot \mathcal{C}^{\bar j} \subseteq \mathcal{C}^{\overline{
i+j}}, \qquad i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_2.
$$
Since $\alpha$ is an **algebra automorphism**, for $x\in\mathcal{C}^{\bar i}$, $
y\in\mathcal{C}^{\bar j}$:
$$
\alpha(xy) = \alpha(x)\alpha(y) = (-1)^i(-1)^j\, xy = (-1)^{i+j}\,xy,
$$
so $xy\in\mathcal{C}^{\overline{i+j}}$. Concretely: the product of two even-grad
e monomials is even, the product of two odd-grade monomials is even, and the pro
duct of an even with an odd is odd. (When multiplying monomials one uses the Cli
fford relations to reorder indices; each swap introduces a sign, but the **total
 number** of generators in the resulting reduced monomial has parity equal to $k
+\ell \pmod 2$ for grades $k,\ell$, since the relations $e_i^2=\pm 1$ remove gen
erators in pairs.)

### Dimensions

$$
\dim \mathcal{C}^{\bar 0}(n,m) = \sum_{k\text{ even}}\binom{n+m}{k} = 2^{n+m-1},
 \qquad
\dim \mathcal{C}^{\bar 1}(n,m) = \sum_{k\text{ odd}}\binom{n+m}{k} = 2^{n+m-1},
$$
using the binomial identity $\sum_{k\text{ even}}\binom{N}{k}=\sum_{k\text{ odd}
}\binom{N}{k}=2^{N-1}$ for $N=n+m\ge 1$.

### The Even Subalgebra

$\mathcal{C}^{\bar 0}(n,m)$ is closed under multiplication (since $\bar 0+\bar 0
=\bar 0$) and thus forms a **subalgebra**, the *even Clifford algebra*. It is it
self isomorphic to a Clifford algebra of lower dimension:
$$
\mathcal{C}^{\bar 0}(n,m) \cong \mathcal{C}(n,m-1) \cong \mathcal{C}(n-1,m),
$$
(the two isomorphisms hold depending on conventions; the key point is the even s
ubalgebra of $\mathcal{C}(p,q)$ is isomorphic to $\mathcal{C}(p,q-1)$ when $q\ge
 1$, and to $\mathcal{C}(q,p-1)$ in the alternative signature). This is seen by
fixing a unit vector $e_{n+m}$ and mapping a generator $v\in V$ to the bivector
$v\,e_{n+m}\in\mathcal{C}^{\bar 0}$, which satisfies the Clifford relations of t
he reduced space.

$\mathcal{C}^{\bar 1}(n,m)$ is **not** a subalgebra (it is not closed under mult
iplication: odd $\times$ odd $=$ even), but it is a **module** over $\mathcal{C}
^{\bar 0}(n,m)$.

### Summary

The $\mathbb{Z}_2$-grading of $\mathcal{C}(n,m)$ is the decomposition
$$
\boxed{\mathcal{C}(n,m) = \mathcal{C}^{\bar 0}(n,m)\oplus\mathcal{C}^{\bar 1}(n,
m)}
$$
induced by the grade involution $\alpha$ ($\alpha|_V=-\mathrm{id}$), where $\mat
hcal{C}^{\bar 0}$ (resp. $\mathcal{C}^{\bar 1}$) is the span of all even-grade (
resp. odd-grade) basis monomials. The multiplication respects the grading: $\mat
hcal{C}^{\bar i}\mathcal{C}^{\bar j}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\overline{i+j}}$. The
even part $\mathcal{C}^{\bar 0}$ is a subalgebra (isomorphic to a Clifford algeb
ra in one fewer dimension), while the odd part $\mathcal{C}^{\bar 1}$ is a modul
e over it. Both parts have dimension $2^{n+m-1}$.
