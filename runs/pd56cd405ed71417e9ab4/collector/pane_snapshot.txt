## Relationship between VC dimension and Littlestone dimension

**General relationship.** For every hypothesis class $\mathcal{H}\subseteq\{0,1\
}^{\mathcal X}$,
$$
\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal H)\;\le\;\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal H).
$$

**Proof of the inequality.** Recall that a Littlestone tree of depth $d$ is a fu
ll binary tree of depth $d$ whose internal nodes are labelled by points of $\mat
hcal X$; it is *shattered* by $\mathcal H$ if for every root-to-leaf path $v_1,\
dots,v_d$ with labels $x_{v_1},\dots,x_{v_d}$ and every bit sequence $b_1,\dots,
b_d\in\{0,1\}$ there exists $h\in\mathcal H$ with $h(x_{v_i})=b_i$ for all $i$.
The Littlestone dimension is the largest $d$ for which some depth-$d$ tree is sh
attered.

Suppose $\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal H)\ge d$, and let $S=\{x_1,\dots,x_d\}$ b
e a set shattered by $\mathcal H$ (every one of the $2^d$ labelings of $S$ is re
alized by some $h\in\mathcal H$). Build a tree of depth $d$ in which **every** n
ode at level $i$ is labelled by the same point $x_i$. For any path $(b_1,\dots,b
_d)$, the required condition $h(x_i)=b_i$ for $i=1,\dots,d$ is exactly a labelin
g of $S$, which is realized because $S$ is shattered. Hence this tree is shatter
ed, so $\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal H)\ge d$. Taking $d=\operatorname{VCdim}(\m
athcal H)$ (and, when $\operatorname{VCdim}=\infty$, taking arbitrarily large $d
$) gives
$$
\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal H)\le\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal H). \qquad\black
square
$$

## The inequality can be strict

Yes. The gap can be arbitrarily large, even infinite.

**Example (thresholds on the real line).** Let $\mathcal X=\mathbb R$ and
$$
\mathcal H=\{\,h_\theta(x)=\mathbf 1[x\le\theta]\;:\;\theta\in\mathbb R\,\}.
$$

**VC dimension equals $1$.** A single point is shattered (label it $1$ by choosi
ng $\theta\ge x$, label it $0$ by choosing $\theta<x$). No two-point set $\{a,b\
}$ with $a<b$ is shattered: the labeling $h(a)=0,\,h(b)=1$ would require $a>\the
ta$ and $b\le\theta$, i.e. $b\le\theta<a<b$, a contradiction. Hence $\operatorna
me{VCdim}(\mathcal H)=1$.

**Littlestone dimension equals $\infty$.** We show by induction on $d$ that $\ma
thcal H$ shatters a Littlestone tree of depth $d$ for every $d\ge 1$. The key ob
servation: a depth-$d$ tree is shattered by thresholds on a linear order $(X,<)$
 iff $X$ contains a strictly increasing chain of $2^d-1$ points.

*Base case $d=1$:* a single node labelled $x_1$ is shattered because $\mathbb R$
 has points both below and above $x_1$ (paths $0$ and $1$ realized by $\theta<x_
1$ and $\theta\ge x_1$).

*Inductive step:* assume depth-$(d-1)$ trees are shattered whenever there is a c
hain of $2^{d-1}-1$ points. Pick a root $x_1\in\mathbb R$. The left subtree is t
raversed only when $h(x_1)=0$, i.e. under the constraint $\theta<x_1$; by the in
duction hypothesis applied to the set $\{x\in\mathbb R:x<x_1\}$ (which contains
chains of every finite length), the left subtree is shattered using a chain of $
2^{d-1}-1$ points all $<x_1$. The right subtree is traversed only when $h(x_1)=1
$, i.e. $\theta\ge x_1$; applying the induction hypothesis to $\{x\in\mathbb R:x
\ge x_1\}$ shatters it using a chain of $2^{d-1}-1$ points all $\ge x_1$. Concat
enating these two chains with the root yields a chain of $(2^{d-1}-1)+1+(2^{d-1}
-1)=2^d-1$ points, and the resulting depth-$d$ tree is shattered.

Since $\mathbb R$ contains chains of every finite length, $\mathcal H$ shatters
a tree of depth $d$ for every $d$, so
$$
\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal H)=\infty.
$$

**Conclusion.** For this class,
$$
\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal H)=1\;<\;\infty=\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal H),
$$
so the inequality $\operatorname{VCdim}\le\operatorname{Ldim}$ is strict — indee
d, the Littlestone dimension can be infinite while the VC dimension is as small
as $1$.

*(A finite version of the same phenomenon: thresholds on $\{1,2,\dots,2^k-1\}$ h
ave $\operatorname{VCdim}=1$ and $\operatorname{Ldim}=k$, giving a strict gap of
 any desired finite size.)*
