## Answer

**Yes**, every prime ideal of $C(X, \mathbb{R})$ is maximal.

### Proof

**Step 1: The topology on $X$ is discrete.**

Since $X$ is a finite metric space, for each $x \in X$ set
$$r_x = \tfrac{1}{2}\min\{d(x,y) : y \in X,\; y \neq x\}$$
(with $r_x = 1$ if $X = \{x\}$). Then the open ball $B(x, r_x) = \{x\}$, so ever
y singleton is open. Hence $X$ carries the discrete topology.

**Step 2: Identify the ring.**

Because $X$ is discrete, every function $f: X \to \mathbb{R}$ is continuous. Thu
s
$$C(X, \mathbb{R}) = \{f : X \to \mathbb{R}\} \cong \mathbb{R}^{|X|}.$$
Let $n = |X|$; we work with $R = \mathbb{R}^n = \underbrace{\mathbb{R} \times \c
dots \times \mathbb{R}}_{n}$.

**Step 3: Classify the ideals of $\mathbb{R}^n$.**

Since $\mathbb{R}^n$ is a finite product of fields, every ideal is a product of
ideals of the factors, and the only ideals of $\mathbb{R}$ are $\{0\}$ and $\mat
hbb{R}$. Therefore every ideal of $\mathbb{R}^n$ has the form
$$I_S = \{(a_1,\dots,a_n) \in \mathbb{R}^n : a_i = 0 \text{ for all } i \in S\}$
$
for some subset $S \subseteq \{1,\dots,n\}$ (where $J_i = 0$ for $i \in S$ and $
J_i = \mathbb{R}$ for $i \notin S$).

**Step 4: Determine which ideals are prime.**

The quotient is
$$\mathbb{R}^n / I_S \;\cong\; \mathbb{R}^{|S|},$$
via projection onto the coordinates indexed by $S$. Now $I_S$ is prime $\iff$ $\
mathbb{R}^n/I_S$ is an integral domain $\iff$ $\mathbb{R}^{|S|}$ is an integral
domain $\iff$ $|S| \leq 1$.

- **$|S| = 0$:** $I_S = \mathbb{R}^n$, the whole ring — not a proper ideal, henc
e not prime.
- **$|S| = 1$:** $\mathbb{R}^n/I_S \cong \mathbb{R}$, a field, so $I_S$ is **max
imal** (and prime).
- **$|S| \geq 2$:** $\mathbb{R}^{|S|}$ has zero divisors, e.g. $(1,0,\dots,0)\cd
ot(0,1,0,\dots,0) = (0,\dots,0)$, so $I_S$ is **not prime**.

**Conclusion.**

The prime ideals of $C(X,\mathbb{R}) \cong \mathbb{R}^n$ are exactly the ideals
$I_S$ with $|S|=1$, and these are precisely the maximal ideals. Hence **every pr
ime ideal of $C(X, \mathbb{R})$ is maximal**. $\blacksquare$

*(Remark: equivalently, $\mathbb{R}^n$ is an Artinian ring of Krull dimension $0
$, and in any zero-dimensional ring every prime ideal is maximal.)*
