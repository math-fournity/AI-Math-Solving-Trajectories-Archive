I'll solve all three surface integrals directly.

## a) $\iint_{\sigma}|x|\,dS$, where $\sigma: x^2+y^2+z^2=1,\ z\geqslant 0$ (upp
er hemisphere)

**Parametrization.** Use spherical coordinates on the unit sphere:
$$x=\sin\varphi\cos\theta,\quad y=\sin\varphi\sin\theta,\quad z=\cos\varphi,\qua
d \varphi\in[0,\pi/2],\ \theta\in[0,2\pi],\quad dS=\sin\varphi\,d\varphi\,d\thet
a.$$

Then $|x|=\sin\varphi\,|\cos\theta|$, and the integral separates:
$$I=\int_0^{2\pi}|\cos\theta|\,d\theta\cdot\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^2\varphi\,d\varphi
.$$

**Evaluate each factor:**
- $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}|\cos\theta|\,d\theta=4\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos\theta\,d\
theta=4.$
- $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^2\varphi\,d\varphi=\frac{\pi}{4}.$

Therefore
$$\boxed{I_a=4\cdot\frac{\pi}{4}=\pi.}$$

## b) $\iint_{\sigma}(x^2+y^2)\,dS$, where $\sigma: x^2+y^2=2z,\ z=1$

The surface is the paraboloid $z=\tfrac{1}{2}(x^2+y^2)$ truncated by the plane $
z=1$, i.e. over the disk $x^2+y^2\leqslant 2$.

**Surface element.** With $z=\tfrac{1}{2}(x^2+y^2)$:
$$\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}=x,\quad \frac{\partial z}{\partial y}=y,\quad dS
=\sqrt{1+x^2+y^2}\,dx\,dy.$$

**Set up in polar** ($r^2=x^2+y^2$, $dx\,dy=r\,dr\,d\theta$, $0\leqslant r\leqsl
ant\sqrt{2}$):
$$I_b=\int_0^{2\pi}d\theta\int_0^{\sqrt{2}} r^2\sqrt{1+r^2}\,r\,dr=2\pi\int_0^{\
sqrt{2}}r^3\sqrt{1+r^2}\,dr.$$

**Substitution** $u=1+r^2$, $du=2r\,dr$, $r^2=u-1$, $r^3\,dr=\tfrac{1}{2}(u-1)\,
du$; limits $u:1\to 3$:
$$I_b=2\pi\cdot\frac{1}{2}\int_1^3(u-1)\,u^{1/2}\,du=\pi\int_1^3\!\bigl(u^{3/2}-
u^{1/2}\bigr)\,du.$$

$$=\pi\Bigl[\tfrac{2}{5}u^{5/2}-\tfrac{2}{3}u^{3/2}\Bigr]_1^3=\pi\Bigl[\tfrac{2}
{5}(9\sqrt3)-\tfrac{2}{3}(3\sqrt3)-\tfrac{2}{5}+\tfrac{2}{3}\Bigr].$$

$$=\pi\Bigl[\tfrac{18\sqrt3}{5}-2\sqrt3+\tfrac{4}{15}\Bigr]=\pi\Bigl[\tfrac{8\sq
rt3}{5}+\tfrac{4}{15}\Bigr]=\pi\cdot\frac{24\sqrt3+4}{15}.$$

Therefore
$$\boxed{I_b=\frac{4\pi}{15}\bigl(6\sqrt3+1\bigr).}$$

## c) $\iint_{\sigma}(x^2+y^2+z^2)\,dS$, where $\sigma$ is the cone $z^2=x^2+y^2
,\ z\geqslant 0$, cut by the cylinder $x^2+y^2-2x=0$

**Simplify the integrand on the cone.** Since $z^2=x^2+y^2$ on $\sigma$:
$$x^2+y^2+z^2=2(x^2+y^2)=2r^2.$$

**Surface element of the cone** $z=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}=r$:
$$\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}=\frac{x}{r},\quad \frac{\partial z}{\partial y}=
\frac{y}{r},\quad dS=\sqrt{1+\frac{x^2+y^2}{r^2}}\,dx\,dy=\sqrt{2}\,dx\,dy=\sqrt
{2}\,r\,dr\,d\theta.$$

**Region in polar.** The cylinder $x^2+y^2-2x=0$ becomes $r^2-2r\cos\theta=0$, i
.e. $r=2\cos\theta$, valid for $\cos\theta\geqslant 0$, so $\theta\in[-\pi/2,\pi
/2]$.

**Compute:**
$$I_c=\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\!\!d\theta\int_0^{2\cos\theta}2r^2\cdot\sqrt{2}\,r\,
dr=2\sqrt{2}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\!\!d\theta\int_0^{2\cos\theta}r^3\,dr.$$

$$=2\sqrt{2}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\frac{(2\cos\theta)^4}{4}\,d\theta=2\sqrt{2}\cd
ot 4\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos^4\theta\,d\theta=8\sqrt{2}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\co
s^4\theta\,d\theta.$$

**Use the standard integral** $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos^4\theta\,d\theta=
\frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}=\frac{3\pi}{16}$, so by evenness
$$\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos^4\theta\,d\theta=2\cdot\frac{3\pi}{16}=\frac{3\pi}{8
}.$$

Therefore
$$\boxed{I_c=8\sqrt{2}\cdot\frac{3\pi}{8}=3\sqrt{2}\,\pi.}$$
