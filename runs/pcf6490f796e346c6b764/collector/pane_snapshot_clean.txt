## Solution

Let $U = X - Y$ and $V = X + Y$. Since $X, Y \stackrel{\text{iid}}{\sim} \mathca
l{N}(0,1)$, the pair $(U, V)$ is jointly Gaussian with
$$\operatorname{Cov}(U, V) = \operatorname{Var}(X) - \operatorname{Var}(Y) = 1 -
 1 = 0.$$
For jointly Gaussian variables, zero covariance implies **independence**, so $U
\perp V$, with $U, V \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 2)$.

Inverting,
$$X = \frac{U+V}{2}, \qquad Y = \frac{V-U}{2}.$$

Rewrite the integrand:
$$X^2 Y + X Y^2 = XY(X+Y) = XY \cdot V.$$

Compute $XY$:
$$XY = \frac{(U+V)(V-U)}{4} = \frac{V^2 - U^2}{4}.$$

Hence
$$XY \cdot V = \frac{V^2 - U^2}{4}\cdot V = \frac{V^3 - U^2 V}{4}.$$

Now condition on $U = X - Y$:
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{V^3 - U^2 V}{4}\;\Big|\; U\right] = \frac{1}{4}\Big(\m
athbb{E}[V^3 \mid U] - U^2\,\mathbb{E}[V \mid U]\Big).$$

Since $U$ and $V$ are independent, conditioning on $U$ does nothing to $V$:
- $\mathbb{E}[V \mid U] = \mathbb{E}[V] = 0$,
- $\mathbb{E}[V^3 \mid U] = \mathbb{E}[V^3] = 0$ (the third moment of a zero-mea
n Gaussian vanishes).

Therefore
$$\mathbb{E}(X^2 Y + X Y^2 \mid X - Y) = \frac{1}{4}\big(0 - U^2 \cdot 0\big) =
0.$$

$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}(X^2 Y + X Y^2 \mid X - Y) = 0}$$
