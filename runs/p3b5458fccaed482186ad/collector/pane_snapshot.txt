## Proof

Assume \(f\) is holomorphic on the open unit disk \(\mathbb D\) (this is the nat
ural interpretation, since the complex derivative \(f'(0)\) is being discussed).

**Cauchy's estimate.** For any \(0<r<1\), apply the Cauchy integral formula for
the derivative at the origin:

\[
f'(0)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=r}\frac{f(z)}{z^{2}}\,dz.
\]

Taking moduli and using the standard ML-estimate,

\[
|f'(0)|\le \frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot 2\pi r\cdot \frac{\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|}{r^{2}}=\fra
c{M(r)}{r},
\qquad M(r):=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|.
\]

**Using the hypothesis.** The hypothesis \(|f(z)|\le \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-|z|^{2}}}
\) holds for every \(z\in\mathbb D\), so in particular on the circle \(|z|=r\),

\[
M(r)\le \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}.
\]

Hence, for every \(r\in(0,1)\),

\[
|f'(0)|\le \frac{1}{r\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}.
\]

**Optimizing in \(r\).** It remains to minimize the right-hand side over \(r\in(
0,1)\), i.e. to maximize

\[
\phi(r):=r\sqrt{1-r^{2}}.
\]

Squaring, \(\phi(r)^{2}=r^{2}(1-r^{2})=r^{2}-r^{4}\). Setting the derivative to
zero:

\[
\frac{d}{dr}\bigl(r^{2}-r^{4}\bigr)=2r-4r^{3}=2r(1-2r^{2})=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r^{2}=\frac12,\quad r=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}.
\]

At this point,

\[
\phi\!\left(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\right)^{2}=\frac12\left(1-\frac12\right)=\frac14,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
\phi\!\left(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\right)=\frac12.
\]

This is the global maximum of \(\phi\) on \((0,1)\) (it vanishes at the endpoint
s and is positive inside, with a single critical point). Therefore

\[
\min_{0<r<1}\frac{1}{r\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}
=\frac{1}{\phi(1/\sqrt2)}
=\frac{1}{1/2}=2.
\]

**Conclusion.** Since the inequality \(|f'(0)|\le \dfrac{1}{r\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\) h
olds for **every** \(r\in(0,1)\), we may take the infimum over \(r\) and obtain

\[
|f'(0)|\le \inf_{0<r<1}\frac{1}{r\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}=2.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{|f'(0)|\le 2}.
\]
