## Proof

The system is conservative, so it derives from a potential. Write

$$\ddot{x} = x - x^2 = -\frac{dV}{dx}, \qquad V(x) = -\frac{x^{2}}{2} + \frac{x^
{3}}{3}.$$

The conserved energy is

$$E = \frac{1}{2}\dot{x}^{2} + V(x) = \frac{1}{2}\dot{x}^{2} - \frac{x^{2}}{2} +
 \frac{x^{3}}{3}.$$

**Equilibria.** Setting $\ddot{x}=0$ gives $x-x^{2}=0$, i.e. $x=0$ and $x=1$. Th
eir potential values are $V(0)=0$ and $V(1)=-\tfrac12+\tfrac13=-\tfrac16$. Since

$$V''(x) = -1 + 2x, \qquad V''(0)=-1<0, \quad V''(1)=1>0,$$

$x=0$ is a local maximum of $V$ (a saddle/unstable equilibrium in the phase plan
e) and $x=1$ is a local minimum of $V$ (a center). Trajectories with $-\tfrac16
< E < 0$ are closed orbits encircling $(x,\dot{x})=(1,0)$; trajectories with $E>
0$ are unbounded (non-closed). The separatrix between them is the trajectory at
the saddle energy $E=0$, which is precisely the homoclinic orbit: it leaves the
saddle $(0,0)$, loops around, and returns to it.

**Homoclinic orbit.** Setting $E=0$:

$$\frac{1}{2}\dot{x}^{2} - \frac{x^{2}}{2} + \frac{x^{3}}{3} = 0
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\dot{x}^{2} = x^{2} - \frac{2}{3}x^{3} = x^{2}\!\left(1 - \frac{2x}{3}\right).$$

Hence the homoclinic orbit is

$$\boxed{\;\dot{x} = \pm\, x\sqrt{\,1 - \tfrac{2x}{3}\,}\;}$$

with $x\in[0,\tfrac32]$ (the turning point where $\dot{x}=0$ besides $x=0$ is $x
=\tfrac32$). The two signs give the upper and lower branches of the loop, joinin
g at $x=0$ (the saddle, approached as $t\to\pm\infty$) and meeting again at $x=\
tfrac32$.

**Explicit form (verification).** Solving $\dot{x}=x\sqrt{1-2x/3}$ by the substi
tution $u=\sqrt{1-2x/3}$ yields

$$x(t) = \frac{3}{2}\operatorname{sech}^{2}\!\left(\frac{t}{2}\right).$$

Check: $\dot{x} = -\frac{3}{2}\operatorname{sech}^{2}(t/2)\tanh(t/2) = -x\tanh(t
/2)$, so

$$\dot{x}^{2} = x^{2}\tanh^{2}(t/2) = x^{2}\bigl(1-\operatorname{sech}^{2}(t/2)\
bigr) = x^{2}\!\left(1-\frac{2x}{3}\right),$$

confirming $E=0$. Also $x\to0$ as $t\to\pm\infty$, so the orbit is asymptotic to
 the saddle $(0,0)$ in both time directions — a homoclinic loop, as required. It
 separates the closed orbits ($-\tfrac16<E<0$) around the center $(1,0)$ from th
e unbounded trajectories ($E>0$).
