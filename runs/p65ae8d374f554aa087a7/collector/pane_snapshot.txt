# Mean Square Convergence of Orthonormal Series

## Setup

Let $\{\phi_i\}_{i=1}^{\infty}$ be a complete orthonormal system on $[0,1]$ (wit
h respect to the standard $L^2$ inner product), and let $R_n \to 0$ as $n\to\inf
ty$, where $R_n$ is the mean square error of the $n$-th partial sum approximatio
n. For a function $f \in L^2[0,1]$, the generalized Fourier coefficients are

$$a_i = \int_0^1 f(x)\,\phi_i(x)\,dx.$$

## Definition of Mean Square Convergence

The infinite series $\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} a_i \phi_i(x)$ **converges to $f(x)$ in
 the mean square sense** (i.e., in the $L^2$ norm) if

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} R_n \;=\; \lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1 \Bigl|\,f(x) - \sum_{i=1
}^{n} a_i\,\phi_i(x)\Bigr|^2 dx \;=\; 0.$$

Equivalently, denoting $S_n(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i \phi_i(x)$, we require

$$\|f - S_n\|_{L^2} \to 0 \quad \text{as } n\to\infty.$$

This is precisely convergence in the norm of the Hilbert space $L^2[0,1]$.

## Why Mean Square Convergence Holds Here

By the orthonormality of $\{\phi_i\}$, the partial sums $S_n$ are the **best app
roximation** to $f$ in the span of $\{\phi_1,\dots,\phi_n\}$, and Parseval's ide
ntity gives

$$\|f - S_n\|_{L^2}^2 = \|f\|_{L^2}^2 - \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i^2.$$

The hypothesis $R_n \to 0$ is exactly the statement that this remainder vanishes
, i.e.,

$$\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} a_i^2 = \|f\|_{L^2}^2 \qquad \text{(Parseval's identity)},
$$

which is the **completeness** condition on the orthonormal system. Thus the seri
es $\sum a_i \phi_i$ converges to $f$ in the $L^2$ sense.

## Implications Compared to Pointwise Convergence

Mean square convergence is **strictly weaker** than pointwise convergence, and t
he two notions differ in important ways:

**1. No pointwise guarantee.** Mean square convergence does **not** imply that $
S_n(x) \to f(x)$ at any particular point $x$, nor even at any point at all. The
$L^2$ norm only controls the *integrated* squared error; it permits the differen
ce $f - S_n$ to be large on small-measure sets. Formally, convergence in $L^p$ f
or $p<\infty$ does not imply pointwise convergence anywhere without additional h
ypotheses.

**2. Convergence in measure (subsequence).** Mean square convergence *does* impl
y convergence in measure, and hence there exists a subsequence $\{S_{n_k}\}$ tha
t converges to $f$ pointwise almost everywhere. But the full sequence need not c
onverge pointwise a.e.

**3. Energy / Parseval interpretation.** Mean square convergence has a direct ph
ysical interpretation: the "energy" $\|f\|^2$ equals the sum of the squared coef
ficients $\sum a_i^2$. Pointwise convergence has no such energy identity.

**4. Hilbert space structure.** Mean square convergence is convergence in the no
rm of the Hilbert space $L^2[0,1]$, so it respects the inner-product geometry (o
rthogonality, projection, Riesz representation). Pointwise convergence does not
respect this geometry.

**5. Sufficient conditions for pointwise convergence.** To upgrade mean square c
onvergence to pointwise convergence one needs additional regularity. For instanc
e, if $f$ is piecewise smooth, the classical results (Dirichlet–Jordan, Dini, Ca
rleson) give:
   - **Carleson's theorem** (for the trigonometric system): the Fourier series o
f any $f\in L^2$ converges to $f$ pointwise a.e.
   - For general complete orthonormal systems, however, pointwise a.e. convergen
ce can **fail** — there exist complete orthonormal systems whose Fourier series
diverge a.e. for some $f\in L^2$ (counterexamples due to Kolmogorov and later re
finements). So mean square convergence is the "safe" notion that always holds fo
r a complete orthonormal system, while pointwise convergence is system-dependent
 and requires extra structure.

**6. Uniqueness up to a.e. equality.** Mean square convergence determines the li
mit only up to sets of measure zero: if $\sum a_i\phi_i$ converges in mean squar
e to both $f$ and $g$, then $f = g$ a.e. Pointwise convergence, when it holds, i
dentifies values at individual points.

## Summary

The series $\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} a_i \phi_i(x)$ converges to $f$ in the mean squa
re sense precisely when

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1\Bigl|f(x)-\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\phi_i(x)\Bigr|^2 dx = 0,$
$

which, for a complete orthonormal system, is guaranteed by Parseval's identity $
\sum a_i^2 = \|f\|_{L^2}^2$. This $L^2$-norm convergence is weaker than pointwis
e convergence: it controls only the integrated error, does not in general yield
pointwise values, identifies the limit only a.e., and is the natural notion with
in the Hilbert-space framework of $L^2[0,1]$. Pointwise convergence requires add
itional regularity of $f$ and/or special structure of the orthonormal system, an
d may fail for general complete orthonormal systems even though mean square conv
ergence always holds.
