**Claim.** A primitive element of $F = \mathbb{Q}(i,\sqrt{5}+i)$ is
$$k = \sqrt{5}+i.$$

**Proof.** First observe that since both $i$ and $\sqrt{5}+i$ lie in $F$, their
difference $(\sqrt{5}+i)-i = \sqrt{5}$ lies in $F$. Hence
$$F = \mathbb{Q}(i,\sqrt{5}+i) = \mathbb{Q}(i,\sqrt{5}).$$
This is a degree‑$4$ extension of $\mathbb{Q}$: $\mathbb{Q}(i)/\mathbb{Q}$ and $
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})/\mathbb{Q}$ are both quadratic, and $\sqrt{5}\notin\mathbb{
Q}(i)$ (since $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ is imaginary while $\sqrt{5}$ is real), so the two
 quadratic extensions are linearly disjoint and $[F:\mathbb{Q}]=4$.

It suffices to show that $i\in\mathbb{Q}(k)$, for then $\sqrt{5}=k-i\in\mathbb{Q
}(k)$ and consequently $F=\mathbb{Q}(i,\sqrt{5})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(k)\subseteq
F$.

Set $k=\sqrt{5}+i$. Compute powers of $k$:
$$k^{2}=(\sqrt{5}+i)^{2}=5+2i\sqrt{5}+i^{2}=4+2i\sqrt{5},$$
$$k^{3}=k\cdot k^{2}=(\sqrt{5}+i)(4+2i\sqrt{5})=4\sqrt{5}+2i\cdot 5+4i+2i^{2}\sq
rt{5}=2\sqrt{5}+14i.$$

Now form the $\mathbb{Q}$‑linear combination $k^{3}-2k$:
$$k^{3}-2k=(2\sqrt{5}+14i)-2(\sqrt{5}+i)=2\sqrt{5}+14i-2\sqrt{5}-2i=12i.$$

Therefore
$$i=\frac{k^{3}-2k}{12}\in\mathbb{Q}(k),$$
and subsequently
$$\sqrt{5}=k-i=k-\frac{k^{3}-2k}{12}=\frac{14k-k^{3}}{12}\in\mathbb{Q}(k).$$

Thus $\mathbb{Q}(k)$ contains both $i$ and $\sqrt{5}$, giving $F=\mathbb{Q}(i,\s
qrt{5})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(k)$. The reverse inclusion $\mathbb{Q}(k)\subseteq F$
 is immediate since $k=\sqrt{5}+i\in F$. Hence $\mathbb{Q}(k)=F$, and $k=\sqrt{5
}+i$ is a primitive element of $F$. $\blacksquare$

A primitive element of $F=\mathbb{Q}(i,\sqrt{5}+i)$ is $\boxed{\sqrt{5}+i}$.
