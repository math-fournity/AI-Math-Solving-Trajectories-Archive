Looking at this problem, I need to prove that no constant $k$ exists such that e
very finite unit distance graph in $\mathbb{R}^2$ with girth $\geq k$ is 3-color
able. This is O'Donnell's theorem. I'll present a constructive proof using odd s
ubdivision of the Moser spindle combined with a geometric realizability argument
.

---

**Theorem (O'Donnell, 1999).** For every integer $g \geq 3$, there exists a fini
te unit distance graph $G$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ with $\operatorname{girth}(G) \geq
g$ and $\chi(G) \geq 4$. Consequently, there is no $k$ such that every finite un
it distance graph with girth $\geq k$ satisfies $\chi \leq 3$.

**Proof.**

The argument has two parts: a *combinatorial* construction (odd subdivision of a
 4-chromatic unit distance graph preserves chromatic number and multiplies girth
) and a *geometric* realizability argument (unit-distance chains in the plane ar
e flexible enough to realize the subdivision as a unit distance graph).

---

**Step 1: The Moser spindle.**

The Moser spindle $M$ is a unit distance graph on 7 vertices in $\mathbb{R}^2$ w
ith $\chi(M)=4$ and girth $3$. It is built from two copies of the *Moser diamond
* $D \cong K_4 - e$, realized as a unit distance graph: vertices $a,b,c,d$ with
$|ab|=|ac|=|ad|=|bc|=|bd|=1$ (two equilateral triangles sharing edge $ab$), and
$|cd|=\sqrt{3}\neq 1$ (so $cd$ is not an edge).

*Color-forcing property of the diamond:* In any 3-coloring of $D$, vertices $a$
and $b$ receive distinct colors (they are adjacent), say colors $1$ and $2$. The
n $c$, being adjacent to both $a$ and $b$, must receive color $3$; likewise $d$
must receive color $3$. **The two tips $c,d$ of a diamond always receive the sam
e color in any 3-coloring.**

The Moser spindle identifies one tip of each of two diamonds and joins the two r
emaining free tips by a unit-distance edge. Both free tips must share the color
of the identified tip, hence receive the same color — yet they are adjacent, a c
ontradiction. So $M$ is not 3-colorable: $\chi(M)=4$.

In the standard embedding of $M$ (7 vertices, 11 edges), the only vertex pairs a
t distance $1$ are the 11 edges; all $\binom{7}{2}-11 = 10$ non-adjacent pairs a
re at distance $\neq 1$.

---

**Step 2: Odd subdivision preserves chromatic number.**

Let $G$ be any graph and $G'$ the graph obtained by replacing every edge of $G$
by an internally vertex-disjoint path of odd length $L = 2m+1$. Then $\chi(G') =
 \chi(G)$.

*Proof.* Any proper coloring of $G$ extends to $G'$: color each odd path alterna
tely, which forces its endpoints to receive different colors — consistent with t
he original edge. Conversely, any proper coloring of $G'$ restricts to a proper
coloring of $G$, since each odd path forces its endpoints to differ. $\square$

---

**Step 3: Odd subdivision multiplies girth.**

With $G'$ as above, every cycle of length $\ell$ in $G$ becomes a cycle of lengt
h $\ell L$ in $G'$. Since the replacement paths are internally vertex-disjoint a
nd share no edges with each other, no new cycles are created: every cycle in $G'
$ projects to a cycle in $G$. Hence
$$\operatorname{girth}(G') = L \cdot \operatorname{girth}(G).$$

---

**Step 4: Long unit-distance paths between points at distance 1.**

**Lemma.** For any integer $L \geq 2$ and any $d \in [0, L]$, there exists a pat
h of $L$ unit segments in $\mathbb{R}^2$ whose endpoints are at distance $d$.

*Proof.* The configuration space of an open chain of $L$ unit segments is the to
rus $(S^1)^{L-1}$, which is connected. The endpoint distance $f: (S^1)^{L-1} \to
 \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ is continuous. When all segments are aligned, $f = L$; whe
n the chain is folded back on itself (possible for $L \geq 2$), $f$ can be made
$0$. Since $f$ is continuous and its image is a connected subset of $\mathbb{R}$
 containing $0$ and $L$, the image is $[0,L]$. $\square$

In particular, for $d=1$ and any odd $L \geq 3$, a unit-distance path of length
$L$ exists between any two points at distance $1$.

---

**Step 5: Generic positioning avoids unwanted unit-distance adjacencies.**

Fix the standard embedding of $M$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ (7 vertices at fixed positio
ns, 11 edges). For each edge $e = uv$ of $M$, choose a unit-distance path of len
gth $L = 2m+1$ from $u$ to $v$ (existing by the Lemma), introducing $L-1 = 2m$ n
ew internal vertices per edge. The 11 paths are chosen independently, giving a c
onfiguration space
$$\mathcal{C} = (S^1)^{11 \cdot 2m},$$
a connected real-analytic manifold of dimension $22m$.

The positions of all internal vertices are real-analytic functions of the config
uration. For each pair $(p, q)$ of distinct vertices that are *not* consecutive
on any path and *not* an original edge of $M$, the condition $|p - q|^2 = 1$ def
ines a real-analytic subset $Z_{pq} \subset \mathcal{C}$.

**Claim:** Each $Z_{pq}$ is a *proper* subset of $\mathcal{C}$ (i.e., not all of
 $\mathcal{C}$).

*Proof of claim.* The pair $(p,q)$ involves at least one internal vertex whose p
osition varies with the path angles. By varying the angles of the path containin
g that vertex (while maintaining the endpoint distance constraint), the position
 of that vertex moves continuously, and hence $|p-q|$ varies. In particular, one
 can find configurations where $|p-q| \neq 1$. (For pairs of internal vertices o
n the same path, varying the path shape changes their distance; for pairs on dif
ferent paths, varying either path independently changes the distance; for pairs
involving a fixed original vertex, varying the path moves the internal vertex re
lative to the fixed point.) $\square$

A proper real-analytic subset of a connected real-analytic manifold has measure
zero. There are finitely many unwanted pairs (at most $\binom{22m+7}{2}$), so th
e union
$$Z = \bigcup_{(p,q)\text{ unwanted}} Z_{pq}$$
has measure zero in $\mathcal{C}$.

Therefore, for a generic configuration $\theta \in \mathcal{C} \setminus Z$, **n
o unwanted pair of vertices is at distance 1**. The resulting graph $G_m$ has ve
rtex set consisting of the 7 original vertices plus $22m$ internal vertices, and
 edge set consisting of exactly the $11L = 11(2m+1)$ path edges — all of unit le
ngth, with no spurious edges. This is a finite unit distance graph in $\mathbb{R
}^2$.

---

**Step 6: Properties of $G_m$ and conclusion.**

The graph $G_m$ is:
- **Finite unit distance graph in $\mathbb{R}^2$** (Step 5): $7 + 22m$ vertices,
 all edges of length 1.
- **Odd subdivision of $M$**: each of the 11 edges replaced by an internally ver
tex-disjoint path of length $L = 2m+1$ (odd).
- **$\chi(G_m) = \chi(M) = 4$** (Step 2): odd subdivision preserves chromatic nu
mber.
- **$\operatorname{girth}(G_m) = 3L = 3(2m+1)$** (Step 3): girth of $M$ is 3, mu
ltiplied by $L$.

Given any $g \geq 3$, choose $m = \left\lceil \frac{g-3}{6} \right\rceil$, so th
at $3(2m+1) \geq g$. Then $G_m$ is a finite unit distance graph with
$$\operatorname{girth}(G_m) \geq g \quad \text{and} \quad \chi(G_m) = 4 > 3.$$

Since this holds for every $g$, for any proposed $k$ we can set $g = k$ and obta
in a finite unit distance graph with girth $\geq k$ and chromatic number $4$. Th
erefore:

**There is no $k$ such that every finite unit distance graph in $\mathbb{R}^2$ w
ith girth $\geq k$ has chromatic number $\leq 3$.** $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark on the formal statement.** The right-hand side $\exists k,\, \forall V,
\, \operatorname{girth} \geq k \Rightarrow \chi \leq 3$ is the formalization of
the Erdős conjecture (that such a $k$ exists). The proof above shows this statem
ent is **false**: for every $k$, the graph $G_m$ with $m = \lceil(k-3)/6\rceil$
is a counterexample (girth $\geq k$, $\chi = 4$). The answer to Erdős's question
 is **False**, confirming `answer(False)`.
