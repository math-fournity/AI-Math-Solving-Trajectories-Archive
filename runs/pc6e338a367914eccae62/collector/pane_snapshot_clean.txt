## Solution

**Setup.** Let the four roots of the monic quartic be the vertices of a square i
n the complex plane. A square centered at $w$ with circumradius $R>0$ has vertic
es
$$w+R,\quad w+Ri,\quad w-R,\quad w-Ri,$$
i.e. $w + R\,i^k$ for $k=0,1,2,3$ (rotating the square only permutes the vertice
s, so we may take the axes aligned with the diagonals).

**The polynomial.** Grouping opposite vertices,
$$\prod_{k=0}^{3}\bigl(x-(w+Ri^k)\bigr)=\bigl((x-w)^2-R^2\bigr)\bigl((x-w)^2+R^2
\bigr)=(x-w)^4-R^4.$$
Expanding:
$$x^4-4wx^3+6w^2x^2-4w^3x+(w^4-R^4).$$

**Integer-coefficient constraints.** With $p=-4w,\;q=6w^2,\;r=-4w^3,\;s=w^4-R^4$
 all integers:

- $p=-4w\in\mathbb Z$ gives $w=-p/4$.
- $q=6w^2=\tfrac{3p^2}{8}\in\mathbb Z$ forces $8\mid p^2$, hence $4\mid p$. Writ
e $p=4m$, so $w=-m$ with $m\in\mathbb Z$.
- Then $r=-4w^3=4m^3\in\mathbb Z$ automatically.
- $s=m^4-R^4\in\mathbb Z$ forces $R^4=m^4-s\in\mathbb Z$.

Since the square is non-degenerate, $R>0$, so $R^4$ is a **positive integer**: $
R^4\ge 1$.

**Area.** The side length of the square is $R\sqrt2$ (circumradius $R$), so the
area is
$$A=2R^2=2\sqrt{R^4}\ge 2\sqrt{1}=2.$$

**Achievability.** Take $m=0$ (so $w=0$) and $R^4=1$ (so $R=1$). The polynomial
is
$$x^4-1=0,$$
with coefficients $p=q=r=0,\;s=-1$, all integers. Its roots $1,i,-1,-i$ are the
vertices of a square with side $\sqrt2$ and area $2$.

Therefore the minimum possible area is

$$\boxed{2}.$$
