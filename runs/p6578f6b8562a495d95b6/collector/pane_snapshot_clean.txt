## Solution

**Setup.** The ellipse is $C: x^2/a^2 + y^2/b^2 = 1$ with $a>b>0$ and eccentrici
ty $e = c/a = 4/5$. Hence $c = 4a/5$ and $b^2 = a^2 - c^2 = a^2 - 16a^2/25 = 9a^
2/25$, so $b = 3a/5$.

Place the ellipse in standard position:
- Vertices: $A = (-a, 0)$, $B = (a, 0)$.
- Foci: $F_1 = (-c, 0)$, $F_2 = (c, 0)$.

Parametrize $P = (a\cos\theta,\, b\sin\theta)$ with $\sin\theta \neq 0$ (since $
P \neq A, B$). By symmetry we may take $\sin\theta > 0$.

---

**Computing $\tan 2\alpha$ where $\angle APB = 2\alpha$.**

$$\vec{PA} = (-a(1+\cos\theta),\; -b\sin\theta), \qquad \vec{PB} = (a(1-\cos\the
ta),\; -b\sin\theta).$$

Cross product (z-component):
$$\vec{PA}\times\vec{PB} = (-a(1+\cos\theta))(-b\sin\theta) - (-b\sin\theta)(a(1
-\cos\theta)) = 2ab\sin\theta.$$

Dot product:
$$\vec{PA}\cdot\vec{PB} = -a^2(1+\cos\theta)(1-\cos\theta) + b^2\sin^2\theta = -
a^2\sin^2\theta + b^2\sin^2\theta = -(a^2-b^2)\sin^2\theta = -c^2\sin^2\theta.$$

Since the dot product is negative, $\angle APB$ is obtuse (consistent with the e
llipse lying inside its circumscribed circle, where the inscribed angle over the
 diameter is $90°$). Therefore
$$\tan 2\alpha = \frac{\vec{PA}\times\vec{PB}}{\vec{PA}\cdot\vec{PB}} = \frac{2a
b\sin\theta}{-c^2\sin^2\theta} = -\frac{2ab}{c^2\sin\theta}. \tag{1}$$

---

**Computing $\tan\beta$ where $\angle F_1PF_2 = 2\beta$.**

Let $r_1 = |PF_1|$, $r_2 = |PF_2|$, so $r_1 + r_2 = 2a$ (ellipse property). Dire
ctly:
$$r_1^2 = (a\cos\theta + c)^2 + b^2\sin^2\theta = a^2 + c^2\cos^2\theta + 2ac\co
s\theta = (a + c\cos\theta)^2,$$
so $r_1 = a + c\cos\theta$ and likewise $r_2 = a - c\cos\theta$, giving
$$r_1 r_2 = a^2 - c^2\cos^2\theta. \tag{2}$$

By the **Law of Cosines** in $\triangle F_1PF_2$ (with $|F_1F_2| = 2c$):
$$\cos 2\beta = \frac{r_1^2 + r_2^2 - 4c^2}{2r_1 r_2} = \frac{(r_1+r_2)^2 - 2r_1
r_2 - 4c^2}{2r_1r_2} = \frac{4a^2 - 2r_1r_2 - 4c^2}{2r_1r_2} = \frac{2b^2}{r_1r_
2} - 1.$$

Using $1 + \cos 2\beta = 2\cos^2\beta$:
$$\cos^2\beta = \frac{b^2}{r_1 r_2}, \qquad \sin^2\beta = \frac{r_1 r_2 - b^2}{r
_1 r_2}, \qquad \tan^2\beta = \frac{r_1 r_2 - b^2}{b^2}.$$

Substituting (2):
$$\tan^2\beta = \frac{a^2 - c^2\cos^2\theta - b^2}{b^2} = \frac{c^2 - c^2\cos^2\
theta}{b^2} = \frac{c^2\sin^2\theta}{b^2}.$$

Since $\sin\theta > 0$ and $\beta \in (0, \pi/2)$:
$$\tan\beta = \frac{c\sin\theta}{b}. \tag{3}$$

---

**The product.** Multiplying (1) and (3):

$$\tan\beta \cdot \tan 2\alpha = \frac{c\sin\theta}{b} \cdot \left(-\frac{2ab}{c
^2\sin\theta}\right) = -\frac{2a}{c} = -\frac{2}{e} = -\frac{2}{4/5} = -\frac{5}
{2}.$$

The product is **constant** (independent of $P$), so the minimum value is:

$$\boxed{-\dfrac{5}{2}}.$$
