## Solution

We evaluate $I = \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{\sin^3 x}{x^2+4}\,dx$.

**Step 1: Triple-angle identity.** Using $\sin^3 x = \frac{3\sin x - \sin 3x}{4}
$,

$$I = \frac{3}{4}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x^2+4}\,dx - \frac{1}{4}\
int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{\sin 3x}{x^2+4}\,dx.$$

**Step 2: Standard integral.** For $a>0,\, b>0$,

$$\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{\sin(ax)}{x^2+b^2}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{b}e^{-ab}.$$

*Proof:* Consider $J(a)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{iax}}{x^2+b^2}\,dx$. Th
e integrand has simple poles at $x=\pm ib$. For $a>0$, close the contour in the
upper half-plane, enclosing $x=ib$ with residue

$$\operatorname{Res}_{x=ib}\frac{e^{iax}}{x^2+b^2}=\frac{e^{ia(ib)}}{2ib}=\frac{
e^{-ab}}{2ib}.$$

By the residue theorem, $J(a)=2\pi i\cdot\frac{e^{-ab}}{2ib}=\frac{\pi}{b}e^{-ab
}$. Taking the imaginary part (since $\frac{1}{x^2+b^2}$ is even and $\cos(ax)$
is even while $\sin(ax)$ is odd, only the sine term survives as the imaginary pa
rt):

$$\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{\sin(ax)}{x^2+b^2}\,dx=\frac{\pi}{b}e^{-ab}.$$

**Step 3: Apply with $b=2$.**

- First integral ($a=1$): $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x^
2+4}\,dx=\frac{\pi}{2}e^{-2}.$
- Second integral ($a=3$): $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{\sin 3x}{
x^2+4}\,dx=\frac{\pi}{2}e^{-6}.$

**Step 4: Combine.**

$$I=\frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}e^{-2}-\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}e^{-6}=\fra
c{\pi}{8}\left(3e^{-2}-e^{-6}\right).$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{\sin^3 x}{x^2+4}\,dx=\frac{\
pi}{8}\left(3e^{-2}-e^{-6}\right).}$$
