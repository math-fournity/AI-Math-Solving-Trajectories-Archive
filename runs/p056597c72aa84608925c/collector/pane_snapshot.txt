**Problem.** Is there a rational map with rational coefficients that has order 5
?

---

## Proof

We interpret "rational map with rational coefficients" as a rational function $f
(x) = p(x)/q(x)$ with $p, q \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$, defining a self-map $f : \mathbb
{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^1$, and "order 5" means $f^{\circ 5} = \mathrm{id}$ (the id
entity $x \mapsto x$) while $f^{\circ k} \neq \mathrm{id}$ for $1 \le k \le 4$.

### Step 1. Any rational map of finite order has degree 1.

For rational maps on $\mathbb{P}^1$, the degree is multiplicative under composit
ion:
$$\deg(f \circ g) = \deg(f)\cdot \deg(g).$$
If $f^{\circ 5} = \mathrm{id}$, then taking degrees gives $(\deg f)^5 = \deg(\ma
thrm{id}) = 1$, hence $\deg f = 1$.

Therefore any rational map of finite order (in particular order 5) must be a **M
öbius transformation** $f(x) = \frac{ax+b}{cx+d}$ with $a,b,c,d \in \mathbb{Q}$
and $ad - bc \neq 0$.

### Step 2. Reduction to a matrix question.

The Möbius transformation $f$ corresponds to the matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} a &
 b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Q})$, well-defined up to a n
onzero scalar, so we work in $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q})$. The condition $f^{\ci
rc 5} = \mathrm{id}$ translates to
$$A^5 = \lambda I \quad\text{for some } \lambda \in \mathbb{Q}^*,$$
with $A^k \notin \mathbb{Q}^* I$ for $1 \le k \le 4$.

Let $\chi_A(t) = t^2 - (\mathrm{tr}\, A)\, t + \det A \in \mathbb{Q}[t]$ be the
characteristic polynomial, and let $\alpha, \beta$ be its roots (the eigenvalues
 of $A$, in some extension of $\mathbb{Q}$).

From $A^5 = \lambda I$, the eigenvalues of $A^5$ are both $\lambda$, so $\alpha^
5 = \beta^5 = \lambda$, giving
$$\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\right)^5 = 1.$$
Since $f$ has order **exactly** 5, the ratio $\alpha/\beta$ must be a **primitiv
e** 5th root of unity $\zeta_5 = e^{2\pi i/5}$.

### Step 3. The three cases for $\chi_A$.

**Case 1: $\chi_A$ splits over $\mathbb{Q}$.** Then $\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{Q
}$, so $\alpha/\beta \in \mathbb{Q}$. The only rational 5th root of unity is $1$
, forcing $\alpha = \beta$. If $A$ is diagonalizable then $A = \alpha I$ and $f
= \mathrm{id}$ (order 1); if not, $A = \alpha I + N$ with $N$ nilpotent and nonz
ero, but then $A^5 = \alpha^5 I + (\text{terms involving } N) \neq \lambda I$ un
less $\alpha = 0$ (in which case $A^5 = 0$). Either way, no order 5.

**Case 2: $\chi_A$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ (degree 2).** Then $\alpha,
\beta$ are conjugate in a quadratic extension $K = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ with $[K:
\mathbb{Q}] = 2$. Since $\det A = \alpha\beta \in \mathbb{Q}^*$, we have $\alpha
/\beta = \alpha^2 / (\alpha\beta) \in K$. But $\alpha/\beta = \zeta_5$ is a prim
itive 5th root of unity, whose minimal polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}$ is the cyclo
tomic polynomial
$$\Phi_5(t) = t^4 + t^3 + t^2 + t + 1,$$
of degree $\varphi(5) = 4$. Thus $[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5):\mathbb{Q}] = 4$, which c
annot be contained in the degree-2 extension $K$. Contradiction.

**Case 3: $\chi_A$ has a repeated root.** Then $\alpha = \beta \in \mathbb{Q}$ (
the discriminant $0$ with rational coefficients forces the root $\mathrm{tr}(A)/
2 \in \mathbb{Q}$). This reduces to Case 1.

### Conclusion

In every case, the requirement that $\alpha/\beta$ be a primitive 5th root of un
ity is incompatible with the eigenvalues coming from a $2\times 2$ matrix over $
\mathbb{Q}$ (which only accesses extensions of degree $\le 2$, whereas $\zeta_5$
 requires degree 4). Hence no Möbius transformation over $\mathbb{Q}$ has order
5, and by Step 1 no rational map over $\mathbb{Q}$ of any degree has order 5.

**Answer: No, there is no rational map with rational coefficients that has order
 5.** $\blacksquare$
