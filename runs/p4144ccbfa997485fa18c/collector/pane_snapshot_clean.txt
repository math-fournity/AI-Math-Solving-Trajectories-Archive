## Solution

**Substitution.** Let $u = x+y$ and $t = \dfrac{x}{x+y}$, so that $x = ut$, $\;y
 = u(1-t)$.

The Jacobian is
$$\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,t)} = \begin{vmatrix} t & u \\ 1-t & -u \end{v
matrix} = -ut - u(1-t) = -u, \qquad |J| = u.$$

**Domain.** The constraints $x\ge0,\; y\ge0,\; x+y\le1$ become $0\le t\le 1$, $\
;0\le u\le 1$.

**Transformed integrand.** We have $x+y = u$, $\;1+y/x = 1 + \frac{1-t}{t} = \fr
ac{1}{t}$, and $1-x-y = 1-u$. Hence
$$\frac{(x+y)\ln(1+y/x)}{\sqrt{1-x-y}} = \frac{u\,\ln(1/t)}{\sqrt{1-u}} = \frac{
-u\ln t}{\sqrt{1-u}}.$$

Including the Jacobian factor $u$:
$$I = \int_0^1\!\int_0^1 \frac{-u^2 \ln t}{\sqrt{1-u}}\,dt\,du = \left(\int_0^1
\frac{u^2}{\sqrt{1-u}}\,du\right)\!\left(\int_0^1 (-\ln t)\,dt\right).$$

**First factor.** With $w=1-u$:
$$\int_0^1 \frac{u^2}{\sqrt{1-u}}\,du = \int_0^1 \frac{(1-w)^2}{\sqrt{w}}\,dw =
\int_0^1\!\left(w^{-1/2}-2w^{1/2}+w^{3/2}\right)dw = 2 - \frac{4}{3} + \frac{2}{
5} = \frac{16}{15}.$$

**Second factor.**
$$\int_0^1 (-\ln t)\,dt = \bigl[-t\ln t + t\bigr]_0^1 = 1.$$

**Result.**
$$I = \frac{16}{15}\cdot 1 = \boxed{\dfrac{16}{15}}.$$
