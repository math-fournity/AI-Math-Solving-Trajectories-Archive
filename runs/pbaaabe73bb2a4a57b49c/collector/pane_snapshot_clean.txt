## Proof that $T^{(g)}$ is homeomorphic to the CW complex with the standard pres
entation

Let $T^{(g)} = \underbrace{T^2 \# \cdots \# T^2}_{g}$ denote the connected sum o
f $g$ tori. We construct an explicit homeomorphism between this surface and the
CW complex $X_g$ defined by:

- one 0-cell $v$,
- $2g$ 1-cells $a_1, b_1, \ldots, a_g, b_g$ (each a loop at $v$),
- one 2-cell $e^2$ attached via the map $\varphi : S^1 \to X^{(1)}$ reading the
word $w = a_1 b_1 a_1^{-1} b_1^{-1} \cdots a_g b_g a_g^{-1} b_g^{-1}$.

### Step 1. The standard polygon model of $T^{(g)}$

It is a classical fact (obtained by induction on $g$ via the connected-sum opera
tion on the standard $4g$-gon) that $T^{(g)}$ is homeomorphic to the quotient sp
ace $P_g / \sim$, where $P_g$ is a regular $4g$-gon with edges labeled, counterc
lockwise,

$$a_1,\ b_1,\ a_1^{-1},\ b_1^{-1},\ a_2,\ b_2,\ a_2^{-1},\ b_2^{-1},\ \ldots,\ a
_g,\ b_g,\ a_g^{-1},\ b_g^{-1},$$

and $\sim$ identifies edges carrying the same label (respecting orientation for
the un-barred label, reversing for the barred). Concretely:

- **Base case $g=1$.** $T^{(2)} = T^2$ is the square with boundary word $aba^{-1
}b^{-1}$, the standard fundamental polygon of the torus. This is the textbook id
entification $[0,1]^2/{(x,0)\sim(x,1),\,(0,y)\sim(1,y)}$.

- **Inductive step.** Suppose $T^{(g)} \cong P_g/\sim$ with the $4g$-gon and the
 word $w_g = a_1b_1a_1^{-1}b_1^{-1}\cdots a_gb_ga_g^{-1}b_g^{-1}$. Form $T^{(g+1
)} = T^{(g)} \# T^2$ by removing an open disk from the interior of each polygon
and gluing along the new boundary circles. The two boundary circles can be defor
med (within the punctured polygons) to a single arc connecting two points on the
 boundary, and a standard cut-and-paste argument (cutting each polygon along an
arc from the puncture to a boundary vertex, then regluing along the puncture-arc
) merges the two $4g$-gon and $4$-gon into a single $(4g+4)$-gon whose boundary
reads $w_{g+1}$. The relabeling of edges is exactly the concatenation $w_g \cdot
 (a_{g+1}b_{g+1}a_{g+1}^{-1}b_{g+1}^{-1})$.

This establishes $T^{(g)} \cong P_g / \sim$ for all $g \geq 1$.

### Step 2. The quotient $P_g/\sim$ is exactly the claimed CW complex

We now exhibit the CW structure on $P_g / \sim$ matching the description.

**0-skeleton.** All $4g$ vertices of $P_g$ are identified to a single point unde
r $\sim$. To see this, walk around the boundary: the head of edge $a_1$ is the t
ail of $b_1$; the head of $b_1$ is the tail of $a_1^{-1}$, which (being the same
 edge as $a_1$ with reversed orientation) is the head of $a_1$ — already identif
ied. Continuing, the identification of edge $a_i$ with $a_i^{-1}$ and $b_i$ with
 $b_i^{-1}$ forces each successive vertex to coincide with the previous one. Aft
er traversing all $4g$ edges, every vertex is identified to a single point $v$.
So the 0-skeleton is $\{v\}$: **a single 0-cell**.

**1-skeleton.** Each pair of identified edges $(a_i, a_i^{-1})$ and $(b_i, b_i^{
-1})$ becomes a single 1-cell (a loop at $v$) in the quotient. There are $g$ pai

thus a wedge $\bigvee_{i=1}^{g}(S^1_{a_i} \vee S^1_{b_i})$ of $2g$ circles.

**2-cell.** The interior of $P_g$ survives the quotient intact (only the boundar
y is identified), so it contributes a single open 2-cell $e^2$. Its attaching ma
p is the quotient of the boundary $\partial P_g \to X^{(1)}$, which traverses th

$$w = a_1 b_1 a_1^{-1} b_1^{-1} \cdots a_g b_g a_g^{-1} b_g^{-1}.$$

So $P_g/\sim$ has the structure: one 0-cell, $2g$ 1-cells, one 2-cell attached v

### Step 3. The CW structure is well-defined and gives a homeomorphism

The quotient map $q : P_g \to P_g/\sim$ is a closed map (since $P_g$ is compact
and $P_g/\sim$ is Hausdorff — the latter because $\sim$ is generated by gluing a
long closed subsets, i.e. it is a proper identification). The CW axioms are sati
sfied:

- *(Closure finite.)* The closure of each 1-cell meets only $v$; the closure of
$e^2$ meets the 1-skeleton in its boundary image, which is compact and contained
 in finitely many cells. ✓
- *(Weak topology.)* A subset of $P_g/\sim$ is closed iff its preimage under eac
h characteristic map is closed. The characteristic maps are: the constant map fo

quotient of the polygon's interior-plus-boundary map $\overline{e^2} = P_g \to P
_g/\sim$ for the 2-cell. Since $P_g/\sim$ carries the quotient topology from $P_
g$ and $P_g$ is the single 2-cell's closure, the weak topology coincides with th

Therefore $P_g/\sim$ **is** (not merely contains) the CW complex $X_g$ with the
prescribed cells and attaching map. Combined with Step 1,

$$T^{(g)} \;\cong\; P_g/\sim \;=\; X_g\Bigl(\text{one 0-cell},\ 2g\text{ 1-cells
},\ \text{one 2-cell attached via } a_1b_1a_1^{-1}b_1^{-1}\cdots a_gb_ga_g^{-1}b
