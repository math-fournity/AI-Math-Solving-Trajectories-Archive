## Proof

We are given

$$g(z) = \int_C \frac{2s^2 - s - 2}{s - z}\, ds,$$

where $C$ is the circle $|s| = 3$ (traversed once, positively oriented), and we
must evaluate $g(z)$ for $|z| > 3$.

**Key observation.** View the integrand as a function of the integration variabl
e $s$:

$$h(s) = \frac{2s^2 - s - 2}{s - z}.$$

The numerator $2s^2 - s - 2$ is a polynomial, hence an entire function of $s$. T
he denominator $s - z$ vanishes only at $s = z$. Therefore the only singularity
of $h(s)$ in the $s$-plane is at $s = z$.

**Location of the singularity.** Since we are given $|z| > 3$, the point $z$ lie
s strictly **outside** the circle $C = \{s : |s| = 3\}$ and, in particular, outs
ide the closed disk $\{s : |s| \le 3\}$ bounded by $C$.

**Analyticity on and inside $C$.** Because the sole singularity $s = z$ is exter
ior to $C$, the function $h(s)$ is analytic on an open set containing both the c
ontour $C$ and its interior $\{s : |s| \le 3\}$.

**Applying Cauchy's integral theorem.** Cauchy's theorem states that if a functi
on is analytic on and inside a simple closed contour, then its integral around t
hat contour is zero. Since $h(s)$ satisfies this hypothesis on $C$,

$$g(z) = \int_C \frac{2s^2 - s - 2}{s - z}\, ds = 0.$$

**Verification via the Cauchy integral formula (cross-check).** For $|z| > 3$, w
e may expand the kernel in powers of $s/z$:

$$\frac{1}{s - z} = -\frac{1}{z}\cdot\frac{1}{1 - s/z} = -\frac{1}{z}\sum_{n=0}^
{\infty}\left(\frac{s}{z}\right)^n, \qquad \left|\frac{s}{z}\right| < 1 \text{ o
n } C.$$

Then

$$g(z) = -\frac{1}{z}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} z^{-n}\int_C s^n(2s^2 - s - 2)\,ds.$$

Every term $\int_C s^n(2s^2 - s - 2)\,ds = \int_C (2s^{n+2} - s^{n+1} - 2s^n)\,d
s$ is the integral of a polynomial (entire function) around $C$, which is $0$ by
 Cauchy's theorem. Hence every term vanishes, confirming

$$g(z) = 0.$$

**Conclusion.** For $|z| > 3$,

$$\boxed{g(z) = 0}.$$
