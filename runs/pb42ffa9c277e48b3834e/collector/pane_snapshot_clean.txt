## Solution

**Answer: No.** The condition $m \otimes 1 = m' \otimes b$ in $M \otimes_A B$ do
es **not** imply that $b \in f(A)$. I provide an explicit counterexample.

### Counterexample

Let $A = \mathbb{C}[t^2, t^3]$ and $B = \mathbb{C}[t]$, with $f: A \hookrightarr
ow B$ the natural inclusion.

**Verification of hypotheses:**

1. **$\mathbb{C}$-algebras and integral domains:** Both $A$ and $B$ are $\mathbb
{C}$-algebras. Since $B = \mathbb{C}[t]$ is an integral domain and $A$ is a subr
ing of $B$, both are integral domains. ✓

2. **$f$ is injective:** The inclusion $A \subset B$ is injective. ✓

3. **$f$ is a finite morphism:** As an $A$-module, $B$ is generated by $\{1, t\}
$. Indeed, any element $\sum_{n \geq 0} a_n t^n \in \mathbb{C}[t]$ decomposes as
:
$$\sum_{k \geq 0} a_{2k}\, t^{2k} \;+\; t \sum_{k \geq 0} a_{2k+1}\, t^{2k}$$
where $\sum a_{2k}\, t^{2k} \in A$ (since $t^{2k} = (t^2)^k \in A$) and $\sum a_
{2k+1}\, t^{2k} \in A$ (same reason). So $B = A\cdot 1 + A\cdot t$, a finite $A$
-module. ✓

**The module and the relation:**

Take $M = A$ (a finitely generated $A$-module), $m = t^3$, $m' = t^2$, and $b =
t$.

Under the canonical isomorphism $M \otimes_A B = A \otimes_A B \xrightarrow{\;\s
im\;} B$ sending $a \otimes b' \mapsto a\cdot b'$:

- $m \otimes 1 = t^3 \otimes 1 \;\mapsto\; t^3$,
- $m' \otimes b = t^2 \otimes t \;\mapsto\; t^2 \cdot t = t^3$.

Both sides map to $t^3 \in B$, so $m \otimes 1 = m' \otimes b$ in $M \otimes_A B
$. ✓

**But $b \notin f(A)$:** We have $b = t$. The ring $A = \mathbb{C}[t^2, t^3]$ co
nsists of polynomials in $t$ whose coefficient of $t^1$ is zero (every element o
f $A$ is a polynomial $\sum c_n t^n$ with $c_1 = 0$, since $A$ is generated by $
t^2$ and $t^3$ over $\mathbb{C}$, and all monomials $(t^2)^a (t^3)^b = t^{2a+3b}
$ have $2a+3b \neq 1$ for $a, b \geq 0$). Since $t$ has a nonzero coefficient of
 $t^1$, we have $t \notin A$, i.e., $b \notin f(A)$. ✓

### Why the counterexample works

The key point is that $A = \mathbb{C}[t^2, t^3]$ is **not integrally closed**. T
he element $t \in \operatorname{Frac}(A) = \mathbb{C}(t)$ is integral over $A$ (
it satisfies $X^2 - t^2 = 0$ with $t^2 \in A$) and lies in $B$, yet $t \notin A$
. The relation $m \otimes 1 = m' \otimes b$ with $M = A$ reduces to $m = m' \cdo
t b$ in $B$, which forces $b = m/m' \in \operatorname{Frac}(A) \cap B$. When $A$
 is not integrally closed, this intersection $\operatorname{Frac}(A) \cap B$ can
 be strictly larger than $A$, allowing $b$ to escape the image of $f$.
