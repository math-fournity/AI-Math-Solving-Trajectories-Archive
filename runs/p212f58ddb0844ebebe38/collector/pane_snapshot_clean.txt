## Proof

**Setup.** We have 2014 nodes in a circle, 106 protocols, each protocol assigned
 to exactly 19 nodes (since 106 × 19 = 2014). We seek the minimum segment length
 $n$ such that every contiguous segment of $n$ nodes contains at least 53 distin
ct protocols, regardless of the arrangement.

**Key observation.** If a segment contains at most 52 distinct protocols, then a
ll nodes in that segment belong to at most 52 protocol types. Since each protoco
l type has exactly 19 nodes in the entire ring, the segment can contain at most
$52 \times 19 = 988$ nodes. Therefore, **any segment of length $\geq 989$ must c
ontain at least 53 distinct protocols.**

**Tightness — there exists an arrangement where a segment of 988 nodes has only
52 protocols.** Arrange the ring so that all 19 nodes of each of 52 chosen proto
cols are placed contiguously, forming a block of $52 \times 19 = 988$ consecutiv
e nodes. The remaining $2014 - 988 = 1026$ nodes (belonging to the other 54 prot
ocols) fill the rest of the circle. The segment covering exactly this 988-node b
lock contains precisely 52 distinct protocols. Thus $n = 988$ does **not** guara
ntee 53 distinct protocols.

**Conclusion.**
- $n = 988$ fails: the clustering arrangement above yields a segment of 988 node
s with only 52 protocols.
- $n = 989$ succeeds: any segment of 989 nodes uses at most 52 protocols would r
equire at most $52 \times 19 = 988$ nodes, but the segment has 989 nodes — contr
adiction. So every segment of 989 nodes has $\geq 53$ distinct protocols, for ev
ery arrangement.

$$\boxed{n = 989}$$
