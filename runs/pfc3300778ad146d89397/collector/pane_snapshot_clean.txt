## Solution

We consider the ODE
$$\ddot{x} = \dot{x}^2 + x^3, \qquad x(0)=0,\quad \dot{x}(0)=A,$$
and denote its solution by $x(t;A)$. We seek
$$y(t) \;:=\; \frac{\partial x}{\partial A}(t;A)\Big|_{A=0}.$$

**Step 1. The solution at $A=0$.**

When $A=0$ the initial data are $x(0)=0$, $\dot{x}(0)=0$. The identically zero f
unction $x(t;0)\equiv 0$ satisfies both the ODE ($0 = 0^2+0^3$) and the initial
data. By uniqueness of solutions to ODEs (the right-hand side $f(\dot x, x)=\dot
 x^2+x^3$ is smooth, hence locally Lipschitz), this is the solution:
$$x(t;0) \equiv 0, \qquad \dot x(t;0)\equiv 0.$$

**Step 2. The variational equation.**

Differentiate the ODE with respect to the parameter $A$:
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial A}\ddot{x} = 2\,\dot{x}\,\frac{\partial \dot{x}}{\par
tial A} + 3x^2\frac{\partial x}{\partial A},$$
i.e.
$$\ddot{y} = 2\,\dot{x}\,\dot{y} + 3x^2\, y,$$
where $y(t) = \partial_A x(t;A)$.

**Step 3. Evaluate at $A=0$.**

Using $x(t;0)\equiv 0$ and $\dot x(t;0)\equiv 0$, the variational equation colla
pses to
$$\ddot{y}(t) = 0.$$

**Step 4. Initial conditions for $y$.**

- From $x(0;A)=0$ for all $A$: $\;y(0) = \partial_A x(0;A)\big|_{A=0} = 0$.
- From $\dot x(0;A)=A$: $\;\dot y(0) = \partial_A \dot x(0;A)\big|_{A=0} = \part
ial_A(A)\big|_{A=0} = 1$.

**Step 5. Solve.**

The IVP $\ddot y = 0$, $y(0)=0$, $\dot y(0)=1$ has the unique solution
$$y(t) = t.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \frac{\partial x}{\partial A}(t;A)\Big|_{A=0} \;=\; t.}$$
