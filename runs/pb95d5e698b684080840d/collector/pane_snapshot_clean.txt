## Proof

**Setting.** Let $X$ be a locally compact Hausdorff space and $\Lambda$ a positi
ve linear functional on $C_c(X)$. By the Riesz Representation Theorem (Rudin, *R
eal and Complex Analysis*, Theorem 2.14), there exist a $\sigma$-algebra $\mathf
rak{M}$ on $X$ and a positive measure $\mu$ on $\mathfrak{M}$ satisfying, among
others, the properties:

- (a) $\Lambda f = \int_X f\,d\mu$ for all $f \in C_c(X)$;
- (c) $\mu(E) = \inf\{\mu(V): E \subset V,\ V \text{ open}\}$ for every $E \in \
mathfrak{M}$ (outer regularity);
- (e) If $E \in \mathfrak{M}$, $A \subset E$, and $\mu(E)=0$, then $A \in \mathf
rak{M}$ (completeness).

The $\sigma$-algebra $\mathfrak{M}$ contains every Borel set (open sets lie in $
\mathfrak{M}$ by construction, and $\mathfrak{M}$ is a $\sigma$-algebra), and by
 (e) it contains all subsets of $\mu$-null sets. Hence $\mathfrak{M}$ is in gene
ral **strictly larger** than the Borel $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{B}(X)$.

Let $\mu_B := \mu\big|_{\mathcal{B}(X)}$ be the restriction of $\mu$ to the Bore
l $\sigma$-algebra. We claim:

$$\int_X f\,d\mu = \int_X f\,d\mu_B \qquad \text{for every Borel measurable } f:
 X \to \overline{\mathbb{R}}.$$

The proof proceeds by the standard machine (monotone class / approximation), usi
ng one key lemma.

---

**Lemma.** *For every $E \in \mathfrak{M}$ there exist Borel sets $A, B \in \mat
hcal{B}(X)$ with $A \subset E \subset B$ and $\mu(B \setminus A) = 0$.*

*Proof of Lemma.* By outer regularity (c), for each $n \geq 1$ there is an open
set $V_n \supset E$ with $\mu(V_n) \leq \mu(E) + 1/n$. Set $B = \bigcap_n V_n$;
then $B$ is a Borel set (in fact a $G_\delta$), $E \subset B$, and $\mu(B) = \mu
(E)$, so $\mu(B \setminus E) = 0$.

Applying the same argument to $E^c \in \mathfrak{M}$, we obtain a Borel set $C \
supset E^c$ with $\mu(C \setminus E^c) = 0$. Set $A = C^c$, a Borel set with $A
\subset E$ and $\mu(E \setminus A) = \mu(C \setminus E^c) = 0$. Then $A \subset
E \subset B$ and $\mu(B \setminus A) = \mu(B \setminus E) + \mu(E \setminus A) =
 0$. $\square$

---

**Step 1: Indicator functions.** Let $E \in \mathcal{B}(X)$. Then $E \in \mathfr
ak{M}$ and
$$\int \mathbf{1}_E\,d\mu = \mu(E) = \mu_B(E) = \int \mathbf{1}_E\,d\mu_B.$$

**Step 2: Non-negative simple Borel functions.** Let $s = \sum_{i=1}^n a_i\,\mat
hbf{1}_{E_i}$ with $E_i \in \mathcal{B}(X)$. By linearity and Step 1,
$$\int s\,d\mu = \sum a_i\,\mu(E_i) = \sum a_i\,\mu_B(E_i) = \int s\,d\mu_B.$$

**Step 3: Non-negative Borel functions.** Let $f \geq 0$ be Borel measurable. Th
en there exists an increasing sequence $(s_n)$ of non-negative simple **Borel**
functions with $s_n \nearrow f$. By the Monotone Convergence Theorem (applied to
 both $\mu$ and $\mu_B$) and Step 2,
$$\int f\,d\mu = \lim_n \int s_n\,d\mu = \lim_n \int s_n\,d\mu_B = \int f\,d\mu_
B.$$

Here MCT applies to $\mu$ because every Borel function is $\mathfrak{M}$-measura
ble (since $\mathcal{B}(X) \subset \mathfrak{M}$), and to $\mu_B$ because $f$ is
 Borel measurable by assumption.

**Step 4: Integrable Borel functions.** For a general Borel $f$, write $f = f^+
- f^-$ with $f^\pm \geq 0$ Borel. If either side is finite (i.e., $f$ is integra
ble with respect to one measure), Step 3 gives
$$\int f\,d\mu = \int f^+\,d\mu - \int f^-\,d\mu = \int f^+\,d\mu_B - \int f^-\,
d\mu_B = \int f\,d\mu_B,$$
with the convention that $\infty - \infty$ does not arise precisely when $f$ is
integrable. The equality of integrability follows because $\int f^\pm\,d\mu = \i
nt f^\pm\,d\mu_B$ by Step 3, so $f \in L^1(\mu)$ if and only if $f \in L^1(\mu_B
)$, and the integrals coincide.

---

**Remark on why the larger $\sigma$-algebra $\mathfrak{M}$ does not affect Borel
-function integrals.** One might worry that allowing $\mathfrak{M}$-measurable (
non-Borel) simple functions in the definition of $\int f\,d\mu$ could yield a la
rger value. But by the Lemma, every $\mathfrak{M}$-measurable set $E$ differs fr
om a Borel set only on a $\mu$-null set, so any $\mathfrak{M}$-measurable simple
 function $s$ has a Borel counterpart $s'$ with $s = s'$ $\mu$-a.e. and hence $\
int s\,d\mu = \int s'\,d\mu = \int s'\,d\mu_B$. Thus the supremum defining $\int
 f\,d\mu$ is unchanged whether we use $\mathfrak{M}$-measurable or Borel simple
functions, and equals $\int f\,d\mu_B$.

---

**Conclusion.** The restriction of $\mu$ to the Borel $\sigma$-algebra does not
alter the integral of any Borel measurable function. The integrals with respect
to $\mu$ and $\mu_B$ are equal for all Borel functions. The extra sets in $\math
frak{M}$ (subsets of null sets and, more generally, sets differing from Borel se
ts by null sets) contribute measure zero and therefore do not affect integrals o
f Borel functions.
