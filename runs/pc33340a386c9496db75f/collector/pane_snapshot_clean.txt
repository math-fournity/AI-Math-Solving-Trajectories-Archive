**Claim:** The statement is **true**.

**Proof.**

Let $M = V$ and $N = W$ be vector spaces (finite-dimensional, as manifolds). The
 canonical isomorphism $l_v : V \to T_v V$ is defined by
$$l_v(\xi)(f) = \frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0} f(v + t\xi), \qquad \xi \in V,\; f \in
C^\infty(V).$$

Since $F : V \to W$ is a map between manifolds and the pushforward $dF_p$ is def
ined, $F$ is smooth.

**Step 1: Identify $X$ with a vector in $V$.**

The derivation $X \in T_p V$ corresponds, via $l_p^{-1} : T_p V \to V$, to a uni
que vector $\xi := l_p^{-1}(X) \in V$. The notation $p + tX$ in the problem is i
nterpreted, under this canonical identification, as $p + t\xi$.

**Step 2: Compute $dF_p(X)(f)$ directly from the definition.**

For any $f \in C^\infty(W)$:
$$dF_p(X)(f) = X(f \circ F) = l_p(\xi)(f \circ F) = \frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0} (f
\circ F)(p + t\xi) = \frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0} f\big(F(p + t\xi)\big).$$

**Step 3: The difference quotient exists and gives a vector $w \in W$.**

Since $F$ is smooth, the curve $\gamma(t) = F(p + t\xi)$ is a smooth curve in $W
$ with $\gamma(0) = F(p)$. Its velocity at $t=0$ is
$$w := \gamma'(0) = \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{F(p + t\xi) - F(p)}{t} \in W,$$
which exists by smoothness of $F$.

**Step 4: Apply the chain rule to relate Steps 2 and 3.**

By the chain rule for smooth maps between vector spaces:
$$\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0} f(\gamma(t)) = df_{\gamma(0)}(\gamma'(0)) = df_{F(p)}
(w).$$

**Step 5: Identify $df_{F(p)}(w)$ with $l_{F(p)}(w)(f)$.**

By definition of the canonical isomorphism at $F(p)$:
$$l_{F(p)}(w)(f) = \frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0} f(F(p) + tw) = df_{F(p)}(w),$$
where the last equality is the standard identification of the directional deriva
tive with the differential.

**Step 6: Conclude.**

Combining Steps 2, 4, and 5:
$$dF_p(X)(f) = \frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0} f(F(p+t\xi)) = df_{F(p)}(w) = l_{F(p)}(w
)(f)$$
for every $f \in C^\infty(W)$. Since derivations are determined by their action
on smooth functions:
$$dF_p(X) = l_{F(p)}(w).$$

Applying $l_{F(p)}^{-1}$ to both sides:
$$l_{F(p)}^{-1}\big(dF_p(X)\big) = w = \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{F(p + t\xi) - F(p)}{
t}.$$

Under the canonical identifications $T_p V \cong V$ (via $l_p$) and $T_{F(p)} W
\cong W$ (via $l_{F(p)}$), this is precisely the statement
$$dF_p(X) = \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{F(p + tX) - F(p)}{t}. \qquad \blacksquare$$
