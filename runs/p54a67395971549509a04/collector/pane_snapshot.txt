## Solution

We work in $R = \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-17}]$, with norm $N(a+b\sqrt{-17}) = a^2 + 17b
^2$. The norm is multiplicative, and the units are exactly the elements of norm
1, i.e., $\pm 1$.

**Claim.** The element $18 \in R$ can be written both as a product of two irredu
cibles and as a product of three irreducibles:
$$18 = (1+\sqrt{-17})(1-\sqrt{-17}) = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 3.$$

**Verification of the identity.** $(1+\sqrt{-17})(1-\sqrt{-17}) = 1 - (\sqrt{-17
})^2 = 1+17 = 18$. ✓

**The four elements are irreducible.** An element $\pi$ is irreducible if $\pi =
 \alpha\beta$ implies $\alpha$ or $\beta$ is a unit (norm 1). We use: if $\pi =
\alpha\beta$ nontrivially, then $N(\alpha), N(\beta) > 1$ and $N(\alpha)N(\beta)
 = N(\pi)$.

The norms achievable as $a^2 + 17b^2$ (with $a,b\in\mathbb{Z}$) begin:
$$1,\ 4,\ 9,\ 16,\ 17,\ 18,\ 21,\ 25,\ 26,\ 33,\dots$$
In particular, **2, 3, 6 are not norms** in this ring.

- **$N(2) = 4$.** A nontrivial factorization needs $N(\alpha)=N(\beta)=2$, but 2
 is not a norm. So $2$ is irreducible.
- **$N(3) = 9$.** A nontrivial factorization needs $N(\alpha)=N(\beta)=3$, but 3
 is not a norm. So $3$ is irreducible.
- **$N(1\pm\sqrt{-17}) = 18$.** A nontrivial factorization needs $N(\alpha)\cdot
 N(\beta)=18$ with both $>1$, i.e. $(N(\alpha),N(\beta)) \in \{(2,9),(3,6),(6,3)
,(9,2)\}$. But 2, 3, 6 are not norms, so no such factorization exists. Hence $1+
\sqrt{-17}$ and $1-\sqrt{-17}$ are irreducible.

**The two factorizations are genuinely distinct.** Associates share the same nor
m. Since $N(2)=4$, $N(3)=9$, and $N(1\pm\sqrt{-17})=18$ are all different, none
of $2, 3$ is an associate of $1\pm\sqrt{-17}$. Thus the factorization into two i
rreducibles cannot be obtained from the factorization into three irreducibles by
 reassociating and regrouping.

Therefore $18 = (1+\sqrt{-17})(1-\sqrt{-17}) = 2\cdot 3\cdot 3$ is an element of
 $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-17}]$ expressible both as a product of **two** irreducible e
lements and as a product of **three** irreducible elements. $\blacksquare$
