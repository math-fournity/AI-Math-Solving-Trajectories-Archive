## Proof

The integral $\int \frac{e^{z^2}}{z^3}\,dz$ is to be evaluated as a **contour in
tegral** over a closed positively oriented contour $C$ enclosing the origin (the
 only interpretation under which Cauchy's Integral Formula applies; the natural
choice is $|z|=1$). We determine its value and check whether it equals $\pi i$.

**Step 1: Match the integrand to Cauchy's Integral Formula for derivatives.**

The generalized Cauchy Integral Formula states that for $f$ analytic inside and
on a simple closed positively oriented contour $C$ enclosing $a$,

$$
f^{(n)}(a)=\frac{n!}{2\pi i}\oint_C \frac{f(z)}{(z-a)^{n+1}}\,dz.
$$

Rewrite the integrand:

$$
\frac{e^{z^2}}{z^3}=\frac{e^{z^2}}{(z-0)^3}.
$$

Thus $a=0$, $n+1=3$ so $n=2$, and $f(z)=e^{z^2}$, which is entire (hence analyti
c inside and on any contour enclosing the origin).

**Step 2: Compute $f''(0)$.**

$$
f(z)=e^{z^2},\qquad f'(z)=2z\,e^{z^2},
$$
$$
f''(z)=2e^{z^2}+4z^2 e^{z^2}.
$$

Evaluating at $z=0$:

$$
f''(0)=2e^{0}+0=2.
$$

**Step 3: Apply the formula.**

$$
\oint_C \frac{e^{z^2}}{z^3}\,dz=\frac{2\pi i}{2!}\,f''(0)=\frac{2\pi i}{2}\cdot
2=2\pi i.
$$

**Step 4: Independent verification via Laurent series / residue.**

Expanding around $z=0$:

$$
e^{z^2}=1+z^2+\frac{z^4}{2!}+\frac{z^6}{3!}+\cdots
$$

$$
\frac{e^{z^2}}{z^3}=\frac{1}{z^3}+\frac{1}{z}+\frac{z}{2!}+\frac{z^3}{3!}+\cdots
$$

The coefficient of $z^{-1}$ (the residue at $z=0$) is $1$. By the Residue Theore
m,

$$
\oint_C \frac{e^{z^2}}{z^3}\,dz=2\pi i\cdot\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{e^{z^2}
}{z^3}=2\pi i\cdot 1=2\pi i.
$$

Both methods agree.

**Conclusion.**

$$
\oint_C \frac{e^{z^2}}{z^3}\,dz = 2\pi i.
$$

Since $2\pi i \neq \pi i$, the integral does **not** equal $\pi i$. (The value $
\pi i$ would arise only if the residue were $1/2$, but the residue is in fact $1
$.)
