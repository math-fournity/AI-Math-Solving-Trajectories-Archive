## Proof

Let points on the parabola $C_1: x^2=y$ be parametrized as $(t,t^2)$. Write
$$P=(p,p^2),\quad A=(a,a^2),\quad B=(b,b^2),$$
with $p,a,b$ pairwise distinct and $p\neq 0$.

**Step 1 — Equations of the tangent lines.** The line through $P$ and $A$ has sl
ope $a+p$ (since $\frac{a^2-p^2}{a-p}=a+p$), giving
$$PA:\; y=(a+p)x-ap.$$
The signed distance from the circle center $(0,4)$ to this line, $(a+p)x-y-ap=0$
, equals the radius $1$:
$$\frac{|{-4-ap}|}{\sqrt{(a+p)^2+1}}=1 \;\Longrightarrow\; (ap+4)^2=(a+p)^2+1. \
tag{1}$$
Likewise
$$(bp+4)^2=(b+p)^2+1. \tag{2}$$

**Step 2 — A common quadratic in the slope.** Set $s=a+p$ and $t=b+p$ (the slope
s of $PA$, $PB$). Since $ap=(s-p)p=sp-p^2$, equation (1) becomes
$$(sp-p^2+4)^2=s^2+1.$$
With $c=-p^2+4$, this is $(ps+c)^2=s^2+1$, i.e.
$$(p^2-1)s^2+2pcs+(c^2-1)=0. \tag{Q}$$
The same equation holds with $t$ in place of $s$. Since $A\neq B$ we have $s\neq
 t$, so $s,t$ are the **two distinct roots** of (Q). By Vieta,
$$S:=s+t=\frac{-2pc}{p^2-1}=\frac{2p(p^2-4)}{p^2-1},\qquad R:=st=\frac{c^2-1}{p^
2-1}=\frac{(p^2-4)^2-1}{p^2-1}.$$

**Step 3 — The equal-length condition.** Since $a-p=s-2p$ and $1+(a+p)^2=1+s^2$,
$$|PA|^2=(s-2p)^2(1+s^2),\qquad |PB|^2=(t-2p)^2(1+t^2).$$
The condition $|PA|=|PB|$ gives $f(s)=f(t)$ where $f(u)=(u-2p)^2(1+u^2)$. Dividi
ng $f(s)-f(t)$ by $s-t\neq 0$ yields
$$S^3-2SR-4p(S^2-R)+(4p^2+1)S-4p=0,$$
i.e.
$$R(-2S+4p)+\bigl[S^3-4pS^2+(4p^2+1)S-4p\bigr]=0. \tag{3}$$

**Step 4 — Substitution.** Let $u=p^2-4$ (so $p^2=u+4$, $c=-u$, $p^2-1=u+3$). Th
en
$$S=\frac{2pu}{u+3},\qquad R=\frac{u^2-1}{u+3},\qquad -2S+4p=\frac{12p}{u+3}.$$
A direct expansion gives
$$S^3-4pS^2+(4p^2+1)S-4p=\frac{-2p\,(u^3-24u^2-99u+54)}{(u+3)^3}.$$
Substituting into (3) and clearing the common factor $\dfrac{p}{(u+3)^3}\neq 0$:
$$12(u^2-1)(u+3)-2(u^3-24u^2-99u+54)=0,$$
$$10u^3+84u^2+186u-144=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; 5u^3+42u^2+93u-72=0. \tag{4}$$

**Step 5 — Solving the cubic.** Testing $u=\tfrac35$:
$$5\!\left(\tfrac{27}{125}\right)+42\!\left(\tfrac{9}{25}\right)+93\!\left(\tfra
c35\right)-72=\tfrac{27}{25}+\tfrac{378}{25}+\tfrac{279}{5}-72=\tfrac{405}{25}+\
tfrac{1395}{25}-\tfrac{1800}{25}=0.\;\checkmark$$
Factoring,
$$5u^3+42u^2+93u-72=5\!\left(u-\tfrac35\right)\!\left(u^2+9u+24\right).$$
The discriminant of $u^2+9u+24$ is $81-96=-15<0$, so the **unique real root** is
 $u=\tfrac35$.

**Step 6 — Recovering $y_P$.** Since $u=p^2-4=\tfrac35$,
$$p^2=4+\tfrac35=\tfrac{23}{5}.$$
The $y$-coordinate of $P$ is $p^2$.

**Step 7 — Validity check.** With $p^2=\tfrac{23}{5}$: $p^2-1=\tfrac{18}{5}\neq
0$; the discriminant of (Q) is $4(p^2+c^2-1)=4\!\left(\tfrac{23}{5}+\tfrac{9}{25
}-1\right)=\tfrac{396}{25}>0$, giving two distinct real slopes $s,t$; and $Q(2p)
=\tfrac{1364}{25}\neq 0$, so neither root equals $2p$, ensuring $A\neq P$ and $B
\neq P$. Hence a valid configuration exists.

$$\boxed{y_P=\dfrac{23}{5}}$$
