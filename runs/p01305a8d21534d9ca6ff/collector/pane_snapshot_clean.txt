## Proof

We claim such a group exists. We use the **Tarski monster group** constructed by
 A. Yu. Olshanskii.

**Background.** For every sufficiently large prime $p$ (specifically $p > 10^{75
}$), Olshanskii proved the existence of an infinite group $G$ of exponent $p$ in
 which every proper nontrivial subgroup is cyclic of order $p$. Such a group is
called a *Tarski monster group*.

---

**Step 1: $G$ is infinite and non-abelian.**

$G$ is infinite by construction. It is non-abelian: an infinite abelian group of
 exponent $p$ is a vector space over $\mathbb{F}_p$ and has subgroups of every o
rder $p^k$, contradicting the fact that every proper subgroup of $G$ has order $
p$.

---

**Step 2: $G$ is simple.**

Suppose $N \trianglelefteq G$ is a nontrivial normal subgroup. Pick $1 \neq a \i
n N$; then $\langle a \rangle$ has order $p$. For any $g \in G$, $gag^{-1} \in N
$, so $g\langle a\rangle g^{-1} \subseteq N$.

*Case 1:* $gag^{-1} \in \langle a \rangle$ for all $g \in G$. Then $\langle a \r
angle \trianglelefteq G$. Consider $G/\langle a \rangle$. Every nontrivial eleme
nt of $G$ has order $p$; any element $g \notin \langle a \rangle$ has order $p$,
 so $g\langle a\rangle$ has order $p$ in the quotient (it cannot have order $1$,
 else $g \in \langle a \rangle$). Any proper subgroup of $G/\langle a\rangle$ li
fts to a proper subgroup of $G$ containing $\langle a\rangle$; but every proper
subgroup of $G$ has order $p$, and $\langle a\rangle$ already has order $p$, so
the only such subgroup is $\langle a\rangle$ itself. Thus $G/\langle a\rangle$ h
as no proper nontrivial subgroups, hence is cyclic of prime order $p$. This give
s $|G| = p^2$, contradicting $G$ being infinite.

*Case 2:* There exists $g$ with $gag^{-1} \notin \langle a \rangle$. Then $N$ co
ntains two distinct subgroups of order $p$ (namely $\langle a \rangle$ and $g\la
ngle a\rangle g^{-1}$), which intersect trivially, so $|N| \geq 2p - 1 > p$. Sin
ce any proper subgroup of $G$ has order $p$, $N$ cannot be proper, so $N = G$.

In both cases $N = G$, proving $G$ is simple.

---

**Step 3: $G$ is non-nilpotent.**

A nilpotent group satisfies the **normalizer condition**: every proper subgroup
$H$ is properly contained in $N_G(H)$. We show $G$ violates this. Take any prope
r nontrivial $H$ (which has order $p$). Since $H \leq N_G(H)$, the subgroup $N_G
(H)$ either equals $H$, or is a proper subgroup of order $p$ (forcing $N_G(H) =
H$), or equals $G$ (forcing $H \trianglelefteq G$). But $G$ is simple and $H$ is
 proper nontrivial, so $H$ is not normal, ruling out $N_G(H) = G$. Therefore $N_
G(H) = H$, violating the normalizer condition. Hence $G$ is not nilpotent.

---

**Step 4: Every proper nontrivial subgroup is self-normalizing.**

This was established in Step 3: for any proper nontrivial $H \leq G$, we have $N
_G(H) = H$.

---

**Conclusion.** The Tarski monster group $G$ is an infinite, non-nilpotent (inde
ed simple) group in which every proper nontrivial subgroup is self-normalizing.
Therefore, **yes**, such a group exists.
