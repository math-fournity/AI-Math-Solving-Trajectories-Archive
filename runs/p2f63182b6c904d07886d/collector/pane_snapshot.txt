## Proof

We prove that $\mathcal{H}^s([0,1]) = +\infty$ for every $s \in [0,1)$ using a *
*concavity-based optimization argument**, which differs from the standard approa
ch that relies on the pointwise inequality $\ell^s \geq \delta^{s-1}\ell$.

### Setup

For $\delta > 0$, the Hausdorff content is:
$$\mathcal{H}^s_\delta([0,1]) = \inf\left\{\sum_{i} (\operatorname{diam} U_i)^s
: [0,1] \subseteq \bigcup_i U_i,\ \operatorname{diam}(U_i) \leq \delta\right\}.$
$

Without loss of generality, each $U_i$ may be taken as a closed interval $[a_i,
b_i]$ with $\ell_i = b_i - a_i \leq \delta$ (replacing a set by its convex hull
does not decrease the diameter, so the infimum is attained by intervals).

**Key constraint.** Since $\{[a_i, b_i]\}$ covers $[0,1]$, by subadditivity of L
ebesgue measure $\lambda$:
$$\sum_i \ell_i \;\geq\; \lambda([0,1]) \;=\; 1.$$

### Case $s \in (0,1)$: Concavity-based optimization

The function $f(x) = x^s$ is **concave** on $[0,\infty)$ for $s \in (0,1)$. We s
eek the minimum of $\sum_i \ell_i^s$ over all sequences $(\ell_i)$ satisfying $0
 \leq \ell_i \leq \delta$ and $\sum_i \ell_i \geq 1$.

Since $f$ is increasing, the minimum is attained when $\sum_i \ell_i = 1$ (equal
ity). The feasible set
$$\mathcal{P} = \Big\{(\ell_i) : 0 \leq \ell_i \leq \delta,\ \textstyle\sum_i \e
ll_i = 1\Big\}$$
is a convex polytope, and $\sum_i \ell_i^s$ is a **concave** function on $\mathc
al{P}$. A concave function on a compact convex set attains its **minimum at an e
xtreme point**.

**Characterization of extreme points.** At an extreme point of $\mathcal{P}$, at
 most one coordinate lies strictly in $(0, \delta)$; all others are either $0$ o
r $\delta$. Indeed, if two coordinates $\ell_j, \ell_k$ were both strictly in $(
0, \delta)$, we could perturb them by $\pm\epsilon$ (for small $\epsilon$) while
 preserving $\sum \ell_i = 1$ and the box constraints, contradicting extremality
.

Therefore, the minimum of $\sum_i \ell_i^s$ is achieved by the configuration:
$$\underbrace{\delta,\, \delta,\, \ldots,\, \delta}_{q \text{ times}},\quad r,$$
where $q = \lfloor 1/\delta \rfloor$ and $r = 1 - q\delta \in [0, \delta)$.

The minimum value is:
$$\sum_i \ell_i^s \;=\; q\,\delta^s + r^s.$$

**Lower bound.** Since $q = \lfloor 1/\delta \rfloor \geq \frac{1}{\delta} - 1$
and $r^s \geq 0$:
$$\sum_i \ell_i^s \;\geq\; q\,\delta^s \;\geq\; \left(\frac{1}{\delta} - 1\right
)\delta^s \;=\; \delta^{s-1} - \delta^s \;=\; \delta^{s-1}(1 - \delta).$$

For $\delta \leq \frac{1}{2}$, this gives:
$$\sum_i \ell_i^s \;\geq\; \frac{1}{2}\,\delta^{s-1}.$$

Since this holds for **every** $\delta$-cover, we conclude:
$$\mathcal{H}^s_\delta([0,1]) \;\geq\; \frac{1}{2}\,\delta^{s-1} \qquad \text{fo
r all } \delta \leq \tfrac{1}{2}.$$

### Case $s = 0$

Here $\ell_i^0 = 1$ for each $\ell_i > 0$, so $\sum_i \ell_i^0$ equals the numbe
r of non-degenerate intervals in the cover. Covering $[0,1]$ with intervals of l
ength $\leq \delta$ requires at least $\lceil 1/\delta \rceil$ intervals, hence:
$$\mathcal{H}^0_\delta([0,1]) \;\geq\; \left\lceil \frac{1}{\delta} \right\rceil
 \;\geq\; \frac{1}{\delta}.$$

### Conclusion

For $s \in (0,1)$ and $\delta \leq 1/2$:
$$\mathcal{H}^s_\delta([0,1]) \;\geq\; \frac{1}{2}\,\delta^{s-1}.$$

Since $s - 1 < 0$, we have $\delta^{s-1} \to +\infty$ as $\delta \to 0^+$, and t
herefore:
$$\mathcal{H}^s([0,1]) \;=\; \lim_{\delta \to 0^+} \mathcal{H}^s_\delta([0,1]) \
;=\; +\infty.$$

For $s = 0$: $\mathcal{H}^0_\delta([0,1]) \geq 1/\delta \to +\infty$, so $\mathc
al{H}^0([0,1]) = +\infty$.

**For every $s \in [0,1)$:**
$$\boxed{\mathcal{H}^s([0,1]) \;=\; +\infty.}$$

### Remark on the strategy

The **standard proof** uses the pointwise bound $\ell_i^s \geq \delta^{s-1}\,\el
l_i$ (valid since $\ell_i \leq \delta$ and $s < 1$), then sums to get $\sum \ell
_i^s \geq \delta^{s-1}\sum \ell_i \geq \delta^{s-1}$.

Our approach is fundamentally different: it exploits the **concavity of $x \maps
to x^s$** to characterize the *exact minimizer* of $\sum \ell_i^s$ over all feas
ible covering configurations. The minimum is attained at an **extreme point** of
 the constraint polytope—where interval lengths are pushed to the boundary value
s $\{0, \delta\}$—yielding the explicit lower bound
$$\mathcal{H}^s_\delta([0,1]) \;\geq\; \lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor\,\delta^s + \bigl
(1 - \lfloor 1/\delta\rfloor\,\delta\bigr)^s \;\sim\; \delta^{s-1} \;\to\; \inft
y.$$

The key structural insight is that for $s < 1$, the concavity of $x^s$ forces th
e *worst-case* (minimum-cost) covering to use intervals of maximal allowed lengt
h $\delta$—the opposite of the $s > 1$ regime where convexity would push toward
equal-sized intervals.
