## Proof

### Setup and conventions

Let $M$ be a smooth manifold, $X$ a vector field, $u$ a smooth function, and $\a
lpha$ a differential form. We use:

- **Cartan's magic formula**: $\mathcal{L}_Y\alpha = d(\iota_Y\alpha) + \iota_Y(
d\alpha)$
- **Linearity of interior product over functions**: $\iota_{uX}\alpha = u\,\iota
_X\alpha$
- **Graded Leibniz rule for interior product**: for a $p$-form $\beta$ and a $q$
-form $\gamma$,
$$\iota_X(\beta\wedge\gamma) = (\iota_X\beta)\wedge\gamma + (-1)^p\,\beta\wedge(
\iota_X\gamma).$$

---

### Identity 1: $\mathcal{L}_{uX}\alpha = u\,\mathcal{L}_X\alpha + du\wedge(\iot
a_X\alpha)$

Apply Cartan's formula with $Y = uX$:

$$\mathcal{L}_{uX}\alpha = d(\iota_{uX}\alpha) + \iota_{uX}(d\alpha).$$

Using $\iota_{uX}\alpha = u\,\iota_X\alpha$ (and the same for $d\alpha$):

$$\mathcal{L}_{uX}\alpha = d(u\,\iota_X\alpha) + u\,\iota_X(d\alpha).$$

Expand the first term by the Leibniz rule for $d$ (with $\iota_X\alpha$ a form o
f degree $\deg\alpha - 1$):

$$d(u\,\iota_X\alpha) = du\wedge(\iota_X\alpha) + u\,d(\iota_X\alpha).$$

Substituting back:

$$\mathcal{L}_{uX}\alpha = du\wedge(\iota_X\alpha) + u\,d(\iota_X\alpha) + u\,\i
ota_X(d\alpha).$$

Factor $u$ from the last two terms and recognize Cartan's formula for $\mathcal{
L}_X\alpha$:

$$\mathcal{L}_{uX}\alpha = du\wedge(\iota_X\alpha) + u\bigl[d(\iota_X\alpha) + \
iota_X(d\alpha)\bigr] = du\wedge(\iota_X\alpha) + u\,\mathcal{L}_X\alpha.$$

Rearranging gives the stated identity:

$$\boxed{\mathcal{L}_{uX}\alpha = u\,\mathcal{L}_X\alpha + du\wedge(\iota_X\alph
a).}$$

---

### Identity 2: $du\wedge(\iota_X\alpha) = -\iota_X(du\wedge\alpha) + (Xu)\alpha
$

Apply the graded Leibniz rule for the interior product with $\beta = du$ (a $1$-
form, so $p=1$) and $\gamma = \alpha$:

$$\iota_X(du\wedge\alpha) = (\iota_X\,du)\wedge\alpha + (-1)^1\,du\wedge(\iota_X
\alpha).$$

Since $\iota_X\,du = du(X) = Xu$ is the directional derivative of $u$ along $X$
(a $0$-form), this becomes:

$$\iota_X(du\wedge\alpha) = (Xu)\,\alpha - du\wedge(\iota_X\alpha).$$

Solving for $du\wedge(\iota_X\alpha)$:

$$\boxed{du\wedge(\iota_X\alpha) = -\iota_X(du\wedge\alpha) + (Xu)\,\alpha.}$$

### Relation to the divergence theorem

Let $M$ be an oriented $n$-manifold with volume form $\omega$. The **divergence*
* of a vector field $X$ is the function $\operatorname{div}X$ defined by

$$\mathcal{L}_X\omega = (\operatorname{div}X)\,\omega.$$

By Cartan's formula, $\mathcal{L}_X\omega = d(\iota_X\omega) + \iota_X(d\omega)
= d(\iota_X\omega)$ (since $d\omega = 0$ for a top-degree form). Stokes' theorem
 then gives the **divergence theorem**:

$$\int_{\partial M}\iota_X\omega = \int_M d(\iota_X\omega) = \int_M \mathcal{L}_
X\omega = \int_M (\operatorname{div}X)\,\omega.$$

The two identities illuminate this in several ways:

1. **Identity 1 (scaling of Lie derivative)** applied to $\omega$ yields the **p
roduct rule for divergence**:
$$\operatorname{div}(uX)\,\omega = \mathcal{L}_{uX}\omega = u\,\mathcal{L}_X\ome
ga + du\wedge\iota_X\omega = u(\operatorname{div}X)\,\omega + du\wedge\iota_X\om
ega.$$
Using Identity 2 with $\alpha = \omega$ and noting $\iota_X(du\wedge\omega) = (X
u)\omega - du\wedge\iota_X\omega$, we obtain
$$du\wedge\iota_X\omega = (Xu)\omega - \iota_X(du\wedge\omega).$$
Since $du\wedge\omega$ is an $(n+1)$-form on an $n$-manifold, it vanishes, so $\
iota_X(du\wedge\omega)=0$ and thus $du\wedge\iota_X\omega = (Xu)\omega$. Therefo
re
$$\operatorname{div}(uX) = u\,\operatorname{div}X + Xu,$$
the familiar Leibniz rule for divergence — a direct consequence of the two ident
ities.

2. **Identity 2** is the graded Leibniz rule for the interior product, the algeb
raic backbone of Cartan's formula. Since the divergence theorem is just Stokes a
pplied to $d(\iota_X\omega) = \mathcal{L}_X\omega$, and Cartan's formula is what
 connects $d$, $\iota_X$, and $\mathcal{L}_X$, Identity 2 is the underlying alge
braic identity that makes the whole machinery consistent.

3. Together, the identities show that the Lie derivative decomposes into a **sca
ling part** ($u\mathcal{L}_X$) and a **correction** ($du\wedge\iota_X$), and tha
t this correction is itself governed by the interior-product Leibniz rule. This
is exactly the structure that, when integrated against the volume form, produces
 the divergence theorem and its weighted/product-rule variants (e.g., integratio
n by parts for vector fields).
