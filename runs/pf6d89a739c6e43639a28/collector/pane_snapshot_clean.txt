## Sectional Curvature as a Generalization of Gaussian Curvature

### Setup and definitions

Let $(M, g)$ be an $n$-dimensional Riemannian manifold with Levi-Civita connecti
on $\nabla$. The **Riemann curvature tensor** (as a $(0,4)$-tensor) is

$$R(X,Y,Z,W) = \langle R(X,Y)Z,\, W\rangle, \qquad R(X,Y)Z = \nabla_X\nabla_Y Z
- \nabla_Y\nabla_X Z - \nabla_{[X,Y]}Z.$$

It is skew in the first two and last two slots, and symmetric under swapping the
 two pairs: $R(X,Y,Z,W)=R(Z,W,X,Y)$.

**Sectional curvature.** For a point $p\in M$ and a 2-plane $\pi\le T_pM$ spanne
d by linearly independent vectors $X,Y$, define

$$\operatorname{sec}(\pi) \;=\; \frac{R(X,Y,X,Y)}{\,|X|^2|Y|^2 - \langle X,Y\ran
gle^2\,}.$$

The denominator is $\|X\wedge Y\|^2$, the squared area of the parallelogram span
ned by $X,Y$, so the quotient is independent of the choice of basis of $\pi$. Wh
en $X,Y$ are chosen **orthonormal**, the denominator is $1$ and

$$\operatorname{sec}(\pi) = R(X,Y,X,Y).$$

This is the convention used in the problem statement.

**Gaussian curvature.** Classically, for a surface $S\subset\mathbb{R}^3$ with s
hape operator $S_p$ (derivative of the Gauss map), the Gaussian curvature is $K(
p)=\det S_p = \kappa_1\kappa_2$, the product of the principal curvatures. The *T
heorema Egregium* of Gauss asserts that $K$ is **intrinsic**: it depends only on
 the induced metric $g$, not on the embedding. Hence $K$ is well-defined for any
 abstract 2-dimensional Riemannian manifold.

### The 2-dimensional case: only one 2-plane

Now let $\dim M = 2$. At every point $p$, the tangent space $T_pM$ is itself 2-d
imensional, so the **only** 2-plane in $T_pM$ is $\pi = T_pM$ itself. Consequent
ly the sectional curvature at $p$ is a single number,

$$\operatorname{sec}(T_pM) = R(e_1,e_2,e_1,e_2),$$

for any orthonormal frame $\{e_1,e_2\}$ at $p$.

### The Riemann tensor in 2D has one independent component

By the symmetries of $R$, on a 2-manifold the only possibly-nonzero component (i
n an orthonormal frame) is $R_{1212}=R(e_1,e_2,e_1,e_2)$; all other components a
re either zero or equal to $\pm R_{1212}$ by skew-symmetry and pair-symmetry. Co
ncretely,

$$R_{1212} = -R_{2112} = -R_{1221} = R_{2121}, \qquad R_{ijkl}=0 \text{ whenever
 } \{i,j\}=\{k,l\}\text{ with a repeated index in a pair}.$$

So the entire curvature tensor is determined by the scalar $R_{1212}$.

### Identification with Gaussian curvature

The intrinsic characterization of Gaussian curvature (equivalent to the Theorema
 Egregium) is:

$$K(p) \;=\; R_{1212}(p) \;=\; R(e_1,e_2,e_1,e_2)(p)$$

in any orthonormal frame. Equivalently, in an arbitrary (non-orthonormal) frame
this reads

$$K \;=\; \frac{R_{1221}}{\det g} \;=\; \frac{R(X,Y,X,Y)}{|X|^2|Y|^2-\langle X,Y
\rangle^2},$$

which is *exactly* the formula for $\operatorname{sec}(\pi)$ with $\pi = T_pM$.

Therefore, on a 2-dimensional Riemannian manifold,

$$\boxed{\;\operatorname{sec}(T_pM) \;=\; K(p)\;}$$

—the sectional curvature of the unique tangent 2-plane coincides with the Gaussi
an curvature.

### Why this is a genuine generalization

The identification above shows that sectional curvature is the natural higher-di
mensional successor of Gaussian curvature, in three precise senses:

1. **Dimensional reduction.** When $n=2$, the sectional curvature *collapses* to
 the Gaussian curvature: there is only one 2-plane, and its sec-value is $K$. Th
us $\operatorname{sec}$ is not a new invariant in 2D—it *is* $K$, just written i
n a frame-independent way.

2. **Plane-by-plane measurement.** For $n\ge 3$, $T_pM$ contains many 2-planes,
and $\operatorname{sec}(\pi)$ measures the curvature "in the direction of $\pi$.
" Each value $\operatorname{sec}(\pi)$ is the Gaussian curvature that the 2-dime
nsional submanifold generated by geodesics tangent to $\pi$ would have at $p$ (t
his is the content of the *geometric interpretation*: the sectional curvature eq
uals the Gaussian curvature of the totally geodesic surface tangent to $\pi$, wh
en such a surface exists locally). So sectional curvature literally *samples Gau
ssian curvature along each 2-direction*.

3. **Recovery of the full tensor.** The sectional curvature determines the entir
e Riemann curvature tensor by polarization (the *Brianchon–Cartan lemma*): knowi
ng $\operatorname{sec}(\pi)$ for all 2-planes $\pi$ determines $R$ uniquely. In
2D this is trivial because there is only one plane; in higher dimensions it is a
 nontrivial theorem. Hence $\operatorname{sec}$ carries exactly the same curvatu
re information as $R$, packaged as a function on the Grassmannian $\operatorname
{Gr}_2(T_pM)$ rather than as a 4-tensor.

### Concrete consistency check (constant curvature)

On the round sphere $S^2(r)\subset\mathbb{R}^3$, the principal curvatures are bo
th $1/r$, so the classical Gaussian curvature is $K=1/r^2$. The Riemann tensor o
f a space of constant curvature $c$ satisfies $R(X,Y,Z,W)=c\bigl(\langle X,Z\ran
gle\langle Y,W\rangle-\langle X,W\rangle\langle Y,Z\rangle\bigr)$. For orthonorm
al $e_1,e_2$ this gives $R(e_1,e_2,e_1,e_2)=c$. For $S^2(r)$, $c=1/r^2$, so

$$\operatorname{sec}(T_pS^2(r)) = R(e_1,e_2,e_1,e_2) = \frac{1}{r^2} = K,$$

confirming the identification on a standard example. The same check works for th
e hyperbolic plane $H^2$ (where $K=-1$ and $\operatorname{sec}=-1$) and for the
flat plane $\mathbb{R}^2$ (where both are $0$).

### Summary

The sectional curvature $\operatorname{sec}(\pi)=R(X,Y,X,Y)$ (for orthonormal $X
,Y$ spanning $\pi$) generalizes the Gaussian curvature as follows. On a 2-dimens
ional Riemannian manifold there is a single tangent 2-plane $\pi=T_pM$ at each p
oint, and the only independent component of the Riemann tensor is $R_{1212}$. By
 the intrinsic (Theorema-Egregium) characterization of Gaussian curvature, $K(p)
=R_{1212}(p)=\operatorname{sec}(T_pM)(p)$. Thus in 2D the sectional curvature *i
s* the Gaussian curvature, while in higher dimensions it extends the same idea b
y measuring, for each 2-plane $\pi\le T_pM$, the Gaussian curvature associated t
o that 2-direction—and these values collectively determine the full Riemann curv
ature tensor.
