## Fourier Series of $f(x)=\int_0^x \ln\sqrt{|\cot(t/2)|}\,dt$

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains only the problem; no solut
ion, sketch, or answer is embedded. Proceeding.

---

### Step 1. Simplify the integrand

Set $g(t)=\ln\sqrt{|\cot(t/2)|}=\tfrac12\ln|\cot(t/2)|$. Since $\cot(t/2)$ is od
d, $|\cot(t/2)|$ is even, hence $g$ is **even**. Consequently $f(x)=\int_0^x g(t
)\,dt$ is **odd**:
$$f(-x)=\int_0^{-x}g(t)\,dt=-\int_0^{x}g(-u)\,du=-\int_0^x g(u)\,du=-f(x).$$
So $f$ admits a **pure sine series** $f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n\sin(nx)$.

### Step 2. Fourier series of $g(t)=\frac12\ln|\cot(t/2)|$

We use the two classical Fourier expansions (obtained from $\operatorname{Re}\ln
(1-e^{it})$ and $\operatorname{Re}\ln(1+e^{it})$):

$$\ln\!\left|2\sin\tfrac{t}{2}\right|=-\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\cos(nt)}{n},\qqua
d \ln\!\left|2\cos\tfrac{t}{2}\right|=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{(-1)^{n+1}\cos(nt)}
{n}.$$

Subtracting (the $\ln 2$ terms cancel):

$$\ln|\cot(t/2)|=\ln|\cos(t/2)|-\ln|\sin(t/2)|=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{(-1)^{n+1}
+1}{n}\cos(nt).$$

For **even** $n$: $(-1)^{n+1}+1=0$. For **odd** $n=2k+1$: $(-1)^{2k+2}+1=2$. Hen
ce

$$\boxed{\;\ln|\cot(t/2)|=2\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{\cos((2k+1)t)}{2k+1}\;},\qquad
 g(t)=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{\cos((2k+1)t)}{2k+1}.$$

The singularities of $g$ at $t=0,\pm\pi$ are logarithmic, so $g\in L^1[-\pi,\pi]
$, and the above is its Fourier series (with $a_{2k+1}=\frac{1}{2k+1}$, all othe
r coefficients zero, $a_0=0$).

### Step 3. Term-by-term integration

By the standard theorem: if $g\in L^1[-\pi,\pi]$ has Fourier series $\frac{a_0}{
2}+\sum(a_n\cos nt+b_n\sin nt)$, then
$$\int_0^x g(t)\,dt=\frac{a_0}{2}x+\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{a_n\sin(nx)}{n}+\sum_{
n=1}^\infty\frac{b_n(1-\cos(nx))}{n},$$
and the integrated series converges **uniformly** to the integral.

Here $a_0=0$, $b_n=0$ for all $n$, $a_{2k+1}=\frac{1}{2k+1}$, $a_{2k}=0$. Theref
ore

$$f(x)=\int_0^x g(t)\,dt=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{1}{2k+1}\cdot\frac{\sin((2k+1)x)
}{2k+1}=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{\sin((2k+1)x)}{(2k+1)^2}.$$

### Step 4. Verification that this is the Fourier series of $f$

Since the integrated series converges uniformly to $f$ and is a trigonometric se
ries, it **is** the Fourier series of $f$. We confirm the coefficients directly.
 As $f$ is odd, $a_n=0$ and
$$b_n=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi f(x)\sin(nx)\,dx.$$
Integration by parts, using $f(0)=0$ and $f(\pi)=\frac12\int_0^\pi\ln|\cot(t/2)|
\,dt=\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln(\cot u)\,du=0$ (since $\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln\sin u\,du=\int_0
^{\pi/2}\ln\cos u\,du=-\frac{\pi}{2}\ln 2$):
$$b_n=\frac{2}{n\pi}\int_0^\pi g(x)\cos(nx)\,dx=\frac{2}{n\pi}\cdot\frac12\int_{
-\pi}^{\pi}g(x)\cos(nx)\,dx=\frac{a_n}{n}.$$
Thus $b_{2k+1}=\frac{1}{(2k+1)^2}$ and $b_{2k}=0$, exactly matching the series o
btained.

### Result

$$\boxed{\,f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin\big((2k+1)x\big)}{(2k+1)^{2}}\,},\
qquad x\in[-\pi,\pi].$$

Equivalently, $f(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{\substack{n=1\\ n\text{ odd}}}^{\infty}\f
rac{\sin(nx)}{n^{2}}$, with all even sine coefficients and all cosine coefficien
ts equal to zero. The series converges uniformly (hence absolutely) on $[-\pi,\p
i]$.
