$$\langle (\partial_x^2 - \partial_y^2)u,\, \phi\rangle = \int_\Omega u\,(\parti

artial_y)(\partial_x + \partial_y)$. Set $\psi := (\partial_x + \partial_y)\phi$

$$I = \int_{T_+} (\partial_x - \partial_y)\psi\;dx\,dy.$$

**Divergence theorem.** The operator $\partial_x - \partial_y = \nabla \cdot F$

$$I = \int_{\partial T_+} F \cdot n\;d\sigma.$$

The boundary $\partial T_+$ consists of three pieces:

$$F \cdot n\,d\sigma = \frac{(\psi,-\psi)\cdot(-1,1)}{\sqrt{2}}\;\sqrt{2}\,dt =
-2\,\psi(t,t)\,dt.$$

**Evaluate on the diagonal.** Since $\psi = (\partial_x+\partial_y)\phi$,
$$\psi(t,t) = \partial_x\phi(t,t) + \partial_y\phi(t,t) = \frac{d}{dt}\big[\phi(
t,t)\big].$$

Therefore
$$I = -2\int_0^1 \frac{d}{dt}\phi(t,t)\,dt = -2\big[\phi(t,t)\big]_{0}^{1} = -2\
big(\phi(1,1) - \phi(0,0)\big).$$
