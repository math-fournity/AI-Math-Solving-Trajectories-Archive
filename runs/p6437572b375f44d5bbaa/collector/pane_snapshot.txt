**Claim.** Yes. For every $1 \le p \le \infty$, if $(f_n) \subset L^p([0,1])$ sa
tisfies $\|f_n\|_p \to 0$, then there exists a subsequence $(f_{n_k})$ with $f_{
n_k}(x) \to 0$ for almost every $x \in (0,1)$.

**Proof.**

**Step 1. Reduction to convergence in measure.** Assume first $1 \le p < \infty$
. For any $\varepsilon > 0$, Chebyshev's (Markov's) inequality gives
$$
m\bigl(\{x \in [0,1] : |f_n(x)| > \varepsilon\}\bigr)
\;\le\; \frac{1}{\varepsilon^p}\int_0^1 |f_n|^p\,dm
\;=\; \frac{\|f_n\|_p^p}{\varepsilon^p}.
$$
Since $\|f_n\|_p \to 0$, the right-hand side tends to $0$, so
$$
m\bigl(\{|f_n| > \varepsilon\}\bigr) \to 0 \qquad \text{for every } \varepsilon
> 0,
$$
i.e. $f_n \to 0$ in measure.

For $p = \infty$, $\|f_n\|_\infty \to 0$ means $f_n \to 0$ uniformly, hence a.e.
 (in fact everywhere up to a common null set), so the conclusion is immediate. H
enceforth assume $1 \le p < \infty$.

**Step 2. Extracting an a.e.-convergent subsequence.** Since $f_n \to 0$ in meas
ure, for each $k \ge 1$ (with $\varepsilon = 1/k$ and $\delta = 2^{-k}$) there e
xists $N_k$ such that
$$
n \ge N_k \implies m\bigl(\{|f_n| > 1/k\}\bigr) < 2^{-k}.
$$
Choose indices recursively: pick $n_1 \ge N_1$, and having chosen $n_{k-1}$, pic
k $n_k > n_{k-1}$ with $n_k \ge N_k$. This is possible because the tail conditio
n holds for all large $n$. Then
$$
m\bigl(\{|f_{n_k}| > 1/k\}\bigr) < 2^{-k} \qquad \text{for all } k,
$$
so
$$
\sum_{k=1}^\infty m\bigl(\{|f_{n_k}| > 1/k\}\bigr) < \sum_{k=1}^\infty 2^{-k} <
\infty.
$$

**Step 3. Borel–Cantelli.** Define $E_k = \{x : |f_{n_k}(x)| > 1/k\}$. Since $\s
um_k m(E_k) < \infty$, the Borel–Cantelli lemma yields
$$
m\bigl(\limsup_{k\to\infty} E_k\bigr) = 0,
$$
where $\limsup E_k = \bigcap_{K}\bigcup_{k\ge K} E_k$ is the set of $x$ belongin
g to infinitely many $E_k$.

Let $x \notin \limsup E_k$. Then $x$ belongs to only finitely many $E_k$, so the
re exists $K(x)$ such that for all $k \ge K(x)$, $x \notin E_k$, i.e.
$$
|f_{n_k}(x)| \le \frac{1}{k} \qquad \text{for all } k \ge K(x).
$$
Letting $k \to \infty$ gives $f_{n_k}(x) \to 0$.

Thus $f_{n_k}(x) \to 0$ for every $x$ outside the null set $\limsup E_k$, i.e. f
or almost every $x \in [0,1]$, and in particular for almost every $x \in (0,1)$.

**Conclusion.** In every case ($1 \le p \le \infty$) the desired subsequence exi
sts. $\blacksquare$
