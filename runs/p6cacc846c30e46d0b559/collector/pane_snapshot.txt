Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a global section of $\Omega
^1_X$ (sheaf of holomorphic/regular 1-forms) on a smooth irreducible curve that
vanishes on $X - \{p\}$ must be zero.

**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

$X$ is an irreducible curve of dimension 1 with closed points. Since $\dim X = 1
$, the space is not a single point, so $U := X - \{p\}$ is a **nonempty** open s
ubset. By irreducibility, every nonempty open is dense, so $U$ is dense in $X$.

**Key claim:** For $F = \Omega^1_X$ on a smooth curve, the restriction map $\Ome
ga^1_X(X) \to \Omega^1_X(U)$ is **injective**. Hence $s|_U = 0 \implies s = 0$.

I verify this in both the analytic and algebraic settings.

---

**Case 1 — Analytic ($X$ a Riemann surface, $\Omega^1_X$ = holomorphic 1-forms).
**

Let $\omega \in \Omega^1_X(X)$ with $\omega|_U = 0$. Pick any point $q \in X$ an
d a coordinate chart $(V, z)$ around $q$. On $V$, write $\omega|_V = f\,dz$ with
 $f$ holomorphic. The set $V \cap U$ is a nonempty open subset of $V$ (nonempty
because $U$ is dense). On $V \cap U$, we have $f\,dz = 0$, so $f|_{V \cap U} = 0
$. By the **identity theorem** for holomorphic functions, a holomorphic function
 vanishing on a nonempty open set vanishes identically: $f \equiv 0$ on $V$. Sin
ce $q \in X$ was arbitrary, $\omega = 0$ on all of $X$. $\square$

---

**Case 2 — Algebraic ($X$ a smooth algebraic curve, $\Omega^1_X$ = sheaf of regu
lar/Kähler 1-forms).**

Since $X$ is smooth of dimension 1, $\Omega^1_X$ is **locally free of rank 1** (
an invertible sheaf). On an integral scheme (irreducible + reduced, which holds
here), a locally free sheaf is **torsion-free**.

The function field $K(X)$ is the stalk at the generic point. Both $\Omega^1_X(X)
$ and $\Omega^1_X(U)$ embed into the one-dimensional $K(X)$-vector space $\Omega
^1_{K(X)/k}$ of rational 1-forms:

$$\Omega^1_X(X) \hookrightarrow \Omega^1_{K(X)/k} \hookleftarrow \Omega^1_X(U).$
$

The restriction map $\Omega^1_X(X) \to \Omega^1_X(U)$ is, under these embeddings
, simply the **inclusion** of subsets of $\Omega^1_{K(X)/k}$ (a regular 1-form o
n $X$ is a rational 1-form regular at every point; restricting to $U$ doesn't ch
ange the underlying rational 1-form). Therefore the restriction map is **injecti
ve**.

Concretely: if $s|_U = 0$, then as a rational 1-form, $s = 0$ in $\Omega^1_{K(X)
/k}$ (it vanishes on a dense open), hence $s = 0$ as a global section. $\square$

---

**Why the general sheaf axiom alone is insufficient — and why $\Omega^1_X$ is sp
ecial.**

For an **arbitrary** sheaf of abelian groups $F$, the answer would be **No**. Th
e sheaf axiom (gluing) only says that a section is determined by its restriction
s to an **open cover** with agreement on overlaps. Here $U = X - \{p\}$ is a sin
gle open set, not a cover, so the axiom gives no constraint on $s$ near $p$.

**Counterexample for general $F$:** The skyscraper sheaf $F = i_{p,*}A$ (pushfor
ward of an abelian group $A$ along $i: \{p\} \hookrightarrow X$) satisfies $F(U)
 = 0$ (since $p \notin U$) but $F(X) = A$ (since $p \in X$). Any nonzero $a \in
A$ gives a global section $s$ with $s|_U = 0$ but $s \neq 0$.

The crucial property of $\Omega^1_X$ that makes the answer **Yes** is that it is
 **torsion-free** (being locally free on an integral scheme). Torsion-freeness m
eans sections are determined by their values on any dense open set — equivalentl
y, the restriction map to a dense open is injective. The skyscraper sheaf has to
rsion (sections supported at a point), so it fails this property.

---

**Conclusion.** For $X$ a smooth irreducible curve and $F = \Omega^1_X$, the res
triction map $\Omega^1_X(X) \to \Omega^1_X(X - \{p\})$ is injective (by the iden
tity theorem in the analytic case, or by torsion-freeness/embedding into rationa
l 1-forms in the algebraic case). Therefore $s|_{X-\{p\}} = 0$ implies $s = 0$.
