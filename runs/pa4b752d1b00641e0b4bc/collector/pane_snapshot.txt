## Proof

Let $f \colon (\mathbb{Q}, +) \to (\mathbb{Q}, +)$ be a group homomorphism. We d
etermine all such $f$, and in particular all automorphisms.

**Step 1: Every homomorphism is $\mathbb{Q}$-linear (as a map of $\mathbb{Q}$-ve
ctor spaces).**

Since $(\mathbb{Q}, +)$ is a divisible, torsion-free abelian group, it carries a
 natural $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space structure, where scalar multiplication is jus
t the group operation repeated: $n \cdot x$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, and $\frac{p
}{q}\cdot x$ is the unique element $y$ with $qy = px$.

For any group homomorphism $f$ and any $n \in \mathbb{Z}$:
$$f(nx) = n f(x).$$
For $\frac{p}{q} \in \mathbb{Q}$, let $x \in \mathbb{Q}$. Then $q\!\left(\frac{p
}{q}x\right) = px$, so
$$q\, f\!\left(\tfrac{p}{q}x\right) = f(px) = p\, f(x),$$
hence $f\!\left(\frac{p}{q}x\right) = \frac{p}{q} f(x)$. Thus $f$ is $\mathbb{Q}
$-linear.

**Step 2: $f$ is determined by $f(1)$.**

Since $\mathbb{Q}$ is a $1$-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space with basis $\{
1\}$, every $\mathbb{Q}$-linear map $f$ satisfies
$$f(x) = x\, f(1) \quad \text{for all } x \in \mathbb{Q}.$$
Conversely, for any $a \in \mathbb{Q}$, the map $x \mapsto ax$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$
-linear (hence group) homomorphism. So homomorphisms $\mathbb{Q} \to \mathbb{Q}$
 are in bijection with $\mathbb{Q}$ via $f \leftrightarrow f(1)$.

**Step 3: Automorphisms correspond to $\mathbb{Q}^{\times} = \mathbb{Q}\setminus
\{0\}$.**

The map $x \mapsto ax$ is an isomorphism iff it is bijective, i.e., iff $a \neq
0$. Therefore
$$\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Q}, +) \;\cong\; (\mathbb{Q}^{\times}, \cdot) \;=\;
 \mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\}.$$

**Step 4: Cardinality.**

The set $\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\}$ is countably infinite, so
$$\left|\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Q}, +)\right| = |\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\}| =
\aleph_0.$$
