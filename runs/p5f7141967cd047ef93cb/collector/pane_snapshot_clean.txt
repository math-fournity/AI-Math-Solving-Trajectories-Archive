## Proof

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains no solutions, hints, or fo
rmalization notes. Proceeding.

Let $g(x) = \frac{1}{1+P(x)^2}$. We compute
$$f(t)^{1/t} = \underbrace{(\sqrt{t})^{1/t}}_{\to\, 1} \cdot \left(\int_{-\infty
}^{\infty} g(x)^t\, dx\right)^{1/t},$$
so the limit equals $\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
 g(x)^t\,dx\right)^{1/t}$, provided this limit exists.

---

### Setup: $g$ is bounded, integrable, and attains its supremum

Since $P$ has no real roots, $P(x)\neq 0$ for all $x\in\mathbb{R}$. A real polyn
omial with no real roots has even degree $2d\geq 2$, so $|P(x)|\to\infty$ as $|x
|\to\infty$. The continuous function $P(x)^2$ therefore tends to $+\infty$ at bo
th ends and attains a **positive** minimum
$$M := \min_{x\in\mathbb{R}} P(x)^2 > 0 \quad\text{at some }x_0\in\mathbb{R}.$$

Consequences for $g$:
- $0 < g(x) \leq 1$ for all $x$, with $g(x)<1$ everywhere (since $P(x)^2>0$).
- $\displaystyle\sup_{x} g(x) = \frac{1}{1+M}$, attained at $x_0$.
- As $|x|\to\infty$, $g(x)\sim C/x^{4d}$, so $g\in L^1(\mathbb{R})$ and $g\in L^
t$ for every $t\geq 1$.

---

### Lower bound: $\liminf \geq \sup g$

Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and set $A_\varepsilon = \{x : g(x) \geq \sup g - \varepsilo
n\}$. Since $g$ is continuous and attains its maximum at $x_0$, the set $A_\vare
psilon$ contains a neighborhood of $x_0$, hence has positive (in fact finite) me
asure $|A_\varepsilon|>0$. Then
$$\int g(x)^t\,dx \geq \int_{A_\varepsilon} g(x)^t\,dx \geq (\sup g - \varepsilo
n)^t\,|A_\varepsilon|.$$
Taking $1/t$ powers:
$$\left(\int g^t\right)^{1/t} \geq (\sup g - \varepsilon)\,|A_\varepsilon|^{1/t}
 \xrightarrow{t\to\infty} \sup g - \varepsilon.$$
Since $\varepsilon>0$ is arbitrary, $\liminf_{t\to\infty}\left(\int g^t\right)^{
1/t} \geq \sup g$.

---

### Upper bound: $\limsup \leq \sup g$

Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and let $B = A_\varepsilon = \{g \geq \sup g - \varepsilon\}
$ (bounded, finite measure). Split:
$$\int g^t = \int_B g^t + \int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus B} g^t.$$

- On $B$: $g \leq \sup g$, so $\int_B g^t \leq (\sup g)^t\,|B|$.
- On $\mathbb{R}\setminus B$: $g < \sup g - \varepsilon$, and since $g\leq 1$ we
 have $g^t = g\cdot g^{t-1} \leq g\cdot(\sup g - \varepsilon)^{t-1}$, giving
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus B} g^t \leq (\sup g-\varepsilon)^{t-1}\int_{\mathbb{
R}\setminus B} g \leq (\sup g-\varepsilon)^{t-1}\|g\|_{L^1}.$$

Combining and using $(a+b)^{1/t}\leq 2^{1/t}\max(a,b)^{1/t}$ for $a,b>0$:
$$\left(\int g^t\right)^{1/t} \leq 2^{1/t}\max\!\Big((\sup g)\,|B|^{1/t},\;(\sup
 g-\varepsilon)^{(t-1)/t}\,\|g\|_{L^1}^{1/t}\Big).$$
As $t\to\infty$: $2^{1/t}\to 1$, $|B|^{1/t}\to 1$, $(\sup g-\varepsilon)^{(t-1)/
t}\to \sup g-\varepsilon$, $\|g\|_{L^1}^{1/t}\to 1$. Hence
$$\limsup_{t\to\infty}\left(\int g^t\right)^{1/t} \leq \max(\sup g,\;\sup g - \v
arepsilon) = \sup g.$$

---

### Conclusion

The lower and upper bounds together give
$$\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} g(x)^t\,dx\right)^{1/t} = \sup_
{x\in\mathbb{R}} g(x) = \frac{1}{1+M} = \frac{1}{1+\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}} P(x)^2}
.$$

Since $(\sqrt{t})^{1/t}\to 1$, we obtain

$$\boxed{\lim_{t\to\infty} f(t)^{1/t} = \frac{1}{1+\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathb
b{R}} P(x)^2}}.$$
