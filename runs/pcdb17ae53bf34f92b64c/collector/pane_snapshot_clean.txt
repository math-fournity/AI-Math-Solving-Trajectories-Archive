## Proof

**Series representation.** Using $(1)_n = n!$ and $\frac{(b)_n}{(b+1)_n} = \frac
{b}{b+n}$:

$$_2F_1(1,b;b+1;z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(1)_n(b)_n}{(b+1)_n}\frac{z^n}{n!
} = b\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{n+b}$$

This series converges for $|z|<1$ and diverges at $z=1$ (harmonic-type), so both
 terms in the limit diverge — but their difference may converge.

**Euler integral representation.** For $\operatorname{Re}(b)>0$:

$$_2F_1(1,b;b+1;z) = b\int_0^1 \frac{t^{b-1}}{1-zt}\,dt$$

**Evaluation at $z = 1-\epsilon$ (inside the disk, no singularity).** The integr
and $\frac{t^{b-1}}{1-(1-\epsilon)t}$ has no pole in $[0,1]$ since $1-(1-\epsilo
n)t > 0$ for $t\in[0,1]$. The value is purely real.

**Evaluation at $z = 1+\epsilon$ (on the branch cut, principal branch = limit fr
om above).** Set $z = (1+\epsilon)+i\delta$, $\delta\to 0^+$. The integrand has
a pole at $t_0 = \frac{1}{1+\epsilon}\in(0,1)$. Near $t_0$:

$$1-zt \approx -(1+\epsilon)\bigl(t - t_0 + i\eta\bigr), \quad \eta = \frac{\del
ta\, t_0}{1+\epsilon} > 0$$

By the Sokhotski–Plemelj formula, $\frac{1}{t-t_0+i\eta}\to \operatorname{P.V.}\
frac{1}{t-t_0} - i\pi\delta(t-t_0)$, so:

$$\int_0^1 \frac{t^{b-1}}{1-zt}\,dt \;\longrightarrow\; \frac{1}{1+\epsilon}\ope
ratorname{P.V.}\!\int_0^1 \frac{t^{b-1}}{t_0-t}\,dt \;+\; \frac{i\pi}{1+\epsilon
}\,t_0^{\,b-1}$$

**Imaginary part of the difference.** Since $_2F_1(1,b;b+1;1-\epsilon)$ is real:

$$\operatorname{Im}\bigl[\text{diff}\bigr] = \frac{b\pi}{1+\epsilon}\cdot\frac{1
}{(1+\epsilon)^{b-1}} = \frac{b\pi}{(1+\epsilon)^b} \;\xrightarrow{\epsilon\to 0
^+}\; b\pi$$

**Real part of the difference.** Write $t^{b-1} = t_0^{b-1} + (t^{b-1}-t_0^{b-1}
)$ and similarly for $t_1 = \frac{1}{1-\epsilon}$. The singular parts give:

$$\operatorname{P.V.}\!\int_0^1 \frac{dt}{t_0 - t} = \ln\frac{t_0}{1-t_0} = -\ln
\epsilon, \qquad \int_0^1 \frac{dt}{t_1-t} = \ln\frac{t_1}{t_1-1} = -\ln\epsilon
$$

These are **equal**, so the logarithmic divergences cancel. The remaining real p
art is:

$$b\ln\epsilon\left[\frac{1}{(1-\epsilon)^b}-\frac{1}{(1+\epsilon)^b}\right] + b
\left[\frac{R_0}{1+\epsilon}-\frac{R_1}{1-\epsilon}\right]$$

where $R_0 = \int_0^1 \frac{t^{b-1}-t_0^{b-1}}{t_0-t}\,dt$ and $R_1 = \int_0^1 \
frac{t^{b-1}-t_1^{b-1}}{t_1-t}\,dt$ are regular (bounded) integrals. As $\epsilo
n\to 0^+$: $t_0,t_1\to 1$, so $R_0,R_1 \to \int_0^1\frac{t^{b-1}-1}{1-t}\,dt =:
R^*$ (the same limit), and:

- First term: $\frac{1}{(1-\epsilon)^b}-\frac{1}{(1+\epsilon)^b} \approx 2b\epsi
lon$, so $2b^2\epsilon\ln\epsilon \to 0$.
- Second term: $\frac{R_0}{1+\epsilon}-\frac{R_1}{1-\epsilon} \to R^* - R^* = 0$
.

Therefore $\operatorname{Re}[\text{diff}] \to 0$.

**Verification via connection formula ($c=a+b$ logarithmic case).** The analytic
 continuation around $z=1$ gives:

$$_2F_1(1,b;b+1;z) = b\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(b)_n}{n!}\bigl[\psi(n+1)-\psi(b+
n)-\ln(1-z)\bigr](1-z)^n$$

For $z=1+\epsilon+i0^+$: $1-z = -\epsilon-i0^+$, so $\ln(1-z)=\ln\epsilon - i\pi

For $z=1-\epsilon$: $1-z=\epsilon$, so $\ln(1-z)=\ln\epsilon$.

The $n=0$ term difference is $b\cdot[-(\ln\epsilon-i\pi)+\ln\epsilon] = i\pi b$.
 All $n\geq 1$ terms are $O(\epsilon^n\ln\epsilon)\to 0$. This confirms the resu

**Check at $b=1$:** $_2F_1(1,1;2;z)=\frac{-\ln(1-z)}{z}$. Then $\frac{-(\ln\epsi
