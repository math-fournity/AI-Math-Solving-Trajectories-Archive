## Solution

Consider the helicoid $\sigma(u,v) = (u\cos v,\, u\sin v,\, v)$, with the origin
 $p=(0,0,0)$ corresponding to $(u,v)=(0,0)$.

### First partial derivatives

$$\sigma_u = (\cos v,\, \sin v,\, 0), \qquad \sigma_v = (-u\sin v,\, u\cos v,\,
1).$$

At $(u,v)=(0,0)$:
$$\sigma_u = (1,0,0), \qquad \sigma_v = (0,0,1).$$

### First fundamental form at the origin

$$E = \sigma_u\cdot\sigma_u = 1, \quad F = \sigma_u\cdot\sigma_v = 0, \quad G =
\sigma_v\cdot\sigma_v = 1.$$

Hence $\{\sigma_u, \sigma_v\} = \{(1,0,0),(0,0,1)\}$ is already an orthonormal b
asis of the tangent plane $T_p\sigma$.

### Unit normal

$$\sigma_u\times\sigma_v\big|_{(0,0)} = (1,0,0)\times(0,0,1) = (0,-1,0), \qquad
|\sigma_u\times\sigma_v|=1.$$

So $N = (0,-1,0)$.

### Second partial derivatives

$$\sigma_{uu}=(0,0,0), \qquad \sigma_{uv}=(-\sin v,\,\cos v,\, 0), \qquad \sigma
_{vv}=(-u\cos v,\,-u\sin v,\, 0).$$

At $(0,0)$:
$$\sigma_{uu}=(0,0,0), \qquad \sigma_{uv}=(0,1,0), \qquad \sigma_{vv}=(0,0,0).$$

### Second fundamental form at the origin

$$e = N\cdot\sigma_{uu} = 0, \qquad f = N\cdot\sigma_{uv} = (0,-1,0)\cdot(0,1,0)
 = -1, \qquad g = N\cdot\sigma_{vv} = 0.$$

### General unit tangent vector

Since $E=G=1$, $F=0$, a general unit tangent vector is
$$T = \cos\theta\,\sigma_u + \sin\theta\,\sigma_v = (\cos\theta,\, 0,\, \sin\the
ta),$$
where $\theta$ is the angle between $T$ and $\sigma_u$.

### Normal curvature

The normal curvature in direction $T=\cos\theta\,\sigma_u+\sin\theta\,\sigma_v$
is

$$\kappa_n = \frac{e\cos^2\theta + 2f\cos\theta\sin\theta + g\sin^2\theta}{E\cos
^2\theta + 2F\cos\theta\sin\theta + G\sin^2\theta}.$$

The denominator equals $1$. Substituting $e=0$, $f=-1$, $g=0$:

$$\boxed{\kappa_n = -2\cos\theta\,\sin\theta = -\sin 2\theta.}$$

### Verification (Euler's theorem)

The principal curvatures are the eigenvalues of the shape operator. With $F=0$ a
nd $E=G=1$, the shape operator matrix is $\begin{pmatrix} e & f \\ f & g \end{pm
atrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, whose eigenvalues are
$\kappa_1 = +1$, $\kappa_2 = -1$ (mean curvature $H=0$, confirming the helicoid
is minimal). The principal directions are $\theta = \pi/4$ (for $\kappa_1=+1$) a
nd $\theta = -\pi/4$ (for $\kappa_2=-1$). Euler's formula gives

$$\kappa_n = \kappa_1\cos^2(\theta-\pi/4) + \kappa_2\sin^2(\theta-\pi/4) = \cos^
2(\theta-\pi/4)-\sin^2(\theta-\pi/4) = \cos(2\theta-\pi/2)=\sin 2\theta.$$

Wait — this differs by a sign from the direct computation. The sign depends on t
he choice of normal orientation. With $N=(0,-1,0)$ the shape operator is $S = -d
N$, and the matrix of $S$ in the basis $\{\sigma_u,\sigma_v\}$ is $\begin{pmatri
x} e & f \\ f & g \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}
$, giving eigenvalues $\pm 1$ and $\kappa_n = -\sin 2\theta$ as computed directl
y. The Euler check with this convention: principal direction for eigenvalue $-1$
 is $(1,1)/\sqrt2$ i.e. $\theta=\pi/4$, and for $+1$ is $(1,-1)/\sqrt2$ i.e. $\t
heta=-\pi/4$. Then
$$\kappa_n = (-1)\cos^2(\theta-\pi/4) + (+1)\sin^2(\theta-\pi/4) = -\cos(2\theta
-\pi/2) = -\sin 2\theta,$$
confirming consistency.

### Result

The normal curvature of the helicoid at the origin in the direction of a general
 unit tangent vector making angle $\theta$ with $\sigma_u$ is

$$\kappa_n = -\sin 2\theta.$$
