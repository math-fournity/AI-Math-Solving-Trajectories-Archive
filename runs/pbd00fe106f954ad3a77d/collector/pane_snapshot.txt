## Proof

We evaluate \( \int_{\mathbb{R}} \lfloor x \rfloor \, d\mu \), where \( \mu(A) =
 \#(\mathbb{N} \cap A) \) is the counting measure concentrated on \( \mathbb{N}
\).

**Step 1: Identify the measure.** For any Borel set \( A \subseteq \mathbb{R} \)
, \( \mu(A) = \#(\mathbb{N} \cap A) \). This is a purely atomic measure supporte
d on \( \mathbb{N} = \{1, 2, 3, \dots\} \) (taking \( \mathbb{N} \) to start at
1; the convention does not affect the conclusion), with each natural number carr
ying mass 1.

**Step 2: Reduce the integral to a sum.** For a non-negative measurable function
 \( f \), integration against a counting measure on a countable set \( S \) give
s
\[
\int_{\mathbb{R}} f \, d\mu = \sum_{n \in S} f(n).
\]
Here \( S = \mathbb{N} \) and \( f(x) = \lfloor x \rfloor \). Since \( \lfloor x
 \rfloor \geq 0 \) for \( x \geq 0 \), and the measure is supported on \( \mathb
b{N} \subseteq [0,\infty) \), the function is non-negative on the support, so th
e integral (as an extended non-negative real) equals
\[
\int_{\mathbb{R}} \lfloor x \rfloor \, d\mu = \sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \lfloor n
\rfloor.
\]

**Step 3: Evaluate.** For every \( n \in \mathbb{N} \), since \( n \) is an inte
ger, \( \lfloor n \rfloor = n \). Hence
\[
\int_{\mathbb{R}} \lfloor x \rfloor \, d\mu = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n = 1 + 2 + 3
+ \cdots.
\]

**Step 4: Divergence.** The partial sums satisfy
\[
S_N = \sum_{n=1}^{N} n = \frac{N(N+1)}{2} \xrightarrow[N \to \infty]{} +\infty.
\]
Therefore the series diverges to \( +\infty \).

**Conclusion.** Since the integrand is non-negative on the support of \( \mu \),
 the integral is well-defined as an element of \( [0, +\infty] \), and we have s
hown it equals a divergent series of positive terms. Thus
\[
\boxed{\int_{\mathbb{R}} \lfloor x \rfloor \, d\mu = +\infty.}
\]
