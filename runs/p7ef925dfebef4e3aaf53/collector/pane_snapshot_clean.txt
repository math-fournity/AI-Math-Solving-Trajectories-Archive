## Proof

The answer is **yes**. We show that in any locally compact Hausdorff (LCH) space
 $X$, for every closed $F\subseteq X$ and every $y\in X\setminus F$, there is a
continuous $f:X\to[0,1]$ with $f(y)=1$, $f|_F\equiv 0$, and $f$ vanishing at inf
inity (i.e. for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is compact $K\subseteq X$ with $|f|<
\varepsilon$ on $X\setminus K$).

---

**Step 1. A precompact open neighborhood of $y$ avoiding $F$.**

Since $X$ is LCH, $y$ has a compact neighborhood $C$ (so $y\in \operatorname{int
}C$). The set $O:=X\setminus F$ is an open neighborhood of $y$. We claim there i
s an open $V\subseteq X$ with
$$y\in V,\qquad \overline{V}\subseteq O,\qquad \overline{V}\text{ compact}.$$

Indeed, $C$ is a compact Hausdorff subspace, hence regular. The set $O\cap\opera
torname{int}C$ is an open neighborhood of $y$ in $C$. By regularity of $C$, ther
e exists $W$ open in $C$ with
$$y\in W\subseteq \overline{W}^{\,C}\subseteq O\cap\operatorname{int}C.$$
Write $W=C\cap U$ for some $U$ open in $X$, and set $V:=U\cap\operatorname{int}C
$. Then $y\in V$, $V$ is open in $X$, and
$$\overline{V}\subseteq \overline{U}\cap C=\overline{U\cap C}^{\,C}=\overline{W}
^{\,C}\subseteq O.$$
Here $\overline{V}\subseteq C$ because $C$ is compact (hence closed in the Hausd
orff space $X$); thus $\overline{V}$ is a closed subset of the compact set $C$,
so $\overline{V}$ is compact. In particular $\overline{V}\cap F=\varnothing$.

---

**Step 2. Urysohn on the compact set $\overline{V}$.**

The set $\overline{V}$ is compact Hausdorff. Inside it, $\{y\}$ and $\overline{V
}\setminus V$ are disjoint closed subsets (since $y\in V$). By Urysohn's lemma,
there is a continuous function
$$h:\overline{V}\to[0,1]\quad\text{with}\quad h(y)=1,\quad h|_{\overline{V}\setm
inus V}\equiv 0.$$

---

**Step 3. Extension by zero.**

Define $f:X\to[0,1]$ by
$$f(x)=\begin{cases} h(x), & x\in\overline{V},\\ 0, & x\in X\setminus V. \end{ca
ses}$$
This is well defined because on $\overline{V}\setminus V$ both definitions give
$0$.

*Continuity.* The set $V$ is open and $f|_V=h$ is continuous there. The set $X\s
etminus\overline{V}$ is open (since $\overline{V}$ is compact, hence closed in t
he Hausdorff space $X$) and $f\equiv 0$ there. The remaining part is $\partial V
=\overline{V}\setminus V$, where $f\equiv 0$; approaching $\partial V$ from insi
de $V$, $h\to 0$ by continuity of $h$ and the fact that $h=0$ on $\overline{V}\s
etminus V$, and from outside $V$, $f\equiv 0$. Hence $f$ is continuous on all of
 $X$.

---

**Step 4. Verification of the required properties.**

- **Range.** $f(X)\subseteq[0,1]$ by construction.
- **Value at $y$.** $f(y)=h(y)=1$.
- **Vanishing on $F$.** Since $\overline{V}\subseteq X\setminus F$, we have $F\s
ubseteq X\setminus\overline{V}\subseteq X\setminus V$, where $f\equiv 0$. Thus $
f|_F\equiv 0$.
- **Vanishing at infinity.** The support of $f$ is contained in $\overline{V}$,
which is compact. For any $\varepsilon>0$, take $K:=\overline{V}$. Then for ever
y $x\in X\setminus K\subseteq X\setminus V$ we have $f(x)=0<\varepsilon$. (Equiv
alently, $\{x:|f(x)|\ge\varepsilon\}$ is a closed subset of the compact set $\ov
erline{V}$, hence compact.)

---

Therefore such a function $f$ always exists: the locally compact Hausdorff prope
rty guarantees separation of a point from a closed set by a continuous $[0,1]$-v
alued function that is **compactly supported** (hence vanishes at infinity). $\b
lacksquare$
