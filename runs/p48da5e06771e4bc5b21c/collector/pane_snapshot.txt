## Proof

**Answer: Yes, it is possible.**

We exhibit an explicit example. Let $E = F = \ell^1$ and let $T: \ell^1 \to \ell
^1$ be the identity operator $T = I$.

**Claim.** $T = I: \ell^1 \to \ell^1$ is a bounded linear operator that is not c
ompact, yet it maps every weakly convergent sequence in $\ell^1$ to a strongly (
norm) convergent sequence.

**1. $T$ is bounded and linear.** The identity is trivially linear, and $\|Ix\|_
1 = \|x\|_1$, so $\|T\| = 1 < \infty$.

**2. $T$ is not compact.** A compact operator maps the closed unit ball $B_{\ell
^1}$ to a relatively compact set. But $\ell^1$ is infinite-dimensional, so by Ri
esz's lemma the closed unit ball $B_{\ell^1}$ is not compact (equivalently, not
relatively compact in itself). Since $T(B_{\ell^1}) = B_{\ell^1}$ is not relativ
ely compact, $T$ is not a compact operator.

**3. $T$ maps weakly convergent sequences to strongly convergent sequences.** Th
is is the content of **Schur's theorem**: in $\ell^1$, every weakly convergent s
equence is norm convergent. Precisely, if $(x_n) \subset \ell^1$ satisfies $x_n
\rightharpoonup x$ weakly in $\ell^1$, then $\|x_n - x\|_1 \to 0$.

*Sketch of Schur's theorem (for completeness).* Suppose $x_n \rightharpoonup 0$
weakly in $\ell^1$ but $\|x_n\|_1 \not\to 0$. Passing to a subsequence (still de
noted $x_n$) with $\|x_n\|_1 \geq \varepsilon > 0$, one constructs inductively a
 further subsequence $(x_{n_k})$ and increasing coordinate index sets such that
the "mass" of each $x_{n_k}$ is concentrated on a block of coordinates disjoint
from the others. The functional $f \in \ell^\infty = (\ell^1)^*$ defined by $f(e
_j) = \operatorname{sgn}((x_{n_k})_j)$ on the $k$-th block (and $0$ elsewhere) t
hen satisfies $\langle f, x_{n_k}\rangle \geq \varepsilon/2$, contradicting weak
 convergence to $0$. Hence $\|x_n\|_1 \to 0$. $\square$

Applying Schur's theorem: if $x_n \rightharpoonup x$ weakly in $\ell^1$, then $x
_n \to x$ in norm, and therefore
$$\|Tx_n - Tx\|_1 = \|Ix_n - Ix\|_1 = \|x_n - x\|_1 \to 0.$$
Thus $T$ maps weakly convergent sequences to strongly convergent sequences.

**Conclusion.** The identity operator $I: \ell^1 \to \ell^1$ is bounded, linear,
 non-compact (since $\ell^1$ is infinite-dimensional), and completely continuous
 (maps weakly convergent sequences to norm convergent sequences) by Schur's theo
rem. This shows that such an operator can indeed be constructed.

The essential mechanism is that $\ell^1$ possesses the **Schur property** (weak
sequential convergence coincides with norm sequential convergence), which decoup
les complete continuity from compactness: on a space with the Schur property, *e
very* bounded operator is completely continuous, so any non-compact bounded oper
ator — in particular the identity on an infinite-dimensional space with the Schu
r property — furnishes the desired example.
