Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the isomorphism $R[I/a] \co
ng \mathbb{Z}[X][X/x_n] \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[X]} R$ holds when $a$ is a non-zerod
ivisor in $R$.

## Setup and Key Observations

The map $\mathbb{Z}[X] = \mathbb{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_n] \to R$ sends $x_i \mapsto f_i
$ ($i \leq n-1$) and $x_n \mapsto a$, giving $R$ the structure of a $\mathbb{Z}[
X]$-algebra.

**Computing the tensor product.** Since $\mathbb{Z}[X][X/x_n] = \mathbb{Z}[x_1,\
dots,x_n][x_1/x_n, \dots, x_{n-1}/x_n]$, we get:
$$S := \mathbb{Z}[X][X/x_n] \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[X]} R \cong R[y_1,\dots,y_{n-1}]
/(a\,y_i - f_i : i=1,\dots,n-1),$$
where $y_i$ corresponds to $x_i/x_n$, subject to the relation $a\,y_i = f_i$.

**Computing $R[I/a]$.** The Rees algebra $\operatorname{Bl}_I(R) = \bigoplus_{n\
geq 0} I^n$ embeds into $R[t]$ via $b \in I^n \mapsto bt^n$. Localizing at $a^{(
1)} = at$ and taking degree $0$ yields:
$$R[I/a] = R[f_1/a, \dots, f_{n-1}/a] \subseteq R_a.$$

**The natural map.** There is a natural surjection $\varphi: S \twoheadrightarro
w R[I/a]$ sending $y_i \mapsto f_i/a$. Localizing $S$ at $a$ gives $S_a \cong R_
a$ (since $y_i = f_i/a$ after inverting $a$), and $\varphi$ coincides with the l
ocalization map $S \to S_a \cong R_a$ (with image restricted to $R[I/a]$). There
fore:
$$\ker(\varphi) = \{s \in S : a^N s = 0 \text{ for some } N \geq 0\},$$
the $a$-torsion of $S$. So $\varphi$ is an isomorphism **if and only if** $a$ is
 a non-zerodivisor on $S$.

## The Key Question: Is $a$ a Non-zerodivisor on $S$?

The hypothesis only says $a$ is a non-zerodivisor on $R$. This does **not** impl
y $a$ is a non-zerodivisor on $S$.

**Snake lemma analysis (one-variable case).** For $S = A[y]/(ay - f)$ with $a$ a
 non-zerodivisor on $A$: the element $ay - f$ is a non-zerodivisor on $A[y]$ (by
 degree comparison: if $g \cdot (ay-f) = 0$, the leading coefficient gives $a \c
dot (\text{lead coeff of } g) = 0$, hence $0$). So we have the exact sequence $0
 \to A[y] \xrightarrow{\cdot(ay-f)} A[y] \to S \to 0$. Applying the snake lemma
to multiplication by $a$:

$$\ker(a \cdot \text{ on } S) \cong \ker\!\big(\overline{f} \cdot \text{ on } (A
/(a))[y]\big),$$

where $\overline{f}$ is the image of $f$ in $A/(a)$. This kernel is **nonzero**
whenever $\overline{f}$ is a zerodivisor in $A/(a)$ — which can happen even when
 $a$ is a non-zerodivisor on $A$.

## Counterexample

Let $R = k[x, y, z]/(yz)$ for a field $k$, with $I = (y, x)$ and $a = x$.

**$a = x$ is a non-zerodivisor in $R$:** If $xg \in (yz)$ in $k[x,y,z]$, then si
nce $\gcd(x, yz) = 1$ in the UFD $k[x,y,z]$, we get $g \in (yz)$, so $g = 0$ in
$R$. ✓

Here $n = 2$, $f_1 = y$, and the map $\mathbb{Z}[x_1, x_2] \to R$ sends $x_1 \ma
psto y$, $x_2 \mapsto x$.

**The tensor product:**
$$S = R[t]/(xt - y) = k[x,y,z,t]/(yz,\, xt - y) = k[x, z, t]/(xzt).$$
(Substituting $y = xt$ into $yz = 0$ gives $xzt = 0$.)

**The affine blowup algebra:** $R[I/a] = R[y/x] \subseteq R_x = k[x, x^{-1}, y,
z]/(yz)$. Setting $u = y/x$, in $R_x$ we have $uz = (y/x)z = yz/x = 0$. So:
$$R[I/a] = k[x, y, z, u]/(yz,\, xu - y,\, uz) = k[x, z, u]/(uz).$$
(Again $y = xu$, and $(xuz, uz) = (uz)$ since $xuz \in (uz)$.)

**The map $\varphi: S \to R[I/a]$, $t \mapsto u$:** We have $\varphi(zt) = zu =
0$ in $R[I/a] = k[x,z,u]/(uz)$. However, $zt \neq 0$ in $S = k[x,z,t]/(xzt)$, be
cause $zt \notin (xzt)$ (the quotient $zt/(xzt) = 1/x$ is not a polynomial).

Therefore $\ker(\varphi) \neq 0$, and $\varphi$ is **not an isomorphism**.

## Conclusion

The isomorphism $R[I/a] \cong \mathbb{Z}[X][X/x_n] \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[X]} R$ **
fails in general** even when $a$ is a non-zerodivisor in $R$. The natural map fr
om the tensor product to $R[I/a]$ has kernel equal to the $a$-torsion of $S = \m
athbb{Z}[X][X/x_n] \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[X]} R$, and $a$ being a non-zerodivisor o
n $R$ does not guarantee that $a$ is a non-zerodivisor on $S$. The counterexampl
e $R = k[x,y,z]/(yz)$, $I = (y,x)$, $a = x$ demonstrates this concretely: $x$ is
 a non-zerodivisor on $R$, yet the element $zt$ is nonzero $x$-torsion in $S$, s
