## Proof

**Goal.** Determine the holonomy group of $\mathbb{O}P^1$ (the octonionic projec
tive line), equipped with its natural Riemannian metric.

---

### Step 1. $\mathbb{O}P^1$ is diffeomorphic to $S^8$.

The octonionic projective line is the set of right-equivalence classes of nonzer
o pairs in $\mathbb{O}^2$:
$$\mathbb{O}P^1 = \big(\mathbb{O}^2 \setminus \{0\}\big)\big/\,(a,b)\sim (a\lamb
da,\,b\lambda),\quad \lambda\in\mathbb{O}\setminus\{0\}.$$
(Octonionic multiplication is non-associative, but the projective line over $\ma
thbb{O}$ is still well-defined because the relevant Moufang identities suffice f
or the transition-function calculus; in particular $\mathbb{O}P^n$ exists precis
ely for $n\le 2$.)

Cover $\mathbb{O}P^1$ by the two standard charts
$$U_0 = \{[1:\,x] : x\in\mathbb{O}\},\qquad U_1 = \{[y:\,1] : y\in\mathbb{O}\},$
$
with overlap $U_0\cap U_1 \cong \mathbb{O}\setminus\{0\}$ and transition map $x\
mapsto x^{-1}$. Identifying $\mathbb{O}\cong\mathbb{R}^8$, this is exactly the t
wo-chart description of $S^8$ via stereographic projection (the transition $x\ma
psto x^{-1}/|x|^2$ on $\mathbb{R}^8\setminus\{0\}$ is the inversion that realize
s the antipodal stereographic gluing). Hence
$$\mathbb{O}P^1 \;\cong\; S^8.$$
This is the octonionic analogue of $\mathbb{C}P^1\cong S^2$ and $\mathbb{H}P^1\c
ong S^4$.

### Step 2. The natural metric on $\mathbb{O}P^1$ is the round metric on $S^8$.

$\mathbb{O}P^1$ carries its canonical Riemannian metric, induced by the octonion
ic Hopf fibration
$$S^{15}\longrightarrow S^{8},$$
which is a Riemannian submersion from the round $S^{15}\subset\mathbb{O}^2=\math
bb{R}^{16}$ onto $S^8$ with the round metric (up to an overall constant scale).
Scaling a metric does not change its holonomy group. Thus the holonomy question
for $\mathbb{O}P^1$ reduces to the holonomy of the round $S^8$.

### Step 3. Holonomy of the round sphere $S^8$.

The round sphere $S^n$ is the irreducible simply connected symmetric space
$$S^n \;=\; SO(n+1)\,/\,SO(n).$$

For a simply connected, irreducible, non-flat Riemannian symmetric space $G/K$,
the (full) holonomy group at the base point equals the isotropy group $K$. This
is because the curvature tensor is parallel, the holonomy algebra is generated b
y the curvature operators, and for a symmetric space these span exactly the Lie
algebra $\mathfrak{k}$ of the isotropy group; connectedness of $S^n$ ($n\ge 2$)
then gives the full holonomy group $K$.

Applying this with $n=8$:
$$\operatorname{Hol}(S^8) \;=\; SO(8).$$

(Equivalently and more concretely: $S^8$ is orientable, so its holonomy is conta
ined in $SO(8)$; it is not flat, not Kähler (so not $U(4)$), not quaternionic-Kä
hler, and not one of Berger's special cases — the round sphere is the prototypic
al example whose holonomy is the full $SO(n)$.)

### Step 4. Conclusion.

Combining Steps 1–3:
$$\boxed{\operatorname{Hol}\big(\mathbb{O}P^1\big)\;=\;SO(8).}$$

The holonomy group of the 1-dimensional octonionic projective space is $\,SO(8)$
.
