## Proof

**Setup.** Let $T = V/\Lambda$ be a complex torus of dimension $g = 3$, where $V
 \cong \mathbb{C}^3$ and $\Lambda \subset V$ is a lattice of rank $6$. A line bu
ndle $\mathcal{L}$ on $T$ gives a polarization, which by definition means $\math
cal{L}$ is ample. The polarization type $(p, q, r)$ records the elementary divis
ors of the alternating form $E := \operatorname{Im}(H)|_\Lambda$, where $H$ is t
he Riemann form (Hermitian form) associated to $\mathcal{L}$ via the Appell–Humb
ert theorem. By convention $p \mid q \mid r$.

Since $\mathcal{L}$ is ample, $T$ is in fact an abelian variety, and the full ma
chinery of abelian varieties applies.

**Step 1: Riemann–Roch gives the Euler characteristic.**

The Riemann–Roch theorem for an ample line bundle on an abelian variety of dimen
sion $g$ states:

$$\chi(T, \mathcal{L}) = \frac{(\mathcal{L})^g}{g!} = d_1 d_2 \cdots d_g,$$

where $(\mathcal{L})^g$ is the top self-intersection number and $(d_1, \ldots, d
_g)$ is the polarization type. The equality $\frac{(\mathcal{L})^g}{g!} = d_1 \c
dots d_g$ holds because the self-intersection equals the Pfaffian of the element
ary-divisor form, which is the product of the elementary divisors.

For $g = 3$ and type $(p, q, r)$:

$$\chi(T, \mathcal{L}) = p\, q\, r.$$

**Step 2: Vanishing of higher cohomology.**

By Mumford's vanishing theorem (the abelian-variety analogue of Kodaira vanishin
g), if $\mathcal{L}$ is ample on an abelian variety, then

$$H^i(T, \mathcal{L}) = 0 \quad \text{for all } i \geq 1.$$

(Concretely: $\mathcal{L}$ is ample, so for the index $i(\mathcal{L})$ — the num
ber of negative eigenvalues of the associated Hermitian form — we have $i(\mathc
al{L}) = 0$, and Mumford's theorem gives $H^i(T, \mathcal{L}) = 0$ for $i \neq i
(\mathcal{L}) = 0$.)

**Step 3: Conclude.**

Since all higher cohomology vanishes:

$$\dim H^0(T, \mathcal{L}) = \chi(T, \mathcal{L}) = p\, q\, r.$$

$$\boxed{\dim H^0(T, \mathcal{L}) = p\, q\, r.}$$
