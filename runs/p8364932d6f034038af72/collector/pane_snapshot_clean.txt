## Solution

We seek a non-constant polynomial $f$ (with rational coefficients) satisfying
$$f(f(x)) = 3f(x)^4 - 1.$$

**Step 1: Determine the degree.**

Let $\deg f = d \geq 1$. Then $\deg(f(f(x))) = d^2$ and $\deg(3f(x)^4 - 1) = 4d$
. Equating:
$$d^2 = 4d \implies d = 4.$$

**Step 2: Extract $f$ from the functional equation.**

Set $y = f(x)$. The equation becomes
$$f(y) = 3y^4 - 1.$$

Since $f$ is a non-constant polynomial, as $x$ ranges over $\mathbb{C}$ (or $\ma
thbb{R}$), the value $y = f(x)$ takes **infinitely many** distinct values. The p
olynomial identity $f(y) = 3y^4 - 1$ holds for every such $y$. Two polynomials a
greeing on an infinite set are identical, so
$$f(y) = 3y^4 - 1 \quad \text{for all } y.$$

**Step 3: Verify.**

With $f(x) = 3x^4 - 1$:
$$f(f(x)) = 3(3x^4 - 1)^4 - 1, \qquad 3f(x)^4 - 1 = 3(3x^4 - 1)^4 - 1.$$

These are identical. ✓

The coefficients are rational, $f$ is non-constant, and the degree check is sati
sfied.

$$\boxed{f(x) = 3x^4 - 1}$$
