## Proof

We compute $\displaystyle I = \int_{|z|=1} \operatorname{Re}(z)\, e^{z^2}\, dz$,
 with the unit circle traversed once counterclockwise.

**Step 1. Express $\operatorname{Re}(z)$ on $|z|=1$.**

For $|z|=1$ we have $z\bar z = 1$, hence $\bar z = 1/z$. Therefore
$$
\operatorname{Re}(z) = \frac{z+\bar z}{2} = \frac{z + z^{-1}}{2}.
$$

**Step 2. Identify the holomorphic part (the hint).**

Decompose
$$
\operatorname{Re}(z)\, e^{z^2} = \frac{z\, e^{z^2}}{2} + \frac{e^{z^2}}{2z}\quad
\text{on } |z|=1.
$$

The first term $\frac{z\,e^{z^2}}{2}$ is entire, so it is the holomorphic functi
on $f(z)\,e^{z^2}$ with $f(z)=\tfrac{z}{2}$, which satisfies $f(z)=\operatorname
{Re}(z)$ on $|z|=1$. The second term $\frac{e^{z^2}}{2z}$ is the non‑holomorphic
 remainder (it has a pole at the origin).

**Step 3. Integrate each piece.**

- The function $g(z) = \tfrac{z}{2}\,e^{z^2}$ is entire, so by Cauchy's theorem
$$
\int_{|z|=1} g(z)\, dz = 0.
$$

- The function $h(z) = \dfrac{e^{z^2}}{2z}$ has an isolated singularity at $z=0$
. Since $e^{z^2}$ is entire with $e^{0}=1$, the Laurent expansion near $0$ is
$$
h(z) = \frac{1}{2z}\left(1 + z^2 + \tfrac{z^4}{2!} + \cdots\right) = \frac{1}{2z
} + \frac{z}{2} + \frac{z^3}{4} + \cdots,
$$
so the residue at $z=0$ is
$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} h(z) = \frac{1}{2}.
$$

By the residue theorem,
$$
\int_{|z|=1} h(z)\, dz = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}_{z=0} h(z) = 2\pi i \cd
ot \frac{1}{2} = \pi i.
$$

**Step 4. Combine.**

$$
I = \int_{|z|=1} \operatorname{Re}(z)\, e^{z^2}\, dz = \int_{|z|=1} g(z)\, dz +
\int_{|z|=1} h(z)\, dz = 0 + \pi i = \pi i.
$$

$$
\boxed{\displaystyle \int_{|z|=1} \operatorname{Re}(z)\, e^{z^2}\, dz = \pi i.}
$$
