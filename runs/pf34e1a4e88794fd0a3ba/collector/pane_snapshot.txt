Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a $C^k$ diffeomorphism $f:
X \to Y$ between Banach spaces must satisfy $\inf_{x \in X} |\det[d_x f]| > 0$.

**Answer: No.** Here is a counterexample.

---

**Counterexample.** Take $X = Y = \mathbb{R}$ (a 1-dimensional Banach space) and
 define
$$f(x) = \operatorname{arcsinh}(x) = \ln\!\bigl(x + \sqrt{1 + x^2}\bigr).$$

**$f$ is $C^\infty$.** The function $x \mapsto x + \sqrt{1+x^2}$ is smooth and s
trictly positive for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ (since $\sqrt{1+x^2} > |x| \geq -x$)
, so the composition with $\ln$ is $C^\infty$.

**$f$ is a diffeomorphism.** We have
$$f'(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + x^2}} > 0 \quad \text{for all } x \in \mathbb{R},$$
so $f$ is strictly increasing. Moreover, as $x \to +\infty$,
$$f(x) = \ln\!\bigl(x + x\sqrt{1 + 1/x^2}\bigr) \sim \ln(2x) \to +\infty,$$
and as $x \to -\infty$,
$$x + \sqrt{1+x^2} = x + |x|\sqrt{1 + 1/x^2} \approx x - x\Bigl(1 + \frac{1}{2x^
2}\Bigr) = -\frac{1}{2x} \to 0^+,$$
so $f(x) \to -\infty$. Thus $f$ is surjective, and being strictly increasing it
is bijective. Its inverse is
$$f^{-1}(y) = \sinh(y) = \frac{e^y - e^{-y}}{2},$$
which is $C^\infty$. Hence $f$ is a $C^\infty$ diffeomorphism of $\mathbb{R}$ on
to $\mathbb{R}$.

**The infimum of $|\det[d_x f]|$ is zero.** In dimension one, $\det[d_x f] = f'(
x)$, so
$$\inf_{x \in \mathbb{R}} \bigl|\det[d_x f]\bigr| = \inf_{x \in \mathbb{R}} \fra
c{1}{\sqrt{1 + x^2}} = \lim_{|x| \to \infty} \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + x^2}} = 0.$$

**Conclusion.** The $C^\infty$ diffeomorphism $f = \operatorname{arcsinh}: \math
bb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfies $\inf_{x \in \mathbb{R}} |\det[d_x f]| = 0$, so
the statement is **false** in general.

**Remark.** The failure is tied to the inverse having unbounded derivative: $(f^
{-1})'(y) = \cosh(y)$, which grows without bound. A $C^k$ diffeomorphism does no
t require the derivative of the inverse to be globally bounded, and this is exac
tly what allows the Jacobian determinant to approach zero.
