## Proof

Let $b_i=\ln a_i$ (so $a_i=e^{b_i}>0$). The four constraints
$$a_1\ge a_2a_3^2,\quad a_2\ge a_3a_4^2,\quad a_3\ge a_4a_1^2,\quad a_4\ge a_1a_
2^2$$
are equivalent (with indices taken cyclically, the pattern being $a_i\ge a_{i+1}
a_{i+2}^2$) to
$$b_i\ge b_{i+1}+2b_{i+2},\qquad i=1,2,3,4.$$

Introduce nonnegative slack variables
$$e_i:=b_i-b_{i+1}-2b_{i+2}\ge 0,$$
so that
$$a_i=a_{i+1}a_{i+2}^2\,e^{e_i}.\tag{1}$$

---

**Step 1. Express the target in terms of the slacks $e_i$.**

Define $d_i:=a_i-a_{i+1}a_{i+2}^2\ge 0$. From (1),
$$d_i=a_{i+1}a_{i+2}^2\bigl(e^{e_i}-1\bigr).$$

Let $P:=a_1a_2a_3a_4$. Then
$$\prod_{i=1}^4 d_i=\prod_{i=1}^4\!\bigl[a_{i+1}a_{i+2}^2\bigr]\cdot\prod_{i=1}^
4\!\bigl(e^{e_i}-1\bigr).$$
Because the indices are cyclic, $\prod_i a_{i+1}=P$ and $\prod_i a_{i+2}^2=P^2$,
 hence
$$\prod_{i=1}^4 d_i=P^3\prod_{i=1}^4\!\bigl(e^{e_i}-1\bigr).$$
Therefore the expression to be maximized is
$$E:=P\prod_{i=1}^4 d_i=P^4\prod_{i=1}^4\!\bigl(e^{e_i}-1\bigr).\tag{2}$$

---

**Step 2. Express $P$ in terms of the slacks.**

Summing the identities $b_i=b_{i+1}+2b_{i+2}+e_i$ over $i=1,\dots,4$ and using t
hat cyclic shifts preserve $\sum b_i$:
$$\sum_i b_i=\sum_i b_{i+1}+2\sum_i b_{i+2}+\sum_i e_i=3\sum_i b_i+\sum_i e_i,$$
so
$$\sum_i b_i=-\tfrac12\sum_i e_i,\qquad P=e^{\sum_i b_i}=e^{-\frac12\sum_i e_i},
\qquad P^4=e^{-2\sum_i e_i}.$$

Substituting into (2):
$$E=e^{-2\sum_i e_i}\prod_{i=1}^4\!\bigl(e^{e_i}-1\bigr)=\prod_{i=1}^4\Bigl[e^{-
2e_i}\bigl(e^{e_i}-1\bigr)\Bigr]=\prod_{i=1}^4\bigl(e^{-e_i}-e^{-2e_i}\bigr).\ta
g{3}$$

---

**Step 3. Maximize each factor.**

For $t\ge 0$ set $u:=e^{-t}\in(0,1]$. The single factor in (3) is
$$f(t)=e^{-t}-e^{-2t}=u-u^2=u(1-u).$$
The quadratic $u(1-u)$ on $(0,1]$ attains its maximum $\tfrac14$ at $u=\tfrac12$
, i.e. at $t=\ln 2$. Hence
$$f(t)\le \tfrac14\qquad(t\ge 0),$$
with equality iff $t=\ln 2$.

---

**Step 4. Conclude.**

From (3) and Step 3,
$$E=\prod_{i=1}^4 f(e_i)\le\Bigl(\tfrac14\Bigr)^4=\frac1{256}.$$

Equality holds iff $e_1=e_2=e_3=e_4=\ln 2$.

---

**Step 5. Verify attainability.**

We must check that the system $b_i=b_{i+1}+2b_{i+2}+\ln 2$ admits real solutions
. The symmetric ansatz $b_1=b_2=b_3=b_4=b$ gives $b=b+2b+\ln 2$, i.e. $b=-\tfrac
12\ln 2=-\ln\sqrt2$, yielding
$$a_1=a_2=a_3=a_4=\frac1{\sqrt2}.$$
Indeed $a_{i+1}a_{i+2}^2=\frac1{\sqrt2}\cdot\frac12=\frac1{2\sqrt2}$ and $\frac{
a_i}{a_{i+1}a_{i+2}^2}=2=e^{\ln2}$, so all four slacks equal $\ln 2$, all constr
aints hold, and
$$E=\Bigl(\tfrac14\Bigr)^4=\frac1{256}.$$

(More generally, adding any solution of the homogeneous system $b_i=b_{i+1}+2b_{
i+2}$ — namely $b_1=b_3=s,\ b_2=b_4=-s$ — gives the whole optimizer family $a_1=
a_3=e^s/\sqrt2,\ a_2=a_4=e^{-s}/\sqrt2$ with $a_1a_2=\tfrac12$.)

---

The maximum value of the expression is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{256}}.$$
