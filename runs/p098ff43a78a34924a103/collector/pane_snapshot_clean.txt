## Proof

The answer is **yes**: $H^{2n}(X, \mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Q}^r$.

We prove by induction on $r$ the following (slightly stronger) statement:

> **(★)** For any proper algebraic variety $X/\mathbb{C}$ of pure dimension $n$
with $r$ irreducible components (not necessarily connected), $H^{2n}(X, \mathbb{
Q}) \cong \mathbb{Q}^r$.

---

### Base case ($r = 1$): $X$ irreducible

Let $X$ be an irreducible proper variety of dimension $n$ over $\mathbb{C}$. The
 associated analytic space $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ is compact. The singular locus $S \
subset X$ is a proper closed subset of the irreducible variety $X$, so $\dim_{\m
athbb{C}} S < n$, hence $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} S \leq 2n - 2$.

The smooth locus $U = X \setminus S$ is a connected complex manifold of dimensio
n $n$ (connectedness of the smooth locus of an irreducible variety is standard).
 Thus $U$ is a connected orientable real manifold of dimension $2n$, dense in $X
$.

Since $X$ is a compact, connected, orientable pseudomanifold of real dimension $
2n$ (the unique top-dimensional stratum $U$ is connected and orientable), $X$ po
ssesses a fundamental class $[X] \in H_{2n}(X, \mathbb{Z})$ generating $H_{2n}(X
, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.

By the Universal Coefficient Theorem with $\mathbb{Q}$-coefficients:
$$H^{2n}(X, \mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(H_{2n}(X), \mathbb{Q}) \oplus \mathrm
{Ext}(H_{2n-1}(X), \mathbb{Q}).$$
Since $\mathbb{Q}$ is divisible (hence an injective $\mathbb{Z}$-module), $\math
rm{Ext}(-, \mathbb{Q}) = 0$. Therefore:
$$H^{2n}(X, \mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb
{Q}. \quad \checkmark$$

---

### Inductive step: $r \geq 2$

Write $X = X_1 \cup Y$ where $X_1$ is one irreducible component and $Y = X_2 \cu
p \cdots \cup X_r$ is the union of the remaining $r-1$ components. Both $X_1$ an
d $Y$ are closed subvarieties of $X$.

**Claim:** $Z := X_1 \cap Y$ has $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} Z \leq n - 1$.

*Proof of claim:* $Z = \bigcup_{j=2}^{r} (X_1 \cap X_j)$. For each $j \geq 2$, s
ince $X_1$ and $X_j$ are distinct irreducible components of the same dimension $
n$, $X_1 \cap X_j$ is a proper closed subset of the irreducible variety $X_1$, s
o $\dim(X_1 \cap X_j) < n$. Hence $\dim Z \leq n - 1$. $\square$

Since $Z$ has complex dimension $\leq n-1$, it has topological (covering) dimens
ion $\leq 2n - 2$. Therefore:
$$H^{2n-1}(Z, \mathbb{Q}) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad H^{2n}(Z, \mathbb{Q}) = 0,$
$
as singular cohomology vanishes above the topological dimension.

**Applying Mayer–Vietoris.** Since $X$ is a complex algebraic variety, it is tri
angulable (by Łojasiewicz's theorem), and we may choose a triangulation in which
 all irreducible components $X_1, \ldots, X_r$ are subcomplexes. Then $X_1$ and
$Y$ are subcomplexes with $X = X_1 \cup Y$, forming an excisive couple. Equivale
ntly, taking open neighborhoods (e.g., open stars in a barycentric subdivision)
that deformation-retract onto $X_1$, $Y$, and $Z = X_1 \cap Y$ respectively, the
 Mayer–Vietoris sequence for singular cohomology gives:

$$\cdots \to H^{2n-1}(Z, \mathbb{Q}) \to H^{2n}(X, \mathbb{Q}) \to H^{2n}(X_1, \
mathbb{Q}) \oplus H^{2n}(Y, \mathbb{Q}) \to H^{2n}(Z, \mathbb{Q}) \to \cdots$$

Substituting the vanishing results for $Z$:

$$0 \to H^{2n}(X, \mathbb{Q}) \to H^{2n}(X_1, \mathbb{Q}) \oplus H^{2n}(Y, \math
bb{Q}) \to 0$$

This short exact sequence yields an isomorphism:
$$H^{2n}(X, \mathbb{Q}) \cong H^{2n}(X_1, \mathbb{Q}) \oplus H^{2n}(Y, \mathbb{Q
}).$$

Now $X_1$ is irreducible (base case): $H^{2n}(X_1, \mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Q}$
. The variety $Y$ is proper (closed in $X$), of pure dimension $n$, with $r - 1$
 irreducible components. By the induction hypothesis (★):
$$H^{2n}(Y, \mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Q}^{r-1}.$$

Therefore:
$$H^{2n}(X, \mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Q} \oplus \mathbb{Q}^{r-1} = \mathbb{Q}^r.
 \quad \square$$

---

### Conclusion

By induction, for every proper algebraic variety $X/\mathbb{C}$ of pure dimensio
n $n$ with $r$ irreducible components, $H^{2n}(X, \mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Q}^r
$. In particular, this holds under the given hypothesis that $X$ is connected.

The key ingredients are:
1. Each irreducible component carries a fundamental class giving $H^{2n}(X_i, \m
athbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Q}$ (via the pseudomanifold structure and UCT).
2. The intersection of any component with the union of the others has complex di
mension $\leq n-1$, hence real dimension $\leq 2n-2$, forcing the relevant cohom
ology of the intersection to vanish in degrees $2n-1$ and $2n$.
3. Mayer–Vietoris then splits $H^{2n}$ as a direct sum over components.
