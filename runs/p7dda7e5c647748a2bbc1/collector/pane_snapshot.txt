## Proof

We evaluate $I = \int_0^{2\pi} e^{i\theta}\, e^{-i e^{in\theta}}\, d\theta$ for
integer $n$ via contour integration.

**Contour substitution.** Set $z = e^{i\theta}$, so $dz = i e^{i\theta}\,d\theta
 = iz\,d\theta$, giving $d\theta = \frac{dz}{iz}$. As $\theta$ ranges over $[0,2
\pi]$, $z$ traverses the unit circle $|z|=1$ counterclockwise. Under this substi
tution:
- $e^{i\theta} = z$,
- $e^{in\theta} = z^n$.

Therefore
$$I = \oint_{|z|=1} z\, e^{-iz^n}\, \frac{dz}{iz} = \frac{1}{i}\oint_{|z|=1} e^{
-iz^n}\, dz.$$

**Analyzing the integrand.** The function $f(z) = e^{-iz^n}$ is entire: it is th
e composition of the entire exponential with the polynomial $-iz^n$, hence has n
o singularities anywhere in $\mathbb{C}$.

By **Cauchy's theorem**, the integral of an entire function over any closed cont
our is zero:
$$\oint_{|z|=1} e^{-iz^n}\, dz = 0.$$

**Conclusion.**
$$I = \frac{1}{i}\cdot 0 = 0.$$

**Verification via series expansion (independent check).** Expanding $e^{-i e^{i
n\theta}} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-i)^k}{k!} e^{ink\theta}$ and integrating
 term-by-term:
$$I = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-i)^k}{k!}\int_0^{2\pi} e^{i(nk+1)\theta}\,d\th
eta.$$
Since $\int_0^{2\pi} e^{im\theta}\,d\theta = 0$ for every nonzero integer $m$ an
d $=2\pi$ for $m=0$, a nonzero contribution requires $nk+1=0$, i.e., $k = -1/n$.
 For positive integer $n$, this gives $k<0$, which lies outside the summation ra
nge $k\geq 0$. Hence every term vanishes and $I=0$, confirming the contour resul
t.

$$\boxed{\int_0^{2\pi} e^{i\theta}\, e^{-i e^{in\theta}}\, d\theta = 0}$$
