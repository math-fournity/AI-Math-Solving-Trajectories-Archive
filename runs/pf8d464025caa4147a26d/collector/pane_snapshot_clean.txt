Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether 6 pairwise non-parallel lin
es in 3D space can have all pairwise angles equal (a set of 6 **equiangular line
s**).

**Answer: Yes, it is possible.**

## Construction

Let $\phi = \frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$ (the golden ratio). Consider the 6 lines throu
gh the origin with direction vectors:

$$v_1=(0,1,\phi),\quad v_2=(0,1,-\phi),\quad v_3=(1,\phi,0),$$
$$v_4=(1,-\phi,0),\quad v_5=(\phi,0,1),\quad v_6=(\phi,0,-1).$$

These are the 6 lines connecting antipodal pairs of vertices of a regular icosah
edron (whose 12 vertices are $(0,\pm1,\pm\phi)$, $(\pm1,\pm\phi,0)$, $(\pm\phi,0
,\pm1)$).

## Verification

**Equal norms.** Every $v_i$ satisfies $\|v_i\|^2 = 1+\phi^2 = 1+(\phi+1)=\phi+2
$, using $\phi^2=\phi+1$.

**Equal pairwise angles.** I compute all $\binom{6}{2}=15$ dot products. Using $
\phi^2=\phi+1$:

| Pair | Dot product | Absolute value |
|------|------------|----------------|
| $v_1\cdot v_2$ | $0+1-\phi^2 = 1-\phi-1=-\phi$ | $\phi$ |
| $v_1\cdot v_3$ | $0+\phi+0=\phi$ | $\phi$ |
| $v_1\cdot v_4$ | $0-\phi+0=-\phi$ | $\phi$ |
| $v_1\cdot v_5$ | $0+0+\phi=\phi$ | $\phi$ |
| $v_1\cdot v_6$ | $0+0-\phi=-\phi$ | $\phi$ |
| $v_2\cdot v_3$ | $0+\phi+0=\phi$ | $\phi$ |
| $v_2\cdot v_4$ | $0-\phi+0=-\phi$ | $\phi$ |
| $v_2\cdot v_5$ | $0+0-\phi=-\phi$ | $\phi$ |
| $v_2\cdot v_6$ | $0+0+\phi=\phi$ | $\phi$ |
| $v_3\cdot v_4$ | $1-\phi^2+0=1-\phi-1=-\phi$ | $\phi$ |
| $v_3\cdot v_5$ | $\phi+0+0=\phi$ | $\phi$ |
| $v_3\cdot v_6$ | $\phi+0+0=\phi$ | $\phi$ |
| $v_4\cdot v_5$ | $\phi+0+0=\phi$ | $\phi$ |
| $v_4\cdot v_6$ | $\phi+0+0=\phi$ | $\phi$ |
| $v_5\cdot v_6$ | $\phi^2+0-1=\phi+1-1=\phi$ | $\phi$ |

Every pairwise absolute dot product equals $\phi$. Therefore the cosine of the a
ngle between any two lines is:

$$\cos\theta = \frac{|v_i\cdot v_j|}{\|v_i\|\|v_j\|} = \frac{\phi}{\phi+2}.$$

**Computing the common value.** Rationalizing:

$$\frac{\phi}{\phi+2}=\frac{(1+\sqrt{5})/2}{(5+\sqrt{5})/2}=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{5+
\sqrt{5}}\cdot\frac{5-\sqrt{5}}{5-\sqrt{5}}=\frac{4\sqrt{5}}{20}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{
5}}.$$

So all 15 pairwise angles equal $\theta=\arccos(1/\sqrt{5})\approx 63.43°$.

**Non-parallelism.** Since $\cos\theta=1/\sqrt{5}\neq \pm1$, no two lines are pa
rallel (or antiparallel). Each $v_i$ is also not a scalar multiple of any other
$v_j$, which is directly visible from the coordinate patterns.

## Conclusion

The 6 lines through antipodal vertex pairs of a regular icosahedron are pairwise
 non-parallel and share the common angle $\arccos(1/\sqrt{5})$. Therefore, **suc
h an arrangement exists**.
