## Proof

**Setup.** Let $S \subset \mathbb{P}^3$ be a general (smooth, nonsingular) cubic
 surface over an algebraically closed field. A classical theorem (Cayley–Salmon,
 Clebsch) states that $S$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^2$ blown up at six points
 $P_1,\dots,P_6$ in general position, embedded by the anticanonical linear syste
m $|-K_S|$. The 27 lines on $S$ correspond to the classes

- $E_i$ — the six exceptional divisors (6 lines),
- $H - E_i - E_j$ for $i<j$ — strict transforms of lines through pairs of points
 (15 lines),
- $2H - \sum_{j\neq i} E_j$ for each $i$ — strict transforms of conics through f
ive of the six points (6 lines),

giving $6+15+6 = 27$ lines, with $-K_S = 3H - E_1 - \cdots - E_6$.

**Tritangent planes.** A plane $\Pi\subset\mathbb{P}^3$ cuts $S$ in a member of
$|-K_S|$, i.e., a plane cubic. A *tritangent plane* is one for which this cubic
splits completely into three lines. Hence **each tritangent plane contains exact
ly 3 of the 27 lines**, and corresponds to a decomposition
$$-K_S \;=\; L_1 + L_2 + L_3$$
where each $L_i$ is one of the 27 line classes.

**Each line lies on exactly 5 tritangent planes.** Consider $\ell = E_1$. We mus
t find unordered pairs $\{L_2, L_3\}$ of line classes with
$$L_2 + L_3 \;=\; -K_S - E_1 \;=\; 3H - 2E_1 - E_2 - E_3 - E_4 - E_5 - E_6.$$

Checking all type combinations:

- Two $E$-classes or two $H{-}E_i{-}E_j$ classes give at most $2H$: insufficient
.
- Two conic classes give $4H$: too much.
- A line class $H - E_a - E_b$ plus a conic class $2H - \sum_{j\neq c} E_j$ give
s $3H - E_a - E_b - \sum_{j\neq c}E_j$. Setting this equal to $3H - 2E_1 - E_2 -
 \cdots - E_6$ forces $E_a + E_b = E_1 + E_c$, i.e. $\{a,b\}=\{1,c\}$. This yiel
ds exactly the five pairs
$$\bigl(H - E_1 - E_i,\; 2H - \textstyle\sum_{j\neq i}E_j\bigr),\qquad i\in\{2,3
,4,5,6\}.$$

No other combination works, so $E_1$ lies on exactly **5** tritangent planes. Th
e Weyl group $W(E_6)$ acts transitively on the 27 lines (a classical fact), so *
*every** line lies on exactly 5 tritangent planes.

**Double counting.** Count incidences $(\ell, \Pi)$ where $\ell$ is one of the 2
7 lines and $\Pi$ is a tritangent plane containing it. Counting by lines: $27 \t
imes 5$. Counting by planes: $N \times 3$, where $N$ is the number of tritangent
 planes. Therefore
$$27 \times 5 \;=\; 3N \quad\Longrightarrow\quad N \;=\; \frac{135}{3} \;=\; 45.
$$

**Answer.** A general nonsingular cubic surface contains $\boxed{45}$ tritangent
 planes.
