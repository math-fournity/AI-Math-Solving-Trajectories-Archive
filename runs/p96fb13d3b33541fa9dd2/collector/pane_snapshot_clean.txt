## Step 1: Upper bound — showing $p \leq 1$

Take $c = 0$, $a = 2$, $b = 1$, and $r = k$ with $k \to \infty$.

Then $f(x) = (a-b)(a^{x+1} - b^{x+1}) = 2^{x+1} - 1$, and $S_{pk} = 2^{pk} + 1$.
 The inequality becomes:
$$(2^{pk}+1)^{1/p}(2^{k+1}-1) \geq 2^{2k+1}-1.$$

For large $k$, using $(2^{pk}+1)^{1/p} \approx 2^k\!\left(1 + \frac{2^{-pk}}{p}\
right)$ and $2^{k+1}-1 \approx 2^{k+1}(1 - 2^{-(k+1)})$:

$$\text{LHS} \approx 2^{2k+1}\!\left(1 + \frac{2^{-pk}}{p} - 2^{-(k+1)}\right),
\quad \text{RHS} \approx 2^{2k+1}\!\left(1 - 2^{-(2k+1)}\right).$$

The inequality requires $\frac{2^{-pk}}{p} \geq 2^{-(k+1)}(1 - o(1))$, i.e., $\f
rac{1}{p} \geq 2^{k(p-1)-1}(1-o(1))$.

- If $p > 1$: the exponent $k(p-1)-1 \to \infty$, so the RHS $\to \infty$ while
$1/p$ is fixed — **contradiction**.
- If $p = 1$: $\frac{1}{p} = 1 \geq 2^{-1} = \frac{1}{2}$ — **satisfied**.

Hence $p \leq 1$.
