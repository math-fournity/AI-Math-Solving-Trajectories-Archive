### Setup

We want
$$L = \lim_{x\to\infty}\left[x - \frac{x-1}{x}\,2\,e^{-1/x}\,K_1(2/x)\right].$$

Set $t = 1/x$, so $t\to 0^+$ and $x = 1/t$. Then $\frac{x-1}{x} = 1 - t$, and th
e expression becomes
$$L = \lim_{t\to 0^+}\left[\frac{1}{t} - 2(1-t)\,e^{-t}\,K_1(2t)\right].$$

### Small-argument expansion of $K_1$

For integer order $\nu=1$, the standard expansion (from the logarithmic form of
$K_\nu$ at $z=0$) is
$$K_1(z) = \frac{1}{z} + \frac{z}{2}\!\left(\ln\frac{z}{2} + \gamma - \tfrac{1}{
2}\right) + O(z^3\ln z),\qquad z\to 0^+,$$
where $\gamma$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant. (This follows from $K_1(z)=\fra
c{1}{z}+\sum_{k\ge 0}\frac{(z/2)^{2k+1}}{k!(k+1)!}\bigl[\ln\frac{z}{2}-\tfrac12\
psi(k+1)-\tfrac12\psi(k+2)\bigr]$ with $\psi(1)=-\gamma$, $\psi(2)=1-\gamma$.)

With $z = 2t$:
$$K_1(2t) = \frac{1}{2t} + t\!\left(\ln t + \gamma - \tfrac{1}{2}\right) + O(t^3
\ln t).$$

Denote $A := \ln t + \gamma - \tfrac{1}{2}$, so $K_1(2t) = \frac{1}{2t} + tA + O
(t^3\ln t)$.

### Expand the prefactor

$$e^{-t} = 1 - t + \tfrac{t^2}{2} - \tfrac{t^3}{6} + \cdots,$$
$$(1-t)e^{-t} = (1-t)\!\left(1 - t + \tfrac{t^2}{2} - \cdots\right) = 1 - 2t + \
tfrac{3t^2}{2} + O(t^3).$$

### Multiply out

$$2(1-t)e^{-t}K_1(2t) = \bigl(1 - 2t + \tfrac{3t^2}{2} + O(t^3)\bigr)\!\left(\fr
ac{1}{t} + 2tA + O(t^3\ln t)\right).$$

Expanding term by term:
- From $\frac{1}{t}\cdot(1-2t+\frac{3t^2}{2}+\cdots)$: $\;\frac{1}{t} - 2 + \fra
c{3t}{2} + O(t^2)$.
- From $2tA\cdot(1-2t+\cdots)$: $\;2tA - 4t^2A + O(t^3\ln t)$.
- Remainder: $O(t^3\ln t)$.

Hence
$$2(1-t)e^{-t}K_1(2t) = \frac{1}{t} - 2 + \frac{3t}{2} + 2tA + O(t^2\ln t).$$

### Take the difference

$$\frac{1}{t} - 2(1-t)e^{-t}K_1(2t) = \frac{1}{t} - \left[\frac{1}{t} - 2 + \fra
c{3t}{2} + 2tA + O(t^2\ln t)\right]$$
$$= 2 - \frac{3t}{2} - 2tA + O(t^2\ln t)$$
$$= 2 - 2t\!\left(\ln t + \gamma - \tfrac{1}{2}\right) - \frac{3t}{2} + O(t^2\ln
 t)$$
$$= 2 - 2t\ln t - 2\gamma\, t + t - \frac{3t}{2} + O(t^2\ln t)$$
$$= 2 - 2t\ln t - \left(2\gamma + \tfrac{1}{2}\right)t + O(t^2\ln t).$$

### Take the limit

As $t\to 0^+$:
- $t\ln t \to 0$,
- $t \to 0$,
- $t^2\ln t \to 0$.

Therefore every correction term vanishes, and
$$L = \lim_{t\to 0^+}\left[2 - 2t\ln t - \left(2\gamma + \tfrac{1}{2}\right)t +
O(t^2\ln t)\right] = 2.$$

### Conclusion

The limit is indeed $\boxed{2}$. The claimed value is correct; there is no error
 in the reasoning. The leading singular term $\frac{1}{t}$ of $K_1(2t)$ exactly
cancels the $\frac{1}{t}$ from $x = 1/t$, and all remaining terms are $O(t\ln t)
$ or smaller, which vanish as $t\to 0^+$.
