## Proof

**Claim:** A compact topological manifold of positive dimension cannot be covere
d by a single chart. (The only exception is the trivial zero-dimensional one-poi
nt manifold.)

**Setup.** Let $M$ be a compact topological $n$-manifold (without boundary), and
 suppose for contradiction that $M$ is covered by a single chart $(U, \varphi)$,
 where $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is open and $\varphi: U \to M$ is a homeomorph
ism.

**Step 1: $U$ is compact.** Since $M$ is compact and $\varphi$ is a homeomorphis
m, $U = \varphi^{-1}(M)$ is compact (compactness is a topological invariant).

**Step 2: $U$ is closed in $\mathbb{R}^n$.** $\mathbb{R}^n$ is Hausdorff, and ev
ery compact subset of a Hausdorff space is closed. Since $U$ is compact, $U$ is
closed in $\mathbb{R}^n$.

**Step 3: $U$ is both open and closed.** By hypothesis $U$ is open (it is the do
main of a chart), and by Step 2 it is closed.

**Step 4: Connectedness forces $U \in \{\emptyset, \mathbb{R}^n\}$.** For $n \ge
q 1$, $\mathbb{R}^n$ is connected, so its only clopen subsets are $\emptyset$ an
d $\mathbb{R}^n$.

- If $U = \emptyset$, then $M = \varphi(\emptyset) = \emptyset$, contradicting t
he definition of a manifold (which is nonempty).
- If $U = \mathbb{R}^n$, then $\mathbb{R}^n$ must be compact (being homeomorphic
 to the compact space $M$). But $\mathbb{R}^n$ is **not** compact: the open cove
r $\{B(0,k)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}$ has no finite subcover.

Both cases yield a contradiction. Therefore no compact $n$-manifold with $n \geq
 1$ can be covered by a single chart. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on the boundary case.** The same argument applies to manifolds with bou
ndary, where charts map to open subsets of the closed half-space $\mathbb{H}^n =
 \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : x_n \geq 0\}$. For $n \geq 1$, $\mathbb{H}^n$ is connect
ed (it is convex), so its only clopen subsets are $\emptyset$ and $\mathbb{H}^n$
; the latter is non-compact (the cover $\{B(0,k) \cap \mathbb{H}^n\}$ has no fin
ite subcover). The identical contradiction arises.

**Remark on dimension zero.** A $0$-manifold is a discrete space; a compact one
is finite. A single chart has domain an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^0 = \{\ast\}$
, hence covers exactly one point. Thus a compact $0$-manifold is coverable by a
single chart **if and only if** it is a single point — the unique trivial except
ion.

**Conclusion.** Apart from the degenerate one-point case, a compact topological
manifold **cannot** be covered by a single chart.
