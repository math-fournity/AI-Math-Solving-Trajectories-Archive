## Proof

**Claim:** The series $S = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \left[e - \left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right
)^n\right]$ diverges to $+\infty$.

**Step 1: Asymptotic expansion of $\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^n$.**

Using the Taylor expansion $\ln(1+x) = x - \frac{x^2}{2} + \frac{x^3}{3} - \cdot
s$ with $x = \frac{1}{n}$:

$$n\ln\!\left(1+\tfrac{1}{n}\right) = 1 - \frac{1}{2n} + \frac{1}{3n^2} - \frac{
1}{4n^3} + \cdots$$

Set $u_n = -\frac{1}{2n} + \frac{1}{3n^2} - \cdots = -\frac{1}{2n} + O(n^{-2})$.
 Then:

$$\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^n = e \cdot e^{u_n}.$$

Expanding $e^{u_n} = 1 + u_n + \frac{u_n^2}{2} + \cdots$ and noting $u_n = -\fra
c{1}{2n} + O(n^{-2})$:

$$e^{u_n} = 1 - \frac{1}{2n} + O(n^{-2}).$$

Therefore:

$$\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^n = e\left(1 - \frac{1}{2n} + O(n^{-2})\right) = e
- \frac{e}{2n} + O(n^{-2}).$$

**Step 2: Asymptotics of the summand.**

$$e - \left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^n = \frac{e}{2n} + O(n^{-2}).$$

**Step 3: Limit comparison with the harmonic series.**

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{e - \left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^n}{\frac{1}{n}} = \lim_
{n\to\infty} \frac{\frac{e}{2n} + O(n^{-2})}{\frac{1}{n}} = \frac{e}{2} > 0.$$

Since $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n}$ diverges (harmonic series) and the limit a
bove is a positive finite constant, by the **Limit Comparison Test**, the series

$$S = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \left[e - \left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^n\right]$$

**diverges to $+\infty$**.
