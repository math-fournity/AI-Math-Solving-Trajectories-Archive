## Proof

Let $x' = (x_1,\dots,x_{n-1}) \in \mathbb{R}^{n-1}$ and write $f_{x'}(t) := f(x'
,t)$. We are given:

- $f \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$,
- $f$ is weakly differentiable w.r.t. $x_n$ with weak derivative $\partial_{x_n}
 f \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$, i.e. $\|\partial_{x_n} f\|_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^n)} < \inf
ty$.

**Step 1 (Fubini on the derivative).** Since $|\partial_{x_n} f| \in L^1(\mathbb
{R}^n)$, Fubini–Tonelli gives
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}} \!\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}} |\partial_{x_n} f(x',t)|\,dt\Bi
g)\,dx' = \|\partial_{x_n} f\|_{L^1(\mathbb{R}^n)} < \infty.
$$
Hence for Lebesgue-a.e. $x' \in \mathbb{R}^{n-1}$,
$$
\partial_{x_n} f(x',\cdot) \in L^1(\mathbb{R}). \tag{1}
$$
Likewise $f \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$ implies $f_{x'} \in L^1(\mathbb{R})$ for a.e.

**Step 2 (Slicing the weak-derivative identity).** Weak differentiability w.r.t.

$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} f\,\partial_{x_n}\varphi \;=\; -\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} (\partia

$$
Take tensor-product test functions $\varphi(x',t) = \psi(x')\,\eta(t)$ with $\ps

ting into (2) and using Fubini (justified by $f,\partial_{x_n}f \in L^1$ and $\p
si,\eta$ bounded/compactly supported):
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}} \psi(x')\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}} f_{x'}(t)\,\eta'(t)\,dt\B
ig] dx'
= -\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}} \psi(x')\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}} \partial_{x_n} f(x',t)
\,\eta(t)\,dt\Big] dx'.
$$
Since both bracketed inner integrals are locally integrable functions of $x'$ (a
gain by Fubini and (1)), and the identity holds for **all** $\psi \in C_c^\infty
(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})$, the du Bois-Reymond / fundamental lemma of the calculus of
variations yields: for Lebesgue-a.e. $x' \in \mathbb{R}^{n-1}$,
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}} f_{x'}(t)\,\eta'(t)\,dt = -\int_{\mathbb{R}} \partial_{x_n} f(
x',t)\,\eta(t)\,dt \qquad \forall\,\eta \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}). \tag{3}

**Step 3 (1D weak differentiability ⟹ absolute continuity).** Equation (3) is pr

c}}(\mathbb{R})$ is **weakly differentiable** on $\mathbb{R}$ with weak derivati

The classical 1D characterization of weak differentiability (see e.g. Brezis, *F

> A function $g \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})$ is weakly differentiable wit

t interval, with $\tilde g'(t) = h(t)$ for a.e. $t$. Explicitly,
> $$\tilde g(t) = \tilde g(a) + \int_a^t h(s)\,ds, \qquad t \in \mathbb{R},$$
> for any reference point $a$, and the right-hand side is absolutely continuous

Applying this with $g = f_{x'}$ and $h = h_{x'}$ (valid for a.e. $x'$, using (1)

$$

athrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})$) **is** absolutely continuous — its unique continuous r

Therefore, for Lebesgue almost every $(x_1,\dots,x_{n-1}) \in \mathbb{R}^{n-1}$,
