## Green's Function for the 2D Dirichlet Problem via Conformal Mapping

### Step 1: Green's function for the upper half-plane

Let $D = \{\zeta \in \mathbb{C} : \Im\zeta > 0\}$ be the upper half $\zeta$-plan
e, with pole at $\zeta_0 \in D$. The Green's function $G_H(\zeta, \zeta_0)$ must
 satisfy:

- $G_H$ is harmonic in $D \setminus \{\zeta_0\}$,
- $G_H \to +\infty$ as $\zeta \to \zeta_0$ (logarithmic singularity),
- $G_H = 0$ on $\partial D$ (the real axis).

Consider the Möbius transformation
$$w = \frac{\zeta - \zeta_0}{\zeta - \bar{\zeta}_0},$$
where $\bar{\zeta}_0$ is the complex conjugate of $\zeta_0$ (its reflection acro
ss the real axis). This map sends:
- $\zeta_0 \mapsto 0$,
- $\bar{\zeta}_0 \mapsto \infty$,
- the real axis $\Im\zeta = 0$ to the unit circle $|w| = 1$ (since for real $\ze
ta = t$, $|t - \zeta_0| = |t - \bar{\zeta}_0|$),
- the upper half-plane to the unit disk $|w| < 1$.

Therefore $|w| \leq 1$ in $D$, with $|w| = 1$ on $\partial D$ and $|w| \to 0$ as
 $\zeta \to \zeta_0$. The function
$$\boxed{G_H(\zeta, \zeta_0) = \ln\left|\frac{\zeta - \bar{\zeta}_0}{\zeta - \ze
ta_0}\right| = -\ln|w|}$$
is the Green's function for the upper half-plane. Indeed:
- **Harmonicity:** $\ln|\zeta - \bar{\zeta}_0|$ and $\ln|\zeta - \zeta_0|$ are b
oth harmonic (logarithms of moduli of analytic functions), so their difference i
s harmonic away from $\zeta_0$.
- **Singularity:** As $\zeta \to \zeta_0$, $|\zeta - \zeta_0| \to 0$, so $G_H \t
o +\infty$ logarithmically.
- **Boundary condition:** On the real axis ($\zeta = t \in \mathbb{R}$), $|t - \
zeta_0| = |t - \bar{\zeta}_0|$, so $G_H = 0$.

### Step 2: Green's function for a general region via conformal mapping

Let $D$ be a simply connected region in the $z$-plane, and let $\zeta = \zeta(z)
$ be a conformal map from $D$ onto the upper half-plane $\Im\zeta > 0$. Let $z_0
 \in D$ and set $\zeta_0 = \zeta(z_0)$.

**Claim.** The Green's function for $D$ with pole at $z_0$ is:
$$\boxed{G(z, z_0) = \ln\left|\frac{\zeta(z) - \overline{\zeta(z_0)}}{\zeta(z) -
 \zeta(z_0)}\right|}$$

**Proof.** We verify the three defining properties:

1. **Harmonicity in $D \setminus \{z_0\}$:** The function $G_H(\zeta, \zeta_0) =
 \ln|(\zeta - \bar{\zeta}_0)/(\zeta - \zeta_0)|$ is harmonic in the upper half-p
lane away from $\zeta_0$. Since $\zeta(z)$ is analytic (hence conformal) on $D$,
 the composition $G(z,z_0) = G_H(\zeta(z), \zeta(z_0))$ is harmonic in $D \setmi
nus \{z_0\}$ (harmonic functions composed with analytic functions remain harmoni
c).

2. **Singularity at $z_0$:** As $z \to z_0$, $\zeta(z) \to \zeta(z_0)$, so $|\ze
ta(z) - \zeta(z_0)| \to 0$ and $G \to +\infty$. Near $z_0$, $\zeta(z) - \zeta(z_
0) \approx \zeta'(z_0)(z - z_0)$ with $\zeta'(z_0) \neq 0$ (conformality), so $G
 \sim -\ln|z - z_0| + \text{const}$, which is the correct logarithmic singularit
y.

3. **Boundary condition $G = 0$ on $\partial D$:** The conformal map $\zeta(z)$
sends $\partial D$ to the real axis $\Im\zeta = 0$. For $\zeta$ on the real axis
, $G_H(\zeta, \zeta_0) = 0$ as shown in Step 1. Hence $G = 0$ on $\partial D$.

This completes the derivation of the general formula. $\square$
