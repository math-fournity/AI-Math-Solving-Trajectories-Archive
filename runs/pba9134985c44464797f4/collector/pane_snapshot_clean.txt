## Proof

**Setup and notation.** Let $A$ be a Jacobson ring and $B$ a finitely generated
$A$-algebra that is a field, with $B$ finite over $A$ (as an $A$-module) and gen
erated by at most two elements as an $A$-module. We interpret $[B:A]$ as the deg
ree of $B$ over the residue field of $A$ (the only sense in which a field degree
 is defined here, since $A$ need not itself be a field).

**Step 1 — The kernel is maximal (Zariski's lemma, Jacobson form).** Let $\varph
i:A\to B$ be the structure map and let $\mathfrak p=\ker\varphi$. Since $B$ is a
 field, $\mathfrak p$ is prime and $A/\mathfrak p$ embeds into $B$. Because $B$
is finite as an $A$-module, it is finite (hence integral) as an $A/\mathfrak p$-
module. For any nonzero $\bar a\in A/\mathfrak p$, its inverse $\bar a^{-1}$ lie
s in $B$ and is integral over $A/\mathfrak p$; integrality forces $\bar a^{-1}\i
n A/\mathfrak p$. Thus $A/\mathfrak p$ is a field, so $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak m$
is a **maximal** ideal. Setting $k:=A/\mathfrak m$, the map $A\to B$ factors as
$A\twoheadrightarrow k\hookrightarrow B$, and $B$ is a finite field extension of
 $k$. (This is exactly the Jacobson-ring version of Zariski's lemma: a field tha
t is a finitely generated algebra over a Jacobson ring is a finite extension of
a residue field $A/\mathfrak m$.)

**Step 2 — Upper bound from two module generators.** The hypothesis "generated b
y at most two elements over $A$" means $B$ is generated as an $A$-module by a se
t $\{e_1,e_2\}$ (possibly fewer). Since the $A$-action on $B$ factors through $k
=A/\mathfrak m$, the same set $\{e_1,e_2\}$ generates $B$ as a $k$-vector space.
 Therefore
$$[B:A]\;=\;[B:k]\;\le\;2.$$

**Step 3 — The bound is attained.** Take $A=\mathbb Q$, which is a Jacobson ring
 (every field is Jacobson). Let $B=\mathbb Q(i)=\mathbb Q[x]/(x^2+1)$. Then:
- $B$ is a field and a finitely generated $\mathbb Q$-algebra (generated by the
single element $x$);
- $B$ is finite over $\mathbb Q$ as a module, of rank $2$;
- $B$ is generated as a $\mathbb Q$-module by $\{1,i\}$ — two elements;
- $[B:\mathbb Q]=2$.

All hypotheses are satisfied and the degree equals $2$, so the upper bound of St
ep 2 is sharp.

**Conclusion.** The maximum possible degree is

$$\boxed{[B:A]=2}.$$
