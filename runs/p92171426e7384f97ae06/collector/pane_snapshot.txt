## Proof

**Theorem.** *Let $G$ be a connected linear semisimple Lie group with no compact
 factors. Then $G$ is generated by its unipotent elements.*

**Convention.** "Semisimple Lie group" is taken to mean connected (the standard
convention). If $G$ were disconnected, all unipotent elements lie in $G^0$ (sinc
e $\exp$ of a nilpotent element lies in the identity component), so they could n
ot generate $G$; the content of the theorem is for the connected case.

---

### Step 1. Reduction to simple factors

The Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ of $G$ is semisimple, so it decomposes as a direc
t sum of simple ideals:
$$\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{g}_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathfrak{g}_k.$$
"No compact factors" means each $\mathfrak{g}_i$ is a **non-compact** simple rea
l Lie algebra (the Killing form $B|_{\mathfrak{g}_i}$ is not negative definite).
 Let $G_i$ be the connected Lie subgroup with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}_i$. Sinc
e $G$ is connected, $G = G_1 \cdot G_2 \cdots G_k$, and the factors commute. If
each $G_i$ is generated by unipotent elements, then so is $G$. Thus it suffices
to prove the theorem for a single non-compact simple factor.

### Step 2. Restricted root space decomposition

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a non-compact simple real Lie algebra, and $G$ the connect
ed Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$. Choose a Cartan involution $\theta
$, giving the Cartan decomposition
$$\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{k} \oplus \mathfrak{p}.$$
Since $\mathfrak{g}$ is non-compact, $\mathfrak{p} \neq \{0\}$. Let $\mathfrak{a
} \subset \mathfrak{p}$ be a maximal abelian subspace; then $\mathfrak{a} \neq \
{0\}$. The simultaneous eigenspace decomposition of $\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak
{a})$ gives the **restricted root space decomposition**:
$$\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{g}_0 \oplus \bigoplus_{\alpha \in \Sigma} \mathfrak{g
}_\alpha,$$
where $\Sigma \subset \mathfrak{a}^* \setminus \{0\}$ is the set of restricted r
oots, $\mathfrak{g}_0 = \{X \in \mathfrak{g} : [H,X]=0 \;\forall H \in \mathfrak
{a}\}$, and $\mathfrak{g}_\alpha = \{X \in \mathfrak{g} : [H,X] = \alpha(H)X \;\
forall H \in \mathfrak{a}\}$.

**Claim:** $\Sigma \neq \emptyset$. If $\Sigma = \emptyset$, then $\mathfrak{g}
= \mathfrak{g}_0$, meaning $\mathfrak{a}$ centralizes all of $\mathfrak{g}$, so
$\mathfrak{a} \subset \mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}) = \{0\}$ (since $\mathfrak{g}$
is semisimple), contradicting $\mathfrak{a} \neq \{0\}$.

### Step 3. Root space elements are nilpotent, hence give unipotent group elemen
ts

For each $\alpha \in \Sigma$ and $X \in \mathfrak{g}_\alpha$, the operator $\ope
ratorname{ad}(X)$ is **nilpotent**: it maps $\mathfrak{g}_\beta \to \mathfrak{g}
_{\beta+\alpha}$ and $\mathfrak{g}_0 \to \mathfrak{g}_\alpha$, and since there a
re finitely many restricted roots, $\operatorname{ad}(X)^N = 0$ for $N$ large en
ough.

Since $G$ is **linear**, it admits a faithful finite-dimensional representation
$\rho: G \to \mathrm{GL}(V)$ with differential $d\rho: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfra
k{gl}(V)$. By the **preservation of the Jordan decomposition**, $\operatorname{a
d}$-nilpotency of $X$ implies $d\rho(X)$ is nilpotent. Therefore
$$\rho(\exp(X)) = \exp(d\rho(X)) = I + d\rho(X) + \tfrac{1}{2}d\rho(X)^2 + \cdot
s$$
is a **unipotent** matrix (sole eigenvalue $1$). So $\exp(X) \in G$ is a unipote
nt element for every $X \in \mathfrak{g}_\alpha$, $\alpha \in \Sigma$.

### Step 4. The root spaces Lie-generate $\mathfrak{g}$

Let $\mathfrak{h} = \langle \mathfrak{g}_\alpha : \alpha \in \Sigma \rangle_{\te
xt{Lie}}$ be the Lie subalgebra generated by all restricted root spaces. We show
 $\mathfrak{h}$ is an **ideal** of $\mathfrak{g}$.

Since $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{g}_0 \oplus \bigoplus_\alpha \mathfrak{g}_\alpha
$, it suffices to check $[\mathfrak{g}_\beta, \mathfrak{h}] \subset \mathfrak{h}
$ for each $\beta \in \Sigma \cup \{0\}$:

- **$[\mathfrak{g}_0, \mathfrak{h}] \subset \mathfrak{h}$:** $\mathfrak{g}_0$ no
rmalizes each root space ($[\mathfrak{g}_0, \mathfrak{g}_\alpha] \subset \mathfr
ak{g}_\alpha$), so it normalizes the algebra they generate.

- **$[\mathfrak{g}_\beta, \mathfrak{h}] \subset \mathfrak{h}$ for $\beta \in \Si
gma$:** For $X \in \mathfrak{g}_\beta$, $Y \in \mathfrak{g}_\alpha$, we have $[X
,Y] \in \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha+\beta}$ if $\alpha+\beta \in \Sigma$, or $[X,Y] \in
 \mathfrak{g}_0$ if $\alpha + \beta = 0$, or $[X,Y] = 0$ otherwise. In the first
 and third cases the bracket lands in $\mathfrak{h}$ trivially. In the second ca
se ($\alpha = -\beta$), both $\mathfrak{g}_\beta$ and $\mathfrak{g}_{-\beta}$ li
e in $\mathfrak{h}$, so $[\mathfrak{g}_\beta, \mathfrak{g}_{-\beta}] \subset \ma
thfrak{h}$.

Thus $\mathfrak{h} \trianglelefteq \mathfrak{g}$. Since $\mathfrak{g}$ is **simp
le** and $\Sigma \neq \emptyset$ gives $\mathfrak{h} \neq \{0\}$, we conclude
$$\mathfrak{h} = \mathfrak{g}.$$

### Step 5. The unipotent elements generate $G$

Let $\Sigma^+ \subset \Sigma$ be a choice of positive restricted roots, and set
$\Sigma^- = -\Sigma^+$. Define:
$$N^+ = \langle \exp(X) : X \in \mathfrak{g}_\alpha,\; \alpha \in \Sigma^+ \rang
le, \qquad N^- = \langle \exp(X) : X \in \mathfrak{g}_\alpha,\; \alpha \in \Sigm
a^- \rangle.$$

These are connected nilpotent Lie groups (with Lie algebras $\mathfrak{n}^\pm =
\bigoplus_{\alpha \in \Sigma^\pm} \mathfrak{g}_\alpha$), and every element of $N
^\pm$ is unipotent (a product of unipotent elements in a nilpotent group is unip
otent, or directly: $\mathfrak{n}^\pm$ consists of nilpotent elements).

Let $W = \langle N^+, N^- \rangle$ be the subgroup generated by $N^+$ and $N^-$.
 Then:

1. **$W$ is connected**, since it is generated by connected subgroups (any eleme
nt is a finite product of elements from $N^+$ and $N^-$, and the product of conn
ected sets containing the identity is connected).

2. **$\operatorname{Lie}(W) \supseteq \mathfrak{h} = \mathfrak{g}$:** The Lie al
gebra of $W$ is a Lie subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g}$ containing $\mathfrak{n}^+$ a
nd $\mathfrak{n}^-$, hence containing all root spaces $\mathfrak{g}_\alpha$ ($\a
lpha \in \Sigma$), hence containing the Lie algebra they generate, which is $\ma
thfrak{g}$ by Step 4. So $\operatorname{Lie}(W) = \mathfrak{g}$.

3. Since $W$ is a connected subgroup of the connected group $G$ with $\operatorn
ame{Lie}(W) = \operatorname{Lie}(G) = \mathfrak{g}$, we have $W = G$.

Every element of $N^+$ and $N^-$ is unipotent, so $W = G$ is generated by unipot
ent elements. Since this holds for each simple factor $G_i$, the original group
$G = G_1 \cdots G_k$ is generated by unipotent elements. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark on necessity of hypotheses.** Both hypotheses are essential:
- **No compact factors:** If $G$ is compact semisimple (e.g., $\mathrm{SU}(2)$),
 every element is semisimple (the adjoint representation is orthogonal, hence di
agonalizable over $\mathbb{C}$), so $G$ has no nontrivial unipotent elements and
 cannot be generated by them.
- **Linearity:** The argument uses a faithful finite-dimensional representation
to conclude that $\operatorname{ad}$-nilpotent elements map to nilpotent matrice
s, giving unipotent group elements. For non-linear (covering) groups, the center
 may be infinite, and the theorem can fail.
