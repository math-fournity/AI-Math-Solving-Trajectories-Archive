## Proof

**Claim.** The only linear functional $\varphi: X \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfying $|\
varphi(x)| \leq \|x\|^2$ for all $x \in X$ is the zero functional $\varphi \equi
v 0$.

**Proof.** Let $\varphi: X \to \mathbb{R}$ be a linear functional with $|\varphi
(x)| \leq \|x\|^2$ for every $x \in X$.

Fix any $x \in X$ with $x \neq 0$. For every scalar $t \in \mathbb{R}$ with $t \
neq 0$, by linearity of $\varphi$ and homogeneity of the norm,

$$
|\varphi(tx)| = |t|\,|\varphi(x)| \leq \|tx\|^2 = t^2 \|x\|^2.
$$

Dividing both sides by $|t| > 0$ gives

$$
|\varphi(x)| \leq |t|\,\|x\|^2.
$$

This inequality holds for **every** $t \neq 0$. Letting $|t| \to 0^+$, the right
-hand side tends to $0$, so

$$
|\varphi(x)| \leq 0.
$$

Hence $\varphi(x) = 0$. Since $x \neq 0$ was arbitrary, $\varphi$ vanishes on ev
ery nonzero vector. Trivially $\varphi(0) = 0$ by linearity. Therefore $\varphi
\equiv 0$.

**Conversely**, the zero functional is linear and satisfies $|\varphi(x)| = 0 \l
eq \|x\|^2$ for all $x \in X$.

Thus the unique linear functional satisfying the given bound is $\varphi \equiv
0$. $\blacksquare$
