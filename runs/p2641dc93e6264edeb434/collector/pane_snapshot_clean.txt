## Residue of $\frac{e^{z}}{\sin(iz)}$ at $z=-n\pi i$

**Locating the singularity.** The poles of $f(z)=\dfrac{e^{z}}{\sin(iz)}$ occur
where the denominator vanishes:
$$\sin(iz)=0 \iff iz=k\pi,\; k\in\mathbb{Z} \iff z=-k\pi i.$$
Thus $z_{0}=-n\pi i$ corresponds to $k=n$.

**Type of singularity.** The zeros of $\sin(iz)$ are simple, since
$$\frac{d}{dz}\sin(iz)=i\cos(iz),$$
and at $z=-k\pi i$ we have $iz=k\pi$, so $i\cos(iz)=i\cos(k\pi)=i(-1)^{k}\neq 0$
. Hence $z_{0}=-n\pi i$ is a **simple pole**.

**Computing the residue.** For a simple pole of $g(z)/h(z)$,
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_{0}}\frac{g(z)}{h(z)}=\frac{g(z_{0})}{h'(z_{0})}.$$
With $g(z)=e^{z}$ and $h(z)=\sin(iz)$, $h'(z)=i\cos(iz)$. Evaluating at $z_{0}=-
n\pi i$:

- $g(z_{0})=e^{-n\pi i}=(-1)^{n}$,
- $iz_{0}=i(-n\pi i)=n\pi$, so $h'(z_{0})=i\cos(n\pi)=i(-1)^{n}$.

Therefore
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=-n\pi i}\frac{e^{z}}{\sin(iz)}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{i(-1)^{n}
}=\frac{1}{i}=-i.$$

**Result.** The residue is $\boxed{-i}$, independent of $n$.
