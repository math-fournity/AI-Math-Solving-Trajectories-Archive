## Proof

**Step 1: Show the parameter-dependent factors tend to 1.**

As $k\to\infty$:
- $e^{-m/(2k)} \to e^0 = 1$.
- $\frac{m}{2k}\ln(m+2k) = \frac{m}{2k}\bigl(\ln(2k)+\ln(1+\tfrac{m}{2k})\bigr)
= \frac{m\ln(2k)}{2k}+O(1/k^2)\to 0$, so $(m+2k)^{m/(2k)}\to 1$.
- $c^{m/(2k)}\to 1$.

Thus the limit reduces to:
$$L = \lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{2k(m+2k)}{(k!)^{1/k}\,\Gamma\!\left(k+\tfrac{p}{2}\
right)^{1/k}}.$$

**Step 2: Asymptotics of the numerator.**

$$2k(m+2k) = 4k^2\left(1+\frac{m}{2k}\right) = 4k^2\bigl(1+O(1/k)\bigr).$$

**Step 3: Stirling's approximation for $(k!)^{1/k}$.**

Stirling's formula gives $\ln(k!) = k\ln k - k + \tfrac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi k)+O(1/k)$
, so:

$$\frac{1}{k}\ln(k!) = \ln k - 1 + \frac{\ln(2\pi k)}{2k}+O(1/k^2).$$

Therefore:
$$(k!)^{1/k} = k\,e^{-1}\cdot\exp\!\left(\frac{\ln(2\pi k)}{2k}+O(1/k^2)\right)
= \frac{k}{e}\bigl(1+O(\tfrac{\ln k}{k})\bigr).$$

**Step 4: Stirling's approximation for $\Gamma(k+p/2)^{1/k}$.**

Using $\ln\Gamma(z) = (z-\tfrac12)\ln z - z + \tfrac12\ln(2\pi)+O(1/z)$ with $z=
k+\tfrac{p}{2}$:

$$\ln\Gamma\!\left(k+\tfrac{p}{2}\right) = \left(k+\tfrac{p}{2}-\tfrac12\right)\
ln\!\left(k+\tfrac{p}{2}\right) - \left(k+\tfrac{p}{2}\right) + \tfrac12\ln(2\pi
)+O(1/k).$$

Expanding $\ln(k+\tfrac{p}{2}) = \ln k + \tfrac{p}{2k}+O(1/k^2)$:

$$\ln\Gamma\!\left(k+\tfrac{p}{2}\right) = k\ln k + \tfrac{p-1}{2}\ln k + \tfrac
{p}{2} - k - \tfrac{p}{2} + \tfrac12\ln(2\pi)+O(1/k)$$
$$= k\ln k - k + \tfrac{p-1}{2}\ln k + \tfrac12\ln(2\pi)+O(1/k).$$

Dividing by $k$:
$$\frac{1}{k}\ln\Gamma\!\left(k+\tfrac{p}{2}\right) = \ln k - 1 + \frac{(p-1)\ln
 k}{2k}+\frac{\ln(2\pi)}{2k}+O(1/k^2).$$

Therefore:
$$\Gamma\!\left(k+\tfrac{p}{2}\right)^{1/k} = \frac{k}{e}\bigl(1+O(\tfrac{\ln k}
{k})\bigr).$$

**Step 5: Combine.**

$$L = \lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{4k^2\bigl(1+O(1/k)\bigr)}{\dfrac{k}{e}\bigl(1+O(\tf
rac{\ln k}{k})\bigr)\cdot\dfrac{k}{e}\bigl(1+O(\tfrac{\ln k}{k})\bigr)} = \lim_{
k\to\infty}\frac{4k^2}{k^2/e^2}\cdot\frac{1+O(1/k)}{\bigl(1+O(\tfrac{\ln k}{k})\
bigr)^2}.$$

Since $\frac{1+O(1/k)}{(1+O(\ln k/k))^2}\to 1$, we obtain:

$$L = \lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{4k^2}{k^2/e^2} = 4e^2.$$

The limit is independent of the parameters $m$, $p$, and $c$, since all their co
ntributions vanish as $O(1/k)$ or $O(\ln k/k)$.

$$\boxed{L = 4e^2}$$
