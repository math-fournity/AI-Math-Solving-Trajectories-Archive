## Proof

The integrand $f(x) = \sin(x)\sin(x^2)$ is continuous on $[0,\infty)$, so the on
ly concern is convergence at infinity. Using the product-to-sum identity,
$$\sin(x)\sin(x^2) = \tfrac{1}{2}\cos(x - x^2) - \tfrac{1}{2}\cos(x + x^2),$$
it suffices to show that $\int_0^\infty \cos(x - x^2)\,dx$ and $\int_0^\infty \c
os(x + x^2)\,dx$ both converge.

**Lemma (integration-by-parts for oscillatory integrals).** Let $\phi \in C^2([a
,\infty))$ with $\phi'$ monotone and $|\phi'(x)| \geq c > 0$ on $[a,\infty)$. Th
en $\int_a^\infty \cos(\phi(x))\,dx$ converges.

*Proof of Lemma.* Since $\phi' \neq 0$, write $\cos(\phi(x)) = \frac{1}{\phi'(x)
}\frac{d}{dx}\sin(\phi(x))$ and integrate by parts on $[a,B]$:
$$\int_a^B \cos(\phi(x))\,dx = \left[\frac{\sin(\phi(x))}{\phi'(x)}\right]_a^B +
 \int_a^B \frac{\phi''(x)}{(\phi'(x))^2}\sin(\phi(x))\,dx.$$

The boundary term is bounded in absolute value by $\frac{1}{|\phi'(a)|}+\frac{1}
{|\phi'(B)|} \leq \frac{2}{c}$. For the integral term, since $\phi'$ is monotone
, $\phi''$ does not change sign, so
$$\int_a^B \frac{|\phi''(x)|}{|\phi'(x)|^2}\,dx = \left|\int_a^B \frac{\phi''(x)
}{(\phi'(x))^2}\,dx\right| = \left|\left[-\frac{1}{\phi'(x)}\right]_a^B\right| =
 \left|\frac{1}{\phi'(a)} - \frac{1}{\phi'(B)}\right| \leq \frac{2}{c}.$$

Thus $\int_a^\infty \frac{\phi''(x)}{(\phi'(x))^2}\sin(\phi(x))\,dx$ converges a
bsolutely, and the boundary term $\frac{\sin(\phi(B))}{\phi'(B)} \to 0$ as $B\to
\infty$ (since $|\phi'(B)|\to\infty$ or at least $|\phi'(B)|\geq c$... in fact m
onotonicity and $|\phi'|\geq c$ gives $|\sin(\phi(B))/\phi'(B)| \leq 1/c \to$ bo
unded, but we need the limit to exist).

More precisely: the right-hand side equals $\frac{\sin\phi(B)}{\phi'(B)} - \frac
{\sin\phi(a)}{\phi'(a)} + \int_a^B \frac{\phi''}{(\phi')^2}\sin\phi\,dx$. The in
tegral converges (absolutely) as $B\to\infty$. For the boundary term at $B$: sin
ce $\phi'$ is monotone and bounded away from $0$, either $|\phi'(B)|\to\infty$ (
giving $\frac{\sin\phi(B)}{\phi'(B)}\to 0$) or $|\phi'(B)|\to L\geq c$ (in which
 case $\phi''\to 0$ and the integral still converges, and $\frac{\sin\phi(B)}{\p
hi'(B)}$ is bounded but need not have a limit).

To handle the boundary term cleanly: note $\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(\frac{\sin\phi(x)
}{\phi'(x)}\right) = \cos\phi(x) - \frac{\phi''(x)\sin\phi(x)}{(\phi'(x))^2}$, s
o $\frac{\sin\phi(B)}{\phi'(B)} = \frac{\sin\phi(a)}{\phi'(a)} + \int_a^B \cos\p
hi\,dx - \int_a^B \frac{\phi''\sin\phi}{(\phi')^2}\,dx$. This is circular. Inste
ad, observe that $\int_a^B \frac{\phi''}{(\phi')^2}\sin\phi\,dx$ converges to a
limit $L_0$ as $B\to\infty$ (absolute convergence, established above). Hence
$$\int_a^B \cos\phi\,dx = \frac{\sin\phi(B)}{\phi'(B)} - \frac{\sin\phi(a)}{\phi
'(a)} + \int_a^B \frac{\phi''\sin\phi}{(\phi')^2}\,dx.$$
The last integral $\to L_0 - 0$ (converges). It remains to show $\frac{\sin\phi(
B)}{\phi'(B)}$ has a limit. Since $\int_a^\infty \frac{|\phi''|}{|\phi'|^2}dx <
\infty$ (shown above, $\leq 2/c$), and $\frac{1}{\phi'}$ is monotone (as $\phi'$
 is monotone and of constant sign) with $\frac{1}{\phi'(x)}\to 0$ (because $|\ph
i'|\geq c$ and monotone forces $|\phi'|\to\infty$ or a finite limit $\geq c$; if
 it tends to a finite limit $\ell$, then $\phi''\to 0$ and $\int\frac{|\phi''|}{
|\phi'|^2}<\infty$ still holds, and $\frac{\sin\phi(B)}{\phi'(B)}\to \frac{\sin(
\text{?})}{\ell}$ which need not converge).

The cleanest fix: require $|\phi'(x)|\to\infty$, which holds in our applications
. Then $\frac{\sin\phi(B)}{\phi'(B)}\to 0$, and $\int_a^\infty \cos\phi\,dx = -\
frac{\sin\phi(a)}{\phi'(a)} + \int_a^\infty \frac{\phi''\sin\phi}{(\phi')^2}\,dx
$ converges. ∎

*(In our two applications below, $|\phi'|\to\infty$, so the Lemma applies.)*

**Application 1: $\int_0^\infty \cos(x+x^2)\,dx$.** Take $\phi(x)=x+x^2$ on $[1,
\infty)$. Then $\phi'(x)=1+2x\geq 3$, $\phi''(x)=2>0$ (so $\phi'$ is monotone in
creasing), and $|\phi'(x)|\to\infty$. By the Lemma, $\int_1^\infty \cos(x+x^2)\,
dx$ converges; on $[0,1]$ it is a proper Riemann integral. Hence $\int_0^\infty
\cos(x+x^2)\,dx$ converges.

**Application 2: $\int_0^\infty \cos(x-x^2)\,dx$.** Take $\phi(x)=x-x^2$, so $\p
hi'(x)=1-2x$ vanishes at $x=\tfrac12$. Split: $\int_0^1 \cos(x-x^2)\,dx$ is a pr
oper integral (continuous on a compact interval). On $[1,\infty)$, $\phi'(x)=1-2
x\leq -1$, so $|\phi'(x)|=2x-1\geq 1$ with $|\phi'(x)|\to\infty$, and $\phi''(x)
=-2<0$ (so $\phi'$ is monotone). By the Lemma, $\int_1^\infty \cos(x-x^2)\,dx$ c
onverges. Hence $\int_0^\infty \cos(x-x^2)\,dx$ converges.

**Conclusion.** Both component integrals converge, so
$$\int_0^\infty \sin(x)\sin(x^2)\,dx = \tfrac12\int_0^\infty \cos(x-x^2)\,dx - \
tfrac12\int_0^\infty \cos(x+x^2)\,dx$$
converges. $\blacksquare$
