## Proof

**Answer: Yes, the order of the Galois group of $g(x)$ is at most $m!$.**

### Setup and the key construction

Let $f(x) \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ be a palindromic (reciprocal) polynomial of even de
gree $n = 2m$, so $f(x) = x^{2m} f(1/x)$. Writing $f(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{2m} a_k x^
k$ with $a_k = a_{2m-k}$, we form

$$x^{-m} f(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{2m} a_k\, x^{k-m} = a_m + \sum_{j=1}^{m} a_{m+j}\big
l(x^j + x^{-j}\bigr).$$

For each $j \geq 1$, the Laurent polynomial $x^j + x^{-j}$ is a polynomial of de
gree $j$ in the variable $t = x + x^{-1}$ (this follows from the recurrence $x^j
 + x^{-j} = (x^{j-1}+x^{-(j-1)})(x+x^{-1}) - (x^{j-2}+x^{-(j-2)})$, with $x^0+x^
0 = 2$ and $x^1+x^{-1} = t$). Therefore

$$x^{-m} f(x) = g(x + x^{-1})$$

for a unique polynomial $g(t) \in \mathbb{Q}[t]$ of degree $m$. This confirms th
at $g$ is a well-defined polynomial of degree $m$ with rational coefficients.

### The Galois group bound

The **Galois group of $g(x)$** over $\mathbb{Q}$ is $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathb
b{Q})$, where $K$ is the splitting field of $g$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Let $\beta_1,
 \beta_2, \dots, \beta_d$ be the **distinct** roots of $g$ in $K$, where $d \leq
 m$ (since $g$ has degree $m$).

Every $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ permutes the roots of $g$, h
ence permutes $\{\beta_1, \dots, \beta_d\}$. This yields a group homomorphism

$$\Phi: \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \longrightarrow S_d, \qquad \sigma \map
sto \bigl(\text{permutation induced on } \{\beta_1,\dots,\beta_d\}\bigr).$$

**$\Phi$ is injective.** If $\sigma \in \ker\Phi$, then $\sigma$ fixes every $\b
eta_i$. Since $K = \mathbb{Q}(\beta_1, \dots, \beta_d)$ is generated over $\math
bb{Q}$ by the roots of $g$, fixing all generators means $\sigma = \mathrm{id}$.
Hence $\ker\Phi$ is trivial.

Therefore $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \Phi(\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mat
hbb{Q})) \leq S_d$, and

$$|\operatorname{Gal}(g)| = |\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})| \leq |S_d| = d! \
leq m!.$$

### Conclusion

The palindromic structure of $f$ and the substitution $t = x + x^{-1}$ serve to
**construct** $g$ as a degree-$m$ polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}$. Once $g$ is esta
blished as a polynomial of degree $m$, the general fact that the Galois group of
 any degree-$m$ polynomial embeds into $S_m$ gives the bound directly. No additi
onal structure from the palindromic relation is needed for the inequality itself
—the bound $|\operatorname{Gal}(g)| \leq m!$ holds for **every** polynomial of d
egree $m$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

$$\boxed{|\operatorname{Gal}(g)| \leq m!\,.}$$
