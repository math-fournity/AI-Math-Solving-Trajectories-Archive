Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum number with all distinct dig

## Setting Up the Framework

Let $A = d_1d_2\cdots d_n$ with all digits distinct. Let $P_k$ be the number for
med by the first $k$ digits. The condition is: $P_k \mid A$ for all $k = 1, \ldo
ts, n-1$.

**Key relation:** $A = P_k \cdot 10^{n-k} + S_k$, where $S_k$ is the number form
ed by the last $n-k$ digits. So $P_k \mid S_k$.

## Ruling Out Long Numbers

**For $n \geq 5$:** The constraint $P_{n-1} \mid d_n$ with $P_{n-1} \geq 10^{n-2
} \geq 1000$ forces $d_n = 0$. Then $P_{n-2} \mid (10d_{n-1} + 0) = 10d_{n-1}$,
but $P_{n-2} \geq 10^{n-3} \geq 100$ while $10d_{n-1} \leq 90$, forcing $d_{n-1}
 = 0$ — contradicting distinct digits. So **no number with 5+ digits works**.

## Analyzing 4-Digit Numbers

For $n=4$: $A = d_1d_2d_3d_4$, all distinct, $d_1 \neq 0$.

- **Constraint from $P_3$:** $P_3 \mid d_4$. Since $P_3 \geq 100 > 9 \geq d_4$,
we get $d_4 = 0$.
- **Constraint from $P_2$:** $P_2 \mid (10d_3 + d_4) = 10d_3$, where $P_2 = 10d_
1 + d_2 \geq 10$.
- **Constraint from $P_1$:** $d_1 \mid (100d_2 + 10d_3)$, i.e., $d_1 \mid 10(10d
_2 + d_3)$.

Since $P_2 \geq 10$ and $10d_3 \leq 90$, we need $P_2 \mid 10d_3$ with $10d_3$ a
 positive multiple of 10 (since $d_3 \neq 0$). This means $P_2$ must divide some
 value in $\{10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90\}$.

**Checking $d_1 = 9$:** $P_2 \in \{91,\ldots,98\}$, all $> 90$. Only $10d_3 = 0$
 works — contradiction.

**Checking $d_1 = 8, 7, 6, 5$:** $P_2 \geq 51$, and $2P_2 \geq 102 > 90$. Since
$P_2$ is never a multiple of 10 (as $d_2 \neq 0$), no multiple of $P_2$ in $\{10
,\ldots,90\}$ is also a multiple of 10. All fail.

**Checking $d_1 = 4$:** $P_2 \in \{41,42,43,45,46,47,48,49\}$. Need $P_2 \mid 10
d_3$ with $d_3$ even (from $4 \mid 10(10d_2+d_3) \Rightarrow d_3$ even). Only $P
_2 = 45$ gives $2 \cdot 45 = 90 = 10 \cdot 9$, but $d_3 = 9$ is odd — fails. All
 others: $2P_2$ isn't a multiple of 10. **Fails.**

**Checking $d_1 = 3$:** $P_2 \in \{31,32,34,35,36,37,38,39\}$. Need $P_2 \mid 10
d_3$ and $3 \mid (d_2 + d_3)$ (from $3 \mid 10(10d_2+d_3)$).

- $P_2 = 35$ ($d_2 = 5$): $2 \times 35 = 70 = 10 \times 7$, so $d_3 = 7$. Check:
 $d_3 = 7 \notin \{3, 5, 0\}$ ✓, and $d_2 + d_3 = 5 + 7 = 12 \equiv 0 \pmod{3}$
✓.

This gives $A = 3570$.

**No other $P_2$ works** for $d_1 = 3$: only multiples of 5 in the set give $2P_
2$ as a multiple of 10, and $35$ is the only one.
