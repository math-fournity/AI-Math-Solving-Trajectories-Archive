## Proof

We evaluate $\lim_{x \to 1}(x-1)\,\zeta(x)$.

**Key fact.** The Riemann zeta function $\zeta(s)$ has a simple pole at $s=1$ wi
th residue $1$. Equivalently, near $s=1$,

$$\zeta(s) = \frac{1}{s-1} + \gamma + O(s-1),$$

where $\gamma$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant. Let me justify this from first
principles.

**Laurent expansion at $s=1$.** Write

$$\zeta(s) = \sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n^{s}} + \sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{s}
}.$$

Using the integral comparison, for $\operatorname{Re}(s)>1$,

$$\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{s}} = \int_{N}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x^{s}} + O(N
^{-\sigma}) = \frac{N^{1-s}}{s-1} + O(N^{-\sigma}),\qquad \sigma=\operatorname{R
e}(s).$$

A standard Mellin-transform / analytic-continuation argument (subtracting $\frac
{1}{s-1}$) shows that

$$\zeta(s) - \frac{1}{s-1}$$

extends to an entire function in a neighborhood of $s=1$; its value at $s=1$ is
the Euler–Mascheroni constant $\gamma$. Hence $s=1$ is a simple pole of $\zeta$
with residue

$$\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\zeta(s)=\lim_{s\to 1}(s-1)\zeta(s)=1.$$

**Computing the limit.** Using the Laurent expansion,

$$(x-1)\zeta(x)=(x-1)\left(\frac{1}{x-1}+\gamma+O(x-1)\right)=1+\gamma(x-1)+O\!\
left((x-1)^{2}\right).$$

Taking $x\to 1$,

$$\lim_{x\to 1}(x-1)\zeta(x)=1.$$
