## Proof

Let $X_1, X_2, \dots$ be i.i.d. random variables with common density $f$. Then

$$I_n(r) = \mathbb{P}\!\left(X_1^2 + X_2^2 + \cdots + X_n^2 \le r^2\right) = \ma
thbb{P}(S_n \le r^2),$$

where $S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2$.

**Step 1: $\mathbb{E}[X_1^2] > 0$.**

Since $f$ is continuous, $f \ge 0$, and $\int f = 1$, the function $f$ is not id
entically zero. Hence there exists $x_0 \in \mathbb{R}$ with $f(x_0) > 0$. By co
ntinuity, $f > 0$ on some open interval $U$ containing $x_0$. The interval $U$ c
ontains points $x \ne 0$ at which $x^2 > 0$ and $f(x) > 0$, so

$$\mathbb{E}[X_1^2] = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x^2 f(x)\,dx \ge \int_U x^2 f(x)\,
dx > 0.$$

Thus $\mathbb{E}[X_1^2] \in (0, +\infty]$.

**Step 2: $S_n \to +\infty$ almost surely.**

- If $\mathbb{E}[X_1^2] < \infty$: the random variables $X_i^2 \ge 0$ are i.i.d.
 with finite positive mean $\mu = \mathbb{E}[X_1^2] > 0$. By the Strong Law of L
arge Numbers,
$$\frac{S_n}{n} \to \mu > 0 \quad \text{a.s.},$$
so $S_n \to +\infty$ a.s.

- If $\mathbb{E}[X_1^2] = +\infty$: since $X_i^2 \ge 0$ are i.i.d. with infinite
 mean, the Strong Law for nonnegative random variables gives
$$\frac{S_n}{n} \to +\infty \quad \text{a.s.},$$
and again $S_n \to +\infty$ a.s.

**Step 3: The limit.**

Since $S_n \to +\infty$ a.s., for every fixed $r \ge 0$ the event $\{S_n \le r^2
\}$ occurs only finitely many times almost surely. Therefore

$$\mathbf{1}_{\{S_n \le r^2\}} \to 0 \quad \text{a.s.}$$

As these indicators are bounded by $1$, the Dominated Convergence Theorem yields

$$I_n(r) = \mathbb{P}(S_n \le r^2) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{S_n \le r^2
\}}\right] \to 0.$$

Hence, for every fixed $r \ge 0$,

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} I_n(r) = 0.}$$
