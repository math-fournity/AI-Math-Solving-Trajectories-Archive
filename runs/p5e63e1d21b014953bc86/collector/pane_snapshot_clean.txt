## Solution

We solve
$$u_t = k\,u_{xx} + e^{-t} + e^{-2t}\cos\!\tfrac{3\pi x}{L},\qquad u_x(0,t)=u_x(
L,t)=0,\qquad u(x,0)=f(x),$$
assuming $2\neq k(3\pi/L)^2$.

### 1. Eigenfunction expansion (cosine series)

The Neumann eigenfunctions on $[0,L]$ are $\varphi_0(x)=1$ and $\varphi_n(x)=\co
s(n\pi x/L)$ for $n\ge 1$, with eigenvalues $\lambda_n = k(n\pi/L)^2$ (and $\lam
bda_0=0$). They form a complete orthogonal system on $[0,L]$. Expand

$$u(x,t)=A_0(t)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_n(t)\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L}.$$

Expand the initial data correspondingly:
$$f(x)=a_0+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L},\qquad a_0=\frac1L\int_0^L
 f\,dx,\quad a_n=\frac2L\int_0^L f(x)\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L}\,dx.$$

The forcing term decomposes as
$$e^{-t}+e^{-2t}\cos\frac{3\pi x}{L}=e^{-t}\cdot\varphi_0(x)+e^{-2t}\cdot\varphi
_3(x),$$
i.e. it has **only** the $n=0$ and $n=3$ modes.

### 2. Term-by-term differentiation (justification)

For every $t_0>0$ and every $m\ge 0$, the coefficients $A_n(t)$ decay at least a
s fast as $e^{-\lambda_n t}$ (shown below), so on $[0,L]\times[t_0,\infty)$ the
series for $u$, $u_t$, and $u_{xx}$ (which pick up factors $\lambda_n$) converge
 **uniformly** by comparison with $\sum n^{2m}e^{-\lambda_n t_0}<\infty$. Unifor
m convergence permits term-by-term differentiation of $u_t$ and $u_{xx}$ for $t>
0$. The Neumann boundary conditions are satisfied automatically since $\varphi_n
'(0)=\varphi_n'(L)=0$ for all $n$.

### 3. ODEs for the coefficients

Substituting the series into the PDE and matching modes:

- **$n=0$:** $\;A_0'(t)=e^{-t}$, $\;A_0(0)=a_0$.
  $$A_0(t)=a_0+1-e^{-t}.$$

- **$n=3$:** $\;A_3'(t)+\lambda_3 A_3(t)=e^{-2t}$, $\;A_3(0)=a_3$, where $\lambd
a_3=k(3\pi/L)^2$.
  Integrating factor $e^{\lambda_3 t}$:
  $$A_3(t)=e^{-\lambda_3 t}\!\left[a_3+\int_0^t e^{(\lambda_3-2)s}\,ds\right].$$
  Since $2\neq\lambda_3$,
  $$\int_0^t e^{(\lambda_3-2)s}\,ds=\frac{e^{(\lambda_3-2)t}-1}{\lambda_3-2},$$
  hence
  $$A_3(t)=a_3\,e^{-\lambda_3 t}+\frac{e^{-2t}-e^{-\lambda_3 t}}{\lambda_3-2}.$$

- **$n\neq 0,3$:** $\;A_n'(t)+\lambda_n A_n(t)=0$, $\;A_n(0)=a_n$.
  $$A_n(t)=a_n\,e^{-\lambda_n t},\qquad \lambda_n=k\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{L}\right)
^2.$$

### 4. The solution

$$\boxed{\;u(x,t)=\bigl(a_0+1-e^{-t}\bigr)+\sum_{\substack{n=1\\ n\neq 3}}^{\inf
ty}a_n\,e^{-k(n\pi/L)^2 t}\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L}+\left[a_3\,e^{-k(3\pi/L)^2 t}+\fr
ac{e^{-2t}-e^{-k(3\pi/L)^2 t}}{k(3\pi/L)^2-2}\right]\cos\frac{3\pi x}{L}\;}$$

where
$$a_0=\frac1L\int_0^L f(x)\,dx,\qquad a_n=\frac2L\int_0^L f(x)\cos\frac{n\pi x}{
L}\,dx.$$

### 5. Verification

- **PDE:** By the uniform convergence justified in §2, term-by-term substitution
 gives, for each mode, exactly the ODE solved above, so the PDE holds for $t>0$.
- **Boundary conditions:** Every $\cos(n\pi x/L)$ has zero derivative at $x=0,L$
, so $u_x(0,t)=u_x(L,t)=0$.
- **Initial condition:** As $t\to 0^+$, $A_0(0)=a_0$, $A_n(0)=a_n$ for $n\neq 3$
, and $A_3(0)=a_3+0=a_3$ (the fraction vanishes at $t=0$). Thus $u(x,0)=a_0+\sum
_{n\ge1}a_n\cos(n\pi x/L)=f(x)$, using the (assumed) continuity/convergence of t
he cosine series for $f$.

All differentiations are justified by the exponential decay of the coefficients
for $t>0$.
