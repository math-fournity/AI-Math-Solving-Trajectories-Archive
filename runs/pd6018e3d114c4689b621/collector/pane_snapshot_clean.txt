## Proof

We evaluate
$$I = \int_S x\,dy\,dz + y\,dz\,dx + z\,dx\,dy,$$
where $S = \{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^3 : x^2+y^2+z^2=1\}$ is the unit sphere, taken
 with the standard outward orientation.

**Step 1: Identify the 2-form.** The integrand is the 2-form
$$\omega = x\,dy\wedge dz + y\,dz\wedge dx + z\,dx\wedge dy.$$
This is the standard 2-form associated to the vector field $\mathbf{F} = (x,\, y
,\, z)$, i.e., $\omega = \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$ in the language of flux in
tegrals. Integrating $\omega$ over $S$ with outward orientation computes the flu
x of $\mathbf{F}$ through the unit sphere.

**Step 2: Apply the Divergence Theorem (Gauss / generalized Stokes).** Let
$$V = \{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^3 : x^2+y^2+z^2 \le 1\}$$
be the closed unit ball, whose boundary is $S$. By the divergence theorem,
$$\int_S \omega = \int_S \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = \int_V (\nabla\cdot \math
bf{F})\,dV.$$

Equivalently, via the exterior derivative:
$$d\omega = d(x\,dy\wedge dz) + d(y\,dz\wedge dx) + d(z\,dx\wedge dy).$$
Computing each term:
- $d(x\,dy\wedge dz) = dx\wedge dy\wedge dz$,
- $d(y\,dz\wedge dx) = dy\wedge dz\wedge dx = dx\wedge dy\wedge dz$ (cyclic perm
utation, even, so sign is $+$),
- $d(z\,dx\wedge dy) = dz\wedge dx\wedge dy = dx\wedge dy\wedge dz$ (cyclic perm
utation, even, so sign is $+$).

Hence
$$d\omega = 3\,dx\wedge dy\wedge dz.$$

**Step 3: Integrate over the unit ball.** By Stokes' theorem,
$$I = \int_V d\omega = \int_V 3\,dx\,dy\,dz = 3\cdot \operatorname{Vol}(V).$$

The volume of the unit ball in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is
$$\operatorname{Vol}(V) = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi.$$

Therefore
$$I = 3 \cdot \tfrac{4}{3}\pi = 4\pi.$$

**Conclusion.**
$$\boxed{\int_S x\,dy\,dz + y\,dz\,dx + z\,dx\,dy = 4\pi.}$$
