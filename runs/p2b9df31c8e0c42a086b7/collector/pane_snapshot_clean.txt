## Reduction of \(K\) to \(K_0\)

**Goal.** Exhibit a total computable function \(f\) such that for every \(e\),
\[
e \in K \iff f(e) \in K_0.
\]

**Construction.** Define a partial computable function \(g\) of two variables by
\[
g(e, x) \;=\; \begin{cases}
0 & \text{if } \varphi_e(e)\downarrow,\\
\text{undefined} & \text{if } \varphi_e(e)\uparrow.
\end{cases}
\]
Concretely, on input \((e,x)\), the program for \(g\) ignores \(x\), simulates t
he computation \(\varphi_e(e)\), and—should that simulation halt—immediately hal
ts returning \(0\). If the simulation never halts, \(g(e,x)\) never halts either
. Hence
\[
g(e,x)\downarrow \iff \varphi_e(e)\downarrow \qquad\text{for every }x.
\tag{1}
\]

Since \(g\) is partial computable, by the **s-m-n theorem** there exists a total
 computable function \(f\) such that
\[
\varphi_{f(e)}(x) = g(e,x) \qquad\text{for all }e,x.
\tag{2}
\]

**Verification.** For every index \(e\):
\[
e \in K
\iff \varphi_e(e)\downarrow
\iff g(e,0)\downarrow \quad\text{by (1)}
\iff \varphi_{f(e)}(0)\downarrow \quad\text{by (2)}
\iff f(e) \in K_0.
\]

Thus \(f\) is a many-one reduction of \(K\) to \(K_0\), i.e. \(K \leq_m K_0\).
