## Solution

We evaluate $I = \int_0^{2\pi}\frac{\cos(2\theta)}{5+4\cos\theta}\,d\theta$ via
the residue theorem.

**Substitution.** Set $z = e^{i\theta}$, so $d\theta = \frac{dz}{iz}$, $\cos\the
ta = \frac{z+z^{-1}}{2}$, $\cos(2\theta)=\frac{z^2+z^{-2}}{2}=\frac{z^4+1}{2z^2}
$, and the unit circle $|z|=1$ is the contour. The denominator becomes
$$5+4\cos\theta = 5+2(z+z^{-1}) = \frac{2z^2+5z+2}{z} = \frac{(2z+1)(z+2)}{z}.$$

Hence
$$
I = \oint_{|z|=1} \frac{z^4+1}{2z^2}\cdot\frac{z}{(2z+1)(z+2)}\cdot\frac{dz}{iz}
   = \oint_{|z|=1} \frac{z^4+1}{2i\,z^2(2z+1)(z+2)}\,dz.
$$

**Poles inside $|z|=1$.** The integrand $f(z)=\frac{z^4+1}{2i\,z^2(2z+1)(z+2)}$
has:
- $z=0$: pole of order $2$ (inside),
- $z=-\tfrac12$: simple pole from $2z+1=0$ (inside, since $|-1/2|<1$),
- $z=-2$: simple pole (outside, since $|-2|>1$).

**Residue at $z=-\tfrac12$.** Writing $f=N/D$ with $N=z^4+1$, $D=2i\,z^2(2z+1)(z
+2)$:
$$D'(z)=2i\bigl[2z(2z+1)(z+2)+2z^2(z+2)+z^2(2z+1)\bigr].$$
At $z=-\tfrac12$: the first and third terms vanish (factor $2z+1=0$), and $2z^2(
z+2)=2\cdot\tfrac14\cdot\tfrac32=\tfrac34$. Thus $D'(-\tfrac12)=\tfrac{3i}{2}$,
and $N(-\tfrac12)=\tfrac{1}{16}+1=\tfrac{17}{16}$, giving
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=-1/2} f = \frac{17/16}{3i/2} = \frac{17}{24i} = -\frac{1
7i}{24}.$$

**Residue at $z=0$ (order 2).** Write $f(z)=\frac{g(z)}{z^2}$ with $g(z)=\frac{z
^4+1}{2i(2z+1)(z+2)}$. The residue is $g'(0)$. Let $h(z)=\frac{1}{2i(2z+1)(z+2)}
=\frac{1}{2i}\,p(z)^{-1}$ with $p(z)=(2z+1)(z+2)=2z^2+5z+2$, so $p(0)=2$, $p'(0)
=5$. Then
$$h'(0)=\frac{1}{2i}\cdot\frac{-p'(0)}{p(0)^2}=\frac{1}{2i}\cdot\frac{-5}{4}=-\f
rac{5}{8i}=\frac{5i}{8}.$$
Since $g(z)=(z^4+1)h(z)$, $g'(0)=4\cdot0^3\cdot h(0)+(0^4+1)h'(0)=h'(0)=\frac{5i
}{8}$.

**Sum of residues.**
$$\sum \operatorname{Res} = \frac{5i}{8}-\frac{17i}{24} = \frac{15i-17i}{24} = -
\frac{i}{12}.$$

**Apply the residue theorem.**
$$I = 2\pi i\cdot\Bigl(-\frac{i}{12}\Bigr) = \frac{2\pi}{12} = \frac{\pi}{6}.$$

**Verification (Fourier expansion).** For $a>|b|$, $\frac{1}{a+b\cos\theta}=\fra
c{1}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}\Bigl[1+2\sum_{n\ge1}\Bigl(\frac{-b}{a+\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}\Bigr)
^n\cos n\theta\Bigr]$. With $a=5,b=4$: $\sqrt{a^2-b^2}=3$, ratio $=-\tfrac12$. B
y orthogonality, only the $n=2$ term contributes:
$$I = \frac{1}{3}\cdot 2\cdot\Bigl(-\frac12\Bigr)^2\cdot\pi = \frac{1}{3}\cdot 2
\cdot\frac14\cdot\pi = \frac{\pi}{6},$$
confirming the result.

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{\cos(2\theta)}{5+4\cos\theta}\,d\theta
= \frac{\pi}{6}}$$
