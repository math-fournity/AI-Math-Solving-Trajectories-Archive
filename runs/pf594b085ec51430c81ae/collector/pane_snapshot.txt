## Proof

**Yes, such a function exists.**

We construct a Borel set $E\subset[0,1]$ such that for every nonempty open inter
val $I\subset[0,1]$, both $m(E\cap I)>0$ and $m(E^c\cap I)>0$. Then $f=\mathbf{1
}_E$ is the desired function.

### Construction of $E$

al endpoints in $[0,1]$. We build sequences $\{A_n\},\{B_n\}$ of closed nowhere-
dense sets inductively.

**Inductive step $n$:** Assume $A_1,B_1,\dots,A_{n-1},B_{n-1}$ have been chosen,

 and $m(A_k),m(B_k)>0$.

Set $S_{n-1}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{n-1}(A_k\cup B_k)$. Since $S_{n-1}$ is a **finite**
union of closed nowhere-dense sets, it is closed and nowhere dense (a finite uni
on of closed sets with empty interior has empty interior — this follows by a sim
ple induction: if a nonempty open $U\subset F_1\cup\cdots\cup F_n$ with each $\o
peratorname{int}(F_i)=\varnothing$, then $U\cap F_1^c$ is a nonempty open set co

of $F_n$, contradiction).

Since $S_{n-1}$ is nowhere dense, it cannot contain the open interval $I_n$, so
$I_n\setminus S_{n-1}$ is a **nonempty open set**. Let $J$ be a connected compon

**Define** $E=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_n$, an $F_\sigma$ set, hence Borel measura

### Verification of the interval property

For any nonempty open interval $I\subset[0,1]$, there exists a rational interval

- $m(E\cap I)\ge m(E\cap I_n)\ge m(A_n)>0$.
- $m(E^c\cap I)\ge m(E^c\cap I_n)\ge m(B_n)>0$, since $B_n\subset I_n$ and $B_n\
cap E=\varnothing$ (because $B_n$ is disjoint from every $A_k$).

### Proof that $f=\mathbf{1}_E$ has the required property

By continuity, there exists $\delta>0$ such that $|g(x)-c|<\tfrac14$ for all $x\

For $x\in U_\delta\setminus N$, we have $g(x)=f(x)\in\{0,1\}$ and $|f(x)-c|<\tfr
