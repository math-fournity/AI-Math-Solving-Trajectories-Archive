## Solution

We compute the local homology group $H_n(M, M-x)$ for a closed $n$-manifold $M$
and a point $x \in M$.

**Step 1: Excision.** Let $U$ be any open neighborhood of $x$ in $M$ with $\over
line{U} \subset V$ for some open $V \subset M$. Excision of $M - U$ gives
$$H_k(M, M-x) \cong H_k(U, U-x)$$
for every $k$. So the local homology at $x$ depends only on an arbitrarily small
 neighborhood of $x$.

**Step 2: Choose a coordinate ball.** Since $M$ is an $n$-manifold, $x$ has an o
pen neighborhood $U$ homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^n$, with $x$ corresponding to t
he origin. Under this homeomorphism,
$$H_k(M, M-x) \cong H_k(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R}^n - \{0\}).$$

**Step 3: Use the long exact sequence of the pair.** The pair $(\mathbb{R}^n, \m
athbb{R}^n - \{0\})$ yields the long exact sequence
$$\cdots \to H_k(\mathbb{R}^n) \to H_k(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R}^n - \{0\}) \to H
_{k-1}(\mathbb{R}^n - \{0\}) \to H_{k-1}(\mathbb{R}^n) \to \cdots$$

Since $\mathbb{R}^n$ is contractible, $H_k(\mathbb{R}^n) = 0$ for $k > 0$ and $H
_0(\mathbb{R}^n) \cong \mathbb{Z}$. Also $\mathbb{R}^n - \{0\}$ deformation retr
acts onto $S^{n-1}$, so $H_{k-1}(\mathbb{R}^n - \{0\}) \cong H_{k-1}(S^{n-1})$.

**Step 4: Compute.** For $k \geq 2$, the exact sequence reduces (using $H_k(\mat
hbb{R}^n) = H_{k-1}(\mathbb{R}^n) = 0$) to an isomorphism
$$H_k(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R}^n - \{0\}) \cong H_{k-1}(S^{n-1}).$$

The homology of $S^{n-1}$ is:
$$H_{n-1}(S^{n-1}) \cong \mathbb{Z}, \qquad H_j(S^{n-1}) = 0 \text{ for } j \neq
 0, n-1.$$

Therefore, for $k = n$ (with $n \geq 2$):
$$H_n(M, M-x) \cong H_{n-1}(S^{n-1}) \cong \mathbb{Z}.$$

For $k \neq n$ with $k \geq 2$: $H_k(M, M-x) = 0$.

**Step 5: The case $n = 1$.** For $n=1$, $S^{0}$ consists of two points, $H_0(S^
0) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$. The exact sequence at $k=1$:
$$0 = H_1(\mathbb{R}) \to H_1(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{R}-\{0\}) \to H_0(\mathbb{R}-\
{0\}) \to H_0(\mathbb{R}) \to H_0(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{R}-\{0\}) \to 0.$$
Here $H_0(\mathbb{R}-\{0\}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$ (two components) and $H_0(\mathb
b{R}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, with the map $\mathbb{Z}^2 \to \mathbb{Z}$ sending both
 generators to $1$ (surjective, kernel $\cong \mathbb{Z}$). Hence
$$H_1(M, M-x) \cong \ker(\mathbb{Z}^2 \to \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}.$$

**Step 6: The case $n = 0$.** A closed $0$-manifold is a finite set of points, a
nd $M - x$ is the complement of one point. Then $H_0(M, M-x) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ (
generated by the component containing $x$), consistent with the general formula.

## Conclusion

For a closed $n$-dimensional manifold $M$ and any point $x \in M$:

$$\boxed{H_n(M, M - x) \cong \mathbb{Z}.}$$

More generally, the full local homology is
$$H_k(M, M-x) \cong \begin{cases} \mathbb{Z} & k = n, \\ 0 & k \neq n. \end{case
s}$$

The "closed" hypothesis (compact, without boundary) is not actually needed for t
his local computation — the result holds for any $n$-manifold, since excision re
duces the problem to an arbitrarily small coordinate ball around $x$, which is p
urely local.
