## Proof

Let
$$f_n(x)=\frac{n\sin(x/n)}{x(1+x^{2})},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},$$
with the value at $x=0$ defined by continuity as $f_n(0)=\frac{1}{1+0}=1$ (since
 $\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{n\sin(x/n)}{x}=1$). The integral is taken over $\mathbb{R}$
, consistent with the hint $\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{dx}{1+x^{2}}=\pi$.

**Step 1. Pointwise limit.** For every fixed $x\in\mathbb{R}$, using $\sin t = t
 + o(t)$ as $t\to 0$ with $t=x/n$,
$$\frac{n\sin(x/n)}{x}=\frac{n}{x}\cdot\sin\!\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)=\frac{n}{x
}\cdot\left(\frac{x}{n}+o\!\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)\right)\xrightarrow[n\to\inft
y]{}1.$$
Hence
$$f_n(x)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\frac{1}{1+x^{2}}=:f(x).$$

**Step 2. Dominating function.** Using the elementary bound $|\sin u|\le |u|$ fo
r all $u\in\mathbb{R}$,
$$\left|\frac{n\sin(x/n)}{x}\right|\le \frac{n\,|x/n|}{|x|}=1\qquad(x\neq 0),$$
and the bound also holds at $x=0$ by continuity. Therefore
$$|f_n(x)|=\left|\frac{n\sin(x/n)}{x}\right|\cdot\frac{1}{1+x^{2}}\le \frac{1}{1
+x^{2}}=g(x).$$
The function $g(x)=\frac{1}{1+x^{2}}$ is integrable over $\mathbb{R}$, and by th
e hint
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} g(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{dx}{1+x^{2}}=\pi<\infty.$$

**Step 3. Apply the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem.** We have:
- $f_n\to f$ pointwise a.e. on $\mathbb{R}$,
- $|f_n|\le g$ a.e. with $g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$.

By LDCT,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{n\sin(x/n)}{x(1+x^{2})}\,dx=\int_{\mat
hbb{R}}\lim_{n\to\infty}f_n(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{dx}{1+x^{2}}=\pi.$$
