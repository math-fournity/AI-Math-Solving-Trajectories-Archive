## Proof

**Indexing note.** With $F_1=0, F_2=1, F_n=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}$, the sequence is $0,
1,1,2,3,5,8,\dots$, i.e. $F_n = \mathrm{fib}(n-1)$ where $\mathrm{fib}(0)=0,\mat
hrm{fib}(1)=1$ is the standard Fibonacci.

**Step 1. Closed form for the partial sum.** The standard identity gives $\sum_{
i=1}^{n} F_i = F_{n+2}-1$. Quick check: $n=5$: $0+1+1+2+3=7=F_7-1=8-1$. ✓

So the series becomes
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{F_{n+2}-1}{3^n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{F_{n+2}}
{3^n} - \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3^n}.$$

**Step 2. The geometric part.**
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3^n} = \frac{1/3}{1-1/3} = \frac12.$$

**Step 3. The Fibonacci part.** Since $F_{n+2}=\mathrm{fib}(n+1)$, we need $\sum
_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{fib}(n+1)\,x^n$ at $x=\tfrac13$.

Using the generating function $\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{fib}(m)\,x^m = \dfrac{
x}{1-x-x^2}$:

$$\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\mathrm{fib}(m)\,x^m = \frac{x}{1-x-x^2} - \mathrm{fib}(0)
- \mathrm{fib}(1)x = \frac{x}{1-x-x^2}-x.$$

With $m=n+1$ (so $x^m = x^{n+1}=x\cdot x^n$):
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{fib}(n+1)\,x^n = \frac1x\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\mathrm{
fib}(m)\,x^m = \frac1x\!\left(\frac{x}{1-x-x^2}-x\right)=\frac{1}{1-x-x^2}-1.$$

At $x=\tfrac13$:
$$\frac{1}{1-\frac13-\frac19}-1 = \frac{1}{\frac59}-1 = \frac95-1 = \frac45.$$

**Step 4. Combine.**
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}F_i}{3^n} = \frac45 - \frac12 = \frac{8
-5}{10} = \frac{3}{10}.$$

The fraction $\frac{3}{10}$ is irreducible ($\gcd(3,10)=1$), so $a=3,\ b=10$.

$$a+b = 3+10 = \boxed{13}.$$
