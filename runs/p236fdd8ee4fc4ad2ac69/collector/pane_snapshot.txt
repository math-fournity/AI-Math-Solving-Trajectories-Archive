### Preliminaries

We use the following standard facts from Pontryagin duality and topological grou

**(F1) Monothetic criterion.** A compact abelian group $G$ is *monothetic* (admi

*Proof of (F1):* If $g \in G$ topologically generates $G$, the evaluation map $\
mathrm{ev}_g : \widehat{G} \to \mathbb{T}$, $\chi \mapsto \chi(g)$, is injective
: if $\chi(g) = 1$, then $\chi$ vanishes on the dense subgroup $\langle g \rangl
e$, hence $\chi = 1$ by continuity. Conversely, any injective homomorphism $\phi

widehat{\widehat{G}}$, so elements of $G$ are homomorphisms $\widehat{G} \to \ma
thbb{T}$), realized as $\phi = \mathrm{ev}_g$ for some $g \in G$. Injectivity of
 $\phi$ means $\chi(g) = 1 \Rightarrow \chi = 1$, which by duality is equivalent
 to $\overline{\langle g \rangle} = G$. $\square$

**(F2) First countability criterion.** For a compact abelian group $G$, the foll
owing are equivalent: (i) $G$ is first countable, (ii) $G$ is metrizable, (iii)
$G$ is second countable, (iv) $\widehat{G}$ is countable.

*Proof of (F2):* (i)$\Leftrightarrow$(ii) is the Birkhoff–Kakutani theorem (a Ha
usdorff topological group is metrizable iff it is first countable). (ii)$\Leftri
ghtarrow$(iii) holds for compact Hausdorff spaces. (iii)$\Leftrightarrow$(iv): t
he weight of a compact abelian group $G$ equals $\max(\aleph_0, |\widehat{G}|)$,

s $\chi \in \widehat{G}$, and a base is generated by $\chi^{-1}(U)$ for $\chi \i
n \widehat{G}$ and $U$ ranging over a countable base of $\mathbb{T}$. Thus $G$ i
s second countable iff $|\widehat{G}| \leq \aleph_0$. $\square$

### The Counterexample: $G = b\mathbb{Z}$

$$b\mathbb{Z} := \widehat{\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{disc}}},$$
the Pontryagin dual of $\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{disc}}$. This is the **Bohr compacti

**$b\mathbb{Z}$ is a compact Hausdorff abelian group.** Since $\mathbb{T}_{\math
rm{disc}}$ is a discrete abelian group, its Pontryagin dual $b\mathbb{Z}$ is a c

**$\mathbb{Z}$ embeds as a dense subgroup of $b\mathbb{Z}$.** There is a natural

$$\iota : \mathbb{Z} \longrightarrow b\mathbb{Z}, \qquad n \longmapsto \bigl(t \

To see that $\iota(\mathbb{Z})$ is dense in $b\mathbb{Z}$: by Pontryagin duality

The image $\iota(\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ (since $\iota$ is injective), so

$$\widehat{b\mathbb{Z}} \cong \mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{disc}},$$
