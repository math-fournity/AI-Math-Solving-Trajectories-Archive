## Counterexample

Define:
$$u_1(x,t) = \cos\!\big((x-t)^2\big), \qquad u_2(x,t) = \sin\!\big((x-t)^2\big).
$$

### Verification of all hypotheses

**Wave equation.** Both $u_1$ and $u_2$ are functions of $s = x-t$ alone. Any $C
^2$ function $\varphi(x-t)$ satisfies $\partial_t^2 \varphi = \varphi''(s) = \pa
rtial_x^2 \varphi$ (since $\partial_t s = -1$ and $\partial_x s = 1$, giving $\p
artial_{tt}\varphi = \varphi''$ and $\partial_{xx}\varphi = \varphi''$). Thus bo
th solve $\partial_t^2 u_i = \partial_x^2 u_i$.

**Spatial condition.** We have
$$u_1^2 + u_2^2 = \cos^2\!\big((x-t)^2\big) + \sin^2\!\big((x-t)^2\big) = 1,$$
so $\partial_x(u_1^2 + u_2^2) = 0$ identically.

**Boundedness.** $|u_1| \le 1$ and $|u_2| \le 1$ everywhere, so both are globall
y bounded.

### The counterexample is not of the proposed form

The proposed form (taking $z = x$) gives, at $t = 0$:
$$u_1(x,0) = C_1 \sin(kx)\sin(\phi_1) + C_2 \cos(kx)\sin(\phi_2) = A\sin(kx) + B
\cos(kx),$$
where $A = C_1\sin\phi_1$ and $B = C_2\sin\phi_2$ are constants. This is:

- **periodic** in $x$ with period $2\pi/|k|$ when $k \neq 0$, or
- **constant** when $k = 0$ (in which case $u_1 \equiv 0$).

However, our counterexample gives $u_1(x,0) = \cos(x^2)$, which is **neither per
iodic nor constant**:

- **Not constant:** $\cos(0^2) = 1 \neq \cos(1^2) \approx 0.540$.
- **Not periodic:** Suppose $\cos(x^2)$ had period $T > 0$. Then $\cos\!\big((x+
T)^2\big) = \cos(x^2)$ for all $x$, requiring $(x+T)^2 - x^2 = 2xT + T^2$ to be
an integer multiple of $2\pi$ for every $x$. But $2xT + T^2$ is a non-constant l
inear function of $x$, so it cannot be integer-valued (let alone a fixed multipl
e of $2\pi$) for all real $x$. Contradiction.

Therefore $\cos(x^2)$ cannot be expressed as $A\sin(kx) + B\cos(kx)$ for any con
stants $A, B, k$, and hence $(u_1, u_2)$ is **not** of the proposed form.

## Conclusion

The pair $\big(\cos((x-t)^2),\; \sin((x-t)^2)\big)$ is a pair of globally bounde

$), showing that the condition permits a vast family of solutions beyond the pro
posed form.

The answer is **No**.
