## Setting Up the Model

Let each relay be represented by $x_{ij} \in \{0,1\}$ where $0$ = Mode 1 (draw 1
) and $1$ = Mode 2 (draw 2). The draw of relay $(i,j)$ is $1 + x_{ij}$.

**Total draw constraint:** $\sum_{i,j}(1+x_{ij}) = 16 + \sum x_{ij} = 22$, so $\
sum x_{ij} = 6$ (exactly 6 relays in Mode 2).

**Parity constraints:** The row draw sum is $4 + \sum_j x_{ij}$, which is even i
ff $\sum_j x_{ij}$ is even. Similarly for columns and diagonals (each has 4 cell
s). So we need:
- Every row sum of $x$ is even.
- Every column sum of $x$ is even.
- Both main diagonal sums of $x$ are even.

## Determining Row and Column Sum Patterns

All four row sums are even and sum to 6. Possible patterns (values in $\{0,2,4\}
$):
- $(4,2,0,0)$ and permutations
- $(2,2,2,0)$ and permutations

**Eliminating $(4,2,0,0)$:** If one row is all 1s, every column receives at leas
t 1 from that row. The row with 2 ones adds 1 to two columns, giving column sums
 $(2,2,1,1)$ — but 1 is odd. **Impossible.**

So row sums must be a permutation of $(2,2,2,0)$, and by the same argument, colu
mn sums must be a permutation of $(2,2,2,0)$.

## Diagonal Constraint Analysis

I claim that for any matrix with all row and column sums even, $d_1 \equiv d_2 \
pmod{2}$ automatically.

**Proof:** The span of row and column indicator vectors over $\mathbb{F}_2$ cons
ists of all matrices of the form $a_i + b_j$. One can verify that $d_1 + d_2$ (1
s on both diagonals) equals such a matrix: taking $a_1=a_4=0, a_2=a_3=1, b_1=b_4
=1, b_2=b_3=0$ gives $a_i+b_j=1$ exactly on the 8 diagonal cells. Therefore $d_1
 + d_2 \equiv 0$, so $d_1 \equiv d_2$. It suffices to enforce $d_1$ even.

## Counting Valid Configurations

Choose the zero row $a$ (4 choices) and zero column $b$ (4 choices). The remaini

J - P$ where $P$ is a perfect matching (bijection from row-indices to column-ind

**Case A: $a = b$ (4 choices).** The 3 diagonal cells $(i,i)$, $i \neq a$, lie i

- Identity (3 fixed points): sum $= 0$ (even) — 1 permutation
- Transpositions (1 fixed point): sum $= 2$ (even) — 3 permutations
- 3-cycles (0 fixed points): sum $= 3$ (odd) — 2 permutations

**4 out of 6** have even $d_1$. Contribution: $4 \times 4 = 16$.

**Case B: $a \neq b$ (12 choices).** The cells $(a,a)=0$ (zero row) and $(b,b)=0

- **Both fixed:** $\sigma(i)=i, \sigma(j)=j, \sigma(b)=a$ — 1 bijection.

  3 bijections.
