## Proof

**Answer: Yes, such a sequence exists.**

Since $f$ is continuous on the compact interval $[0,1]$ and has no zeros, $f$ ha
s constant sign on $[0,1]$ and $|f|$ attains a positive minimum. Set
$$m := \min_{x\in[0,1]} |f(x)| > 0.$$

**Case 1: $f > 0$ on $[0,1]$.** Then $f \ge m > 0$. Define $g := \sqrt{f}$, whic
h is continuous on $[0,1]$ with $g \ge \sqrt{m} > 0$. By the Weierstrass approxi
mation theorem, there is a sequence of polynomials $(r_n)$ converging uniformly
to $g$ on $[0,1]$. Choose $N$ so that for all $n \ge N$,
$$\|r_n - g\|_{\infty} < \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{m}.$$
Then for every $x \in [0,1]$ and every $n \ge N$,
$$r_n(x) > g(x) - \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{m} \ge \sqrt{m} - \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{m} = \tf
rac{1}{2}\sqrt{m} > 0.$$
Now set $p_n := r_{n+N}^2$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$. Each $p_n$ is a polynomial,
 and since $r_{n+N}(x) > 0$ for all $x \in [0,1]$, we have $p_n(x) > 0$ on $[0,1
]$, so $p_n$ has **no zeros** on $[0,1]$. Moreover,
$$\|p_n - f\|_{\infty} = \|r_{n+N}^2 - g^2\|_{\infty} \le \bigl(\|r_{n+N}\|_{\in
fty} + \|g\|_{\infty}\bigr)\,\|r_{n+N} - g\|_{\infty} \to 0,$$
so $p_n \to f$ uniformly.

**Case 2: $f < 0$ on $[0,1]$.** Apply Case 1 to $-f$ to obtain polynomials $q_n
> 0$ on $[0,1]$ with $q_n \to -f$ uniformly. Set $p_n := -q_n$. Then each $p_n <
 0$ on $[0,1]$, hence has no zeros, and $p_n \to f$ uniformly.

In both cases we obtain a sequence of polynomials, none of which vanish on $[0,1
]$, converging uniformly to $f$. $\blacksquare$
