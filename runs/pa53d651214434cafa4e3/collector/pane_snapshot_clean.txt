## Proof

We compute $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}$.

**Claim:** $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q} = 0$.

Recall that $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ is a torsion $\mathbb{Z}$-module: every elem
ent $[a] \in \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ (with $a \in \mathbb{Q}$) has finite order.
Indeed, writing $a = p/q \in \mathbb{Q}$, we have $q \cdot a = p \in \mathbb{Z}$
, so $q \cdot [a] = [p] = 0$ in $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$.

Now consider an arbitrary simple tensor $[a] \otimes b \in \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
 \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}$, where $[a] \in \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ and $b
\in \mathbb{Q}$.

Let $n$ be the order of $[a]$, so $n \cdot [a] = 0$ in $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ (
equivalently, $n \cdot a \in \mathbb{Z}$). Since $\mathbb{Q}$ is divisible, $b/n
 \in \mathbb{Q}$ exists. Using the $\mathbb{Z}$-bilinearity of the tensor produc
t:

$$[a] \otimes b = [a] \otimes \left(n \cdot \frac{b}{n}\right) = (n \cdot [a]) \
otimes \frac{b}{n} = 0 \otimes \frac{b}{n} = 0.$$

Thus every simple tensor in $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{
Q}$ is zero. Since the tensor product is generated (as an abelian group) by simp
le tensors, we conclude:

$$\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q} = 0.$$

**Remark.** This is a special case of the general fact that for any torsion $\ma
thbb{Z}$-module $T$ and any divisible, torsion-free $\mathbb{Z}$-module $D$ (in
particular $D = \mathbb{Q}$), one has $T \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} D = 0$. The argume
nt is identical: the torsion relation $n \cdot t = 0$ combined with divisibility
 of $D$ (which lets us divide $d$ by $n$) forces every simple tensor $t \otimes
d$ to vanish.
