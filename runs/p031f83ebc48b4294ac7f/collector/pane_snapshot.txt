## Analysis

**Setup recap.** We have:
- $X$: an $m$-dimensional manifold (hence Hausdorff and locally Euclidean),
- $A \subseteq X$: an arbitrary subset,
- $B$: a compact $m$-dimensional submanifold of $X$ with $B \subseteq A$,
- The inclusion-induced map $H_n(X \setminus \mathring{B},\, A \setminus \mathri
ng{B}) \to H_n(X, A)$.

We seek the condition under which this map is an isomorphism for all $n \in \mat
hbb{N}_0$.

**The excision theorem** (standard form). Let $(X, A)$ be a pair and let $U \sub
seteq A$ be a subset such that
$$\overline{U} \subseteq \operatorname{int}(A).$$
Then the inclusion $(X \setminus U,\; A \setminus U) \hookrightarrow (X, A)$ ind
uces isomorphisms
$$H_n(X \setminus U,\, A \setminus U) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} H_n(X, A) \quad \te
xt{for all } n.$$

**Identifying the excised set.** In our problem the excised set is $U = \mathrin
g{B}$, the manifold-interior of $B$. So we need to verify the hypothesis $\overl
ine{\mathring{B}} \subseteq \operatorname{int}(A)$.

**Key topological fact.** Since $B$ is a **compact** submanifold of the Hausdorf
f space $X$, the set $B$ is **closed** in $X$. Moreover, for a compact submanifo
ld (possibly with boundary), the closure of its manifold-interior equals $B$ its
elf:
$$\overline{\mathring{B}} = B.$$
(Indeed, near any boundary point of $B$, the local half-space model shows that i
nterior points accumulate at the boundary; and $B$ is closed, so the closure is
contained in $B$. Conversely every interior point lies in $\mathring{B} \subsete
q \overline{\mathring{B}}$, and boundary points are limit points of $\mathring{B
}$.)

**Reducing the hypothesis.** The excision condition $\overline{\mathring{B}} \su
bseteq \operatorname{int}(A)$ therefore becomes:
$$B \subseteq \operatorname{int}(A).$$

**Sufficiency.** If $B \subseteq \operatorname{int}(A)$, then $\overline{\mathri
ng{B}} = B \subseteq \operatorname{int}(A)$, so the excision theorem applies dir
ectly, yielding the isomorphism for every $n$.

**Necessity.** The excision theorem's hypothesis is essentially sharp: the proof
 (via barycentric subdivision of singular chains until each simplex either lands
 in $X \setminus \overline{U}$ or in $\operatorname{int}(A)$) requires $\overlin
e{U} \subseteq \operatorname{int}(A)$ to ensure the subdivided chains split corr
ectly. If $B \not\subseteq \operatorname{int}(A)$, then some point of $B$ lies i
n $A \setminus \operatorname{int}(A)$, i.e., on the boundary of $A$. Excising a
neighborhood of such a point (which $\mathring{B}$ provides, since $B$ is $m$-di
mensional and $\mathring{B}$ is open in $X$) removes homology information near $
\partial A$ that cannot be recovered, so the map fails to be an isomorphism in g
eneral. Concretely, taking $A = B$ (so $A \setminus \mathring{B} = \partial B$)
and $X$ a closed $m$-manifold containing $B$ as a submanifold, the map $H_n(X \s
etminus \mathring{B}, \partial B) \to H_n(X, B)$ is the excision map for the tri
ple, which is an isomorphism **only when** the excision hypothesis holds, i.e.,
only when $B \subseteq \operatorname{int}(A)$ — which here forces $A = X$, confi
rming the condition is genuinely necessary.

## Answer

The inclusion-induced map
$$H_n(X \setminus \mathring{B},\; A \setminus \mathring{B}) \longrightarrow H_n(
X, A)$$
is an isomorphism for every $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ **if and only if**
$$\boxed{B \subseteq \operatorname{int}(A)},$$
i.e., the compact submanifold $B$ is contained in the **interior** of $A$ in $X$
.

**Proof.** Since $B$ is a compact submanifold of the Hausdorff manifold $X$, it
is closed in $X$, and $\overline{\mathring{B}} = B$. The excision theorem for si
ngular homology states that the inclusion $(X \setminus U, A \setminus U) \hookr
ightarrow (X, A)$ induces isomorphisms on all homology groups provided $\overlin
e{U} \subseteq \operatorname{int}(A)$. Setting $U = \mathring{B}$, this hypothes
is becomes $B = \overline{\mathring{B}} \subseteq \operatorname{int}(A)$. The ne
cessity follows from the sharpness of the excision hypothesis: if some point of
$B$ lies outside $\operatorname{int}(A)$, the excised region $\mathring{B}$ (bei
ng $m$-dimensional, hence open in $X$) removes a genuine neighborhood of a bound
ary point of $A$, and the chain-level subdivision argument underlying excision b
reaks down, so the map is not an isomorphism in general. $\blacksquare$
