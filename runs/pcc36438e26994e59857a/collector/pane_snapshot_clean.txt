## Proof

We determine the value of $0^0$ in the sense of **ordinal exponentiation**.

### Definition of ordinal exponentiation

Ordinal exponentiation $\alpha^\beta$ is defined by transfinite recursion on the
 exponent $\beta$:

1. **Base case:** $\alpha^0 = 1$ for every ordinal $\alpha$.
2. **Successor case:** $\alpha^{\beta+1} = \alpha^\beta \cdot \alpha$.
3. **Limit case:** $\alpha^\lambda = \sup\{\alpha^\beta : \beta < \lambda\}$ for
 every limit ordinal $\lambda$.

This is a well-defined recursion on $\beta$; for each fixed $\alpha$ the three c
lauses uniquely determine $\alpha^\beta$ for all ordinals $\beta$.

### Applying the base case

Setting $\alpha = 0$ and $\beta = 0$, we use the base case (clause 1), since the
 exponent is $0$:

$$0^0 = 1.$$

The base case applies to **every** ordinal $\alpha$ with no exclusion; in partic
ular it applies to $\alpha = 0$. There is no special-casing that would leave $0^
0$ undefined — unlike the situation for cardinal exponentiation or real-valued e
xponentiation where $0^0$ may be left indeterminate, ordinal exponentiation is d
efined by transfinite recursion and the recursion *starts* at $\beta = 0$ with t
he uniform value $1$.

### Consistency check

We verify that this value is consistent with the successor clause. Taking $\beta
 = 0$ in the successor clause:

$$\alpha^{0+1} = \alpha^0 \cdot \alpha = 1 \cdot \alpha = \alpha.$$

For $\alpha = 0$ this gives $0^1 = 1 \cdot 0 = 0$, which is correct. For $\alpha
 = 0$ and the next step, $0^2 = 0^1 \cdot 0 = 0 \cdot 0 = 0$, and by induction $
0^\beta = 0$ for all $\beta \geq 1$. The single exception is $\beta = 0$, where
the base clause forces $0^0 = 1$. This is fully consistent: the recursion is wel
l-founded and yields a unique total function $\beta \mapsto 0^\beta$ on the ordi
nals, with value $1$ at $\beta = 0$ and value $0$ at every $\beta \geq 1$.

### Conclusion

In ordinal arithmetic, $0^0 = 1$.
