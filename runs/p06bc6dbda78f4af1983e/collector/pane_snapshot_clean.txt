## Evolute of the Parabola $y^2 = 12x$

The **evolute** of a curve is the locus of its centers of curvature. I compute t
he center of curvature at a generic point and then eliminate the parameter.

### Setup

Write $y^2 = 12x = 4ax$ with $4a = 12$, so $a = 3$. Use the standard parametriza
tion

$$x = at^2 = 3t^2, \qquad y = 2at = 6t.$$

### Center of curvature formula

For a parametric curve $(x(t), y(t))$, the center of curvature $(\xi, \eta)$ is

$$\xi = x - \frac{\dot y(\dot x^2 + \dot y^2)}{\dot x\ddot y - \dot y\ddot x}, \
qquad \eta = y + \frac{\dot x(\dot x^2 + \dot y^2)}{\dot x\ddot y - \dot y\ddot
x}.$$

### Compute the derivatives

$$\dot x = 6t,\quad \dot y = 6,\quad \ddot x = 6,\quad \ddot y = 0.$$

Hence

$$\dot x^2 + \dot y^2 = 36t^2 + 36 = 36(t^2+1),$$
$$\dot x\ddot y - \dot y\ddot x = 6t\cdot 0 - 6\cdot 6 = -36.$$

### Substitute

$$\xi = 3t^2 - \frac{6\cdot 36(t^2+1)}{-36} = 3t^2 + 6(t^2+1) = 9t^2 + 6,$$

$$\eta = 6t + \frac{6t\cdot 36(t^2+1)}{-36} = 6t - 6t(t^2+1) = -6t^3.$$

So the evolute is parametrized by

$$\xi = 9t^2 + 6, \qquad \eta = -6t^3.$$

### Eliminate $t$

From $\xi = 9t^2 + 6$:

$$t^2 = \frac{\xi - 6}{9}.$$

From $\eta = -6t^3 = -6t\cdot t^2$:

$$\eta = -6t\cdot\frac{\xi-6}{9} = -\frac{2}{3}t(\xi-6).$$

Squaring:

$$\eta^2 = \frac{4}{9}t^2(\xi-6)^2 = \frac{4}{9}\cdot\frac{\xi-6}{9}\cdot(\xi-6)
^2 = \frac{4(\xi-6)^3}{81}.$$

Therefore

$$81\,\eta^2 = 4(\xi-6)^3.$$

### Result

Replacing $(\xi, \eta)$ by $(x, y)$, the equation of the evolute of $y^2 = 12x$
is

$$\boxed{81\,y^2 = 4(x-6)^3}.$$

This is a **semicubical parabola** (Neil's parabola) with a cusp at the point $(
6, 0)$, which is the focus of the original parabola $y^2 = 12x$ (since the focus
 of $y^2 = 4ax$ is $(a, 0) = (3,0)$... here the cusp is at $(2a, 0) = (6, 0)$, t
he center of curvature at the vertex).
