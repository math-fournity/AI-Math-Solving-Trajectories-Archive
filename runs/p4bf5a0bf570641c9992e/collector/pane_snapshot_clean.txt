## Proof

We prove that **every** irreducible polynomial of degree 5 in $F_2[x]$ has disti
nct roots in any algebraic closure of $F_2$. In fact, we establish the stronger
statement that every irreducible polynomial over the finite field $F_2$ is separ
able, and then note that degree 5 is a special (and particularly clean) case.

### Key fact: separability criterion

A polynomial $f \in F[x]$ (over any field $F$) has distinct roots in an algebrai
c closure — i.e., is *separable* — if and only if $\gcd(f, f') = 1$, where $f'$
is the formal derivative of $f$.

### Setup

Let $f \in F_2[x]$ be irreducible of degree 5. We show $\gcd(f, f') = 1$.

**Step 1: $f' \neq 0$.**

In characteristic 2, the formal derivative vanishes identically ($f' = 0$) if an
d only if $f$ is a polynomial in $x^2$, i.e., $f(x) = g(x^2)$ for some $g \in F_
2[x]$. This is because the derivative of $\sum a_i x^i$ is $\sum i a_i x^{i-1}$,
 which is zero exactly when $a_i = 0$ for all odd $i$ — leaving only even powers
, each of which is $(x^2)^{i/2}$.

If $f(x) = g(x^2)$, then $\deg f = 2 \deg g$, so $\deg f$ is even. But $\deg f =
 5$ is **odd**. Contradiction. Therefore $f' \neq 0$.

**Step 2: $\gcd(f, f') = 1$.**

Since $f$ is irreducible, its only monic divisors are $1$ and $f$ itself. The fo
rmal derivative satisfies $\deg f' < \deg f = 5$ (in fact $\deg f' \leq 4$). Sin
ce $f' \neq 0$, any common divisor $d = \gcd(f, f')$ divides $f$ (so $d \in \{1,
 f\}$ up to units) and divides $f'$ (so $\deg d \leq \deg f' < 5 = \deg f$). The
 latter rules out $d = f$. Hence $d = 1$.

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

Since $\gcd(f, f') = 1$, the polynomial $f$ is separable, meaning it has 5 disti
nct roots in any algebraic closure of $F_2$.

### Remark (the general principle)

This is a special case of the general theorem that **every irreducible polynomia
l over a finite field is separable** (finite fields are *perfect*). The proof ab
ove uses only that $5$ is odd, which forces $f' \neq 0$. More generally, for *an
y* irreducible $f$ over $F_2$: if $f' = 0$ then $f(x) = g(x^2) = g(x)^2$ (since
$a^2 = a$ for $a \in F_2$, so $g(x^2) = g(x)^2$), contradicting irreducibility.
Thus $f' \neq 0$ always, and irreducibility forces $\gcd(f,f')=1$. The degree-5
case is the cleanest instance since odd degree already rules out $f' = 0$ by the
 parity argument alone.

### Answer

**Yes.** Every irreducible polynomial of degree 5 in $F_2[x]$ has distinct roots
 in any algebraic closure of $F_2$. The proof: such a polynomial $f$ has odd deg
ree, so it cannot be a polynomial in $x^2$, hence $f' \neq 0$; since $f$ is irre
ducible and $\deg f' < \deg f$, we get $\gcd(f, f') = 1$, which is exactly the c
riterion for $f$ to have distinct (separable) roots.
