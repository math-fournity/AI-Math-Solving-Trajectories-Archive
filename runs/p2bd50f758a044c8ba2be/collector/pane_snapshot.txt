## Proof

**Answer: True.**

Let $X$ be a smooth affine variety over $k$, where $k = \mathbb{R}$ or $k = \mat
hbb{C}$. We show that the set $X(k)$ of $k$-rational points carries the structur
e of a (real or complex) manifold.

**Setup.** Since $X$ is affine, $X = \operatorname{Spec} k[x_1,\dots,x_n]/(f_1,\
dots,f_m)$ for some polynomials $f_i \in k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$. The set of $k$-point
s is

$$X(k) = \{ p \in k^n : f_1(p) = \cdots = f_m(p) = 0 \} \subseteq k^n.$$

**Smoothness hypothesis.** Smoothness of $X$ over $k$ means that at every point
$p \in X(k)$, the local ring $\mathcal{O}_{X,p}$ is a regular local ring and the
 structure morphism $X \to \operatorname{Spec} k$ is smooth. Concretely, by the
**Jacobian criterion**, smoothness at $p$ is equivalent to: the rank of the Jaco
bian matrix

$$J(p) = \left( \frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x_j}(p) \right)_{i,j}$$

equals the codimension $c = n - \dim X$ at every point $p \in X(k)$.

**Case $k = \mathbb{C}$.** At each $p \in X(\mathbb{C})$, the Jacobian has rank
$c$. By the **holomorphic implicit function theorem** (which applies since the $
f_i$ are polynomials, hence holomorphic, and the Jacobian has maximal rank), the
re exists a neighborhood $U \subseteq \mathbb{C}^n$ of $p$ and a biholomorphic c
hange of coordinates on $U$ such that $X(\mathbb{C}) \cap U$ is mapped to a line
ar subspace $\mathbb{C}^{\dim X} \times \{0\}$. This provides local $\mathbb{C}^
{\dim X}$-valued charts that are biholomorphically compatible on overlaps, givin
g $X(\mathbb{C})$ the structure of a complex manifold of dimension $\dim X$.

**Case $k = \mathbb{R}$.** The same argument applies with the **real implicit fu
nction theorem**: the $f_i$ are real polynomials (hence smooth real-valued funct
ions), and the Jacobian $J(p)$ has rank $c$ at every $p \in X(\mathbb{R})$. The
implicit function theorem yields local $\mathbb{R}^{\dim X}$-valued charts with
smooth (in fact real-analytic) transition functions, giving $X(\mathbb{R})$ the
structure of a real manifold of dimension $\dim X$. (If $X(\mathbb{R}) = \varnot
hing$, it is the empty manifold, which is trivially a manifold.)

**Why affineness does not obstruct the conclusion.** The manifold structure is c
onstructed locally from the Jacobian condition; affineness only provides the glo
bal embedding $X \hookrightarrow k^n$ and the global defining equations $f_1,\do
ts,f_m$, which is in fact *convenient* for applying the implicit function theore
m. The argument never uses any property that would fail for affine varieties. (I
ndeed, the same conclusion holds for any smooth variety, affine or not, since sm
oothness is a local property and manifold charts are constructed locally.)

**Conclusion.** For both $k = \mathbb{R}$ and $k = \mathbb{C}$, every smooth aff
ine variety over $k$ is a (real or complex) manifold. The statement is **true**.
