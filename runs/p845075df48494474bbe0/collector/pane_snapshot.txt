Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a point in the convex hull
of a union of convex sets can always be written as a convex combination using ex
actly one point from each set.

**Answer: Yes** (assuming each $A_i$ is nonempty).

**Proof.**

Since $x \in \operatorname{co}(A_1 \cup A_2 \cup \dotsb \cup A_n)$, by definitio
n of the convex hull in a vector space, there exist finitely many points $b_1, b
_2, \ldots, b_m \in A_1 \cup A_2 \cup \dotsb \cup A_n$ and scalars $s_1, s_2, \l
dots, s_m \ge 0$ with $\sum_{j=1}^m s_j = 1$ such that

$$x = \sum_{j=1}^m s_j\, b_j.$$

Each $b_j$ belongs to at least one of the sets $A_1, \ldots, A_n$. For each $j$,
 choose (arbitrarily) one index $\sigma(j) \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$ such that $b_j
\in A_{\sigma(j)}$. This assigns every term to a specific set.

For each $i \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$, define

$$\alpha_i = \sum_{\{j \,:\, \sigma(j) = i\}} s_j.$$

Then $\alpha_i \ge 0$ for all $i$, and

$$\sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i = \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{\{j:\sigma(j)=i\}} s_j = \sum_{j=1}
^m s_j = 1.$$

**Case 1: $\alpha_i > 0$.** Define

$$a_i = \frac{1}{\alpha_i} \sum_{\{j:\sigma(j)=i\}} s_j\, b_j.$$

This is a convex combination of points $b_j \in A_i$ (since $\sigma(j) = i$ mean
s $b_j \in A_i$, and the coefficients $s_j / \alpha_i$ are nonnegative and sum t
o 1). Since $A_i$ is **convex**, $a_i \in A_i$.

**Case 2: $\alpha_i = 0$.** Then no $b_j$ was assigned to $A_i$. Since $A_i \neq
 \emptyset$, pick any $a_i \in A_i$ and set $t_i = 0$.

Now set $t_i = \alpha_i$ for all $i$. We verify:

$$\sum_{i=1}^n t_i\, a_i = \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i \left(\frac{1}{\alpha_i}\sum_{\
{j:\sigma(j)=i\}} s_j\, b_j\right) = \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{\{j:\sigma(j)=i\}} s_j\,
 b_j = \sum_{j=1}^m s_j\, b_j = x,$$

where in Case 2 ($\alpha_i = 0$) the term $t_i\, a_i = 0 \cdot a_i = 0$ contribu
tes nothing, so the choice of $a_i$ is irrelevant.

We have $t_i \ge 0$, $\sum t_i = 1$, and $a_i \in A_i$ for each $i$. $\blacksqua
re$

**Remark on the nonemptiness assumption.** The statement requires each $A_i$ to
be nonempty, since the conclusion demands $a_i \in A_i$ for every $i$. If some $
A_i = \emptyset$, no such $a_i$ exists and the conclusion fails trivially (regar
dless of $x$). Under the standard convention that the convex sets in question ar
e nonempty — which is implicitly assumed when one writes $a_i \in A_i$ — the res
ult holds as proved above.

The key insight is that **convexity of each $A_i$** allows us to collapse all th
e points selected from $A_i$ (with their accumulated weight $\alpha_i$) into a s
ingle representative point $a_i \in A_i$, reducing an arbitrary finite convex co
mbination over the union to a convex combination over a system of representative
s, one per set.
