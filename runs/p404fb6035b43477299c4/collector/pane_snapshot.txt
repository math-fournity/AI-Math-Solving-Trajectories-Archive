Let me restate the problem with a small notational fix: the target tangent space
 should be $T_q N$ for some point $q \in N$ (the symbol $f(p)$ in the statement
is undefined and is evidently a typo for a chosen base point $q$). We are given
smooth manifolds $M, N$, a point $p \in M$, a point $q \in N$, and a linear map
$L: T_p M \to T_q N$. We claim there is a smooth map $F$, defined on a neighborh
ood of $p$ in $M$ with values in $N$, such that $F(p) = q$ and $dF_p = L$.

**Construction.** Choose a coordinate chart $(U, \varphi)$ centered at $p$, so $
U \subset M$ is an open neighborhood of $p$ and $\varphi: U \to \mathbb{R}^m$ is
 a diffeomorphism onto its image with $\varphi(p) = 0$, where $m = \dim M$. Choo
se likewise a coordinate chart $(V, \psi)$ centered at $q$, with $V \subset N$ o
pen, $\psi: V \to \mathbb{R}^n$ a diffeomorphism onto its image, and $\psi(q) =
0$, where $n = \dim N$.

The coordinate map $\varphi$ identifies $T_p M$ with $\mathbb{R}^m$ via the isom
orphism $d\varphi_p: T_p M \to \mathbb{R}^m$, and similarly $d\psi_q: T_q N \to
\mathbb{R}^n$ identifies $T_q N$ with $\mathbb{R}^n$. Conjugating $L$ by these i
somorphisms gives a linear map of Euclidean spaces:
$$A := d\psi_q \circ L \circ (d\varphi_p)^{-1} : \mathbb{R}^m \to \mathbb{R}^n.$
$
Being linear, $A$ is represented by an $n \times m$ matrix, hence is smooth.

Define $\widetilde{F}: \mathbb{R}^m \to \mathbb{R}^n$ by $\widetilde{F}(x) = A x
$. This is a smooth (in fact linear) map with $\widetilde{F}(0) = 0$ and $d\wide
tilde{F}_0 = A$.

Now shrink $U$ if necessary so that $\varphi(U) \subset \widetilde{F}^{-1}(\psi(
V))$, i.e., so that $A(\varphi(U)) \subset \psi(V)$. This is possible because $\
varphi(U)$ is an open neighborhood of $0$, $A \cdot 0 = 0 \in \psi(V)$, and $\ps
i(V)$ is an open neighborhood of $0$; by continuity of $A$ and shrinking $U$ aro
und $p$ we ensure the image lands in $\psi(V)$.

Define
$$F := \psi^{-1} \circ \widetilde{F} \circ \varphi : U \to V \subset N.$$
This is a composition of smooth maps, hence smooth. We verify the required prope
rties:

- **Value at $p$:** $F(p) = \psi^{-1}(\widetilde{F}(\varphi(p))) = \psi^{-1}(\wi
detilde{F}(0)) = \psi^{-1}(0) = q$. ✓

- **Differential at $p$:** By the chain rule,
$$dF_p = d(\psi^{-1})_{\widetilde{F}(\varphi(p))} \circ d\widetilde{F}_{\varphi(
p)} \circ d\varphi_p = d(\psi^{-1})_0 \circ A \circ d\varphi_p.$$
Since $\psi$ is a diffeomorphism, $d(\psi^{-1})_0 = (d\psi_q)^{-1}$. Therefore
$$dF_p = (d\psi_q)^{-1} \circ A \circ d\varphi_p = (d\psi_q)^{-1} \circ \bigl(d\
psi_q \circ L \circ (d\varphi_p)^{-1}\bigr) \circ d\varphi_p = L.$$
✓

Thus $F$ is a smooth map defined on the neighborhood $U$ of $p$, taking values i
n $N$, with $F(p) = q$ and $dF_p = L$.

**Conclusion.** Every linear map $L: T_p M \to T_q N$ between tangent spaces of
smooth manifolds can be realized as the differential at $p$ of some smooth map $
F$ defined on a neighborhood of $p$. The key ingredients are: (1) coordinate cha
rts trivialize tangent spaces and identify arbitrary linear maps with matrices;
(2) every matrix defines a smooth (linear) map between Euclidean spaces; (3) cha
rts are diffeomorphisms, so the linear map can be pulled back to the manifold wh
ile preserving both smoothness and the prescribed differential. No condition on
$L$ (such as being invertible, or rank constraints) is required — the constructi
on works for *any* linear map.
