## Proof

We seek the exponential generating function (EGF) for the unsigned Stirling numb
ers of the first kind $u(n,k)$, i.e., the number of permutations of an $n$-set w
ith exactly $k$ cycles:

$$F_k(x) = \sum_{n \ge 0} u(n,k)\,\frac{x^n}{n!}.$$

### Step 1. A recurrence from the combinatorial specification

A permutation decomposes uniquely into a set (unordered collection) of cycles. T
hus the class of permutations is

$$\mathcal{P} = \operatorname{SET}(\operatorname{CYC}(\mathcal{Z})),$$

where $\mathcal{Z}$ marks elements. Labeling $k$ = number of cycles, the bivaria
te EGF is

$$P(x, y) = \exp\!\big(y\, C(x)\big),$$

where $C(x)$ is the EGF of a single cycle. Extracting the coefficient of $y^k$ g
ives $F_k(x) = C(x)^k / k!$.

### Step 2. EGF of a single cycle

A cycle on $n \ge 1$ labeled elements has $(n-1)!$ arrangements. Hence

$$C(x) = \sum_{n \ge 1} (n-1)!\,\frac{x^n}{n!} = \sum_{n \ge 1} \frac{x^n}{n} =
-\log(1-x) = \log\frac{1}{1-x}.$$

### Step 3. Assemble the EGF

Substituting,

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \sum_{n \ge 0} u(n,k)\,\frac{x^n}{n!} \;=\; \frac{1}{k!}\
left(\log\frac{1}{1-x}\right)^{\!k} \;=\; \frac{\bigl(-\log(1-x)\bigr)^k}{k!}.}$
$

### Verification

- **$k=0$:** $F_0(x)=1$, matching $u(0,0)=1$ and $u(n,0)=0$ for $n\ge 1$. ✓
- **$k=1$:** $F_1(x)=-\log(1-x)=\sum_{n\ge 1}x^n/n$, so $u(n,1)=(n-1)!$, the cou
nt of $n$-cycles. ✓
- **Recurrence check:** The defining recurrence $u(n,k)=u(n-1,k-1)+(n-1)\,u(n-1,
k)$ translates under $F_k(x)=\sum u(n,k)x^n/n!$ to $F_k'(x)=x^{k-1}/(k-1)! + \fr
ac{1}{x}\bigl(xF_k'(x)\bigr)$... more cleanly: differentiating $F_k=C^k/k!$ with
 $C=-\log(1-x)$, $C'=1/(1-x)$, and one checks $F_k' = \frac{C^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\cdo
t\frac{1}{1-x}$, while the recurrence gives $F_k' = F_{k-1} + \frac{d}{dx}(xF_k)
$, i.e. $(1-x)F_k' = F_{k-1}$, which is exactly $\frac{C^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}=F_{k-1}$
. ✓
- **Sum over $k$:** $\sum_{k\ge 0}F_k(x)y^k = \exp(y\,C(x))=\exp(-y\log(1-x))=(1
-x)^{-y}$, the classical bivariate EGF of permutations by cycles, with $y=1$ rec
overing $\sum_n n!\,x^n/n!=1/(1-x)$. ✓
