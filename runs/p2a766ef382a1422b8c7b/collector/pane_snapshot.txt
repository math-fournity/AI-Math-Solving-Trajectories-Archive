## Solution

**Understanding $\{-x\}$ on $[0, \pi]$.**

For $x \in (k, k+1)$ with $k$ a non-negative integer, we have $-x \in (-(k+1), -
k)$, so $\lfloor -x \rfloor = -(k+1)$ and
$$\{-x\} = -x - (-(k+1)) = k+1 - x \in (0,1).$$
At integer points $x = k$, $\{-k\} = 0$ (measure zero, irrelevant to the integra
l).

**Splitting the integral.** Since $\pi \in (3,4)$, the intervals are $[0,1)$, $[
1,2)$, $[2,3)$, and $[3, \pi]$.

On each full unit interval $[k, k+1)$ (for $k = 0, 1, 2$), substituting $u = k+1
 - x$:
$$\int_k^{k+1} (k+1-x)^n\, dx = \int_0^1 u^n\, du = \frac{1}{n+1}.$$

On the partial interval $[3, \pi]$, we have $\{-x\} = 4 - x$. Substituting $u =
4 - x$ (so $u$ runs from $1$ down to $4-\pi$):
$$\int_3^{\pi} (4-x)^n\, dx = \int_{4-\pi}^{1} u^n\, du = \frac{1 - (4-\pi)^{n+1
}}{n+1}.$$

**Combining.**
$$\int_0^\pi \{-x\}^n\, dx = \frac{3}{n+1} + \frac{1 - (4-\pi)^{n+1}}{n+1} = \fr
ac{4 - (4-\pi)^{n+1}}{n+1}.$$

**Taking the limit.** Since $4 - \pi \approx 0.8584 < 1$, we have $(4-\pi)^{n+1}
 \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$. Therefore:
$$n \int_0^\pi \{-x\}^n\, dx = \frac{n}{n+1}\Big(4 - (4-\pi)^{n+1}\Big) \;\xrigh
tarrow{n\to\infty}\; 1 \cdot (4 - 0) = \boxed{4}.$$
