**Answer: Yes, such a measurable set exists.**

**Construction.** Let $\{J_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ be an enumeration of all open int
ervals with rational endpoints. For each $n$, choose a closed subinterval $[a_n,
 b_n] \subseteq J_n$ with $a_n < b_n$, and construct a **fat Cantor set** $F_n \
subseteq [a_n, b_n]$ (à la Smith–Volterra–Cantor): a closed, nowhere dense, perf
ect subset of $[a_n,b_n]$ with positive Lebesgue measure. Being perfect, each $F
_n$ is uncountable (indeed of cardinality $\mathfrak{c}$).

Define
$$A = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} F_n.$$

**$A$ is measurable.** Each $F_n$ is closed, so $A$ is a countable union of clos
ed sets, i.e., an $F_\sigma$ set. Hence $A$ is Borel and therefore Lebesgue meas
urable.

**$A \cap I$ is uncountable for every open interval $I$.** Given any open interv
al $I$, there exists a rational-endpoint interval $J_n \subseteq I$ (rational-en
dpoint intervals form a basis for the topology of $\mathbb{R}$). Then
$$A \cap I \supseteq F_n,$$
and $F_n$ is uncountable (perfect). Hence $A \cap I$ is uncountable.

**$A^\complement \cap I$ is uncountable for every open interval $I$.** Each $F_n
$ is closed with empty interior, hence nowhere dense. Therefore $A = \bigcup_n F
_n$ is a **meager** subset of $\mathbb{R}$ (countable union of nowhere dense set
s). By the Baire Category Theorem, $\mathbb{R}$ (a complete metric space without
 isolated points) is not meager, so the complement
$$A^\complement = \mathbb{R} \setminus A$$
is **comeager** (residual). A comeager subset of $\mathbb{R}$ is comeager in eve
ry open interval $I$ (since $A \cap I$ is meager in $I$), and a comeager set in
a nonempty open interval has cardinality $\mathfrak{c}$ (it cannot be countable,
 since a countable set is meager and the complement of a meager set in a Baire s
pace is nonempty—in fact dense and of cardinality continuum). Concretely: $A^\co
mplement \cap I$ is a dense $G_\delta$ subset of $I$, and any dense $G_\delta$ i
n a complete metric space without isolated points contains a homeomorphic copy o
f the Baire space $\mathbb{N}^\mathbb{N}$, hence is uncountable.

Therefore $A^\complement \cap I$ is uncountable for every open interval $I$.

**Conclusion.** The set $A = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} F_n$ is Lebesgue measurable,
 and for every open interval $I$, both $A \cap I$ and $A^\complement \cap I$ are
 uncountable. $\blacksquare$
