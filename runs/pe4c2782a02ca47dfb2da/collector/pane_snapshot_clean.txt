**Theorem.** The map $\sigma \colon \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\to\mathbb{R}^m$ sendi
ng a matrix $X$ to its ordered singular values $\bigl(\sigma_1(X)\ge \cdots \ge
\sigma_m(X)\bigr)$ is continuous.

**Proof.**

We factor $\sigma$ as a composition of three continuous maps.

**Map 1 — $f\colon \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\to \mathrm{Sym}(m)$, $\;f(X)=XX^{\math
sf T}$.**

Each entry of $XX^{\mathsf T}$ is a finite sum of products of entries of $X$, he
nce a polynomial in the entries of $X$. Therefore $f$ is continuous (with respec
t to any norms, since the spaces are finite-dimensional). Moreover $XX^{\mathsf
T}$ is symmetric positive semidefinite, so its eigenvalues are real and nonnegat
ive.

**Map 2 — $g\colon \mathrm{Sym}(m)\to\mathbb{R}^m$, $\;g(A)=(\lambda_1(A),\dots,
\lambda_m(A))$,**

where $\lambda_1(A)\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_m(A)$ are the eigenvalues of the symmetri
c matrix $A$ in decreasing order. We show $g$ is continuous using the **Courant–
Fischer min–max characterization**:

$$
\lambda_i(A)=\max_{\substack{V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^m\\ \dim V=i}}\;\min_{\substac
k{x\in V\\ \|x\|=1}}\langle Ax,x\rangle .
$$

Let $A,B\in\mathrm{Sym}(m)$ and set $\delta=\|A-B\|_{\mathrm{op}}$. For every un
it vector $x$,
$$
|\langle (A-B)x,x\rangle|\le \|A-B\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\delta.
$$
Hence for every subspace $V$ with $\dim V=i$,
$$
\min_{x\in V,\,\|x\|=1}\langle Bx,x\rangle \;\ge\; \min_{x\in V,\,\|x\|=1}\langl
e Ax,x\rangle - \delta.
$$
Taking the maximum over all such $V$ gives $\lambda_i(B)\ge \lambda_i(A)-\delta$
. By symmetry (swapping $A$ and $B$), $\lambda_i(A)\ge \lambda_i(B)-\delta$. The
refore
$$
|\lambda_i(A)-\lambda_i(B)|\le \|A-B\|_{\mathrm{op}},\qquad i=1,\dots,m.
$$
Since the operator norm is controlled by any fixed norm on the finite-dimensiona
l space $\mathrm{Sym}(m)$, this proves $g$ is (Lipschitz) continuous.

**Map 3 — $h\colon \mathbb{R}^m\to\mathbb{R}^m$, $\;h(t_1,\dots,t_m)=(\sqrt{t_1}
,\dots,\sqrt{t_m})$.**

The square-root function is continuous on $[0,\infty)$. The eigenvalues of $XX^{
\mathsf T}$ are nonnegative, so $h$ is applied only on its domain of continuity.
 Thus $h$ is continuous.

**Composition.** The singular values of $X$ are exactly the square roots of the
eigenvalues of $XX^{\mathsf T}$, arranged in decreasing order:
$$
\sigma(X)=\bigl(\sqrt{\lambda_1(XX^{\mathsf T})},\dots,\sqrt{\lambda_m(XX^{\math
sf T})}\bigr)=h\bigl(g(f(X))\bigr).
$$
Therefore $\sigma = h\circ g\circ f$. Each of $f$, $g$, $h$ is continuous, and a
 composition of continuous maps is continuous.

Hence $\sigma\colon \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\to\mathbb{R}^m$ is continuous. $\blac
ksquare$
