## Solution

By the **Divergence Theorem**,

$$\iint_S \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iiint_E \nabla\cdot\mathbf{F}\, dV.$$

**Compute the divergence.** With $\mathbf{F}=\langle x^3+y^2,\; 2yz+e^z,\; y^2-z
^2\rangle$:

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x^3+y^2)+\frac{\partial}{\
partial y}(2yz+e^z)+\frac{\partial}{\partial z}(y^2-z^2) = 3x^2+2z-2z = 3x^2.$$

**Determine the region $E$.** The paraboloids intersect where
$$x^2+y^2 = 12-2x^2-2y^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; 3(x^2+y^2)=12 \;\Longrightarrow\; x
^2+y^2=4.$$
So $E$ is the solid with $x^2+y^2\le 4$ and $x^2+y^2 \le z \le 12-2(x^2+y^2)$.

**Set up in cylindrical coordinates** $(x,y,z)=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,z)$, wit
h $0\le r\le 2$, $0\le\theta\le 2\pi$, $r^2\le z\le 12-2r^2$, and $dV = r\,dz\,d
r\,d\theta$:

$$\iiint_E 3x^2\,dV = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^2\!\!\int_{r^2}^{12-2r^2} 3r^2\cos
^2\theta\cdot r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta.$$

**Integrate over $z$:**
$$\int_{r^2}^{12-2r^2} dz = 12-2r^2-r^2 = 12-3r^2.$$

**Separate the integrals:**
$$= \left(\int_0^{2\pi}3\cos^2\theta\,d\theta\right)\left(\int_0^2 r^3(12-3r^2)\
,dr\right).$$

Using $\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^2\theta\,d\theta = \pi$:
$$\int_0^{2\pi}3\cos^2\theta\,d\theta = 3\pi.$$

**Integrate over $r$:**
$$\int_0^2 (12r^3-3r^5)\,dr = \left[3r^4-\frac{r^6}{2}\right]_0^2 = 3\cdot 16-\f
rac{64}{2}=48-32=16.$$

**Combine:**
$$\iint_S \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = 3\pi\cdot 16 = \boxed{48\pi}.$$
