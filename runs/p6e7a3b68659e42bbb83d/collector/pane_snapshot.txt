# Proof: A Locally Compact Group with an Abelian Normal Subgroup of Finite Index
 is Amenable

**Claim.** Let $G$ be a locally compact group. If $G$ admits an abelian normal s
ubgroup $A$ of finite index, then $G$ is amenable.

---

## Step 1: Reduction to the case where $A$ is closed

Amenability of locally compact groups is defined via the existence of a left-inv
ariant mean on $L^\infty(G)$ (equipped with the left Haar measure). For the quot
ient $G/A$ to be a locally compact group in the usual sense, we need $A$ to be *
*closed**.

If $A$ is not closed, consider its closure $\bar{A}$. Since the group operations
 (multiplication and inversion) are continuous and $A$ is a subgroup, $\bar{A}$
is a subgroup. Since $A$ is normal ($gAg^{-1} = A$ for all $g$, and conjugation
is continuous, so $g\bar{A}g^{-1} = \overline{gAg^{-1}} = \bar{A}$), $\bar{A}$ i
s normal. Since $A$ is abelian, the commutator map $(x,y) \mapsto xyx^{-1}y^{-1}
$ is identically the identity on $A \times A$; by continuity it is the identity
on $\bar{A} \times \bar{A}$, so $\bar{A}$ is abelian. Finally, $A \subseteq \bar
{A}$ gives $[G : \bar{A}] \leq [G : A] < \infty$.

Thus, replacing $A$ by $\bar{A}$, **we may assume $A$ is a closed abelian normal
 subgroup of finite index**.

---

## Step 2: $A$ is amenable

**Every abelian locally compact group is amenable.** We prove this using the **M
arkov–Kakutani fixed point theorem**: *a family of commuting continuous affine m
aps on a compact convex subset of a locally convex topological vector space has
a common fixed point.*

Let $K$ denote the set of all means on $L^\infty(A)$, i.e., positive linear func
tionals $m: L^\infty(A) \to \mathbb{R}$ with $m(\mathbf{1}) = 1$. Equipped with
the weak$^*$ topology (as a subset of $(L^\infty(A))^*$), $K$ is a compact conve
x set (by the Banach–Alaoglu theorem, since $K$ is a weak$^*$-closed subset of t
he unit ball).

Each $a \in A$ acts on $K$ by the left-translation operator:
$$
(L_a m)(f) = m(f_a), \quad \text{where } f_a(x) = f(a^{-1}x).
$$
This map $L_a: K \to K$ is continuous and affine. Since $A$ is abelian, for any
$a, b \in A$:
$$
L_a \circ L_b = L_{ab} = L_{ba} = L_b \circ L_a,
$$
so the family $\{L_a\}_{a \in A}$ consists of **pairwise commuting** continuous
affine self-maps of $K$.

By the Markov–Kakutani fixed point theorem, there exists $m_0 \in K$ such that $
L_a(m_0) = m_0$ for all $a \in A$. This $m_0$ is a **left-invariant mean** on $L
^\infty(A)$, proving $A$ is amenable.

---

## Step 3: $G/A$ is amenable

Since $A$ is closed and normal of finite index, $G/A$ is a **finite group** (wit
h the quotient topology, it is a discrete locally compact group). Every finite g
roup is amenable: the functional
$$
m(f) = \frac{1}{|G/A|}\sum_{\bar{g} \in G/A} f(\bar{g})
$$
is a left-invariant mean on $L^\infty(G/A)$.

---

## Step 4: Extension theorem — amenability passes from $A$ and $G/A$ to $G$

**Theorem (Extension).** *Let $N$ be a closed normal subgroup of a locally compa
ct group $G$. If $N$ and $G/N$ are both amenable, then $G$ is amenable.*

*Proof of the theorem.* Let $m_N$ be a left-invariant mean on $L^\infty(N)$ (wit
h respect to a left Haar measure on $N$) and $m_Q$ a left-invariant mean on $L^\
infty(G/N)$ (with respect to a left Haar measure on $G/N$).

We use the **Weil formula** (also called the quotient integral formula): there e
xists a left Haar measure $\mu_G$ on $G$, a left Haar measure $\mu_N$ on $N$, an
d a left Haar measure $\mu_Q$ on $G/N$ such that for every $f \in L^1(G)$,
$$
\int_G f\,d\mu_G = \int_{G/N}\left(\int_N f(gn)\,d\mu_N(n)\right)d\mu_Q(gN).
$$
This identifies $L^\infty(G) \hookrightarrow L^\infty(G/N \times N)$ in a $G$-eq
uivariant way.

For $f \in L^\infty(G)$, define $\Phi(f) \in L^\infty(G/N)$ by
$$
\Phi(f)(gN) = m_N\big(n \mapsto f(gn)\big).
$$
**Well-definedness:** If $g'N = gN$, then $g' = gn_0$ for some $n_0 \in N$, and
the function $n \mapsto f(g'n) = f(gn_0 n)$ is the left translate (by $n_0$) of
$n \mapsto f(gn)$. Since $m_N$ is left-$N$-invariant, $m_N(n \mapsto f(gn_0 n))
= m_N(n \mapsto f(gn))$, so $\Phi(f)(g'N) = \Phi(f)(gN)$.

**Measurability and boundedness:** $\Phi(f)$ is measurable (by Fubini-type argum
ents using the Weil formula) and $\|\Phi(f)\|_\infty \leq \|f\|_\infty$.

Now define $m: L^\infty(G) \to \mathbb{R}$ by
$$
m(f) = m_Q\big(\Phi(f)\big).
$$

- **$m$ is a mean:** Positivity and linearity follow from those of $m_N$ and $m_
Q$. Also $m(\mathbf{1}_G) = m_Q(\mathbf{1}_{G/N}) = 1$.

- **$m$ is left-$G$-invariant:** For $h \in G$ and $f \in L^\infty(G)$, let $f_h
(x) = f(h^{-1}x)$. Then
$$
\Phi(f_h)(gN) = m_N\big(n \mapsto f(h^{-1}gn)\big) = \Phi(f)(h^{-1}gN) = [\Phi(f
)]_h(gN),
$$
where $[\Phi(f)]_h$ denotes the left translate of $\Phi(f)$ by $hN \in G/N$. The
refore
$$
m(f_h) = m_Q\big([\Phi(f)]_h\big) = m_Q\big(\Phi(f)\big) = m(f),
$$
using the left-invariance of $m_Q$ under $G/N$. $\square$

---

## Conclusion

Applying the extension theorem with $N = A$:
- $A$ is amenable (Step 2, since $A$ is abelian and locally compact),
- $G/A$ is amenable (Step 3, since $G/A$ is finite),

therefore **$G$ is amenable**.
