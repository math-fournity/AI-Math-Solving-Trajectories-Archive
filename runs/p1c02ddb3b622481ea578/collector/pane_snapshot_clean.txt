## Proof

**Definition.** In a topological space $X$, the *quasi-component* of a point $x
\in X$ is the intersection of all clopen (simultaneously closed and open) subset
s of $X$ that contain $x$. Equivalently, two points $x, y$ lie in the same quasi
-component if and only if there is no clopen subset of $X$ that contains one but
 not the other.

**Claim.** The quasi-components of $\mathbb{Q}$ (with the subspace topology inhe
rited from $\mathbb{R}$) are exactly the singletons $\{q\}$ for $q \in \mathbb{Q
}$.

**Proof.** Let $a, b \in \mathbb{Q}$ with $a \neq b$; without loss of generality
 assume $a < b$. Since $\mathbb{R} \setminus \mathbb{Q}$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}
$, there exists an irrational number $\alpha$ with $a < \alpha < b$.

Consider the sets
$$U = (-\infty, \alpha) \cap \mathbb{Q}, \qquad V = (\alpha, +\infty) \cap \math
bb{Q}.$$

- $U$ is open in $\mathbb{Q}$, being the intersection of $\mathbb{Q}$ with the o
pen set $(-\infty, \alpha) \subseteq \mathbb{R}$.
- $V$ is open in $\mathbb{Q}$, being the intersection of $\mathbb{Q}$ with the o
pen set $(\alpha, +\infty) \subseteq \mathbb{R}$.
- $U \cup V = \mathbb{Q}$, since no rational equals the irrational $\alpha$.
- $U \cap V = \emptyset$.

Therefore $U$ and $V$ form a separation of $\mathbb{Q}$ into two disjoint open s
ets. It follows that $U$ is clopen in $\mathbb{Q}$: it is open, and its compleme
nt $V$ is open, so $U$ is also closed.

Now $a \in U$ (since $a < \alpha$) and $b \in V$ (since $b > \alpha$), so $b \no
tin U$. Thus $U$ is a clopen subset of $\mathbb{Q}$ containing $a$ but not $b$.

By the characterization of quasi-components, $a$ and $b$ lie in different quasi-
components. Since $a$ and $b$ were arbitrary distinct rationals, every quasi-com
ponent contains at most one point. As every quasi-component is non-empty (it con
tains its defining point), each quasi-component is a singleton.

Conversely, each singleton $\{q\}$ is indeed a quasi-component, being the quasi-
component of $q$.

**Conclusion.** The quasi-components of $\mathbb{Q}$ with the subspace topology
from $\mathbb{R}$ are precisely the singletons $\{q\}$ for each $q \in \mathbb{Q
}$. $\blacksquare$
