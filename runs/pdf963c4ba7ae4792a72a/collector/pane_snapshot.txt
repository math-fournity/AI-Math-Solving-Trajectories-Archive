## Proof

**Claim:** The infimum is $1$, and it is attained by $u(t) = t$.

**Lower bound.** Let $u \in C^1([0,1])$ with $u(0)=0$ and $\max_{t\in[0,1]}|u(t)
|=1$. Then there exists $t_0 \in [0,1]$ with $|u(t_0)|=1$. Since $u(0)=0$,

$$|u(t_0)| = |u(t_0)-u(0)| = \left|\int_0^{t_0} u'(t)\,dt\right| \le \int_0^{t_0
}|u'(t)|\,dt.$$

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

$$\int_0^{t_0}|u'(t)|\,dt \le \left(\int_0^{t_0}(u'(t))^2\,dt\right)^{1/2}\cdot
t_0^{1/2}.$$

Combining,

$$1 \le \left(\int_0^{t_0}(u'(t))^2\,dt\right)^{1/2}\cdot t_0^{1/2} \le \left(\i
nt_0^1(u'(t))^2\,dt\right)^{1/2}\cdot t_0^{1/2}.$$

Since $t_0 \le 1$, we have $t_0^{1/2}\le 1$, hence

$$\int_0^1 (u'(t))^2\,dt \ge \frac{1}{t_0} \ge 1.$$

**Attainment.** The function $u(t)=t$ is $C^1$ on $[0,1]$, satisfies $u(0)=0$, a
nd $\max_{t\in[0,1]}|u(t)|=1$ (attained at $t=1$). Its energy is

$$\int_0^1 (u'(t))^2\,dt = \int_0^1 1\,dt = 1.$$

Therefore the infimum equals $1$ (and is in fact a minimum).
