# Solution

**Key geometric claim:** The intersection $P$ of the tangents to the circumcircl
e of $\triangle BIC$ at $B$ and $C$ is the midpoint of arc $BC$ containing $A$ o
n the circumcircle of $\triangle ABC$.

**Proof of claim.** It is well known that the circumcenter of $\triangle BIC$ is
 $M$, the midpoint of arc $BC$ not containing $A$ on the circumcircle of $\trian
gle ABC$, since $MB=MC=MI$ (as $M$ lies on the angle bisectors and satisfies $MI
=MB=MC=2R\sin\tfrac{A}{2}$, where $R$ is the circumradius of $\triangle ABC$). T
he circumradius of $\triangle BIC$ is $R'=MI=2R\sin\tfrac{A}{2}$.

Since $\angle BIC = \tfrac{\pi}{2}+\tfrac{A}{2}$, the central angle $\angle BMC
= \pi + A$. The tangent at $B$ is perpendicular to $MB$, so in right triangle $\
triangle MBP$ (right angle at $B$):

$$MP = \frac{MB}{|\cos\angle BMP|} = \frac{R'}{|\cos\frac{\pi+A}{2}|} = \frac{R'
}{\sin\frac{A}{2}} = \frac{2R\sin\frac{A}{2}}{\sin\frac{A}{2}} = 2R.$$

Both $M$ and $P$ lie on the perpendicular bisector of $BC$ (by symmetry of the t
angent construction). Since $OM = R$ (as $M$ is on the circumcircle of $\triangl
e ABC$) and $MP = 2R$ with $P$ on the side of $BC$ opposite $M$, we get $OP = R$
, so **$P$ lies on the circumcircle of $\triangle ABC$**, diametrically opposite
 $M$. Thus $P$ is the midpoint of arc $BC$ containing $A$. $\square$

**Computing $BC$.** Set $a=BC$, $b=CA=16$, $c=AB=10$, and $s=\tfrac{B+C}{2}=\tfr
ac{\pi-A}{2}$, $d=\tfrac{B-C}{2}$, so $B=s+d$, $C=s-d$.

Since $P$ is the midpoint of arc $BC$ containing $A$, and $A$ divides that arc i
nto arcs $2C$ (from $B$) and $2B$ (from $C$), the arc $PA = |B-C| = 2|d|$. There
fore:

$$PA = 2R\sin|d| = 8. \tag{1}$$

From the side lengths: $\sin B = \tfrac{b}{2R}=\tfrac{8}{R}$, $\sin C = \tfrac{c
}{2R}=\tfrac{5}{R}$, so $\frac{\sin(s+d)}{\sin(s-d)}=\frac{8}{5}$, giving:

$$5(\sin s\cos d+\cos s\sin d)=8(\sin s\cos d-\cos s\sin d) \implies 13\cos s\si
n d = 3\sin s\cos d,$$

$$\tan d = \frac{3}{13}\tan s. \tag{2}$$

From (1): $R\sin d = 4$, and $R = \tfrac{8}{\sin B}=\tfrac{8}{\sin(s+d)}$, so $\
frac{\sin d}{\sin(s+d)}=\frac{1}{2}$, i.e., $2\sin d = \sin s\cos d+\cos s\sin d
$, yielding:

$$\tan d = \frac{\sin s}{2-\cos s}. \tag{3}$$

Equating (2) and (3): $\frac{\sin s}{2-\cos s}=\frac{3\sin s}{13\cos s}$, so $13
\cos s = 3(2-\cos s)$, giving $\cos s = \tfrac{3}{8}$ and $\sin s = \tfrac{\sqrt
{55}}{8}$.

Then $\tan d = \tfrac{3}{13}\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt{55}/8}{3/8}=\tfrac{\sqrt{55}}{13}$
, so $\sin d = \tfrac{\sqrt{55}}{\sqrt{224}}=\tfrac{\sqrt{55}}{4\sqrt{14}}$ and
$\cos d = \tfrac{13}{4\sqrt{14}}$.

Now:
$$\sin B = \sin(s+d)=\sin s\cos d+\cos s\sin d=\frac{\sqrt{55}}{8}\cdot\frac{13}
{4\sqrt{14}}+\frac{3}{8}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{55}}{4\sqrt{14}}=\frac{16\sqrt{55}}{32\
sqrt{14}}=\frac{\sqrt{55}}{2\sqrt{14}}.$$

$$R = \frac{8}{\sin B}=\frac{16\sqrt{14}}{\sqrt{55}}.$$

Finally, $\sin A = \sin(\pi-2s)=\sin 2s = 2\sin s\cos s = 2\cdot\frac{\sqrt{55}}
{8}\cdot\frac{3}{8}=\frac{3\sqrt{55}}{32}$, so:

$$a = 2R\sin A = 2\cdot\frac{16\sqrt{14}}{\sqrt{55}}\cdot\frac{3\sqrt{55}}{32} =
 3\sqrt{14}.$$

**Verification:** $PA = 2R\sin d = 2\cdot\frac{16\sqrt{14}}{\sqrt{55}}\cdot\frac
{\sqrt{55}}{4\sqrt{14}}=8$ ✓; $b=2R\sin B=16$ ✓; $c=2R\sin C=10$ ✓.

$$\boxed{BC = 3\sqrt{14}}$$
