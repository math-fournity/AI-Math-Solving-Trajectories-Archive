## Proof

Let the equilateral triangle be $ABC$ with side length $s$, and let $P$ be the i
nterior point. Label the vertices so that $PA = 3$, $PB = 4$, $PC = 5$.

**Rotation construction.** Rotate the entire figure by $60°$ about vertex $A$, i
n the direction that sends $B$ to $C$. Let $P$ map to $P'$ under this rotation.

Since the rotation sends $B \mapsto C$ and $P \mapsto P'$:
- $AP' = AP = 3$, and $\angle PAP' = 60°$, so $\triangle APP'$ is equilateral, h
ence $PP' = 3$.
- $P'C = PB = 4$ (rotation preserves distances, and $B \mapsto C$).
- $PC = 5$ (unchanged).

**Key triangle.** Triangle $P'PC$ has side lengths $PP' = 3$, $P'C = 4$, $PC = 5
$. Since $3^2 + 4^2 = 5^2$, this is a right triangle with the right angle at $P'
$:
$$\angle PP'C = 90°.$$

**Finding the side length.** Since $\triangle APP'$ is equilateral, $\angle AP'P
 = 60°$. The point $A$ lies on the opposite side of line $P'P$ from $C$ (as $P$
is interior and the rotation is outward), so:
$$\angle AP'C = \angle AP'P + \angle PP'C = 60° + 90° = 150°.$$

Now apply the Law of Cosines in $\triangle AP'C$ with $AP' = 3$, $P'C = 4$, $\an
gle AP'C = 150°$:
$$AC^2 = 3^2 + 4^2 - 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4 \cdot \cos 150° = 25 - 24\left(-\frac{\sq
rt{3}}{2}\right) = 25 + 12\sqrt{3}.$$

Thus $s^2 = 25 + 12\sqrt{3}$.

**Area.** The area of an equilateral triangle with side $s$ is $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{
4}s^2$:
$$\text{Area} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\left(25 + 12\sqrt{3}\right) = \frac{25\sqrt{3
} + 36}{4}.$$

$$\boxed{\dfrac{36 + 25\sqrt{3}}{4}}$$
