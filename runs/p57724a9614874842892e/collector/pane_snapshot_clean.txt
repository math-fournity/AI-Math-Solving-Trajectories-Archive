Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether, for a separable bounded me
tric space $X$ with $d(A,B)>0$, we can always separate $A$ and $B$ by finite uni
ons of open balls.

**Answer: No.** Here is a counterexample.

---

## Counterexample

Let $X = \mathbb{N} = \{0, 1, 2, 3, \dots\}$ equipped with the discrete metric
$$d(m, n) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } m = n, \\ 1 & \text{if } m \neq n. \end
{cases}$$

**$X$ is separable:** $X = \mathbb{N}$ is countable, so it is a countable dense
subset of itself.

**$X$ is bounded:** $\operatorname{diam}(X) = 1$.

Let
$$A = \{0, 2, 4, 6, \dots\} \quad \text{(even numbers)}, \qquad B = \{1, 3, 5, 7
, \dots\} \quad \text{(odd numbers)}.$$

Then $d(A, B) = \inf\{d(a,b) : a \in A,\, b \in B\} = 1 > 0$.

---

## Analysis of open balls

In this metric space, the open balls are:
- $B(x, r) = \{x\}$ for $0 < r \leq 1$,
- $B(x, r) = X$ for $r > 1$.

Therefore, **any finite union of open balls** is either:
- a **finite** subset of $X$ (if every ball used has radius $\leq 1$, hence is a
 singleton), or
- the **entire space** $X$ (if any ball used has radius $> 1$).

---

## No valid $A', B'$ exist

Suppose $A'$ and $B'$ are finite unions of open balls with $A \subset A'$, $B \s
ubset B'$, and $A' \cap B' = \emptyset$.

**Case 1: $A' = X$.** Then $A' \cap B' = X \cap B' = B' \supset B \neq \emptyset
$, contradicting $A' \cap B' = \emptyset$.

**Case 2: $A'$ is finite.** Then $A'$ cannot contain $A$, since $A$ is infinite.
 This contradicts $A \subset A'$.

Both cases lead to contradictions, so no such $A'$ and $B'$ exist.

---

## Conclusion

The answer is **No**. The key insight is that "bounded + separable" does not imp
ly "totally bounded." In a totally bounded space, one could cover $X$ with finit
ely many balls of radius $d(A,B)/3$ and separate $A$ and $B$ as desired. But the
 discrete metric on $\mathbb{N}$ is bounded and separable yet not totally bounde
d, and the open balls are too coarse (singletons or the whole space) to allow fi
nite separation of two infinite sets at positive distance.
