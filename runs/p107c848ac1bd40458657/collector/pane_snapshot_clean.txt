## Answer

**No.** A projection onto a closed subspace of a Banach space need not be bounde
d.

### Proof

We exhibit a Banach space $X$, a closed subspace $M\subseteq X$, and a linear pr
ojection $P:X\to X$ with $\operatorname{ran}(P)=M$ that is unbounded.

**Setup.** Let $X=\ell^\infty$ (bounded scalar sequences with the sup norm) and
let $M=c_0$ (sequences converging to $0$). Then $M$ is a closed subspace of $X$:
 if $x^{(n)}\in c_0$ and $\|x^{(n)}-x\|_\infty\to 0$ for some $x\in\ell^\infty$,
 then for every $\varepsilon>0$ pick $n$ with $\|x^{(n)}-x\|_\infty<\varepsilon/
2$ and $N$ with $|x^{(n)}_k|<\varepsilon/2$ for $k\ge N$; then $|x_k|\le |x^{(n)
}_k|+|x_k-x^{(n)}_k|<\varepsilon$, so $x\in c_0$.

**Key fact (Phillips, 1940).** The subspace $c_0$ is *not complemented* in $\ell
^\infty$: there exists no closed subspace $N\subseteq\ell^\infty$ such that $\el
l^\infty=c_0\oplus N$ (topological direct sum). Equivalently, there is no bounde
d linear projection from $\ell^\infty$ onto $c_0$.

**Constructing an algebraic projection.** Viewing $c_0$ as a vector space, choos
e a Hamel basis $\mathcal{B}_0$ of $c_0$ and extend it to a Hamel basis $\mathca
l{B}=\mathcal{B}_0\cup\mathcal{B}_1$ of $\ell^\infty$ (using the Axiom of Choice
). Define $P:\ell^\infty\to\ell^\infty$ on basis vectors by
$$P(b)=b\ \text{for } b\in\mathcal{B}_0,\qquad P(b)=0\ \text{for } b\in\mathcal{
B}_1,$$
and extend linearly to all of $\ell^\infty$. Then $P$ is linear, $P^2=P$ (idempo
tent), and $\operatorname{ran}(P)=c_0$. Hence $P$ is a projection onto the close
d subspace $c_0$.

**$P$ is unbounded.** Suppose, for contradiction, that $P$ is bounded (continuou
s). Then $\ker(P)$ is a closed subspace of $\ell^\infty$ (kernel of a continuous
 linear map), and $\ell^\infty = c_0 \oplus \ker(P)$ is a topological direct sum
. This means $c_0$ is complemented in $\ell^\infty$, contradicting Phillips' the
orem. Hence $P$ cannot be bounded.

**Conclusion.** There exists a closed subspace $c_0$ of the Banach space $\ell^\
infty$ and a (linear, idempotent) projection onto it that is unbounded. Therefor
e a projection on a closed subspace of a Banach space is **not** always bounded.

**Remark.** The general principle is: a closed subspace $M$ of a Banach space $X
$ admits a *bounded* projection onto $M$ if and only if $M$ is *complemented* in
 $X$ (i.e., $X=M\oplus N$ for some closed $N$). Since not every closed subspace
is complemented (Phillips' theorem gives $c_0\subset\ell^\infty$; Sobczyk's theo
rem shows $c_0$ is complemented only in separable spaces, etc.), unbounded proje
ctions onto closed subspaces necessarily exist whenever the subspace is not comp
lemented.
