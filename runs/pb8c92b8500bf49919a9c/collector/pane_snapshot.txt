## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, such a Schwartz function $g$ exists.

**Construction and argument.**

Since $f \not\equiv 0$ and $f$ is continuous, there exists a point $x_0 \in \mat
hbb{R}^n$ with $f(x_0) \neq 0$. Without loss of generality, assume $f(x_0) > 0$
(the case $f(x_0) < 0$ is handled identically with signs reversed).

By continuity of $f$, there exists $r > 0$ such that
$$f(x) \geq \tfrac{1}{2}f(x_0) > 0 \quad \text{for all } x \in B(x_0, r).$$

Now choose a standard smooth bump function $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)
$ with $\operatorname{supp} \varphi \subset B(x_0, r)$ and $\varphi \not\equiv 0
$ (for instance, $\varphi(x) = \psi(x - x_0)$ where $\psi$ is a nonnegative smoo
th function supported in $B(0,r)$ that is positive on $B(0, r/2)$). Set $g := \v
arphi$.

Since $g$ is smooth and compactly supported, $g \in \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ —
 every compactly supported smooth function is a Schwartz function.

Now consider the integral:
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} g^2 f \, dx = \int_{B(x_0, r)} \varphi^2 f \, dx.$$

This integral is well-defined (finite): $\varphi^2$ is bounded and compactly sup
ported, and $f$ is continuous hence bounded on the compact set $\overline{B(x_0,
 r)}$, so the integrand is bounded with compact support.

On the support of $\varphi$, we have $f \geq \frac{1}{2}f(x_0) > 0$, and $\varph
i^2 \geq 0$ with $\varphi^2 > 0$ on a nonempty open set (where $\varphi > 0$). T
herefore $\varphi^2 f \geq 0$ everywhere and $\varphi^2 f > 0$ on a set of posit
ive Lebesgue measure. Hence
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} g^2 f \, dx > 0 \neq 0.$$

The case $f(x_0) < 0$ is analogous: we obtain $\int g^2 f \, dx < 0 \neq 0$.

In either case, we have produced a Schwartz function $g$ with $\int_{\mathbb{R}^
n} g^2 f \, dx \neq 0$. $\blacksquare$
