Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum number of multichromatic col
orings over all positive integers $N$ that are not prime powers.

## Setting Up the Framework

A coloring $c: D(N) \to \{1,2,3,4\}$ is **multichromatic** if whenever $a, b$ ar
e incomparable divisors (neither divides the other), the three values $c(a), c(b
), c(\gcd(a,b))$ are pairwise distinct. (The condition "$a, b, \gcd(a,b)$ pairwi
se distinct" is equivalent to $a, b$ being incomparable, since $\gcd(a,b) \mid a
$ always, so $a \neq \gcd(a,b) \iff a \nmid b$, and similarly $b \neq \gcd(a,b)
\iff b \nmid a$.)

## Step 1: $N$ has at most two distinct prime factors

Suppose $N$ has three distinct prime factors $p, q, r$. The primes are pairwise
incomparable with $\gcd = 1$, so $c(1), c(p), c(q), c(r)$ must be pairwise disti
nct — using all four colors.

Now $p$ and $qr$ are incomparable with $\gcd = 1$, so $c(pq) \neq c(r), c(1)$ (f
rom the pair $(r, pq)$). Also $pq$ and $pr$ are incomparable with $\gcd = p$, so
 $c(pq) \neq c(p)$. Since $\{c(1), c(p), c(q), c(r)\}$ exhausts all four colors
and $c(pq) \neq c(1), c(r), c(p)$, we get $c(pq) = c(q)$.

But $pq$ and $qr$ are incomparable with $\gcd = q$, requiring $c(pq), c(qr), c(q
)$ distinct — contradicting $c(pq) = c(q)$. So **no multichromatic coloring exis
ts** when $N$ has $\ge 3$ prime factors.

## Step 2: Structure for $N = p^a q^b$

Now $N = p^a q^b$ with $a, b \ge 1$. Divisors form an $(a{+}1) \times (b{+}1)$ g
rid; write $c(i,j)$ for the color of $p^i q^j$. Incomparability means $(i_1 - i_
2)(j_1 - j_2) < 0$; WLOG $i_1 > i_2, j_1 < j_2$, giving $\gcd = (i_2, j_1)$ and
the constraint:

$$c(i_1, j_1),\; c(i_2, j_2),\; c(i_2, j_1) \text{ pairwise distinct.}$$

This yields three conditions:
- **(A)** Each column $j < b$ has all $a{+}1$ entries distinct → $a \le 3$.
- **(B)** Each row $i < a$ has all $b{+}1$ entries distinct → $b \le 3$.
- **(C)** Cross-diagonal: $c(i_1, j_1) \neq c(i_2, j_2)$ for $i_1 > i_2, j_1 < j
_2$.

## Step 3: Ruling out large exponents

**$a = 3$ (or $b = 3$):** Column 0 uses all 4 colors. Then $c(0,1) \neq c(0,0)$
(row 0) and $c(0,1) \neq c(1,0), c(2,0), c(3,0)$ (condition C), so $c(0,1)$ diff
ers from all four colors — impossible. So $a, b \le 2$.

**$a = b = 2$:** By (A), $c(0,0), c(1,0), c(2,0)$ are 3 distinct colors. By (B),
 $c(0,0), c(0,1), c(0,2)$ are 3 distinct. By (C), $c(1,0) \neq c(0,1), c(0,2)$,
so $c(1,0) \notin \{c(0,0), c(0,1), c(0,2)\}$ — it's the 4th color. Then $c(2,0)
 \neq c(0,1), c(0,2)$ (condition C) and $c(2,0) \neq c(0,0), c(1,0)$ (column 0),
 so $c(2,0)$ differs from all four — impossible.

## Step 4: Counting the remaining cases

**$(a,b) = (1,1)$, $N = pq$:** Divisors $1, p, q, pq$. Only incomparable pair: $
(p,q)$, $\gcd = 1$. Constraint: $c(p), c(q), c(1)$ distinct; $c(pq)$ free.

$$\text{Count} = 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 4 = 96.$$

**$(a,b) = (2,1)$, $N = p^2 q$:** Divisors $1, p, p^2, q, pq, p^2q$. Incomparabl
e pairs:
- $(p, q)$, $\gcd = 1$: $c(p), c(q), c(1)$ distinct.
- $(p^2, q)$, $\gcd = 1$: $c(p^2), c(q), c(1)$ distinct.
- $(p^2, pq)$, $\gcd = p$: $c(p^2), c(pq), c(p)$ distinct.

$c(p^2q)$ is free (comparable to all divisors). Counting:
- $c(1)$: 4 choices
- $c(q)$: 3 choices ($\neq c(1)$)
- $c(p)$: 2 choices ($\neq c(1), c(q)$)
- $c(p^2)$: $\neq c(1), c(q)$ (constraint 2) and $\neq c(p)$ (constraint 3) → **
1 choice** (4th color)
- $c(pq)$: $\neq c(p^2), c(p)$ (constraint 3) → **2 choices**
- $c(p^2q)$: **4 choices**

$$\text{Count} = 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 4 = 192.$$

**$(a,b) = (1,2)$, $N = pq^2$:** By symmetry (swapping $p \leftrightarrow q$), a
lso **192**.

## Step 5: Conclusion

The viable cases and their counts are:

| $(a,b)$ | $N$ | Count |
|---------|-----|-------|
| $(1,1)$ | $pq$ | 96 |
| $(2,1)$ | $p^2 q$ | **192** |
| $(1,2)$ | $pq^2$ | **192** |
| all others | — | 0 |

The maximum number of multichromatic colorings is $\boxed{192}$, achieved by $N
= p^2 q$ (or $pq^2$) for any two distinct primes $p, q$.
