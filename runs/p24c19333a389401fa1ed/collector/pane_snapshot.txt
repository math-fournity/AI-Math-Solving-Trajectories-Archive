Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest positive integer $n$ such t
hat a homogeneous polynomial of degree $n$ with integer coefficients (all in $\{
-1,0,1\}$) equals 1 at all 26 points of $S = \{-1,0,1\}^3 \setminus \{(0,0,0)\}$
, then count such polynomials.

## Ruling out small $n$

**Odd $n$:** At $(1,0,0)$: $a_{(n,0,0)}=1$. At $(-1,0,0)$: $(-1)^n a_{(n,0,0)}=1
$, giving $a_{(n,0,0)}=-1$. Contradiction. So no odd $n$ works.

**$n=2$:** At $(1,0,0),(0,1,0),(0,0,1)$: $a_{200}=a_{020}=a_{002}=1$. At $(1,1,0
)$: $1+1+a_{110}=1 \Rightarrow a_{110}=-1$. At $(1,-1,0)$: $1+1-(-1)=3\neq 1$. C
ontradiction.

**$n=4$:** The 26 points form 3 orbits under signed permutations: axis (6 pts),
edge (12 pts), corner (8 pts). By even degree, sign flips on individual coordina
tes relate values, giving 13 independent constraints on 15 monomials.

- **Axis:** $a_{400}=a_{040}=a_{004}=1$
- **Edge ($z=0$):** $a_{400}+a_{220}+a_{040}=1 \Rightarrow a_{220}=-1$; similarl
y $a_{202}=a_{022}=-1$
- **Corner parity sum:** $S_{000}=a_{400}+a_{040}+a_{004}+a_{220}+a_{202}+a_{022
}=3-3=0\neq 1$. **Contradiction.**

## $n=6$ works

For $n=6$, there are 28 monomials. Parity classes (since $i+j+k=6$ even): $(0,0,
0)$ with 10 monomials, and $(1,1,0),(1,0,1),(0,1,1)$ each with 6 monomials.

**Constraints from the 26 points (13 total, all independent after substitution):
**

**Type A (axis, 3 constraints):** $a_{600}=a_{060}=a_{006}=1$

**Type B (edge, 6 constraints):** For $z=0$: even-exponent sum $a_{600}+a_{420}+
a_{240}+a_{060}=1 \Rightarrow a_{420}+a_{240}=-1$; odd-exponent sum $a_{510}+a_{
330}+a_{150}=0$. Similarly for $y=0$ and $x=0$, giving 3 pair constraints and 3
triple constraints.

**Type C (corner, 4 constraints → 3 independent after substitution):** The Walsh
-Hadamard analysis gives $S_{000}=1$, $S_{110}=S_{101}=S_{011}=0$. Substituting
the Type A and B constraints:
- $S_{000}=3+(-1)+(-1)+(-1)+a_{222}=a_{222}=1$, so $a_{222}=1$
- $S_{110}=0+(a_{312}+a_{132}+a_{114})=0 \Rightarrow a_{312}+a_{132}+a_{114}=0$
- Similarly $a_{321}+a_{123}+a_{141}=0$ and $a_{231}+a_{213}+a_{411}=0$

## Counting solutions

| Constraint type | Variables | Count | Solutions each |
|---|---|---|---|
