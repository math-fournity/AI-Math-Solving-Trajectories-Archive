## Proof

Let $n = |p^{-1}(x_0)|$. By hypothesis $n$ is a finite positive integer.

**Step 1: Index of the subgroup.** For a covering map $p:(\tilde X,\tilde x_0)\t
o(X,x_0)$, the induced homomorphism
$$p_*:\pi_1(\tilde X,\tilde x_0)\hookrightarrow \pi_1(X,x_0)$$
is injective, and the index of its image equals the cardinality of the fiber:
$$\bigl[\pi_1(X,x_0):p_*\pi_1(\tilde X,\tilde x_0)\bigr]=|p^{-1}(x_0)|=n.$$

**Step 2: Subgroups of $\mathbb Z$.** Since $\pi_1(X,x_0)\cong\mathbb Z$, every
subgroup is cyclic of the form $m\mathbb Z$ for a unique $m\ge 0$. The subgroup
$m\mathbb Z$ has index $m$ in $\mathbb Z$ (with $0\mathbb Z=\{0\}$ giving infini
te index). Because the index here is the finite number $n\ge 1$, we must have
$$p_*\pi_1(\tilde X,\tilde x_0)=n\mathbb Z.$$

**Step 3: Isomorphism.** The map $p_*$ is injective, so it gives an isomorphism
$$\pi_1(\tilde X,\tilde x_0)\;\cong\;p_*\pi_1(\tilde X,\tilde x_0)=n\mathbb Z.$$
But $n\mathbb Z\cong\mathbb Z$ (via $k\mapsto nk$).

**Conclusion.**
$$\boxed{\pi_1(\tilde X,\tilde x_0)\cong\mathbb Z.}$$
