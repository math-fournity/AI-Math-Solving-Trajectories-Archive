## Solution

**Parametrization.** The surface $S$ is the portion of the cone $z = \sqrt{x^2+y
^2}$ lying inside the cylinder $(x-1)^2+y^2=1$. Using polar coordinates $x = r\c
os\theta$, $y = r\sin\theta$, on the cone we have $z = r$.

**Region of integration.** The cylinder becomes $(r\cos\theta - 1)^2 + r^2\sin^2
\theta = 1$, i.e. $r^2 - 2r\cos\theta = 0$, giving $r = 2\cos\theta$. The interi
or requires $\cos\theta \ge 0$, so $\theta \in [-\tfrac{\pi}{2}, \tfrac{\pi}{2}]
$ and $0 \le r \le 2\cos\theta$.

**Surface element.** For $z = f(x,y) = \sqrt{x^2+y^2}$:
$$dS = \sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\par
tial z}{\partial y}\right)^2}\,dA = \sqrt{1 + \frac{x^2}{x^2+y^2} + \frac{y^2}{x
^2+y^2}}\,dA = \sqrt{2}\,dA = \sqrt{2}\,r\,dr\,d\theta.$$

**Integrand on the cone.** Since $z = r$:
$$2z^2 - x^2 - y^2 = 2r^2 - r^2 = r^2.$$

**Computing the integral:**
$$\iint_S (2z^2 - x^2 - y^2)\,dS = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\int_0^{2\cos\theta} r^2
 \cdot \sqrt{2}\,r\,dr\,d\theta = \sqrt{2}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\int_0^{2\cos\the
ta} r^3\,dr\,d\theta.$$

Inner integral:
$$\int_0^{2\cos\theta} r^3\,dr = \frac{(2\cos\theta)^4}{4} = 4\cos^4\theta.$$

So:
$$= 4\sqrt{2}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos^4\theta\,d\theta = 8\sqrt{2}\int_0^{\pi/2
}\cos^4\theta\,d\theta.$$

Using the Wallis-type formula $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos^4\theta\,d\theta
= \frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2} = \frac{3\pi}{16}$:

$$= 8\sqrt{2}\cdot\frac{3\pi}{16} = \frac{24\pi\sqrt{2}}{16} = \frac{3\pi\sqrt{2
}}{2}.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\iint_S (2z^2 - x^2 - y^2)\,dS = \frac{3\pi\sqrt{2}}{2}}$$
