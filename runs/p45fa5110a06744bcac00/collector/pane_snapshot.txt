## Answer

**No**, it does not follow in general. The convergence of each individual entry
of a triangular array to 0 in probability does **not** imply the convergence of
the Cesàro average to 0 in probability. Below is a counterexample.

---

### Counterexample

For each $n \geq 1$ and $i = 1, \ldots, n$, define the random variables on a com
mon probability space so that $\{\xi_{ni}\}_{i=1}^{n}$ are **independent**, with

$$
\xi_{ni} = \begin{cases} n, & \text{with probability } \dfrac{1}{n},\\[4pt] 0, &
 \text{with probability } 1 - \dfrac{1}{n}. \end{cases}
$$

**Step 1. Each $\xi_{ni} \xrightarrow{p} 0$.**

Fix any index $i \geq 1$ and any $\varepsilon > 0$. For all $n \geq i$,

$$
\mathbb{P}\!\bigl(|\xi_{ni}| > \varepsilon\bigr) = \mathbb{P}(\xi_{ni} = n) = \f
rac{1}{n} \xrightarrow{n \to \infty} 0.
$$

Hence $\xi_{ni} \xrightarrow{p} 0$ for every fixed $i$.

**Step 2. The average does not converge to 0 in probability.**

Consider

$$
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \xi_{ni} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} n \cdot \mathbf{1
}_{\{\xi_{ni}=n\}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbf{1}_{\{\xi_{ni}=n\}}.
$$

Let $K_n := \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbf{1}_{\{\xi_{ni}=n\}}$. Since the $\xi_{ni}$ ar
e independent Bernoulli trials each with success probability $1/n$,

$$
K_n \sim \mathrm{Binomial}\!\left(n, \frac{1}{n}\right).
$$

By the classical Poisson limit theorem (law of rare events),

$$
K_n \xrightarrow{d} \mathrm{Poisson}(1) \quad \text{as } n \to \infty.
$$

Therefore

$$
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \xi_{ni} = K_n \xrightarrow{d} \mathrm{Poisson}(1).
$$

Since $\mathrm{Poisson}(1)$ is a non-degenerate distribution (in particular $\ma
thbb{P}(\mathrm{Poisson}(1) = 0) = e^{-1} \neq 1$), the average does **not** con
verge to $0$ in probability. For instance, with $\varepsilon = 1/2$,

$$
\mathbb{P}\!\left(\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{ni}\right| > \tfrac{1}{2}\
right) = \mathbb{P}(K_n \geq 1) = 1 - \left(1 - \tfrac{1}{n}\right)^{n} \xrighta
rrow{n\to\infty} 1 - e^{-1} \neq 0.
$$

---

### Conclusion

The implication fails: even though every entry $\xi_{ni}$ of the triangular arra
y converges to $0$ in probability, the average $\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{ni
}$ converges in distribution to $\mathrm{Poisson}(1)$, and hence does **not** co
nverge to $0$ in probability. The obstruction is that the "rare but large" event
s (each $\xi_{ni}$ taking the value $n$ with probability $1/n$) are individually
 negligible but collectively contribute a non-vanishing amount to the sum. $\bla
cksquare$
