## Proof

**Claim:** The limit $\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{p_n}{n}$ exists and equals $1$.

**Setup.** Let $p_n$ denote the largest prime $\leq n$. Since $p_n \leq n$, we a
lways have $\frac{p_n}{n} \leq 1$. It suffices to show that for every $\varepsil
on > 0$, there exists $N$ such that for all $n \geq N$,
$$\frac{p_n}{n} > 1 - \varepsilon,$$
i.e., there is a prime in the interval $(n(1-\varepsilon),\, n]$.

**Using the Prime Number Theorem.** The PNT states $\pi(x) \sim \frac{x}{\ln x}$
, i.e.,
$$\lim_{x\to\infty} \frac{\pi(x)}{x/\ln x} = 1.$$

Fix $\varepsilon \in (0,1)$ and set $a = 1-\varepsilon \in (0,1)$. Consider
$$\pi(n) - \pi(an) = \frac{n}{\ln n}\cdot\frac{\pi(n)}{n/\ln n} - \frac{an}{\ln(
an)}\cdot\frac{\pi(an)}{an/\ln(an)}.$$

By PNT, $\frac{\pi(n)}{n/\ln n} \to 1$ and $\frac{\pi(an)}{an/\ln(an)} \to 1$. A
lso $\frac{\ln(an)}{\ln n} = \frac{\ln n + \ln a}{\ln n} \to 1$ since $\ln a$ is
 a fixed constant. Hence
$$\pi(n) - \pi(an) \sim \frac{n}{\ln n} - \frac{an}{\ln n} = \frac{(1-a)\,n}{\ln
 n} = \frac{\varepsilon\, n}{\ln n} \xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{} \infty.$$

In particular, there exists $N$ such that for all $n \geq N$,
$$\pi(n) - \pi(an) \geq 1.$$

**Conclusion.** For every $n \geq N$, the inequality $\pi(n) - \pi(an) \geq 1$ m
eans there is at least one prime $q$ with $an < q \leq n$. Since $p_n$ is the *l
argest* prime $\leq n$, we have $p_n \geq q > an = n(1-\varepsilon)$. Therefore
$$1 - \varepsilon < \frac{p_n}{n} \leq 1 \qquad \text{for all } n \geq N.$$

Since $\varepsilon > 0$ was arbitrary,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{p_n}{n} = 1.$$

The limit exists. $\blacksquare$
