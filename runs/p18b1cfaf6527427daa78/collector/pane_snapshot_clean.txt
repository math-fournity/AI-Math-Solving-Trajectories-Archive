## Proof

Let the three distinct positive integer frequencies be $a, b, c$, and let their
signal densities be $x = 1/a$, $y = 1/b$, $z = 1/c$. The condition states that f
or each pair, the sum of their densities is an integer multiple of the third den
sity:

$$x + y = m\,z, \quad x + z = n\,y, \quad y + z = p\,x$$

for some positive integers $m, n, p$ (positivity holds since all densities are p
ositive).

**Step 1: A constraint on $m, n, p$.**

The system is homogeneous in $x, y, z$. For a nontrivial solution to exist, the
determinant of the coefficient matrix must vanish:

$$\det\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & -m \\ 1 & -n & 1 \\ -p & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = mnp
 - m - n - p - 2 = 0.$$

Hence $\boxed{mnp = m + n + p + 2}$.

**Step 2: Find all positive integer solutions.**

Assume WLOG $m \le n \le p$.

- $m = 1$: $np = n + p + 3 \Rightarrow (n-1)(p-1) = 4$, giving $(n,p) = (2,5)$ o
r $(3,3)$. So $(1,2,5)$ and $(1,3,3)$.
- $m = 2$: $2np = n + p + 4 \Rightarrow (2n-1)(2p-1) = 9$. With $n \ge 2$: only
$(n,p) = (2,2)$, giving $(2,2,2)$.
- $m = 3$: $(3n-1)(3p-1) = 16$ with $3n-1 \ge 8$; no factorization of $16$ has b
oth factors $\ge 8$, so none.
- $m \ge 3, n \ge m$: $mnp \ge 3 \cdot 3 \cdot p = 9p$ while $m+n+p+2 \le 3p + (
m+n-2p)+2$; checking directly, $mnp$ outgrows the RHS, yielding no further solut
ions.

Thus the complete list (up to permutation) is $(1,2,5)$, $(1,3,3)$, $(2,2,2)$.

**Step 3: Check which give distinct $a, b, c$.**

- $(2,2,2)$: $x+y=2z,\ x+z=2y,\ y+z=2x$ forces $x=y=z$, i.e. $a=b=c$. Reject.
- $(1,3,3)$: $x+y=z$ and $x+z=3y$ give $2x = 2y$, so $x=y$, i.e. $a=b$. Reject.
- $(1,2,5)$: $x+y = z$, $x+z = 2y$, $y+z = 5x$. From the first two: $x + (x+y) =
 2y \Rightarrow y = 2x$, and then $z = x + y = 3x$. So $x : y : z = 1 : 2 : 3$,
i.e.

$$\frac{1}{a} : \frac{1}{b} : \frac{1}{c} = 1 : 2 : 3.$$

Thus $a = L,\ b = L/2,\ c = L/3$ for some positive integer $L$ divisible by $6$.
 The smallest admissible $L$ is $6$, giving

$$\{a, b, c\} = \{6, 3, 2\}, \quad a + b + c = 11.$$

All permutations of $(m,n,p) = (1,2,5)$ merely permute the roles, yielding the s
ame set $\{2,3,6\}$.

**Step 4: Verification.**

With $(a,b,c) = (2,3,6)$:
- $1/2 + 1/3 = 5/6 = 5 \cdot (1/6)$ — integer multiple of the third. ✓
- $1/2 + 1/6 = 2/3 = 2 \cdot (1/3)$ — integer multiple of the third. ✓
- $1/3 + 1/6 = 1/2 = 1 \cdot (1/2)$ — integer multiple of the third. ✓

All three are distinct positive integers, and no smaller sum is possible since $
(1,2,5)$ is the unique admissible triple giving distinct frequencies, and $L=6$
is its minimal realization.

**The smallest possible sum is $\boxed{11}$.**
