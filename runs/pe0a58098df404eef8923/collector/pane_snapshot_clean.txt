**Solution.**

The fractions are $\frac{k}{n+k+2}$ for $k = 19, 20, \ldots, 91$ (since the nume
rator $k$ and denominator $n+k+2$ differ by the constant $n+2$).

A fraction $\frac{k}{n+k+2}$ is irreducible iff $\gcd(k, n+k+2) = 1$. Using $\gc
d(k, n+k+2) = \gcd(k, n+2)$, the condition becomes:

$$\gcd(k,\, n+2) = 1 \quad \text{for every } k \in \{19, 20, \ldots, 91\}.$$

This is equivalent to: **$n+2$ shares no prime factor with any integer in $[19,
91]$**.

Every prime $p \le 91$ divides some integer in $[19,91]$:
- If $p \ge 19$, then $p$ itself lies in the interval.
- If $p < 19$ (i.e. $p \in \{2,3,5,7,11,13,17\}$), then a multiple $mp$ lies in
$[19,91]$: e.g. $20=2^2\!\cdot 5$, $21=3\cdot 7$, $22=2\cdot 11$, $26=2\cdot 13$
, $34=2\cdot 17$.

Hence $n+2$ must have **no prime factor $\le 91$**, i.e. every prime factor of $
n+2$ is $> 91$.

The smallest positive integer all of whose prime factors exceed $91$ is the smal
lest prime greater than $91$, which is $97$. Therefore

$$n + 2 = 97 \implies n = 95.$$

**Verification.** With $n = 95$, we have $n+2 = 97$, which is prime and larger t
han $91$. Thus $\gcd(k, 97) = 1$ for every $k \in [19,91]$, so every fraction $\
frac{k}{k+97}$ is irreducible. No smaller $n$ works, since any $n+2 < 97$ has a
prime factor $\le 91$, which divides some $k \in [19,91]$.

$$\boxed{n = 95}$$
