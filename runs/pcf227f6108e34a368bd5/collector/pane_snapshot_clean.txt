## Problem

Determine the number of $\mathbb{Q}$-field homomorphisms from $\overline{\mathbb
{Q}}$ (the algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}$) to $\mathbb{C}$ that fix $\mathbb{
Q}$ pointwise.

## Proof

**Step 1. Every such homomorphism is an automorphism of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$
over $\mathbb{Q}$.**

Let $\sigma : \overline{\mathbb{Q}} \to \mathbb{C}$ be a field homomorphism fixi
ng $\mathbb{Q}$. Since $\sigma$ is a field homomorphism, it is injective. For an
y $\alpha \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, let $f(x) \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ be its minima
l polynomial. Then $f(\alpha) = 0$, and applying $\sigma$ (which fixes $\mathbb{
Q}$) gives $f(\sigma(\alpha)) = 0$. Hence $\sigma(\alpha)$ is also a root of $f$
, so $\sigma(\alpha)$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$, i.e., $\sigma(\alpha) \in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}} \subset \mathbb{C}$. Therefore $\sigma(\overline{\mathbb{Q
}}) \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$.

Since $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ is algebraically closed and $\sigma : \overline{\m
athbb{Q}} \to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ is an injective endomorphism, the image $\s
igma(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$ is an algebraically closed subfield of $\overline{\
mathbb{Q}}$ containing $\mathbb{Q}$. But $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ is an algebraic
 extension of $\mathbb{Q}$, and $\sigma(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$ is algebraic ove
r $\mathbb{Q}$ (being a subfield of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$). Any element $\beta
 \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$, hence algebraic over
 $\sigma(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}) \supseteq \mathbb{Q}$; but $\sigma(\overline{\ma
thbb{Q}})$ is algebraically closed, so $\beta \in \sigma(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$
. Thus $\sigma(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}) = \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, and $\sigma$ is
an automorphism.

So the set of $\mathbb{Q}$-homomorphisms $\overline{\mathbb{Q}} \to \mathbb{C}$
equals $G_{\mathbb{Q}} := \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$.

**Step 2. Upper bound: $|G_{\mathbb{Q}}| \leq 2^{\aleph_0}$.**

The group $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is profinite:
$$G_{\mathbb{Q}} = \varprojlim_{K/\mathbb{Q}} \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}),$
$
where $K$ ranges over all finite Galois extensions of $\mathbb{Q}$. Since $\over
line{\mathbb{Q}}$ is countable, there are only countably many such $K$. Each $\o
peratorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ is finite. Thus $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ embeds as a clo
sed subgroup of the product $\prod_{K} \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$, a prod
uct of countably many finite groups. This product has cardinality at most $\prod
_{n=1}^{\infty} \aleph_0 = 2^{\aleph_0}$ (each factor is finite, and there are c
ountably many factors). Hence
$$|G_{\mathbb{Q}}| \leq 2^{\aleph_0}.$$

**Step 3. Lower bound: $|G_{\mathbb{Q}}| \geq 2^{\aleph_0}$.**

Fix an odd prime $p$. Consider the cyclotomic extension $\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^\inft
y}) = \bigcup_{n \geq 1} \mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^n})$, where $\mu_{p^n}$ denotes the g
roup of $p^n$-th roots of unity. This is an infinite Galois extension of $\mathb
b{Q}$ contained in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, and
$$\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^\infty})/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}_p^{
\times} \cong \mathbb{Z}/(p-1)\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}_p,$$
where $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is the ring of $p$-adic integers. The group $\mathbb{Z}_p$
is uncountable with $|\mathbb{Z}_p| = 2^{\aleph_0}$ (it is the inverse limit of
$\mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$, a closed subgroup of $\prod_n \mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{
Z}$, and surjects onto each factor; equivalently, $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is in bijection

The restriction map $G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^\in
fty})/\mathbb{Q})$ is surjective (by the extension theorem for embeddings of Gal
ois extensions). Therefore
$$|G_{\mathbb{Q}}| \geq |\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^\infty})/\mathbb{Q

**Step 4. Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 2 and 3:
$$|G_{\mathbb{Q}}| = 2^{\aleph_0}.$$

The number of $\mathbb{Q}$-field homomorphisms from $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ to $
\mathbb{C}$ fixing $\mathbb{Q}$ is $2^{\aleph_0}$, the cardinality of the contin
