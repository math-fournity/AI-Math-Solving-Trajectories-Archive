## Solution

We work in the affine chart $X_0 = 1$ near $P = (1:0:0)$. Setting $x = X_1/X_0$,
 $y = X_2/X_0$ (so $P$ becomes the origin), and dividing by $X_0^3$:

$$f = 2y - 4x^2 + xy + x^2y = y(2+x+x^2) - 4x^2$$
$$g = 4y - 4x^2 + xy - x^2y = y(4+x-x^2) - 4x^2$$

Both vanish at the origin, so $P$ is indeed an intersection point. The intersect
ion multiplicity is
$$I_P(F,G) = \dim_k k[[x,y]]/(f,g).$$

**Step 1: Solve for $y$ from $f$.** Since $2+x+x^2$ has constant term $2 \neq 0$
, it is a unit in $k[[x,y]]$. From $f=0$:
$$y = \frac{4x^2}{2+x+x^2} = 4x^2 \cdot u(x), \quad u(x) := \frac{1}{2+x+x^2} \i
n k[[x]]^\times,\ u(0)=\tfrac12.$$

**Step 2: Substitute into $g$.** Using $y = 4x^2 u(x)$ in $g = y(4+x-x^2) - 4x^2
$:
$$g = 4x^2 u(x)(4+x-x^2) - 4x^2 = 4x^2\bigl[u(x)(4+x-x^2) - 1\bigr].$$

Let $v(x) = u(x)(4+x-x^2) - 1$. Its constant term is
$$v(0) = \tfrac12 \cdot 4 - 1 = 1 \neq 0,$$
so $v(x) \in k[[x]]^\times$ is a unit. Therefore $g = 4x^2 \cdot (\text{unit})$,
 and the ideal $(f,g)$ is equivalent (after eliminating $y$ via $f$) to $(y - 4x
^2 u(x),\ x^2)$, since $4x^2 v(x)$ generates the same ideal as $x^2$.

**Step 3: Compute the dimension.** With $y$ expressed in terms of $x$ and $x^2 =
 0$ imposed:
$$k[[x,y]]/(f,g) \cong k[[x]]/(x^2),$$
which has $k$-basis $\{1, x\}$, hence dimension $2$.

**Sanity check (tangent cones).** The lowest-degree homogeneous parts are $\oper
atorname{in}(f) = 2y$ and $\operatorname{in}(g) = 4y$, both giving the tangent l
ine $y = 0$. Since the tangent lines coincide, the curves are tangent at $P$, fo
rcing multiplicity $\geq 2$ — consistent with our result.

$$\boxed{I_{(1:0:0)}(F,G) = 2}$$
