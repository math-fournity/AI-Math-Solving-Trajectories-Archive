## Proof

We want to compute $\frac{\partial f}{\partial X}$ for $f(X) = \log\det(XX^T A^{
-1})$, where $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times r}$ and $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$.

**Step 1: Separate the constant part.**

Using $\det(BC)=\det(B)\det(C)$:

$$f(X)=\log\det(XX^T A^{-1})=\log\!\big(\det(XX^T)\det(A^{-1})\big)=\log\det(XX^
T)+\log\det(A^{-1}).$$

Since $A$ is constant, $\log\det(A^{-1})$ is constant, so

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial X}=\frac{\partial}{\partial X}\log\det(XX^T).$$

(We assume $r\ge n$ and $X$ has full row rank so that $XX^T$ is invertible.)

**Step 2: Differential of $\log\det$.**

Let $B=XX^T$ (symmetric, $n\times n$). The standard identity gives

$$d(\log\det B)=\operatorname{tr}(B^{-1}\,dB).$$

**Step 3: Differential of $B=XX^T$.**

$$dB=dX\,X^T+X\,dX^T.$$

**Step 4: Combine.**

$$df=\operatorname{tr}\!\big(B^{-1}(dX\,X^T+X\,dX^T)\big)=\operatorname{tr}(B^{-
1}dX\,X^T)+\operatorname{tr}(B^{-1}X\,dX^T).$$

*First term*, using cyclicity of trace:

$$\operatorname{tr}(B^{-1}dX\,X^T)=\operatorname{tr}(X^T B^{-1}\,dX).$$

*Second term*, using $\operatorname{tr}(M)=\operatorname{tr}(M^T)$:

$$\operatorname{tr}(B^{-1}X\,dX^T)=\operatorname{tr}(dX^T B^{-1}X)=\operatorname
{tr}\!\big((B^{-1}X)^T dX\big)=\operatorname{tr}(X^T B^{-T}dX).$$

Since $B=XX^T$ is symmetric, $B^{-T}=B^{-1}$, so the second term equals $\operat
orname{tr}(X^T B^{-1}dX)$.

**Step 5: Identify the gradient.**

$$df=2\operatorname{tr}(X^T B^{-1}\,dX)=\operatorname{tr}\!\big((2B^{-1}X)^T dX\
big),$$

where we used $B^{-1}$ symmetric so $(2B^{-1}X)^T=2X^T B^{-1}$.

By the identification $df=\operatorname{tr}(G^T\,dX)\Rightarrow \frac{\partial f
}{\partial X}=G$:

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial f}{\partial X}=2\,(XX^T)^{-1}X.}$$

**Verification (scalar case).** Let $n=1$, so $X=x^T\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times r}$,
$A=a$. Then $f=\log(\|x\|^2/a)$, giving $\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}=\frac{2x}
{\|x\|^2}$. The formula yields $2(XX^T)^{-1}X=2\frac{x^T}{\|x\|^2}=\frac{2x}{\|x
\|^2}$ (as a row). ✓
