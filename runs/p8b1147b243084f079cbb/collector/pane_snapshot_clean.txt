The statement is **true** for $1 \leq p < \infty$. Here is the proof.

---

**Theorem.** Let $1 \leq p < \infty$, let $q$ be the conjugate exponent defined
by $\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1$ (so $q=\infty$ when $p=1$), and let $\mu$ be Lebe
sgue measure on $\mathbb{R}$. Then for every $f\in L^p(\mathbb{R})$,
$$
\|f\|_p \;=\; \sup\Bigl\{\,\int_{\mathbb{R}} fg\,d\mu \;:\; g\in L^q(\mathbb{R})
,\ \|g\|_q\le 1\,\Bigr\}.
$$

*Proof.* The case $f=0$ a.e. is trivial (both sides equal $0$), so assume $f\neq
 0$ in $L^p$.

**Upper bound ($\sup \le \|f\|_p$).** Let $g\in L^q(\mathbb{R})$ with $\|g\|_q\l
e 1$. Since $fg\in L^1$ by Hölder's inequality,
$$
\Bigl|\int_{\mathbb{R}} fg\,d\mu\Bigr| \;\le\; \int_{\mathbb{R}} |fg|\,d\mu \;\l
e\; \|f\|_p\,\|g\|_q \;\le\; \|f\|_p.
$$
Taking the supremum over all such $g$ gives $\sup \le \|f\|_p$.

**Lower bound ($\sup \ge \|f\|_p$).** We exhibit a witness $g\in L^q$ with $\|g\
|_q\le 1$ and $\int fg\,d\mu=\|f\|_p$.

*Case $p=1$, $q=\infty$.* Define
$$
g(x)=\operatorname{sgn}(f(x)) = \begin{cases} \overline{f(x)}/|f(x)| & f(x)\neq
0,\\ 0 & f(x)=0.\end{cases}
$$
Then $|g(x)|\le 1$ everywhere, so $g\in L^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ with $\|g\|_\infty
\le 1$, and
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}} fg\,d\mu = \int_{\mathbb{R}} |f|\,d\mu = \|f\|_1.
$$

*Case $1<p<\infty$, $1<q<\infty$.* Set
$$
g(x)=\frac{|f(x)|^{p-1}\operatorname{sgn}(f(x))}{\|f\|_p^{\,p-1}}.
$$
First, $g\in L^q$: using $(p-1)q=p$ (which follows from $q=\frac{p}{p-1}$),
$$
\|g\|_q^q = \int_{\mathbb{R}} |g|^q\,d\mu = \frac{1}{\|f\|_p^{(p-1)q}}\int_{\mat
hbb{R}} |f|^{(p-1)q}\,d\mu = \frac{1}{\|f\|_p^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}} |f|^{p}\,d\m
u = \frac{\|f\|_p^{p}}{\|f\|_p^{p}} = 1,
$$
so $\|g\|_q=1\le 1$. Second,
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}} fg\,d\mu = \frac{1}{\|f\|_p^{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}} |f|^{p}\,d
\mu = \frac{\|f\|_p^{p}}{\|f\|_p^{p-1}} = \|f\|_p.
$$

Combining the two cases, in each instance we have found $g\in L^q$ with $\|g\|_q
\le 1$ achieving $\int fg\,d\mu=\|f\|_p$, so the supremum is at least $\|f\|_p$.

Together with the upper bound, $\sup = \|f\|_p$. $\blacksquare$
