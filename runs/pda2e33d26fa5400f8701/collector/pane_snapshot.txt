## Proof

Let $f(x)$ be a separable, irreducible polynomial of degree $n$ over a field $F$
, and let $E$ be its splitting field over $F$. We show that $[E:F] \leq n!$, and
 this bound is sharp.

**Construction of the splitting field by successive adjunctions.**

Let $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \ldots, \alpha_n$ be the $n$ distinct roots of $f$ in $
E$ (distinct because $f$ is separable). Since $f$ is irreducible, adjoining any
single root gives an extension of degree $n$:

$$[F(\alpha_1):F] = n.$$

Now we build up to $E$ step by step. Set $F_0 = F$ and $F_1 = F(\alpha_1)$. Over
 $F_1$, the polynomial $f$ factors as

$$f(x) = (x - \alpha_1) \cdot g_1(x),$$

where $g_1(x) \in F_1[x]$ has degree $n - 1$. The remaining roots $\alpha_2, \ld
ots, \alpha_n$ are all roots of $g_1$.

Adjoin $\alpha_2$ to $F_1$. Since $g_1$ has degree $n-1$, the minimal polynomial
 of $\alpha_2$ over $F_1$ divides $g_1$, so

$$[F_1(\alpha_2):F_1] \leq n - 1.$$

Set $F_2 = F_1(\alpha_2) = F(\alpha_1, \alpha_2)$. Over $F_2$, we have

$$f(x) = (x-\alpha_1)(x-\alpha_2)\cdot g_2(x),$$

with $g_2(x) \in F_2[x]$ of degree $n-2$. Adjoining $\alpha_3$ gives

$$[F_2(\alpha_3):F_2] \leq n - 2.$$

Continuing inductively, at the $k$-th step ($1 \leq k \leq n$) we have already a
djoined $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_{k-1}$, the remaining factor $g_{k-1}(x)$ over
 $F_{k-1}$ has degree $n - (k-1)$, and

$$[F_{k-1}(\alpha_k):F_{k-1}] \leq n - (k-1).$$

**Degree bound.**

After adjoining all $n$ roots, we obtain $F_n = F(\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n) =
E$. By the tower law,

$$[E:F] = [F_n:F_{n-1}]\cdot[F_{n-1}:F_{n-2}]\cdots[F_1:F_0] \leq 1 \cdot 2 \cdo
ts (n-1) \cdot n = n!.$$

(The last factor $[F_n:F_{n-1}] \leq 1$ since after adjoining $n-1$ roots, the l
ast root is already determined, and in fact $[F_n:F_{n-1}]=1$.)

Therefore,

$$\boxed{[E:F] \leq n!}.$$

**Sharpness of the bound.**

The bound $n!$ is attained in general. For example, take $F = \mathbb{Q}$ and $f
(x)$ to be a "generic" polynomial of degree $n$ (e.g., $f(x) = x^n - x - 1$ for
suitable $n$, or more rigorously, a polynomial whose Galois group over $\mathbb{
Q}$ is the full symmetric group $S_n$). Such polynomials exist for every $n$: fo
r instance, $x^n - x - 1$ has Galois group $S_n$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ for all $n \g
eq 2$ (a classical result). When the Galois group is $S_n$, the splitting field
degree equals $|S_n| = n!$.

Thus $n!$ is the tight upper bound for the degree of the splitting field of a se
parable irreducible polynomial of degree $n$.
