## Proof

$$L=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\right)^{1/(n(n+1))}.$$

Since $\binom{n}{0}=1$, we may write $P_n:=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}=\prod_{k=
0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}$, and $L=\exp\!\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log P_n}{n(n+1)}
\right)$.

**Asymptotics of $\log\binom{n}{k}$.** Using Stirling's approximation $\log(m!)=
m\log m-m+O(\log m)$,

$$\log\binom{n}{k}=\log(n!)-\log(k!)-\log((n-k)!)$$
$$=n\log n-n-(k\log k-k)-((n-k)\log(n-k)-(n-k))+O(\log n)$$
$$=n\log n-k\log k-(n-k)\log(n-k)+O(\log n).$$

Setting $x=k/n\in(0,1)$ and expanding $\log(k)=\log n+\log x$, $\log(n-k)=\log n
+\log(1-x)$:

$$n\log n-k\log k-(n-k)\log(n-k)=n\log n-nx(\log n+\log x)-n(1-x)(\log n+\log(1-
x))$$
$$=n\log n\bigl[1-x-(1-x)\bigr]-n\bigl[x\log x+(1-x)\log(1-x)\bigr]$$
$$=n\,H(x),$$

where $H(x):=-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x)$ is the binary entropy (natural log). Thus,
 uniformly in $k$,

$$\log\binom{n}{k}=n\,H(k/n)+O(\log n).$$

**Summing.** Therefore

$$\log P_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log\binom{n}{k}=n\sum_{k=1}^{n}H(k/n)+O(n\log n).$$

The sum is a Riemann sum for the continuous function $H$ on $[0,1]$ (note $H(0)=
H(1)=0$ by continuity, so including/excluding endpoints is harmless):

$$\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}H(k/n)\;\longrightarrow\;\int_{0}^{1}H(x)\,dx.$$

**Evaluating the integral.** By symmetry $H(x)=H(1-x)$,

$$\int_{0}^{1}H(x)\,dx=-2\int_{0}^{1}x\log x\,dx.$$

Integrating by parts, $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}x\log x\,dx=\Bigl[\tfrac{x^{2}}{
2}\log x\Bigr]_{0}^{1}-\int_{0}^{1}\tfrac{x}{2}\,dx=0-\tfrac{1}{4}=-\tfrac{1}{4}
.$

Hence $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}H(x)\,dx=-2\cdot\bigl(-\tfrac{1}{4}\bigr)=\tfrac
{1}{2}.$

$$\log P_n=n^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}H(k/n)+O(n\log n)=n^{2}\left(\frac
{1}{2}+o(1)\right)+O(n\log n).$$

Since $O(n\log n)=o(n^{2})$,

$$\frac{\log P_n}{n(n+1)}=\frac{\frac{1}{2}n^{2}+o(n^{2})}{n^{2}+n}\longrightarr

Therefore

$$L=\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=\sqrt{e}.$$
