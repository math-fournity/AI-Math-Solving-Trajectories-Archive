where $\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor$. Since $10^{j-1}\log_{10}(n)=\log_{10}(n^{10^{j
-1}})$, setting $k=10^{j-1}$, $\alpha=1/k=10^{1-j}$, and $\delta=1/(10k)=10^{-j}

$$\{\log_{10}(n^k)\}\in[g\delta,\,(g+1)\delta).$$
This holds iff $n^k\in[10^{m+g\delta},\,10^{m+(g+1)\delta})$ for some non-negati

r$ and $\theta\in[0,\alpha)$, the count is
$$v_g(N)=\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\#\bigl(\mathbb{Z}\cap I_m\bigr)+\#\bigl(\mathbb{Z}\cap
 I_M\cap[1,N]\bigr).$$
The number of integers in $[A,B)$ is $(B-A)+\{A\}-\{B\}$, with error in $(-1,1)$

$$\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\bigl(10^{m\alpha+(g+1)\delta}-10^{m\alpha+g\delta}\bigr)=(10^
\delta-1)\,10^{g\delta}\,\frac{10^{M\alpha}-1}{10^\alpha-1}.$$
Set $C:=\frac{(10^\delta-1)\,10^{g\delta}}{10^\alpha-1}$. The total error over $
M$ decades is $O(M)=O(\log N)$, and the partial-decade error is $O(1)$. Since $N
\geq 10^{M\alpha}$, we have $M/N=O(\log N/N)\to 0$.

**The limiting function.** Define $f:[0,\alpha)\to\mathbb{R}$ by:

$$f(\theta)=C\cdot 10^{\alpha-\theta}.$$

One verifies $f$ is continuous at $\theta=g\delta$ and $\theta=(g+1)\delta$, and

**Derivative analysis.** In Segment 2:

- **Global minimum** at $\theta=g\delta$: $f_{\min}=C\cdot 10^{-g\delta}=\dfrac{

**The interval has positive length.** The length is:
$$f_{\max}-f_{\min}=\frac{(10^\delta-1)(10^{9\delta}-1)}{10^{10\delta}-1}.$$

$$\frac{v_g(N)}{N}=f(\theta_N)+O\!\left(\frac{\log N}{N}\right).$$

Since this interval contains more than one point, $v_g(N)/N$ does not converge a
