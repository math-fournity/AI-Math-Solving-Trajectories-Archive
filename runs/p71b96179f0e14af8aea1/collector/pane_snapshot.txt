## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. If $f \in L^1(-\pi,\pi)$ satisfies $\hat f(n) = 0$ for every $n
\in \mathbb{Z}$, then $f = 0$ almost everywhere on $(-\pi,\pi)$.

**Proof.** Recall the partial Fourier sums and the Fejér (Cesàro) means:
$$
S_N f(\theta) = \sum_{|k|\le N} \hat f(k)\, e^{ik\theta}, \qquad
\sigma_N f(\theta) = \frac{1}{N+1}\sum_{m=0}^{N} S_m f(\theta).
$$

Since $\hat f(k) = 0$ for every $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, we have $S_m f \equiv 0$ for
 every $m \ge 0$, and consequently
$$
\sigma_N f(\theta) \equiv 0 \quad \text{for every } N \ge 0. \tag{1}
$$

Now we use the standard fact that the Fejér kernel
$$
F_N(\theta) = \frac{1}{N+1}\sum_{m=0}^{N} D_m(\theta) = \frac{1}{N+1}\frac{\sin^
2\!\bigl(\tfrac{N+1}{2}\theta\bigr)}{\sin^2\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\theta\bigr)}
$$
forms an **approximate identity** on the torus $\mathbb{T} = (-\pi,\pi]$. Concre
tely, $F_N$ satisfies:
1. $F_N \ge 0$,
2. $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} F_N(\theta)\,d\theta = 1$ for all $N$,
3. For every $\delta > 0$, $\int_{\delta \le |\theta| \le \pi} F_N(\theta)\,d\th
eta \to 0$ as $N \to \infty$.

The Fejér means are the convolution $\sigma_N f = f * F_N$ (with the normalized
measure $\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}$). A classical theorem of Fejér–Lebesgue states:

> **(Fejér's theorem, $L^1$ form.)** For every $f \in L^1(\mathbb{T})$, the Fejé
r means $\sigma_N f$ converge to $f$ in $L^1$-norm:
> $$\lim_{N\to\infty} \|\sigma_N f - f\|_{L^1} = 0.$$

This follows directly from properties (1)–(3): writing
$$
\sigma_N f(\theta) - f(\theta) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \bigl[f(\theta-
\varphi) - f(\theta)\bigr]\,F_N(\varphi)\,d\varphi,
$$
splitting the integral into $|\varphi|<\delta$ and $|\varphi|\ge\delta$, using t
he $L^1$-continuity of translations for the first piece and the uniform bound $\
|f\|_1$ together with property (3) for the second, and then sending $\delta \to
0$.

Applying this theorem to our $f$ and using (1):
$$
\|f\|_{L^1} = \|\sigma_N f - f\|_{L^1} \xrightarrow{N\to\infty} 0.
$$
(The first equality holds because $\sigma_N f \equiv 0$.) Hence $\|f\|_{L^1(-\pi
,\pi)} = 0$, which forces $f = 0$ almost everywhere on $(-\pi,\pi)$. $\blacksqua
re$
