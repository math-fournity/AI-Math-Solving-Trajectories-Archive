## Proof

**Construction of the Cantor measure.** The Cantor set $C \subset [0,1]$ is the
set of all numbers of the form
$$t = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2a_n}{3^n}, \qquad a_n \in \{0,1\},$$
where the ternary expansion uses only digits $0$ and $2$. The Cantor measure $m$
 is the unique probability measure on $C$ obtained by pushing forward the fair-c
oin product measure $\big(\tfrac12\delta_0+\tfrac12\delta_1\big)^{\mathbb N}$ un
der the above map. Equivalently, $m$ is the distribution of the random variable
$$T = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2\xi_n}{3^n},$$
where $(\xi_n)_{n\ge 1}$ are i.i.d. Bernoulli random variables with $\mathbb P(\
xi_n=0)=\mathbb P(\xi_n=1)=\tfrac12$.

**Computing the Fourier transform.** By independence and the product structure o
f the series (which converges uniformly, justifying the interchange of expectati
on and the infinite product),
$$F(x) = \int_{[0,1]} e^{itx}\,dm(t) = \mathbb E\!\left[e^{ixT}\right] = \mathbb
 E\!\left[\prod_{n=1}^{\infty} \exp\!\left(\frac{2ix\,\xi_n}{3^n}\right)\right]
= \prod_{n=1}^{\infty} \mathbb E\!\left[\exp\!\left(\frac{2ix\,\xi_n}{3^n}\right
)\right].$$

For each $n$, since $\xi_n$ is fair Bernoulli,
$$\mathbb E\!\left[\exp\!\left(\frac{2ix\,\xi_n}{3^n}\right)\right] = \frac12\!\
left(1 + e^{\,2ix/3^n}\right).$$

Using the identity $\frac12(1+e^{i\theta}) = e^{i\theta/2}\cos(\theta/2)$ with $
\theta = 2x/3^n$,
$$\frac12\!\left(1 + e^{\,2ix/3^n}\right) = e^{\,ix/3^n}\cos\!\left(\frac{x}{3^n
}\right).$$

Therefore
$$F(x) = \prod_{n=1}^{\infty} e^{\,ix/3^n}\cos\!\left(\frac{x}{3^n}\right) = \ex
p\!\left(ix\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3^n}\right)\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\cos\!\lef
t(\frac{x}{3^n}\right).$$

The geometric series sums to
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3^n} = \frac{1/3}{1-1/3} = \frac12.$$

Hence
$$\boxed{\,F(x) = e^{\,ix/2}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\cos\!\left(\frac{x}{3^n}\right)
\,}.$$

**Verification of key properties.**
- $F(0)=1$, as expected for a probability measure.
- The infinite product converges absolutely since $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|1-\cos(x/
3^n)| \le \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^2}{2\cdot 9^n} < \infty$.
- $|F(x)| = \prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\cos(x/3^n)\right|$, which does not decay
to $0$ as $|x|\to\infty$ (e.g. along $x=3^k\pi$ the partial product is bounded a
way from $0$), reflecting the well-known fact that the Cantor measure is singula
r with respect to Lebesgue measure — a measure with an $L^1$ density would have
Fourier transform vanishing at infinity by the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma.
