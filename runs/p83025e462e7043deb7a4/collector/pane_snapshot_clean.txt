## Proof

**Claim:** There exists a non-transitive embedding of $S_4$ into $S_8$.

**Construction.** Partition the set $\{1,2,\dots,8\}$ into two disjoint blocks o
f size 4:
$$\Omega_1 = \{1,2,3,4\}, \qquad \Omega_2 = \{5,6,7,8\}.$$
Define a map
$$\varphi \colon S_4 \longrightarrow S_8$$
as follows. Identify $S_4$ with $\operatorname{Sym}(\{1,2,3,4\})$ in the usual w
ay, and fix a bijection $f \colon \{1,2,3,4\} \to \{5,6,7,8\}$ (say $f(i) = i+4$
). For $\sigma \in S_4$, let $\varphi(\sigma)$ be the permutation of $\{1,\dots,
8\}$ that acts as $\sigma$ on $\Omega_1$ and as $f \circ \sigma \circ f^{-1}$ on
 $\Omega_2$. Equivalently,
$$\varphi(\sigma) = (\sigma,\; f\sigma f^{-1}) \in \operatorname{Sym}(\Omega_1)
\times \operatorname{Sym}(\Omega_2) \cong S_4 \times S_4 \;\leq\; S_8.$$

**$\varphi$ is a homomorphism.** For $\sigma, \tau \in S_4$,
$$\varphi(\sigma\tau) = (\sigma\tau,\; f\sigma\tau f^{-1}) = (\sigma,\,f\sigma f
^{-1})\cdot(\tau,\,f\tau f^{-1}) = \varphi(\sigma)\,\varphi(\tau),$$
since the two components act on disjoint sets and hence commute componentwise. T
hus $\varphi$ is a homomorphism.

**$\varphi$ is injective (hence an embedding).** If $\varphi(\sigma) = \mathrm{i
d}$, then in particular $\sigma$ acts as the identity on $\Omega_1 = \{1,2,3,4\}
$, so $\sigma = e$. Therefore $\ker\varphi = \{e\}$, and $\varphi$ is an injecti
ve homomorphism $S_4 \hookrightarrow S_8$.

**$\varphi$ is non-transitive.** The action of $S_4$ on $\{1,\dots,8\}$ via $\va
rphi$ preserves the two blocks $\Omega_1$ and $\Omega_2$: every $\varphi(\sigma)
$ maps $\Omega_1$ to itself and $\Omega_2$ to itself. Hence there are (at least)
 two orbits, $\Omega_1$ and $\Omega_2$, each of size 4. The action is therefore
not transitive.

**Conclusion.** The homomorphism $\varphi \colon S_4 \hookrightarrow S_8$ constr
ucted above is an injective group homomorphism whose induced action on $\{1,\dot
s,8\}$ has two orbits. Hence $\varphi$ is a non-transitive embedding of $S_4$ in
to $S_8$.
