## Proof

**Claim:** The quotient group $(\mathbb{R},+)/(\mathbb{Q},+)$ contains **no** no
n-zero element of order 10.

**Key observation:** $\mathbb{Q}$ is divisible as an abelian group, and more con
cretely, $\mathbb{Q}$ is closed under division by every positive integer.

**Proof.** Suppose, for contradiction, that $[r] = r + \mathbb{Q} \in \mathbb{R}
/\mathbb{Q}$ is a non-zero element of order 10. By definition of order:

1. $[r] \neq 0$, i.e., $r \notin \mathbb{Q}$.
2. $10[r] = [0]$, i.e., $10r \in \mathbb{Q}$.

From condition (2), $10r \in \mathbb{Q}$. Since $\mathbb{Q}$ is a field (in part
icular, closed under multiplication by $\frac{1}{10} \in \mathbb{Q}$), we comput
e:

$$r = \frac{1}{10} \cdot (10r) \in \mathbb{Q}.$$

This contradicts condition (1), which requires $r \notin \mathbb{Q}$.

**Generalization.** The same argument shows that $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}$ is **to
rsion-free**: for any positive integer $n$ and any $r \in \mathbb{R}$, if $nr \i
n \mathbb{Q}$, then $r = \frac{1}{n}(nr) \in \mathbb{Q}$, so $[r] = 0$. Thus $\m
athbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}$ has no non-zero elements of finite order whatsoever.

**Conclusion.** Since every element of finite order in $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}$ i
s the identity, there is no non-zero element of order 10 (or any finite order).
$\blacksquare$
