## Proof

**Answer: Yes, it is possible.**

**Construction.** Let $\{q_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ be an enumeration of the rational n
umbers in $[0,1]$. Define

$$f(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} 2^{-n}\,(x - q_n)_+^{\,1/3}, \qquad x \in [0,1],$$

where $(t)_+ = \max(t, 0)$.

**$f$ is well-defined and continuous.** Each term satisfies $0 \le 2^{-n}(x-q_n)
_+^{1/3} \le 2^{-n}$ on $[0,1]$. Since $\sum 2^{-n} < \infty$, the series conver
ges uniformly by the Weierstrass $M$-test. Each term is continuous, so $f$ is co
ntinuous.

**$f$ is non-decreasing (monotone).** Each function $g_n(x) = (x - q_n)_+^{1/3}$
 is non-decreasing: it equals $0$ for $x \le q_n$ and $(x-q_n)^{1/3}$ for $x \ge
 q_n$, both of which are non-decreasing. A sum of non-negative multiples of non-
decreasing functions is non-decreasing, so $f$ is non-decreasing.

**$f'$ is unbounded on every interval.** Let $I \subset [0,1]$ be any non-degene
rate open interval. Since the rationals are dense, there exists some $q_n \in I$
. Choose $\delta > 0$ so that $(q_n,\, q_n + \delta) \subset I$.

Write $f(x) = 2^{-n}(x - q_n)_+^{1/3} + R_n(x)$, where $R_n(x) = \sum_{m \ne n}
2^{-m}(x-q_m)_+^{1/3}$ is non-decreasing (being a sum of non-decreasing function
s).

By Lebesgue's theorem, a monotone function is differentiable almost everywhere.
Thus $f'$ exists a.e. and $R_n'$ exists a.e. At any point $x \in (q_n, q_n+\delt
a)$ where both derivatives exist,

$$f'(x) = \frac{2^{-n}}{3}\,(x - q_n)^{-2/3} + R_n'(x).$$

Since $R_n$ is non-decreasing, $R_n'(x) \ge 0$ a.e. Therefore

$$f'(x) \;\ge\; \frac{2^{-n}}{3}\,(x - q_n)^{-2/3}.$$

As $x \to q_n^+$, the right-hand side tends to $+\infty$. Since $f'$ exists at a
lmost every point of $(q_n, q_n + \delta)$, the values of $f'$ on this subinterv
al are arbitrarily large. Hence

$$\sup\{f'(x) : x \in I,\; f'(x) \text{ exists}\} = +\infty.$$

Since $I$ was an arbitrary open subinterval of $[0,1]$, the derivative of $f$ is
 unbounded on every interval. $\blacksquare$
