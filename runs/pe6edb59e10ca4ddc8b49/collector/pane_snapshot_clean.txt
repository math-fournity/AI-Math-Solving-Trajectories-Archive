Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a genus $g > 0$ curve over
an algebraically closed field (char $\neq 2$) always has a non-trivial 2-torsion
 line bundle.

**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

Let $C$ be a nonsingular irreducible curve of genus $g > 0$ over an algebraicall
y closed field $k$ with $\operatorname{char}(k) \neq 2$.

**Step 1: Reduce to $\operatorname{Pic}^0(C)[2]$.** A line bundle $L$ with $L^{\
otimes 2} \cong \mathcal{O}_C$ satisfies $2\deg(L) = \deg(L^{\otimes 2}) = 0$, s
o $\deg(L) = 0$ and $L \in \operatorname{Pic}^0(C)$. Thus the 2-torsion line bun
dles are exactly $\operatorname{Pic}^0(C)[2] = \ker\bigl([2]: J(C) \to J(C)\bigr
)$, where $J(C) = \operatorname{Pic}^0(C)$ is the Jacobian, an abelian variety o
f dimension $g$.

**Step 2: Compute $J(C)[2]$ via the Kummer sequence.** Since $2$ is invertible i
n $k$, the Kummer sequence of étale sheaves
$$0 \to \mu_2 \to \mathbb{G}_m \xrightarrow{(\cdot)^2} \mathbb{G}_m \to 0$$
is exact. The associated long exact sequence in étale cohomology gives:
$$0 \to H^0_{\text{ét}}(C, \mathbb{G}_m)\,/\,H^0_{\text{ét}}(C, \mathbb{G}_m)^2
\to H^1_{\text{ét}}(C, \mu_2) \to \operatorname{Pic}(C)[2] \to 0.$$

Since $k$ is algebraically closed, $H^0_{\text{ét}}(C, \mathbb{G}_m) = k^*$ and
$k^{*2} = k^*$ (every element of an algebraically closed field is a square). The
refore the left term vanishes, and we obtain an isomorphism:
$$H^1_{\text{ét}}(C, \mu_2) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \operatorname{Pic}(C)[2].$$

**Step 3: Compute $H^1_{\text{ét}}(C, \mu_2)$.** Over an algebraically closed fi
eld of characteristic $\neq 2$, the étale sheaf $\mu_2$ is isomorphic to the con
stant sheaf $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. Therefore:
$$H^1_{\text{ét}}(C, \mu_2) \cong H^1_{\text{ét}}(C, \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}) \co
ng \operatorname{Hom}_{\text{cont}}\bigl(\pi_1^{\text{ét}}(C),\, \mathbb{Z}/2\ma
thbb{Z}\bigr).$$

For a smooth projective curve of genus $g$ over an algebraically closed field of
 characteristic $p > 0$ (with $p \neq 2$), the prime-to-$p$ quotient of the étal
e fundamental group is isomorphic to the prime-to-$p$ completion of the topologi
cal fundamental group of a Riemann surface of genus $g$. Since $2 \neq p$, the m
od-2 abelianization is:
$$H^1_{\text{ét}}(C, \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2g}
.$$

(In characteristic $0$, this follows from the comparison theorem with singular c
ohomology; in characteristic $p \neq 2$, it follows from the fact that $[\ell]:
A \to A$ is a finite étale separable isogeny of degree $\ell^{2g}$ for any abeli
an variety $A$ of dimension $g$ and any $\ell \neq p$, so $A[\ell] \cong (\mathb
b{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{2g}$ as an étale group scheme over an algebraically closed
 field.)

**Step 4: Conclude.** Combining Steps 2 and 3:
$$\operatorname{Pic}(C)[2] \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2g}, \qquad |\operato
rname{Pic}(C)[2]| = 2^{2g}.$$

For $g > 0$, we have $2^{2g} \geq 4 > 1$. Since the trivial line bundle $\mathca
l{O}_C$ accounts for exactly one element, there are $2^{2g} - 1 \geq 3$ non-triv
ial line bundles $L$ with $L^{\otimes 2} \cong \mathcal{O}_C$.

Therefore, **yes**, for every genus $g > 0$ nonsingular irreducible curve over a
n algebraically closed field where $2$ is invertible, there always exists a non-
trivial line bundle $L$ such that $L^{\otimes 2} = \mathcal{O}$. $\blacksquare$
