## Proof

**Simplify the exponent.** We compute:

$$
(n+1)!(n+1) - n!\cdot n.
$$

Since $(n+1)! = (n+1)\,n!$, we get

$$
(n+1)!(n+1) = (n+1)^2\, n!,
$$

so the exponent equals

$$
(n+1)^2\, n! - n\cdot n! = n!\bigl[(n+1)^2 - n\bigr] = n!\bigl[n^2 + n + 1\bigr]
.
$$

Thus the expression becomes

$$
\frac{n+1}{n}\; z^{\,n!(n^2+n+1)}.
$$

**Behavior of the prefactor.** Clearly

$$
\frac{n+1}{n} = 1 + \frac{1}{n} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 1.
$$

**Behavior of the power.** The exponent $E_n = n!(n^2+n+1)$ satisfies $E_n \to +
\infty$ as $n\to\infty$ (since $n!\to\infty$ and $n^2+n+1\ge 1$). Given $|z|>1$,
 we have

$$
\bigl|z^{E_n}\bigr| = |z|^{E_n} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} +\infty,
$$

because $|z|>1$ raised to an exponent tending to $+\infty$ diverges to $+\infty$
.

**Combining.** The modulus of the full expression is

$$
\left|\frac{n+1}{n}\,z^{E_n}\right| = \frac{n+1}{n}\,|z|^{E_n}.
$$

Both factors are positive; the first tends to $1$ and the second tends to $+\inf
ty$. Hence

$$
\left|\frac{n+1}{n}\,z^{E_n}\right| \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} +\infty.
$$

A complex sequence whose modulus tends to $+\infty$ diverges to infinity; in par
ticular it has no finite limit.

**Conclusion.** For $|z|>1$,

$$
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n+1}{n}\,z^{(n+1)!(n+1)-n!\,n} = \infty,}
$$

i.e., the limit diverges (the modulus grows without bound).
