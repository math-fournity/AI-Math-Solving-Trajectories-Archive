Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a tamely ramified extension
 $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ of degree $n$ must contain an intermediate field of degree $q$
 over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, where $q \mid n$ and $q \neq p$.

**Answer: No.** I'll construct an explicit counterexample.

## Counterexample

Let $p = 2$, $q = 3$. Set $K_0 = \mathbb{Q}_2(\zeta_3)$ and $K = K_0(\alpha)$ wh
ere $\alpha^3 = 2\zeta_3$.

### Verification that $K/\mathbb{Q}_2$ is tamely ramified of degree $n = 6$

**$K_0/\mathbb{Q}_2$ is unramified of degree 2.** Since $\zeta_3$ is a primitive
 3rd root of unity and $\operatorname{ord}_3(2) = 2$ (as $2^2 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$
 but $2 \not\equiv 1$), the extension $\mathbb{Q}_2(\zeta_3)/\mathbb{Q}_2$ is un
ramified of degree 2. So $f = 2$ and $K_0$ has residue field $\mathbb{F}_4$.

**$K/K_0$ is totally tamely ramified of degree 3.** The polynomial $x^3 - 2\zeta
_3$ over $K_0$: since $v_{K_0}(2\zeta_3) = v_{K_0}(2) = 1$ (as $K_0/\mathbb{Q}_2
$ is unramified, so $2$ is a uniformizer of $K_0$) and all non-leading coefficie
nts are $0$ (divisible by the uniformizer), Eisenstein's criterion gives irreduc
ibility. So $[K:K_0] = 3$, and $e = 3$. Since $\gcd(3, 2) = 1$, the extension is
 tamely ramified.

Thus $[K:\mathbb{Q}_2] = n = ef = 6$, and $K/\mathbb{Q}_2$ is tamely ramified. T
he prime $q = 3$ divides $n = 6$ and $q \neq p = 2$.

### Proof that $K$ contains no subfield of degree 3 over $\mathbb{Q}_2$

Suppose for contradiction that $L$ satisfies $\mathbb{Q}_2 \subset L \subset K$
with $[L:\mathbb{Q}_2] = 3$.

**Step 1: $L/\mathbb{Q}_2$ must be totally ramified.** Write $e_L \cdot f_L = 3$
 where $e_L \mid e = 3$ and $f_L \mid f = 2$. Since $3$ is prime, either $(e_L,
f_L) = (3,1)$ or $(1,3)$. But $f_L \mid 2$ rules out $f_L = 3$. So $(e_L, f_L) =
 (3,1)$: $L$ is totally (tamely) ramified of degree 3.

**Step 2: Structure of $L$.** Any totally tamely ramified extension of $\mathbb{
Q}_2$ of degree 3 has the form $L = \mathbb{Q}_2(\beta)$ where $\beta^3 = 2v$ fo
r some $v \in \mathbb{Z}_2^*$.

**Step 3: $LK_0 = K$.** Since $[L:\mathbb{Q}_2] = 3$ and $[K_0:\mathbb{Q}_2] = 2
$ are coprime, $L \cap K_0 = \mathbb{Q}_2$, so $[LK_0:\mathbb{Q}_2] = 6 = [K:\ma
thbb{Q}_2]$, giving $LK_0 = K$.

**Step 4: Kummer theory over $K_0$.** Since $K_0 \ni \zeta_3$, both $K = K_0(\al
pha)$ (with $\alpha^3 = 2\zeta_3$) and $K_0(\beta)$ (with $\beta^3 = 2v$) are cy
clic Kummer extensions of degree 3. By Kummer theory, $K_0(\beta) = K_0(\alpha)$
 iff $2v$ and $2\zeta_3$ generate the same cyclic subgroup of $K_0^*/(K_0^*)^3$,
 i.e., $2v \equiv (2\zeta_3)^j \pmod{(K_0^*)^3}$ for some $j \in \{1, 2\}$.

**Step 5: Ruling out $j = 2$.** This requires $v = 2\zeta_3^2 \cdot w^3$ for som
e $w \in K_0^*$. Then $v_{K_0}(v) = 1 + 3\,v_{K_0}(w)$. But $v \in \mathbb{Z}_2^
*$ gives $v_{K_0}(v) = 0$, so $v_{K_0}(w) = -1/3 \notin \mathbb{Z}$, impossible.

**Step 6: Ruling out $j = 1$.** This requires $v = \zeta_3 \cdot w^3$ for some $
w \in K_0^*$. Write $w = 2^a \cdot w_0$ with $w_0 \in \mathcal{O}_{K_0}^*$ and $
a \in \mathbb{Z}$. Then $v = \zeta_3 \cdot 2^{3a} \cdot w_0^3$, so $v_{K_0}(v) =
 3a$. For $v \in \mathbb{Z}_2^*$, we need $a = 0$, so $w \in \mathcal{O}_{K_0}^*
$.

Now use the Teichmüller decomposition $\mathcal{O}_{K_0}^* \cong \mu_3 \times U_
1$ (where $U_1 = 1 + \mathfrak{m}_{K_0}$, valid since $|\mathbb{F}_4^*| = 3$ is
prime to $p = 2$). Write $w = \zeta_3^i \cdot u$ with $i \in \{0,1,2\}$ and $u \
in U_1$. Then $w^3 = u^3 \in U_1$ (since $\zeta_3^{3i} = 1$ and $U_1$ is a group
). So $v = \zeta_3 \cdot u^3$ with $u^3 \in U_1$.

Reducing modulo $\mathfrak{m}_{K_0}$: the residue $\bar{v} = \bar{\zeta}_3 \cdot
 \overline{u^3} = \bar{\zeta}_3 \cdot 1 = \bar{\zeta}_3$ in $\mathbb{F}_4$. But
$\bar{\zeta}_3$ is a generator of $\mathbb{F}_4^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$,
 so $\bar{\zeta}_3 \notin \mathbb{F}_2$. If $v \in \mathbb{Z}_2^*$, then $\bar{v
} \in \mathbb{F}_2$, contradiction.

### Conclusion

Both cases $j = 1$ and $j = 2$ are impossible, so no $v \in \mathbb{Z}_2^*$ can
satisfy the Kummer condition. Therefore $K$ contains **no** intermediate extensi
on of degree $q = 3$ over $\mathbb{Q}_2$, even though $K/\mathbb{Q}_2$ is tamely
 ramified of degree $n = 6$ with $q = 3 \mid n$ and $q \neq p$.

The answer to the question is **No**.
