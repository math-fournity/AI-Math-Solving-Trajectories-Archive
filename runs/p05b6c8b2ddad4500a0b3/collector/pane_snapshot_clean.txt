## Proof

Let \(F = \mathbb{Q}\), \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})\), and \(E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt
[4]{2})\). Then
\[
\mathbb{Q} \;\leq\; \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}) \;\leq\; \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2}).
\]

### \(K/F\) is normal

\(K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})\) is the splitting field of the irreducible polynomia
l \(x^2 - 2 \in \mathbb{Q}[x]\) over \(\mathbb{Q}\), since its roots \(\pm\sqrt{
2}\) both lie in \(K\). Hence \(K/F\) is a normal extension.

### \(E/K\) is normal

The element \(\alpha = \sqrt[4]{2}\) satisfies \(\alpha^2 = \sqrt{2} \in K\), so
 its minimal polynomial over \(K\) is
\[
x^2 - \sqrt{2} \in K[x],
\]
whose roots are \(\pm\sqrt[4]{2}\), both contained in \(E\). Thus \(E\) is the s
plitting field of \(x^2 - \sqrt{2}\) over \(K\), and \(E/K\) is normal.

### \(E/F\) is **not** normal

The minimal polynomial of \(\alpha = \sqrt[4]{2}\) over \(\mathbb{Q}\) is
\[
x^4 - 2 \in \mathbb{Q}[x],
\]
which is irreducible over \(\mathbb{Q}\) by Eisenstein's criterion (at the prime
 \(2\)). Its roots are
\[
\sqrt[4]{2},\quad -\sqrt[4]{2},\quad i\sqrt[4]{2},\quad -i\sqrt[4]{2}.
\]
The field \(E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2})\) is a subfield of \(\mathbb{R}\), so it
 contains only the two real roots \(\pm\sqrt[4]{2}\) and **not** the complex roo
ts \(\pm i\sqrt[4]{2}\).

Since \(E\) does not contain all roots of the minimal polynomial \(x^4 - 2\) of
\(\alpha\) over \(\mathbb{Q}\), the extension \(E/F\) is **not** normal.

### Conclusion

We have exhibited a tower \(F \leq K \leq E\) with \(F = \mathbb{Q}\), \(K = \ma
thbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})\), \(E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2})\), in which both \(E/K\) and
\(K/F\) are normal, yet \(E/F\) is not normal. This shows that normality of exte
nsions is not transitive.
