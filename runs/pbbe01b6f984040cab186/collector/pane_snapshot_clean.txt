## Proof

We want the largest $k$ such that $2^k \mid 1996!$, i.e. the 2-adic valuation $v
_2(1996!)$.

By Legendre's formula, for a prime $p$,

$$v_p(n!) = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \left\lfloor \frac{n}{p^i} \right\rfloor.$$

For $p=2$, $n=1996$, the sum terminates once $2^i > 1996$. Since $2^{10}=1024 \l
e 1996 < 2048 = 2^{11}$, the terms $i=1,\dots,10$ contribute:

| $i$ | $2^i$ | $\lfloor 1996/2^i \rfloor$ |
|-----|-------|----------------------------|
| 1   | 2     | 998                        |
| 2   | 4     | 499                        |
| 3   | 8     | 249                        |
| 4   | 16    | 124                        |
| 5   | 32    | 62                         |
| 6   | 64    | 31                         |
| 7   | 128   | 15                         |
| 8   | 256   | 7                          |
| 9   | 512   | 3                          |
| 10  | 1024  | 1                          |

Summing:

$$998 + 499 + 249 + 124 + 62 + 31 + 15 + 7 + 3 + 1.$$

Running total:
- $998 + 499 = 1497$
- $1497 + 249 = 1746$
- $1746 + 124 = 1870$
- $1870 + 62 = 1932$
- $1932 + 31 = 1963$
- $1963 + 15 = 1978$
- $1978 + 7 = 1985$
- $1985 + 3 = 1988$
- $1988 + 1 = 1989$

Therefore

$$v_2(1996!) = 1989.$$

**Verification.** Each multiple of $2$ contributes one factor of $2$; each multi
ple of $4$ contributes an additional factor; each multiple of $8$ yet another; a
nd so on. The count of integers $\le 1996$ divisible by $2^i$ is exactly $\lfloo
r 1996/2^i \rfloor$, so the total number of factors of $2$ in $1996!$ is precise
ly the sum above. Since $1996 < 2^{11}$, no higher power contributes, and the su
m is complete.

The highest power of $2$ that divides exactly into $1996!$ is

$$\boxed{2^{1989}}.$$
