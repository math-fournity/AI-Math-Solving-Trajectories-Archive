## Proof

**Claim.** For every positive integer $N$, there exist two consecutive primes $p
 < q$ such that $q - p \geq N$. Equivalently, there are arbitrarily long runs of
 consecutive composite integers.

**Construction.** Fix any integer $N \geq 1$. Consider the $N$ consecutive integ
ers

$$M + 2,\; M + 3,\; \ldots,\; M + N,\; M + (N+1),$$

where $M = (N+1)!$.

For each $k$ with $2 \leq k \leq N+1$, we have $k \mid (N+1)! = M$, so $k \mid (
M + k)$. Moreover, $M + k > k$ (since $M = (N+1)! \geq 2$), hence $M + k$ is com
posite: it is divisible by $k$ and strictly larger than $k$.

Therefore each of the $N$ integers

$$M+2,\; M+3,\; \ldots,\; M+(N+1)$$

is composite. These are $N$ consecutive composite integers.

**Deriving the prime gap.** Let $p$ be the largest prime strictly less than $M+2
$, and let $q$ be the smallest prime strictly greater than $M+(N+1)$. By constru
ction, every integer in the interval $[M+2,\, M+(N+1)]$ is composite, so there i
s no prime in this interval. Thus $p < M+2$ and $q > M+(N+1)$, and every integer
 strictly between $p$ and $q$ is composite (it lies within the composite run, or
 between $p$ and $M+2$, or between $M+(N+1)$ and $q$ — in each case no prime exi
sts by definition of $p$ and $q$). Hence $p$ and $q$ are consecutive primes, and

$$q - p \;\geq\; \bigl(M+(N+1)\bigr) - (M+2) + 2 \;=\; N+1 \;>\; N.$$

(At minimum, the gap spans from below $M+2$ to above $M+(N+1)$, a span exceeding
 $N$.)

**Conclusion.** Since $N$ was arbitrary, for every desired gap size $N$ we have
exhibited a run of $N$ consecutive composites, forcing a gap between consecutive
 primes exceeding $N$. Therefore the gaps between consecutive primes are unbound
ed; equivalently, there are arbitrarily large gaps between consecutive prime num
bers as they tend towards infinity. $\blacksquare$
