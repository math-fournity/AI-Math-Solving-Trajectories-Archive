$$G = \langle\, a, b \mid a\,b\,a^{-1} = b^{-1}\,\rangle.$$
This is the fundamental group of the Klein bottle. It is finitely presentable (o

**Step 1: $G$ is torsion-free.**

The relation $aba^{-1} = b^{-1}$ shows that $\langle b\rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}$

mathbb{Z}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}$, where the generator $a$ acts on $\langle b\rangle$
by inversion: $a\cdot b^k = b^{-k}$.

Every element of $G$ has a unique normal form $b^m a^n$ with $m,n\in\mathbb{Z}$.
 The multiplication rule is
$$(b^m a^n)(b^k a^\ell) = b^{\,m + (-1)^n k}\, a^{n+\ell},$$
since $a^n b^k = b^{(-1)^n k} a^n$.

Now compute the order of $b^m a^n$:
- If $n \neq 0$: the $a$-exponent of $(b^m a^n)^r$ is $rn$, which is nonzero for

Therefore no non-identity element has finite order; $G$ is torsion-free.
