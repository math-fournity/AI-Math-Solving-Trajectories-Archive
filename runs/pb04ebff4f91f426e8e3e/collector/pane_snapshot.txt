## Answer

**Yes**, the functor $X \times - : \mathbf{Loc} \to \mathbf{Loc}$ preserves smal
l colimits for every locale $X$.

## Proof

The argument rests on two standard facts about the category of locales.

**Fact 1 (Cartesian closedness).** The category $\mathbf{Loc}$ of locales is car
tesian closed. That is, for every locale $Y$, the functor $(-)\times Y : \mathbf
{Loc} \to \mathbf{Loc}$ admits a right adjoint, the exponential $(-)^{Y}$, so th
at there is a natural bijection
$$\mathbf{Loc}(X \times Y,\, Z) \;\cong\; \mathbf{Loc}(X,\, Z^{Y})$$
for all locales $X, Z$.

*Sketch of construction.* Recall $\mathbf{Loc} = \mathbf{Frm}^{\mathrm{op}}$, wh
ere $\mathbf{Frm}$ is the category of frames (complete lattices in which finite
meets distribute over arbitrary joins) and frame homomorphisms (preserving finit
e meets and arbitrary joins). Given locales $Y, Z$ with underlying frames $B = \
mathcal{O}(Y)$ and $C = \mathcal{O}(Z)$, the frame $\mathcal{O}(Z^{Y})$ is prese
nted by generators $[b, c]$ for $b \in B$, $c \in C$, subject to the relations:
- For each $b \in B$: the map $c \mapsto [b,c]$ preserves finite meets and arbit
rary joins.
- For each $c \in C$: the map $b \mapsto [b,c]$ preserves arbitrary joins.
- $[b_1 \wedge b_2,\, c] = [b_1, c] \wedge [b_2, c]$.

One verifies that this presentation yields a well-defined frame and that the res
ulting locale $Z^Y$ satisfies the universal property of the exponential. (See, e
.g., Johnstone, *Stone Spaces*, Ch. IV; or Picado–Pultr, *Frames and Locales*.)
$\square$

**Fact 2 (Cocompleteness).** The category $\mathbf{Loc}$ has all small colimits.

*Justification.* The category $\mathbf{Frm}$ is a variety of algebras (defined b
y equations involving operations of bounded arity), hence it is complete: it has
 all small limits, and these are created by the forgetful functor to $\mathbf{Se
t}$. Since $\mathbf{Loc} = \mathbf{Frm}^{\mathrm{op}}$, the category $\mathbf{Lo
c}$ has all small colimits. $\square$

**Conclusion.** Fix a locale $X$. By Fact 1, the functor
$$X \times - : \mathbf{Loc} \to \mathbf{Loc}$$
is a left adjoint, with right adjoint $(-)^{X}$. A fundamental theorem of catego
ry theory states that every left adjoint preserves all colimits that exist in it
s domain. By Fact 2, all small colimits exist in $\mathbf{Loc}$. Therefore $X \t
imes -$ preserves all small colimits.

Since $X$ was an arbitrary locale, the functor $X \times -$ preserves small coli
mits **for all** locales $X$. $\blacksquare$
