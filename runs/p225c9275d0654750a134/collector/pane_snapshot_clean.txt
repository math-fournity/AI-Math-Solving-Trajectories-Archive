No. The canonical map need not be an isomorphism in a pre-abelian category; it i
s an isomorphism for every morphism precisely when the category is **abelian**.
Here is an explicit counterexample.

---

**Counterexample.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be the category of Hausdorff topological a
belian groups with continuous group homomorphisms. This category is pre-abelian:

- **Kernels** exist: the kernel of $f:A\to B$ is $\ker f$ with the subspace topo
logy (Hausdorff, as a subspace of a Hausdorff group).
- **Cokernels** exist: the cokernel of $f:A\to B$ is $B/\overline{f(A)}$ with th
e quotient topology, which is Hausdorff precisely because we quotient by the *cl
osure* of the image.
- The category is additive (finite biproducts = direct sums with product topolog
y, zero object = trivial group).

Now consider the quotient map
$$\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to S^{1}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z},\qquad t\mapsto t+\mathbb{Z}
,$$
where $\mathbb{R}$ has the usual topology and $S^{1}$ the quotient topology. Thi
s is a continuous, surjective group homomorphism.

**Compute $\operatorname{coker}(\ker\varphi)$.** The kernel is $\ker\varphi=\mat
hbb{Z}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}$. Its cokernel is
$$\operatorname{coker}(\ker\varphi)=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}=S^{1}.$$

**Compute $\ker(\operatorname{coker}\varphi)$.** Since $\varphi$ is surjective,
$\overline{\varphi(\mathbb{R})}=S^{1}$, so
$$\operatorname{coker}\varphi=S^{1}/S^{1}=0,$$
and hence
$$\ker(\operatorname{coker}\varphi)=\ker(0\to 0)=0.$$

**The canonical map.** The canonical map $\operatorname{coker}(\ker\varphi)\to\k
er(\operatorname{coker}\varphi)$ is the unique map $S^{1}\to 0$ induced by the u
niversal properties. Concretely, $\varphi$ factors as $\mathbb{R}\twoheadrightar
row\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}=S^{1}\xrightarrow{\;\varphi'\;}S^{1}$ where $\varphi'=\
mathrm{id}$; composing with $S^{1}\to\operatorname{coker}\varphi=0$ gives zero,
so $\varphi'$ factors through $\ker(\operatorname{coker}\varphi)=0$. The resulti
ng map is
$$S^{1}\longrightarrow 0,$$
the zero map, which is **not** an isomorphism (since $S^{1}\neq 0$).

---

**Conclusion.** In the pre-abelian category of Hausdorff topological abelian gro
ups, the canonical map $\operatorname{coker}(\ker\varphi)\to\ker(\operatorname{c
oker}\varphi)$ is not an isomorphism for the quotient map $\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to
 S^{1}$. Therefore the answer is **no**: this map does not always induce an isom
orphism in a pre-abelian category. (The property holds for all morphisms exactly
 in **abelian** categories, which is one of the standard characterizations of ab
elianness.)
