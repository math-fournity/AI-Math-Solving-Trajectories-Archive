$$L = \lim_{x\to\infty} \frac{x \int_x^\infty \overline{\Phi}(s)\,ds}{\overline{
\Phi}(x)}.$$

As $x\to\infty$, both numerator and denominator tend to $0$, so we apply **L'Hôp
ital's rule**.

**Derivatives.** Let $N(x) = x\int_x^\infty \overline{\Phi}(s)\,ds$ and $D(x) =
\overline{\Phi}(x)$.

$$N'(x) = \int_x^\infty \overline{\Phi}(s)\,ds + x\cdot\bigl(-\overline{\Phi}(x)
\bigr) = \int_x^\infty \overline{\Phi}(s)\,ds - x\,\overline{\Phi}(x),$$

$$D'(x) = -\phi(x).$$

Thus:
$$L = \lim_{x\to\infty} \frac{\int_x^\infty \overline{\Phi}(s)\,ds - x\,\overlin
e{\Phi}(x)}{-\phi(x)}. \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Asymptotic expansion of $\overline{\Phi}(x)$.** Integration by parts ($\phi'(t
) = -t\,\phi(t)$) gives:

$$\overline{\Phi}(x) = \int_x^\infty \phi(t)\,dt = \int_x^\infty \frac{1}{t}\cdo
t t\,\phi(t)\,dt = \frac{\phi(x)}{x} - \int_x^\infty \frac{\phi(t)}{t^2}\,dt.$$

Repeating integration by parts on the remainder:

$$\overline{\Phi}(x) = \frac{\phi(x)}{x}\left(1 - \frac{1}{x^2} + \frac{3}{x^4}
- \cdots\right). \tag{1}$$

**Asymptotic expansion of $\int_x^\infty \overline{\Phi}(s)\,ds$.** Using (1) an

$$\int_x^\infty \frac{\phi(s)}{s}\,ds = \frac{\phi(x)}{x^2} - 2\int_x^\infty \fr
ac{\phi(s)}{s^3}\,ds = \frac{\phi(x)}{x^2}\left(1 - \frac{2}{x^2}+\cdots\right),
$$

$$\int_x^\infty \frac{\phi(s)}{s^3}\,ds = \frac{\phi(x)}{x^4}\left(1 - \frac{4}{
x^2}+\cdots\right), \quad \text{etc.}$$

Combining:

$$\int_x^\infty \overline{\Phi}(s)\,ds = \frac{\phi(x)}{x^2}\left(1 - \frac{3}{x
^2} + \cdots\right). \tag{2}$$

**Evaluating ($\ast$).** From (1) and (2):

$$\int_x^\infty \overline{\Phi}(s)\,ds - x\,\overline{\Phi}(x) = \phi(x)\!\left[
\frac{1}{x^2} - \frac{3}{x^4}+\cdots\right] - \phi(x)\!\left[1 - \frac{1}{x^2} +

$$= \phi(x)\left[-1 + \frac{2}{x^2} - \frac{6}{x^4}+\cdots\right].$$

Substituting into ($\ast$):

$$L = \lim_{x\to\infty} \frac{\phi(x)\left[-1 + \frac{2}{x^2} - \cdots\right]}{-
\phi(x)} = \lim_{x\to\infty}\left(1 - \frac{2}{x^2} + \cdots\right) = \boxed{1}.
$$

**Interpretation.** The ratio $\frac{\int_x^\infty \overline{\Phi}(s)\,ds}{\over
line{\Phi}(x)}$ is the mean excess function $e(x) = \mathbb{E}[X - x \mid X > x]
$. The result $L = \lim_{x\to\infty} x\,e(x) = 1$ reflects the light-tailed natu
re of the Gaussian: $e(x) \sim 1/x$, so the excess over a high threshold $x$ is
of order $1/x$, vanishing relative to $x$. $\blacksquare$
