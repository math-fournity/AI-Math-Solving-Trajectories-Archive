Looking at this problem, I need to determine the smallest degree of a "bad" irre
ducible polynomial over ℤ, where "bad" means irreducible over ℤ but reducible mo
d p for all sufficiently large primes p.

## Analysis

**Key connection to Galois theory:** For an irreducible polynomial $f$ of degree
 $n$ over ℤ, the factorization pattern of $f \pmod{p}$ (for $p \nmid \text{disc}
(f)$) corresponds to the cycle type of the Frobenius element in $\text{Gal}(f) \
subseteq S_n$. Specifically, $f$ is irreducible mod $p$ if and only if the Frobe
nius acts as an $n$-cycle.

By the Chebotarev density theorem, the density of primes $p$ for which $f$ is ir
reducible mod $p$ equals the proportion of $n$-cycles in $\text{Gal}(f)$.

**Therefore:** $f$ is "bad" if and only if $\text{Gal}(f)$ contains **no** $n$-c
ycle — for then $f$ is reducible mod every prime $p \nmid \text{disc}(f)$, hence
 reducible mod all sufficiently large primes.

## Checking small degrees

**Degree 1:** Any linear polynomial is irreducible mod every prime. Not bad.

**Degree 2:** An irreducible quadratic has $\text{Gal}(f) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mat
hbb{Z}$, generated by a transposition (a 2-cycle). Contains a 2-cycle, so there
exist infinitely many primes where $f$ is irreducible mod $p$. **Not bad.**

**Degree 3:** An irreducible cubic has a transitive Galois group inside $S_3$. T
he transitive subgroups of $S_3$ are $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ and $S_3$ itself.
Both contain 3-cycles. **Not bad.**

**Degree 4:** Here bad polynomials first become possible. Consider $f(x) = x^4 +
 1$, the 8th cyclotomic polynomial.

- **Irreducible over ℤ:** $x^4+1 = \Phi_8(x)$ is a cyclotomic polynomial, hence
irreducible over ℚ.
- **Galois group:** $\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_8)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z
}/8\mathbb{Z})^\times = \{1,3,5,7\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{
Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \cong V_4$. Every non-identity element has order 2, so there is *
*no 4-cycle**.
- **Reducible mod every prime:** Since $V_4$ has no 4-cycle, $f$ is reducible mo
d every prime $p \nmid \text{disc}(f) = 256$, i.e., every odd prime. For $p=2$:
$x^4+1 \equiv (x+1)^4 \pmod{2}$, also reducible.

**Explicit verification:**
- $\pmod{3}$: $x^4+1 = (x^2+x+2)(x^2-x+2)$
- $\pmod{5}$: $x^4+1 = (x^2+2)(x^2+3)$
- $\pmod{7}$: $x^4+1 = (x^2+3x+1)(x^2-3x+1)$

All reducible. ✓

## Conclusion

- For degrees 1, 2, 3: every irreducible polynomial over ℤ is automatically **no
t bad** (the Galois group, being a transitive subgroup of $S_n$, always contains
 an $n$-cycle for $n \leq 3$).
- For degree 4: bad polynomials exist (e.g., $x^4+1$ with Galois group $V_4$).

The smallest possible degree of a **bad** irreducible polynomial — equivalently,
 the smallest degree where the "not bad" condition becomes a non-trivial constra
int — is:

$$\boxed{4}$$
