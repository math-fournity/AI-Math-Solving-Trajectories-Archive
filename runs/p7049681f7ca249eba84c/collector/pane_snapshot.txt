## Proof

**Setup and interpretation.** For primes $p \leq n$, the factor $1 - n/p$ is non
-positive, so the product $\prod_{p<x}(1-n/p)$ cannot be positive for $x > n$. T
he meaningful statement (standard in sieve theory) is:

> For each fixed positive integer $n$, there exists a constant $d = d(n) > 0$ su
ch that for all $x > n$,
> $$\prod_{\substack{p < x \\ p > n}} \left(1 - \frac{n}{p}\right) \geq d\,(\log
 x)^{-n}.$$

---

### Step 1: Factorization

For every prime $p > n$, write
$$1 - \frac{n}{p} = \left(1 - \frac{1}{p}\right)^{\!n} \cdot \underbrace{\frac{1
 - n/p}{(1-1/p)^n}}_{=:\, r_p}.$$

Hence
$$\prod_{\substack{p < x \\ p > n}} \left(1 - \frac{n}{p}\right) = \left[\prod_{
\substack{p < x \\ p > n}} \left(1 - \frac{1}{p}\right)\right]^n \cdot \prod_{\s
ubstack{p < x \\ p > n}} r_p. \tag{1}$$

---

### Step 2: The correction product $C(n) = \prod_{p > n} r_p$ converges to a pos
itive constant

**Each $r_p \in (0,1)$.** By Bernoulli's inequality, for $n \geq 2$ and $p \geq
2$:
$$(1 - 1/p)^n \geq 1 - n/p,$$
with **strict** inequality when $n \geq 2$ and $p \geq 2$ (equality only if $n=1
$ or $1/p = 0$). Since $p > n \geq 2$ gives $1/p \in (0,1)$, we get $(1-1/p)^n >
 1 - n/p > 0$, so $0 < r_p < 1$. (For $n=1$, $r_p = 1$ identically.)

**Asymptotic expansion.** For large $p$:
$$(1-1/p)^n = 1 - \frac{n}{p} + \frac{\binom{n}{2}}{p^2} + O(p^{-3}),$$
so
$$r_p = \frac{1 - n/p}{1 - n/p + \binom{n}{2}/p^2 + O(p^{-3})} = 1 - \frac{\bino
m{n}{2}}{p^2} + O(p^{-3}).$$

Therefore $\log r_p = -\frac{\binom{n}{2}}{p^2} + O(p^{-3})$, and since $\sum_{p
} p^{-2} < \infty$, the series $\sum_{p>n} |\log r_p|$ converges. By the standar
d criterion for infinite products,
$$C(n) := \prod_{p > n} r_p$$
converges to a **positive** finite constant.

Since each $r_p < 1$, the partial products $\prod_{n < p < x} r_p$ are **decreas
ing** in $x$, giving the uniform lower bound:
$$\prod_{\substack{p < x \\ p > n}} r_p \geq C(n) > 0 \quad \text{for all } x >
n. \tag{2}$$

---

### Step 3: Apply Mertens' theorem to the main product

**Mertens' theorem** states $\prod_{p \leq x}(1-1/p) \sim \frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\lo
g x}$ as $x \to \infty$. The Rosser–Schoenfeld explicit form yields the lower bo
und: there exists an absolute constant $c_0 > 0$ (one may take $c_0 = e^{-\gamma
}$) such that
$$\prod_{p \leq x}\left(1 - \frac{1}{p}\right) \geq \frac{c_0}{\log x} \quad \te
xt{for all } x \geq 2. \tag{3}$$

Split the product at $n$:
$$\prod_{\substack{p < x \\ p > n}} \left(1 - \frac{1}{p}\right) = \frac{\prod_{
p < x}(1-1/p)}{\prod_{p \leq n}(1-1/p)} \geq \frac{c_0}{M_n \cdot \log x}, \tag{
4}$$
where $M_n := \prod_{p \leq n}(1-1/p) > 0$ is a positive constant (finite produc
t of positive terms).

Raising to the $n$-th power:
$$\left[\prod_{\substack{p < x \\ p > n}} \left(1 - \frac{1}{p}\right)\right]^n
\geq \frac{c_0^n}{M_n^n \,(\log x)^n}. \tag{5}$$

---

### Step 4: Combine (1), (2), (5)

$$\prod_{\substack{p < x \\ p > n}} \left(1 - \frac{n}{p}\right) \geq \frac{c_0^
n}{M_n^n\,(\log x)^n} \cdot C(n) = \underbrace{\frac{c_0^n \cdot C(n)}{M_n^n}}_{
=:\, d(n)} \cdot (\log x)^{-n}.$$

---

### Step 5: Handle small $x$ (finite verification)

For $x$ in the bounded range $n < x \leq x_0$ (where $x_0$ is any fixed threshol
d, say $x_0 = \max(2n, e)$), the left-hand side $\prod_{n < p < x}(1-n/p)$ is a
**fixed positive number** (the product ranges over finitely many primes, each fa
ctor positive since $p > n$; if no primes lie in $(n,x)$, the empty product equa
ls $1$). The right-hand side $d(n)(\log x)^{-n}$ is continuous and bounded on th
is compact interval. Since the asymptotic bound from Step 4 already gives a vali
d $d(n)$ for all large $x$, and the LHS is bounded below by a positive constant
on the compact small-$x$ range, we can replace $d(n)$ by
$$d(n) = \min\!\left(\frac{c_0^n \, C(n)}{M_n^n},\; \inf_{n < x \leq x_0} \frac{
(\log x)^n \prod_{n<p<x}(1-n/p)}{1}\right) > 0,$$
which remains positive (a minimum of finitely many positive quantities) and make
s the inequality hold for **all** $x > n$.

---

### Conclusion

The constant is:
$$\boxed{d = d(n) = \frac{c_0^{\,n}}{M_n^{\,n}} \prod_{p > n} \frac{1 - n/p}{(1
- 1/p)^n}}$$

where:
- $c_0 = e^{-\gamma}$ (from Mertens' theorem, satisfying $\prod_{p \leq x}(1-1/p
) \geq c_0/\log x$ for all $x \geq 2$),
- $M_n = \prod_{p \leq n}(1 - 1/p)$,
- $C(n) = \prod_{p > n} \frac{1 - n/p}{(1-1/p)^n} > 0$ (convergent infinite prod
uct, since $\frac{1-n/p}{(1-1/p)^n} = 1 - \binom{n}{2}/p^2 + O(p^{-3})$ and $\su
m_p p^{-2} < \infty$).

Equivalently:
$$d(n) = \frac{e^{-n\gamma}}{M_n^n} \prod_{p > n} \frac{1 - n/p}{(1 - 1/p)^n}.$$

**Verification of key properties:**
- **Positivity:** $c_0 > 0$, $M_n > 0$, $C(n) > 0$ (convergent product of terms
in $(0,1)$ with $\sum|\log r_p| < \infty$). ✓
- **Validity for all $x > n$:** The correction product is bounded below by $C(n)
$ (partial products decrease); Mertens gives the $(\log x)^{-n}$ decay; small $x
$ handled by compactness. ✓
- **Consistency with $n=1$:** $r_p = 1$ for all $p$, so $C(1) = 1$, $M_1 = 1$ (e
mpty product), giving $d(1) = e^{-\gamma}$, recovering the classical Mertens low
er bound $\prod_{p<x}(1-1/p) \geq e^{-\gamma}/\log x$. ✓
