**Answer: Yes, $e^{i\pi\theta}$ is algebraic.**

**Proof.**

Let $\theta$ be a rational number. Then $\theta = p/q$ for some integers $p$ and
 $q$ with $q \neq 0$ (we may take $q > 0$).

Consider
$$\alpha = e^{i\pi\theta} = e^{i\pi p/q}.$$

Raise $\alpha$ to the $q$-th power:
$$\alpha^q = \left(e^{i\pi p/q}\right)^q = e^{i\pi p} = \left(e^{i\pi}\right)^p
= (-1)^p.$$

**Case 1: $p$ is even.** Then $(-1)^p = 1$, so $\alpha^q = 1$, meaning $\alpha$
is a root of the polynomial
$$x^q - 1 \in \mathbb{Z}[x].$$

**Case 2: $p$ is odd.** Then $(-1)^p = -1$, so $\alpha^q = -1$, meaning $\alpha$
 is a root of the polynomial
$$x^q + 1 \in \mathbb{Z}[x].$$

In both cases, $\alpha$ is a root of a nonzero polynomial with integer (hence ra
tional) coefficients. Moreover, $|\alpha| = 1 \neq 0$, so $\alpha$ is a nonzero
complex number satisfying a polynomial equation over $\mathbb{Q}$.

By definition, $\alpha = e^{i\pi\theta}$ is an **algebraic number**. $\blacksqua
