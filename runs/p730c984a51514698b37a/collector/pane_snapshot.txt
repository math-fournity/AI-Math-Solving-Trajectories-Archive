## Proof

**Answer: Yes, such a real number $r$ exists.**

Let $a$ and $b$ be transcendental (hence nonzero, so all integer powers includin
g negative ones are well-defined). Define the set of all real numbers expressibl
e as finite $\mathbb{Q}$-linear combinations of integral powers of $a$ and $b$:

$$S = \left\{\sum_{k=1}^{n} q_k\, a^{i_k} b^{j_k} \;:\; n \in \mathbb{N},\; q_k
\in \mathbb{Q},\; i_k, j_k \in \mathbb{Z}\right\}.$$

**Claim: $S$ is at most countable.**

Consider the set $\mathcal{E}$ of all *formal expressions* $\sum_{k=1}^{n} q_k\,
 x^{i_k} y^{j_k}$ where $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $q_k \in \mathbb{Q}$, and $i_k, j_k
\in \mathbb{Z}$. Each element of $\mathcal{E}$ is specified by a finite amount o
f data: an integer $n$ and a finite sequence of triples $(q_k, i_k, j_k) \in \ma
thbb{Q} \times \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$. Since $\mathbb{Q} \times \mathbb{Z
} \times \mathbb{Z}$ is countable, and the collection of all finite sequences fr
om a countable set is countable, $\mathcal{E}$ is countable.

The set $S$ is the image of $\mathcal{E}$ under the evaluation homomorphism that
 substitutes $x \mapsto a$, $y \mapsto b$. The image of a countable set is at mo
st countable. Hence $S$ is at most countable.

**Conclusion.** Since $\mathbb{R}$ is uncountable and $S$ is at most countable,
we have $S \subsetneq \mathbb{R}$. Therefore there exists a real number $r \in \
mathbb{R} \setminus S$; that is, $r$ cannot be expressed as any finite combinati
on of integral powers of $a$ and $b$ with rational coefficients. In fact, $\math
bb{R} \setminus S$ has the cardinality of the continuum, so "almost all" real nu
mbers (in the sense of cardinality) cannot be so expressed.

**Remark.** The transcendentality of $a$ and $b$ is not essential to the argumen
t — it merely guarantees $a, b \neq 0$ so that negative powers are defined. The
cardinality argument works for any nonzero real $a, b$: the set of finite $\math
bb{Q}$-linear combinations of a countable family of monomials is always countabl
e, while $\mathbb{R}$ is uncountable.
