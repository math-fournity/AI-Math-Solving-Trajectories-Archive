## Proof

We seek all $n \in [100, 999]$ such that $\binom{n}{3} \equiv n \pmod{100}$.

**Reformulation.** The condition $\binom{n}{3} \equiv n \pmod{100}$ is equivalen
t to $100 \mid \binom{n}{3} - n$. Compute:
$$\binom{n}{3} - n = \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{6} - n = \frac{n\big[(n-1)(n-2) - 6\big]
}{6} = \frac{n(n^2 - 3n - 4)}{6} = \frac{n(n-4)(n+1)}{6}.$$
So the condition becomes $600 \mid n(n-4)(n+1)$, where $600 = 2^3 \cdot 3 \cdot
5^2$.

**Three divisibility conditions.**

*Condition 2 (mod 3):* Since $-4 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$, we have $n(n-4)(n+1) \equiv
 n(n+2)(n+1) = n(n+1)(n+2) \pmod{3}$, a product of three consecutive integers, a
lways divisible by 3. ✓ (automatic)

*Condition 1 (mod 8):* Since $n-4 \equiv n+4 \pmod{8}$, we need $8 \mid n(n+4)(n
+1)$. Checking all residues mod 8:

| $n \bmod 8$ | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| product mod 8 | 0 | 2 | 4 | 4 | 0 | 6 | 4 | 0 |

So we need $n \equiv 0, 4, 7 \pmod{8}$.

*Condition 3 (mod 25):* The factors $n-4$ and $n+1$ differ by 5, so both are div
isible by 5 exactly when $n \equiv 4 \pmod{5}$, giving $25 \mid (n-4)(n+1)$. Oth
erwise, we need a single factor divisible by 25; the only case not already cover
ed by $n \equiv 4 \pmod 5$ is $n \equiv 0 \pmod{25}$. So we need $n \equiv 0 \pm
od{25}$ **or** $n \equiv 4 \pmod{5}$.

**Combining (mod 200).** Since the mod-3 condition is automatic, the full condit
ion depends only on $n \bmod 200$.

*Case A:* $n \equiv 0 \pmod{25}$ and $n \equiv 0, 4, 7 \pmod{8}$. Since $25 \equ
iv 1 \pmod 8$, writing $n = 25j$ gives $j \equiv 0, 4, 7 \pmod 8$. Residues mod
200: $\{0, 100, 175\}$ — **3 values**.

*Case B:* $n \equiv 4 \pmod{5}$ and $n \equiv 0, 4, 7 \pmod{8}$. By CRT (mod 40)
, each sub-case yields 5 residues mod 200:
- $n \equiv 0 \pmod 8$: $\{24, 64, 104, 144, 184\}$
- $n \equiv 4 \pmod 8$: $\{4, 44, 84, 124, 164\}$
- $n \equiv 7 \pmod 8$: $\{39, 79, 119, 159, 199\}$

That's **15 values**. Cases A and B are disjoint (Case A has $n \equiv 0 \pmod 5
$, Case B has $n \equiv 4 \pmod 5$). Total: **18 valid residues mod 200**.

**Counting $n \in [100, 999]$.** The 18 residues, sorted:

$$\{0, 4, 24, 39, 44, 64, 79, 84\} \cup \{100, 104, 119, 124, 144, 159, 164, 175
, 184, 199\}.$$

- **8 residues** in $[0, 99]$: each contributes $n = r+200, r+400, r+600, r+800$
 (4 values, all $\le 999$). Total: $8 \times 4 = 32$.
- **10 residues** in $[100, 199]$: each contributes $n = r, r+200, r+400, r+600,
 r+800$ (5 values, since $r \ge 100$ and $r+800 \le 999$). Total: $10 \times 5 =
 50$.

**Grand total:** $32 + 50 = \boxed{82}$.

**Spot checks:**
- $n = 100$: $\binom{100}{3} = 161700$, ends in $00 = 100 \bmod 100$. ✓
- $n = 175$: $\binom{175}{3} = 877975$, ends in $75 = 175 \bmod 100$. ✓
- $n = 199$: $\binom{199}{3} = 1293699$, ends in $99 = 199 \bmod 100$. ✓
- $n = 101$ (excluded, $101 \equiv 5 \pmod 8$): $\binom{101}{3} = 166650$, ends
in $50 \neq 01$. ✓
