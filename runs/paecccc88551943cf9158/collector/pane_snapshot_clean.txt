Looking at this problem, I need to construct the complex potential for a doublet
 in a strip with vertical walls, subject to a uniform flow at infinity.

## Solution

**Setup.** The strip is $S: 0 < x < \omega$, with walls at $x=0$ and $x=\omega$
(both must be stream lines). The doublet $(a; p)$ is at $a \in S$ with complex m
oment $p$. The velocity at $y \to \pm\infty$ is $iV$ (uniform upward flow of spe
ed $V$), so $dW/dz \to -iV$.

**Conformal mapping.** Map the strip to the upper half-plane via
$$\zeta = e^{i\pi z/\omega}, \qquad z = \frac{\omega}{i\pi}\ln\zeta.$$
Under this map: $|\zeta| = e^{-\pi y/\omega}$, $\arg\zeta = \pi x/\omega$, so $x
=0 \mapsto$ positive real axis, $x=\omega \mapsto$ negative real axis, and $S \m
apsto \{\operatorname{Im}\zeta > 0\}$. The point $a$ maps to $\alpha = e^{i\pi a
/\omega}$, and $y\to+\infty \mapsto \zeta\to 0$, $y\to-\infty \mapsto |\zeta|\to
\infty$.

**Uniform flow in $\zeta$-plane.** The uniform flow $W_0 = -iVz$ becomes
$$W_0 = -iV\cdot\frac{\omega}{i\pi}\ln\zeta = -\frac{V\omega}{\pi}\ln\zeta,$$
which is a sink of strength $2V\omega$ at $\zeta=0$ and a source at $\zeta=\inft
y$.

**Doublet transformation.** A doublet at $z=a$ with moment $p$ has local potenti
al $p/(z-a)$. Since $\zeta - \alpha \approx g'(a)(z-a)$ with $g'(a) = (i\pi/\ome
ga)\alpha$, the doublet moment in the $\zeta$-plane is
$$p' = p\cdot g'(a) = \frac{i\pi p}{\omega}\,\alpha.$$

**Method of images in the upper half-plane.** To make the real axis a stream lin
e, we place an image doublet at $\bar\alpha$ with moment $\bar{p}'$. The complex
 potential in the $\zeta$-plane is:
$$W(\zeta) = -\frac{V\omega}{\pi}\ln\zeta + \frac{p'}{\zeta - \alpha} + \frac{\b
ar{p}'}{\zeta - \bar\alpha}.$$

**Return to the $z$-plane.** Substituting $\zeta = e^{i\pi z/\omega}$ and using
the identity
$$\frac{1}{e^{i\pi z/\omega} - e^{i\pi a/\omega}} = e^{-i\pi a/\omega}\left(-\tf
rac{1}{2} - \tfrac{i}{2}\cot\frac{\pi(z-a)}{2\omega}\right),$$
the doublet terms simplify (the constant terms combine into an irrelevant additi
ve constant), yielding:

$$\boxed{W(z) = -iVz + \frac{\pi p}{2\omega}\cot\frac{\pi(z-a)}{2\omega} - \frac
{\pi\bar{p}}{2\omega}\cot\frac{\pi(z+\bar{a})}{2\omega}}$$

**Verification of boundary conditions.**

*Wall $x=0$ ($z=iy$):* Note that $iy + \bar{a} = -\overline{(iy - a)}$, so
$$\cot\frac{\pi(iy+\bar{a})}{2\omega} = -\overline{\cot\frac{\pi(iy-a)}{2\omega}
}.$$
Setting $C = \cot\frac{\pi(iy-a)}{2\omega}$, the doublet part becomes
$$W_d(iy) = \frac{\pi}{2\omega}\bigl(p\,C + \bar{p}\,\bar{C}\bigr) = \frac{\pi}{
\omega}\operatorname{Re}(pC) \in \mathbb{R}.$$
Also $W_0(iy) = -iV(iy) = Vy \in \mathbb{R}$. Hence $\operatorname{Im}W(iy)=0$:
the wall $x=0$ is the stream line $\psi = 0$. ✓

*Wall $x=\omega$ ($z=\omega+iy$):* Using $\cot(\pi/2 + w) = -\tan w$ and the sam
e conjugation relation for $\tan$:
$$W_d(\omega+iy) = -\frac{\pi}{\omega}\operatorname{Re}\!\bigl(p\tan\tfrac{\pi(i
y-a)}{2\omega}\bigr) \in \mathbb{R},$$
and $W_0(\omega+iy) = -iV\omega + Vy$, so $\operatorname{Im}W = -V\omega = \text
{const}$. The wall $x=\omega$ is the stream line $\psi = -V\omega$. ✓

*Velocity at infinity:* As $y\to\pm\infty$, $\cot\frac{\pi(z-a)}{2\omega}\to \mp
 i$ and $\cot\frac{\pi(z+\bar a)}{2\omega}\to \mp i$, so the doublet terms remai
n bounded and $dW/dz \to -iV$, giving velocity $iV$. ✓

*Doublet singularity:* Near $z=a$, $\cot\frac{\pi(z-a)}{2\omega} \approx \frac{2
\omega}{\pi(z-a)}$, so $W_d \approx \frac{p}{z-a}$, confirming the doublet has m
oment $p$ at $a$. ✓

**Velocity field:**
$$\frac{dW}{dz} = -iV - \frac{\pi^2 p}{4\omega^2}\csc^2\frac{\pi(z-a)}{2\omega}
+ \frac{\pi^2\bar{p}}{4\omega^2}\csc^2\frac{\pi(z+\bar{a})}{2\omega}.$$

**Schematic description of the flow pattern:**

*Stream lines* ($\psi = \operatorname{Im}W = \text{const}$):
1. The walls $x=0$ ($\psi=0$) and $x=\omega$ ($\psi=-V\omega$) are stream lines.
2. Far from the doublet ($|y|\to\infty$), the stream lines are nearly horizontal
 lines $x=\text{const}$, corresponding to the uniform upward flow $iV$.
3. Near $z=a$, the doublet distorts the stream lines into the characteristic dip
ole pattern: stream lines emerging from one side of the doublet and returning to
 the other, forming closed loops around $a$ (when the doublet is not too strong
relative to $V$).
4. Stagnation points (where $dW/dz=0$) occur on the stream line passing through
the doublet, typically above and below $a$ (for a doublet oriented along the flo
w) or to the sides (for a doublet oriented across the flow). These are points wh
ere the stream lines split.
5. The stream line $\psi=0$ (the wall $x=0$) and $\psi=-V\omega$ (the wall $x=\o
mega$) confine the flow; interior stream lines bend around the doublet region.

*Equipotential lines* ($\varphi = \operatorname{Re}W = \text{const}$):
1. Far from the doublet, equipotential lines are nearly horizontal (perpendicula
r to the vertical uniform flow).
2. Near $z=a$, they form the orthogonal dipole pattern—perpendicular to the stre
am lines at every point, in accordance with the orthogonality of $\varphi$ and $
\psi$.
3. Equipotential lines meet the walls $x=0$ and $x=\omega$ at right angles (sinc
e the walls are stream lines and the two families are orthogonal).
4. The equipotential lines crowd together near the doublet, reflecting the high
velocity in that region.

The overall picture: a uniform vertical flow in the strip, perturbed locally nea
r $z=a$ by the doublet, which introduces a dipole-like circulation pattern. The
walls channel the flow, and the perturbation decays exponentially with distance
from the doublet (due to the cotangent's exponential decay in the strip).
