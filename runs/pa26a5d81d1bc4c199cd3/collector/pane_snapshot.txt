## Proof

Place coordinates with $D$ at the origin and $BC$ along the $x$-axis. Let $B = (
-1, 0)$, $C = (1, 0)$, $A = (a, b)$ with $b \neq 0$. Set $s = a^2 + b^2$.

The midpoints are $E = \left(\frac{a+1}{2}, \frac{b}{2}\right)$, $F = \left(\fra
c{a-1}{2}, \frac{b}{2}\right)$, and the centroid is $G = \left(\frac{a}{3}, \fra
c{b}{3}\right)$.

**Circumcircle.** Its center is $O = (0, k)$ with $k = \frac{s-1}{2b}$, and radi
us $R$ with $R^2 = 1 + k^2$.

**Point $X$.** Line $AD$ is parametrized as $(ta, tb)$. Substituting into the ci
rcumcircle equation $x^2 + (y-k)^2 = R^2$ gives $t^2 s - 2tbk - 1 = 0$, with roo
ts $t = 1$ (point $A$) and $t = -1/s$ (point $X$). Thus $X = \left(-\frac{a}{s},
 -\frac{b}{s}\right)$.

**Circle $\Omega$.** Since $X$ lies on both $\Omega$ and the circumcircle, and t
he two circles are tangent, $X$ is the tangency point. The center $C_\Omega$ of
$\Omega$ lies on line $OX$ and on the perpendicular bisector of $DX$. Writing $C
_\Omega = O + \lambda(X - O)$ and imposing $|C_\Omega D| = |C_\Omega X|$ yields
$\lambda = \frac{s}{s+1}$, giving:
$$C_\Omega = \left(\frac{-a}{s+1}, \frac{a^2 - b^2 - 1}{2b(s+1)}\right).$$

**Tangent to $\Omega$ at $D$.** This is perpendicular to $C_\Omega D$, so it has
 direction $(a^2 - b^2 - 1, \, 2ab)$, passing through $D = (0,0)$.

**Points $Y$ and $Z$.** The perpendicular bisector of $DE$ has direction $(-b, a
+1)$ through $\left(\frac{a+1}{4}, \frac{b}{4}\right)$. Intersecting with the ta
ngent line $(t(a^2-b^2-1), 2abt)$ gives $t = \frac{1}{4(a-1)}$, so:
$$Y = \left(\frac{a^2-b^2-1}{4(a-1)}, \frac{ab}{2(a-1)}\right).$$

Similarly, the perpendicular bisector of $DF$ has direction $(-b, a-1)$ through
$\left(\frac{a-1}{4}, \frac{b}{4}\right)$, giving $t = \frac{1}{4(a+1)}$ and:
$$Z = \left(\frac{a^2-b^2-1}{4(a+1)}, \frac{ab}{2(a+1)}\right).$$

**Point $P$.** Parametrize line $YE$ as $Y + \mu(E-Y)$ and line $ZF$ as $Z + \nu
(F-Z)$. Using $E - Y = \frac{1}{4(a-1)}(s-1, -2b)$ and $F - Z = \frac{1}{4(a+1)}
(s-1, 2b)$, solving the system gives $\mu = \frac{(a-1)^2+b^2}{s-1}$, and:
$$P = \left(\frac{a}{2}, \frac{b(s+1)}{2(s-1)}\right).$$

**Tangents at $B$ and $C$.** The tangent at $B = (-1,0)$ has direction $(k, -1)$
; the tangent at $C = (1,0)$ has direction $(k, 1)$. Their intersection is:
$$T = \left(0, \frac{-1}{k}\right) = \left(0, \frac{-2b}{s-1}\right).$$

**Concurrence.** Line $PG$ passes through $G = \left(\frac{a}{3}, \frac{b}{3}\ri
ght)$ with direction $P - G = \frac{1}{6}\left(a, \frac{b(s+5)}{s-1}\right)$. Se
tting the $x$-coordinate to $0$ gives $t = -\frac{1}{3}$, and the corresponding
$y$-coordinate is:
$$\frac{b}{3} + \frac{b(s+5)}{s-1} \cdot \left(-\frac{1}{3}\right) = \frac{b}{3}
\left(1 - \frac{s+5}{s-1}\right) = \frac{b}{3} \cdot \frac{-6}{s-1} = \frac{-2b}
{s-1}.$$

Thus $T = \left(0, \frac{-2b}{s-1}\right)$ lies on line $PG$. Therefore the tang
ents at $B$ and $C$ to the circumcircle of $ABC$ and the line $PG$ are concurren
t (at $T$). $\blacksquare$
