# Problem

In the interconnected world of global logistics, there are several distribution hubs linked by specialized cargo transport routes. Each transport route connects exactly two distinct hubs and facilitates the movement of goods in both directions. Every route is managed by one of $k=15$ independent logistics corporations.

Due to strict regional licensing laws, any two routes managed by the same logistics corporation must share at least one common hub (either as a mutual starting point, a mutual destination, or a connection point).

The Global Logistics Oversight Committee wants to assign a "scheduling zone" to every hub. To avoid local congestion, the committee mandates that if any two hubs are directly connected by a transport route, they cannot be assigned to the same scheduling zone.

Find the minimum number of scheduling zones $N$ such that, regardless of the number of hubs or how the routes are distributed among the 15 corporations, it is always possible to partition all hubs into these $N$ zones while satisfying the congestion mandate.
