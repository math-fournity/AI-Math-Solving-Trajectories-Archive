### Substitution

Let $u = 2x - y$ and $v = 2y - x$. The Jacobian of the transformation $(x,y) \ma
psto (u,v)$ is

$$J = \begin{vmatrix} \dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x} & \dfrac{\partial u}{\parti
al y} \\[4pt] \dfrac{\partial v}{\partial x} & \dfrac{\partial v}{\partial y} \e
nd{vmatrix} = \begin{vmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = 4 - 1 = 3.$$

Since $J = 3 \neq 0$, the linear map is invertible and hence a bijection from $\
mathbb{R}^2$ onto $\mathbb{R}^2$. Therefore $dx\,dy = \dfrac{1}{3}\,du\,dv$, and
 the integral becomes

$$I = \frac{1}{3}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^2} e^{-|u|-|v|}\,du\,dv.$$

### Separation

The integrand factors as $e^{-|u|}\cdot e^{-|v|}$, so by Fubini's theorem,

$$I = \frac{1}{3}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-|u|}\,du\right)\left(\int_{-\
infty}^{\infty} e^{-|v|}\,dv\right).$$

Each factor is the same elementary integral:

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-|t|}\,dt = \int_{-\infty}^{0} e^{t}\,dt + \int_{0}
^{\infty} e^{-t}\,dt = 1 + 1 = 2.$$

### Result

$$I = \frac{1}{3}\cdot 2 \cdot 2 = \boxed{\dfrac{4}{3}}.$$
