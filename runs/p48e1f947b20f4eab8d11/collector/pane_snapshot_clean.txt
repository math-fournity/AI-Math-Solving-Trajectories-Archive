## Proof

Let $p$ be an odd prime and define $f(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\dfrac{x^{k}}{k}\in\mat
hbb{F}_{p}[x]$. Note that $S_{a}\equiv f(a)\pmod p$ for every integer $a$ (since
 $1/k\equiv k^{-1}\pmod p$). The denominator of $S_{3}+S_{4}-3S_{2}$ (before red
uction) divides $(p-1)!$, which is coprime to $p$; hence, once written in lowest
 terms $m/n$, we have $p\nmid n$, and $p\mid m$ is equivalent to
$$
S_{3}+S_{4}-3S_{2}\equiv 0 \pmod p \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(3)+f(4)-3f(2)
\equiv 0\pmod p.
$$

**Key lemma.** In $\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]$,
$$
f(x)\equiv \frac{1-x^{p}-(1-x)^{p}}{p}\pmod p.
$$

*Proof of lemma.* Expand $(1-x)^{p}=\sum_{j=0}^{p}\binom{p}{j}(-x)^{j}=1+\sum_{j
=1}^{p-1}\binom{p}{j}(-x)^{j}-x^{p}$ (since $p$ is odd). Thus
$$
1-x^{p}-(1-x)^{p}=-\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\binom{p}{j}(-x)^{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}(-1)^{j+
1}\binom{p}{j}x^{j}.
$$
For $1\le j\le p-1$, Wilson's theorem gives $\binom{p}{j}/p=\dfrac{(p-1)!}{j!(p-
j)!}\equiv\dfrac{(-1)^{j-1}}{j}\pmod p$ (using $(p-1)!=(-1)^{j-1}(j-1)!(p-j)!\bm
od p$). Therefore
$$
\frac{1-x^{p}-(1-x)^{p}}{p}\equiv\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}(-1)^{j+1}\cdot\frac{(-1)^{j-1}
}{j}\,x^{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\frac{x^{j}}{j}=f(x).\qquad\square
$$

**Evaluating the combination.** Using the lemma (valid as an integer congruence
modulo $p$, since $1-a^{p}-(1-a)^{p}\equiv 1-a-(1-a)=0\pmod p$ by Fermat, so the
 division by $p$ is legitimate):
$$
f(3)+f(4)-3f(2)\equiv \frac{N}{p}\pmod p,
$$
where
$$
N=\bigl(1-3^{p}-(-2)^{p}\bigr)+\bigl(1-4^{p}-(-3)^{p}\bigr)-3\bigl(1-2^{p}-(-1)^
{p}\bigr).
$$
Since $p$ is odd, $(-2)^{p}=-2^{p}$, $(-3)^{p}=-3^{p}$, $(-1)^{p}=-1$, so
$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=(1-3^{p}+2^{p})+(1-4^{p}+3^{p})-3(2-2^{p})\\
&=2+2^{p}-4^{p}+(3^{p}-3^{p})-6+3\cdot 2^{p}\\
&=-4+4\cdot 2^{p}-4^{p}\\
&=-(2^{p}-2)^{2}\qquad(\text{since }4^{p}=(2^{p})^{2}).
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence
$$
f(3)+f(4)-3f(2)\equiv -\frac{(2^{p}-2)^{2}}{p}\pmod p.
$$
By Fermat's little theorem $2^{p-1}\equiv 1\pmod p$, so $2^{p}-2=2(2^{p-1}-1)$ i
s divisible by $p$, whence $(2^{p}-2)^{2}$ is divisible by $p^{2}$, and therefor
e
$$
\frac{(2^{p}-2)^{2}}{p}\equiv 0\pmod p.
$$
Thus $f(3)+f(4)-3f(2)\equiv 0\pmod p$, i.e. $S_{3}+S_{4}-3S_{2}\equiv 0\pmod p$.

Since the denominator of $S_{3}+S_{4}-3S_{2}$ is coprime to $p$, the reduced fra
ction $m/n$ satisfies $p\nmid n$ and $p\mid m$. $\blacksquare$
