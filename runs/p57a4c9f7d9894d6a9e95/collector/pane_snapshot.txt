## Proof

The object commonly called the "Dirac delta function" is not a function in the c
lassical sense; it is properly the **Dirac measure** $\delta_0$, the Borel measu
re on $\mathbb{R}$ concentrated at the origin, defined by

$$
\delta_0(A) = \begin{cases} 1 & 0 \in A \\ 0 & 0 \notin A \end{cases}
$$

for every Borel set $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}$. Equivalently, as a distribution it
 acts by $\langle \delta_0, \varphi \rangle = \varphi(0)$ for every test functio
n $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$.

**Definition of total variation of a measure.** For any signed (or complex) Bore
l measure $\mu$ on $\mathbb{R}$, the Hahn–Jordan decomposition gives $\mu = \mu^
+ - \mu^-$ with $\mu^+ \perp \mu^-$. The **total variation measure** is $|\mu| =
 \mu^+ + \mu^-$, and the **total variation** (the total variation norm) is

$$
\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}} = |\mu|(\mathbb{R}) = \mu^+(\mathbb{R}) + \mu^-(\mathbb{R}
).
$$

**Apply to $\delta_0$.** The Dirac measure $\delta_0$ is a **positive** measure:
 $\delta_0(A) \ge 0$ for all Borel $A$. Therefore its Hahn decomposition is triv
ial — the whole space $\mathbb{R}$ is a positive set and $\varnothing$ is a nega
tive set — giving

$$
\delta_0^+ = \delta_0, \qquad \delta_0^- = 0.
$$

Hence the total variation measure is

$$
|\delta_0| = \delta_0^+ + \delta_0^- = \delta_0 + 0 = \delta_0,
$$

and the total variation is

$$
\|\delta_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}} = |\delta_0|(\mathbb{R}) = \delta_0(\mathbb{R}) = 1.
$$

**Verification via approximation (consistency check).** Approximate $\delta_0$ b
y the standard Gaussian mollifiers

$$
\delta_\varepsilon(x) = \frac{1}{\varepsilon\sqrt{\pi}}\, e^{-x^2/\varepsilon^2}
, \qquad \varepsilon > 0.
$$

Each $\delta_\varepsilon$ is a nonnegative smooth density with $\int_{\mathbb{R}
} \delta_\varepsilon(x)\,dx = 1$, and $\delta_\varepsilon \to \delta_0$ weakly (
vaguely) as measures when $\varepsilon \to 0$. Interpreting each $\delta_\vareps
ilon$ as the measure $\delta_\varepsilon(x)\,dx$ (a positive measure of total ma
ss $1$), its total variation as a measure is

$$
\|\delta_\varepsilon(x)\,dx\|_{\mathrm{TV}} = \int_{\mathbb{R}} \delta_\varepsil
on(x)\,dx = 1,
$$

which is constant $= 1$ for every $\varepsilon > 0$ and converges to $\|\delta_0
\|_{\mathrm{TV}} = 1$. This confirms the measure-theoretic answer is stable unde
r regularization.

**Conclusion.** The Dirac delta, understood as the unit point-mass measure $\del
ta_0$, is a positive probability measure. Its total variation equals its total m
ass:

$$
\boxed{\|\delta_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}} = 1.}
$$
