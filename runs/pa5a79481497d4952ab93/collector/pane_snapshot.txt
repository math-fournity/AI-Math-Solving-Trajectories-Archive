## Proof

**Combinatorial interpretation via compositions.**

Recall that a *composition* of $m$ into $h$ parts is an ordered $h$-tuple $(a_1,
 a_2, \ldots, a_h)$ of **positive** integers satisfying
$$a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_h = m.$$
The number of such compositions is $\binom{m-1}{h-1}$: this is the classical sta
rs-and-bars count, obtained by placing $h-1$ dividers among the $m-1$ gaps betwe
en $m$ unit stars.

We now show that the left-hand side counts exactly the same set of compositions.

**From a composition to a frequency vector.** Given a composition $(a_1, \ldots,
 a_h)$ of $m$ into $h$ positive parts, let $k = \max_i a_i$ and define
$$s_i = \#\{j : a_j = i\}, \qquad i = 1, 2, \ldots, k.$$
That is, $s_i$ is the number of parts equal to $i$. Then:
- Since there are $h$ parts in total: $\;s_1 + s_2 + \cdots + s_k = h.$
- Since the parts sum to $m$: $\;1\cdot s_1 + 2\cdot s_2 + \cdots + k\cdot s_k =
 m.$

Thus every composition yields a frequency vector $(s_1, \ldots, s_k)$ satisfying
 exactly the two constraints in the problem, with $k \geq 1$ (since $h \geq 1$).

**Counting compositions with a fixed frequency vector.** Conversely, fix any $k
\geq 1$ and any $(s_1, \ldots, s_k)$ with $s_i \geq 0$ satisfying the two constr
aints. A composition with exactly $s_i$ parts equal to $i$ (for each $i$) is det
ermined by the *order* in which the $h$ parts appear. Among the $h$ positions, w
e choose which $s_1$ positions hold the value $1$, which $s_2$ positions hold th
e value $2$, and so on. The number of such arrangements is the multinomial coeff
icient
$$\binom{h}{s_1, s_2, \ldots, s_k} = \frac{h!}{s_1!\, s_2!\, \cdots\, s_k!}.$$

**Summing over all frequency vectors.** Every composition of $m$ into $h$ positi
ve parts corresponds to exactly one frequency vector $(s_1, \ldots, s_k)$ (for s
ome $k \geq 1$) satisfying the two constraints, and for each such frequency vect
or there are exactly $\binom{h}{s_1, \ldots, s_k}$ compositions. Therefore, summ
ing the multinomial coefficient over all admissible $(s_1, \ldots, s_k)$ and ove
r all $k \geq 1$ counts every composition exactly once:
$$\sum_{k \geq 1}\; \sum_{\substack{s_1,\ldots,s_k \geq 0 \\ s_1+\cdots+s_k = h
\\ s_1+2s_2+\cdots+ks_k = m}} \binom{h}{s_1, \ldots, s_k} \;=\; \#\{(a_1,\ldots,
a_h) : a_j \geq 1,\; a_1+\cdots+a_h = m\} \;=\; \binom{m-1}{h-1}.$$

This establishes the identity. $\blacksquare$
