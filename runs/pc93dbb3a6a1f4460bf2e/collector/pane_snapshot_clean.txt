## Solution

**Singularities of** $f(z) = \dfrac{e^{z^2}}{z^2(z-1-i)}$:

- $z = 0$: pole of order 2 (from $z^2$)
- $z = 1+i$: simple pole (from $z - 1 - i$)

**Location relative to contours:**

| Singularity | $\|z\|$ | Inside $\|z\|=2$? | Inside $\|z\|=1$? |
|---|---|---|---|
| $0$ | $0$ | Yes | Yes |
| $1+i$ | $\sqrt{2}\approx 1.414$ | Yes ($\sqrt2<2$) | No ($\sqrt2>1$) |

**Applying the Residue Theorem to each piece:**

$$\oint_{|z|=2\ (\text{CCW})} f\,dz = 2\pi i\bigl(\operatorname{Res}_{0}f + \ope
ratorname{Res}_{1+i}f\bigr),\qquad \oint_{|z|=1\ (\text{CW})} f\,dz = -2\pi i\,\
operatorname{Res}_{0}f.$$

Adding (the contour $C$ is the sum of these two pieces):

$$\oint_C f\,dz = 2\pi i\bigl(\operatorname{Res}_{0}f + \operatorname{Res}_{1+i}
f\bigr) - 2\pi i\,\operatorname{Res}_{0}f = 2\pi i\,\operatorname{Res}_{1+i}f.$$

The residue at $z=0$ cancels exactly — only the residue at $z=1+i$ contributes.

**Computing $\operatorname{Res}_{1+i} f$ (simple pole):**

$$\operatorname{Res}_{1+i}f = \lim_{z\to 1+i}(z-1-i)\,\frac{e^{z^2}}{z^2(z-1-i)}
 = \frac{e^{(1+i)^2}}{(1+i)^2}.$$

Now $(1+i)^2 = 1 + 2i + i^2 = 2i$, so

$$\operatorname{Res}_{1+i}f = \frac{e^{2i}}{2i}.$$

**Final value:**

$$\oint_C \frac{e^{z^2}}{z^2(z-1-i)}\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot \frac{e^{2i}}{2i} = \pi\
, e^{2i} = \pi(\cos 2 + i\sin 2).$$

**Verification of the residue at $z=0$ (order-2 pole):**

$$\operatorname{Res}_{0}f = \frac{d}{dz}\!\left[\frac{e^{z^2}}{z-1-i}\right]_{z=
0} = \frac{e^{z^2}\bigl[2z(z-1-i)-1\bigr]}{(z-1-i)^2}\bigg|_{z=0} = \frac{1\cdot
(-1)}{(1+i)^2} = \frac{-1}{2i}.$$

Cross-check: $2\pi i(\operatorname{Res}_0 + \operatorname{Res}_{1+i}) = 2\pi i\!
\left(\frac{-1}{2i}+\frac{e^{2i}}{2i}\right)=\pi(e^{2i}-1)$, and $-2\pi i\,\oper
atorname{Res}_0 = -2\pi i\cdot\frac{-1}{2i}=\pi$. Sum $= \pi(e^{2i}-1)+\pi = \pi
 e^{2i}$. ✓

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \oint_C \frac{e^{z^2}}{z^2(z-1-i)}\,dz = \pi\, e^{2i} = \
pi(\cos 2 + i\sin 2)}$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
