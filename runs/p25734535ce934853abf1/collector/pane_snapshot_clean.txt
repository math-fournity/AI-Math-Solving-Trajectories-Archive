Looking at this problem, I need to determine which statement must hold for every
 infinite topological space $X$ with $X \cong X \times X$. I'll show that statem
ents 1, 2, and 3 each fail by providing counterexamples, making statement 4 the
answer.

## Proof

We exhibit, for each of statements 1–3, an infinite topological space homeomorph
ic to its own square that violates the claimed property.

### Statement 1 is false: a connected example

Let $X = [0,1]^{\omega}$, the **Hilbert cube**, equipped with the product topolo
gy.

- **Infinite:** $[0,1]^{\omega}$ is uncountable.
- **Connected:** Each factor $[0,1]$ is connected, and an arbitrary product of c
onnected spaces is connected (Tychonoff). So $X$ is connected.
- **$X \cong X \times X$:** We have
$$X \times X = [0,1]^{\omega} \times [0,1]^{\omega} \cong [0,1]^{\,\omega \sqcup
 \omega}.$$
Since the disjoint union $\omega \sqcup \omega$ is countably infinite, there is
a bijection $\omega \sqcup \omega \to \omega$, which induces a homeomorphism $[0
,1]^{\,\omega \sqcup \omega} \cong [0,1]^{\omega} = X$.

Thus $X$ is an infinite, **connected** space with $X \cong X \times X$, contradi
cting statement 1. $\square$

### Statement 2 is false: a compact example

The same Hilbert cube $X = [0,1]^{\omega}$ serves here:

- **Compact:** By Tychonoff's theorem, the product of compact spaces is compact.
 Each $[0,1]$ is compact, so $X$ is compact.
- As shown above, $X \cong X \times X$ and $X$ is infinite.

Thus $X$ is an infinite, **compact** space with $X \cong X \times X$, contradict
ing statement 2. $\square$

### Statement 3 is false: a subset of $\mathbb{R}$

Let $X = C$, the **Cantor set**, viewed as a subset of $\mathbb{R}$.

- **Infinite:** $C$ is uncountable.
- **Subset of $\mathbb{R}$:** $C \subseteq [0,1] \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ by constr
uction.
- **$X \cong X \times X$:** The Cantor set is homeomorphic to Cantor space $\{0,
1\}^{\omega}$ (with product topology). Then
$$C \times C \cong \{0,1\}^{\omega} \times \{0,1\}^{\omega} \cong \{0,1\}^{\,\om
ega \sqcup \omega} \cong \{0,1\}^{\omega} \cong C,$$
again using that $\omega \sqcup \omega \cong \omega$.

Thus $C$ is an infinite space, **homeomorphic to a subset of $\mathbb{R}$**, wit
h $C \cong C \times C$, contradicting statement 3. $\square$

### Conclusion

Each of statements 1, 2, and 3 admits a counterexample — an infinite space $X \c
ong X \times X$ violating the claimed property. Therefore none of statements 1–3
 is necessarily true, and the correct answer is:

**4. None of the above.**
