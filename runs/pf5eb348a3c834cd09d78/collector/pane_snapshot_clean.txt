## Proof

**Setup.** Let the five vertices of the pentagon lie on a circle of radius $R$ w
ith center $O$, labeled in order. Let $2\alpha_i$ be the central angle subtended
 by the $i$-th side, so $\alpha_i > 0$ and $\sum_{i=1}^{5} \alpha_i = \pi$. The
side lengths are $a_i = 2R\sin\alpha_i$, giving:

$$a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2+e^2 = 4R^2 \sum_{i=1}^{5} \sin^2 \alpha_i.$$

It suffices to find $M := \sup\left\{\sum_{i=1}^5 \sin^2\alpha_i : \alpha_i \ge
0,\ \sum \alpha_i = \pi\right\}$, then $k = 4M$.

---

### Key Lemma (Merging)

**Lemma.** If $\alpha, \beta \ge 0$ and $\alpha + \beta \le \pi/2$, then $\sin^2
\alpha + \sin^2\beta \le \sin^2(\alpha+\beta)$.

*Proof.* Direct computation:
$$\sin^2(\alpha+\beta) - \sin^2\alpha - \sin^2\beta = 2\sin\alpha\,\sin\beta\,\c
os(\alpha+\beta) \ge 0,$$
since all three factors are non-negative when $\alpha,\beta \ge 0$ and $\alpha+\
beta \le \pi/2$. $\square$

---

### Reduction to three variables

For $n \ge 4$ variables $\alpha_i \ge 0$ with $\sum \alpha_i = \pi$, let $\alpha
_{(1)} \le \alpha_{(2)} \le \cdots$ be the sorted values. The two smallest satis
fy $\alpha_{(1)} + \alpha_{(2)} \le 2\pi/n \le \pi/2$. By the Lemma, replacing $
\alpha_{(1)}, \alpha_{(2)}$ with $\alpha_{(1)}+\alpha_{(2)}$ does not decrease $
\sum \sin^2\alpha_i$, reducing to $n-1$ variables. Iterating:

$$\sum_{i=1}^{5} \sin^2\alpha_i \le \sum_{j=1}^{3} \sin^2\beta_j, \qquad \beta_1
+\beta_2+\beta_3 = \pi,\ \beta_j \ge 0.$$

---

### The three-variable bound

**Claim.** For $\beta_1+\beta_2+\beta_3 = \pi$, $\beta_i \ge 0$:
$$\sin^2\beta_1 + \sin^2\beta_2 + \sin^2\beta_3 \le \frac{9}{4}.$$

*Proof.* Using $\sin^2 x = \frac{1-\cos 2x}{2}$:
$$\sum \sin^2\beta_i = \frac{3}{2} - \frac{1}{2}\sum \cos 2\beta_i.$$

A standard identity for $\beta_1+\beta_2+\beta_3 = \pi$ gives:
$$\cos 2\beta_1 + \cos 2\beta_2 + \cos 2\beta_3 = -1 - 4\cos\beta_1\cos\beta_2\c
os\beta_3.$$

*(Verification:* $\cos 2A + \cos 2B = -2\cos C\cos(A-B)$ using $A+B=\pi-C$, and
$\cos 2C = 2\cos^2 C - 1$; combining yields $-1 - 4\cos A\cos B\cos C$.* )*

Therefore:
$$\sum \sin^2\beta_i = \frac{3}{2} + \frac{1}{2} + 2\cos\beta_1\cos\beta_2\cos\b
eta_3 = 2 + 2\cos\beta_1\cos\beta_2\cos\beta_3.$$

It remains to show $\cos\beta_1\cos\beta_2\cos\beta_3 \le \frac{1}{8}$.

- **Case 1: All $\beta_i \le \pi/2$.** Then $\cos\beta_i \ge 0$. Since $\cos$ is
 concave on $[0, \pi/2]$, Jensen gives $\sum \cos\beta_i \le 3\cos(\pi/3) = \fra
c{3}{2}$. By AM-GM:
$$\cos\beta_1\cos\beta_2\cos\beta_3 \le \left(\frac{\sum \cos\beta_i}{3}\right)^
3 \le \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^3 = \frac{1}{8}.$$

- **Case 2: Some $\beta_i > \pi/2$.** Then $\cos\beta_i < 0$ while the other two
 have $\cos > 0$ (since they sum to $< \pi/2$). The product is negative, hence $
\le \frac{1}{8}$.

In both cases the bound holds, with equality iff $\beta_1 = \beta_2 = \beta_3 =
\pi/3$. $\square$

### Combining

$$\sum_{i=1}^{5}\sin^2\alpha_i \le \frac{9}{4} \implies a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2+e^2 \le
4R^2 \cdot \frac{9}{4} = 9R^2.$$

### Tightness

The bound $\frac{9}{4}$ is attained at $\beta_1=\beta_2=\beta_3=\pi/3$, $\beta_4
=\beta_5=0$, corresponding to a degenerate pentagon (equilateral triangle with t
wo coincident vertices). For any valid convex pentagon ($\alpha_i > 0$), the sum
 is strictly less than $\frac{9}{4}$, but by taking $\alpha_4, \alpha_5 \to 0^+$
 and $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3 \to \pi/3$, the sum approaches $\frac{9}{4}$ ar
bitrarily closely. Concretely, three sides approach $2R\sin(\pi/3) = R\sqrt{3}$
and two sides approach $0$, giving $3 \cdot 3R^2 = 9R^2$.

Therefore:
- $k = 9$ **works**: $a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2+e^2 < 9R^2 \le 9R^2$ for every convex pent
agon.
- Any $k < 9$ **fails**: a near-degenerate pentagon achieves $a^2+\cdots+e^2 > k
R^2$.

---

$$\boxed{k = 9}$$
