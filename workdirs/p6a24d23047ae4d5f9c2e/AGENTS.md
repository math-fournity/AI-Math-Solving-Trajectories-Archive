# Problem

A high-tech manufacturing plant utilizes a massive 3D storage grid organized into a $10 \times 10 \times 10$ arrangement of exactly $1,000$ modular storage bins. Every individual bin is assigned a specific "Access Code" (a designation that represents its color).

The facility is managed through three different scanning perspectives:
1.  **Floor Scans (Horizontal):** There are $10$ horizontal layers. Each layer is a $10 \times 10 \times 1$ slab of bins.
2.  **Wall Scans (Vertical-Front):** There are $10$ vertical slabs spanning from the front to the back. Each slab is a $10 \times 10 \times 1$ arrangement.
3.  **Side Scans (Vertical-Lateral):** There are $10$ vertical slabs spanning from left to right. Each slab is a $10 \times 10 \times 1$ arrangement.

For each of these $30$ slabs, the central computer generates a "Catalog," which is the set of all unique Access Codes present within that specific slab’s $100$ bins. 

The system operates under a strict "Cross-Reference Symmetry" rule: any Catalog generated by a Floor Scan must also be generated by at least one Wall Scan and at least one Side Scan. This symmetry holds for all directions; every Catalog found in one orientation group is guaranteed to exist in the other two orientation groups.

Based on this configuration, what is the maximum possible number of unique Access Codes that could have been used across the entire $1,000$-bin grid?
