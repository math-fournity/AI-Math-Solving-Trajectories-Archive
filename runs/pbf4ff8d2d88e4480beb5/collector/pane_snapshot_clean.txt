## Proof

Let $P(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{2m}{2k}x^k$. The equation $|P(x)|=|x-1|^m$ is equ
ivalent to $P(x)^2=(x-1)^{2m}$, which factors as
$$\bigl(P(x)-(x-1)^m\bigr)\bigl(P(x)+(x-1)^m\bigr)=0.$$
Define $Q_1(x)=P(x)-(x-1)^m$ and $Q_2(x)=P(x)+(x-1)^m$. The real solutions are e
xactly the real roots of $Q_1\cdot Q_2=0$.

**Key identity.** For any $y$, setting $x=y^2$:
$$P(y^2)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{2m}{2k}y^{2k}=\frac{(1+y)^{2m}+(1-y)^{2m}}{2}.$$
Therefore:
$$Q_1(y^2)=\frac{(1+y)^{2m}+(1-y)^{2m}-2(1+y)^m(1-y)^m}{2}=\frac{\bigl((1+y)^m-(
1-y)^m\bigr)^2}{2},$$
$$Q_2(y^2)=\frac{\bigl((1+y)^m+(1-y)^m\bigr)^2}{2}.$$

Set $A(y)=(1+y)^m-(1-y)^m$ (odd polynomial in $y$) and $B(y)=(1+y)^m+(1-y)^m$ (e
ven polynomial in $y$). Write $A(y)=y\,R(y^2)$ and $B(y)=S(y^2)$ for polynomials
 $R,S$ in $x$. Then:
$$Q_1(x)=\frac{x\,R(x)^2}{2},\qquad Q_2(x)=\frac{S(x)^2}{2}.$$

The distinct real roots are: $\{0\}$ (from $Q_1$), the real roots of $R$ (from $
Q_1$), and the real roots of $S$ (from $Q_2$).

**No shared roots.** If $R(\alpha)=S(\alpha)=0$ for some real $\alpha<0$ (writin
g $\alpha=-s^2$, $s>0$), then $\operatorname{Im}((1+is)^m)=0$ and $\operatorname
{Re}((1+is)^m)=0$, so $(1+is)^m=0$, impossible. Also $R(0)=2m\neq0$ and $S(0)=2\
neq0$, so $0$ is not a root of $R$ or $S$.

**No positive real roots.** For $x=t^2>0$: $S(t^2)=(1+t)^m+(1-t)^m>0$ always (bo
th terms positive when $t<1$; when $t\geq1$, checking even/odd $m$ separately gi
ves positivity). Similarly $R(t^2)=\frac{(1+t)^m-(1-t)^m}{t}>0$ for $t>0$. So al
l real roots of $R$ and $S$ are strictly negative.

**Counting negative roots.** For $x=-s^2$, $s>0$: let $1+is=\sqrt{1+s^2}\,e^{i\t
heta}$ with $\theta=\arctan(s)\in(0,\pi/2)$.

- $S(-s^2)=2\operatorname{Re}((1+is)^m)=2(1+s^2)^{m/2}\cos(m\theta)=0 \iff m\the
ta=\frac{\pi}{2}+k\pi$, i.e., $\theta=\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{2m}$, requiring $0<2k+1<m
$.
  - $m$ even: $k\in\{0,\ldots,m/2-1\}$, giving $m/2$ roots.
  - $m$ odd: $k\in\{0,\ldots,(m-3)/2\}$, giving $(m-1)/2$ roots.
  - This equals $\lfloor m/2\rfloor=\deg S$, so all roots of $S$ are real and ne
gative. ✓

- $R(-s^2)=\frac{2\operatorname{Im}((1+is)^m)}{s}=2(1+s^2)^{m/2}\frac{\sin(m\the
ta)}{s}=0 \iff m\theta=k\pi$, i.e., $\theta=\frac{k\pi}{m}$, requiring $0<k<m/2$
.
  - $m$ even: $k\in\{1,\ldots,m/2-1\}$, giving $m/2-1$ roots.
  - $m$ odd: $k\in\{1,\ldots,(m-1)/2\}$, giving $(m-1)/2$ roots.
  - This equals $\lfloor(m-1)/2\rfloor=\deg R$, so all roots of $R$ are real and
 negative. ✓

**Total count.** The number of distinct real solutions is:
$$1+\deg R+\deg S = 1+\left\lfloor\frac{m-1}{2}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{m
}{2}\right\rfloor = 1+(m-1)=m,$$
using the identity $\lfloor(m-1)/2\rfloor+\lfloor m/2\rfloor=m-1$.

**Verification.** $m=1$: $x=0$ (1 solution). $m=2$: $x=0,-1$ (2 solutions). $m=3
$: $x=0,-1/3,-3$ (3 solutions). All confirmed by direct computation.
