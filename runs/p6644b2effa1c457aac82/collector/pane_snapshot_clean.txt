## Proof

We determine all continuous group homomorphisms $f \colon \hat{\mathbb{Z}} \to \
mathbb{Z}$, where $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ carries the profinite topology and $\mathbb
{Z}$ carries its usual (discrete) topology.

**Key topological facts.**

1. $\hat{\mathbb{Z}} = \varprojlim_{n} \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is an inverse lim
it of finite discrete (hence compact Hausdorff) groups. An inverse limit of comp
act Hausdorff spaces is compact Hausdorff, so **$\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is compact**.

2. $\mathbb{Z}$ with its usual topology (the subspace topology inherited from $\
mathbb{R}$) is **discrete**: every singleton $\{n\}$ is open. A discrete group i
s Hausdorff, and its compact subsets are exactly the finite subsets.

**The argument.**

Let $f \colon \hat{\mathbb{Z}} \to \mathbb{Z}$ be a continuous group homomorphis
m.

- Since $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is compact and $f$ is continuous, the image $f(\hat{\
mathbb{Z}})$ is a **compact** subset of $\mathbb{Z}$.
- Since $\mathbb{Z}$ is discrete, every compact subset of $\mathbb{Z}$ is **fini
te**. Hence $f(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})$ is finite.
- Since $f$ is a group homomorphism, $f(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})$ is a **subgroup** of
$\mathbb{Z}$.
- The only finite subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}$ is $\{0\}$ (any nonzero $k \in \mathb
b{Z}$ generates the infinite subgroup $k\mathbb{Z}$).
- Therefore $f(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}) = \{0\}$, i.e., $f$ is the trivial homomorphism
.

**Conclusion.** The trivial homomorphism is clearly continuous, and we have show
n it is the only continuous group homomorphism. Thus

$$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cts}}\!\bigl(\hat{\mathbb{Z}},\, \mathbb{Z}\bigr)
\;=\; \{0\},$$

the set consisting of the single trivial homomorphism.
