^2z^4 - xyz^3 \in I = (x^2y - xz,\; y^2 + 2yz)$ in $\mathbb{R}[x,y,z]$.

 formalization notes. Proceeding.

## Strategy

I compute a Gröbner basis of $I$ (using grevlex, $x > y > z$), then reduce $f$ m
odulo it. The remainder is zero iff $f \in I$, and this yields an equation in $b
$.

## Gröbner Basis Computation

Set $g_1 = x^2y - xz$ (LM $x^2y$), $g_2 = y^2 + 2yz$ (LM $y^2$).

**$S(g_1,g_2)$:** $\text{lcm}(x^2y, y^2)=x^2y^2$.
$$S = y\,g_1 - x^2\,g_2 = (x^2y^2 - xyz) - (x^2y^2 + 2x^2yz) = -xyz - 2x^2yz.$$
Reduce LT $-2x^2yz$ by $g_1$ (quotient $-2z$): subtract $(-2z)g_1 = -2x^2yz + 2x
z^2$, giving $-xyz - 2xz^2$. No further reduction possible. Set
$$g_3 = xyz + 2xz^2 = xz(y+2z), \quad \text{LM } xyz.$$

**$S(g_1,g_3)$:** $\text{lcm}(x^2y, xyz)=x^2yz$.
$$S = z\,g_1 - x\,g_3 = (x^2yz - xz^2) - (x^2yz + 2x^2z^2) = -xz^2 - 2x^2z^2.$$
No leading monomial divides $x^2z^2$. Set
$$g_4 = 2x^2z^2 + xz^2 = xz^2(2x+1), \quad \text{LM } x^2z^2.$$

**Remaining S-polynomials all reduce to 0:**
- $S(g_2,g_3) = xz\,g_2 - y\,g_3 = 0$.
- $S(g_1,g_4) = z^2 g_1 - y\,g_4$: reduces via $g_1$ then $g_3$ to $0$.
- $S(g_2,g_4) = x^2z^2 g_2 - y^2 g_4$: reduces via $g_1, g_2, g_3$ to $0$.
- $S(g_3,g_4) = xz\,g_3 - y\,g_4$: reduces via $g_1, g_4, g_3$ to $0$.

The Gröbner basis is $\{g_1, g_2, g_3, g_4\}$ with leading monomials $\{x^2y,\;
y^2,\; xyz,\; x^2z^2\}$.

## Reducing $f = bx^2z^4 - xyz^3$

**Step 1.** LM of $f$ is $x^2z^4$ (degree 6 > degree 5 of $xyz^3$). Only $x^2z^2
 \mid x^2z^4$ (quotient $z^2$):
$$f \;\longrightarrow\; f - \tfrac{b}{2}\,z^2\,g_4 = -xyz^3 - \tfrac{b}{2}\,xz^4
.$$

**Step 2.** In grevlex, $xyz^3 > xz^4$ (since $\alpha-\beta = (0,1,-1)$, rightmo
st nonzero entry is negative). So LM is $xyz^3$, and $xyz \mid xyz^3$ (quotient
$z^2$):
$$-xyz^3 - \tfrac{b}{2}xz^4 \;\longrightarrow\; \bigl(-xyz^3 - \tfrac{b}{2}xz^4\
bigr) - (-z^2)\,g_3 = \bigl(2 - \tfrac{b}{2}\bigr)\,xz^4.$$

**Step 3.** The monomial $xz^4$ is **standard** (not divisible by any of $x^2y,
y^2, xyz, x^2z^2$). No further reduction. The remainder is
$$\operatorname{rem}(f, G) = \left(2 - \frac{b}{2}\right) xz^4.$$

By the fundamental theorem of Gröbner bases, $f \in I$ iff the remainder is zero
, i.e.,
$$2 - \frac{b}{2} = 0 \implies b = 4.$$

## Verification for $b = 4$

Expressing $g_3, g_4$ in terms of $g_1, g_2$:
- $g_3 = -(y+2z)\,g_1 + x^2\,g_2$ (direct expansion checks).
- $g_4 = -(z + xy + 2xz)\,g_1 + x^3\,g_2$ (direct expansion checks).

For $b=4$: $f = 2z^2\,g_4 - z^2\,g_3$, so
$$f = z^2\bigl(y - 2xy - 4xz\bigr)\,g_1 \;+\; x^2z^2(2x-1)\,g_2.$$

Expanding the right-hand side:
$$z^2(y-2xy-4xz)(x^2y-xz) + x^2z^2(2x-1)(y^2+2yz)$$
$$= \bigl[x^2y^2z^2 - 2x^3y^2z^2 + 2x^2yz^3 - 4x^3yz^3 - xyz^3 + 4x^2z^4\bigr]$$
$$\quad + \bigl[2x^3y^2z^2 + 4x^3yz^3 - x^2y^2z^2 - 2x^2yz^3\bigr]$$
$$= 4x^2z^4 - xyz^3 = f. \quad\checkmark$$

## Conclusion

The unique value is $b = 4$. For any $b \neq 4$, the Gröbner basis remainder $(2
 - b/2)\,xz^4$ is a nonzero polynomial in the standard monomial $xz^4$, proving
$f \notin I$.

$$\boxed{b = 4}$$
