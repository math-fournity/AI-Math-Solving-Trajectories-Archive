## Proof

**Step 1: Symmetry reduction.**

Let $g(x) = \frac{1}{1+2^x}$. Observe that
$$g(x) + g(-x) = \frac{1}{1+2^x} + \frac{1}{1+2^{-x}} = \frac{1}{1+2^x} + \frac{
2^x}{1+2^x} = 1.$$

Let $f(x) = \frac{\sin(13x)}{\sin x}$. Since $\sin$ is odd,
$$f(-x) = \frac{\sin(-13x)}{\sin(-x)} = \frac{-\sin(13x)}{-\sin x} = f(x),$$
so $f$ is **even** (with removable singularities at multiples of $\pi$, where $f
 \to 13$).

Splitting the integral and substituting $x \mapsto -x$ in the negative part:
$$I = \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(x)\,g(x)\,dx = \int_0^{\pi} f(x)\,g(x)\,dx + \int_0^{\
pi} f(-x)\,g(-x)\,dx = \int_0^{\pi} f(x)\bigl[g(x)+g(-x)\bigr]\,dx = \int_0^{\pi
} f(x)\,dx.$$

So
$$I = \int_0^{\pi} \frac{\sin(13x)}{\sin x}\,dx.$$

**Step 2: Dirichlet kernel identity.**

The standard identity (obtained from $D_6(t) = \sum_{k=-6}^{6} e^{ikt} = \frac{\
sin(13t/2)}{\sin(t/2)}$ with $t = 2x$) gives
$$\frac{\sin(13x)}{\sin x} = 1 + 2\sum_{k=1}^{6} \cos(2kx).$$

**Step 3: Integrate term by term.**

$$I = \int_0^{\pi}\!\left(1 + 2\sum_{k=1}^{6}\cos(2kx)\right)dx = \pi + 2\sum_{k
=1}^{6}\int_0^{\pi}\cos(2kx)\,dx.$$

For every $k \ge 1$,
$$\int_0^{\pi}\cos(2kx)\,dx = \frac{\sin(2kx)}{2k}\Bigg|_0^{\pi} = \frac{\sin(2k
\pi)}{2k} = 0.$$

Hence every cosine term vanishes and
$$I = \pi.$$

$$\boxed{\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \frac{\sin(13x)}{\sin x}\cdot\frac{1}{1+2^x}\,dx = \p
i}$$
