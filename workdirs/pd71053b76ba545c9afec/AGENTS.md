# Problem

In a specialized nanotechnology lab, a sequence of 2013 experimental high-stability sensors is arranged in a linear array. Each sensor, denoted by its position $i$ (where $i=1, 2, \dots, 2013$), must be calibrated to one of two specific voltage settings: either $(\sqrt{2}-1)$ volts or $(\sqrt{2}+1)$ volts.

The total energy interference of the array, $S$, is calculated by summing the products of the voltage settings of every pair of adjacent sensors in the sequence. That is:
$$S = x_1x_2 + x_2x_3 + \dots + x_{2012}x_{2013}$$
where $x_i$ represents the voltage setting of the $i$-th sensor.

Due to the nature of these specific voltage constants, the total interference $S$ will occasionally result in a perfect integer value depending on the configuration of settings chosen for the 2013 sensors. Find the total number of different integer values that $S$ can take.
