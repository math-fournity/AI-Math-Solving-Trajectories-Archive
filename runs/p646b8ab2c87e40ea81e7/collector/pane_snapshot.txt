# Optimality Conditions for the Logistic Harvest Control Problem

## Problem Setup

We maximize the functional
$$J(u) = \int_0^T u(t)\,x^u(t)\,dt - c\int_0^T u(t)^2\,dt$$
subject to
$$x'(t) = r\,x(t) - k\,x(t)^2 - u(t)\,x(t), \quad x(0)=x_0>0, \quad 0\le u(t)\le
 M \text{ a.e.}$$

Here $x^u$ denotes the state trajectory driven by control $u$. All constants $c,
 r, k, x_0, M$ are positive.

## Well-posedness of the State Equation

For any $u\in L^2(0,T)$ with $0\le u\le M$, the ODE $x'=rx-kx^2-ux$, $x(0)=x_0>0
$ has a unique global solution on $[0,T]$. The right-hand side $f(t,x)=rx-kx^2-u
(t)x$ is Lipschitz in $x$ locally (in fact globally Lipschitz on bounded sets),
and since $x(0)>0$ and $f(t,0)=0$, the solution remains nonnegative: $x(t)>0$ fo
r all $t\in[0,T]$ (by uniqueness, $x\equiv 0$ is the only solution through $x=0$
, so a positive initial condition never reaches $0$).

## Applying Pontryagin's Maximum Principle

Define the **Hamiltonian**:
$$H(t,x,u,p) = u\,x - c\,u^2 + p\,\bigl(r\,x - k\,x^2 - u\,x\bigr).$$

### State Equation (Hamiltonian System, Part 1)

$$x'(t) = \frac{\partial H}{\partial p} = r\,x(t) - k\,x(t)^2 - u(t)\,x(t), \qqu
ad x(0)=x_0.$$

### Adjoint (Costate) Equation (Hamiltonian System, Part 2)

$$p'(t) = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}\Big|_{(t,x^*(t),u^*(t),p(t))}.$$

Computing:
$$\frac{\partial H}{\partial x} = u + p\,(r - 2k\,x - u),$$
so
$$\boxed{p'(t) = -\,u^*(t) - p(t)\bigl(r - 2k\,x^*(t) - u^*(t)\bigr).}$$

### Transversality (Terminal) Condition

There is no terminal cost ($\Phi(x(T))=0$) and the terminal state $x(T)$ is free
. Therefore:
$$\boxed{p(T) = 0.}$$

### Maximization (Stationarity) Condition

The optimal control $u^*$ maximizes $H$ pointwise over the admissible set $[0,M]
$:
$$u^*(t) \in \arg\max_{0\le u\le M} H\bigl(t, x^*(t), u, p(t)\bigr).$$

Since $H$ is **strictly concave** in $u$ (the coefficient of $u^2$ is $-c<0$), t
he maximizer is unique. The unconstrained stationary point satisfies
$$\frac{\partial H}{\partial u} = x - 2c\,u - p\,x = x\,(1-p) - 2c\,u = 0,$$
giving
$$u = \frac{x\,(1-p)}{2c}.$$

Projecting onto $[0,M]$ (the unique maximizer of a concave function over a conve
x interval is the projection of the unconstrained maximizer):

$$\boxed{u^*(t) = \mathbb{P}_{[0,M]}\!\left(\frac{x^*(t)\,\bigl(1-p(t)\bigr)}{2c
}\right) = \min\!\left(M,\;\max\!\left(0,\;\frac{x^*(t)\,(1-p(t))}{2c}\right)\ri
ght).}$$

Equivalently, writing the projection via the normal cone $\partial I_{[0,M]}(u^*
)$ of the indicator function of $[0,M]$:
$$x^*(t)\,(1-p(t)) - 2c\,u^*(t) \in \partial I_{[0,M]}(u^*(t)) \quad\text{a.e. }
 t\in(0,T),$$
i.e.,
$$\begin{cases} x^*(t)(1-p(t)) - 2c\,u^*(t) \le 0, & u^*(t)=M,\\ x^*(t)(1-p(t))
- 2c\,u^*(t) = 0, & 0 < u^*(t) < M,\\ x^*(t)(1-p(t)) - 2c\,u^*(t) \ge 0, & u^*(t
)=0. \end{cases}$$

## Summary: The Optimality System

An optimal pair $(x^*, u^*)$ with associated costate $p$ satisfies the following
 coupled system a.e. on $(0,T)$:

1. **State equation:**
$$x^{*\prime}(t) = r\,x^*(t) - k\,x^*(t)^2 - u^*(t)\,x^*(t), \qquad x^*(0)=x_0.$
$

2. **Adjoint equation:**
$$p'(t) = -u^*(t) - p(t)\bigl(r - 2k\,x^*(t) - u^*(t)\bigr), \qquad p(T)=0.$$

3. **Stationarity / projection condition:**
$$u^*(t) = \mathbb{P}_{[0,M]}\!\left(\frac{x^*(t)\,(1-p(t))}{2c}\right) \quad\te
xt{a.e.}$$

## Sufficiency (Sketch)

The Hamiltonian $H(t,x,u,p)$ is **concave in $(x,u)$ jointly** (it is linear in
$x$ plus the term $-pkx^2$ with $-pk\le 0$ when $p\ge 0$, and $-cu^2$ with $c>0$
; the cross term $-pux$ is bilinear). Under the concavity of the running payoff
in $(x,u)$ and the convexity structure of the dynamics, Mangasarian's sufficienc
y theorem applies: any triple $(x^*,u^*,p)$ satisfying the above PMP system with
 $p(t)\ge 0$ is a global maximizer. The nonnegativity of $p$ follows from the ad
joint equation with terminal data $p(T)=0$ and the structure of the dynamics (th
e costate measures the shadow value of the stock, which is nonnegative for a har
vest problem).
