## Proof

**Setup.** Let $S=\{1,\dots,n\}$ and let $A_1,\dots,A_m$ be 3-element subsets sa
tisfying conditions (1) and (2), with $m>1$.

**Step 1: Replication numbers.** For each $x\in S$, let $r_x$ be the number of t
riples $A_i$ containing $x$. The triples containing $x$ each contribute a pair $
\{x,y\}$ with $y\neq x$, and by condition (1) every pair $\{x,y\}$ lies in exact
ly one triple. Hence the $2r_x$ "other elements" (counted with multiplicity acro
ss the $r_x$ triples through $x$) are exactly the $n-1$ elements of $S\setminus\
{x\}$, each appearing once. Thus
$$2r_x = n-1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad r_x = \frac{n-1}{2} \text{ for every } x.
$$
In particular $n$ is odd. Counting incidences $(x,A_i)$:
$$3m = \sum_{x} r_x = n\cdot\frac{n-1}{2} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad m = \frac{n(
n-1)}{6}. \tag{∗}$$

**Step 2: Using condition (2).** By condition (2), every pair of distinct triple
s shares exactly one element. Count the pairs $\{A_i,A_j\}$ ($i<j$) in two ways.

First, the total number of such pairs is $\binom{m}{2}$.

Second, count by the shared element. For a fixed $x$, the $r_x=\frac{n-1}{2}$ tr
iples through $x$ pairwise share **exactly** $x$ (they cannot share a second ele
ment $y$, else the pair $\{x,y\}$ would lie in two triples, violating (1)). So t
he $\binom{r_x}{2}$ pairs of triples through $x$ are exactly the pairs whose uni
que common element is $x$. Summing over $x$:
$$\binom{m}{2} = \sum_{x\in S}\binom{r_x}{2} = n\binom{(n-1)/2}{2} = n\cdot\frac
{(n-1)(n-3)}{8}.$$

**Step 3: Solve for $n$.** Using $(∗)$,
$$\binom{m}{2}=\frac{m(m-1)}{2}=\frac{\frac{n(n-1)}{6}\left(\frac{n(n-1)}{6}-1\r
ight)}{2}=\frac{n(n-1)\bigl(n(n-1)-6\bigr)}{72}.$$
Equating and cancelling $n(n-1)\neq 0$:
$$\frac{n(n-1)-6}{72}=\frac{n-3}{8} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad n^2-n-6=9(n-3),$$
$$n^2-10n+21=0=(n-3)(n-7).$$
So $n=3$ or $n=7$.

**Step 4: Exclude $n=3$.** If $n=3$, then $m=\frac{3\cdot 2}{6}=1$, contradictin
g $m>1$. Hence $n=7$.

**Step 5: Existence for $n=7$.** With $n=7$, $(∗)$ gives $m=\frac{7\cdot 6}{6}=7
$. The Fano plane (the projective plane of order 2) realizes this: its 7 points
and 7 lines, each line a 3-element subset, form a Steiner triple system $S(2,3,7
)$ in which any two lines meet in exactly one point. Thus both conditions are sa
tisfiable, and the value $m=7$ is attained.

**Conclusion.** The value of $m$ is $\boxed{7}$.
