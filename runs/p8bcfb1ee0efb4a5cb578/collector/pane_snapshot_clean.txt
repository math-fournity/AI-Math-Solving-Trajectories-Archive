## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. If $E \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is closed and $f: E \to \mathbb{R}
$ is Lipschitz continuous, then $f$ is differentiable at almost every point of $
E$ (with respect to Lebesgue measure restricted to $E$), where differentiability
 is understood in the restricted sense: the limit is taken along points of $E$.

The proof combines the **McShane–Whitney extension theorem** with **Rademacher's
 theorem**.

---

### Step 1: Lipschitz Extension to All of $\mathbb{R}^n$

Let $f: E \to \mathbb{R}$ be Lipschitz with constant $L$, i.e., $|f(x)-f(y)| \le
 L|x-y|$ for all $x,y \in E$. Assume $E \neq \emptyset$ (the empty case is vacuo
us). Define the McShane extension:

$$\widetilde{f}(x) = \inf_{y \in E}\bigl\{ f(y) + L\,|x - y| \bigr\}, \qquad x \
in \mathbb{R}^n.$$

**$\widetilde{f}$ agrees with $f$ on $E$:** For $x \in E$ and any $y \in E$, the
 Lipschitz condition gives $f(y) + L|x-y| \ge f(x)$, so $\widetilde{f}(x) \ge f(
x)$. Taking $y = x$ in the infimum gives $\widetilde{f}(x) \le f(x)$. Hence $\wi
detilde{f}(x) = f(x)$.

**$\widetilde{f}$ is Lipschitz with constant $L$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$:** For any $x
_1, x_2 \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and any $y \in E$:
$$f(y) + L|x_1 - y| \le f(y) + L|x_2 - y| + L|x_1 - x_2|.$$
Taking the infimum over $y$ on the right side: $\widetilde{f}(x_1) \le \widetild
e{f}(x_2) + L|x_1 - x_2|$. By symmetry, $|\widetilde{f}(x_1) - \widetilde{f}(x_2
)| \le L|x_1 - x_2|$.

---

### Step 2: Apply Rademacher's Theorem

**Rademacher's theorem** states that every Lipschitz function $g: \mathbb{R}^n \
to \mathbb{R}$ is differentiable almost everywhere (with respect to $n$-dimensio
nal Lebesgue measure $m_n$).

Applying this to $\widetilde{f}$: there exists a set $N \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$
with $m_n(N) = 0$ such that $\widetilde{f}$ is (Fréchet) differentiable at every
 $x \in \mathbb{R}^n \setminus N$. That is, for each such $x$, there exists a li
near map $D\widetilde{f}(x): \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ with

$$\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\widetilde{f}(x+h) - \widetilde{f}(x) - D\widetilde{f}(x)
(h)}{|h|} = 0.$$

---

### Step 3: Restricted Differentiability of $f$ on $E$

Let $x \in E \setminus N$. Since $\widetilde{f}$ is differentiable at $x$, for a
ny sequence $y_k \in E$ with $y_k \to x$ and $y_k \neq x$, set $h_k = y_k - x$.
Then $h_k \to 0$ and, using $\widetilde{f}|_E = f$:

$$\frac{f(y_k) - f(x) - D\widetilde{f}(x)(y_k - x)}{|y_k - x|} = \frac{\widetild
e{f}(x + h_k) - \widetilde{f}(x) - D\widetilde{f}(x)(h_k)}{|h_k|} \longrightarro
w 0.$$

Therefore $f$ is differentiable at $x$ in the restricted sense (the limit along
$E$ exists and equals the linear map $D\widetilde{f}(x)$).

---

### Step 4: Almost Everywhere on $E$

The set of points in $E$ where $f$ fails to be differentiable is contained in $E
 \cap N$. Since $m_n(N) = 0$, we have $m_n(E \cap N) = 0$. Therefore:

$$f \text{ is differentiable at every point of } E \setminus (E \cap N), \quad m
_n(E \cap N) = 0.$$

This means $f$ is differentiable almost everywhere on $E$ (with respect to Lebes
gue measure).

**Edge case:** If $m_n(E) = 0$, the statement "differentiable a.e. on $E$" holds
 vacuously, since every subset of $E$ has measure zero.

---

### Conclusion

A Lipschitz continuous function defined on a closed set $E \subseteq \mathbb{R}^
n$ **is** differentiable almost everywhere on $E$. The proof proceeds by:

1. **Extending** $f$ to a Lipschitz function $\widetilde{f}$ on all of $\mathbb{
R}^n$ via the McShane–Whitney extension (preserving the Lipschitz constant),
2. **Applying Rademacher's theorem** to conclude $\widetilde{f}$ is differentiab
le a.e. on $\mathbb{R}^n$,
3. **Restricting back** to $E$: at every differentiability point of $\widetilde{
f}$ that lies in $E$, the function $f$ is differentiable in the restricted sense
.

The set of non-differentiability points of $f$ in $E$ is contained in a Lebesgue
-null set, confirming the result. $\blacksquare$
