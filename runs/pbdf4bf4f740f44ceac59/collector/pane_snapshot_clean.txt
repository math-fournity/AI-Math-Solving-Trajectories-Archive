## Proof

**Setup.** Place the circumcenter $O$ at the origin. Let $\vec{A}, \vec{B}, \vec
{C}$ denote the position vectors of the triangle's vertices. Since $O$ is the ci
rcumcenter, $|A|=|B|=|C|=r$ (the circumradius).

**Key relations.**

- **Orthocenter:** By the Euler relation, the orthocenter $R$ satisfies $\vec{R}
 = \vec{A}+\vec{B}+\vec{C}$.
- **Midpoint of $BC$:** $\vec{M} = \tfrac{1}{2}(\vec{B}+\vec{C})$.

Combining these: $\vec{R} = \vec{A} + 2\vec{M}$, so $\vec{A} = \vec{R} - 2\vec{M
}$.

**Rectangle conditions.** $ROMN$ is a rectangle with consecutive vertices $R, O,
 M, N$. The adjacent sides $RO$ and $OM$ are perpendicular, with $|RO|=11$ and $
|OM|=5$. Since $O$ is the origin:

$$|\vec{R}| = 11, \qquad |\vec{M}| = 5, \qquad \vec{R}\cdot\vec{M} = 0.$$

(The perpendicularity $\vec{RO}\cdot\vec{OM}=(-\vec{R})\cdot\vec{M}=0$ gives $\v
ec{R}\cdot\vec{M}=0$.)

**Computing the circumradius.** Since $A$ lies on the circumcircle:

$$r^2 = |A|^2 = |\vec{R}-2\vec{M}|^2 = |\vec{R}|^2 - 4\,\vec{R}\cdot\vec{M} + 4|
\vec{M}|^2 = 121 - 0 + 100 = 221.$$

**Computing $BC$.** Since $M = \tfrac{1}{2}(B+C)$:

$$|B+C|^2 = 4|M|^2 = 4\cdot 25 = 100.$$

Also $|B+C|^2 = |B|^2 + 2\,\vec{B}\cdot\vec{C} + |C|^2 = 2r^2 + 2\,\vec{B}\cdot\
vec{C}$, so:

$$2(221) + 2\,\vec{B}\cdot\vec{C} = 100 \implies \vec{B}\cdot\vec{C} = \frac{100
 - 442}{2} = -171.$$

Now:

$$|B-C|^2 = |B|^2 - 2\,\vec{B}\cdot\vec{C} + |C|^2 = 2r^2 - 2\,\vec{B}\cdot\vec{
C} = 442 + 342 = 784.$$

Therefore:

$$BC = |B-C| = \sqrt{784} = 28.$$

**Verification.** The altitude from $A$ has direction $\vec{R}-\vec{A} = 2\vec{M
}$, and $BC$ has direction $\vec{B}-\vec{C}$. Their dot product is $2\vec{M}\cdo
t(\vec{B}-\vec{C}) = (\vec{B}+\vec{C})\cdot(\vec{B}-\vec{C}) = |B|^2-|C|^2 = 0$,
 confirming the altitude is perpendicular to $BC$. All conditions are consistent
.

$$\boxed{BC = 28}$$
