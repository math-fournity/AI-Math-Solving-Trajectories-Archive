## Key Structural Observation

**Claim:** The minimum number of turns to sort permutation $\sigma$ is $n - m$,
where $m$ is the length of the longest increasing suffix of $\sigma$.

**Lower bound:** Let $S$ be the set of cards never moved (never taken from the t
op). Two constraints:
1. **Suffix constraint:** If a card at position $i$ is never moved, then when it
 rises to the top (after all cards above it are moved), we cannot access any car
d below it without moving it. So all cards below position $i$ must also be unmov
ed. Thus $S$ forms a **suffix** of the original deck.
2. **Increasing constraint:** Unmoved cards retain their relative order, so for
the final deck to be sorted, $S$ must be **increasing**.

Therefore $|S| \leq m$ (longest increasing suffix), and since each non-$S$ card
requires at least one turn, the minimum is $\geq n - m$.

**Upper bound (constructive strategy):** Keep the longest increasing suffix $\si
gma_{n-m+1}, \ldots, \sigma_n$ (which is sorted). Process the first $n-m$ cards
from top to bottom. At each step, take the top card and insert it at its correct
 sorted position among the already-sorted portion (kept suffix + previously plac
ed cards). Since we can insert at *any* position, this always works in exactly $
n-m$ turns.

**Verified on n=3:** [1,2,3]→0, [1,3,2]→2, [2,1,3]→1, [2,3,1]→2, [3,1,2]→1, [3,2
,1]→2, sum = 8. ✓

## Counting

I need $\sum_{\sigma \in S_6} (6 - \text{LIS}(\sigma))$ where LIS = longest incr
easing suffix length.

Let $g(n,k) = \frac{n!}{k!}$ = number of permutations where the last $k$ element
s are increasing (choose $k$ elements for the last $k$ positions: $\binom{n}{k}$
; arrange remaining: $(n-k)!$; last $k$ forced increasing).

Then $f(n,k) = g(n,k) - g(n,k+1)$ counts permutations with longest increasing su
ffix exactly $k$.

**For n=6:**

| $k$ | $g(6,k) = 720/k!$ | $f(6,k)$ | $(6-k) \cdot f(6,k)$ |
|-----|-------------------|----------|-----------------------|
| 1   | 720               | 360      | 1800                  |
| 2   | 360               | 240      | 960                   |
| 3   | 120               | 90       | 270                   |
| 4   | 30                | 24       | 48                    |
| 5   | 6                 | 5        | 5                     |
| 6   | 1                 | 1        | 0                     |

**Verification:** $f$ sums to $360+240+90+24+5+1 = 720 = 6!$ ✓

**Computation:**
$$6! \times E = \sum_{k=1}^{6} (6-k) \cdot f(6,k) = 1800 + 960 + 270 + 48 + 5 +
0 = \boxed{3083}$$
