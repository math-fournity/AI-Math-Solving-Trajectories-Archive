## Proof

We find the operator norm of $T: C[0,\pi] \to C[0,\pi]$, $(Tx)(t) = \int_0^t \co
s(t-s)\, x(s)\, ds$, where $C[0,\pi]$ carries the sup norm $\|x\|_\infty = \max_
{t\in[0,\pi]}|x(t)|$.

### Upper bound

For any $x$ with $\|x\|_\infty \le 1$:

$$|Tx(t)| \le \int_0^t |\cos(t-s)|\,|x(s)|\,ds \le \int_0^t |\cos(t-s)|\,ds.$$

Substituting $u = t-s$:

$$g(t) := \int_0^t |\cos(t-s)|\,ds = \int_0^t |\cos u|\,du.$$

We compute $g(t)$ explicitly. Since $|\cos u| = \cos u$ on $[0,\pi/2]$ and $|\co
s u| = -\cos u$ on $[\pi/2,\pi]$:

- For $t \in [0, \pi/2]$: $g(t) = \sin t$.
- For $t \in [\pi/2, \pi]$: $g(t) = \int_0^{\pi/2}\cos u\,du + \int_{\pi/2}^{t}(
-\cos u)\,du = 1 + \bigl[-\sin u\bigr]_{\pi/2}^{t} = 2 - \sin t$.

Check continuity at $t=\pi/2$: $\sin(\pi/2)=1$ and $2-\sin(\pi/2)=1$. ✓

Now examine monotonicity:
- On $[0,\pi/2]$: $g'(t) = \cos t \ge 0$, so $g$ increases from $0$ to $1$.
- On $[\pi/2,\pi]$: $g'(t) = -\cos t \ge 0$ (since $\cos t \le 0$), so $g$ incre
ases from $1$ to $2-\sin\pi = 2$.

Thus $g$ is non-decreasing on $[0,\pi]$, and

$$\max_{t\in[0,\pi]} g(t) = g(\pi) = \int_0^\pi |\cos u|\,du = 2.$$

Therefore $\|Tx\|_\infty \le 2\,\|x\|_\infty$, giving $\|T\| \le 2$.

### Lower bound (norm is attained in the limit)

The maximum of $g$ is achieved at $t_0 = \pi$. At this point:

$$Tx(\pi) = \int_0^\pi \cos(\pi - s)\, x(s)\, ds.$$

Note $\cos(\pi - s) \le 0$ for $s \in [0, \pi/2]$ and $\cos(\pi - s) \ge 0$ for
$s \in [\pi/2, \pi]$. The "ideal" maximizer is the step function

$$x^*(s) = \begin{cases} -1, & s \in [0, \pi/2), \\ +1, & s \in (\pi/2, \pi], \e
nd{cases}$$

which would give $Tx^*(\pi) = \int_0^\pi |\cos(\pi-s)|\,ds = 2$. While $x^* \not
in C[0,\pi]$, we can approximate it: for any $\epsilon > 0$, choose $\delta > 0$
 small and define a continuous function $x_\delta$ with $\|x_\delta\|_\infty \le
 1$ that equals $-1$ on $[0, \pi/2 - \delta]$, equals $+1$ on $[\pi/2 + \delta,
\pi]$, and connects linearly on $[\pi/2-\delta, \pi/2+\delta]$. Then

$$|Tx_\delta(\pi)| = \int_0^\pi \cos(\pi-s)\,x_\delta(s)\,ds \ge \int_0^\pi |\co
s(\pi-s)|\,ds - 2\!\!\int_{\pi/2-\delta}^{\pi/2+\delta} |\cos(\pi-s)|\,ds \ge 2
- 2\delta.$$

As $\delta \to 0$, $|Tx_\delta(\pi)| \to 2$, so $\|T\| \ge 2$.

### Conclusion

Combining both bounds:

$$\boxed{\|T\| = 2.}$$
