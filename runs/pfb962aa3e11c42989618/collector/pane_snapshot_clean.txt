## Proof

**Setup and interpretation.** The $3\times 3\times 3$ cube rests on a horizontal
 plane, so one of its three dimensions is vertical. A $3\times 3\times 1$ block
is a slab obtained by fixing one coordinate to a single layer. If the "$1$" were
 the vertical dimension, all 9 cube-centers would share the same height, every j
oining line would be horizontal, and no $45^{\circ}$ angle could occur — a trivi
al (zero) answer inconsistent with the problem. Hence the "$1$" is a *horizontal
* direction: the block is a **vertical wall**, a $3\times 3$ grid of unit cubes
standing $3$ high and $3$ wide (and $1$ deep). By the symmetry of the cube, ever
y such vertical slab is congruent, so it suffices to count for one slab.

**Coordinate model.** Index the 9 cubes of the slab by $(i,k)$ with $i,k\in\{0,1
,2\}$, where $i$ is the horizontal index and $k$ the vertical index. Place each
cube's center at $(i,k)$ in a 2D coordinate system (the common depth coordinate
is irrelevant). For two distinct cubes $(i_1,k_1)$ and $(i_2,k_2)$, the horizont
al separation of their centers is $|i_1-i_2|$ and the vertical separation is $|k
_1-k_2|$. The line joining the centers makes angle $\theta$ with the horizontal
plane where
$$\tan\theta=\frac{|k_1-k_2|}{|i_1-i_2|}.$$
The angle is $45^{\circ}$ exactly when $\tan\theta=1$, i.e.
$$|i_1-i_2|=|k_1-k_2|>0.$$
(The strict inequality excludes identical cubes and the degenerate $0^{\circ}$ c
ase.)

**Counting.** With $i,k\in\{0,1,2\}$, the possible positive values of $|i_1-i_2|
$ and $|k_1-k_2|$ are $1$ and $2$.

- **$|i_1-i_2|=|k_1-k_2|=1$.** Index-pairs at horizontal distance $1$: $\{0,1\},
\{1,2\}$ — 2 choices. Index-pairs at vertical distance $1$: $\{0,1\},\{1,2\}$ —
2 choices. Each combination of an $i$-pair and a $k$-pair determines exactly one
 unordered pair of cubes. This gives $2\times 2=4$ pairs.

- **$|i_1-i_2|=|k_1-k_2|=2$.** Index-pairs at distance $2$: only $\{0,2\}$ — 1 c
hoice each for $i$ and $k$. This gives $1\times 1=1$ pair: cubes $(0,0)$ and $(2
,2)$.

No other cases exist (the only positive distances are $1$ and $2$, and equality
is required).

**Total:** $4+1=5$.

The number of ways of selecting two distinct unit cubes from a $3\times 3\times
1$ block such that the line joining their centers makes a $45^{\circ}$ angle wit
h the horizontal plane is $\boxed{5}$.
