## Proof

**Construction of the Riemann surface.** The Riemann surface $S$ of $\log z$ is
the universal covering space of $\mathbb{C}^* = \mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}$. Concr
etely, we realize it as

$$S = \{(z,w) \in \mathbb{C}^* \times \mathbb{C} : e^w = z\},$$

with covering projection $\pi(z,w)=z$ and with the holomorphic function $\log$ o
n $S$ given by $\log(z,w)=w$. The "spiral ramp" picture arises by cutting each s
heet along a ray from the origin and gluing the upper edge of the slit on sheet
$n$ to the lower edge on sheet $n+1$, producing an infinite-order branch point a
t the origin: winding once around $0$ advances one sheet and never returns.

**Conformal equivalence to $\mathbb{C}$.** The map

$$\Phi: \mathbb{C} \to S, \qquad w \mapsto (e^w,\, w)$$

is a biholomorphism, with inverse $\Phi^{-1}(z,w)=w$. Thus $S$ is conformally eq
uivalent to $\mathbb{C}$.

**Equivalence to a sphere with one point removed.** The Riemann sphere $\widehat
{\mathbb{C}} = \mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}$ is obtained from $\mathbb{C}$ by one-po
int (Alexandroff) compactification. Removing any single point from $\widehat{\ma
thbb{C}}$ yields a surface conformally equivalent to $\mathbb{C}$ (via a Möbius
transformation sending the removed point to $\infty$ and then applying $z\mapsto
 1/z$). Since $S\cong\mathbb{C}$, the surface $S$ is conformally equivalent to a
 sphere with exactly one point missing.

**Why a single point suffices — the two "ends" coincide.** On the spiral ramp (i
dentified with the $w$-plane via $\Phi$), there appear to be two distinguished m
issing points:

- **The branch point at $z=0$**: approached as $\operatorname{Re}(w)\to -\infty$
 (since $|z|=e^{\operatorname{Re}(w)}\to 0$).
- **The point $z=\infty$**: approached as $\operatorname{Re}(w)\to +\infty$ (sin
ce $|z|\to\infty$).

However, $\mathbb{C}$ (the $w$-plane) is **connected at infinity**: it has exact
ly **one topological end**. Both limits $\operatorname{Re}(w)\to-\infty$ and $\o
peratorname{Re}(w)\to+\infty$ escape to the same single point $\infty$ of the co
mpactification. Thus the branch point and the point at infinity are not two sepa
rate missing points — they are one and the same.

**Why the replacement is unambiguous.** We verify that the conformal structure e
xtends uniquely to the added point, with no choice involved.

*Near the branch-point end* ($\operatorname{Re}(w)\to-\infty$): the coordinate $
\zeta = e^{w}$ maps the half-plane $\{\operatorname{Re}(w)<-R\}$ conformally ont
o the punctured disk $\{0<|\zeta|<e^{-R}\}$. So a neighborhood of this end is a
punctured disk.

*Near the infinity end* ($\operatorname{Re}(w)\to+\infty$): the coordinate $\zet
a = e^{-w}$ maps $\{\operatorname{Re}(w)>R\}$ conformally onto the punctured dis
k $\{0<|\zeta|<e^{-R}\}$. So a neighborhood of this end is also a punctured disk
.

Since both ends are the same end, a punctured neighborhood of the compactificati
on point is (from either side) a punctured disk. By the **Riemann removable sing
ularity theorem**, a punctured disk carries a unique extension of its conformal
structure to the center: any bounded holomorphic function extends, and the confo
rmal type of the filling is forced. There is no freedom in how the point is inse
rted — the local coordinate $\zeta$ (or $1/\zeta$) is determined by the existing
 conformal structure, and the transition maps remain holomorphic.

Consequently the one-point compactification $\widehat{\mathbb{C}}$ is the unique
 conformal compactification of $S$: the added point is a removable singularity o
f the conformal structure, and the result is the complete Riemann sphere.

**Summary.** The Riemann surface of $\log z$ is the universal cover of $\mathbb{
C}^*$, conformally equivalent to $\mathbb{C}$, hence to a sphere with one point
removed. The infinite-order (logarithmic) branch point at the origin and the poi
nt at infinity are the same topological end of the surface, so only one point is
 missing. Near this end the surface is a punctured disk (from either direction),
 so the Riemann removable singularity theorem forces a unique conformal filling,
 yielding the complete sphere unambiguously.
