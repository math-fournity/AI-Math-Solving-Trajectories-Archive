## Solution

We evaluate the infinite product
$$P = \prod_{r=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{r+1}}\right).$$

**Step 1: Each factor lies in $(0,1)$.**

For $r \geq 1$, we have $r+1 \geq 2$, so $\sqrt{r+1} \geq \sqrt{2} > 1$, hence
$$0 < \frac{1}{\sqrt{r+1}} < 1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 0 < 1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{
r+1}} < 1.$$
Thus every factor is positive and strictly less than $1$, so the partial product
s $P_N = \prod_{r=1}^{N}\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{r+1}}\right)$ form a positive, st
rictly decreasing sequence. The limit $P = \lim_{N\to\infty} P_N$ exists in $[0,
1)$.

**Step 2: Reduce to a divergent series via logarithms.**

Since all factors are positive,
$$\log P_N = \sum_{r=1}^{N}\log\!\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{r+1}}\right).$$
Using the standard inequality $\log(1-x) \leq -x$ valid for $x \in [0,1)$,
$$\log P_N \leq -\sum_{r=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\sqrt{r+1}}.$$

**Step 3: The series diverges.**

The series $\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{r+1}}$ is a $p$-series with $p = \
tfrac{1}{2} \leq 1$, hence it diverges:
$$\sum_{r=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\sqrt{r+1}} \xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{} +\infty.$$
(Equivalently, $\frac{1}{\sqrt{r+1}} \geq \frac{1}{\sqrt{2r}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2
}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}$ for $r\geq 1$, and $\sum 1/\sqrt{r}$ diverges.)

**Step 4: Conclude the product is $0$.**

From Step 2 and Step 3,
$$\log P_N \leq -\sum_{r=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\sqrt{r+1}} \xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{} -
\infty,$$
so $\log P_N \to -\infty$, which means $P_N \to 0$. Since $P_N$ is decreasing an
d bounded below by $0$, the limit exists and equals $0$:
$$P = \lim_{N\to\infty} P_N = 0.$$

**Conclusion.** The infinite product diverges to zero because the factors $1 - 1
/\sqrt{r+1}$ approach $1$ too slowly — specifically, the associated series $\sum
 1/\sqrt{r+1}$ diverges, forcing the product to vanish.

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\prod_{r=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{r+1}}\right) =
0}$$
