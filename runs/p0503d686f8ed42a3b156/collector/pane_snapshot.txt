## Counterexample

The statement is **false** in general. The openness of the $A_i$ allows "escapin
g mass" to leak outside $B$ at every finite stage while vanishing in the limit.

### Construction

Work in $\mathbb{R}$ (so $n=1$). Let
$$B = (-1,\,1), \qquad A_i = \Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{i},\,\tfrac{1}{i}\Bigr) \;\cup\; \
Bigl(1,\,1+\tfrac{1}{i}\Bigr), \quad i = 1,2,\dots$$

### Verification of the hypotheses

1. **Non-empty, open, bounded.** Each $A_i$ is a union of two non-empty open int
ervals and $A_i \subset (-1,\,2)$, so it is open and bounded.

2. **Monotone decreasing.** For every $i$,
$$\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{i+1},\,\tfrac{1}{i+1}\Bigr) \subset \Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{i},\,\tf
rac{1}{i}\Bigr), \qquad \Bigl(1,\,1+\tfrac{1}{i+1}\Bigr) \subset \Bigl(1,\,1+\tf
rac{1}{i}\Bigr),$$
hence $A_{i+1} \subset A_i$.

3. **Intersection.** Since $-1/i \to 0$ and $1/i \to 0$,
$$\bigcap_{i=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{i},\,\tfrac{1}{i}\Bigr) = \{0\}.$$
For the second piece, $x \in \bigcap_{i}(1,\,1+1/i)$ would require $x > 1$ and $
x < 1+1/i$ for all $i$, forcing $x \leq 1$ (let $i\to\infty$), a contradiction;
so
$$\bigcap_{i=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(1,\,1+\tfrac{1}{i}\Bigr) = \varnothing.$$
Therefore
$$\bigcap_{i=1}^{\infty} A_i = \{0\}.$$

4. **Compact embedding in $B$.** $\{0\}$ is compact and $\overline{\{0\}}=\{0\}\
subset (-1,1)=B$, so $\bigcap_i A_i \subset\subset B$.

### No $A_m$ lies in $B$

For every $i$, the point $x_i = 1 + \dfrac{1}{2i}$ satisfies
$$x_i \in \Bigl(1,\,1+\tfrac{1}{i}\Bigr) \subset A_i, \qquad x_i \geq 1,$$
so $x_i \notin (-1,1) = B$. Hence $A_i \not\subset B$ for **every** $i$.

### Why the standard compactness argument fails

The usual result (some $A_m \subset B$) holds when the $A_i$ are **compact** and
 decreasing: one covers $K=\bigcap A_i \subset B$ by the open sets $\mathbb{R}^n
 \setminus A_i$ and uses the finite-intersection property of compact sets. Here
the $A_i$ are only **open**, so the "escaping" interval $(1,\,1+1/i)$ shrinks to
 the point $1 \in \partial B$, which is never captured by any $A_i$'s membership
 test (it lies outside every $A_i$), yet every $A_i$ still protrudes beyond $B$.
 The limit operation removes the protrusion only at $i=\infty$, not at any finit
e stage.

### Conclusion

There need **not** exist an integer $m$ with $A_m \subset B$. The above $(A_i)$
is an explicit counterexample.
