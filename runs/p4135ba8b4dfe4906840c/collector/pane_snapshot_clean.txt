## Solution

**Substitution.** Let $u = x/n$, so $x = nu$ and $dx = n\,du$. The integral beco
mes

$$I_n = \int_0^\infty \frac{\sin(x/n)}{(1+x/n)^n}\,dx = n\int_0^\infty \frac{\si
n u}{(1+u)^n}\,du.$$

**Key bound.** For $u \geq 0$, we use $|\sin u| \leq u$. Then

$$|I_n| \leq n\int_0^\infty \frac{u}{(1+u)^n}\,du.$$

**Evaluating the bounding integral.** Substitute $t = 1+u$ (so $u = t-1$, $du =
dt$):

$$\int_0^\infty \frac{u}{(1+u)^n}\,du = \int_1^\infty \frac{t-1}{t^n}\,dt = \int
_1^\infty t^{1-n}\,dt - \int_1^\infty t^{-n}\,dt.$$

For $n > 2$ both integrals converge, and

$$\int_1^\infty t^{1-n}\,dt = \frac{1}{n-2}, \qquad \int_1^\infty t^{-n}\,dt = \
frac{1}{n-1}.$$

Therefore

$$\int_0^\infty \frac{u}{(1+u)^n}\,du = \frac{1}{n-2} - \frac{1}{n-1} = \frac{1}
{(n-2)(n-1)}.$$

**Squeeze.** Combining,

$$0 \leq |I_n| \leq \frac{n}{(n-2)(n-1)}.$$

As $n \to \infty$,

$$\frac{n}{(n-2)(n-1)} = \frac{n}{n^2 - 3n + 2} \sim \frac{1}{n} \longrightarrow
 0.$$

By the squeeze theorem, $|I_n| \to 0$, hence $I_n \to 0$.

**Conclusion.**

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin(x/n)}{(1+x/n)^n}\,dx = \boxed{0}.$$
