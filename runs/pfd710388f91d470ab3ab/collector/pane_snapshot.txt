## Solution

Let $H_n = 1 + \tfrac{1}{2} + \cdots + \tfrac{1}{n}$ denote the $n$-th harmonic
number. We study

$$S(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{H_n}{n}\,\sin(nx).$$

We claim the series converges for **every** $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

---

### Case 1: $x \equiv 0 \pmod{2\pi}$

Then $\sin(nx) = 0$ for every $n$, so $S(x) = 0$. The series converges trivially
.

---

### Case 2: $x \not\equiv 0 \pmod{2\pi}$

We apply **Dirichlet's test**: if $(a_n)$ is a monotonically decreasing sequence
 tending to $0$, and the partial sums $B_N = \sum_{n=1}^{N} b_n$ are bounded, th
en $\sum a_n b_n$ converges.

Here $a_n = \dfrac{H_n}{n}$ and $b_n = \sin(nx)$.

**Verification that $a_n \to 0$:** Since $H_n = \ln n + \gamma + O(1/n)$, we hav
e $\dfrac{H_n}{n} = O\!\left(\dfrac{\ln n}{n}\right) \to 0$.

**Verification that $(a_n)$ is monotonically decreasing:** Compute

$$a_{n+1} - a_n = \frac{H_{n+1}}{n+1} - \frac{H_n}{n} = \frac{n\,H_{n+1} - (n+1)
\,H_n}{n(n+1)}.$$

Using $H_{n+1} = H_n + \tfrac{1}{n+1}$:

$$n\,H_{n+1} - (n+1)\,H_n = n\!\left(H_n + \tfrac{1}{n+1}\right) - (n+1)\,H_n =
\frac{n}{n+1} - H_n.$$

Since $H_n \geq H_1 = 1 > \dfrac{n}{n+1}$ for every $n \geq 1$, the numerator is
 negative, so

$$a_{n+1} - a_n = \frac{\frac{n}{n+1} - H_n}{n(n+1)} < 0 \quad \text{for all } n
 \geq 1.$$

Thus $(a_n)$ is strictly decreasing.

**Verification that partial sums of $\sin(nx)$ are bounded:** Using the standard
 identity

$$\sum_{n=1}^{N} \sin(nx) = \frac{\sin\!\left(\frac{Nx}{2}\right)\sin\!\left(\fr
ac{(N+1)x}{2}\right)}{\sin(x/2)},$$

which holds when $\sin(x/2) \neq 0$, i.e., $x \not\equiv 0 \pmod{2\pi}$. Therefo
re

$$\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N} \sin(nx)\right| \leq \frac{1}{|\sin(x/2)|},$$

a uniform bound independent of $N$.

**Conclusion of Case 2:** Both conditions of Dirichlet's test are satisfied, so
$S(x)$ converges for every $x \not\equiv 0 \pmod{2\pi}$.

---

### Combining the cases

The series converges for $x \equiv 0 \pmod{2\pi}$ (Case 1) and for $x \not\equiv
 0 \pmod{2\pi}$ (Case 2). Therefore:

$$\boxed{\text{The series converges for all } x \in \mathbb{R}.}$$

**Remark on the nature of convergence.** For $x \not\equiv 0 \pmod{2\pi}$ the co
nvergence is *conditional*, not absolute: since $H_n/n \sim (\ln n)/n$ and $|\si
n(nx)|$ does not tend to $0$ along any subsequence (it is bounded away from $0$
for infinitely many $n$), the series $\sum \frac{H_n}{n}|\sin(nx)|$ diverges by
comparison with $\sum \frac{\ln n}{n}$ restricted to appropriate subsequences.
