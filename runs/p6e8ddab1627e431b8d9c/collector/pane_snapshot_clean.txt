## Proof

**Yes.** Analyticity of $u$ on $\mathbb{T}^2$ implies exponential decay of its F
ourier coefficients: there exist $K>0$ and $a>0$ such that $|\hat{u}_k| \leq K\,
e^{-a\|k\|}$ for all $k\in\mathbb{Z}^2$.

---

### Step 1: Real analyticity on $\mathbb{T}^2$ gives a holomorphic extension to
a strip

Since $u$ is real analytic on $\mathbb{T}^2$, for every $x_0\in\mathbb{T}^2$ the
re exists $r(x_0)>0$ such that $u$ equals a convergent power series $\sum_\alpha
 c_\alpha(x-x_0)^\alpha$ on the polydisc $\{x\in\mathbb{R}^2:\|x-x_0\|_\infty<r(
x_0)\}$.

A power series that converges absolutely on a real polydisc also converges on th
e corresponding complex polydisc (the domain of absolute convergence is determin
ed by the magnitudes $|c_\alpha|$ and is the same for real or complex variables)
. Hence $u$ extends holomorphically to $\{z\in\mathbb{C}^2:|z_j-x_{0,j}|<r(x_0)\
}$.

By compactness of $\mathbb{T}^2$, finitely many such polydiscs cover the real to
rus. Set
$$r_0=\tfrac{1}{2}\min_i r(x_0^{(i)})>0.$$
For any $z$ with $\|z-x_0^{(i)}\|_\infty < r(x_0^{(i)})$, the local holomorphic
extension is available. On overlaps of two such polydiscs, the extensions agree
(both equal $u$ on real points, which form a set with limit points; by the ident
ity theorem they coincide). Thus they patch to a single holomorphic function $\t
ilde u$ on the strip
$$S_{r_0}=\bigl\{z\in\mathbb{C}^2:|\operatorname{Im}(z_1)|<r_0,\;|\operatorname{
Im}(z_2)|<r_0\bigr\},$$
$2\pi$-periodic in each real variable, with $\tilde u|_{\mathbb{T}^2}=u$.

For any $0<\delta<r_0$, the set $\overline{S_\delta}$ modulo $2\pi$-periodicity
is compact, so
$$M:=\sup_{z\in\overline{S_\delta}}|\tilde u(z)|<\infty.$$

---

### Step 2: Shift the integration contour into the complex strip

The Fourier coefficients are
$$\hat u_k=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}u(x_1,x_2)\,e^{-i(k_1
 x_1+k_2 x_2)}\,dx_1\,dx_2.$$

Fix $k=(k_1,k_2)\in\mathbb{Z}^2$ and $0<\delta<r_0$. For each variable $x_j$ wit
h $k_j\neq 0$, shift the contour $x_j\mapsto x_j-i\delta\,\operatorname{sgn}(k_j
)$; if $k_j=0$ no shift is needed (set $\operatorname{sgn}(0)=0$).

**Justification (variable $x_1$, fixed real $x_2$):** The function $g(z_1)=\tild
e u(z_1,x_2)\,e^{-ik_1 z_1}$ is holomorphic in $z_1$ on $|\operatorname{Im}z_1|<
r_0$. Integrate $g$ over the rectangle with vertices $0,\;2\pi,\;2\pi-i\delta\op
eratorname{sgn}(k_1),\;-i\delta\operatorname{sgn}(k_1)$. By Cauchy's theorem the
 integral vanishes. The two vertical sides cancel due to $2\pi$-periodicity ($g(
2\pi+iy)=g(iy)$ since $e^{-ik_1\cdot 2\pi}=1$ and $\tilde u$ is periodic). There
fore
$$\int_0^{2\pi}g(x_1)\,dx_1=\int_0^{2\pi}g\bigl(x_1-i\delta\operatorname{sgn}(k_
1)\bigr)\,dx_1.$$

Repeating for $x_2$ and combining:
$$\hat u_k=\frac{e^{-\delta(|k_1|+|k_2|)}}{(2\pi)^2}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{2\p
i}\tilde u\!\bigl(x_1-i\delta\operatorname{sgn}(k_1),\;x_2-i\delta\operatorname{
sgn}(k_2)\bigr)\,e^{-ik\cdot x}\,dx_1\,dx_2.$$

---

### Step 3: Extract the exponential bound

Taking absolute values and using $|\tilde u|\le M$ on $\overline{S_\delta}$:

$$|\hat u_k|\;\le\; M\,e^{-\delta\|k\|_1},\qquad \|k\|_1=|k_1|+|k_2|.$$

Since $\|k\|_1\ge\|k\|_2=\|k\|$ (Euclidean norm), we have $e^{-\delta\|k\|_1}\le
 e^{-\delta\|k\|}$, so

$$\boxed{|\hat u_k|\;\le\;K\,e^{-a\|k\|}}$$

with $K=M$ and $a=\delta>0$, valid for every $k\in\mathbb{Z}^2$.

---

**Remark (equivalence).** The converse also holds: if $|\hat u_k|\le K e^{-a\|k\
|}$, then $\sum_k\hat u_k e^{ik\cdot z}$ converges uniformly on the strip $|\ope
ratorname{Im}(z_j)|<a/\sqrt{2}$, yielding a holomorphic extension and hence real
 analyticity. Thus analyticity on $\mathbb{T}^2$ and exponential decay of Fourie
r coefficients are equivalent. $\blacksquare$
