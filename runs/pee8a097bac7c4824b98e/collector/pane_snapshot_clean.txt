## Proof

**Step 1: $f$ fixes $\mathbb{Q}$ pointwise.**

Both $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and $\mathbb{Q}_q$ have characteristic 0, so their prime sub
field is $\mathbb{Q}$. Since $f$ is a ring isomorphism, $f(1)=1$, and by additiv
ity/multiplicativity, $f$ fixes every rational number.

**Step 2: $f$ preserves the group of roots of unity.**

If $\zeta^n = 1$ in $\mathbb{Q}_p$, then $f(\zeta)^n = f(\zeta^n) = f(1) = 1$ in
 $\mathbb{Q}_q$. So $f$ maps roots of unity to roots of unity, preserving the gr
oup structure. The groups of roots of unity in $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and $\mathbb{Q}_q$
 must be isomorphic.

**Step 3: Determine the roots of unity in $\mathbb{Q}_p$.**

*For $p$ odd:* Any root of unity $\zeta \in \mathbb{Q}_p$ satisfies $v_p(\zeta)=
0$ (since $|\zeta|^n=1$ forces $|\zeta|=1$), so $\zeta \in \mathbb{Z}_p^*$. Its
reduction mod $p$ lies in $\mathbb{F}_p^*$, which is cyclic of order $p-1$. By H
ensel's lemma, each element of $\mathbb{F}_p^*$ lifts uniquely to a $(p-1)$-th r
oot of unity in $\mathbb{Z}_p$ (Teichmüller representatives), and these are all
roots of unity since any root of unity must reduce to an element of $\mathbb{F}_
p^*$, hence has order dividing $p-1$. So the roots of unity form a **cyclic grou
p of order $p-1$**.

*For $p=2$:* We have $\mathbb{Z}_2^* \cong \{\pm 1\} \times (1+4\mathbb{Z}_2)$,
where $1+4\mathbb{Z}_2 \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$ (the 2-adic integers) is torsion-free
. So the only roots of unity are $\{1, -1\}$, a **cyclic group of order 2**.

**Step 4: Derive the constraint.**

Since the groups of roots of unity must be isomorphic (same order):

| Case | Condition | Result |
|------|-----------|--------|
| Both $p,q$ odd | $p-1 = q-1$ | $p=q$ (trivial) |
| $p=2$, $q$ odd | $2 = q-1$ | $q=3$ |
| $p$ odd, $q=2$ | $p-1 = 2$ | $p=3$ |
| $p=q=2$ | — | trivial |

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

For $f$ to be non-trivial ($p \neq q$), the only possibility is $\{p, q\} = \{2,
 3\}$.

$$p + q = 2 + 3 = \boxed{5}$$
