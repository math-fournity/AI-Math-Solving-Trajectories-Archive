## Proof

Let $A = iI + \tfrac{1}{2}U$, where $U$ is the bilateral shift on $\ell^2(\mathb
b{Z})$.

**Step 1: $U$ is unitary.** The bilateral shift satisfies $U^*U = UU^* = I$, sin
ce $U^*\delta_k = \delta_{k-1}$. Hence $U$ is a unitary (in particular, normal)
operator.

**Step 2: Spectrum of $U$.** A standard result for the bilateral shift gives
$$\sigma(U) = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| = 1\},$$
the unit circle. (One way to see this: $U$ is unitarily equivalent via the Fouri
er transform to multiplication by $e^{i\theta}$ on $L^2(\mathbb{T})$, whose spec
trum is the essential range of $e^{i\theta}$, i.e., the unit circle.)

**Step 3: Spectrum of $A$ via the spectral mapping theorem.** Since $U$ is norma
l and $A = f(U)$ with $f(z) = i + \tfrac{1}{2}z$ (a polynomial, hence continuous
), the spectral mapping theorem for normal operators gives
$$\sigma(A) = f(\sigma(U)) = \left\{i + \tfrac{1}{2}z : |z| = 1\right\}.$$
This is the circle in $\mathbb{C}$ of radius $\tfrac{1}{2}$ centered at $i$.

**Step 4: Invertibility.** The point $0$ lies at distance $|i| = 1$ from the cen
ter $i$. Since $1 > \tfrac{1}{2}$, we have $0 \notin \sigma(A)$, so $A$ is inver
tible.

**Step 5: Norm of $A^{-1}$.** Since $A$ is normal, $A^{-1}$ is also normal, and
for a normal operator $T$,
$$\|T\| = \sup_{\lambda \in \sigma(T)} |\lambda|.$$
By the spectral mapping theorem applied to $g(z) = 1/z$ (valid since $0 \notin \
sigma(A)$),
$$\sigma(A^{-1}) = \left\{\frac{1}{\lambda} : \lambda \in \sigma(A)\right\}.$$
Therefore
$$\|A^{-1}\| = \sup_{\lambda \in \sigma(A)} \frac{1}{|\lambda|} = \frac{1}{\disp
laystyle\min_{\lambda \in \sigma(A)} |\lambda|}.$$

**Step 6: Minimum modulus on the spectrum.** The spectrum of $A$ is the circle $
\{w : |w - i| = \tfrac{1}{2}\}$. The distance from the origin to a point $w$ on
this circle satisfies
$$|\,|w| - |i|\,| \leq |w - i| = \tfrac{1}{2},$$
so $|w| \geq |i| - \tfrac{1}{2} = 1 - \tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{1}{2}$, with equalit
y attained at $w = \tfrac{1}{2}i$ (which corresponds to $z = -1 \in \sigma(U)$).
 Hence
$$\min_{\lambda \in \sigma(A)} |\lambda| = \tfrac{1}{2}.$$

**Conclusion.**
$$\left\|\left(iI + \tfrac{1}{2}U\right)^{-1}\right\| = \frac{1}{1/2} = 2.$$
