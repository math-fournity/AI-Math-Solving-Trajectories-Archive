$$\frac{3a}{2^j} + \frac{3b}{2^k} = \frac{3(a\cdot 2^k + b\cdot 2^j)}{2^{j+k}} \
in R.$$
It is closed under multiplication:
$$\frac{3a}{2^j}\cdot\frac{3b}{2^k} = \frac{9ab}{2^{j+k}} = \frac{3(3ab)}{2^{j+k

**R does not contain 1.** If $1 = 3a/2^k$, then $2^k = 3a$, requiring $3 \mid 2^
k$, which is impossible.

**R is not Noetherian.** For each $n \geq 1$, let $I_n$ be the ideal of $R$ gene
rated by $\{3/2,\, 3/4,\, \dots,\, 3/2^n\}$. I claim $I_1 \subsetneq I_2 \subset
neq I_3 \subsetneq \cdots$ is a strictly ascending chain.

*Step 1: Every element of $I_n$ lies in $9\mathbb{Z}[1/2]$.* Any element of $I_n

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} r_i \cdot \frac{3}{2^i} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{3a_i}{2^{j_i}} \
cdot \frac{3}{2^i} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{9a_i}{2^{j_i+i}} = 9\sum_{i=1}^{n} \fr
ac{a_i}{2^{j_i+i}}.$$
Since $\sum a_i/2^{j_i+i} \in \mathbb{Z}[1/2]$ (the ring of dyadic rationals), e

*Step 2: $3/2^{n+1} \notin 9\mathbb{Z}[1/2]$.* Suppose for contradiction that $3
