# Closed Subschemes: Unions and Intersections

## Setup and definitions

A **closed subscheme** of a scheme $X$ is an equivalence class of closed immersi
ons $Z \hookrightarrow X$, or equivalently (and concretely) a closed subset $Y \
subseteq X$ together with a structure sheaf $\mathcal{O}_Z$ fitting into a short
 exact sequence
$$0 \to \mathcal{I} \to \mathcal{O}_X \to i_*\mathcal{O}_Z \to 0$$
where $i: Z \hookrightarrow X$ is the inclusion and $\mathcal{I}$ is a **quasi-c
oherent ideal sheaf** of $\mathcal{O}_X$. Conversely, every quasi-coherent ideal
 sheaf $\mathcal{I} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X$ determines a unique closed subschem
e $Z = V(\mathcal{I})$ with structure sheaf $\mathcal{O}_X/\mathcal{I}$. This gi
ves a bijection
$$\{\text{closed subschemes of } X\} \;\longleftrightarrow\; \{\text{quasi-coher
ent ideal sheaves } \mathcal{I} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X\}.$$

## Finite union of closed subschemes

Let $Z_1, \dots, Z_n$ be closed subschemes of $X$, corresponding to quasi-cohere
nt ideal sheaves $\mathcal{I}_1, \dots, \mathcal{I}_n \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X$.

**Definition.** The **union** $Z_1 \cup \cdots \cup Z_n$ is the closed subscheme
 of $X$ defined by the ideal sheaf
$$\mathcal{I}_{1} \cap \cdots \cap \mathcal{I}_{n}.$$

**Justification.** The underlying topological space of the closed subscheme $V(\
mathcal{J})$ is $\{x \in X : \mathcal{J}_x \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_x\}$, i.e. the
 set of points where $\mathcal{J}$ is contained in the maximal ideal of the loca
l ring. We have
$$V(\mathcal{I}_1 \cap \cdots \cap \mathcal{I}_n) = V(\mathcal{I}_1) \cup \cdots
 \cup V(\mathcal{I}_n),$$
since a prime (maximal) ideal contains $\bigcap \mathcal{I}_i$ if and only if it
 contains some $\mathcal{I}_i$. (For prime $\mathfrak{p}$: $\bigcap \mathcal{I}_
i \subseteq \mathfrak{p} \Rightarrow \mathcal{I}_i \subseteq \mathfrak{p}$ for s
ome $i$, because if every $\mathcal{I}_i \not\subseteq \mathfrak{p}$, pick $a_i
\in \mathcal{I}_i \setminus \mathfrak{p}$; then $\prod a_i \in \bigcap \mathcal{
I}_i$ but $\prod a_i \notin \mathfrak{p}$, contradiction.) The reverse inclusion
 is immediate. Hence the underlying set is the union of the underlying sets, whi
ch is the correct topological behavior. The scheme structure is the one induced
by $\bigcap \mathcal{I}_i$.

**Why finite.** The intersection $\mathcal{I}_1 \cap \cdots \cap \mathcal{I}_n$
of finitely many quasi-coherent ideal sheaves is again quasi-coherent (it is the
 kernel of the map $\mathcal{O}_X \to \bigoplus \mathcal{O}_X/\mathcal{I}_i$, a
map of quasi-coherent sheaves, and kernels of morphisms of quasi-coherent sheave
s are quasi-coherent). For an **arbitrary** (possibly infinite) family, the inte
rsection of quasi-coherent subsheaves need not be quasi-coherent in general, so
the construction does not extend to infinite unions of closed subschemes within
the category of schemes.

## Arbitrary intersection of closed subschemes

Let $\{Z_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in A}$ be an arbitrary (possibly infinite) family of
closed subschemes of $X$, corresponding to quasi-coherent ideal sheaves $\{\math
cal{I}_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in A}$.

**Definition.** The **intersection** $\bigcap_{\alpha} Z_\alpha$ is the closed s
ubscheme of $X$ defined by the ideal sheaf
$$\mathcal{I} = \sum_{\alpha \in A} \mathcal{I}_\alpha \;\subseteq\; \mathcal{O}
_X,$$
i.e. the sheaf generated by all the $\mathcal{I}_\alpha$ (the sheaf-theoretic su
m, which is the image of $\bigoplus_\alpha \mathcal{I}_\alpha \to \mathcal{O}_X$
).

**Justification.** The sum $\sum_\alpha \mathcal{I}_\alpha$ is quasi-coherent: i
t is the image of a morphism of quasi-coherent sheaves (the direct sum $\bigoplu
s \mathcal{I}_\alpha$ is quasi-coherent, being a direct sum of quasi-coherent sh
eaves — this holds for arbitrary direct sums on a scheme), and images of morphis
ms of quasi-coherent sheaves are quasi-coherent. Hence $\sum_\alpha \mathcal{I}_
\alpha$ defines a closed subscheme.

On underlying topological spaces:
$$V\!\left(\sum_\alpha \mathcal{I}_\alpha\right) = \bigcap_\alpha V(\mathcal{I}_
\alpha),$$
since a prime/maximal ideal contains $\sum \mathcal{I}_\alpha$ if and only if it
 contains every $\mathcal{I}_\alpha$. This is the correct set-theoretic intersec
tion, and it works for **arbitrary** families because the sum of quasi-coherent
sheaves is always quasi-coherent (unlike the intersection, which may fail to be)
.

**Summary of the duality.** The reversal of operations is the hallmark of the Ga
lois-type correspondence between ideals and their vanishing sets:

| Operation on closed subschemes | Operation on ideal sheaves |
|---|---|
| Finite union $Z_1 \cup \cdots \cup Z_n$ | Intersection $\mathcal{I}_1 \cap \cd
ots \cap \mathcal{I}_n$ |
| Arbitrary intersection $\bigcap_\alpha Z_\alpha$ | Sum $\sum_\alpha \mathcal{I
}_\alpha$ |

The asymmetry (finite vs. arbitrary) comes entirely from the quasi-coherence req
uirement: finite intersections and arbitrary sums of quasi-coherent sheaves are
quasi-coherent, but arbitrary intersections need not be.

## The affine case

Now suppose $X = \operatorname{Spec} R$ is affine. The global section functor gi
ves an equivalence of categories between quasi-coherent sheaves on $X$ and $R$-m
odules, and under this equivalence, quasi-coherent ideal sheaves $\mathcal{I} \s
ubseteq \mathcal{O}_X$ correspond bijectively to **ideals** $I \subseteq R$. Exp
licitly, $\mathcal{I} = \widetilde{I}$, the tilde construction, and $\Gamma(X, \
widetilde{I}) = I$. A closed subscheme $Z \subseteq X$ with ideal sheaf $\wideti
lde{I}$ is the affine scheme $\operatorname{Spec}(R/I)$ with the natural closed
immersion $\operatorname{Spec}(R/I) \to \operatorname{Spec}(R)$.

### Finite union in the affine case

Let $Z_i = V(I_i) = \operatorname{Spec}(R/I_i)$ for ideals $I_1, \dots, I_n \sub
seteq R$. The union is:
$$Z_1 \cup \cdots \cup Z_n = V(I_1 \cap \cdots \cap I_n) = \operatorname{Spec}\!
\left(R/(I_1 \cap \cdots \cap I_n)\right).$$

**Explicit description.** The ideal sheaf of the union is $\widetilde{I_1 \cap \
cdots \cap I_n}$, and since the tilde construction commutes with finite intersec
tions of submodules (kernels of maps between quasi-coherent sheaves correspond t
o kernels of the corresponding module maps), we have
$$\widetilde{I_1} \cap \cdots \cap \widetilde{I_n} = \widetilde{I_1 \cap \cdots
\cap I_n}.$$
The structure sheaf of the union is $\mathcal{O}_X / \widetilde{I_1 \cap \cdots
\cap I_n} = \widetilde{R/(I_1 \cap \cdots \cap I_n)}$.

**Remark (Chinese remainder).** When the ideals $I_1, \dots, I_n$ are pairwise c
omaximal ($I_i + I_j = R$ for $i \neq j$), the Chinese Remainder Theorem gives
$$R/(I_1 \cap \cdots \cap I_n) \cong R/I_1 \times \cdots \times R/I_n,$$
so the union is a disjoint union (coproduct) of the $Z_i$ as schemes. In general
, $R/(I_1 \cap \cdots \cap I_n)$ retains information about how the $Z_i$ overlap
 scheme-theoretically.

### Arbitrary intersection in the affine case

Let $Z_\alpha = V(I_\alpha)$ for an arbitrary family of ideals $\{I_\alpha\}_{\a
lpha \in A}$ in $R$. The intersection is:
$$\bigcap_\alpha Z_\alpha = V\!\left(\sum_\alpha I_\alpha\right) = \operatorname
{Spec}\!\left(R \big/ \sum_\alpha I_\alpha\right).$$

**Explicit description.** The ideal $\sum_\alpha I_\alpha$ is the ideal of $R$ g
enerated by all the $I_\alpha$, i.e. the set of all *finite* sums $\sum_{\text{f
inite}} a_{\alpha}$ with $a_\alpha \in I_\alpha$ (only finitely many nonzero ter
ms, since $R$ is a ring and ideals are closed under finite sums). The ideal shea
f of the intersection is
$$\widetilde{\sum_\alpha I_\alpha} = \sum_\alpha \widetilde{I_\alpha},$$
where the equality holds because the tilde construction commutes with arbitrary
sums of submodules (the sum is the image of $\bigoplus_\alpha \widetilde{I_\alph
a} \to \widetilde{R} = \mathcal{O}_X$, and tilde commutes with arbitrary direct
sums and with images). The structure sheaf is $\widetilde{R/\sum_\alpha I_\alpha
}$.

**Special case: two closed subschemes.** For $Z_1 = V(I)$, $Z_2 = V(J)$:
- Union: $Z_1 \cup Z_2 = V(I \cap J) = \operatorname{Spec}(R/(I\cap J))$.
- Intersection: $Z_1 \cap Z_2 = V(I + J) = \operatorname{Spec}(R/(I+J))$.

## Verification of the key sheaf-module correspondences

We confirm the two commutativity facts used above, which are the heart of the af
fine description:

1. **Tilde commutes with finite intersections of submodules.** For submodules $M
_1, M_2 \subseteq M$ with $M$ finitely presented (in particular for ideals $I_1,
 I_2 \subseteq R$), we have $\widetilde{M_1 \cap M_2} = \widetilde{M_1} \cap \wi
detilde{M_2}$. This follows from the left-exactness of the tilde functor applied
 to the exact sequence $0 \to M_1 \cap M_2 \to M_1 \oplus M_2 \to M_1 + M_2 \to
0$ (the map being $(a,b) \mapsto a - b$), using that tilde is exact on finitely
presented modules. For ideals of $R$ (which are submodules of the finitely prese
nted module $R$), this applies. Finite intersections follow by induction.

2. **Tilde commutes with arbitrary sums of submodules.** For any family of submo
dules $M_\alpha \subseteq M$, the sum $\sum M_\alpha$ is the image of $\bigoplus
 M_\alpha \to M$. The tilde functor commutes with arbitrary direct sums ($\widet
ilde{\bigoplus M_\alpha} = \bigoplus \widetilde{M_\alpha}$, since on a distingui
shed open $D(f)$, sections are $(\bigoplus M_\alpha)_f = \bigoplus (M_\alpha)_f$
 as $R_f$-modules) and with cokernels (always, for any module map), hence with i
mages. Therefore $\widetilde{\sum_\alpha M_\alpha} = \sum_\alpha \widetilde{M_\a
lpha}$. Applied to ideals $I_\alpha \subseteq R$: $\widetilde{\sum I_\alpha} = \
sum \widetilde{I_\alpha}$.

These two facts are precisely what make the affine description work, and they al
so explain the finite/arbitrary asymmetry: fact (1) requires finite presentation
 hypotheses (hence finiteness), while fact (2) holds without restriction (hence
arbitrary families are allowed).

## Conclusion

- A **finite union** of closed subschemes $Z_1, \dots, Z_n$ of $X$ (with ideal s
heaves $\mathcal{I}_1, \dots, \mathcal{I}_n$) is the closed subscheme defined by
 $\mathcal{I}_1 \cap \cdots \cap \mathcal{I}_n$. Finiteness is required because
finite intersections of quasi-coherent ideal sheaves are quasi-coherent, while a
rbitrary intersections need not be.

- An **arbitrary intersection** of closed subschemes $\{Z_\alpha\}$ (with ideal
sheaves $\{\mathcal{I}_\alpha\}$) is the closed subscheme defined by $\sum_\alph
a \mathcal{I}_\alpha$. Arbitrariness is permitted because arbitrary sums of quas
i-coherent sheaves are quasi-coherent.

- When $X = \operatorname{Spec} R$ is affine and $Z_i = V(I_i)$, these become:
  - **Union:** $Z_1 \cup \cdots \cup Z_n = V(I_1 \cap \cdots \cap I_n) = \operat
orname{Spec}\bigl(R/(I_1 \cap \cdots \cap I_n)\bigr)$.
  - **Intersection:** $\bigcap_\alpha Z_\alpha = V\bigl(\sum_\alpha I_\alpha\big
r) = \operatorname{Spec}\bigl(R/\sum_\alpha I_\alpha\bigr)$.

The operations on ideals are reversed relative to the operations on subschemes,
reflecting the order-reversing nature of the correspondence $I \mapsto V(I)$.
