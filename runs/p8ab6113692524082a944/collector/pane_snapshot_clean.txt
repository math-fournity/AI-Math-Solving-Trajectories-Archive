## Proof

**Claim.** Yes. There exists a signature $L$ and an $L$-theory $T$ that is not t
he theory of any finite set of its models.

**Construction.** Let $L = \{+,\, \times,\, 0,\, 1\}$ be the language of rings,
and let $T = \mathrm{ACF}$, the theory of algebraically closed fields—i.e., the
set of all first-order $L$-sentences true in every algebraically closed field (o
f any characteristic). This is a consistent, deductively closed $L$-theory.

**Completions of $T$.** A standard result in model theory states that $\mathrm{A
CF}$ becomes complete once the characteristic is fixed. Concretely, the completi
ons of $T$ are:
- $\mathrm{ACF}_0$: the complete theory of algebraically closed fields of charac
teristic $0$,
- $\mathrm{ACF}_p$: the complete theory of algebraically closed fields of charac
teristic $p$, for each prime $p$.

Every model of $T$ is an algebraically closed field of some characteristic $c \i
n \{0\} \cup \{\text{primes}\}$, and its complete theory is the corresponding $\
mathrm{ACF}_c$. There are infinitely many completions (one for each prime, plus
characteristic $0$).

**No finite set of models suffices.** Suppose, for contradiction, that there exi
st models $M_1, \dots, M_n \models T$ with
$$T = \mathrm{Th}(\{M_1, \dots, M_n\}) = \bigcap_{i=1}^{n} \mathrm{Th}(M_i).$$
Each $M_i$ has some characteristic $c_i \in \{0\} \cup \{\text{primes}\}$. The s
et $S = \{c_1, \dots, c_n\}$ is finite, so since there are infinitely many prime
s, we can pick a prime $q \notin S$.

Consider the first-order $L$-sentence:
$$\sigma_q \;:=\; \neg\!\bigl(\underbrace{1 + 1 + \cdots + 1}_{q\text{ times}} =
 0\bigr),$$
which asserts "the characteristic is not $q$."

- **Each $M_i$ satisfies $\sigma_q$:** since $M_i$ has characteristic $c_i \neq
q$, the sum of $q$ copies of $1$ is nonzero in $M_i$. Hence $\sigma_q \in \mathr
m{Th}(M_i)$ for every $i$, so $\sigma_q \in \bigcap_{i=1}^n \mathrm{Th}(M_i) = \
mathrm{Th}(\{M_1,\dots,M_n\})$.

- **$\sigma_q \notin T$:** the theory $T$ has a model of characteristic $q$, nam
ely $\overline{\mathbb{F}_q}$ (the algebraic closure of the finite field $\mathb
b{F}_q$), and this model satisfies $\neg\,\sigma_q$. Since $T$ is the set of sen
tences true in *all* algebraically closed fields, $\sigma_q \notin T$.

This gives $\sigma_q \in \mathrm{Th}(\{M_1,\dots,M_n\})$ but $\sigma_q \notin T$
, contradicting the assumed equality $T = \mathrm{Th}(\{M_1,\dots,M_n\})$.

**Conclusion.** For every finite collection $\{M_1,\dots,M_n\}$ of models of $T$
, we exhibited a sentence $\sigma_q$ that belongs to $\mathrm{Th}(\{M_1,\dots,M_
n\})$ but not to $T$. Therefore $T = \mathrm{ACF}$ is not the theory of any fini
te set of its models. $\blacksquare$
