## Proof

**Yes**, such a triangle exists. I construct one explicitly.

**Construction.** Consider the isosceles triangle with vertices:
$$A = (0, 18), \quad B = (-24, 0), \quad C = (24, 0).$$

All three vertices are lattice points. The side lengths are:
- $a = BC = 48$ (opposite $A$),
- $b = CA = \sqrt{24^2 + 18^2} = \sqrt{900} = 30$ (opposite $B$),
- $c = AB = \sqrt{24^2 + 18^2} = 30$ (opposite $C$).

So the triangle has sides $30, 30, 48$, and $(24, 18, 30)$ is a Pythagorean trip
le ($24^2 + 18^2 = 576 + 324 = 900 = 30^2$).

**Centroid.** By symmetry the centroid lies on the $y$-axis:
$$G = \frac{A+B+C}{3} = \frac{(0,18)+(-24,0)+(24,0)}{3} = \frac{(0,18)}{3} = (0,
 6). \quad \checkmark$$

**Circumcenter.** By symmetry, $O = (0, y_0)$. Equating distances to $A$ and $B$
:
$$|18 - y_0|^2 = 24^2 + y_0^2 \implies 324 - 36y_0 + y_0^2 = 576 + y_0^2 \implie
s y_0 = \frac{324-576}{36} = -7.$$
So $O = (0, -7)$. Verification: distance to $A$ is $|18-(-7)|=25$; distance to $
B$ is $\sqrt{576+49}=\sqrt{625}=25$. $\checkmark$

**Orthocenter.** Using the Euler line relation $H = 3G - 2O$:
$$H = 3(0,6) - 2(0,-7) = (0, 18) - (0, -14) = (0, 32). \quad \checkmark$$
Direct verification: the altitude from $B$ to $AC$ (direction $(4,-3)$, perpendi
cular direction $(3,4)$) parametrized as $(-24+3t,\, 4t)$ meets the $y$-axis at
$t=8$, giving $(0,32)$. $\checkmark$

**Incenter.** By symmetry, $I = (0, r)$ where $r$ is the inradius. The area and
semi-perimeter are:
$$\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 48 \cdot 18 = 432, \qquad s = \frac{48+30+30}
{2} = 54.$$
$$r = \frac{\text{Area}}{s} = \frac{432}{54} = 8.$$
So $I = (0, 8)$. $\checkmark$

Cross-check via the barycentric formula:
$$I = \frac{aA + bB + cC}{a+b+c} = \frac{48(0,18) + 30(-24,0) + 30(24,0)}{108} =
 \frac{(0,864) + (0,0)}{108} = (0, 8). \quad \checkmark$$

**Summary.** The triangle with vertices $A=(0,18)$, $B=(-24,0)$, $C=(24,0)$ has:
| Center | Coordinates |
|---|---|
| Incenter | $(0, 8)$ |
| Circumcenter | $(0, -7)$ |
| Orthocenter | $(0, 32)$ |
| Centroid | $(0, 6)$ |

All four centers are lattice points, and all three vertices are lattice points.
Therefore, such a triangle exists. $\blacksquare$
