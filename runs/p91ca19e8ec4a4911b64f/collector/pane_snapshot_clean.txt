Looking at this problem, I need to find the locus of the incenter of triangle AB

 tangent to the smaller circle.

**Proof.**

Let $\Omega$ be the larger circle (center $O$, radius $R$) and $\omega$ the smal
ler circle (center $O'$, radius $r$), with $\omega$ inside $\Omega$. Set $d = OO
'$.

**Step 1: The incenter lies on line $CO'$.**

Since $CA$ and $CB$ are tangent to $\omega$, the two tangent lines from $C$ to $
\omega$ are symmetric about line $CO'$. Thus $CO'$ bisects $\angle ACB$. The inc
enter $I$ of $\triangle ABC$ lies on the bisector of $\angle C$, so **$I$ lies o
n line $CO'$**.

**Step 2: Establish the key relation $O'I \cdot O'C = \text{const}$.**

Let $L = O'C$ and $\beta = \frac{1}{2}\angle ACB$. Since $CA$ is tangent to $\om
ega$: $\sin\beta = r/L$.

The inradius $\rho = CI \cdot \sin\beta = CI \cdot r/L$. By Euler's formula $OI^
2 = R^2 - 2R\rho$:

$$OI^2 = R^2 - \frac{2Rr \cdot CI}{L}$$

Place $O$ at the origin, $O' = (d,0)$, $C = (R\cos\theta, R\sin\theta)$, so $L^2
 = R^2 + d^2 - 2Rd\cos\theta$. Set $u = CI/L$, so $I = (1-u)C + uO'$. Then:

$$OI^2 = (1-u)^2 R^2 + 2u(1-u)Rd\cos\theta + u^2 d^2$$

Equating and simplifying (expand, cancel $R^2$, divide by $u$, use $Rd\cos\theta
 = (R^2+d^2-L^2)/2$):

$$u \cdot L^2 = R^2 - d^2 + L^2 - 2Rr$$

$$u = 1 + \frac{R^2 - d^2 - 2Rr}{L^2}, \qquad CI = L + \frac{R^2 - d^2 - 2Rr}{L}
$$

Therefore:

$$O'I = |CI - L| = \frac{|R^2 - d^2 - 2Rr|}{L} = \frac{|R^2 - d^2 - 2Rr|}{O'C}$$

$$\boxed{O'I \cdot O'C = |R^2 - d^2 - 2Rr| \quad (\text{constant})}$$

**Step 3: The locus is a circle.**

The relation $O'I \cdot O'C = \text{const}$ with $I$ on line $CO'$ means $I$ is
the image of $C$ under an inversion centered at $O'$ (with power $p = R^2 - d^2
- 2Rr$, composed with reflection through $O'$ when $p < 0$). Since $O'$ lies str
ictly inside $\Omega$ (as $\omega \subset \Omega$), the point $O'$ is not on $\O
mega$, so the inversion is well-defined on all of $\Omega$. Inversion maps a cir
cle not passing through its center to a circle, so **the locus of $I$ is a circl
e**.

**Step 4: Explicit description.**

Substituting the inverse relation $C = O' + p\,\frac{O'-I}{|O'-I|^2}$ into $|C|^
2 = R^2$ and completing the square yields the locus circle with:

- **Center** $Q$ on line $OO'$ (same side as $O'$) at distance $\displaystyle OQ
 = \frac{2Rrd}{R^2 - d^2}$ from $O$,
- **Radius** $\displaystyle \rho = \frac{R\,|R^2 - d^2 - 2Rr|}{R^2 - d^2}$.

**Verification (concentric case $d=0$):** The center becomes $Q = O$ and $\rho =

a$ give $\sin\beta = r/R$; the resulting isoceles triangle has incenter at dista
