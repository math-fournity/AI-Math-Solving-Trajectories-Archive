**Claim.** If $\bigoplus_{i \in I} P_i$ is a projective $R$-module, then each $P
_i$ is projective for every $i \in I$.

**Proof.**

Recall that an $R$-module $Q$ is projective if and only if $Q$ is a direct summa
nd of a free $R$-module. Equivalently (and we will use this characterization), a
 module $Q$ is projective if and only if every short exact sequence $0 \to A \to
 B \to Q \to 0$ splits, i.e., $Q$ is a direct summand of every module that surje
cts onto it.

We use the following elementary fact:

> **Lemma.** A direct summand of a projective module is projective.

*Proof of Lemma.* Suppose $M$ is projective and $M = N \oplus N'$ with $N$ a dir
ect summand. Since $M$ is projective, $M$ is a direct summand of a free module $
F$: $F = M \oplus M' = N \oplus N' \oplus M'$. Hence $N$ is a direct summand of
the free (hence projective) module $F$, so $N$ is projective. ∎

Now let $P = \bigoplus_{i \in I} P_i$ and assume $P$ is projective. Fix an index
 $j \in I$. We show $P_j$ is a direct summand of $P$.

Define the **inclusion** and the **canonical projection**:
$$\iota_j : P_j \hookrightarrow P, \qquad \pi_j : P \twoheadrightarrow P_j,$$
where $\iota_j$ sends $x \in P_j$ to the tuple with $x$ in the $j$-th coordinate
 and $0$ elsewhere, and $\pi_j$ reads off the $j$-th coordinate. By the definiti
on of the direct sum, these satisfy
$$\pi_j \circ \iota_j = \mathrm{id}_{P_j}.$$

This identity means precisely that the short exact sequence
$$0 \longrightarrow \bigoplus_{i \neq j} P_i \longrightarrow P \longrightarrow P
_j \longrightarrow 0$$
splits, with splitting map $\iota_j$. Therefore
$$P \cong P_j \oplus \bigoplus_{i \neq j} P_i,$$
so $P_j$ is a direct summand of $P$.

Since $P$ is projective and $P_j$ is a direct summand of $P$, the Lemma gives th
at $P_j$ is projective.

As $j \in I$ was arbitrary, every $P_i$ is projective. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** The converse is more subtle: an arbitrary direct sum of projective m
odules need not be projective (it is projective if and only if the ring is left
perfect, by the Bass–Papp theorem). But the direction asked here—projectivity of
 the sum implies projectivity of each summand—holds unconditionally and follows
purely from the fact that each summand is a direct summand of the whole.
