## Solution

We work on $c_0$, the Banach space of sequences converging to $0$, equipped with
 the supremum norm $\|x\|_\infty = \sup_{n\ge1}|x_n|$.

**Claim.** The functional $\psi(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}3^n x_n$ is **unbounded** on $c_0
$; equivalently $\|\psi\|=+\infty$. In fact it is not even defined on all of $c_
0$.

---

### Step 1. $\psi$ is unbounded on $c_0$.

For each integer $N\ge1$ define the truncated sequence
$$
x^{(N)}_n=\begin{cases}1,&1\le n\le N,\\[2pt]0,&n>N.\end{cases}
$$
Each $x^{(N)}$ is eventually zero, hence $x^{(N)}\in c_0$, and $\|x^{(N)}\|_\inf
ty=1$. Applying $\psi$,
$$
\psi\!\bigl(x^{(N)}\bigr)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}3^n=3+3^2+\cdots+3^N=\frac{3\,(3^N-1)}{3
-1}=\frac{3(3^N-1)}{2}.
$$
Therefore, for every $N$,
$$
\|\psi\|\;\ge\;\frac{\bigl|\psi(x^{(N)})\bigr|}{\|x^{(N)}\|_\infty}\;=\;\frac{3(
3^N-1)}{2}\;\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}\;+\infty.
$$
Hence $\|\psi\|=+\infty$; the functional is **not bounded** (not continuous) on
$c_0$.

### Step 2. $\psi$ is not even defined on all of $c_0$.

Take $x_n=\dfrac{1}{n}$. Then $x_n\to0$, so $x\in c_0$, but
$$
\sum_{n\ge1}3^n\,x_n=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{3^n}{n}=+\infty,
$$
since $\dfrac{3^n}{n}\to\infty$. Thus the series defining $\psi$ diverges for th
is element of $c_0$, so $\psi$ is not a well-defined functional on the whole spa
ce $c_0$.

### Step 3. General principle (context).

A linear functional of the form $\psi_a(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}a_n x_n$ is bounded on $c
_0$ **if and only if** $(a_n)\in\ell^1$, in which case
$$
\|\psi_a\|=\|(a_n)\|_1=\sum_{n\ge1}|a_n|.
$$
Here $a_n=3^n$, and $\sum_{n\ge1}3^n=+\infty\notin\ell^1$, confirming Steps 1–2:
 the functional is unbounded (and not everywhere defined) on $c_0$.

---

### Conclusion

The functional $\psi(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}3^n x_n$ is **not a bounded linear functiona
l** on $c_0$; its norm is
$$
\boxed{\|\psi\|=+\infty.}
$$
