## Proof

**Setup.** Let $S_0 = (0,0)$ be the origin and $S_k = \sum_{j=1}^{k} a_j$ for $1
 \le k \le 4n$. Each step $a_j \in \{+1, -1, +i, -i\}$ is a unit move in one of
four directions.

**Constraint 1 (Containment):** For every $1 \le k \le 4n$, both $\operatorname{
Re}(S_k)$ and $\operatorname{Im}(S_k)$ lie in $[0,3]$. Since each step changes o
ne coordinate by exactly $\pm 1$, every position $S_k$ has integer coordinates i
n $\{0,1,2,3\} \times \{0,1,2,3\}$, a set of $16$ grid points. The origin $S_0 =
 (0,0)$ also lies in this grid.

**Constraint 2 (No repeated positions):** The condition $\sum_{i=j}^{k} a_i \ne
0$ for all $1 \le j < k \le 4n$ means $S_k \ne S_{j-1}$ whenever $k - (j-1) \ge
2$. For consecutive indices, $S_k - S_{k-1} = a_k \ne 0$ automatically since eve
ry step is a nonzero unit vector. Therefore **all** positions $S_0, S_1, \ldots,
 S_{4n}$ are pairwise distinct.

**Upper bound.** Since all $4n+1$ positions are distinct and lie in the $16$-poi
nt grid:
$$4n + 1 \le 16 \implies n \le \frac{15}{4} = 3.75 \implies n \le 3.$$

**Construction for $n = 3$ (12 steps, 13 distinct positions).** Consider the sna
ke path on the $4 \times 4$ grid starting at the origin:

$$(0,0) \to (1,0) \to (2,0) \to (3,0) \to (3,1) \to (2,1) \to (1,1) \to (0,1) \t
o (0,2) \to (1,2) \to (2,2) \to (3,2) \to (3,3).$$

The corresponding commands are:
$$+1,\; +1,\; +1,\; +i,\; -1,\; -1,\; -1,\; +i,\; +1,\; +1,\; +1,\; +i.$$

**Verification:**
- *Containment:* Every position has both coordinates in $\{0,1,2,3\} \subset [0,
3]$. ✓
- *Distinctness:* The 13 positions listed are all different. ✓
- *No return to origin:* $(0,0)$ appears only as the starting point. ✓

All constraints are satisfied with $n = 3$.

**Conclusion.** The upper bound forces $n \le 3$, and the explicit construction
achieves $n = 3$.

$$\boxed{n = 3}$$
