# Limit of a Diagram in a Category

## Setup

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a category and let $J$ be a **small category** (the indexin
g category / shape). A **diagram** of shape $J$ in $\mathcal{C}$ is a functor

$$D : J \to \mathcal{C}.$$

Concretely, this consists of:
- For each object $j \in \operatorname{Ob}(J)$, an object $D(j) \in \mathcal{C}$
;
- For each morphism $\alpha : j \to k$ in $J$, a morphism $D(\alpha) : D(j) \to
D(k)$ in $\mathcal{C}$;
- Satisfying functoriality: $D(\operatorname{id}_j) = \operatorname{id}_{D(j)}$
and $D(\beta \circ \alpha) = D(\beta) \circ D(\alpha)$ whenever $\beta \circ \al
pha$ is defined in $J$.

## Commutative Cone

A **cone** over the diagram $D : J \to \mathcal{C}$ with **apex** (or **vertex**
) $L \in \operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal{C})$ consists of:

- An object $L \in \mathcal{C}$;
- For each object $j \in \operatorname{Ob}(J)$, a morphism $\lambda_j : L \to D(
j)$ in $\mathcal{C}$ (called the **leg** or **projection** of the cone at $j$);

such that the cone is **commutative**, meaning: for every morphism $\alpha : j \
to k$ in $J$, the following diagram commutes:

$$\begin{array}{ccc}
L & \xrightarrow{\lambda_j} & D(j) \\
& \searrow{\lambda_k} & \downarrow{D(\alpha)} \\
& & D(k)
\end{array}$$

i.e.,

$$D(\alpha) \circ \lambda_j \;=\; \lambda_k.$$

Equivalently, the family $\{\lambda_j\}_{j \in \operatorname{Ob}(J)}$ is a **nat
ural transformation** from the constant functor $\Delta_L : J \to \mathcal{C}$ (
sending every object to $L$ and every morphism to $\operatorname{id}_L$) to the
diagram $D$:

$$\lambda : \Delta_L \Rightarrow D.$$

## Morphism of Cones

Given two cones $(L, \{\lambda_j\})$ and $(M, \{\mu_j\})$ over the same diagram
$D$, a **morphism of cones** $f : (L, \{\lambda_j\}) \to (M, \{\mu_j\})$ is a mo
rphism $f : L \to M$ in $\mathcal{C}$ such that for every $j \in \operatorname{O
b}(J)$:

$$\mu_j \circ f \;=\; \lambda_j.$$

That is, $f$ is compatible with all the legs. This defines the **category of con
es** $\operatorname{Cone}(D)$ over $D$.

## Definition of Limit

A **limit** of the diagram $D : J \to \mathcal{C}$ is a cone $(L, \{\lambda_j\}_
{j \in \operatorname{Ob}(J)})$ over $D$ satisfying the following **universal pro
perty**:

> For every cone $(M, \{\mu_j\}_{j \in \operatorname{Ob}(J)})$ over $D$, there e
xists a **unique** morphism $u : M \to L$ in $\mathcal{C}$ such that for every $
j \in \operatorname{Ob}(J)$:
> $$\lambda_j \circ u \;=\; \mu_j.$$

In other words, every cone over $D$ factors uniquely through the limit cone.

### Equivalent formulations

1. **Terminal object in the category of cones.** A limit of $D$ is a terminal ob
ject in $\operatorname{Cone}(D)$: the cone $(L, \{\lambda_j\})$ such that for ev
ery cone $(M, \{\mu_j\})$ there is a unique cone morphism $(M, \{\mu_j\}) \to (L
, \{\lambda_j\})$.

2. **Representing object.** The limit is an object $L$ together with a natural i
somorphism
$$\mathcal{C}(-,\, L) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Nat}(\Delta_{-},\, D),$$
i.e., the functor $M \mapsto \{\text{cones over } D \text{ with apex } M\}$ is r
epresentable, represented by $L$.

## Summary of Necessary Conditions

For a commutative cone $(L, \{\lambda_j\}_{j \in \operatorname{Ob}(J)})$ to be a
 **limit** of $D : J \to \mathcal{C}$, the following conditions are **all** nece
ssary and (jointly) sufficient:

1. **Cone condition (commutativity):** For every morphism $\alpha : j \to k$ in
$J$,
   $$D(\alpha) \circ \lambda_j = \lambda_k.$$

2. **Universal property (existence):** For every cone $(M, \{\mu_j\})$ over $D$,
 there exists a morphism $u : M \to L$ with $\lambda_j \circ u = \mu_j$ for all
$j$.

3. **Universal property (uniqueness):** Such a morphism $u$ is unique. That is,
if $u, u' : M \to L$ both satisfy $\lambda_j \circ u = \lambda_j \circ u' = \mu_
j$ for all $j \in \operatorname{Ob}(J)$, then $u = u'$.

### Uniqueness up to unique isomorphism

Limits, when they exist, are **unique up to unique isomorphism**: if $(L, \{\lam
bda_j\})$ and $(L', \{\lambda'_j\})$ are both limits of $D$, there exists a uniq
ue isomorphism $\varphi : L \to L'$ such that $\lambda'_j \circ \varphi = \lambd
a_j$ for all $j$.

### Remark on existence

A category $\mathcal{C}$ is called **complete** if it has all small limits (i.e.
, limits of all diagrams $D : J \to \mathcal{C}$ with $J$ small). It is a standa
rd theorem that $\mathcal{C}$ is complete if and only if it has all small produc
ts and all equalizers.
