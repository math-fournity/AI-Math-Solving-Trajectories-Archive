## Fundamental Solution of $\partial_1^2 \partial_2^2$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$

**Claim.** A fundamental solution of the operator $L = \dfrac{\partial^2}{\parti
al x_1^2}\dfrac{\partial^2}{\partial x_2^2}$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ is

$$E(x_1, x_2) = \frac{|x_1|\,|x_2|}{4}.$$

That is, $LE = \delta$ in the sense of distributions.

---

### Proof

**Step 1: One-dimensional building block.** In one dimension, consider $f(t) = \
dfrac{|t|}{2}$, which is a locally integrable function and hence a distribution
on $\mathbb{R}$. Its distributional derivative is

$$f'(t) = \frac{\operatorname{sgn}(t)}{2},$$

which is again locally integrable. Differentiating once more (in the distributio
nal sense) and using $\partial_t \operatorname{sgn}(t) = 2\delta(t)$, we obtain

$$f''(t) = \frac{1}{2}\cdot 2\,\delta(t) = \delta(t).$$

Thus

$$\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2}\left(\frac{|t|}{2}\right) = \delta(t). \tag{1}
$$

**Step 2: Tensor-product construction.** Set $f_1(x_1) = \dfrac{|x_1|}{2}$ and $
f_2(x_2) = \dfrac{|x_2|}{2}$, and define

$$E(x_1,x_2) = f_1(x_1)\,f_2(x_2) = \frac{|x_1|\,|x_2|}{4}.$$

Since $f_1$ and $f_2$ are continuous (hence locally integrable), their tensor pr
oduct $E = f_1 \otimes f_2$ is a well-defined distribution on $\mathbb{R}^2$ (in
 fact a continuous function).

A standard property of tensor products of distributions states that if $T_1, T_2
$ are distributions on $\mathbb{R}$ and $\partial_1, \partial_2$ act on the resp
ective variables, then

$$\partial_1^2\,\partial_2^2\,(T_1 \otimes T_2) = (\partial_1^2 T_1)\otimes(\par
tial_2^2 T_2). \tag{2}$$

Applying (2) with $T_i = f_i$ and using (1):

$$\partial_1^2\,\partial_2^2\, E = \bigl(\partial_1^2 f_1\bigr)\otimes\bigl(\par
tial_2^2 f_2\bigr) = \delta(x_1)\otimes\delta(x_2) = \delta(x_1,x_2).$$

**Step 3: Verification by test functions.** For completeness, let $\varphi \in C
_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^2)$. By definition of distributional derivatives,

$$\langle LE, \varphi\rangle = \langle E, \partial_1^2\partial_2^2\varphi\rangle
 = \int_{\mathbb{R}^2}\frac{|x_1|\,|x_2|}{4}\;\partial_1^2\partial_2^2\varphi(x_
1,x_2)\,dx_1\,dx_2.$$

Since the integrand is compactly supported and $|x_1||x_2|/4$ is continuous, Fub
ini's theorem applies:

$$= \int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|x_2|}{2}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|x_1|}{2}\,\par
tial_1^2\!\left(\partial_2^2\varphi(x_1,x_2)\right)dx_1\right]dx_2.$$

The inner integral is $\langle f_1, \partial_1^2\psi\rangle$ with $\psi(x_1)=\pa
rtial_2^2\varphi(x_1,x_2)$, which by (1) equals $\psi(0)=\partial_2^2\varphi(0,x
_2)$. Hence

$$= \int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|x_2|}{2}\,\partial_2^2\varphi(0,x_2)\,dx_2 = \langle
 f_2, \partial_2^2\varphi(0,\cdot)\rangle = \varphi(0,0) = \langle\delta,\varphi
\rangle.$$

Since this holds for every test function $\varphi$, we conclude $LE = \delta$. $
\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** The fundamental solution is unique up to addition of any distributio
n $H$ satisfying $LH = 0$, i.e., any $H$ of the form $H(x_1,x_2) = a(x_1) + x_1\
,b(x_1) + c(x_2) + x_2\,d(x_2)$ (solutions of $\partial_1^2\partial_2^2 H = 0$).
 The choice $E = \frac{|x_1||x_2|}{4}$ is the natural symmetric one obtained fro
m the tensor product of the one-dimensional fundamental solutions.
