## Solution

**Setup.** Let $p = 2017$ (prime) and $n = p - 1 = 2016$. We count ordered pairs
 $(a, b)$ with $1 \le a, b \le p(p-1)$ and $p \mid a^b - b^a$.

**Case analysis by divisibility of $a, b$ by $p$:**

**Case 1:** $p \mid a$ and $p \mid b$. Then $a^b \equiv 0 \equiv b^a \pmod{p}$.
Always synchronized. The multiples of $p$ in $[1, p(p-1)]$ are $p, 2p, \ldots, (
p-1)p$, giving $(p-1)^2 = n^2$ pairs.

**Cases 2 & 3:** Exactly one of $a, b$ is divisible by $p$. Then $a^b - b^a \not
\equiv 0 \pmod{p}$. Zero pairs.

**Case 4:** $p \nmid a$ and $p \nmid b$. We need $a^b \equiv b^a \pmod{p}$.

**Reduction via CRT.** For each $a$ with $p \nmid a$, write $r = a \bmod p \in \
{1, \ldots, p-1\}$ and $\alpha = a \bmod (p-1) \in \{0, \ldots, n-1\}$. Since $\
gcd(p, p-1) = 1$, the map $a \mapsto (r, \alpha)$ is a bijection from the non-mu
ltiples of $p$ in $[1, p(p-1)]$ to $\{1, \ldots, p-1\} \times \{0, \ldots, n-1\}
$ (each residue $r$ gives $p-1$ values whose residues mod $(p-1)$ run through al
l of $\{0, \ldots, n-1\}$ since $p \equiv 1 \pmod{p-1}$).

By Fermat's little theorem, $a^b \equiv r^\beta \pmod{p}$ and $b^a \equiv s^\alp
ha \pmod{p}$, where $\beta = b \bmod (p-1)$, $s = b \bmod p$.

**Discrete logarithm.** Let $g$ be a primitive root mod $p$. Write $r = g^R$, $s
 = g^S$ with $R, S \in \{0, \ldots, n-1\}$. The condition becomes:
$$R\beta \equiv S\alpha \pmod{n}$$

So $N_4 = \#\{(R, S, \alpha, \beta) \in \{0, \ldots, n-1\}^4 : R\beta \equiv S\a
lpha \pmod{n}\}$.

**Counting $N_4$.** Fix $(\alpha, \beta)$. The congruence $S\alpha \equiv R\beta
 \pmod{n}$ in $S$ has $\gcd(\alpha, n)$ solutions when $\gcd(\alpha, n) \mid R\b
eta$. The number of valid $R$ is $\frac{n \cdot \gcd(\gcd(\alpha,n), \beta)}{\gc
d(\alpha, n)}$, giving:
$$C(\alpha, \beta) = n \cdot \gcd(\alpha, \beta, n)$$

This holds for all $\alpha, \beta$ (including zeros, since $\gcd(0, \beta, n) =
\gcd(\beta, n)$ and $\gcd(0,0,n) = n$). Therefore:
$$N_4 = n \sum_{\alpha=0}^{n-1}\sum_{\beta=0}^{n-1} \gcd(\alpha, \beta, n) = n \
cdot T(n)$$

**Computing $T(n)$.** By inclusion-exclusion over divisors:
$$T(n) = \sum_{f \mid n} \phi(f) \cdot (n/f)^2$$

This is a Dirichlet convolution of multiplicative functions, hence multiplicativ
e. For a prime power $p^k$:
$$T(p^k) = p^{2k} + p^{2k-1} - p^{k-1}$$

With $n = 2016 = 2^5 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 7$:

| Factor | Computation | $T$ |
|--------|-------------|-----|
| $2^5$ | $2^{10} + 2^9 - 2^4 = 1024 + 512 - 16$ | $1520$ |
| $3^2$ | $3^4 + 3^3 - 3^1 = 81 + 27 - 3$ | $105$ |
| $7^1$ | $7^2 + 7^1 - 7^0 = 49 + 7 - 1$ | $55$ |

$$T(2016) = 1520 \times 105 \times 55 = 159600 \times 55 = 8{,}778{,}000$$

**Final count:**
$$N_4 = 2016 \times 8{,}778{,}000 = 17{,}696{,}448{,}000$$
$$N = n^2 + N_4 = 4{,}064{,}256 + 17{,}696{,}448{,}000 = 17{,}700{,}512{,}256$$

**Verification with small case.** For $p = 3$, $n = 2$: $T(2) = 5$, $N_4 = 10$,
$N = 4 + 10 = 14$. Brute-force checking all 16 pairs $(a,b) \in \{1,2,4,5\}^2$ c
onfirms exactly 10 satisfy $3 \mid a^b - b^a$, plus 4 from Case 1, totaling 14.
✓
