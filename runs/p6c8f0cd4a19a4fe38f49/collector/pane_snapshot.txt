## Solution

**Parametrization.** Let the ellipse be $x=3\cos t,\ y=2\sin t$. Set
$$A=(3\cos t_1,2\sin t_1),\quad B=(3\cos t_2,2\sin t_2),\quad C=(3\cos t_3,2\sin
 t_3).$$

**Area formula.** A direct determinant computation gives
$$[\triangle ABC]=\tfrac12\bigl|ab\bigr|\cdot\bigl|\sin(t_2-t_1)+\sin(t_3-t_2)+\
sin(t_1-t_3)\bigr|.$$
Using $\sin A+\sin B+\sin C=-4\sin\tfrac A2\sin\tfrac B2\sin\tfrac C2$ when $A+B
+C=0$, with $a=3,b=2$:
$$[\triangle ABC]=12\left|\sin\tfrac{t_2-t_1}{2}\,\sin\tfrac{t_3-t_2}{2}\,\sin\t
frac{t_1-t_3}{2}\right|.$$

**The constraint $AB\ni P(1,0)$.** Collinearity of $P,A,B$:
$$(3\cos t_1-1)(2\sin t_2)-(2\sin t_1)(3\cos t_2-1)=0.$$
Expanding and using $s=\frac{t_1+t_2}{2},\ d=\frac{t_2-t_1}{2}$ (so $t_1=s-d,\ t
_2=s+d$):
$$6\sin(t_2-t_1)-2(\sin t_2-\sin t_1)=0\;\Longrightarrow\; \sin d\,(12\cos d-4\c
os s)=0.$$
The degenerate case $\sin d=0$ is excluded, so
$$\boxed{\cos s=3\cos d},\qquad\text{hence }|\cos d|\le\tfrac13.$$

**Reducing the area.** Put $u=\frac{t_3-s}{2}$. Then
$$\sin\tfrac{t_3-t_2}{2}\sin\tfrac{t_1-t_3}{2}=\sin\!\bigl(u-\tfrac d2\bigr)\sin
\!\bigl(-u-\tfrac d2\bigr)=\sin^2\tfrac d2-\sin^2 u.$$
Thus, for fixed $d$,
$$[\triangle ABC]=12\,|\sin d|\cdot\bigl|\sin^2\tfrac d2-\sin^2 u\bigr|.$$
Since $\sin^2 u\in[0,1]$, the maximum over $t_3$ is
$$12\,|\sin d|\cdot\max\!\Bigl(\sin^2\tfrac d2,\ \cos^2\tfrac d2\Bigr).$$

**Optimizing in $d$.** Let $c=\cos^2\frac d2\in[0,1]$. Then $\cos d=2c-1$, so th
e constraint $|\cos d|\le\frac13$ becomes $c\in\bigl[\tfrac13,\tfrac23\bigr]$, a
nd $|\sin d|=2\sqrt{c(1-c)}$.

- If $c\ge\frac12$: area $=24\,c^{3/2}(1-c)^{1/2}$. The critical point $c=\frac3
4$ lies outside $\bigl[\frac12,\frac23\bigr]$, so the max is at $c=\frac23$:
$$24\Bigl(\frac23\Bigr)^{3/2}\Bigl(\frac13\Bigr)^{1/2}=24\cdot\frac{2\sqrt2}{9}=
\frac{16\sqrt2}{3}.$$

- If $c\le\frac12$: area $=24\,(1-c)^{3/2}c^{1/2}$. The critical point $c=\frac1
4$ lies outside $\bigl[\frac13,\frac12\bigr]$, so the max is at $c=\frac13$, giv
ing the same value $\frac{16\sqrt2}{3}$.

**Attainment.** Take $c=\frac23$: $\cos d=\frac13,\ s=0$, so $t_1=-d,\ t_2=d$ wi
th $d=\arccos\frac13$, and the maximizing $t_3$ has $\sin^2u=1$, i.e. $t_3=\pi$.
 Concretely
$$A=\Bigl(1,-\tfrac{4\sqrt2}{3}\Bigr),\quad B=\Bigl(1,\tfrac{4\sqrt2}{3}\Bigr),\
quad C=(-3,0).$$
Then $AB$ is the vertical line $x=1$ through $P(1,0)$, and
$$[\triangle ABC]=\tfrac12\Bigl|0\cdot\tfrac{4\sqrt2}{3}-\tfrac{8\sqrt2}{3}\cdot
(-4)\Bigr|=\frac{16\sqrt2}{3}.$$

This is below the unconstrained maximum $\frac{9\sqrt3}{2}\approx7.79$ (the cons
traint reduces it to $\approx7.54$), as expected.

Final Answer: $\boxed{\dfrac{16\sqrt2}{3}}$
