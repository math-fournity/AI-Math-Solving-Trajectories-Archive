## Proof

We evaluate $\oint_{\gamma} \tanh(z)\,dz$ where $\gamma$ is the circle $\left|z
- \tfrac{\pi}{4}i\right| = \tfrac{1}{2}$.

**Poles of $\tanh(z)$.** Since $\tanh(z) = \dfrac{\sinh z}{\cosh z}$, the poles
occur exactly where $\cosh(z) = 0$ and $\sinh(z)\neq 0$. Solving $\cosh(z)=0$:

$$\cosh(z) = 0 \iff e^{z}+e^{-z}=0 \iff e^{2z}=-1 \iff 2z = (2k+1)\pi i,\quad k\
in\mathbb{Z},$$

so the poles are at

$$z_k = \frac{(2k+1)\pi}{2}\,i,\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}.$$

These are all simple poles, with residue $\operatorname{Res}(\tanh, z_k) = \dfra
c{\sinh z_k}{\sinh z_k} = 1$ (using $\cosh'(z)=\sinh(z)$).

**Locating poles relative to $\gamma$.** The contour is centered at $c = \tfrac{
\pi}{4}i$ with radius $R = \tfrac{1}{2}$.

The distance from the center to each pole $z_k = \tfrac{(2k+1)\pi}{2}i$ is

$$|z_k - c| = \left|\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{2}i - \frac{\pi}{4}i\right| = \left|\frac{(
2k+1)\pi}{2} - \frac{\pi}{4}\right| = \frac{\pi}{4}\,|2(2k+1)-1| = \frac{\pi}{4}
\,|4k+1|.$$

For $k=0$: $\frac{\pi}{4}\cdot 1 = \frac{\pi}{4} \approx 0.7854 > \frac{1}{2} =
0.5$.

For $k=-1$: $\frac{\pi}{4}\cdot|-3| = \frac{3\pi}{4} \approx 2.356 > 0.5$.

For all other $k$, $|4k+1|\geq 1$, so every distance is at least $\frac{\pi}{4}
> \frac{1}{2}$.

Hence **no pole of $\tanh(z)$ lies inside $\gamma$**.

**Applying the residue theorem.** $\tanh(z)$ is meromorphic on $\mathbb{C}$, and
 inside and on $\gamma$ it is holomorphic (no poles enclosed, and the contour it
self does not pass through any pole). By Cauchy's theorem (equivalently, the res
idue theorem with zero enclosed residues),

$$\oint_{\gamma} \tanh(z)\,dz = 0.$$
