# Proof: Hyperbolic Triangle Groups are Gromov-Hyperbolic

## Setup and Definitions

A **hyperbolic triangle group** arises from a triangle $T$ in the hyperbolic pla
ne $\mathbb{H}^2$ with interior angles $\pi/p, \pi/q, \pi/r$ satisfying
$$\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}<1,$$
so that $T$ is a genuine hyperbolic triangle. Let $W = W(p,q,r)$ denote the grou
p generated by reflections in the three sides of $T$, and let $\Delta(p,q,r) = \
langle a,b \mid a^p = b^q = (ab)^r = 1\rangle$ denote its orientation-preserving
 subgroup of index 2. Both are referred to as "triangle groups"; we show both ar
e word-hyperbolic (Gromov-hyperbolic).

## Key Ingredients

**Fact 1 (Švarc–Milnor Lemma).** If a group $G$ acts properly discontinuously an
d cocompactly by isometries on a proper geodesic metric space $X$, then $G$ is f
initely generated and (with any word metric) quasi-isometric to $X$.

**Fact 2.** The hyperbolic plane $\mathbb{H}^2$ is a proper geodesic metric spac
e and is $\delta$-hyperbolic (it is $\mathrm{CAT}(-1)$, hence has $\delta$-thin
triangles; in fact every triangle is $\delta$-thin with $\delta = \log 2$ in the
 curvature $-1$ model).

**Fact 3.** Word-hyperbolicity is a quasi-isometry invariant among finitely gene
rated groups.

**Fact 4.** Word-hyperbolicity passes to and from finite-index subgroups.

## The Proof

**Step 1: The fundamental domain is compact.** The triangle $T$ is a closed geod
esic triangle in $\mathbb{H}^2$. Since $1/p+1/q+1/r<1$, the triangle has positiv
e area and is a compact subset of $\mathbb{H}^2$ (it is bounded by three geodesi
c segments meeting at three finite vertices).

**Step 2: The action of $W$ on $\mathbb{H}^2$ is properly discontinuous and coco
mpact.** The reflections in the sides of $T$ generate a discrete subgroup $W \le
q \mathrm{Isom}(\mathbb{H}^2)$. The triangle $T$ serves as a compact fundamental
 domain: the $W$-translates of $T$ tile $\mathbb{H}^2$ (this is the classical Po
incaré polygon theorem applied to the reflection setup). Hence:
- **Cocompactness:** the quotient $W \backslash \mathbb{H}^2$ is the compact orb
ifold obtained from $T$ by identifying edges via reflections.
- **Proper discontinuity:** $W$ is discrete, so its action on $\mathbb{H}^2$ is
properly discontinuous (a standard fact: a discrete subgroup of $\mathrm{Isom}(\
mathbb{H}^2)$ acts properly discontinuously).

**Step 3: Apply Švarc–Milnor.** By Fact 1, $W$ is finitely generated and quasi-i
sometric to $\mathbb{H}^2$.

**Step 4: Conclude $W$ is word-hyperbolic.** By Fact 2, $\mathbb{H}^2$ is $\delt
a$-hyperbolic. By Fact 3, word-hyperbolicity is a quasi-isometry invariant. Sinc
e $W$ is quasi-isometric to the $\delta$-hyperbolic space $\mathbb{H}^2$, the gr
oup $W$ is word-hyperbolic.

**Step 5: The orientation-preserving subgroup $\Delta(p,q,r)$ is also word-hyper
bolic.** The subgroup $\Delta(p,q,r) \leq W$ has index 2 (it consists of the ori
entation-preserving elements, i.e., products of an even number of reflections).
Its fundamental domain is $T \cup T'$ (two adjacent copies of $T$), which is sti
ll compact, so $\Delta(p,q,r)$ also acts cocompactly and properly discontinuousl
y on $\mathbb{H}^2$, and the same argument applies directly. Alternatively, by F
act 4, word-hyperbolicity passes from $W$ to its finite-index subgroup $\Delta(p
,q,r)$.

## Conclusion

Both the full reflection group $W(p,q,r)$ and the orientation-preserving triangl
e group $\Delta(p,q,r)$ act properly discontinuously and cocompactly by isometri
es on the $\delta$-hyperbolic space $\mathbb{H}^2$. By the Švarc–Milnor lemma th
ey are quasi-isometric to $\mathbb{H}^2$, and since word-hyperbolicity is a quas
i-isometry invariant, both are Gromov-hyperbolic (word-hyperbolic) groups.

Therefore, **yes, hyperbolic triangle groups are hyperbolic according to Gromov'
s definition.**
