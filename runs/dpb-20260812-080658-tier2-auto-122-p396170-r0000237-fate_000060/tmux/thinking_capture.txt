Proof.

Let ${N_i}{i \in I}$ be an arbitrary (possibly empty) family of normal subgroups
of a group $G$, indexed by a set $I$. We show that $$N := \bigcap{i \in I} N_i$$
is a normal subgroup of $G$.

Case 1: $I = \emptyset$. Then $N = \bigcap_{i \in \emptyset} N_i = G$, which is
trivially a normal subgroup of $G$.

Case 2: $I \neq \emptyset$. Pick any $j \in I$ as a reference index.

Subgroup test. We use the two-element subgroup criterion: it suffices to show
that $N$ is nonempty and that for all $a, b \in N$, the element $ab^{-1}$ lies
in $N$.

  • Nonempty. Each $N_i$ is a subgroup, hence contains the identity $e$.
  Therefore $e \in N_i$ for every $i \in I$, so $e \in N$. Thus $N \neq
  \emptyset$.
  • Closure under $ab^{-1}$. Let $a, b \in N$. Then $a, b \in N_i$ for every $i
  \in I$. Since each $N_i$ is a subgroup, $ab^{-1} \in N_i$ for every $i \in I$.
  Hence $ab^{-1} \in \bigcap_{i \in I} N_i = N$.

By the two-element subgroup criterion, $N$ is a subgroup of $G$.

Normality. We must show $N$ is closed under conjugation by every element of $G$;
equivalently, $gNg^{-1} = N$ for all $g \in G$.

Let $g \in G$ and $n \in N$. Then $n \in N_i$ for every $i \in I$. Since each
$N_i \trianglelefteq G$, we have $g n g^{-1} \in N_i$ for every $i \in I$.
Therefore $$gng^{-1} \in \bigcap_{i \in I} N_i = N.$$ This shows $gNg^{-1}
\subseteq N$ for every $g \in G$.

Conversely, let $m \in N$ and $g \in G$. Set $h = g^{-1}$, so $g = h^{-1}$.
Since $m \in N_i$ for every $i$ and each $N_i \trianglelefteq G$, we have $h m
h^{-1} \in N_i$ for every $i$, i.e., $g^{-1} m g \in N$. Writing $m' = g^{-1} m
g \in N$, we get $m = g m' g^{-1}$ with $m' \in N$, which shows $m \in
gNg^{-1}$. Hence $N \subseteq gNg^{-1}$.

Combining both inclusions, $gNg^{-1} = N$ for every $g \in G$, so $N
\trianglelefteq G$.

Conclusion. In both cases, the intersection $N = \bigcap_{i \in I} N_i$ is a
normal subgroup of $G$. $\blacksquare$
