# Derivation of the Weyl Dimensionality Formula

## Setup and notation

Let $G$ be a compact, connected Lie group with complexified Lie algebra $\mathfr
ak{g}$, Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$, root system $\Phi \subset \mathfrak{h}
^*$, and a fixed choice of positive roots $\Phi^+$. Let $W$ be the Weyl group. F
or each irreducible representation $V_\lambda$ of highest weight $\lambda \in \m
athfrak{h}^*$ (where $\lambda$ is dominant integral), the **Weyl character formu
la** gives its character $\chi_\lambda$ evaluated at $h \in \mathfrak{h}$:

$$\chi_\lambda(e^h) = \frac{\sum_{w \in W} \varepsilon(w)\, e^{\langle w(\lambda
+\delta),\, h\rangle}}{\sum_{w \in W} \varepsilon(w)\, e^{\langle w\delta,\, h\r
angle}}$$

where:
- $\delta = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha \in \Phi^+} \alpha$ is the **Weyl vector**
(half the sum of positive roots),
- $\varepsilon(w) = \det(w) = \pm 1$ is the sign of the Weyl group element,
- $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is the pairing between weights and the Cartan.

The denominator is the **Weyl denominator**:
$$A(h) := \sum_{w \in W} \varepsilon(w)\, e^{\langle w\delta, h\rangle} = \prod_
{\alpha \in \Phi^+}\bigl(e^{\langle \alpha, h\rangle/2} - e^{-\langle \alpha, h\
rangle/2}\bigr),$$
where the product form is the **Weyl denominator identity**.

## The dimension as the character at the identity

The **dimension** of $V_\lambda$ is the value of the character at the identity e
lement of the group:
$$\dim V_\lambda = \chi_\lambda(e) = \chi_\lambda(e^h)\Big|_{h=0}.$$

However, setting $h = 0$ directly in the Weyl character formula yields the indet
erminate form $\tfrac{0}{0}$, since both numerator and denominator vanish at $h
= 0$ (every term $e^{\langle \cdot, 0\rangle} = 1$, and $\sum_{w}\varepsilon(w)
= 0$ for $|W| > 1$). We must therefore compute the limit $h \to 0$.

## The limit $h \to 0$

Write the character as
$$\chi_\lambda(e^h) = \frac{N(h)}{A(h)}, \qquad N(h) := \sum_{w \in W} \varepsil
on(w)\, e^{\langle w(\lambda+\delta), h\rangle}.$$

Using the product form of the denominator and the analogous product form of the
numerator (obtained by replacing $\delta$ with $\lambda + \delta$ in the denomin
ator identity, which is valid since $\lambda + \delta$ is strictly dominant):

$$N(h) = \prod_{\alpha \in \Phi^+}\bigl(e^{\langle \lambda+\delta, \alpha^\vee\r
angle \langle \alpha, h\rangle/2} - e^{-\langle \lambda+\delta, \alpha^\vee\rang
le \langle \alpha, h\rangle/2}\bigr)$$

Wait — let me be more careful. The numerator $N(h)$ is the Weyl alternant for th
e weight $\mu := \lambda + \delta$, and the denominator $A(h)$ is the Weyl alter
nant for $\delta$. Both admit product forms via the denominator identity applied
 to the respective strictly dominant weights. Specifically, for any strictly dom
inant weight $\mu$:

$$\sum_{w \in W} \varepsilon(w)\, e^{\langle w\mu, h\rangle} = \prod_{\alpha \in
 \Phi^+} \bigl(e^{\langle \mu, \alpha^\vee\rangle \langle \alpha, h\rangle / 2}
- e^{-\langle \mu, \alpha^\vee\rangle \langle \alpha, h\rangle / 2}\bigr).$$

Here $\alpha^\vee = 2\alpha/\langle \alpha, \alpha\rangle$ is the coroot, and we
 use $\langle \mu, \alpha^\vee \rangle$ to express the pairing of $\mu$ with the
 root direction. (This identity follows from the same proof as the Weyl denomina
tor identity: both sides are $W$-alternating and agree on the Weyl chamber walls
.)

Setting $\mu = \lambda + \delta$ for the numerator and $\mu = \delta$ for the de
nominator:

$$N(h) = \prod_{\alpha \in \Phi^+}\Bigl(e^{\frac{1}{2}\langle \lambda+\delta,\,
\alpha^\vee\rangle\langle \alpha, h\rangle} - e^{-\frac{1}{2}\langle \lambda+\de
lta,\, \alpha^\vee\rangle\langle \alpha, h\rangle}\Bigr),$$

$$A(h) = \prod_{\alpha \in \Phi^+}\Bigl(e^{\frac{1}{2}\langle \delta,\, \alpha^\
vee\rangle\langle \alpha, h\rangle} - e^{-\frac{1}{2}\langle \delta,\, \alpha^\v
ee\rangle\langle \alpha, h\rangle}\Bigr).$$

Now factor each factor using $e^x - e^{-x} = 2\sinh(x)$:

$$N(h) = \prod_{\alpha \in \Phi^+} 2\sinh\!\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\langle \lambda+\de
lta,\, \alpha^\vee\rangle\langle \alpha, h\rangle\Bigr),$$
$$A(h) = \prod_{\alpha \in \Phi^+} 2\sinh\!\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\langle \delta,\, \
alpha^\vee\rangle\langle \alpha, h\rangle\Bigr).$$

Forming the ratio factor by factor (the product structure allows this):

$$\chi_\lambda(e^h) = \prod_{\alpha \in \Phi^+} \frac{\sinh\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\
langle \lambda+\delta,\, \alpha^\vee\rangle\langle \alpha, h\rangle\bigr)}{\sinh
\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\langle \delta,\, \alpha^\vee\rangle\langle \alpha, h\rangle
\bigr)}.$$

## Taking $h \to 0$

Now take the limit $h \to 0$. For each factor, use $\sinh(x) \sim x$ as $x \to 0
$:

$$\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\sinh\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\langle \lambda+\delta,\, \alpha
^\vee\rangle\langle \alpha, h\rangle\bigr)}{\sinh\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\langle \de
lta,\, \alpha^\vee\rangle\langle \alpha, h\rangle\bigr)} = \frac{\tfrac{1}{2}\la
ngle \lambda+\delta,\, \alpha^\vee\rangle\langle \alpha, h\rangle}{\tfrac{1}{2}\
langle \delta,\, \alpha^\vee\rangle\langle \alpha, h\rangle} = \frac{\langle \la
mbda+\delta,\, \alpha^\vee\rangle}{\langle \delta,\, \alpha^\vee\rangle}.$$

The factors $\langle \alpha, h\rangle$ cancel, leaving a finite, $h$-independent
 ratio for each positive root.

## The Weyl dimensionality formula

Multiplying over all positive roots:

$$\boxed{\dim V_\lambda \;=\; \prod_{\alpha \in \Phi^+} \frac{\langle \lambda +
\delta,\, \alpha^\vee \rangle}{\langle \delta,\, \alpha^\vee \rangle}.}$$

Equivalently, writing $\ell_\mu(\alpha) := \langle \mu, \alpha^\vee\rangle = \fr
ac{2\langle \mu, \alpha\rangle}{\langle \alpha, \alpha\rangle}$:

$$\dim V_\lambda = \prod_{\alpha \in \Phi^+} \frac{\ell_{\lambda+\delta}(\alpha)
}{\ell_{\delta}(\alpha)}.$$

## Role of the three ingredients

**Highest weight $\lambda$.** This is the label of the irreducible representatio
n $V_\lambda$; it is a dominant integral weight. The formula shows that the dime
nsion depends on $\lambda$ only through the shifted quantity $\lambda + \delta$.
 The integrality of $\lambda$ guarantees that each factor $\langle \lambda + \de
lta, \alpha^\vee\rangle$ is a positive integer (since $\lambda$ is dominant inte
gral and $\delta$ is strictly dominant, $\lambda + \delta$ is strictly dominant,
 so $\langle \lambda+\delta, \alpha^\vee\rangle \geq 1$ for all $\alpha \in \Phi
^+$). This ensures the dimension is a positive integer, as it must be.

**Weyl vector $\delta = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha \in \Phi^+}\alpha$.** The Weyl
vector plays a dual role:
1. It appears in the **shift** $\lambda \mapsto \lambda + \delta$ in the numerat
or — this is the same shift that appears in the Weyl character formula itself, o
riginating from the denominator (the " $\delta$-shift" is forced by the structur
e of the Weyl alternant).
2. It appears in the **denominator** of the dimension formula, providing the "re
ference" strictly dominant weight $\delta$ against which $\lambda + \delta$ is c
ompared. The denominator $\langle \delta, \alpha^\vee\rangle$ equals $1$ for eac
h simple root $\alpha$ (a standard identity: $\langle \delta, \alpha_i^\vee \ran
gle = 1$ for all simple coroots $\alpha_i^\vee$), which reflects that $\delta$ i
s the "smallest" strictly dominant weight.

**Positive roots $\alpha \in \Phi^+$.** The product runs over the full set of po
sitive roots. Each positive root contributes one multiplicative factor. The numb
er of factors is $|\Phi^+|$, which equals the number of positive roots — this is
 the rank of the symmetric space $\mathrm{rank}\,(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})$ an
d controls the "degree" of the dimension as a polynomial in the Dynkin labels of
 $\lambda$. Indeed, $\dim V_\lambda$ is a polynomial of degree $|\Phi^+|$ in the
 components of $\lambda$.

## Verification on $\mathrm{SU}(2)$

As a sanity check, take $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_2$. There is a single posi
tive root $\alpha$, with $\Phi^+ = \{\alpha\}$, $\delta = \alpha/2$, and $\alpha
^\vee = \alpha$ (since $\langle \alpha, \alpha\rangle = 2$). A highest weight is
 $\lambda = n\,\alpha/2$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$ (so $n = \langle \lambd
a, \alpha^\vee\rangle$ is the Dynkin label). Then:

$$\dim V_\lambda = \frac{\langle \lambda + \delta, \alpha^\vee\rangle}{\langle \
delta, \alpha^\vee\rangle} = \frac{\langle \lambda, \alpha^\vee\rangle + \langle
 \delta, \alpha^\vee\rangle}{\langle \delta, \alpha^\vee\rangle} = \frac{n + 1}{
1} = n + 1.$$

This recovers the well-known result that the $(n+1)$-dimensional irreducible rep
resentation of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ has spin $n/2$ and dimension $n+1$, confirming t
he formula.

## Summary

Starting from the Weyl character formula $\chi_\lambda = N/A$, we recognized tha
t $\dim V_\lambda = \chi_\lambda(e)$ requires evaluating at $h = 0$, yielding $0
/0$. Expressing both numerator and denominator as products over positive roots (
via the Weyl denominator identity applied to the strictly dominant weights $\lam
bda + \delta$ and $\delta$), converting to hyperbolic sines, and taking the limi
t $h \to 0$ factor-by-factor, each $\sinh$ ratio collapses to a ratio of linear
terms. The $\langle \alpha, h\rangle$ factors cancel, leaving the **Weyl dimensi
onality formula**:

$$\dim V_\lambda = \prod_{\alpha \in \Phi^+} \frac{\langle \lambda + \delta,\, \
alpha^\vee \rangle}{\langle \delta,\, \alpha^\vee \rangle},$$

with $\lambda$ the highest weight, $\delta = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha>0}\alpha$
the Weyl vector, and the product over all positive roots $\alpha \in \Phi^+$.
