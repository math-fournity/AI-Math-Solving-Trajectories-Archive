## Proof

Recall the Möbius inversion identity relating the prime zeta function $P(s)$ and
 $\ln\zeta(s)$:

$$P(s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\mu(n)}{n}\,\ln\zeta(sn), \qquad \ln\zeta(s)
= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{P(sn)}{n}.$$

The $n=1$ term in the expansion of $P(s)$ is $\mu(1)\ln\zeta(s) = \ln\zeta(s)$.
Therefore, separating the $n=1$ term,

$$P(s) - \ln\zeta(s) = \sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{\mu(n)}{n}\,\ln\zeta(sn). \tag{
1}$$

We now take $s \to 1^+$. For each fixed $n \geq 2$, $sn \to n \geq 2$, and since
 $\zeta$ is continuous and finite on $(1,\infty)$, we have pointwise

$$\ln\zeta(sn) \;\longrightarrow\; \ln\zeta(n).$$

To justify exchanging the limit with the infinite sum, we establish a dominating
 series. For $s$ in a neighborhood of $1$, say $s \in [1, \tfrac{3}{2}]$, and $n
 \geq 2$, we have $sn \geq 2$. Using the standard bound $\zeta(x) \leq 1 + 2^{-x
}(1 + \frac{1}{1-2^{1-x}})$ for $x>1$, one obtains $\ln\zeta(x) \leq C\,2^{-x}$
for a universal constant $C$ and all $x \geq 2$. Hence for $s \in [1,\tfrac{3}{2
}]$,

$$\left|\frac{\mu(n)}{n}\ln\zeta(sn)\right| \leq \frac{C}{n}\,2^{-sn} \leq \frac
{C}{n}\,2^{-2n} \leq C\,2^{-2n}.$$

The series $\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} C\,2^{-2n}$ converges. By the dominated converge
nce theorem,

$$\lim_{s\to 1^+}\bigl(P(s)-\ln\zeta(s)\bigr) = \sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{\mu(n)
}{n}\,\ln\zeta(n). \tag{2}$$

Now we use the given definition of the Mertens constant:

$$M = \gamma + \sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{\mu(n)}{n}\,\ln\zeta(n).$$

Rearranging,

$$\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{\mu(n)}{n}\,\ln\zeta(n) = M - \gamma.$$

Substituting into (2),

$$\boxed{\lim_{s\to 1^+}\bigl(P(s)-\ln\zeta(s)\bigr) = M - \gamma.}$$
