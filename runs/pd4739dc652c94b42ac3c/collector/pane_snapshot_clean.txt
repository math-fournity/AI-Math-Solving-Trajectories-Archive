## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, the series $\sum_k x_k$ converges in the Fréchet topology.

**Setup.** Let $X$ be a Fréchet space, i.e., a complete Hausdorff locally convex
 topological vector space whose topology is generated by a countable separating
family of seminorms $\{P_i\}_{i=1}^\infty$. Let $s_n = \sum_{k=1}^n x_k$ be the
partial sums. Assume $\sum_{k=1}^\infty P_i(x_k) < \infty$ for every $i$.

**Step 1: The partial sums are Cauchy in the Fréchet topology.**

The Fréchet topology is the coarsest topology making every $P_i$ continuous. A b
ase of neighborhoods of $0$ is given by sets of the form
$$U(i_1,\dots,i_r;\varepsilon) = \{x \in X : P_{i_j}(x) < \varepsilon,\ j=1,\dot
s,r\},$$
for finite collections of indices and $\varepsilon > 0$. Hence a sequence $(a_n)
$ is Cauchy iff for every $i$ and every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists $N$ such
 that $P_i(a_m - a_n) < \varepsilon$ for all $m, n > N$.

For $m > n$,
$$P_i(s_m - s_n) = P_i\!\left(\sum_{k=n+1}^{m} x_k\right) \le \sum_{k=n+1}^{m} P
_i(x_k) \le \sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty} P_i(x_k).$$

Since $\sum_{k=1}^\infty P_i(x_k) < \infty$, the tail $T_n^{(i)} := \sum_{k=n+1}
^{\infty} P_i(x_k) \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$. Thus for any $\varepsilon > 0$ ther
e exists $N_i$ with $T_{N_i}^{(i)} < \varepsilon$, giving $P_i(s_m - s_n) < \var
epsilon$ for all $m > n > N_i$. Since this holds for every $i$, $(s_n)$ is Cauch
y in the Fréchet topology.

**Step 2: Completeness gives convergence.**

A Fréchet space is by definition complete: every Cauchy sequence converges. Ther
efore $(s_n)$ converges to some $s \in X$, i.e., $\sum_k x_k$ converges in the F
réchet topology.

**Remark (the limit is independent of ordering).** The argument shows absolute c
onvergence in every seminorm, which is stronger than needed; in particular the s
eries is unconditionally convergent since any reordering also satisfies the same
 tail estimate.
