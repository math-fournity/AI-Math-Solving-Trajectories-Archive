## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle I = \oint_{|z|=1} \exp(1/z)\,\sin(1/z)\,dz$, traverse
d counterclockwise.

**Step 1: Identify the singularity.** The integrand $f(z)=e^{1/z}\sin(1/z)$ has
an essential singularity at $z=0$, which lies inside the contour $|z|=1$. By the
 residue theorem,

$$I = 2\pi i \,\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z).$$

**Step 2: Laurent expansion.** Set $w = 1/z$. Then $f(z) = e^{w}\sin w$, and the
 Laurent expansion of $f$ in $z$ corresponds to the Taylor expansion of $g(w)=e^
{w}\sin w$ in $w$. The residue (coefficient of $z^{-1}$) equals the coefficient
of $w^{1}$ in $g(w)$.

**Step 3: Compute the coefficient of $w^1$.** Using the product expansion

$$e^{w}\sin w = \left(1 + w + \tfrac{w^2}{2}+\cdots\right)\left(w - \tfrac{w^3}{
6}+\cdots\right),$$

the coefficient of $w^1$ is

$$1\cdot 1 + 1\cdot 0 = 1,$$

since $\sin w$ has no constant term.

**Verification via the identity** $e^{w}\sin w = \dfrac{e^{(1+i)w}-e^{(1-i)w}}{2
i}$: the coefficient of $w^1$ is

$$\frac{(1+i)-(1-i)}{2i} = \frac{2i}{2i}=1. \quad\checkmark$$

**Step 4: Conclude.** Therefore $\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z)=1$, and

$$\boxed{I = 2\pi i}.$$
