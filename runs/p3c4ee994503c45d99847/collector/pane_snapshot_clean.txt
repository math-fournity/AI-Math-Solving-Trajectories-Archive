## Solution

Let $W_t=(X_t,Y_t)$ be standard 2D Brownian motion started at $(1,1)$. Let
$$\tau=\inf\{t\ge 0:Y_t=0\}$$
be the first hitting time of the $x$-axis. Since 2D Brownian motion is recurrent
, $\tau<\infty$ a.s., and the process hits the $x$-axis exactly once at a well-d
efined point $(X_\tau,0)$. The desired probability is
$$\mathbb P(X_\tau>0).$$

**Poisson kernel / harmonic measure on the upper half-plane.** The process $W_t$
 lives in the open upper half-plane $\mathbb H=\{(x,y):y>0\}$ until time $\tau$.
 The harmonic measure (exit distribution) of $\mathbb H$ from a point $(x_0,y_0)
\in\mathbb H$ has density on the boundary $\partial\mathbb H=\mathbb R$ given by
 the Poisson kernel
$$\mu_{(x_0,y_0)}(d\xi)=\frac{1}{\pi}\,\frac{y_0}{(\xi-x_0)^2+y_0^2}\,d\xi.$$
Equivalently, for any bounded Borel $f$ on $\mathbb R$,
$$\mathbb E_{(x_0,y_0)}\bigl[f(X_\tau)\bigr]=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(\xi)\,\fra
c{1}{\pi}\frac{y_0}{(\xi-x_0)^2+y_0^2}\,d\xi.$$

This is the standard harmonic-measure formula for the upper half-plane (it follo
ws, for instance, from conformal invariance: the map $z\mapsto z$ is the identit
y and the Cauchy distribution is the harmonic measure of $\mathbb H$; or directl
y from the fact that $u(x,y)=\mathbb E_{(x,y)}[f(X_\tau)]$ is the unique bounded
 harmonic extension of $f$ to $\mathbb H$, solved by the Poisson integral).

**Apply to $(x_0,y_0)=(1,1)$.** With $f=\mathbf 1_{(0,\infty)}$,
$$\mathbb P(X_\tau>0)=\int_0^\infty \frac{1}{\pi}\,\frac{1}{(\xi-1)^2+1}\,d\xi.$
$

Compute the integral:
$$\int_0^\infty \frac{d\xi}{(\xi-1)^2+1}=\Bigl[\arctan(\xi-1)\Bigr]_{\xi=0}^{\xi
\to\infty}=\frac{\pi}{2}-\arctan(-1)=\frac{\pi}{2}-\Bigl(-\frac{\pi}{4}\Bigr)=\f
rac{3\pi}{4}.$$

Therefore
$$\mathbb P(X_\tau>0)=\frac{1}{\pi}\cdot\frac{3\pi}{4}=\boxed{\frac34}.$$
