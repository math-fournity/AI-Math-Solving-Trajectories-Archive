Spectrum of $T = S_r + S_l$ on $\ell_2[0,\infty)$

Answer: The spectrum is $\sigma(T) = [-2, 2] \subset \mathbb{C}$.

Proof

$T$ is the Jacobi (discrete Schrödinger) operator on $\ell_2(\mathbb{N}_0)$ with
zero diagonal and unit off-diagonal entries:

$$(Tx)n = x{n-1} + x_{n+1}, \quad n \ge 0, \qquad (x_{-1} := 0).$$

This is the half-line free Jacobi operator $J_0$ with $a_n \equiv 1$, $b_n
\equiv 0$.

Step 1. $T$ is bounded and self-adjoint

$|S_r| = |S_l| = 1$, so $|T| \le 2$. Both $S_r$ and $S_l$ are bounded, and
$S_r^* = S_l$, hence $T^* = S_l^* + S_r^* = S_r + S_l = T$. So $T$ is bounded
self-adjoint, and $\sigma(T) \subset \mathbb{R}$.

Step 2. $\sigma(T) \subseteq [-2,2]$

For any $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$ with $|\lambda| > 2$, the resolvent $(T -
\lambda I)^{-1}$ exists as a bounded operator. Indeed, the half-line Green's
function is constructed by the decaying solution. The characteristic equation $z
+ z^{-1} = \lambda$ has roots $z, z^{-1}$ with $|z| < 1 < |z^{-1}|$ when $|
\lambda| > 2$. The sequence $u_n = z^n$ satisfies $u_{n-1} + u_{n+1} = \lambda
u_n$ and decays. The resolvent kernel is

$$G_{n,m}(\lambda) = \frac{z^{n+1} z^{-\min(n,m)} \cdot (\text{bounded})}
{\dots}$$

concretely $G_{n,m}(\lambda) = z^{|n-m|+1}/(z - z^{-1})$ (with the half-line
reflection at the boundary), which is $\ell_2$ in each index and bounded as an
operator since $|z|<1$. Hence $\lambda \in \rho(T)$, giving $\sigma(T) \subseteq
[-2,2]$.

Step 3. $[-2,2] \subseteq \sigma(T)$ — purely absolutely continuous spectrum

We show every $\lambda \in (-2,2)$ lies in the spectrum (endpoints follow by
closedness of $\sigma$). For $\lambda \in (-2,2)$, write $\lambda = 2\cos\theta$
with $\theta \in (0,\pi)$. The generalized eigenfunction $u_n =
\sin((n+1)\theta)$ satisfies

$$u_{n-1} + u_{n+1} = 2\cos\theta \cdot u_n = \lambda u_n, \quad u_{-1} = 0,$$

so it solves $(T - \lambda)u = 0$ on the half-line with the Dirichlet boundary
condition $u_{-1}=0$. Since $|u_n| \le 1$ but $u \notin \ell_2$ (it oscillates
without decay: $\sum |\sin((n+1)\theta)|^2 = \infty$), $\lambda$ is not an
eigenvalue.

To confirm $\lambda \in \sigma(T)$: the resolvent $(T-\lambda)^{-1}$ is
unbounded. Indeed, take the truncated vectors $u^{(N)}$ with $u^{(N)}n =
\sin((n+1)\theta)$ for $0 \le n \le N$ and $0$ otherwise, normalized: $|u^{(N)}|
^2 = \sum{n=0}^{N}\sin^2((n+1)\theta) \sim N/2$. Then

$$|(T-\lambda)u^{(N)}|^2 = |u^{(N)}N|^2 + |u^{(N)}{N+1\text{-boundary}}|^2 \le
2,$$

so $|(T-\lambda)u^{(N)}|/|u^{(N)}| \to 0$. Thus $(T-\lambda)^{-1}$ is unbounded,
$\lambda \in \sigma(T)$.

The spectral measure of $T$ is the well-known half-line free Jacobi spectral
measure $d\mu(\lambda) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{4-\lambda^2},d\lambda$ on $[-2,2]$
(the semi-circle/Kesten density), whose support is exactly $[-2,2]$. The
spectrum equals the support of the spectral measure for self-adjoint operators,
confirming $\sigma(T) = [-2,2]$.

Step 4. No residual/eigenvalues outside

$T$ self-adjoint $\Rightarrow$ residual spectrum is empty. The point spectrum is
empty: any $\ell_2$ solution of $u_{n-1}+u_{n+1}=\lambda u_n$ on the half-line
is a linear combination of $e^{\pm in\theta}$ (for $\lambda \in (-2,2)$, non-
decaying) or $z^n$ with $|z|<1$ (for $|\lambda|>2$, but the boundary condition
$u_{-1}=0$ forces the combination to vanish). For $\lambda = \pm 2$, solutions
are linear/constant, not $\ell_2$. Hence no eigenvalues, and the entire spectrum
is purely absolutely continuous.

Conclusion

$$\boxed{\sigma(T) = [-2,, 2] \subset \mathbb{C}}$$

a real interval, purely absolutely continuous, with no eigenvalues and no
residual spectrum.
