## Proof

**Answer: Yes.**

**Definitions.** Let $f(z)=\sum_{n\ge 0}a_n z^n$ have radius of convergence $R$.
 A point $\zeta$ with $|\zeta|=R$ is called a *regular point* of $f$ if there ex
ists an open disk $D$ centered at $\zeta$ and a holomorphic function $g$ on $D$
such that $g=f$ on $D\cap\{|z|<R\}$ (i.e., $f$ admits an analytic continuation t
o a neighborhood of $\zeta$).

**Key implication.** If $\zeta$ is a regular point of $f$, then $f$ can be conti
nued *continuously* to $\zeta$.

Indeed, by definition there is a holomorphic (hence continuous) function $g$ on
a neighborhood $D$ of $\zeta$ agreeing with $f$ on $D\cap\{|z|<R\}$. Setting $f(
\zeta):=g(\zeta)$ extends $f$ to $\zeta$, and since $g$ is continuous at $\zeta$
, for every sequence $z_k\to\zeta$ with $z_k\in D\cap\{|z|<R\}$ we have $f(z_k)=
g(z_k)\to g(\zeta)=f(\zeta)$. Thus $f$ is continuous at $\zeta$ after the extens
ion. So:

$$\zeta \text{ regular} \;\Longrightarrow\; f \text{ can be continued continuous
ly to } \zeta.$$

**Contrapositive.** Taking the contrapositive:

$$f \text{ cannot be continued continuously to } \zeta \;\Longrightarrow\; \zeta
 \text{ is not a regular point}.$$

**Applying the hypothesis.** The hypothesis states that $f$ cannot be continued
continuously to *any* point $\zeta$ on $|z|=R$. By the contrapositive above, eve
ry such $\zeta$ fails to be a regular point. Hence there are no regular points o
f $f$ on the circle $|z|=R$.

**Conclusion.** The implication holds: the absence of continuous continuation at
 every boundary point forces the absence of regular (analytically continuable) p
oints, since analytic continuation is strictly stronger than continuous continua
tion.
