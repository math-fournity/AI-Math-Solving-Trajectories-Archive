# Problem

In a remote industrial complex, three specialized energy reactors—**P**, **Q**, and **R**—operate in a closed-loop grid. The efficiency of the grid is determined by the ratios of the power outputs between these reactors. 

The Chief Engineer monitors two specific "Interference Factors" generated by the interactions of these three power levels ($p, q,$ and $r$). 

The first factor, the **Synergy Coefficient**, is calculated by taking the product of the sums of the reactor outputs in pairs and dividing by the product of all three outputs. Data logs show that:
$$\frac{(p+q)(q+r)(r+p)}{pqr} = 24$$

The second factor, the **Stress Gradient**, is calculated by taking the product of the differences between one reactor and twice the next (in a cyclic sequence) and dividing by the product of all three outputs. Current readings show:
$$\frac{(p-2q)(q-2r)(r-2p)}{pqr} = 10$$

To calibrate the system for a safety inspection, the engineer needs to find the **Total Relative Load**, defined as the sum of the ratios of the power outputs: $\frac{p}{q} + \frac{q}{r} + \frac{r}{p}$. 

If this Total Relative Load is expressed as a simplified fraction $\frac{m}{n}$, where $m$ and $n$ are relatively prime positive integers, compute the value of $m+n$.
