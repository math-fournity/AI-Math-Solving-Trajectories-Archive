## Proof

Let $\mathbb{D}=\{z:|z|<1\}$ and $\overline{\mathbb{D}}=\{z:|z|\le 1\}$. We clai
m the solutions are exactly the **finite Blaschke products**

$$
f(z)=e^{i\theta}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{z-a_k}{1-\overline{a_k}\,z},\qquad \theta\i
n\mathbb{R},\; |a_k|<1,
$$

with the convention that $n=0$ gives the constant $f\equiv e^{i\theta}$.

---

### Necessity

Let $f$ be analytic in $\mathbb{D}$, continuous on $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$, with
 $|f(z)|=1$ on $|z|=1$.

**$f\not\equiv 0$.** Since $|f|=1$ on the boundary, $f$ is not identically zero.

**$f$ has only finitely many zeros in $\mathbb{D}$.** Suppose $f$ had infinitely
 many zeros $\{z_j\}\subset\mathbb{D}$. By compactness of $\overline{\mathbb{D}}
$, a subsequence converges to some $\zeta\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}$.

- If $\zeta\in\mathbb{D}$, the identity theorem forces $f\equiv 0$, contradictin
g $|f|=1$ on $|z|=1$.
- If $|\zeta|=1$, then continuity gives $f(\zeta)=\lim f(z_j)=0$, contradicting
$|f(\zeta)|=1$.

Hence $f$ has finitely many zeros $a_1,\dots,a_n$ in $\mathbb{D}$, listed with m
ultiplicity.

**Form the finite Blaschke product.** Set

$$
B(z)=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{z-a_k}{1-\overline{a_k}\,z}.
$$

Each factor $\varphi_{a_k}(z)=\frac{z-a_k}{1-\overline{a_k}z}$ is analytic in $\
mathbb{D}$ and continuous on $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$ (the denominator $1-\overli
ne{a_k}z\neq 0$ for $|z|\le 1$ since $|a_k|<1$), and satisfies $|\varphi_{a_k}(z
)|=1$ on $|z|=1$ (a direct computation: $|z-a_k|^2=|1-\overline{a_k}z|^2$ when $
|z|=1$). Thus $B$ is analytic in $\mathbb{D}$, continuous on $\overline{\mathbb{
D}}$, and $|B(z)|=1$ on $|z|=1$.

**Cancel the zeros.** Define $g(z)=f(z)/B(z)$. The zeros of $B$ at $a_1,\dots,a_
n$ (with the same multiplicities as those of $f$) cancel, so:

- $g$ is analytic in $\mathbb{D}$ and continuous on $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$,
- $g$ has **no zeros** in $\mathbb{D}$,
- on $|z|=1$: $|g(z)|=|f(z)|/|B(z)|=1/1=1$.

**$g$ is constant of modulus $1$.** Since $g$ has no zeros in $\mathbb{D}$, the
function $h=1/g$ is also analytic in $\mathbb{D}$ and continuous on $\overline{\
mathbb{D}}$, with $|h(z)|=1$ on $|z|=1$. By the maximum modulus principle,

$$
|g(z)|\le \max_{|w|=1}|g(w)|=1,\qquad |h(z)|\le \max_{|w|=1}|h(w)|=1\;\;(\text{i
.e. }|g(z)|\ge 1)
$$

for all $z\in\mathbb{D}$. Therefore $|g(z)|=1$ for every $z\in\mathbb{D}$.

An analytic function with constant modulus on a domain is constant (by the open
mapping theorem: a nonconstant analytic map sends open sets to open sets, but $g
(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\{|w|=1\}$, which has empty interior). Hence $g(z)\equiv e^
{i\theta}$ for some $\theta\in\mathbb{R}$.

**Conclusion.** $f(z)=e^{i\theta}\,B(z)=e^{i\theta}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{z-a_k}{1
-\overline{a_k}z}$ with $|a_k|<1$.

### Sufficiency (Converse)

Let $f(z)=e^{i\theta}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{z-a_k}{1-\overline{a_k}z}$ with $|a_k|
<1$.

- **Analytic in $\mathbb{D}$:** each factor is analytic in $\mathbb{D}$ (its onl
y pole is at $z=1/\overline{a_k}$, which lies outside $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$ si
nce $|a_k|<1$).
- **Continuous on $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$:** the denominators $1-\overline{a_k}z
$ are nonzero on $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$.
- **$|f(z)|=1$ on $|z|=1$:** each factor has modulus $1$ on the unit circle, and
 $|e^{i\theta}|=1$.

So every finite Blaschke product satisfies the stated conditions.

The functions satisfying the hypotheses are precisely the **finite Blaschke prod

uad \theta\in\mathbb{R},\; a_k\in\mathbb{D},\; n\ge 0,
$$

where $n=0$ is interpreted as the constant $f\equiv e^{i\theta}$ (a unimodular c
