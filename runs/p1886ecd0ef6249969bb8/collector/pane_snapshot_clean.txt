# Proof

**Claim:** Yes. If $G$ is a finite-dimensional complex Lie group, $f:G\to\mathbb
 C$ is holomorphic, and $X$ is a left-invariant vector field on $G$, then $Xf$ i
s holomorphic.

---

Let $\mu:G\times G\to G$ be the (holomorphic) multiplication, $e$ the identity,
and $L_g(h)=\mu(g,h)$. The complex Lie algebra is $\mathfrak g = T^{1,0}_eG$. A
left-invariant vector field $X$ is determined by $v:=X_e\in\mathfrak g$ via
$$X_g = (dL_g)_e(v).$$

**Step 1 — $X$ is a holomorphic vector field.**

Work in local holomorphic coordinates $(z_1,\dots,z_n)$ near $g$ and $(w_1,\dots
,w_n)$ near $e$. Write $\mu^j(z,w)$ for the $j$-th coordinate of $\mu$. Since $\
mu$ is holomorphic, each $\mu^j$ is holomorphic in $(z,w)$, hence each partial d
erivative $\frac{\partial \mu^j}{\partial w^k}(z,e)$ is holomorphic in $z$. If $
v = \sum_k v^k\frac{\partial}{\partial w^k}\big|_e$, then
$$X_g = \sum_{j,k} \frac{\partial \mu^j}{\partial w^k}(z,e)\,v^k\;\frac{\partial
}{\partial z_j}\Big|_g.$$
Thus the coefficient functions $X^j(z)=\sum_k \frac{\partial \mu^j}{\partial w^k
}(z,e)v^k$ are holomorphic. Equivalently, the section $g\mapsto X_g$ is holomorp
hic, being the composition of the holomorphic map $g\mapsto(g,e)$ with the holom
orphic differential $d\mu$ (whose coordinate expression is the holomorphic Jacob
ian of $\mu$). So $X$ is a holomorphic vector field of type $(1,0)$.

**Step 2 — $Xf$ is holomorphic.**

Since $f$ is holomorphic, in the same local coordinates $f=f(z)$ is independent
of $\bar z$, and each $\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_j}$ is holomorphic. As a der
ivation,
$$Xf = \sum_{j=1}^n X^j(z)\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_j}.$$
Each $X^j(z)$ is holomorphic (Step 1) and each $\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_j}$
 is holomorphic, so $Xf$ is a finite sum of products of holomorphic functions, h
ence holomorphic on every coordinate chart. Holomorphicity is local, so $Xf$ is
holomorphic on all of $G$.

---

**Conclusion.** Left-invariant vector fields on a complex Lie group are holomorp
hic (their coefficients in holomorphic coordinates are holomorphic, because they
 are built from the holomorphic Jacobian of the multiplication map). Acting by s
uch a field on a holomorphic function produces a sum of products of holomorphic
functions, which is again holomorphic. Therefore $Xf$ must be holomorphic.
