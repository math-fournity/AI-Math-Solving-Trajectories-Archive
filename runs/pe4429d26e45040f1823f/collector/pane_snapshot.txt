## Proof

Let $f \in H^\infty(D)$ (holomorphic and bounded on the open unit disk $D$), and
 let $I \subset [-\pi,\pi]$ be an open interval such that
$$\lim_{r\to 1^-} f(re^{i\theta}) = 0 \quad \text{for a.e. } \theta \in I.$$

We prove $f \equiv 0$ on $D$.

---

**Step 1: Boundary values and the vanishing set.**

Since $f \in H^\infty(D)$, by Fatou's theorem the radial limit
$$\tilde{f}(e^{i\theta}) := \lim_{r\to 1^-} f(re^{i\theta})$$
exists for a.e. $\theta \in [-\pi,\pi]$, and $\tilde{f} \in L^\infty(\mathbb{T})
$ (where $\mathbb{T} = \partial D$). Define the set
$$E := \{\theta \in [-\pi,\pi] : \tilde{f}(e^{i\theta}) = 0\}.$$
By hypothesis, $\tilde{f} = 0$ a.e. on the open interval $I$, so $|E| \geq |I| >
 0$; in particular $E$ has positive Lebesgue measure.

---

**Step 2: Inner–outer factorization.**

Suppose for contradiction that $f \not\equiv 0$. By the inner–outer factorizatio
n theorem for $H^\infty$, we can write
$$f = B \cdot S \cdot F,$$
where:
- $B$ is a Blaschke product (inner),
- $S$ is a singular inner function,
- $F$ is an outer function in $H^\infty$.

Since $f \not\equiv 0$ and inner functions are not identically zero, we have $F
\not\equiv 0$.

A fundamental property of inner functions is that their boundary values have mod
ulus $1$ a.e.:
$$|\tilde{B}(e^{i\theta})| = 1 \quad \text{a.e.}, \qquad |\tilde{S}(e^{i\theta})
| = 1 \quad \text{a.e.}$$
Therefore
$$|\tilde{F}(e^{i\theta})| = |\tilde{f}(e^{i\theta})| \quad \text{a.e.}$$

In particular, $\tilde{F} = 0$ a.e. on $E$, so $|\tilde{F}| = 0$ on $E$ (a set o
f positive measure), and hence
$$\log|\tilde{F}(e^{i\theta})| = -\infty \quad \text{for a.e. } \theta \in E.$$

---

**Step 3: Contradiction via the outer function representation.**

Since $F$ is a non-zero outer function in $H^\infty$, it admits the representati
on
$$F(z) = \exp\!\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}} \frac{\zeta + z}{\zeta - z}\, h(\zeta)\,
dm(\zeta)\right), \qquad |z| < 1,$$
where $m$ is normalized Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{T}$ and $h \in L^1(\mathbb{
T}, dm)$ is a real-valued integrable function. Moreover, the boundary modulus sa
tisfies
$$\log|\tilde{F}(e^{i\theta})| = h(e^{i\theta}) \quad \text{a.e.}$$
Since $h \in L^1$, the function $\log|\tilde{F}|$ is finite a.e. (an $L^1$ funct
ion takes values in $(-\infty, +\infty)$ a.e.).

But from Step 2, $\log|\tilde{F}| = -\infty$ on $E$, a set of positive measure.
This contradicts $\log|\tilde{F}| = h \in L^1$ being finite a.e.

---

**Conclusion.**

The contradiction shows that our assumption $f \not\equiv 0$ is false. Therefore
 $f \equiv 0$ on $D$. $\blacksquare$
