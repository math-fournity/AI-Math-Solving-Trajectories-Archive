Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether, in ZF without the Axiom of
 Choice, one can extract a subset of order type λ from a well-ordered set of ord
er type λ⁺, where λ > ω.

**Answer: Yes, it is possible.**

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $X$ be a $\lambda^+$-ordered set, meaning $X$ is equipped with a
well-ordering $<$ of order type $\lambda^+$ (the successor cardinal of $\lambda$
). Since $(X, <)$ is well-ordered of type $\lambda^+$, there exists a **unique**
 order-isomorphism
$$f : \lambda^+ \xrightarrow{\;\cong\;} X,$$
where $\lambda^+$ is identified with its initial ordinal.

**Construction of $Y$.** Since $\lambda$ is an ordinal and $\lambda < \lambda^+$
 (as $\lambda^+$ is the successor cardinal), $\lambda$ is a proper initial segme
nt of the ordinal $\lambda^+$. Define:
$$Y := f[\lambda] = \{\, f(\alpha) : \alpha < \lambda \,\}.$$

**Verification.**

1. **$Y \subseteq X$:** Immediate, since $f$ maps into $X$.

2. **$Y$ is well-ordered by $<:$** Any subset of a well-ordered set is well-orde
red by the inherited order. Since $(X, <)$ is well-ordered and $Y \subseteq X$,
the restriction $< \upharpoonright Y$ is a well-ordering.

3. **The order type of $(Y, <)$ is exactly $\lambda$:** The restriction $f \upha
rpoonright \lambda : \lambda \to Y$ is an order-isomorphism from the ordinal $\l
ambda$ (with its natural well-ordering) onto $(Y, <)$. Indeed:
   - *Injectivity:* $f$ is an order-isomorphism, hence injective.
   - *Surjectivity onto $Y$:* By definition of $Y$.
   - *Order-preservation:* $f$ preserves order on all of $\lambda^+$, hence on t
he initial segment $\lambda$.

   Therefore $(Y, <) \cong \lambda$, i.e., $Y$ is $\lambda$-ordered.

**No Axiom of Choice is used.** The construction requires only:
- The given well-ordering $<$ on $X$ (which is part of the hypothesis that $X$ i
s $\lambda^+$-ordered),
- The fixed ordinal $\lambda$ (a specific well-ordered set, not a choice among a
lternatives),
- The unique order-isomorphism $f$, which is **uniquely determined** by the well
-ordering $<$ — no selection from a family of sets is involved.

At no point do we invoke the Axiom of Choice, the Axiom of Countable Choice, or
any choice principle. The subset $Y$ is defined **explicitly** as the image of t
he initial segment $\lambda$ under the canonical isomorphism.

**Remark on the condition $\lambda > \omega$.** The argument works for any ordin
al $\lambda$ with $0 < \lambda < \lambda^+$, including $\lambda = \omega$. The r
estriction $\lambda > \omega$ ensures we are dealing with an uncountable cardina
l, which is the regime where questions about the necessity of AC typically becom
e interesting. However, for *well-ordered* sets — which is what "$\lambda^+$-ord
ered" means — initial segments are always available in ZF alone, regardless of t
he size of $\lambda$. The essential point is that the well-ordering of $X$ is **
given**, not merely asserted to exist; this is what distinguishes the situation
from the cases (involving non-well-orderable sets) where AC would be required.

$\blacksquare$
