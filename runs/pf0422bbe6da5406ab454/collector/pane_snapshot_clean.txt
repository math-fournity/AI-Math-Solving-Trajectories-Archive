Looking at this problem, I need to count valid stacking configurations where 5 f
oundation crates support aluminum crates, each requiring two adjacent supporters
 below.

## Proof

**Setup.** The foundation is a fixed row of 5 crates (level 0). Level 1 has 4 po
sitions (each straddling an adjacent pair from level 0), level 2 has 3, level 3
has 2, level 4 has 1. A crate at level $k+1$ exists only if both its supporting
crates at level $k$ exist. We count all valid configurations (including the null
 configuration).

**Key Decomposition.** Fix which level-1 crates are present. The present level-1
 positions form contiguous runs. A level-2 crate requires two *adjacent* level-1
 crates, so it must lie within a single run. Therefore, **different runs are ind
ependent**: a run of length $k$ at level 1 generates a sub-structure identical t
o the original problem with foundation size $k$.

Let $f(n)$ = number of valid configurations with foundation $n$. Then:

$$f(n) = \sum_{S \subseteq [n-1]} \prod_{\text{runs } R \text{ in } S} f(|R|)$$

with $f(0)=1$, $f(1)=1$ (no crate can be placed above a single crate).

**Base cases.**
- $f(1) = 1$: no pairs available, only the null configuration.
- $f(2) = 2$: one level-1 position, present or absent.
- $f(3)$: level-1 subsets of $\{1,2\}$: $\emptyset \to 1$; $\{1\}\to 1$; $\{2\}\
to 1$; $\{1,2\}\to f(2)=2$. Total $= 5$.
- $f(4)$: level-1 subsets of $\{1,2,3\}$:

| Subset | Runs | Value |
|--------|------|-------|
| $\emptyset$ | — | 1 |
| $\{1\},\{2\},\{3\}$ | length 1 | $3 \times 1$ |
| $\{1,2\},\{2,3\}$ | length 2 | $2 \times 2$ |
| $\{1,3\}$ | two runs of 1 | $1$ |
| $\{1,2,3\}$ | length 3 | $f(3)=5$ |

Total $= 1 + 3 + 4 + 1 + 5 = 14$.

**Computing $f(5)$.** Level 1 has 4 positions $\{1,2,3,4\}$; there are $2^4 = 16
$ subsets:

| Subset type | Count | Value each | Subtotal |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\emptyset$ | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| singletons | 4 | $f(1)=1$ | 4 |
| adjacent pairs $\{1,2\},\{2,3\},\{3,4\}$ | 3 | $f(2)=2$ | 6 |
| non-adjacent pairs $\{1,3\},\{1,4\},\{2,4\}$ | 3 | $f(1)\cdot f(1)=1$ | 3 |
| contiguous triples $\{1,2,3\},\{2,3,4\}$ | 2 | $f(3)=5$ | 10 |
| split triples $\{1,2,4\},\{1,3,4\}$ | 2 | $f(2)\cdot f(1)=2$ | 4 |
| full set $\{1,2,3,4\}$ | 1 | $f(4)=14$ | 14 |

**Total** $= 1 + 4 + 6 + 3 + 10 + 4 + 14 = \boxed{42}$.

**Verification of independence.** For subset $\{1,2,4\}$: runs are $\{1,2\}$ (le
ngth 2) and $\{4\}$ (length 1). Above $\{1,2\}$: one level-2 crate (optional) $\
to f(2)=2$ configs. Above $\{4\}$: nothing $\to f(1)=1$. Product $= 2$. No level
-2 crate can straddle positions 2 and 4 (non-adjacent), confirming independence.
 ✓

**Verification of the full set.** For $\{1,2,3,4\}$: this is $f(4)=14$, which we
 computed independently above. ✓

**Remark.** The sequence $f(1),f(2),f(3),f(4),f(5) = 1,2,5,14,42$ are the Catala
n numbers $C_1,\ldots,C_5$, confirming $f(5) = C_5 = 42$.

The total number of unique stacking arrangements is **42**.
