### Setup and identification

The operator is
$$A^* = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(x - \frac{d}{dx}\right), \qquad \text{on } L^2(\
mathbb{R}).$$

This is the **creation (raising) operator** $a^\dagger$ of the quantum harmonic
oscillator. Its Hilbert-space adjoint is the **annihilation operator**
$$A = (A^*)^* = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(x + \frac{d}{dx}\right) =: a.$$

Both are densely defined (on the Schwartz space $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})$, say)
and closable; we work with their closures.

---

### Step 1. Point spectrum of $A^*$ is empty

Suppose $A^*\psi = \lambda\psi$ for some $\psi\in L^2(\mathbb{R})$, $\psi\neq 0$
, $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$. Then
$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(x\psi - \psi'\right) = \lambda\psi \quad\Longrightarro
w\quad \psi' = (x - \sqrt{2}\,\lambda)\,\psi.$$

This first-order ODE has the general solution
$$\psi(x) = C\exp\!\left(\tfrac{x^2}{2} - \sqrt{2}\,\lambda\,x\right) = C\,e^{-\
lambda^2}\exp\!\left(\tfrac{(x-\sqrt{2}\lambda)^2}{2}\right).$$

Completing the square shows the exponent's real part behaves as $+\tfrac{x^2}{2}
$ (up to shifts and an imaginary oscillation when $\lambda$ is non-real). Concre
tely, writing $\lambda = \alpha+i\beta$,
$$|\psi(x)|^2 = |C|^2\exp\!\big((x-\sqrt{2}\alpha)^2 - 2\alpha^2\big),$$
which grows like $e^{x^2}$ and is **not integrable** for any value of $\lambda\i
n\mathbb{C}$ and any $C\neq 0$.

Hence no nonzero $L^2$ solution exists for any $\lambda$:
$$\boxed{\sigma_p(A^*) = \varnothing.}$$

---

### Step 2. The adjoint $A = a$ has every complex number as an eigenvalue

Solve $a\varphi = \mu\varphi$:
$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(x\varphi + \varphi'\right) = \mu\varphi \quad\Longrigh
tarrow\quad \varphi' = (\sqrt{2}\,\mu - x)\,\varphi,$$
$$\varphi(x) = C\exp\!\left(\sqrt{2}\,\mu\,x - \tfrac{x^2}{2}\right) = C\,e^{-\m
u^2}\exp\!\left(-\tfrac{(x-\sqrt{2}\mu)^2}{2}\right).$$

For $\mu = \alpha+i\beta$,
$$|\varphi(x)|^2 = |C|^2\exp\!\big(-(x-\sqrt{2}\alpha)^2 + 2\alpha^2\big),$$
a shifted Gaussian, integrable for **every** $\mu\in\mathbb{C}$. Thus
$$\sigma_p(a) = \mathbb{C}, \qquad \ker(a-\mu)\neq\{0\}\ \text{for all }\mu\in\m
athbb{C}.$$

---

### Step 3. Spectrum of $A^*$ is the entire complex plane

For a closed, densely defined operator $T$ one has the general relation
$$\overline{\operatorname{Ran}(T)} = \big(\ker T^*\big)^\perp.$$

Apply this to $T = A^* - \lambda$, so $T^* = a - \bar\lambda$. By Step 2, $\ker(
a-\bar\lambda)\neq\{0\}$ for every $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$. Therefore
$$\overline{\operatorname{Ran}(A^*-\lambda)} \neq L^2(\mathbb{R}) \qquad\text{fo
r all }\lambda\in\mathbb{C}.$$

A non-dense range cannot be all of $L^2(\mathbb{R})$, so $A^*-\lambda$ is not su
rjective for any $\lambda$. Hence every $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$ belongs to the sp
ectrum:
$$\boxed{\sigma(A^*) = \mathbb{C}.}$$

---

### Step 4. Classification — the spectrum is entirely residual

Recall the standard partition of the spectrum of a closed densely defined operat
or:

| Type | Condition on $T-\lambda$ |
|---|---|
| point $\sigma_p$ | not injective |
| continuous $\sigma_c$ | injective, **dense** range, inverse unbounded |
| residual $\sigma_r$ | injective, **non-dense** range |

For $T = A^*$ and every $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$:
- **Injective:** $\ker(A^*-\lambda)=\{0\}$ by Step 1, so $\lambda\notin\sigma_p$
.
- **Non-dense range:** $\overline{\operatorname{Ran}(A^*-\lambda)}\neq L^2(\math
bb{R})$ by Step 3.

Both conditions together place every spectral point in the **residual spectrum**
:
$$\boxed{\sigma(A^*) = \sigma_r(A^*) = \mathbb{C}, \qquad \sigma_p(A^*)=\sigma_c
(A^*)=\varnothing.}$$

---

### Summary

- **Eigenvalues (point spectrum) of $A^*$:** none — $\sigma_p(A^*)=\varnothing$.
- **Spectrum of $A^*$:** the whole complex plane, $\sigma(A^*)=\mathbb{C}$.
- **Type:** entirely **residual spectrum**. The mechanism is that the adjoint $a
=(A^*)^*$ has every complex number as an eigenvalue (coherent states), which for
ces the range of $A^*-\lambda$ to be non-dense for every $\lambda$, while $A^*-\
lambda$ itself remains injective.
