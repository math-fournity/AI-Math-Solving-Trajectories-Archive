## Proof

on. The quotient map is the Hopf fibration, which we write explicitly as
$$\pi(z_1,z_2) = \bigl(\operatorname{Re}(z_1\bar z_2),\; \operatorname{Im}(z_1\b
ar z_2),\; \tfrac{1}{2}(|z_1|^2-|z_2|^2)\bigr).$$
One checks $|\pi(z_1,z_2)|^2 = \tfrac{1}{4}(|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2)^2 = \tfrac{1}{4}$,
so $\pi$ maps $S^3$ to $S^2(1/2)$. It is constant on $S^1$-orbits since $z_1\bar
 z_2$ and $|z_1|^2-|z_2|^2$ are invariant under $(z_1,z_2)\mapsto(gz_1,gz_2)$. T
his is a Riemannian submersion with totally geodesic fibers (the fibers are grea
t circles).

**Geodesics of $S^3$.** With the round metric, geodesics are great circles $\gam
ma(t)=p\cos t + q\sin t$, where $p,q\in\mathbb{R}^4$ are orthonormal.

**Horizontal vs. vertical.** The vertical space at $(z_1,z_2)$ is spanned by $J(
z_1,z_2)=(iz_1,iz_2)$, where $J$ is the complex structure. The geodesic $\gamma(
t)=p\cos t+q\sin t$ is horizontal (i.e., $\gamma'(t)\perp J\gamma(t)$ for all $t
$) if and only if
$$\langle p, Jq\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^4} = 0.$$
A direct computation shows $\langle \gamma'(t), J\gamma(t)\rangle = -\langle p,
Jq\rangle$ is constant in $t$, confirming this. Equivalently, using the Hermitia
n inner product $\langle p,q\rangle_{\mathbb{C}} = \bar p_1 q_1 + \bar p_2 q_2$,
 the condition is $\operatorname{Im}\langle p,q\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}=0$.

**Key fact.** For a Riemannian submersion with totally geodesic fibers, a horizo
ntal geodesic projects to a geodesic. A geodesic with nonzero vertical component
 need not project to a geodesic (O'Neill's tensor $A$ is nonzero for the Hopf fi
bration, since the horizontal distribution is non-integrable).

**Explicit non-horizontal geodesic.** Take
$$p = (1,\,0) \in \mathbb{C}^2, \qquad q = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(i,\,1) \in \mathb
b{C}^2.$$
These are orthonormal in $\mathbb{R}^4$ ($|p|=|q|=1$, $\operatorname{Re}\langle
p,q\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}=0$). The Hermitian inner product is
$$\langle p,q\rangle_{\mathbb{C}} = 1\cdot\tfrac{i}{\sqrt{2}} + 0 = \tfrac{i}{\s
qrt{2}},$$
so $\operatorname{Im}\langle p,q\rangle_{\mathbb{C}} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \neq
0$, meaning $\langle p,Jq\rangle = -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\neq 0$. The geodesic is
**not horizontal** (and not a fiber, since $q\neq \pm Jp$):
$$\gamma(t) = \Bigl(\cos t + \tfrac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\sin t,\;\; \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\
sin t\Bigr).$$

**Computing the projection.** With $z_1 = \cos t + \frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\sin t$ and

Thus
$$\pi(\gamma(t)) = \left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\sin 2t,\;\; \frac{1}{4}(1-\cos 2t),
\;\; \frac{1}{4}(1+\cos 2t)\right).$$

**The projection is not a geodesic.** Observe that the second and third componen

$$y + z = \frac{1}{4}(1-\cos 2t) + \frac{1}{4}(1+\cos 2t) = \frac{1}{2} \quad\te

**Verification that the image is a small circle.** Using orthonormal coordinates
