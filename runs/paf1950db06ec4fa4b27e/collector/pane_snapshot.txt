**Problem.** Find all $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ with $f(x+f(x+y))=f(2x)+y$ for

**Proof.** Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion $f(x+f(x+y))=f(2x)+y$.

**Step 1: Surjectivity.** For any fixed $x$, the right-hand side $f(2x)+y$ range
s over all of $\mathbb{R}$ as $y$ ranges over $\mathbb{R}$. Hence $f$ is surject
ive.

**Step 2: Injectivity.** Suppose $f(u)=f(v)$. Apply $P(x,u-x)$ and $P(x,v-x)$:
$$f(x+f(u))=f(2x)+u-x,\qquad f(x+f(v))=f(2x)+v-x.$$
Since $f(u)=f(v)$, the left sides are equal, so $u-x=v-x$, i.e., $u=v$. Thus $f$
 is injective, hence bijective.

**Step 3: A recurrence from a zero.** Since $f$ is surjective, pick $a$ with $f(
a)=0$. Setting $y=a-x$ in $P(x,y)$:
$$f(x+f(a))=f(2x)+a-x \;\Longrightarrow\; f(x)=f(2x)+a-x,$$
so
$$f(2x)=f(x)+x-a \quad\text{for all }x. \tag{1}$$

**Step 4: A two-variable identity.** Substitute $y=t-x$ into $P(x,y)$ and use (1
):
$$f(x+f(t))=f(2x)+t-x=\bigl(f(x)+x-a\bigr)+t-x=f(x)+t-a.$$
Thus
$$f(x+f(t))=f(x)+t-a \quad\text{for all }x,t. \tag{2}$$

**Step 5: Cauchy form.** Since $f$ is bijective, set $u=f(t)$ (so $t=f^{-1}(u)$)
 in (2):
$$f(x+u)=f(x)+f^{-1}(u)-a.$$
The difference $f(x+u)-f(x)$ is independent of $x$; setting $x=0$ gives $f(x+u)-
f(x)=f(u)-f(0)$. Hence
$$f(x+u)=f(x)+f(u)-f(0).$$
Defining $h(x)=f(x)-f(0)$, we get $h(x+u)=h(x)+h(u)$: **$h$ is additive** (Cauch
y equation), and $f(x)=h(x)+f(0)$.

**Step 6: Pinning down $h$.** From (1) and $f(x)=h(x)+f(0)$:
$$h(2x)+f(0)=h(x)+f(0)+x-a.$$
By additivity $h(2x)=2h(x)$, so $2h(x)=h(x)+x-a$, giving
$$h(x)=x-a \quad\text{for all }x. \tag{3}$$
But $h$ is additive, so $h(x+y)=h(x)+h(y)$ applied to (3):
$$x+y-a=(x-a)+(y-a)=x+y-2a,$$
which forces $a=0$. Therefore $h(x)=x$ for all $x$, and $f(x)=x+f(0)$.

**Step 7: Determining the constant.** With $f(x)=x+d$ where $d=f(0)$, substitute
 into the original equation:
$$f(x+f(x+y))=f(x+(x+y+d))=f(2x+y+d)=2x+y+2d,$$
$$f(2x)+y=(2x+d)+y=2x+y+d.$$
Equating: $2x+y+2d=2x+y+d$, so $d=0$.

**Conclusion.** The unique solution is $f(x)=x$.
