# Proof

**Theorem.** For every directed multigraph $G = (V, E)$ of arbitrary cardinality
, there exists a $G$-spacious topological space $T$ that is clean and admits an
embedding of $G$.

**Answer: Yes.**

## Construction

Given a directed multigraph $G = (V, E)$ (where $E$ is a multiset of ordered pai
rs from $V$, so parallel edges and loops are permitted), define the **geometric
realization** $T_G$ as follows.

Let $X = \bigl(V \times \{0\}\bigr) \;\sqcup\; \bigsqcup_{e \in E} [0,1]_e$, the
 disjoint union of one copy of $\{0\}$ for each vertex and one copy of $[0,1]$ f
or each edge $e$. Define an equivalence relation $\sim$ on $X$ by: for each edge
 $e \colon u \to v$ (i.e., $e$ is a directed edge from $u$ to $v$),
$$(e, 0) \sim (u, 0), \qquad (e, 1) \sim (v, 0).$$

Set $T_G = X / \sim$ equipped with the quotient topology. For infinite $E$, this
 is equivalently a **1-dimensional CW complex** with the weak topology: the 0-sk
eleton is $V$ (the image of $V \times \{0\}$), and each edge $e$ contributes a 1
-cell attached via the characteristic map $[0,1]_e \to T_G$ that sends $0 \mapst
o u$ and $1 \mapsto v$.

## $T_G$ admits an embedding of $G$

Define $\iota \colon G \to T_G$ by:
- On vertices: $\iota(v) = $ the equivalence class of $(v,0)$, which is a 0-cell
 of $T_G$.
- On each edge $e \colon u \to v$: $\iota(e)$ is the image of $[0,1]_e$ in the q
uotient, traversed from $\iota(u)$ to $\iota(v)$.

**$\iota$ is an embedding.** The geometric realization of a graph is *defined* s
o that the natural inclusion is a homeomorphism onto its image when we view $G$
as a 1-dimensional CW complex. Concretely:

1. **Injectivity on vertices:** Distinct vertices $u \neq v$ map to distinct 0-c
ells, since no equivalence identifies $(u,0)$ with $(v,0)$ for $u \neq v$.

2. **Injectivity on edges:** Distinct edges $e \neq e'$ (even parallel edges bet
ween the same pair of vertices) correspond to distinct copies $[0,1]_e$ and $[0,
1]_{e'}$ in the disjoint union. Their interiors $(0,1)_e$ and $(0,1)_{e'}$ are n
ever identified, so $\iota(e)$ and $\iota(e')$ are distinct arcs in $T_G$.

3. **Homeomorphism onto image:** The map from the abstract graph (viewed as a 1-
complex) to $T_G$ is a continuous bijection onto $T_G$ itself. By the definition
 of the weak topology on a CW complex, a subset of $T_G$ is closed iff its inter
section with each closed cell is closed. This is precisely the topology making $
\iota$ a homeomorphism. (For finite graphs this is immediate from compactness of
 the domain; for infinite graphs the weak topology is *defined* to make this wor
k for arbitrary cardinality.)

## $T_G$ is clean

A drawing is **clean** if distinct edges intersect only at common endpoints. In
$T_G$:

- The open 1-cell $\iota\bigl((0,1)_e\bigr)$ for edge $e$ is the image of the in
terior of $[0,1]_e$, which is identified with nothing else (the equivalence rela
tion only identifies endpoints).
- For distinct edges $e \neq e'$, the interiors $\iota\bigl((0,1)_e\bigr)$ and $
\iota\bigl((0,1)_{e'}\bigr)$ are disjoint, since they come from distinct summand
s of the disjoint union and are never identified.
- Two edges $e, e'$ can only meet at a 0-cell, i.e., at a vertex that is a commo
n endpoint.

Thus the drawing is clean. $\square$

## $T_G$ is $G$-spacious

By definition, $T_G$ is $G$-spacious because it admits a (clean) $G$-drawing — n
amely $\iota$ itself.

## Arbitrary cardinality

The construction places no restriction on $|V|$ or $|E|$:

- **1-dimensional CW complexes of arbitrary size:** The definition of a CW compl
ex via the weak topology (a set is closed iff it meets each closed cell in a clo
sed set) is valid for any indexing set, including proper classes in the NBG sens
e, and certainly for any set-sized cardinality. No dimension bound or separabili
ty assumption is needed.

- **Contrast with $\mathbb{R}^n$:** The obstruction for the example graph $G = (
V, E)$ with $V = 2^{\mathbb{R}}$ and $\{A, B\} \in E \iff A \triangle B$ is fini
te — which cannot be drawn in any $\mathbb{R}^n$ — arises from the *specific str
ucture* of $\mathbb{R}^n$ (it is a finite-dimensional, separable, metrizable man
ifold with bounded local cardinality). The geometric realization $T_G$ dispenses
 with all these constraints: it is typically non-metrizable, non-separable, and
not finite-dimensional in any meaningful sense for large $G$, but it is a perfec
tly valid topological space.

- **Every finite graph embeds on a surface / in $\mathbb{R}^3$:** These known re
sults are special cases. For finite $G$, $T_G$ is a finite 1-complex, which embe
ds in $\mathbb{R}^3$ (and on some surface), recovering the cited facts. The poin
t is that by *choosing $T$ freely* rather than fixing it to $\mathbb{R}^n$, we r
emove all cardinality-based obstructions.

## Conclusion

For **every** directed multigraph $G$ of arbitrary cardinality, the geometric re
alization $T_G$ is a $G$-spacious topological space that is clean and admits an
embedding of $G$. The answer to the question is therefore **yes, unconditionally
**: such a space always exists.
