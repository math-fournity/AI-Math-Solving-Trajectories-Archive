**Claim:** Yes, it follows that all $p_i(t) \equiv 0$.

**Proof.** We proceed by induction on $n$.

**Base case ($n=1$).** If $e^{\lambda_1 t}p_1(t)=0$ for all $t\ge 0$, then since
 $e^{\lambda_1 t}\neq 0$ for every $t$, we have $p_1(t)=0$ for all $t\ge 0$. A p
olynomial vanishing on $[0,\infty)$ (an infinite set) is identically zero, so $p
_1\equiv 0$.

**Inductive step.** Assume the result holds for any collection of $n-1$ distinct
 exponents. Suppose
$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} e^{\lambda_i t}\,p_i(t)=0 \qquad \text{for all } t\ge 0, \tag{$
\ast$}$$
with $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n$ distinct. Let $d_n=\deg p_n$ (set $d_n=-1$ if $
p_n\equiv 0$, in which case we are already in the $n-1$ case). Consider the diff
erential operator
$$L := (D-\lambda_n)^{d_n+1}, \qquad D=\frac{d}{dt}.$$

A standard computation gives, for any polynomial $q$ and any $\lambda$,
$$(D-\lambda_n)\bigl(e^{\lambda t}q(t)\bigr)=e^{\lambda t}\bigl((\lambda-\lambda
_n)\,q(t)+q'(t)\bigr).$$

*Effect on the $i=n$ term.* Each application of $(D-\lambda_n)$ lowers the degre
e of the polynomial factor by exactly $1$ (the term $(\lambda_n-\lambda_n)q$ van
ishes, leaving only $q'$). After $d_n+1$ applications,
$$L\bigl(e^{\lambda_n t}p_n(t)\bigr)=e^{\lambda_n t}\,p_n^{(d_n+1)}(t)=0,$$
since $p_n^{(d_n+1)}\equiv 0$.

*Effect on the $i\neq n$ terms.* For $i\neq n$, the factor $(\lambda_i-\lambda_n
)\neq 0$ means the leading coefficient of the polynomial factor is multiplied by
 the nonzero constant $(\lambda_i-\lambda_n)$ at each application, so **the degr
ee is preserved**. Concretely, after $d_n+1$ applications,
$$L\bigl(e^{\lambda_i t}p_i(t)\bigr)=e^{\lambda_i t}\,\widetilde{p}_i(t),$$
where $\widetilde{p}_i$ is a polynomial with $\deg \widetilde{p}_i=\deg p_i$ and
 leading coefficient $(\lambda_i-\lambda_n)^{d_n+1}\cdot(\text{leading coeff of

$$\widetilde{p}_i\equiv 0 \iff p_i\equiv 0. \tag{$\dagger$}$$

Applying $L$ to both sides of $(\ast)$ (differentiation preserves identities on
$[0,\infty)$), the $i=n$ term drops out and we obtain
$$\sum_{i=1}^{n-1} e^{\lambda_i t}\,\widetilde{p}_i(t)=0 \qquad \text{for all }
t\ge 0.$$

By the induction hypothesis (applied to the $n-1$ distinct exponents $\lambda_1,

(\dagger)$, $p_i\equiv 0$ for $i=1,\dots,n-1$.

$, hence $p_n\equiv 0$ by the base-case argument.

This completes the induction. $\blacksquare$
