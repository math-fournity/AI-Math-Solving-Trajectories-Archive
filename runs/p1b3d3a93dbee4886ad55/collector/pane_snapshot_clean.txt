## Solution

**Answer: No — the inverse transition map is not necessarily C∞** (if C∞-compati
bility is taken to mean that the transition map φ∘ψ⁻¹ is C∞ in one direction onl
y). The smoothness of the inverse must be required **explicitly** in the definit
ion; it does not follow automatically.

### Counterexample

Let $M = \mathbb{R}$ with the standard topology (a 1-dimensional topological man
ifold). Consider two charts:

- $(\mathbb{R},\, \varphi)$ with $\varphi = \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}}$,
- $(\mathbb{R},\, \psi)$ with $\psi(x) = x^3$.

Both $\varphi$ and $\psi$ are homeomorphisms from $\mathbb{R}$ onto $\mathbb{R}$
 (an open subset of $\mathbb{R}$), so both are valid charts on $M$.

**Transition map** $\psi \circ \varphi^{-1} = \psi \circ \mathrm{id} = \psi:\; \
mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$, given by $x \mapsto x^3$. This is $C^\infty$ (it is a
 polynomial).

**Inverse transition map** $\varphi \circ \psi^{-1} = \mathrm{id} \circ \psi^{-1
}:\; \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$, given by $x \mapsto \sqrt[3]{x} = x^{1/3}$. Thi
s is **not** $C^\infty$ — in fact, it is not even $C^1$ at the origin, since its
 would-be derivative

$$\frac{d}{dx}\,x^{1/3} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,x^{-2/3}$$

diverges to $+\infty$ as $x \to 0$. Hence $\sqrt[3]{x}$ is continuous but not di
fferentiable at $x=0$.

### Why the inverse function theorem does not rescue the situation

One might hope to argue: $\psi \circ \varphi^{-1}$ is a $C^\infty$ bijection (an
d homeomorphism) between open subsets of $\mathbb{R}^n$, so by the inverse funct
ion theorem its inverse is $C^\infty$.

This reasoning **fails**. The inverse function theorem requires the derivative $
D(\psi \circ \varphi^{-1})$ to be **invertible at every point**. A bijective $C^
\infty$ map need **not** have an invertible derivative everywhere. In our exampl
e:

$$\frac{d}{dx}(x^3) = 3x^2 = 0 \quad \text{at } x = 0,$$

so the derivative is singular at the origin. Bijectivity of the map does **not**
 imply invertibility of its derivative — $x \mapsto x^3$ is globally bijective y
et has vanishing derivative at one point.

### Conclusion

The smoothness of the inverse transition map does **not** follow from the smooth
ness of the transition map alone. This is precisely why the **standard definitio
n** of $C^\infty$-compatibility between two charts $(U,\varphi)$ and $(V,\psi)$
on a topological manifold requires the transition map

$$\varphi \circ \psi^{-1}:\; \psi(U \cap V) \to \varphi(U \cap V)$$

to be a **diffeomorphism** — i.e., $C^\infty$ **with $C^\infty$ inverse** — rath
er than merely $C^\infty$ in one direction. The counterexample above ($x^3$ vs.
$\sqrt[3]{x}$) demonstrates that the two conditions are genuinely independent: o
ne-direction $C^\infty$-ness does not imply $C^\infty$-ness of the inverse.
