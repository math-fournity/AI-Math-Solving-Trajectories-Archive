## Proof

**Step 1: Recognize the sum as an expectation.**

Let $K \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(n, x)$. Then
$$\sum_{k=0}^n \sqrt{k(n-k)}\binom{n}{k}x^k(1-x)^{n-k} = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\sqrt
{K(n-K)}\right].$$

Hence
$$f(x) = \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sqrt{K(n-K)}\right] =
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\sqrt{K(n-K)}}{n}\right] = \lim_{n\to\i
nfty}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sqrt{\tfrac{K}{n}\left(1-\tfrac{K}{n}\right)}\right].$$

**Step 2: Identify the almost-sure limit.**

By the Strong Law of Large Numbers, $\frac{K}{n} \to x$ almost surely as $n\to\i
nfty$. The map $t \mapsto \sqrt{t(1-t)}$ is continuous on $[0,1]$, so by the con
tinuous mapping theorem
$$\sqrt{\tfrac{K}{n}\left(1-\tfrac{K}{n}\right)} \;\longrightarrow\; \sqrt{x(1-x
)} \quad\text{a.s.}$$

**Step 3: Justify passing the limit through the expectation.**

Since $\frac{K}{n}\in[0,1]$, we have $t(1-t)\le\frac14$ for $t\in[0,1]$, hence
$$0 \le \sqrt{\tfrac{K}{n}\left(1-\tfrac{K}{n}\right)} \le \tfrac12.$$

The constant $\frac12$ is integrable, so by the **Dominated Convergence Theorem*
*,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sqrt{\tfrac{K}{n}\left(1-\tfrac{K}{n}\righ
t)}\right] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\sqrt{x(1-x)}\right] = \sqrt{x(1-x)}.$$

Therefore
$$f(x) = \sqrt{x(1-x)}, \qquad x\in[0,1].$$

**Step 4: Maximize.**

The function $g(x)=x(1-x)=x-x^2$ is a downward parabola with maximum at $x=\frac
12$, where $g\!\left(\frac12\right)=\frac14$. Thus
$$f(x) = \sqrt{x(1-x)} \le \sqrt{\tfrac14} = \tfrac12,$$
with equality at $x=\frac12$.

**Conclusion.** The maximum value of $f$ on $[0,1]$ is $\boxed{\dfrac12}$, attai
ned at $x=\dfrac12$.
