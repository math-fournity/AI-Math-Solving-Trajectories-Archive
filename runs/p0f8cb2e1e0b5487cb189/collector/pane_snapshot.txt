# Stationary Phase Approximation

## Setup

We want the asymptotic behavior as $\varepsilon \to 0^+$ of
$$I(\varepsilon)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\, e^{i\varphi(x)/\varepsilon}\,dx,
$$
where $f,\varphi\in C^\infty$, and $\varphi$ has a **single non-degenerate** sta
tionary point at $x=c$:
$$\varphi'(c)=0,\qquad \varphi''(c)\ne 0.$$
We assume $f$ decays (or is compactly supported) so the integral converges, and
that $\varphi'$ vanishes only at $c$.

## Step 1 — Localization

Away from $c$, $\varphi'(x)\ne 0$. The phase oscillates rapidly with frequency $
\sim 1/\varepsilon$, so repeated integration by parts shows those regions contri
bute only $O(\varepsilon^N)$ for every $N$ — i.e. **faster than any power of $\v
arepsilon$**. Concretely, on a region $U$ with $|\varphi'|\ge \delta>0$,
$$\int_U f\,e^{i\varphi/\varepsilon}dx=\frac{\varepsilon}{i}\int_U \frac{f}{\var
phi'}\,d\!\left(e^{i\varphi/\varepsilon}\right)=O(\varepsilon),$$
and each subsequent integration by parts gains another factor of $\varepsilon$.
Hence the leading contribution comes entirely from an arbitrarily small neighbor
hood of $c$.

## Step 2 — Reduce to a neighborhood of $c$

Choose $\rho>0$ small and split
$$I(\varepsilon)=\int_{|x-c|<\rho}f(x)e^{i\varphi(x)/\varepsilon}dx+O(\varepsilo
n^N)\ \forall N.$$
On $|x-c|<\rho$, by the non-degeneracy and the inverse function theorem, $y=\var
phi(x)-\varphi(c)$ is a valid change of variable (with $x$ as a smooth function
of $y$, $x=g(y)$, $g(0)=c$). Then
$$I(\varepsilon)=e^{i\varphi(c)/\varepsilon}\int_{|y|<\eta} f(g(y))\,g'(y)\,e^{i
y/\varepsilon}\,dy+O(\varepsilon^N).$$

## Step 3 — Morse lemma (normal form)

Because $\varphi''(c)\ne 0$, the **Morse lemma** gives a smooth change of variab
les $x=\psi(u)$ near $c$ with $\psi(0)=c$, $\psi'(0)=1$, such that
$$\varphi(\psi(u))=\varphi(c)+\tfrac12\,\varphi''(c)\,u^2.$$
(Proof sketch: write $\varphi(x)-\varphi(c)=(x-c)^2 h(x)$ with $h(c)=\tfrac12\va
rphi''(c)$ by Hadamard's lemma; since $h(c)\ne 0$, set $u=(x-c)\sqrt{2h(x)/\varp
hi''(c)}$, which is a smooth invertible map near $c$.)

Writing $\sigma=\operatorname{sgn}\varphi''(c)$ and $a=\tfrac12|\varphi''(c)|$,
this becomes $\varphi=\varphi(c)+\sigma a\,u^2$. With $F(u)=f(\psi(u))\psi'(u)$
(smooth, $F(0)=f(c)$),
$$I(\varepsilon)=e^{i\varphi(c)/\varepsilon}\int F(u)\,e^{i\sigma a u^2/\varepsi
lon}\,du+O(\varepsilon^N).$$

## Step 4 — Taylor expand the amplitude

Write $F(u)=F(0)+u\,G(u)$ with $G$ smooth. The odd part $u\,G(u)$ integrates aga
inst the even Gaussian kernel $e^{i\sigma a u^2/\varepsilon}$ and contributes at
 higher order. At leading order we replace $F$ by $F(0)=f(c)$:
$$I(\varepsilon)=f(c)\,e^{i\varphi(c)/\varepsilon}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i\si
gma a u^2/\varepsilon}\,du+O(\varepsilon).$$
(Extending the integral to $\mathbb R$ only adds $O(\varepsilon^N)$ by Step 1's
argument, since the integrand away from $0$ is non-stationary.)

## Step 5 — Evaluate the Fresnel/Gaussian integral

For $\lambda>0$,
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i\sigma\lambda u^2}\,du=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\lambda}}\,
e^{i\sigma\pi/4},$$
interpreted via the standard Fresnel integral (or by analytic continuation: rota
te the contour by $\mp\pi/4$ according to $\sigma=\pm1$ to convert to a real Gau
ssian $\int e^{-\lambda t^2}dt=\sqrt{\pi/\lambda}$, picking up the phase $e^{i\s
igma\pi/4}$).

With $\lambda=a/\varepsilon$, so $\sqrt{\pi/\lambda}=\sqrt{\pi\varepsilon/a}$:
$$\int e^{i\sigma a u^2/\varepsilon}du=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\varepsilon}{|\varphi''(c
)|}}\,e^{i\sigma\pi/4}.$$

## Step 6 — Assemble the leading term

Combining,
$$\boxed{\,I(\varepsilon)\sim f(c)\,\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\varepsilon}{|\varphi''(c)|}
}\;\exp\!\left(\frac{i\varphi(c)}{\varepsilon}+\frac{i\pi}{4}\operatorname{sgn}\
varphi''(c)\right),\qquad \varepsilon\to 0^+.\,}$$

## Step 7 — Higher-order terms (sketch)

Retaining the next Taylor term $F(u)=f(c)+\tfrac12 F''(0)u^2+\cdots$ (the linear
 term vanishes by symmetry), and using
$$\int u^{2n}e^{i\sigma a u^2/\varepsilon}du=\left(\frac{i\varepsilon}{2\sigma a
}\right)^n(2n{-}1)!!\,\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\varepsilon}{|\varphi''(c)|}}\,e^{i\sigma\
pi/4},$$
one obtains a full asymptotic expansion
$$I(\varepsilon)\sim e^{i\varphi(c)/\varepsilon+i\sigma\pi/4}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\va
repsilon}{|\varphi''(c)|}}\sum_{n\ge 0}(i\varepsilon)^n\,L_n,$$
where $L_0=f(c)$ and the $L_n$ are computable from the jets of $f$ and $\varphi$
 at $c$. Each correction is $O(\varepsilon^n)$ relative to the leading term.

## Verification of the leading formula

- **Scaling**: $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ factor — correct, since the effective width
of the contributing region is $\sim\sqrt{\varepsilon/a}$ (where the phase varies
 by $O(1)$), giving a $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ volume times $O(1)$ amplitude.
- **Phase**: $e^{i\varphi(c)/\varepsilon}$ — the value of the phase at the stati
onary point, as expected.
- **Morse index**: $e^{i\pi\operatorname{sgn}\varphi''(c)/4}$ — the signature of
 the Hessian (here 1D, so $\pm1$), the universal Fresnel phase.
- **Degenerate limit**: as $|\varphi''(c)|\to 0$ the prefactor diverges, signali
ng breakdown of the non-degenerate hypothesis — consistent.
