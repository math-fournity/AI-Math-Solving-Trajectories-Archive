No. A Hamel basis of an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space cannot be an orthonor
mal set.

## Proof

Let $H$ be an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space with inner product $\langle\cdo
t,\cdot\rangle$, and suppose for contradiction that $B\subseteq H$ is simultaneo
usly a Hamel basis of $H$ and an orthonormal set, i.e. $\langle b,b\rangle=1$ fo
r every $b\in B$ and $\langle b,b'\rangle=0$ for distinct $b,b'\in B$.

Since $H$ is infinite-dimensional, $B$ is infinite. In particular $B$ contains a
 countably infinite subset $\{e_1,e_2,e_3,\dots\}$.

Consider the series
$$v \;=\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n}\,e_n.$$
Because the $e_n$ are orthonormal,
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|\frac{1}{n}e_n\right\|^2 = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{
1}{n^2} < \infty,$$
so by the Riesz–Fischer theorem (equivalently, completeness of $H$) the series c
onverges to some vector $v\in H$.

Since $B$ is a **Hamel** basis, $v$ must be expressible as a *finite* linear com
bination of elements of $B$:
$$v = \sum_{k=1}^{m} a_k\, b_k,\qquad b_1,\dots,b_m\in B,\quad a_k\in\mathbb{C}\
 (\text{or }\mathbb{R}).$$

The set $\{b_1,\dots,b_m\}$ is finite, so we may pick an index $N$ with $e_N\not
in\{b_1,\dots,b_m\}$ (possible since $\{e_n\}$ is infinite).

Compute $\langle v, e_N\rangle$ in two ways.

**Using the finite Hamel expansion.** By orthonormality of $B$ and the fact that
 $e_N$ is distinct from every $b_k$:
$$\langle v, e_N\rangle = \sum_{k=1}^{m} a_k\,\langle b_k, e_N\rangle = 0.$$

**Using the convergent series.** The inner product is continuous in its first ar
gument, so
$$\langle v, e_N\rangle = \left\langle \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}e_n,\ e_N\r
ight\rangle = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\langle e_n, e_N\rangle = \frac{1}{N
}\cdot 1 = \frac{1}{N},$$
where the last equality uses orthonormality: $\langle e_n,e_N\rangle = \delta_{n
N}$.

Combining the two computations gives
$$0 = \frac{1}{N},$$
a contradiction.

Therefore no such Hamel basis exists: **a Hamel basis of an infinite-dimensional
 Hilbert space cannot be an orthonormal set.** $\blacksquare$
