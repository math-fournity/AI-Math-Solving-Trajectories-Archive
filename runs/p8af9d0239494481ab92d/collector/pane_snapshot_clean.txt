*Proof of this fact:* Suppose $h$ is convex and bounded above by $M$. If $h$ is
not constant, there exist $a < b$ with $h(a) \neq h(b)$. WLOG $h(a) < h(b)$. By
convexity, for $x > b$, writing $b = \lambda a + (1-\lambda)x$ with $\lambda = \

$$h(b) \leq \lambda\, h(a) + (1-\lambda)\, h(x)$$
$$h(x) \geq \frac{h(b) - \lambda\, h(a)}{1-\lambda} = h(b) + \frac{\lambda}{1-\l
ambda}(h(b)-h(a)) = h(b) + \frac{(x-b)(h(b)-h(a))}{b-a}.$$
As $x \to \infty$, the right side $\to +\infty$, contradicting $h \leq M$. The c
ase $h(a) > h(b)$ is symmetric (let $x \to -\infty$). Hence $h$ is constant. $\s
quare$

So $h(x) = \sin(f(x)) = c$ for some constant $c \in [-1,1]$.

**Step 3: $f$ is constant.** Since $\sin(f(x)) = c$ for all $x$, the continuous
