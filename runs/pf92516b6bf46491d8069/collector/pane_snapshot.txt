## Proof

The derivative is computed via the product rule. Let $\gamma(t) = e^{-tX} g e^{t
X}$. Then $\gamma(0) = g$, and

$$
\gamma'(t) = \frac{d}{dt}e^{-tX}\cdot g\cdot e^{tX} + e^{-tX}\cdot g\cdot \frac{
d}{dt}e^{tX}.
$$

Using $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0} e^{tX} = X$ and $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0} e^{-tX}
= -X$, we obtain

$$
\gamma'(0) = -X\, g + g\, X = gX - Xg.
$$

Now we use the hypothesis. The given condition is

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0} g\, e^{tX}\, g^{-1} = X.
$$

Computing the left side via the product rule,

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0} g\, e^{tX}\, g^{-1} = g\, X\, g^{-1} = \operatorname{Ad
}_g(X).
$$

Hence the hypothesis says $\operatorname{Ad}_g(X) = gXg^{-1} = X$, i.e. $gX = Xg
$ (multiplying on the right by $g$).

Therefore

$$
\gamma'(0) = gX - Xg = 0.
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0} e^{-tX} g\, e^{tX} = 0.}
$$
