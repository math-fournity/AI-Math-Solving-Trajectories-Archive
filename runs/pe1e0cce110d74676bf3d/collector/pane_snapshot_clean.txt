## Solution

**Setup.** Let the cylinder $OO'$ have radius $r$ and height $h$, with axis alon
g the $z$-axis, base at $z=0$ and top at $z=h$.

**The cross-section plane.** The plane $\alpha$ makes a $60°$ dihedral angle wit
h the base. Place it as $z = \sqrt{3}\,x + c$ (slope $\tan 60° = \sqrt{3}$). Its
 normal $(\sqrt{3},0,-1)$ makes angle $60°$ with $\hat{z}$, confirming the dihed
ral angle is $60°$. ✓

**Dandelin spheres.** This is the classic Dandelin sphere construction. Each sph
ere is inscribed in the cylinder (tangent to the lateral surface from inside), s
o:
- Its center lies on the axis, and its **radius equals $r$** (distance from axis
 to lateral wall).
- $C_1$ tangent to the bottom base $z=0$: center $(0,0,r)$, radius $r$.
- $C_2$ tangent to the top base $z=h$: center $(0,0,h-r)$, radius $r$.

**Tangency to $\alpha$.** The plane is $\sqrt{3}\,x - z + c = 0$ with $\|\mathbf
{n}\| = 2$.

Distance from $(0,0,r)$ to $\alpha$: $|c - r|/2 = r \implies c = 3r$ or $c = -r$
.

Distance from $(0,0,h-r)$ to $\alpha$: $|c - h + r|/2 = r \implies c = h+r$ or $
c = h-3r$.

**Opposite-sides condition.** The signed values of $\sqrt{3}x - z + c$ at the tw
o centers are $c - r$ and $c - h + r$. For the spheres to be on opposite sides,
these must have opposite signs.

| $c$ (from $C_1$) | $c$ (from $C_2$) | $h$ | $c-r$ | $c-h+r$ | Opposite? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $3r$ | $h+r$ | $2r$ | $2r$ | $2r$ | ✗ |
| $3r$ | $h-3r$ | $6r$ | $2r$ | $-2r$ | ✓ |
| $-r$ | $h+r$ | $-2r$ | — | — | invalid ($h<0$) |
| $-r$ | $h-3r$ | $2r$ | $-2r$ | $-2r$ | ✗ |

The unique valid solution: $c = 3r$, $\boldsymbol{h = 6r}$.

**Verification that $\alpha$ cuts an ellipse.** At $x = \pm r$: $z = 3r \mp \sqr
t{3}\,r \in [3r-\sqrt3\,r,\; 3r+\sqrt3\,r] \approx [1.27r,\; 4.73r] \subset (0,
6r)$. The plane enters and exits through the lateral surface only, producing a f
ull ellipse. ✓

**Volume computation.**
$$V_1 = V_2 = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3, \qquad V = \pi r^2 h = \pi r^2 \cdot 6r = 6\p
i r^3.$$

$$\frac{V_1 + V_2}{V} = \frac{\tfrac{8}{3}\pi r^3}{6\pi r^3} = \frac{8/3}{6} = \
frac{8}{18} = \boxed{\dfrac{4}{9}}.$$
