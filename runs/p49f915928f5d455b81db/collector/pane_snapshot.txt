## Proof

Let $p, q$ be primes with $p \mid q-1$ and $p^2 \nmid q-1$. In particular $p < q
$, so $p^2 < q^2$ and the unique subgroup of order $p$ in $(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{
Z})^\times$ exists (since $p \mid q-1 = |\!(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times\!|$).
 Pick an element $\omega \in (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times$ of order exactly $
p$, and let $\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_q)$ be the automorphism $a
 \mapsto a^\omega$.

We exhibit two semidirect products $\mathbb{Z}_q \rtimes P$ with nontrivial acti
on, differing in the choice of the $p$-group $P$.

---

### Group $G_1$: $\mathbb{Z}_q \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_{p^2}$

Define the homomorphism $\varphi_1 : \mathbb{Z}_{p^2} \to \operatorname{Aut}(\ma
thbb{Z}_q) \cong \mathbb{Z}_{q-1}$ by sending a generator of $\mathbb{Z}_{p^2}$
to $\sigma$. This is well-defined because $\sigma$ has order $p$, and $p \mid p^
2$, so $\sigma^{p^2} = (\sigma^p)^p = \mathrm{id}$. The image of $\varphi_1$ has
 order $p$ (it cannot have order $p^2$ since $p^2 \nmid q-1 = |\operatorname{Aut
}(\mathbb{Z}_q)|$, so $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_q)$ has no element of order
 $p^2$). The kernel is the unique subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}_{p^2}$ of order $p$.

Set
$$G_1 = \mathbb{Z}_q \rtimes_{\varphi_1} \mathbb{Z}_{p^2}.$$
Concretely,
$$G_1 = \langle\, a, b \mid a^q = 1,\; b^{p^2} = 1,\; bab^{-1} = a^\omega \,\ran
gle.$$

- $|G_1| = q \cdot p^2 = p^2 q$.
- $G_1$ is **nonabelian**: $bab^{-1} = a^\omega \neq a$ (since $\omega \not\equi
v 1 \pmod q$, as $\omega$ has order $p > 1$).
- A Sylow $p$-subgroup is $\langle b \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}_{p^2}$ (cyclic).

---

### Group $G_2$: $\mathbb{Z}_q \rtimes (\mathbb{Z}_p \times \mathbb{Z}_p)$

Define $\varphi_2 : \mathbb{Z}_p \times \mathbb{Z}_p \to \operatorname{Aut}(\mat
hbb{Z}_q)$ by sending $(1,0) \mapsto \sigma$ and $(0,1) \mapsto \mathrm{id}$. Th
is is a well-defined homomorphism (both generators have order dividing $p = \ope
ratorname{ord}(\sigma)$, and they commute in the abelian source). The image has
order $p$; the kernel is $\langle (0,1) \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$.

Set
$$G_2 = \mathbb{Z}_q \rtimes_{\varphi_2} (\mathbb{Z}_p \times \mathbb{Z}_p).$$
Concretely,
$$G_2 = \langle\, a, b, c \mid a^q = 1,\; b^p = c^p = 1,\; bc = cb,\; bab^{-1} =
 a^\omega,\; cac^{-1} = a \,\rangle.$$

- $|G_2| = q \cdot p^2 = p^2 q$.
- $G_2$ is **nonabelian**: $bab^{-1} = a^\omega \neq a$.
- A Sylow $p$-subgroup is $\langle b, c \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}_p \times \mathb
b{Z}_p$ (elementary abelian, non-cyclic).

---

### Non-isomorphism

In $G_1$ every Sylow $p$-subgroup is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_{p^2}$ (cyclic of
 order $p^2$), while in $G_2$ every Sylow $p$-subgroup is isomorphic to $\mathbb
{Z}_p \times \mathbb{Z}_p$ (non-cyclic). Since isomorphic groups have isomorphic
 Sylow subgroups, and $\mathbb{Z}_{p^2} \not\cong \mathbb{Z}_p \times \mathbb{Z}
_p$ (the former has elements of order $p^2$, the latter does not), we conclude
$$G_1 \not\cong G_2.$$

Both are nonabelian groups of order $p^2 q$, and they are not isomorphic. $\blac
ksquare$
