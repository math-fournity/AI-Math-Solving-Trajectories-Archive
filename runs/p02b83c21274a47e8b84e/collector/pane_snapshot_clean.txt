## Solution

The point $(1,1)$ on the curve $x^3 - y^2 = 0$ corresponds to the prime ideal
$$\mathfrak{p} = (\bar{x}-1,\ \bar{y}-1) \subset A := \mathbb{Z}[x,y]/(x^3-y^2),
$$
where $\bar{x}, \bar{y}$ are the images of $x, y$.

**Step 1: Verify $\mathfrak{p}$ is well-defined and prime.** Since $1^3 - 1^2 =
0$, the point $(1,1)$ lies on the curve, so the ideal $(x-1, y-1) \subset \mathb
b{Z}[x,y]$ contains $(x^3 - y^2)$, and its image $\mathfrak{p}$ in $A$ is a prop
er ideal. Computing the quotient:
$$A/\mathfrak{p} \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}[x,y]/(x^3-y^2,\ x-1,\ y-1) \;\cong\; \math
bb{Z}[x,y]/(x-1,\ y-1) \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}.$$
Since $\mathbb{Z}$ is an integral domain, $\mathfrak{p}$ is a prime ideal of $A$
. (It is not maximal, since $\mathbb{Z}$ is not a field.)

**Step 2: Compute the residue field.** For an affine scheme $\operatorname{Spec}
(A)$, the residue field at a prime $\mathfrak{p}$ is
$$\kappa(\mathfrak{p}) = A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}} \;\cong\;
 \operatorname{Frac}(A/\mathfrak{p}).$$
From Step 1, $A/\mathfrak{p} \cong \mathbb{Z}$, so
$$\kappa(\mathfrak{p}) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Frac}(\mathbb{Z}) \;=\; \mathbb{Q
}.$$

**Conclusion.** The residue field of $Y = \operatorname{Spec}\!\left(\mathbb{Z}[
x,y]/(x^3-y^2)\right)$ at the point $(1,1)$ is

$$\boxed{\mathbb{Q}}.$$
