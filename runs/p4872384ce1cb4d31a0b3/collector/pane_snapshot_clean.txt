## Proof

We wish to evaluate

$$\mathbb{E}[N(X)] = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} N(x)\,\varphi(x)\,dx,$$

where $\varphi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/2}$ is the standard normal dens
ity and $N(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\varphi(u)\,du$ is the standard normal CDF.

### Method 1: Antiderivative (fundamental theorem of calculus)

Since $N'(x)=\varphi(x)$, the integrand is $N(x)\,N'(x)$. Hence

$$\mathbb{E}[N(X)]=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}N(x)\,N'(x)\,dx=\left[\frac{N(x)^{2}}{
2}\right]_{-\infty}^{\infty}=\frac{N(\infty)^{2}-N(-\infty)^{2}}{2}=\frac{1^{2}-
0^{2}}{2}=\boxed{\tfrac{1}{2}}.$$

### Method 2: Polar coordinates (as suggested by the problem)

Expanding the definition,

$$\mathbb{E}[N(X)]=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{x}
e^{-u^{2}/2}\,du\right)e^{-x^{2}/2}\,dx=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\{u\le x\}} e^{-(u^
{2}+x^{2})/2}\,du\,dx.$$

The region $\{u\le x\}$ is the half-plane of $\mathbb{R}^{2}$ lying below/on the
 line $u=x$, which passes through the origin. The integrand $e^{-(u^{2}+x^{2})/2
}=e^{-r^{2}/2}$ is **rotationally symmetric** about the origin. Therefore the in
tegral over any half-plane whose boundary passes through the origin equals exact
ly half of the total integral over $\mathbb{R}^{2}$:

$$\iint_{\{u\le x\}} e^{-(u^{2}+x^{2})/2}\,du\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}
^{2}} e^{-(u^{2}+x^{2})/2}\,du\,dx.$$

Computing the full integral in polar coordinates $(r,\theta)$:

$$\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}} e^{-r^{2}/2}\,du\,dx=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^
{-r^{2}/2}\,r\,dr\,d\theta=2\pi\left[-e^{-r^{2}/2}\right]_{0}^{\infty}=2\pi\cdot
 1=2\pi.$$

Hence the half-plane integral equals $\pi$, and

$$\mathbb{E}[N(X)]=\frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot\pi=\boxed{\tfrac{1}{2}}.$$

### Verification of the symmetry argument

More explicitly, rotate coordinates by $\pi/4$: let $s=(x-u)/\sqrt{2}$, $t=(x+u)
/\sqrt{2}$. The region $u\le x$ becomes $s\ge 0$, a half-plane bounded by the $t
$-axis (through the origin), and $u^{2}+x^{2}=s^{2}+t^{2}$. So

$$\iint_{\{u\le x\}} e^{-(u^{2}+x^{2})/2}\,du\,dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty
}^{\infty}e^{-(s^{2}+t^{2})/2}\,dt\,ds=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-(s^{
2}+t^{2})/2}\,ds\,dt=\pi,$$

confirming the result.

### Conclusion

Both methods agree:

$$\mathbb{E}[N(X)]=\frac{1}{2}.$$
