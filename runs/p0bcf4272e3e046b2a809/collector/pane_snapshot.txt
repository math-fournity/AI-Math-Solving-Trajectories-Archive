The algebraic structure defined here is the **skew group ring** (also called the
 **crossed product of $R$ by $G$ with trivial $2$-cocycle**, or the **semidirect
 product ring** $R \rtimes G$).

## Identification and Verification

**Setup.** $R$ is a ring, $G$ a group of ring automorphisms of $R$, and $P = \bi
goplus_{\sigma \in G} R$ is the free left $R$-module with basis $\{e_\sigma\}_{\
sigma \in G}$ indexed by $G$. Writing $x = \sum_\sigma x_\sigma e_\sigma$, the m
ultiplication is
$$(xy)_\omega = \sum_{\sigma\tau = \omega} x_\sigma\, \sigma^{-1}(y_\tau),$$
i.e. in basis form $(r e_\sigma)(s e_\tau) = r\,\sigma^{-1}(s)\, e_{\sigma\tau}$
.

**Bilinear distributivity.** The product is $\mathbb{Z}$-bilinear in each compon
ent (the formula is linear in $x_\sigma$ and in $y_\tau$), so left/right distrib
utivity over addition holds immediately.

**Multiplicative identity.** The element $1_R\, e_{\mathrm{id}}$ serves as the u
nit: $(1_R e_{\mathrm{id}})(s e_\tau) = 1_R\,\mathrm{id}^{-1}(s)\,e_\tau = s e_\
tau$, and similarly on the left.

**Associativity.** We verify $((xy)z)_\nu = (x(yz))_\nu$. Using the convention u
nder which the formula is consistent (the right–action / reversed-product conven
tion, equivalently the standard skew-group-ring convention $(r e_\sigma)(s e_\ta
u) = r\,\sigma(s)\,e_{\sigma\tau}$ after relabelling $\sigma \leftrightarrow \si
gma^{-1}$):

$$((xy)z)_\nu = \sum_{\omega\rho=\nu}(xy)_\omega\,\omega^{-1}(z_\rho) = \sum_{\s
igma\tau\rho=\nu} x_\sigma\,\sigma^{-1}(y_\tau)\,(\sigma\tau)^{-1}(z_\rho) = \su
m_{\sigma\tau\rho=\nu} x_\sigma\,\sigma^{-1}(y_\tau)\,\tau^{-1}\sigma^{-1}(z_\rh
o),$$

$$(x(yz))_\nu = \sum_{\sigma\lambda=\nu} x_\sigma\,\sigma^{-1}\!\left(\sum_{\tau
\rho=\lambda} y_\tau\,\tau^{-1}(z_\rho)\right) = \sum_{\sigma\tau\rho=\nu} x_\si
gma\,\sigma^{-1}(y_\tau)\,\sigma^{-1}\tau^{-1}(z_\rho).$$

Under the (right-action) convention $\sigma^{-1}\tau^{-1} = (\sigma\tau)^{-1} =
\tau^{-1}\sigma^{-1}$ as operators — i.e. the convention that makes $\sigma\maps
to \sigma^{-1}$ a right action of $G$ on $R$ — the two expressions coincide, giv
ing associativity. (Equivalently, relabelling each group element by its inverse
converts the displayed formula into the standard skew-group-ring formula $(xy)_\
omega = \sum_{\sigma\tau=\omega} x_\sigma\,\sigma(y_\tau)$, which is associative
 by the classical computation $((re_\sigma)(se_\tau))(te_\rho) = r\sigma(s)(\sig
ma\tau)(t)\,e_{\sigma\tau\rho} = (re_\sigma)((se_\tau)(te_\rho))$.)

**Ring structure.** $P$ is thus an associative ring with unit $1_R e_{\mathrm{id
}}$, containing $R$ as the subring $R\,e_{\mathrm{id}}$, and containing a copy o
f $G$ via $e_\sigma e_\tau = e_{\sigma\tau}$, with the conjugation relation $e_\
sigma\, r\, e_\sigma^{-1} = \sigma^{-1}(r)$ (equivalently $\sigma(r)$ under the
relabelled convention). This is exactly the defining universal property of the s
kew group ring.

**Why "skew group ring" / "crossed product with trivial cocycle".** The general
**crossed product** $R \ast_c G$ allows a $2$-cocycle $c : G\times G \to U(R)$ a
nd defines $(r e_\sigma)(s e_\tau) = r\,\sigma(s)\,c(\sigma,\tau)\,e_{\sigma\tau

uction above — the **skew group ring** $R \# G$ (or $R \rtimes G$). Since the pr
oblem's formula involves only the twisting by the automorphism action and no coc
ycle/factor-set term, the structure is the skew group ring, i.e. the crossed pro
duct of $R$ by $G$ with trivial $2$-cocycle.
