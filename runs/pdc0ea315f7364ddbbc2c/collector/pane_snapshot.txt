# Solution

## Setup and Key Theorem

Let $v = |\cdot|_\infty$ be the archimedean (usual absolute value) valuation on
$\mathbb{Q}$, with completion $K_v = \mathbb{R}$.

**Key Theorem (Extension of valuations to finite extensions).** Let $L/K$ be a f
inite extension and $v$ a valuation on $K$ with completion $K_v$. The extensions
 $w$ of $v$ to $L$ are in bijection with the factors in the decomposition
$$L \otimes_K K_v \;\cong\; \prod_{w \mid v} L_w,$$
where $L_w$ is the completion of $L$ at $w$. In particular, **the number of exte
nsions of $v$ to $L$ equals the number of irreducible factors** of the minimal p
olynomial defining $L$ when factored over $K_v$.

## Applying to $L = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[n]{2})$

Since $x^n - 2$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ (Eisenstein at $p=2$), we have
$$L = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[n]{2}) \;\cong\; \mathbb{Q}[x]/(x^n - 2), \qquad [L:\math
bb{Q}] = n.$$

Therefore
$$L \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{R} \;\cong\; \mathbb{R}[x]/(x^n - 2).$$

The number of extensions of $|\cdot|_\infty$ to $L$ equals the number of irreduc
ible factors of $x^n - 2$ over $\mathbb{R}$.

## Factoring $x^n - 2$ over $\mathbb{R}$

The roots of $x^n - 2$ in $\mathbb{C}$ are
$$\alpha_k = 2^{1/n}\, e^{2\pi i k/n}, \qquad k = 0, 1, \ldots, n-1.$$

Over $\mathbb{R}$, irreducible polynomials are either linear (real root) or quad
ratic (pair of complex-conjugate roots). A root $\alpha_k$ is real if and only i
f $e^{2\pi i k/n} \in \{+1, -1\}$, i.e.:

- **$k = 0$:** always gives the real root $2^{1/n}$.
- **$k = n/2$ (only when $n$ is even):** gives the real root $-2^{1/n}$.

The remaining non-real roots pair up as $(\alpha_k, \overline{\alpha_k}) = (\alp
ha_k, \alpha_{n-k})$, each pair contributing one irreducible quadratic factor ov
er $\mathbb{R}$.

### Case 1: $n$ odd

- Real roots: $1$ (namely $2^{1/n}$).
- Non-real conjugate pairs: $\dfrac{n-1}{2}$.
- **Number of irreducible factors** $= 1 + \dfrac{n-1}{2} = \dfrac{n+1}{2}$.

### Case 2: $n$ even

- Real roots: $2$ (namely $\pm 2^{1/n}$).
- Non-real conjugate pairs: $\dfrac{n-2}{2}$.
- **Number of irreducible factors** $= 2 + \dfrac{n-2}{2} = \dfrac{n+2}{2} = \df
rac{n}{2}+1$.

## Unified Formula

Both cases are captured by:

$$\boxed{\left\lfloor \frac{n}{2} \right\rfloor + 1}$$

The archimedean valuation on $\mathbb{Q}$ has exactly $\left\lfloor \dfrac{n}{2}
 \right\rfloor + 1$ extensions to $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[n]{2})$.
