**Answer: Yes.** Such a normed space exists. The standard example is $X = c_0$,
the space of real sequences converging to $0$, equipped with the supremum norm $
\|x\|_\infty = \sup_n |x_n|$.

---

**Construction.** Define $f : c_0 \to \mathbb{R}$ by
$$f(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x_n}{2^n}.$$

**Step 1: $f$ is a well-defined linear continuous functional.**

Linearity is immediate. For continuity, note that for every $x \in c_0$,
$$|f(x)| \le \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{|x_n|}{2^n} \le \|x\|_\infty \sum_{n=1}^{
\infty} \frac{1}{2^n} = \|x\|_\infty.$$
So $f$ is bounded (hence continuous) with $\|f\| \le 1$.

**Step 2: $\|f\| = 1$.**

For each $N \ge 1$, define $x^{(N)} \in c_0$ by $x^{(N)}_n = 1$ for $1 \le n \le
 N$ and $x^{(N)}_n = 0$ for $n > N$. Then $\|x^{(N)}\|_\infty = 1$ and
$$f\!\left(x^{(N)}\right) = \sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{1}{2^n} = 1 - \frac{1}{2^N} \xr
ightarrow{N\to\infty} 1.$$
Therefore $\|f\| = \sup_{\|x\|\le 1} |f(x)| \ge 1$, and combined with Step 1, $\
|f\| = 1$.

**Step 3: No $x \in c_0$ with $\|x\|_\infty = 1$ satisfies $f(x) = 1$.**

Suppose for contradiction that $x \in c_0$ with $\|x\|_\infty = 1$ and $f(x) = 1
$. Then
$$1 = f(x) \le |f(x)| \le \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{|x_n|}{2^n} \le \sum_{n=1}^{
\infty} \frac{1}{2^n} = 1.$$
All inequalities must be equalities. In particular:
- $|f(x)| = f(x)$, so $f(x) \ge 0$ (no cancellation).
- $\sum \frac{|x_n|}{2^n} = 1 = \sum \frac{1}{2^n}$, which forces $|x_n| = 1$ fo
r **every** $n \ge 1$.

But $x \in c_0$ means $x_n \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$, which contradicts $|x_n| =
1$ for all $n$. Hence no such $x$ exists.

---

**Conclusion.** The space $X = c_0$ with the supremum norm, together with the fu
nctional $f(x) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty x_n/2^n$, provides a normed space in which th
ere is a linear continuous functional $f$ with $\|f\| = 1$ that does **not** att
ain its norm on the unit sphere. $\blacksquare$
