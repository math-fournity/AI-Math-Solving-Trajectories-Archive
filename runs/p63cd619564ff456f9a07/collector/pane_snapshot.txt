## Proof

**Claim:** The Banach space \(C([0,1])\) (with the supremum norm \(\|\cdot\|_\in
fty\)) **cannot** be written as a countable union of compact sets \(K_1 \subset
K_2 \subset K_3 \subset \cdots\).

**Step 1. \(C([0,1])\) is a complete metric space.**

With the supremum norm, \(C([0,1])\) is a Banach space: the uniform limit of con
tinuous functions is continuous, so every Cauchy sequence converges. Hence \(C([
0,1])\) is a complete metric space, and Baire's Category Theorem applies: *a non
-empty complete metric space is not a countable union of nowhere-dense closed se
ts* (equivalently, it is of second category in itself).

**Step 2. Each \(K_n\) is closed.**

\(C([0,1])\) is a metric space, hence Hausdorff. In a Hausdorff space every comp
act subset is closed. Thus each \(K_n\) is closed.

**Step 3. Each \(K_n\) has empty interior (is nowhere dense).**

It suffices to show that no compact subset of \(C([0,1])\) contains a non-empty
open ball. Suppose for contradiction that some \(K_n\) contains an open ball \(B
(f_0, r) = \{f : \|f - f_0\|_\infty < r\}\) with \(r > 0\). Then the closed ball

\[
\overline{B}(f_0, r/2) = \{f : \|f - f_0\|_\infty \le r/2\}
\]

is a closed subset of \(K_n\). Being a closed subset of a compact set, \(\overli
ne{B}(f_0, r/2)\) is itself compact. By translation and scaling, this implies th
e closed unit ball \(\overline{B}(0,1)\) of \(C([0,1])\) is compact.

But \(C([0,1])\) is **infinite-dimensional** (e.g., the functions \(1, x, x^2, \
dots\) are linearly independent). By **Riesz's lemma**, in any infinite-dimensio
nal normed space the closed unit ball is **not** compact. (Riesz's lemma: if \(Y
\) is a proper closed subspace of a normed space \(X\), then for every \(\theta
\in (0,1)\) there exists \(x_\theta\) with \(\|x_\theta\|=1\) and \(\inf_{y\in Y
}\|x_\theta - y\| \ge \theta\). Iterating this on an increasing sequence of fini
te-dimensional subspaces produces a sequence in the unit ball with no convergent
 subsequence.) This is a contradiction.

Therefore every compact \(K_n \subset C([0,1])\) has empty interior. Since each
\(K_n\) is also closed, each \(K_n\) is **nowhere dense**.

**Step 4. Apply Baire's theorem.**

If \(C([0,1]) = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} K_n\) with each \(K_n\) compact, then by
Steps 2–3 each \(K_n\) is closed and nowhere dense. This expresses the complete
metric space \(C([0,1])\) as a countable union of nowhere-dense closed sets, i.e
., \(C([0,1])\) would be **meager in itself**. This directly contradicts Baire's
 Category Theorem.

**Conclusion.** The contradiction shows that no such representation exists. \(C(
[0,1])\) cannot be expressed as a countable union of compact sets.
