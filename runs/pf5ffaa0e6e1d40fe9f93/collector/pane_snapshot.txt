## Proof

**Claim:** It is **not** always possible to find a real number $s < 1$ such that
 $f(x) \le s\,g(x)$ for all $x \in [a,b]$, even when $f$ and $g$ are discontinuo
us on $[a,b]$.

**Counterexample.** Take $[a,b] = [0,1]$. Define

$$g(x) = \begin{cases} 2 & \text{if } x = \tfrac{1}{2},\\ 1 & \text{if } x \neq
\tfrac{1}{2},\end{cases} \qquad f(x) = g(x).$$

**Both functions are discontinuous.** The function $g$ has a jump at $x = \tfrac
{1}{2}$: $\lim_{x \to 1/2} g(x) = 1 \neq 2 = g(\tfrac{1}{2})$, so $g$ is discont
inuous at $\tfrac{1}{2}$. Since $f = g$, the same holds for $f$. Thus $f$ and $g
$ are discontinuous on $[0,1]$.

**No $s < 1$ works.** Since $f = g$, the inequality $f(x) \le s\,g(x)$ becomes $
g(x) \le s\,g(x)$, i.e., $(1 - s)\,g(x) \le 0$ for all $x \in [0,1]$. Now $g(x)
\ge 1 > 0$ for every $x$, and $1 - s > 0$ whenever $s < 1$. Hence $(1-s)\,g(x) >
 0$ for every $x$, so the inequality $f(x) \le s\,g(x)$ **fails at every point**
.

Therefore there exists **no** $s < 1$ satisfying $f(x) \le s\,g(x)$ for all $x \
in [0,1]$.

**Conclusion.** The statement is **false**: it is not always possible to find su
ch an $s < 1$. The counterexample shows that even with both $f$ and $g$ disconti
nuous, the ratio $f/g$ can be identically $1$, forcing $s \ge 1$.
