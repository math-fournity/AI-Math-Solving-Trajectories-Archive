## Proof

Since $f$ is holomorphic and non-vanishing on the simply connected domain $D$, t
here exists a holomorphic function $g : D \to \mathbb{C}$ such that $f = e^g$ (a
 holomorphic logarithm of $f$). Write $g = u + iv$ with $u = \operatorname{Re} g
 = \log|f|$.

**Step 1: $u$ is harmonic and vanishes at the boundary.**

The function $u = \log|f|$ is harmonic on $D$ (being the real part of the holomo
rphic function $g$). The hypothesis $|f(z)| \to 1$ as $|z| \to 1$ gives
$$
u(z) = \log|f(z)| \to \log 1 = 0 \quad \text{as } |z| \to 1.
$$

**Step 2: Apply the maximum principle to conclude $u \equiv 0$.**

Fix $\varepsilon > 0$. By the boundary limit, there exists $r_0 \in (0,1)$ such
that $|u(z)| < \varepsilon$ whenever $r_0 \le |z| < 1$.

For any $r$ with $r_0 \le r < 1$, $u$ is harmonic on the closed disk $\overline{
D(0,r)}$, so by the maximum principle its maximum and minimum on $\overline{D(0,
r)}$ are attained on $|z| = r$. On $|z| = r$ we have $|u| < \varepsilon$, hence
$$
-\varepsilon < u(z) < \varepsilon \quad \text{for all } |z| \le r.
$$
Since this holds for every $r \in [r_0, 1)$, it holds for all $z \in D$:
$$
-\varepsilon < u(z) < \varepsilon \quad \text{for all } z \in D.
$$
Letting $\varepsilon \to 0$, we obtain $u \equiv 0$ on $D$.

**Step 3: $g$ is constant, hence $f$ is constant.**

Since $g$ is holomorphic and $\operatorname{Re} g = u \equiv 0$ is constant, the
 Cauchy–Riemann equations force $\operatorname{Im} g = v$ to be constant as well
: $u_x = v_y = 0$ and $u_y = -v_x = 0$, so $v_x = v_y = 0$ on the connected doma
in $D$, giving $v \equiv \theta \in \mathbb{R}$.

Thus $g \equiv i\theta$ is constant, and
$$
f(z) = e^{g(z)} = e^{i\theta}
$$
is a constant function (of modulus $1$).

**Conclusion.** Yes, $f$ must be constant. In fact $f$ is a constant of unit mod
ulus.
