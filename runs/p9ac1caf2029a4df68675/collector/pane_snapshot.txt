## Solution

 leaked answers. Proceeding.

**Goal:** Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_\beta \frac{e^z}{e^z-\pi}\,dz$, where $\be
ta\subset\{10<|z|<12\}$ winds $N$ times counterclockwise about the origin.

### Step 1. Locate the poles

The integrand $f(z)=\dfrac{e^z}{e^z-\pi}$ is meromorphic on $\mathbb{C}$. Its po
les occur where $e^z=\pi$, i.e. at
$$z_k=\ln\pi+2\pi i k,\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z},$$
where $\ln\pi\approx 1.1442$ is the real logarithm. Each zero of $e^z-\pi$ is si
mple (since $\frac{d}{dz}(e^z-\pi)=e^z\neq 0$ at $z_k$), so every $z_k$ is a sim
ple pole.

### Step 2. Determine which poles lie inside the loop

Since $\beta$ lies in the annulus $\{10<|z|<12\}$ and winds $N$ times about the
origin, the winding number satisfies
$$n(\beta,a)=N\ \text{for every }|a|<10,\qquad n(\beta,a)=0\ \text{for every }|a
|>12.$$
(The loop is homotopic in $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{a\}$ to the circle $|z|=11$ trav
ersed $N$ times when $|a|<10$, and to a point when $|a|>12$.)

Compute $|z_k|^2=(\ln\pi)^2+4\pi^2 k^2\approx 1.309+39.478\,k^2$:

| $k$ | $|z_k|^2$ | $|z_k|$ | location |
|-----|-----------|---------|----------|
| $0$ | $1.31$ | $1.14$ | inside $\|z\|<10$ |
| $\pm1$ | $40.79$ | $6.39$ | inside $\|z\|<10$ |
| $\pm2$ | $159.22$ | $12.62$ | outside $\|z\|<12$ |

So exactly three poles $z_{-1},z_0,z_1$ lie inside the disk $|z|<10$, hence insi
de $\beta$ with winding number $N$ each. No poles lie in the annulus $10<|z|<12$
 itself, and all poles with $|k|\ge 2$ satisfy $|z_k|>12$.

### Step 3. Compute the residues

At a simple pole $z_k$ (simple zero of $e^z-\pi$):
$$\operatorname{Res}(f,z_k)=\frac{e^{z_k}}{(e^z-\pi)'\big|_{z=z_k}}=\frac{e^{z_k
}}{e^{z_k}}=\frac{\pi}{\pi}=1.$$

### Step 4. Apply the residue theorem (with winding numbers)

$$\int_\beta f(z)\,dz=2\pi i\sum_{k}n(\beta,z_k)\operatorname{Res}(f,z_k)=2\pi i
\cdot N\cdot(1+1+1)=2\pi i\cdot 3N.$$

### Result

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_\beta \frac{e^z}{e^z-\pi}\,dz \;=\; 6\pi i\,N.}$$
