## Proof

We solve the Helmholtz equation

$$\nabla^2 \psi + k^2 \psi = 0$$

on the rectangle $S = [0,l_x]\times[0,l_y]$ with periodic boundary conditions. T
he periodic conditions require that the field and its normal derivative match on
 opposite faces:

$$\psi(0,y) = \psi(l_x,y),\quad \partial_x\psi(0,y)=\partial_x\psi(l_x,y),$$
$$\psi(x,0) = \psi(x,l_y),\quad \partial_y\psi(x,0)=\partial_y\psi(x,l_y).$$

(The statement's pairing $\psi|_{S_2}=\psi|_{S_4}$, $\psi|_{S_1}=\psi|_{S_3}$ is
 interpreted as the standard periodic identification of opposite faces $S_1\left
rightarrow S_2$ and $S_3\leftrightarrow S_4$, which is the only geometrically co
nsistent periodic structure on a rectangle.)

### Step 1 — Separation of variables

Set $\psi(x,y) = X(x)\,Y(y)$. Substituting:

$$X''Y + XY'' + k^2 XY = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{X''}{X} + \frac{Y''}{Y} = -
k^2.$$

Introduce separation constants $k_x, k_y$ with

$$\frac{X''}{X} = -k_x^2,\qquad \frac{Y''}{Y} = -k_y^2,\qquad k_x^2 + k_y^2 = k^
2.$$

The general solutions are

$$X(x) = A\,e^{i k_x x} + B\,e^{-i k_x x},\qquad Y(y) = C\,e^{i k_y y} + D\,e^{-
i k_y y}.$$

### Step 2 — Apply periodic boundary conditions in $x$

Periodicity $X(0)=X(l_x)$ and $X'(0)=X'(l_x)$ forces the basis functions $e^{\pm
 i k_x x}$ to be single-valued under $x\mapsto x+l_x$:

$$e^{i k_x l_x} = 1 \;\Longrightarrow\; k_x l_x = 2\pi n,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.$$

Hence

$$k_x = \frac{2\pi n}{l_x},\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}.$$

### Step 3 — Apply periodic boundary conditions in $y$

Identically,

$$e^{i k_y l_y} = 1 \;\Longrightarrow\; k_y = \frac{2\pi m}{l_y},\qquad m\in\mat
hbb{Z}.$$

### Step 4 — Eigensolutions and allowable wavevectors

For each pair of integers $(n,m)\in\mathbb{Z}^2$, the corresponding separated so
lutions are plane waves

$$\psi_{n,m}^{(\pm,\pm)}(x,y) = \exp\!\Bigl[\pm i\frac{2\pi n}{l_x}x \pm i\frac{
2\pi m}{l_y}y\Bigr].$$

Equivalently, the complete set of eigensolutions can be written as

$$\boxed{\;\psi_{n,m}(x,y) = \exp\!\bigl(i\,\mathbf{k}_{n,m}\cdot\mathbf{r}\bigr
),\qquad \mathbf{k}_{n,m} = \Bigl(\frac{2\pi n}{l_x},\,\frac{2\pi m}{l_y}\Bigr),
\quad n,m\in\mathbb{Z}\;}$$

with eigenvalues

$$\boxed{\;k^2 = k_{n,m}^2 = \Bigl(\frac{2\pi n}{l_x}\Bigr)^2 + \Bigl(\frac{2\pi
 m}{l_y}\Bigr)^2.\;}$$

The general (real) eigensolution is an arbitrary linear combination / superposit
ion over the allowed integer pairs:

$$\psi(x,y) = \sum_{n,m\in\mathbb{Z}} c_{n,m}\,\exp\!\bigl(i\,\mathbf{k}_{n,m}\c
dot\mathbf{r}\bigr),$$

with the reality condition $c_{-n,-m} = c_{n,m}^*$ when a real-valued field is r
equired.

### Step 5 — Verification

Each $\psi_{n,m}$ satisfies $\nabla^2\psi_{n,m} = -|\mathbf{k}_{n,m}|^2\psi_{n,m
} = -k_{n,m}^2\psi_{n,m}$, so the Helmholtz equation holds with $k^2=k_{n,m}^2$.
 Periodicity holds because

$$\psi_{n,m}(x+l_x,y) = e^{i 2\pi n}\psi_{n,m}(x,y)=\psi_{n,m}(x,y),\qquad \psi_
{n,m}(x,y+l_y) = e^{i 2\pi m}\psi_{n,m}(x,y)=\psi_{n,m}(x,y),$$

and the normal derivatives inherit the same periodicity. Completeness of the Fou
rier basis on the torus $\mathbb{T}^2 = [0,l_x]\times[0,l_y]$ with identified op
posite faces guarantees that no eigensolutions are missed. $\blacksquare$
