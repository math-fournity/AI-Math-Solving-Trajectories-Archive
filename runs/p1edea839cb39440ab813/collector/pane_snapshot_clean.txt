# Solution

**Answer: No.** Simplicity of $V$ does not imply $L \cong \mathcal{O}_C$. We giv
e an explicit counterexample on an elliptic curve.

## Counterexample

Let $C = E$ be an elliptic curve over an algebraically closed field $k$. Fix an
integer $r \geq 2$, and let $V$ be a **stable** vector bundle of rank $r$ and de
gree $1$ on $E$. Such bundles exist by Atiyah's classification of vector bundles
 on elliptic curves, since $\gcd(r, 1) = 1$.

**$V$ is simple.** A stable bundle has only scalar endomorphisms: any nonzero en
domorphism is an isomorphism (by stability), and since $k$ is algebraically clos
ed the endomorphism algebra is $k$ itself. Hence $\operatorname{End}(V) = k$, so
 $V$ is simple.

**Key fact (Atiyah).** When $\gcd(r, d) = 1$, the determinant map
$$\det \colon M(r, d) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Pic}^d(E)$$
from the moduli space of stable bundles of rank $r$ and degree $d$ to the Picard
 variety is an **isomorphism**. In particular it is injective on isomorphism cla
sses: two stable bundles of the same rank and degree with isomorphic determinant
s are isomorphic.

**Choosing $L$.** Since $r \geq 2$, the group of $r$-torsion line bundles $\oper
atorname{Pic}^0(E)[r] \cong (\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z})^2$ has $r^2 - 1 \geq 3$ **n
on-trivial** elements. Pick any non-trivial $L \in \operatorname{Pic}^0(E)[r]$,
so
$$L^r \cong \mathcal{O}_E, \qquad L \not\cong \mathcal{O}_E.$$

**Verifying $V \cong V \otimes L$.** The bundle $V \otimes L$ has:
- **Rank** $r$ (unchanged by tensoring with a line bundle).
- **Degree** $\deg(V \otimes L) = \deg(V) + r \cdot \deg(L) = 1 + r \cdot 0 = 1$
 (since $\deg(L) = 0$).
- **Stability** preserved (tensoring by a degree-$0$ line bundle preserves stabi
lity).

Its determinant is:
$$\det(V \otimes L) \cong \det(V) \otimes L^r \cong \det(V) \otimes \mathcal{O}_
E \cong \det(V).$$

Both $V$ and $V \otimes L$ are stable of rank $r$ and degree $1$ with the **same
 determinant**. By injectivity of the determinant map (Atiyah's theorem), they a
re isomorphic:
$$V \otimes L \cong V.$$

**Conclusion.** We have produced a simple vector bundle $V$ on the algebraic cur
ve $E$ and a **non-trivial** line bundle $L \not\cong \mathcal{O}_E$ such that $
V \cong V \otimes L$. Therefore, the simplicity of $V$ does **not** force $L \co
ng \mathcal{O}_C$.

**Remark.** What simplicity *does* constrain is weaker: from $V \cong V \otimes
L$ one deduces (via determinants) that $L^{\operatorname{rank}(V)} \cong \mathca
l{O}_C$, and (via degrees) that $\deg(L) = 0$. So $L$ must be a torsion line bun
dle of order dividing $\operatorname{rank}(V)$, but it need not be trivial — as
the elliptic curve example shows.
