## Solution

Let $N = 10^{10}$. We compute
$$\Sigma := \sum_{\substack{k \geq 1 \\ k \text{ square-free}}} \left\lfloor \sq
rt{\tfrac{N}{k}} \right\rfloor.$$

**Step 1: Rewrite the floor as a counting sum.**

For a positive integer $k$,
$$\left\lfloor \sqrt{\tfrac{N}{k}} \right\rfloor = \sum_{m \geq 1} \mathbf{1}\!\
left[m \le \sqrt{\tfrac{N}{k}}\right] = \sum_{m \geq 1} \mathbf{1}\!\left[k \le
\tfrac{N}{m^{2}}\right].$$
(When $k > N$ the floor is $0$, so only $k \le N$ contribute.)

**Step 2: Swap the order of summation.**

$$\Sigma = \sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor} \;\sum_{\substack{k \text{ sq-fre
e} \\ k \le \lfloor N/m^{2}\rfloor}} 1 = \sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor} Q\!
\left(\left\lfloor \tfrac{N}{m^{2}}\right\rfloor\right),$$
where $Q(x) = \#\{k \le x : k \text{ square-free}\}$.

**Step 3: Use the standard identity for $Q$.**

Using $\mu^{2}(k) = \sum_{d^{2}\mid k}\mu(d)$,
$$Q(n) = \sum_{k \le n}\mu^{2}(k) = \sum_{d=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor}\mu(d)\le
ft\lfloor \tfrac{n}{d^{2}}\right\rfloor.$$

**Step 4: Substitute and swap again.**

$$\Sigma = \sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\;\sum_{d=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{\lfloor
 N/m^{2}\rfloor}\rfloor}\mu(d)\left\lfloor \frac{\lfloor N/m^{2}\rfloor}{d^{2}}\
right\rfloor.$$

The condition $d \le \sqrt{\lfloor N/m^{2}\rfloor}$ is equivalent to $d^{2} \le
\lfloor N/m^{2}\rfloor$, i.e. $(md)^{2} \le N$. Also $\lfloor\lfloor N/m^{2}\rfl
oor/d^{2}\rfloor = \lfloor N/(md)^{2}\rfloor$. Setting $t = md$ and grouping fac
torizations $t = md$ (i.e. $d \mid t$):

$$\Sigma = \sum_{t=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\left\lfloor \tfrac{N}{t^{2}}\righ
t\rfloor \sum_{d\mid t}\mu(d).$$

**Step 5: Apply the Möbius identity.**

The classical identity $\sum_{d\mid t}\mu(d) = \mathbf{1}[t=1]$ kills every term
 except $t=1$:

$$\Sigma = \left\lfloor \tfrac{N}{1}\right\rfloor \cdot 1 = N = 10^{10}.$$

**Verification (small cases).**
- $N=4$: square-free $k \le 4$ are $1,2,3$; values $2,1,1$; sum $= 4$. ✓
- $N=9$: square-free $k \le 9$ are $1,2,3,5,6,7$; values $3,2,1,1,1,1$; sum $= 9
$. ✓

Both match $N$, confirming the identity.
