- If A wears red (liar): A's statement is false, so B is blue.

In both cases, A and B have **different** hat colors. The same conclusion follow

> **Whenever two dwarfs meet, they must have different hat colors.**

Every pair of the 1000 dwarfs meets exactly once, giving $\binom{1000}{2}$ meeti
ngs. We choose the order of meetings and the color schedule (each dwarf may chan
ge colors, costing 1 per change) to minimize total changes.

**Step 2: Lower bound.**

Call a dwarf *constant* if they never change color (same color at every meeting)

- At most **one** always-red dwarf: if v, w are both always-red, then at meeting
 (v,w), v is red so w must be blue — contradiction.
- At most **one** always-blue dwarf: symmetric argument.

So at most **2** dwarfs are constant. The remaining **≥ 998** dwarfs each change

$$\text{total changes} \geq 998.$$

**Schedule** (process meetings in this order):

1. **Phase 0** (before any switch): All meetings $(1, i)$ for $i = 2, \ldots, 99
9$, and $(1, 1000)$.
