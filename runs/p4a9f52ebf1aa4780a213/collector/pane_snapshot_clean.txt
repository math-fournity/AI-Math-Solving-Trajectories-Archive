**Answer:** Yes, $\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{W}(\nu_k,\nu_\infty)=0$, provided $\
nu_\infty$ belongs to the Wasserstein space $\mathcal{P}_1(X)$ (finite first mom
ent). The convergence holds with an explicit quantitative bound.

---

**Proof.**

Let $B=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\beta_i$, $B_k=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\beta_i$, and fix a base
point $x_0\in X$. Set $M_i=\int_X d(x,x_0)\,d\mu_i(x)<\infty$ (finite since $\mu
_i\in\mathcal{P}_1(X)$).

**Well-posedness.** The measure $\nu_\infty=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\beta_
i\mu_i$ is a well-defined probability measure (countable convex combination with
 summable weights). For $\mathcal{W}(\nu_k,\nu_\infty)$ to be finite we require
$\nu_\infty\in\mathcal{P}_1(X)$, equivalently:
$$S:=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\beta_i M_i = B\int_X d(x,x_0)\,d\nu_\infty(x)<\infty.$$
We assume this holds. Define $S_k=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\beta_i M_i$ and $T_k=S-S_k=\sum
_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\beta_i M_i$.

**Decomposition.** Write
$$\nu_\infty=\frac{B_k}{B}\,\nu_k+\frac{B-B_k}{B}\,\tau_k,\qquad \tau_k:=\frac{1
}{B-B_k}\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\beta_i\,\mu_i.$$
Here $\tau_k$ is a well-defined probability measure in $\mathcal{P}_1(X)$ with $
M_1(\tau_k)=T_k/(B-B_k)<\infty$.

**Coupling argument.** Construct a coupling $\pi_k$ of $(\nu_k,\nu_\infty)$: let
 $Z\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(B_k/B)$.
- If $Z=1$: draw $X\sim\nu_k$, output $(X,X)$ (zero cost).
- If $Z=0$: draw $X\sim\nu_k$, $Y\sim\tau_k$ under an optimal coupling, output $
(X,Y)$.

The first marginal is $\nu_k$; the second is $\frac{B_k}{B}\nu_k+\frac{B-B_k}{B}
\tau_k=\nu_\infty$. The transport cost satisfies:
$$\mathcal{W}(\nu_k,\nu_\infty)\le\frac{B-B_k}{B}\,\mathcal{W}(\nu_k,\tau_k).$$

**Bounding $\mathcal{W}(\nu_k,\tau_k)$.** By the triangle inequality through $\d
elta_{x_0}$:
$$\mathcal{W}(\nu_k,\tau_k)\le\mathcal{W}(\nu_k,\delta_{x_0})+\mathcal{W}(\delta
_{x_0},\tau_k)=M_1(\nu_k)+M_1(\tau_k),$$
where $M_1(\mu)=\int d(x,x_0)\,d\mu$. Computing:
$$M_1(\nu_k)=\frac{1}{B_k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\beta_i M_i=\frac{S_k}{B_k},\qquad M_1(\
tau_k)=\frac{1}{B-B_k}\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\beta_i M_i=\frac{T_k}{B-B_k}.$$

**Quantitative bound.** Combining:
$$\boxed{\mathcal{W}(\nu_k,\nu_\infty)\;\le\;\frac{(B-B_k)\,S_k}{B\cdot B_k}\;+\
;\frac{T_k}{B}.}$$

**Limit as $k\to\infty$.** Since $\sum\beta_i=B<\infty$: $B_k\to B$ and $B-B_k\t
o 0$. Since $S=\sum\beta_i M_i<\infty$: $S_k\to S$ and $T_k\to 0$.

- **First term:** $\frac{(B-B_k)S_k}{B\cdot B_k}\le\frac{(B-B_k)\,S}{B\cdot B_k}
\to 0$, since $B-B_k\to 0$, $B_k\to B>0$, and $S<\infty$.
- **Second term:** $\frac{T_k}{B}\to 0$ since $T_k\to 0$.

Therefore $\mathcal{W}(\nu_k,\nu_\infty)\to 0$ as $k\to\infty$. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark (necessity of the finite-moment condition).** The hypothesis $S=\sum\be
ta_i M_i<\infty$ (i.e., $\nu_\infty\in\mathcal{P}_1(X)$) is essential and is **n
ot** automatic from $\sum\beta_i<\infty$ and $\mu_i\in\mathcal{P}_1(X)$. Without
 it, $\nu_\infty$ has infinite first moment and $\mathcal{W}(\nu_k,\nu_\infty)=\
infty$ for every $k$.

*Counterexample:* On $X=\mathbb{R}$, take $\mu_i=\delta_{2^i}$ and $\beta_i=2^{-
i}$. Then each $M_i=2^i<\infty$ and $\sum\beta_i=1<\infty$, but $\sum\beta_i M_i
=\sum 2^{-i}\cdot 2^i=\sum 1=\infty$, so $\nu_\infty\notin\mathcal{P}_1(\mathbb{
R})$ and $\mathcal{W}(\nu_k,\nu_\infty)=\infty$ for all $k$.

**Remark (generalization to $\mathcal{W}_p$).** For the $p$-Wasserstein distance
 ($p\ge 1$), the same coupling yields
$$\mathcal{W}_p^p(\nu_k,\nu_\infty)\le\frac{2^{p-1}}{B}\!\left(\frac{(B-B_k)\,S_
k^{(p)}}{B_k}+T_k^{(p)}\right),$$
where $S_k^{(p)}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\beta_i\!\int d(x,x_0)^p\,d\mu_i$ and $T_k^{(p)}=
\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\beta_i\!\int d(x,x_0)^p\,d\mu_i$, provided $S^{(p)}:=\sum_
{i=1}^{\infty}\beta_i\!\int d(x,x_0)^p\,d\mu_i<\infty$. Both terms vanish as $k\
to\infty$ by the same argument.
