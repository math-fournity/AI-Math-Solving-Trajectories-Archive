## Proof

**Notation and setup.** Identify $\{1,\ldots,p-1\}$ with the multiplicative grou
p $\mathbb{F}_p^{*}$, which is cyclic of order $p-1$. For $a\in\mathbb{F}_p^{*}$
 and $S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_p^{*}$, we have $S_a = aS = \{as \bmod p : s\in S\}$
(note $a,s\neq 0$ so $as\not\equiv 0$). Thus the sequence $S_1,S_2,\ldots,S_{p-1
}$ is precisely the orbit of $S$ under the natural action of $\mathbb{F}_p^{*}$
on $2^{\mathbb{F}_p^{*}}$ by left multiplication.

**Orbit–stabilizer.** Let
$$H_S := \{a\in\mathbb{F}_p^{*} : aS = S\}$$
be the stabilizer of $S$. It is a subgroup of $\mathbb{F}_p^{*}$, and the number
 of distinct sets among $S_1,\ldots,S_{p-1}$ equals
$$|\mathrm{Orb}(S)| = \frac{|\mathbb{F}_p^{*}|}{|H_S|} = \frac{p-1}{|H_S|}.$$
Since $\mathbb{F}_p^{*}$ is cyclic, it has a **unique** subgroup of each order d
ividing $p-1$.

---

### Part (b): all possible values of $k$

**Necessity.** The number of distinct sets is $(p-1)/|H_S|$ where $|H_S|\mid(p-1
)$, so $k\mid(p-1)$.

**Sufficiency.** Let $k$ be any positive divisor of $p-1$. Since $\mathbb{F}_p^{
*}$ is cyclic, it has a unique subgroup $H$ of order $(p-1)/k$. Take $S=H$. Then
$$H_S = \{a\in\mathbb{F}_p^{*}: aH=H\} = H,$$
because $aH=H\iff a\in H$ (as $H$ is a subgroup). Hence the orbit size is
$$\frac{p-1}{|H|} = \frac{p-1}{(p-1)/k}=k.$$
So every positive divisor of $p-1$ is attained.

> **Answer to (b):** the possible values of $k$ are exactly the **positive divis
ors of $p-1$**.

---

### Part (a): sets giving exactly two distinct elements

Exactly two distinct sets means orbit size $2$, i.e.
$$|H_S| = \frac{p-1}{2}.$$
Since $\mathbb{F}_p^{*}$ is cyclic, the unique subgroup of order $(p-1)/2$ is th
e group of quadratic residues $QR$. Thus $H_S=QR$.

The condition $H_S=QR$ means:
- $S$ is invariant under $QR$ (so $QR\subseteq H_S$), and
- $S$ is **not** invariant under all of $\mathbb{F}_p^{*}$ (otherwise $H_S=\math
bb{F}_p^{*}$, giving orbit size $1$).

Invariance under $QR$ means $S$ is a union of $QR$-cosets. There are two cosets:
 $QR$ and $QNR$ (the quadratic non-residues). The $QR$-invariant subsets are
$$\emptyset,\quad QR,\quad QNR,\quad QR\cup QNR=\mathbb{F}_p^{*}.$$
Among these, $\emptyset$ and $\mathbb{F}_p^{*}$ are fixed by every element of $\
mathbb{F}_p^{*}$ (orbit size $1$), so they are excluded. The remaining two, $QR$
 and $QNR$, have stabilizer exactly $QR$:

- $S=QR$: $a\cdot QR=QR\iff a\in QR$ (since $QR$ is a subgroup), so $H_S=QR$. ✓
- $S=QNR$: $a\cdot QNR=QNR\iff a\in QR$ (since $QR\cdot QNR=QNR$ and $QNR\cdot Q
NR=QR$), so $H_S=QR$. ✓

Both have orbit $\{QR,\,QNR\}$, of size $2$.

> **Answer to (a):** there are exactly $\boxed{2}$ such sets, namely the set of
quadratic residues and the set of quadratic non-residues modulo $p$.

---

**Summary.**
- (a) $2$.
- (b) All positive divisors of $p-1$.
