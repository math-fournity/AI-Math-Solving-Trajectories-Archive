## Proof

$$yz \text{ is perfect} \iff D(y)D(z) = 2\,s(y)\,s(z)$$

holds. We establish this by showing both sides reduce to the single condition $\
sigma(y)\sigma(z) = 2yz$.

### Step 1. Algebraic identity relating $D(y)D(z)$ and $2\,s(y)\,s(z)$

$$D(x) = 2x - \sigma(x), \qquad s(x) = \sigma(x) - x.$$

$$D(y)D(z) = (2y - \sigma(y))(2z - \sigma(z)) = 4yz - 2y\,\sigma(z) - 2z\,\sigma

$$2\,s(y)\,s(z) = 2(\sigma(y) - y)(\sigma(z) - z) = 2\sigma(y)\sigma(z) - 2z\,\s

Therefore:

$$\boxed{D(y)D(z) = 2\,s(y)\,s(z) \iff \sigma(y)\sigma(z) = 2yz.} \tag{$\star$}$

This is a **pure algebraic identity**, valid for *all* positive integers $y, z$

### Step 2. Perfection of $yz$ under coprimality

Since $\gcd(y, z) = 1$, the sum-of-divisors function $\sigma$ is multiplicative:

$$\sigma(yz) = \sigma(y)\,\sigma(z).$$

By definition, $yz$ is perfect if and only if $\sigma(yz) = 2yz$. Substituting:

$$yz \text{ is perfect} \iff \sigma(y)\sigma(z) = 2yz. \tag{$\star\star$}$$

### Step 3. Combining $(\star)$ and $(\star\star)$

Both conditions reduce to the identical equation $\sigma(y)\sigma(z) = 2yz$:

$$yz \text{ is perfect} \iff \sigma(y)\sigma(z) = 2yz \iff D(y)D(z) = 2\,s(y)\,s
(z).$$

Hence:

$$yz \text{ is perfect} \iff D(y)D(z) = 2\,s(y)\,s(z).$$

### Step 4. Application to odd perfect numbers $q^k n^2$

An odd perfect number (should one exist) has the Eulerian form $N = q^k n^2$, wh

**Yes, the statement is always true** for odd perfect numbers $q^k n^2$ (and ind
