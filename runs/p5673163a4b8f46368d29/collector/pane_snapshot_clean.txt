## Solution

**Setup.** Let the right triangle have legs $a > b$ and hypotenuse $c$. Rotating
 around the larger leg $a$ produces a cone with height $a$ and base radius $b$:

$$V = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi b^2 a = 100\pi \implies b^2 a = 300.$$

**Using the diameter condition.** For a right triangle:
- Inscribed circle radius: $r = \frac{a+b-c}{2}$
- Circumscribed circle radius: $R = \frac{c}{2}$ (hypotenuse is the diameter)

The sum of diameters is $2r + 2R = 17$, so:

$$r + R = \frac{a+b-c}{2} + \frac{c}{2} = \frac{a+b}{2} = \frac{17}{2} \implies
a + b = 17.$$

**Solving the system.** From $b = 17 - a$ and $b^2 a = 300$:

$$(17-a)^2 a = 300 \implies a^3 - 34a^2 + 289a - 300 = 0.$$

Testing $a = 12$: $1728 - 4896 + 3468 - 300 = 0$. ✓

So $a = 12$, $b = 5$, $c = 13$ — the classic **5-12-13** right triangle.

**Finding the incenter.** Place the right-angle vertex at the origin:
$$A = (0,0),\quad B = (12,0),\quad C = (0,5).$$

The inradius is $r = \frac{12+5-13}{2} = 2$. The incenter (intersection of angle
