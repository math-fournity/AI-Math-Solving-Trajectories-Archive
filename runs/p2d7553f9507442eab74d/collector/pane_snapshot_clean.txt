## General Solution of Laplace's Equation in Two Dimensions

We seek all (sufficiently smooth) functions $u(x,y)$ satisfying

$$\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2} = 0. \ta
g{1}$$

---

### Step 1. Separation of variables (Cartesian)

Look for product solutions $u(x,y) = X(x)\,Y(y)$. Substituting into (1) and divi
ding by $XY$:

$$\frac{X''(x)}{X(x)} + \frac{Y''(y)}{Y(y)} = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{
X''}{X} = -\frac{Y''}{Y} = \lambda,$$

where $\lambda$ is a separation constant. Three cases arise:

- **$\lambda = k^2 > 0$:** $X = A\cosh kx + B\sinh kx$, $\;Y = C\cos ky + D\sin
ky$.
- **$\lambda = -k^2 < 0$:** $X = A\cos kx + B\sin kx$, $\;Y = C\cosh ky + D\sinh
 ky$.
- **$\lambda = 0$:** $X = A + Bx$, $\;Y = C + Dy$, giving $u = (A+Bx)(C+Dy)$.

Each product solution is harmonic. By linearity, any (convergent) superposition
of such separated solutions is again harmonic. This yields the **Fourier-type in
tegral representation**

$$\boxed{u(x,y) = \int_{0}^{\infty}\!\Big[\big(A(k)\cos kx + B(k)\sin kx\big)\co
sh ky + \big(C(k)\cos kx + D(k)\sin kx\big)\sinh ky\Big]\,dk,} \tag{2}$$

with the analogous form obtained by swapping the roles of $x$ and $y$. The four
arbitrary functions $A,B,C,D$ (subject to convergence conditions) encode the fre
edom of prescribing, e.g., Cauchy data on a horizontal line.

---

### Step 2. Polar-coordinate form

Writing $x = r\cos\theta$, $y = r\sin\theta$, equation (1) becomes

$$u_{rr} + \frac{1}{r}\,u_r + \frac{1}{r^2}\,u_{\theta\theta} = 0. \tag{3}$$

Separating $u = R(r)\,\Theta(\theta)$ gives $\Theta'' + n^2\Theta = 0$ and the E
uler equation $r^2 R'' + r R' - n^2 R = 0$, whose solutions are $r^{\pm n}$ (for
 $n\neq 0$) and $1,\ln r$ (for $n=0$). Periodicity $\Theta(\theta+2\pi)=\Theta(\
theta)$ forces $n\in\mathbb{Z}$. Superposing:

$$\boxed{u(r,\theta) = a_0 + b_0\ln r + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big[\big(a_n r^n + b
_n r^{-n}\big)\cos n\theta + \big(c_n r^n + d_n r^{-n}\big)\sin n\theta\Big].} \
tag{4}$$

This is the **general harmonic expansion in polar coordinates**. The coefficient
s $a_n,b_n,c_n,d_n$ are arbitrary (subject to convergence and to the domain excl
uding/including the origin as appropriate).

---

### Step 3. Complex-analytic characterization

Let $z = x+iy$. A fundamental fact: **$u$ is harmonic on a simply connected doma
in $\Omega$ if and only if $u = \operatorname{Re} F(z)$ for some holomorphic fun
ction $F$ on $\Omega$.**

*Proof of the "if" direction:* If $F = u + iv$ is holomorphic, the Cauchy–Rieman
n equations $u_x = v_y$, $u_y = -v_x$ hold, and differentiating gives $u_{xx} =
v_{yx}$, $u_{yy} = -v_{xy}$. Since $v$ is $C^2$ (in fact $C^\infty$), $v_{yx}=v_
{xy}$, so $u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0$.

*Proof of the "only if" direction:* Given harmonic $u$, define $v(x,y) = \int_{(
x_0,y_0)}^{(x,y)} (-u_y\,dx + u_x\,dy)$. The 1-form $-u_y\,dx + u_x\,dy$ is clos
ed because $(-u_y)_y = (u_x)_x$, i.e. $-u_{yy} = u_{xx}$, which is exactly $u_{x
x}+u_{yy}=0$. Hence (on a simply connected domain) $v$ is well defined up to a c
onstant, and the Cauchy–Riemann equations are satisfied by construction, so $F =
 u+iv$ is holomorphic. ∎

Therefore the **most compact general form** is

$$\boxed{u(x,y) = \operatorname{Re}\,F(x+iy), \qquad F \text{ holomorphic},} \ta
g{5}$$

equivalently $u = \operatorname{Re} F = \tfrac12(F + \overline{F})$, and every h
armonic function is locally of this form.

---

### Step 4. Mean-value / Poisson representation

A harmonic function satisfies the **mean-value property**

$$u(x_0,y_0) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi} u(x_0 + R\cos\theta,\, y_0 + R\sin\
theta)\,d\theta, \tag{6}$$

and conversely any continuous function satisfying (6) is harmonic. On the unit d
isk this yields the **Poisson integral formula**

$$u(r,\theta) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi} \frac{1-r^2}{1-2r\cos(\theta-\varp
hi)+r^2}\,f(\varphi)\,d\varphi, \tag{7}$$

which gives the unique harmonic function with boundary values $f$ on $|z|=1$ — i
.e. it realizes the coefficients in (4) from prescribed Dirichlet data.

---

### Summary

The general solution of the two-dimensional Laplace equation $u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0$ i
s the class of all **harmonic functions**, which can be written equivalently as:

1. **Cartesian separated / Fourier form** (2) — superposition of $\{\cos kx,\sin
 kx\}\otimes\{\cosh ky,\sinh ky\}$ and the swapped version, with arbitrary spect
ral functions;
2. **Polar harmonic expansion** (4) — a Fourier series in $\theta$ with radial p
owers $r^{\pm n}$ (and a $\ln r$ mode), with arbitrary coefficients;
3. **Complex form** (5) — the real part of an arbitrary holomorphic function $F(
z)$, $z=x+iy$ (the most concise characterization on simply connected domains);
4. **Poisson integral** (7) — reconstruction from arbitrary boundary data.

All four representations describe the **same** class of functions: the real part
s of holomorphic functions, i.e. the harmonic functions on $\mathbb{R}^2$.
