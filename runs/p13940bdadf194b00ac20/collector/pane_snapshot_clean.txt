## Proof

Let $k = \mathbb{C}(t)$ and $f(x) = x^3 + x + t \in k[x]$.

**Step 1: $f$ is irreducible over $k$.**

By Gauss's Lemma, irreducibility over $k = \mathbb{C}(t)$ is equivalent to irred
ucibility over $\mathbb{C}[t]$ (as a primitive polynomial in $x$). Since $\deg_x
 f = 3$, reducibility would require a root in $\mathbb{C}(t)$. Suppose $r(t)/s(t
) \in \mathbb{C}(t)$ (with $\gcd(r,s)=1$, $s$ monic) is a root. Substituting and
 clearing denominators gives
$$r(t)^3 + r(t)\,s(t)^2 + t\,s(t)^3 = 0.$$
Thus $s^3 \mid r^3$, forcing $s$ to be a unit, so WLOG $s = 1$ and $r \in \mathb
b{C}[t]$ satisfies $r^3 + r + t = 0$, i.e. $t = -r^3 - r$. If $r$ is nonconstant
, $\deg(-r^3 - r) = 3\deg r \geq 3 \neq 1 = \deg t$; if $r$ is constant, $t$ is
constant. Both are contradictions. Hence $f$ has no root in $k$ and is irreducib
le.

**Step 2: Discriminant.**

For a depressed cubic $x^3 + px + q$, the discriminant is $\Delta = -4p^3 - 27q^
2$. Here $p = 1$, $q = t$, so
$$\Delta = -4 - 27t^2.$$

**Step 3: $\Delta$ is not a square in $k$.**

Suppose $-4 - 27t^2 = g(t)^2$ for some $g \in \mathbb{C}(t)$. Over $\mathbb{C}$,
 the polynomial $-27t^2 - 4$ has two distinct simple roots $t = \pm\frac{2i}{3\s
qrt{3}}$. But the square of any rational function has all its zeros (and poles)
of **even** order. Since $-27t^2-4$ has two simple zeros, it cannot be a square
in $\mathbb{C}(t)$. Contradiction.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

For an irreducible cubic over a field of characteristic $\neq 2$, the Galois gro
up (of its splitting field over the base field) is a transitive subgroup of $S_3
$, hence either $A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ or $S_3$. The Galois group is
 contained in $A_3$ if and only if the discriminant is a square in the base fiel
d. Since $\Delta = -4 - 27t^2$ is **not** a square in $k = \mathbb{C}(t)$, the G
alois group is not contained in $A_3$.

Therefore the Galois group of $f(x) = x^3 + x + t$ over $\mathbb{C}(t)$ is

$$\boxed{S_3}.$$
