Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether "Either π+e is rational or
π−e is rational" follows from the assumption that π and e are irrational.

## Analysis

**The statement is FALSE** — it does not follow from the given assumptions. In f
act, the given assumptions support the *opposite* conclusion: that at least one
of π+e, π−e is **irrational** (not rational).

## Proof

**Claim:** Given that π and e are irrational, at least one of π+e and π−e is irr
ational. Consequently, the statement "either π+e is rational or π−e is rational"
 cannot be established from the given assumptions.

**Proof by contradiction.** Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that **both*
* π+e and π−e are rational. Then:

$$
(\pi + e) + (\pi - e) = 2\pi
$$

Since the sum of two rational numbers is rational, $2\pi$ would be rational. Div
iding by 2 (a rational, nonzero number) preserves rationality, so:

$$
\pi = \frac{2\pi}{2}
$$

would be **rational**. This directly contradicts the given fact that π is irrati
onal.

Therefore, it is **impossible** for both π+e and π−e to be rational. At least on
e of them must be **irrational**. $\square$

## Why the original statement is false

The proven result is:

> **At least one of π+e, π−e is irrational** (equivalently: NOT both are rationa
l).

The problem's statement claims:

> **At least one of π+e, π−e is rational.**

These are **not equivalent** — they are not even negations of each other:

- The negation of "at least one is rational" is "both are irrational."
- What we proved is "at least one is irrational" = "not both are rational."

The proof rules out the case where **both** are rational, but it does **not** ru
le out the case where **both** are irrational. Indeed, the scenario where both π
+e and π−e are irrational is entirely consistent with π and e being irrational (
just as √2+√3 and √2−√3 are both irrational). Since:

1. We **cannot** prove that at least one of π+e, π−e is rational from the given
assumptions, and
2. The scenario making the statement false (both irrational) is **consistent** w
ith all given assumptions,

the statement **does not follow** from the premise that π and e are irrational.

## Conclusion

The statement **"Either π+e is rational or π−e is rational"** is **FALSE** — it
cannot be concluded from the given information. What the given assumptions **do*
* establish is the opposite: that at least one of π+e, π−e must be **irrational*
*. The actual rationality of π+e or π−e remains an open problem in mathematics.
