**Answer: Yes. The statement is true.** If $f \in \mathcal{D}'(\mathbb{R}^n)$ sa
tisfies $D^\alpha f = 0$ (as a distribution) for every multi-index $\alpha$ with
 $|\alpha|=k$, then $f$ is a polynomial of degree at most $k-1$.

Below is the proof by induction on $k$, on the connected domain $\mathbb{R}^n$.

---

### Preliminary lemma (divergence decomposition)

**Lemma 1.** Fix $\rho \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ with $\int \rho = 1$. Every
 $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ admits a decomposition
$$\varphi = \Big(\int \varphi\Big)\rho + \sum_{i=1}^n \partial_i \psi_i, \qquad
\psi_i \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n).$$

*Proof.* Set $\tilde\varphi = \varphi - (\int\varphi)\rho$; then $\int \tilde\va
rphi = 0$, and we must show $\tilde\varphi = \sum_i \partial_i\psi_i$. We argue
by induction on the dimension $n$.

For $n=1$: $\tilde\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ with $\int\tilde\varphi=0$.
Then $\psi_1(x)=\int_{-\infty}^x \tilde\varphi(t)\,dt$ is compactly supported (i
t vanishes for $x\to\pm\infty$ because the total integral is $0$) and $\partial_
1\psi_1=\tilde\varphi$.

For $n>1$: pick $\eta\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ with $\int\eta=1$ and set $\chi(
x_2,\dots,x_n)=\int_{\mathbb R}\tilde\varphi(x_1,x_2,\dots,x_n)\,dx_1$, so $\chi
\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^{n-1})$ and $\int_{\mathbb R^{n-1}}\chi = \int\tilde\va
rphi = 0$. Write
$$\tilde\varphi(x) = \underbrace{\big[\tilde\varphi(x)-\chi(x')\eta(x_1)\big]}_{
=:\,g(x)} + \chi(x')\eta(x_1), \qquad x'=(x_2,\dots,x_n).$$
For each fixed $x'$, $\int_{\mathbb R} g(x_1,x')\,dx_1 = \chi(x')-\chi(x')=0$, s
o $g=\partial_1\Psi_1$ with $\Psi_1(x_1,x')=\int_{-\infty}^{x_1}g(t,x')\,dt\in C
_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$. By the $(n-1)$-dimensional induction hypothesis applied
 to $\chi$, there exist $\tilde\psi_i\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^{n-1})$ with $\chi
=\sum_{i=2}^n\partial_i\tilde\psi_i$, hence $\chi(x')\eta(x_1)=\sum_{i=2}^n\part
ial_i[\tilde\psi_i(x')\eta(x_1)]$. Combining gives the desired decomposition. $\
square$

---

### Base case $k=1$

Suppose $\partial_i f = 0$ in $\mathcal{D}'(\mathbb{R}^n)$ for all $i=1,\dots,n$
. Let $c=\langle f,\rho\rangle$. For any $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$, u
sing Lemma 1,
$$\langle f,\varphi\rangle = \Big(\int\varphi\Big)\langle f,\rho\rangle + \sum_i
\langle f,\partial_i\psi_i\rangle = c\int\varphi - \sum_i\langle \partial_i f,\p
si_i\rangle = c\int\varphi.$$
Thus $f$ acts as the constant $c$, i.e. $f\equiv c$, a polynomial of degree $0\l
e k-1$. $\checkmark$

---

### Inductive step

Assume the result for $k-1$: any distribution whose order-$(k-1)$ weak derivativ
es all vanish is a polynomial of degree $\le k-2$. Suppose $D^\alpha f=0$ for al
l $|\alpha|=k$.

For each $i$, the distribution $g_i:=\partial_i f$ satisfies $D^\beta g_i = D^{\
beta+e_i}f = 0$ for every $|\beta|=k-1$. By the induction hypothesis, each $g_i=
\partial_i f$ is a polynomial $p_i$ of degree $\le k-2$. Compatibility gives $\p
artial_i p_j = \partial_i\partial_j f = \partial_j\partial_i f = \partial_j p_i$
.

**Lemma 2 (Polynomial Poincaré lemma).** If $p_1,\dots,p_n$ are polynomials of d
egree $\le k-2$ with $\partial_i p_j=\partial_j p_i$, there exists a polynomial
$P$ of degree $\le k-1$ with $\partial_i P = p_i$.

*Proof.* Define
$$P(x)=\int_0^1\sum_{i=1}^n x_i\,p_i(tx)\,dt.$$
Since $\deg p_i\le k-2$, each summand $x_i\,p_i(tx)$ has degree $\le k-1$ in $x$
, so $P$ is a polynomial of degree $\le k-1$. Differentiating,
$$\partial_j P(x)=\int_0^1\Big[p_j(tx)+\sum_i x_i\,t\,(\partial_j p_i)(tx)\Big]d
t.$$
Using $\partial_j p_i=\partial_i p_j$, the second term becomes $\sum_i x_i\,t\,(
\partial_i p_j)(tx)=t\,\frac{d}{dt}p_j(tx)$. Hence
$$\partial_j P=\int_0^1\Big[p_j(tx)+t\,\tfrac{d}{dt}p_j(tx)\Big]dt=\int_0^1\frac
{d}{dt}\big[t\,p_j(tx)\big]dt=\big[t\,p_j(tx)\big]_0^1=p_j(x).\quad\square$$

Applying Lemma 2, obtain a polynomial $P$ of degree $\le k-1$ with $\partial_i P
=p_i=\partial_i f$. Then $\partial_i(f-P)=0$ for all $i$, so by the base case $f
-P\equiv c$ for some constant $c$. Therefore
$$f = P + c,$$
a polynomial of degree $\le k-1$. $\checkmark$

---

### Conclusion

By induction on $k$, every distribution $f\in\mathcal{D}'(\mathbb{R}^n)$ whose w
eak derivatives of order $k$ all vanish is a polynomial of degree at most $k-1$.
 The statement is **true**. (The hypothesis that the domain is all of $\mathbb{R
}^n$ — connected and without boundary — is essential for the base case, which fo
rces a *single* global constant.)
