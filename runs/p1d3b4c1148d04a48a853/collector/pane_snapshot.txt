Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether there exists an elliptic cu
rve $E/\mathbb{Q}(T)$ such that every morphism $X_1(N) \to E$ (over $\mathbb{Q}(
T)$, for any $N > 0$) is constant.

**Answer: Yes, such an elliptic curve exists.** Any non-isotrivial elliptic curv
e over $\mathbb{Q}(T)$ has this property.

---

## Proof

**Construction.** Take $E: y^2 = x^3 + x + T$ over $\mathbb{Q}(T)$. Its discrimi
nant is $\Delta = -16(4 + 27T^2) \neq 0$ in $\mathbb{Q}(T)$, so this defines an
elliptic curve. Its $j$-invariant is
$$j(E) = \frac{-1728 \cdot 4}{4 + 27T^2},$$
which is non-constant in $T$. Hence $E$ is **non-isotrivial** (not isomorphic ov
er $\overline{\mathbb{Q}(T)}$ to a constant elliptic curve $E_0 \times_{\mathbb{
Q}} \mathbb{Q}(T)$).

We claim that for every $N > 0$, every morphism $X_1(N)_{\mathbb{Q}(T)} \to E$ o
ver $\mathbb{Q}(T)$ is constant.

---

**Step 1: Reduction to homomorphisms of Jacobians.**

Suppose $f: X_1(N)_{\mathbb{Q}(T)} \to E$ is a non-constant morphism over $\math
bb{Q}(T)$. Choose a base point $P_0 \in X_1(N)(\mathbb{Q})$ (which exists since
$X_1(N)$ has rational cusps). The Abel–Jacobi map $\iota: X_1(N) \hookrightarrow
 J_1(N)$ (sending $P \mapsto [P - P_0]$) induces, via $f$, a homomorphism of abe
lian varieties:
$$\phi: J_1(N)_{\mathbb{Q}(T)} \longrightarrow E, \qquad \phi\!\left(\textstyle\
sum n_i[P_i - P_0]\right) = \sum n_i\bigl(f(P_i) - f(P_0)\bigr).$$
Since $f$ is non-constant, the image of $\phi$ contains $f(X_1(N)) - f(P_0)$, wh
ich generates $E$ (as $E$ is 1-dimensional and $f(X_1(N))$ is a curve). Thus $\p
hi$ is **surjective**, i.e., $E$ is a quotient of $J_1(N)_{\mathbb{Q}(T)}$.

---

**Step 2: $J_1(N)_{\mathbb{Q}(T)}$ is a constant abelian variety.**

By definition, $J_1(N)_{\mathbb{Q}(T)} = J_1(N) \times_{\operatorname{Spec}\math
bb{Q}} \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Q}(T)$, i.e., it is the base change of the abe
lian variety $J_1(N)/\mathbb{Q}$ to $\mathbb{Q}(T)$. This is a **constant** abel
ian variety over $\mathbb{Q}(T)$.

---

**Step 3: Quotients of constant abelian varieties over $\mathbb{Q}(T)$ are const
ant.**

We use the following standard fact from the theory of abelian varieties:

> **Lemma.** Let $k$ be a field and $K/k$ a **primary** extension (i.e., $k$ is
algebraically closed in $K$). For abelian varieties $A, B$ over $k$,
> $$\operatorname{Hom}_K(A \times_k K,\; B \times_k K) \;=\; \operatorname{Hom}_
k(A, B).$$

*Proof of Lemma.* The Hom scheme $\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(A, B)$ is an ét
ale (unramified) scheme locally of finite type over $k$. Its $k$-points are $\op
eratorname{Hom}_k(A,B)$. For an étale scheme $X/k$, the natural map $X(k) \to X(
K)$ is a bijection whenever $k$ is algebraically closed in $K$: an étale $k$-sch
eme is a disjoint union of spectra of finite separable extensions $L_i/k$, and $
L_i \otimes_k K$ is a field (giving one $K$-point) if and only if $L_i = k$, sin
ce $k$ is separably closed in $K$. $\square$

Since $\mathbb{Q}(T)/\mathbb{Q}$ is a purely transcendental extension, $\mathbb{
Q}$ is algebraically closed in $\mathbb{Q}(T)$, so $\mathbb{Q}(T)/\mathbb{Q}$ is
 primary. Applying the lemma with $A = B = J_1(N)$:
$$\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}(T)}\!\bigl(J_1(N)_{\mathbb{Q}(T)}\bigr) = \oper
atorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}}\!\bigl(J_1(N)\bigr).$$

Now, by the Poincaré complete reducibility theorem, abelian subvarieties of an a
belian variety $A$ correspond to idempotents in $\operatorname{End}(A) \otimes \
mathbb{Q}$. Since the endomorphism algebra is unchanged by the primary extension
 $\mathbb{Q}(T)/\mathbb{Q}$, every abelian subvariety of $J_1(N)_{\mathbb{Q}(T)}
$ over $\mathbb{Q}(T)$ descends to an abelian subvariety of $J_1(N)$ over $\math
bb{Q}$.

Consequently, every quotient of $J_1(N)_{\mathbb{Q}(T)}$ over $\mathbb{Q}(T)$ is
 of the form $Q_0 \times_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}(T)$ for some quotient $Q_0$ of
$J_1(N)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$—i.e., **every quotient is constant**.

---

**Step 4: Contradiction.**

From Step 1, if a non-constant morphism $X_1(N)_{\mathbb{Q}(T)} \to E$ existed,
then $E$ would be a quotient of $J_1(N)_{\mathbb{Q}(T)}$. By Step 3, this would
force $E$ to be constant (isotrivial). But $E$ was chosen to be non-isotrivial (
Step 0). **Contradiction.**

Therefore, no non-constant morphism $X_1(N)_{\mathbb{Q}(T)} \to E$ exists for an
y $N > 0$.

---

**Remark on small $N$.** For $N = 1$ and $N = 2$, $X_1(N) \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ ha
s genus 0, so there are no non-constant morphisms to any elliptic curve regardle
ss. For $N \geq 3$, $X_1(N)$ has positive genus and the argument above applies.

---

**Conclusion.** There exists an elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}(T)$—namely, any non
-isotrivial one, such as $E: y^2 = x^3 + x + T$—such that every morphism $X_1(N)
 \to E$ over $\mathbb{Q}(T)$ is constant for all $N > 0$. The key mechanism is t
hat non-constant morphisms from modular curves to $E$ would force $E$ to be a qu
otient of a constant abelian variety (the Jacobian $J_1(N)$ base-changed to $\ma
thbb{Q}(T)$), but quotients of constant abelian varieties over the primary exten
sion $\mathbb{Q}(T)/\mathbb{Q}$ remain constant, contradicting non-isotriviality
.
