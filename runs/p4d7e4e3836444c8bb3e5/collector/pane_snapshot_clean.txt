## Proof

**Step 1: Reduce to the unit disk.**

The integrand $\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2-y^2}}$ is real-valued only on the unit disk
$D = \{(x,y): x^2+y^2 \le 1\}$. On $[-1,1]^2 \setminus D$, we have $1-x^2-y^2 <
0$, so the integrand is not real. The integral is therefore taken over $D$:

$$I = \int_D \frac{dx\,dy}{\sqrt{1-x^2-y^2}}.$$

**Step 2: Identify the integral as the surface area of the upper hemisphere.**

Let $S$ be the upper hemisphere of the unit sphere: $S = \{(x,y,z): x^2+y^2+z^2=
1,\ z \ge 0\}$, parametrized as the graph $z = \sqrt{1-x^2-y^2}$ over $D$. The s
urface area element is:

$$dS = \sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\par
tial z}{\partial y}\right)^2}\,dx\,dy = \sqrt{1 + \frac{x^2+y^2}{1-x^2-y^2}}\,dx
\,dy = \frac{dx\,dy}{\sqrt{1-x^2-y^2}}.$$

Therefore:

$$I = \int_S dS = \operatorname{Area}(S).$$

**Step 3: Apply Stokes' Theorem.**

We seek a vector field $\mathbf{F}$, smooth on a neighborhood of $S$, such that
$(\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) \cdot \mathbf{n} = \frac{1}{2}$ on $S$, where $\math
bf{n} = (x,y,z)$ is the outward unit normal to the unit sphere. Then:

$$\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Area}(S) = \int_S (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) \cdot \m
athbf{n}\,dS = \oint_{\partial S} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r},$$

and $\operatorname{Area}(S) = 2\oint_{\partial S} \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}$.

**Claim:** The vector field

$$\mathbf{F} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{-y}{1+z},\ \frac{x}{1+z},\ 0\right)$$

is smooth on the upper hemisphere (since $z \ge 0$ ensures $1+z \ge 1 > 0$) and
satisfies $(\nabla \times \mathbf{F})\cdot \mathbf{n} = \frac{1}{2}$ on the unit
 sphere.

**Verification of the curl.** Computing component-wise:

$$(\nabla \times \mathbf{F})_x = \frac{\partial F_z}{\partial y} - \frac{\partia
l F_y}{\partial z} = 0 - \frac{x}{2}\cdot\frac{-1}{(1+z)^2} = \frac{x}{2(1+z)^2}
,$$

$$(\nabla \times \mathbf{F})_y = \frac{\partial F_x}{\partial z} - \frac{\partia
l F_z}{\partial x} = \frac{-y}{2}\cdot\frac{-1}{(1+z)^2} - 0 = \frac{y}{2(1+z)^2
},$$

$$(\nabla \times \mathbf{F})_z = \frac{\partial F_y}{\partial x} - \frac{\partia
l F_x}{\partial y} = \frac{1}{2(1+z)} - \frac{-1}{2(1+z)} = \frac{1}{1+z}.$$

Dotting with $\mathbf{n} = (x,y,z)$ and using $x^2+y^2 = 1-z^2$ on the unit sphe
re:

$$(\nabla \times \mathbf{F})\cdot \mathbf{n} = \frac{x^2+y^2}{2(1+z)^2} + \frac{
z}{1+z} = \frac{1-z^2}{2(1+z)^2} + \frac{z}{1+z} = \frac{(1-z)(1+z)}{2(1+z)^2} +
 \frac{z}{1+z}$$

$$= \frac{1-z}{2(1+z)} + \frac{z}{1+z} = \frac{1-z+2z}{2(1+z)} = \frac{1+z}{2(1+
z)} = \frac{1}{2}. \quad\checkmark$$

**Step 4: Evaluate the boundary integral.**

The boundary $\partial S$ is the equator $\{(x,y,0): x^2+y^2=1\}$, oriented coun
terclockwise (consistent with the outward normal of the upper hemisphere). On th
e equator, $z=0$, so:

$$\mathbf{F}\big|_{\partial S} = \frac{1}{2}(-y,\, x,\, 0).$$

Parametrize $\partial S$ by $x = \cos\theta$, $y = \sin\theta$, $\theta \in [0,
2\pi]$:

$$\oint_{\partial S} \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{2\pi}\left
(-\sin\theta\cdot(-\sin\theta) + \cos\theta\cdot\cos\theta\right)d\theta = \frac
{1}{2}\int_0^{2\pi}(\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta)\,d\theta = \frac{1}{2}\cdot 2\p
i = \pi.$$

**Step 5: Conclude.**

By Stokes' Theorem:

$$\operatorname{Area}(S) = 2\oint_{\partial S} \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = 2\p
i.$$

Since $I = \operatorname{Area}(S)$, we obtain:

$$\boxed{\int_{[-1,1]^2} \frac{dx\,dy}{\sqrt{1-x^2-y^2}} = 2\pi.}$$
