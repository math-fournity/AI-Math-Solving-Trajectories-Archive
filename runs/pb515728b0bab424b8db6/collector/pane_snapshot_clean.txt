## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** The orthogonal projection of $w$ onto $T_pN$ equals $u$ at ever
y point $p \in N$.

---

### Setup and notation

Let $(M, g)$ be a Riemannian manifold with Levi-Civita connection $\nabla^M$, an
d let $N \subset M$ be an embedded submanifold equipped with the induced metric
$\bar{g} = g|_N$ and its own Levi-Civita connection $\nabla^N$. Let $\gamma: I \
to N$ be a smooth curve and $v$ a vector field on $N$ (so $v(p) \in T_pN$ for al
l $p \in N$). Define:

$$u = \nabla^N_{\dot\gamma} v, \qquad w = \nabla^M_{\dot\gamma} v,$$

where in the second expression, $\gamma$ and $v$ have been extended (locally) to
 $M$.

We must show that $\operatorname{proj}_{T_pN}(w) = u$ for every $p \in N$.

---

### Key tool: the Gauss formula

**Claim (Gauss formula).** For any vector fields $X, Y$ on $N$ (extended locally
 to $M$),

$$\nabla^M_X Y = \nabla^N_X Y + \mathrm{I\!I}(X, Y),$$

where $\mathrm{I\!I}(X,Y) \in (T_pN)^\perp$ is the **second fundamental form** o
f $N$ in $M$. In particular, the tangential component of $\nabla^M_X Y$ is $\nab
la^N_X Y$.

**Proof of the claim.** Both $\nabla^M$ and $\nabla^N$ are Levi-Civita connectio
ns, hence determined by the Koszul formula. For vector fields $X, Y, Z$ tangent
to $N$, the Koszul formula on $M$ gives:

$$2\,g(\nabla^M_X Y, Z) = X\bigl(g(Y,Z)\bigr) + Y\bigl(g(Z,X)\bigr) - Z\bigl(g(X
,Y)\bigr) - g(X,[Y,Z]) + g(Y,[Z,X]) + g(Z,[X,Y]). \tag{1}$$

The Koszul formula on $N$ (with the induced metric $\bar{g}$) gives:

$$2\,\bar{g}(\nabla^N_X Y, Z) = X\bigl(\bar{g}(Y,Z)\bigr) + Y\bigl(\bar{g}(Z,X)\
bigr) - Z\bigl(\bar{g}(X,Y)\bigr) - \bar{g}(X,[Y,Z]) + \bar{g}(Y,[Z,X]) + \bar{g
}(Z,[X,Y]). \tag{2}$$

We now observe that the right-hand sides of (1) and (2) are identical:

1. **Metric agreement:** Since $X, Y, Z$ are all tangent to $N$, we have $\bar{g
}(X,Y) = g(X,Y)$, and similarly for all pairs. Hence every $g(\cdot,\cdot)$ term
 in (1) equals the corresponding $\bar{g}(\cdot,\cdot)$ term in (2).

2. **Lie brackets stay tangent:** If $X, Y$ are tangent to $N$, then $[X, Y]$ is
 tangent to $N$. (Indeed, for any smooth function $f$ vanishing on $N$, both $X(
f)$ and $Y(f)$ vanish on $N$, so $[X,Y](f) = X(Yf) - Y(Xf)$ vanishes on $N$; thi
s characterizes tangency.) Thus $[X,Y], [Y,Z], [Z,X]$ are all tangent to $N$, an
d the Lie bracket terms also agree.

3. **Derivatives agree:** $X(g(Y,Z)) = X(\bar{g}(Y,Z))$ since $g(Y,Z) = \bar{g}(
Y,Z)$ along $N$ and $X$ is tangent to $N$ (so it differentiates only along $N$).

Therefore the right-hand sides of (1) and (2) coincide, yielding:

$$g(\nabla^M_X Y, Z) = \bar{g}(\nabla^N_X Y, Z) = g(\nabla^N_X Y, Z)$$

for all $Z$ tangent to $N$. Hence

$$g\bigl(\nabla^M_X Y - \nabla^N_X Y,\; Z\bigr) = 0 \quad \text{for all } Z \in
T_pN.$$

This means $\nabla^M_X Y - \nabla^N_X Y \in (T_pN)^\perp$. Defining $\mathrm{I\!
I}(X,Y) := \nabla^M_X Y - \nabla^N_X Y$, we obtain the Gauss formula, and $\math
rm{I\!I}(X,Y)$ is normal to $N$ by construction. $\square$

---

### Applying the Gauss formula to the problem

The curve $\gamma$ lies in $N$, so $\dot\gamma(t) \in T_{\gamma(t)}N$ for all $t
$. The vector field $v$ is defined on $N$, so $v(p) \in T_pN$. Both $\dot\gamma$
 and $v$ are tangent to $N$.

Locally extend $\dot\gamma$ to a vector field $X$ on $N$ (tangent to $N$) with $
X|_\gamma = \dot\gamma$, and extend $v$ to a vector field $Y$ on $N$. The covari
ant derivative along a curve depends only on the values of the vector fields alo
ng the curve, so:

$$w = \nabla^M_{\dot\gamma} v = \nabla^M_X Y\Big|_\gamma, \qquad u = \nabla^N_{\
dot\gamma} v = \nabla^N_X Y\Big|_\gamma.$$

By the Gauss formula:

$$w = \nabla^M_X Y\Big|_\gamma = \nabla^N_X Y\Big|_\gamma + \mathrm{I\!I}(X, Y)\
Big|_\gamma = u + \mathrm{I\!I}(\dot\gamma, v).$$

Since $\mathrm{I\!I}(\dot\gamma, v) \in (T_pN)^\perp$, it is orthogonal to $T_pN
$. Therefore, taking the orthogonal projection onto $T_pN$:

$$\operatorname{proj}_{T_pN}(w) = \operatorname{proj}_{T_pN}\bigl(u + \mathrm{I\
!I}(\dot\gamma, v)\bigr) = u + 0 = u.$$

---

### Conclusion

For every point $p$ on $N$, the orthogonal projection of $w = \nabla^M_{\dot\gam
ma} v$ onto $T_pN$ equals $u = \nabla^N_{\dot\gamma} v$. The difference $w - u =
 \mathrm{I\!I}(\dot\gamma, v)$ is precisely the second fundamental form, which i
s entirely normal to $N$ and thus annihilated by the tangential projection.
