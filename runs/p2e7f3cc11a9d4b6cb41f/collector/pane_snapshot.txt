## Solution

**Step 1: Reduction mod 2 and Frobenius decomposition.**

The number of odd coefficients equals the number of nonzero coefficients of $(x^
2+x+1)^n$ in $\mathbb{F}_2[x]$. Write $n = \sum a_i 2^i$ in binary. By the Frobe
nius endomorphism, $(x^2+x+1)^{2^k} = x^{2^{k+1}}+x^{2^k}+1$ in $\mathbb{F}_2[x]
$, so setting $f_k(x) = 1+x^{2^k}+x^{2^{k+1}}$:

$$(x^2+x+1)^n = \prod_{k:\, a_k=1} f_k(x) \quad \text{in } \mathbb{F}_2[x].$$

**Step 2: Runs of 1s decouple.**

Write $n$'s binary expansion as maximal runs of consecutive 1s of lengths $r_1,
r_2, \ldots, r_k$, where run $i$ occupies bit positions $\{j_i, j_i+1, \ldots, j
_i+r_i-1\}$. The factor $f_m$ has exponents using only bit positions $m$ and $m+
1$. Since runs are separated by at least one zero bit, the bit-position sets $\{
j_i, \ldots, j_i+r_i\}$ for different runs are **disjoint**. Therefore exponents
 contributed by different runs occupy disjoint bit positions, so no cancellation
 occurs between runs. The total count factors as:

$$\text{Answer} = \prod_{i=1}^{k} g(r_i),$$

where $g(r)$ is the number of nonzero coefficients of $P_r(x) := (1+x+x^2)^{2^r
- 1}$ in $\mathbb{F}_2[x]$ (a single run of length $r$ starting at bit 0).

**Step 3: Recurrence for $g(r)$.**

Let $A_r \subseteq \{0,1,\ldots,2^{r+1}-2\}$ be the support (set of exponents wi
th nonzero coefficient) of $P_r$, and set $a_r = |A_r|$, $t_r$ = number of maxim
al consecutive-integer intervals in $A_r$.

**Key recurrence.** Since $2^{r+1}-1 = 2(2^r-1)+1$:

$$P_{r+1}(x) = (P_r(x))^2 \cdot (1+x+x^2) \quad \text{in } \mathbb{F}_2[x].$$

By Frobenius, $(P_r)^2 = \sum_{m \in A_r} x^{2m}$. Multiplying by $(1+x+x^2)$:

- **Odd exponents** $2j+1 \in A_{r+1}$ iff $j \in A_r$: contributes $a_r$ odd ex
ponents.
- **Even exponents** $2j \in A_{r+1}$ iff $[j \in A_r] \neq [j-1 \in A_r]$ (a "t
ransition"): the number of transitions equals $2t_r$ (each of the $t_r$ interval
s has a left and right boundary).

Therefore: $a_{r+1} = a_r + 2t_r$.

**Claim:** $t_{r+1} = a_r$ for all $r \geq 1$.

*Proof of claim.* Consider one interval $[c,d]$ of length $\ell = d-c+1$ in $A_r
$. Its contribution to $A_{r+1}$ consists of:
- Even exponents $2c$ (left boundary transition) and $2d+2$ (right boundary tran
sition),
- Odd exponents $2c+1, 2c+3, \ldots, 2d+1$ (one per element).

These form the ordered set $\{2c,\; 2c+1,\; 2c+3,\; 2c+5,\; \ldots,\; 2d+1,\; 2d
+2\}$. The consecutive pairs are only $(2c, 2c{+}1)$ and $(2d{+}1, 2d{+}2)$; all
 other gaps equal 2. Checking by cases:
- $\ell = 1$ ($c=d$): the set is $\{2c,2c{+}1,2c{+}2\}$, **one** interval of len
gth 3.
- $\ell \geq 2$: the set splits into $[2c,2c{+}1]$, then $\ell-2$ isolated point
s $[2c{+}3,2c{+}3], \ldots, [2d{-}1,2d{-}1]$, then $[2d{+}1,2d{+}2]$ — exactly *
*$\ell$** intervals.

Different intervals of $A_r$ produce disjoint exponent sets (the last exponent $
2d+2$ from one interval and the first $2c'$ from the next satisfy $2c' \geq 2d+4
 > 2d+3$), so no merging. Summing over all intervals: $t_{r+1} = \sum \ell_i = a
_r$. $\square$

**Combined recurrence:** $a_{r+1} = a_r + 2a_{r-1}$, with $a_1 = 3$ (from $1+x+x
^2$) and $a_2 = 5$ (from $(1+x+x^2)^3 = 1+x+x^3+x^5+x^6$ in $\mathbb{F}_2[x]$).

**Step 4: Solve the recurrence.**

Characteristic equation $\lambda^2 - \lambda - 2 = 0$ gives $\lambda = 2$ and $\
lambda = -1$, so:

$$a_r = \alpha \cdot 2^r + \beta \cdot (-1)^r.$$

From $a_1 = 2\alpha - \beta = 3$ and $a_2 = 4\alpha + \beta = 5$: solving yields
 $\alpha = 4/3$, $\beta = -1/3$. Thus:

$$\boxed{g(r) = a_r = \frac{2^{r+2} - (-1)^r}{3}.}$$

**Verification:** $g(1)=\frac{8+1}{3}=3$ ✓, $g(2)=\frac{16-1}{3}=5$ ✓, $g(3)=\fr
ac{32+1}{3}=11$ ✓, $g(4)=\frac{64-1}{3}=21$ ✓ (all confirmed by direct computati
on).

**Step 5: Final answer.**

The number of odd coefficients of $(x^2+x+1)^n$ is:

$$\boxed{\prod_{i=1}^{k} \frac{2^{r_i+2}-(-1)^{r_i}}{3}}$$

where $r_1, r_2, \ldots, r_k$ are the lengths of the maximal runs of consecutive
 1s in the binary expansion of $n$.
