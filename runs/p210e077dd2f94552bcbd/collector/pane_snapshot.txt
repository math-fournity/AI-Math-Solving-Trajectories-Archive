## Solution

**The equation.** We solve
$$\rho_t - \rho^2 \rho_x = 0, \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \rho_t + f(\rho)_x
 = 0,\quad f(\rho)=-\tfrac13\rho^3.$$
The characteristic speed is $a(\rho)=f'(\rho)=-\rho^2$, and $f''(\rho)=-2\rho$,
so the flux has an **inflection point at $\rho=0$**: it is convex on $\rho<0$ an
d concave on $\rho>0$. The equation is therefore *not genuinely nonlinear*, and
this is the source of the nonuniform (composite) shocks asked about.

---

### 1. General solution (method of characteristics)

Along a characteristic $\dot x = a(\rho)=-\rho^2$ we have $\dot\rho=0$, so $\rho
$ is constant $=\rho_0$ and the characteristic is the straight line
$$x = x_0 - \rho_0^{\,2}\,t,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad x_0 = x+\rho^2 t.$$
Hence the general implicit solution is
$$\boxed{\;\rho = F\!\bigl(x+\rho^{2}t\bigr)\;}$$
for an arbitrary function $F$ determined by the initial data $\rho(x,0)=F(x)$.

**Wave breaking.** Differentiating implicitly, $\rho_x = F'/(1-2\rho F' t)$, so
a gradient catastrophe occurs at
$$t_b = -\frac{1}{2\,\min\,\rho F'(\rho)}$$
(where the minimum is taken over negative values of $\rho F'$). After $t_b$ a sh
ock must be inserted.

---

### 2. Rankine–Hugoniot condition (uniform shock)

For a discontinuity $x=s(t)$ separating constant states $\rho_l,\rho_r$,
$$\frac{ds}{dt} = \frac{f(\rho_l)-f(\rho_r)}{\rho_l-\rho_r}
   = -\frac{\rho_l^{\,3}-\rho_r^{\,3}}{3(\rho_l-\rho_r)}
   = \boxed{\;-\frac{\rho_l^{\,2}+\rho_l\rho_r+\rho_r^{\,2}}{3}\;}.$$
The Lax entropy condition $f'(\rho_l)>ds/dt>f'(\rho_r)$, i.e. $-\rho_l^{\,2}>ds/
dt>-\rho_r^{\,2}$, is equivalent (for $\rho_l\ne\rho_r$) to
$$\rho_r<-2\rho_l \quad\text{and}\quad \rho_l<-2\rho_r.$$
A **uniform** (constant-speed, constant-state) Lax shock is therefore admissible
 *only* when the two states satisfy both inequalities — roughly when they stradd
le the inflection point $\rho=0$ with comparable magnitudes, $-2\rho_l<\rho_r<-\
rho_l/2$ (for $\rho_l>0$). Outside this window a single uniform shock **violates
 the entropy condition**, and the Oleinik/Kružkov entropy solution is instead a
**composite (nonuniform) wave**: a rarefaction glued to a shock.

---

### 3. Differential equation of the nonuniform shock

Suppose the entropy solution is a rarefaction on the left, with self-similar pro
file $\rho=G(\xi)$, $\xi=x/t$, attached at $x=s(t)$ to a constant right state $\
rho_r$ (the symmetric situation is identical with signs reversed). The value car
ried by the rarefaction up to the shock is
$$\rho_l(t)=G\!\bigl(s(t)/t\bigr).$$
The shock curve is governed by the Rankine–Hugoniot condition evaluated on the *
local* (time-dependent) states:
$$\boxed{\;\frac{ds}{dt}
   = -\frac{\rho_l(t)^{2}+\rho_l(t)\,\rho_r+\rho_r^{2}}{3},
   \qquad \rho_l(t)=G\!\bigl(s(t)/t\bigr)\;}$$
together with the **characteristic matching condition** at the attachment point
(the rarefaction's outermost characteristic must run into the shock, i.e. be tan
gent to it):
$$f'\bigl(\rho_l(t)\bigr)=\frac{ds}{dt}
   \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
   -\rho_l(t)^{2}=\frac{ds}{dt}.$$
Eliminating $ds/dt$ gives the algebraic compatibility relation
$$\rho_l^{2}=\frac{\rho_l^{2}+\rho_l\rho_r+\rho_r^{2}}{3}
   \;\Longrightarrow\;
   2\rho_l^{2}-\rho_l\rho_r-\rho_r^{2}=0
   \;\Longrightarrow\;
   \rho_l=\rho_r\;\text{or}\;\rho_l=-\tfrac12\rho_r.$$
The nontrivial root $\rho_l=-\rho_r/2$ fixes the intermediate state $\rho_*$ at
which the rarefaction detaches from the shock, and the shock speed is then
$$V = -\rho_*^{2} = -\tfrac14\rho_r^{2}.$$
For the self-similar Riemann solution $s(t)=Vt$ this is constant; for general (n
on-Riemann) data the ODE above with the time-dependent $\rho_l(t)$ is the **diff
erential equation of the nonuniform shock**.

---

### 4. Does the nonuniform shock persist?

**Yes — I believe it persists (it is not replaced by a uniform shock).**

Reasoning:

1. **Structural origin.** The nonuniform shock is forced by the non-convexity of
 the flux ($f''=-2\rho$ changes sign at $\rho=0$). For the Riemann data that pro
duce it, a single uniform shock across the inflection point *violates the Oleini
k entropy condition* (one of the two Lax inequalities fails, as shown in §2). Th
e only Kružkov-entropy solution is the composite wave, so the nonuniform shock i
s not an artifact that can be "smoothed away."

2. **Self-similarity.** The Riemann solution is self-similar in $\xi=x/t$; the c
omposite wave keeps its shape (rarefaction of slope $\propto 1/t$ glued to a sho
ck at fixed $\xi=V$) for all $t>0$. The rarefaction spreads linearly in $t$ but
its attachment state $\rho_*=-\rho_r/2$ and the shock speed $V=-\rho_r^2/4$ are
*constants* fixed by the matching conditions. Nothing in this structure decays o
r disappears.

3. **Asymptotic stability.** For general (non-Riemann) initial data, the large-t
ime asymptotics of scalar conservation laws with non-convex flux is governed by
the Riemann solutions of the corresponding Riemann problem (Oleinik-type $N$-wav
e / similarity asymptotics). The nonuniform shock is therefore the *attractor*,
not a transient: perturbations diffuse into the rarefaction portion while the sh
ock–rarefaction composite itself remains.

4. **Why a uniform shock cannot take over.** Replacing the composite by a unifor
m shock joining $\rho_l$ directly to $\rho_r$ would require the Lax inequalities
 $\rho_r<-2\rho_l$ and $\rho_l<-2\rho_r$ to hold simultaneously; for exactly the
 data that produce the nonuniform shock these fail (by construction — that is wh
y Oleinik's convex-hull construction inserted the rarefaction in the first place
). Hence the uniform shock is entropy-forbidden and cannot be the long-time limi
t.

The only regime in which a uniform shock appears is the *different* data window
$-2\rho_l<\rho_r<-\rho_l/2$ where a uniform Lax shock is already admissible from
 the start; there was never a nonuniform shock to replace. Within its own data r
egime, the nonuniform shock is permanent.

---

**Summary.** The general solution is $\rho=F(x+\rho^2 t)$. Shocks satisfy $ds/dt
=-(\rho_l^2+\rho_l\rho_r+\rho_r^2)/3$. Because $f(\rho)=-\rho^3/3$ has an inflec
tion at $\rho=0$, the entropy solution for straddling data is a **nonuniform (co
mposite) shock** whose differential equation is
$$\frac{ds}{dt}=-\frac{\rho_l(t)^2+\rho_l(t)\rho_r+\rho_r^2}{3},\qquad \rho_l(t)
=G(s(t)/t),\qquad -\rho_l(t)^2=\frac{ds}{dt},$$
with the matching fixing $\rho_l=-\rho_r/2$ and $V=-\rho_r^2/4$. The nonuniform
shock **persists**: it is the unique entropy solution, it is self-similar, and a
 uniform shock is entropy-forbidden for the data that generate it.
