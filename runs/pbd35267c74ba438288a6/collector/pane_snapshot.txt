## Notational Issue

The problem labels $C_b(X)$ as "continuous functions with compact support." This
 is **non-standard notation**. In standard functional analysis:
- $C_c(X)$ = continuous functions with **compact support**
- $C_b(X)$ = **bounded** continuous functions
- $C_0(X)$ = continuous functions **vanishing at infinity**

I will address both interpretations, since the answer differs critically.

---

## Interpretation 1: $C_b(X)$ = bounded continuous functions (standard notation)

**Answer: No, the duals are not the same in general.**

**Proof.** Take $X = \mathbb{N}$ with the discrete topology. This is a locally c
ompact Hausdorff space.

- $C_0(\mathbb{N}) = c_0$, the space of sequences converging to $0$. By the Ries
z–Markov–Kakutani theorem (or directly), $(c_0)^* \cong \ell^1(\mathbb{N})$, the
 space of absolutely summable sequences, which corresponds to the **countably ad
ditive** finite regular Borel measures on $\mathbb{N}$.

- $C_b(\mathbb{N}) = \ell^\infty(\mathbb{N})$, the space of bounded sequences. S
ince $\mathbb{N}$ is discrete, $\ell^\infty(\mathbb{N}) = C(\beta\mathbb{N})$ wh
ere $\beta\mathbb{N}$ is the Stone–Čech compactification. Thus $(\ell^\infty)^*
\cong M(\beta\mathbb{N})$, the space of regular Borel measures on $\beta\mathbb{
N}$.

Now $\beta\mathbb{N} \setminus \mathbb{N}$ (the set of free ultrafilters) is non
empty, and there exist regular Borel probability measures on $\beta\mathbb{N}$ s
upported on $\beta\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{N}$ (for instance, any limit of poi
nt masses along a free ultrafilter). Such a measure $\mu$ defines a bounded line
ar functional on $\ell^\infty$ via $f \mapsto \int_{\beta\mathbb{N}} f\,d\mu$, b
ut this functional does **not** come from any $\ell^1$ sequence, because:

If it did, say $\mu = \sum_n a_n \delta_n$ with $\sum |a_n| < \infty$, then $\mu
$ would be supported on $\mathbb{N} \subset \beta\mathbb{N}$. But we chose $\mu$
 supported on $\beta\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{N}$, a contradiction.

Therefore $(C_0(\mathbb{N}))^* = \ell^1 \subsetneq (\ell^\infty)^* = (C_b(\mathb
b{N}))^*$, so the duals are **not** the same. $\square$

**General reason:** For any locally compact Hausdorff $X$, $C_b(X) \cong C(\beta
 X)$ via the Stone–Čech compactification, so $(C_b(X))^* \cong M(\beta X)$. Mean
while $(C_0(X))^* \cong M(X)$. Since $X$ embeds as an open dense subset of $\bet
a X$ and $\beta X \setminus X \neq \emptyset$ whenever $X$ is non-compact, $M(\b
eta X)$ is strictly larger than $M(X)$ (the latter corresponds to measures on $\
beta X$ supported on $X$). The duals coincide **if and only if $X$ is compact**
(in which case $C_0(X) = C_b(X) = C(X)$ already).

## Interpretation 2: $C_b(X)$ means continuous functions with compact support (i
.e., $C_c(X)$, as the problem text states)

**Answer: Yes, the duals are the same (when both are equipped with the sup-norm)
.**

**Proof.** We use the following standard fact: for a locally compact Hausdorff s
pace $X$, $C_c(X)$ is **dense** in $C_0(X)$ with respect to the sup-norm $\|\cdo
t\|_\infty$.

*Proof of density:* Let $f \in C_0(X)$ and $\varepsilon > 0$. Since $f$ vanishes
 at infinity, the set $K = \{x \in X : |f(x)| \geq \varepsilon\}$ is compact. By
 Urysohn's lemma for locally compact Hausdorff spaces, there exists $\varphi \in
 C_c(X)$ with $0 \leq \varphi \leq 1$ and $\varphi \equiv 1$ on $K$. Then $g = f
\varphi \in C_c(X)$ (since $\operatorname{supp}(g) \subseteq \operatorname{supp}
(\varphi)$, which is compact), and
$$\|f - g\|_\infty = \|f(1-\varphi)\|_\infty.$$
On $K$, $1-\varphi = 0$, so $f(1-\varphi)=0$. On $X\setminus K$, $|f|<\varepsilo
n$ and $|1-\varphi|\leq 1$, so $|f(1-\varphi)|<\varepsilon$. Hence $\|f-g\|_\inf
ty \leq \varepsilon$. This proves density.

Now, $C_c(X)$ equipped with $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ is a normed space whose completio
n is $C_0(X)$. A fundamental theorem of functional analysis states:

> **If $Y$ is a dense subspace of a normed space $Z$, then the restriction map $
R: Z^* \to Y^*$, $\Lambda \mapsto \Lambda|_Y$, is an isometric isomorphism.**

*Proof of this fact:* $R$ is clearly linear and $\|R(\Lambda)\| \leq \|\Lambda\|
$. For the reverse inequality, given $\Lambda \in Z^*$ and $y \in Y$, $|\Lambda(
y)| \leq \|\Lambda\|\|y\|$, so $\|R(\Lambda)\| \leq \|\Lambda\|$; conversely, fo
r any $z \in Z$ with $\|z\|\leq 1$, pick $y_n \in Y$ with $y_n \to z$, then $|\L
ambda(z)| = \lim |\Lambda(y_n)| = \lim |R(\Lambda)(y_n)| \leq \|R(\Lambda)\|$, s
o $\|\Lambda\| \leq \|R(\Lambda)\|$. For surjectivity: given $T \in Y^*$, define
 $\Lambda$ on $Z$ by $\Lambda(z) = \lim T(y_n)$ where $y_n \to z$; this is well-
defined (if $y_n' \to z$ too, then $\|y_n - y_n'\|\to 0$ so $|T(y_n)-T(y_n')|\le
q \|T\|\|y_n-y_n'\|\to 0$), linear, bounded with $\|\Lambda\|=\|T\|$, and $R(\La
mbda)=T$.

Applying this with $Y = C_c(X)$ and $Z = C_0(X)$:
$$\boxed{(C_c(X))^* \cong (C_0(X))^* \cong M(X),}$$
where $M(X)$ is the Banach space of finite regular Borel measures on $X$ (by Rie
sz–Markov–Kakutani). $\square$
