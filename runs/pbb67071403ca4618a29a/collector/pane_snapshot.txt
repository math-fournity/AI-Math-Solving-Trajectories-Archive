## Solution

**Setting up the parity reduction.** For three lattice points $P_i=(x_i,y_i)$, t
wice the signed area is given by the shoelace formula
$$2A = x_1(y_2-y_3)+x_2(y_3-y_1)+x_3(y_1-y_2),$$
which is always an integer. The area $A=|2A|/2$ is an integer **iff** $2A$ is ev
en, i.e. $2A\equiv 0\pmod 2$.

**Counting parities.** The square $[-99,100]\times[-99,100]$ contains $200$ inte
gers per axis. Even integers: $\{-98,-96,\dots,98,100\}$ gives $100$ values; odd
 integers: $\{-99,-97,\dots,99\}$ gives $100$ values. So each coordinate is even
 or odd with probability $1/2$, independently. Thus each point's parity $(x_i\bm
od 2,\, y_i\bmod 2)$ is uniform over $\mathbb{F}_2^2$ (4 points), independently
across the three points.

**Reformulating mod 2.** Modulo 2,
$$2A \equiv \det\begin{pmatrix}1&x_1&y_1\\1&x_2&y_2\\1&x_3&y_3\end{pmatrix}\pmod
 2.$$
This determinant vanishes mod 2 iff the three rows are affinely dependent over $
\mathbb{F}_2$, i.e. iff the three parity-points in $\mathbb{F}_2^2$ are **affine
ly dependent**.

In $\mathbb{F}_2^2$, every (affine) line has exactly $2$ points (a line $ax+by=c
$ with $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$ has exactly $2$ solutions). Therefore any three **distinc
t** points are automatically affinely independent (they cannot lie on a 2-point
line). Conversely, three points are affinely dependent iff at least two of them
coincide.

So $2A$ is **odd** iff the three parity-points are all distinct.

**Computing the probability.** Three points chosen iid uniformly from the $4$ po
ints of $\mathbb{F}_2^2$:
$$\Pr(\text{all distinct})=\frac{4\cdot 3\cdot 2}{4^3}=\frac{24}{64}=\frac{3}{8}
.$$

Hence
$$\Pr(\text{area is integer})=\Pr(2A\text{ even})=1-\frac{3}{8}=\frac{5}{8}.$$

With $\gcd(5,8)=1$, we have $m=5,\ n=8$, and
$$m+n=13.$$
