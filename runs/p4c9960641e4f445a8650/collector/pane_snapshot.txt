**Answer: Yes.** Every locally convex topological vector space has a local base
at the origin consisting of convex balanced (circled) neighborhoods.

**Proof.**

Let $X$ be a locally convex topological vector space (TVS). By definition, the o
rigin has a local base of convex neighborhoods. We show that every neighborhood
of $0$ contains a convex balanced neighborhood of $0$; this yields the desired l
ocal base.

Let $U$ be an arbitrary neighborhood of $0$. By local convexity, there exists a
convex neighborhood $V$ of $0$ with $V \subseteq U$.

Since scalar multiplication $(t, x) \mapsto tx$ is continuous (jointly in $t$ an
d $x$) and equals $0$ at $(0,0)$, applied to the neighborhood $V$ of $0$, there
exist $\delta > 0$ and a neighborhood $N$ of $0$ such that
$$tN \subseteq V \quad \text{whenever } |t| \le \delta.$$

Define
$$W = \bigcup_{|t| \le \delta} tN.$$

**$W$ is a neighborhood of $0$.** Pick any $t_0$ with $0 < |t_0| \le \delta$ (e.
g. $t_0 = \delta$). Then $t_0 N \subseteq W$, and $t_0 N$ is a neighborhood of $
0$ since multiplication by the nonzero scalar $t_0$ is a homeomorphism. Hence $W
$ is a neighborhood of $0$.

**$W \subseteq V$.** Immediate from the defining property $tN \subseteq V$ for $
|t| \le \delta$.

**$W$ is balanced.** Let $x \in W$, so $x = t_0 n$ for some $|t_0| \le \delta$ a
nd $n \in N$. For any scalar $s$ with $|s| \le 1$, we have $sx = (s t_0) n$ with
 $|s t_0| \le |t_0| \le \delta$, so $sx \in W$.

Now set $B = \operatorname{conv}(W)$, the convex hull of $W$.

**$B$ is convex** by construction.

**$B$ is balanced.** For $|s| \le 1$, $sB = s\operatorname{conv}(W) = \operatorn
ame{conv}(sW)$. Since $W$ is balanced, $sW \subseteq W$, so $\operatorname{conv}
(sW) \subseteq \operatorname{conv}(W) = B$.

**$B$ is a neighborhood of $0$**, since it contains $W$, which is a neighborhood
 of $0$.

**$B \subseteq V$.** Since $V$ is convex and $W \subseteq V$, the convex hull sa
tisfies $B = \operatorname{conv}(W) \subseteq V \subseteq U$.

Thus $B$ is a convex, balanced neighborhood of $0$ contained in $U$. Since $U$ w
as an arbitrary neighborhood of $0$, the collection of all convex balanced neigh
borhoods of $0$ forms a local base at the origin.

Therefore every locally convex topological vector space admits a convex balanced
 local base. $\blacksquare$
