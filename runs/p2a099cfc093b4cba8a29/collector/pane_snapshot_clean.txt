## Solution

**Answer: Yes, such 100 triangles exist.**

**Construction.** For $k=1,2,\dots,100$, let $T_k$ be the right triangle whose t
wo legs have lengths $a_k$ and $2/a_k$, where $a_k=k+2$ (so $a_1=3, a_2=4, \dots
, a_{100}=102$). Each $T_k$ has area
$$\tfrac12 \cdot a_k \cdot \tfrac{2}{a_k}=1,$$
so all 100 triangles have the **same area** $1$.

**Claim 1. The triangles are pairwise non‑congruent.**

$T_k$ has legs $\{a_k,\,2/a_k\}$ and $T_m$ has legs $\{a_m,\,2/a_m\}$. Two right
 triangles are congruent iff their leg multisets coincide. So $T_k\cong T_m$ req
uires either $a_m=a_k$ (hence $m=k$), or $a_m=2/a_k$ and $2/a_m=a_k$. But the se
cond case gives $a_m=2/a_k\le 2/3<3\le a_m$, a contradiction. Hence $T_k\cong T_
m\Rightarrow m=k$.

**Claim 2. No $T_k$ can be covered by any single $T_m$ with $m\ne k$.**

Suppose, for contradiction, that $T_k\subseteq g(T_m)$ for some rigid motion $g$
 (a congruent copy of $T_m$). Taking areas,
$$\operatorname{area}(T_k)\le \operatorname{area}(g(T_m))=\operatorname{area}(T_
m)=1=\operatorname{area}(T_k).$$
Thus $\operatorname{area}(T_k)=\operatorname{area}(g(T_m))$, with $T_k\subseteq
g(T_m)$ and both sets closed triangles. A closed triangle properly contained in
another closed triangle would lose a relatively open (hence positive‑area) regio
n, contradicting equality of areas. Therefore $T_k=g(T_m)$, i.e. $T_k\cong T_m$—
contradicting Claim 1 since $m\ne k$.

**Conclusion.** The 100 triangles $T_1,\dots,T_{100}$ all have equal area and ar
e pairwise non‑congruent, so no one of them can be covered by any single other.
Hence there exist 100 triangles such that none of them can be covered by the oth
er 99.
