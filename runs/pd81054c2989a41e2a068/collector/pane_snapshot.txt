**Theorem.** The complete totally geodesic submanifolds of $\mathbb{R}P^n$ (with
 the round metric of constant sectional curvature $+1$) are exactly the projecti
ve subspaces
$$\mathbb{R}P^k \;=\; \pi\bigl(S^n \cap V\bigr), \qquad 0 \le k \le n,$$
where $\pi: S^n \to \mathbb{R}P^n$ is the antipodal quotient map and $V \subset
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ is a $(k+1)$-dimensional linear subspace. In particular, every
 totally geodesic submanifold is a projective subspace $\mathbb{R}P^k \subset \m
athbb{R}P^n$.

---

**Proof.**

Equip $\mathbb{R}P^n$ with the metric making $\pi: S^n \to \mathbb{R}P^n$ a Riem
annian covering (local isometry), where $S^n \subset \mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ carries t
he round metric. We use two classical facts:

*Fact 1.* The complete totally geodesic submanifolds of $S^n$ are exactly the **
great spheres** $S^k = S^n \cap V$, where $V \le \mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ is a linear s
ubspace with $\dim V = k+1$.

*Justification:* A geodesic of $S^n$ through $p$ in direction $u \in T_pS^n$ is
the great circle $p\cos t + \hat u \sin t$ (with $\hat u \perp p$, $|\hat u|=1$)
. A totally geodesic submanifold $N \ni p$ with $T_pN = W$ must contain every ge
odesic through $p$ in directions of $W$; these sweep out exactly $S^n \cap \oper
atorname{span}(p, W)$, a great sphere. Completeness forces $N$ to be the entire
great sphere. ∎

*Fact 2.* A local isometry $f: \tilde M \to M$ pulls back totally geodesic subma
nifolds to totally geodesic submanifolds: $N \subset M$ totally geodesic $\Right
arrow$ $f^{-1}(N)$ totally geodesic in $\tilde M$ (each component). This is beca
use the second fundamental form is a local invariant.

---

**Step 1 (Lifting).** Let $M \subset \mathbb{R}P^n$ be a connected totally geode
sic submanifold of dimension $k$. Set $\widetilde M := \pi^{-1}(M) \subset S^n$.
 By Fact 2, $\widetilde M$ is totally geodesic in $S^n$. Hence each connected co
mponent of $\widetilde M$ is a complete totally geodesic submanifold of $S^n$, a
nd by Fact 1 each component is a great $k$-sphere $S^k$.

**Step 2 (Antipodal invariance forces a single component).** Since $M = \pi(\wid
etilde M)$ and $\pi(-x)=\pi(x)$, the set $\widetilde M$ is invariant under the a
ntipodal map $a: x\mapsto -x$. Every great sphere is itself antipodally invarian
t: $S^n\cap V = -(S^n\cap V)$ because $V$ is a *linear* subspace. Therefore if $
\widetilde M$ had two distinct components $C_1, C_2$, the antipodal image $-C_1$
 would be a component equal to $C_1$ itself (a great sphere is antipodally invar
iant), so $-C_1 = C_1 \ne C_2$; but then $C_2$ is disjoint from $-C_1$, contradi
cting the requirement that $\widetilde M = -\widetilde M$ be the full antipodal
preimage of $M$ under a *double* cover (the preimage of a connected $M$ under a
double cover is either connected or two antipodal copies; the second option is i
mpossible here because each copy would already be antipodally invariant). Concre
tely: $\pi^{-1}(M)$ is a double cover of $M$; if it split as $C\sqcup(-C)$ with
$C=-C$, then $C=-C$ and $-C=C$ give $C\sqcup C$, impossible unless $C=\widetilde
 M$. Hence $\widetilde M$ is connected.

Thus $\widetilde M$ is a single great sphere $S^k = S^n \cap V$ for some $(k+1)$
-dimensional linear $V\le\mathbb R^{n+1}$.

**Step 3 (Projecting down).** Since $\widetilde M = S^n\cap V$ is antipodally in
variant, it descends to
$$M \;=\; \pi(S^n\cap V) \;=\; \mathbb{R}P^n \cap \mathbb{P}(V) \;=:\; \mathbb{R
}P^k \subset \mathbb{R}P^n,$$
a standard projective $k$-subspace, and $\pi|_{S^k}: S^k \to \mathbb{R}P^k$ is t
he usual double cover. Being the isometric quotient of a totally geodesic subman
ifold, $M=\mathbb{R}P^k$ is totally geodesic in $\mathbb{R}P^n$.

**Step 4 (Converse — every $\mathbb{R}P^k$ occurs).** Conversely, let $V\le\math
bb R^{n+1}$ be any $(k+1)$-dimensional linear subspace. Then $S^k := S^n\cap V$
is a great sphere, hence totally geodesic in $S^n$ (Fact 1). It is antipodally i
nvariant, so it descends to $\mathbb{R}P^k = \pi(S^k)\subset\mathbb{R}P^n$. To s
ee $\mathbb{R}P^k$ is totally geodesic: take a geodesic $\gamma$ of $\mathbb{R}P
^n$ with $\gamma(0),\dot\gamma(0)\in\mathbb{R}P^k$. Lift to a geodesic $\tilde\g
amma$ of $S^n$ starting in $S^k$ with tangent in $T(S^k)$; since $S^k$ is totall
y geodesic, $\tilde\gamma\subset S^k$, hence $\gamma = \pi\circ\tilde\gamma \sub
set \mathbb{R}P^k$. So $\mathbb{R}P^k$ is totally geodesic.

---

Combining Steps 1–4: the connected complete totally geodesic submanifolds of $\m
athbb{R}P^n$ are precisely the projective subspaces $\mathbb{R}P^k$ for $0\le k\
le n$ (with $k=0$ giving points, $k=n$ giving the whole space). A general (possi
bly disconnected) totally geodesic submanifold is a disjoint union of such proje
ctive subspaces of a common ambient space, but each connected component is some
$\mathbb{R}P^k$. $\qquad\blacksquare$
