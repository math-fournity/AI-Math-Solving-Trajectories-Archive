## Solution

**Step 1: Check whether the curve of intersection exists.**

The curve is the intersection of the unit sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$ with the
plane $x + y = 14$.

On the unit sphere, every point satisfies $|x| \le 1$ and $|y| \le 1$, so by the
 triangle inequality:
$$|x + y| \le |x| + |y| \le 2.$$

But the plane requires $x + y = 14 > 2$. **No point on the unit sphere can lie o
n this plane.** The intersection curve is empty.

**Step 2: Conclude the line integral.**

Since the curve of intersection is empty, there is no curve to traverse. The lin
e integral of $F$ around an empty (degenerate) curve is:
$$\oint_C F \cdot d\mathbf{r} = 0.$$

**Step 3: Verify via Stokes' Theorem (as the problem requests).**

By Stokes' Theorem:
$$\oint_C F \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_S (\nabla \times F) \cdot \mathbf{n} \, dA
,$$
where $S$ is the surface in the plane $x + y = 14$ bounded by the curve $C$.

Using the given values:
- $\nabla \times F = \langle 2, -1, 4 \rangle$
- $\mathbf{n} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\langle 1, 1, 0 \rangle$

The dot product is:
$$(\nabla \times F) \cdot \mathbf{n} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(2 \cdot 1 + (-1) \cdot
 1 + 4 \cdot 0) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(2 - 1) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.$$

However, the surface $S$ (the disk in the plane $x+y=14$ cut out by the unit sph
ere) has **zero area**, since the plane does not intersect the sphere. Therefore
:
$$\iint_S (\nabla \times F) \cdot \mathbf{n} \, dA = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \cdot \u
nderbrace{\text{Area}(S)}_{=\,0} = 0.$$

**Result:**

$$\boxed{\iint_S (\nabla \times F) \cdot \mathbf{n} \, dA = 0}$$

The line integral is **0**, because the plane $x + y = 14$ does not intersect th
e unit sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$ (since $|x+y| \le 2 < 14$ on the sphere), ma
king the bounding curve and enclosed surface both empty.
