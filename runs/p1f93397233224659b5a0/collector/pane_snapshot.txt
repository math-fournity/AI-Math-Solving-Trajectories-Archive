## Solution

**Claim:** Yes, $M/K$ is a normal extension.

**Key characterization (standard).** For an algebraic extension $K \subseteq M \
subseteq \overline{K}$, the following are equivalent:

1. $M/K$ is normal;
2. For every $K$-embedding $\varphi: M \hookrightarrow \overline{K}$, one has $\
varphi(M) = M$;
3. $M$ is **stable** under $G_K := \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)$, i.e. $\s
igma(M) = M$ for every $\sigma \in G_K$.

We prove the equivalence, then apply it.

---

### Proof of the characterization

**(2) $\Leftrightarrow$ (3).** Every $\sigma \in G_K$ fixes $K$, so its restrict
ion $\sigma|_M : M \to \overline{K}$ is a $K$-embedding of $M$ into $\overline{K
}$. Conversely, by the extension theorem for algebraic extensions into an algebr
aically closed field, every $K$-embedding $\varphi : M \hookrightarrow \overline
{K}$ extends to a $K$-automorphism $\widetilde{\varphi} \in \operatorname{Gal}(\
overline{K}/K)$. Hence conditions (2) and (3) assert exactly the same thing: $\v
arphi(M) = M$ for all $K$-embeddings $\varphi$ iff $\sigma(M) = M$ for all $\sig
ma \in G_K$.

**(1) $\Leftrightarrow$ (2).** This is the usual definition-equivalent form of n
ormality: $M/K$ is normal iff every irreducible $f \in K[x]$ having one root in
$M$ splits completely over $M$. Equivalently, for every $\alpha \in M$ and every
 $K$-conjugate $\beta$ of $\alpha$ (i.e. every root of $\min_K(\alpha)$ in $\ove
rline{K}$), $\beta \in M$. The map sending $\alpha \mapsto \beta$ extends to a $
K$-embedding $K(\alpha) \hookrightarrow \overline{K}$, which in turn extends to
a $K$-embedding $\varphi : M \hookrightarrow \overline{K}$; then $\beta = \varph
i(\alpha) \in \varphi(M)$. Thus "all conjugates lie in $M$" is equivalent to "$\
varphi(M) \subseteq M$ for every $K$-embedding $\varphi$". Applying the same to
$\varphi^{-1}$ (or noting $\varphi(M) \subseteq M$ with $[\varphi(M):K] = [M:K]$
 in the finite case, and using the group/inverse argument in general) yields $\v
arphi(M) = M$. So (1) $\Leftrightarrow$ (2).

---

### Applying to the problem

The hypothesis is that $G_K = \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)$ **acts natural
ly on $M$**. The natural action of $G_K$ is its action on $\overline{K}$; saying
 it "acts on $M$" means this action **restricts to an action on $M$**, i.e. ever
y $\sigma \in G_K$ satisfies $\sigma(M) \subseteq M$.

Because $G_K$ is a **group**, it is closed under inverses: $\sigma \in G_K \Righ
tarrow \sigma^{-1} \in G_K$, so $\sigma^{-1}(M) \subseteq M$ as well. Applying $
\sigma$ to both sides gives
$$M = \sigma(\sigma^{-1}(M)) \subseteq \sigma(M) \subseteq M,$$
hence $\sigma(M) = M$ for every $\sigma \in G_K$. This is precisely condition (3
) of the characterization.

By the equivalence proved above, $M/K$ is normal. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** The crux is that a *group* action forces equality, not merely inclus
ion: stability under all $\sigma$ together with stability under all $\sigma^{-1}
$ pins $M$ to be exactly invariant. A single embedding (or a semigroup action) w
ould only give $\sigma(M) \subseteq M$, which is weaker; the group structure of
$G_K$ is what upgrades inclusion to equality and hence to normality.
