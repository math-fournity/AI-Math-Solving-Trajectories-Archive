# Proof

**Claim.** Yes. The Galois group of an irreducible separable polynomial of degre
e $n$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $S_n$, hence to a permutation group on $n$
letters.

---

**Setup.** Let $F$ be a field and let $f \in F[x]$ be an irreducible separable p
olynomial of degree $n$. Let $K$ be the splitting field of $f$ over $F$, and let
 $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n \in K$ be the $n$ distinct roots of $f$ (distinct by
 separability). The Galois group is $\operatorname{Gal}(K/F) = \{\sigma \in \ope
ratorname{Aut}(K) : \sigma|_F = \mathrm{id}\}$.

Since $f$ is separable, $K/F$ is a Galois extension, so $|\operatorname{Gal}(K/F
)| = [K:F]$.

---

**Step 1 — Each automorphism permutes the roots.**

Take $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(K/F)$. Write $f(x) = c_n x^n + \cdots + c_0$
 with $c_i \in F$. For any root $\alpha_i$,
$$0 = \sigma(f(\alpha_i)) = \sigma\!\left(\sum_j c_j \alpha_i^j\right) = \sum_j
\sigma(c_j)\,\sigma(\alpha_i)^j = \sum_j c_j\,\sigma(\alpha_i)^j = f(\sigma(\alp
ha_i)),$$
since $\sigma$ fixes each $c_j \in F$. Thus $\sigma(\alpha_i)$ is again a root o
f $f$. Because $\sigma$ is a field automorphism it is injective, and it maps the
 finite set $\{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n\}$ into itself, so it restricts to a **pe
rmutation** of this set.

---

**Step 2 — The action homomorphism.**

Labeling the roots, the assignment $\sigma \mapsto \pi_\sigma$, where $\pi_\sigm
a \in S_n$ is defined by $\sigma(\alpha_i) = \alpha_{\pi_\sigma(i)}$, gives a gr
oup homomorphism
$$\varphi \colon \operatorname{Gal}(K/F) \longrightarrow S_n, \qquad \sigma \map
sto \pi_\sigma.$$
Indeed, $\pi_{\sigma\tau}(i) = \pi_\sigma(\pi_\tau(i))$ because $(\sigma\tau)(\a
lpha_i) = \sigma(\tau(\alpha_i)) = \sigma(\alpha_{\pi_\tau(i)}) = \alpha_{\pi_\s
igma(\pi_\tau(i))}$, so $\varphi(\sigma\tau) = \varphi(\sigma)\varphi(\tau)$.

---

**Step 3 — Injectivity.**

Suppose $\varphi(\sigma) = \mathrm{id}$, i.e., $\sigma(\alpha_i) = \alpha_i$ for
 every $i$. The splitting field is generated over $F$ by the roots:
$$K = F(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n).$$
Any element of $K$ is a rational expression in the $\alpha_i$ with coefficients
in $F$. Since $\sigma$ fixes $F$ and fixes every $\alpha_i$, it fixes every such
 expression, hence $\sigma = \mathrm{id}_K$. Therefore $\ker\varphi$ is trivial
and $\varphi$ is **injective**.

---

**Step 4 — Transitivity (bonus, from irreducibility).**

Because $f$ is irreducible, the action is moreover **transitive**: for any $i, j
$ there exists $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(K/F)$ with $\sigma(\alpha_i) = \al
pha_j$. (The minimal polynomial of $\alpha_i$ over $F$ is $f$ itself, so there i
s an $F$-embedding $F(\alpha_i) \hookrightarrow K$ sending $\alpha_i \mapsto \al
pha_j$; this extends to an automorphism of $K$ since $K/F$ is normal.) Thus the
image of $\varphi$ is a **transitive subgroup** of $S_n$.

---

**Conclusion.** The map $\varphi$ embeds $\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)$ as a subgroup
 of $S_n$, i.e., as a permutation group on the $n$ letters $\{1,\dots,n\}$ (in f
act a transitive one). Hence:

**Yes, the Galois group of an irreducible separable polynomial of degree $n$ is
isomorphic to a permutation group on $n$ letters.** $\blacksquare$
