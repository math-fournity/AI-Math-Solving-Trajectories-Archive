## Proof

**Statement.** Let $\{a,b,c,d\}$ be algebraically independent over $\mathbb{Q}$.
 Then
$$\mathbb{Q}(a,b)\cap \mathbb{Q}(c,d)=\mathbb{Q}.$$

The statement is **true**.

---

### Step 1. $a,b$ are algebraically independent over $\mathbb{Q}(c,d)$.

Suppose for contradiction that $a,b$ are algebraically dependent over $\mathbb{Q
}(c,d)$. Then there exists a nonzero polynomial $P(X,Y)\in \mathbb{Q}(c,d)[X,Y]$
 with $P(a,b)=0$. Clearing denominators, we obtain a nonzero polynomial $\wideti
lde P\in \mathbb{Q}[c,d][X,Y]=\mathbb{Q}[X,Y,c,d]$ with $\widetilde P(a,b,c,d)=0
$. This is a nontrivial polynomial relation among $a,b,c,d$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, c
ontradicting algebraic independence. Hence $a,b$ are algebraically independent o
ver $\mathbb{Q}(c,d)$, and in particular over any intermediate field $\mathbb{Q}
\subseteq L\subseteq \mathbb{Q}(c,d)$.

### Step 2. $\mathbb{Q}$ is algebraically closed in $\mathbb{Q}(a,b)$.

This is the standard fact that a field $k$ is algebraically closed in the ration
al function field $k(X_1,\dots,X_n)$. It suffices to prove it for $k(X)$; the ge
neral case follows by induction since $k(X_1,\dots,X_n)=k(X_1)(X_2,\dots,X_n)$ a
nd algebraic-closedness is preserved under iteration.

For $k(X)$: let $r=f/g\in k(X)$ (with $f,g\in k[X]$ coprime, $g\neq 0$) be algeb
raic over $k$. Let $r$ satisfy its monic minimal polynomial
$$T^m + \alpha_{m-1}T^{m-1}+\cdots+\alpha_0 = 0,\qquad \alpha_i\in k.$$
Substituting and clearing denominators gives
$$f^m + \alpha_{m-1}f^{m-1}g+\cdots+\alpha_0 g^m = 0 \quad\text{in } k[X].$$
Since $\gcd(f,g)=1$, we have $\gcd(f^m,g)=1$, so $g\mid 1$; thus $g$ is constant
. Then $f$ satisfies a monic polynomial over $k$ inside $k[X]$, forcing $f$ to b
e constant. Hence $r\in k$.

Applying this with $k=\mathbb{Q}$ and using $\mathbb{Q}(a,b)\cong \mathbb{Q}(X,Y
)$ (via $a\mapsto X,\ b\mapsto Y$, which is an isomorphism precisely because $a,
b$ are algebraically independent), we conclude: **every element of $\mathbb{Q}(a
,b)$ algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$ lies in $\mathbb{Q}$.**

### Step 3. The intersection is $\mathbb{Q}$.

Let $\alpha\in \mathbb{Q}(a,b)\cap \mathbb{Q}(c,d)$. Since $\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}(
c,d)\supseteq\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$, Step 1 implies that $a,b$ are algebraically in
dependent over $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$. Therefore
$$\operatorname{trdeg}\!\big(\mathbb{Q}(a,b)/\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)\big)=2.$$

Also $\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}(a,b)$. By the tower formula for transcendence degree,
$$\operatorname{trdeg}\!\big(\mathbb{Q}(a,b)/\mathbb{Q}\big)=\operatorname{trdeg
}\!\big(\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)/\mathbb{Q}\big)+\operatorname{trdeg}\!\big(\mathbb{Q}
(a,b)/\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)\big).$$

The left side equals $2$ (since $a,b$ are algebraically independent over $\mathb
b{Q}$). The right side equals $\operatorname{trdeg}(\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)/\mathbb{Q
})+2$. Hence
$$\operatorname{trdeg}\!\big(\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)/\mathbb{Q}\big)=0,$$
i.e. $\alpha$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$.

Since $\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}(a,b)$ and is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$, Step 2 forc
es $\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}$.

Thus $\mathbb{Q}(a,b)\cap\mathbb{Q}(c,d)\subseteq\mathbb{Q}$. The reverse inclus
ion $\mathbb{Q}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(a,b)\cap\mathbb{Q}(c,d)$ is immediate. Theref
ore
$$\boxed{\mathbb{Q}(a,b)\cap\mathbb{Q}(c,d)=\mathbb{Q}.}$$
