## Proof

**Step 1: $\pi$ is a $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundle.**

Since $X$ is nonsingular and $\pi: X \to \mathbb{P}^1$ is a morphism whose every
 fiber is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$, the morphism $\pi$ is proper, flat (fibe
rs are all 1-dimensional, equidimensional, and $X$, $\mathbb{P}^1$ are nonsingul
ar), and smooth (no singular fibers). By the Fischer–Grauert theorem (in the ana
lytic category) / its algebraic analogue, a proper smooth morphism with all fibe
rs isomorphic is locally trivial for the étale (in fact Zariski, over $\mathbb{P
}^1$) topology. Hence $\pi$ is a $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundle, so
$$X \cong \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})$$
for a rank-$2$ vector bundle $\mathcal{E}$ on $\mathbb{P}^1$.

**Step 2: Classification of rank-$2$ bundles on $\mathbb{P}^1$.**

By the Birkhoff–Grothendieck theorem, every vector bundle on $\mathbb{P}^1$ spli
ts as a direct sum of line bundles:
$$\mathcal{E} \cong \mathcal{O}(a) \oplus \mathcal{O}(b), \quad a, b \in \mathbb
{Z}.$$

Since $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}) \cong \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E} \otimes \mathcal{L})
$ for any line bundle $\mathcal{L}$, we may tensor by $\mathcal{O}(-a)$ and assu
me $a = 0$. Setting $m = b - a \geq 0$ (relabeling so that $b \geq a$), we get
$$X \cong \mathbb{P}\bigl(\mathcal{O} \oplus \mathcal{O}(m)\bigr) =: \mathbb{F}_
m,$$
the $m$-th **Hirzebruch surface**.

**Step 3: The self-intersection of the section determines $m$.**

On $\mathbb{F}_m = \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O} \oplus \mathcal{O}(m))$, there are two
 distinguished sections coming from the two surjections:
- $s_0: \mathcal{O} \oplus \mathcal{O}(m) \twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{O}$, givin
g a section $S_0$ with $S_0^2 = +m$,
- $s_\infty: \mathcal{O} \oplus \mathcal{O}(m) \twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{O}(m)
$, giving a section $S_\infty$ with $S_\infty^2 = -m$.

This is standard: if $\xi$ is the tautological class on $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})
$ and $f$ the fiber class, then $S_0 \sim \xi$ and $S_\infty \sim \xi - mf$, giv
ing $S_0^2 = m$ and $S_\infty^2 = -m$.

More generally, every section of $\mathbb{F}_m$ is linearly equivalent to $S_0 +
 kf$ for some $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, and its self-intersection is $m + 2k$. The uni
que section with self-intersection $-m$ is $S_\infty$ (corresponding to $k = -m$
); it is the unique irreducible curve with negative self-intersection on $\mathb
b{F}_m$ when $m > 0$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

The given section $E$ has $E^2 = -n$. By Step 3, this forces $m = n$. Therefore
$$\boxed{X \cong \mathbb{F}_n = \mathbb{P}\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1} \oplu
s \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(n)\bigr),}$$
the $n$-th Hirzebruch surface.
