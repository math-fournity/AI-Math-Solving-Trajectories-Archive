Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a Lebesgue point of $f$ (bo
unded measurable) is also a Lebesgue point of $g \circ f$ (with $g$ absolutely c
ontinuous), under the strong definition.

**Answer: Yes, the statement is TRUE.**

**Proof.**

Since $f$ is bounded, there exists $M > 0$ such that $|f(y)| \le M$ for all $y \
in \mathbb{R}^n$. Since $g$ is absolutely continuous, $g$ is continuous, and hen
ce **uniformly continuous** and **bounded** on the compact interval $[-M, M]$. L
et $K := \sup_{t \in [-M,M]} |g(t)|$.

The composition $g \circ f$ is measurable (continuous $\circ$ measurable) and bo
unded by $K$, so $g \circ f \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, making the Le
besgue point question well-posed.

We work with the **strong definition**: $x$ is a Lebesgue point of $f$ if for ev
ery family of measurable sets $E_r$ shrinking nicely to $x$ (i.e., $E_r \subset
B(x,r)$, $|E_r| \ge c\,|B(x,r)|$ for some fixed $c > 0$, and $r \to 0$),
$$\frac{1}{|E_r|}\int_{E_r} |f(y) - f(x)|\,dy \to 0.$$

We must show the same holds for $g \circ f$.

**Key estimate.** Let $\varepsilon > 0$. By uniform continuity of $g$ on $[-M, M
]$, there exists $\delta > 0$ such that for all $a, b \in [-M, M]$ with $|a - b|
 < \delta$, we have $|g(a) - g(b)| < \varepsilon$.

Split the integral over $E_r$ into two parts:

$$\frac{1}{|E_r|}\int_{E_r} |g(f(y)) - g(f(x))|\,dy = \underbrace{\frac{1}{|E_r|
}\int_{E_r \cap \{|f - f(x)| < \delta\}} |g(f(y)) - g(f(x))|\,dy}_{\text{(I)}} +
 \underbrace{\frac{1}{|E_r|}\int_{E_r \cap \{|f - f(x)| \ge \delta\}} |g(f(y)) -
 g(f(x))|\,dy}_{\text{(II)}}.$$

**Term (I):** On the region $\{|f(y) - f(x)| < \delta\}$, both $f(y)$ and $f(x)$
 lie in $[-M, M]$ and differ by less than $\delta$, so $|g(f(y)) - g(f(x))| < \v
arepsilon$ by the choice of $\delta$. Thus:
$$\text{(I)} \le \varepsilon.$$

**Term (II):** Using $|g(f(y)) - g(f(x))| \le 2K$ and Markov's inequality:
$$\text{(II)} \le 2K \cdot \frac{|\{y \in E_r : |f(y) - f(x)| \ge \delta\}|}{|E_
r|} \le \frac{2K}{\delta} \cdot \frac{1}{|E_r|}\int_{E_r} |f(y) - f(x)|\,dy.$$

Since $x$ is a (strong) Lebesgue point of $f$, the integral average on the right
 tends to $0$ as $r \to 0$. Hence there exists $r_0 > 0$ such that for all $r <
r_0$:
$$\text{(II)} < \varepsilon.$$

**Combining:** For all $r < r_0$,
$$\frac{1}{|E_r|}\int_{E_r} |g(f(y)) - g(f(x))|\,dy < 2\varepsilon.$$

Since $\varepsilon > 0$ was arbitrary, the limit is $0$. This holds for every fa
mily of sets shrinking nicely to $x$, so $x$ is a Lebesgue point of $g \circ f$
in the strong sense. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The proof only requires $g$ to be continuous (not absolutely continu
ous). The boundedness of $f$ is essential: it confines the range of $f$ to a com
pact interval $[-M, M]$, on which $g$ is automatically uniformly continuous and
bounded. The absolute continuity hypothesis is stronger than needed.
