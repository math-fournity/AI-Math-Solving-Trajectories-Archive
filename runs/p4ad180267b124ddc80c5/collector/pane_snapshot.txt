# Grothendieck Group of the Ring of Laurent Polynomials over a Field

**Theorem.** Let $k$ be a field and $R = k[t, t^{-1}]$ the ring of Laurent polyn
omials. Then the Grothendieck group $K_0(R)$ of finitely generated projective $R
$-modules is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}$.

## Proof

### Step 1. $R = k[t, t^{-1}]$ is a principal ideal domain.

The polynomial ring $k[t]$ is a Euclidean domain (hence a PID). The ring $R = k[
t, t^{-1}]$ is the localization $S^{-1}k[t]$ where $S = \{1, t, t^2, \dots\}$. A
 standard result in commutative algebra states that any localization of a PID is
 again a PID: if $A$ is a PID and $S \subset A$ is a multiplicative set, then ev
ery ideal of $S^{-1}A$ is of the form $S^{-1}I$ for some ideal $I = (a)$ of $A$,
 and $S^{-1}I = (a/1)$ in $S^{-1}A$. Therefore $R$ is a PID.

### Step 2. Every finitely generated projective module over a PID is free.

Let $P$ be a finitely generated projective $R$-module. Then $P$ is a direct summ
and of a finite-rank free module $R^n$, i.e., there exists $Q$ with $P \oplus Q
\cong R^n$. In particular, $P$ embeds into $R^n$ as a direct summand, hence as a
 submodule. A fundamental theorem for modules over a PID states that **every sub
module of a free module of finite rank is free** (of rank at most the rank of th
e ambient free module). Therefore $P$ is free, i.e., $P \cong R^r$ for some $r \
geq 0$.

### Step 3. Computation of $K_0(R)$.

The Grothendieck group $K_0(R)$ is defined as the Grothendieck completion (group
 completion) of the commutative monoid $\mathcal{P}(R)$ of isomorphism classes o
f finitely generated projective $R$-modules under direct sum.

By Step 2, every finitely generated projective $R$-module is isomorphic to $R^r$
 for a unique $r \in \mathbb{N}$ (the rank, which is well-defined since $R$ is a
 nonzero commutative ring, so $R^m \cong R^n$ implies $m = n$). The direct sum o
peration corresponds to addition of ranks:
$$[R^r] \oplus [R^s] = [R^{r+s}].$$

Therefore the monoid $\mathcal{P}(R)$ is isomorphic to $(\mathbb{N}, +)$ via the
 rank map $[P] \mapsto \mathrm{rank}(P)$. The Grothendieck completion of $(\math
bb{N}, +)$ is $(\mathbb{Z}, +)$.

Hence
$$K_0(k[t, t^{-1}]) \cong \mathbb{Z},$$
generated by the class $[R]$ of the free module of rank one, with the isomorphis
m given by $[P] \mapsto \mathrm{rank}(P)$. $\blacksquare$
