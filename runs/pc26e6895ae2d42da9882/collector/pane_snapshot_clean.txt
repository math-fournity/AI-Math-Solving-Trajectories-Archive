### Explicit form

$$\boxed{\delta(x_1,x_2)=\min(x_1,x_2)}$$

### Proof

Let $F$ be the common CDF of $X_1$ and $X_2$:
$$F(t)=\frac{\exp\!\big((t-\mu)/\tau\big)}{1+\exp\!\big((t-\mu)/\tau\big)},\qqua
d t\in\mathbb R.$$
Since $X_1,X_2$ are i.i.d. logistic$(\mu,\tau)$, both have CDF $F$, and $0<F(t)<
1$ for every $t\in\mathbb R$ (the logistic CDF is strictly increasing with limit
s $0$ and $1$).

**Symmetry.** The minimum is symmetric:
$$\delta(x_1,x_2)=\min(x_1,x_2)=\min(x_2,x_1)=\delta(x_2,x_1),$$
so $\delta$ is a symmetric function as required.

**Stochastic domination.** For every $t\in\mathbb R$, using independence of $X_1
$ and $X_2$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Pr\!\big(\delta(X_1,X_2)\le t\big)
&=\Pr\!\big(\min(X_1,X_2)\le t\big)\\
&=1-\Pr(X_1>t,\;X_2>t)\\
&=1-\big(1-F(t)\big)^{2}\\
&=2F(t)-F(t)^{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
On the other hand $\Pr(X_i\le t)=F(t)$ for $i=1,2$. Comparing,
$$
\Pr\!\big(\delta(X_1,X_2)\le t\big)-\Pr(X_i\le t)
=\big(2F(t)-F(t)^{2}\big)-F(t)
=F(t)\big(1-F(t)\big).
$$
Since $0<F(t)<1$ for all $t\in\mathbb R$,
$$F(t)\big(1-F(t)\big)>0\qquad\forall\,t\in\mathbb R.$$
Hence
$$\Pr\!\big(\delta(X_1,X_2)\le t\big)>\Pr(X_i\le t)\qquad\forall\,t\in\mathbb R,
\quad i=1,2.$$
This is exactly the definition of $\delta(X_1,X_2)$ being stochastically smaller
 (in fact, strictly stochastically smaller) than both $X_1$ and $X_2$.

Therefore $\delta(x_1,x_2)=\min(x_1,x_2)$ is a symmetric function with the requi
red property, giving an explicit form of $\delta$.
