## Proof

We work in the framework of combinatorial species / exponential generating funct
ions (EGFs). A set partition of a finite labeled set is a decomposition of the s
et into pairwise disjoint nonempty blocks, whose collection is unordered. The ad
ditional constraint is that **all block sizes are pairwise distinct**.

**Single block of fixed size.** A block is simply a nonempty set. A block of siz
e exactly $k$ is a $k$-element subset of the label set; its EGF is
$$\frac{x^k}{k!}.$$

**Distinct-size constraint as an independent choice per size.** Since all block
sizes must be distinct, for each integer $k\ge 1$ the partition contains **eithe
r zero or one** block of size $k$. The choice for size $k$ is independent of the
 choice for every other size (the blocks are disjoint and unordered, and the "at
 most one block of size $k$" condition does not interact across different $k$).
The EGF contribution of the size-$k$ slot is therefore
$$1 + \frac{x^k}{k!},$$
where the term $1$ represents "no block of size $k$" and $\frac{x^k}{k!}$ repres
ents "exactly one block of size $k$."

**Product over all sizes.** Combining the independent choices for all $k\ge 1$ v
ia the product rule for EGFs (the product of species corresponds to the product
of EGFs), the EGF for the class of set partitions with all parts of distinct siz
es is
$$F(x) = \prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{x^k}{k!}\right).$$

**Verification by small cases.** Let $a_n = n!\,[x^n]\,F(x)$ be the number of su
ch partitions of $[n]$.

- $n=1$: The only partition is $\{\{1\}\}$, with distinct sizes $\{1\}$. So $a_1
=1$. From the product, $[x^1](1+x)(\cdots)=1$, and $1!\cdot 1 = 1$. ✓

- $n=2$: Partitions are $\{\{1,2\}\}$ (sizes $\{2\}$, valid) and $\{\{1\},\{2\}\
}$ (sizes $\{1,1\}$, invalid). So $a_2=1$. From $(1+x)(1+x^2/2)=1+x+x^2/2+\cdots
$, $[x^2]=1/2$, and $2!\cdot(1/2)=1$. ✓

- $n=3$: Valid partitions are $\{\{1,2,3\}\}$ (1) and $\{\{i\},\{j,k\}\}$ for th
e 3 choices of the singleton (3); $\{\{1\},\{2\},\{3\}\}$ is invalid. So $a_3=4$
. From $(1+x)(1+x^2/2)(1+x^3/6)$, the $x^3$ coefficient is $\tfrac12+\tfrac16=\t
frac23$, and $3!\cdot\tfrac23=4$. ✓

All checks agree, confirming the formula.

### Conclusion

The exponential generating function for the class of set partitions in which all
 parts have distinct sizes is

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{x^k}{k!}\right)}.$$
