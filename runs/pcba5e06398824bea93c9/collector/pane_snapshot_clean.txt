## Proof

**Construction.** For $n \ge 2$ set
$$
z_n = \tfrac{1}{n} + \tfrac{i}{2n^2}, \qquad \bar z_n = \tfrac{1}{n} - \tfrac{i}
{2n^2},
$$
and define, for $x \in (0,1)$,
$$
f(x) \;=\; \sum_{n=2}^{\infty} 2^{-n}\!\left(\frac{1}{x-z_n}+\frac{1}{x-\bar z_n
}\right).
$$

---

**Step 1 — $f$ is real-analytic on $(0,1)$.**

Fix $x_0 \in (0,1)$ and let $\delta = x_0/2$, so the interval $I=(x_0-\delta,x_0
+\delta)=(x_0/2,\,3x_0/2)\subset(0,1)$. Since $z_n,\bar z_n \to 0$, for all larg
e $n$ (say $n\ge N$) we have $1/n < x_0/4$, hence for every $x\in I$
$$
|x-z_n| \;\ge\; |x-\tfrac1n| \;\ge\; \tfrac{x_0}{2}-\tfrac{x_0}{4} \;=\; \tfrac{
x_0}{4},
$$
and likewise $|x-\bar z_n|\ge x_0/4$. The $k$-th derivative of the $n$-th summan
d is bounded in absolute value by
$$
2^{-n}\, k!\,\Big(\tfrac{4}{x_0}\Big)^{k+1},
$$
which is summable in $n$. Hence the series and all its derivatives converge unif
ormly on $I$, so $f$ is represented by a convergent power series around $x_0$. S
ince $x_0$ was arbitrary, $f$ is real-analytic on $(0,1)$. (Each summand is pair
ed with its complex conjugate, so $f$ is real-valued on $(0,1)$.)

---

**Step 2 — $f$ does not extend holomorphically to any strip $S_\varepsilon=(0,1)
+i(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$, $\varepsilon>0$.**

Suppose, for contradiction, that $F$ is holomorphic on $S_\varepsilon$ with $F|_
{(0,1)}=f$. Consider the meromorphic function
$$
M(z) \;=\; \sum_{n=2}^{\infty} 2^{-n}\!\left(\frac{1}{z-z_n}+\frac{1}{z-\bar z_n
}\right),
$$
which converges normally on compact subsets of $\mathbb C\setminus\{z_n,\bar z_n
\}_n$ (the factor $2^{-n}$ dominates the denominators on any compact set avoidin
g the poles). In particular $M$ is meromorphic on $S_\varepsilon$ with poles exa
ctly at those $z_n,\bar z_n$ lying in $S_\varepsilon$.

For all sufficiently large $n$ we have $0<1/n<1$ and $|\operatorname{Im} z_n|=1/
(2n^2)<\varepsilon$, so $z_n,\bar z_n \in S_\varepsilon$. These poles are isolat
ed interior points of $S_\varepsilon$; their only accumulation point is $0$, whi
ch lies on the boundary $\{x=0\}$ of $S_\varepsilon$ and hence is **not** in $S_
\varepsilon$. Therefore
$$
\Omega \;=\; S_\varepsilon \setminus \{z_n,\bar z_n : n\ge 2\}
$$
is a connected open set (removing a closed discrete set from a domain in $\mathb
b C$ preserves connectedness).

Both $F$ and $M$ are holomorphic on $\Omega$, and they agree on the real segment
 $(0,1)\subset\Omega$, which has a limit point in $\Omega$. By the identity theo
rem,
$$
F = M \quad\text{on } \Omega.
$$
But $z_n\in S_\varepsilon$ (for large $n$) is an interior point of $S_\varepsilo
n$ at which $M$ has a pole, so $M(z)\to\infty$ as $z\to z_n$. Since $F=M$ on a p
unctured neighborhood of $z_n$ (contained in $\Omega$), $F$ must also have a pol
e at $z_n$ — contradicting the holomorphicity of $F$ on $S_\varepsilon$.

Hence no holomorphic extension of $f$ to $S_\varepsilon$ exists, for any $\varep
silon>0$.

---

**Conclusion.** The function
$$
f(x)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}2^{-n}\!\left(\frac{1}{x-\frac1n-\frac{i}{2n^2}}+\frac{1
}{x-\frac1n+\frac{i}{2n^2}}\right),\qquad x\in(0,1),
$$
is real-analytic on $(0,1)$ yet admits no holomorphic extension to any complex s
trip $(0,1)+i(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$, $\varepsilon>0$. $\blacksquare$
