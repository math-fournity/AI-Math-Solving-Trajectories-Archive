# Solution: The Eikonal Equation and Its Role in Solving Hyperbolic PDEs

## 1. Setup and the High-Frequency Ansatz

Let $P$ be a hyperbolic partial differential operator of order $m$ with principa
l symbol $H(x,t,\xi,\tau)$. We seek solutions to $Pu = 0$ with prescribed initia
l data on a non-characteristic hypersurface $\Sigma$ (typically $t = 0$).

The central idea of the **geometric optics / WKB method** is to look for solutio
ns of the form

$$u(x,t) = \sum_{j=0}^{\infty} (i\omega)^{-j}\, a_j(x,t)\, e^{i\omega\,\phi(x,t)
}, \qquad \omega \to \infty,$$

where:
- $\phi(x,t)$ is a real-valued **phase function**,
- $a_0, a_1, a_2, \dots$ are **amplitude functions** with $a_0 \not\equiv 0$,
- $\omega$ is a large parameter (frequency).

Substituting this ansatz into $Pu = 0$ and collecting powers of $\omega$ yields
a hierarchy of equations. At the highest order $(i\omega)^m$, the leading term g
ives precisely

$$H\!\left(x, t,\, \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x},\, \frac{\partial \phi}{\par
tial t}\right) = 0.$$

This is the **eikonal equation**. It is a first-order nonlinear PDE for $\phi$ t
hat encodes the propagation of wave fronts.

## 2. Solving the Eikonal Equation via the Method of Characteristics

The eikonal equation is a first-order nonlinear PDE of Hamilton–Jacobi type. It
is solved by the **method of characteristics**, which reduces it to a system of
ODEs.

### 2.1 The Hamiltonian System

Set $p = \partial\phi/\partial x$ and $q = \partial\phi/\partial t$ (the momenta
 conjugate to $x$ and $t$). The eikonal equation is $H(x,t,p,q) = 0$.

The **characteristic (bicharacteristic) equations** are Hamilton's equations:

$$\dot{x} = \frac{\partial H}{\partial p}, \qquad \dot{t} = \frac{\partial H}{\p
artial q}, \qquad \dot{p} = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}, \qquad \dot{q} = -\f
rac{\partial H}{\partial t},$$

where the dot denotes differentiation with respect to a parameter $s$ along the
bicharacteristic curve.

**Key conservation property:** Along any bicharacteristic,

$$\frac{d}{ds} H(x(s), t(s), p(s), q(s)) = \frac{\partial H}{\partial x}\dot{x}
+ \frac{\partial H}{\partial t}\dot{t} + \frac{\partial H}{\partial p}\dot{p} +
\frac{\partial H}{\partial q}\dot{q} = 0,$$

so $H = 0$ is preserved along the flow. The bicharacteristics therefore stay on
the **characteristic variety** $\{(x,t,p,q) : H = 0\}$.

### 2.2 Construction of $\phi$ from Initial Data

Given initial data on the hypersurface $\Sigma = \{t = 0\}$, we proceed as follo
ws:

1. **Prescribe initial phase.** Choose $\phi(x, 0) = \phi_0(x)$ on $\Sigma$, whe
re $\phi_0$ is a smooth function. This determines $p(x,0) = \nabla_x \phi_0(x)$.

2. **Solve for the initial $q$.** The eikonal equation restricted to $t = 0$ giv
es $H(x, 0, \nabla_x \phi_0, q) = 0$. Since $\Sigma$ is non-characteristic (i.e.
, $\partial H / \partial q \neq 0$ on $\Sigma$), the **implicit function theorem
** yields $q = q(x,0)$ as a smooth function of $x$. (Hyperbolicity ensures there
 are real roots $q$; the number of roots corresponds to the number of wave modes
.)

3. **Integrate the Hamiltonian system.** For each point $x_0$ on $\Sigma$, launc
h a bicharacteristic with initial data $(x(0), t(0), p(0), q(0)) = (x_0, 0, \nab
la_x\phi_0(x_0), q(x_0,0))$. Integrate the ODE system to obtain $(x(s), t(s), p(
s), q(s))$.

4. **Recover $\phi$ by integration.** Along each bicharacteristic, the phase is
recovered via

$$\phi(x(s), t(s)) = \phi_0(x_0) + \int_0^s \bigl(p \cdot \dot{x} + q\,\dot{t}\b
igr)\, ds',$$

which is well-defined because $d\phi = p\,dx + q\,dt$ along the characteristic (
this is the compatibility condition guaranteed by the Hamiltonian structure).

5. **Non-caustic region.** As long as the map $(x_0, s) \mapsto (x(s), t(s))$ is
 a diffeomorphism (i.e., no **caustics** form), this construction yields a smoot
h $\phi(x,t)$ in a neighborhood of $\Sigma$. At caustics, the map loses rank and
 $\phi$ becomes multi-valued; one must then use more refined techniques (Maslov
canonical operator, Fourier integral operators).

### 2.3 Summary of the Solution

The phase $\phi$ is obtained by:
- Specifying $\phi_0$ on the initial surface,
- Solving the Hamilton–Jacobi eikonal equation by integrating the bicharacterist
ic ODEs,
- Reconstructing $\phi$ by path integration along characteristics.

The level sets $\{\phi = \text{const}\}$ are the **wave fronts**, and the bichar
acteristic curves $(x(s), t(s))$ are the **rays** along which energy propagates.

## 3. Role in Solving $Pu = 0$ with Initial Conditions

### 3.1 The Transport Equations for Amplitudes

Once $\phi$ is determined, the lower-order terms in the $\omega$-expansion yield
 a recursive system of **transport equations** for the amplitudes $a_j$:

$$(i\omega)^m: \quad H\bigl(x,t,\nabla\phi\bigr)\, a_0 = 0 \quad \text{(eikonal,
 satisfied by construction)},$$

$$(i\omega)^{m-1}: \quad L_1\, a_0 = 0 \quad \text{(first transport equation)},$
$

$$(i\omega)^{m-j}: \quad L_1\, a_j = F_j(a_0, \dots, a_{j-1}) \quad \text{(highe
r transport equations)},$$

where $L_1$ is a first-order linear differential operator (the **transport opera
tor**) along the bicharacteristics, and $F_j$ depends on previously determined a
mplitudes.

Each transport equation is an ODE **along the bicharacteristic curves** and is s
olved with initial data $a_j(x,0)$ determined by the prescribed initial conditio
ns of the PDE.

### 3.2 The Complete Solution Procedure

1. **Solve the eikonal equation** $\to$ obtain $\phi(x,t)$ via characteristics.
This determines the geometry: wave fronts and rays.

2. **Solve the transport equations** $\to$ obtain $a_0, a_1, a_2, \dots$ along t
he rays, using the initial data of the PDE to fix $a_j(x,0)$.

3. **Assemble the asymptotic solution:**

$$u(x,t) \sim \sum_{j=0}^{N} (i\omega)^{-j}\, a_j(x,t)\, e^{i\omega\,\phi(x,t)}.
$$

### 3.3 Conceptual Role

The eikonal equation plays the following roles:

**(a) Geometric skeleton.** It determines the **phase geometry** — the wave fron
ts and rays — independently of the amplitudes. This is the **kinematic** part of
 the solution: where the waves go.

**(b) Separation of kinematics and dynamics.** The eikonal equation (kinematics:
 propagation paths) is decoupled from the transport equations (dynamics: how the
 amplitude evolves along those paths). This separation is the hallmark of the hi
gh-frequency asymptotic approach.

**(c) Reduction from PDE to ODE.** The original PDE $Pu = 0$ (a partial differen
tial equation in $(x,t)$) is reduced to:
- a first-order nonlinear PDE (the eikonal equation), itself reduced to ODEs via
 characteristics, and
- a sequence of first-order linear ODEs (the transport equations) along those ch
aracteristics.

This is a dramatic simplification: the PDE is solved by integrating ODEs.

**(d) Foundation for rigorous theory.** The eikonal equation and its characteris
tics underpin the rigorous theory of **Fourier integral operators** (FIOs). The
operator that maps initial data to the solution $u$ is, to leading order, an FIO
 whose canonical relation is the bicharacteristic flow. The phase $\phi$ is the
**phase function** of the FIO, and the amplitude $a_0$ is its **principal symbol
**. This gives:
- **Propagation of singularities:** singularities of $u$ propagate along bichara
cteristics of $H$;
- **Parametrix construction:** the asymptotic sum provides a **parametrix** — an
 approximate inverse that agrees with the true solution modulo smooth errors.

**(e) Consistency with initial conditions.** The initial data for $\phi$ (i.e.,
$\phi_0$) and for $a_j$ (i.e., $a_j(x,0)$) are chosen so that the asymptotic exp
ansion matches the prescribed Cauchy data of $Pu = 0$ on $\Sigma$. Specifically,
 if the initial data is $u|_{t=0} = g$, then one decomposes $g$ into oscillatory
 components (via Fourier transform), each component corresponding to a choice of
 $\phi_0(x) = x \cdot \xi_0$ and an amplitude, and superposes the resulting para
metrix solutions.

## 4. Concrete Illustration: The Wave Equation

For the wave operator $P = \partial_t^2 - c^2 \Delta_x$, the principal symbol is
 $H = \tau^2 - c^2|\xi|^2$. The eikonal equation is

$$\phi_t^2 = c^2\,|\nabla_x \phi|^2.$$

The two roots $\phi_t = \pm c\,|\nabla_x \phi|$ correspond to the two wave modes
 (forward and backward propagating). For each, the bicharacteristics satisfy $\d
ot{x} = \pm c\,\xi/|\xi|$, $\dot{t} = \pm 2\tau$, giving rays traveling at speed
 $c$. The transport equations reduce to $a_0$ being constant along rays (in the
homogeneous case), recovering the classical fact that wave amplitude is transpor
ted along characteristics.

## 5. Conclusion

The eikonal equation $H(x,t,\partial_x\phi,\partial_t\phi) = 0$ is solved for $\
phi$ by the **method of characteristics**: one prescribes $\phi$ on the initial
surface, solves the associated Hamiltonian ODE system (bicharacteristic equation
s), and reconstructs $\phi$ by integration along these curves. The resulting pha
se function $\phi$ determines the **wave front geometry** (kinematics), while th
e amplitudes are determined by **transport equations** (dynamics) solved along t
he same characteristics with initial data from the Cauchy problem. Together, the
 eikonal equation and transport equations reduce the PDE $Pu=0$ to a system of O
DEs, produce an asymptotic (parametrix) solution, and form the foundation of the
 theory of propagation of singularities and Fourier integral operators for hyper
bolic equations.
