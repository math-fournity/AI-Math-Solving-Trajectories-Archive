**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

Recall that a $C^*$-algebra is a Banach $\ast$-algebra satisfying the $C^*$-iden
tity $\|a^*a\|=\|a\|^2$ for all $a$. We show the given condition forces this ide
ntity.

We use two standard facts valid in any Banach algebra (unital or not, via the un
itization):
- $\rho(x^2)=\rho(x)^2$ for every $x$ (since $\sigma(x^2)=\{\lambda^2:\lambda\in
\sigma(x)\}$, or equivalently from the limit formula $\rho(x)=\lim_n\|x^n\|^{1/n
}$ applied to the subsequence $2n$);
- $\rho(xy)=\rho(yx)$ for all $x,y$ (since $\sigma(xy)\setminus\{0\}=\sigma(yx)\
setminus\{0\}$).

**Step 1: Self-adjoint elements satisfy $\|a\|=\rho(a)$.**

Let $a=a^*$. Then $a^*a=a^2$, so the hypothesis gives
$$\|a\|^2=\rho(a^*a)=\rho(a^2)=\rho(a)^2.$$
Since norms and spectral radii are non-negative, $\|a\|=\rho(a)$.

**Step 2: The $C^*$-identity holds.**

For arbitrary $a\in A$, the element $a^*a$ is self-adjoint. By Step 1,
$$\|a^*a\|=\rho(a^*a).$$
By the hypothesis $\rho(a^*a)=\|a\|^2$, hence
$$\|a^*a\|=\|a\|^2\qquad\text{for all }a\in A.$$

**Step 3: The involution is isometric (consistency check).**

From the $C^*$-identity just established, $\|a\|^2=\|a^*a\|\le\|a^*\|\|a\|$, so
$\|a\|\le\|a^*\|$; applying this to $a^*$ yields $\|a^*\|\le\|a\|$. Thus $\|a^*\
|=\|a\|$. (Equivalently, directly from the hypothesis and $\rho(a^*a)=\rho(aa^*)
$: $\|a\|^2=\rho(a^*a)=\rho(aa^*)=\|a^*\|^2$.)

Therefore $(A,\ast,\|\cdot\|)$ satisfies the $C^*$-identity $\|a^*a\|=\|a\|^2$ w
ith an isometric involution, i.e. it is a $C^*$-algebra. $\blacksquare$
