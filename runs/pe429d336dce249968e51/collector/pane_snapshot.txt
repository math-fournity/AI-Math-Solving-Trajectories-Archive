## Solution

**Setup.** The rationals in $[0,1]$ have Lebesgue measure $0$, so they contribut
e nothing to the integral. We integrate only over the irrationals.

For an irrational $x \in [0,1]$, the decimal expansion $x = 0.d_1 d_2 d_3\ldots$
 is unique (no terminating/ambiguous expansion issue, since those only affect ra
tionals). The number $n$ of leading zeros is the largest $n$ such that $d_1 = \c
dots = d_n = 0$ and $d_{n+1} \neq 0$ (with $n=0$ if $d_1 \neq 0$).

**Decomposition.** Let
$$A_n = \{x \in [0,1]\setminus\mathbb{Q} : x \text{ has exactly } n \text{ leadi
ng zeros}\}.$$
The condition $d_1=\cdots=d_n=0$ means $x \in [0, 10^{-n})$, and $d_1=\cdots=d_{
n+1}=0$ means $x \in [0, 10^{-(n+1)})$. Hence
$$A_n = \big(\text{irrationals in }[10^{-(n+1)},\, 10^{-n})\big),$$
whose Lebesgue measure is
$$m(A_n) = 10^{-n} - 10^{-(n+1)} = 9\cdot 10^{-(n+1)}.$$
On $A_n$ we have $f(x) = 9^n$.

**Computing the integral.** The $A_n$ are disjoint measurable sets covering all
irrationals in $[0,1]$ except possibly $\{0\}$ (which is rational anyway). Since
 $f \geq 0$, by the monotone convergence theorem (or basic properties of the Leb
esgue integral of nonnegative simple-function limits),
$$\int_0^1 f\,dx = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} 9^n \cdot m(A_n) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} 9^
n \cdot 9\cdot 10^{-(n+1)} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\tfrac{9}{10}\right)^{n+1}
.$$
This is a geometric series:
$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\tfrac{9}{10}\right)^{n+1} = \frac{9/10}{1-9/10} = \f
rac{9/10}{1/10} = 9.$$

**Verification (first few terms).** $0.9 + 0.81 + 0.729 + \cdots = 0.9\cdot\frac
{1}{1-0.9} = 9$. ✓

**Answer.**

$$\boxed{\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx = 9}$$
