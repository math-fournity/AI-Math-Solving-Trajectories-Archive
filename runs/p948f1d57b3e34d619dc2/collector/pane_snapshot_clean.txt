## Proof

**Setup and notation.** Let $\mu = \mathbb{E}[X_1]$, $\sigma^2 = \operatorname{V
ar}(X_1)$, and $\mu_4 = \mathbb{E}[(X_1 - \mu)^4]$. The assumption $\mathbb{E}[X
_1^4] < \infty$ guarantees $\mu_4 < \infty$. Let $z_{\alpha/2}$ denote the upper
 $\alpha/2$ quantile of $N(0,1)$ (i.e., $P(Z > z_{\alpha/2}) = \alpha/2$), and $
\Phi$ the standard normal CDF. We use the convention that $\chi^2_{\nu, p}$ is t
he upper $p$-quantile of $\chi^2_\nu$, so $\chi^2_{n-1,\alpha/2} > \chi^2_{n-1,1
-\alpha/2}$, giving $A_n < B_n$.

**Step 1: Reformulate the event.** Define $W_n = \frac{(n-1)S_n^2}{\sigma^2}$. T
he event $\sigma^2 \in (A_n, B_n)$ is:

$$\frac{(n-1)S_n^2}{\chi^2_{n-1,\alpha/2}} < \sigma^2 < \frac{(n-1)S_n^2}{\chi^2
_{n-1,1-\alpha/2}}$$

Dividing by $\sigma^2$ and substituting $W_n$:

$$\frac{W_n}{\chi^2_{n-1,\alpha/2}} < 1 < \frac{W_n}{\chi^2_{n-1,1-\alpha/2}}$$

which is equivalent to:

$$\chi^2_{n-1,1-\alpha/2} < W_n < \chi^2_{n-1,\alpha/2} \tag{1}$$

So $P(\sigma^2 \in (A_n, B_n)) = P\!\left(\chi^2_{n-1,1-\alpha/2} < W_n < \chi^2
_{n-1,\alpha/2}\right)$.

**Step 2: Asymptotic distribution of $W_n$.** Write $Y_i = (X_i - \mu)^2$, so $\
mathbb{E}[Y_i] = \sigma^2$ and $\operatorname{Var}(Y_i) = \mu_4 - \sigma^4$. We
have:

$$S_n^2 = \frac{n}{n-1}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i - (\bar{X}-\mu)^2\right
]$$

By the classical CLT applied to $Y_i$: $\sqrt{n}\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum Y_i - \s
igma^2\right) \xrightarrow{d} N(0,\, \mu_4 - \sigma^4)$.

Since $\sqrt{n}(\bar{X}-\mu) = O_p(1)$, we get $\sqrt{n}(\bar{X}-\mu)^2 = \frac{
1}{\sqrt{n}}[\sqrt{n}(\bar{X}-\mu)]^2 = o_p(1)$. Also $\frac{n}{n-1} \to 1$ and
$\sqrt{n}\sigma^2\!\left(\frac{n}{n-1}-1\right) \to 0$. By Slutsky's theorem:

$$\sqrt{n}(S_n^2 - \sigma^2) \xrightarrow{d} N\!\left(0,\, \mu_4 - \sigma^4\righ
t) \tag{2}$$

Now $W_n - (n-1) = \frac{(n-1)(S_n^2 - \sigma^2)}{\sigma^2}$, so:

$$\frac{W_n - (n-1)}{\sqrt{2(n-1)}} = \sqrt{\frac{n-1}{n}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{n}(
S_n^2 - \sigma^2)}{\sigma^2\sqrt{2}} \xrightarrow{d} N\!\left(0,\, \frac{\mu_4 -
 \sigma^4}{2\sigma^4}\right) \tag{3}$$

**Step 3: Asymptotic expansion of the chi-square quantiles.** If $Y \sim \chi^2_
{n-1}$, then $\frac{Y-(n-1)}{\sqrt{2(n-1)}} \xrightarrow{d} N(0,1)$ by the CLT (
since $\chi^2_{n-1} = \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} Z_i^2$ with $Z_i \stackrel{iid}{\sim} N(0
,1)$). By convergence of quantiles:

$$\frac{\chi^2_{n-1,\alpha/2} - (n-1)}{\sqrt{2(n-1)}} \to z_{\alpha/2}, \qquad \
frac{\chi^2_{n-1,1-\alpha/2} - (n-1)}{\sqrt{2(n-1)}} \to z_{1-\alpha/2} = -z_{\a
lpha/2} \tag{4}$$

**Step 4: Take the limit.** Dividing the event (1) by $\sqrt{2(n-1)}$ after subt
racting $(n-1)$:

$$\frac{\chi^2_{n-1,1-\alpha/2}-(n-1)}{\sqrt{2(n-1)}} < \frac{W_n-(n-1)}{\sqrt{2
(n-1)}} < \frac{\chi^2_{n-1,\alpha/2}-(n-1)}{\sqrt{2(n-1)}}$$

Using (3) and (4) together with Slutsky's theorem (the boundary terms converge t
o constants $\pm z_{\alpha/2}$):

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} P(\sigma^2 \in (A_n, B_n)) = P\!\left(-z_{\alpha/2} < Z < z_
{\alpha/2}\right)$$

where $Z \sim N\!\left(0,\, \frac{\mu_4 - \sigma^4}{2\sigma^4}\right)$. Standard
izing with $Z = \sqrt{\frac{\mu_4-\sigma^4}{2\sigma^4}}\, Z_0$, $Z_0 \sim N(0,1)
$:

$$P\!\left(-z_{\alpha/2} < Z < z_{\alpha/2}\right) = P\!\left(-\frac{z_{\alpha/2
}}{\sqrt{(\mu_4-\sigma^4)/(2\sigma^4)}} < Z_0 < \frac{z_{\alpha/2}}{\sqrt{(\mu_4
-\sigma^4)/(2\sigma^4)}}\right)$$

$$= 2\,\Phi\!\left(z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{2\sigma^4}{\mu_4 - \sigma^4}}\right)
- 1$$

**Verification under normality.** If $X_1 \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2)$, then $\mu_4 =
3\sigma^4$, so $\frac{2\sigma^4}{\mu_4 - \sigma^4} = \frac{2\sigma^4}{2\sigma^4}
 = 1$, and the limit becomes $2\Phi(z_{\alpha/2}) - 1 = 1 - \alpha$, recovering
the exact coverage of the chi-square interval. $\checkmark$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\lim_{n \to \infty} P\!\left(\sigma^2 \in (A_n, B_n)\right) = 2\,\Phi\!
\left(z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{2\sigma^4}{\mu_4 - \sigma^4}}\right) - 1}$$

where $\mu_4 = \mathbb{E}[(X_1 - \mu)^4]$, $\sigma^2 = \operatorname{Var}(X_1)$,
 $z_{\alpha/2}$ is the upper $\alpha/2$ quantile of the standard normal, and $\P
hi$ is the standard normal CDF. Equivalently, in terms of the kurtosis $\kappa =
 \mu_4/\sigma^4$:

$$= 2\,\Phi\!\left(z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\kappa - 1}}\right) - 1.$$
