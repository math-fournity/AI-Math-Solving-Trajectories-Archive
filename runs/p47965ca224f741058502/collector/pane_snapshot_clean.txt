# Solution

**Claim.** Yes. For every Banach space $B$ there exists a Hilbert space $H$ that
 is homeomorphic to $B$.

The argument splits into two cases (finite-dimensional and infinite-dimensional)
, the second relying on the Toruńczyk–Anderson–Kadec theorem from infinite-dimen
sional topology.

---

## Case 1: $B$ is finite-dimensional, $\dim B = n < \infty$.

A finite-dimensional real Banach space is, as a topological vector space, isomor
phic (hence homeomorphic) to $\mathbb{R}^n$ equipped with any norm — all norms o
n a finite-dimensional space are equivalent, and the underlying vector space is
$\mathbb{R}^n$ once a basis is chosen. Concretely, fixing a basis $e_1,\dots,e_n
$ gives a linear homeomorphism
$$B \;\longrightarrow\; \mathbb{R}^n,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^n a_i e_i \;\mapsto\; (a_
1,\dots,a_n).$$
Now $\mathbb{R}^n$ with its standard inner product $\langle x,y\rangle = \sum_i
x_i y_i$ is the $n$-dimensional (real) Hilbert space. Thus $H = \mathbb{R}^n$ is
 a Hilbert space homeomorphic to $B$.

---

## Case 2: $B$ is infinite-dimensional.

Let $\kappa = d(B)$ be the **density character** of $B$ — the smallest cardinali
ty of a dense subset. Since $B$ is infinite-dimensional, $\kappa \ge \aleph_0$.

We use the following deep theorem of infinite-dimensional topology.

> **Theorem (Toruńczyk, 1981).** *Two Banach spaces are homeomorphic if and only
 if they have the same density character. Moreover, every Banach space of densit
y character $\kappa$ is homeomorphic to the Hilbert space $\ell^2(\kappa)$ of th
e same density character.*

A few words on what underlies this. The separable case is the classical **Anders
on–Kadec theorem**: every infinite-dimensional separable Fréchet space (in parti
cular every infinite-dimensional separable Banach space) is homeomorphic to $\ma
thbb{R}^{\omega}$, the countable product of lines, which Anderson showed is home
omorphic to the separable Hilbert space $\ell^2$. Toruńczyk later extended this
to arbitrary density characters via his **characterization of Hilbert spaces by
the discrete approximation property**: a complete metrizable topological vector
space $X$ is homeomorphic to the Hilbert space of density $d(X)$ precisely when,
 roughly, every map from a "small" polyhedron into $X$ can be uniformly approxim
ated by maps whose images are locally finite Z-sets. He verified this property f
or all Banach spaces, and showed that the homeomorphism type of a Hilbert space
is completely determined by its density character. Combining these two facts yie
lds the theorem above.

Applying the theorem to our $B$: let
$$H \;=\; \ell^2(\kappa) \;=\; \Bigl\{x = (x_\alpha)_{\alpha\in A} : \sum_{\alph
a\in A} |x_\alpha|^2 < \infty\Bigr\},\qquad |A| = \kappa,$$
the Hilbert space of square-summable families indexed by a set of cardinality $\
kappa$, with inner product $\langle x,y\rangle = \sum_\alpha x_\alpha y_\alpha$.
 This is a Hilbert space of density character $\kappa$, and by Toruńczyk's theor
em it is homeomorphic to $B$.

---

## Conclusion

In both cases we have produced a Hilbert space $H$ homeomorphic to $B$:

- $\dim B < \infty$: take $H = \mathbb{R}^{\dim B}$.
- $\dim B = \infty$: take $H = \ell^2(d(B))$, using the Anderson–Kadec (separabl
e) / Toruńczyk (general) theorem.

Therefore **for every Banach space $B$ there exists a Hilbert space $H$ homeomor
phic to $B$**, and $H$ may be chosen to have the same density character as $B$.
