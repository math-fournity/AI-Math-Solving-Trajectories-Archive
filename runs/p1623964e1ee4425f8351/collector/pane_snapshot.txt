# Solution

**Answer: No.**

## Proof

**Definition.** A splitting field of a polynomial $f(x) \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ over
$\mathbb{Q}$ is the smallest field extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ in which $f$ splits
 completely into linear factors. Such a field is generated over $\mathbb{Q}$ by
the (finitely many) roots of $f$, hence is always a **finite** extension of $\ma
thbb{Q}$.

**Step 1: $L$ is an infinite extension of $\mathbb{Q}$.**

The field $L = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p} : p \text{ prime})$ contains, for every $n \g
eq 1$, the subfield
$$K_n = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p_1}, \sqrt{p_2}, \ldots, \sqrt{p_n})$$
where $p_1, \ldots, p_n$ are the first $n$ primes. A classical result (proved by
 induction using the fact that $\sqrt{p_{n+1}} \notin \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p_1}, \ld
ots, \sqrt{p_n})$, which follows from unique factorization and the linear indepe
ndence of square roots of distinct squarefree integers over $\mathbb{Q}$) gives:
$$[K_n : \mathbb{Q}] = 2^n.$$
Since $K_n \subseteq L$ for every $n$, we have $[L : \mathbb{Q}] \geq 2^n$ for e
very $n$, so
$$[L : \mathbb{Q}] = \infty.$$

**Step 2: A splitting field of a polynomial is always finite.**

If $f(x) \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ has degree $d$, then $f$ has at most $d$ roots $\alp
ha_1, \ldots, \alpha_d$, and its splitting field is $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_1, \ldots
, \alpha_d)$, which is a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ (of degree at most $d!
$).

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

Since $L$ has infinite degree over $\mathbb{Q}$, it cannot equal the splitting f
ield of any polynomial $f(x) \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$. Therefore $L$ is **not** a spli
tting field over $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Remark.** Although $L$ is not a splitting field of any single polynomial, it *
is* a normal (Galois) extension of $\mathbb{Q}$: it is the splitting field of th
e **infinite family** $\{x^2 - p : p \text{ prime}\}$. Indeed, every element $\a
lpha \in L$ lies in some finite subextension $K_n = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p_1}, \ldot
s, \sqrt{p_n})$, which is a Galois extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ (with Galois group
$(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^n$). Hence the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\m
athbb{Q}$ splits completely in $K_n \subseteq L$, confirming normality. However,
 normality as an infinite extension does not make $L$ the splitting field of a s
ingle polynomial.
