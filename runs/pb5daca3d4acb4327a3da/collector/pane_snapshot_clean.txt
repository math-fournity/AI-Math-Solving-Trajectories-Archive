## Proof

 solution sketch, or formalization notes. Proceeding.

Let $K$ be an algebraic number field of degree $n = r_1 + 2r_2$ over $\mathbb{Q}
$, with $r_1$ real embeddings, $r = r_2$ complex (conjugate) pairs, discriminant
 $D$, and ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$. Define the **Minkowski bound**

$$
M_K \;=\; \left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{r_2}\sqrt{|D|}.
$$

(Here $r$ in the problem is $r_2$, the number of complex places.) We describe a
procedure that produces a maximal set of pairwise-nonequivalent integral divisor
s $\mathfrak{a}$ with $N(\mathfrak{a}) \le M_K$, and we prove its correctness.

### The procedure

**Step 1 — Compute the bound.** Compute $M_K = (2/\pi)^{r_2}\sqrt{|D|}$ and set
$B = \lfloor M_K \rfloor$.

**Step 2 — Enumerate all integral ideals of norm $\le M_K$.** For each integer $
1 \le n \le B$:
- Factor $n = \prod p_i^{a_i}$.
- For each prime $p \mid n$, factor $p\mathcal{O}_K = \prod_j \mathfrak{p}_{j}^{
e_{j}}$ into prime ideals of $\mathcal{O}_K$ (via the Dedekind–Kummer / factorin
g-mod-$p$ algorithm, using a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis of $\mathcal{O}_K$ and the minim
al polynomial of a primitive element).
- Form all products $\prod_{i,j} \mathfrak{p}_{ij}^{b_{ij}}$ with $0 \le b_{ij}
\le a_i \cdot e_{j}^{-1}$-adjusted exponents such that the resulting norm equals
 $n$. (The norm of $\mathfrak{p}_{ij}$ is $p_i^{f_{ij}}$ where $f_{ij}$ is the r
esidue degree; one solves $\sum_j b_{ij} f_{ij} = a_i$ for each $i$.)

Collect every integral ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ obtained this way into a finite set

$$
S \;=\; \{\,\mathfrak{a} \trianglelefteq \mathcal{O}_K \;:\; N(\mathfrak{a}) \le
 M_K\,\}.
$$

**Step 3 — Partition into ideal classes.** Define the equivalence relation on $S
$:

$$
\mathfrak{a} \sim \mathfrak{b} \;\iff\; \mathfrak{a}\,\mathfrak{b}^{-1} \text{ i
s a principal fractional ideal } (\alpha),\;\alpha \in K^{\times}.
$$

This is exactly the usual ideal-class equivalence. Test equivalence concretely b
y computing, for each pair, whether $\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}^{-1}$ is principal
 — e.g. via Hermite/Pohst reduced-basis enumeration of the lattice $\mathfrak{a}
\mathfrak{b}^{-1}$ seeking a generator of norm $1$, or by comparing class invari
ants.

**Step 4 — Select one representative per class.** Pick one ideal from each equiv
alence class of $S$. Call the resulting set

$$
\mathcal{R} \;=\; \{\mathfrak{a}_1, \dots, \mathfrak{a}_h\}.
$$

### Correctness

**Claim 1 (Finiteness of $S$).** For each $n \le B$ there are only finitely many
 integral ideals of norm $n$: they correspond to the finitely many ways of distr
ibuting the prime-ideal factorization of $n\mathcal{O}_K$. Hence $S$ is finite a
nd Step 2 terminates.

**Claim 2 (Pairwise nonequivalence of $\mathcal{R}$).** By construction we selec
ted at most one element from each equivalence class, so no two elements of $\mat
hcal{R}$ are equivalent: $\mathfrak{a}_i \not\sim \mathfrak{a}_j$ for $i \ne j$.

**Claim 3 (Maximality of $\mathcal{R}$).** Let $\mathfrak{c}$ be any integral di
visor with $N(\mathfrak{c}) \le M_K$. Then $\mathfrak{c} \in S$, so $\mathfrak{c
}$ lies in some equivalence class of $S$, from which $\mathcal{R}$ contains a re
presentative $\mathfrak{a}_k \sim \mathfrak{c}$. Therefore $\mathfrak{c}$ is equ
ivalent to an element already in $\mathcal{R}$, and adding $\mathfrak{c}$ would
violate pairwise nonequivalence. Hence $\mathcal{R}$ is maximal among pairwise-n
onequivalent subsets of $\{ \mathfrak{a} : N(\mathfrak{a}) \le M_K \}$.

**Claim 4 (Completeness — $\mathcal{R}$ represents every ideal class).** By **Mi
nkowski's lattice-point theorem** applied to the lattice $\sigma(\mathfrak{a}) \
subset \mathbb{R}^{r_1} \times \mathbb{C}^{r_2} \cong \mathbb{R}^n$ (where $\sig
ma$ is the Minkowski embedding), every ideal class $[\mathfrak{b}] \in \mathrm{C
l}(K)$ contains an integral representative $\mathfrak{a}$ with

$$
N(\mathfrak{a}) \;\le\; \left(\frac{4}{\pi}\right)^{r_2}\frac{n!}{n^n}\sqrt{|D|}
 \;\le\; \left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{r_2}\sqrt{|D|} \;=\; M_K,
$$

the last inequality being the standard simplification $\frac{n!}{n^n}2^{r_1+r_2}
 \le 1$ (equivalently $n!/n^n \le (1/2)^{r_1}(1/4)^{r_2}$, which follows from $n
! \le n^n / 2^{r_1+r_2}$ after noting $r_1 + 2r_2 = n$). Consequently every clas
s $[\mathfrak{b}]$ has a representative inside $S$, so the equivalence classes o
f $S$ are **all** $h_K$ classes of $\mathrm{Cl}(K)$. Therefore

$$
|\mathcal{R}| \;=\; h_K,
$$

the class number of $K$, and $\mathcal{R}$ is a complete set of representatives
of the ideal class group.

### Summary

To find a maximal set of pairwise-nonequivalent integral divisors satisfying $N(
\mathfrak{a}) \le (2/\pi)^r\sqrt{|D|}$:

1. Compute the Minkowski bound $M_K = (2/\pi)^{r_2}\sqrt{|D|}$.
2. Enumerate all integral ideals of $\mathcal{O}_K$ with norm $\le M_K$ by facto
ring each $n \le \lfloor M_K\rfloor$ and lifting its prime factorization to prim
e ideals of $\mathcal{O}_K$.
3. Partition this finite set by ideal-class equivalence ($\mathfrak{a}\sim\mathf
rak{b} \iff \mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}^{-1}$ principal).
4. Pick one representative from each class.

The resulting set is maximal (no further ideal with norm $\le M_K$ can be added
without creating an equivalent pair) and, by Minkowski's theorem, it is in fact
a complete set of representatives of $\mathrm{Cl}(K)$, hence of cardinality $h_K
$.
