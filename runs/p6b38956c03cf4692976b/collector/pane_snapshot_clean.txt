## Solution

The "Complexity Rating" is the function Ω(n) — the total number of prime factors
 of n counted with multiplicity. I need the smallest positive integer n such tha
t Ω(n) = 4 and Ω(n+1) = 4.

### Strategy

I systematically enumerate all integers with Ω = 4 up to a sufficient bound, the
n check for consecutive pairs.

A number has Ω = 4 exactly when it takes one of these forms:
- **p⁴** (one prime to the 4th power)
- **p³q** (one prime cubed times one other prime)
- **p²q²** (two primes each squared)
- **p²qr** (one prime squared times two distinct other primes)
- **pqrs** (four distinct primes)

### Enumeration of all Ω = 4 numbers up to 136

**p⁴ form:** 2⁴=16, 3⁴=81 (5⁴=625 exceeds bound)

**p³q form:** 2³·3=24, 2³·5=40, 2³·7=56, 2³·11=88, 2³·13=104, 2³·17=136, 3³·2=54
, 3³·5=135 (3³·7=189 exceeds)

**p²q² form:** 2²·3²=36, 2²·5²=100 (2²·7²=196 exceeds)

**p²qr form:** 2²·3·5=60, 2²·3·7=84, 2²·3·11=132, 3²·2·5=90, 3²·2·7=126 (next: 2
²·3·13=156 exceeds)

**pqrs form:** smallest is 2·3·5·7=210 (exceeds bound)

### Complete sorted list

16, 24, 36, 40, 54, 56, 60, 81, 84, 88, 90, 100, 104, 126, 132, **135**, **136**

### Checking for consecutive pairs

Scanning the sorted list for adjacent integers:
- (54, 56): differ by 2 — not consecutive
- (88, 90): differ by 2 — not consecutive
- **(135, 136): differ by 1 — consecutive! ✓**

No earlier consecutive pair exists.

### Verification

- **135 = 3 × 3 × 3 × 5** → Ω(135) = 4 ✓
- **136 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 17** → Ω(136) = 4 ✓

Both are confirmed to have Complexity Rating exactly 4, and they are consecutive
 integers.

### Answer

The identification number of the smaller container is **n = 135**.
