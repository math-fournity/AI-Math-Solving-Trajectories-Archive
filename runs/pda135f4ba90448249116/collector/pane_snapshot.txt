Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every reducible representat
ion (not necessarily decomposable) must have a non-scalar endomorphism commuting
 with the group action.

**Answer: No.** Here is a counterexample.

---

**Proof.**

Let $G$ be the affine group of the complex line:
$$G = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} : a \in \mathbb{C}^*,
\, b \in \mathbb{C} \right\} \subset \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{C}).$$

This is a group (identity at $a{=}1, b{=}0$; products and inverses are readily v
erified). Let $V = \mathbb{C}^2$ and let $D: G \to \mathrm{GL}(V)$ be the inclus
ion homomorphism.

**$D$ is reducible.** The line $W = \mathbb{C}\cdot e_1$ is $G$-invariant, since
$$D(g)\,e_1 = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0
 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} a \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \in W \quad\text{for all }
 g \in G.$$

**$D$ is indecomposable (not a direct sum of invariant subspaces).** Suppose $V
= W \oplus W'$ with $W'$ $G$-invariant. Then $W' = \mathbb{C}\cdot v$ for some $
v=(x,y)^T$ with $y \neq 0$. Since $G$ acts on the quotient $V/W$ by the trivial
character ($D(g)$ acts as $1$ on $V/W$), invariance of $W'$ requires $D(g)v = v$
 for all $g$:
$$\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatri
x} = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} \implies (a-1)x + by = 0 \quad \text{f
or all } a\in\mathbb{C}^*,\, b\in\mathbb{C}.$$
Setting $b=0$ and varying $a$ forces $x=0$; then $by=0$ for all $b$ forces $y=0$
, contradicting $y\neq 0$. So no complementary invariant subspace exists.

**The commutant $\mathrm{End}_G(V)$ is $\mathbb{C}\cdot\mathrm{Id}$.** Let $T =
\begin{pmatrix} p & q \\ r & s \end{pmatrix}$ satisfy $TD(g) = D(g)T$ for every
$g = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Computing both products:

$$TD(g) = \begin{pmatrix} pa & pb+q \\ ra & rb+s \end{pmatrix}, \qquad D(g)T = \
begin{pmatrix} ap+br & aq+bs \\ r & s \end{pmatrix}.$$

Equating entries:

- **(2,1)-entry:** $ra = r$ for all $a \in \mathbb{C}^*$, so $r = 0$.
- **(1,2)-entry:** $pb + q = aq + bs$ for all $a, b$, i.e., $b(p - s) = q(a - 1)
$. The left side depends only on $b$, the right only on $a$; since $a$ and $b$ r
ange independently, both sides are identically zero: $p = s$ and $q = 0$.
- The remaining entries are then automatically satisfied.

Therefore $T = p\,\mathrm{Id}_V$ for some $p \in \mathbb{C}$, and $\mathrm{End}_
G(V) = \mathbb{C}\cdot\mathrm{Id}_V$ contains **no non-scalar map**.

**Conclusion.** The representation $D$ of $G$ on $V = \mathbb{C}^2$ is reducible
 (the line $\mathbb{C}\cdot e_1$ is invariant) yet every linear map commuting wi
th all $D(g)$ is a scalar multiple of the identity. Hence the answer to the ques
tion is **no**: reducibility alone does not guarantee the existence of a non-sca
lar commuting endomorphism.

The key obstruction is that the composition factors $W \cong (\text{character }
a)$ and $V/W \cong (\text{trivial character})$ are **non-isomorphic** irreducibl
es joined by a **non-split extension**, which forces any $G$-endomorphism to act
 by the same scalar on both factors (a non-scalar endomorphism would split the e
xtension, yielding decomposability). $\blacksquare$
