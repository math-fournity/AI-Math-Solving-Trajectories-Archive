## Solution

**Setup.** Two robots independently and uniformly choose crates from $\{1, 2, \l
dots, 60\}$. There are $60 \times 60 = 3600$ equally likely ordered pairs $(a, b
)$. We need the product $ab$ to be divisible by $60 = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5$.

**Key reduction.** For a fixed choice $a$, the product $ab$ is divisible by $60$
 if and only if $b$ is a multiple of $\frac{60}{\gcd(a, 60)}$. Indeed, writing $
g = \gcd(a, 60)$, $a = g a'$, $60 = g m$ with $\gcd(a', m) = 1$, the condition $
60 \mid ab$ becomes $m \mid a' b$, which (since $\gcd(a',m)=1$) is equivalent to
 $m \mid b$, where $m = 60/g$.

The number of multiples of $m = 60/g$ in $\{1, \ldots, 60\}$ is $60/m = g = \gcd
(a, 60)$.

Therefore the total number of favorable pairs is
$$\sum_{a=1}^{60} \gcd(a, 60).$$

**Computing the sum.** Group values of $a$ by $d = \gcd(a, 60)$. The number of $
a \in \{1,\ldots,60\}$ with $\gcd(a,60) = d$ is $\varphi(60/d)$. Using the divis
ors of $60$:

| $d$ | $60/d$ | $\varphi(60/d)$ | $d \cdot \varphi(60/d)$ |
|-----|--------|------------------|------------------------|
| 1   | 60     | 16               | 16                     |
| 2   | 30     | 8                | 16                     |
| 3   | 20     | 8                | 24                     |
| 4   | 15     | 8                | 32                     |
| 5   | 12     | 4                | 20                     |
| 6   | 10     | 4                | 24                     |
| 10  | 6      | 2                | 20                     |
| 12  | 5      | 4                | 48                     |
| 15  | 4      | 2                | 30                     |
| 20  | 3      | 2                | 40                     |
| 30  | 2      | 1                | 30                     |
| 60  | 1      | 1                | 60                     |

The counts $\varphi(60/d)$ sum to $16+8+8+8+4+4+2+4+2+2+1+1 = 60$ ✓ (every $a$ c
ounted once).

The weighted sum is
$$16+16+24+32+20+24+20+48+30+40+30+60 = 360.$$

**Verification via inclusion–exclusion.** Let $A, B, C$ be the sets of pairs whe
re $4 \nmid ab$, $3 \nmid ab$, $5 \nmid ab$ respectively. Computing each by trac
king 2-adic valuations among the relevant subsets:
- $|A| = 1800$, $|B| = 1600$, $|C| = 2304$
- $|A\cap B| = 800$, $|A\cap C| = 1152$, $|B\cap C| = 1024$
- $|A\cap B\cap C| = 512$

Then
$$|A\cup B\cup C| = 1800+1600+2304-800-1152-1024+512 = 3240,$$
so favorable pairs $= 3600 - 3240 = 360$, confirming the direct count.

**Final probability.**
$$\frac{360}{3600} = \frac{1}{10}.$$
