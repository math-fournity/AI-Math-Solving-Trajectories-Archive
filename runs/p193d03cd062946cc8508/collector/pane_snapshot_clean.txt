Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest number of teams $n_0$ such
that some team $T$ can simultaneously have **strictly the most points** and **st
rictly the fewest wins**.

## Proof

**Setup.** Let team $T$ have $w$ wins, $d$ draws, $\ell$ losses ($w+d+\ell = n-1
$), and points $p = 3w+d$. We need $p > p_i$ for every other team $i$, and $w <
w_i$ for every other team $i$ (so each has $\geq w+1$ wins).

**Necessary Condition 1: $d \geq 5$.** Every other team has $\geq w+1$ wins, giv
ing $\geq 3(w+1)$ points from wins alone. Any team that draws with $T$ gets 1 ad
ditional point, totaling $\geq 3w+4$. For $T$ to strictly exceed this: $3w+d > 3
w+4$, so $d \geq 5$. (Since $d \geq 1$, at least one team draws with $T$.)

**Necessary Condition 2: $w \geq \ell+1$.** Total wins $W \geq w + (n-1)(w+1) =
nw+n-1$. Total tournament points $= W + n(n-1)$ (since $3W+2D = W + 2(W+D) = W+n
(n-1)$). The sum of others' points is $W+n(n-1)-(3w+d)$, which must be $< (n-1)(
3w+d)$. This gives $W < n(3w+d-n+1)$. Combining with $W \geq nw+n-1$:

$$nw+n-1 < n(3w+d-n+1) \implies 2w+d \geq n.$$

Since $w+d+\ell = n-1$, this yields $w \geq \ell+1$.

**Lower bound: $n \geq 7$.** From $d \geq 5$ and $w \geq 1$: $n-1 = w+d+\ell \ge
q 1+5+0 = 6$.

**$n=7$ is impossible.** The only parameter choice satisfying both conditions is
 $w=1, d=5, \ell=0$ (points $= 8$). Team $T$ beats one team $A$ and draws five t
eams $B_1,\dots,B_5$. In the sub-tournament (6 teams, 15 matches):

- Each $B_i$: gets 1 point from $T$, needs $\geq 2$ sub-wins, total $< 8 \Righta
rrow$ sub-points $\leq 6$. With $\geq 2$ wins: $6+d_i \leq 6 \Rightarrow d_i=0$
(exactly 2 wins, 0 draws, 3 losses).
- $A$: gets 0 from $T$, needs $\geq 2$ sub-wins, sub-points $\leq 7 \Rightarrow
d_A \leq 1$.

Total sub-wins $= 2 + 5\cdot2 = 12$. The identity $2\sum w_i + \sum d_i = 30$ gi
ves $\sum d_i = 6$. But $\sum d_i = d_A \leq 1 < 6$. **Contradiction.**

**$n=8$ is achievable.** Take $w=2, d=5, \ell=0$ (points $= 11$). Team $T$ beats
 $A_1, A_2$ and draws $B_1,\dots,B_5$. In the sub-tournament of 7 teams, arrange
 a **regular tournament**: each team has exactly 3 wins, 0 draws, 3 losses (sub-
points $= 9$). This exists for 7 teams — label teams $0,\dots,6$ on a circle; te
am $i$ beats $i+1, i+2, i+3 \pmod{7}$.

**Verification:**
- $A_1, A_2$: $0 + 9 = 9 < 11$ points; $3 > 2$ wins. ✓
- $B_1,\dots,B_5$: $1 + 9 = 10 < 11$ points; $3 > 2$ wins. ✓
- $T$: $11$ points (strictly maximum), $2$ wins (strictly minimum). ✓

Since $n=7$ is impossible and $n=8$ is achievable:

$$n_0 = 8$$
