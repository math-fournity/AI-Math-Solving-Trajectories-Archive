## Solution

**Setup.** Let $f(x) = x^{p-1} + a_{p-2}x^{p-2} + \cdots + a_0$ be monic of degr
ee $p-1$ with integer coefficients and $p-1$ consecutive positive integer roots.
 Since $f$ is monic of degree $p-1$ with exactly $p-1$ roots, we have
$$f(x) = \prod_{k=0}^{p-2}(x - (m+k))$$
for some positive integer $m$, with roots $m, m+1, \ldots, m+p-2$.

**Computing $f(i)f(-i)$.**
$$f(i)\,f(-i) = \prod_{k=0}^{p-2}(i-(m+k))(-i-(m+k)) = \prod_{k=0}^{p-2}\bigl[-(
i-(m+k))(i+(m+k))\bigr] = \prod_{k=0}^{p-2}\bigl[-(i^2-(m+k)^2)\bigr] = \prod_{k
=0}^{p-2}\bigl(1+(m+k)^2\bigr).$$

So the condition $p^2 \mid f(i)f(-i)$ becomes:
$$p^2 \;\Big|\; \prod_{k=0}^{p-2}\bigl((m+k)^2+1\bigr).$$

**The residues covered.** The integers $m, m+1, \ldots, m+p-2$ are $p-1$ consecu
tive integers. Modulo $p$, they cover all residue classes except $(m-1) \bmod p$
. By choosing $m$, we control which single residue class is missing.

**When is any factor divisible by $p$?** A factor $(m+k)^2+1 \equiv 0 \pmod{p}$
requires $(m+k)^2 \equiv -1 \pmod{p}$, i.e., $-1$ is a quadratic residue mod $p$
. This happens iff $p \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$ (for odd $p$), or $p=2$.

**Case $p=2$.** Then $p-1=1$, so $f(x)=x-m$ with $m\geq 1$, and $f(i)f(-i) = 1+m
^2$. We need $4 \mid 1+m^2$. If $m$ even: $1+m^2 \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$; if $m$ odd:
 $1+m^2 \equiv 2 \pmod{4}$. Neither is $\equiv 0 \pmod{4}$. So **$p=2$ fails**.

**Case $p \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$ (odd).** Then $-1$ is not a QR mod $p$, so no facto
r $(m+k)^2+1$ is divisible by $p$. Hence $p \nmid f(i)f(-i)$, so certainly $p^2
\nmid f(i)f(-i)$. **All such $p$ fail**.

**Case $p \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$, $p \geq 5$.** Then $-1$ is a QR mod $p$; let $r$ s
atisfy $r^2 \equiv -1 \pmod{p}$, with $r \not\equiv -r \pmod{p}$ (since $p\geq 5
$ is odd and $r\not\equiv 0$). The two solutions to $x^2\equiv -1\pmod{p}$ are $
r$ and $-r \bmod p$.

Choose $m$ so that the missing residue $(m-1)\bmod p$ is **neither** $r$ **nor**
 $-r$. This is possible since $p \geq 5$ gives at least $5$ residue classes and
we exclude only $2$. Then both $r$ and $-r$ appear among $\{m, m+1, \ldots, m+p-
2\} \bmod p$, so exactly **two** factors $(m+k)^2+1$ are divisible by $p$. Each
contributes $v_p \geq 1$, so
$$v_p\!\left(\prod_{k=0}^{p-2}((m+k)^2+1)\right) \geq 2,$$
hence $p^2 \mid f(i)f(-i)$.

10\cdot 17 = 1700 = 25 \cdot 68$. Indeed $25 \mid 1700$. ✓
