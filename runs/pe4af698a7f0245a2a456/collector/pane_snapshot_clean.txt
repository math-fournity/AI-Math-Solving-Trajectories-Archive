## Proof

**Lower bound.** Let $f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}$ be continuous with $f(x)+f(y)\ge |x-
y|$ for all $x,y\in[0,1]$. Substitute $y=1-x$:

$$f(x)+f(1-x)\;\ge\;|x-(1-x)|\;=\;|2x-1|.$$

Integrate over $x\in[0,1]$:

$$\int_0^1\!\bigl[f(x)+f(1-x)\bigr]\,dx\;\ge\;\int_0^1|2x-1|\,dx.$$

The left-hand side equals $2\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx$ (via the substitution $u=1-x$ in
the second term). The right-hand side is

$$\int_0^1|2x-1|\,dx=2\int_0^{1/2}(1-2x)\,dx=2\Bigl[\tfrac12-\tfrac14\Bigr]=\tfr
ac12.$$

Hence $2\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx\ge \tfrac12$, i.e.

$$\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx\;\ge\;\frac14.$$

**Upper bound (attainment).** Take $f(x)=\bigl|x-\tfrac12\bigr|$, which is conti
nuous on $[0,1]$. For any $x,y\in[0,1]$, the triangle inequality gives

$$f(x)+f(y)=\bigl|x-\tfrac12\bigr|+\bigl|y-\tfrac12\bigr|\;\ge\;\bigl|(x-\tfrac1
2)-(y-\tfrac12)\bigr|=|x-y|,$$

so $f$ is feasible. Its integral is

$$\int_0^1\bigl|x-\tfrac12\bigr|\,dx=2\int_0^{1/2}\!\bigl(\tfrac12-x\bigr)\,dx=2
\cdot\frac18=\frac14.$$

**Conclusion.** The lower bound $\frac14$ is achieved by $f(x)=|x-\tfrac12|$, so
 the minimum of $\int_0^1 f$ over all preferred functions is

$$\boxed{\dfrac14}.$$
