## Solution

ate — no solution, hint, or answer is embedded. Proceeding.

---

We evaluate
$$I=\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^{2}}
{y^{2}}-\frac{y^{2}}{z^{2}}-\bigl[z-1-\log z\bigr]\right)dx\,dy\,dz.$$

### Step 1 — Integrate over $x$

With $u=x/y$ (so $dx=y\,du$),
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}/y^{2}}\,dx \;=\; y\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u^{2}}\,du \
;=\; \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\,y.$$

The remaining integrand is
$$\exp\!\left(-\frac{y^{2}}{z^{2}}-z+1-\log z\right)=\frac{e^{-z+1}}{z}\,e^{-y^{
2}/z^{2}},$$
since $e^{-\log z}=1/z$. Hence
$$I=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-z+1}}{z}\int_{0}^{\infty}y\,e
^{-y^{2}/z^{2}}\,dy\,dz.$$

### Step 2 — Integrate over $y$

With $t=y^{2}/z^{2}$ (so $y\,dy=\frac{z^{2}}{2}\,dt$),
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}y\,e^{-y^{2}/z^{2}}\,dy=\frac{z^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t
}\,dt=\frac{z^{2}}{2}.$$

Substituting,
$$I=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-z+1}}{z}\cdot\frac{z^{2}}{2}\
,dz=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4}\,e\int_{1}^{\infty}z^{3}e^{-z}\,dz.$$

### Step 3 — Integrate over $z$

We use $\int_{0}^{\infty}z^{3}e^{-z}\,dz=\Gamma(4)=6$ and compute the complement
ary part $\int_{0}^{1}z^{3}e^{-z}\,dz$ by repeated integration by parts:

$$\begin{aligned}
\int_{0}^{1}ze^{-z}\,dz &= 1-2e^{-1},\\
\int_{0}^{1}z^{2}e^{-z}\,dz &= -e^{-1}+2(1-2e^{-1})=2-5e^{-1},\\
\int_{0}^{1}z^{3}e^{-z}\,dz &= -e^{-1}+3(2-5e^{-1})=6-16e^{-1}.
\end{aligned}$$

Therefore
$$\int_{1}^{\infty}z^{3}e^{-z}\,dz=6-\bigl(6-16e^{-1}\bigr)=16e^{-1}.$$

### Step 4 — Combine

$$I=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4}\,e\cdot 16\,e^{-1}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4}\cdot 16=4\sqrt{
\pi}.$$

$$\boxed{I=4\sqrt{\pi}}$$
