## Setting Up the Formula for $f(n)$

For $k \in \{1,\ldots,n-1\}$, reducing $k/n$ gives numerator $k/\gcd(k,n)$. Grou
ping by $d = \gcd(k,n)$, setting $q = n/d$, the inner sum over $m$ coprime to $q
$ is the well-known $\frac{q\,\varphi(q)}{2}$ for $q > 1$.

**Claim:** $f(n) = \sum_{\substack{q \mid n \\ q > 1}} g(q)$, where $g(q) = \fra
c{q\,\varphi(q)}{2}$.

**Verification:** $n=2$: $g(2)=1$, $f=1$ ✓. $n=4$: $g(2)+g(4)=1+4=5$, and $\frac
14,\frac24,\frac34 \to 1,1,3$, sum $5$ ✓. $n=6$: $g(2)+g(3)+g(6)=1+3+6=10$, and
numerators $1,1,1,2,5=10$ ✓.

## Parity of $g(q)$

$f(n)$ is odd iff an odd number of terms $g(q)$ are odd. So I need: when is $g(q
) = q\varphi(q)/2$ odd? This requires $q\varphi(q) \equiv 2 \pmod{4}$.

**Case $q$ even:** Write $q = 2^a r$, $r$ odd. Then $q\varphi(q) = 2^{2a-1}\,r\,
\varphi(r)$. For $\equiv 2\pmod{4}$: need $2a-1=1$ (so $a=1$) **and** $r\,\varph
i(r)$ odd. The latter holds only if $r=1$. So $g(q)$ odd iff $q=2$.

**Case $q$ odd, $q>1$:** $\varphi(q)$ is even; $q\varphi(q) = 2^{v_2(\varphi(q))
}\cdot(\text{odd})$. Need $v_2(\varphi(q))=1$. Since $v_2(\varphi(q)) = \sum_{p\
mid q} v_2(p-1)$ and every odd prime has $v_2(p-1)\ge 1$, we need exactly one pr
ime factor with $v_2(p-1)=1$ and no others — i.e., $q = p^e$ for a single prime
$p \equiv 3\pmod{4}$.

**Summary:** $g(q)$ is odd $\iff$ $q = 2$ or $q = p^e$ with $p \equiv 3\pmod{4}$
.

Checks: $g(3)=3$ odd ✓, $g(5)=10$ even ✓, $g(7)=21$ odd ✓, $g(9)=27$ odd ✓, $g(1
3)=78$ even ✓, $g(6)=6$ even ✓.

## Parity of $f(n)$

Let $h(n)$ = number of divisors $q>1$ of $n$ with $g(q)$ odd. Then $f(n)$ odd $\
iff h(n)$ odd.

The odd-$g$ divisors of $n$ are: $2$ (if $2\mid n$) and $p^e$ for $1\le e\le v_p
(n)$ for each prime $p\equiv 3\pmod{4}$ dividing $n$. So:

$$h(n) = [2\mid n] + \nu(n), \quad \text{where } \nu(n) = \sum_{\substack{p\mid
n\\p\equiv 3\pmod 4}} v_p(n).$$

## Comparing $f(n)$ and $f(2015n)$

Factor $2015 = 5\cdot 13\cdot 31$, where $5,13\equiv 1\pmod{4}$ and $31\equiv 3\
pmod{4}$.

- $[2\mid 2015n] = [2\mid n]$ (since 2015 is odd).
- $\nu(2015n) = \nu(n) + 1$ (the factor $31$ adds exactly 1 to the sum of expone
nts of primes $\equiv 3\pmod 4$; primes $5,13\equiv 1\pmod 4$ contribute nothing
).

Therefore:
$$h(2015n) = [2\mid n] + \nu(n) + 1 = h(n) + 1.$$

**$h(2015n)$ and $h(n)$ always differ by exactly 1, so they always have opposite
 parity.** This means $f(n)$ and $f(2015n)$ **always** have opposite parity — ex
actly one is always odd.
