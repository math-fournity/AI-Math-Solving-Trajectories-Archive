## Derivation of the Quotient Norm on $\ell_\infty / c_0$

**Claim.** For $a = (a_i)_{i=1}^\infty \in \ell_\infty$,
$$\|[a]\| \;=\; \inf_{y \in c_0}\|a + y\|_\infty \;=\; \limsup_{i \to \infty}|a_
i|.$$

Set $L := \limsup_{i\to\infty}|a_i|$. Since $a \in \ell_\infty$, the sequence $(
|a_i|)$ is bounded, so $L$ is a well-defined finite nonnegative real. We prove $
\|[a]\| \ge L$ and $\|[a]\| \le L$.

---

### Step 1 — Lower bound: $\|[a]\| \ge L$

Let $y = (y_i) \in c_0$ be arbitrary. Then $y_i \to 0$, so
$$\limsup_{i\to\infty}|a_i + y_i| \;\le\; \limsup_{i\to\infty}\bigl(|a_i| + |y_i
|\bigr) \;=\; \limsup_{i\to\infty}|a_i| + \lim_{i\to\infty}|y_i| \;=\; L + 0 \;=
\; L.$$
(The inequality uses subadditivity of $\limsup$ together with the fact that $|y_
i|\to 0$ has an ordinary limit.) On the other hand, by the reverse triangle ineq
uality,
$$\limsup_{i\to\infty}|a_i + y_i| \;\ge\; \limsup_{i\to\infty}\bigl(|a_i| - |y_i
|\bigr) \;=\; L - 0 \;=\; L,$$
since $|y_i|\to 0$. Hence $\limsup_{i\to\infty}|a_i + y_i| = L$ for every $y \in
 c_0$.

Now $\|a + y\|_\infty = \sup_{i}|a_i + y_i| \ge \limsup_{i\to\infty}|a_i + y_i|
= L$. Taking the infimum over all $y \in c_0$,
$$\|[a]\| = \inf_{y \in c_0}\|a+y\|_\infty \;\ge\; L.$$

---

### Step 2 — Upper bound: $\|[a]\| \le L$

Fix $\varepsilon > 0$. By definition of $L = \limsup |a_i| = \inf_{n}\sup_{i \ge
 n}|a_i|$, there exists $N \in \mathbb{N}$ such that
$$\sup_{i \ge N}|a_i| \;<\; L + \varepsilon,$$
i.e. $|a_i| < L + \varepsilon$ for every $i \ge N$.

Construct $y = (y_i) \in c_0$ by
$$y_i \;=\; \begin{cases} -a_i, & i < N, \\ 0, & i \ge N. \end{cases}$$
Then $y_i = 0$ for all $i \ge N$, so $y \in c_0$. Moreover,
$$|a_i + y_i| \;=\; \begin{cases} |a_i - a_i| = 0, & i < N, \\ |a_i| < L + \vare
psilon, & i \ge N. \end{cases}$$
Therefore
$$\|a + y\|_\infty \;=\; \sup_{i \ge 1}|a_i + y_i| \;<\; L + \varepsilon.$$
Since such a $y$ exists for every $\varepsilon > 0$,
$$\|[a]\| = \inf_{y \in c_0}\|a+y\|_\infty \;\le\; L + \varepsilon \quad\text{fo
r all }\varepsilon>0,$$
and letting $\varepsilon \to 0^+$ gives $\|[a]\| \le L$.

---

### Step 3 — Conclusion

Combining the two inequalities,
$$L \;\le\; \|[a]\| \;\le\; L,$$
so
$$\boxed{\;\|[a]\|_{\ell_\infty/c_0} \;=\; \limsup_{i\to\infty}|a_i|\;}$$

---

### Remarks on the argument

* **Lower bound — robustness.** The key fact is that $\limsup_{i\to\infty}|a_i +
 y_i| = L$ for *every* $y \in c_0$. This is what makes the lower bound hold unif
ormly over the entire coset: adding any null sequence cannot change the limiting
 upper envelope of $(|a_i|)$. The supremum norm dominates the $\limsup$, so ever
y representative has norm $\ge L$.

* **Upper bound — construction.** The correcting sequence $y$ zeros out the *fin
itely many* initial coordinates where $|a_i|$ might exceed $L + \varepsilon$, an
d leaves the tail untouched (where $|a_i|$ is already $< L + \varepsilon$ by def
inition of $\limsup$). Being eventually zero, $y$ certainly lies in $c_0$, and t
he resulting representative $a + y$ has $\ell_\infty$-norm $< L + \varepsilon$.
Letting $\varepsilon \to 0$ recovers $L$ exactly.

* **Why $\limsup$ and not $\lim$.** A general bounded sequence need not converge
, so $\lim |a_i|$ may not exist. The $\limsup$ captures precisely the "essential
 supremum at infinity" — the part of $\|a\|_\infty$ that cannot be cancelled by
any $c_0$ perturbation. Equivalently, $\|[a]\|$ measures the norm of $a$ *modulo
* behavior on any finite initial segment, which is exactly what quotienting by $
c_0$ accomplishes.
