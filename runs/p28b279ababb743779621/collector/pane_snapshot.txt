Looking at this problem, I need to find the ratio of volumes when a diagonal pla
ne cuts through a conical frustum.

## Setup

Place the frustum with axis along the z-axis: bottom base at z=0 (radius 4), top
 base at z=5 (radius 1). The radius at height z is:
$$r(z) = 4 - \frac{3}{5}z$$

The side wall consists of generators at each angle θ: points $(r(z)\cos\theta, r
(z)\sin\theta, z)$.

## Finding the Plane

The plane must be tangent to the top circle (z=5, radius 1) and tangent to the b
ottom circle (z=0, radius 4). By symmetry, place the tangent point on the top ri
m at $(1, 0, 5)$ and on the bottom rim at $(-4, 0, 0)$.

Tangency to the top circle at $(1,0,5)$ requires the plane to contain the tangen
t line $x=1, z=5$. Tangency to the bottom circle at $(-4,0,0)$ requires containi
ng $x=-4, z=0$. These two parallel lines determine the plane:

$$z = x + 4$$

**Verification that it intersects every generator:** Substituting into the side
wall $x = r(z)\cos\theta$:
$$z = (4 - 0.6z)\cos\theta + 4 \implies z = \frac{4(1+\cos\theta)}{1+0.6\cos\the
ta}$$

This gives $z=5$ at $\cos\theta=1$ and $z=0$ at $\cos\theta=-1$, with $0 \le z \
le 5$ for all $\theta$. ✓

## Computing the Upper Chamber Volume

The upper chamber is $z > x + 4$, i.e., $x < z - 4$. At each height $z$, the cro
ss-section is the portion of the disk $x^2+y^2 \le R^2$ (where $R = 4-0.6z$) wit
h $x < d$ (where $d = z-4$).

The area of the region $x < d$ in a disk of radius $R$ is:
$$A = R^2\arccos\!\left(-\frac{d}{R}\right) + d\sqrt{R^2 - d^2}$$

One can verify $|d| \le R$ for all $z \in [0,5]$ (tangent at both endpoints), so
 this is well-defined.

**Substitution** $u = R = 4 - 0.6z$, so $z = \frac{20-5u}{3}$, $dz = -\frac{5}{3
}\,du$, $d = \frac{8-5u}{3}$, and $-d/R = \frac{5u-8}{3u}$:

$$V_{\text{upper}} = \frac{5}{3}\int_1^4 \left[u^2 \arccos\!\left(\frac{5u-8}{3u
}\right) + \frac{4(8-5u)}{9}\sqrt{(4-u)(u-1)}\right] du$$

### First Integral (by parts)

Let $f = \arccos\!\left(\frac{5u-8}{3u}\right)$, $g' = u^2$. Then $f' = \frac{-2
}{u\sqrt{(4-u)(u-1)}}$ (after simplification using $1 - h^2 = \frac{16(4-u)(u-1)
}{9u^2}$).

$$I_1 = \left[\frac{u^3}{3}\arccos\!\left(\frac{5u-8}{3u}\right)\right]_1^4 + \f
rac{2}{3}\int_1^4 \frac{u^2}{\sqrt{(4-u)(u-1)}}\,du$$

Boundary term: $0 - \frac{\pi}{3} = -\frac{\pi}{3}$.

For the remaining integral, substitute $u = 1 + 3\sin^2\phi$:

$$\int_1^4 \frac{u^2}{\sqrt{(4-u)(u-1)}}\,du = 2\int_0^{\pi/2}(1+6\sin^2\phi+9\s
in^4\phi)\,d\phi = 2\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{3\pi}{2}+\frac{27\pi}{16}\right) =
 \frac{59\pi}{8}$$

$$I_1 = -\frac{\pi}{3} + \frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{59\pi}{8} = -\frac{4\pi}{12}+\fra
c{59\pi}{12} = \frac{55\pi}{12}$$

### Second Integral

$$I_2 = \frac{4}{9}\int_1^4 (8-5u)\sqrt{(4-u)(u-1)}\,du$$

With $u = 1+3\sin^2\phi$:

$$\int_1^4 (8-5u)\sqrt{(4-u)(u-1)}\,du = 54\int_0^{\pi/2}\left(\sin^2\phi\cos^2\
phi - 5\sin^4\phi\cos^2\phi\right)d\phi$$

$$= 54\left(\frac{\pi}{16} - \frac{5\pi}{32}\right) = 54\cdot\frac{-3\pi}{32} =
-\frac{81\pi}{16}$$

$$I_2 = \frac{4}{9}\cdot\left(-\frac{81\pi}{16}\right) = -\frac{9\pi}{4}$$

### Combining

$$I_1 + I_2 = \frac{55\pi}{12} - \frac{27\pi}{12} = \frac{28\pi}{12} = \frac{7\p
i}{3}$$

$$V_{\text{upper}} = \frac{5}{3}\cdot\frac{7\pi}{3} = \frac{35\pi}{9}$$

## Total Volume and Ratio

The frustum volume is:
$$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi\cdot 5\cdot(16+4+1) = 35\pi$$

$$V_{\text{lower}} = 35\pi - \frac{35\pi}{9} = \frac{280\pi}{9}$$

**Ratio:**
$$\frac{V_{\text{upper}}}{V_{\text{lower}}} = \frac{35\pi/9}{280\pi/9} = \frac{3
5}{280} = \boxed{\frac{1}{8}}$$
