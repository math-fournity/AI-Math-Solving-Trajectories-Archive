## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle \oint_{|z|=1} \cos\!\left(\tfrac{1}{z}\right)\cos(z)\
,dz$.

**Singularities.** The integrand $f(z)=\cos(1/z)\cos(z)$ is analytic everywhere
except at $z=0$, which lies inside the contour $|z|=1$. At $z=0$ the factor $\co
s(1/z)$ has an essential singularity, so $f$ has an essential singularity there.
 By the residue theorem,

$$
\oint_{|z|=1} f(z)\,dz \;=\; 2\pi i\,\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z),
$$

where the residue is the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ in the Laurent expansion of $f$
 about $0$.

**Laurent expansion.** Recall the Taylor series

$$
\cos(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(2k)!}\,z^{2k}
=1-\frac{z^{2}}{2!}+\frac{z^{4}}{4!}-\cdots,
$$

which contains **only even, non-negative** powers of $z$. Replacing $z$ by $1/z$
 gives

$$
\cos\!\left(\tfrac{1}{z}\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{(2j)!}\,z^{-2
j}
=1-\frac{1}{2!\,z^{2}}+\frac{1}{4!\,z^{4}}-\cdots,
$$

which contains **only even, non-positive** powers of $z$.

Multiplying,

$$
f(z)=\cos\!\left(\tfrac{1}{z}\right)\cos(z)
=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
\frac{(-1)^{j+k}}{(2j)!\,(2k)!}\,z^{\,2k-2j}.
$$

Every exponent in this double sum is of the form $2(k-j)$, hence **even**. There
fore the Laurent series of $f$ about $0$ contains only even powers of $z$:

$$
\cdots,\; z^{-4},\; z^{-2},\; z^{0},\; z^{2},\; z^{4},\;\ldots
$$

In particular, the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ (an odd power) is **zero**:

$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\cos\!\left(\tfrac{1}{z}\right)\cos(z)=0.
$$

**Conclusion.** Applying the residue theorem,

$$
\oint_{|z|=1}\cos\!\left(\tfrac{1}{z}\right)\cos(z)\,dz
=2\pi i\cdot 0 = 0.
$$

$$
\boxed{\,\displaystyle \oint_{|z|=1}\cos\!\left(\tfrac{1}{z}\right)\cos(z)\,dz =
 0\,}
$$
