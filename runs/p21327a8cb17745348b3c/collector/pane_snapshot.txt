## Answer: No

A dense $G_\delta$ subset of a complete metric space with a full-support σ-finit
e regular Radon measure need **not** have positive measure. Here is an explicit
counterexample.

### Setup

Take $X = \mathbb{R}$ with the usual metric $d(x,y)=|x-y|$, and $\mu$ = Lebesgue
 measure. Then:
- $(\mathbb{R}, d)$ is a complete metric space.
- Lebesgue measure is a σ-finite regular Radon measure.
- Every non-empty open interval has positive Lebesgue measure, so $\mu$ does not
 assign measure $0$ to non-empty open sets (full support).

### Constructing a measure-zero dense $G_\delta$

Let $\{q_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ be an enumeration of $\mathbb{Q}$. For each $k \in
\mathbb{N}$, choose radii $\varepsilon_{n,k} > 0$ small enough that
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} 2\,\varepsilon_{n,k} \;<\; \frac{1}{k}.$$
Define
$$U_k \;=\; \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} B(q_n,\, \varepsilon_{n,k}).$$

**Each $U_k$ is open and dense.** It is open as a union of open balls. It is den
se because it contains every rational $q_n$, and $\mathbb{Q}$ is dense in $\math
bb{R}$.

**Each $U_k$ has small measure.** By countable subadditivity,
$$\mu(U_k) \;\leq\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \mu\bigl(B(q_n,\varepsilon_{n,k})\bigr)
\;=\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} 2\,\varepsilon_{n,k} \;<\; \frac{1}{k}.$$

**The intersection is a dense $G_\delta$.** Set
$$Y \;=\; \bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty} U_k.$$
This is a countable intersection of open sets, hence a $G_\delta$ set. Since eac
h $U_k$ is open and dense in the **complete** metric space $\mathbb{R}$, the Bai
re Category Theorem guarantees that $Y$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}$.

**The intersection has measure zero.** For every $k$,
$$\mu(Y) \;\leq\; \mu(U_k) \;<\; \frac{1}{k}.$$
Letting $k \to \infty$ gives $\mu(Y) = 0$.

### Conclusion

We have produced a dense $G_\delta$ set $Y \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ with $\mu(Y) =
0$, under a complete metric and a σ-finite regular Radon measure of full support
. Therefore the answer is **no**: a dense $G_\delta$ set need not have positive
$\mu$-measure.
