## Proof

Let $\zeta$ be a regular singular point of the system $\partial Y(z) = A(z)\,Y(z
)$. By definition of a regular singular point, $A(z)$ has at most a simple pole
at $\zeta$, so we may write the Laurent expansion

$$A(z) = \frac{A_0}{z-\zeta} + A_1 + A_2(z-\zeta) + \cdots,$$

where $A_0 = \operatorname{Res}_{z=\zeta} A(z)$ is the **residue matrix** (an $m
\times m$ constant matrix) and $A_1, A_2, \ldots$ are the higher coefficients of
 the regular part.

### Step 1. The indicial polynomial

We seek solutions of the form $Y(z) = (z-\zeta)^{\alpha}\,\varphi(z)$ with $\var
phi$ holomorphic near $\zeta$ and $\varphi(\zeta) \neq 0$. Substituting into the
 system and examining the leading (most singular) term — i.e., the coefficient o
f $(z-\zeta)^{\alpha-1}$ — gives

$$\alpha\,\varphi(\zeta) = A_0\,\varphi(\zeta),$$

so $\varphi(\zeta)$ must be an eigenvector of $A_0$ with eigenvalue $\alpha$. He
nce the admissible exponents $\alpha$ are the eigenvalues of $A_0$, i.e., the ro
ots of

$$\chi(\alpha) := \det(\alpha\, I - A_0) = 0.$$

This is the **indicial polynomial** at $\zeta$. Let its roots be $\alpha_1, \dot
s, \alpha_m$ (counted with multiplicity).

### Step 2. Fuchs–Frobenius construction (non-resonant case)

The hypothesis states that **no two roots of $\chi$ differ by an integer** (the
*non-resonance* or *non-integer-gap* condition). This is precisely the condition
 that excludes logarithmic terms in the Frobenius expansion.

Under this condition, the classical **Fuchs–Frobenius theorem for systems** (equ
ivalently, the theory of regular singular points of Fuchsian systems) guarantees
 the following. For each root $\alpha_k$ of the indicial polynomial, there exist
s a solution

$$Y_k(z) = (z-\zeta)^{\alpha_k}\,\varphi_k(z),$$

where:

- $\varphi_k(z)$ is **holomorphic** (single-valued and analytic) in a punctured
neighborhood of $\zeta$, and extends holomorphically to $\zeta$ itself;
- $\varphi_k(\zeta) \neq 0$ (in fact $\varphi_k(\zeta)$ is the eigenvector of $A
_0$ for eigenvalue $\alpha_k$);
- the power series $\varphi_k(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \varphi_{k,n}\,(z-\zeta)^
n$ converges in the same disk (punctured at other singularities) where $A(z)$ is
 meromorphic.

The non-resonance condition is what makes this work cleanly: when one attempts t
o determine the coefficients $\varphi_{k,n}$ recursively by matching powers of $
(z-\zeta)$, the recursion at level $n$ involves the matrix $\bigl((\alpha_k+n)\,
I - A_0\bigr)$. Its determinant is $\chi(\alpha_k + n)$. Since no other root $\a
lpha_j$ differs from $\alpha_k$ by an integer, $\alpha_k + n$ is never a root of
 $\chi$ for $n \geq 1$, so $\chi(\alpha_k+n)\neq 0$ and the matrix $(\alpha_k+n)
\,I - A_0$ is invertible. The recursion is therefore **uniquely solvable** at ev
ery order, and no logarithmic terms are forced.

### Step 3. Linear independence and completeness

The $m$ solutions $Y_1, \dots, Y_m$ are linearly independent: their leading beha
viors $(z-\zeta)^{\alpha_k}\varphi_k(\zeta)$ involve distinct exponents (distinc
t modulo the integer-gap-free hypothesis, in fact genuinely distinct since equal
 roots would differ by $0$, an integer — so the roots are all simple), and disti
nct powers of $(z-\zeta)$ are linearly independent over the field of meromorphic
 functions. Since the system is $m$-dimensional, these $m$ solutions form a **fu
ndamental system** (a basis of the solution space).

### Conclusion

Near the regular singular point $\zeta$, under the hypothesis that no two roots
of the indicial polynomial differ by an integer, every solution of $\partial Y(z
) = A(z)\,Y(z)$ is a linear combination of solutions of the form

$$\boxed{Y_k(z) = (z-\zeta)^{\alpha_k}\,\varphi_k(z), \qquad k=1,\dots,m,}$$

where $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_m$ are the (distinct) roots of the indicial polyn
omial $\det(\alpha\, I - A_0)=0$ (equivalently, the eigenvalues of the residue m
atrix $A_0$), and each $\varphi_k(z)$ is **holomorphic** at $\zeta$ with $\varph
i_k(\zeta)\neq 0$. There are **no logarithmic terms**. This is the asymptotic fo
rm of the solutions near $\zeta$.
