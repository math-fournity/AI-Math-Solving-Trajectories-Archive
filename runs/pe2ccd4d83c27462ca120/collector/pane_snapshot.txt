## Proof

**Claim:** $X = \{f \in C^\infty[0,1] : f^{(n)}(0)=f^{(n)}(1)=0 \ \forall n\geq
0\}$ is a dense subspace of $L^2[0,1]$.

---

### Step 1. $X$ is a linear subspace of $L^2[0,1]$.

Every $f \in C^\infty[0,1]$ is continuous on the compact interval $[0,1]$, hence
 bounded and in $L^2[0,1]$. If $f, g \in X$ and $\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{R}$,
then $(\alpha f + \beta g)^{(n)}(0) = \alpha f^{(n)}(0) + \beta g^{(n)}(0) = 0$
and similarly at $1$, for every $n \geq 0$. So $\alpha f + \beta g \in X$. Thus
$X$ is a subspace.

---

### Step 2. $C_c^\infty(0,1) \subseteq X$.

Let $f \in C_c^\infty(0,1)$, so $f$ is smooth on $(0,1)$ with compact support $K
 \subset (0,1)$. Then there exists $\delta > 0$ with $K \subseteq [\delta, 1-\de
lta]$, so $f \equiv 0$ on $(0,\delta) \cup (1-\delta, 1)$. Extending $f$ by $0$
to all of $[0,1]$ gives a function (still denoted $f$) that is identically zero
in neighborhoods of both endpoints. Consequently $f$ is $C^\infty$ on $[0,1]$ an
d $f^{(n)}(0) = f^{(n)}(1) = 0$ for every $n \geq 0$. Hence $f \in X$.

---

### Step 3. $C_c^\infty(0,1)$ is dense in $L^2[0,1]$.

We show that every $\mathbf{1}_{[a,b]}$ with $0 \leq a < b \leq 1$ can be approx
imated in $L^2$ by elements of $C_c^\infty(0,1)$; since linear combinations of s
uch characteristic functions (simple functions) are dense in $L^2[0,1]$, this su
ffices.

**Case 1: $0 < a < b < 1$.** Let $\eta \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ be the standa
rd mollifier, $\eta \geq 0$, $\operatorname{supp} \eta \subseteq [-1,1]$, $\int
\eta = 1$, and set $\eta_\epsilon(x) = \epsilon^{-1}\eta(x/\epsilon)$. The convo
lution
$$g_\epsilon := \mathbf{1}_{[a,b]} * \eta_\epsilon$$
is $C^\infty$ on $\mathbb{R}$, with $\operatorname{supp} g_\epsilon \subseteq [a
-\epsilon, b+\epsilon]$. For $\epsilon < \min(a, 1-b)$, this support lies in $(0
,1)$, so $g_\epsilon \in C_c^\infty(0,1)$. By standard properties of mollificati
on, $\|g_\epsilon - \mathbf{1}_{[a,b]}\|_{L^2} \to 0$ as $\epsilon \to 0^+$.

**Case 2: $a = 0$ or $b = 1$.** For $\mathbf{1}_{[0,b]}$ with $0 < b < 1$: first
 approximate by $\mathbf{1}_{[\delta, b]}$ (with $\delta \downarrow 0$), noting
$\|\mathbf{1}_{[0,b]} - \mathbf{1}_{[\delta,b]}\|_{L^2} = \sqrt{\delta} \to 0$.
Then approximate $\mathbf{1}_{[\delta,b]}$ by $C_c^\infty(0,1)$ functions via Ca
se 1. The case $b = 1$ is symmetric, and $\mathbf{1}_{[0,1]}$ is handled by appr
oximating with $\mathbf{1}_{[\delta, 1-\delta]}$.

Since simple functions (finite linear combinations of characteristic functions o
f intervals) are dense in $L^2[0,1]$, and each such characteristic function is a
pproximable by $C_c^\infty(0,1)$ functions, we conclude $C_c^\infty(0,1)$ is den
se in $L^2[0,1]$.

---

### Step 4. $X$ is dense in $L^2[0,1]$.

From Step 2, $C_c^\infty(0,1) \subseteq X \subseteq L^2[0,1]$. Therefore
$$\overline{C_c^\infty(0,1)} \subseteq \overline{X} \subseteq L^2[0,1].$$
By Step 3, $\overline{C_c^\infty(0,1)} = L^2[0,1]$, so $\overline{X} = L^2[0,1]$
.

---

**Conclusion:** $X$ is a dense subspace of $L^2[0,1]$. $\blacksquare$
