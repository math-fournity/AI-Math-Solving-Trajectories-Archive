## Proof

We evaluate the contour integral
$$I(z) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{x=0} e^{\sqrt{z}\,(x+1/x)} \frac{dx}{x}$$
by extracting the residue of the integrand at $x=0$.

**Step 1: Laurent expansion.** Factor the exponential:
$$e^{\sqrt{z}\,(x+1/x)} = e^{\sqrt{z}\,x}\cdot e^{\sqrt{z}/x}.$$

Expanding each factor as a power series (valid for all $x\neq 0$):
$$e^{\sqrt{z}\,x} = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\sqrt{z})^{m}\,x^{m}}{m!}, \qquad
e^{\sqrt{z}/x} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\sqrt{z})^{n}\,x^{-n}}{n!}.$$

Multiplying (absolute convergence justifies termwise multiplication):
$$e^{\sqrt{z}\,(x+1/x)} = \sum_{m,n\ge 0}\frac{(\sqrt{z})^{m+n}\,x^{m-n}}{m!\,n!
}.$$

**Step 2: Extract the residue.** Dividing by $x$ and integrating around $x=0$, t
he integral $\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint$ picks out exactly the coefficient of $x^{-1}
$ in the Laurent expansion of $\frac{1}{x}e^{\sqrt{z}(x+1/x)}$. This coefficient
 comes from the term $x^{m-n-1}$ with $m-n-1=-1$, i.e. $m=n$. Hence:
$$I(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\sqrt{z})^{2n}}{(n!)^{2}} = \sum_{n=0}^{\inft
y}\frac{z^{n}}{(n!)^{2}}.$$

**Step 3: Identify the closed form.** The modified Bessel function of the first
kind of order zero has the series representation
$$I_{0}(w) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(w/2)^{2n}}{(n!)^{2}}.$$

Setting $w = 2\sqrt{z}$ gives $(w/2)^{2n} = (\sqrt{z})^{2n} = z^{n}$, so
$$I_{0}(2\sqrt{z}) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{(n!)^{2}} = I(z).$$

**Conclusion.** The integral admits the closed-form expression
$$\boxed{\displaystyle \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{x=0} e^{\sqrt{z}\,(x+1/x)}\,\frac{
dx}{x} \;=\; I_{0}\!\bigl(2\sqrt{z}\bigr),}$$
where $I_{0}$ is the modified Bessel function of the first kind of order zero. T
he series converges for all $z\in\mathbb{C}$ (entire in $z$), and the residue co
mputation is justified by the absolute and uniform convergence of the double ser
ies on the contour.
