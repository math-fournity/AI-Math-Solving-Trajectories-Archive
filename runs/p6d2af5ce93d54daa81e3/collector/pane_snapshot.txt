## Proof

Let $s(x)$ denote the sum of digits of $x$. A "Certified Sequence" requires
$$s(n)\mid n,\quad s(n+1)\mid (n+1),\quad s(n+2)\mid (n+2),\quad s(n+3)\mid (n+3
),$$
with $n\equiv 8\pmod{10}$. Let $S=s(n)$, and let $a$ be the tens digit of $n$.

**Claim:** $a\le 8$ is impossible, forcing $a=9$.

### Digit sums when $a\le 8$ (no carry cascade)

Since $n$ ends in $8$ and the tens digit $a\le 8$:

- $n$ ends in $\ldots a\,8$, so $s(n)=S$.
- $n+1$ ends in $\ldots a\,9$ (units $8\to 9$, no carry): $s(n+1)=S+1$.
- $n+2$: units $9\to 0$ carries into tens, $a\to a+1$ (valid since $a\le 8$), no
 further carry. So $s(n+2)=S+1-9+1=S-7$.
- $n+3$ ends in $\ldots(a+1)\,1$ (no carry): $s(n+3)=S-7+1=S-6$.

(Note $S\ge 8$ since $n$ ends in $8$, so $S-7\ge 1$ and $S-6\ge 2$ are legitimat
e positive digit sums.)

### Translating divisibility into congruences

The four Harshad conditions become
$$n\equiv 0\pmod{S},\quad n\equiv -1\pmod{S+1},\quad n\equiv -2\pmod{S-7},\quad
n\equiv -3\pmod{S-6}.$$

For this system to be solvable, every pair must be consistent: the difference of
 residues must be divisible by the gcd of the corresponding moduli.

**Pair $(n\equiv -1\pmod{S+1},\; n\equiv -2\pmod{S-7})$:**
$$\gcd(S+1,\,S-7)=\gcd(S+1,\,8).$$
The residue difference is $(-1)-(-2)=1$, so we need $\gcd(S+1,8)\mid 1$, i.e. $\
gcd(S+1,8)=1$. Hence $S+1$ is odd, so **$S$ is even**.

**Pair $(n\equiv 0\pmod{S},\; n\equiv -3\pmod{S-6})$:**
$$\gcd(S,\,S-6)=\gcd(S,\,6).$$
The residue difference is $0-(-3)=3$, so we need $\gcd(S,6)\mid 3$. Since $S$ is
 even, $2\mid S$, giving $\gcd(S,6)\in\{2,6\}$. But neither $2$ nor $6$ divides
$3$.

**Contradiction.** Therefore no Certified Sequence with $n$ ending in $8$ can ha
ve tens digit $a\le 8$.

### The remaining possibility

The tens digit is a decimal digit, so $a\in\{0,1,\ldots,9\}$. Since all values $
a\le 8$ are ruled out, we must have $a=9$.
