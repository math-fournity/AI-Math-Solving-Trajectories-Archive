**Proof by contradiction.** Suppose $e$ is isolated in $K$, i.e., $\{e\}$ is ope
n in the subspace topology of $K$.

Since $K$ is a subgroup of $G$ (the closure of a subgroup in a topological group
 is a subgroup), $K$ is itself a topological group. In particular, for every $h
\in K$, the left-translation map $L_h : K \to K$, $x \mapsto hx$, is a homeomorp
hism.

Applying $L_h$ to the open set $\{e\}$, we get that $\{h\} = L_h(\{e\})$ is open
 in $K$. Since $h$ was arbitrary, **every singleton is open in $K$**, so $K$ is
discrete.

Now, $K$ is a closed subset of the compact space $G$, hence $K$ is compact. A co
mpact discrete space must be finite (the open cover by singletons has no proper
subcover). Therefore $K$ is finite.

But $\langle g \rangle \subseteq K$, and $g$ has infinite order, so $\langle g \
rangle$ is infinite — contradicting the finiteness of $K$.

This contradiction shows that $e$ cannot be isolated in $K = \overline{\langle g
 \rangle}$. $\blacksquare$

al group is a subgroup, so $K$ inherits the topological group structure; (2) tra
nslation invariance means isolating one point isolates all points, forcing discr
eteness; (3) compact + discrete $\Rightarrow$ finite, which is incompatible with
 the infinite cyclic subgroup contained in $K$.
