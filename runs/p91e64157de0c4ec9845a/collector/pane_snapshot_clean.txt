# Serre's Conjecture Implies Fermat's Last Theorem

## Step 1: Reduction to Prime Exponent

Fermat's Last Theorem states that for $n \geq 3$, there are no positive integers
 $a, b, c$ with $a^n + b^n = c^n$. It suffices to prove this for $n = p$ prime a
nd $p \geq 5$: the case $n = 4$ was settled by Fermat himself, and any composite
 exponent $n \geq 3$ either is divisible by 4 (reducing to the $n = 4$ case) or
has an odd prime divisor $p \geq 5$ (reducing to the prime case, since $a^n + b^
n = c^n$ implies $(a^{n/p})^p + (b^{n/p})^p = (c^{n/p})^p$).

## Step 2: Assume a Counterexample

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exist pairwise coprime positive integers
$a, b, c$ with
$$a^p + b^p = c^p, \quad p \geq 5 \text{ prime}.$$
Since $a, b, c$ are pairwise coprime and $p$ is odd, exactly one of $a, b, c$ is
 even. By relabeling (and possibly swapping $a$ and $b$), we may assume $a$ is e
ven and $b, c$ are odd. (If both $a, b$ were odd, then $c$ would be even; we can
 always arrange the labeling so that one of $a, b$ is even.)

## Step 3: The Frey–Hellegouarch Curve

Associate to this hypothetical solution the elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$:
$$E: \quad y^2 = x(x - a^p)(x + b^p).$$
This is the **Frey curve** (or Frey–Hellegouarch curve). The cubic on the right
has three distinct roots $0, \, a^p, \, -b^p$, so $E$ is a nonsingular elliptic
curve.

## Step 4: Invariants of the Frey Curve

### Discriminant

The three roots of the cubic $f(x) = x(x - a^p)(x + b^p)$ are $e_1 = 0$, $e_2 =
a^p$, $e_3 = -b^p$. The discriminant of the elliptic curve is:
$$\Delta = 16 \prod_{i < j}(e_i - e_j)^2 = 16 \cdot (0 - a^p)^2 \cdot (0 - (-b^p
))^2 \cdot (a^p - (-b^p))^2.$$
Since $a^p + b^p = c^p$, the last factor is $(c^p)^2 = c^{2p}$. Therefore:
$$\boxed{\Delta = 16 \, (abc)^{2p}.}$$

**Key observation:** Up to the bounded factor $16 = 2^4$, the discriminant is a
perfect $p$-th power: $\Delta = 2^4 \cdot (abc)^{2p}$. In particular, for every
**odd** prime $\ell \mid abc$,
$$v_\ell(\Delta) = 2p \cdot v_\ell(abc) \equiv 0 \pmod{p}.$$
This divisibility by $p$ of the $\ell$-adic valuation of the discriminant is the
 crucial property that drives the level-lowering argument.

### $j$-invariant

$$j(E) = 256 \cdot \frac{(a^{2p} + a^p b^p + b^{2p})^3}{a^{2p} b^{2p} c^{2p}}.$$
For any odd prime $\ell \mid abc$, we have $v_\ell(j) < 0$, confirming that $E$
has **multiplicative reduction** at such $\ell$.

### Conductor

- At odd primes $\ell \mid abc$: $E$ has multiplicative reduction, so the conduc
tor exponent is $f_\ell = 1$.
- At odd primes $\ell \nmid abc$: $E$ has good reduction, so $f_\ell = 0$.
- At $\ell = 2$: the conductor exponent $f_2 = \alpha$ is bounded (one checks $\
alpha \leq 5$, and with our parity choice $\alpha = 1$ or a small value).

Thus:
$$N(E) = 2^\alpha \cdot \prod_{\substack{\ell \mid abc \\ \ell \text{ odd}}} \el
l = 2^\alpha \cdot \operatorname{rad}_{\text{odd}}(abc),$$
where $\operatorname{rad}_{\text{odd}}(abc)$ denotes the product of odd primes d
ividing $abc$.

### Semistability

$E$ has multiplicative or good reduction at every odd prime, and with the approp
riate choice of model (and parity of $a, b$), $E$ is semistable at $2$ as well.
Hence **$E$ is a semistable elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$**.

### Rational 2-torsion

The curve $E: y^2 = x(x - a^p)(x + b^p)$ has three rational $2$-torsion points:
$(0,0)$, $(a^p, 0)$, and $(-b^p, 0)$. Thus $E[2] \subset E(\mathbb{Q})$ — **all
$2$-torsion is rational**.

## Step 5: The Mod $p$ Galois Representation

The $p$-torsion subgroup $E[p]$ is a 2-dimensional $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector space.
The Galois group $G_\mathbb{Q} = \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathb
b{Q})$ acts on $E[p]$, giving a **mod $p$ Galois representation**:
$$\rho_{E,p}: G_\mathbb{Q} \longrightarrow \operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_p).$$

### $\rho_{E,p}$ is odd

The determinant of $\rho_{E,p}$ is the mod $p$ cyclotomic character $\bar{\chi}_
p$, which sends complex conjugation to $-1$. Hence $\rho_{E,p}$ is **odd**.

### $\rho_{E,p}$ is irreducible

If $\rho_{E,p}$ were reducible, then $E$ would admit a **rational $p$-isogeny**
(a $G_\mathbb{Q}$-stable cyclic subgroup of order $p$ in $E[p]$). By **Mazur's t
heorem** (1978) on rational isogenies, the possible prime degrees of rational is
ogenies of elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$ are severely restricted: the only p
ossible primes are $\ell \in \{2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 37, 43, 67, 163\}$.

For $p \geq 5$ not in this exceptional set, irreducibility is immediate. For the
 exceptional primes $p \in \{5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 37, 43, 67, 163\}$, one uses
the **specific structure of the Frey curve** to rule out a rational $p$-isogeny.
 The argument (due to Mazur, Serre, and Ribet) proceeds as follows:

- If $E$ has a rational $p$-isogeny $\phi: E \to E'$, then $E$ and $E'$ are rela
ted by a degree-$p$ isogeny, and their discriminants satisfy $\Delta_E = \Delta_
{E'} \cdot (\text{isogeny factor})^p$.
- The Frey curve has discriminant $\Delta = 16(abc)^{2p}$, which is essentially
a $p$-th power. This severely constrains the possible isogenies.
- Combined with the fact that $E[2] \subset E(\mathbb{Q})$ (full rational 2-tors
ion) and the specific arithmetic of the Frey curve, one shows that no rational $
p$-isogeny can exist for any $p \geq 5$.

Thus **$\rho_{E,p}$ is irreducible for all primes $p \geq 5$**.

## Step 6: Serre's Conjecture

**Serre's conjecture** (1987, proved by Khare–Wintenberger 2008–2009) states:

> Every odd, irreducible, continuous representation $\rho: G_\mathbb{Q} \to \ope
ratorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$ is **modular**: there exists a mod $p$ eigenform
$f$ (a cusp form, unless $\rho$ is reducible) of weight $k(\rho)$, level $N(\rho
)$, and character $\chi(\rho)$ — all determined by $\rho$ via **Serre's recipe**
 — such that $\rho_f \cong \rho$.

Since $\rho_{E,p}$ is odd and irreducible, Serre's conjecture applies. We must c
ompute the invariants $(N(\rho_{E,p}), \, k(\rho_{E,p}), \, \chi(\rho_{E,p}))$.

## Step 7: The Level $N(\rho_{E,p}) = 2$

Serre's recipe gives $N(\rho)$ as the **prime-to-$p$ Artin conductor** of $\rho_
{E,p}$.

### At odd primes $\ell \mid abc$, $\ell \neq p$

$E$ has multiplicative reduction at $\ell$, so by the theory of **Tate curves**,
 $E$ is isomorphic over $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ to a Tate curve $E_q$ with Tate parame
ter $q \in \mathbb{Q}_\ell^*$ satisfying $v_\ell(q) = v_\ell(\Delta) = 2p \cdot
v_\ell(abc)$.

The mod $p$ representation restricted to $G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}$ fits in the exact
 sequence
$$0 \to \mu_p \to E[p] \to \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z} \to 0,$$
and the extension class is determined by $q \in \mathbb{Q}_\ell^* / (\mathbb{Q}_
\ell^*)^p$ (Kummer theory).

Since $v_\ell(q) = 2p \cdot v_\ell(abc)$ is **divisible by $p$**, we can write $
q = u \cdot \ell^{2p \cdot v_\ell(abc)} = u \cdot (\ell^{2v_\ell(abc)})^p$ where
 $u$ is a unit. The Kummer class of $q$ is then determined by $u \in \mathbb{Z}_
\ell^* / (\mathbb{Z}_\ell^*)^p$.

- If $\ell \not\equiv 1 \pmod{p}$: the $p$-th power map is an automorphism of $\
mathbb{Z}_\ell^*$ (since $p \nmid (\ell-1)$ and $p \neq \ell$), so $u$ is a $p$-
th power, $q$ is a $p$-th power in $\mathbb{Q}_\ell^*$, the Kummer extension is
trivial, and **$\rho_{E,p}$ is unramified at $\ell$**. The conductor exponent is
 $0$.

- If $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$: the unit $u$ might not be a $p$-th power, and $\r
ho_{E,p}$ could be ramified at $\ell$ with conductor exponent $1$. However, Ribe
t's **level-lowering theorem** (1990) shows that even in this case, since $v_\el
l(\Delta) \equiv 0 \pmod{p}$ (the "peu ramifié" condition), the representation $
\rho_{E,p}$ — if it arises from a modular form of level divisible by $\ell$ — al
so arises from a modular form of level $N/\ell$. This allows us to **remove $\el
l$ from the level**.

In either case, the contribution of $\ell$ to the minimal level is **$0$**.

### At $\ell = 2$

The conductor of $\rho_{E,p}$ at $2$ is $2^\alpha$ with $\alpha$ bounded. With t
he appropriate choice of Frey curve model (and the parity condition that one of
$a, b$ is even), one obtains $\alpha = 1$, so the contribution is $2$.

### At $\ell = p$ (first case: $p \nmid abc$)

$E$ has good reduction at $p$, so $\rho_{E,p}$ is at worst tamely ramified at $p
$, and the prime-to-$p$ Artin conductor does not include $p$.

### Conclusion on the level

After removing all odd primes dividing $abc$ (either because $\rho$ is unramifie
d there, or by Ribet's level lowering), the minimal level is:
$$\boxed{N(\rho_{E,p}) = 2.}$$

## Step 8: The Weight $k(\rho_{E,p})$

The weight is determined by the local behavior of $\rho_{E,p}$ at $p$.

### First case: $p \nmid abc$ (good reduction at $p$)

$E$ has good reduction at $p$. The inertia group $I_p$ acts on $E[p]$ via the **
fundamental characters**:

- If $E$ is **ordinary** at $p$: $I_p$ acts via $\omega$ (the level-1 fundamenta
l character, i.e., the mod $p$ cyclotomic character). Serre's recipe gives $k(\r
ho) = 2$.

- If $E$ is **supersingular** at $p$: $I_p$ acts via $\omega_2$ (the level-2 fun
damental character). Serre's recipe gives $k(\rho) = p + 1$.

In the supersingular case, we invoke the **companion forms theorem** (Edixhoven,
 1992): if $\rho$ arises from a mod $p$ form of weight $p+1$ and level $N$ with
trivial character, then $\rho$ **also** arises from a mod $p$ form of weight $2$
 and level $N$ (a "companion form"). Thus, in both the ordinary and supersingula
r subcases, $\rho_{E,p}$ arises from a form of **weight $2$ and level $2$**.

### Second case: $p \mid c$ (multiplicative reduction at $p$)

$E$ has multiplicative reduction at $p$. The weight computation is more delicate
 and depends on $v_p(c)$ and the Tamagawa numbers. The analysis (due to Serre, w
ith refinements by Darmon–Merel and others) shows that the weight is $k = 2$ (or
 reduces to $k = 2$ via companion forms). The level remains $2$.

### Conclusion on the weight

In all cases, $\rho_{E,p}$ arises from a mod $p$ form of:
$$\boxed{k(\rho_{E,p}) = 2, \quad N(\rho_{E,p}) = 2, \quad \chi(\rho_{E,p}) = \t
ext{trivial}.}$$

## Step 9: The Contradiction

By Serre's conjecture, $\rho_{E,p}$ arises from a mod $p$ eigenform of weight $2
$, level $2$, and trivial character.

Since $\rho_{E,p}$ is **irreducible**, it must arise from a **cusp form** (Eisen
stein series give reducible representations of the form $\chi_1 \oplus \chi_2$).

But the space of cusp forms of weight $2$ and level $2$ is:
$$S_2(\Gamma_0(2)) = \{0\}.$$
This is because the modular curve $X_0(2)$ has **genus $0$** (it is isomorphic t
o $\mathbb{P}^1$), so there are no nontrivial weight-2 cusp forms at this level.

The only mod $p$ modular forms of weight $2$ and level $2$ are **Eisenstein seri
es**, whose attached Galois representations are **reducible**:
$$\rho_{\text{Eis}} \cong \mathbf{1} \oplus \bar{\chi}_p.$$

Therefore, $\rho_{E,p}$ would have to be reducible — contradicting the **irreduc
ibility** established in Step 5.

## Step 10: Conclusion

The contradiction shows that **no non-trivial solution** $(a, b, c)$ to $a^p + b
^p = c^p$ can exist for any prime $p \geq 5$. Combined with the classical result
s for $n = 4$ (Fermat) and $n = 3$ (Euler), this proves:

> **Fermat's Last Theorem:** For every integer $n \geq 3$, there are no positive
 integers $a, b, c$ satisfying $a^n + b^n = c^n$.

## Summary of the Logical Structure

The implication **Serre's Conjecture $\Rightarrow$ FLT** proceeds as follows:

1. **Hypothetical counterexample** $a^p + b^p = c^p$ ($p \geq 5$) $\Rightarrow$
**Frey curve** $E: y^2 = x(x-a^p)(x+b^p)$ with discriminant $\Delta = 16(abc)^{2
p}$ (a $p$-th power up to bounded factor).

2. **Mod $p$ representation** $\rho_{E,p}$ is **odd** (det $= \bar{\chi}_p$) and
 **irreducible** (Mazur's isogeny theorem + Frey curve structure).

3. **Serre's conjecture** $\Rightarrow$ $\rho_{E,p}$ is modular, arising from a
mod $p$ eigenform of weight $k(\rho)$, level $N(\rho)$, character $\chi(\rho)$.

4. **Serre's recipe + level lowering** (using $v_\ell(\Delta) \equiv 0 \pmod{p}$
 for $\ell \mid abc$) $\Rightarrow$ $N(\rho) = 2$, $k(\rho) = 2$, $\chi = \text{
trivial}$.

5. **$S_2(\Gamma_0(2)) = 0$** (genus of $X_0(2)$ is $0$) $\Rightarrow$ no cusp f
orm exists $\Rightarrow$ $\rho_{E,p}$ must be reducible.

6. **Contradiction** with irreducibility $\Rightarrow$ **no counterexample exist
s** $\Rightarrow$ **FLT holds**.

The essential difference from the historical proof (Wiles + Ribet, 1995) is that
 Serre's conjecture provides the modularity of $\rho_{E,p}$ **directly** — witho
ut first proving that the Frey curve $E$ itself is modular (i.e., without Wiles'
 modularity theorem for semistable elliptic curves). Serre's conjecture was prov
ed by Khare (2005, for the level-1 case) and Khare–Wintenberger (2008–2009, in f
ull generality), using Serre's modularity conjecture and the proof of Serre's mo
dularity conjecture via the modularity lifting machinery. This gives an independ
ent proof of FLT.
