# Problem

A specialized deep-sea research station is shaped like a perfect sphere with a radius of exactly 1 kilometer. To monitor seismic activity, a team of engineers needs to install $n=10$ fiber-optic communication cables along the surface of the station.

Due to strict interference protocols, each cable must follow the path of a "great circle" (a path with a radius of curvature equal to the station’s radius) and no two cables are permitted to touch or cross at any point. Safety regulations require that every cable installed must have the exact same length, denoted as $\alpha$.

The engineers aim to maximize the reach of these sensors to cover as much of the station as possible. Let $L$ be the supremum of the lengths $\alpha$ such that it is mathematically possible to arrange $10$ such non-intersecting cables on the sphere's surface.

Determine the value of $L$.
