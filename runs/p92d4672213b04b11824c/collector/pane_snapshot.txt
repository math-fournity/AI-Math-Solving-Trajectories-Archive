## Proof

**Claim.** Let $X$ be a set and $F \subseteq X$ a finite subset. The number of u
ltrafilters on $X$ that contain $F$ (i.e. ultrafilters $\mathcal U$ with $F \in
\mathcal U$) is

$$
\begin{cases}
|F|, & F \neq \varnothing,\\[4pt]
0, & F = \varnothing.
\end{cases}
$$

In particular, the maximum (and in fact exact) number is $|F|$ when $F$ is nonem
pty.

---

### Step 1. No free ultrafilter contains a finite set.

Recall that an ultrafilter $\mathcal U$ on $X$ is **principal** if $\mathcal U =
 \{A \subseteq X : x \in A\}$ for some $x \in X$ (the principal ultrafilter at $
x$), and **free** (non-principal) otherwise. A key property of a free ultrafilte
r is that it contains **no finite set**: it contains every cofinite set, and sin
ce for any finite $S$ the complement $X \setminus S$ is cofinite (hence in $\mat
hcal U$), we cannot also have $S \in \mathcal U$, because that would force $\var
nothing = S \cap (X\setminus S) \in \mathcal U$, contradicting that $\mathcal U$
 is a proper filter.

So any ultrafilter containing a finite set must be principal.

### Step 2. An ultrafilter containing a finite set is principal at a point of th
at set.

Let $\mathcal U$ be an ultrafilter on $X$ with $F \in \mathcal U$, where $F = \{
x_1, \dots, x_n\}$ is finite. We use the defining property of ultrafilters:

> If $A \cup B \in \mathcal U$, then $A \in \mathcal U$ or $B \in \mathcal U$.

Applying this inductively to $F = \{x_1\} \cup \{x_2\} \cup \cdots \cup \{x_n\}$
, we obtain some $i$ with $\{x_i\} \in \mathcal U$.

Now for any $A \subseteq X$: since $\{x_i\} \in \mathcal U$ and $\mathcal U$ is
an ultrafilter, exactly one of $A$, $X \setminus A$ lies in $\mathcal U$. But $\
{x_i\} \subseteq A \iff x_i \in A$, and if $x_i \in A$ then $\{x_i\} \subseteq A
$ forces $A \in \mathcal U$ (upward closure); if $x_i \notin A$ then $\{x_i\} \s
ubseteq X\setminus A$ forces $X \setminus A \in \mathcal U$, i.e. $A \notin \mat
hcal U$. Hence

$$
A \in \mathcal U \iff x_i \in A,
$$

so $\mathcal U$ is exactly the principal ultrafilter at $x_i \in F$.

### Step 3. Count the ultrafilters containing $F$.

By Step 2, every ultrafilter containing $F$ is the principal ultrafilter at some
 point of $F$. Conversely, the principal ultrafilter at any $x \in F$ contains $
F$ (since $x \in F$). Distinct points give distinct principal ultrafilters. Ther
efore the ultrafilters on $X$ containing $F$ are in bijection with the points of
 $F$:

$$
\{\mathcal U \text{ ultrafilter on } X : F \in \mathcal U\} \;\longleftrightarro
w\; F, \qquad x \mapsto \{A \subseteq X : x \in A\}.
$$

Hence the number of such ultrafilters is exactly $|F|$.

### Step 4. The empty case.

If $F = \varnothing$, then no ultrafilter contains $F$, since every ultrafilter
is a proper filter and $\varnothing$ is excluded by definition. The count is $0
= |F|$, consistent with the formula.

---

### Conclusion.

The maximum (indeed exact) number of ultrafilters on $X$ that contain the finite
 subset $F$ is

$$
\boxed{|F|}.
$$

This maximum is attained precisely by the principal ultrafilters at the points o
f $F$, and no free ultrafilter can contribute, since free ultrafilters contain n
o finite sets.
