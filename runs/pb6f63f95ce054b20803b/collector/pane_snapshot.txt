We want the last five digits of $T_{1001}$, where $T_1 = 9$ and $T_{n+1} = 9^{T_
n}$ (a tower of 1001 nines). We compute $T_{1001} \bmod 10^5$.

**CRT decomposition.** Since $10^5 = 2^5 \cdot 5^5 = 32 \cdot 3125$ and $\gcd(32
,3125)=1$, we find $T_{1001} \bmod 32$ and $T_{1001} \bmod 3125$ separately.

**Mod 32.** $9^2 = 81 \equiv 17$, $9^4 \equiv 17^2 = 289 \equiv 1 \pmod{32}$, so
 $\operatorname{ord}_{32}(9)=4$. We need $T_{1000} \bmod 4$. Since $9 \equiv 1 \
pmod 4$, every $T_n \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ for $n \ge 1$. Thus
$$T_{1001} \equiv 9^{1} \equiv 9 \pmod{32}.$$

**Mod 3125.** Since $3$ is a primitive root mod $5^k$ for all $k$ (as $3^4 = 81
\not\equiv 1 \pmod{25}$), $\operatorname{ord}_{5^5}(3) = 4\cdot 5^4 = 2500$, and
 $\operatorname{ord}_{3125}(9) = 2500/2 = 1250$. So we need $T_{1000} \bmod 1250
$.

**Iterated lifting.** We repeatedly use: $9 \equiv 1 \pmod 2$ (so every $T_n$ is
 odd), and the order of $9$ modulo $5^k$ is $\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 4\cdot 5^{k-1} =
2\cdot 5^{k-1}$.
