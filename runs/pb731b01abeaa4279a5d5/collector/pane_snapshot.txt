## Proof

Let $X$ be a Banach space (the argument only uses the metric structure), and let
 $\mathcal{C}$ denote the collection of non-empty bounded closed convex subsets
of $X$, equipped with the Hausdorff distance
$$d_H(A,B)=\max\Bigl(\sup_{a\in A}d(a,B),\;\sup_{b\in B}d(b,A)\Bigr).$$

Fix $x\in X$. We claim the map $C\mapsto d(x,C)$ is **1-Lipschitz** on $\mathcal
{C}$, hence (uniformly) continuous with respect to $d_H$.

**Key estimate.** Let $A,B\in\mathcal{C}$. For every $a\in A$ and every $b\in B$
,
$$d(x,B)\le d(x,b)\le d(x,a)+d(a,b).$$
Taking the infimum over $b\in B$ gives
$$d(x,B)\le d(x,a)+d(a,B)\le d(x,a)+d_H(A,B),\qquad\forall\,a\in A.$$
Now take the infimum over $a\in A$:
$$d(x,B)\le d(x,A)+d_H(A,B). \tag{1}$$

By symmetry (swapping the roles of $A$ and $B$),
$$d(x,A)\le d(x,B)+d_H(A,B). \tag{2}$$

Combining (1) and (2),
$$\boxed{\;\bigl|d(x,A)-d(x,B)\bigr|\le d_H(A,B)\;}\tag{$\ast$}$$

**Conclusion.** The estimate $(\ast)$ shows that $C\mapsto d(x,C)$ is 1-Lipschit
z continuous on $(\mathcal{C},d_H)$. In particular it is continuous: for every $
\varepsilon>0$, choosing $\delta=\varepsilon$ gives
$$d_H(A,B)<\delta\implies\bigl|d(x,A)-d(x,B)\bigr|<\varepsilon.$$

**Remark on the hypotheses.** The proof uses only that $A$ and $B$ are non-empty
 bounded closed subsets of a metric space. The Banach-space structure and convex
ity are not needed for the continuity result; they only ensure that $d_H$ is wel
l-defined (finite) on the class $\mathcal{C}$ and that $\mathcal{C}$ is a natura
l domain for the map. The distance-to-a-set function $d(x,\cdot)$ is always 1-Li
pschitz with respect to Hausdorff distance on any class of non-empty bounded clo
sed sets.

Therefore, **yes**, the mapping $C\mapsto d(x,C)$ is continuous with respect to
the Hausdorff distance (indeed, 1-Lipschitz).
