## Proof

Let $f(z) = (z^2 - 1)^{1/3} = \bigl((z-1)(z+1)\bigr)^{1/3}$, with the branch cho
sen so that $f(3) = \sqrt[3]{8} = 2$ (the real positive cube root). The branch p
oints are $z = 1$ and $z = -1$, both lying inside the circle $|z| = 3$.

We analytically continue $f$ along the semicircle $\gamma: z = 3e^{i\theta}$, $\
theta: 0 \to -\pi$ (the lower half-plane), from $z = 3$ to $z = -3$.

**Tracking the argument of $(z-1)$.** Along $\gamma$, $\operatorname{Im}(z) \le
0$, hence $\operatorname{Im}(z-1) \le 0$: the curve traced by $z - 1$ lies entir
ely in the closed lower half-plane. It starts at $z - 1 = 2$ (argument $0$) and
ends at $z - 1 = -4$ (argument $-\pi$, reached through the lower half-plane). Si
nce the curve never crosses the origin (the branch point $z = 1$ lies on the rea
l axis, i.e. on the chord closing the semicircle, not strictly inside the lower
half-disk), the argument varies continuously:

$$\Delta\arg(z - 1) = -\pi.$$

**Tracking the argument of $(z+1)$.** By the identical reasoning, $z + 1$ traces
 a curve in the closed lower half-plane from $4$ (argument $0$) to $-2$ (argumen
t $-\pi$), never crossing the origin:

$$\Delta\arg(z + 1) = -\pi.$$

**Total change of argument.** Therefore

$$\Delta\arg(z^2 - 1) = \Delta\arg(z-1) + \Delta\arg(z+1) = -\pi + (-\pi) = -2\p
i.$$

**Effect on the cube root.** Under analytic continuation, $(z^2-1)^{1/3}$ acquir
es the phase factor $e^{i\,\Delta\arg(z^2-1)/3} = e^{-2\pi i/3}$.

At the endpoint $z = -3$: $z^2 - 1 = 8$, and with the continued branch,

$$f(-3) = \sqrt[3]{8}\cdot e^{-2\pi i/3} = 2\,e^{-2\pi i/3}.$$

Computing explicitly:

$$e^{-2\pi i/3} = \cos\!\left(-\tfrac{2\pi}{3}\right) + i\sin\!\left(-\tfrac{2\p
i}{3}\right) = -\tfrac{1}{2} - i\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2},$$

$$f(-3) = 2\left(-\tfrac{1}{2} - i\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) = -1 - i\sqrt{3}.$$

$$\boxed{f(-3) = -1 - i\sqrt{3}.}$$
