## Proof

Substituting $Y = X^2$, we get $Y^2 - 6Y - 2 = 0$, so $Y = 3 \pm \sqrt{11}$. The

$$\alpha = \sqrt{3+\sqrt{11}},\quad -\alpha,\quad \beta = \sqrt{3-\sqrt{11}},\qu
ad -\beta.$$
Note $3 - \sqrt{11} < 0$, so $\beta$ is purely imaginary. A key relation is:
$$\alpha\beta = \sqrt{(3+\sqrt{11})(3-\sqrt{11})} = \sqrt{9-11} = \sqrt{-2}.$$

**Step 2: Irreducibility over $\mathbb{Q}$.**

By the rational root theorem, $f$ has no rational roots ($\pm1, \pm2$ all fail).
 To factor as two quadratics over $\mathbb{Q}$, write $f = (X^2+aX+b)(X^2-aX+c)$
, giving $b+c-a^2=-6$, $a(c-b)=0$, $bc=-2$. If $a=0$: $b+c=-6$, $bc=-2$, so $b,c
 = -3\pm\sqrt{11}\notin\mathbb{Q}$. If $c=b$: $b^2=-2$, impossible over $\mathbb
{R}$. Hence $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, and $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\math
bb{Q}]=4$.

**Step 3: The splitting field and its degree.**

Since $\sqrt{11} = \alpha^2 - 3 \in \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ and $\beta = \sqrt{-2}/\
alpha$, the splitting field is:
$$K = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha, \beta) = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha, \sqrt{-2}).$$
The field $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ is a subfield of $\mathbb{R}$ (since $\alpha$ is
real), so $\sqrt{-2}\notin\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$. Therefore:
$$:\mathbb{Q}] = :\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)]\cdot[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}] =
2 \cdot 4 = 8.$$
Since $f$ is separable (char 0), $K/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois and $|G| = 8$.

**Step 4: Identify the group structure.**

Since $f$ is irreducible, $G$ acts transitively on the 4 roots, so $G$ is a tran
sitive subgroup of $S_4$ of order 8. The only such subgroup (up to isomorphism)
is the dihedral group $D_4$: any subgroup of order 8 in $S_4$ is a Sylow 2-subgr
oup, and all Sylow 2-subgroups of $S_4$ are isomorphic to $D_4$.

To confirm the structure explicitly, define two automorphisms:
- $r$: $r(\alpha) = \beta$, $r(\beta) = -\alpha$. This cycles $\alpha \to \beta
\to -\alpha \to -\beta \to \alpha$, a 4-cycle, so $r$ has order 4.
- $s$: $s(\alpha) = -\alpha$, $s(\beta) = \beta$. This is a transposition $(\alp
ha\; -\alpha)$, so $s$ has order 2.

Check the dihedral relation $srs = r^{-1}$:
$$srs(\alpha) = sr(-\alpha) = s(-\beta) = -\beta = r^{-1}(\alpha). \quad\checkma
rk$$

So $G = \langle r, s \mid r^4 = s^2 = e,\; srs = r^{-1}\rangle \cong D_4$.

**Step 5: Cross-check via the discriminant.**

$$\Delta = \prod_{i<j}(r_i-r_j)^2 = 16(\alpha\beta)^2(\alpha^2-\beta^2)^4 = 16(-
2)(2\sqrt{11})^4 = -2^9 \cdot 11^2.$$
This is not a square in $\mathbb{Q}$, so $G \not\subseteq A_4$, which is consist
ent with $D_4 \not\subseteq A_4$ (as $D_4$ contains odd permutations).

**Conclusion.** The Galois group of $f(X) = X^4 - 6X^2 - 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is

$$\operatorname{Gal}(f/\mathbb{Q}) \cong D_4.$$
