## Proof

**Key fact.** For any natural number $n$ with $d(n)$ divisors, the product of al
l natural divisors of $n$ equals $n^{d(n)/2}$. (Divisors pair as $(d, n/d)$, eac
h pair contributing a factor of $n$; when $n$ is a perfect square the middle div
isor $\sqrt{n}$ is self-paired, and the formula still holds.)

**Trailing zeros.** The number of trailing zeros of $n^{d(n)/2}$ is
$$v_{10}\!\left(n^{d(n)/2}\right) = \min\!\big(v_2(n),\, v_5(n)\big)\cdot \tfrac
{d(n)}{2},$$
where $v_p$ denotes the $p$-adic valuation. We need this to equal $2001$, i.e.
$$\min(a,b)\cdot d(n) = 4002, \qquad a := v_2(n),\; b := v_5(n).$$

**Factorization.** $4002 = 2\times 2001 = 2\times 3\times 23\times 29$.

**Construction.** Set $m = \min(a,b) = 1$, so $d(n) = 4002$. Take
$$n = 2^{1}\cdot 5^{2}\cdot 3^{22}\cdot 7^{28}.$$

Then:
- $a = v_2(n) = 1$, $b = v_5(n) = 2$, so $\min(a,b) = 1 \geq 1$ (both valuations
 positive, so trailing zeros are well-defined).
- $d(n) = (1+1)(2+1)(22+1)(28+1) = 2\times 3\times 23\times 29 = 4002$.
- Product of all divisors $= n^{d(n)/2} = n^{2001}$.
- $v_2\!\left(n^{2001}\right) = 1\times 2001 = 2001$.
- $v_5\!\left(n^{2001}\right) = 2\times 2001 = 4002$.
- $v_{10}\!\left(n^{2001}\right) = \min(2001,\, 4002) = 2001$.

Therefore the product of all natural divisors of $n = 2^{1}\cdot 5^{2}\cdot 3^{2
2}\cdot 7^{28}$ ends with **exactly 2001 zeros**.

**Conclusion.** Such a natural number exists. $\blacksquare$
