The Riemann curvature tensor measures the failure of parallel transport to be pa
th-independent, or equivalently the non-commutativity of covariant derivatives.
Its components are defined by the action on a vector field $V^\alpha$:

$$[\nabla_\gamma, \nabla_\delta] V^\alpha = R^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}\, V^\
beta$$

(with the sign convention $R^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta} V^\beta = \nabla_\gamm
a\nabla_\delta V^\alpha - \nabla_\delta\nabla_\gamma V^\alpha$).

---

## General expression

For an affine connection $\nabla$ with connection coefficients (Christoffel symb
ols) $\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma}$, the components of the Riemann tensor are

$$\boxed{\;R^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta} \;=\; \partial_\gamma\,\Gamma^\alpha{}
_{\beta\delta} \;-\; \partial_\delta\,\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma} \;+\; \Gamma
^\alpha{}_{\mu\gamma}\,\Gamma^\mu{}_{\beta\delta} \;-\; \Gamma^\alpha{}_{\mu\del
ta}\,\Gamma^\mu{}_{\beta\gamma}\;}$$

This is the **general expression**, valid in any frame (coordinate or orthonorma
l), and it is the form requested.

---

## Derivation (sketch)

Expand the covariant derivative of a vector:
$$\nabla_\gamma V^\alpha = \partial_\gamma V^\alpha + \Gamma^\alpha{}_{\mu\gamma
} V^\mu.$$

Applying $\nabla_\delta$ and then $\nabla_\gamma$ and subtracting (in the revers
ed order), the terms involving second derivatives of $V$ cancel, leaving

$$[\nabla_\gamma,\nabla_\delta]V^\alpha = \bigl(\partial_\gamma\Gamma^\alpha{}_{
\beta\delta} - \partial_\delta\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma} + \Gamma^\alpha{}_{\
mu\gamma}\Gamma^\mu{}_{\beta\delta} - \Gamma^\alpha{}_{\mu\delta}\Gamma^\mu{}_{\
beta\gamma}\bigr)V^\beta.$$

The coefficient of $V^\beta$ is, by definition, $R^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}$
, giving the boxed formula.

---

## Specialization to an orthonormal frame with torsion-free connection

### Orthonormal frame

In an orthonormal frame $\{e_a\}$ the metric components are constant,
$$g_{ab} = \delta_{ab} \quad (\text{Riemannian}), \qquad \eta_{ab} \quad (\text{
pseudo-Riemannian}),$$
so raising and lowering indices is done with $\delta_{ab}$ (or $\eta_{ab}$) and
introduces **no position-dependent factors**. Consequently the fully covariant f
orm reads, with all indices lowered,

$$R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta} = \partial_\gamma\,\Gamma_{\alpha\beta\delta} - \p
artial_\delta\,\Gamma_{\alpha\beta\gamma} + \Gamma_{\alpha\mu\gamma}\,\Gamma^\mu
{}_{\beta\delta} - \Gamma_{\alpha\mu\delta}\,\Gamma^\mu{}_{\beta\gamma},$$

where $\Gamma_{\alpha\beta\gamma} := g_{\alpha\mu}\Gamma^\mu{}_{\beta\gamma}$. I
n the strictly orthonormal (Riemannian) case $g_{\alpha\mu}=\delta_{\alpha\mu}$,
 so $\Gamma_{\alpha\beta\gamma} = \Gamma^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma}$ numerically, an
d the mixed and fully-covariant expressions coincide component-wise.

### Torsion-free

Torsion-free means the connection is symmetric in its lower indices:
$$\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma} = \Gamma^\alpha{}_{\gamma\beta} \quad\Longleftri
ghtarrow\quad T^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma} := \Gamma^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma} - \Gamma
^\alpha{}_{\gamma\beta} = 0.$$

This symmetry does **not** change the algebraic form of $R^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma
\delta}$; it is an additional constraint on the $\Gamma$'s that appear in the fo
rmula. Combined with metric compatibility ($\nabla g = 0$, automatic in an ortho
normal frame with $\partial g = 0$), it yields the standard metric (Levi-Civita)
 connection, for which the Christoffel symbols reduce to

$$\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma} = \tfrac{1}{2} g^{\alpha\mu}\bigl(\partial_\beta
 g_{\mu\gamma} + \partial_\gamma g_{\mu\beta} - \partial_\mu g_{\beta\gamma}\big
r).$$

In an orthonormal frame $g_{\mu\nu}=\delta_{\mu\nu}$ is constant **at each point
**, but the frame vectors themselves vary from point to point, so the $\Gamma^\a
lpha{}_{\beta\gamma}$ are generally **non-zero** (they encode the rotation of th
e orthonormal frame as one moves across the manifold). The Riemann tensor theref
ore is generally non-vanishing and is still given by the boxed formula with thes
e connection coefficients.

## Explanation of the terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma}$ | Connection coefficients (Christoffel symbols
); encode how the basis changes from point to point and how vectors are parallel
-transported. |
| $\partial_\gamma \Gamma^\alpha{}_{\beta\delta}$ | Ordinary partial derivative
of the connection; measures the "rate of change" of the connection in the $e_\ga
mma$ direction. |
| $\partial_\gamma\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\beta\delta} - \partial_\delta\Gamma^\alpha{}
_{\beta\gamma}$ | Antisymmetric part of the connection's derivative; captures th
e "curl"-like contribution to curvature. |
| $\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\mu\gamma}\Gamma^\mu{}_{\beta\delta} - \Gamma^\alpha{}_{\mu\
delta}\Gamma^\mu{}_{\beta\gamma}$ | Quadratic (commutator) term in the connectio
n; reflects the non-abelian (matrix) nature of the connection as a Lie-algebra-v
alued object. |
| $R^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}$ | The result: measures the rotation a vector
undergoes when parallel-transported around an infinitesimal loop spanned by $e_\
gamma, e_\delta$. |
| Torsion-free condition $\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma}=\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\gamma\
beta}$ | Guarantees the connection comes from a metric (Levi-Civita) and removes
 the antisymmetric-in-lower-indices part of $\Gamma$, so curvature is purely met
ric in origin. |
| Orthonormal frame $g_{ab}=\delta_{ab}$ | Makes index raising/lowering trivial
and constant; the connection coefficients here are the **spin connection / Ricci
 rotation coefficients**, and $R^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}$ coincides numeric
ally with $R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}$. |

## Symmetries (consequences of orthonormal + torsion-free)

With a metric-compatible, torsion-free connection in an orthonormal frame, the R
iemann tensor satisfies:

1. **Antisymmetry in the last two indices:** $R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta} = -R_{
\alpha\beta\delta\gamma}$ (built in by the $\partial_\gamma\Gamma_{\cdot\cdot\de
lta}-\partial_\delta\Gamma_{\cdot\cdot\gamma}$ and commutator structure).
2. **Antisymmetry in the first two indices:** $R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta} = -R_
{\beta\alpha\gamma\delta}$ (follows from metric compatibility).
3. **Symmetry under pair exchange:** $R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta} = R_{\gamma\de
lta\alpha\beta}$.
4. **First Bianchi identity (cyclic):** $R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta} + R_{\alpha
\delta\beta\gamma} + R_{\alpha\gamma\delta\beta} = 0$ (this uses torsion-freenes
s explicitly).

These reduce the $\tfrac{n^2(n^2-1)}{12}$ naive components to the independent se
t, e.g. $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\cdot\frac{n(n-1)}{2}/... $ — for $n=4$ giving 20 indep
endent components.

## Summary

The general expression for the Riemann curvature tensor components, valid in an
orthonormal frame with a torsion-free (metric) connection, is

$$R^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta} = \partial_\gamma\,\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\beta\delta
} - \partial_\delta\,\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma} + \Gamma^\alpha{}_{\mu\gamma}
\,\Gamma^\mu{}_{\beta\delta} - \Gamma^\alpha{}_{\mu\delta}\,\Gamma^\mu{}_{\beta\
gamma},$$

where:
- $\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma}$ are the connection coefficients (Ricci rotatio
n coefficients in the orthonormal frame), symmetric in $\beta,\gamma$ because th
e connection is torsion-free;
- the first two terms are the antisymmetrized partial derivative of the connecti
on (the "curl" part);
- the last two terms are the commutator of the connection viewed as a matrix-val
ued 1-form (the "non-abelian" part);
- the orthonormality of the frame makes $g_{ab}=\delta_{ab}$ constant, so index
raising/lowering is trivial and $R^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta} = R_{\alpha\beta
\gamma\delta}$ component-wise, while the connection coefficients remain generall
y non-zero because the frame itself rotates across the manifold.
