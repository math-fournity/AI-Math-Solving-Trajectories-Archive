We evaluate
$$L = \lim_{k \to \infty} \int_{0}^{1} x^{-1/2}\cos(x^{k})\,e^{-x^{2}/k}\,dx.$$

**Step 1: Pointwise limit of the integrand.**

Define $f_k(x) = x^{-1/2}\cos(x^{k})\,e^{-x^{2}/k}$ on $(0,1]$.

- For $x \in (0,1)$: as $k \to \infty$, $x^{k} \to 0$, so $\cos(x^{k}) \to \cos(
0) = 1$; and $e^{-x^{2}/k} \to e^{0} = 1$. Hence $f_k(x) \to x^{-1/2}$.
- For $x = 1$: $f_k(1) = 1 \cdot \cos(1) \cdot e^{-1/k} \to \cos(1)$. This diffe
rs from $x^{-1/2}\big|_{x=1} = 1$, but $\{1\}$ is a set of measure zero and does
 not affect the integral.

Thus $f_k \to x^{-1/2}$ a.e. on $[0,1]$.

**Step 2: Dominating function.**

For all $k \ge 1$ and $x \in (0,1]$:
$$|f_k(x)| = x^{-1/2}\,|\cos(x^{k})|\,e^{-x^{2}/k} \le x^{-1/2}\cdot 1 \cdot 1 =
 x^{-1/2}.$$

The function $g(x) = x^{-1/2}$ is integrable on $(0,1]$:
$$\int_0^1 x^{-1/2}\,dx = \left[2x^{1/2}\right]_0^1 = 2 < \infty.$$

**Step 3: Apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem.**

Since $|f_k| \le g$ with $g \in L^1([0,1])$, and $f_k \to x^{-1/2}$ a.e., the DC
T gives
$$L = \int_0^1 \lim_{k\to\infty} f_k(x)\,dx = \int_0^1 x^{-1/2}\,dx = 2.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\lim_{k \to \infty} \int_{0}^{1} x^{-1/2}\cos(x^{k})\,e^{-x^{2}/k}\,dx
= 2.}$$
