**Setup.** There are 7 doors, 2 hiding prizes. Bob picks one door. The host (who
 knows where the prizes are) then opens 3 of the remaining 6 doors, and exactly
one of those three hides a prize. Bob switches to one of the 3 still-unopened do
ors (other than his original). We find the probability his new door hides a priz
e.

**Condition on Bob's initial pick.** Let $A$ = "Bob's initial door hides a prize
" and $B$ = "Bob's initial door is empty."

$$P(A)=\tfrac{2}{7},\qquad P(B)=\tfrac{5}{7}.$$

**Case A: Bob initially picked a prize (probability $2/7$).**
Among the other 6 doors there is $1$ prize and $5$ empties. The host opens 3 doo
rs containing exactly 1 prize, so he opens that single prize plus 2 empties. The
 3 remaining unopened doors (among the 6) therefore contain **0 prizes**. If Bob
 switches to any one of them,

$$P(\text{prize}\mid A,\text{switch})=0.$$

**Case B: Bob initially picked an empty door (probability $5/7$).**
Among the other 6 doors there are $2$ prizes and $4$ empties. The host opens 3 d
oors containing exactly 1 prize, i.e. $1$ prize $+$ $2$ empties. The 3 remaining
 unopened doors therefore contain $2-1=1$ prize among 3 doors. Since the host's
choice is uniform among valid configurations, that single prize is uniformly dis
tributed over the 3 remaining doors. Bob switches to one of them:

$$P(\text{prize}\mid B,\text{switch})=\tfrac{1}{3}.$$

**Total probability.**

$$P(\text{prize}\mid\text{switch})=P(A)\cdot 0+P(B)\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}=\tfrac{5}{7
}\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}=\tfrac{5}{21}.$$

**Irreducibility.** $\gcd(5,21)=1$, so $\frac{5}{21}$ is irreducible with $a=5,\
;b=21$.

$$a+b=5+21=26.$$
