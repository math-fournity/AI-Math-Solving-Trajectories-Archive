Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether an infinite non-abelian gro
up can have all its irreducible (finite-dimensional complex) characters be linea
r (degree 1).

**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

**Construction.** Let $S$ be a finitely generated infinite simple group. Such gr
oups exist — for example, Higman (1974) constructed a finitely presented infinit
e simple group, and Burger–Mozes (2000) gave further examples.

We claim $S$ is an infinite non-abelian group all of whose irreducible character
s are linear.

---

**Step 1: $S$ is infinite and non-abelian.**

$S$ is infinite by construction. The only abelian simple groups are the cyclic g
roups $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ of prime order, which are finite. Since $S$ is in
finite and simple, it must be non-abelian. ✓

---

**Step 2: Every finite-dimensional complex representation of $S$ is trivial.**

Let $\rho: S \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})$ be any finite-dimensional representa
tion. The kernel $\ker(\rho)$ is a normal subgroup of $S$. Since $S$ is simple,
$\ker(\rho) \in \{1, S\}$.

Suppose for contradiction that $\ker(\rho) = 1$, so $\rho$ is faithful. Then $S$
 embeds into $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})$, i.e., $S$ is a **linear group**.

By **Mal'cev's theorem**, every finitely generated linear group (over a field) i
s **residually finite**.

But a **residually finite simple group is finite**: for any $g \neq 1$, residual
 finiteness provides a normal subgroup $N$ of finite index with $g \notin N$. Si
mplicity forces $N = 1$ (since $N \neq G$), so $S$ embeds into the finite group
$S/N$, making $S$ finite — contradicting the infinitude of $S$.

Therefore $S$ is **not** linear, so $\ker(\rho) \neq 1$, which forces $\ker(\rho
) = S$. Hence $\rho$ is trivial. ✓

---

**Step 3: All irreducible characters are linear.**

Since every finite-dimensional representation of $S$ is trivial, the only irredu
cible finite-dimensional representation is the trivial representation $\mathbf{1
}: S \to \mathbb{C}^*$, which has **degree 1**. Its character $\chi(g) = 1$ for
all $g \in S$ is linear. ✓

---

**Remark (a non-degenerate example).** For an example with infinitely many irred
ucible characters, take $G = \mathbb{Z} \times S$. Then $G$ is infinite and non-
abelian. Any finite-dimensional representation $\rho: G \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathb
b{C})$ restricts to a representation of $S$, which is trivial by Step 2, so $\rh
o$ factors through $G/S \cong \mathbb{Z}$. Since $\mathbb{Z}$ is abelian, all it
s irreducible representations are 1-dimensional. Thus every irreducible characte
r of $G$ is linear, and there are infinitely many of them (parametrized by $\mat
hbb{C}^*$).

---

**Conclusion.** A finitely generated infinite simple group $S$ is an infinite no
n-abelian group whose only irreducible character is the trivial (linear) charact
er. Therefore, such a group exists. $\blacksquare$
