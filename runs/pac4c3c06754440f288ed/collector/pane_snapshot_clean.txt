## Proof

We seek the number of maximal ideals of the form $(x-a, y-b)$, with $a,b \in \ma
thbb{C}$, that contain the principal ideal $(f(x,y))$, where $f$ is irreducible
in $\mathbb{C}[x,y]$.

**Key observation.** A maximal ideal $(x-a, y-b)$ contains $(f)$ if and only if
$f \in (x-a, y-b)$, which (via the evaluation homomorphism $\mathbb{C}[x,y] \to
\mathbb{C}$, $x\mapsto a$, $y\mapsto b$) holds if and only if
$$f(a,b) = 0.$$
Thus the maximal ideals in question are in bijection with the points $(a,b)\in\m
athbb{C}^2$ lying on the affine variety $V(f)$.

We show $V(f)$ is **infinite** for every irreducible $f\in\mathbb{C}[x,y]$.

**Case 1: $f$ involves $x$.** Write $f = c_d(y)\,x^d + \cdots + c_0(y)$ with $d\
ge 1$ and $c_d(y)\neq 0$. For every $b\in\mathbb{C}$ with $c_d(b)\neq 0$, the sp
ecialization $f(x,b)$ is a non-constant polynomial in $x$ of degree $d$. Since $
\mathbb{C}$ is algebraically closed, $f(x,b)$ has at least one root $a\in\mathbb
{C}$, giving a point $(a,b)\in V(f)$. There are infinitely many such $b$ (all of
 $\mathbb{C}$ minus finitely many zeros of $c_d$), and each yields at least one
point. Hence $V(f)$ is infinite.

**Case 2: $f$ does not involve $x$, i.e., $f\in\mathbb{C}[y]$.** Since $f$ is ir
reducible in $\mathbb{C}[x,y]\cong\mathbb{C}[y][x]$ and lies in $\mathbb{C}[y]$,
 it is irreducible in $\mathbb{C}[y]$ (a nontrivial factorization in $\mathbb{C}
[y]$ would be one in $\mathbb{C}[x,y]$). Because $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraically c
losed, the only irreducible polynomials in $\mathbb{C}[y]$ are linear, so $f = \
alpha(y - b_0)$ for some $\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^\times$, $b_0\in\mathbb{C}$. Then
$$V(f) = \{(a, b_0) : a\in\mathbb{C}\},$$
which is again infinite.

In both cases $V(f)$ is infinite, so there are **infinitely many** maximal ideal
s of the form $(x-a, y-b)$ containing $(f)$.
