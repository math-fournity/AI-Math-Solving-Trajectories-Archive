## Proof

We wish to maximize $\displaystyle I = \int_1^3 \frac{f(x)}{x}\,dx$ subject to $
-1\le f(x)\le 1$ and $\int_1^3 f(x)\,dx=0$.

**Candidate.** Since $g(x)=1/x$ is strictly decreasing, we should make $f$ as la
rge as possible where $g$ is large (small $x$) and as small as possible where $g
$ is small (large $x$). Set
$$
f^*(x)=\begin{cases}+1,&1\le x\le 2,\\ -1,&2<x\le 3.\end{cases}
$$
Then $\int_1^3 f^*=(2-1)-(3-2)=0$, so $f^*$ is feasible, and
$$
I^*=\int_1^2\frac{dx}{x}-\int_2^3\frac{dx}{x}=\ln 2-(\ln 3-\ln 2)=2\ln 2-\ln 3=\
ln\frac{4}{3}.
$$

**Optimality.** Let $f$ be any feasible function and set $h=f^*-f$. Then $\int_1
^3 h\,dx=0$. On $[1,2]$, $f^*=1\ge f$, so $h\ge 0$; on $[2,3]$, $f^*=-1\le f$, s
o $h\le 0$.

Since $g(x)=1/x$ is decreasing with $g(2)=1/2$:
- On $[1,2]$: $h\ge 0$ and $g(x)\ge 1/2$, so $h\,g(x)\ge \tfrac12 h$.
- On $[2,3]$: $h\le 0$ and $g(x)\le 1/2$, so $h\bigl(g(x)-\tfrac12\bigr)\ge 0$,
i.e. $h\,g(x)\ge \tfrac12 h$.

Adding,
$$
\int_1^3 h\,g\,dx\;\ge\;\frac12\int_1^2 h\,dx+\frac12\int_2^3 h\,dx=\frac12\int_
1^3 h\,dx=0.
$$
Therefore $\int_1^3 f^*\,g\,dx\ge\int_1^3 f\,g\,dx$ for every feasible $f$.

Equality requires $h\bigl(g-\tfrac12\bigr)=0$ a.e.; since $g\ne 1/2$ a.e., this
forces $h=0$ a.e., i.e. $f=f^*$ a.e. Hence the maximum is unique (up to measure
zero) and equals

$$
\boxed{\ln\frac{4}{3}}.
$$
