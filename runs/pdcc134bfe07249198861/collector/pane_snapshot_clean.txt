## Solution

**Simplifying the inner trig expression.**

Using $\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{4}-x\right)=\frac{1-\tan x}{1+\tan x}$:

$$1-\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{4}-x\right)=\frac{2\tan x}{1+\tan x}.$$

Let $u(x)=\frac{2\tan x}{1+\tan x}$. Note $u(x)\to 0$ as $x\to 0$, and expanding
:

$$u(x)=2\tan x-2\tan^2 x+\cdots = 2x-2x^2+\tfrac{2}{3}x^3+\cdots$$

So the first factor in the numerator is

$$A(x):=1-\cos(u(x))=\tfrac{u(x)^2}{2}+\cdots = 2x^2-4x^3+\cdots$$

hence $\dfrac{A(x)}{x^2}\to 2$ as $x\to 0$.

**Simplifying the oscillatory argument.**

$$n-\sqrt{n^2-8n}=\frac{8n}{n+\sqrt{n^2-8n}}\to \frac{8n}{2n}=4\quad(n\to\infty)
.$$

Thus $\sin\!\big((n-\sqrt{n^2-8n})x\big)\to \sin(4x)$.

**Right limit ($x\to 0^+$, small positive $x$).** Here $1+x>1$ so $(1+x)^n\to\in
fty$. The dominant terms give

$$f(x)=\frac{A(x)(1+x)^n+\lambda\sin(4x)}{x^2(1+x)^n+x}\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{
A(x)}{x^2}\;\longrightarrow\;2.$$

**Left limit ($x\to 0^-$, with $-1<x<0$).** Here $0<1+x<1$ so $(1+x)^n\to 0$. Th
e limit becomes

$$f(x)=\frac{0+\lambda\sin(4x)}{0+x}=\frac{\lambda\sin(4x)}{x}\;\longrightarrow\
;4\lambda.$$

**Continuity at $x=0$.** Requiring the two one-sided limits to agree:

$$4\lambda=2\implies \lambda=\tfrac12,\qquad f(0)=2.$$

**Final value:**

$$f(0)+2\lambda = 2 + 2\cdot\tfrac12 = \boxed{3}.$$
