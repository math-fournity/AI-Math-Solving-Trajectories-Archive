We compute $\sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\, n^{\ell(\sigma)}$
, where $\ell(\sigma)$ is the number of cycles of $\sigma$.

**Step 1: Relate sign to cycle count.** A permutation $\sigma$ with $\ell(\sigma
)$ cycles satisfies
$$\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma) = (-1)^{n - \ell(\sigma)},$$
since each cycle of length $m$ contributes $(-1)^{m-1}$ to the sign, and $\sum (
m_i - 1) = n - \ell(\sigma)$.

**Step 2: Rewrite the sum via Stirling numbers.** Let $c(n,k) = \left[{n \atop k
}\right]$ denote the unsigned Stirling number of the first kind, i.e. the number
 of permutations of $n$ elements with exactly $k$ cycles. Then
$$\sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\, n^{\ell(\sigma)} = \sum_{k=
1}^{n} c(n,k)\, (-1)^{n-k}\, n^{k}.$$

**Step 3: Use the falling-factorial identity.** The signed Stirling numbers of t
he first kind are $s(n,k) = (-1)^{n-k} c(n,k)$, and they give the expansion of t
he falling factorial:
$$x^{\underline{n}} = x(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-n+1) = \sum_{k=0}^{n} s(n,k)\, x^{k}.$
$
Substituting $x = n$:
$$\sum_{k=0}^{n} s(n,k)\, n^{k} = n^{\underline{n}} = n(n-1)(n-2)\cdots 1 = n!.$
$
Since $s(n,0) = 0$ for $n \ge 1$, the $k=0$ term vanishes, so
$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} (-1)^{n-k} c(n,k)\, n^{k} = n!.$$

**Step 4: Verification.** For $n=2$: identity contributes $2^2\cdot(+1)=4$, tran
sposition contributes $2^1\cdot(-1)=-2$; total $2 = 2!$. ✓
For $n=3$: identity $3^3=27$; three transpositions $3\cdot 3^2\cdot(-1) = -27$;
two 3-cycles $2\cdot 3^1\cdot(+1)=6$; total $6 = 3!$. ✓

Therefore,
$$\boxed{\sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\, n^{\ell(\sigma)} = n
!}.$$
