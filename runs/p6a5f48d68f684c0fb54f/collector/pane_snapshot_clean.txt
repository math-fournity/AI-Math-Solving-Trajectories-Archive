## Galois Group of $x^5 - x - 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$

**Claim.** The Galois group of $f(x) = x^5 - x - 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is $S_5$.

### Step 1: Irreducibility over $\mathbb{Q}$

Reduce $f$ modulo 3: $\bar{f}(x) = x^5 + 2x + 2 \in \mathbb{F}_3[x]$.

**No linear factors:** $\bar{f}(0)=2$, $\bar{f}(1)=1+2+2\equiv 2$, $\bar{f}(2)=3
2+4+2\equiv 2$. No roots in $\mathbb{F}_3$.

**No quadratic factors:** The three monic irreducible quadratics over $\mathbb{F
}_3$ are $x^2+1$, $x^2+x+2$, $x^2+2x+2$. Polynomial long division of $\bar{f}$ b
y each yields nonzero remainders ($2$, $x+2$, $x+2$ respectively), so none divid
e $\bar{f}$.

For a degree-5 polynomial, every nontrivial factorization involves either a line
ar factor or a quadratic factor (the only partition of 5 without parts 1 or 2 is
 $\{5\}$ itself, and $\{3,2\}$ requires a quadratic). Since both are ruled out,
$\bar{f}$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_3$. By Gauss's lemma, $f$ is irreducib
le over $\mathbb{Q}$.

Hence the Galois group $G$ acts transitively on the 5 roots, so $5 \mid |G|$.

### Step 2: Discriminant

Using the formula for the discriminant of $x^n + ax + b$:
$$\Delta = (-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\bigl[n^n b^{n-1} + (-1)^{n-1}(n-1)^{n-1}a^n\bigr],$$
with $n=5$, $a=-1$, $b=-1$:
$$\Delta = (-1)^{10}\bigl[5^5\cdot(-1)^4 + (-1)^4\cdot 4^4\cdot(-1)^5\bigr] = 31
25 - 256 = 2869 = 19 \times 151.$$

Since $2869$ is squarefree, it is not a perfect square, so $G \not\subseteq A_5$
.

### Step 3: Real roots and complex conjugation

$f'(x) = 5x^4 - 1$, with critical points at $x = \pm 5^{-1/4} \approx \pm 0.669$

- $f(5^{-1/4}) \approx 0.134 - 0.669 - 1 \approx -1.535 < 0$ (local min),
- $f(-5^{-1/4}) \approx -0.134 + 0.669 - 1 \approx -0.465 < 0$ (local max).

Since $f(x)\to -\infty$ as $x\to -\infty$ and $f$ increases to the local max (st
ill negative), then decreases to the local min (still negative), then increases
to $+\infty$, the function crosses the $x$-axis **exactly once**. So $f$ has **1
 real root** and **4 non-real roots** (two conjugate pairs).

Complex conjugation fixes the real root and swaps each conjugate pair, giving an
 element of $G$ with cycle type $(2,2)$ — a product of two disjoint transpositio
ns.

### Step 4: Dedekind's theorem mod 2

Since $2 \nmid \Delta = 2869$, we may apply Dedekind's theorem. Reduce $f$ mod 2

$$\bar{f}(x) = x^5 + x + 1 \in \mathbb{F}_2[x].$$

Factorization: $x^5 + x + 1 = (x^2+x+1)(x^3+x^2+1)$, where both factors are irre
ducible over $\mathbb{F}_2$ (neither has roots: $x^2+x+1$ gives $1$ at both $0,1
$; $x^3+x^2+1$ gives $1$ at both $0,1$).

By Dedekind's theorem, $G$ contains an element with cycle type $(2,3)$ — a produ
ct of a 2-cycle and a disjoint 3-cycle — which has order $\operatorname{lcm}(2,3
)=6$.

### Step 5: Identifying $G$

The transitive subgroups of $S_5$ are:

| Subgroup | Order | Element orders |
|----------|-------|---------------|
| $\mathbb{Z}_5$ | 5 | $1, 5$ |
| $D_5$ | 10 | $1, 2, 5$ |
| $F_{20}=\operatorname{GA}(1,5)$ | 20 | $1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20$ |
| $A_5$ | 60 | $1, 2, 3, 5$ |
| $S_5$ | 120 | $1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, \ldots$ |

An element of order 6 appears **only** in $S_5$ (since $6 \nmid 5, 10, 20$ and $
A_5$ has no order-6 element). Therefore $G = S_5$.
