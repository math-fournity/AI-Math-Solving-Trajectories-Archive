**Claim:** The statement is **true**: $\mathbb{Q}(a,b)\cap\mathbb{Q}(c,d)=\mathb
b{Q}$.

**Proof.**

Since $\{a,b,c,d\}$ is algebraically independent over $\mathbb{Q}$, the evaluati
on map
$$\mathbb{Q}[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]\to\mathbb{Q}[a,b,c,d],\quad x_1\mapsto a,\;x_2\map
sto b,\;x_3\mapsto c,\;x_4\mapsto d$$
is an isomorphism. In particular, $\mathbb{Q}[a,b,c,d]$ is a UFD, and a polynomi
al in $\{a,b\}$ alone and a non-constant polynomial in $\{c,d\}$ alone share no
common irreducible factor (they involve disjoint variable sets).

Let $\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}(a,b)\cap\mathbb{Q}(c,d)$. Write
$$\alpha=\frac{f(a,b)}{g(a,b)}=\frac{h(c,d)}{k(c,d)},\qquad g\neq 0,\;k\neq 0,$$
with $f,g\in\mathbb{Q}[x_1,x_2]$, $h,k\in\mathbb{Q}[x_3,x_4]$. Reduce to lowest
terms: assume $\gcd(f,g)=1$ in $\mathbb{Q}[x_1,x_2]$ and $\gcd(h,k)=1$ in $\math
bb{Q}[x_3,x_4]$.

Cross-multiplying gives an identity in the UFD $\mathbb{Q}[a,b,c,d]$:
$$f(a,b)\,k(c,d)=g(a,b)\,h(c,d).\tag{$\ast$}$$

**Step 1: $g$ is constant.** Since $g(a,b)\mid f(a,b)\,k(c,d)$ and $\gcd(f,g)=1$
 in $\mathbb{Q}[x_1,x_2]$, every irreducible factor of $g$ (which lies in $\math
bb{Q}[x_1,x_2]$) must divide $k(c,d)$. But an irreducible in $\{x_1,x_2\}$ canno
t divide a polynomial in $\{x_3,x_4\}$ unless it is a unit. Hence $g$ has no non
-constant irreducible factors, so $g\in\mathbb{Q}^*$.

**Step 2: $h$ is constant.** Symmetrically, $h(c,d)\mid f(a,b)\,k(c,d)$ and $\gc
d(h,k)=1$ in $\mathbb{Q}[x_3,x_4]$, so every irreducible factor of $h$ (in $\{x_
3,x_4\}$) must divide $f(a,b)$ (in $\{x_1,x_2\}$). This forces $h\in\mathbb{Q}^*
$.

**Step 3: $f$ and $k$ are constant.** With $g=c_1$, $h=c_2$ ($c_1,c_2\in\mathbb{
Q}^*$), equation $(\ast)$ becomes
$$f(a,b)\,k(c,d)=c_1 c_2.$$
The left side is a product of a polynomial in $\{a,b\}$ and a polynomial in $\{c
,d\}$; for their product to be a nonzero constant, both must themselves be const
ants: $f=c_3$, $k=c_4$ with $c_3 c_4=c_1 c_2$.

**Conclusion.** $\alpha = c_3/c_1\in\mathbb{Q}$. Since $\alpha$ was arbitrary,
$$\mathbb{Q}(a,b)\cap\mathbb{Q}(c,d)\subseteq\mathbb{Q}.$$
The reverse inclusion $\mathbb{Q}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(a,b)\cap\mathbb{Q}(c,d)$ is
 immediate. Therefore
$$\boxed{\mathbb{Q}(a,b)\cap\mathbb{Q}(c,d)=\mathbb{Q}.}$$

The statement is **true**. $\blacksquare$
