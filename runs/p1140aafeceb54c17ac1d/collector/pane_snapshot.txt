## Proof

Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ be open. By hypothesis, each $u_n$ is harmonic
 in $\Omega$, i.e. $\Delta u_n = 0$ in $\Omega$, and $u_n \rightharpoonup u$ wea
kly in $L^2(\Omega)$.

**Step 1: Distributional equation for $u_n$.** Since $\Delta u_n = 0$ classicall
y, for every test function $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$, integrating by part
s twice (no boundary terms because $\varphi$ is compactly supported in $\Omega$)
 gives
$$
\int_\Omega u_n \,\Delta\varphi \,dx = \int_\Omega (\Delta u_n)\,\varphi\,dx = 0
. \tag{1}
$$

**Step 2: Pass to the weak limit.** For any fixed $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\Omega
)$, the function $g := \Delta\varphi$ belongs to $C_c^\infty(\Omega) \subset L^2
(\Omega)$. Weak convergence $u_n \rightharpoonup u$ in $L^2(\Omega)$ means preci
sely that for every $g \in L^2(\Omega)$,
$$
\int_\Omega u_n\, g\,dx \;\longrightarrow\; \int_\Omega u\, g\,dx.
$$
Taking $g = \Delta\varphi$ and using (1),
$$
0 = \int_\Omega u_n\,\Delta\varphi\,dx \;\longrightarrow\; \int_\Omega u\,\Delta
\varphi\,dx.
$$
Hence
$$
\int_\Omega u\,\Delta\varphi\,dx = 0 \qquad \forall\,\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\Ome
ga). \tag{2}
$$

**Step 3: Conclude $\Delta u = 0$.** Equation (2) says exactly that $\Delta u =
0$ in the sense of distributions on $\Omega$. By Weyl's lemma (elliptic regulari
ty for the Laplacian), any distributional solution of $\Delta u = 0$ is represen
ted by a smooth harmonic function; in particular $u$ has a smooth representative
 and $\Delta u = 0$ pointwise in $\Omega$.

Therefore $\Delta u = 0$ in $\Omega$. $\blacksquare$
