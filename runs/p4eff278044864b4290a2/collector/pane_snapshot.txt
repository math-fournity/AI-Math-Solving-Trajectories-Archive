## Proof

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains only the problem; no solut
ion, answer, or sketch is present. Proceeding.

**Setup.** For a positive integer $i$, let $v_5(i)$ denote the exponent of $5$ i
n the prime factorization of $i$. Since
$$P_n := 1^1\cdot 2^2\cdot 3^3\cdots n^n = \prod_{i=1}^{n} i^{\,i},$$
the $5$-adic valuation is
$$E(n) = v_5(P_n) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} i\,v_5(i).$$

**Reformulation via divisibility indicators.** Using $v_5(i)=\sum_{j\ge 1}\mathb
f{1}_{5^j\mid i}$ and switching finite sums (only $j\le \log_5 n$ contribute),
$$E(n)=\sum_{j\ge 1}\sum_{\substack{1\le i\le n\\ 5^j\mid i}} i.$$
The inner sum is the sum of all multiples of $5^j$ up to $n$. Writing $q_j:=\big
l\lfloor n/5^j\bigr\rfloor$, those multiples are $5^j,2\cdot 5^j,\dots,q_j\cdot
5^j$, hence
$$E(n)=\sum_{j\ge 1} 5^j\cdot\frac{q_j(q_j+1)}{2}=\frac12\sum_{j\ge 1}5^j\bigl(q
_j^2+q_j\bigr). \tag{1}$$

**Asymptotics of a single term.** Since $q_j = n/5^j - \{n/5^j\}$ with fractiona
l part in $[0,1)$,
$$q_j = \frac{n}{5^j}+O(1),\qquad q_j^2 = \frac{n^2}{5^{2j}}+O\!\left(\frac{n}{5
^j}\right).$$
Therefore
$$5^j q_j^2 = \frac{n^2}{5^j}+O(n),\qquad 5^j q_j = n+O(5^j).$$
Only $j$ with $5^j\le n$ give $q_j\ge 1$; let $J:=\lfloor\log_5 n\rfloor$, so $J
=O(\log n)$ and $5^J\le n<5^{J+1}$, in particular $5^j\le n$ for every contribut
ing $j$.

**Summing.** From (1),
$$E(n)=\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{J}\frac{n^2}{5^j}+\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{J}O(n)+\frac12\su
m_{j=1}^{J}\bigl(n+O(5^j)\bigr).$$
The main sum is a geometric series:
$$\sum_{j=1}^{J}\frac{1}{5^j}=\frac{1/5\,(1-5^{-J})}{1-1/5}=\frac14\bigl(1-5^{-J
}\bigr)=\frac14+O(5^{-J})=\frac14+O(1/n).$$
Hence
$$\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{J}\frac{n^2}{5^j}=\frac{n^2}{8}+O(n).$$
The error terms are
$$\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{J}O(n)=O(n\log n),\qquad \frac12\sum_{j=1}^{J}n=O(n\log n),
\qquad \frac12\sum_{j=1}^{J}O(5^j)=O(5^J)=O(n).$$
Combining,
$$E(n)=\frac{n^2}{8}+O(n\log n).$$

**Conclusion.** Dividing by $n^2$ and noting $O(n\log n)/n^2\to 0$,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{E(n)}{n^2}=\frac18.$$
