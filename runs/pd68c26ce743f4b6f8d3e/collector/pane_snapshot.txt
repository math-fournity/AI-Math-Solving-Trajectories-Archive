## Solution

**Interpretation.** The map $r:S^1\to S^1$, $r(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)=(\cos(\the
ta+2\pi/n),\sin(\theta+2\pi/n))$, is rotation by $2\pi/n$. It generates a cyclic
 group $G=\langle r\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ acting on $S^1$. The prob
lem asks for $\pi_1$ of the orbit space $S^1/G$, i.e. the circle with each orbit
 of $n$ points identified to a single point.

**The action is free and properly discontinuous.** For any $z\in S^1$, the orbit
 is $\{z, rz, r^2z,\dots, r^{n-1}z\}$, which has exactly $n$ distinct points (si
nce $r^k=\mathrm{id}$ iff $n\mid k$, and $r^k z = z$ with $0<k<n$ would require
$e^{2\pi i k/n}=1$, impossible). The action of a finite group is automatically p
roperly discontinuous. Hence the quotient map $q:S^1\to S^1/G$ is a normal cover
ing map with deck group $G\cong\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$.

**The quotient is homeomorphic to $S^1$.** Identify $S^1\subset\mathbb{C}$ as $\
{z:|z|=1\}$. The map
$$\varphi:S^1\to S^1,\qquad \varphi(z)=z^n$$
is continuous, surjective, and constant exactly on each $G$-orbit (since $z^n =
w^n$ iff $w = z\cdot e^{2\pi i k/n} = r^k z$ for some $k$). Thus $\varphi$ desce
nds to a continuous bijection $\bar\varphi:S^1/G\to S^1$. Since $S^1/G$ is compa
ct (image of compact $S^1$) and $S^1$ is Hausdorff, $\bar\varphi$ is a homeomorp
hism.

**Fundamental group.** Therefore
$$\pi_1(S^1/G)\cong\pi_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb{Z}.$$

*Cross-check via covering theory.* The covering $q:S^1\to S^1/G\cong S^1$ is the
 $n$-fold covering $z\mapsto z^n$, whose induced map on $\pi_1$ is multiplicatio
n by $n$ on $\mathbb{Z}$. The deck group is $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, consistent
 with $\mathrm{Deck}(q)\cong \pi_1(S^1/G)/q_*(\pi_1(S^1)) = \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{
Z}$. Everything is consistent, and $\pi_1(S^1/G)=\mathbb{Z}$.

### Answer

$$\boxed{\pi_1\!\left(S^1/\langle r\rangle\right)\cong\mathbb{Z}}$$
