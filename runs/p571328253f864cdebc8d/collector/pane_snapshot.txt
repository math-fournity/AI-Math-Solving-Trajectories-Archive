## Proof

We seek all composite $n$ such that every proper divisor $d$ with $1 < d < n$ sa
tisfies
$$n - 20 \le d \le n - 12.$$

**Key observation.** For composite $n$, let $p$ be its smallest prime factor. Th
en:
- Every proper divisor $d$ (with $1<d<n$) satisfies $p \le d \le n/p$, since the
 smallest divisor $>1$ is $p$, and the largest proper divisor is $n/p$.
- Hence the condition holds for **all** proper divisors iff it holds at the extr
emes:
  $$p \ge n - 20 \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{n}{p} \le n - 12.$$

**Bounding $n$.** Since $n$ is composite, $p \le \sqrt{n}$. Combining with $p \g
e n-20$:
$$n - 20 \le \sqrt{n} \implies n - \sqrt{n} \le 20.$$
The function $f(n) = n - \sqrt{n}$ is increasing for $n \ge 1$. Since $f(25) = 2
0$ and $f(26) \approx 20.9 > 20$, we get $n \le 25$. Also $n \ge 4$ (smallest co
mposite). So we need only check composite $n \in \{4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, 15, 1
6, 18, 20, 21, 22, 24, 25\}$.

**Checking each candidate** (range is $[n-20,\, n-12]$):

| $n$ | proper divisors $>1$ | range $[n{-}20, n{-}12]$ | valid? |
|-----|----------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| 4  | $\{2\}$ | $[-16,-8]$ | ✗ ($2>-8$) |
| 6  | $\{2,3\}$ | $[-14,-6]$ | ✗ |
| 8  | $\{2,4\}$ | $[-12,-4]$ | ✗ |
| 9  | $\{3\}$ | $[-11,-3]$ | ✗ |
| 10 | $\{2,5\}$ | $[-10,-2]$ | ✗ |
| 12 | $\{2,3,4,6\}$ | $[-8,0]$ | ✗ |
| 14 | $\{2,7\}$ | $[-6,2]$ | ✗ ($7>2$) |
| 15 | $\{3,5\}$ | $[-5,3]$ | ✗ ($5>3$) |
| 16 | $\{2,4,8\}$ | $[-4,4]$ | ✗ ($8>4$) |
| 18 | $\{2,3,6,9\}$ | $[-2,6]$ | ✗ ($9>6$) |
| 20 | $\{2,4,5,10\}$ | $[0,8]$ | ✗ ($10>8$) |
| 21 | $\{3,7\}$ | $[1,9]$ | ✓ ($3,7 \in [1,9]$) |
| 22 | $\{2,11\}$ | $[2,10]$ | ✗ ($11>10$) |
| 24 | $\{2,3,4,6,8,12\}$ | $[4,12]$ | ✗ ($2<4$) |
| 25 | $\{5\}$ | $[5,13]$ | ✓ ($5 \in [5,13]$) |

**Verification of the two solutions:**

- **$n = 21 = 3 \times 7$:** proper divisors $>1$ are $3$ and $7$. Range $[21-20
, 21-12] = [1, 9]$. Both $3$ and $7$ lie in $[1,9]$. ✓
- **$n = 25 = 5^2$:** the only proper divisor $>1$ is $5$. Range $[25-20, 25-12]
 = [5, 13]$. Indeed $5 \in [5,13]$. ✓

**Conclusion.** The composite numbers satisfying the stability requirements are
exactly
$$\boxed{n \in \{21,\, 25\}}.$$
