Let $W=\{W_t:t\ge 0\}$ be a standard Brownian motion, and define
$$X_t=\int_0^t W_s\,ds,\qquad t\ge 0.$$

We compute the (total) variation of $X$ on $[0,t]$,
$$\operatorname{Var}[X]_t=\sup_{\pi}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl|X_{t_i}-X_{t_{i-1}}\bigr
|,$$
where the supremum runs over all partitions $\pi=\{0=t_0<t_1<\dots<t_n=t\}$ of $
[0,t]$.

### Step 1. $X$ is absolutely continuous with derivative $W$

Since $s\mapsto W_s(\omega)$ is continuous for every $\omega$ in a probability-o
ne event (Brownian sample paths are continuous), the map
$$t\mapsto X_t(\omega)=\int_0^t W_s(\omega)\,ds$$
is continuously differentiable on $[0,t]$ with
$$\frac{dX_t}{dt}(\omega)=W_t(\omega).$$
Hence, on this event, $X(\omega)$ is an **absolutely continuous** function on $[
0,t]$.

### Step 2. Variation of an absolutely continuous function

A classical result in real analysis states: if $f$ is absolutely continuous on $
[a,b]$, then its total variation is
$$\operatorname{Var}[f]_{[a,b]}=\int_a^b |f'(u)|\,du.$$
Applying this to $f=X(\omega)$ on $[0,t]$ gives
$$\boxed{\;\operatorname{Var}[X]_t=\int_0^t |W_s|\,ds.\;}$$

*Direct verification (optional).* For any partition $\pi$,
$$\sum_i|X_{t_i}-X_{t_{i-1}}|=\sum_i\Bigl|\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_i}W_s\,ds\Bigr|\le\s
um_i\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_i}|W_s|\,ds=\int_0^t|W_s|\,ds,$$
so $\operatorname{Var}[X]_t\le\int_0^t|W_s|\,ds$. Conversely, since $|W_s|$ is c
ontinuous, given $\varepsilon>0$ choose a partition fine enough that $W_s$ has c
onstant sign and varies by less than $\varepsilon$ on each subinterval; then
$$\sum_i\Bigl|\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_i}W_s\,ds\Bigr|\ge\int_0^t|W_s|\,ds-\varepsilon\
,t.$$
Letting the mesh go to $0$ and $\varepsilon\to0$ yields equality.

### Step 3. Finiteness of $\operatorname{Var}[X]_t$

On the probability-one event where $W$ is continuous, the sample path $s\mapsto
W_s(\omega)$ is continuous on the **compact** interval $[0,t]$. A continuous fun
ction on a compact set is bounded, so
$$M_t(\omega):=\sup_{0\le s\le t}|W_s(\omega)|<\infty.$$
Therefore
$$\operatorname{Var}[X]_t(\omega)=\int_0^t|W_s(\omega)|\,ds\le\int_0^t M_t(\omeg
a)\,ds=t\,M_t(\omega)<\infty.$$

Hence $\operatorname{Var}[X]_t<\infty$ almost surely (in fact, with probability
one).

### Summary

- **Variation:** $\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}[X]_t=\int_0^t|W_s|\,ds$.
- **Why finite:** $X_t=\int_0^t W_s\,ds$ is absolutely continuous (its derivativ
e is the continuous process $W_t$), so its variation equals $\int_0^t|W_s|\,ds$;
 since $W$ has continuous sample paths, $|W_s|$ is bounded on the compact interv
al $[0,t]$, making the integral — and thus the variation — finite almost surely.

Intuitively, $X_t$ is a *smoothed* (integrated) version of Brownian motion: inte
gration turns the nowhere-differentiable, infinite-variation process $W$ into an
 absolutely continuous process of finite variation. The roughness of $W$ is "ave
raged out" by the integral, leaving a process whose total fluctuation on any bou
nded interval is finite.
