## Proof

**Setup.** The Dirac delta function $\delta(x)$ is the probability distribution
(in the sense of distributions/generalized functions) concentrated at the origin
, characterized by the two properties:

1. $\delta(x) = 0$ for all $x \neq 0$,
2. $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \delta(x)\,dx = 1$.

More precisely, for any test function $f$ that is continuous at $0$,

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,\delta(x)\,dx = f(0).$$

**Definition of characteristic function.** For a probability measure $\mu$ on $\
mathbb{R}$, its characteristic function is

$$\varphi(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{itx}\,d\mu(x), \qquad t \in \mathbb{R}
.$$

When $\mu$ has density $p(x)$, this becomes $\varphi(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty
} e^{itx} p(x)\,dx$, i.e. the Fourier transform of the density (up to sign conve
ntion).

**Computation.** Taking $\mu = \delta_0$, the Dirac measure at the origin, and u
sing the sifting property with the test function $f(x) = e^{itx}$ (which is cont
inuous everywhere, in particular at $0$):

$$\varphi(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{itx}\,\delta(x)\,dx = e^{it\cdot 0} =
e^{0} = 1.$$

**Verification of characteristic-function axioms.** The constant function $\varp
hi(t) \equiv 1$ satisfies the defining properties of a characteristic function:

- $\varphi(0) = 1$ — normalization holds.
- $|\varphi(t)| = 1$ for all $t$ — boundedness holds.
- Positive definiteness: for any $t_1,\dots,t_n$ and $z_1,\dots,z_n \in \mathbb{
C}$,
  $$\sum_{j,k} z_j \overline{z_k}\,\varphi(t_j - t_k) = \sum_{j,k} z_j \overline
{z_k} = \left|\sum_j z_j\right|^2 \ge 0,$$
  so $\varphi$ is positive definite.
- Continuity is trivial since $\varphi$ is constant.

By Bochner's theorem, $\varphi(t)\equiv 1$ is therefore the characteristic funct
ion of a probability measure, and that measure is uniquely determined — it is ex
actly $\delta_0$. This confirms consistency.

**Interpretation.** A constant characteristic function of modulus $1$ correspond
s to a degenerate (deterministic) random variable. Here $X \equiv 0$ a.s., whose
 characteristic function is $\mathbb{E}[e^{itX}] = e^{it\cdot 0} = 1$. The Dirac
 delta at the origin is precisely the law of such a random variable, so the resu
lt agrees with the probabilistic interpretation.

**Conclusion.** The characteristic function of the Dirac delta function $\delta(
x)$ (centered at the origin) is the constant function

$$\boxed{\varphi(t) = 1 \quad \text{for all } t \in \mathbb{R}.}$$

More generally, for a Dirac delta $\delta(x - a)$ centered at $a$, the character
istic function is $\varphi(t) = e^{ita}$.
