## Solution

**Setup.** Place coordinates with $A = (0,0)$ and $C = (26,0)$. Let $B = (b_x, b
_y)$ with $b_y > 0$ (triangle above the $x$-axis). From $AB = 16$, $BC = 20$:

$$b_x^2 + b_y^2 = 256, \qquad (b_x - 26)^2 + b_y^2 = 400.$$

Subtracting: $52\,b_x - 676 = -144$, so $b_x = \tfrac{133}{13}$, and $b_y = \tfr
ac{5\sqrt{1023}}{13}$ (consistent with the area of $\triangle ABC$, which by Her
on is $\sqrt{31\cdot 15\cdot 11\cdot 5} = 5\sqrt{1023}$, and $b_y = \tfrac{2[\tr
iangle ABC]}{AC}$).

**Locating the squares (all outside $\triangle ABC$).**

- **Square $ACDE$** on $AC$, below the $x$-axis: rotate $\overrightarrow{AC}=(26
,0)$ by $-90°$ to get $(0,-26)$.
  $$D = (26,-26),\quad E = (0,-26).$$

- **Square $ABFG$** on $AB$, away from $C$: rotate $\overrightarrow{AB}=(b_x,b_y
)$ by $+90°$ to get $(-b_y,\,b_x)$ (outward, since $C$ lies on the negative side
 of this normal).
  $$G = A + (-b_y, b_x) = (-b_y,\, b_x),\quad F = B + (-b_y, b_x).$$

- **Square $BCHI$** on $BC$, away from $A$: rotate $\overrightarrow{BC}=(26-b_x,
\,-b_y)$ by $+90°$ to get $(b_y,\,26-b_x)$ (outward, since $A$ lies on the negat
ive side).
  $$H = C + (b_y,\,26-b_x) = (26+b_y,\;26-b_x),\quad I = B + (b_y,\,26-b_x).$$

**Midpoints.**

$$J = \text{mid}(E,H) = \left(\tfrac{26+b_y}{2},\; -\tfrac{b_x}{2}\right),$$
$$K = \text{mid}(D,G) = \left(\tfrac{26-b_y}{2},\; \tfrac{b_x-26}{2}\right),$$
$$L = \text{mid}(A,C) = (13,\,0).$$

**Area of $\triangle JKL$.** Vectors from $L$:

$$\vec{LJ} = J - L = \left(\tfrac{b_y}{2},\; -\tfrac{b_x}{2}\right),\qquad \vec{
LK} = K - L = \left(-\tfrac{b_y}{2},\; \tfrac{b_x-26}{2}\right).$$

$$\det(\vec{LJ},\vec{LK}) = \frac{b_y}{2}\cdot\frac{b_x-26}{2} - \left(-\frac{b_
x}{2}\right)\!\left(-\frac{b_y}{2}\right) = \frac{b_y(b_x-26) - b_x\,b_y}{4} = \
frac{-26\,b_y}{4}.$$

$$[\triangle JKL] = \tfrac{1}{2}\left|\det\right| = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{26\,b
_y}{4} = \frac{13\,b_y}{4}.$$

Substituting $b_y = \tfrac{5\sqrt{1023}}{13}$:

$$[\triangle JKL] = \frac{13}{4}\cdot\frac{5\sqrt{1023}}{13} = \frac{5\sqrt{1023
}}{4}.$$

**Remark.** The determinant simplified to $-26\,b_y/4$, independent of $b_x$. Si
nce $[\triangle ABC] = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot AC\cdot b_y = 13\,b_y$, this gives the
clean structural identity $[\triangle JKL] = \tfrac{1}{4}[\triangle ABC]$, confi
rming the result.
