Looking at this problem, I need to find harmonic measures for the diameter and a

## Setup

The domain is $D = \{z : |z| < R,\; \operatorname{Im} z > 0\}$, with boundary sp
lit into:
- $\Delta$: the diameter $\{z = x \in (-R,R),\; y=0\}$,
- $\Gamma$: the semicircular arc $\{|z|=R,\; \operatorname{Im} z > 0\}$.

Since $\partial D = \Delta \cup \Gamma$, we have $\omega(z,\Delta,D) + \omega(z,
\Gamma,D) = 1$, so it suffices to find one.

## Conformal mapping

Apply the Möbius transformation
$$w = \frac{R+z}{R-z}.$$
This maps $|z|<R$ to $\operatorname{Re} w > 0$. Let me check where the two bound
ary pieces go:

**On $\Delta$** ($z = x \in (-R,R)$, real): $\frac{R+x}{R-x} > 0$, so $w$ is on
the **positive real axis** ($\arg w = 0$).

**On $\Gamma$** ($z = Re^{i\theta}$, $0<\theta<\pi$):
$$\frac{R+Re^{i\theta}}{R-Re^{i\theta}} = \frac{1+e^{i\theta}}{1-e^{i\theta}} =
\frac{2\cos(\theta/2)}{-2i\sin(\theta/2)} = i\cot(\theta/2),$$
which lies on the **positive imaginary axis** ($\arg w = \pi/2$).

So $w$ maps $D$ conformally onto the **first quadrant** $Q = \{\operatorname{Re}
 w > 0,\; \operatorname{Im} w > 0\}$, with $\Delta \mapsto$ positive real axis a
nd $\Gamma \mapsto$ positive imaginary axis.

## Harmonic measure in the quadrant

In the first quadrant $Q$ (a sector of opening angle $\pi/2$), the harmonic meas
ure of the positive imaginary axis at $w = \rho\, e^{i\varphi}$ (with $0 < \varp
hi < \pi/2$) is the normalized angle:
$$\omega(w,\;\text{positive imag. axis},\; Q) = \frac{2\varphi}{\pi} = \frac{2}{
\pi}\arg w.$$

(This follows since $w \mapsto w^2$ maps $Q$ to the upper half-plane, the positi
ve imaginary axis maps to the negative real axis, and the harmonic measure there
 is $(\pi - 2\varphi)/\pi$, giving $1 - (\pi-2\varphi)/\pi = 2\varphi/\pi$ for t
he positive imaginary axis.)

## Result

Pulling back via $w = \frac{R+z}{R-z}$:

$$\boxed{\omega(z,\,\Gamma,\,D) = \frac{2}{\pi}\arg\!\left(\frac{R+z}{R-z}\right
)}$$

$$\boxed{\omega(z,\,\Delta,\,D) = 1 - \frac{2}{\pi}\arg\!\left(\frac{R+z}{R-z}\r
ight)}$$

**Explicit form.** Writing $z = x+iy$ with $x^2+y^2 < R^2$, $y > 0$:
$$\frac{R+z}{R-z} = \frac{(R+z)(R-\bar z)}{|R-z|^2} = \frac{R^2 - |z|^2 + 2iRy}{
|R-z|^2},$$
so (since $R^2 - |z|^2 > 0$ in $D$):
$$\arg\frac{R+z}{R-z} = \arctan\!\left(\frac{2Ry}{R^2 - |z|^2}\right).$$

Therefore:
$$\omega(z,\Gamma,D) = \frac{2}{\pi}\arctan\!\left(\frac{2R\,\operatorname{Im}z}
{R^2 - |z|^2}\right), \qquad \omega(z,\Delta,D) = \frac{2}{\pi}\arctan\!\left(\f
rac{R^2 - |z|^2}{2R\,\operatorname{Im}z}\right).$$

**Boundary verification:**
- On $\Delta$: $y=0 \Rightarrow \arg = 0 \Rightarrow \omega_\Gamma = 0$, $\omega
_\Delta = 1$. ✓
- On $\Gamma$: $\arg = \pi/2 \Rightarrow \omega_\Gamma = 1$, $\omega_\Delta = 0$
. ✓

## Level lines

Setting $\omega(z,\Gamma,D) = c$ for $0 < c < 1$ gives
$$\frac{2Ry}{R^2 - x^2 - y^2} = \tan\!\left(\frac{\pi c}{2}\right) =: k > 0.$$

Rearranging: $kx^2 + ky^2 + 2Ry = kR^2$, i.e.,
$$x^2 + \left(y + \frac{R}{k}\right)^2 = \frac{R^2(1+k^2)}{k^2}.$$

Substituting $k = \tan(\pi c/2)$ and using $\sqrt{1+\tan^2\alpha} = 1/\cos\alpha
$:

$$\boxed{x^2 + \Big(y + R\cot\frac{\pi c}{2}\Big)^2 = \frac{R^2}{\sin^2(\pi c/2)
}}$$

These are **circles passing through $z = \pm R$**, with centers on the imaginary
 axis at $z_0 = -iR\cot(\pi c/2)$ and radius $\rho = R/\sin(\pi c/2)$.

**Verification that they pass through $\pm R$:** $|R - z_0| = |R + iR\cot(\pi c/
2)| = R\sqrt{1+\cot^2(\pi c/2)} = R/\sin(\pi c/2) = \rho$. ✓ (Same for $-R$ by s
ymmetry.)

**Limiting cases:**
- $c \to 0$: center $\to -i\infty$, radius $\to \infty$; the circle degenerates
to the real axis ($\Delta$). ✓
- $c \to 1$: center $\to 0$, radius $\to R$; the circle degenerates to $|z|=R$ (
$\Gamma$). ✓

Since $\omega_\Delta = 1 - \omega_\Gamma$, the level lines of $\omega(z,\Delta,D
)$ are the **same family** of circles: $\omega_\Delta = c$ corresponds to $\omeg
a_\Gamma = 1-c$, i.e., the circle with parameter $k' = \cot(\pi c/2) = 1/k$.

**Summary.** Both harmonic measures share a single coaxial family of level lines
: the circles through the two endpoints $\pm R$ with centers on the imaginary ax
is (the "pencil of circles" through $\pm R$ orthogonal to the family with center
s on the real axis).
