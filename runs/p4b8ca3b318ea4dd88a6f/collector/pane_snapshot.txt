The statement is **false**. Here is a counterexample.

**Setup.** Let $k$ be a field and set $R = k[t^2, t^3] \subset k[t]$, the subalg
ebra of $k[t]$ generated by $t^2$ and $t^3$. Then $R$ is an integral domain with
 fraction field $K = k(t)$.

**The polynomial.** Consider $f(x) = t^2 x - t^3 \in R[x]$. Note $t^2, t^3 \in R
$ so $f \in R[x]$.

**$f$ is irreducible in $R[x]$.** The units of $R$ are $k^\times$ (the nonzero c
onstants). Since $\deg f = 1$, any factorization $f = g \cdot h$ in $R[x]$ has o
ne factor of degree $0$ (a constant $c \in R$) and one of degree $1$. Then $c \m
id t^2$ and $c \mid t^3$ in $R$, with $t^2/c,\, t^3/c \in R$. The divisors of $t
^2$ in $R$ of positive degree are scalar multiples of $t^2$ itself (since $t^2$
has degree $2$ and the only degree-$2$ element dividing it is $\alpha t^2$). But
 then $t^3/(\alpha t^2) = t/\alpha \notin R$ (as $t \notin R$). Hence no non-uni
t $c$ works, and $f$ is irreducible in $R[x]$.

**The root.** The element $r = t$ lies in $K = k(t) \subseteq \overline{K}$, and
 $f(r) = t^2 \cdot t - t^3 = 0$, so $r$ is a root of $f$ in an algebraic closure
 of the fraction field.

**The subring $R[r]$.** Since $r = t$, we have $R[r] = k[t^2, t^3, t] = k[t]$.

**The natural map and its kernel.** The evaluation map $\phi: R[x] \to R[r] = k[
t]$, $\phi(g) = g(t)$, is surjective with $\ker\phi \supseteq (f)$. We show the
containment is strict.

Consider $g(x) = t^3 x - t^4 \in R[x]$ (note $t^4 = (t^2)^2 \in R$). Then $g(t)
= t^3 \cdot t - t^4 = 0$, so $g \in \ker\phi$.

We claim $g \notin (f)$. If $g = f \cdot h$ for some $h \in R[x]$, then since $\
deg g = \deg f = 1$, $h$ must be a constant $c \in R$. This gives $c \cdot t^2 =
 t^3$ and $c \cdot (-t^3) = -t^4$, hence $c = t^3/t^2 = t \notin R$. Contradicti
on. Therefore $g \notin (f)$.

**Conclusion.** We have $(f) \subsetneq \ker\phi$, so the natural surjection $R[
x]/(f) \twoheadrightarrow R[r]$ is not an isomorphism. Hence $R[x]/(f) \not\cong
 R[r]$.

The failure occurs because $R = k[t^2, t^3]$ is not integrally closed (it is not
 a UFD), so Gauss's-lemma-type arguments break down: the irreducible polynomial
$f$ has a "hidden" content factor ($t^2$) that is a non-unit, and the kernel of
evaluation is strictly larger than the principal ideal $(f)$.
