## Solution

We solve the Dirichlet problem for the Helmholtz equation in the unit disk:

$$\Delta u + 4u = 0,\quad 0\le r<1,\qquad u|_{r=1}=\sin^3\varphi.$$

Here $k^2=4$, so $k=2$.

### Separation of variables in polar coordinates

Writing $u(r,\varphi)=R(r)\Phi(\varphi)$, the angular part gives $\Phi''+n^2\Phi
=0$ (with integer $n$ for $2\pi$-periodicity), and the radial part satisfies the
 Bessel equation

$$r^2 R'' + r R' + (k^2 r^2 - n^2)R = 0.$$

The regular (bounded at $r=0$) solutions are $J_n(kr)$. The singular solutions $
Y_n(kr)$ are excluded by the requirement that $u$ be finite at the origin. Hence
 the general regular solution is

$$u(r,\varphi)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigl[A_n\cos(n\varphi)+B_n\sin(n\varphi)\bigr
]J_n(2r).$$

### Applying the boundary condition

At $r=1$:

$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigl[A_n\cos(n\varphi)+B_n\sin(n\varphi)\bigr]J_n(2)=\sin^
3\varphi.$$

Using the trigonometric identity

$$\sin^3\varphi=\frac{3\sin\varphi-\sin(3\varphi)}{4},$$

we match Fourier coefficients:

- $n=1$: $\;B_1\,J_1(2)=\dfrac{3}{4}\;\Longrightarrow\; B_1=\dfrac{3}{4\,J_1(2)}
$,
- $n=3$: $\;B_3\,J_3(2)=-\dfrac{1}{4}\;\Longrightarrow\; B_3=-\dfrac{1}{4\,J_3(2
)}$,
- all other $A_n, B_n$ are zero.

### Verification

**PDE.** Each term $J_n(2r)\sin(n\varphi)$ satisfies $\Delta u+4u=0$ in $0\le r<
1$ by construction (Bessel's equation with $k=2$). Linearity preserves this.

**Boundary.** At $r=1$:
$$u(1,\varphi)=\frac{3}{4}\sin\varphi-\frac{1}{4}\sin(3\varphi)=\sin^3\varphi.\;
\checkmark$$

**Regularity.** $J_n(2r)$ is smooth at $r=0$, so $u$ is bounded and smooth in th
e disk. $\checkmark$

### Solution

$$\boxed{\,u(r,\varphi)=\frac{3\,J_1(2r)}{4\,J_1(2)}\,\sin\varphi-\frac{J_3(2r)}
{4\,J_3(2)}\,\sin(3\varphi)\,}$$

where $J_1$ and $J_3$ are Bessel functions of the first kind. (Note $J_1(2)\appr
ox 0.5767$ and $J_3(2)\approx 0.1289$ are both nonzero, so the denominators are
well-defined.)
