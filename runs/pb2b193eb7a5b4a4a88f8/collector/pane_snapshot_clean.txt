## Solution

We determine whether $\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{p(n)}{p(n-5)}$ exists, and whether
 it equals $1$.

### Asymptotic formula for $p(n)$

The Hardy–Ramanujan asymptotic formula for the partition function states

$$
p(n) \sim \frac{1}{4n\sqrt{3}}\exp\!\Big(\pi\sqrt{\tfrac{2n}{3}}\Big), \qquad n\
to\infty.
$$

More precisely, there is a constant $C>0$ such that

$$
p(n) = \frac{1}{4n\sqrt{3}}\exp\!\Big(\pi\sqrt{\tfrac{2n}{3}}\Big)\big(1 + O(n^{
-1/2})\big).
$$

In particular $p(n) = \Theta\!\big(n^{-1}\exp(\pi\sqrt{2n/3})\big)$, and the rat
io of the leading asymptotic to $p(n)$ tends to $1$.

### Forming the ratio

Using the asymptotic for both $p(n)$ and $p(n-5)$,

$$
\frac{p(n)}{p(n-5)} \sim \frac{\dfrac{1}{4n\sqrt{3}}\exp\!\big(\pi\sqrt{2n/3}\bi
g)}{\dfrac{1}{4(n-5)\sqrt{3}}\exp\!\big(\pi\sqrt{2(n-5)/3}\big)} = \frac{n-5}{n}
\,\exp\!\Big(\pi\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}}\big(\sqrt{n}-\sqrt{n-5}\big)\Big).
$$

### Evaluating the two factors

**Prefactor.** $\dfrac{n-5}{n} = 1 - \dfrac{5}{n} \to 1$.

**Exponential factor.** Rationalize the difference of square roots:

$$
\sqrt{n}-\sqrt{n-5} = \frac{n-(n-5)}{\sqrt{n}+\sqrt{n-5}} = \frac{5}{\sqrt{n}+\s
qrt{n-5}}.
$$

Since $\sqrt{n}+\sqrt{n-5} \sim 2\sqrt{n}\to\infty$, we get

$$
\sqrt{n}-\sqrt{n-5} \to 0,
$$

and therefore

$$
\pi\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}}\big(\sqrt{n}-\sqrt{n-5}\big) \to 0,
\qquad
\exp\!\Big(\pi\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}}\big(\sqrt{n}-\sqrt{n-5}\big)\Big) \to e^{0}=1.
$$

### Rigorous justification of the limit

To make the asymptotic argument rigorous, write

$$
p(n) = A(n)\big(1+\varepsilon(n)\big), \qquad A(n)=\frac{1}{4n\sqrt{3}}\exp\!\Bi
g(\pi\sqrt{\tfrac{2n}{3}}\Big), \qquad \varepsilon(n)\to 0.
$$

Then

$$
\frac{p(n)}{p(n-5)} = \frac{A(n)}{A(n-5)}\cdot\frac{1+\varepsilon(n)}{1+\varepsi
lon(n-5)}.
$$

We showed $\frac{A(n)}{A(n-5)}\to 1$. Since $\varepsilon(n)\to 0$ and $\varepsil
on(n-5)\to 0$, the second factor $\frac{1+\varepsilon(n)}{1+\varepsilon(n-5)}\to
\frac{1}{1}=1$. Hence

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{p(n)}{p(n-5)} = 1\cdot 1 = 1.
$$

### Conclusion

The limit exists and equals $1$.
