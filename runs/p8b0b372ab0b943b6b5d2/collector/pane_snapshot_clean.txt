## Proof

**Claim:** For $\lambda \in \rho(A)$, the norm $\|R_\lambda\| = \|(\lambda - A)^
{-1}\|$ cannot be zero, provided the underlying Banach space $X$ is nontrivial (
i.e., $X \neq \{0\}$).

**Proof.**

Let $X$ be a Banach space with $X \neq \{0\}$, and let $A$ be a closed (typicall
y bounded) linear operator on $X$. By definition of the resolvent set, $\lambda
\in \rho(A)$ means that $\lambda - A : D(A) \to X$ is bijective and its inverse
$$R_\lambda = (\lambda - A)^{-1} : X \to D(A) \subseteq X$$
is a bounded linear operator.

Recall the standard fact: for any bounded linear operator $T$ on a normed space,
$$\|T\| = 0 \iff T = 0 \text{ (the zero operator)}.$$

So suppose, for contradiction, that $\|R_\lambda\| = 0$. Then $R_\lambda = 0$, m
eaning $R_\lambda x = 0$ for every $x \in X$.

But $R_\lambda$ is the inverse of $\lambda - A$, hence it is **bijective** (in p
articular, surjective). Surjectivity of $R_\lambda : X \to D(A)$ means $R_\lambd
a(X) = D(A)$. If $R_\lambda = 0$, then $R_\lambda(X) = \{0\}$, so $D(A) = \{0\}$
.

Now apply $R_\lambda$ to any nonzero $x \in X$ (which exists since $X \neq \{0\}
$). We get $R_\lambda x = 0$. But $R_\lambda$ is also **injective** (being an in
verse), so $R_\lambda x = 0$ implies $x = 0$. This contradicts the choice of $x
\neq 0$.

Therefore $R_\lambda \neq 0$, and consequently
$$\|R_\lambda\| > 0.$$

**Remark on the trivial case.** If $X = \{0\}$ is the zero Banach space, then th
e only linear operator is the zero operator, which coincides with the identity o
n $\{0\}$. In this degenerate case every $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$ belongs to $\r
ho(A)$, $R_\lambda = 0$, and $\|R_\lambda\| = 0$. This is the unique exception,
and it is excluded by the standard convention that Banach spaces in spectral the
ory are nontrivial.

**Conclusion.** In any nontrivial Banach space, the resolvent $R_\lambda = (\lam
bda - A)^{-1}$ is a bijective (hence nonzero) bounded operator for every $\lambd
a \in \rho(A)$, so its norm is strictly positive:
$$\boxed{\|R_\lambda\| > 0 \quad \text{for all } \lambda \in \rho(A).}$$
