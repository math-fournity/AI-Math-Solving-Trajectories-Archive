## Answer

**No.** An analytic function in the upper half‑plane that is continuous on the r
eal axis and belongs to $L^{2}(\mathbb{R})$ need **not** be bounded on the real
axis. Below is an explicit counterexample.

---

### Construction

For $n\ge 1$ set
$$
\varepsilon_n=n^{-5},\qquad b_n=\sqrt n,\qquad a_n=b_n\varepsilon_n=n^{-9/2},
$$
and place poles in the **lower** half‑plane at
$$
p_n=n-i\varepsilon_n=n-i\,n^{-5}.
$$
Define
$$
f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_n}{z-p_n}
      =\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{-9/2}}{z-n+i\,n^{-5}}.
$$

We verify that $f$ has all the required properties yet is unbounded on $\mathbb
R$.

---

### 1. $f$ is analytic in the upper half‑plane

Every pole $p_n$ satisfies $\operatorname{Im}p_n=-n^{-5}<0$, so no singularity l
ies in $\{z:\operatorname{Im}z>0\}$. On any compact $K\subset\{\operatorname{Im}
z>0\}$ we have $\operatorname{Im}z\ge\delta>0$, hence
$$
\left|\frac{a_n}{z-p_n}\right|\le \frac{n^{-9/2}}{\delta},
$$
and $\sum n^{-9/2}<\infty$. The series therefore converges uniformly on $K$, so
$f$ is analytic in the upper half‑plane.

---

### 2. $f$ is continuous on the real axis

Let $K=[-R,R]\subset\mathbb R$. For $n>2R$ and $x\in K$,
$$
|x-n|\ge n-R\ge \tfrac{n}{2},\qquad
\left|\frac{a_n}{x-n+i\varepsilon_n}\right|\le \frac{n^{-9/2}}{n/2}=2\,n^{-11/2}
,
$$
which is summable. The finitely many remaining terms ($n\le 2R$) are continuous
on $K$ because their denominators $x-n+i\varepsilon_n$ never vanish on $\mathbb
R$ (the imaginary part $\varepsilon_n\neq0$). Uniform convergence on every compa
ct gives continuity on $\mathbb R$.

---

### 3. $f\in L^{2}(\mathbb R)$

Write $\varphi_n(x)=\dfrac{1}{x-n+i\varepsilon_n}$, so $f(x)=\sum a_n\varphi_n(x
)$. A direct residue computation gives
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\varphi_n(x)\overline{\varphi_m(x)}\,dx
=\frac{2\pi i}{(m-n)+i(\varepsilon_m+\varepsilon_n)},\qquad n\neq m,
$$
and $\int|\varphi_n|^{2}\,dx=\pi/\varepsilon_n$. Hence
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(x)|^{2}\,dx
=\sum_n |a_n|^{2}\frac{\pi}{\varepsilon_n}
+2\operatorname{Re}\sum_{n<m}a_n\overline{a_m}\,
   \frac{2\pi i}{(m-n)+i(\varepsilon_m+\varepsilon_n)}.
$$

**Diagonal terms.**
$$
|a_n|^{2}\frac{\pi}{\varepsilon_n}
=\pi\, n^{-9}\cdot n^{5}=\pi\, n^{-4},\qquad \sum n^{-4}<\infty.
$$

**Cross terms.** Since $|m-n|\ge1$ and $|\varepsilon_m+\varepsilon_n|\le 2$,
$$
\left|\frac{2\pi i}{(m-n)+i(\varepsilon_m+\varepsilon_n)}\right|\le \frac{2\pi}{
|m-n|}\le 2\pi,
$$
so
$$
\sum_{n<m}|a_n a_m|\cdot\frac{2\pi}{|m-n|}
\le 2\pi\sum_{n<m}n^{-9/2}m^{-9/2}
\le 2\pi\Bigl(\sum_{n}n^{-9/2}\Bigr)^{2}<\infty.
$$

Both parts converge absolutely, so $\int|f|^{2}<\infty$.

---

### 4. $f$ is unbounded on $\mathbb R$

Evaluate at $x=n$. The $k=n$ term equals
$$
\frac{a_n}{i\varepsilon_n}=\frac{b_n\varepsilon_n}{i\varepsilon_n}=-i\,b_n=-i\sq
rt n.
$$
The remaining terms satisfy
$$
R_n:=\sum_{k\neq n}\frac{a_k}{n-k+i\varepsilon_k},
\qquad
|R_n|\le\sum_{k\neq n}\frac{k^{-9/2}}{|n-k|}
      \le\sum_{k\neq n}k^{-9/2}=:C<\infty,
$$
where $C$ is independent of $n$. Therefore
$$
|f(n)|=\bigl|-i\sqrt n+R_n\bigr|\ge \sqrt n-C\;\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\;\inft
y.
$$

Thus no constant $M$ can bound $|f(x)|$ for all $x\in\mathbb R$.

---

### Conclusion

The hypotheses — analyticity in the upper half‑plane, continuity on $\mathbb R$,
 and $L^{2}$‑integrability on $\mathbb R$ — do **not** imply boundedness on the
real axis. The function constructed above satisfies all three hypotheses yet sat
isfies $|f(n)|\to\infty$.
