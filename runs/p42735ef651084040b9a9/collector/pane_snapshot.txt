irreducible representations are the one-dimensional characters $\sigma_n(k_\thet
a) = e^{in\theta}$, $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. The restriction $\rho := \pi|_K : K \to

$$P_n : H \to H, \qquad P_n(v) = \int_0^{2\pi} e^{-in\theta}\, \pi(k_\theta)\, v

where $k_\theta = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & \sin\theta \\ -\sin\theta & \cos\

eta}\pi(k_\theta)v$ is continuous (by continuity of $\pi$), so the integral is w

**Step 3: The image of $P_n$ consists of $K$-finite vectors.** For any $\varphi

$$\pi(k_\varphi)\, P_n(v) = \int_0^{2\pi} e^{-in\theta}\, \pi(k_\varphi k_\theta

$K$-finite.

**Step 4: $K$-finite vectors are dense (Peter–Weyl).** We show that for every $v

$$v = \lim_{N \to \infty} \sum_{|n| \leq N-1} \left(1 - \frac{|n|}{N}\right) P_n
(v),$$
with convergence in the norm of $H$.

Consider the continuous function $f_v : K \to H$ defined by $f_v(k_\theta) = \pi
(k_\theta)\, v$. The **Fejér kernel**
$$F_N(\theta) = \sum_{|n| \leq N-1}\left(1 - \frac{|n|}{N}\right) e^{in\theta} =
 \frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{\sin(N\theta/2)}{\sin(\theta/2)}\right)^2$$
is a well-known approximate identity on $S^1$: it satisfies $F_N \geq 0$, $\int_
0^{2\pi} F_N(\theta)\,\frac{d\theta}{2\pi} = 1$, and $F_N(\theta) \to 0$ uniform

The convolution $(F_N * f_v)(k_\theta) = \int_0^{2\pi} F_N(\varphi)\, f_v(k_{\th
eta - \varphi})\,\frac{d\varphi}{2\pi}$ converges **uniformly** to $f_v$ as $N \

$$(F_N * f_v)(e) = \int_0^{2\pi} F_N(\varphi)\, \pi(k_{-\varphi})\, v \;\frac{d\

$$v = \lim_{N \to \infty} \sum_{|n| \leq N-1}\left(1 - \frac{|n|}{N}\right) P_n(
v).$$

Each partial sum is a finite linear combination of $K$-finite vectors, hence $K$

ite vectors.

**Step 5: Conclusion.** Since $\pi$ is an irreducible representation of $G$, we
have $H \neq \{0\}$. Pick any nonzero $v \in H$. By Step 4, $v$ is a norm-limit
of $K$-finite vectors. Since $v \neq 0$, at least one of these approximating $K$
