## Theorem

Let $X$ be a Polish space, $V$ a separable normed space, and $\mu$ a $\sigma$-fi
nite Borel measure on $X$. Then:

- **$L_p(X,\mu,V)$ is separable for every $1 \le p < \infty$.**
- **$L_\infty(X,\mu,V)$ is not separable in general** (even under these hypothes
es).

---

## Proof for $1 \le p < \infty$

### Step 1 — Reduction to finite measure

Since $\mu$ is $\sigma$-finite, write $X = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty B_n$ with $\mu(B
_n)<\infty$, and set $A_n = B_n \setminus \bigcup_{k<n} B_k$. Then $\{A_n\}$ are
 disjoint Borel sets, $\mu(A_n)<\infty$, and $X = \bigsqcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n$.

The map
$$\Phi : L_p(X,\mu,V) \longrightarrow \Bigl(\bigoplus_{n=1}^\infty L_p(A_n,\mu|_
{A_n},V)\Bigr)_{\ell_p}, \qquad \Phi(f) = (f|_{A_n})_{n\ge1}$$
is an isometric isomorphism: $\|f\|_p^p = \sum_n \|f|_{A_n}\|_p^p$.

A countable $\ell_p$-sum of separable Banach spaces is separable: if $D_n \subse
t E_n$ is countable dense, then $\{(d_1,\dots,d_N,0,0,\dots): N\ge1,\; d_i\in D_
i\}$ is countable and dense in $(\bigoplus E_n)_{\ell_p}$.

Hence it suffices to prove separability when **$\mu(X)<\infty$**.

### Step 2 — Finite-measure case: construction of a countable dense set

Since $X$ is Polish, it has a countable topological base $\{U_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$.
 Let $\mathcal{A}$ be the **algebra** generated by $\{U_n\}$ (finite unions of f
inite intersections of the $U_n$ and their complements). Then $\mathcal{A}$ is c
ountable and $\sigma(\mathcal{A}) = \mathcal{B}(X)$.

Let $D \subset V$ be countable dense. Define the countable family of simple func
tions
$$\mathcal{S} = \Bigl\{\sum_{i=1}^n v_i\,\mathbf{1}_{A_i} : n\in\mathbb{N},\; v_
i \in D,\; A_i \in \mathcal{A}\Bigr\}.$$

We show $\mathcal{S}$ is dense in $L_p(X,\mu,V)$ via three claims.

**Claim 1 (Simple functions are dense).** For every $f \in L_p(X,\mu,V)$ there i
s a sequence of simple functions $s_n \to f$ in $L_p$.

*Proof.* Since $f$ is strongly measurable (by definition of Bochner $L_p$), ther
e exist simple functions $s_n \to f$ pointwise with $\|s_n(x)\| \le 2\|f(x)\|$ (
standard construction via range truncation and rounding). Since $\mu(X)<\infty$,
 $2\|f\|\in L_p$, and dominated convergence gives $\|f - s_n\|_p \to 0$. $\squar
e$

**Claim 2 (Borel sets are approximable by $\mathcal{A}$).** For every $E \in \ma
thcal{B}(X)$ and $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $A \in \mathcal{A}$ with $\mu(E \t
riangle A) < \varepsilon$.

*Proof.* Let $\mathcal{G} = \{E \in \mathcal{B}(X) : \forall\,\varepsilon>0,\;\e
xists A\in\mathcal{A},\;\mu(E\triangle A)<\varepsilon\}$. We show $\mathcal{G}$
is a $\sigma$-algebra containing $\mathcal{A}$:

- $\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{G}$: take $A=E$.
- $E\in\mathcal{G} \Rightarrow E^c\in\mathcal{G}$: $\mu(E^c\triangle A^c)=\mu(E\
triangle A)$ and $A^c\in\mathcal{A}$.
- $E_n\in\mathcal{G} \Rightarrow \bigcup E_n \in\mathcal{G}$: Given $\varepsilon
>0$, pick $A_n\in\mathcal{A}$ with $\mu(E_n\triangle A_n)<\varepsilon/2^{n+1}$.
Since $\mu(X)<\infty$, $\mu\bigl(\bigcup_{n>N}E_n\bigr)\to 0$; choose $N$ so thi
s tail is $<\varepsilon/2$. Then $\bigcup_{n=1}^N A_n \in \mathcal{A}$ and
$$\mu\Bigl(\bigcup_{n\ge1}E_n \;\triangle\; \bigcup_{n=1}^N A_n\Bigr) \le \sum_{
n=1}^N \mu(E_n\triangle A_n) + \mu\Bigl(\bigcup_{n>N}E_n\Bigr) < \frac{\varepsil
on}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=\varepsilon.$$

So $\mathcal{G}$ is a $\sigma$-algebra with $\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{G}\subse
t\mathcal{B}(X)$, hence $\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{B}(X)$. $\square$

**Claim 3 ($\mathcal{S}$ is dense).** Given $f\in L_p(X,\mu,V)$ and $\varepsilon
>0$:

1. By Claim 1, pick a simple function $s = \sum_{i=1}^n w_i\,\mathbf{1}_{E_i}$ (
disjoint Borel $E_i$, $w_i\in V$) with $\|f-s\|_p < \varepsilon/3$.
2. Pick $v_i \in D$ with $\|w_i - v_i\| < \varepsilon/(3n\,\mu(X)^{1/p})$ (trivi
al if $\mu(X)=0$). Set $s' = \sum v_i\,\mathbf{1}_{E_i}$. Then
$$\|s - s'\|_p \le \sum_{i=1}^n \|w_i-v_i\|\;\mu(E_i)^{1/p} \le n\cdot\frac{\var
epsilon}{3n\,\mu(X)^{1/p}}\cdot\mu(X)^{1/p} = \frac{\varepsilon}{3}.$$
3. Let $M = \max_i\|v_i\|$. If $M=0$ take $s''=0$. Otherwise, by Claim 2 pick $A
_i\in\mathcal{A}$ with $\mu(E_i\triangle A_i) < (\varepsilon/(3nM))^p$. Set $s''
 = \sum v_i\,\mathbf{1}_{A_i} \in \mathcal{S}$. Then
$$\|s'-s''\|_p \le \sum_{i=1}^n \|v_i\|\;\mu(E_i\triangle A_i)^{1/p} \le nM\cdot
\frac{\varepsilon}{3nM} = \frac{\varepsilon}{3}.$$

By the triangle inequality, $\|f - s''\|_p < \varepsilon$. Since $\mathcal{S}$ i
s countable, $L_p(X,\mu,V)$ is separable. $\square$

---

## Proof that $L_\infty$ is not separable in general

Take $X=[0,1]$ (Polish), $\mu$ = Lebesgue measure ($\sigma$-finite), $V=\mathbb{
R}$ (separable). Consider the uncountable family
$$\mathcal{F} = \{\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]} : t \in [0,1]\} \subset L_\infty([0,1]).$$
For $s \neq t$ (say $s < t$), the set $(s,t]$ has positive Lebesgue measure, so
$\|\mathbf{1}_{[0,s]} - \mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}\|_\infty = 1$ (the essential supremum
 is 1).

Thus $\mathcal{F}$ is an uncountable $1$-separated set. In a separable metric sp
ace, every $\varepsilon$-separated set (with $\varepsilon > 0$) is countable: ea
ch point $x$ in such a set requires a distinct element of the countable dense se
t within the ball $B(x,\varepsilon/2)$, and these balls are pairwise disjoint. T
his contradiction shows $L_\infty([0,1])$ is **not separable**.

---

## Summary

| Range of $p$ | Separability of $L_p(X,\mu,V)$ |
|:---:|:---:|
| $1 \le p < \infty$ | **Yes** (always, under the stated hypotheses) |
| $p = \infty$ | **No** (in general, even for $X=[0,1]$, $V=\mathbb{R}$) |

The key ingredients for $1\le p<\infty$ are: (i) $\sigma$-finiteness reduces to
the finite-measure case via an $\ell_p$-decomposition; (ii) second-countability
of $X$ gives a countable algebra generating the Borel $\sigma$-algebra; (iii) fi
niteness of $\mu$ makes every Borel set approximable in measure by algebra sets;
 (iv) separability of $V$ provides a countable dense set of coefficients. The fa
ilure at $p=\infty$ comes from the lack of an $L_p$-norm control that would forc
e approximation in measure.
