## Solution

I need to evaluate $\displaystyle I = \int_{|z|=1} \frac{1-\cos z}{(e^z-1)\sin z
}\,dz$ using Cauchy's residue theorem.

### Step 1: Locate the singularities inside $|z|=1$

The integrand $f(z) = \dfrac{1-\cos z}{(e^z-1)\sin z}$ has singularities where t
he denominator vanishes, i.e., where $e^z - 1 = 0$ or $\sin z = 0$.

- **Zeros of $e^z - 1$:** $z = 2\pi i k$, $k \in \mathbb{Z}$. Inside $|z|<1$: on
ly $k=0$, giving $z=0$. (For $k=\pm1$, $|z|=2\pi>1$.)
- **Zeros of $\sin z$:** $z = n\pi$, $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. Inside $|z|<1$: only $n
=0$, giving $z=0$. (For $n=\pm1$, $|z|=\pi>1$.)

So the **only** singularity inside $|z|=1$ is at $z=0$.

### Step 2: Classify the singularity at $z=0$

Expand each factor in a Taylor series around $z=0$:

- Numerator: $1 - \cos z = \dfrac{z^2}{2} - \dfrac{z^4}{24} + \cdots = \dfrac{z^
2}{2}\!\left(1 - \dfrac{z^2}{12} + \cdots\right)$
- $e^z - 1 = z + \dfrac{z^2}{2} + \dfrac{z^3}{6} + \cdots = z\!\left(1 + \dfrac{
z}{2} + \dfrac{z^2}{6} + \cdots\right)$
- $\sin z = z - \dfrac{z^3}{6} + \cdots = z\!\left(1 - \dfrac{z^2}{6} + \cdots\r
ight)$

Therefore the denominator is

$$(e^z-1)\sin z = z^2\!\left(1+\tfrac{z}{2}+\cdots\right)\!\left(1-\tfrac{z^2}{6
}+\cdots\right).$$

Both the numerator and the denominator vanish to **order 2** at $z=0$. The integ
rand becomes

$$f(z) = \frac{\tfrac{z^2}{2}\left(1 - \tfrac{z^2}{12}+\cdots\right)}{z^2\left(1
+\tfrac{z}{2}+\cdots\right)} = \frac{\tfrac{1}{2}\left(1 - \tfrac{z^2}{12}+\cdot
s\right)}{1+\tfrac{z}{2}+\cdots},$$

which is a power series in $z$ with a **finite, nonzero** value at $z=0$ (namely
 $f(0)=\tfrac{1}{2}$). Hence $z=0$ is a **removable singularity**, not a pole.

### Step 3: Compute the residue

Since $z=0$ is a removable singularity, the Laurent expansion of $f$ at $z=0$ ha
s **no principal part**. Therefore

$$\operatorname{Res}(f, 0) = 0.$$

### Step 4: Apply Cauchy's residue theorem

The function $f$, after removing the singularity at $z=0$, is analytic on and in
side the contour $|z|=1$ (no other singularities lie inside). By Cauchy's residu
e theorem,

$$I = 2\pi i \sum_{\text{inside}} \operatorname{Res}(f, z_k) = 2\pi i \cdot \ope
ratorname{Res}(f,0) = 2\pi i \cdot 0 = 0.$$

Equivalently, since $f$ extends to an analytic function inside $|z|\le 1$, Cauch
y's theorem directly gives $I=0$.

### Result

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{|z|=1} \frac{1-\cos z}{(e^z-1)\sin z}\,dz = 0}$$
