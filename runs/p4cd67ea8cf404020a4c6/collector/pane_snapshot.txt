## Proof

**Claim.** Yes. If $G$ is a Lie group of dimension $g$ and $H\trianglelefteq G$
is a normal Lie subgroup of dimension $h$, then the quotient group $G/H$ is a Li
e group of dimension $g-h$.

We prove this in two parts: (1) $G/H$ is a Lie group, and (2) $\dim(G/H)=g-h$.

---

### Part 1. The quotient $G/H$ is a Lie group

Since $H$ is normal, the set of cosets $G/H=\{gH:g\in G\}$ is a group under the
well-defined operation $(g_1H)(g_2H)=(g_1g_2)H$, with identity $eH$ and inverse
$(gH)^{-1}=g^{-1}H$.

It remains to give $G/H$ the structure of a smooth manifold so that the group op
erations are smooth. Let $\pi:G\to G/H$ be the canonical projection $g\mapsto gH
$.

**$H$ is a closed embedded submanifold.** As a Lie subgroup, $H$ is by definitio
n an immersed submanifold. Being a subgroup, $H$ is closed in $G$ (any Lie subgr
oup is closed if and only if it is an embedded submanifold; here $H$ is given as
 a *Lie subgroup*, i.e., a subgroup that is a submanifold, and we use the standa
rd theorem that a subgroup which is an embedded submanifold is closed). Concrete
ly, $H=\pi^{-1}(eH)$ is the fiber over the identity, hence closed.

**Construction of charts on $G/H$.** Since $H$ is an embedded submanifold of $G$
 of dimension $h$, there exist local coordinates $(U,x^1,\dots,x^g)$ centered at
 $e\in G$ such that
$$U\cap H=\{x^{h+1}=\cdots=x^g=0\}.$$
By homogeneity (translating by left-multiplication), analogous "slice coordinate
s" exist near every point of $G$. The map
$$\varphi:U\to\mathbb{R}^{g-h},\qquad \varphi(p)=(x^{h+1}(p),\dots,x^g(p))$$
is constant on each coset of $H$ within $U$ (since two points of $U$ lie in the
same $H$-coset iff they differ by an element of $U\cap H$, which changes only $x
^1,\dots,x^h$). One verifies that $\varphi$ descends to a bijection from $\pi(U)
\subset G/H$ onto an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^{g-h}$, and these descended maps
 form a smooth atlas on $G/H$ with smooth transition functions (inherited from t
hose of $G$). Thus $G/H$ is a smooth manifold of dimension $g-h$.

**Smoothness of group operations.** With this manifold structure, $\pi:G\to G/H$
 is a smooth submersion. The multiplication $\mu:G/H\times G/H\to G/H$ satisfies
 $\mu\circ(\pi\times\pi)=\pi\circ m$, where $m:G\times G\to G$ is the (smooth) m
ultiplication of $G$. By the universal property of submersions (a map $f$ out of
 $G/H$ is smooth iff $f\circ\pi$ is smooth), $\mu$ is smooth. Likewise the inver
sion and the identity inclusion are smooth. Hence $G/H$ is a Lie group.

---

### Part 2. $\dim(G/H)=g-h$

The projection $\pi:G\to G/H$ is a smooth submersion (its differential is surjec
tive at every point, by construction of the slice charts above). For a submersio
n, the fiber dimension is constant and equals
$$\dim G-\dim(G/H).$$
The fiber over the identity coset is $\pi^{-1}(eH)=H$, which has dimension $h$.
Therefore
$$h=\dim G-\dim(G/H)=g-\dim(G/H),$$
giving
$$\boxed{\dim(G/H)=g-h.}$$

---

### Conclusion

The dimension of the quotient group $G/H$ is **always** $g-h$. The argument uses
 only that $H$ is a Lie subgroup (so that $G/H$ is a smooth manifold and $\pi$ i
s a submersion); normality is used solely to ensure $G/H$ carries a *group* stru
cture compatible with the smooth structure. The dimension formula itself holds f
or any closed Lie subgroup, normal or not.
