## Solution

---

**Goal.** Compute $\displaystyle L=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1 \min\!\left(\frac{e
^{-nx^{2}}}{\sqrt{x}},\,n\right)d\lambda.$

---

### Step 1. Locate the crossover point

Define $a_n\in(0,1)$ by $\dfrac{e^{-n a_n^{2}}}{\sqrt{a_n}}=n$, i.e. the unique
point where the two arguments of $\min$ agree. Then

$$\min\!\left(\frac{e^{-nx^{2}}}{\sqrt{x}},\,n\right)=\begin{cases} n, & 0<x<a_n
,\\[4pt] \dfrac{e^{-nx^{2}}}{\sqrt{x}}, & a_n\le x\le 1.\end{cases}$$

**Asymptotics of $a_n$.** Write $a_n=c_n\,n^{-2}$. Then $n a_n^{2}=c_n^{2}\,n^{-
3}\to 0$, so $e^{-n a_n^{2}}\to 1$, and the defining equation gives $\dfrac{1}{\
sqrt{c_n\,n^{-2}}}=n$, i.e. $\dfrac{n}{\sqrt{c_n}}=n$, hence $c_n\to 1$. Therefo
re

$$a_n\sim n^{-2},\qquad n\,a_n\sim n^{-1}\to 0.$$

---

### Step 2. Reduce to the uncapped integral

Splitting at $a_n$,

$$I_n:=\int_0^1\min\!\left(\frac{e^{-nx^{2}}}{\sqrt{x}},n\right)dx
= n\,a_n+\int_{a_n}^{1}\frac{e^{-nx^{2}}}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx
= \int_0^{1}\frac{e^{-nx^{2}}}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx \;+\;\underbrace{n\,a_n}_{\to\,0}\;
-\;\underbrace{\int_0^{a_n}\frac{e^{-nx^{2}}}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx}_{\displaystyle\le\,
\int_0^{a_n}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}}=2\sqrt{a_n}\sim 2n^{-1}\,\to\,0}.$$

Hence

$$I_n = J_n + o(1),\qquad J_n:=\int_0^{1}\frac{e^{-nx^{2}}}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx.$$

---

### Step 3. Evaluate $J_n$ asymptotically

Substitute $u=\sqrt{x}$ ($x=u^{2},\;dx=2u\,du$):

$$J_n=\int_0^{1}\frac{e^{-n u^{4}}}{u}\,2u\,du=2\int_0^{1}e^{-n u^{4}}\,du.$$

Now substitute $t=n^{1/4}u$ ($du=n^{-1/4}dt$):

$$J_n=2\,n^{-1/4}\int_0^{\,n^{1/4}} e^{-t^{4}}\,dt.$$

As $n\to\infty$, the integral converges to $\displaystyle\int_0^{\infty}e^{-t^{4
}}\,dt=\frac{1}{4}\,\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac14\right)$ (via $s=t^{4}$). Therefore

$$J_n \;\sim\; 2\,n^{-1/4}\cdot\frac{1}{4}\,\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac14\right)=\frac{
\Gamma(1/4)}{2}\,n^{-1/4}\;\longrightarrow\;0.$$

---

### Step 4. Conclusion

Both $J_n\to 0$ and the correction terms $n\,a_n\to 0$, $\int_0^{a_n}\frac{e^{-n
x^{2}}}{\sqrt{x}}dx\to 0$, so

$$\boxed{L=\lim_{n\to\infty}I_n=0.}$$
