The answer is **no**. Here is a counterexample.

---

**Setup.** Let $R = k[x,y]$ (a field $k$, polynomial ring in two variables — Noe
therian). Define a filtration $\{I_n\}_{n \geq 0}$ by
$$
I_n = \bigl(x^a y^b : a, b \geq 0,\ a + 2b = n\bigr) \subseteq R, \qquad I_0 = R
.
$$
Equivalently $I_n$ is the ideal generated by all monomials $x^a y^b$ with $a+2b=
n$. The first few are:
$$
I_1=(x),\quad I_2=(x^2,y),\quad I_3=(x^3,xy),\quad I_4=(x^4,x^2y,y^2),\quad I_5=
(x^5,x^3y,xy^2),\dots
$$

**The associated ring $T$ is Noetherian.** Consider the elements $xX$ (degree 1)
 and $yX^2$ (degree 2) in $R[X]$. The $R$-subalgebra they generate is
$$
T' = R[xX,\, yX^2] \subseteq R[X].
$$
Its degree-$n$ component is the $R$-span of $\{(xX)^a(yX^2)^b : a+2b=n\} = \{x^a
 y^b X^n : a+2b=n\}$, which is exactly $I_n X^n$. Hence
$$
T = R + I_1 X + I_2 X^2 + \cdots = R[xX,\, yX^2].
$$
Since $R=k[x,y]$ is Noetherian and $T$ is a finitely generated $R$-algebra, $T$
is Noetherian by the Hilbert basis theorem.

**The filtration condition holds.** Since $T = R[xX, yX^2]$ is a subring of $R[X
]$, multiplying the degree-$k$ and degree-$j$ graded pieces lands in the degree-
$(k+j)$ piece, i.e. $I_k I_j \subseteq I_{k+j}$.

**No ideal $J$ satisfies $I_n = J^n$ for all large $n$.** We use radicals. For e
ven $n = 2m$:
$$
I_{2m} = (x^2, y)^m, \qquad \text{so } \sqrt{I_{2m}} = \sqrt{(x^2,y)} = (x,y) \q
uad \text{for all } m \geq 1.
$$
For odd $n = 2m+1$: the generators are $x^{2m+1}, x^{2m-1}y, \dots, xy^m$, i.e.
$$
I_{2m+1} = x\cdot (x^2,y)^m,
$$
so a prime $\mathfrak p \supseteq I_{2m+1}$ iff $\mathfrak p \supseteq (x)$ or $
\mathfrak p \supseteq (x^2,y)$, giving
$$
\sqrt{I_{2m+1}} = (x) \cap (x,y) = (x) \quad \text{for all } m \geq 0.
$$

Now suppose, for contradiction, that some ideal $J \subseteq R$ satisfies $I_n =
 J^n$ for all $n \geq N$. Then $\sqrt{J^n} = \sqrt{J}$ is constant for all $n \g
eq N$. But:
- For even $n \geq N$: $\sqrt{J} = \sqrt{I_n} = (x,y)$.
- For odd $n \geq N$: $\sqrt{J} = \sqrt{I_n} = (x)$.

This forces $(x,y) = (x)$, i.e. $y \in (x)$, which is false in $k[x,y]$. Contrad
iction.

---

**Conclusion.** The ring $T = R[xX, yX^2]$ is Noetherian, the filtration $\{I_n\
}$ satisfies $I_k I_j \subseteq I_{k+j}$, yet there is **no** ideal $J$ with $I_
n = J^n$ for all sufficiently large $n$. The implication fails in general.
