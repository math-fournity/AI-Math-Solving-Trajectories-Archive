## Solution

**Notation.** Let $R_d(k) = \underbrace{\overline{dd\cdots d}}_{k} = d\cdot\frac
{10^k - 1}{9}$ denote the repdigit with digit $d$ repeated $k$ times.

The equation is $\sqrt{R_x(2n) - R_y(n)} = R_z(n)$, i.e.,

$$\sqrt{x\cdot\frac{10^{2n}-1}{9} - y\cdot\frac{10^n-1}{9}} = z\cdot\frac{10^n-1
}{9}.$$

**Squaring and clearing denominators.** Squaring both sides and multiplying by $
81$:

$$9x(10^{2n}-1) - 9y(10^n-1) = z^2(10^n-1)^2.$$

Since $10^{2n}-1 = (10^n-1)(10^n+1)$, factor out $(10^n-1) \neq 0$:

$$9x(10^n+1) - 9y = z^2(10^n-1).$$

Rearranging:

$$(9x - z^2)\cdot 10^n + (9x - 9y + z^2) = 0. \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Key step.** Equation $(\ast)$ is linear in $10^n$. If $9x - z^2 \neq 0$, it de
termines at most **one** value of $10^n$, hence at most one $n$. Since the probl
em requires the equality to hold for **at least two** values of $n$, we must hav
e both coefficients equal to zero:

$$9x - z^2 = 0, \qquad 9x - 9y + z^2 = 0.$$

**Solving the system.** From the first equation, $z^2 = 9x$. Since $x$ is a digi
t with $1 \le x \le 9$ (the repdigit $R_x(2n)$ must be positive), $9 \mid z^2$,
hence $3 \mid z$. With $z \in \{1,\dots,9\}$, the candidates are $z \in \{3, 6,
9\}$:

| $z$ | $x = z^2/9$ | $y = 2x$ (from second equation) | Valid? |
|-----|-------------|----------------------------------|--------|
| $3$ | $1$ | $2$ | ✓ |
| $6$ | $4$ | $8$ | ✓ |
| $9$ | $9$ | $18$ | ✗ (not a digit) |

From the second equation: $9y = 9x + z^2 = 9x + 9x = 18x$, so $y = 2x$.

This yields two solutions: $(x,y,z) = (1,2,3)$ and $(x,y,z) = (4,8,6)$.

**Both coefficients being zero means $(\ast)$ holds for every $n$.** Therefore t
he equality holds for **all** $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

**Verification.**

- $(x,y,z)=(1,2,3)$, $n=1$: $\sqrt{11 - 2} = \sqrt{9} = 3$ ✓; $n=2$: $\sqrt{1111
-22}=\sqrt{1089}=33$ ✓ ($33^2=1089$).
- $(x,y,z)=(4,8,6)$, $n=1$: $\sqrt{44-8}=\sqrt{36}=6$ ✓; $n=2$: $\sqrt{4444-88}=
\sqrt{4356}=66$ ✓ ($66^2=4356$).

**Conclusion.** The digit triples are $\boxed{(x,y,z) = (1,2,3)}$ and $\boxed{(x
,y,z) = (4,8,6)}$. In both cases, the equality holds for **all** $n \in \mathbb{
N}$.
