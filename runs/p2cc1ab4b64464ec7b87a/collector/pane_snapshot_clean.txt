Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $|f'(x)| > 1$ at a point im
plies sensitivity to initial conditions at that point.

**Answer: The statement is FALSE.**

## Proof

The key observation is that $|f'(x)| > 1$ measures **local expansion for a singl
e step** at $x$. Sensitivity, however, depends on the **long-term behavior** of
nearby orbits. If the orbit of $x$ subsequently enters regions of strong contrac
tion, the initial expansion can be completely undone, making $x$ non-sensitive.

### Counterexample

We construct a smooth map $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ possessing a **super-at
tracting 2-cycle** $\{0, 1\}$ where the point $x = 0$ satisfies $|f'(0)| > 1$ ye
t is not sensitive to initial conditions.

**Construction.** Define $f$ piecewise with smooth interpolation:
- For $|x| < \tfrac{1}{4}$: $f(x) = 1 + 2x$, giving $f(0) = 1$ and $f'(0) = 2$.
- For $|x - 1| < \tfrac{1}{4}$: $f(x) = (x-1)^2$, giving $f(1) = 0$ and $f'(1) =
 0$.
- On the remaining regions, smoothly interpolate using a partition of unity so t
hat $f$ is $C^\infty$.

Such a smooth map exists by standard smooth extension techniques (bump functions
 / partition of unity).

**Verification that $|f'(0)| > 1$.** We have $f'(0) = 2$, so $|f'(0)| = 2 > 1$.
✓

**Verification that $0$ is not sensitive.** The 2-cycle $\{0, 1\}$ has multiplie
r:
$$\lambda = f'(0) \cdot f'(1) = 2 \cdot 0 = 0.$$
Since $|\lambda| = 0 < 1$, this 2-cycle is **super-attracting**.

For $\bar{x}$ sufficiently close to $0$ (say $|\bar{x}| < \tfrac{1}{8}$), we can
 track the orbit explicitly:
- $f(\bar{x}) = 1 + 2\bar{x}$, so $|f(\bar{x}) - f(0)| = 2|\bar{x}|$.
- $f^2(\bar{x}) = f(1 + 2\bar{x}) = (2\bar{x})^2 = 4\bar{x}^2$, so $|f^2(\bar{x}
) - f^2(0)| = 4\bar{x}^2$.
- $f^3(\bar{x}) = f(4\bar{x}^2) = 1 + 8\bar{x}^2$, so $|f^3(\bar{x}) - f^3(0)| =
 8\bar{x}^2$.
- $f^4(\bar{x}) = f(1 + 8\bar{x}^2) = 64\bar{x}^4$, so $|f^4(\bar{x}) - f^4(0)|
= 64\bar{x}^4$.

In general, for $n \geq 1$:
$$|f^{2n}(\bar{x}) - f^{2n}(0)| = O(|\bar{x}|^{2^n}) \to 0, \qquad |f^{2n+1}(\ba
r{x}) - f^{2n+1}(0)| = O(|\bar{x}|^{2^n}) \to 0.$$

The **maximum** separation over all $k \geq 0$ is achieved at $k = 1$:
$$\sup_{k \geq 0} |f^k(\bar{x}) - f^k(0)| = 2|\bar{x}|.$$

Now, for any candidate sensitivity threshold $d > 0$, choose $\varepsilon = \fra
c{d}{3}$. Then for **every** $\bar{x}$ with $|\bar{x}| < \varepsilon$:
- At $k = 0$: $|\bar{x} - 0| < \varepsilon = d/3 < d$.
- At $k = 1$: $|f(\bar{x}) - f(0)| = 2|\bar{x}| < 2\varepsilon = 2d/3 < d$.
- At $k \geq 2$: $|f^k(\bar{x}) - f^k(0)| \leq C|\bar{x}|^2 < C\varepsilon^2 < d
$ (for $\varepsilon$ small enough).

Thus, no $\bar{x}$ with $|\bar{x}| < \varepsilon$ satisfies $|f^k(\bar{x}) - f^k
(0)| \geq d$ for any $k$. Since this holds for every $d > 0$ (by choosing $\vare
psilon$ accordingly), the point $x = 0$ is **not sensitive** to initial conditio
ns.

### Why the intuition fails

The condition $|f'(x)| > 1$ guarantees expansion for **one step** near $x$. Howe
ver, sensitivity requires that nearby orbits **eventually** separate by a defini
te amount $d > 0$ that does not shrink to zero as $\bar{x} \to x$. In our counte
rexample, the one-step expansion at $x = 0$ (factor $2$) is overwhelmed by the s
uper-contraction at $f(0) = 1$ (factor $0$), so the net effect over two steps is
 contraction with multiplier $0$. The orbits of nearby points converge to the 2-
cycle, and the maximum separation $2|\bar{x}|$ vanishes as $\bar{x} \to 0$.

**Remark.** If $x$ were a **fixed point** ($f(x) = x$) with $|f'(x)| > 1$, then
the statement would be true: the expansion compounds at every step since the orb
it remains at $x$, and any nearby point is repelled exponentially. The problem,
however, does not assume $x$ is a fixed point—it only assumes $|f'(x)| > 1$, whi
ch is insufficient.

### Conclusion

The statement is **false**. The condition $|f'(x)| > 1$ at a single point does n
ot imply sensitivity to initial conditions at that point, because subsequent con
traction along the orbit can negate the local expansion. A concrete counterexamp
le is a smooth map with a super-attracting 2-cycle $\{0, 1\}$ where $f'(0) = 2 >
 1$ but all nearby orbits converge to the cycle, precluding any uniform separati
on $d > 0$.
