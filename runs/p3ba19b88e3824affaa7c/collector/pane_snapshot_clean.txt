Since $F$ preserves composition and is surjective:
$$F(f)\circ F(\mathrm{id}) = F(f\circ\mathrm{id})=F(f)\quad\forall f\in C.$$
As $f$ ranges over all of $C$ and $F$ is surjective, $F(\mathrm{id})$ acts as a
left-identity on all of $C$, so $F(\mathrm{id})=\mathrm{id}$.

**Step 2: $F$ maps constant functions to constant functions.**

The constant functions are precisely the **left zeros** of $(C,\circ)$: $c$ is c

- *If $c=c_a$:* $c_a\circ f = c_a$ for any $f$. ✓
- *Conversely,* if $c\circ f=c$ for all $f$, then in particular $c\circ f=c$ for

Now apply $F$: if $c$ is a left zero, then for all $g\in C$ (writing $g=F(f)$, u

$$F(c)\circ g = F(c)\circ F(f)=F(c\circ f)=F(c).$$
So $F(c)$ is a left zero, hence constant. Since $F$ is a bijection, it bijects t

**Step 3: Define $h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$.**

Since $F$ bijects constants to constants, define $h$ by:
$$F(c_a)=c_{h(a)},\qquad a\in\mathbb{R}.$$
Then $h$ is a bijection of $\mathbb{R}$ (as $F$ bijects $\{c_a:a\in\mathbb{R}\}$

**Step 4: Derive the conjugation formula $F(f)=h\circ f\circ h^{-1}$.**

For any $f\in C$ and $a\in\mathbb{R}$:
$$f\circ c_a = c_{f(a)}.$$

$$F(f)\circ F(c_a)=F(c_{f(a)}),$$
$$F(f)\circ c_{h(a)}=c_{h(f(a))},$$
$$c_{F(f)(h(a))}=c_{h(f(a))}.$$
Therefore:
$$\boxed{F(f)(h(a))=h(f(a))\quad\forall\,a\in\mathbb{R},\;\forall\,f\in C.}$$

^{-1}(t)$, giving:
$$F(f)(t)=h\!\left(f\!\left(h^{-1}(t)\right)\right),\qquad\text{i.e.,}\quad F(f)
=h\circ f\circ h^{-1}.$$
