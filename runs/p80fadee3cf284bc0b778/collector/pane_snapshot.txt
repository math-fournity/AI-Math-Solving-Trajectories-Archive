Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether non-normal trace-class oper
ators can have trace norm 1 while having arbitrarily small (even zero) sum of ab
solute diagonal entries in some orthonormal basis.

**Answer: YES, such a sequence exists.**

## Proof

Work on $H = \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$ with standard orthonormal basis $\{e_i\}_{i\in\
mathbb{N}}$.

**Construction.** For each $n \in \mathbb{N}$, define the rank-1 operator
$$X_n = |e_1\rangle\langle e_{n+1}|,$$
i.e., $X_n e_{n+1} = e_1$ and $X_n e_i = 0$ for $i \neq n+1$.

**Verification of each required property:**

**1. Trace-class.** Each $X_n$ is rank-1, hence finite-rank, hence trace-class.

**2. Trace norm equals 1.** Compute $X_n^*X_n = |e_{n+1}\rangle\langle e_1|\cdot
|e_1\rangle\langle e_{n+1}| = |e_{n+1}\rangle\langle e_{n+1}|$, so $|X_n| = (X_n
^*X_n)^{1/2} = |e_{n+1}\rangle\langle e_{n+1}|$, whose only nonzero eigenvalue i
s $1$. Therefore
$$\|X_n\|_{tc} = \operatorname{tr}|X_n| = 1.$$

**3. Non-normal.** We have $X_n^*X_n = |e_{n+1}\rangle\langle e_{n+1}|$ but $X_n
X_n^* = |e_1\rangle\langle e_1|$. Since $n+1 \neq 1$ (as $n \geq 1$), these are
distinct projections, so $X_n^*X_n \neq X_nX_n^*$, confirming $X_n$ is non-norma
l.

**4. Zero diagonal in the standard basis.** For each $i$:
$$\langle e_i, X_n e_i\rangle = \langle e_i,\, \delta_{i,n+1}\, e_1\rangle = \de
lta_{i,n+1}\,\langle e_i, e_1\rangle = 0,$$
since $i = n+1 \geq 2$ implies $\langle e_i, e_1\rangle = 0$. Therefore
$$\sum_{i=1}^\infty |\langle e_i, X_n e_i\rangle| = 0 \quad \text{for all } n.$$

**Conclusion.** Taking the orthonormal basis $\{v_i\} = \{e_i\}$ (fixed, indepen
dent of $n$), we have:
- Each $X_n$ is a **non-normal** trace-class operator.
- $\|X_n\|_{tc} = 1$ for all $n$.
- $\sum_{i=1}^\infty |\langle v_i, X_n v_i\rangle| = 0 \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$.

**Key insight.** For a *normal* trace-class operator, the eigenbasis achieves $\
sum_i |\langle e_i, Te_i\rangle| = \sum_i |\lambda_i| = \|T\|_{tc}$, so the diag
onal sum equals the trace norm. The obstruction to making the diagonal small is
absent for *non-normal* operators: their left and right singular vectors differ,
 and one can choose a basis that completely misaligns them. A nilpotent rank-1 o
perator $|e_1\rangle\langle e_{n+1}|$ is the simplest instance—its trace is $0$,
 and in the standard basis every diagonal entry vanishes, yet its trace norm is
$1$.
