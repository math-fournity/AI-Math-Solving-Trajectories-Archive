Looking at this problem, I need to find the concurrence point T of the B-chords
and compute its distance from the center C of Ω.

## Setup

Place C at the origin, so Ω: x² + y² = R² = 400, and O = (d, 0) = (10, 0). Set δ
 = d/R = 1/2.

**Parametrization.** A point on Ω at angle α has coordinates (R cos α, R sin α).
 Using the half-angle substitution t = tan(α/2), the point is:
$$\left(\frac{R(1-t^2)}{1+t^2},\; \frac{2Rt}{1+t^2}\right).$$

**Chord equation.** The chord of Ω through points with half-angle parameters t₁,
 t₂ is:
$$(1 - t_1 t_2)\,x + (t_1 + t_2)\,y = R(1 + t_1 t_2). \tag{1}$$

## Condition for A-chord through O

Substituting O = (Rδ, 0) into (1): δ(1 − t₁t₂) = 1 + t₁t₂, giving:
$$t_1 \cdot t_2 = \frac{\delta - 1}{1 + \delta} = -k, \qquad k := \frac{1-\delta
}{1+\delta}. \tag{2}$$

## The map Aᵢ → Bᵢ

The circle with diameter OAᵢ meets Ω at Aᵢ and Bᵢ. Computing the radical axis (t
he line through both intersection points), Bᵢ = (R cos β, R sin β) satisfies:
$$\cos(\beta - \alpha) + \delta\cos\beta = 1 + \delta\cos\alpha.$$

Solving (this linear equation in cos β, sin β has the trivial solution β = α and
 a second solution), one obtains:
$$\cos\beta = \frac{(1+\delta^2)\cos\alpha + 2\delta}{1 + 2\delta\cos\alpha + \d
elta^2}, \qquad \sin\beta = \frac{(1-\delta^2)\sin\alpha}{1 + 2\delta\cos\alpha
+ \delta^2}.$$

In complex form with z = e^{iα}, w = e^{iβ}, this is the **Blaschke/Möbius autom
orphism**:
$$w = \frac{z + \delta}{1 + \delta\, z}.$$

Translating to half-angle parameters via z = (1+it)/(1−it), a direct computation
 yields:
$$\tan\frac{\beta}{2} = \frac{1-\delta}{1+\delta}\cdot\tan\frac{\alpha}{2} = k\c
dot t. \tag{3}$$

So the map Aᵢ → Bᵢ simply **scales the half-angle parameter by k**.

## The B-chords concur at T

For a chord through O with A-endpoints having parameters t₁, t₂ (so t₁t₂ = −k by
 (2)), the B-endpoints have parameters s₁ = kt₁, s₂ = kt₂. Their product is:
$$s_1 \cdot s_2 = k^2 \cdot t_1 t_2 = k^2 \cdot (-k) = -k^3. \tag{4}$$

This is **constant** — independent of the chord! Applying the chord formula (1)
to the B-chord:
$$(1 - s_1 s_2)\,x + (s_1 + s_2)\,y = R(1 + s_1 s_2).$$

Using s₁s₂ = −k³:
$$(1 + k^3)\,x + (s_1 + s_2)\,y = R(1 - k^3). \tag{5}$$

The coefficient of x and the right-hand side are **fixed**; only the y-coefficie
nt s₁+s₂ = k(t₁+t₂) varies. Setting y = 0, every B-chord passes through:
$$T = \left(\frac{R(1 - k^3)}{1 + k^3},\; 0\right).$$

## Computing the distance

With δ = 1/2:
$$k = \frac{1 - 1/2}{1 + 1/2} = \frac{1}{3}, \qquad k^3 = \frac{1}{27}.$$

$$|CT| = R\cdot\frac{1 - k^3}{1 + k^3} = 20 \cdot \frac{1 - 1/27}{1 + 1/27} = 20
 \cdot \frac{26/27}{28/27} = 20 \cdot \frac{26}{28} = 20 \cdot \frac{13}{14} = \
frac{130}{7}.$$

**Verification with explicit chords.** I computed three specific chords through
O (directions θ = π/2, π/4, π/3) and found the B-chord equations:
- B₁B₂: 7x = 130
- B₃B₄: 7x − 3y = 130
- B₅B₆: 7x − √3 y = 130

All three pass through (130/7, 0), confirming |CT| = 130/7.

**General formula** (for completeness): $|CT| = \frac{d(3R^2 + d^2)}{R^2 + 3d^2}
$, which gives 10·1300/700 = 130/7.

## Answer

The distance from the center of Ω to T is $\boxed{\dfrac{130}{7}}$.
