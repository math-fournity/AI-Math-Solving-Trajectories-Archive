## Proof

**Yes**, the condition implies $R(k,q) > n$.

**Setup.** Consider the probability space of all edge 2-colorings of $K_n$, wher
e each edge is colored red with probability $p$ and blue with probability $1-p$,
 independently, with $0 < p < 1$. Define the events

- $A$ = "there exists a monochromatic $K_k$" (a $k$-clique all of one color),
- $B$ = "there exists a monochromatic $K_q$" (a $q$-clique all of one color).

The hypothesis is $\Pr(A) + \Pr(B) < 1$.

**Step 1: Existence of a coloring avoiding both events.** By the union bound,

$$\Pr(A \cup B) \le \Pr(A) + \Pr(B) < 1,$$

so

$$\Pr\!\bigl(\overline{A} \cap \overline{B}\bigr) = 1 - \Pr(A \cup B) > 0.$$

Since $0 < p < 1$, every individual coloring of $K_n$ has strictly positive prob
ability (each edge-coloring with $e$ red edges has probability $p^{e}(1-p)^{\bin
om{n}{2}-e} > 0$). An event of positive probability in this space is necessarily
 **non-empty**: there exists at least one concrete coloring $c$ belonging to $\o
verline{A} \cap \overline{B}$.

**Step 2: This coloring witnesses $R(k,q) > n$.** The coloring $c$ has:
- no monochromatic $K_k$, meaning in particular **no red $K_k$** (and no blue $K
_k$);
- no monochromatic $K_q$, meaning in particular **no blue $K_q$** (and no red $K
_q$).

Recall $R(k,q)$ is the smallest $N$ such that every red/blue coloring of $K_N$ c
ontains a red $K_k$ or a blue $K_q$. The coloring $c$ of $K_n$ contains neither
a red $K_k$ nor a blue $K_q$, so $K_n$ admits a coloring avoiding both forbidden
 subgraphs. Hence $R(k,q) > n$.

**Remark on the role of $0 < p < 1$.** The strict inequality $0 < p < 1$ is esse
ntial: it guarantees the probability measure has full support on all $2^{\binom{
n}{2}}$ colorings, so that "positive probability" rigorously entails "existence.
" With a degenerate $p \in \{0,1\}$, a positive-probability event could be suppo
rted on a single (trivial) coloring that does not represent a genuine 2-coloring
, and the conclusion would not follow.
