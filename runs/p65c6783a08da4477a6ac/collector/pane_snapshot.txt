## Proof

**Question.** Does every (finite) sequence of digits appear infinitely often in
a normal number?

**Answer.** Yes.

**Definition.** A real number $x$ is *normal in base* $b$ if, for every $k \geq
1$ and every digit string $w \in \{0,1,\dots,b-1\}^k$, the asymptotic frequency
of $w$ among consecutive length-$k$ blocks of the base-$b$ expansion of $x$ equa
ls $b^{-k}$:

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#\{0\le i < n : \text{the block } x_i x_{i+1}\cdots x_
{i+k-1} = w\}}{n} = b^{-k}.$$

**Claim.** If $x$ is normal in base $b$, then every finite digit string $w$ occu
rs infinitely often in the base-$b$ expansion of $x$.

**Proof.** Let $w$ be any finite digit string of length $k$. By normality, the a
symptotic frequency of $w$ is $b^{-k} > 0$.

Suppose for contradiction that $w$ occurs only finitely many times, say $N$ time
s total, in the expansion of $x$. Then for all $n$,

$$\#\{0\le i < n : \text{block at position } i = w\} \le N,$$

so

$$\frac{\#\{0\le i < n : \text{block at position } i = w\}}{n} \le \frac{N}{n} \
xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.$$

This forces the asymptotic frequency to be $0$, contradicting the normality requ
irement that it equal $b^{-k} > 0$.

Therefore $w$ must occur infinitely often. $\square$

**Remark.** The converse is false: a number in which every finite string appears
 infinitely often need not be normal (the frequencies could fail to converge to
$b^{-k}$, or could converge to wrong limits). Thus "every finite block appears i
nfinitely often" is strictly weaker than normality—it is a necessary but not suf
ficient condition. The argument above shows only the forward direction, which is
 what the question asks.
