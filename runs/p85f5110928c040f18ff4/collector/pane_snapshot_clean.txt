Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $P(G) \cong P(H)$ implies $
G \cong H$.

## Analysis

**The category $P(G)$:** Objects are elements of $G$, and $\text{Hom}(a,b) = \{a
b^{-1}\}$ is always a singleton. So there is exactly one morphism between any tw
o objects — $P(G)$ is a **codiscrete** (chaotic) preorder.

**Composition check:** The morphism $a \to b$ is $ab^{-1}$; the morphism $b \to
c$ is $bc^{-1}$. Their composite must be the morphism $a \to c$, namely $ac^{-1}
$. Indeed $(ab^{-1})(bc^{-1}) = a(b^{-1}b)c^{-1} = ac^{-1}$, so composition is w
ell-defined and coincides with group multiplication. The identity on $a$ is $aa^
{-1} = e$. Associativity is automatic since every hom-set is a singleton.

**Key observation:** As a *category*, $P(G)$ is the codiscrete preorder on $|G|$
 objects. The labels $ab^{-1}$ on morphisms are not part of the category structu
re — a morphism in a category is determined by its source, target, and role in c
omposition, not by any external label. The composition law is forced: "the uniqu
e morphism $a \to b$ composed with the unique morphism $b \to c$ gives the uniqu
e morphism $a \to c$." This holds for any codiscrete preorder regardless of the
group.

**When are two codiscrete preorders isomorphic?** A functor $F: P(G) \to P(H)$ b
etween codiscrete preorders is any function on objects (since every pair is comp
arable, every function is order-preserving). It is an isomorphism of categories
if and only if it is a bijection on objects — bijectivity on objects automatical
ly yields bijectivity on morphisms (since each hom-set is a singleton and the nu
mber of hom-sets is $|G|^2 = |H|^2$).

Therefore:
$$P(G) \cong P(H) \iff |G| = |H|.$$

## Counterexample

Take $G = \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ (cyclic of order 4) and $H = (\mathbb{Z}/2\mat
hbb{Z})^2$ (Klein four-group, not cyclic). Both have 4 elements, so $P(G) \cong
P(H)$ as categories (both are the codiscrete preorder on 4 objects). Yet $G \not
\cong H$ since $G$ is cyclic and $H$ is not.

## Conclusion

**No**, $G$ need not be isomorphic to $H$. The category $P(G)$ only records the
cardinality of $G$, not its group structure. Two groups of the same order always
 yield isomorphic categories $P(G) \cong P(H)$, regardless of whether the groups
 themselves are isomorphic.
