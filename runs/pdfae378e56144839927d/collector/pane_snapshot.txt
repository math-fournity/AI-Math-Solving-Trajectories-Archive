# Solution: Minimum-Time Rocket Ascent with Fixed Fuel Energy

## Setup

We minimize the final time $T$ subject to:
- Dynamics: $\ddot{x} = U(t) - g$, with $x(0)=0$, $\dot{x}(0)=0$.
- Terminal constraint: $x(T) = h$.
- Isoperimetric constraint: $\int_0^T U^2(t)\,dt = k^2$.
- Terminal velocity $\dot{x}(T)$ is **free** (not specified).

Introduce state variables $x_1 = x$, $x_2 = \dot{x}$, and an additional state $x
_3$ with $\dot{x}_3 = U^2$, $x_3(0)=0$, $x_3(T)=k^2$ to convert the integral con
straint into a terminal constraint.

## Pontryagin's Minimum Principle

The Hamiltonian (for the minimum-time cost $\int_0^T 1\,dt$) is:

$$H = 1 + p_1\, x_2 + p_2\,(U - g) + p_3\, U^2$$

**Costate equations:**
- $\dot{p}_1 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; p_1 = \text{const}$
- $\dot{p}_2 = -p_1 \;\Rightarrow\; p_2(t) = -p_1\, t + c$
- $\dot{p}_3 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; p_3 = \text{const}$

**Optimal control** (minimize $H$ over $U$):

$$\frac{\partial H}{\partial U} = p_2 + 2p_3\, U = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad U(t)
= -\frac{p_2(t)}{2p_3}$$

Since $p_2(t)$ is linear in $t$, **$U(t)$ is linear in $t$**.

## Transversality Conditions

Since $x_1(T)=h$ is fixed and $x_3(T)=k^2$ is fixed, but $x_2(T)=\dot{x}(T)$ is
**free**:

$$p_2(T) = 0$$

From $p_2(t) = -p_1 t + c$ and $p_2(T)=0$: $\;c = p_1 T$, so

$$p_2(t) = p_1(T - t)$$

Therefore:

$$\boxed{U(t) = \frac{p_1}{2p_3}(t - T) = A(t - T)}$$

where $A = p_1/(2p_3)$ is a constant. Note that $U(T) = 0$ automatically.

The free-terminal-time condition $H(T)=0$ determines the multiplier $p_1$ (and d
oes not over-determine the system), so the physical unknowns are $A$ and $T$.

## Applying the Boundary Conditions

**Integrating the equations of motion** with $U(t) = A(t-T)$:

$$\ddot{x} = A(t-T) - g$$

$$\dot{x}(t) = \tfrac{1}{2}A\,t^2 - (AT + g)\,t$$

$$x(t) = \tfrac{1}{6}A\,t^3 - \tfrac{1}{2}(AT+g)\,t^2$$

**Terminal position** $x(T) = h$:

$$x(T) = \tfrac{1}{6}AT^3 - \tfrac{1}{2}AT^3 - \tfrac{1}{2}gT^2 = -\tfrac{1}{3}A
T^3 - \tfrac{1}{2}gT^2 = h$$

$$\boxed{A = -\frac{3\!\left(h + \tfrac{1}{2}gT^2\right)}{T^3}} \tag{1}$$

Since $h > 0$ and $g > 0$, we have $A < 0$.

**Fuel-energy constraint** $\int_0^T U^2\,dt = k^2$:

$$\int_0^T A^2(t-T)^2\,dt = A^2\cdot\frac{T^3}{3} = k^2$$

$$\boxed{A^2\,\frac{T^3}{3} = k^2} \tag{2}$$

## Determining $T$

Substituting (1) into (2):

$$\frac{9\!\left(h+\tfrac{1}{2}gT^2\right)^2}{T^6}\cdot\frac{T^3}{3} = k^2$$

$$\boxed{3\!\left(h + \tfrac{1}{2}gT^2\right)^{\!2} = k^2\, T^3} \tag{3}$$

Equivalently, expanding:

$$\tfrac{3}{4}g^2\,T^4 - k^2\,T^3 + 3hg\,T^2 + 3h^2 = 0$$

This is a quartic in $T$. Define $f(T) = 3(h+\tfrac{1}{2}gT^2)^2/T^3$, the minim
um fuel energy required to reach height $h$ in time $T$. Since $f(T)\to\infty$ a
s $T\to 0^+$ and $f(T)\to\infty$ as $T\to\infty$, $f$ has a global minimum at so
me finite $T^*$. Equation (3) has solutions iff $k^2 \geq f(T^*)$, and the **min
imum time** $T$ is the **smallest positive root** of (3).

## Verification of Optimality (Sufficiency)

For any **fixed** $T$, the subproblem "minimize $\int_0^T U^2\,dt$ subject to $x
(T)=h$" is a convex optimization problem (quadratic objective, linear dynamics,
affine terminal constraint). Its unique solution is precisely $U(t)=A(t-T)$ with
 $A$ given by (1), yielding minimum energy $f(T) = 3(h+\tfrac{1}{2}gT^2)^2/T^3$.

The overall problem reduces to: find the smallest $T$ such that $f(T) \leq k^2$.
 Since the constraint is active at the optimum ($f(T)=k^2$), and $f$ is decreasi
ng on $(0, T^*)$, the smallest positive root of $f(T)=k^2$ gives the global mini
mum time. The PMP extremal is therefore the **global optimizer**.

## Final Answer

The particular thrust function that minimizes $T$ is:

$$\boxed{U(t) = \frac{3\!\left(h + \tfrac{1}{2}\,g\,T^2\right)}{T^3}\;(T - t), \
qquad 0 \le t \le T}$$

where $T$ is the **smallest positive root** of

$$\boxed{3\!\left(h + \tfrac{1}{2}\,g\,T^2\right)^{\!2} = k^2\, T^3}$$

**Physical interpretation:** The optimal thrust is a **linearly decreasing funct
ion of time**, starting at $U(0) = 3(h+\tfrac{1}{2}gT^2)/T^2 > 0$ (maximum thrus
t at launch) and decreasing to $U(T) = 0$ (engine shuts off at the terminal time
). The rocket burns fuel hardest at the beginning to build up velocity, then coa
sts with decreasing thrust to zero at arrival — a consequence of the free termin
al velocity condition $p_2(T)=0$.
