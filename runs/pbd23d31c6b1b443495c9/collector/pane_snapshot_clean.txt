## Answer

**Yes, $B$ must be continuous.**

**Proof.** Since $B$ is symmetric and bilinear over the real Hilbert space $H$,
we use the polarization identity (valid for real symmetric bilinear forms):

$$B(x,y) = \tfrac{1}{4}\bigl[B(x+y,\,x+y) - B(x-y,\,x-y)\bigr].$$

Applying the hypothesis $|B(z,z)| \le C\|z\|^2$ to $z = x+y$ and $z = x-y$:

$$|B(x,y)| \le \tfrac{1}{4}\bigl(|B(x+y,x+y)| + |B(x-y,x-y)|\bigr) \le \tfrac{C}
{4}\bigl(\|x+y\|^2 + \|x-y\|^2\bigr).$$

By the parallelogram law in the Hilbert space,

$$\|x+y\|^2 + \|x-y\|^2 = 2\|x\|^2 + 2\|y\|^2.$$

Hence

$$|B(x,y)| \le \tfrac{C}{2}\bigl(\|x\|^2 + \|y\|^2\bigr). \tag{$\ast$}$$

Now for arbitrary $x, y \in H$. If either is zero, $B(x,y)=0$ by bilinearity and
 the desired bound holds trivially. Otherwise set $u = x/\|x\|$, $v = y/\|y\|$,
so $\|u\|=\|v\|=1$. By bilinearity and $(\ast)$ applied to the unit vectors $u,
v$:

$$|B(u,v)| \le \tfrac{C}{2}(1 + 1) = C.$$

Therefore

$$|B(x,y)| = \|x\|\,\|y\|\,|B(u,v)| \le C\,\|x\|\,\|y\|.$$

This shows $B$ is a bounded bilinear form (with constant $C$), and a bounded bil
inear form on a normed space is continuous. $\blacksquare$

**Justification summary:** The point is that for *symmetric* bilinear forms on a
 real Hilbert space, the quadratic form $q(x)=B(x,x)$ determines $B$ entirely vi
a the polarization identity. The bound on $q$ together with the parallelogram la
w transfers the bound to $B$ itself, yielding $|B(x,y)|\le C\|x\|\|y\|$, i.e. co
ntinuity. (Without symmetry, or in the complex setting without the Hermitian-typ
e hypothesis, the conclusion need not hold; the symmetry is essential here.)
