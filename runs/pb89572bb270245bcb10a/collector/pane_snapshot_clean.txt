## Solution

We evaluate $L = \lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dfrac{\binom{n}{k}}{k^{2}2^{n}+
n^{2}}$ using the local central limit approximation
$$\binom{n}{k}\sim \sqrt{\tfrac{2}{n\pi}}\,2^{n}\,e^{-(n-2k)^{2}/(2n)}.$$

**Step 1. Locate the dominant region.** The mass of $\binom{n}{k}/2^{n}$ is conc
entrated where $|n-2k|=O(\sqrt n)$, i.e. $k=n/2+O(\sqrt n)$. Set
$$k=\tfrac n2+\tfrac{\sqrt n}{2}\,x,\qquad x=\frac{2k-n}{\sqrt n},\qquad dk\appr
ox \tfrac{\sqrt n}{2}\,dx.$$
Then $(n-2k)^{2}/(2n)=x^{2}/2$, and
$$k^{2}=\tfrac{n^{2}}{4}\Bigl(1+\tfrac{x}{\sqrt n}\Bigr)^{2}.$$

**Step 2. Simplify the denominator in the bulk.** For $x=O(1)$,
$$k^{2}2^{n}=\tfrac{n^{2}}{4}\Bigl(1+\tfrac{x}{\sqrt n}\Bigr)^{2}2^{n}\gg n^{2},
$$
so $k^{2}2^{n}+n^{2}\sim k^{2}2^{n}$. The boundary terms where this fails ($k$ s
mall) are negligible: $k=0$ contributes $1/n^{2}\to0$, and $k=1$ contributes $n/
(2^{n}+n^{2})\to0$; the Gaussian tail $|x|\gg\sqrt n$ is exponentially suppresse
d. Hence the whole sum is asymptotic to its bulk part.

**Step 3. Apply the Gaussian approximation.** Replacing the sum by an integral o
ver the bulk,
$$\sum_{k}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{k^{2}2^{n}+n^{2}}\;\sim\;\int \frac{\sqrt{2/(n\pi)
}\,2^{n}\,e^{-x^{2}/2}}{k^{2}2^{n}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt n}{2}\,dx
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\frac{e^{-x^{2}/2}}{k^{2}}\,dx.$$
Substituting $k^{2}=\frac{n^{2}}{4}(1+x/\sqrt n)^{2}$,
$$=\frac{4}{n^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-x^{
2}/2}}{(1+x/\sqrt n)^{2}}\,dx.$$

**Step 4. Take the limit.** For fixed $x$, $(1+x/\sqrt n)^{-2}\to1$, and the int
egrand is dominated (on compact $x$-sets) by an integrable Gaussian; the tails a
re controlled by the $e^{-x^{2}/2}$ factor. Therefore by dominated convergence,
$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-x^{2}/2}}{(1+x/\sqrt n)^
{2}}\,dx\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}
/2}\,dx=1.$$
Consequently
$$\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{k^{2}2^{n}+n^{2}}\;\sim\;\frac{4}{n^{2}}\;\l
ongrightarrow\;0.$$

The leading behavior is $4/n^{2}\to 0$, so the limit is

$$\boxed{\,0\,}.$$
