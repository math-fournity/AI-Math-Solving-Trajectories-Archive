## Proof

$$\int_1^\infty \frac{f(1/t)}{t}\,dt = \int_0^1 \frac{f(x)}{x}\,dx.$$

**Second term (each summand):** When $t: k \to k+1$, $x: 1/k \to 1/(k+1)$. The i

$$\int_k^{k+1} \frac{f(1/t)}{t^2}\,dt = \int_{1/(k+1)}^{1/k} f(x)\,dx.$$

Thus the equation becomes:
$$\int_0^1 \frac{f(x)}{x}\,dx = \sum_{k=1}^\infty k\int_{1/(k+1)}^{1/k} f(x)\,dx
. \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Step 2: Combine into a single integral.** Partition $(0,1] = \bigcup_{k=1}^\in
fty \big(\frac{1}{k+1}, \frac{1}{k}\big]$ and split the left side accordingly:
$$\int_0^1 \frac{f(x)}{x}\,dx = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \int_{1/(k+1)}^{1/k} \frac{f(x
)}{x}\,dx.$$

Substituting into $(\ast)$:
$$\sum_{k=1}^\infty \int_{1/(k+1)}^{1/k} f(x)\!\left(\frac{1}{x} - k\right)dx =
0.$$

On the interval $\big(\frac{1}{k+1}, \frac{1}{k}\big)$, we have $k < \frac{1}{x}
 < k+1$, so $\frac{1}{x} - k = \{1/x\}$, the **fractional part** of $1/x$. There
fore the equation is:

$$\int_0^1 f(x)\,\{1/x\}\,dx = 0. \tag{$\ast\ast$}$$

**Step 3: Well-definedness.** Define $g(x) = \{1/x\}$ for $x \in (0,1)$. Then $g

= 0$ only when $1/x \in \mathbb{Z}$, i.e., $x = 1/k$, a countable set of measure

For any $f \in L^2(0,1)$, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality:
$$\left|\int_0^1 f(x)\,g(x)\,dx\right| \le \|f\|_{L^2}\,\|g\|_{L^2} < \infty,$$
so the integral in $(\ast\ast)$ is well-defined for every $f \in L^2(0,1)$.

$$g^\perp = \left\{f \in L^2(0,1) : \langle f, g\rangle = 0\right\}.$$

Since $g \neq 0$ in $L^2(0,1)$, the subspace $g^\perp$ is a **proper closed subs
pace of codimension 1**. Since $L^2(0,1)$ is infinite-dimensional, $g^\perp$ is
non-trivial—indeed, infinite-dimensional. Therefore there exist non-zero functio
ns $f \in L^2(0,1)$ satisfying $(\ast\ast)$, and hence the original equation.

**Step 5: Concrete example.** Take $h(x) = \mathbf{1}_{(0,1)}(x) \in L^2(0,1)$.
Then
$$\langle h, g\rangle = \int_0^1 \{1/x\}\,dx = 1 - \gamma \neq 0,$$
where $\gamma$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant (this is a classical identity).
Since $h$ is constant while $g$ is not, $h$ is not a scalar multiple of $g$. Set
$$f(x) = h(x) - \frac{\langle h, g\rangle}{\|g\|^2}\,g(x) = 1 - \frac{1-\gamma}{
\|g\|_{L^2}^2}\,\{1/x\}.$$
Then $f \in L^2(0,1)$, $\langle f, g\rangle = 0$ (so the equation holds), and $f
