athbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}}$ (with the product topology of pointwise convergence) is s

**Answer: No.** Not all compact subsets of $\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}}$ are separab
le.

**Proof.**

We construct an explicit compact subset of $\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}}$ that is not
 separable.

**The counterexample space.** Let $D$ be a set of cardinality $\mathfrak{c}$ (th
e cardinality of the continuum), equipped with the discrete topology. Let $X = D
 \sqcup \{\infty\}$ be the one-point (Alexandroff) compactification of $D$. The
topology on $X$ is: each point of $D$ is isolated, and neighborhoods of $\infty$
 are of the form $\{\infty\} \cup (D \setminus F)$ for finite $F \subseteq D$.

**$X$ is compact Hausdorff.** Compactness: any open cover of $X$ includes a neig
hborhood $U$ of $\infty$ covering all but finitely many points of $D$; the remai
ning finitely many points are covered by finitely many additional open sets. Hau
sdorff: distinct points of $D$ are separated by their singleton neighborhoods; a
 point $d \in D$ and $\infty$ are separated by $\{d\}$ and $X \setminus \{d\}$.

**$X$ is not separable.** Each singleton $\{d\}$ for $d \in D$ is open in $X$. T
herefore, any dense subset of $X$ must intersect every $\{d\}$, hence must conta
in all of $D$. Since $|D| = \mathfrak{c} > \aleph_0$, no countable subset of $X$
 is dense.

**Embedding $X$ into $\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}}$.** Choose a bijection $\phi: D \t
o \mathbb{R}$ (possible since $|D| = |\mathbb{R}| = \mathfrak{c}$). Define a map
 $e: X \to \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}}$ by:

$$e(x)(r) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } x \in D \text{ and } r = \phi(x), \\ 0
& \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$$

Equivalently, $e(d)$ is the indicator function $\mathbf{1}_{\{\phi(d)\}} \in \ma
thbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}}$ for each $d \in D$, and $e(\infty)$ is the zero function.

**Continuity.** For each coordinate $r \in \mathbb{R}$, the map $e_r: X \to \mat
hbb{R}$ is $e_r(x) = 1$ if $x = \phi^{-1}(r)$ and $e_r(x) = 0$ otherwise. This i
s the indicator of the singleton $\{\phi^{-1}(r)\}$, which is a clopen set in $X
$ (since points of $D$ are isolated). Hence each $e_r$ is continuous. By the uni
versal property of the product topology, $e: X \to \mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}}$ is c
ontinuous.

**Injectivity.** For distinct $x, y \in X$:
- If $x, y \in D$: then $e(x)(\phi(x)) = 1$ but $e(y)(\phi(x)) = 0$.
- If $x \in D, y = \infty$: then $e(x)(\phi(x)) = 1$ but $e(\infty)(\phi(x)) = 0
$.

So $e$ is injective.

**Conclusion.** Since $X$ is compact and $\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}}$ is Hausdorff,
 the continuous injection $e$ is a topological embedding (a continuous bijection
 from a compact space onto its image in a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism). T
hus $K := e(X)$ is a compact subset of $\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}}$ homeomorphic to
 $X$. Since $X$ is not separable and separability is preserved by homeomorphisms
, $K$ is a compact subset of $\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}}$ that is not separable.

Therefore, **not all compact subsets of $\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}}$ are separable.
** $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The key mechanism is that the index set $\mathbb{R}$ has cardinality
 $\mathfrak{c}$, providing $\mathfrak{c}$-many coordinates—enough to separate $\
mathfrak{c}$-many isolated points from the point at infinity. This contrasts wit
h the countable-index case $\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}$ (a Polish space), where eve
ry compact subset is separable since second countability is hereditary to subspa
ces.
