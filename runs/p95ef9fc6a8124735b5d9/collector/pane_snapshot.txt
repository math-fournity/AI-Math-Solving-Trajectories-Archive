## Proof

We prove $\dim R[[X]] = \dim R + 1$ for any Noetherian ring $R$ (with the conven
tion $\infty + 1 = \infty$).

### Step 0: Preliminaries

Since $R$ is Noetherian, $R[[X]]$ is Noetherian. Every ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ of $
R[[X]]$ is closed in the $(X)$-adic topology: the closure is $\bigcap_N (\mathfr
ak{a}+(X)^N)$, whose image in $R[[X]]/\mathfrak{a}$ is $\bigcap_N \overline{(X)}
^N$. By Krull's intersection theorem, any $\bar{f}$ in this intersection satisfi
es $(1-X\bar{g})\bar{f}=0$ for some $\bar{g}$. But $1-Xg$ is a unit in $R[[X]]$
(constant term $1$), so $\bar{f}=0$.

### Step 1: Classification of primes

**Lemma.** Every prime $\mathfrak{P}$ of $R[[X]]$ is either $\mathfrak{p}[[X]]$
or $\mathfrak{p}[[X]]+(X)$ for some $\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)$.

*Proof.* Let $\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{P}\cap R$. First, $\mathfrak{p}[[X]]\subset
eq\mathfrak{P}$: if $a\in\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{P}$, then $aX^n\in\mathf
rak{P}$ for all $n$; any $f=\sum a_nX^n\in\mathfrak{p}[[X]]$ is the $(X)$-adic l
imit of partial sums in $\mathfrak{P}$, and $\mathfrak{P}$ is closed, so $f\in\m
athfrak{P}$.

**Case $X\in\mathfrak{P}$:** Then $\mathfrak{p}[[X]]+(X)\subseteq\mathfrak{P}$.
Conversely, $f=\sum a_nX^n\in\mathfrak{P}$ gives $a_0\equiv f\pmod{(X)}\in\mathf
rak{P}\cap R=\mathfrak{p}$, and $f-a_0\in(X)$. So $\mathfrak{P}=\mathfrak{p}[[X]
]+(X)$.

**Case $X\notin\mathfrak{P}$:** We show $\mathfrak{P}=\mathfrak{p}[[X]]$. Quotie
nting by $\mathfrak{p}[[X]]$ reduces to: $R$ a Noetherian domain, $\mathfrak{P}\
cap R=(0)$, $X\notin\mathfrak{P}$; show $\mathfrak{P}=(0)$.

Let $K=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$. Localizing at $S=R\setminus\{0\}$ gives $K[[X]]=
S^{-1}R[[X]]$, and $S^{-1}\mathfrak{P}$ is a prime of $K[[X]]$ not containing $X
$. Since $K$ is a field, $K[[X]]$ is a DVR with primes $(0)$ and $(X)$ only; $X\
notin S^{-1}\mathfrak{P}$ forces $S^{-1}\mathfrak{P}=(0)$. So every $f\in\mathfr
ak{P}$ is killed by some $s\in S$. But $R[[X]]$ is torsion-free over the domain
$R$, so $sf=0$ with $s\neq 0$ gives $f=0$. Hence $\mathfrak{P}=(0)$. $\square$

### Step 2: Height of $\mathfrak{p}[[X]]$

**Claim:** $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}[[X]])=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})
$.

Any prime $\mathfrak{Q}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}[[X]]$ cannot contain $X$ (since $X\
notin\mathfrak{p}[[X]]$: the coefficient of $X^1$ in $X$ is $1\notin\mathfrak{p}
$). By the Lemma, $\mathfrak{Q}=\mathfrak{q}[[X]]$ with $\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\m
athfrak{p}$. The correspondence $\mathfrak{q}\leftrightarrow\mathfrak{q}[[X]]$ i
s order-preserving and bijective, so the longest chain below $\mathfrak{p}[[X]]$
 equals the longest chain below $\mathfrak{p}$.

### Step 3: Height of $\mathfrak{p}[[X]]+(X)$

**Claim:** $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}[[X]]+(X))=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak
{p})+1$.

*Lower bound:* A chain $\mathfrak{p}_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak{p}_s=\
mathfrak{p}$ of length $s=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})$ lifts to
$$\mathfrak{p}_0[[X]]\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak{p}_s[[X]]\subsetneq\mat
hfrak{p}_s[[X]]+(X),$$
a chain of length $s+1$.

*Upper bound:* Localize at $\mathfrak{P}=\mathfrak{p}[[X]]+(X)$: $A=R[[X]]_{\mat
hfrak{P}}$ is local with $X$ a non-zero-divisor in the maximal ideal, and $A/(X)
\cong R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ has dimension $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})$. By the
dimension inequality for non-zero-divisors in local Noetherian rings ($\dim A\le
q\dim A/(x)+1$ when $x\in\mathfrak{m}$ is a non-zero-divisor, which follows from
 Krull's height theorem: if $y_1,\dots,y_d$ generate an $\mathfrak{m}/(x)$-prima
ry ideal in $A/(x)$, then $(x,y_1,\dots,y_d)$ is $\mathfrak{m}$-primary in $A$),
 we get $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{P})\leq\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})+1$.

### Step 4: Dimension formula

Combining Steps 1–3:
$$\dim R[[X]]=\sup_{\mathfrak{P}}\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{P})=\sup_{\mathfrak
{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)}\max\bigl(\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}),\,\operat
orname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})+1\bigr)=\sup_{\mathfrak{p}}\bigl(\operatorname{ht}(\mat
hfrak{p})+1\bigr)=\dim R+1.$$

If $\dim R=\infty$, the lower bound from Step 3 gives $\dim R[[X]]\geq\infty$, s
o both sides are $\infty$.

$$\boxed{\dim R[[X]] = \dim R + 1.}$$
