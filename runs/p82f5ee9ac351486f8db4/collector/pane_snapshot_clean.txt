Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a curl-free vector field $\
vec{F}$ on $\mathbb{R}^3\setminus\{0\}$ can restrict to $\vec{G}$ on the $z=0$ p
lane.

**Answer: No such $\vec{F}$ exists.**

## Proof

**Step 1: $\mathbb{R}^3\setminus\{0\}$ is simply connected.**

Any closed curve in $\mathbb{R}^3\setminus\{0\}$ can be continuously contracted
to a point by "going around" the removed origin (unlike $\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{
0\}$, which is not simply connected). This is a standard topological fact.

**Step 2: Curl-free on a simply connected domain implies $\vec{F}=\nabla\varphi$
.**

By the Poincaré lemma (or the standard characterization of conservative fields),
 if $\vec{F}$ has continuous partial derivatives and $\operatorname{Curl}\vec{F}
=\vec{0}$ on a simply connected domain, then $\vec{F}=\nabla\varphi$ for some sc
alar potential $\varphi$ on that domain. Hence for **any** closed curve $C$ in $
\mathbb{R}^3\setminus\{0\}$:
$$\oint_C \vec{F}\cdot d\vec{r}=0.$$

**Step 3: Choose a specific closed curve in the plane $z=0$.**

Let $C$ be the ellipse parametrized by
$$x=\cos t,\quad y=\tfrac{1}{2}\sin t,\quad z=0,\quad t\in[0,2\pi].$$
This lies on $x^2+4y^2=1$, so $x^2+4y^2=1$ along $C$. The distance from the orig
in is $\sqrt{x^2+y^2}=\sqrt{\cos^2 t+\frac{1}{4}\sin^2 t}\geq\frac{1}{2}>0$, so
$C\subset\mathbb{R}^3\setminus\{0\}$.

**Step 4: Compute $\oint_C \vec{G}\cdot d\vec{r}$.**

Since $C$ lies in the plane $z=0$, condition (iii) gives $\vec{F}=\vec{G}$ on $C
$, so
$$\oint_C \vec{F}\cdot d\vec{r}=\oint_C \vec{G}\cdot d\vec{r}.$$

On $C$ we have $x^2+4y^2=1$, so
$$\vec{G}\cdot d\vec{r}=\frac{-y}{1}\,dx+\frac{x}{1}\,dy=-y\,dx+x\,dy.$$

With $x=\cos t,\;y=\frac{1}{2}\sin t,\;dx=-\sin t\,dt,\;dy=\frac{1}{2}\cos t\,dt
$:
$$-y\,dx+x\,dy=\tfrac{1}{2}\sin t\cdot\sin t\,dt+\cos t\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\cos t\,
dt=\tfrac{1}{2}(\sin^2 t+\cos^2 t)\,dt=\tfrac{1}{2}\,dt.$$

Therefore
$$\oint_C \vec{G}\cdot d\vec{r}=\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{1}{2}\,dt=\pi\neq 0.$$

**Step 5: Contradiction.**

From Step 2, $\oint_C \vec{F}\cdot d\vec{r}=0$. From Step 4, $\oint_C \vec{F}\cd
ot d\vec{r}=\pi\neq 0$. This is a contradiction.

**Conclusion.** The three conditions (i), (ii), (iii) are mutually inconsistent.
 No vector field $\vec{F}$ satisfying all three exists. $\blacksquare$
