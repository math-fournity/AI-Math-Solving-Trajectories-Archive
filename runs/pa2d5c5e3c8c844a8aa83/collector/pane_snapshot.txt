**Answer: Yes, it is possible.**

**Construction.** Let $X = \mathbb{R}^2$ with the standard topology (which is no

**Each $C_n$ is closed.** The three pieces are all closed in $\mathbb{R}^2$: the

\infty)\times[-1,1]$ is closed. A finite union of closed sets is closed.

**Each $C_n$ is connected.** Let $A = \mathbb{R}\times\{1\}$, $B = \mathbb{R}\ti

**The sequence is decreasing.** Since $K_{n+1} = [n+1,\infty)\times[-1,1] \subse

are non-empty, $A\cap B=\emptyset$, and $A\cup B = (A\cup B)$. Thus $A\cup B$ is

**Why compactness matters.** In a compact Hausdorff space, this cannot happen: i

and compactness cannot force it to vanish.
