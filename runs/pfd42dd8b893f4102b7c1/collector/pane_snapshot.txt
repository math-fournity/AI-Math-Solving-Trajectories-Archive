## Proof

We show that χ is bounded on 𝒢. The argument proceeds by bounding each of the th
ree factors of χ on 𝒢.

**Setup.** Recall
$$\mathcal{G} = \Big\{b\in GL(\mathbb{R}^n):|\det b|\ge \varepsilon>0,\;|b_{ij}|
\le \tfrac{1}{t_o}\Big\},$$
and
$$\chi(\alpha)=\Big(1+\max_{i,j}|\alpha_{ij}|\Big)^{\ell}\Big(1+\max_{i,j}|(\alp
ha^{-1})_{ij}|\Big)^{m}|\det\alpha|^{1/p}.$$

---

**Step 1. 𝒢 is compact.** The conditions $|b_{ij}|\le 1/t_o$ and $|\det b|\ge\va
repsilon$ each define closed subsets of $\mathbb{R}^{n^2}$. Their intersection i
s closed, and the entry bound $|b_{ij}|\le 1/t_o$ makes it bounded in $\mathbb{R
}^{n^2}$. Hence 𝒢 is closed and bounded, therefore compact (Heine–Borel). In par
ticular every continuous function on 𝒢 is bounded — but we verify boundedness of
 χ directly to make the mechanism explicit.

---

**Step 2. The first factor is bounded.** For every $\alpha\in\mathcal{G}$,
$$\max_{i,j}|\alpha_{ij}|\le \frac{1}{t_o}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Big(1+\max_
{i,j}|\alpha_{ij}|\Big)^{\ell}\le\Big(1+\frac{1}{t_o}\Big)^{\ell}=:M_1<\infty.$$

---

**Step 3. The determinant factor is bounded.** The determinant is a polynomial i
n the entries, each bounded by $1/t_o$. Expanding,
$$|\det\alpha|=\Big|\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{n
}\alpha_{i,\sigma(i)}\Big|\le \sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}|\alpha_{i,\sig
ma(i)}|\le n!\Big(\frac{1}{t_o}\Big)^{n}=:D_{\max}.$$
Together with $|\det\alpha|\ge\varepsilon$ we have $\varepsilon\le|\det\alpha|\l
e D_{\max}$. Hence
$$|\det\alpha|^{1/p}\le\begin{cases}D_{\max}^{1/p},&1\le p<\infty,\\[2pt]1,&p=\i
nfty,\end{cases}=:M_2<\infty.$$

---

**Step 4. The inverse-entry factor is bounded — the key step.** By the adjugate
formula,
$$\alpha^{-1}=\frac{\operatorname{adj}(\alpha)}{\det\alpha},\qquad (\alpha^{-1})
_{ij}=\frac{C_{ji}(\alpha)}{\det\alpha},$$
where $C_{ji}(\alpha)$ is the $(j,i)$-cofactor of $\alpha$, i.e. $(-1)^{i+j}$ ti
mes the $(n-1)\times(n-1)$ minor obtained by deleting row $j$ and column $i$. Ea
ch minor is itself a determinant of an $(n-1)\times(n-1)$ matrix whose entries a

ep 3,
$$|C_{ji}(\alpha)|\le (n-1)!\Big(\frac{1}{t_o}\Big)^{n-1}=:C_{\max}\quad\text{fo
r all }i,j.$$
Since $|\det\alpha|\ge\varepsilon$ on 𝒢,
$$|(\alpha^{-1})_{ij}|=\frac{|C_{ji}(\alpha)|}{|\det\alpha|}\le\frac{C_{\max}}{\
varepsilon}=:I_{\max}\quad\text{for all }i,j,\;\alpha\in\mathcal{G}.$$
Therefore
$$\Big(1+\max_{i,j}|(\alpha^{-1})_{ij}|\Big)^{m}\le(1+I_{\max})^{m}=:M_3<\infty.
$$

---

**Step 5. Combine.** For every $\alpha\in\mathcal{G}$,

erbrace{\Big(1+\max|(\alpha^{-1})_{ij}|\Big)^{m}}_{\le M_3}\;\underbrace{|\det\a

Thus $\chi$ is bounded on 𝒢. $\blacksquare$
