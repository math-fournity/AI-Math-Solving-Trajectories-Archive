### Part 1: The dual space $c^*$

**Claim.** $c^*$ is isometrically isomorphic to $\ell^1$. Concretely, every $f \
in c^*$ admits a unique representation
$$f(x) = \alpha\, L(x) + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \alpha_n\, x_n, \qquad \alpha \in \
mathbb{C},\; (\alpha_n) \in \ell^1,$$
with $\|f\| = |\alpha| + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} |\alpha_n|$.

**Proof.** Introduce the special elements of $c$:
- $e^{(0)} = (1,1,1,\dots)$ (the constant sequence, with limit $1$),
- $e^{(n)}$ = the $n$-th standard unit vector (with limit $0$), for $n \geq 1$.

**Decomposition of an arbitrary $x \in c$.** Set $L = L(x)$. Then
$$x = L\, e^{(0)} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (x_n - L)\, e^{(n)},$$
where the series converges in sup norm: the tail $r^{(N)} = \sum_{n>N}(x_n - L)e
^{(n)}$ satisfies $\|r^{(N)}\|_\infty = \sup_{n>N}|x_n - L| \to 0$ since $x_n \t
o L$.

**Defining the coefficients.** For $f \in c^*$, set $\alpha_0 := f(e^{(0)})$ and
 $\alpha_n := f(e^{(n)})$ for $n \geq 1$. By continuity and linearity,
$$f(x) = L\,\alpha_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(x_n - L)\,\alpha_n = \Bigl(\alpha_0 -
 \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_n\Bigr) L + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \alpha_n\, x_n.$$

**Showing $(\alpha_n)_{n\geq 1} \in \ell^1$.** For $N \geq 1$ define $y^{(N)} =
\sum_{n=1}^{N} \operatorname{sgn}(\overline{\alpha_n})\, e^{(n)} \in c_0 \subset
 c$. Then $\|y^{(N)}\|_\infty \leq 1$ and
$$f(y^{(N)}) = \sum_{n=1}^{N} |\alpha_n| \;\leq\; \|f\|\,\|y^{(N)}\|_\infty \leq
 \|f\|.$$
Letting $N \to \infty$: $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_n| \leq \|f\| < \infty$, so
$(\alpha_n) \in \ell^1$. Hence $\alpha := \alpha_0 - \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \alpha_
n$ is well-defined, giving the claimed representation.

**Norm equality $\|f\| = |\alpha| + \sum |\alpha_n|$.**

*Upper bound:* $|f(x)| \leq |\alpha|\,|L| + \sum |\alpha_n|\,|x_n| \leq \bigl(|\
alpha| + \sum|\alpha_n|\bigr)\|x\|_\infty$, so $\|f\| \leq |\alpha| + \sum|\alph
a_n|$.

*Lower bound:* For $N \geq 1$ define $x^{(N)} \in c$ by
$$x^{(N)}_n = \begin{cases} \operatorname{sgn}(\overline{\alpha_n}), & n \leq N,
 \\ \operatorname{sgn}(\overline{\alpha}), & n > N. \end{cases}$$
Then $L(x^{(N)}) = \operatorname{sgn}(\overline{\alpha})$ and $\|x^{(N)}\|_\inft
y = 1$, so
$$f(x^{(N)}) = |\alpha| + \sum_{n=1}^{N}|\alpha_n| + \operatorname{sgn}(\overlin
e{\alpha})\sum_{n>N}\alpha_n.$$
The last term $\to 0$ as $N \to \infty$ (since $(\alpha_n) \in \ell^1$). Taking
real parts and the limit:
$$|\alpha| + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_n| = \lim_{N\to\infty} \operatorname{Re}
 f(x^{(N)}) \leq \limsup_{N} |f(x^{(N)})| \leq \|f\|.$$
Combining both inequalities: $\|f\| = |\alpha| + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_n|$.

**Isometric isomorphism.** The map $\Phi: c^* \to \ell^1$ (indexing $\ell^1$ by
$\{0,1,2,\dots\}$) given by
$$\Phi(f) = (\alpha,\, \alpha_1,\, \alpha_2,\, \dots)$$
is linear, and $\|\Phi(f)\|_1 = |\alpha| + \sum|\alpha_n| = \|f\|$, so it is an
isometry. It is surjective: given any $(\alpha, \alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots)$ with
 $|\alpha| + \sum|\alpha_n| < \infty$, the formula $f(x) = \alpha\, L(x) + \sum
\alpha_n x_n$ defines a bounded functional on $c$ with $\|f\| \leq |\alpha| + \s
um|\alpha_n|$, and $\Phi(f)$ recovers the data. Hence
$$\boxed{c^* \cong \ell^1 \quad\text{(isometrically).}}$$

### Part 2: The bidual space $c^{**}$

Since $c^* \cong \ell^1$ isometrically, $c^{**} \cong (\ell^1)^*$. By the classi
cal representation of the dual of $\ell^1$: every $g \in (\ell^1)^*$ has the for
m
$$g(a) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \beta_n\, a_n \quad\text{for some } (\beta_n)_{n\ge
q 0} \in \ell^\infty,$$
with $\|g\| = \|(\beta_n)\|_\infty$, and the correspondence $g \leftrightarrow (
\beta_n)$ is an isometric isomorphism $(\ell^1)^* \cong \ell^\infty$. Therefore
$$\boxed{c^{**} \cong \ell^\infty \quad\text{(isometrically).}}$$

**Remark (non-reflexivity).** Under the identifications $c^{**} \cong \ell^\inft
y$ and $c \hookrightarrow \ell^\infty$, the canonical embedding $J: c \to c^{**}
$ corresponds exactly to the natural inclusion $c \hookrightarrow \ell^\infty$.
Since this inclusion is proper (e.g. $((-1)^n)_{n\geq 1} \in \ell^\infty \setmin
us c$), $c$ is **not reflexive**: $J(c) = c \subsetneq \ell^\infty = c^{**}$.
