## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, it is true that for all $\varepsilon > 0$, $F$ is a positive set
 for the signed measure $\nu - \varepsilon\mu$.

**Definitions.** A measurable set $A$ is a *positive set* for a signed measure $
\sigma$ if every measurable subset $B \subseteq A$ satisfies $\sigma(B) \geq 0$.
 The measure $\mu$ is *concentrated on* $E$ means $\mu(X \setminus E) = 0$; like
wise $\nu$ concentrated on $F$ means $\nu(X \setminus F) = 0$.

**Proof.** Fix any $\varepsilon > 0$ and let $\sigma = \nu - \varepsilon\mu$. We
 must show that for every measurable $B \subseteq F$,
$$
\sigma(B) = \nu(B) - \varepsilon\,\mu(B) \geq 0.
$$

Let $B \subseteq F$ be measurable. Since $E \cap F = \emptyset$, we have $B \sub
seteq F \subseteq X \setminus E$. Because $\mu$ is concentrated on $E$, i.e. $\m
u(X \setminus E) = 0$, it follows that
$$
\mu(B) \leq \mu(X \setminus E) = 0.
$$
Since $\mu$ is a positive measure, $\mu(B) \geq 0$, so $\mu(B) = 0$.

Therefore
$$
\sigma(B) = \nu(B) - \varepsilon \cdot \mu(B) = \nu(B) - 0 = \nu(B).
$$
As $\nu$ is a positive measure, $\nu(B) \geq 0$ for every measurable $B$. Hence
$\sigma(B) = \nu(B) \geq 0$.

Since $B \subseteq F$ was arbitrary, every measurable subset of $F$ has non-nega
tive $\sigma$-measure. Thus $F$ is a positive set for $\nu - \varepsilon\mu$.

This holds for every $\varepsilon > 0$ (the argument never used the value of $\v
arepsilon$, only its positivity, and the term $\varepsilon\mu(B)$ vanished becau
se $\mu(B)=0$). $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The mutual singularity $\mu \perp \nu$ with the witnessing pair $(E,
 F)$, $E \cap F = \emptyset$, is exactly what makes $\mu$ vanish on all subsets
of $F$. This decouples $F$ from $\mu$ entirely, so the sign of $(\nu - \varepsil
on\mu)$ on subsets of $F$ is governed by $\nu$ alone, which is non-negative.
