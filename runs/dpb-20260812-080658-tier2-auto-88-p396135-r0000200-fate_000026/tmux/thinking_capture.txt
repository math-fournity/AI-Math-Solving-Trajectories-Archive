Proof

Let $D$ be an integral domain that is not a field. We show that $D[x]$ is not a
PID.

Key observation. Since $D$ is not a field, there exists a nonzero element $a \in
D$ that is not a unit (i.e., $a \neq 0$ and $a$ has no multiplicative inverse in
$D$).

Consider the ideal $$I = (a, x) = {a \cdot f(x) + x \cdot g(x) : f(x), g(x) \in
D[x]} \subseteq D[x].$$ This is the ideal generated by $a$ and $x$. We claim
that $I$ is not principal, which will show that $D[x]$ is not a PID.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 1: Characterize the elements of $I$.

Every element of $I$ has the form $a,f(x) + x,g(x)$. Writing $f(x) = f_0 + f_1 x
+ \cdots$ and $g(x) = g_0 + g_1 x + \cdots$, the constant term of $a,f(x) +
x,g(x)$ is $a f_0$, which is a multiple of $a$. Conversely, any polynomial whose
constant term is a multiple of $a$ lies in $I$: if $h(x) = a c + h_1 x + h_2 x^2
+ \cdots$, then $h(x) = a \cdot c + x \cdot (h_1 + h_2 x + \cdots) \in I$.
Therefore

$$I = {h(x) \in D[x] : \text{the constant term of } h \text{ is divisible by }
a}.$$

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 2: Suppose for contradiction that $I$ is principal, say $I = (d(x))$ for
some $d(x) \in D[x]$.

Since $a \in I$ and $x \in I$, both $a$ and $x$ must be multiples of $d(x)$:

  • $a = d(x), p(x)$ for some $p(x) \in D[x]$,
  • $x = d(x), q(x)$ for some $q(x) \in D[x]$.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 3: Analyze $\deg d$.

From $a = d(x),p(x)$: the left side is a nonzero constant (degree $0$), so $\deg
d + \deg p = 0$, giving $\deg d = 0$, i.e., $d(x) = d$ is a constant in $D$, and
$d \neq 0$ (since $I \neq (0)$).

From $x = d \cdot q(x)$: we get $q(x) = d^{-1} x$ as a polynomial identity in
$D[x]$. For $q(x)$ to have coefficients in $D$, the coefficient $d^{-1}$ must
lie in $D$. That is, $d$ must be a unit of $D$.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 4: Derive the contradiction.

If $d$ is a unit, then $(d) = D[x]$, so $I = D[x]$. But $I \neq D[x]$, because
$1 \notin I$: the constant term of $1$ is $1$, which is not divisible by $a$
(since $a$ is a nonzero non-unit in an integral domain, $a \cdot c = 1$ would
make $a$ a unit, contradicting the choice of $a$).

This is a contradiction. Therefore $I$ is not principal.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Conclusion.

We have exhibited an ideal $I = (a, x)$ of $D[x]$ that is not principal. Hence
$D[x]$ is not a principal ideal domain. $\blacksquare$
