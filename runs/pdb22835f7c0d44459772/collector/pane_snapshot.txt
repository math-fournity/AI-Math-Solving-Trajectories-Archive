## Proof

Let $R = k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ and let $I = \bigcap_{j} I_j$ be a (minimal) primary
decomposition, with $\sqrt{I_j} = \mathfrak{p}_j$ prime. The primes $\mathfrak{p
}_j \in \operatorname{Ass}(R/I)$ are the **associated primes** of $I$.

### 1. What the irreducible decomposition captures, and what it misses

The irreducible decomposition of the variety $V(I)$ is
$$V(I) = V(\sqrt{I}) = \bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\,\in\,\operatorname{Min}(I)} V(\mat
hfrak{p}),$$
where $\operatorname{Min}(I) = \operatorname{Min}(\sqrt{I})$ is the set of **min
imal** primes of $I$. This decomposition sees only the *reduced* closed set $V(I
)$; it discards all nilpotent / scheme-theoretic information. Equivalently, it o
nly uses the radical $\sqrt{I} = \bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Min}(I)}
\mathfrak{p}$.

When $I$ is **not** radical ($I \subsetneq \sqrt{I}$), the primary decomposition
 carries strictly more data than the irreducible decomposition, in two respects:

**(a) Embedded associated primes.** The set $\operatorname{Ass}(R/I)$ may be str
ictly larger than $\operatorname{Min}(I)$. Primes in $\operatorname{Ass}(R/I)\se
tminus \operatorname{Min}(I)$ are called **embedded primes**. They are invisible
 to $V(I)$, since $V(\mathfrak{q}) \subseteq V(\mathfrak{p})$ whenever $\mathfra
k{p}\subseteq \mathfrak{q}$, so the embedded component $V(\mathfrak{q})$ is set-
theoretically swallowed by a larger irreducible component $V(\mathfrak{p})$.

**(b) The primary ideals $I_j$ themselves.** Even for a minimal prime $\mathfrak
{p}_j$, the $\mathfrak{p}_j$-primary ideal $I_j$ need not equal $\mathfrak{p}_j$
; the difference $I_j \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_j$ encodes a nilpotent thickening
of $V(\mathfrak{p}_j)$ — multiplicities, infinitesimal neighborhoods, and "fuzz"
 along that irreducible component.

Together, (a) and (b) are exactly the scheme structure of $\operatorname{Spec}(R
/I)$ that the reduced decomposition forgets.

### 2. When a prime $\mathfrak{p}$ can appear with $\mathfrak{p}\subseteq \mathf
rak{q}$ for another associated prime $\mathfrak{q}$

We have two associated primes $\mathfrak{p}, \mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}(R
/I)$ coming from (the radicals of) two distinct primary components, with
$$\mathfrak{p} \subseteq \mathfrak{q}.$$
If the containment is strict, $\mathfrak{p}\subsetneq \mathfrak{q}$, then $\math
frak{q}$ is an **embedded prime** of $I$ (it strictly contains another associate
d prime), and $\mathfrak{p}$ is necessarily a minimal prime of $I$.

This situation occurs **precisely when $I$ is not radical and $\operatorname{Spe
c}(R/I)$ has embedded components** — i.e., when $\operatorname{Ass}(R/I) \supset
neq \operatorname{Min}(I)$. The geometric meaning is:

- $V(\mathfrak{q}) \subsetneq V(\mathfrak{p})$: the subvariety supporting the $\
mathfrak{q}$-primary component is a *proper* closed subset of the irreducible co
mponent $V(\mathfrak{p})$.
- The $\mathfrak{q}$-primary component $I_{\mathfrak{q}}$ records nilpotent stru
cture concentrated along $V(\mathfrak{q})$ *inside* $V(\mathfrak{p})$ — an embed
ded infinitesimal neighborhood that is invisible to the reduced/irreducible deco
mposition.
- Set-theoretically the $\mathfrak{q}$-component is redundant (removing it does
not change $V(I)$), but scheme-theoretically it is essential: dropping $I_{\math
frak{q}}$ from the intersection changes $I$ and changes $\operatorname{Spec}(R/I
)$.

A necessary condition is therefore $I \ne \sqrt{I}$: if $I$ were radical, then $
I = \sqrt{I} = \bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Min}(I)} \mathfrak{p}$ is t
he intersection of its minimal primes, every primary component is prime, and $\o
peratorname{Ass}(R/I) = \operatorname{Min}(I)$, so no strict containment between
 associated primes is possible.

### 3. Concrete example

Take $R = k[x,y]$ and
$$I = (x^2, xy) = (x)\,\cap\,(x^2,\,y).$$
Here $(x)$ is prime ($\mathfrak{p} = (x)$), and $(x^2,y)$ is $(x,y)$-primary ($\
mathfrak{q} = (x,y)$). We have
$$(x) \subsetneq (x,y), \qquad \mathfrak{p}\subsetneq \mathfrak{q}.$$
The variety is $V(I) = V(x)$, the $y$-axis — a single irreducible component, so
the irreducible decomposition sees only a line. The embedded prime $\mathfrak{q}
=(x,y)$ records an embedded nilpotent structure concentrated at the origin $(0,0
)\in V(\mathfrak{p})$: a non-reduced "fuzz" at that point, invisible to $V(I)$ b
ut present in $\operatorname{Spec}(R/I)$.

### 4. Conclusion

The primary decomposition provides, beyond the irreducible decomposition, the **
full scheme structure** of $\operatorname{Spec}(R/I)$: the embedded associated p
rimes together with the primary ideals encoding nilpotent thickenings. A strict
containment $\mathfrak{p}\subsetneq \mathfrak{q}$ between two associated primes
— i.e. an embedded prime $\mathfrak{q}$ sitting over a minimal prime $\mathfrak{
p}$ — occurs exactly when $I$ is non-radical and $\operatorname{Spec}(R/I)$ has
embedded components supported on proper subvarieties $V(\mathfrak{q})\subsetneq
V(\mathfrak{p})$ of irreducible components. This is impossible when $I$ is radic
al, since then $\operatorname{Ass}(R/I)=\operatorname{Min}(I)$ and no associated
 prime strictly contains another.
