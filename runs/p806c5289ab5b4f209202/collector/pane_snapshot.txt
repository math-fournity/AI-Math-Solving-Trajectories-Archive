## Proof

We use the classification theorem for compact connected surfaces (without bounda
ry), and the standard identities for connected sums of non-orientable surfaces.

**Setup and notation.** Let $\mathbb{RP}^2$ denote the real projective plane (a
crosscap). A non-orientable closed surface is classified up to homeomorphism by
its genus $k \geq 1$, i.e. the number of crosscaps in its standard decomposition
, and is denoted $k\mathbb{RP}^2 = \underbrace{\mathbb{RP}^2 \# \cdots \# \mathb
b{RP}^2}_{k}$. Its Euler characteristic is
$$\chi(k\mathbb{RP}^2) = 2 - k.$$

**Key fact: the Klein bottle is $2\mathbb{RP}^2$.** The Klein bottle $K$ is a cl
osed non-orientable surface with $\chi(K) = 0$. Setting $2 - k = 0$ gives $k = 2
$, so by the classification theorem,
$$K \;\cong\; 2\mathbb{RP}^2 \;=\; \mathbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2.$$

(Equivalently, this is the standard polygonal-schema identity: both $K$ and $\ma
thbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2$ have the normal form word $a a b b$, i.e. two cross
caps, confirming they are the same non-orientable surface of genus 2.)

**Compute the two sides.**

*Left side:* Klein bottle $\#$ crosscap:
$$K \# \mathbb{RP}^2 \;\cong\; (\mathbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2) \# \mathbb{RP}^2
 \;\cong\; \mathbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2 \;=\; 3\mathbb{RP}^2,
$$
using associativity of the connected sum (up to homeomorphism).

*Right side:* three crosscaps:
$$\mathbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2 \;=\; 3\mathbb{RP}^2.$$

**Verification via Euler characteristic (independent check).** Using $\chi(M \#
N) = \chi(M) + \chi(N) - 2$:
- $\chi(K \# \mathbb{RP}^2) = \chi(K) + \chi(\mathbb{RP}^2) - 2 = 0 + 1 - 2 = -1
$.
- $\chi(\mathbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2) = 2 - 3 = -1$.

Both are closed, connected, non-orientable surfaces with Euler characteristic $-
1$, i.e. genus $k = 2 - (-1) = 3$. By the classification theorem, they are homeo
morphic.

**Conclusion.** The connected sum of a Klein bottle and a crosscap is homeomorph
ic to the connected sum of three crosscaps:
$$K \# \mathbb{RP}^2 \;\cong\; \mathbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2.$
$

The answer is **yes**, they are homeomorphic (both being the non-orientable clos
ed surface of genus 3).
