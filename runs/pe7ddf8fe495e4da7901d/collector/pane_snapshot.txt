# Proof

**Setup.** Let $X$ be a compact $n$-manifold with boundary $\partial X$ (so $\di
m(X\setminus\partial X)=n$ and $\dim\partial X = n-1$), with both $X$ and $\part
ial X$ aspherical. Set $\Sigma = \partial X$ and define
$$Y = X \cup_{\partial X} C(\partial X),$$
the space obtained by attaching the cone on $\partial X$ to $X$ along $\partial
X$. We are given $H^n(X,\partial X)\cong\mathbb{Z}$ (by Lefschetz duality, so $X
$ is orientable and connected) and must determine $H^n(Y)$.

---

**Step 1: Homotopy type of $Y$.** The cone $C(\partial X)$ is contractible. Coll
apsing the cone to its apex gives a deformation retraction
$$Y = X\cup_{\partial X} C(\partial X) \;\simeq\; X/\partial X.$$
So $H^*(Y)\cong H^*(X,\partial X)$ via the quotient map, but we verify this prec
isely through the long exact sequence below.

---

**Step 2: Excision.** Consider the pair $(Y,\, C(\partial X))$. Excising the con
e point $v$ (the apex of the cone) together with a small open neighborhood in th
e cone yields
$$H^*(Y,\, C(\partial X)) \;\cong\; H^*(X,\,\partial X),$$
since removing the cone interior from $Y$ leaves $X$ with $\partial X$ as the re
maining boundary of the pair.

---

**Step 3: Long exact sequence of the pair $(Y, C(\partial X))$.** Since $C(\part
ial X)$ is contractible,
$$H^k(C(\partial X)) = \begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}, & k=0,\\ 0, & k\geq 1.\end{cases
}$$

The long exact sequence reads, for $k\geq 2$:
$$\cdots \to \underbrace{H^{k-1}(C(\partial X))}_{=\,0} \to H^k(Y, C(\partial X)
) \to H^k(Y) \to \underbrace{H^k(C(\partial X))}_{=\,0} \to \cdots$$

Hence for every $k\geq 2$:
$$H^k(Y) \;\cong\; H^k(Y, C(\partial X)) \;\cong\; H^k(X, \partial X).$$

In particular, for $k = n \geq 2$:
$$\boxed{H^n(Y) \cong H^n(X,\partial X) = \mathbb{Z}.}$$

---

**Step 4: The case $n = 1$.** Here $X$ is a compact connected $1$-manifold with
boundary, so $X\cong[0,1]$ and $\partial X = \{0,1\}$ (two points, aspherical).
The relevant portion of the LES of $(Y, C(\partial X))$ is:
$$H^0(Y) \to H^0(C(\partial X)) \xrightarrow{\;\delta\;} H^1(Y, C(\partial X)) \
to H^1(Y) \to \underbrace{H^1(C(\partial X))}_{=0}.$$

Under the excision isomorphism $H^1(Y,C(\partial X))\cong H^1(X,\partial X)$, th
e connecting map $\delta$ factors as
$$H^0(C(\partial X)) \xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{res}\;} H^0(\partial X) \xrightarrow
{\;\delta'\;} H^1(X,\partial X),$$
where $\mathrm{res}$ sends the generator of $H^0(C(\partial X))\cong\mathbb{Z}$
(the constant function $1$) to $(1,1)\in H^0(\partial X)\cong\mathbb{Z}^2$, and
$\delta'$ is the connecting homomorphism of the pair $(X,\partial X)$.

From the LES of $(X,\partial X)$:
$$H^0(X)\to H^0(\partial X)\xrightarrow{\delta'} H^1(X,\partial X)\to H^1(X)\to\
cdots$$
Since $X\simeq *$, $H^1(X)=0$, so $\delta'$ is surjective with $\ker\delta' = \o
peratorname{im}(H^0(X)\to H^0(\partial X))$. The map $H^0(X)\to H^0(\partial X)$
 sends $1\mapsto(1,1)$, so $\delta'(1,1)=0$. Therefore
$$\delta = \delta'\circ\mathrm{res}(1) = \delta'(1,1) = 0.$$

Thus $H^1(Y,C(\partial X))\hookrightarrow H^1(Y)$ is injective and (since $H^1(C
(\partial X))=0$) surjective, giving
$$H^1(Y)\cong H^1(X,\partial X)=\mathbb{Z}.$$

(Concretely, $Y = [0,1]\cup C(\{0,1\})\cong S^1$, confirming $H^1(Y)=\mathbb{Z}$
.)

---

**Step 5: Role of asphericity.** The asphericity of $X$ and $\partial X$ ensures
 the geometric setting is consistent (e.g., the boundary inclusion $\partial X\h
ookrightarrow X$ is $\pi_1$-injective in many natural cases, and the Lefschetz d
uality hypothesis $H^n(X,\partial X)=\mathbb{Z}$ is compatible with the aspheric
al structure). However, the computation of $H^n(Y)$ via the long exact sequence
of the pair $(Y, C(\partial X))$ together with excision relies only on:
- contractibility of the cone $C(\partial X)$,
- the excision isomorphism $H^*(Y,C(\partial X))\cong H^*(X,\partial X)$,
- the given $H^n(X,\partial X)=\mathbb{Z}$.

These hold for all $n\geq 1$, yielding the same result.

---

**Conclusion.** For every $n\geq 1$,
$$H^n\!\Big(X \cup_{\partial X} C(\partial X)\Big) \;\cong\; H^n(X,\partial X) \
;=\; \mathbb{Z}.$$
