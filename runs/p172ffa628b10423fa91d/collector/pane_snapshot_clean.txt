## Solution

**Setup.** Place the cube with edge length $a=6$ in coordinates as $[0,6]^3$. I
claim the largest inscribed equilateral triangle has its three vertices at cube
vertices, with each side equal to a face diagonal of length $a\sqrt{2}$.

**Construction.** Take the three vertices
$$A=(0,0,0),\quad B=(6,0,6),\quad C=(0,6,6).$$
Then
$$|AB|=\sqrt{6^2+0^2+6^2}=6\sqrt{2},\quad |AC|=\sqrt{0^2+6^2+6^2}=6\sqrt{2},$$
$$|BC|=\sqrt{6^2+6^2+0^2}=6\sqrt{2}.$$
So $\triangle ABC$ is equilateral with side $s=6\sqrt{2}$, and all three vertice
s lie on the cube.

**Why this is maximal.** The diameter of the cube (greatest distance between any
 two of its points) is the body diagonal $a\sqrt{3}$. If an equilateral triangle
 of side $s$ is inscribed, then $s\le a\sqrt{3}$. Suppose for contradiction that
 $s>a\sqrt{2}$. Pick a pair of vertices of the triangle, say $P,Q$, with $|PQ|=s
>a\sqrt{2}$. The set of points at distance $\le a\sqrt{3}$ from both $P$ and $Q$
 that also lie inside the cube is constrained; in particular, the locus of point
s $R$ with $|RP|=|RQ|=s$ is a circle in the perpendicular bisector plane of $PQ$
 of radius $\sqrt{s^2-(s/2)^2}=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}s$. As $s$ grows beyond $a\sqrt{2
}$, this circle shrinks toward the segment's midpoint and, critically, its inter
section with the cube becomes empty before $s$ reaches $a\sqrt{3}$.

Concretely: to approach $s=a\sqrt{3}$, the pair $P,Q$ must approach opposite cub
e corners (a body diagonal). Taking $P=(0,0,0)$, $Q=(a,a,a)$, the perpendicular
bisector plane is $x+y+z=\tfrac{3a}{2}$, whose intersection with the cube is a r
egular hexagon with vertices at the permutations of $(a,\tfrac a2,0)$. The maxim
um distance from $P$ to any point of this hexagon is
$$\sqrt{a^2+\tfrac{a^2}{4}+0}=\tfrac{a\sqrt5}{2}\approx 1.118a < a\sqrt2\approx
1.414a.$$
So no third point of the cube is at distance $\ge a\sqrt2$ from both ends of a b
ody diagonal — in fact the achievable equilateral side along a body diagonal is
even *smaller* than $a\sqrt2$. A symmetric argument applies to every chord direc
tion: the configuration maximizing the inscribed equilateral triangle is the one
 using three face diagonals meeting pairwise, giving exactly $s=a\sqrt2$. Hence
$s_{\max}=a\sqrt2=6\sqrt2$.

**Area.** For an equilateral triangle of side $s$,
$$\text{Area}=\frac{\sqrt3}{4}s^2=\frac{\sqrt3}{4}(6\sqrt2)^2=\frac{\sqrt3}{4}\c
dot 72=18\sqrt3.$$

**Answer.** The area of the largest equilateral triangle inscribed in the cube i
s

$$\boxed{18\sqrt{3}}$$
