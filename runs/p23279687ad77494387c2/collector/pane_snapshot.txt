## Proof

**Claim:** $x^p$ is algebraic over $(K(x))^p$. In fact, $x^p \in (K(x))^p$, so t
he statement holds trivially.

**Notation.** For a field $F$ of characteristic $p > 0$, we write $F^p = \{a^p :
 a \in F\}$ for the image of the Frobenius endomorphism $\varphi: F \to F$, $\va
rphi(a) = a^p$. This is a subfield of $F$ (the Frobenius map is an injective rin
g homomorphism in characteristic $p$).

**Step 1: $x \in K(x)$.**

The element $x$ is, by definition, an element of the rational function field $K(
x)$.

**Step 2: $x^p \in (K(x))^p$.**

Since $(K(x))^p = \{f^p : f \in K(x)\}$ and $x \in K(x)$, we may take $f = x$ to
 obtain
$$x^p = f^p \in (K(x))^p.$$

**Step 3: $x^p$ is algebraic over $(K(x))^p$.**

Any element of a field is algebraic over that field: $x^p \in (K(x))^p$ satisfie
s the linear polynomial
$$T - x^p \in (K(x))^p[T],$$
so $x^p$ is algebraic over $(K(x))^p$ (with degree $1$). $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The more substantive question in this setting is whether $x$ (not $x
^p$) is algebraic over $(K(x))^p$. There the answer is also yes, but non-trivial
ly: $x$ satisfies $T^p - x^p \in (K(x))^p[T]$, so $x$ is algebraic over $(K(x))^
p$ of degree dividing $p$. One can further show $(K(x))^p = K^p(x^p)$, so $[K(x)
:(K(x))^p] = [K:K^p]\cdot p$, and $T^p - x^p$ is irreducible over $K^p(x^p)$, gi
ving $[K^p(x):K^p(x^p)]=p$.
