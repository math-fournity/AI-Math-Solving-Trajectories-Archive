## Answer: **Yes**

**Proof.**

Let $N = mn$ be the number of vertices of $G$. The hypothesis is that every vert
ex has degree strictly greater than $m(n-1)$.

**Step 1: Counting edges.** By the handshaking lemma,
$$
2e(G) = \sum_{v \in V(G)} \deg(v) > mn \cdot m(n-1) = m^{2}n(n-1),
$$
so
$$
e(G) > \frac{m^{2}n(n-1)}{2}. \tag{1}
$$

**Step 2: The Turán bound.** Turán's theorem states that the maximum number of e
dges in a graph on $N$ vertices containing no $K_{r+1}$ is attained by the Turán
 graph $T(N, r)$, the complete $r$-partite graph with part sizes as equal as pos
sible. Setting $N = mn$ and $r = n$, the parts are all equal (each of size $m$),
 so
$$
\operatorname{ex}(mn,\, K_{n+1}) = e\bigl(T(mn,\, n)\bigr).
$$
The Turán graph $T(mn, n)$ is the complete $n$-partite graph with $n$ parts of s
ize $m$. Its edges are exactly the pairs lying in distinct parts, so
$$
e\bigl(T(mn,\, n)\bigr) = \binom{n}{2} \cdot m^{2} = \frac{n(n-1)}{2}\,m^{2} = \
frac{m^{2}n(n-1)}{2}. \tag{2}
$$

**Step 3: Conclusion.** Combining (1) and (2),
$$
e(G) > \frac{m^{2}n(n-1)}{2} = \operatorname{ex}(mn,\, K_{n+1}).
$$
Since $G$ has strictly more edges than the Turán number $\operatorname{ex}(mn, K
_{n+1})$, Turán's theorem forces $G$ to contain a $K_{n+1}$ subgraph.

**Tightness remark.** The bound is sharp: the Turán graph $T(mn, n)$ itself has
$mn$ vertices, minimum degree exactly $m(n-1)$ (each vertex is adjacent to all $
mn - m = m(n-1)$ vertices outside its own part), and contains no $K_{n+1}$. Thus
 the strict inequality $\deg(v) > m(n-1)$ is exactly what is needed. $\blacksqua
