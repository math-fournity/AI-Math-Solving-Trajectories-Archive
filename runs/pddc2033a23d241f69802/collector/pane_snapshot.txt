## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** $E(\phi_m\mid\mathcal{B}(\xi)) \to E(\phi\mid\mathcal{B}(\xi))$
 pointwise (a.e.).

In fact the result holds for *any* sub-$\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{G}\subseteq\ma
thcal{B}(X)$; the special structure of $\xi$ and the approximating algebras $\ma
thcal{B}_n$ are not needed. We prove the general statement, which immediately sp
ecializes to $\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{B}(\xi)$.

Let $\mu$ be the underlying probability measure on $X$ (conditional expectation
is taken with respect to $\mu$). Set $g_m:=\phi_m-\phi$. Then

$$g_m\to 0 \quad\text{a.e.,}\qquad |g_m|\le 2M \;\text{a.e.}$$

for some constant $M$ (uniform boundedness of $\phi_m$ plus pointwise convergenc
e giving $|\phi|\le M$ a.e.). We must show $E(g_m\mid\mathcal{G})\to 0$ a.e.

**Step 1 (monotone envelope).** Define
$$h_n:=\sup_{k\ge n}|g_k|.$$
Each $h_n$ is measurable (supremum of countably many measurable functions), $0\l
e h_n\le 2M$, and since $g_k\to 0$ a.e., for a.e. $\omega$ the tail supremum sat
isfies $h_n(\omega)\downarrow 0$. Thus
$$h_n\downarrow 0 \quad\text{a.e.},\qquad h_n\le 2M.$$

**Step 2 (conditional expectations form a decreasing sequence).** Since $h_n\le
h_{n-1}$ a.e. and conditional expectation preserves order a.e.,
$$E(h_n\mid\mathcal{G})\le E(h_{n-1}\mid\mathcal{G})\quad\text{a.e.}$$
After modifying on a null set, $\{E(h_n\mid\mathcal{G})\}_{n\ge 1}$ is a decreas
ing sequence of non-negative, $\mathcal{G}$-measurable functions bounded by $2M$
. Hence it converges a.e. to some $\mathcal{G}$-measurable limit $Z\ge 0$:
$$E(h_n\mid\mathcal{G})\downarrow Z\ge 0\quad\text{a.e.}$$

**Step 3 (the limit is zero).** By the tower property / definition of conditiona
l expectation,
$$\int_X E(h_n\mid\mathcal{G})\,d\mu=\int_X h_n\,d\mu.$$
Since $h_n\to 0$ a.e. and $|h_n|\le 2M\in L^1$, the (ordinary) Dominated Converg
ence Theorem gives $\int h_n\,d\mu\to 0$, hence
$$\int_X E(h_n\mid\mathcal{G})\,d\mu\;\longrightarrow\;0.$$
On the other hand, $E(h_n\mid\mathcal{G})$ is decreasing and bounded by $2M$, so
 applying MCT to the increasing sequence $2M-E(h_n\mid\mathcal{G})\uparrow 2M-Z$
 yields
$$\int_X E(h_n\mid\mathcal{G})\,d\mu\;\longrightarrow\;\int_X Z\,d\mu.$$
Comparing the two limits: $\int_X Z\,d\mu=0$. Since $Z\ge 0$ and $\mathcal{G}$-m
easurable, $Z=0$ a.e. Therefore
$$E(h_n\mid\mathcal{G})\downarrow 0\quad\text{a.e.}$$

**Step 4 (sandwich).** For every $m\ge n$ we have $|g_m|\le h_n$, so by the cont
raction property of conditional expectation,
$$\bigl|E(g_m\mid\mathcal{G})\bigr|\le E(|g_m|\mid\mathcal{G})\le E(h_n\mid\math
cal{G}).$$
Given $\varepsilon>0$, since $E(h_n\mid\mathcal{G})\to 0$ a.e., for a.e. $\omega
$ there exists $N(\omega)$ with $E(h_{N(\omega)}\mid\mathcal{G})(\omega)<\vareps
ilon$. Then for all $m\ge N(\omega)$,
$$\bigl|E(g_m\mid\mathcal{G})(\omega)\bigr|<\varepsilon.$$
Thus $E(g_m\mid\mathcal{G})\to 0$ a.e., i.e.
$$E(\phi_m\mid\mathcal{G})\to E(\phi\mid\mathcal{G})\quad\text{a.e.}$$

**Conclusion.** Taking $\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{B}(\xi)=\sigma(E_1,E_2,\dots)=\bigv
ee_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}_n$, we obtain
$$\boxed{\,E(\phi_m\mid\mathcal{B}(\xi))\to E(\phi\mid\mathcal{B}(\xi))\quad\tex
t{pointwise (a.e.)}\,}.$$

**Remarks.**
1. The hypothesis of *uniform* boundedness is essential: it provides the dominat
ing function $2M\in L^1$ that drives both the ordinary DCT in Step 3 and the bou
ndedness needed for the decreasing MCT. Mere pointwise convergence without a uni
form $L^1$ bound would not suffice for a.e. convergence of the conditional expec
tations (only $L^1$ convergence would survive).
2. The finite approximating algebras $\mathcal{B}_n$ and the martingale $E(\cdot
\mid\mathcal{B}_n)$ are not required for this conclusion; the argument works dir
ectly for the full $\mathcal{B}(\xi)$. (They would be relevant if one instead as
ked about the *double* limit $E(\phi_m\mid\mathcal{B}_n)$ as $m,n\to\infty$, whe
re Doob's martingale convergence and the above result would be combined.)
3. "Pointwise" is necessarily understood a.e., since conditional expectations ar
e defined only up to $\mu$-null sets.
