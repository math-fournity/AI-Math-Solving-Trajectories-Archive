**Setting up the triangle.** The sides 5, 12, 13 satisfy $5^2+12^2=13^2$, so thi
s is a right triangle with the right angle between the legs of length 5 and 12.

**Locating the incenter.** Place the right angle at the origin $A=(0,0)$, with $
B=(12,0)$ and $C=(0,5)$. The inradius of a right triangle is
$$r_{\text{in}} = \frac{a+b-c}{2} = \frac{5+12-13}{2}=2.$$
Using the barycentric formula $I = \frac{aA+bB+cC}{a+b+c}$ with $a=13,\,b=5,\,c=
12$:
$$I = \frac{13(0,0)+5(12,0)+12(0,5)}{30} = \frac{(60,60)}{30}=(2,2).$$
The distance from $I=(2,2)$ to each side is indeed $2$ (to the $x$-axis, to the
$y$-axis, and to the hypotenuse $5x+12y=60$, where $\frac{|10+24-60|}{13}=2$). T
his confirms $I$ is equidistant from all three sides at distance $d=2$.

**Chord angle geometry.** The circle $\omega$ of radius $r$ centered at $I$ inte
rsects each side in a chord. For a line at distance $d$ from the center, the ang
le subtended by the chord at the center is
$$\theta = 2\arccos\!\left(\frac{d}{r}\right).$$
Since $I$ is the incenter, $d=2$ for **all three sides**, so each of the three s
ector angles equals
$$\theta = 2\arccos\!\left(\frac{2}{r}\right).$$

**Applying the calibration condition.** The sum of the three angles is $180°=\pi
$:
$$3\cdot 2\arccos\!\left(\frac{2}{r}\right) = \pi \implies \arccos\!\left(\frac{
2}{r}\right) = \frac{\pi}{6}.$$
Therefore
$$\frac{2}{r} = \cos\frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \implies r = \frac{4}{\sq
rt{3}} = \frac{4\sqrt{3}}{3}.$$

**Computing the area.**
$$\text{Area} = \pi r^2 = \pi\cdot\frac{16}{3}.$$

Dividing by $\pi$:
$$\boxed{\dfrac{16}{3}}.$$
