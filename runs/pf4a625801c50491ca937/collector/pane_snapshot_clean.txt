## Laplace–Beltrami operator for a conformally Euclidean metric

**Setup.** Let $U \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ be open and let $g$ be the metric with c
omponents
$$g_{ij} = \lambda^2\,\delta_{ij}, \qquad \lambda \in C^\infty(U),\ \lambda > 0.
$$
We want the Laplace–Beltrami operator $\Delta_g u$ acting on a smooth function $
u$.

**General formula.** In any coordinates, the Laplace–Beltrami operator is
$$\Delta_g u \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{\det g}}\,\partial_i\!\left(\sqrt{\det g}\; g^
{ij}\,\partial_j u\right),$$
with the Einstein summation convention.

**Compute the metric data.** Since $g_{ij}=\lambda^2\delta_{ij}$, the inverse me
tric is
$$g^{ij} = \lambda^{-2}\,\delta^{ij},$$
and the determinant is
$$\det g = \lambda^{2n}, \qquad \sqrt{\det g} = \lambda^{n}.$$

**Substitute.** Plugging these in:
$$\Delta_g u \;=\; \frac{1}{\lambda^{n}}\,\partial_i\!\left(\lambda^{n}\,\lambda
^{-2}\,\delta^{ij}\,\partial_j u\right) \;=\; \frac{1}{\lambda^{n}}\,\partial_i\
!\left(\lambda^{n-2}\,\partial_i u\right),$$
where in the last step we used $\delta^{ij}\partial_j u = \partial_i u$ (Euclide
an identification).

**Expand the product.** Applying the product rule to $\partial_i(\lambda^{n-2}\p
artial_i u)$:
$$\partial_i(\lambda^{n-2}\partial_i u) \;=\; \partial_i(\lambda^{n-2})\,\partia
l_i u \;+\; \lambda^{n-2}\,\partial_i^2 u.$$
Since $\partial_i(\lambda^{n-2}) = (n-2)\lambda^{n-3}\,\partial_i\lambda$,
$$\partial_i(\lambda^{n-2}\partial_i u) \;=\; (n-2)\lambda^{n-3}(\partial_i\lamb
da)(\partial_i u) \;+\; \lambda^{n-2}\,\partial_i^2 u.$$

**Assemble.** Dividing through by $\lambda^{n}$:
$$\Delta_g u \;=\; (n-2)\lambda^{n-3-n}(\partial_i\lambda)(\partial_i u) \;+\; \
lambda^{n-2-n}\,\partial_i^2 u.$$
Using $\lambda^{n-3-n}=\lambda^{-3}$ and $\lambda^{n-2-n}=\lambda^{-2}$, and sum
ming over $i$ (Euclidean inner products):
$$\boxed{\;\Delta_g u \;=\; \frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\,\Delta_{\mathrm{euc}}\,u \;+\
; \frac{n-2}{\lambda^{3}}\,\langle \nabla \lambda,\,\nabla u\rangle\;}$$
where $\Delta_{\mathrm{euc}} u = \sum_i \partial_i^2 u$ is the ordinary Euclidea
n Laplacian and $\langle\nabla\lambda,\nabla u\rangle = \sum_i (\partial_i\lambd
a)(\partial_i u)$ is the Euclidean inner product of the gradients.

**Equivalent form.** Writing $\lambda = e^{\varphi}$ (so $\varphi = \log\lambda$
), we have $\nabla\lambda = \lambda\nabla\varphi$, and the formula becomes
$$\Delta_g u \;=\; \lambda^{-2}\!\left(\Delta_{\mathrm{euc}}\,u \;+\; (n-2)\,\la
ngle\nabla\varphi,\nabla u\rangle\right).$$

**Sanity checks.**
- For $n=2$ the second term vanishes and $\Delta_g u = \lambda^{-2}\Delta_{\math
rm{euc}}u$, the well-known conformal invariance of the Laplacian in two dimensio
ns.
- For $\lambda \equiv 1$ (flat metric) we recover $\Delta_g u = \Delta_{\mathrm{
euc}}u$.
- For $\lambda \equiv \text{const} = c$, $\nabla\lambda = 0$ and $\Delta_g u = c
^{-2}\Delta_{\mathrm{euc}}u$, consistent with scaling lengths by $c$.
