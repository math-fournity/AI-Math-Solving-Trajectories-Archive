## Proof

We model the cake as a convex body (a large cube) in 3-dimensional space. Each s
traight cut is a plane slicing through the cake. Since the pieces cannot be rear
ranged between cuts, every cut is a single plane applied to the current configur
ation, and the number of pieces equals the number of regions into which the cake
 is divided by the collection of cutting planes.

**Upper bound on pieces from $n$ cuts.** The maximum number of regions into whic
h $n$ planes can divide 3-space is given by the well-known formula

$$R(n) = \binom{n}{0} + \binom{n}{1} + \binom{n}{2} + \binom{n}{3} = \frac{n^3 +
 5n + 6}{6}.$$

This is achieved when the planes are in *general position*: no two planes are pa
rallel, no three planes share a line, and no four planes pass through a common p
oint. For a sufficiently large cube, we can always choose $n$ planes in general
position that all intersect the cube, so the cube is cut into exactly $R(n)$ pie
ces (the boundary of the cube does not reduce the count, since every region form
ed by the planes in general position meets the interior of a large enough cube).

**Evaluating $R(n)$ around 100:**

- $R(8) = \dfrac{512 + 40 + 6}{6} = \dfrac{558}{6} = 93 < 100.$
- $R(9) = \dfrac{729 + 45 + 6}{6} = \dfrac{780}{6} = 130 \ge 100.$

**Lower bound: 8 cuts are insufficient.** With at most 8 planes, the number of r
egions is at most $R(8) = 93 < 100$, regardless of how the cuts are arranged. He
nce 8 cuts cannot produce 100 pieces.

**Upper bound: 9 cuts suffice.** Choose 9 planes in general position, each inter
secting the cube. They divide space into $R(9) = 130$ regions, all of which meet
 a sufficiently large cube, yielding 130 pieces. Since $130 \ge 100$, we can cer
tainly obtain at least 100 pieces (and in fact up to 130); obtaining *exactly* 1
00 is achievable by tilting one plane slightly so that it grazes only a subset o
f the existing pieces, reducing the count from 130 down to any desired value bet
ween 93 and 130, in particular 100.

**Conclusion.** The minimum number of straight cuts needed is

$$\boxed{9}.$$
