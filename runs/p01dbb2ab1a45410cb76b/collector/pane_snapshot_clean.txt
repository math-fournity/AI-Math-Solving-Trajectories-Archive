Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum number of points in the plan
e (not all collinear) such that no three form an obtuse triangle.

## Construction: 5 points work

Take a square $ABCD$ with center $P$. I verify all $\binom{5}{3}=10$ triples are
 non-obtuse:

- **Three vertices of the square**: any three corners form a right triangle (rig
ht angle at one corner). ✓
- **$P$ with two adjacent vertices** (e.g., $A, P, B$): Since $P$ is the center,
 $\angle APB = 90°$ (the center subtends each side at $90°$). Right triangle. ✓
(Similarly for $BPC, CPD, DPA$.)
- **$P$ with two opposite vertices** (e.g., $A, P, C$): $P$ lies on diagonal $AC
$, so these three are collinear — degenerate, not obtuse. ✓ (Similarly for $B, P
, D$.)

So **5 is achievable**.

## Upper bound: at most 5

Let $S$ be such a set. Consider its convex hull.

**Case 1: Hull has $\geq 5$ vertices.** The interior angles of a convex $n$-gon
($n \geq 5$) sum to $(n-2)\cdot 180°$, with average $\geq 108° > 90°$. So some i
nterior angle exceeds $90°$, and three consecutive hull vertices at that corner
form an obtuse triangle. ✗

**Case 2: Hull has 4 vertices** $A,B,C,D$. Each interior angle $\leq 90°$ (else
three consecutive vertices give an obtuse triangle). Since the sum is $360°$, al
l angles equal $90°$: the hull is a **rectangle**. Any interior point $P$ satisf
ies
$$\angle APB + \angle BPC + \angle CPD + \angle DPA = 360°,$$
with each $\leq 90°$, forcing each $= 90°$. The conditions $\angle APB = 90°$ an
d $\angle BPC = 90°$ place $P$ on the circles with diameters $AB$ and $BC$; thei
r unique intersection (other than $B$) is the center of the rectangle. So **at m
ost one interior point**, giving $\leq 5$.

**Case 3: Hull has 3 vertices** $A,B,C$ (a non-obtuse triangle). Any *strictly i
nterior* point $P$ has $\angle APB + \angle BPC + \angle CPA = 360°$, so some an
gle $> 90°$: obtuse. ✗ Thus all points lie on the hull edges.

On edge $BC$, a point $Q$ strictly between $B$ and $C$: if $Q$ lies between $B$
and $H_A$ (the foot of the altitude from $A$), then in triangle $ABQ$ the angle
at $Q$ is $180° - \alpha > 90°$ (obtuse); if $Q$ lies between $H_A$ and $C$, the
n triangle $ACQ$ is obtuse at $Q$. Only $Q = H_A$ works (giving right triangles
$ABH_A$ and $ACH_A$). Similarly, each edge admits at most its altitude foot.

**Two altitude feet cannot coexist.** If $H_A$ (on $BC$) and $H_B$ (on $CA$) are
 both present, then $A, B, H_A, H_B$ are concyclic on the circle with diameter $
AB$ (since $\angle AH_AB = \angle AH_BB = 90°$). The points $H_B$ and $B$ lie on
 opposite sides of chord $AH_A$ (the altitude from $A$ separates $B$ from $C$, a
nd $H_B \in CA$ is on the $C$-side), so by the inscribed angle theorem:
$$\angle AH_BH_A = 180° - \angle ABH_A = 180° - B > 90°$$
(since $B < 90°$ in a non-obtuse, non-degenerate triangle). Thus triangle $AH_AH
_B$ is obtuse. ✗ The same argument applies to any pair of feet. So **at most one
 altitude foot** can be added, giving $\leq 4$.

**Case 4: Hull has $\leq 2$ vertices.** All points are collinear, which is forbi
dden.

## Conclusion

Combining all cases: $|S| \leq 5$, and the construction achieves 5. The largest
number is $\boxed{5}$.
