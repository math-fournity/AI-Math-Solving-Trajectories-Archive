## Proof

**Key fact.** A projective variety is irreducible. The continuous image of an ir
reducible set is irreducible. An irreducible topological space cannot be express
ed as a union of two proper closed subsets.

**Setup.** Let $\sigma:\mathbb{P}^m\times\mathbb{P}^n\to\mathbb{P}^N$ be the Seg
re embedding. We are given $\sigma(U\times V)=E_1\cup E_2$ with $E_1,E_2$ closed
 in $\mathbb{P}^N$, and
$$V_i=\{v\in V\mid \sigma(U\times\{v\})\nsubseteq E_i\},\qquad i=1,2.$$

**Claim.** $V_1\cap V_2=\varnothing$.

**Proof.** Fix any $v\in V$. Since $U$ is a projective variety, it is irreducibl
e. The fiber $U\times\{v\}\cong U$ is therefore irreducible, and since $\sigma$
is continuous (in fact a closed embedding), the image
$$\sigma(U\times\{v\})$$
is an irreducible closed subset of $\mathbb{P}^N$.

Because $\sigma(U\times\{v\})\subseteq\sigma(U\times V)=E_1\cup E_2$, we may wri
te
$$\sigma(U\times\{v\})=\bigl(\sigma(U\times\{v\})\cap E_1\bigr)\;\cup\;\bigl(\si
gma(U\times\{v\})\cap E_2\bigr).$$
Both pieces on the right are closed in $\sigma(U\times\{v\})$ (as intersections
with closed sets of $\mathbb{P}^N$). By irreducibility, one of them must equal t
he whole space:

$$\sigma(U\times\{v\})\subseteq E_1 \quad\text{or}\quad \sigma(U\times\{v\})\sub
seteq E_2.$$

Now reason by cases:

- If $\sigma(U\times\{v\})\subseteq E_1$, then $v\notin V_1$ (by definition of $
V_1$).
- If $\sigma(U\times\{v\})\subseteq E_2$, then $v\notin V_2$ (by definition of $
V_2$).

In either case, $v$ fails to belong to at least one of $V_1,V_2$. Since $v\in V$
 was arbitrary, no point of $V$ lies in both $V_1$ and $V_2$. Therefore

$$V_1\cap V_2=\varnothing. \qquad\blacksquare$$

**Remark on the mechanism.** The argument hinges entirely on the irreducibility
of $\sigma(U\times\{v\})\cong U$. An irreducible space admits no non-trivial dec
omposition into two closed pieces, so the covering $\sigma(U\times\{v\})\subsete
q E_1\cup E_2$ forces the entire fiber into one of the two summands. This is pre
cisely the step used in the standard proof that the Segre image of a product of
projective varieties is again a projective variety (irreducible): the sets $V_1$
 and $V_2$ are the "bad" loci where the fiber escapes one summand, and their dis
jointness prevents $V$ from being split into two proper closed pieces, preservin
g irreducibility of $\sigma(U\times V)$.
