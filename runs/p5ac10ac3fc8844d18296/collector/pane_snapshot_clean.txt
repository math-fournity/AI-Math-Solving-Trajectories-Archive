**Problem.** Determine the minimal integer $n$ such that any finite abstract gra
ph $(V,E,\varphi)$ can be realized as a topological graph in $\mathbb{R}^n$.

**Answer: $n = 3$.**

---

## Proof

We must show two things: (i) $n=2$ does **not** suffice (some finite graph canno
t be realized in $\mathbb{R}^2$), and (ii) $n=3$ **does** suffice (every finite
graph can be realized in $\mathbb{R}^3$).

### (i) $n=2$ is insufficient

A realization of a graph as a topological graph in $\mathbb{R}^2$ is precisely a
 planar embedding. By **Kuratowski's theorem**, a graph is planar if and only if
 it contains no subdivision of $K_5$ or $K_{3,3}$. The complete graph $K_5$ is t
herefore not planar, hence cannot be realized as a topological graph in $\mathbb
{R}^2$. Since the problem requires *every* finite graph to be realizable, $n=2$
fails.

Clearly $n=1$ also fails (a 1-dimensional arc cannot even host $K_{1,3}$ without
 overlaps), so $n\le 2$ is ruled out.

### (ii) $n=3$ is sufficient

Let $G=(V,E,\varphi)$ be any finite abstract graph with $|V|=m$ and $|E|=k$. We
construct an explicit embedding in $\mathbb{R}^3$.

**Placing the vertices.** Identify the $z$-axis $L=\{(0,0,z):z\in\mathbb{R}\}$ a
nd place the $m$ vertices at distinct points on $L$:
$$v_i \mapsto p_i = (0,0,z_i), \qquad z_1 < z_2 < \cdots < z_m.$$

**Assigning each edge a private half-plane.** For each edge $e\in E$, assign a d
istinct angle $\theta_e \in [0,2\pi)$, possible since $E$ is finite. Let $H_e$ b
e the closed half-plane through $L$ making angle $\theta_e$ with the $xz$-plane:
$$H_e = \{(r\cos\theta_e,\, r\sin\theta_e,\, z) : r\ge 0,\; z\in\mathbb{R}\}.$$
The key geometric fact is:

> **Claim.** For distinct angles $\theta_e\ne\theta_f$, the half-planes $H_e$ an
d $H_f$ intersect exactly in $L$: $H_e\cap H_f = L$.

*Proof of claim.* A point in $H_e$ has coordinates $(r\cos\theta_e, r\sin\theta_
e, z)$ with $r\ge 0$; a point in $H_f$ has $(r'\cos\theta_f, r'\sin\theta_f, z')
$. Equality forces $r\cos\theta_e = r'\cos\theta_f$ and $r\sin\theta_e = r'\sin\
theta_f$. Since $\theta_e\ne\theta_f$ (mod $\pi$ for the half-plane identificati
on; using distinct angles in $[0,\pi)$ for half-planes, or simply distinct angle
s in $[0,2\pi)$ for the full radial direction), the only solution with $r,r'\ge
0$ is $r=r'=0$, giving a point on $L$. $\square$

(To be fully precise about half-planes: a half-plane through $L$ is determined b
y an angle modulo $\pi$, so we pick the $k$ angles $\theta_e$ distinct in $[0,\p
i)$. The intersection of two such distinct half-planes is exactly $L$.)

**Drawing each edge.** For an edge $e$ with endpoints $p_i=(0,0,z_i)$ and $p_j=(
0,0,z_j)$, $z_i<z_j$, draw within $H_e$ a simple arc $\gamma_e:[0,1]\to H_e$ wit
h $\gamma_e(0)=p_i$, $\gamma_e(1)=p_j$, and such that $\gamma_e((0,1))\subset H_
e\setminus L$. Concretely, for instance:
$$\gamma_e(t) = (\rho(t)\cos\theta_e,\; \rho(t)\sin\theta_e,\; (1-t)z_i + t z_j)
, \qquad t\in[0,1],$$
where $\rho:[0,1]\to[0,\infty)$ is any continuous function with $\rho(0)=\rho(1)
=0$ and $\rho(t)>0$ for $t\in(0,1)$ (e.g. $\rho(t)=\sin(\pi t)$). This is a simp
le arc (a "bump" going out from $L$ and returning) lying entirely in $H_e$, touc
hing $L$ only at its endpoints.

**Verification of the embedding.** We check the defining properties of a topolog
ical graph realization:

1. **Vertices are distinct points:** the $p_i$ are distinct by construction.

2. **Each edge is an arc joining its endpoints:** $\gamma_e$ is a homeomorphic i
mage of $[0,1]$ from $p_i$ to $p_j$.

3. **Edges are disjoint except at shared endpoints:** Take two distinct edges $e
,f$.
   - If $\theta_e\ne\theta_f$: by the Claim, $H_e\cap H_f = L$. Now $\gamma_e((0
,1))\subset H_e\setminus L$ and $\gamma_f((0,1))\subset H_f\setminus L$, which a
re disjoint (they lie in different half-planes off $L$). The only possible inter
section is on $L$, i.e. at endpoints. So $\gamma_e([0,1])\cap\gamma_f([0,1])\sub
seteq\{p_i,p_j\}\cap\{p_{i'},p_{j'}\}$, which is nonempty exactly when $e$ and $
f$ share a vertex — and in that case the intersection is precisely that common v
ertex.
   - (Each edge has a unique angle, so this covers all pairs of distinct edges.)

Thus the map $v_i\mapsto p_i$, $e\mapsto \gamma_e$ is a faithful realization of
$G$ as a topological graph in $\mathbb{R}^3$.

### Conclusion

Since $n=2$ fails (witness $K_5$) and $n=3$ succeeds (the half-plane constructio
n above works for every finite graph), the minimal integer is

$$\boxed{n=3}.$$
