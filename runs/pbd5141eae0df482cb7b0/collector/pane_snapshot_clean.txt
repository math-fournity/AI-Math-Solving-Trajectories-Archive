Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the natural (isometric) act
ion of any discrete subgroup G of E(n) on R^n is always proper.

**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

We show that for any discrete subgroup G of E(n), the standard action of G on R^
n (by Euclidean isometries, via the inclusion G ↪ E(n) = Isom(R^n)) is proper. T
he argument proceeds in three steps.

---

**Step 1: E(n) acts properly on R^n.**

Recall E(n) = O(n) ⋉ R^n, with an element (A, t) acting by x ↦ Ax + t. We use th
e characterization: an action is proper iff for every compact K ⊆ R^n, the set S
_K = {g ∈ E(n) : gK ∩ K ≠ ∅} is compact.

Let K ⊆ R^n be compact. Then K ⊆ B̄(0, R) for some R > 0. If (A, t) ∈ S_K, there
exist x, y ∈ K with Ax + t = y, so t = y − Ax and ‖t‖ ≤ ‖y‖ + ‖Ax‖ = ‖y‖ + ‖x‖ ≤
 2R. Thus:

S_K ⊆ O(n) × B̄(0, 2R),

which is compact (O(n) is compact; B̄(0, 2R) is compact).

To see S_K is closed: consider the set C = {((A,t), x) ∈ E(n) × K : Ax + t ∈ K}.
 This is closed (it is the preimage of K under the continuous map ((A,t), x) ↦ A
x + t). The projection π: E(n) × K → E(n) is a closed map because K is compact (
by the closed map lemma). Hence S_K = π(C) is closed.

Being a closed subset of the compact set O(n) × B̄(0, 2R), S_K is compact. ✓

---

**Step 2: A discrete subgroup of a Hausdorff topological group is closed.**

Let H be a Hausdorff topological group and G ≤ H a discrete subgroup. Since G is
 discrete, there exists an open neighborhood U of e in H with U ∩ G = {e}.

Suppose for contradiction that G is not closed. Then there exists x ∈ cl(G) \ G
and a net (g_α) in G with g_α → x. By continuity of (h₁, h₂) ↦ h₁⁻¹h₂, there exi
sts an index α₀ such that for all α, β ≥ α₀, we have g_α⁻¹g_β ∈ U. But g_α⁻¹g_β
∈ G, so g_α⁻¹g_β ∈ U ∩ G = {e}, meaning g_α = g_β for all α, β ≥ α₀. The net is
eventually constant, say g_α = g for all α ≥ α₀, so x = lim g_α = g ∈ G — contra
diction. Hence G is closed in H. ✓

---

**Step 3: A closed subgroup of a group acting properly also acts properly.**

Let H act properly on X, and let G ≤ H be a closed subgroup. For any compact K ⊆
 X:

{g ∈ G : gK ∩ K ≠ ∅} = G ∩ {h ∈ H : hK ∩ K ≠ ∅}.

The set {h ∈ H : hK ∩ K ≠ ∅} is compact (H acts properly), and G is closed, so t
he intersection is a closed subset of a compact set, hence compact. ✓

---

**Conclusion.**

Let G be any discrete subgroup of E(n). By Step 2, G is closed in E(n). By Step
1, E(n) acts properly on R^n. By Step 3, the restricted action of G on R^n is pr
oper: for every compact K ⊆ R^n, the set {g ∈ G : gK ∩ K ≠ ∅} is compact. Since
G is discrete, this compact set is finite. Therefore the action of G on R^n is p
roper. ∎
