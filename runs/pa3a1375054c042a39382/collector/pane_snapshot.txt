## Proof

Let $R = k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ and let $I \subset R$ be a homogeneous ideal. We c
onsider the graded minimal free resolution of $R/I$ as a graded $R$-module:

$$\cdots \to R_1 \to R_0 \to R/I \to 0.$$

We address two cases.

**Case 1: $I$ is a proper ideal ($I \neq R$).** Then $R/I \neq 0$. The minimal f
ree resolution is constructed so that $R_0$ is a free $R$-module whose rank equa
ls the minimal number of homogeneous generators of $R/I$. Since $R/I$ is a cycli
c $R$-module (generated by the class of $1$), it is generated by a single elemen
t. Moreover, this single generator is minimal: $R/I$ cannot be generated by zero
 elements since it is nonzero. Hence the minimal number of generators is $\mu(R/
I) = 1$, so

$$R_0 \cong R(-a)$$

for some shift $a \in \mathbb{Z}$. Since $I$ is homogeneous and $R/I$ is generat
ed in degree $0$ by the element $\overline{1} \in (R/I)_0$ (the class of $1$, wh
ich has degree $0$ and is nonzero because $I$ is proper and homogeneous, so $I \
subseteq \mathfrak{m} = (x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ forces $1 \notin I$), the generator si
ts in degree $0$. Therefore $a = 0$ and

$$R_0 = R.$$

The augmentation $R_0 = R \to R/I$ is the natural quotient map, which is indeed
surjective and degree-preserving.

**Case 2: $I = R$.** Then $R/I = 0$. The minimal free resolution of the zero mod
ule is the zero complex, so $R_0 = 0 \neq R$.

**Conclusion.** For every *proper* homogeneous ideal $I \subsetneq R$, the zerot
h term of the graded minimal free resolution of $R/I$ is $R_0 = R$. The only exc
eption is the degenerate case $I = R$, where $R/I = 0$ and $R_0 = 0$. In particu
lar, since a homogeneous ideal $I$ is proper if and only if $I \subseteq (x_1, \
ldots, x_n)$ (because homogeneity forces $1 \in I \Rightarrow I = R$), the stand
ard setting where one studies $R/I$ as a nontrivial quotient always has $R_0 = R
$.

Thus, the answer is **yes**, $R_0 = R$, for every nontrivial (proper) homogeneou
s ideal $I$; the only failure is the vacuous case $I = R$.
