## Proof

**Claim.** There exists a strict subalgebra of the hyperfinite $II_1$ factor $R$
 that is both separable and a type $II_1$ factor.

**Construction via the UHF model.**

Realize $R$ as the weak operator closure of the UHF $C^*$-algebra
$$A = \bigotimes_{k=1}^{\infty} M_2(\mathbb{C})$$
in the GNS representation associated with the unique normalized trace $\tau$ on
$A$. The GNS Hilbert space $L^2(A,\tau)$ is separable (since $A$ is separable),
so $R$ acts on a separable Hilbert space.

Consider the unital $*$-subalgebra
$$B = \mathbb{C}\cdot 1 \;\otimes\; \bigotimes_{k=2}^{\infty} M_2(\mathbb{C}) \;
\subset\; A.$$
By reindexing, $B \cong \bigotimes_{k=1}^{\infty} M_2(\mathbb{C}) = A$ as a $C^*
$-algebra; in particular $B$ is a UHF algebra, hence a factor, with unique norma
lized trace.

Let $N = \overline{B}^{\,\mathrm{WOT}} \subseteq R$ be the weak operator closure
 of $B$ inside $R$. We verify the required properties:

1. **$N$ is a von Neumann subalgebra of $R$.** It is a weakly closed, unital $*$
-subalgebra.

2. **$N$ is strict: $N \subsetneq R$.** The element $e \otimes 1 \otimes 1 \otim
es \cdots$ with $e \in M_2$ non-scalar belongs to $A \subset R$ but not to $B$,
hence not to $N = \overline{B}^{\mathrm{WOT}}$ (since $N$ is the weak closure of
 $B$, and $B$ sits in the commutant of the first tensor factor, so $N \subseteq
\{1\otimes \cdots\}''$ which excludes non-trivial first-factor operators). Thus
$R \supsetneq N$.

3. **$N$ is a factor.** The center $\mathcal{Z}(B) = \mathbb{C}\cdot 1$ (UHF alg
ebras are factors). The weak closure preserves the center: $\mathcal{Z}(N) = \ov
erline{\mathcal{Z}(B)}^{\mathrm{WOT}} = \mathbb{C}\cdot 1$. Hence $N$ is a facto
r.

4. **$N$ is type $II_1$.** $N$ is infinite-dimensional (it contains $1 \otimes M
_2 \otimes 1 \otimes \cdots \cong M_2$ and much more). It carries a faithful nor
mal tracial state, namely the restriction $\tau|_N$ (faithful because $\tau$ is
faithful on $R$ and $N$ is a von Neumann subalgebra; normal because $\tau$ is no
rmal on $R$). A factor with a faithful normal trace is either type $I_n$ (finite
-dimensional) or type $II_1$. Since $N$ is infinite-dimensional, it is type $II_
1$.

5. **$N$ is separable.** $R$ acts on the separable Hilbert space $L^2(A,\tau)$.
Any von Neumann subalgebra of a von Neumann algebra acting on a separable Hilber

**Alternative argument via tensor product decomposition.** By Connes' classifica

**Conclusion.** In either construction, we obtain a strict von Neumann subalgebr

ch a subalgebra exists.
