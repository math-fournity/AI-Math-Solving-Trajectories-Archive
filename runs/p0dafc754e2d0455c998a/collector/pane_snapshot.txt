## Proof

**Setup.** Arrange the numbers $1, 2, \dots, 2024$ in a $4 \times 506$ grid, whe
re position $(r, c)$ (with $r \in \{0,1,2,3\}$, $c \in \{1,\dots,506\}$) holds t
he number $506r + c$. Note $2024 = 4 \times 506$.

The allowed edges are:
- **Horizontal (within-row) edges:** consecutive numbers in the same row, i.e.,
$(r,c)$–$(r,c+1)$, plus three **wrap edges** connecting the end of one row to th
e start of the next: $506$–$507$ [$(0,506)$–$(1,1)$], $1012$–$1013$ [$(1,506)$–$
(2,1)$], and $1518$–$1519$ [$(2,506)$–$(3,1)$]. These are all the $|a-b|=1$ edge
s.
- **Vertical edges:** $(r,c)$–$(r+1,c)$, i.e., $\{c, c+506\}$, $\{c+506, c+1012\
}$, $\{c+1012, c+1518\}$. These are the $|a-b|=506$ edges.

**Chessboard coloring.** Color vertex $(r,c)$ **black** if $r+c$ is even, **whit
e** if $r+c$ is odd. Each row has $253$ black and $253$ white vertices, so there
 are $1012$ black and $1012$ white vertices total.

Color analysis of edge types:
- *Within-row horizontal edges* $(r,c)$–$(r,c+1)$: opposite colors (since $c$ an
d $c+1$ have opposite parity). ✓
- *Vertical (506-) edges* $(r,c)$–$(r+1,c)$: opposite colors (since $r$ and $r+1
$ have opposite parity). ✓
- *Wrap edges*:
  - $506$–$507$: $(0,506)$ has $0+506=506$ (even, black); $(1,1)$ has $1+1=2$ (e
ven, black). **Same color (both black).**
  - $1012$–$1013$: $(1,506)$ has $507$ (odd, white); $(2,1)$ has $3$ (odd, white
). **Same color (both white).**
  - $1518$–$1519$: $(2,506)$ has $508$ (even, black); $(3,1)$ has $4$ (even, bla
ck). **Same color (both black).**

**Balance argument.** In a perfect matching, every opposite-color edge covers on
e black and one white vertex, while every same-color (wrap) edge covers two of t
he same color. Since the total counts of black and white vertices are equal ($10
12$ each), the number of black vertices covered by wrap edges must equal the num
ber of white vertices covered by wrap edges:

$$2\cdot[\text{edge }506\text{-}507\text{ used}] + 2\cdot[\text{edge }1518\text{
-}1519\text{ used}] = 2\cdot[\text{edge }1012\text{-}1013\text{ used}].$$

We are given that edge $1518$-$1519$ is used. Let $e_1, e_2 \in \{0,1\}$ denote
whether edges $506$-$507$ and $1012$-$1013$ are used. Then:

$$e_1 + 1 = e_2.$$

The only solution with $e_1, e_2 \in \{0,1\}$ is $e_1 = 0$, $e_2 = 1$. Therefore
:
- Edge $506$-$507$ is **not** in the matching.
- Edge $1012$-$1013$ **is** in the matching.

**Forcing the pair of 505.** Now examine vertex $506 = (0, 506)$. Its neighbors
in the graph are exactly:
- $505$ (since $|506-505|=1$),
- $507$ (since $|506-507|=1$),
- $1012$ (since $|1012-506|=506$).

We have established:
- $507$ is **not** matched with $506$ (edge $506$-$507$ is not in the matching).
- $1012$ is matched with $1013$ (edge $1012$-$1013$ is in the matching), so $101
2$ is unavailable.

Therefore $506$ must be matched with $505$.

**Existence check.** The following matching is valid (confirming consistency): f
or columns $c = 2, \dots, 504$, use vertical edges $\{c, c+506\}$ and $\{c+1012,
 c+1518\}$; then use $\{1, 507\}$, $\{1011, 1517\}$ (both $506$-edges), and $\{5
05, 506\}$, $\{1012, 1013\}$, $\{1518, 1519\}$, $\{2023, 2024\}$ (all $1$-edges)
. This covers all $2024$ vertices with valid edges and satisfies the given condi
tion.

**Conclusion.** The pair of $505$ in the partition is $\boxed{\{505, 506\}}$.
