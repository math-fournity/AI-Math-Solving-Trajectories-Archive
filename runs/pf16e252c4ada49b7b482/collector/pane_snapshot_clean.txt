## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** Every finite group elementarily equivalent to $C_2$ (the cyclic
 group of order 2) has exactly two elements, hence at most two.

**Key observation.** The property "the group has exactly two elements" is expres
sible as a first-order sentence in the language of groups $\{\cdot, {}^{-1}, e\}
$.

Consider the first-order sentence:
$$\varphi \;:=\; \exists x\, \exists y\,\bigl[\, x \neq y \;\wedge\; \forall z\,
(z = x \,\vee\, z = y)\,\bigr].$$

This sentence asserts: *there exist two distinct elements $x, y$ such that every
 element $z$ equals $x$ or $y$*, i.e., the underlying set has cardinality exactl
y 2.

**Verification that $C_2 \models \varphi$.** Let $C_2 = \{e, a\}$ with $a^2 = e$
. Take $x = e$, $y = a$. They are distinct, and every element of $C_2$ is either
 $e$ or $a$. So $\varphi$ holds in $C_2$.

**Conclusion.** Let $G$ be any finite group satisfying the same first-order sent
ences as $C_2$ (i.e., $G \equiv C_2$). Since $\varphi$ is a first-order sentence
 true in $C_2$, it must also be true in $G$. Therefore $G$ has exactly two eleme
nts.

In particular, $|G| = 2 \leq 2$, so $G$ has at most two elements. $\blacksquare$
