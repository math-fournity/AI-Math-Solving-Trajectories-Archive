**Proof.**

We exhibit an explicit invertible linear operator on an infinite-dimensional com
plex vector space whose inverse is a polynomial in the operator.

Let $V$ be the complex vector space with countable basis $\{e_1, e_2, e_3, \dots
\}$ (e.g. $V = \bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty} \mathbb{C}$, the space of finitely suppo
rted sequences). Define the linear operator $T: V \to V$ on the basis by

$$
T(e_n) = \begin{cases} e_n & n \text{ odd}, \\ 2\,e_n & n \text{ even}. \end{cas
es}
$$

$T$ is diagonal with respect to this basis, with eigenvalues drawn from $\{1, 2\
}$, both nonzero. Hence $T$ is invertible, and $T^{-1}$ acts by

$$
T^{-1}(e_n) = \begin{cases} e_n & n \text{ odd}, \\ \tfrac{1}{2} e_n & n \text{
even}. \end{cases}
$$

Now consider the polynomial $p(x) = \tfrac{3 - x}{2} \in \mathbb{C}[x]$. We eval
uate $p(T) = \tfrac{1}{2}(3I - T)$ on each basis vector:

- For $n$ odd: $T(e_n) = e_n$, so $p(T)(e_n) = \tfrac{1}{2}(3 e_n - e_n) = e_n =
 T^{-1}(e_n)$.
- For $n$ even: $T(e_n) = 2 e_n$, so $p(T)(e_n) = \tfrac{1}{2}(3 e_n - 2 e_n) =
\tfrac{1}{2} e_n = T^{-1}(e_n)$.

Since $p(T)$ and $T^{-1}$ agree on a basis, $p(T) = T^{-1}$.

Thus $T^{-1}$ is represented by the polynomial $p(x) = \tfrac{3-x}{2}$ in $T$, o
n an infinite-dimensional complex vector space. $\blacksquare$
