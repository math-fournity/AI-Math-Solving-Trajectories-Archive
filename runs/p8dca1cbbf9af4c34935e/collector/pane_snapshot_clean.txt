# Solution

**Claim:** The maximum value of $C$ is $\boxed{4}$.

## Proof

We seek the largest constant $C$ such that for every $f \in C^1([0,1])$ with $f(
0)=f(1)=0$,
$$\max_{x\in[0,1]}|f'(x)| \;\ge\; C\int_0^1|f(x)|\,dx.$$

Equivalently, $C = \inf_{f\ne 0}\dfrac{\max|f'|}{\int|f|}$.

### Upper bound on $\int|f|$ in terms of $\max|f'|$

Let $M = \max_{x\in[0,1]}|f'(x)|$. Since $f(0)=0$, for any $x\in[0,1]$,
$$|f(x)| = \left|\int_0^x f'(t)\,dt\right| \le \int_0^x |f'(t)|\,dt \le Mx.$$
Since $f(1)=0$, similarly,
$$|f(x)| = \left|\int_x^1 f'(t)\,dt\right| \le \int_x^1 |f'(t)|\,dt \le M(1-x).$
$
Combining,
$$|f(x)| \le M\min(x,\,1-x).$$
Integrating,
$$\int_0^1|f(x)|\,dx \le M\int_0^1\min(x,1-x)\,dx = M\left(\int_0^{1/2}x\,dx+\in
t_{1/2}^1(1-x)\,dx\right)=M\left(\tfrac18+\tfrac18\right)=\frac{M}{4}.$$
Therefore $\max|f'| = M \ge 4\int_0^1|f|\,dx$, so $C=4$ is **valid**.

### Sharpness: the tent function attains equality

Define $f(x)=\min(x,1-x)$, i.e.
$$f(x)=\begin{cases}x,&0\le x\le\tfrac12,\\ 1-x,&\tfrac12\le x\le 1.\end{cases}$
$
This is continuous on $[0,1]$ with $f(0)=f(1)=0$, and $f'(x)=1$ on $(0,\tfrac12)
$, $f'(x)=-1$ on $(\tfrac12,1)$, so $f\in C^1([0,1]\setminus\{\tfrac12\})$. To o
btain a $C^1$ function, round the corner at $x=\tfrac12$ over an interval of wid
th $\varepsilon$: let $f_\varepsilon$ agree with $f$ outside $(\tfrac12-\varepsi
lon,\tfrac12+\varepsilon)$ and be smoothly interpolated inside, with $|f_\vareps
ilon'|\le 1$ and $f_\varepsilon(\tfrac12)=\tfrac12$. Then
$$\max|f_\varepsilon'|=1,\qquad \int_0^1|f_\varepsilon|\,dx \;\to\; \int_0^1\min
(x,1-x)\,dx = \tfrac14 \quad(\varepsilon\to 0).$$
Hence $\dfrac{\max|f_\varepsilon'|}{\int|f_\varepsilon|}\to 4$, so no constant l
arger than $4$ can work.

### Conclusion

The largest constant for which the inequality holds for all admissible $f$ is
$$C = 4.$$
