# Proof: Invariance of Metric Ball Volume and Sphere Area on the Pseudosphere Un
der Translation of the Centerpoint

## Setup and Key Property

The pseudosphere (tractricoid) is a surface of revolution carrying **constant ne
gative Gaussian curvature** $K = -1$. We show that, on account of this constancy
, the area (2-dimensional "volume") of a geodesic ball $B(p, R)$ and the length
(1-dimensional "area") of a geodesic sphere $S(p, R) = \partial B(p, R)$ depend
only on the radius $R$ and not on the centerpoint $p$—provided $R$ is small enou
gh that $B(p,R)$ lies within a geodesically normal neighborhood of $p$ that does
 not meet the boundary/singularity of the pseudosphere.

## Geodesic Polar Coordinates

Fix any point $p$ on the pseudosphere and introduce **geodesic polar coordinates
** $(r, \theta)$ centered at $p$, where $r$ is the geodesic distance from $p$ an
d $\theta$ is the angle. Within a normal neighborhood of $p$, the Riemannian met
ric takes the form

$$ds^2 = dr^2 + G(r,\theta)\, d\theta^2,$$

with the initial conditions $G(r,\theta) \to 0$ and $\sqrt{G(r,\theta)}/r \to 1$
 as $r \to 0$ (the metric is Euclidean to first order at the origin).

## The Jacobi Equation (Geodesic Deviation)

A classical result in differential geometry relates the Gaussian curvature to $G
$ via the **Jacobi equation**. Setting $f(r,\theta) = \sqrt{G(r,\theta)}$, one h
as

$$\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial r^2} + K\, f = 0, \qquad f(0,\theta) = 0,\quad \l
eft.\frac{\partial f}{\partial r}\right|_{r=0} = 1.$$

This equation governs the spreading of geodesics emanating from $p$ and is deriv
ed from the geodesic deviation equation (the linearized geodesic equation for a
one-parameter family of geodesics).

## Solving for Constant $K = -1$

For the pseudosphere, $K = -1$ is **constant**. The Jacobi equation becomes

$$\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial r^2} - f = 0, \qquad f(0,\theta) = 0,\quad f'(0,\
theta) = 1.$$

The general solution is $f(r,\theta) = A(\theta)\cosh r + B(\theta)\sinh r$. The
 initial conditions force $A(\theta) = 0$ and $B(\theta) = 1$ for **every** $\th
eta$. Therefore

$$f(r,\theta) = \sqrt{G(r,\theta)} = \sinh r,$$

which is **independent of $\theta$** and, crucially, **independent of the choice
 of centerpoint $p$**. The metric in geodesic polar coordinates is universally

$$ds^2 = dr^2 + \sinh^2 r\, d\theta^2.$$

The constancy of $K$ is what makes the coefficient $\sqrt{G}$ a function of $r$
alone, with a universal form that does not involve $p$.

## Computing the Volume (Area) of a Geodesic Ball

The area element is $\sqrt{G}\, dr\, d\theta = \sinh r\, dr\, d\theta$. The area
 of the geodesic ball $B(p, R)$ is

$$\operatorname{Vol}\bigl(B(p,R)\bigr) = \int_0^{R}\!\int_0^{2\pi} \sinh r\, d\t
heta\, dr = 2\pi\int_0^{R} \sinh r\, dr = 2\pi\bigl(\cosh R - 1\bigr).$$

This depends only on $R$ and the constant $K = -1$, **not on $p$**.

## Computing the Area (Length) of a Geodesic Sphere

The geodesic sphere $S(p, R) = \partial B(p, R)$ is the curve $r = R$. Its lengt
h is

$$\operatorname{Area}\bigl(S(p,R)\bigr) = \int_0^{2\pi} \sqrt{G(R,\theta)}\, d\t
heta = \int_0^{2\pi} \sinh R\, d\theta = 2\pi \sinh R.$$

Again, this depends only on $R$, **not on $p$**.

## Invariance Under Translation of the Centerpoint

Let $p$ and $q$ be any two points on the pseudosphere. For any radius $R$ such t
hat both $B(p, R)$ and $B(q, R)$ lie within normal neighborhoods that do not enc
ounter the boundary/singularity of the pseudosphere, we have

$$\operatorname{Vol}\bigl(B(p,R)\bigr) = 2\pi(\cosh R - 1) = \operatorname{Vol}\
bigl(B(q,R)\bigr),$$
$$\operatorname{Area}\bigl(S(p,R)\bigr) = 2\pi\sinh R = \operatorname{Area}\bigl
(S(q,R)\bigr).$$

Both quantities are **invariant under translation of the centerpoint**. The mech
anism is that constant Gaussian curvature forces the Jacobi equation to have a u
niversal solution $\sqrt{G} = \sinh r$ (for $K=-1$), which is the same function
regardless of where the geodesic polar coordinate system is centered. Hence the
area and length integrals, which depend only on $\sqrt{G}$, yield the same value
s at every centerpoint.

## Caveat on Completeness

The pseudosphere (tractricoid) is an **incomplete** surface: it has a singular r
im where the tractrix meets its axis, and geodesics reach this rim in finite dis
tance. For radii $R$ large enough that $B(p, R)$ would extend past the rim, the
ball is truncated by the boundary, and its area then depends on how far $p$ sits
 from the rim. Thus the invariance proven above holds **locally**—for all $R$ wi
thin the normal-injectivity range that keeps the ball away from the boundary. Th
is is the natural domain of the geodesic polar coordinate formula. Within this d
omain, the invariance is exact and complete.

## Conclusion

**Yes.** On the pseudosphere, the volume (area) of metric balls and the area (le
ngth) of metric spheres remain invariant under translation of the centerpoint, f
or all radii within the geodesically normal range. The reason is that the pseudo
sphere has constant Gaussian curvature $K = -1$, which makes the solution of the
 Jacobi equation in geodesic polar coordinates universal—$\sqrt{G(r,\theta)} = \
sinh r$, depending only on $r$ and $K$, not on the centerpoint $p$ or the angle
$\theta$. Consequently:

$$\operatorname{Vol}\bigl(B(p,R)\bigr) = 2\pi(\cosh R - 1), \qquad \operatorname
{Area}\bigl(S(p,R)\bigr) = 2\pi\sinh R,$$

both independent of $p$.
