### Setup and definitions

The **field polynomial** $g(X)$ of $\pi$ over $F$ is the characteristic polynomi
al of the $F$-linear endomorphism $m_\pi : K \to K$, $x \mapsto \pi x$, where $K
$ is viewed as an $n$-dimensional $F$-vector space ($n = [K:F] = e$). This is a
monic polynomial of degree $n$ in $F[X]$.

Let $m(X) \in F[X]$ be the minimal (irreducible) polynomial of $\pi$ over $F$. T
hen
$$g(X) = m(X)^{d}, \qquad d = [K : F(\pi)].$$

### Part 1: All coefficients $c_j$ lie in $R$

Since $\pi$ is a uniformizer of $K$, we have $\pi \in T$. The valuation ring $T$
 is the integral closure of $R$ in $K$, so every element of $T$ is integral over
 $R$; in particular **$\pi$ is integral over $R$**.

Since $\pi$ is integral over $R$, it satisfies a monic polynomial with coefficie
nts in $R$. The minimal polynomial $m(X)$ of $\pi$ over $F$ (the fraction field
of $R$) divides this polynomial in $F[X]$. Because **$R$ is a valuation ring, he
nce integrally closed**, Gauss's Lemma applies: $m(X) \in R[X]$.

Therefore $g(X) = m(X)^d \in R[X]$, and all coefficients $c_j$ belong to $R$. $\
checkmark$

### Part 2: $v_K(c_j)$ is divisible by $n$ for each $j$

The coefficients $c_j$ lie in $F$ (by definition of the field polynomial over $F
$), and we have just shown $c_j \in R \subset F$.

Since $K/F$ is **totally ramified** with ramification index $e = n$ and residue
degree $f = 1$, the restriction of the normalized valuation $v_K$ to $F$ satisfi
es:
$$v_K(a) = e \cdot v_F(a) = n \cdot v_F(a) \qquad \text{for all } a \in F^*,$$
where $v_F$ is the normalized discrete valuation of $F$ (with $v_F(F^*) = \mathb
b{Z}$). This is the fundamental relation between the normalized valuations in a
totally ramified extension.

Applying this to each $c_j \in F$:
$$v_K(c_j) = n \cdot v_F(c_j).$$

Since $v_F(c_j) \in \mathbb{Z}$ (and $v_F(0) = \infty$ by convention), we conclu
de that **$v_K(c_j)$ is divisible by $n$** for every $j$. $\checkmark$
