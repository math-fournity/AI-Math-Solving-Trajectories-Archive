## Solution

**The example.** Let $p = 3$ (any prime $\geq 3$ works). Set
- $K = \mathbb{F}_p(s,t)$, the rational function field in two indeterminates ove
r $\mathbb{F}_p$ (an infinite field of characteristic $p$),
- $d = 3$,
- $G = \mathrm{UT}_3(K) = \left\{\begin{pmatrix} 1 & a & c \\ 0 & 1 & b \\ 0 & 0
 & 1 \end{pmatrix} : a, b, c \in K\right\}$, the group of $3\times 3$ upper unit
riangular matrices over $K$,
- $N = p = 3$.

**$G$ is a subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_3(K)$.** Every element of $G$ is upper trian
gular with all diagonal entries equal to $1$, hence has determinant $1$ and is i
nvertible. The set $G$ is closed under multiplication and inversion (it is the k
ernel of the natural map from the upper triangular group to the diagonal group),
 so $G \leq \mathrm{GL}_3(K)$.

**$G$ is infinite.** The map
$$a \;\longmapsto\; \begin{pmatrix} 1 & a & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pma
trix}$$
is an injective map from $K$ into $G$. Since $K = \mathbb{F}_p(s,t)$ is infinite
 (it contains the infinitely many elements $s, s^2, s^3, \dots$), the group $G$
is infinite.

**Every element of $G$ has order dividing $p$.** Let $g \in G$ and write $g = I
+ N$ where
$$N = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & a & c \\ 0 & 0 & b \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$
A direct computation shows $N^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & ab \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 &
 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and $N^3 = 0$.

Now compute $g^p = (I+N)^p$ using the binomial theorem (valid in any commutative
 ring):
$$g^p = \sum_{k=0}^{p} \binom{p}{k} N^k.$$
For $1 \leq k \leq p-1$, the binomial coefficient $\binom{p}{k}$ is divisible by
 $p$, hence $\binom{p}{k} = 0$ in $K$ (since $\mathrm{char}(K) = p$). Therefore
$$g^p = I + N^p.$$
Since $N^3 = 0$ and $p \geq 3$, we have $N^p = 0$, and thus
$$g^p = I.$$

**Conclusion.** We have exhibited a field $K = \mathbb{F}_p(s,t)$, an integer $d
 = 3$, and an infinite subgroup $G = \mathrm{UT}_3(K) \leq \mathrm{GL}_3(K)$, to
gether with $N = p$, such that $g^N = I_3$ for every $g \in G$. $\blacksquare$
