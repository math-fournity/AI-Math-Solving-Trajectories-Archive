## Proof

**Claim.** Yes. If $f_n \to f$ in $L^p$ (for $0 < p \le \infty$), then there exi
sts a subsequence $\{f_{n_k}\}$ converging to $f$ almost everywhere.

---

**Case 1: $p = \infty$.**

Convergence $\|f_n - f\|_\infty \to 0$ means: there exists a measurable set $E$
with $\mu(E) = 0$ such that $f_n \to f$ uniformly on $E^c$. Hence $f_n(x) \to f(
x)$ for every $x \in E^c$, i.e. $f_n \to f$ a.e. The whole sequence works, so ce
rtainly a subsequence does.

---

**Case 2: $0 < p < \infty$.**

Since $\|f_n - f\|_p \to 0$, we may select a subsequence $\{f_{n_k}\}_{k=1}^\inf
ty$ with

$$\|f_{n_k} - f\|_p^p \;\le\; 2^{-k}, \qquad k = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$$

(At each step $k$, choose $n_k > n_{k-1}$ so large that $\|f_{n_k} - f\|_p^p \le
 2^{-k}$; this is possible since $\|f_n - f\|_p^p \to 0$.)

Set $h_k := f_{n_k} - f$, so that $\int |h_k|^p \, d\mu = \|h_k\|_p^p \le 2^{-k}
$.

Define the non-negative measurable functions

$$S_m(x) := \sum_{k=1}^{m} |h_k(x)|^p, \qquad S(x) := \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} |h_k(x
)|^p = \sup_m S_m(x).$$

The sequence $\{S_m\}$ is non-negative and increasing, so by the **Monotone Conv
ergence Theorem**,

$$\int S \, d\mu \;=\; \lim_{m \to \infty} \int S_m \, d\mu \;=\; \lim_{m \to \i
nfty} \sum_{k=1}^{m} \int |h_k|^p \, d\mu \;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \|h_k\|_p^p
\;\le\; \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} 2^{-k} \;=\; 1 \;<\; \infty.$$

Since $S \in L^1(\mu)$, we have $S(x) < \infty$ for $\mu$-almost every $x$. That
 is, for a.e. $x$,

$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} |f_{n_k}(x) - f(x)|^p \;<\; \infty.$$

Convergence of a series forces its terms to tend to zero, so for a.e. $x$,

$$|f_{n_k}(x) - f(x)|^p \longrightarrow 0 \quad \text{as } k \to \infty,$$

and since $t \mapsto t^{1/p}$ is continuous,

$$f_{n_k}(x) \longrightarrow f(x) \quad \text{as } k \to \infty, \quad \text{for
 a.e. } x.$$

---

**Conclusion.** In both cases, a subsequence converging almost everywhere exists
. $\blacksquare$
