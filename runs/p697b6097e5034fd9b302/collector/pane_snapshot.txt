## Proof

Let $\alpha = 3+\sqrt{11}$ and $\beta = 3-\sqrt{11}$. Then $\alpha+\beta=6$, $\a
lpha\beta = 9-11=-2$, and $\alpha,\beta$ are roots of $x^2-6x-2=0$.

**Step 1: Reduce the floor to an integer sequence.**

Since $|\beta| = \sqrt{11}-3 \approx 0.317 < 1$, for every $m\ge 1$ we have $|\b
eta|^m<1$. As $\beta<0$, for odd $m=2n-1$ the power $\beta^{2n-1}$ is negative,
so $\beta^{2n-1}\in(-1,0)$.

Define $S_m = \alpha^m+\beta^m$. Then $S_m\in\mathbb{Z}$ (it satisfies $S_m=6S_{
m-1}+2S_{m-2}$, $S_0=2$, $S_1=6$), and
$$\alpha^{2n-1} = S_{2n-1}-\beta^{2n-1}=S_{2n-1}+t,\qquad t=-\beta^{2n-1}\in(0,1
).$$
Hence $\lfloor\alpha^{2n-1}\rfloor = S_{2n-1}$, and we must find $v_2(S_{2n-1})$
.

**Step 2: Recurrence for the odd-indexed subsequence.**

From $\alpha^2+\beta^2=(\alpha+\beta)^2-2\alpha\beta=36+4=40$ and $(\alpha\beta)
^2=4$, the step-2 recurrence is
$$S_{m+2}=40\,S_m-4\,S_{m-2}.$$
Set $T_n=S_{2n-1}$. Then
$$T_{n+1}=40\,T_n-4\,T_{n-1}=4\,(10T_n-T_{n-1}),\qquad T_1=6,\;T_2=252.$$

**Step 3: Extract the exact 2-adic valuation.**

Write $T_n=2^n U_n$. Substituting:
$$2^{n+1}U_{n+1}=4\bigl(10\cdot 2^n U_n-2^{n-1}U_{n-1}\bigr)=2^{n+1}(20U_n-U_{n-
1}),$$
so
$$U_{n+1}=20\,U_n-U_{n-1},\qquad U_1=\tfrac{6}{2}=3,\;U_2=\tfrac{252}{4}=63.$$

Reducing modulo $2$: $U_{n+1}\equiv -U_{n-1}\equiv U_{n-1}\pmod 2$ (since $20$ i
s even). With $U_1\equiv U_2\equiv 1\pmod 2$, induction gives $U_n$ **odd** for
all $n\ge 1$.

Therefore
$$v_2(S_{2n-1})=v_2(2^n U_n)=n\qquad(\text{since }U_n\text{ is odd}).$$

**Step 4: Verification.**

- $n=1$: $S_1=6=2\cdot 3$, $v_2=1=k$. ✓
- $n=2$: $S_3=252=4\cdot 63$, $v_2=2=k$. ✓
- $n=3$: $S_5=10056=8\cdot 1257$, $v_2=3=k$. ✓
- $n=4$: $S_7=401232=16\cdot 25077$, $v_2=4=k$. ✓

**Conclusion.** For each natural number $n$, the largest positive integer $k$ su
ch that $\bigl\lfloor(3+\sqrt{11})^{2n-1}\bigr\rfloor$ is divisible by $2^k$ is

$$\boxed{k=n}.$$
