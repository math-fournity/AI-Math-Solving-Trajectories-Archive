**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.** Assume $m$ is a finite positive Borel measure on $[0,1]$ that is nona
tomic and satisfies $m([0,1])\ge \theta$ (so the hypothesis is nonvacuous; this
covers in particular Lebesgue measure, the canonical measure on $[0,1]$).

Consider the Lebesgue decomposition of $\mu$ with respect to $m$:
$$\mu = \mu_{\mathrm{ac}} + \mu_s, \qquad \mu_{\mathrm{ac}} = g\,dm \ll m,\qquad
 \mu_s \perp m,$$
where $g\ge 0$ is Borel and $\mu_s$ is supported on a Borel set $S$ with $m(S)=0
$.

Since $m(S)=0$, we have $m([0,1]\setminus S)=m([0,1])\ge\theta$. The restriction
 of $m$ to $[0,1]\setminus S$ is nonatomic with total mass $\ge\theta$; by Sierp
iński's theorem (a nonatomic finite measure attains every value in $[0,\text{tot
al}]$), there exists a Borel set $A\subset[0,1]\setminus S$ with $m(A)=\theta$.

Fix such an $A$. Now let $F\subset S$ be **any** Borel set, and set $E=A\cup F$.
 Then $A$ and $F$ are disjoint, $E$ is Borel, and
$$m(E)=m(A)+m(F)=\theta+0=\theta,$$
since $F\subset S$ and $m(S)=0$. By the hypothesis, $\mu(E)=\theta/2$.

Computing $\mu(E)$ via the decomposition:
$$\mu(E)=\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}(A)+\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}(F)+\mu_s(A)+\mu_s(F).$$
- $\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}(F)=\int_F g\,dm=0$, since $m(F)=0$;
- $\mu_s(A)=\mu_s(A\cap S)=\mu_s(\varnothing)=0$, since $A\subset[0,1]\setminus
S$.

Hence
$$\mu(E)=\int_A g\,dm+\mu_s(F)=\frac{\theta}{2}.$$

The quantity $\int_A g\,dm$ is a fixed constant (independent of $F$). Therefore
$$\mu_s(F)=\frac{\theta}{2}-\int_A g\,dm$$
is the **same constant** for every Borel $F\subset S$. Taking $F=\varnothing$ gi
ves $\mu_s(\varnothing)=0$, so this constant is $0$. Thus
$$\mu_s(F)=0\quad\text{for every Borel }F\subset S.$$

Since $\mu_s$ is supported on $S$, for any Borel $B\subset[0,1]$ we have $\mu_s(
B)=\mu_s(B\cap S)=0$. So $\mu_s\equiv 0$, and consequently
$$\mu = g\,dm \ll m.$$

Therefore $\mu$ is absolutely continuous with respect to $m$. $\blacksquare$
