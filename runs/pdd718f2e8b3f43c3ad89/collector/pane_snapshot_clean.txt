## Solution

We claim the completion of $(\ell^1(\mathbb{N}), d_*)$ is isometric to $\big(c_0
(\mathbb{N}), \|\cdot\|_\infty\big)$, where
$$c_0 = \{x = (x_n) : x_n \in \mathbb{R},\ x_n \to 0\}, \qquad \|x\|_\infty = \s
up_n |x_n|.$$

The strategy: realize the abstract completion as the closure of $\ell^1$ inside
the complete space $(\ell^\infty, \|\cdot\|_\infty)$, then identify that closure
 with $c_0$.

---

### Step 1. Setup and the embedding

Recall $d_*(a,b) = \sup_n |a_n - b_n| = \|a-b\|_\infty$, so $(\ell^1, d_*)$ is t
he space $\ell^1$ equipped with the sup-norm. Every absolutely summable sequence
 is bounded (in fact $|a_n| \le \sum|a_k| < \infty$), so $\ell^1 \subseteq \ell^
\infty$, and the inclusion
$$\iota: (\ell^1, \|\cdot\|_\infty) \hookrightarrow (\ell^\infty, \|\cdot\|_\inf
ty)$$
is an **isometry** onto its image. Since $(\ell^\infty, \|\cdot\|_\infty)$ is a
**complete** metric space (it is a Banach space), the completion of $(\ell^1, d_
*)$ is realized concretely as
$$\overline{\iota(\ell^1)}^{\,\ell^\infty} \subseteq \ell^\infty.$$
This is a standard fact: if $X$ embeds isometrically as a dense subset of a comp
lete space $Y$, then $Y$ is (a concrete model of) the completion of $X$; here $Y
 = \overline{\iota(\ell^1)}$.

So it remains to compute $\overline{\ell^1}^{\,\ell^\infty}$.

---

### Step 2. Two inclusions

Let $c_{00}$ denote the finitely supported sequences. We have the chain
$$c_{00} \subseteq \ell^1 \subseteq c_0 \subseteq \ell^\infty,$$
where the middle inclusion $\ell^1 \subseteq c_0$ holds because absolute summabi
lity forces $a_n \to 0$ (the tail $\sum_{n>N}|a_n| \to 0$, so $|a_n| \to 0$).

**Claim A.** $\overline{\ell^1}^{\,\ell^\infty} \subseteq c_0$.

*Proof.* The space $c_0$ is a **closed** subspace of $\ell^\infty$: if $x^{(k)}
\in c_0$ and $\|x^{(k)} - x\|_\infty \to 0$, then for every $\varepsilon > 0$ pi
ck $k$ with $\|x^{(k)}-x\|_\infty < \varepsilon/2$, and since $x^{(k)}_n \to 0$
pick $N$ with $|x^{(k)}_n| < \varepsilon/2$ for $n > N$; then $|x_n| \le |x_n -
x^{(k)}_n| + |x^{(k)}_n| < \varepsilon$ for $n > N$, so $x \in c_0$. Since $\ell
^1 \subseteq c_0$ and $c_0$ is closed, $\overline{\ell^1} \subseteq c_0$. $\squa
re$

**Claim B.** $c_0 \subseteq \overline{\ell^1}^{\,\ell^\infty}$, i.e. $\ell^1$ is
 dense in $c_0$ under the sup-norm.

*Proof.* It suffices to show $c_{00}$ is dense in $c_0$ under $\|\cdot\|_\infty$
, since $c_{00} \subseteq \ell^1$. Let $x \in c_0$ and $\varepsilon > 0$. Since
$x_n \to 0$, there exists $N$ with $|x_n| < \varepsilon$ for all $n > N$. Define
 the truncation
$$y_n = \begin{cases} x_n & n \le N,\\ 0 & n > N.\end{cases}$$
Then $y \in c_{00} \subseteq \ell^1$ and $\|x - y\|_\infty = \sup_{n > N}|x_n| \
le \varepsilon$. Hence $c_{00}$ (and thus $\ell^1$) is dense in $c_0$. $\square$

---

### Step 3. Conclusion

From Claims A and B,
$$\overline{\ell^1}^{\,\ell^\infty} = c_0.$$

The space $(c_0, \|\cdot\|_\infty)$ is **complete** (closed subspace of the Bana
ch space $\ell^\infty$, as shown in Claim A), and $\ell^1$ embeds isometrically
into it as a **dense** subspace (Claim B). By the uniqueness of metric completio
ns up to isometry, $(c_0, \|\cdot\|_\infty)$ is the completion of $(\ell^1, d_*)
$.

---

### Explicit description of the isometry

Concretely, the isometry $\Phi$ from the abstract completion (equivalence classe
s $[\,(a^{(k)})\,]$ of $d_*$-Cauchy sequences in $\ell^1$) to $c_0$ is
$$\Phi\big([\,(a^{(k)})\,]\big) \;=\; \lim_{k\to\infty} a^{(k)} \quad\text{(limi
t in } \ell^\infty\text{)},$$
which is well defined because $(a^{(k)})$ is $\|\cdot\|_\infty$-Cauchy in the co
mplete space $\ell^\infty$, the limit lands in $c_0$ by Claim A, and equivalent
Cauchy sequences have the same limit. Its inverse sends $x \in c_0$ to the class
 of any $\ell^1$-sequence converging to $x$ in sup-norm (e.g. the truncations $x
^{(N)} = (x_1,\dots,x_N,0,0,\dots) \in c_{00} \subseteq \ell^1$).

---

**Answer.** The completion of $\big(\ell^1(\mathbb{N}),\, d_*\big)$ is the space
 $\big(c_0(\mathbb{N}),\, \|\cdot\|_\infty\big)$ of real sequences converging to
 $0$, equipped with the sup-norm $d(x,y) = \sup_n |x_n - y_n|$.
