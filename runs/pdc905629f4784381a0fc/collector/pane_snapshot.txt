## Proof

Let $n = \frac{m}{m-1}$, so that $\frac{1}{m} + \frac{1}{n} = 1$. Note that sinc
e $m > 1$, we have $n > 1$ as well, and the key identity $(m-1)(n-1) = 1$ holds
throughout.

We work in the first quadrant where $x \ge 0,\, y \ge 0$, since $u, v \ge 0$ mea
ns any supporting line $ux + vy = 1$ touches the curve there.

---

### (⇒) Tangency implies $u^n + v^n = 1$.

Suppose $ux + vy = 1$ is tangent to $x^m + y^m = 1$ at a point $(a, b)$ with $a,
 b > 0$ (the boundary cases $a=0$ or $b=0$ are treated at the end).

**(i) The point lies on the curve:** $a^m + b^m = 1$.

**(ii) The point lies on the line:** $ua + vb = 1$.

**(iii) Tangency condition:** The normal to the line, namely $(u, v)$, is parall
el to the gradient of $F(x,y) = x^m + y^m - 1$ at $(a,b)$, which is $(ma^{m-1},\
, mb^{m-1})$. Hence there exists $\lambda > 0$ with
$$
(u,\, v) = \lambda\,(a^{m-1},\, b^{m-1}).
$$

Substituting into the line equation (ii):
$$
\lambda\, a^{m-1}\cdot a + \lambda\, b^{m-1}\cdot b = \lambda(a^m + b^m) = \lamb
da \cdot 1 = 1,
$$
so $\lambda = 1$. Therefore
$$
u = a^{m-1}, \qquad v = b^{m-1}.
$$

Now compute, using $(m-1)n = m$:
$$
u^n + v^n = a^{(m-1)n} + b^{(m-1)n} = a^m + b^m = 1. \qquad \checkmark
$$

---

### (⇐) The condition $u^n + v^n = 1$ implies tangency.

Assume $u \ge 0,\, v \ge 0$ and $u^n + v^n = 1$. We exhibit the tangency point.
Set
$$
a = u^{n-1}, \qquad b = v^{n-1}.
$$

**(i) The point is on the curve.** Using $(n-1)m = n$ (which follows from $n-1 =
 \frac{1}{m-1}$):
$$
a^m + b^m = u^{(n-1)m} + v^{(n-1)m} = u^n + v^n = 1. \qquad \checkmark
$$

**(ii) The point is on the line.**
$$
ua + vb = u\cdot u^{n-1} + v\cdot v^{n-1} = u^n + v^n = 1. \qquad \checkmark
$$

**(iii) The line is tangent at $(a,b)$.** The gradient of $x^m + y^m - 1$ at $(a
,b)$ is $(ma^{m-1},\, mb^{m-1})$. Using $(n-1)(m-1) = 1$:
$$
a^{m-1} = \bigl(u^{n-1}\bigr)^{m-1} = u^{(n-1)(m-1)} = u^1 = u,
$$
and likewise $b^{m-1} = v$. So the gradient is $m(u, v)$, which is a positive sc
alar multiple of the line's normal $(u,v)$. Hence the line is tangent to the cur
ve at $(a, b)$. $\checkmark$

---

### Boundary cases ($u = 0$ or $v = 0$).

- **$u = 0$:** The condition $u^n + v^n = 1$ forces $v = 1$, giving the line $y
= 1$. On the curve, the point $(0,1)$ satisfies $0^m + 1^m = 1$, and the gradien
t there is $(0, m)$, pointing purely in the $y$-direction — so the tangent line
is $y = 1$. $\checkmark$

- **$v = 0$:** Symmetrically $u = 1$, the line is $x = 1$, tangent at $(1, 0)$.
$\checkmark$

- **$u = v = 0$:** Then $u^n + v^n = 0 \ne 1$, so this is excluded by the hypoth
esis, and indeed $0 = 1$ is not a valid line.

---

### Conclusion

The line $ux + vy = 1$ (with $u, v \ge 0$) is tangent to the curve $x^m + y^m =
1$ (with $m > 1$) **if and only if** $u^n + v^n = 1$, where $n = \frac{m}{m-1}$
is the unique exponent satisfying $\frac{1}{m} + \frac{1}{n} = 1$. $\blacksquare
$
