## Proof

**Setup.** The large equilateral triangle has side length $n = 10$ and is partit
ioned into $n^2 = 100$ unit equilateral triangles. A "gemstone cluster" (rhombus
/lozenge) is formed by joining two unit triangles that share a common edge.

**Key observation.** Every internal edge of the grid is shared by exactly two un
it triangles of opposite orientation (one pointing up ▲, one pointing down ▼), a
nd joining those two triangles along that shared edge produces exactly one rhomb
us. Conversely, every rhombus arises from exactly one such shared internal edge.
 Therefore:

$$\text{Number of rhombuses} = \text{Number of internal edges in the grid}.$$

**Counting internal edges by direction.** The triangular grid has three edge dir
ections (horizontal, 60°, and 120°), related by the 3-fold symmetry of the equil
ateral triangle. We count edges in one direction and multiply by 3.

**Horizontal edges.** The horizontal edges form rows at heights $h = 0, 1, \ldot
s, n-1$. At height $h$, there are $(n - h)$ horizontal edges. The total number o
f horizontal edges is:

$$\sum_{h=0}^{n-1}(n - h) = n + (n-1) + \cdots + 1 = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}.$$

Of these, only the bottom row ($h = 0$) lies on the boundary (the base of the la
rge triangle), contributing $n$ boundary edges. All other horizontal rows are in
terior. So the number of **internal** horizontal edges is:

$$\frac{n(n+1)}{2} - n = \frac{n(n-1)}{2}.$$

**All three directions.** By the 3-fold symmetry of the equilateral triangle, ea
ch direction has the same count of total edges ($\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$) and the same
 count of boundary edges ($n$, one side of the triangle). Therefore:

$$\text{Total internal edges} = 3 \times \frac{n(n-1)}{2}.$$

**Substituting $n = 10$:**

$$\text{Total rhombuses} = 3 \times \frac{10 \times 9}{2} = 3 \times 45 = 135.$$

**Verification with small cases.**
- $n = 1$: $3 \times \frac{1 \times 0}{2} = 0$. One triangle, no rhombuses. ✓
- $n = 2$: $3 \times \frac{2 \times 1}{2} = 3$. The single downward triangle sha
res 3 internal edges with 3 upward triangles, giving 3 rhombuses. ✓
- $n = 3$: $3 \times \frac{3 \times 2}{2} = 9$. Three downward triangles, each w
ith 3 internal edges, all distinct, giving 9 rhombuses. ✓

**Cross-check via edge accounting.** Each unit triangle has 3 edges, so the tota
l edge-incidence count is $3 \times 100 = 300$. Each internal edge is counted tw
ice (shared by 2 triangles) and each boundary edge once. The perimeter has $3n =
 30$ boundary edges. So:

$$2 \times (\text{internal edges}) + 30 = 300 \implies \text{internal edges} = 1
35. \quad \checkmark$$

**Answer:** The total number of distinct gemstone clusters (rhombuses) in the 10
-unit triangular grid is $\boxed{135}$.
