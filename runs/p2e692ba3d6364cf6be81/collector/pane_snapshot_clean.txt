### Sketch of the region

The region is a **half-annulus** in the upper half-plane: the annular strip betw
een the circles $r=1$ and $r=e$, restricted to $0<\theta<\pi$. It is bounded by
- the inner semicircle $r=1$, $0<\theta<\pi$ (where $u=0$),
- the outer semicircle $r=e$, $0<\theta<\pi$ (where $u=0$),
- the segment on the positive $x$-axis, $\theta=0$, $1<r<e$ (where $u=0$),
- the segment on the negative $x$-axis, $\theta=\pi$, $1<r<e$ (where $u=1$).

---

### Change of variable $s=\ln r$

Set $s=\ln r$, so $r=e^{s}$ and $s\in(0,1)$. Since
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial r}=\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial s},\qquad r\fr
ac{\partial u}{\partial r}=\frac{\partial u}{\partial s},$$
we get
$$\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left(r\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\r
ight)=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial s^{2}}.$$
The PDE becomes (the factor $1/r^{2}$ cancels)
$$\boxed{\;\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial s^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial\th
eta^{2}}=0,\qquad 0<s<1,\;0<\theta<\pi.\;}$$

The boundary conditions transform to
$$u(0,\theta)=0,\quad u(1,\theta)=0,\quad u(s,0)=0,\quad u(s,\pi)=1.$$

So we must solve **Laplace's equation on the rectangle** $(0,1)\times(0,\pi)$ wi
th $u=0$ on three sides and $u=1$ on the side $\theta=\pi$.

---

### Separation of variables

Seek $u(s,\theta)=S(s)\Theta(\theta)$. Then $S''\Theta+S\Theta''=0$, i.e.
$$\frac{S''}{S}=-\frac{\Theta''}{\Theta}=-\lambda.$$

**The $s$-problem** (homogeneous BCs at $s=0,1$):
$$S''+\lambda S=0,\quad S(0)=S(1)=0\;\Longrightarrow\;\lambda_n=(n\pi)^2,\quad S
_n(s)=\sin(n\pi s),\quad n=1,2,\dots$$

**The $\theta$-problem** (homogeneous BC at $\theta=0$):
$$\Theta''-(n\pi)^2\Theta=0,\quad \Theta(0)=0\;\Longrightarrow\;\Theta_n(\theta)
=\sinh(n\pi\theta).$$

Superposing,
$$u(s,\theta)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_n\sin(n\pi s)\sinh(n\pi\theta).$$

---

### Determining the coefficients from $u(s,\pi)=1$

Setting $\theta=\pi$:
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_n\sinh(n\pi^2)\sin(n\pi s)=1,\qquad 0<s<1.$$

This is the Fourier sine series of the constant function $1$ on $(0,1)$. Its coe
fficients are
$$b_n\sinh(n\pi^2)=2\int_0^1 1\cdot\sin(n\pi s)\,ds=\frac{2\bigl(1-(-1)^n\bigr)}
{n\pi}.$$

Hence
$$b_n=\frac{2\bigl(1-(-1)^n\bigr)}{n\pi\,\sinh(n\pi^2)}.$$

Only **odd** $n$ contribute. Writing $n=2k+1$ ($k=0,1,2,\dots$), since $1-(-1)^{
2k+1}=2$:

$$u(s,\theta)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{4}{(2k+1)\pi\,\sinh\!\bigl((2k+1)\pi^2\bi
gr)}\,\sin\!\bigl((2k+1)\pi s\bigr)\,\sinh\!\bigl((2k+1)\pi\theta\bigr).$$

---

### Returning to $(r,\theta)$ via $s=\ln r$

$$\boxed{\;u(r,\theta)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{4\,\sin\!\bigl((2k+1)\pi\ln r\bi
gr)\,\sinh\!\bigl((2k+1)\pi\theta\bigr)}{(2k+1)\pi\,\sinh\!\bigl((2k+1)\pi^2\big
r)}\;}$$

---

### Verification of the boundary conditions

- **$r=1$ ($s=0$):** $\sin(0)=0\;\Rightarrow\;u(1,\theta)=0$. ✓
- **$r=e$ ($s=1$):** $\sin((2k+1)\pi)=0\;\Rightarrow\;u(e,\theta)=0$. ✓
- **$\theta=0$:** $\sinh(0)=0\;\Rightarrow\;u(r,0)=0$. ✓
- **$\theta=\pi$:** $\sinh((2k+1)\pi^2)$ cancels, leaving
$$u(r,\pi)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{4}{(2k+1)\pi}\sin\!\bigl((2k+1)\pi\ln r\bigr
),$$
which is precisely the Fourier sine series of $1$ on $0<\ln r<1$, i.e. $u(r,\pi)
=1$. ✓

### Verification of the PDE

Each summand $f_k=\sin((2k+1)\pi s)\sinh((2k+1)\pi\theta)$ satisfies
$$\frac{\partial^2 f_k}{\partial s^2}=-(2k+1)^2\pi^2 f_k,\qquad \frac{\partial^2
 f_k}{\partial\theta^2}=(2k+1)^2\pi^2 f_k,$$
so $\partial_{ss}f_k+\partial_{\theta\theta}f_k=0$. By linearity the series sati
sfies Laplace's equation in $(s,\theta)$, which is equivalent to the original po
lar-coordinates equation. ✓

The series converges absolutely and uniformly on compact subsets of the open rec
tangle (exponential decay from $\sinh((2k+1)\pi^2)$ in the denominator vs. $\sin
h((2k+1)\pi\theta)$ with $\theta<\pi$), justifying termwise differentiation. By
the maximum principle this is the unique solution.
