## Proof

Let a sequence of points be *carioca* if no three consecutive points of the sequ
ence are collinear. We determine the greatest $k$ such that any sequence of $k$
points can be extended (by inserting $2023-k$ new points, keeping the relative o
rder of the originals) to a carioca sequence of $2023$ points.

### Upper bound: $k \le 1349$ (i.e. $k=1350$ is impossible)

Take $1350$ points all lying on a single line $\ell$, given in sequence as $P_1,
\dots,P_{1350}$. Suppose we could add $s = 2023-1350 = 673$ points and arrange a
ll $2023$ points in a carioca sequence preserving the order of the $P_i$.

In the final sequence, mark the $1350$ positions occupied by the collinear point
s $P_i$. Any three *consecutive* positions all occupied by these collinear point
s form a collinear triple, violating carioca. Hence among the $1350$ collinear-p
oint positions, no three are consecutive.

The $673$ added points can only separate consecutive $P_i$'s by being placed in
the $1349$ internal gaps (between $P_i$ and $P_{i+1}$); placing them at the ends
 does not separate anything. A gap is "filled" if at least one added point lies
in it. To avoid three consecutive collinear points, **no two consecutive interna
l gaps may both be empty**. With only $673$ added points, at most $673$ of the $
1349$ gaps are filled, so at least $1349-673 = 676$ gaps are empty. But among $1
349$ gaps arranged in a row, the maximum number of pairwise non-adjacent (empty)
 gaps is $\lceil 1349/2\rceil = 675$. Since $676 > 675$, two consecutive gaps mu
st both be empty — say gaps after $P_i$ and after $P_{i+1}$. Then $P_i, P_{i+1},
 P_{i+2}$ appear consecutively in the final sequence and are collinear, contradi
cting carioca.

Therefore $k=1350$ fails, so $k \le 1349$.

### Lower bound: $k = 1349$ always works

Let $P_1,\dots,P_{1349}$ be any sequence of $1349$ points. We insert $674$ new p
oints $Q_1,\dots,Q_{674}$ as follows: place $Q_j$ immediately after $P_{2j}$, fo
r $j=1,\dots,674$ (i.e. after $P_2,P_4,\dots,P_{1348}$). The resulting sequence
is
$$P_1,\,P_2,\,Q_1,\,P_3,\,P_4,\,Q_2,\,P_5,\,P_6,\,Q_3,\,\dots,\,P_{1347},\,P_{13
48},\,Q_{674},\,P_{1349}.$$
This has $1349+674 = 2023$ points and preserves the order of the $P_i$.

Choose the $Q_j$ in **general position** relative to the $P_i$ and to each other
: no $Q_j$ lies on any line through two of the $P_i$; no three $Q_j$'s are colli
near; and no line through two $Q_j$'s passes through any $P_i$. These are finite
ly many algebraic conditions, so such a placement exists.

Now consider any three consecutive points of the final sequence. The longest run
 of consecutive original points $P_i$ has length $2$ (each pair $P_{2j-1},P_{2j}
$ is followed by $Q_j$), so every three consecutive positions include at least o
ne $Q_j$. By the general-position choice:
- a triple with one $Q_j$ and two $P_i$'s is non-collinear ($Q_j$ is off the lin
e through those two $P_i$'s);
- a triple with two $Q_j$'s and one $P_i$ is non-collinear (the line through the
 two $Q_j$'s avoids every $P_i$);
- a triple of three $Q_j$'s is non-collinear.

Hence no three consecutive points are collinear; the sequence is carioca. Thus a
ny $1349$ points can be extended to a carioca sequence of $2023$ points, so $k \
ge 1349$.

### Conclusion

Combining both bounds, the greatest such $k$ is

$$\boxed{k = 1349}.$$
