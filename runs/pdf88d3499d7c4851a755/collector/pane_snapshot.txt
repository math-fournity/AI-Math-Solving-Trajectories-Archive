## (a) Green's function by the method of images

The forced wave equation on the half-line is

$$u_{tt}=c^{2}u_{xx}+Q(x,t),\qquad x>0,\ t>0,$$

with $u(x,0)=f(x)$, $u_t(x,0)=g(x)$, and the Dirichlet boundary condition $u(0,t
)=h(t)$.

**Free-space Green's function.** On the full line, the retarded (causal) Green's
 function of $L=\partial_t^2-c^2\partial_x^2$ is

$$G_0(x,t;\xi,\tau)=\frac{1}{2c}\,H\!\bigl(c(t-\tau)-|x-\xi|\bigr),$$

where $H$ is the Heaviside step function. It satisfies $LG_0=\delta(x-\xi)\delta
(t-\tau)$ and $G_0=0$ for $t<\tau$.

**Image construction for the Dirichlet condition.** To enforce $G(0,t;\xi,\tau)=
0$ for a source at $\xi>0$, place an opposite-sign image source at $-\xi$ (outsi
de the physical domain). Defining

$$\boxed{\;G(x,t;\xi,\tau)=\frac{1}{2c}\Bigl[H\!\bigl(c(t-\tau)-|x-\xi|\bigr)-H\
!\bigl(c(t-\tau)-|x+\xi|\bigr)\Bigr],\qquad x>0,\ \xi>0\;}$$

we have $G(0,t;\xi,\tau)=\frac{1}{2c}[H(c(t-\tau)-\xi)-H(c(t-\tau)-\xi)]=0$, so
the boundary condition on the Green's function is satisfied. Both terms obey the
 wave equation away from their sources, and the jump condition at $(\xi,\tau)$ g
ives the correct $\delta$-source since the image lies outside the physical domai
n. This is the Dirichlet Green's function on the half-line obtained by the metho
d of images.

## (b) Solution when $Q=0$, $f=0$, $g=0$

With zero forcing and zero initial data, the only driving term is the boundary v
alue $h(t)$. The general representation formula on the half-line (using the Gree
n's function of part (a) plus a boundary term) is

$$u(x,t)=\int_0^\infty\!\Bigl[G_t(x,t;\xi,0)\,f(\xi)-G(x,t;\xi,0)\,g(\xi)\Bigr]\
,d\xi+\int_0^\infty\!\!\int_0^t G(x,t;\xi,\tau)\,Q(\xi,\tau)\,d\tau\,d\xi-c^{2}\
int_0^t G(x,t;0,\tau)\,h(\tau)\,d\tau.$$

(The last term is the boundary contribution; it comes from integrating by parts
in $x$ and using $G(0,t;\xi,\tau)=0$ together with $u(0,\tau)=h(\tau)$. The fact
or $-c^2$ and the minus sign reflect the outward-normal direction at $x=0$.)

With $Q=f=g=0$ only the boundary term survives:

$$u(x,t)=-c^{2}\int_0^t G(x,t;0,\tau)\,h(\tau)\,d\tau.$$

Now evaluate $G(x,t;0,\tau)$ from part (a) with $\xi=0$:

$$G(x,t;0,\tau)=\frac{1}{2c}\Bigl[H\!\bigl(c(t-\tau)-|x|\bigr)-H\!\bigl(c(t-\tau
)-|x|\bigr)\Bigr]=0.$$

This vanishing is expected: the image of a source *at* the boundary coincides wi
th the source itself, so the image construction degenerates. The correct way to
handle the boundary source is to take the limit $\xi\to0^+$ of $G(x,t;\xi,\tau)$
, or equivalently to differentiate the representation with respect to the bounda
ry point. Using the standard half-line formula (obtained by letting $\xi\to0^+$
before cancelling, i.e. using $G(x,t;\xi,\tau)$ with $\xi>0$ and then the bounda
ry integral $\int_0^t \partial_\xi G(x,t;\xi,\tau)|_{\xi=0}\,h(\tau)\,d\tau$ wit
h the appropriate sign), one obtains

$$u(x,t)=-c^{2}\int_0^t\,\partial_\xi G(x,t;\xi,\tau)\big|_{\xi=0}\,h(\tau)\,d\t
au.$$

Compute $\partial_\xi G|_{\xi=0}$. With

$$G=\frac{1}{2c}\Bigl[H(c(t-\tau)-|x-\xi|)-H(c(t-\tau)-|x+\xi|)\Bigr],$$

and $\partial_\xi|x-\xi|=-\operatorname{sgn}(x-\xi)$, $\partial_\xi|x+\xi|=+1$ (
for $\xi>0$, $x+\xi>0$):

$$\partial_\xi G=\frac{1}{2c}\Bigl[\delta(c(t-\tau)-|x-\xi|)\operatorname{sgn}(x
-\xi)-\delta(c(t-\tau)-|x+\xi|)\cdot(-1)\cdot(-1)\Bigr].$$

Evaluating at $\xi=0$ (so $|x-\xi|=|x+\xi|=x$ for $x>0$, $\operatorname{sgn}(x-\
xi)=+1$):

$$\partial_\xi G\big|_{\xi=0}=\frac{1}{2c}\Bigl[\delta(c(t-\tau)-x)\cdot 1-\delt
a(c(t-\tau)-x)\cdot 1\Bigr]\cdot(\text{sign factors}).$$

Carefully: $\partial_\xi[-H(c(t-\tau)-|x+\xi|)]=-\bigl[-\delta(c(t-\tau)-|x+\xi|
)\cdot\partial_\xi|x+\xi|\bigr]=\delta(c(t-\tau)-|x+\xi|)\cdot(+1)$, and at $\xi
=0$ this is $\delta(c(t-\tau)-x)$. The first term: $\partial_\xi H(c(t-\tau)-|x-
\xi|)=\delta(c(t-\tau)-|x-\xi|)\cdot\operatorname{sgn}(x-\xi)$, at $\xi=0$ equal
s $\delta(c(t-\tau)-x)\cdot(+1)$. So

$$\partial_\xi G\big|_{\xi=0}=\frac{1}{2c}\bigl[\delta(c(t-\tau)-x)-\delta(c(t-\
tau)-x)\bigr]=0?$$

The two contributions again cancel at $\xi=0$ exactly. The resolution is that th
e boundary integral must be evaluated as the limit $\xi\to0^+$ of the *full* exp
ression *before* setting $\xi=0$, treating the image term as already separated.
The clean derivation uses d'Alembert directly:

**Direct derivation (d'Alembert + boundary).** On the full line, zero initial da
ta and a source concentrated at the boundary gives a pure right-traveling wave.
The general solution of $u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}$ is $u(x,t)=F(t-x/c)+G(t+x/c)$. Zero i
nitial data on the half-line ($f=g=0$ for $x>0$) forces $F$ and $G$ to vanish fo
r arguments $>0$ coming from the interior, i.e. $G\equiv0$ (it would require ini
tial data at $x>0$) and $F(s)=0$ for $s>0$ from the interior. The boundary condi
tion $u(0,t)=F(t)+G(t)=h(t)$ with $G\equiv0$ gives $F(t)=h(t)$. Hence

$$\boxed{\;u(x,t)=h\!\left(t-\frac{x}{c}\right)H\!\left(t-\frac{x}{c}\right)\;}$
$

where $H$ is the Heaviside function (needed so that $u=0$ before the boundary si
gnal arrives, consistent with zero initial data).

**Verification.** (i) Wave equation: $u(x,t)=h(t-x/c)$ is a function of $t-x/c$,
 hence satisfies $u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}$. (ii) Boundary: $u(0,t)=h(t)H(t)=h(t)$ for $
t>0$. (iii) Initial displacement: for $x>0$, $u(x,0)=h(-x/c)H(-x/c)=0$. (iv) Ini
tial velocity: $u_t(x,0)=h'(-x/c)H(-x/c)+h(-x/c)\delta(-x/c)=0$ for $x>0$. All c
onditions are satisfied.

## (c) When does $h(t)$ influence $u(x_1,t_1)$?

From part (b),

$$u(x_1,t_1)=h\!\left(t_1-\frac{x_1}{c}\right)H\!\left(t_1-\frac{x_1}{c}\right).
$$

Thus $h$ influences $u(x_1,t_1)$ only through its value at the single instant $\
tau=t_1-x_1/c$, and this influence is present precisely when

$$\boxed{\;0\le \tau=t_1-\frac{x_1}{c},\quad\text{i.e.}\quad t_1\ge \frac{x_1}{c
}.\;}$$

Equivalently, the boundary value $h(\tau)$ (with $\tau\ge0$) influences $u(x_1,t
_1)$ for all $\tau$ satisfying

$$0\le \tau \le t_1-\frac{x_1}{c},$$

since the signal emitted at the boundary at time $\tau$ reaches $x_1$ at time $\
tau+x_1/c$, and it must arrive no later than $t_1$.

**Physical interpretation.** Disturbances imposed at the boundary $x=0$ propagat
e into the domain as right-traveling waves at speed $c$. A signal leaving the bo
undary at time $\tau$ needs time $x_1/c$ to travel the distance $x_1$, arriving
at $x_1$ at time $\tau+x_1/c$. Therefore the boundary value $h(\tau)$ can affect
 the field at $(x_1,t_1)$ only if $\tau+x_1/c\le t_1$, i.e. $\tau\le t_1-x_1/c$.
 Before time $t_1=x_1/c$ (the "arrival time" of the first boundary signal), the
point $x_1$ is still undisturbed and $u(x_1,t_1)=0$; this is the causal, finite-
propagation-speed character of the wave equation. In the special homogeneous cas
e of part (b) only the latest admissible boundary value, $h(t_1-x_1/c)$, actuall
y contributes (a single characteristic carries the information), reflecting the
Huygens principle in one spatial dimension.
