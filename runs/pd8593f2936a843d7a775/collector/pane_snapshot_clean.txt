## Proof

Let
$$S = \{(a,b) \in \mathbb{R}^2 \mid a - b \in \mathbb{Q}\}.$$

For each $q \in \mathbb{Q}$, define the line
$$L_q = \{(a,b) \in \mathbb{R}^2 \mid a - b = q\}.$$

Then
$$S = \bigcup_{q \in \mathbb{Q}} L_q.$$

**Each $L_q$ has Lebesgue measure 0.** Indeed, $L_q$ is an affine line in $\math
bb{R}^2$ (a translate of the 1-dimensional subspace $\{(t,t) : t \in \mathbb{R}\
}$). Any affine subspace of dimension $< 2$ has 2-dimensional Lebesgue measure $
0$. Concretely, $L_q$ is contained in the strip $\{(a,b) : q-\varepsilon \le a-b

**$\mathbb{Q}$ is countable**, so $S$ is a countable union of measure-zero sets.

$$m(S) \le \sum_{q \in \mathbb{Q}} m(L_q) = \sum_{q \in \mathbb{Q}} 0 = 0.$$
