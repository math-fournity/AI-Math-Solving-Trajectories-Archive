**Answer: No.**

**Counterexample.** Let $X = \ell^2$, the Banach (Hilbert) space of square-summa
ble sequences with norm $\|x\| = \bigl(\sum_{k=1}^\infty |x_k|^2\bigr)^{1/2}$. L
et $x_n = e_n$, the $n$-th standard basis vector (with $1$ in position $n$ and $
0$ elsewhere).

**Claim 1:** For every bounded linear functional $f$ on $\ell^2$, $f(e_n) \to 0$
.

*Proof.* By the Riesz representation theorem, every bounded linear functional $f
$ on $\ell^2$ has the form $f(x) = \langle x, y\rangle$ for some $y = (y_k) \in
\ell^2$. Then
$$f(e_n) = \langle e_n, y\rangle = y_n.$$
Since $y \in \ell^2$, we have $\sum_{k=1}^\infty |y_k|^2 < \infty$, which forces
 $|y_k|^2 \to 0$, hence $y_n \to 0$. Therefore $f(e_n) \to 0$ for every bounded
linear functional $f$. $\square$

**Claim 2:** $e_n \not\to 0$ in norm.

*Proof.* $\|e_n\| = 1$ for every $n$, so $\|e_n - 0\| = 1 \not\to 0$. $\square$

**Conclusion.** The sequence $\{e_n\}$ in $\ell^2$ satisfies $f(e_n) \to 0$ for
every bounded linear functional $f$ on $\ell^2$, yet $\|e_n\| = 1$ for all $n$,
so $e_n$ does not converge to $0$ in the norm topology. Hence the implication fa
ils in general.

**Remark.** The hypothesis "$f(x_n)\to 0$ for every bounded linear functional $f
$" is precisely the statement that $x_n \to 0$ *weakly*. This counterexample sho
ws that weak convergence does not imply norm (strong) convergence. (The converse
 is immediate from $|f(x_n)| \le \|f\|\,\|x_n\|$.)
