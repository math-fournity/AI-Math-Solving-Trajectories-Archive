## Solution

**Step 1: Recognize the angle.** Since $\frac{\pi k}{2002} = \frac{2\pi k}{4004}
$, setting $n = 4004$ we have

$$S = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \gcd(k, n)\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi k}{n}\right) = \operatorna
me{Re}\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n} \gcd(k,n)\, e^{2\pi i k/n}\right).$$

**Step 2: Apply the divisor-sum identity.** Using the classical identity $\gcd(k
,n) = \sum_{d \mid \gcd(k,n)} \varphi(d)$ (equivalently $\sum_{d\mid m}\varphi(d
)=m$ with $m=\gcd(k,n)$), we swap the order of summation:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} \gcd(k,n)\, e^{2\pi i k/n} = \sum_{d\mid n} \varphi(d)\!\!\sum_
{\substack{1\le k\le n\\ d\mid k}} e^{2\pi i k/n}.$$

Writing $k = dj$ with $j = 1,\dots, n/d$, the inner sum becomes

$$\sum_{j=1}^{n/d} e^{2\pi i\, dj/n} = \sum_{j=1}^{n/d} e^{2\pi i\, j/(n/d)}.$$

This is the sum of all $(n/d)$-th roots of unity, which equals $0$ when $n/d > 1
$, and equals $1$ when $n/d = 1$ (i.e. $d = n$).

**Step 3: Conclude.** Only the term $d = n$ survives, giving

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} \gcd(k,n)\, e^{2\pi i k/n} = \varphi(n)\cdot 1 = \varphi(n),$$

which is real. Hence $S = \varphi(4004)$.

$$\varphi(4004) = 4004\left(1-\tfrac12\right)\left(1-\tfrac17\right)\left(1-\tfr

- $2002 \cdot \tfrac67 = 1716$,
- $1716 \cdot \tfrac{10}{11} = 1560$,
