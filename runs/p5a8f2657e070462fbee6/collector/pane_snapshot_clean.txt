## Proof

Let $X_n$ be defined by $P(X_n = 2^n) = 2^{-n}$ and $P(X_n = 0) = 1 - 2^{-n}$.

### Convergence in distribution: YES, with $Z = 0$

The CDF of $X_n$ is
$$F_{X_n}(x) = \begin{cases} 0, & x < 0, \\ 1 - 2^{-n}, & 0 \le x < 2^n, \\ 1, &
 x \ge 2^n. \end{cases}$$

For every **fixed** $x$: if $x < 0$, then $F_{X_n}(x) = 0$ for all $n$. If $x \g
e 0$, then for all sufficiently large $n$ we have $2^n > x$, so $F_{X_n}(x) = 1
- 2^{-n} \to 1$. Thus
$$F_{X_n}(x) \to F(x) := \begin{cases} 0, & x < 0, \\ 1, & x \ge 0, \end{cases}$
$
which is the CDF of the degenerate random variable $Z \equiv 0$. The set of cont
inuity points of $F$ is $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}$, and convergence holds at eve
ry such point. Hence $X_n \xrightarrow{d} Z = 0$.

### Convergence in probability: YES, with $Z = 0$

For any $\varepsilon > 0$,
$$P(|X_n - 0| > \varepsilon) = P(X_n = 2^n) = 2^{-n} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.
$$
Therefore $X_n \xrightarrow{P} 0$.

### Convergence in $L_2$: NO such $Z$ exists

First compute the second moment:
$$E[X_n^2] = (2^n)^2 \cdot 2^{-n} + 0^2 \cdot (1-2^{-n}) = 2^{2n}\cdot 2^{-n} =
2^n \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} \infty.$$

Suppose, for contradiction, that $X_n \xrightarrow{L_2} Z$ for some random varia
ble $Z$. Then $X_n \xrightarrow{L_2} Z$ implies $X_n \xrightarrow{P} Z$. But lim
its in probability are unique up to a.s. equality, and we have already shown $X_
n \xrightarrow{P} 0$. Hence $Z = 0$ a.s., so $E[Z^2] = 0$.

But $L_2$-convergence to $Z = 0$ requires $E[X_n^2] \to E[0^2] = 0$, whereas $E[
X_n^2] = 2^n \to \infty$. This is a contradiction. Therefore no random variable
$Z$ satisfies $X_n \xrightarrow{L_2} Z$.

### Summary

There exists a random variable $Z$ (namely $Z \equiv 0$) such that $X_n \to Z$ *
*in distribution** and **in probability**, but there is **no** random variable $
Z$ such that $X_n \to Z$ in $L_2$ norm.
