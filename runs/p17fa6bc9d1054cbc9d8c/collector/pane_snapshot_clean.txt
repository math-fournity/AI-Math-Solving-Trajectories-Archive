## Proof

**Interpretation.** A partition of $\mathbb{N}$ is a collection of nonempty, pai
rwise disjoint sets whose union is $\mathbb{N}$. Observe two automatic facts:

- Every part is a subset of $\mathbb{N}$, hence **countable** (finite or countab
ly infinite).
- Every partition of $\mathbb{N}$ is itself **countable** (has at most countably
 many parts): each nonempty part contains a least element (well-ordering of $\ma
thbb{N}$), and distinct parts have distinct least elements, giving an injection
from the set of parts into $\mathbb{N}$.

Thus *every* partition of $\mathbb{N}$ is automatically a "countable partition i
nto countable sets." (Even under the stricter reading where "countable" means *c
ountably infinite*, the answer is unchanged — see the lower-bound construction b
elow, which uses only infinite parts.)

So the problem asks: **how many partitions of $\mathbb{N}$ are there?**

---

**Upper bound: at most $2^{\aleph_0}$.**

A partition of $\mathbb{N}$ is the same thing as an equivalence relation on $\ma
thbb{N}$, which is a subset of $\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}$. Since $\mathbb{N}\t
imes\mathbb{N}$ is countable, the number of subsets of $\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{
N}$ is $2^{\aleph_0}$. Hence the number of partitions is at most $2^{\aleph_0}$.

---

**Lower bound: at least $2^{\aleph_0}$.**

We exhibit $2^{\aleph_0}$ distinct partitions of $\mathbb{N}$, even using only p
artitions into **two countably infinite parts** (so the bound holds under either
 interpretation of "countable").

For each subset $A\subseteq\mathbb{N}$ with both $A$ and $\mathbb{N}\setminus A$
 infinite, form the two-block partition
$$\mathcal{P}_A = \{A,\;\mathbb{N}\setminus A\}.$$
Both blocks are countably infinite by assumption. Two such subsets $A,A'$ yield
the same partition iff $A'=A$ or $A'=\mathbb{N}\setminus A$, so each partition i
s counted at most twice.

The number of subsets $A\subseteq\mathbb{N}$ that are infinite with infinite com
plement is $2^{\aleph_0}$: indeed, the collection of subsets that are *finite or
 cofinite* has cardinality only $\aleph_0$ (countably many finite sets and count
ably many cofinite sets), while the total number of subsets of $\mathbb{N}$ is $
2^{\aleph_0}$. Removing $\aleph_0$ from $2^{\aleph_0}$ leaves $2^{\aleph_0}$.

Therefore the number of distinct partitions $\mathcal{P}_A$ is $2^{\aleph_0}/2 =
 2^{\aleph_0}$. This gives at least $2^{\aleph_0}$ partitions of $\mathbb{N}$.

---

**Conclusion.**

The number of partitions of $\mathbb{N}$ is bounded above and below by $2^{\alep
h_0}$, so it equals

$$\boxed{2^{\aleph_0}}$$

the cardinality of the continuum $\mathfrak{c}$.
