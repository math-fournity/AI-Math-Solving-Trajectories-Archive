No, the Cantor set is not homeomorphic to the unit interval.

**Proof.**

Let $C \subseteq [0,1]$ denote the standard middle-thirds Cantor set, and let $I
 = [0,1]$ be the unit interval. We show $C \not\cong I$ by exhibiting a topologi
cal property preserved by homeomorphism that holds for one but not the other.

**Key property: connectedness.**

The unit interval $I = [0,1]$ is connected. (It is a convex subset of $\mathbb{R
}$, and every convex subset of $\mathbb{R}$ is connected.)

The Cantor set $C$ is **totally disconnected**: every connected component is a s
ingle point. To see this, recall that $C$ is constructed as $C = \bigcap_{n=0}^{
\infty} C_n$, where $C_0 = [0,1]$ and each $C_n$ is a union of $2^n$ closed inte
rvals of length $3^{-n}$, obtained by deleting the open middle third of every in
terval in $C_{n-1}$.

Take any two distinct points $x, y \in C$ with $x < y$. Choose $n$ large enough
that $3^{-n} < y - x$. Then $x$ and $y$ cannot lie in the same component interva
l of $C_n$ (each such interval has length $3^{-n} < y-x$). Since $C \subseteq C_
n$, the points $x$ and $y$ lie in different component intervals of $C_n$, which
are separated by an open gap (a deleted middle third). This gap is an open inter
val disjoint from $C$, so $C$ can be separated as

$$C = \bigl(C \cap (-\infty, a)\bigr) \cup \bigl(C \cap (b, +\infty)\bigr)$$

for suitable $a, b$ with the gap $(a,b)$ lying between $x$ and $y$. Both pieces
are nonempty (containing $x$ and $y$ respectively), open in $C$, and disjoint. H
ence no two distinct points of $C$ lie in the same connected component, so $C$ i
s totally disconnected.

**Conclusion.** Connectedness is a topological invariant: any homeomorphism $f:
I \to C$ would map the connected space $I$ to a connected space, but $C$ is tota
lly disconnected (hence disconnected, since $C$ is uncountable and thus not a si
ngle point). This is a contradiction. Therefore no homeomorphism exists between
$C$ and $I$.

**Answer: No.**
