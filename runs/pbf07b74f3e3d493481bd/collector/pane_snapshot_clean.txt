## Proof

The parallel propagator \( P^\mu_{\ \rho}(\lambda,\lambda_0) \) is defined by th
e relation
\[
V^\mu(\lambda)=P^\mu_{\ \rho}(\lambda,\lambda_0)\,V^\rho(\lambda_0),
\]
i.e. it maps the vector at the initial point \(\gamma(\lambda_0)\) to the parall
el-transported vector at \(\gamma(\lambda)\).

**Initial condition.** At \(\lambda=\lambda_0\) the transport is trivial, so
\[
P^\mu_{\ \rho}(\lambda_0,\lambda_0)=\delta^\mu_{\ \rho}.
\]

**Differential equation for \(P\).** Differentiate the defining relation and use
 the transport equation \(\frac{dV^\mu}{d\lambda}=A^\mu_{\ \rho}(\lambda)V^\rho\
):
\[
\frac{dP^\mu_{\ \rho}}{d\lambda}\,V^\rho(\lambda_0)
=\frac{dV^\mu}{d\lambda}
=A^\mu_{\ \sigma}(\lambda)\,V^\sigma(\lambda)
=A^\mu_{\ \sigma}(\lambda)\,P^\sigma_{\ \rho}(\lambda,\lambda_0)\,V^\rho(\lambda
_0).
\]
Since this holds for arbitrary \(V^\rho(\lambda_0)\),
\[
\frac{dP^\mu_{\ \rho}}{d\lambda}=A^\mu_{\ \sigma}(\lambda)\,P^\sigma_{\ \rho}(\l
ambda,\lambda_0),
\qquad P^\mu_{\ \rho}(\lambda_0,\lambda_0)=\delta^\mu_{\ \rho}.
\]

**Matrix form.** Treating \(P\) and \(A\) as matrices with indices \((\mu,\rho)\
), this is
\[
\frac{dP}{d\lambda}=A(\lambda)\,P(\lambda),\qquad P(\lambda_0)=I.
\]

**Solution via path-ordered exponential.** When \(A(\lambda)\) commutes with its
elf at different parameter values, the solution would be the ordinary exponentia
l \(\exp\!\big(\int_{\lambda_0}^{\lambda}A(\lambda')\,d\lambda'\big)\). In gener
al the matrices \(A(\lambda')\) at different \(\lambda'\) do **not** commute, so
 the correct solution is the **path-ordered exponential**:
\[
\boxed{\;P^\mu_{\ \rho}(\lambda,\lambda_0)=\Big[\mathcal{P}\exp\!\Big(\int_{\lam
bda_0}^{\lambda}A(\lambda')\,d\lambda'\Big)\Big]^\mu_{\ \rho}\;}
\]
where \(\mathcal{P}\) denotes path ordering — factors with larger \(\lambda'\) p
laced to the left — defined by the Dyson series
\[
\mathcal{P}\exp\!\Big(\int_{\lambda_0}^{\lambda}A(\lambda')\,d\lambda'\Big)
=I+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\lambda_0}^{\lambda}d\lambda_1\int_{\lambda_0}^{\lam
bda_1}d\lambda_2\cdots\int_{\lambda_0}^{\lambda_{n-1}}d\lambda_n\;
A(\lambda_1)A(\lambda_2)\cdots A(\lambda_n).
\]

**Verification.** Differentiating the Dyson series term-by-term: the \(n=0\) ter
m \(I\) is constant; the \(n\geq 1\) terms, upon differentiating the upper limit
 of the outermost integral, give \(A(\lambda)\) multiplying the \((n-1)\)-fold o
rdered integral, which is exactly \(A(\lambda)\) times the full series. Hence \(
\frac{dP}{d\lambda}=A(\lambda)P\). At \(\lambda=\lambda_0\) every integral vanis
hes, leaving \(P=I=\delta^\mu_{\ \rho}\). Both the differential equation and the
 initial condition are satisfied.

**Substituting \(A\).** Using \(A^\mu_{\ \rho}(\lambda)=-\Gamma^\mu_{\ \sigma\rh
o}\frac{dx^\sigma}{d\lambda}\),
\[
P^\mu_{\ \rho}(\lambda,\lambda_0)
=\Big[\mathcal{P}\exp\!\Big(-\int_{\lambda_0}^{\lambda}\Gamma^\mu_{\ \sigma\rho}
(x(\lambda'))\,\frac{dx^\sigma}{d\lambda'}\,d\lambda'\Big)\Big]^\mu_{\ \rho}
=\Big[\mathcal{P}\exp\!\Big(-\int_{\gamma:\lambda_0\to\lambda}\Gamma^\mu_{\ \sig
ma\rho}\,dx^\sigma\Big)\Big]^\mu_{\ \rho},
\]
which is the standard path-ordered exponential form of the parallel propagator,
with the path-ordering \(\mathcal{P}\) required precisely because the connection
 matrices at different points along \(\gamma\) generally do not commute.
