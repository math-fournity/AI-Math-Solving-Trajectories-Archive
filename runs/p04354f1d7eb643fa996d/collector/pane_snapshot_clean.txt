## Solution

We evaluate whether

$$I = \int_{\mathbb{R}^2} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^4}}\, dx\, dy$$

is finite.

**Claim.** The integral diverges to $+\infty$; it is **not finite**.

**Proof.** The integrand $f(x,y) = (x^2 + y^4)^{-1/2}$ is non-negative and measu
rable on $\mathbb{R}^2$. By Tonelli's theorem, we may interchange the order of i
ntegration, and the double integral (as a Lebesgue integral of a non-negative fu
nction) equals the iterated integral:

$$I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2
 + y^4}}\right) dy.$$

It suffices to show that the inner integral is $+\infty$ for every fixed $y \neq
 0$.

Fix $y \neq 0$ and set $a = y^2 > 0$. For all $x \geq a$ we have $x^2 \geq a^2 =
 y^4$, hence

$$x^2 + y^4 \leq x^2 + x^2 = 2x^2,$$

so

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^4}} \geq \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\, x}.$$

Therefore

$$\int_{a}^{\infty} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^4}} \;\geq\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_
{a}^{\infty} \frac{dx}{x} \;=\; +\infty.$$

Thus for every $y \neq 0$,

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^4}} = +\infty.$$

Since the inner integral is $+\infty$ on a set of positive (in fact infinite) me
asure in $y$, Tonelli's theorem gives

$$I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(+\infty)\, dy = +\infty.$$

**Conclusion.** The integral $\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^2} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^
2 + y^4}}\, dx\, dy$ is **not finite**; it diverges to $+\infty$.
