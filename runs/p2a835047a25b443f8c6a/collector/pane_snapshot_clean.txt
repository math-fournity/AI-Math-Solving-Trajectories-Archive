Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether spectral radius continuity
holds when the limit element has discrete spectrum.

## Proof

The statement is **true**.

**Key facts used:**

**Fact 1 (Upper semicontinuity of the spectrum).** For any $x \in A$ and any ope
n set $U \supseteq \sigma(x)$, there exists $\delta > 0$ such that $\|y - x\| <
\delta \implies \sigma(y) \subseteq U$.

*Proof sketch:* On the compact set $K = \mathbb{C} \setminus U$ (which avoids $\
sigma(x)$), the resolvent $(z - x)^{-1}$ exists and is bounded. For $y$ close to
 $x$, a Neumann series shows $(z - y)^{-1}$ also exists on $K$, so $\sigma(y) \c
ap K = \emptyset$.

**Corollary (Upper semicontinuity of spectral radius).** If $x_n \to x$, then $\
limsup_{n \to \infty} \rho(x_n) \leq \rho(x)$.

*Proof:* Given $\varepsilon > 0$, take $U = \{z : \operatorname{dist}(z, \sigma(
x)) < \varepsilon\}$. For large $n$, $\sigma(x_n) \subseteq U$, so $\rho(x_n) \l
eq \rho(x) + \varepsilon$.

**Fact 2 (Persistence of isolated spectral points).** If $\lambda$ is an isolate
d point of $\sigma(x)$ and $x_n \to x$, then there exist $\lambda_n \in \sigma(x
_n)$ with $\lambda_n \to \lambda$.

*Proof:* Since $\lambda$ is isolated, choose $r > 0$ so that the circle $\gamma
= \{z : |z - \lambda| = r\}$ is disjoint from $\sigma(x)$. The Riesz projection
$$P = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \int_\gamma (z - x)^{-1}\,dz$$
is a nonzero idempotent (nonzero because $\lambda \in \sigma(x)$; if $P = 0$, th
e resolvent would extend analytically inside $\gamma$, contradicting $\lambda \i
n \sigma(x)$).

By Fact 1, for $n$ sufficiently large, $\sigma(x_n) \cap \gamma = \emptyset$, so
 the Riesz projection
$$P_n = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \int_\gamma (z - x_n)^{-1}\,dz$$
is well-defined. Since $(z - x_n)^{-1} \to (z - x)^{-1}$ uniformly on $\gamma$ (
continuity of inversion on the open set of invertible elements, applied pointwis
e on $\gamma$ with a uniform bound), we have $P_n \to P$.

Since $P \neq 0$ and $P_n \to P$, we have $P_n \neq 0$ for all large $n$. But $P
_n \neq 0$ implies $\sigma(x_n) \cap \operatorname{int}(\gamma) \neq \emptyset$
(an empty spectrum inside $\gamma$ would force $P_n = 0$). Thus for each $\varep
silon > 0$ and all sufficiently large $n$, $\sigma(x_n) \cap B(\lambda, \varepsi
lon) \neq \emptyset$. Selecting $\lambda_n \in \sigma(x_n) \cap B(\lambda, \vare
psilon_n)$ with $\varepsilon_n \to 0$ gives $\lambda_n \to \lambda$. $\square$

**Main argument.**

Since $\sigma(x)$ is discrete and compact (the spectrum of any element in a unit
al Banach algebra is a nonempty compact subset of $\mathbb{C}$), every point of
$\sigma(x)$ is isolated. (A compact discrete set is finite, so in particular eve
ry point is isolated.)

By compactness of $\sigma(x)$, the spectral radius is attained: there exists $\l
ambda^* \in \sigma(x)$ with $|\lambda^*| = \rho(x)$.

Since $\sigma(x)$ is discrete, $\lambda^*$ is an isolated point of $\sigma(x)$.
By Fact 2, there exist $\lambda_n^* \in \sigma(x_n)$ with $\lambda_n^* \to \lamb
da^*$. Therefore:
$$\rho(x_n) \geq |\lambda_n^*| \xrightarrow{n \to \infty} |\lambda^*| = \rho(x),
$$
which gives $\liminf_{n \to \infty} \rho(x_n) \geq \rho(x)$.

Combining with the upper semicontinuity (Corollary to Fact 1):
$$\rho(x) \leq \liminf_{n \to \infty} \rho(x_n) \leq \limsup_{n \to \infty} \rho
(x_n) \leq \rho(x).$$

Hence $\lim_{n \to \infty} \rho(x_n) = \rho(x)$. $\blacksquare$
