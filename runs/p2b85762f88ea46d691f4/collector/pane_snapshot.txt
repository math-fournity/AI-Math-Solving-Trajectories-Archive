Looking at this problem, I need to find the area a probe can reach when tethered
 by a cable of length $L = 10\pi$ to a point on a circular obstacle of radius $R
 = 10$, where the cable can wrap around the obstacle.

## Setup

Place the laboratory as a disk of radius $R = 10$ centered at the origin $O$. Th
e docking point is $P = (R, 0)$. The cable length is $L = \pi R$.

The reachable region has three components:
1. **Non-wrapping region**: points reachable by a straight cable from $P$
2. **Two wrapping regions** (upper/lower): points reachable when the cable wraps
 around the hull

## Non-Wrapping Region

A point $Q$ outside the laboratory is reachable without wrapping if the segment
$PQ$ doesn't enter the laboratory's interior. Parametrizing the segment as $P +
t(Q - P)$, the condition $|P + t(Q-P)|^2 > R^2$ for all $t \in (0,1]$ reduces to
 $P \cdot (Q - P) \geq 0$, i.e., $Q_x \geq R$.

So the non-wrapping region is $\{Q : |Q - P| \leq L,\; Q_x \geq R\}$. Since the
disk $|Q-P| \leq L$ is centered at $(R, 0)$, the half-plane $x \geq R$ bisects i
t exactly:

$$A_{\text{non-wrap}} = \frac{\pi L^2}{2} = \frac{\pi^3 R^2}{2}$$

## Wrapping Region (One Side)

When the cable wraps counterclockwise by angle $\theta \in [0, \pi]$, it contact
s the circle along arc length $R\theta$, leaving free length $r(\theta) = L - R\
theta = R(\pi - \theta)$. The free cable extends tangentially from the contact p
oint $Q(\theta) = (R\cos\theta, R\sin\theta)$.

The maximum wrap is $\theta = \pi$ (free length $\to 0$), reaching the point dia
metrically opposite $P$.

**Outer boundary (involute):** The free end traces
$$E(\theta) = \bigl(R\cos\theta - R(\pi-\theta)\sin\theta,\; R\sin\theta + R(\pi
-\theta)\cos\theta\bigr)$$

**Computing the area via Green's theorem.** The wrapping region is bounded by th
e involute ($\theta: 0 \to \pi$) and the upper semicircle ($\theta: \pi \to 0$).
 Using $\oint \frac{1}{2}(x\,dy - y\,dx)$:

**Involute part:** With $x'(\theta) = -R(\pi-\theta)\cos\theta$ and $y'(\theta)
= -R(\pi-\theta)\sin\theta$:

$$x\,y' - y\,x' = R^2(\pi-\theta)^2(\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta) = R^2(\pi-\thet
a)^2$$

$$\frac{1}{2}\int_0^\pi R^2(\pi-\theta)^2\,d\theta = \frac{R^2\pi^3}{6}$$

**Semicircle part:** On the circle $x = R\cos\theta,\; y = R\sin\theta$, so $x\,
dy - y\,dx = R^2\,d\theta$:

$$\frac{1}{2}\int_\pi^0 R^2\,d\theta = -\frac{R^2\pi}{2}$$

**Wrapping area (one side):**
$$A_{\text{wrap}} = \frac{R^2\pi^3}{6} - \frac{R^2\pi}{2} = \frac{R^2\pi(\pi^2 -
 3)}{6}$$

**Verification:** The sector-sweep integral $\int_0^\pi \frac{1}{2}r(\theta)^2\,
d\theta = \frac{R^2\pi^3}{6}$ counts the wrapping region *plus* the semicircle a
rea $\frac{\pi R^2}{2}$. Subtracting gives the same result. ✓

## No Overlap

- Non-wrapping region: $Q_x \geq R$; wrapping regions: $Q_x \leq R$ — they share
 only the boundary line $x = R$.
- Upper and lower wrapping regions are mirror images meeting only at $(-R, 0)$.

## Total Area

$$A = \frac{\pi^3 R^2}{2} + 2 \cdot \frac{R^2\pi(\pi^2 - 3)}{6} = R^2\pi\!\left[
\frac{\pi^2}{2} + \frac{\pi^2 - 3}{3}\right] = \frac{R^2\pi(5\pi^2 - 6)}{6}$$

Substituting $R = 10$:

$$\boxed{A = \frac{100\pi(5\pi^2 - 6)}{6} = \frac{50\pi(5\pi^2 - 6)}{3} \text{ s
quare meters}}$$

Numerically: $A \approx 2269.8 \text{ m}^2$, which is less than the full disk ar
ea $\pi L^2 \approx 3100.6 \text{ m}^2$ and greater than the half-disk $\approx
1550.3 \text{ m}^2$, as expected.
