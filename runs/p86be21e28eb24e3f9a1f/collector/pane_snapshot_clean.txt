Solution

Answer: Yes. The group $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbb{N})$ of all permutations of $
\mathbb{N}$ contains a subgroup isomorphic to $SO(2)$.

Proof

We identify $SO(2)$ with the circle group $S^1 = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ (both
are the group of planar rotations). It suffices to exhibit a faithful action of
$S^1$ on a countable set $X$, since then $S^1$ embeds into $\operatorname{Sym}
(X) \cong \operatorname{Sym}(\mathbb{N})$.

Step 1. Structure of $S^1$ as an abelian group

$S^1 = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ is a divisible abelian group. Divisible abelian
groups split as a direct sum of indecomposable divisible groups, namely copies
of $\mathbb{Q}$ and the Prüfer groups $\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$.

  • Torsion part. The torsion subgroup of $S^1$ is $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} =
  \bigoplus_p \mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$, which is countable. Call it $T$.
  • Torsion-free part. The quotient $S^1/T \cong \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}$ is a $
  \mathbb{Q}$-vector space of dimension $\mathfrak{c} = 2^{\aleph_0}$. Since
  $S^1$ is divisible, the torsion subgroup splits, so $$S^1 ;\cong; T \oplus V,
  \qquad T = \bigoplus_p \mathbb{Z}(p^\infty),\quad V \cong \mathbb{Q}
  ^{(\mathfrak{c})}.$$

Step 2. A countable family of countable-index subgroups of $V$ with trivial
intersection

Both $V \cong \mathbb{Q}^{(\mathfrak{c})}$ (direct sum, finite support) and $
\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}} \mathbb{Q}$ (countable product) are $\mathbb{Q}$-vector
spaces of cardinality $\mathfrak{c}$, hence both have $\mathbb{Q}$-dimension $
\mathfrak{c}$. Therefore $$V ;\cong; \prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}} \mathbb{Q}.$$ Fix
such an isomorphism and let $\pi_n : V \to \mathbb{Q}$ be the $n$-th coordinate
projection, with kernel $K_n = \ker \pi_n$. Then:

  • $V/K_n \cong \mathbb{Q}$, which is countable;
  • $\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}} K_n = {0}$, since a sequence in $\prod \mathbb{Q}$
  that is zero in every coordinate is zero.

Step 3. The faithful action on a countable set

Let $$X ;=; T ;\sqcup; \bigsqcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}} V/K_n.$$ This set is
countable: $T$ is countable, each $V/K_n \cong \mathbb{Q}$ is countable, and we
take a countable union.

Define an action of $S^1 \cong T \oplus V$ on $X$ as follows. For $(t, v) \in T
\oplus V$:

  • On the $T$-component: $(t,v)$ acts on $T$ by left translation $x \mapsto t +
  x$.
  • On each $V/K_n$-component: $(t,v)$ acts by left translation $[w] \mapsto [v
  + w]$.

These are well-defined group actions (translation on coset spaces).

Step 4. Faithfulness

Suppose $(t, v) \in T \oplus V$ acts trivially on all of $X$.

  • Acting trivially on the $T$-orbit (where the action is the regular/free
  action of $T$ on itself) forces $t = 0$.
  • Acting trivially on every $V/K_n$ forces $v \in K_n$ for all $n$, i.e. $v
  \in \bigcap_n K_n = {0}$.

Hence the kernel of the action is trivial, and the action is faithful.

Step 5. Conclusion

A faithful action of $S^1 \cong SO(2)$ on the countable set $X$ yields an
injective homomorphism $$SO(2) ;\hookrightarrow; \operatorname{Sym}(X) ;\cong;
\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbb{N}).$$

Therefore $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbb{N})$ contains a subgroup isomorphic to
$SO(2)$. $\blacksquare$
