## Proof

Let $\ell^2$ denote the Hilbert space of square-summable sequences $x = \{x_n\}_
{n=0}^\infty$ over $\mathbb{K}$ (where $\mathbb{K} = \mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$
), with inner product $\langle x, y\rangle = \sum_{n=0}^\infty x_n \overline{y_n
}$ and norm $\|x\|_2 = \left(\sum_{n=0}^\infty |x_n|^2\right)^{1/2}$.

Define the linear functional
$$L: \ell^2 \to \mathbb{K}, \qquad L(x) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{x_n}{\sqrt{2^n
}}.$$

**Step 1: Well-definedness and boundedness.** Consider the sequence $a = \{a_n\}
_{n=0}^\infty$ with $a_n = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2^n}} = 2^{-n/2}$. We compute its $\el
l^2$-norm:
$$\|a\|_2^2 = \sum_{n=0}^\infty |a_n|^2 = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{2^n} = \fra
c{1}{1 - \frac{1}{2}} = 2.$$
Hence $a \in \ell^2$ with $\|a\|_2 = \sqrt{2}$.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, for every $x \in \ell^2$:
$$|L(x)| = \left|\sum_{n=0}^\infty x_n \, \overline{\overline{a_n}}\right| = \le
ft|\sum_{n=0}^\infty x_n \overline{a_n}\right| = |\langle x, a\rangle| \le \|x\|
_2 \, \|a\|_2 = \sqrt{2}\,\|x\|_2.$$
(When $\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}$, conjugation is trivial and the same bound holds.)
 Therefore $L$ is bounded with
$$\|L\| \le \sqrt{2}.$$

**Step 2: Attainment of the bound.** Take $x = a = \{2^{-n/2}\}_{n=0}^\infty$, i
.e., $x_n = a_n = 2^{-n/2}$. Then $x \in \ell^2$ with $\|x\|_2 = \sqrt{2}$, and
$$L(x) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{2^{-n/2}}{\sqrt{2^n}} = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac
{2^{-n/2}}{2^{n/2}} = \sum_{n=0}^\infty 2^{-n} = 2.$$
Therefore
$$|L(x)| = 2 = \sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{2} = \sqrt{2}\,\|x\|_2,$$
which shows the upper bound is achieved, giving
$$\|L\| \ge \sqrt{2}.$$

**Conclusion.** Combining both inequalities,
$$\boxed{\|L\| = \sqrt{2}}.$$
