# Problem

In the high-tech logistics hub of Neo-Zion, two central processing units, Model M and Model N, are assigned to encrypt data packets. The efficiency of these units is determined by their positive integer power settings, $m$ and $n$, respectively. 

The security strength of a generated encryption key is calculated based on the product of two specific "Mersenne-type" bit-lengths: $(2^m - 1)$ and $(2^n - 1)$. 

A central mainframe operates with a master security protocol of magnitude $2^{10!} - 1$. For a specific synchronization task, the combined security strength of the two units, represented by the product $(2^m - 1)(2^n - 1)$, must be a perfect divisor of the mainframe’s protocol magnitude $2^{10!} - 1$.

An "ordered configuration" is defined by the specific power settings $(m, n)$ assigned to the units. How many such ordered pairs $(m, n)$ of positive integers exist that satisfy this synchronization requirement?
