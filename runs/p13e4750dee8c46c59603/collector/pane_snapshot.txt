## Proof

Let $R := S(I - \lambda S)^{-1}$. By hypothesis, $R$ is a bounded (two-sided) in
verse of $T - \lambda I$, so

$$(T - \lambda I)\,R = I \qquad \text{and} \qquad R\,(T - \lambda I) = I.$$

### Step 1: Establish $TS = I$.

From the first identity $(T - \lambda I)\,S(I - \lambda S)^{-1} = I$, compose on
 the right with $(I - \lambda S)$:

$$(T - \lambda I)\,S = I - \lambda S.$$

Expanding the left side: $TS - \lambda S = I - \lambda S$, and canceling $-\lamb
da S$ from both sides yields

$$TS = I.$$

### Step 2: Establish $ST = I$.

From the second identity $S(I - \lambda S)^{-1}(T - \lambda I) = I$, compose on
the left with $(I - \lambda S)$:

$$S(T - \lambda I) = I - \lambda S.$$

Expanding: $ST - \lambda S = I - \lambda S$, and canceling gives

$$ST = I.$$

### Step 3: Verify $S$ is bounded (so it is a *bounded* inverse of $T$).

From Steps 1–2, $S = T^{-1}$ as a two-sided inverse. Write $T = (T - \lambda I)
+ \lambda I = R^{-1} + \lambda I$ and factor:

$$T = R^{-1}(I + \lambda R).$$

Since $T$ is bijective (it has inverse $S$) and $R^{-1} = T - \lambda I$ is bije
ctive from $\mathscr{D}(T)$ onto $H$, the bounded operator $I + \lambda R : H \t
o H$ must be bijective. By the **Bounded Inverse Theorem** (a bounded bijection
on a Banach space has a bounded inverse), $(I + \lambda R)^{-1}$ is bounded. The
refore

$$S = T^{-1} = (I + \lambda R)^{-1} R$$

is a product of bounded operators, hence bounded.

(The case $\lambda = 0$ is trivial: then $R = S$ is directly a bounded inverse o
f $T$ by hypothesis.)

### Conclusion

The algebraic identities $TS = I$ and $ST = I$ follow directly from the hypothes
is by composing with $(I - \lambda S)$, and the Bounded Inverse Theorem confirms
 $S$ is bounded. Therefore **yes**, $S$ is an inverse for $T$ (indeed, a bounded
 inverse).
