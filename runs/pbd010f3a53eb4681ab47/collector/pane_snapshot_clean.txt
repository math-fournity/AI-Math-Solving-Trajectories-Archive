## Proof

We wish to find the residue of $g(z) = \dfrac{\tan z\,\tanh z}{z^{5}}$ at $z=0$,
 i.e. the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ in the Laurent expansion of $g$ about $0$. Equ
ivalently, this is the coefficient of $z^{4}$ in the Taylor expansion of $\tan z
\,\tanh z$ (since dividing by $z^{5}$ lowers every exponent by $5$, and we need
the exponent to become $-1$).

**Step 1: Parity observation.** Both $\tan z$ and $\tanh z$ are odd functions, s
o their product $\tan z\,\tanh z$ is **even**. Hence its Taylor series contains
only even powers of $z$:
$$\tan z\,\tanh z = a_2 z^{2} + a_4 z^{4} + a_6 z^{6} + \cdots$$

**Step 2: Compute the relevant coefficients.** Using the standard expansions
$$\tan z = z + \tfrac{z^{3}}{3} + \tfrac{2z^{5}}{15} + \cdots, \qquad \tanh z =
z - \tfrac{z^{3}}{3} + \tfrac{2z^{5}}{15} - \cdots$$

(the second follows from $\tanh z = -i\tan(iz)$), we multiply, keeping terms up
to $z^{4}$:

$$\tan z\,\tanh z = \left(z + \tfrac{z^{3}}{3} + \cdots\right)\!\left(z - \tfrac
{z^{3}}{3} + \cdots\right).$$

- Coefficient of $z^{2}$: $z\cdot z = 1$, so $a_2 = 1$.
- Coefficient of $z^{4}$: contributions come from $z\cdot\!\left(-\tfrac{z^{3}}{
3}\right)$ and $\tfrac{z^{3}}{3}\cdot z$:
$$a_4 = -\tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1}{3} = 0.$$

**Step 3: Conclude.** Since the coefficient of $z^{4}$ in $\tan z\,\tanh z$ is $
0$, the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ in $g(z) = \tan z\,\tanh z\,/\,z^{5}$ is also $0
$.

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\, g(z) = 0}$$
