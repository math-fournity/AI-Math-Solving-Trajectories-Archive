## Proof

Let $z_n = \frac{1-n}{1+n}$ for $n \in \mathbb{N}$. These are the prescribed zer
os of $h$. Note $z_1 = 0$, $z_2 = -\tfrac{1}{3}$, $z_3 = -\tfrac{1}{2}$, and in
general $z_n = -\tfrac{n-1}{n+1}$, so $|z_n| = \tfrac{n-1}{n+1} \in [0,1)$ and $
z_n \to -1 \in \partial\mathbb{D}$.

Since the zeros accumulate only on the boundary, the ordinary identity theorem d
oes not directly apply. We use the **Blaschke condition** for bounded analytic f
unctions.

**Blaschke's theorem.** Let $f$ be bounded and analytic on $\mathbb{D}$, not ide
ntically zero, with zeros $\{a_k\}$ (listed with multiplicity, $a_k \neq 0$; a z
ero at the origin is handled separately and contributes a factor $z^m$). Then
$$\sum_{k} \bigl(1 - |a_k|\bigr) < \infty.$$
Conversely, if this sum converges, the corresponding Blaschke product converges
and gives a bounded analytic function with exactly those zeros.

**Applying it here.** The zeros of $h$ include $\{z_n\}_{n\ge 1}$. Discarding $z
_1 = 0$ (which only contributes a factor $z$ and is irrelevant to the Blaschke s
um), the remaining zeros satisfy
$$1 - |z_n| = 1 - \frac{n-1}{n+1} = \frac{2}{n+1}, \qquad n \ge 2.$$
Hence
$$\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \bigl(1 - |z_n|\bigr) = \sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{2}{n+1}
= 2\sum_{m=3}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m} = +\infty,$$
since the harmonic series diverges.

The Blaschke condition fails. Therefore no nonzero bounded analytic function on
$\mathbb{D}$ can vanish at every $z_n$. Since $h$ is bounded and analytic on $\m
athbb{D}$ and vanishes at all these points, $h$ must be **identically zero** on
$\mathbb{D}$.

**Answer: Yes, $h \equiv 0$ on $\mathbb{D}$.**
