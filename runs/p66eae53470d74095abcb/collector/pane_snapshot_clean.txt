**Proof.**

**Step 1: $f(0)=0$ and $f$ is even.**

Setting $a=b=c=0$ (which satisfies $ab+bc+ca=0$): $3f(0)=2f(0)$, so $f(0)=0$.

Setting $b=c=0$ (so $ab+bc+ca=0$): $f(a)+f(0)+f(-a)=2f(a)$, giving $f(-a)=f(a)$.
 Thus $f$ is even, so $f(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n} p_{2k}\,x^{2k}$.

**Step 2: Reformulation.**

Let $s=a+b+c$. Since $ab+bc+ca=0$, we have $a^2+b^2+c^2=s^2$. Set $u=a-\frac{s}{
3},\; v=b-\frac{s}{3},\; w=c-\frac{s}{3}$, so $u+v+w=0$ and $u^2+v^2+w^2=\frac{2
s^2}{3}$.

Then $a-b=u-v,\; b-c=v-w,\; c-a=w-u$. Set $p=u-v,\; q=v-w$, so $p+q=u-w$ and $w-
u=-(p+q)$.

Computing $p^2+q^2+(p+q)^2=(u-v)^2+(v-w)^2+(w-u)^2=3(u^2+v^2+w^2)=2s^2$, hence $
p^2+pq+q^2=s^2$.

Since $f$ is even, $f(w-u)=f(p+q)$, and the functional equation becomes:

$$f(p)+f(q)+f(p+q)=2f(s),\qquad s^2=p^2+pq+q^2.$$

This holds for all real $p,q$ (every pair $(p,q)$ arises from some valid $(a,b,c
)$, and $f$ even makes the sign of $s$ irrelevant):

$$\boxed{f(p)+f(q)+f(p+q)=2f\!\left(\sqrt{p^2+pq+q^2}\right)\quad\text{for all }
p,q\in\mathbb{R}.}\tag{$\star$}$$

**Step 3: Degree separation.**

Substituting $f(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{2k}\,x^{2k}$ into $(\star)$:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{2k}\big[p^{2k}+q^{2k}+(p+q)^{2k}\big]=2\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{2k}\,
(p^2+pq+q^2)^k.$$

Both sides are polynomial identities in $p,q$. The degree-$2k$ homogeneous part
of the LHS is $p_{2k}[p^{2k}+q^{2k}+(p+q)^{2k}]$ and of the RHS is $2p_{2k}(p^2+
pq+q^2)^k$. By uniqueness of homogeneous decomposition, for each $k$:

$$\text{either }p_{2k}=0,\quad\text{or}\quad p^{2k}+q^{2k}+(p+q)^{2k}=2(p^2+pq+q
^2)^k.\tag{$\star\star$}$$

**Step 4: Determine which $k$ satisfy $(\star\star)$.**

Set $q=1$ and compare the coefficient of $t^{2k-2}$ on both sides of $t^{2k}+1+(
t+1)^{2k}=2(t^2+t+1)^k$.

- **LHS:** From $t^{2k}$: $0$. From $1$: $0$ (for $k\geq 2$). From $(t+1)^{2k}$:
 $\binom{2k}{2}=k(2k-1)$. Total: $k(2k-1)$.

- **RHS:** The coefficient of $t^{2k-2}$ in $(t^2+t+1)^k$ counts choosing, from
$k$ copies of $(t^2+t+1)$, terms summing to degree $2k-2$ (i.e., losing 2 from t
he max $2k$):
  - One copy contributes $t^0=1$, rest contribute $t^2$: $\binom{k}{1}=k$ ways.
  - Two copies contribute $t^1$, rest contribute $t^2$: $\binom{k}{2}$ ways.

  Total: $k+\binom{k}{2}=\frac{k(k+1)}{2}$. Multiplied by 2: $k(k+1)$.

Equating: $k(2k-1)=k(k+1)$, so $2k-1=k+1$, giving $k=2$.

Thus for $k\geq 3$, the coefficients differ, so $(\star\star)$ fails and $p_{2k}
=0$.

For $k=1$: direct verification gives $t^2+1+(t+1)^2=2t^2+2t+2=2(t^2+t+1)$ ✓.

For $k=2$: $t^4+1+(t+1)^4=2t^4+4t^3+6t^2+4t+2=2(t^2+t+1)^2$ ✓ (expand $(t^2+t+1)
^2=t^4+2t^3+3t^2+2t+1$).

**Step 5: Verification.**

Both $f(x)=x^2$ and $f(x)=x^4$ satisfy $(\star)$, hence the original equation. B
y linearity, $f(x)=\alpha x^2+\beta x^4$ works for all $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R
}$.

- $f(x)=x^2$: LHS $=(a-b)^2+(b-c)^2+(c-a)^2=2(a^2+b^2+c^2)-2(ab+bc+ca)=2s^2=2f(s
)$ ✓.
- $f(x)=x^4$: Using $x=a-b,y=b-c,z=c-a$ with $x+y+z=0$: $x^2+y^2+z^2=2s^2$ and $
xy+yz+zx=-s^2$, so $x^4+y^4+z^4=(x^2+y^2+z^2)^2-2(xy+yz+zx)^2=4s^4-2s^4=2s^4=2f(
s)$ ✓.

**Conclusion.** The polynomials satisfying the condition are exactly

$$f(x)=\alpha\, x^2+\beta\, x^4,\qquad \alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}.$$
