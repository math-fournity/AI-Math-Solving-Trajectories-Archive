## Solution

We have $X_n = e^{a S_n - b n^2}$ with $S_n = \sum_{k=1}^n \xi_k$, where $\xi_k
\stackrel{\text{iid}}{\sim} N(0,1)$. Hence $S_n \sim N(0, n)$, i.e. $S_n = \sqrt
{n}\, Z$ with $Z \sim N(0,1)$.

Since $X_n > 0$ a.s., convergence $X_n \to 0$ in $\mathcal{L}^r$ (for $r \geq 1$
) is equivalent to
$$E[X_n^r] \to 0.$$

**Computing the $r$-th moment.** Using the moment generating function of a Gauss
ian ($E[e^{tS_n}] = e^{t^2 n/2}$ since $S_n \sim N(0,n)$):
$$E[X_n^r] = E\!\left[e^{r a S_n - r b n^2}\right] = e^{-r b n^2}\, E\!\left[e^{
r a S_n}\right] = e^{-r b n^2}\, e^{(ra)^2 n/2}.$$

Therefore
$$E[X_n^r] = \exp\!\left(-r b\, n^2 + \frac{r^2 a^2}{2}\, n\right) = \exp\!\left
(n\!\left(-r b\, n + \frac{r^2 a^2}{2}\right)\right).$$

**Analyzing the exponent** $\Phi_n := -r b\, n^2 + \dfrac{r^2 a^2}{2}\, n$.

- **If $b > 0$:** The leading term $-r b\, n^2 \to -\infty$ dominates the linear
 term $\frac{r^2 a^2}{2}n$, for **every** $a \in \mathbb{R}$. Thus $\Phi_n \to -
\infty$ and $E[X_n^r] \to 0$. So $X_n \to 0$ in $\mathcal{L}^r$.

- **If $b < 0$:** Then $-r b\, n^2 = r|b|\, n^2 \to +\infty$ dominates, so $\Phi
_n \to +\infty$ and $E[X_n^r] \to +\infty$. No $\mathcal{L}^r$ convergence to $0
$.

- **If $b = 0$:** $\Phi_n = \dfrac{r^2 a^2}{2}\, n$.
  - When $a \neq 0$: $\Phi_n \to +\infty$, so $E[X_n^r] \to +\infty$.
  - When $a = 0$: $X_n \equiv 1$, so $E[X_n^r] = 1 \not\to 0$.

  In neither subcase does $X_n \to 0$ in $\mathcal{L}^r$.

**Conclusion.** The quadratic term $-b n^2$ always dominates the linear-in-$n$ c
ontribution coming from the Gaussian fluctuation of $S_n$. Hence the condition d
epends only on $b$:

$$\boxed{X_n \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}^r} 0 \iff b > 0 \quad (\text{for all } a \
in \mathbb{R},\ r \geq 1).}$$

The condition is both necessary and sufficient, and is independent of $a$ and of
 $r \geq 1$.
