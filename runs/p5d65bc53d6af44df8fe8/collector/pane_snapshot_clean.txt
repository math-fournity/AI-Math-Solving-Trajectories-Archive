## Proof

**Definition (Devaney chaos).** A continuous map $f: X \to X$ on a metric space
$(X,d)$ is *chaotic* if:
1. **Topological transitivity:** for every pair of nonempty open sets $U,V \subs
et X$, there exists $n \geq 0$ with $f^n(U) \cap V \neq \emptyset$;
2. **Dense periodic points:** the set of periodic points of $f$ is dense in $X$;
3. **Sensitive dependence on initial conditions:** there exists $\delta > 0$ suc
h that for every $x \in X$ and every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists $y$ with $d
(x,y) < \varepsilon$ and $n \geq 0$ with $d(f^n(x), f^n(y)) > \delta$.

**Theorem (Banks–Brooks–Cairns–Davis–Stacey, 1992).** If $f$ is a continuous map
 on a metric space without isolated points that is topologically transitive and
has dense periodic points, then $f$ has sensitive dependence on initial conditio
ns. Thus conditions (1) and (2) suffice.

### Step 1: Topological conjugacy between the logistic map and the tent map

The logistic map at parameter $r=4$ is $f:[0,1]\to[0,1]$, $f(x) = 4x(1-x)$.

Define the **tent map** $T:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ by
$$T(x) = \begin{cases} 2x & 0 \le x \le \tfrac12, \\ 2-2x & \tfrac12 \le x \le 1
. \end{cases}$$

Define $h:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ by $h(\theta) = \sin^2\!\bigl(\tfrac{\pi\theta}{2}\bigr
)$.

**Claim.** $h$ is a homeomorphism and $f \circ h = h \circ T$.

*Proof.* $h$ is continuous and strictly increasing on $[0,1]$ since $h'(\theta)
= \frac{\pi}{2}\sin(\pi\theta) > 0$ for $\theta \in (0,1)$, with $h(0)=0$, $h(1)
=1$. Hence $h$ is a homeomorphism.

For $\theta \in [0,\tfrac12]$: $T(\theta)=2\theta$, so $h(T(\theta)) = \sin^2(\p
i\theta)$. Meanwhile
$$f(h(\theta)) = 4\sin^2\!\bigl(\tfrac{\pi\theta}{2}\bigr)\Bigl(1-\sin^2\!\bigl(
\tfrac{\pi\theta}{2}\bigr)\Bigr) = 4\sin^2\!\bigl(\tfrac{\pi\theta}{2}\bigr)\cos
^2\!\bigl(\tfrac{\pi\theta}{2}\bigr) = \sin^2(\pi\theta).$$

For $\theta \in [\tfrac12,1]$: $T(\theta)=2-2\theta$, so $h(T(\theta)) = \sin^2\
!\bigl(\tfrac{\pi(2-2\theta)}{2}\bigr) = \sin^2(\pi-\pi\theta) = \sin^2(\pi\thet
a)$, which equals $f(h(\theta))$ as computed above.

Thus $f \circ h = h \circ T$, i.e., $f = h \circ T \circ h^{-1}$. $\square$

Since $h$ is a homeomorphism, $f$ and $T$ are **topologically conjugate**, and a
ll three Devaney chaos properties are preserved under conjugacy.

### Step 2: The tent map is chaotic

**(2) Dense periodic points.** For each $n \ge 1$, the iterate $T^n$ consists of
 $2^n$ linear branches, each of slope $\pm 2^n$, mapping a subinterval of length
 $2^{-n}$ onto $[0,1]$. The breakpoints are at $k/2^n$, $k=0,\dots,2^n$. On each
 subinterval $I_k = [k/2^n, (k+1)/2^n]$, the map $T^n: I_k \to [0,1]$ is onto, s
o by the Intermediate Value Theorem, $T^n(x) - x$ has a zero in $I_k$. Each zero
 is a fixed point of $T^n$, hence a periodic point of $T$ with period dividing $
n$. These $2^n$ points are distributed one per subinterval of length $2^{-n}$, s
o every point of $[0,1]$ lies within distance $2^{-n}$ of a periodic point. Sinc
e $n$ is arbitrary, periodic points are dense. $\checkmark$

**(1) Topological transitivity (in fact, topological mixing).** Let $U, V \subse
t [0,1]$ be nonempty open intervals. For $n$ large enough, $U$ contains a comple
te branch interval $I_k$ of $T^n$ (since the branch length $2^{-n} \to 0$). Then
 $T^n(U) \supseteq T^n(I_k) = [0,1] \supseteq V$, so $T^n(U) \cap V = V \neq \em
ptyset$. This proves topological mixing, which is stronger than transitivity. $\
checkmark$

**(3) Sensitive dependence.** Since $T$ is a continuous map on $[0,1]$ (no isola
ted points) that is topologically transitive with dense periodic points, the Ban
ks–Brooks–Cairns–Davis–Stacey theorem guarantees sensitive dependence on initial
 conditions. $\checkmark$

*(Direct argument also available: $|T'(x)|=2$ for $x \neq 1/2$, so $T$ is unifor
mly expanding. For any $x$ and $\varepsilon>0$, pick $y$ with $0<|x-y|<\varepsil
on$. As long as $T^k(x)$ and $T^k(y)$ lie in the same linear branch, $|T^k(x)-T^
k(y)|=2^k|x-y|$, which exceeds $1/4$ for $k$ large enough. If they separate into
 different branches at some earlier step, they straddle $1/2$ and subsequent ite
rates separate them further. In either case, $\delta = 1/4$ is a sensitivity con
stant.)*

### Step 3: Transfer to the logistic map

Since $f = h \circ T \circ h^{-1}$ with $h$ a homeomorphism:

- **Transitivity:** For open $U,V \subset [0,1]$, the sets $h^{-1}(U), h^{-1}(V)
$ are open, and transitivity of $T$ gives $n$ with $T^n(h^{-1}(U)) \cap h^{-1}(V
) \neq \emptyset$. Applying $h$ yields $f^n(U) \cap V \neq \emptyset$.

- **Dense periodic points:** If $p$ is periodic for $T$ with period $n$, then $f
^n(h(p)) = h(T^n(p)) = h(p)$, so $h(p)$ is periodic for $f$. A homeomorphism map
s dense sets to dense sets.

- **Sensitive dependence:** If $\delta$ is a sensitivity constant for $T$, then
by uniform continuity of $h^{-1}$ on the compact set $[0,1]$, there exists $\del
ta' > 0$ such that $|h(a)-h(b)| > \delta'$ whenever $|a-b| > \delta$. This $\del
ta'$ is a sensitivity constant for $f$.

Therefore **$f(x) = 4x(1-x)$ is chaotic in the sense of Devaney.**

### Step 4: Generic trajectories exhibit chaotic behavior

**Topological genericity (residual set).** By the Birkhoff Transitivity Theorem,
 topological transitivity of $f$ on the complete metric space $[0,1]$ (without i
solated points) implies that the set
$$\mathcal{D} = \bigl\{x \in [0,1] : \overline{\{f^n(x) : n \ge 0\}} = [0,1]\big
r\}$$
of points with dense orbit is a **residual set** (countable intersection of open
 dense sets). Thus, topologically generic trajectories are dense in $[0,1]$.

**Measure-theoretic genericity (almost every point).** The tent map $T$ is ergod
ic with respect to Lebesgue measure: writing $\theta \in [0,1]$ in binary as $\t
heta = 0.b_1 b_2 b_3 \cdots$, the map $T$ acts as the left shift $(b_1, b_2, b_3
, \ldots) \mapsto (b_2, b_3, \ldots)$, which is a Bernoulli shift, hence ergodic
. By Birkhoff's Ergodic Theorem, for Lebesgue-a.e. $\theta$, the orbit $\{T^n(\t
heta)\}$ is equidistributed and therefore dense in $[0,1]$.

Via the conjugacy $h$, the logistic map $f$ is ergodic with respect to the pushf
orward measure $\mu = h_*(\text{Leb})$, which is the **arcsine distribution**:
$$\mu(dx) = \frac{dx}{\pi\sqrt{x(1-x)}}.$$
For $\mu$-a.e. $x \in [0,1]$, the orbit $\{f^n(x)\}$ is dense in $[0,1]$ and equ
idistributed according to $\mu$.

**Lyapunov exponent.** The Lyapunov exponent of $T$ with respect to Lebesgue mea
sure is $\lambda_T = \ln 2 > 0$ (since $|T'| = 2$ a.e.). By conjugacy, the Lyapu
nov exponent of $f$ with respect to $\mu$ is also $\lambda_f = \ln 2 > 0$, confi
rming **exponential sensitivity** to initial conditions for generic trajectories
.

### Conclusion

The logistic map $f(x) = 4x(1-x)$ at parameter $r = 4$ **exhibits chaotic behavi
or for generic trajectories**. Specifically:

- $f$ is Devaney-chaotic: topologically transitive, with dense periodic points a
nd sensitive dependence on initial conditions.
- Generic trajectories — in both the topological sense (residual set) and the me
asure-theoretic sense (full $\mu$-measure) — have **dense orbits** in $[0,1]$.
- The Lyapunov exponent is $\ln 2 > 0$, giving exponential divergence of nearby
trajectories.

The key mechanism is the topological conjugacy $f = h \circ T \circ h^{-1}$ with
 the uniformly expanding tent map $T$, which transfers all chaotic properties fr
om $T$ to $f$.
