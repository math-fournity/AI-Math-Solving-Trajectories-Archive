## Proof

**Claim.** The maximum number of points in $\mathbb{R}^n$ with all pairwise dist
ances equal to $1$ is $\boxed{n+1}$.

---

### Upper bound: at most $n+1$ points

Suppose $p_1, p_2, \dots, p_m \in \mathbb{R}^n$ satisfy $\|p_i - p_j\| = 1$ for
all $i \neq j$. Set $v_i = p_i - p_m$ for $i = 1, \dots, m-1$. These are $m-1$ v
ectors in $\mathbb{R}^n$. We show they are linearly independent.

Compute their Gram matrix $G$, with entries $G_{ij} = \langle v_i, v_j \rangle$:

- **Diagonal** ($i = j$): $\|v_i\|^2 = \|p_i - p_m\|^2 = 1$, so $G_{ii} = 1$.
- **Off-diagonal** ($i \neq j$, both in $\{1,\dots,m-1\}$): Using $\|v_i - v_j\|
^2 = \|p_i - p_j\|^2 = 1$ and expanding,
$$\|v_i - v_j\|^2 = \|v_i\|^2 + \|v_j\|^2 - 2\langle v_i, v_j\rangle = 1 + 1 - 2
\langle v_i, v_j\rangle = 1,$$
so $\langle v_i, v_j\rangle = \tfrac{1}{2}$.

Thus $G = \tfrac{1}{2}I_{m-1} + \tfrac{1}{2}J_{m-1}$, where $J_{m-1}$ is the all
-ones matrix. The eigenvalues of $J_{m-1}$ are $m-1$ (once, for the all-ones vec
tor) and $0$ (with multiplicity $m-2$). Hence the eigenvalues of $G$ are
$$\tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{2}(m-1) = \tfrac{m}{2} \quad\text{(once)}, \qquad \tf
rac{1}{2} + 0 = \tfrac{1}{2} \quad\text{(multiplicity } m-2\text{)}.$$
All eigenvalues are strictly positive, so $G$ is positive definite. A positive d
efinite Gram matrix means $v_1, \dots, v_{m-1}$ are linearly independent. Since
they lie in $\mathbb{R}^n$,
$$m - 1 \le n \implies m \le n+1.$$

### Lower bound: $n+1$ points are achievable (regular simplex)

We exhibit $n+1$ points in an $n$-dimensional space with all pairwise distances
equal to $1$.

Consider the standard basis vectors $e_1, e_2, \dots, e_{n+1} \in \mathbb{R}^{n+
1}$. For $i \neq j$,
$$\|e_i - e_j\| = \sqrt{2}.$$
These $n+1$ points all lie in the affine hyperplane $H = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^{n+1
} : x_1 + x_2 + \cdots + x_{n+1} = 1\}$, which is an $n$-dimensional affine subs
pace (hence isometric to $\mathbb{R}^n$).

Scale by the factor $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$: define $q_i = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} e_i$.
 Then for $i \neq j$,
$$\|q_i - q_j\| = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\|e_i - e_j\| = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \cdot \sq
rt{2} = 1.$$

The points $q_1, \dots, q_{n+1}$ lie in the $n$-dimensional affine hyperplane $\
sum x_i = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, which is isometric to $\mathbb{R}^n$. Therefore,
identifying this hyperplane with $\mathbb{R}^n$, we have produced $n+1$ points i
n $\mathbb{R}^n$ with all pairwise distances exactly $1$.

The upper bound gives $m \le n+1$ and the lower bound gives $m \ge n+1$. Therefo
