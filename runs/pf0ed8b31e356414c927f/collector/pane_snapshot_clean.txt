## Proof

Let $A = L^1(\mathbb{R})$ be the Banach algebra of Lebesgue-integrable functions
 $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}$ with multiplication given by convolution
$$
(f*g)(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}} f(t-s)\,g(s)\,ds .
$$
This algebra is **non-unital**: there is no $L^1$ function $e$ satisfying $e*f=f
$ for all $f\in L^1$, since such an $e$ would have to act as the Dirac delta, wh
ich is not a function.

### Construction of the unitization (Lemma 63)

For a non-unital Banach algebra $A$, the (forced) unitization is the Banach alge
bra
$$
\widetilde{A}=A\oplus\mathbb{C}
$$
with norm $\|(a,\lambda)\|=\|a\|+\lvert\lambda\rvert$ and multiplication
$$
(a,\lambda)\,(b,\mu)=\bigl(ab+\lambda b+\mu a,\;\lambda\mu\bigr).
$$
The unit is the element
$$
\mathbf{1}_{\widetilde{A}}=(0,1),
$$
since $(0,1)(b,\mu)=(0\cdot b+1\cdot b+\mu\cdot 0,\,1\cdot\mu)=(b,\mu)$, and sim
ilarly on the left. The original algebra embeds isometrically and as a closed tw
o-sided ideal via
$$
\iota:A\hookrightarrow\widetilde{A},\qquad \iota(a)=(a,0).
$$

### Natural identification of the unit

The algebra $L^1(\mathbb{R})$ embeds canonically into the Banach algebra $M(\mat
hbb{R})$ of finite complex Borel measures on $\mathbb{R}$, where multiplication
is convolution of measures, via $f\mapsto f\,dx$ (the measure with density $f$ a
gainst Lebesgue measure). The algebra $M(\mathbb{R})$ **is unital**: its unit is
 the Dirac point mass
$$
\delta_{0}=\text{the probability measure concentrated at }0,
$$
because for any measure $\nu$,
$$
(\delta_{0}*\nu)(E)=\nu(E-0)=\nu(E),
$$
so $\delta_{0}*\nu=\nu=\nu*\delta_{0}$.

Now define
$$
\Phi:\widetilde{L^{1}}=L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathbb{C}\longrightarrow M(\mathb
b{R}),\qquad
\Phi(f,\lambda)=f\,dx+\lambda\,\delta_{0}.
$$
This is an isometric algebra homomorphism:

- **Multiplicativity.** Using that $\delta_{0}*\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}$, that $\de
lta_{0}*(g\,dx)=g\,dx$, and that $(f\,dx)*(g\,dx)=(f*g)\,dx$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi(f,\lambda)*\Phi(g,\mu)
&=(f\,dx+\lambda\delta_{0})*(g\,dx+\mu\delta_{0})\\
&=(f*g)\,dx+\mu f\,dx+\lambda g\,dx+\lambda\mu\,\delta_{0}\\
&=\bigl((f*g)+\mu f+\lambda g\bigr)\,dx+\lambda\mu\,\delta_{0}\\
&=\Phi\bigl((f,\lambda)(g,\mu)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

- **Isometry.** $\|\Phi(f,\lambda)\|_{M}=|f\,dx|(\mathbb{R})+|\lambda|=\|f\|_{1}
+|\lambda|=\|(f,\lambda)\|_{\widetilde{L^{1}}}$ (the total-variation norm splits
 because $f\,dx$ is absolutely continuous and $\delta_{0}$ is purely atomic, so
they are mutually singular).

Under this embedding, the unit of $\widetilde{L^{1}}$ is
$$
\Phi(0,1)=0\,dx+1\cdot\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}.
$$

### Conclusion

A natural identification for the unit of the unitization $\widetilde{L^{1}}$ is
the **Dirac delta (point mass) $\delta_{0}$ at the origin**, viewed as an elemen
t of the measure algebra $M(\mathbb{R})$. Concretely, the unitization identifies
 with the subalgebra
$$
\{\,f\,dx+\lambda\,\delta_{0}:f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,\}\
subseteq M(\mathbb{R}),
$$
and the unit $(0,1)\in L^{1}\oplus\mathbb{C}$ corresponds to $\delta_{0}$. This
is natural because $\delta_{0}$ is precisely the convolution identity that $L^{1
}(\mathbb{R})$ lacks as a function, and adjoining it is exactly what the abstrac
t unitization accomplishes.
