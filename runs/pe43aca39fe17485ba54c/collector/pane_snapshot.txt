**Setup.** Let $r_n$ denote the radius of sphere $S_n$. We establish the relatio
nship between consecutive spheres.

**Cube inscribed in a sphere.** When a cube $K_n$ is inscribed in sphere $S_n$ (
corners touching the sphere), the space diagonal of the cube equals the diameter
 of the sphere:
$$\text{diagonal of } K_n = 2r_n, \quad \text{so side length } a_n = \frac{2r_n}
{\sqrt{3}}.$$

**Sphere inscribed in a cube.** When sphere $S_{n+1}$ is inscribed in cube $K_n$
 (tangent to all six faces), its radius equals half the side length:
$$r_{n+1} = \frac{a_n}{2} = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2r_n}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{r_n}{\
sqrt{3}}.$$

**Radius sequence.** Starting from $r_0 = r$:
$$r_n = \frac{r}{(\sqrt{3})^{\,n}} = r\cdot 3^{-n/2}.$$

**Volume of each sphere.**
$$V(S_n) = \frac{4}{3}\pi r_n^3 = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \cdot 3^{-3n/2}.$$

**Total volume (geometric series).** The common ratio is $q = 3^{-3/2} = \frac{1
}{3\sqrt{3}} < 1$, so the series converges:
$$V_{\text{total}} = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(3^{-3/2}\right)
^n = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \cdot \frac{1}{1 - \frac{1}{3\sqrt{3}}}.$$

**Simplifying:**
$$\frac{1}{1 - \frac{1}{3\sqrt{3}}} = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{3\sqrt{3}-1}.$$

Therefore:
$$V_{\text{total}} = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \cdot \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{3\sqrt{3}-1} = 4\
pi r^3 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3\sqrt{3}-1}.$$

**Rationalizing** by multiplying numerator and denominator by $(3\sqrt{3}+1)$:
$$\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3\sqrt{3}-1}\cdot\frac{3\sqrt{3}+1}{3\sqrt{3}+1} = \frac{\sqrt
{3}(3\sqrt{3}+1)}{(3\sqrt{3})^2 - 1} = \frac{9+\sqrt{3}}{27-1} = \frac{9+\sqrt{3
}}{26}.$$

**Final result:**
$$\boxed{V_{\text{total}} = \frac{2\pi r^3\,(9+\sqrt{3})}{13}}.$$
