## Proof

**Claim:** Yes — under the standard cryptographic assumption that the Discrete L
ogarithm Problem (DLP) is hard, one can construct such a function. I give an exp
licit construction in which `a` and `b` are genuinely *mixed* (not merely placed
 in separate output components), so that `b` is efficiently recoverable while `a
` is not.

### Setup

Let $p = 2q+1$ be a large safe prime, and let $\mathbb{G}$ be the prime-order su
bgroup of quadratic residues of $\mathbb{Z}_p^*$, with generator $g$ of order $q
$. All arithmetic on exponents is modulo $q$.

- **Input `a`** (the *secret*): an exponent $a \in \{1,\dots,q\}$.
- **Input `b`** (the *recoverable* value): a group element $b \in \mathbb{G}$.

### The construction

Define
$$
g(a,b) \;=\; \bigl(\,g^{a}\bmod p,\;\; b\cdot g^{a}\bmod p\,\bigr) \;\in\; \math
bb{G}\times\mathbb{G}.
$$
Denote the output $(X, Y) = (g^{a},\, b\cdot g^{a})$.

### Recovering `b` is feasible

Given $(X,Y)$, compute the group inverse $X^{-1}\bmod p$ via the extended Euclid
ean algorithm (polynomial time, $\approx O(\log p)$). Then
$$
b \;=\; Y\cdot X^{-1}\bmod p \;=\; (b\cdot g^{a})\cdot (g^{a})^{-1}\bmod p \;=\;
 b.
$$
This is a constant number of modular multiplications — trivially feasible.

### Recovering `a` is infeasible

Given $(X,Y)$, recovering $a$ requires solving
$$
X = g^{a}\bmod p \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a = \log_{g}(X)\bmod q,
$$
i.e. the **Discrete Logarithm Problem** in $\mathbb{G}$. The second component $Y
$ carries no information about $a$ beyond $g^{a}$ (it is $b$ masked by the *same
* $g^{a}$, and $b$ is unknown to the attacker, so $Y$ is essentially a random el
ement of $\mathbb{G}$ independent of $a$ given $X$). Formally, for uniformly ran
dom $b\in\mathbb{G}$, the pair $(g^{a},\, b\,g^{a})$ makes $Y$ uniformly distrib
uted and independent of $a$ conditional on $X$; hence $Y$ adds zero information
about $a$.

Thus recovering $a$ reduces exactly to DLP in a prime-order group. Under the sta
ndard, widely-accepted hardness assumption for DLP (no polynomial-time algorithm
 is known; the best known algorithms are sub-exponential and utterly infeasible
for cryptographically sized $p$, e.g. $q$ of 2048+ bits), determining $a$ from $
g(a,b)$ is **computationally infeasible**.

### Why `a` and `b` are genuinely entangled (not a trivial split)

Note that neither component alone reveals `b`: $X=g^{a}$ is independent of $b$,
and $Y=b\,g^{a}$ is a one-time-pad-style masking of $b$ under the pad $g^{a}$. T
he recoverability of $b` depends on *both* components being present (so the pad
can be divided out), while the secrecy of `a` depends on the pad $g^{a}$ being a
 one-way image of $a`. This is precisely the algebraic structure underlying ElGa
mal-style masking, and it shows the asymmetry is structural rather than an artif
act of "putting `b` in a separate output slot."

### A weaker-assumption variant (one-way functions only)

If one is willing to accept the (even more standard) existence of a one-way func
tion $H$, a degenerate construction $g(a,b)=(H(a),\,b)$ also satisfies the requi
rement: `b` is read off directly, `a` requires inverting $H`. The DLP constructi
on above is strictly stronger in that it mixes the two inputs non-trivially whil
e still achieving the asymmetry.

### Conclusion

Under the standard hardness assumption for the Discrete Logarithm Problem, the f
unction
$$
g(a,b) = (g^{a}\bmod p,\; b\cdot g^{a}\bmod p)
$$
makes `b` efficiently recoverable (one modular inversion + one multiplication) w
hile making `a` recoverable only by solving DLP (infeasible). Therefore **yes, s
uch a function can be constructed**.
