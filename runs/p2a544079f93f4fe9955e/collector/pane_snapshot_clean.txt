## Proof

**Claim.** There exists a function $f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}$ that is differentiable
 everywhere on $[0,1]$ yet has unbounded variation.

**Construction.** Define
$$
f(x)=\begin{cases}x^{2}\sin\!\bigl(1/x^{2}\bigr), & x\in(0,1],\\[4pt] 0, & x=0.\
end{cases}
$$

---

### Step 1. $f$ is differentiable everywhere on $[0,1]$.

For $x\in(0,1]$, the usual product/chain rule gives
$$
f'(x)=2x\sin\!\bigl(1/x^{2}\bigr)-\frac{2}{x}\cos\!\bigl(1/x^{2}\bigr),
$$
so $f$ is differentiable at every interior point.

At $x=0$, we use the definition:
$$
\frac{f(h)-f(0)}{h}=\frac{h^{2}\sin(1/h^{2})}{h}=h\sin\!\bigl(1/h^{2}\bigr)\xrig
htarrow{h\to 0}0,
$$
since $|h\sin(1/h^{2})|\le |h|\to 0$. Hence $f'(0)=0$ exists.

Thus $f$ is differentiable at every point of $[0,1]$ (with one-sided derivatives
 at the endpoints). $\checkmark$

---

### Step 2. $f$ has unbounded variation on $[0,1]$.

For each integer $n\ge 0$, set
$$
x_{n}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{(2n+1)\pi}},\qquad\text{so that}\quad \frac{1}{x_{n}^{2}}=\
frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2}.
$$
Then $x_{n}\in(0,1]$ (with $x_{0}=\sqrt{2/\pi}<1$), $x_{n}\searrow 0$, and
$$
\sin\!\bigl(1/x_{n}^{2}\bigr)=\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{(2n+1)\pi}{2}\Bigr)=(-1)^{n}.
$$
Therefore
$$
f(x_{n})=x_{n}^{2}\,(-1)^{n}=\frac{2}{(2n+1)\pi}\,(-1)^{n}.
$$

For any $N\ge 1$, consider the partition
$$
0 < x_{N} < x_{N-1} < \cdots < x_{1} < x_{0} \le 1.
$$
The variation of $f$ over $[0,1]$ is bounded below by the sum of successive osci
llations among the $x_{n}$:
$$
V(f)\;\ge\;\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\bigl|f(x_{n})-f(x_{n+1})\bigr|.
$$
Since consecutive signs alternate,
$$
\bigl|f(x_{n})-f(x_{n+1})\bigr|
=\Bigl|\tfrac{2(-1)^{n}}{(2n+1)\pi}-\tfrac{2(-1)^{n+1}}{(2n+3)\pi}\Bigr|
=\frac{2}{\pi}\Bigl(\frac{1}{2n+1}+\frac{1}{2n+3}\Bigr).
$$
Hence
$$
V(f)\;\ge\;\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\Bigl(\frac{1}{2n+1}+\frac{1}{2n+3}\Bigr
)
\;\ge\;\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{2n+1}.
$$
As $N\to\infty$, the series $\sum_{n\ge 0}\frac{1}{2n+1}$ diverges (it is the ha
rmonic series restricted to odd terms, which diverges). Therefore
$$
V(f)=+\infty.
$$

---

### Conclusion.

The function $f(x)=x^{2}\sin(1/x^{2})$ (with $f(0)=0$) is differentiable at ever
y point of $[0,1]$, yet its total variation on $[0,1]$ is infinite. Hence such a
 function **does exist**.
