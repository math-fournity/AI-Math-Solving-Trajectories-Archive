## Proof

**Claim.** Every proper subfield of $\mathbb{R}$, equipped with the subspace top
ology inherited from the usual topology on $\mathbb{R}$, is disconnected. In fac
t it is not connected as an additive topological subgroup.

Let $K\subsetneq\mathbb{R}$ be a proper subfield, given the subspace topology. S
ince $K$ is a subfield of a characteristic‑$0$ field, it contains the prime subf
ield $\mathbb{Q}$; in particular $0,1\in K$, so $|K|\ge 2$.

The addition map $+\colon\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ and negation $
-\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ are continuous. Restricting to $K\times K\to K$
shows that $K$ is a **topological subgroup** of $(\mathbb{R},+)$.

**Lemma.** The only connected subgroups of $(\mathbb{R},+)$ (usual topology) are
 $\{0\}$ and $\mathbb{R}$.

*Proof of lemma.* Let $H\le\mathbb{R}$ be connected. Connected subsets of $\math
bb{R}$ are intervals, so $H$ is an interval. If $H=\{0\}$ we are done. Otherwise
 pick $a\in H$, $a\ne 0$; by replacing $a$ with $-a$ (using that $H$ is a subgro
up) assume $a>0$. Since $0,a\in H$ and $H$ is an interval, $[0,a]\subseteq H$, h
ence the open interval $(0,a)\subseteq H$. As $H$ is a subgroup, $-[0,a]=[-a,0]\
subseteq H$, so $[-a,a]\subseteq H$ and in particular $(-a,a)\subseteq H$ with $
a>0$.

Now for any $r\in\mathbb{R}$ choose $n\in\mathbb{N}$ with $|r|/n<a$. Then $r/n\i
n(-a,a)\subseteq H$, and $r=n\cdot(r/n)\in H$ (closed under repeated addition).
Thus $H=\mathbb{R}$. $\square$

Applying the lemma to $K$: $K$ is a connected subgroup of $\mathbb{R}$ iff $K\in
\bigl\{\{0\},\,\mathbb{R}\bigr\}$. But

- $K\ne\{0\}$, since $K$ is a field containing $\mathbb{Q}$ (so $1\in K$);
- $K\ne\mathbb{R}$, since $K$ is a **proper** subfield.

Hence $K$ is **not** connected; equivalently, $K$ is disconnected.

Therefore a proper subfield of $\mathbb{R}$, considered as a topological field w
ith the induced topology, **must** be disconnected. $\blacksquare$
