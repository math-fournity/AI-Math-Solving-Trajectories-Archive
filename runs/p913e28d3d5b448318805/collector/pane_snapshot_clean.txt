## Proof

We are given $F(x,y) = x + y + x^5 - y^5$ and the implicit function $\phi$ defin
ed by $F(x,\phi(x))=0$ near $x=0$.

**Step 1: Determine $\phi(0)$.**

Setting $x=0$: $F(0,y) = y - y^5 = y(1-y^4)=0$, giving $y \in \{0, \pm 1\}$.

Since $F_y(0,0) = 1 - 5y^4\big|_{y=0} = 1 \neq 0$, the Implicit Function Theorem
 selects the branch $\phi(0)=0$.

**Step 2: Establish that $\phi$ is an odd function.**

Observe the symmetry of $F$:
$$F(-x,-y) = -x - y - x^5 + y^5 = -F(x,y).$$

Define $\psi(x) = -\phi(-x)$. We verify that $\psi$ also satisfies the implicit
relation:
$$F(x,\psi(x)) = F(x,-\phi(-x)) = x - \phi(-x) + x^5 + \phi(-x)^5.$$

Since $F(-x,\phi(-x))=0$, we have $-x + \phi(-x) - x^5 - \phi(-x)^5 = 0$, i.e.,
$\phi(-x) - \phi(-x)^5 = x + x^5$. Substituting:
$$F(x,\psi(x)) = (x+x^5) - \bigl(\phi(-x)-\phi(-x)^5\bigr) = (x+x^5)-(x+x^5)=0.$
$

Also $\psi(0) = -\phi(0) = 0$. By the **uniqueness** of the implicit function ne
ar $(0,0)$ (guaranteed by $F_y(0,0)\neq 0$), we conclude
$$\phi(x) = \psi(x) = -\phi(-x),$$
so $\phi$ is **odd**.

**Step 3: Conclude $\phi^{(2004)}(0)=0$.**

An odd smooth function has a Taylor expansion containing only odd powers of $x$:
$$\phi(x) = a_1 x + a_3 x^3 + a_5 x^5 + \cdots$$

Hence every even-order derivative at $x=0$ vanishes. Since $2004$ is even,
$$\phi^{(2004)}(0) = 0.$$

**Verification (low-order terms):** From $x+\phi+x^5-\phi^5=0$:
- Coefficient of $x$: $1+a_1=0 \Rightarrow a_1=-1$.
- Coefficient of $x^3$: $a_3=0$ (since $\phi^5$ begins at $x^5$).
- Coefficient of $x^5$: $a_5+1-a_1^5=0 \Rightarrow a_5+1-(-1)=0 \Rightarrow a_5=
-2$.

So $\phi(x) = -x - 2x^5 + \cdots$, confirming all even coefficients are $0$.

$$\boxed{\phi^{(2004)}(0) = 0}$$
