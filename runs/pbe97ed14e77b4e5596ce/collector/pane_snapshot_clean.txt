## Proof

We glue a Möbius band $M$ to a disk $D$ by identifying the single boundary circl
e $\partial M$ with the boundary circle $\partial D$. We claim the resulting spa
ce is homeomorphic to the real projective plane $\mathbb{RP}^2$.

**Construction of $\mathbb{RP}^2$ as a quotient of $S^2$.** The real projective
plane is the quotient of the 2-sphere $S^2$ by the antipodal equivalence relatio
n $x \sim -x$. Equivalently, it is the quotient of a closed disk $D^2$ (a fundam
ental domain for this action, namely the upper hemisphere) in which antipodal po
ints on the boundary circle $\partial D^2 = S^1$ are identified.

**Exhibiting a Möbius band inside this quotient.** Consider the disk model of $\
mathbb{RP}^2$: a closed disk with antipodal boundary points identified. Place a
smaller concentric closed disk $D' \subset D^2$ of radius $r < 1$. The annulus $
A = D^2 \setminus \operatorname{int}(D')$ is the region between the outer circle
 (radius 1) and the inner circle (radius $r$).

Under the antipodal identification on the outer boundary, the annulus $A$ become
s a Möbius band. Indeed, parametrize $A$ by $(\rho, \theta)$ with $r \le \rho \l
e 1$ and $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$. The identification on the outer edge $\rho = 1$
is $(1, \theta) \sim (1, \theta + \pi)$. This is exactly the standard constructi
on of a Möbius band: take a rectangle $[r,1] \times [0, 2\pi]$ and glue the two
ends $\{1\}\times\{0\}$ to $\{1\}\times\{2\pi\}$ with a twist (the twist being t
he half-turn $\theta \mapsto \theta + \pi$). The inner edge $\rho = r$ remains a
 free boundary circle, which is $\partial M$.

**Capping off with the disk.** The inner disk $D'$ (with its boundary $\rho = r$
 not subject to any identification, since the antipodal relation only acts on th
e outer boundary $\rho = 1$) is an ordinary closed disk. Its boundary $\partial
D'$ is exactly the boundary circle of the Möbius band $M = A / \sim$ obtained ab
ove. Thus $\mathbb{RP}^2$ is decomposed as

$$\mathbb{RP}^2 \;=\; M \;\cup_{\partial M\,=\,\partial D'}\; D',$$

i.e. a Möbius band glued to a disk along their common boundary circle.

**Conclusion.** The space obtained by gluing a Möbius band to the boundary of a
disk is homeomorphic to the real projective plane $\mathbb{RP}^2$.
