**Goal.** Let $X$ be a connected CW complex with $\pi_k(X)=0$ for all $k\neq n$
and $\pi_n(X)\cong\pi$. Construct a homotopy equivalence $K(\pi,n)\to X$.

The strategy is: (1) build a CW model of $K(\pi,n)$, (2) construct a map $f:K(\p
i,n)\to X$ inducing the given isomorphism on $\pi_n$, (3) verify $f$ is a weak h
omotopy equivalence, (4) invoke Whitehead's theorem.

---

### Step 1. A CW model for $K(\pi,n)$

Choose a presentation of $\pi$: generators $\{g_i\}_{i\in I}$ and relations $\{r
_j\}_{j\in J}$ (for $n\geq 2$ this is a presentation of $\pi$ as an abelian grou
p; for $n=1$ as a group). Construct $K(\pi,n)$ inductively:

- **$n$-skeleton:** $K^{(n)}=\bigvee_{i\in I} S^n_i$, a wedge of $n$-spheres, on
e per generator. Then $\pi_n(K^{(n)})\cong\bigoplus_{i}\mathbb{Z}$ (free abelian
 for $n\geq 2$; free group for $n=1$).

- **$(n+1)$-skeleton:** For each relation $r_j=\sum_i a_{ij}\,g_i$, attach an $(
n+1)$-cell $e^{n+1}_j$ via an attaching map $S^n\to K^{(n)}$ representing $\sum_
i a_{ij}[S^n_i]\in\pi_n(K^{(n)})$. After this, $\pi_n(K^{(n+1)})\cong\pi$ by con
struction, and $\pi_k(K^{(n+1)})=0$ for $k<n$.

- **Higher skeleta ($k\geq n+2$):** Attach $k$-cells inductively to kill $\pi_{k
-1}$. Specifically, for each generator of $\pi_{k-1}(K^{(k-1)})$, attach a $k$-c
ell along a representing map. This makes $\pi_{k-1}(K^{(k)})=0$ while preserving
 $\pi_n\cong\pi$. Taking the union $K(\pi,n)=\bigcup_k K^{(k)}$ gives a CW compl
ex with $\pi_n\cong\pi$ and $\pi_k=0$ for $k\neq n$.

---

### Step 2. Constructing $f:K(\pi,n)\to X$

Fix an isomorphism $\varphi:\pi\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\pi_n(X)$. We build $f$ ske
leton by skeleton.

**On the $n$-skeleton.** For each generator $g_i$, the element $\varphi(g_i)\in\
pi_n(X)$ is represented by a map $\alpha_i:S^n\to X$. Define
$$f^{(n)}:K^{(n)}=\bigvee_i S^n_i\longrightarrow X$$
by $f^{(n)}|_{S^n_i}=\alpha_i$.

**Extension over the $(n+1)$-skeleton.** The attaching map of the $(n+1)$-cell $
e^{n+1}_j$ is $\sum_i a_{ij}[S^n_i]\in\pi_n(K^{(n)})$. Under $f^{(n)}_*$ this ma
ps to
$$\sum_i a_{ij}\,\varphi(g_i)=\varphi\!\left(\sum_i a_{ij}\,g_i\right)=\varphi(r
_j)=0\in\pi_n(X),$$
since $r_j$ is a relation. Thus $f^{(n)}\circ(\text{attaching map})$ is null-hom
otopic, so $f^{(n)}$ extends over each $(n+1)$-cell. This yields $f^{(n+1)}:K^{(
n+1)}\to X$.

**Extension over higher skeleta.** For $k\geq n+1$, the obstruction to extending
 $f^{(k)}:K^{(k)}\to X$ to the $(k+1)$-skeleton lies in
$$H^{k+1}\!\bigl(K(\pi,n);\,\pi_k(X)\bigr).$$
Since $\pi_k(X)=0$ for every $k\geq n+1$ (as $k\neq n$), all obstruction groups
vanish. Therefore $f$ extends inductively over all of $K(\pi,n)$, giving a map $
f:K(\pi,n)\to X$.

---

### Step 3. $f$ is a weak homotopy equivalence

**On $\pi_n$:** By construction, $f_*$ sends each generator $[S^n_i]\in\pi_n(K(\
pi,n))$ (corresponding to $g_i\in\pi$) to $\varphi(g_i)\in\pi_n(X)$. Since the i
dentification $\pi_n(K(\pi,n))\cong\pi$ sends $[S^n_i]\mapsto g_i$, the diagram
$$\pi_n(K(\pi,n))\xrightarrow{\;f_*\;}\pi_n(X)$$
is exactly the isomorphism $\varphi:\pi\xrightarrow{\sim}\pi_n(X)$. Hence $f_*$
is an isomorphism on $\pi_n$.

**On $\pi_k$ for $k\neq n$:** Both $\pi_k(K(\pi,n))=0$ and $\pi_k(X)=0$, so $f_*
:\pi_k(K(\pi,n))\to\pi_k(X)$ is trivially an isomorphism.

Thus $f$ induces isomorphisms on all homotopy groups, i.e., $f$ is a weak homoto
py equivalence.

---

### Step 4. Whitehead's theorem

Both $K(\pi,n)$ and $X$ are CW complexes (the former by construction, the latter
 by hypothesis). By Whitehead's theorem, a weak homotopy equivalence between CW
complexes is a homotopy equivalence. Therefore $f:K(\pi,n)\to X$ is a homotopy e
quivalence. $\blacksquare$
