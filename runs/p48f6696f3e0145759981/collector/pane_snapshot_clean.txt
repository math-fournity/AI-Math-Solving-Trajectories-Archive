## Spectrum of a Self-Adjoint Idempotent in a Unital $C^*$-Algebra

**Setup.** Let $A$ be a unital $C^*$-algebra with unit $1$, and let $p \in A$ sa
tisfy
$$p^2 = p, \qquad p^* = p.$$
Such an element is called a **projection**. We determine $\sigma(p) := \{\lambda
 \in \mathbb{C} : \lambda 1 - p \text{ is not invertible in } A\}$.

---

**Step 1. $\sigma(p) \subseteq \mathbb{R}$.**

Since $p^* = p$, the element $p$ is self-adjoint, hence normal. A standard theor
em of $C^*$-algebras states that the spectrum of any self-adjoint element is a c
ompact subset of $\mathbb{R}$.

---

**Step 2. $\sigma(p) \subseteq \{0, 1\}$.**

The relation $p^2 = p$ means the polynomial $f(x) = x^2 - x = x(x-1)$ annihilate
s $p$:
$$f(p) = p^2 - p = 0.$$
By the **spectral mapping theorem for polynomials**,
$$\sigma(f(p)) = f(\sigma(p)) = \{\lambda^2 - \lambda : \lambda \in \sigma(p)\}.
$$
Since $f(p) = 0$ and $\sigma(0) = \{0\}$, we obtain
$$\{\lambda^2 - \lambda : \lambda \in \sigma(p)\} = \{0\}.$$
Thus every $\lambda \in \sigma(p)$ satisfies $\lambda^2 - \lambda = 0$, i.e. $\l
ambda(\lambda - 1) = 0$, giving $\lambda \in \{0, 1\}$.

---

**Step 3. The spectrum is nonempty.**

In a unital complex Banach algebra (hence in a unital $C^*$-algebra), the spectr
um of every element is nonempty. Therefore $\sigma(p) \in \bigl\{\{0\},\, \{1\},
\, \{0,1\}\bigr\}$.

---

**Step 4. Pinning down which case occurs.**

We use two observations about projections:

- **A projection is invertible iff it equals $1$.** If $p$ is invertible, then f
rom $p^2 = p$ we multiply by $p^{-1}$ to get $p = 1$. Conversely $1$ is invertib
le.

- **$1 - p$ is again a projection.** Indeed $(1-p)^2 = 1 - 2p + p^2 = 1 - 2p + p
 = 1 - p$, and $(1-p)^* = 1 - p^* = 1 - p$.

Now:

**(a) $0 \in \sigma(p) \iff p \neq 1$.** The point $0 \in \sigma(p)$ means $p$ i
s not invertible, which by the observation above is equivalent to $p \neq 1$.

**(b) $1 \in \sigma(p) \iff p \neq 0$.** The point $1 \in \sigma(p)$ means $1\cd
ot 1 - p = 1 - p$ is not invertible. Since $1 - p$ is a projection, it is invert
ible iff $1 - p = 1$, i.e. iff $p = 0$. Hence $1 \in \sigma(p) \iff p \neq 0$.

Combining (a) and (b) with Step 3:

| Element | Spectrum |
|---|---|
| $p = 0$ | $\sigma(p) = \{0\}$ |
| $p = 1$ | $\sigma(p) = \{1\}$ |
| $p \neq 0$ and $p \neq 1$ | $\sigma(p) = \{0, 1\}$ |

---

**Conclusion.** For a self-adjoint idempotent (projection) $p$ in a unital $C^*$
-algebra,
$$\boxed{\sigma(p) \subseteq \{0,1\},}$$
with the precise value determined by the element:
- $\sigma(0) = \{0\}$,
- $\sigma(1) = \{1\}$,
- $\sigma(p) = \{0, 1\}$ for every projection $p \notin \{0, 1\}$.

In particular, any **nontrivial** projection (neither $0$ nor the unit) has spec
trum exactly $\{0, 1\}$.
