## Proof

**Step 1: Construct a two-sided identity element.**

Fix any element $c \in S$. By the solvability property applied to $x = c$, $y =
c$, there exists an element $e \in S$ such that
$$c * e = c.$$

**Claim:** $e$ is a two-sided identity for every element of $S$.

Let $a \in S$ be arbitrary. By the solvability property applied to $x = c$, $y =
 a$, there exists $z \in S$ such that $c * z = a$. Then:
$$a * e = (c * z) * e = c * (z * e) \quad \text{(associativity)} = c * (e * z) \
quad \text{(commutativity)} = (c * e) * z \quad \text{(associativity)} = c * z =
 a.$$

So $a * e = a$ for every $a \in S$. By commutativity, $e * a = a$ as well. Thus
$e$ is a two-sided identity element for $(S, *)$.

**Step 2: Every element has an inverse.**

Let $a \in S$ be arbitrary. By the solvability property applied to $x = a$, $y =
 e$, there exists $a' \in S$ such that
$$a * a' = e.$$
By commutativity, $a' * a = e$ as well. Thus $a'$ is a two-sided inverse of $a$.

**Step 3: $(S, *)$ is an abelian group, hence cancellation holds.**

Steps 1 and 2 show that $(S, *)$ is an abelian group (commutative, associative,
with identity $e$ and inverses). Now suppose $a, b, c \in S$ and $a * c = b * c$
. Let $c'$ be the inverse of $c$. Then:
$$a = a * e = a * (c * c') = (a * c) * c' = (b * c) * c' = b * (c * c') = b * e
= b.$$

Therefore $a = b$, as required. $\blacksquare$
