## Proof

**Theorem.** Let $K/k$ be a Galois extension and let $k \subset F \subset K$ be
an intermediate field. Then $K/F$ is Galois.

**Proof.**

Since $K/k$ is Galois, by the standard characterization $K$ is the splitting fie
ld over $k$ of a separable polynomial $f(x) \in k[x]$. Write

$$f(x) = (x - \alpha_1)(x - \alpha_2)\cdots(x - \alpha_n) \in K[x],$$

where the roots $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n$ are distinct (separability of $f$) a
nd

$$K = k(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n).$$

Now consider $f$ as a polynomial in $F[x]$ (this is legitimate since $k \subset
F$, so $k[x] \subset F[x]$). Because $k \subset F \subset K$, we have the chain
of inclusions

$$K = k(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n) \;\subset\; F(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n) \;\s
ubset\; K,$$

where the first inclusion holds since adjoining the same elements to a larger ba
se field can only give a larger (or equal) field, and the second holds because $
F \subset K$ and every $\alpha_i \in K$, so $F(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n) \subset
K$. Hence both inclusions are equalities:

$$K = F(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n).$$

Therefore $K$ is the splitting field of $f(x)$ over $F$. Moreover, $f$ still has
 $n$ distinct roots $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n$ in $K$ (the roots do not change
when we enlarge the base field), so $f$ is separable as a polynomial over $F$.

By the fundamental characterization, the splitting field of a separable polynomi
al over a field is a Galois extension of that field. Consequently $K/F$ is Galoi
s. $\blacksquare$

**Remark (equivalent viewpoint via automorphisms).** One may also see this throu
gh the automorphism characterization: $K/k$ Galois means $|\operatorname{Aut}(K/
k)| = [K:k]$ and $K = K^{\operatorname{Aut}(K/k)}$. Every $F$-automorphism of $K
$ is in particular a $k$-automorphism of $K$, so $\operatorname{Aut}(K/F) \leq \
operatorname{Aut}(K/k) = \operatorname{Gal}(K/k)$. The subgroup $\operatorname{A
ut}(K/F)$ fixes $F$ pointwise, and one checks $K^{\operatorname{Aut}(K/F)} = F$
(any element of $K \setminus F$ is moved by some automorphism of $K/k$, and a su
itable conjugate can be chosen to lie in $\operatorname{Aut}(K/F)$), giving $|\o
peratorname{Aut}(K/F)| = [K:F]$ and hence $K/F$ Galois. This is the content of t
he fundamental theorem of Galois theory, under which intermediate fields $F$ cor
respond to subgroups $H = \operatorname{Gal}(K/F) \leq \operatorname{Gal}(K/k)$,
 and $K/F$ is always Galois (only $F/k$ need not be).
