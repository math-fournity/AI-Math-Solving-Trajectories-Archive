## Solution

We compute
$$I(n)=\int_{|x|<n}\int_{|y|<n}\sin(x^2+y^2)\,dx\,dy.$$

**Step 1: Product-to-sum decomposition.**

Using $\sin(A+B)=\sin A\cos B+\cos A\sin B$ with $A=x^2$, $B=y^2$:
$$\sin(x^2+y^2)=\sin(x^2)\cos(y^2)+\cos(x^2)\sin(y^2).$$

Since the integrand is bounded on the finite square $[-n,n]^2$, Fubini's theorem
 separates the double integral:
$$I(n)=\left(\int_{-n}^{n}\sin(x^2)\,dx\right)\!\left(\int_{-n}^{n}\cos(y^2)\,dy
\right)+\left(\int_{-n}^{n}\cos(x^2)\,dx\right)\!\left(\int_{-n}^{n}\sin(y^2)\,d
y\right).$$

**Step 2: Use evenness.**

Both $\sin(x^2)$ and $\cos(x^2)$ are even functions, so
$$\int_{-n}^{n}\sin(x^2)\,dx=2\int_0^n\sin(x^2)\,dx=:2S(n),\qquad \int_{-n}^{n}\
cos(x^2)\,dx=2\int_0^n\cos(x^2)\,dx=:2C(n).$$

Substituting:
$$I(n)=(2S(n))(2C(n))+(2C(n))(2S(n))=8\,S(n)\,C(n).$$

**Step 3: Fresnel integrals.**

The classical Fresnel integrals converge (conditionally) to
$$\int_0^{\infty}\sin(x^2)\,dx=\int_0^{\infty}\cos(x^2)\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\fr
ac{\pi}{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8}}.$$

(These follow from the Gaussian integral via the substitution $u=x^2$ and contou
r integration of $e^{iz^2}$, giving $\int_0^\infty e^{ix^2}dx=\frac{1+i}{2}\sqrt
{\frac{\pi}{2}}$.)

Hence $S(n)\to\sqrt{\pi/8}$ and $C(n)\to\sqrt{\pi/8}$ as $n\to\infty$.

**Step 4: Take the limit.**

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}I(n)=8\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8}}=8\cd
ot\frac{\pi}{8}=\pi.$$

$$\boxed{\pi}$$
