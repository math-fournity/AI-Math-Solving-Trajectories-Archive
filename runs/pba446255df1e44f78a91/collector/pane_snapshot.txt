## Proof

We determine for which $p$ the series $f(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^{k}-\c
os k}{k^{3}}$ converges in $L^{p}([-1,1])$.

**Claim:** The series converges (absolutely) in $L^{p}([-1,1])$ for every $1 \le
 p \le \infty$.

**Key tool:** $L^{p}([-1,1])$ is a Banach space for every $1 \le p \le \infty$.
Hence, if $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \|g_k\|_{L^p} < \infty$ (absolute convergence), t
hen $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} g_k$ converges in $L^p$.

Set $g_k(x) = \frac{x^k - \cos k}{k^3}$. We bound $\|g_k\|_{L^p}$.

---

**Case $1 \le p < \infty$.** By the triangle inequality in $L^p$,

$$\|g_k\|_{L^p} = \frac{1}{k^3}\|x^k - \cos k\|_{L^p} \le \frac{1}{k^3}\Big(\|x^
k\|_{L^p} + |\cos k|\cdot\|1\|_{L^p}\Big).$$

Compute each piece on $[-1,1]$:

$$\|x^k\|_{L^p}^p = \int_{-1}^{1}|x|^{kp}\,dx = \frac{2}{kp+1}, \qquad \text{so
} \|x^k\|_{L^p} = \left(\frac{2}{kp+1}\right)^{1/p} \le 2^{1/p},$$

$$\|1\|_{L^p} = \left(\int_{-1}^{1}1\,dx\right)^{1/p} = 2^{1/p}, \qquad |\cos k|
 \le 1.$$

Therefore

$$\|g_k\|_{L^p} \le \frac{1}{k^3}\big(2^{1/p} + 2^{1/p}\big) = \frac{2^{1+1/p}}{
k^3}.$$

Since $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^3} < \infty$ (convergent $p$-series, $3>1$)
,

$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|g_k\|_{L^p} \le 2^{1+1/p}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^3}
 < \infty.$$

So the series converges absolutely in $L^p$.

---

**Case $p = \infty$.** For $x \in [-1,1]$, $|x^k| \le 1$ and $|\cos k| \le 1$, h
ence

$$\|g_k\|_{L^\infty} = \frac{1}{k^3}\sup_{x\in[-1,1]}|x^k - \cos k| \le \frac{1}
{k^3}(1+1) = \frac{2}{k^3}.$$

Thus

$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|g_k\|_{L^\infty} \le 2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^3} <
\infty,$$

and the series converges absolutely in $L^\infty$.

---

**Conclusion.** For every $1 \le p \le \infty$, the partial sums $S_N(x) = \sum_
{k=1}^{N}\frac{x^k - \cos k}{k^3}$ satisfy

$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|g_k\|_{L^p} < \infty,$$

which, by completeness of the Banach space $L^p([-1,1])$, implies that $S_N$ con
verges in the $L^p$ norm to a limit $f \in L^p([-1,1])$. Hence $f(x) = \sum_{k=1
}^{\infty}\frac{x^k - \cos k}{k^3}$ **converges in $L^p([-1,1])$ for all $1 \le
p \le \infty$**.
