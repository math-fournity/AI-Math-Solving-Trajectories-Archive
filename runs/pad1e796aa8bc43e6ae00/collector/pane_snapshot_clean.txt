Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every punctured smooth mani
fold $M \setminus \{p\}$ admits a non-complete smooth vector field.

**Answer: TRUE** (for manifolds of positive dimension, the standard setting for
this question).

## Proof

Let $M$ be a smooth manifold with $\dim M = n \geq 1$, and let $p \in M$. Set $N
 = M \setminus \{p\}$.

**Construction of a non-complete vector field on $N$.**

Choose a coordinate chart $(U, \varphi)$ around $p$ with $\varphi(p) = 0 \in \ma
thbb{R}^n$. Pick $\varepsilon > 0$ small enough that $\overline{B(0, 2\varepsilo
n)} \subset \varphi(U)$. Let $\psi \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ be a smooth bump
function satisfying:
- $\psi \equiv 1$ on $\overline{B(0, \varepsilon)}$,
- $\psi \equiv 0$ on $\mathbb{R}^n \setminus B(0, 2\varepsilon)$.

Define a vector field $X$ on $M$ by:
$$X_q = \begin{cases} \psi(\varphi(q))\, (\varphi^* \tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_
1})_q & \text{if } q \in U, \\ 0 & \text{if } q \notin U. \end{cases}$$

Since $\psi$ vanishes near the boundary of $\varphi(U)$, this is well-defined an
d smooth on all of $M$. Restricting to $N$, we obtain a smooth vector field $X|_
N$ on $N$.

**Showing $X|_N$ is not complete.**

Consider the point $q_0 \in N$ with $\varphi(q_0) = (-\varepsilon/2,\, 0,\, \ldo
ts,\, 0)$. Note $q_0 \in U$ and $\varphi(q_0) \in B(0,\varepsilon)$, so $q_0 \ne
q p$ and $q_0 \in N$.

Let $\gamma: I \to N$ be the maximal integral curve of $X|_N$ with $\gamma(0) =
q_0$. For as long as $\varphi(\gamma(t))$ remains in $B(0, \varepsilon)$ (where
$\psi \equiv 1$), the ODE in coordinates reads:
$$\frac{d}{dt}\varphi(\gamma(t)) = (1, 0, \ldots, 0),$$
so $\varphi(\gamma(t)) = (-\varepsilon/2 + t,\, 0,\, \ldots,\, 0)$.

At $t = \varepsilon/2$, this gives $\varphi(\gamma(\varepsilon/2)) = (0, 0, \ldo
ts, 0) = \varphi(p)$, i.e., $\gamma(\varepsilon/2) = p$. But $p \notin N$, so th
e integral curve **cannot be extended** to $t = \varepsilon/2$ within $N$. The m
aximal interval of definition $I$ is contained in $(-\infty,\, \varepsilon/2)$,
hence $I \neq \mathbb{R}$.

Therefore $X|_N$ is **not complete**: there exists an integral curve that is not
 defined for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$.

**Conclusion.** For every smooth manifold $M$ of positive dimension and every $p
 \in M$, the punctured manifold $M \setminus \{p\}$ admits a non-complete global
 smooth vector field. The statement is **TRUE**. $\blacksquare$
