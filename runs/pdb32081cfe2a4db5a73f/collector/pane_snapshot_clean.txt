Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum number of unit squares whose
 borders must be drawn so that every edge of the 25×25 grid is covered.

## Setup

The 25×25 grid has:
- **Boundary edges** (outer perimeter): 4 × 25 = 100 edges
- **Interior edges**: 2 × 25 × 26 − 100 = 1200 edges
- **Total**: 1300 unit edges

Each drawn unit square covers 4 edges. Shared edges between two drawn squares ar
e covered once but "cost" nothing extra.

## Step 1: Boundary squares are mandatory

Every boundary edge is adjacent to **exactly one** unit square (the one inside t
he grid). Therefore, all squares touching the boundary must be drawn. These are
the first/last rows and first/last columns:

$$4 \times 25 - 4 = 96 \text{ boundary squares (the "frame")}$$

These 96 squares cover all 100 boundary edges plus the 92 edges between the fram
e and the interior, plus 96 edges shared among frame squares themselves.

## Step 2: Remaining uncovered edges

After drawing the frame, the uncovered edges are exactly the **interior edges of
 the 23×23 interior grid** (rows 2–24, columns 2–24). Each such edge lies betwee
n two adjacent interior squares. To cover every such edge, at least one of its t
wo adjacent squares must be drawn.

This is precisely a **vertex cover problem** on the 23×23 grid graph (529 vertic
es, 1012 edges).

## Step 3: Minimum vertex cover via König's theorem

The grid graph is **bipartite**. By König's theorem, the minimum vertex cover eq
uals the maximum matching.

Coloring the 23×23 grid as a chessboard:
- Color A (corners): 265 squares
- Color B: 264 squares

The maximum matching size is 264 (all vertices of the smaller color class can be
 matched). Selecting all 264 squares of color B forms a vertex cover, since ever
y edge connects different colors.

**Verification that 264 is optimal:** No vertex cover can be smaller than the ma
ximum matching (264), so this is tight.

## Step 4: Verification

**Total squares:** 96 + 264 = 360.

**Edge count check:**
- 4 × 360 = 1440 edge-drawings total.
- Shared edges among frame squares: 96.
- Shared edges between frame and selected interior: 44 (the 44 color-B squares o
n the boundary of the 23×23 region each share 1 edge with the frame).
- Shared edges among selected interior: 0 (all same color, no adjacencies).
- Total shared: 96 + 44 = 140.
- Distinct edges covered: 1440 − 140 = **1300** ✓

**Optimality:** The 96 boundary squares are forced (each boundary edge has a uni
que adjacent square). The 264 interior squares form a minimum vertex cover (tigh
t by König's theorem). No smaller solution exists.

## Answer

$$\boxed{360}$$
