## Proof

Let $\gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_n$ be the roots of $p(x) = x^n + nx^{n-1} + a_2 x^{
n-2} + \cdots + a_n$. By Vieta's formulas,

$$\gamma_1 + \gamma_2 + \cdots + \gamma_n = -n.$$

**Step 1: Triangle inequality gives a lower bound on $\sum|\gamma_i|$.**

$$|\gamma_1 + \cdots + \gamma_n| \leq |\gamma_1| + \cdots + |\gamma_n|,$$

so

$$n = |{-n}| = \left|\sum \gamma_i\right| \leq \sum_{i=1}^n |\gamma_i|. \tag{1}$
$

**Step 2: Power-mean inequality gives a lower bound on $\sum|\gamma_i|^{16}$.**

For $p = 16 > 1$, the power-mean inequality states

$$\left(\frac{|\gamma_1|^{16} + \cdots + |\gamma_n|^{16}}{n}\right)^{1/16} \geq
\frac{|\gamma_1| + \cdots + |\gamma_n|}{n}.$$

Raising to the 16th power and using (1):

$$\frac{\sum|\gamma_i|^{16}}{n} \geq \left(\frac{\sum|\gamma_i|}{n}\right)^{16}
\geq \left(\frac{n}{n}\right)^{16} = 1.$$

Hence

$$\sum_{i=1}^n |\gamma_i|^{16} \geq n. \tag{2}$$

**Step 3: Equality forces all roots to coincide.**

The hypothesis is $\sum|\gamma_i|^{16} = n$, which is exactly equality in (2). E
quality in the chain (2) requires equality in **both** inequalities used:

- **Equality in the power-mean inequality** (with $p=16>1$) holds if and only if
 all the $|\gamma_i|$ are equal. Combined with $\sum|\gamma_i| \geq n$ and $\sum
|\gamma_i|^{16}/n = 1$, we get $|\gamma_i| = 1$ for every $i$.

- **Equality in the triangle inequality** $|\sum\gamma_i| \leq \sum|\gamma_i|$ h
olds if and only if all the $\gamma_i$ have the same argument (they lie on the s
ame ray from the origin). Since $\sum\gamma_i = -n$ is a negative real number, t
his common direction is the negative real axis.

Therefore every $\gamma_i$ has modulus $1$ and argument $\pi$, i.e.

$$\gamma_1 = \gamma_2 = \cdots = \gamma_n = -1.$$

**Step 4: Verification.**

If all roots equal $-1$, then $p(x) = (x+1)^n = x^n + nx^{n-1} + \binom{n}{2}x^{
n-2} + \cdots + 1$, whose coefficient of $x^{n-1}$ is $n$, matching the given po
lynomial. Moreover,

$$\sum_{i=1}^n |\gamma_i|^{16} = n \cdot |-1|^{16} = n \cdot 1 = n,$$

satisfying the hypothesis.

**Conclusion.** All the roots of $p(x)$ are $-1$; that is, $\gamma_1 = \gamma_2
= \cdots = \gamma_n = -1$ (with multiplicity $n$).
