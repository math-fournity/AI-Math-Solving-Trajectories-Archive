Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a compact, connected, orien
table $n$-dimensional homology manifold has a fundamental class.

**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

Let $X$ be a compact, connected, orientable $n$-dimensional homology manifold. W
e show $X$ admits a fundamental class $[X] \in H_n(X;\mathbb{Z})$.

### Step 1: The local homology sheaf

By definition of homology $n$-manifold, for every $x \in X$:
$$H_i(X, X\setminus\{x\};\mathbb{Z}) \cong \begin{cases} \mathbb{Z} & i = n, \\
0 & i \neq n.\end{cases}$$

Define the **local homology sheaf** $\mathcal{H}_n$ as the sheafification of the
 presheaf $U \mapsto H_n(X, X\setminus U;\mathbb{Z})$. Its stalk at $x$ is $\mat
hcal{H}_{n,x} = H_n(X, X\setminus\{x\};\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$. Since $X$
is a homology manifold, $\mathcal{H}_n$ is locally constant (locally isomorphic
to the constant sheaf $\underline{\mathbb{Z}}$).

### Step 2: Orientation trivializes the sheaf

An **orientation** of $X$ is a global section $\mu \in \Gamma(X, \mathcal{H}_n)$
 that generates each stalk $\mathcal{H}_{n,x} \cong \mathbb{Z}$. The existence o
f such a section (orientability) implies $\mathcal{H}_n \cong \underline{\mathbb
{Z}}$ (the constant sheaf). Under this isomorphism, $\mu$ corresponds to a globa
l section of $\underline{\mathbb{Z}}$ taking value $\pm 1$ at each stalk. Since
$X$ is connected, $\Gamma(X, \underline{\mathbb{Z}}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, so:
$$\Gamma(X, \mathcal{H}_n) \cong \mathbb{Z}, \quad \text{generated by } \mu.$$

### Step 3: Key isomorphism $H_n(X;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \Gamma(X, \mathcal{H}_n)$

There is a natural homomorphism
$$\Phi: H_n(X;\mathbb{Z}) \longrightarrow \Gamma(X, \mathcal{H}_n), \qquad \Phi(
\alpha)(x) = \text{image of } \alpha \text{ in } H_n(X, X\setminus\{x\};\mathbb{
Z}).$$

We claim $\Phi$ is an isomorphism. Consider the **Borel–Moore homology spectral
sequence** (see Bredon, *Sheaf Theory*, or Iversen, *Cohomology of Sheaves*):
$$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(X;\, \mathcal{H}_q) \;\Longrightarrow\; H^{\mathrm{BM}}_{q-p}(
X;\mathbb{Z}),$$
where $\mathcal{H}_q$ is the $q$-th local homology sheaf. Since $X$ is a homolog
y $n$-manifold, $\mathcal{H}_q = 0$ for $q \neq n$, so the spectral sequence has
 a single nonzero row at $q = n$:
$$E_2^{p,n} = H^p(X; \mathcal{H}_n) \;\Longrightarrow\; H^{\mathrm{BM}}_{n-p}(X;
\mathbb{Z}).$$

We check the differentials. The differential $d_r: E_r^{p,q} \to E_r^{p+r, q-r+1
}$. From $E_r^{0,n}$, the target is $E_r^{r, n-r+1}$, which vanishes for $r \geq
 2$ since $n - r + 1 \neq n$ (and $\mathcal{H}_q = 0$ for $q \neq n$). No differ
ential hits $E_r^{0,n}$ either, since that would require a term at $E_r^{-r, n+r
-1}$ with $p = -r < 0$, which is zero. Therefore:
$$E_\infty^{0,n} = E_2^{0,n} = H^0(X; \mathcal{H}_n) = \Gamma(X, \mathcal{H}_n),
$$
and the spectral sequence collapses, giving:
$$H^{\mathrm{BM}}_n(X;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \Gamma(X, \mathcal{H}_n), \qquad H^{\mat
hrm{BM}}_k(X;\mathbb{Z}) = 0 \text{ for } k \neq n.$$

Since $X$ is **compact**, Borel–Moore homology agrees with singular homology: $H
^{\mathrm{BM}}_n(X;\mathbb{Z}) = H_n(X;\mathbb{Z})$. Hence:
$$\Phi: H_n(X;\mathbb{Z}) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \Gamma(X, \mathcal{H}_n)$$
is an isomorphism.

### Step 4: Construction of the fundamental class

Combining Steps 2 and 3:
$$H_n(X;\mathbb{Z}) \;\xrightarrow{\;\Phi\;}\; \Gamma(X, \mathcal{H}_n) \;\cong\
; \mathbb{Z}.$$

The orientation class $\mu \in \Gamma(X, \mathcal{H}_n)$ corresponds under $\Phi
^{-1}$ to a unique class:
$$[X] := \Phi^{-1}(\mu) \in H_n(X;\mathbb{Z}).$$

This is the **fundamental class** of $X$. It satisfies:
- $[X]$ generates $H_n(X;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$;
- For every $x \in X$, the image of $[X]$ under $H_n(X;\mathbb{Z}) \to H_n(X, X\
setminus\{x\};\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ is the local orientation $\mu_x$.

### Conclusion

Yes, $X$ has a fundamental class. The compactness ensures Borel–Moore homology c
oincides with singular homology; the homology manifold condition ensures the loc
al homology spectral sequence collapses to a single row; orientability trivializ
es the local homology sheaf; and connectedness identifies the global sections wi
th $\mathbb{Z}$. The fundamental class $[X] \in H_n(X;\mathbb{Z})$ is the unique
 class whose local restriction at every point gives the chosen orientation. $\bl
acksquare$
