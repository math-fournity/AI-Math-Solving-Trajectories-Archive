## Solution

**Step 1: Factorize the divisor count constraint.**

Since $d(\phi(n)) = 2017$ and $2017$ is prime (checking: $\sqrt{2017} \approx 44
.9$, and $2017$ is not divisible by any prime up to $43$), the only way $d(m) =
2017$ for a positive integer $m$ is $m = p^{2016}$ for some prime $p$. This is b
ecause if $m = \prod p_i^{a_i}$, then $d(m) = \prod(a_i+1) = 2017$, and since $2
017$ is prime, we must have a single factor with $a_1 + 1 = 2017$, i.e., $a_1 =
2016$.

So we need $\phi(n) = p^{2016}$ for some prime $p$.

**Step 2: Determine which primes $p$ are feasible.**

Write $n = \prod q_i^{e_i}$. Then $\phi(n) = \prod q_i^{e_i - 1}(q_i - 1) = p^{2
016}$.

Each factor $q_i^{e_i-1}(q_i-1)$ must be a power of $p$.

**Case A: $q_i = p$.** Then the factor is $p^{e_i-1}(p-1)$. For this to be a pow
er of $p$, we need $p-1 = p^k$ for some $k \geq 0$. This gives $p - 1 = 1$ (i.e.
, $k=0$, $p=2$). For any $p > 2$, $p-1$ satisfies $1 < p-1 < p$, so it cannot be
 a power of $p$. **Thus $p$ can divide $n$ only if $p = 2$.**

**Case B: $q_i \neq p$.** Then $q_i^{e_i-1}$ must be a power of $p$. Since $q_i
\neq p$, this forces $e_i = 1$, and then $q_i - 1$ must be a power of $p$: $q_i
- 1 = p^{a_i}$, i.e., $q_i = p^{a_i} + 1$ must be prime.

**For odd $p$:** $p^{a_i}$ is odd, so $q_i = p^{a_i}+1$ is even, hence $q_i = 2$
 (the only even prime), giving $a_i = 0$. This contributes $p^0 = 1$ to the prod
uct — **zero exponent**. Since $p$ itself cannot divide $n$ (Case A fails), ever
y prime factor contributes exponent $0$. We cannot reach exponent $2016$. **Ever
y odd prime $p$ is impossible.**

**Step 3: Optimize for $p = 2$.**

We need $\phi(n) = 2^{2016}$. The prime factors of $n$ are:
- $2^{e_0}$, contributing $2^{e_0 - 1}$ (exponent $e_0 - 1$).
- Distinct odd primes $q_i = 2^{a_i}+1$ (Fermat primes), each contributing $2^{a
_i}$ (exponent $a_i$), with $e_i = 1$.

The constraint is $e_0 - 1 + \sum a_i = 2016$, so $e_0 = 2017 - \sum a_i$.

The candidate is $n = 2^{2017 - S} \cdot \prod (2^{a_i}+1)$, where $S = \sum a_i
$.

**Including any Fermat prime increases $n$.** Compare including a prime $q = 2^a
 + 1$ vs. obtaining that exponent from the $2$-part:
- Including $q$: multiplies $n$ by $2^a + 1$, gains exponent $a$.
- Using the $2$-part instead: multiplies $n$ by $2^a$ (increasing $e_0$ by $a$),
 gains exponent $a$.

Since $2^a + 1 > 2^a$ for all $a \geq 1$, every Fermat prime inclusion strictly
increases $n$. Formally, $\ln(n)$ changes by $\ln(1 + 2^{-a}) > 0$ when adding a
 prime with parameter $a$.

Therefore the minimum is achieved with **no Fermat primes** ($S = 0$, $e_0 = 201
7$):

$$n = 2^{2017}.$$

**Step 4: Verification.**

$$\phi(2^{2017}) = 2^{2016}, \qquad d(2^{2016}) = 2016 + 1 = 2017. \checkmark$$

Any other valid $n$ (with $p=2$ and Fermat primes included) is strictly larger,
and no odd $p$ works. Hence $2^{2017}$ is the smallest natural number satisfying
 $d(\phi(n)) = 2017$.

$$\boxed{n = 2^{2017}}$$
