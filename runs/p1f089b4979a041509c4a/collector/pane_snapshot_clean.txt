## Proof

Let $a = xz$, $b = yz$, $c = xy$, so $L = F(a,b,c)$ and $K = F(x,y,z)$.

**Step 1: $a, b, c$ are algebraically independent, so $K/L$ is algebraic.**

A monomial $a^i b^j c^k = (xz)^i(yz)^j(xy)^k = x^{i+k}y^{j+k}z^{i+j}$. The linea
r map $(i,j,k) \mapsto (i+k,\, j+k,\, i+j)$ has matrix $\begin{pmatrix}1&0&1\\0&
1&1\\1&1&0\end{pmatrix}$ with determinant $-2 \neq 0$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, hence i
s injective over $\mathbb{Z}$. Thus distinct monomials in $a,b,c$ yield distinct
 monomials in $x,y,z$. If $P(a,b,c)=0$ in $F[x,y,z]$, expanding gives a sum of d
istinct monomials in $x,y,z$ equal to zero, forcing every coefficient of $P$ to
be zero. So $a,b,c$ are algebraically independent and $\operatorname{trdeg}_F L
= 3 = \operatorname{trdeg}_F K$, making $K/L$ algebraic.

**Step 2: Key elements of $L$.**

$$x^2 = \frac{(xz)(xy)}{yz} = \frac{ac}{b} \in L, \qquad y^2 = \frac{bc}{a} \in
L, \qquad z^2 = \frac{ab}{c} \in L.$$

Also:
$$\frac{y}{x} = \frac{yz}{xz} = \frac{b}{a} \in L, \qquad \frac{z}{x} = \frac{yz
}{xy} = \frac{b}{c} \in L.$$

**Step 3: $K = L(x)$ and $:L] \leq 2$.**

Since $y = (b/a)\,x$ and $z = (b/c)\,x$ with $b/a,\, b/c \in L$, we get $y, z \i
n L(x)$, hence $K = F(x,y,z) = L(x)$. Since $x^2 = ac/b \in L$, the element $x$
satisfies $T^2 - ac/b \in L[T]$, so $:L] = [L(x):L] \leq 2$.

**Step 4: $x \notin L$, so $:L] = 2$.**

Give $F[x,y,z]$ the standard grading with $\deg x = \deg y = \deg z = 1$. The ge
nerators $a = xz$, $b = yz$, $c = xy$ are homogeneous of degree $2$. For any pol
ynomial $P \in F[a,b,c]$, writing $P = \sum_d P_d$ with $P_d$ homogeneous of deg
ree $d$ in $a,b,c$, the substitution $P_d(a,b,c)$ is homogeneous of degree $2d$
(even) in $x,y,z$.

Suppose for contradiction that $x \in L$, so $x = P(a,b,c)/Q(a,b,c)$ for some $P
, Q \in F[a,b,c]$, $Q \neq 0$. Then $x \cdot Q(a,b,c) = P(a,b,c)$ in $F[x,y,z]$.
 Every homogeneous component of $x \cdot Q(a,b,c)$ has **odd** degree (even degr
ee from $Q$ plus $1$ from $x$), while every homogeneous component of $P(a,b,c)$
has **even** degree. Equality forces both sides to be zero, contradicting $x \ne
q 0$.

Therefore $x \notin L$, which gives $:L] = [L(x):L] = 2$.

**Conclusion.** The extension $K/L$ is finite and

$$:L] = 2. \qquad \blacksquare$$
