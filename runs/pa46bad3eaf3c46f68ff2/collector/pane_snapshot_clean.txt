Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum number of 3-ton trucks that
**guarantees** transporting any set of containers totaling 10 tons, each ≤ 1 ton
.

**Answer: 5 trucks.**

---

## Proof

### Lower Bound: 4 trucks are not enough

**Counterexample.** Consider 13 containers each weighing 0.76 tons, plus 1 conta
iner weighing 0.12 tons.

- Total weight: $13 \times 0.76 + 0.12 = 9.88 + 0.12 = 10.00$ tons. ✓
- Each container ≤ 1 ton. ✓

Each truck has capacity 3 tons. A single truck can carry **at most 3** container
s of 0.76 tons, since $4 \times 0.76 = 3.04 > 3$.

With 4 trucks, the maximum number of 0.76-ton containers transportable is $4 \ti
mes 3 = 12$. But we have **13** such containers. The 0.12-ton container cannot s
ubstitute for a 0.76-ton container, so at least one 0.76-ton container remains u
ntransported.

Therefore, **4 trucks do not suffice** in general. ∎

### Upper Bound: 5 trucks always suffice

**Proof.** Use the **First Fit** algorithm: process containers one at a time, pl
acing each into the first truck with sufficient remaining capacity.

Suppose for contradiction that a **6th truck** is required. Let $w$ be the weigh
t of the first container placed into the 6th truck. This container did not fit i
n any of trucks 1–5, so each of trucks 1–5 has remaining capacity $< w$, meaning
 each has load $> 3 - w$.

Since every container weighs at most 1 ton, $w \leq 1$, so:

$$\text{load of each of trucks 1–5} > 3 - 1 = 2 \text{ tons}.$$

The total load in trucks 1–5 is therefore:

$$> 5 \times 2 = 10 \text{ tons}.$$

But the total weight of **all** containers is exactly 10 tons, and the container
 of weight $w$ (along with any remaining unplaced containers) has not yet been l
oaded. This means the total weight exceeds 10 tons — a **contradiction**.

Therefore, First Fit never opens a 6th truck, and **5 trucks always suffice**. ∎

### Conclusion

- 4 trucks are insufficient (counterexample above).
- 5 trucks are always sufficient (First Fit argument above).

The minimum number of trucks needed is $\boxed{5}$.
