Let $S_N = \sum_{n=1}^{N} X_n$ and let $S = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} X_n$ denote the
a.s. limit (which exists and is finite by hypothesis).

**Step 1: The partial sums converge in $L^2$.**

The random variables $(X_n)$ are independent with $E[X_n]=0$ and $E[X_n^2]=1/n^2
$. For $M < N$,

$$
E\big[(S_N - S_M)^2\big] = E\Big[\Big(\sum_{n=M+1}^{N} X_n\Big)^2\Big] = \sum_{n
=M+1}^{N} E[X_n^2] = \sum_{n=M+1}^{N} \frac{1}{n^2},
$$

where the cross terms vanish by independence and the zero-mean assumption. Since
 $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} 1/n^2 < \infty$, the right-hand side tends to $0$ as $M,N
\to \infty$. Thus $(S_N)$ is Cauchy in $L^2$, hence converges in $L^2$ to some r
andom variable $S_\infty$.

**Step 2: The $L^2$ limit equals the a.s. limit.**

A subsequence of $(S_N)$ converges a.s. to $S_\infty$ (since $L^2$ convergence i
mplies convergence in probability, which yields an a.s.-convergent subsequence).
 But the full sequence $(S_N)$ converges a.s. to $S$ by hypothesis. Therefore $S
_\infty = S$ a.s.

**Step 3: Exchange limit and expectation.**

$L^2$ convergence implies $L^1$ convergence, so

$$
E[S] = E[S_\infty] = \lim_{N\to\infty} E[S_N].
$$

By linearity of expectation (finitely many terms),

$$
E[S_N] = \sum_{n=1}^{N} E[X_n] = \sum_{n=1}^{N} 0 = 0
$$

for every $N$. Hence

$$
E\!\left[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} X_n\right] = E[S] = \lim_{N\to\infty} E[S_N] = 0.
$$
