## Solution

**Claim:** The positive integer is $n = 2$ (and in fact the property holds for e
very $n \geq 2$, while it fails for $n = 1$).

Let $T^2 = S^1 \times S^1$ denote the torus.

**Step 1: $T^2$ is aspherical.**

The universal cover of $T^2$ is $\mathbb{R}^2$, via the covering map
$$p: \mathbb{R}^2 \to S^1 \times S^1, \qquad (x,y) \mapsto (e^{2\pi i x}, e^{2\p
i i y}).$$
Since $\mathbb{R}^2$ is contractible, $T^2$ is a $K(\mathbb{Z}^2, 1)$ Eilenberg–
MacLane space. Consequently
$$\pi_k(T^2) = 0 \quad \text{for all } k \geq 2.$$

**Step 2: Every map $S^n \to T^2$ is nullhomotopic for $n \geq 2$.**

For $n \geq 2$, the set of based homotopy classes $[S^n, T^2]_*$ is the group $\
pi_n(T^2) = 0$. Hence every based map is based-nullhomotopic, and therefore ever
y free map $S^n \to T^2$ is nullhomotopic. In particular this holds for $n = 2$.

**Step 3: The property fails for $n = 1$.**

Consider the inclusion into the first factor
$$f: S^1 \to S^1 \times S^1, \qquad f(z) = (z, 1).$$
This map induces on $\pi_1$ the injection $\mathbb{Z} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Z}
^2$ sending $1 \mapsto (1,0)$, which is nonzero. A nullhomotopic map induces the
 zero map on $\pi_1$, so $f$ is **not** nullhomotopic. Thus $n = 1$ does not sat
isfy the requirement.

**Conclusion.**

The property "every continuous map $S^n \to S^1 \times S^1$ is nullhomotopic" ho
lds precisely for $n \geq 2$ and fails for $n = 1$. The smallest (and the reques
ted) positive integer is

$$\boxed{n = 2}.$$
