## Solution

Let $a_n$ denote the unique positive solution of $x^n = \cos x$. As $n\to\infty$
, for $0<x<1$ we have $x^n\to 0$, while $\cos x$ stays bounded away from $0$ nea
r $x=1$ (since $\cos 1\approx 0.5403>0$). At $x=1$, $x^n=1>\cos 1$. Hence the so
lution $a_n$ approaches $1$ from below. Write

$$a_n = 1 - \varepsilon_n,\qquad \varepsilon_n\to 0^+.$$

The defining equation becomes

$$(1-\varepsilon_n)^n = \cos(1-\varepsilon_n). \tag{1}$$

**Asymptotics of the two sides.** For the left side, using $\ln(1-u) = -u + O(u^
2)$,

$$\ln\bigl[(1-\varepsilon_n)^n\bigr] = n\ln(1-\varepsilon_n) = -n\varepsilon_n +
 O(n\varepsilon_n^2).$$

For the right side, expand $\cos$ around $1$:

$$\cos(1-\varepsilon_n) = \cos 1\cos\varepsilon_n + \sin 1\sin\varepsilon_n = \c
os 1 + \varepsilon_n\sin 1 + O(\varepsilon_n^2).$$

Hence

$$\ln\cos(1-\varepsilon_n) = \ln\cos 1 + \ln\!\Bigl(1+\varepsilon_n\tan 1 + O(\v
arepsilon_n^2)\Bigr) = \ln\cos 1 + \varepsilon_n\tan 1 + O(\varepsilon_n^2).$$

**Set $t_n := n\varepsilon_n$** (so $\varepsilon_n = t_n/n$). Taking logarithms
of (1) and substituting:

$$-t_n + O\!\left(\frac{t_n^2}{n}\right) = \ln\cos 1 + \frac{t_n}{n}\tan 1 + O\!
\left(\frac{1}{n^2}\right). \tag{2}$$

**Passing to the limit.** The sequence $t_n$ is bounded: indeed if $t_n\to+\inft
y$ the left side of (1) would tend to $0$ while the right side tends to $\cos 1>
0$; if $t_n\to -\infty$ (i.e. $\varepsilon_n$ not tending to $0$) this contradic
ts $a_n\to 1$. Any subsequential limit $t$ of $t_n$ satisfies, by letting $n\to\
infty$ in (2),

$$-t = \ln\cos 1,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad t = -\ln\cos 1.$$

Since every subsequential limit agrees, $t_n\to -\ln\cos 1$.

**Conclusion.**

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} n(1-a_n) = \lim_{n\to\infty} n\varepsilon_n = \lim_{n\to\inf
ty} t_n = -\ln\cos 1.$$

$$\boxed{\,\lim_{n\to\infty} n(1-a_n) = -\ln(\cos 1)\,}$$
