## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $\|A\| \ge \|B\|$.

**Key fact.** For a positive operator $T$ on a Hilbert space $H$,

$$\|T\| = \sup_{\|x\|=1} \langle Tx, x\rangle.$$

*Proof of the key fact.* Positivity gives $T = S^2$ for the unique positive squa
re root $S = T^{1/2}$ (via the continuous functional calculus for self-adjoint o
perators). Then by Cauchy–Schwarz,

$$|\langle Tx, x\rangle| = |\langle Sx, Sx\rangle| = \|Sx\|^2 \le \|S\|^2\|x\|^2
.$$

Since $T$ is self-adjoint (positive operators are self-adjoint), $\|T\| = \sup_{
\|x\|=1}|\langle Tx,x\rangle|$, and the bound above with $\|S\|^2 = \|T\|$ (beca
use $\|S^2\| = \|S\|^2$ for self-adjoint $S$) gives $\langle Tx,x\rangle \le \|T
\|\|x\|^2$. Hence

$$\sup_{\|x\|=1}\langle Tx,x\rangle \le \|T\|.$$

For the reverse inequality, set $M = \sup_{\|x\|=1}\langle Tx,x\rangle$. Using t
he polarization-type identity for the self-adjoint $T$,

$$\langle T(x+y), x+y\rangle - \langle T(x-y), x-y\rangle = 4\,\mathrm{Re}\,\lan
gle Tx, y\rangle,$$

and applying the bound $\langle Tz,z\rangle \le M\|z\|^2$ to $z = x\pm y$ with $
\|x\|=\|y\|=1$ and $x\perp y$ yields $|\langle Tx,y\rangle|\le M$. Taking the su
premum over unit $y$ gives $\|Tx\|\le M$ for every unit $x$, so $\|T\|\le M$. Co
mbining, $\|T\| = M$. $\square$

**Main argument.** Since $A \ge B$, the operator $A - B$ is positive, so for eve
ry $x \in H$,

$$\langle (A-B)x, x\rangle \ge 0 \implies \langle Ax, x\rangle \ge \langle Bx, x
\rangle.$$

Restricting to unit vectors and taking suprema,

$$\|A\| = \sup_{\|x\|=1}\langle Ax, x\rangle \ge \sup_{\|x\|=1}\langle Bx, x\ran
gle = \|B\|,$$

where both equalities use the key fact above (applicable since $A$ and $B$ are p
ositive, hence self-adjoint). $\blacksquare$
