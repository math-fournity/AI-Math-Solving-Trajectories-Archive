## Proof

**Setup.** Let $c_{00}$ denote the space of finitely-supported complex sequences
, equipped with the $\ell^1$ norm $\|x\|_1 = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} |x_n|$. Let $\e
ll^1$ be the Banach space of absolutely summable sequences with the same norm. N
ote that $c_{00}$ is **dense** in $\ell^1$: every $x \in \ell^1$ is the $\ell^1$
-limit of its truncations $x^{(N)} = (x_1, \dots, x_N, 0, 0, \dots) \in c_{00}$,
 since $\|x - x^{(N)}\|_1 = \sum_{n>N} |x_n| \to 0$.

Let $f: c_{00} \to \mathbb{C}$ be a non-zero continuous (bounded) linear functio
nal.

**Key fact (unique extension by continuity).** Let $X$ be a normed space, $Y \su
bseteq X$ a dense subspace, and $T: Y \to Z$ a bounded linear map into a Banach
space $Z$. Then $T$ extends **uniquely** to a bounded linear map $\widetilde{T}:
 X \to Z$ with $\|\widetilde{T}\| = \|T\|$.

*Proof of the key fact.* For $x \in X$, pick any sequence $(y_n) \subseteq Y$ wi
th $y_n \to x$. Then $(y_n)$ is Cauchy, and
$$\|T y_n - T y_m\| \leq \|T\|\,\|y_n - y_m\| \to 0,$$
so $(T y_n)$ is Cauchy in the complete space $Z$, hence converges. Define $\wide
tilde{T} x = \lim T y_n$. This is well-defined (independent of the approximating
 sequence, by a standard interleaving argument), linear, bounded with $\|\wideti
lde{T}\| = \|T\|$, and agrees with $T$ on $Y$. Uniqueness: any bounded extension
 agreeing with $T$ on the dense set $Y$ is forced by continuity to agree everywh
ere. $\square$

**Applying the key fact.** Here $X = \ell^1$, $Y = c_{00}$ (dense in $\ell^1$),
$Z = \mathbb{C}$ (complete), and $T = f$. Thus $f$ extends uniquely to a bounded
 linear functional $\widetilde{f}: \ell^1 \to \mathbb{C}$ with $\|\widetilde{f}\
| = \|f\|$.

**Every Hahn–Banach extension coincides with $\widetilde{f}$.** A Hahn–Banach ex
tension of $f$ to $\ell^1$ is, by definition, a bounded linear functional $F: \e
ll^1 \to \mathbb{C}$ such that $F|_{c_{00}} = f$ and $\|F\| = \|f\|$. In particu
lar $F$ is a bounded extension of $f$ from the dense subspace $c_{00}$, so by th
e **uniqueness** in the key fact, $F = \widetilde{f}$.

Conversely, $\widetilde{f}$ itself is a Hahn–Banach extension (it extends $f$ an
d preserves the norm), so at least one exists.

**Conclusion.** There is exactly **one** Hahn–Banach extension of $f$ to $\ell^1
$.
