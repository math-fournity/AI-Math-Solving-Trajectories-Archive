## Proof

We consider the simple random walk on an $n$-regular tree $T$ (a tree in which e
very vertex has exactly $n$ neighbors), with $n \geq 2$ (for $n=1$ the only such

**Reduction to a birth–death chain.** Fix the starting vertex $o$ as the root. D
efine $D_t$ to be the graph-distance from the walk $X_t$ to $o$. We claim $(D_t)
$ is a Markov chain on $\{0,1,2,\dots\}$ with transitions:
- From $0$: $D_{t+1}=1$ with probability $1$ (all $n$ neighbors are at distance
$1$).
- From $k\geq 1$: among the $n$ neighbors of a vertex at distance $k$ from $o$,
exactly one is at distance $k-1$ (the neighbor on the unique path back to $o$) a
nd $n-1$ are at distance $k+1$ (by the tree property). Hence
$$P(D_{t+1}=k-1\mid D_t=k)=\tfrac{1}{n},\qquad P(D_{t+1}=k+1\mid D_t=k)=\tfrac{n
-1}{n}.$$

The walk $X_t$ returns to $o$ iff $D_t$ returns to $0$. So transience of $X_t$ i
s equivalent to transience of $(D_t)$.

**Transience criterion for birth–death chains.** A birth–death chain on $\{0,1,2
,\dots\}$ with $p_k=P(k\to k+1)$, $q_k=P(k\to k-1)$, $r_k=P(k\to k)$ is transien
t iff
$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{q_i}{p_i}<\infty.$$
(This is the standard criterion obtained by computing the hitting probability of
 $0$ from $k$ via the recurrence $h_k=q_k h_{k-1}+p_k h_{k+1}$; the chain is tra
nsient iff $h_k<1$ for some $k$, equivalently the series converges.)

**Applying the criterion.** For $k\geq 1$ we have $p_k=\frac{n-1}{n}$ and $q_k=\
frac{1}{n}$, so
$$\frac{q_k}{p_k}=\frac{1}{n-1},\qquad \prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{q_i}{p_i}=\left(\fra

Thus
$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{n-1}\right)^{k}.$$

This geometric series converges iff $\frac{1}{n-1}<1$, i.e. $n-1>1$, i.e. $n\geq
 3$.

- **$n\geq 3$:** $\frac{1}{n-1}<1$, the series converges; the chain is **transie
nt**.

**Conclusion.** The Markov chain is transient precisely for $n\geq 3$.
