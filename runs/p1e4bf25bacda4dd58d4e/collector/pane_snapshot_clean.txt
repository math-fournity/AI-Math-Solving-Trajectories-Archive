## Proof

**Setup.** Let $X_1,\dots,X_n$ be i.i.d. with density $f(x,c)=c\,x^{c-1}$ on $(0
,1)$, $c>0$. The statistic $T=X_1X_2\cdots X_n$ is given to be complete sufficie
nt for $c$. By the Lehmann–Scheffé theorem, any unbiased estimator of $c$ that i
s a function of $T$ alone is the unique UMVUE.

**Change of variables.** Define $Y_i=-\ln X_i$. Since $x=e^{-y}$, $dx=-e^{-y}dy$
, and as $x:0\to 1$ we have $y:\infty\to 0$, the density of $Y_i$ is
$$f_Y(y)=c\,e^{-(c-1)y}\,e^{-y}=c\,e^{-cy},\qquad y>0.$$
Thus $Y_i\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\text{rate }c)=\mathrm{Gamma}(1,\text{rate }c)$, indep
endently.

**Distribution of the sum.** Let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i=-\ln T$. Then
$$S\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(n,\text{rate }c),\qquad f_S(s)=\frac{c^n}{\Gamma(n)}\,s^{n
-1}e^{-cs},\quad s>0.$$

**Finding an unbiased estimator.** For $n\ge 2$,
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{1}{S}\right]=\int_0^\infty \frac{1}{s}\,\frac{c^n}{\Ga
mma(n)}\,s^{n-1}e^{-cs}\,ds=\frac{c^n}{\Gamma(n)}\int_0^\infty s^{n-2}e^{-cs}\,d
s=\frac{c^n}{\Gamma(n)}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(n-1)}{c^{n-1}}=\frac{c\,\Gamma(n-1)}{\G
amma(n)}=\frac{c}{n-1}.$$

Hence
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{n-1}{S}\right]=c.$$

**Expressing in terms of $T$.** Since $S=-\ln T=-\ln(X_1\cdots X_n)$, the estima
tor
$$\widehat{c}=\frac{n-1}{-\ln(X_1X_2\cdots X_n)}=\frac{n-1}{-\sum_{i=1}^n \ln X_
i}$$
is unbiased for $c$ and is a function of the complete sufficient statistic $T$ a
lone.

**Applying Lehmann–Scheffé.** Because $T$ is complete sufficient for $c$, and $\
widehat{c}=g(T)$ is unbiased for $c$, the Lehmann–Scheffé theorem guarantees tha
t $\widehat{c}$ is the **unique** Uniform Minimum Variance Unbiased Estimator of
 $c$.

**Conclusion.** For $n\ge 2$, the UMVUE of $c$ is
$$\boxed{\widehat{c}_{\text{UMVUE}}=\frac{n-1}{-\ln(X_1X_2\cdots X_n)}=\frac{n-1
}{-\sum_{i=1}^n \ln X_i}.}$$

(For $n=1$ the expectation $\mathbb{E}[1/S]$ does not exist, so no unbiased esti
mator of this form is available; the result requires $n\ge 2$.)
