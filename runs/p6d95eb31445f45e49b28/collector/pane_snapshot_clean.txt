Looking at this problem, I need to analyze when $P(x,y) = 4 + x^2y^4 + x^4y^2 -
kx^2y^2$ can be written as a sum of squares of real polynomials, and find the cr
itical threshold $k$.

## Proof

**Step 1: Nonnegativity threshold.**

Setting $u = x^2 \geq 0$, $v = y^2 \geq 0$, we get $P = 4 + uv(u+v-k)$. By AM-GM
, $uv \leq \frac{(u+v)^2}{4}$. Let $t = u+v$. When $t < k$, the negative term $u
v(k-t)$ is maximized in magnitude at $\frac{t^2(k-t)}{4}$, which achieves its ma
ximum at $t = \frac{2k}{3}$, giving $\frac{k^3}{27}$. Thus:
$$\min P = 4 - \frac{k^3}{27}, \quad \text{achieved at } x^2 = y^2 = \frac{k}{3}
.$$
So $P \geq 0$ for all real $(x,y)$ if and only if $k^3 \leq 108$, i.e., $k \leq
3\sqrt[3]{4}$.

**Step 2: SOS decomposition structure.**

Suppose $P = \sum_i Q_i(x,y)^2$ with each $Q_i$ of degree $\leq 3$. Write:
$$Q_i = c_i + \underbrace{(u_i x + v_i y)}_{\ell_i} + \underbrace{(p_i x^2 + q_i
 xy + r_i y^2)}_{s_i} + \underbrace{(\beta_i x^2 y + \gamma_i xy^2)}_{t_i}$$

Matching homogeneous components of $P$ (which has only degrees 0, 4, 6):

**Degree 6** ($\sum t_i^2 = x^4y^2 + x^2y^4$): Expanding forces $\sum \alpha_i^2
 = 0$ (coefficient of $x^6$) and $\sum \delta_i^2 = 0$ (coefficient of $y^6$), s
o no $x^3$ or $y^3$ terms. Then $\sum \beta_i^2 = 1$, $\sum \gamma_i^2 = 1$, $\s
um \beta_i\gamma_i = 0$.

**Degree 4** ($\sum(s_i^2 + 2\ell_i t_i) = -kx^2y^2$): The $x^4$ coefficient giv
es $\sum p_i^2 = 0 \Rightarrow p_i = 0$. The $y^4$ coefficient gives $\sum r_i^2
 = 0 \Rightarrow r_i = 0$. So $s_i = q_i xy$.

**Degree 2** ($\sum(\ell_i^2 + 2c_i s_i) = 0$): With $p_i = r_i = 0$, the $x^2$
coefficient gives $\sum u_i^2 = 0 \Rightarrow u_i = 0$, and the $y^2$ coefficien
t gives $\sum v_i^2 = 0 \Rightarrow v_i = 0$. So $\ell_i = 0$.

**Degree 4 (revisited):** With $\ell_i = 0$ and $s_i = q_i xy$, the $x^2y^2$ coe
fficient gives:
$$\sum_i q_i^2 = -k.$$

**Step 3: The contradiction.**

Since each $q_i$ is real, $\sum q_i^2 \geq 0$. The equation $\sum q_i^2 = -k$ re
quires $k \leq 0$. For any $k > 0$, this is a **logical contradiction within the
 real number system** — a sum of squares of real numbers cannot equal a negative
 value.

**Step 4: Identifying the critical threshold.**

The nonnegativity threshold is $k = 3\sqrt[3]{4}$. At this value, $P$ is nonnega
tive (touching zero at $x^2 = y^2 = \sqrt[3]{4}$) but **cannot** be decomposed a
s a sum of squares, because the decomposition would require:
$$\sum_i q_i^2 = -k = -3\sqrt[3]{4} < 0,$$
which is impossible over $\mathbb{R}$. This is precisely the Motzkin phenomenon:
 a nonnegative polynomial that is not a sum of squares.

The sum of the squares of the coefficients of the $xy$ terms across all quadrati
c parts $s_i(x,y)$ equals exactly $-k$, and at the critical breaking point:

$$k = 3\sqrt[3]{4}.$$
