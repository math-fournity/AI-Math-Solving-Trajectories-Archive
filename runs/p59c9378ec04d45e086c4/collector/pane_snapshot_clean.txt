**Answer: Yes, they represent the same oriented knot.**

**Proof.**

Let $K = f_0(S^1) = f_1(S^1) \subset \mathbb{R}^3$. Since $f_0$ and $f_1$ are em
beddings with the same image, both are homeomorphisms $S^1 \xrightarrow{\sim} K$
. Set $\phi := f_1^{-1} \circ f_0 : S^1 \to S^1$, which by hypothesis is an orie
ntation-preserving homeomorphism. Equivalently, $f_0 = f_1 \circ \phi$.

We must show there exists an orientation-preserving ambient isotopy $H_t : \math
bb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ ($t \in [0,1]$, $H_0 = \mathrm{id}$) with $H_1 \circ f
_0 = f_1$.

The proof uses two standard facts:

**Fact 1 (Path-connectedness of $\mathrm{Homeo}^+(S^1)$).** Every orientation-pr
eserving homeomorphism of $S^1$ is isotopic to the identity.

*Proof of Fact 1.* Identify $S^1 = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$. An orientation-preser
ving homeomorphism $\phi$ lifts to a strictly increasing homeomorphism $\widetil
de{\phi} : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfying $\widetilde{\phi}(x+1) = \widet
ilde{\phi}(x)+1$. Define
$$\widetilde{\phi}_t(x) = (1-t)\,x + t\,\widetilde{\phi}(x), \qquad t \in [0,1].
$$
For $x < y$, both $\widetilde{\phi}(y)-\widetilde{\phi}(x) > 0$ and $y - x > 0$,
 so $\widetilde{\phi}_t(y) - \widetilde{\phi}_t(x) = (1-t)(y-x) + t(\widetilde{\
phi}(y)-\widetilde{\phi}(x)) > 0$; thus each $\widetilde{\phi}_t$ is strictly in
creasing. Moreover $\widetilde{\phi}_t(x+1) = \widetilde{\phi}_t(x)+1$, so $\wid
etilde{\phi}_t$ descends to an orientation-preserving homeomorphism $\phi_t : S^
1 \to S^1$ with $\phi_0 = \mathrm{id}$ and $\phi_1 = \phi$. $\square$

**Fact 2 (Isotopy Extension Theorem).** Let $M$ be a smooth (or PL) manifold and
 $K \subset M$ a compact submanifold. Any isotopy $F_t : K \to M$ ($F_0 = \mathr
m{id}_K$) extends to an ambient isotopy $H_t : M \to M$ with $H_0 = \mathrm{id}_
M$ and $H_t|_K = F_t$.

*Construction of the ambient isotopy.* Using Fact 1, let $\phi_t : S^1 \to S^1$
be an isotopy with $\phi_0 = \mathrm{id}$ and $\phi_1 = \phi = f_1^{-1} \circ f_
0$. Conjugating by $f_0$, define
$$F_t := f_0 \circ \phi_t \circ f_0^{-1} : K \longrightarrow K \subset \mathbb{R
}^3.$$
This is an isotopy of $K$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$ with $F_0 = \mathrm{id}_K$ and
$$F_1 = f_0 \circ \phi \circ f_0^{-1} = f_0 \circ f_1^{-1} \circ f_0 \circ f_0^{
-1} = f_0 \circ f_1^{-1}.$$

By Fact 2 (Isotopy Extension), $F_t$ extends to an ambient isotopy $H_t : \mathb
b{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ with $H_0 = \mathrm{id}$ and $H_t|_K = F_t$. In particu
lar $H_1|_K = F_1 = f_0 \circ f_1^{-1}$.

Now evaluate on $f_0$:
$$H_1 \circ f_0 = F_1 \circ f_0 = (f_0 \circ f_1^{-1}) \circ f_0 = f_0 \circ (f_
1^{-1} \circ f_0) = f_0 \circ \phi = f_1.$$

Thus $H_1 \circ f_0 = f_1$, so $f_0$ and $f_1$ are related by an orientation-pre
serving ambient isotopy. By definition, they represent the **same oriented knot*
*. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on the necessity of the orientation condition.** The hypothesis that $\
phi = f_1^{-1} \circ f_0$ is orientation-preserving is essential. If $\phi$ were
 orientation-reversing (so $f_0, f_1$ traverse $K$ in opposite directions), then
 $f_0$ and $f_1$ would represent a knot and its *mirror reversal*, which are gen
erally distinct as oriented knots (e.g., the two orientations of a chiral knot).
 An orientation-reversing self-homeomorphism of $S^1$ is **not** isotopic to the
 identity, so the argument above breaks down — and indeed the conclusion fails f
or chiral knots.
