## Answer

**No.** There is no metric on a non-trivial finite-dimensional real vector space
 $V$ that is compatible with the vector-space structure and makes $V$ compact. (
In fact, the argument works for *any* Hausdorff topological vector space, finite
-dimensional or not.)

---

### Proof

Interpret "a metric on $V$" in the natural sense for a vector space: a metric $d
$ such that $(V,d)$ is a **topological vector space**, i.e. addition $V\times V\
to V$ and scalar multiplication $\mathbb{R}\times V\to V$ are continuous. We sho
w that if such a metric makes $V$ compact, then $V=\{0\}$.

Suppose for contradiction that $(V,d)$ is compact and $V\neq\{0\}$. Pick $v\in V
$ with $v\neq 0$, and consider the sequence
$$x_n = n\cdot v,\qquad n=1,2,3,\dots$$
Since $(V,d)$ is compact (hence sequentially compact, being a compact metric spa
ce), $(x_n)$ admits a convergent subsequence $(x_{n_k})$ with $n_1<n_2<\cdots$,
say $x_{n_k}\to w\in V$.

By continuity of addition,
$$x_{n_{k+1}}-x_{n_k}=(n_{k+1}-n_k)\cdot v \;\longrightarrow\; w-w=0.$$
Set $m_k:=n_{k+1}-n_k\geqslant 1$. We have $m_k\cdot v\to 0$.

**Case 1: $(m_k)$ is bounded.** Then some integer $m\geqslant 1$ occurs infinite
ly often, so along a subsequence $m\cdot v\to 0$, which forces $m\cdot v=0$, hen
ce $v=0$ (since $m\neq 0$ in $\mathbb{R}$). Contradiction.

**Case 2: $(m_k)$ is unbounded.** Pass to a subsequence with $m_k\to\infty$. We
still have $m_k\cdot v\to 0$. By **joint** continuity of scalar multiplication $
\mathbb{R}\times V\to V$,
$$\tfrac{1}{m_k}\cdot(m_k\cdot v)\;\longrightarrow\; 0\cdot 0 = 0.$$
But $\tfrac{1}{m_k}\cdot(m_k\cdot v)=v$ for every $k$. Hence $v=0$. Contradictio
n.

Both cases contradict $v\neq 0$. Therefore no non-zero vector exists, so $V=\{0\
}$, contradicting the assumption that $V$ is non-trivial. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark on the interpretation.** If one drops compatibility with the vector-spa
ce structure and allows *any* metric on the underlying set, then the answer beco
mes trivially **yes**: $V\cong\mathbb{R}^n$ has the cardinality of the continuum
, as does $[0,1]$, so any bijection $V\to[0,1]$ pulls back the standard compact
metric on $[0,1]$. The specification "non-trivial vector space" signals that the
 intended reading is the structure-compatible one above, under which the answer
is **no**.
