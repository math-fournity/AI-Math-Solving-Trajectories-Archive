## Proof

Let $x = (x_1, x_2, x_3) \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and $\nabla = (\partial_1, \partial_2
, \partial_3)$. The operator $x \cdot \nabla$ is the Euler (radial derivative) o
perator:

$$x \cdot \nabla = \sum_{j=1}^{3} x_j \,\partial_j.$$

**Step 1. Compute $(x\cdot\nabla)\,x$.**

Treating $x$ as the identity vector field $x \mapsto x$, its $i$-th component is
 $x_i$. Applying the operator componentwise:

$$\bigl[(x\cdot\nabla)\,x\bigr]_i = \sum_{j=1}^{3} x_j\,\partial_j x_i = \sum_{j
=1}^{3} x_j\,\delta_{ij} = x_i.$$

Hence $(x\cdot\nabla)\,x = x$. (Equivalently, $x$ is homogeneous of degree $1$,
so Euler's theorem gives $(x\cdot\nabla)x = 1\cdot x = x$.)

**Step 2. Iterate.**

Since $(x\cdot\nabla)\,x = x$, applying the operator again to the result:

$$(x\cdot\nabla)^2 x = (x\cdot\nabla)\bigl((x\cdot\nabla)\,x\bigr) = (x\cdot\nab
la)\,x = x.$$

Repeating once more:

$$(x\cdot\nabla)^3 x = (x\cdot\nabla)\bigl((x\cdot\nabla)^2 x\bigr) = (x\cdot\na
bla)\,x = x.$$

**Conclusion.** The operator $(x\cdot\nabla)$ fixes the identity vector field $x
$, so every positive power of it fixes $x$ as well. Therefore

$$\boxed{(x\cdot\nabla)^3\,x = x.}$$
