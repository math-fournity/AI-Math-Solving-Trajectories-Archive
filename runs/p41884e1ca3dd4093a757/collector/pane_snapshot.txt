## Proof

Define
$$F(x) = f(x) + g(x) = \left(\int_0^x e^{-t^2}\,dt\right)^2 + \int_0^1 \frac{e^{
-x^2(1+t^2)}}{1+t^2}\,dt.$$

We compute $F'(x)$ and show it vanishes identically.

**Derivative of $f$.** By the chain rule and the fundamental theorem of calculus
,
$$f'(x) = 2\left(\int_0^x e^{-t^2}\,dt\right) e^{-x^2}.$$

**Derivative of $g$.** Differentiating under the integral sign,
$$g'(x) = \int_0^1 \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[\frac{e^{-x^2(1+t^2)}}{1+t^2
}\right] dt = \int_0^1 \frac{-2x(1+t^2)\,e^{-x^2(1+t^2)}}{1+t^2}\,dt = -2x\int_0
^1 e^{-x^2(1+t^2)}\,dt.$$

**Simplifying the integral in $g'$.** Substitute $u = xt$, so $dt = du/x$, with
$u:0\to x$:
$$\int_0^1 e^{-x^2(1+t^2)}\,dt = \int_0^x e^{-x^2 - u^2}\,\frac{du}{x} = \frac{e
^{-x^2}}{x}\int_0^x e^{-u^2}\,du.$$

Hence
$$g'(x) = -2x\cdot\frac{e^{-x^2}}{x}\int_0^x e^{-u^2}\,du = -2e^{-x^2}\int_0^x e
^{-u^2}\,du.$$

**Combining.** Therefore
$$F'(x) = f'(x) + g'(x) = 2e^{-x^2}\int_0^x e^{-t^2}\,dt - 2e^{-x^2}\int_0^x e^{
-u^2}\,du = 0.$$

So $F(x) = f(x) + g(x)$ is constant. Evaluating at $x = 0$:
$$f(0) = \left(\int_0^0 e^{-t^2}\,dt\right)^2 = 0, \qquad g(0) = \int_0^1 \frac{
1}{1+t^2}\,dt = \arctan(1) = \frac{\pi}{4}.$$

Thus $F(x) = \pi/4$ for all $x$, and in particular $F'(x) = 0$ for all $x$. Sett
ing $x = \sqrt{\pi}$:

$$f'(\sqrt{\pi}) + g'(\sqrt{\pi}) = F'(\sqrt{\pi}) = 0.$$
