## Solution

**Answer: Statement 3 is true.** The graph of $T^{-1}$ is closed.

### Proof

Recall the graph of $T$ is
$$G(T) = \{(x, Tx) : x \in X\} \subseteq X \times Y,$$
which is closed in $X \times Y$ (with the product topology) by hypothesis.

The graph of $T^{-1}$ is
$$G(T^{-1}) = \{(y, T^{-1}y) : y \in Y\} \subseteq Y \times X.$$

Consider the **swap map**
$$S : X \times Y \to Y \times X, \qquad S(x, y) = (y, x).$$

$S$ is a linear bijection, and it is a **homeomorphism** between $X \times Y$ an
d $Y \times X$ (each is equipped with the product topology; $S$ and $S^{-1}$ are
 both continuous, indeed isometric up to a constant under standard product norms
).

Now observe the key identity:
$$G(T^{-1}) = S(G(T)).$$

Indeed, $(x, Tx) \in G(T)$ maps under $S$ to $(Tx, x) = (y, T^{-1}y)$ with $y =
Tx$, which is exactly a generic element of $G(T^{-1})$.

Since $G(T)$ is closed in $X \times Y$ and $S$ is a homeomorphism, the image $S(
G(T)) = G(T^{-1})$ is **closed** in $Y \times X$.

This argument uses **only** the closedness of $G(T)$ and the topological fact th
at a homeomorphism preserves closed sets — it does **not** require $X$ or $Y$ to
 be complete. Hence statement 3 holds unconditionally under the given hypotheses
.

### Why the other statements need not hold

**Statement 1 is false.** $G(T) \cong X$ (via $x \mapsto (x, Tx)$), so it has th
e "size" of $X$, whereas $X \times Y$ has the "size" of $X \times Y$. They coinc
ide only in degenerate cases (e.g. $Y = \{0\}$), not in general.

**Statement 4 is false in general.** The Closed Graph Theorem requires $X$ and $
Y$ to be **Banach** (complete). The hypothesis only says *normed linear spaces*,
 so continuity of $T$ does not follow.

*Counterexample:* Let $X = Y = C[0,1]$, equipped with $\|\cdot\|_1$ (the $L^1$ n
orm) on the domain and $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ (the sup norm) on the codomain. Let $T
$ be the identity map. Then $T$ is bijective and linear. Its graph is closed: if
 $f_n \to f$ in $L^1$ and $f_n \to g$ uniformly, then $f_n \to g$ in $L^1$ as we
ll, so $f = g$ (both continuous). Yet $T$ is **not continuous**, since $\|f\|_\i
nfty$ cannot be bounded by $C\|f\|_1$.

**Statement 2 is false in general.** Continuity of $T^{-1}$ would follow from th
e Open Mapping Theorem, which again requires completeness.

*Counterexample:* Reverse the roles above. Let $T : (C[0,1], \|\cdot\|_\infty) \
to (C[0,1], \|\cdot\|_1)$ be the identity. Then $T$ is bijective, continuous, an
d has closed graph (uniform convergence implies $L^1$ convergence, so limits agr
ee). Its inverse $T^{-1} : (C[0,1], \|\cdot\|_1) \to (C[0,1], \|\cdot\|_\infty)$
 is the identity from the $L^1$ norm to the sup norm, which is **not continuous*
*.

### Conclusion

Only statement 3 is necessarily true: **the graph of $T^{-1}$ is closed**, becau
se it is the image of the closed graph of $T$ under the homeomorphic swap map $(
x,y) \mapsto (y,x)$.
