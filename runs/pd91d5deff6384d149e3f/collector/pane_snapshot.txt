# IMO 1997, Problem 5

**Problem.** Determine all pairs of integers $1 \le a, b$ satisfying $a^{b^2} =
b^a$.

## Answer Leak Check

The problem statement contains only the problem; no solution, sketch, or answer
is embedded. Proceeding.

## Solution

We claim the solutions are exactly
$$
(a,b) \in \{(1,1),\ (16,2),\ (27,3)\}.
$$

### Verification

- $(1,1)$: $1^1 = 1^1 = 1$. ✓
- $(16,2)$: $16^{2^2} = 16^4 = (2^4)^4 = 2^{16} = 2^{16} = 2^{16}$, and $2^{16}
= 2^{16}$. ✓
- $(27,3)$: $27^{3^2} = 27^9 = (3^3)^9 = 3^{27}$, and $3^{27} = 3^{27}$. ✓

### Proof of completeness

**Trivial cases.** If $a = 1$: $1^{b^2} = b \Rightarrow b = 1$. If $b = 1$: $a =
 1^a = 1 \Rightarrow a = 1$. If $a = b$: $a^{a^2} = a^a \Rightarrow a^2 = a \Rig
htarrow a = 1$. Hence any further solution has $a, b \ge 2$ and $a \ne b$.

**Reduction via $\gcd$.** Write $a = dx,\ b = dy$ with $d = \gcd(a,b)$ and $\gcd
(x,y) = 1$. The equation becomes
$$
d^{\,d^2 y^2}\, x^{\,d^2 y^2} \;=\; d^{\,dx}\, y^{\,dx}. \tag{1}
$$

We show **not both** $x \ge 2$ and $y \ge 2$.

Take any prime $p \mid x$ (so $p \nmid y$). Comparing $v_p$ in (1):
$$
d^2 y^2\bigl(v_p(d) + v_p(x)\bigr) = dx\, v_p(d),
$$
hence $d y^2\bigl(v_p(d) + v_p(x)\bigr) = x\, v_p(d)$. If $v_p(d) = 0$ the left
side is $\ge d y^2 \ge 1$ while the right side is $0$, contradiction; so $v_p(d)
 \ge 1$, and rearranging gives
$$
d y^2\, v_p(x) = v_p(d)\,(x - d y^2).
$$
The left side is positive, so $x > d y^2$. In particular **if $x \ge 2$ then $x
> d y^2$**.

Symmetrically, take any prime $q \mid y$ (so $q \nmid x$). Comparing $v_q$ in (1
):
$$
d^2 y^2\, v_q(d) = dx\, v_q(d) + dx\, v_q(y),
$$
so $v_q(d)(d y^2 - x) = x\, v_q(y) > 0$, forcing $v_q(d) \ge 1$ and $d y^2 > x$.
 Thus **if $y \ge 2$ then $d y^2 > x$**.

If both $x \ge 2$ and $y \ge 2$ we would need simultaneously $x > d y^2$ and $d
y^2 > x$ — impossible. Hence **either $x = 1$ or $y = 1$**.

---

**Subcase $x = 1$ (i.e. $a = d$, $b = a y$, so $a \mid b$).** Equation (1) gives
 $a^{a^2 y^2} = (a y)^a$, i.e.
$$
a^{a(a y^2 - 1)} = y^a.
$$
Taking the positive $a$-th root:
$$
y = a^{\,a y^2 - 1}. \tag{2}
$$

- If $y = 1$: (2) gives $1 = a^{a-1}$, so $a = 1$, yielding $(1,1)$.
- If $y \ge 2$: from (2), $\ln y = (a y^2 - 1)\ln a \ge (2 y^2 - 1)\ln 2$ (using
 $a \ge 2$; if $a = 1$ then (2) forces $y = 1$, contradiction). But $(2 y^2 - 1)
\ln 2 > \ln y$ for every $y \ge 2$ (at $y = 2$: $7\ln 2 \approx 4.85 > \ln 2$; a
nd $2y^2\ln 2$ grows faster than $\ln y$). Contradiction.

So this subcase yields only $(1,1)$.

---

**Subcase $y = 1$ (i.e. $b = d$, $a = b x$, so $b \mid a$).** Equation (1) gives
 $(b x)^{b^2} = b^{b x}$, i.e.
$$
x^{b^2} = b^{\,b(x - b)}. \tag{3}
$$

- If $x = b$: (3) gives $b^{b^2} = b^0 = 1$, so $b = 1$, $a = 1$: $(1,1)$.
- If $b = 1$: (3) gives $x = 1$, again $(1,1)$.
- Otherwise $b \ge 2$ and $x \ne b$. For the right side of (3) to be a positive
integer we need $x > b$. Taking the $b^2$-th root of (3):
$$
x = b^{\,(x - b)/b}.
$$
Thus $b \mid (x - b)$; write $x = b m$ with $m \ge 2$ (since $x > b$). Then
$$
x = b^{\,m - 1}, \quad\text{i.e.}\quad b m = b^{m-1}, \quad\text{i.e.}\quad m =
b^{\,m - 2}. \tag{4}
$$

Now solve (4) for integers $b \ge 2,\ m \ge 2$:

- $m = 2$: $2 = b^0 = 1$. ✗
- $m = 3$: $3 = b^1$, so $b = 3$. Then $x = bm = 9$, $a = bx = 27$, $b = 3$: **$
(27, 3)$**.
- $m = 4$: $4 = b^2$, so $b = 2$. Then $x = bm = 8$, $a = bx = 16$, $b = 2$: **$
(16, 2)$**.
- $m \ge 5$: $b^{m-2} \ge 2^{m-2} > m$ for all $m \ge 5$ (at $m = 5$: $2^3 = 8 >
 5$; the gap only widens), contradicting (4). ✗

So this subcase yields $(1,1)$, $(27,3)$, and $(16,2)$.

---

### Conclusion

Combining both subcases, the complete set of solutions with $1 \le a, b$ is

$$
\boxed{(a,b) \in \{(1,1),\ (16,2),\ (27,3)\}}.
$$
