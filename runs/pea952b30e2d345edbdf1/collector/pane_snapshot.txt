## Solution

**Setup.** The tangent to the parabola $y^2 = 4ax$ at the point $(at^2, 2at)$ is
$$ty = x + at^2. \tag{1}$$

Take three tangents at parameters $t_1, t_2, t_3$. The intersection of the tange
nts at $t_i$ and $t_j$ (solving (1) pairwise) is
$$V_{ij} = \bigl(a\,t_i t_j,\; a(t_i+t_j)\bigr). \tag{2}$$

So the three vertices of the tangent-triangle are
$$V_{12}=(a t_1 t_2,\, a(t_1+t_2)),\quad V_{13}=(a t_1 t_3,\, a(t_1+t_3)),\quad
V_{23}=(a t_2 t_3,\, a(t_2+t_3)).$$

**Imposing the condition.** Any two of the three vertices share a common tangent
 parameter, so without loss of generality suppose $V_{12}$ and $V_{13}$ (the two
 sharing $t_1$) lie on $x^2 = 4by$. Then
$$a^2 t_1^2 t_2^2 = 4b\cdot a(t_1+t_2) \;\Longrightarrow\; a\,t_1^2 t_2^2 = 4b(t
_1+t_2), \tag{3}$$
$$a\,t_1^2 t_3^2 = 4b(t_1+t_3). \tag{4}$$

Subtracting (4) from (3):
$$a\,t_1^2(t_2^2 - t_3^2) = 4b(t_2 - t_3).$$

For distinct vertices ($t_2 \neq t_3$), divide by $t_2 - t_3$:
$$a\,t_1^2(t_2 + t_3) = 4b. \tag{5}$$

**Finding the third vertex.** Let $(X, Y) = V_{23} = (a\,t_2 t_3,\; a(t_2+t_3))$
.

From (3), using $a\,t_1^2 = \dfrac{4b}{t_2+t_3}$ from (5):
$$\frac{4b}{t_2+t_3}\,t_2^2 = 4b(t_1+t_2) \;\Longrightarrow\; t_2^2 = (t_1+t_2)(
t_2+t_3).$$

Expanding the right side:
$$t_2^2 = t_1 t_2 + t_1 t_3 + t_2^2 + t_2 t_3 \;\Longrightarrow\; t_1(t_2+t_3) +
 t_2 t_3 = 0. \tag{6}$$

Hence
$$t_2 t_3 = -t_1(t_2+t_3) = -t_1\cdot\frac{4b}{a\,t_1^2} = -\frac{4b}{a\,t_1}. \
tag{7}$$

Therefore the coordinates of the third vertex are
$$X = a\,t_2 t_3 = -\frac{4b}{t_1}, \qquad Y = a(t_2+t_3) = \frac{4b}{t_1^2}. \t
ag{8}$$

**Eliminating the parameter.** From $X = -4b/t_1$ we get $t_1 = -4b/X$, and
$$Y = \frac{4b}{t_1^2} = \frac{4b\,X^2}{16b^2} = \frac{X^2}{4b}.$$

Equivalently,
$$X^2 = 4b\,Y. \tag{9}$$

**Conclusion.** The third vertex $(X,Y)$ satisfies $x^2 = 4by$. Thus the locus o
f the third vertex is precisely the same parabola $x^2 = 4by$ on which the other
 two vertices were required to lie.
