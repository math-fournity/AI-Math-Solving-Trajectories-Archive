## Solution

We find all Möbius (fractional linear) transformations $T(z) = \dfrac{az+b}{cz+d
}$, $ad-bc\neq 0$, that map the upper half-plane $H=\{\operatorname{Im}z>0\}$ bi
jectively onto the unit disk $D=\{|w|<1\}$.

**Claim.** The complete family is
$$\boxed{\,T(z) \;=\; e^{i\theta}\,\frac{z-z_0}{z-\overline{z_0}}\,},\qquad z_0\
in H,\ \theta\in\mathbb R.$$

---

### Proof that every such $T$ is an isomorphism $H\to D$

Fix $z_0\in H$ (so $\operatorname{Im}z_0>0$, hence $\overline{z_0}\in$ lower hal
f-plane) and $\theta\in\mathbb R$. The map
$$T(z)=e^{i\theta}\frac{z-z_0}{z-\overline{z_0}}$$
is a Möbius transformation (its pole $\overline{z_0}$ lies in the **lower** half
-plane, so $T$ is holomorphic on $H$) with determinant $z_0-\overline{z_0}=2i\,\
operatorname{Im}z_0\neq 0$.

**Boundary behavior.** For $x\in\mathbb R$,
$$|T(x)|=\frac{|x-z_0|}{|x-\overline{z_0}|}=\frac{|x-z_0|}{\,\overline{|x-z_0|}\
,}=1,$$
since $\overline{x-z_0}=x-\overline{z_0}$ for real $x$. Thus $T$ maps $\partial
H=\mathbb R\cup\{\infty\}$ into $\partial D=\{|w|=1\}$; being a Möbius transform
ation (hence a bijection of the Riemann sphere), it maps $\partial H$ **onto** $
\partial D$.

**Interior behavior.** $T(z_0)=0\in D$, and $z_0\in H$. A Möbius transformation
maps connected components of the complement of a circle/line to connected compon
ents of the complement of the image circle/line. Since $\partial H\mapsto\partia
l D$ and the single test point $z_0\in H$ lands in $D$, we conclude $T(H)=D$. Be
ing a non-constant Möbius transformation, $T$ is bijective $H\to D$. ✓

---

### Proof that every isomorphism $H\to D$ is of this form

Let $T(z)=\dfrac{az+b}{cz+d}$ be any Möbius transformation with $T(H)=D$.

1. **The pole lies in the lower half-plane.** Since $T(H)=D$ consists of finite
values, $T$ has no pole in $H$. The pole $p:=T^{-1}(\infty)$ lies in the complem
ent of $\overline H$ in the sphere, i.e. in the lower half-plane $\{\operatornam
e{Im}z<0\}$ (it cannot lie on $\mathbb R$, since $\mathbb R=\partial H$ maps to
$\partial D$, which does not contain $\infty$).

2. **The zero lies in $H$.** Let $z_0:=T^{-1}(0)$. Since $0\in D=T(H)$, we have
$z_0\in H$.

3. **Form is forced.** A Möbius transformation is determined up to a multiplicat
ive constant by its zero and pole:
$$T(z)=k\,\frac{z-z_0}{z-p},\qquad k\in\mathbb C^*.$$

4. **The boundary condition pins down $p$ and $|k|$.** Since $T(\partial H)=\par
tial D$, for every $x\in\mathbb R$,
$$1=|T(x)|=|k|\,\frac{|x-z_0|}{|x-p|}.$$
Squaring and expanding:
$$|x-z_0|^2=\frac{1}{|k|^2}|x-p|^2,\qquad\forall\,x\in\mathbb R.$$
Comparing the coefficient of $x^2$ gives $1=\dfrac{1}{|k|^2}$, so $|k|=1$. Then
$|x-z_0|^2=|x-p|^2$ for all real $x$; expanding and comparing the linear and con
stant terms yields $p=\overline{z_0}$.

5. **Conclusion.** With $p=\overline{z_0}$ and $|k|=1$, write $k=e^{i\theta}$, $
\theta\in\mathbb R$. Then
$$T(z)=e^{i\theta}\frac{z-z_0}{z-\overline{z_0}},\qquad z_0\in H,\ \theta\in\mat
hbb R. \qquad\blacksquare$$

---

### Remarks on the parameters

- $z_0\in H$ is the (unique) point sent to $0$; it is a free parameter ranging o
ver the 2-dimensional set $H$.
- $\theta\in\mathbb R$ is a free phase, ranging over the circle $S^1$.
- Together they give a 3-real-parameter family, matching $\dim\operatorname{Aut}
(\widehat{\mathbb C})-\dim(\text{stabilizer})=6-3=3$.
- Special case: $z_0=i$, $\theta=0$ gives the classical Cayley map $T(z)=\dfrac{
z-i}{z+i}$.
