## Proof

Yes. In the Hilbert space $L^2([0,1])$, weak convergence together with convergen
ce of norms implies strong (norm) convergence. This is the **Radon–Riesz (Kadec–
Klee) property**, which holds in every Hilbert space.

Let $f_n \rightharpoonup f$ weakly in $L^2([0,1])$ and suppose $\|f_n\|_{2} \to
\|f\|_{2}$. We show $\|f_n - f\|_{2} \to 0$.

Recall that $L^2([0,1])$ is a Hilbert space with inner product
$$\langle g, h\rangle = \int_0^1 g(t)\,\overline{h(t)}\,dt,$$
and the norm satisfies $\|g\|_2^2 = \langle g, g\rangle$.

Expand the squared distance:
$$\|f_n - f\|_2^2 = \langle f_n - f,\, f_n - f\rangle = \|f_n\|_2^2 - \langle f_
n, f\rangle - \langle f, f_n\rangle + \|f\|_2^2.$$

Over the reals this is simply
$$\|f_n - f\|_2^2 = \|f_n\|_2^2 - 2\langle f_n, f\rangle + \|f\|_2^2.$$

(Over $\mathbb{C}$ the same conclusion follows since $\langle f, f_n\rangle = \o
verline{\langle f_n, f\rangle}$, and both terms converge to $\|f\|_2^2$.)

Now use the two hypotheses:

1. **Weak convergence** $f_n \rightharpoonup f$ means, by definition, that for e
very $g \in L^2([0,1])$,
   $$\langle f_n, g\rangle \to \langle f, g\rangle.$$
   Taking $g = f$ (which belongs to $L^2$), we get
   $$\langle f_n, f\rangle \to \langle f, f\rangle = \|f\|_2^2.$$

2. **Norm convergence** $\|f_n\|_2 \to \|f\|_2$ gives, by continuity of squaring
,
   $$\|f_n\|_2^2 \to \|f\|_2^2.$$

Substituting both limits into the expansion:
$$\|f_n - f\|_2^2 \to \|f\|_2^2 - 2\|f\|_2^2 + \|f\|_2^2 = 0.$$

Hence $\|f_n - f\|_2 \to 0$, i.e. $f_n \to f$ strongly in $L^2([0,1])$.

**Remark on the role of the Hilbert structure.** The argument hinges on the iden
tity $\|f_n - f\|^2 = \|f_n\|^2 - 2\langle f_n,f\rangle + \|f\|^2$, which expres
ses the norm squared as a bilinear (inner-product) quantity. Weak convergence co
ntrols the cross term $\langle f_n, f\rangle$ precisely because it is an inner p
roduct against the fixed element $f$. The hypothesis $\|f_n\|\to\|f\|$ then forc
es the distance to vanish. This is exactly the Radon–Riesz property, valid in al
l Hilbert spaces (and more generally in spaces with the Kadec–Klee property, e.g
. uniformly convex Banach spaces such as $L^p$ for $1<p<\infty$).

Therefore, weak convergence plus convergence of norms **does** imply strong conv
ergence in $L^2([0,1])$.
