## Solution

**Answer: Yes, the property holds.** That is,

$$P\!\left(\liminf_{t\to\infty} e^{\mu t+\sigma B_t}>1\right)=1.$$

### Proof

Set $f(t)=\mu t+\sigma B_t$. Since $\exp$ is continuous and strictly increasing,
 for any real-valued function $f$,

$$\liminf_{t\to\infty} e^{f(t)}=e^{\liminf_{t\to\infty} f(t)}.$$

Hence the event $\bigl\{\liminf_{t\to\infty} e^{\mu t+\sigma B_t}>1\bigr\}$ is i
dentical to

$$\Bigl\{\liminf_{t\to\infty}(\mu t+\sigma B_t)>0\Bigr\}.$$

Factoring out $\sigma>0$, with $a:=\mu/\sigma>0$, this becomes

$$\Bigl\{\liminf_{t\to\infty}\bigl(B_t+at\bigr)>0\Bigr\}.$$

So it suffices to show that, almost surely, $B_t+at\to+\infty$ as $t\to\infty$;
then its $\liminf$ equals $+\infty>0$.

**Key fact (strong law for Brownian motion).** $B_t/t\to 0$ almost surely as $t\
to\infty$.

*Proof of the fact.* The increments $X_n:=B_n-B_{n-1}$ ($n\ge 1$) are i.i.d. $N(
0,1)$, so by the classical strong law of large numbers, $B_n/n=\frac1n\sum_{k=1}
^n X_k\to 0$ a.s. For $t\in[n,n+1)$, write $B_t = B_n + (B_t-B_n)$. By the refle
ction principle and a union bound,

$$P\!\Bigl(\sup_{0\le s\le 1}|B_{n+s}-B_n|>\varepsilon n\Bigr)\le 4\,P\bigl(|N(0
,1)|>\varepsilon n\bigr),$$

which is summable in $n$. By Borel–Cantelli, $\sup_{s\in[0,1]}|B_{n+s}-B_n|/n\to
 0$ a.s. Combining,

$$\sup_{t\in[n,n+1)}\frac{|B_t|}{t}\le \frac{|B_n|+\sup_{s\in[0,1]}|B_{n+s}-B_n|
}{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0\quad\text{a.s.},$$

so $B_t/t\to 0$ a.s. ∎

Applying the fact,

$$\frac{B_t+at}{t}=\frac{B_t}{t}+a\;\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{}\;a>0\quad\text{a.
s.}$$

In particular $B_t+at\to+\infty$ a.s., and therefore

$$\liminf_{t\to\infty}(B_t+at)=+\infty>0\quad\text{a.s.}$$

Consequently

$$P\!\left(\liminf_{t\to\infty} e^{\mu t+\sigma B_t}>1\right)=P\!\left(\liminf_{
t\to\infty}(B_t+at)>0\right)=1.$$

The property holds for every $\mu>0$ and $\sigma>0$. $\blacksquare$
