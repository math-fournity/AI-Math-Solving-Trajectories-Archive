## Proof

**Claim:** Up to rigid motion (rotation and translation of $\mathbb{R}^3$), ther
e is exactly **one** analytic isometric embedding of a 2-dimensional manifold ca
rrying the round metric of $S^2$ into $\mathbb{R}^3$, namely the standard round
sphere.

---

**Setup.** Let $(M, g)$ be a 2-dimensional manifold isometric to the unit sphere
 $S^2$ with its standard round metric. Thus $M$ is compact, boundaryless, and ha
s constant Gaussian curvature $K \equiv 1$. Let $\mathbf{r}: M \to \mathbb{R}^3$
 be an analytic isometric embedding, meaning $\mathbf{r}$ is given by analytic f
unctions of two parameters and the induced metric $\mathbf{r}^*(\delta) = g$.

**Key fact (Gauss's Theorema Egregium).** The Gaussian curvature is an intrinsic
 invariant of the induced metric. Since $(M,g)$ has $K \equiv 1$ intrinsically,
the image surface $\Sigma = \mathbf{r}(M) \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ has constant ext
rinsic Gaussian curvature $K \equiv 1$.

**Key fact (Liebmann's Theorem, 1900).** *Every closed (compact, without boundar
y) surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$ with constant positive Gaussian curvature is a roun
d sphere.*

*Proof sketch of Liebmann's theorem.* A closed surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$ with $K
 > 0$ everywhere is strictly convex (by Hadamard's theorem), hence homeomorphic
to $S^2$. On such a surface, if $K \equiv c > 0$ is constant, one uses the Minko
wski support function and the fact that the principal curvatures $\kappa_1, \kap
pa_2$ satisfy $\kappa_1 \kappa_2 = c$. A classical argument (using the maximum p
rinciple applied to the principal curvatures on the convex surface, or equivalen
tly via Hilbert's lemma on the Codazzi equations) shows that $\kappa_1 = \kappa_
2$ everywhere, i.e., every point is an umbilic. A surface all of whose points ar
e umbilic is contained in a plane or a sphere; since $K > 0$, it is a round sphe
re. $\square$

**Application.** Since $M \cong S^2$ is compact and boundaryless, $\Sigma = \mat
hbf{r}(M)$ is a closed surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$ with constant $K = 1 > 0$. By L
iebmann's theorem, $\Sigma$ is a round sphere of radius $R = 1/\sqrt{K} = 1$.

**Uniqueness up to rigid motion (Cohn-Vossen Rigidity, 1927).** Two closed conve
x surfaces in $\mathbb{R}^3$ that are isometric are congruent (related by a rigi
d motion). Since $\Sigma$ is a round unit sphere, any two such embeddings differ
 only by a rigid motion of $\mathbb{R}^3$.

**Analyticity is automatic.** The standard parametrization of the unit sphere,
$$\mathbf{r}(\theta, \phi) = (\sin\theta\cos\phi,\; \sin\theta\sin\phi,\; \cos\t
heta),$$
is analytic. Moreover, by the analytic version of the rigidity theorem, the isom
etric embedding of a real-analytic metric is itself real-analytic, so the analyt
icity requirement imposes no additional restriction—it is satisfied by the uniqu
e embedding.

---

**Conclusion.** Up to rigid motion, there is exactly **one** analytic isometric
embedding of the round $S^2$ metric into $\mathbb{R}^3$: the standard unit spher
e.
