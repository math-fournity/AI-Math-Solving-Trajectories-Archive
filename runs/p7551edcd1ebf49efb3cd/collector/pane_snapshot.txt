## Proof

**Step 1: Simplify the inequality.**

Expanding both sides:
$$x^2+y^2+z^2 + 2k(x+y+z) + 3k^2 \ge 3k^2+6k+3$$

$$\iff f(x,y,z) := x^2+y^2+z^2 + 2k(x+y+z) - 6k - 3 \ge 0.$$

At $x=y=z=1$ (which satisfies $xyz=1$): $f = 3+6k-6k-3 = 0$. So the inequality i
s **tight** at $(1,1,1)$ for every $k$. We need $(1,1,1)$ to be a **global minim
um** of $f$ on $\{xyz=1, x,y,z>0\}$.

**Step 2: Second-order condition at $(1,1,1)$.**

Set $x=1+a, y=1+b, z=1+c$ with constraint $a+b+c+ab+bc+ca+abc=0$. To first order
 $a+b+c=0$ (so $c=-a-b$); to second order $a+b+c=-(ab+bc+ca)$.

$$f = (2+2k)(a+b+c) + (a^2+b^2+c^2) \approx -(2+2k)(ab+bc+ca) + (a^2+b^2+c^2).$$

With $c=-a-b$: $ab+bc+ca = -a^2-b^2-ab$ and $a^2+b^2+c^2 = 2a^2+2b^2+2ab$. So:

$$f \approx (2+2k)(a^2+b^2+ab) + 2(a^2+b^2+ab) = (4+2k)(a^2+b^2+ab).$$

Since $a^2+b^2+ab = (a+b/2)^2+3b^2/4 \ge 0$, we need $4+2k \ge 0$, i.e., **$k \g
e -2$** for $(1,1,1)$ to be a local minimum.

**Step 3: Find all critical points via Lagrange multipliers.**

From $\nabla f = \lambda \nabla(xyz)$: $2x+2k=\lambda yz$, etc. Comparing pairs
gives $(x_i - x_j)(x_i+x_j+k)=0$. So at any critical point, either all variables
 are equal (giving $(1,1,1)$), or two are equal and the third satisfies a sum co
ndition.

**Two-equal critical points:** Let $x=y=t>0$, $z=1/t^2$. The Lagrange condition
gives $k = -t - 1/t^2$, equivalently $t^3 + kt^2 + 1 = 0$.

**Step 4: Evaluate $f$ at two-equal critical points.**

Substituting $x=y=t$, $z=1/t^2$, $k=-t-1/t^2$ into $f$:

$$f = -2t^2 + 6t - \frac{6}{t} + \frac{6}{t^2} - \frac{1}{t^4} - 3.$$

Multiplying by $t^4 > 0$ and factoring (verified by polynomial division):

$$t^4 f = (t-1)^3(t+1)(1+2t-2t^2).$$

The factor $1+2t-2t^2=0$ has positive root $t_0 = \frac{1+\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

**Sign analysis for $t>0$:**
- $(t+1)>0$ and $t^4>0$ always.
- $(t-1)^3$: negative for $0<t<1$, positive for $t>1$.
- $(1+2t-2t^2)$: positive for $0<t<t_0$, negative for $t>t_0$.

Therefore:
- $f<0$ when $0<t<1$ or $t>t_0$,
- $f>0$ when $1<t<t_0$,
- $f=0$ when $t=1$ (i.e., $k=-2$) or $t=t_0$.

**Step 5: Determine the threshold for $k$.**

For a given $k$, two-equal critical points exist when $p(t)=t^3+kt^2+1=0$ has po
sitive roots. The critical point at $t=t_0=\frac{1+\sqrt{3}}{2}$ corresponds to:

$$k_0 = -t_0 - \frac{1}{t_0^2} = -\frac{1+\sqrt{3}}{2} - (4-2\sqrt{3}) = \frac{-
9+3\sqrt{3}}{2}.$$

(Using $t_0^2 = \frac{2+\sqrt{3}}{2}$, so $1/t_0^2 = 4-2\sqrt{3}$.)

**For $k \ge 0$:** $p(t) = t^3+kt^2+1 > 0$ for all $t>0$ (since all terms positi
ve). No two-equal critical points; only $(1,1,1)$ with $f=0$. Inequality holds.
✓

**For $k_0 < k < 0$ (where $k_0 = \frac{-9+3\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx -1.902$):** Eit
her no positive roots exist, or both positive roots lie in $(1, t_0)$ where $f>0
$. Inequality holds. ✓

**At $k = k_0$:** The larger root is exactly $t_0$ where $f=0$; the smaller root
 is in $(1,t_0)$ where $f>0$. So $f\ge 0$ everywhere. Inequality holds. ✓

**For $k < k_0$ (with $k > -2$):** The larger root exceeds $t_0$, giving $f<0$ a
t that critical point. Inequality **fails**. ✗

**For $k \le -2$:** Either $(1,1,1)$ is not a local minimum ($k<-2$), or at $k=-
2$ the root $t=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2} > t_0$ gives $f<0$. Inequality **fails**. ✗

**Step 6: Boundary behavior confirms global minimum.**

As any variable $\to 0^+$ or $\to+\infty$ (with $xyz=1$), the $x^2+y^2+z^2$ term
 dominates, so $f\to+\infty$. Thus $f$ attains its minimum at a critical point,
and our analysis is complete.

**Step 7: Verification at the threshold.**

At $k=k_0$, $x=y=\frac{1+\sqrt{3}}{2}$, $z=4-2\sqrt{3}$:
- $xyz = \left(\frac{1+\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^2(4-2\sqrt{3}) = \frac{2+\sqrt{3}}{2}
\cdot(4-2\sqrt{3}) = \frac{(2+\sqrt{3})(4-2\sqrt{3})}{2} = \frac{8-6}{2}=1$. ✓
- $k = -\frac{1+\sqrt{3}}{2}-(4-2\sqrt{3}) = \frac{-9+3\sqrt{3}}{2}$. ✓
- $f=0$ at this point, confirming tightness.

$$\boxed{k = \dfrac{3(\sqrt{3}-3)}{2}}$$
