## Proof

Let $\sigma(n)$ denote the sum of all positive divisors of $n$. We are given $24
 \mid (n+1)$, i.e.

$$n \equiv -1 \pmod{24}.$$

In particular:
- $n \equiv 7 \pmod 8$ (since $-1 \equiv 7 \pmod 8$), so **$n$ is odd**, and
- $n \equiv 2 \pmod 3$ (since $-1 \equiv 2 \pmod 3$), so **$3 \nmid n$**.

We prove $\sigma(n) \equiv 0 \pmod 8$ and $\sigma(n) \equiv 0 \pmod 3$ separatel
y; since $\gcd(8,3)=1$, this yields $\sigma(n) \equiv 0 \pmod{24}$.

### Step 1: $n$ is not a perfect square

Squares modulo $8$ take only the values $\{0,1,4\}$ (checking $0^2,\dots,7^2 \bm
od 8$). Since $n \equiv 7 \pmod 8$, $n$ cannot be a perfect square. Hence the di
visors of $n$ pair up perfectly as $\bigl(d,\; n/d\bigr)$ with $d < n/d$, and **
every divisor belongs to exactly one such pair**.

### Step 2: $\sigma(n) \equiv 0 \pmod 8$

Since $n$ is odd, every divisor $d$ of $n$ is odd, so $d \in \{1,3,5,7\} \pmod 8
$. For each pair $(d, n/d)$,

$$d \cdot (n/d) = n \equiv 7 \pmod 8.$$

Among odd residues mod $8$, the solutions to $ab \equiv 7 \pmod 8$ are exactly $
(a,b) \in \{(1,7),(3,5),(5,3),(7,1)\}$, and in **every** case

$$a + b \equiv 0 \pmod 8.$$

Thus each pair $(d, n/d)$ contributes $d + n/d \equiv 0 \pmod 8$. Summing over a
ll pairs,

$$\sigma(n) = \sum_{\text{pairs}} \bigl(d + n/d\bigr) \equiv 0 \pmod 8.$$

### Step 3: $\sigma(n) \equiv 0 \pmod 3$

Since $3 \nmid n$, no divisor of $n$ is divisible by $3$; every divisor is $\equ
iv 1$ or $2 \pmod 3$. For each pair $(d, n/d)$,

$$d \cdot (n/d) = n \equiv 2 \pmod 3.$$

If $d \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ then $n/d \equiv 2 \pmod 3$, and if $d \equiv 2 \pmod 3$
 then $n/d \equiv 1 \pmod 3$. In either case,

$$d + n/d \equiv 1 + 2 \equiv 0 \pmod 3.$$

(There is no unpaired divisor, since $n$ is not a perfect square, as shown in St
ep 1 — equivalently, $n \equiv 2 \pmod 3$ already rules out $n$ being a square,
since squares mod $3$ are $\{0,1\}$.) Summing over all pairs,

$$\sigma(n) = \sum_{\text{pairs}} \bigl(d + n/d\bigr) \equiv 0 \pmod 3.$$

### Step 4: Conclusion

From Steps 2 and 3, $\sigma(n) \equiv 0 \pmod 8$ and $\sigma(n) \equiv 0 \pmod 3
$. Since $\gcd(8,3) = 1$,

$$\sigma(n) \equiv 0 \pmod{24}.$$

That is, the sum of all divisors of $n$ is divisible by $24$. $\blacksquare$
