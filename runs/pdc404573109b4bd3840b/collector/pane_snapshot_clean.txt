njugation: for $g \in G$ and $H \in \mathcal{S}$, the action is $g \cdot H = gHg
^{-1}$.

**Fixed points are normal subgroups.** A subgroup $H \in \mathcal{S}$ is a fixed
 point of this action if and only if $gHg^{-1} = H$ for all $g \in G$, which is
precisely the condition that $H$ is a **normal** subgroup of $G$ of order $p^k$.

**Orbit sizes are powers of $p$.** Since $G$ is a $p$-group, for any $H \in \mat
hcal{S}$, the orbit-stabilizer theorem gives:
$$|\operatorname{Orb}(H)| = [G : \operatorname{Stab}(H)]$$
where $\operatorname{Stab}(H) = \{g \in G : gHg^{-1} = H\} = N_G(H)$ is the norm
alizer of $H$. Since $|G|$ is a power of $p$, the index $[G : N_G(H)]$ is also a
 power of $p$. Thus every orbit has size $p^j$ for some $j \geq 0$.

**Orbits of size $> 1$ are divisible by $p$.** An orbit of size $p^j$ with $j \g
eq 1$ is divisible by $p$. Orbits of size $p^0 = 1$ are exactly the fixed points
 (the normal subgroups).

$$|\mathcal{S}| = \underbrace{(\text{number of normal subgroups of order } p^k)}
_{\text{fixed points}} + \sum_{\substack{\text{orbits} \\ \text{of size} > 1}} (
\text{orbit size})$$

$$|\mathcal{S}| \equiv \text{(number of normal subgroups of order } p^k) \pmod{p
}$$

$$\text{Number of normal subgroups of order } p^k \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$$
