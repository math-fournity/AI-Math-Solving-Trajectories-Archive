## Proof

Let $K$ be the Klein bottle, realized as the quotient of the square $[0,2\pi]\ti
mes[0,2\pi]$ by the identifications
$$(u,0)\sim(u,2\pi), \qquad (0,v)\sim(2\pi,\,2\pi-v).$$
The first identification glues the top and bottom edges preserving orientation;
the second glues the left and right edges **reversing** orientation (the twist).
 This is the standard presentation of the Klein bottle.

---

### 1. The explicit embedding $F:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb R^4$

Fix constants $R>r>0$. Define $F:[0,2\pi]^2\to\mathbb R^4$ by

$$
F(u,v)=\bigl((R+r\cos v)\cos u,\;(R+r\cos v)\sin u,\;r\sin v\cos\tfrac{u}{2},\;r
\sin v\sin\tfrac{u}{2}\bigr).
$$

**Well-definedness on the quotient.** We check $F$ respects both identifications
.

- $(u,0)\sim(u,2\pi)$: since $\sin 0=\sin 2\pi=0$ and $\cos 0=\cos 2\pi=1$,
$$F(u,0)=((R+r)\cos u,(R+r)\sin u,0,0)=F(u,2\pi). \checkmark$$

- $(0,v)\sim(2\pi,2\pi-v)$: using $\cos(2\pi-v)=\cos v$, $\sin(2\pi-v)=-\sin v$,
 $\cos\pi=-1$, $\sin\pi=0$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
F(2\pi,2\pi-v)&=\bigl((R+r\cos v)\cos 2\pi,\;0,\;-r\sin v\cos\pi,\;-r\sin v\sin\
pi\bigr)\\
&=\bigl(R+r\cos v,\;0,\;r\sin v,\;0\bigr)=F(0,v).\ \checkmark
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence $F$ descends to a smooth map $F:K\to\mathbb R^4$.

### 2. $F$ is an immersion

Compute the partial derivatives:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_u F&=\bigl(-(R+r\cos v)\sin u,\;(R+r\cos v)\cos u,\;-\tfrac12 r\sin v\s
in\tfrac u2,\;\tfrac12 r\sin v\cos\tfrac u2\bigr),\\
\partial_v F&=\bigl(-r\sin v\cos u,\;-r\sin v\sin u,\;r\cos v\cos\tfrac u2,\;r\c
os v\sin\tfrac u2\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
The first two components of $\partial_u F,\partial_v F$ span the $(x,y)$-plane d
irections $(R+r\cos v)(-\sin u,\cos u)$ and $r\sin v(-\cos u,-\sin u)$, which ar
e orthogonal with lengths $R+r\cos v>0$ and $r|\sin v|$. Whenever $\sin v\neq 0$
 these two vectors are already linearly independent, so $\operatorname{rank}=2$.
 When $\sin v=0$ (i.e. $v=0$ or $\pi$), the last two components of $\partial_v F
$ become $(\pm r\cos\frac u2,\pm r\sin\frac u2)$, which is nonzero, while $\part
ial_u F$ has nonzero $(x,y)$-part $(R\pm r)(-\sin u,\cos u)$; they are independe
nt. Thus $\operatorname{rank} dF=2$ everywhere: $F$ is an immersion.

### 3. $F$ is injective on $K$ (hence an embedding)

Suppose $F(u,v)=F(u',v')$.

- From $|(x,y)|=(R+r\cos v)=(R+r\cos v')$ (both positive since $R>r$), we get $\
cos v=\cos v'$, so $v'\equiv v$ or $v'\equiv 2\pi-v\pmod{2\pi}$.
- From the direction of $(x,y)$: $(\cos u,\sin u)=(\cos u',\sin u')$, so $u'\equ
iv u\pmod{2\pi}$.
- From $(z,w)=r\sin v\,(\cos\frac u2,\sin\frac u2)$ and $(z',w')=r\sin v'\,(\cos
\frac{u'}2,\sin\frac{u'}2)$:

  **Case $v'=v$.** Then $\sin v'=\sin v$. If $\sin v\neq 0$, $(\cos\frac u2,\sin
\frac u2)=(\cos\frac{u'}2,\sin\frac{u'}2)$, giving $u'\equiv u\pmod{4\pi}$, whic
h together with $u'\equiv u\pmod{2\pi}$ yields $u'=u$ in $[0,2\pi]$. If $\sin v=
0$, the $(z,w)$-equation is $0=0$ and $u'\equiv u\pmod{2\pi}$ suffices. Either w
ay $(u,v)=(u',v')$ in the quotient.

  **Case $v'=2\pi-v$.** Then $\sin v'=-\sin v$. If $\sin v\neq 0$,
  $$(\cos\tfrac u2,\sin\tfrac u2)=-(\cos\tfrac{u'}2,\sin\tfrac{u'}2)=(\cos(\tfra
c{u'}2+\pi),\sin(\tfrac{u'}2+\pi)),$$
  so $u\equiv u'+2\pi\pmod{4\pi}$. Combined with $u'\equiv u\pmod{2\pi}$, this f
orces $u'=u-2\pi$ or $u'=u+2\pi$, i.e. in $[0,2\pi]$ either $(u,v)=(0,v),(u',v')
=(2\pi,2\pi-v)$ or $(u,v)=(2\pi,v),(u',v')=(0,2\pi-v)$ — precisely the identific
ation $(0,v)\sim(2\pi,2\pi-v)$. If $\sin v=0$, then $v'=2\pi-v\equiv v$, reducin
g to the previous case.

In every case $(u,v)\sim(u',v')$ in $K$. So $F$ is injective on the quotient.

Since $K$ is compact (quotient of a compact square) and $\mathbb R^4$ is Hausdor
ff, a continuous injective immersion $F:K\to\mathbb R^4$ is a smooth **embedding
**. Its image is a smoothly embedded Klein bottle in $\mathbb R^4$.

### 4. Orthogonal projection into $\mathbb R^3$

Let $\pi:\mathbb R^4\to\mathbb R^3$ be the orthogonal projection dropping the fo
urth coordinate $w$. The projected map is
$$
\pi\circ F(u,v)=\bigl((R+r\cos v)\cos u,\;(R+r\cos v)\sin u,\;r\sin v\cos\tfrac
u2\bigr).
$$
Geometrically, this is the **"figure-8" Klein bottle**: a tube of radius $r$ who
se axis is the circle of radius $R$ in the $(x,y)$-plane, and whose circular cro
ss-section at parameter $u$ is squeezed in the $z$-direction by the factor $\cos
(u/2)$. As $u$ runs from $0$ to $2\pi$, the factor $\cos(u/2)$ runs $1\to 0\to -
1$, so the cross-section rotates through a half-turn — exactly implementing the
twist of the Klein bottle.

#### Properties of the projection

**(a) Smooth, but not an immersion everywhere.** The Jacobian of $\pi\circ F$ ha
s rank $2$ except where $\partial_v(\pi\circ F)=0$, which (solving $\sin v=0$ an
d $\cos v\cos(u/2)=0$) occurs exactly at the two points
$$P_1=(\pi,0)\sim(\pi,2\pi),\qquad P_2=(\pi,\pi).$$
At these two points the rank drops to $1$: they are **pinch-point (Whitney-umbre
lla) singularities** of the projected surface. Everywhere else $\pi\circ F$ is a
n immersion.

**(b) Not injective — self-intersection along a segment.** Solving $\pi F(u,v)=\
pi F(u',v')$ with $(u,v)\not\sim(u',v')$ (the same analysis as in §3 but using o
nly the first three coordinates, so the fourth coordinate $w$ no longer separate
s the sheets) yields the unique nontrivial family
$$
(\pi,v)\ \longleftrightarrow\ (\pi,\,2\pi-v),\qquad v\in(0,\pi).
$$
Indeed at $u=\pi$: $z=r\sin v\cos(\pi/2)=0$ for both sheets, $x=-(R+r\cos v)$, $
y=0$, so both map to the same point $(-(R+r\cos v),0,0)\in\mathbb R^3$, while th
e suppressed fourth coordinates are $w=r\sin v$ and $w'=-r\sin v\neq w$. The sel
f-intersection set in $\mathbb R^3$ is therefore the **closed line segment**
$$
\Sigma=\bigl\{\,(-(R+r\cos v),\,0,\,0):v\in[0,\pi]\,\bigr\}=\bigl[-(R+r),\,-(R-r
)\bigr]\times\{0\}\times\{0\}
$$
on the $x$-axis. Every interior point of $\Sigma$ has exactly **two transverse p
reimages** (one on each sheet); the two endpoints $P_1\mapsto (-(R+r),0,0)$ and
$P_2\mapsto (-(R-r),0,0)$ are the pinch points, where the two sheets coalesce in
to one and transversality fails.

**(c) The fourth coordinate resolves the self-intersection.** The embedding coor
dinate $w=r\sin v\sin(u/2)$ is precisely what separates the two sheets that cros
s in $\mathbb R^3$: along the self-intersection segment ($u=\pi$, so $\sin(u/2)=
1$) the two sheets sit at $w=+r\sin v$ and $w=-r\sin v$, so they are disjoint in
 $\mathbb R^4$ even though their projections coincide. This is the mechanism by
which the embedding in $\mathbb R^4$ is injective while its orthogonal projectio
n into $\mathbb R^3$ is not.

**(d) Summary.** The orthogonal projection of the embedded Klein bottle is the c
lassical figure-8 immersion of the Klein bottle in $\mathbb R^3$: a smooth map t
hat is an immersion off two pinch points, with a single self-intersection segmen
t (a closed interval on the symmetry axis) along which the surface meets itself
transversely in the interior, terminating at the two Whitney-umbrella pinch poin
ts at its ends.
