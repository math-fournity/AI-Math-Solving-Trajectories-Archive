# Solution

**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

We construct an explicit example on the Hilbert space $H = \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$ w
ith standard orthonormal basis $\{e_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$.

**The operator.** Define $T: H \to H$ by $T e_n = \frac{1}{n} e_n$ for each $n$,
 extended linearly and continuously. Explicitly, for $x = (x_1, x_2, \ldots) \in
 \ell^2$,
$$Tx = \left(\frac{x_1}{1}, \frac{x_2}{2}, \frac{x_3}{3}, \ldots\right).$$

**$T$ is self-adjoint and compact.** Self-adjointness is immediate since $T$ is
diagonal with real entries. For compactness: $T$ is the norm-limit of the finite
-rank operators $T_N$ defined by $T_N e_n = \frac{1}{n}e_n$ for $n \le N$ and $T
_N e_n = 0$ for $n > N$. Indeed,
$$\|T - T_N\| = \sup_{n > N} \frac{1}{n} = \frac{1}{N+1} \to 0,$$
so $T$ is a norm-limit of finite-rank operators, hence compact.

**The vector.** Set $v = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n} e_n = (1, \tfrac{1}{2}, \
tfrac{1}{3}, \ldots)$. Since $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}
< \infty$, we have $v \in \ell^2$.

**Key lemma.** *Every finite-dimensional $T$-invariant subspace $M$ is spanned b
y eigenvectors of $T$.*

*Proof of lemma.* Let $M$ be finite-dimensional with $T(M) \subseteq M$. Since $
T$ is self-adjoint, for any $w \in M^\perp$ and $m \in M$:
$$\langle Tw, m\rangle = \langle w, Tm\rangle = 0,$$
because $Tm \in M$. So $T(M^\perp) \subseteq M^\perp$, meaning $M$ is a **reduci
ng** subspace. The restriction $T|_M : M \to M$ is then a self-adjoint operator
on a finite-dimensional inner product space, so by the finite-dimensional spectr
al theorem, $M$ admits an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors of $T|_M$. Since $M$
 is reducing, these are eigenvectors of $T$ on $H$. $\square$

**Eigenspace structure of $T$.** The eigenvalues of $T$ are $\{1/n : n \ge 1\}$,
 each with one-dimensional eigenspace $E_{1/n} = \operatorname{span}(e_n)$. The
point $0$ is in the spectrum but is not an eigenvalue (since $T$ is injective: $
Tx = 0$ implies $x_n/n = 0$ for all $n$, so $x = 0$). Thus every eigenspace is $
\operatorname{span}(e_n)$ for some $n$.

**$v$ lies in no finite-dimensional invariant subspace.** Suppose for contradict
ion that $v \in M$ for some finite-dimensional $T$-invariant subspace $M$. By th
e lemma, $M$ is spanned by finitely many eigenvectors of $T$. Since each eigensp
ace is $\operatorname{span}(e_n)$, we have $M \subseteq \operatorname{span}(e_1,
 \ldots, e_N)$ for some $N$. But
$$v = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n} e_n$$
has infinitely many nonzero coordinates $1/n \neq 0$, so $v \notin \operatorname
{span}(e_1, \ldots, e_N)$ for any finite $N$. This is a contradiction.

Therefore, $v$ does not belong to any finite-dimensional invariant subspace of $
T$. $\blacksquare$
