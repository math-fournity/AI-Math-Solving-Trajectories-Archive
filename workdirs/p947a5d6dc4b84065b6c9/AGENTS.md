# Problem

In a futuristic city, an urban planner is designing a high-speed transit network based on a hexagonal infrastructure. The city consists of 19 stations arranged in a perfectly regular hexagonal grid: there is one central station, a first ring of 6 stations forming a regular hexagon around the center, and a second outer ring of 12 stations forming a larger regular hexagon.

To minimize construction costs, the planner will only build straight-line magnetic rails that pass through exactly two stations. If a straight line would pass through three or more stations, it is considered a "high-capacity corridor" and is excluded from this specific count of "dual-link" lines.

How many unique straight-line paths can be drawn that pass through exactly two of these 19 stations?
