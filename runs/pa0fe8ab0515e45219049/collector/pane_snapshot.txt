# Solution

**Problem:** Find the maximum number of zeros of the Laplace transform of a func
tion that is the difference of two sums, each containing $k$ exponential terms w
ith positive exponents.

## Setup

The function has the form

$$f(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{k} a_i\, e^{\alpha_i t} \;-\; \sum_{j=1}^{k} b_j\, e^{\beta
_j t}, \qquad \alpha_i, \beta_j > 0,$$

where $a_i, b_j$ are real coefficients (not all zero in each sum). Its Laplace t
ransform (for $\operatorname{Re}(s) > \max(\alpha_i,\beta_j)$) is

$$F(s) = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \frac{a_i}{s - \alpha_i} \;-\; \sum_{j=1}^{k} \frac{b_j}
{s - \beta_j}.$$

## Upper bound: at most $2k-1$ zeros

Assume all $2k$ exponents $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k,\beta_1,\dots,\beta_k$ are di
stinct (this only increases the number of poles, hence the potential number of z
eros). Put $F(s)$ over the common denominator

$$D(s) = \prod_{i=1}^{k}(s-\alpha_i)\prod_{j=1}^{k}(s-\beta_j), \qquad \deg D =
2k.$$

Then $F(s) = N(s)/D(s)$ where

$$N(s) = \sum_{i=1}^{k} a_i\!\prod_{i'\neq i}(s-\alpha_{i'})\prod_{j}(s-\beta_j)
 \;-\; \sum_{j=1}^{k} b_j\!\prod_{i}(s-\alpha_i)\prod_{j'\neq j}(s-\beta_{j'}).$
$

Each summand is a polynomial of degree $2k-1$, so $\deg N \le 2k-1$. The coeffic
ient of $s^{2k-1}$ in $N$ is $\sum_i a_i - \sum_j b_j$; when this is nonzero, $\
deg N = 2k-1$, and otherwise $\deg N \le 2k-2$.

Since $N$ is a polynomial of degree at most $2k-1$, it has **at most $2k-1$ zero
s** (counting multiplicity, hence at most $2k-1$ distinct zeros). The zeros of $
F$ are exactly the zeros of $N$ that are not poles, so $F$ has at most $2k-1$ ze
ros.

## Lower bound: $2k-1$ zeros are achievable

Consider the linear map

$$\Phi : \mathbb{R}^{2k} \longrightarrow \{\text{polynomials of degree} \le 2k-1
\}, \qquad (a_1,\dots,a_k,b_1,\dots,b_k) \;\mapsto\; N(s).$$

Both the domain and codomain have dimension $2k$, and $\Phi$ is linear. We show
$\Phi$ is **injective**: if $N \equiv 0$, then $F \equiv 0$, i.e.,

$$\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{a_i}{s-\alpha_i} = \sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{b_j}{s-\beta_j}.$$

Since all $2k$ exponents are distinct, the partial-fraction decomposition of a r
ational function with these poles is **unique**. Comparing residues at each pole
 gives $a_i = 0$ for every $i$ and $b_j = 0$ for every $j$. Hence $\ker\Phi = \{
0\}$, so $\Phi$ is an isomorphism.

Therefore $N(s)$ can be **any** polynomial of degree $\le 2k-1$. In particular,
choose

$$N(s) = \prod_{m=1}^{2k-1}(s - r_m)$$

for $2k-1$ distinct real numbers $r_1,\dots,r_{2k-1}$, all different from every
$\alpha_i$ and $\beta_j$. By the isomorphism, there exist coefficients $(a_i, b_
j)$ producing this $N$, and since the leading coefficient of $N$ is $1 \neq 0$,
we have $\sum a_i - \sum b_j = 1 \neq 0$, confirming $\deg N = 2k-1$.

All $2k-1$ roots $r_m$ are distinct from the poles, so $F(s) = N(s)/D(s)$ has ex
actly $2k-1$ distinct zeros.

## Verification with small cases

- **$k=1$:** $F(s) = \frac{a}{s-\alpha} - \frac{b}{s-\beta} = \frac{(a-b)s - (a\
beta - b\alpha)}{(s-\alpha)(s-\beta)}$. Numerator has degree $1$ (when $a \neq b
$), giving $1 = 2(1)-1$ zero. ✓
- **$k=2$:** Numerator has degree $3$, giving up to $3 = 2(2)-1$ zeros. ✓

## Conclusion

The maximum number of zeros of the Laplace transform is

$$\boxed{2k - 1}.$$
