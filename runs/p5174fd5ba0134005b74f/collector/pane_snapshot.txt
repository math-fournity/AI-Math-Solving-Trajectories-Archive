## Proof

The problem refers to the standard contour for this classical exercise: $\gamma_
r$ is the upper semicircle $z = re^{i\theta}$, $0 \le \theta \le \pi$ (the canon
ical setting for Jordan-type estimates of $e^{iz}/z^n$ as $r\to\infty$). I evalu
ate the limit under this (the only standard) interpretation.

**Parametrization.** With $z = re^{i\theta}$, $dz = ire^{i\theta}\,d\theta$,

$$I(r) = \int_0^\pi \frac{e^{i\,re^{i\theta}}}{r^2 e^{2i\theta}}\,ire^{i\theta}\
,d\theta = \frac{i}{r}\int_0^\pi e^{ir\cos\theta - r\sin\theta}\,e^{-i\theta}\,d
\theta.$$

**Estimate.** Since $|e^{-i\theta}|=1$ and $|e^{ir\cos\theta}|=1$,

$$|I(r)| \le \frac{1}{r}\int_0^\pi e^{-r\sin\theta}\,d\theta = \frac{2}{r}\int_0
^{\pi/2} e^{-r\sin\theta}\,d\theta,$$

using the symmetry $\sin(\pi-\theta)=\sin\theta$.

**Jordan's inequality.** For $0 \le \theta \le \pi/2$, the concavity of $\sin$ o
n $[0,\pi/2]$ gives $\sin\theta \ge \tfrac{2\theta}{\pi}$. Hence

$$\int_0^{\pi/2} e^{-r\sin\theta}\,d\theta \le \int_0^{\pi/2} e^{-2r\theta/\pi}\
,d\theta = \frac{\pi}{2r}\bigl(1 - e^{-r}\bigr).$$

**Combining,**

$$|I(r)| \le \frac{2}{r}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2r}\bigl(1-e^{-r}\bigr) = \frac{\pi}{r^2
}\bigl(1-e^{-r}\bigr) \xrightarrow{r\to\infty} 0.$$

Therefore, by the squeeze theorem,

$$\boxed{\lim_{r\to\infty} I(r) = 0.}$$

**Remark.** The decay is actually $O(1/r^2)$ here (faster than the $O(1/r)$ give
n by Jordan's lemma for $e^{iz}/z$), precisely because the denominator is $z^2$:
 the extra factor $1/z$ contributes an additional $1/r$, so even the crude bound
 $|e^{iz}|\le 1$ on the semicircle would give $|I(r)|\le \pi/r \to 0$; the Jorda
n refinement above yields the sharper $O(1/r^2)$ rate. Either way the limit is $
0$.
