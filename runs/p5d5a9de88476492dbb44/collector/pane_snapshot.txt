## Solution

We work on the Riemann sphere $\hat{\mathbb{C}} = \mathbb{C} \cup \{\infty\}$. T
he vector space $V$ consists of functions holomorphic on $\hat{\mathbb{C}} \setm
inus \{0, i\}$, with poles of order at most $2$ at each of $0$ and $i$.

**Key fact.** A function meromorphic on the whole Riemann sphere is a rational f
unction. Since our function is holomorphic everywhere on $\hat{\mathbb{C}}$ exce
pt possibly at $0$ and $i$, its only possible poles are at $0$ and $i$ (each of
order $\le 2$). In particular it must be **holomorphic at $\infty$**.

**Partial fraction decomposition.** Any rational function whose only finite pole
s are at $0$ (order $\le 2$) and $i$ (order $\le 2$) can be written as

$$f(z) = c + \frac{a_1}{z} + \frac{a_2}{z^2} + \frac{b_1}{z-i} + \frac{b_2}{(z-i
)^2}, \qquad c, a_1, a_2, b_1, b_2 \in \mathbb{C}.$$

**Checking holomorphicity at $\infty$.** Set $w = 1/z$ and examine $f(1/w)$ near
 $w = 0$:

$$f(1/w) = c + a_1 w + a_2 w^2 + \frac{b_1\, w}{1 - iw} + \frac{b_2\, w^2}{(1-iw
)^2}.$$

Each term $\frac{b_1 w}{1-iw}$ and $\frac{b_2 w^2}{(1-iw)^2}$ is holomorphic at
$w=0$ (the denominators equal $1$ at $w=0$). Hence $f(1/w)$ is holomorphic at $w
=0$ for **every** choice of the five constants. So the holomorphicity-at-$\infty
$ condition imposes no constraint, and every such partial-fraction expression be
longs to $V$.

Conversely, every element of $V$ has such a decomposition (subtract the principa
l parts at $0$ and $i$; the remainder is holomorphic on all of $\hat{\mathbb{C}}
$, hence constant by Liouville's theorem applied on the sphere).

**Linear independence.** Suppose

$$c + \frac{a_1}{z} + \frac{a_2}{z^2} + \frac{b_1}{z-i} + \frac{b_2}{(z-i)^2} =
0 \quad \text{identically.}$$

Multiplying by $z^2$ and letting $z \to 0$ isolates the principal part at $0$: t
he terms $\frac{a_1}{z}\cdot z^2 = a_1 z \to 0$, $\frac{a_2}{z^2}\cdot z^2 = a_2
$, while the $b$-terms and $c$-term vanish (their factors $z^2/(z-i)^k \to 0$).
Hence $a_2 = 0$; similarly $a_1 = 0$. Multiplying by $(z-i)^2$ and letting $z \t
o i$ gives $b_2 = 0$, then $b_1 = 0$. Finally $c = 0$. So the five functions are
 linearly independent.

**Conclusion.** The space $V$ is spanned by

$$\left\{\, 1,\ \frac{1}{z},\ \frac{1}{z^2},\ \frac{1}{z-i},\ \frac{1}{(z-i)^2}\
,\right\},$$

and these are linearly independent. Therefore

$$\dim V = 5,$$

with the above set as an explicit basis.
