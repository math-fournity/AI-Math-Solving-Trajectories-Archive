Yes, such an automorphism exists. Here is the proof.

**Proof.** Regard $\mathbb{R}$ as a vector space over $\mathbb{Q}$. By the Axiom
 of Choice (Zorn's lemma), $\mathbb{R}$ admits a Hamel basis $B$ over $\mathbb{Q
}$, i.e. a set $B\subseteq\mathbb{R}$ such that every real number is a *unique*
finite $\mathbb{Q}$-linear combination of elements of $B$.

The group automorphisms of $(\mathbb{R},+)$ are exactly the $\mathbb{Q}$-linear
bijections $\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$: indeed an additive homomorphism automatica
lly satisfies $\varphi(qx)=q\varphi(x)$ for every $q\in\mathbb{Q}$, so additivit
y = $\mathbb{Q}$-linearity; and a $\mathbb{Q}$-linear bijection is in particular
 an additive bijection. Such a map is completely determined by its values on $B$
, and any bijection of $B$ onto itself extends uniquely to a $\mathbb{Q}$-linear
 automorphism of $\mathbb{R}$.

Since $B$ is uncountable (because $\mathbb{R}$ is uncountable while every finite
-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}$-space is countable), in particular $|B|\geqslant 2$. P
ick two distinct elements $b_1,b_2\in B$ and define a permutation $\sigma$ of $B
$ by
$$
\sigma(b_1)=b_2,\qquad \sigma(b_2)=b_1,\qquad \sigma(b)=b\ \text{for all }b\in B
\setminus\{b_1,b_2\}.
$$
Let $\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ be the unique $\mathbb{Q}$-linear extensio
n of $\sigma$. Then:

- $\varphi$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-linear bijection, hence a group automorphism of $(
\mathbb{R},+)$.
- $\varphi$ is **not** the identity, since $\varphi(b_1)=b_2\neq b_1$.
- $\varphi$ fixes pointwise the subset
$$
S\;=\;\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\!\bigl(B\setminus\{b_1,b_2\}\bigr)\;\subs
eteq\;\mathbb{R}.
$$
Indeed every element of $S$ is a finite $\mathbb{Q}$-linear combination of basis
 elements *other than* $b_1,b_2$, each of which is fixed by $\sigma$, so $\varph
i$ fixes it. The set $S$ is infinite — in fact uncountable, since it contains th
e uncountable set $B\setminus\{b_1,b_2\}$.

Thus $\varphi$ is a non-identity group automorphism of $(\mathbb{R},+)$ that fix
es the (infinite) subset $S$ pointwise. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The obstruction to fixing "too much" is the only constraint: if a su
bset $T\subseteq\mathbb{R}$ has $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}(T)=\mathbb{R}$
, then any automorphism fixing $T$ pointwise is forced to be the identity. The c
onstruction above works precisely because $B\setminus\{b_1,b_2\}$ does not span
all of $\mathbb{R}$, leaving two basis directions free to be permuted.
