**Theorem.** For any $n \times n$ matrix $A$ over $\mathbb{R}$ (or $\mathbb{C}$)
, there exists a diagonal matrix $D$ such that $A + D$ has $n$ distinct eigenval
ues.

**Proof.**

Let $D = \operatorname{diag}(d_1, \dots, d_n)$ and consider the characteristic p
olynomial
$$p_D(\lambda) = \det(\lambda I - A - D),$$
which is monic of degree $n$ in $\lambda$, with coefficients that are polynomial
s in $d_1, \dots, d_n$.

The **discriminant** $\Delta(d_1, \dots, d_n)$ of $p_D$ (as a polynomial in $\la
mbda$) is itself a polynomial in $d_1, \dots, d_n$. The matrix $A + D$ has $n$ d
istinct eigenvalues (over the algebraic closure) if and only if $\Delta \neq 0$.
 Thus it suffices to show that $\Delta$ is **not the zero polynomial**.

To do this, we exhibit a single choice of $D$ for which $A + D$ has $n$ distinct
 eigenvalues.

**Specialization.** Set $d_i = i \cdot t$, i.e., $D = t \cdot B$ where $B = \ope
ratorname{diag}(1, 2, \dots, n)$, and let
$$M(t) = A + tB.$$
We show that for sufficiently large $t > 0$, $M(t)$ has $n$ distinct eigenvalues
.

Substitute $\lambda = t\mu$ into the characteristic polynomial:
$$p_t(t\mu) = \det(t\mu\, I - A - tB) = t^n \det\!\left(\mu\, I - B - \tfrac{1}{
t}A\right).$$

Define $q_t(\mu) = \det(\mu\, I - B - \tfrac{1}{t}A)$, a monic polynomial of deg
ree $n$ in $\mu$. As $t \to \infty$:
$$q_t(\mu) \;\longrightarrow\; q_\infty(\mu) = \det(\mu\, I - B) = \prod_{i=1}^{
n}(\mu - i).$$

The coefficients of $q_t$ are continuous functions of $1/t$ (hence of $t$ for $t
 \neq 0$), since they are polynomials in the entries of $B + A/t$.

**Key fact (continuity of roots):** The roots of a monic polynomial vary continu
ously with its coefficients. Since $q_\infty(\mu) = \prod_{i=1}^n(\mu - i)$ has
$n$ **distinct** simple roots $1, 2, \dots, n$, there exists $T > 0$ such that f
or all $t > T$, the polynomial $q_t$ also has $n$ distinct roots $\mu_1(t), \dot
s, \mu_n(t)$, with $\mu_i(t) \to i$ as $t \to \infty$.

The eigenvalues of $M(t)$ are $\lambda_i(t) = t \cdot \mu_i(t)$ for $i = 1, \dot
s, n$. Since the $\mu_i(t)$ are distinct for $t > T$, the $\lambda_i(t)$ are dis
tinct as well. Therefore $M(t) = A + tB$ has $n$ distinct eigenvalues for all su
fficiently large $t$.

**Conclusion.** We have shown that $\Delta(d_1, \dots, d_n)$ is nonzero at the p
oint $(t, 2t, \dots, nt)$ for large $t$, so $\Delta$ is not the zero polynomial.
 Since $\Delta$ is a nonzero polynomial and $\mathbb{R}$ (resp. $\mathbb{C}$) is
 an infinite field, the set
$$\{(d_1, \dots, d_n) \in \mathbb{R}^n : \Delta(d_1, \dots, d_n) \neq 0\}$$
is a nonempty Zariski-open (hence Euclidean-dense) set. In particular, there exi
st $d_1, \dots, d_n$ such that $A + \operatorname{diag}(d_1, \dots, d_n)$ has $n
$ distinct eigenvalues. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** In fact, "most" choices of $D$ work: the bad set (where $A+D$ has a
repeated eigenvalue) is contained in the zero set of the nonzero polynomial $\De
lta$, which has measure zero and is nowhere dense.
