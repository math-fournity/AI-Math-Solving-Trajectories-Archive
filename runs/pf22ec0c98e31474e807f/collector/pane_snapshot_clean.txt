## Proof

In the universal enveloping algebra $U(\mathfrak{g})$, the Hopf algebra structur
e is defined so that the coproduct $\Delta: U(\mathfrak{g}) \to U(\mathfrak{g})
\otimes U(\mathfrak{g})$ is a **unital algebra homomorphism**.

### Step 1: $\Delta$ is a unital algebra homomorphism

By the definition of a bialgebra (and hence a Hopf algebra), the coproduct $\Del
ta$ must be a homomorphism of unital algebras. This means it must preserve the u
nit:

$$\Delta(1) = 1 \otimes 1,$$

where $1 \otimes 1$ is the unit element of the tensor product algebra $U(\mathfr
ak{g}) \otimes U(\mathfrak{g})$.

### Step 2: The counit axiom rules out $1 \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes 1$

Suppose, for contradiction, that $\Delta(1) = 1 \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes 1 = 2(1 \o
times 1)$.

The counit axiom of a Hopf algebra requires:

$$(\varepsilon \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta = \mathrm{id} \quad \text{and}
\quad (\mathrm{id} \otimes \varepsilon) \circ \Delta = \mathrm{id}.$$

Applying the left-hand side to $1$:

$$(\varepsilon \otimes \mathrm{id})(\Delta(1)) = (\varepsilon \otimes \mathrm{id
})(2 \cdot 1 \otimes 1) = 2\,\varepsilon(1) \otimes 1 = 2 \cdot 1 \otimes 1 \;\m
apsto\; 2 \cdot 1 = 2 \neq 1.$$

Here we used $\varepsilon(1) = 1$ (the counit is also a unital algebra homomorph
ism, so it sends the unit to the unit of the base field). This yields $2 \neq 1$
, **violating the counit axiom**. Contradiction.

### Step 3: The explicit Hopf structure on $U(\mathfrak{g})$

For completeness, the standard Hopf algebra structure on $U(\mathfrak{g})$ is:

- **Coproduct**: $\Delta(x) = x \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes x$ for all $x \in \mathfra
k{g}$, extended as an algebra homomorphism.
- **Counit**: $\varepsilon(x) = 0$ for all $x \in \mathfrak{g}$, $\varepsilon(1)
 = 1$.
- **Antipode**: $S(x) = -x$ for all $x \in \mathfrak{g}$.

The formula $\Delta(x) = x \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes x$ applies to elements $x \in \
mathfrak{g}$ (i.e., the generators), **not** to the unit $1 \in U(\mathfrak{g})$
. The unit $1$ is the empty product, not a generator in $\mathfrak{g}$, so this
formula does not apply to it. Applying the algebra-homomorphism property to $1$
gives $\Delta(1) = 1 \otimes 1$ directly.

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{\Delta(1) = 1 \otimes 1}$$

The alternative $1 \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes 1$ is incorrect: it would equal $2(1 \o
times 1)$, which violates both the unital-homomorphism property of $\Delta$ and
the counit axiom of the Hopf algebra. The formula $\Delta(x) = x \otimes 1 + 1 \
otimes x$ is reserved for $x \in \mathfrak{g}$ (the Lie algebra generators), and
 must not be applied to the unit $1$.
