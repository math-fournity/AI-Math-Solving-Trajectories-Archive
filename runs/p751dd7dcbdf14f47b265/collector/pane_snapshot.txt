**Answer: Yes, such a sequence exists.**

## Proof

Let $E = [0,1]$, which is compact. We construct a sequence of strictly positive
continuous functions $f_n: [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}$ exhibiting the "escape of mass"
 phenomenon.

**Construction.** For each $n \geq 1$, define the tent function
$$
g_n(x) = \begin{cases}
2n^2 x, & 0 \le x \le \tfrac{1}{2n},\\[4pt]
2n - 2n^2 x, & \tfrac{1}{2n} \le x \le \tfrac{1}{n},\\[4pt]
0, & \tfrac{1}{n} \le x \le 1.
\end{cases}
$$
This is a continuous, non-negative triangular bump of height $n$ supported on $[
0, 1/n]$, with
$$
\int_0^1 g_n(x)\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\tfrac{1}{n}\cdot n = \tfrac{1}{2}.
$$

Now set
$$
f_n(x) = g_n(x) + \tfrac{1}{n}.
$$

**Properties of $f_n$.**

1. **Continuous:** $g_n$ is continuous and $1/n$ is constant, so $f_n$ is contin
uous.
2. **Strictly positive:** $g_n(x) \ge 0$ for all $x$, and $1/n > 0$, so $f_n(x)
\ge 1/n > 0$ everywhere on $[0,1]$.
3. **Integral:**
$$
\int_0^1 f_n(x)\,dx = \int_0^1 g_n(x)\,dx + \int_0^1 \tfrac{1}{n}\,dx = \tfrac{1
}{2} + \tfrac{1}{n} \;\longrightarrow\; \tfrac{1}{2} =: C < \infty.
$$

**Pointwise limit.** We claim $f_n(x) \to 0$ for every $x \in [0,1]$.

- **At $x = 0$:** $g_n(0) = 0$ for all $n$, so $f_n(0) = 1/n \to 0$.
- **At $x > 0$:** Choose $N > 1/x$. For all $n \ge N$, we have $1/n \le 1/N < x$
, so $x \in [1/n, 1]$ and thus $g_n(x) = 0$. Hence $f_n(x) = 1/n \to 0$.

Therefore $f_n \to f$ pointwise with $f \equiv 0$, and $f \in L^\infty([0,1])$ (
indeed $\|f\|_\infty = 0$).

**The discrepancy.** We have
$$
C = \lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1 f_n(x)\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2}, \qquad \text{but} \qqua
d \int_0^1 f(x)\,dx = 0.
$$
Thus $C = \tfrac{1}{2} \ne 0 = \int_E f(x)\,dx$.

**Consistency with Fatou's lemma.** Since $f_n \ge 0$, Fatou's lemma gives
$$
\int_0^1 f \le \liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1 f_n = \tfrac{1}{2},
$$
i.e., $0 \le 1/2$, which is satisfied — with strict inequality, reflecting the m
ass $\frac{1}{2}$ that "escapes" by concentrating into the shrinking interval $[
0, 1/n]$ and vanishing pointwise. $\blacksquare$
