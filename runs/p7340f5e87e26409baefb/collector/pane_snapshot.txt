### Setup and notation

- Two classes: $\omega_1: x \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, I)$ and $\omega_2: x \sim \mat
hcal{N}(-\mu, I)$, with $\mu = [1,\, 1/\sqrt{2},\, 1/\sqrt{3},\, \ldots,\, 1/\sq
rt{l}]^T \in \mathbb{R}^l$.
- Training data $\{x_k\}_{k=1}^N$ with labels $s_k = +1$ for $\omega_1$, $s_k =
-1$ for $\omega_2$.
- MLE estimator: $\hat{\mu} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^N s_k x_k$.
- Test sample $x$ is drawn from $\omega_1$ (i.e. $x \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, I)$),
independent of the training data.
- We seek the mean and variance of $z = x^T \hat{\mu}$.

---

### Step 1: Distribution of $\hat{\mu}$

For each training sample $x_k$:

- If $x_k \in \omega_1$: $s_k = 1$, so $s_k x_k = x_k \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, I)$.
- If $x_k \in \omega_2$: $s_k = -1$, so $s_k x_k = -x_k$ where $x_k \sim \mathca
l{N}(-\mu, I)$, hence $-x_k \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, I)$.

In both cases, $s_k x_k \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, I)$, independently. Therefore:

$$\hat{\mu} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^N s_k x_k \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(\mu,\; \fr
ac{I}{N}\right).$$

So $\mathbb{E}[\hat{\mu}] = \mu$ and $\operatorname{Cov}(\hat{\mu}) = \frac{I}{N
}$.

---

### Step 2: Mean of $z = x^T \hat{\mu}$

Since $x$ (test) and $\hat{\mu}$ (training) are independent:

$$\mathbb{E}[z] = \mathbb{E}[x]^T \,\mathbb{E}[\hat{\mu}] = \mu^T \mu = \|\mu\|^
2.$$

Computing the squared norm:

$$\|\mu\|^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{l} \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}\right)^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{l}
\frac{1}{i} = H_l,$$

where $H_l$ is the $l$-th harmonic number.

$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}[z] = H_l = \sum_{i=1}^{l} \frac{1}{i}.}$$

---

### Step 3: Variance of $z = x^T \hat{\mu}$

We use the law of total variance, conditioning on $\hat{\mu}$:

$$\operatorname{Var}(z) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\operatorname{Var}(z \mid \hat{\mu})
\right] + \operatorname{Var}\!\left(\mathbb{E}[z \mid \hat{\mu}]\right).$$

**Conditional moments.** Given $\hat{\mu}$, $z = x^T \hat{\mu}$ where $x \sim \m
athcal{N}(\mu, I)$, so:

$$\mathbb{E}[z \mid \hat{\mu}] = \mu^T \hat{\mu}, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(z \m
id \hat{\mu}) = \hat{\mu}^T I\, \hat{\mu} = \|\hat{\mu}\|^2.$$

**Second term:** $\operatorname{Var}(\mu^T \hat{\mu}) = \mu^T \operatorname{Cov}
(\hat{\mu})\, \mu = \mu^T \frac{I}{N} \mu = \frac{\|\mu\|^2}{N} = \frac{H_l}{N}.
$

**First term:** $\mathbb{E}[\|\hat{\mu}\|^2] = \|\mathbb{E}[\hat{\mu}]\|^2 + \op
eratorname{tr}(\operatorname{Cov}(\hat{\mu})) = \|\mu\|^2 + \frac{l}{N} = H_l +
\frac{l}{N}.$

Combining:

$$\operatorname{Var}(z) = \left(H_l + \frac{l}{N}\right) + \frac{H_l}{N} = H_l +
 \frac{H_l + l}{N} = H_l\!\left(1 + \frac{1}{N}\right) + \frac{l}{N}.$$

---

### Verification (direct expansion)

Write $z = \sum_{i=1}^l x_i \hat{\mu}_i$. By independence of $x$ and $\hat{\mu}$
:

$$\mathbb{E}[z^2] = \sum_{i,j} \mathbb{E}[x_i x_j]\,\mathbb{E}[\hat{\mu}_i \hat{
\mu}_j],$$

with $\mathbb{E}[x_i x_j] = \mu_i\mu_j + \delta_{ij}$ and $\mathbb{E}[\hat{\mu}_
i \hat{\mu}_j] = \mu_i\mu_j + \delta_{ij}/N$. Expanding:

$$\mathbb{E}[z^2] = \sum_{i,j}\left(\mu_i^2\mu_j^2 + \mu_i\mu_j\delta_{ij}\!\lef
t(1+\tfrac{1}{N}\right) + \frac{\delta_{ij}}{N}\right) = H_l^2 + \left(1+\frac{1
}{N}\right)H_l + \frac{l}{N}.$$

Then $\operatorname{Var}(z) = \mathbb{E}[z^2] - (\mathbb{E}[z])^2 = H_l^2 + (1+1
/N)H_l + l/N - H_l^2 = (1+1/N)H_l + l/N$. ✓

---

### Result

For a test sample $x \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, I)$ from class $\omega_1$, independen
t of the training data:

$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}[z] = H_l = \sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{1}{i}, \qquad \operatorname{V
ar}(z) = H_l\!\left(1 + \frac{1}{N}\right) + \frac{l}{N} = H_l + \frac{H_l + l}{
N}.}$$

**Remark.** If $x$ is drawn from $\omega_2$ instead ($x \sim \mathcal{N}(-\mu, I
)$), the mean becomes $\mathbb{E}[z] = -H_l$ while the variance remains the same
, since $\operatorname{Var}(z)$ depends only on $\|\mu\|^2$ and the dimension $l
$, not on the sign of $\mu$.
