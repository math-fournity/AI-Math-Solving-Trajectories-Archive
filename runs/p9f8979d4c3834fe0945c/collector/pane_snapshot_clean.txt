## Solution

**Setup.** Place the table as the rectangle $[0, 2010] \times [0, 2010]$, tiled
by unit squares $[i, i+1] \times [j, j+1]$ for $i, j \in \{0, 1, \dots, 2009\}$.
 The coin has radius $1$. Given the coin lands completely on the table, its cent
er $(X, Y)$ is uniformly distributed over the region $[1, 2009] \times [1, 2009]
$, which has area $2008^2$.

**Covering condition for a single tile.** The coin completely covers tile $(i, j
)$ (with center $(i+\tfrac12, j+\tfrac12)$) if and only if every point of the ti
le is within distance $1$ of $(X, Y)$. The farthest point of the tile from the c
oin's center is always a corner, so the condition is:
$$\left(\tfrac12 + |X - (i+\tfrac12)|\right)^2 + \left(\tfrac12 + |Y - (j+\tfrac
12)|\right)^2 \le 1.$$

Let $x = X - (i+\tfrac12)$, $y = Y - (j+\tfrac12)$. The "covering region" for ti
le $(i,j)$ is:
$$\mathcal{R} = \left\{(x, y) : \left(\tfrac12 + |x|\right)^2 + \left(\tfrac12 +
 |y|\right)^2 \le 1\right\}.$$

Note that if $|x| > \tfrac12$ then $(\tfrac12 + |x|)^2 > 1$, making the sum exce
ed $1$. So $\mathcal{R} \subseteq [-\tfrac12, \tfrac12]^2$, meaning **the coveri
ng region for each tile is contained entirely within that tile itself**. Consequ
ently, covering regions of distinct tiles are disjoint (tiles share only boundar
ies, which have measure zero).

**Computing the area of $\mathcal{R}$.** By the four-fold symmetry, compute the
first-quadrant portion and multiply by $4$. In the first quadrant, set $u = \tfr
ac12 + x$, $v = \tfrac12 + y$ with $u, v \ge \tfrac12$:
$$\text{Area}_{Q_1} = \iint_{\substack{u \ge 1/2,\; v \ge 1/2 \\ u^2 + v^2 \le 1
}} du\, dv = \int_{1/2}^{\sqrt{3}/2} \left(\sqrt{1 - u^2} - \tfrac12\right) du.$
$

(The upper limit $\sqrt{3}/2$ comes from setting $v = 1/2$: $u^2 = 3/4$.)

For the first integral, substitute $u = \sin\theta$, $du = \cos\theta\, d\theta$
, with limits $\theta = \pi/6$ to $\pi/3$:
$$\int_{1/2}^{\sqrt{3}/2} \sqrt{1-u^2}\, du = \int_{\pi/6}^{\pi/3} \cos^2\theta\
, d\theta = \left[\frac{\theta}{2} + \frac{\sin 2\theta}{4}\right]_{\pi/6}^{\pi/
3}.$$
$$= \left(\frac{\pi}{6} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{8}\right) - \left(\frac{\pi}{12} + \fr
ac{\sqrt{3}}{8}\right) = \frac{\pi}{12}.$$

The second part:
$$\int_{1/2}^{\sqrt{3}/2} \frac12\, du = \frac12\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - \frac
12\right) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} - \frac14.$$

So:
$$\text{Area}_{Q_1} = \frac{\pi}{12} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} + \frac14, \qquad \tex
t{Area}(\mathcal{R}) = 4 \cdot \text{Area}_{Q_1} = \frac{\pi}{3} - \sqrt{3} + 1.
$$

**Boundary analysis.** The covering region for tile $(i, j)$ lies in $[i, i+1] \
times [j, j+1]$. The allowed center region is $[1, 2009]^2$.

- **Interior tiles** ($1 \le i \le 2008$, $1 \le j \le 2008$): their covering re
gions lie fully inside $[1, 2009]^2$. There are $2008 \times 2008$ such tiles, e
ach contributing area $\frac{\pi}{3} - \sqrt{3} + 1$.
- **Boundary tiles** ($i = 0$, $i = 2009$, $j = 0$, or $j = 2009$): their coveri
ng regions lie in $[0,1]$, $[2009, 2010]$, etc., which intersect $[1, 2009]^2$ o
nly along line segments (measure zero). These contribute $0$.

**Final computation.** Since the covering regions are disjoint:
$$\text{Favorable area} = 2008^2 \times \left(\frac{\pi}{3} - \sqrt{3} + 1\right
).$$

$$\boxed{P = \frac{2008^2 \left(\frac{\pi}{3} - \sqrt{3} + 1\right)}{2008^2} = \
frac{\pi}{3} - \sqrt{3} + 1.}$$
