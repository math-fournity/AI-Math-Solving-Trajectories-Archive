**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.** The result follows from the Homotopy Extension Property (HEP) of the
inclusion $\partial M \hookrightarrow M$, together with a relative smooth-approx
imation step.

---

**Step 1 — The inclusion $i:\partial M\hookrightarrow M$ is a cofibration (HEP).
**

Since $M$ is a smooth manifold with (non-empty) boundary, $\partial M$ admits a
collar neighborhood: there is a smooth embedding $c:\partial M\times[0,1)\hookri
ghtarrow M$ with $c(x,0)=x$. This exhibits $\partial M$ as a neighborhood deform
ation retract of $M$, so the pair $(M,\partial M)$ satisfies the Homotopy Extens
ion Property: for any space $Y$, any map $F:M\to Y$, and any homotopy $H:\partia
l M\times I\to Y$ with $H_{0}=F|_{\partial M}$, there exists a continuous homoto
py $K:M\times I\to Y$ with
$$K_{0}=F,\qquad K|_{\partial M\times I}=H.$$

(Concretely, the collar yields a continuous retraction $r:M\times I\to M\times\{
0\}\cup\partial M\times I$ that is the identity on $M\times\{0\}\cup\partial M\t
imes I$; then $K=(F\cup H)\circ r$.)

---

**Step 2 — Build a continuous homotopy extending $H$.**

Apply the HEP with $Y=N$, the given smooth map $F:M\to N$, and the given smooth
homotopy $H:\partial M\times I\to N$ (satisfying $H_{0}=f=F|_{\partial M}$, $H_{
1}=g$). We obtain a continuous homotopy
$$K:M\times I\to N,\qquad K_{0}=F,\quad K|_{\partial M\times I}=H.$$

---

**Step 3 — Smooth the homotopy relative to where it is already smooth.**

Set
$$A:=(M\times\{0\})\cup(\partial M\times I)\;\subset\; M\times I.$$
This is a closed subset. On $A$, the map $K$ is smooth: on $M\times\{0\}$ it equ
als $F$ (smooth), and on $\partial M\times I$ it equals $H$ (smooth); the two de
finitions agree on the overlap $\partial M\times\{0\}$, where both equal $f$.

By the relative form of the Whitney smooth approximation theorem — a continuous
map between smooth manifolds (with corners) that is already smooth on a closed s
ubset can be approximated by a smooth map agreeing with it on that closed subset
 — there exists a smooth homotopy
$$\widetilde K:M\times I\to N$$
with $\widetilde K|_{A}=K|_{A}$. (The space $M\times I$ is a smooth manifold wit
h corners since $M$ has boundary; the relative approximation theorem is standard
 in this setting, e.g. via an embedding of $N$ into some $\mathbb R^{k}$ togethe
r with a tubular-neighborhood retraction, applying ordinary smooth approximation
 relative to the closed set $A$ and projecting back.)

Consequently:
- $\widetilde K_{0}=K_{0}=F$ on $M\times\{0\}$,
- $\widetilde K|_{\partial M\times I}=H$.

---

**Step 4 — Define $G$ and verify the required properties.**

Let $G:=\widetilde K_{1}:M\to N$. Then:

1. **Smoothness:** $G$ is the time-$1$ slice of the smooth homotopy $\widetilde
K$, hence smooth.
2. **Boundary value:** $G|_{\partial M}=\widetilde K_{1}|_{\partial M}=H_{1}=g$.
3. **Homotopy to $F$:** $\widetilde K$ is a smooth homotopy from $\widetilde K_{
0}=F$ to $\widetilde K_{1}=G$, so $G$ is (smoothly) homotopic to $F$.

Thus $G:M\to N$ is a smooth map, homotopic to $F$, whose restriction to $\partia
l M$ is exactly $g$.

---

**Conclusion.** Such a map $G$ necessarily exists: the homotopy of the boundary
restriction always extends to a homotopy of the whole map, by the Homotopy Exten
sion Property of the collar pair $(M,\partial M)$, with smoothness restored by r
elative approximation. $\blacksquare$
