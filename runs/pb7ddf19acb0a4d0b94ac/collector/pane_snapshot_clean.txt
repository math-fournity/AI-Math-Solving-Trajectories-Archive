Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $\overline{A \setminus B}$
can strictly contain $A \setminus B$, where $A$ and $B$ are relatively open sets
 in a Banach space.

**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

Consider the Banach space $X = \mathbb{R}$ with the standard absolute value norm
. Let $S = [0, 2] \subset \mathbb{R}$ be the ambient subset, and equip it with t
he relative topology inherited from $\mathbb{R}$.

Define two sets:

$$A = [0, 1), \qquad B = (1, 2].$$

**Both are relatively open in $S$:**
- $A = S \cap (-\infty, 1)$, and $(-\infty, 1)$ is open in $\mathbb{R}$, so $A$
is relatively open in $S$.
- $B = S \cap (1, +\infty)$, and $(1, +\infty)$ is open in $\mathbb{R}$, so $B$
is relatively open in $S$.

**Compute the set difference:**

Since $[0, 1)$ consists of points $x < 1$ and $(1, 2]$ consists of points $x > 1
$, these two sets are disjoint. Therefore:

$$A \setminus B = [0, 1) \setminus (1, 2] = [0, 1).$$

**Compute the closure:**

The closure of $[0, 1)$ in $\mathbb{R}$ is:

$$\overline{A \setminus B} = \overline{[0, 1)} = [0, 1].$$

(Even taking the closure relative to $S$: $\overline{[0,1)} \cap S = [0,1] \cap
[0,2] = [0,1]$, the same result.)

**Identify the extra point:**

The point $1$ belongs to $\overline{A \setminus B} = [0, 1]$, but $1 \notin [0,
1) = A \setminus B$.

Therefore:

$$\overline{A \setminus B} \setminus (A \setminus B) = \{1\} \neq \emptyset.$$

**Conclusion.** The closure of the difference of two relatively open sets can in
deed contain a point that is not in the difference of those sets. The essential
reason is that the set difference $A \setminus B = A \cap B^c$ is the intersecti
on of a (relatively) open set with a (relatively) closed set, which is in genera
l neither open nor closed. Boundary points of $A$ that lie outside $B$ can be li
mit points of $A \setminus B$ without belonging to $A \setminus B$ itself.
