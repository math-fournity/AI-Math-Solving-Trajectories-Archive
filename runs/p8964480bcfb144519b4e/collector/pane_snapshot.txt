# Torsion in Fundamental Groups of Connected Smooth Orientable 2-Manifolds

**Claim.** The fundamental group of every connected smooth orientable 2-manifold
 is torsion-free. In particular, no such manifold can have a torsion element in
its fundamental group.

---

## Proof

Let $M$ be a connected smooth orientable 2-manifold, and let $\widetilde{M}$ be
its universal cover, with deck transformation group $\pi_1(M)$ acting freely and
 properly discontinuously on $\widetilde{M}$.

### Step 1. Classification of the universal cover

By the classical uniformization/classification theorem for surfaces, the univers
al cover $\widetilde{M}$ is one of:

- $S^2$, if $M = S^2$ (the 2-sphere),
- $\mathbb{R}^2$, if $M$ is the plane, the open cylinder $S^1 \times \mathbb{R}$
, or the torus $T^2$,
- $\mathbb{H}^2$ (the hyperbolic plane), for all remaining orientable surfaces.

Since $M$ is orientable, the non-orientable cases ($\mathbb{RP}^2$, Klein bottle
) are excluded.

### Step 2. The sphere case

If $M = S^2$, then $\pi_1(M) = 1$, the trivial group, which is trivially torsion
-free.

### Step 3. The remaining cases: $\widetilde{M}$ is contractible

For every other orientable surface, $\widetilde{M}$ is either $\mathbb{R}^2$ or
$\mathbb{H}^2$, both of which are contractible. Hence $M$ is a $K(\pi_1(M), 1)$
space (an Eilenberg–MacLane space): its universal cover is contractible and $\pi
_1(M)$ acts freely and properly discontinuously on it.

### Step 4. Torsion-free via cohomological dimension

We now show $\pi_1(M)$ is torsion-free. Suppose for contradiction that $g \in \p
i_1(M)$ is a torsion element of order $n \geq 2$. Then the cyclic subgroup $C =
\langle g \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is a finite subgroup of the deck
 transformation group, and therefore acts **freely** on $\widetilde{M}$ (deck tr
ansformations have no fixed points by definition).

Since $\widetilde{M}$ is a contractible 2-manifold (hence has the homotopy type
of a 2-dimensional CW complex), the quotient $\widetilde{M}/C$ is also a 2-manif
old and serves as a $K(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z},\, 1)$: it is a finite-dimensional
 (dimension 2) model for the classifying space of $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$.

This gives a contradiction via **group cohomology**. The group $\mathbb{Z}/n\mat
hbb{Z}$ has cohomological dimension infinity, since:

$$H^{2k}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z};\, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \qua
d \text{for all } k \geq 1,$$

so its cohomology is nonzero in arbitrarily high degrees. However, any space wit
h the homotopy type of a 2-dimensional CW complex has trivial cohomology in degr
ees $> 2$. Since $\widetilde{M}/C$ is a 2-manifold, $H^k(\widetilde{M}/C;\, \mat
hbb{Z}) = 0$ for all $k > 2$.

But $K(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}, 1)$ spaces have isomorphic cohomology (they are u
nique up to homotopy), so we would need:

$$H^k(\widetilde{M}/C;\, \mathbb{Z}) \cong H^k(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z};\, \mathbb
{Z}) \quad \text{for all } k.$$

Taking $k = 4$: the left side is $0$ (dimension reason) while the right side is
$\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \neq 0$. **Contradiction.**

### Step 5. Conclusion

The contradiction shows that no element of $\pi_1(M)$ can have finite order $> 1
$. Combined with the trivial case $M = S^2$, we conclude:

> **Every connected smooth orientable 2-manifold has a torsion-free fundamental
group.** Equivalently, connected smooth orientable 2-manifolds **cannot** have t
orsion elements in their fundamental group.

**Remark.** The essential mechanism is general: any group acting freely and prop
erly discontinuously on a contractible space of finite covering dimension $n$ ha
s cohomological dimension $\leq n$, and hence is torsion-free (since finite-orde
r elements force infinite cohomological dimension). For orientable surfaces, the
 universal cover is at most 2-dimensional and contractible (except for $S^2$ its
elf, which has trivial $\pi_1$), so the result follows.
