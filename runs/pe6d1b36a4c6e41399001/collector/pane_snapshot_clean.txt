Looking at this problem, I need to find all integers $n > 1$ such that $\mathbb{
N}^*$ can be partitioned into $n$ non-empty disjoint subsets, each closed under
$f(a,b,c) = abc - b - c + 2$.

**Key Substitution.** For $a \in \mathbb{N}^*$, let $v_2(a-1)$ denote the 2-adic
 valuation of $a-1$ (with $v_2(0) = \infty$ for $a=1$). Define:
- $V_k = \{a \in \mathbb{N}^* : v_2(a-1) = k\}$ for $k \geq 0$ (so $V_0$ = even
numbers, $V_1 = \{3,7,11,\ldots\}$, etc.)
- $V_{\geq m} = \{a \in \mathbb{N}^* : v_2(a-1) \geq m\}$ for $m \geq 1$.

**Core Formula.** Write $a = 1 + 2^p\alpha$, $b = 1 + 2^q\beta$, $c = 1 + 2^r\ga
mma$ with $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ odd. Expanding:

$$f(a,b,c) - 1 = abc - b - c + 1 = 2^p\alpha(1+2^q\beta)(1+2^r\gamma) + 2^{q+r}\
beta\gamma.$$

This can be verified by direct expansion of $(1+2^p\alpha)(1+2^q\beta)(1+2^r\gam
ma) - (1+2^q\beta) - (1+2^r\gamma) + 1$.

**Closure Lemma.** The following sets are each closed under $f$:

1. **$V_0$ (even numbers):** If $a,b,c$ are even, then $abc$ is even and $b+c$ i
s even, so $f = abc - b - c + 2$ is even. ✓

2. **$\{1\}$:** $f(1,1,1) = 1 - 1 - 1 + 2 = 1$. ✓

3. **$V_k$ for $k \geq 1$:** With $p=q=r=k$ and $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ odd: the f
irst term $2^k\alpha(1+2^k\beta)(1+2^k\gamma)$ has $v_2 = k$ (since $k \geq 1$ m
akes $(1+2^k\beta)$ and $(1+2^k\gamma)$ odd). The second term $2^{2k}\beta\gamma
$ has $v_2 = 2k > k$. So $v_2(f-1) = k$, giving $f \in V_k$. ✓

4. **$V_{\geq m} \setminus \{1\}$ for $m \geq 1$:** With $p,q,r \geq m \geq 1$:
the first term has $v_2 = p \geq m$ (factors $(1+2^q\beta), (1+2^r\gamma)$ are o
dd since $q,r \geq 1$), and the second term has $v_2 = q+r \geq 2m \geq m$. So $
v_2(f-1) \geq m$. Since $a,b,c \geq 3$, we get $f \geq 27 - 6 + 2 = 23 > 1$, so
$f \in V_{\geq m} \setminus \{1\}$. ✓

5. **$\{1\} \cup V_{\geq 1}$ (all odd numbers):** If $a,b,c$ are all odd, then $
abc$ is odd and $b+c$ is even, so $f = \text{odd} - \text{even} + 2 = \text{odd}
$. ✓

**Construction for $n = 2$:** Partition into $A_1 = \{1\} \cup V_{\geq 1}$ (odd)
 and $A_2 = V_0$ (even). Both are closed by items 5 and 1 above. ✓

**Construction for $n \geq 3$:** Partition into:
$$A_1 = \{1\},\quad A_2 = V_0,\quad A_3 = V_1,\quad \ldots,\quad A_{n-1} = V_{n-
3},\quad A_n = V_{\geq\, n-2} \setminus \{1\}.$$

This gives exactly $n$ parts. Each $V_k$ ($0 \leq k \leq n-3$) is closed by item
s 1–3, $\{1\}$ is closed by item 2, and $V_{\geq n-2} \setminus \{1\}$ is closed
 by item 4 (since $n-2 \geq 1$). All parts are non-empty ($V_k$ contains $2^k+1$
; $V_{\geq m}\setminus\{1\}$ contains $2^m+1 \geq 3$). The parts are pairwise di
sjoint and cover $\mathbb{N}^*$ since every $a \neq 1$ has a unique $v_2(a-1) \g
eq 0$. ✓

**Conclusion.** Every integer $n \geq 2$ admits such a partition, so $S = \{2, 3
, 4, \ldots\}$.

The sum of elements of $S$ that are $\leq 100$:

$$\sum_{k=2}^{100} k = \frac{100 \cdot 101}{2} - 1 = 5050 - 1 = \boxed{5049}.$$
