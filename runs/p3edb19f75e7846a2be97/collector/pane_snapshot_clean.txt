## Solution

We solve Laplace's equation $\Delta u = 0$ on the semi-infinite strip $\Omega =
\{(x,y): 0 < x < L,\; y < 0\}$. Throughout we impose the **boundedness/decay con
dition** $u \to 0$ (or bounded) as $y \to -\infty$, which is necessary for uniqu
eness on an unbounded domain.

The key tool for identifying solvability conditions is the **flux-balance (compa
tibility) identity**, obtained by integrating $\Delta u = 0$ over $\Omega$ (trun
cated at $y = -R$) and applying the divergence theorem:

$$0 = \int_{\Omega_R} \Delta u\,dA = \oint_{\partial\Omega_R} \frac{\partial u}{
\partial n}\,ds.$$

As $R\to\infty$, the contribution from the bottom edge vanishes (by decay), givi
ng

$$\int_0^L u_y(x,0)\,dx \;-\; \int_{-\infty}^0 u_x(0,y)\,dy \;+\; \int_{-\infty}
^0 u_x(L,y)\,dy \;=\; 0. \tag{$\star$}$$

This says: **net flux through the boundary must vanish**. For mixed (Dirichlet +
 Neumann) problems the Dirichlet data anchors the solution and no condition is n
eeded. For **pure Neumann** problems, ($\star$) becomes a genuine constraint on
the data.

### (a) $u_x(0,y)=g_1(y),\; u_x(L,y)=g_2(y),\; u(x,0)=f(x)$

**Type:** Mixed — Neumann on both vertical sides, Dirichlet on the top.

**Solvability condition:** **None.** The Dirichlet data $f(x)$ on the top anchor
s the solution; the mixed Dirichlet–Neumann problem for Laplace's equation is we
ll-posed without compatibility conditions.

**Solution method.** Subtract a function carrying the nonhomogeneous Neumann dat
a. Let

$$\phi(x,y) = x\,g_1(y) + \frac{x^2}{2L}\bigl[g_2(y)-g_1(y)\bigr],$$

so that $\phi_x(0,y)=g_1(y)$ and $\phi_x(L,y)=g_2(y)$. Set $u = \phi + \tilde u$
. Then $\tilde u$ satisfies

$$\Delta\tilde u = -\Delta\phi, \quad \tilde u_x(0,y)=\tilde u_x(L,y)=0, \quad \
tilde u(x,0) = f(x)-\phi(x,0), \quad \tilde u \text{ bounded as } y\to-\infty.$$

The homogeneous Neumann conditions in $x$ allow a **cosine expansion**:

$$\tilde u(x,y) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \tilde u_n(y)\cos\!\Bigl(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\
Bigr),$$

where each mode satisfies $\tilde u_n'' - (n\pi/L)^2 \tilde u_n = F_n(y)$ (with
$F_n$ the cosine coefficients of $-\Delta\phi$), $\tilde u_n(0) = $ (cosine coef
ficient of $f-\phi(x,0)$), and $\tilde u_n$ bounded as $y\to-\infty$. Each ODE h
as two boundary conditions (one at $y=0$, one at $y\to-\infty$), so every mode —
 including $n=0$ — is uniquely determined. No constraint on the data arises.

### (b) $u(0,y)=g_1(y),\; u_x(L,y)=0,\; u_y(x,0)=0$

**Type:** Mixed — Dirichlet on the left, Neumann on the right and top.

**Solvability condition:** **None.** The Dirichlet condition on the left side an
chors the solution.

**Solution.** The $x$-eigenfunctions satisfying $X(0)=0$ (Dirichlet) and $X'(L)=
0$ (Neumann) are

$$X_n(x) = \sin(\lambda_n x), \qquad \lambda_n = \frac{(n+\tfrac12)\pi}{L}, \qua
d n=0,1,2,\dots$$

For the homogeneous version ($g_1\equiv 0$), separation of variables gives

$$u(x,y) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} B_n \sin(\lambda_n x)\,e^{\lambda_n y},$$

which decays as $y\to-\infty$ (since $\lambda_n>0$). For nonhomogeneous $g_1(y)$
, subtract a particular solution, e.g. $\psi(x,y)=g_1(y)$ (constant in $x$, so $
\psi_x(L,y)=0$), and solve the resulting Poisson problem for $\tilde u = u - \ps
i$ via the same sine expansion. Each mode satisfies a well-posed second-order OD
E in $y$; no compatibility condition emerges.

### (c) $u_x(0,y)=0,\; u_x(L,y)=0,\; u_y(x,0)=f(x)$

**Type:** Pure Neumann on all three sides.

**Solvability condition:** **Yes.**

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_0^L f(x)\,dx = 0.}$$

**Derivation via separation of variables.** Homogeneous Neumann in $x$ gives cos
ine eigenfunctions:

$$u(x,y) = A_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n \cos\!\Bigl(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\Bigr)\,e^{
n\pi y/L}.$$

(For $n\geq 1$, $Y_n = e^{n\pi y/L}\to 0$ as $y\to-\infty$. For $n=0$, $Y_0''=0$
 gives $Y_0 = A_0 + B_0 y$; decay forces $B_0=0$, leaving the constant $A_0$.)

Differentiating at $y=0$:

$$u_y(x,0) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n\pi}{L}\,A_n \cos\!\Bigl(\frac{n\pi x}{L
}\Bigr) = f(x).$$

The right-hand side is a cosine series with **no constant ($n=0$) term**. Theref
ore the $n=0$ cosine coefficient of $f$ must vanish:

$$\frac{1}{L}\int_0^L f(x)\,dx = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \int_0^L f(x)\,dx =
 0.$$

This is exactly the flux-balance ($\star$): the sides contribute zero flux, the
bottom (at infinity) contributes zero, so the top flux $\int_0^L f\,dx$ must van
ish.

When the condition holds, the coefficients are

$$A_n = \frac{2}{n\pi}\int_0^L f(\xi)\cos\!\Bigl(\frac{n\pi\xi}{L}\Bigr)d\xi, \q
uad n\geq 1,$$

and $A_0$ is **undetermined** — the solution is unique up to an additive constan
t, as expected for a pure Neumann problem.

**Physical explanation.** Laplace's equation governs steady-state temperature di
stribution. Neumann data prescribe **heat flux** through the boundary. If $\int_
0^L f\,dx \neq 0$, there is a net heat source (or sink) through the top, so heat
 accumulates (or depletes) and no steady state can exist — the temperature would
 change indefinitely. The condition $\int_0^L f\,dx = 0$ says the total heat flu
x through the boundary is balanced, which is necessary for thermal equilibrium.
The undetermined constant $A_0$ reflects that only temperature *differences* are
 physically meaningful when all boundary conditions specify flux.

### (d) $u_x(0,y)=0,\; u_x(L,y)=g(y),\; u_y(x,0)=0$

**Type:** Pure Neumann on all three sides.

**Solvability condition:** **Yes.**

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0} g(y)\,dy = 0.}$$

**Derivation via flux balance ($\star$).** The contributions are:
- Top ($y=0$): $u_y(x,0)=0 \;\Rightarrow\;$ flux $= 0$.
- Left ($x=0$): $u_x(0,y)=0 \;\Rightarrow\;$ flux $= -\int_{-\infty}^0 0\,dy = 0
$.
- Right ($x=L$): $u_x(L,y)=g(y) \;\Rightarrow\;$ flux $= \int_{-\infty}^0 g(y)\,
dy$.
- Bottom ($y\to-\infty$): flux $= 0$ by decay.

Equation ($\star$) gives $\int_{-\infty}^0 g(y)\,dy = 0$.

**Solution method.** Subtract $\phi(x,y) = \frac{x^2}{2L}\,g(y)$, which satisfie
s $\phi_x(0,y)=0$ and $\phi_x(L,y)=g(y)$. Then $\tilde u = u - \phi$ has homogen
eous Neumann on both sides and $\tilde u_y(x,0) = -\frac{x^2}{2L}g'(0)$, with $\
Delta\tilde u = -\Delta\phi$. Expand in cosine series $\tilde u = \sum \tilde u_
n(y)\cos(n\pi x/L)$. The $n=0$ mode equation is $\tilde u_0''(y) = F_0(y)$ with
$\tilde u_0'(0) = $ (cosine coefficient of $-\frac{x^2}{2L}g'(0)$) and $\tilde u
_0$ bounded as $y\to-\infty$. The solvability of this mode requires $\int_{-\inf
ty}^0 F_0(y)\,dy$ to match the boundary data — which reduces precisely to $\int_
{-\infty}^0 g(y)\,dy = 0$.

When the condition holds, the solution exists and is **unique up to an additive
constant**.

**Physical explanation.** Here $g(y)$ prescribes the heat flux through the right
 wall, while the left wall and top are insulated (zero flux). If $\int_{-\infty}
^0 g\,dy \neq 0$, there is a net heat input (or output) through the right wall w
ith nowhere for it to go (all other boundaries insulated), so heat accumulates a
nd no steady state exists. The condition $\int_{-\infty}^0 g\,dy = 0$ requires t
he total flux through the right wall to be self-balancing (equal amounts enterin
g and leaving over the infinite wall), permitting thermal equilibrium. Again, th
e additive constant reflects the arbitrariness of absolute temperature under pur
e flux conditions.

### Summary

| Part | Boundary type | Solvability condition? |
|------|--------------|----------------------|
| (a) | Neumann–Neumann sides + Dirichlet top (mixed) | **No** |
| (b) | Dirichlet left + Neumann right + Neumann top (mixed) | **No** |
| (c) | Pure Neumann | **Yes:** $\displaystyle\int_0^L f(x)\,dx = 0$ |
| (d) | Pure Neumann | **Yes:** $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0} g(y)\,dy = 0$ |

The general principle: **mixed** Dirichlet–Neumann problems for Laplace's equati
on are well-posed (the Dirichlet data fixes the solution level); **pure Neumann*
* problems require zero net flux through the boundary (steady-state heat balance
), and when solvable, the solution is unique only up to an additive constant.
