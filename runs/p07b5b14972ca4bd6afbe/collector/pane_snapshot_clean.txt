## Proof

We construct a serial euclidean Kripke frame with 3 worlds on which an instance
of axiom 4 (□p → □□p) fails. Since axiom 4 is a theorem of KT4 = S4 but is not v
alid over the class of serial euclidean frames (the frames of KD5), this shows K
D5 ≠ S4.

### The Frame

Let $W = \{a, b, c\}$ with accessibility relation

$$R = \{(a,b),\, (b,b),\, (b,c),\, (c,b),\, (c,c)\}.$$

### Seriality

Every world has a successor: $a \to b$, $b \to b$, $c \to b$. ✓

### Euclideanity

We verify: whenever $wRv$ and $wRu$, we have $vRu$.

- **World $a$**: sole successor is $b$. The only pair is $(b,b)$, and $bRb$ hold
s. ✓
- **World $b$**: successors are $\{b, c\}$. All four pairs $(b,b), (b,c), (c,b),
 (c,c)$ are in $R$. ✓
- **World $c$**: successors are $\{b, c\}$. Same as above — all four pairs are i
n $R$. ✓

So the frame is euclidean.

### Non-transitivity

We have $aRb$ and $bRc$, but $a \not\!R\, c$. So the frame is **not transitive**
.

### Valuation making Axiom 4 fail

Define the valuation $V(p) = \{b\}$, i.e., $p$ is true at $b$ and false at $a$ a
nd $c$.

Evaluate $\Box p \to \Box\Box p$ at world $a$:

- **Successors of $a$**: $\{b\}$.
- $\Box p$ at $a$: $p$ holds at every successor of $a$, i.e., $p(b) = \text{true
}$. So $\Box p$ holds at $a$.
- $\Box\Box p$ at $a$: $\Box p$ holds at every successor of $a$, i.e., at $b$.
  - $\Box p$ at $b$: $p$ holds at every successor of $b = \{b, c\}$, i.e., $p(b)
 \wedge p(c) = \text{true} \wedge \text{false} = \text{false}$.
  - So $\Box p$ fails at $b$, hence $\Box\Box p$ fails at $a$.

Therefore $\Box p \to \Box\Box p$ is **false** at $a$.

### Conclusion

The frame $(W, R)$ is serial and euclidean, hence it is a KD5-frame (seriality v
alidates axiom D, euclideanity validates axiom 5, and K is valid on all frames).
 Yet the instance $\Box p \to \Box\Box p$ of axiom 4 fails at world $a$ under th
e valuation $V(p) = \{b\}$.

Since axiom 4 is a theorem of KT4 = S4 but is not valid over the class of all se
rial euclidean frames, axiom 4 is not a theorem of KD5. Hence $\mathrm{KD5} \neq
 \mathrm{KT4} = \mathrm{S4}$. $\blacksquare$
