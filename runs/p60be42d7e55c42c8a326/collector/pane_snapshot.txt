$$B_p = \{(i,j) \in E(C_9) : v_p(a_i) + v_p(a_j) < e_p\}$$
The conditions translate to:

or at least one $p \mid n$. Thus $\bigcup_p B_p \supseteq E(C_9)$.
- **Non-adjacent pairs**: $n \mid a_i a_j$, so $(i,j) \notin B_p$ for every $p$.
 Thus $B_p$ contains **no non-adjacent pair** for any $p$.

**Key Lemma.** *For any prime $p \mid n$ with exponent $e \geq 1$, and any valid

*Proof of Lemma.* We show $B_p$ cannot contain 3 edges. On $C_9$, any 3 edges ei

**Case 1: Path of 3 consecutive edges** $(1,2), (2,3), (3,4) \in B_p$. Then $x_1
+x_2 \leq e-1$, $x_2+x_3 \leq e-1$, $x_3+x_4 \leq e-1$. Since $(1,3)$ is non-adj
acent: $x_1+x_3 \geq e$, giving $x_3 \geq x_2+1$. Since $(2,4)$ is non-adjacent:
 $x_2+x_4 \geq e$, giving $x_4 \geq e - x_2$. Then $x_3+x_4 \geq (x_2+1)+(e-x_2)
 = e+1 > e-1$, contradicting $x_3+x_4 \leq e-1$.

**Case 2: Two disjoint edges** $(1,2), (4,5) \in B_p$. Then $x_1+x_2 \leq e-1$,
$x_4+x_5 \leq e-1$. Since $(1,4)$ non-adjacent: $x_4 \geq e-x_1$. Since $(2,5)$
non-adjacent: $x_5 \geq e-x_2$. Then $x_4+x_5 \geq 2e-(x_1+x_2) \geq 2e-(e-1) =
e+1 > e-1$, contradicting $x_4+x_5 \leq e-1$.

Both cases yield contradictions, so $|B_p| \leq 2$. If $|B_p|=2$ with disjoint e
