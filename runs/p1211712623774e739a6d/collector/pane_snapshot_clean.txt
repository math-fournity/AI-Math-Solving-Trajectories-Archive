## Proof

We compute
$$L := \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}|\zeta(\sigma+it)|^2\,dt, \qqua
d \sigma>1.$$

**Step 1: Dirichlet series expansion.** For $\sigma>1$ the Dirichlet series for
$\zeta$ is absolutely and uniformly convergent on the line $\Re s=\sigma$:
$$\zeta(\sigma+it)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-\sigma-it},\qquad \zeta(\sigma-it)=\ov
erline{\zeta(\sigma+it)}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{-\sigma+it}.$$
Since both series converge absolutely, their product equals the double sum:
$$|\zeta(\sigma+it)|^2=\sum_{n,m\ge1}(nm)^{-\sigma}\,e^{it\log(m/n)}.$$

**Step 2: Integrate term-by-term.** The double series $\sum_{n,m}(nm)^{-\sigma}=
\zeta(\sigma)^2<\infty$ converges (as $\sigma>1$), and each summand is bounded b
y $(nm)^{-\sigma}$, so by Fubini/dominated convergence we may integrate termwise
:
$$\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}|\zeta(\sigma+it)|^2\,dt=\sum_{n,m\ge1}(nm)^{-\sigma}
\,\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}e^{it\log(m/n)}\,dt.$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the mean of the exponential.** For $\alpha=\log(m/n)$:
$$\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}e^{it\alpha}\,dt=\begin{cases}1,&\alpha=0\;(m=n),\\[4
pt]\dfrac{\sin(T\alpha)}{T\alpha},&\alpha\ne0.\end{cases}$$
When $m\ne n$, $|\sin(T\alpha)/(T\alpha)|\le 1/(T|\alpha|)\to0$ as $T\to\infty$.
 Hence
$$\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}e^{it\log(m/n)}\,dt=\mathbf{1}_{m=n}
.$$

**Step 4: Pass the limit through the sum.** Each averaged integral is bounded in
 modulus by $1$, and $\sum_{n,m}(nm)^{-\sigma}<\infty$. By the dominated converg
ence theorem for series,
$$L=\sum_{n,m\ge1}(nm)^{-\sigma}\,\mathbf{1}_{m=n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-2\sigm
a}=\zeta(2\sigma).$$

Therefore
$$\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}|\zeta(\sigma+it
)|^2\,dt=\zeta(2\sigma).}$$
