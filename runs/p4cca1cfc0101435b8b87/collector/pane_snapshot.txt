## Solution

**Answer: Yes.** The convergence $T_j(\phi) \to T(\phi)$ for all $\phi \in C_0^\
infty(\Omega)$ implies $T_j(\phi) \to T(\phi)$ for all $\phi \in C_0(\Omega)$, *
*provided the distributions are positive**. The positivity is essential; it furn
ishes the uniform local mass bounds needed to pass from smooth test functions to
 merely continuous ones.

---

### Proof

**Step 1. Positive distributions are positive Radon measures.**

Recall that a distribution $T \in \mathcal{D}'(\Omega)$ is *positive* if $T(\phi
)\ge 0$ for every $\phi\in C_0^\infty(\Omega)$ with $\phi\ge 0$. A classical the
orem of L. Schwartz states that every positive distribution is a positive Radon
measure: there exists a positive, locally finite Borel measure $\mu$ on $\Omega$
 such that

$$T(\phi)=\int_\Omega \phi\,d\mu, \qquad \forall\,\phi\in C_0^\infty(\Omega),$$

and this representation extends to all $\phi\in C_0(\Omega)$ (the integral is we
ll-defined since $\mu$ is locally finite and $\phi$ has compact support). See, e
.g., Rudin, *Functional Analysis*, Theorem 6.12.

Accordingly, write $T_j = \mu_j$ and $T = \mu$ for the corresponding positive Ra
don measures. The hypothesis becomes

$$\int \phi\,d\mu_j \;\longrightarrow\; \int \phi\,d\mu, \qquad \forall\,\phi\in
 C_0^\infty(\Omega). \tag{$\star$}$$

**Step 2. A uniform local mass bound.**

Fix $\psi\in C_0(\Omega)$ and let $K=\operatorname{supp}\psi$. Since $K$ is comp
act and $\Omega$ is open, choose an open set $U$ with $K\subset U\Subset\Omega$
(i.e., $\overline U$ is compact and $\overline U\subset\Omega$). Pick a cutoff $
\chi\in C_0^\infty(\Omega)$ with $0\le\chi\le 1$, $\chi\equiv 1$ on $\overline U
$, and $\operatorname{supp}\chi\Subset\Omega$.

By $(\star)$, $\mu_j(\chi)\to\mu(\chi)$, so the sequence $\{\mu_j(\chi)\}_{j\ge
1}$ is bounded. Set

$$M:=\sup_{j\ge 1}\mu_j(\chi)+\mu(\chi)<\infty. \tag{1}$$

**Step 3. Smooth approximation of $\psi$.**

Since $\psi$ is continuous with $\operatorname{supp}\psi\subset K\subset U$, sta
ndard mollification yields a family $\{\psi_\varepsilon\}_{\varepsilon>0}\subset
 C_0^\infty(U)$ with

$$\|\psi_\varepsilon-\psi\|_\infty\to 0 \quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0, \qquad
\operatorname{supp}\psi_\varepsilon\subset U\Subset\Omega. \tag{2}$$

(Convolving $\psi$ with a smooth mollifier supported in a sufficiently small bal
l keeps the support inside $U$.)

**Step 4. The key estimate.**

For any $j$ and any $\varepsilon>0$, decompose:

$$\bigl|\mu_j(\psi)-\mu(\psi)\bigr|\;\le\;\underbrace{\bigl|\mu_j(\psi)-\mu_j(\p
si_\varepsilon)\bigr|}_{(I)}+\underbrace{\bigl|\mu_j(\psi_\varepsilon)-\mu(\psi_
\varepsilon)\bigr|}_{(II)}+\underbrace{\bigl|\mu(\psi_\varepsilon)-\mu(\psi)\big
r|}_{(III)}.$$

We bound $(I)$ and $(III)$ using positivity. Since $\operatorname{supp}(\psi-\ps
i_\varepsilon)\subset\overline U$ and $\chi\equiv 1$ on $\overline U$, we have p
ointwise

$$|\psi-\psi_\varepsilon|\;\le\;\|\psi-\psi_\varepsilon\|_\infty\cdot\chi \quad\
text{on }\Omega$$

(the inequality is trivial outside $\overline U$ where $\psi-\psi_\varepsilon=0$
, and on $\overline U$ it holds since $\chi=1$). Because $\mu_j$ and $\mu$ are *
positive* measures,

$$(I)=\mu_j\bigl(|\psi-\psi_\varepsilon|\bigr)\le\|\psi-\psi_\varepsilon\|_\inft
y\,\mu_j(\chi)\le\|\psi-\psi_\varepsilon\|_\infty\cdot M, \tag{3}$$

$$(III)=\mu\bigl(|\psi-\psi_\varepsilon|\bigr)\le\|\psi-\psi_\varepsilon\|_\inft
y\,\mu(\chi)\le\|\psi-\psi_\varepsilon\|_\infty\cdot M. \tag{4}$$

Combining (1)–(4):

$$\bigl|\mu_j(\psi)-\mu(\psi)\bigr|\;\le\;2M\,\|\psi-\psi_\varepsilon\|_\infty \
;+\;\bigl|\mu_j(\psi_\varepsilon)-\mu(\psi_\varepsilon)\bigr|. \tag{5}$$

**Step 5. Conclusion.**

Let $\delta>0$. By (2), choose $\varepsilon_0>0$ small enough that $\|\psi_{\var
epsilon_0}-\psi\|_\infty<\delta/(4M)$. Since $\psi_{\varepsilon_0}\in C_0^\infty
(\Omega)$, hypothesis $(\star)$ gives $J$ such that for all $j\ge J$,

$$\bigl|\mu_j(\psi_{\varepsilon_0})-\mu(\psi_{\varepsilon_0})\bigr|<\delta/2.$$

Applying (5) with $\varepsilon=\varepsilon_0$: for all $j\ge J$,

$$\bigl|\mu_j(\psi)-\mu(\psi)\bigr|<2M\cdot\frac{\delta}{4M}+\frac{\delta}{2}=\d
elta.$$

Since $\delta>0$ was arbitrary, $\mu_j(\psi)\to\mu(\psi)$, i.e., $T_j(\psi)\to T
(\psi)$.

As $\psi\in C_0(\Omega)$ was arbitrary, we conclude

$$T_j(\phi)\to T(\phi)\quad\text{for all }\phi\in C_0(\Omega). \qquad\blacksquar
e$$

### Remark on the role of positivity

The positivity hypothesis is indispensable. It provides two things simultaneousl
y: (a) the identification of each $T_j$ with a *measure* (so that $T_j(\psi)$ is
 even defined for non-smooth $\psi$), and (b) the **uniform local mass bound** (
1), $\mu_j(\chi)\le M$, which converts the approximation error $\|\psi_\varepsil
on-\psi\|_\infty$ into a controlled term $2M\|\psi_\varepsilon-\psi\|_\infty$ in
dependent of $j$. For general (signed) distributions, convergence on $C_0^\infty
$ does **not** imply convergence on $C_0$: the "mass" of $T_j$ can concentrate o
r oscillate in ways that are invisible to smooth tests but visible to continuous
 ones, and no uniform bound is available.
