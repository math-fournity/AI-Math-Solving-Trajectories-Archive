## Proof

**Interpretation.** The *separability measure* $\textrm{sep}(B_X)$ is the **dens
ity character** of $B_X$: the minimal cardinality of a norm-dense subset of the
unit ball. Note that $\textrm{dens}(B_X) = \textrm{dens}(X)$, since the open uni
t ball $\{x : \|x\| < 1\}$ is homeomorphic to $X$ via scaling and sits inside $B
_X$, while $B_X \subseteq X$. Similarly $\textrm{dens}(B_{X^*}) = \textrm{dens}(
X^*)$.

**Claim.** For every Banach space $X$, $\textrm{dens}(X) \leq \textrm{dens}(X^*)
$. This yields $\textrm{sep}(B_X) \leq \textrm{sep}(B_{X^*})$ with universal con
stant $C = 1$.

This generalizes the classical theorem that *a Banach space with separable dual
is separable*.

---

**Proof of the claim.** Let $\kappa = \textrm{dens}(X^*)$, and let $\{f_\alpha :
 \alpha < \kappa\}$ be a norm-dense subset of $X^*$.

**Step 1: Construct a candidate dense set in $X$.** For each $\alpha$ with $f_\a
lpha \neq 0$, by the definition of the operator norm $\|f_\alpha\| = \sup_{x \in
 B_X} |f_\alpha(x)|$, choose $x_\alpha \in B_X$ such that
$$|f_\alpha(x_\alpha)| \geq \frac{\|f_\alpha\|}{2}.$$

Set $Y = \overline{\textrm{span}}\{x_\alpha : \alpha < \kappa\}$. Then $Y$ is a
closed subspace of $X$ with
$$\textrm{dens}(Y) \leq \kappa,$$
since the collection of $\mathbb{Q}$-linear combinations of $\{x_\alpha\}$ is de
nse in $Y$ and has cardinality $\kappa \cdot \aleph_0 = \kappa$.

**Step 2: Show $Y = X$ by contradiction.** Suppose $Y \subsetneq X$. By the Hahn
–Banach theorem, there exists $f \in X^*$ with $\|f\| = 1$ and $f|_Y = 0$.

Since $\{f_\alpha\}$ is dense in $X^*$, for any $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $
\alpha$ with $\|f - f_\alpha\| < \varepsilon$.

Because $x_\alpha \in Y$, we have $f(x_\alpha) = 0$, so:
$$\frac{\|f_\alpha\|}{2} \leq |f_\alpha(x_\alpha)| = |f_\alpha(x_\alpha) - f(x_\
alpha)| \leq \|f_\alpha - f\| \cdot \|x_\alpha\| < \varepsilon \cdot 1 = \vareps
ilon.$$

This gives $\|f_\alpha\| < 2\varepsilon$. By the triangle inequality:
$$\|f\| \leq \|f - f_\alpha\| + \|f_\alpha\| < \varepsilon + 2\varepsilon = 3\va
repsilon.$$

Since $\varepsilon > 0$ is arbitrary, $\|f\| = 0$, contradicting $\|f\| = 1$.

**Step 3: Conclude.** Therefore $Y = X$, and
$$\textrm{dens}(X) = \textrm{dens}(Y) \leq \kappa = \textrm{dens}(X^*).$$

Since $\textrm{dens}(B_X) = \textrm{dens}(X)$ and $\textrm{dens}(B_{X^*}) = \tex
trm{dens}(X^*)$, we obtain:
$$\textrm{sep}(B_X) = \textrm{dens}(B_X) \leq \textrm{dens}(B_{X^*}) = \textrm{s
ep}(B_{X^*}).$$

**Verification of edge cases.** For finite-dimensional $X$ with $\dim X \geq 1$,
 both $B_X$ and $B_{X^*}$ are compact metric spaces, so $\textrm{sep}(B_X) = \te
xtrm{sep}(B_{X^*}) = \aleph_0$, and the inequality holds. For $X = \{0\}$, both
sides are trivially equal.

**Conclusion.** The universal constant $C = 1$ satisfies $\textrm{sep}(B_X) \leq
 C \cdot \textrm{sep}(B_{X^*})$ for every Banach space $X$. $\blacksquare$
