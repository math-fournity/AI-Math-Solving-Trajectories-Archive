## Proof

**Yes, $P$ is irreducible.**

Recall the standard characterization: a closed subset $P$ of a topological space
 is **irreducible** if and only if for every pair of open sets $U_1, U_2$ with $
P \cap U_1 \neq \emptyset$ and $P \cap U_2 \neq \emptyset$, we have $P \cap U_1
\cap U_2 \neq \emptyset$.

*(Proof of this equivalence: If $P \cap U_1 \cap U_2 = \emptyset$, then $P \subs
eteq (X \setminus U_1) \cup (X \setminus U_2)$, so $P = (P \setminus U_1) \cup (
P \setminus U_2)$, a union of two proper closed subsets of $P$ when both $P \cap
 U_i \neq \emptyset$, contradicting irreducibility. Conversely, if $P = P_1 \cup
 P_2$ with $P_1, P_2$ proper closed, set $U_i = X \setminus P_i$; then $P \cap U
_i \neq \emptyset$ but $P \cap U_1 \cap U_2 = \emptyset$.)*

The hypothesis only gives us this intersection property for **quasi-compact** op
en subsets. The key observation is that in a **spectral space**, the quasi-compa
ct open subsets form a **basis** for the topology (this is part of Hochster's ch
aracterization). We use this to reduce from arbitrary opens to quasi-compact ope
ns.

**Reduction step.** Let $U_1, U_2$ be arbitrary open sets with $P \cap U_1 \neq
\emptyset$ and $P \cap U_2 \neq \emptyset$. We must show $P \cap U_1 \cap U_2 \n
eq \emptyset$.

- Since $P \cap U_1 \neq \emptyset$, pick $x \in P \cap U_1$. Because quasi-comp
act opens form a basis, there exists a quasi-compact open $V$ with $x \in V \sub
seteq U_1$. Then $P \cap V \neq \emptyset$ (it contains $x$).

- Similarly, since $P \cap U_2 \neq \emptyset$, pick $y \in P \cap U_2$. There e
xists a quasi-compact open $W$ with $y \in W \subseteq U_2$. Then $P \cap W \neq
 \emptyset$.

- Apply the hypothesis to the quasi-compact opens $V$ and $W$: since $P \cap V \
neq \emptyset$ and $P \cap W \neq \emptyset$, we conclude $P \cap V \cap W \neq
\emptyset$.

- Since $V \subseteq U_1$ and $W \subseteq U_2$, we have $V \cap W \subseteq U_1
 \cap U_2$, hence $P \cap U_1 \cap U_2 \supseteq P \cap V \cap W \neq \emptyset$
.

This shows the intersection property holds for **all** open sets, not just quasi
-compact ones. By the characterization above, $P$ is irreducible. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on the empty case.** If $P = \emptyset$, the hypothesis is vacuously sa
tisfied (no quasi-compact open meets $P$), and $\emptyset$ is irreducible by the
 open-set characterization (also vacuously). The argument is consistent in all c
ases.
