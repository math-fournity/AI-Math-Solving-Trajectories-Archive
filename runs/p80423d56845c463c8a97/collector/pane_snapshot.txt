## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $E$ is necessarily dense in $X$.

The key fact we need is:

> **Lemma.** A Haar measure $\mu$ on a locally compact group $X$ is strictly pos
itive on every non-empty open Borel set: if $U \neq \emptyset$ is open, then $\m
u(U) > 0$.

*Proof of Lemma.* Since $\mu$ is by definition a nonzero regular Borel measure,
there exists a Borel set $B$ with $\mu(B) > 0$. By inner regularity, $\mu(B) = \
sup\{\mu(K) : K \subseteq B,\, K \text{ compact}\}$, so there is a compact set $
K$ with $\mu(K) > 0$.

Let $U$ be any non-empty open set. Pick $x \in U$. By continuity of the group op
eration, there is an open neighborhood $V$ of the identity $e$ such that $xV \su
bseteq U$. The family $\{gV : g \in X\}$ is an open cover of $X$, hence of $K$.
By compactness of $K$, there exist $g_1, \dots, g_n \in X$ with
$$K \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} g_i V.$$
By left-translation-invariance of $\mu$,
$$0 < \mu(K) \leq \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mu(g_i V) = n\,\mu(V).$$
Hence $\mu(V) > 0$, and since $xV \subseteq U$,
$$\mu(U) \geq \mu(xV) = \mu(V) > 0. \qquad \blacksquare$$

**Main proof.** Suppose for contradiction that $E$ is **not** dense in $X$. Then
 $\overline{E} \subsetneq X$, so the set
$$U := X \setminus \overline{E}$$
is a **non-empty open** set. Since $E \subseteq \overline{E}$, we have $U \cap E
 = \emptyset$, and therefore
$$U \subseteq X \setminus E.$$

By the Lemma, $\mu(U) > 0$. But $U \subseteq X \setminus E$ gives
$$0 < \mu(U) \leq \mu(X \setminus E) = 0,$$
a contradiction.

Therefore $E$ must be dense in $X$. $\blacksquare$
