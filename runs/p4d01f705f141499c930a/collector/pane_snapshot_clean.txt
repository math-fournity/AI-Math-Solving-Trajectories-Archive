## Proof

Place the triangle in a coordinate system with $B$ at the origin and $C = (c, 0)
$ on the $x$-axis, so $BC$ is the $x$-axis. Let $A = (a, b)$ with $b \neq 0$.

**Orthocenter.** The altitude from $A$ to $BC$ is the vertical line $x = a$. The
 altitude from $B$ is perpendicular to $AC$; since $AC$ has direction $(c-a, -b)
$, the altitude from $B$ has direction $(b, c-a)$, giving parametric form $(tb,\
, t(c-a))$. Setting $x = a$ gives $t = a/b$, so
$$H = \left(a,\; \frac{a(c-a)}{b}\right).$$
Denote $h_H = \frac{a(c-a)}{b}$, so $H = (a, h_H)$.

**Points $X$ and $Y$.** Let $P = (p, 0)$ on line $BC$. The perpendicular to $BC$
 through $P$ is the line $x = p$.

Line $BH$: parametrized as $(ta,\, t\,h_H)$. At $x = p$: $t = p/a$, giving $X =
\left(p,\; \frac{p(c-a)}{b}\right)$.

Line $CH$: parametrized as $(c + t(a-c),\, t\,h_H)$. At $x = p$: $t = \frac{p-c}
{a-c}$, giving $Y = \left(p,\; \frac{a(c-p)}{b}\right)$.

**Point $Q$.** The line through $H$ parallel to $BC$ is $y = h_H$. Line $AP$: fr
om $A = (a, b)$ to $P = (p, 0)$, parametrized as $(a + t(p-a),\, b - tb)$. Setti
ng $y = h_H$: $t = 1 - h_H/b$. Define
$$D = b^2 + a^2 - ac = b^2 - a(c-a),$$
so $t = D/b^2$, and
$$Q = \left(a + \frac{D(p-a)}{b^2},\; h_H\right) = \left(\frac{a^2(c-a) + pD}{b^
2},\; h_H\right).$$

**Point $U$ (line $QX$ meets $BC$).** Using the two-point form with $Q = (x_Q, h
_H)$ and $X = (p, \frac{p(c-a)}{b})$, the $x$-intercept ($y = 0$) is:
$$x_U = x_Q - \frac{h_H}{y_X - h_H}(p - x_Q).$$
Since $y_X - h_H = \frac{(c-a)(p-a)}{b}$ and $h_H = \frac{a(c-a)}{b}$, we get $\
frac{h_H}{y_X - h_H} = \frac{a}{p-a}$. Thus
$$x_U = x_Q - \frac{a}{p-a}(p - x_Q) = \frac{p(x_Q - a)}{p-a}.$$
Since $x_Q - a = \frac{D(p-a)}{b^2}$, we obtain
$$\boxed{U = \left(\frac{pD}{b^2},\; 0\right)}.$$

**Point $V$ (line $QY$ meets $BC$).** With $Y = (p, \frac{a(c-p)}{b})$:
$$y_Y - h_H = \frac{a(a-p)}{b} = -\frac{a(p-a)}{b}, \qquad \frac{h_H}{y_Y - h_H}
 = \frac{a-c}{p-a}.$$
Then
$$x_V = x_Q - \frac{a-c}{p-a}(p - x_Q) = \frac{x_Q(p-c) + (c-a)p}{p-a}.$$
Substituting $x_Q = \frac{a^2(c-a) + pD}{b^2}$ and performing polynomial divisio
n of the numerator by $(p-a)$ yields
$$\boxed{V = \left(\frac{pD + ac(c-a)}{b^2},\; 0\right)}.$$

**The triangle $QUV$ is congruent for all $P$.** Define the constants:
$$L = \frac{ac(c-a)}{b^2}, \qquad \alpha = \frac{a^2(c-a)}{b^2}, \qquad h_H = \f
rac{a(c-a)}{b}.$$
Setting $U_0 = \left(\frac{pD}{b^2}, 0\right)$, we can write:
- $U = U_0$,
- $V = U_0 + (L,\, 0)$, so $|UV| = L$ (constant),
- $Q = U_0 + (\alpha,\, h_H)$, so $|UQ| = \sqrt{\alpha^2 + h_H^2}$ (constant) an
d $|VQ| = \sqrt{(\alpha - L)^2 + h_H^2}$ (constant).

As $p$ varies, the entire triangle $QUV$ undergoes a **pure translation** along
$BC$. All side lengths and angles are invariant.

**Locus of the incenter.** The incenter $I$ of triangle $QUV$ (with $|UV| = L$ o
pposite $Q$, $|QV|$ opposite $U$, $|QU|$ opposite $V$, all constant) is:
$$I = \frac{L \cdot Q + |QV| \cdot U + |QU| \cdot V}{L + |QV| + |QU|}.$$
Substituting $Q = U_0 + (\alpha, h_H)$, $U = U_0$, $V = U_0 + (L, 0)$:
$$I = U_0 + \frac{(L\alpha + |QU|\cdot L,\; L\cdot h_H)}{L + |QV| + |QU|} = U_0
+ \text{(fixed offset)}.$$
Therefore:
- The $x$-coordinate of $I$ is $\frac{pD}{b^2} + \text{const}$, varying linearly
 with $p$.
- The $y$-coordinate of $I$ is $r = \frac{L \cdot h_H}{L + |QV| + |QU|}$, a **co
nstant** (the inradius of the congruent triangle $QUV$).

**Conclusion.** As $P$ ranges over line $BC$, the incenter of triangle $QUV$ tra
ces a **line parallel to $BC$**, situated at constant distance $r$ from $BC$ on
the same side as $A$. $\blacksquare$
