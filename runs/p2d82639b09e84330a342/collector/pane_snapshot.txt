## Solution

**Claim:** The set of all obtuse triangles in the Cartesian plane has cardinalit
y equal to the continuum $\mathfrak{c} = 2^{\aleph_0}$.

### Upper bound

A triangle in the Cartesian plane is determined by three points (its vertices) i
n $\mathbb{R}^2$. Thus the set of all triangles is a subset of $(\mathbb{R}^2)^3
 \cong \mathbb{R}^6$. Since $|\mathbb{R}^6| = \mathfrak{c}$, the set of all obtu
se triangles has cardinality at most $\mathfrak{c}$.

### Lower bound

It suffices to exhibit a family of obtuse triangles parametrized by a set of car
dinality $\mathfrak{c}$.

For each $t \in (-\infty, 0)$, consider the triangle with vertices
$$A = (0,0), \quad B = (1,0), \quad C = (t,\, 1).$$

**Non-degeneracy:** Since $C$ has $y$-coordinate $1 \neq 0$, the three points ar
e not collinear, so this is a genuine triangle for every $t$.

**Obtuseness at $A$:** The angle at $A$ is determined by the vectors
$$\overrightarrow{AB} = (1, 0), \qquad \overrightarrow{AC} = (t, 1).$$
Their dot product is
$$\overrightarrow{AB} \cdot \overrightarrow{AC} = 1 \cdot t + 0 \cdot 1 = t.$$
The angle at $A$ is obtuse precisely when this dot product is negative, i.e., wh
en $t < 0$.

Since we chose $t \in (-\infty, 0)$, every triangle in this family is obtuse (wi
th the obtuse angle at $A$).

**Distinctness:** Distinct values of $t$ give distinct vertices $C = (t,1)$, hen
ce distinct triangles.

The parametrizing set $(-\infty, 0)$ has cardinality $\mathfrak{c}$, so this fam
ily alone provides $\mathfrak{c}$ distinct obtuse triangles.

### Conclusion

The set of obtuse triangles has cardinality $\leq \mathfrak{c}$ (upper bound) an
d $\geq \mathfrak{c}$ (lower bound). Therefore:

$$\boxed{|\{\text{obtuse triangles in } \mathbb{R}^2\}| = \mathfrak{c} = 2^{\ale
ph_0}.}$$
