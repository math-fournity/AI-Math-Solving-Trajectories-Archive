## Solution

Define
$$f_n(x) = \int_x^{2x} e^{-t^n}\,dt,\qquad x\ge 0.$$

We seek $a_n = \arg\max_{x\ge 0} f_n(x)$, then $\lim_{n\to\infty}\ln a_n$.

### Step 1. Differentiate (Leibniz rule)

$$f_n'(x) = 2\,e^{-(2x)^n} - e^{-x^n}.$$

### Step 2. Locate the critical point

Set $f_n'(x)=0$:
$$2\,e^{-(2x)^n} = e^{-x^n}
\iff 2 = e^{(2x)^n - x^n}
\iff \ln 2 = (2x)^n - x^n = x^n(2^n-1).$$

Hence the unique critical point is
$$a_n = \left(\frac{\ln 2}{\,2^n-1\,}\right)^{1/n}.$$

### Step 3. Confirm it is a maximum

Factor
$$f_n'(x) = e^{-x^n}\Bigl(2\,e^{-x^n(2^n-1)}-1\Bigr).$$

The sign is governed by $x^n(2^n-1)$ versus $\ln 2$:
- at $x=0$: $f_n'(0)=2-1=1>0$;
- the derivative is positive for $x^n(2^n-1)<\ln 2$ and negative for $x^n(2^n-1)
>\ln 2$.

So $f_n$ increases then decreases, with a single peak at $a_n$. Also $f_n(0)=0$
and $f_n(x)\to 0$ as $x\to\infty$ (the integrand decays super-exponentially), co
nfirming $a_n$ is the global maximizer.

### Step 4. Compute the limit

$$\ln a_n = \frac{1}{n}\ln\!\left(\frac{\ln 2}{2^n-1}\right) = \frac{\ln(\ln 2)
- \ln(2^n-1)}{n}.$$

Since $2^n-1 \sim 2^n$ as $n\to\infty$,
$$\ln(2^n-1) = n\ln 2 + \ln\!\left(1-2^{-n}\right) = n\ln 2 + o(1).$$

Therefore
$$\ln a_n = \frac{\ln(\ln 2) - n\ln 2 + o(1)}{n} = -\ln 2 + \frac{\ln(\ln 2)+o(1
)}{n}\;\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\; -\ln 2.$$

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\ln a_n = -\ln 2.}$$
