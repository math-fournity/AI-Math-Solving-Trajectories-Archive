**Answer: No.** We construct a simple, locally finite, acyclic graph (a tree) on
 which the simple random walk is transient (so it returns to its start with prob
ability $<1$) yet has zero speed, $\limsup_{n\to\infty} d(v_0,X_n)/n = 0$ a.s.

---

### The counterexample

Let $T$ be the spherically symmetric tree rooted at $v_0$ defined as follows. Th
e vertex set is partitioned into levels $L_0,L_1,L_2,\dots$ with $L_0=\{v_0\}$.
Every vertex in $L_k$ has $a_k$ children in $L_{k+1}$, where
$$
a_0 = 1, \qquad a_k = \begin{cases} 2 & \text{if } k = m^2 \text{ for some } m\i
n\mathbb{N},\\ 1 & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases} \quad (k\ge 1).
$$
So degrees are $1$ (root), $2$ (at non-square positive levels), or $3$ (at squar
e levels). The graph is a tree: simple, connected, acyclic, and locally finite.

Let $b_k = |L_k| = \prod_{i=0}^{k-1} a_i$. Since $a_i=2$ exactly when $i$ is a p
ositive perfect square,
$$
b_k = 2^{\,\lfloor \sqrt{k-1}\rfloor}, \qquad k\ge 1 \quad (\text{and } b_0=1).
$$

---

### The walk is transient

For a spherically symmetric tree, the simple random walk is transient iff the ef
fective resistance from the root to infinity is finite, i.e. iff
$$
R \;=\; \sum_{k\ge 1} \frac{1}{b_k} \;<\; \infty
$$
(each layer $L_{k-1}\!-\!L_k$ contributes resistance $1/b_k$, as $b_k$ unit resi
stors in parallel). Here
$$
R = \sum_{k\ge 1} 2^{-\lfloor\sqrt{k-1}\rfloor} \;\le\; 2\sum_{k\ge 2} 2^{-\sqrt
{k-1}} \;=\; 2\sum_{m\ge 1} 2^{-\sqrt{m}}.
$$
The last series converges by the integral test ($\int_1^\infty 2^{-\sqrt{x}}\,dx
 = \int_1^\infty 2u\,2^{-u}\,du <\infty$). Hence $R<\infty$, the walk is **trans
ient**, and
$$
\mathbb{P}\big(\exists\, t>0 : X_t = v_0\big) \;<\; 1.
$$

---

### The walk has zero speed

On a tree, $h_n := d(v_0,X_n)$ equals the level of $X_n$. The process $(h_n)$ is
 a birth–death chain on $\mathbb{N}_0$ with
$$
p_k = \mathbb{P}(k\to k{+}1)=\frac{a_k}{a_k+1}, \qquad q_k = \mathbb{P}(k\to k{-
}1)=\frac{1}{a_k+1}\quad (k\ge 1),\qquad p_0=1.
$$
Since the chain is transient to $+\infty$, $h_n\to\infty$ a.s. and every level i
s hit. Let $\tau_n = \inf\{t : h_t = n\}$. A standard result for transient birth
–death chains (justified by the strong Markov property plus Kolmogorov's SLLN fo
r the independent increments $\tau_{k+1}-\tau_k$, whose variances are $O((\mathb
b{E}_k\tau_{k+1})^2)$) gives
$$
\frac{h_n}{n} \;\xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}}\; \ell \;=\; \Big(\lim_{n\to\infty}\fr
ac{\mathbb{E}_0\tau_n}{n}\Big)^{-1} \;\in\; [0,1].
$$
(As a sanity check: for the binary tree, $a_k\equiv 2$, this gives $\ell = 1/3$.
)

It remains to compute $\mathbb{E}_0\tau_n$. By the strong Markov property,
$$
\mathbb{E}_0\tau_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \mathbb{E}_k\tau_{k+1},
$$
and the standard birth–death hitting-time formula yields
$$
\mathbb{E}_k\tau_{k+1} = \frac{1}{b_{k+1}}\sum_{j=0}^{k} \frac{1}{p_j}\, b_{j+1}
,
\qquad\text{since } \prod_{i=j+1}^{k}\frac{q_i}{p_i}=\prod_{i=j+1}^{k}\frac{1}{a
_i}=\frac{b_{j+1}}{b_{k+1}}.
$$
Here $1/p_j = (a_j+1)/a_j \in \{2,\tfrac32\} = \Theta(1)$, so it suffices to est
imate $\sum_{j=0}^{k} b_{j+1}$. Grouping by the value $m = \lfloor\sqrt{i-1}\rfl
oor$ (there are $2m+1$ indices $i$ with this value, each contributing $2^m$):
$$
\sum_{i=1}^{k+1} b_i = \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} 2^{\lfloor\sqrt{i-1}\rfloor} = \sum_{m=0
}^{\lfloor\sqrt{k}\rfloor} (2m+1)\,2^{m} \;+\; O(\sqrt{k}\,2^{\sqrt{k}}).
$$
The last term dominates a geometrically growing sum, so $\sum_{m=0}^{M}(2m+1)2^m
 = \Theta(M\,2^M)$. With $M=\lfloor\sqrt{k}\rfloor$ and $b_{k+1}=2^{\lfloor\sqrt
{k}\rfloor}$,
$$
\sum_{j=0}^{k} b_{j+1} = \Theta\!\big(\sqrt{k}\,b_{k+1}\big), \qquad\text{hence}
\qquad \mathbb{E}_k\tau_{k+1} = \Theta(\sqrt{k}).
$$
Therefore
$$
\mathbb{E}_0\tau_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Theta(\sqrt{k}) = \Theta\!\big(n^{3/2}\big
), \qquad \frac{\mathbb{E}_0\tau_n}{n} = \Theta(\sqrt{n}) \;\longrightarrow\; \i
nfty.
$$
So the speed is $\ell = 0$; in particular
$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{d(v_0,X_n)}{n} = \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h_n}{n} = 0 \q
uad\text{a.s.}
$$

### Conclusion

The tree $T$ is simple, locally finite, and acyclic. The simple random walk on $
T$ satisfies $\limsup_{n\to\infty} d(v_0,X_n)/n = 0$ almost surely, yet the walk
 is transient, so $\mathbb{P}(\exists\, t>0 : X_t = v_0) < 1$. The proposed impl
ication **fails**.
