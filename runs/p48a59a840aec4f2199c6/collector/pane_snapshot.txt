## Proof

**Answer: Yes, such a set exists.**

We construct the **Smith–Volterra–Cantor (SVC) set** and verify it has all three
 required properties.

### Construction

Start with the closed interval $C_0 = [0,1]$.

**Step 1.** Remove from $C_0$ the open middle interval of length $\tfrac{1}{4}$,
 namely $\left(\tfrac{3}{8},\tfrac{5}{8}\right)$. The remainder $C_1$ is the uni
on of $2$ closed intervals with total length $1 - \tfrac{1}{4} = \tfrac{3}{4}$.

**Step $n$ (inductively).** From each of the $2^{n-1}$ closed intervals comprisi
ng $C_{n-1}$, remove the open middle sub-interval of length $\tfrac{1}{4^n}$. Th
is removes $2^{n-1}$ open intervals, total length removed at step $n$ being
$$2^{n-1}\cdot\frac{1}{4^n} = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n} \cdot
 2 = \frac{2^{n-1}}{4^n}.$$

The remainder $C_n$ is a union of $2^n$ closed intervals with total length
$$|C_n| = 1 - \sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2^{k-1}}{4^k}.$$

Define the SVC set
$$S = \bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty} C_n.$$

### Property 1: $S$ is Lebesgue measurable

Each $C_n$ is a finite union of closed intervals, hence closed (and Borel measur
able). $S$ is a countable intersection of closed sets, hence closed, hence Borel
, hence Lebesgue measurable.

### Property 2: $S$ has positive measure

By continuity from above (the $C_n$ are nested and $C_0$ has finite measure),
$$|S| = \lim_{n\to\infty}|C_n| = 1 - \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{k-1}}{4^k}.$$

Compute the series:
$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{k-1}}{4^k} = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2}\cdot\f
rac{2^k}{4^k} = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^k = \frac
{1}{2}\cdot 1 = \frac{1}{2}.$$

Therefore
$$|S| = 1 - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2} > 0.$$

### Property 3: $S$ is totally disconnected

We show $S$ contains no non-degenerate interval. Since $S\subseteq\mathbb{R}$ an
d the connected subsets of $\mathbb{R}$ are exactly the intervals, this is equiv
alent to $S$ being totally disconnected.

It suffices to show $S$ has empty interior (since $S$ is closed, empty interior
$=$ nowhere dense $=$ contains no non-degenerate interval).

**Claim:** $\operatorname{int}(S)=\varnothing$.

Let $(a,b)$ be any non-empty open interval with $(a,b)\subseteq[0,1]$. We show $
(a,b)\not\subseteq S$. At each step $n$, every component interval of $C_{n-1}$ h
as its open middle removed. The maximum length of a component interval of $C_n$
tends to $0$ as $n\to\infty$ (each step at least halves every interval's length,
 since we remove a positive open middle from each). Hence for $n$ large enough,
every component of $C_n$ has length $< (b-a)$. Then $(a,b)$ cannot be contained
in a single component of $C_n$, so it must meet one of the removed open gaps, i.
e. $(a,b)\cap([0,1]\setminus C_n)\neq\varnothing$. Since $[0,1]\setminus C_n\sub
seteq[0,1]\setminus S$, we get $(a,b)\not\subseteq S$.

Thus no non-empty open interval lies in $S$; $\operatorname{int}(S)=\varnothing$
. Being closed with empty interior, $S$ contains no non-degenerate interval, so
every connected component of $S$ is a single point. Hence $S$ is totally disconn
ected.

### Conclusion

The Smith–Volterra–Cantor set $S$ is:
- Lebesgue measurable (it is closed),
- of Lebesgue measure $\tfrac{1}{2}>0$,
- totally disconnected (it contains no interval).

Therefore, **yes**, there exists a totally disconnected set of real numbers that
 is Lebesgue measurable and has positive measure. $\blacksquare$
