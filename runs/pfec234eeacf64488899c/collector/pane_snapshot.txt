**Claim.** The series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_n}{S_n^c}$ converges for every
 sequence $(a_n)$ with $a_n>0$ and $S_n\to\infty$ **if and only if** $c>1$. Henc
e the critical (threshold) value is $\boxed{c=1}$: convergence holds for every $
c>1$ and fails for every $c\le 1$.

---

### Part 1. For $c>1$, the series always converges.

Write $a_n = S_n - S_{n-1}$ (with $S_0=0$). For $n\ge 2$ we have $S_{n-1}>0$. Ap
ply the mean value theorem to $f(x)=x^{1-c}$ (which is decreasing since $1-c<0$)
 on $[S_{n-1},S_n]$: there exists $\xi_n\in(S_{n-1},S_n)$ with

$$S_{n-1}^{\,1-c}-S_n^{\,1-c} = (c-1)\,\xi_n^{-c}(S_n-S_{n-1}) = (c-1)\,\xi_n^{-
c}\,a_n.$$

Since $\xi_n \le S_n$ and $c>0$, we have $\xi_n^{-c}\ge S_n^{-c}$, giving

$$\frac{a_n}{S_n^c}\;\le\;\frac{1}{c-1}\bigl(S_{n-1}^{\,1-c}-S_n^{\,1-c}\bigr),\
qquad n\ge 2.$$

Summing from $n=2$ to $N$ (telescoping):

$$\sum_{n=2}^{N}\frac{a_n}{S_n^c}\;\le\;\frac{1}{c-1}\bigl(S_1^{\,1-c}-S_N^{\,1-
c}\bigr).$$

As $N\to\infty$, $S_N\to\infty$ and $1-c<0$ so $S_N^{\,1-c}\to 0$. Therefore

$$\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{a_n}{S_n^c}\;\le\;\frac{S_1^{\,1-c}}{c-1}<\infty.$$

Adding the finite first term $\frac{a_1}{S_1^c}$, the full series converges. ∎

---

### Part 2. For $c=1$, the series always diverges.

We have $\frac{a_n}{S_n}=1-\frac{S_{n-1}}{S_n}$, with $0<\frac{S_{n-1}}{S_n}<1$.
 Set $x_n=\frac{S_{n-1}}{S_n}$. Then

$$\prod_{n=2}^{N}x_n=\prod_{n=2}^{N}\frac{S_{n-1}}{S_n}=\frac{S_1}{S_N}\xrightar
row[N\to\infty]{}0.$$

Since $\log x_n\le x_n-1=-(1-x_n)$ for $x_n\in(0,1)$,

$$\sum_{n=2}^{N}(1-x_n)\;\ge\;-\sum_{n=2}^{N}\log x_n=-\log\frac{S_1}{S_N}=\log\
frac{S_N}{S_1}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}\infty.$$

Thus $\sum\frac{a_n}{S_n}=\sum(1-x_n)=\infty$ for **every** admissible sequence.
 ∎

---

### Part 3. For $c<1$, the series always diverges.

Since $S_n\to\infty$, eventually $S_n\ge 1$. For such $n$ and $c<1$, we have $S_
n^c\le S_n$, hence

$$\frac{a_n}{S_n^c}\;\ge\;\frac{a_n}{S_n}.$$

By Part 2, $\sum\frac{a_n}{S_n}$ diverges, so by comparison $\sum\frac{a_n}{S_n^
c}$ diverges. ∎

---

### Conclusion

- $c>1$: converges for **all** admissible sequences (Part 1).
- $c=1$: diverges for **all** admissible sequences (Part 2).
- $c<1$: diverges for **all** admissible sequences (Part 3).

Therefore the series converges (for every valid $(a_n)$) if and only if $c>1$. T
he critical threshold — the smallest value of $c$ at and beyond which convergenc
e is guaranteed (in the sense of the infimum of the convergence region) — is

$$c=1.$$
