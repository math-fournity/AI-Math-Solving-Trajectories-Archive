# Solution

**Claim:** No such polyhedron exists.

**Proof:**

Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that there exists a polyhedron whose sur
face is formed by gluing together 43 equal non-planar 47-gons.

Let $F$, $E$, and $V$ denote the number of faces, edges, and vertices of this po
lyhedron, respectively. By hypothesis, $F = 43$, and each face is a 47-gon (plan
ar or not — this is irrelevant to the counting argument).

**Key observation (edge-pairing / handshaking for faces):** In any polyhedron, e
very edge is shared by exactly two faces. Equivalently, when we glue polygons to
gether to form a closed polyhedral surface, every edge of every polygon must be
paired (identified) with exactly one edge of some polygon. This means the total
number of polygon-edges, counted with multiplicity over all faces, must be even
— it equals $2E$.

**Counting incidences two ways:**

- *Face side:* Each of the 43 faces contributes 47 edges, giving a total of
$$43 \times 47 = 2021$$
face-edge incidences.

- *Edge side:* Each of the $E$ edges of the polyhedron is incident to exactly 2
faces, giving a total of $2E$ face-edge incidences.

Setting these equal:
$$2E = 2021.$$

**Contradiction:** Since $2021 = 43 \times 47$ is odd, $E = 2021/2$ is not an in
teger. But $E$ — the number of edges of a polyhedron — must be a positive intege
r. This is impossible.

**Remark on the "non-planar" hypothesis:** The argument uses only the combinator
ial structure of the gluing (each edge shared by exactly two faces) and is compl
etely independent of whether the 47-gons are planar or non-planar. The parity ob
struction $43 \times 47 \equiv 1 \pmod{2}$ rules out any closed polyhedral surfa
ce formed from 43 polygons each having an odd number (47) of edges, since the pr
oduct of two odd numbers is odd and cannot equal $2E$.

Therefore, no polyhedron can be formed by gluing together 43 equal non-planar 47
-gons. $\blacksquare$
