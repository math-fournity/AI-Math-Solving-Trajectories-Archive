## Answer

**Yes**, $\{f_n\}$ converges to $f$ in the $L^1$ norm.

## Proof

Let $\|g\|_1 = \int_X \|g(x)\|_E\,d\mu$ denote the $L^1$ seminorm for $E$-valued
 functions.

**Step 1: Completeness gives an $L^1$-limit.** The step functions $f_n$ belong t
o the Bochner space $L^1(X;E)$. Since $E$ is a Banach space (complete), $L^1(X;E
)$ is complete in the $L^1$ seminorm. (This is the standard Bochner integral com
pleteness theorem: a.e.-limits of Cauchy sequences of simple functions with $\|\
cdot\|_1$-Cauchy property converge in $L^1$ to a Bochner-integrable limit, using
 completeness of $E$ to extract a.e. limits.) Since $\{f_n\}$ is Cauchy in $\|\c
dot\|_1$, there exists $g \in L^1(X;E)$ such that
$$\|f_n - g\|_1 \to 0.$$

**Step 2: A subsequence converges a.e. to $g$.** Since $f_n \to g$ in $L^1$, cho
ose a subsequence $\{f_{n_k}\}$ with $\sum_{k=1}^\infty \|f_{n_{k+1}} - f_{n_k}\
|_1 < \infty$. By Tonelli's theorem (applied to the nonnegative scalar function
$\sum_k \|f_{n_{k+1}}(x) - f_{n_k}(x)\|_E$),
$$\int_X \sum_{k=1}^\infty \|f_{n_{k+1}}(x) - f_{n_k}(x)\|_E\,d\mu = \sum_{k=1}^
\infty \|f_{n_{k+1}} - f_{n_k}\|_1 < \infty.$$
Hence $\sum_{k} \|f_{n_{k+1}}(x) - f_{n_k}(x)\|_E < \infty$ for $\mu$-a.e. $x$,
so $\{f_{n_k}(x)\}$ is Cauchy in $E$ for a.e. $x$. Since $E$ is complete, $f_{n_
k}(x)$ converges for a.e. $x$. The limit must agree with $g(x)$ a.e. (since $L^1
$-convergence implies convergence in measure, and a.e.-convergence also implies
convergence in measure, giving uniqueness of the limit in measure). Thus
$$f_{n_k} \to g \quad \mu\text{-a.e.}$$

**Step 3: Identify $f = g$ a.e.** By hypothesis, $f_n \to f$ $\mu$-a.e., so the
subsequence satisfies $f_{n_k} \to f$ $\mu$-a.e. as well. A.e. limits are unique
 (up to null sets), so $f = g$ $\mu$-a.e.

**Step 4: Conclude.** Since $f = g$ a.e. and $g \in L^1(X;E)$, we have $f \in L^
1(X;E)$ and
$$\|f_n - f\|_1 = \|f_n - g\|_1 \to 0.$$

Therefore $\{f_n\}$ converges to $f$ in the $L^1$ norm. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** Completeness of $E$ is essential: it is used both for completeness o
f $L^1(X;E)$ and for extracting a.e.-convergent subsequences from $L^1$-converge
nt sequences. Without $E$ being Banach, the argument fails.
