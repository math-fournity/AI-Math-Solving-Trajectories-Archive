## Proof

Rewrite the event. Let
$$Y_i := 2X_i^2 - X_i = X_i(2X_i-1).$$
Then
$$\sum_{i=1}^n X_i \le 2\sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2 \iff \sum_{i=1}^n (2X_i^2-X_i)\ge 0 \
iff \sum_{i=1}^n Y_i \ge 0.$$
So the desired limit is $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty} P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^n Y_
i \ge 0\right)$.

Compute the mean of $Y_i$. Since $X_i\sim U(0,1)$,
$$E[X_i]=\int_0^1 x\,dx=\frac12,\qquad E[X_i^2]=\int_0^1 x^2\,dx=\frac13.$$
Hence
$$E[Y_i]=2E[X_i^2]-E[X_i]=2\cdot\frac13-\frac12=\frac23-\frac12=\frac16>0.$$

The $Y_i$ are i.i.d. (as measurable functions of the i.i.d. $X_i$) and integrabl
e, so by the **Strong Law of Large Numbers**,
$$\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} E[Y_i]=\frac16.$$
Almost-sure convergence implies convergence in probability, so
$$\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i \xrightarrow{P} \frac16.$$

Since $\frac16>0$, pick $\varepsilon=\frac16>0$ (or any $\varepsilon\in(0,\frac1
6)$). Then
$$P\!\left(\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i \ge 0\right) \ge P\!\left(\left|\frac1n\sum_{
i=1}^n Y_i - \frac16\right| < \frac16\right) \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 1.$$
Also trivially $P\!\left(\frac1n\sum Y_i \ge 0\right)\le 1$. Therefore, by the s
queeze theorem,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i \ge 0\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty} P\!
\left(\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i \ge 0\right)=1.$$

Thus
$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i \le 2 \sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2\
right)=1.}$$
