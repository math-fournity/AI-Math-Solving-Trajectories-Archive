### Proof

Let $F = \mathbb{F}_q$ be a finite field. Recall that $F$ is a perfect field (ev
ery irreducible polynomial over $F$ is separable), since finite fields are perfe
ct.

**Definition.** A matrix $A \in M_n(F)$ is *semisimple* if its minimal polynomia
l $m_A(x)$ is squarefree (equivalently, $A$ is diagonalizable over the algebraic
 closure $\bar{F}$).

**Key fact (Rational Canonical Form).** Every matrix is similar to a unique rati
onal canonical form, determined by its multiset of *elementary divisors* — these
 are prime powers $p(x)^e$ where $p$ is irreducible over $F$. Two matrices are s
imilar if and only if they have the same multiset of elementary divisors.

The invariant factors $f_1 \mid f_2 \mid \cdots \mid f_k$ satisfy $f_k = m_A(x)$
 (the minimal polynomial) and $\chi_A(x) = f_1 f_2 \cdots f_k$ (the characterist
ic polynomial). Each elementary divisor is a prime-power factor of some $f_i$.

**Semisimplicity constrains the elementary divisors.** Since $A$ is semisimple,
$m_A(x) = f_k$ is squarefree. Because $f_i \mid f_k$ for all $i$, every invarian
t factor $f_i$ is also squarefree. Therefore every elementary divisor of $A$ is
an irreducible polynomial $p(x)$ raised to the **first** power only.

**The characteristic polynomial determines the elementary divisors.** The charac
teristic polynomial is the product of all elementary divisors:
$$\chi_A(x) = \prod_j p_j(x).$$
Since each elementary divisor is an irreducible polynomial to the first power, f
actoring $\chi_A(x)$ into irreducibles over $F$,
$$\chi_A(x) = \prod_{i} p_i(x)^{m_i},$$
directly reveals the multiset of elementary divisors: the irreducible $p_i(x)$ a
ppears exactly $m_i$ times as an elementary divisor. Thus the characteristic pol
ynomial **uniquely determines** the multiset of elementary divisors.

**Conclusion.** If $A$ and $B$ are both semisimple over $F$ and $\chi_A(x) = \ch
i_B(x)$, then the factorization into irreducibles is the same, so $A$ and $B$ ha
ve the same multiset of elementary divisors, hence the same rational canonical f
orm, hence are similar. $\blacksquare$
