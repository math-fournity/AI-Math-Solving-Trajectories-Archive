## Proof

We claim that the entire functions $f$ with $|f|$ harmonic are exactly the **con
stant functions**.

---

**Sufficiency.** If $f \equiv c$ is constant, then $|f| \equiv |c|$ is constant,
 hence harmonic. In particular $f \equiv 0$ works.

---

**Necessity.** Let $f$ be entire with $|f|$ harmonic on $\mathbb{C}$. We show $f
$ is constant.

If $f \equiv 0$ we are done, so assume $f \not\equiv 0$. Then the zeros of $f$ a
re isolated, and the set
$$\Omega := \{z \in \mathbb{C} : f(z) \neq 0\} = \mathbb{C} \setminus \{\text{is
olated points}\}$$
is open and **connected** (the plane minus a discrete set is path-connected).

On $\Omega$, $|f| > 0$ and, by hypothesis, $|f|$ is harmonic. Since $f$ is holom
orphic and nonzero on $\Omega$, the function $\log f$ is locally well defined (h
olomorphically) on $\Omega$, and
$$\log|f| = \operatorname{Re}(\log f)$$
is harmonic on $\Omega$.

Now write $|f| = e^{g}$ where $g := \log|f|$ is harmonic on $\Omega$. For a real
-valued $C^2$ function $g$,
$$\Delta(e^{g}) = e^{g}\bigl(\Delta g + |\nabla g|^{2}\bigr).$$
Since $g$ is harmonic, $\Delta g = 0$, and since $|f| = e^{g}$ is harmonic, $\De
lta(e^{g}) = 0$. Thus
$$0 = \Delta|f| = |f|\,|\nabla g|^{2} \quad \text{on } \Omega.$$
Because $|f| > 0$ on $\Omega$, we obtain $|\nabla g|^{2} = 0$ on $\Omega$, i.e.
$g \equiv \log|f|$ is **constant** on $\Omega$. Hence $|f|$ is constant on $\Ome
ga$.

By continuity, $|f|$ is constant on all of $\mathbb{C}$ (the isolated zeros are
limit points of $\Omega$). A holomorphic function with constant modulus is const
ant: indeed, if $|f| \equiv c$ with $c > 0$, then $f$ maps $\mathbb{C}$ into the
 circle $\{|w| = c\}$, which has empty interior, contradicting the open mapping
theorem unless $f$ is constant (equivalently, $f$ is bounded and Liouville's the
orem applies). Therefore $f$ is constant.

---

**Conclusion.** The entire functions $f$ for which $|f|$ is harmonic are precise
ly the constant functions $f(z) \equiv c$, $c \in \mathbb{C}$.
