## Proof

**Construction.** For each $n \ge 1$, define
$$a_n = \frac{k_n}{10^n}, \qquad k_n = \max\{\,k \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0} : k^2 \l
e 2\cdot 10^{2n}\,\}.$$
The set on the right is finite and nonempty (it contains $k=0$ and is bounded ab
ove, e.g. by $2\cdot 10^n$ since $(2\cdot 10^n)^2 = 4\cdot 10^{2n} > 2\cdot 10^{
2n}$), so the maximum exists. Each $a_n$ is a nonnegative rational number.

**Boundedness.** Since $k_n^2 \le 2\cdot 10^{2n}$, we have $a_n^2 \le 2$, hence
$0 \le a_n \le \sqrt{2} < 2$. So $(a_n)$ is a bounded sequence in $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Convergence to $\sqrt{2}$.** By maximality of $k_n$, we have $(k_n+1)^2 > 2\cd
ot 10^{2n}$, i.e.
$$a_n^2 \le 2 < \left(a_n + \tfrac{1}{10^n}\right)^2.$$
The left inequality gives $a_n \le \sqrt{2}$. The right inequality gives $\sqrt{
2} < a_n + 10^{-n}$, i.e. $\sqrt{2} - 10^{-n} < a_n$. Combining,
$$\sqrt{2} - 10^{-n} < a_n \le \sqrt{2}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad |a_n - \sqrt{2}|
< 10^{-n} \to 0.$$
Thus $a_n \to \sqrt{2}$ in $\mathbb{R}$.

**No subsequence converges in $\mathbb{Q}$.** Let $(a_{n_j})$ be any subsequence
. Since the full sequence converges to $\sqrt{2}$, every subsequence also conver
ges to $\sqrt{2}$. Therefore the only possible limit of any subsequence is $\sqr
t{2}$.

It remains to note that $\sqrt{2}\notin\mathbb{Q}$. Indeed, suppose $\sqrt{2}=p/
q$ with $p,q\in\mathbb{Z}$, $q\neq 0$, and $\gcd(p,q)=1$. Then $p^2=2q^2$, so $2
\mid p$; write $p=2r$. Then $4r^2=2q^2$, so $q^2=2r^2$, giving $2\mid q$, contra
dicting $\gcd(p,q)=1$. Hence $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational.

Since every subsequence of $(a_n)$ converges to $\sqrt{2}\notin\mathbb{Q}$, no s
ubsequence converges to a point of $\mathbb{Q}$.
