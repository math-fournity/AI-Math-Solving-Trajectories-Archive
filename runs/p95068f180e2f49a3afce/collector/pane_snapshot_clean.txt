## Proof

Place the square in coordinates with center $O$ at the origin and side length $2
$:
$$A=(-1,1),\quad B=(1,1),\quad C=(1,-1),\quad D=(-1,-1).$$

**Locating $J$.** Triangle $BCJ$ is isosceles with base $BC$ and is built outsid
e the square. The segment $BC$ is vertical ($x=1$), so its perpendicular bisecto
r is the horizontal line $y=0$. Being outside the square, the apex lies to the r

$$J=(1+t,\,0),\qquad t>0.$$

**Locating $K$.** Triangle $CDK$ is isosceles with base $CD$ and is built outsid
e the square. The segment $CD$ is horizontal ($y=-1$), so its perpendicular bise
ctor is the vertical line $x=0$. Being outside the square, the apex lies below:
$$K=(0,\,-1-t),$$
where we used that $BCJ\cong CDK$ (congruent, with equal bases $BC=CD=2$) forces
 the apex heights to be equal, both equal to $t$.

**Computing $M$.** Since $M$ is the midpoint of $CJ$ with $C=(1,-1)$ and $J=(1+t
,0)$:
$$M=\left(\frac{1+(1+t)}{2},\,\frac{-1+0}{2}\right)=\left(1+\tfrac{t}{2},\,-\tfr

**Direction vectors.**
$$\overrightarrow{OM}=M-O=\left(1+\tfrac{t}{2},\,-\tfrac{1}{2}\right),$$
$$\overrightarrow{BK}=K-B=(0-1,\,-1-t-1)=(-1,\,-2-t).$$

**Dot product.**
$$\overrightarrow{OM}\cdot\overrightarrow{BK}=\left(1+\tfrac{t}{2}\right)(-1)+\l

$$=-1-\tfrac{t}{2}+\tfrac{2+t}{2}=-1-\tfrac{t}{2}+1+\tfrac{t}{2}=0.$$

Since both $\overrightarrow{OM}$ and $\overrightarrow{BK}$ are nonzero (as $t>0$
) and their dot product vanishes, the two lines $OM$ and $BK$ are perpendicular.
