## Solution

The key observation is a direct consequence of the definition of $\tau$.

**Definition of $\tau$.** $\tau = \inf\{n \ge 0 : S_n = b \text{ or } S_n = -a\}
$, the first hitting time of the set $\{b, -a\}$.

**The conditioning event.** "The walk reaches $b$ before $-a$" is precisely the
event $\{S_\tau = b\}$, since at the stopping time $\tau$ the walk is exactly at
 one of the two absorbing barriers $b$ or $-a$, and reaching $b$ first means $S_
\tau = b$.

**Determinism on the conditioning event.** On the event $\{S_\tau = b\}$, the va
lue of $S_\tau$ is fixed: $S_\tau = b$ with probability 1 (conditional on $\{S_\
tau = b\}$). Therefore $S_\tau^3 = b^3$ is deterministic on this event, regardle
ss of the path taken to get there, the value of $a$, or the probability of reach
ing $b$ first.

**Computation.**
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[S_\tau^3 \,\middle|\, S_\tau = b\right] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[
b^3 \,\middle|\, S_\tau = b\right] = b^3.$$

The expected value is

$$\boxed{b^3}$$
