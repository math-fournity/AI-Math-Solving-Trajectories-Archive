## Solution

**Interpretation.** The expression is the (generalized) continued fraction in wh
ich the digit $2$ appears $2013$ times as the partial quotient, with subtraction
 at each level:

$$E_n \;=\; 2-\cfrac{1}{\,2-\cfrac{1}{\,2-\cfrac{1}{\,\ddots-\cfrac{1}{\,2\,}\,}
\,}\,},$$

where the digit $2$ appears $n$ times (here $n=2013$). Equivalently, $E_1=2$ and
 for $n\ge 2$,

$$E_n \;=\; 2-\frac{1}{E_{n-1}}.$$

**Claim.** $\displaystyle E_n=\frac{n+1}{n}$ for every $n\ge 1$.

**Proof by induction.**

*Base case.* $E_1=2=\dfrac{2}{1}=\dfrac{1+1}{1}$. ✓

*Inductive step.* Suppose $E_{n-1}=\dfrac{n}{n-1}$ for some $n\ge 2$. Then

$$E_n \;=\; 2-\frac{1}{E_{n-1}} \;=\; 2-\frac{1}{\,n/(n-1)\,} \;=\; 2-\frac{n-1}
{n} \;=\; \frac{2n-(n-1)}{n} \;=\; \frac{n+1}{n}.$$

This closes the induction. ∎

**Applying to $n=2013$.**

$$E_{2013} \;=\; \frac{2013+1}{2013} \;=\; \frac{2014}{2013}.$$

**Irreducibility.** $\gcd(2014,2013)=\gcd(2013,1)=1$, so $\dfrac{2014}{2013}$ is
 already in lowest terms. Hence $a=2014$ and $b=2013$.

**Final computation.**

$$a+b \;=\; 2014+2013 \;=\; \boxed{4027}.$$
