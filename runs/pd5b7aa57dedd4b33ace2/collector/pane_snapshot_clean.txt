## Proof

Let $X$ be a compact Riemann surface of genus $g$. We determine $\deg(K_X^{-1})$
.

**Step 1: Degree of the canonical bundle.**

For a compact Riemann surface of genus $g$, the canonical line bundle $K_X$ is t
he bundle of holomorphic 1-forms. Its global sections are the holomorphic differ
entials, and by the Riemann–Roch theorem (or equivalently by the Gauss–Bonnet /
degree-of-canonical formula),

$$\deg(K_X) = 2g - 2.$$

A quick derivation: a nonvanishing meromorphic section of $K_X$ is given by any
meromorphic 1-form $\omega$. The degree of $K_X$ equals $\deg(\operatorname{div}
(\omega))$, the degree of the divisor of $\omega$. Writing $\omega = df/f \cdot
\eta$ for a meromorphic function $f$ and a reference 1-form $\eta$, or more dire
ctly: on $\mathbb{P}^1$ ($g=0$) the form $dz$ has a double pole at infinity, giv
ing $\deg K = -2 = 2(0)-2$. In general, by Riemann–Roch applied with $D = K_X$,

$$h^0(K_X) - h^0(\mathcal{O}) = \deg(K_X) - g + 1,$$

and using $h^0(K_X) = g$ (the dimension of the space of holomorphic 1-forms) and
 $h^0(\mathcal{O}) = 1$,

$$g - 1 = \deg(K_X) - g + 1 \implies \deg(K_X) = 2g - 2.$$

**Step 2: Degree of the dual (anti-canonical) bundle.**

For any line bundle $L$ on a curve, $\deg(L^{-1}) = -\deg(L)$, since if $s$ is a
 meromorphic section of $L$ with divisor $D$, then $s^{-1}$ is a meromorphic sec
tion of $L^{-1}$ with divisor $-D$, and degree is additive under tensor product
(equivalently, $\deg$ is a homomorphism $\operatorname{Pic}(X) \to \mathbb{Z}$).

Applying this to $L = K_X$:

$$\deg(K_X^{-1}) = -\deg(K_X) = -(2g - 2) = 2 - 2g.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\deg(K_X^{-1}) = 2 - 2g.}$$

As a sanity check: for $g = 0$ (the Riemann sphere), $\deg(K_{\mathbb{P}^1}^{-1}
) = 2$, which agrees with $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(2) = T_{\mathbb{P}^1}$, th
e tangent bundle of $\mathbb{P}^1$. For $g = 1$ (an elliptic curve), the answer
is $0$, consistent with the fact that $K_X$ is trivial on an elliptic curve.
