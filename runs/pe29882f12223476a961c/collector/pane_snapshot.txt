Answer

No. Pointwise solvability does not guarantee a continuous global solution. Below
is a counterexample.

Counterexample

Take $X = [0,1]$ (compact Hausdorff), $m = n = 1$. Define $$a(x) = x, \qquad
b(x) = \begin{cases} x,\sin(1/x), & x \in (0,1],\ 0, & x = 0.\end{cases}$$

Both $a$ and $b$ belong to $C([0,1])$: $a$ is the identity (continuous), and $b$
is continuous since $|b(x)| \le |x| \to 0 = b(0)$ as $x \to 0^+$, and $b$ is a
product of continuous functions on $(0,1]$.

Pointwise condition is satisfied

For each $x \in [0,1]$, we check $b(x) \in a(x),\mathbb{C} =
\operatorname{range}(A(x))$:

  • $x > 0$: $a(x) = x \neq 0$, so $\operatorname{range}(A(x)) = \mathbb{C}$,
  and $b(x) \in \mathbb{C}$. ✓
  (Explicitly, $b(x) = a(x)\cdot\sin(1/x)$.)
  • $x = 0$: $a(0) = 0$, so $\operatorname{range}(A(0)) = {0}$, and $b(0) = 0
  \in {0}$. ✓

Thus $b(x) \in \operatorname{range}(A(x))$ for every $x \in X$.

No continuous global solution exists

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists $y \in C([0,1])$ with $a,y = b$,
i.e., $x,y(x) = b(x)$ for all $x$.

For every $x > 0$: $$y(x) = \frac{b(x)}{a(x)} = \frac{x\sin(1/x)}{x} = \sin(1/
x).$$

Since $y$ is continuous on $[0,1]$, it must be continuous at $x = 0$, so $
\lim_{x\to 0^+} y(x) = y(0)$ must exist. But $$\lim_{x\to 0^+} \sin(1/x)
\text{ does not exist}$$ (the function oscillates between $-1$ and $1$ along the
sequences $x_k = 1/(2k\pi)$ and $x_k = 1/((2k+\tfrac12)\pi)$). This is a
contradiction.

Conclusion

Therefore no $y \in C([0,1])$ satisfies $a,y = b$, even though $b(x) \in
\operatorname{range}(A(x))$ for every $x \in X$.

The answer to the question is no: pointwise solvability of $A(x),y(x) = b(x)$
for all $x \in X$ does not, in general, imply the existence of a continuous
global solution $y \in C(X)^{n\times 1}$ with $Ay = b$.

The essential obstruction is that the pointwise solution $y(x) = b(x)/a(x)$,
while defined on the open set ${a \neq 0}$, need not extend continuously to the
zero set ${a = 0}$; the ring $C(X)$ is not a principal ideal domain (nor even a
Bézout domain) in general, so divisibility conditions that hold pointwise cannot
always be "glued" into global continuous sections.
