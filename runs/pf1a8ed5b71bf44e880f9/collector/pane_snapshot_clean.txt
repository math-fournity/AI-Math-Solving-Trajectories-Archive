## Proof

We prove the inequality by contradiction. Assume no such constant $C(\alpha, n)$
 exists. Then for each $k \ge 1$ there exists $u_k \in H^1(U)$ with
$$|\{x \in U : u_k(x) = 0\}| \ge \alpha, \qquad \int_U u_k^2\,dx = 1, \qquad \in
t_U |Du_k|^2\,dx \le \frac{1}{k}.$$

**Step 1 (Compactness).** The sequence $\{u_k\}$ is bounded in $H^1(U)$ since $\
|u_k\|_{H^1}^2 = \int u_k^2 + \int|Du_k|^2 \le 1 + 1$. By the Rellich–Kondrachov
 compactness theorem (the embedding $H^1(U) \hookrightarrow L^2(U)$ is compact f
or the bounded Lipschitz domain $U = B(0,1)$), there exists a subsequence (still
 denoted $u_k$) and $u \in L^2(U)$ such that
$$u_k \to u \quad \text{strongly in } L^2(U).$$

Moreover, since $\|Du_k\|_{L^2} \to 0$, the sequence $\{u_k\}$ is bounded in $H^
1$ and we may extract a further subsequence with $u_k \rightharpoonup u$ weakly
in $H^1(U)$ for some $u \in H^1(U)$. By weak lower semicontinuity,
$$\int_U |Du|^2\,dx \le \liminf_{k\to\infty} \int_U |Du_k|^2\,dx = 0,$$
so $Du = 0$ a.e. in $U$. Since $U$ is connected, $u$ is constant a.e.: $u \equiv
 c$ for some constant $c \in \mathbb{R}$.

By the $L^2$ convergence, $\int_U u^2\,dx = \lim_k \int_U u_k^2\,dx = 1$, so $c
\ne 0$.

**Step 2 (Passing the zero-set condition to the limit).** We claim $|\{x \in U :
 u(x)=0\}| \ge \alpha$.

Since $u_k \to u$ in $L^2(U)$, we have convergence in measure. Fix $\varepsilon
> 0$. For every $k$,
$$\{x: |u(x)| < \varepsilon\} \supseteq \{x: |u_k(x)| < \varepsilon/2\} \cap \{x
: |u_k(x)-u(x)| < \varepsilon/2\},$$
since if both $|u_k|<\varepsilon/2$ and $|u_k - u|<\varepsilon/2$, then $|u| \le
 |u_k| + |u-u_k| < \varepsilon$.

By inclusion–exclusion,
$$|\{|u|<\varepsilon\}| \ge |\{|u_k|<\varepsilon/2\}| + |\{|u_k-u|<\varepsilon/2
\}| - |U|.$$

Since $\{u_k = 0\} \subseteq \{|u_k|<\varepsilon/2\}$, we have $|\{|u_k|<\vareps
ilon/2\}| \ge \alpha$. Since $u_k \to u$ in measure, $|\{|u_k - u|<\varepsilon/2
\}| \to |U|$. Taking $\liminf_{k\to\infty}$,
$$|\{|u|<\varepsilon\}| \ge \alpha + |U| - |U| = \alpha.$$

This holds for every $\varepsilon > 0$. The sets $\{|u|<\varepsilon\}$ decrease
to $\{u = 0\}$ as $\varepsilon \searrow 0$, so by continuity of measure from abo
ve,
$$|\{u = 0\}| = \lim_{\varepsilon \to 0^+} |\{|u|<\varepsilon\}| \ge \alpha.$$

**Step 3 (Contradiction).** We have shown $u \equiv c \ne 0$ a.e., which gives $
|\{u = 0\}| = 0$. But Step 2 gives $|\{u = 0\}| \ge \alpha$. Since $\alpha > 0$
(otherwise the statement is vacuous and false—take $u \equiv 1$), this is a cont
radiction.

Therefore, the assumed sequence $\{u_k\}$ cannot exist, and there must be a cons
tant $C = C(\alpha, n) > 0$ such that
$$\int_U u^2\,dx \le C \int_U |Du|^2\,dx$$
for all $u \in H^1(U)$ with $|\{x \in U : u(x) = 0\}| \ge \alpha$. $\blacksquare
$
