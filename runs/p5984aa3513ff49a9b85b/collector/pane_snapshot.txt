## Proof

**Claim:** There are infinitely many prime numbers of the form $k \cdot 2^n + 1$
, where $k$ and $n$ range over positive integers.

**Proof:**

Every odd prime $p$ satisfies $p \geq 3$, so $p - 1 \geq 2$ is an even positive
integer. We can therefore factor out all powers of 2 from $p - 1$:

$$p - 1 = 2^n \cdot k$$

where $n \geq 1$ and $k$ is a positive odd integer. Rearranging:

$$p = k \cdot 2^n + 1.$$

Thus **every odd prime** is expressible in the form $k \cdot 2^n + 1$ for some p
ositive integers $k$ and $n$.

By Euclid's theorem, there are infinitely many primes. Since $2$ is the only eve
n prime, infinitely many of these primes are odd. Each such odd prime has the re
quired form.

Therefore, there are infinitely many primes of the form $k \cdot 2^n + 1$. $\bla
cksquare$
