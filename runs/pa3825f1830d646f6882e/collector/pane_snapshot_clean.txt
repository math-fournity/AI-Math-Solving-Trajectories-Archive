## Solution

**Answer check:** The problem text contains no leaked solution. Proceeding.

We find all $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ satisfying
$$(x+y)f(x)+f(y^2)=(x+y)f(y)+f(x^2).$$

**Step 1: Reduce to a homogeneous equation.**

Rearrange:
$$(x+y)\bigl(f(x)-f(y)\bigr)=f(x^2)-f(y^2).\tag{1}$$

Set $y=0$:
$$x\,f(x)+f(0)=x\,f(0)+f(x^2),$$
hence
$$f(x^2)=x\bigl(f(x)-f(0)\bigr)+f(0).\tag{2}$$

Define $h(x)=f(x)-f(0)$. Then (2) becomes
$$h(x^2)=x\,h(x)\qquad\text{for all }x.\tag{3}$$

**Step 2: Determine $h$.**

Substituting $f(x)-f(y)=h(x)-h(y)$ and $f(x^2)-f(y^2)=h(x^2)-h(y^2)=x\,h(x)-y\,h
(y)$ (using (3)) into (1):
$$(x+y)\bigl(h(x)-h(y)\bigr)=x\,h(x)-y\,h(y).$$

Expanding the left side:
$$x\,h(x)-x\,h(y)+y\,h(x)-y\,h(y)=x\,h(x)-y\,h(y),$$
which simplifies to
$$y\,h(x)=x\,h(y)\qquad\text{for all }x,y.\tag{4}$$

Setting $y=1$ in (4): $h(x)=x\,h(1)$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$. Thus $h(x)=cx$
where $c=h(1)\in\mathbb{R}$ is a constant (this also gives $h(0)=0$, consistent
with the definition).

**Step 3: Verify consistency with (3).**

For $h(x)=cx$: $h(x^2)=cx^2=x\cdot cx=x\,h(x)$. ✓

**Step 4: Form of $f$ and verification.**

Since $h(x)=f(x)-f(0)=cx$, we have $f(x)=cx+d$ where $d=f(0)$ is an arbitrary re
al constant.

Check in the original equation:
- LHS: $(x+y)(cx+d)+(cy^2+d)=cx^2+cxy+dx+dy+cy^2+d$.
- RHS: $(x+y)(cy+d)+(cx^2+d)=cxy+cy^2+dx+dy+cx^2+d$.

LHS $=$ RHS. ✓

**Conclusion.** The solutions are precisely all affine functions
$$\boxed{f(x)=ax+b,\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{R}.}$$
