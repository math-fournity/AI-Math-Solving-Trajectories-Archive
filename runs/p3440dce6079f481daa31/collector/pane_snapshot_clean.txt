## Solution

### Setup: the possible Galois groups

Let $f \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ be an irreducible quintic. Its Galois group $G = \math
rm{Gal}(f/\mathbb{Q})$, acting on the five roots, is a transitive subgroup of $S
_5$. Up to conjugacy there are exactly five:

| Group | Order | Contained in $A_5$? | Contained in $F_{20}$? |
|-------|-------|---------------------|------------------------|
| $C_5$ | 5  | Yes | Yes |
| $D_5$ | 10 | Yes | Yes |
| $F_{20}$ | 20 | No  | Yes |
| $A_5$ | 60 | Yes | No  |
| $S_5$ | 120 | No  | No  |

Here $F_{20} \cong \mathrm{AGL}(1,5)$ is the Frobenius group of order 20, the no
rmalizer of a Sylow-$5$ subgroup in $S_5$.

### Invariant 1 — the discriminant

The discriminant $\Delta = \mathrm{disc}(f) \in \mathbb{Q}$ satisfies

$$\Delta \text{ is a square in } \mathbb{Q} \iff G \leq A_5.$$

This is the classical discriminant criterion. Applying it partitions the five ca
ndidates:

- **$\Delta$ a square** $\Rightarrow G \in \{C_5,\, D_5,\, A_5\}$.
- **$\Delta$ not a square** $\Rightarrow G \in \{F_{20},\, S_5\}$.

### Invariant 2 — the Weber sextic resolvent

The **Weber sextic resolvent** $W_f(x) \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ is the degree-$6$ reso
lvent associated to the subgroup $F_{20} \leq S_5$, i.e. to the index-$6$ action
 of $S_5$ on the six Sylow-$5$ subgroups (equivalently, on $S_5 / F_{20}$). Its
fundamental property is:

$$W_f(x) \text{ has a rational root} \iff G \leq F_{20} \text{ (up to conjugacy)
}.$$

The transitive subgroups of $S_5$ lying inside $F_{20}$ are precisely $C_5$, $D_
5$, and $F_{20}$ itself. Hence:

- **$W_f$ has a rational root** $\Rightarrow G \in \{C_5,\, D_5,\, F_{20}\}$.
- **$W_f$ has no rational root** $\Rightarrow G \in \{A_5,\, S_5\}$.

### Combining the two tests

Each test is a binary partition of the five candidates. Their intersection refin
es the possibilities as follows:

| $\Delta$ square? | $W_f$ has rational root? | Conclusion |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| No  | No  | $G = S_5$ |
| No  | Yes | $G = F_{20}$ |
| Yes | No  | $G = A_5$ |
| Yes | Yes | $G \in \{C_5,\, D_5\}$ — **ambiguous** |

In three of the four cases the group is pinned down uniquely. The reasoning for
each row:

- **(No, No):** Not in $A_5$ and not in $F_{20}$ leaves only $S_5$.
- **(No, Yes):** In $F_{20}$ but not in $A_5$ leaves only $F_{20}$ (since $C_5,
D_5 \leq A_5$).
- **(Yes, No):** In $A_5$ but not in $F_{20}$ leaves only $A_5$.
- **(Yes, Yes):** In both $A_5$ and $F_{20}$ leaves $\{C_5,\, D_5\}$ — unresolve
d.

### The limitation

The method **cannot distinguish $C_5$ from $D_5$**. Both groups lie in $A_5$ (ev
ery element of $C_5$ and $D_5$ is an even permutation: a $5$-cycle is a product
of $4$ transpositions, and a reflection of the pentagon is a product of $2$ tran
spositions), so the discriminant is a square in both cases. Both groups also lie
 in $F_{20}$ (they are subgroups of the normalizer of a Sylow-$5$ subgroup), so
the Weber sextic resolvent has a rational root in both cases. Consequently the c
ombined data $(\Delta \text{ square},\, W_f \text{ has a root})$ is identical fo
r $G = C_5$ and $G = D_5$, and the two invariants together are insufficient to s
eparate them.

To resolve the remaining ambiguity one needs a further resolvent exploiting the
index-$2$ subgroup $C_5 \trianglelefteq D_5$, for instance a **quadratic resolve
nt** (or equivalently testing whether a certain quadratic subfield of the splitt
ing field is rational): a rational root forces $G = C_5$, while irreducibility o
f that quadratic factor forces $G = D_5$. The discriminant–Weber pair alone does
 not provide this.

### PROOF COMPLETE
