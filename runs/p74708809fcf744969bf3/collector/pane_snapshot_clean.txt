## Solution

We interpret "the partial derivative of $f$ with respect to the matrix $H$" as t
he **Fréchet (directional) derivative** of $f$ at $A$ in the direction $H \in L(
\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R}^k)$:

$$Df(A)[H] \;=\; \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{f(A + tH) - f(A)}{t}.$$

This is the standard meaning in matrix calculus: $H$ is the perturbation/directi
on in which we differentiate the matrix-valued argument of $f$.

---

### Setup

We have $A, B, H \in L(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R}^k)$, $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^n$, an
d

$$f(A) = \langle Ax,\, By \rangle,$$

where $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$ is the Euclidean inner product on $\mathbb{
R}^k$. Note $B$, $x$, $y$ are held fixed; $f$ is a function of $A$ alone.

### Compute the difference quotient

Evaluate $f$ at the perturbed point $A + tH$:

$$f(A + tH) = \langle (A + tH)x,\, By \rangle.$$

By linearity of the operator and bilinearity of the inner product,

$$(A + tH)x = Ax + tHx,$$

so

$$f(A + tH) = \langle Ax + tHx,\, By \rangle = \langle Ax, By \rangle + t\langle
 Hx, By \rangle.$$

Therefore the difference is

$$f(A + tH) - f(A) = t\,\langle Hx, By \rangle,$$

and the difference quotient becomes

$$\frac{f(A + tH) - f(A)}{t} = \langle Hx, By \rangle,$$

which is **independent of $t$**.

### Take the limit

$$Df(A)[H] = \lim_{t \to 0} \langle Hx, By \rangle = \langle Hx, By \rangle.$$

The limit exists for every $H$, and the map $H \mapsto \langle Hx, By \rangle$ i
s linear in $H$. Hence $f$ is Fréchet-differentiable, and the derivative is exac
tly this linear map.

### Verification (coordinate form)

Writing the inner product as $\langle u, v\rangle = u^{\!\top} v$,

$$f(A) = (Ax)^{\!\top}(By) = x^{\!\top} A^{\!\top} B\, y.$$

Perturbing $A \mapsto A + tH$:

$$f(A + tH) = x^{\!\top}(A + tH)^{\!\top} B\, y = x^{\!\top} A^{\!\top} B\, y +
t\, x^{\!\top} H^{\!\top} B\, y.$$

Thus

$$Df(A)[H] = x^{\!\top} H^{\!\top} B\, y = (Hx)^{\!\top}(By) = \langle Hx, By \r
angle,$$

confirming the result.

---

### Result

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \frac{\partial f}{\partial H} \;=\; Df(A)[H] \;=\; \langl
e Hx,\, By \rangle.}$$

The derivative of $f(A) = \langle Ax, By\rangle$ in the matrix direction $H$ is
the linear functional $H \mapsto \langle Hx, By\rangle$.
