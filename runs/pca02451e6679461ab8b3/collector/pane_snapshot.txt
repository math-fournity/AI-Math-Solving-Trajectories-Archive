## Proof

Let $B$ be the symmetric bilinear form associated to $Q$, so $Q(v) = B(v,v)$. Si
nce $Q$ is non-singular, $B$ is non-degenerate.

**Step 1: $W$ is totally isotropic.**

Let $W \subseteq V$ be the $m$-dimensional subspace on which $Q$ vanishes, i.e.,
 $Q(w) = 0$ for all $w \in W$. By the polarization identity,
$$B(w_1, w_2) = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(Q(w_1 + w_2) - Q(w_1) - Q(w_2)\bigr) = 0 \quad
 \text{for all } w_1, w_2 \in W.$$
Thus $W$ is a **totally isotropic** subspace: $W \subseteq W^\perp$.

**Step 2: Upper bound on $\dim W$.**

Since $W \subseteq W^\perp$ and $B$ is non-degenerate ($\dim W^\perp = 2m - \dim
 W$), we get
$$m = \dim W \leq \dim W^\perp = 2m - m = m.$$
So equality holds throughout: $\dim W = m = \dim W^\perp$, hence $W = W^\perp$.
The subspace $W$ is **maximally** totally isotropic (a Lagrangian subspace).

**Step 3: Diagonalize and compute the Witt index.**

By Sylvester's law of inertia, $Q$ has signature $(p, q)$ with $p + q = 2m$. In
a diagonalizing basis,
$$Q = x_1^2 + \cdots + x_p^2 - x_{p+1}^2 - \cdots - x_{p+q}^2.$$
The **Witt index** (maximal dimension of a totally isotropic subspace) of this f
orm is $\min(p, q)$. This is seen by forming $\min(p, q)$ hyperbolic pairs: each
 pair of one positive and one negative direction $e_i, f_j$ yields the isotropic
 vector $e_i + f_j$ with $Q(e_i + f_j) = 1 - 1 = 0$, and these are linearly inde
pendent. The remaining $|p - q|$ directions form an anisotropic form (no nonzero
 isotropic vectors), so no further extension is possible.

**Step 4: Determine $p$ and $q$.**

Since $W$ is a totally isotropic subspace of dimension $m$, the Witt index satis
fies $\min(p, q) \geq m$. Combined with $p + q = 2m$, which gives $\min(p, q) \l
eq m$, we obtain:
$$\min(p, q) = m \quad \Longrightarrow \quad p = q = m.$$

**Conclusion.** The signature of $Q$ is $\boxed{(m,\, m)}$ — that is, $m$ positi
ve and $m$ negative eigenvalues.

### PROOF COMPLETE
