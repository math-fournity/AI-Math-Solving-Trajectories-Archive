## Problem Setup

A measuring tape of length ℓ = 25 m is supported at two equally high endpoints.
It is loaded by:

The tape length (arc length) is fixed at 25 m. The horizontal span between the s

horizontal force S enters as a **natural (force) boundary condition**.

**Coordinate system:** Let s ∈ [0, ℓ] be the arc-length coordinate along the tap

length, (dx/ds)² + (dw/ds)² = 1.

## (a) Strong Formulation

### Derivation of equilibrium equations

Consider an infinitesimal element of the tape of length ds. The tension acts alo
ng the tangent to the tape. At the left face (s), the tension force is −T(dx/ds,
 dw/ds); at the right face (s+ds), it is T(dx/ds, dw/ds)|_{s+ds}. The dead load
on the element is (0, q·ds) (vertical, downward).

**Horizontal equilibrium** (no horizontal distributed load):

$$\frac{d}{ds}\!\left(T\,\frac{dx}{ds}\right) = 0, \quad 0 < s < \ell \tag{1}$$

**Vertical equilibrium** (w positive downward, q positive downward):

$$\frac{d}{ds}\!\left(T\,\frac{dw}{ds}\right) = -q, \quad 0 < s < \ell \tag{2}$$

### Boundary conditions

**At s = 0 (left support):**
- x(0) = 0 — **essential** (fixes the horizontal reference position)
- w(0) = 0 — **essential** (support at zero height)

**At s = ℓ (right support):**
- w(ℓ) = 0 — **essential** (equal-height support)
- T(dx/ds)|_{s=ℓ} = S = 100 N — **natural** (horizontal tensile force applied)

### Consequence of the horizontal equation

From equation (1), T(dx/ds) = H (constant). The natural BC at s = ℓ gives H = S,
 so:

$$T\,\frac{dx}{ds} = S \quad \text{for all } s \in [0,\ell]$$

This states that the horizontal component of tension is constant and equal to S
= 100 N throughout the tape — the expected result for a cable with no horizontal
 distributed load. The tension itself is T = S/(dx/ds) = S√(1 + (dw/dx)²), which
 varies along the tape.

### Summary of the strong form

Find (x(s), w(s)) and T(s) > 0 satisfying:

$$\frac{d}{ds}\!\left(T\,\frac{dx}{ds}\right) = 0, \qquad 0 < s < \ell \tag{1}$$

$$\frac{d}{ds}\!\left(T\,\frac{dw}{ds}\right) = -q, \qquad 0 < s < \ell \tag{2}$
$

$$(dx/ds)^2 + (dw/ds)^2 = 1 \tag{3}$$

with:
- **Essential BCs:** x(0) = 0, w(0) = 0, w(ℓ) = 0
- **Natural BC:** T(dx/ds)|_{s=ℓ} = S = 100 N

where ℓ = 25 m, q = 0.4 N/m, S = 100 N.

### Small-sag reduction (1D string equation)

For small sag, dx/ds ≈ 1, T ≈ S, and x(s) ≈ s. Equation (2) reduces to the class
ical **string equation**:

$$-S\,w''(s) = q, \qquad 0 < s < \ell \tag{4}$$

with w(0) = 0, w(ℓ) = 0 (both essential BCs), and S = 100 N, q = 0.4 N/m, ℓ = 25
 m.

The solution is:

$$w(s) = \frac{q}{2S}\,s(\ell - s) = \frac{0.4}{200}\,s(25-s) = 0.002\,s(25-s)$$

Maximum sag at s = ℓ/2 = 12.5 m:

$$w_{\max} = \frac{q\ell^2}{8S} = \frac{0.4 \times 625}{800} = 0.3125 \text{ m}$
$

The horizontal span is L = x(ℓ) ≈ ℓ − ½∫₀^ℓ (w')² ds ≈ 24.99 m, confirming the s
mall-sag approximation is excellent (max slope |w'| = qℓ/(2S) = 0.05 ≪ 1).

**Note:** In this 1D reduction, the natural BC (horizontal force S) has been *an
alytically eliminated* by solving the horizontal equilibrium equation. S now app
ears as a **coefficient** in the differential equation rather than as a boundary
 condition. Only essential BCs (w = 0 at both ends) remain.

## (b) Weak Formulation

### Full 2D weak form

**Vertical equation (2):** Multiply by a test function v(s) satisfying the essen
tial BCs v(0) = v(ℓ) = 0, and integrate over [0, ℓ]:

$$\int_0^\ell \frac{d}{ds}\!\left(T\,\frac{dw}{ds}\right) v\,ds = -\int_0^\ell q
\,v\,ds$$

Integration by parts:

$$\left[T\,\frac{dw}{ds}\,v\right]_0^\ell - \int_0^\ell T\,\frac{dw}{ds}\,v'\,ds
 = -\int_0^\ell q\,v\,ds$$

Since v(0) = v(ℓ) = 0, the boundary term vanishes:

$$\boxed{\int_0^\ell T\,\frac{dw}{ds}\,\frac{dv}{ds}\,ds = \int_0^\ell q\,v\,ds
\quad \forall\, v \in V_w} \tag{5}$$

where $V_w = \{v \in H^1(0,\ell) : v(0) = v(\ell) = 0\}$.

**Horizontal equation (1):** Multiply by a test function v_x(s) satisfying the e
ssential BC v_x(0) = 0 (free at s = ℓ), and integrate:

$$\int_0^\ell \frac{d}{ds}\!\left(T\,\frac{dx}{ds}\right) v_x\,ds = 0$$

Integration by parts:

$$\left[T\,\frac{dx}{ds}\,v_x\right]_0^\ell - \int_0^\ell T\,\frac{dx}{ds}\,v_x'
\,ds = 0$$

Since v_x(0) = 0 and T(dx/ds)|_{ℓ} = S (natural BC):

$$\boxed{\int_0^\ell T\,\frac{dx}{ds}\,\frac{dv_x}{ds}\,ds = S\,v_x(\ell) \quad
\forall\, v_x \in V_x} \tag{6}$$

where $V_x = \{v_x \in H^1(0,\ell) : v_x(0) = 0\}$.

The full 2D weak form is: Find (x, w) ∈ V_x × V_w such that (5) and (6) hold for
 all test functions, with T = S/(dx/ds) (making the problem **nonlinear**).

### 1D reduced weak form (small-sag)

Setting T ≈ S in equation (5), the weak form becomes:

$$\boxed{\text{Find } w \in V \text{ such that } \int_0^\ell S\,w'\,v'\,ds = \in
t_0^\ell q\,v\,ds \quad \forall\, v \in V} \tag{7}$$

where $V = \{v \in H^1(0,\ell) : v(0) = v(\ell) = 0\}$.

This is the standard weak form of the string equation: the bilinear form is $a(w
,v) = \int_0^\ell S\,w'v'\,ds$ and the linear form is $\ell(v) = \int_0^\ell q\,
v\,ds$.

## (c) Comments on Natural and Essential Boundary Conditions

### In the strong formulation

**Essential (geometric) BCs** are stated as explicit constraints on the **primar
y unknowns** themselves:
- x(0) = 0, w(0) = 0, w(ℓ) = 0

These prescribe positions (displacements) at the supports and must be satisfied
pointwise by the solution.

**Natural (force) BCs** are stated as conditions on the **flux** (the derivative
 of the unknown multiplied by the material coefficient):
- T(dx/ds)|_{s=ℓ} = S

This prescribes the horizontal force at the right end. It involves the derivativ
e of the unknown, not the unknown itself.

### In the weak formulation

**Essential BCs** are **built into the function spaces** (trial and test spaces)
:
- V_x = {v_x ∈ H¹(0,ℓ) : v_x(0) = 0} — encodes x(0) = 0
- V_w = {v ∈ H¹(0,ℓ) : v(0) = v(ℓ) = 0} — encodes w(0) = w(ℓ) = 0

The essential BCs are **strongly enforced**: every function in the space satisfi
es them by construction. They do not appear explicitly in the weak statement — t
hey have been "absorbed" into the definition of V.

**Natural BCs** appear in the **linear form** (right-hand side) of the weak stat
ement through boundary terms arising from integration by parts:
- The term S·v_x(ℓ) in equation (6) is the contribution of the natural BC T(dx/d
s)|_{ℓ} = S.

The natural BC is **weakly enforced**: it is not imposed on the function space b
ut emerges automatically when the weak form is satisfied. If no natural BC were
prescribed (homogeneous natural BC), the boundary term would simply vanish.

### Key observations

1. **Duality:** At each boundary point and in each direction, exactly one type o
f BC is specified — either essential (displacement) or natural (force), never bo
th. At s = 0, both x and w are essential (position fixed, reactions unknown). At
 s = ℓ, w is essential (position fixed) while x is natural (force prescribed, po
sition free).

2. **Role of the horizontal force S:** In the full 2D formulation, S = 100 N is
a **natural BC** (prescribed horizontal force at the free end). It determines th
e unknown horizontal span. In the 1D small-sag reduction, the horizontal equilib
rium is solved analytically (T dx/ds = S = const.), and S becomes a **coefficien
t** in the bilinear form of the vertical equation. The natural BC is thereby "co
nsumed" — it no longer appears explicitly as a boundary term but is embedded in
the equation itself. This is why the 1D weak form (7) has only essential BCs in
its function space and no boundary term on the right-hand side.

3. **Physical interpretation:** The essential BCs (w = 0 at both ends) represent
 the geometric constraint that the tape is supported at two equally high points.
 The natural BC (horizontal force S) represents the physical loading that stretc
hes the tape horizontally. The vertical support reactions (T dw/ds at s = 0 and
s = ℓ) are **neither prescribed nor essential** — they are unknown reactions tha
t emerge from the solution, which is precisely the hallmark of essential BCs (th
e reactions to geometric constraints).

4. **Weak form advantage:** The weak formulation reduces the differentiability r
equirement from C² (strong form needs w'') to C⁰/H¹ (weak form needs only w'), a
nd automatically incorporates the natural BCs through integration by parts, maki
ng it the natural starting point for finite element discretization.
