## Proof

**Claim.** Let $V$ be an infinite-dimensional vector space over $\mathbb{F}$ ($\
mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$) with $\dim V = \alpha$ (an infinite cardinal). Then
$$\dim V^* = |\mathbb{F}|^{\alpha} = 2^{\alpha}.$$

---

### Step 1. Reduction to computing $\dim \mathbb{F}^{\alpha}$

Let $B = \{e_i : i \in I\}$ be a basis of $V$ with $|I| = \alpha$. A linear func
tional $\varphi \in V^*$ is completely determined by its values on $B$, and conv
ersely every function $f : I \to \mathbb{F}$ extends uniquely to a linear functi
onal (set $\varphi(e_i) = f(i)$ and extend linearly, using that every $v \in V$
is a *finite* linear combination of basis elements). The map
$$V^* \longrightarrow \mathbb{F}^{I}, \qquad \varphi \longmapsto \bigl(\varphi(e
_i)\bigr)_{i \in I}$$
is an $\mathbb{F}$-linear isomorphism. Hence $V^* \cong \mathbb{F}^{\alpha}$ as
$\mathbb{F}$-vector spaces, and it suffices to prove
$$\dim_{\mathbb{F}} \mathbb{F}^{\alpha} = |\mathbb{F}|^{\alpha}.$$

---

### Step 2. Upper bound

Trivially $\dim \mathbb{F}^{\alpha} \le |\mathbb{F}^{\alpha}| = |\mathbb{F}|^{\a
lpha}$.

---

### Step 3. Lower bound: a linearly independent set of size $|\mathbb{F}|^{\alph
a}$

Since $\mathbb{F} = \mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, we have $|\mathbb{F}| = 2^{\ale
ph_0}$, and therefore
$$|\mathbb{F}|^{\alpha} = (2^{\aleph_0})^{\alpha} = 2^{\aleph_0 \cdot \alpha} =
2^{\alpha},$$
using $\aleph_0 \cdot \alpha = \alpha$ for infinite $\alpha$. We split into two
cases.

**Case A: $\alpha > \aleph_0$.** Then $|\mathbb{F}|^{\alpha} = 2^{\alpha} > 2^{\
aleph_0} = |\mathbb{F}|$ (by Cantor, since $\alpha > \aleph_0$). We build a line
arly independent family $\{v_{\beta} : \beta < 2^{\alpha}\}$ in $\mathbb{F}^{\al
pha}$ by transfinite recursion. Suppose $\{v_{\gamma} : \gamma < \beta\}$ has be
en chosen for some $\beta < 2^{\alpha}$. The span of these $|\beta| < 2^{\alpha}
$ vectors consists of all finite $\mathbb{F}$-linear combinations, so its cardin
ality is at most
$$\max\bigl(|\mathbb{F}|,\, |\beta|\bigr) < 2^{\alpha} = |\mathbb{F}^{\alpha}|,$
$
since both $|\mathbb{F}| < 2^{\alpha}$ and $|\beta| < 2^{\alpha}$. Hence the spa
n is a proper subset of $\mathbb{F}^{\alpha}$, and we may pick $v_{\beta} \in \m
athbb{F}^{\alpha}$ outside it. This produces $2^{\alpha} = |\mathbb{F}|^{\alpha}
$ linearly independent elements.

**Case B: $\alpha = \aleph_0$.** Then $|\mathbb{F}|^{\aleph_0} = (2^{\aleph_0})^
{\aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph_0} = |\mathbb{F}|$. We exhibit $|\mathbb{F}|$ linearly in
dependent elements in $\mathbb{F}^{\omega}$ explicitly. For each $r \in \mathbb{
F}$, define the sequence
$$f_r = (1,\, r,\, r^{2},\, r^{3},\, \dots) \in \mathbb{F}^{\omega}.$$
We claim $\{f_r : r \in \mathbb{F}\}$ is linearly independent. Suppose $\sum_{k=
1}^{n} c_k\, f_{r_k} = 0$ for distinct $r_1, \dots, r_n \in \mathbb{F}$ and scal
ars $c_k \in \mathbb{F}$. This means
$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} c_k\, r_k^{m} = 0 \qquad \text{for all } m \ge 0.$$
Taking $m = 0, 1, \dots, n-1$ yields the system $V^{T} c = 0$ where $V$ is the $
n \times n$ Vandermonde matrix $V_{ij} = r_i^{\,j-1}$. Since the $r_k$ are disti
nct,
$$\det V = \prod_{1 \le i < j \le n} (r_j - r_i) \neq 0,$$
so $c_1 = \cdots = c_n = 0$. Thus $\{f_r : r \in \mathbb{F}\}$ is linearly indep
endent and has cardinality $|\mathbb{F}| = 2^{\aleph_0} = |\mathbb{F}|^{\aleph_0
}$.

---

### Step 4. Conclusion

In both cases we have exhibited $|\mathbb{F}|^{\alpha}$ linearly independent ele
ments of $\mathbb{F}^{\alpha}$, so
$$\dim_{\mathbb{F}} \mathbb{F}^{\alpha} = |\mathbb{F}|^{\alpha}.$$
By the isomorphism of Step 1,
$$\boxed{\dim V^* = |\mathbb{F}|^{\alpha} = 2^{\alpha}.}$$

**Consistency check.** Since $\alpha$ is infinite, Cantor's theorem gives $\dim
V^* = 2^{\alpha} > \alpha = \dim V$, confirming the known strict inequality $\di
m V < \dim V^*$.
