# Problem

A specialized logistics company manages 100 shipping containers, indexed with unique identification numbers from 1 to 100. Due to a warehouse reorganization, exactly 50 of these containers must be removed and sent to a secondary site.

The facility supervisor mandates a strict "Non-Divisibility Protocol" for the 50 containers remaining in the primary warehouse: for any two distinct containers left in the warehouse with identification numbers $a$ and $b$, the number $a$ must not be a divisor of $b$ (that is, $a \nmid b$).

To optimize the tax write-off for the relocation project, the supervisor wants to select the 50 containers for removal such that the sum of their identification numbers is as large as possible. 

Find the maximum possible value of the sum of the identification numbers of the 50 removed containers.
