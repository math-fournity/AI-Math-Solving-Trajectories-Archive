## Proof

Both the Poincaré disk and Beltrami-Klein models are realized on the open unit d
isk $D = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^2 : |x|<1\}$. We identify the two models along commo
n geodesic rays emanating from the origin (both models share the same radial geo
desics through $O$, since in each model the diameter through a point is a geodes
ic).

Let $z \in [0,1)$ be the Euclidean radial distance of a point in the **Poincaré*
* disk, and $w \in [0,1)$ the Euclidean radial distance of the **same hyperbolic
 point** in the **Beltrami-Klein** disk.

### Hyperbolic distance from the origin

**Poincaré disk.** The metric is $ds^2 = \frac{4\,|dx|^2}{(1-|x|^2)^2}$. Along a
 radial ray $x(t)=t\,\hat u$ with $t\in[0,z]$,
$$d_P(O,z) = \int_0^z \frac{2\,dt}{1-t^2} = 2\,\operatorname{artanh}(z) = \ln\!\
frac{1+z}{1-z}.$$

**Beltrami-Klein disk.** The metric is $ds^2 = \frac{|dx|^2}{1-|x|^2} + \frac{(x
\cdot dx)^2}{(1-|x|^2)^2}$. Along a radial ray $x(t)=t\,\hat u$, we have $x\cdot
 dx = t\,dt$, so
$$ds = \frac{dt}{1-t^2}, \qquad d_K(O,w) = \int_0^w \frac{dt}{1-t^2} = \operator
name{artanh}(w) = \tfrac12\ln\!\frac{1+w}{1-w}.$$

### Equating the two expressions for the same hyperbolic distance

Since $z$ and $w$ label the **same** hyperbolic point, the hyperbolic distance f
rom $O$ is identical:
$$2\,\operatorname{artanh}(z) = \operatorname{artanh}(w).$$

Applying $\tanh$ to both sides and using the double-argument identity $\tanh(2u)
 = \dfrac{2\tanh u}{1+\tanh^2 u}$ with $\tanh(\operatorname{artanh} z)=z$:
$$w = \tanh\!\bigl(2\,\operatorname{artanh}(z)\bigr) = \frac{2z}{1+z^2}.$$

### The radial scaling factor

Writing $w = \lambda(z)\,z$, the radial scaling factor that converts the Poincar
é radial distance $z$ into the Klein radial distance $w$ is

$$\boxed{\;\lambda(z) = \frac{2}{1+z^{2}}\;}$$

so that $w = \dfrac{2z}{1+z^2}$.

### Consistency checks

- At $z=0$: $\lambda=2$, and $w=0$ — the origin is fixed, as required.
- As $z\to 1^-$: $w\to \dfrac{2}{2}=1^-$ — the boundary circle is preserved, as
required (both models share the same ideal boundary).
- The map $z\mapsto \tfrac{2z}{1+z^2}$ is monotone increasing on $[0,1)$ (its de
rivative $\frac{2(1-z^2)}{(1+z^2)^2}>0$), so it is a bijection $[0,1)\to[0,1)$,
consistent with the two models being isometric.
- The inverse is $z = \dfrac{w}{1+\sqrt{1-w^2}}$, the standard formula recoverin
g the Poincaré coordinate from the Klein coordinate.

This confirms that the radial scaling factor relating the Poincaré radial distan
ce $z$ to the Beltrami-Klein radial distance $w$ is $\dfrac{2}{1+z^2}$.
