# Solution

We find all two-variable polynomials $f(x,y)$ with $f(1,2)=2$ and
$$y\,f(x,f(x,y)) \;=\; x\,f(f(x,y),y) \;=\; (f(x,y))^{2}.$$

## Step 1. Degree in $y$ forces $\deg_y f = 1$

Let $p=\deg_y f$ (the highest power of $y$ appearing with nonzero coefficient).
Write
$$f(x,y)=\sum_{i,j} a_{ij}\,x^{i}y^{j}, \qquad j\le p.$$
Then
$$f(x,f(x,y))=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\,x^{i}\,\bigl(f(x,y)\bigr)^{j}.$$
The $y$-degree of $(f(x,y))^{j}$ is $pj$. The maximum is attained at $j=p$ (the
term $a_{i_{0},p}\,x^{i_{0}}(f)^{p}$, which is nonzero by definition of $p$), gi
ving $y$-degree $p^{2}$; all terms with $j<p$ have $y$-degree $\le p(p-1)<p^{2}$
. Hence
$$\deg_y f(x,f(x,y))=p^{2}.$$
From $y\,f(x,f(x,y))=(f(x,y))^{2}$, comparing $y$-degrees:
$$p^{2}+1 = 2p \;\Longrightarrow\; (p-1)^{2}=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; p=1.$$

## Step 2. Degree in $x$ forces $\deg_x f = 1$

Let $q=\deg_x f$. By the symmetric argument on $x\,f(f(x,y),y)=(f(x,y))^{2}$: su
bstituting $f(x,y)$ into the first variable of $f$ gives $x$-degree $q^{2}$ (fro
m the $i=q$ term), so
$$q^{2}+1 = 2q \;\Longrightarrow\; q=1.$$

## Step 3. General form and substitution

With $\deg_x f=\deg_y f=1$, we have
$$f(x,y)=axy+bx+cy+d$$
for constants $a,b,c,d$. Let $g=f(x,y)=axy+bx+cy+d$.

**Compute $f(x,g)$:**
$$f(x,g)=axg+bx+cg+d = a^{2}x^{2}y+abx^{2}+2acxy+(ad+b+bc)x+c^{2}y+(cd+d).$$

**Compute $y\,f(x,g)$:**
$$y\,f(x,g)=a^{2}x^{2}y^{2}+abx^{2}y+2acxy^{2}+(ad+b+bc)xy+c^{2}y^{2}+(cd+d)y.$$

**Compute $(f)^{2}=(axy+bx+cy+d)^{2}$:**
$$(f)^{2}=a^{2}x^{2}y^{2}+b^{2}x^{2}+c^{2}y^{2}+d^{2}+2abx^{2}y+2acxy^{2}+2adxy+
2bcxy+2bdx+2cdy.$$

Setting $y\,f(x,g)=(f)^{2}$ and comparing coefficients:

| monomial | LHS | RHS | conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| $x^{2}$ | $0$ | $b^{2}$ | $b=0$ |
| const | $0$ | $d^{2}$ | $d=0$ |
| $x^{2}y$ | $ab$ | $2ab$ | $ab=0$ (automatic) |
| $xy$ | $ad+b+bc$ | $2ad+2bc$ | $b=ad+bc$ (automatic) |
| $y$ | $cd+d$ | $2cd$ | $d(c-1)=0$ (automatic) |

So $b=0$ and $d=0$, giving $f(x,y)=y(ax+c)$.

## Step 4. Use the second identity

Now $g=f(x,y)=y(ax+c)$. Compute
$$f(g,y)=a\,g\,y+cy = a(ax+c)y^{2}+cy,$$
$$x\,f(g,y)=a(ax+c)xy^{2}+cxy = a^{2}x^{2}y^{2}+acxy^{2}+cxy.$$
Also
$$(f)^{2}=y^{2}(ax+c)^{2}=a^{2}x^{2}y^{2}+2acxy^{2}+c^{2}y^{2}.$$

Setting $x\,f(g,y)=(f)^{2}$ and comparing coefficients:

| monomial | LHS | RHS | conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| $xy^{2}$ | $ac$ | $2ac$ | $ac=0$ |
| $xy$ | $c$ | $0$ | $c=0$ |
| $y^{2}$ | $0$ | $c^{2}$ | $c=0$ |

Hence $c=0$ (and then $ac=0$ automatically).

## Step 5. Determine $a$ and verify

With $b=c=d=0$, we have $f(x,y)=axy$. The condition $f(1,2)=2$ gives $2a=2$, so
$a=1$.

**Verification.** For $f(x,y)=xy$:
- $f(1,2)=1\cdot 2=2$. ✓
- $f(x,f(x,y))=f(x,xy)=x\cdot xy=x^{2}y$, so $y\,f(x,f(x,y))=x^{2}y^{2}$.
- $f(f(x,y),y)=f(xy,y)=xy\cdot y=xy^{2}$, so $x\,f(f(x,y),y)=x^{2}y^{2}$.
- $(f(x,y))^{2}=(xy)^{2}=x^{2}y^{2}$.

All three quantities equal $x^{2}y^{2}$. ✓

## Conclusion

The unique two-variable polynomial satisfying the given conditions is

$$\boxed{f(x,y)=xy}.$$
