# Problem

A specialized deep-sea research station is built in the shape of a regular hexagon on a flat coordinate grid representing a section of the ocean floor, with its center located at $(0,0)$. The station is oriented such that two of its boundary walls are perfectly parallel to the y-axis. The distance measured directly between any two opposite parallel walls is exactly one kilometer.

An automated drone is programmed to patrol the vast "Outer Zone," defined as the entire infinite region of the seafloor lying outside the perimeter of this hexagonal station.

A marine biologist is studying the distribution of a specific mineral. They apply a mathematical transformation to every coordinate $(x, y)$ within the Outer Zone to map a new region of interest. Each point $z = x + iy$ in the Outer Zone is mapped to a new position $w$ in the "Mapped Zone" using the reciprocal function $w = 1/z$.

The total area of this resulting Mapped Zone can be expressed in the form $a\pi + \sqrt{b}$ square kilometers, where $a$ and $b$ are positive integers.

Find the value of $a + b$.
