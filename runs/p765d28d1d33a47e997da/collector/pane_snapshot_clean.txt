**Note on scaling:** The natural scale of $Y_N$ is $\sqrt{N}$ (since $\operatorn

## Proof

**Step 1: Donsker's Invariance Principle.**

Let $S_i = \sum_{j=1}^i X_j$. Define the rescaled process
$$W_N(t) = \frac{S_{\lfloor Nt \rfloor}}{\sqrt{N}}, \quad t \in [0,1].$$
By Donsker's theorem, $W_N \Rightarrow B$ in $D[0,1]$ with the Skorokhod topolog
y, where $B = \{B_t\}_{t \in [0,1]}$ is standard Brownian motion.

The functional $\phi(x) = \sup_{0 \leq t \leq 1} |x(t)|$ is continuous on $C[0,1
]$ (with the uniform norm). Since Brownian motion has continuous paths and the c
onvergence $W_N \Rightarrow B$ holds, by the continuous mapping theorem:
$$\frac{Y_N}{\sqrt{N}} = \max_{1 \leq i \leq N} \frac{|S_i|}{\sqrt{N}} \xrightar
row{d} M := \sup_{0 \leq t \leq 1} |B_t|.$$

Therefore:
$$\lim_{N \to \infty} \Pr\!\left(\frac{Y_N}{\sqrt{N}} \leq \alpha\right) = \Pr(M
 \leq \alpha) = \Pr\!\left(|B_t| \leq \alpha \;\;\forall\, t \in [0,1]\right).$$

**Step 2: Survival probability of Brownian motion in $[-\alpha, \alpha]$.**

We compute $\Pr(M \leq \alpha)$, the probability that Brownian motion started at
 $0$ remains in the interval $(-\alpha, \alpha)$ up to time $t = 1$. This is the
 survival probability for Brownian motion killed at $\pm \alpha$.

The killed transition density on $(-\alpha, \alpha)$ is obtained by the eigenfun
ction expansion (method of images / separation of variables). The eigenfunctions
 of $-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d^2}{dx^2}$ on $(-\alpha,\alpha)$ with Dirichlet boundary
 conditions are:
$$\varphi_n(x) = \sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi(x+\alpha)}{2\alpha}\right), \quad \lambd
a_n = \frac{n^2 \pi^2}{8\alpha^2}, \quad n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$$

The killed transition density is:
$$p^*(t, x, y) = \frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi(x+\a
lpha)}{2\alpha}\right) \sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi(y+\alpha)}{2\alpha}\right) e^{-n^2
 \pi^2 t/(8\alpha^2)}.$$

The survival probability starting from $x = 0$ at time $t = 1$ is:
$$\Pr(M \leq \alpha) = \int_{-\alpha}^{\alpha} p^*(1, 0, y)\, dy.$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the integral.**

Substituting $x = 0$: $\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi(0+\alpha)}{2\alpha}\right) = \sin\
!\left(\frac{n\pi}{2}\right)$, which equals $0$ for even $n$ and $(-1)^k$ for $n
 = 2k+1$.

The remaining integral:
$$\int_{-\alpha}^{\alpha} \sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi(y+\alpha)}{2\alpha}\right) dy =
 \frac{2\alpha}{n\pi}\Big[-\cos\!\left(\frac{n\pi(y+\alpha)}{2\alpha}\right)\Big
]_{-\alpha}^{\alpha} = \frac{2\alpha}{n\pi}\big(1 - \cos(n\pi)\big) = \frac{2\al
pha}{n\pi}\big(1 - (-1)^n\big).$$

This vanishes for even $n$ and equals $\frac{4\alpha}{(2k+1)\pi}$ for $n = 2k+1$
.

Assembling (only odd $n = 2k+1$ survive):
$$\Pr(M \leq \alpha) = \frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} (-1)^k \cdot \frac{4\
alpha}{(2k+1)\pi} \cdot e^{-(2k+1)^2 \pi^2/(8\alpha^2)} = \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{k=0
}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k}{2k+1}\, e^{-(2k+1)^2 \pi^2/(8\alpha^2)}.$$

**Step 4: Upper bound via alternating series.**

The series is alternating. The magnitudes $a_k = \frac{1}{2k+1} e^{-(2k+1)^2 \pi
^2/(8\alpha^2)}$ are strictly decreasing in $k$ (both factors decrease). By the
alternating series estimation theorem, the partial sum through $k = 0$ overestim
ates the limit:

$$\boxed{\Pr(M \leq \alpha) = \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k}{2k+
1}\, e^{-(2k+1)^2 \pi^2/(8\alpha^2)} \;\leq\; \frac{4}{\pi}\, e^{-\pi^2/(8\alpha
^2)}.}$$

**Conclusion.**

By Donsker's theorem and the eigenfunction expansion for the killed Brownian mot
ion:

$$\lim_{N \to \infty} \Pr\!\left(\frac{Y_N}{\sqrt{N}} \leq \alpha\right) = \frac
{4}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k}{2k+1}\, e^{-(2k+1)^2 \pi^2/(8\alpha^2)
} \;\leq\; \frac{4}{\pi}\, e^{-\pi^2/(8\alpha^2)}.$$

The upper bound $\frac{4}{\pi} e^{-\pi^2/(8\alpha^2)}$ is sharp for small $\alph
a$ (the higher-order terms are exponentially suppressed by $(2k+1)^2$ in the exp
onent), and decays rapidly as $\alpha \to 0$, consistent with the intuition that
 confining a diffusive process to a narrow strip is exponentially unlikely.
