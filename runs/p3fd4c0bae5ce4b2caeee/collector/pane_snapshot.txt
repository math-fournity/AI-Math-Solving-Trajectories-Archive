**Answer: Yes, $f$ is convex on the entire set $X$.**

**Proof.**

Let $X \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ be a nonempty convex set with nonempty interior, an
d let $f: X \to \mathbb{R}$ be continuous and convex on $\operatorname{int}(X)$.
 We show that $f$ is convex on all of $X$.

We must verify that for all $x, y \in X$ and all $\lambda \in [0,1]$,
$$
f(\lambda x + (1-\lambda)y) \le \lambda f(x) + (1-\lambda) f(y). \tag{$\ast$}
$$

When both $x, y \in \operatorname{int}(X)$, ($\ast$) holds by assumption. The on
ly difficulty is when at least one of $x, y$ lies on the boundary $\partial X$.

**Key approximation lemma.** Since $\operatorname{int}(X) \neq \emptyset$, pick
a point $z_0 \in \operatorname{int}(X)$, so there exists $r > 0$ with $B(z_0, r)
 \subseteq X$. For any $x \in X$ and any $t \in (0, 1]$, define
$$
x_t := (1-t)\,x + t\,z_0.
$$
Because $X$ is convex and $x \in X$, the set $(1-t)\,x + t\,B(z_0, r) = B(x_t,\,
 tr)$ is contained in $X$. Hence $x_t \in \operatorname{int}(X)$ for every $t \i
n (0, 1]$, and $x_t \to x$ as $t \to 0^+$.

**Completing the proof.** Fix $x, y \in X$ and $\lambda \in [0,1]$. For $t \in (
0,1]$, set $x_t = (1-t)x + t z_0$ and $y_t = (1-t)y + t z_0$, so that $x_t, y_t
\in \operatorname{int}(X)$ by the lemma. Their convex combination is
$$
\lambda\, x_t + (1-\lambda)\, y_t = (1-t)\bigl(\lambda x + (1-\lambda) y\bigr) +
 t\, z_0.
$$
Since $X$ is convex, $w := \lambda x + (1-\lambda) y \in X$, so the right-hand s
ide is of the form $(1-t)w + t z_0$ with $w \in X$; by the lemma again, $\lambda
 x_t + (1-\lambda) y_t \in \operatorname{int}(X)$.

All three points $x_t, y_t, \lambda x_t + (1-\lambda) y_t$ lie in $\operatorname
{int}(X)$, so convexity of $f$ on the interior gives
$$
f\bigl(\lambda x_t + (1-\lambda) y_t\bigr) \le \lambda\, f(x_t) + (1-\lambda)\,
f(y_t), \qquad t \in (0,1].
$$

Now let $t \to 0^+$. By continuity of $f$ on $X$:
- $f(x_t) \to f(x)$,
- $f(y_t) \to f(y)$,
- $f\bigl(\lambda x_t + (1-\lambda) y_t\bigr) \to f\bigl(\lambda x + (1-\lambda)
 y\bigr)$.

Passing to the limit in the inequality yields
$$
f\bigl(\lambda x + (1-\lambda) y\bigr) \le \lambda\, f(x) + (1-\lambda)\, f(y).
$$

Since $x, y \in X$ and $\lambda \in [0,1]$ were arbitrary, $f$ is convex on $X$.
 $\blacksquare$
