Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{1}
_{\{x+\inf_{0 \le t \le 2} W_t > 0\}} (W_{\tau} - y)^+\right] > 0$ for all $x, y
 > 0$ and all stopping times $\tau \in [1,2]$.

**Answer: Yes, the expectation is strictly positive.**

## Proof

Let $M = \inf_{0 \le t \le 2} W_t$. The indicator is $\mathbf{1}_{\{M > -x\}}$,
and the expectation is
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{M > -x\}}(W_\tau - y)^+\right].$$

This is strictly positive if and only if $\mathbb{P}(M > -x,\; W_\tau > y) > 0$
(since on the intersection, $(W_\tau - y)^+ > 0$, and positivity of the probabil
ity of a set where the integrand is bounded below by a positive constant suffice
s).

**Key construction.** Fix $\varepsilon > 0$ and define the event
$$E_\varepsilon = \left\{\inf_{0 \le t \le 1} W_t > -\tfrac{x}{2}\right\} \cap \
left\{\inf_{1 \le t \le 2} W_t > y + \varepsilon\right\}.$$

Since $y + \varepsilon > 0 > -x/2$, the second event implies $\inf_{1 \le t \le
2} W_t > -x/2$, so on $E_\varepsilon$:
$$M = \inf_{0 \le t \le 2} W_t = \min\!\left(\inf_{0 \le t \le 1} W_t,\; \inf_{1
 \le t \le 2} W_t\right) > -\frac{x}{2} > -x.$$

Thus $E_\varepsilon \subseteq \{M > -x\}$, giving $\mathbf{1}_{\{M > -x\}} = 1$
on $E_\varepsilon$.

Moreover, on $E_\varepsilon$, we have $W_t > y + \varepsilon$ for all $t \in [1,
2]$. Since $\tau \in [1,2]$ a.s., it follows that $W_\tau > y + \varepsilon$, he
nce $(W_\tau - y)^+ = W_\tau - y > \varepsilon$.

**Lower bound.** Therefore:
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{M > -x\}}(W_\tau - y)^+\right] \geq \mathbb{E}
\!\left[\mathbf{1}_{E_\varepsilon}(W_\tau - y)^+\right] \geq \varepsilon \cdot \
mathbb{P}(E_\varepsilon).$$

**Positivity of $\mathbb{P}(E_\varepsilon)$.** By the Markov property and condit
ioning on $W_1$:
$$\mathbb{P}(E_\varepsilon) = \int_{y+\varepsilon}^{\infty} \mathbb{P}\!\left(\i
nf_{0 \le t \le 1} W_t > -\tfrac{x}{2}\;\Big|\; W_1 = w\right) \cdot \mathbb{P}\
!\left(\inf_{0 \le s \le 1}(W_{1+s} - W_1) > y + \varepsilon - w\right) \varphi(
w)\, dw,$$
where $\varphi$ is the density of $W_1 \sim N(0,1)$.

For $w > y + \varepsilon$:
- $\mathbb{P}\!\left(\inf_{0 \le t \le 1} W_t > -x/2 \mid W_1 = w\right) > 0$: t
his is the survival probability of a Brownian bridge from $0$ to $w$ over $[0,1]
$ staying above $-x/2$, which is strictly positive since both endpoints $0$ and
$w$ are above $-x/2$.
- $\mathbb{P}\!\left(\inf_{0 \le s \le 1}(W_{1+s} - W_1) > y + \varepsilon - w\r
ight) > 0$: this is the survival probability of a standard Brownian motion (star
ting at $0$) staying above $y + \varepsilon - w < 0$ over $[0,1]$, which is stri
ctly positive.
- $\varphi(w) > 0$.

The integrand is strictly positive and continuous on $(y+\varepsilon, \infty)$,
so the integral is strictly positive. Hence $\mathbb{P}(E_\varepsilon) > 0$.

**Conclusion.** Combining the lower bound:
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{x + \inf_{0 \le t \le 2} W_t > 0\}}(W_\tau - y
)^+\right] \geq \varepsilon \cdot \mathbb{P}(E_\varepsilon) > 0.$$

This holds for every $x, y > 0$, every $\varepsilon > 0$, and every stopping tim
e $\tau$ with $\tau \in [1,2]$ a.s. The argument uses only that $\tau \in [1,2]$
, so $W_\tau$ is evaluated at a time where the path is constrained to be above $
y + \varepsilon$ on $E_\varepsilon$; the specific stopping rule is irrelevant si
nce the bound holds uniformly over all paths in $E_\varepsilon$.
