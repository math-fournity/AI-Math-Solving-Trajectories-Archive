## Solution to the Cauchy Problem

**Setting and Assumptions.** Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ be a bounded doma
in with smooth boundary $\partial\Omega$. Let $\Delta^N$ denote the Neumann Lapl
acian, i.e., $\Delta^N u = \Delta u$ with Neumann boundary condition $\partial_\
nu u = 0$ on $\partial\Omega$. Let $\kappa_0 > 0$ and $\nu_1 \in \mathbb{R}$ be
constants. Let $\tilde{w}$ be a given function such that $(\log \tilde{w})_t \in
 L^2(0,T; L^2(\Omega))$. The initial data satisfies $v_0 \in H^1(\Omega) \cap L^
\infty(\Omega)$.

**Goal.** Prove that the Cauchy problem
$$
\begin{cases}
v_t(t) - \kappa_0 \Delta^N v(t) = \nu_1 (\log \tilde{w})_t(t) & \text{in } L^2(\
Omega),\ \text{a.e. } t \in (0,T],\\
v(0) = v_0,
\end{cases}
$$
admits a unique strong solution with appropriate regularity, and give its repres
entation.

---

### Step 1. Functional Framework

The Neumann Laplacian $\Delta^N$ is a self-adjoint, non-positive operator on $L^
2(\Omega)$ with domain
$$D(\Delta^N) = \bigl\{u \in H^2(\Omega) : \partial_\nu u = 0 \text{ on } \parti
al\Omega\bigr\}.$$
Define $A := -\kappa_0 \Delta^N$. Since $\kappa_0 > 0$ and $\Delta^N \leq 0$ is
self-adjoint, $A$ is a non-negative, self-adjoint operator on $L^2(\Omega)$. By
the spectral theorem for self-adjoint operators (or equivalently, by the Hille–Y
osida theorem), $-A = \kappa_0 \Delta^N$ generates a strongly continuous **analy
tic** semigroup $\{e^{t(-A)}\}_{t \geq 0} = \{e^{-tA}\}_{t \geq 0}$ on $L^2(\Ome
ga)$.

The Cauchy problem rewrites as the abstract evolution equation:
$$
v_t(t) + A\,v(t) = f(t), \qquad v(0) = v_0, \tag{$\star$}
$$
where $f(t) := \nu_1\,(\log \tilde{w})_t(t) \in L^2(0,T; L^2(\Omega))$ by assump
tion.

---

### Step 2. Existence of a Mild Solution

Since $-A$ generates a $C_0$-semigroup $\{e^{-tA}\}_{t\ge 0}$ on $L^2(\Omega)$,
$v_0 \in L^2(\Omega)$ (as $H^1(\Omega) \hookrightarrow L^2(\Omega)$), and $f \in
 L^1(0,T; L^2(\Omega))$ (as $L^2 \subset L^1$ on a finite interval), the **varia
tion of constants formula** (Duhamel's principle) yields a unique **mild solutio
n** (Pazy, *Semigroups of Linear Operators*, Thm 1.2–1.3):

$$
\boxed{v(t) = e^{-tA}\,v_0 + \int_0^t e^{-(t-s)A}\,f(s)\,ds, \qquad t \in [0,T].
} \tag{1}
$$

This solution belongs to $C\bigl([0,T];\, L^2(\Omega)\bigr)$.

---

### Step 3. Regularity: Strong Solution

We now upgrade to a **strong solution** using the analyticity of the semigroup a
nd the regularity of the data.

**(a) Regularity of the homogeneous part.** Since $A$ is self-adjoint and non-ne
gative, the form domain of $A$ is $H^1(\Omega)$ (the Neumann form domain). The i
nitial data $v_0 \in H^1(\Omega)$ lies in the form domain. For the analytic semi
group, $e^{-tA}: L^2(\Omega) \to D(A)$ for $t > 0$, and by interpolation/energy
estimates, $e^{-\cdot A}v_0 \in L^2(0,T; D(A)) \cap H^1(0,T; L^2(\Omega))$ when
$v_0 \in D(A^{1/2}) = H^1(\Omega)$. (See, e.g., Henry, *Geometric Theory of Semi
linear Parabolic Equations*, or Tanabe.)

**(b) Regularity of the inhomogeneous part.** Since $f \in L^2(0,T; L^2(\Omega))
$ and the semigroup is analytic, the convolution
$$
g(t) := \int_0^t e^{-(t-s)A}\,f(s)\,ds
$$
satisfies (by maximal $L^2$-regularity of analytic semigroups on Hilbert spaces,
 see de Simon, 1964):
$$
g \in L^2(0,T; D(A)) \cap H^1(0,T; L^2(\Omega)).
$$

Since $D(A) = D(\Delta^N) \subset H^2(\Omega)$, combining (a) and (b):

$$
v \in L^2\bigl(0,T;\, H^2(\Omega)\bigr) \cap H^1\bigl(0,T;\, L^2(\Omega)\bigr) \
hookrightarrow C\bigl([0,T];\, H^1(\Omega)\bigr). \tag{2}
$$

This means $v$ is a **strong solution**: the equation $v_t - \kappa_0 \Delta^N v
 = \nu_1(\log \tilde{w})_t$ holds in $L^2(\Omega)$ for a.e. $t \in (0,T]$, and $
v(0) = v_0$ in $H^1(\Omega)$.

---

### Step 4. Uniqueness

Suppose $v_1, v_2$ are two strong solutions. Set $w := v_1 - v_2$. Then $w$ sati
sfies:
$$
w_t + A\,w = 0, \qquad w(0) = 0.
$$
Taking the $L^2$-inner product with $w$ and using the Neumann boundary condition
 (so that $\langle -\Delta^N w, w\rangle_{L^2} = \|\nabla w\|_{L^2}^2$ by integr
ation by parts):
$$
\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|w(t)\|_{L^2}^2 + \kappa_0\,\|\nabla w(t)\|_{L^2}^2 = 0
\quad \text{for a.e. } t.
$$
Since $\kappa_0 > 0$ and $\|\nabla w\|_{L^2}^2 \geq 0$:
$$
\frac{d}{dt}\|w(t)\|_{L^2}^2 \leq 0.
$$
With $\|w(0)\|_{L^2} = 0$, Grönwall's inequality (or direct integration) gives $
\|w(t)\|_{L^2} = 0$ for all $t \in [0,T]$. Hence $w \equiv 0$ and $v_1 = v_2$.

---

### Step 5. Continuous Dependence on Data

For two solutions $v, \hat{v}$ with data $v_0, \hat{v}_0$ and sources $f, \hat{f
}$, the energy estimate gives:
$$
\|v(t) - \hat{v}(t)\|_{L^2}^2 \leq \|v_0 - \hat{v}_0\|_{L^2}^2 + \int_0^t \|f(s)
 - \hat{f}(s)\|_{L^2}\,\|v(s)-\hat{v}(s)\|_{L^2}\,ds,
$$
which by Grönwall yields continuous dependence on $(v_0, f)$ in the $L^2$-norm.

---

### Step 6. $L^\infty$-preservation (bonus regularity)

Since $v_0 \in L^\infty(\Omega)$ and the semigroup $e^{-tA}$ generated by the Ne
umann Laplacian is **positivity-preserving and $L^\infty$-contractive** (by the
maximum principle / Beurling–Deny theory for symmetric Markov semigroups), and i
f $f \in L^1(0,T; L^\infty(\Omega))$, then:
$$
\|v(t)\|_{L^\infty} \leq \|v_0\|_{L^\infty} + \int_0^t \|f(s)\|_{L^\infty}\,ds,
$$
so $v \in L^\infty(0,T; L^\infty(\Omega))$. This uses the fact that the Neumann
heat semigroup is a symmetric sub-Markov semigroup, hence $L^\infty$-contractive
.

---

### Summary

**Theorem.** Under the assumptions that $\Omega$ is a bounded smooth domain, $\k
appa_0 > 0$, $(\log \tilde{w})_t \in L^2(0,T; L^2(\Omega))$, and $v_0 \in H^1(\O
mega) \cap L^\infty(\Omega)$, the Cauchy problem admits a **unique strong soluti
on**
$$
v \in L^2\bigl(0,T;\, H^2(\Omega)\bigr) \cap H^1\bigl(0,T;\, L^2(\Omega)\bigr) \
cap C\bigl([0,T];\, H^1(\Omega)\bigr),
$$
given explicitly by the **variation of constants formula**:
$$
v(t) = e^{t\kappa_0 \Delta^N}\,v_0 + \nu_1 \int_0^t e^{(t-s)\kappa_0 \Delta^N}\,
(\log \tilde{w})_t(s)\,ds.
$$
The solution depends continuously on the data, and if additionally $(\log\tilde{
w})_t \in L^1(0,T; L^\infty(\Omega))$, then $v \in L^\infty(0,T; L^\infty(\Omega
))$.
