## Proof

If $d = \gcd(x, y)$, then $d^2 \mid (x^2 - 101y^2) = 5$. Since $5$ is prime, $d^
2 \mid 5$ forces $d = 1$.

**Step 2: Reduction to positive solutions.**

If $(x, y)$ is a solution, so is $(\pm x, \pm y)$. The cases $y = 0$ (giving $x^
2 = 5$) and $x = 0$ (giving $-101y^2 = 5$) yield no integer solutions. Thus it s
uffices to show there are no solutions with $x, y > 0$.

**Step 3: Apply Legendre's theorem — any solution must be a convergent of $\sqrt
{101}$.**

Suppose $x, y > 0$ with $x^2 - 101y^2 = 5$. Then $x/y > \sqrt{101}$, so $x/y + \
sqrt{101} > 2\sqrt{101}$. We compute:

$$\left|\frac{x}{y} - \sqrt{101}\right| = \frac{|x^2 - 101y^2|}{y^2\!\left(\frac
{x}{y} + \sqrt{101}\right)} = \frac{5}{y^2\!\left(\frac{x}{y} + \sqrt{101}\right
)} < \frac{5}{y^2 \cdot 2\sqrt{101}} = \frac{5}{2\sqrt{101}} \cdot \frac{1}{y^2}
.$$

Since $5 < \sqrt{101}$ (as $25 < 101$), we get $\frac{5}{2\sqrt{101}} < \frac{1}
{2}$, hence:

$$\left|\frac{x}{y} - \sqrt{101}\right| < \frac{1}{2y^2}.$$

By **Legendre's theorem**, $x/y$ must be a convergent of the continued fraction
expansion of $\sqrt{101}$.

**Step 4: Compute the continued fraction of $\sqrt{101}$ and the norms of its co
nvergents.**

Since $10^2 = 100 < 101 < 121 = 11^2$, we have $a_0 = \lfloor\sqrt{101}\rfloor =

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{101} - 10} = \frac{\sqrt{101} + 10}{101 - 100} = \sqrt{101} + 1
0, \qquad \lfloor \sqrt{101}+10 \rfloor = 20,$$

$$\frac{1}{(\sqrt{101}+10) - 20} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{101}-10} = \sqrt{101}+10.$$

The process repeats immediately, so $\sqrt{101} = [10;\, \overline{20}]$ with pe
riod $1$.

The convergents $p_n/q_n$ satisfy the recurrence $p_n = 20\,p_{n-1} + p_{n-2}$,
$\;q_n = 20\,q_{n-1} + q_{n-2}$, with $p_{-1}=1, q_{-1}=0, p_0=10, q_0=1$. Compu

| $n$ | $p_n$ | $q_n$ | $p_n^2 - 101\,q_n^2$ |

| 2   | 4030   | 401    | $16240900 - 16240901 = -1$ |
| 3   | 80801  | 8040   | $+1$ (by pattern)     |

By induction (using the recurrence and the periodicity of the continued fraction

$$p_n^2 - 101\,q_n^2 = (-1)^{n+1} \quad \text{for all } n \geq 0.$$

Thus every convergent of $\sqrt{101}$ produces norm $\pm 1$, and **never** $5$.

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{x^2 - 101\,y^2 = 5 \text{ has no integer solutions.}}$$
