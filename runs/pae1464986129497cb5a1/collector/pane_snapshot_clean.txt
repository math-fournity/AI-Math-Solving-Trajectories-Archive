## Proof

**Key observation.** The condition $\int_{\Omega} |v(x)|^2\,dx = 0$ combined wit
h the regularity of $v$ forces $v \equiv 0$ on $\Omega$.

**Step 1: The $L^2$-norm condition forces $v = 0$ pointwise on $\Omega$.**

Since $v \in C^2(\Omega)$, the function $|v|^2$ is continuous and non-negative o
n $\Omega$. Suppose for contradiction that there exists $x_0 \in \Omega$ with $|
v(x_0)|^2 > 0$. By continuity of $|v|^2$, there exists $r > 0$ such that $B(x_0,
 r) \subset \Omega$ and
$$|v(x)|^2 \geq \frac{|v(x_0)|^2}{2} > 0 \quad \text{for all } x \in B(x_0, r).$
$
Then
$$\int_{\Omega} |v(x)|^2\,dx \geq \int_{B(x_0,r)} |v(x)|^2\,dx \geq \frac{|v(x_0
)|^2}{2} \cdot \operatorname{vol}(B(x_0,r)) > 0,$$
which contradicts $\int_{\Omega} |v(x)|^2\,dx = 0$. Therefore $v(x) = 0$ for eve
ry $x \in \Omega$.

**Step 2: The line integral vanishes.**

Since $\gamma: [0,1] \to \Omega$ and $v \equiv 0$ on $\Omega$, we have $v(\gamma
(t)) = 0$ for all $t \in [0,1]$. Hence
$$\int_0^1 v(\gamma(t)) \cdot \dot{\gamma}(t)\,dt = \int_0^1 0 \cdot \dot{\gamma
}(t)\,dt = 0.$$

**Step 3: The bound holds.**

For every admissible $v$, we have shown
$$\left|\int_0^1 v(\gamma(t)) \cdot \dot{\gamma}(t)\,dt\right| = |0| = 0 \leq C$
$
for **any** $C > 0$.

**Conclusion.** Yes, such a constant $C > 0$ exists. In fact, the line integral
is identically zero for every $v$ satisfying the hypotheses, so any positive con
stant (e.g., $C = 1$) serves as a valid bound. The condition $\operatorname{curl
} v = 0$ plays no role; the essential point is that the vanishing $L^2$-norm, to
gether with the continuity of $v$ on $\Omega$, forces $v \equiv 0$ pointwise.
