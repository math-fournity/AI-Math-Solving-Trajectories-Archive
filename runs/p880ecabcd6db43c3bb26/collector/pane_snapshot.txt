## Proof

Let $S = \{1, 2, \dots, 2018\}$ and $T = \{ab : a, b \in S,\ a \ne b\}$.

**Step 1: The 2017 smallest elements of $T$ are $2, 3, \dots, 2018$.**

For every $k \in \{2, 3, \dots, 2018\}$, we have $k = 1 \cdot k$ with $1, k \in
S$ and $1 \ne k$, so $k \in T$. This gives 2017 distinct elements $\{2, 3, \dots
, 2018\} \subseteq T$.

No element of $T$ is smaller than $2$: the smallest product of two distinct posi
tive integers is $1 \cdot 2 = 2$. Hence the 2017 smallest elements of $T$ are ex
actly $2, 3, \dots, 2018$.

**Step 2: The 2018th smallest element of $T$ is the smallest element of $T$ grea
ter than $2018$.**

We seek $\min\{ab : a, b \in S,\ a \ne b,\ ab > 2018\}$.

- $a = 1$: $1 \cdot b \le 2018$ for all $b \in S$, so no product exceeds $2018$.
- $a = 2$: smallest $b$ with $2b > 2018$ is $b = 1010$, giving $2 \cdot 1010 = 2
020$.
- $a = 3$: smallest $b$ with $3b > 2018$ is $b = 673$, giving $3 \cdot 673 = 201
9$.
- $a = 4$: $4 \cdot 505 = 2020$.
- $a \ge 5$: the minimum product exceeding $2018$ is at least $2020$ (e.g., $5 \
cdot 404 = 2020$, $6 \cdot 337 = 2022$, $7 \cdot 289 = 2023$, and larger $a$ onl
y increases the product).

Thus the smallest product in $T$ exceeding $2018$ is $3 \cdot 673 = 2019$, with
$3, 673 \in S$ distinct.

**Conclusion.** The 2017 smallest members of $T$ are $2, 3, \dots, 2018$, and th
e next smallest is $2019$. Therefore the 2018th smallest member of $T$ is $\boxe
d{2019}$.
