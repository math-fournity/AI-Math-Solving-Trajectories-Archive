the $\mathfrak{p}$-adic valuation of a nonzero ideal $J$, so that $J =
\prod_{\mathfrak{p}} \mathfrak{p}^{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(J)}$. A prime $\mathfrak{p}$
appears in the factorization of $J$ iff $v_{\mathfrak{p}}(J) \geq 1$.

Key fact (ideal sum = gcd). In a Dedekind domain, the sum of two ideals
corresponds to taking the minimum of valuations: $$v_{\mathfrak{p}}(I + (a)) =
\min\big(v_{\mathfrak{p}}(I),; v_{\mathfrak{p}}((a))\big).$$

Step 1: Every prime in $\mathcal{P}$ divides $I$.

A prime $\mathfrak{p}$ belongs to $\mathcal{P}$ iff it appears in the
factorization of $(I,a)$ or $(I,b)$, i.e., iff $v_{\mathfrak{p}}(I+(a)) \geq 1$

v_{\mathfrak{p}}((a))) \geq 1$, which forces $v_{\mathfrak{p}}(I) \geq 1$.

If $v_{\mathfrak{p}}(I+(b)) \geq 1$, then $\min(v_{\mathfrak{p}}(I),
v_{\mathfrak{p}}((b))) \geq 1$, which forces $v_{\mathfrak{p}}(I) \geq 1$.
