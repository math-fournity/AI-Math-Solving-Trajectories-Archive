## Proof

We seek the smallest order of a finite group $G$ containing elements of every or
der $1, 2, \dots, 12$.

**Lower bound.** By Lagrange's theorem, the order of any element of $G$ divides
$|G|$. Therefore, for $G$ to contain an element of order $k$, we must have $k \m
id |G|$. Since $G$ must contain elements of orders $1, 2, \dots, 12$, the order
$|G|$ must be divisible by every integer from $1$ to $12$, hence by their least
common multiple:

$$\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\dots,12) = 2^3 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 \cdot 11 = 8
\cdot 9 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 \cdot 11 = 27720.$$

(Here $2^3$ comes from $8$, $3^2$ from $9$, and $5, 7, 11$ are the primes in the
 range; all other values $1$–$12$ divide this product.) Thus $|G| \geq 27720$.

**Upper bound (construction).** The cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}/27720\mathbb{Z}$ ha
s order $27720$. A cyclic group of order $n$ has exactly one subgroup of each or
der $d \mid n$, and in particular contains elements of every order $d \mid n$. S
ince every integer $1, 2, \dots, 12$ divides $27720 = \operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots
,12)$, the cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}/27720\mathbb{Z}$ contains elements of every
order from $1$ to $12$.

**Conclusion.** The lower bound $27720$ is achieved by the cyclic group of order
 $27720$, so the smallest possible order is

$$\boxed{27720}.$$
