We compute $I = \int_{\mathbb{R}-10i} \frac{e^{iz}}{1+z^2}\,dz$, where the conto
ur is the horizontal line $\operatorname{Im}(z)=-10$, oriented left to right.

### Poles

The integrand $f(z)=\frac{e^{iz}}{1+z^2}=\frac{e^{iz}}{(z-i)(z+i)}$ has simple p
oles at $z=i$ and $z=-i$. Both lie **above** the contour since $\operatorname{Im
}(i)=1>-10$ and $\operatorname{Im}(-i)=-1>-10$.

### Closing the contour

Consider the rectangle with vertices $-R-10i,\; R-10i,\; R+Hi,\; -R+Hi$ for $H>1
$, traversed counterclockwise. This encloses both poles.

- **Bottom side** ($z=x-10i$, $x:-R\to R$): tends to $I$ as $R\to\infty$.
- **Right/left sides** ($z=\pm R+iy$, $y\in[-10,H]$): $|f(z)|\le \frac{e^{-y}}{R
^2/2}$ for large $R$, so the integral is $O(1/R^2)\to 0$.
- **Top side** ($z=x+Hi$, $x:R\to -R$): tends to $-e^{-H}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{e^{ix}}{1+(x+Hi)^2}\,dx$.

For the top integral, the poles of $\frac{1}{1+(x+Hi)^2}$ in the $x$-plane are a
t $x=i(-H\pm1)$, both with negative imaginary part (since $H>1$). Closing the $x
$-contour in the **upper** half-plane (where $e^{ix}$ decays) encloses no poles,
 so by Jordan's lemma the integral is **zero**. Thus the top side contributes $0
$.

### Residue computation

By the residue theorem (counterclockwise orientation):

$$I = 2\pi i\left[\operatorname{Res}_{z=i}f(z)+\operatorname{Res}_{z=-i}f(z)\rig
ht].$$

**At $z=i$:**
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=i} = \frac{e^{i\cdot i}}{i+i} = \frac{e^{-1}}{2i}.$$

**At $z=-i$:**
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=-i} = \frac{e^{i(-i)}}{-i-i} = \frac{e^{1}}{-2i} = -\fra
c{e}{2i}.$$

### Final result

$$I = 2\pi i\left(\frac{e^{-1}}{2i}-\frac{e}{2i}\right) = 2\pi i\cdot\frac{e^{-1
}-e}{2i} = \pi\left(\frac{1}{e}-e\right).$$

**Consistency check:** When the contour is the real axis ($\operatorname{Im}=0$)
, only $z=i$ is enclosed, giving $\frac{\pi}{e}$ (the standard Fourier transform
 result). Shifting the contour down to $\operatorname{Im}=-10$ additionally encl
oses $z=-i$, adding $2\pi i\cdot\frac{-e}{2i}=-\pi e$. The total $\frac{\pi}{e}-
\pi e$ is consistent.

$$\boxed{I = \pi\!\left(\frac{1}{e}-e\right) = \frac{\pi}{e}-\pi e.}$$
