## Proof

The equality does **not** hold in general. We provide an explicit counterexample
.

**Counterexample.** Set $a = 0$ and $c = -2$, and define $M(s) = e^{-s}$.

**Verification that $\lim_{x\to\infty} M(x) = a$:** Since $M(s) = e^{-s} \to 0 =
 a$ as $s \to \infty$, the hypothesis is satisfied.

**Right-hand side.** We compute
$$\int_1^x s^c\,ds = \int_1^x s^{-2}\,ds = \left[-\frac{1}{s}\right]_1^x = 1 - \
frac{1}{x} \xrightarrow{x\to\infty} 1.$$
Hence
$$\limsup_{x\to\infty}\int_1^x s^{-2}\,ds = 1,$$
and therefore
$$a\limsup_{x\to\infty}\int_1^x s^{-2}\,ds = 0 \cdot 1 = 0.$$

**Left-hand side.** We compute
$$\int_1^x M(s)\,s^c\,ds = \int_1^x e^{-s}\,s^{-2}\,ds.$$
Since $e^{-s}s^{-2}$ is positive and continuous on $[1,\infty)$ and integrable (
as $e^{-s}$ decays exponentially), the improper integral converges:
$$\lim_{x\to\infty}\int_1^x e^{-s}\,s^{-2}\,ds = \int_1^\infty e^{-s}\,s^{-2}\,d
s.$$
The integrand $e^{-s}s^{-2}$ is strictly positive for all $s \ge 1$, so
$$\int_1^\infty e^{-s}\,s^{-2}\,ds > 0.$$
Since the limit exists (and is finite), the limsup equals the limit:
$$\limsup_{x\to\infty}\int_1^x M(s)\,s^c\,ds = \int_1^\infty e^{-s}\,s^{-2}\,ds
> 0.$$

**Conclusion.** We have
$$\limsup_{x\to\infty}\int_1^x M(s)\,s^c\,ds > 0 = a\limsup_{x\to\infty}\int_1^x
 s^c\,ds,$$
so the proposed equality fails.

**Remark on the mechanism.** The failure occurs in the regime $c < -1$, where $s
^c$ is integrable on $[1,\infty)$. In this case both $\int_1^x s^c\,ds$ and $\in
t_1^x M(s)s^c\,ds$ converge to finite limits. Writing
$$\int_1^x M(s)\,s^c\,ds = a\int_1^x s^c\,ds + \int_1^x \bigl(M(s)-a\bigr)\,s^c\
,ds,$$
the second integral converges to $\int_1^\infty (M(s)-a)\,s^c\,ds$, which is a f
inite constant that depends on the *rate* at which $M(s)\to a$, not just on the
limit $a$ itself. This residual term vanishes only when $M(s) = a$ for all suffi
ciently large $s$ (or when the weighted integral of $M(s)-a$ happens to be zero)
, neither of which is guaranteed by the hypothesis $M(s)\to a$ alone. Thus the e
quality does not hold in general. $\blacksquare$
