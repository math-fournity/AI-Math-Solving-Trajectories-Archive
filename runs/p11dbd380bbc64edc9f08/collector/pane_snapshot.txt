**Unfolding Method.** When light bounces between two reflective panels forming a
ngle θ, we "unfold" the path by reflecting the wedge across its sides. The light

Since θ_n = 2π/(n+2), exactly **n+2 copies** of the wedge tile the full 2π aroun
d the vertex. The boundary rays are at angles 0, θ, 2θ, …, (n+1)θ.

**Counting Reflections.** The light enters the wedge from the open end (far from
 the vertex). In the unfolded picture, it is a ray (half-line) starting at a poi
nt P in sector 0 (between angles 0 and θ), traveling toward the vertex region.

As the ray passes close to the vertex, its angular range (as seen from the verte
x) approaches **π**. The boundary rays crossed are those kθ strictly inside the
angular range (φ, φ+π), where φ ∈ (0, θ) is the angle of P.

**Maximizing.** The rays crossed are kθ for k = 1, 2, …, j_max, where j_max is t
he largest integer with j_max·θ < φ + π.

Since φ + π ∈ (π, π + θ), the binding constraint is j_max·θ < π + θ, giving j_ma
x < (n+4)/2.

- **n even:** (n+4)/2 is an integer, so j_max = (n+2)/2. (Achievable with any φ
∈ (0, θ).)
- **n odd:** (n+4)/2 is not an integer, so j_max = ⌊(n+4)/2⌋ = (n+3)/2. (Achieva
ble with φ ∈ (θ/2, θ).)
