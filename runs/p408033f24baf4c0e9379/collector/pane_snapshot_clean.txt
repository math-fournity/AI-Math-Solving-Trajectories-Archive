## Proof

**Step 1: $f$ is constant by the maximum/minimum modulus principle.**

Since $f$ is holomorphic on $D$, continuous on $\overline{D}$, and $|f(z)|=1$ fo
r all $|z|=1$, the **maximum modulus principle** gives
$$|f(z)| \le 1 \quad \text{for all } z \in \overline{D}.$$

Since $f$ has **no zeros in $D$**, the function $g := 1/f$ is also holomorphic o
n $D$ and continuous on $\overline{D}$. On the boundary $|z|=1$,
$$|g(z)| = \frac{1}{|f(z)|} = 1.$$
Applying the maximum modulus principle to $g$,
$$|g(z)| \le 1 \quad \text{for all } z \in \overline{D},$$
that is, $|f(z)| \ge 1$ for all $z \in \overline{D}$.

Combining the two inequalities, $|f(z)| = 1$ for every $z \in \overline{D}$.

**Step 2: A holomorphic function with constant modulus is constant.**

Write $f = u + iv$. Since $|f|^2 = u^2 + v^2 = 1$ is constant, differentiating v
ia the Cauchy–Riemann equations (or equivalently, using $\Delta |f|^2 = 4|f'|^2
\ge 0$, which must vanish since $|f|^2$ is constant) yields
$$f'(z) = 0 \quad \text{for all } z \in D.$$
Hence $f$ is constant on $D$, and by continuity, constant on $\overline{D}$.

**Step 3: Determine the constant.**

Since $|f|\equiv 1$, the constant has the form $f \equiv e^{i\theta}$ for some $
\theta \in \mathbb{R}$. The condition $f(-1)=1$ forces $e^{i\theta}=1$, so $\the
ta \equiv 0 \pmod{2\pi}$.

Therefore,
$$\boxed{f(z) \equiv 1.}$$

**Verification.** The constant function $f(z)\equiv 1$ is holomorphic on $D$, co
ntinuous on $\overline{D}$, satisfies $|f(z)|=1$ for $|z|=1$, $f(-1)=1$, and has
 no zeros in $D$. All conditions are met.

### PROOF COMPLETE
