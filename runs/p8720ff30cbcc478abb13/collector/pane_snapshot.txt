Looking at this problem, I need to classify a first-order linear system of PDEs
in two different 2D projections.

## Setup

Let $U = (u, v)^T$. The system becomes:

$$\partial_t U + A\,\partial_x U + B\,\partial_y U = 0$$

where

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B = \begin{pmatrix}
-1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

For a first-order system $U_t + M\,U_s = 0$ in variables $(t,s)$, the **characte
ristic equation** is

$$\det(\phi_t\, I + \phi_s\, M) = 0.$$

- If real characteristic directions exist → **hyperbolic** (well-posed Cauchy pr
oblem).
- If no real characteristics exist (the form is definite) → **elliptic** (Cauchy
 problem ill-posed; boundary-value problem appropriate).

Equivalently, the system is hyperbolic in $(t,s)$ iff $M$ has real eigenvalues,
and elliptic iff $M$ has non-real eigenvalues.

---

## Classification in $(t, x)$ space

Here $y$ is treated as a parameter and the relevant matrix is $A$. The character
istic equation for surfaces $\phi(t,x)=\text{const}$ is:

$$\det(\phi_t I + \phi_x A) = \det\begin{pmatrix} \phi_t & \phi_x \\ -\phi_x & \
phi_t \end{pmatrix} = \phi_t^2 + \phi_x^2 = 0.$$

For any **real** $(\phi_t, \phi_x) \neq (0,0)$, we have $\phi_t^2 + \phi_x^2 > 0
$. Therefore **no real characteristics exist**.

Consistent with this, the eigenvalues of $A$ satisfy $\lambda^2 + 1 = 0$, giving
 $\lambda = \pm i$ (purely imaginary, non-real).

**Conclusion: The system is elliptic in $(t,x)$ space.** The Cauchy problem (pre
scribing $u,v$ on a curve in the $(t,x)$-plane) is ill-posed; instead one expect
s a boundary-value formulation.

---

## Classification in $(t, y)$ space

Here $x$ is treated as a parameter and the relevant matrix is $B$. The character
istic equation for surfaces $\phi(t,y)=\text{const}$ is:

$$\det(\phi_t I + \phi_y B) = \det\begin{pmatrix} \phi_t - \phi_y & 0 \\ 0 & \ph
i_t + \phi_y \end{pmatrix} = (\phi_t - \phi_y)(\phi_t + \phi_y) = \phi_t^2 - \ph
i_y^2 = 0.$$

This factors over the reals as $\phi_t = \pm\,\phi_y$, yielding two real charact
eristic families:

$$t + y = \text{const}, \qquad t - y = \text{const}.$$

Consistent with this, the eigenvalues of $B$ are $\lambda = -1$ and $\lambda = +
1$ (real and distinct).

**Conclusion: The system is strictly hyperbolic in $(t,y)$ space.** The Cauchy p
roblem is well-posed. The two characteristic speeds are $dy/dt = -1$ and $dy/dt
= +1$, corresponding to left- and right-going waves in the $y$-direction.

---

## Summary

| Projection | Relevant matrix | Eigenvalues | Real characteristics? | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $(t, x)$ | $A = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ | $
\pm i$ | No ($\phi_t^2+\phi_x^2=0$ has no nontrivial real solution) | **Elliptic
** |
| $(t, y)$ | $B = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ | $
\pm 1$ | Yes ($t\pm y=\text{const}$) | **Strictly hyperbolic** |

The system exhibits a **mixed-type character**: it is elliptic when viewed in th
e $(t,x)$-plane and hyperbolic when viewed in the $(t,y)$-plane. This reflects t
he fact that $A$ is a rotation matrix (generating elliptic behavior, akin to the
 Cauchy–Riemann equations) while $B$ is a reflection/hyperbolic matrix (generati
ng wave-like propagation along $t \pm y = \text{const}$).
