## Proof

We show that $j(X')$ is dense in $Y'$ in the weak* topology $\sigma(Y',Y)$.

**Setup recap.** We have a continuous inclusion $i:(Y,\|\cdot\|_2)\hookrightarro
w (X,\|\cdot\|_1)$ with $Y$ dense in $X$. The restriction map $j:X'\to Y'$, $j(f
)=f|_Y$, is well-defined (continuity of $i$ makes every $f\in X'$ continuous on
$Y$), injective (density of $Y$ in $X$ means $f|_Y=0\Rightarrow f=0$), and conti
nuous in dual norm.

**Key criterion (Hahn–Banach separation).** For a linear subspace $M\subseteq Y'
$, its $\sigma(Y',Y)$-closure equals
$$(M^\perp)^\perp := \{h\in Y' : h(y)=0\ \text{for all } y\in M^\perp\},\qquad M
^\perp:=\{y\in Y : g(y)=0\ \forall\, g\in M\}.$$
This is the bipolar theorem applied to the dual pair $(Y',Y)$: the closure of $M
$ in $\sigma(Y',Y)$ is the double annihilator. Consequently,
$$M \text{ is } \sigma(Y',Y)\text{-dense in } Y' \iff M^\perp=\{0\}.$$

**Apply to $M=j(X')$.** We compute
$$M^\perp = \{y\in Y : f(y)=0 \text{ for all } f\in X'\}.$$
Since $Y\subseteq X$, every $y\in M^\perp$ is an element of $X$ annihilated by a
ll of $X'$. By the Hahn–Banach theorem, for any nonzero $x\in X$ there exists $f
\in X'$ with $f(x)\neq 0$ (e.g. a norming functional with $f(x)=\|x\|_1$). Hence
 the only element of $X$ annihilated by all of $X'$ is $0$, and therefore
$$M^\perp = \{0\}.$$

**Conclusion.** By the criterion above, $j(X')$ is $\sigma(Y',Y)$-dense in $Y'$.

**Answer:** Yes, $X'$ is dense in $Y'$ in the weak* topology.
