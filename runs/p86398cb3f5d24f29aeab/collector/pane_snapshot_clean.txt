# Solution

**Answer: No.** In general, a Tychonoff space $X$ need not be a $G_\delta$ subse
t of $\beta X$, even though $X$ is always *dense* in $\beta X$.

## Preliminaries

Recall that a **compactification** of $X$ is a pair $(K, e)$ where $K$ is compac
t Hausdorff and $e: X \to K$ is a dense embedding. By definition, $X$ is dense i
n every compactification, in particular in $\beta X$. So the question reduces to
 whether $X$ is a $G_\delta$ (a countable intersection of open sets) in $\beta X
$.

A space $X$ is called **Čech-complete** if $X$ is a $G_\delta$ subset of some (e
quivalently, every) compactification. The equivalence "some $\Leftrightarrow$ ev
ery" follows from the fact that if $X$ is a $G_\delta$ in one compactification $
K_1$, and $K_2$ is another, the natural map between compactifications preserves
$G_\delta$-ness of the embedded copy of $X$. Thus:

> $X$ is a $G_\delta$ in $\beta X$ $\iff$ $X$ is Čech-complete.

A standard characterization (see Engelhardt, *General Topology*, or the Čech–Bai
re theorem) is:

> A **metrizable** space is Čech-complete $\iff$ it is **completely metrizable**
 (i.e., admits a complete metric compatible with its topology).

## A counterexample: $X = \mathbb{Q}$

Take $X = \mathbb{Q}$ with the subspace topology inherited from $\mathbb{R}$. Th
en:

1. **$\mathbb{Q}$ is Tychonoff.** It is metrizable (subspace of a metrizable spa
ce), and every metrizable space is Tychonoff.

2. **$\mathbb{Q}$ is not completely metrizable.** Suppose, for contradiction, th
at $\mathbb{Q}$ admitted a complete metric $d$ inducing its topology. Then $(\ma
thbb{Q}, d)$ would be a complete metric space, hence a **Baire space** by the Ba
ire Category Theorem. But:
   - $\mathbb{Q}$ is countable: $\mathbb{Q} = \{q_1, q_2, q_3, \dots\}$.
   - $\mathbb{Q}$ has **no isolated points** (every neighborhood of a rational c
ontains other rationals).
   - Each singleton $\{q_n\}$ is closed and has empty interior (since $q_n$ is n
ot isolated), so each $\{q_n\}$ is **nowhere dense**.
   - Hence $\mathbb{Q} = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} \{q_n\}$ is a countable union of
 nowhere dense sets, i.e., $\mathbb{Q}$ is **meager in itself**.

   A Baire space cannot be meager in itself (by definition, a Baire space is one
 in which countable unions of nowhere dense sets have empty interior, yet $\math
bb{Q}$ itself has nonempty interior — namely all of $\mathbb{Q}$). This is a con
tradiction.

   Therefore $\mathbb{Q}$ is **not** completely metrizable.

3. **Consequence.** Since $\mathbb{Q}$ is metrizable but not completely metrizab
le, by the characterization above $\mathbb{Q}$ is **not Čech-complete**. Hence $
\mathbb{Q}$ is **not** a $G_\delta$ subset of $\beta\mathbb{Q}$.

## Conclusion

$\mathbb{Q}$ is a Tychonoff space, it is dense in $\beta\mathbb{Q}$ (as in any c
ompactification), but it is **not** a $G_\delta$ subset of $\beta\mathbb{Q}$. Th
erefore the statement "$X$ is a dense $G_\delta$ subset of $\beta X$" is **false
 in general**.

The statement becomes true precisely for the class of **Čech-complete** spaces —
 a proper subclass of Tychonoff spaces that includes all locally compact Hausdor
ff spaces and all completely metrizable spaces, but excludes spaces like $\mathb
b{Q}$.
