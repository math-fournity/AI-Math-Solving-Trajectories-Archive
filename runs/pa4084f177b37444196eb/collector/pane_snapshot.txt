**Claim:** $x = z$. The statement is true.

**Proof.**

Let $X$ be a Banach space with continuous dual $X^*$. By hypothesis:
- $x_n \xrightarrow{w} x$, i.e. for every $f \in X^*$, $f(x_n) \to f(x)$;
- $x_n \xrightarrow{s} z$, i.e. $\|x_n - z\| \to 0$.

We show weak limits are unique, which immediately forces $x = z$.

**Step 1. Strong convergence implies weak convergence.**

For any $f \in X^*$, by definition of the operator norm,
$$
|f(x_n) - f(z)| = |f(x_n - z)| \le \|f\| \cdot \|x_n - z\|.
$$
Since $\|x_n - z\| \to 0$ and $\|f\|$ is a fixed finite constant, $f(x_n) - f(z)
 \to 0$, i.e. $f(x_n) \to f(z)$. Thus $x_n \xrightarrow{w} z$.

**Step 2. Uniqueness of weak limits.**

Suppose $x_n \xrightarrow{w} x$ and $x_n \xrightarrow{w} z$. Then for every $f \
in X^*$,
$$
f(x) = \lim_{n\to\infty} f(x_n) = f(z),
$$
so $f(x - z) = 0$ for all $f \in X^*$.

**Step 3. Apply the Hahn–Banach separation theorem.**

If $x - z \neq 0$, then $\{x - z\}$ is a closed, convex, nonempty proper subset
of $X$, and $\{0\}$ is a point outside it (since $\|x-z\| > 0$). By the Hahn–Ban
ach separation theorem (geometric form, valid on any normed space, hence on the
Banach space $X$), there exists $f \in X^*$ and $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ with
$$
\operatorname{Re} f(x - z) \ge \alpha > \operatorname{Re} f(0) = 0.
$$
In particular $f(x - z) \neq 0$, contradicting $f(x - z) = 0$ for all $f \in X^*
$.

(Equivalently, one may use the analytic corollary of Hahn–Banach: for any nonzer
o $y \in X$ there exists $f \in X^*$ with $\|f\| = 1$ and $f(y) = \|y\| \neq 0$;
 applied to $y = x - z$ this gives the same contradiction.)

Therefore $x - z = 0$, i.e. $x = z$.

**Conclusion.** Combining Steps 1–3: strong convergence $x_n \xrightarrow{s} z$
implies weak convergence $x_n \xrightarrow{w} z$, and weak limits are unique by
Hahn–Banach, so the weak limit $x$ must equal the strong limit $z$.
