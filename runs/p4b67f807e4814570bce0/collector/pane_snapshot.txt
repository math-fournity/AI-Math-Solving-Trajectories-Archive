# Proof: Every finite digraph has a unique (up to isomorphism) irreducible reduc
tion

**Answer: Yes.**

## Definitions

A **retract** of a digraph $G$ is a subdigraph $H \subseteq G$ for which there e
xists a homomorphism $r: G \to H$ (a **retraction**) with $r|_H = \mathrm{id}_H$
.

A digraph is **irreducible** (a **core**) if it has no proper retract (i.e., no
retract other than itself).

An **irreducible reduction** of $G$ is a retract of $G$ that is itself irreducib
le.

## Key Lemma

**Lemma.** A finite digraph $H$ is irreducible if and only if every endomorphism
 of $H$ is an automorphism.

*Proof of Lemma.*

$(\Leftarrow)$: If every endomorphism is an automorphism, then any retraction $r
: H \to H'$ onto a proper subdigraph $H' \subsetneq H$ would yield an endomorphi
sm $r: H \to H$ that is not surjective (since $r(H) = H' \subsetneq H$), contrad
icting the assumption. So $H$ is irreducible.

$(\Rightarrow)$: Suppose $H$ is irreducible and let $\varphi: H \to H$ be an end
omorphism. Consider the descending chain of subdigraphs:
$$H \supseteq \varphi(H) \supseteq \varphi^2(H) \supseteq \cdots$$
Since $H$ is finite, this chain stabilizes: there exists $n$ with $H' := \varphi
^n(H) = \varphi^{n+1}(H) = \cdots$. The restriction $\varphi|_{H'}: H' \to H'$ i
s surjective (since $\varphi(H') = H'$), hence bijective (as $H'$ is finite), so
 it is an automorphism $\sigma$ of $H'$.

Define $r = \sigma^{-1} \circ \varphi^n: H \to H'$. Then $r|_{H'} = \sigma^{-1}
\circ \sigma = \mathrm{id}_{H'}$, so $r$ is a retraction from $H$ onto $H'$. Sin
ce $H$ is irreducible, $H' = H$, meaning $\varphi^n(H) = H$. In particular $\var
phi(H) \supseteq \varphi^n(H) = H$, so $\varphi(H) = H$, i.e., $\varphi$ is surj
ective. Since $H$ is finite, $\varphi$ is bijective, hence an automorphism. $\sq
uare$

## Existence of an irreducible reduction

Let $G$ be a finite digraph. If $G$ is irreducible, then $G$ itself is an irredu
cible reduction. Otherwise, $G$ has a proper retract $H_1$ with $|H_1| < |G|$. I
f $H_1$ is irreducible, we are done. If not, $H_1$ has a proper retract $H_2$ wi
th $|H_2| < |H_1|$. Continuing, we obtain a strictly decreasing sequence $|G| >
|H_1| > |H_2| > \cdots \geq 1$, which must terminate at some irreducible $H_k$.

Retractions compose: if $r_i: H_{i-1} \to H_i$ is a retraction for each $i$, the
n $r_k \circ r_{k-1} \circ \cdots \circ r_1: G \to H_k$ is a retraction (its res
triction to $H_k$ is the identity, by induction). So $H_k$ is a retract of $G$ a
nd is irreducible—hence an irreducible reduction of $G$.

## Uniqueness up to isomorphism

Suppose $H$ and $K$ are both irreducible reductions of $G$. Then there exist ret
ractions:
- $r: G \to H$ with $r|_H = \mathrm{id}_H$,
- $s: G \to K$ with $s|_K = \mathrm{id}_K$.

Let $i: H \hookrightarrow G$ and $j: K \hookrightarrow G$ be the inclusion homom
orphisms. Define:
$$f = s \circ i: H \to K, \qquad g = r \circ j: K \to H.$$

Both $f$ and $g$ are homomorphisms. Consider the compositions:
- $g \circ f: H \to H$ is an endomorphism of $H$. By the Lemma, since $H$ is irr
educible, $g \circ f$ is an automorphism of $H$.
- $f \circ g: K \to K$ is an endomorphism of $K$. By the Lemma, since $K$ is irr
educible, $f \circ g$ is an automorphism of $K$.

Since $g \circ f$ is bijective, $f$ is injective. Since $f \circ g$ is bijective
, $f$ is surjective. Therefore $f: H \to K$ is a bijective homomorphism, i.e., a
n isomorphism.

Thus $H \cong K$. $\square$

## Conclusion

Every finite digraph possesses an irreducible reduction (by iterative retraction
, terminating by finiteness), and any two irreducible reductions of the same fin
ite digraph are isomorphic (by the Lemma and the composition argument above). Th
erefore:

**Every finite digraph has a unique (up to isomorphism) irreducible reduction.**
