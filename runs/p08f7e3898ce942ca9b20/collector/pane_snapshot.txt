## Solution of Riemann's P-Equation

### Step 1. The Riemann P-Symbol

The given Fuchsian equation has three regular singular points $z_1, z_2, z_3$ wi
th exponent pairs $(\alpha, \alpha')$, $(\beta, \beta')$, $(\gamma, \gamma')$ re
spectively, satisfying the Fuchsian relation

$$\alpha + \alpha' + \beta + \beta' + \gamma + \gamma' = 1.$$

The general solution is encoded by the **Riemann P-symbol**:

$$y(z) = P\left\{\begin{matrix} z_1 & z_2 & z_3 \\ \alpha & \beta & \gamma \\ \a
lpha' & \beta' & \gamma' \end{matrix}\;;\; z \right\},$$

which means: near each $z_k$, the two linearly independent local solutions behav
e as $(z - z_k)^{\text{exponent}}$.

### Step 2. Möbius Transformation to $\{0, 1, \infty\}$

Apply the Möbius (linear fractional) transformation

$$x = \frac{(z - z_1)(z_2 - z_3)}{(z - z_3)(z_2 - z_1)},$$

which maps $z_1 \mapsto 0$, $z_2 \mapsto 1$, $z_3 \mapsto \infty$. Under this ma
p, the exponent pairs are preserved: $(\alpha,\alpha')$ at $x=0$, $(\beta,\beta'
)$ at $x=1$, $(\gamma,\gamma')$ at $x=\infty$ (where the convention at $\infty$
is $y \sim x^{-\gamma}$ or $x^{-\gamma'}$).

### Step 3. Gauge Reduction to the Hypergeometric Equation

The hypergeometric equation $x(1-x)w'' + [c - (a+b+1)x]w' - ab\,w = 0$ has expon
ents $(0,\,1-c)$ at $x=0$, $(0,\,c-a-b)$ at $x=1$, and $(a,\,b)$ at $x=\infty$.
To match the Riemann exponents (which are not zero in general), we perform the *
*gauge transformation**

$$y = x^{\alpha}(1-x)^{\beta}\, w(x).$$

This shifts the exponents:

| Singular point | Exponents of $y$ | Exponents of $w$ |
|---|---|---|
| $x = 0$ | $(\alpha,\, \alpha')$ | $(0,\, \alpha'-\alpha)$ |
| $x = 1$ | $(\beta,\, \beta')$ | $(0,\, \beta'-\beta)$ |
| $x = \infty$ | $(\gamma,\, \gamma')$ | $(\alpha+\beta+\gamma,\, \alpha+\beta+\
gamma')$ |

Matching with the hypergeometric exponents gives the **hypergeometric parameters
**:

$$\boxed{a = \alpha + \beta + \gamma, \qquad b = \alpha + \beta + \gamma', \qqua
d c = 1 + \alpha - \alpha'.}$$

**Consistency check.** The Fuchsian relation for $w$: $0 + (\alpha'-\alpha) + 0
+ (\beta'-\beta) + (\alpha+\beta+\gamma) + (\alpha+\beta+\gamma') = 2\alpha + 2\
beta + \gamma + \gamma' + \alpha' + \beta' - \alpha - \beta = \alpha + \beta + \
gamma + \gamma' + \alpha' + \beta' = 1$. ✓

Also $c - a - b = (1+\alpha-\alpha') - (2\alpha+2\beta+\gamma+\gamma') = 1 - \al
pha - \alpha' - 2\beta - \gamma - \gamma' = \beta' - \beta$ (using the Fuchsian
relation). ✓

### Step 4. Kummer's 24 Solutions

The hypergeometric equation admits **24 local solution representations** (Kummer
, 1834), arising from:
- **6** permutations of assigning $(z_1, z_2, z_3) \to \{0, 1, \infty\}$,
- **4** independent choices of which exponent to factor out at two of the three
points,

giving $6 \times 4 = 24$ representations of the 2-dimensional solution space.

**Solutions expanded around $x = 0$:**

$$w_1 = F(a,\, b;\, c;\, x), \qquad w_2 = x^{1-c}\, F(a-c+1,\, b-c+1;\, 2-c;\, x
).$$

**Solutions expanded around $x = 1$:**

$$w_3 = F(a,\, b;\, a+b-c+1;\, 1-x), \qquad w_4 = (1-x)^{c-a-b}\, F(c-a,\, c-b;\
, c-a-b+1;\, 1-x).$$

**Solutions expanded around $x = \infty$:**

$$w_5 = x^{-a}\, F\!\left(a,\, a-c+1;\, a-b+1;\, \tfrac{1}{x}\right), \qquad w_6
 = x^{-b}\, F\!\left(b,\, b-c+1;\, b-a+1;\, \tfrac{1}{x}\right).$$

Here $F(a,b;c;x) = {}_2F_1(a,b;c;x)$ is the Gauss hypergeometric function. Any t
wo solutions from the same expansion point are linearly independent (provided no
 exponent difference is an integer); solutions from different points are connect
ed by **connection formulas** (linear relations with Gamma-function coefficients
).

The remaining 18 representations are obtained by permuting the roles of the sing
ular points (each of the 6 permutations of $\{z_1, z_2, z_3\} \to \{0, 1, \infty
\}$ yields a set of 4 solutions by choosing which pair of exponents to factor ou
t).

### Step 5. The General Solution

Combining the Möbius transformation, the gauge factor, and the hypergeometric so
lutions, the **general solution** of Riemann's P-equation is:

$$\boxed{y(z) = x^{\alpha}(1-x)^{\beta}\Big[C_1\, F(a,b;c;x) + C_2\, x^{1-c}\, F
(a-c+1,\, b-c+1;\, 2-c;\, x)\Big],}$$

where

$$x = \frac{(z - z_1)(z_2 - z_3)}{(z - z_3)(z_2 - z_1)}, \qquad a = \alpha+\beta
+\gamma,\quad b = \alpha+\beta+\gamma',\quad c = 1+\alpha-\alpha',$$

and $C_1, C_2$ are arbitrary constants.

Equivalently, in Riemann P-symbol notation:

$$y(z) = P\left\{\begin{matrix} z_1 & z_2 & z_3 \\ \alpha & \beta & \gamma \\ \a
lpha' & \beta' & \gamma' \end{matrix}\;;\; z \right\} = x^{\alpha}(1-x)^{\beta}\
, P\left\{\begin{matrix} 0 & 1 & \infty \\ 0 & 0 & a \\ \alpha'-\alpha & \beta'-
\beta & b \end{matrix}\;;\; x \right\},$$

where the right-hand side is the hypergeometric equation in P-symbol form, with
$a = \alpha+\beta+\gamma$, $b = \alpha+\beta+\gamma'$, $c = 1+\alpha-\alpha'$.

The six Kummer solutions listed in Step 4, when pulled back via $y = x^{\alpha}(
1-x)^{\beta}w$, give six explicit local representations of the general solution
(two around each singular point), and the full set of 24 is obtained by the six
permutations of singular-point assignments. Any two linearly independent ones sp
an the solution space.
