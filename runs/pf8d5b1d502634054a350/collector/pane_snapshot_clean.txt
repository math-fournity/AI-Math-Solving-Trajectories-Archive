We solve the Cauchy problem
$$u_t - u\,u_x = 0,\qquad u(x,0)=x,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.$$

Rewrite as a conservation/transport law $u_t + a(u)\,u_x = 0$ with characteristi
c speed $a(u) = -u$.

**Method of characteristics.** Along a characteristic curve $(x(t),t)$,
$$\frac{du}{dt}=0,\qquad \frac{dx}{dt}=a(u)=-u.$$
Hence $u$ is constant along each characteristic. Let the characteristic originat
e at $x_0$ when $t=0$; the initial data gives $u = x_0$ along that curve. Theref
ore
$$\frac{dx}{dt} = -x_0 \;\Longrightarrow\; x(t) = x_0 - x_0 t = x_0(1-t).$$

**Explicit solution.** Inverting $x = x_0(1-t)$ gives $x_0 = \dfrac{x}{1-t}$, so
$$u(x,t) = x_0 = \frac{x}{1-t}.$$
This is valid as long as the map $x_0 \mapsto x_0(1-t)$ is a diffeomorphism of $
\mathbb{R}$, i.e. as long as $1-t \neq 0$.

**Breakdown time.** The Jacobian of the characteristic map is
$$\frac{\partial x}{\partial x_0} = 1-t.$$
Characteristics cross (gradient catastrophe) when this Jacobian vanishes:
$$1-t = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; t = 1.$$
At $t=1$ every characteristic collapses to $x=0$, and $u_x = \dfrac{1}{1-t} \to
\infty$ as $t\to 1^-$. For $t<1$ the map is invertible and the classical solutio
n exists; for $t\ge 1$ no classical solution exists.

Therefore the correct statement is:

**The solution $u$ exists for $t < 1$ and breaks down at $t = 1$.**
