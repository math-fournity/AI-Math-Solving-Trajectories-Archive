## Solution

**Answer: Every positive integer $n$ can be realized.**

### Setup

The Klein bottle $K$ has fundamental group
$$\pi_1(K) = \langle a, b \mid aba^{-1} = b^{-1}\rangle.$$
Every element has a unique normal form $a^i b^j$ ($i,j \in \mathbb{Z}$), using t
he relation $ba = ab^{-1}$ to push $a$'s to the left. The group is a semidirect
product $\mathbb{Z} \rtimes \mathbb{Z}$, where $\langle b\rangle \cong \mathbb{Z
}$ is normal and $\langle a\rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}$ acts on it by inversion.

**Covering spaces ↔ subgroups.** An $n$-sheeted covering $p:\tilde{K}\to K$ corr
esponds to a subgroup $H\le \pi_1(K)$ of index $n$. The total space $\tilde{K}$
is a closed surface (since $K$ is closed) with $\chi(\tilde{K})=n\cdot\chi(K)=0$
.

**Orientability criterion.** The Klein bottle is non-orientable; its orientation
 double cover is the torus. The orientation character $w:\pi_1(K)\to\mathbb{Z}/2
\mathbb{Z}$ sends $a^i b^j\mapsto i\bmod 2$ (the loop $a$ traverses the "twisted
" direction of the square, hence is orientation-reversing; $b$ traverses the cyl
inder direction, hence is orientation-preserving). The covering $\tilde{K}$ is:
- **non-orientable** (hence a Klein bottle, being a closed surface with $\chi=0$
) iff $H\not\subseteq\ker w$, i.e., $H$ contains some $a^i b^j$ with $i$ odd;
- **orientable** (hence a torus) iff $H\subseteq\ker w$.

### Construction for every $n$

For any positive integer $n$, define
$$H_n = \langle a,\; b^n\rangle = \{a^i b^{nj} : i,j\in\mathbb{Z}\}.$$

**$H_n$ is a subgroup.** It contains $a$ and $b^n$. Closure: $a\cdot b^n = ab^n\
in H_n$; $b^n\cdot a = ab^{-n} = a(b^n)^{-1}\in H_n$; $a^i\cdot a^{i'}=a^{i+i'}\
in H_n$; $b^{nj}\cdot b^{nj'}=b^{n(j+j')}\in H_n$. ✓

**Index of $H_n$ is $n$.** Since $a\in H_n$, every coset has a representative $b
^j$ (because $a^i b^j H_n = b^j H_n$). Since $b^n\in H_n$, we have $b^j H_n = b^
{j'} H_n$ iff $n\mid(j-j')$. So the distinct cosets are $H_n, bH_n, \ldots, b^{n
-1}H_n$, giving $[\pi_1(K):H_n]=n$. ✓

**The covering space is a Klein bottle.** Since $a\in H_n$ and $w(a)=1$ (odd), $
H_n$ contains an orientation-reversing element, so $\tilde{K}$ is non-orientable
. Being a closed non-orientable surface with $\chi=0$, it is a Klein bottle. ✓

**Direct verification via fundamental group.** The fundamental group of the cove
ring space is $H_n$ with the relation inherited from $\pi_1(K)$:
$$\pi_1(\tilde{K}) = \langle a,\; b^n \mid a\,b^n\,a^{-1} = b^{-n}\rangle,$$
which is exactly the standard presentation of $\pi_1(K)$ (with $b^n$ playing the

For every positive integer $n$, the subgroup $H_n=\langle a, b^n\rangle$ has ind
