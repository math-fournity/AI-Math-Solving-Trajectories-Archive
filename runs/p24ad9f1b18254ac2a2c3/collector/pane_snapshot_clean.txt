ral measurable functions, where $h \geq 0$, $f$ is an arbitrary measurable funct
ion, and $g$ ranges over step functions.

**Answer: The equality does NOT hold in general.** It holds if and only if $f \g
eq 0$ a.e. on $\{h > 0\}$. The critical issue is that $f$ is only assumed measur
able, not non-negative.

---

## Proof

### Part 1: Counterexample showing the equality fails when $f$ takes negative va
lues

Let $X = \{0\}$ with $\mu(\{0\}) = 1$. Set $h(0) = 1$ and $f(0) = -1$.

**Left-hand side:** Step functions $g$ satisfying $0 \leq g \leq h$ means $g(0)
\in [0, 1]$. Then
$$\int_X fg\,d\mu = f(0)\cdot g(0) = -g(0).$$
Taking the supremum over $g(0) \in [0,1]$:
$$\text{LHS} = \sup_{t \in [0,1]}(-t) = 0.$$

**Right-hand side:** We have $fh = f(0)\cdot h(0) = -1$. The constraint $0 \leq
g \leq fh$ requires $0 \leq g(0) \leq -1$, which is **impossible**. The feasible
 set is empty, so
$$\text{RHS} = \sup(\emptyset) = -\infty.$$

Therefore $\text{LHS} = 0 \neq -\infty = \text{RHS}$, and the equality **fails**
. $\square$

The essential problem: when $f < 0$ on a set where $h > 0$, the product $fh$ bec
omes negative there, making the constraint $0 \leq g \leq fh$ infeasible on that
 set, which empties (or restricts) the RHS feasible set, while the LHS remains w
ell-defined and non-trivial.

---

### Part 2: The equality DOES hold when $f \geq 0$ a.e.

Assume $f \geq 0$ a.e. (We may modify $f$ on a null set to assume $f \geq 0$ eve
rywhere without affecting any integrals.)

**LHS $= \int_X fh\,d\mu$:**

*Upper bound:* For any step function $g$ with $0 \leq g \leq h$, since $f \geq 0
$, we have $0 \leq fg \leq fh$, hence $\int fg\,d\mu \leq \int fh\,d\mu$. Taking
 the supremum: $\text{LHS} \leq \int fh\,d\mu$.

*Lower bound:* Since $h \geq 0$ is measurable, there exists a sequence of step f
unctions $(g_n)$ with $0 \leq g_n \leq h$ and $g_n \nearrow h$ pointwise (the st
andard dyadic construction: $g_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n2^n} \frac{k}{2^n}\mathbf{1}_{\{
k/2^n \leq h < (k+1)/2^n\}}$). Since $f \geq 0$, we have $fg_n \geq 0$ and $fg_n
 \nearrow fh$ pointwise. By the Monotone Convergence Theorem:
$$\int fg_n\,d\mu \nearrow \int fh\,d\mu.$$
Each $\int fg_n\,d\mu$ belongs to the set over which we take the supremum on the
 LHS, so $\text{LHS} \geq \int fh\,d\mu$.

Combining: $\text{LHS} = \int fh\,d\mu$.

**RHS $= \int_X fh\,d\mu$:**

Since $f \geq 0$ and $h \geq 0$, the product $fh$ is non-negative and measurable
. By the standard definition/characterization of the Lebesgue integral of a non-
negative measurable function:
$$\int fh\,d\mu = \sup\left\{\int g\,d\mu : 0 \leq g \leq fh,\; g \text{ step fu
nction}\right\} = \text{RHS}.$$

**Conclusion:** When $f \geq 0$ a.e., both sides equal $\int fh\,d\mu$, so $\tex
t{LHS} = \text{RHS}$. $\square$

### Summary

The equality
$$\sup_g\left\{\int_X(fg)\,d\mu:0\leq g\leq h\right\}=\sup_g\left\{\int_Xg\,d\mu
:0\leq g\leq fh\right\}$$
**does not hold in general** for arbitrary measurable $f$. It holds if and only
if $f \geq 0$ a.e. on the set $\{h > 0\}$. The necessity of the non-negativity o
f $f$ is demonstrated by the counterexample above, and the sufficiency is proved
 by showing both sides equal $\int fh\,d\mu$ via the Monotone Convergence Theore
m and the standard supremum characterization of the integral.
