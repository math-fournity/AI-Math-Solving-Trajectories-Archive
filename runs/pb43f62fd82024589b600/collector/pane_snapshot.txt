We compute $P!\left(\inf_{t\in[1,2]} W_t < 0 ;\middle|; W_1>0,; W_2>0\right)$
for standard Brownian motion $(W_t)_{t\ge 0}$.

Step 1: Reduce via the Markov property

Condition on $W_1 = x$ with $x > 0$. By the Markov property, for $t \in [1,2]$,
$$W_t = x + B_{t-1}, \qquad B_s := W_{1+s}-W_1,; s\in[0,1],$$ where $(B_s)
{s\in[0,1]}$ is a standard Brownian motion independent of $W_1$. Hence $$
\inf{t\in[1,2]} W_t < 0 \iff \inf_{s\in[0,1]} B_s < -x,$$ and the condition $W_2
> 0$ becomes $B_1 > -x$.

Step 2: Reflection principle

For standard Brownian motion $B$ on $[0,1]$ and $x > 0$, the reflection
principle gives the joint density $$P!\left(B_1 \in dy,; \inf_{0\le s\le 1} B_s
\ge -x\right) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\Big[e^{-y^2/2} - e^{-(y+2x)^2/2}\Big],dy,
\quad y > -x.$$ Therefore $$P!\left(\inf_{s\in[0,1]} B_s \ge -x ;\middle|; B_1 =
y\right) = 1 - e^{-2x(y+x)}, \qquad y > -x,$$ and $$P!\left(\inf_{s\in[0,1]} B_s
< -x ;\middle|; B_1 = y\right) = e^{-2x(y+x)}.$$

Averaging over $y > -x$ with the $N(0,1)$ density: $$P!\left(\inf B_s < -x ;
\middle|; B_1 > -x\right) = \frac{\displaystyle\int_{-x}^{\infty} e^{-2x(y+x)},
\phi(y),dy}{\displaystyle\int_{-x}^{\infty}\phi(y),dy},$$ where $
\phi(y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-y^2/2}$.

Numerator. Substitute $u = y+x$: $$\int_0^\infty e^{-2xu},\phi(u-x),du =
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^2/2}\int_0^\infty e^{-u^2/2 - xu},du = \frac{1}
{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_x^\infty e^{-v^2/2},dv = \Phi(-x),$$ using $u^2/2 + xu =
(u+x)^2/2 - x^2/2$ and $v = u+x$.

Denominator. $\displaystyle\int_{-x}^{\infty}\phi(y),dy = \Phi(x)$.

Thus $$\boxed{P!\left(\inf_{s\in[0,1]} B_s < -x ;\middle|; B_1 > -x\right) =
\frac{\Phi(-x)}{\Phi(x)}}.$$

Since $W_2 = W_1 + (W_2-W_1)$ with $W_2-W_1 \sim N(0,1)$ independent of $W_1$, $
$W_2 \mid W_1 = x \sim N(x,1), \qquad P(W_2>0\mid W_1=x) = \Phi(x).$$

The pair $(W_1,W_2)$ is bivariate normal with correlation $\rho = \frac{1}
{\sqrt{1\cdot 2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, so $$P(W_1>0,,W_2>0) = \frac14 +
\frac{1}{2\pi}\arcsin!\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac14 + \frac{1}
{2\pi}\cdot\frac{\pi}{4} = \frac38.$$

Hence, for $x > 0$, $$f_{W_1\mid W_1>0,,W_2>0}(x) = \frac{\phi(x),\Phi(x)}{3/8}
= \frac{8}{3},\phi(x),\Phi(x).$$

Step 4: Integrate

$$P = \int_0^\infty \frac{\Phi(-x)}{\Phi(x)}\cdot \frac{8}{3},\phi(x),\Phi(x),dx
= \frac{8}{3}\int_0^\infty \Phi(-x),\phi(x),dx.$$

Therefore $$P = \frac{8}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{8} = \boxed{\dfrac{1}{3}}.$$
