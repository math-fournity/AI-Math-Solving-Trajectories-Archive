## Solution

Let $X_t = \int_0^t B_s\,ds$. We wish to find $dX_t$.

### Key observation

The integral $\int_0^t B_s\,ds$ is **not** an Itô integral. An Itô integral has
the form $\int_0^t \sigma(s,B_s)\,dB_s$, i.e. integration *with respect to Brown
ian motion*. Here the integrator is $ds$ (Lebesgue measure), so this is an **ord
inary (Lebesgue/Riemann) integral**.

Since $B_s$ has continuous sample paths almost surely, $s \mapsto B_s(\omega)$ i
s continuous for a.e. $\omega$, and the ordinary integral is well defined. By th
e classical fundamental theorem of calculus,

$$dX_t = B_t\,dt.$$

There is **no $dB_t$ term** because the integration is with respect to $ds$, not
 $dB_s$.

### Verification via Itô's formula

To confirm this through the stochastic calculus machinery (which is what "interp
reted as an Itô integral" likely invites), apply the Itô product rule (integrati
on by parts) to $t \cdot B_t$:

$$d(t\,B_t) = t\,dB_t + B_t\,dt.$$

Integrating from $0$ to $t$:

$$t\,B_t = \int_0^t s\,dB_s + \int_0^t B_s\,ds,$$

so

$$X_t = \int_0^t B_s\,ds = t\,B_t - \int_0^t s\,dB_s.$$

Now take the stochastic differential of both sides. Using the Itô product rule $
d(tB_t) = t\,dB_t + B_t\,dt$, and the fundamental theorem of the Itô integral $d
\!\left(\int_0^t s\,dB_s\right) = t\,dB_t$:

$$dX_t = \bigl(t\,dB_t + B_t\,dt\bigr) - t\,dB_t = B_t\,dt.$$

The $t\,dB_t$ terms cancel exactly, leaving only the drift term.

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{d\!\left(\int_0^t B_s\,ds\right) = B_t\,dt.}$$

The differential is purely the ordinary differential $B_t\,dt$; there is no mart
ingale ($dB_t$) component. This is consistent with the fact that $\int_0^t B_s\,
ds$ is a finite-variation process (it is absolutely continuous in $t$), hence a
semimartingale whose martingale part is identically zero.
