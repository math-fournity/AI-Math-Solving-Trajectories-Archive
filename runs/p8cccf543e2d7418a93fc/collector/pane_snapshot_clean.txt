## Proof

The immersion is
$$f(\theta,\phi)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\cos\theta,\sin\theta,\cos\phi,\sin\phi).$$

**Step 1 — Compute the tangent vectors.**

$$f_\theta=\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial\theta}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(-\sin\theta,\c
os\theta,0,0),$$
$$f_\phi=\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial\phi}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,0,-\sin\phi,\cos
\phi).$$

**Step 2 — Compute the induced metric $g=f^*\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{R
}^4}$.**

$$g_{\theta\theta}=\langle f_\theta,f_\theta\rangle=\tfrac{1}{2}(\sin^2\theta+\c
os^2\theta)=\tfrac{1}{2},$$
$$g_{\phi\phi}=\langle f_\phi,f_\phi\rangle=\tfrac{1}{2}(\sin^2\phi+\cos^2\phi)=
\tfrac{1}{2},$$
$$g_{\theta\phi}=\langle f_\theta,f_\phi\rangle=0.$$

Hence the induced metric on $\mathbb{T}^2$ is
$$g=\tfrac{1}{2}\,d\theta^2+\tfrac{1}{2}\,d\phi^2.$$

**Step 3 — Christoffel symbols vanish.**

Since every component $g_{ij}$ is constant, all partial derivatives $\partial_k
g_{ij}$ are zero. The Christoffel symbols
$$\Gamma^{k}_{ij}=\tfrac{1}{2}g^{k\ell}\bigl(\partial_i g_{j\ell}+\partial_j g_{
i\ell}-\partial_\ell g_{ij}\bigr)$$
therefore vanish identically: $\Gamma^{k}_{ij}\equiv 0$.

**Step 4 — Riemann curvature tensor vanishes.**

The Riemann curvature tensor is
$$R^{\ell}{}_{ijk}=\partial_j\Gamma^{\ell}_{ik}-\partial_i\Gamma^{\ell}_{jk}+\Ga
mma^{\ell}_{jm}\Gamma^{m}_{ik}-\Gamma^{\ell}_{im}\Gamma^{m}_{jk}.$$

With all $\Gamma^{k}_{ij}=0$, every term is zero, so $R^{\ell}{}_{ijk}\equiv 0$.

**Step 5 — Sectional curvature is zero.**

The sectional curvature of the unique $2$-plane (the tangent plane of the surfac
e, spanned by $\partial_\theta,\partial_\phi$) is
$$K=\frac{\langle R(\partial_\theta,\partial_\phi)\partial_\phi,\partial_\theta\
rangle}{g_{\theta\theta}\,g_{\phi\phi}-g_{\theta\phi}^{\,2}}.$$

The numerator vanishes since $R\equiv 0$, while the denominator is $\tfrac{1}{2}
\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}-0=\tfrac{1}{4}\neq 0$. Therefore
$$K=0.$$

---

**Remark.** The result is geometrically natural: $f$ splits as $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{
2}}(c_1(\theta),c_2(\phi))$, a product of two unit circles rescaled by $1/\sqrt{
2}$. The induced metric is a constant multiple of the flat product metric $d\the
ta^2+d\phi^2$ on $S^1\times S^1$, so the flat torus $\mathbb{T}^2=\mathbb{R}^2/\
mathbb{Z}^2$ inherits zero sectional curvature. This is the standard *flat torus
* (Clifford torus) embedded in $\mathbb{R}^4$.
