## Setup

Let the grid have entries $a_{ij}$. Each row/column sums to 7 (total = 21). Non-
zero entries must be distinct within each row and column.

**Key observation on row types.** A row summing to 7 with distinct non-negative
integers can only be:
- **3 non-zero cells:** $\{1,2,4\}$ (the only 3 distinct positive integers summi
ng to 7)
- **2 non-zero cells:** one of $\{1,6\},\{2,5\},\{3,4\}$
- **1 non-zero cell:** $\{7\}$

The same classification applies to columns. I enumerate by the **zero pattern**
(which cells are zero), since it determines how many non-zero cells each row/col
umn has, which forces the value sets.

## Case analysis by total number of zeros

### 0 zeros (all cells positive)
Every row and column is a permutation of $\{1,2,4\}$. This is exactly a **Latin
square of order 3** on symbols $\{1,2,4\}$. Count: $1 \times 3! \times 2! = \mat
hbf{12}$.

### 1 zero
The zero sits at some $(i,j)$. Row $i$ has 2 non-zero cells (from $\{1,6\},\{2,5
\},\{3,4\}$), but column $j$ has 3 non-zero cells forcing $\{1,2,4\}$. The cell
$(i,j')$ in the zero-row must belong to both a pair-summing-to-7 and the set $\{
1,2,4\}$. Checking: $1\to 6\notin\{1,2,4\}$, $2\to 5\notin\{1,2,4\}$, $4\to 3\no
tin\{1,2,4\}$. **Impossible.** Count: $\mathbf{0}$.

### 2 zeros
**Same row or same column:** Forces a 7 in a column/row that also has 3 non-zero
 cells from $\{1,2,4\}$, giving sum $>7$. Impossible.

**Different rows, different columns (non-attacking):** WLOG zeros at $(1,1),(2,2
)$. Row 3 and column 3 are full ($\{1,2,4\}$). Setting row 3 $= (a,b,c)$, a perm
utation of $\{1,2,4\}$, everything is determined:
$$\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 7-b & b \\ 7-a & 0 & a \\ a & b & c \end{pmatrix}$$
All constraints are automatically satisfied (verified: row/col sums = 7, all non
-zero values distinct). This gives $3! = 6$ fillings per pattern.

Number of non-attacking zero patterns: $\binom{3}{2}\binom{3}{2}\cdot 2! = 18$.
Count: $18 \times 6 = \mathbf{108}$.

### 3 zeros
**Row distribution $(2,1,0)$:** The row with a 7 forces its column to have sum $
>7$ (since the zero-free row contributes positively). Impossible.

**Row distribution $(1,1,1)$, column distribution $(2,1,0)$:** The column with 2
 zeros forces a lone 7, making the row containing it have its other cell = 0, co
ntradicting that row having only 1 zero. Impossible.

**Row distribution $(1,1,1)$, column distribution $(1,1,1)$ (permutation matrix)
:** WLOG zeros on diagonal. Setting $(1,2)=a$, all cells are determined by one p
arameter $b$:
$$\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 7-b & b \\ b & 0 & 7-b \\ 7-b & b & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad
 b \in \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$$
All constraints satisfied. Number of permutation-matrix patterns: $3! = 6$. Coun
t: $6 \times 6 = \mathbf{36}$.

### 4 zeros
**Row distribution $(2,2,0)$:** Two 7s in different columns force the zero-free
row to contribute 0 to both those columns — contradiction. Impossible.

**Row distribution $(2,1,1)$:** The row with 2 zeros has a 7 in column $j$. For
column $j$ to sum to 7, both other rows must have zeros in column $j$. This forc
es the structure (WLOG row 1 has 7 in column 1):
$$\begin{pmatrix} 7 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & a & 7-a \\ 0 & 7-a & a \end{pmatrix}, \quad a
 \in \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$$
All constraints satisfied. Patterns: choose the 7-row (3 ways) × choose the 7-co
lumn (3 ways) = 9. Count: $9 \times 6 = \mathbf{54}$.

### 5 zeros
Row distribution $(2,2,1)$. Two 7s in different columns force the third row to h
ave zeros in both those columns (2 zeros), contradicting its allocation of 1 zer
o. If both 7s share a column, sum $\geq 14$. **Impossible.** Count: $\mathbf{0}$
.

### 6 zeros
Every row has 2 zeros and one 7. Each column must have exactly one 7 (to sum to
7). The 7s form a **permutation matrix**. Count: $3! = \mathbf{6}$.
