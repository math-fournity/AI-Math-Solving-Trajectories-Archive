# Problem

A high-tech logistics firm is designing a new automated warehouse facility. The layout consists of $2n$ sensor hubs (where $n$ is a positive integer) placed at distinct coordinates on a large, flat grid. To ensure maximum signal coverage, no two sensor hubs are aligned exactly on the same north-south line or east-west line.

Each sensor hub must be programmed to project exactly two laser beams: one beam must point precisely North or precisely South, and the other beam must point precisely East or precisely West. These beams are treated as rays that extend infinitely from their respective hubs across the floor.

The firm wants to determine a standardized configuration for the directions of these $4n$ laser beams (two per hub) such that they partition the warehouse floor into the maximum possible number of distinct bounded and unbounded zones.

Determine the largest integer $k$ such that, no matter where the $2n$ sensor hubs are positioned (as long as they satisfy the non-alignment constraint), the firm can always choose the directions of the lasers to create at least $k$ zones.
