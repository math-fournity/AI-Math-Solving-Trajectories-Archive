## Proof

**Claim.** The largest possible cardinality of a collection of pairwise disjoint
 infinite subsets of the integers is $\aleph_0$ (countably infinite).

**Upper bound: at most $\aleph_0$.**

Let $\mathcal{F} = \{A_i\}_{i \in I}$ be any collection of pairwise disjoint inf
inite subsets of $\mathbb{Z}$. Since each $A_i$ is nonempty (being infinite), by
 the Axiom of Choice we may select an element $a_i \in A_i$ for each $i \in I$.
Because the $A_i$ are pairwise disjoint, the map $i \mapsto a_i$ is injective: i
f $i \neq j$, then $a_i \in A_i$ and $a_j \in A_j$ with $A_i \cap A_j = \emptyse
t$, so $a_i \neq a_j$. This gives an injection $I \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Z}$. S
ince $\mathbb{Z}$ is countable, $I$ is at most countable, i.e., $|\mathcal{F}| \
le \aleph_0$.

**Lower bound: at least $\aleph_0$ (construction).**

We exhibit countably many pairwise disjoint infinite subsets of $\mathbb{Z}$.

Let $p_1 = 2, p_2 = 3, p_3 = 5, \ldots$ be the primes in increasing order. For e
ach $n \ge 1$, define
$$A_n = \{\, p_n^k : k \ge 1 \,\} = \{p_n, p_n^2, p_n^3, \ldots\}.$$

Each $A_n$ is an infinite subset of $\mathbb{Z}_{>0} \subset \mathbb{Z}$. They a
re pairwise disjoint: if $m \in A_i \cap A_j$ with $i \neq j$, then $m = p_i^a =
 p_j^b$ for some $a, b \ge 1$, which contradicts unique prime factorization (a p
ositive integer has a unique prime divisor of the form $p_i$ appearing to a posi
tive power — here $p_i \neq p_j$ so $p_i^a$ and $p_j^b$ are distinct). Thus $A_i
 \cap A_j = \emptyset$ for $i \neq j$.

The collection $\{A_n : n \ge 1\}$ is indexed by $\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$, so it has ca
rdinality $\aleph_0$.

**Conclusion.**

The upper bound shows no collection can exceed $\aleph_0$ in size, and the const
ruction achieves $\aleph_0$. Therefore the largest possible cardinality is exact
ly $\aleph_0$.
