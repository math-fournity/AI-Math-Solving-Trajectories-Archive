## Proof

Consider the dynamical system
$$\dot x = -y,\qquad \dot y = x,\qquad \dot z = -(x^2+y^2).$$

**Step 1. The $(x,y)$–subsystem is a rigid rotation.**

Computing the time derivative of $r^2 = x^2+y^2$:
$$\frac{d}{dt}(x^2+y^2) = 2x\dot x + 2y\dot y = 2x(-y)+2y(x) = 0.$$
Hence $r^2 = x^2+y^2 = r_0^2$ is a conserved quantity, and the $(x,y)$ motion is
 uniform rotation on the circle of radius $r_0$. Explicitly,
$$x(t) = r_0\cos(t+\varphi),\qquad y(t) = r_0\sin(t+\varphi)$$
for constants $r_0\ge 0$ and $\varphi$.

**Step 2. The $z$–component decreases linearly.**

Since $x^2+y^2 = r_0^2$ is constant,
$$\dot z = -r_0^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad z(t) = z_0 - r_0^2\, t.$$

Thus every nontrivial trajectory (with $r_0>0$) lies on the helix
$$\gamma(t) = \bigl(r_0\cos(t+\varphi),\; r_0\sin(t+\varphi),\; z_0 - r_0^2 t\bi
gr),$$
winding around the $z$–axis at angular speed $1$ while descending at the constan
t speed $r_0^2$. This is precisely the trajectory described in the problem.

**Step 3. The trajectory is unbounded and escapes to $z=-\infty$.**

As $t\to+\infty$,
$$z(t) = z_0 - r_0^2 t \;\longrightarrow\; -\infty.$$
Therefore $\|\gamma(t)\|\to\infty$; in particular the trajectory eventually leav
es every bounded subset of $\mathbb{R}^3$.

**Step 4. The omega limit set is empty.**

Recall the definition
$$\omega(\gamma) = \bigcap_{T>0}\,\overline{\{\gamma(t): t\ge T\}}.$$
A point $p\in\mathbb{R}^3$ belongs to $\omega(\gamma)$ iff there exists a sequen
ce $t_n\to+\infty$ with $\gamma(t_n)\to p$.

Fix any $p=(p_x,p_y,p_z)\in\mathbb{R}^3$ and let $B$ be the ball of radius $R$ c
entered at the origin containing $p$. Since $z(t)\to-\infty$, there exists $T_0$
 such that for all $t\ge T_0$, $|z(t)|>R$, hence $\gamma(t)\notin B$. Consequent
ly no sequence $t_n\to+\infty$ can have $\gamma(t_n)\to p$. Since $p$ was arbitr
ary,
$$\omega(\gamma) = \varnothing.$$

**Conclusion.** The trajectory lies on the helix $\bigl(r_0\cos(t+\varphi),\,r_0
\sin(t+\varphi),\,z_0-r_0^2 t\bigr)$ and descends at constant speed $r_0^2$, so
$z(t)\to-\infty$ and the trajectory escapes every compact set. Hence the omega l
imit set is the empty set:

$$\boxed{\,\omega(\gamma)=\varnothing\,}.$$
