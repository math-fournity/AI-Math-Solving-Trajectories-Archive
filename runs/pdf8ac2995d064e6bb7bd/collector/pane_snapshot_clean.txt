## Answer

**Yes.** Every bounded sequence in a separable Hilbert space has a weakly conver
gent subsequence.

### Proof

Let $H$ be a separable Hilbert space with inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rang
le$, and let $(x_n)_{n=1}^\infty$ be a bounded sequence in $H$, say $\|x_n\|\le
M$ for all $n$.

If $H$ is finite-dimensional, the result follows immediately from the Bolzano–We
ierstrass theorem (bounded sets are relatively compact). Hence assume $H$ is inf
inite-dimensional and separable, so $H$ admits a countable orthonormal basis $\{
e_k\}_{k=1}^\infty$.

---

**Step 1 — Diagonal extraction.** For each fixed $k$, the scalar sequence $\lang
le x_n, e_k\rangle$ is bounded (by $M$, via Cauchy–Schwarz), hence has a converg
ent subsequence by Bolzano–Weierstrass. We apply a diagonal procedure:

- Extract a subsequence $(x_n^{(1)})$ so that $\langle x_n^{(1)}, e_1\rangle$ co
nverges.
- From $(x_n^{(1)})$ extract a subsequence $(x_n^{(2)})$ so that $\langle x_n^{(
2)}, e_2\rangle$ converges (the convergence for $e_1$ is preserved).
- Inductively, $(x_n^{(k+1)})$ is a subsequence of $(x_n^{(k)})$ with $\langle x
_n^{(k+1)}, e_{k+1}\rangle$ converging.

Let $n_j$ be the $j$-th index of the $j$-th subsequence $(x_n^{(j)})$. Then for
every fixed $k$, the tail $\{x_{n_j}:j\ge k\}$ lies inside the subsequence $(x_n
^{(k)})$, so $\langle x_{n_j},e_k\rangle$ converges as $j\to\infty$. Denote
$$a_k:=\lim_{j\to\infty}\langle x_{n_j},e_k\rangle.$$

---

**Step 2 — Constructing the weak limit.** Define $f$ on the dense subspace $V:=\
operatorname{span}\{e_k\}$ by linear extension of $f(e_k)=a_k$. For any $y=\sum_
{k=1}^N c_k e_k\in V$,
$$f(y)=\sum_{k=1}^N c_k a_k=\lim_{j\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^N c_k\langle x_{n_j},e_k
\rangle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\langle x_{n_j},y\rangle.$$
By Cauchy–Schwarz, $|\langle x_{n_j},y\rangle|\le M\|y\|$, so $|f(y)|\le M\|y\|$
. Thus $f$ is a bounded linear functional on $V$ with $\|f\|\le M$, and since $V
$ is dense in $H$, $f$ extends uniquely to a bounded linear functional on $H$ (s
till denoted $f$, with $\|f\|\le M$).

By the **Riesz representation theorem**, there exists a unique $x\in H$ with $\|
x\|\le M$ such that
$$f(y)=\langle y,x\rangle\quad\text{for all }y\in H.$$

---

**Step 3 — Verifying weak convergence $x_{n_j}\rightharpoonup x$.** We must show
 $\langle x_{n_j},y\rangle\to\langle x,y\rangle$ for every $y\in H$.

Fix $y\in H$ and $\varepsilon>0$. Since $V$ is dense, pick $y_0=\sum_{k=1}^N c_k
 e_k\in V$ with $\|y-y_0\|<\varepsilon/(3M)$. Then
$$|\langle x_{n_j},y\rangle-\langle x,y\rangle|\le|\langle x_{n_j},y-y_0\rangle|
+|\langle x_{n_j},y_0\rangle-\langle x,y_0\rangle|+|\langle x,y_0-y\rangle|.$$
The first and third terms are each bounded by $M\|y-y_0\|<\varepsilon/3$ (using
$\|x_{n_j}\|\le M$ and $\|x\|\le M$). For the middle term,
$$\langle x_{n_j},y_0\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^N c_k\langle x_{n_j},e_k\rangle\longrigh
tarrow\sum_{k=1}^N c_k a_k=f(y_0)=\langle x,y_0\rangle,$$
so for all sufficiently large $j$, $|\langle x_{n_j},y_0\rangle-\langle x,y_0\ra
ngle|<\varepsilon/3$. Combining,
$$|\langle x_{n_j},y\rangle-\langle x,y\rangle|<\varepsilon\quad\text{for all la
rge }j.$$
Since $y$ and $\varepsilon$ were arbitrary, $x_{n_j}\rightharpoonup x$.

---

**Conclusion.** The subsequence $(x_{n_j})$ converges weakly to $x\in H$. Theref
ore every bounded sequence in a separable Hilbert space admits a weakly converge
nt subsequence. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The result in fact holds for every Hilbert space (separable or not)
since Hilbert spaces are reflexive and the Eberlein–Šmulian theorem equates weak
 compactness with weak sequential compactness of bounded closed sets in reflexiv
e Banach spaces. The separability hypothesis allows the elementary diagonal argu
ment given above.
