## Proof

**Yes**, the mean value formula holds. We prove it by applying the classical mea
n value property iteratively in each variable.

**Setup.** Let $f: \mathbb{R}^4 \times \mathbb{R}^4 \to \mathbb{R}$ be separatel
y harmonic, i.e., $\Delta_1 f = 0$ and $\Delta_2 f = 0$, where $\Delta_i$ denote
s the Laplacian in the $i$-th block of variables. Let $x_1, x_2 \in \mathbb{R}^4
$ and $r_1, r_2 > 0$.

**Step 1 (MVP in the first variable).** Fix $x_2 \in \mathbb{R}^4$ and define $g
(\cdot) := f(\cdot, x_2): \mathbb{R}^4 \to \mathbb{R}$. Since $\Delta_1 f = 0$,
the function $g$ is harmonic on $\mathbb{R}^4$. By the classical spherical mean
value property for harmonic functions,

$$f(x_1, x_2) = g(x_1) = \frac{1}{|\partial B_{r_1}(x_1)|}\int_{\partial B_{r_1}
(x_1)} g(y_1)\, d\sigma(y_1) = \frac{1}{|\partial B_{r_1}(x_1)|}\int_{\partial B
_{r_1}(x_1)} f(y_1, x_2)\, d\sigma(y_1). \tag{1}$$

**Step 2 (MVP in the second variable).** For each fixed $y_1 \in \partial B_{r_1
}(x_1)$, define $h(\cdot) := f(y_1, \cdot): \mathbb{R}^4 \to \mathbb{R}$. Since
$\Delta_2 f = 0$, the function $h$ is harmonic on $\mathbb{R}^4$. Applying the s
pherical mean value property again,

$$f(y_1, x_2) = h(x_2) = \frac{1}{|\partial B_{r_2}(x_2)|}\int_{\partial B_{r_2}
(x_2)} f(y_1, y_2)\, d\sigma(y_2). \tag{2}$$

**Step 3 (Substitution and Fubini).** Substituting (2) into (1):

$$f(x_1, x_2) = \frac{1}{|\partial B_{r_1}(x_1)|}\int_{\partial B_{r_1}(x_1)} \l
eft[\frac{1}{|\partial B_{r_2}(x_2)|}\int_{\partial B_{r_2}(x_2)} f(y_1, y_2)\,
d\sigma(y_2)\right] d\sigma(y_1).$$

Since $f$ is harmonic in each variable separately, it is smooth (in fact real-an
alytic) in each variable, hence continuous on the compact product $\partial B_{r
_1}(x_1) \times \partial B_{r_2}(x_2)$. By Fubini's theorem, the iterated integr
al equals the integral over the product with respect to the product measure:

$$f(x_1, x_2) = \frac{1}{|\partial B_{r_1}(x_1)|\cdot|\partial B_{r_2}(x_2)|}\in
t_{\partial B_{r_1}(x_1) \times \partial B_{r_2}(x_2)} f(y_1, y_2)\, d\sigma(y_1
)\times d\sigma(y_2).$$

**Step 4 (Identification of the normalizing constant).** The product manifold $\
partial B_{r_1}(x_1) \times \partial B_{r_2}(x_2)$ is a 6-dimensional submanifol
d of $\mathbb{R}^8$ (each factor being a 3-sphere). Its area with respect to the
 product surface measure $d\sigma(y_1)\times d\sigma(y_2)$ is

$$\text{Area}\bigl(\partial B_{r_1}(x_1)\times \partial B_{r_2}(x_2)\bigr) = |\p
artial B_{r_1}(x_1)|\cdot|\partial B_{r_2}(x_2)| = 2\pi^2 r_1^3 \cdot 2\pi^2 r_2
^3 = 4\pi^4 r_1^3 r_2^3,$$

where we used that the surface area of a 3-sphere of radius $r$ in $\mathbb{R}^4
$ is $2\pi^2 r^3$.

**Conclusion.** Combining Steps 3 and 4, we obtain exactly the claimed formula:

$$\boxed{f(x_1,x_2) = \frac{1}{\text{Area}(\partial B_{r_1}(x_1) \times \partial
 B_{r_2}(x_2))} \int_{\partial B_{r_1}(x_1) \times \partial B_{r_2}(x_2)} f(y_1,
 y_2) \, d\sigma(y_1) \times d\sigma(y_2).}$$

The formula holds for all $x_1, x_2 \in \mathbb{R}^4$ and all $r_1, r_2 > 0$, fo
r every separately harmonic function $f$. The proof requires only: (i) the class
ical spherical mean value property applied in each variable block, and (ii) Fubi
ni's theorem to convert the iterated integral into an integral over the product.
 No joint harmonicity (i.e., $(\Delta_1 + \Delta_2)f = 0$) is needed; separate h
armonicity suffices. $\blacksquare$
