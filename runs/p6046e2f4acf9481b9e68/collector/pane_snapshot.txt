## Proof

**Step 1: Reformulate via a counting function.**

Define $N(t) = \#\{(i,j) \in \mathbb{N}^2 : a_{i,j} \geq t\} = \#\{(i,j) \in \ma
thbb{N}^2 : i^2 j^2 \geq 1/t\} = \#\{(i,j) \in \mathbb{N}^2 : ij \leq t^{-1/2}\}
$.

Setting $M = t^{-1/2}$, we have $N(t) = \#\{(i,j) \in \mathbb{N}^2 : ij \leq M\}
$.

**Step 2: Asymptotics of $N(t)$.**

We compute:
$$N(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor M \rfloor} \left\lfloor \frac{M}{i} \right\rfloor.$
$

By the classical Dirichlet divisor sum estimate:
$$\sum_{i=1}^{M} \left\lfloor \frac{M}{i} \right\rfloor = M \ln M + (2\gamma - 1
)M + O(M^{1/2}),$$
where $\gamma$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant. Therefore:
$$N(t) = M \ln M + O(M) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{t}} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{1}{t} + O
\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\right).$$

In particular, $N(t) \sim \frac{1}{2\sqrt{t}} \ln\frac{1}{t}$ as $t \to 0^+$.

**Step 3: Relate $b_n$ to $N$.**

Since $b_n$ is the $n$-th largest value, we have $N(b_n) \geq n$ and $N(b_n + \v
arepsilon) < n$ for any $\varepsilon > 0$ such that $b_n + \varepsilon$ is stric
tly between $b_n$ and $b_{n-1}$. Asymptotically, $N(b_n) \sim n$.

**Step 4: Solve for $b_n$.**

We need to solve $n \sim \frac{1}{2\sqrt{b_n}} \ln\frac{1}{b_n}$.

**Claim:** $b_n \sim \dfrac{(\ln n)^2}{n^2}$.

*Verification.* Set $b_n = \dfrac{(\ln n)^2}{n^2}$. Then:
$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_n}} = \frac{n}{\ln n}, \qquad \ln\frac{1}{b_n} = 2\ln n - 2\l
n\ln n \sim 2\ln n.$$

Therefore:
$$\frac{1}{2\sqrt{b_n}} \ln\frac{1}{b_n} \sim \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{n}{\ln n}
\cdot 2\ln n = n. \quad \checkmark$$

More precisely, substituting $b_n = \frac{(\ln n)^2}{n^2}$:
$$\frac{1}{2\sqrt{b_n}}\ln\frac{1}{b_n} = \frac{n}{2\ln n}\left(2\ln n - 2\ln\ln
 n\right) = n\left(1 - \frac{\ln\ln n}{\ln n}\right) = n - \frac{n\ln\ln n}{\ln
n}.$$

The relative error is $\frac{\ln\ln n}{\ln n} \to 0$, confirming $N(b_n) \sim n$
.

**Step 5: Determine the $O(n^{-k})$ behavior.**

Since $b_n \sim \frac{(\ln n)^2}{n^2}$:

- For any $k < 2$: $(\ln n)^2 = o(n^{2-k})$, so $b_n = o(n^{-k})$, hence $b_n =
O(n^{-k})$.
- For $k = 2$: $b_n / n^{-2} = (\ln n)^2 \to \infty$, so $b_n \neq O(n^{-2})$.
- For $k > 2$: $b_n / n^{-k} = n^{k-2}(\ln n)^2 \to \infty$, so $b_n \neq O(n^{-
k})$.

**Conclusion.**

The precise asymptotic is:
$$\boxed{b_n \sim \frac{(\ln n)^2}{n^2}}.$$

Therefore $b_n = O(n^{-k})$ for every $k < 2$ (and indeed $b_n = o(n^{-k})$ for
every $k < 2$), but $b_n \neq O(n^{-2})$. The sequence decays just slightly fast
er than $n^{-2}$, with a polylogarithmic correction $(\ln n)^2$.
