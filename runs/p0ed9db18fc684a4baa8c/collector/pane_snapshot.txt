## Setup

The positive roots $\lambda_n$ of $\tan x = x$ are precisely the positive zeros
of
$$f(x) = x\cos x - \sin x.$$
Note $f$ is odd, and $x=0$ is a zero. Expanding near $0$:
$$f(x) = x\!\left(1-\tfrac{x^{2}}{2}+\cdots\right)-\!\left(x-\tfrac{x^{3}}{6}+\c
dots\right)= -\tfrac{x^{3}}{3}+O(x^{5}),$$
so $x=0$ is a **triple** zero. All other zeros are $\pm\lambda_n$ ($n=1,2,\dots$
), simple, and non-real zeros do not exist (a standard check: if $\tan z = z$ wi
th $z=a+bi$, $b\ne0$, one derives $b=0$).

## Product expansion

Since $f$ is entire of order $1$, Hadamard/Mittag-Leffler gives
$$f(x) = -\frac{x^{3}}{3}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\!\left(1-\frac{x^{2}}{\lambda_n^{2
}}\right),$$
the constant being fixed by the leading term $-x^{3}/3$ at the origin.

## Logarithmic derivative

Differentiating $\log f$:
$$\frac{f'(x)}{f(x)} = \frac{3}{x} - \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2x}{\lambda_n^{2}-
x^{2}}.$$
On the other hand $f'(x) = \cos x - x\sin x - \cos x = -x\sin x$, hence
$$\frac{-x\sin x}{x\cos x - \sin x} = \frac{3}{x} - \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2x}
{\lambda_n^{2}-x^{2}}.$$

## Evaluate at $x=1$

Since $1$ is not a root, set $x=1$:
$$\frac{-\sin 1}{\cos 1 - \sin 1} = 3 - 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_n^{
2}-1}.$$
Solving for the desired sum $S$:
$$S = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_n^{2}-1} = \frac{1}{2}\!\left(3 + \fra
c{\sin 1}{\cos 1 - \sin 1}\right) = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{3\cos 1 - 2\sin 1}{\co
s 1 - \sin 1}.$$

## Numerical check

$\cos 1\approx 0.5403$, $\sin 1\approx 0.8415$:
$$S \approx \tfrac12\cdot\frac{1.6209-1.6830}{0.5403-0.8415} = \tfrac12\cdot\fra
c{-0.0621}{-0.3012}\approx 0.1031.$$
Directly summing the first few terms ($\lambda_1\approx4.4934,\lambda_2\approx7.

the tail $\sum_{n\ge8}1/((n+\tfrac12)^2\pi^2)\approx 0.0119$, totaling $\approx
