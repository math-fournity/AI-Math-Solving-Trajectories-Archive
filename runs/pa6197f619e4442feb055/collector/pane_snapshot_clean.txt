## Solution

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains only the PDE, boundary/ini
tial conditions, and a structural hint (form of solution). No solution, answer v
alue, or proof sketch is leaked. Proceeding.

---

We follow the hint and set

$$y(x,t)=u(x,t)+\phi(x),$$

with $\phi$ chosen to absorb the inhomogeneous forcing term $x$.

### 1. Choosing $\phi$

Substituting $y=u+\phi$ into the PDE gives

$$u_{tt}=a^{2}u_{xx}+a^{2}\phi''(x)+x.$$

We kill the forcing by requiring

$$a^{2}\phi''(x)+x=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \phi''(x)=-\frac{x}{a^{2}}.$$

Integrating twice,

$$\phi(x)=-\frac{x^{3}}{6a^{2}}+C_{1}x+C_{2}.$$

We want the homogeneous boundary conditions to pass to $u$, i.e. $u(0,t)=u(\pi,t
)=0$, so we impose $\phi(0)=0$ and $\phi(\pi)=0$:

- $\phi(0)=0\Rightarrow C_{2}=0$,
- $\phi(\pi)=0\Rightarrow -\dfrac{\pi^{3}}{6a^{2}}+C_{1}\pi=0\Rightarrow C_{1}=\
dfrac{\pi^{2}}{6a^{2}}$.

Hence

$$\boxed{\phi(x)=\frac{x(\pi^{2}-x^{2})}{6a^{2}}.}$$

With this choice $u$ satisfies the **homogeneous** wave equation

$$u_{tt}=a^{2}u_{xx},\qquad 0<x<\pi,\ t>0,$$

with boundary conditions $u(0,t)=u(\pi,t)=0$, and initial conditions obtained fr
om $y(x,0)=0$, $y_{t}(x,0)=0$:

$$u(x,0)=y(x,0)-\phi(x)=-\phi(x)=-\frac{x(\pi^{2}-x^{2})}{6a^{2}}\equiv f(x),\qq
uad u_{t}(x,0)=0.$$

### 2. Separation of variables for $u$

The eigenvalue problem $X''+\lambda X=0$, $X(0)=X(\pi)=0$ gives eigenfunctions $
\sin(nx)$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_{n}=n^{2}$, $n=1,2,\dots$. The time factors
satisfy $T''+a^{2}n^{2}T=0$, so

$$u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl[A_{n}\cos(ant)+B_{n}\sin(ant)\bigr]\sin(nx).$$

From $u_{t}(x,0)=0$:

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{n}\,an\,\sin(nx)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad B_{n}=0\ \te
xt{for all }n.$$

From $u(x,0)=f(x)$:

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}\sin(nx)=f(x)=-\frac{x(\pi^{2}-x^{2})}{6a^{2}},$$

so the coefficients are (in integral form, as the hint permits)

$$\boxed{A_{n}=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}f(x)\sin(nx)\,dx=-\frac{1}{3a^{2}\pi}\
int_{0}^{\pi}x(\pi^{2}-x^{2})\sin(nx)\,dx.}$$

### 3. Evaluating the coefficients

Let $I_{n}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}x(\pi^{2}-x^{2})\sin(nx)\,dx=\pi^{2}\!\int
_{0}^{\pi}x\sin(nx)\,dx-\int_{0}^{\pi}x^{3}\sin(nx)\,dx$.

Standard integrations by parts give

$$\int_{0}^{\pi}x\sin(nx)\,dx=\frac{\pi(-1)^{n+1}}{n},$$

$$\int_{0}^{\pi}x^{3}\sin(nx)\,dx=\pi(-1)^{n}\!\left(\frac{6}{n^{3}}-\frac{\pi^{
2}}{n}\right).$$

Therefore

$$I_{n}=\frac{\pi^{3}(-1)^{n+1}}{n}-\pi(-1)^{n}\!\left(\frac{6}{n^{3}}-\frac{\pi
^{2}}{n}\right)=\frac{\pi^{3}(-1)^{n+1}}{n}+\frac{\pi^{3}(-1)^{n+1}}{n}-\frac{6\
pi(-1)^{n}}{n^{3}}.$$

Wait — carefully: the second term is $-\pi(-1)^{n}(6/n^{3}-\pi^{2}/n)=\pi(-1)^{n
+1}(6/n^{3}-\pi^{2}/n)=\frac{6\pi(-1)^{n+1}}{n^{3}}-\frac{\pi^{3}(-1)^{n+1}}{n}$
. Adding the first term:

$$I_{n}=\frac{\pi^{3}(-1)^{n+1}}{n}+\frac{6\pi(-1)^{n+1}}{n^{3}}-\frac{\pi^{3}(-
1)^{n+1}}{n}=\frac{6\pi(-1)^{n+1}}{n^{3}}.$$

Hence

$$A_{n}=-\frac{1}{3a^{2}\pi}\cdot\frac{6\pi(-1)^{n+1}}{n^{3}}=\frac{2(-1)^{n}}{a
^{2}n^{3}}.$$

### 4. The solution

$$\boxed{\,y(x,t)=\frac{x(\pi^{2}-x^{2})}{6a^{2}}+\frac{2}{a^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\in
fty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n^{3}}\cos(ant)\,\sin(nx).\,}$$

### 5. Verification

- **PDE.** $y_{tt}=u_{tt}=a^{2}u_{xx}$ and $y_{xx}=u_{xx}+\phi''=u_{xx}-x/a^{2}$
, so $a^{2}y_{xx}+x=a^{2}u_{xx}-x+x=a^{2}u_{xx}=y_{tt}$. ✓
- **Boundary.** $\phi(0)=\phi(\pi)=0$ and $\sin(0)=\sin(n\pi)=0$, so $y(0,t)=y(\
pi,t)=0$. ✓
- **Initial displacement.** Using the classical Fourier series $\displaystyle\su
m_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}\sin(nx)}{n^{3}}=\frac{x(\pi^{2}-x^{2})}{12}$ on
 $[0,\pi]$, we get $u(x,0)=\frac{2}{a^{2}}\cdot\!\Big(-\frac{x(\pi^{2}-x^{2})}{1
2}\Big)=-\frac{x(\pi^{2}-x^{2})}{6a^{2}}=-\phi(x)$, hence $y(x,0)=\phi(x)+u(x,0)
=0$. ✓
- **Initial velocity.** $y_{t}(x,0)=u_{t}(x,0)=\sum A_{n}\cdot(-an)\sin(0\cdot\l
dots)$; more directly, $u_{t}=-\sum A_{n}\,an\,\sin(ant)\sin(nx)$, which vanishe
s at $t=0$. ✓

All conditions are satisfied, so the displayed formula is the (unique) solution.
