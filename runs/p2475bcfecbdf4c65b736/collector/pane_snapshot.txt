## Proof

**Goal.** Find a closed form for $\displaystyle S_a = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{
\ln n}{n^a}$, $a>1$.

---

### Step 1. Convergence

For $a>1$, pick $\varepsilon>0$ with $a-\varepsilon>1$. Since $\ln n = o(n^\vare
psilon)$, we have $\frac{\ln n}{n^a} \le \frac{C}{n^{a-\varepsilon}}$ for all la
rge $n$, and $\sum n^{-(a-\varepsilon)}$ converges. Hence $S_a$ converges (absol
utely) for every $a>1$.

### Step 2. Termwise differentiation of the zeta function

The Riemann zeta function
$$\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^{-s}$$
converges absolutely and locally uniformly on the half-plane $\{s\in\mathbb{C}:\
Re(s)>1\}$, so it is holomorphic there and may be differentiated term by term on
 compact subsets. Differentiating each summand $n^{-s}=e^{-s\ln n}$,
$$\frac{d}{ds}\,n^{-s}=-(\ln n)\,n^{-s},$$
gives
$$\zeta'(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}-(\ln n)\,n^{-s}=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ln n}
{n^{s}},\qquad \Re(s)>1.$$

### Step 3. Closed form

Restricting to the real value $s=a>1$,
$$\boxed{\;S_a=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ln n}{n^{a}}=-\,\zeta'(a),\qquad a>1.\;
}$$

### Step 4. Equivalent form via the functional equation (optional refinement)

Using the symmetric functional equation $\zeta(s)=2^{s}\pi^{s-1}\sin\!\bigl(\tfr
ac{\pi s}{2}\bigr)\,\Gamma(1-s)\,\zeta(1-s)$ and taking the logarithmic derivati
ve (with $\psi=\Gamma'/\Gamma$ the digamma function) yields
$$\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)}=\ln(2\pi)+\frac{\pi}{2}\cot\!\Bigl(\frac{\pi s}{2}\
Bigr)-\psi(1-s)-\frac{\zeta'(1-s)}{\zeta(1-s)},$$
so an equivalent closed form is
$$S_a=-\zeta(a)\!\left[\ln(2\pi)+\frac{\pi}{2}\cot\!\Bigl(\frac{\pi a}{2}\Bigr)-
\psi(1-a)-\frac{\zeta'(1-a)}{\zeta(1-a)}\right],\qquad a>1.$$

The primary closed form is $S_a=-\zeta'(a)$.
