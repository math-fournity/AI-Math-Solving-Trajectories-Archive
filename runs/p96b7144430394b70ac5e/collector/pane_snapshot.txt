Looking at this problem, I need to count regions formed when two adjacent corner
s of a square each send out 7 lines (to make 8 equal-area parts).

## Setup

Place the square with corners at (0,0), (1,0), (1,1), (0,1). Lines emanate from
**(0,0)** and the adjacent corner **(1,0)**.

**Lines from (0,0):** A line to (1, h) sweeps area h/2; a line to (x, 1) sweeps
cumulative area 1 − x/2. Setting cumulative areas to k/8 for k = 1,…,7 gives 7 l
ines to:
- (1, 1/4), (1, 1/2), (1, 3/4), (1, 1) — slopes s = 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, 1
- (3/4, 1), (1/2, 1), (1/4, 1) — slopes s = 4/3, 2, 4

**Lines from (1,0):** By symmetry, 7 lines to (0, 1/4), (0, 1/2), (0, 3/4), (0,
1), (1/4, 1), (1/2, 1), (3/4, 1) with coefficients c = 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, 1, 4/3, 2,
 4.

## Counting Interior Intersections

A line from (0,0) with slope s and a line from (1,0) with coefficient c intersec
t at (c/(s+c), sc/(s+c)). This lies **strictly inside** the square iff (s−1)(c−1
) < 1.

- When s ≤ 1 or c ≤ 1: the product (s−1)(c−1) ≤ 0 < 1, so always interior. That'
s 4×7 + 3×4 = 40 pairs (but avoiding double-count: 7×7 − 3×3 = 40 pairs with at
least one ≤ 1).
- When both s > 1, c > 1 (9 pairs from {4/3, 2, 4}²):
  - (4/3, 4/3): (1/3)² = 1/9 < 1 ✓
  - (4/3, 2): 1/3 < 1 ✓; (2, 4/3): 1/3 < 1 ✓
  - (4/3, 4): 1 = 1 → **boundary** at (3/4, 1)
  - (2, 2): 1 = 1 → **boundary** at (1/2, 1)
  - (4, 4/3): 1 = 1 → **boundary** at (1/4, 1)
  - (2, 4): 3 > 1 ✗; (4, 2): 3 > 1 ✗; (4, 4): 9 > 1 ✗

So: **3 boundary intersections** (at existing top-edge vertices) and **3 non-int
ersecting pairs**.

**Interior intersections = 49 − 3 − 3 = 43.**

## Applying Euler's Formula
