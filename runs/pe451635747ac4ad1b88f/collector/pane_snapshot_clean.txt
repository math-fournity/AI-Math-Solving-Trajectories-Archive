The statement is **false**. Here is a counterexample and the general reason.

## Counterexample

Take $X = \mathbb{RP}^2$, the real projective plane. Its first homology group is

$$H_1(\mathbb{RP}^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.$$

Now compute the dual:

$$\text{Hom}(H_1(\mathbb{RP}^2),\, \mathbb{Z}) = \text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{
Z},\, \mathbb{Z}).$$

Any homomorphism $\varphi: \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}$ must send the
generator $[1]$ to an element $a \in \mathbb{Z}$ satisfying $2a = 0$ (since $2[1
] = 0$ in the domain). The only such integer is $a = 0$, so $\varphi$ is the zer
o map. Hence

$$\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},\, \mathbb{Z}) = 0.$$

Therefore

$$H_1(\mathbb{RP}^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \;\not\cong\; 0 = \text{Hom}(H
_1(\mathbb{RP}^2),\, \mathbb{Z}),$$

contradicting the claimed isomorphism.

## General reason

For any finitely generated abelian group, the structure theorem gives

$$H_n(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}^r \oplus T$$

where $T$ is the (finite) torsion subgroup. Since $\text{Hom}(T, \mathbb{Z}) = 0
$ for any torsion group $T$ (the same divisibility argument as above), we have

$$\text{Hom}(H_n(X),\, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^r.$$

Thus $H_n(X) \cong \text{Hom}(H_n(X), \mathbb{Z})$ holds **if and only if** $H_n
(X)$ is free abelian (i.e., $T = 0$). Whenever $H_n(X)$ has torsion — as occurs
for $\mathbb{RP}^2$, lens spaces, and countless other spaces — the torsion part
is killed by $\text{Hom}(-, \mathbb{Z})$, and the isomorphism fails.

This is precisely why the Universal Coefficient Theorem for cohomology includes
the $\text{Ext}$ correction term:

$$0 \to \text{Ext}(H_{n-1}(X), \mathbb{Z}) \to H^n(X) \to \text{Hom}(H_n(X), \ma
thbb{Z}) \to 0,$$

reflecting the fact that $\text{Hom}(-, \mathbb{Z})$ loses torsion information.
