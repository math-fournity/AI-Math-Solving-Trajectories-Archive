## Proof

**Answer: True.**

The interval $(0,1)$ with the usual topology can be equipped with a complete met
ric.

**Construction.** Consider the map
$$h:(0,1)\to\mathbb{R},\qquad h(x)=\tan\!\bigl(\pi(x-\tfrac12)\bigr).$$
This is a homeomorphism: it is continuous, strictly increasing, with $h(x)\to-\i
nfty$ as $x\to 0^+$ and $h(x)\to+\infty$ as $x\to 1^-$, so it is a bijection ont
o $\mathbb{R}$ with continuous inverse $h^{-1}(t)=\frac12+\frac{1}{\pi}\arctan(t
)$.

Define a metric on $(0,1)$ by
$$d(x,y)=|h(x)-h(y)|.$$

**$d$ is a metric.** Positivity, symmetry, and the triangle law follow immediate
ly from the corresponding properties of the absolute value on $\mathbb{R}$, sinc
e $h$ is injective.

**$d$ induces the usual topology on $(0,1)$.** A set $U\subseteq(0,1)$ is $d$-op
en iff $h(U)$ is open in $\mathbb{R}$ (under the usual metric), which (since $h$
 is a homeomorphism for the usual topologies) holds iff $U$ is open in the usual
 topology of $(0,1)$.

**$((0,1),d)$ is complete.** Let $(x_n)$ be a $d$-Cauchy sequence. Then $(h(x_n)
)$ is Cauchy in $\mathbb{R}$ with the usual metric. Since $\mathbb{R}$ is comple
te, there exists $t\in\mathbb{R}$ with $h(x_n)\to t$. Set $x=h^{-1}(t)\in(0,1)$.
 By continuity of $h^{-1}$, $x_n=h^{-1}(h(x_n))\to h^{-1}(t)=x$ in the $d$-metri
c. Hence every $d$-Cauchy sequence converges in $(0,1)$, so $d$ is complete.

**Remark.** This does not contradict the well-known fact that $(0,1)$ is *not* c
omplete under its *usual* (Euclidean) metric — the sequence $x_n=1/n$ is Euclide
an-Cauchy but has no limit in $(0,1)$. The point is that completeness is a prope
rty of the metric, not of the topology. A topological space is called *completel
y metrizable* if *some* metric inducing its topology is complete. The argument a
bove shows $(0,1)$ is completely metrizable, which is also consistent with the B
aire category theorem characterization: $(0,1)$ is a $G_\delta$ subset of the co
mplete metric space $\mathbb{R}$, and by Alexandrov's theorem every $G_\delta$ s
ubset of a complete metric space is completely metrizable.
