## Proof

Let $r = \operatorname{rank}(G)$, the dimension of a maximal torus $T$ of $G$. W
e prove that

$$\deg(\psi_k) = k^r.$$

### Step 1. Rational cohomology of $G$

By Hopf's theorem, the rational cohomology of a compact connected Lie group is a
 free exterior algebra:

$$H^*(G; \mathbb{Q}) = \Lambda(x_1, \dots, x_r),$$

where $x_i$ has odd degree $n_i = 2d_i - 1$ with $d_1, \dots, d_r$ the degrees o
f the fundamental polynomial invariants of the Weyl group $W$ of $G$. The number
 of generators equals $r = \operatorname{rank}(G)$, and $\sum_i n_i = \dim G$.

### Step 2. Rational homotopy type of $G$

Since $H^*(G;\mathbb{Q})$ is a free exterior algebra (the minimal model has zero
 differential), $G$ is rationally formal and its rational homotopy type is a pro
duct of Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces:

$$G \simeq_{\mathbb{Q}} \prod_{i=1}^r K(\mathbb{Q}, n_i),$$

with $\pi_{n_i}(G) \otimes \mathbb{Q} \cong \mathbb{Q}$ and $\pi_m(G)\otimes\mat
hbb{Q} = 0$ for $m \notin \{n_1,\dots,n_r\}$ (for $m\geq 2$; note $\pi_1(G)$ is
finite, so $\pi_1(G)\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0$).

### Step 3. Action of $\psi_k$ on homotopy groups

For a topological group $G$ and $n \geq 1$, the group operation on $\pi_n(G)$ is
 induced by **pointwise multiplication** in $G$: given $f, g: (S^n, *) \to (G, e
)$, the product $(f \cdot g)(x) = f(x)\,g(x)$ represents $[f] + [g]$ in $\pi_n(G
)$ (by the Eckmann–Hilton argument, this agrees with the usual concatenation ope
ration; $\pi_1(G)$ is abelian since $G$ is a topological group).

Now compute $\psi_{k*}[f] = [\psi_k \circ f]$. We have

$$(\psi_k \circ f)(x) = f(x)^k = \underbrace{f(x) \cdots f(x)}_{k \text{ factors
}} = (f \cdot f \cdots f)(x),$$

so $[\psi_k \circ f] = k[f]$ in $\pi_n(G)$. Therefore

$$\psi_{k*} = k \cdot \mathrm{id} \quad \text{on } \pi_n(G) \text{ for all } n \
geq 1.$$

### Step 4. Action on rational cohomology

Under the rational equivalence $G \simeq_{\mathbb{Q}} \prod_i K(\mathbb{Q}, n_i)
$, the map $\psi_k$ corresponds to the product of maps $K(\mathbb{Q}, n_i) \to K
(\mathbb{Q}, n_i)$, each acting as multiplication by $k$ on $\pi_{n_i}$. A self-
map of $K(\mathbb{Q}, n)$ (with $n$ odd) acting by $k$ on $\pi_n$ induces multip
lication by $k$ on $H^n(K(\mathbb{Q},n);\mathbb{Q}) = \mathbb{Q}\cdot \iota_n$.
Since $\phi^*: H^*(G;\mathbb{Q}) \xrightarrow{\sim} H^*(\prod K(\mathbb{Q},n_i);
\mathbb{Q})$ is an isomorphism sending $x_i \mapsto \iota_{n_i}$ (up to nonzero
scalar), we obtain

$$\psi_k^*(x_i) = k \cdot x_i, \quad i = 1, \dots, r.$$

### Step 5. The degree

The top-dimensional cohomology $H^{\dim G}(G; \mathbb{Q})$ is one-dimensional, g
enerated by $x_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge x_r$. By Step 4,

$$\psi_k^*(x_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge x_r) = (k\,x_1) \wedge \cdots \wedge (k\,x_r
) = k^r\,(x_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge x_r).$$

The mapping degree is the scalar by which the map acts on top-degree cohomology,
 so

$$\boxed{\deg(\psi_k) = k^{\operatorname{rank}(G)}}.$$
