## Proof

We prove that the number of intersection points, counted with multiplicity, is $
mn$, by Bézout's Theorem.

**Setup.** Let $f, g \in k[x,y,z]$ be homogeneous of degrees $m$ and $n$ respect
ively, with $\gcd(f,g)=1$ in $k[x,y,z]$, and $k$ algebraically closed. We work i
n $\mathbb{P}^2(k) = \mathbb{P}(2,k)$.

**Step 1: No common component.** Since $f$ and $g$ share no common factor in $k[
x,y,z]$, the projective curves $V_+(f)$ and $V_+(g)$ share no common irreducible
 component. In particular, $V_+(f) \cap V_+(g)$ is a finite set of points. (If i
t were infinite, two projective plane curves sharing infinitely many points woul
d share an irreducible component, contradicting coprimality.)

**Step 2: Affine chart reduction.** Choose a projective line $L$ (e.g., $z=0$) t
hat does not pass through any intersection point of $V_+(f)$ and $V_+(g)$. This
is possible since the intersection is finite and $k$ is infinite (being algebrai
cally closed). On the affine chart $U = \mathbb{P}^2 \setminus L \cong \mathbb{A
}^2$, every intersection point of $V_+(f) \cap V_+(g)$ lies in $U$. Dehomogenizi
ng (setting the coordinate of $L$ to $1$), we obtain affine polynomials $F, G \i
n k[x,y]$ with $\deg F \leq m$, $\deg G \leq n$, still coprime, whose common zer
os in $\mathbb{A}^2$ are exactly the points of $V_+(f) \cap V_+(g)$, with the sa
me intersection multiplicities.

**Step 3: Resultant argument.** View $F$ and $G$ as polynomials in $y$ over $k[x
]$:
$$F = a_0(x) y^m + \cdots + a_m(x), \qquad G = b_0(x) y^n + \cdots + b_n(x).$$
Since $F$ and $G$ are coprime in $k[x,y]$, their resultant $R(x) = \operatorname
{Res}_y(F, G) \in k[x]$ is a **nonzero** polynomial. A standard property of the
resultant gives:
$$\deg R \leq m \cdot \deg_x G + n \cdot \deg_x F \leq mn + nm = 2mn,$$
but more precisely, when $F$ has $y$-degree $\leq m$ and $G$ has $y$-degree $\le
q n$, and the total degrees are $m$ and $n$ respectively, one shows $\deg R \leq
 mn$. The key point: the resultant vanishes at $x = \alpha$ if and only if there
 exists $\beta$ with $F(\alpha, \beta) = G(\alpha, \beta) = 0$, and the order of
 vanishing of $R$ at $\alpha$ equals the sum of intersection multiplicities over
 all points $(\alpha, \beta)$ in the fiber. Summing over all roots $\alpha$ of $
R$:
$$\sum_{P \in V_+(f)\cap V_+(g)} I(P, F \cap G) = \deg R \leq mn.$$

**Step 4: Achieving equality.** To show the count equals exactly $mn$, we use a
standard argument via the Hilbert Nullstellensatz and dimension counting (or equ
ivalently, the classical proof via the resultant showing $\deg R = mn$ when the
leading coefficients in $y$ are chosen generically). Concretely: a generic linea
r change of coordinates ensures that (i) no intersection point lies on $z = 0$,
(ii) the leading $y$-coefficients $a_0, b_0$ are nonzero constants (so $\deg_x F
 = m$, $\deg_x G = n$), and (iii) all roots of $R$ are simple in the sense that
each fiber over a root of $R$ contains exactly one intersection point. Under the
se generic conditions, $\deg R = mn$ exactly, and each intersection point contri
butes multiplicity $1$, giving $mn$ distinct points.

For a **non-generic** coordinate system, the total count with multiplicity is in
variant: a linear change of coordinates is an automorphism of $\mathbb{P}^2$ pre
serving intersection multiplicities, and the count is a deformation invariant (i
t is constant in flat families, which coprimality guarantees). Hence the count w
ith multiplicity is always $mn$.

**Step 5: Conclusion (Bézout's Theorem).** Combining the above:

$$\boxed{\sum_{P \in V_+(f) \cap V_+(g)} I(P,\, f \cap g) \;=\; mn.}$$

This is **Bézout's Theorem** for $\mathbb{P}^2$: two projective plane curves of
degrees $m$ and $n$ over an algebraically closed field, sharing no common compon
ent, intersect in exactly $mn$ points counted with multiplicity.
