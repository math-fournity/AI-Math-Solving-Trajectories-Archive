## Solution

We evaluate
$$I = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\int_{-1}^{1} \delta(\sin 2x)\,\delta(x-y)\,dy\,dx.$$

**Step 1: Inner integral over $y$.**

Using the substitution $u = x - y$ (so $dy = -du$; when $y=-1$, $u=x+1$; when $y
=1$, $u=x-1$):
$$\int_{-1}^{1}\delta(x-y)\,dy = \int_{x-1}^{x+1}\delta(u)\,du = \begin{cases}1,
 & 0\in[x-1,\,x+1],\\[2pt] 0, & \text{otherwise},\end{cases} = \mathbf{1}_{[-1,\
,1]}(x).$$

**Step 2: Reduce to a single integral.**

$$I = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\delta(\sin 2x)\,\mathbf{1}_{[-1,\,1]}(x)\,dx.$$

**Step 3: Expand $\delta(\sin 2x)$.**

The zeros of $f(x)=\sin 2x$ in $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ are $x\in\{-\pi/2,\,0,\,\pi/2\}$
. Since $f'(x)=2\cos 2x$:
- $|f'(0)| = |2\cos 0| = 2$,
- $|f'(\pm\pi/2)| = |2\cos(\pm\pi)| = 2$.

By the standard identity $\delta(f(x))=\sum_i \frac{\delta(x-x_i)}{|f'(x_i)|}$:
$$\delta(\sin 2x) = \tfrac{1}{2}\delta(x) + \tfrac{1}{2}\delta\!\left(x-\tfrac{\
pi}{2}\right) + \tfrac{1}{2}\delta\!\left(x+\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right).$$

**Step 4: Apply the indicator $\mathbf{1}_{[-1,1]}(x)$.**

- $x=0\in[-1,1]$: contributes $\tfrac{1}{2}$.
- $x=\pi/2\approx 1.5708\notin[-1,1]$: contributes $0$.
- $x=-\pi/2\approx -1.5708\notin[-1,1]$: contributes $0$.

(The boundary points $x=\pm1$ of the indicator are irrelevant, since none of the
 zeros of $\sin 2x$ coincide with $\pm1$.)

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

$$I = \frac{1}{2}.$$
