# Girsanov's Theorem for a Single-Valued Itô Process

## Setup

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \{\mathcal{F}_t\}_{t \ge 0}, \mathbb{P})$ be a filter
ed probability space satisfying the usual conditions, and let $\{W_t\}_{t \ge 0}
$ be a standard $\mathcal{F}_t$-Brownian motion under $\mathbb{P}$. Let the filt
ration be the $\mathbb{P}$-augmentation of the natural filtration of $W$ (or mor
e generally, assume $W$ is an $\mathcal{F}_t$-Brownian motion with $\mathcal{F}_
t = \mathcal{F}_t^W$ up to null sets).

## The Process

Let $\{\theta_t\}_{0 \le t \le T}$ be an $\mathcal{F}_t$-adapted, progressively
measurable, **real-valued** (single-valued, i.e. scalar) process satisfying the
**Novikov condition**

$$
\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\!\left[\exp\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\int_0^T \theta_s^2\, ds\
right)\right] < \infty.
$$

Define the exponential martingale (Doléans–Dade exponential)

$$
Z_t \;=\; \exp\!\left(-\int_0^t \theta_s\, dW_s \;-\; \tfrac{1}{2}\int_0^t \thet
a_s^2\, ds\right), \qquad 0 \le t \le T.
$$

## Conditions

1. **Adaptedness and measurability.** $\theta$ is $\mathcal{F}_t$-progressively
measurable.
2. **Integrability.** $\int_0^T \theta_s^2\, ds < \infty$ $\mathbb{P}$-a.s., so
that the stochastic integral $\int_0^t \theta_s\, dW_s$ is well defined.
3. **Novikov condition** (sufficient, not necessary). $\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\!
\big[\exp\big(\tfrac{1}{2}\int_0^T \theta_s^2\, ds\big)\big] < \infty$.

Under the Novikov condition, $\{Z_t\}$ is a **strictly positive martingale** on
$[0,T]$ with $\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}[Z_T] = 1$.

## Change of Measure

Define a new probability measure $\mathbb{Q}$ on $\mathcal{F}_T$ by its Radon–Ni
kodym density with respect to $\mathbb{P}$:

$$
\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}\Big|_{\mathcal{F}_T} \;=\; Z_T \;=\; \exp\!\left
(-\int_0^T \theta_s\, dW_s \;-\; \tfrac{1}{2}\int_0^T \theta_s^2\, ds\right).
$$

Because $Z_T > 0$ $\mathbb{P}$-a.s. and $\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}[Z_T] = 1$, the
measure $\mathbb{Q}$ is a probability measure **equivalent** to $\mathbb{P}$ on
$\mathcal{F}_T$ (i.e. $\mathbb{Q} \sim \mathbb{P}$).

## Transformation of the Brownian Motion (Conclusion)

Define the process

$$
\widetilde{W}_t \;:=\; W_t \;+\; \int_0^t \theta_s\, ds, \qquad 0 \le t \le T.
$$

Then **$\{\widetilde{W}_t\}_{0 \le t \le T}$ is a standard $\mathcal{F}_t$-Brown
ian motion under $\mathbb{Q}$**.

Equivalently, under $\mathbb{Q}$ the original process $W_t$ has drift $-\int_0^t
 \theta_s\, ds$:

$$
W_t \;=\; \widetilde{W}_t \;-\; \int_0^t \theta_s\, ds,
$$

so that an Itô process

$$
dX_t \;=\; b_t\, dt \;+\; \sigma_t\, dW_t
$$

under $\mathbb{P}$ becomes, under $\mathbb{Q}$,

$$
dX_t \;=\; \big(b_t - \sigma_t\,\theta_t\big)\, dt \;+\; \sigma_t\, d\widetilde{
W}_t.
$$

In particular, choosing $\theta_t = b_t/\sigma_t$ (when $\sigma_t \neq 0$) remov
es the drift, yielding the driftless process $dX_t = \sigma_t\, d\widetilde{W}_t
$ under $\mathbb{Q}$ — the standard use in mathematical finance for passing from
 the physical measure to the risk-neutral measure.

## Remarks on the Conditions

- **Novikov is sufficient but not necessary.** A weaker sufficient condition is
**Kazamaki's condition**: $\sup_{0 \le t \le T} \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\!\big[\e
xp\big(\tfrac{1}{2}\int_0^t \theta_s\, dW_s\big)\big] < \infty$. The weakest gen
eral statement requires only that $\{Z_t\}$ be a *true* martingale (not merely a
 local martingale) on $[0,T]$, i.e. $\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}[Z_T] = 1$.
- **Equivalence.** Because $Z_T > 0$ $\mathbb{P}$-a.s., the measures are equival
ent on $\mathcal{F}_T$; only null sets are shared. If one allows $Z_T = 0$ on a
set of positive $\mathbb{P}$-measure, $\mathbb{Q}$ becomes merely *absolutely co
ntinuous* (a singular extension), and the theorem must be stated with care.
- **Finite horizon.** The theorem is stated on a finite interval $[0,T]$. On $[0
,\infty)$ one requires the martingale property on every $[0,T]$, and $\mathbb{Q}
$ is then defined consistently on $\mathcal{F}_\infty = \bigvee_T \mathcal{F}_T$
.
- **Single-valued (scalar) case.** Here $\theta$ and $W$ are $\mathbb{R}$-valued
. In the multivariate version, $W$ is $\mathbb{R}^d$-valued, $\theta$ is $\mathb
b{R}^d$-valued, the Novikov condition reads $\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big(\tfrac{1}{2
}\int_0^T \|\theta_s\|^2\, ds\big)\big] < \infty$, and $\widetilde{W}_t = W_t +
\int_0^t \theta_s\, ds$ is a $d$-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}$-Brownian motion.
