## Proof

**Setup.** Let $g(y) = \Gamma(1+y)$ be the factorial function (extended via the
Gamma function), and let $f_k(x) = g^{\circ k}(x)$ denote its $k$-fold iterate,
so that
$$f_1(x)=x!,\quad f_2(x)=(x!)!,\quad f_3(x)=((x!)!)!,\ \ldots$$
The product in question is $P(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty} f_k(x)$, and we must show
$P(x)^{-1/x}\to e$ as $x\to 0$, equivalently $\frac{\ln P(x)}{x}\to -1$.

**Key properties of $g$.** Since $g(y)=\Gamma(1+y)$ and $g'(y)=\Gamma(1+y)\,\psi
(1+y)$:
- $g(0)=\Gamma(1)=1$, $\ g(1)=\Gamma(2)=1$ — so $y=1$ is a fixed point.
- $g'(0)=\psi(1)=-\gamma$, $\ g'(1)=\psi(2)=1-\gamma$, where $\gamma\approx 0.57
72$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant.
- $|g'(1)|=|1-\gamma|\approx 0.4228<1$, so $y=1$ is an **attracting** fixed poin
t.

**Linearization near the fixed point.** Set $\delta_k := f_k(x)-1$ and $\epsilon
:=\delta_1=f_1(x)-1=g(x)-1$. For $x$ near $0$:
$$\epsilon = g(x)-1 = g'(0)\,x+O(x^2)=-\gamma\,x+O(x^2)=O(x).$$
For $k\ge 2$, expanding $g$ around the fixed point $1$:
$$\delta_k = g(1+\delta_{k-1})-1 = (1-\gamma)\,\delta_{k-1}+O(\delta_{k-1}^2).$$
Setting $\lambda:=1-\gamma$ (with $|\lambda|<1$), a standard induction gives
$$\delta_k = \lambda^{k-1}\epsilon + O(\epsilon^2), \qquad\text{uniformly in }k,
$$
and in particular $|\delta_k|\le C\,|\lambda|^{k-1}|\epsilon|$ for all $k\ge 1$.

**Convergence of the infinite product.** Since $|\ln f_k(x)|=|\ln(1+\delta_k)|\l
e 2|\delta_k|\le C'|\lambda|^{k-1}|x|$ and $|\lambda|<1$, the series $\sum_k \ln
 f_k(x)$ converges absolutely, so $P(x)$ is well-defined for $x$ near $0$ (on bo
th sides, since all $f_k(x)>0$).

**Computing $\ln P(x)$.** We have
$$\ln P(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\ln f_k(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigl[\delta_k+O(\de
lta_k^2)\bigr].$$
The error term satisfies $\sum_k O(\delta_k^2)=O\!\bigl(\epsilon^2\sum|\lambda|^
{2(k-1)}\bigr)=O(\epsilon^2)=O(x^2)$. For the main term, using $\delta_k=\lambda
^{k-1}\epsilon+O(\epsilon^2)$:
$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\delta_k=\frac{\epsilon}{1-\lambda}+O(\epsilon^2)=\frac{\ep
silon}{\gamma}+O(x^2).$$
Substituting $\epsilon=-\gamma\,x+O(x^2)$:
$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\delta_k=\frac{-\gamma\,x+O(x^2)}{\gamma}+O(x^2)=-x+O(x^2).
$$
Therefore
$$\ln P(x)=-x+O(x^2).$$

**The limit.** Dividing by $x$ and negating:
$$\frac{-\ln P(x)}{x}=1+O(x)\xrightarrow{x\to 0}1.$$
Exponentiating:
$$P(x)^{-1/x}=\exp\!\left(\frac{-\ln P(x)}{x}\right)\xrightarrow{x\to 0}e^1=e.$$

The limit is **true**. $\blacksquare$
