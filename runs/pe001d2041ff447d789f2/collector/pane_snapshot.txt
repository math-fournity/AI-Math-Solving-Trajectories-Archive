## Problem Setup

Consider a hemisphere of radius $a$ occupying $0 \le r \le a$, $0 \le \theta \le
 \pi/2$ (upper hemisphere, $\theta$ measured from the $z$-axis). The temperature
 $T(r,\theta)$ satisfies:

- **Laplace's equation:** $\nabla^2 T = 0$ inside the hemisphere.
- **Curved surface** ($r = a$, $0 \le \theta \le \pi/2$): $T = \cos^2\theta$.
- **Equatorial plane** ($\theta = \pi/2$, $0 \le r \le a$): $T = 0$.

By axial symmetry, $T = T(r,\theta)$ only.

## Method: Odd Extension to Full Sphere

The key observation is that the boundary condition $T = 0$ on the equatorial pla
ne can be enforced by **antisymmetric extension** to the full sphere. Define the
 extended problem on the full ball $0 \le r \le a$, $0 \le \theta \le \pi$ with
boundary data on $r = a$:

$$f(\theta) = \begin{cases} \cos^2\theta, & 0 \le \theta \le \pi/2 \\ -\cos^2\th
eta, & \pi/2 \le \theta \le \pi \end{cases}$$

This satisfies $f(\pi - \theta) = -f(\theta)$, so the solution is antisymmetric
about $\theta = \pi/2$, automatically giving $T = 0$ on the equatorial plane. Th
e solution in the upper hemisphere is then the desired answer.

In terms of $x = \cos\theta$, the boundary function is $g(x) = x|x|$ on $[-1, 1]
$, which is an **odd function** of $x$. Therefore only **odd Legendre polynomial
s** $P_{2n+1}(x)$ appear in the expansion.

## Series Solution

The general solution to Laplace's equation in a sphere (regular at the origin) i
s:

$$T(r,\theta) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} A_{2n+1}\left(\frac{r}{a}\right)^{2n+1} P_{2
n+1}(\cos\theta)$$

where the coefficients are determined by the boundary condition at $r = a$:

$$A_{2n+1} = (4n+3)\int_0^1 x^2\, P_{2n+1}(x)\, dx$$

(using the orthogonality of Legendre polynomials and the odd symmetry, which red
uces the integral to $[0,1]$).

### Computing $A_1$ ($n = 0$)

$$A_1 = 3\int_0^1 x^2 \cdot x\, dx = 3 \cdot \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4}$$

### General formula for $A_{2n+1}$ ($n \ge 1$)

Using the Rodrigues formula $P_l(x) = \frac{1}{2^l\, l!}\frac{d^l}{dx^l}(x^2-1)^
l$ and integrating by parts $l$ times with $u = x^2$:

Since $\frac{d^l}{dx^l}(x^2) = 0$ for $l \ge 3$, the integral reduces to boundar
y terms. At $x=1$, all derivatives of $(x^2-1)^l$ of order $< l$ vanish. At $x=0
$, only the term with $g''(0) = 2$ survives (since $g(0) = g'(0) = 0$). This giv
es:

$$\int_0^1 x^2 P_{2n+1}(x)\, dx = \frac{(-1)^{n+1}(2n-2)!}{2^{2n}(n-1)!(n+2)!}$$

Therefore:

$$A_{2n+1} = \frac{(4n+3)(-1)^{n+1}(2n-2)!}{2^{2n}(n-1)!(n+2)!}, \quad n \ge 1$$

### Verification of coefficients

- $A_1 = \frac{3}{4}$ ✓
- $A_3 = \frac{7 \cdot (+1) \cdot 0!}{4 \cdot 0! \cdot 3!} = \frac{7}{24}$ ✓
- $A_5 = \frac{11 \cdot (-1) \cdot 2!}{16 \cdot 1! \cdot 4!} = -\frac{11}{192}$
✓
- $A_7 = \frac{15 \cdot (+1) \cdot 4!}{64 \cdot 2! \cdot 5!} = \frac{3}{128}$ ✓

## Verification of Boundary Conditions

1. **Curved surface** ($r = a$, $0 \le \theta \le \pi/2$): $T(a,\theta) = \sum A
_{2n+1} P_{2n+1}(\cos\theta) = g(\cos\theta) = \cos^2\theta$ since $\cos\theta \
ge 0$. ✓

2. **Equatorial plane** ($\theta = \pi/2$): $P_{2n+1}(0) = 0$ for all $n$ (odd L
egendre polynomials vanish at the origin), so $T = 0$. ✓

3. **Harmonicity**: Each term $r^{2n+1}P_{2n+1}(\cos\theta)$ is a solid harmonic
, so $T$ satisfies $\nabla^2 T = 0$. ✓

4. **Regularity**: All powers of $r$ are positive, so $T$ is finite at the origi
n. ✓

## Solution

$$\boxed{T(r,\theta) = \frac{3}{4}\frac{r}{a}\cos\theta + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\fr
ac{(4n+3)\,(-1)^{n+1}\,(2n-2)!}{2^{2n}\,(n-1)!\,(n+2)!}\left(\frac{r}{a}\right)^
{2n+1}P_{2n+1}(\cos\theta)}$$

where $P_{2n+1}$ are Legendre polynomials. The first few terms are:

$$T(r,\theta) = \frac{3}{4}\frac{r}{a}\cos\theta + \frac{7}{24}\left(\frac{r}{a}
\right)^3 P_3(\cos\theta) - \frac{11}{192}\left(\frac{r}{a}\right)^5 P_5(\cos\th
eta) + \frac{3}{128}\left(\frac{r}{a}\right)^7 P_7(\cos\theta) + \cdots$$
