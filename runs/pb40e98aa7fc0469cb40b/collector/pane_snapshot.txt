## Proof

**Claim:** The statement is **true**.

**Step 1: Simplify the ring via base change.**

We use the standard isomorphism for scalar extension of a quotient algebra. Sinc
e $\mathbb{Q}[x]/(f) \cong \mathbb{Q}[x]/(f)$ as a $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra, extendi
ng scalars from $\mathbb{Q}$ to $\mathbb{C}$ gives:

$$\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}[x]/(f) \;\cong\; \mathbb{C}[x]/(f),$
$

where on the right-hand side $(f)$ denotes the ideal generated by $f$ viewed as
a polynomial in $\mathbb{C}[x]$.

*Justification:* The map $\varphi: \mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}[x] \
to \mathbb{C}[x]$, $\varphi(c\otimes g) = c\cdot g$, is a $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra i
somorphism (both sides are free $\mathbb{C}$-modules with basis $\{x^n\}$, and $
\varphi$ sends basis to basis). Under this isomorphism, the ideal $\mathbb{C}\ot
imes_{\mathbb{Q}}(f)\subset \mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}[x]$ maps to
 the ideal $(f)\subset \mathbb{C}[x]$. Taking quotients yields the claimed isomo
rphism.

**Step 2: $\gcd(f,f')=1$ in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ implies $\gcd(f,f')=1$ in $\mathbb{C
}[x]$.**

By Bézout's identity in the PID $\mathbb{Q}[x]$, there exist $a,b\in\mathbb{Q}[x
]$ with

$$a(x)\,f(x) + b(x)\,f'(x) = 1.$$

Since $\mathbb{Q}[x]\subset \mathbb{C}[x]$, this same identity holds in $\mathbb
{C}[x]$. Hence $\gcd(f,f')=1$ in $\mathbb{C}[x]$ as well.

**Step 3: $\gcd(f,f')=1$ in $\mathbb{C}[x]$ means $f$ is squarefree over $\mathb
b{C}$.**

Over the algebraically closed field $\mathbb{C}$, write $f = c\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x-
\alpha_i)^{m_i}$. Then $f' = c\sum_{i} m_i(x-\alpha_i)^{m_i-1}\prod_{j\neq i}(x-
\alpha_j)^{m_j}$. A root $\alpha_i$ is a common root of $f$ and $f'$ if and only
 if $m_i \geq 2$. Thus $\gcd(f,f')=1$ forces every $m_i=1$, i.e., $f$ is squaref
ree in $\mathbb{C}[x]$.

**Step 4: Squarefree $f$ over $\mathbb{C}$ gives a reduced ring.**

With $f = c\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x-\alpha_i)$ and the $\alpha_i$ distinct, the ideals
$(x-\alpha_i)\subset \mathbb{C}[x]$ are pairwise comaximal. By the Chinese Remai
nder Theorem:

$$\mathbb{C}[x]/(f) \;\cong\; \prod_{i=1}^{n} \mathbb{C}[x]/(x-\alpha_i) \;\cong
\; \mathbb{C}^n.$$

Each factor $\mathbb{C}$ is a field, so $\mathbb{C}^n$ is a product of fields. A
 product of fields is **reduced** (has no nonzero nilpotent elements): if $(z_1,
\dots,z_n)^k = 0$ then each $z_i^k=0$, hence each $z_i=0$ (fields are reduced),
so the element is zero.

**Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 1–4:

$$\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}[x]/(f) \;\cong\; \mathbb{C}[x]/(f) \;
\cong\; \mathbb{C}^n,$$

which is reduced. Therefore there are **no nonzero nilpotent elements** in $\mat
hbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}[x]/(f)$. The statement is **true**. $\black
square$
