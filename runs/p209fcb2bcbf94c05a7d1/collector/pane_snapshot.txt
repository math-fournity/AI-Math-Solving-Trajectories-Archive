## Solution

**Setup.** In triangle $ABC$: $\angle A = 180° - 48° - 22° = 110°$. Let $x = \an
gle MAB$, so $\angle MAC = 110° - x$.

The cevians $AM$, $BM$, $CM$ meet at $M$. Reading off the split angles at each v
ertex:

| Vertex | Left part | Right part |
|---|---|---|
| $B$ | $\angle ABM = 48°-38° = 10°$ | $\angle MBC = 38°$ |
| $C$ | $\angle BCM = 18°$ | $\angle MCA = 22°-18° = 4°$ |
| $A$ | $\angle CAM = 110°-x$ | $\angle MAB = x$ |

**Trigonometric Ceva.** Since $AM, BM, CM$ are concurrent at $M$,

$$\frac{\sin\angle ABM}{\sin\angle MBC}\cdot\frac{\sin\angle BCM}{\sin\angle MCA
}\cdot\frac{\sin\angle CAM}{\sin\angle MAB}=1,$$

i.e.

$$\frac{\sin 10°}{\sin 38°}\cdot\frac{\sin 18°}{\sin 4°}\cdot\frac{\sin(110°-x)}
{\sin x}=1,$$

so

$$\frac{\sin(110°-x)}{\sin x}=\frac{\sin 38°\,\sin 4°}{\sin 10°\,\sin 18°}. \tag
{$\star$}$$

**Uniqueness.** The function $f(x)=\sin(110°-x)/\sin x$ has

$$f'(x)=\frac{-\bigl(\cos(110°-x)\sin x+\sin(110°-x)\cos x\bigr)}{\sin^2 x}=\fra
c{-\sin 110°}{\sin^2 x}<0,$$

so $f$ is strictly decreasing on $(0°,110°)$; equation $(\star)$ has at most one
 solution.

**Verifying $x=64°$.** We must show

$$\frac{\sin 46°}{\sin 64°}=\frac{\sin 38°\,\sin 4°}{\sin 10°\,\sin 18°},\qquad\
text{i.e.}\qquad \sin 46°\sin 10°\sin 18°=\sin 64°\sin 38°\sin 4°. \tag{$\dagger
$}$$

Using the identity $4\sin\alpha\sin\beta\sin\gamma=\sin(\alpha{+}\beta{-}\gamma)
+\sin(\beta{+}\gamma{-}\alpha)+\sin(\gamma{+}\alpha{-}\beta)-\sin(\alpha{+}\beta
{+}\gamma)$:

$$4\,\text{LHS}=\sin 38°-\sin 18°+\sin 54°-\sin 74°,$$
$$4\,\text{RHS}=\sin 98°-\sin 22°+\sin 30°-\sin 106°.$$

Since $\sin 98°=\sin 82°$, $\sin 106°=\sin 74°$, $\sin 30°=\tfrac12$:

$$4(\text{LHS}-\text{RHS})=\sin 38°+\sin 22°+\sin 54°-\sin 82°-\sin 18°-\tfrac12
.$$

Now $\sin 38°+\sin 22°=2\sin 30°\cos 8°=\cos 8°$, and $\sin 82°=\cos 8°$, so tho
se cancel:

$$4(\text{LHS}-\text{RHS})=\sin 54°-\sin 18°-\tfrac12=2\cos 36°\sin 18°-\tfrac12
.$$

Using the exact values $\cos 36°=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{4}$ and $\sin 18°=\frac{\sqrt5-
1}{4}$:

$$2\cos 36°\sin 18°=2\cdot\frac{1+\sqrt5}{4}\cdot\frac{\sqrt5-1}{4}=\frac{2\cdot
 4}{16}=\frac12.$$

Hence $4(\text{LHS}-\text{RHS})=\tfrac12-\tfrac12=0$, proving $(\dagger)$.

**Conclusion.** $x=64°$ satisfies $(\star)$, and by strict monotonicity it is th
e unique solution.

$$\boxed{\angle MAB = 64°}$$
