## Proof

**Setup.** Let $G$ be the centroid of $\triangle ABC$. A line through $G$ meets
$AB$ at $E$ and $BC$ at $R$, with $A, E, R, C$ concyclic. We are given $AC = a$
and $EG = b$; we seek $GR$.

**Step 1: Establishing similarity $\triangle RBE \sim \triangle ABC$.**

Since $A, E, R, C$ lie on a circle (in that cyclic order), opposite angles of cy
clic quadrilateral $AERC$ are supplementary:

$$\angle AER + \angle RCA = 180^\circ, \qquad \angle EAC + \angle ERC = 180^\cir
c.$$

Now $\angle EAC = \angle BAC = A$ (since $E$ lies on $AB$) and $\angle RCA = \an
gle BCA = C$ (since $R$ lies on $BC$). Hence

$$\angle AER = 180^\circ - C, \qquad \angle ERC = 180^\circ - A.$$

Since $A, E, B$ are collinear (with $E$ between $A$ and $B$) and $B, R, C$ are c
ollinear (with $R$ between $B$ and $C$):

$$\angle BER = 180^\circ - \angle AER = C, \qquad \angle BRE = 180^\circ - \angl
e ERC = A.$$

The third angle of $\triangle BER$ is $\angle EBR = \angle ABC = B$. Thus $\tria
ngle RBE$ has angles $A, B, C$ at vertices $R, B, E$ respectively, giving the si
milarity

$$\triangle RBE \sim \triangle ABC \quad (R \leftrightarrow A,\; B \leftrightarr
ow B,\; E \leftrightarrow C).$$

Let $k$ be the ratio of similarity. Then

$$RE = k \cdot AC = ka, \qquad BE = k \cdot BC, \qquad BR = k \cdot AB. \tag{1}$
$

**Step 2: Barycentric parametrization and the centroid condition.**

Write $E = (1-t)A + tB$ on $AB$ and $R = (1-s)B + sC$ on $BC$ in barycentric coo
rdinates, so

$$E = (\alpha,\, 1-\alpha,\, 0), \quad R = (0,\, 1-\delta,\, \delta), \quad \tex
t{where } \alpha = 1-t,\; \delta = s.$$

Then $BE = \alpha \cdot AB$ and $BR = \delta \cdot BC$. From (1):

$$\alpha \cdot AB = k \cdot BC, \qquad \delta \cdot BC = k \cdot AB. \tag{2}$$

Multiplying these: $\alpha\delta \cdot AB \cdot BC = k^2 \cdot AB \cdot BC$, so
$k^2 = \alpha\delta$, i.e., $k = \sqrt{\alpha\delta}$.

Since $G = \frac{1}{3}(A+B+C)$ lies on segment $ER$, write $G = uE + vR$ with $u
+v=1$. In barycentric coordinates:

$$\left(\tfrac{1}{3},\tfrac{1}{3},\tfrac{1}{3}\right) = u(\alpha,1{-}\alpha,0) +
 v(0,1{-}\delta,\delta).$$

The first and third coordinates give $u\alpha = \frac{1}{3}$ and $v\delta = \fra
c{1}{3}$, so $u = \frac{1}{3\alpha}$, $v = \frac{1}{3\delta}$. The condition $u+
v=1$ yields

$$\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\delta} = 3. \tag{3}$$

Since $G = uE + vR$ divides $ER$ with $EG = v \cdot RE$ and $GR = u \cdot RE$:

$$\frac{EG}{GR} = \frac{v}{u} = \frac{\alpha}{\delta}. \tag{4}$$

**Step 3: Combining everything.**

From (1), $RE = ka$, and $RE = EG + GR = b + GR$. From (4), $GR = b\cdot\frac{\d
elta}{\alpha}$, so

$$RE = b\left(1 + \frac{\delta}{\alpha}\right) = b\cdot\frac{\alpha+\delta}{\alp
ha}.$$

Also $k = \sqrt{\alpha\delta}$, so $RE = a\sqrt{\alpha\delta}$. Equating:

$$b\cdot\frac{\alpha+\delta}{\alpha} = a\sqrt{\alpha\delta}. \tag{5}$$

From (3): $\frac{\alpha+\delta}{\alpha\delta} = 3$, so $\alpha + \delta = 3\alph
a\delta$. Substituting into (5):

$$b\cdot\frac{3\alpha\delta}{\alpha} = a\sqrt{\alpha\delta} \implies 3b\,\delta
= a\sqrt{\alpha\delta} \implies 9b^2\delta^2 = a^2\alpha\delta \implies \frac{\d
elta}{\alpha} = \frac{a^2}{9b^2}.$$

Therefore:

$$GR = b\cdot\frac{\delta}{\alpha} = b \cdot \frac{a^2}{9b^2} = \frac{a^2}{9b}.$
$

**Verification (numerical).** Take $A=(0,0)$, $B=(4,0)$, $C=(1,3)$, so $a = AC =
 \sqrt{10}$ and $G=(5/3,1)$. Solving the system yields $E=(7/6,0)$, $R=(19/9,17/
9)$, giving $EG = \sqrt{5}/2$ and $GR = 4\sqrt{5}/9$. Check: $\frac{a^2}{9b} = \
frac{10}{9\cdot\sqrt{5}/2} = \frac{20}{9\sqrt{5}} = \frac{4\sqrt{5}}{9}$. ✓ The
concyclicity of $A,E,R,C$ is also confirmed by direct substitution into the circ
le equation.

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{GR = \dfrac{a^2}{9b}}$$
