**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

Consider the function

$$f(z) = z + \frac{\sin z}{z - 1}.$$

**Meromorphicity and pole count.** The function $\sin z$ is entire, and $z - 1$
is entire with a simple zero at $z = 1$. Since $\sin(1) \neq 0$, the quotient $\
frac{\sin z}{z-1}$ has a genuine simple pole at $z = 1$ and no other singulariti
es in $\mathbb{C}$. Adding the entire function $z$ preserves meromorphicity, so
$f$ is meromorphic on the entire complex plane with **exactly one pole** (at $z
= 1$).

**Fixed points.** The fixed points of $f$ are the solutions of

$$f(z) = z \iff \frac{\sin z}{z - 1} = 0 \iff \sin z = 0 \text{ and } z \neq 1.$
$

The zeros of $\sin z$ are $z = n\pi$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. Since $\pi$ is irra
tional, $n\pi \neq 1$ for every integer $n$. Therefore the fixed-point set is

$$\{\, n\pi : n \in \mathbb{Z} \,\},$$

which is **infinite** (in fact, countably infinite and unbounded in both directi
ons).

**Conclusion.** The function $f(z) = z + \frac{\sin z}{z-1}$ is meromorphic on $
\mathbb{C}$ with at most one pole and possesses infinitely many fixed points. He
nce such a function can indeed have infinitely many fixed points.

*(Remark: even in the zero-pole case, the entire function $f(z) = z + \sin z$ ha
