## Proof

**Claim:** There do not exist infinitely many positive integers $n$ such that $p
(2^n - 1) < p(2^n) < p(2^n + 1)$. In fact, there are **no** such positive intege
rs at all.

**Key observation.** For every positive integer $n$, we have $2^n = 2 \cdot 2^{n
-1}$, so the only prime factor of $2^n$ is $2$. Hence:
$$p(2^n) = 2 \quad \text{for all } n \geq 1.$$

**Analysis of the left inequality.** The condition requires $p(2^n - 1) < p(2^n)
 = 2$. Since $p(m)$ is the greatest prime dividing $m$, and the smallest prime i
s $2$, the inequality $p(2^n - 1) < 2$ demands that $p(2^n - 1) \leq 1$, which m
eans $2^n - 1$ has no prime factors at all—i.e., $2^n - 1 = 1$.

This forces $n = 1$.

**Checking $n = 1$.** We have $2^1 - 1 = 1$, $2^1 = 2$, $2^1 + 1 = 3$. The value
 $p(1)$ is undefined (1 has no prime divisors), so the expression $p(2^1 - 1) <
p(2^1) < p(2^1 + 1)$ is not even well-defined. Even under the non-standard conve
ntion $p(1) = 1$, this yields only a single value $n = 1$, not infinitely many.

**For all $n \geq 2$, the left inequality fails.** When $n \geq 2$, we have $2^n
 - 1 \geq 3$. Since $2^n - 1$ is odd, every prime factor of $2^n - 1$ is odd, he
nce $p(2^n - 1) \geq 3$. Therefore:
$$p(2^n - 1) \geq 3 > 2 = p(2^n),$$
which contradicts $p(2^n - 1) < p(2^n)$.

**Conclusion.** The left inequality $p(2^n - 1) < p(2^n)$ is impossible for ever
y $n \geq 2$ (since $p(2^n - 1) \geq 3 > 2 = p(2^n)$), and the case $n = 1$ is d
egenerate/undefined. Therefore there are **no** positive integers $n$ satisfying
 $p(2^n - 1) < p(2^n) < p(2^n + 1)$, and consequently there do not exist infinit
ely many such $n$.

$\blacksquare$
