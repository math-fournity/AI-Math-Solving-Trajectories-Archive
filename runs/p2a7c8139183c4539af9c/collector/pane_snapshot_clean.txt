## Solution

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains no leaked solutions, answe
r values, or proof sketches. Proceeding.

We determine all $P \in \mathbb{R}[x]$ satisfying
$$P(x)\,P(y)=P\!\left(\tfrac{x+y}{2}\right)^{2}-P\!\left(\tfrac{x-y}{2}\right)^{
2}\qquad\forall\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}.\tag{$\ast$}$$

**Step 1. Reduction to a cleaner form.** Set $u=\frac{x+y}{2},\;v=\frac{x-y}{2}$
, so $x=u+v,\;y=u-v$. Then ($\ast$) becomes
$$P(u+v)\,P(u-v)=P(u)^{2}-P(v)^{2}\qquad\forall\,u,v\in\mathbb{R}.\tag{1}$$

**Step 2. $P(0)=0$ (unless $P\equiv0$).** Put $y=0$ in ($\ast$): $P(x)P(0)=P(x/2
)^{2}-P(x/2)^{2}=0$ for all $x$. Hence either $P\equiv0$ or $P(0)=0$. The zero p
olynomial clearly works; from now on assume $P\not\equiv0$, so $P(0)=0$.

**Step 3. $P$ is odd.** Put $u=0$ in (1): $P(v)P(-v)=P(0)^{2}-P(v)^{2}=-P(v)^{2}
$, i.e.
$$P(v)\bigl(P(v)+P(-v)\bigr)=0\quad\forall v.$$
As a polynomial identity this forces $P(v)+P(-v)\equiv0$; hence $P$ is odd. Cons
equently $P$ contains only odd powers:
$$P(x)=a_{1}x+a_{3}x^{3}+\cdots+a_{2m+1}x^{2m+1},\qquad a_{2m+1}\neq0,\;\deg P=2
m+1.$$

**Step 4. Degree comparison forces $\deg P=1$.** Write $P(x)=a\,x^{2m+1}+(\text{
terms of degree}\le 2m-1)$ with $a\neq0$.

- **LHS of (1), top-degree part.** The degree-$2(2m+1)=4m+2$ homogeneous part of
 $P(u+v)P(u-v)$ comes only from the leading term:
$$a^{2}(u+v)^{2m+1}(u-v)^{2m+1}=a^{2}(u^{2}-v^{2})^{2m+1}.$$
Lower terms of $P$ contribute total degree $\le(2m+1)+(2m-1)=4m<4m+2$.

- **RHS of (1), top-degree part.** $P(u)^{2}-P(v)^{2}=a^{2}u^{4m+2}-a^{2}v^{4m+2
}+(\text{degree}\le 4m)$.

Equating the degree-$4m+2$ parts gives the polynomial identity
$$(u^{2}-v^{2})^{2m+1}=u^{4m+2}-v^{4m+2}.\tag{2}$$

Expand the left side:
$$(u^{2}-v^{2})^{2m+1}=\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\binom{2m+1}{j}(-1)^{j}\,u^{4m+2-2j}\,v^
{2j}.$$
The $j=0$ and $j=2m+1$ terms are $u^{4m+2}$ and $-v^{4m+2}$, matching the right
side. For (2) to hold, **every middle term** ($1\le j\le 2m$) must vanish. But o
ver $\mathbb{R}$,
$$\binom{2m+1}{j}\neq0\quad\text{for }1\le j\le 2m,$$
and the monomials $u^{4m+2-2j}v^{2j}$ are linearly independent. Hence (2) is imp
ossible whenever $m\ge1$.

Therefore $m=0$, i.e. $\deg P=1$. Combined with $P$ odd and $P(0)=0$:
$$P(x)=ax\qquad\text{for some }a\in\mathbb{R}.$$

**Step 5. Verification.** For $P(x)=ax$:
- LHS: $P(x)P(y)=a^{2}xy$.
- RHS: $a^{2}\!\left(\tfrac{x+y}{2}\right)^{2}-a^{2}\!\left(\tfrac{x-y}{2}\right
)^{2}=\dfrac{a^{2}}{4}\bigl[(x+y)^{2}-(x-y)^{2}\bigr]=\dfrac{a^{2}}{4}\cdot4xy=a
^{2}xy$.

LHS $=$ RHS for all $x,y$. (The case $a=0$ recovers the trivial solution $P\equi
v0$.)

**Conclusion.** The polynomials satisfying the equation are exactly
$$\boxed{\,P(x)=ax,\quad a\in\mathbb{R}\,}.$$
