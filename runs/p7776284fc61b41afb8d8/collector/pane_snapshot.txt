The answer is **NO**. Not all entries of $A^{-1}$ need be (strictly) positive.

## Counterexample

Take $A = I_n$ (the $n \times n$ identity matrix) for $n \ge 2$.

- $A$ is symmetric.
- $A$ is positive definite (all eigenvalues equal $1 > 0$).
- Every off-diagonal entry is $0 \le 0$.
- $A^{-1} = I_n$, whose off-diagonal entries are $0$, **not** positive.

So the statement "all entries of $A^{-1}$ are positive" fails.

## The correct (weaker) statement

What *is* true is that every entry of $A^{-1}$ is **non-negative**. A symmetric
positive definite matrix with non-positive off-diagonal entries is a **Stieltjes
 matrix** (a symmetric nonsingular M-matrix), and such matrices have non-negativ
e inverses. Here is the proof:

Write $A = D - C$ where $D = \operatorname{diag}(A)$ and $C = D - A$. Then $C$ h
as non-negative entries: its off-diagonal entries are $-a_{ij} \ge 0$ (since $a_
{ij} \le 0$ for $i \ne j$) and its diagonal entries are $0$.

Since $A$ is positive definite, $D$ is invertible with $D > 0$, and
$$A = D\bigl(I - D^{-1}C\bigr).$$

**Claim:** $\rho(D^{-1}C) < 1$, where $\rho$ denotes the spectral radius.

Indeed, $A$ positive definite means $x^\top A x > 0$ for all $x \ne 0$, i.e. $x^
\top D x > x^\top C x$ for all $x \ne 0$. Let $y = D^{1/2}x$; then $y^\top y > y
^\top D^{-1/2} C D^{-1/2}\, y$ for all $y \ne 0$. The matrix $M := D^{-1/2} C D^
{-1/2}$ is entrywise non-negative and satisfies $y^\top y > y^\top M y$ for all
$y \ne 0$, i.e. $I - M$ is positive definite. In particular $I - M$ is invertibl
e, so $1$ is not an eigenvalue of $M$. Since $M \ge 0$ entrywise, by the Perron–
Frobenius theorem $\rho(M)$ is an eigenvalue of $M$; combined with $I - M$ posit
ive definite (which forces every eigenvalue of $M$ to be $< 1$), we get $\rho(M)
 < 1$. Since $D^{-1}C$ is similar to $M$ (via $D^{1/2}$), $\rho(D^{-1}C) = \rho(
M) < 1$.

Therefore the Neumann series converges:
$$\bigl(I - D^{-1}C\bigr)^{-1} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} (D^{-1}C)^k.$$

Every term $(D^{-1}C)^k$ is entrywise non-negative (product of non-negative matr
ices), so the sum is entrywise non-negative. Hence
$$A^{-1} = \bigl(I - D^{-1}C\bigr)^{-1} D^{-1} \ge 0 \quad \text{entrywise},$$
since $D^{-1} > 0$ is diagonal with positive entries.

## When strict positivity holds

$A^{-1}$ is entrywise **strictly** positive if and only if $A$ is **irreducible*
* (equivalently, the directed graph of $C$ is strongly connected). The identity
matrix is maximally reducible (fully block-decomposed into $1 \times 1$ blocks),
 which is exactly why its inverse has zeros.

## Conclusion

The inverse of a Stieltjes matrix is always **non-negative**, but need not be **
positive**. The identity matrix provides an explicit counterexample, so the answ
er to the question is **No**.
