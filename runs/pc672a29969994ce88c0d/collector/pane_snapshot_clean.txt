Looking at this problem, I need to find the necessary and sufficient condition o
n the space $E$ (equipped with nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form $b$ over al
gebraically closed $K$) such that every $b$-self-adjoint endomorphism is diagona
lizable.

**Answer:** The condition is that $b$ is **anisotropic**, i.e., $b(x,x) \neq 0$
for every nonzero $x \in E$. Over an algebraically closed field, this is equival
ent to $n \leq 1$.

---

## Proof

### Necessity: If $b$ is isotropic, there exists a non-diagonalizable self-adjoi
nt endomorphism

Suppose $b$ is isotropic: there exists $v \neq 0$ with $b(v,v) = 0$. Since $b$ i
s nondegenerate, there exists $w \in E$ with $b(w, v) \neq 0$.

Define $u: E \to E$ by $u(x) = b(x, v)\, v$.

**$u$ is self-adjoint:** For all $x, y \in E$,
$$b(u(x), y) = b(b(x,v)\,v,\; y) = b(x,v)\,b(v,y) = b(x,v)\,b(y,v) = b(x,\; b(y,
v)\,v) = b(x, u(y)),$$
using the symmetry of $b$.

**$u$ is nilpotent with $u^2 = 0$:**
$$u^2(x) = u(b(x,v)\,v) = b(b(x,v)\,v,\; v)\,v = b(x,v)\,b(v,v)\,v = 0,$$
since $b(v,v) = 0$.

**$u \neq 0$:** We have $u(w) = b(w,v)\,v \neq 0$.

Thus $u$ is a nonzero nilpotent self-adjoint endomorphism. Since $u^2 = 0$ but $
u \neq 0$, its only eigenvalue is $0$ (algebraic multiplicity $n$) but $\ker(u)
\neq E$, so $u$ is **not diagonalizable**. Hence anisotropy is necessary.

### Sufficiency: If $b$ is anisotropic, every self-adjoint endomorphism is diago
nalizable

We proceed by induction on $n = \dim E$.

**Base case** $n \leq 1$: Every endomorphism is diagonalizable (trivially).

**Inductive step** ($n \geq 2$): Let $u$ be self-adjoint. Since $K$ is algebraic
ally closed, $u$ has an eigenvalue $\lambda$. Let $V_\lambda = \ker(u - \lambda
I) \neq \{0\}$ be the eigenspace.

**Key observation:** For every $x \in V_\lambda \setminus \{0\}$, anisotropy giv
es $b(x,x) \neq 0$. Therefore $b|_{V_\lambda}$ is **nondegenerate** (any $x \in
V_\lambda$ with $b(x, V_\lambda) = 0$ would in particular have $b(x,x)=0$, forci
ng $x=0$).

Since $b|_{V_\lambda}$ is nondegenerate, we have the orthogonal decomposition:
$$E = V_\lambda \oplus V_\lambda^\perp.$$

**$V_\lambda^\perp$ is $u$-invariant:** For $y \in V_\lambda^\perp$ and $x \in V
_\lambda$:
$$b(x,\; u(y)) = b(u(x),\; y) = b(\lambda x,\; y) = \lambda\, b(x, y) = 0.$$
So $u(y) \in V_\lambda^\perp$.

**Restriction to $V_\lambda^\perp$:** The form $b|_{V_\lambda^\perp}$ is nondege
nerate (since $b|_{V_\lambda}$ is) and remains anisotropic. The restriction $u|_
{V_\lambda^\perp}$ is self-adjoint with respect to $b|_{V_\lambda^\perp}$. By th
e induction hypothesis, $u|_{V_\lambda^\perp}$ is diagonalizable.

**Conclusion:** On $V_\lambda$, $u = \lambda \cdot \mathrm{id}$ (diagonalizable)
. On $V_\lambda^\perp$, $u$ is diagonalizable by induction. Since $E = V_\lambda
 \oplus V_\lambda^\perp$, $u$ is diagonalizable on $E$. $\square$

### Over an algebraically closed field, anisotropy $\iff$ $n \leq 1$

For $n \geq 2$, every nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form over an algebraicall
y closed field is **isotropic**:

- **Characteristic $\neq 2$:** $b$ is equivalent to the identity form. The vecto
r $v = e_1 + \alpha\, e_2$, where $\alpha^2 = -1$ (which exists in $K$), satisfi
es $b(v,v) = 1 + \alpha^2 = 0$.

- **Characteristic $2$, non-alternating form:** $b$ is equivalent to the identit
y. The vector $v = e_1 + e_2$ satisfies $b(v,v) = 1 + 1 = 0$ (since $\mathrm{cha
r}\, K = 2$).

- **Characteristic $2$, alternating form:** Every vector is isotropic by definit
ion ($b(x,x) = 0$ for all $x$).

Therefore, over an algebraically closed field, the condition that $b$ be anisotr
opic is equivalent to $n \leq 1$.

---

**Summary:** The necessary and sufficient condition is that **$b$ be anisotropic
** ($b(x,x) \neq 0$ for all $x \neq 0$). Over an algebraically closed field, thi
s forces $n \leq 1$; conversely, for $n \leq 1$ every endomorphism is trivially
diagonalizable.
