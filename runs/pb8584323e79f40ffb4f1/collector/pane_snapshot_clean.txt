The expression inside the parentheses is:
$$\cos x \cot x - \log(\sin x)^{\sin x} = \frac{\cos^2 x}{\sin x} - \sin x \log(

**Step 2: Recognize the integrand as a total derivative.**

Let $f(x) = (\sin x)^{\cos x}$. Taking the logarithmic derivative:
$$\log f(x) = \cos x \cdot \log(\sin x)$$
$$\frac{f'(x)}{f(x)} = -\sin x \cdot \log(\sin x) + \cos x \cdot \frac{\cos x}{\
sin x} = \frac{\cos^2 x}{\sin x} - \sin x \log(\sin x)$$

Therefore:
$$f'(x) = (\sin x)^{\cos x}\left(\frac{\cos^2 x}{\sin x} - \sin x \log(\sin x)\r

This is precisely the integrand.

**Step 3: Evaluate via the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.**

$$\int_0^{\pi/2} f'(x)\,dx = f\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) - f(0)$$

- At $x = \pi/2$: $f(\pi/2) = (\sin\frac{\pi}{2})^{\cos(\pi/2)} = 1^0 = 1$.

**Result:**

$$\int_0^{\pi/2} (\sin x)^{\cos x} \left( \cos x \cot x - \log (\sin x)^{\sin x}
