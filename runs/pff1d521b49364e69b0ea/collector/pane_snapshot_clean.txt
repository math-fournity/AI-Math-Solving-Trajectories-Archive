## Proof

We are given that $G$ is **not finitely generated**, and we must find the minimu
m $n$ such that the subgroup $\langle a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_n \rangle$ is a **prop
er** subgroup of $G$.

**Key fact.** A group $G$ is finitely generated if and only if there exists a fi
nite set $\{a_1, \ldots, a_n\} \subseteq G$ with $\langle a_1, \ldots, a_n \rang
le = G$. Since $G$ is **not** finitely generated, **no** finite subset of $G$ ca
n generate all of $G$. Equivalently, for every finite set $\{a_1, \ldots, a_n\}
\subseteq G$ (with $n \geq 1$), the subgroup $\langle a_1, \ldots, a_n \rangle$
is a **proper** subgroup of $G$.

**Checking $n = 1$.** For any single element $a_1 \in G$, the subgroup $\langle
a_1 \rangle$ is cyclic, hence finitely generated (by one element). If $\langle a
_1 \rangle = G$, then $G$ would be cyclic and therefore finitely generated, cont
radicting the hypothesis. Hence $\langle a_1 \rangle \subsetneq G$ for every $a_
1 \in G$.

**Minimality.** The indexing $\{a_1, \ldots, a_n\}$ begins at $a_1$, so $n \geq
1$. Since $n = 1$ already yields a proper subgroup, and no smaller positive valu
e of $n$ is admissible, the minimum is $n = 1$.

**The role of $S$.** The set $S$ (the smallest generating set, i.e., a minimal g
enerating set) must be infinite, because $G$ is not finitely generated. Any fini
te selection $\{a_1, \ldots, a_n\}$ from $S$ (or from $G$) generates only a prop
er subgroup, consistent with the conclusion above.

$$\boxed{n = 1}$$
