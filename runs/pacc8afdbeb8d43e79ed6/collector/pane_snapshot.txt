## Proof

Let $A = \prod_{k=1}^{45}\sin(2k-1)^\circ$, so the desired product is $P = 1/A^2
$.

Using the identity $\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi k}{n}\right)=\frac{n}
{2^{n-1}}$:

- With $n=180$: $\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{179}\sin(k^\circ)=\frac{180}{2^{179}}
.$
- With $n=90$: $\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{89}\sin(2j^\circ)=\frac{90}{2^{89}}.$

Split the full product $\prod_{k=1}^{179}\sin(k^\circ)$ into even and odd indice
s:

$$\prod_{k=1}^{179}\sin(k^\circ)=\underbrace{\prod_{j=1}^{89}\sin(2j^\circ)}_{\t
ext{even}}\cdot\underbrace{\prod_{\substack{k=1\\k\text{ odd}}}^{179}\sin(k^\cir
c)}_{\text{odd}}.$$

The odd-indexed product runs over $1^\circ,3^\circ,\dots,179^\circ$ (90 terms).
By symmetry $\sin(180^\circ-x)=\sin x$, pairing $(1^\circ,179^\circ),(3^\circ,17
7^\circ),\dots,(89^\circ,91^\circ)$ gives

$$\prod_{\substack{k=1\\k\text{ odd}}}^{179}\sin(k^\circ)=\left(\prod_{k=1}^{45}
\sin(2k-1)^\circ\right)^2=A^2.$$

Therefore
$$\frac{180}{2^{179}}=\frac{90}{2^{89}}\cdot A^2 \implies A^2=\frac{180}{2^{179}
}\cdot\frac{2^{89}}{90}=2\cdot 2^{-90}=2^{-89}.$$

Hence
$$P=\frac{1}{A^2}=2^{89}.$$

So $m=2$, $n=89$, and $m+n=\boxed{91}$.
