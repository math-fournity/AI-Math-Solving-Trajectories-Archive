# Problem

A specialized logistics firm is designing a delivery route that connects a central warehouse to two rural distribution hubs, Hub A and Hub B. The distance from the warehouse to Hub A is $a$ units, and the distance to Hub B is $b$ units (where $a$ and $b$ are positive values). The warehouse and the two hubs are positioned such that the paths from the warehouse to each hub are perfectly perpendicular to one another.

Due to fuel subsidies and regional regulations, the distances must satisfy a specific operational constraint: the distance to Hub A plus $k$ times the distance to Hub B must equal the product of the two distances (where $k$ is a fixed constant greater than 1). 

The firm is planning a "triangular circuit" that starts at Hub A, travels in a straight line to the warehouse, then moves in a straight line to Hub B, and finally returns directly from Hub B to Hub A in a straight line.

Determine the minimum possible total length of this triangular circuit, expressed in terms of the constant $k$.
