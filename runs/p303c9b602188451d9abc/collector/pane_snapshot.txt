# Every Infinite Set Has a Derangement

**Answer: Yes.** In ZFC, every infinite set admits a derangement (a fixed-point-
free bijection from the set to itself).

## Proof

Let $S$ be an infinite set.

**Step 1 (Well-ordering).** By the Axiom of Choice (equivalently, the Well-Order
ing Theorem), $S$ can be well-ordered. Hence there exists a bijection between $S
$ and an infinite cardinal $\kappa$ (viewed as its initial ordinal). It suffices
 to construct a derangement of $\kappa$, since composing with the bijection $S \
cong \kappa$ transports it to $S$.

**Step 2 (Cardinal arithmetic).** Since $\kappa$ is an infinite cardinal, cardin
al arithmetic in ZFC gives
$$\kappa \cdot 2 = \max(\kappa, 2) = \kappa.$$
Therefore there exists a bijection
$$\phi : \kappa \times \{0, 1\} \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; \kappa.$$

**Step 3 (Construction of the derangement).** Define $f : \kappa \to \kappa$ by
$$f\bigl(\phi(\alpha, i)\bigr) = \phi(\alpha,\, 1 - i), \qquad \alpha < \kappa,\
; i \in \{0,1\}.$$

Since $\phi$ is a bijection, every element of $\kappa$ is *uniquely* of the form
 $\phi(\alpha, i)$, so $f$ is well-defined on all of $\kappa$.

**Step 4 (Bijectivity).** The map $f$ is its own inverse:
$$f\bigl(f(\phi(\alpha, i))\bigr) = f\bigl(\phi(\alpha, 1-i)\bigr) = \phi(\alpha
, i).$$
Hence $f \circ f = \mathrm{id}_\kappa$, so $f$ is a bijection.

**Step 5 (No fixed points).** Suppose for contradiction that $f(\phi(\alpha, i))
 = \phi(\alpha, i)$ for some $\alpha, i$. Then
$$\phi(\alpha, 1 - i) = \phi(\alpha, i).$$
But $(\alpha, 1-i) \neq (\alpha, i)$ (since $1 - i \neq i$), which contradicts t
he injectivity of $\phi$. Therefore $f$ has no fixed points.

**Conclusion.** $f$ is a fixed-point-free bijection $\kappa \to \kappa$, i.e., a
 derangement of $\kappa$. Transporting $f$ through the bijection $S \cong \kappa
$ yields a derangement of $S$. $\blacksquare$
