## Proof

**Step 1: Closed form of the sum.**

Define $S_n(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}x^{2k-1} = x - x^3 + x^5 - \cdots + (-1
)^{n-1}x^{2n-1}$.

Factoring out $x$ and recognizing a finite geometric series with ratio $-x^2$:

$$S_n(x) = x\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-x^2)^k = x \cdot \frac{1-(-x^2)^n}{1-(-x^2)} = \fr
ac{x\bigl(1-(-1)^n x^{2n}\bigr)}{1+x^2}.$$

**Step 2: $S_n$ is odd.**

Since every term $(-1)^{k-1}x^{2k-1}$ is an odd power of $x$, the sum $S_n$ is a
n odd function: $S_n(-x) = -S_n(x)$.

**Step 3: $\arccos$ is well-defined on $[-1,1]$.**

For $|x| \le 1$, we have $|x|^{2n} \le 1$, so:

$$|S_n(x)| = \frac{|x|\,\bigl|1-(-1)^n x^{2n}\bigr|}{1+x^2} \le \frac{|x|\,(1+|x
|^{2n})}{1+x^2} \le \frac{2|x|}{1+x^2} \le 1,$$

where the last inequality is $(|x|-1)^2 \ge 0$, i.e., $2|x| \le 1+x^2$. Equality
 holds only at $|x|=1$. Hence $S_n(x)\in[-1,1]$ for all $x\in[-1,1]$, so $\arcco
s(S_n(x))$ is defined throughout.

**Step 4: Symmetry gives the value.**

Using the identity $\arccos(-t) = \pi - \arccos(t)$ for $t\in[-1,1]$, and the od
dness of $S_n$:

$$\arccos\bigl(S_n(-x)\bigr) = \arccos\bigl(-S_n(x)\bigr) = \pi - \arccos\bigl(S
_n(x)\bigr).$$

Now split the integral and substitute $x\mapsto -x$ in the part over $[-1,0]$:

$$I_n = \int_{-1}^{0}\arccos(S_n(x))\,dx + \int_{0}^{1}\arccos(S_n(x))\,dx.$$

For the first piece, let $u=-x$:

$$\int_{-1}^{0}\arccos(S_n(x))\,dx = \int_{0}^{1}\arccos(S_n(-u))\,du = \int_{0}
^{1}\bigl[\pi - \arccos(S_n(u))\bigr]\,du.$$

Adding the two pieces:

$$I_n = \int_{0}^{1}\bigl[\pi - \arccos(S_n(u))\bigr]\,du + \int_{0}^{1}\arccos(
S_n(u))\,du = \int_{0}^{1}\pi\,du = \pi.$$

**Step 5: Verification ($n=1$).**

For $n=1$, $S_1(x)=x$, and $\displaystyle I_1 = \int_{-1}^{1}\arccos(x)\,dx = \B
igl[x\arccos(x)-\sqrt{1-x^2}\Bigr]_{-1}^{1} = (0-0)-(-\pi-0) = \pi.$ ✓

## Result

$$\boxed{I_n = \pi \quad \text{for all positive integers } n.}$$

The key observation is that the summand inside $\arccos$ is always an **odd func
tion** of $x$ (being a sum of odd powers), and the identity $\arccos(-t)=\pi-\ar
ccos(t)$ forces the integral over $[-1,1]$ to equal $\pi$ regardless of the spec
ific odd function, provided it stays in $[-1,1]$.
