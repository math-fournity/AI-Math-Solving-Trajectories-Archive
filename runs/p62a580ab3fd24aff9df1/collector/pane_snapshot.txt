## Proof

We prove that for every $x \in [0,1]$ and every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists
$n \in \mathbb{N}$ with $|\{n\sqrt{2}\} - x| < \varepsilon$.

**Step 1 (Dirichlet's pigeonhole principle).** Fix an integer $N \geq 1$. Partit
ion $[0,1)$ into $N$ half-open intervals
$$I_j = \left[\tfrac{j}{N},\, \tfrac{j+1}{N}\right), \quad j = 0, 1, \ldots, N-1
.$$
Consider the $N+1$ numbers $\{0\cdot\sqrt{2}\}, \{1\cdot\sqrt{2}\}, \{2\cdot\sqr
t{2}\}, \ldots, \{N\cdot\sqrt{2}\}$ in $[0,1)$. By the pigeonhole principle, two
 of them fall in the same interval $I_j$. That is, there exist integers $0 \leq
i < k' \leq N$ with
$$\bigl|\{k'\sqrt{2}\} - \{i\sqrt{2}\}\bigr| < \tfrac{1}{N}.$$

Set $k = k' - i$ (so $1 \leq k \leq N$) and $m = \lfloor k'\sqrt{2}\rfloor - \lf
loor i\sqrt{2}\rfloor \in \mathbb{Z}$. Then
$$\{k'\sqrt{2}\} - \{i\sqrt{2}\} = (k'-i)\sqrt{2} - m = k\sqrt{2} - m.$$
Hence $k\sqrt{2} - m$ has absolute value less than $1/N$, which means $\{k\sqrt{
2}\}$ lies within $1/N$ of either $0$ or $1$. Since $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational, $\
{k\sqrt{2}\} \neq 0$, so defining
$$\alpha_N := \min\bigl(\{k\sqrt{2}\},\; 1 - \{k\sqrt{2}\}\bigr),$$
we have $0 < \alpha_N < 1/N$.

**Step 2 (filling the circle with small steps).** The sequence $\{mk\sqrt{2}\} \
bmod 1$ for $m = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$ moves around the unit circle in steps of size
$\{k\sqrt{2}\}$ (mod 1). In terms of circular distance, each step has size $\alp
ha_N$. Concretely, the points
$$0,\; \{k\sqrt{2}\},\; \{2k\sqrt{2}\},\; \{3k\sqrt{2}\},\; \ldots$$
are successive translates by $\{k\sqrt{2}\}$ modulo 1, and consecutive points on
 the circle are separated by circular distance exactly $\alpha_N < 1/N$.

Since $\alpha_N$ is irrational (it is derived from the irrational $k\sqrt{2}$),
all these points are distinct. After $M = \lceil 1/\alpha_N \rceil$ steps, the p
oints have wrapped around the circle at least once, and the maximal gap between
consecutive points (in circular order) is at most $\alpha_N < 1/N$. Therefore ev
ery point of $[0,1)$ is within distance $\alpha_N < 1/N$ of some $\{mk\sqrt{2}\}
$ with $0 \leq m \leq M$.

**Step 3 (conclusion).** Let $x \in [0,1]$ and $\varepsilon > 0$ be given. Choos
e $N > 1/\varepsilon$. By Steps 1–2, there exist integers $k \geq 1$ and $m \geq
 0$ such that
$$\bigl|\{mk\sqrt{2}\} - x\bigr| < \alpha_N < \frac{1}{N} < \varepsilon.$$
Setting $n = mk \in \mathbb{N}$ (if $m = 0$ use $n = k$ instead, which gives a p
oint within $1/N$ of $0$, covering $x$ near $0$; or simply note $m \geq 1$ suffi
ces for $x$ bounded away from $0$, and the case $x$ near $0$ is handled by takin
g $n = k$ directly), we obtain $n \in \mathbb{N}$ with
$$\bigl|\{n\sqrt{2}\} - x\bigr| < \varepsilon.$$

Since $x \in [0,1]$ and $\varepsilon > 0$ were arbitrary, the set $\bigl\{\{n\sq
rt{2}\} : n \in \mathbb{N}\bigr\}$ is dense in $[0,1]$. $\blacksquare$
