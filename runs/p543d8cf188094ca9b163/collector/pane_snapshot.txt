**Theorem (Remmert's Proper Mapping Theorem).** Let $F: X \to Y$ be a proper hol
omorphic map between complex spaces. Then the image $F(X)$ is an analytic subset
 of $Y$.

---

## Proof

We prove that $F(X)$ is a closed analytic subset of $Y$.

### Step 1. $F(X)$ is closed in $Y$.

Since $F$ is proper, it is in particular a closed map (a topological fact: a con
tinuous proper map between locally compact Hausdorff spaces is closed). Indeed,
let $C \subseteq X$ be closed. To show $F(C)$ is closed, take a net $\{y_\alpha\
}$ in $F(C)$ converging to $y \in Y$. Write $y_\alpha = F(x_\alpha)$ with $x_\al
pha \in C$. The set $K = \{y\} \cup \{y_\alpha\}$ is compact in $Y$, so $F^{-1}(
K)$ is compact in $X$ (by properness). The net $\{x_\alpha\}$ lies in the compac
t set $F^{-1}(K) \cap C$, hence has a convergent subnet $x_{\alpha_\beta} \to x
\in C$. By continuity, $F(x) = y$, so $y \in F(C)$. Thus $F$ is closed, and $F(X
)$ is closed in $Y$.

### Step 2. Reduction to the local statement.

It remains to show that $F(X)$ is locally analytic. Being closed, $F(X)$ is a cl
osed subset of $Y$, and we need to show that every point $y_0 \in Y$ has an open
 neighborhood $V$ such that $F(X) \cap V$ is defined by the vanishing of a coher
ent sheaf of ideals (i.e., is an analytic subset of $V$).

If $y_0 \notin F(X)$, then since $F(X)$ is closed, there is an open neighborhood
 $V$ of $y_0$ with $F(X) \cap V = \emptyset$, which is trivially analytic.

So we may assume $y_0 \in F(X)$. The fiber $F^{-1}(y_0)$ is compact (it is a clo
sed subset of the compact set $F^{-1}(\{y_0\})$, which is compact by properness)
.

### Step 3. Local setup and the key sheaf-theoretic construction.

Choose an open Stein neighborhood $V$ of $y_0$ in $Y$ that is relatively compact
. Set $U = F^{-1}(V)$. Since $F$ is proper and $\overline{V}$ is compact, $\over
line{U} = F^{-1}(\overline{V})$ is compact, so $F|_U : U \to V$ is a proper holo
morphic map with $U$ relatively compact.

We work with the sheaves of holomorphic functions. Let $\mathcal{O}_X$ and $\mat
hcal{O}_Y$ denote the structure sheaves. The map $F$ gives a morphism of sheaves
 of $\mathbb{C}$-algebras $F^\#: \mathcal{O}_Y \to F_*\mathcal{O}_X$.

**Key construction.** Consider the direct image sheaf $F_*\mathcal{O}_X$ on $Y$.
 We claim:

> **Claim (Grauert's coherence theorem / finite generation).** If $F: X \to Y$ i
s a proper holomorphic map, then $F_*\mathcal{O}_X$ is a coherent $\mathcal{O}_Y
$-module.

This is a deep theorem. Let us recall its proof in outline, since it is the engi
ne of the entire argument.

### Step 4. Proof of coherence of $F_*\mathcal{O}_X$ (Grauert–Remmert).

The proof proceeds by induction on the fiber dimension and uses the following in
gredients:

**(a) Reduction to the case where $X$ is reduced and irreducible at a generic po
int of each fiber.** This is a standard reduction using the primary decompositio
n of coherent ideal sheaves on $X$.

**(b) The flatification / normalization step.** One reduces to the situation whe
re $F$ has equidimensional fibers by Hironaka-type elimination or by passing to
the normalization and using the fact that properness is preserved.

**(c) The induction step via the Oka–Cartan theory.** The base case is when all
fibers are zero-dimensional, i.e., $F$ is a finite map. In that case, $F_*\mathc
al{O}_X$ is a locally free (hence coherent) $\mathcal{O}_Y$-module of finite ran
k — this is the classical fact that a finite holomorphic map makes $\mathcal{O}_
X$ a finite $\mathcal{O}_Y$-algebra.

For the inductive step, suppose the fiber dimension is at most $d$. One uses a h
olomorphic function $g$ on $X$ that is not identically zero on any positive-dime
nsional irreducible component of any fiber (such a function exists by generic ch
oice, using the fact that $X$ is a complex space and the fibers form an analytic
 family). Then multiplication by $g$ gives an exact sequence of coherent sheaves
 on $X$:

$$0 \to \mathcal{O}_X \xrightarrow{\cdot g} \mathcal{O}_X \to \mathcal{O}_X / g\
mathcal{O}_X \to 0$$

(after localizing to remove the zero locus of $g$ on fibers). The quotient $\mat
hcal{O}_X/g\mathcal{O}_X$ is supported on a complex space whose fibers under $F$
 have dimension at most $d-1$. By the inductive hypothesis, $F_*(\mathcal{O}_X/g
\mathcal{O}_X)$ is coherent over $\mathcal{O}_Y$. The long exact sequence of dir
ect images then shows that the cokernel of $F_*\mathcal{O}_X \xrightarrow{\cdot
g} F_*\mathcal{O}_X$ is coherent, and one uses the Oka–Cartan theorem (coherence
 of the kernel of a morphism of coherent sheaves) to conclude $F_*\mathcal{O}_X$
 is coherent.

This establishes the coherence of $F_*\mathcal{O}_X$.

### Step 5. $F(X)$ is the support of $F_*\mathcal{O}_X$.

We now identify the image $F(X)$ with the support of the coherent sheaf $F_*\mat
hcal{O}_X$:

$$F(X) = \operatorname{Supp}(F_*\mathcal{O}_X) := \{y \in Y : (F_*\mathcal{O}_X)
_y \neq 0\}.$$

**Proof of the equality.** If $y \in F(X)$, then $F^{-1}(y) \neq \emptyset$, so
there exists $x \in X$ with $F(x) = y$. The stalk $(F_*\mathcal{O}_X)_y = \varin
jlim_{y \in W} \mathcal{O}_X(F^{-1}(W))$ contains the germs of holomorphic funct
ions on neighborhoods of $F^{-1}(y)$, which is nonempty, so $(F_*\mathcal{O}_X)_
y \neq 0$ (it contains at least the germ of the constant function $1$). Converse
ly, if $y \notin F(X)$, then $F^{-1}(y) = \emptyset$, so there exists an open ne
ighborhood $W$ of $y$ with $F^{-1}(W) = \emptyset$ (since $F(X)$ is closed), hen
ce $(F_*\mathcal{O}_X)_y = 0$.

### Step 6. The support of a coherent sheaf is analytic.

This is a standard consequence of the Oka–Cartan coherence theorem and the analy
tic Nullstellensatz (Rückert–Forster). Let $\mathcal{F} = F_*\mathcal{O}_X$, a c
oherent $\mathcal{O}_Y$-module. For each $y \in Y$, choose a local presentation:

$$\mathcal{O}_Y^p|_V \xrightarrow{\varphi} \mathcal{O}_Y^q|_V \to \mathcal{F}|_V
 \to 0$$

where $\varphi$ is given by a matrix $(f_{ij})$ of holomorphic functions on an o
pen neighborhood $V$ of $y$. Then:

$$\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal{F}) \cap V = \{y' \in V : \mathcal{F}_{y'} \neq 0
\} = \{y' \in V : \operatorname{rank}(\varphi_{y'}) < q\}$$

which is the locus where all $q \times q$ minors of the matrix $(f_{ij})$ vanish
. This is precisely the zero locus of the ideal sheaf generated by the $q \times
 q$ minors of $\varphi$ — a coherent ideal sheaf — and hence is an analytic subs
et of $V$.

### Step 7. Conclusion.

Combining Steps 5 and 6:

$$F(X) = \operatorname{Supp}(F_*\mathcal{O}_X)$$

is the support of a coherent $\mathcal{O}_Y$-module, hence is an analytic subset
 of $Y$. By Step 1, it is closed. Therefore:

> **The image $F(X)$ of a proper holomorphic map $F: X \to Y$ between complex sp
aces is a closed analytic subset of $Y$.**

This is the **Remmert Proper Mapping Theorem** (also called the Remmert–Stein th
eorem in some formulations). $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** The theorem is optimal in the sense that properness cannot in genera
l be dropped: a non-proper holomorphic map can have an image that is not analyti
c (e.g., $F: \mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \{0\} \to \mathbb{C}^2$ the inclusion has im
age $\mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \{0\}$, which is not analytic in $\mathbb{C}^2$). Th
e key analytic input is Grauert's coherence theorem for direct images of coheren
t sheaves under proper holomorphic maps, from which the result follows by the sh
eaf-theoretic identification of the image with the support of $F_*\mathcal{O}_X$
.
