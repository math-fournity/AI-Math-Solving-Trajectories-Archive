## Proof

**Claim.** The relation of being locally isometric is transitive. That is, if $\
phi: (M_1, g_1) \to (M_2, g_2)$ is a local isometry and $\psi: (M_2, g_2) \to (M
_3, g_3)$ is a local isometry, then $\psi \circ \phi: (M_1, g_1) \to (M_3, g_3)$
 is a local isometry.

**Proof.** The composition $\psi \circ \phi: M_1 \to M_3$ is a smooth map (compo
sition of smooth maps). We must show that for every $m \in M_1$ and every $X, Y
\in T_m M_1$,

$$g_3\bigl((\psi \circ \phi)_* X,\; (\psi \circ \phi)_* Y\bigr) = g_1(X, Y).$$

**Step 1 (Chain rule).** By the chain rule for derivatives of smooth maps betwee
n manifolds, the derivative of the composition at $m$ is the composition of the
derivatives:

$$(\psi \circ \phi)_{*,m} = \psi_{*,\,\phi(m)} \circ \phi_{*,m} : T_m M_1 \longr
ightarrow T_{\phi(m)} M_2 \longrightarrow T_{\psi(\phi(m))} M_3.$$

Hence $(\psi \circ \phi)_* X = \psi_*(\phi_* X)$ and $(\psi \circ \phi)_* Y = \p
si_*(\phi_* Y)$, where $\phi_* X, \phi_* Y \in T_{\phi(m)} M_2$.

**Step 2 (Apply the local isometry condition of $\psi$).** The vectors $\phi_* X
$ and $\phi_* Y$ are tangent vectors at the point $\phi(m) \in M_2$. Since $\psi
: M_2 \to M_3$ is a local isometry, its defining condition holds at **every** po
int of $M_2$, in particular at $p = \phi(m)$, and for **all** tangent vectors at
 that point, in particular for $U = \phi_* X$ and $V = \phi_* Y$:

$$g_3\bigl(\psi_* U,\; \psi_* V\bigr) = g_2(U, V), \qquad U, V \in T_{\phi(m)} M
_2.$$

Substituting $U = \phi_* X$ and $V = \phi_* Y$:

$$g_3\bigl(\psi_*(\phi_* X),\; \psi_*(\phi_* Y)\bigr) = g_2(\phi_* X,\; \phi_* Y
).$$

By Step 1, the left-hand side is $g_3\bigl((\psi \circ \phi)_* X,\; (\psi \circ
\phi)_* Y\bigr)$, so:

$$g_3\bigl((\psi \circ \phi)_* X,\; (\psi \circ \phi)_* Y\bigr) = g_2(\phi_* X,\
; \phi_* Y).$$

**Step 3 (Apply the local isometry condition of $\phi$).** Since $\phi: M_1 \to
M_2$ is a local isometry, its defining condition holds at $m$ for all $X, Y \in
T_m M_1$:

$$g_2(\phi_* X,\; \phi_* Y) = g_1(X, Y).$$

**Step 4 (Combine).** Chaining the equalities from Steps 2 and 3:

$$g_3\bigl((\psi \circ \phi)_* X,\; (\psi \circ \phi)_* Y\bigr) = g_2(\phi_* X,\
; \phi_* Y) = g_1(X, Y).$$

This holds for every $m \in M_1$ and every $X, Y \in T_m M_1$, which is precisel
y the defining condition for $\psi \circ \phi: (M_1, g_1) \to (M_3, g_3)$ to be
a local isometry.

**Remark on the same-dimension hypothesis.** The local isometry condition $g_2(\
phi_* X, \phi_* Y) = g_1(X, Y)$ forces $\phi_{*,m}$ to be injective: if $\phi_*
X = 0$ then $g_1(X, X) = g_2(0, 0) = 0$, so $X = 0$ by positive-definiteness. Si
nce $\dim M_1 = \dim M_2$, the injective linear map $\phi_{*,m}: T_m M_1 \to T_{
\phi(m)} M_2$ is in fact a linear isomorphism, and likewise for $\psi$. Thus $\p
si \circ \phi$ is a local diffeomorphism (by the inverse function theorem), cons
istent with it being a local isometry. The same-dimension assumption for $M_3$ (
inherited transitively, since $\dim M_2 = \dim M_3$) ensures $(\psi \circ \phi)_
{*,m}$ is also a linear isomorphism. However, the metric-preservation identity i
tself follows purely from the chain rule and the pointwise conditions, without n
eeding to invoke this injectivity argument.

**Conclusion.** The composition of two local isometries (between manifolds of th

ally isometric is **transitive**. $\blacksquare$
