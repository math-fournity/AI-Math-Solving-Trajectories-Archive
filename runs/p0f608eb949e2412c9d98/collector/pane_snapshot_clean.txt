## Solution

Define $f(x) = x^{13} + x - e^{-x} - \sin(x)$.

**Step 1: Behavior at infinity.**

- As $x \to +\infty$: the term $x^{13}$ dominates (the other terms are $O(x)$, $
O(1)$, $O(1)$), so $f(x) \to +\infty$.
- As $x \to -\infty$: $x^{13} \to -\infty$ and $-e^{-x} \to -\infty$ (since $e^{
-x}\to+\infty$), while $x$ and $-\sin x$ are $o(x^{13})$ or bounded. Hence $f(x)
 \to -\infty$.

By the Intermediate Value Theorem, $f$ has **at least one** real root.

**Step 2: Strict monotonicity.**

Compute the derivative:
$$f'(x) = 13x^{12} + 1 + e^{-x} - \cos x = \underbrace{13x^{12}}_{\geq\, 0} + \u
nderbrace{(1 - \cos x)}_{\geq\, 0} + \underbrace{e^{-x}}_{>\,0}.$$

Each of the three grouped terms is non-negative, and $e^{-x} > 0$ for every real
 $x$. Therefore
$$f'(x) > 0 \quad \text{for all } x \in \mathbb{R}.$$

So $f$ is **strictly increasing** on all of $\mathbb{R}$.
