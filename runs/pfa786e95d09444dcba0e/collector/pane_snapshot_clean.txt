## Proof

Place the circle as $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ with center $O=(0,0)$, $A=(-1,0)$, $B=(1,0)$
, and $P=(d,0)$ where $d = OP$, $0 \le d < 1$.

A line through $P$ meets the circle at $C$ and $D$. For $ABCD$ to be convex, $C$
 and $D$ lie on opposite sides of $AB$. Write
$$C = (\cos\alpha,\, \sin\alpha),\quad 0 < \alpha < \pi, \qquad D = (\cos\gamma,
\, -\sin\gamma),\quad 0 < \gamma < \pi.$$

**Area via right triangles.** Since $AB$ is a diameter, $\angle ACB = \angle ADB
 = 90°$ (angle in semicircle). Using $AC = 2\cos\frac{\alpha}{2}$, $BC = 2\sin\f
rac{\alpha}{2}$ (and similarly for $AD, BD$):

$$\text{Area}(\triangle ACB) = \tfrac12 \cdot AC \cdot BC = \sin\alpha, \qquad \
text{Area}(\triangle ADB) = \sin\gamma.$$

With $C, D$ on opposite sides of $AB$:

$$\text{Area}(ABCD) = \sin\alpha + \sin\gamma. \tag{1}$$

**Collinearity constraint.** Requiring $C, D, P$ to be collinear (substituting $
P=(d,0)$ into the line through $C, D$) gives

$$\sin(\alpha+\gamma) = d\,(\sin\alpha + \sin\gamma). \tag{2}$$

**Substitution.** Let $u = \frac{\alpha+\gamma}{2}$, $v = \frac{\alpha-\gamma}{2
}$. Then $\sin\alpha+\sin\gamma = 2\sin u\cos v$ and $\sin(\alpha+\gamma) = 2\si
n u\cos u$, so (2) becomes

$$\cos u = d\cos v \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \cos v = \frac{\cos u}{d} \quad (d>
0). \tag{3}$$

Substituting into (1):

$$\text{Area} = 2\sin u\cos v = \frac{2\sin u\cos u}{d} = \frac{\sin(2u)}{d}. \t
ag{4}$$

**Feasibility.** We need $|\cos v|\le 1$, i.e., $|\cos u| \le d$, plus $0 < u\pm
 v < \pi$.

---

**Case 1: $d \ge \frac{\sqrt2}{2}$.** Then $u = \pi/4$ is feasible since $\cos(\
pi/4)=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\le d$, and $\sin(2u)=\sin(\pi/2)=1$ is the global max of
$\sin(2u)$. One verifies $v = \arccos\!\bigl(\frac{\sqrt2/2}{d}\bigr)$ gives val
id $\alpha,\gamma \in (0,\pi)$. Hence

$$\text{Area}_{\max} = \frac{1}{d}.$$

**Case 2: $0 < d < \frac{\sqrt2}{2}$.** Here $u=\pi/4$ is infeasible ($\cos(\pi/
4) > d$). For $u\in(0,\pi/2)$ (where $\sin(2u)>0$), the constraint $|\cos u|\le
d$ forces $\cos u \le d$, i.e., $u \ge \arccos d > \pi/4$. Since $\sin(2u)$ is d
ecreasing on $(\pi/4,\pi/2)$, the maximum is at $u = \arccos d$, where $\cos v =
 1$ (so $v=0$, $\alpha=\gamma$, line $\perp AB$):

$$\text{Area}_{\max} = \frac{\sin(2\arccos d)}{d} = \frac{2d\sqrt{1-d^2}}{d} = 2
\sqrt{1-d^2}.$$

**Case 3: $d = 0$.** Constraint (2) gives $\alpha+\gamma = \pi$, so $\text{Area}
 = 2\sin\alpha \le 2$ (at $\alpha=\pi/2$), matching $2\sqrt{1-0}=2$.

**Continuity.** At $d = \frac{\sqrt2}{2}$: $2\sqrt{1-\tfrac12} = \sqrt2 = \frac{
1}{\sqrt2/2}$. ✓

---

**Conclusion.** With $d = OP$ (distance from the center to $P$), the maximum are
a of quadrilateral $ABCD$ is

$$\boxed{\begin{cases} 2\sqrt{1-d^2} & \text{if } 0 \le d \le \dfrac{\sqrt2}{2},
\\[6pt] \dfrac{1}{d} & \text{if } \dfrac{\sqrt2}{2} \le d < 1. \end{cases}}$$
