## Solution

We analyze the general term of the series
$$a_n = \frac{\sqrt{n-\sqrt{n}}}{n^{1/n+3/2}}.$$

**Step 1: Algebraic simplification.**

Factor the numerator:
$$\sqrt{n-\sqrt{n}} = \sqrt{n\!\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)} = n^{1/2}\sqrt
{1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}}.$$

Factor the denominator:
$$n^{1/n+3/2} = n^{1/n}\cdot n^{3/2}.$$

Therefore
$$a_n = \frac{n^{1/2}\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}}}{n^{1/n}\cdot n^{3/2}} = \frac{
\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}}}{n^{1/n}\cdot n} = \frac{\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}}
}{n^{1/n+1}}.$$

**Step 2: Asymptotic behavior.**

We use two standard limits:
- $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}=0$, so $\displaystyle\sqrt{1
-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}}\to 1$.
- $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ln n}{n}=0$, so $n^{1/n}=e^{\ln n/n}\to
e^0=1$.

Hence
$$a_n = \frac{\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}}}{n^{1/n+1}} \sim \frac{1}{n}\quad\text
{as }n\to\infty.$$

**Step 3: Limit comparison test.**

Compare with $b_n = \dfrac{1}{n}$ (the harmonic series, which diverges):
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_n}{b_n} = \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\sq
rt{n}}}}{n^{1/n}} = \frac{1}{1} = 1.$$

Since the limit is a **positive finite number** ($1\in(0,\infty)$) and $\display
style\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}$ diverges, by the **limit comparison test**,
 the series
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{n-\sqrt{n}}}{n^{1/n+3/2}}$$
also **diverges**.

**Numerical sanity check.** For $n=10000$: $a_{10000}\approx 0.0000995$ while $1
/10000=0.0001$, giving a ratio $\approx 0.995$, confirming the asymptotic $a_n\s
im 1/n$.

**Conclusion.** The series diverges; its sum is $+\infty$.
