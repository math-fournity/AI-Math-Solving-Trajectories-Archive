## Proof

**Setup and notation.** Let $G,H$ be topological groups with $H\subset G$ a subg
roup. Let $p:G\to G/H$ be the canonical quotient map, which is surjective and (f
or topological groups) open. Let $f:G\to G$ be continuous. The commutativity of
the diagram means

$$h\circ p \;=\; p\circ f, \qquad\text{i.e.}\quad h(p(g))=p(f(g))\ \text{for all
 }g\in G.$$

This relation is exactly what defines the induced map $h:G/H\to G/H$ well-define
dly (it is the unique map making the diagram commute, granted $f$ descends to th
e quotient — which the commutativity hypothesis asserts).

**Goal.** Show $h$ is continuous, using the quotient topology on $G/H$.

**Quotient topology characterization.** By definition of the quotient topology o
n $G/H$, a subset $U\subseteq G/H$ is open if and only if

$$p^{-1}(U)\ \text{is open in } G.$$

Equivalently (and this is the form we use), a map $h:G/H\to G/H$ is continuous i
f and only if the composition $h\circ p:G\to G/H$ is continuous — because $p$ is
 a quotient map. We verify this directly.

**Continuity of $h\circ p$.** By commutativity, $h\circ p = p\circ f$. We show $
p\circ f$ is continuous. Let $U\subseteq G/H$ be open. Then

$$(p\circ f)^{-1}(U) \;=\; f^{-1}\!\bigl(p^{-1}(U)\bigr).$$

Since $U$ is open in the quotient topology, $p^{-1}(U)$ is open in $G$. Since $f
:G\to G$ is continuous, $f^{-1}(p^{-1}(U))$ is open in $G$. Hence $(p\circ f)^{-
1}(U)$ is open in $G$ for every open $U\subseteq G/H$, so $p\circ f = h\circ p$
is continuous.

**Descending continuity to the quotient.** Since $p:G\to G/H$ is a quotient map
(surjective, with $G/H$ carrying the quotient topology), the universal property
of the quotient topology gives:

> A map $h:G/H\to Y$ (for any space $Y$) is continuous if and only if $h\circ p:
G\to Y$ is continuous.

Applying this with $Y=G/H$: $h\circ p = p\circ f$ is continuous (shown above), t
herefore $h$ is continuous.

**Explicit verification (without invoking the universal property abstractly).**
For completeness, let $U\subseteq G/H$ be open. We must show $h^{-1}(U)$ is open
 in $G/H$, i.e. (by the quotient topology) that $p^{-1}(h^{-1}(U))$ is open in $
G$. Compute:

$$p^{-1}\!\bigl(h^{-1}(U)\bigr) \;=\; (h\circ p)^{-1}(U) \;=\; (p\circ f)^{-1}(U
) \;=\; f^{-1}\!\bigl(p^{-1}(U)\bigr).$$

As above, $p^{-1}(U)$ is open in $G$ (quotient topology), and $f$ is continuous,
 so $f^{-1}(p^{-1}(U))$ is open in $G$. Thus $p^{-1}(h^{-1}(U))$ is open in $G$,
 which by definition of the quotient topology means $h^{-1}(U)$ is open in $G/H$
. Since $U$ was an arbitrary open set, $h$ is continuous.

**Remark on the hypotheses.** The argument uses only:
- $p$ is a quotient map (surjective + quotient topology on $G/H$),
- $f$ is continuous,
- the diagram commutes ($h\circ p = p\circ f$).

The openness of $p$ is **not** required for this direction (continuity of the in
duced map on the quotient). Openness of $p$ would be used for the *converse* typ
e of question (e.g., showing $h$ is open when $f$ is open), but for continuity o
f $h$ the quotient property alone suffices.

**Conclusion.** Yes — the induced map $h:G/H\to G/H$ is continuous. The proof re
sts entirely on the quotient topology characterization: $h^{-1}(U)$ is open in $
G/H$ iff $p^{-1}(h^{-1}(U))$ is open in $G$, and the latter equals $f^{-1}(p^{-1
}(U))$, which is open by continuity of $f$ and openness of $p^{-1}(U)$ in $G$.
