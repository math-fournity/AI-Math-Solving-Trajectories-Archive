## Solution

The morphism $\pi: \operatorname{Spec} k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2) \to \operatornam
e{Spec} k[x] = \mathbb{A}^1_k$ is induced by the ring homomorphism
$$\varphi: k[x] \longrightarrow k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2), \qquad x \longmapsto 0
.$$

Since both schemes are affine, the scheme-theoretic image of $\pi$ is the closed
 subscheme of $\mathbb{A}^1_k$ cut out by the sheaf of ideals $\ker(\mathcal{O}_
{\mathbb{A}^1_k} \to \pi_*\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spec} k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon
^2)})$, which on global sections is simply $\ker(\varphi)$.

**Computing the kernel.** For any polynomial $f(x) \in k[x]$,
$$\varphi(f(x)) = f(0) \in k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2).$$
Thus $\varphi(f) = 0$ if and only if $f(0) = 0$, i.e., if and only if $x \mid f(
x)$. Hence
$$\ker(\varphi) = (x) \subset k[x].$$

**The scheme-theoretic image.** The scheme-theoretic image is therefore
$$\operatorname{Spec}\bigl(k[x]/(x)\bigr) = \operatorname{Spec} k,$$
the closed point at the origin of $\mathbb{A}^1_k$, equipped with the structure
sheaf $k[x]/(x) \cong k$.

**Why the image is reduced.** The ideal $(x) \subset k[x]$ is prime (since $k[x]
/(x) \cong k$ is a field, or equivalently since $x$ is irreducible in the UFD $k
[x]$). Every prime ideal is radical, so $(x) = \sqrt{(x)}$. Consequently the coo
rdinate ring of the image is
$$k[x]/(x) \cong k,$$
which is a field and hence reduced (it contains no nonzero nilpotents).

**Remark on the conceptual point.** Although the source $\operatorname{Spec} k[\
epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$ is *non-reduced* — it carries the nilpotent $\epsilon \ne
q 0$ with $\epsilon^2 = 0$ — this nilpotent structure is invisible to the scheme
-theoretic image. The reason is that the map $\varphi$ factors through the reduc
tion $k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2) \twoheadrightarrow k$ (sending $\epsilon \mapsto
0$), and the kernel on the $k[x]$-side, namely $(x)$, is already radical. The ni
lpotent $\epsilon$ lives entirely in the "fiber direction" and contributes nothi
ng to the ideal defining the image: the scheme-theoretic image only records the
*smallest closed subscheme through which $\pi$ factors*, and since $\varphi$ sen
ds $x$ to $0$ (a reduced point), the resulting image is the reduced closed point
 $\operatorname{Spec} k \hookrightarrow \mathbb{A}^1_k$.

In summary: **the scheme-theoretic image of $\pi$ is the reduced closed point $\
operatorname{Spec} k$ at the origin of $\mathbb{A}^1_k$**, and it is reduced bec
ause the defining ideal $(x) \subset k[x]$ is prime (hence radical), so the coor
dinate ring $k[x]/(x) \cong k$ has no nilpotents.
