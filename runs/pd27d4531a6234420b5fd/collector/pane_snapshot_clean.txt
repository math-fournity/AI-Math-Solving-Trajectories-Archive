**Convention.** We take "subring" to mean a subset containing $1$ that is closed
 under $+$, $-$, and $\cdot$. (A remark at the end addresses the convention with
out $1$; the cardinality is the same.)

---

**Step 1. Every subring of $\mathbb{Q}$ contains $\mathbb{Z}$.**

If $R\subseteq\mathbb{Q}$ is a subring, then $1\in R$, and closure under additio
n and additive inverses gives $\mathbb{Z}\subseteq R$.

**Step 2. Classification by subsets of primes.**

For a subring $R$ with $\mathbb{Z}\subseteq R$, set
$$S_R \;=\; \{\,p\text{ prime} : 1/p\in R\,\}\;\subseteq\;\mathbb{P},$$
and define the localization
$$\mathbb{Z}[S_R^{-1}] \;=\; \{\,a/b\in\mathbb{Q} : \text{every prime dividing }
 b \text{ lies in } S_R\,\}.$$

*Claim:* $R=\mathbb{Z}[S_R^{-1}]$.

($\supseteq$) If $p\in S_R$ then $1/p\in R$, hence $1/p^k\in R$ for all $k\ge 0$
 (closure under multiplication), and $a/p^k\in R$ for all $a\in\mathbb{Z}\subset
eq R$. Taking products over the primes of $b$ shows $a/b\in R$ whenever all prim
es of $b$ lie in $S_R$.

($\subseteq$) Let $a/b\in R$ with $\gcd(a,b)=1$. Bézout gives $u,v\in\mathbb{Z}$
 with $ua+vb=1$, so
$$u\cdot(a/b)+v \;=\; 1/b \;\in\; R.$$
Write $b=p_1^{e_1}\cdots p_k^{e_k}$. Since $1/b\in R$ and $p_2^{e_2}\cdots p_k^{
e_k}\in\mathbb{Z}\subseteq R$,
$$\frac{1}{p_1^{e_1}} \;=\; \frac{p_2^{e_2}\cdots p_k^{e_k}}{b}\;\in\; R,$$
and then $p_1^{e_1-1}\cdot(1/p_1^{e_1})=1/p_1\in R$, so $p_1\in S_R$. Repeating
for each $p_i$, every prime divisor of $b$ lies in $S_R$, hence $a/b\in\mathbb{Z
}[S_R^{-1}]$. ∎

**Step 3. The map $R\mapsto S_R$ is a bijection.**

- *Injective:* $R=\mathbb{Z}[S_R^{-1}]$ by Step 2, so $R$ is determined by $S_R$
.
- *Surjective:* For any $S\subseteq\mathbb{P}$, the ring $\mathbb{Z}[S^{-1}]$ is
 a subring of $\mathbb{Q}$ (closure under $+,-,\cdot$ is immediate, and $1\in\ma
thbb{Z}[S^{-1}]$), and $S_{\mathbb{Z}[S^{-1}]}=S$.

Thus subrings of $\mathbb{Q}$ are in bijection with the power set $2^{\mathbb{P}
}$.

**Step 4. Cardinality.**

The set $\mathbb{P}$ of primes is countably infinite, so
$$|\{\text{subrings of }\mathbb{Q}\}| \;=\; |2^{\mathbb{P}}| \;=\; 2^{\aleph_0}.
$$

---

**Remark (convention without $1$).** If subrings need not contain $1$, they are
in particular additive subgroups of $\mathbb{Q}$. Every nonzero additive subgrou
p $G\le\mathbb{Q}$ is a rational multiple $g\cdot H$ of a subgroup $H$ with $\ma
thbb{Z}\subseteq H$ (pick $0\ne g\in G$; then $g^{-1}G\ni 1$). Subgroups $H$ wit
h $\mathbb{Z}\subseteq H$ are determined by independent choices, for each prime
$p$, of a $p$-height in $\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}$, giving $(\aleph_0)^{\aleph_0
}=2^{\aleph_0}$ such subgroups. Hence the total number of additive subgroups — a
nd a fortiori of subrings without $1$ — is at most $|\mathbb{Q}^*|\cdot 2^{\alep
h_0}=2^{\aleph_0}$, and at least $2^{\aleph_0}$ (from the unital subrings above)
. So the cardinality is unchanged.

---

**Conclusion.** The set of subrings of $\mathbb{Q}$ has cardinality $2^{\aleph_0
}$, the cardinality of the continuum.
