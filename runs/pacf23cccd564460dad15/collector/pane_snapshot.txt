## Proof

**Answer: No.** The condition does not imply $c < m(\Omega)$.

We exhibit a counterexample in which all hypotheses are satisfied yet $c \geq m(
\Omega)$.

**Setup.** Let $\Omega = (0,1) \subset \mathbb{R}$, so $m(\Omega)=1$. Let $f(x)=
1$, which is positive everywhere on $\Omega$. Set $c = 1 = m(\Omega)$ and $M = \
tfrac{1}{2} > 0$.

**Verification of the hypothesis.** Take any (Lebesgue-measurable) subset $V \su
bseteq \Omega$ with $m(V) \geq c = 1$. Since $V \subseteq \Omega$, we have $m(V)
 \leq m(\Omega) = 1$. Combined with $m(V) \geq 1$, this forces $m(V)=1$, i.e., $
V = \Omega$ up to a null set. Therefore
$$\int_V f(x)\,dx = \int_\Omega 1\,dx = 1 \geq \tfrac{1}{2} = M.$$
So the integral condition holds with the positive constant $M=\tfrac12$.

**Conclusion of the counterexample.** All hypotheses are satisfied: $\Omega$ is
bounded and open, $f>0$ a.e. on $\Omega$, and there exists $M>0$ such that every
 $V\subseteq\Omega$ with $m(V)\geq c$ satisfies $\int_V f \geq M$. Yet $c = m(\O
mega)$, so $c < m(\Omega)$ is **false**.

**Even stronger (vacuous case).** Taking $c = 2 > m(\Omega)=1$ with the same $\O
mega$ and $f$: no subset $V\subseteq\Omega$ can have $m(V)\geq 2$, so the univer
sal statement "for all $V$ with $m(V)\geq c$, $\int_V f \geq M$" is vacuously tr
ue for any $M>0$. Again all hypotheses hold with $c > m(\Omega)$.

Since the hypotheses can be satisfied with $c \geq m(\Omega)$, they do **not** i
mply $c < m(\Omega)$.
