## Proof

Let the circle have radius $R$, be centered at the origin, and lie in a plane $\
Pi$ (which must pass through the origin). Choose orthonormal coordinates $(u,v)$
 in $\Pi$, so every point of the circle satisfies
$$u^{2}+v^{2}=R^{2}. \tag{1}$$

A point on $\Pi$ has 3D position $u\hat e_{1}+v\hat e_{2}$. For either focus $P_
{i}=(x_{i},y_{i},z_{i})$, its squared distance to a point of $\Pi$ is
$$|u\hat e_{1}+v\hat e_{2}-P_{i}|^{2}=u^{2}+v^{2}-2(u\,\hat e_{1}\!\cdot\! P_{i}
+v\,\hat e_{2}\!\cdot\! P_{i})+|P_{i}|^{2}.$$

The crucial observation: the quadratic part is exactly $u^{2}+v^{2}$. **On the c
ircle (1) this quadratic part collapses to the constant $R^{2}$.** Hence, restri
cted to the circle, each squared distance becomes an *affine-linear* function of
 $(u,v)$:
$$d_{i}^{2}=R^{2}+|P_{i}|^{2}-2\bigl(u\,\hat e_{1}\!\cdot\! P_{i}+v\,\hat e_{2}\
!\cdot\! P_{i}\bigr)=:L_{i}(u,v).$$

The Cassini oval (in 3D a surface) is defined by $d_{1}^{2}\,d_{2}^{2}=C^{2}$. R
estricted to the circle this becomes
$$L_{1}(u,v)\,L_{2}(u,v)=C^{2}. \tag{2}$$

Since $L_{1},L_{2}$ are linear, equation (2) is a **quadratic** curve in the $(u
,v)$-plane. The intersection of this quadratic with the circle (1) — also a quad
ratic (conic) — is governed by Bézout's theorem: two conics meet in at most $2\c
dot 2=4$ points (over $\mathbb C$, counting multiplicity). Therefore the number
of real intersection points is

$$\boxed{\le 4}.$$

It remains to show $4$ is attainable. Take $\Pi$ to be the $xy$-plane, $P_{1}=(a
,0,0)$, $P_{2}=(-a,0,0)$, and parametrize the circle by $(u,v)=(R\cos\theta,R\si
n\theta)$. Then
$$d_{1}^{2}=R^{2}+a^{2}-2aR\cos\theta,\qquad d_{2}^{2}=R^{2}+a^{2}+2aR\cos\theta
,$$
so the Cassini condition reads
$$(R^{2}+a^{2})^{2}-4a^{2}R^{2}\cos^{2}\theta=C^{2}
\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
\cos^{2}\theta=\frac{(R^{2}+a^{2})^{2}-C^{2}}{4a^{2}R^{2}}.$$
Choosing parameters so that the right-hand side lies strictly in $(0,1)$ yields
four distinct values $\theta\in[0,2\pi)$, hence **four distinct intersection poi
nts**.

Thus the maximum (and the answer to "how many points can they have in common") i
s

$$\boxed{4}.$$
