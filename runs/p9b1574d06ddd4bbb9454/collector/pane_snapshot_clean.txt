# Steepest-Ascent and Steepest-Descent Curves of $e^{i\Phi(t)}$ near $t=0$

## Setup

Let $t = u + iv$. Given $\rho(t) = t^2$, we have

$$\rho(t) = (u+iv)^2 = \underbrace{(u^2 - v^2)}_{\Phi(t)\,=\,\text{Re}\,\rho} +
i\underbrace{(2uv)}_{\Psi(t)\,=\,\text{Im}\,\rho}.$$

So $\Phi(u,v) = u^2 - v^2$ and its harmonic conjugate is $\Psi(u,v) = 2uv$.

The saddle point is $t=0$ (where $\rho'(t)=2t=0$), at which $\Phi(0,0)=0$ and $\
Psi(0,0)=0$.

## Key Principle

The steepest-ascent and steepest-descent curves of the real part $\Phi$ are the
**level curves of the conjugate harmonic function** $\Psi = \text{Im}(\rho)$. Th
is is because $\nabla\Phi$ and $\nabla\Psi$ are orthogonal (Cauchy–Riemann), so
moving along a level curve of $\Psi$ means moving in the direction of $\nabla\Ph
i$—the direction of steepest change of $\Phi$.

**Direct verification.** The direction of steepest ascent of $\Phi$ is $\nabla\P
hi = (2u,\,-2v)$. A curve $(u(s),v(s))$ tangent to this field satisfies

$$\frac{dv}{du} = \frac{-2v}{2u} = -\frac{v}{u},$$

so $u\,dv + v\,du = 0$, i.e. $d(uv)=0$, giving $uv = \text{const}$, i.e. $\Psi =
 2uv = \text{const}$. ∎

## Curves Through the Saddle Point

The steepest-ascent/descent curves passing through the saddle $t=0$ correspond t
o the level $\Psi = 0$:

$$2uv = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad u = 0 \;\text{or}\; v = 0.$$

These are the two coordinate axes:

### Steepest-Ascent Curve: $v = 0$ (the real axis)

On this curve $\Phi(u,0) = u^2$, which **increases** as $|u|$ grows away from th
e origin. The magnitude of $e^{i\Phi}$ (in the steepest-descent formulation of o
scillatory integrals, the relevant decay factor $e^{-\text{Im}\,\rho}$) is gover
ned by $\Psi = 0$ (constant), while the phase $\Phi = u^2 \to +\infty$. This is
the **steepest-ascent** direction of $\Phi$.

### Steepest-Descent Curve: $u = 0$ (the imaginary axis)

On this curve $\Phi(0,v) = -v^2$, which **decreases** as $|v|$ grows away from t
he origin. Again $\Psi = 0$ is constant, and $\Phi = -v^2 \to -\infty$. This is
the **steepest-descent** direction of $\Phi$.

## General Family (away from the saddle)

For arbitrary constant $c$, the steepest-ascent/descent curves are the rectangul
ar hyperbolas

$$\boxed{uv = \tfrac{c}{2} \quad (c \in \mathbb{R})}.$$

- On each such hyperbola, $\Psi = c$ is constant and $\Phi = u^2 - v^2$ varies m
onotonically, achieving its extremum where the hyperbola is closest to the origi
n.
- The two branches of each hyperbola are respectively ascent and descent traject
ories of $\Phi$.

## Summary

| Curve | Equation | Behavior of $\Phi$ | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Real axis | $v=0$ | $\Phi = u^2 \nearrow +\infty$ | Steepest **ascent** |
| Imaginary axis | $u=0$ | $\Phi = -v^2 \searrow -\infty$ | Steepest **descent**
 |
| Hyperbolas | $uv = c/2$ | $\Phi$ monotone along each branch | General steepest
-ascent/descent curves |

The steepest-ascent and steepest-descent curves of $e^{i\Phi(t)}$ near the saddl
e point $t=0$ are the level curves of $\text{Im}(\rho) = 2uv$. Through the saddl
e these are the real axis ($v=0$, ascent) and the imaginary axis ($u=0$, descent
); in general they form the family of rectangular hyperbolas $uv = \text{const}$
.
