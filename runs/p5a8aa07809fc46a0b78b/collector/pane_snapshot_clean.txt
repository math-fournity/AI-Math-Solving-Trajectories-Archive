## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. If $B: X \to X$ is bounded on $X_1$ and bounded on $X_2$, then $
B$ is bounded on $X_1 \cap X_2$.

**Setup.** Let $X$ be a Hilbert space with norm $\|\cdot\|$, and let $X_1, X_2$
be subspaces of $X$. Let $B: X \to X$ be a linear operator. The statement "$B$ i
s bounded on $X_i$" means there exists a constant $C_i \geq 0$ such that

$$\|Bx\| \leq C_i \|x\| \quad \text{for all } x \in X_i, \quad i = 1, 2.$$

**Argument.** Let $x \in X_1 \cap X_2$ be arbitrary. By definition of intersecti
on, $x \in X_1$ and $x \in X_2$.

- Since $x \in X_1$, the boundedness of $B$ on $X_1$ gives $\|Bx\| \leq C_1 \|x\
|$.
- Since $x \in X_2$, the boundedness of $B$ on $X_2$ gives $\|Bx\| \leq C_2 \|x\
|$.

Combining these two inequalities:

$$\|Bx\| \leq \min(C_1, C_2)\,\|x\|.$$

This holds for every $x \in X_1 \cap X_2$. Setting $C = \min(C_1, C_2)$, we have

$$\sup_{\substack{x \in X_1 \cap X_2 \\ x \neq 0}} \frac{\|Bx\|}{\|x\|} \leq C <
 \infty,$$

so the restriction $B\big|_{X_1 \cap X_2} : X_1 \cap X_2 \to X$ is a bounded ope
rator with operator norm at most $\min(C_1, C_2)$.

**Edge case.** If $X_1 \cap X_2 = \{0\}$, then $B$ is trivially bounded on the i
ntersection (the operator norm on the zero space is $0$ by convention).

**Remark.** The key observation is that membership in the intersection is *stron
ger* than membership in either subspace individually: every element of $X_1 \cap
 X_2$ enjoys the bounds from *both* subspaces simultaneously. Thus the intersect
ion inherits the better (smaller) of the two bounds. This is in contrast to the
sum $X_1 + X_2$, where boundedness on $X_1$ and $X_2$ separately does **not** in
 general imply boundedness on the sum without additional hypotheses (e.g., that
the sum is closed or that the subspaces form a direct sum with a bounded project
ion).
