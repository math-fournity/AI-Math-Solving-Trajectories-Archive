Let $Z_i \sim N(0,1)$ independent, and define
$$S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{Z_i + 1/n}{\sqrt{n}}.$$

### Step 1: Simplify the sum

$$S_n = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^n \left(Z_i + \frac{1}{n}\right) = \frac{1}
{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^n Z_i + \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\cdot\frac{n}{n} = \frac{1}{\sqrt
{n}}\sum_{i=1}^n Z_i + \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}.$$

### Step 2: Compute the MGF

Using independence of the $Z_i$ and the MGF of $N(0,1)$, namely $M_{Z}(t) = e^{t
^2/2}$:

$$M_{S_n}(t) = E\!\left[e^{t S_n}\right] = E\!\left[\exp\!\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{n
}}\sum_{i=1}^n Z_i + \frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]$$

$$= e^{t/\sqrt{n}} \prod_{i=1}^n E\!\left[\exp\!\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}} Z_i\rig
ht)\right] = e^{t/\sqrt{n}} \prod_{i=1}^n \exp\!\left(\frac{t^2}{2n}\right)$$

$$= e^{t/\sqrt{n}} \cdot \exp\!\left(\frac{t^2}{2n}\cdot n\right) = \exp\!\left(
\frac{t^2}{2} + \frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right).$$

### Step 3: Take the limit $n \to \infty$

Since $\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}} \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$ for every fixed $t$,

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} M_{S_n}(t) = \lim_{n\to\infty} \exp\!\left(\frac{t^2}{2} + \
frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right) = \exp\!\left(\frac{t^2}{2}\right).$$

### Step 4: Identify the limiting distribution

$\exp(t^2/2)$ is the MGF of the standard normal distribution $N(0,1)$. Since the
 MGFs converge pointwise to the MGF of $N(0,1)$, and the normal MGF is continuou
s at $t=0$ (indeed everywhere), by the continuity theorem for moment generating
functions,

$$S_n \xrightarrow{d} N(0,1).$$

### Intuition

The shift $1/n$ applied to each $Z_i$ contributes a total deterministic shift of
 $n \cdot (1/n) = 1$ to the unnormalized sum, which after division by $\sqrt{n}$
 becomes $1/\sqrt{n} \to 0$. So the perturbation vanishes in the limit, and the
sum behaves like the standard normalized sum of i.i.d. normals, which is exactly
 $N(0,1)$.

---

**The limiting distribution is $\boxed{N(0,1)}$.**
