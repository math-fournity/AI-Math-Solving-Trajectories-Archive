## Proof

**Interpreting the problem.** The expression $1990^{19911992}+1992^{19911990}$ m
ust be read as $1990^{1991^{1992}}+1992^{1991^{1990}}$ (the only reading yieldin
g a positive answer, since for plain-integer exponents the sum is $\equiv 2\pmod
{1991}$, giving $k=0$).

Let $p=1991=11\times 181$. We seek the largest $k$ with
$$p^k \mid N:=(p-1)^{p^{1992}}+(p+1)^{p^{1990}}.$$
Since $p=11\cdot 181$ is squarefree, $p^k\mid N\iff 11^k\mid N$ and $181^k\mid N
$, so $k=\min\bigl(v_{11}(N),\,v_{181}(N)\bigr)$.

**Reduction via LTE.** Set $A=(p-1)^{p^2}$, $B=p+1$, $n=p^{1990}$. Then
$$N=(p-1)^{p^{1992}}+(p+1)^{p^{1990}}=\bigl((p-1)^{p^2}\bigr)^{p^{1990}}+(p+1)^{
p^{1990}}=A^n+B^n,$$
and $n=p^{1990}$ is **odd**.

For each prime $q\in\{11,181\}$:
- $q\nmid A$: since $p-1\equiv -1\pmod q$, we have $q\nmid(p-1)$.
- $q\nmid B$: since $B=p+1\equiv 1\pmod q$.
- $q\mid A+B$: $A+B=(p-1)^{p^2}+(p+1)\equiv(-1)^{p^2}+1=-1+1=0\pmod q$ (as $p^2$
 is odd).

All hypotheses of **LTE** (odd prime $q$, $q\mid A+B$, $q\nmid AB$, $n$ odd) are
 satisfied, so
$$v_q(A^n+B^n)=v_q(A+B)+v_q(n).$$

**Computing $v_q(n)$.** Since $p=11\times 181$ is squarefree, $v_q(p)=1$, hence
$$v_q(n)=v_q(p^{1990})=1990.$$

**Computing $v_q(A+B)$.** Expand by the binomial theorem:
$$(p-1)^{p^2}=(-1+p)^{p^2}=\sum_{j=0}^{p^2}\binom{p^2}{j}(-1)^{p^2-j}\,p^j=-1+p^
3+\sum_{j\ge 2}\binom{p^2}{j}(-1)^{p^2-j}p^j.$$
Therefore
$$A+B=(p-1)^{p^2}+(p+1)=p+p^3+\sum_{j\ge 2}\binom{p^2}{j}(-1)^{p^2-j}p^j=p\!\lef
t(1+p^2+\sum_{j\ge 2}\binom{p^2}{j}(-1)^{p^2-j}p^{j-1}\right).$$
Let $W=1+p^2+\sum_{j\ge 2}\binom{p^2}{j}(-1)^{p^2-j}p^{j-1}$. Every term of $W$
except the constant $1$ is divisible by $p$ (hence by $q$), so $W\equiv 1\pmod q
$, i.e. $v_q(W)=0$. Thus
$$v_q(A+B)=v_q(p)+v_q(W)=1+0=1.$$

**Conclusion.** For both $q=11$ and $q=181$:
$$v_q(N)=v_q(A+B)+v_q(n)=1+1990=1991.$$
Hence
$$k=\min\bigl(v_{11}(N),\,v_{181}(N)\bigr)=\min(1991,1991)=1991.$$

The largest positive integer $k$ is $\boxed{1991}$.
