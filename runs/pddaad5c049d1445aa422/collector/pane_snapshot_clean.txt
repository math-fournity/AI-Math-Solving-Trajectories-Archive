## Proof

Let $X(t) := B(t) - at$. Then $X$ is a Brownian motion with drift $\mu = -a < 0$
 and diffusion coefficient $\sigma = 1$, started at $X(0)=0$, and
$$T_{a,b} = \inf\{t>0 : X(t) = -b\}.$$

Since the drift $-a$ is negative and the target level $-b$ is also negative, the
 process is pushed toward the level, so $T_{a,b}<\infty$ a.s.

**ODE for the mean hitting time.** Define $f(x) := \mathbb{E}_x[T]$, the expecte
d time to reach $-b$ starting from $x>-b$. Standard diffusion theory (Dynkin/Itô
 on $f(X_t)$, or the backward equation) gives, for $x>-b$,
$$\tfrac12 f''(x) + \mu f'(x) = -1, \qquad f(-b)=0,$$
with the growth condition that $f(x)$ grows at most linearly as $x\to+\infty$ (t
he process drifts downward at speed $a$, so reaching $-b$ from height $x$ takes
time $\asymp (x+b)/a$).

**Solving.** A particular solution is $f_p(x) = -x/\mu = x/a$, since $\mu f_p' =
 \mu\cdot(-1/\mu) = -1$ and $f_p''=0$. The homogeneous equation $\tfrac12 f''+\m
u f'=0$ has solutions $D + C e^{-2\mu x}$. With $\mu=-a$, the exponential term i
s $e^{2ax}$, which blows up as $x\to+\infty$; the growth condition forces $C=0$.
 Hence
$$f(x) = D + \frac{x}{a}.$$
The boundary condition $f(-b)=0$ gives $D - b/a = 0$, i.e. $D = b/a$. Therefore
$$f(x) = \frac{x+b}{a}.$$

**Conclusion.** The process starts at $x=0$, so
$$\mathbb{E}[T_{a,b}] = f(0) = \frac{b}{a}.$$
