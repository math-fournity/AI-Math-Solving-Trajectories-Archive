## Solution

**Claim.** The Borel probability measures on $\mathbb{R}^n$ that are (i) $O(n)$-
invariant and (ii) a product $\mu_1\otimes\mu_2$ of two measures concentrated on
 nontrivial orthogonal subspaces $V_1,V_2$ are exactly the **centered isotropic
Gaussians**
$$
\mu_\sigma \;=\; \mathcal N(0,\sigma^2 I_n),\qquad \sigma\ge 0,
$$
where $\sigma=0$ is the Dirac mass $\delta_0$ at the origin. It factors as
$$
\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2 I_n)\big|_{V_1\oplus V_2}
 \;=\; \mathcal N(0,\sigma^2 I_{V_1})\;\otimes\;\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2 I_{V_2}).
$$

---

### Proof

Let $\dim V_1=k\ge 1$, $\dim V_2=m=n-k\ge 1$ (both nontrivial), $V_1\perp V_2$.
Write $\mathbb R^n=V_1\oplus V_2$ (if $V_1\oplus V_2\subsetneq\mathbb R^n$, the
support of $\mu$ lies in a proper subspace; $O(n)$-invariance forces the support
 to be $O(n)$-invariant, hence $\{0\}$, giving $\mu=\delta_0$, which is the case
 $\sigma=0$ below). So assume $V_1\oplus V_2=\mathbb R^n$.

Let $\varphi(t)=\int e^{i\langle t,x\rangle}\,d\mu(x)$ be the characteristic fun
ction (CF), and $\varphi_j$ that of $\mu_j$ on $V_j\cong\mathbb R^{k(m)}$.

**Step 1 — Each factor is rotationally invariant.** A block-diagonal orthogonal
map $U=\operatorname{diag}(U_k,I_m)$ with $U_k\in O(k)$ fixes $V_2$ pointwise an
d rotates $V_1$. Since $\mu$ is $O(n)$-invariant, $\mu$ is invariant under $U$;
because $\mu=\mu_1\otimes\mu_2$ and $U$ acts only on the first factor, $\mu_1$ i
s $O(k)$-invariant. Likewise $\mu_2$ is $O(m)$-invariant. Hence
$$
\varphi_1(t_1)=\psi_1(|t_1|),\qquad \varphi_2(t_2)=\psi_2(|t_2|)
$$
for continuous $\psi_1,\psi_2$ on $[0,\infty)$. Likewise $O(n)$-invariance of $\
mu$ gives $\varphi(t)=\psi(|t|)$.

**Step 2 — Functional equation.** For $t=t_1+t_2$ with $t_j\in V_j$, the product
 structure gives $\varphi(t)=\varphi_1(t_1)\varphi_2(t_2)$, i.e.
$$
\psi\!\bigl(\sqrt{|t_1|^2+|t_2|^2}\bigr)=\psi_1(|t_1|)\,\psi_2(|t_2|).
$$
Since $k,m\ge 1$, the quantities $u:=|t_1|^2$ and $v:=|t_2|^2$ range independent
ly over $[0,\infty)$. Define $F(s)=\psi(\sqrt s)$, $F_j(s)=\psi_j(\sqrt s)$ for
$s\ge 0$. Then
$$
F(u+v)=F_1(u)\,F_2(v),\qquad u,v\ge 0. \tag{$\ast$}
$$
Setting $v=0$ and using $F_2(0)=\varphi_2(0)=1$ gives $F(u)=F_1(u)$; setting $u=
0$ and using $F_1(0)=1$ gives $F(v)=F_2(v)$. Thus $F=F_1=F_2$ and $(\ast)$ becom
es the multiplicative Cauchy equation
$$
F(u+v)=F(u)F(v),\qquad u,v\ge 0.
$$
$F$ is continuous (CFs are continuous) and $F(0)=1$, so $F(s)=e^{cs}$ for some c
onstant $c\in\mathbb C$. Therefore
$$
\psi(r)=e^{c r^2},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \varphi(t)=e^{c|t|^2}. \tag{$\dagger$}
$$

**Step 3 — $c$ is real and $\le 0$.** Since $\varphi$ is a CF, $|\varphi(t)|\le
1$ for all $t$, so $e^{\operatorname{Re}(c)|t|^2}\le 1$, giving $\operatorname{R
e}(c)\le 0$. Restrict to any line: $g(t):=\varphi(t e_1)=e^{c t^2}$ is the CF of
 the real random variable $\langle X,e_1\rangle$, hence hermitian: $g(-t)=\overl
ine{g(t)}$. But $g(-t)=e^{c t^2}=g(t)$, so $g(t)=\overline{g(t)}$ is real for al
l $t$. Writing $c=a+ib$, $e^{(a+ib)t^2}$ real for all $t$ forces $b\,t^2\in\pi\m
athbb Z$ for all $t$, hence $b=0$. So $c\in\mathbb R$ and $c\le 0$.

Set $\sigma^2:=-2c\ge 0$. Then $(\dagger)$ becomes
$$
\varphi(t)=e^{-\frac{\sigma^2}{2}|t|^2},
$$
which is precisely the CF of $\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2 I_n)$ (with $\sigma=0$ givin
g $\delta_0$). By uniqueness of CFs, $\mu=\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2 I_n)$.

**Step 4 — Converse.** For any $\sigma\ge 0$, $\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2 I_n)$ is $O
(n)$-invariant (covariance is scalar), and on $V_1\oplus V_2$ it factors as
$$
\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2 I_{V_1})\otimes\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2 I_{V_2}),
$$
a product of two measures concentrated on the nontrivial orthogonal subspaces $V
_1,V_2$. $\square$

---

**Summary.** The only $O(n)$-invariant Borel probability measures on $\mathbb R^
n$ that split as a product over a nontrivial orthogonal decomposition are the ce
ntered isotropic Gaussians $\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2 I_n)$, $\sigma\ge 0$ (includin
g the Dirac mass at the origin as the degenerate case $\sigma=0$).
