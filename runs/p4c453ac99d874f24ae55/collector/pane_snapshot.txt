# Evaluation of $\oint_{|z|=1} \frac{\log|1-z|}{z}\,dz$

## Setup and parametrization

Parametrize the unit circle by $z = e^{i\theta}$, $\theta \in [0, 2\pi)$, so $dz
 = ie^{i\theta}\,d\theta$ and $1/z = e^{-i\theta}$. Then

$$
\oint_{|z|=1} \frac{\log|1-z|}{z}\,dz
= \int_0^{2\pi} \log|1-e^{i\theta}|\cdot e^{-i\theta}\cdot i e^{i\theta}\,d\thet
a
= i\int_0^{2\pi} \log|1-e^{i\theta}|\,d\theta.
$$

The factor $e^{-i\theta}\cdot e^{i\theta}=1$ collapses the integrand to a **pure
ly real** function of $\theta$, so the whole problem reduces to evaluating a sin
gle real integral.

## Reducing $|1-e^{i\theta}|$

Compute
$$
|1-e^{i\theta}|^2 = (1-\cos\theta)^2 + \sin^2\theta = 2 - 2\cos\theta = 4\sin^2(
\theta/2),
$$
hence $|1-e^{i\theta}| = 2|\sin(\theta/2)|$ and
$$
\log|1-e^{i\theta}| = \log\!\bigl(2|\sin(\theta/2)|\bigr).
$$

The only singularity on the contour is at $\theta = 0$ (i.e. $z=1$), where $\log
|1-z|\to -\infty$; this is a logarithmic (integrable) singularity, so the integr
al is well-defined as an improper integral.

Substituting $u = \theta/2$, $d\theta = 2\,du$, with $\theta:0\to 2\pi$ giving $
u:0\to\pi$:
$$
\int_0^{2\pi} \log\!\bigl(2|\sin(\theta/2)|\bigr)\,d\theta
= 2\int_0^{\pi} \log(2\sin u)\,du,
$$
where on $(0,\pi)$ we have $\sin u > 0$, so the absolute value drops.

## The classical integral $\int_0^{\pi}\log(2\sin u)\,du = 0$

Use the Fourier expansion (valid for $u\in(0,\pi)$):
$$
\log(2\sin u) = -\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\cos(2nu)}{n}.
$$
This follows from $-\log(1-e^{2iu}) = \sum_{n\ge 1} e^{2inu}/n$ and taking real
parts: $\operatorname{Re}\log(1-e^{2iu}) = \log|1-e^{2iu}| = \log(2\sin u)$, whi
le $\operatorname{Re}\sum_{n\ge1} e^{2inu}/n = \sum_{n\ge1}\cos(2nu)/n$.

Integrating term-by-term (justified by $L^1$ convergence of the series, or by Ab
el summation via $r\uparrow 1$):
$$
\int_0^{\pi}\log(2\sin u)\,du
= -\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_0^{\pi}\cos(2nu)\,du
= -\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\cdot\frac{\sin(2nu)}{2n}\Big|_0^{\pi}
= -\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\cdot 0 = 0,
$$
since $\sin(2n\pi)=\sin 0 = 0$ for every integer $n$.

(Equivalently, this recovers the standard identity $\int_0^{\pi}\log(\sin u)\,du
 = -\pi\log 2$.)

## Conclusion

$$
\int_0^{2\pi} \log|1-e^{i\theta}|\,d\theta = 2\cdot 0 = 0,
$$
and therefore
$$
\oint_{|z|=1} \frac{\log|1-z|}{z}\,dz = i\cdot 0 = 0.
$$

$$
\boxed{\,\oint_{|z|=1} \frac{\log|1-z|}{z}\,dz = 0\,}
$$
