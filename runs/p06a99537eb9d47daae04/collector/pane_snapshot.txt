# Computing $\pi_1(X_n)$

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains only the problem, no solut
ion, answer, or proof sketch. Proceeding.

---

## Setup

$X_n$ is the **mapping torus** of the map $f:S^1\to S^1$, $f(z)=z^n$, since
$$X_n=\bigl([0,1]\times S^1\bigr)\big/\bigl((0,z)\sim(1,f(z))\bigr).$$

We compute $\pi_1(X_n)$ by giving $X_n$ a CW structure and applying van Kampen (
equivalently, reading off the relation from the single 2-cell).

---

## CW structure

Model $S^1=[0,1]/(0\sim 1)$ and write the gluing as $(0,u)\sim(1,\,nu\bmod 1)$ f
or $u\in[0,1]$. Then $X_n$ is the quotient of the square $[0,1]\times[0,1]$ by

- $(s,0)\sim(s,1)$ for all $s$ (the $S^1$-factor; identifies top and bottom edge
s),
- $(0,u)\sim(1,nu\bmod 1)$ for all $u$ (the mapping-torus gluing; identifies lef
t and right edges).

**0-cells.** All four corners are identified: $(0,0)\sim(0,1)\sim(1,0)\sim(1,1)$
 (since $n\cdot 0\equiv 0$). So there is a single 0-cell $v$.

**1-cells.** Two 1-cells, both loops at $v$:
- $t$: the horizontal edge $[0,1]\times\{0\}$ (the interval direction; top $\equ
iv$ bottom by the first relation),
- $a$: the vertical edge $\{0\}\times[0,1]$ (the $S^1$ direction).

The right edge $\{1\}\times[0,1]$ is identified with the left edge via $u\mapsto
 nu$, which wraps the left edge $n$ times; hence traversing the right edge once
represents $a^n$.

**2-cell.** The interior of the square, a single 2-cell $e^2$.

The 1-skeleton is therefore a wedge of two circles, with $\pi_1=\langle a,t\rang
le$ (free group on two generators).

---

## Reading off the relation

Traverse $\partial e^2$ counterclockwise:

| side | traversal | element |
|------|-----------|---------|
| bottom, left→right | $[0,1]\times\{0\}$ | $t$ |
| right, bottom→top | $\{1\}\times[0,1]$ | $a^n$ (degree-$n$ wrap) |
| top, right→left | $[0,1]\times\{1\}\equiv[0,1]\times\{0\}$, reversed | $t^{-1}
$ |
| left, top→bottom | $\{0\}\times[0,1]$, reversed | $a^{-1}$ |

So the attaching map spells the word
$$t\cdot a^{n}\cdot t^{-1}\cdot a^{-1},$$
and van Kampen imposes the relation
$$t\,a^{n}\,t^{-1}\,a^{-1}=1,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad t\,a^{n}\,t^{-1}=a.$$

Equivalently (conjugating),
$$t^{-1}a\,t=a^{n}.$$

---

## Result

$$\boxed{\;\pi_1(X_n)\;\cong\;\bigl\langle\,a,\;t\;\bigm|\;t^{-1}a\,t=a^{\,n}\,\
bigr\rangle\;\cong\;\mathbb{Z}\rtimes_{\times n}\mathbb{Z}\;}$$

where the generator $t$ of the second $\mathbb{Z}$ acts on the first $\mathbb{Z}
=\langle a\rangle$ by $a\mapsto a^{n}$ (multiplication by $n$).

**Sanity checks.**
- $n=1$: the relation becomes $t^{-1}at=a$, i.e. $[a,t]=1$, giving $\mathbb{Z}^2
$. Indeed $X_1=([0,1]\times S^1)/(0,z)\sim(1,z)$ is the 2-torus. ✓
- $n=2$: $\langle a,t\mid t^{-1}at=a^{2}\rangle$, the Baumslag–Solitar group $\m
athrm{BS}(1,2)$, consistent with $X_2$ being the mapping torus of the degree-2 s
elf-map of $S^1$. ✓
