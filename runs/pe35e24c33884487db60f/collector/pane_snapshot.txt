Looking at this problem, I need to compute the fundamental group of a space form
ed by attaching two 2-spheres to a circle at two points each.

## Solution

**Setup.** Let $Y = X_3 \cup X_1 \cup X_2$ where $X_3 = S^1$ with distinguished
points $n, s$, and $X_1, X_2$ are copies of $S^2$ with $N_i \sim n$ and $S_i \si
m s$ for $i = 1, 2$.

**Key observation.** Each $S^2$ is simply connected, so attaching it at two poin
ts creates a new path between $n$ and $s$ without imposing any relations.

**Proof via the groupoid Seifert–van Kampen theorem.**

Build $Y$ in stages.

**Stage 0.** $Z_0 = X_3 = S^1$, so $\pi_1(Z_0, n) = \langle a \rangle \cong \mat
hbb{Z}$, where $a$ is the loop traversing $S^1$ once.

**Stage 1.** Attach $X_1 = S^2$ by identifying $N_1 \sim n$, $S_1 \sim s$. Set $
Z_1 = Z_0 \cup X_1$ with $Z_0 \cap X_1 = \{n, s\}$ (two points).

Apply the groupoid van Kampen theorem with $A = Z_0$, $B = X_1$, $A \cap B = \{n
, s\}$:
- $\Pi_1(A)$: the fundamental groupoid of $S^1$. At object $n$: $\operatorname{H
om}(n,n) = \langle a \rangle$. Paths from $n$ to $s$ form a torsor over $\langle
 a \rangle$ (two arcs of $S^1 \setminus \{n,s\}$, differing by $a$).
- $\Pi_1(B)$: the fundamental groupoid of $S^2$, which is simply connected. Ther
e is a unique homotopy class of path $p_1$ from $n$ to $s$ in $S^2$.
- $\Pi_1(A \cap B)$: the discrete groupoid on $\{n, s\}$ (only identity morphism
s, since each point is contractible).

The pushout groupoid $\Pi_1(Z_1)$ has:
$$\operatorname{Hom}_{Z_1}(n, n) = \langle a,\; c_1 \rangle$$
where $c_1 = q \cdot p_1^{-1}$ is the loop: travel from $n$ to $s$ along a fixed
 arc $q$ of $X_3$, then return from $s$ to $n$ through $S^2_1$ via $p_1^{-1}$. S
ince $S^2$ is simply connected, $p_1$ is unique (no relation from $B$), and sinc
e $A \cap B$ is discrete (no amalgamation relations), we get:
$$\pi_1(Z_1, n) \cong \langle a, c_1 \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z} = F_2
.$$

**Stage 2.** Attach $X_2 = S^2$ by identifying $N_2 \sim n$, $S_2 \sim s$. Set $
Y = Z_1 \cup X_2$ with $Z_1 \cap X_2 = \{n, s\}$.

Apply the same argument with $A = Z_1$, $B = X_2$:
- $\pi_1(A) = \langle a, c_1 \rangle \cong F_2$.
- $B = S^2$ is simply connected; there is a unique path $p_2$ from $n$ to $s$ in
 $S^2_2$.
- $A \cap B = \{n, s\}$ is discrete.

The pushout gives:
$$\pi_1(Y, n) = \langle a, c_1, c_2 \rangle$$
where $c_2 = q \cdot p_2^{-1}$ is the loop: travel from $n$ to $s$ along the sam
e arc $q$ of $X_3$, then return through $S^2_2$. No relations are imposed (since
 $S^2_2$ is simply connected and the intersection is discrete).

**Verification that no hidden relations arise.** The equator $E_i$ of each $S^2_
i$ is a loop that bounds a disk (a hemisphere) within $S^2_i \subset Y$, hence i
s null-homotopic in $Y$. This null-homotopy lives entirely inside $S^2_i$ and do
es not involve $X_3$, so it imposes no relation on $a$, $c_1$, or $c_2$. The gen
erators are genuinely free.

**CW-complex cross-check.** Give $Y$ a CW structure with 0-cells $\{n, s, p_1, p
_2\}$ (where $p_i$ is a point on the equator of $S^2_i$), 1-cells $\{\alpha, \be
ta, \gamma_1, \delta_1, \epsilon_1, \gamma_2, \delta_2, \epsilon_2\}$ (two arcs
of $X_3$, equator loops, and meridian halves for each sphere), and 2-cells (hemi
spheres of each $S^2_i$). The 1-skeleton is a graph with $\pi_1 \cong F_{8-4+1}
= F_5$. Choosing spanning tree $\{\alpha, \delta_1, \delta_2\}$, the generators
are $\{g_\beta, g_{\gamma_1}, g_{\epsilon_1}, g_{\gamma_2}, g_{\epsilon_2}\}$. T
he four 2-cells (two hemispheres per sphere) yield only the relations $g_{\gamma
_1} = 1$ and $g_{\gamma_2} = 1$ (each equator is null-homotopic). After eliminat
ing $\gamma_1, \gamma_2$, three free generators remain: $\pi_1(Y) \cong F_3$. ✓

**Conclusion.** The fundamental group of the resulting space is the free group o
n three generators:

$$\boxed{\pi_1(Y) \cong \mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z} \cong F_3}$$

generated by:
- $a$: the loop going around $X_3 = S^1$ once,
- $c_1$: the loop traveling from $n$ to $s$ along an arc of $S^1$ and returning
through $S^2_1$,
- $c_2$: the loop traveling from $n$ to $s$ along the same arc and returning thr
ough $S^2_2$.
