## Proof

**Claim.** Let $R$ be a UFD and let $P$ be a prime ideal minimal over a principa
l ideal $(a)$. Then $\operatorname{ht}(P) \le 1$.

**Proof.** If $a = 0$, then since a UFD is by definition an integral domain, $(0
)$ is prime, and the unique prime minimal over $(0)$ is $(0)$ itself, which has
height $0 \le 1$. If $a$ is a unit, $(a) = R$ and no prime contains it. So assum
e $a$ is a nonzero nonunit.

Factor $a$ in the UFD:
$$a = u\, p_1^{e_1} p_2^{e_2} \cdots p_n^{e_n},$$
where $u$ is a unit and the $p_i$ are pairwise non-associate prime (irreducible)
 elements, $e_i \ge 1$.

**Step 1. Each $(p_i)$ is a prime ideal of height $1$.**

Since $p_i$ is a prime element, $(p_i)$ is a prime ideal. To compute its height,
 let $Q$ be a prime ideal with $(0) \subsetneq Q \subseteq (p_i)$. Pick $0 \ne x
 \in Q$ and factor $x = v\, q_1^{f_1}\cdots q_m^{f_m}$ with $q_j$ prime elements
. Since $Q$ is prime and $x \in Q$, some $q_j \in Q$, hence $(q_j) \subseteq Q$.
 On the other hand $Q \subseteq (p_i)$ gives $q_j \in (p_i)$, i.e. $p_i \mid q_j
$. Both $p_i$ and $q_j$ are irreducible, so they are associates and $(q_j) = (p_
i)$. Therefore
$$(p_i) = (q_j) \subseteq Q \subseteq (p_i),$$
so $Q = (p_i)$. Thus the only prime strictly between $(0)$ and $(p_i)$ is $(p_i)
$ itself; the chain $(0) \subsetneq (p_i)$ is maximal, and $\operatorname{ht}(p_
i) = 1$.

**Step 2. The minimal primes over $(a)$ are exactly $(p_1),\dots,(p_n)$.**

Each $(p_i)$ contains $a$ (since $p_i \mid a$), so each $(p_i)$ is a prime conta
ining $(a)$.

Now let $P$ be any prime minimal over $(a)$. Since $a = u\, p_1^{e_1}\cdots p_n^
{e_n} \in P$ and $P$ is prime, some $p_i \in P$, giving $(p_i) \subseteq P$. But
 $(p_i)$ is itself a prime ideal containing $(a)$. By minimality of $P$ over $(a
)$, no prime strictly contained in $P$ can contain $(a)$; since $(p_i) \subseteq
 P$ and $(p_i) \supseteq (a)$, minimality forces $(p_i) = P$.

Hence every prime minimal over $(a)$ equals some $(p_i)$, and by Step 1 each has
 height $1$.

**Conclusion.** In every case (zero, unit, or nonzero nonunit generator), a prim
e ideal minimal over a principal ideal in a UFD has height at most $1$. $\blacks
quare$
