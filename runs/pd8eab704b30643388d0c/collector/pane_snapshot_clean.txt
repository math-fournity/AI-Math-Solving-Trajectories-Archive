**Uniqueness.** Let $f$ be any solution. For each $x$, let $y(x)$ denote the uni

*Preliminary on the ring.* A totally ordered commutative ring has $1>0$ (since $
1=1^2\ge 0$, $1\ne 0$) and is an integral domain (products of same-sign elements

$F$ when needed, interpreting results back in $R$.

o $x$ is a witness for $z$. By uniqueness, $y(z)=x$. Thus $y(y(x))=x$.

**Step 2: $y(x)=x$ for all $x$.** Suppose $y(a)=b\ne a$. By Step 1, $y(b)=a$, an

$$af(b)+bf(a)\le 2. \tag{$\star$}$$
Since $b$ is the *unique* witness for $a$: for all $c\ne b$, $af(c)+cf(a)>2$. Ta
king $c=a$ (valid as $a\ne b$): $2af(a)>2$, so $af(a)>1$.

Since $a$ is the unique witness for $b$: for all $c\ne a$, $bf(c)+cf(b)>2$. Taki
ng $c=b$ (valid as $b\ne a$): $2bf(b)>2$, so $bf(b)>1$.

Set $s=af(a)-1>0$, $t=bf(b)-1>0$ (both in $R$). In $F$:
$$af(b)+bf(a)-2 = \frac{a^2\cdot bf(b)+b^2\cdot af(a)-2ab}{ab} = \frac{a^2(1+t)+
b^2(1+s)-2ab}{ab} = \frac{(a-b)^2+a^2 t+b^2 s}{ab}.$$
The numerator is positive in $R$: $(a-b)^2>0$ (since $a\ne b$), and $a^2t>0$, $b
^2s>0$. With $ab>0$, we get $af(b)+bf(a)-2>0$, i.e., $af(b)+bf(a)>2$, contradict

**Step 3: $xf(x)=1$ for all $x$.** From Step 2, $y(x)=x$, so $2xf(x)\le 2$, givi

For $z\ne x$, $z$ is not the witness for $x$, so $xf(z)+zf(x)>2$. Writing $f(z)=
(1-q(z))/z$, $f(x)=(1-q(x))/x$ in $F$:
$$\frac{x^2(1-q(z))+z^2(1-q(x))}{xz} > 2,$$
so $(x-z)^2 > x^2 q(z)+z^2 q(x)\ge z^2 q(x)$. $\tag{$\dagger$}$

Suppose $q(x_0)>0$ for some $x_0$. Set $z=x_0(1+q(x_0))\in R$. Since $x_0>0$ and

$$(x_0-z)^2 > z^2 q(x_0).$$

$$x_0^2 q(x_0)^2 > x_0^2(1+q(x_0))^2 q(x_0).$$

Contradiction. So $q(x)=0$ for all $x$, i.e., $xf(x)=1$, i.e., $\boxed{f(x)=1/x}
