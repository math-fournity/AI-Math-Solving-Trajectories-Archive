## Proof

Let $X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,p)$, $q=1-p$, and $P_k=\Pr(X=k)=\binom{n}{k}p^kq^
{n-k}$. Set $\sigma_n=\sqrt{npq}$. We must show
$$a_n=\frac{1}{\sqrt n}\sum_{k=0}^n\sqrt{P_k}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.$$
Since every term is nonnegative, it suffices to prove $\sum_k\sqrt{P_k}=o(\sqrt
n)$.

---

### A local upper bound for $P_k$

For $u=k/n\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]$ (any fixed $\varepsilon>0$), Stirling'
s formula gives
$$P_k=\binom{n}{k}p^{nu}q^{n(1-u)}=\frac{1+O(1/n)}{\sqrt{2\pi n\,u(1-u)}}\,e^{-n
D(u\,\|\,p)},$$
where $D(u\|p)=u\ln\frac{u}{p}+(1-u)\ln\frac{1-u}{q}$. Near $u=p$ we have $D(u\|
p)=\frac{(u-p)^2}{2pq}+O((u-p)^3)$, so for $|u-p|$ small enough there are consta
nts $c,C>0$ with
$$D(u\|p)\ge c(u-p)^2,\qquad \frac{1}{\sqrt{u(1-u)}}\le C.$$
Hence, for all $k$ with $|k-np|\le \delta n$ (fixed small $\delta$),
$$P_k\le \frac{C_1}{\sqrt n}\,e^{-c_1(k-np)^2/n}=\frac{C_1'}{\sigma_n}\,e^{-c_1'
(k-np)^2/\sigma_n^2}. \tag{1}$$

---

### Bulk estimate

Let $B=\{k:\ |k-np|\le \sigma_n\sqrt{\ln n}\}$. For large $n$, $B\subset\{|k-np|
\le\delta n\}$, so (1) applies throughout $B$. Using $\sqrt{e^{-x}}=e^{-x/2}$,
$$\sum_{k\in B}\sqrt{P_k}\le \frac{\sqrt{C_1'}}{\sigma_n^{1/2}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb
 Z}e^{-c_1'(k-np)^2/(2\sigma_n^2)}\le \frac{\sqrt{C_1'}}{\sigma_n^{1/2}}\cdot\sq
rt{\frac{2\pi\sigma_n^2}{c_1'}}=C_2\,\sigma_n^{1/2}=O(n^{1/4}). \tag{2}$$
(The integer sum is bounded by the corresponding Gaussian integral $\int e^{-x^2
/(2\sigma_n^2)}\,dx=\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma_n/\sqrt{c_1'}$.)

---

### Tail estimate

For the tail $T=\{k:\ |k-np|>\sigma_n\sqrt{\ln n}\}$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives
$$\sum_{k\in T}\sqrt{P_k}\le\sqrt{(n+1)\,\Pr\!\bigl(|X-np|>\sigma_n\sqrt{\ln n}\
bigr)}.$$
Hoeffding's inequality yields $\Pr(|X-np|>t)\le 2e^{-2t^2/n}$. With $t=\sigma_n\
sqrt{\ln n}=\sqrt{npq\ln n}$,
$$\Pr\!\bigl(|X-np|>\sigma_n\sqrt{\ln n}\bigr)\le 2e^{-2pq\ln n}=2\,n^{-2pq}.$$
Therefore
$$\sum_{k\in T}\sqrt{P_k}\le\sqrt{2(n+1)\,n^{-2pq}}=O\!\bigl(n^{1/2-pq}\bigr)=o(
\sqrt n). \tag{3}$$

---

### Conclusion

Combining (2) and (3),
$$\sum_{k=0}^n\sqrt{P_k}=O(n^{1/4})+o(\sqrt n)=o(\sqrt n),$$
since $n^{1/4}=o(\sqrt n)$. Consequently
$$a_n=\frac{1}{\sqrt n}\sum_{k=0}^n\sqrt{P_k}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}a_n=0.}$$
