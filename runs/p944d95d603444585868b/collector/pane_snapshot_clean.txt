## Proof

**Setup.** Let $F = \mathbb{Z}_p$ and let $C$ be a $(p-1)\times(p-1)$ circulant
matrix with first row $(c_0, c_1, \ldots, c_{p-2})$. Its representer polynomial
is
$$p_C(x) = c_0 + c_1 x + \cdots + c_{p-2}\, x^{p-2},$$
a polynomial of degree $\le p-2$ over $\mathbb{Z}_p$.

**Eigenvalues of a circulant matrix.** The eigenvalues of an $n\times n$ circula
nt matrix are $p_C(\omega^j)$ for $j = 0, 1, \ldots, n-1$, where $\omega$ is a p
rimitive $n$-th root of unity (in a splitting field). The matrix is invertible i
f and only if none of these eigenvalues is zero.

**Key observation: the roots of unity live in $\mathbb{Z}_p$ itself.** Here $n =
 p-1$. The multiplicative group $\mathbb{Z}_p^*$ is cyclic of order $p-1$; let $
g$ be a generator (primitive root). Then
$$g^0, g^1, g^2, \ldots, g^{p-2}$$
are exactly all $(p-1)$-th roots of unity, and they are precisely the nonzero el
ements $\{1, 2, \ldots, p-1\}$ of $\mathbb{Z}_p$. Thus the eigenvalues of $C$ ar
e
$$p_C(1),\; p_C(2),\; \ldots,\; p_C(p-1),$$
all evaluated within $\mathbb{Z}_p$ (no field extension needed).

**Invertibility criterion.** $C$ is invertible $\iff$ $p_C(a) \neq 0$ for every
$a \in \mathbb{Z}_p^* = \{1, 2, \ldots, p-1\}$.

**Counting via the Vandermonde bijection.** Consider the evaluation map
$$\Phi: \mathbb{Z}_p^{p-1} \longrightarrow \mathbb{Z}_p^{p-1}, \qquad (c_0, c_1,
 \ldots, c_{p-2}) \;\longmapsto\; \bigl(p_C(1),\, p_C(2),\, \ldots,\, p_C(p-1)\b
igr).$$
This is a linear map represented by the $(p-1)\times(p-1)$ Vandermonde matrix $V
$ with entries $V_{j,i} = j^{\,i}$ for $j \in \{1,\ldots,p-1\}$ and $i \in \{0,\
ldots,p-2\}$. Since the nodes $1, 2, \ldots, p-1$ are distinct elements of $\mat
hbb{Z}_p$, the Vandermonde determinant
$$\det V = \prod_{1 \le i < j \le p-1}(j - i) \neq 0 \pmod{p},$$
so $V$ is invertible over $\mathbb{Z}_p$. Hence $\Phi$ is a **bijection**: every
 tuple of values $(v_1, \ldots, v_{p-1}) \in \mathbb{Z}_p^{p-1}$ is realized by
exactly one representer polynomial of degree $\le p-2$.

**Counting invertible matrices.** Under this bijection, the invertible circulant
 matrices correspond exactly to those value-tuples in which **every** component
is nonzero:
$$v_j = p_C(j) \neq 0 \quad \text{for all } j \in \{1, \ldots, p-1\}.$$
Each $v_j$ can be chosen freely from $\mathbb{Z}_p^* = \{1, 2, \ldots, p-1\}$, g
iving $p-1$ independent choices. Therefore the number of invertible $(p-1)\times
(p-1)$ circulant matrices over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is

$$\boxed{(p-1)^{p-1}}.$$

**Verification ($p=3$, $n=2$).** The $2\times 2$ circulant matrices over $\mathb
b{Z}_3$ are $\begin{pmatrix} c_0 & c_1 \\ c_1 & c_0 \end{pmatrix}$ with eigenval
ues $c_0+c_1$ and $c_0-c_1$ (roots of $x^2-1$: $1$ and $2$). Invertible requires
 $c_0+c_1\neq 0$ and $c_0-c_1\neq 0$. Enumerating: $(c_0,c_1) \in \{(0,1),(0,2),
(1,0),(2,0)\}$, giving $4 = (3-1)^{3-1} = 2^2$. ✓
