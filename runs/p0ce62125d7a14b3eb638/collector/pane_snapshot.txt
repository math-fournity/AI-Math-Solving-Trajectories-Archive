# Solution

## Step 1: Simplify the integrand using a trigonometric identity

The key observation is that $\mathrm{sinc}(8t)\cos(8\pi t)$ collapses into a sin
gle sinc function. Since
$$\mathrm{sinc}(8t) = \frac{\sin(8\pi t)}{8\pi t},$$
we get
$$\mathrm{sinc}(8t)\cos(8\pi t) = \frac{\sin(8\pi t)\cos(8\pi t)}{8\pi t} = \fra
c{\tfrac{1}{2}\sin(16\pi t)}{8\pi t} = \frac{\sin(16\pi t)}{16\pi t} = \mathrm{s
inc}(16t),$$
using the double-angle identity $\sin(2\theta)=2\sin\theta\cos\theta$ with $\the
ta = 8\pi t$.

Therefore the integral reduces to
$$I = \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} \mathrm{sinc}(16t)\,\mathrm{sinc}^2(t)\,dt.$$

## Step 2: Fourier transform setup

Use the convention $\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(t)\,e^{-2\pi i\xi
 t}\,dt$, under which the standard pairs are:
- $\mathcal{F}\{\mathrm{rect}(t)\} = \mathrm{sinc}(\xi)$, where $\mathrm{rect}(t
)=\mathbf{1}_{[-1/2,\,1/2]}(t)$;
- By duality, $\mathcal{F}\{\mathrm{sinc}(t)\} = \mathrm{rect}(\xi)$;
- Scaling: $\mathcal{F}\{\mathrm{sinc}(at)\} = \tfrac{1}{|a|}\mathrm{rect}(\xi/a
)$.

Let $f(t)=\mathrm{sinc}(16t)$ and $g(t)=\mathrm{sinc}^2(t)$.

**Transform of $f$:**
$$\widehat{f}(\xi) = \frac{1}{16}\mathrm{rect}\!\left(\frac{\xi}{16}\right) = \b
egin{cases} \frac{1}{16}, & |\xi|\le 8,\\ 0, & |\xi|>8.\end{cases}$$

**Transform of $g$:** Since $\mathrm{sinc}^2(t) = \mathrm{sinc}(t)\cdot\mathrm{s
inc}(t)$, multiplication in time gives convolution in frequency:
$$\widehat{g}(\xi) = (\mathrm{rect}*\mathrm{rect})(\xi) = \mathrm{tri}(\xi) = \b
egin{cases} 1-|\xi|, & |\xi|\le 1,\\ 0, & |\xi|>1,\end{cases}$$
the triangle function (convolution of two unit rects supported on $[-1/2,1/2]$ y
ields a triangle on $[-1,1]$ peaking at $1$).

## Step 3: Apply Parseval's theorem

Both $f$ and $g$ are real and even, so their transforms are real and even, and P
arseval gives
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(t)\,g(t)\,dt = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \widehat{f}(\
xi)\,\widehat{g}(\xi)\,d\xi.$$

Thus
$$I = \int_{-8}^{8} \frac{1}{16}\,\mathrm{tri}(\xi)\,d\xi.$$

Since $\mathrm{tri}(\xi)=0$ for $|\xi|>1$, the integral collapses to $[-1,1]$:
$$I = \frac{1}{16}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-|\xi|)\,d\xi = \frac{1}{16}\cdot 2\int_{0}^{1}
(1-\xi)\,d\xi = \frac{1}{16}\cdot 2\cdot\frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{16}.$$

## Result

$$\boxed{\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{sinc}(8t)\,\mathrm{sinc}^2(t)\,\cos(8\p
i t)\,dt = \frac{1}{16}}$$
