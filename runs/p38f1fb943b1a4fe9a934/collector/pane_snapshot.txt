## Solution

**Example.** Let $M \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ be the ellipsoid of revolution (sphero
id)
$$M = \left\{(x,y,z) \in \mathbb{R}^3 : \frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{a^2} + \fra
c{z^2}{b^2} = 1\right\}, \qquad a,b > 0,\ a \neq b.$$
I claim $M$ is a closed Weingarten surface with $K>0$ that does not have constan
t curvature.

---

### 1. $M$ is closed

$M$ is the preimage of $\{1\}$ under the continuous function $F(x,y,z)=x^2/a^2+y
^2/a^2+z^2/b^2$, so it is closed in $\mathbb{R}^3$; it is bounded (contained in
the box $|x|,|y|\le a,\ |z|\le b$), hence compact. It is a smooth 2-manifold wit
hout boundary (the gradient of $F$ never vanishes on $M$). ✓

### 2. $K > 0$

Parametrize $M$ by
$$\mathbf{r}(\theta,\phi) = (a\sin\theta\cos\phi,\ a\sin\theta\sin\phi,\ b\cos\t
heta), \qquad \theta\in(0,\pi),\ \phi\in[0,2\pi).$$
This is a surface of revolution with profile curve $f(\theta)=a\sin\theta$, $g(\
theta)=b\cos\theta$. Set
$$w(\theta) := \sqrt{f'(\theta)^2+g'(\theta)^2} = \sqrt{a^2\cos^2\theta + b^2\si
n^2\theta} > 0.$$
The principal curvatures (with the outward normal) are the meridional and parall
el curvatures:
$$\kappa_\mu = \frac{ab}{w(\theta)^3}, \qquad \kappa_\pi = \frac{b}{a\,w(\theta)
}.$$
Both are strictly positive, so
$$K = \kappa_\mu\,\kappa_\pi = \frac{b^2}{w(\theta)^4} = \frac{b^2}{\bigl(a^2\co
s^2\theta + b^2\sin^2\theta\bigr)^2} > 0 \quad \text{everywhere.} \checkmark$$

(Equivalently: an ellipsoid is strictly convex, so both principal curvatures sha
re the same sign with respect to the outward normal, giving $K>0$.)

### 3. $M$ is Weingarten

Since $M$ is a surface of revolution, both principal curvatures depend on the si
ngle parameter $\theta$, so the pair $(\kappa_\mu,\kappa_\pi)$ traces a curve in
 the $(\kappa_1,\kappa_2)$-plane. We exhibit the relation explicitly. From
$$\kappa_\pi = \frac{b}{a\,w} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad w = \frac{b}{a\,\kappa_\
pi}.$$
Substituting into $\kappa_\mu = ab/w^3$:
$$\kappa_\mu = \frac{ab}{\bigl(b/(a\,\kappa_\pi)\bigr)^3} = \frac{ab\cdot a^3\ka
ppa_\pi^3}{b^3} = \frac{a^4}{b^2}\,\kappa_\pi^3.$$
Hence $M$ satisfies the non-trivial functional relation
$$\boxed{\;\kappa_\mu = \tfrac{a^4}{b^2}\,\kappa_\pi^3\;}$$
between its principal curvatures, which is precisely the Weingarten condition. ✓

### 4. $M$ does not have constant curvature

From the formula above:
$$K(\theta) = \frac{b^2}{\bigl(a^2\cos^2\theta + b^2\sin^2\theta\bigr)^2}.$$
- At the pole $\theta = 0$: $\;K = \dfrac{b^2}{a^4}$.
- At the equator $\theta = \pi/2$: $\;K = \dfrac{b^2}{b^4} = \dfrac{1}{b^2}$.

If $K$ were constant, we would need $b^2/a^4 = 1/b^2$, i.e. $b^4 = a^4$, i.e. $a
 = b$ — contradicting $a\neq b$. Therefore $K$ is not constant. ✓

### Conclusion

The non-spherical spheroid $M = \{x^2/a^2 + y^2/a^2 + z^2/b^2 = 1\}$ with $a\neq
 b$ is:
- **closed** (compact, no boundary),
- **Weingarten** ($\kappa_\mu = (a^4/b^2)\,\kappa_\pi^3$),
- **$K > 0$** ($K = b^2/w(\theta)^4 > 0$),
- **not of constant curvature** ($K$ takes distinct values at the poles and the
equator).

This gives the required example. $\blacksquare$
