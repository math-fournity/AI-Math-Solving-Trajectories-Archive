## Proof

Let $\alpha = x/\pi$ so that $\cos(nx) = \cos(\pi n\alpha)$. The series is $2\pi
$-periodic in $x$, so it suffices to work on $[0,1]$ in the $\alpha$ variable. W
rite $\|y\|$ for the distance from $y$ to the nearest integer.

**Key inequality.** For all $\theta$, $|\cos(\pi\theta)| \leq e^{-\pi^2\|\theta\
|^2/2}$. This follows because $f(\phi) = \log\cos(\pi\phi) + \pi^2\phi^2/2$ sati
sfies $f(0)=0$, $f'(0)=0$, and $f''(\phi) = -\pi^2\tan^2(\pi\phi) \leq 0$ on $[0
,1/2)$, so $f$ is concave with maximum $0$, giving $\log\cos(\pi\phi) \leq -\pi^
2\phi^2/2$ for $\phi \in [0,1/2]$. By symmetry and periodicity, $|\cos(\pi\theta
)| = \cos(\pi\|\theta\|) \leq e^{-\pi^2\|\theta\|^2/2}$.

Thus $|\cos(\pi n\alpha)|^{n^r} \leq \exp\!\left(-\frac{\pi^2}{2}\, n^r \|n\alph
a\|^2\right)$ for all $n, \alpha$.

### Divergence for $r \leq 2$

**Khintchine's Theorem.** For non-increasing $\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq
 0}$, the inequality $\|n\alpha\|<\psi(n)$ has infinitely many solutions for a.e
. $\alpha$ iff $\sum \psi(n)=\infty$.

**Case $r=2$:** Take $\psi(n)=1/n$. Since $\sum 1/n = \infty$, for a.e. $\alpha$
 there are infinitely many $n$ with $\|n\alpha\|<1/n$. For such $n$:
$$|\cos(\pi n\alpha)|^{n^2} \geq \cos(\pi/n)^{n^2} \geq \left(1-\frac{\pi^2}{2n^
2}\right)^{n^2} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} e^{-\pi^2/2} > 0.$$
So $|(\cos(nx))^{n^2}| \not\to 0$ for a.e. $x$, and the series diverges.

**Cases $r \leq 1$:** Take $\psi(n) = n^{-r/2}$ (for $r=1$: $n^{-1/2}$; for $r=0
$: $1$). Since $\sum n^{-r/2} = \infty$ for $r\leq 2$, Khintchine gives infinite
ly many $n$ with $\|n\alpha\|<n^{-r/2}$. Then $n^r\|n\alpha\|^2 < 1$, so $|\cos(
\pi n\alpha)|^{n^r} \geq e^{-\pi^2/2}>0$ infinitely often. Terms don't tend to $
0$; divergence. (For $r<0$, $n^r\to 0$ so $(\cos(nx))^{n^r}\to 1$ trivially; als
o divergent.)

### Convergence for $r = 3$

By Khintchine's theorem with $\psi(n) = n^{-5/4}$ (monotone, $\sum n^{-5/4}<\inf
ty$ since $5/4>1$): for a.e. $\alpha$, $\|n\alpha\| < n^{-5/4}$ only finitely of
ten. So there exists $N(\alpha)$ such that for all $n \geq N$:
$$\|n\alpha\| \geq n^{-5/4}.$$

Applying the key inequality with $r=3$:
$$|\cos(\pi n\alpha)|^{n^3} \leq \exp\!\left(-\frac{\pi^2}{2}\, n^3 \cdot n^{-5/
2}\right) = \exp\!\left(-\frac{\pi^2}{2}\, n^{1/2}\right).$$

Since $e^{-c\sqrt{n}} = o(n^{-k})$ for every $k>0$ (as $c\sqrt{n} \gg k\log n$),
 the series $\sum_{n\geq N} e^{-\pi^2\sqrt{n}/2}$ converges. Therefore:
$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty |\cos(nx)|^{n^3} < \infty \quad \text{for a.e. } x,$$
so $\sum_{n=0}^\infty (\cos(nx))^{n^3}$ converges (absolutely) for a.e. $x$.

---

### Conclusion

The series diverges a.e. for $r \leq 2$ and converges a.e. for $r = 3$. The smal
lest integer value of $r$ for which the series converges for almost every $x$ is
:

$$\boxed{r = 3}$$
