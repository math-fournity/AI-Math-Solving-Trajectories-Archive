## Proof

Let the right triangle have legs $a, b$ and hypotenuse $c$, all positive integer
s. Its inradius is
$$r = \frac{a+b-c}{2}.$$

**Primitive parametrization.** Every right triangle with positive integer sides
is uniquely expressible as a scalar multiple $d\ge 1$ of a primitive Pythagorean
 triple:
$$a = d(m^2-k^2),\quad b = 2dmk,\quad c = d(m^2+k^2),$$
where $m>k>0$, $\gcd(m,k)=1$, and $m-k$ is odd. The inradius of such a triangle
is
$$r = \frac{a+b-c}{2} = \frac{d(m^2-k^2+2mk-m^2-k^2)}{2} = d\,k(m-k).$$

**Reparametrize.** Set $u = k$ and $v = m-k$, so $m = u+v$ with $u,v>0$. The con
ditions become $\gcd(u,v)=1$ and $v$ odd, and
$$r = d\,u\,v.$$

Thus nice triangles are in bijection with triples $(d,u,v)$ of positive integers
 satisfying
$$d\,u\,v = n,\qquad \gcd(u,v)=1,\qquad v\text{ odd}.$$

**Apply to $n = p_1p_2\cdots p_{2017}$.** Since every $p_i$ is an odd prime, $n$
 is odd and squarefree. Hence any divisor of $n$ is odd, so the condition "$v$ o
dd" is automatic. Because $n$ is squarefree, each prime $p_i$ must be assigned e
ntirely to exactly one of $d$, $u$, $v$. Once each prime is placed in a single b
ox, $u$ and $v$ share no prime factor, so $\gcd(u,v)=1$ holds automatically as w
ell.

Therefore the constraint $\gcd(u,v)=1$ and $v$ odd impose **no restriction** bey
ond $d\,u\,v = n$. The count of admissible triples equals the number of ways to
assign each of the $2017$ distinct primes to one of three boxes $(d, u, v)$, nam
ely
$$3^{2017}.$$

**Bijectivity check.** The standard parametrization $(d,m,k)\mapsto$ triangle is
 a bijection onto integer-sided right triangles (uniqueness follows since one le
g $d(m^2-k^2)$ is odd and the other $2dmk$ is even, so the two legs are distingu
ishable). Distinct assignments of primes yield distinct $(d,u,v)$, hence distinc
t triangles, and every nice triangle arises. The small case $n=p$ (a single odd
prime) gives exactly $3$ triangles, confirming $3^1$.

The number of nice triangles is $\boxed{3^{2017}}$.
