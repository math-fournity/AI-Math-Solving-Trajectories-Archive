## Answer: **Yes**

### Proof

We show that if a knot $K$ admits only trivial (constant) colourings for every q
uandle, then $K$ must be the unknot.

**Key fact (Joyce 1982, Matveev 1982).** The *fundamental quandle* $Q(K)$ is a c
omplete invariant of oriented knots: two oriented knots $K_1, K_2$ are equivalen
t if and only if $Q(K_1) \cong Q(K_2)$ as quandles. In particular, $K$ is the un
knot if and only if $Q(K)$ is the free quandle on one generator (the trivial one
-element quandle, up to the relevant structure).

**Step 1: The fundamental quandle detects the unknot.** The fundamental quandle
of the unknot is the one-element (trivial) quandle. For any non-trivial knot $K$
, the fundamental quandle $Q(K)$ is non-trivial — in fact it is infinite. This f
ollows from the Wirtinger presentation: $Q(K)$ is presented with one generator p
er arc and relations $x_i \triangleright x_j = x_k$ at each crossing. The abelia
nization of $Q(K)$ is $H_1(\widetilde{X}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ (the infinite cyclic
 cover), so $Q(K)$ surjects onto $\mathbb{Z}$ and hence is infinite whenever $K$
 is non-trivial. (More directly: the abelianization map $Q(K) \to \mathbb{Z}$ se
nding every Wirtinger generator to $1$ is a quandle homomorphism onto the trivia
l-action quandle $\mathbb{Z}$, which is non-trivial as a set whenever $\mathbb{Z
}$ has more than one element — which it does.)

**Step 2: A non-trivial fundamental quandle yields a non-trivial colouring.** Su
ppose $K$ is not the unknot. Then $Q(K)$ has at least two distinct elements. The
 identity map
$$\mathrm{id}: Q(K) \to Q(K)$$
is a quandle homomorphism (trivially), and since $Q(K)$ has at least two element
s, this is a **non-constant** colouring of the knot diagram by the quandle $Q(K)
$. By the universal property of the fundamental quandle, every colouring of $K$
by any quandle $X$ corresponds to a quandle homomorphism $Q(K) \to X$; the ident
ity map corresponds to the "universal" colouring where each arc of the diagram i
s coloured by its own Wirtinger generator. Since $K$ is non-trivial, at least tw
o arcs receive distinct colours, so this is a non-trivial colouring.

**Step 3: Contrapositive.** We have shown: if $K$ is not the unknot, then there
exists a quandle (namely $Q(K)$ itself) and a non-trivial colouring of $K$ by th
at quandle. Taking the contrapositive:

> If $K$ admits only trivial colourings for **every** quandle, then $K$ is the u
nknot.

**Conclusion.** The hypothesis that $K$ admits only trivial colourings with any
quandle forces $Q(K)$ to be trivial (one element), which by the completeness of
the fundamental quandle invariant means $K$ is the unknot. $\blacksquare$
