## Proof

**Statement:** The statement is **true**: if $A$, $B$, $C$ are finitely generate
d modules over a PID $F$ and $A \oplus B \cong A \oplus C$, then $B \cong C$.

**Key tool — Structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a PID.** Ever
y finitely generated module $M$ over a PID $F$ decomposes as
$$
M \;\cong\; F^r \;\oplus\; \bigoplus_{i=1}^{s} F/(d_i),
$$
where $r \geq 0$ is the **free rank**, $d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_s$ are n
on-unit, non-zero elements of $F$ (the **invariant factors**), and this decompos
ition is *unique up to isomorphism*. Equivalently, factoring each $d_i$ into pri
me powers, $M$ is determined up to isomorphism by:

- the free rank $r$, and
- for each prime $p \in F$ and each exponent $n \geq 1$, the multiplicity $\mu_{
p,n}(M)$ of cyclic summands isomorphic to $F/(p^n)$.

These data — call them collectively the **invariant list** $\mathrm{Inv}(M)$ — f
orm a complete set of isomorphism invariants: $M \cong M'$ if and only if $\math
rm{Inv}(M) = \mathrm{Inv}(M')$.

**Additivity of invariants under direct sum.** Direct sum corresponds to adding
invariants componentwise. Concretely, decomposing
$$
M \cong F^{r_M} \oplus \bigoplus_{p,n} \bigl(F/(p^n)\bigr)^{\mu_{p,n}(M)},
$$
we have, for the direct sum $M \oplus N$:
- $r_{M \oplus N} = r_M + r_N$,
- $\mu_{p,n}(M \oplus N) = \mu_{p,n}(M) + \mu_{p,n}(N)$ for every $(p, n)$.

This is immediate: the free parts add, and the cyclic torsion summands of the sa
me type $F/(p^n)$ combine, with the invariant-factor or elementary-divisor form
recovered by re-collecting the elementary divisors (the elementary-divisor form
is itself a complete invariant, so no reordering issue arises).

**Applying the hypothesis.** Suppose $A \oplus B \cong A \oplus C$. Then their i
nvariant lists coincide:
$$
\mathrm{Inv}(A \oplus B) = \mathrm{Inv}(A \oplus C).
$$
By additivity, for every invariant $\iota$ (the free rank, and each multiplicity
 $\mu_{p,n}$),
$$
\iota(A) + \iota(B) \;=\; \iota(A) + \iota(C).
$$
Each $\iota(\cdot)$ is a **non-negative integer**, so we may cancel $\iota(A)$ f
rom both sides (ordinary subtraction in $\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$, which is valid si
nce both sides are equal and $\iota(A)$ is the same term on each side):
$$
\iota(B) \;=\; \iota(C) \quad \text{for every invariant } \iota.
$$
Therefore $\mathrm{Inv}(B) = \mathrm{Inv}(C)$, and by completeness of the invari
ant list,
$$
B \;\cong\; C. \qquad \blacksquare
$$

**Remark on why the PID / finite-generation hypotheses are essential.** The canc
ellation above works because the invariants take values in $\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$
, where addition is cancellative. The structure theorem supplying such invariant
s requires both that $F$ be a PID and that the modules be finitely generated. Wi
thout finite generation, one can construct non-cancellative examples even over $
\mathbb{Z}$: for instance, taking $A = \mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}}$ (countable-rank
free) and suitable $B, C$ yields $A \oplus B \cong A \oplus C$ with $B \not\cong
 C$. Over non-PID rings, cancellation for finitely generated modules can also fa
il. The hypotheses as stated are exactly what makes the argument go through.
