## Proof

We determine the radius of convergence $R$ of $p(x) = \sum_{n} b_n x^n$, where $
R = 1/\limsup_{n\to\infty} |b_n|^{1/n}$.

**Step 1: $R \geq 1$.**

The series $\sum b_n$ is conditionally convergent, hence convergent. This is pre
cisely $p(1) = \sum b_n 1^n$, so the power series converges at $x = 1$. A power
series converges absolutely at every point strictly inside its disk of convergen
ce $|x| < R$, and diverges at every point strictly outside $|x| > R$. Since $x =
 1$ is a point of convergence, we must have $1 \leq R$ (if $R < 1$, then $x = 1$
 would lie outside the disk and the series would diverge there, contradicting co
nvergence).

**Step 2: $R \leq 1$.**

The series $\sum b_n$ is not absolutely convergent, i.e., $\sum |b_n|$ diverges.
 Now $\sum |b_n| = \sum |b_n|\, 1^n$ is the absolute-value series evaluated at $
x = 1$. If $R > 1$, then $x = 1$ would lie strictly inside the disk of convergen
ce $|x| < R$, where the power series converges absolutely — meaning $\sum |b_n|
1^n = \sum |b_n|$ would converge. This contradicts the hypothesis that $\sum b_n
$ is not absolutely convergent. Hence $R \leq 1$.

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 1 and 2, $1 \leq R \leq 1$, so

$$R = 1.$$

Geometrically, this is consistent: conditional (non-absolute) convergence at $x
= 1$ forces $x = 1$ to lie exactly on the boundary of the disk of convergence, s
ince inside the disk convergence is always absolute and outside the series diver
ges. The only point consistent with "converges but not absolutely" is the bounda
ry, giving $R = 1$.
