## Proof

**Question.** Does Frobenius' theorem guarantee the existence of a submanifold t
angent to two vector fields $A_1$ and $A_2$ if their Lie bracket $[A_1, A_2]$ is
 zero?

**Answer: Yes**, provided $A_1$ and $A_2$ are smooth vector fields that are line
arly independent on an open set (so that they span a rank‑2 distribution). The v
anishing bracket is exactly the involutivity condition that Frobenius' theorem r
equires.

---

### Setup and definitions

Let $M$ be a smooth $n$‑manifold and let $A_1, A_2 \in \mathfrak{X}(M)$ be smoot
h vector fields. Assume that on an open set $U \subseteq M$ the two fields are *
*linearly independent** at every point, so that
$$
\mathcal{D}_p := \operatorname{span}\{A_1(p),\, A_2(p)\} \subseteq T_p M
$$
defines a smooth rank‑2 distribution $\mathcal{D}$ on $U$.

A submanifold $S \subseteq U$ is **tangent to $\mathcal{D$** (or an *integral su
bmanifold* of $\mathcal{D}$) if $T_p S = \mathcal{D}_p$ for every $p \in S$.

### Frobenius' theorem (integrability form)

A distribution $\mathcal{D}$ is **integrable** — i.e., through every point $p \i
n U$ there passes a maximal integral submanifold of $\mathcal{D}$ — if and only
if $\mathcal{D}$ is **involutive**: for every pair of vector fields $X, Y$ with
values in $\mathcal{D}$, the Lie bracket $[X, Y]$ also takes values in $\mathcal
{D}$.

### Involutivity from a vanishing bracket

The distribution $\mathcal{D} = \operatorname{span}\{A_1, A_2\}$ is involutive i
f and only if the brackets of the defining fields lie back in $\mathcal{D}$:
$$
[A_1, A_2] \in \Gamma(\mathcal{D}).
$$
Since $\mathcal{D}$ is spanned by $A_1, A_2$, this means there must exist smooth
 functions $f, g$ on $U$ with
$$
[A_1, A_2] = f A_1 + g A_2.
$$

The hypothesis of the problem is stronger than this: it states
$$
[A_1, A_2] = 0.
$$
The zero vector field lies in *every* subspace, so in particular $[A_1, A_2] = 0
 \in \Gamma(\mathcal{D})$ (take $f = g = 0$). Hence $\mathcal{D}$ is involutive.

### Applying Frobenius

Since $\mathcal{D}$ is a smooth, constant‑rank, involutive distribution on $U$,
Frobenius' theorem applies directly: through each point $p \in U$ there exists a
 maximal connected integral submanifold $S_p$ of $\mathcal{D}$, with
$$
T_q S_p = \mathcal{D}_q = \operatorname{span}\{A_1(q), A_2(q)\} \quad \text{for
all } q \in S_p.
$$
In particular $S_p$ is tangent to both $A_1$ and $A_2$ at every one of its point
s, and $\dim S_p = 2$.

### Why the hypotheses matter

The conclusion requires two things beyond the vanishing bracket:

1. **Linear independence.** If $A_1, A_2$ become linearly dependent somewhere, t
he rank of $\mathcal{D}$ drops and Frobenius (in its constant‑rank form) no long
er applies at those points. On the open set where they are independent, the argu
ment above is valid.

2. **Smoothness.** Frobenius' theorem is a theorem about *smooth* (at least $C^2
$) distributions and vector fields; the bracket must be well defined, which requ
ires sufficient differentiability.

With these standard hypotheses satisfied, the vanishing of $[A_1, A_2]$ is a suf
ficient — indeed stronger than necessary — condition for involutivity, and Frobe
nius' theorem guarantees the existence of the tangent submanifold.

### Conclusion

Yes. When $A_1$ and $A_2$ are smooth, linearly independent vector fields whose L
ie bracket vanishes, the rank‑2 distribution they span is involutive, and Froben
ius' theorem guarantees that through every point there passes a 2‑dimensional in
tegral submanifold tangent to both $A_1$ and $A_2$.
