## Solution

We claim that the unique solution is \(f(x)=x\), for every real number \(t\).

**Verification.** With \(f(x)=x\): LHS \(= x+t+f(y)=x+t+y\), RHS \(= f(f(x))+f(t
)+y = x+t+y\). ✓

---

### Step 1 — \(f\) is bijective

**Surjective.** Fix \(x\). As \(y\) ranges over \(\mathbb R\), the RHS \(f(f(x))
+f(t)+y\) ranges over all of \(\mathbb R\). The LHS \(f(x+t+f(y))\) lies in \(\o
peratorname{range}(f)\). Hence \(\operatorname{range}(f)=\mathbb R\).

**Injective.** Suppose \(f(y_1)=f(y_2)\). Then for every \(x\),
\[
f(x+t+f(y_1))=f(x+t+f(y_2)),
\]
so the RHSs are equal: \(f(f(x))+f(t)+y_1=f(f(x))+f(t)+y_2\), giving \(y_1=y_2\)
.

---

### Step 2 — Two key substitutions

Let \(a:=f(0)\).

* \(x=0\): \(\quad f(t+f(y))=f(f(0))+f(t)+y=f(a)+f(t)+y.\) \hfill (1)
* \(y=0\): \(\quad f(x+t+a)=f(f(x))+f(t).\)\hfill (2)

Substituting (2) into the original equation eliminates \(f(f(x))+f(t)\):
\[
f(x+t+f(y))=f(x+t+a)+y.
\]
Writing \(u=x+t\):
\[
f(u+f(y))=f(u+a)+y. \tag{12}
\]
Setting \(u=0\):
\[
f(f(y))=f(a)+y. \tag{13}
\]

---

### Step 3 — \(f\) is affine-additive

From (13), \(y=f(f(y))-f(a)\). Insert into (12):
\[
f(u+f(y))=f(u+a)+f(f(y))-f(a).
\]
Since \(f\) is surjective, \(w:=f(y)\) ranges over all of \(\mathbb R\), and \(f
(f(y))=f(w)\):
\[
f(u+w)=f(u+a)+f(w)-f(a),\qquad \forall u,w\in\mathbb R. \tag{19}
\]

Define \(h(x):=f(x)-a\), so \(h(0)=0\). Rewriting (19):
\[
h(u+w)=h(u+a)+h(w). \tag{21}
\]
Setting \(w=0\): \(h(u)=h(u+a)\). Hence (21) becomes
\[
h(u+w)=h(u)+h(w),
\]
so **\(h\) is additive** (Cauchy).

---

### Step 4 — \(h\) is an involution and \(a=0\)

Rewrite (13) with \(f=h+a\) and additivity:
\[
f(f(y))=h(h(y)+a)+a=h(h(y))+h(a)+a,
\]
while the RHS of (13) is \(f(a)+y=h(a)+a+y\). Thus
\[
h(h(y))=y,\qquad\forall y. \tag{26}
\]
So \(h\circ h=\mathrm{id}\).

From \(h(u+a)=h(u)\) with \(u=0\): \(h(a)=h(0)=0\). Then by (26) with \(y=a\): \
(a=h(h(a))=h(0)=0\).

---

### Step 5 — \(f=\mathrm{id}\)

With \(a=0\), we have \(f=h\), an additive involution. Substitute directly into
the original equation:
\[
\text{LHS}=f(x+t+f(y))=h(x+t+h(y))=h(x)+h(t)+h(h(y))=h(x)+h(t)+y,
\]
\[
\text{RHS}=f(f(x))+f(t)+y=h(h(x))+h(t)+y=x+h(t)+y.
\]
Equating: \(h(x)=x\) for all \(x\). Hence \(h=\mathrm{id}\), and therefore
\[
\boxed{f(x)=x}.
\]

This holds for every real number \(t\), and it is the unique solution.
