## Solution: Exterior Dirichlet Problem for Laplace's Equation

### Setup and boundedness condition

We solve
$$\nabla^2 u = 0, \quad r > a, \qquad u(a,\theta) = f(\theta),$$
with the physical requirement that $u$ remain **bounded as $r \to \infty$** (the
 natural exterior analogue of the regularity-at-origin condition for the interio
r problem). Without a condition at infinity the exterior problem is not uniquely
 determined, since $\ln r$ and $r^n$ are also harmonic.

### Separation of variables in polar coordinates

With $u(r,\theta) = R(r)\Theta(\theta)$, Laplace's equation $u_{rr} + \tfrac{1}{
r}u_r + \tfrac{1}{r^2}u_{\theta\theta} = 0$ separates into
$$\Theta'' + n^2\Theta = 0, \qquad r^2 R'' + rR' - n^2 R = 0,\qquad n = 0,1,2,\d
ots$$

The angular solutions are $\{1,\cos n\theta,\sin n\theta\}$. The radial equation
 gives:
- $n = 0$: $R = C_1 + C_2 \ln r$,
- $n \ge 1$: $R = C_1 r^n + C_2 r^{-n}$.

**Boundedness at $r=\infty$** forces us to discard $\ln r$ (for $n=0$) and $r^n$
 (for $n\ge 1$). The admissible radial factors are therefore $1$ and $r^{-n}$. I
t is conventional to write them as $(a/r)^n$ so that the boundary $r=a$ is reach
ed at unit factor. The general bounded exterior solution is
$$u(r,\theta) = \frac{A_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{a}{r}\right)^{\!n
}\bigl[A_n\cos n\theta + B_n\sin n\theta\bigr], \qquad r > a.$$

### Imposing the boundary condition

Setting $r = a$ gives
$$f(\theta) = \frac{A_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl[A_n\cos n\theta + B_n\sin
 n\theta\bigr],$$
so the coefficients are the Fourier coefficients of $f$:
$$A_n = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(\varphi)\cos n\varphi\,d\varphi,\qquad B_n
= \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(\varphi)\sin n\varphi\,d\varphi,\qquad n\ge 0$$
(with $B_0 = 0$). Substituting back and exchanging sum and integral (justified f
or $r > a$ by absolute convergence, since $a/r < 1$):
$$u(r,\theta) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(\varphi)\left[1 + 2\sum_{n=1}^{\in
fty}\left(\frac{a}{r}\right)^{\!n}\cos n(\theta-\varphi)\right]d\varphi.$$

### Summing the series (Poisson kernel)

Let $\rho = a/r \in (0,1)$ and $\alpha = \theta - \varphi$. The standard identit
y, obtained from the real part of the geometric series $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\rho
 e^{i\alpha})^n = \frac{1}{1-\rho e^{i\alpha}}$, is
$$1 + 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rho^n\cos n\alpha = \frac{1-\rho^2}{1 - 2\rho\cos\alp
ha + \rho^2}.$$

Substituting $\rho = a/r$ and $\alpha = \theta-\varphi$, and clearing denominato
rs by multiplying numerator and denominator by $r^2$:
$$1 + 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{a}{r}\right)^{\!n}\cos n(\theta-\varphi) =
 \frac{r^2 - a^2}{r^2 - 2ar\cos(\theta-\varphi) + a^2}.$$

### The exterior Poisson integral formula

$$\boxed{\;u(r,\theta) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(\varphi)\,\frac{r^2 - a^2
}{\,r^2 - 2ar\cos(\theta-\varphi) + a^2\,}\,d\varphi,\qquad r > a.\;}$$

### Verification

1. **Harmonicity.** For fixed $\varphi$, the kernel $P_{\mathrm{ext}}(r,\theta;\
varphi) = \dfrac{r^2-a^2}{r^2-2ar\cos(\theta-\varphi)+a^2}$ is harmonic in $(r,\
theta)$ for $r>a$ (it is the real part of $\dfrac{r+ae^{i(\theta-\varphi)}}{r-ae
^{i(\theta-\varphi)}}$ up to an additive constant, i.e. a bounded analytic funct
ion of $z=re^{i\theta}$ in $|z|>a$). Integrating against $f$ preserves harmonici
ty.

2. **Boundary condition.** As $r \to a^+$, the kernel tends to the periodic delt
a: for $\varphi \ne \theta$ it tends to $0$, while $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}P
_{\mathrm{ext}}\,d\varphi = 1$ (set $\rho=a/r$ in the summed identity with $f\eq
uiv 1$, giving $u\equiv A_0/2 = 1$). Hence $u(r,\theta)\to f(\theta)$ as $r\to a
^+$.

3. **Boundedness at infinity.** As $r\to\infty$, $P_{\mathrm{ext}} \sim \dfrac{r
^2}{r^2} = 1$ pointwise but more precisely $P_{\mathrm{ext}} = 1 + O(a/r)$, so $
u(r,\theta) \to \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f(\varphi)\,d\varphi = A_0/2$, a fini
te constant. Thus $u$ is bounded at infinity, consistent with the discarded $\ln
 r$ and $r^n$ modes. (If one additionally imposes $u\to 0$ at infinity, the $n=0
$ constant term is removed and the formula is replaced by the same integral minu
s $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f\,d\varphi$; equivalently one keeps only the $n\g
e 1$ modes.)

4. **Relation to the interior formula.** The interior Poisson formula is $u_{\ma
thrm{int}}(r,\theta)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f(\varphi)\frac{a^2-r^2}{a^2-2ar
\cos(\theta-\varphi)+r^2}\,d\varphi$ for $r<a$. The exterior formula is obtained
 from it by the **inversion (Kelvin transform) $r \mapsto a^2/r$**, which swaps
$r^2-a^2 \leftrightarrow a^2-r^2$ and maps the exterior $r>a$ to the interior. T
his confirms the formula is the correct exterior analogue.
