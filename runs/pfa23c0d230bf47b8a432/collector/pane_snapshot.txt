**Setup and parity observation.** Let the sequence be $a_1, a_2, \ldots$ where $
a_{n+1} = a_n + D(a_n)$ and $D(n)$ denotes the largest digit of $n$. Since $a_{n
+1} = a_n + D(a_n)$:
- If $a_n$ is odd and $D(a_n)$ is even, then $a_{n+1}$ is odd.
- If $a_n$ is odd and $D(a_n)$ is odd, then $a_{n+1}$ is even.
- If $a_n$ is even and $D(a_n)$ is even, then $a_{n+1}$ is even.
- If $a_n$ is even and $D(a_n)$ is odd, then $a_{n+1}$ is odd.

So a run of consecutive odd terms $a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_k$ (with $a_{k+1}$ even)
has: $D(a_1), \ldots, D(a_{k-1})$ all even, and $D(a_k)$ odd.

**Key Lemma (the largest digit is constant throughout the run).** *In any run of
 consecutive odd terms $a_1, \ldots, a_k$, we have $D(a_1) = D(a_2) = \cdots = D
(a_{k-1}) =: d$, where $d \in \{2,4,6,8\}$.*

*Proof.* Consider $a_i$ with $D(a_i) = d_i$ (even), so $a_{i+1} = a_i + d_i$. Wh

- No digit equal to $d_i$ may receive a carry, since it would become $d_i + 1$,

Under these conditions, every digit of $a_{i+1}$ is $\le d_i$ (non-units digits
were $\le d_i$ and only increase to $\le d_i$; units $\le d_i$), and at least on

d is a largest digit of a positive integer, $d \in \{2,4,6,8\}$. $\square$
