**Claim:** The only possible function is $g \equiv 2$.

**Step 1: Each point of $L$ is a removable singularity.**

By hypothesis $g$ is bounded on $\mathbb{C} \setminus L$, say $|g(z)| \le M$ for

<r\}$ (choosing $r<1$ so the disk avoids all other points of $L$) we have $|g(z)
| \le M$. By Riemann's theorem on removable singularities, a holomorphic functio
n bounded in a punctured neighborhood of an isolated singularity extends holomor
phically across that singularity. Hence $n$ is removable.

**Step 2: $g$ extends to an entire function.**

Since every point of $L$ is removable, $g$ extends to a holomorphic function $\t
ilde g$ on all of $\mathbb{C}$. The extension agrees with $g$ on $\mathbb{C}\set
minus L$ and is defined at each $n\in L$ by the removable-singularity limit. Mor
eover $\tilde g$ is bounded: on $\mathbb{C}\setminus L$ we have $|\tilde g|=|g|\
le M$, and at each $n\in L$ the value $\tilde g(n)=\lim_{z\to n}g(z)$ satisfies
$|\tilde g(n)|\le M$ by continuity of the modulus. Thus $\tilde g$ is a bounded
entire function.

**Step 3: Apply Liouville's theorem.**

A bounded entire function is constant. So $\tilde g \equiv c$ for some constant
$c$. Since $0 \in \mathbb{C}\setminus L$ and $g(0)=2$, we have $c = \tilde g(0)
= g(0) = 2$.

**Conclusion:** $g(z) = 2$ for all $z \in \mathbb{C}\setminus L$.

$$\boxed{g \equiv 2}$$
