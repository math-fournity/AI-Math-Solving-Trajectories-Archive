# Solution

## Problem Analysis

We need to find the largest 12-digit prime $n$, then find all integers $j < n$ s
uch that every integer from $1$ to $n-1$ is a power of $j$ modulo $n$, and compu
te their product modulo $n$.

The integers $j$ with the property that $\{j^1, j^2, \ldots, j^{n-1}\} = \{1, 2,
 \ldots, n-1\} \pmod{n}$ are exactly the **primitive roots** modulo $n$.

Since $n$ is prime, primitive roots exist modulo $n$.

## Key Theorem

**Claim:** For any prime $p > 3$, the product of all primitive roots modulo $p$
is congruent to $1 \pmod{p}$.

**Proof:**

Let $g$ be a fixed primitive root modulo $p$. Every primitive root has the form
$g^a$ where $1 \le a \le p-1$ and $\gcd(a, p-1) = 1$. The number of such exponen
ts is $\varphi(p-1)$.

The product of all primitive roots is:
$$\prod_{\substack{1 \le a \le p-1 \\ \gcd(a,p-1)=1}} g^a = g^{S}$$
where $S = \sum_{\substack{1 \le a \le p-1 \\ \gcd(a,p-1)=1}} a$.

**Computing $S$:** Let $m = p - 1$. We sum all $a$ with $1 \le a \le m$ and $\gc
d(a, m) = 1$.

For each such $a$, since $\gcd(a, m) = 1$, we also have $\gcd(m - a, m) = 1$. Th
e elements coprime to $m$ pair up as $(a, m-a)$, each pair summing to $m$.

- This pairing has no fixed point (no $a$ with $a = m - a$, i.e., $2a = m$) when
 $m > 2$: if $m$ is odd, $2a = m$ has no integer solution; if $m$ is even, $a =
m/2$ satisfies $\gcd(m/2, m) = m/2 = 1$ only when $m = 2$.

Since $p$ is a 12-digit prime, $p \ge 10^{11}$, so $m = p - 1 > 2$, and the pair
ing is perfect. There are $\varphi(m)$ elements forming $\varphi(m)/2$ pairs, gi
ving:

$$S = m \cdot \frac{\varphi(m)}{2} = (p-1) \cdot \frac{\varphi(p-1)}{2}$$

**Evaluating the product:**

$$g^S = g^{(p-1) \cdot \varphi(p-1)/2} = \left(g^{p-1}\right)^{\varphi(p-1)/2}$$

By Fermat's little theorem, $g^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$, so:

$$g^S \equiv 1^{\varphi(p-1)/2} = 1 \pmod{p}$$

This requires $\varphi(p-1)/2$ to be an integer, i.e., $\varphi(p-1)$ is even. F
or $m \ge 3$, $\varphi(m)$ is always even. Since $p - 1 \ge 10^{11} - 1 \ge 3$,
this holds.

## Verification with small primes

- **$p = 7$:** Primitive roots are $3, 5$. Product $= 15 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$. ✓
- **$p = 11$:** Primitive roots are $2, 6, 7, 8$. Product $= 672 \equiv 1 \pmod{
11}$. ✓

## Conclusion

The largest 12-digit prime $n$ is certainly greater than $3$, so the theorem app
lies. The product of all primitive roots modulo $n$ is:

$$\boxed{1}$$
