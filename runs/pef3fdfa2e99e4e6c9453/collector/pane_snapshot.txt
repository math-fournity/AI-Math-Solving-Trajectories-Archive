# Transverse Vibrations of a Hinged Rod under a Concentrated Harmonic Force

## Problem Setup

Consider a rod (Euler–Bernoulli beam) of length $l$, flexural rigidity $EI$, mas
s density $\rho$, and cross-sectional area $S$. The ends at $x=0$ and $x=l$ are
hinged. A concentrated transverse force $P(t) = P_0 \sin\omega t$ is applied at
the point $x=x_0$, starting at $t=0$. The medium offers no resistance (no dampin
g).

**Governing equation** (for $t>0$, $0<x<l$):

$$EI\,\frac{\partial^4 w}{\partial x^4} + \rho S\,\frac{\partial^2 w}{\partial t
^2} = P_0\sin\omega t\;\delta(x-x_0)$$

**Boundary conditions** (hinged ends — zero displacement and zero bending moment
):

$$w(0,t)=0,\quad w''(0,t)=0,\quad w(l,t)=0,\quad w''(l,t)=0$$

**Initial conditions** (rod initially at rest):

$$w(x,0)=0,\quad \dot w(x,0)=0$$

## Eigenfunction Expansion

The eigenfunctions of the hinged–hinged beam satisfying the boundary conditions
are

$$\varphi_n(x)=\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l},\qquad n=1,2,3,\dots$$

with eigenvalues $\lambda_n = (n\pi/l)^2$ and **natural frequencies**

$$\omega_n = \left(\frac{n\pi}{l}\right)^2\sqrt{\frac{EI}{\rho S}}.$$

These eigenfunctions are orthogonal on $[0,l]$:

$$\int_0^l \sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}\sin\frac{m\pi x}{l}\,dx = \frac{l}{2}\,\delta_{n
m}.$$

We expand the solution in a modal series:

$$w(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} q_n(t)\,\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}.$$

## Deriving the Modal Equations

Substituting the expansion into the governing equation:

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big[ EI\Big(\frac{n\pi}{l}\Big)^4 q_n + \rho S\,\ddot q_n
\Big]\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l} = P_0\sin\omega t\;\delta(x-x_0).$$

Multiply both sides by $\sin(m\pi x/l)$ and integrate over $[0,l]$. Using orthog
onality on the left and the sifting property of the delta function on the right:

$$\frac{l}{2}\Big[EI\Big(\frac{m\pi}{l}\Big)^4 q_m + \rho S\,\ddot q_m\Big] = P_
0\sin\omega t\;\sin\frac{m\pi x_0}{l}.$$

Dividing through and using $\omega_m^2 = EI(m\pi/l)^4/(\rho S)$:

$$\boxed{\ddot q_m + \omega_m^2\, q_m = \frac{2P_0}{\rho S\, l}\sin\frac{m\pi x_
0}{l}\;\sin\omega t,\qquad q_m(0)=0,\;\dot q_m(0)=0.}$$

## Solving the Modal ODE

Define the modal forcing amplitude $F_m = \dfrac{2P_0}{\rho S\, l}\sin\dfrac{m\p
i x_0}{l}$.

### Non-resonant case ($\omega \neq \omega_m$)

The solution of $\ddot q_m + \omega_m^2 q_m = F_m\sin\omega t$ with zero initial
 conditions is the sum of a particular solution and the homogeneous solution cho
sen to satisfy $q_m(0)=0$, $\dot q_m(0)=0$:

$$q_m(t) = \frac{F_m}{\omega_m^2 - \omega^2}\left[\sin\omega t - \frac{\omega}{\
omega_m}\sin\omega_m t\right].$$

**Verification of initial conditions:** At $t=0$: $q_m(0) = \frac{F_m}{\omega_m^
2-\omega^2}[0 - 0] = 0$. ✓

$\dot q_m(t) = \frac{F_m}{\omega_m^2-\omega^2}\left[\omega\cos\omega t - \omega\
cos\omega_m t\right]$, so $\dot q_m(0) = \frac{F_m}{\omega_m^2-\omega^2}[\omega
- \omega] = 0$. ✓

### Resonant case ($\omega = \omega_k$ for some mode $k$)

When the forcing frequency coincides with a natural frequency, we take the limit
 $\omega\to\omega_k$ (or solve directly). The resonant modal response grows line
arly in time:

$$q_k(t) = \frac{F_k}{2\omega_k^2}\Big[\sin\omega_k t - \omega_k\, t\cos\omega_k
 t\Big].$$

**Derivation:** With $\omega = \omega_k$, the equation is $\ddot q_k + \omega_k^
2 q_k = F_k \sin\omega_k t$. The particular solution is $q_k^{(p)} = -\frac{F_k}
{2\omega_k}t\cos\omega_k t$ (obtained by the method of undetermined coefficients
 / variation of parameters, since $\sin\omega_k t$ is a solution of the homogene
ous equation). Adding the homogeneous solution $A\sin\omega_k t + B\cos\omega_k
t$ and applying $q_k(0)=0$, $\dot q_k(0)=0$:

- $q_k(0) = B = 0 \Rightarrow B=0$.
- $\dot q_k(0) = A\omega_k - \frac{F_k}{2\omega_k}[\cos 0 - \omega_k\cdot 0\cdot
\sin 0] = A\omega_k - \frac{F_k}{2\omega_k} = 0 \Rightarrow A = \frac{F_k}{2\ome
ga_k^2}$.

Hence $q_k(t) = \frac{F_k}{2\omega_k^2}\sin\omega_k t - \frac{F_k}{2\omega_k}t\c
os\omega_k t = \frac{F_k}{2\omega_k^2}[\sin\omega_k t - \omega_k t\cos\omega_k t
]$. ✓

## Complete Solution

### General (non-resonant) solution

$$\boxed{w(x,t) = \frac{2P_0}{\rho S\, l}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin\dfrac{n\p
i x_0}{l}\;\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{l}}{\omega_n^2 - \omega^2}\left[\sin\omega t - \fr
ac{\omega}{\omega_n}\sin\omega_n t\right]}$$

where $\omega_n = \left(\dfrac{n\pi}{l}\right)^2\sqrt{\dfrac{EI}{\rho S}}$.

Each term in the series consists of two parts:
- **$\sin\omega t$** — the steady-state (forced) vibration at the driving freque
ncy $\omega$;
- **$\dfrac{\omega}{\omega_n}\sin\omega_n t$** — the transient free vibration at
 the natural frequency $\omega_n$, which ensures the initial conditions are sati
sfied. (In a real system with damping this transient would decay; here, with no
resistance, it persists.)

### Resonant solution ($\omega = \omega_k$)

When $\omega = \omega_k$, the $k$-th modal term is replaced by its resonant form
, and all other terms remain as above:

$$w(x,t) = \frac{2P_0}{\rho S\, l}\left[\frac{\sin\frac{k\pi x_0}{l}\sin\frac{k\
pi x}{l}}{2\omega_k^2}\big(\sin\omega_k t - \omega_k t\cos\omega_k t\big) + \sum
_{\substack{n=1\\n\neq k}}^{\infty}\frac{\sin\frac{n\pi x_0}{l}\sin\frac{n\pi x}
{l}}{\omega_n^2-\omega_k^2}\left(\sin\omega_k t - \frac{\omega_k}{\omega_n}\sin\
omega_n t\right)\right].$$

The resonant mode exhibits **linearly growing amplitude** (the term $-\omega_k t
\cos\omega_k t$), which is the classical resonance phenomenon — the amplitude in
creases without bound in the absence of damping.

## Summary

The transverse vibrations of the hinged rod driven by a concentrated harmonic fo
rce $P_0\sin\omega t$ at $x=x_0$ are given by the modal superposition above. The
 response is a sum over all natural modes, each oscillating at both the driving
frequency $\omega$ (steady-state) and its own natural frequency $\omega_n$ (tran
sient). At resonance ($\omega = \omega_k$), the $k$-th mode grows linearly in am
plitude — the hallmark of undamped resonance.
