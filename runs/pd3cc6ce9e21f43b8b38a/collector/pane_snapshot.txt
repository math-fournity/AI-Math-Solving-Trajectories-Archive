**Answer: Yes, such an $M$ exists.**

**Construction.** Let $R = \mathbb{Z}$. Let $M = \mathbb{Z}$ as a $\mathbb{Z}$-m
odule (finitely generated, by $1$). Define the $R[x]=\mathbb{Z}[x]$-module struc
ture on $M$ by letting $x$ act as multiplication by $2$:
$$x \cdot m = 2m, \quad m \in \mathbb{Z}.$$
This is the module structure induced by the evaluation homomorphism $\phi: \math
bb{Z}[x] \to \mathbb{Z}$, $\phi(f) = f(2)$, so $M \cong \mathbb{Z}[x]/(x-2)$ as
a $\mathbb{Z}[x]$-module.

**Computing the tensor product.** We have:
$$M \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[x]} \mathbb{Z}[x,x^{-1}] \cong \mathbb{Z}[x,x^{-1}]/(x-2
)\,\mathbb{Z}[x,x^{-1}].$$

Define the ring homomorphism $\psi: \mathbb{Z}[x,x^{-1}] \to \mathbb{Z}[1/2]$ by
 $\psi(x)=2$, $\psi(x^{-1})=1/2$. This is well-defined since $2$ is a unit in $\
mathbb{Z}[1/2]$, and it is surjective (since $\mathbb{Z}[1/2]$ is generated over
 $\mathbb{Z}$ by $1/2 = \psi(x^{-1})$).

**Claim:** $\ker\psi = (x-2)$.

*Proof of claim.* The containment $(x-2) \subseteq \ker\psi$ is clear since $\ps
i(x-2)=2-2=0$. For the reverse, take $f = \sum_{k=-N}^{N} a_k x^k \in \ker\psi$,
 so $\sum a_k 2^k = 0$ in $\mathbb{Z}[1/2] \subset \mathbb{Q}$, hence $\sum a_k
2^k = 0$ as an integer (after clearing denominators, but it's already an integer
). Write $f = x^{-N}p(x)$ where $p(x) = \sum_{j=0}^{2N} a_{j-N}x^j \in \mathbb{Z
}[x]$. Then $p(2) = 2^N \cdot \psi(f) \cdot 2^N$... more directly: $\psi(f) = 2^
{-N}p(2) = 0$ implies $p(2)=0$. By the factor theorem over $\mathbb{Z}$, $p(x) =
 (x-2)q(x)$ for some $q(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]$. Thus $f = (x-2)\cdot x^{-N}q(x) \in
 (x-2)$. $\square$

By the first isomorphism theorem:
$$M \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[x]} \mathbb{Z}[x,x^{-1}] \cong \mathbb{Z}[1/2].$$

**$\mathbb{Z}[1/2]$ is not finitely generated as a $\mathbb{Z}$-module.** Suppos
