## Proof

**Claim:** Yes — over a finite field, two semisimple square matrices with the sa
me characteristic polynomial are similar.

**Key fact.** Over any field $F$, a matrix is *semisimple* iff its minimal polyn
omial is squarefree. Similarity is governed by the rational canonical form, i.e.
 by the invariant factors
$$f_1 \mid f_2 \mid \cdots \mid f_k,$$
where $f_k$ is the minimal polynomial and the characteristic polynomial is $\chi
 = f_1 f_2 \cdots f_k$.

**Setup.** Let $A$ be semisimple with characteristic polynomial
$$\chi(x) = \prod_{j} p_j(x)^{m_j},$$
where the $p_j$ are distinct monic irreducibles over $F$ and $m_j \ge 1$.

Since $A$ is semisimple, its minimal polynomial $f_k$ is squarefree. A standard
fact: every irreducible factor of $\chi$ divides the minimal polynomial. Hence
$$f_k = \prod_j p_j.$$

**Each invariant factor is a sub-product of the $p_j$.** Because $f_1 \mid \cdot
s \mid f_k$ and $f_k$ is squarefree, every $f_i$ is squarefree and divides $f_k$
; thus each $f_i$ is a product of a subset of $\{p_j\}$.

**The multiplicities pin down the chain.** For each irreducible $p_j$, define
$$S_j = \{i : p_j \mid f_i\} \subseteq \{1,\dots,k\}.$$
By the divisibility chain condition, $S_j$ is a *suffix*: if $p_j \mid f_i$ then
 $p_j \mid f_{i+1}$. The size $|S_j|$ equals the multiplicity $m_j$ of $p_j$ in
$\chi$, since $\chi = \prod_i f_i$ and each $f_i$ is squarefree in $p_j$. Theref
ore
$$p_j \mid f_i \iff i \ge k - m_j + 1.$$

**The number of invariant factors is forced.** Let $m^* = \max_j m_j$. The irred
ucible $p_{j^*}$ attaining this maximum has $|S_{j^*}| = m^*$, so its suffix is
$\{1,2,\dots,k\}$, giving $k = m^*$. Thus $k$ is determined by $\chi$ alone.

**Conclusion.** With $k = \max_j m_j$ fixed, each invariant factor is now unique
ly determined:
$$f_i = \prod_{\,j\,:\, m_j \,\ge\, k+1-i} p_j, \qquad i = 1,\dots,k.$$
This formula depends *only* on the characteristic polynomial $\chi$. Hence the e
ntire rational canonical form — and therefore the similarity class — is determin
ed by $\chi$ for semisimple matrices.

Since $A$ and $B$ are both semisimple and share the same characteristic polynomi
al, they have identical invariant factors, hence identical rational canonical fo
rms, hence are similar.
