## Proof

We wish to maximize
$$g(x) = (x_1 x_2 \cdots x_n)^{4/n}$$
over $S_{n-1} = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : \|x\|_2 = 1\}$.

**Step 1. Reduce to nonnegative variables.**

Since $g$ depends on $x$ only through the product $P = x_1\cdots x_n$ raised to
the power $4/n$, and $4/n > 0$, the value $g(x) = P^{4/n}$ is maximized when $P$
 is as large as possible (and positive). Replacing any $x_i$ by $|x_i|$ preserve
s $\|x\|_2 = 1$ and does not decrease $|P|$; choosing all signs so that $P \geq
0$ makes $g = |P|^{4/n}$. Hence it suffices to maximize $|x_1\cdots x_n|$ subjec
t to $x_i \geq 0$ and $\sum x_i^2 = 1$.

**Step 2. Apply AM–GM to $x_i^2$.**

Set $y_i = x_i^2 \geq 0$. Then $\sum_{i=1}^n y_i = 1$, and
$$|x_1\cdots x_n| = (y_1 \cdots y_n)^{1/2}.$$
By the AM–GM inequality applied to the nonnegative $y_i$ with $\sum y_i = 1$:
$$\frac{y_1 + \cdots + y_n}{n} \geq (y_1\cdots y_n)^{1/n},$$
i.e.,
$$\frac{1}{n} \geq (y_1\cdots y_n)^{1/n}.$$
Raising both sides to the power $n/2$:
$$(y_1\cdots y_n)^{1/2} \leq \left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{n/2 \cdot \frac{1}{n} \cd
ot n} = \left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{1/2 \cdot 1}.$$

More directly: $(y_1\cdots y_n)^{1/n} \leq 1/n$, so $(y_1\cdots y_n)^{1/2} \leq
(1/n)^{n/2}$, giving
$$|x_1\cdots x_n| \leq n^{-n/2}.$$

**Step 3. Compute the bound on $g$.**

$$g(x) = |x_1\cdots x_n|^{4/n} \leq \left(n^{-n/2}\right)^{4/n} = n^{-2} = \frac
{1}{n^2}.$$

**Step 4. Verify equality is attained.**

Equality in AM–GM holds iff $y_1 = \cdots = y_n = 1/n$, i.e., $|x_i| = 1/\sqrt{n
}$ for all $i$. Choosing $x = (1/\sqrt{n}, \ldots, 1/\sqrt{n})$ gives $\|x\|_2 =
 1$ and
$$x_1\cdots x_n = n^{-n/2}, \qquad g(x) = (n^{-n/2})^{4/n} = n^{-2} = \frac{1}{n
^2}.$$

Thus the upper bound is achieved.

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\max_{x \in S_{n-1}} g(x) = \frac{1}{n^2}}.$$
