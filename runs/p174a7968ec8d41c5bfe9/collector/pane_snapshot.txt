## Distribution of the First Passage Time $T_x$

**Problem.** Let $\{W(t)\}_{t \geq 0}$ be a standard Brownian motion with $W(0)=
0$, and let $x>0$. Define the first passage time
$$T_x := \inf\{t \geq 0 : W(t) = x\}.$$
We determine the distribution of $T_x$.

---

### Step 1. Reformulation via the running maximum

Since Brownian motion has continuous paths, the event $\{T_x \leq t\}$ is equiva
lent to the event that the running maximum reaches level $x$ by time $t$:
$$\{T_x \leq t\} = \left\{\max_{0 \leq s \leq t} W(s) \geq x\right\}.$$
(Continuity ensures that hitting $\geq x$ is the same as hitting $=x$.) Thus
$$P(T_x \leq t) = P\!\left(\max_{0 \leq s \leq t} W(s) \geq x\right).$$

### Step 2. The reflection principle

**Claim (Reflection Principle).** For $x > 0$,
$$P\!\left(\max_{0 \leq s \leq t} W(s) \geq x\right) = 2\,P(W(t) \geq x).$$

*Proof of the claim.* On the event $\{T_x \leq t\}$, define the reflected path
$$\widetilde{W}(s) = \begin{cases} W(s), & 0 \leq s \leq T_x, \\ 2x - W(s), & s
> T_x. \end{cases}$$
Geometrically, after the first hit of level $x$, we reflect the path about the h
orizontal line at height $x$. By the strong Markov property of Brownian motion (
applied at the stopping time $T_x$), the post-$T_x$ increment $\{W(T_x+u)-x\}_{u
\geq 0}$ is an independent standard Brownian motion, and $\{x - W(T_x+u)\}_{u\ge
q 0}$ has the same law. Hence $\widetilde{W}$ is again a standard Brownian motio
n.

The reflection swaps the events
$$\{W(t) \geq x\} \cap \{T_x \leq t\} \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad \{W(t) \leq
x\} \cap \{T_x \leq t\},$$
so these two events have equal probability. Since $\{W(t) \geq x\} \subseteq \{T
_x \leq t\}$ (to be above $x$ at time $t$, the continuous path must have hit $x$
 earlier), we have
$$P(W(t) \geq x) = P\bigl(\{W(t) \geq x\}\cap\{T_x\leq t\}\bigr) = P\bigl(\{W(t)
 \leq x\}\cap\{T_x\leq t\}\bigr).$$
Adding,
$$P(T_x \leq t) = P\bigl(\{W(t)\geq x\}\cap\{T_x\leq t\}\bigr) + P\bigl(\{W(t)\l
eq x\}\cap\{T_x\leq t\}\bigr) = 2\,P(W(t)\geq x).$$
This proves the claim. $\square$

### Step 3. The CDF of $T_x$

Since $W(t) \sim N(0,t)$, we have $W(t)/\sqrt{t} \sim N(0,1)$ with CDF $\Phi$. T
herefore
$$\boxed{\;P(T_x \leq t) = 2\,P(W(t) \geq x) = 2\!\left(1 - \Phi\!\left(\frac{x}
{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right) = 2\,\Phi\!\left(-\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\right),\quad t>0.\
;}$$

Equivalently, using the standard normal tail bound form, $P(T_x \leq t) = \opera
torname{erfc}\!\left(\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{2t}}\right)$.

### Step 4. The density of $T_x$

Differentiate the CDF with respect to $t$. Writing $u = x/\sqrt{t}$ so that $du/
dt = -\tfrac{1}{2}x\,t^{-3/2}$, and using $\Phi'(z)=\varphi(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\
pi}}e^{-z^2/2}$:
$$f_{T_x}(t) = \frac{d}{dt}\,2\Phi\!\left(-\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\right) = 2\,\varph
i\!\left(-\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\cdot\frac{x}{2}\,t^{-3/2} = \frac{x}{\sqrt{2
\pi}\,t^{3/2}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{2t}\right).$$

Thus
$$\boxed{\;f_{T_x}(t) = \frac{x}{\sqrt{2\pi\,t^3}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{2t}\ri
ght),\quad t>0.\;}$$

This is the **Lévy distribution** (a stable law of index $\tfrac{1}{2}$), also d
enoted $T_x \sim \text{Lévy}(0, x^2)$ in the standard parameterization, or $T_x
\sim x^2\,\text{Lévy}(0,1)$ by Brownian scaling.

### Step 5. Verification of key properties

- **Positivity:** $f_{T_x}(t)>0$ for all $t>0$, and $f_{T_x}(t)\to 0$ as $t\to 0
^+$ (the density vanishes at the origin) and $f_{T_x}(t)\to 0$ as $t\to\infty$.
- **Total mass = 1:** With the substitution $u = x/\sqrt{t}$ (so $t = x^2/u^2$,
$dt = -2x^2 u^{-3}\,du$),
$$\int_0^\infty f_{T_x}(t)\,dt = \int_0^\infty \frac{x}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,t^{3/2}}e^{
-x^2/(2t)}\,dt = \int_0^\infty \frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-u^2/2}\,du = 1.$$
Hence $P(T_x < \infty) = 1$: Brownian motion hits every level $x>0$ almost surel
y.
- **Infinite mean:** $\mathbb{E}[T_x] = \infty$, since $\int_0^\infty t\,f_{T_x}
(t)\,dt = \infty$ (the integrand $\sim t^{-1/2}$ as $t\to\infty$ is not integrab
le). This reflects the heavy right tail of the Lévy law.
- **Laplace transform:** $\mathbb{E}[e^{-\lambda T_x}] = e^{-x\sqrt{2\lambda}}$
for $\lambda\geq 0$, the hallmark of a $\tfrac12$-stable law. (This follows, e.g
., from the CDF via a standard Gaussian-integral identity, or from the optional-
stopping/martingale argument applied to $\exp(\theta W(t)-\tfrac12\theta^2 t)$.)
- **Scaling:** $T_x \overset{d}{=} x^2\,T_1$, consistent with Brownian scaling $
W(t)\overset{d}{=}\sqrt{c}\,W(t/c)$.

### Conclusion

The first passage time $T_x$ of a standard Brownian motion (started at $0$) to a
 level $x>0$ has the **Lévy distribution** with CDF
$$P(T_x \leq t) = 2\left(1-\Phi\!\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right) = \opera
torname{erfc}\!\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{2t}}\right),\quad t>0,$$
and density
$$f_{T_x}(t) = \frac{x}{\sqrt{2\pi\,t^3}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{2t}\right),\qua
d t>0.$$
The hitting is almost sure ($P(T_x<\infty)=1$) but the mean hitting time is infi
nite ($\mathbb{E}[T_x]=\infty$).
