### Evaluation of $\displaystyle I = \int_0^1 \frac{\ln(x+1)}{x^2+1}\,dx$

**Step 1: Trigonometric substitution.**

Set $x = \tan\theta$, so $dx = \sec^2\theta\,d\theta$ and $x^2+1 = \sec^2\theta$
. The limits transform as $x=0 \Rightarrow \theta=0$ and $x=1 \Rightarrow \theta
=\frac{\pi}{4}$. Thus

$$I = \int_0^{\pi/4} \frac{\ln(1+\tan\theta)}{\sec^2\theta}\,\sec^2\theta\,d\the
ta = \int_0^{\pi/4} \ln(1+\tan\theta)\,d\theta.$$

**Step 2: Symmetry via $\theta \mapsto \frac{\pi}{4}-\theta$.**

Applying the substitution $\theta \to \frac{\pi}{4}-\theta$ (which preserves the
 interval $[0,\frac{\pi}{4}]$):

$$I = \int_0^{\pi/4} \ln\!\left(1+\tan\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{4}-\theta\right)\right
)d\theta.$$

Using the tangent subtraction formula,

$$\tan\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{4}-\theta\right) = \frac{1-\tan\theta}{1+\tan\theta},$
$

so

$$1 + \tan\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{4}-\theta\right) = 1 + \frac{1-\tan\theta}{1+\tan\
theta} = \frac{(1+\tan\theta)+(1-\tan\theta)}{1+\tan\theta} = \frac{2}{1+\tan\th
eta}.$$

Therefore

$$I = \int_0^{\pi/4} \ln\!\left(\frac{2}{1+\tan\theta}\right)d\theta = \int_0^{\
pi/4}\bigl(\ln 2 - \ln(1+\tan\theta)\bigr)\,d\theta = \frac{\pi}{4}\ln 2 - I.$$

**Step 3: Solve for $I$.**

$$2I = \frac{\pi}{4}\ln 2 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad I = \frac{\pi}{8}\ln 2.$$

### Result

$$\boxed{\int_0^1 \frac{\ln(x+1)}{x^2+1}\,dx = \frac{\pi\ln 2}{8}}$$
