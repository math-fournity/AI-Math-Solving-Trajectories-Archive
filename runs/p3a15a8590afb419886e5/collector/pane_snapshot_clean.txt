## Direct Computation of the Line Integral

**Parametrize the curve.** The curve $C$ is the intersection of the cylinder $x^
2+y^2=1$ and the plane $x+y+z=1$. Set

$$x=\cos t,\qquad y=\sin t,\qquad z=1-x-y=1-\cos t-\sin t,\qquad t\in[0,2\pi].$$

Then

$$dx=-\sin t\,dt,\qquad dy=\cos t\,dt,\qquad dz=(\sin t-\cos t)\,dt.$$

**Substitute into the integrand.**

$$\int_C -y^3\,dx+x^3\,dy-z^3\,dz=\int_0^{2\pi}\Big[-(\sin t)^3(-\sin t)+(\cos t
)^3(\cos t)-(1-\cos t-\sin t)^3(\sin t-\cos t)\Big]dt$$

$$=\int_0^{2\pi}\Big[\sin^4 t+\cos^4 t-(1-\cos t-\sin t)^3(\sin t-\cos t)\Big]dt
.$$

**The third term vanishes.** Let $u(t)=1-\cos t-\sin t$. Then $u'(t)=\sin t-\cos
 t$, so the third term is $-u^3 u'$, i.e. an exact derivative:

$$-\int_0^{2\pi}u^3 u'\,dt=-\left[\frac{u^4}{4}\right]_0^{2\pi}.$$

Since $u(0)=1-1-0=0$ and $u(2\pi)=1-1-0=0$, this equals $0$.

**Evaluate the remaining integral.**

$$\int_0^{2\pi}(\sin^4 t+\cos^4 t)\,dt.$$

Use the identity

$$\sin^4 t+\cos^4 t=(\sin^2 t+\cos^2 t)^2-2\sin^2 t\cos^2 t=1-\frac{1}{2}\sin^2(
2t).$$

Hence

$$\int_0^{2\pi}(\sin^4 t+\cos^4 t)\,dt=\int_0^{2\pi}1\,dt-\frac{1}{2}\int_0^{2\p
i}\sin^2(2t)\,dt.$$

The first integral is $2\pi$. For the second, $\int_0^{2\pi}\sin^2(2t)\,dt=\pi$
(average value $1/2$ over a period, length $2\pi$). Therefore

$$\int_0^{2\pi}(\sin^4 t+\cos^4 t)\,dt=2\pi-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\pi=2\pi-\frac{\pi}{
2}=\frac{3\pi}{2}.$$

**Combine.**

$$\int_C -y^3\,dx+x^3\,dy-z^3\,dz=\frac{3\pi}{2}+0=\boxed{\frac{3\pi}{2}}.$$

This confirms the stated value. $\blacksquare$
