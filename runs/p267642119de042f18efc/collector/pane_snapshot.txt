**Answer: Yes, $s$ must be identically zero on $X$.**

**Proof.**

We prove that a holomorphic section of a holomorphic vector bundle over a connec
ted complex manifold that vanishes on a non-empty open subset must vanish identi
cally.

**Step 1: Reduction to local coordinates.**

Let $\pi: E \to X$ be the holomorphic vector bundle, and let $s: X \to E$ be a h
olomorphic section with $s|_U = 0$ for some non-empty open set $U \subset X$. We
 must show $s \equiv 0$ on $X$.

Since $X$ is a complex manifold, it is in particular connected and hence irreduc
ible as a complex analytic space. The key tool is the **identity theorem for hol
omorphic functions on connected complex manifolds**, applied fiberwise to the bu
ndle.

**Step 2: Local trivialization and component functions.**

Choose an open cover $\{V_\alpha\}$ of $X$ with holomorphic local trivialization
s $\phi_\alpha: \pi^{-1}(V_\alpha) \to V_\alpha \times \mathbb{C}^r$, where $r =
 \operatorname{rank} E$. Under each trivialization, the section $s$ is represent
ed by a tuple of holomorphic functions
$$
\phi_\alpha \circ s|_{V_\alpha} = (s_{\alpha,1}, \dots, s_{\alpha,r}) : V_\alpha
 \to \mathbb{C}^r,
$$
where each $s_{\alpha,j}$ is a holomorphic function on $V_\alpha$.

**Step 3: The vanishing locus is both open and closed.**

Define the **zero locus** of $s$:
$$
Z(s) := \{x \in X : s(x) = 0 \in E_x\}.
$$

We claim $Z(s)$ is both open and closed in $X$.

*$Z(s)$ is open:* Suppose $x_0 \in Z(s)$. Choose a trivialization around $x_0$ o
n some open neighborhood $V$ of $x_0$. Then $s|_V = (s_1, \dots, s_r)$ with each
 $s_j$ holomorphic on $V$ and $s_j(x_0) = 0$ for all $j$. Since $s \equiv 0$ on
$U$ and $U$ is open, the set $U \cap V$ (if non-empty) gives us that each $s_j$
vanishes on the open set $U \cap V$. By the classical identity theorem for holom
orphic functions of several variables, each $s_j \equiv 0$ on the connected comp
onent of $V$ containing points of $U \cap V$.

More carefully: $Z(s)$ is open because $s$ is continuous and the zero section is
 closed, but we need the *holomorphic* structure. Actually, the correct argument
 is:

- $Z(s)$ is **closed**: $Z(s) = s^{-1}(\text{zero section})$, and the zero secti
on is a closed analytic subset of $E$, while $s$ is continuous. So $Z(s)$ is clo
sed.

- $Z(s)$ is **open**: Let $x_0 \in Z(s)$. We show $s$ vanishes in a neighborhood
 of $x_0$. Take a trivialization around $x_0$ on $V$. Then $s|_V = (s_1, \dots,
s_r)$. Since $x_0 \in Z(s)$, we have $s_j(x_0) = 0$ for all $j$. But we need mor
e: we need $s$ to vanish on an open set near $x_0$.

Here is the precise argument. Consider the interior $\operatorname{int}(Z(s))$.
By hypothesis $U \subset Z(s)$, so $\operatorname{int}(Z(s)) \supset U \neq \emp
tyset$.

**Step 4: The interior of $Z(s)$ is both open and closed.**

Let $W = \operatorname{int}(Z(s))$. Clearly $W$ is open. We show $W$ is also clo
sed (in $X$).

Take a point $x_0 \in \overline{W}$ (closure of $W$). Choose a connected open ne
ighborhood $V$ of $x_0$ with a trivialization, so $s|_V = (s_1, \dots, s_r)$, ea
ch $s_j$ holomorphic on $V$. Since $x_0 \in \overline{W}$, the set $V \cap W$ is
 a non-empty open subset of $V$ on which all $s_j$ vanish. By the **identity the
orem for holomorphic functions** (on the connected open set $V \subset \mathbb{C
}^n$), each $s_j \equiv 0$ on $V$. Therefore $s \equiv 0$ on $V$, so $V \subset
Z(s)$, and since $V$ is open, $V \subset W$. In particular $x_0 \in W$.

This shows $\overline{W} \subset W$, hence $W$ is closed.

**Step 5: Conclusion by connectedness.**

$W$ is non-empty (since $U \subset W$), open, and closed in $X$. Since $X$ is co
nnected, $W = X$. Therefore $s \equiv 0$ on $X$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The essential ingredients are: (1) the classical identity theorem fo
r holomorphic functions on connected open subsets of $\mathbb{C}^n$ (a holomorph
ic function vanishing on a non-empty open set vanishes identically on each conne
cted component), (2) local trivialization of the bundle reducing the section to
component holomorphic functions, and (3) connectedness of $X$ forcing the open-a
nd-closed vanishing set to be all of $X$.
