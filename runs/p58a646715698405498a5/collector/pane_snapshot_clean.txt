We use **spherical coordinates**:
$$x = \rho\sin\phi\cos\theta,\quad y = \rho\sin\phi\sin\theta,\quad z = \rho\cos
\phi,$$
with $r^2 = x^2+y^2 = \rho^2\sin^2\phi$, Jacobian $\rho^2\sin\phi$.

### Determining the region in $(\rho, \phi, \theta)$

**Sphere** $x^2+y^2+z^2\le 4$ gives $\rho \le 2$.

**Cone constraints** $\frac{1}{3}(x^2+y^2)\le z^2 \le 3(x^2+y^2)$, with $z\ge 0$
 (so $0\le\phi\le\pi/2$ and $\cos\phi\ge 0$):

$$\frac{1}{3}\rho^2\sin^2\phi \le \rho^2\cos^2\phi \le 3\,\rho^2\sin^2\phi$$

Dividing by $\rho^2\cos^2\phi > 0$ (valid since $z\ge 0$ and the region excludes
 the equator):

$$\frac{1}{3}\tan^2\phi \le 1 \le 3\tan^2\phi \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{1}{\sqrt
 3}\le \tan\phi \le \sqrt 3 \;\Longrightarrow\; \boxed{\frac{\pi}{6}\le \phi \le
 \frac{\pi}{3}}.$$

**Azimuthal symmetry** gives $\theta\in[0,2\pi]$.

### Setting up the integral

The integrand:
$$z(x^2+y^2) = \rho\cos\phi\cdot\rho^2\sin^2\phi = \rho^3\cos\phi\sin^2\phi.$$

Including the Jacobian $\rho^2\sin\phi$, the full integral separates:

$$I = \int_0^{2\pi}\!d\theta\;\int_{\pi/6}^{\pi/3}\!\cos\phi\sin^3\phi\,d\phi\;\
int_0^{2}\!\rho^5\,d\rho.$$

### Evaluating each factor

**Radial:**
$$\int_0^{2}\rho^5\,d\rho = \frac{\rho^6}{6}\bigg|_0^{2} = \frac{64}{6} = \frac{
32}{3}.$$

**Polar** (substitute $u=\sin\phi$, $du=\cos\phi\,d\phi$):
$$\int_{\pi/6}^{\pi/3}\cos\phi\sin^3\phi\,d\phi = \frac{\sin^4\phi}{4}\bigg|_{\p
i/6}^{\pi/3} = \frac{1}{4}\!\left[\left(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)^{\!4}-\left(\fra
c{1}{2}\right)^{\!4}\right] = \frac{1}{4}\!\left[\frac{9}{16}-\frac{1}{16}\right
] = \frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{8}{16}=\frac{1}{8}.$$

**Azimuthal:** $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}d\theta = 2\pi$.

### Combining

$$I = 2\pi \cdot \frac{1}{8}\cdot\frac{32}{3} = 2\pi\cdot\frac{32}{24} = 2\pi\cd
ot\frac{4}{3} = \boxed{\dfrac{8\pi}{3}}.$$

### Verification (cylindrical coordinates)

With $r^2=x^2+y^2$, the integrand times Jacobian is $r^3 z\,dr\,d\theta\,dz$, gi
ving a $2\pi$ factor. The $(r,z)$ region (with $z\ge r/\sqrt3$, $z\le r\sqrt3$,
$r^2+z^2\le 4$) splits at $r=1$ (where the upper cone meets the sphere) and $r=\
sqrt3$ (where the lower cone meets the sphere):

- **Part A** ($0\le r\le 1$, $z\in[r/\sqrt3,\, r\sqrt3]$): $\int z\,dz = \tfrac{
4r^2}{3}$, contributing $2\pi\cdot\tfrac{4}{3}\int_0^1 r^5\,dr = \tfrac{4\pi}{9}
$.
- **Part B** ($1\le r\le\sqrt3$, $z\in[r/\sqrt3,\,\sqrt{4-r^2}]$): $\int z\,dz =
 2-\tfrac{2r^2}{3}$, contributing $2\pi\big[\tfrac{r^4}{2}-\tfrac{r^6}{9}\big]_1
^{\sqrt3} = 2\pi\cdot\tfrac{10}{9}=\tfrac{20\pi}{9}$.

Total: $\tfrac{4\pi}{9}+\tfrac{20\pi}{9}=\tfrac{24\pi}{9}=\dfrac{8\pi}{3}$. ✓ Bo
th methods agree.
