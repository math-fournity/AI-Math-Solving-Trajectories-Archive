## Proof

**Claim.** The linear operator in quantum mechanics whose spectrum corresponds t
o the continuous spectrum of physical light is the **Hamiltonian (energy operato
r) $\hat{H}$** of the free electromagnetic field.

---

### Step 1 — Observables correspond to self-adjoint linear operators

A foundational postulate of quantum mechanics states that every physical observa
ble is represented by a self-adjoint (Hermitian) linear operator acting on the H
ilbert space of states, and the **spectrum** of that operator is precisely the s
et of values the observable can take upon measurement. Therefore, to identify th
e operator corresponding to the spectrum of light, we must identify which observ
able's possible values *are* the spectrum of light.

### Step 2 — The spectrum of light is the set of possible photon frequencies

Physical light is decomposed into its frequency (equivalently, color/wavelength)
 components. The "spectrum of light" is the collection of frequencies $\nu$ (or
angular frequencies $\omega = 2\pi\nu$) present in the electromagnetic field. Fo
r freely traveling photons — i.e., photons not confined to a cavity or subjected
 to boundary conditions that quantize allowed modes — the frequency can take any
 value in $[0, \infty)$. This is the **continuous spectrum** of light.

### Step 3 — Photon energy is proportional to frequency

The problem states (and the Planck–Einstein relation confirms) that each photon'
s energy is proportional to its frequency:

$$E = h\nu = \hbar\omega.$$

This means that measuring a photon's energy is equivalent to measuring its frequ
ency, up to the constant of proportionality $h$. The set of possible energies $\
{E\}$ and the set of possible frequencies $\{\nu\}$ are in bijection via $E = h\
nu$. Consequently, the spectrum of the energy operator and the spectrum of the f
requency are the *same* spectrum (rescaled by $h$).

### Step 4 — The energy operator is the Hamiltonian

In quantum mechanics, the operator corresponding to the observable "energy" is t
he **Hamiltonian** $\hat{H}$. For the free electromagnetic field (the quantum fi
eld describing traveling photons), the Hamiltonian is

$$\hat{H} = \int d^3k\;\hbar\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\,\hat{a}^\dagger(\mathbf{k})\hat
{a}(\mathbf{k}) \;+\; \text{(zero-point)},$$

where $\omega_{\mathbf{k}} = c|\mathbf{k}|$ is the continuous dispersion relatio
n for massless photons, and $\hat{a}^\dagger(\mathbf{k}), \hat{a}(\mathbf{k})$ a
re the creation and annihilation operators for a photon of momentum $\hbar\mathb
f{k}$.

### Step 5 — The Hamiltonian of the free field has a continuous spectrum

Because the mode label $\mathbf{k}$ (and hence $\omega_{\mathbf{k}} = c|\mathbf{
k}|$) ranges continuously over $\mathbb{R}^3$, the eigenvalues of $\hat{H}$ — th
e allowed photon energies $E = \hbar\omega$ — fill the continuous interval $[0,
\infty)$. This is a **continuous spectrum**, not a discrete one.

This stands in contrast to a photon (or any field mode) confined to a cavity, wh
ere boundary conditions force $\omega$ to take only discrete values, making the
spectrum of $\hat{H}$ discrete. The *physical*, *traveling* (free) nature of the
 photons is precisely what makes the spectrum continuous — matching the continuo
us spectrum of physical light.

### Step 6 — Identification

Combining Steps 1–5:

| Physical fact | Quantum-mechanical counterpart |
|---|---|
| Spectrum of light = set of frequencies $\nu \in [0,\infty)$ | Spectrum of an o
perator = set of possible measurement values |
| $E = h\nu$ (energy ∝ frequency) | Energy observable ↔ Hamiltonian $\hat{H}$ |
| Free traveling photons → continuous $\nu$ | Free-field $\hat{H}$ has continuou
s spectrum $[0,\infty)$ |

The operator whose continuous spectrum reproduces the continuous spectrum of phy
sical light is therefore the **Hamiltonian (energy operator) $\hat{H}$ of the fr
ee electromagnetic field**. Its continuous spectrum $[0,\infty)$ corresponds, vi
a $E = h\nu$, to the continuous frequency spectrum of light.

---

**Conclusion.** The linear operator in quantum mechanics that corresponds to the
 continuous spectrum of physical light is the **Hamiltonian operator $\hat{H}$**
 (the energy operator) of the free electromagnetic field. The proportionality $E
 = h\nu$ makes the continuous spectrum of $\hat{H}$ — the allowed photon energie
s — identical (up to the constant $h$) to the continuous spectrum of light frequ
encies.
