## Proof

The radius of the random walk is defined as
$$\rho = \limsup_{k\to\infty} \bigl(p^{(k)}(0,0)\bigr)^{1/k}.$$

**Step 1: Reduction to even steps.** In the symmetric nearest-neighbor walk on $
\mathbb{Z}$, each step changes the parity of the position. Hence $p^{(k)}(0,0)=0
$ for odd $k$, and only even $k=2n$ contribute to the limsup:
$$\rho = \limsup_{n\to\infty} \bigl(p^{(2n)}(0,0)\bigr)^{1/(2n)}.$$

**Step 2: Exact formula.** To return to $0$ in $2n$ steps, exactly $n$ steps mus
t be $+1$ and $n$ steps must be $-1$. Each such path has probability $(1/2)^{2n}
$, and there are $\binom{2n}{n}$ such paths. Thus
$$p^{(2n)}(0,0) = \binom{2n}{n}\frac{1}{2^{2n}} = \frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^n}.$$

**Step 3: Asymptotics via Stirling.** By Stirling's formula $n! \sim \sqrt{2\pi
n}\,(n/e)^n$,
$$\binom{2n}{n} = \frac{(2n)!}{(n!)^2} \sim \frac{\sqrt{4\pi n}\,(2n/e)^{2n}}{2\
pi n\,(n/e)^{2n}} = \frac{4^n}{\sqrt{\pi n}}.$$
Therefore
$$p^{(2n)}(0,0) = \frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^n} \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi n}}.$$

**Step 4: Taking the $2n$-th root.** Since $p^{(2n)}(0,0) \sim (\pi n)^{-1/2}$,
$$\bigl(p^{(2n)}(0,0)\bigr)^{1/(2n)} \sim \bigl(\pi n\bigr)^{-1/(4n)}.$$
Now $\log\bigl((\pi n)^{-1/(4n)}\bigr) = -\frac{\log(\pi n)}{4n} \to 0$ as $n\to
\infty$, so $(\pi n)^{-1/(4n)}\to 1$. Hence
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\bigl(p^{(2n)}(0,0)\bigr)^{1/(2n)} = 1.$$

**Step 5: Conclusion.** The limsup equals this limit:
$$\rho = \limsup_{k\to\infty}\bigl(p^{(k)}(0,0)\bigr)^{1/k} = 1.$$

The radius of the symmetric random walk on $\mathbb{Z}$ is $\boxed{1}$.
