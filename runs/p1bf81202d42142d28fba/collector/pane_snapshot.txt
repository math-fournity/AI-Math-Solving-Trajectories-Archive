## Solution

**Rescaling.** Let $X=10x,\;Y=10y,\;Z=10z$. Then $(X,Y,Z)$ is uniform on the sim
plex
$$\Delta:\; X+Y+Z=1000,\quad X,Y,Z>0,$$
and "rounding $x$ to the nearest $0.1$" becomes "rounding $X$ to the nearest int
eger." The alarm is silent iff
$$\operatorname{round}(X)+\operatorname{round}(Y)+\operatorname{round}(Z)=1000=X
+Y+Z.$$

**Decomposition into unit cubes.** Write $X=a+u,\;Y=b+v,\;Z=c+w$ with $a,b,c\in\
mathbb Z_{\ge 0}$ and $u,v,w\in[0,1)$. Within a fixed cube $[a,a{+}1]\times[b,b{
+}1]\times[c,c{+}1]$, the cross-section with the plane $X+Y+Z=1000$ is
$$u+v+w = k,\qquad k = 1000-(a+b+c),\quad u,v,w\in[0,1).$$
This cross-section has positive area only for $k\in\{1,2\}$ (for $k=0$ it is a s
ingle point). Hence $a+b+c\in\{999,998\}$.

- $k=1$: the section is the triangle $T_1:\;u+v+w=1$ in the unit cube, with vert
ices $(1,0,0),(0,1,0),(0,0,1)$. Number of cubes: $\binom{999+2}{2}=\binom{1001}{
2}$.
- $k=2$: the section is the triangle $T_2:\;u+v+w=2$ in the unit cube, with vert
ices $(1,1,0),(1,0,1),(0,1,1)$. Number of cubes: $\binom{998+2}{2}=\binom{1000}{
2}$.

Every cross-section (for either value of $k$) has the same area $\tfrac{\sqrt3}{
2}$, and since the density on $\Delta$ is constant, the distribution of $(u,v,w)
$ is the mixture: pick $k\in\{1,2\}$ with probability proportional to the cube c
ount, then sample uniformly on $T_k$.

**The silent condition in terms of $k$.** Since $\operatorname{round}(X)=a+\math
bf 1_{\{u>1/2\}}$ (the case $u=\tfrac12$ has measure $0$),
$$\operatorname{round}(X)+\operatorname{round}(Y)+\operatorname{round}(Z)=(a+b+c
)+N_{\uparrow}=(1000-k)+N_{\uparrow},$$
where $N_{\uparrow}=\#\{i:u_i>1/2\}$. The sum equals $1000$ iff $N_{\uparrow}=k$
.

**Probability on $T_1$ ($k=1$, need $N_{\uparrow}=1$).** On $T_1$, at most one c
oordinate can exceed $\tfrac12$ (two would force the sum $>1$). The region $u>\t
frac12$ is the corner triangle with vertices $(1,0,0),(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,0),(\tf
rac12,0,\tfrac12)$, similar to $T_1$ with scale $\tfrac12$, hence area ratio $\t
frac14$. By symmetry the three corner regions are disjoint, so
$$\Pr(N_{\uparrow}=1\mid T_1)=3\cdot\tfrac14=\tfrac34.$$

**Probability on $T_2$ ($k=2$, need $N_{\uparrow}=2$).** Apply the change of var
iables $u'=1-u,\;v'=1-v,\;w'=1-w$. This maps $T_2$ isometrically onto $T_1$ (sin
ce $u'+v'+w'=3-2=1$), and $u>\tfrac12\iff u'<\tfrac12$. Thus "$N_{\uparrow}=2$ o
n $T_2$" $\iff$ "exactly two of $u',v',w'$ are $<\tfrac12$ on $T_1$" $\iff$ "exa
ctly one of $u',v',w'$ is $>\tfrac12$ on $T_1$" (the third is then automatically
 $<\tfrac12$). Therefore
$$\Pr(N_{\uparrow}=2\mid T_2)=\Pr(N_{\uparrow}=1\mid T_1)=\tfrac34.$$

(Equivalently, on $T_2$ the medial triangle "all three $>\tfrac12$" has area $\t
frac14$, leaving $1-\tfrac14=\tfrac34$ for "exactly two $>\tfrac12$".)

**Combining.** Both conditional probabilities equal $\tfrac34$, so
$$\Pr(\text{silent})=\Pr(k=1)\cdot\tfrac34+\Pr(k=2)\cdot\tfrac34=\tfrac34\bigl(\
Pr(k=1)+\Pr(k=2)\bigr)=\tfrac34.$$

Thus $\dfrac{m}{n}=\dfrac{3}{4}$ with $m=3,\;n=4$ (coprime), and
$$100m+n=100\cdot 3+4=\boxed{304}.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
