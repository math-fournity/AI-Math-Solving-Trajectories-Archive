# Derivation of the Euler–Lagrange Equations on a Smooth Manifold

## Setup

Let $M$ be a smooth $n$-dimensional manifold with tangent bundle $\pi: TM \to M$
. A **Lagrangian** is a smooth function
$$L: TM \to \mathbb{R}.$$

A **curve** $\gamma: [a,b] \to M$ has **action**
$$S[\gamma] = \int_a^b L\!\bigl(\gamma(t), \dot\gamma(t)\bigr)\,dt.$$

We seek conditions characterizing **extremals** of $S$ among curves with fixed e
ndpoints $\gamma(a)=p$, $\gamma(b)=q$.

---

## Step 1. Local coordinates on $TM$

Choose a chart $(U, x^1,\dots,x^n)$ on $M$. The associated bundle chart on $\pi^
{-1}(U) \subset TM$ has coordinates
$$(x^1,\dots,x^n, v^1,\dots,v^n),$$
where a tangent vector $w \in T_m M$ is written $w = \sum_i v^i \tfrac{\partial}
{\partial x^i}\big|_m$.

In these coordinates the Lagrangian becomes a function
$$L(x^1,\dots,x^n, v^1,\dots,v^n).$$

For a curve $\gamma(t)$ we write $x^i(t) = x^i(\gamma(t))$ and $\dot x^i(t) = v^
i(\dot\gamma(t))$, so
$$S[\gamma] = \int_a^b L\!\bigl(x(t),\dot x(t)\bigr)\,dt.$$

---

## Step 2. First variation

Let $\gamma_\varepsilon$ be a smooth one-parameter family of curves with $\gamma
_0 = \gamma$, fixed endpoints $\gamma_\varepsilon(a)=p$, $\gamma_\varepsilon(b)=
q$, and **variation field**
$$\eta(t) = \left.\tfrac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{\varepsilon=0} \gamma_\varepsi
lon(t) \in T_{\gamma(t)}M, \qquad \eta(a)=\eta(b)=0.$$

In coordinates write $x^i(t,\varepsilon) = x^i(\gamma_\varepsilon(t))$ and $\eta
^i(t) = \partial_\varepsilon x^i(t,\varepsilon)\big|_{\varepsilon=0}$, with $\et
a^i(a)=\eta^i(b)=0$.

The first variation is
$$\delta S := \left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{\varepsilon=0} S[\gamma_\vare
psilon] = \int_a^b \sum_{i=1}^n \left( \frac{\partial L}{\partial x^i}\,\eta^i +
 \frac{\partial L}{\partial v^i}\,\dot\eta^i \right) dt,$$
where $\partial L/\partial x^i$ and $\partial L/\partial v^i$ are evaluated at $
(x(t),\dot x(t))$.

Integrate the second term by parts:
$$\int_a^b \frac{\partial L}{\partial v^i}\,\dot\eta^i\,dt = \left[\frac{\partia
l L}{\partial v^i}\eta^i\right]_a^b - \int_a^b \frac{d}{dt}\!\left(\frac{\partia
l L}{\partial v^i}\right)\eta^i\,dt.$$

The boundary term vanishes because $\eta^i(a)=\eta^i(b)=0$. Hence
$$\delta S = \int_a^b \sum_{i=1}^n \left( \frac{\partial L}{\partial x^i} - \fra
c{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial v^i} \right)\eta^i\,dt.$$

---

## Step 3. Fundamental lemma and the Euler–Lagrange equations

For $\gamma$ to be an extremal, $\delta S = 0$ for **every** variation field $\e
ta$ vanishing at the endpoints. By the fundamental lemma of the calculus of vari
ations (the integrand is continuous and $\eta^i$ are arbitrary smooth functions
vanishing at endpoints), each coefficient must vanish identically:

$$\boxed{\;\frac{\partial L}{\partial x^i}\!\left(x(t),\dot x(t)\right) - \frac{
d}{dt}\!\left[\frac{\partial L}{\partial v^i}\!\left(x(t),\dot x(t)\right)\right
] = 0, \qquad i = 1,\dots,n.\;}$$

Equivalently,
$$\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial v^i} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial x^i},
 \qquad i=1,\dots,n.$$

These are the **Euler–Lagrange equations** in local coordinates.

---

## Step 4. Coordinate independence

We must verify the equations are intrinsic, i.e. independent of the chart. Under
 a coordinate change $x^i \mapsto \tilde x^a = \tilde x^a(x)$, the velocity tran
sforms by the chain rule
$$\tilde v^a = \sum_i \frac{\partial \tilde x^a}{\partial x^i}\,v^i.$$

The partial derivatives of $L$ transform as follows. First,
$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial \tilde v^a} = \sum_i \frac{\partial L}{\partial v^i
}\frac{\partial v^i}{\partial \tilde v^a} = \sum_i \frac{\partial L}{\partial v^
i}\frac{\partial x^i}{\partial \tilde x^a},$$
so the covector $\displaystyle p_i := \frac{\partial L}{\partial v^i}$ transform
s as a covector on $M$ — it is the **fiber derivative** (Legendre map)
$$\mathbb{F}L: TM \to T^*M, \qquad (x,v) \mapsto \left(x,\, \tfrac{\partial L}{\
partial v^i}dx^i\right),$$
which is coordinate-independent.

Next, differentiating $p_i$ along the curve and using the chain rule,
$$\frac{d}{dt}p_i = \sum_a \frac{\partial p_i}{\partial \tilde x^a}\dot{\tilde x
}^a + \sum_a \frac{\partial p_i}{\partial \tilde v^a}\dot{\tilde v}^a,$$
and similarly $\partial L/\partial x^i$ transforms as a covector (since $L$ is a
 scalar on $TM$). A direct computation shows that the combination
$$E_i(L) := \frac{\partial L}{\partial x^i} - \frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\par
tial v^i}$$
transforms as a covector along $\gamma$:
$$\tilde E_a(L) = \sum_i \frac{\partial x^i}{\partial \tilde x^a}\,E_i(L).$$

Therefore the single intrinsic condition
$$\boxed{\;E(L) = 0 \quad \text{along } \gamma, \;}$$
i.e. the vanishing of the **Euler–Lagrange one-form** along the curve, is well-d
efined on $M$ independent of coordinates. Concretely, $E(L) \in T^*_{\gamma(t)}M
$ is the covector with components $E_i(L)$, and the Euler–Lagrange equations ass
ert
$$\mathbb{F}L(\gamma,\dot\gamma)\text{ is covariantly constant along }\gamma \te
xt{ in the sense } \frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{F}L = d\!\left(L - \langle \mathbb{F}L, \
dot\gamma\rangle\right).$$

---

## Step 5. Intrinsic (coordinate-free) formulation

Define the **Liouville/Poincaré one-form** $\theta$ on $T^*M$ and, when $\mathbb
{F}L$ is a local diffeomorphism (regular Lagrangian), pull it back to $TM$:
$$\theta_L := (\mathbb{F}L)^*\theta, \qquad \omega_L := -d\theta_L \quad \text{(
Lagrangian symplectic form)}.$$

Let $X_E$ be the **Euler vector field** on $TM$ determined by
$$\omega_L(X_E, \cdot) = dE,$$
where $E := \langle \mathbb{F}L, \dot{}\rangle - L$ is the **energy**. Then the
integral curves of $X_E$ projected to $M$ are precisely the solutions of the Eul
er–Lagrange equations. This is the **coordinate-free** statement: the Euler–Lagr
ange equations are the projection to $M$ of the Hamiltonian flow of $E$ on $(TM,
 \omega_L)$.

---

## Summary

For a Lagrangian $L: TM \to \mathbb{R}$, a curve $\gamma$ is an extremal of the
action $S[\gamma]=\int L\,dt$ (with fixed endpoints) if and only if, in any loca
l coordinates $(x^i, v^i)$ on $TM$,

$$\frac{d}{dt}\!\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial v^i}\bigg|_{(x(t),\dot x(t))}\r
ight) = \frac{\partial L}{\partial x^i}\bigg|_{(x(t),\dot x(t))}, \qquad i = 1,\
dots,n.$$

These equations are coordinate-independent: the expression $E_i(L) = \partial L/
\partial x^i - \tfrac{d}{dt}(\partial L/\partial v^i)$ transforms as a covector
along $\gamma$, so the intrinsic condition is the vanishing of the Euler–Lagrang
e one-form $E(L)=0$ along the curve. Equivalently, extremals are the projections
 to $M$ of the integral curves of the Euler vector field $X_E$ on the Lagrangian
 symplectic manifold $(TM,\omega_L)$.
