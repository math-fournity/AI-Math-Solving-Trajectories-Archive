## Solution

We want to evaluate:
$$I(r) = \int_{\pi}^{0} \frac{e^{ire^{i\theta}}}{r^2 e^{i2\theta}+1}\,d\theta \q
uad \text{as } r \to 0.$$

### Step 1: Identify the contour

The parametrization $z = re^{i\theta}$ with $\theta$ running from $\pi$ to $0$ t
races the upper semicircle of radius $r$ from $z = -r$ to $z = r$ (clockwise dir
ection). This is a small semicircular indentation around $z = 0$.

### Step 2: Pointwise limit of the integrand

For each fixed $\theta \in [0,\pi]$, as $r \to 0$:

- **Numerator:** $e^{ire^{i\theta}} \to e^{0} = 1$, since $|ire^{i\theta}| = r \
to 0$.
- **Denominator:** $r^2 e^{i2\theta} + 1 \to 0 + 1 = 1$.

Therefore the integrand converges pointwise to $1$.

### Step 3: Uniform convergence on $[0,\pi]$

We show the convergence is uniform so that we may exchange limit and integral.

Using Taylor expansions valid for small $r$ (uniformly in $\theta$ since $|e^{i\
theta}|=1$):

$$e^{ire^{i\theta}} = 1 + ire^{i\theta} + O(r^2), \qquad \frac{1}{1+r^2 e^{i2\th
eta}} = 1 - r^2 e^{i2\theta} + O(r^4).$$

Thus:
$$\frac{e^{ire^{i\theta}}}{r^2 e^{i2\theta}+1} = \left(1 + ire^{i\theta} + O(r^2
)\right)\left(1 + O(r^2)\right) = 1 + ire^{i\theta} + O(r^2),$$

where the $O(r^2)$ terms are uniform in $\theta \in [0,\pi]$. Hence:
$$\sup_{\theta \in [0,\pi]}\left|\frac{e^{ire^{i\theta}}}{r^2 e^{i2\theta}+1} -
1\right| \to 0 \quad \text{as } r \to 0.$$

### Step 4: Exchange limit and integral

Since the integrand converges **uniformly** to $1$ on the fixed interval $[\pi,
0]$ (the integration is over $\theta$, not over a varying contour), we may pass
the limit inside:

$$\lim_{r\to 0} I(r) = \int_{\pi}^{0} \lim_{r\to 0}\frac{e^{ire^{i\theta}}}{r^2
e^{i2\theta}+1}\,d\theta = \int_{\pi}^{0} 1\,d\theta = 0 - \pi = -\pi.$$

### Step 5: Verification via residue calculus (cross-check)

Setting $z = re^{i\theta}$, $dz = ire^{i\theta}\,d\theta$, so $d\theta = \frac{d
z}{iz}$. The integral becomes:

$$I(r) = \frac{1}{i}\int_{C_r} \frac{e^{iz}}{z(z^2+1)}\,dz,$$

where $C_r$ is the upper semicircle of radius $r$ traversed **clockwise** (from
$\theta=\pi$ to $\theta=0$). The integrand $\frac{e^{iz}}{z(z^2+1)}$ has a simpl
e pole at $z=0$ with residue:

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{e^{iz}}{z(z^2+1)} = \lim_{z\to 0} z\cdot\frac{e^
{iz}}{z(z^2+1)} = \frac{e^{0}}{0+1} = 1.$$

For a clockwise semicircular indentation around a simple pole, the integral tend
s to $-\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}$. Therefore:

$$\lim_{r\to 0} I(r) = \frac{1}{i}\left(-\pi i \cdot 1\right) = \frac{-\pi i}{i}
 = -\pi.$$

Both methods agree.

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{\lim_{r\to 0}\int_{\pi}^{0}\frac{e^{ire^{i\theta}}}{r^2 e^{i2\theta}+1}
\,d\theta = -\pi}$$
