## Solution

We evaluate $I = \int_0^{2\pi}\cos\!\big(\sin(e^{i\theta})\big)\,d\theta$.

**Step 1: Convert to a contour integral.**

Set $z = e^{i\theta}$, so as $\theta$ runs from $0$ to $2\pi$, $z$ traverses the
 unit circle $|z|=1$ once counterclockwise. Since $dz = ie^{i\theta}\,d\theta =
iz\,d\theta$, we have $d\theta = \dfrac{dz}{iz}$. Thus

$$I = \oint_{|z|=1}\cos(\sin z)\,\frac{dz}{iz} = \frac{1}{i}\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{\
cos(\sin z)}{z}\,dz.$$

**Step 2: Identify the singularities.**

The function $\cos(\sin z)$ is entire (composition of entire functions), so the
integrand $\dfrac{\cos(\sin z)}{z}$ has a single isolated singularity: a simple
pole at $z = 0$ (inside $|z|=1$).

**Step 3: Compute the residue.**

Since $\cos(\sin z)$ is analytic at $z=0$, the residue of $\dfrac{\cos(\sin z)}{
z}$ at $z=0$ equals the constant term of the Taylor expansion of $\cos(\sin z)$,
 i.e.

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{\cos(\sin z)}{z} = \cos(\sin 0) = \cos(0) = 1.$$

(Equivalently, expand $\cos(\sin z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n(\sin z)^{2n}
}{(2n)!}$; dividing by $z$, only the $n=0$ term $\frac{1}{z}$ contributes a resi
due, namely $1$, since $(\sin z)^{2n}\big|_{z=0}=0$ for $n\geq 1$.)

**Step 4: Apply the residue theorem.**

$$I = \frac{1}{i}\cdot 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{\cos(\sin z)}{
z} = \frac{1}{i}\cdot 2\pi i \cdot 1 = 2\pi.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\!\big(\sin(e^{i\theta})\big)\,d\theta = 2\pi.}$$
