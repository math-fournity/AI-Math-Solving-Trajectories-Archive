## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. In fact, *every* open subset of ℝ² (with no topological hypothes
es whatsoever) can be expressed as a countable union of convex open subsets of ℝ
². The hypotheses "connected" and "simply connected" are unnecessary.

**Key observation.** ℝ² is separable: the set ℚ² of points with rational coordin
ates is countable and dense. Consequently ℝ² is **second countable**: the collec
tion

$$\mathcal{B} = \{ B(q, r) : q \in \mathbb{Q}^2,\; r \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0} \}$$

of open balls with rational centers and rational radii is a **countable basis**
for the standard topology on ℝ².

**Why this is a basis.** For any open set $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ and any poi
nt $x \in U$, there exists $\varepsilon > 0$ with $B(x, \varepsilon) \subseteq U
$. Choose $q \in \mathbb{Q}^2$ with $\|x - q\| < \varepsilon/3$ (density of ℚ²)
and choose $r \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ with $\|x - q\| < r < \varepsilon/2$ (density
 of ℚ in ℝ). Then:

$$x \in B(q, r) \subseteq B(x, \varepsilon) \subseteq U.$$

The first inclusion holds because for any $y \in B(q,r)$, $\|y - x\| \le \|y - q
\| + \|q - x\| < r + \varepsilon/3 < \varepsilon/2 + \varepsilon/3 < \varepsilon
$.

**Constructing the countable union.** Let $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ be any open
 set. Define

$$\mathcal{B}_U = \{ B \in \mathcal{B} : B \subseteq U \}.$$

This is a subcollection of the countable family $\mathcal{B}$, hence $\mathcal{B
}_U$ is itself **countable**. By the basis property established above, every $x
\in U$ lies in some $B \in \mathcal{B}_U$, so

$$U = \bigcup_{B \in \mathcal{B}_U} B.$$

**Convexity.** Each open ball $B(q, r)$ is **convex**: for any $y, z \in B(q,r)$
 and $t \in [0,1]$,

$$\|(ty + (1-t)z) - q\| = \|t(y-q) + (1-t)(z-q)\| \le t\|y-q\| + (1-t)\|z-q\| <
tr + (1-t)r = r.$$

Each ball is also **open in ℝ²** by definition.

**Conclusion.** We have expressed $U$ as a countable union of convex open subset
s of ℝ². Since the argument used only that $U$ is open, it applies in particular
 to every connected and simply connected open subset of ℝ². $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The conditions "connected" and "simply connected" play no role. The
result is a direct consequence of second countability of ℝ² and the convexity of
 open balls. The same argument shows that every open subset of ℝⁿ is a countable
 union of convex open sets for any $n \geq 1$.
