## Solution

**Setup.** The roots of $x^{2n}=1$ are $e^{\pi i j/n}$ for $j=0,1,\dots,2n-1$. A
 root lies in the first quadrant iff its argument $\theta=\pi j/n$ satisfies $0<
\theta<\pi/2$, i.e., $0<j<n/2$. Thus the valid indices are $j=1,2,\dots,m_n$ whe
re $m_n=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$.

**Computing $a_n$.** The product of the first-quadrant roots is:
$$a_n=\prod_{j=1}^{m_n} e^{\pi i j/n}=e^{\pi i \cdot m_n(m_n+1)/(2n)}.$$

**Values of $m_n$ and the exponent $e_n = m_n(m_n+1)/(2n)$ for $n=1,\dots,10$:**

| $n$ | $m_n$ | $e_n$ |
|-----|-------|-------|
| 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | 0 | 0 |
| 3 | 1 | $1/3$ |
| 4 | 1 | $1/4$ |
| 5 | 2 | $3/5$ |
| 6 | 2 | $1/2$ |
| 7 | 3 | $6/7$ |
| 8 | 3 | $3/4$ |
| 9 | 4 | $10/9$ |
| 10 | 4 | $1$ |

**Summing the exponents.** With LCD $= \text{lcm}(3,4,5,2,7,9)=1260$:
$$S=\sum_{n=1}^{10} e_n = \frac{420+315+756+630+1080+945+1400+1260}{1260}=\frac{
6806}{1260}=\frac{3403}{630}.$$

So $r = e^{i\pi\cdot 3403/630}$.

**Finding $k$.** We need $r^k=1$, i.e., $e^{i\pi k\cdot 3403/630}=1$, which requ
ires:
$$\frac{3403\,k}{630}=2m \quad\text{for some integer } m \quad\Longleftrightarro
w\quad 3403\,k = 1260\,m.$$

The smallest positive $k$ is $k = \dfrac{1260}{\gcd(3403,1260)}$.

**Computing the GCD** via the Euclidean algorithm:
$$3403=2\cdot1260+883,\; 1260=883+377,\; 883=2\cdot377+129,\; 377=2\cdot129+119,
\; 129=119+10,\; 119=11\cdot10+9,\; 10=9+1,\; 9=9\cdot1.$$

So $\gcd(3403,1260)=1$. (Indeed $1260=2^2\cdot3^2\cdot5\cdot7$, and $3403$ is od
d, has digit sum $10$ so is not divisible by $3$, doesn't end in $0$ or $5$, and
 $3403\equiv 1\pmod 7$.)

Therefore:
$$k = \frac{1260}{1} = 1260.$$
