## Proof

**Yes**, such a group exists. I give two examples — one elementary (trivial auto
morphism group) and one nontrivial.

### Example 1 (Elementary): $G = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$

The group $G = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ has a unique non-identity element, so the
 only automorphism is the identity. Thus $\text{Aut}(G) = 1$, the trivial group.

The trivial group is perfect: $[1, 1] = 1$, so $1 = [1,1]$. Hence $\text{Aut}(G)
$ is perfect. $\square$

### Example 2 (Nontrivial): $G = M_{11}$, the Mathieu group of degree 11

Let $G = M_{11}$, a sporadic simple group of order $7920 = 2^4 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5
 \cdot 11$.

**Step 1: $G$ is perfect.** Since $G$ is a non-abelian simple group, the commuta
tor subgroup $[G, G]$ is a normal subgroup of $G$. Because $G$ is non-abelian, $
[G, G] \neq 1$. Because $G$ is simple, $[G, G] = G$. So $G$ is perfect.

**Step 2: $Z(G) = 1$.** Since $G$ is simple and non-abelian, its center $Z(G)$ i
s a proper normal subgroup, hence $Z(G) = 1$.

**Step 3: $\text{Out}(G) = 1$.** It is a known fact that the Mathieu group $M_{1
1}$ has trivial outer automorphism group.

**Step 4: $\text{Aut}(G) \cong G$.** Every automorphism group fits into the exac
t sequence
$$1 \to \text{Inn}(G) \to \text{Aut}(G) \to \text{Out}(G) \to 1.$$
Since $\text{Out}(G) = 1$, we have $\text{Aut}(G) = \text{Inn}(G) \cong G/Z(G) =
 G/1 = G$.

**Conclusion.** Since $G$ is perfect and $\text{Aut}(G) \cong G$, the automorphi
sm group $\text{Aut}(G)$ is perfect. $\square$

### General Principle

More generally, any **complete perfect group** — a centerless group with trivial
 outer automorphism group that equals its own commutator subgroup — satisfies $\
text{Aut}(G) \cong G$ and hence has a perfect automorphism group. Every non-abel
ian simple group with trivial outer automorphism group (such as $M_{11}$, the Mo
nster group $M$, the Janko group $J_1$, the Conway group $Co_3$, etc.) is such a
