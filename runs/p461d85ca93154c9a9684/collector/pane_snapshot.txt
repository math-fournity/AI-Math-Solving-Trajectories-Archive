## Proof

**Setup.** Let $2\alpha_1, \dots, 2\alpha_5$ be the central angles subtended by
sides $a,\dots,e$, with $\alpha_i > 0$ and $\sum \alpha_i = \pi$. Each side $a_i
 = 2R\sin\alpha_i$, so
$$a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2+e^2 = 4R^2 \sum_{i=1}^5 \sin^2\alpha_i.$$
We find the supremum of $S = \sum \sin^2\alpha_i$ over $\alpha_i > 0$, $\sum \al
pha_i = \pi$.

**Merging Lemma.** For $a,b > 0$ with $a+b \le \pi/2$:
$$\sin^2(a+b) - \sin^2 a - \sin^2 b = \frac{4\cos(a+b)\sin a\sin b + 1}{2} \ge \
frac{1}{2} > 0,$$
since $\cos(a+b) \ge 0$. Hence merging two such angles strictly increases $S$.

**Reduction to 3 variables.** Among $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_5 > 0$ summing to $\p
i$, the two smallest satisfy $\alpha_4+\alpha_5 \le 2\pi/5 < \pi/2$. By the Lemm
a:
$$S \le \sin^2\alpha_1+\sin^2\alpha_2+\sin^2\alpha_3+\sin^2(\alpha_4+\alpha_5).$
$
Now four non-negative values sum to $\pi$; their two smallest sum to $\le \pi/2$
. Merging again:
$$S \le \sin^2\beta_1+\sin^2\beta_2+\sin^2\beta_3, \qquad \beta_i \ge 0,\; \beta
_1+\beta_2+\beta_3 = \pi.$$

**Maximizing over 3 variables.** We prove $\sum \sin^2\beta_i \le 9/4$.

*Case 1: Some $\beta_i \ge \pi/2$.* WLOG $\beta_1 \ge \pi/2$, so $\beta_2+\beta_
3 \le \pi/2$. Then $\sin^2\beta_1 \le 1$ and, by the Lemma plus monotonicity of
$\sin^2$ on $[0,\pi/2]$, $\sin^2\beta_2+\sin^2\beta_3 \le \sin^2(\beta_2+\beta_3
) \le 1$. So $S \le 2 < 9/4$.

*Case 2: All $\beta_i < \pi/2$.*

*Subcase 2a: All $\beta_i \ge \pi/4$.* Since $(\sin^2)'' = 2\cos 2x \le 0$ on $[
\pi/4, \pi/2]$, $\sin^2$ is concave there. By Jensen:
$$\sum \sin^2\beta_i \le 3\sin^2\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{3}\right) = 3\cdot\tfrac{3}{
4} = \tfrac{9}{4}.$$

*Subcase 2b: Some $\beta_i < \pi/4$*, say $\beta_3 < \pi/4$. Then $\beta_1+\beta
_2 = \pi-\beta_3 > 3\pi/4$, and since both $< \pi/2$, both $> \pi/4$. By concavi
ty on $[\pi/4,\pi/2]$:
$$\sin^2\beta_1+\sin^2\beta_2 \le 2\sin^2\!\left(\tfrac{\pi-\beta_3}{2}\right) =
 2\cos^2\!\left(\tfrac{\beta_3}{2}\right).$$
So $S \le 2\cos^2(\beta_3/2)+\sin^2\beta_3 = 2+\cos\beta_3-\cos^2\beta_3 =: g(\b
eta_3)$.

For $t \in (0,\pi/4)$: $g'(t) = \sin t\,(2\cos t - 1) > 0$ since $\cos t > \cos(
\pi/4) > 1/2$. Thus $g$ is increasing and
$$g(t) < g(\pi/4) = \tfrac{3}{2}+\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \approx 2.207 < \tfrac{9}{4
}.$$

**Conclusion of upper bound.** In all cases $S \le 9/4$, hence
$$a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2+e^2 \le 4R^2 \cdot \tfrac{9}{4} = 9R^2.$$

**Sharpness.** Take $\alpha_1=\alpha_2=\alpha_3 = \pi/3-\varepsilon$ and $\alpha
_4=\alpha_5 = 3\varepsilon/2$ for small $\varepsilon > 0$. This gives a valid co
nvex cyclic pentagon, and
$$S = 3\sin^2\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{3}-\varepsilon\right) + 2\sin^2\!\left(\tfrac{3
\varepsilon}{2}\right) \;\longrightarrow\; 3\cdot\tfrac{3}{4} = \tfrac{9}{4} \qu
ad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0^+.$$
So the supremum $9R^2$ is approached but never attained (the limiting figure deg
enerates to an equilateral triangle).

**Answer.** The inequality holds for all cyclic convex pentagons inscribed in $\
omega$ if and only if $\boxed{k \ge 9}$.
