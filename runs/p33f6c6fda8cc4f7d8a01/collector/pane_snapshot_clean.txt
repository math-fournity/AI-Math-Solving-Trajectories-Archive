## Proof

**Claim:** $S$ is closed as a subset of $M$.

**Setup.** Let $Q \subset \mathbb{R}^{n-1}$ be the open set of vectors with stri
ctly positive components, $R = (0,\infty) \subset \mathbb{R}$, and $M = Q \times
 R$ (an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$, equipped with the subspace topology). Let
 $f: Q \to R$ be a $C^1$ (hence continuous) strictly positive function, and let
$$S = \{(x, f(x)) : x \in Q\} \subset M$$
be its graph.

**Key theorem.** *The graph of any continuous function $g: X \to Y$, where $Y$ i
s Hausdorff, is closed in $X \times Y$.*

We apply this with $X = Q$ (a topological space under the subspace topology inhe
rited from $\mathbb{R}^{n-1}$) and $Y = R$ (a subspace of $\mathbb{R}$, hence Ha
usdorff). Since $f$ is $C^1$, it is continuous. Therefore $S = \operatorname{gra
ph}(f)$ is closed in $Q \times R = M$.

**Direct proof of the theorem (for completeness).** Let $(x_0, y_0) \in M \setmi
nus S$, so $y_0 \neq f(x_0)$. Since $R$ is Hausdorff, there exist disjoint open
neighborhoods $U \ni y_0$ and $V \ni f(x_0)$ in $R$. By continuity of $f$, the p
reimage $W = f^{-1}(V)$ is an open neighborhood of $x_0$ in $Q$. Then $W \times
U$ is an open neighborhood of $(x_0, y_0)$ in $M$. For any $(x, y) \in W \times
U$, we have $f(x) \in V$ and $y \in U$; since $U \cap V = \varnothing$, we get $
y \neq f(x)$, so $(x, y) \notin S$. Thus $W \times U \subset M \setminus S$, pro
ving $M \setminus S$ is open, hence $S$ is closed in $M$.

**Remark on the submanifold hypothesis.** The hypothesis that $S$ is an embedded
 submanifold of $M$ of codimension one is automatically satisfied for the graph
of a $C^1$ function (the map $x \mapsto (x, f(x))$ is a $C^1$ embedding with the
 projection onto the first $n-1$ coordinates as a global inverse), and it plays
no role in the closedness argument. Closedness follows purely from continuity of
 $f$ and the Hausdorff property of $R$.

**Conclusion.** $S$ is closed as a subset of $M$.
