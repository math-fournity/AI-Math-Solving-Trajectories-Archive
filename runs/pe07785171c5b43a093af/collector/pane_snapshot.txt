### Key examples

**Example A — The Cantor set $C$.**

The Cantor set is homeomorphic to $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$ (the countable product
of the discrete two-point space, with the product topology). Then:
$$C \times C \;\cong\; \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}} \times \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}} \;\cong\
; \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}\,\sqcup\,\mathbb{N}} \;\cong\; \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}} \;\con
g\; C,$$
since $\mathbb{N} \sqcup \mathbb{N}$ is countably infinite, hence in bijection w
ith $\mathbb{N}$, and the product topology is preserved under reindexing. The Ca
ntor set is:
- **Infinite:** yes.
- **Compact:** yes (closed subset of $[0,1]$, or Tychonoff).
- **Connected:** no — it is totally disconnected.
- **Subset of $\mathbb{R}$:** yes, it embeds in $[0,1]$.

**Example B — The Hilbert cube $H = [0,1]^{\mathbb{N}}$.**

Similarly:
$$H \times H \;\cong\; [0,1]^{\mathbb{N}} \times [0,1]^{\mathbb{N}} \;\cong\; [0
,1]^{\mathbb{N}\,\sqcup\,\mathbb{N}} \;\cong\; [0,1]^{\mathbb{N}} \;\cong\; H.$$
The Hilbert cube is:
- **Infinite:** yes.
- **Compact:** yes (Tychonoff's theorem — product of compact spaces).
- **Connected:** yes (product of connected spaces is connected).
- **Homeomorphic to a subset of $\mathbb{R}$:** no. The Hilbert cube has infinit
e covering dimension (it contains copies of $[0,1]^n$ for every $n$), whereas ev
ery subspace of $\mathbb{R}$ has covering dimension $\leq 1$. Since covering dim
ension is a topological invariant, $H$ cannot embed in $\mathbb{R}$.

**Example C — The rationals $\mathbb{Q}$.**

By Sierpiński's theorem, $\mathbb{Q}$ is the unique (up to homeomorphism) counta
ble metrizable space with no isolated points. The space $\mathbb{Q} \times \math
bb{Q}$ is also countable, metrizable, and has no isolated points, so $\mathbb{Q}
 \times \mathbb{Q} \cong \mathbb{Q}$. The rationals are:
- **Infinite:** yes.
- **Compact:** no.
- **Connected:** no (totally disconnected).
- **Subset of $\mathbb{R}$:** yes.

The Hilbert cube $H$ is connected, infinite, and satisfies $H \cong H \times H$.
