**Proof.**

Consider triangle $ACE$. The three diagonals $AD$, $BE$, $CF$ each pass through
a vertex of this triangle:
- $AD$ passes through $A$ and meets line $CE$ at a point $X$,
- $CF$ passes through $C$ and meets line $AE$ at a point $Z$,
- $BE$ passes through $E$ and meets line $AC$ at a point $Y$.

By Ceva's theorem (trigonometric/ratio form), $AD$, $BE$, $CF$ are concurrent if
 and only if
$$\frac{CX}{XE}\cdot\frac{EZ}{ZA}\cdot\frac{AY}{YC}=1.$$

We compute each ratio using the sine rule and the fact that $A,B,C,D,E,F$ lie on
 one circle (of radius $R$).

**Ratio $CX/XE$.** Since $X$ lies on line $CE$, the angles $\angle AXC$ and $\an
gle AXE$ are supplementary, so their sines are equal. Applying the sine rule in
$\triangle AXC$ and $\triangle AXE$:
$$\frac{CX}{XE}=\frac{AC\cdot\sin\angle CAX}{AE\cdot\sin\angle XAE}=\frac{AC\cdo
t\sin\angle CAD}{AE\cdot\sin\angle DAE}.$$
Since $A,C,D,E$ are concyclic, $\angle CAD=\angle CED$ (both subtend arc $CD$) a
nd $\angle DAE=\angle DCE$ (both subtend arc $DE$). By the sine rule in $\triang
le CDE$,
$$\frac{\sin\angle CED}{\sin\angle DCE}=\frac{CD}{DE}.$$
Hence
$$\frac{CX}{XE}=\frac{AC\cdot CD}{AE\cdot DE}.$$

**Ratio $AY/YC$.** With $Y$ on line $AC$, the angles $\angle AYB$ and $\angle BY
C$ are supplementary. The sine rule in $\triangle BYA$ and $\triangle BYC$ gives
$$\frac{AY}{YC}=\frac{AB\cdot\sin\angle ABE}{BC\cdot\sin\angle EBC}.$$
The inscribed angle $\angle ABE$ subtends arc $AE$, so $\sin\angle ABE=\frac{AE}
{2R}$; likewise $\angle EBC$ subtends arc $EC$, so $\sin\angle EBC=\frac{EC}{2R}
$. Thus
$$\frac{AY}{YC}=\frac{AB\cdot AE}{BC\cdot EC}.$$

**Ratio $EZ/ZA$.** With $Z$ on line $AE$, the angles $\angle EZC$ and $\angle CZ
A$ are supplementary. The sine rule in $\triangle CZE$ and $\triangle CZA$ gives
$$\frac{EZ}{ZA}=\frac{EC\cdot\sin\angle ECF}{CA\cdot\sin\angle FCA}.$$
Here $\angle ECF$ subtends arc $EF$, so $\sin\angle ECF=\frac{EF}{2R}$, and $\an
gle FCA$ subtends arc $FA$, so $\sin\angle FCA=\frac{FA}{2R}$. Thus
$$\frac{EZ}{ZA}=\frac{EC\cdot EF}{CA\cdot FA}.$$

**The Ceva product.** Multiplying the three ratios:
$$\frac{CX}{XE}\cdot\frac{EZ}{ZA}\cdot\frac{AY}{YC}=\frac{AC\cdot CD}{AE\cdot DE
}\cdot\frac{EC\cdot EF}{CA\cdot FA}\cdot\frac{AB\cdot AE}{BC\cdot EC}.$$
Cancelling $AC=CA$, $EC$, and $AE$:
$$=\frac{AB\cdot CD\cdot EF}{BC\cdot DE\cdot FA}.$$
By the hypothesis $AB\cdot CD\cdot EF = BC\cdot DE\cdot FA$, this product equals
 $1$.

By Ceva's theorem, the cevians $AD$, $CF$, $BE$ of triangle $ACE$ are concurrent
. Therefore the diagonals $AD$, $BE$, and $CF$ are concurrent. $\blacksquare$
