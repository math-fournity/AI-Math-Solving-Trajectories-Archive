Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a nonconstant morphism $f:
X_0(p) \to E$ (for prime $p$) forces $\operatorname{cond}(E) = p$.

## Proof

**Setup and interpretation.** We work over $\mathbb{Q}$. The modular curve $X_0(
p)$ is a smooth projective curve over $\mathbb{Q}$, and $E$ is an elliptic curve
 over $\mathbb{Q}$. We interpret "morphism" as a *nonconstant* morphism defined
over $\mathbb{Q}$ (a constant morphism is trivial and carries no information). W
e will show that $\operatorname{cond}(E) = p$.

**Step 1: Factoring through the Jacobian.**

The curve $X_0(p)$ has rational cusps; pick one as a base point $c_0 \in X_0(p)(
\mathbb{Q})$. Given a nonconstant morphism $f: X_0(p) \to E$, define
$$\varphi: X_0(p) \to E, \qquad c \mapsto f(c) - f(c_0).$$
By the universal property of the Jacobian (Albanese variety of a curve), $\varph
i$ extends uniquely to a homomorphism of abelian varieties
$$\widetilde{\varphi}: J_0(p) := \operatorname{Jac}(X_0(p)) \longrightarrow E.$$
Since $f$ is nonconstant, $\widetilde{\varphi} \neq 0$. An elliptic curve is sim
ple as an abelian variety (it has dimension 1, so its only abelian subvarieties
are $0$ and itself), hence any nonzero homomorphism *to* $E$ is surjective. Ther
efore $E$ is a quotient of $J_0(p)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Step 2: Elliptic curve quotients of $J_0(N)$.**

By the Eichler–Shimura theory and the new/old decomposition, the Jacobian $J_0(N
)$ decomposes (up to isogeny) as
$$J_0(N) \sim \bigoplus_{M \mid N} J_0(M)^{\mathrm{new}}$$
where $J_0(M)^{\mathrm{new}}$ is the new part at level $M$. An elliptic curve $E
/\mathbb{Q}$ of conductor $M_0$ corresponds to a normalized newform of weight 2,
 level $M_0$, and rational coefficients, and it appears as a quotient of $J_0(M_
0)^{\mathrm{new}}$.

Consequently:
- ($\Leftarrow$) If $\operatorname{cond}(E) = M_0$ and $M_0 \mid N$, then $E$ is
 a quotient of $J_0(M_0)^{\mathrm{new}}$, which embeds into $J_0(N)$, so $E$ is
a quotient of $J_0(N)$.
- ($\Rightarrow$) If $E$ is a quotient of $J_0(N)$, then $E$ arises from a newfo
rm of some level $M_0 \mid N$, and by the Eichler–Shimura–Deligne theory the con
ductor of the associated $\ell$-adic Galois representation — hence the conductor
 of $E$ — equals $M_0$. Thus $\operatorname{cond}(E) \mid N$.

In summary: **$E$ is a quotient of $J_0(N)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ if and only if $\o
peratorname{cond}(E) \mid N$.**

**Step 3: Applying to $N = p$.**

From Steps 1 and 2, $\operatorname{cond}(E) \mid p$. Since $p$ is prime, $\opera
torname{cond}(E) \in \{1, p\}$.

**Step 4: Ruling out conductor 1.**

There are no elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$ with conductor 1. Indeed, by the
modularity theorem (Wiles–Taylor–Breuil–Conrad–Diamond), every elliptic curve $E
/\mathbb{Q}$ of conductor $N$ is modular, i.e., it arises from a newform in $S_2
(\Gamma_0(N))$. For $N = 1$, the space $S_2(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}))$ has dime
nsion $0$ (the modular curve $X(1)$ has genus $0$), so no such newform exists. E
quivalently, there are no elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$ with everywhere good
 reduction.

(One can also see this without the full modularity theorem: an elliptic curve ov
er $\mathbb{Q}$ with conductor 1 would have good reduction at every prime, forci
ng the minimal discriminant $\Delta_{\min} = \pm 1$; but no elliptic curve over
$\mathbb{Q}$ has $|\Delta_{\min}| = 1$, as follows from a 2-descent or from the
fact that $|\Delta_{\min}| \geq 11$ for all elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$.)

Therefore $\operatorname{cond}(E) \neq 1$, and we conclude $\operatorname{cond}(
E) = p$.

**Remark on small primes.** For $p \in \{2, 3, 5, 7, 13\}$, $X_0(p)$ has genus $
0$, so no nonconstant morphism to an elliptic curve exists — the hypothesis is v
acuously unsatisfiable, and the implication holds trivially. For $p = 11, 17, 19
$, $X_0(p)$ has genus $1$, so $X_0(p)$ is itself an elliptic curve of conductor
$p$, consistent with the conclusion. For $p \geq 23$ (genus $\geq 2$), the argum
ent above applies directly.

## Conclusion

Yes. The existence of a nonconstant morphism $f: X_0(p) \to E$ over $\mathbb{Q}$
 implies that the conductor of $E$ is $p$.
