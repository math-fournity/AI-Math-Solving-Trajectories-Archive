## Proof

Let \(W_t\) be a standard Brownian motion at time \(t\). By definition, \(W_t \s
im \mathcal{N}(0, t)\), i.e., \(W_t\) is normally distributed with mean \(0\) an
d variance \(t\).

We compute the third moment:

\[
E(W_t^3) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x^3 \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}} e^{-x^2/(2t
)}\, dx.
\]

The density \(f(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}} e^{-x^2/(2t)}\) is an **even functi
on** of \(x\), while \(x^3\) is an **odd function** of \(x\). Their product \(x^
3 f(x)\) is therefore an **odd function**.

The integral of an odd function over the symmetric domain \((-\infty, \infty)\)
is zero, provided the integral converges. Convergence holds here since the Gauss
ian density decays faster than any polynomial grows, so \(x^3 f(x)\) is absolute
ly integrable.

Therefore:

\[
E(W_t^3) = 0.
\]

**Alternative verification via the moment generating function.** The MGF of \(W_
t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,t)\) is

\[
M(\lambda) = E(e^{\lambda W_t}) = e^{\lambda^2 t / 2}.
\]

The third moment is the third derivative evaluated at zero:

\[
E(W_t^3) = M'''(0).
\]

Computing:

\[
M'(\lambda) = \lambda t\, e^{\lambda^2 t/2}, \qquad
M''(\lambda) = (t + \lambda^2 t^2)\, e^{\lambda^2 t/2}, \qquad
M'''(\lambda) = (3\lambda t^2 + \lambda^3 t^3)\, e^{\lambda^2 t/2}.
\]

Evaluating at \(\lambda = 0\):

\[
M'''(0) = 0.
\]

Both methods confirm:

\[
\boxed{E(W_t^3) = 0.}
\]
