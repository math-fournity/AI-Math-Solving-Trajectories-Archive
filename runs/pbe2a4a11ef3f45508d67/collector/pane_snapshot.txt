Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether there exists a metric on ℝ
such that every non-empty open set in the induced topology contains a standard E
uclidean open interval (a,b).

**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

Consider the standard Euclidean metric on ℝ:

$$d(x, y) = |x - y|.$$

This is a well-defined metric on ℝ (non-negativity, identity of indiscernibles,
symmetry, and the triangle inequality all follow from the corresponding properti
es of the absolute value).

The topology induced by $d$ is the Euclidean topology, whose base consists of al
l open intervals $(a, b)$ with $a < b$—equivalently, the collection of all open
balls $B(x, \varepsilon) = (x - \varepsilon, x + \varepsilon)$.

Now let $U$ be any **non-empty** open set in this topology. I claim $U$ contains
 an interval of the form $(a, b)$.

Since $U$ is non-empty, there exists a point $x \in U$. Since $U$ is open in the
 metric topology, by definition there exists $\varepsilon > 0$ such that the ope
n ball

$$B(x, \varepsilon) = (x - \varepsilon,\; x + \varepsilon) \subseteq U.$$

Setting $a = x - \varepsilon$ and $b = x + \varepsilon$, we have $a < b$ and

$$(a, b) \subseteq U.$$

Therefore every non-empty open set in the topology induced by $d$ contains an in
terval of the form $(a, b)$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The Euclidean metric is the canonical example. More generally, any m
etric on ℝ that induces a topology **coarser than or equal to** the Euclidean to
pology (in the sense that every open set is a union of Euclidean intervals) sati
sfies the condition. For instance, the bounded metric $d'(x,y) = \min(|x-y|, 1)$
 induces the same topology and also works. One can also construct metrics induci
ng **strictly coarser** metrizable topologies that still satisfy the condition—f
or example, the pullback of the circle metric via the quotient map $\pi: \mathbb
{R} \to \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$, where every non-empty open set is a union of per
iodic intervals and thus contains a Euclidean interval.
