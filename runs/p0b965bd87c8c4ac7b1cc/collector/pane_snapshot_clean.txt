No. A pointwise limit of Riemann integrable functions need not be Riemann integr

**Counterexample.** Work on the interval $[0,1]$. Enumerate the rationals in $[0
,1]$ as a sequence $\{r_1, r_2, r_3, \dots\}$. For each $n \geq 1$, define

$$
f_n(x) = \begin{cases} 1, & x \in \{r_1, r_2, \dots, r_n\},\\ 0, & \text{otherwi
se.} \end{cases}
$$

Each $f_n$ is Riemann integrable on $[0,1]$: it is bounded, and its set of disco
ntinuities is the finite set $\{r_1,\dots,r_n\}$, which has measure zero. By Leb
esgue's criterion (a bounded function on a compact interval is Riemann integrabl
e iff its discontinuities form a measure-zero set), $f_n$ is Riemann integrable,
 with $\int_0^1 f_n = 0$.

Now consider the pointwise limit. For any $x \in [0,1]$:

- If $x$ is rational, say $x = r_k$, then for all $n \geq k$ we have $f_n(x) = 1
$, so $f_n(x) \to 1$.
- If $x$ is irrational, then $f_n(x) = 0$ for every $n$, so $f_n(x) \to 0$.

Thus $f_n \to f$ pointwise, where

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases} 1, & x \in \mathbb{Q} \cap [0,1],\\ 0, & x \in [0,1] \setmi
nus \mathbb{Q}. \end{cases}
$$

This is the Dirichlet function. It is **not** Riemann integrable: every lower Da
rboux sum is $0$ (every interval contains an irrational) and every upper Darboux
 sum is $1$ (every interval contains a rational), so the lower and upper integra
ls are $0$ and $1$ respectively, and they do not agree.

Therefore the pointwise limit of a sequence of Riemann integrable functions is n
ot necessarily Riemann integrable.
