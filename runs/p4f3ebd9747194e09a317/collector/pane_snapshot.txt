## Proof

We compute the Gaussian curvature of the metric
\[
ds^2 = \frac{4\,(dx^2+dy^2)}{(1-x^2-y^2)^2}
\]
on the unit disk \(D=\{(x,y):x^2+y^2<1\}\).

**Conformal form.** The metric is conformal to the Euclidean metric:
\[
ds^2 = e^{2u}(dx^2+dy^2),\qquad e^{2u}=\frac{4}{(1-r^2)^2},\quad r^2=x^2+y^2.
\]
Hence
\[
u = \ln 2 - \ln(1-r^2).
\]

**Curvature formula for a conformal metric.** For \(ds^2=e^{2u}(dx^2+dy^2)\) in
dimension 2, the Gaussian curvature is
\[
K = -e^{-2u}\,\Delta u,
\]
where \(\Delta = \partial_x^2+\partial_y^2\) is the Euclidean Laplacian.

**Compute \(\Delta u\).** Since \(u=\ln 2 - \ln(1-r^2)\) and \(\ln 2\) is consta
nt,
\[
\Delta u = -\Delta\ln(1-r^2).
\]
Let \(f=\ln(1-r^2)\). Then
\[
f_x = \frac{-2x}{1-r^2},\qquad
f_{xx} = \frac{-2}{1-r^2} - \frac{4x^2}{(1-r^2)^2},
\]
and symmetrically
\[
f_{yy} = \frac{-2}{1-r^2} - \frac{4y^2}{(1-r^2)^2}.
\]
Adding,
\[
\Delta f = \frac{-4}{1-r^2} - \frac{4(x^2+y^2)}{(1-r^2)^2}
        = \frac{-4(1-r^2)-4r^2}{(1-r^2)^2}
        = \frac{-4}{(1-r^2)^2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\Delta u = -\Delta f = \frac{4}{(1-r^2)^2}.
\]

**Compute \(K\).** Since
\[
e^{-2u} = \frac{(1-r^2)^2}{4},
\]
we obtain
\[
K = -e^{-2u}\,\Delta u
  = -\,\frac{(1-r^2)^2}{4}\cdot\frac{4}{(1-r^2)^2}
  = -1.
\]

Thus the Gaussian curvature is constant and equals \(-1\) everywhere on the unit
 disk.

**Verification at \((0,0)\).** At the origin, \(r=0\), so
\[
e^{2u}\big|_{(0,0)} = 4,\qquad \Delta u\big|_{(0,0)} = 4,\qquad e^{-2u}\big|_{(0
,0)}=\tfrac14,
\]
and
\[
K(0,0) = -\tfrac14\cdot 4 = -1.
\]

This confirms that the curvature at \((0,0)\) is \(-1\), as required. ∎
