## Proof

Let $F(x) = \int_0^x \frac{\sin t}{t}\,dt - x$ and $G(x) = x^3$.

**Step 1: Identify the indeterminate form.**

The integral $\int_0^x \frac{\sin t}{t}\,dt = \operatorname{Si}(x)$ is the sine
integral, which converges to $\frac{\pi}{2}$ as $x \to \infty$ (a standard resul
t, since $\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin t}{t}\,dt = \frac{\pi}{2}$, proved by the Dir
ichlet test or by evaluating $\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_0^R\frac{\sin t}{t}dt$ via t
he identity $\frac{\sin t}{t} = \int_0^1 \cos(tu)\,du$ and Fubini).

Therefore:
$$F(x) = \operatorname{Si}(x) - x \;\longrightarrow\; \tfrac{\pi}{2} - \infty =
-\infty, \qquad G(x) = x^3 \;\longrightarrow\; +\infty.$$

This is a $\frac{-\infty}{+\infty}$ indeterminate form, so L'Hôpital's Rule appl
ies.

**Step 2: Apply L'Hôpital's Rule.**

By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus:
$$F'(x) = \frac{\sin x}{x} - 1, \qquad G'(x) = 3x^2.$$

We examine:
$$\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{F'(x)}{G'(x)} = \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\dfrac{\si
n x}{x} - 1}{3x^2}.$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the limit of the derivative ratio.**

Since $|\sin x| \le 1$ for all $x$:
$$\left|\frac{\sin x}{x}\right| \le \frac{1}{x} \;\longrightarrow\; 0 \quad \tex
t{as } x \to \infty.$$

Hence the numerator satisfies:
$$\frac{\sin x}{x} - 1 \;\longrightarrow\; 0 - 1 = -1.$$

The numerator is therefore **bounded** (it converges to $-1$, and in particular
$|\frac{\sin x}{x} - 1| \le \frac{1}{x} + 1 \le 2$ for $x \ge 1$).

The denominator $3x^2 \to +\infty$.

A bounded quantity divided by a quantity tending to $+\infty$ tends to $0$:
$$\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\dfrac{\sin x}{x} - 1}{3x^2} = 0.$$

**Step 4: Conclude by L'Hôpital's Rule.**

Since:
- $F(x) \to -\infty$ and $G(x) \to +\infty$ (the $\infty/\infty$ hypothesis is s
atisfied),
- $F'(x)$ and $G'(x)$ exist for all $x > 0$, with $G'(x) = 3x^2 \neq 0$,
- $\lim_{x\to\infty} \frac{F'(x)}{G'(x)} = 0$ exists,

L'Hôpital's Rule gives:

$$\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\int_0^x \frac{\sin t}{t}\,dt - x}{x^3} = \lim_{x \t
o \infty} \frac{F'(x)}{G'(x)} = 0.$$

$$\boxed{\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\int_0^x\frac{\sin t}{t}\,dt - x}{x^3} = 0}$$

**Intuition:** For large $x$, $\operatorname{Si}(x) \approx \frac{\pi}{2}$ (a co
nstant), so the numerator $\operatorname{Si}(x) - x \approx -x$ grows only linea
rly, while the denominator $x^3$ grows cubically. The ratio $\sim -1/x^2 \to 0$.
