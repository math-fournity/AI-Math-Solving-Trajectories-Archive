## Proof

We compute the two-dimensional Lebesgue measure (area) of the set
$$A = \{(x,y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 : xy = 1\}.$$

**Claim.** The Lebesgue measure of $A$ is $0$.

**Proof.** The set $A$ is the graph of the function $f : \mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\
} \to \mathbb{R}$ defined by $f(x) = 1/x$. We show that the graph of any continu
ous function on an open subset of $\mathbb{R}$ has two-dimensional Lebesgue meas
ure zero.

**Step 1. Reduce to a bounded region.** Write $\mathbb{R}^2 = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\in
fty} [-n,n]^2$, so
$$A = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} \bigl(A \cap [-n,n]^2\bigr).$$
By countable subadditivity it suffices to show each $A \cap [-n,n]^2$ has measur
e zero. On $[-n,n]$, the function $f(x)=1/x$ is defined and continuous on $[-n,n
]\setminus\{0\}$, which is the union of two intervals $[-n,0)$ and $(0,n]$. It s
uffices to handle each interval separately; we treat $I = (0, n]$, the other bei
ng analogous.

**Step 2. Cover the graph by small rectangles.** Let $I = (0, n]$ and let $\vare
psilon > 0$. Since $f$ is continuous on $I$, it is uniformly continuous on every
 compact subinterval $[\delta, n]$ for $\delta > 0$. We split $I$ into two parts
: $(0, \delta)$ and $[\delta, n]$, choosing $\delta$ small enough so that the co
ntribution from $(0,\delta)$ is controlled.

On $(0, \delta)$: the graph lies in the rectangle $(0, \delta) \times [1/\delta,
 \infty)$. To control the vertical extent, note that for $x \in (0,\delta)$, $f(
x) = 1/x \geq 1/\delta$. The graph over $(0,\delta)$ is contained in the strip $
(0,\delta)\times\mathbb{R}$, which has area $\delta \cdot \infty$—this is not di
rectly useful, so instead we use the uniform-continuity covering argument on the
 whole of $I$ directly.

**Cleaner argument (uniform continuity on compact sets).** Fix $n$ and $\varepsi
lon > 0$. For each $m \geq 1$, consider the compact interval $I_m = [1/m,\, n]$.
 The function $f(x) = 1/x$ is continuous on $I_m$, hence uniformly continuous. P
artition $I_m$ into subintervals of length $\ell$ (to be chosen). On each subint
erval $[x_{k-1}, x_k]$ of length $\ell$, the oscillation of $f$ is at most
$$\omega_k := \sup_{x \in [x_{k-1},x_k]} f(x) - \inf_{x \in [x_{k-1},x_k]} f(x).
$$
Since $f$ is decreasing, $\omega_k = f(x_{k-1}) - f(x_k) = \frac{1}{x_{k-1}} - \
frac{1}{x_k} = \frac{x_k - x_{k-1}}{x_{k-1}x_k} \leq \frac{\ell}{(1/m)^2} = m^2
\ell.$

The graph of $f$ over $[x_{k-1}, x_k]$ is contained in the rectangle
$$[x_{k-1}, x_k] \times [f(x_k) - \omega_k,\; f(x_{k-1})],$$
which has area $\ell \cdot \omega_k \leq \ell \cdot m^2 \ell = m^2 \ell^2$.

Summing over all $\lceil m \cdot n \rceil$ subintervals (since $|I_m| = n - 1/m
\leq n$, the number of subintervals is at most $mn/\ell + 1$), the total area of
 the covering rectangles is at most
$$\frac{mn}{\ell}\cdot m^2 \ell^2 = m^3 n \,\ell.$$
Choosing $\ell = \varepsilon / (m^3 n)$ makes this at most $\varepsilon$.

**Step 3. Handle the part near zero.** The remaining part of the graph over $(0,
 1/m)$ is contained in the rectangle $(0, 1/m) \times [m, \infty)$. To control t
his, cover it by the rectangle $(0, 1/m) \times [m, M]$ for large $M$, plus the
part above $M$. The part over $(0, 1/m)$ with $y > M$ corresponds to $x < 1/M$,
contained in $(0, 1/M) \times [M, \infty)$, which has infinite height—again not
directly useful.

Instead, observe that the graph over $(0, 1/m)$ is itself the graph of a continu
ous function on the open interval $(0, 1/m)$. We can repeat the argument of Step
 2 on $[1/m', 1/m]$ for $m' > m$ and let $m' \to \infty$. Equivalently, the grap
h over $(0, 1/m)$ is the countable union of graphs over $[1/(m+k), 1/(m+k-1)]$ f
or $k = 1, 2, \ldots$, each of which is a graph of a continuous function on a co
mpact interval, hence has measure zero by Step 2. By countable subadditivity, th
e graph over $(0, 1/m)$ has measure zero.

**Step 4. Conclude.** Combining Steps 2 and 3, the graph of $f$ over $(0, n]$ ha
s measure zero. By the same argument, the graph over $[-n, 0)$ has measure zero.
 Hence $A \cap [-n,n]^2$ has measure zero for every $n$. By countable subadditiv
ity,
$$m(A) = m\!\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A \cap [-n,n]^2\right) \leq \sum_{n=1}^
{\infty} 0 = 0.$$

Since Lebesgue measure is non-negative, $m(A) = 0$.

**Remark.** More generally, the graph of any continuous function $g : U \to \mat
hbb{R}$ where $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ is open has two-dimensional Lebesgue meas
ure zero, by exactly this argument (cover by countably many compact subintervals
, use uniform continuity on each). The set $A$ is such a graph (in two pieces),
so its measure is $0$.

$$\boxed{m(A) = 0}$$
