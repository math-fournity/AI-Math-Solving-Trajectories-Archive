## Answer

**Yes**, $M$ and $N$ must have the same dimension.

## Proof

**Key fact.** Let $M$ be a closed, connected, smooth $n$-manifold. Then with $\m
athbb{Z}/2$ coefficients:
- $H_n(M;\,\mathbb{Z}/2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$, and
- $H_k(M;\,\mathbb{Z}/2) = 0$ for all $k > n$.

*Justification.* Every closed connected $n$-manifold is $\mathbb{Z}/2$-orientabl
e, so it carries a $\mathbb{Z}/2$-fundamental class $[M] \in H_n(M;\,\mathbb{Z}/
2)$, giving $H_n(M;\,\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong \mathbb{Z}/2$. For the vanishing above d
imension $n$: every smooth manifold admits a compatible triangulation (Cairns–Wh
itehead), so a closed smooth $n$-manifold is a finite simplicial complex — hence
 a finite CW complex — of dimension exactly $n$. A CW complex of dimension $n$ h
as trivial cellular (and therefore singular) homology in degrees $> n$. $\square
$

**Consequence.** For a closed, connected, smooth manifold $M$,
$$\dim M \;=\; \max\bigl\{\,k \ge 0 \;:\; H_k(M;\,\mathbb{Z}/2) \neq 0\,\bigr\}.
$$
The right-hand side is well-defined because the top group $H_{\dim M}(M;\,\mathb
b{Z}/2)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2$ is nonzero and all higher groups vanish.

**Applying homotopy equivalence.** Since $M$ and $N$ are homotopy equivalent, th
ey have isomorphic homology groups in every degree:
$$H_k(M;\,\mathbb{Z}/2) \;\cong\; H_k(N;\,\mathbb{Z}/2) \qquad \text{for all } k
 \ge 0.$$
Therefore the maximum degree in which mod-2 homology is nonzero is the same for
both:
$$\max\{k : H_k(M;\,\mathbb{Z}/2)\neq 0\} \;=\; \max\{k : H_k(N;\,\mathbb{Z}/2)\
neq 0\}.$$
By the consequence above, the left side equals $\dim M$ and the right side equal
s $\dim N$. Hence
$$\dim M \;=\; \dim N. \qquad \blacksquare$$
