## Setup and Rules

Starting value $n \in \{2, 3, \dots, 1000\}$. Each cycle: A moves, then F moves.
 After each move, check win conditions.

**A's options:** always add 4; if $n$ is even, may divide by 2 instead.
**F's options:** always subtract 2; if $n$ is odd, may multiply by 2 instead.

**A wins** if $n$ reaches 0 or 1 at any point. **F wins** if $n > 1000$ at any p
oint, or if A fails to reach $\{0,1\}$ within 100 cycles.

## Key Structural Observation

The critical asymmetry: **when $n$ is even, the player whose turn it is has a ch
oice; when $n$ is odd, the player is forced** (A must add 4; F must subtract 2 —
 wait, F *can* multiply by 2 when odd). Let me restate precisely:

- **A's turn, $n$ even:** A chooses $n/2$ or $n+4$.
- **A's turn, $n$ odd:** A must do $n+4$ (forced).
- **F's turn, $n$ even:** F must do $n-2$ (forced, since $n$ is even, F cannot m
ultiply by 2).
- **F's turn, $n$ odd:** F chooses $n-2$ or $2n$.

## Odd Starting Values: F Wins

**Claim:** For every odd $n \in \{3, 5, \dots, 999\}$, F has a winning strategy:
 **always subtract 2**.

*Proof.* When $n$ is odd on A's turn, A is forced to add 4, giving $n+4$ (odd).
F then subtracts 2, giving $n+2$ (odd). The value at the start of the next A-tur
n is $n+2$, which is odd and **larger** than $n$.

The sequence of values at A's turns is: $n_0,\; n_0+2,\; n_0+4,\; \dots$ — stric
tly increasing, always odd, always $\geq 3$. Since all values are odd and $\geq
3$, the value **never** reaches 0 (even) or 1 (too small and increasing). After
100 cycles, A has not reached $\{0,1\}$, so F wins by timeout. (For large odd $n
$, F may also win earlier by exceeding 1000.)

F's strategy never accidentally creates $\{0,1\}$: F subtracts 2 from values $\g
eq 7$ (since A first adds 4 to $n \geq 3$), producing values $\geq 5$. $\square$

## Even Starting Values: A Wins

**Claim:** For every even $n \in \{2, 4, 6, \dots, 1000\}$, A has a winning stra
tegy.

**A's strategy:** Keep $n$ even at every A-turn.
- If $n \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$: divide by 2 → $n/2$ (even). F is forced to subtract
2 → $n/2 - 2$ (even, since $n/2$ is even).
- If $n \equiv 2 \pmod{4}$: add 4 → $n+4$ (even). F is forced to subtract 2 → $n
+2$ (even, and $\equiv 0 \pmod{4}$).

**F never has a choice**, because $n$ is always even on F's turn. A completely c
ontrols the game.

**Termination:** The recurrence is:
- $n \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$, $n \geq 8$: $n \to n/2 - 2$ (roughly halves $n$).
- $n \equiv 2 \pmod{4}$, $n \geq 6$: $n \to n+2 \to (n+2)/2 - 2 = n/2 - 1$ (two
cycles, roughly halves $n$).

Base cases: $n=2$: A divides by 2 → 1, wins. $n=4$: A divides by 2 → 2, F forced
 to 0, A wins.

All intermediate values remain even and $\geq 2$, and the value roughly halves e
very 1–2 cycles. For $n \leq 1000$, this takes at most $\sim 11$ cycles — well w
ithin 100.

**Verification of examples:**
- $n=6$: $6 \to 8 \to 2 \to$ win (3 cycles).
- $n=1000$: $1000 \to 498 \to 500 \to 248 \to 122 \to 124 \to 60 \to 28 \to 12 \
to 4 \to$ win (10 cycles).
- $n=998$: $998 \to 1000 \to \cdots \to$ win (11 cycles).

Since A has a winning strategy from every even $n$, F **cannot** guarantee a win
 from any even $n$. So no even $n$ belongs to $S$.

## Computing the Sum

$$S = \{3, 5, 7, 9, \dots, 999\}$$

This is an arithmetic sequence with first term 3, last term 999, common differen
ce 2.

**Number of terms:** $\frac{999 - 3}{2} + 1 = 498 + 1 = 499$.

**Sum:**
$$\sum_{n \in S} n = 499 \cdot \frac{3 + 999}{2} = 499 \cdot 501 = (500-1)(500+1
) = 500^2 - 1 = 250000 - 1 = \boxed{249999}$$
