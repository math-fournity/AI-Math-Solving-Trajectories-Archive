## Proof

**Claim.** The number of maximal ideals of the form $(x-a, y-b)$ in $\mathbb{C}[
x,y]$ containing a given irreducible polynomial $f(x,y)$ is **infinite**.

---

**Step 1. Reduction to counting points on $V(f)$.**

Since $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraically closed, the weak Nullstellensatz gives a bij
ection
$$
\mathbb{C}^2 \longrightarrow \{\text{maximal ideals of } \mathbb{C}[x,y]\}, \qqu
ad (a,b) \mapsto (x-a,\, y-b).
$$
Moreover, $f \in (x-a, y-b)$ if and only if $f(a,b) = 0$ (the evaluation homomor
phism $\mathbb{C}[x,y] \to \mathbb{C}$, $x\mapsto a$, $y\mapsto b$, has kernel $
(x-a,y-b)$). Hence the number of maximal ideals $(x-a,y-b)$ containing $f$ equal
s the cardinality of the affine variety
$$
V(f) = \{(a,b) \in \mathbb{C}^2 : f(a,b) = 0\}.
$$

**Step 2. $f$ is nonconstant.**

An irreducible element of $\mathbb{C}[x,y]$ is by definition nonzero and non-uni
t, hence nonconstant (the only units are the nonzero constants). So $\deg f \geq
 1$.

**Step 3. $V(f)$ is infinite.**

We treat two cases.

*Case A: $f$ depends only on $x$.* Then $f \in \mathbb{C}[x]$, and irreducibilit
y in $\mathbb{C}[x,y]$ forces irreducibility in $\mathbb{C}[x]$. Since $\mathbb{
C}$ is algebraically closed, every irreducible in $\mathbb{C}[x]$ is linear: $f
= x - a_0$ for some $a_0 \in \mathbb{C}$. Then
$$
V(f) = \{(a_0, b) : b \in \mathbb{C}\},
$$
which is in bijection with $\mathbb{C}$ and hence infinite.

*Case B: $f$ depends on $y$.* Write $f$ as a polynomial in $y$ with coefficients
 in $\mathbb{C}[x]$:
$$
f(x,y) = g_n(x)\, y^n + g_{n-1}(x)\, y^{n-1} + \cdots + g_0(x), \qquad n \geq 1,
\quad g_n(x) \neq 0.
$$
The leading coefficient $g_n \in \mathbb{C}[x]$ is a nonzero polynomial, so it h
as only finitely many zeros. For every $a \in \mathbb{C}$ with $g_n(a) \neq 0$,
the specialized polynomial $f(a, y) \in \mathbb{C}[y]$ has degree $n \geq 1$. Si
nce $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraically closed, $f(a,y)$ has at least one root $b_a \i
n \mathbb{C}$, giving a point $(a, b_a) \in V(f)$.

There are infinitely many such $a$ (all of $\mathbb{C}$ minus finitely many poin
ts), and distinct $a$'s give distinct points (their first coordinates differ). T
herefore $V(f)$ is infinite.

**Conclusion.** In both cases $V(f)$ is infinite, so the number of maximal ideal
s $(x-a, y-b)$ containing $f$ is **infinite**. $\blacksquare$
