## Proof

Let $R_n = \underbrace{11\ldots1}_{n} = \frac{10^n-1}{9}$ denote the repunit wit
h $n$ ones. The number consisting of 100 threes is
$$N = \underbrace{33\ldots3}_{100} = 3\,R_{100} = 3\cdot\frac{10^{100}-1}{9} = \
frac{10^{100}-1}{3}.$$

We seek the smallest $n$ such that $N \mid R_n$.

**Step 1: The factor $R_{100}$.**
Since $\gcd(10,9)=1$ and $R_m = \frac{10^m-1}{9}$, the standard repunit divisibi
lity fact gives
$$R_a \mid R_b \iff a \mid b.$$
Hence $R_{100}\mid R_n$ requires $100\mid n$.

**Step 2: The factor $3$.**
We also need $3\mid R_n$. The digit sum of $R_n$ is $n$, so $3\mid R_n \iff 3\mi
d n$.

**Step 3: Combining.**
We need both $100\mid n$ and $3\mid n$, i.e. $\operatorname{lcm}(100,3)=300\mid
n$.

**Step 4: Sufficiency.** For $n=300$: $100\mid 300$ gives $R_{100}\mid R_{300}$,
 and $3\mid 300$ gives $3\mid R_{300}$. Since $R_{100}\equiv 1\pmod 3$ (its digi
t sum $100\equiv 1\pmod 3$), the factors $3$ and $R_{100}$ are coprime. Therefor
e $3R_{100}\mid R_{300}$, i.e. $N\mid R_{300}$.

**Step 5: Minimality.** Any $n$ with $N\mid R_n$ must satisfy $300\mid n$ by Ste
ps 1–3, so $n\ge 300$.

Thus the smallest whole number consisting only of the digit $1$ and divisible by
 $\underbrace{33\ldots3}_{100}$ is the number with **300 ones**, and there is **
no** smaller such number.
