## Proof that $n = 2$

### Step 1: One element is insufficient ($n \geq 2$)

For any $g \in SO_4(\mathbb{R})$, the cyclic subgroup $\langle g \rangle = \{g^k
 : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ is abelian. Since multiplication and inversion are contin
uous, the closure $\overline{\langle g \rangle}$ is also abelian (if $a = \lim a
_i$, $b = \lim b_i$ with $a_i, b_i \in \langle g \rangle$, then $ab = \lim a_i b
_i = \lim b_i a_i = ba$).

But $SO_4(\mathbb{R})$ is **non-abelian** (e.g., rotations about distinct axes i
n $\mathbb{R}^4$ fail to commute). Therefore $\overline{\langle g \rangle} \neq
SO_4(\mathbb{R})$ for every $g$, so $n \geq 2$.

### Step 2: Setup for $n = 2$ via the universal cover

The universal cover of $SO_4(\mathbb{R})$ is $\operatorname{Spin}(4) \cong SU(2)
 \times SU(2)$, with covering map $\pi: SU(2) \times SU(2) \to SO_4(\mathbb{R})$
 whose kernel is $\{(e,e), (-e,-e)\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$.

**Key reduction:** If two elements topologically generate $SU(2)\times SU(2)$, t
heir images under $\pi$ topologically generate $SO_4(\mathbb{R})$ (continuous im
ages of dense sets under surjective continuous maps are dense). So it suffices t

### Step 3: Generic pairs generate each factor densely

*Proof of fact.* A pair fails to generate densely iff it lies in some maximal pr

- Conjugates of $N(T)$ (normalizer of the maximal torus $T$), each 1-dimensional

Pairs lying in some conjugate of $N(T)$ form a set of dimension $\leq 2\cdot 1 +

So the non-generating set is contained in a countable union of closed sets of di
