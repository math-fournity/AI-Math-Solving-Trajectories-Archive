wastes slots. Two distinct primitive vectors have distinct slopes $a/b$ (with sl
ope $\infty$ for $(1,0)$), so we order them:
$$\operatorname{slope}(v_1) < \operatorname{slope}(v_2) < \cdots < \operatorname
{slope}(v_{100}).$$

**Key Lemma (Outerplanarity).** *The unimodular graph on any finite set of disti
nct primitive nonneg vectors, ordered by slope, is outerplanar: there do not exi
st indices $i < j < k < l$ with both $(v_i, v_k)$ and $(v_j, v_l)$ being unimodu
lar pairs.*

*Proof of Lemma.* Suppose for contradiction that $i<j<k<l$ and both pairs are un
imodular. Since $v_k$ has larger slope than $v_i$, we have $a_k b_i - a_i b_k =
1$ (the positive determinant). Since $\{v_i, v_k\}$ is a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis (det
erminant $\pm 1$), every integer vector decomposes uniquely: $v_j = \alpha\, v_i
 + \beta\, v_k$ for integers $\alpha, \beta$.

Computing: $\alpha = a_k b_j - a_j b_k$ and $\beta = a_j b_i - a_i b_j$. Since $
v_j$'s slope lies strictly between those of $v_i$ and $v_k$, both $\alpha > 0$ a
nd $\beta > 0$, hence $\alpha \geq 1,\; \beta \geq 1$.

Now $(v_j, v_l)$ unimodular with $v_l$ having larger slope gives $a_l b_j - a_j
b_l = 1$. Substituting $v_j = \alpha v_i + \beta v_k$:
$$\alpha\underbrace{(a_l b_i - a_i b_l)}_{\geq\, 1} \;+\; \beta\underbrace{(a_l
b_k - a_k b_l)}_{\geq\, 1} \;=\; 1.$$
Both bracketed terms are positive integers (since $v_l$ has slope exceeding both
 $v_i$ and $v_k$). With $\alpha, \beta \geq 1$, the left side is $\geq 2$, contr
adicting $= 1$.

This argument also covers boundary cases: if $v_l = (1,0)$ (slope $\infty$), the
n $a_l b_i - a_i b_l = b_i \geq 1$ and $a_l b_k - a_k b_l = b_k \geq 1$; if $v_i
 = (0,1)$ (slope $0$), then $a_l b_i - a_i b_l = a_l \geq 1$. The contradiction
holds in all cases. $\square$

**Upper bound.** An outerplanar graph on $n \geq 2$ vertices has at most $2n - 3
$ edges (a maximal outerplanar graph is a triangulated polygon with $n$ vertices
, $n-1$ boundary edges, and $n-2$ diagonals). Applying this with $n = 100$:
$$N \leq 2(100) - 3 = 197.$$

**Construction achieving 197.** Take the 100 vectors:
$$v_0 = (1, 0), \quad v_a = (a, 1) \text{ for } a = 0, 1, 2, \ldots, 98.$$
These are 100 distinct primitive nonneg vectors. Count the unimodular pairs:

- **$(1,0)$ with each $(a,1)$:** $|1 \cdot 1 - a \cdot 0| = 1$ for all $a$. This
 gives **99** pairs.
- **$(a_i, 1)$ with $(a_j, 1)$:** $|a_i \cdot 1 - a_j \cdot 1| = |a_i - a_j| = 1
$ iff $|a_i - a_j| = 1$, i.e., consecutive $a$-values. With $a \in \{0,1,\ldots,
98\}$, this gives **98** pairs.

Total: $99 + 98 = \mathbf{197}$.

This graph is the **fan graph** $F_{100}$: one central vertex $(1,0)$ connected
to all 99 others (a star), plus a path of 98 edges among the 99 leaf vertices —
a maximal outerplanar graph with exactly $2(100)-3 = 197$ edges.

**Conclusion.** The largest possible value of $N$ is $\boxed{197}$.
