# Problem

In a specialized laboratory facility, there are 100 chemical storage canisters, each uniquely labeled with an identification integer from 1 to 100. A technician is tasked with selecting two distinct canisters for a reaction, designated as Canister $a$ and Canister $b$. To ensure safety, the canisters must be chosen such that the ID of the first is strictly less than the ID of the second ($1 \le a < b \le 100$).

The lab supervisor defines a combination as "stable" if there exists at least one positive integer power $k$ such that the combined capacity required by the expression $a^k + b^k$ is exactly divisible by the product of their identification numbers, $a \times b$.

Calculate the total number of unique stable pairs $(a, b)$ that can be formed from the 100 available canisters.
