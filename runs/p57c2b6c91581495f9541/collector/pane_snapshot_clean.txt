**Answer: Yes, $\mu \geq 0$.**

**Proof.**

Recall that a Radon measure $\nu$ on $\Omega$ is non-negative (i.e. $\nu \geq 0$
) if and only if
$$\int_\Omega \phi \, d\nu \geq 0 \quad \text{for every } \phi \in C_0(\Omega) \
text{ with } \phi \geq 0.$$
(The forward direction is immediate from the definition of a non-negative measur
e; the reverse direction follows because if $\nu$ assigned negative mass to some
 Borel set, one could approximate its indicator by compactly supported continuou
s functions and obtain a negative integral — equivalently, this is the standard
positivity criterion from the Riesz–Markov representation theorem.)

Now let $\phi \in C_0(\Omega)$ be arbitrary with $\phi \geq 0$. By hypothesis ea
ch $\mu_n$ is non-negative, so
$$\int_\Omega \phi \, d\mu_n \geq 0 \quad \text{for all } n.$$
By weak-* convergence $\mu_n \rightharpoonup^* \mu$, the sequence of real number
s
$$a_n := \int_\Omega \phi \, d\mu_n$$
converges to
$$a := \int_\Omega \phi \, d\mu.$$
A limit of non-negative real numbers is non-negative, so
$$\int_\Omega \phi \, d\mu = \lim_{n\to\infty} \int_\Omega \phi \, d\mu_n \geq 0
.$$
Since this holds for every non-negative $\phi \in C_0(\Omega)$, the positivity c
riterion above yields $\mu \geq 0$.

**Remark.** No additional hypothesis (uniform boundedness of total masses, absol
ute continuity, etc.) is needed for the conclusion $\mu \geq 0$. The weak-* limi
t of non-negative measures is always non-negative, because positivity is tested
against non-negative continuous compactly supported functions, and non-negativit
y is preserved under pointwise limits of real sequences. (Uniform boundedness of
 $\{\mu_n(\Omega)\}$ would be required only to guarantee that a weak-* limit *ex
ists* as a finite Radon measure; here existence of $\mu$ is already assumed.)
