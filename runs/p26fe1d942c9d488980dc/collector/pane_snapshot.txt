## Solution

We seek the smallest positive integer $a$ such that $f(t) = at^2 + bt + c$ (with
 $a,b,c \in \mathbb{Z}$, $a>0$) has two distinct roots $\alpha, \beta$ with $0 <
 \alpha < \beta < 1$.

### Setting up the conditions

Let $m = -b > 0$. The roots are $\alpha, \beta = \frac{m \pm \sqrt{m^2 - 4ac}}{2
a}$, requiring discriminant $m^2 - 4ac > 0$.

For both roots in $(0,1)$ with $a > 0$ (parabola opens upward), the following ar
e **necessary and sufficient**:
- **Sum:** $\alpha + \beta = \frac{m}{a} \in (0, 2)$, so $0 < m < 2a$, i.e. $m \
in \{1, \ldots, 2a-1\}$.
- **Product:** $\alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a} \in (0,1)$, so $c \in \{1, \ldots, a-1
\}$.
- **$f(1) > 0$:** $a - m + c > 0$, so $m \leq a + c - 1$.
- **Discriminant:** $m^2 > 4ac$.

**Sufficiency argument:** With $a>0$ and two distinct real roots $\alpha < \beta
$, conditions $f(0)=c>0$ and $f(1)>0$ place both $0$ and $1$ outside $[\alpha,\b
eta]$. The sum condition forces the midpoint $\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2} \in (0,1)$.
 If both $0,1$ were $< \alpha$, the midpoint would be $< 0$; if both were $> \be
ta$, the midpoint would be $> 1$. Contradiction — so $0 \leq \alpha$ and $\beta
\leq 1$, and strictness of $f(0), f(1) > 0$ gives $0 < \alpha < \beta < 1$. ✓

### Searching for the smallest $a$

We need integers $c \in \{1,\ldots,a-1\}$ and $m \in \{1, \ldots, \min(2a-1,\, a
+c-1)\}$ with $m^2 > 4ac$.

| $a$ | $c$ range | Best candidate (max $m$, min $c$) | $m^2$ vs $4ac$ | Result
|
|-----|-----------|-------------------------------------|-----------------|-----
---|
| 1 | empty | — | — | ✗ |
| 2 | $\{1\}$ | $m \leq \min(3,2)=2$; $m=2$ | $4 > 8$? No | ✗ |
| 3 | $\{1,2\}$ | $c=1$: $m\leq 3$, $m=3$; $c=2$: $m\leq 4$, $m=4$ | $9>12$? No;
 $16>24$? No | ✗ |
| 4 | $\{1,2,3\}$ | $c=1$: $m\leq 4$; $c=2$: $m\leq 5$; $c=3$: $m\leq 6$ | $16>1
6$? No; $25>32$? No; $36>48$? No | ✗ |
| 5 | $\{1,2,3,4\}$ | $c=1$: $m\leq 5$, $m=5$ | $25 > 20$? **Yes** | ✓ |

For $a=4$, the closest case is $c=1, m=4$: $m^2 = 16 = 4ac$ exactly — discrimina
nt is zero (double root), not two distinct roots. All other cases are worse. So
$a \leq 4$ is impossible.

### Verification for $a = 5$

Take $a=5,\; b=-5,\; c=1$: $f(t) = 5t^2 - 5t + 1$.

Roots: $\alpha, \beta = \dfrac{5 \pm \sqrt{25 - 20}}{10} = \dfrac{5 \pm \sqrt{5}
}{10}$.

- $\alpha = \frac{5 - \sqrt{5}}{10} \approx 0.2764 > 0$ ✓
- $\beta = \frac{5 + \sqrt{5}}{10} \approx 0.7236 < 1$ ✓
- $\alpha \neq \beta$ (since $\sqrt{5} > 0$) ✓

All conditions satisfied.

### Conclusion

The values $a = 1, 2, 3, 4$ admit no valid integer pair $(b, c)$, while $a = 5$
works (e.g., $f(t) = 5t^2 - 5t + 1$).

$$\boxed{a = 5}$$
