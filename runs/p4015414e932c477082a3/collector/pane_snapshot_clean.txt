Looking at this problem, I need to determine when a subring of the Galois ring $
\mathbb{Z}_2^{s^k}$ (of size $s^k$) is isomorphic to the Galois ring $\mathbb{Z}
_s^k$.

**Interpretation of notation.** The Galois ring $\text{GR}(p^a, r)$ has characte
ristic $p^a$, contains $p^{ar}$ elements, and is the unique unramified extension
 of $\mathbb{Z}_{p^a}$ of degree $r$. I interpret:
- $\mathbb{Z}_2^{s^k} = \text{GR}(2, s^k)$: characteristic 2, $2^{s^k}$ elements
.
- $\mathbb{Z}_s^k = \text{GR}(s, k)$: characteristic $s$ (requiring $s$ to be a
prime power, say $s = p^a$), $s^k = p^{ak}$ elements.

A "subset of size $s^k$" admitting a ring isomorphism to $\text{GR}(s,k)$ must b
e a **subring** of $\text{GR}(2, s^k)$ (operations inherited from the ambient ri
ng), with exactly $s^k$ elements, isomorphic to $\text{GR}(s, k)$.

---

**Proof.**

**Step 1: Classification of subrings of $\text{GR}(2, s^k)$.**

The Galois ring $\text{GR}(p^n, r)$ has subrings (with identity) exactly of the
form $\text{GR}(p^n, d)$ where $d \mid r$. This is because $\text{GR}(p^n, r)$ i
s the unique unramified extension of degree $r$ over $\mathbb{Z}_{p^n}$, and its
 subrings correspond to intermediate unramified extensions, indexed by divisors
of $r$.

Thus the subrings of $\text{GR}(2, s^k)$ are precisely $\text{GR}(2, d)$ for eac
h divisor $d$ of $s^k$, with $|\text{GR}(2, d)| = 2^d$.

**Step 2: Matching the characteristic forces $s = 2$.**

For $\text{GR}(s, k) = \text{GR}(p^a, k)$ to be isomorphic to a subring $\text{G
R}(2, d)$, their characteristics must agree:
$$\text{char}(\text{GR}(p^a, k)) = p^a = 2 = \text{char}(\text{GR}(2, d)).$$
This forces $p = 2$ and $a = 1$, hence $s = 2$.

**Step 3: Matching the size and the divisibility condition.**

With $s = 2$, the target ring is $\text{GR}(2, k)$ with $2^k$ elements, and $s^k
 = 2^k$. We need $\text{GR}(2, k) \cong \text{GR}(2, d)$ for some $d \mid s^k =
2^k$.

Since $\text{GR}(2, k) \cong \text{GR}(2, d)$ if and only if $k = d$ (Galois rin
gs are determined up to isomorphism by their characteristic and degree), we need
:
$$k \mid 2^k.$$

**Step 4: Characterizing $k$ such that $k \mid 2^k$.**

Write $k = 2^m \cdot q$ where $q$ is odd. Then $k \mid 2^k = 2^{2^m \cdot q}$ re
quires $q \mid 2^{2^m \cdot q}$. Since $2^{2^m \cdot q}$ is a power of $2$ and $
q$ is odd, this holds if and only if $q = 1$.

Therefore $k \mid 2^k$ if and only if $k$ is a power of $2$ (including $k = 1 =
2^0$).

**Step 5: Verification.**

For $s = 2$ and $k = 2^m$ ($m \geq 0$):
- $\text{GR}(2, s^k) = \text{GR}(2, 2^{2^m})$ has $2^{2^{2^m}}$ elements. ✓ (Gal
ois ring exists)
- $\text{GR}(s, k) = \text{GR}(2, 2^m)$ has $2^{2^m} = s^k$ elements. ✓ (Galois
ring exists)
- $k = 2^m \mid 2^{2^m} = s^k$, so $\text{GR}(2, k)$ is a subring of $\text{GR}(
2, s^k)$. ✓
- This subring has $2^k = s^k$ elements and is isomorphic to $\text{GR}(s, k)$.
✓

**Conversely**, if $s \neq 2$, the characteristics differ (Step 2), so no isomor
phism exists. If $s = 2$ but $k$ is not a power of $2$, then $k \nmid 2^k$ (Step
 4), so $\text{GR}(2, k)$ is not a subring of $\text{GR}(2, 2^k)$, and no subset
 of size $2^k$ forming such a subring exists.

---

**Conclusion.** The values are $s = 2$ and $k = 2^m$ for any non-negative intege
r $m$. The smallest non-trivial pair is:

$$\boxed{(s,\, k) = (2,\, 2)}$$

representing the family $(s, k) = (2, 2^m)$, $m \geq 0$.
