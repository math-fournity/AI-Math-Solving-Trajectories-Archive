## Flux of $F_1 = \frac{\mathbf{r}}{|\mathbf{r}|^3}$

**Key observation (solid angle):** $F_1 = -\nabla\!\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)$ is
divergence-free away from the origin, so the flux through any surface equals the
 solid angle it subtends from the origin (with sign determined by orientation).

The origin is the center of the ellipsoid. Every ray from the origin with direct
ion $(\sin\phi\cos\theta,\, \sin\phi\sin\theta,\, \cos\phi)$, $\phi \in [0,\pi/2
]$, strikes the upper half-ellipsoid; every ray with $\phi\in(\pi/2,\pi]$ strike
s the lower half. Thus the upper half-ellipsoid subtends exactly the **upper hem
isphere** of directions, with solid angle $2\pi$.

With the **outward** normal (pointing away from the origin), the sign is positiv
e:

$$\iint_S F_1 \cdot d\mathbf{S} = +\,2\pi.$$

**Verification via parameterization.** Setting $x=a\sin\phi\cos\theta$, $y=b\sin
\phi\sin\theta$, $z=c\cos\phi$, the outward normal vector is

$$\mathbf{n} = (bc\sin^2\!\phi\cos\theta,\; ac\sin^2\!\phi\sin\theta,\; ab\sin\p
hi\cos\phi),$$

and $\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{n} = abc\sin\phi$. For the sphere $a=b=c=R$, the int
egral becomes $\int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin\phi\,d\phi\,d\theta = 2\pi$. ✓
The solid-angle argument confirms this holds for **all** $a,b,c$.

## Flux of $F_2 = (z,\, 0,\, -x)$

**Divergence:** $\nabla\cdot F_2 = \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} + 0 + \frac{\pa
rtial(-x)}{\partial z} = 0$, so $F_2$ is divergence-free.

Close the upper half-ellipsoid $S$ with the base disk $D = \bigl\{\frac{x^2}{a^2
}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}\le 1,\; z=0\bigr\}$, whose outward normal (for the half-ellips
oidal volume) is $-\mathbf{k}$. By the divergence theorem:

$$\iint_S F_2\cdot d\mathbf{S} + \iint_D F_2\cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iiint_V \nabla\
cdot F_2\,dV = 0.$$

On $D$: $z=0$, so $F_2 = (0,0,-x)$, and

$$F_2\cdot(-\mathbf{k}) = (0,0,-x)\cdot(0,0,-1) = x.$$

Therefore

$$\iint_D F_2\cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iint_D x\,dA = 0,$$

since $D$ is symmetric under $x\mapsto -x$ while $x$ is odd. Hence

$$\iint_S F_2\cdot d\mathbf{S} = 0.$$

## Total flux

$$\iint_S F\cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iint_S F_1\cdot d\mathbf{S} + \iint_S F_2\cdot d
\mathbf{S} = 2\pi + 0 = \boxed{2\pi}.$$

**Remark on the orientation condition.** The outward normal on the upper half-el
lipsoid has $N\cdot\mathbf{k}>0$ (positive $z$-component) everywhere, confirming
 the outward direction. The result $2\pi$ is independent of the semi-axes $a,b,c
$.
