## Proof

Fix any index $i \geq 1$. We must show that infinitely many terms $a_n$ can be w
ritten as $a_n = r\,a_i + s\,a_j$ with $r, s$ positive integers and $j > i$.

**Step 1: Pigeonhole on residue classes.**

Consider the terms $a_{i+1}, a_{i+2}, a_{i+3}, \ldots$ (all terms with index gre
ater than $i$). Reduce each modulo $a_i$. Since there are only $a_i$ residue cla
sses modulo $a_i$ but infinitely many terms, by the pigeonhole principle there e
xists a residue class $c \pmod{a_i}$ containing infinitely many of these terms.

Let these terms be
$$a_{n_1} < a_{n_2} < a_{n_3} < \cdots,$$
where $i < n_1 < n_2 < n_3 < \cdots$ and every $a_{n_k} \equiv c \pmod{a_i}$.

**Step 2: Differences are multiples of $a_i$.**

For every $k \geq 2$, since $a_{n_k} \equiv a_{n_1} \equiv c \pmod{a_i}$, we hav
e
$$a_{n_k} - a_{n_1} \equiv 0 \pmod{a_i}.$$
Since $n_k > n_1$, the sequence is strictly increasing, so $a_{n_k} > a_{n_1}$,
hence $a_{n_k} - a_{n_1} > 0$. Therefore there exists a positive integer $m_k$ s
uch that
$$a_{n_k} - a_{n_1} = m_k\, a_i, \qquad m_k \geq 1.$$

**Step 3: Express each $a_{n_k}$ in the required form.**

Rearranging:
$$a_{n_k} = m_k\, a_i + 1 \cdot a_{n_1}.$$

Set:
- $r = m_k \geq 1$ — a **positive integer**,
- $s = 1$ — a **positive integer**,
- $j = n_1 > i$ — an index **greater than $i$**.

Then $a_{n_k} = r\,a_i + s\,a_j$ as required.

**Step 4: Infinitely many such terms.**

This holds for every $k = 2, 3, 4, \ldots$, yielding the infinitely many terms
$$a_{n_2},\; a_{n_3},\; a_{n_4},\; \ldots$$
each expressible in the form $r\,a_i + s\,a_j$ with $r, s$ positive integers and
 $j > i$.

Since $i \geq 1$ was arbitrary, the statement holds for every $i$. $\blacksquare
$
