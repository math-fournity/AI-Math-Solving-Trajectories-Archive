### Step 1: The integral diverges in the ordinary sense

Near the origin, $\sin x = x - \frac{x^3}{6} + O(x^5)$, so

$$\frac{\sin x}{x^3} = \frac{1}{x^2} - \frac{1}{6} + O(x^2).$$

The leading term $\frac{1}{x^2}$ is not integrable at $0$:

$$\int_0^1 \frac{\sin x}{x^3}\,dx \;\ge\; \int_0^1\!\Bigl(\frac{1}{x^2}-\frac{1}
{6}\Bigr)dx = +\infty.$$

Hence $\displaystyle\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin x}{x^3}\,dx$ **diverges to $+\infty
$**. To obtain a finite value we regularize by subtracting the divergent part, i
.e. we evaluate the **Hadamard finite part**

$$\operatorname{F.p.}\!\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin x}{x^3}\,dx \;=\; \int_0^\infty
\frac{\sin x - x}{x^3}\,dx,$$

which converges (the numerator is $O(x^3)$ at $0$ and $O(x)$ at $\infty$, so the
 integrand is $O(1)$ at $0$ and $O(x^{-2})$ at $\infty$).

### Step 2: Complex-analysis setup

Define the **entire** function (the singularity at $0$ is removable)

$$h(z)=\frac{e^{iz}-1-iz+\tfrac{z^2}{2}}{z^3}, \qquad h(0)=-\tfrac{i}{6}.$$

Integrate $h$ over the standard upper-half-plane contour $\Gamma$: the segment $
[-R,R]$ on the real axis closed by the semicircle $C_R=\{Re^{i\theta}:0\le\theta
\le\pi\}$. Since $h$ is entire, Cauchy's theorem gives

$$\oint_\Gamma h(z)\,dz = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \int_{-R}^{R} h(x)\,dx = -
\int_{C_R} h(z)\,dz.$$

---

### Step 3: The large-arc contribution

Split the polynomial part:

$$h(z)=\frac{e^{iz}}{z^3}-\frac{1}{z^3}-\frac{i}{z^2}+\frac{1}{2z}.$$

On $C_R$ with $z=Re^{i\theta}$, $dz=iRe^{i\theta}d\theta$:

| term | integral over $C_R$ | limit $R\to\infty$ |
|------|---------------------|--------------------|
| $\dfrac{e^{iz}}{z^3}$ | bounded by $\dfrac{1}{R^3}\displaystyle\int_0^\pi e^{-
R\sin\theta}d\theta \to 0$ (Jordan) | $0$ |
| $\dfrac{1}{z^3}$ | $\dfrac{i}{R^2}\displaystyle\int_0^\pi e^{-2i\theta}d\theta
 = 0$ | $0$ |
| $\dfrac{1}{z^2}$ | $\dfrac{i}{R}\displaystyle\int_0^\pi e^{-i\theta}d\theta =
\dfrac{2}{R}$ | $0$ |
| $\dfrac{1}{2z}$ | $\dfrac{i}{2}\displaystyle\int_0^\pi d\theta = \dfrac{i\pi}{
2}$ | $\dfrac{i\pi}{2}$ |

Therefore

$$\int_{C_R} h(z)\,dz \;\longrightarrow\; \frac{i\pi}{2}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(x)\,dx = -\frac{i\pi}{2}.$$

---

### Step 4: Extract the real and imaginary parts

Write $h(x)=\dfrac{\cos x - 1 + \tfrac{x^2}{2}}{x^3} + i\,\dfrac{\sin x - x}{x^3
}$.

- $\dfrac{\cos x-1+\tfrac{x^2}{2}}{x^3}$ is **odd** (even numerator, odd denomin
ator), so its integral over $(-\infty,\infty)$ vanishes.
- $\dfrac{\sin x - x}{x^3}$ is **even** (odd/odd), so

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(x)\,dx \;=\; 0 \;+\; i\cdot 2\!\int_0^\infty \frac{\
sin x - x}{x^3}\,dx.$$

Equating with $-\dfrac{i\pi}{2}$:

$$2\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin x - x}{x^3}\,dx = -\frac{\pi}{2} \quad\Longrightarro
w\quad \int_0^\infty \frac{\sin x - x}{x^3}\,dx = -\frac{\pi}{4}.$$

---

### Step 5: Verification by integration by parts

As an independent check, for $\epsilon>0$:

$$\int_\epsilon^\infty \frac{\sin x}{x^3}\,dx = \frac{\sin\epsilon}{2\epsilon^2}
+\frac{\cos\epsilon}{2\epsilon}-\frac{1}{2}\int_\epsilon^\infty\frac{\sin x}{x}\
,dx.$$

Using $\sin\epsilon=\epsilon-\epsilon^3/6+\cdots$, $\cos\epsilon=1-\epsilon^2/2+
\cdots$:

$$\frac{\sin\epsilon}{2\epsilon^2}+\frac{\cos\epsilon}{2\epsilon} = \frac{1}{\ep
silon}+O(\epsilon), \qquad \int_\epsilon^\infty\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx \to \frac{\p
i}{2}.$$

Hence

$$\int_\epsilon^\infty \frac{\sin x}{x^3}\,dx = \frac{1}{\epsilon}-\frac{\pi}{4}
+O(\epsilon),$$

confirming both the **divergent** $\frac{1}{\epsilon}$ part and the **finite par
t** $-\dfrac{\pi}{4}$.

### Conclusion

The integral $\displaystyle\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin x}{x^3}\,dx$ **diverges** (t
he singularity $\frac{1}{x^2}$ at the origin is non-integrable). Its **Hadamard
finite part**—equivalently $\displaystyle\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin x - x}{x^3}\,d
x$—obtained via complex analysis by integrating the entire function $h(z)=\frac{
e^{iz}-1-iz+z^2/2}{z^3}$ over the upper-half-plane contour, is

$$\boxed{\operatorname{F.p.}\!\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin x}{x^3}\,dx \;=\; -\,\fra
