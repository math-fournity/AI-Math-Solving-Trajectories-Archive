## Proof

Recall that a function $f$ meromorphic on $\mathbb{C}$ is **meromorphic on the R
iemann sphere** $\mathbb{C}_\infty = \mathbb{C} \cup \{\infty\}$ if and only if
it is meromorphic at every point of $\mathbb{C}_\infty$, including the point at
infinity.

Since $f$ is already meromorphic on $\mathbb{C}$ by hypothesis, it remains only
to verify that $f$ is meromorphic at $\infty$.

**Definition (meromorphy at $\infty$).** A function $f$ meromorphic on $\mathbb{
C}$ is meromorphic at $\infty$ if and only if the function
$$g(w) = f\!\left(\tfrac{1}{w}\right)$$
is meromorphic at $w = 0$. (This is the standard criterion: the coordinate $w =
1/z$ maps a neighborhood of $\infty$ to a neighborhood of $0$.)

**Applying the criterion.** We are given that $g$ is meromorphic on $\mathbb{C}$
, so in particular $g$ is meromorphic at $w = 0$. Moreover, $g$ is *defined* (i.
e., takes a finite value) at $w = 0$, which means $w = 0$ is a regular point of
$g$ (not a pole). In either case—whether $g(0)$ is finite (holomorphic) or $g$ h
as a pole at $0$—the function $g$ is meromorphic at $w = 0$, which is exactly th
e condition required for $f$ to be meromorphic at $\infty$.

**Conclusion.** The function $f$ is meromorphic at every point of $\mathbb{C}$ (
by hypothesis) and meromorphic at $\infty$ (by the criterion above, since $g = f
(1/w)$ is meromorphic at $w=0$). Therefore $f$ is meromorphic on the entire Riem
ann sphere $\mathbb{C}_\infty$.

**Remark.** The additional information that $g$ is *defined* (finite) at $w=0$ t
ells us further that $f$ is actually **holomorphic** at $\infty$ (i.e., $\infty$
 is not a pole of $f$). Combined with $f$ being meromorphic on $\mathbb{C}$, thi
s means $f$ has only finitely many poles in $\mathbb{C}$ (a meromorphic function
 on the compact Riemann sphere can have only finitely many poles), so $f$ is in
fact a **rational function**. But the question only asks about meromorphy on $\m
athbb{C}_\infty$, which holds regardless.
