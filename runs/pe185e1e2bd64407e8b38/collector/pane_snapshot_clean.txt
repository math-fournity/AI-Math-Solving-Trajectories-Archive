### Step 1. Trivial solution

$z \equiv 0$ satisfies $-z''z^3 = 0 = |z|$. Henceforth assume $z \not\equiv 0$ a
nd work on intervals where $z \neq 0$.

### Step 2. Polar decomposition

Write $z(t) = r(t)\,e^{i\theta(t)}$ with $r = |z| > 0$ and $\theta$ real. Then $
|z| = r$, and

$$z'' = \bigl(r'' - r(\theta')^2 + i(2r'\theta' + r\theta'')\bigr)e^{i\theta}, \
qquad z^3 = r^3 e^{3i\theta}.$$

The equation $-z''z^3 = r$ (real, $\geq 0$) becomes

$$-r^3\bigl(r'' - r(\theta')^2 + i(2r'\theta' + r\theta'')\bigr)e^{4i\theta} = r
,$$

i.e., for $r > 0$:

$$\bigl(r'' - r(\theta')^2 + i(2r'\theta' + r\theta'')\bigr)e^{4i\theta} = -\fra
c{1}{r^2}. \tag{$\ast$}$$

Since the right side is real, the imaginary part of the left side must vanish. S
eparating real and imaginary parts gives the **general system**:

$$\boxed{r'' - r(\theta')^2 = -\frac{\cos 4\theta}{r^2}, \qquad (r^2\theta')' =
\frac{\sin 4\theta}{r}.} \tag{S}$$

Every nontrivial solution of $-z''z^3=|z|$ corresponds to a solution of (S) with
 $r>0$, and vice versa.

### Step 3. Constant-phase solutions (explicitly integrable subfamily)

Set $\theta' = 0$, i.e., $\theta \equiv \alpha$ (constant). Then (S) reduces to

$$r'' = -\frac{\cos 4\alpha}{r^2}, \qquad 0 = \frac{\sin 4\alpha}{r}.$$

The second equation forces $\sin 4\alpha = 0$, i.e., $\alpha = \frac{k\pi}{4}$,
$k \in \mathbb{Z}$. Two cases arise:

- **$k$ even** ($\cos 4\alpha = 1$): $\;r'' = -\dfrac{1}{r^2}$.
- **$k$ odd** ($\cos 4\alpha = -1$): $\;r'' = +\dfrac{1}{r^2}$.

**First integrals.** Multiplying by $r'$ and integrating:

| Phase | ODE | First integral |
|-------|-----|----------------|
| $k$ even | $r'' = -1/r^2$ | $(r')^2 = \dfrac{2}{r} + C_1$ |
| $k$ odd  | $r'' = +1/r^2$ | $(r')^2 = -\dfrac{2}{r} + C_1$ |

where $C_1 \in \mathbb{R}$ is arbitrary (with $C_1 > 0$ required for the odd cas
e to have $r > 0$).

**Implicit general solution by quadrature.** Separating variables:

$$\int \frac{\sqrt{r}\;dr}{\sqrt{2 + C_1 r}} = \pm(t - t_0) \qquad (k\text{ even
}), \tag{Q$_+$}$$

$$\int \frac{\sqrt{r}\;dr}{\sqrt{C_1 r - 2}} = \pm(t - t_0) \qquad (k\text{ odd}
,\; C_1 > 0). \tag{Q$_-$}$$

These quadratures are elementary (substitute $r = \frac{2}{|C_1|}\sinh^2 u$ or $
\sin^2 u$ according to the sign of $C_1$) and yield $r$ implicitly as a function
 of $t$, involving $\sinh$, $\cosh$, and $\text{arcsinh}$ (or their trigonometri
c analogues). The two constants $(C_1, t_0)$ are the two integration constants e
xpected for a second-order ODE.

### Step 4. Explicit special solution ($C_1 = 0$, $k$ even)

Setting $C_1 = 0$ in the even case: $(r')^2 = 2/r$, so $\sqrt{r}\,dr = \pm\sqrt{
2}\,dt$, giving

$$\tfrac{2}{3}r^{3/2} = \pm\sqrt{2}\,(t - t_0), \qquad r(t) = \left(\tfrac{3\sqr
t{2}}{2}\right)^{2/3}|t-t_0|^{2/3} = \left(\tfrac{9}{2}\right)^{1/3}|t-t_0|^{2/3
}.$$

**Verification.** Let $A = (9/2)^{1/3}$ and $r = A\tau^{2/3}$ with $\tau = t - t
_0 > 0$. Then
$$r' = \tfrac{2}{3}A\tau^{-1/3}, \quad r'' = -\tfrac{2}{9}A\tau^{-4/3}, \quad -r
''r^3 = \tfrac{2}{9}A\cdot A^3\tau^{-4/3+2} = \tfrac{2}{9}A^4\tau^{2/3}.$$
Since $A^3 = 9/2$, we get $\tfrac{2}{9}A^4 = \tfrac{2}{9}\cdot\tfrac{9}{2}\cdot
A = A$, so $-r''r^3 = A\tau^{2/3} = r = |z|$. ✓

Thus

$$z(t) = \left(\tfrac{9}{2}\right)^{1/3}(t-t_0)^{2/3}\,e^{ik\pi/4}, \qquad k \in
 \{0,2,4,6\}, \quad t > t_0$$

is an explicit one-parameter family of solutions (valid on any interval not cont
aining $t_0$; at $t_0$ the solution reaches $z=0$ where $z''$ is singular).

### Step 5. Summary of all solutions

The complete solution set of $-z''z^3 = |z|$ is:

1. **Trivial:** $z \equiv 0$.

2. **General nontrivial:** $z(t) = r(t)\,e^{i\theta(t)}$ with $r > 0$ and $(r,\t
heta)$ solving the coupled system (S):
   $$r'' - r(\theta')^2 = -\frac{\cos 4\theta}{r^2}, \qquad (r^2\theta')' = \fra
c{\sin 4\theta}{r}.$$

3. **Constant-phase subfamily** ($\theta \equiv k\pi/4$): reduces to a single re
al ODE for $r$ with first integral $(r')^2 = \pm 2/r + C_1$ and implicit solutio
n by quadrature (Q$_\pm$). The special case $C_1 = 0$, $k$ even, gives the expli
cit power law $z = (9/2)^{1/3}(t-t_0)^{2/3}e^{ik\pi/4}$.

The constant-phase family carries two free parameters $(C_1, t_0)$ as expected f
or a second-order ODE; the full non-constant-phase family carries four (the syst
em (S) is second-order in $r$ and $\theta$, i.e., four initial conditions $(r, r
', \theta, \theta')$ at a point, subject to no further constraint since (S) is a
lready the full reduction).
