Looking at this problem, I need to count positive integer sequences $a_1, \ldots
, a_{13}$ with $a_1 = a_{13} = 6$ and $a_i \leq \frac{a_{i-1}+a_{i+1}}{2}$ for $
1 < i < 13$.

## Setting Up the Difference Sequence

**Define differences:** Let $d_i = a_{i+1} - a_i$ for $i = 1, \ldots, 12$.

**Convexity → non-decreasing differences:** The condition $2a_i \leq a_{i-1} + a
_{i+1}$ rearranges to $d_i \geq d_{i-1}$. So $d_1 \leq d_2 \leq \cdots \leq d_{1
2}$ (non-decreasing).

**Boundary condition:** $a_1 = a_{13} = 6$ gives $\sum_{i=1}^{12} d_i = 0$.

**Positivity constraint:** Each $a_k = 6 + \sum_{j=1}^{k-1} d_j \geq 1$, so ever
y partial sum $S_k = \sum_{j=1}^k d_j \geq -5$ for $k = 1, \ldots, 11$.

## Exploiting the Structure

Since the $d_i$ are non-decreasing and sum to 0, all negative terms come first,
then zeros, then positive terms. The partial sums $S_k$ decrease to a minimum (a
t the last negative term) then increase back to 0.

**The minimum partial sum** equals $-\sum_{d_i < 0} |d_i|$. Setting $N = \sum_{d
_i > 0} d_i = \sum_{d_i < 0} |d_i|$ (the total "drop"), the constraint becomes:

$$N \leq 5$$

## Counting via Partitions

For a given $N$, the sequence decomposes into:
- **$p$ negative terms** $d_1 \leq \cdots \leq d_p \leq -1$: their absolute valu
es $|d_p| \geq \cdots \geq |d_1| \geq 1$ form a partition of $N$ into $p$ parts.
- **$z$ zeros**: $d_{p+1} = \cdots = d_{p+z} = 0$.
- **$r$ positive terms** $1 \leq d_{p+z+1} \leq \cdots \leq d_{12}$: these form
a partition of $N$ into $r$ parts.

with $p + z + r = 12$.

**Key observation:** Since $N \leq 5$, any partition of $N$ has at most $N \leq
5$ parts. So $p \leq 5$ and $r \leq 5$, giving $p + r \leq 10 \leq 12$. The cons
traint $p + r \leq 12$ is **automatically satisfied** — there are always enough
slots for zeros.

For each $N \geq 1$, the count is:
$$\sum_{p=1}^{N} \sum_{r=1}^{N} P(N,p)\, P(N,r) = \left(\sum_{k=1}^{N} P(N,k)\ri
ght)^2 = p(N)^2$$

where $P(N,k)$ is the number of partitions of $N$ into exactly $k$ parts, and $p
(N)$ is the total number of partitions of $N$.
