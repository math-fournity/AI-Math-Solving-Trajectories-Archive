## Proof

**Step 1 — Distribution of $Y_n$.**

We claim that for $m \ge n$,
$$\mathbb{P}(Y_n \le m)=\prod_{k=m-n+1}^{m}\bigl(1-2^{-k}\bigr),$$
and $\mathbb{P}(Y_n\le m)=0$ for $m<n$.

Indeed, $Y_n\le m$ iff every $X_1,\dots,X_n\in\{1,\dots,m\}$. Suppose $X_1,\dots
,X_j\in\{1,\dots,m\}$ have already been chosen. Then $j$ values have been remove
d from $\{1,\dots,m\}$, leaving $m-j$ integers there; these are the $m-j$ smalle
st remaining integers and so occupy ranks $1,\dots,m-j$, while all integers $>m$
 occupy ranks $m-j+1,m-j+2,\dots$. Hence
$$\mathbb{P}\bigl(X_{j+1}\le m\mid X_1,\dots,X_j\in\{1,\dots,m\}\bigr)=\sum_{i=1
}^{m-j}2^{-i}=1-2^{-(m-j)}.$$
Multiplying over $j=0,1,\dots,n-1$ (with $k=m-j$) gives the claimed product. For
 $m<n$ the product contains the factor $1-2^{0}=0$, consistent with $\mathbb{P}(
Y_n\le m)=0$.

**Step 2 — Tail-sum formula.**

Since $Y_n$ is a positive integer,
$$\mathbb{E}[Y_n]=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(Y_n>m)=\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}1+\sum_{m
=n}^{\infty}\Bigl(1-\prod_{k=m-n+1}^{m}(1-2^{-k})\Bigr)=n+\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}(1-
P_m),$$
where $P_m:=\prod_{k=m-n+1}^{m}(1-2^{-k})$.

**Step 3 — Decompose $1-P_m$ via partial products.**

Set $G_m^{(j)}:=\prod_{k=m-n+1}^{m-n+j}(1-2^{-k})$ for $j=0,\dots,n$ (empty prod
uct $=1$), so $G_m^{(0)}=1$ and $G_m^{(n)}=P_m$. Telescoping,
$$1-P_m=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigl(G_m^{(j-1)}-G_m^{(j)}\bigr)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}G_m^{(j-1)
}\cdot 2^{-(m-n+j)}.$$

**Step 4 — Swap sums and evaluate each piece.**

All summands are non-negative, so Tonelli justifies swapping:
$$\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}(1-P_m)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}2^{-(m-n+j)}\prod_
{k=m-n+1}^{m-n+j-1}(1-2^{-k}).$$
Substituting $t=m-n\ge 0$,
$$\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}(1-P_m)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\underbrace{\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}2^{-(t
+j)}\prod_{k=t+1}^{t+j-1}(1-2^{-k})}_{(\star_j)}.$$

**Lemma.** For every $j\ge 1$,
$$(\star_j)=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}2^{-(t+j)}\prod_{k=t+1}^{t+j-1}(1-2^{-k})=\frac{1
}{2^{j}-1}.$$

*Proof of Lemma.* Let $H_t:=\prod_{k=t+1}^{t+j-1}(1-2^{-k})$ (with $H_t\equiv 1$
 when $j=1$), $A_t:=2^{-t}H_t$, and $B_t:=\prod_{k=t}^{t+j-1}(1-2^{-k})=(1-2^{-t
})H_t$. Then
$$B_{t+1}=\prod_{k=t+1}^{t+j}(1-2^{-k})=H_t\bigl(1-2^{-(t+j)}\bigr),$$
so
$$B_{t+1}-B_t=H_t\bigl[(1-2^{-(t+j)})-(1-2^{-t})\bigr]=H_t\bigl(2^{-t}-2^{-(t+j)
}\bigr)=(1-2^{-j})\,A_t.$$
Thus $A_t=\dfrac{B_{t+1}-B_t}{1-2^{-j}}$, and
$$\sum_{t=0}^{T}A_t=\frac{B_{T+1}-B_0}{1-2^{-j}}.$$
Now $B_0=\prod_{k=0}^{j-1}(1-2^{-k})=0$ (the $k=0$ factor is $1-1=0$), and $B_{T
+1}=\prod_{k=T+1}^{T+j}(1-2^{-k})\to 1$ as $T\to\infty$. Therefore
$$\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}A_t=\frac{1}{1-2^{-j}}=\frac{2^{j}}{2^{j}-1},$$
and consequently
$$(\star_j)=2^{-j}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}A_t=\frac{2^{-j}\cdot 2^{j}}{2^{j}-1}=\frac
{1}{2^{j}-1}. \qquad\square$$

**Step 5 — Conclusion.**

Applying the lemma,
$$\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}(1-P_m)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{j}-1},$$
and so
$$\mathbb{E}[Y_n]=n+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{j}-1}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(1+\frac{
1}{2^{j}-1}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{2^{j}}{2^{j}-1}.$$
