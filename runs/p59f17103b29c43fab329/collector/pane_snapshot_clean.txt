### Setup

Let $A:=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}} A_n$. Since $A_{n+1}\subseteq A_n$ for all $n$,
 the sequence is decreasing, so $A\subseteq A_n\subseteq A_1$ for every $n$.

For each $n$, define the **increasing** sequence
$$B_n := A_1 \setminus A_n.$$
Because $A_{n+1}\subseteq A_n$, we have $A_1\setminus A_{n+1}\supseteq A_1\setmi
nus A_n$, i.e. $B_n\subseteq B_{n+1}$. Thus $\{B_n\}$ is an increasing sequence
of measurable sets.

### Step 1 — Measure of each $B_n$

Since $A_n\subseteq A_1$ and $\mu(A_1)=1<\infty$, the set $A_1$ has finite measu
re, so we may freely subtract:
$$A_1 = A_n \;\sqcup\; (A_1\setminus A_n) = A_n \;\sqcup\; B_n,$$
a disjoint union. By finite additivity (valid because $\mu(A_1)<\infty$ lets us
write $\mu(A_1\setminus A_n)=\mu(A_1)-\mu(A_n)$),
$$\mu(B_n)=\mu(A_1)-\mu(A_n)=1-1=0.$$

### Step 2 — Apply continuity from below

Continuity from below holds for **every** measure (no finiteness hypothesis need
ed): for an increasing sequence $B_n\uparrow B$,
$$\mu(B)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu(B_n).$$

Here
$$\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}(A_1\setminus A_n)=A_1\setm
inus\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}A_n = A_1\setminus A.$$

Therefore
$$\mu(A_1\setminus A)=\mu\!\left(\bigcup_{n}B_n\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu(B_n)
=\lim_{n\to\infty}0=0.$$

### Step 3 — Conclude

We have the disjoint union
$$A_1 = A \;\sqcup\; (A_1\setminus A),\qquad A\subseteq A_1.$$
Since $\mu(A_1)=1<\infty$, finite additivity gives
$$\mu(A_1)=\mu(A)+\mu(A_1\setminus A)=\mu(A)+0.$$
Hence
$$\mu(A)=\mu(A_1)=1.$$

That is,
$$\boxed{\,\mu\!\left(\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}} A_n\right)=1.\,}$$
