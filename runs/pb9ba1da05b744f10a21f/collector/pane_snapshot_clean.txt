## Solution

We use the standard definition: the **spatial dimension** $\text{dim}_s(G)$ is t
he minimum integer $d$ such that $G$ admits a unit-distance embedding in $\mathb
b{R}^d$ (vertices mapped to distinct points, every edge to a segment of length $
1$).

**Claim.** For every integer $n \geq 1$, the complete graph $K_{n+1}$ satisfies
$\text{dim}_s(K_{n+1}) = n$.

### Upper bound: $\text{dim}_s(K_{n+1}) \leq n$

The regular simplex with $n+1$ vertices in $\mathbb{R}^n$ has all pairwise dista
nces equal to $1$. Concretely, the $n+1$ points can be taken as the standard sim
plex vertices, e.g. $e_1, e_2, \dots, e_{n+1}$ projected appropriately, or equiv
alently the columns of $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(I_{n+1} - \frac{1}{n+1}J_{n+1})$ view
ed in the $n$-dimensional subspace orthogonal to the all-ones vector. This gives
 a unit-distance embedding of $K_{n+1}$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$, so $\text{dim}_s(K_{n
+1}) \leq n$.

### Lower bound: $\text{dim}_s(K_{n+1}) \geq n$

Suppose for contradiction that $K_{n+1}$ embeds as a unit-distance graph in $\ma
thbb{R}^d$ with $d < n$. Let the $n+1$ vertices be mapped to points $v_0, v_1, \
dots, v_n \in \mathbb{R}^d$. Since $K_{n+1}$ is complete, every pair is at dista
nce $1$.

Consider the $n$ vectors $u_i = v_i - v_0$ for $i = 1, \dots, n$. We compute the
ir Gram matrix $G$, where $G_{ij} = \langle u_i, u_j \rangle$.

- **Diagonal:** $\|u_i\|^2 = \|v_i - v_0\|^2 = 1$, so $G_{ii} = 1$.
- **Off-diagonal** ($i \neq j$): Using $\|v_i - v_j\|^2 = 1$,
$$1 = \|u_i - u_j\|^2 = \|u_i\|^2 + \|u_j\|^2 - 2\langle u_i, u_j \rangle = 2 -
2G_{ij},$$
so $G_{ij} = \tfrac{1}{2}$.

Thus $G = \tfrac{1}{2}I_n + \tfrac{1}{2}J_n$ where $J_n$ is the all-ones matrix.
 The eigenvalues of $J_n$ are $n$ (multiplicity $1$) and $0$ (multiplicity $n-1$
), so the eigenvalues of $G$ are:
$$\lambda_1 = \tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{n}{2} = \tfrac{n+1}{2} > 0, \qquad \lambda_2
 = \cdots = \lambda_n = \tfrac{1}{2} > 0.$$

All eigenvalues are strictly positive, so $G$ is positive definite. This means $
u_1, \dots, u_n$ are **linearly independent**. But they are $n$ linearly indepen
dent vectors in $\mathbb{R}^d$, forcing $d \geq n$ — contradicting $d < n$.

Therefore no embedding exists in $\mathbb{R}^{n-1}$ (or lower), giving $\text{di
m}_s(K_{n+1}) \geq n$.

### Conclusion

Combining both bounds: $\text{dim}_s(K_{n+1}) = n$ for every $n \geq 1$.

**Verification of base case:** For $n=1$, $K_2$ is a single edge, embedding in $
\mathbb{R}^1$ as two points at distance $1$, and $\mathbb{R}^0$ has only one poi
nt so no embedding is possible. ✓

The required example for each $n \geq 1$ is $G = K_{n+1}$.
