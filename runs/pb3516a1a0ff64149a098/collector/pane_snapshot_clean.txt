## Proof

We seek primes $p > q$ with $240 \nmid p^4 - q^4$, maximizing $\frac{q}{p}$.

**Factorization.** $p^4 - q^4 = (p-q)(p+q)(p^2+q^2)$, and $240 = 2^4 \cdot 3 \cd
ot 5$.

---

**Case 1: Both $p, q$ odd primes.**

*Divisibility by 16.* Since $p, q$ are odd, $p-q$ and $p+q$ are both even. Their
 sum is $2p \equiv 2 \pmod{4}$, so exactly one of them is $\equiv 0 \pmod{4}$ (g
iving $v_2 \geq 2$) and the other $\equiv 2 \pmod{4}$ (giving $v_2 = 1$). Also $
p^2 + q^2 \equiv 1+1 = 2 \pmod{4}$, so $v_2(p^2+q^2)=1$. Hence $v_2(p^4-q^4) \ge
q 2+1+1 = 4$, i.e., **$16 \mid p^4 - q^4$ always**.

*Divisibility by 3.* If $3 \nmid p$ and $3 \nmid q$, then by Fermat $p^4 \equiv
q^4 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$, so $3 \mid p^4-q^4$. If one of them equals $3$, then $p^
4 - q^4 \equiv \pm 1 \pmod{3}$, so $3 \nmid p^4-q^4$.

*Divisibility by 5.* Similarly, if $5 \nmid p$ and $5 \nmid q$, then $p^4 \equiv
 q^4 \equiv 1 \pmod{5}$, so $5 \mid p^4-q^4$. If one equals $5$, then $5 \nmid p
^4-q^4$.

Therefore, for **both odd and neither equal to $3$ or $5$**: $16 \mid$, $3 \mid$
, $5 \mid$, so $240 \mid p^4 - q^4$ — **alarm triggers, forbidden**.

To keep the alarm silent (with both odd), we need $3 \nmid$ **or** $5 \nmid$, i.
e., **at least one of $p, q$ is $3$ or $5$**.

- **$q = 3$, $p > 3$ prime:** $3 \nmid p^4 - q^4$, so $240 \nmid$ for **all** pr
imes $p > 3$. Maximize $\frac{3}{p}$: smallest $p = 5$, giving $\frac{3}{5} = 0.
6$.
- **$q = 5$, $p > 5$ prime:** $5 \nmid p^4 - q^4$, so $240 \nmid$ for **all** pr
imes $p > 5$. Maximize $\frac{5}{p}$: smallest $p = 7$, giving $\frac{5}{7} \app
rox 0.7143$.
- **$p = 3$, $q < 3$:** only $q = 2$ (handled in Case 2).
- **$p = 5$, $q < 5$:** $q \in \{2, 3\}$, giving $\frac{2}{5}$ or $\frac{3}{5}$.

**Verification of $p=7, q=5$:** $7^4 - 5^4 = 2401 - 625 = 1776 = 240 \cdot 7 + 9
6$, so $240 \nmid 1776$. ✓

---

**Case 2: $q = 2$ (the only even prime), $p$ odd prime $> 2$.**

$p^4 - 2^4 = p^4 - 16$. Since $p$ is odd, $p^4$ is odd, so $p^4 - 16$ is **odd**
. Hence $16 \nmid p^4 - 16$, so $240 \nmid$ for **all** odd primes $p > 2$. Maxi
mize $\frac{2}{p}$: smallest $p = 3$, giving $\frac{2}{3} \approx 0.6667$.

($p = 2$ is impossible since $q < p$ and no prime is less than $2$.)

---

**Comparison.** The candidate maxima are:

| Pair | $q/p$ |
|------|-------|
| $(p,q)=(7,5)$ | $5/7 \approx 0.7143$ |
| $(p,q)=(3,2)$ | $2/3 \approx 0.6667$ |
| $(p,q)=(5,3)$ | $3/5 = 0.6$ |

The largest is $\dfrac{5}{7}$, achieved at $p = 7$, $q = 5$.

No other pairs are feasible: for odd primes with neither equal to $3$ or $5$, th
e alarm always triggers; for $q = 2$, the ratio is at most $2/3$; and larger $q$
 (odd, not $3$ or $5$) require $p \in \{3,5\} < q$, which is impossible.

---

**The maximum possible value of $\dfrac{q}{p}$ is $\boxed{\dfrac{5}{7}}$.**
