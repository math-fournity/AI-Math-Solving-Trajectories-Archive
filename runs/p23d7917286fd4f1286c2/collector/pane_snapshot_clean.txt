## Proof

Let $f(x) \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ be irreducible of prime degree $p$, with exactly tw
o nonreal roots in $\mathbb{C}$. Label the roots $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_{p-2}
\in \mathbb{R}$ and $\alpha_{p-1} = \overline{\alpha_p} \notin \mathbb{R}$. Let
$K$ be the splitting field of $f$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ and $G = \operatorname{Gal}(
K/\mathbb{Q})$, viewed as a permutation group on the $p$ roots.

**Step 1: $G$ is transitive.** Since $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, $G$ a
cts transitively on the roots. Hence $p \mid |G|$.

**Step 2: $G$ contains a $p$-cycle.** By Cauchy's theorem, $G$ contains an eleme
nt of order $p$. In $S_p$ (with $p$ prime), the only permutations of order $p$ a
re $p$-cycles: the order is the lcm of cycle lengths, each length divides $p$ (s
o is $1$ or $p$), and the lengths sum to $p$, forcing a single $p$-cycle. Thus $
G$ contains a $p$-cycle.

**Step 3: $G$ contains a transposition.** Since $f$ has real (rational) coeffici
ents, complex conjugation $\tau$ permutes the roots of $f$, hence restricts to a
n automorphism $\tau|_K \in G$ fixing $\mathbb{Q}$. Now $\tau$ fixes every real
number, so it fixes the $p-2$ real roots $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_{p-2}$ and swa
ps the two nonreal conjugate roots $\alpha_{p-1} \leftrightarrow \alpha_p$. As a
 permutation, $\tau|_K$ is a transposition.

**Step 4: A transitive subgroup of $S_p$ ($p$ prime) containing a $p$-cycle and
a transposition equals $S_p$.** Relabel so the $p$-cycle is $\rho = (0\;1\;2\;\c
dots\;p-1)$ (indices in $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$) and the transposition is $(i\;
j)$ with $d = j - i \not\equiv 0 \pmod{p}$. Conjugating by powers of $\rho$ give
s all transpositions
$$(a,\; a+d), \quad a \in \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}.$$
Since $p$ is prime and $1 \le d \le p-1$, we have $\gcd(d,p)=1$, so the edges $\
{a, a+d\}$ form a single cycle $C_p$ on the $p$ vertices — a connected graph. Th
e transpositions along the edges of a connected graph on $p$ vertices generate t
he full symmetric group $S_p$. Therefore $G \supseteq S_p$, and since $G \le S_p
$, we conclude $G = S_p$.

**Conclusion.** The Galois group of $f(x)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is the full symmetr
ic group $S_p$.
