# Problem

In a futuristic industrial park, a landscape architect is designing a decorative water feature. The terrain follows a specific elevation profile defined by the function $E(x) = -x^4 + 2x^2$, where $x$ represents the horizontal distance from the center of the park. This profile creates two symmetrical hills and a central valley.

The architect decides to flood the area with a specialized reflective liquid up to a constant elevation of $k$ units. This creates three distinct regions of liquid: two "reservoirs" where the liquid fills the basins beneath the peaks of the hills (above the line $y = k$ but below the terrain curve), and one central "pool" where the liquid sits in the valley (below the line $y = k$ but above the terrain curve).

For aesthetic symmetry and fluid balance, the architect requires that the cross-sectional area of the central pool be exactly equal to the cross-sectional area of each of the two side reservoirs.

Find the specific value of the elevation $k$ that achieves this perfect balance.
