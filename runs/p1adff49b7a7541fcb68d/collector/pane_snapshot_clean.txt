## Proof

Let $v_p$ denote the extension of the $p$-adic valuation to $\overline{\mathbb{Q
}_p}$, normalized so $v_p(p) = 1$.

**Valuation of $a$.** Since $a$ is a primitive $p$-th root of unity, $a^p = 1$.
Applying $v_p$:
$$p \cdot v_p(a) = v_p(a^p) = v_p(1) = 0,$$
so $v_p(a) = 0$.

**Valuation of $b$.** Since $b$ is a root of $X^{p-1}+p$, we have $b^{p-1} = -p$
. Applying $v_p$:
$$(p-1)\cdot v_p(b) = v_p(b^{p-1}) = v_p(-p) = 1,$$
so $v_p(b) = \dfrac{1}{p-1} > 0$.

**Key observation.** The $(p-1)$-th roots of unity all lie in $\mathbb{Q}_p$ (by
 Hensel's lemma, since $\mathbb{F}_p^*$ is cyclic of order $p-1$ and $\gcd(p, p-
1) = 1$). Therefore every root of $X^{p-1}+p$ is of the form $b_0 \cdot \zeta_{p
-1}^k$ where $b_0$ is any fixed root and $\zeta_{p-1}$ is a primitive $(p-1)$-th
 root of unity. Since $v_p(\zeta_{p-1}) = 0$, all roots share the same valuation
 $\frac{1}{p-1}$. In particular, regardless of which root $b$ is chosen, $v_p(b)
 = \frac{1}{p-1}$.

Similarly, every primitive $p$-th root of unity $a = \zeta_p^j$ ($1 \le j \le p-
1$) satisfies $v_p(a) = 0$.

**Computing $v_p(a-b)$.** We have:
$$v_p(a) = 0 \neq \frac{1}{p-1} = v_p(b).$$

Since the two valuations are **distinct**, the non-Archimedean (ultrametric) pro
perty of $v_p$ gives:
$$v_p(a - b) = \min\big(v_p(a),\; v_p(b)\big) = \min\!\left(0,\; \tfrac{1}{p-1}\
right) = 0.$$

**Conclusion.** The $p$-adic absolute value is $|a-b|_p = p^{-v_p(a-b)} = p^0 =
1$, and its $p$-adic valuation is:

$$\boxed{0}$$
