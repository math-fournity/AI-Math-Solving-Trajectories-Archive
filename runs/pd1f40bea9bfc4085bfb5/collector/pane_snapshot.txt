## Proof

We seek the maximum size of a family $\mathcal{F}$ of subsets of $N_n = \{1, 2,
\ldots, n\}$ such that for any two distinct $A, B \in \mathcal{F}$, we have $A \
cup B = N_n$.

**Reformulation via complements.** For each $A \in \mathcal{F}$, let $\bar{A} =
N_n \setminus A$. The condition $A \cup B = N_n$ is equivalent to $\bar{A} \cap
\bar{B} = \emptyset$. Thus the family $\mathcal{G} = \{\bar{A} : A \in \mathcal{
F}\}$ consists of pairwise disjoint subsets of $N_n$, and $|\mathcal{G}| = |\mat
hcal{F}|$. The problem reduces to finding the maximum number of pairwise disjoin
t subsets of $N_n$.

**Upper bound.** Among the pairwise disjoint subsets in $\mathcal{G}$, at most o
ne can be the empty set (since subsets are distinct). Every non-empty member of
$\mathcal{G}$ contains at least one element of $N_n$, and since the members are
pairwise disjoint, each element of $N_n$ belongs to at most one non-empty member
. Therefore the number of non-empty members is at most $n$. Including the possib
le empty set, we get $|\mathcal{G}| \leq n + 1$.

**Construction achieving $n+1$.** Take
$$\mathcal{G} = \{\emptyset\} \cup \{\{1\}, \{2\}, \ldots, \{n\}\},$$
which has $n + 1$ pairwise disjoint subsets. The corresponding family is
$$\mathcal{F} = \{N_n\} \cup \{N_n \setminus \{i\} : i = 1, \ldots, n\}.$$

**Verification.**
- $N_n \cup (N_n \setminus \{i\}) = N_n$ for every $i$. ✓
- $(N_n \setminus \{i\}) \cup (N_n \setminus \{j\}) = N_n$ for $i \neq j$, since
 $i \in N_n \setminus \{j\}$ and $j \in N_n \setminus \{i\}$, so every element i
s covered. ✓

All $n + 1$ subsets are distinct, and every pair has union equal to $N_n$.

**Conclusion.** The maximum number of subsets that can be chosen is $\boxed{n+1}
$.
