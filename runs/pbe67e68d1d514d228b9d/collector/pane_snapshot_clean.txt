## Linearization of the Continuous Delay Logistic Equation with Strong Delay Ker
nel

### Step 1. Setup and the kernel

The equation is
$$\dot x(t) = r\,x(t)\left(1 - \frac{1}{K}\int_0^\infty k(\tau)\,x(t-\tau)\,d\ta
u\right),$$
with the **strong (gamma-2) delay kernel**
$$k(t) = \frac{t}{T^2}\,e^{-t/T},\qquad t\ge 0,$$
which is normalized:
$$\int_0^\infty k(t)\,dt = \frac{1}{T^2}\int_0^\infty t\,e^{-t/T}\,dt = \frac{1}
{T^2}\cdot T^2 = 1.\ \checkmark$$

The constant equilibrium is $x^*(t)\equiv K$, since then $\int_0^\infty k(\tau)K
\,d\tau = K$ and the bracket vanishes.

### Step 2. Linearization about $x = K$

Set $x(t) = K + y(t)$ with $|y|$ small. Then
$$\int_0^\infty k(\tau)\,x(t-\tau)\,d\tau = \int_0^\infty k(\tau)\bigl(K + y(t-\
tau)\bigr)\,d\tau = K + \int_0^\infty k(\tau)\,y(t-\tau)\,d\tau,$$
using $\int k = 1$. Hence
$$1 - \frac{1}{K}\int_0^\infty k(\tau)\,x(t-\tau)\,d\tau = -\frac{1}{K}\int_0^\i
nfty k(\tau)\,y(t-\tau)\,d\tau.$$

Substituting,
$$\dot y(t) = r\bigl(K + y(t)\bigr)\left(-\frac{1}{K}\int_0^\infty k(\tau)\,y(t-
\tau)\,d\tau\right).$$

Discarding the second-order term $y(t)\cdot\int k\,y$ (product of two small quan
tities), the **linearized equation** is
$$\boxed{\;\dot y(t) = -\,r\int_0^\infty k(\tau)\,y(t-\tau)\,d\tau.\;}$$

### Step 3. Characteristic equation

Seek exponential solutions $y(t) = e^{\lambda t}$. Then $\dot y = \lambda e^{\la
mbda t}$ and
$$\int_0^\infty k(\tau)\,y(t-\tau)\,d\tau = e^{\lambda t}\int_0^\infty k(\tau)\,
e^{-\lambda\tau}\,d\tau = e^{\lambda t}\,\widehat{k}(\lambda),$$
where $\widehat{k}(\lambda)$ is the Laplace transform of $k$. The characteristic

$$\lambda + r\,\widehat{k}(\lambda) = 0.$$

### Step 4. Laplace transform of the strong kernel

$$\widehat{k}(\lambda) = \int_0^\infty \frac{t}{T^2}\,e^{-t/T}\,e^{-\lambda t}\,
dt = \frac{1}{T^2}\int_0^\infty t\,e^{-(\lambda + 1/T)t}\,dt = \frac{1}{T^2}\cdo
t\frac{1}{(\lambda + 1/T)^2} = \frac{1}{(1+\lambda T)^2}.$$

### Step 5. The characteristic equation in polynomial form

$$\lambda + \frac{r}{(1+\lambda T)^2} = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \lambda(1+\l
ambda T)^2 + r = 0.$$

Expanding:
$$\boxed{\;T^2\lambda^3 + 2T\lambda^2 + \lambda + r = 0.\;}$$

### Step 6. Verification that there are no positive real eigenvalues

Let $\lambda > 0$ be real. Consider the left-hand side
$$F(\lambda) = \lambda(1+\lambda T)^2 + r.$$

With the biological parameters $r > 0$ and $T > 0$:
- $\lambda > 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \lambda > 0$,
- $1 + \lambda T > 1 > 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; (1+\lambda T)^2 > 0$,
- therefore $\lambda(1+\lambda T)^2 > 0$,
- and $r > 0$,

so
$$F(\lambda) = \underbrace{\lambda(1+\lambda T)^2}_{>\,0} + \underbrace{r}_{>\,0

Equivalently, in the expanded form every term $T^2\lambda^3$, $2T\lambda^2$, $\l
ambda$, $r$ is strictly positive for $\lambda > 0$, so their sum cannot vanish.

Since $F(\lambda) > 0$ for all $\lambda > 0$, the characteristic equation $F(\la
mbda) = 0$ admits **no positive real root**. Hence the linearized system about t
