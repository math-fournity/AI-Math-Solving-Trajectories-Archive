No, it is not true in general that $N = M$. Here is a counterexample and the sur
rounding analysis.

**Counterexample.** Take $R = \mathbb{Z}$, which is a PID. Let $M = \mathbb{Z}$,
 a free $\mathbb{Z}$-module of rank $a = 1$. Let $N = 2\mathbb{Z} = \{2k : k \in
 \mathbb{Z}\}$.

- $N$ is a submodule of $M$: it is closed under addition and under multiplicatio
n by integers.
- $N$ is free of rank $1$: the map $\mathbb{Z} \to N$, $k \mapsto 2k$, is a $\ma
thbb{Z}$-module isomorphism (injective and surjective onto $N$). So $\operatorna
me{rank}(N) = 1 = \operatorname{rank}(M) = a$.
- Yet $N \neq M$, since $1 \in M$ but $1 \notin 2\mathbb{Z}$.

Thus the statement fails: $N$ is a free submodule of full rank $a$ that is stric
tly contained in $M$.

**Why this happens (structural explanation).** Over a PID, every submodule of a
free module of rank $a$ is free of rank $\leq a$ (this is the standard structure
 theorem). When $\operatorname{rank}(N) = a$, the quotient $M/N$ is a finitely g
enerated module of rank $0$, hence a **torsion** module. The structure theorem f
or finitely generated modules over a PID gives

$$M/N \cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^{s} R/(d_i), \qquad d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_
s, \quad d_i \notin R^{\times}.$$

This quotient is zero (i.e., $N = M$) if and only if $s = 0$, i.e., there are no
 nontrivial invariant factors. In the counterexample above, $M/N = \mathbb{Z}/2\
mathbb{Z} \cong R/(2)$, a nonzero torsion module, reflecting the single invarian
t factor $d_1 = 2$.

Equivalently, using the stacked-basis theorem for PIDs: there exist a basis $e_1
, \dots, e_a$ of $M$ and elements $d_1, \dots, d_a \in R$ (with $d_1 \mid \cdots
 \mid d_a$) such that $d_1 e_1, \dots, d_a e_a$ is a basis of $N$. Then $N = M$
holds precisely when every $d_i$ is a unit. When $\operatorname{rank}(N) = a$, a
ll $a$ slots are occupied (no $d_i$ is zero), but the $d_i$ may be non-units — a
s with $d_1 = 2$ in the example — giving a proper inclusion $N \subsetneq M$.

**Conclusion.** The implication "$\operatorname{rank}(N) = \operatorname{rank}(M
) = a \Rightarrow N = M$" is **false** over a PID. The rank condition only force
s $M/N$ to be torsion, not zero. A concrete witness is $R = \mathbb{Z}$, $M = \m
athbb{Z}$, $N = 2\mathbb{Z}$.
