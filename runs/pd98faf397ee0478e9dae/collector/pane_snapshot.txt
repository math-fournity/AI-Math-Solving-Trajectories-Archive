## Proof

**Setup.** We have a smooth embedding $f: S^1 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ with $0 \notin f
(S^1)$ (so that $\lambda$ is well-defined), and $f(S^1) \cap \{z=0\}$ consists o
f exactly two points. The map $\lambda: S^1 \to S^2$ is smooth.

**Key observation: dimension mismatch.** The domain $S^1$ is a compact oriented
1-manifold, while the codomain $S^2$ is a compact oriented 2-manifold. The (Brou
wer) degree of a smooth map is defined as

$$\deg(\lambda) = \sum_{x \in \lambda^{-1}(p)} \operatorname{sgn}(d\lambda_x)$$

for any regular value $p \in S^2$.

**Applying Sard's theorem.** By Sard's theorem, the set of regular values of $\l
ambda$ has full measure in $S^2$, so regular values exist. For any regular value
 $p \in S^2$, the preimage $\lambda^{-1}(p)$ is a smooth submanifold of $S^1$ of
 dimension

$$\dim S^1 - \dim S^2 = 1 - 2 = -1.$$

A smooth manifold of negative dimension is necessarily empty. Therefore $\lambda
^{-1}(p) = \varnothing$ for every regular value $p$, and

$$\deg(\lambda) = \sum_{x \in \varnothing} \operatorname{sgn}(d\lambda_x) = 0.$$

**Consistency with the $z = 0$ condition.** The condition that $f(S^1)$ intersec
ts $\{z=0\}$ at exactly two points tells us that $\lambda(S^1)$ crosses the equa
tor $E = S^2 \cap \{z=0\}$ at exactly two points (since the sign of the $z$-comp
onent of $\lambda(x) = f(x)/\|f(x)\|$ agrees with that of $f(x)$). Because $\lam
bda(S^1)$ is a **closed** curve (image of $S^1$), it must return to its starting
 hemisphere, so the number of upward crossings equals the number of downward cro
ssings. With exactly two crossings, one is upward and one is downward, giving a
net crossing count of $0$—consistent with $\deg(\lambda) = 0$.

**Homotopy-theoretic confirmation.** Since $\pi_1(S^2) = 0$, every map $S^1 \to
S^2$ is null-homotopic. A null-homotopic map has degree $0$, providing an indepe
ndent verification.
