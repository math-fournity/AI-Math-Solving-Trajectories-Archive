## Proof

Let $H = F - G$. We will show $H \equiv 0$ in $V$.

From the hypotheses, in $V$:
- $\operatorname{curl} H = \operatorname{curl} F - \operatorname{curl} G = 0$,
- $\operatorname{div} H = \operatorname{div} F - \operatorname{div} G = 0$,

and on $S$:
- $H \cdot \vec{n} = F \cdot \vec{n} - G \cdot \vec{n} = 0$.

Since $V$ is **convex**, it is in particular simply connected (any loop in $V$ c
ontracts to a point within $V$). A vector field with vanishing curl on a simply
connected region is a gradient: there exists a scalar function $\varphi \in C^2(
V)$ such that

$$H = \nabla \varphi \quad \text{in } V.$$

The condition $\operatorname{div} H = 0$ becomes

$$\Delta \varphi = \operatorname{div}(\nabla \varphi) = 0 \quad \text{in } V,$$

so $\varphi$ is **harmonic** in $V$.

The boundary condition $H \cdot \vec{n} = 0$ on $S$ becomes

$$\frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial n} = \nabla \varphi \cdot \vec{n} = 0 \quad \
text{on } S.$$

Now apply Green's first identity with $u = v = \varphi$:

$$\int_V \bigl(|\nabla \varphi|^2 + \varphi\,\Delta \varphi\bigr)\,dV = \oint_S
\varphi\,\frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial n}\,dS.$$

Both terms on the right-hand integrand vanish: $\Delta\varphi = 0$ in $V$, and $
\frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial n} = 0$ on $S$. Hence

$$\int_V |\nabla \varphi|^2\,dV = 0.$$

Since $|\nabla \varphi|^2 \geq 0$ is continuous, this forces $\nabla \varphi \eq
uiv 0$ throughout $V$. Therefore

$$H = \nabla \varphi = 0 \quad \text{in } V,$$

i.e.,

$$F = G \quad \text{everywhere in } V.$$

**Conclusion:** Yes, $F = G$ everywhere in $V$. The three conditions (equal curl
, equal divergence, equal normal component on the boundary) together uniquely de
termine a $C^1$ vector field on a convex region.
