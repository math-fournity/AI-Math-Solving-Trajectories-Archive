## Proof

**Claim.** Let $R$ be a non-zero ring and $X$ an $R$-scheme. Then $H^0(X,\mathca
l{O}_X)$ is the zero ring if and only if $X=\varnothing$. In particular, **yes**
, it is possible: take $X$ to be the empty scheme.

---

### The empty scheme is an $R$-scheme

The empty scheme $\varnothing$ is a scheme (it is covered by the empty affine co
ver). For any scheme $S$ there is a unique morphism $\varnothing\to S$ (the empt
y morphism), so in particular there is a unique morphism $\varnothing\to\operato
rname{Spec}R$. Hence $\varnothing$ is an $R$-scheme.

### $H^0(\varnothing,\mathcal{O}_\varnothing)$ is the zero ring

For any sheaf of rings $\mathcal{F}$ on a topological space, the sections over t
he empty set are the terminal object in the category of rings, i.e. the zero rin
g: this follows from the sheaf axiom applied to the empty cover of $\varnothing$
. Concretely, $\mathcal{F}(\varnothing)$ must be a ring $B$ such that the empty
product condition forces $B$ to be the zero ring (the unique ring $B$ with $1_B=
0_B$). Thus

$$H^0(\varnothing,\mathcal{O}_\varnothing)=\mathcal{O}_\varnothing(\varnothing)\
cong 0.$$

So the empty $R$-scheme gives an example.

### Necessity: $H^0(X,\mathcal{O}_X)=0\Rightarrow X=\varnothing$

Suppose $X$ is a scheme with $1=0$ in $\mathcal{O}_X(X)$. Assume for contradicti
on that $X\neq\varnothing$, and pick a point $x\in X$. Let $U=\operatorname{Spec
}A$ be an affine open neighborhood of $x$. The restriction homomorphism

$$\rho_{X,U}:\mathcal{O}_X(X)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_X(U)=A$$

is a unital ring homomorphism, so it sends $1\mapsto 1$ and $0\mapsto 0$. Since
$1=0$ in $\mathcal{O}_X(X)$, their images coincide, giving $1=0$ in $A$. Hence $
A$ is the zero ring.

But the zero ring has no prime ideals: a prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}\subset A$ mus
t be a **proper** ideal ($\mathfrak{p}\neq A$), yet in the zero ring the only id
eal is $\{0\}=A$ itself, which is not proper. Therefore

$$\operatorname{Spec}A=\operatorname{Spec}(0)=\varnothing,$$

contradicting $x\in U=\operatorname{Spec}A$. Hence $X=\varnothing$.

### Conclusion

Combining both directions:

$$H^0(X,\mathcal{O}_X)\text{ is the zero ring}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad X=\v
arnothing.$$

Since the empty scheme is a valid $R$-scheme for every (non-zero) ring $R$, **it
 is possible** for $H^0(X,\mathcal{O}_X)$ to be a zero ring, and this occurs exa
ctly for the empty $R$-scheme.
