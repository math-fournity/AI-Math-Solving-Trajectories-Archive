## Answer: Yes.

**Claim.** If $R = \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{c}]$ (with $c$ a non-square integer) is a UF
D, then $R$ is a PID.

**Proof.** The argument proceeds in three steps.

**Step 1: $R$ is a one-dimensional Noetherian domain.**

Since $c$ is not a perfect square, $\sqrt{c}\notin\mathbb{Q}$, so $R$ is a genui
ne domain (a subring of $\mathbb{C}$). As a $\mathbb{Z}$-module, $R$ is generate
d by $\{1,\sqrt{c}\}$, hence is finitely generated over $\mathbb{Z}$; in particu
lar $R$ is Noetherian (being a finitely generated module over the Noetherian rin
g $\mathbb{Z}$, hence a Noetherian ring). The element $\sqrt{c}$ satisfies the m
onic polynomial $x^2 - c \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$, so $\sqrt{c}$ is integral over $\ma
thbb{Z}$, making $R$ an integral extension of $\mathbb{Z}$. Integral extensions
preserve Krull dimension, so
$$\dim R = \dim \mathbb{Z} = 1.$$

**Step 2: A UFD is integrally closed; hence $R$ is a Dedekind domain.**

*UFDs are integrally closed.* Let $R$ be a UFD with fraction field $K$, and let
$x = a/b \in K^\times$ (with $a,b\in R$, $\gcd(a,b)=1$) be integral over $R$. Th
en $x$ satisfies $x^n + r_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+r_0 = 0$ with $r_i \in R$. Multipl
ying through by $b^n$:
$$a^n + r_{n-1}a^{n-1}b + \cdots + r_0 b^n = 0,$$
so $a^n = -b\,(r_{n-1}a^{n-1}+\cdots+r_0 b^{n-1})$, giving $b \mid a^n$. Since $
\gcd(a,b)=1$ in a UFD, $b$ must be a unit, so $x = a/b \in R$. ∎

By hypothesis $R$ is a UFD, so by the above $R$ is integrally closed. Combined w
ith Step 1 ($R$ is Noetherian, one-dimensional, and a domain), we conclude:

$$R \text{ is Noetherian} + \dim R = 1 + R \text{ integrally closed} \;\Longrigh
tarrow\; R \text{ is a Dedekind domain.}$$

**Step 3: A Dedekind domain that is a UFD is a PID.**

In a Dedekind domain, every nonzero ideal factors uniquely as a product of nonze
ro prime ideals. Since $R$ is one-dimensional, every nonzero prime ideal has hei
ght $1$. In a UFD, every height-$1$ prime ideal is principal (generated by a pri
me element). Therefore every nonzero prime ideal of $R$ is principal, and conseq
uently every nonzero ideal — being a product of principal prime ideals — is prin
cipal. Including the zero ideal, **every ideal of $R$ is principal**, i.e., $R$
is a PID. ∎

**Conclusion.** For $R = \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{c}]$ with $c$ a non-square integer: if
 $R$ is a UFD, then $R$ is a PID. The answer is **yes**.
