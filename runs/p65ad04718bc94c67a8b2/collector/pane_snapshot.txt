Compactness of an Infinite-Rank Operator with Complete Eigenbasis and
Eigenvalues Tending to Zero

Setup

Let $H$ be a separable Hilbert space and $T: H \to H$ a bounded linear operator.
Suppose:

  • ${e_i}_{i=1}^\infty$ is a complete orthonormal system of eigenvectors for
  $T$, i.e., $\overline{\operatorname{span}}{e_i} = H$ and $Te_i = \lambda_i
  e_i$.
  • Each nonzero eigenvalue has finite multiplicity.
  • The eigenvalues can be ordered so that $\lambda_i \to 0$.
  • $T$ has infinite rank (infinitely many nonzero $\lambda_i$).

Claim: $T$ is compact.

Proof

Step 1: Spectral expansion of $T$.

Since ${e_i}$ is a complete orthonormal system, every $x \in H$ admits the
expansion

$$x = \sum_{i=1}^\infty \langle x, e_i\rangle, e_i.$$

Because $T$ is bounded (hence continuous), we may interchange $T$ with the
convergent sum:

$$Tx = T!\left(\sum_{i=1}^\infty \langle x,e_i\rangle, e_i\right) = \sum_{i=1}
^\infty \langle x,e_i\rangle, Te_i = \sum_{i=1}^\infty \lambda_i \langle x,
e_i\rangle, e_i. \tag{1}$$

Step 2: Finite-rank approximation.

For each $n \in \mathbb{N}$, define the finite-rank operator

$$T_n x = \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i \langle x, e_i\rangle, e_i.$$

Each $T_n$ has range contained in $\operatorname{span}{e_1, \dots, e_n}$, so $
\operatorname{rank}(T_n) \leq n$; in particular $T_n$ is compact.

Step 3: Norm convergence $T_n \to T$.

From (1), the remainder is

$$(T - T_n)x = \sum_{i=n+1}^\infty \lambda_i \langle x, e_i\rangle, e_i.$$

By orthogonality of ${e_i}$ (Pythagorean theorem):

$$|(T - T_n)x|^2 = \sum_{i=n+1}^\infty |\lambda_i|^2, |\langle x, e_i\rangle|^2.
$$

Since $\lambda_i \to 0$, define $M_n := \sup_{i > n} |\lambda_i|$. Then $M_n \to
0$ as $n \to \infty$. Bounding each term:

$$|(T - T_n)x|^2 \leq M_n^2 \sum_{i=n+1}^\infty |\langle x, e_i\rangle|^2 \leq
M_n^2, |x|^2,$$

where the last inequality uses Bessel's inequality ($\sum_{i=1}^\infty |\langle
x, e_i\rangle|^2 \leq |x|^2$). Taking the supremum over $|x| = 1$:

$$\boxed{|T - T_n| \leq M_n = \sup_{i > n} |\lambda_i| \xrightarrow{n\to\infty}
0.}$$

Step 4: Conclusion.

$T$ is the operator-norm limit of finite-rank operators $T_n$. A classical
theorem in functional analysis states:

│ A bounded operator on a Hilbert space is compact if and only if it is the norm
│ limit of finite-rank operators.

Therefore $T$ is compact. Since $T$ has infinitely many nonzero eigenvalues
(infinite rank), it is specifically an infinite-rank compact operator — a
legitimate class exemplified by diagonal operators $\operatorname{diag}(1, 1/2,
1/3, \dots)$ on $\ell^2$.

Remark on the hypotheses. Each assumption plays an essential role:

  • Completeness of the eigenbasis ensures the expansion (1) represents $T$ on
  all of $H$, not just a dense subspace.
  • Orthonormality of the eigenfunctions gives the Pythagorean identity that
  yields the clean norm bound $|T - T_n| \leq \sup_{i>n}|\lambda_i|$.
  • $\lambda_i \to 0$ is precisely the condition forcing $M_n \to 0$, i.e., norm
  convergence of the finite-rank approximants.
  • Finite multiplicity of nonzero eigenvalues is consistent with (and in fact
  implied by) compactness, since a compact operator can only have $0$ as an
  accumulation point of its spectrum, with each nonzero spectral value being an
  eigenvalue of finite multiplicity.

Conversely, these conditions are also necessary for a self-adjoint compact
operator (spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators), so the
characterization is tight: an operator with a complete orthonormal eigenbasis,
finite-multiplicity eigenvalues, and $\lambda_i \to 0$ is compact if and only if
these conditions hold.
