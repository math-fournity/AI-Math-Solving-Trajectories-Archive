## Proof

**Setup.** Let $q(\zeta)=(\zeta-e_1)(\zeta-e_2)(\zeta-e_3)$ and let $\wp(z)$ be
the Weierstrass elliptic function for the lattice $\Lambda$ whose invariants are
 $(g_2,g_3)$ determined by the roots $e_1,e_2,e_3$, so that

$$
\wp'(z)^2=4\wp(z)^3-g_2\wp(z)-g_3=4(\wp(z)-e_1)(\wp(z)-e_2)(\wp(z)-e_3)=4q(\wp(z
)). \tag{1}
$$

The map $\wp:\mathbb{C}/\Lambda\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}$ is a double cover branch
ed at $e_1,e_2,e_3$ (and $\infty$), hence it uniformizes the algebraic curve $w^
2=q(\zeta)$.

---

**Step 1 — Split into even/odd parts in $w=\sqrt{q(\zeta)}$.** Any rational func
tion $R(\zeta,w)$ can be decomposed (by grouping even and odd powers of $w$, usi
ng $w^2=q(\zeta)$) as

$$
R(\zeta,w)=A(\zeta)+B(\zeta)\,w, \qquad A,B\in\mathbb{C}(\zeta). \tag{2}
$$

Hence

$$
\int_{\zeta_0}^{\zeta_1}R(\zeta,\sqrt{q(\zeta)})\,d\zeta
=\underbrace{\int_{\zeta_0}^{\zeta_1}A(\zeta)\,d\zeta}_{\text{(I)}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\int_{\zeta_0}^{\zeta_1}B(\zeta)\sqrt{q(\zeta)}\,d\zeta}_{\text{(II)
}}. \tag{3}
$$

Part (I) is the integral of a *rational function* of $\zeta$, so it is **element
ary** (a combination of rational functions and logarithms) and carries no ellipt
ic content. The genuinely elliptic part is (II).

---

**Step 2 — Uniformize (II) by $\zeta=\wp(z)$.** Choose $z_0,z_1$ with $\wp(z_0)=
\zeta_0$, $\wp(z_1)=\zeta_1$ (possible since $\wp$ is surjective onto $\widehat{
\mathbb{C}}$), and fix a branch of $\sqrt{q}$ along the path. Under the substitu
tion $\zeta=\wp(z)$,

$$
d\zeta=\wp'(z)\,dz, \qquad \sqrt{q(\wp(z))}=\tfrac12\wp'(z) \quad\text{(by (1),
on the chosen branch).} \tag{4}
$$

Therefore

$$
\text{(II)}=\int_{z_0}^{z_1}B(\wp(z))\cdot\tfrac12\wp'(z)\cdot\wp'(z)\,dz
=\frac12\int_{z_0}^{z_1}B(\wp(z))\,\wp'(z)^2\,dz. \tag{5}
$$

Using (1) again, $\wp'(z)^2=4q(\wp(z))$, so

$$
\text{(II)}=\int_{z_0}^{z_1}B(\wp(z))\cdot 2q(\wp(z))\,dz
=\int_{z_0}^{z_1}R_1(\wp(z))\,dz, \tag{6}
$$

where

$$
R_1(\wp):=2\,B(\wp)\,q(\wp)\in\mathbb{C}(\wp) \tag{7}
$$

is a **rational function of $\wp$ alone** — the factor $\wp'$ has been eliminate
d precisely because the odd power of $\sqrt{q}$ supplied one factor of $\wp'$ wh
ich, multiplied by the $d\zeta=\wp'\,dz$ factor, produced $\wp'^2=4q(\wp)$, a ra
tional function of $\wp$.

---

**Step 3 — The even part (I) is elementary, hence harmless.** With $\zeta=\wp(z)
$,

$$
\text{(I)}=\int_{z_0}^{z_1}A(\wp(z))\,\wp'(z)\,dz=\int_{\zeta_0}^{\zeta_1}A(\zet
a)\,d\zeta, \tag{8}
$$

i.e. it is just the integral of the rational function $A$, computable by partial
 fractions. It does not require elliptic functions. Thus, up to an explicitly co
mputable elementary term, the original elliptic integral is

$$
\boxed{\;\int_{\zeta_0}^{\zeta_1}R(\zeta,\sqrt{q(\zeta)})\,d\zeta
\;=\;\text{(elementary term)}\;+\;\int_{z_0}^{z_1}R_1(\wp(z))\,dz\;} \tag{9}
$$

with $R_1$ rational and given by (7).

---

**Why this works — the structural reason.** The curve $C:w^2=q(\zeta)$ is genus
$1$. The Weierstrass map

$$
\phi:\mathbb{C}/\Lambda\longrightarrow C,\qquad z\longmapsto(\wp(z),\tfrac12\wp'
(z)) \tag{10}
$$

is a biholomorphism (an isomorphism of elliptic curves). Under $\phi$, the holom
orphic differential $d\zeta$ on $C$ pulls back to $\wp'(z)\,dz$, and the differe
ntial $\sqrt{q(\zeta)}\,d\zeta=w\,d\zeta$ pulls back to

$$
\tfrac12\wp'(z)\cdot\wp'(z)\,dz=\tfrac12\wp'(z)^2\,dz=2q(\wp(z))\,dz, \tag{11}
$$

which is a rational multiple of $dz$, i.e. of the invariant differential on $\ma
thbb{C}/\Lambda$. Since every abelian differential on a genus-$1$ curve is a rat
ional multiple of the invariant differential, *every* integral of a rational dif
ferential on $C$ — which is exactly what $\int R(\zeta,\sqrt{q(\zeta)})\,d\zeta$
 is — reduces, modulo elementary terms, to $\int R_1(\wp(z))\,dz$. The even part
 contributes the elementary term (pole/residue and logarithmic pieces), and the
odd part contributes the pure $\wp$-integral. $\blacksquare$
