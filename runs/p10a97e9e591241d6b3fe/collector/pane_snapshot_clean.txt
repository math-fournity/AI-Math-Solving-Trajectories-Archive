## Proof

**Claim:** For every $n \geq 1$, there exists a basis of $M_n(\mathbb{R})$ consi
sting entirely of diagonalizable matrices.

**Key Lemma.** A rank-one matrix $A = uv^\top$ (with $u, v \in \mathbb{R}^n \set
minus \{0\}$) is diagonalizable over $\mathbb{R}$ if and only if $v^\top u \neq
0$.

*Proof of Lemma.* The matrix $A = uv^\top$ has rank 1. Its eigenvalues are $v^\t
op u$ (with eigenvector $u$, since $Au = u(v^\top u) = (v^\top u)u$) and $0$ wit
h algebraic multiplicity $n-1$. The null space of $A$ is $\ker(uv^\top) = \{x \i
n \mathbb{R}^n : v^\top x = 0\} = v^\perp$, which has dimension $n-1$.

- If $v^\top u \neq 0$: the eigenvalue $v^\top u \neq 0$ has geometric multiplic
ity 1 (eigenvector $u$), and the eigenvalue $0$ has geometric multiplicity $n-1$
 (the space $v^\perp$), matching its algebraic multiplicity. Hence $A$ is diagon
alizable.
- If $v^\top u = 0$: the only eigenvalue is $0$ with algebraic multiplicity $n$,
 but geometric multiplicity $n-1 < n$, so $A$ is not diagonalizable (in fact $A^
2 = u(v^\top u)v^\top = 0$, so $A$ is nilpotent). $\square$

**Construction.** Let $\{e_1, \dots, e_n\}$ be the standard basis of $\mathbb{R}
^n$, and let $E_{ij} = e_i e_j^\top$ denote the standard matrix units. Define $n
^2$ matrices:

$$A_{ii} = E_{ii} = e_i e_i^\top \quad (1 \leq i \leq n),$$

$$A_{ij} = E_{ij} + E_{ii} = e_i(e_j + e_i)^\top \quad (1 \leq i \neq j \leq n).
$$

**Each $A_{ij}$ is diagonalizable:**

- $A_{ii} = E_{ii}$ is diagonal, hence diagonalizable.
- For $i \neq j$: $A_{ij} = e_i(e_j + e_i)^\top$ is rank one, and $(e_j + e_i)^\
top e_i = 0 + 1 = 1 \neq 0$, so by the Lemma, $A_{ij}$ is diagonalizable.

**Linear independence.** Suppose

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} c_{ii}\, A_{ii} + \sum_{\substack{i,j=1 \\ i \neq j}}^{n} c_{ij
}\, A_{ij} = 0.$$

Expanding using $A_{ij} = E_{ij} + E_{ii}$ for $i \neq j$:

$$\sum_{i \neq j} c_{ij}\, E_{ij} + \sum_{i=1}^{n}\!\left(c_{ii} + \sum_{j \neq
i} c_{ij}\right) E_{ii} = 0.$$

Since the matrix units $\{E_{ij}\}$ are linearly independent:

- The coefficient of $E_{ij}$ for $i \neq j$ gives $c_{ij} = 0$ for all $i \neq
j$.
- The coefficient of $E_{ii}$ then gives $c_{ii} + 0 = 0$, so $c_{ii} = 0$ for a
ll $i$.

All coefficients vanish, so $\{A_{ij}\}_{1 \leq i,j \leq n}$ is linearly indepen
dent. Since there are $n^2 = \dim M_n(\mathbb{R})$ such matrices, they form a ba
sis.

**Conclusion.** The collection $\{A_{ii}\}_{i=1}^n \cup \{A_{ij}\}_{i \neq j}$ i
s a basis of $M_n(\mathbb{R})$ in which every element is diagonalizable. Therefo
re, such a basis exists for every $n \geq 1$. $\blacksquare$
