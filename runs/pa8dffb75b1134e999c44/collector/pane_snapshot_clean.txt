## Solution

**Problem.** In $A_5$, find the probability that a randomly selected pair of dis
tinct elements generates a cyclic subgroup.

### Step 1: Element orders in $A_5$

$|A_5| = 60$. The conjugacy classes and orders are:

| Order | Type | Count |
|-------|------|-------|
| 1 | identity | 1 |
| 2 | double transposition $(ab)(cd)$ | $\binom{5}{4}\cdot 3 = 15$ |
| 3 | 3-cycle $(abc)$ | $\binom{5}{3}\cdot 2 = 20$ |
| 5 | 5-cycle $(abcde)$ | $4! = 24$ |

Check: $1+15+20+24 = 60$. ✓

**Key fact:** Every non-identity element of $A_5$ has **prime** order ($2, 3,$ o
r $5$). Hence every nontrivial cyclic subgroup has prime order.

### Step 2: When is $\langle a, b\rangle$ cyclic?

$\langle a, b\rangle$ is cyclic iff $a$ and $b$ both lie in a common cyclic subg
roup $\langle g\rangle$.

Suppose $a, b \neq e$ and $\langle a, b\rangle$ is cyclic of order $r$. Then $a,
 b \in \langle g\rangle$ with $|g| = r$, so $\operatorname{ord}(a) \mid r$ and $
\operatorname{ord}(b) \mid r$. Since $r \in \{1,2,3,5\}$ is prime (or 1), two di
stinct primes cannot both divide $r$. Therefore:

- If exactly one of $a, b$ is the identity, then $\langle a, b\rangle = \langle\
text{non-identity one}\rangle$, always cyclic.
- If both are non-identity, then $\langle a, b\rangle$ is cyclic **iff** $a$ and
 $b$ have the **same** (prime) order **and** lie in the **same** cyclic subgroup
 of that order.

### Step 3: Count cyclic subgroups

| Order $p$ | Elements of order $p$ | Elements per subgroup | Number of subgroup
s |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------------|---------------------
|
| 2 | 15 | 1 | 15 |
| 3 | 20 | 2 | 10 |
| 5 | 24 | 4 | 6 |

### Step 4: Count unordered pairs $\{a,b\}$, $a\neq b$, with $\langle a,b\rangle
$ cyclic

- **Pairs with the identity:** $\{e, b\}$ for each $b \neq e$: $59$ pairs.
- **Order 2 subgroups:** each has only 1 non-identity element → $0$ pairs.
- **Order 3 subgroups:** each has 2 non-identity elements → $\binom{2}{2}=1$ pai
r × $10$ subgroups = $10$ pairs.
- **Order 5 subgroups:** each has 4 non-identity elements → $\binom{4}{2}=6$ pai
rs × $6$ subgroups = $36$ pairs.

**Total cyclic pairs:** $59 + 0 + 10 + 36 = 105$.

### Step 5: Compute the probability

Total unordered pairs of distinct elements: $\binom{60}{2} = 1770$.

$$P = \frac{105}{1770} = \frac{105 \div 15}{1770 \div 15} = \frac{7}{118}.$$

(Verification with ordered pairs: $210$ cyclic ordered pairs out of $60\cdot 59
= 3540$, giving $210/3540 = 7/118$. ✓)

### Answer

$$\boxed{\dfrac{7}{118}}$$
