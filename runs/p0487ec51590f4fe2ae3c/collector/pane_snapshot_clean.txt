## Fundamental Group of the Klein Bottle

### Construction of $K$ as a quotient

The Klein bottle $K$ is obtained from the unit square $[0,1]\times[0,1]$ by iden
tifying:
- $(0, y) \sim (1, y)$ for all $y \in [0,1]$ — the two vertical edges glued with
 the **same** orientation (producing a cylinder),
- $(x, 0) \sim (1-x, 1)$ for all $x \in [0,1]$ — the two horizontal edges glued
with **opposite** orientation.

Label the bottom edge $a$ (traversed left-to-right), the right edge $b$ (travers
ed bottom-to-top), the top edge $a$ (traversed right-to-left, hence $a^{-1}$), a
nd the left edge $b$ (traversed top-to-bottom). Reading around the boundary of t
he square counterclockwise gives the **edge word**

$$w = a\, b\, a^{-1}\, b.$$

### CW-structure

This polygonal presentation gives $K$ the structure of a CW complex with:
- **one 0-cell** $v$ (all four corners are identified to a single vertex),
- **two 1-cells** $a$ and $b$ (the two pairs of identified edges),
- **one 2-cell** $e$ attached along the loop determined by $w = a\, b\, a^{-1}\,
 b$.

### Applying the standard theorem

**Theorem** (Fundamental group of a CW complex via the 2-skeleton). *If $X$ is a
 connected CW complex with a single 0-cell $v$, 1-cells $\{e_i\}_{i\in I}$, and
2-cells $\{f_j\}_{j\in J}$ attached along loops $\{w_j\}$, then*
$$\pi_1(X, v) \cong \langle\, \{e_i\}_{i\in I} \;\big|\; \{w_j = 1\}_{j\in J}\,\
rangle.$$

*Proof sketch of the theorem.* The 1-skeleton $X^{(1)}$ is a wedge of $|I|$ circ
les, so $\pi_1(X^{(1)}, v) \cong F_I$, the free group on $\{e_i\}$. Each 2-cell
$f_j$ is attached along a loop $w_j$ in $X^{(1)}$; by the Seifert–van Kampen the
orem, attaching $f_j$ kills exactly the class $[w_j]$, imposing the relation $w_
j = 1$. Attaching cells of dimension $\geq 3$ does not change $\pi_1$ (by cellul
ar approximation, any map $S^1 \to X$ and any homotopy can be pushed into the 2-
skeleton). $\square$

### Applying to $K$

For the Klein bottle, $I = \{a, b\}$ and there is a single 2-cell attached along
 $w = a\, b\, a^{-1}\, b$. Therefore:

$$\boxed{\pi_1(K) \;\cong\; \langle\, a,\, b \;\mid\; a\, b\, a^{-1}\, b = 1\,\r
angle.}$$

### Equivalent reformulations

The relation $a\, b\, a^{-1}\, b = 1$ can be rewritten as:

1. $a\, b\, a^{-1} = b^{-1}$, i.e. **conjugation by $a$ inverts $b$**.
2. $a\, b = b^{-1}\, a$.

### Structure of the group

The subgroup $\langle b \rangle$ is normal (since $a\, b\, a^{-1} = b^{-1} \in \
langle b \rangle$), and the quotient by it is $\langle a \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z
}$. Thus we have a short exact sequence

$$1 \longrightarrow \langle b \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z} \longrightarrow \pi_1(K)
\longrightarrow \langle a \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z} \longrightarrow 1,$$

exhibiting $\pi_1(K)$ as a **semidirect product** $\mathbb{Z} \rtimes \mathbb{Z}
$, where the generator $a$ of the quotient $\mathbb{Z}$ acts on the kernel $\mat
hbb{Z} = \langle b \rangle$ by inversion $b \mapsto b^{-1}$ (the unique nontrivi
al automorphism of $\mathbb{Z}$):

$$\pi_1(K) \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z} \rtimes_{-1} \mathbb{Z}.$$

### Distinctness from the torus

For comparison, the torus $T$ has edge word $a\, b\, a^{-1}\, b^{-1}$, giving $\
pi_1(T) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$. The Klein bottle's relation $a\, b\, a^{-1}\, b = 1
$ (note: $b$, not $b^{-1}$, in the last position) is what makes the group **non-
abelian**: indeed $a\, b = b^{-1}\, a \neq b\, a$ (unless $b = b^{-1}$, i.e. $b^
2 = 1$, which does not hold in this torsion-free group). One can verify $\pi_1(K
)$ is torsion-free (it acts freely on $\mathbb{R}^2$ as a deck group of the univ
ersal cover), so $b$ has infinite order and the group is genuinely non-abelian.

### Universal cover (sanity check)

The group $\pi_1(K) = \langle a, b \mid a b a^{-1} = b^{-1}\rangle$ acts on $\ma
thbb{R}^2$ by isometries:
$$a \cdot (x, y) = (x+1,\; -y), \qquad b \cdot (x, y) = (x,\; y+1).$$
Indeed $a\, b\, a^{-1}(x,y) = a\, b\,(x-1, -y) = a\,(x-1, -y+1) = (x, y-1) = b^{
-1}(x,y)$, confirming the relation. This action is free and properly discontinuo
us with quotient $\mathbb{R}^2 / \pi_1(K) \cong K$, so $\mathbb{R}^2$ is the uni
versal cover and $\pi_1(K)$ is as computed. $\square$
