## Solution

**Find all real-coefficient polynomials $P$ such that for all real $a,b,c$ with
$ab+bc+ca=0$:**
$$P(a-b)+P(b-c)+P(c-a)=2P(a+b+c).$$

---

### Step 1. $P$ is even and $P(0)=0$.

Set $a=b=c=0$ (satisfies $ab+bc+ca=0$): $3P(0)=2P(0)$, so $P(0)=0$.

Set $a=t,\ b=0,\ c=0$ (satisfies $0=0$): $P(t)+P(0)+P(-t)=2P(t)$, giving $P(-t)=
P(t)$ for all $t$. Hence **$P$ is even**, so $P(x)=Q(x^2)$ for some polynomial $
Q$, with $Q(0)=0$.

### Step 2. Reformulation.

Let $u=a-b,\ v=b-c,\ w=c-a$. Then $u+v+w=0$ (so $w=-(u+v)$), and
$$(a-b)^2+(b-c)^2+(c-a)^2=2(a^2+b^2+c^2)-2(ab+bc+ca)=2(a^2+b^2+c^2).$$
Also $(a+b+c)^2=a^2+b^2+c^2+2(ab+bc+ca)=a^2+b^2+c^2$. Therefore
$$u^2+v^2+w^2=2(a+b+c)^2,\quad\text{i.e.}\quad s^2:=u^2+uv+v^2.$$

Conversely, for **any** real $u,v$, setting $a=b+u,\ c=b-v$ and solving $ab+bc+c
a=0$ gives $3b^2+2(u-v)b-uv=0$ with discriminant $4(u^2+uv+v^2)\ge 0$ (since $u^
2+uv+v^2=(u+\tfrac v2)^2+\tfrac34 v^2\ge0$). So every $(u,v)\in\mathbb R^2$ is a
chievable, and $s=\pm\sqrt{u^2+uv+v^2}$. Since $P$ is even, $2P(s)=2P(\sqrt{u^2+
uv+v^2})$ regardless of sign.

Substituting $P(x)=Q(x^2)$, the identity becomes: **for all $u,v\in\mathbb R$**,
$$Q(u^2)+Q(v^2)+Q((u+v)^2)=2Q(u^2+uv+v^2).\tag{$\star$}$$

### Step 3. $\deg Q\le 2$.

Let $Q(x)=c_n x^n+\cdots$ with $c_n\ne 0$, $n\ge 1$. The degree-$2n$ homogeneous
 part of $(\star)$ gives
$$u^{2n}+v^{2n}+(u+v)^{2n}=2(u^2+uv+v^2)^n.\tag{$\star_n$}$$

Compare the coefficient of $u^{2n-2}v^2$ (valid for $n\ge 2$):

- **LHS:** only $(u+v)^{2n}$ contributes (since $u^{2n}$ has no $v$, and $v^{2n}
$ has no $u$ when $n\ge2$): $\binom{2n}{2}=n(2n-1)$.
- **RHS:** in $(u^2+uv+v^2)^n$, the monomial $u^{2n-2}v^2$ arises from $(a,b,c)=
(n-2,2,0)$ giving $\frac{n!}{(n-2)!\,2!}=\frac{n(n-1)}2$, and from $(n-1,0,1)$ g
iving $n$. Total: $\frac{n(n-1)}2+n=\frac{n(n+1)}2$. Multiplied by $2$: $n(n+1)$
.

Equating: $n(2n-1)=n(n+1)\Rightarrow 2n-1=n+1\Rightarrow n=2$.

So for $n\ge 3$ the leading homogeneous parts disagree — contradiction. For $n=1
$ and $n=2$, $(\star_n)$ is verified directly:
- $n=1$: $u^2+v^2+(u+v)^2=2u^2+2uv+2v^2=2(u^2+uv+v^2)$. ✓
- $n=2$: $u^4+v^4+(u+v)^4=2u^4+2v^4+4u^3v+6u^2v^2+4uv^3=2(u^2+uv+v^2)^2$. ✓

Hence $\deg Q\le 2$. With $Q(0)=0$, we get $Q(x)=\beta x+\alpha x^2$, i.e.
$$P(x)=\beta x^2+\alpha x^4,\qquad \alpha,\beta\in\mathbb R.$$

### Step 4. Verification that both work.

The equation is linear in $P$, so it suffices to check $P(x)=x^2$ and $P(x)=x^4$
.

**$P(x)=x^2$:** LHS $=(a-b)^2+(b-c)^2+(c-a)^2=2(a^2+b^2+c^2)=2(a+b+c)^2=2P(a+b+c
)$, using $ab+bc+ca=0$. ✓

**$P(x)=x^4$:** Using $P$ even and the reduction $(\star)$ with $Q(x)=x^2$, whic
h is exactly the $n=2$ case verified above. ✓

Equivalently, directly: $P(a-b)+P(b-c)+P(c-a)=2Q(u^2+uv+v^2)=2(a+b+c)^4=2P(a+b+c
)$. ✓

### Conclusion.

The real-coefficient polynomials satisfying the given condition are precisely
$$\boxed{P(x)=\alpha x^4+\beta x^2,\qquad \alpha,\beta\in\mathbb R.}$$
