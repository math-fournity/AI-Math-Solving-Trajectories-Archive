Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether geodesic paths in the Cayle
y graph of a Coxeter system $(W,S)$ correspond to geodesic paths in the Davis co
mplex $\Sigma$.

**Answer: No.** The embedding does not preserve geodesics. Here is a proof by ex
plicit counterexample.

---

## Proof

### Counterexample: Coxeter system of type $A_2$

Consider $(W, S)$ of type $A_2$, where $W = S_3 = \langle s_1, s_2 \mid s_1^2 =
s_2^2 = (s_1 s_2)^3 = 1\rangle$ and $S = \{s_1, s_2\}$.

**The Cayley graph.** The Cayley graph $\Gamma(W, S)$ is a 6-cycle (hexagon) wit
h vertices $1, s_1, s_1s_2, s_1s_2s_1, s_2s_1, s_2$ in cyclic order. The graph m
etric is the word metric $d_W$.

**The Davis complex.** Since $W$ is finite, every subset of $S$ is spherical. Th
e Coxeter cells are:
- $D_\emptyset$: a point (vertices of $\Sigma$),
- $D_{\{s_i\}}$: an interval (edges of $\Sigma$),
- $D_{\{s_1,s_2\}}$: a regular hexagon — the convex hull of the orbit of a gener
ic point under the dihedral group $W \cong I_2(3)$ of order 6 acting on $\mathbb
{R}^2$.

The cells of $\Sigma$ are indexed by cosets $wW_T$ with $W_T$ finite:
- **6 vertices**: cosets $wW_\emptyset = \{w\}$ for $w \in W$.
- **6 edges**: 3 cosets of $W_{\{s_1\}} = \{1, s_1\}$ and 3 cosets of $W_{\{s_2\
}} = \{1, s_2\}$.
- **1 two-cell**: the single coset $W_{\{s_1,s_2\}} = W$, giving one hexagonal 2
-cell $D_{\{s_1,s_2\}}$.

Euler characteristic: $6 - 6 + 1 = 1$, consistent with a disk. The 1-skeleton is
 exactly the Cayley graph (a 6-cycle), and the single 2-cell is a regular hexago
n filling it in. Thus **$\Sigma$ is a hexagonal disk** with the Cayley graph as
its boundary.

**Metric comparison.** Equip $\Sigma$ with its standard piecewise Euclidean metr
ic. Normalize the Coxeter cell so that edge length is 1. The regular hexagon the
n has circumradius $R = 1$ and diameter $2R = 2$.

Consider the identity vertex $e$ and the longest element $w_0 = s_1 s_2 s_1 = s_
2 s_1 s_2$, which are **opposite vertices** of the hexagon.

| Metric | Distance $e$ to $w_0$ |
|---|---|
| Graph distance $d_W(e, w_0)$ | $\ell_S(w_0) = 3$ (traverse 3 edges around the
boundary) |
| CAT(0) distance $d_\Sigma(e, w_0)$ | $2$ (straight line through the interior o
f the 2-cell) |

A geodesic path in the Cayley graph from $e$ to $w_0$ has length 3 (e.g., $e \to
 s_1 \to s_1s_2 \to s_1s_2s_1$). The geodesic in $\Sigma$ is the straight-line s
egment of length 2 passing through the interior of the hexagonal 2-cell — **not*
* contained in the 1-skeleton at all.

Since $d_\Sigma(e, w_0) = 2 < 3 = d_W(e, w_0)$, the graph geodesic is **not** a
geodesic in $\Sigma$.

### Infinite counterexample: type $\tilde{A}_2$

For a proper CAT(0) (infinite) example, consider the affine Coxeter system of ty
pe $\tilde{A}_2$ with $S = \{s_0, s_1, s_2\}$ and $m(s_i, s_j) = 3$ for all $i \
neq j$. The spherical subsets are all subsets of size $\leq 2$ (since $W_S = W$
is infinite). The maximal Coxeter cells are regular hexagons $D_{\{s_i, s_j\}}$,
 and the Davis complex is the Euclidean plane tiled by regular hexagons (the dua
l of the triangular tiling). Its 1-skeleton is the hexagonal lattice — the Cayle
y graph of $W$.

Two opposite vertices of any hexagonal tile have graph distance 3 but Euclidean
(CAT(0)) distance 2. The graph geodesic (3 edges around the boundary) is not a $
\Sigma$-geodesic (the straight chord of length 2 through the tile interior).

### Why this happens in general

The CAT(0) metric on $\Sigma$ allows paths to cut through the interiors of highe
r-dimensional Coxeter cells. Whenever two vertices of $\Sigma$ lie on a common c
ell of dimension $\geq 2$, the straight-line distance through that cell can be s
trictly shorter than any path constrained to the 1-skeleton. The word metric $d_
W$ is the induced path metric on the 1-skeleton, which is always an **upper boun
d** on $d_\Sigma$ restricted to vertices, and the inequality is strict precisely
 when a shortcut through a cell exists.

### Conclusion

The canonical embedding of the Cayley graph into the Davis complex does **not**
preserve geodesics. The CAT(0) metric on $\Sigma$ permits shortcuts through high
er-dimensional cells, making the distance between vertices strictly shorter than
 the word metric whenever the vertices are opposite (or sufficiently separated)
on a cell of dimension $\geq 2$.
