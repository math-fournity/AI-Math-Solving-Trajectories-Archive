## Proof

We evaluate
$$I = \int_\gamma \frac{e^{-z^2}}{z^2}\,dz,$$
where $\gamma$ is the ellipse $z(t) = a\cos t + i\,b\sin t$, $0\le t\le 2\pi$, w
ith $a,b>0$.

**Step 1: Locate the singularity.** The integrand $f(z) = e^{-z^2}/z^2$ has its
only singularity at $z=0$. Since $a,b>0$, the ellipse is a non-degenerate closed
 curve centered at the origin, so $z=0$ lies in the interior of $\gamma$. The cu
rve is traversed once counterclockwise (as $t$ goes from $0$ to $2\pi$, $z$ trac
es the ellipse counterclockwise).

**Step 2: Classify the singularity.** Since $e^{-z^2}$ is entire, $f(z) = e^{-z^
2}/z^2$ has a pole of order $2$ at $z=0$.

**Step 3: Compute the residue via Laurent expansion.** Expanding the entire func
tion $e^{-z^2}$ about $0$:
$$e^{-z^2} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n z^{2n}}{n!} = 1 - z^2 + \frac{z^4}
{2!} - \frac{z^6}{3!} + \cdots$$

Dividing by $z^2$:
$$\frac{e^{-z^2}}{z^2} = \frac{1}{z^2} - 1 + \frac{z^2}{2!} - \frac{z^4}{3!} + \
cdots$$

This Laurent series contains only even powers of $z$ (i.e., $z^{-2}, z^0, z^2, z
^4, \dots$). In particular, **the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ is $0$**, so
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{e^{-z^2}}{z^2} = 0.$$

**Step 4: Apply the residue theorem.** The only singularity enclosed by $\gamma$
 is at $z=0$, so
$$I = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{e^{-z^2}}{z^2} = 2\pi i \cdot 0
 = 0.$$

**Conclusion.** The value of the contour integral is

$$\boxed{0}.$$
