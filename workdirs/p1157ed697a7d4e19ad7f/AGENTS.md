# Problem

Deep in a subterranean laboratory, a team of astrophysicists is studying a solid, three-dimensional gravitational anomaly shaped like a perfect ellipsoid, denoted as $E$. To analyze the anomaly, they use a high-powered scanner that projects the shadow of $E$ onto a flat sensor screen $p$. As the team rotates the scanner to different orientations, the area of the resulting 2D shadow, $E(p)$, changes.

After extensive measurements, the team records the following data:
1. The smallest possible shadow area ever recorded is $9\pi$ square units.
2. The largest possible shadow area ever recorded is $25\pi$ square units.
3. At a specific orientation, the scanner captures a shadow that is a perfect circle with an area of $16\pi$ square units.

Let $V$ be the total volume of the 3D anomaly $E$. Calculate the value of $V / \pi$.
