## Proof

**Recall** the Dirichlet integral: $\displaystyle\int_0^{\infty}\frac{\sin t}{t}
\,dt = \frac{\pi}{2}$.

---

**Step 1 — Rewrite the numerator as a tail integral.**

$$\frac{\pi}{6}-\frac{1}{3}\int_0^{1/x^{3}}\frac{\sin t}{t}\,dt \;=\; \frac{1}{3
}\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\int_0^{1/x^{3}}\frac{\sin t}{t}\,dt\right) \;=\; \frac{1
}{3}\int_{1/x^{3}}^{\infty}\frac{\sin t}{t}\,dt.$$

So the limit is

$$L \;=\; \frac{1}{3}\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{1/x^{3}}^{\infty}
\frac{\sin t}{t}\,dt}{x}.$$

**Step 2 — Substitute $u=1/x^{3}$.**

As $x\to 0^{+}$ we have $u\to\infty$ and $x=u^{-1/3}$, giving

$$L \;=\; \frac{1}{3}\lim_{u\to\infty}\,u^{1/3}\!\int_{u}^{\infty}\frac{\sin t}{
t}\,dt.$$

**Step 3 — Estimate the tail.** Integrate by parts (with $f=1/t$, $g'=\sin t$):

$$\int_{u}^{\infty}\frac{\sin t}{t}\,dt \;=\; \left[-\frac{\cos t}{t}\right]_{u}
^{\infty}-\int_{u}^{\infty}\frac{\cos t}{t^{2}}\,dt \;=\; \frac{\cos u}{u}-\int_
{u}^{\infty}\frac{\cos t}{t^{2}}\,dt.$$

The boundary term at $\infty$ vanishes since $|\cos t/t|\le 1/t\to 0$. Bounding
the remainder:

$$\left|\int_{u}^{\infty}\frac{\cos t}{t^{2}}\,dt\right|\;\le\;\int_{u}^{\infty}
\frac{dt}{t^{2}}\;=\;\frac{1}{u}.$$

Hence

$$\left|\int_{u}^{\infty}\frac{\sin t}{t}\,dt\right|\;\le\;\frac{|\cos u|}{u}+\f
rac{1}{u}\;\le\;\frac{2}{u}.$$

**Step 4 — Conclude.**

$$\left|u^{1/3}\!\int_{u}^{\infty}\frac{\sin t}{t}\,dt\right|\;\le\;\frac{2}{u^{
2/3}}\;\xrightarrow[u\to\infty]{}\;0.$$

Therefore

$$L \;=\; \frac{1}{3}\cdot 0 \;=\; 0. \qquad\blacksquare$$
