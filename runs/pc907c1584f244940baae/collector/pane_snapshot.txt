## Proof

Let the 21 vertices $A_1, \ldots, A_{21}$ lie equally spaced on the circle. A tr
iangle $A_iA_jA_k$ contains the center $O$ in its interior if and only if the th
ree vertices are **not** all contained in any open semicircle (arc of length $<
180°$).

**Total triangles:**
$$\binom{21}{3} = \frac{21\cdot 20\cdot 19}{6} = 1330.$$

**Counting triangles NOT containing $O$:**

A triangle fails to contain $O$ iff its three vertices all lie within some open
semicircle. Since $n=21$ is odd, for any such "bad" triangle there is a **unique
** vertex (call it the leading vertex) such that the other two vertices lie stri
ctly within the arc of the next $\frac{n-1}{2}=10$ vertices clockwise from it (a
n arc subtending $10\cdot\frac{360°}{21} < 180°$).

Conversely, fixing any vertex as the leading vertex, the open semicircle startin
g at it contains exactly the next $10$ vertices, and choosing any $2$ of these $
10$ yields a bad triangle with that fixed leading vertex. This gives
$$\binom{10}{2} = 45$$
bad triangles per leading vertex.

Summing over all $21$ choices of the leading vertex, and noting each bad triangl
e is counted exactly once (the unique leading vertex is well-defined because $n$
 is odd, so no vertex lies diametrically opposite another), the number of bad tr
iangles is
$$21 \cdot \binom{10}{2} = 21 \cdot 45 = 945.$$

**Triangles containing $O$:**
$$1330 - 945 = 385.$$
