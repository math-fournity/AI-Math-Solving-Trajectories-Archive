Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a subprojection $e$ of a su
m of mutually orthogonal Abelian projections can itself be decomposed into mutua
lly orthogonal Abelian projections.

**Answer: Yes.** Moreover, $m \leq n$.

## Proof

Let $M$ be a von Neumann algebra, $f_1,\dots,f_n$ mutually orthogonal Abelian pr
ojections, $F=\sum f_i$, and $e\leq F$.

### Key Facts about Abelian Projections

**Fact 1.** If $f$ is Abelian, then $fMf = fZf$ (where $Z=Z(M)$ is the center).
Consequently, every subprojection $p\leq f$ is of the form $p=fz$ for some centr
al projection $z$, hence is itself Abelian.

**Fact 2.** Abelian projections with the same central support are Murray–von Neu
mann equivalent. *Proof:* If $f,g$ are Abelian with $z(f)=z(g)=z$, by the compar
ison theorem there exists central $w\leq z$ with $wf\precsim wg$ and $(z{-}w)g\p
recsim(z{-}w)f$. Since $f$ is Abelian, any subprojection of $wf$ with central su
pport $w$ must equal $wf$ (by Fact 1), forcing $wf\sim wg$. Likewise $(z{-}w)f\s
im(z{-}w)g$. Hence $f\sim g$. $\square$

### Step 1: Reduction to Common Central Support

Let $z_i=z(f_i)$. Central projections are pairwise equal or orthogonal, so we gr
oup indices: let $w_1,\dots,w_k$ be the distinct central supports and $S_\alpha=
\{i:z_i=w_\alpha\}$. Set $e^{(\alpha)}=w_\alpha\, e$. Then $e^{(\alpha)}\leq\sum
_{i\in S_\alpha}f_i$ and $e=\sum_\alpha e^{(\alpha)}$, with the $e^{(\alpha)}$ m
utually orthogonal (different central summands). If each $e^{(\alpha)}$ decompos
es into mutually orthogonal Abelian projections, the union over $\alpha$ gives t
he full decomposition. **We henceforth assume all $f_i$ share central support $z
$.**

### Step 2: The Algebra $FMF\cong M_n(zZ)$

By Fact 2, all $f_i$ are equivalent. Choose partial isometries $v_{i1}$ with $v_
{i1}^*v_{i1}=f_1$, $v_{i1}v_{i1}^*=f_i$, and set $v_{ij}=v_{i1}v_{j1}^*$. These
satisfy matrix-unit relations: $v_{ij}v_{jk}=v_{ik}$, $v_{ij}^*=v_{ji}$, $v_{ii}
=f_i$.

Since $f_i$ is Abelian, $f_iMf_i=f_iZf_i\cong zZ$. For $i\neq j$, any $x\in f_iM
f_j$ satisfies $v_{ij}^*x\in f_jMf_j=f_jZf_j$, so $x=v_{ij}(v_{ij}^*x)\in v_{ij}
(f_jZf_j)$; conversely $v_{ij}(f_jZf_j)\subseteq f_iMf_j$. Thus $f_iMf_j=v_{ij}(
zZ)$.

Therefore $FMF=\sum_{i,j}f_iMf_j\cong M_n(zZ)$, with $F\leftrightarrow I_n$ and
$e\leftrightarrow E$, a projection in $M_n(zZ)$ with $E\leq I_n$.

### Step 3: Decomposition in $M_n(A)$, $A$ Abelian

Since $zZ$ is abelian, $zZ\cong L^\infty(X,\mu)$, so $M_n(zZ)\cong L^\infty(X;M_
n(\mathbb{C}))$. We decompose $E$ into mutually orthogonal rank-$\leq 1$ project
ions constructively.

Set $E^{(1)}=E$. For $j=1,\dots,n$:
- Let $a_j=(E^{(j)})_{jj}\in zZ$ (a positive contraction).
- Let $s_j=\chi_{\{a_j>0\}}\in zZ$ (support projection of $a_j$).
- Set $P_j = E^{(j)}\,e_j\,e_j^*\,E^{(j)}\cdot s_j/a_j \in M_n(zZ)$, where $e_j$
 is the $j$-th standard basis vector and $s_j/a_j$ is the inverse on the support
.
- Set $E^{(j+1)}=E^{(j)}-P_j$.

**Verification:**
- $P_j$ is a projection: on $\{a_j>0\}$, $P_j=u_ju_j^*$ where $u_j=E^{(j)}e_j/\s
qrt{a_j}$ is a unit vector in $\operatorname{ran}(E^{(j)})$, so $P_j\leq E^{(j)}
$ and has rank 1. On $\{a_j=0\}$, $P_j=0$.
- $E^{(j+1)}=E^{(j)}-P_j$ is a projection since $P_j\leq E^{(j)}$.
- $(E^{(j+1)})_{jj}=a_j-a_j=0$ on $\{a_j>0\}$ and $=0$ on $\{a_j=0\}$. Since $E^
{(j+1)}$ is a projection, $(E^{(j+1)})_{jj}=\sum_k|(E^{(j+1)})_{jk}|^2=0$ forces
 the entire $j$-th row and column to vanish.
- The $P_j$ are mutually orthogonal: $P_j\leq E^{(j)}=E-\sum_{\ell<j}P_\ell$, so
 $P_j\perp P_\ell$ for $\ell<j$.
- After $n$ steps, all rows/columns of $E^{(n+1)}$ vanish, so $E^{(n+1)}=0$ and
$\sum_{j=1}^n P_j=E$.

Each $P_j$ has rank $\leq 1$ pointwise, so $P_j\,M_n(zZ)\,P_j\cong zZ$ is abelia
n.

### Step 4: Return to $M$

Under the isomorphism $FMF\cong M_n(zZ)$, each $P_j$ maps to a projection $e_j\i
n M$ with $e_j\leq F$. Since $e_j\leq F$, we have $e_jMe_j=e_j(FMF)e_j\cong P_j\
,M_n(zZ)\,P_j\cong zZ$, which is abelian. So each $e_j$ is Abelian. The $e_j$ ar
e mutually orthogonal and $\sum e_j=e$.

### Combining Steps 1 and 4

Applying the above to each central summand $e^{(\alpha)}$ yields at most $|S_\al
pha|$ Abelian projections. Taking the union over all $\alpha$ gives mutually ort
hogonal Abelian projections $e_1,\dots,e_m$ with $e=\sum e_j$ and $m\leq\sum_\al
pha|S_\alpha|=n$. $\blacksquare$
