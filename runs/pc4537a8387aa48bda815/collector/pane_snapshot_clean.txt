## Interpretation

The notation $|A-B|$ here denotes the **entrywise absolute value**: the matrix w
hose $(i,j)$-entry is $|A_{ij}-B_{ij}|$. (If $|A-B|$ meant the matrix modulus $(
M^*M)^{1/2}$, it would be positive semi-definite for *any* matrix $M$ by definit
ion, making the hypotheses on $A,B$ irrelevant and the problem trivial.)

## Answer: No, $|A-B|$ need not be positive semi-definite.

## Counterexample

Take the following $2\times 2$ matrices:

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} \tfrac{1}{2} & \tfrac{1}{2} \\[4pt] \tfrac{1}{2} & \tfrac{
1}{2} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B = \begin{pmatrix} \tfrac{1}{2} & -\tfrac{1}{2} \\[
4pt] -\tfrac{1}{2} & \tfrac{1}{2} \end{pmatrix}.$$

**Verification that $A$ and $B$ satisfy the hypotheses.**

- **Hermitian:** Both are real symmetric, hence Hermitian. ✓
- **Positive semi-definite:**
  - $A = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\end{pmatr
ix}$ is rank 1 with eigenvalues $1$ and $0$, so PSD. ✓
  - $B = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\end{pma
trix}$ is rank 1 with eigenvalues $1$ and $0$, so PSD. ✓
- **Unit trace:** $\operatorname{tr}(A) = \frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}=1$ and $\opera
torname{tr}(B)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}=1$. ✓

**Computing $A-B$ and its entrywise absolute value.**

$$A - B = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Taking entrywise absolute values (every entry is already non-negative except non
e change):

$$|A-B| = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

**Checking positive semi-definiteness.**

The characteristic polynomial of $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$
is $\lambda^2 - 1 = 0$, giving eigenvalues $\lambda = 1$ and $\lambda = -1$.

Since $|A-B|$ has a **negative eigenvalue** $-1$, it is **not** positive semi-de
finite.

## Conclusion

There exist Hermitian positive semi-definite unit trace matrices $A$ and $B$ (as
 exhibited above) for which the entrywise absolute value $|A-B|$ fails to be pos
itive semi-definite. Therefore the answer to the question is **No**.
