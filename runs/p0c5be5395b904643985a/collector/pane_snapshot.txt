## Solution

**Setup.** Place $A$ at the origin with the angle bisector $AD$ along the positi
ve $x$-axis. Since $\angle A = 60°$, the sides $AB$ and $AC$ make $30°$ on eithe
r side of the bisector:

$$B = (12\cos 30°, 12\sin 30°) = (6\sqrt 3, 6), \qquad C = (14\cos 30°, -14\sin
30°) = (7\sqrt 3, -7).$$

**Point D (angle bisector foot).** By the angle-bisector theorem, $BD/DC = AB/AC
 = 6/7$, so
$$D = B + \tfrac{6}{13}(C-B) = \bigl(6\sqrt 3 + \tfrac{6\sqrt 3}{13},\; 6-6\bigr
) = \Bigl(\tfrac{84\sqrt 3}{13},\,0\Bigr),$$
confirming $D$ lies on the $x$-axis.

**Circumcircle of $ABC$ and point M.** The circumcircle through $A=(0,0)$ has eq
uation $x^2+y^2+ux+vy=0$. Substituting $B$ and $C$:
$$24+\sqrt3\,u+v=0,\qquad 28+\sqrt3\,u-v=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; u=-\tfrac{26\sqrt
3}{3},\; v=2.$$
Intersecting with the $x$-axis ($y=0$): $x\bigl(x-\tfrac{26\sqrt3}{3}\bigr)=0$,
giving $A$ and
$$M = \Bigl(\tfrac{26\sqrt3}{3},\,0\Bigr).$$

**Circle $(B,D,M)$ and point K.** Since $D,M$ lie on the $x$-axis, the circle $x
^2+y^2+px+qy+r=0$ through them has $p=-(x_D+x_M)=-\tfrac{590\sqrt3}{39}$ and $r=
x_D x_M = 168$. Substituting $B$ gives $q=-\tfrac{86}{13}$. Parametrize $AB$ as
$(6\sqrt3\,t,\,6t)$. Substituting into the circle:
$$144t^2 - \tfrac{4056}{13}t + 168 = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; 78t^2-169t+91=0 \;\Lo
ngrightarrow\; t=\tfrac{7}{6}\;\text{or}\;1.$$
$t=1$ is $B$, so $K$ corresponds to $t=\tfrac76$:
$$K = (7\sqrt3,\,7).$$

**Circle $(C,D,M)$.** Same $p$ and $r$ (depends only on $D,M$); substituting $C=
(7\sqrt3,-7)$ gives $q=+\tfrac{86}{13}$:
$$x^2+y^2-\tfrac{590\sqrt3}{39}x+\tfrac{86}{13}y+168=0.$$

**Line $KM$ and point L.** Direction $\vec{MK}=K-M=\bigl(7\sqrt3-\tfrac{26\sqrt3
}{3},\,7\bigr)=\bigl(-\tfrac{5\sqrt3}{3},\,7\bigr)$. Parametrize from $M$:
$$P(\lambda)=M+\lambda\,\vec{MK}=\Bigl(\tfrac{\sqrt3(26-5\lambda)}{3},\;-7\lambd
a\Bigr).$$
(Here $\lambda=0$ gives $M$; $\lambda=1$ gives $K$.) Substituting into circle $(
C,D,M)$ and multiplying by $39$:
$$13(26-5\lambda)^2+1911\lambda^2-590(26-5\lambda)+1806\lambda+6552=0.$$
Expanding:
$$2236\lambda^2-1376\lambda=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \lambda\bigl(2236\lambda-1376\
bigr)=0.$$
So $\lambda=0$ (point $M$) or $\lambda=\tfrac{1376}{2236}=\tfrac{8}{13}$ (point
$L$).

**Ratio.** Since $L=P\bigl(\tfrac{8}{13}\bigr)$ lies on the ray from $M$ toward
$K$,
$$ML = \tfrac{8}{13}\,MK \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{KM}{ML}=\frac{1}{8/13}=\boxed
{\dfrac{13}{8}}.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
