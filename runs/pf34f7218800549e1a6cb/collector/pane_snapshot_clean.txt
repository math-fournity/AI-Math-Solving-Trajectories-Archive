## Solution

**Problem.** Find all polynomials $P \in \mathbb{R}[x,y]$ satisfying $P(x,y)\,P(
z,t)=P(xz-yt,\;xt+yz)$.

---

### Step 1. Preliminary observations

Set $(z,t)=(1,0)$:
$$P(x,y)\,P(1,0)=P(x,y)\quad\text{for all }(x,y).$$
So either $P\equiv 0$ or $P(1,0)=1$. The zero polynomial is clearly a solution;
henceforth assume $P\not\equiv 0$, so $P(1,0)=1$.

Set $(z,t)=(0,0)$:
$$P(x,y)\,P(0,0)=P(0,0)\quad\text{for all }(x,y).$$
If $P(0,0)\neq 0$, then $P\equiv 1$, which is indeed a solution. If $P(0,0)=0$,
then $P$ is nonconstant (since $P(1,0)=1\neq 0$).

### Step 2. Change of variables via complex structure

The map $(x,y)\cdot(z,t)=(xz-yt,\,xt+yz)$ is exactly complex multiplication unde
r $\mathbb{R}^2\cong\mathbb{C}$, $x+iy\leftrightarrow(x,y)$. Introduce the inver
tible $\mathbb{C}$-linear change of variables
$$u=x+iy,\qquad v=x-iy\qquad\bigl(x=\tfrac{u+v}{2},\; y=\tfrac{u-v}{2i}\bigr),$$
and similarly $u_1=x_1+iy_1,\;v_1=x_1-iy_1,\;u_2=x_2+iy_2,\;v_2=x_2-iy_2$. Defin
e
$$Q(u,v):=P\!\left(\tfrac{u+v}{2},\,\tfrac{u-v}{2i}\right)\in\mathbb{C}[u,v].$$
Then $P(x_1,y_1)=Q(u_1,v_1)$, $P(x_2,y_2)=Q(u_2,v_2)$, and
$$x_1x_2-y_1y_2=\tfrac{u_1u_2+v_1v_2}{2},\qquad x_1y_2+y_1x_2=\tfrac{u_1u_2-v_1v
_2}{2i},$$
so $P(x_1x_2-y_1y_2,\,x_1y_2+y_1x_2)=Q(u_1u_2,\,v_1v_2)$. The given identity, be
ing a polynomial identity in $\mathbb{R}[x_1,y_1,x_2,y_2]$, becomes the identity
 in $\mathbb{C}[u_1,v_1,u_2,v_2]$:
$$\boxed{Q(u_1,v_1)\,Q(u_2,v_2)=Q(u_1u_2,\,v_1v_2).}\tag{$\ast$}$$

### Step 3. Solving the functional equation $(\ast)$

Write $Q(u,v)=\sum_{i,j\ge 0}a_{ij}\,u^i v^j$. Expanding both sides of $(\ast)$:

- **Left:** $\displaystyle Q(u_1,v_1)Q(u_2,v_2)=\sum_{i,j,k,l}a_{ij}\,a_{kl}\,u_
1^i v_1^j\,u_2^k v_2^l$.
  The coefficient of $u_1^a v_1^b\,u_2^c v_2^d$ is $a_{ab}\,a_{cd}$.

- **Right:** $\displaystyle Q(u_1u_2,v_1v_2)=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\,u_1^i v_1^j\,u_2^
i v_2^j$.
  The monomial $u_1^a v_1^b\,u_2^c v_2^d$ occurs only when $a=c$ and $b=d$, with
 coefficient $a_{ab}$.

Equating coefficients for all $(a,b,c,d)$:

1. **If $a=c$ and $b=d$:** $\;a_{ab}=a_{ab}^2$, hence $a_{ab}\in\{0,1\}$.
2. **If $(a,b)\neq(c,d)$:** $\;0=a_{ab}\,a_{cd}$.

Condition 2 implies **at most one** coefficient $a_{ij}$ is nonzero: if two dist
inct pairs $(a,b)\neq(c,d)$ both had $a_{ab}\neq 0$ and $a_{cd}\neq 0$, then $a_
{ab}a_{cd}\neq 0$, contradicting condition 2.

Therefore either $Q\equiv 0$ (giving $P\equiv 0$) or $Q(u,v)=u^m v^n$ for some $
m,n\ge 0$ (the unique nonzero coefficient equals $1$ by condition 1).

### Step 4. Enforcing real coefficients

We have $P(x,y)=(x+iy)^m(x-iy)^n$. Since $P$ has **real** coefficients, $P=\over
line{P}$ (coefficient-wise). Conjugating:
$$\overline{(x+iy)^m(x-iy)^n}=(x-iy)^m(x+iy)^n=(x+iy)^n(x-iy)^m.$$
Thus $P=\overline P$ forces $(x+iy)^m(x-iy)^n=(x+iy)^n(x-iy)^m$. Since $x+iy$ an
d $x-iy$ are non-associate irreducible elements of $\mathbb{C}[x,y]$, unique fac
torization gives $m=n$.

Hence
$$P(x,y)=(x+iy)^n(x-iy)^n=\bigl((x+iy)(x-iy)\bigr)^n=(x^2+y^2)^n.$$

### Step 5. Verification

For $P(x,y)=(x^2+y^2)^n$ with $n\ge 0$:
$$P(x,y)P(z,t)=(x^2+y^2)^n(z^2+t^2)^n=\bigl((x^2+y^2)(z^2+t^2)\bigr)^n.$$
By the Brahmagupta–Fibonacci identity,
$$(x^2+y^2)(z^2+t^2)=(xz-yt)^2+(xt+yz)^2,$$
so the above equals $\bigl((xz-yt)^2+(xt+yz)^2\bigr)^n=P(xz-yt,\,xt+yz)$. ✓

The zero polynomial also satisfies the identity trivially. ✓

### Conclusion

The polynomials $P\in\mathbb{R}[x,y]$ satisfying $P(x,y)P(z,t)=P(xz-yt,\,xt+yz)$
 are exactly

$$\boxed{P(x,y)\equiv 0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad P(x,y)=(x^2+y^2)^n\ \text{for }n=
0,1,2,\dots}$$
