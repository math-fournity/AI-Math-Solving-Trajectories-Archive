**Answer: Yes, such a $k$ must exist.**

**Proof.**

**Step 1: $V$ is necessarily finite-dimensional.**

Each $f_i \in S^d(V^*)$ is a finite sum of $d$-fold products of linear forms in
$V^*$. Let $U \subseteq V^*$ be the finite-dimensional subspace spanned by all l
inear forms appearing in $f_1, \dots, f_n$. Consider the natural map
$$\phi: V \to U^*, \quad v \mapsto (\ell \mapsto \ell(v)).$$
Each $f_i$ factors through $\phi$ (viewing $f_i \in S^d(U^*)$). If $v \in \ker(\
phi)$, then $\ell(v) = 0$ for all $\ell \in U$, so $f_i(v) = 0$ for every $i$. B
y the hypothesis $V(f_1, \dots, f_n) = \{0\}$, this forces $v = 0$. Hence $\ker(
\phi) = \{0\}$, so $V$ embeds into the finite-dimensional space $U^*$. Thus $V$
is finite-dimensional.

**Step 2: Apply the Nullstellensatz.**

Set $N = \dim V$ and choose coordinates $x_1, \dots, x_N$ for $V^*$, identifying
 $S(V^*) \cong \mathbb{C}[x_1, \dots, x_N]$. Let $I = (f_1, \dots, f_n)$ and $\m
athfrak{m} = (x_1, \dots, x_N)$ be the maximal ideal at the origin.

The hypothesis $V(f_1, \dots, f_n) = \{0\}$ says $V(I) = \{0\}$. By Hilbert's Nu
llstellensatz, $\sqrt{I} = I(V(I)) = I(\{0\}) = \mathfrak{m}$.

**Step 3: A power of $\mathfrak{m}$ lies in $I$.**

Since $\sqrt{I} = \mathfrak{m}$, for each generator $x_j$ there exists $r_j > 0$
 with $x_j^{r_j} \in I$. Set $R = r_1 + \cdots + r_N$. Any monomial $x_1^{a_1} \
cdots x_N^{a_N}$ of degree $\geq R$ satisfies $\sum a_j \geq \sum r_j$, so by th
e pigeonhole principle some $a_j \geq r_j$, meaning the monomial is divisible by
 $x_j^{r_j} \in I$. Hence every monomial of degree $\geq R$ lies in $I$, i.e., $
\mathfrak{m}^R \subseteq I$.

**Step 4: Conclude.**

For any $k \geq R$, every monomial of degree $k$ lies in $I$, so $S^k(V^*) \subs
eteq I_k$ (the degree-$k$ homogeneous part of $I$). Since each $f_i$ is homogene
ous of degree $d$, the degree-$k$ part of the ideal is:
$$I_k = \left\{\sum_{i=1}^n g_i f_i \,:\, g_i \in S^{k-d}(V^*)\right\}.$$
(This holds because decomposing any $h_i \in S(V^*)$ into homogeneous parts, onl
y the degree-$(k-d)$ part contributes to a degree-$k$ product $h_i f_i$.)

Now set $k = \max(R, d)$. Then $k \geq d$ and $k \geq R$, so $S^k(V^*) \subseteq
 I_k$. Therefore, for every $v \in S^k(V^*)$, there exist $g_1, \dots, g_n \in S
^{k-d}(V^*)$ such that $v = \sum_{i=1}^n g_i f_i$.

This shows such a positive integer $k \geq d$ must exist. $\blacksquare$
