## Solution

On the contour $L$, we have $|z| = 1$, hence $z\bar{z} = |z|^2 = 1$. Therefore

$$\cos(2\pi z\bar{z}) = \cos(2\pi \cdot 1) = \cos(2\pi) = 1,$$

and the integral reduces to

$$\int_L z\cos(2\pi z\bar{z})\,dz = \int_L z\,dz.$$

Since $f(z) = z$ is an entire function, it admits the primitive $F(z) = \tfrac{z
^2}{2}$ on all of $\mathbb{C}$. The contour $L$ is the upper semicircle $|z|=1$,
 $0 \le \arg z \le \pi$, traversed from $z = 1$ (at $\arg z = 0$) to $z = -1$ (a
t $\arg z = \pi$). By the fundamental theorem of contour integration,

$$\int_L z\,dz = F(-1) - F(1) = \frac{(-1)^2}{2} - \frac{1^2}{2} = \frac{1}{2} -
 \frac{1}{2} = 0.$$

**Verification by direct parametrization.** Let $z = e^{i\theta}$, $0 \le \theta
 \le \pi$, so $dz = ie^{i\theta}\,d\theta$. Then

$$\int_L z\,dz = \int_0^\pi e^{i\theta}\cdot ie^{i\theta}\,d\theta = i\int_0^\pi
 e^{2i\theta}\,d\theta = i\left[\frac{e^{2i\theta}}{2i}\right]_0^\pi = \frac{1}{
2}\bigl(e^{2\pi i} - e^{0}\bigr) = \frac{1}{2}(1 - 1) = 0.$$

Both methods agree.

$$\boxed{\int_L z\cos(2\pi z\bar{z})\,dz = 0}$$
