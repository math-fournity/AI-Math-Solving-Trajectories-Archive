# Solution

**Answer: No.** In general $v \notin W^{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^3)$, even for smooth, co
mpactly supported $f \in L^2(\mathbb{R}^3)$.

## Counterexample

Take $f \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^3)$ with $f \geq 0$ and $\int_{\mathbb{R}^3} f
(y)\,dy = 1$ (for instance, a standard non-negative mollifier). Then $f \in L^2(
\mathbb{R}^3)$ with compact support, so $f$ satisfies all the hypotheses.

Let $\operatorname{supp} f \subseteq B(0,R)$. For $|x| > 2R$ and $y \in B(0,R)$,
 the triangle inequality gives
$$
|x - y| \leq |x| + |y| \leq |x| + R \leq \tfrac{3}{2}|x|,
$$
so
$$
\frac{1}{|x-y|} \geq \frac{2}{3|x|}.
$$

Since $f \geq 0$ and $\int f = 1$, the potential satisfies, for every $|x| > 2R$
,
$$
v(x) = \int_{B(0,R)} \frac{f(y)}{|x-y|}\,dy \;\geq\; \frac{2}{3|x|}\int_{B(0,R)}
 f(y)\,dy \;=\; \frac{2}{3|x|}.
$$

Therefore
$$
|v(x)|^2 \geq \frac{4}{9|x|^2}, \qquad |x| > 2R.
$$

Integrating in spherical coordinates,
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}^3} |v(x)|^2\,dx \;\geq\; \int_{|x|>2R} \frac{4}{9|x|^2}\,dx \;=
\; \frac{4}{9}\int_{2R}^{\infty}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^2} \frac{1}{r^2}\,r^2\,d\omeg
a\,dr \;=\; \frac{16\pi}{9}\int_{2R}^{\infty} dr \;=\; +\infty.
$$

Hence $v \notin L^2(\mathbb{R}^3)$, and consequently $v \notin W^{2,2}(\mathbb{R
}^3)$.

## Remark on why this happens

The kernel $\Phi(x) = 1/|x|$ is the fundamental solution of $-\Delta$ in $\mathb
b{R}^3$ (up to the factor $4\pi$), so $v = \Phi * f$ solves $-\Delta v = 4\pi f$
 in the distributional sense. Calderón–Zygmund theory does give the second-deriv
ative estimate
$$
\|D^2 v\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^3)} \leq C\,\|\Delta v\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^3)} = 4\pi C\
,\|f\|_{L^2},
$$
but this controls only the *second derivatives*, not $v$ itself. The obstruction
 to $v \in W^{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^3)$ is the low-frequency / far-field behavior: unl
ess $\int f = 0$, the potential decays like $|x|^{-1}$ at infinity, and $|x|^{-1
} \notin L^2(\mathbb{R}^3)$ (since $\int^\infty r^{-2}\cdot r^2\,dr = \infty$).
Thus $v$ fails to be in $L^2$, and the claimed estimate $\|v\|_{2,2} \leq C\|f\|
_2$ is false in general.
