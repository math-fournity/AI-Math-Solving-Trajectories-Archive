## Proof

**Claim:** Every convex function $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ (with $n > 1$)
 is Borel-measurable.

The proof proceeds in two stages: (A) show $f$ is continuous, and (B) deduce Bor
el-measurability.

---

### Stage A: A convex function $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ is continuous.

We prove local Lipschitz continuity at an arbitrary point $x_0 \in \mathbb{R}^n$
.

**Step 1 (Local upper bound).** Let $S = \operatorname{conv}\{v_0, v_1, \dots, v
_n\}$ be a non-degenerate simplex whose interior contains a neighborhood of $x_0
$ (such a simplex exists since $n > 1$ gives us enough vertices to form a full-d
imensional simplex around any point). Every $x \in S$ admits a representation $x
 = \sum_{i=0}^{n} \lambda_i v_i$ with $\lambda_i \geq 0$ and $\sum_i \lambda_i =
 1$. By convexity,

$$f(x) = f\!\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n} \lambda_i v_i\right) \leq \sum_{i=0}^{n} \lambd
a_i f(v_i) \leq \max_{0 \leq i \leq n} f(v_i) =: M.$$

So $f \leq M$ on $S$, hence $f$ is bounded above on a neighborhood of $x_0$.

**Step 2 (Local lower bound).** Since $x_0$ lies in the interior of $S$, there e
xists $\delta > 0$ such that $B(x_0, \delta) \subset S$ and also $2x_0 - x \in S
$ whenever $x \in B(x_0, \delta)$. For such $x$, write $x_0 = \tfrac{1}{2}x + \t
frac{1}{2}(2x_0 - x)$. Convexity gives

$$f(x_0) \leq \tfrac{1}{2}f(x) + \tfrac{1}{2}f(2x_0 - x),$$

so

$$f(x) \geq 2f(x_0) - f(2x_0 - x) \geq 2f(x_0) - M.$$

Thus $f$ is bounded below on $B(x_0, \delta)$. Combined with Step 1, there exist
s $C > 0$ with $|f| \leq C$ on $B(x_0, \delta)$.

**Step 3 (Local Lipschitz $\Rightarrow$ continuity).** Pick $r > 0$ with $B(x_0,
 2r) \subset B(x_0, \delta)$, so $|f| \leq C$ on $B(x_0, 2r)$. Let $x, y \in B(x
_0, r)$, $x \neq y$. Set $z = y + \frac{r}{\lvert y - x\rvert}(y - x)$; then $z
\in B(x_0, 2r)$ and

$$y = \frac{\lvert y - x\rvert}{\lvert y - x\rvert + r}\, z + \frac{r}{\lvert y
- x\rvert + r}\, x.$$

By convexity,

$$f(y) \leq \frac{\lvert y - x\rvert}{\lvert y - x\rvert + r} f(z) + \frac{r}{\l
vert y - x\rvert + r} f(x),$$

hence

$$f(y) - f(x) \leq \frac{\lvert y - x\rvert}{\lvert y - x\rvert + r}\bigl(f(z) -
 f(x)\bigr) \leq \frac{\lvert y - x\rvert}{r}\cdot 2C.$$

Swapping the roles of $x$ and $y$ yields

$$\lvert f(y) - f(x)\rvert \leq \frac{2C}{r}\lvert y - x\rvert \qquad \forall\,
x, y \in B(x_0, r).$$

So $f$ is Lipschitz (in particular continuous) on $B(x_0, r)$. Since $x_0$ was a
rbitrary, $f$ is continuous on all of $\mathbb{R}^n$.

---

### Stage B: Continuity implies Borel-measurability.

For any open set $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, the preimage $f^{-1}(U)$ is open in $
\mathbb{R}^n$ by continuity. Every open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$ is a Borel set.
 Since the open sets generate the Borel $\sigma$-algebra on $\mathbb{R}$, and $f
^{-1}$ preserves set operations (unions, intersections, complements), $f$ is Bor
el-measurable.

---

### Conclusion

A convex function $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ is continuous (in fact locall
y Lipschitz), and every continuous function is Borel-measurable. Therefore **yes
**, $f$ is Borel-measurable.
