**Key facts.** For a discriminant $D$, proper equivalence uses $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})
$ (det $=+1$); ordinary equivalence uses $GL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ (det $=\pm 1$). Sinc
e $GL_2(\mathbb{Z}) = SL_2(\mathbb{Z}) \rtimes \langle\begin{psmallmatrix}1&0\\0
&-1\end{psmallmatrix}\rangle$, applying a determinant $-1$ matrix to a form $q$
yields (up to proper equivalence) the *opposite* form $q^{-1}$, whose proper cla
ss is the inverse of $[q]$ in the class group $H$. Hence the ordinary class cont
aining the proper class $[q]$ is exactly
$$f([q]) = \{[q],\, [q]^{-1}\},$$
a singleton when $[q]$ is ambiguous (order $\le 2$) and a 2-element set otherwis
e.

---

**Proof.** Take $D = -23$. We exhibit the obstruction in general, then verify th
is instance.

*General obstruction.* Suppose $H'$ admitted a group structure making $f:H\to H'
$ a homomorphism. Let $e$ be the identity of $H$. Then $f(e)=e_{H'}$, so
$$\ker f = \{[q]\in H : f([q]) = f(e)\} = \{[q] : [q]\sim_{\mathrm{ord}} e\} = \
{e,\, e^{-1}\} = \{e\},$$
since $e^{-1}=e$. Thus $f$ is injective. But $f([q])=f([q]^{-1})$ for every $[q]
\in H$; whenever some $[q]$ satisfies $[q]\ne [q]^{-1}$ (i.e. has order $>2$), t
he map $f$ is **not** injective — contradiction. Therefore:

> If $H$ contains an element of order $>2$, no group structure on $H'$ makes $f$
 a homomorphism.

*The instance $D=-23$.* Reduced forms of discriminant $-23$ satisfy $b^2-4ac=-23
$ with $|b|\le a\le c$:
- $a=1$: $b=\pm1$, $c=(1+23)/4=6$, giving $(1,\pm1,6)$. With $|b|=a$ we take $b\
ge0$, so both represent the single proper class of the principal form $(1,1,6)$.
- $a=2$: $b=\pm1$, $c=(1+23)/8=3$, giving $(2,1,3)$ and $(2,-1,3)$, with $a=2<c=
3$; both are reduced and **not** properly equivalent (the substitution $x\mapsto
 -x$ has determinant $-1$), so they are two distinct proper classes, inverse to
each other.
- $a=3$: $c=(b^2+23)/12$ is never an integer $\ge 3$; no forms.

Hence $h(-23)=3$, with classes $\{e, a, a^{-1}\}$ where $e=[(1,1,6)]$ and $a=[(2
,1,3)]\ne a^{-1}=[(2,-1,3)]$. The class group is cyclic of order $3$, so $a$ has
 order $3>2$.

By the general obstruction, $f$ cannot be injective here (indeed $f(a)=f(a^{-1})
$ with $a\ne a^{-1}$), yet any homomorphic group structure on $H'$ would force $
f$ to be injective. This contradiction shows $H'$ admits **no** group structure
for which $f$ is a homomorphism.
