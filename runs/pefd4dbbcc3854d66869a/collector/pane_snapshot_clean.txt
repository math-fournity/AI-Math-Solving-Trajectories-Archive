## Solution

**Setup.** Let ABCD be a trapezoid with AB ∥ CD. Denote the angles ∠DAB = α, ∠AB
C = β, ∠CDA = δ, ∠BCD = γ. Since AB ∥ CD, the co-interior angles satisfy α + δ =
 180° and β + γ = 180°.

**Key observation — E and F lie on the midline.** Point E is on the bisectors of
 ∠CDA and ∠DAB, so E is equidistant from lines CD, DA, and AB. In particular, E
is equidistant from the parallel lines AB and CD, placing E on the midline (the
line parallel to AB and CD at half the height). Similarly, F (on the bisectors o
f ∠ABC and ∠BCD) is equidistant from AB, BC, and CD, so F also lies on the midli
ne.

Let h be the distance between AB and CD, so the midline is at height h/2 from ea
ch. Let AB = u, CD = v, and EF = w (the segment on the midline).

**Computing the ratio u/v.** Since E, F are on the midline and A, B are on the t
op line, EABF is a trapezoid with parallel sides AB and EF and height h/2:

$$[\text{EABF}] = \tfrac{h}{4}(u + w) = 24 \implies h(u+w) = 96.$$

Similarly, EDCF is a trapezoid with parallel sides CD and EF and height h/2:

$$[\text{EDCF}] = \tfrac{h}{4}(v + w) = 36 \implies h(v+w) = 144.$$

The total area gives $h(u+v) = 120$. Adding the two equations: $h(u+v) + 2hw = 2
40$, so $hw = 60$. Subtracting: $h(v - u) = 48$. Therefore:

$$hu = 96 - 60 = 36, \quad hv = 144 - 60 = 84 \implies \frac{u}{v} = \frac{36}{8
4} = \frac{3}{7}.$$

**Identifying H and G via the apex P.** Since v > u (CD > AB), extending legs DA
 and BC upward, they meet at a point P above AB. Then A lies between P and D, an
d B lies between P and C.

Triangles PAB and PCD are **similar**: both have angle (180° − α) at A/D, angle
(180° − β) at B/C, and angle (α + β − 180°) at P. The similarity ratio is:

$$k = \frac{AB}{CD} = \frac{PA}{PD} = \frac{PB}{PC} = \frac{3}{7}.$$

**H is the excenter of △PAB opposite P.** The bisector of ∠DAB = α bisects the *
external* angle at A of △PAB (whose internal angle at A is 180° − α). The bisect
or of ∠ABC = β bisects the external angle at B. The intersection of two external
 bisectors is the **excenter opposite P**, with exradius $r_H$ = distance from H
 to AB.

**G is the excenter of △PCD opposite P** by the same reasoning (bisectors of ∠BC
D and ∠CDA are external bisectors at C and D of △PCD), with exradius $r_G$ = dis
tance from G to CD.

**Exradius ratio.** Since △PAB ~ △PCD with ratio k, all corresponding lengths sc
ale by k, including the exradii opposite P:

$$\frac{r_H}{r_G} = k = \frac{3}{7}.$$

**Computing [CDG].** We have:

$$[\text{ABH}] = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot AB \cdot r_H = 25, \qquad [\text{CDG}] = \tf
rac{1}{2} \cdot CD \cdot r_G.$$

Substituting $AB = k \cdot CD$ and $r_H = k \cdot r_G$:

$$[\text{ABH}] = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot k \cdot CD \cdot k \cdot r_G = k^2 \cdot [\t
ext{CDG}].$$

Therefore:

$$[\text{CDG}] = \frac{[\text{ABH}]}{k^2} = \frac{25}{(3/7)^2} = 25 \cdot \frac{
49}{9} = \frac{1225}{9}.$$

Since $\gcd(1225, 9) = 1$ (as $1225 = 5^2 \cdot 7^2$ and $9 = 3^2$), the fractio
n is irreducible: $a = 1225$, $b = 9$.

$$a + b = 1225 + 9 = \boxed{1234}.$$
