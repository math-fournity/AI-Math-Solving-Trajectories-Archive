## Proof

**Parity analysis.** The four quantities $a+b-c,\; a+c-b,\;b+c-a,\;a+b+c$ are pa
irwise of the same parity (any two differ by an even number). If they were all e
ven, they would all equal $2$ (the only even prime), contradicting distinctness.
 Hence they are all **odd**. In particular $a+b+c$ is odd.

Since $a,b,c$ are themselves primes among the seven, and $a+b+c$ is odd, either
all three of $a,b,c$ are odd, or exactly one is odd and two are even. The latter
 forces two of them to equal $2$, contradicting distinctness. So **$a,b,c$ are a
ll odd primes**, and consequently all seven primes are odd, so the smallest poss
ible value among them is $3$.

**Setting up.** Relabel so that $a+b=800$. Then the seven primes are
$$a,\;\;b,\;\;c,\;\;800-c,\;\;a+c-b,\;\;b+c-a,\;\;800+c.$$

Since $800-c$ is a positive odd prime, $800-c\ge 3$, giving $c\le 797$.

**Bounding $d$.** The largest of the seven is $800+c$: it exceeds $a$ and $b$ (e
ach $<800$), exceeds $c$, exceeds $800-c$, and exceeds $a+c-b$ and $b+c-a$ (sinc
e $800+b>a$ and $800+a>b$). The smallest is at least $3$. Therefore
$$d = (800+c) - (\text{least prime}) \le (800+797)-3 = 1597-3 = 1594.$$

**Achieving the bound.** Take $c=797$ (prime), $a=13$, $b=787$ (both prime, $13+
787=800$). The seven values are:
$$13,\;\;787,\;\;797,\;\;800-797=3,\;\;13+797-787=23,\;\;787+797-13=1571,\;\;800
+797=1597.$$

All are prime ($787, 797, 1571, 1597$ verified prime by trial division up to $\s
qrt{}$; $3,13,23$ trivially prime) and all seven are distinct:
$$\{3,\;13,\;23,\;787,\;797,\;1571,\;1597\}.$$

The largest is $1597$, the least is $3$, giving $d = 1597-3 = 1594$.

Since the upper bound $1594$ is attained, the maximum value of $d$ is

$$\boxed{1594}$$
