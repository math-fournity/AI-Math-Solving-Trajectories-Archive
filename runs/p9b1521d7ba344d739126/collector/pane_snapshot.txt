**Problem.** Evaluate $\displaystyle I = \int_0^{\infty} \frac{\cos(2\alpha q)}{
\sqrt{1+q^2}}\,dq$.

## Proof

Assume first $\alpha > 0$ (the extension to general real $\alpha$ follows at the
 end since $\cos$ is even). We reduce the integral to the standard representatio
n of the modified Bessel function $K_0$.

### Step 1 — Laplace transform of $J_0$

We use the classical identity (valid for $\operatorname{Re}(a)>0$):
$$\int_0^\infty e^{-as}\,J_0(bs)\,ds \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}.$$
Setting $a=1$, $b=q$ gives a representation of our weight function:
$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+q^2}} \;=\; \int_0^\infty e^{-s}\,J_0(qs)\,ds. \tag{1}$$

### Step 2 — Weber–Schafheitlin discontinuous integral

We use the standard discontinuous integral (derivable from the Fourier–Bessel re
presentation of $J_0$ and the distributional identity $\int_0^\infty \cos(kt)\,d
t=\pi\delta(k)$):
$$\int_0^\infty J_0(qs)\,\cos(\omega q)\,dq \;=\; \begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{s
^2-\omega^2}}, & 0<\omega<s,\\[6pt] 0, & 0\le s<\omega.\end{cases} \tag{2}$$

### Step 3 — Combine the two transforms

Insert (1) into $I$ and interchange the order of integration (justified by inser
ting a regularizing factor $e^{-\varepsilon q}$, applying Fubini to the now abso
lutely convergent double integral, then letting $\varepsilon\to 0^+$):
$$I \;=\; \int_0^\infty \cos(2\alpha q)\left(\int_0^\infty e^{-s}J_0(qs)\,ds\rig
ht)dq \;=\; \int_0^\infty e^{-s}\left(\int_0^\infty J_0(qs)\cos(2\alpha q)\,dq\r
ight)ds.$$

Applying (2) with $\omega = 2\alpha$, the inner integral vanishes for $s<2\alpha
$ and equals $(s^2-4\alpha^2)^{-1/2}$ for $s>2\alpha$. Hence
$$I \;=\; \int_{2\alpha}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-s}}{\sqrt{s^2-4\alpha^2}}\,ds. \tag{
3}$$

### Step 4 — Reduce to $K_0$

In (3) substitute $s = 2\alpha\cosh u$, so $ds = 2\alpha\sinh u\,du$ and $\sqrt{
s^2-4\alpha^2}=2\alpha\sinh u$. The limits $s=2\alpha$ and $s\to\infty$ correspo
nd to $u=0$ and $u\to\infty$. Therefore
$$I \;=\; \int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-2\alpha\cosh u}}{2\alpha\sinh u}\,(2\alpha\sin
h u)\,du \;=\; \int_0^\infty e^{-2\alpha\cosh u}\,du.$$

By the standard integral representation of the modified Bessel function of the s
econd kind,
$$K_0(z) \;=\; \int_0^\infty e^{-z\cosh u}\,du, \qquad \operatorname{Re}(z)>0,$$
we conclude
$$I \;=\; K_0(2\alpha), \qquad \alpha>0.$$

### General real $\alpha$

Since $\cos(2\alpha q)=\cos(2|\alpha|q)$ for all real $q$, the integral is an ev
en function of $\alpha$. Thus for any real $\alpha\neq 0$,
$$I = K_0(2|\alpha|).$$
(At $\alpha=0$ the integral diverges, consistent with $K_0(z)\to+\infty$ as $z\t
o 0^+$.)

---

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_0^{\infty} \frac{\cos(2\alpha q)}{\sqrt{1+q^2}}\,dq \
;=\; K_0\!\bigl(2|\alpha|\bigr)}$$

where $K_0$ is the modified Bessel function of the second kind of order zero.
