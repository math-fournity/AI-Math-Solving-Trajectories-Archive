Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether non-embeddability of a surf
ace $X$ into $\mathbb{R}^n$ is preserved under taking the connected sum $X \# X$
.

**Answer: Yes, it is true.**

**Proof.**

We assume $X$ is a closed (compact, without boundary) connected surface, as conn
ected sum is most naturally defined in this setting. We analyze by the value of
$n$.

---

**Case 1: $n \geq 4$.** Every closed surface embeds into $\mathbb{R}^4$, and hen
ce into $\mathbb{R}^n$ for all $n \geq 4$.

- Orientable surfaces embed into $\mathbb{R}^3 \subset \mathbb{R}^4$.
- Non-orientable surfaces embed into $\mathbb{R}^4$: the real projective plane $
\mathbb{RP}^2$ embeds into $\mathbb{R}^4$ (classical, e.g., via the Veronese-typ
e construction), and every non-orientable closed surface is a connected sum of c
opies of $\mathbb{RP}^2$. Since connected sums of surfaces embedded in $\mathbb{
R}^4$ can be formed by joining with tubes in $\mathbb{R}^4$ (there is enough amb
ient dimension to avoid self-intersection), all non-orientable closed surfaces e
mbed into $\mathbb{R}^4$.

Therefore, there is **no** closed surface $X$ that fails to embed into $\mathbb{
R}^n$ for $n \geq 4$. The hypothesis is vacuously satisfied.

---

**Case 2: $n = 3$.** A closed surface embeds into $\mathbb{R}^3$ if and only if
it is orientable.

- *(Orientable $\Rightarrow$ embeds):* Every closed orientable surface is a sphe
re with $g$ handles, which embeds into $\mathbb{R}^3$ by the standard handle con
struction.
- *(Embeds $\Rightarrow$ orientable):* If a closed surface $M$ embeds into $\mat
hbb{R}^3$, then by the **Jordan–Brouwer separation theorem**, $M$ separates $\ma
thbb{R}^3$ into a bounded interior and an unbounded exterior. The unit normal ve
ctor field pointing toward the exterior provides a global continuous normal fiel
d, which orients $M$. Hence $M$ is orientable.

Now suppose $X$ cannot be embedded into $\mathbb{R}^3$. By the equivalence above
, $X$ is **non-orientable**. The connected sum of two non-orientable surfaces is
 non-orientable (orientability is preserved under connected sum in the sense tha
t $X \# Y$ is orientable iff both $X$ and $Y$ are orientable). Therefore $X \# X
$ is non-orientable, and consequently **$X \# X$ cannot be embedded into $\mathb
b{R}^3$**.

---

**Case 3: $n = 2$.** No closed surface embeds into $\mathbb{R}^2$.

Suppose a closed surface $M$ embeds into $\mathbb{R}^2$. By **invariance of doma
in**, the image of $M$ (a 2-manifold without boundary) in $\mathbb{R}^2$ is open
. Since $M$ is compact, its image is also closed. Being both open and closed in
the connected space $\mathbb{R}^2$, the image is either empty or all of $\mathbb
{R}^2$. It cannot be empty, and it cannot be all of $\mathbb{R}^2$ (since $\math
bb{R}^2$ is non-compact but the image is compact). Contradiction.

Thus every closed surface $X$ fails to embed into $\mathbb{R}^2$, and $X \# X$ i
s also a closed surface, so it also fails to embed into $\mathbb{R}^2$.

---

**Case 4: $n \leq 1$.** No 2-dimensional manifold embeds into $\mathbb{R}^n$ for
 $n \leq 1$ (by invariance of domain / dimension theory). The hypothesis is alwa
ys satisfied, and $X \# X$ is still a 2-manifold, so it also fails to embed.

---

**Conclusion.** In every case, if $X$ is a closed surface that cannot be embedde
d into $\mathbb{R}^n$, then $X \# X$ also cannot be embedded into $\mathbb{R}^n$
. The key mechanism is:

- For $n \geq 4$: the hypothesis never holds (vacuous truth).
- For $n = 3$: non-embeddability is equivalent to non-orientability, and non-ori
entability is preserved under connected sum.
- For $n \leq 2$: no closed surface embeds, so the conclusion holds trivially.
