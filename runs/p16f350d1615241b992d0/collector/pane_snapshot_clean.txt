We determine the values of \(p\) for which
\[
I(p)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-x}-1}{x^{p}}\,dx
\]
converges. The integrand has two potential trouble points: \(x=0\) (where the nu
merator vanishes but the denominator also vanishes) and \(x=\infty\). We split t
he integral at, say, \(x=1\):
\[
I(p)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-x}-1}{x^{p}}\,dx+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-x}-1}{x^{
p}}\,dx.
\]

**Behavior near \(x=0\).** Using the Taylor expansion \(e^{-x}=1-x+\frac{x^{2}}{
2}-\cdots\), we get
\[
e^{-x}-1=-x+\frac{x^{2}}{2}-O(x^{3}),
\]
so
\[
\frac{e^{-x}-1}{x^{p}}=\frac{-x+O(x^{2})}{x^{p}}=-x^{1-p}+O(x^{2-p}).
\]
The leading term is \(-x^{1-p}\). The integral \(\int_{0}^{1}x^{1-p}\,dx\) conve
rges if and only if \(1-p>-1\), i.e. \(p<2\). The remainder \(O(x^{2-p})\) is in
tegrable near \(0\) whenever \(p<3\), which is certainly satisfied when \(p<2\).
 Hence
\[
\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-x}-1}{x^{p}}\,dx \text{ converges} \iff p<2.
\]

**Behavior near \(x=\infty\).** For large \(x\), \(e^{-x}\to 0\), so
\[
\frac{e^{-x}-1}{x^{p}}=\frac{e^{-x}}{x^{p}}-\frac{1}{x^{p}}.
\]
The term \(\frac{e^{-x}}{x^{p}}\) is integrable on \([1,\infty)\) for every real
 \(p\) (exponential decay dominates any polynomial growth). Thus the convergence
 of \(\int_{1}^{\infty}\) is governed by \(\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^{p}}\,dx\
), which converges if and only if \(p>1\). Hence
\[
\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-x}-1}{x^{p}}\,dx \text{ converges} \iff p>1.
\]

**Combining.** The full integral converges if and only if both pieces converge s
imultaneously:
\[
p<2 \quad\text{and}\quad p>1,
\]
that is,
\[
\boxed{1<p<2}.
\]
