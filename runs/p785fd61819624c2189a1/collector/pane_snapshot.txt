## Solution: Laplace's Equation in a Disk with Dirichlet Boundary Conditions

We solve Laplace's equation in the disk $\rho \le a$ (plane polar coordinates $\
rho, \phi$) with boundary condition $u(a,\phi) = f(\phi)$.

### Step 1. Separation of variables

Laplace's equation in plane polar coordinates is
$$\nabla^2 u = \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial \rho^2} + \frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\parti
al u}{\partial \rho} + \frac{1}{\rho^2}\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial \phi^2} = 0.
$$

Seeking solutions of the form $u(\rho,\phi) = R(\rho)\,\Phi(\phi)$ and substitut
ing, then dividing by $R\Phi/\rho^2$:
$$\frac{\rho^2 R'' + \rho R'}{R} + \frac{\Phi''}{\Phi} = 0.$$

The angular part must be periodic with period $2\pi$, so $\Phi'' + n^2 \Phi = 0$
 with $n \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$, giving $\Phi_n(\phi) = A_n \cos n\phi + B_n \s
in n\phi$ (with $n=0$ giving the constant). The radial equation becomes
$$\rho^2 R'' + \rho R' - n^2 R = 0,$$
the Euler–Cauchy equation, with general solution $R_n = C_n \rho^n + D_n \rho^{-
n}$ (and $R_0 = C_0 + D_0 \ln \rho$).

### Step 2. Regularity at the origin

Since the solution must be finite (regular) at $\rho = 0$, we discard the singul
ar terms $\rho^{-n}$ and $\ln \rho$. Thus the general regular harmonic function
in the disk is
$$u(\rho,\phi) = \frac{A_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left(\frac{\rho}{a}\right)
^n \bigl[a_n \cos n\phi + b_n \sin n\phi\bigr],$$
where we have written the radial factor as $(\rho/a)^n$ for convenience (absorbi
ng constants into the coefficients).

### Step 3. Applying the boundary condition

Setting $\rho = a$:
$$u(a,\phi) = f(\phi) = \frac{A_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl[a_n \cos n\phi
+ b_n \sin n\phi\bigr].$$

This is the Fourier series of $f(\phi)$, so the coefficients are determined by t
he standard orthogonality relations:
$$a_n = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(\varphi)\cos n\varphi\,d\varphi, \qquad b_n
 = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(\varphi)\sin n\varphi\,d\varphi, \qquad n \ge 0$
$
(with $b_0 = 0$ understood).

### Step 4. The Poisson integral formula

Substituting the Fourier coefficients back and using the identity
$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} r^{|n|} e^{in(\phi-\varphi)} = \frac{1
-r^2}{2\pi\bigl(1 - 2r\cos(\phi-\varphi) + r^2\bigr)}, \qquad |r|<1,$$
with $r = \rho/a$, we obtain the closed-form **Poisson integral formula**:

$$\boxed{u(\rho,\phi) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} \frac{a^2 - \rho^2}{a^2 - 2a
\rho\cos(\phi-\varphi) + \rho^2}\, f(\varphi)\,d\varphi, \qquad \rho < a.}$$

### Verification

- **Harmonicity**: The kernel $P(\rho,\phi;\varphi) = \frac{a^2-\rho^2}{a^2 - 2a
\rho\cos(\phi-\varphi)+\rho^2}$ is harmonic in $(\rho,\phi)$ for $\rho < a$ (it
is the real part of an analytic function of the complex variable $z = \rho e^{i\
phi}$), so $u$ is harmonic by differentiation under the integral sign.
- **Boundary values**: As $\rho \to a^-$, the Poisson kernel tends to $2\pi\,\de
lta(\phi - \varphi)$ (an approximate identity), so $u(\rho,\phi) \to f(\phi)$, r
ecovering the boundary condition. More precisely, for $f$ continuous, $u(\rho,\p
hi) \to f(\phi)$ pointwise; for $f$ merely integrable, convergence holds at ever
y Lebesgue point.
- **Regularity at origin**: At $\rho = 0$, $u(0,\phi) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\
pi} f(\varphi)\,d\varphi = a_0/2$, the mean value of $f$—finite and independent
of $\phi$, as required by the mean value property.
- **Uniqueness**: By the maximum principle, a harmonic function continuous on th
e closed disk is uniquely determined by its boundary values, so this is the uniq
ue solution.

### Summary

The solution to Laplace's equation $\nabla^2 u = 0$ in the disk $\rho \le a$ wit
h $u(a,\phi) = f(\phi)$ is given by the Poisson integral formula above (equivale
ntly, by the Fourier series with coefficients $a_n, b_n$ and radial factors $(\r
ho/a)^n$). The solution is harmonic inside the disk, regular at the origin, atta
ins the boundary data $f$ on $\rho = a$, and is unique by the maximum principle.
