Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every valuation of a one-va
riable function field $K/k$, trivial on $k^\times$, is necessarily discrete.

**Answer: Yes, $v$ is always a discrete valuation.**

## Proof

**Step 1: The absolute value is non-archimedean.**

Since $|k^\times|=1$, for every nonzero $n\cdot 1\in k$ we have $|n\cdot 1|=1\le
q 1$. By the standard Ostrowski criterion, an absolute value with $|n\cdot 1|\le
q 1$ for all $n$ is non-archimedean. Thus $v(x)=-\log|x|$ satisfies the ultramet
ric inequality $v(x+y)\geq\min(v(x),v(y))$, and $v:K\to\mathbb{R}$ is a genuine
valuation trivial on $k^\times$.

**Step 2: Reduction to $k(t)$.**

Let $w=v|_{k(t)}$ be the restriction. We claim: if $w$ is trivial on $k(t)$, the
n $v$ is trivial on $K$.

*Proof of claim.* Every $\alpha\in K$ is algebraic over $k(t)$, satisfying $\alp
ha^n+a_{n-1}\alpha^{n-1}+\cdots+a_0=0$ with $a_i\in k(t)$. Suppose $v(\alpha)>0$
. Then $v(\alpha^n)=nv(\alpha)>0$ and $v(a_i\alpha^i)=w(a_i)+iv(\alpha)=iv(\alph
a)>0$ for $i\geq 1$, while $v(a_0)=w(a_0)=0$. The term $a_0$ achieves the unique
 minimum valuation $0$ among all summands, so by the ultrametric property $v(\al
pha^n+\cdots+a_0)=0$, contradicting $v(0)=+\infty$. Similarly, if $v(\alpha)<0$,
 the leading term $\alpha^n$ has the strictly smallest (and unique) valuation $n
v(\alpha)$ among all summands, giving $v(0)=nv(\alpha)\neq+\infty$, a contradict
ion. Hence $v(\alpha)=0$. $\square$

So if $v$ is non-trivial on $K$, then $w$ is non-trivial on $k(t)$.

**Step 3: Non-trivial valuations of $k(t)$ trivial on $k$ are discrete.**

Let $w:k(t)\to\mathbb{R}$ be non-trivial with $w(k^\times)=\{0\}$.

- **$w(t)>0$:** For $f=\sum a_i t^i\in k[t]$, the terms $a_i t^i$ have distinct
valuations $i\,w(t)$, so $w(f)=\min_i\, i\,w(t)=(\operatorname{ord}_t f)\cdot w(
t)$. Thus $w=w(t)\cdot v_t$ with value group $w(t)\,\mathbb{Z}$.

- **$w(t)<0$:** By the same argument with $t^{-1}$, we get $w=|w(t)|\cdot v_\inf
ty$ (the valuation at infinity) with value group $|w(t)|\,\mathbb{Z}$.

- **$w(t)=0$:** Then $w$ is zero on $k[t]\setminus\{0\}$ unless the set $\mathfr
ak{m}=\{f\in k[t]:w(f)>0\}\cup\{0\}$ is a nonzero prime ideal. Since $w(t)=0$, w
e have $t\notin\mathfrak{m}$, so $\mathfrak{m}=(p(t))$ for some irreducible $p\n
eq t$. Writing $f=p^e g$ with $p\nmid g$: $w(f)=e\,w(p)+w(g)=e\,w(p)$ (since $g\
notin\mathfrak{m}$ gives $w(g)=0$). So $w=w(p)\cdot v_p$ with value group $w(p)\
,\mathbb{Z}$.

In every non-trivial case, $w(k(t)^\times)=\alpha\,\mathbb{Z}$ for some $\alpha>
0$.

**Step 4: Extension to the finite extension $K/k(t)$ preserves discreteness.**

Let $n=:k(t)]<\infty$. The valuation $v$ on $K$ extends $w$ on $k(t)$. By the
fundamental inequality for extensions of valuations to finite field extensions,
the value groups satisfy $\Gamma_w\subseteq\Gamma_v\subseteq\mathbb{R}$ with $[\
Gamma_v:\Gamma_w]=e$ (the ramification index) and $ef\leq n$, so $e$ is a positi
ve integer.

Now $\Gamma_w=\alpha\,\mathbb{Z}$ and $\Gamma_v/\Gamma_w$ is a subgroup of $\mat
hbb{R}/\alpha\,\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ of order $e$. Any element o
f order dividing $e$ in $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ lies in $\frac{1}{e}\mathbb{Z}/\
mathbb{Z}$ (the unique $e$-torsion subgroup), so $\Gamma_v/\alpha\,\mathbb{Z}=\f
rac{\alpha}{e}\mathbb{Z}/\alpha\,\mathbb{Z}$, giving:

$$\Gamma_v = v(K^\times)=\frac{\alpha}{e}\,\mathbb{Z}.$$

Setting $t=\alpha/e>0$, we have $v(K^\times)=t\,\mathbb{Z}$.

**Conclusion.** For any non-trivial absolute value on $K$ with $|k^\times|=1$, t
he induced valuation satisfies $v(K^\times)=t\,\mathbb{Z}$ for some $t>0$. (The
trivial absolute value gives $v(K^\times)=\{0\}$, the degenerate case.) Therefor
e $v$ is always a discrete valuation. $\blacksquare$
