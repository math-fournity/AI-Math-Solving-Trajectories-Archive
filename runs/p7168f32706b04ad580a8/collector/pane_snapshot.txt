## Answer: No.

**Counterexample.** Let $X = \mathbb{R}$ equipped with the **fine uniformity** $
\mathcal{U}_f$ — the finest uniformity compatible with the usual topology on $\m
athbb{R}$. Since $\mathbb{R}$ is paracompact Hausdorff, the entourages of $\math
cal{U}_f$ are precisely all neighbourhoods of the diagonal $\Delta_X$ in $X \tim
es X$.

---

### The diagonal is a closed $G_\delta$

The topology induced by $\mathcal{U}_f$ is the usual topology on $\mathbb{R}$, s
o $X \times X$ carries the usual product topology. Since $\mathbb{R}$ is Hausdor
ff, $\Delta_X$ is closed in $X \times X$. Since $X \times X \cong \mathbb{R}^2$
is metrisable, every closed subset is $G_\delta$; explicitly,

$$\Delta_X = \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\,\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^2 : |x-y|<1/n\}$$

is a countable intersection of open sets. So $\Delta_X$ is a closed $G_\delta$ i
n $X\times X$.

---

### The uniformity is not metrisable

Recall (Birkhoff–Kakutani): a Hausdorff uniform space is metrisable (its uniform
ity is induced by a metric) if and only if its uniformity has a **countable base
**.

Suppose for contradiction that $\mathcal{U}_f$ has a countable base $\{U_n\}_{n=
1}^\infty$. Each $U_n$ is a neighbourhood of $\Delta_X$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$, so th
ere exists a continuous $f_n:\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)$ with the variable-width st
rip

$$W_{f_n}=\{(x,y):|x-y|<f_n(x)\}\subseteq U_n.$$

(This holds because $U_n$ contains an open neighbourhood of $\Delta_X$, and ever
y open neighbourhood of the diagonal contains such a strip $W_f$ for some contin
uous $f>0$.)

Let $\{\varphi_n\}$ be a smooth partition of unity on $\mathbb{R}$ with $\operat
orname{supp}(\varphi_n)\subseteq(n-1,n+1)$. Define

$$g(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_n(x)\cdot\frac{f_n(x)}{2}.$$

Then $g:\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)$ is continuous (locally the sum is finite), so $
W_g$ is an open neighbourhood of $\Delta_X$, hence an entourage of $\mathcal{U}_
f$.

Since $\{U_n\}$ is a base, there exists $k$ with $U_k\subseteq W_g$. But $W_{f_k
}\subseteq U_k\subseteq W_g$, so $f_k(x)\le g(x)$ for all $x$. Choose $x_0$ with
 $\varphi_k(x_0)=1$ (hence $\varphi_n(x_0)=0$ for $n\ne k$). Then

$$g(x_0)=\frac{f_k(x_0)}{2},$$

so $f_k(x_0)\le\frac{f_k(x_0)}{2}$, giving $f_k(x_0)\le 0$ — contradicting $f_k>
0$.

Therefore $\mathcal{U}_f$ has **no countable base**, and $(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{U
}_f)$ is not metrisable.

---

### Conclusion

The uniform space $(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{U}_f)$ has a diagonal that is a closed $
G_\delta$ subset of $X\times X$, yet the space is not metrisable. The $G_\delta$
-diagonal condition is purely topological (it depends only on the topology of $X
$, not on the specific uniformity), so it cannot distinguish between the metrisa
ble usual uniformity and the non-metrisable fine uniformity, both of which induc
e the same topology on $\mathbb{R}$.

**The answer is no.**
