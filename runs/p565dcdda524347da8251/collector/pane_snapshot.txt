## Proof

We determine the cardinality of a Hamel basis of $\mathbb{R}$ viewed as a vector
 space over $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Notation.** Let $|\mathbb{N}| = \aleph_0$ and $|\mathbb{R}| = 2^{\aleph_0} = \
mathfrak{c}$.

**Step 1: The basis exists and is infinite.**

By the Axiom of Choice (equivalently, Zorn's Lemma), every vector space has a ba
sis. So $\mathbb{R}$ has a Hamel basis $B$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Since $\mathbb{Q}$
 is countable and $\mathbb{R}$ is uncountable, $B$ must be infinite (a finite-di
mensional space over a countable field is countable).

**Step 2: Upper bound — $|B| \le \mathfrak{c}$.**

Every element of $\mathbb{R}$ is a *finite* $\mathbb{Q}$-linear combination of e
lements of $B$. So $\mathbb{R}$ is the union, over all $n \ge 1$, of the sets of
 $\mathbb{Q}$-linear combinations using exactly $n$ basis elements. The set of s
uch combinations using $n$ basis elements has cardinality at most
$$|B|^n \cdot |\mathbb{Q}|^n = |B|^n \cdot \aleph_0^n.$$
For infinite $|B|$, $|B|^n = |B|$, and $\aleph_0^n = \aleph_0$, so each level ha
s cardinality $\le |B| \cdot \aleph_0 = \max(|B|,\aleph_0) = |B|$. The countable
 union over $n$ gives
$$|\mathbb{R}| \le \aleph_0 \cdot |B| = |B|.$$
Wait — this gives $|B| \ge \mathfrak{c}$, the *lower* bound direction. Let me re
do this carefully to get both bounds.

**Step 2 (corrected): Upper bound $|B| \le \mathfrak{c}$.**

Trivially $B \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, so $|B| \le |\mathbb{R}| = \mathfrak{c}$.

**Step 3: Lower bound — $|B| \ge \mathfrak{c}$.**

Every real number is a finite $\mathbb{Q}$-linear combination of basis elements.
 Thus
$$\mathbb{R} = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} S_n, \qquad S_n = \left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n} q_
i b_i : q_i \in \mathbb{Q},\; b_i \in B\right\}.$$
For each $n$, $|S_n| \le |B|^n \cdot |\mathbb{Q}|^n$.

- If $|B|$ is finite, then $|S_n| \le \aleph_0$ for all $n$, making $\mathbb{R}$
 a countable union of countable sets — countable. Contradiction. So $|B|$ is inf
inite.
- For infinite $|B|$: $|B|^n = |B|$ and $|\mathbb{Q}|^n = \aleph_0$, so $|S_n| \
le |B| \cdot \aleph_0 = |B|$.

Hence
$$\mathfrak{c} = |\mathbb{R}| = \left|\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} S_n\right| \le \ale
ph_0 \cdot |B| = |B|.$$
So $|B| \ge \mathfrak{c}$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 2 and 3:
$$\mathfrak{c} \le |B| \le \mathfrak{c},$$
so $|B| = \mathfrak{c} = 2^{\aleph_0}$.

**Remark on the role of AC.** The existence of the basis uses Zorn's Lemma (AC).
 It is consistent with ZF (without AC) that $\mathbb{R}$ has no Hamel basis over
 $\mathbb{Q}$ at all. Under the standard ZFC foundation, the basis exists and ha
s the cardinality computed above.

---

**Answer.** The cardinality of a basis of $\mathbb{R}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is $\bo
xed{2^{\aleph_0}}$, the cardinality of the continuum (equivalently, $|\mathbb{R}
|$).
