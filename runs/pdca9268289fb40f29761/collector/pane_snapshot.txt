## Solution

We want to compute $\mathbb{E}\!\left[\Phi\!\left(\dfrac{W_{t+a}}{\sqrt{2+t}}\ri
ght)\right]$, where $W$ is a standard Brownian motion and $\Phi$ is the standard
 normal CDF.

**Step 1: Distribution of the argument.**

Since $W_{t+a}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\,t+a)$, we have
$$\frac{W_{t+a}}{\sqrt{2+t}} \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\;\sigma^2\right),\qquad
\sigma^2=\frac{t+a}{2+t}.$$

So we may write $\frac{W_{t+a}}{\sqrt{2+t}}=\sigma Z$ with $Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1
)$.

**Step 2: A useful identity.**

For $Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ and any constant $b$,
$$\mathbb{E}\big[\Phi(bZ)\big]=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi(bz)\,\varphi(z)\,dz.$
$

Introduce an independent $X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$. Then $\Phi(bz)=\mathbb{P}(X\le
 bz)$, hence
$$\mathbb{E}\big[\Phi(bZ)\big]=\mathbb{P}(X\le bZ)=\mathbb{P}(X-bZ\le 0).$$

Since $X-bZ\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\,1+b^2)$ is centered (mean $0$), the probability t
hat it is $\le 0$ equals $\tfrac12$.

**Step 3: Apply to our case.**

Here $b=\sigma=\sqrt{\frac{t+a}{2+t}}$, so
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\Phi\!\left(\frac{W_{t+a}}{\sqrt{2+t}}\right)\right]=\mathbb
{E}\big[\Phi(\sigma Z)\big]=\frac12.$$

The result is independent of $t$ and $a$; it relies only on the fact that $W_{t+
a}$ is a centered Gaussian, so after scaling it remains centered, and $\Phi$ of
a centered Gaussian has expectation $\tfrac12$.

$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\Phi\!\left(\frac{W_{t+a}}{\sqrt{2+t}}\right)\right]=
\frac12}$$
