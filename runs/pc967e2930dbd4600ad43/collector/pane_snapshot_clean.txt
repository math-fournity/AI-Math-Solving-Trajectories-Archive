## Proof

**Yes**, such a subfield exists.

**Setup.** We identify $K = \mathbb{Q}(x)$, the rational function field in one v
ariable over $\mathbb{Q}$. We seek a subfield $F \subseteq K$ with $F \cong \mat
hbb{Q}(x)$ and $:F]=2$.

**Construction.** Consider the map $\sigma : \mathbb{Q}(x) \to \mathbb{Q}(x)$ de
fined by $\sigma(f(x)) = f(-x)$. This is a $\mathbb{Q}$-automorphism of $\mathbb
{Q}(x)$ (induced by the Möbius transformation $x \mapsto -x$), and $\sigma^2 = \
mathrm{id}$, so $\sigma$ has order $2$.

Let $F = K^{\sigma} = \{g \in \mathbb{Q}(x) : \sigma(g) = g\}$ be the fixed fiel
d, i.e., the field of even rational functions.

**Claim 1: $F = \mathbb{Q}(x^2)$.** Every element of $\mathbb{Q}(x^2)$ is fixed
by $\sigma$ since $(-x)^2 = x^2$. Conversely, write any $g(x) \in \mathbb{Q}(x)$
 as $g(x) = p(x)/q(x)$ with $p, q \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$, $\gcd(p,q)=1$. Decompose $
p = p_e + p_o$, $q = q_e + q_o$ into even and odd parts. The condition $g(-x) =
g(x)$ gives $p(-x)q(x) = p(x)q(-x)$, i.e.,
$$(p_e - p_o)(q_e + q_o) = (p_e + p_o)(q_e - q_o).$$
Expanding and simplifying yields $p_e q_o = p_o q_e$. Since $\gcd(p,q)=1$, one c
hecks this forces $p_o/q_o = p_e/q_e$ (when nonzero), so $g = p_e/q_e \in \mathb
b{Q}(x^2)$. Hence $F = \mathbb{Q}(x^2)$.

**Claim 2: $F \cong \mathbb{Q}(x)$.** The element $x^2$ is transcendental over $
\mathbb{Q}$ (since $x$ is transcendental), so $\mathbb{Q}(x^2) \cong \mathbb{Q}(
t) \cong \mathbb{Q}(x)$.

**Claim 3: $:F] = 2$.** The element $x \in K$ satisfies the polynomial $T^2 -
x^2 \in F[T]$, so $:F] \leq 2$. Since $x \notin F = \mathbb{Q}(x^2)$ (as $x$ i
s an odd function, not even), we have $:F] > 1$. Therefore $:F] = 2$.

(Equivalently, by Artin's theorem on fixed fields of finite automorphism groups,
 $ : K^{\sigma}] = |\langle \sigma \rangle| = 2$ directly.)

**Conclusion.** Taking $F = \mathbb{Q}(x^2) \subset \mathbb{Q}(x) = K$ gives $F
\cong \mathbb{Q}(x) \cong K$ and $:F] = 2$. $\blacksquare$
