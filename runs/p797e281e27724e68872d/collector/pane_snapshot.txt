Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum number of vertices for graph
s with maximum degree 3 and diameter 2 (with and without a triangle constraint).

## Proof

**Setup.** A graph with maximum degree 3 and the property that any two non-adjac
ent vertices share a common neighbor is exactly a graph of diameter at most 2.

**Moore Bound.** For a graph with maximum degree $d$ and diameter 2, from any ve
rtex $v$ the number of vertices within distance 2 is at most $1 + d + d(d-1)$. F
or $d = 3$, this gives $n \leq 1 + 3 + 6 = 10$.

**Computing $M_1$.** The Petersen graph has 10 vertices, is 3-regular, and has d
iameter 2. So $M_1 \geq 10$. Combined with the Moore bound, $M_1 = 10$.

**Computing $M_2$ — ruling out $n = 10$.** If $n = 10$, the Moore bound is tight
 for every vertex. This forces: (i) every vertex has degree exactly 3, (ii) no t
wo neighbors of any vertex are adjacent, and (iii) no two neighbors of any verte
x share a common neighbor other than the vertex itself. These conditions define
a Moore graph of degree 3, diameter 2 — and the unique such graph is the **Peter
sen graph**, which has girth 5 (no triangles). So no graph with $n = 10$, max de
gree 3, diameter 2 contains a triangle.

**Ruling out $n = 9$.** For $n = 9$, consider any vertex $v$ with degree $d$. Th
e number of vertices within distance 2 is at most $1 + d + \sum_{w \sim v}(\deg(
w) - 1)$. For diameter 2 we need this $\geq 9$:
- If $d = 3$: need $\sum \deg(w) \geq 8$, achievable since each $\deg(w) \leq 3$
.
- If $d = 2$: need $\sum \deg(w) \geq 9$, but $\sum \deg(w) \leq 3 + 3 = 6 < 9$.
 **Impossible.**
- If $d \leq 1$: even worse. **Impossible.**

So every vertex must have degree exactly 3. But $9 \times 3 = 27$ is odd, contra
dicting the handshaking lemma. **No such graph exists.** Thus $M_2 \leq 8$.

**Constructing $n = 8$ with a triangle.** Define a graph on vertices $\{1, 2, \l
dots, 8\}$ with edges:
- Triangle: $\{1,2\}, \{2,3\}, \{1,3\}$
- Spokes: $\{1,4\}, \{2,5\}, \{3,6\}$
- Complete bipartite $K_{3,2}$ between $\{4,5,6\}$ and $\{7,8\}$: $\{4,7\}, \{4,
8\}, \{5,7\}, \{5,8\}, \{6,7\}, \{6,8\}$

**Degree check:** Every vertex has degree exactly 3:
- $1: \{2,3,4\}$, $2: \{1,3,5\}$, $3: \{1,2,6\}$
- $4: \{1,7,8\}$, $5: \{2,7,8\}$, $6: \{3,7,8\}$
- $7: \{4,5,6\}$, $8: \{4,5,6\}$

**Diameter 2 check (all non-adjacent pairs have a common neighbor):**
- Pairs within $\{1,2,3\}$: all adjacent (triangle). ✓
- $1$ with $\{5,6,7,8\}$: common neighbors $2, 3, 4, 4$ respectively. ✓
- $2$ with $\{4,6,7,8\}$: common neighbors $1, 3, 5, 5$ respectively. ✓
- $3$ with $\{4,5,7,8\}$: common neighbors $1, 2, 6, 6$ respectively. ✓
- Pairs within $\{4,5,6\}$: $4\&5$ share $\{7,8\}$; $4\&6$ share $\{7,8\}$; $5\&
6$ share $\{7,8\}$. ✓
- $7\&8$: share $\{4,5,6\}$. ✓
- Pairs $\{4,5,6\}$ with $\{7,8\}$: all adjacent. ✓

All non-adjacent pairs have a common neighbor, so diameter is 2. The graph conta
ins triangle $\{1,2,3\}$.

Therefore $M_2 \geq 8$, and combined with $M_2 \leq 8$, we get $M_2 = 8$.

**Final answer:**

$$M_1 \times M_2 = 10 \times 8 = \boxed{80}$$
