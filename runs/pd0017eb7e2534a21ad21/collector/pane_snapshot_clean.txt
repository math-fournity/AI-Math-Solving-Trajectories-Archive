## Setting Up the Sequences

Using standard Knuth up-arrow notation where $a \uparrow^k b$ denotes $a$ with $
k$ up-arrows followed by $b$:

- $G_1 = 3 \uparrow^4 3$, and $G_n = 3 \uparrow^{G_{n-1}} 3$ for $n \geq 2$ (the
 number of arrows equals $G_{n-1}$).
- $H_1 = 4 \uparrow^4 4$, and $H_n = 4 \uparrow^{H_{n-1}} 4$ for $n \geq 2$.

This is the Graham's number construction, applied to both base 3 and base 4.

## Key Monotonicity Property

For $a \geq 2$, $b \geq 2$, and $k \geq 1$, the function $a \uparrow^k b$ is **s
trictly increasing** in each of its three arguments $a$, $k$, and $b$.

**Proof of monotonicity:**
- *In $a$*: For $k=1$, $a^b$ is increasing in $a$. For higher $k$, use the recur
sion $a \uparrow^{k+1} b = a \uparrow^k(a \uparrow^{k+1}(b{-}1))$ inductively.
- *In $b$*: $a \uparrow^{k+1} b = a \uparrow^k(a \uparrow^{k+1}(b{-}1))$, and $a
 \uparrow^{k+1}(b{-}1)$ is increasing in $b$ by induction on $b$.
- *In $k$*: For $a \geq 3, b \geq 2$: $a \uparrow^{k+1} b = a \uparrow^k(a \upar
row^{k+1}(b{-}1))$, where $a \uparrow^{k+1}(b{-}1) \geq a \uparrow^{k+1} 1 = a \
geq 3 > b$ in our range, so $a \uparrow^k(\text{large}) > a \uparrow^k b$.

## Proof that $H_n > G_n$ for all $n \geq 1$

**Base case ($n=1$):** Compare $H_1 = 4 \uparrow^4 4$ with $G_1 = 3 \uparrow^4 3
$.

Since $4 > 3$ (base) and $4 > 3$ (argument), with the same number of arrows $k=4
$, strict monotonicity in $a$ and $b$ gives:
$$H_1 = 4 \uparrow^4 4 > 3 \uparrow^4 3 = G_1. \quad \checkmark$$

**Inductive step:** Assume $H_{n-1} > G_{n-1}$ for some $n \geq 2$. Then:
$$H_n = 4 \uparrow^{H_{n-1}} 4, \qquad G_n = 3 \uparrow^{G_{n-1}} 3.$$

We compare all three arguments:
- **Base:** $4 > 3$ ✓
- **Number of arrows:** $H_{n-1} > G_{n-1}$ (by induction hypothesis) ✓
- **Argument:** $4 > 3$ ✓

Since $a \uparrow^k b$ is strictly increasing in all three arguments simultaneou
sly:
$$H_n = 4 \uparrow^{H_{n-1}} 4 > 3 \uparrow^{G_{n-1}} 3 = G_n. \quad \checkmark$
$

By induction, $H_n > G_n$ for **all** $n \geq 1$.

## Conclusion

Since $H_n > G_n$ holds starting from $n = 1$ (and indeed for every $n$), the sm
allest $n$ such that $H_n > G_n$ is:

$$\boxed{n = 1}$$

The key insight is that $H$ dominates $G$ in **every** component of the up-arrow
