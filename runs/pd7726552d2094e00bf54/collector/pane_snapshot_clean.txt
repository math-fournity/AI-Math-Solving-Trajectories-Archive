## Solution

**Model.** There are 4 outlets A, B, C, D, each with 2 sockets (8 sockets total)
. Three wires, each with 2 ends, give 6 plug-ends placed into 6 distinct sockets
 chosen from 8. Each wire connects the two outlets owning its end-sockets. This
yields a multigraph on vertices {A, B, C, D} with 3 edges (self-loops possible i
f both ends land in the same outlet's two sockets; parallel edges possible if tw
o wires connect the same outlet pair).

**Short circuit = cycle in the multigraph.** The example A₁↔B₁, B₂↔C₁, A₂↔C₂ giv
es edges A–B, B–C, A–C (a triangle = cycle). The example A₁↔A₂ is a self-loop (c
ycle of length 1). A single edge A₁↔B₁ has no cycle. ✓

**No short circuit ⟺ the multigraph is a forest.** A forest on 4 vertices with 3
 edges has exactly 4 − 3 = 1 connected component, so it must be a **spanning tre
e** on all 4 vertices (no isolated vertices, since 3 edges on ≤3 vertices always
 create a cycle).

**Feasibility constraint.** A spanning tree on 4 vertices has 3 edges. The degre
e of each vertex equals the number of sockets used at that outlet, which cannot
exceed 2. The two possible degree sequences for spanning trees on 4 vertices are
:
- (3, 1, 1, 1) — star: the center needs 3 sockets, but only 2 are available. **I
nfeasible.**
- (1, 1, 2, 2) — path: max degree 2. **Feasible.**

By Cayley's formula, there are 4² = 16 spanning trees on 4 labeled vertices: 4 s
tars + 12 paths. Only the **12 path-trees** are feasible.

**Counting (distinguishable plug-ends model).** Total assignments: P(8,6) = 8·7·
6·5·4·3 = **20160**.

For each path tree (e.g., A–B–C–D with edges AB, BC, CD):
- **Socket set:** choose 1 of 2 A-sockets and 1 of 2 D-sockets (B and C use both
 sockets): 2 × 2 = 4 choices.
- **Assign wires to edges:** 3! = 6 ways.
- **Edge AB:** choose which B-socket (2) × assign the wire's two ends to the A-s
ocket and B-socket (2) = 4.
- **Edge BC:** choose which C-socket (2) × assign ends to remaining B-socket and
 C-socket (2) = 4.
- **Edge CD:** assign ends to remaining C-socket and D-socket (2) = 2.
- Per socket set: 6 × 4 × 4 × 2 = 192.
- Per path: 4 × 192 = 768.

Total no-short-circuit assignments: 12 × 768 = **9216**.

**Probability.**

$$P(\text{no short circuit}) = \frac{9216}{20160} = \frac{2^{10} \cdot 3^2}{2^6
\cdot 3^2 \cdot 5 \cdot 7} = \frac{16}{35}$$

$$P(\text{short circuit}) = 1 - \frac{16}{35} = \frac{19}{35}$$

Since 19 is prime and does not divide 35, the fraction 19/35 is irreducible. Thu
s a = 19, b = 35.

$$a + b = 19 + 35 = \boxed{54}$$
