## Proof

**Condition.** The kernel $\ker f$ and the image $\operatorname{im} f$ are vecto
r bundles over $X$ provided that $f$ has **locally constant rank**, i.e. the fun
ction
$$
x \;\longmapsto\; \operatorname{rank} f_x : E_x \to F_x
$$
is locally constant on $X$ (equivalently, constant on every connected component
of $X$).

---

### Why the condition is sufficient

Fix a point $x_0 \in X$ and let $r = \operatorname{rank} f_{x_0}$. By local cons
tancy there is an open neighborhood $U$ of $x_0$ on which $\operatorname{rank} f
_x = r$ for all $x \in U$.

Choose local trivializations of $E|_U \cong U \times \mathbb{R}^m$ and $F|_U \co
ng U \times \mathbb{R}^n$. In these trivializations $f$ is represented by a cont
inuous (smooth/holomorphic, depending on the category) matrix-valued map $A: U \
to \operatorname{Mat}_{n \times m}(\mathbb{R})$ with $\operatorname{rank} A(x) =
 r$ everywhere on $U$.

At $x_0$ some $r \times r$ minor of $A(x_0)$ is nonzero. By continuity that same
 minor remains nonzero on a (possibly smaller) neighborhood $V \subseteq U$ of $
x_0$. After permuting coordinates in source and target (which is just a change o
f trivialization), we may assume this is the leading principal minor, so
$$
A(x) = \begin{pmatrix} B(x) & C(x) \\ D(x) & E(x) \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B(x) \in
 \mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb{R}), \quad x \in V.
$$

Since $B(x)$ is invertible, a column-reduction (left-multiplication by the inver
tible matrix $\begin{pmatrix} B^{-1} & 0 \\ -DB^{-1} & I \end{pmatrix}$, which d
epends continuously on $x$) brings $A(x)$ to the form
$$
\begin{pmatrix} I_r & B^{-1}C \\ 0 & E - DB^{-1}C \end{pmatrix}.
$$
The rank being exactly $r$ forces the lower-right block $E - DB^{-1}C$ to vanish
 identically on $V$. Hence, after a continuous change of trivialization of $F|_V
$,
$$
A(x) \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} I_r & * \end{pmatrix},
$$
i.e. $f|_V$ is, in suitable trivializations, the projection onto the first $r$ c
oordinates followed by an inclusion $\mathbb{R}^r \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}^n$.

From this normal form we read off:

- $\ker f|_V \cong V \times \mathbb{R}^{m-r}$, the subbundle spanned by the last
 $m-r$ coordinate vectors. So $\ker f$ is locally trivial of rank $m - r$.
- $\operatorname{im} f|_V \cong V \times \mathbb{R}^{r}$, the subbundle spanned
by the first $r$ coordinate vectors of $F$. So $\operatorname{im} f$ is locally
trivial of rank $r$.

Both transition functions are inherited from those of $E$ and $F$ (conjugated by
 the continuous changes of trivialization above), so they satisfy the cocycle co
ndition. Hence $\ker f$ and $\operatorname{im} f$ are genuine vector bundles ove
r $X$.

---

### Why the condition is necessary

We show that if $\ker f$ and $\operatorname{im} f$ are vector bundles, then $\op
eratorname{rank} f_x$ must be locally constant.

Indeed, if $\ker f$ is a vector bundle, its rank $x \mapsto \dim \ker f_x$ is a
locally constant function (the rank of a vector bundle is locally constant by de
finition of local triviality). Likewise, if $\operatorname{im} f$ is a vector bu
ndle, $x \mapsto \dim \operatorname{im} f_x$ is locally constant. But for every
$x$,
$$
\operatorname{rank} f_x \;=\; \dim \operatorname{im} f_x \;=\; \dim E_x - \dim \
ker f_x,
$$
which is the difference of two locally constant functions, hence locally constan
t.

Concretely, without the condition the dimensions of fiber of $\ker f$ (or $\oper
atorname{im} f$) jump, and a family of subspaces whose dimension jumps cannot be
 locally trivial: a vector bundle must have a well-defined, locally constant ran
k, so any jump in fiber dimension violates local triviality at the jumping point
.

**Example illustrating necessity.** Let $X = \mathbb{R}$, $E = F = \underline{\m
athbb{R}}^1$ the trivial line bundle, and define $f: E \to F$ by $f(x, v) = (x,
x\,v)$, i.e. $f_x = $ multiplication by $x$. Then
$$
\ker f_x = \begin{cases} 0 & x \neq 0, \\ \mathbb{R} & x = 0, \end{cases}
\qquad
\operatorname{im} f_x = \begin{cases} \mathbb{R} & x \neq 0, \\ 0 & x = 0. \end{
cases}
$$
The rank of $f_x$ is $1$ for $x \neq 0$ and $0$ for $x = 0$, so it is **not** lo
cally constant at $0$. Correspondingly $\ker f$ has fiber dimension $0$ away fro
m the origin and $1$ at the origin, so it cannot be a vector bundle (it fails lo
cal triviality at $0$); likewise $\operatorname{im} f$ has fiber dimension $1$ a
way from $0$ and $0$ at $0$, again not a vector bundle. This confirms that const
ant rank is genuinely necessary.

---

**Conclusion.** The kernel $\ker f$ and image $\operatorname{im} f$ of a morphis
m $f: E \to F$ of vector bundles over $X$ are themselves vector bundles if and o
nly if $f$ has locally constant rank. The condition is necessary because a vecto
r bundle has locally constant fiber dimension, and $\dim \ker f_x = \dim E_x - \
operatorname{rank} f_x$, $\dim \operatorname{im} f_x = \operatorname{rank} f_x$
must each be locally constant; equivalently, any jump in the rank of $f_x$ produ
ces a jump in the fiber dimension of $\ker f$ or $\operatorname{im} f$, destroyi
ng local triviality.
