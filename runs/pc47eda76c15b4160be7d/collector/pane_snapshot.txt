## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. There exists a finite group $G$ and a non-zero $x \in \mathbb{C}
(G)$ with $x^2 = 0$.

**Key tool — Wedderburn's theorem for complex group algebras.** By Maschke's the
orem, $\mathbb{C}(G)$ is semisimple. Since $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraically closed,
 Wedderburn's structure theorem gives an algebra isomorphism

$$\mathbb{C}(G) \;\cong\; \bigoplus_{\rho \in \widehat{G}} M_{\dim \rho}(\mathbb
{C}),$$

where $\widehat{G}$ is the set of (isomorphism classes of) irreducible complex r
epresentations of $G$, and $\dim \rho$ is the dimension of $\rho$. The isomorphi
sm sends $g \mapsto \bigoplus_\rho \rho(g)$.

**Sufficient condition.** If $G$ has an irreducible representation $\rho$ with $
\dim \rho \geq 2$, then the summand $M_{\dim \rho}(\mathbb{C})$ contains non-zer
o nilpotent elements. For instance, the matrix unit $E_{12}$ (1 in position $(1,
2)$, 0 elsewhere) satisfies $E_{12}^2 = 0$. Pulling $E_{12}$ back through the is
omorphism (placing 0 in all other summands) yields a non-zero $x \in \mathbb{C}(
G)$ with $x^2 = 0$.

**Concrete example: $G = S_3$.** The symmetric group $S_3$ has three irreducible
 complex representations:

| Representation | Dimension |
|---|---|
| Trivial | 1 |
| Sign | 1 |
| Standard | 2 |

Hence

$$\mathbb{C}(S_3) \;\cong\; \mathbb{C} \oplus \mathbb{C} \oplus M_2(\mathbb{C}).
$$

The matrix $E_{12} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \in M_2(\mathb
b{C})$ is non-zero and satisfies $E_{12}^2 = 0$. Under the isomorphism, the elem
ent

$$x \;=\; \big(0,\; 0,\; E_{12}\big) \;\in\; \mathbb{C} \oplus \mathbb{C} \oplus
 M_2(\mathbb{C})$$

corresponds to a non-zero element of $\mathbb{C}(S_3)$, and $x^2 = (0, 0, E_{12}
^2) = (0, 0, 0) = 0$.

**Explicit element in $\mathbb{C}(S_3)$.** For completeness, we can write $x$ ex
plicitly. Label $S_3 = \{e, (12), (13), (23), (123), (132)\}$. The standard 2-di
mensional representation $\rho$ is given by the action of $S_3$ on the subspace
$\{(a,b,c) \in \mathbb{C}^3 : a+b+c=0\}$. One computes:

$$\rho(e) = I, \quad \rho(12) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \q
uad \rho(123) = \begin{pmatrix} \omega & 0 \\ 0 & \omega^2 \end{pmatrix},$$

(where $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$), and similarly for the other elements. The isomo
rphism $\Phi: \mathbb{C}(S_3) \to \mathbb{C} \oplus \mathbb{C} \oplus M_2(\mathb
b{C})$ is $\Phi(g) = (\chi_{\text{triv}}(g),\; \chi_{\text{sign}}(g),\; \rho(g))
$. To find $x$ with $\Phi(x) = (0, 0, E_{12})$, we invert $\Phi$ using the ortho
gonality of matrix coefficients:

$$x = \frac{\dim \rho}{|G|} \sum_{g \in S_3} \overline{\rho(g)_{21}} \cdot g = \
frac{2}{6} \sum_{g \in S_3} \overline{\rho(g)_{21}} \cdot g = \frac{1}{3}\sum_{g
 \in S_3} \overline{\rho(g)_{21}} \cdot g.$$

This is a well-defined, non-zero element of $\mathbb{C}(S_3)$ (since $E_{12} \ne
q 0$), and $\Phi(x^2) = (0,0,E_{12}^2) = 0$, so $x^2 = 0$.

**Conclusion.** Taking $G = S_3$ and $x$ as above (the preimage of $(0, 0, E_{12
})$ under the Wedderburn isomorphism), we obtain a non-zero $x \in \mathbb{C}(S_
3)$ with $x^2 = 0$. $\blacksquare$
