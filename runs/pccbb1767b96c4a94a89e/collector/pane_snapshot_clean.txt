## Proof: Laurent Series Expansion in an Annulus

**Theorem.** Let $V = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : r < |z-a| < R\}$ be an annulus (with
$0 \le r < R \le \infty$), and let $f$ be holomorphic in $V$. Then there exist u
nique coefficients $\{c_n\}_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}$ such that

$$f(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n (z-a)^n$$

with the series converging absolutely and locally uniformly in $V$. The coeffici
ents are given by

$$c_n = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_{|\zeta - a| = \rho} \frac{f(\zeta)}{(\zeta - a)^
{n+1}}\, d\zeta, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z},$$

for any $\rho$ with $r < \rho < R$.

---

### Step 1. Setup and the key decomposition

Fix a point $z \in V$, so $r < |z-a| < R$. Choose two radii $r_1, r_2$ with

$$r < r_1 < |z - a| < r_2 < R.$$

Let $\gamma_1$ be the circle $|\zeta - a| = r_1$ and $\gamma_2$ the circle $|\ze
ta - a| = r_2$, both traversed **counterclockwise** (positively oriented).

The annular region $A = \{r_1 < |\zeta - a| < r_2\}$ is a bounded domain whose b
oundary consists of $\gamma_2$ (positively oriented) and $\gamma_1$ (negatively
oriented, i.e., clockwise). Since $f$ is holomorphic on $A$ and continuous on $\
overline{A}$ (indeed holomorphic on a neighborhood of $\overline{A}$), we apply
**Cauchy's integral formula for doubly-connected domains**:

$$f(z) = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_{\gamma_2} \frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta - z}\, d\zeta -
 \frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_{\gamma_1} \frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta - z}\, d\zeta. \tag{1}$
$

(The minus sign on $\gamma_1$ accounts for the fact that the inner boundary is t
raversed clockwise to give the positive orientation of the annulus.)

---

### Step 2. Expand the outer integral (non-negative powers)

For $\zeta \in \gamma_2$ we have $|\zeta - a| = r_2 > |z - a|$, so

$$\frac{1}{\zeta - z} = \frac{1}{(\zeta - a) - (z - a)} = \frac{1}{\zeta - a} \c
dot \frac{1}{1 - \frac{z-a}{\zeta - a}}.$$

Since $\left|\frac{z-a}{\zeta - a}\right| = \frac{|z-a|}{r_2} < 1$, we use the g
eometric series $\frac{1}{1-w} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} w^n$ for $|w|<1$:

$$\frac{1}{\zeta - z} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(z-a)^n}{(\zeta - a)^{n+1}}. \
tag{2}$$

This series converges **uniformly** for $\zeta \in \gamma_2$ (since the ratio $|
z-a|/r_2 < 1$ is bounded away from $1$). Substituting into the first integral of
 (1) and interchanging sum and integral (justified by uniform convergence):

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_{\gamma_2} \frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta - z}\, d\zeta = \sum_{
n=0}^{\infty} \left[\frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_{\gamma_2} \frac{f(\zeta)}{(\zeta - a
)^{n+1}}\, d\zeta\right] (z-a)^n. \tag{3}$$

This gives the **non-negative power** part of the expansion.

---

### Step 3. Expand the inner integral (negative powers)

For $\zeta \in \gamma_1$ we have $|\zeta - a| = r_1 < |z - a|$, so we factor dif
ferently:

$$\frac{1}{\zeta - z} = \frac{1}{(\zeta - a) - (z - a)} = -\frac{1}{(z-a)} \cdot
 \frac{1}{1 - \frac{\zeta - a}{z - a}}.$$

Since $\left|\frac{\zeta - a}{z - a}\right| = \frac{r_1}{|z-a|} < 1$, the geomet
ric series gives:

$$\frac{1}{\zeta - z} = -\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(\zeta - a)^n}{(z-a)^{n+1}} =
 -\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(\zeta - a)^{n-1}}{(z-a)^{n}}.$$

Re-indexing with $m = -n$ (i.e., $n = -m$, $m = -1, -2, \ldots$):

$$\frac{1}{\zeta - z} = -\sum_{m=-\infty}^{-1} \frac{(z-a)^m}{(\zeta - a)^{m+1}}
. \tag{4}$$

Again, convergence is uniform on $\gamma_1$. Substituting into the second integr
al of (1):

$$-\frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_{\gamma_1} \frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta - z}\, d\zeta = \sum_
{m=-\infty}^{-1} \left[\frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_{\gamma_1} \frac{f(\zeta)}{(\zeta
- a)^{m+1}}\, d\zeta\right] (z-a)^m. \tag{5}$$

This gives the **negative power** part.

---

### Step 4. Combine the two parts

Adding (3) and (5), and writing $n$ for the unified index:

$$f(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{-1} c_n (z-a)^n + \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n (z-a)^n = \
sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n (z-a)^n, \tag{6}$$

where for $n \ge 0$ the coefficient comes from $\gamma_2$ and for $n \le -1$ fro
m $\gamma_1$:

$$c_n = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_{\gamma_2} \frac{f(\zeta)}{(\zeta-a)^{n+1}}\, d\z
eta \quad (n \ge 0), \qquad c_n = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_{\gamma_1} \frac{f(\zet
a)}{(\zeta-a)^{n+1}}\, d\zeta \quad (n \le -1). \tag{7}$$

---

### Step 5. The coefficient is independent of the chosen circle

We now show that for **any** $\rho$ with $r < \rho < R$, the integral

$$c_n = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_{|\zeta - a| = \rho} \frac{f(\zeta)}{(\zeta - a)^
{n+1}}\, d\zeta \tag{8}$$

gives the same value, so the two formulas in (7) are unified into a single one.

**Case $n \ge 0$:** The function $g(\zeta) = \frac{f(\zeta)}{(\zeta-a)^{n+1}}$ i
s holomorphic in $V$ (the denominator $(\zeta-a)^{n+1}$ is nonzero in $V$ since
$r \ge 0$ and $V$ excludes $\zeta = a$ when $r \ge 0$; if $r = 0$ the point $a$
is excluded from $V$ as well). For any two circles $\gamma_\rho$ and $\gamma_{\r
ho'}$ with $r < \rho < \rho' < R$, the region between them lies in $V$ where $g$
 is holomorphic. By **Cauchy's theorem for doubly-connected domains**:

$$\oint_{\gamma_{\rho'}} g(\zeta)\, d\zeta = \oint_{\gamma_\rho} g(\zeta)\, d\ze
ta.$$

So $c_n$ is the same for all $\rho \in (r, R)$.

**Case $n \le -1$:** Write $n = -m$ with $m \ge 1$. Then

$$\frac{f(\zeta)}{(\zeta - a)^{n+1}} = f(\zeta) \cdot (\zeta - a)^{m - 1}.$$

Since $m - 1 \ge 0$, this is $f(\zeta)$ times a polynomial in $(\zeta - a)$, whi
ch is holomorphic in $V$. The same argument as above applies: the integral over
any circle $|\zeta - a| = \rho$ with $r < \rho < R$ gives the same value.

Thus in **both** cases, formula (8) holds for every $\rho \in (r, R)$, and the s
plit in (7) is unified. $\square$

---

### Step 6. Convergence of the full Laurent series

We must verify that the two-sided series (6) converges (absolutely and locally u
niformly) in all of $V$.

Fix any **compact** $K \subset V$. Then there exist $r', R'$ with $r < r' < R' <
 R$ such that $K \subset \{r' \le |z-a| \le R'\}$. Choose $\rho_1, \rho_2$ with
$r < \rho_1 < r'$ and $R' < \rho_2 < R$.

**Estimate for $n \ge 0$:** From (7) and the standard ML-estimate on $|\zeta - a
| = \rho_2$:

$$|c_n| \le \frac{1}{2\pi} \cdot \frac{M_2}{\rho_2^{n+1}} \cdot 2\pi \rho_2 = \f
rac{M_2}{\rho_2^n}, \qquad M_2 = \max_{|\zeta-a|=\rho_2} |f(\zeta)|.$$

For $z \in K$, $|z - a| \le R' < \rho_2$, so

$$|c_n (z-a)^n| \le M_2 \left(\frac{R'}{\rho_2}\right)^n, \qquad n \ge 0.$$

Since $R'/\rho_2 < 1$, the non-negative part $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n (z-a)^n$ c
onverges absolutely and uniformly on $K$ by the Weierstrass M-test.

**Estimate for $n \le -1$:** Write $n = -m$, $m \ge 1$. From (7) and the ML-esti
mate on $|\zeta - a| = \rho_1$:

$$|c_{-m}| \le \frac{1}{2\pi} \cdot \frac{M_1}{\rho_1^{-m+1}} \cdot 2\pi \rho_1
= M_1 \rho_1^{m}, \qquad M_1 = \max_{|\zeta-a|=\rho_1} |f(\zeta)|.$$

For $z \in K$, $|z - a| \ge r' > \rho_1$, so

$$|c_{-m}(z-a)^{-m}| \le M_1 \left(\frac{\rho_1}{r'}\right)^m, \qquad m \ge 1.$$

Since $\rho_1/r' < 1$, the negative part $\sum_{m=1}^{\infty} c_{-m}(z-a)^{-m}$
converges absolutely and uniformly on $K$ by the Weierstrass M-test.

Combining both parts, the full Laurent series $\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n (z-
a)^n$ converges **absolutely** and **uniformly on every compact subset of $V$**
(i.e., locally uniformly in $V$). $\square$

---

### Step 7. Uniqueness of the coefficients

Suppose $f(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n (z-a)^n = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty
} c_n' (z-a)^n$ are two Laurent expansions converging in $V$. Fix any $\rho \in
(r, R)$ and integrate both sides against $(z-a)^{-k-1}$ over the circle $|z - a|
 = \rho$:

$$\oint_{|z-a|=\rho} f(z)(z-a)^{-k-1}\, dz = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n \oint
_{|z-a|=\rho} (z-a)^{n-k-1}\, dz.$$

By uniform convergence on the circle, we may interchange sum and integral. The i
ntegral

$$\oint_{|z-a|=\rho} (z-a)^{n-k-1}\, dz = \begin{cases} 2\pi i & \text{if } n -
k - 1 = -1, \text{ i.e., } n = k, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$$

(This is the standard orthogonality of monomials on a circle: $\oint (z-a)^m\, d
z = 2\pi i\, \delta_{m,-1}$.) Therefore only the $n = k$ term survives:

$$\oint_{|z-a|=\rho} f(z)(z-a)^{-k-1}\, dz = 2\pi i\, c_k.$$

The same holds for $c_k'$, so $c_k = c_k'$ for every $k \in \mathbb{Z}$. The Lau
rent expansion is **unique**. $\square$

### Summary

| Component | Result |
|---|---|
| **Existence** | Cauchy's integral formula on the annulus + geometric series ex
pansion of $\frac{1}{\zeta - z}$ (split into $|z-a|<|\zeta-a|$ and $|z-a|>|\zeta
-a|$ cases) yields the two-sided series. |
| **Convergence** | ML-estimates on inner/outer circles + Weierstrass M-test giv
e absolute, locally uniform convergence in $V$. |
| **Coefficient formula** | $c_n = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_{|\zeta-a|=\rho} \frac
{f(\zeta)}{(\zeta-a)^{n+1}}\, d\zeta$, valid for **any** $\rho \in (r,R)$ and al
l $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ (independence of $\rho$ follows from Cauchy's theorem, sinc
e the integrand is holomorphic in $V$). |
| **Uniqueness** | Orthogonality of monomials on circles ($\oint (z-a)^m\, dz =
2\pi i\,\delta_{m,-1}$) isolates each coefficient. |

The coefficient $c_n$ is precisely the $n$-th Laurent coefficient, generalizing
the Taylor coefficient (the case $r = 0$, where $c_n = 0$ for $n < 0$ by Cauchy'
s theorem, recovering the Taylor series). The negative-index coefficients $c_{-1
}, c_{-2}, \ldots$ encode the "singularity" of $f$ at the inner boundary and are
 absent when $f$ extends holomorphically to the full disk $|z-a| < R$.
