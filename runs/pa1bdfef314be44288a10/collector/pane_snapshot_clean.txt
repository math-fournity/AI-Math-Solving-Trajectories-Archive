## Construction

**Claim:** Yes, such a function exists. Define

$$f(x) = \sin\!\bigl(\sqrt{x}\bigr), \qquad x \in (0,\infty).$$

We verify the four required properties.

### 1. Boundedness

Since $|\sin(t)| \le 1$ for all real $t$, we have $|f(x)| = |\sin(\sqrt{x})| \le
 1$ for all $x \in (0,\infty)$. Hence $f$ is bounded.

### 2. Differentiability on $(0,\infty)$

The map $x \mapsto \sqrt{x}$ is differentiable on $(0,\infty)$ (with derivative
$\frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$), and $\sin$ is differentiable everywhere. By the chain ru
le, $f$ is differentiable on $(0,\infty)$ with

$$f'(x) = \frac{\cos(\sqrt{x})}{2\sqrt{x}}.$$

### 3. The derivative tends to zero as $x \to \infty$

For $x > 0$,

$$|f'(x)| = \frac{|\cos(\sqrt{x})|}{2\sqrt{x}} \le \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}.$$

As $x \to \infty$, the right-hand side $\frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}} \to 0$, so by the sq
ueeze theorem,

$$\lim_{x \to \infty} f'(x) = 0.$$

### 4. $f$ does not converge to a limit as $x \to \infty$

We exhibit two sequences tending to infinity along which $f$ has different limit
s. Set

$$a_n = \Bigl(2n\pi + \tfrac{\pi}{2}\Bigr)^2, \qquad b_n = \Bigl(2n\pi - \tfrac{
\pi}{2}\Bigr)^2, \qquad n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$$

Both $a_n \to \infty$ and $b_n \to \infty$. Now

$$f(a_n) = \sin\!\Bigl(\sqrt{\bigl(2n\pi + \tfrac{\pi}{2}\bigr)^2}\Bigr) = \sin\
!\Bigl(2n\pi + \tfrac{\pi}{2}\Bigr) = 1,$$

$$f(b_n) = \sin\!\Bigl(\sqrt{\bigl(2n\pi - \tfrac{\pi}{2}\bigr)^2}\Bigr) = \sin\
!\Bigl(2n\pi - \tfrac{\pi}{2}\Bigr) = -1.$$

(Here $\sqrt{(2n\pi \pm \pi/2)^2} = 2n\pi \pm \pi/2$ since $2n\pi \pm \pi/2 > 0$
 for $n \ge 1$.)

Thus $f(a_n) \to 1$ and $f(b_n) \to -1$ along two sequences both tending to $+\i
nfty$. If $\lim_{x\to\infty} f(x)$ existed, all subsequential limits would coinc
ide; since $1 \ne -1$, no limit exists.

---

## Conclusion

The function $f(x) = \sin(\sqrt{x})$ is bounded, differentiable on $(0,\infty)$,
 satisfies $\lim_{x\to\infty} f'(x) = 0$, and yet does not converge as $x \to \i
nfty$. Therefore such a function **can** be constructed.
