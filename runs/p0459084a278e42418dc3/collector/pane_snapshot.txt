## Proof

Place the unit cube as $C = [0,1]^3$ with the space diagonal along the vector $(
1,1,1)$, connecting vertices $O=(0,0,0)$ and $P=(1,1,1)$.

**Rotation matrix.** By Rodrigues' formula with $\cos(\pi/3)=1/2$, $\sin(\pi/3)=
\sqrt{3}/2$, and axis $\hat{u}=(1,1,1)/\sqrt{3}$:

$$R = \begin{pmatrix} 2/3 & -1/3 & 2/3 \\ 2/3 & 2/3 & -1/3 \\ -1/3 & 2/3 & 2/3 \
end{pmatrix}$$

One verifies $R$ is orthogonal and $R(1,1,1)^T = (1,1,1)^T$ (axis fixed).

**Intersection constraints.** A point $y=(a,b,c) \in C \cap C'$ iff $y \in [0,1]
^3$ and $R^{-1}y = R^T y \in [0,1]^3$. Computing:

$$R^T y = \tfrac{1}{3}\big(2a+2b-c,\;\;{-a+2b+2c},\;\;{2a-b+2c}\big)$$

Requiring each component in $[0,1]$ yields six inequalities:

| Constraint | Cuts off vertex |
|---|---|
| $c \le 2a+2b$ | $(0,0,1)$ |
| $a \le 2b+2c$ | $(1,0,0)$ |
| $b \le 2a+2c$ | $(0,1,0)$ |
| $c \ge 2a+2b-3$ | $(1,1,0)$ |
| $a \ge 2b+2c-3$ | $(0,1,1)$ |
| $b \ge 2a+2c-3$ | $(1,0,1)$ |

The two axis-vertices $O$ and $P$ satisfy all constraints (they are fixed by the
 rotation), so they remain. The other six vertices each violate exactly one cons
traint.

**Each cut is a tetrahedron of volume $1/24$.** Consider the region violating $a
 \le 2b+2c$ (near $(1,0,0)$). The plane $a = 2b+2c$ passes through $(0,0,0)$ and
 intersects the cube faces $c=0$ and $b=0$ along $a=2b$ and $a=2c$ respectively,
 meeting face $a=1$ at $b+c=\tfrac12$. The cut-off tetrahedron is:

$$T_1 = \operatorname{conv}\!\left\{(0,0,0),\;(1,0,0),\;(1,\tfrac12,0),\;(1,0,\t
frac12)\right\}$$

$$\operatorname{Vol}(T_1) = \frac{1}{6}\left|\det\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\0&\frac12
&0\\0&0&\frac12\end{pmatrix}\right| = \frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{24}
$$

One checks all other constraints are satisfied throughout $T_1$, so it is exactl
y the removed region near $(1,0,0)$.

**All six tetrahedra are congruent.** The cube has 3-fold cyclic symmetry about
its diagonal (permuting coordinates), so the three "lower" cuts (near $(1,0,0)$,
 $(0,1,0)$, $(0,0,1)$) are congruent. Since the cube is invariant under rotation
 by $2\pi/3$ about this diagonal, angles $\theta$ and $\theta - 2\pi/3$ produce
the same cube; thus $\pi/3 \equiv -\pi/3 \pmod{2\pi/3}$, making the configuratio
n self-conjugate. This maps each lower cut to a corresponding upper cut, so all
six have volume $1/24$.

**The six tetrahedra are pairwise disjoint** (up to measure-zero boundaries). Fo
r any two cuts, their defining strict inequalities are mutually contradictory: e
.g., $a > 2b+2c$ and $b > 2a+2c$ together imply $a > 2b+2c > 4a+4c \ge 4a$, impo
ssible for $a>0$. Similar contradictions hold for every pair (verified for all 1
5 pairs by the same algebraic argument).

**Final computation.**

$$\operatorname{Vol}(C \cap C') = 1 - 6 \cdot \frac{1}{24} = 1 - \frac{1}{4} = \
boxed{\dfrac{3}{4}}$$
