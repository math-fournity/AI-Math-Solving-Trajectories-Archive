## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $M$ is uniformly integrable, i.e.,
$$\lim_{K \to \infty} \sup_{t \in \mathbb{R}^+} \mathbb{E}\!\left[M_t^2 \,\mathb
f{1}_{\{M_t^2 > K\}}\right] = 0.$$

---

**Step 1 — $L^2$ martingale convergence.** Since $M$ is a martingale with $\sup_
{t \geq 0} \mathbb{E}[M_t^2] < \infty$, Doob's $L^2$ martingale convergence theo
rem guarantees the existence of $M_\infty \in L^2$ such that $M_t \to M_\infty$
almost surely and in $L^2$.

**Step 2 — $\{M_t\}$ is UI and $M_t = \mathbb{E}[M_\infty \mid \mathcal{F}_t]$.*
* The bound $\sup_t \mathbb{E}[M_t^2] < \infty$ means the family $\{M_t\}$ is bo
unded in $L^2$. By the de la Vallée-Poussin theorem (applied with $G(x) = x^2$,
for which $G(x)/x \to \infty$), $\{M_t\}$ is uniformly integrable. Together with
 a.s. convergence, this yields $M_t \to M_\infty$ in $L^1$, and the martingale p
roperty gives
$$M_t = \mathbb{E}[M_\infty \mid \mathcal{F}_t] \quad \text{for all } t \geq 0.$
$

**Step 3 — Jensen's inequality.** Since $M_t = \mathbb{E}[M_\infty \mid \mathcal
{F}_t]$ and $x \mapsto x^2$ is convex, Jensen's inequality gives
$$M_t^2 = \bigl(\mathbb{E}[M_\infty \mid \mathcal{F}_t]\bigr)^2 \leq \mathbb{E}[
M_\infty^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_t].$$

**Step 4 — Bounding the tail expectation.** Set $C := \sup_{t \geq 0} \mathbb{E}
[M_t^2] < \infty$. For any $K > 0$ and any $t \geq 0$, using the above and the t
ower property (since $\mathbf{1}_{\{M_t^2 > K\}}$ is $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable)
:
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[M_t^2 \,\mathbf{1}_{\{M_t^2 > K\}}\right] \leq \mathbb{E}\!\
left[\mathbb{E}[M_\infty^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_t]\,\mathbf{1}_{\{M_t^2 > K\}}\right
] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[M_\infty^2 \,\mathbf{1}_{\{M_t^2 > K\}}\right].$$

**Step 5 — Splitting.** For any $L > 0$:
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[M_\infty^2 \,\mathbf{1}_{\{M_t^2 > K\}}\right] \leq \mathbb{
E}\!\left[M_\infty^2 \,\mathbf{1}_{\{M_\infty^2 > L\}}\right] + L\,\mathbb{P}(M_
t^2 > K).$$
By Markov's inequality, $\mathbb{P}(M_t^2 > K) \leq \mathbb{E}[M_t^2]/K \leq C/K
$. Hence, uniformly over $t$:
$$\sup_{t \geq 0}\, \mathbb{E}\!\left[M_t^2 \,\mathbf{1}_{\{M_t^2 > K\}}\right]
\leq \mathbb{E}\!\left[M_\infty^2 \,\mathbf{1}_{\{M_\infty^2 > L\}}\right] + \fr
ac{LC}{K}.$$

**Step 6 — Conclusion.** Let $\varepsilon > 0$. Since $M_\infty^2 \in L^1$, choo
se $L$ large enough that $\mathbb{E}[M_\infty^2 \,\mathbf{1}_{\{M_\infty^2 > L\}
}] < \varepsilon/2$. Then choose $K > 2LC/\varepsilon$. For every $t \geq 0$:
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[M_t^2 \,\mathbf{1}_{\{M_t^2 > K\}}\right] < \frac{\varepsilo
n}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.$$
Taking the supremum over $t$ and noting $\varepsilon$ was arbitrary:
$$\lim_{K \to \infty} \sup_{t \in \mathbb{R}^+} \mathbb{E}\!\left[M_t^2 \,\mathb
f{1}_{\{M_t^2 > K\}}\right] = 0.$$

Therefore $M$ is uniformly integrable. $\blacksquare$
