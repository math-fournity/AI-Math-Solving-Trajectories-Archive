## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, every geodesic $\gamma:[0,1]\to S=\mathbb{H}/\Gamma$ can be lift
ed to a geodesic $\tilde\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb{H}$.

**Setup.** A Fuchsian group $\Gamma$ is a discrete subgroup of $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\
mathbb{R})$, hence it acts on $\mathbb{H}$ by orientation-preserving isometries.
 Discreeness of $\Gamma$ implies the action is *properly discontinuous*; we assu
me the action is free (or we pass to the orbifold/covering interpretation, which
 does not affect the local argument below). Under these hypotheses the quotient
projection
$$\pi:\mathbb{H}\longrightarrow S=\mathbb{H}/\Gamma$$
is a smooth covering map and a **local isometry**: every $z\in\mathbb{H}$ has a
neighborhood $U$ such that $\pi|_U:U\to\pi(U)$ is a diffeomorphism preserving th
e hyperbolic metric, since $\Gamma$ acts by isometries.

**Step 1 — Path lifting.** The interval $[0,1]$ is simply connected and locally
path-connected, so the standard path-lifting theorem for covering maps applies.
Given any choice of basepoint $\tilde p\in\mathbb{H}$ with $\pi(\tilde p)=\gamma
(0)$, there exists a **unique** continuous lift $\tilde\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb{H}
$ satisfying
$$\pi\circ\tilde\gamma=\gamma,\qquad \tilde\gamma(0)=\tilde p.$$

**Step 2 — Smoothness of the lift.** Because $\pi$ is a local diffeomorphism, th
e lift $\tilde\gamma$ is smooth: locally on each small subinterval where $\gamma
$ stays inside an evenly covered chart, $\tilde\gamma$ is obtained by composing
$\gamma$ with a smooth local inverse of $\pi$. Since $[0,1]$ is compact, finitel
y many such charts cover the image, and the local inverses agree on overlaps by
uniqueness of lifts. Hence $\tilde\gamma\in C^\infty([0,1],\mathbb{H})$.

**Step 3 — The lift is a geodesic.** Let $t_0\in[0,1]$. Choose a neighborhood $U
$ of $\tilde\gamma(t_0)$ on which $\pi|_U$ is an isometry onto its image. On a s
mall interval $I\ni t_0$, both $\tilde\gamma(I)\subset U$ and $\gamma(I)\subset\
pi(U)$. A local isometry pulls back the Levi-Civita connection and sends geodesi
cs to geodesics: equivalently, it preserves the covariant derivative $\nabla_{\d
ot{\tilde\gamma}}\dot{\tilde\gamma}$, so
$$\nabla_{\dot{\tilde\gamma}}\dot{\tilde\gamma}\big|_{I} \;=\; (\pi|_U)^*\!\Big(
\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma\Big)\Big|_{I} \;=\; 0,$$
because $\gamma$ is a geodesic in $S$, i.e. $\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma=0$. T
hus $\nabla_{\dot{\tilde\gamma}}\dot{\tilde\gamma}=0$ on $I$. Covering $[0,1]$ b
y finitely many such intervals, we obtain
$$\nabla_{\dot{\tilde\gamma}}\dot{\tilde\gamma}\equiv 0\quad\text{on }[0,1],$$
which is precisely the geodesic equation in $\mathbb{H}$. Therefore $\tilde\gamm
a$ is a geodesic of $\mathbb{H}$.

**Step 4 — Uniqueness up to the deck group.** Different choices of the initial l
ift $\tilde p$ with $\pi(\tilde p)=\gamma(0)$ differ by an element of $\Gamma$ (
the deck group of the cover). Since $\Gamma$ acts by isometries, the resulting l
ifts are all geodesics, related by $\tilde\gamma_g=g\circ\tilde\gamma$ for $g\in
\Gamma$. In particular, for each choice of $\tilde p$ the lift is unique.

**Conclusion.** The projection $\pi:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}/\Gamma$ is a coverin
g map and a local isometry. By the path-lifting theorem, any geodesic $\gamma:[0
,1]\to S$ admits a unique smooth lift $\tilde\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb{H}$ once a b
asepoint over $\gamma(0)$ is chosen; and because $\pi$ is a local isometry, this
 lift satisfies the geodesic equation in $\mathbb{H}$. Hence **yes**, every geod
esic in the quotient lifts to a geodesic in $\mathbb{H}$, uniquely up to the act
ion of $\Gamma$.
