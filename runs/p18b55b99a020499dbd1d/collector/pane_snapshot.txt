## Solution

We evaluate
$$
I(\varepsilon)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{i x^{2} / \varepsilon}\, d x,
\qquad \varepsilon>0.
$$

(The case $\varepsilon<0$ is obtained by complex conjugation at the end.)

### Step 1. Rescale

Set $t = x/\sqrt{\varepsilon}$, so $dx = \sqrt{\varepsilon}\,dt$ and $x^2/\varep
silon = t^2$. Then
$$
I(\varepsilon)=\sqrt{\varepsilon}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{i t^{2}}\,dt.
$$
It remains to compute the Fresnel integral $\displaystyle F:=\int_{-\infty}^{\in
fty} e^{i t^{2}}\,dt$.

### Step 2. Regularize

The integral $F$ is only conditionally convergent (the integrand has modulus $1$
). We define it via the absolutely convergent Gaussian regularization: for $\eta
>0$,
$$
F_\eta := \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{(i-\eta)t^{2}}\,dt,
$$
and set $F := \lim_{\eta\to 0^+} F_\eta$. (One checks that $\lim_{\eta\to0^+}F_\
eta = \lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{-R}^{R}e^{it^2}dt$ by a standard Abel-type argument
; the regularization is consistent with the symmetric improper integral.)

### Step 3. Evaluate $F_\eta$ by completing the square / Gaussian formula

Since $\operatorname{Re}(i-\eta) = -\eta < 0$, the Gaussian integral formula app
lies:
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-a t^{2}}\,dt = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}, \qquad \operato
rname{Re} a > 0,
$$
with the principal branch of the square root. Here $-a = i-\eta$, i.e. $a = \eta
 - i$, and $\operatorname{Re}a = \eta>0$. Hence
$$
F_\eta = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\eta - i}}.
$$

### Step 4. Take $\eta\to 0^+$

Write $\eta - i$ in polar form. For $\eta>0$,
$$
\eta - i = \sqrt{\eta^2+1}\; e^{-i\arctan(1/\eta)},
\qquad \arg(\eta-i) = -\arctan(1/\eta)\in(-\pi/2,0).
$$
Therefore
$$
\frac{1}{\eta-i} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\eta^2+1}}\; e^{i\arctan(1/\eta)},
$$
and
$$
F_\eta = \sqrt{\pi}\;\big(\eta^2+1\big)^{-1/4}\, e^{\,i\,\tfrac12\arctan(1/\eta)
}.
$$
As $\eta\to 0^+$:
- $(\eta^2+1)^{-1/4}\to 1$,
- $\arctan(1/\eta)\to \pi/2$, so $\tfrac12\arctan(1/\eta)\to \pi/4$.

Thus
$$
F = \lim_{\eta\to 0^+} F_\eta = \sqrt{\pi}\, e^{i\pi/4}.
$$

### Step 5. Assemble

$$
I(\varepsilon) = \sqrt{\varepsilon}\,F = \sqrt{\pi\varepsilon}\;e^{i\pi/4}, \qqu
ad \varepsilon>0.
$$

### Step 6. Case $\varepsilon<0$

If $\varepsilon<0$, write $\varepsilon = -|\varepsilon|$. Then $e^{ix^2/\varepsi
lon} = e^{-ix^2/|\varepsilon|} = \overline{e^{ix^2/|\varepsilon|}}$, so
$$
I(\varepsilon) = \overline{I(|\varepsilon|)} = \sqrt{\pi|\varepsilon|}\,e^{-i\pi
/4}.
$$
Equivalently, for real $\varepsilon\neq 0$,
$$
I(\varepsilon) = \sqrt{\pi|\varepsilon|}\,\exp\!\Big(i\,\operatorname{sgn}(\vare
psilon)\,\tfrac{\pi}{4}\Big).
$$

### Verification (sanity checks)

1. **Modulus.** $|I(\varepsilon)| = \sqrt{\pi|\varepsilon|}$, which scales like
$\sqrt{|\varepsilon|}$ — consistent with the substitution $t=x/\sqrt{|\varepsilo
n|}$.
2. **Phase.** For $\varepsilon>0$ the phase is $+\pi/4$; for $\varepsilon<0$ it
is $-\pi/4$ (complex conjugate), matching the sign of the exponent.
3. **Real/imaginary parts** ($\varepsilon>0$): $I = \sqrt{\pi\varepsilon/2}\,(1+
i)$, recovering the classical Fresnel values $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cos t^2\,d
t = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\sin t^2\,dt = \sqrt{\pi/2}$.

### Result

$$
\boxed{\,I(\varepsilon)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{i x^{2} / \varepsilon}\,dx =
\sqrt{\pi\,|\varepsilon|}\;\exp\!\left(\frac{i\pi}{4}\operatorname{sgn}\varepsil
on\right),\qquad \varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}.\,}
$$

In particular, for $\varepsilon>0$: $\;I(\varepsilon)=\sqrt{\pi\varepsilon}\,e^{
i\pi/4}=\sqrt{\tfrac{\pi\varepsilon}{2}}\,(1+i)$.
