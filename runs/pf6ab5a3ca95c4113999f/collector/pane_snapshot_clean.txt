## Proof

We wish to determine the number of solutions of $bz^m = e^z$ in the disk $|z|<2$
, given $|b|>e^2$ and $m\geq 1$ a positive integer.

**Step 1: Apply Rouché's theorem.**

Write $F(z) = bz^m - e^z = f(z) + g(z)$ with $f(z) = bz^m$ and $g(z) = -e^z$.

On the circle $|z|=2$:
- $|f(z)| = |b|\cdot 2^m > e^2 \cdot 2^m$.
- $|g(z)| = |e^z| = e^{\operatorname{Re} z} \leq e^{|z|} = e^2$.

Since $m\geq 1$, we have $2^m \geq 2 > 1$, hence
$$|f(z)| > e^2 \cdot 2^m \geq 2e^2 > e^2 \geq |g(z)| \quad\text{for all } |z|=2.
$$

Thus $|g(z)| < |f(z)|$ strictly on $|z|=2$. By Rouché's theorem, $F(z)=bz^m-e^z$
 has the same number of zeros (counting multiplicity) as $f(z)=bz^m$ in $|z|<2$.
 Since $bz^m$ has a single zero of order $m$ at the origin, $F$ has exactly **$m
$ zeros counting multiplicity** in $|z|<2$.

**Step 2: Show every zero is simple (so the $m$ zeros are distinct).**

A zero $z_0$ of $F$ is multiple only if $F(z_0)=0$ and $F'(z_0)=0$. We have
$$F'(z) = bmz^{m-1} - e^z.$$
At a zero, $e^{z_0}=bz_0^m$, so
$$F'(z_0) = bmz_0^{m-1} - bz_0^m = bz_0^{m-1}(m - z_0).$$

Hence $F'(z_0)=0$ iff $z_0 = 0$ or $z_0 = m$.

- **$z_0 = 0$:** $F(0) = -1 \neq 0$, so $0$ is not a zero of $F$.
- **$z_0 = m$:** $F(m)=0$ requires $bm^m = e^m$, i.e. $|b| = e^m/m^m = (e/m)^m$.
 For $m\geq 1$, $(e/m)^m \leq e < e^2$ (since $e/m \leq e$ for $m\geq 1$, and $(
e/m)^m \leq e^m/m^m$; in fact $(e/1)^1 = e < e^2$, and for $m\geq 2$, $(e/m)^m \
leq (e/2)^2 < e^2$). In all cases $|b| = (e/m)^m < e^2$, contradicting the hypot
hesis $|b|>e^2$.

Therefore no zero of $F$ in $|z|<2$ is multiple; every zero is simple.

**Conclusion.**

$F(z)=bz^m - e^z$ has exactly $m$ zeros in $|z|<2$, each simple. Hence the equat
ion $bz^m = e^z$ has **exactly $m$ solutions** in the disk of radius $2$ centere
d at the origin.
