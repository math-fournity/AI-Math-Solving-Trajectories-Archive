## Proof

**Critical point equations.** The critical points satisfy $x' = 0$ and $y' = 0$
simultaneously:

$$y = p(x), \qquad x = q(y).$$

Substituting the first into the second yields $x = q(p(x))$, i.e.,

$$F(x) := q(p(x)) - x = 0.$$

Each real root $x_0$ of $F$ determines a unique critical point $(x_0,\, p(x_0))$
, and distinct roots give distinct critical points. Thus the number of critical
points equals the number of distinct real roots of $F$.

---

### Upper Bound: at most $n^2$ critical points

Since $p$ has degree $n$ and $q$ has degree $n$, the composition $q \circ p$ has
 degree $n \cdot n = n^2$.

**Case $n \geq 2$:** Then $n^2 \geq 4 > 1$, so the leading term of $q(p(x))$ (de
gree $n^2$) dominates the $-x$ term (degree 1). Hence $F(x) = q(p(x)) - x$ is a
polynomial of degree exactly $n^2$. A polynomial of degree $d$ has at most $d$ r
eal roots, so $F$ has at most $n^2$ real roots, giving at most $n^2$ critical po
ints.

**Case $n = 1$:** Write $p(x) = ax + b$, $q(y) = cy + d$ with $a, c \neq 0$. The
n $F(x) = (ac - 1)x + (bc + d)$, which has degree at most 1. If $ac \neq 1$, $F$
 has exactly one root, giving one critical point. (If $ac = 1$ and $bc + d = 0$,
 then $F \equiv 0$ and every point on the line $y = p(x)$ is critical — a degene
rate case with infinitely many *non-isolated* critical points; the maximum numbe
r of *isolated* critical points is $1 = n^2$.)

---

### Achievability: $n^2$ critical points are attainable

**Case $n = 1$:** Take $p(x) = 2x$, $q(y) = y$. Then $F(x) = 2x - x = x$, which
has root $x = 0$, giving the critical point $(0, 0)$. So $1 = n^2$ is achieved.

**Case $n \geq 2$:** We use **Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind** $T_n$, d
efined by $T_n(\cos\theta) = \cos(n\theta)$. Set

$$p(x) = T_n(x), \qquad q(y) = T_n(y).$$

Both are polynomials of degree $n$. The fundamental composition identity gives:

$$q(p(x)) = T_n(T_n(x)) = T_{n^2}(x),$$

since $T_n(T_n(\cos\theta)) = T_n(\cos(n\theta)) = \cos(n^2\theta) = T_{n^2}(\co
s\theta)$.

Thus $F(x) = T_{n^2}(x) - x$, a polynomial of degree $n^2$ (leading coefficient
$2^{n^2-1} \neq 0$). We show it has exactly $n^2$ distinct real roots, all in $[
-1, 1]$.

**Counting the roots.** Set $x = \cos\theta$, $\theta \in [0, \pi]$ (so $\cos$ i
s injective). The equation $T_{n^2}(x) = x$ becomes $\cos(n^2\theta) = \cos\thet
a$, yielding two families:

- **Family A:** $(n^2 - 1)\theta = 2k\pi$, i.e., $\theta = \frac{2k\pi}{n^2 - 1}
$, for $k = 0, 1, \ldots, \left\lfloor\frac{n^2-1}{2}\right\rfloor$.
- **Family B:** $(n^2 + 1)\theta = 2k\pi$, i.e., $\theta = \frac{2k\pi}{n^2 + 1}
$, for $k = 0, 1, \ldots, \left\lfloor\frac{n^2+1}{2}\right\rfloor$.

Let $m = n^2$. The overlap (values in both families) satisfies $\frac{k}{m-1} =
\frac{j}{m+1}$, i.e., $k(m+1) = j(m-1)$.

- **$n$ even ($m$ even):** $\gcd(m-1, m+1) = 1$ (both odd), so only $k = j = 0$
(i.e., $\theta = 0$) overlaps. Family A gives $m/2$ values, Family B gives $m/2
+ 1$ values. Total: $m/2 + (m/2 + 1) - 1 = m = n^2$.

- **$n$ odd ($m$ odd):** $\gcd(m-1, m+1) = 2$ (both even), so $\theta = 0$ and $
\theta = \pi$ overlap. Family A gives $(m+1)/2$ values, Family B gives $(m+3)/2$
 values. Total: $(m+1)/2 + (m+3)/2 - 2 = m = n^2$.

In both cases, $F$ has exactly $n^2$ distinct real roots.

**All roots are simple.** At a root $x_0 = \cos\theta_0$ with $\theta_0 \neq 0,
\pi$, we use $T_m'(\cos\theta) = \frac{m\sin(m\theta)}{\sin\theta}$:

- **Family A** ($m\theta_0 = \theta_0 + 2k\pi$): $T_m'(x_0) = \frac{m \sin\theta
_0}{\sin\theta_0} = m$, so $F'(x_0) = m - 1 \neq 0$.
- **Family B** ($m\theta_0 = -\theta_0 + 2k\pi$): $T_m'(x_0) = \frac{m(-\sin\the
ta_0)}{\sin\theta_0} = -m$, so $F'(x_0) = -m - 1 \neq 0$.

At $\theta_0 = 0$ ($x_0 = 1$): $T_m'(1) = m^2$, so $F'(1) = m^2 - 1 \neq 0$ for
$m \geq 2$.
At $\theta_0 = \pi$ ($x_0 = -1$, only when $m$ odd): $T_m'(-1) = (-1)^{m-1}m^2 =
 m^2$, so $F'(-1) = m^2 - 1 \neq 0$.

All $n^2$ roots are simple, confirming $n^2$ distinct critical points $(x_0, T_n
(x_0))$.

---

### Conclusion

The system $x' = y - p(x)$, $y' = x - q(y)$ with $p, q$ polynomials of degree $n
$ has at most $n^2$ critical points (for $n \geq 2$, by the degree bound on $q(p
(x)) - x$; for $n = 1$, trivially at most 1). This bound is achieved by the Cheb
yshev construction $p = T_n$, $q = T_n$ (for $n \geq 2$) and by $p(x) = 2x$, $q(
y) = y$ (for $n = 1$).

The maximum number of critical points is $\boxed{n^2}$.
