## Solution

**Answer: Yes.** For any set $E \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ with positive Lebesgue mea
sure, there always exists a point $x \in E$ such that for every $r > 0$, the set
 $B(x,r) \cap E$ has positive Lebesgue measure.

### Proof

The key tool is the **Lebesgue Density Theorem**:

> If $E \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is Lebesgue measurable, then for almost every $x \
in E$,
> $$\lim_{r \to 0^+} \frac{|B(x,r) \cap E|}{|B(x,r)|} = 1,$$
> where $|\cdot|$ denotes Lebesgue measure.

Such points are called **density points** of $E$.

**Step 1: A density point exists in $E$.**

Since $|E| > 0$ and the set of density points of $E$ has full measure in $E$ (it
s complement in $E$ has measure zero), the set of density points of $E$ has meas
ure equal to $|E| > 0$, hence is nonempty. Pick any density point $x \in E$.

**Step 2: At a density point, $|B(x,r) \cap E| > 0$ for all $r > 0$.**

First, observe that $r \mapsto |B(x,r) \cap E|$ is **non-decreasing** in $r$, si
nce $B(x,r_1) \subseteq B(x,r_2)$ whenever $r_1 \le r_2$, and so $B(x,r_1)\cap E
 \subseteq B(x,r_2)\cap E$.

Since $x$ is a density point, there exists some $r_0 > 0$ such that for all $0 <
 r \le r_0$,
$$\frac{|B(x,r) \cap E|}{|B(x,r)|} \ge \frac{1}{2}.$$
In particular, $|B(x,r_0) \cap E| \ge \tfrac{1}{2}|B(x,r_0)| > 0$.

Now for **any** $r > 0$:
- If $r \le r_0$: by the density property, $|B(x,r)\cap E| \ge \tfrac{1}{2}|B(x,
r)| > 0$.
- If $r > r_0$: by monotonicity, $|B(x,r)\cap E| \ge |B(x,r_0)\cap E| > 0$.

In both cases, $|B(x,r) \cap E| > 0$.

**Step 3: $x \in E$.**

This is given, since we chose $x$ to be a density point of $E$, and density poin
ts of $E$ belong to $E$ (up to a null set; we pick one that is actually in $E$,
which is possible since the density points have full measure in $E$).

### Conclusion

By the Lebesgue Density Theorem, any measurable set $E$ with $|E| > 0$ possesses
 a density point $x \in E$. At such a point, the measure $|B(x,r)\cap E|$ is pos
itive for all sufficiently small $r$, and by monotonicity in $r$, it remains pos
itive for all larger $r$ as well. Hence $|B(x,r)\cap E| > 0$ for **every** $r >
0$.

Therefore, such a point $x$ always exists. $\blacksquare$
