This is **Erdős Problem 212** (also known as the Erdős–Ulam problem): *Is there
a dense subset of ℝ² such that all pairwise distances are rational?*

## Analysis

**What is known:**

1. **Lines (trivial):** ℚ on any line gives a dense subset with all pairwise dis
tances rational.

2. **Circles (constructive):** There exists a dense subset of any circle with al
l pairwise distances rational. I can construct this explicitly: on the circle of
 radius 1/2, parametrize points by P(t) = ((1−t²)/(2(1+t²)), t/(1+t²)). Choose t
 = (1/u − u)/2 for u ∈ ℚ\{0}, which ensures 1+t² is a rational square. Then the
distance between P(t₁) and P(t₂) works out to 2|(u₁−u₂)(1+u₁u₂)| / (|u₁²+1|·|u₂²
+1|), which is rational. This set is dense on the circle.

3. **Full plane (open):** The challenge is extending beyond a single curve. If w
e take unions of circles, distances *between* points on different circles involv
e √(r₁² + r₂² − 2r₁r₂cos(α−β)), and making this rational for all cross-circle pa
irs imposes extremely stringent simultaneous Diophantine conditions.

**Why the problem is hard:**

- The set of "Pythagorean complex numbers" {z ∈ ℚ(i) : |z| ∈ ℚ} forms a group un
der *multiplication* but **not** under addition. The distance condition requires
 *additive* structure (differences must be Pythagorean), and no nontrivial 2-dim
ensional additive subgroup of the Pythagorean complex numbers is known.
- An inductive construction (adding points one at a time at rational distance fr
om all prior points) faces the geometric obstruction that intersecting ≥3 ration
al-radius circles generically yields no common point, making it unclear whether
density can be maintained.
- The problem has been open since Erdős posed it (referenced at erdosproblems.co
m/212, marked `research open`).

## Conclusion

This is a genuinely open research problem in combinatorial geometry. No construc
tion of a dense rational-distance subset of ℝ² is known, and no impossibility pr
oof exists either. The answer to the yes/no question is unknown.
