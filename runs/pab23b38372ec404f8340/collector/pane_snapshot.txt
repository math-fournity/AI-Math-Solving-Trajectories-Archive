## Solution

**Answer: No.** The condition $f(x)=0$ for all $x\in A$ does **not** imply $f(x)
=0$ almost everywhere on $[0,1]$.

### Key observation

A countable set has Lebesgue measure zero. Indeed, enumerate $A=\{a_1,a_2,\dots\
}$ and cover each $a_n$ by an interval of length $\varepsilon/2^n$; the total le
ngth is $\varepsilon$, and since $\varepsilon>0$ is arbitrary, $m(A)=0$.

Therefore $A$ is a **null set**, and the value of $f$ on a null set carries no i
nformation about the behavior of $f$ on the complement $[0,1]\setminus A$, which
 has full measure $m([0,1]\setminus A)=1$.

### Counterexample

Take $A=\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]$, which is countable and dense in $[0,1]$. Define

$$
f(x)=\begin{cases}0,& x\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1],\\[4pt] 1,& x\in[0,1]\setminus\mat
hbb{Q}.\end{cases}
$$

Then $f(x)=0$ for every $x\in A$, satisfying the hypothesis. However, the irrati
onals in $[0,1]$ have Lebesgue measure $1$, so $f(x)=1$ on a set of full measure
. Hence $f(x)=0$ holds only on the null set $A$, and

$$
m\big(\{x\in[0,1]:f(x)\neq 0\}\big)=m\big([0,1]\setminus\mathbb{Q}\big)=1.
$$

Thus $f$ is **not** zero almost everywhere; in fact it equals $1$ almost everywh
ere.

### Conclusion

Since $A$ is countable (hence of measure zero), prescribing $f$ on $A$ imposes n
o constraint on $f$ almost everywhere. The hypothesis is compatible with $f$ bei
ng any constant value, or indeed any measurable function, on the full-measure co
mplement $[0,1]\setminus A$. The implication fails.
