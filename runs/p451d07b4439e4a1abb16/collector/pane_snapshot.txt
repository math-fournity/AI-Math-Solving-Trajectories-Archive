## Proof

**Key idea.** Multiply $f$ by $z$ to convert the problem into one about analytic
 polynomials, then apply the maximum modulus principle.

**Step 1: Reformulation.** Define
$$g(z) := z\,f(z) = 1 - \sum_{j=0}^{n} a_j\, z^{j+1}.$$
Then $g$ is a polynomial of degree $\leq n+1$ satisfying $g(0) = 1$.

On the unit circle $\mathbb{T}$, we have $|z| = 1$, so
$$|f(z)| = |z\,f(z)| = |g(z)| \quad \text{for all } z \in \mathbb{T}.$$
Therefore
$$\sup_{z \in \mathbb{T}} |f(z)| = \sup_{|z|=1} |g(z)|.$$

**Step 2: Lower bound via the maximum modulus principle.** Since $g$ is a polyno
mial (hence analytic on all of $\mathbb{C}$), the maximum modulus principle give
s
$$\sup_{|z|=1} |g(z)| = \max_{|z| \leq 1} |g(z)| \geq |g(0)| = 1.$$
Thus for every choice of $a_0, \ldots, a_n$:
$$\sup_{z \in \mathbb{T}} |f(z)| \geq 1.$$

**Step 3: The bound is attained.** Set $a_0 = a_1 = \cdots = a_n = 0$. Then $f(z
) = \frac{1}{z}$, and for every $z \in \mathbb{T}$:
$$|f(z)| = \frac{1}{|z|} = 1.$$
Hence $\sup_{z \in \mathbb{T}} |f(z)| = 1$.

**Conclusion.** The minimum possible value is:

$$\boxed{1}$$
