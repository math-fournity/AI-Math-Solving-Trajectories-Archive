## Proof

We prove that every solution $(m,n)$ (odd, $\geq 1$, with $m\mid n^{2}+2$ and $n
\mid m^{2}+2$) is a pair of consecutive terms of $(u_k)$, hence both $m$ and $n$
 are terms of the sequence.

---

**Lemma 1 (Coprimality).** $\gcd(m,n)=1$.

*Proof.* If a prime $p\mid\gcd(m,n)$, then $p\mid n$ and $m\mid n^{2}+2$ gives $
p\mid n^{2}+2$, so $p\mid 2$, hence $p=2$. But $m,n$ are odd, contradiction. $\s
quare$

---

**Lemma 2 (Descent).** Let $(m,n)$ be a solution with $m\geq n$. Define $a=\dfra
c{n^{2}+2}{m}\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$. Then:

(i) $a$ is a positive odd integer with $a\leq n$, and $\max(n,a)<m$ unless $(m,n
)=(1,1)$;

(ii) $(n,a)$ is again a solution: $n\mid a^{2}+2$ and $a\mid n^{2}+2$.

*Proof.* Since $m\mid n^{2}+2$, $a$ is a positive integer; $n^{2}+2$ and $m$ are
 odd, so $a$ is odd. Because $m\geq n$ and $m\mid n^{2}+2$, we have $m\leq n^{2}
+2$, so $a\geq 1$.

For $a\leq n$: $a\leq n\iff n^{2}+2\leq mn\iff 2\leq n(m-n)$. If $m=n$, then $m\
mid m^{2}+2\Rightarrow m\mid 2\Rightarrow m=1$ (odd), giving $(m,n)=(1,1)$. Othe
rwise $m>n$; since both are odd, $m-n\geq 2$ and $n\geq 1$, so $n(m-n)\geq 2$, h
ence $a\leq n$. Moreover $n<m$, so $\max(n,a)\leq n<m$. This gives (i).

For (ii): $a\mid n^{2}+2$ holds since $n^{2}+2=am$. It remains to show $n\mid a^
{2}+2$. Working modulo $n$: since $m^{2}+2\equiv 0\pmod n$ (from $n\mid m^{2}+2$
), we have $m^{2}\equiv -2\pmod n$. By Lemma 1, $m$ is invertible mod $n$. From
$n^{2}+2=am$ and $n^{2}\equiv 0\pmod n$, we get $am\equiv 2\pmod n$, so $a\equiv
 2m^{-1}\pmod n$. Thus
$$
a^{2}\equiv 4m^{-2}\equiv 4\cdot(-2)^{-1}\cdot m^{-2}\cdot(-2)\equiv \dots
$$
More directly: $a^{2}\equiv 4m^{-2}\pmod n$, and we need $a^{2}\equiv -2\pmod n$
, i.e. $4\equiv -2m^{2}\pmod n$, i.e. $m^{2}\equiv -2\pmod n$, which holds. Henc
e $n\mid a^{2}+2$. $\square$

---

**Corollary 3.** Every solution descends to $(1,1)$.

*Proof.* Repeatedly applying Lemma 2 (renaming $(n,a)\to$ new $(m,n)$ and reorde
ring so the larger is first), the value $\max(m,n)$ strictly decreases at each n
on-base step. The process terminates at the unique base solution $(1,1)$. $\squa
re$

**Lemma 4 (Ascent generates the sequence).** Starting from $(1,1)$, the unique r
everse operation produces exactly the pairs $(u_{k+1},u_k)$.

*Proof.* Given a solution $(n,a)$ with $n\geq a$, the parent is $(m,n)$ where $m
=\dfrac{n^{2}+2}{a}$ (an integer since $a\mid n^{2}+2$). By the same modular arg
ument as in Lemma 2 (with roles swapped, using $n\mid a^{2}+2$), one checks $n\m
id m^{2}+2$; and $m\mid n^{2}+2$ is immediate. Also $m=(n^{2}+2)/a\geq (n^{2}+2)
/n>n$, so the chain is strictly increasing. Thus the ascent from $(1,1)$ is uniq
ue and infinite.

Define $F(x,y)=x^{2}+y^{2}-4xy$. One verifies $F(1,1)=-2$. If $F(n,a)=-2$ and $m
=4n-a$, then
$$
F(m,n)=m^{2}+n^{2}-4mn=(4n-a)^{2}+n^{2}-4n(4n-a)=a^{2}+n^{2}-4na=F(n,a)=-2.
$$
Moreover $F(n,a)=-2\iff n^{2}+a^{2}+2=4na\iff n^{2}+2=a(4n-a)=am$, so the parent
 is $m=4n-a$. Therefore the ascent follows the recurrence $m_{\text{new}}=4n-a$,
 i.e. $u_{k+2}=4u_{k+1}-u_k$, starting from $(u_2,u_1)=(1,1)$. The invariant $F\
equiv -2$ also confirms each pair is a genuine solution:
$$
u_{k+1}^{2}+2=u_k(4u_{k+1}-u_k)=u_k\,u_{k+2},\qquad u_k^{2}+2=u_{k+1}(4u_k-u_{k+
1})=u_{k+1}\,u_{k-1}.
$$
Hence the chain of solutions is precisely $\{(u_{k+1},u_k):k\geq 1\}$. $\square$

**Conclusion.** By Corollary 3 any solution $(m,n)$ descends to $(1,1)$; by Lemm

(u_{k+1},u_k)$. Therefore every solution satisfies $(m,n)=(u_{k+1},u_k)$ for som
