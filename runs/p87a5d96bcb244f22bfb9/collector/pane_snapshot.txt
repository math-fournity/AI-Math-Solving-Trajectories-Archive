Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether weak convergence in $L^2(0,
\tau;X)$ of a bounded sequence in $C(0,\tau;X)$ guarantees a pointwise weakly co
nvergent subsequence at some time $t$.

**Answer: No**, such a subsequence need not exist.

**Counterexample.**

Let $X = \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$, which is a Hilbert space and hence reflexive. Set
$\tau = 2\pi$ and define
$$x_n(t) = e_1 \cos(nt) + e_2 \sin(nt), \qquad t \in [0, 2\pi],$$
where $e_1, e_2$ are the first two standard basis vectors of $\ell^2$.

**Verification of hypotheses.**

1. **$x_n \in C(0, 2\pi; \ell^2)$:** The map $t \mapsto e_1 \cos(nt) + e_2 \sin(
nt)$ is smooth as an $\ell^2$-valued function. ✓

2. **Bounded sequence:** $\|x_n(t)\|_{\ell^2} = \sqrt{\cos^2(nt) + \sin^2(nt)} =
 1$ for all $n$ and $t$, so $\sup_n \|x_n\|_{C(0,2\pi;\,\ell^2)} = 1 < \infty$.
✓

3. **Weak convergence in $L^2(0, 2\pi; \ell^2)$:** Since $\ell^2$ is reflexive,
$(L^2(0,2\pi;\ell^2))^* \cong L^2(0,2\pi;\ell^2)$. For any test function $g \in
L^2(0,2\pi;\ell^2)$ with components $g_j(t)$:
$$\langle g, x_n \rangle_{L^2} = \int_0^{2\pi} \bigl[g_1(t)\cos(nt) + g_2(t)\sin
(nt)\bigr]\,dt.$$
Since $g_1, g_2 \in L^2(0,2\pi)$, the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma gives $\int_0^{2\pi
} g_1(t)\cos(nt)\,dt \to 0$ and $\int_0^{2\pi} g_2(t)\sin(nt)\,dt \to 0$. Hence
$x_n \rightharpoonup 0$ in $L^2(0,2\pi;\ell^2)$, i.e., $x \equiv 0$. ✓

**No subsequence converges weakly to $x(t)$ at any $t$.**

Fix any $t_0 \in [0, 2\pi]$ and any subsequence $\{n_k\}$. Then
$$x_{n_k}(t_0) = e_1 \cos(n_k t_0) + e_2 \sin(n_k t_0),$$
which lies in the two-dimensional subspace $\operatorname{span}(e_1, e_2) \subse
t \ell^2$. On this finite-dimensional subspace, weak convergence coincides with
norm convergence. Testing against $f = e_1$ and $f = e_2$:
$$\langle e_1, x_{n_k}(t_0)\rangle = \cos(n_k t_0), \qquad \langle e_2, x_{n_k}(
t_0)\rangle = \sin(n_k t_0).$$
For $x_{n_k}(t_0) \rightharpoonup 0 = x(t_0)$, we would need both $\cos(n_k t_0)
 \to 0$ and $\sin(n_k t_0) \to 0$. But
$$\cos^2(n_k t_0) + \sin^2(n_k t_0) = 1 \quad \text{for all } k,$$
so it is impossible for both to converge to $0$. Therefore $x_{n_k}(t_0) \not\ri
ghtharpoonup x(t_0)$ for **every** choice of $t_0$ and **every** subsequence $\{
n_k\}$.

**Conclusion.** The weak convergence $x_n \rightharpoonup x$ in $L^2(0,\tau;X)$
together with boundedness in $C(0,\tau;X)$ does **not** guarantee the existence
of a subsequence converging weakly to $x(t)$ in $X$ at any time $t \in [0,\tau]$
. The obstruction is that pointwise the values $x_n(t)$ may remain uniformly bou
nded away from $x(t)$ in a finite-dimensional subspace (where weak and norm conv
ergence coincide), while the oscillatory nature of $x_n(t)$ in $t$ still produce
s weak convergence to $0$ in the $L^2$ sense via the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma.
