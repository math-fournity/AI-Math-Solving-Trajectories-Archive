## Proof

**Claim:** Every weakly totally bounded metric space is separable.

**Setup.** Let $(X, d)$ be a weakly totally bounded metric space. By definition,
 for every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists a countable set $C_\varepsilon = \{x_
i\}_{i=1}^\infty \subseteq X$ such that
$$X \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^\infty B_\varepsilon(x_i).$$

**Constructing a candidate countable dense set.** For each $n \in \mathbb{N}$, a
pply the weak total boundedness hypothesis with $\varepsilon = 1/n$ to obtain a
countable set $C_n \subseteq X$ whose $(1/n)$-balls cover $X$. Define
$$D \;=\; \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} C_n.$$

**$D$ is countable.** Each $C_n$ is countable by hypothesis, and $D$ is a counta
ble union of countable sets, hence countable.

**$D$ is dense.** We show $\overline{D} = X$. Let $x \in X$ and let $\delta > 0$
 be arbitrary. Choose $n \in \mathbb{N}$ with $1/n < \delta$. Since the $(1/n)$-
balls centered at points of $C_n$ cover $X$, there exists $c \in C_n \subseteq D
$ with
$$d(x, c) < \frac{1}{n} < \delta.$$
Thus every $\delta$-neighborhood of $x$ meets $D$. Since $x \in X$ and $\delta >
 0$ were arbitrary, $X \subseteq \overline{D}$, i.e. $D$ is dense in $X$.

**Conclusion.** $D$ is a countable dense subset of $X$, so $X$ is separable. The
refore every weakly totally bounded metric space is separable. $\blacksquare$
