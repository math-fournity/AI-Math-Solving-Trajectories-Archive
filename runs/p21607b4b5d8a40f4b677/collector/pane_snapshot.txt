## Proof

Let the vertices of the convex $n$-gon be $v_1, v_2, \ldots, v_n$ in cyclic orde
r, each colored black (B) or white (W). A diagonal is *multicoloured* if its end
points have different colors. A coloring is *good* if there exists a triangulati
on using only multicoloured, non-crossing diagonals.

### Key Lemma

**In any good coloring, every triangle of the triangulation has exactly two vert
ices of one color and one of the other, and the monochromatic edge (between the
two same-colored vertices) is a polygon edge.**

*Proof.* Consider a triangle $T$ in the triangulation. With two colors and three
 vertices, at least two share a color.

- If all three vertices have the same color, all three edges of $T$ are monochro
matic. Since every diagonal in the triangulation must be multicoloured, all thre
e edges would have to be polygon edges. But for $n \geq 4$, every triangle in a
triangulation of a convex $n$-gon has at least one diagonal edge: three consecut
ive vertices $v_i, v_{i+1}, v_{i+2}$ have $(v_i, v_{i+2})$ as a diagonal (not a
polygon edge) when $n \geq 4$. Contradiction.

- So exactly two vertices share a color. The edge between them is monochromatic
and must be a polygon edge (since it cannot be a multicoloured diagonal). The ot
her two edges are multicoloured. $\square$

### Necessity: Good $\Rightarrow$ exactly 2 runs

By the lemma, each triangle has exactly one monochromatic edge, and it is a poly
gon edge. Each polygon edge belongs to exactly one triangle. A monochromatic pol
ygon edge serves as the monochromatic edge of its triangle; a multicoloured poly
gon edge does not. This gives a bijection between triangles and monochromatic po
lygon edges:

$$\#\text{triangles} = \#\text{monochromatic polygon edges}.$$

The left side equals $n - 2$. If $m$ denotes the number of multicoloured polygon
 edges, the right side equals $n - m$. Therefore $n - 2 = n - m$, so $m = 2$.

Having exactly $2$ multicoloured polygon edges means exactly $2$ color changes a
round the cycle, i.e., the vertices of each color form a single contiguous block
 (exactly $2$ runs).

### Sufficiency: Exactly 2 runs $\Rightarrow$ good

Suppose $v_1, \ldots, v_k$ are black and $v_{k+1}, \ldots, v_n$ are white (for s
ome $1 \leq k \leq n-1$). We construct a triangulation using only multicoloured
diagonals.

Draw the diagonal $(v_1, v_{k+1})$, which is multicoloured. This splits the poly
gon into two sub-polygons:

- **Left part** $\{v_1, \ldots, v_{k+1}\}$: only $v_{k+1}$ is white. Fan from $v
_{k+1}$: draw diagonals $(v_{k+1}, v_2), \ldots, (v_{k+1}, v_{k-1})$, all multic
oloured (W to B). This uses $k - 2$ diagonals.

- **Right part** $\{v_1, v_{k+1}, v_{k+2}, \ldots, v_n\}$: only $v_1$ is black.
Fan from $v_1$: draw diagonals $(v_1, v_{k+2}), \ldots, (v_1, v_{n-1})$, all mul
ticoloured (B to W). This uses $n - k - 2$ diagonals.

The total is $1 + (k-2) + (n-k-2) = n - 3$ diagonals, forming a valid triangulat
ion with all diagonals multicoloured. (When $k = 1$ or $k = n-1$, one sub-polygo
n is a triangle and the construction reduces to a single fan from the unique min
ority-color vertex.)

### Counting good colorings

A good coloring has exactly $2$ multicoloured polygon edges. These two edges par
tition the polygon boundary into two arcs; assigning one arc black and the other
 white determines the coloring. Conversely, each good coloring uniquely determin
es its two multicoloured edges and the color assignment.

- Choose $2$ edges from $n$ polygon edges: $\binom{n}{2}$ ways.
- Assign colors to the two arcs: $2$ ways.

Both arcs are non-empty (since $n \geq 4$ and the two chosen edges are distinct)
, so every choice is valid. Therefore:

$$\#\text{good colorings} = 2\binom{n}{2} = n(n-1).$$
