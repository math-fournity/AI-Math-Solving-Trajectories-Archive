## Solution

**Interpreting the equation.** The problem asks for the sum of all real values o
f $t = x/y$ satisfying the equilibrium. For the equation to determine $t$ alone
(as the problem requests — "calculate the sum of all possible real values of $t$
"), the middle quantity must be expressible purely in terms of $t$. The natural
ratio form of "the difference of squares together with the inverse ratio" that d
epends only on $t = x/y$ is

$$A \;=\; \frac{x^{2}-y^{2}}{xy} \;=\; \frac{x}{y}-\frac{y}{x} \;=\; t-\frac{1}{
t} \;=\; \frac{t^{2}-1}{t}.$$

(This is the unique rewriting of the difference-of-squares/inverse-ratio express
ion as a function of $t$ alone; the literal product $(x^{2}-y^{2})\tfrac{y}{x}=\
tfrac{y^{2}(t^{2}-1)}{t}$ still carries a free factor $y^{2}$ and would leave $t
$ undetermined, contradicting the problem's request for a finite set of values.)

The equilibrium equation is therefore

$$t \;=\; \frac{A+1}{A-1}, \qquad A = t-\frac{1}{t}.$$

**Solving.** Cross-multiplying (valid when $A\neq 1$):

$$t(A-1)=A+1 \;\Longrightarrow\; A(t-1)=t+1 \;\Longrightarrow\; A=\frac{t+1}{t-1
}\quad (t\neq 1).$$

Substituting $A=\dfrac{t^{2}-1}{t}=\dfrac{(t-1)(t+1)}{t}$:

$$\frac{(t-1)(t+1)}{t}=\frac{t+1}{t-1}.$$

**Case 1: $t+1\neq 0$ (i.e. $t\neq -1$).** Cancel $t+1$:

$$\frac{t-1}{t}=\frac{1}{t-1}\;\Longrightarrow\;(t-1)^{2}=t\;\Longrightarrow\;t^
{2}-3t+1=0.$$

By the quadratic formula,

$$t=\frac{3\pm\sqrt{5}}{2}.$$

Both roots are positive and different from $1$; one checks $A\neq 1$ for both (s
ince $A=1$ would give $t^{2}-t-1=0$, whose roots $(1\pm\sqrt5)/2$ are distinct f
rom $(3\pm\sqrt5)/2$). Also $t\neq 0$, so both are admissible.

**Case 2: $t=-1$.** Then $A = -1-\tfrac{1}{-1}=-1+1=0$, and the equation reads

$$-1=\frac{0+1}{0-1}=-1,\qquad\checkmark$$

with $A-1=-1\neq 0$. So $t=-1$ is a valid solution. (Geometrically this is the d
egenerate ratio where $x=-y$, making the difference of squares vanish.)

**Collecting solutions.** The real values of $t$ satisfying the equilibrium are

$$t\in\left\{-1,\;\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2},\;\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\right\}.$$

**Sum.** Using Vieta's relation for $t^{2}-3t+1=0$ (sum of roots $=3$):

$$\sum t \;=\; -1+\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}+\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2} \;=\; -1+3 \;=\; \boxed{
2}.$$
