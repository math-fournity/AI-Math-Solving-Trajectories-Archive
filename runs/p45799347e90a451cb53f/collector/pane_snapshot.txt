## Proof

**Setup and notation.** Let $a_k = 2m_k + 1$ and $b_k = 2n_k + 1$ (both odd posi
tive integers), and let $g_k = \gcd(a_k, b_k)$. Since $m_k/n_k$ is the reduced f
orm of $a_{k-1}/b_{k-1}$, we have
$$m_k = \frac{a_{k-1}}{g_{k-1}}, \qquad n_k = \frac{b_{k-1}}{g_{k-1}}.$$
We must show $g_k = 1$ for all but finitely many $k$.

**Step 1: A divisibility relation.** Define $\delta_k = m_k - n_k$. Since $g_k \
mid a_k$ and $g_k \mid b_k$, we get $g_k \mid (a_k - b_k) = 2(m_k - n_k) = 2\del
ta_k$. Because $g_k$ is odd (as $a_k, b_k$ are odd), in fact
$$g_k \mid \delta_k. \tag{1}$$

**Step 2: Recurrence for $\delta_k$.** Using the definitions,
$$\delta_{k+1} = m_{k+1} - n_{k+1} = \frac{a_k - b_k}{g_k} = \frac{2\delta_k}{g_
k}. \tag{2}$$
By (1), $g_k \mid \delta_k$, so $\delta_{k+1}$ is indeed an integer. Since $\del
ta_0 = m_0 - n_0 \neq 0$ (as $m_0, n_0$ are distinct) and $\delta_{k+1} = 2\delt
a_k/g_k$ with $g_k \mid \delta_k$ and $g_k \geq 1$, an easy induction gives $\de
lta_k \neq 0$ for every $k$.

**Step 3: Growth of the 2-adic valuation.** Since $g_k$ is odd, equation (2) giv
es
$$v_2(\delta_{k+1}) = v_2(\delta_k) + 1,$$
so $v_2(\delta_k) = v_2(\delta_0) + k$ for all $k \geq 0$.

**Step 4: Bounding the product of the $g_i$.** Write $\delta_k = 2^{v_2(\delta_0
)+k} \cdot u_k$ where $u_k$ is the (signed) odd part of $\delta_k$. Iterating (2
),
$$|\delta_k| = \frac{2^k\,|\delta_0|}{\prod_{i=0}^{k-1} g_i},$$
so, using $|\delta_0| = 2^{v_2(\delta_0)}|u_0|$,
$$|u_k| = \frac{|u_0|}{\prod_{i=0}^{k-1} g_i}. \tag{3}$$
Since $\delta_k \neq 0$, its odd part satisfies $|u_k| \geq 1$. Substituting int
o (3),
$$\prod_{i=0}^{k-1} g_i \leq |u_0| \qquad \text{for every } k \geq 1. \tag{4}$$

**Step 5: Conclusion.** The partial products $P_k = \prod_{i=0}^{k-1} g_i$ form
a non-decreasing sequence of positive integers (each $g_i \geq 1$) that is bound
ed above by the fixed integer $|u_0|$ by (4). Hence $P_k$ stabilizes: there exis
ts $K$ such that $P_k = P_K$ for all $k \geq K$, which forces $g_k = 1$ for ever
y $k \geq K$.

Therefore $\gcd(2m_k+1,\, 2n_k+1) = g_k = 1$ for all but finitely many positive
integers $k$. $\blacksquare$
