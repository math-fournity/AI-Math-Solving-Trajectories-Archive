## Proof

Let $W(t)$ be a standard Brownian motion. We use the defining property that Brow
nian motion has **independent increments**: for any $0 \le s < t$, the increment
 $W(t) - W(s)$ is independent of $W(s)$ (and of all earlier increments), and is
distributed as $N(0, t-s)$.

Define the three independent increments:
$$X_1 = W(1) - W(0) = W(1), \quad X_2 = W(2) - W(1), \quad X_3 = W(3) - W(2).$$

By the independent increments property, $X_1, X_2, X_3$ are mutually independent
, each distributed as $N(0,1)$.

Now express the values of $W$ at times $1, 2, 3$ in terms of these increments:
$$W(1) = X_1, \quad W(2) = X_1 + X_2, \quad W(3) = X_1 + X_2 + X_3.$$

The desired inequality $W(3) > W(2) > W(1)$ decomposes as two independent condit
ions:

1. **$W(2) > W(1)$**: $\;X_1 + X_2 > X_1 \iff X_2 > 0$.
2. **$W(3) > W(2)$**: $\;X_1 + X_2 + X_3 > X_1 + X_2 \iff X_3 > 0$.

Thus the event $\{W(3) > W(2) > W(1)\}$ is exactly the event $\{X_2 > 0 \text{ a
nd } X_3 > 0\}$.

Since $X_2$ and $X_3$ are independent and each is a standard normal (symmetric a
bout $0$):
$$P(X_2 > 0) = \tfrac{1}{2}, \qquad P(X_3 > 0) = \tfrac{1}{2}.$$

By independence:
$$P\bigl(W(3) > W(2) > W(1)\bigr) = P(X_2 > 0)\,P(X_3 > 0) = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot
\tfrac{1}{2} = \boxed{\tfrac{1}{4}}.$$
