## Proof

**Claim.** It is possible. For every $n \ge 1$, there exist $n \times n$ complex
 matrices $A, B$ with $ABA - BAB = I$.

**Construction.** Let $\varphi = \dfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$ be the golden ratio, so
that $\varphi$ satisfies the quadratic $\varphi^{2}-\varphi-1=0$, i.e. $\varphi^
{2}=\varphi+1$.

Set
$$B = I_{n}, \qquad A = \varphi\, I_{n}.$$

Both are $n\times n$ complex matrices (since $\varphi\in\mathbb{C}$). Now comput
e:
$$ABA = A\,I_{n}\,A = A^{2} = \varphi^{2} I_{n},$$
$$BAB = I_{n}\,A\,I_{n} = A = \varphi\, I_{n}.$$

Therefore
$$ABA - BAB = \varphi^{2} I_{n} - \varphi\, I_{n} = (\varphi^{2}-\varphi)\,I_{n}
.$$

Using the defining relation $\varphi^{2}-\varphi = 1$,
$$ABA - BAB = 1\cdot I_{n} = I_{n}.$$

**Remark (why the "obvious" obstruction fails).** One might expect a trace obstr
uction as in the classical identity $AB-BA=I$ (impossible since $\operatorname{t
r}(AB-BA)=0\neq n$). But here
$$\operatorname{tr}(ABA-BAB)=\operatorname{tr}(A^{2}B)-\operatorname{tr}(AB^{2})
,$$
which is **not** a commutator trace and is not forced to vanish. Indeed, for our
 solution $\operatorname{tr}(ABA-BAB)=\operatorname{tr}(I_{n})=n$, consistent wi
th $\operatorname{tr}\bigl((\varphi^{2}-\varphi)I_{n}\bigr)=n\cdot 1=n$. So no c
ontradiction arises, and the explicit construction above settles the question.

Hence such matrices $A$ and $B$ exist for every $n\ge 1$.
