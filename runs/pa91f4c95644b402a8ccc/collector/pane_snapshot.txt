## Proof

**The domain.** Let $D = \mathbb{C}^* \setminus \bigl(\overline{\mathbb{D}(1,1)}
 \cup \overline{\mathbb{D}(-1,1)}\bigr)$, the exterior (in $\mathbb{C}^*$) of th
e two closed disks $|z-1|\le 1$ and $|z+1|\le 1$. The two circles are tangent at
 the origin (both pass through $0$ with vertical tangent), so their union is a c
onnected, full, compact set; hence $D$ is simply connected and conformally equiv
alent to a strip.

**Step 1 — Inversion $w = 1/z$.** Rewrite each circle as an equation in $|z|^2$
and $\operatorname{Re} z$:

$$|z-1|^2 = 1 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; |z|^2 - 2\operatorname{Re} z = 0 \;\Longle
ftrightarrow\; |z|^2 = 2\operatorname{Re} z,$$
$$|z+1|^2 = 1 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; |z|^2 + 2\operatorname{Re} z = 0 \;\Longle
ftrightarrow\; |z|^2 = -2\operatorname{Re} z.$$

Under $w = 1/z$ we have $|z|^2 = 1/|w|^2$ and $\operatorname{Re} z = \operatorna
me{Re} w/|w|^2$. Substituting:

- $|z-1|=1 \;\Longrightarrow\; 1/|w|^2 = 2\operatorname{Re} w/|w|^2 \;\Longright
arrow\; \operatorname{Re} w = \tfrac12$,
- $|z+1|=1 \;\Longrightarrow\; 1/|w|^2 = -2\operatorname{Re} w/|w|^2 \;\Longrigh
tarrow\; \operatorname{Re} w = -\tfrac12$.

The point $z=\infty \in D$ maps to $w=0$, which lies between the two lines. Henc
e

$$w = \frac{1}{z} \quad\text{maps } D \text{ conformally onto the vertical strip
 } \;\Sigma = \bigl\{w : -\tfrac12 < \operatorname{Re} w < \tfrac12\bigr\}.$$

**Step 2 — Rotate, scale, and shift to obtain $S$.** For $w = u+iv$ with $u \in
(-\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$, set

$$f := 2iw + i.$$

Then $\operatorname{Im} f = 2\operatorname{Im}(iw) + 1 = 2\operatorname{Re} w +
1 = 2u + 1 \in (0,2)$. Thus $f$ maps $\Sigma$ onto $S = \{z : 0 < \operatorname{
Im} z < 2\}$, sending the boundary line $\operatorname{Re} w = \tfrac12$ to $\op
eratorname{Im} f = 2$ and $\operatorname{Re} w = -\tfrac12$ to $\operatorname{Im
} f = 0$.

**Composition.** Combining the two steps,

$$\boxed{\,f(z) = \frac{2i}{z} + i = \frac{i(z+2)}{z}\,}$$

maps $D$ onto $S$.

**Verification of the required properties.**

- *Holomorphic on $D$:* $z=0$ lies on $\partial D$ (it is the common tangent poi
nt of the two circles), so $1/z$ has no pole in $D$; $f$ is holomorphic on $D$.
- *One-to-one:* $1/z$ is a Möbius map (bijective on $\mathbb{C}^*$), and $w \map
sto 2iw+i$ is affine and injective; their composition is injective.
- *Onto $S$:* established above — the two boundary circles map to the two bounda
ry lines $\operatorname{Im} = 2$ and $\operatorname{Im} = 0$, and the interior m
aps onto $0 < \operatorname{Im} < 2$.
- *Sanity check:* $z=\infty \mapsto f=i$ (midline of $S$); $z=2i \mapsto f = 2i/
(2i)+i = 1+i \in S$; $z=1$ (inside $|z-1|\le1$) $\mapsto f = 2i+i = 3i$, $\opera
torname{Im}=3\notin(0,2)$, confirming the disk interior is excluded.

Therefore $f(z) = \dfrac{i(z+2)}{z}$ is the desired Riemann map of $D$ onto $S$.
