## Distributional Derivative of the Heaviside Function

**Definition.** The Heaviside function is
$$
H(x) = \begin{cases} 0, & x < 0, \\ 1, & x >  0, \end{cases}
$$
with the value at $x=0$ being irrelevant for distributional purposes (any choice
 in $[0,1]$ defines the same locally integrable function, hence the same distrib
ution).

**Goal.** Compute $H'$ in the sense of distributions, i.e. find the distribution
 $T$ such that for every test function $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$,
$$
\langle H', \varphi \rangle = -\langle H, \varphi' \rangle.
$$

---

### Computation

By the definition of the distributional derivative,
$$
\langle H', \varphi \rangle = -\langle H, \varphi' \rangle = -\int_{-\infty}^{\i
nfty} H(x)\,\varphi'(x)\,dx.
$$

Since $H(x)=0$ for $x<0$ and $H(x)=1$ for $x>0$,
$$
\langle H', \varphi \rangle = -\int_{0}^{\infty} \varphi'(x)\,dx.
$$

Because $\varphi$ is compactly supported, there exists $R>0$ with $\varphi(x)=0$
 for all $x \ge R$. Hence
$$
\int_{0}^{\infty} \varphi'(x)\,dx = \int_{0}^{R} \varphi'(x)\,dx = \varphi(R) -
\varphi(0) = 0 - \varphi(0) = -\varphi(0).
$$

Therefore
$$
\langle H', \varphi \rangle = -(-\varphi(0)) = \varphi(0).
$$

---

### Identification with the Dirac delta

The Dirac delta distribution $\delta_0$ is defined by
$$
\langle \delta_0, \varphi \rangle = \varphi(0), \qquad \forall\,\varphi \in C_c^
\infty(\mathbb{R}).
$$

Since $\langle H', \varphi \rangle = \varphi(0) = \langle \delta_0, \varphi\rang
le$ for every test function $\varphi$, and distributions are determined by their
 action on all test functions, we conclude
$$
H' = \delta_0.
$$

---

### Conclusion

The distributional derivative of the Heaviside function is the Dirac delta distr
ibution at the origin:

$$
\boxed{\,H' = \delta_0\,}
$$

Equivalently, in physics notation, $\dfrac{dH}{dx} = \delta(x)$.
