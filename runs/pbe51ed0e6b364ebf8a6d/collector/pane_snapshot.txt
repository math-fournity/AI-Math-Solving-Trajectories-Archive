**Domain.** We need $x+4\ge0$, $3-x\ge0$, $12-x-x^2\ge0$, and $2x+5\ge0$. Since
$12-x-x^2=(x+4)(3-x)$, the first three conditions give $-4\le x\le 3$, and the l
ast gives $x\ge -\tfrac52$. So the domain is $\boxed{-\tfrac52\le x\le 3}$.

**Key substitution.** Set $a=\sqrt{x+4}$, $b=\sqrt{3-x}$. Then:
$$a^2+b^2=(x+4)+(3-x)=7,\qquad ab=\sqrt{(x+4)(3-x)}=\sqrt{12-x-x^2}.$$

The left-hand side becomes $a+b+ab$. Observe:
$$a+b+ab=(1+a)(1+b)-1.$$

**Relating to the right-hand side.** Let $u=a+b=\sqrt{x+4}+\sqrt{3-x}$. Then:
$$u^2=a^2+2ab+b^2=7+2\sqrt{12-x-x^2},$$
so $\sqrt{12-x-x^2}=\frac{u^2-7}{2}$, and the LHS equals:
$$u+\frac{u^2-7}{2}=\frac{u^2+2u-7}{2}=\frac{(u+1)^2-8}{2}.$$

Setting this equal to the RHS $=x-1+\sqrt{2x+5}$:
$$\frac{(u+1)^2-8}{2}=x-1+\sqrt{2x+5},$$
$$(u+1)^2=2x+6+2\sqrt{2x+5}=\bigl(\sqrt{2x+5}\bigr)^2+2\sqrt{2x+5}+1=\bigl(\sqrt
{2x+5}+1\bigr)^2.$$

Since $u\ge0$ and $\sqrt{2x+5}\ge0$, both $u+1$ and $\sqrt{2x+5}+1$ are positive

$$u+1=\sqrt{2x+5}+1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sqrt{x+4}+\sqrt{3-x}=\sqrt{2x+5}.$
