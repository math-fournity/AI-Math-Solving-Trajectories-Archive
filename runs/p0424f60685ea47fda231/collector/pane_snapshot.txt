Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every infinite set of posit
ive integers contains an infinite subset that is either **pairwise coprime** (ev
ery pair has gcd = 1) or **pairwise non-coprime** (every pair has gcd > 1).

**Answer: Yes, this is always possible.**

## Proof

Let $S$ be an infinite set of positive integers. We consider two cases.

---

### Case 1: Some prime $p$ divides infinitely many elements of $S$.

Define $T = \{s \in S : p \mid s\}$. By assumption, $T$ is infinite. For any two
 elements $a, b \in T$, we have $p \mid a$ and $p \mid b$, so $\gcd(a,b) \geq p
> 1$. Thus $T$ is an infinite subset in which every pair has greatest common div
isor greater than 1. ✓

---

### Case 2: Every prime divides only finitely many elements of $S$.

We construct an infinite **pairwise coprime** subset by induction.

**Base step.** Pick any $a_1 \in S$. Since $a_1$ is a positive integer, it has f
initely many prime factors; call them $p_1, \dots, p_k$. By the case assumption,
 each $p_i$ divides only finitely many elements of $S$, so the set
$$F_1 = \{s \in S : \gcd(s, a_1) > 1\} \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^{k} \{s \in S : p
_i \mid s\}$$
is a finite union of finite sets, hence finite. Set $S_1 = S \setminus F_1$; thi
s is infinite (we removed finitely many elements from an infinite set), and ever
y element of $S_1$ is coprime to $a_1$.

**Inductive step.** Suppose we have chosen pairwise coprime $a_1, \dots, a_n \in
 S$, and $S_n \subseteq S$ is infinite with every element of $S_n$ coprime to ea
ch of $a_1, \dots, a_n$. Pick $a_{n+1} \in S_n$. The element $a_{n+1}$ has finit
ely many prime factors $q_1, \dots, q_m$. By the case assumption, each $q_j$ div
ides only finitely many elements of $S$ (hence of $S_n \subseteq S$), so
$$F_{n+1} = \{s \in S_n : \gcd(s, a_{n+1}) > 1\}$$
is finite. Set $S_{n+1} = S_n \setminus F_{n+1}$, which is infinite. Then $a_{n+
1}$ is coprime to $a_1, \dots, a_n$ (since $a_{n+1} \in S_n$), and every element
 of $S_{n+1}$ is coprime to all of $a_1, \dots, a_{n+1}$.

**Conclusion of Case 2.** By induction we obtain an infinite sequence $a_1, a_2,
 a_3, \dots$ of elements of $S$ that are pairwise coprime. ✓

---

### Combining the cases

Cases 1 and 2 are exhaustive (either some prime divides infinitely many elements
, or every prime divides only finitely many). In Case 1 we obtain an infinite su
bset that is pairwise non-coprime; in Case 2 we obtain an infinite subset that i
s pairwise coprime. In either case, the infinite subset satisfies the required p
roperty: any two of its members are either all relatively prime, or all share a
common divisor greater than 1.

Therefore, for **every** infinite set of positive integers, such an infinite sub
set always exists. $\blacksquare$
