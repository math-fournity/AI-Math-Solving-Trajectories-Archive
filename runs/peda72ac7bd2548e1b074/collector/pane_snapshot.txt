Looking at this problem, I need to find the largest decay rate $\alpha$ certifia
ble by a quadratic Lyapunov function for the LTI system $\dot{x} = Ax$.

## Proof

**Setup.** Consider the LTI system $\dot{x} = Ax$ with $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \tim
es n}$ Hurwitz (all eigenvalues have strictly negative real part). A quadratic L
yapunov function $V(x) = x^T P x$ with $P \succ 0$ certifies a decay rate $\alph
a > 0$ if

$$\dot{V}(x) = x^T(A^T P + PA)x \leq -2\alpha\, x^T P x = -2\alpha\, V(x) \quad
\forall\, x,$$

which holds if and only if

$$A^T P + PA \preceq -2\alpha P. \tag{$\star$}$$

This is equivalent to $(A + \alpha I)^T P + P(A + \alpha I) \preceq 0$, i.e., th
e shifted matrix $B := A + \alpha I$ admits a quadratic Lyapunov function provin
g its (non-strict) stability.

**Definition.** The *certifiable decay rate* is
$$\alpha^* := \sup\bigl\{\alpha \geq 0 : \exists\, P \succ 0 \text{ satisfying }
 (\star)\bigr\}.$$

**Claim.** $\alpha^* = -\alpha(A)$, where $\alpha(A) := \max_{i} \operatorname{R
e}(\lambda_i(A))$ is the spectral abscissa of $A$.

---

**Part 1: $\alpha^* \leq -\alpha(A)$ (upper bound).**

Suppose $P \succ 0$ satisfies $(\star)$ for some $\alpha$. Set $B = A + \alpha I
$. The condition $B^T P + PB \preceq 0$ with $P \succ 0$ implies that $V(x) = x^
T P x$ is non-increasing along trajectories of $\dot{x} = Bx$.

Pick any eigenvalue $\lambda_i$ of $A$ with eigenvector $v_i$ (possibly complex;
 work over $\mathbb{C}$ with $P$ extended Hermitian). Along the trajectory $x(t)
 = e^{\lambda_i t} v_i$ of $\dot{x}=Ax$, condition $(\star)$ gives:

$$\frac{d}{dt}V = 2\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_i)\, V \leq -2\alpha\, V.$$

Since $V > 0$ (as $P \succ 0$ and $v_i \neq 0$), we divide to obtain $\operatorn
ame{Re}(\lambda_i) \leq -\alpha$ for every $i$. Hence $\alpha \leq -\max_i \oper
atorname{Re}(\lambda_i(A)) = -\alpha(A)$. Taking the supremum, $\alpha^* \leq -\
alpha(A)$.

---

**Part 2: $\alpha^* \geq -\alpha(A)$ (achievability).**

For any $\alpha < -\alpha(A)$, the shifted matrix $B = A + \alpha I$ has all eig
envalues satisfying

$$\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_i(B)) = \operatorname{Re}(\lambda_i(A)) + \alpha < \
alpha(A) + \alpha < 0,$$

so $B$ is Hurwitz. By the **Lyapunov theorem** (strict version): a matrix $B$ is
 Hurwitz if and only if for every $Q \succ 0$ there exists a unique $P \succ 0$
solving the Lyapunov equation $B^T P + PB = -Q$. Choosing any $Q \succ 0$ (e.g.,
 $Q = I$) yields $P \succ 0$ with

$$B^T P + PB = -Q \prec 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad (A+\alpha I)^T P + P(A+\alph
a I) \prec 0,$$

which is exactly $(\star)$ with strict inequality. Therefore every $\alpha < -\a
lpha(A)$ is certifiable, giving $\alpha^* \geq -\alpha(A)$.

---

**Combining Parts 1 and 2:** $\alpha^* = -\alpha(A) = -\max_i \operatorname{Re}(
\lambda_i(A)) = \min_i \bigl|\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_i(A))\bigr|$.

**Tightness remark.** This certifiable rate equals the *true* exponential decay
rate of the system: $\|x(t)\| \leq M e^{-\alpha t}$ holds for all $\alpha < -\al
pha(A)$ (and fails for $\alpha > -\alpha(A)$, as seen from the modal/eigenvalue
decomposition). Thus quadratic Lyapunov functions are **non-conservative** for L
TI decay-rate certification — they recover the full spectral abscissa. (At the b
oundary $\alpha = -\alpha(A)$ exactly, a non-strict $P \succ 0$ exists iff the e
igenvalue(s) attaining the spectral abscissa are semisimple; regardless, the sup
remum is $-\alpha(A)$.)

---

**Answer.** The largest lower bound on the decay rate of $\dot{x} = Ax$ certifia
ble by a quadratic Lyapunov function $V = x^T P x$ is

$$\boxed{-\alpha(A) \;=\; -\max_{i}\operatorname{Re}\!\bigl(\lambda_i(A)\bigr),}
$$

the negative of the spectral abscissa of $A$ (equivalently, the minimum over eig
envalues of $|\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_i(A))|$, assuming $A$ is Hurwitz).
