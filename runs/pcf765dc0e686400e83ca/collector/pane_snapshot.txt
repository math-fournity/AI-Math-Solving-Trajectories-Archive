## Proof

**Setup and reformulation.** Since $f(m(x,y)) = \frac{f(x)+f(y)}{2}$, the transl
ative condition $m(x+t,y+t) = m(x,y)+t$ is equivalent to

$$f(m(x,y)+t) = \frac{f(x+t)+f(y+t)}{2}, \qquad \text{for all } x,y>0,\; t\in\ma
thbb{R}. \tag{1}$$

Subtracting $f(m(x,y)) = \frac{f(x)+f(y)}{2}$ from (1):

$$f(m(x,y)+t) - f(m(x,y)) = \frac{[f(x+t)-f(x)] + [f(y+t)-f(y)]}{2}. \tag{2}$$

**Step 1: Reducing to a functional equation for $f$.**

Define $\phi_t(x) := f(x+t) - f(x)$ for each fixed $t$. Then (2) reads

$$\phi_t(m(x,y)) = \frac{\phi_t(x) + \phi_t(y)}{2}. \tag{3}$$

That is, $\phi_t$ is *Jensen-affine* with respect to the quasiarithmetic mean $m
$. Setting $u = f(x)$, $v = f(y)$ and $H_t := \phi_t \circ f^{-1}$, equation (3)
 becomes

$$H_t\!\left(\tfrac{u+v}{2}\right) = \tfrac{H_t(u) + H_t(v)}{2},$$

so $H_t$ is midpoint-affine. Since $f$ is continuous and strictly monotone, $f^{
-1}$ is continuous, and $\phi_t$ is continuous, hence $H_t$ is continuous. A con
tinuous midpoint-affine function is affine, so

$$H_t(u) = a(t)\,u + b(t)$$

for functions $a, b$ of $t$. Therefore

$$\phi_t(x) = a(t)\,f(x) + b(t),$$

i.e.,

$$f(x+t) = \underbrace{[1+a(t)]}_{=:\,\alpha(t)}\,f(x) + \underbrace{b(t)}_{=:\,
\beta(t)}. \tag{4}$$

Setting $t=0$ gives $\alpha(0)=1$, $\beta(0)=0$.

**Step 2: Solving the functional equation (4).**

Computing $f(x+s+t)$ in two ways:

- Via $f((x+s)+t)$: $\;\alpha(t)\,f(x+s)+\beta(t) = \alpha(t)\bigl[\alpha(s)f(x)
+\beta(s)\bigr]+\beta(t)$.
- Via $f(x+(s+t))$: $\;\alpha(s+t)\,f(x)+\beta(s+t)$.

Equating coefficients of $f(x)$ and the constant term:

$$\alpha(s+t) = \alpha(s)\,\alpha(t), \qquad \beta(s+t) = \alpha(t)\,\beta(s) +
\beta(t). \tag{5}$$

*Continuity of $\alpha,\beta$:* Pick $x_1 \neq x_2$ (so $f(x_1)\neq f(x_2)$ by s
trict monotonicity). From (4), $f(x_1+t)-f(x_2+t) = \alpha(t)[f(x_1)-f(x_2)]$; t
he left side is continuous in $t$, so $\alpha$ is continuous. Then $\beta(t) = f
(x_0+t)-\alpha(t)f(x_0)$ is also continuous.

**Case 1: $\alpha \equiv 1$.** Then $\beta(s+t)=\beta(s)+\beta(t)$ (additive Cau
chy). By continuity, $\beta(t) = ct$ for some constant $c$. Equation (4) gives $
f(x+t) = f(x)+ct$, so

$$\boxed{f(x) = cx + d, \qquad c \neq 0.} \tag{6}$$

This generates the arithmetic mean $m(x,y)=\frac{x+y}{2}$, which is clearly tran
slative. ✓

**Case 2: $\alpha \not\equiv 1$.** The continuous multiplicative Cauchy equation
 gives $\alpha(t) = e^{\lambda t}$ for some $\lambda \neq 0$. From the second eq
uation in (5) and symmetry $s \leftrightarrow t$:

$$e^{\lambda t}\beta(s) + \beta(t) = e^{\lambda s}\beta(t) + \beta(s),$$

$$(e^{\lambda t}-1)\,\beta(s) = (e^{\lambda s}-1)\,\beta(t),$$

so $\beta(t) = K(e^{\lambda t}-1)$ for a constant $K$. Substituting into (4):

$$f(x+t) = e^{\lambda t}f(x) + K(e^{\lambda t}-1) = e^{\lambda t}(f(x)+K) - K.$$

Setting $F(x) := f(x)+K$, we get $F(x+t) = e^{\lambda t}F(x)$, hence $F(x) = Ae^
{\lambda x}$ for a constant $A \neq 0$. Therefore

$$\boxed{f(x) = Ae^{\lambda x} + B, \qquad A\neq 0,\;\lambda\neq 0,} \tag{7}$$

where $B = -K$. This generates the mean

$$m(x,y) = \frac{1}{\lambda}\ln\frac{e^{\lambda x}+e^{\lambda y}}{2},$$

and translativity is verified:

$$m(x+t,y+t) = \frac{1}{\lambda}\ln\frac{e^{\lambda t}(e^{\lambda x}+e^{\lambda
y})}{2} = t + m(x,y). \quad \checkmark$$

**Step 3: Completeness.** Every continuous, strictly monotone generating functio
n $f$ of a translative quasiarithmetic mean must satisfy (4), which forces $\alp
ha$ to solve the multiplicative Cauchy equation, yielding exactly Cases 1 and 2.
 No other solutions exist.

**Conclusion.** The generating functions $f$ (up to the irrelevant affine freedo
m in $A, B$ or $c, d$, which do not change the induced mean) are precisely:

$$f(x) = cx + d \quad (c \neq 0) \qquad \text{and} \qquad f(x) = Ae^{\lambda x}
+ B \quad (A \neq 0,\;\lambda \neq 0).$$

The first generates the **arithmetic mean**; the second generates the **exponent
ial (quasiarithmetic) mean** $m(x,y)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\ln\frac{e^{\lambda x}+e^{
\lambda y}}{2}$. These are the only two translative quasiarithmetic means.
