m classes of topologies on an infinite set $X$ with $|X| = \kappa$.

## Proof

Let $X$ be an infinite set with $|X| = \kappa \geq \aleph_0$. The symmetric grou
p $\operatorname{Sym}(X)$ acts on the set of all topologies on $X$ via $\sigma \
cdot \tau = \{\sigma(U) : U \in \tau\}$. The homeomorphism classes are precisely
 the orbits of this action. Let $N$ denote the number of orbits.

### Step 1: $|\operatorname{Sym}(X)| = 2^\kappa$

**Upper bound.** $|\operatorname{Sym}(X)| \leq \kappa^\kappa$. For infinite $\ka
ppa$: $\kappa^\kappa \leq (2^\kappa)^\kappa = 2^{\kappa \cdot \kappa} = 2^\kappa
$.

**Lower bound.** Partition $X$ into $\kappa$ many disjoint pairs $\{a_\alpha, b_
\alpha\}$, $\alpha < \kappa$. For each subset $S \subseteq \kappa$, define the p
ermutation that swaps $a_\alpha, b_\alpha$ for $\alpha \in S$ and fixes them oth
erwise. This gives $2^\kappa$ distinct permutations.

Hence $|\operatorname{Sym}(X)| = 2^\kappa$, and each orbit has size $\leq 2^\kap
pa$.

### Step 2: The total number of topologies on $X$ is $2^{2^\kappa}$

**Upper bound.** Every topology is a subset of $\mathcal{P}(X)$, so there are at
 most $|\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(X))| = 2^{2^\kappa}$ topologies.

**Lower bound.** Fix a point $p \in X$. For each ultrafilter $\mathcal{U}$ on $X
 \setminus \{p\}$, define:
$$\tau_{\mathcal{U}} = \{U \subseteq X : p \notin U\} \cup \{\{p\} \cup V : V \i
n \mathcal{U}\}.$$
One verifies this is a topology: the open sets not containing $p$ are all subset
s of $X \setminus \{p\}$ (so arbitrary unions and finite intersections of those
remain open), and sets containing $p$ correspond to ultrafilter members (closed
under finite intersections and supersets, and the ultrafilter property ensures t
he whole space is open). Distinct ultrafilters yield distinct topologies, since
the neighborhood filter at $p$ recovers $\mathcal{U}$ exactly.

By a classical theorem of Pospíšil (1937), the number of ultrafilters on a set o
f cardinality $\kappa \geq \aleph_0$ is $2^{2^\kappa}$. Therefore there are at l
east $2^{2^\kappa}$ topologies on $X$.

Combining, the total number of topologies is exactly $2^{2^\kappa}$.

### Step 3: Counting the orbits

The total number of topologies equals the sum of orbit sizes:
$$2^{2^\kappa} = \sum_{\text{orbits}} |\text{orbit}| \leq N \cdot 2^\kappa.$$

For infinite cardinals, $N \cdot 2^\kappa = \max(N, 2^\kappa)$. Thus:
$$\max(N,\, 2^\kappa) \geq 2^{2^\kappa}.$$

Since $2^\kappa < 2^{2^\kappa}$ (strictly, by Cantor's theorem applied twice), w
e must have:
$$N \geq 2^{2^\kappa}.$$

### Step 4: Conclusion

- **Upper bound:** $N \leq$ (total number of topologies) $= 2^{2^\kappa}$.
- **Lower bound:** $N \geq 2^{2^\kappa}$ (from Step 3).

Therefore $N = 2^{2^\kappa} = 2^{2^{|X|}}$.

$$\boxed{2^{2^{|X|}}}$$
