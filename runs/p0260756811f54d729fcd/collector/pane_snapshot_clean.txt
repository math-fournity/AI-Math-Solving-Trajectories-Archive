## Tangent Space to the Grassmannian

**Claim.** The tangent space to the Grassmannian $G(k, V)$ at a point $E$ (a $k$
-dimensional subspace of the $n$-dimensional vector space $V$) is canonically is
omorphic to
$$T_E\, G(k, V) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Hom}(E,\, V/E).$$

---

### Proof

Let $\dim V = n$ and fix a $k$-dimensional subspace $E \subseteq V$, so $E \in G
(k,V)$. We work over a field (e.g. $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$); the argument i
s field-independent.

**Step 1 — A chart around $E$.** Choose a complementary subspace $F \subseteq V$
 so that $V = E \oplus F$ (hence $\dim F = n - k$). Every $k$-dimensional subspa
ce $E'$ sufficiently close to $E$ is the graph of a unique linear map $\varphi :
 E \to F$. Concretely, writing the projection $V \to E$ along $F$ as $\pi_E$, th
e subspace $E'$ is transverse to $F$ (i.e. $\pi_E|_{E'} : E' \to E$ is an isomor
phism) precisely when $E'$ lies in the open chart $U_F$ around $E$, and then
$$E' = \{e + \varphi(e) : e \in E\}$$
for a unique $\varphi \in \operatorname{Hom}(E, F)$. This gives the standard coo
rdinate identification
$$U_F \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; \operatorname{Hom}(E, F), \qquad E' \longmapsto
 \varphi,$$
with $E$ itself corresponding to $\varphi = 0$.

**Step 2 — The tangent space in coordinates.** Since $E$ corresponds to the orig
in in the vector space $\operatorname{Hom}(E, F) \cong \mathbb{A}^{k(n-k)}$, the
 tangent space at $E$ in this chart is simply
$$T_E\, G(k,V) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Hom}(E, F).$$

**Step 3 — Independence of the complement (canonical form).** The identification
 $T_E G(k,V) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(E,F)$ appears to depend on the choice of c
omplement $F$. We remove this dependence. The quotient map $q: V \to V/E$ restri
cts on $F$ to an isomorphism $F \xrightarrow{\sim} V/E$ (since $V = E \oplus F$)
. Composing,
$$\operatorname{Hom}(E, F) \;\xrightarrow{\;q_*\;}\; \operatorname{Hom}(E, V/E),
 \qquad \varphi \longmapsto q \circ \varphi,$$
is an isomorphism of vector spaces. Transporting the identification of Step 2 th
rough it yields
$$T_E\, G(k,V) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Hom}(E, V/E).$$

It remains to check this is independent of $F$. Take another complement $F'$, wi
th quotient isomorphism $q'_*: \operatorname{Hom}(E,F') \xrightarrow{\sim} \oper
atorname{Hom}(E,V/E)$. The change of chart $\operatorname{Hom}(E,F) \to \operato
rname{Hom}(E,F')$ sends $\varphi$ to the unique $\varphi'$ whose graph is the sa
me $E'$; equivalently $\varphi' = p_{F'} \circ (\mathrm{id}_E + \varphi) \circ (
\mathrm{id}_E)^{-1}$ where $p_{F'}$ is projection to $F'$ along $E$. At $\varphi
 = 0$ (the point $E$) its differential is the linear map $\varphi \mapsto p_{F'}
|_F \circ \varphi$. Under the quotient isomorphisms,
$$q'_* \circ (p_{F'}|_F \circ -) = q \circ - = q_*,$$
because $q' \circ p_{F'}|_F = q$ (both sides send $f \in F$ to its class in $V/E
$, since $V = E \oplus F' = E \oplus F$). Hence the two transported identificati
ons $T_E G(k,V) \to \operatorname{Hom}(E,V/E)$ agree at the origin. So the isomo
rphism $T_E G(k,V) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(E, V/E)$ is canonical — independent
of the chosen complement.

**Step 4 — Dimension.** As a consistency check,
$$\dim T_E\, G(k,V) = \dim \operatorname{Hom}(E, V/E) = (\dim E)(\dim V/E) = k(n
-k),$$
which equals $\dim G(k,V)$, as expected.

**Step 5 — Intrinsic interpretation (optional but clarifying).** A tangent vecto
r at $E$ is an equivalence class of smooth curves $E(t)$ in $G(k,V)$ with $E(0)
= E$. Given such a curve, choose (locally) a smooth family of bases $\{e_1(t),\d
ots,e_k(t)\}$ of $E(t)$ with $e_i(0) = e_i$ a basis of $E$, and define
$$\delta e_i = e_i'(0) \in V.$$
The map $\Phi : E \to V/E$, $\Phi(e_i) = [\delta e_i] \in V/E$, is well defined
(independent of the choice of moving basis: changing $e_i(t)$ by a $t$-dependent
 invertible matrix $A(t)$ with $A(0) = I$ alters $\delta e_i$ by elements of $E$
, which vanish in $V/E$). This $\Phi \in \operatorname{Hom}(E, V/E)$ is precisel
y the tangent vector, and in the chart $V = E \oplus F$ it recovers the linear m
ap $\varphi$ of Step 1. This gives the canonical identification directly, withou
t choosing a complement.

---

Therefore the tangent space to the Grassmannian $G(k, V)$ at the point $E$ is ca
nonically
$$\boxed{\,T_E\, G(k, V) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Hom}(E,\, V/E)\,}.$$
