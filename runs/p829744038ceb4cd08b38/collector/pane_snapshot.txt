## Proof

**Yes**, such a line always exists, regardless of the characteristic of $K$.

**Setup.** Let $G$ be a 1-dimensional algebraic subgroup of $\mathbb{G}_a^3 = (K
,+)^3$. Since $G$ is an algebraic subgroup, it is a closed subvariety of $K^3$ o
f dimension 1, and $0 \in G$. A line $L$ with $L \cap G = \{0\}$ must pass throu
gh the origin (since $0 \in G$ and we need $0 \in L \cap G$). So we seek a line
through the origin whose only intersection with $G$ is $\{0\}$.

**Parameter space of lines.** Lines through the origin in $K^3$ are in bijection
 with $\mathbb{P}^2(K)$ via $L = K\cdot v \leftrightarrow [v]$. This parameter s
pace has dimension 2.

**The projection map.** Consider the natural projection:
$$\pi : K^3 \setminus \{0\} \to \mathbb{P}^2, \qquad p \mapsto [p].$$
Restrict to $G \setminus \{0\}$. Since $\dim G = 1$ and $\{0\}$ is a proper clos
ed subset of $G$ (as $\dim\{0\} = 0 < 1 = \dim G$), removing it preserves dimens
ion:
$$\dim(G \setminus \{0\}) = 1.$$

**Image dimension bound.** The restriction $\pi|_{G\setminus\{0\}} : G \setminus
 \{0\} \to \mathbb{P}^2$ is a morphism of algebraic varieties. By Chevalley's th
eorem, its image $\pi(G \setminus \{0\})$ is a constructible subset of $\mathbb{
P}^2$, and:
$$\dim \pi(G \setminus \{0\}) \leq \dim(G \setminus \{0\}) = 1.$$

**Existence of a good direction.** Since $\dim \mathbb{P}^2 = 2 > 1 \geq \dim \p
i(G \setminus \{0\})$, the image $\pi(G \setminus \{0\})$ is a **proper** subset
 of $\mathbb{P}^2$. (A constructible subset of dimension $\leq 1$ is contained i
n its Zariski closure, a proper closed subset of $\mathbb{P}^2$; its complement
is nonempty—in fact, dense and open.)

Choose any $[v] \in \mathbb{P}^2 \setminus \pi(G \setminus \{0\})$ and set $L =
K \cdot v$.

**Verification.**
- $0 \in L \cap G$: clear, since $L$ passes through the origin and $G$ is a subg
roup.
- $L \cap G \subseteq \{0\}$: Suppose $p \in L \cap G$ with $p \neq 0$. Then $p
\in G \setminus \{0\}$ and $p \in L \setminus \{0\}$, so $[p] = [v]$. But $[p] \
in \pi(G \setminus \{0\})$ while $[v] \notin \pi(G \setminus \{0\})$—contradicti
on.

Therefore $L \cap G = \{0\}$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on characteristic.** This argument is characteristic-independent. In ch
aracteristic 0, the only 1-dimensional algebraic subgroups of $\mathbb{G}_a^3$ a
re lines (vector subspaces), making the result immediate. In characteristic $p >
 0$, there are additional 1-dimensional algebraic subgroups (e.g., images of $\m
athbb{G}_a$ under maps given by additive polynomials, such as $x \mapsto (x, x^p
, x^{p^2})$), which are not linear subspaces. The dimension-counting argument ab
ove handles all cases uniformly: the "forbidden" directions form a set of dimens
ion $\leq 1$ inside the 2-dimensional projective plane, so a good line always ex
ists.
