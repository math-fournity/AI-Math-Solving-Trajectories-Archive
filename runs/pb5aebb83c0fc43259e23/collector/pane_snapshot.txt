## Solution

**Setup and dimensions.** Let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix with $m \neq n$ (non
-square). For $A^\top W A$ to be square and invertible as stated, we need $A^\to
p W A$ to be $n \times n$, which forces $W$ to be $m \times m$. The product $J =
 A(A^\top W A)^{-1}A^\top W$ is then $m \times m$.

**The wide case is impossible.** If $n > m$ (wide $A$), then $\operatorname{rank
}(A) \le m < n$, so $\operatorname{rank}(A^\top W A) \le \operatorname{rank}(A)
< n$, making the $n \times n$ matrix $A^\top W A$ singular — contradicting the h
ypothesis that it is invertible. Hence $A$ must be **tall**: $m > n$.

**$J$ is idempotent.** Assuming $W$ is invertible (so that $A^\top W A$ invertib
le is consistent with $A$ full column rank), compute:
$$J^2 = A(A^\top W A)^{-1}A^\top W \cdot A(A^\top W A)^{-1}A^\top W = A(A^\top W
 A)^{-1}(A^\top W A)(A^\top W A)^{-1}A^\top W = A(A^\top W A)^{-1}A^\top W = J.$
$

**Rank of $J$.** Since $A^\top W A$ is $n \times n$ invertible and $A$ is $m \ti
mes n$ with full column rank $n$,
$$\operatorname{rank}(J) = \operatorname{rank}\!\big(A\,(A^\top W A)^{-1}A^\top
W\big) = n,$$
because the right factor $(A^\top W A)^{-1}A^\top W$ has rank $n$ (its leading $
n\times n$ block $(A^\top W A)^{-1}$ is invertible), and $A$ maps its $n$-dimens
ional row space injectively into $\mathbb{R}^m$.

**Eigenvalues and determinant.** An idempotent matrix has only eigenvalues $0$ a
nd $1$. With $\operatorname{rank}(J) = n$ and $J$ being $m \times m$ with $m > n
$, the spectrum of $J$ consists of $1$ with multiplicity $n$ and $0$ with multip
licity $m - n \ge 1$. Therefore $J$ is singular, and
$$\det(J) = 0.$$
