## Solution

**Interpreting the set.** The set is
$$S = \Bigl\{x \in l_2 : \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x_n}{n} = 0\Bigr\}.$$

**Key observation: the defining sequence belongs to $l_2$.** Let $a = (a_n)_{n\g
e 1}$ with $a_n = \tfrac{1}{n}$. Then
$$\|a\|^2 = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6} < \infty,$$
so $a \in l_2$, and $a \neq 0$.

**Reformulation as a kernel.** The condition defining $S$ is precisely
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x_n}{n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} x_n\, a_n = \langle x,
 a\rangle = 0.$$
Hence $S = \ker f$, where $f(x) = \langle x, a\rangle$ is the linear functional
associated with $a$.

**$f$ is continuous (bounded).** By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,
$$|f(x)| = |\langle x, a\rangle| \le \|x\|\,\|a\|,$$
so $f$ is a bounded (continuous) linear functional on $l_2$, with $\|f\| = \|a\|
 > 0$.

**$S$ is a proper closed subspace.** As the kernel of a continuous linear functi
onal, $S$ is closed. It is proper since $a \notin S$:
$$f(a) = \langle a, a\rangle = \|a\|^2 = \frac{\pi^2}{6} \neq 0.$$

**A proper subspace of a normed space has empty interior.** Suppose for contradi
ction that $S$ has a nonempty interior. Then there exists an open ball $B(x_0, r
) \subseteq S$ with $r > 0$. For any $y \in l_2$ with $\|y\| < r$, both $x_0 + y
 \in S$ and $x_0 \in S$, so $y = (x_0+y) - x_0 \in S$. Thus $B(0,r) \subseteq S$
. Now any $z \in l_2$ with $z \neq 0$ satisfies $\|\tfrac{r}{2\|z\|}z\| = \tfrac
{r}{2} < r$, so $\tfrac{r}{2\|z\|}z \in S$, and by linearity $z \in S$. Hence $S
 = l_2$, contradicting $a \notin S$.

**Conclusion.** $S$ has empty interior; it contains no interior points.
