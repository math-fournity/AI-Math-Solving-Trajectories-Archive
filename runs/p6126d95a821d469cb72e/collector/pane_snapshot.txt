## Solution

**Answer: Yes, the intersection $\bigcap_{v=0}^{\infty} G^v$ is trivial, i.e., $
\{1\}$.**

### Proof

**Setup and definitions.** Let $K$ be a $p$-adic local field and $G_K = \operato
rname{Gal}(\bar{K}/K)$ its absolute Galois group. The ramification groups in upp
er numbering $\{G^v\}_{v \geq -1}$ form a decreasing filtration of $G_K$ by clos
ed normal subgroups, with $G^{-1} = G_K$ and $G^0 = I_K$ (the inertia group).

The ramification groups of the absolute Galois group are defined as the inverse
limit of the ramification groups of finite quotients:

$$G^v = \varprojlim_{L/K \text{ finite Galois}} G^v(L/K),$$

where $G^v(L/K)$ denotes the $v$-th ramification group in upper numbering of the
 finite Galois extension $L/K$. This inverse limit is well-defined because the u
pper numbering is compatible with quotients (a fundamental property of the Hasse
–Herbrand transition): if $L' \supseteq L$ are both finite Galois over $K$, the
natural projection $\operatorname{Gal}(L'/K) \to \operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ maps $
G^v(L'/K)$ onto $G^v(L/K)$.

Concretely, an element $\sigma = (\sigma_L)_L \in G_K = \varprojlim \operatornam
e{Gal}(L/K)$ belongs to $G^v$ if and only if $\sigma_L \in G^v(L/K)$ for **every
** finite Galois extension $L/K$.

**Key fact.** For any finite Galois extension $L/K$, the ramification groups $G^
v(L/K)$ are eventually trivial: there exists $v_0 = v_0(L/K) \geq 0$ such that

$$G^v(L/K) = \{1\} \quad \text{for all } v > v_0.$$

This holds because $L/K$ has **bounded ramification**. In lower numbering, the g
roups $G_u(L/K)$ are $\{1\}$ for all $u$ exceeding the largest ramification brea
k of $L/K$ (which is finite since $[L:K] < \infty$). The upper numbering is obta
ined from the lower numbering via the Hasse–Herbrand function $\varphi_{L/K} : [
-1,\infty) \to [-1,\infty)$, which is a strictly increasing, piecewise-linear ho
meomorphism. Hence $G^v(L/K) = G_{\varphi_{L/K}(v)}(L/K) = \{1\}$ for all suffic
iently large $v$.

**Main argument.** Let $\sigma \in \bigcap_{v=0}^{\infty} G^v$. We show $\sigma
= 1$.

Suppose for contradiction that $\sigma \neq 1$. Since $G_K$ is the inverse limit
 of finite groups $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$, the element $\sigma$ is non-trivial
 if and only if its image $\sigma_L$ is non-trivial in some finite Galois quotie
nt $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$. Fix such an extension $L/K$ with $\sigma_L \neq 1$
.

By the key fact above, there exists $v_0$ such that $G^v(L/K) = \{1\}$ for all $
v > v_0$. Since $\sigma \in G^v$ for **all** $v \geq 0$ (by assumption $\sigma \
in \bigcap_{v=0}^{\infty} G^v$), in particular $\sigma \in G^{v_0 + 1}$. By the
definition of $G^v$ as an inverse limit, this forces $\sigma_L \in G^{v_0+1}(L/K
) = \{1\}$, i.e., $\sigma_L = 1$.

This contradicts our choice of $L$ with $\sigma_L \neq 1$. Therefore $\sigma = 1
$, and we conclude:

$$\bigcap_{v=0}^{\infty} G^v = \{1\}. \qquad \blacksquare$$

**Remark.** The essential mechanism is that the absolute Galois group is profini
te (an inverse limit of finite groups), while each finite quotient has ramificat
ion groups that are eventually trivial. The intersection $\bigcap_{v \geq 0} G^v
$ tests membership across **all** finite quotients simultaneously, and any non-t
rivial element is detected by some finite quotient where it survives—but in that
 quotient, sufficiently high ramification groups are trivial, excluding the elem
ent. This is the local-field analogue of the fact that the intersection of all h
igher ramification groups detects only the identity.
