## Proof

We evaluate the principal value integral

$$I_0 = \text{P.V.}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{ix}}{x}\,dx$$

using contour integration, and verify whether $I_0 = i\pi$.

### Step 1. Choose the integrand and contour

Set $f(z) = \dfrac{e^{iz}}{z}$, which has a simple pole at $z = 0$ with residue
$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f = 1$.

Since the pole lies on the real axis, we use an **indented contour** $\Gamma$ co
nsisting of four pieces (all in the upper half-plane):

1. $\gamma_1$: the real segment from $-R$ to $-\varepsilon$,
2. $C_\varepsilon$: the small semicircle $z = \varepsilon e^{i\theta}$, $\theta:
 \pi \to 0$ (clockwise, passing **above** the origin),
3. $\gamma_2$: the real segment from $\varepsilon$ to $R$,
4. $C_R$: the large semicircle $z = R e^{i\theta}$, $\theta: 0 \to \pi$ (counter
clockwise).

The pole at $z=0$ is **excluded** from the interior of $\Gamma$, so $f$ is holom
orphic inside $\Gamma$ and

$$\oint_\Gamma f(z)\,dz = 0.$$

### Step 2. Large arc $C_R$ vanishes (Jordan's lemma)

On $C_R$, $z = Re^{i\theta}$ with $0 \le \theta \le \pi$, so $\operatorname{Im}
z \ge 0$ and $|e^{iz}| = e^{-\operatorname{Im}z} \le 1$. Hence

$$\left|\int_{C_R} \frac{e^{iz}}{z}\,dz\right| \le \int_0^\pi \frac{e^{-R\sin\th
eta}}{R}\,R\,d\theta = \int_0^\pi e^{-R\sin\theta}\,d\theta \;\xrightarrow{R\to\
infty}\; 0$$

by Jordan's lemma. So $\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{C_R} f = 0$.

### Step 3. Small arc $C_\varepsilon$ contributes $-i\pi$

Parametrize $C_\varepsilon$: $z = \varepsilon e^{i\theta}$, $\theta: \pi \to 0$
(clockwise). Then $dz = i\varepsilon e^{i\theta}\,d\theta$ and

$$\int_{C_\varepsilon} \frac{e^{iz}}{z}\,dz = \int_\pi^0 \frac{e^{i\varepsilon e
^{i\theta}}}{\varepsilon e^{i\theta}}\,i\varepsilon e^{i\theta}\,d\theta = i\int
_\pi^0 e^{i\varepsilon e^{i\theta}}\,d\theta.$$

As $\varepsilon \to 0$, $e^{i\varepsilon e^{i\theta}} \to 1$ uniformly, so

$$\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{C_\varepsilon} f = i\int_\pi^0 1\,d\theta = i(0 -
 \pi) = -i\pi.$$

### Step 4. Take the limits

From $\oint_\Gamma f = 0$:

$$\int_{-R}^{-\varepsilon}\frac{e^{ix}}{x}\,dx \;+\; \int_{C_\varepsilon} f \;+\
; \int_{\varepsilon}^{R}\frac{e^{ix}}{x}\,dx \;+\; \int_{C_R} f = 0.$$

Letting $R\to\infty$ and $\varepsilon\to 0$:

$$\text{P.V.}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{ix}}{x}\,dx \;-\; i\pi \;+\; 0 =
0,$$

hence

$$\boxed{I_0 = \text{P.V.}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{ix}}{x}\,dx = i\pi.}
$$

### Step 5. Independent verification via real/imaginary split

Write $\dfrac{e^{ix}}{x} = \dfrac{\cos x}{x} + i\,\dfrac{\sin x}{x}$.

- **Real part:** $\dfrac{\cos x}{x}$ is odd, so $\text{P.V.}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\i
nfty}\dfrac{\cos x}{x}\,dx = 0$.
- **Imaginary part:** $\dfrac{\sin x}{x}$ is even and continuous at $0$ (limit $
1$), so no principal value is needed. The classical Dirichlet integral gives

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx = \pi.$$

Therefore

$$I_0 = 0 + i\pi = i\pi,$$

confirming the contour-integration result.

### Conclusion

The claimed result $I_0 = i\pi$ is **correct**. The key mechanism is that the in
dented semicircle above the pole at the origin contributes $-i\pi$ (half of $2\p
i i \cdot \operatorname{Res}$, with a sign from the clockwise orientation), whic
h exactly yields $i\pi$ for the principal value integral.
