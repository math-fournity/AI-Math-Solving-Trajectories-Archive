## Proof

**Claim.** For every $s = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$, the Sobolev space $H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R
}^n)$ is dense in $H^s(\mathbb{R}^n)$.

The proof rests on the classical fact that $C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is dense i
n $H^s(\mathbb{R}^n)$ for every $s \geq 0$. Since
$$C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n) \subset H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^n) \subset H^s(\mathbb{R}^
n),$$
the density of $C_c^\infty$ in $H^s$ immediately implies the density of $H^{s+1}
$ in $H^s$. It remains to establish the density of $C_c^\infty$ in $H^s$.

---

### Density of $C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ in $H^s(\mathbb{R}^n)$

Recall that for integer $s \geq 0$,
$$H^s(\mathbb{R}^n) = \bigl\{u \in L^2(\mathbb{R}^n) : D^\alpha u \in L^2(\mathb
b{R}^n) \text{ for all } |\alpha| \leq s\bigr\},$$
with norm $\|u\|_{H^s}^2 = \sum_{|\alpha|\leq s}\|D^\alpha u\|_{L^2}^2$ (derivat
ives in the distributional sense).

Let $u \in H^s(\mathbb{R}^n)$. We approximate $u$ by $C_c^\infty$ functions in t
wo steps.

**Step 1 (Cutoff).** Let $\eta \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ satisfy $\eta \equi
v 1$ on $B(0,1)$ and $\operatorname{supp}\eta \subset B(0,2)$. Set $\eta_R(x) =
\eta(x/R)$. We claim $\eta_R u \to u$ in $H^s$ as $R \to \infty$.

For any multi-index $\alpha$ with $|\alpha| \leq s$, the Leibniz rule gives
$$D^\alpha(\eta_R u) = \sum_{\beta \leq \alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\beta} D^{\alpha-\
beta}\eta_R\, D^\beta u.$$

- **The term $\beta = \alpha$:** This is $\eta_R\, D^\alpha u$. Since $|\eta_R|
\leq 1$ and $\eta_R \to 1$ pointwise, dominated convergence (applied to $|D^\alp
ha u|^2$) yields $\|\eta_R D^\alpha u - D^\alpha u\|_{L^2} \to 0$.

- **The terms $\beta < \alpha$:** Here $D^{\alpha-\beta}\eta_R(x) = R^{-|\alpha-
\beta|}(D^{\alpha-\beta}\eta)(x/R)$, which is bounded by $C\,R^{-|\alpha-\beta|}
$ (with $|\alpha-\beta|\geq 1$) and supported in $\{R \leq |x| \leq 2R\}$. Hence
$$\|D^{\alpha-\beta}\eta_R\, D^\beta u\|_{L^2} \leq C\, R^{-|\alpha-\beta|}\|D^\
beta u\|_{L^2} \xrightarrow{R\to\infty} 0.$$

Summing over $|\alpha| \leq s$, we conclude $\|\eta_R u - u\|_{H^s} \to 0$. Note
 $\eta_R u$ has compact support (in $\overline{B(0,2R)}$) and belongs to $H^s$.

**Step 2 (Mollification).** Let $\rho \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ be a standar
d mollifier ($\rho \geq 0$, $\operatorname{supp}\rho \subset B(0,1)$, $\int \rho
 = 1$), and set $\rho_\varepsilon(x) = \varepsilon^{-n}\rho(x/\varepsilon)$. For
 fixed $R$, the convolution $\rho_\varepsilon * (\eta_R u)$ belongs to $C_c^\inf
ty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ for all $\varepsilon > 0$ (it is smooth and supported in a sli
ghtly enlarged compact set).

We claim $\rho_\varepsilon * (\eta_R u) \to \eta_R u$ in $H^s$ as $\varepsilon \
to 0$. Indeed, for $|\alpha| \leq s$,
$$D^\alpha\bigl(\rho_\varepsilon * (\eta_R u)\bigr) = \rho_\varepsilon * D^\alph
a(\eta_R u),$$
and since $D^\alpha(\eta_R u) \in L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)$ (with compact support), the
 standard property of mollification in $L^2$ gives
$$\|\rho_\varepsilon * D^\alpha(\eta_R u) - D^\alpha(\eta_R u)\|_{L^2} \to 0 \qu
ad\text{as } \varepsilon \to 0.$$
Summing over $|\alpha| \leq s$ yields $\|\rho_\varepsilon * (\eta_R u) - \eta_R
u\|_{H^s} \to 0$.

**Combining the two steps.** Given $\varepsilon' > 0$, choose $R$ large enough t
hat $\|u - \eta_R u\|_{H^s} < \varepsilon'/2$, then choose $\varepsilon$ small e
nough that $\|\eta_R u - \rho_\varepsilon * (\eta_R u)\|_{H^s} < \varepsilon'/2$
. By the triangle inequality,
$$\|u - \rho_\varepsilon * (\eta_R u)\|_{H^s} < \varepsilon'.$$
Since $\rho_\varepsilon * (\eta_R u) \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$, this proves
$C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is dense in $H^s(\mathbb{R}^n)$.

---

### Conclusion

We have the chain of inclusions
$$C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n) \subset H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^n) \subset H^s(\mathbb{R}^
n).$$
The leftmost space is dense in $H^s(\mathbb{R}^n)$ (proved above). Since $H^{s+1
}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ contains the dense subset $C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$, it is its
elf dense in $H^s(\mathbb{R}^n)$.

Therefore, **yes**, $H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is dense in $H^s(\mathbb{R}^n)$ for
every $s = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$. $\blacksquare$
