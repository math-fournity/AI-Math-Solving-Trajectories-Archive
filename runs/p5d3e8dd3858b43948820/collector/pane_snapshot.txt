### What a Riemannian submersion requires

Recall that $f:(M,g)\to (N,h)$ is a *Riemannian submersion* if:

1. $h$ is a well-defined Riemannian metric on $N$;
2. For every $p\in M$, the restricted differential $df_p\big|_{\mathcal{H}_p}:\m
athcal{H}_p\to T_{f(p)}N$ is a **linear isometry**.

The construction in the problem *forces* condition (2) by definition of $h$: set
ting $h_{f(p)}(X,Y)=g_p\bigl(\varphi_p^{-1}(X),\varphi_p^{-1}(Y)\bigr)$ makes $\
varphi_p$ an isometry by fiat. So the entire question reduces to **condition (1)
: is $h$ well-defined?**

### The well-definedness issue

For $h$ to be a metric on $N$, the value $h_q(X,Y)$ must depend only on $q\in N$
 and $X,Y\in T_qN$—not on the particular lift $p\in f^{-1}(q)$. But the formula
picks a point $p$ in the fiber and uses the metric $g_p$ there. If $p'\neq p$ is
 another point with $f(p')=q$, the formula gives

$$h_q(X,Y) \;\stackrel{?}{=}\; g_{p'}\!\bigl(\varphi_{p'}^{-1}(X),\,\varphi_{p'}
^{-1}(Y)\bigr).$$

There is no reason, for an *arbitrary* Riemannian metric $g$ on $M$, that the $g
$-inner product of horizontal lifts should be constant along fibers. This is an
extra condition on $g$, not a consequence of $f$ being a submersion.

### Counterexample

Let $M=\mathbb{R}^2$ with coordinates $(x,y)$, $N=\mathbb{R}$, and $f(x,y)=x$ (a
 submersion). Equip $M$ with the metric

$$g = e^{2y}\,dx^2 + dy^2.$$

- **Vertical distribution:** $\mathcal{V}=\ker(df)=\operatorname{span}(\partial_
y)$.
- **Horizontal distribution:** Since $g(\partial_x,\partial_y)=0$, we have $\mat
hcal{H}=\operatorname{span}(\partial_x)$.
- **Isomorphism:** $\varphi_{(x,y)}(\partial_x)=\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}\big
|_{x}$, so $\varphi_{(x,y)}^{-1}\!\bigl(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}\big|_{x}\bi
gr)=\partial_x\big|_{(x,y)}$.

Now compute the proposed metric at $q=x\in\mathbb{R}$, using the standard tangen
t vector $X=Y=\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}\big|_x$:

$$h_x\!\left(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x},\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}\right) =
g_{(x,y)}(\partial_x,\partial_x) = e^{2y}.$$

This value **depends on $y$**, but $y$ is free along the fiber $f^{-1}(x)=\{(x,y
):y\in\mathbb{R}\}$. Choosing two different points $(x,0)$ and $(x,1)$ in the sa
me fiber gives

$$h_x\bigl(\partial_x,\partial_x\bigr)=1 \quad\text{at }(x,0), \qquad h_x\bigl(\
partial_x,\partial_x\bigr)=e^{2}\quad\text{at }(x,1).$$

These disagree, so **$h$ is not well-defined** as a metric on $N$.

### Conclusion

The construction yields a well-defined Riemannian metric on $N$ (and hence makes
 $f$ a Riemannian submersion) **if and only if** the horizontal inner product is
 constant along fibers:

$$g_p\!\bigl(\varphi_p^{-1}(X),\varphi_p^{-1}(Y)\bigr) \text{ is independent of
} p\in f^{-1}(q) \text{ for all } q\in N,\; X,Y\in T_qN.$$

This is a genuine additional constraint on $g$ relative to the fibration $f$; it
 is **not** automatic. The counterexample above shows it can fail. Therefore:

> **No**, this construction does not in general make $f$ a Riemannian submersion
. It does so only when the metric $g$ satisfies the fiber-constancy condition ab
ove; for a generic Riemannian metric on $M$, the proposed $h$ is not even well-d
efined.
