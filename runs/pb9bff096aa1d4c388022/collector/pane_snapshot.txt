This matrix is the **companion matrix** of the monic polynomial
$$p(t) = t^n + a_{n-1}t^{n-1} + \cdots + a_1 t + a_0.$$

I claim that **both the characteristic polynomial and the minimal polynomial of
this operator equal $p(t)$**.

---

## Setup and notation

Let $V = \mathbb{C}^n$ with standard basis $e_1, \dots, e_n$, and let $T$ be the
 operator whose matrix (in this basis) is the given matrix $A$. Reading the matr
ix columns:

$$Te_1 = e_2, \quad Te_2 = e_3, \quad \dots, \quad Te_{n-1} = e_n, \quad Te_n =
-a_0 e_1 - a_1 e_2 - \cdots - a_{n-1} e_n.$$

Equivalently, $T e_i = e_{i+1}$ for $i < n$, and
$$T e_n = -a_0 e_1 - a_1 e_2 - \cdots - a_{n-1} e_n.$$

---

## Step 1. The characteristic polynomial is $p(t)$

Expand $\det(tI - A)$ along the first row. The matrix $tI - A$ is

$$\begin{pmatrix} t & & & & a_0 \\ -1 & t & & & a_1 \\ & \ddots & \ddots & & \vd
ots \\ & & -1 & t & a_{n-2} \\ & & & -1 & t-a_{n-1} \end{pmatrix}.$$

Expanding along the first row, the $(1,1)$ cofactor is $\det(tI - A_{n-1})$ wher
e $A_{n-1}$ is the same companion-type block of size $n-1$ (with the same sub-di
agonal of $-1$'s and the same structure). The $(1,n)$ entry $a_0$ multiplies the
 cofactor obtained by deleting row 1 and column $n$, which is a lower-triangular
 matrix with all diagonal entries $-1$, giving determinant $(-1)^{n-1}$, and the
 cofactor sign is $(-1)^{1+n}$. So that term contributes $a_0 \cdot (-1)^{1+n} \
cdot (-1)^{n-1} = a_0$.

By induction on $n$ (base case $n=1$: $\det(t - (-a_0)) = t + a_0$), the $(1,1)$
 cofactor equals $t^{n-1} + a_{n-1}t^{n-2} + \cdots + a_1$. Hence

$$\det(tI - A) = t\bigl(t^{n-1} + a_{n-1}t^{n-2} + \cdots + a_1\bigr) + a_0 = t^
n + a_{n-1}t^{n-1} + \cdots + a_1 t + a_0 = p(t).$$

So the **characteristic polynomial is $p(t)$**.

---

## Step 2. The minimal polynomial is also $p(t)$

Since the minimal polynomial $m_T(t)$ divides the characteristic polynomial $p(t
)$ and shares the same irreducible factors (over $\mathbb{C}$, the same roots),
it suffices to show that $\deg m_T = n$, i.e., that no polynomial of degree $< n
$ annihilates $T$.

Suppose $q(T) = 0$ for some polynomial $q(t) = c_0 + c_1 t + \cdots + c_{n-1} t^
{n-1}$ of degree $\le n-1$. Apply $q(T)$ to the cyclic vector $e_1$:

$$0 = q(T)e_1 = c_0 e_1 + c_1 Te_1 + \cdots + c_{n-1} T^{n-1} e_1.$$

Using $T^k e_1 = e_{k+1}$ for $0 \le k \le n-1$:

$$0 = c_0 e_1 + c_1 e_2 + \cdots + c_{n-1} e_n.$$

Since $\{e_1, \dots, e_n\}$ is linearly independent, $c_0 = c_1 = \cdots = c_{n-
1} = 0$, so $q = 0$.

Therefore no nonzero polynomial of degree $< n$ annihilates $T$, which forces $\
deg m_T \ge n$. Since $m_T \mid p$ and $\deg p = n$, we conclude

$$m_T(t) = p(t).$$

---

## Conclusion

The operator $T$ is **cyclic** (generated by $e_1$), and for a cyclic operator t
he minimal and characteristic polynomials coincide. Both equal:

$$\boxed{m_T(t) = \chi_T(t) = t^n + a_{n-1}t^{n-1} + \cdots + a_1 t + a_0.}$$
