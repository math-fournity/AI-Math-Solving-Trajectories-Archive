## Proof

**Setup.** The OU process satisfies $dV(t) = -\lambda V(t)\,dt + \sigma\,dW(t)$
with $\lambda, \sigma > 0$. Its explicit solution is
$$V(t) = V(0)\,e^{-\lambda t} + \sigma\int_0^t e^{-\lambda(t-s)}\,dW(s).$$

The position is $X(t) = X(0) + \int_0^t V(s)\,ds$, and the rescaled process is $
X_n(t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}X(nt)$.

**Step 1: Explicit formula for $X(t)$.** Substituting the OU solution and applyi
ng stochastic Fubini:
$$X(t) = X(0) + V(0)\frac{1-e^{-\lambda t}}{\lambda} + \sigma\int_0^t\!\int_0^s
e^{-\lambda(s-u)}\,dW(u)\,ds = X(0) + V(0)\frac{1-e^{-\lambda t}}{\lambda} + \fr
ac{\sigma}{\lambda}\int_0^t\bigl(1-e^{-\lambda(t-u)}\bigr)\,dW(u).$$

**Step 2: Decompose $X_n(t)$.** Replacing $t$ by $nt$ and dividing by $\sqrt{n}$

$$X_n(t) = \underbrace{\frac{X(0)}{\sqrt{n}}}_{\text{(I)}} + \underbrace{\frac{V
(0)}{\sqrt{n}}\frac{1-e^{-\lambda nt}}{\lambda}}_{\text{(II)}} + \underbrace{\fr
ac{\sigma}{\lambda\sqrt{n}}\int_0^{nt} dW(u)}_{\text{(III)}} - \underbrace{\frac
{\sigma}{\lambda\sqrt{n}}\int_0^{nt} e^{-\lambda(nt-u)}\,dW(u)}_{\text{(IV)}}.$$

**Step 3: The key term (III).** By Brownian scaling, $\widetilde{W}_n(t) := \fra
c{W(nt)}{\sqrt{n}}$ is a standard Brownian motion for every $n$. Thus
$$\text{(III)} = \frac{\sigma}{\lambda}\,\widetilde{W}_n(t),$$
which has the **same law** as $\frac{\sigma}{\lambda}W(t)$ for each $n$.

**Step 4: The remainder vanishes.** Terms (I) and (II) converge to $0$ a.s. (ass
uming $X(0), V(0)$ have finite second moments), since they are $O(1/\sqrt{n})$.

For term (IV), compute its $L^2$-norm via Itô isometry:
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\int_0^{nt} e^{-\lambda(nt-u)}\,dW(u
)\right|^2\right] = \frac{1}{n}\int_0^{nt} e^{-2\lambda(nt-u)}\,du = \frac{1}{n}
\cdot\frac{1-e^{-2\lambda nt}}{2\lambda} \;\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\; 0.$$

For **uniform** convergence on compact $[0,T]$, apply Doob's maximal inequality
to the martingale $M_n(t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\int_0^{nt} e^{-\lambda(nt-u)}\,dW
(u)$:
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sup_{0\le t\le T} M_n(t)^2\right] \le \frac{4}{n}\sup_{0\le
 t\le T}\int_0^{nt} e^{-2\lambda(nt-u)}\,du = \frac{4}{n}\cdot\frac{1}{2\lambda}
 = \frac{2}{n\lambda}\;\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\;0.$$

Hence $\sup_{0\le t\le T}|(\text{IV})| \to 0$ in $L^2$, and therefore in probabi
lity.

**Step 5: Identify the limit.** We have shown
$$X_n(t) = \frac{\sigma}{\lambda}\,\widetilde{W}_n(t) + R_n(t), \qquad \sup_{0\l
e t\le T}|R_n(t)| \xrightarrow{P} 0,$$
where $\widetilde{W}_n$ is a standard Brownian motion (in law, identically distr
ibuted for all $n$). By the converging-together lemma (Slutsky's theorem on path

W}_n \stackrel{d}{=} W$ (standard Brownian motion),
$$\boxed{X_n \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{\sigma}{\lambda}\,W \quad\text{as } n\to\inft
y,}$$
where $W$ is a standard Brownian motion.

**Verification of the diffusion coefficient.** The stationary variance of the OU

$$\text{Var}(X_n(t)) = \frac{1}{n}\cdot\frac{\sigma^2}{2\lambda}\int_0^{nt}\!\in

which matches $\text{Var}\!\left(\frac{\sigma}{\lambda}W(t)\right) = \frac{\sigm
