## Solution

**Setup.** The hexagon has 6 vertices. Its 6 sides are fixed red. The remaining
$\binom{6}{2}-6 = 9$ diagonals are each colored red or blue. A "bad" triangle (a
ll-blue) can only arise from 3 vertices whose 3 pairwise edges are **all diagona
ls** (since any side is red). So we need: the blue-diagonal graph is triangle-fr
ee.

**The diagonal graph.** Let $D$ be the graph on vertices $\{1,\dots,6\}$ whose e
dges are the 9 diagonals (i.e., $D = K_6 \setminus C_6$, the complement of the 6
-cycle). A triangle in $D$ corresponds to 3 vertices pairwise non-adjacent in $C
_6$, i.e., an independent set of size 3 in $C_6$.

The independent sets of size 3 in $C_6 = 1\text{-}2\text{-}3\text{-}4\text{-}5\t
ext{-}6\text{-}1$ are exactly:
$$T_1 = \{1,3,5\}, \qquad T_2 = \{2,4,6\}.$$
(Any other 3-subset contains an adjacent pair, hence a side.) So $D$ has exactly
 **2 triangles**, with edge sets:
- $T_1$: $(1,3),(1,5),(3,5)$
- $T_2$: $(2,4),(2,6),(4,6)$

These two edge sets are **disjoint** (3+3 = 6 distinct edges).

**Counting via inclusion–exclusion.** We count subsets $S$ of the 9 diagonal-edg
es that contain **neither** all 3 edges of $T_1$ **nor** all 3 edges of $T_2$.

- Total subsets: $2^9 = 512$.
- Subsets containing all of $T_1$'s edges (3 fixed, 6 free): $2^6 = 64$.
- Subsets containing all of $T_2$'s edges (3 fixed, 6 free): $2^6 = 64$.
- Subsets containing both (6 fixed edges, 3 free): $2^3 = 8$.

$$N = 512 - 64 - 64 + 8 = 392.$$

**Verification of the triangle structure.** Every triangle in $D$ is one of $T_1
, T_2$; avoiding both guarantees every 3-vertex set has at least one red edge (e
ither a red side, or a red diagonal). ✓

**Final computation.** The digits of $N = 392$ are $3, 9, 2$:
$$3^2 + 9^2 + 2^2 = 9 + 81 + 4 = \boxed{94}.$$
