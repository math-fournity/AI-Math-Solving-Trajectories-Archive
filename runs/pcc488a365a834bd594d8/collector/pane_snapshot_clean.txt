## Proof

Let $G = (0,\infty)$ under multiplication. We show that the only compact subgrou
p of $G$ is the trivial subgroup $\{1\}$.

**Step 1: Reduce to $(\mathbb{R}, +)$.**

The logarithm map
$$\varphi : (0,\infty) \to \mathbb{R}, \qquad \varphi(x) = \ln x$$
is a group isomorphism from $(G, \cdot)$ to $(\mathbb{R}, +)$:
- It is a bijection (with inverse $\varphi^{-1}(t) = e^t$).
- It is a homomorphism: $\varphi(xy) = \ln(xy) = \ln x + \ln y = \varphi(x) + \v
arphi(y)$.

Moreover $\varphi$ and $\varphi^{-1}$ are both continuous, so $\varphi$ is a hom
eomorphism. Hence a subgroup $H \leq G$ is compact if and only if $\varphi(H)$ i
s a compact subgroup of $(\mathbb{R}, +)$. Thus compact subgroups of $G$ are in
bijection with compact subgroups of $(\mathbb{R}, +)$.

**Step 2: The only compact subgroup of $(\mathbb{R}, +)$ is $\{0\}$.**

Let $K \leq (\mathbb{R}, +)$ be a subgroup. If $K = \{0\}$ we are done. Suppose
$K \neq \{0\}$, and pick $a \in K$ with $a \neq 0$. Since $K$ is a subgroup, $-a
 \in K$ as well, so replacing $a$ by $-a$ if necessary we may assume $a > 0$.

For every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, the closure property under addition gives $na \in
K$. The sequence $(na)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ satisfies $na \to +\infty$, so the se
t $\{na : n \in \mathbb{N}\} \subseteq K$ is unbounded. Therefore $K$ is unbound
ed.

A compact subset of $\mathbb{R}$ (with the usual topology) must be closed and bo
unded. Since $K$ is unbounded, $K$ is not compact.

Therefore the only compact subgroup of $(\mathbb{R}, +)$ is $\{0\}$.

**Step 3: Conclude for $G$.**

By the correspondence in Step 1, the compact subgroups of $G$ are exactly $\varp
hi^{-1}(K)$ where $K$ ranges over compact subgroups of $(\mathbb{R}, +)$. By Ste
p 2 the only such $K$ is $\{0\}$, and
$$\varphi^{-1}(\{0\}) = \{e^{0}\} = \{1\}.$$

Hence $\{1\}$ is the unique compact subgroup of $G = (0,\infty)$ under multiplic
ation.

**Answer to the question:** Yes, $\{1\}$ is the only compact subgroup of $G$.
