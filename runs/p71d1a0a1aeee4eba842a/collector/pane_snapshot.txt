## Solution

We compute the Cauchy principal value
$$I = \text{PV}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{x}{(x^2+4)(x-1)}\,dx = \lim_{\epsil
on\to 0^+}\int_{S_\epsilon}\frac{x}{(x^2+4)(x-1)}\,dx.$$

**Setup.** Let $f(z)=\dfrac{z}{(z^2+4)(z-1)}=\dfrac{z}{(z-2i)(z+2i)(z-1)}$, with
 simple poles at $z=1$ (on the real axis) and $z=\pm 2i$.

**Contour.** For $R\gg 1$ and $\epsilon\ll 1$, take the positively oriented cont
our $\gamma$ consisting of:
- the real segment $[-R,\,1-\epsilon]$,
- the small semicircle $C_\epsilon:\ z=1+\epsilon e^{i\theta},\ \theta\in[\pi,0]
$ (upper half-plane, traversed **clockwise**),
- the real segment $[1+\epsilon,\,R]$,
- the large semicircle $\Gamma_R:\ z=Re^{i\theta},\ \theta\in[0,\pi]$ (upper hal
f-plane, counterclockwise).

The indentation places $z=1$ **outside** $\gamma$, so the only pole enclosed is
$z=2i$.

**Residue theorem.**
$$\int_{-R}^{1-\epsilon}f(x)\,dx+\int_{C_\epsilon}f(z)\,dz+\int_{1+\epsilon}^{R}
f(x)\,dx+\int_{\Gamma_R}f(z)\,dz=2\pi i\,\mathrm{Res}(f,2i).$$

**Large arc.** Since $|f(z)|=O(1/R^2)$ on $\Gamma_R$, Jordan/estimation gives $\
displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\Gamma_R}f(z)\,dz=0$.

**Small arc.** Near $z=1$, $f(z)=\dfrac{\mathrm{Res}(f,1)}{z-1}+O(1)$. The clock
wise half-circle contributes
$$\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{C_\epsilon}f(z)\,dz=-\pi i\,\mathrm{Res}(f,1).$$

**Residues.**
$$\mathrm{Res}(f,1)=\lim_{z\to 1}(z-1)f(z)=\frac{1}{1^2+4}=\frac{1}{5}.$$
$$\mathrm{Res}(f,2i)=\lim_{z\to 2i}(z-2i)f(z)=\frac{2i}{(2i+2i)(2i-1)}=\frac{2i}
{4i(2i-1)}=\frac{1}{2(2i-1)}=\frac{1}{-2+4i}=\frac{-1-2i}{10}.$$

**Assemble.** Taking $R\to\infty$ and $\epsilon\to 0$:
$$I-\pi i\cdot\mathrm{Res}(f,1)=2\pi i\cdot\mathrm{Res}(f,2i),$$
$$I=2\pi i\cdot\frac{-1-2i}{10}+\pi i\cdot\frac{1}{5}=\frac{-2\pi i-4\pi i^2}{10
}+\frac{\pi i}{5}=\frac{4\pi-2\pi i}{10}+\frac{2\pi i}{10}=\frac{4\pi}{10}=\frac
{2\pi}{5}.$$

**Verification (real-valued sanity check).** Partial fractions give
$$\frac{x}{(x^2+4)(x-1)}=\frac{-x+4}{5(x^2+4)}+\frac{1}{5(x-1)}.$$
The PV of $\frac{1}{x-1}$ over $\mathbb{R}$ is $0$ (logarithmic cancellation), $
\int\frac{-x}{x^2+4}dx=0$ (odd integrand), and $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dx}
{x^2+4}=\frac{\pi}{2}$, so
$$I=\frac{4}{5}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}=\frac{2\pi}{5},\qquad\checkmark$$
consistent with the residue computation.

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^+}\int_{S_\epsilon}\frac{x}{(x^2+4)(x-
1)}\,dx=\frac{2\pi}{5}}$$
