## Solution

We compute $I = \int_0^1 g(z)\,dz$ where $g(z) = z\int_1^N [t]\,e^{2\pi i t z}\,
dt$.

**Step 1: Split the inner integral.** Since $[t] = k$ for $t \in [k, k+1)$, $k =
 1,\dots,N-1$ (the endpoint $t=N$ has measure zero):

$$\int_1^N [t]\,e^{2\pi i t z}\,dt = \sum_{k=1}^{N-1} k\int_k^{k+1} e^{2\pi i t
z}\,dt.$$

**Step 2: Evaluate each piece.** For $z \neq 0$,

$$\int_k^{k+1} e^{2\pi i t z}\,dt = \frac{e^{2\pi i(k+1)z} - e^{2\pi i k z}}{2\p
i i z}.$$

Setting $a_k = e^{2\pi i k z}$ and applying summation by parts:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{N-1} k(a_{k+1}-a_k) = \sum_{k=2}^{N}(k-1)a_k - \sum_{k=1}^{N-1} k\
,a_k = (N-1)a_N - \sum_{k=1}^{N-1} a_k.$$

Therefore

$$\int_1^N [t]\,e^{2\pi i t z}\,dt = \frac{1}{2\pi i z}\left((N-1)e^{2\pi i N z}
 - \sum_{k=1}^{N-1} e^{2\pi i k z}\right).$$

**Step 3: Integrate over $z \in [0,1]$.** The factor $z$ cancels the $\frac{1}{z
}$:

$$I = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_0^1\left((N-1)e^{2\pi i N z} - \sum_{k=1}^{N-1} e^{2\
pi i k z}\right)dz.$$

(The integrand is bounded near $z=0$ since the original $g(z)$ is continuous the
re, so the cancellation is legitimate.)

**Step 4: Use orthogonality.** For any nonzero integer $m$,

$$\int_0^1 e^{2\pi i m z}\,dz = \frac{e^{2\pi i m}-1}{2\pi i m} = 0.$$

Since $N$ is a positive integer, $N \geq 1$, so $N \neq 0$; and each $k \in \{1,
\dots,N-1\}$ is nonzero. Hence every term integrates to $0$:

$$I = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\big((N-1)\cdot 0 - 0\big) = 0.$$

**Verification.** For $N=1$: $g(z)=z\int_1^1[\cdots]=0$, so $I=0$. ✓  For $N=2$:
 $g(z)=\frac{e^{4\pi i z}-e^{2\pi i z}}{2\pi i}$, and $\int_0^1 g(z)\,dz = \frac
{0-0}{2\pi i}=0$. ✓

$$\boxed{\int_0^1 g(z)\,dz = 0}$$
