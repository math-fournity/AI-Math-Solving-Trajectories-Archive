## Proof

Let $a_i = f^{(i)}(1)$, i.e. $a_1=f(1),\ a_2=f(f(1)),\ a_3=f(f(f(1)))$.

**Step 1 — A relation among $a_1,a_2,a_3$.** Set $w=x=y=z=1$:
$$a_3\cdot f\!\big(f(f(1))\big)=f(f(1))\cdot f(1)\;\Longrightarrow\;a_3\cdot a_3
=a_2\,a_1,$$
so
$$a_3^2=a_1a_2.\tag{1}$$

**Step 2 — The $z=1$ specialization.** Setting $z=1$ (and $f(1)=a_1$) in the giv
en equation:
$$a_3\,f\!\big(wx\,f(ya_1)\big)=f(xf(y))\,f(w)\qquad\forall\,w,x,y.\tag{A}$$

**Step 3 — Two further specializations of (A).** Setting $y=1$ in (A) and using
$f(a_1)=a_2$:
$$a_3\,f(wxa_2)=f(xa_1)\,f(w)\qquad\forall\,w,x.\tag{A3}$$
Setting $x=1$ in (A3):
$$a_3\,f(wa_2)=f(a_1)\,f(w)=a_2\,f(w),$$
hence
$$f(wa_2)=\frac{a_2}{a_3}\,f(w)\qquad\forall\,w.\tag{E}$$

**Step 4 — Multiplicativity up to a constant.** Apply (E) with $w\leftarrow wx$:
 $f(wxa_2)=\frac{a_2}{a_3}f(wx)$. Substituting into (A3):
$$a_3\cdot\frac{a_2}{a_3}f(wx)=f(xa_1)f(w)\;\Longrightarrow\;a_2\,f(wx)=f(xa_1)\
,f(w).\tag{*}$$
For fixed $x$, equation $(*)$ shows $\dfrac{f(wx)}{f(w)}=\dfrac{f(xa_1)}{a_2}$ i
s independent of $w$. Setting $w=1$ gives $\dfrac{f(x)}{a_1}=\dfrac{f(xa_1)}{a_2
}$, and therefore
$$f(wx)=\frac{f(w)\,f(x)}{a_1}\qquad\forall\,w,x\in\mathbb N.\tag{M}$$

**Step 5 — Reduction to a completely multiplicative function.** Define $g(n)=f(n
)/a_1$. Then $g(1)=1$ and (M) becomes $g(wx)=g(w)g(x)$; thus $g$ is completely m
ultiplicative, and $f(n)=a_1\,g(n)$.

**Step 6 — Substituting back.** Using $f(n)=a_1g(n)$ and complete multiplicativi
ty of $g$:
- $f(f(f(z)))=a_1\,g(a_1)^2\,g(z)$,
- $f(yf(z))=a_1\,g(a_1)\,g(y)\,g(z)$,
- $f(wxf(yf(z)))=a_1\,g(a_1)^2\,g(w)\,g(x)\,g(y)\,g(z)$,
- $f(xf(y))=a_1\,g(a_1)\,g(x)\,g(y)$,
- $f(w)=a_1\,g(w)$.

The original equation becomes
$$\bigl[a_1g(a_1)^2g(z)\bigr]\bigl[a_1g(a_1)^2g(w)g(x)g(y)g(z)\bigr]=z^2\bigl[a_
1g(a_1)g(x)g(y)\bigr]\bigl[a_1g(w)\bigr],$$
i.e.
$$a_1^2\,g(a_1)^4\,g(w)g(x)g(y)g(z)^2=a_1^2\,g(a_1)\,g(w)g(x)g(y)\,z^2.$$
Canceling the positive common factor $a_1^2\,g(w)g(x)g(y)$:
$$g(a_1)^3\,g(z)^2=z^2\qquad\forall\,z\in\mathbb N.\tag{**}$$

**Step 7 — Determining $g$ and $f$.** Setting $z=1$ in (**): $g(a_1)^3=1$, so $g
(a_1)=1$ (a positive integer). Then (**) yields $g(z)^2=z^2$, hence $g(z)=z$ for
 every $z\in\mathbb N$. Thus $g=\mathrm{id}$, and $f(n)=a_1\,g(n)=a_1n$. Finally
 $g(a_1)=a_1=1$, so $a_1=1$ and
$$f(n)=n\qquad\forall\,n\in\mathbb N.$$

**Conclusion.** The identity function is the unique solution of the functional e
quation (one checks directly that it satisfies the equation). Consequently
$$f(n!)=n!\ge n!$$
for every positive integer $n$. $\blacksquare$
