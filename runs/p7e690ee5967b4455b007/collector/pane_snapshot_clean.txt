## Proof

We are given that $\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{p_{n+1}}{p_{n}}=1$, where $p_k$ denote
s the $k$-th prime. We wish to determine whether this implies
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log p_{n+1}}{\log p_{n}}=1.$$

**Claim: Yes, the implication holds.**

**Proof.** Write $p_{n+1} = p_n \cdot r_n$ where $r_n := \frac{p_{n+1}}{p_n}$. B
y hypothesis $r_n \to 1$.

Taking logarithms,
$$\log p_{n+1} = \log p_n + \log r_n.$$

Therefore
$$\frac{\log p_{n+1}}{\log p_n} = \frac{\log p_n + \log r_n}{\log p_n} = 1 + \fr
ac{\log r_n}{\log p_n}.$$

Now we analyze the two factors in the remainder term $\frac{\log r_n}{\log p_n}$
:

- Since $r_n \to 1$ and $\log$ is continuous at $1$ with $\log 1 = 0$, we have $
\log r_n \to \log 1 = 0$.
- Since $(p_n)$ is the sequence of primes, $p_n \to \infty$ (there are infinitel
y many primes and the sequence is strictly increasing). Hence $\log p_n \to \inf
ty$.

Thus
$$\frac{\log r_n}{\log p_n} \to \frac{0}{\infty} = 0.$$

Consequently,
$$\frac{\log p_{n+1}}{\log p_n} = 1 + \frac{\log r_n}{\log p_n} \to 1 + 0 = 1.$$

**Remark.** The argument uses only two facts: (i) the hypothesis $p_{n+1}/p_n \t
o 1$, and (ii) $p_n \to \infty$ (which follows from the infinitude of primes). N
o deep result such as the Prime Number Theorem is required. In fact, the same co
nclusion holds for *any* sequence $(a_n)$ of positive reals with $a_n \to \infty
$ and $a_{n+1}/a_n \to 1$: one always gets $\log a_{n+1}/\log a_n \to 1$ by the
identical computation.
