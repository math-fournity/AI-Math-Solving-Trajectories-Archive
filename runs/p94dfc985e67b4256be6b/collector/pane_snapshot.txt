# Solution

The function $f(n)$ — the sum of all exponents in the prime factorization of $n$
 — is the classical **total number of prime factors counted with multiplicity**,
 denoted $\Omega(n)$. The sign $(-1)^{f(n)} = (-1)^{\Omega(n)}$ is the **Liouvil
le function** $\lambda(n)$.

So we must evaluate
$$S = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda(n)}{n}.$$

## Step 1. Dirichlet series of the Liouville function

The Liouville function is completely multiplicative, with $\lambda(p) = -1$ for
every prime $p$. Hence its Dirichlet series is, for $\Re(s) > 1$:
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda(n)}{n^s} = \prod_{p} \frac{1}{1 - \lambda(p)
p^{-s}} = \prod_{p} \frac{1}{1 + p^{-s}} = \prod_{p} \frac{1 - p^{-s}}{1 - p^{-2
s}} = \frac{\zeta(2s)}{\zeta(s)}.$$

## Step 2. Limit as $s \to 1^{+}$

Set $s = 1 + \varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon \to 0^{+}$. Then
$$\frac{\zeta(2+2\varepsilon)}{\zeta(1+\varepsilon)}.$$
As $\varepsilon \to 0^{+}$:
- $\zeta(1+\varepsilon) \to +\infty$ (pole of $\zeta$ at $s=1$),
- $\zeta(2+2\varepsilon) \to \zeta(2) = \frac{\pi^2}{6}$ (finite).

Therefore
$$\lim_{\varepsilon \to 0^{+}} \frac{\zeta(2+2\varepsilon)}{\zeta(1+\varepsilon)
} = 0.$$

## Step 3. Passing the limit inside the series (Abel's theorem for Dirichlet ser
ies)

A standard analogue of Abel's theorem for Dirichlet series states:

> If $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n$ converges (as an ordinary series) to $L$, and $F(
s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n n^{-s}$ converges for $s > 0$, then $\lim_{s \to 0^
{+}} F(s) = L$.

Apply this with $a_n = \frac{\lambda(n)}{n}$, so that
$$F(\varepsilon) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda(n)}{n^{1+\varepsilon}} = \f
rac{\zeta(2+2\varepsilon)}{\zeta(1+\varepsilon)}, \qquad \varepsilon > 0.$$

Thus, **provided** $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda(n)}{n}$ converges, its val
ue equals $\lim_{\varepsilon \to 0^{+}} F(\varepsilon) = 0$.

## Step 4. Convergence of the series

The convergence of $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda(n)}{n}$ is a classical equ
ivalent form of the **Prime Number Theorem**. The chain of equivalences is:

- PNT: $\pi(x) \sim \frac{x}{\ln x}$.
- Equivalently (via Möbius inversion / Chebyshev): $M(x) = \sum_{n \le x} \mu(n)
 = o(x)$.
- Equivalently: $L(x) = \sum_{n \le x} \lambda(n) = o(x)$ (the Liouville form of
 PNT; the equivalence between the $\mu$ and $\lambda$ forms follows from $\lambd
a(n) = \sum_{d^2 \mid n} \mu(n/d^2)$, which gives $L(x) = \sum_{d \le \sqrt{x}}
M(x/d^2)$, so $M(x) = o(x) \iff L(x) = o(x)$).

By partial summation (Abel summation), with $L(x) = o(x)$:
$$\sum_{n \le N} \frac{\lambda(n)}{n} = \frac{L(N)}{N} + \int_{1}^{N} \frac{L(t)
}{t^2}\,dt.$$
Since $L(t) = o(t)$, we have $\frac{L(N)}{N} \to 0$, and $\int_{1}^{\infty} \fra
c{L(t)}{t^2}\,dt$ converges (split at $T$: on $[T,\infty)$, $|L(t)| \le \delta t
$ for any $\delta > 0$ once $T$ is large, giving a tail bounded by $\delta$). He
nce the partial sums converge, i.e. $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda(n)}{n}$ c
onverges.

## Step 5. Conclusion

Since the series converges, by the Abel theorem for Dirichlet series (Step 3),
$$S = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda(n)}{n} = \lim_{\varepsilon \to 0^{+}} \f
rac{\zeta(2+2\varepsilon)}{\zeta(1+\varepsilon)} = \frac{\zeta(2)}{\infty} = 0.$
$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{f(n)}}{n} = 0}$$
