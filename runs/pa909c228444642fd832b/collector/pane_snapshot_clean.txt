Yes. The isomorphism of $p$ does imply that $G$ is the internal direct product o

**Proof.**

Let $Q_i = G/H^i$ and let $\varphi = p : G \to Q_1 \times Q_2 \times Q_3$ be the
 given isomorphism. Denote by $\mathrm{pr}_i : Q_1\times Q_2\times Q_3 \to Q_i$
the $i$-th projection, and by $\pi_i : G \to G/H^i$ the quotient map. By definit
ion of $p$,
$$\mathrm{pr}_i \circ \varphi = \pi_i \qquad (i=1,2,3).$$

Since $\varphi$ is an isomorphism, $\varphi(\ker \pi_i) = \ker \mathrm{pr}_i$. B
ecause $H_2, H_3$ are normal, $H^1 = \langle H_2 \cup H_3\rangle = H_2H_3$, and
likewise $H^2 = H_1H_3$, $H^3 = H_1H_2$. Also $\ker \pi_1 = H^1 = H_2H_3$ and $\
ker \mathrm{pr}_1 = \{e\}\times Q_2\times Q_3$. Hence
$$\varphi(H_2H_3) = \{e\}\times Q_2\times Q_3. \tag{1}$$

Now observe where each $H_i$ lands. Since $H_2 \subseteq H^1 = \ker\pi_1$ and $H
_2 \subseteq H^3 = H_1H_2 = \ker\pi_3$, we have $\varphi(H_2) \subseteq \{e\}\ti
mes Q_2\times\{e\}$; similarly $\varphi(H_3)\subseteq \{e\}\times\{e\}\times Q_3
$, $\varphi(H_1)\subseteq Q_1\times\{e\}\times\{e\}$. Set $K_i := \varphi(H_i)$,
 so these lie in the corresponding single factors. Then
$$\varphi(H_2H_3) = \varphi(H_2)\varphi(H_3) = K_2\cdot K_3 = \{e\}\times K_2\ti
mes K_3.$$
Comparing with (1) gives $K_2 = Q_2$ and $K_3 = Q_3$.

Applying the identical argument to $H^2 = H_1H_3$ (using $\ker\mathrm{pr}_2 = Q_
1\times\{e\}\times Q_3$) yields $K_1 = Q_1$ and $K_3 = Q_3$, and to $H^3 = H_1H_
2$ yields $K_1 = Q_1$ and $K_2 = Q_2$. In summary,
$$K_1 = Q_1,\qquad K_2 = Q_2,\qquad K_3 = Q_3. \tag{2}$$

Since the $K_i$ sit in distinct factors of the direct product, $\varphi(H_1H_2H_
3) = K_1\times K_2\times K_3 = Q_1\times Q_2\times Q_3 = \varphi(G)$. By injecti
vity of $\varphi$,
$$H_1H_2H_3 = G. \tag{3}$$

Finally, using (2) and (1),
$$\varphi(H_1 \cap H_2H_3) = \varphi(H_1)\cap \varphi(H_2H_3) = (Q_1\times\{e\}\
times\{e\}) \cap (\{e\}\times Q_2\times Q_3) = \{(e,e,e)\}.$$
Injectivity of $\varphi$ gives $H_1\cap H_2H_3 = \{e\}$. Cyclically,
$$H_2\cap H_1H_3 = \{e\},\qquad H_3\cap H_1H_2 = \{e\}. \tag{4}$$

Combining the hypothesis that each $H_i\trianglelefteq G$ with (3) and (4), we c
onclude that $G$ is the internal direct product of $H_1$, $H_2$, and $H_3$. $\bl
acksquare$
