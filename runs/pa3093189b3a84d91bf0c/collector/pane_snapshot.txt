# Simplicial Homology of the Δ-complex X

## Step 1: Identify the simplices after quotient

**Vertices.** In Δ₀², all three edges are identified to a single edge. The Δ-com
plex face maps are order-preserving, so identifying the 0th face [v₀,v₁] and the
 1st face [v₁,v₂] of Δ₀² to the same edge forces v₁ to be both the second endpoi
nt (from the 0th face) and the first endpoint (from the 1st face), collapsing al
l three vertices to one point *w*. The edge e₀ is a loop at *w*.

For each Δᵢ² (i > 0), the edges [v₀,v₁] and [v₁,v₂] are identified to a single e
dge eᵢ. The same order-preserving argument collapses all three vertices of Δᵢ² t
o a single point wᵢ, making eᵢ a loop at wᵢ.

The edge [v₀,v₂] of Δᵢ² is identified with the edge [v₀,v₁] of Δᵢ₋₁², which is e
ᵢ₋₁. Since [v₀,v₂] of Δᵢ² has both endpoints at wᵢ and eᵢ₋₁ has both endpoints a
t wᵢ₋₁, we get wᵢ = wᵢ₋₁. By induction, **all vertices collapse to a single vert
ex *w***.

**Summary of the Δ-complex structure:**
- 1 vertex: *w*
- n+1 edges: e₀, e₁, …, eₙ (each a loop at *w*)
- n+1 2-simplices: Δ₀², Δ₁², …, Δₙ²

## Step 2: Compute the boundary maps

**∂₁ : C₁ → C₀.** Each eᵢ is a loop at *w*, so ∂₁(eᵢ) = w − w = 0. Thus **∂₁ = 0
**.

**∂₂ : C₂ → C₁.** For a 2-simplex [v₀,v₁,v₂], the boundary is [v₁,v₂] − [v₀,v₂]
+ [v₀,v₁].

- **Δ₀²:** all three edges equal e₀, so ∂₂(Δ₀²) = e₀ − e₀ + e₀ = **e₀**.
- **Δᵢ² (i ≥ 1):** [v₀,v₁] = eᵢ, [v₁,v₂] = eᵢ, [v₀,v₂] = eᵢ₋₁, so ∂₂(Δᵢ²) = eᵢ −
 eᵢ₋₁ + eᵢ = **2eᵢ − eᵢ₋₁**.

The matrix of ∂₂ (columns indexed by Δ₀²,…,Δₙ²; rows by e₀,…,eₙ) is:

$$\partial_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & -1 & \cdots &
0 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & & & \ddots & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots &
2 \end{pmatrix}$$

This is upper triangular with diagonal (1, 2, 2, …, 2), so **det(∂₂) = 2ⁿ ≠ 0**.

## Step 3: Compute homology

**H₀(X) = ker ∂₀ / im ∂₁.** ker ∂₀ = C₀ = ℤ, im ∂₁ = 0. So **H₀(X) = ℤ**.

**H₂(X) = ker ∂₂ / im ∂₃.** Since det(∂₂) ≠ 0, ∂₂ is injective, so ker ∂₂ = 0. T
hus **H₂(X) = 0**.

**H₁(X) = ker ∂₁ / im ∂₂.** Since ∂₁ = 0, ker ∂₁ = C₁ = ℤⁿ⁺¹. The image of ∂₂ is
 a full-rank sublattice of ℤⁿ⁺¹ of index |det ∂₂| = 2ⁿ. To determine the group s
tructure, compute the **Smith normal form** of ∂₂.

**Key claim:** For each 1 ≤ k ≤ n, there exists a k×k minor of ∂₂ equal to ±1.

*Proof of claim:* Take the k×k submatrix on rows {0,1,…,k−1} and columns {1,2,…,
k}:

$$B_k = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 2 & -1 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & \c
dots & 0 \\ \vdots & & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & -1 \end{pmatrix}$$

This is lower triangular with all diagonal entries −1, so det(Bₖ) = (−1)ᵏ = ±1.
∎

Therefore:
- d₁ = gcd of all entries = 1.
- d₁d₂ = gcd of all 2×2 minors = 1 (since B₂ gives a minor of 1), so d₂ = 1.
- d₁d₂⋯dₖ = gcd of all k×k minors = 1 for each k ≤ n (by the claim), so d₃ = ⋯ =
 dₙ = 1.
- d₁d₂⋯dₙ₊₁ = |det ∂₂| = 2ⁿ, so dₙ₊₁ = 2ⁿ.

The Smith normal form is **diag(1, 1, …, 1, 2ⁿ)** (with n entries of 1). Hence:

$$H_1(X) = \mathbb{Z}^{n+1}/\operatorname{im}(\partial_2) \cong \underbrace{\mat
hbb{Z}/1\mathbb{Z} \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{Z}/1\mathbb{Z}}_{n} \oplus \math
bb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z}$$

**Hₖ(X) = 0 for k ≥ 3** (no simplices in those dimensions).

## Result

$$\boxed{H_k(X) = \begin{cases} \mathbb{Z} & k = 0 \\ \mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z} &
 k = 1 \\ 0 & k \geq 2 \end{cases}}$$
