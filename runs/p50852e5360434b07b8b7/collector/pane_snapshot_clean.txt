## Proof

**Claim:** In general, an $n$-dimensional Alexandrov space with curvature $\geq
k$ does **not** satisfy a reverse doubling condition. That is, there need not ex
ist a constant $C > 1$ such that $\mathrm{vol}(B_x(2r)) \geq C \cdot \mathrm{vol
}(B_x(r))$ for all $x \in X$ and $0 < r < \mathrm{diam}(X)/2$.

### Counterexample (1-dimensional case, $n = 1$, $k = 0$)

Let $X = [0, 1]$ be the closed unit interval equipped with the standard Euclidea
n metric. This is a complete length space, and every 1-dimensional complete leng
th space is an Alexandrov space with curvature $\geq k$ for any $k \in \mathbb{R
}$ (triangle comparison conditions are vacuous in dimension 1). In particular, $
X$ is a 1-dimensional Alexandrov space with curvature $\geq 0$.

The diameter is $\mathrm{diam}(X) = 1$, so the condition requires $0 < r < \frac
{1}{2}$.

Take $x = \frac{1}{2}$ (the midpoint) and set $r = \frac{1}{2} - \varepsilon$ fo
r any $0 < \varepsilon < \frac{1}{4}$.

**Computing $B_x(r)$:**
$$B_x(r) = \left[\tfrac{1}{2} - r,\; \tfrac{1}{2} + r\right] \cap [0,1] = \left[
\tfrac{1}{2} - (\tfrac{1}{2}-\varepsilon),\; \tfrac{1}{2} + (\tfrac{1}{2}-\varep
silon)\right] \cap [0,1] = [\varepsilon,\; 1-\varepsilon].$$
Thus $\mathrm{vol}(B_x(r)) = 1 - 2\varepsilon$.

**Computing $B_x(2r)$:**
$$2r = 1 - 2\varepsilon, \quad B_x(2r) = \left[\tfrac{1}{2} - (1-2\varepsilon),\
; \tfrac{1}{2} + (1-2\varepsilon)\right] \cap [0,1] = \left[2\varepsilon - \tfra
c{1}{2},\; \tfrac{3}{2} - 2\varepsilon\right] \cap [0,1].$$
For $\varepsilon < \frac{1}{4}$, we have $2\varepsilon - \frac{1}{2} < 0$ and $\
frac{3}{2} - 2\varepsilon > 1$, so
$$B_x(2r) = [0, 1], \quad \mathrm{vol}(B_x(2r)) = 1.$$

**The ratio:**
$$\frac{\mathrm{vol}(B_x(2r))}{\mathrm{vol}(B_x(r))} = \frac{1}{1 - 2\varepsilon
}.$$

As $\varepsilon \to 0^+$ (i.e., $r \to \frac{\mathrm{diam}(X)}{2}^-$), this rati
o tends to $1$.

### Conclusion

For any candidate constant $C > 1$, choose $\varepsilon < \frac{1}{2}\left(1 - \
frac{1}{C}\right)$. Then
$$\frac{\mathrm{vol}(B_x(2r))}{\mathrm{vol}(B_x(r))} = \frac{1}{1-2\varepsilon}
< C,$$
violating the reverse doubling inequality. Since this holds for **every** $C > 1
$, no such uniform constant exists.

### Remark (higher dimensions)

The same phenomenon occurs in any dimension $n \geq 2$. For instance, $X = [0,1]
^n$ (the unit cube) is a convex subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$, hence an $n$-dimension
al Alexandrov space with curvature $\geq 0$. Taking $x$ to be the center and $r
\to \mathrm{diam}(X)/2 = \frac{\sqrt{n}}{2}^-$, the ball $B_x(r)$ covers all of
$[0,1]^n$ except $O(\varepsilon^2)$-sized regions near the $2^n$ corners (which
lie at distance $\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2}$ from the center), while $B_x(2r)$ covers th
e entire cube. The ratio $\mathrm{vol}(B_x(2r))/\mathrm{vol}(B_x(r)) \to 1$ as $
r \to \mathrm{diam}(X)/2$, so no uniform $C > 1$ exists in any dimension.

The essential obstruction is that when $r$ is close to $\mathrm{diam}(X)/2$, the
 ball $B_x(r)$ already covers most of a compact space $X$, and $B_x(2r)$ covers
essentially all of it, forcing the volume ratio toward $1$. The Bishop–Gromov co
mparison (which gives the *forward* doubling condition $\mathrm{vol}(B_x(2r)) \l
eq C' \cdot \mathrm{vol}(B_x(r))$) does not provide a lower bound on this ratio,
 and no such uniform lower bound $> 1$ exists in general.
