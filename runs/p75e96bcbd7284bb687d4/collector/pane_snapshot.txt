## Solution

A permutation with exactly one descent (exactly one index $i$ with $a_i > a_{i+1
}$) is the classical Eulerian number $A(n,1)$. I'll derive it directly for $n=20
07$.

**Structure.** If a permutation has exactly one descent, then splitting it at th
at descent yields two blocks, each internally increasing (since there are no oth
er descents). Conversely, any partition of $\{1,\dots,n\}$ into two nonempty par
ts $S$ and $T=\overline S$, with each part listed in increasing order and $S$ pl
aced first, produces a permutation whose only possible descent is at the junctio
n between the blocks.

The junction is a descent exactly when $\max S > \min T$.

**Counting.** Every nonempty proper subset $S\subsetneq\{1,\dots,n\}$ (with $S,\
overline S\neq\varnothing$) gives one such two-block permutation. There are $2^n
-2$ of these.

The junction is **not** a descent (so the permutation is fully increasing, zero
descents) exactly when $\max S < \min T$, i.e. every element of $S$ is smaller t
han every element of $T$. This forces $S=\{1,2,\dots,k\}$ for some $k$. For both
 parts to be nonempty, $k\in\{1,2,\dots,n-1\}$, giving $n-1$ subsets.

Therefore the number of permutations with **exactly one** descent is
$$
(2^n-2)-(n-1)=2^n-n-1.
$$

**Sanity check.** For $n=3$: $2^3-3-1=4$, matching $\{132,213,231,312\}$. For $n
=4$: $2^4-4-1=11=A(4,1)$, the Eulerian number. ✓

**Substituting $n=2007$:**
$$
2^{2007}-2007-1 = 2^{2007}-2008.
$$

**Required form.** Write $2^{2007}-2008 = 4\cdot 2^{2005} - 251\cdot 2^{3}$, sin
ce $4\cdot 2^{2005}=2^{2007}$ and $251\cdot 8=2008$.

So with
$$
a=4,\ b=2,\ c=2005,\ d=-251,\ e=2,\ f=3,
$$
we have $a\cdot b^c + d\cdot e^f = 4\cdot 2^{2005} + (-251)\cdot 2^3 = 2^{2007}-
2008$, with $a\nmid b$ (since $4\nmid 2$) and $d\nmid e$ (since $-251\nmid 2$).

$$
\boxed{2^{2007}-2008}
$$
