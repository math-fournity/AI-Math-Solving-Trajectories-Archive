# Computing $\pi_1(Y)$

## Setup

Let $X = S^2 \times [0,1]$ and let $Y = X/{\sim}$ where $(x, 1) \sim (-x, 0)$ fo
r all $x \in S^2$ (antipodal identification between the top and bottom copies of
 $S^2$).

**Claim:** $Y$ is the mapping torus of the antipodal map $a: S^2 \to S^2$, $a(x)
 = -x$.

Indeed, the mapping torus of a map $f: Z \to Z$ is by definition $T_f = Z \times
 [0,1] / (z,1) \sim (f(z), 0)$. Taking $Z = S^2$ and $f = a$, we obtain exactly
$Y = T_a$.

## Key observation: $Y$ is a fiber bundle over $S^1$

The antipodal map $a: S^2 \to S^2$ is a homeomorphism (it is continuous, and $a^
{-1} = a$). The mapping torus of a homeomorphism $h: Z \to Z$ carries a natural
projection
$$\pi: T_h \to S^1, \qquad \pi([z, t]) = [t] \in [0,1]/(0 \sim 1) \cong S^1,$$
which is a **fiber bundle** with fiber $Z$ and monodromy $h$. (Local triviality
over any proper open arc of $S^1$ is immediate; the transition function across t
he gluing point is $h$.)

Thus $\pi: Y \to S^1$ is a fiber bundle with fiber $S^2$. A fiber bundle is a Se
rre fibration, so the long exact sequence of homotopy groups applies.

## Long exact sequence

The fibration $S^2 \hookrightarrow Y \xrightarrow{\pi} S^1$ yields:
$$\cdots \to \pi_2(S^1) \to \pi_1(S^2) \to \pi_1(Y) \to \pi_1(S^1) \to \pi_0(S^2
) \to \cdots$$

Substituting the known values:
- $\pi_2(S^1) = 0$ (since $S^1$ has universal cover $\mathbb{R}$, which is contr
actible, so $\pi_n(S^1) = 0$ for $n \geq 2$),
- $\pi_1(S^2) = 0$ (since $S^2$ is simply connected),
- $\pi_0(S^2) = 0$ (since $S^2$ is path-connected),

the exact sequence becomes:
$$0 \longrightarrow 0 \longrightarrow \pi_1(Y) \longrightarrow \underbrace{\pi_1
(S^1)}_{\cong\, \mathbb{Z}} \longrightarrow 0.$$

The map $\pi_1(Y) \to \pi_1(S^1)$ is therefore an isomorphism, giving:

$$\boxed{\pi_1(Y) \cong \mathbb{Z}.}$$

## Verification via Seifert–van Kampen (independent check)

Cover $S^1$ by two open arcs $J_1, J_2$, each slightly longer than a semicircle,
 so that $J_1 \cap J_2$ has **two** connected components. Set $U = \pi^{-1}(J_1)
$, $V = \pi^{-1}(J_2)$; these form an open cover of $Y$.

- Since $J_1, J_2$ are contractible and the bundle is trivial over them, $U \con
g S^2 \times J_1$ and $V \cong S^2 \times J_2$, so $\pi_1(U) = \pi_1(V) = 1$.
- $U \cap V = \pi^{-1}(J_1 \cap J_2)$ has two components $W_1, W_2$, each a triv
ial bundle over a contractible arc, hence each $\simeq S^2$, so $\pi_1(W_i) = 1$
.

By the Seifert–van Kampen theorem for a disconnected intersection (with $k = 2$
components, basepoint in $W_1$):
$$\pi_1(Y) \cong \pi_1(U) * \pi_1(V) * F_{k-1} \big/ \langle\langle \text{inclus
ion relations} \rangle\rangle.$$
All inclusion relations are trivial (every group involved is trivial), and $F_{k
-1} = F_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}$. Hence $\pi_1(Y) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, confirming the r
esult.

The single generator of $\pi_1(Y)$ corresponds to the loop that travels once aro
und the $S^1$ base — i.e., the path $t \mapsto [x_0, t]$ for $t \in [0,1]$ in th
e quotient, which closes up because $(x_0, 1) \sim (-x_0, 0)$.
