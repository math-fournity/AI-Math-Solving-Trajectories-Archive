## Solution

The mosaic is a 24 × 52 inch rectangle. We must partition it entirely into **con
gruent** (identical) square tiles with edges parallel to the rectangle's sides,
using at most 1994 inches of internal lead stripping.

### Step 1: Determine possible square sizes

If the common square side length is $s$, then $s$ must divide both 24 and 52 to
tile the rectangle completely. Thus

$$s \mid \gcd(24, 52) = 4, \quad\text{so } s \in \{1, 2, 4\}.$$

### Step 2: Compute tiles and stripping for each candidate

If the grid is $m \times n$ tiles ($m = 24/s$ along the 24-inch side, $n = 52/s$
 along the 52-inch side), the internal stripping consists of:
- $(m-1)$ horizontal internal lines, each of length 52,
- $(n-1)$ vertical internal lines, each of length 24.

Total stripping $= (m-1)\cdot 52 + (n-1)\cdot 24$.

| $s$ | $m \times n$ | Tiles | Stripping (in) | Feasible? |
|-----|-------------|-------|----------------|-----------|
| 1 | $24 \times 52$ | 1248 | $23\cdot52 + 51\cdot24 = 1196 + 1224 = 2420$ | No
(2420 > 1994) |
| 2 | $12 \times 26$ | 312 | $11\cdot52 + 25\cdot24 = 572 + 600 = 1172$ | Yes (1
172 ≤ 1994) |
| 4 | $6 \times 13$ | 78 | $5\cdot52 + 12\cdot24 = 260 + 288 = 548$ | Yes (548 ≤
 1994) |

### Step 3: Select the maximum

- $s = 1$ is **infeasible**: it requires 2420 inches of stripping, exceeding the
 1994-inch supply.
- $s = 2$ is feasible and yields **312 tiles**.
- $s = 4$ is feasible but yields only 78 tiles.

Since smaller squares give more tiles (and $s=1$ is ruled out by the stripping b
udget), the maximum feasible tile count is achieved at $s = 2$.

### Verification

With $s = 2$: the rectangle $24 \times 52$ is divided into a $12 \times 26$ grid
 of $2\times2$ squares, giving $12 \times 26 = 312$ congruent tiles. The interna
l stripping required is $11 \times 52 + 25 \times 24 = 572 + 600 = 1172 \le 1994
$. ✓

The largest number of congruent square tiles is $\boxed{312}$.
