**Answer: Yes, such a holomorphic extension g always exists.**

## Proof

Assume $D \subset \mathbb{C}$ is a domain and $K \subset D$ is compact. Let $\{p
_n\}$ be polynomials converging to $f$ uniformly on compact subsets of $\Omega :
= D \setminus K$.

**Key idea:** We show $\{p_n\}$ converges uniformly on compact subsets of *all*
of $D$ (not just $D\setminus K$). The limit is then automatically holomorphic on
 $D$ and agrees with $f$ on $D\setminus K$.

**Setup.** Let $L \subset D$ be an arbitrary compact set. We show $\{p_n\}$ is u
niformly Cauchy on $L$.

Since $L$ and $K$ are both compact in $D$ and $D$ is open, the union $L \cup K$
is compact and contained in $D$, so:
$$\delta := \operatorname{dist}(L \cup K,\; \mathbb{C}\setminus D) > 0$$
(with the convention $\delta = 1$ when $D = \mathbb{C}$, since $L\cup K$ is boun
ded).

**Constructing a bounding region.** Define the open set:
$$U := \{z \in \mathbb{C} : \operatorname{dist}(z,\, L \cup K) < \delta/2\}.$$

We verify the following properties:

1. **$U$ is bounded:** $L \cup K$ is compact (hence bounded), so its $\delta/2$-
neighborhood is bounded.

2. **$L \subset U$:** Every point of $L$ has distance $0$ to $L \cup K < \delta/
2$.

3. **$\overline{U} \subset D$:** For $z \in \overline{U}$, $\operatorname{dist}(
z, L\cup K) \le \delta/2$, so $\operatorname{dist}(z, \mathbb{C}\setminus D) \ge
 \delta - \delta/2 = \delta/2 > 0$.

4. **$\partial U \subset D \setminus K$:** For $z \in \partial U$, $\operatornam
e{dist}(z, L\cup K) = \delta/2$. Since $K \subset L\cup K$, we have $\operatorna
me{dist}(z, K) \ge \delta/2 > 0$, so $z \notin K$.

**Applying the maximum principle.** Each $p_n - p_m$ is a polynomial, hence holo
morphic on $\mathbb{C} \supset \overline{U}$ and continuous on $\overline{U}$. S
ince $U$ is bounded, the maximum principle (applied component-wise, then combine
d) gives:
$$\sup_{z \in L} |p_n(z) - p_m(z)| \;\le\; \sup_{z \in \overline{U}} |p_n(z) - p
_m(z)| \;=\; \sup_{z \in \partial U} |p_n(z) - p_m(z)|.$$

**Using the hypothesis.** The boundary $\partial U$ is a compact subset of $D \s
etminus K$ (compact because $\partial U$ is a closed subset of the compact set $
\overline{U}$). By hypothesis, $p_n \to f$ uniformly on compact subsets of $D \s
etminus K$, so $\{p_n\}$ is uniformly Cauchy on $\partial U$:
$$\sup_{\partial U} |p_n - p_m| \;\longrightarrow\; 0 \quad \text{as } n,m \to \
infty.$$

**Conclusion of convergence.** Therefore:
$$\sup_{L} |p_n - p_m| \;\le\; \sup_{\partial U} |p_n - p_m| \;\longrightarrow\;
 0.$$

Since $L \subset D$ was an arbitrary compact set, $\{p_n\}$ converges uniformly
on compact subsets of $D$.

**Defining the extension.** Set $g(z) := \lim_{n\to\infty} p_n(z)$ for $z \in D$
. Then:
- $g$ is holomorphic on $D$, being a locally uniform limit of holomorphic (polyn
omial) functions.
- For $z \in D \setminus K$: $g(z) = \lim p_n(z) = f(z)$, so $g = f$ on $D \setm
inus K$.

Thus $g: D \to \mathbb{C}$ is the desired holomorphic extension. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on the mechanism.** The essential point is that polynomials are holomor
phic on all of $D$ (not just $D\setminus K$), and the maximum principle lets us
"propagate" convergence from $\partial U \subset D\setminus K$ into the interior
, which contains $K$. The compactness of $K$ in $D$ guarantees a positive buffer
 $\delta$ between $K \cup L$ and $\partial D$, ensuring $\partial U$ stays withi
n $D \setminus K$.
