## Analysis of the Twist Operation

**Base-20 digit interpretation.** Write $k$ in base 20 as digits $d_{m-1} d_{m-2
} \cdots d_1 d_0$, so $k = \sum_{i=0}^{m-1} d_i \cdot 20^i$. In the twist, $a =
\lfloor k/20 \rfloor = d_{m-1} \cdot 20^{m-2} + \cdots + d_1$ and $b = d_0$, giv
ing:

$$k' = 20 \cdot d_0 + a = d_0 \cdot 20 + d_{m-1} \cdot 20^{m-2} + \cdots + d_1$$

So $k'$ has base-20 digits $d_0\, d_{m-1}\, d_{m-2} \cdots d_1$ — the twist is a
 **cyclic right rotation** of the base-20 digit string by one position (last dig
it moves to front). If $d_0 = 0$, the resulting leading zero is dropped, shorten
ing the number.

## Key Invariant

**The multiset of nonzero base-20 digits is invariant under the twist.**

Rotation only permutes digit positions — it does not create, destroy, or alter a
ny digit. Dropping a leading zero removes only a zero. Therefore the collection
of nonzero digits is preserved exactly throughout the entire sequence.

## Characterizing S

For the sequence to contain 1, at some step the value equals 1, whose base-20 re
presentation is the single digit "1" — possessing exactly one nonzero digit, whi
ch is 1.

By the invariant, $n$ must have:
- **Exactly one nonzero base-20 digit**, and
- **That digit must be 1**.

This means $n = 20^j$ for some $j \geq 0$.

**Verification that every $20^j$ reaches 1:** $20^j$ has base-20 representation
"$1\underbrace{00\cdots0}_{j}$". Applying the twist: the last digit (0) moves to
 front, giving "$0\,1\,\underbrace{00\cdots0}_{j-1}$", which drops the leading z
ero to become $20^{j-1}$. Repeating:

$$20^j \to 20^{j-1} \to 20^{j-2} \to \cdots \to 20 \to 1$$

So every power of 20 reaches 1. ✓

**Verification that nothing else reaches 1:** If $n$ has zero nonzero digits, th
en $n=0$ (excluded since $n \geq 1$). If $n$ has $\geq 2$ nonzero digits, or one
 nonzero digit $\neq 1$, the invariant prevents the sequence from ever having th
e digit multiset $\{1\}$, so it can never equal 1. ✓
