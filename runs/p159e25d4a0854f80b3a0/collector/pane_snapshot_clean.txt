## Proof

**Notation.** $s(n)$ denotes the digit sum of $n$. We use the identity $s(n) \eq
uiv n \pmod{9}$, and the carry formula: when computing $a + b$, $s(a+b) = s(a) +
 s(b) - 9C(a,b)$, where $C(a,b)$ is the total number of carries.

### Step 1: $k$ is divisible by 9

Since $s(k) = s(mk)$ for all $m$, we have $k \equiv mk \pmod{9}$ for all $m \in
\{1,\dots,2014\}$. Taking $m=2$: $k \equiv 0 \pmod{9}$.

### Step 2: $k$ has at least 4 digits

Consider $m = 1001$. Since $1001k = 1000k + k$, we use $s(1001k) = s(1000k) + s(
k) - 9C = 2s(k) - 9C$.

If $k$ has **at most 3 digits**, then $1000k$ occupies digit positions $\ge 3$ w
hile $k$ occupies positions $0,1,2$. There is **no overlap**, so $C = 0$, giving
 $s(1001k) = 2s(k)$. Setting this equal to $s(k)$ yields $s(k) = 0$, impossible
for $k > 0$.

Therefore $k \ge 1000$ (at least 4 digits).

### Step 3: Among 4-digit numbers, only $k = 9999$ satisfies the $m=1001$ constr
aint

Write $k = \overline{d_3 d_2 d_1 d_0}$ with digits $d_0$ (units) through $d_3$ (
thousands). In the addition $1000k + k$:

- **Positions 0–2:** digits of $1000k$ are $0$; digits of $k$ are $d_0, d_1, d_2
$. Sums are $d_i < 10$: **no carries**.
- **Position 3:** $d_0$ (from $1000k$) $+ d_3$ (from $k$). Carry iff $d_0 + d_3
\ge 10$.
- **Position 4:** $d_1 + 0 + \text{carry}$. Carry iff $d_1 = 9$ (given carry in)
.
- **Position 5:** $d_2 + 0 + \text{carry}$. Carry iff $d_2 = 9$ (given carry in)
.
- **Position 6:** $d_3 + 0 + \text{carry}$. Carry iff $d_3 = 9$ (given carry in)
.

If $d_0 + d_3 < 10$: $C = 0$, so $s(1001k) = 2s(k)$, requiring $s(k)=0$. **Impos
sible.**

So $d_0 + d_3 \ge 10$, and the carry count $C$ depends on how many of $d_1, d_2,
 d_3$ equal $9$:

| Condition | $C$ | Required $s(k) = 9C$ | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| $d_1 \le 8$ | 1 | $s(k)=9$ | $d_0+d_3 \ge 10$ but $d_0+d_1+d_2+d_3=9 \Rightarr
ow d_1+d_2 \le -1$. **Impossible.** |
| $d_1=9, d_2\le 8$ | 2 | $s(k)=18$ | $d_0+9+d_2+d_3=18 \Rightarrow d_0+d_2+d_3=
9$, but $d_0+d_3\ge 10 \Rightarrow d_2\le -1$. **Impossible.** |
| $d_1=d_2=9, d_3\le 8$ | 3 | $s(k)=27$ | $d_0+18+d_3=27 \Rightarrow d_0+d_3=9$,
 contradicting $d_0+d_3\ge 10$. **Impossible.** |
| $d_1=d_2=d_3=9$ | 4 | $s(k)=36$ | $d_0+27=36 \Rightarrow d_0=9$. **Gives $k =
9999$.** ✓ |

Thus **$k = 9999$ is the only 4-digit candidate**, and no number with fewer digi
ts works.

### Step 4: $k = 9999$ satisfies all constraints

Let $k = 10^4 - 1 = 9999$. For any $m$ with $1 \le m \le 2014 < 10^4$:

$$mk = m(10^4 - 1) = m \cdot 10^4 - m = (m-1)\cdot 10^4 + (10^4 - m).$$

Since $1 \le m \le 2014$, we have $10^4 - m \ge 7986 > 0$, so $10^4 - m$ is a po
sitive integer with at most 4 digits. The number $10^4 - m$ is the **9's complem
ent** of $(m-1)$ written in 4 digits, so:

$$s(10^4 - m) = 36 - s(m-1)$$

(where $s(m-1)$ is computed with $m-1$ padded to 4 digits, i.e., leading zeros c
ontribute 0).

Since $(m-1) \cdot 10^4$ and $(10^4 - m)$ occupy disjoint digit blocks (position
s $\ge 4$ vs. positions $0$–$3$), there are no carries between them:

$$s(mk) = s(m-1) + s(10^4 - m) = s(m-1) + 36 - s(m-1) = 36.$$

This holds for every $m \in \{1, 2, \ldots, 2014\}$, so $s(k) = s(2k) = \cdots =
 s(2014k) = 36$. ✓

### Conclusion

- $k$ must have $\ge 4$ digits (Step 2).
- The only 4-digit number satisfying the $m = 1001$ constraint is $k = 9999$ (St
ep 3).
- $k = 9999$ satisfies all constraints (Step 4).
- Any $k > 9999$ with more digits is larger.

Therefore, the smallest positive integer $k$ is:

$$\boxed{9999}$$
