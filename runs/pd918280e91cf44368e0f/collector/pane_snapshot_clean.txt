## Proof

A standard Wiener process $W = \{W_t, t \in [0,1]\}$ is defined by the propertie
s:
1. $W_0 = 0$ almost surely,
2. $W$ has independent increments,
3. $W_t - W_s \sim \mathcal{N}(0, t-s)$ for $0 \le s \le t \le 1$,
4. $W$ has **continuous sample paths** almost surely.

Property (4) is the decisive feature. It tells us that almost every realization
of $W$ is a continuous function on $[0,1]$. Therefore the natural state space fo
r $W$—the set in which its sample paths live—is the space of continuous real-val
ued functions on $[0,1]$:

$$F = C[0,1] = \{f : [0,1] \to \mathbb{R} \mid f \text{ is continuous}\}.$$

**Why $C[0,1]$ and not a larger function space?**

- The Wiener process does **not** have differentiable paths, so $C^1[0,1]$ is to
o small (it would exclude almost every Brownian sample path).
- The space $L^2[0,1]$ of square-integrable functions identifies functions up to
 equality almost everywhere, losing the pointwise structure that makes the proce
ss $W$ well-defined as a function-valued random variable. Moreover, $C[0,1] \sub
set L^2[0,1]$, and the embedding is dense, so $C[0,1]$ captures the pathwise str
ucture while remaining compatible with $L^2$-based metrics.
- The space of all (measurable) functions $\mathbb{R}^{[0,1]}$ is too large to c
arry useful metrics for functional data analysis.

**The metric.** On $C[0,1]$ the canonical (semi)metric used in functional data a
nalysis is the uniform (supremum) metric:

$$d(f, g) = \sup_{t \in [0,1]} |f(t) - g(t)|.$$

This is a genuine metric (not merely a pseudometric) on $C[0,1]$, and it metrize
s the topology of uniform convergence. It is the natural choice because:
- It respects the continuity of the sample paths (the sup is attained),
- It is the metric under which the Wiener measure has full support on $C[0,1]$,
- It is the metric used in classical results such as Donsker's theorem and the G
livenko–Cantelli-type functional limit theorems.

**Conclusion.** The appropriate set $F$ representing a standard Wiener process i
n functional data analysis is

$$\boxed{F = C[0,1]},$$

the space of continuous real-valued functions on $[0,1]$, equipped with the unif
orm (supremum) metric $d(f,g) = \sup_{t \in [0,1]}|f(t)-g(t)|$.
