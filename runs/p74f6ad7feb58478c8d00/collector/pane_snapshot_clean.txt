## Proof

The set is $A = \{(x,y,z) \mid z > 0,\; 1-x^2 = z,\; y^2+z^2 = z\}$.

**Step 1: Reduce the defining equations.**

From $1 - x^2 = z$ we get $z = 1 - x^2$. The condition $z > 0$ gives $x^2 < 1$,
i.e. $x \in (-1,1)$.

Substituting into $y^2 + z^2 = z$:
$$y^2 = z - z^2 = z(1-z) = (1-x^2)\cdot x^2 = x^2(1-x^2).$$

So $A = \{(x,\, y,\, 1-x^2) : x \in (-1,1),\; y = \pm\, x\sqrt{1-x^2}\}$.

**Step 2: $A$ is a 1-dimensional bounded set.**

$A$ is the image of the continuous map $x \mapsto (x,\, \pm x\sqrt{1-x^2},\, 1-x
^2)$ on the open interval $(-1,1)$, i.e. a union of two smooth curves (the inter
section of the paraboloid $z = 1-x^2$ with the sphere $y^2 + (z-\tfrac12)^2 = \t
frac14$). It is bounded since $|x|<1$, $0 < z \leq 1$, and $|y| \leq \tfrac12$.

**Step 3: $A$ has Jordan measure $0$.**

A smooth curve in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is a 1-dimensional submanifold, hence has 3-dim
ensional Lebesgue (and Jordan) measure $0$. Concretely: for any $\varepsilon > 0
$, cover the bounded curve by finitely many boxes of total volume $< \varepsilon
$ (possible since the curve has finite length and can be covered by boxes of arb
itrarily small total volume). Thus the inner and outer Jordan content are both $
0$.

Therefore the Jordan measure of $A$ is $\boxed{0}$.
