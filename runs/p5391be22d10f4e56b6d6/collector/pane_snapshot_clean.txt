Looking at this problem, I need to find the area of $R = \bigcap_{0<\alpha<1} R(
\alpha)$, where $R(\alpha)$ is the convex pentagon with vertices $(0,1-\alpha),
(\alpha,0), (1,0), (1,1), (0,1)$.

## Setting Up the Half-Plane Constraints

The pentagon $R(\alpha)$ (vertices listed counterclockwise) is defined by five h
alf-plane constraints:

- **Edge** $(0,1)\to(0,1-\alpha)$: $x \geq 0$
- **Edge** $(\alpha,0)\to(1,0)$: $y \geq 0$
- **Edge** $(1,0)\to(1,1)$: $x \leq 1$
- **Edge** $(1,1)\to(0,1)$: $y \leq 1$
- **Edge** $(0,1-\alpha)\to(\alpha,0)$: the line through these points is $\frac{
x}{\alpha} + \frac{y}{1-\alpha} = 1$, i.e., $(1-\alpha)x + \alpha y = \alpha(1-\
alpha)$. Testing the interior point $(1,0)$: $(1-\alpha) \geq \alpha(1-\alpha)$
iff $(1-\alpha)^2 \geq 0$ ✓. So the constraint is $(1-\alpha)x + \alpha y \geq \
alpha(1-\alpha)$.

Thus:
$$R(\alpha) = \{(x,y) \in [0,1]^2 : (1-\alpha)x + \alpha y \geq \alpha(1-\alpha)
\}.$$

## Taking the Intersection Over All $\alpha$

The constraints $x\geq 0, y\geq 0, x\leq 1, y\leq 1$ are common to all $R(\alpha
)$. The $\alpha$-dependent constraint must hold for **all** $\alpha \in (0,1)$:

$$R = \left\{(x,y) \in [0,1]^2 : (1-\alpha)x + \alpha y \geq \alpha(1-\alpha) \;
\;\forall\, \alpha\in(0,1)\right\}.$$

Define $f(\alpha) = (1-\alpha)x + \alpha y - \alpha(1-\alpha) = \alpha^2 + \alph
a(y - x - 1) + x$.

This is a **convex** quadratic in $\alpha$ (leading coefficient $1 > 0$). Its mi
nimum is at:
$$\alpha^* = \frac{x + 1 - y}{2}.$$

For $(x,y) \in [0,1]^2$, we have $x+1-y \in [0,2]$, so $\alpha^* \in [0,1]$. (At
 the corners $(0,1)$ and $(1,0)$, $\alpha^*$ equals $0$ and $1$ respectively, an
d one checks $f(\alpha) = \alpha^2$ and $f(\alpha)=(\alpha-1)^2 \geq 0$.)

So the condition $f(\alpha) \geq 0$ for all $\alpha \in (0,1)$ reduces to $f(\al
pha^*) \geq 0$:

$$f(\alpha^*) = x - \frac{(y-x-1)^2}{4} \geq 0 \iff 4x \geq (x+1-y)^2.$$

## Simplifying the Region

Since $x + 1 - y \geq 0$ on $[0,1]^2$, the inequality $4x \geq (x+1-y)^2$ is equ
ivalent to:

$$x + 1 - y \leq 2\sqrt{x} \iff y \geq x + 1 - 2\sqrt{x} = (\sqrt{x} - 1)^2.$$

Therefore:
$$R = \left\{(x,y) : 0 \leq x \leq 1,\; (\sqrt{x}-1)^2 \leq y \leq 1\right\}.$$

## Computing the Area

$$\text{Area}(R) = \int_0^1 \left[1 - (\sqrt{x}-1)^2\right] dx = \int_0^1 \left[
1 - x + 2\sqrt{x} - 1\right] dx = \int_0^1 (2\sqrt{x} - x)\, dx.$$

$$= \left[\frac{4}{3}x^{3/2} - \frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^1 = \frac{4}{3} - \frac{1}
{2} = \frac{8}{6} - \frac{3}{6} = \frac{5}{6}.$$

## Verification

- At $x=0$: boundary $y = (0-1)^2 = 1$ ✓ (the point $(0,1)$ is in every $R(\alph
a)$).
- At $x=1$: boundary $y = (1-1)^2 = 0$ ✓ (the point $(1,0)$ is in every $R(\alph
a)$).
- At $\alpha = \frac{1}{2}$: constraint is $x+y \geq \frac{1}{2}$. The point $\l
eft(\frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{4}\right)$ lies on the boundary of $R$ (since $(\sqrt{
1/4}-1)^2 = 1/4$), and $x+y = \frac{1}{2}$ ✓.

The area of $R$ is $\boxed{\dfrac{5}{6}}$.
