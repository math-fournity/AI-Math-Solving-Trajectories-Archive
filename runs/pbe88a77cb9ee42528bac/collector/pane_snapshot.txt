## Solution

**Identify the singularities.** The integrand $f(z) = \frac{1}{\sin^2 z}$ has si
ngularities where $\sin z = 0$, i.e. at $z = n\pi$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

**Determine which singularities lie inside the contour.** The contour is $|z| =
1/2$. Since $\pi \approx 3.14159 > 1/2$, the only zero of $\sin z$ inside $|z|<1
/2$ is $z = 0$ (the case $n=0$). All other zeros $z = n\pi$, $n \neq 0$, satisfy
 $|n\pi| \geq \pi > 1/2$ and lie outside.

**Classify the singularity at $z=0$.** Since $\sin z$ has a simple zero at $z=0$
 (with $\sin'0 = \cos 0 = 1 \neq 0$), the function $\sin^2 z$ has a zero of orde
r $2$ there. Hence $f(z) = 1/\sin^2 z$ has a pole of order $2$ at $z = 0$.

**Compute the residue.** For a pole of order $2$,
$$\operatorname{Res}(f, 0) = \lim_{z\to 0}\frac{d}{dz}\!\left[z^2 f(z)\right] =
\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{d}{dz}\!\left[\frac{z^2}{\sin^2 z}\right] = \lim_{z\to 0}\fra
c{d}{dz}\!\left[\left(\frac{z}{\sin z}\right)^{\!2}\right].$$

Using the expansion $\sin z = z - \frac{z^3}{6} + O(z^5)$,
$$\frac{z}{\sin z} = \frac{1}{1 - z^2/6 + O(z^4)} = 1 + \frac{z^2}{6} + O(z^4),$
$
so
$$\left(\frac{z}{\sin z}\right)^{\!2} = 1 + \frac{z^2}{3} + O(z^4).$$

Differentiating:
$$\frac{d}{dz}\!\left[\left(\frac{z}{\sin z}\right)^{\!2}\right] = \frac{2z}{3}
+ O(z^3),$$
and evaluating at $z = 0$ gives
$$\operatorname{Res}(f, 0) = 0.$$

Equivalently, the Laurent expansion of $f$ at $0$ is
$$\frac{1}{\sin^2 z} = \frac{1}{z^2} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{z^2}{15} + \cdots,$$
in which the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ is $0$, confirming the residue is $0$.

**Apply the residue theorem.** By Cauchy's residue theorem,
$$\oint_{|z|=1/2} \frac{dz}{\sin^2 z} = 2\pi i \sum \operatorname{Res} = 2\pi i
\cdot \operatorname{Res}(f,0) = 2\pi i \cdot 0 = 0.$$

**Sanity check.** Note that $\frac{1}{\sin^2 z} = -\frac{d}{dz}\cot z$. Since $\
cot z$ is meromorphic with a single simple pole (residue $1$) at $z=0$ inside th
e contour, its derivative $-\cot' z = 1/\sin^2 z$ has a pole of order $2$ with r
esidue $0$ (the derivative of a meromorphic function always has zero residue at
each pole). This independently confirms $\operatorname{Res}(f,0)=0$.

## Result

$$\boxed{\oint_{|z|=1/2} \frac{dz}{\sin^2 z} = 0}$$
