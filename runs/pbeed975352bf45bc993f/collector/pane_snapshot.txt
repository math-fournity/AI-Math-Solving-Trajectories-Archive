**Construction.** Consider the power series

$$f(z) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{z^{3n}}{n}.$$

**Step 1: Radius of convergence is 1.**

The coefficients are $a_{3k} = 1/k$ and $a_m = 0$ when $3 \nmid m$. By the Cauch
y–Hadamard formula,

$$\frac{1}{R} = \limsup_{m\to\infty} |a_m|^{1/m}.$$

For $m = 3k$: $|a_{3k}|^{1/(3k)} = (1/k)^{1/(3k)} \to 1$ as $k\to\infty$ (since
$k^{1/k}\to 1$). For $m$ not divisible by 3, $|a_m|^{1/m} = 0$. Hence $\limsup =
 1$, giving $R = 1$.

**Step 2: Behavior on $|z|=1$.**

On the unit circle, set $w = z^3$, so $|w| = 1$ and

$$f(z) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{w^n}{n}.$$

This is the well-known series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} w^n/n = -\log(1-w)$.

- If $w = 1$: the series becomes $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} 1/n$, the harmonic series,
 which **diverges**.
- If $w \neq 1$ and $|w| = 1$: the partial sums $\sum_{n=1}^{N} w^n$ are bounded
 (they form a geometric series with ratio $w \neq 1$, so $\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N} w
^n\right| = |w(1-w^N)/(1-w)| \le 2/|1-w|$), and $1/n \to 0$ monotonically. By **
Dirichlet's test**, the series **converges**.

**Step 3: Identifying the divergence points.**

The series diverges exactly when $w = z^3 = 1$, i.e., when $z$ is a cube root of
 unity:

$$z \in \left\{1,\; e^{2\pi i/3},\; e^{4\pi i/3}\right\}.$$

These are exactly three distinct points on $|z| = 1$. At every other point of th
e unit circle, $z^3 \neq 1$, so the series converges.

**Conclusion.** The series $f(z) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{z^{3n}}{n}$ has rad
ius of convergence $1$ and diverges at exactly three points on $|z|=1$, namely t
he three cube roots of unity. Therefore, such a series exists. $\blacksquare$
