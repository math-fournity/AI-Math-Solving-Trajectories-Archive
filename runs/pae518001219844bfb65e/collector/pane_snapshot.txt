## Solution

**Setup.** The checkerboard corners form the integer lattice $\mathbb{Z}^2$. A u
nit square is dropped with uniform random position (equivalently, its center is
uniform over a fundamental domain $[0,1)^2$) and uniform random orientation $\th
eta \in [0, 2\pi)$. By the 4-fold rotational symmetry of the square, $\theta$ is
 effectively uniform on $[0, \pi/2)$.

**Fixing orientation.** Let $S_\theta$ denote the unit square (side 1) centered
at the origin, rotated by angle $\theta$. The dropped square covers lattice poin
t $p$ iff its center $c$ satisfies $p \in c + S_\theta$, i.e., $c \in p - S_\the
ta$. The "bad" set of centers is $\bigcup_{p \in \mathbb{Z}^2}(p - S_\theta)$, w
hich is periodic with period lattice $\mathbb{Z}^2$.

Define $f(x) = \sum_{v \in \mathbb{Z}^2} \mathbf{1}_{S_\theta}(x + v)$ for $x \i
n [0,1)^2$, the number of lattice translates covering $x$. Then $\int_{[0,1)^2}
f = \text{area}(S_\theta) = 1$, and $P(\theta) = \text{area}(\{f = 0\})$.

**Identifying contributing lattice vectors.** Two translates $S_\theta + v_1$ an
d $S_\theta + v_2$ overlap iff $v_1 - v_2 \in S_\theta - S_\theta = 2S_\theta$ (
a square of side 2 rotated by $\theta$). A point $(x,y) \in 2S_\theta$ iff $|x\c
os\theta + y\sin\theta| \leq 1$ and $|-x\sin\theta + y\cos\theta| \leq 1$.

For $\theta \in (0, \pi/2)$, the nonzero lattice points in $2S_\theta$ are exact
ly $(\pm 1, 0)$ and $(0, \pm 1)$:
- $(1,0)$: $|\cos\theta| \leq 1$, $|\sin\theta| \leq 1$ ✓ (always).
- $(0,1)$: same ✓.
- $(1,1)$: requires $\cos\theta + \sin\theta \leq 1$, which fails for $\theta \i
n (0, \pi/2)$ since $\cos\theta + \sin\theta \geq 1$ with equality only at endpo
ints.
- $(2,0)$: requires $2\cos\theta \leq 1$, i.e., $\theta \geq \pi/3$; but even th
en $|{-2\sin\theta}| = 2\sin\theta$ could exceed 1. In fact for $\theta \in [\pi
/3, \pi/2]$, $2\sin\theta \geq 2\sin(\pi/3) = \sqrt{3} > 1$, so $(2,0) \notin 2S
_\theta$. Similarly $(0,2) \notin 2S_\theta$.

**No triple overlaps.** For $f \geq 3$ at some point, we'd need three lattice po
ints pairwise differing by vectors in $\{(\pm1,0),(0,\pm1)\}$. But $(0,0), (1,0)
, (0,1)$ have pairwise difference $(1,-1) \notin 2S_\theta$. No triple of lattic
e points is pairwise connected, so $f \leq 2$ everywhere.

**Key formula.** When $f \leq 2$:
$$\sum_{v \neq 0} \text{area}(S_\theta \cap (S_\theta + v)) = \int_{[0,1)^2} f^2
 - 1 = (1 + 2\,\text{area}(\{f=2\})) - 1 = 2P(\theta).$$

Since only $v \in \{(\pm1,0),(0,\pm1)\}$ contribute:
$$P(\theta) = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[2\,\text{area}(S_\theta \cap (S_\theta+(1,0))) +
 2\,\text{area}(S_\theta \cap (S_\theta+(0,1)))\bigr] = A_1(\theta) + A_2(\theta
).$$

**Computing the overlaps.** In $S_\theta$'s local frame (aligned with its sides)
, the global shift $(1,0)$ becomes $(\cos\theta, -\sin\theta)$. The overlap of t
wo unit squares separated by $(d_x, d_y)$ is $(1-|d_x|)(1-|d_y|)$ when $|d_x|,|d
_y| \leq 1$. Thus:
$$A_1(\theta) = (1-\cos\theta)(1-\sin\theta), \qquad A_2(\theta) = (1-\sin\theta
)(1-\cos\theta).$$

So $P(\theta) = 2(1-\cos\theta)(1-\sin\theta)$ for $\theta \in (0, \pi/2)$.

**Averaging over orientation.** The orientation is uniform on $[0, \pi/2)$:
$$P = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi/2} 2(1-\cos\theta)(1-\sin\theta)\,d\theta = \frac
{4}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi/2}(1 - \cos\theta - \sin\theta + \cos\theta\sin\theta)\,d\th
eta.$$

Evaluating each term:
- $\int_0^{\pi/2} 1\,d\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$
- $\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos\theta\,d\theta = 1$, $\quad\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin\theta\,d\the
ta = 1$
- $\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos\theta\sin\theta\,d\theta = \int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\sin 2\thet
a}{2}\,d\theta = \frac{1}{4}(-\cos\pi+\cos 0) = \frac{1}{2}$

$$P = \frac{4}{\pi}\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - 1 - 1 + \frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{4}{\
pi}\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{3}{2}\right) = \frac{4}{\pi}\cdot\frac{\pi-3}{2}
= \frac{2(\pi-3)}{\pi} = 2 - \frac{6}{\pi}.$$

**Sanity checks.** At $\theta = 0$ (axis-aligned): $P(0) = 2(0)(1) = 0$ ✓ (an ax
is-aligned unit square on an axis-aligned grid always contains a lattice point).
 At $\theta = \pi/4$: $P(\pi/4) = 2(1-1/\sqrt{2})^2 = 3 - 2\sqrt{2} \approx 0.17
2$ (maximum, at worst alignment). The average $\approx 0.0901$ lies between thes
e. ✓

$$\boxed{P = 2 - \frac{6}{\pi}}$$
