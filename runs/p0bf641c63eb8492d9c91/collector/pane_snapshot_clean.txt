**Counterexample.**

Consider four counter-clockwise circles in ℝ²:

- γ₁: circle of radius 1 centered at the origin.
- γ₂: circle of radius 0.5 centered at (7, 0).
- γ₃: circle of radius 3 centered at the origin.
- γ₄: circle of radius 10 centered at the origin.

**Verification of the hypotheses.**

1. **All are counter-clockwise Jordan curves.** ✓ (standard circles, positively
oriented)

2. **γ₂ ⊂ ext(γ₁):** Every point of γ₂ has distance from the origin between 6.5
and 7.5, all strictly greater than 1. ✓

3. **γ₃ ⊂ ext(γ₂):** Every point of γ₃ (distance 3 from origin) has distance fro
m (7,0) between 4 and 10, all strictly greater than 0.5 (the radius of γ₂). So γ
₃ lies entirely in the exterior of γ₂. ✓

4. **γ₄ ⊂ ext(γ₃):** Every point of γ₄ is at distance 10 from the origin, strict
ly greater than 3. ✓

**The region R = ext(γ₁) ∩ int(γ₄)** is the open annulus {z ∈ ℝ² : 1 < |z| < 10}
, which is homeomorphic to a standard annulus. Its fundamental group is π₁(R) ≅
ℤ.

**Both γ₂ and γ₃ lie in R:**
- γ₂: distances from origin range in [6.5, 7.5] ⊂ (1, 10). ✓
- γ₃: distance from origin is exactly 3 ∈ (1, 10). ✓

**However, γ₂ and γ₃ represent different elements of π₁(R):**

- **γ₂ is null-homotopic (represents 0 ∈ ℤ):** The circle γ₂ (centered at (7,0),
 radius 0.5) does not enclose the inner boundary of the annulus. The straight-li
ne homotopy H(s,t) = (7,0) + (1−s)·(0.5 cos t, 0.5 sin t) contracts γ₂ to the po
int (7,0). For all s,t, we have |H(s,t)| ∈ [6.5, 7.5] ⊂ (1,10), so the homotopy
stays within R. Thus γ₂ represents the trivial element 0 ∈ π₁(R).

- **γ₃ represents the generator 1 ∈ ℤ:** The circle γ₃ (radius 3, centered at or
igin) winds once around the inner hole of the annulus (the disk |z| ≤ 1). It sep
arates the two boundary components of R and represents the generator of π₁(R) ≅
ℤ. It is **not** null-homotopic in R.

**Conclusion.** Since π₁(R) ≅ ℤ is abelian, two loops in R are freely homotopic
if and only if they represent the same element of π₁(R). Here γ₂ represents 0 an
d γ₃ represents 1, which are distinct. Therefore **no homotopy from γ₂ to γ₃ exi
sts within R = ext(γ₁) ∩ int(γ₄)**.

The key insight is that the condition "γ_{i+1} ⊂ ext(γ_i)" does not force γ₂ to
wind around γ₁. A curve can lie in the exterior of γ₁ (and in the annular region
 between γ₁ and γ₄) without enclosing γ₁, making it contractible in the annulus,

esent different free homotopy classes and cannot be connected by a homotopy in R
