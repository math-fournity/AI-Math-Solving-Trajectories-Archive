Looking at the problem, I need to determine whether a compact, connected, orient
ed hypersurface $M^n$ in the open hemisphere $S_+^{n+1}$ with nowhere zero Gauss
-Kronecker curvature must have all principal curvatures of the same sign.

**Answer: Yes, all principal curvatures have the same sign.**

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $S^{n+1} \subset \mathbb{R}^{n+2}$ be the unit sphere and $S_+^{n
+1} = \{x \in S^{n+1} : \langle x, e_{n+2}\rangle > 0\}$ the open hemisphere cen
tered at the north pole $a = e_{n+2}$. Let $M^n$ be a compact, connected, orient
ed hypersurface of $S_+^{n+1}$ with unit normal $N$ and shape operator $A$. The
Gauss-Kronecker curvature $\det A = \prod_{i=1}^n k_i$ is nowhere zero, so every
 principal curvature $k_i$ is nonzero at every point.

**Key observation (sign count is constant).** Since all eigenvalues of $A$ are n
onzero everywhere, the number of positive eigenvalues and the number of negative
 eigenvalues are locally constant on $M$. By connectedness of $M$, these counts
are **globally constant**. Thus it suffices to show that at a single point, all
principal curvatures share the same sign.

**The height function.** Define $h: M \to \mathbb{R}$ by $h(p) = \langle p, a \r
angle$. Since $M \subset S_+^{n+1}$, we have $h > 0$ everywhere on $M$. Since $M
$ is compact, $h$ attains its minimum at some point $q \in M$.

**Hessian formula in $S^{n+1}$.** Let $\phi = \langle N, a \rangle$ be the suppo
rt function. A standard computation gives the Hessian of $h$ on $M$:

$$\nabla^2 h(X, Y) = -h\,\langle X, Y\rangle + \phi\,\langle AX, Y\rangle.$$

*Derivation:* In $S^{n+1}$, the position vector field satisfies $\bar\nabla_X x
= X$, and $\bar\nabla^2 h = -h\,\bar g$ (the height function has Hessian $-h\bar
 g$ in the sphere). The Gauss formula then gives $\nabla^2 h(X,Y) = \bar\nabla^2
 h(X,Y) + \langle \alpha(X,Y), (\bar\nabla h)^\perp\rangle$. Since $\bar\nabla h
 = a - hx$ and its normal component to $M$ is $\langle a,N\rangle N = \phi N$, w
hile $\alpha(X,Y) = \langle AX,Y\rangle N$, the formula follows.

**Analysis at the minimum.** At the minimum point $q$:
- $\nabla h(q) = 0$ (critical point),
- $\nabla^2 h(q) \geq 0$ (positive semidefinite).

Applying the Hessian formula at $q$ to a principal direction $e_i$ with eigenval
ue $k_i(q)$:

$$-h(q) + \phi(q)\,k_i(q) \geq 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \phi(q)\,k_i(q) \ge
q h(q) > 0$$

for every $i = 1, \ldots, n$.

**Conclusion.** Since $h(q) > 0$, we have $\phi(q)\,k_i(q) > 0$ for all $i$. In
particular $\phi(q) \neq 0$, and every $k_i(q)$ has the same sign as $\phi(q)$.
Thus **all principal curvatures at $q$ share the same sign**.

Since the number of positive (resp. negative) eigenvalues of $A$ is constant on
the connected manifold $M$ (all eigenvalues are nowhere zero), and at the point
$q$ all eigenvalues have the same sign, it follows that **all principal curvatur
es have the same sign at every point of $M$**. $\blacksquare$
