## Solution

We evaluate $I = \int_0^\infty \frac{\ln^2(1+x)}{x^{3/2}}\,dx$.

**Convergence.** Near $x=0$: $\ln(1+x)\sim x$, so the integrand $\sim x^{1/2}\to
 0$, integrable. Near $\infty$: $\ln^2(1+x)/x^{3/2}\to 0$ since $3/2>1$, integra
ble. The integral converges.

**Integration by parts.** Set $u=\ln^2(1+x)$, $dv=x^{-3/2}\,dx$, so $v=-2x^{-1/2
}$ and $du=\frac{2\ln(1+x)}{1+x}\,dx$. The boundary term vanishes:
- At $\infty$: $-2x^{-1/2}\ln^2(1+x)\to 0$.
- At $0$: $-2x^{-1/2}\ln^2(1+x)\sim -2x^{3/2}\to 0$.

Hence
$$I = 4\int_0^\infty \frac{\ln(1+x)}{\sqrt{x}\,(1+x)}\,dx.$$

**Substitution $x=t^2$.** With $dx=2t\,dt$, $\sqrt{x}=t$, $1+x=1+t^2$:
$$I = 4\int_0^\infty \frac{\ln(1+t^2)}{t(1+t^2)}\cdot 2t\,dt = 8\int_0^\infty \f
rac{\ln(1+t^2)}{1+t^2}\,dt.$$

**Substitution $t=\tan\theta$.** With $dt=\sec^2\theta\,d\theta$ and $1+t^2=\sec
^2\theta$, over $\theta\in[0,\pi/2)$:
$$\int_0^\infty \frac{\ln(1+t^2)}{1+t^2}\,dt = \int_0^{\pi/2}\ln(\sec^2\theta)\,
d\theta = -2\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln(\cos\theta)\,d\theta.$$

**Classical integral.** Let $J=\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln(\cos\theta)\,d\theta$. By the s
ubstitution $\theta\mapsto\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta$, $J=\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln(\sin\theta
)\,d\theta$ as well. Then
$$2J = \int_0^{\pi/2}\ln(\sin\theta\cos\theta)\,d\theta = \int_0^{\pi/2}\ln\!\le
ft(\tfrac{1}{2}\sin 2\theta\right)d\theta = -\tfrac{\pi}{2}\ln 2 + \int_0^{\pi/2
}\ln(\sin 2\theta)\,d\theta.$$
With $u=2\theta$, $\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln(\sin 2\theta)\,d\theta = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^
\pi \ln(\sin u)\,du = \int_0^{\pi/2}\ln(\sin u)\,du = J$. Thus $2J = -\frac{\pi}
{2}\ln 2 + J$, giving $J = -\frac{\pi}{2}\ln 2$.

Therefore
$$\int_0^\infty \frac{\ln(1+t^2)}{1+t^2}\,dt = -2\left(-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\ln 2\righ
t) = \pi\ln 2,$$

and
$$\boxed{I = 8\pi\ln 2}.$$
