The statement is **false**. The error lies at the very first step: a field $A/\m
athfrak{p}$ is divisible as a module over *itself*, but injectivity here concern
s the $A$-module structure, and divisibility as an $A$-module fails.

## Proof that the statement is false

**Counterexample.** Take $A = \mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathfrak{p} = (p)$ for any prime
 $p$. Then $\mathfrak{p}$ is a nonzero prime ideal and

$$A/\mathfrak{p} \;=\; \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z} \;=\; \mathbb{F}_p,$$

which is indeed a field.

**The field $\mathbb{F}_p$ is not divisible as a $\mathbb{Z}$-module.** Recall t
hat a $\mathbb{Z}$-module $M$ is *divisible* if for every nonzero $n \in \mathbb
{Z}$ and every $m \in M$, there exists $x \in M$ with $n \cdot x = m$. Take $n =
 p$ and $m = \bar{1} \in \mathbb{F}_p$. For every $x \in \mathbb{F}_p$,

$$p \cdot x \;=\; \bar{0},$$

since $|\mathbb{F}_p| = p$ implies $p\cdot x = 0$ for all $x$. In particular, th
ere is no $x \in \mathbb{F}_p$ satisfying $p \cdot x = \bar{1}$. Hence $\mathbb{
F}_p$ is **not divisible** as a $\mathbb{Z}$-module.

**$\mathbb{F}_p$ is not injective.** By Baer's criterion specialized to $A = \ma
thbb{Z}$ (a PID), a $\mathbb{Z}$-module is injective if and only if it is divisi
ble. Since $\mathbb{F}_p$ is not divisible, it is **not injective**.

**The consequent claims fail as well.** For $k \geq 2$, the quotient $A/\mathfra
k{p}^k = \mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$ is not even a field (it has the nonzero nilpo
tent ideal $p\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$), so the "field therefore divisible" reas
oning does not even get started. Moreover, $\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$ is a torsi
on module of bounded exponent $p^k$, and no nonzero bounded-torsion module can b
e divisible (multiplication by $p^k$ kills everything, so $p^k \cdot M = 0 \neq
M$ for $M \neq 0$). Hence $\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$ is not injective for any $k
 \geq 1$.

**Diagnosis of the logical error.** The statement conflates two different module
 structures:

- $\mathbb{F}_p = A/\mathfrak{p}$ is a field, and as a module **over itself** ($
\mathbb{F}_p$ as an $\mathbb{F}_p$-module) it is divisible (trivially, since eve
ry nonzero element is a unit).
- But injectivity in the statement is as an **$A$-module**. The $A$-module struc
ture on $A/\mathfrak{p}$ factors through the quotient $A \to A/\mathfrak{p}$, an
d divisibility over $A$ requires solving $a \cdot x = m$ for *all* nonzero $a \i
n A$, including $a \in \mathfrak{p}$. For $a \in \mathfrak{p}$, multiplication b
y $a$ is the zero map on $A/\mathfrak{p}$, so no nonzero element can be "divided
" by $a$. This is exactly what fails in the counterexample with $a = p \in (p) =
 \mathfrak{p}$.

Since the first link in the chain ("$A/\mathfrak{p}$ is injective") is false, th
e entire conclusion — that $A/\mathfrak{p}^k$ is injective for all $k$ and that
finite direct sums of such quotients are injective — is **false**. $\blacksquare
$
