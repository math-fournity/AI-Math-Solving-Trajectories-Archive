## Proof

**Answer: Yes**, such an $N = N(C, \epsilon)$ exists. In particular, $N = \lceil
 C/\epsilon \rceil$ suffices.

**Key observation.** On $S^1$ we write $z = e^{i\theta}$. A Laurent polynomial $
f(z) = \sum_{n=-N}^{N} a_n z^n$ becomes a trigonometric polynomial $f(e^{i\theta
}) = \sum_{n=-N}^{N} a_n e^{in\theta}$. Since condition (1) requires $f \geq 0$
on $S^1$, the $L^1$ norm is
$$\|f\|_{L^1} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta = a_0,$$
by orthogonality of $\{e^{in\theta}\}$. So condition (3) is simply $a_0 < \epsil
on$.

**Construction.** Let $S = \{e^{i\theta_1}, \dots, e^{i\theta_C}\} \subseteq S^1
$ be an arbitrary set of cardinality $C$. Let $K_n$ denote the Fejér kernel of o
rder $n$:
$$K_n(\theta) = \sum_{k=-n}^{n}\left(1 - \frac{|k|}{n+1}\right)e^{ik\theta} = \f
rac{1}{n+1}\left(\frac{\sin\!\bigl(\tfrac{(n+1)\theta}{2}\bigr)}{\sin(\theta/2)}
\right)^{\!2}.$$
The Fejér kernel satisfies:
- $K_n(\theta) \geq 0$ for all $\theta$,
- $K_n(0) = n+1$,
- $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} K_n(\theta)\,d\theta = 1$ (i.e., its constant Fou
rier coefficient is $1$),
- $K_n$ is a trigonometric polynomial of degree $n$.

Define
$$f(z) = \frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{j=1}^{C} K_n(\theta - \theta_j), \qquad z = e^{i\the
ta}.$$

**Verification of the three conditions.**

1. **$f \geq 0$ on $S^1$:** Each $K_n(\theta - \theta_j) \geq 0$, so the sum is
nonneg, and the positive scalar $\frac{1}{n+1}$ preserves nonnegativity.

2. **$f(z) \geq 1$ for each $z \in S$:** At $z = e^{i\theta_k}$ (the $k$-th poin
t of $S$),
$$f(e^{i\theta_k}) = \frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{j=1}^{C} K_n(\theta_k - \theta_j) \geq \
frac{1}{n+1} K_n(0) = \frac{1}{n+1}\cdot(n+1) = 1,$$
using only the $j = k$ term and the nonnegativity of all other terms.

3. **$\|f\|_{L^1} < \epsilon$:** The constant term (i.e., $a_0$) of $f$ is
$$a_0 = \frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{j=1}^{C} 1 = \frac{C}{n+1},$$
since each shifted Fejér kernel $K_n(\theta - \theta_j)$ has constant Fourier co
efficient $1$. By the key observation, $\|f\|_{L^1} = a_0 = \frac{C}{n+1}$.

**Choosing $N$.** We need $\frac{C}{n+1} < \epsilon$, i.e., $n + 1 > \frac{C}{\e
psilon}$. Taking
$$N = n = \left\lceil \frac{C}{\epsilon} \right\rceil$$
gives $N + 1 > \frac{C}{\epsilon}$, hence $\|f\|_{L^1} = \frac{C}{N+1} < \epsilo
n$.

**$f$ is a valid Laurent polynomial of degree $\leq N$.** Each $K_n(\theta - \th
eta_j)$ is a trigonometric polynomial of degree $n = N$ in $\theta$, hence a Lau
rent polynomial $\sum_{k=-N}^{N} b_k z^k$ with $z = e^{i\theta}$. The finite sum
 $f$ is therefore also a Laurent polynomial of the required form $\sum_{k=-N}^{N
} a_k z^k$.

**Uniformity in $S$.** The construction depends on $S$ only through the choice o
f centers $\theta_j$, but the degree $N = \lceil C/\epsilon \rceil$ and the boun
d $\|f\|_{L^1} = C/(N+1) < \epsilon$ are independent of the specific locations o
r spacing of the points in $S$. Therefore the same $N$ works for **every** set $
S \subseteq S^1$ of cardinality $C$.

**Conclusion.** For every $C \geq 1$ and $\epsilon > 0$, the choice $N = \lceil
C/\epsilon \rceil$ satisfies all requirements. $\blacksquare$
