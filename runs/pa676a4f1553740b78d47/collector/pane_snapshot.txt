Looking at this problem, I need to find the number of 2-colorings of a convex $n
$-gon that admit a triangulation using only bichromatic diagonals.

## Proof

**Setup.** Label the vertices $1, 2, \ldots, n$ cyclically. A triangulation of a
 convex $n$-gon uses $n-3$ diagonals and creates $n-2$ triangles. Call a colorin
g *good* if some triangulation uses only bichromatic diagonals.

**Necessary condition: every good coloring has exactly 2 bichromatic sides.**

Consider any good triangulation. In each triangle, the three vertices are colore
d with two colors, so by the pigeonhole principle at least two share a color, gi
ving at least one monochromatic edge. If all three vertices shared one color, th
en all three edges would be monochromatic, but at least one of those edges must
be a diagonal (since $n \geq 4$, no triangle can have all three edges as polygon
 sides)—contradicting the requirement that every diagonal is bichromatic. If exa
ctly two edges were monochromatic, say $(a,b)$ and $(b,c)$, then $a,b$ share a c
olor and $b,c$ share a color, forcing all three to share a color—back to the pre
vious case. So **each triangle has exactly one monochromatic edge**, and this ed
ge must be a polygon side (not a diagonal).

There are $n-2$ triangles, each contributing exactly one monochromatic side. Sin
ce each polygon side belongs to exactly one triangle, these $n-2$ monochromatic
edges are distinct polygon sides. With $n$ sides total, exactly $n-2$ are monoch
romatic and **exactly 2 are bichromatic**.

The number of bichromatic sides equals the number of color changes around the po
lygon. Having exactly 2 color changes means the coloring has **exactly 2 runs**:
 one consecutive block of black vertices and one consecutive block of white vert
ices, both nonempty.

**Sufficient condition: every 2-run coloring is good.**

Suppose vertices $1, 2, \ldots, j$ are black and $j+1, \ldots, n$ are white, wit
h $1 \leq j \leq n-1$. The two bichromatic sides are $(j, j+1)$ and $(n, 1)$.

**Case $j = 1$ or $j = n-1$:** Fan from the unique vertex of one color to all no
n-adjacent vertices of the other color. For $j=1$, draw diagonals $(1,3), (1,4),
 \ldots, (1, n-1)$, all bichromatic. This gives $n-3$ diagonals forming a valid
triangulation. ✓

**Case $2 \leq j \leq n-2$:** Draw the bichromatic diagonal $(1, j+1)$, splittin
g the polygon into:
- **Part A:** $\{1, 2, \ldots, j, j+1\}$, a $(j+1)$-gon with $j$ black vertices
and one white vertex $j+1$. Fan from $j+1$: draw diagonals $(j+1, 2), (j+1, 3),
\ldots, (j+1, j-1)$, giving $j-2$ bichromatic diagonals. ✓
- **Part B:** $\{1, j+1, j+2, \ldots, n\}$, an $(n-j+1)$-gon with one black vert
ex $1$ and $n-j$ white vertices. Fan from $1$: draw diagonals $(1, j+2), (1, j+3
), \ldots, (1, n-1)$, giving $n-j-2$ bichromatic diagonals. ✓

Total diagonals: $1 + (j-2) + (n-j-2) = n-3$. ✓ All bichromatic. This is a valid
 triangulation.

**Counting 2-run colorings.**

A 2-run coloring is uniquely determined by:
- The size $b$ of the black block, where $1 \leq b \leq n-1$ ($n-1$ choices).
- The starting vertex of the black block ($n$ choices, one per vertex of the $n$
-gon).

Each 2-run coloring has a unique black block: its starting vertex is the unique
black vertex whose cyclic predecessor is white, and its size is the number of bl
ack vertices. So no overcounting occurs.

$$\text{Number of good colorings} = n(n-1).$$

**Verification for $n=4$:** $n(n-1) = 12$. The 4 bad colorings are BBBB, WWWW (m
onochromatic) and BWBW, WBWB (4 bichromatic sides). Total $16 - 4 = 12$. ✓

The number of good colorings is $\boxed{n(n-1)}$.
