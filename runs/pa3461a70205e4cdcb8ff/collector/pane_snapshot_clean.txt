## Proof

**Setup and feasibility.** We have $n$ nodes $A_1,\dots,A_n$ on a cycle, node $A
_i$ holding $a_i$ units. A redistribution sends $x_i$ units from $A_i$ to $A_{i+
1}$ (indices mod $n$, so $x_0:=x_n$). The post-redistribution amount at $A_i$ is
 required to equal a common target $N$:

$$a_i - x_i + x_{i-1} = N, \qquad i=1,\dots,n. \tag{1}$$

Summing (1) over all $i$ telescopes the transfers (each $x_i$ leaves one node an
d enters another), giving $\sum_i a_i = nN$, so feasibility forces

$$N = \bar a := \tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n a_i. \tag{2}$$

Henceforth $N=\bar a$.

**Reducing to a one-parameter family.** Define $b_i := a_i - N$ (so $\sum_i b_i=
0$) and partial sums

$$S_k := \sum_{i=1}^{k} b_i = \sum_{i=1}^{k}(a_i-N), \qquad k=1,\dots,n, \quad S
_0:=0. \tag{3}$$

Equation (1) rewrites as $x_i = x_{i-1} + b_i$. Unrolling from $x_0$:

$$x_k = x_0 + S_k, \qquad k=1,\dots,n. \tag{4}$$

Consistency with $x_0=x_n$ is automatic since $S_n=\sum b_i=0$, so $x_n=x_0+S_n=
x_0$. Thus the entire feasible set is parametrized by the single free real numbe
r $x_0$, and

$$F = \sum_{k=1}^{n}|x_k| = \sum_{k=1}^{n}|x_0 + S_k|. \tag{5}$$

Setting $c := -x_0$, the problem becomes

$$\min_{c\in\mathbb R}\; G(c) := \sum_{k=1}^{n}|S_k - c|. \tag{6}$$

**Solving the median problem.** $G$ is convex, being a sum of convex functions.
It is differentiable except at $c\in\{S_1,\dots,S_n\}$, with

$$G'(c) = \#\{k: S_k < c\} - \#\{k: S_k > c\}. \tag{7}$$

A minimizer $c^*$ must have $G'(c^*)=0$ (or $0\in\partial G(c^*)$ at a kink), i.
e. the number of $S_k$ below $c^*$ equals the number above $c^*$. This is exactl
y the **median** condition:

- If $n$ is odd, $c^*$ is the unique middle order statistic of $\{S_1,\dots,S_n\
}$.
- If $n$ is even, any $c^*$ in the closed interval between the two middle order
statistics is optimal.

Denote any such median by $m := \operatorname{med}(S_1,\dots,S_n)$. The minimum
is

$$\boxed{\;\min F \;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{n}\bigl|S_k - m\bigr|,\qquad S_k=\sum_{i=1}^
{k}(a_i-\bar a),\quad m=\operatorname{med}(S_1,\dots,S_n).\;} \tag{8}$$

The optimal transfers are recovered via $x_0 = -m$ and $x_k = S_k - m$ for $k=1,
\dots,n$.

**Verification (sanity check).** Take $n=2$, $a_1=0$, $a_2=2$. Then $\bar a=1$,
$b_1=-1$, $b_2=1$, $S_1=-1$, $S_2=0$. Any $m\in[-1,0]$ is a median; choosing $m=
0$ gives $x_0=0$, $x_1=-1$, $x_2=0$, $F=1$. Check (1): $a_1-x_1+x_0=0-(-1)+0=1=N
$ ✓; $a_2-x_2+x_1=2-0+(-1)=1=N$ ✓. Choosing $m=-1$ gives $x_0=1$, $x_1=0$, $x_2=
1$, $F=1$, also feasible. Both yield $F=1=|{-1}-0|+|0-0|$, matching (8). ∎
