Looking at this problem, I need to find all real polynomials of degree $n \geq 2
$ with $n$ distinct real roots $r_1 < \cdots < r_n$ such that the derivative van
ishes at each consecutive midpoint.

 solutions, sketches, or formalization notes. Proceeding.

## Proof

**Setup.** Since $p$ has degree $n$ with $n$ distinct real roots $r_1 < r_2 < \c
dots < r_n$, we can write
$$p(x) = c\prod_{j=1}^{n}(x - r_j), \qquad c \neq 0.$$

For $x$ not equal to any root, the logarithmic derivative gives:
$$\frac{p'(x)}{p(x)} = \sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{x - r_j}.$$

Set $m_i = \frac{r_i + r_{i+1}}{2}$ and $d_i = r_{i+1} - r_i > 0$, so $m_i - r_i
 = d_i/2$ and $m_i - r_{i+1} = -d_i/2$.

Since $m_i$ lies strictly between $r_i$ and $r_{i+1}$, we have $p(m_i) \neq 0$,
so $p'(m_i) = 0$ if and only if
$$\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m_i - r_j} = 0. \tag{$\star$}$$

**Key simplification.** The terms $j = i$ and $j = i+1$ in $(\star)$ cancel:
$$\frac{1}{m_i - r_i} + \frac{1}{m_i - r_{i+1}} = \frac{1}{d_i/2} + \frac{1}{-d_
i/2} = 0.$$

Thus condition $(\star)$ reduces to:
$$\sum_{\substack{j=1\\j\neq i,\, j\neq i+1}}^{n}\frac{1}{m_i - r_j} = 0. \tag{$
\star\star$}$$

---

**Case $n = 2$: The condition is always satisfied.**

The reduced sum $(\star\star)$ is empty (there are no indices $j \neq 1,2$), so
it equals $0$ automatically. We verify directly:
$$p(x) = c(x-r_1)(x-r_2), \quad p'(x) = c(2x - r_1 - r_2),$$
so $p'(m_1) = p'\!\left(\tfrac{r_1+r_2}{2}\right) = 0$. ✓

Every real quadratic with two distinct real roots works.

---

**Case $n \geq 3$: No solution exists.**

Consider the condition at $m_1 = \frac{r_1 + r_2}{2}$. Since $r_1 < r_2 < r_3 <
\cdots < r_n$, we have
$$m_1 < r_2 < r_j \quad \text{for all } j \geq 3.$$
Therefore $m_1 - r_j < 0$ for every $j \geq 3$, which gives
$$\frac{1}{m_1 - r_j} < 0 \quad \text{for all } j \geq 3.$$

The reduced condition $(\star\star)$ at $i = 1$ becomes:
$$\sum_{j=3}^{n}\frac{1}{m_1 - r_j} = 0.$$

But this is a sum of $n - 2 \geq 1$ strictly negative terms, so the sum is **str
ictly negative** — a contradiction.

(Equivalently, one may use $i = n-1$: then $m_{n-1} > r_{n-2}$, so every term $\
frac{1}{m_{n-1} - r_j}$ for $j \leq n-2$ is strictly positive, giving a strictly
 positive sum $\neq 0$.)

Hence no polynomial of degree $n \geq 3$ satisfies the conditions.

---

## Conclusion

The polynomials satisfying the given conditions are **exactly the real quadratic
s with two distinct real roots**:
$$\boxed{p(x) = a(x - r_1)(x - r_2), \quad a \in \mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},\; r_1
, r_2 \in \mathbb{R},\; r_1 < r_2.}$$

Equivalently, these are all real polynomials of degree $2$ with positive discrim
inant. No polynomial of degree $n \geq 3$ satisfies the conditions.
