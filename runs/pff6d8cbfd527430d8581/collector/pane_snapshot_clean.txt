## Proof of Part (i)

 that $\ell \mid p(m)$.*

*Proof of Key Lemma.*
- **$\ell = 2$:** $p(2) = 2$, so $2 \mid p(2)$. ✓
- **$\ell = 3$:** $p(3) = 3$, so $3 \mid p(3)$. ✓
- **$\ell \geq 5$:** We invoke **Ono's Theorem** (K. Ono, *"Distribution of the
partition function modulo $m$,"* Annals of Mathematics **151** (2000), 293–307):

  > **Theorem (Ono, 2000).** For every positive integer $M$ with $\gcd(M, 6) = 1
$, there exist integers $A \geq 1$ and $B$ with $0 \leq B < A$ such that $p(An +
 B) \equiv 0 \pmod{M}$ for all $n \geq 0$.

  Since $\ell \geq 5$ is prime, $\gcd(\ell, 6) = 1$, so Ono's theorem applies. S
ince $p(0) = 1$ and $\ell \nmid 1$ for $\ell \geq 5$, we must have $B \geq 1$ (o
therwise $p(A \cdot 0 + B) = p(0) = 1 \not\equiv 0 \pmod{\ell}$). Taking $n = 0$
 yields $\ell \mid p(B)$ with $B \geq 1$. $\square$

**Remark on Ono's Theorem.** The partition generating function $P(q) = \sum_{n \
geq 0} p(n)q^n = \prod_{m \geq 1}(1-q^m)^{-1}$ is essentially the reciprocal of
the Dedekind eta function, making it a modular form of weight $-1/2$ on $\mathrm
{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$. Ono's theorem exploits the theory of modular forms modulo $
\ell$ (the Serre–Swinnerton-Dyer filtration theory) to establish that the partit
ion function vanishes modulo $M$ on arithmetic progressions. The classical Raman
ujan congruences ($p(5n+4) \equiv 0 \pmod{5}$, $p(7n+5) \equiv 0 \pmod{7}$, $p(1
1n+6) \equiv 0 \pmod{11}$) are special cases.
