## Proof

**Setup and reformulation.** For a unit vector $u$ (normal to line $K$), set $M(
u)=\max_{x\in S}\langle u,x\rangle$, $m(u)=\min_{x\in S}\langle u,x\rangle$, and
 $w(u)=M(u)-m(u)$. The midline $\overline{L}$ satisfies $\langle u,x\rangle=\fra
c{M+m}{2}$. A point $p\in S$ lies in $B_S(K,t)$ iff
$$\left|\langle u,p\rangle-\tfrac{M+m}{2}\right|\le \tfrac{t}{2}(M-m).$$
Setting $a=M(u)-\langle u,p\rangle\ge 0$ and $b=\langle u,p\rangle-m(u)\ge 0$ (s
o $a+b=w$), this becomes $|a-b|\le t(a+b)$, equivalently
$$\max\!\left(\frac{a}{b},\frac{b}{a}\right)\le \frac{1+t}{1-t}\qquad (t<1).$$
Thus we seek the smallest $t$ such that some $p\in S$ satisfies $\max(a/b,b/a)\l
e\frac{1+t}{1-t}$ for **every** direction $u$.

**Key theorem (centroid bound).** *Let $c$ be the centroid of $S$. For every dir
ection $u$:*
$$m(u)+\frac{w(u)}{3}\le \langle u,c\rangle\le M(u)-\frac{w(u)}{3}.$$

*Proof.* WLOG $m(u)=0$, $M(u)=1$ (rescale). Let $A(t)$ be the length of the cros
s-section $S\cap\{x:\langle u,x\rangle=t\}$ for $t\in[0,1]$. By Brunn–Minkowski,
 $A$ is concave and non-negative. Then
$$\langle u,c\rangle=\frac{\int_0^1 t\,A(t)\,dt}{\int_0^1 A(t)\,dt}.$$
We must show $\int_0^1 t\,g(t)\,dt\ge\frac13\int_0^1 g(t)\,dt$ for concave $g\ge
 0$ on $[0,1]$ (the upper bound $\le\frac23$ follows by symmetry $t\mapsto 1-t$)
.

Write $g(t)=g(0)(1-t)+g(1)\,t+h(t)$, where $h$ is concave with $h(0)=h(1)=0$ and
 $h\ge 0$ (since $g$ lies above its chord). Then
$$\int_0^1 t\,g\,dt-\tfrac13\int_0^1 g\,dt = \frac{g(1)}{6}+\int_0^1\!\left(t-\t
frac13\right)h(t)\,dt.$$
Since $h$ is concave with $h(0)=h(1)=0$, $h\ge 0$, let $a$ be its peak point wit
h $h(a)=H$. By concavity, $h(t)\ge H\,\phi_a(t)$ where $\phi_a(t)=\min(t/a,\,(1-
t)/(1-a))$ is the tent function. A direct computation gives $\int_0^1(t-\frac13)
\phi_a(t)\,dt=\frac{a}{6}\ge 0$, so $\int_0^1(t-\frac13)h(t)\,dt\ge \frac{H\,a}{
6}\ge 0$. Hence the whole expression is $\ge g(1)/6\ge 0$. $\square$

**$t=\frac13$ suffices for all $S$.** At the centroid $c$, the theorem gives $a=
M-\langle u,c\rangle\le\frac23 w$ and $b=\langle u,c\rangle-m\ge\frac13 w$, so $
a/b\le 2$; similarly $b/a\le 2$. Therefore $\max(a/b,b/a)\le 2=\frac{1+1/3}{1-1/
3}$ for every $u$, meaning $c\in B_S(K,\frac13)$ for all $K$. Since $c\in S$ (ce
ntroid of a convex body), the intersection is nonempty.

**$t=\frac13$ is necessary (triangle is extremal).** Let $S$ be the triangle wit
h vertices $(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)$, centroid $c=(\frac13,\frac13)$. For any $p=(x,y)
\in S$ (so $x,y\ge0$, $x+y\le1$), consider three directions:

- $u=(1,0)$: ratio $=\max\!\bigl(\frac{1-x}{x},\frac{x}{1-x}\bigr)$. Both $\le 2
$ requires $\frac13\le x\le\frac23$.
- $u=(0,1)$: similarly requires $\frac13\le y\le\frac23$.
- $u=(1,1)/\sqrt2$: ratio $=\max\!\bigl(\frac{1-x-y}{x+y},\frac{x+y}{1-x-y}\bigr
)$. Both $\le 2$ requires $\frac13\le x+y\le\frac23$.

If all three ratios are $\le 2$, then $x\ge\frac13$, $y\ge\frac13$ forces $x+y\g
e\frac23$, and combined with $x+y\le\frac23$ gives $x+y=\frac23$, hence $x=y=\fr
ac13$. So the **only** point achieving $\max\le 2$ in these three directions is
the centroid, where all three ratios equal exactly $2$.

Therefore $\min_{p\in S}\max_u\max(a/b,b/a)=2$ for the triangle. For any $t<\fra
c13$, we have $\frac{1+t}{1-t}<2$, so **no** point $p\in S$ lies in all bands $B
_S(K,t)$. The intersection is empty.

**Conclusion.** The value $t=\frac13$ works for every convex body $S$ (via the c
entroid), and no smaller value works (the triangle requires $t\ge\frac13$). The
smallest such $t$ is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{3}}.$$
