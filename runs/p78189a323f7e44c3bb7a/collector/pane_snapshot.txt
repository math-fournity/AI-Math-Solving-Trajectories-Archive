# Curvature via the Laplace–Beltrami Operator

## Setup

The surface carries the Riemannian metric (in coordinates $(x, y)$ with $x > 0$)

$$g_{ij} \;=\; \frac{1}{x^{2}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qqu
ad g \;=\; \det(g_{ij}) \;=\; \frac{1}{x^{4}}.$$

This is a **conformally flat (isothermal/isometric) metric**: $g_{ij} = e^{2\phi
}\,\delta_{ij}$ with

$$e^{2\phi} = \frac{1}{x^{2}} \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \phi = -\ln x.$$

## Laplace–Beltrami operator

For a scalar function $f$,

$$\Delta_{g} f \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{|g|}}\,\partial_{i}\!\Bigl(\sqrt{|g|}\,g^{ij
}\,\partial_{j} f\Bigr).$$

We have $g^{ij} = x^{2}\,\delta^{ij}$ and $\sqrt{|g|} = \tfrac{1}{x^{2}}$, so $\
sqrt{|g|}\,g^{ij} = \delta^{ij}$. Therefore

$$\Delta_{g} f \;=\; x^{2}\Bigl(\partial_{x}(\partial_{x} f) + \partial_{y}(\par
tial_{y} f)\Bigr) \;=\; x^{2}\bigl(\partial_{x}^{2} f + \partial_{y}^{2} f\bigr)
 \;=\; x^{2}\,\Delta f,$$

where $\Delta = \partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2}$ is the **flat** Euclidean Lap
lacian.

## Curvature formula from the Laplace–Beltrami operator

For an isothermal metric $g_{ij}=e^{2\phi}\delta_{ij}$, the Gaussian curvature i
s given by the classical identity

$$K \;=\; -\,\frac{1}{e^{2\phi}}\,\Delta\phi \;=\; -\,\Delta_{g}\,\phi,$$

since $\Delta_{g}\phi = e^{-2\phi}\Delta\phi$.

## Computing $\Delta\phi$

With $\phi = -\ln x$:

$$\partial_{x}\phi = -\frac{1}{x}, \qquad \partial_{x}^{2}\phi = \frac{1}{x^{2}}
, \qquad \partial_{y}\phi = 0,\quad \partial_{y}^{2}\phi = 0.$$

Hence

$$\Delta\phi \;=\; \frac{1}{x^{2}}.$$

## Applying the curvature formula

$$K \;=\; -\,\frac{1}{e^{2\phi}}\,\Delta\phi \;=\; -\,\frac{1}{1/x^{2}}\cdot\fra
c{1}{x^{2}} \;=\; -\,x^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{x^{2}} \;=\; -1.$$

Equivalently, via the Laplace–Beltrami operator directly:

$$\Delta_{g}\phi \;=\; x^{2}\,\Delta\phi \;=\; x^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{x^{2}} \;=\; 1
 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; K \;=\; -\,\Delta_{g}\phi \;=\; -1.$$

## Conclusion

The metric $\tfrac{1}{x^{2}}\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\b
igr)$ is the **Poincaré upper half-plane model** of hyperbolic geometry, and the
 Laplace–Beltrami computation confirms its constant Gaussian curvature:

$$\boxed{K \;=\; -1}.$$
