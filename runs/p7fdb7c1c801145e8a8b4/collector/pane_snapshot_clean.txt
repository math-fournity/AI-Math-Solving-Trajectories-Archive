## Proof

**Claim:** The statement is **true**. If $f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_k x^k$ conve
rges for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$, then $f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_k z^k$ converge
s for every $z\in\mathbb{C}$.

**Key fact (Abel).** For a power series $\sum a_k z^k$, if it converges at some
point $z_0$ (with $|z_0|=r$), then it converges **absolutely** for every $z$ wit
h $|z|<r$.

*Proof of the key fact.* Since $\sum a_k z_0^k$ converges, its terms are bounded
: $|a_k z_0^k|\le M$ for some $M$ and all $k$. For $|z|<r=|z_0|$, write $|a_k z^
k|=|a_k z_0^k|\cdot|z/z_0|^k\le M\cdot(|z|/r)^k$. Since $|z|/r<1$, the geometric
 series $\sum M(|z|/r)^k$ converges, so $\sum a_k z^k$ converges absolutely by c
omparison. $\square$

**Main argument.** Let $R\in[0,\infty]$ denote the radius of convergence of $\su
m a_k z^k$, given by the Cauchy–Hadamard formula
$$R=\frac{1}{\limsup_{k\to\infty}|a_k|^{1/k}}.$$
By the key fact, convergence at any point of modulus $r$ forces $R\ge r$.

Now assume $f(x)$ converges for every real $x$. For any $r>0$, choose the real n
umber $x_0=r$ (or $x_0=-r$). Then $|x_0|=r$, and by hypothesis $\sum a_k x_0^k$
converges. By the key fact, $R\ge r$. Since $r>0$ was arbitrary, $R\ge r$ for ev
ery $r>0$, hence
$$R=\infty.$$

**Conclusion.** A power series with radius of convergence $R=\infty$ converges a
bsolutely for every $z\in\mathbb{C}$ (the Cauchy–Hadamard formula gives $\limsup
|a_k|^{1/k}=0$, so for any $z$, $|a_k z^k|^{1/k}=|a_k|^{1/k}|z|\to 0$, and the r
oot test yields absolute convergence). Therefore $f(z)$ converges for every $z\i
n\mathbb{C}$.

The implication holds: **real-line convergence everywhere $\Longrightarrow$ comp
lex-plane convergence everywhere.** (The converse is trivial.) The essential rea
son is that the radius of convergence is governed by $\limsup|a_k|^{1/k}$, which
 depends only on the coefficients and is identical whether we test convergence o
ver $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$; testing on the unbounded real line already for
ces this radius to be infinite.
