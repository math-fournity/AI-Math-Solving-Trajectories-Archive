# Problem

A specialized irrigation system is designed for a triangular field with boundaries measuring exactly 5, 12, and 13 decameters. At the field’s precise center of equilibrium (the incenter $I$), a rotating sprinkler is installed.

The sprinkler projects a circular spray pattern $\omega$ of radius $r$. This circle is sized such that its perimeter intersects the three boundary fences of the triangular field at exactly 6 distinct points. These intersection points create segments along the fences. If you draw lines from the sprinkler head at $I$ to each of these 6 intersection points, three "sector angles" are formed within the circle, each subtending one of the segments of the fences that lie inside the spray zone.

A sensor calibration reveals a specific property of this setup: the sum of the three angles subtended by the chords formed where the circle $\omega$ passes through the interior of the field's three sides is exactly $180^\circ$.

Determine the area of the spray pattern $\omega$ divided by $\pi$.
