Looking at this problem, I need to find all real values of $m$ where the ellipse
 $x^2+xy+y^2=m$ meets the hyperbola $xy=n$ exactly at the hyperbola's two vertic
es, for non-zero integers $n \in [-81, 81]$.

## Finding the Vertices of $xy = n$

The hyperbola $xy = n$ has its center at the origin. Minimizing $x^2+y^2$ subjec
t to $xy=n$:

- **$n > 0$:** vertices at $(\sqrt{n}, \sqrt{n})$ and $(-\sqrt{n}, -\sqrt{n})$ (
where $x = y$)
- **$n < 0$:** vertices at $(\sqrt{|n|}, -\sqrt{|n|})$ and $(-\sqrt{|n|}, \sqrt{
|n|})$ (where $x = -y$)

## Analyzing the Intersection

Substituting $xy = n$ into the ellipse: $x^2 + y^2 = m - n$.

From this:
$$(x+y)^2 = (m-n) + 2n = m+n, \quad (x-y)^2 = (m-n) - 2n = m - 3n$$

The intersection points are determined by the signs of $x+y = \pm\sqrt{m+n}$ and
 $x-y = \pm\sqrt{m-3n}$, giving up to 4 points (when both $m+n > 0$ and $m-3n >
0$).

## Case 1: $n > 0$ (vertices have $x = y$, i.e., $x - y = 0$)

For the intersection to be exactly the two vertices, we need $x - y = 0$, which
requires:
$$m - 3n = 0 \implies m = 3n$$

Then $x + y = \pm\sqrt{m+n} = \pm\sqrt{4n} = \pm 2\sqrt{n}$, giving $x = y = \pm
\sqrt{n}$ — exactly the two vertices. ✓

This is the **unique** $m$ value for each $n > 0$ (any other $m$ gives 0 or 4 in
tersection points, or 2 points that aren't the vertices).

## Case 2: $n < 0$ (vertices have $x = -y$, i.e., $x + y = 0$)

For the intersection to be exactly the two vertices, we need $x + y = 0$, which
requires:
$$m + n = 0 \implies m = -n = |n|$$

Then $x - y = \pm\sqrt{m - 3n} = \pm\sqrt{|n| + 3|n|} = \pm 2\sqrt{|n|}$, giving
 $x = \pm\sqrt{|n|}$, $y = \mp\sqrt{|n|}$ — exactly the two vertices. ✓

This is the **unique** $m$ value for each $n < 0$.

## Collecting All Distinct $m$ Values

- From $n \in \{1, 2, \ldots, 81\}$: $m = 3n \in \{3, 6, 9, \ldots, 243\}$
- From $n \in \{-1, -2, \ldots, -81\}$: $m = |n| \in \{1, 2, 3, \ldots, 81\}$

The **union** of these sets: $\{1, 2, \ldots, 81\} \cup \{3, 6, \ldots, 243\}$.
Since $\{3, 6, \ldots, 81\} \subseteq \{1, 2, \ldots, 81\}$, the distinct values
 are:
$$\{1, 2, \ldots, 81\} \cup \{84, 87, 90, \ldots, 243\}$$

## Computing the Sum

$$\sum_{k=1}^{81} k = \frac{81 \cdot 82}{2} = 3321$$

For the multiples of 3 from 84 to 243 (i.e., $3 \times 28$ through $3 \times 81$
, which is 54 terms):

$$\sum_{j=28}^{81} 3j = 3 \cdot \frac{(28 + 81) \cdot 54}{2} = 3 \cdot \frac{109
 \cdot 54}{2} = 3 \cdot 2943 = 8829$$

**Total sum** $= 3321 + 8829 = 12150$

The last three digits are $\boxed{150}$.
