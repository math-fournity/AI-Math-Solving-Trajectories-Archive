## Proof

**Setup.** We have a closed loop of string of length $N$. We place $N$ points in
dependently and uniformly at random along the loop, then cut at each point, prod
ucing $N$ pieces. Let $X$ denote the length of a (uniformly) randomly chosen pie
ce. The average piece length is $N/N = 1$. We determine the limiting distributio
n of $X$ as $N \to \infty$.

**Survival function of a single piece.** Fix one cut point $P$ on the loop. Cons
ider the piece that begins at $P$ and extends clockwise until the next cut point
. Let $X$ be its length. The event $\{X > x\}$ occurs exactly when **none** of t
he remaining $N-1$ cut points falls in the clockwise arc of length $x$ starting
at $P$.

Since each of the other $N-1$ points is placed independently and uniformly on a
loop of length $N$, the probability that any single one of them avoids this arc
is $1 - x/N$. By independence,

$$\mathbb{P}(X > x) = \left(1 - \frac{x}{N}\right)^{N-1}, \qquad 0 \le x \le N.$
$

**Taking the limit.** As $N \to \infty$, using the classical limit $(1 - x/N)^N
\to e^{-x}$,

$$\lim_{N\to\infty} \mathbb{P}(X > x) = \lim_{N\to\infty}\left(1 - \frac{x}{N}\r
ight)^{N-1} = e^{-x}, \qquad x \ge 0.$$

**Density.** Differentiating the limiting survival function $S(x) = e^{-x}$ give
s the limiting probability density function

$$\boxed{f(x) = e^{-x}, \qquad x \ge 0}$$

(and $f(x) = 0$ for $x < 0$).

**Name.** This is the **exponential distribution** with rate parameter $\lambda
= 1$ (equivalently, mean $1$), denoted $\mathrm{Exp}(1)$.

**Remark on the marginal distribution for finite $N$.** For completeness, the ex
act marginal density for finite $N$ is

$$f_N(x) = \frac{N-1}{N}\left(1 - \frac{x}{N}\right)^{N-2}, \qquad 0 \le x \le N
,$$

which is a scaled $\mathrm{Beta}(1, N-1)$ distribution (the spacings on a circle
 follow a symmetric Dirichlet distribution). One readily checks $\frac{N-1}{N} \
to 1$ and $(1 - x/N)^{N-2} \to e^{-x}$, confirming the limit above. The key poin
t is that although the $N$ pieces are **not** independent (they must sum to $N$)
, each piece's *marginal* distribution converges to $\mathrm{Exp}(1)$, and the d
ependence becomes negligible in the large-$N$ limit in the sense that the spacin
gs behave asymptotically like i.i.d. exponential random variables conditioned on
 summing to $N$.
