## Proof

We compute the surface area of the Steinmetz solid — the intersection of the two
 cylinders
$$C_1: x^2+y^2=a^2, \qquad C_2: x^2+z^2=a^2.$$
The bounded solid is
$$\mathcal S=\{(x,y,z): x^2+y^2\le a^2,\; x^2+z^2\le a^2\}.$$

Its boundary consists of two pieces: the part of $C_1$ lying inside $C_2$, and t
he part of $C_2$ lying inside $C_1$. By the symmetry $(y,z)\mapsto(z,y)$ exchang
ing the two cylinders, these two pieces have equal area. Hence
$$A_{\text{total}} = 2\,A_1,$$
where $A_1$ is the area of the portion of $C_1$ that lies within $C_2$.

**Computing $A_1$.** Parametrize $C_1$ by
$$x=a\cos\theta,\quad y=a\sin\theta,\quad z=z,\qquad \theta\in[0,2\pi),\;z\in\ma
thbb R.$$
The surface element on the cylinder of radius $a$ is $dS = a\,d\theta\,dz$.

A point of $C_1$ belongs to the boundary of $\mathcal S$ precisely when it lies
inside $C_2$, i.e.
$$x^2+z^2\le a^2 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; a^2\cos^2\theta+z^2\le a^2 \;\Longleftr
ightarrow\; z^2\le a^2\sin^2\theta \;\Longleftrightarrow\; |z|\le a|\sin\theta|.
$$

Therefore
$$A_1=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_{-a|\sin\theta|}^{\,a|\sin\theta|} a\,dz\,d\theta
=\int_0^{2\pi} 2a^2|\sin\theta|\,d\theta
=2a^2\cdot 4 = 8a^2,$$
since $\int_0^{2\pi}|\sin\theta|\,d\theta=4$.

**Total.**
$$A_{\text{total}} = 2\,A_1 = 2\cdot 8a^2 = \boxed{16a^2}.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
