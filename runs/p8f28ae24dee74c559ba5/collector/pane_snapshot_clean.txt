**Theorem.** *Let $n$ be an odd perfect integer and $p$ its smallest prime divis
or. Then the exponent of $p$ in the prime factorization of $n$ is even.*

**Proof.** Write $n = p^a \cdot m$ where $a \geq 1$, $p \nmid m$, and $p$ is the
 smallest prime divisor of $n$. Since $n$ is odd, $p$ is an odd prime, so $p \ge
q 3$. Because $p$ is the smallest prime dividing $n$ and $p \nmid m$, **every pr
ime divisor of $m$ is strictly greater than $p$**.

Since $n$ is perfect, $\sigma(n) = 2n$. By multiplicativity of $\sigma$,

$$\sigma(p^a)\,\sigma(m) = 2\,p^a\,m. \tag{1}$$

Now $\sigma(p^a) = 1 + p + p^2 + \cdots + p^a \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$, so $\gcd\bigl(
\sigma(p^a),\, p^a\bigr) = 1$. From (1) it follows that

$$p^a \mid \sigma(m) \qquad\text{and}\qquad \sigma(p^a) \mid 2m. \tag{2}$$

**Suppose for contradiction that $a$ is odd.** Then $a+1$ is even, and we factor

$$\sigma(p^a) = 1 + p + p^2 + \cdots + p^a = (1+p)\bigl(1 + p^2 + p^4 + \cdots +
 p^{a-1}\bigr).$$

Each of the $a+1$ summands $1, p, \ldots, p^a$ is odd, and there is an even numb
er of them, so $\sigma(p^a)$ is **even**. Write $\sigma(p^a) = 2T$; then by (2),

$$2T \mid 2m \implies T \mid m.$$

Using the factorization above,

$$T = \frac{\sigma(p^a)}{2} = \frac{1+p}{2}\cdot\bigl(1 + p^2 + p^4 + \cdots + p
^{a-1}\bigr).$$

Since $p$ is odd, $\frac{1+p}{2}$ is a positive integer; since $p \geq 3$, we ha
ve $\frac{1+p}{2} \geq 2$. Moreover $\frac{1+p}{2} < p$ (because $1 + p < 2p$ fo
r $p > 1$).

Because $\frac{1+p}{2}$ divides $T$ and $T \mid m$, we get $\frac{1+p}{2} \mid m
$. Since $\frac{1+p}{2} \geq 2$, it possesses at least one prime divisor $r$, an
d

$$r \leq \frac{1+p}{2} < p.$$

But $r \mid m$, so $r$ is a prime divisor of $n$ smaller than $p$ — contradictin
g the definition of $p$ as the **smallest** prime divisor of $n$.

The contradiction forces us to reject the assumption that $a$ is odd. Therefore
$a$ is even. $\blacksquare$
