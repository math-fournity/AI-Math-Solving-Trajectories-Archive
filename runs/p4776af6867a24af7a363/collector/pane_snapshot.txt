## Evaluation of $n^{\aleph_0}$ for $n \in \mathbb{N}$

We interpret $n^{\aleph_0}$ as the cardinality of the set of all functions from
a set of cardinality $\aleph_0$ (e.g. $\mathbb{N}$) to a set of cardinality $n$.

### Case 1: $n = 0$

Here the codomain is $\emptyset$. Since $\mathbb{N} \neq \emptyset$, there are *
*no** functions $\mathbb{N} \to \emptyset$. Hence

$$0^{\aleph_0} = 0.$$

### Case 2: $n = 1$

The codomain is a singleton $\{*\}$. There is exactly one function from any set
to a singleton (the constant function). Hence

$$1^{\aleph_0} = 1.$$

### Case 3: $n \geq 2$ (finite)

We claim $n^{\aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph_0}$ for every finite $n \geq 2$.

**Lower bound.** Since $2 \leq n$, any function $\mathbb{N} \to \{0,1\}$ is also
 a function $\mathbb{N} \to \{0,1,\dots,n-1\}$ (via the inclusion $\{0,1\} \hook
rightarrow \{0,1,\dots,n-1\}$). This gives an injection $2^{\mathbb{N}} \hookrig
htarrow n^{\mathbb{N}}$, so

$$2^{\aleph_0} \leq n^{\aleph_0}.$$

**Upper bound.** Since $n$ is finite, $n \leq 2^{\aleph_0}$ (every finite cardin
al is bounded by any infinite cardinal). Therefore

$$n^{\aleph_0} \leq \left(2^{\aleph_0}\right)^{\aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph_0 \cdot \al
eph_0}.$$

Since $\aleph_0 \cdot \aleph_0 = \aleph_0$ (the product of two countable cardina
ls is countable — equivalently, $\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}$ is countable),

$$n^{\aleph_0} \leq 2^{\aleph_0}.$$

**Conclusion by Cantor–Schröder–Bernstein.** Combining both inequalities,

$$\boxed{n^{\aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph_0} \quad \text{for all finite } n \geq 2.}$$

This cardinal $2^{\aleph_0}$ is the **cardinality of the continuum**, denoted $\
mathfrak{c}$.

### Can $n^{\aleph_0}$ be expressed as $\aleph_1$?

For $n = 0$ and $n = 1$, the values are $0$ and $1$ respectively — both finite,
hence certainly not $\aleph_1$.

For $n \geq 2$, we have $n^{\aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph_0} = \mathfrak{c}$. The questi
on of whether

$$2^{\aleph_0} = \aleph_1$$

is precisely the **Continuum Hypothesis (CH)**. By the landmark results of Gödel
 (1940) and Cohen (1963):

- **Gödel** showed that CH is consistent with ZFC (it holds in the constructible
 universe $L$).
- **Cohen** showed that $\neg$CH is consistent with ZFC (via forcing, one can ma
ke $2^{\aleph_0}$ as large as desired, in particular $> \aleph_1$).

Therefore the statement $2^{\aleph_0} = \aleph_1$ is **independent of ZFC** — it
 can neither be proved nor disproved from the standard axioms of set theory.

**Conclusion.** The expression $n^{\aleph_0}$ **cannot be determined** to equal
$\aleph_1$ within ZFC. Specifically:

| $n$ | $n^{\aleph_0}$ | Equals $\aleph_1$? |
|-----|----------------|---------------------|
| $0$ | $0$ | No (finite) |
| $1$ | $1$ | No (finite) |
| $\geq 2$ | $2^{\aleph_0} = \mathfrak{c}$ | Independent of ZFC (this is CH) |

The identity $n^{\aleph_0} = \aleph_1$ for $n \geq 2$ is exactly the Continuum H
ypothesis, which is undecidable in ZFC.
