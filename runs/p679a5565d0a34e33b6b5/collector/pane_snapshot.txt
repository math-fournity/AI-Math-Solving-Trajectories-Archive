## Proof

Let $A = B_1(0) \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ with $n \ge 2$, and $f(x) = |x|^{\alpha}$
(with $f(0)=0$), $\alpha \ne 0$. We determine when $f$ is weakly differentiable
on $A$, i.e. when $f \in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)$: $f \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(A
)$ and each weak partial derivative $\partial_i f$ exists as an $L^1_{\mathrm{lo
c}}(A)$ function.

**Claim.** $f$ is weakly differentiable on $A$ if and only if $\alpha > 1 - n$.

---

### Integrability of $f$ and of the candidate derivative

Near the origin, using polar coordinates $dx = r^{n-1}dr\,d\omega$:

- $|f| = |x|^{\alpha}$ is integrable near $0$ iff $\int_0^{\varepsilon} r^{\alph
a+n-1}dr < \infty$, i.e. $\alpha > -n$.
- Away from $0$, $f$ is smooth, so only the origin matters.

The classical gradient (for $x \ne 0$) is
$$\partial_i f(x) = \alpha\, x_i\, |x|^{\alpha-2}, \qquad |\nabla f(x)| = |\alph
a|\,|x|^{\alpha-1}.$$
This is integrable near $0$ iff $\int_0^{\varepsilon} r^{\alpha-1+n-1}dr = \int_
0^{\varepsilon} r^{\alpha+n-2}dr < \infty$, i.e. $\alpha > 1-n$.

Since $n \ge 2$, we have $1-n \ge -n+1 > -n$, so the condition $\alpha > 1-n$ au
tomatically implies $\alpha > -n$. Thus the binding constraint comes from the de
rivative.

---

### Sufficiency: $\alpha > 1-n \Rightarrow f$ is weakly differentiable

Set $g_i(x) := \alpha\, x_i\,|x|^{\alpha-2}$ for $x \ne 0$ and $g_i(0):=0$. By t
he computation above, $g_i \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)$, and $f \in L^1_{\mathrm{l
oc}}(A)$.

We verify the weak-derivative identity. Let $\varphi \in C_c^{\infty}(A)$. Since
 $\varphi$ vanishes near $\partial A$, choose $\varepsilon$ small and integrate
by parts on the annulus $A_{\varepsilon} := A \setminus \overline{B_{\varepsilon
}(0)}$ (where $f$ is smooth):
$$\int_{A_{\varepsilon}} f\,\partial_i\varphi\,dx = -\int_{A_{\varepsilon}} (\pa
rtial_i f)\,\varphi\,dx + \int_{\partial A_{\varepsilon}} f\,\varphi\,\nu_i\,dS.
$$
The outer boundary term vanishes because $\varphi \equiv 0$ near $\partial A$. O
n the inner sphere $|x|=\varepsilon$, the outward unit normal of $A_{\varepsilon
}$ is $\nu = -x/|x|$, so
$$\left|\int_{|x|=\varepsilon} f\,\varphi\,\nu_i\,dS\right| \le \|\varphi\|_{\in
fty}\,\varepsilon^{\alpha}\cdot \varepsilon^{n-1}\cdot |\mathbb{S}^{n-1}| = C\,\
varepsilon^{\alpha+n-1}.$$
Because $\alpha > 1-n$, the exponent $\alpha+n-1 > 0$, so this term tends to $0$
 as $\varepsilon \to 0$. Passing to the limit (justified by $f, g_i \in L^1_{\ma
thrm{loc}}$ and dominated convergence on the annular integrals),
$$\int_{A} f\,\partial_i\varphi\,dx = -\int_{A} g_i\,\varphi\,dx.$$
Hence $g_i$ is the weak partial derivative of $f$ for every $i$, and $g_i \in L^
1_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)$. So $f$ is weakly differentiable.

### Necessity: $\alpha \le 1-n \Rightarrow f$ is not weakly differentiable

**Case $\alpha \le -n$:** Then $f \notin L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)$ (since $\int_0^{
\varepsilon} r^{\alpha+n-1}dr$ diverges for $\alpha \le -n$), so $f$ is not even
 in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ and cannot be weakly differentiable.

**Case $-n < \alpha \le 1-n$:** Here $f \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$, so we must rule
 out the existence of weak derivatives. Suppose, for contradiction, that a weak
derivative $h_i \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)$ existed. Then for every $\varphi \in
C_c^{\infty}(A)$,
$$\int_A f\,\partial_i\varphi = -\int_A h_i\,\varphi.$$
On the annulus $A_{\varepsilon}$, integration by parts gives
$$\int_{A_{\varepsilon}} f\,\partial_i\varphi = -\int_{A_{\varepsilon}} (\partia
l_i f)\varphi + \int_{|x|=\varepsilon} f\,\varphi\,\nu_i\,dS.$$
Take $\varphi$ supported in $B_{\rho}(0) \Subset A$ with $\varphi \equiv 1$ near
 $0$ (so $\varphi$ and its derivatives vanish on $|x|=\rho$). Letting $\varepsil
on \to 0$:

- The left side tends to $\int_A f\,\partial_i\varphi = -\int_A h_i\varphi$ (fin
ite, since $f,h_i \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$).
- The volume term $\int_{A_{\varepsilon}}(\partial_i f)\varphi$ involves $|\part
ial_i f| \sim |x|^{\alpha-1}$ with $\alpha - 1 \le -n$, which is **not** $L^1$ n
ear $0$; in fact $\int_{|x|<\rho} |x|^{\alpha-1}dx = \int_0^{\rho} r^{\alpha+n-2
}dr$ diverges for $\alpha \le 1-n$. With $\varphi \equiv 1$ near $0$, this integ
ral diverges to $+\infty$ in magnitude (sign depending on $\alpha$ and direction
).

More precisely, choose $\varphi$ radial with $\varphi \equiv 1$ on $B_{\rho/2}$.
 Then $\int_{A_{\varepsilon}} (\partial_i f)\varphi\,dx = \int_{A_{\varepsilon}}
 \alpha x_i |x|^{\alpha-2}\varphi\,dx$. For $i$ fixed and using symmetry, the an
gular average of $x_i$ over spheres vanishes, but with the cutoff $\varphi$ not
exactly constant the leading contribution is governed by the divergence of $\int
_0^{\rho} r^{\alpha+n-2}dr$. The boundary term on $|x|=\varepsilon$ is of order
$\varepsilon^{\alpha+n-1}$, which for $\alpha < 1-n$ blows up, and for $\alpha =
 1-n$ stays bounded away from $0$ (tends to a nonzero constant times $\int_{\mat
hbb{S}^{n-1}} \omega_i\,d\omega$ — which actually vanishes by symmetry for the r
adial $\varphi$; one must use a non-radial test function).

To make the obstruction rigorous and concrete, pick a test function $\varphi$ wi
th $\varphi \equiv 1$ on $B_{\rho/2}$ and $\varphi \equiv 0$ outside $B_{\rho}$,
 and consider the flux term on $|x|=\varepsilon$:
$$\int_{|x|=\varepsilon} f\,\varphi\,\nu_i\,dS = -\varepsilon^{\alpha}\int_{|x|=
\varepsilon}\varphi\,\frac{x_i}{\varepsilon}\,dS = -\varepsilon^{\alpha+n-1}\int
_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\varphi(\varepsilon\omega)\,\omega_i\,d\omega.$$
For $\varepsilon < \rho/2$, $\varphi(\varepsilon\omega) \equiv 1$, so this equal
s $-\varepsilon^{\alpha+n-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega_i\,d\omega = 0$ by sym
metry. So the flux alone does not directly give the contradiction for radial $\v
arphi$; the obstruction is in the **volume integral of $|\nabla f|$**.

The clean argument: if $h_i \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ were the weak derivative, th
en by the identity on $A_{\varepsilon}$ and letting $\varepsilon \to 0$ with a t
est function $\varphi$ that equals $x_i$ near $0$ (so $\partial_i \varphi = 1$ t
here), one derives that $\int_{|x|<\rho} \partial_i f\,dx$ must be finite — but
$\int_{|x|<\rho} \alpha x_i |x|^{\alpha-2}dx = \alpha\int_0^{\rho}\int_{\mathbb{
S}^{n-1}}\omega_i\,d\omega\, r^{\alpha+n-2}dr = 0$ by symmetry, which is not the
 right obstruction either.

The decisive obstruction is **integrability**: a weak derivative, when it exists
 and equals the classical derivative a.e. (which it must, since $f$ is smooth on
 $A\setminus\{0\}$ and $\{0\}$ has measure zero), must coincide with $g_i = \alp
ha x_i|x|^{\alpha-2}$ a.e. But $|g_i| = |\alpha|\,|x|^{\alpha-1} \notin L^1_{\ma
thrm{loc}}(A)$ when $\alpha \le 1-n$ (the integral $\int_0^{\rho} r^{\alpha+n-2}
dr$ diverges). A function not in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ cannot be a weak derivativ
e (which by definition must be $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$). Hence no weak derivative e
xists.

This completes the necessity: for $-n < \alpha \le 1-n$, the pointwise gradient
is not locally integrable, so it cannot serve as a weak derivative, and no other
