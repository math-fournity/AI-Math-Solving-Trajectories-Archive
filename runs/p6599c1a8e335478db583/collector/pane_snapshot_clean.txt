**Answer: No.** A set of positive Lebesgue measure need not contain any nondegen
erate interval. We provide an explicit counterexample: the **Smith–Volterra–Cant
or (SVC) set**, a closed, nowhere dense subset of $[0,1]$ with positive measure.

---

### Construction

Define a nested sequence of closed sets $F_0 \supseteq F_1 \supseteq F_2 \supset
eq \cdots$ as follows.

- $F_0 = [0,1]$.
- $F_1$: remove from $F_0$ the open middle interval of length $\tfrac{1}{4}$, na
mely $\bigl(\tfrac{3}{8},\tfrac{5}{8}\bigr)$. Thus $F_1 = \bigl[0,\tfrac{3}{8}\b
igr]\cup\bigl[\tfrac{5}{8},1\bigr]$.
- Inductively, having formed $F_{n-1}$ as a union of $2^{n-1}$ disjoint closed i
ntervals, obtain $F_n$ by removing from the **middle** of each of those interval
s an open subinterval of length $\dfrac{1}{4^n}$.

Set
$$E \;=\; \bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty} F_n.$$

---

### $E$ has positive Lebesgue measure

At step $n\geq 1$ we remove $2^{n-1}$ open intervals, each of length $4^{-n}$. T
he total length removed is

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} 2^{n-1}\cdot\frac{1}{4^n}
\;=\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{n-1}}{2^{2n}}
\;=\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}
\;=\; \frac{1}{2}.
$$

Since the removed intervals are pairwise disjoint open subintervals of $[0,1]$,

$$
m(E) \;=\; m([0,1]) - \frac{1}{2} \;=\; \frac{1}{2} \;>\; 0.
$$

So $E$ has positive Lebesgue measure.

---

### $E$ contains no nondegenerate interval

After step $n$, the set $F_n$ consists of $2^n$ disjoint closed intervals of equ
al length. The total length of $F_n$ is

$$
m(F_n) \;=\; 1 - \sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{k+1}} \;=\; \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2^
{n+1}},
$$

so each constituent interval of $F_n$ has length

$$
\ell_n \;=\; \frac{m(F_n)}{2^n} \;=\; \frac{1}{2^{n+1}} + \frac{1}{2^{2n+1}} \;\
xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\; 0.
$$

Now suppose, for contradiction, that $E$ contains a nondegenerate interval $J$,
so $|J|>0$. Since $E\subseteq F_n$ for every $n$, we have $J\subseteq F_n$ for e
very $n$. But $J$ is connected and $F_n$ is a finite union of disjoint closed in
tervals, so $J$ must lie entirely within a single constituent interval of $F_n$.
 Consequently

$$
|J| \;\le\; \ell_n \quad\text{for every } n.
$$

Letting $n\to\infty$ gives $|J|\le 0$, contradicting $|J|>0$. Hence $E$ contains
 no nondegenerate interval.

---

### Conclusion

The Smith–Volterra–Cantor set $E\subset\mathbb{R}$ satisfies $m(E)=\tfrac12>0$ y
et contains no nondegenerate interval. Therefore a set of positive Lebesgue meas
ure does **not** necessarily contain a nondegenerate interval.
