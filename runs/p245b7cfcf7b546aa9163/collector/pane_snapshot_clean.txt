## Solution

**Step 1: Identify $f(x)$.**

The general term of $f$ is $(-1)^n \dfrac{x^{2n+1}}{2\cdot 4\cdot 6\cdots (2n)}
= (-1)^n \dfrac{x^{2n+1}}{2^n\, n!}$ for $n=0,1,2,\dots$

$$f(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n \frac{x^{2n+1}}{2^n n!} = x\sum_{n=0}^{\inft
y}\frac{(-x^2/2)^n}{n!} = x\,e^{-x^2/2}.$$

**Step 2: Identify $g(x)$.**

The general term of $g$ is $\dfrac{x^{2n}}{(2\cdot 4\cdot 6\cdots (2n))^2} = \df
rac{x^{2n}}{4^n (n!)^2}$ for $n=0,1,2,\dots$

$$g(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2n}}{4^n (n!)^2} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac
{(x/2)^{2n}}{(n!)^2} = I_0(x),$$

the modified Bessel function of the first kind of order $0$.

**Step 3: Compute the integral.**

$$\int_0^{\infty} f(x)\,g(x)\,dx = \int_0^{\infty} x\,e^{-x^2/2}\,I_0(x)\,dx = \
sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4^n (n!)^2}\int_0^{\infty} x^{2n+1} e^{-x^2/2}\,dx.$$

(Interchange of sum and integral is justified since all terms are non-negative o
n $[0,\infty)$.)

For the inner integral, substitute $u = x^2/2$, $du = x\,dx$, $x^2 = 2u$:

$$\int_0^{\infty} x^{2n+1} e^{-x^2/2}\,dx = \int_0^{\infty} (x^2)^n e^{-x^2/2}\,
x\,dx = \int_0^{\infty} (2u)^n e^{-u}\,du = 2^n\,\Gamma(n+1) = 2^n\, n!.$$

Therefore

$$\int_0^{\infty} f(x)\,g(x)\,dx = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^n\, n!}{4^n (n!)^2
} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^n\, n!} = e^{1/2} = \sqrt{e}.$$

$$\boxed{\int_{0}^{\infty} f(x)\,g(x)\,dx = \sqrt{e}}$$
