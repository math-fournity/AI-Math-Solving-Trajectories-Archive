# Solution

**Claim:** Yes, $R[t]$ is necessarily Noetherian.

**Proof.**

We are given that $R[t, t^{-1}]$ is Noetherian. The argument proceeds in two ste
ps.

**Step 1: $R$ is Noetherian.**

Consider the evaluation map
$$\varphi: R[t, t^{-1}] \longrightarrow R, \qquad f(t) \longmapsto f(1).$$
This is a well-defined ring homomorphism (evaluation at $t = 1$ is valid for Lau
rent polynomials since $1$ is a unit in $R$). It is surjective, since every $a \
in R$ is the image of the constant Laurent polynomial $a \cdot t^0$. Its kernel
is the set of Laurent polynomials vanishing at $t = 1$, which is the ideal $(t -
 1)$. Hence
$$R \;\cong\; R[t, t^{-1}]\,/\,(t-1).$$
Since $R[t, t^{-1}]$ is Noetherian and quotients of Noetherian rings are Noether
ian, $R$ is Noetherian.

**Step 2: $R[t]$ is Noetherian.**

By the Hilbert Basis Theorem, if $R$ is Noetherian then the polynomial ring $R[t
]$ is Noetherian. From Step 1, $R$ is Noetherian, so $R[t]$ is Noetherian. $\bla
cksquare$

### PROOF COMPLETE
