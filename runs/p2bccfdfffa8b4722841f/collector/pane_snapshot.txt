**Answer: The statement is TRUE.**

## Proof

**Setup.** Since $k$ is a field, $R$ is a free $k$-module and $F$ is a flat $k$-
module (being free over $k$). The inclusion $Z(R) \otimes_k F \subseteq Z(R \oti
mes_k F)$ is immediate: if $z \in Z(R)$ and $\alpha \in F$, then $(z \otimes \al
pha)(r \otimes \beta) = zr \otimes \alpha\beta = rz \otimes \beta\alpha = (r \ot
imes \beta)(z \otimes \alpha)$, so $z \otimes \alpha$ is central. We prove the r
everse inclusion.

**Key observation.** For each $r \in R$, define the $k$-linear map
$$\phi_r : R \to R, \quad \phi_r(z) = zr - rz.$$
The center is $Z(R) = \bigcap_{r \in R} \ker(\phi_r)$. Since it suffices to test
 commutativity against basis elements, if $\{e_j\}_{j \in J}$ is a $k$-basis of
$R$, then
$$Z(R) = \bigcap_{j \in J} \ker(\phi_{e_j}).$$

**Extending scalars.** Tensoring $\phi_{e_j}$ with $F$ gives the $F$-linear map
$$\phi_{e_j} \otimes_k F : R \otimes_k F \to R \otimes_k F,$$
which sends $w \mapsto w(e_j \otimes 1) - (e_j \otimes 1)w$. Thus $\ker(\phi_{e_
j} \otimes_k F)$ is exactly the set of elements in $R \otimes_k F$ that commute
with $e_j \otimes 1$.

Since $1 \otimes \beta$ is central in $R \otimes_k F$ for every $\beta \in F$ (a
s $F$ is commutative), an element $w \in R \otimes_k F$ is central if and only i
f it commutes with $e_j \otimes 1$ for every $j$. Therefore:
$$Z(R \otimes_k F) = \bigcap_{j \in J} \ker(\phi_{e_j} \otimes_k F).$$

**Flatness preserves kernels.** Since $F$ is a flat $k$-module, tensoring the ex
act sequence
$$0 \to \ker(\phi_{e_j}) \to R \xrightarrow{\phi_{e_j}} R$$
with $F$ yields the exact sequence
$$0 \to (\ker \phi_{e_j}) \otimes_k F \to R \otimes_k F \xrightarrow{\phi_{e_j}
\otimes F} R \otimes_k F.$$
Hence $\ker(\phi_{e_j} \otimes_k F) = (\ker \phi_{e_j}) \otimes_k F$ for each $j
$.

**Flatness preserves intersections.** Let $W_j = \ker(\phi_{e_j}) \subseteq R$.
Consider the natural $k$-linear map $\pi: R \to \bigoplus_{j} R/W_j$. Then $\ker
(\pi) = \bigcap_j W_j = Z(R)$. By flatness of $F$:
$$\ker(\pi \otimes_k F) = (\ker \pi) \otimes_k F = Z(R) \otimes_k F.$$
On the other hand, $\pi \otimes_k F : R \otimes_k F \to \bigoplus_j (R/W_j) \oti
mes_k F = \bigoplus_j (R \otimes_k F)/(W_j \otimes_k F)$, so
$$\ker(\pi \otimes_k F) = \bigcap_j (W_j \otimes_k F) = \bigcap_j \ker(\phi_{e_j
} \otimes_k F) = Z(R \otimes_k F).$$

**Conclusion.** Combining the two expressions for $\ker(\pi \otimes_k F)$:
$$Z(R \otimes_k F) = Z(R) \otimes_k F.$$

The crucial ingredient is that $F$ is flat over $k$ (which holds because $k$ is
a field, making $F$ a free $k$-module). Flatness ensures that both kernels and i
ntersections of kernels are preserved under base change, which is exactly what i
s needed for the center to commute with tensoring. $\blacksquare$
