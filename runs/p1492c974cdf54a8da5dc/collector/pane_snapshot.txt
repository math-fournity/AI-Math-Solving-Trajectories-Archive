## Proof

**Answer: Yes, there exists a unique such vector field $X_f$.**

**Existence.** Since $\omega$ is a symplectic form, it is by definition a closed
, **non-degenerate** 2-form. Non-degeneracy means that at every point $p \in M$,
 the linear map

$$\flat_p : T_p M \longrightarrow T_p^* M, \qquad v \longmapsto \omega_p(v, \,\c
dot\,)$$

is an isomorphism (its kernel is $\{0\}$, and $\dim T_p M = \dim T_p^* M$ since
both equal $\dim M$).

The differential $df$ is a smooth 1-form on $M$, i.e., a smooth section of $T^*M
$. At each point $p$, the covector $df_p \in T_p^* M$ has a unique preimage unde
r the isomorphism $\flat_p$:

$$v_p := \flat_p^{-1}(df_p) \in T_p M, \qquad \text{so that } \omega_p(v_p, \,\c
dot\,) = df_p(\,\cdot\,).$$

It remains to verify that the assignment $p \mapsto v_p$ is **smooth**, hence de
fines a smooth vector field. The bundle map $\flat : TM \to T^*M$ induced by $\o
mega$ is a smooth vector bundle isomorphism (because $\omega$ is smooth and fibe
rwise invertible, with the inverse depending smoothly on the base point — non-de
generacy is an open condition and the inverse of a smoothly varying invertible l
inear map is smooth). Therefore $\flat^{-1}$ is also a smooth bundle map. Compos
ing the smooth section $df : M \to T^*M$ with the smooth map $\flat^{-1} : T^*M
\to TM$ yields a smooth section

$$X_f := \flat^{-1} \circ df : M \longrightarrow TM.$$

By construction, for every vector field $Y$ on $M$ and every $p \in M$:

$$df_p(Y_p) = \omega_p\bigl((X_f)_p,\, Y_p\bigr),$$

i.e., $df(Y) = \omega(X_f, Y)$ as an equality of smooth functions on $M$.

**Uniqueness.** Suppose $X_f$ and $X_f'$ are two vector fields satisfying $df(Y)
 = \omega(X_f, Y) = \omega(X_f', Y)$ for all vector fields $Y$. Then for all $Y$
:

$$\omega(X_f - X_f',\, Y) = 0.$$

Fix any point $p \in M$ and let $Z := X_f - X_f'$. Then $\omega_p(Z_p, Y_p) = 0$
 for every $Y_p \in T_p M$ (we may choose a vector field $Y$ taking any prescrib
ed value at $p$). By non-degeneracy of $\omega_p$, this forces $Z_p = 0$. Since
$p$ was arbitrary, $X_f = X_f'$ everywhere.

**Conclusion.** For every smooth function $f$ on a symplectic manifold $(M, \ome
ga)$, there exists a unique smooth vector field $X_f$ — the **Hamiltonian vector
 field** of $f$ — satisfying $df(Y) = \omega(X_f, Y)$ for all vector fields $Y$.
 The existence relies on the fiberwise isomorphism $\flat : TM \xrightarrow{\sim
} T^*M$ given by non-degeneracy of $\omega$, and uniqueness follows from the sam
e non-degeneracy.
