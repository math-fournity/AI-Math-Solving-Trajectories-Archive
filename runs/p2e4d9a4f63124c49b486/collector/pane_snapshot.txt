## Solution: Perturbation of Membrane Vibration Frequencies under Mass Density P
erturbation

### Setup

Consider a membrane occupying a fixed domain $\Omega$ with boundary $\partial\Om
ega$. The transverse displacement $w(x,y,t)$ satisfies the wave equation

$$\rho\,\frac{\partial^2 w}{\partial t^2} = T\,\nabla^2 w,$$

where $T$ is the (uniform) tension and $\rho$ is the mass density. Seeking time-
harmonic solutions $w = u(x,y)\,e^{i\omega t}$ and absorbing $T$ into the eigenv
alue (set $T=1$), the spatial problem is

$$\nabla^2 u + \lambda\,\rho\, u = 0 \quad\text{in }\Omega, \qquad u = 0 \quad\t
ext{on }\partial\Omega, \tag{1}$$

where $\lambda = \omega^2$.

**Unperturbed problem.** The density is uniform: $\rho = \rho_0$ (constant). The
n (1) becomes

$$\nabla^2 u_n + \lambda_n\,\rho_0\, u_n = 0, \qquad u_n\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0
, \tag{2}$$

with known eigenvalues $\{\lambda_n\}$ (hence frequencies $\omega_n = \sqrt{\lam
bda_n}$) and eigenmodes $\{u_n\}$. By assumption each frequency is simple (one m
ode per frequency). We normalize the modes by

$$\int_\Omega \rho_0\, u_n^2\, dA = 1. \tag{3}$$

**Perturbed problem.** The density is perturbed: $\rho \to \rho_0 + \delta\rho$,
 where $\|\delta\rho\|$ is small. The perturbed eigenpair satisfies

$$\nabla^2 \tilde u_n + \tilde\lambda_n\,(\rho_0 + \delta\rho)\,\tilde u_n = 0,
\qquad \tilde u_n\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0. \tag{4}$$

We write $\tilde\lambda_n = \lambda_n + \delta\lambda_n$ and $\tilde u_n = u_n +
 \delta u_n$, expand to first order, and solve for $\delta\lambda_n$ (hence $\de
lta\omega_n$).

### Derivation

**Step 1 — First-order equation.** Substituting into (4) and keeping only first-
order terms:

$$\underbrace{\nabla^2 u_n + \lambda_n\,\rho_0\, u_n}_{=\,0\text{ by (2)}} + \na
bla^2(\delta u_n) + \lambda_n\,\rho_0\,\delta u_n + \lambda_n\,\delta\rho\, u_n
+ \delta\lambda_n\,\rho_0\, u_n = 0.$$

The zeroth-order part vanishes by (2), leaving the first-order equation:

$$\nabla^2(\delta u_n) + \lambda_n\,\rho_0\,\delta u_n = -\lambda_n\,\delta\rho\
, u_n - \delta\lambda_n\,\rho_0\, u_n. \tag{5}$$

**Step 2 — Projection onto $u_n$.** Multiply (5) by $u_n$ and integrate over $\O
mega$:

$$\int_\Omega u_n\,\nabla^2(\delta u_n)\,dA + \lambda_n\,\rho_0\int_\Omega u_n\,
\delta u_n\,dA = -\lambda_n\!\int_\Omega u_n^2\,\delta\rho\,dA - \delta\lambda_n
\,\rho_0\!\int_\Omega u_n^2\,dA. \tag{6}$$

**Step 3 — Green's identity (self-adjointness).** Since both $u_n$ and $\delta u
_n$ vanish on $\partial\Omega$, Green's second identity gives

$$\int_\Omega u_n\,\nabla^2(\delta u_n)\,dA = \int_\Omega \delta u_n\,\nabla^2 u
_n\,dA = -\lambda_n\,\rho_0\!\int_\Omega u_n\,\delta u_n\,dA,$$

where the last equality uses (2). Hence the **left-hand side of (6) vanishes ide
ntically**:

$$\int_\Omega u_n\,\nabla^2(\delta u_n)\,dA + \lambda_n\,\rho_0\int_\Omega u_n\,
\delta u_n\,dA = 0.$$

This is the key cancellation: the term involving $\delta u_n$ drops out, so $\de
lta\lambda_n$ is determined **without** solving for $\delta u_n$ — this is preci
sely where the simplicity of the eigenvalue (one mode per frequency) is essentia
l.

**Step 4 — Solve for $\delta\lambda_n$.** Equation (6) reduces to

$$\lambda_n\!\int_\Omega u_n^2\,\delta\rho\,dA + \delta\lambda_n\,\rho_0\!\int_\
Omega u_n^2\,dA = 0,$$

so

$$\boxed{\delta\lambda_n = -\lambda_n\;\frac{\displaystyle\int_\Omega u_n^2\,\de
lta\rho\,dA}{\displaystyle\rho_0\!\int_\Omega u_n^2\,dA}}. \tag{7}$$

**Step 5 — Convert to frequency perturbation.** Since $\lambda_n = \omega_n^2$,
we have $\delta\lambda_n = 2\,\omega_n\,\delta\omega_n$. Dividing (7) by $2\omeg
a_n$:

$$\boxed{\delta\omega_n = -\frac{\omega_n}{2}\;\frac{\displaystyle\int_\Omega u_
n^2\,\delta\rho\,dA}{\displaystyle\int_\Omega \rho_0\, u_n^2\,dA}}. \tag{8}$$

With the normalization (3), $\int_\Omega \rho_0\, u_n^2\,dA = 1$, this simplifie
s to

$$\boxed{\delta\omega_n = -\frac{\omega_n}{2}\int_\Omega u_n^2\,\delta\rho\,dA}.
 \tag{9}$$

Equivalently, the **relative** frequency shift is

$$\boxed{\frac{\delta\omega_n}{\omega_n} = -\frac{1}{2}\;\frac{\displaystyle\int
_\Omega u_n^2\,\delta\rho\,dA}{\displaystyle\int_\Omega \rho_0\, u_n^2\,dA}}. \t
ag{10}$$

### Interpretation and Verification

1. **Physical sign.** Increasing mass density where the mode has large amplitude
 ($\delta\rho > 0$ where $u_n^2$ is large) **lowers** the frequency ($\delta\ome
ga_n < 0$), consistent with the intuition that heavier membranes vibrate more sl
owly. The factor $-\frac{1}{2}$ reflects the square-root relationship $\omega \p
ropto \rho^{-1/2}$ in the uniform case: if $\delta\rho$ is constant, (10) gives
$\delta\omega_n/\omega_n = -\frac{1}{2}\,\delta\rho/\rho_0$, recovering $\omega
\propto \rho^{-1/2}$ exactly.

2. **Mode-selectivity.** The shift of the $n$-th frequency depends only on the $
n$-th mode $u_n$ — different modes are weighted differently by the same $\delta\
rho$, so the perturbation reshuffles the spectrum non-uniformly. This is why the
 simplicity assumption matters: if two modes shared a frequency, the perturbatio
n would couple them through a $2\times 2$ matrix, and a simple scalar formula wo
uld not suffice.

3. **Gauge invariance.** Formula (10) is independent of the normalization of $u_
n$ (numerator and denominator scale identically), so the result is intrinsic.

4. **First-order validity.** The derivation is valid to first order in $\delta\r
ho$. Higher-order corrections require solving (5) for $\delta u_n$ (expanding in
 the unperturbed basis $\{u_m\}$) and iterating; the second-order correction inv
olves sums over $m\neq n$ of coupling terms $\left|\int u_m\,\delta\rho\, u_n\,d
A\right|^2 / (\lambda_n - \lambda_m)$, which is well-defined precisely because t
he spectrum is simple.

### Conclusion
