## Proof

We compute the Laurent expansion of $g(z) = e^{1/z^2}$ about $z = 0$.

Recall the Taylor series of the exponential function, valid for all $w \in \math
bb{C}$:

$$e^{w} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{w^n}{n!}.$$

Substituting $w = \dfrac{1}{z^2}$ (which is valid for all $z \neq 0$, i.e., thro
ughout the punctured plane $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}$), we obtain the Laurent se
ries of $g$ at $z=0$:

$$g(z) = e^{1/z^2} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n!}\left(\frac{1}{z^2}\right)^
n = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n!\, z^{2n}}.$$

Writing out the first few terms explicitly:

$$e^{1/z^2} = 1 + \frac{1}{z^2} + \frac{1}{2!\, z^4} + \frac{1}{3!\, z^6} + \cdo
ts$$

By definition, the **residue** of $g$ at $z = 0$ is the coefficient of the $z^{-
1}$ term in this Laurent expansion:

$$\operatorname{Res}(g, 0) = [z^{-1}]\, g(z).$$

Examining the series, every term is of the form $z^{-2n}$ for $n = 0, 1, 2, \ldo
ts$, i.e., the powers of $z$ that appear are

$$z^0,\; z^{-2},\; z^{-4},\; z^{-6},\; \ldots$$

These are all **even** (non-negative even) powers of $z^{-1}$. In particular, th
ere is **no** $z^{-1}$ term in the Laurent expansion. Therefore its coefficient
is zero:

$$\operatorname{Res}(g, 0) = 0.$$

**Answer:** The residue of $g(z) = e^{1/z^2}$ at $z = 0$ is $\boxed{0}$.
