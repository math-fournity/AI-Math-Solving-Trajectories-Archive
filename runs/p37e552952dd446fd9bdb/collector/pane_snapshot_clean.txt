## Part iii: Ruzsa-Szemerédi Upper Bound ($e(n,r) = o(n^2)$)

**Theorem.** For every fixed $r \geq 2$,
$$\frac{e(n,r)}{n^2} \to 0 \quad \text{as } n \to \infty.$$

**Key tool — Triangle Removal Lemma (Ruzsa–Szemerédi, 1976).** For every $\varep
silon > 0$, there exists $\delta > 0$ and $n_0$ such that for all $n \geq n_0$:
any graph on $n$ vertices with at most $\delta n^3$ triangles can be made triang
le-free by deleting at most $\varepsilon n^2$ edges.

**Proof of the theorem.** Fix $r \geq 2$. Suppose for contradiction that $e(n,r)
 \neq o(n^2)$. Then there exists a constant $c > 0$ and a sequence $n_i \to \inf
ty$ with $e(n_i, r) \geq c\, n_i^2$ for all $i$.

For each such $n = n_i$, by definition of $e(n,r)$ as the minimal threshold, the
re exists a graph $G$ on $n$ vertices with $|E(G)| = e(n,r) - 1 \geq c\,n^2 - 1$
 edges such that:
- (A) every edge of $G$ is contained in at least one triangle, and
- (B) no edge of $G$ is contained in $r$ or more triangles (i.e., every edge is
in at most $r-1$ triangles).

For $n$ sufficiently large, $|E(G)| \geq \frac{c}{2}\,n^2$. Let $T$ denote the n
umber of triangles in $G$.

**Lower bound on $T$.** By (A), every edge is in at least one triangle. Counting
 edge-triangle incidences: each triangle contributes 3 incidences, and each of t
he $|E(G)|$ edges contributes at least 1. Thus:
$$3T \geq |E(G)| \geq \frac{c}{2}\,n^2 \implies T \geq \frac{c}{6}\,n^2. \tag{1}
$$

**Upper bound on $T$.** By (B), every edge is in at most $r-1$ triangles. Counti
ng incidences again:
$$3T \leq (r-1)\,|E(G)| \leq (r-1)\binom{n}{2} < \frac{r-1}{2}\,n^2 \implies T <
 \frac{r-1}{6}\,n^2. \tag{2}$$

**Lower bound on edges that must be removed.** To make $G$ triangle-free, we mus
t destroy every triangle. Removing a single edge destroys at most $r-1$ triangle
s (by (B)). Therefore the number of edges we must remove is at least:
$$\frac{T}{r-1} \geq \frac{|E(G)|}{3(r-1)} \geq \frac{c}{6(r-1)}\,n^2. \tag{3}$$

**Applying the Triangle Removal Lemma.** Set $\varepsilon = \frac{c}{12(r-1)} >
0$. The lemma furnishes $\delta > 0$ such that: if $T \leq \delta\, n^3$, then $
G$ can be made triangle-free by removing at most $\varepsilon\, n^2 = \frac{c}{1
2(r-1)}\,n^2$ edges.

But by (3), any triangle-removal process must delete at least $\frac{c}{6(r-1)}\
,n^2 = 2\varepsilon\, n^2 > \varepsilon\, n^2$ edges. This is a contradiction. T
herefore we must have:
$$T > \delta\, n^3. \tag{4}$$

**Final contradiction.** Combining (2) and (4):
$$\delta\, n^3 < T < \frac{r-1}{6}\,n^2.$$

This gives $\delta\, n < \frac{r-1}{6}$, which fails for $n > \frac{r-1}{6\delta
}$. Since $r$ and $\delta$ are fixed, this is violated for all sufficiently larg
e $n$ — contradicting the existence of such graphs for arbitrarily large $n$.

Therefore, our assumption was false, and $e(n,r) = o(n^2)$ for every fixed $r \g
eq 2$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on the proof structure.** The argument hinges on a tension between two
forces:
1. Every edge being in a triangle forces *many* triangles (lower bound on $T$),
which in turn forces many edge removals to eliminate all triangles.
2. No edge being in too many triangles caps the total number of triangles (upper
 bound on $T$), making it impossible to have the $\Omega(n^3)$ triangles that th
e Removal Lemma demands when the graph has $\Omega(n^2)$ edges.

The Triangle Removal Lemma itself is proved via the Szemerédi Regularity Lemma:
one finds a regular partition, identifies three parts with pairwise dense regula
r bipartite graphs (guaranteed by the edge density and the triangle-covering con
dition), and the Counting Lemma produces $\Theta(n^3)$ triangles among them — ea
ch edge between two such parts lying in $\Theta(n)$ triangles, which already exc
eeds $r-1$ for large $n$.
