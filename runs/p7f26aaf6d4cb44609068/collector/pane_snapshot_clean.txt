## Proof

Let $N = 2 \cdot 10^{10}$. We seek $v_2(S)$ where
$$S = \sum_{n=0}^{N/2} \binom{N}{2n} 5^n.$$

**Step 1: Closed form via even binomial sum.**

The standard identity for even-indexed binomial coefficients gives
$$\sum_{n} \binom{N}{2n} x^{2n} = \frac{(1+x)^N + (1-x)^N}{2}.$$
Setting $x = \sqrt{5}$ (so $x^{2n} = 5^n$):
$$S = \frac{(1+\sqrt{5})^N + (1-\sqrt{5})^N}{2}.$$

**Step 2: Connection to Lucas numbers.**

Let $\varphi = \frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$, $\psi = \frac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}$ (so $\varphi
+\psi=1$, $\varphi\psi=-1$). Then $1+\sqrt{5} = 2\varphi$ and $1-\sqrt{5} = 2\ps
i$, hence
$$(1+\sqrt{5})^N + (1-\sqrt{5})^N = 2^N(\varphi^N + \psi^N) = 2^N \mathcal{L}_N,
$$
where $\mathcal{L}_N = \varphi^N + \psi^N$ is the $N$-th **Lucas number** ($\mat
hcal{L}_0=2,\; \mathcal{L}_1=1,\; \mathcal{L}_{n+2}=\mathcal{L}_{n+1}+\mathcal{L
}_n$). Therefore
$$S = 2^{N-1}\,\mathcal{L}_N, \qquad v_2(S) = (N-1) + v_2(\mathcal{L}_N).$$

**Step 3: 2-adic valuation of Lucas numbers.**

We establish the following complete characterization by computing Lucas numbers
modulo $2$, $4$, and $8$:

- **Mod 2** (period $3$): $\mathcal{L}_n \bmod 2$ cycles as $0,1,1,0,1,1,\ldots$
 Thus $\mathcal{L}_n$ is even $\iff 3 \mid n$.

- **Mod 4** (period $6$): $\mathcal{L}_n \bmod 4$ cycles as $2,1,3,0,3,3,2,1,3,0
,3,3,\ldots$ For $3\mid n$ (indices $0,3,6,9,\ldots$): $\mathcal{L}_n \equiv 2 \
pmod{4}$ when $6\mid n$, and $\mathcal{L}_n \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$ when $n \equiv 3
\pmod{6}$.

- **Mod 8** (period $12$): $\mathcal{L}_n \bmod 8$ cycles as $2,1,3,4,7,3,2,5,7,
4,3,7,\ldots$ For $n \equiv 3 \pmod{6}$ (indices $3,9,15,21,\ldots$, i.e. $n \bm
od 12 \in \{3,9\}$): $\mathcal{L}_n \equiv 4 \pmod{8}$.

Combining:
$$v_2(\mathcal{L}_n) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } 3 \nmid n, \\ 1 & \text{if }
 6 \mid n, \\ 2 & \text{if } n \equiv 3 \pmod{6}. \end{cases}$$

**Step 4: Apply to $N = 2\cdot 10^{10}$.**

Compute $N \bmod 3$: since $10 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$, we have $10^{10} \equiv 1 \pm
od{3}$, so $N = 2\cdot 10^{10} \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$. Thus $3 \nmid N$, giving $v_2
(\mathcal{L}_N) = 0$.

**Step 5: Verification with small cases.**

- $N=2$: $S = \binom{2}{0}+\binom{2}{2}\cdot 5 = 6$, $v_2=1$. Formula: $N-1+v_2(
\mathcal{L}_2)=1+v_2(3)=1$. ✓
- $N=4$: $S = 1+30+25=56=8\cdot 7$, $v_2=3$. Formula: $3+v_2(7)=3$. ✓
- $N=6$: $S = 1+75+375+125=576=64\cdot 9$, $v_2=6$. Formula: $5+v_2(\mathcal{L}_
6)=5+v_2(18)=5+1=6$. ✓
- $N=3$: $S = 1+15=16$, $v_2=4$. Formula: $2+v_2(\mathcal{L}_3)=2+v_2(4)=2+2=4$.
 ✓

**Conclusion.**

$$v_2(S) = (N-1) + v_2(\mathcal{L}_N) = (2\cdot 10^{10} - 1) + 0 = 2\cdot 10^{10
} - 1.$$

$$\boxed{v = 2\cdot 10^{10} - 1 = 19{,}999{,}999{,}999}$$
