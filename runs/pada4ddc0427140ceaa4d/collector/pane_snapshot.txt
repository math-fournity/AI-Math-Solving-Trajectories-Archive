## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. There exists a solvable subgroup $G \leq \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{
F}_p)$ acting transitively on $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$. Concretely, the **no
n-split maximal torus** $T \leq \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$, which is cyclic o
f order $p+1$, acts regularly (freely and transitively) on $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb
{F}_p)$.

---

### Construction of the non-split torus

Let $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$ be the quadratic extension of $\mathbb{F}_p$, and fix an
$\mathbb{F}_p$-basis $\{1,\alpha\}$ of $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$ where $\alpha^2 \in \m
athbb{F}_p$ is a non-square. Multiplication by any $x \in \mathbb{F}_{p^2}^*$ is
 an $\mathbb{F}_p$-linear automorphism of $\mathbb{F}_{p^2} \cong \mathbb{F}_p^2
$, yielding an embedding
$$\iota : \mathbb{F}_{p^2}^* \hookrightarrow \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_p).$$

Under $\iota$, the scalar matrices $\mathbb{F}_p^*\cdot I_2$ correspond exactly
to $\mathbb{F}_p^* \subset \mathbb{F}_{p^2}^*$. Hence $\iota$ descends to an inj
ective homomorphism
$$\bar\iota : \mathbb{F}_{p^2}^* / \mathbb{F}_p^* \hookrightarrow \mathrm{PGL}_2
(\mathbb{F}_p).$$

Define $T := \bar\iota\!\left(\mathbb{F}_{p^2}^* / \mathbb{F}_p^*\right)$. Since
 $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}^*$ is cyclic of order $p^2-1$ and $\mathbb{F}_p^*$ is its uni
que subgroup of order $p-1$, the quotient is cyclic of order
$$|T| = \frac{p^2-1}{p-1} = p+1.$$

In particular, $T$ is **abelian**, hence **solvable**.

---

### The action of $T$ on $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$ is free

The set $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$ is the set of $1$-dimensional $\mathbb{F}_p
$-subspaces of $\mathbb{F}_p^2 \cong \mathbb{F}_{p^2}$. A point $[u:v] \in \math
bb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$ corresponds to the line
$$L_w = \{t\cdot w : t \in \mathbb{F}_p\} \quad \text{for some } w \in \mathbb{F
}_{p^2}^*.$$

An element $\bar{x} \in T$ (represented by $x \in \mathbb{F}_{p^2}^*$) fixes $L_
w$ if and only if $x\cdot L_w = L_w$, i.e., $xw \in L_w$, i.e., $xw = tw$ for so
me $t \in \mathbb{F}_p^*$, i.e., $x = t \in \mathbb{F}_p^*$.

But in $T = \mathbb{F}_{p^2}^*/\mathbb{F}_p^*$, the coset $\bar{x}$ is the ident
ity **if and only if** $x \in \mathbb{F}_p^*$. Therefore:

> The only element of $T$ that fixes any point of $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$ i
s the identity.

Equivalently, $T$ acts **freely** (semiregularly) on $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)
$.

---

### The action is transitive

Since $T$ acts freely, every orbit has size $|T| = p+1$. But
$$|\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)| = p+1 = |T|.$$

Hence there is exactly **one orbit**: $T$ acts **transitively** (in fact, regula
rly) on $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$.

---

### Conclusion

The non-split torus $T \leq \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$ is a **cyclic** (hence
 solvable) subgroup of order $p+1$ that acts transitively on $\mathbb{P}^1(\math
bb{F}_p)$. Therefore, **yes**, the action of a solvable subgroup $G \leq \mathrm
{PGL}_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$ on $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$ can be transitive. $\blac
ksquare$
