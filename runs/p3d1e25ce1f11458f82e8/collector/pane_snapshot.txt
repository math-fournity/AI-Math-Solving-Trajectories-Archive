## Solution

**Answer: The degree of $f$ must be $0$. It cannot be any nonzero integer.**

### Proof

Let $n \geq 2$ be odd, so $\mathbb{RP}^n$ is orientable and $H_n(\mathbb{RP}^n)
\cong \mathbb{Z}$. By the Künneth formula, the top homology of the target is:

$$H_n(S^{n-1} \times S^1) \cong \bigoplus_{i+j=n} H_i(S^{n-1}) \otimes H_j(S^1).
$$

The only nonzero summand is $i = n-1,\, j = 1$, giving $H_{n-1}(S^{n-1}) \otimes
 H_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}$. (All other pair
s vanish: $H_n(S^1) = 0$ since $n \geq 2$, and $H_n(S^{n-1}) = 0$ since $n > n-1
$.) So $H_n(S^{n-1}\times S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, and the degree $f_*(1)$ is wel
l-defined as an integer.

**Key constraint from the fundamental group.** For $n \geq 2$:

$$\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \qquad \pi_1(S^{n-1} \times
 S^1) \cong \pi_1(S^{n-1}) \times \pi_1(S^1).$$

- For $n \geq 3$: $\pi_1(S^{n-1}) = 0$, so $\pi_1(S^{n-1} \times S^1) \cong \mat
hbb{Z}$.
- For $n = 2$: $\pi_1(S^1 \times S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$.

In either case, the target fundamental group is torsion-free. The induced homomo
rphism

$$f_*: \pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \longrightarrow \pi_1(S^{n-1} \ti
mes S^1)$$

must be **trivial**, since any homomorphism from a torsion group $\mathbb{Z}/2$
into a torsion-free abelian group is zero.

**Lifting to the universal cover.** Since $f_*(\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^n)) = \{0\}$, t
he map $f$ lifts through the universal cover. The universal cover of $S^{n-1} \t
imes S^1$ is

$$\widetilde{X} = S^{n-1} \times \mathbb{R}, \qquad p = \mathrm{id} \times \exp:
 S^{n-1} \times \mathbb{R} \to S^{n-1} \times S^1,$$

so there exists a continuous lift $\tilde{f}: \mathbb{RP}^n \to S^{n-1} \times \
mathbb{R}$ with $f = p \circ \tilde{f}$.

**Vanishing of the degree.** Since $S^{n-1} \times \mathbb{R} \simeq S^{n-1}$ (d
eformation retract onto $S^{n-1} \times \{0\}$), we have

$$H_n(S^{n-1} \times \mathbb{R}) \cong H_n(S^{n-1}) = 0 \qquad (n \geq 2).$$

Applying homology to the factorization $f = p \circ \tilde{f}$:

$$f_* = p_* \circ \tilde{f}_*: \underbrace{H_n(\mathbb{RP}^n)}_{\cong\, \mathbb{
Z}} \xrightarrow{\;\tilde{f}_*\;} \underbrace{H_n(S^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R})}_{=\,
 0} \xrightarrow{\;p_*\;} \underbrace{H_n(S^{n-1}\times S^1)}_{\cong\, \mathbb{Z
}}.$$

The middle group is zero, forcing $\tilde{f}_* = 0$, and consequently

$$f_* = p_* \circ \tilde{f}_* = 0.$$

Therefore $f_*(1) = 0$ for **every** continuous map $f: \mathbb{RP}^n \to S^{n-1
} \times S^1$ with $n \geq 2$ odd. The degree is not free to range over all inte
gers; it is rigidly constrained to be **$0$**. $\blacksquare$
