## Derivation of the Normalized Riemann Curvature \(K(\chi; u, v)\)

### 1. Setup

Let \(E_N\) be an \(N\)-dimensional Riemannian space with metric tensor \(g_{ij}
\) (symmetric, positive-definite). At a point \(P \in E_N\), let \(\chi\) be a 2
-plane (a two-dimensional subspace of the tangent space \(T_P E_N\)) spanned by
two linearly independent tangent vectors

\[
u = (u^1, \dots, u^N), \qquad v = (v^1, \dots, v^N).
\]

The Riemann–Christoffel curvature tensor (all covariant indices) is

\[
R_{k\ell mn} = \partial_m \Gamma_{k\ell n} - \partial_n \Gamma_{k\ell m} + \Gamm
a_{kjn}\,\Gamma^j_{\ell m} - \Gamma_{kjm}\,\Gamma^j_{\ell n},
\]

where \(\Gamma_{k\ell n} = g_{kp}\,\Gamma^p_{\ell n}\) are the Christoffel symbo
ls of the first kind. This tensor possesses the symmetries

\[
R_{k\ell mn} = -R_{\ell k mn} = -R_{k\ell nm} = R_{mnk\ell}, \qquad R_{k\ell mn}
 + R_{kmn\ell} + R_{kn\ell m} = 0.
\]

### 2. The Unnormalized Curvature Expression

Contract the curvature tensor with the vectors \(u, v\):

\[
\mathcal{R}(u,v) \;=\; R_{k\ell mn}\, u^k\, v^\ell\, u^m\, v^n.
\]

By the antisymmetry \(R_{k\ell mn} = -R_{\ell k mn}\), swapping \(u \leftrightar
row v\) gives

\[
\mathcal{R}(v,u) = R_{k\ell mn}\, v^k\, u^\ell\, v^m\, u^n = R_{\ell k mn}\, u^k
\, v^\ell\, u^m\, v^n = -\mathcal{R}(u,v),
\]

so \(\mathcal{R}\) is antisymmetric in \((u,v)\), as expected for a 2-form–value
d curvature.

### 3. The Area Element (Denominator)

The squared area of the parallelogram spanned by \(u\) and \(v\) is

\[
|u \wedge v|^2 = \langle u, u\rangle\,\langle v, v\rangle - \langle u, v\rangle^
2,
\]

where \(\langle a, b\rangle = g_{ij}\, a^i b^j\). Expanding in components:

\[
\langle u, u\rangle = g_{ij}\, u^i u^j, \qquad \langle v, v\rangle = g_{km}\, v^
k v^m, \qquad \langle u, v\rangle = g_{ij}\, u^i v^j.
\]

Therefore

\[
|u \wedge v|^2 = (g_{ij}\, u^i u^j)(g_{km}\, v^k v^m) - (g_{ij}\, u^i v^j)(g_{km
}\, u^k v^m).
\]

Relabeling dummy indices in the second term (\(j \leftrightarrow m\) appropriate
ly) to factor out the common vector product \(u^i v^j u^k v^m\):

\[
|u \wedge v|^2 = \bigl(g_{ij}\, g_{km} - g_{im}\, g_{kj}\bigr)\, u^i\, v^j\, u^k
\, v^m.
\]

The quantity \(g_{ij}\, g_{km} - g_{im}\, g_{kj}\) is the **Gram determinant** (
the doubly-contravariant form of the area tensor), which is positive for linearl
y independent \(u, v\).

### 4. Normalization and Basis-Independence

Define the **normalized Riemann curvature** (sectional curvature of the 2-plane
\(\chi\)):

\[
\boxed{K(\chi;\, u, v) \;=\; \frac{R_{k\ell mn}\, u^k\, v^\ell\, u^m\, v^n}{\big
l(g_{ij}\, g_{km} - g_{im}\, g_{kj}\bigr)\, u^i\, v^j\, u^k\, v^m}}.
\]

**Invariance under change of basis of \(\chi\).** Replace \((u, v)\) by any othe
r basis of the same 2-plane:

\[
u' = a\,u + b\,v, \qquad v' = c\,u + d\,v, \qquad \Delta = ad - bc \neq 0.
\]

- **Numerator:** By multilinearity and the antisymmetry of \(R_{k\ell mn}\) in \
((k,\ell)\) and \((m,n)\),

\[
\mathcal{R}(u', v') = R_{k\ell mn}\, u'^k v'^\ell u'^m v'^n = \Delta^2\, R_{k\el
l mn}\, u^k v^\ell u^m v^n.
\]

- **Denominator:** The Gram determinant scales identically:

\[
|u' \wedge v'|^2 = \Delta^2\, |u \wedge v|^2.
\]

Hence the factor \(\Delta^2\) cancels, and

\[
K(\chi;\, u', v') = K(\chi;\, u, v).
\]

This confirms that \(K\) depends **only on the 2-plane \(\chi\)**, not on the pa
rticular basis vectors chosen to span it — justifying the notation \(K(\chi)\).

### 5. Consistency Checks

- **Flat space:** If \(R_{k\ell mn} \equiv 0\), then \(K = 0\) for every 2-plane
, as expected.
- **Constant curvature \(c\):** When \(R_{k\ell mn} = c\,(g_{km}\,g_{\ell n} - g
_{kn}\,g_{\ell m})\), the numerator becomes

\[
c\,(g_{km}\,g_{\ell n} - g_{kn}\,g_{\ell m})\, u^k v^\ell u^m v^n = c\,\bigl[(g_
{km}u^k u^m)(g_{\ell n}v^\ell v^n) - (g_{kn}u^k v^n)(g_{\ell m}v^\ell u^m)\bigr]
 = c\,|u\wedge v|^2,
\]

so \(K = c\), recovering the constant-curvature case.
- **2-dimensional case (\(N=2\)):** There is only one 2-plane, and \(K\) reduces
 to the Gaussian curvature, with \(R_{1212} = K\,(g_{11}g_{22} - g_{12}^2)\).

### 6. Conclusion

The normalized form of the Riemann curvature expression in \(E_N\), expressed th
rough the Riemann–Christoffel tensor \(R_{k\ell mn}\) and the metric tensor \(g_
{ij}\), is

\[
K(\chi;\, u, v) = \frac{R_{k\ell mn}\, u^k\, v^\ell\, u^m\, v^n}{\bigl(g_{ij}\,
g_{km} - g_{im}\, g_{kj}\bigr)\, u^i\, v^j\, u^k\, v^m},
\]

where the denominator is the squared area \(|u \wedge v|^2\) of the parallelogra
m spanned by the basis vectors of the 2-plane \(\chi\). The ratio is invariant u
nder re-basis of \(\chi\), so \(K\) is a well-defined function of the 2-plane al
one — the **sectional curvature** of \(E_N\) at \(P\) in the direction \(\chi\).
