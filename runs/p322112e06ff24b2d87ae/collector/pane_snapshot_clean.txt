## Proof

**Claim.** Yes, there exists a set $A \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ that is compact, con
tains the origin, and has Lebesgue measure zero.

**Construction.** Take $A = \{0\}$, the singleton containing the origin.

We verify the three required properties:

**1. $A \subset \mathbb{R}^n$.** The origin $0 = (0, 0, \dots, 0) \in \mathbb{R}
^n$, so $\{0\} \subset \mathbb{R}^n$.

**2. $A$ contains the origin.** By definition, $0 \in \{0\}$.

**3. $A$ is compact.** A singleton is compact: it is closed (its complement $\ma
thbb{R}^n \setminus \{0\}$ is open) and bounded (it is contained in any ball $B(
0, r)$ for $r > 0$). By the Heine–Borel theorem, a closed and bounded subset of
$\mathbb{R}^n$ is compact.

**4. $A$ has measure zero.** The Lebesgue measure of a single point is zero. To
see this explicitly: for any $\varepsilon > 0$, the open ball $B(0, \varepsilon^
{1/n}/2)$ contains $\{0\}$ and has measure
$$\text{vol}\!\left(B\!\left(0, \varepsilon^{1/n}/2\right)\right) = \omega_n \cd
ot \left(\varepsilon^{1/n}/2\right)^n = \frac{\omega_n}{2^n}\,\varepsilon,$$
where $\omega_n$ is the volume of the unit ball in $\mathbb{R}^n$. As $\varepsil
on \to 0$, this volume tends to $0$. Since $\{0\}$ can be covered by an open set
 of arbitrarily small measure, $\{0\}$ has outer measure zero, hence Lebesgue me
asure zero.

All three conditions are satisfied, so such a set exists. $\blacksquare$
