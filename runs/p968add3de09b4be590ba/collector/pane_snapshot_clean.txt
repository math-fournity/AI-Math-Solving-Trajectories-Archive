The statement is **false**.

## Counterexample

Work over an algebraically closed field $k$, in the affine space $\mathbb{A}^1_k
 = \operatorname{Spec}_{\max} k[x]$. Consider the ideal

$$\mathfrak a = (x^2) \subseteq k[x].$$

**Claim 1: $V(\mathfrak a)$ is irreducible.**

The zero set is
$$V(x^2) = \{p \in \mathbb{A}^1 : x^2(p) = 0\} = \{p : x(p) = 0\} = \{(0)\},$$
a single point. A single point is irreducible: it cannot be written as a proper
union $V_1 \cup V_2$ of two proper closed subsets, since the only proper closed
subset of a point is the empty set.

**Claim 2: $\mathfrak a = (x^2)$ is not a prime ideal.**

Recall that an ideal $\mathfrak p$ is prime iff $fg \in \mathfrak p \Rightarrow
f \in \mathfrak p$ or $g \in \mathfrak p$. Take $f = g = x$. Then

$$f \cdot g = x \cdot x = x^2 \in (x^2),$$

yet $x \notin (x^2)$, because $x$ is not divisible by $x^2$ in $k[x]$ (equivalen
tly, $1 \notin (x)$, so $x$ is not a $k[x]$-multiple of $x^2$). Hence $(x^2)$ fa
ils the primality condition.

## Why the implication fails in general

The correct theorem (a consequence of the Nullstellensatz over an algebraically
closed field) is:

$$V(\mathfrak a) \text{ irreducible} \iff \sqrt{\mathfrak a} \text{ is prime} \i
ff I(V(\mathfrak a)) \text{ is prime}.$$

Irreducibility of the zero set controls only the **radical** $\sqrt{\mathfrak a}
$, not the ideal $\mathfrak a$ itself. An ideal can have a prime radical without
 being prime — precisely the situation with $(x^2)$, whose radical is $\sqrt{(x^
2)} = (x)$, a prime (indeed maximal) ideal. The "thickening" along the irreducib
le variety $V(x)$ encoded by the nilpotent $x^2$ is invisible to the set-theoret
ic operation $V(\cdot)$, since $V(\mathfrak a) = V(\sqrt{\mathfrak a})$.

## Conclusion

We have exhibited an ideal $\mathfrak a = (x^2)$ such that $V(\mathfrak a)$ is i
rreducible (a single point) but $\mathfrak a$ is not prime. Therefore the statem
ent

> "If $V(\mathfrak a)$ is irreducible, then $\mathfrak a$ is a prime ideal"

is **false**. The correct conclusion from irreducibility of $V(\mathfrak a)$ is
that $\sqrt{\mathfrak a}$ is prime, not that $\mathfrak a$ itself is prime.
