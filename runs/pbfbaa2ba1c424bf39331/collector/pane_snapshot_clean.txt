## Proof

We prove that for every $c > 0$, there exist infinitely many $n$ and bipartite g
raphs $G$ on $n$ vertices with bipartition $(X, Y)$, $|X| = \lfloor n^{2/3}\rflo
or$, $|E(G)| \geq cn$, and **no** $C_6$. This shows no such $c$ exists.

### Construction via Generalized Quadrangles

**Generalized Quadrangles.** A generalized quadrangle $\operatorname{GQ}(s,t)$ i
s a point-line incidence structure where each line carries $s+1$ points, each po
int lies on $t+1$ lines, and for any non-incident point-line pair $(P, \ell)$ th
ere is a unique line through $P$ meeting $\ell$. The incidence graph of any $\op
eratorname{GQ}(s,t)$ is bipartite (points vs. lines) with **girth exactly 8** —
in particular, it contains no $C_4$ and no $C_6$.

**Girth 8 verification (no $C_6$).** Suppose a $C_6$ exists: $P_1, L_1, P_2, L_2
, P_3, L_3, P_1$ with $P_i, P_{i+1} \in L_i$ (indices mod 3). If $P_1 \notin L_2
$, then both $L_1$ and $L_3$ are lines through $P_1$ meeting $L_2$ (at $P_2$ and
 $P_3$ respectively). If $P_2 \neq P_3$, this contradicts the uniqueness in the
GQ axiom. If $P_2 = P_3$, the cycle degenerates. If $P_1 \in L_2$, then $P_1, P_
2 \in L_1 \cap L_2$, forcing $L_1 = L_2$ (no two points on two common lines), ag
ain degenerating. So no genuine $C_6$ exists. $\checkmark$

**Classical GQ$(q, q^2)$.** For every prime power $q$, the elliptic quadric $Q(5
,q)$ in $PG(5,q)$ yields $\operatorname{GQ}(q, q^2)$ with:
- Points: $(q+1)(q^3+1) \sim q^4$,
- Lines: $(q^2+1)(q^3+1) \sim q^5$,
- Each point on $q^2+1$ lines,
- Girth 8.

### Subgraph Construction

Fix a prime power $q$. Let $G_q$ be the incidence graph of $Q(5,q)$ with:
- Part $A$ (lines): $|A| = (q^2+1)(q^3+1) = q^5 + q^3 + q^2 + 1 =: N$,
- Part $B$ (points): $|B| = (q+1)(q^3+1) \sim q^4$,
- Each vertex of $B$ has degree exactly $q^2 + 1$.

**Choosing $B'$.** Define $g(b) = \lfloor (N + b)^{2/3} \rfloor$. We seek a fixe
d point $b^* = g(b^*)$. Since $g(0) = \lfloor N^{2/3}\rfloor$ and $g$ is non-dec
reasing with $g(b) \leq (N+b)^{2/3} < b$ for $b > (2N)^{2/3}$ (as $(N+b)^{2/3} <
 b$ when $b^{3/2} > N+b$, i.e., $b > N^{2/3}$ up to constants), a fixed point ex
ists by discrete intermediate value. For large $q$:

$$b^* = \lfloor (N + b^*)^{2/3} \rfloor, \quad b^* \sim N^{2/3} \sim q^{10/3}.$$

The correction term: $(N + b^*)^{2/3} = N^{2/3}(1 + b^*/N)^{2/3} = N^{2/3} + O(N
^{-1/3} \cdot b^*) = N^{2/3} + O(q^{-5/3} \cdot q^{10/3}) = N^{2/3} + O(q^{5/3})
$, which is $o(q^{10/3})$, confirming $b^* = \lfloor N^{2/3}\rfloor + O(q^{5/3})
 \sim q^{10/3}$.

Since $|B| \sim q^4 \gg q^{10/3} \sim b^*$ for large $q$, we can choose $B'_q \s
ubset B$ with $|B'_q| = b^*$.

**The graph $G'_q$.** Let $G'_q$ be the subgraph of $G_q$ induced on $A \cup B'_
q$, with bipartition $(X, Y) = (B'_q, A)$.

**Verification of all conditions:**

1. **Bipartition:** $X = B'_q$, $Y = A$ are disjoint, $X \cup Y = A \cup B'_q =
V(G'_q)$, and all edges go between $X$ and $Y$ (incidence graph). $\checkmark$

2. **Part size:** $|X| = |B'_q| = b^* = \lfloor (N + b^*)^{2/3} \rfloor = \lfloo
r |V(G'_q)|^{2/3} \rfloor = \lfloor n_q^{2/3} \rfloor$ where $n_q = N + b^*$. $\
checkmark$

3. **No $C_6$:** $G'_q$ is a subgraph of $G_q$ which has girth 8. Subgraphs cann
ot decrease girth, so $G'_q$ has girth $\geq 8$; in particular, no $C_6$. $\chec
kmark$

4. **Edge count:** Every vertex of $B'_q$ has degree $q^2 + 1$ in $G_q$ (all its
 neighbors are lines in $A$, which we keep entirely). So:
$$|E(G'_q)| = |B'_q| \cdot (q^2 + 1) = b^* \cdot (q^2+1) \sim q^{10/3} \cdot q^2
 = q^{16/3}.$$

5. **Edges exceed $cn_q$:** We have $n_q = N + b^* \sim q^5$ and:
$$\frac{|E(G'_q)|}{n_q} \sim \frac{q^{16/3}}{q^5} = q^{1/3} \longrightarrow \inf
ty \text{ as } q \to \infty.$$

### Conclusion

For any $c > 0$, by Bertrand's postulate there exists a prime $q > (2c)^3$, henc
e $q^{1/3} > 2c$. For sufficiently large such $q$:

$$|E(G'_q)| = b^*(q^2+1) \geq \tfrac{1}{2} q^{10/3} \cdot q^2 = \tfrac{1}{2} q^{
16/3} > c \cdot q^5 \geq c \cdot n_q.$$

The graph $G'_q$ is bipartite on $n_q$ vertices with one part of size $\lfloor n
_q^{2/3} \rfloor$, has at least $c \cdot n_q$ edges, and contains no $C_6$.

Since this holds for **every** $c > 0$ (with infinitely many valid $q$), no cons
tant $c > 0$ can force the existence of a $C_6$. The answer to Erdős's question
is **False**. $\blacksquare$
