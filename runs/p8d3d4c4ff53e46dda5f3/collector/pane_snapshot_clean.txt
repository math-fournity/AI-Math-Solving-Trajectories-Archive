Looking at this problem, I need to determine the probability that a random subse
t of [0,1] is Lebesgue measurable.

## Answer: The probability is **0**.

## Proof

**Model.** We model a "randomly selected subset" of [0,1] using the product prob
ability space. Let $\Omega = \{0,1\}^{[0,1]}$, equipped with the product measure
 $\mathbb{P}$ (each coordinate $\omega(x)$ is 1 with probability $1/2$, independ
ently). For $\omega \in \Omega$, the random subset is $A(\omega) = \{x \in [0,1]
 : \omega(x) = 1\}$.

**Key fact.** Every event $E$ in the product $\sigma$-algebra of $\{0,1\}^{[0,1]
}$ depends on only countably many coordinates. That is, there exists a countable
 set $S \subseteq [0,1]$ such that membership $\omega \in E$ is determined entir
ely by $\{\omega(x) : x \in S\}$.

**Main claim.** Every measurable event $E$ (in the product $\sigma$-algebra) wit
h $E \subseteq \{\omega : A(\omega) \text{ is Lebesgue measurable}\}$ satisfies
$\mathbb{P}(E) = 0$. Equivalently, the inner measure of $\{A \text{ is measurabl
e}\}$ is $0$.

**Proof of claim.** Let $E$ be such an event, and let $S \subseteq [0,1]$ be cou
ntable such that $E$ depends only on coordinates in $S$. Suppose for contradicti
on that $\mathbb{P}(E) > 0$, so $E \neq \emptyset$. Pick $\omega \in E$; since $
E \subseteq \{A \text{ measurable}\}$, the set $A(\omega)$ is Lebesgue measurabl
e.

Since $S$ is countable, $[0,1] \setminus S$ has Lebesgue measure $1$, and theref
ore contains a non-Lebesgue-measurable subset $N$ (by Vitali's theorem).

Define $\omega' \in \Omega$ by:
- $\omega'(x) = \omega(x)$ for $x \in S$,
- $\omega'(x) = \mathbf{1}_N(x)$ for $x \in [0,1] \setminus S$.

Since $\omega'$ agrees with $\omega$ on $S$ and $E$ depends only on coordinates
in $S$, we have $\omega' \in E$.

Now examine $A(\omega') = (A(\omega) \cap S) \cup N$. Since $S$ is countable, $A
(\omega) \cap S$ is countable and hence Lebesgue measurable. If $A(\omega')$ wer
e Lebesgue measurable, then
$$N = A(\omega') \cap ([0,1] \setminus S)$$
would be Lebesgue measurable (as the intersection of two measurable sets, since
$[0,1]\setminus S$ is measurable). But $N$ is non-measurable by construction — *
*contradiction**.

So $A(\omega')$ is non-measurable, yet $\omega' \in E \subseteq \{A \text{ measu
rable}\}$. This contradiction forces $\mathbb{P}(E) = 0$. $\square$

**Applying the 0-1 law.** The event $\{A \text{ is measurable}\}$ is invariant u
nder finite changes of coordinates (adding or removing finitely many points pres
erves Lebesgue measurability). If this event were in the product $\sigma$-algebr
a, it would depend on countably many coordinates and, by Kolmogorov's 0-1 law, w
ould have probability $0$ or $1$. The claim above rules out probability $1$ (and
 any positive value), so the probability would be $0$.

**Conclusion.** No measurable event of positive probability is contained in $\{A
 \text{ is Lebesgue measurable}\}$. The inner measure of this event is $0$, and
by the 0-1 law the only consistent probability value is $0$. Therefore, a random
ly selected subset of $[0,1]$ is Lebesgue measurable with probability $\boxed{0}
$.
