# Solution

**Theorem.** Every countable Boolean algebra admits a strictly positive finitely
 additive measure.

**Proof.**

Let $B$ be a countable Boolean algebra. We construct a finitely additive measure
 $\mu: B \to [0,1]$ with $\mu(1)=1$ and $\mu(a)>0$ for every $a\neq 0$.

---

**Step 1 (Enumeration).** Since $B$ is countable, enumerate its non-zero element
s as $a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots$ and enumerate *all* elements as $b_1, b_2, b_3, \dot
s$

---

**Step 2 (Ultrafilter construction).** For each $n \geq 1$ we build an ultrafilt
er $U_n$ on $B$ containing $a_n$, by a recursive procedure that exploits countab
ility.

Set $c_0 = a_n \neq 0$ and let $F_0 = \{x \in B : c_0 \leq x\}$, the principal f
ilter generated by $c_0$.

At stage $k \geq 1$, we have $c_{k-1} \neq 0$ generating the proper principal fi
lter $F_{k-1}$. Consider $b_k$:

- **If $c_{k-1} \wedge b_k \neq 0$**: set $c_k = c_{k-1} \wedge b_k$. Then $c_k
\neq 0$, $F_k = \{x : c_k \leq x\}$ extends $F_{k-1}$, and $b_k \in F_k$.
- **If $c_{k-1} \wedge b_k = 0$**: then $c_{k-1} \leq \neg b_k$, so $c_{k-1} \we
dge \neg b_k = c_{k-1} \neq 0$. Set $c_k = c_{k-1}$. Then $F_k = F_{k-1}$ and $\
neg b_k \in F_k$.

In either case $c_k \neq 0$, $F_k$ is a proper principal filter extending $F_{k-
1}$, and $F_k$ *decides* $b_k$ (either $b_k \in F_k$ or $\neg b_k \in F_k$).

Define $U_n = \bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty} F_k$. Then:
- $U_n$ is a filter (nested union of filters, upward-closed, closed under finite
 meets).
- $U_n$ is **proper**: $0 \notin U_n$ since $0 \notin F_k$ for any $k$ (all $c_k
 \neq 0$).
- $U_n$ is an **ultrafilter**: for every $b_k \in B$, either $b_k \in F_k \subse
teq U_n$ or $\neg b_k \in F_k \subseteq U_n$.
- $a_n \in F_0 \subseteq U_n$, so $a_n \in U_n$.

---

**Step 3 (Two-valued measures).** Each ultrafilter $U_n$ defines a $\{0,1\}$-val
ued finitely additive measure $\mu_n : B \to \{0,1\}$ by
$$\mu_n(x) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } x \in U_n, \\ 0 & \text{if } x \notin
U_n. \end{cases}$$
Finite additivity of $\mu_n$ follows from the ultrafilter property: for $x \wedg
e y = 0$, exactly one of $x, y$ can belong to $U_n$ (since $x \wedge y = 0$ mean
s $\neg x \vee \neg y = 1 \in U_n$, so $\neg x \in U_n$ or $\neg y \in U_n$), an
d $x \vee y \in U_n$ iff $x \in U_n$ or $y \in U_n$. Also $\mu_n(1) = 1$.

---

**Step 4 (Convex combination).** Define $\mu : B \to [0,1]$ by
$$\mu(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^n}\, \mu_n(x).$$

- **Well-defined and bounded**: Each $\mu_n(x) \in \{0,1\}$, so $0 \leq \mu(x) \
leq \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} 2^{-n} = 1$.
- **Normalization**: $\mu(1) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} 2^{-n} \cdot 1 = 1$.
- **Finite additivity**: For $x \wedge y = 0$,
$$\mu(x \vee y) = \sum_n 2^{-n}\,\mu_n(x \vee y) = \sum_n 2^{-n}\bigl(\mu_n(x) +
 \mu_n(y)\bigr) = \mu(x) + \mu(y),$$
using finite additivity of each $\mu_n$ and absolute convergence justifying term
wise summation.
- **Strict positivity**: Let $a \in B$, $a \neq 0$. Then $a = a_k$ for some $k$.
 Since $a_k \in U_k$, we have $\mu_k(a_k) = 1$, and therefore
$$\mu(a) \geq \frac{1}{2^k}\,\mu_k(a) = \frac{1}{2^k} > 0.$$

---

**Conclusion.** $\mu$ is a strictly positive finitely additive measure on $B$. S
ince $B$ was an arbitrary countable Boolean algebra, **every countable Boolean a
lgebra admits a strictly positive measure**. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The countability hypothesis is essential: there exist uncountable Bo
olean algebras (e.g., certain quotient algebras $\mathcal{P}(\omega)/\mathcal{I}
$ for suitable ideals $\mathcal{I}$) that carry no strictly positive finitely ad
ditive measure. The argument above breaks down for uncountable algebras because
one cannot enumerate the non-zero elements to form the convergent convex combina
tion.
