Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether triviality of the associate
d bundle $\xi$ forces the covering $p$ to be trivial.

**Answer: Yes.** If $\xi$ is trivial, then $p$ is a trivial 2-sheeted covering.

## Proof

Let $B = X/(\mathbb{Z}/2)$ and let $\sigma$ denote the nontrivial element of $\m
athbb{Z}/2$.

**Step 1: Decompose the representation.**

The action of $\sigma$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$ is $(x,y)\mapsto(y,x)$, given by the ma
trix $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$. This has eigenvalues $+1$ and $-1$
with eigenspaces
$$V_+ = \{(t,t):t\in\mathbb{R}\}, \qquad V_- = \{(t,-t):t\in\mathbb{R}\}.$$
Thus as a $\mathbb{Z}/2$-representation,
$$\mathbb{R}^2 \cong \mathbb{R}_+ \oplus \mathbb{R}_-,$$
where $\mathbb{R}_+$ is the trivial representation ($\sigma$ acts as $+1$) and $
\mathbb{R}_-$ is the sign representation ($\sigma$ acts as $-1$).

**Step 2: Decompose the associated bundle.**

By equivariance of the construction, the associated bundle splits:
$$\xi = X\times_{\mathbb{Z}/2}\mathbb{R}^2 \cong \bigl(X\times_{\mathbb{Z}/2}\ma
thbb{R}_+\bigr)\oplus\bigl(X\times_{\mathbb{Z}/2}\mathbb{R}_-\bigr) = \underline
{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\eta,$$
where $\underline{\mathbb{R}}$ is the trivial line bundle (the action on $\mathb
b{R}_+$ is trivial) and $\eta := X\times_{\mathbb{Z}/2}\mathbb{R}_-$ is the real
 line bundle associated to the principal $\mathbb{Z}/2$-bundle $p:X\to B$ via th
e sign homomorphism $\mathbb{Z}/2\to O(1)=\{\pm1\}$.

**Step 3: Relate $\eta$ to the covering $p$.**

The 2-sheeted covering $p:X\to B$ is a principal $\mathbb{Z}/2$-bundle, classifi
ed by a class $\alpha\in H^1(B;\mathbb{Z}/2)$ (the monodromy homomorphism $\pi_1
(B)\to\mathbb{Z}/2$). The line bundle $\eta$ is obtained from $p$ by the associa
ted-bundle construction with the sign representation, which is precisely the iso
morphism class in $H^1(B;\mathbb{Z}/2)$ giving the first Stiefel–Whitney class:
$$w_1(\eta) = \alpha.$$
Since real line bundles over $B$ are completely classified by $w_1\in H^1(B;\mat
hbb{Z}/2)$:
$$\eta \text{ is trivial} \iff w_1(\eta)=0 \iff \alpha=0 \iff p \text{ is a triv
ial covering}.$$

**Step 4: Use triviality of $\xi$.**

Suppose $\xi$ is trivial. Then all Stiefel–Whitney classes of $\xi$ vanish, in p
articular $w_1(\xi)=0$. By the Whitney product formula and Step 2:
$$w_1(\xi) = w_1(\underline{\mathbb{R}}) + w_1(\eta) = 0 + w_1(\eta) = w_1(\eta)
.$$
Therefore $w_1(\eta)=0$.

**Step 5: Conclude.**

By Step 3, $w_1(\eta)=0$ implies $\eta$ is trivial, which implies $\alpha=0$, wh
ich means the principal $\mathbb{Z}/2$-bundle $p:X\to B$ is trivial. That is,
$$X \cong B\times\mathbb{Z}/2,$$
so $p$ is a trivial 2-sheeted covering. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The key point is that the representation $\mathbb{R}^2$ with the coo
rdinate-swap action decomposes as $\mathbb{R}_+\oplus\mathbb{R}_-$, so $\xi$ con
tains the sign line bundle $\eta$ as a direct summand (up to adding a trivial li
ne). Since $w_1$ is a *stable* invariant, triviality of $\underline{\mathbb{R}}\
oplus\eta$ forces $w_1(\eta)=0$, and for line bundles stable triviality equals t
riviality. The class $w_1(\eta)$ is exactly the class classifying the double cov
er, so its vanishing is equivalent to the cover being trivial.
