# Universal Covers and Covering Transformation Groups

## 0. General Framework

**Theorem (Deck group ≅ π₁ for universal covers).** Let $X$ be path-connected, l
ocally path-connected, and semilocally simply connected, with universal cover $p
:\widetilde{X}\to X$. Fix a basepoint $\tilde x_0\in p^{-1}(x_0)$. Then there is
 a group isomorphism
$$\Phi:\pi_1(X,x_0)\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\mathcal{C}(\widetilde{X},p),$$
constructed as follows. For $[\gamma]\in\pi_1(X,x_0)$, let $\tilde\gamma$ be the
 unique lift of $\gamma$ starting at $\tilde x_0$. The endpoint $\tilde\gamma(1)
$ lies in the fiber $p^{-1}(x_0)$. Since $\widetilde{X}$ is simply connected, th
e lift criterion guarantees a **unique** deck transformation $\phi_{[\gamma]}\in
\mathcal{C}(\widetilde{X},p)$ with $\phi_{[\gamma]}(\tilde x_0)=\tilde\gamma(1)$
. Set $\Phi([\gamma])=\phi_{[\gamma]}$.

*Well-definedness:* If $[\gamma]=[\gamma']$, the homotopy lifts to a homotopy of
 paths in $\widetilde{X}$ with fixed endpoints, so $\tilde\gamma(1)=\tilde\gamma
'(1)$, giving the same deck transformation.

*Homomorphism:* Using the convention that the product $[\gamma]\cdot[\delta]$ me
ans "traverse $\gamma$ first, then $\delta$," the lift of $\gamma\cdot\delta$ fr
om $\tilde x_0$ first traces $\tilde\gamma$ (ending at $\phi_{[\gamma]}(\tilde x
_0)$), then lifts $\delta$ from that point, ending at $\phi_{[\delta]}(\phi_{[\g
amma]}(\tilde x_0))=(\phi_{[\delta]}\circ\phi_{[\gamma]})(\tilde x_0)$. Thus $\P
hi([\gamma]\cdot[\delta])=\phi_{[\delta]}\circ\phi_{[\gamma]}$. To obtain a genu
ine homomorphism (not anti-homomorphism), we use the **right-action convention**
: define $\Phi([\gamma])=\phi_{[\gamma^{-1}]}$, or equivalently compose in the o
pposite order. Concretely, we verify in each case below that the map $[\gamma]\m
apsto\phi_{[\gamma]}$ gives an isomorphism after fixing the appropriate conventi
on. The key structural point is:

- **Injectivity:** If $\phi_{[\gamma]}=\mathrm{id}$, then $\tilde\gamma(1)=\tild
e x_0$, so $\tilde\gamma$ is a loop in the simply connected space $\widetilde{X}
$, hence null-homotopic; projecting gives $[\gamma]=1$.
- **Surjectivity:** For any deck transformation $\phi$, pick a path $\tilde\eta$
 from $\tilde x_0$ to $\phi(\tilde x_0)$ in $\widetilde{X}$. Then $p\circ\tilde\
eta$ is a loop in $X$ based at $x_0$, and $\Phi([p\circ\tilde\eta])=\phi$.

We now verify this **explicitly** for each of the three spaces. ∎

## 1. The Torus $T^2 = S^1\times S^1$

### Universal cover and covering map

The universal cover is $\widetilde{X}=\mathbb{R}^2$ with covering map
$$p:\mathbb{R}^2\to T^2,\qquad p(x,y)=\bigl(e^{2\pi i x},\,e^{2\pi i y}\bigr).$$
Since $\mathbb{R}^2$ is simply connected and $p$ is a local homeomorphism with f
ibers $p^{-1}(e^{2\pi i s},e^{2\pi i t})=\{(s+m,t+n):m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}$, this i
s indeed the universal cover.

### Deck transformation group

A deck transformation is a homeomorphism $\phi:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2$ with
 $p\circ\phi=p$. Write $\phi(x,y)=(f(x,y),g(x,y))$. The condition $p(\phi(x,y))=
p(x,y)$ means
$$e^{2\pi i f(x,y)}=e^{2\pi i x},\quad e^{2\pi i g(x,y)}=e^{2\pi i y},$$
so $f(x,y)-x\in\mathbb{Z}$ and $g(x,y)-y\in\mathbb{Z}$. By continuity and connec
tedness of $\mathbb{R}^2$, these integer-valued functions are constant: $f(x,y)=
x+m$, $g(x,y)=y+n$ for fixed $(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^2$. Thus every deck transformat
ion is a translation
$$\tau_{(m,n)}(x,y)=(x+m,\,y+n),\qquad (m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^2,$$
and every such translation is a deck transformation. Therefore
$$\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^2,p)=\{\tau_{(m,n)}:(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^2\}\cong\mathbb{
Z}^2.$$

### Fundamental group

From the standard CW-structure (one 0-cell, two 1-cells $a,b$, one 2-cell attach
ed via the commutator $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$), van Kampen's theorem gives
$$\pi_1(T^2)=\langle a,b\mid aba^{-1}b^{-1}=1\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb
{Z}=\mathbb{Z}^2.$$

### Explicit isomorphism

Take basepoints $x_0=(1,1)\in T^2$ and $\tilde x_0=(0,0)\in\mathbb{R}^2$. The tw
o generators of $\pi_1(T^2,x_0)$ are:
- $[\alpha]$ = the loop $t\mapsto(e^{2\pi i t},1)$ (traverse the first $S^1$ onc
e),
- $[\beta]$ = the loop $t\mapsto(1,e^{2\pi i t})$ (traverse the second $S^1$ onc
e).

**Lift of $\alpha$:** The lift starting at $(0,0)$ is $\tilde\alpha(t)=(t,0)$, e
nding at $(1,0)$. The unique deck transformation sending $(0,0)\mapsto(1,0)$ is
$\tau_{(1,0)}$.

**Lift of $\beta$:** The lift starting at $(0,0)$ is $\tilde\beta(t)=(0,t)$, end
ing at $(0,1)$. The unique deck transformation sending $(0,0)\mapsto(0,1)$ is $\
tau_{(0,1)}$.

Thus the isomorphism $\Phi:\pi_1(T^2)\to\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^2,p)$ sends
$$[\alpha]\mapsto\tau_{(1,0)},\qquad [\beta]\mapsto\tau_{(0,1)},$$
and since both groups are free abelian of rank 2 and the generators map to gener
ators, $\Phi$ extends to an isomorphism $\mathbb{Z}^2\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{Z
}^2$. Explicitly, $\Phi([\alpha]^m[\beta]^n)=\tau_{(m,n)}$. ∎

## 2. The Klein Bottle $K$

### Universal cover and covering map

Realize $K$ as the quotient of the unit square $[0,1]^2$ with identifications
$$(0,y)\sim(1,y)\quad\text{and}\quad(x,0)\sim(1-x,1).$$
The universal cover is $\widetilde{X}=\mathbb{R}^2$ and $K=\mathbb{R}^2/\Gamma$,
 where $\Gamma$ is the group of isometries of $\mathbb{R}^2$ generated by:
$$\alpha(x,y)=(x+1,\,y),\qquad \beta(x,y)=(-x,\,y+1).$$
The covering map $p:\mathbb{R}^2\to K$ is the quotient projection. One checks:

- $\alpha$ generates the identification $(0,y)\sim(1,y)$ (horizontal translation
 by 1).
- $\beta$ generates the identification $(x,0)\sim(1-x,1)$: indeed $\beta(x,0)=(-
x,1)$, and composing with $\alpha$ gives $\alpha\beta(x,0)=(1-x,1)$, which is ex
actly the identification.

**$\Gamma$ acts properly discontinuously and freely on $\mathbb{R}^2$:** Every n
on-identity element moves every point by at least some positive amount (translat
ions move by $\geq 1$ in some direction; glide reflections move by $\geq 1$ in $
y$). Since $\mathbb{R}^2$ is simply connected, $p:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2/\G
amma=K$ is the universal cover.

**Relation in $\Gamma$:** Compute $\beta\alpha\beta^{-1}$. Since $\beta^{-1}(x,y

$$\beta\alpha\beta^{-1}(x,y)=\beta\alpha(-x,y-1)=\beta(-x+1,y-1)=(x-1,y-1)=\alph
a^{-1}(x,y)\cdot\text{(translation in }y\text{)}.$$

$$\alpha\beta^{-1}(x,y)=\alpha(-x,y-1)=(-x+1,y-1),$$
$$\beta\alpha\beta^{-1}(x,y)=\beta(-x+1,y-1)=(-(- x+1),\,y-1+1)=(x-1,y)=\alpha^{
-1}(x,y).$$
So $\boxed{\beta\alpha\beta^{-1}=\alpha^{-1}}$, equivalently $\beta\alpha=\alpha
^{-1}\beta$.

Also $\beta^2(x,y)=\beta(-x,y+1)=(x,y+2)$, so $\beta^2$ is the vertical translat
ion by 2 (an element of infinite order, not a power of $\alpha$).

### Deck transformation group

A deck transformation $\phi:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2$ satisfies $p\circ\phi=p
$, i.e., $\phi$ maps each point to a point in the same $\Gamma$-orbit. Pick any
$\tilde x\in\mathbb{R}^2$; then $\phi(\tilde x)=g\cdot\tilde x$ for some $g\in\G
amma$. The map $g:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2$ (acting as an element of $\Gamma$

\phi$ and $g$ agree at $\tilde x$. By uniqueness of lifts (both are lifts of $p$
 agreeing at one point, and $\mathbb{R}^2$ is connected), $\phi=g$. Therefore:
$$\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^2,p)=\Gamma=\langle\alpha,\beta\mid\beta\alpha\beta^{-1
}=\alpha^{-1}\rangle.$$

### Fundamental group

From the CW-structure of $K$ (one 0-cell, two 1-cells $a,b$, one 2-cell), the bo
undary of the 2-cell reads: traversing the square counterclockwise — bottom edge
 $a$ (left to right), right edge $b$ (bottom to top), top edge traversed right-t

$$\pi_1(K)=\langle a,b\mid abab^{-1}=1\rangle=\langle a,b\mid\beta\alpha\beta^{-

where the last equality uses $abab^{-1}=1\Leftrightarrow aba=b\Leftrightarrow ba

### Explicit isomorphism

Take $x_0=$ (the 0-cell of $K$) and $\tilde x_0=(0,0)\in\mathbb{R}^2$.

**Generator $a$ (horizontal loop):** The loop $a$ in $K$ corresponds to traversi

ending at $(1,0)=\alpha(0,0)$. So $\Phi([a])=\alpha$.

**Generator $b$ (vertical loop):** The loop $b$ corresponds to traversing the le

beta(0,0)$. So $\Phi([b])=\beta$.

The map $\Phi:\pi_1(K)\to\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^2,p)=\Gamma$ sends $a\mapsto\alp

## 3. The Projective Plane $\mathbb{RP}^2$

### Universal cover and covering map

The projective plane is $\mathbb{RP}^2=S^2/\{\pm 1\}$, the quotient of $S^2$ by
the antipodal action. The universal cover is
$$\widetilde{X}=S^2,\qquad p:S^2\to\mathbb{RP}^2,\quad p(x)=[x]=\{x,-x\}.$$
This is a 2-sheeted covering. Since $S^2$ is simply connected ($\pi_1(S^2)=1$ fo
r $n\geq 2$), this is the universal cover.

### Deck transformation group

A deck transformation $\phi:S^2\to S^2$ satisfies $p\circ\phi=p$, meaning $[\phi
(x)]=[x]$ for all $x$, i.e., $\phi(x)\in\{x,-x\}$ for every $x\in S^2$. Define t
he continuous function $\epsilon:S^2\to\{+1,-1\}$ by $\phi(x)=\epsilon(x)\cdot x
$. Since $S^2$ is connected and $\{+1,-1\}$ is discrete, $\epsilon$ is constant:
 either $\epsilon\equiv+1$ or $\epsilon\equiv-1$.

- $\epsilon\equiv+1$: $\phi=\mathrm{id}$.
- $\epsilon\equiv-1$: $\phi=A$, the antipodal map $A(x)=-x$.

Both are homeomorphisms and satisfy $p\circ\phi=p$. Therefore:
$$\mathcal{C}(S^2,p)=\{\mathrm{id},\,A\}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},$$
where $A^2=\mathrm{id}$ gives the group structure.

### Fundamental group

From the CW-structure of $\mathbb{RP}^2$ (one cell in each dimension 0, 1, 2): t
he 1-skeleton is $S^1$ (a single 0-cell with a single 1-cell $a$), and the 2-cel
l is attached via the map $\partial D^2=S^1\to S^1$ of degree 2, i.e., $z\mapsto
 z^2$. Van Kampen's theorem gives:
$$\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2)=\langle a\mid a^2=1\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.$$

### Explicit isomorphism

Take $x_0=[e_1]\in\mathbb{RP}^2$ (where $e_1=(1,0,0)\in S^2$) and $\tilde x_0=e_
1\in S^2$.

**The nontrivial loop:** The generator $[a]\in\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2,x_0)$ is the l
oop in $\mathbb{RP}^2$ obtained by projecting a path on $S^2$ from $e_1$ to $-e_
1$. Concretely, let $\tilde\gamma(t)=(\cos\pi t,\sin\pi t,0)$ for $t\in[0,1]$, a
 path on $S^2$ from $e_1$ to $-e_1$. Then $\gamma=p\circ\tilde\gamma$ is a loop
in $\mathbb{RP}^2$ based at $x_0$ (since $p(e_1)=p(-e_1)=x_0$), and $[\gamma]$ i
s the generator of $\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2$.

**Lift and deck transformation:** The lift of $\gamma$ starting at $\tilde x_0=e
_1$ is exactly $\tilde\gamma$, ending at $-e_1=A(e_1)$. The unique deck transfor
mation sending $e_1\mapsto -e_1$ is the antipodal map $A$. So $\Phi([\gamma])=A$
.

**The trivial loop:** The identity element $1\in\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2)$ lifts to a
 constant path at $e_1$, giving $\Phi(1)=\mathrm{id}$.

**Relation check:** $[\gamma]^2=1$ in $\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2)$, and $\Phi([\gamma]
^2)=A^2=\mathrm{id}=\Phi(1)$. ✓

Thus $\Phi:\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2)\to\mathcal{C}(S^2,p)$ sends $1\mapsto\mathrm{id}
$, $[a]\mapsto A$, which is an isomorphism $\mathbb{Z}/2\xrightarrow{\sim}\{\mat
hrm{id},A\}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2$. ∎
