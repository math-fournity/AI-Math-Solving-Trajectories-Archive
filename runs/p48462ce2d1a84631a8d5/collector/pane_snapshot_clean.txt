## Proof

**Interpretation.** A 9×9 matrix is "formed using each of the numbers 1 to 9 exa
ctly once" by placing the nine distinct values 1, 2, …, 9 into nine of the 81 po
sitions, with every remaining entry equal to 0. We sum the determinant over all
such matrices (all choices of 9 positions and all assignments of 1–9 to them).

**Key observation.** A 9×9 matrix with only 9 nonzero entries has a nonzero dete
rminant only if those 9 entries occupy exactly one position in each row and exac
tly one position in each column — i.e. the nonzero positions form a *permutation
 pattern* $\{(i,\sigma(i)) : i=1,\dots,9\}$ for some $\sigma\in S_9$.

*Reason.* The determinant is
$$\det A = \sum_{\tau\in S_9} \operatorname{sgn}(\tau)\prod_{i=1}^{9} a_{i,\tau(
i)}.$$
A term is nonzero only if every position $(i,\tau(i))$ carries a nonzero entry.
Since there are just 9 nonzero entries total, all of them must be consumed: the
set $\{(i,\tau(i))\}$ must equal the set of nonzero positions. In particular eac
h row and each column contains exactly one nonzero entry, so the nonzero positio
ns themselves form a permutation pattern $\sigma$, and the only surviving term i
n the determinant expansion is $\tau=\sigma$.

**Determinant for a fixed configuration.** Fix a permutation $\sigma$ (giving th
e 9 positions) and an assignment of the values $1,\dots,9$ to those positions. L
et $v_i$ be the value placed at row $i$ (column $\sigma(i)$). Then
$$\det A = \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\,v_1 v_2\cdots v_9 = \operatorname{sgn}(\s
igma)\cdot 9!,$$
since $\{v_1,\dots,v_9\}=\{1,\dots,9\}$ regardless of the assignment.

**Summing over all assignments for a fixed $\sigma$.** There are $9!$ ways to as
sign $1,\dots,9$ to the 9 positions, each contributing $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigm
a)\cdot 9!$. Hence the contribution of permutation $\sigma$ is
$$9!\cdot \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\cdot 9! = (9!)^{\,2}\,\operatorname{sgn}(\s
igma).$$

**Summing over all permutations.** The total sum over all matrices is
$$\sum_{\sigma\in S_9} (9!)^{\,2}\,\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma) = (9!)^{\,2}\sum_{
\sigma\in S_9}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma).$$

For $n\ge 2$, the even and odd permutations are equinumerous (multiplication by
any transposition, e.g. $(1\;2)$, is a bijection between them). Therefore
$$\sum_{\sigma\in S_9}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma) = 0.$$

**Conclusion.** The sum of the determinants of all such matrices is
$$(9!)^{\,2}\cdot 0 = \boxed{0}.$$
