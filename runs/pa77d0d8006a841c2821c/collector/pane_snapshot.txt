**Answer: Yes, such modules exist.**

**Proof.**

We give an explicit example. Let $R = \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$, the ring of intege
rs of the number field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$. Since $R$ is the ring of integer
s of a number field, it is a **Dedekind domain**. Consider the ideal

$$I = (2,\; 1 + \sqrt{-5}) \subseteq R.$$

This is a finitely generated $R$-module (generated by two elements). We show it
has constant rank $1$ but is not free.

---

**Claim 1: $I$ is not free (equivalently, not principal).**

The norm on $R$ is $N(a + b\sqrt{-5}) = a^2 + 5b^2$. The quotient $R/I$ satisfie
s $2 = 0$ and $1 + \sqrt{-5} = 0$, i.e. $\sqrt{-5} \equiv -1 \equiv 1 \pmod{2}$,
 so $R/I \cong \mathbb{F}_2$. Thus $I$ is a prime ideal of norm $|N(I)| = 2$.

If $I = (\alpha)$ were principal, then $|N(\alpha)| = [R : I] = 2$. But $a^2 + 5
b^2 = 2$ has no integer solutions (if $b \neq 0$ then $5b^2 \geq 5 > 2$; if $b =
 0$ then $a^2 = 2$, impossible). So $I$ is **not principal**.

Since $R$ is an integral domain, an ideal is free as an $R$-module if and only i
f it is principal: a free ideal $I \cong R^n$ embeds in $R$, and over the fracti
on field $K$ we get $K^n \hookrightarrow K$, forcing $n \leq 1$; $n = 1$ means $
I$ is principal. Therefore $I$ is **not free**.

---

**Claim 2: $I$ has constant rank $1$.**

Since $R$ is a Dedekind domain, every nonzero ideal is **invertible**, hence **p
rojective**. In particular $I$ is a finitely generated projective $R$-module, so
 it is locally free: $I_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is free over $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ for ever
y prime $\mathfrak{p}$.

To determine the rank: $I$ is a nonzero ideal in the domain $R$, so $I \otimes_R
 K \cong K$ (where $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$ is the fraction field), since any
 nonzero $a \in I$ gives $I \otimes_R K \twoheadrightarrow K \cdot a \cong K$ an
d $I \subseteq R \subseteq K$ gives $I \otimes_R K \hookrightarrow K$. Thus the
generic rank is $1$.

For a finitely generated projective module, the rank function $\mathfrak{p} \map
sto \mathrm{rk}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I) = \dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}(I \otimes_R \kap
pa(\mathfrak{p}))$ is locally constant on $\mathrm{Spec}(R)$. Since $R$ is a dom
ain, $\mathrm{Spec}(R)$ is **connected**, so the rank is **constant**, equal to
the generic rank $1$.

Equivalently and more directly: $I$ is an invertible ideal, so $I_{\mathfrak{p}}
 \cong R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ for every prime $\mathfrak{p}$, which is exactly the st
atement that $I$ has constant rank $1$.

---

**Conclusion.**

The ideal $I = (2, 1 + \sqrt{-5})$ is a finitely generated module over the commu
tative ring $R = \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$, has constant rank $1$, but is not free.
 Hence such modules exist. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** This is the simplest instance of a general phenomenon: for any Dedek
ind domain (or any commutative ring) with nontrivial Picard group $\mathrm{Pic}(
R)$, a nontrivial element of $\mathrm{Pic}(R)$ is a rank-$1$ projective module t
hat is not free. More generally, nontrivial algebraic vector bundles over $\math
rm{Spec}(R)$ correspond to projective modules of constant rank that are not free
.
