## Proof

Consider a vector field $A^\mu$ defined along a closed curve $C$ in a curved man
ifold equipped with an affine connection $\Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho}$. The curve is pa
rameterized by some parameter $\lambda$, with coordinates $x^\nu(\lambda)$.

### Parallel Transport Equation

The defining condition for **parallel transport** of $A^\mu$ along $C$ is that t
he covariant derivative along the curve vanishes:

$$\frac{DA^\mu}{D\lambda} = \frac{dA^\mu}{d\lambda} + \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho}\, A^\
rho\, \frac{dx^\nu}{d\lambda} = 0.$$

Multiplying through by $d\lambda$ and using $dx^\nu = \frac{dx^\nu}{d\lambda}\,d
\lambda$, the infinitesimal change in the vector components is:

$$dA^\mu = -\,\Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho}\, A^\rho\, dx^\nu.$$

### The Line Integral for a Segment

For a single segment of the closed path $C$, the accumulated change in $A^\mu$ i
s obtained by integrating the above along that segment:

$$\Delta A^\mu\Big|_{\text{segment}} = \int_{\text{segment}} dA^\mu = -\int_{\te
xt{segment}} \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho}\, A^\rho\, dx^\nu.$$

We therefore **define the line integral** $I$ (for the segment) as the connectio
n-weighted integral:

$$\boxed{I = \int_{\text{segment}} \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho}\, A^\rho\, dx^\nu.}$$

Equivalently, in differential form notation, the integrand is the 1-form

$$\omega^\mu = \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho}\, A^\rho\, dx^\nu,$$

so that $I^\mu = \int_{\text{segment}} \omega^\mu$, and the change in the transp
orted vector over the segment is $\Delta A^\mu = -I^\mu$.

### Closing the Loop (Context)

When the segment is the full closed curve $C$, the total change

$$\Delta A^\mu = -\oint_C \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho}\, A^\rho\, dx^\nu = -I^\mu_C$$

is generally **nonzero** in a curved space. Expanding the connection to first or
der around a base point and using $\Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho} \approx \partial_\nu \Ga
mma^\mu_{\nu\rho}\big|_0\, x^\nu + \cdots$ together with Stokes' theorem yields
the familiar result

$$\Delta A^\mu = -\tfrac{1}{2}\, R^\mu{}_{\rho\nu\sigma}\, A^\rho\, \Sigma^{\nu\
sigma},$$

where $R^\mu{}_{\rho\nu\sigma}$ is the Riemann curvature tensor and $\Sigma^{\nu
\sigma}$ the area bivector enclosed by $C$. This confirms that the line integral
 $I$ encodes the curvature of the space.

### Result

The line integral for the segment of the closed path is:

$$I = \int_{\text{segment}} \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho}\, A^\rho\, dx^\nu,$$

with the parallel-transported vector changing by $\Delta A^\mu = -I$ over that s
egment.
