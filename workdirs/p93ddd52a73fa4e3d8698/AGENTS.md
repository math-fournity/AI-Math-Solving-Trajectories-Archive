# Problem

A metropolitan city is designed as a perfect grid of city blocks, forming a large square composed of $n^2$ individual units, each representing a single block with a side length of 1 mile. The grid is defined by a series of horizontal and vertical streets.

The mayor needs to strategically place exactly $n$ rapid-response emergency stations, with each station occupying exactly one city block. The goal is to ensure that no "critical zone" in the city is left without a station. A "critical zone" is defined as any rectangular area formed by the street grid that has an area of exactly $n$ square miles.

What is the greatest possible value of $n$ such that the mayor can select $n$ specific blocks to house the stations, ensuring that every possible rectangle of area $n$ within the grid contains at least one of the selected blocks?
