**Proof.**

Fix a point $Q$ among the given points. Let $S$ be the set of all *other* points
 $P$ such that $Q$ is one of the two nearest neighbors of $P$. We must show $|S|
\le 11$.

For $P\in S$ write $r(P)=d(Q,P)$, and let $f(P)$ denote the (unique) nearest nei
ghbor of $P$. Since all pairwise distances are distinct, the values $r(P)$ for $
P\in S$ are pairwise distinct, and every "nearest neighbor" is unique.

**Lemma.** Let $P_1,P_2\in S$ with $r_1:=r(P_1)<r_2:=r(P_2)$. If $P_1\neq f(P_2)
$ and $P_2\neq f(P_1)$, then $\angle P_1QP_2>60^\circ$.

*Proof.* Since $Q$ is among the two nearest neighbors of $P_2$, the two nearest
neighbors of $P_2$ are exactly $\{f(P_2),Q\}$. By assumption $P_1\neq f(P_2)$ an
d $P_1\neq Q$, so $P_1$ is not among the two nearest neighbors of $P_2$; as all
distances are distinct,
$$d(P_1,P_2)>d(Q,P_2)=r_2.$$
By the law of cosines in $\triangle QP_1P_2$ (non‑degenerate since no three poin
ts are collinear),
$$d(P_1,P_2)^2=r_1^2+r_2^2-2r_1r_2\cos\theta,\qquad \theta=\angle P_1QP_2.$$
Using $d(P_1,P_2)>r_2$,
$$r_1^2+r_2^2-2r_1r_2\cos\theta>r_2^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; r_1^2>2r_1r_2\cos\thet
a \;\Longrightarrow\; \cos\theta<\frac{r_1}{2r_2}<\frac12,$$
so $\theta>60^\circ$. $\square$

**Corollary.** No three points of $S$ lie within a $60^\circ$ arc (sector) with
vertex $Q$.

*Proof.* Suppose $P_1,P_2,P_3\in S$ all lie in some $60^\circ$ sector at $Q$. Th
en every pairwise angle among them is $\le 60^\circ$. By the contrapositive of t
he Lemma, for each pair $\{P_i,P_j\}$ one of $P_i,P_j$ equals $f$ of the other.

We claim this forces a directed $3$‑cycle in the nearest‑neighbor graph, which i
s impossible. Indeed, consider the three pair‑relations. For each $P_i$, its nea
rest neighbor $f(P_i)$ must belong to $\{P_1,P_2,P_3\}$: if, say, $f(P_1)\notin\
{P_2,P_3\}$, then the pairs $(P_1,P_2)$ and $(P_1,P_3)$ force $f(P_2)=P_1$ and $
f(P_3)=P_1$, leaving the pair $(P_2,P_3)$ with no valid nearest‑neighbor relatio
n — contradiction. Hence each $f(P_i)$ lies in the set, and each $P_i$ is the "t
ail" of exactly one of the three pair‑relations. A functional digraph on three n
odes with three edges and no self‑loops is a single $3$‑cycle: relabeling, $f(P_
1)=P_2,\ f(P_2)=P_3,\ f(P_3)=P_1$.

But then
$$d(P_1,P_2)<d(P_1,P_3),\quad d(P_2,P_3)<d(P_2,P_1)=d(P_1,P_2),\quad d(P_3,P_1)<
d(P_3,P_2)=d(P_2,P_3),$$
giving $d(P_1,P_2)<d(P_1,P_3)<d(P_2,P_3)<d(P_1,P_2)$, a contradiction. $\square$

**Counting.** List the points of $S$ in circular order around $Q$ and let $g_1,\
dots,g_k$ be the consecutive angular gaps, so $\sum g_i=360^\circ$. Any three co
nsecutive points $P_i,P_{i+1},P_{i+2}$ lie in an arc of width $g_i+g_{i+1}$; by
the Corollary this arc has width $>60^\circ$, i.e.
$$g_i+g_{i+1}>60^\circ\qquad (i=1,\dots,k,\ \text{cyclic}).$$
Summing over all $i$, each gap is counted twice:
$$720^\circ=2\sum_{i=1}^{k}g_i=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(g_i+g_{i+1})>k\cdot 60^\circ.$$
Thus $k<12$, so $k\le 11$.
