Looking at this problem, I need to find the intersection number of two curves on

## Identifying the Curves on the Surface

The curve defined by $P = (1, t^2)$ corresponds to the locus $x = 1$ on $E_7$. S
ubstituting $x = 1$ into the surface equation:
$$y^2 = 1 + t^7 + 1 = t^7 + 2$$
This gives a 1-dimensional curve $C_P = V(x-1) \cap E_7$ on the surface.

The curve defined by $Q = (t^3, -1)$ corresponds to the locus $y = -1$ on $E_7$.
 Substituting $y = -1$:
$$1 = x^3 + t^7 + 1 \implies x^3 = -t^7$$
This gives a 1-dimensional curve $C_Q = V(y+1) \cap E_7$ on the surface.

## Computing the Intersection

The intersection $C_P \cap C_Q$ occurs where both $x = 1$ and $y = -1$ hold simu
ltaneously on $E_7$:
$$(-1)^2 = (1)^3 + t^7 + 1$$
$$1 = 1 + t^7 + 1$$
$$t^7 = -1$$

## Intersection Multiplicity

The intersection number is computed scheme-theoretically:
$$C_P \cdot C_Q = \dim_k \mathcal{O}_{E_7} / (x-1,\, y+1)$$

Since $\mathcal{O}_{E_7} = k[x,y,t]/(y^2 - x^3 - t^7 - 1)$, we get:
$$\mathcal{O}_{E_7}/(x-1, y+1) \cong k[t]/\big((-1)^2 - (1)^3 - t^7 - 1\big) = k
[t]/(-t^7 - 1) = k[t]/(t^7 + 1)$$

## Degree Calculation

The intersection number equals:
$$\dim_k k[t]/(t^7 + 1) = \deg(t^7 + 1) = 7$$

**Separability check:** The derivative of $t^7 + 1$ is $7t^6 \equiv 2t^6 \pmod{5
}$, which is nonzero at every root (since $t = 0$ is not a root: $0^7 + 1 = 1 \n
eq 0$). So $t^7 + 1$ is separable over $\overline{\mathbb{F}_5}$, confirming all
 7 roots are simple and each contributes multiplicity 1.

## Answer

The intersection number is **7**.
