**Answer: Yes, such an element exists.**

- $\operatorname{depth}(R) > 0$ if and only if $m \notin \operatorname{Ass}(R)$.
- $\operatorname{depth}(M) = 0$ if and only if $m \in \operatorname{Ass}(M)$.
- An element $x \in R$ is $R$-regular (a non-zero-divisor on $R$) if and only if

Since $\operatorname{depth}(R) > 0$, we have $m \notin \operatorname{Ass}(R)$, s

Since $\operatorname{depth}(M) = 0$, we have $m \in \operatorname{Ass}(M)$. The
set $\operatorname{Ass}(M) - \{m\}$ consists of associated primes of $M$ that ar

igr).$$

We seek an element $x \in m$ with:
1. $x \notin \bigcup_{p \in \operatorname{Ass}(R)} p$ (so $x$ is $R$-regular), a

2. $x \notin \bigcup_{p \in \operatorname{Ass}(M) - \{m\}} p$.

Equivalently, we need $x \in m \setminus \bigcup_{p \in \mathcal{P}} p$, i.e., w

By the **Prime Avoidance Lemma**: if an ideal $I$ is contained in a finite union

Suppose for contradiction that $m \subseteq \bigcup_{p \in \mathcal{P}} p$. Then

Therefore $m \not\subseteq \bigcup_{p \in \mathcal{P}} p$, and there exists $x \
in m$ with $x \notin \bigcup_{p \in \mathcal{P}} p$.

This element $x$ satisfies:
- $x \in m$,
- $x \notin \bigcup_{p \in \operatorname{Ass}(R)} p$, so $x$ is $R$-regular,
- $x \notin \bigcup_{p \in \operatorname{Ass}(M) - \{m\}} p$.

This completes the proof. $\blacksquare$
