## Solution

**Setup.** Glass A has 5 red balls. Glass B has 4 red and 1 white ball (5 total)
. At each step, a glass is chosen with probability 1/2, and a ball is drawn unif
ormly at random from it. The process stops when one glass is emptied.

**Key observation.** The white ball is in glass B. If glass B empties first, all
 5 balls of B (including the white) have been drawn, so the white ball **is** dr
awn. Therefore, the white ball escapes being drawn only if **glass A empties fir
st** **and** the white ball is never selected among the draws made from B.

**Conditioning on the glass-choice sequence.** The choices of glass form a seque
nce of fair coin flips (A/B) that stops at the 5th occurrence of either letter.
Say glass A empties first with exactly $k$ draws from B ($0 \le k \le 4$). Then
the final draw is A (the 5th A), and the preceding $4+k$ draws contain 4 A's and
 $k$ B's in any order. The number of such sequences is $\binom{4+k}{k}$, each wi
th probability $(1/2)^{5+k}$, so

$$P(\text{A empties first with } k \text{ B-draws}) = \binom{4+k}{k}\left(\tfrac
{1}{2}\right)^{5+k}.$$

**Probability the white ball survives $k$ draws from B.** The $k$ balls drawn fr
om B form a uniformly random $k$-subset of B's 5 balls. The white ball is not am
ong them with probability

$$\frac{\binom{4}{k}}{\binom{5}{k}} = \frac{5-k}{5}.$$

**Combining.** By total probability over $k = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4$:

$$P(\text{white not drawn}) = \sum_{k=0}^{4} \binom{4+k}{k}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\ri
ght)^{5+k}\cdot \frac{5-k}{5}.$$

Computing each term (in 256ths):

| $k$ | $\binom{4+k}{k}(1/2)^{5+k}\cdot(5-k)/5$ | value |
|-----|------------------------------------------|-------|
| 0 | $1 \cdot \frac{1}{32}\cdot 1$ | $8/256$ |
| 1 | $5 \cdot \frac{1}{64}\cdot\frac{4}{5}$ | $16/256$ |
| 2 | $15 \cdot \frac{1}{128}\cdot\frac{3}{5}$ | $18/256$ |
| 3 | $35 \cdot \frac{1}{256}\cdot\frac{2}{5}$ | $14/256$ |
| 4 | $70 \cdot \frac{1}{512}\cdot\frac{1}{5}$ | $7/256$ |

Summing:

$$P(\text{white not drawn}) = \frac{8+16+18+14+7}{256} = \frac{63}{256}.$$

**Consistency check.** Summing the same terms without the $(5-k)/5$ factor gives
 $P(\text{A empties first}) = \frac{16+32+36+28+14}{256}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\cdot 2
= \frac{1}{2}$, confirming the symmetry $P(A\text{ first})=P(B\text{ first})=1/2
$. Also, conditioning on A emptying first, $E[k]=325/128$, and $E[(5-k)/5]=(5-32
5/128)/5=63/128$, which equals $(63/256)/(1/2)$, as expected. ✓
