Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a division ring $D$ can con
tain an algebraically closed field $K$ as a subfield with $\dim_K D < \infty$.

**Key distinction:** $K$ is required only to be a subfield of $D$, not necessari
ly contained in the center $Z(D)$. This matters critically.

---

## Proof

**Yes**, such a division ring exists.

Consider the **Hamiltonian quaternions**:
$$\mathbb{H} = \{a + bi + cj + dk : a,b,c,d \in \mathbb{R}\},$$
with $i^2 = j^2 = k^2 = ijk = -1$. This is a division ring: every nonzero quater
nion $q$ has inverse $\bar{q}/|q|^2$, where $\bar{q}$ is the conjugate and $|q|^
2 = q\bar{q} \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}$.

**The subfield $K$.** The subset
$$K = \{a + bi : a, b \in \mathbb{R}\} \subset \mathbb{H}$$
is closed under addition and multiplication (since $i^2 = -1$ and $\mathbb{R}$ i
s central in $\mathbb{H}$), contains $1$, and every nonzero element $a+bi$ has i
nverse $(a-bi)/(a^2+b^2) \in K$. Thus $K$ is a subfield of $\mathbb{H}$ isomorph
ic to $\mathbb{C}$, hence **algebraically closed**.

**The dimension.** As a left $K$-vector space, $\mathbb{H}$ has basis $\{1, j\}$
:

- **Spanning:** Every quaternion $a + bi + cj + dk = (a+bi) + (c+di)j$, where $a
+bi, c+di \in K$.
- **Linear independence:** If $\alpha \cdot 1 + \beta \cdot j = 0$ with $\alpha,
 \beta \in K$, write $\alpha = a+bi$, $\beta = c+di$. Then $a + bi + cj + dk = 0
$ forces $a = b = c = d = 0$, so $\alpha = \beta = 0$.

Therefore:
$$\dim_K \mathbb{H} = 2 < \infty.$$

**Remark.** Note that $K \not\subseteq Z(\mathbb{H}) = \mathbb{R}$, since $ij =
k \neq ji = -k$. If $K$ were required to be *central* (i.e., $K \subseteq Z(D)$)
, then no such example would exist: any $d \in D$ would be algebraic over $K$ (b
y finite-dimensionality), and since $K$ is algebraically closed, $d \in K$, forc
ing $D = K$. The non-centrality of $K$ is essential.
