## Solution

**Step 1: Compute the discriminant and identify the Galois group.**

Let $f(X) = X^3 - 3X + 1$. For a cubic $X^3 + pX + q$, the discriminant is $\Del
ta = -4p^3 - 27q^2$. Here $p = -3$, $q = 1$, so

$$\Delta = -4(-3)^3 - 27(1)^2 = 108 - 27 = 81 = 9^2.$$

Since $\Delta = 81$ is a perfect square, the Galois group of $f$ is contained in
 $A_3$.

**Step 2: Verify $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$.**

By the rational root theorem, the only candidates are $\pm 1$. We have $f(1) = 1
 - 3 + 1 = -1 \neq 0$ and $f(-1) = -1 + 3 + 1 = 3 \neq 0$. So $f$ is irreducible
 over $\mathbb{Q}$, hence the Galois group acts transitively. Being a transitive
 subgroup of $A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$, the Galois group is exactly $\m
athbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$.

Therefore the splitting field $K$ of $f$ is a **cyclic Galois extension of $\mat
hbb{Q}$ of degree 3**.

**Step 3: Check that 2 is unramified.**

The discriminant of the polynomial is $\Delta = 81 = 3^4$. The field discriminan
t $\text{disc}(K)$ satisfies

$$[\mathcal{O}_K : \mathbb{Z}[\alpha]]^2 \cdot \text{disc}(K) = \text{disc}(f) =
 81,$$

where $\alpha$ is any root of $f$. Since $\gcd(2, 81) = 1$, the prime 2 does not
 divide the index $[\mathcal{O}_K : \mathbb{Z}[\alpha]]$, so the Dedekind–Kummer
 theorem applies: the factorization of $f \mod 2$ determines the splitting of 2
in $\mathcal{O}_K$.

In particular, 2 is **unramified** (ramification index $e = 1$).

**Step 4: Factor $f$ modulo 2.**

$$f(X) \equiv X^3 + X + 1 \pmod{2}.$$

Check for roots in $\mathbb{F}_2$:
- $f(0) = 0 + 0 + 1 = 1 \neq 0$,
- $f(1) = 1 + 1 + 1 = 1 \neq 0$.

So $X^3 + X + 1$ has no roots in $\mathbb{F}_2$. Being a cubic with no linear fa
ctors, it is **irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_2$**.

**Step 5: Conclude.**

By the Dedekind–Kummer theorem, since $f \mod 2$ is irreducible of degree 3, the
 ideal $(2)$ remains prime in $\mathcal{O}_K$:

$$(2) = \mathfrak{p}, \quad f(\mathfrak{p}/2) = 3, \quad e(\mathfrak{p}/2) = 1.$
$

This is consistent with the Galois structure: in a Galois extension of degree 3,
 an unramified prime either splits completely ($efg = 3$ with $e=1, f=1, g=3$) o
r is inert ($e=1, f=3, g=1$). The irreducibility of $f \mod 2$ selects the latte
r.

**Conclusion.** The prime 2 is **inert** in the splitting field $K$ of $X^3 - 3X
 + 1$: the ideal $(2)$ remains a single prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ in $\mathcal{
O}_K$ with residue degree $f = 3$ and ramification index $e = 1$.
