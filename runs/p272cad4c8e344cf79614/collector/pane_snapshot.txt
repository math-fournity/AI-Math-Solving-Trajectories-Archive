Looking at this problem, I need to clarify the terminology first. In this contex
t:
- **"Matrix group"** = a subgroup of GL_n(F) for some field F and integer n
- **"Linear"** = a linear algebraic group, i.e., a Zariski-closed subgroup of GL
_n(F) (over an algebraically closed field)

A "non-linear matrix group" is then a subgroup of GL_n(F) that is **not** Zarisk
i-closed. The question asks whether such a group can have a normal subgroup that
 **is** Zariski-closed (and nontrivial).

---

## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, such a group exists.

**Construction.** Let $F = \mathbb{C}$. Define
$$G = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a & 0 \\ 0 & b \end{pmatrix} : a \in \mathbb{C}^*,
\; b \in \mathbb{Q}^* \right\} \leq \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{C}).$$

This is a subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{C})$ (closed under multiplication a
nd inversion since both $\mathbb{C}^*$ and $\mathbb{Q}^*$ are groups), hence a *
*matrix group**.

**Step 1: $G$ is not Zariski-closed (non-linear).**

The Zariski closure of $G$ in $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{C})$ is:
$$\overline{G}^{Z} = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a & 0 \\ 0 & b \end{pmatrix} : a \i
n \mathbb{C}^*,\; b \in \mathbb{C}^* \right\} \cong \mathbb{C}^* \times \mathbb{
C}^*.$$

This holds because $\mathbb{Q}^*$ is **Zariski-dense** in $\mathbb{C}^*$: in the
 affine line $\mathbb{A}^1$, the Zariski-closed subsets are exactly the finite s
ets and $\mathbb{A}^1$ itself. Since $\mathbb{Q}^*$ is infinite, its Zariski clo
sure in $\mathbb{A}^1$ is all of $\mathbb{A}^1$, so $\mathbb{Q}^*$ is Zariski-de
nse in $\mathbb{C}^*$.

Since $\mathbb{Q}^* \subsetneq \mathbb{C}^*$, we have $G \subsetneq \overline{G}
^{Z}$, so **$G$ is not Zariski-closed**. Therefore $G$ is a **non-linear** matri
x group.

**Step 2: $G$ has a normal subgroup that is linear.**

Define
$$N = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} : a \in \mathbb{C}^*
\right\} \cong \mathbb{C}^*.$$

- **$N \leq G$:** Every element of $N$ has the form $\mathrm{diag}(a, 1)$ with $
a \in \mathbb{C}^*$ and $1 \in \mathbb{Q}^*$, so $N \subseteq G$. It is clearly
a subgroup.

- **$N \trianglelefteq G$:** The group $G$ is **abelian** (all elements are diag
onal matrices, and diagonal matrices commute). Therefore every subgroup of $G$ i
s normal, in particular $N \trianglelefteq G$.

- **$N$ is Zariski-closed (linear):** The set $N$ is defined inside $\mathrm{GL}
_2(\mathbb{C})$ by the polynomial equations:
$$x_{12} = 0, \quad x_{21} = 0, \quad x_{22} - 1 = 0,$$
together with the open condition $\det = x_{11} x_{22} - x_{12} x_{21} = x_{11}
\neq 0$. Thus $N$ is a **Zariski-closed** subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{C})
$, i.e., a **linear algebraic group**. Concretely, $N \cong \mathbb{G}_m = \math
rm{GL}_1$, the multiplicative group, which is a one-dimensional algebraic torus.

- **$N$ is nontrivial:** $N \cong \mathbb{C}^*$, which is infinite and nontrivia
l.

**Conclusion.** The group $G = \mathbb{C}^* \times \mathbb{Q}^* \leq \mathrm{GL}
_2(\mathbb{C})$ is a **non-linear matrix group** (it is not Zariski-closed), and
 it has a **nontrivial normal subgroup** $N \cong \mathbb{C}^*$ which **is linea
r** (Zariski-closed, hence a linear algebraic group).

Therefore, **yes**, there exists a non-linear matrix group that has a normal sub
group which is linear. $\blacksquare$
