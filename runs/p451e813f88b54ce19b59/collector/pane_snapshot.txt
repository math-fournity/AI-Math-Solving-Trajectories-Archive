Let
$$I(t) = \int_0^1 e^{t\,f(x)}\,dx.$$

Differentiating under the integral sign (justified since $f$ is bounded, so the
integrand and its $t$-derivatives are dominated on any bounded $t$-interval):

$$I''(t) = \int_0^1 f(x)^2\, e^{t\,f(x)}\,dx.$$

Hence
$$I''(0) = \int_0^1 f(x)^2\,dx.$$

So the problem reduces to computing $\int_0^1 f(x)^2\,dx$.

---

### Binary digits as random variables

For $x \in (0,1]$, write its **non-terminating** binary expansion
$$x = 0.b_1 b_2 b_3 \dots, \qquad b_i \in \{0,1\}.$$

The "non-terminating" convention selects, for dyadic rationals, the expansion en
ding in all $1$'s rather than all $0$'s. The set of dyadic rationals in $(0,1]$
is countable, hence has Lebesgue measure $0$, so it does not affect any integral
.

Under the Lebesgue measure on $(0,1]$, the binary digits $b_1, b_2, \dots$ are *
*i.i.d. Bernoulli$(1/2)$** random variables. (Each digit is determined by which
half of the remaining interval $x$ falls in, and these choices are independent a
nd equiprobable.)

---

### Computing $f(x)$

Let $k(x) = b_1 + b_2 + \cdots + b_n$ be the number of $1$'s among the first $n$
 digits. The number of $0$'s is $n - k$. Therefore
$$f(x) = k - (n-k) = 2k - n = 2\!\left(k - \tfrac{n}{2}\right).$$

Since the $b_i$ are i.i.d. Bernoulli$(1/2)$, the sum $k$ is distributed as **Bin
omial$(n, 1/2)$**, with
$$\mathbb{E}[k] = \frac{n}{2}, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(k) = n \cdot \frac{1}{2
}\cdot\frac{1}{2} = \frac{n}{4}.$$

---

### The integral as an expectation

Since $x$ is uniform on $(0,1]$,
$$\int_0^1 f(x)^2\,dx = \mathbb{E}\!\left[f(x)^2\right] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\big
l(2k - n\bigr)^2\right] = 4\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bigl(k - \tfrac{n}{2}\bigr)^2\ri
ght] = 4\,\operatorname{Var}(k).$$

Substituting the variance of a Binomial$(n,1/2)$:
$$I''(0) = 4 \cdot \frac{n}{4} = n.$$

---

### Result

$$\boxed{\dfrac{d^2}{dt^2}\!\left(\int_0^1 e^{t\,f(x)}\,dx\right)\bigg|_{t=0} \;
=\; n.}$$
