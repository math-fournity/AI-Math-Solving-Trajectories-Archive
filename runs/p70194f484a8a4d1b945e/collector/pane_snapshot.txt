## Solution

**Answer: No.** Each $I^k$ need not be a finite $A$-module.

### Counterexample

Take $A = \mathbb{Z}$ (a Noetherian ring) and $M = \mathbb{Z}$ (a finite $\mathb
b{Z}$-module). We compute the minimal injective resolution of $\mathbb{Z}$ over
$\mathbb{Z}$.

**Injective modules over $\mathbb{Z}$.** A $\mathbb{Z}$-module is injective iff
it is divisible. The indecomposable injectives are $\mathbb{Q}$ and the Prüfer $
p$-groups $\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty) = E(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})$ for primes $p$.

**The injective hull of $\mathbb{Z}$ is $\mathbb{Q}$.** Every divisible group co
ntaining $\mathbb{Z}$ must contain $\mathbb{Q}$ (since divisibility forces $1/n$
 to exist for all $n$), and $\mathbb{Q}$ itself is divisible hence injective. Th
us $E(\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Q}$.

**The minimal injective resolution.** Starting from $0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarr
ow{\iota} E(\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Q}$, the cokernel is
$$\operatorname{coker}(\iota) = \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}.$$
The group $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ is divisible (quotient of a divisible group),
hence injective. Therefore its injective hull is itself, and the resolution term
inates:
$$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Q} \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \to 0.$$
So $I^1 = \mathbb{Q}$ and $I^2 = \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \cong \bigoplus_{p}\mathb
b{Z}(p^\infty)$.

**$I^1 = \mathbb{Q}$ is not a finite $\mathbb{Z}$-module.** Suppose $\mathbb{Q}$
 were generated by $\frac{a_1}{b_1},\dots,\frac{a_n}{b_n}$ as a $\mathbb{Z}$-mod
ule. Then every element of $\mathbb{Q}$ would be a $\mathbb{Z}$-linear combinati
on, hence would have denominator dividing $B = \operatorname{lcm}(b_1,\dots,b_n)
$. But $\frac{1}{B+1} \in \mathbb{Q}$ has denominator $B+1$, which does not divi
de $B$ — contradiction. Hence $\mathbb{Q}$ is not finitely generated over $\math
bb{Z}$.

### Conclusion

With $A = \mathbb{Z}$ (Noetherian) and $M = \mathbb{Z}$ (finite), the minimal in
jective resolution has $I^1 = \mathbb{Q}$, which is **not** a finite $\mathbb{Z}
$-module. Therefore the statement is false in general: the terms $I^k$ of a mini
mal injective resolution of a finite module over a Noetherian ring need not be f
inite.

**Remark.** What *is* true over a Noetherian ring is that the Bass numbers $\mu_
k(\mathfrak{p}, M) = \dim_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}\operatorname{Ext}^k_{A_{\mathfrak{p
}}}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}), M_{\mathfrak{p}})$ are finite for finite $M$ (a theore
m of Bass), so each indecomposable summand $E(A/\mathfrak{p})$ appears with fini
te multiplicity. The obstruction to finiteness of $I^k$ is twofold: (i) infinite
ly many primes may contribute, and (ii) the injective hulls $E(A/\mathfrak{p})$
are typically Artinian but not Noetherian, hence not finitely generated. In the
example above, both issues appear: $\mathbb{Q}$ is a single indecomposable injec
tive that is not finitely generated, and $I^2 = \bigoplus_p \mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)
$ is an infinite direct sum.
