No. Every matrix of the form $CC^T$ is symmetric, since $(CC^T)^T = (C^T)^T C^T
= CC^T$. A linear combination of symmetric matrices is again symmetric, because
$(\sum \lambda_i M_i)^T = \sum \lambda_i M_i^T = \sum \lambda_i M_i$. Therefore
any linear combination of matrices of the form $CC^T$ must be symmetric.

However, a product $AB$ of two square matrices need not be symmetric. For a conc
rete counterexample, take over $\mathbb{R}$

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B = \begin{pmatrix} 0
 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Then

$$AB = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix},$$

which is not symmetric ($(AB)^T \neq AB$). Hence this $AB$ cannot be expressed a
s a linear combination of matrices of the form $CC^T$.

Therefore, not every product $AB$ of two square matrices can be expressed as a l
inear combination of matrices of the form $CC^T$.
