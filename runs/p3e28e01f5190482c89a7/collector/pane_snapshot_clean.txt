## Proof

Let $f \colon \mathbb{C}_\infty \to \mathbb{C}_\infty$ be a map preserving all c
ross ratios, i.e. for every quadruple of distinct points $z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4 \in \m
athbb{C}_\infty$,
$$
[f(z_1),f(z_2),f(z_3),f(z_4)] \;=\; [z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4],
$$
where $[z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4]=\dfrac{(z_1-z_3)(z_2-z_4)}{(z_1-z_4)(z_2-z_3)}$ (with t
he usual limiting conventions at $\infty$).

**Step 1. $f$ is injective.**

Suppose $f(z_1)=f(z_2)=w$ with $z_1\neq z_2$. Choose $z_3,z_4$ distinct from eac
h other and from $z_1,z_2$. Then the image cross ratio is
$$
\frac{(w-f(z_3))(w-f(z_4))}{(w-f(z_4))(w-f(z_3))}=1.
$$
Hence $[z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4]=1$. But $[z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4]=1$ iff $(z_1-z_2)(z_3-z_4)=0
$, which is impossible since all four points are distinct. Contradiction. So $f$
 is injective.

**Step 2. Reduction to a Möbius map.**

Fix three distinct points $a,b,c\in\mathbb{C}_\infty$. By Step 1, $f(a),f(b),f(c
)$ are distinct. Define the Möbius transformation
$$
M(z)=[z,a,b,c]=\frac{(z-b)(a-c)}{(z-c)(a-b)},
$$
which sends $a\mapsto 1,\; b\mapsto 0,\; c\mapsto\infty$. Likewise define the Mö
bius transformation
$$
N(z)=[z,f(a),f(b),f(c)],
$$
which sends $f(a)\mapsto 1,\; f(b)\mapsto 0,\; f(c)\mapsto\infty$.

For any $z\notin\{a,b,c\}$, cross-ratio preservation gives
$$
[f(z),f(a),f(b),f(c)]=[z,a,b,c],
$$
i.e. $N(f(z))=M(z)$. This identity also holds at $z\in\{a,b,c\}$ by construction
 ($N(f(a))=1=M(a)$, etc.). Therefore
$$
f(z)=N^{-1}(M(z))\qquad\text{for all }z\in\mathbb{C}_\infty.
$$

**Step 3. Conclusion.**

$N^{-1}\circ M$ is a composition of Möbius transformations, hence a Möbius trans
formation. Thus $f$ is Möbius. (Surjectivity is automatic, since a Möbius map is
 bijective; so cross-ratio preservation forces $f$ to be a bijection of the sphe
re.)

No continuity or measurability hypothesis was needed: preservation of *all* cros
s ratios alone forces $f$ to be a Möbius transformation.

**Answer:** Yes — any map $f\colon\mathbb{C}_\infty\to\mathbb{C}_\infty$ preserv
ing all cross ratios must be a Möbius transformation.

### PROOF COMPLETE
