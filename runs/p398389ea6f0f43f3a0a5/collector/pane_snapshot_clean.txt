## Proof

We evaluate
$$I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\cos x}{(x^2+1)^2}\,dx$$
by contour integration.

**Step 1: Complexify.** Since $\cos x = \operatorname{Re}(e^{ix})$, consider the
 meromorphic function
$$f(z) = \frac{e^{iz}}{(z^2+1)^2} = \frac{e^{iz}}{(z-i)^2(z+i)^2}.$$
The integrand is even, and the imaginary part $\frac{\sin x}{(x^2+1)^2}$ is odd,
 so
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos x}{(x^2+1
)^2}\,dx + i\cdot 0 = I.$$

**Step 2: Choose the contour.** Let $C_R$ be the closed contour consisting of $[
-R,R]$ on the real axis followed by the upper semicircle $\Gamma_R = \{Re^{i\the
ta}:0\le\theta\le\pi\}$. In the upper half-plane the only singularity of $f$ is
at $z=i$, a pole of order $2$ (the pole at $z=-i$ lies in the lower half-plane).

**Step 3: Residue at $z=i$.** For a double pole,
$$\operatorname{Res}(f,i) = \lim_{z\to i}\frac{d}{dz}\left[(z-i)^2 f(z)\right] =
 \lim_{z\to i}\frac{d}{dz}\left[\frac{e^{iz}}{(z+i)^2}\right].$$
Differentiating,
$$\frac{d}{dz}\left[\frac{e^{iz}}{(z+i)^2}\right] = \frac{ie^{iz}(z+i)^2 - 2(z+i
)e^{iz}}{(z+i)^4} = \frac{e^{iz}\bigl[i(z+i)-2\bigr]}{(z+i)^3}.$$
At $z=i$: $z+i = 2i$, so $i(z+i)-2 = i(2i)-2 = -2-2 = -4$, and $(z+i)^3 = (2i)^3
 = -8i$, and $e^{iz}\big|_{z=i} = e^{-1}$. Hence
$$\operatorname{Res}(f,i) = \frac{e^{-1}\cdot(-4)}{-8i} = \frac{1}{2ei} = -\frac
{i}{2e}.$$

**Step 4: Apply the residue theorem.**
$$\oint_{C_R} f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i\,\operatorname{Res}(f,i) = 2\pi i\left(-\frac{i}
{2e}\right) = \frac{\pi}{e}.$$

**Step 5: The arc integral vanishes.** On $\Gamma_R$, $z = Re^{i\theta}$ with $\
theta\in[0,\pi]$, so $|e^{iz}| = e^{-R\sin\theta}\le 1$, and $|z^2+1|\ge R^2-1$.
 Therefore
$$\left|\int_{\Gamma_R} f(z)\,dz\right| \le \frac{\pi R}{(R^2-1)^2}\xrightarrow[
R\to\infty]{}0.$$
(Equivalently, Jordan's lemma applies since the denominator has degree $4>1$.)

**Step 6: Conclude.** Letting $R\to\infty$,
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{ix}}{(x^2+1)^2}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{e}.$$
Taking real parts (the imaginary part is zero by oddness, as a consistency check
):
$$\boxed{\,I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos x}{(x^2+1)^2}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{
e}\,}.$$
