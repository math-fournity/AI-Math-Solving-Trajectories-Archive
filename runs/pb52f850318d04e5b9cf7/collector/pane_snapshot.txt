## Solution

**Unfolding trick.** The flat barrier (x-axis) can be "unfolded" by reflecting t
he semicircle across it, converting the problem into a billiard inside the **ful
l unit circle**. Each bounce off the flat barrier in the original problem become
s a straight-line crossing of the x-axis in the unfolded picture. Each hit on th
e circle boundary (upper or lower arc) corresponds to a bounce off the curved hu
ll (lower-arc hits fold back to upper-arc hits).

**Circular billiard dynamics.** In a circular billiard, if a trajectory hits the
 boundary at angle of incidence α (measured from the tangent), then consecutive
hit points on the circle are separated by a constant central angle of 2α, and α
is preserved at every bounce.

At W = (−1, 0), the tangent to the circle is vertical. The beam fires at 46° to
the x-axis, so its angle with the vertical tangent is:
$$\alpha = 90° - 46° = 44°$$

The central angle step between consecutive circle hits is:
$$2\alpha = 88°$$

**First hit verification.** The beam from (−1, 0) in direction (cos 46°, sin 46°
) hits the circle at parameter $t = 2\cos 46°$, giving the hit point $(\cos 92°,
 \sin 92°)$ — at central angle 92°, confirming the step is $180° - 92° = 88°$. ✓

**Return condition.** The hit points are at angles $180° - 88°n$ (mod 360°) for
$n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$ The beam returns to W = (−1,0) = 180° when:
$$88n \equiv 0 \pmod{360}$$

Since $\gcd(88, 360) = 8$, this gives $11n = 45m$. With $\gcd(11, 45) = 1$, the
smallest solution is:
$$n = 45, \quad m = 11$$

So the beam hits the circle 45 times (at $P_1, P_2, \ldots, P_{45} = P_0$), with
 $P_{45}$ being the **return** (not a bounce).

**Counting bounces off the curved hull.** The 44 hits $P_1$ through $P_{44}$ are
 bounces off the curved hull. (Verified: no $P_i$ for $1 \le i \le 44$ lands at
0° or 180°, so no corner collisions occur.)

**Counting bounces off the flat barrier.** A bounce off the flat barrier corresp
onds to the unfolded trajectory crossing the x-axis, which happens when consecut
ive hit points are on opposite sides (one with $\sin\theta > 0$, the other with
$\sin\theta < 0$).

Computing $\sin(88°n)$ for each $n$ reveals the sign pattern (period 4):
$$n: \quad 0,\; 1,\; 2,\; 3,\; 4,\; 5,\; 6,\; 7,\; 8,\; \ldots$$
$$\text{sign:} \quad 0,\; +,\; +,\; -,\; -,\; +,\; +,\; -,\; -,\; \ldots$$

A sign change (hence an x-axis crossing) occurs at every **even** $n \geq 2$. Th
e full list from $n = 0$ to $n = 45$:

| Segment $n \to n{+}1$ | Signs | Crossing? |
|---|---|---|
| $0 \to 1$ | $0 \to +$ | No (starts on axis) |
| $1 \to 2$ | $+ \to +$ | No |
| $2 \to 3$ | $+ \to -$ | **Yes** |
| $3 \to 4$ | $- \to -$ | No |
| $4 \to 5$ | $- \to +$ | **Yes** |
| $\vdots$ | $\vdots$ | $\vdots$ |
| $42 \to 43$ | $+ \to -$ | **Yes** |
| $43 \to 44$ | $- \to -$ | No |
| $44 \to 45$ | $- \to 0$ | No (return to start) |

Crossings occur at $n = 2, 4, 6, 8, \ldots, 42$, giving:
$$\frac{42 - 2}{2} + 1 = 21 \text{ bounces off the flat barrier}$$

(All crossing points are strictly interior to the diameter, since no $P_i$ is at
 0° or 180° for $1 \le i \le 44$.)

**Total bounce count:**
$$\underbrace{44}_{\text{curved hull}} + \underbrace{21}_{\text{flat barrier}} =
 \boxed{65}$$
