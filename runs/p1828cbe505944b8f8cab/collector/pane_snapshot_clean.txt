Looking at this problem, I need to find all positive integers $n$ such that for
every permutation of vertices, there exist 3 magpies whose initial triangle and
final triangle are of the same type (both acute, both right, or both obtuse).

**Classification.** For three vertices of a regular $n$-gon with arc-gaps $g_1+g
_2+g_3=n$ (each $g_i\geq 1$), the inscribed triangle is:
- **Right** iff $\max(g_i)=n/2$ (requires $n$ even; a diameter is a side),
- **Obtuse** iff $\max(g_i)>n/2$ (three points lie in an open semicircle),
- **Acute** iff $\max(g_i)<n/2$ (center strictly inside).

**Reformulation.** For a triple $T=\{i,j,k\}$, let $\tau(T)\in\{A,R,O\}$ denote
its type. We seek all $n$ where every permutation $\sigma$ admits a triple $T$ w
ith $\tau(T)=\tau(\sigma(T))$. Call $\sigma$ *type-destroying* if $\tau(T)\neq\t
au(\sigma(T))$ for all triples $T$. Then $n$ is a solution iff no type-destroyin
g permutation exists.

---

**$n=5$ is NOT a solution.** Take $\sigma(i)=2i\pmod{5}$. The 5 obtuse triples (
three consecutive vertices, gaps $(1,1,3)$) map to triples with gaps $(2,2,1)$ —
 acute. The 5 acute triples (gaps $(1,2,2)$) map to gaps $(1,1,3)$ — obtuse. Eve
ry triple changes type, so $\sigma$ is type-destroying.

---

**$n=3$ is a solution.** The only triple is $\{0,1,2\}$ (equilateral, acute). An
y permutation maps $\{0,1,2\}$ to itself, preserving type.

---

**All even $n\geq 4$ are solutions.** For $n=2m$, right triples are exactly thos
e containing a diametrically opposite pair (gap $m$).

Suppose $\sigma$ is type-destroying. Then every right triple maps to a non-right
 triple. Fix any diameter $D=\{a, a+m\}$. For each $b\notin D$, the right triple
 $\{a, a+m, b\}$ maps to $\{\sigma(a), \sigma(a+m), \sigma(b)\}$, which must con
tain no diameter. This requires:
1. $\{\sigma(a), \sigma(a+m)\}$ is not a diameter;
2. For all $b\notin D$: $\sigma(b)\neq \sigma(a)+m$ and $\sigma(b)\neq \sigma(a+
m)+m$.

Since $\sigma$ is a permutation, $\{\sigma(b): b\notin D\} = \{0,\dots,n-1\}\set
minus\{\sigma(a),\sigma(a+m)\}$. Condition 2 forces $\sigma(a)+m \in \{\sigma(a)
, \sigma(a+m)\}$. Since $\sigma(a)+m\neq \sigma(a)$, we get $\sigma(a+m)=\sigma(
a)+m$, meaning $\{\sigma(a),\sigma(a+m)\}$ **is** a diameter — contradicting con
dition 1.

So no type-destroying permutation exists for any even $n\geq 4$. $\square$

---

**All odd $n\geq 7$ are solutions.** For odd $n=2m+1$ ($m\geq 3$), there are no
right triangles. A triple is obtuse iff $\max(g_i)\geq m+1$, acute iff $\max(g_i
)\leq m$.

**Counting obtuse triples:** Each obtuse triple has a unique gap $\geq m+1$ (two
 such gaps would sum to $\geq 2m+2>n$). For gap $g$ with $m+1\leq g\leq 2m-1$, t
he remaining $n-g$ splits into two positive parts in $n-g-1=2m-g$ ways. Summing:
$$|O| = n\sum_{g=m+1}^{2m-1}(2m-g) = n\cdot\frac{m(m-1)}{2} = \frac{(2m+1)\,m(m-
1)}{2}.$$

**Counting acute triples:** $|A| = \binom{n}{3}-|O| = \frac{(2m+1)m(m+1)}{6}$.

For $m\geq 3$: $|O|/|A| = \frac{3(m-1)}{m+1} > 1$ (since $3m-3>m+1 \iff m>2$). S
o $|O|>|A|$.

A type-destroying permutation requires $\sigma(O)\subseteq A$ and $\sigma(A)\sub
seteq O$, forcing $|O|\leq|A|$ — impossible when $|O|>|A|$. $\square$

---

**$n=1,2$ are not solutions** (fewer than 3 magpies exist).

---

**Conclusion.** The positive integers $n$ satisfying the condition are:

$$\boxed{n\geq 3,\quad n\neq 5.}$$

Equivalently, all positive integers $n$ except $1, 2,$ and $5$.
