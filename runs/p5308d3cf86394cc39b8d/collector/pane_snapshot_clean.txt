## Proof

**Claim.** In a metric space $(X,d)$ in which a finite subset $A\subseteq X$ is
dense, every subset of $X$ is open. Equivalently, the topology on $X$ is the dis
crete topology $\mathcal{P}(X)$.

**Step 1. The dense finite set $A$ equals all of $X$.**

In any metric space every singleton $\{a\}$ is closed: its complement $X\setminu
s\{a\}$ is open, since for any $x\neq a$ the ball $B\!\left(x,\tfrac{d(x,a)}{2}\
right)$ is contained in $X\setminus\{a\}$ (any $z$ in this ball satisfies $d(z,a
)\ge d(x,a)-d(x,z)>d(x,a)/2>0$, so $z\neq a$). A finite set is a finite union of
 singletons, hence closed. Thus $A$ is closed.

Since $A$ is dense, $\overline{A}=X$. But $A$ is closed, so $\overline{A}=A$. Th
erefore
$$A=\overline{A}=X.$$
In particular $X=A$ is **finite**.

**Step 2. In a finite metric space every singleton is open.**

Let $x\in X$. Since $X$ is finite and $d(x,y)>0$ for every $y\neq x$, the number
$$r_x \;=\; \min\{\,d(x,y):y\in X,\ y\neq x\,\}$$
exists and is strictly positive. Then the open ball
$$B\!\left(x,\tfrac{r_x}{2}\right)=\{x\},$$
because any $y\neq x$ has $d(x,y)\ge r_x > r_x/2$. Hence $\{x\}$ is open.

**Step 3. Every subset of $X$ is open.**

Any $U\subseteq X$ is a union of singletons:
$$U=\bigcup_{x\in U}\{x\}.$$
Each $\{x\}$ is open by Step 2, and an arbitrary union of open sets is open. Hen
ce $U$ is open.

Conversely, every open set is by definition a subset of $X$. So the open sets ar
e exactly the subsets of $X$.

**Conclusion.** The open sets of $(X,d)$ are precisely all subsets of $X$; the t
opology is the discrete topology $\mathcal{P}(X)$.
