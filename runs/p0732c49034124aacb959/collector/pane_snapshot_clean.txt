## Proof

Let $L$ be a field of algebraic functions of one variable over a field $K$ of ch
aracteristic zero (i.e., $L$ is a finitely generated extension of $K$ with $\ope
ratorname{trdeg}(L/K)=1$). We ask whether there exist a subfield $F\subseteq L$
and an element $a\in L$ such that $L=F(a)$ with $a$ transcendental over $F$.

### Key Observation

If $L=F(a)$ with $a$ transcendental over $F$, then $L\cong F(t)$, the rational f
unction field in one variable over $F$. The genus of $F(t)$ over $F$ is $0$.

### The genus is an intrinsic invariant (characteristic $0$)

The **constant field** of $L$ (over a base field) is the algebraic closure of th
at base in $L$. The genus $g(L/k)$ is defined for any field $k$ algebraically cl
osed in $L$ with $\operatorname{trdeg}(L/k)=1$.

**Claim:** In characteristic $0$, the genus is independent of the choice of alge
braically closed constant field.

*Proof of claim.* Let $k_1, k_2$ both be algebraically closed in $L$ with $\oper
atorname{trdeg}(L/k_i)=1$. Consider the compositum $k_1 k_2 \subseteq L$. Since
$k_1$ is algebraically closed in $L$ and $k_2$ is algebraic over the prime field
 relative to $k_1$ (both have transcendence degree $0$ over their intersection w
ithin $L$), the extension $k_1 k_2 / k_1$ is algebraic. In characteristic $0$ it
 is separable. By the standard invariance theorem (see Stichtenoth, *Algebraic F
unction Fields and Codes*, or Chevalley), the genus is preserved under separable
 algebraic constant field extensions:
$$g(L/k_1) = g(L/k_1 k_2) = g(L/k_2). \quad \square$$

### Applying the invariant

Suppose $L=F(a)$ with $a$ transcendental over $F$. Then:

1. **$F$ is algebraically closed in $L$:** Since $L=F(t)$ is a *regular* (purely
 transcendental) extension of $F$, any element of $F(t)$ algebraic over $F$ must
 lie in $F$. So the constant field of $L$ over $F$ is $F$ itself.

2. **The genus over $F$ is $0$:** $L\cong F(t)$ is rational, so $g(L/F)=0$.

3. **The genus over $K$ is also $0$:** Let $k=\overline{K}^{\,\mathrm{alg}\cap L
}$ be the algebraic closure of $K$ in $L$ (the constant field of $L$ over $K$).
Both $F$ and $k$ are algebraically closed in $L$. By the claim above, $g(L/k)=g(
L/F)=0$.

**Therefore, a necessary condition for $L=F(a)$ (with $a$ transcendental over $F
$) is $g(L)=0$.**

### Sufficiency when $g=0$

Conversely, if $L$ has genus $0$, then $L$ is a rational function field over its
 constant field $k$: there exists $t\in L$ transcendental over $k$ with $L=k(t)$
. Taking $F=k$ and $a=t$ gives $L=F(a)$ with $a$ transcendental over $F$.

### Counterexample: genus $\geq 1$

Take $K=\mathbb{Q}$ and $L=\mathbb{Q}(x,y)$ with the relation $y^2=x^3-x$. This
is the function field of the elliptic curve $E: y^2=x^3-x$, which is a smooth pl
ane cubic of **genus $1$**.

By the necessary condition proved above, since $g(L)=1\neq 0$, there exist **no*
* subfield $F\subseteq L$ and element $a\in L$ with $L=F(a)$ and $a$ transcenden
tal over $F$.

### Conclusion

The answer is **no**. A function field $L$ of one variable over a characteristic
-$0$ field $K$ can be written as $F(a)$ with $a$ transcendental over $F$ **if an
d only if** $L$ has genus $0$. The genus provides an intrinsic obstruction: a si
mple transcendental extension $F(a)\cong F(t)$ is always a rational function fie
ld (genus $0$), and in characteristic $0$ the genus is an invariant of the funct
ion field independent of the choice of algebraically closed constant field. Func
tion fields of genus $\geq 1$—such as the function field of any elliptic curve—c
annot be expressed in this form.
