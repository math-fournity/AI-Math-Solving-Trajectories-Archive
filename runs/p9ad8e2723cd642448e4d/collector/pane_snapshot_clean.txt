## Proof

**Setup.** Define
$$L_n := \ln\!\left(\frac{C_n^{F_n}}{F_n^{C_n}}\right) = F_n\ln C_n - C_n\ln F_n
.$$
We determine the sign and magnitude of $L_n$ as $n\to\infty$.

**Asymptotics of the ingredients.**

*Catalan number.* Since $C_n = \frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}$ and $\binom{2n}{n}\si
m\frac{4^n}{\sqrt{\pi n}}$ (Stirling), we have
$$C_n \sim \frac{4^n}{n^{3/2}\sqrt{\pi}}, \qquad \ln C_n = n\ln 4 - \tfrac{3}{2}
\ln n - \tfrac{1}{2}\ln\pi + o(1).$$

*Fermat number.* Since $F_n = 2^{2^n}+1$,
$$\ln F_n = \ln(2^{2^n}+1) = 2^n\ln 2 + \ln(1+2^{-2^n}) = 2^n\ln 2 + o(1).$$

**Estimate Term 1: $F_n\ln C_n$.**
$$F_n\ln C_n = (2^{2^n}+1)\bigl(n\ln 4 - \tfrac{3}{2}\ln n - \tfrac{1}{2}\ln\pi
+ o(1)\bigr).$$
The dominant contribution is
$$F_n\ln C_n = 2^{2^n}\cdot n\ln 4\,\bigl(1 + O(\tfrac{\ln n}{n})\bigr) + O(n).$
$
In particular $F_n\ln C_n \ge \tfrac{1}{2}\,n\ln 4\cdot 2^{2^n}$ for all suffici
ently large $n$.

**Estimate Term 2: $C_n\ln F_n$.**
$$C_n\ln F_n = \frac{4^n}{n^{3/2}\sqrt{\pi}}(1+o(1))\cdot 2^n\ln 2\,(1+o(1)) = \
frac{2^{3n}\ln 2}{n^{3/2}\sqrt{\pi}}(1+o(1)).$$
In particular $C_n\ln F_n \le \frac{2\,\ln 2}{n^{3/2}\sqrt{\pi}}\cdot 2^{3n}$ fo
r all sufficiently large $n$.

**Compare the two terms.** Form the ratio
$$\frac{F_n\ln C_n}{C_n\ln F_n} \;\sim\; \frac{2^{2^n}\cdot n\ln 4}{\,2^{3n}\ln
2\,/\,n^{3/2}\sqrt{\pi}\,} \;=\; 2\,n^{5/2}\sqrt{\pi}\;\cdot\; 2^{\,2^n - 3n}.$$

Since $2^n - 3n \to +\infty$ (already $2^n > 3n$ for $n\ge 5$, and the gap grows
 without bound), the factor $2^{2^n-3n}\to\infty$ super-exponentially. The polyn
omial prefactor $2n^{5/2}\sqrt{\pi}$ only helps. Therefore
$$\frac{F_n\ln C_n}{C_n\ln F_n}\;\longrightarrow\;+\infty.$$

**Conclusion.** Since Term 1 dominates Term 2 completely,
$$L_n = F_n\ln C_n - C_n\ln F_n \;\longrightarrow\; +\infty,$$
and consequently
$$\frac{C_n^{F_n}}{F_n^{C_n}} = e^{L_n}\;\longrightarrow\;+\infty.$$

The limit **diverges to $+\infty$**. $\blacksquare$
