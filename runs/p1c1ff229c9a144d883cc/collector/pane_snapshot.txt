## Proof

**Setup.** Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ be a $C^2$ domain with $0 \in \part
ial\Omega$. The unit vector $e_n = (0,\ldots,0,1)$ is orthogonal to $\partial\Om
ega$ at $0$, meaning $e_n$ is normal to the tangent plane $T_0(\partial\Omega)$.
 By the implicit function theorem (applicable since the normal $e_n$ has a nonze
ro $x_n$-component), in a neighborhood of $0$ the boundary is the graph
$$x_n = \psi(x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1}), \qquad \psi \in C^2.$$

**Step 1: $\psi(0)=0$.** Since $0 \in \partial\Omega$ and the boundary is the gr
aph $x_n = \psi(x')$ (with $x' = (x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1})$), the point $0 = (0',0)$
lies on this graph, giving $\psi(0) = 0$.

**Step 2: Tangent vectors of the graph at $0$.** The boundary surface is paramet
rized by
$$x' \mapsto (x', \psi(x')).$$
The tangent vectors at $x' = 0$ along the coordinate directions are
$$\tau_i = e_i + \psi_i(0)\, e_n, \qquad i = 1,\ldots,n-1,$$
where $\psi_i = \partial\psi/\partial x_i$. These $n-1$ vectors span $T_0(\parti
al\Omega)$.

**Step 3: Orthogonality condition.** The hypothesis that $e_n$ is orthogonal to
$\partial\Omega$ at $0$ means $e_n \perp T_0(\partial\Omega)$, i.e., $e_n$ is or
thogonal to every $\tau_i$:
$$\langle e_n,\, \tau_i \rangle = \langle e_n,\, e_i + \psi_i(0)\,e_n \rangle =
\psi_i(0) = 0, \qquad i = 1,\ldots,n-1.$$

**Step 4: Equivalently, via the normal vector.** Defining $F(x) = x_n - \psi(x')
$, the boundary is the level set $F = 0$, and a normal vector at $0$ is
$$\nabla F(0) = (-\psi_1(0),\ldots,-\psi_{n-1}(0),\,1).$$
The hypothesis says $e_n = (0,\ldots,0,1)$ is parallel to this normal, so there
exists $c \in \mathbb{R}$ with
$$(-\psi_1(0),\ldots,-\psi_{n-1}(0),\,1) = c\,(0,\ldots,0,1).$$
The last component gives $c = 1$, and the first $n-1$ components give $\psi_i(0)
 = 0$ for all $i = 1,\ldots,n-1$. (The sign of the normal—whether it is $+e_n$ o
r $-e_n$—does not affect this conclusion, since the last component is $\pm 1 \ne
q 0$ in either case, forcing $c = \pm 1$ and again $\psi_i(0) = 0$.)

**Conclusion.** The given hypotheses—that $\Omega$ is $C^2$, that $0 \in \partia
l\Omega$, that $e_n$ is orthogonal to $\partial\Omega$ at $0$, and that the boun
dary is locally the graph $x_n = \psi(x')$—are **sufficient** to conclude
$$\psi_i(0) = 0, \qquad i = 1, 2, \ldots, n-1.$$

Geometrically, this says the tangent plane to $\partial\Omega$ at $0$ is the hyp
erplane $\{x_n = 0\}$, which is exactly what $e_n$ being the normal direction en
tails.
