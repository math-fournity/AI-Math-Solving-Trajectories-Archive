**Proof.** First, note that for $s, t \in [0,1]$, the point $a + sx + ty$ lies i
n $U$. Indeed,
$$a + sx + ty = (1-s)(1-t)\,a + s(1-t)\,(a+x) + (1-s)t\,(a+y) + st\,(a+x+y),$$
a convex combination of $a, a+x, a+y, a+x+y \in U$, hence in $U$ by convexity. T
hus all evaluations below are well-defined.

Define $g(t) = f(a + x + ty) - f(a + ty)$ for $t \in [0,1]$. Then
$$g(1) - g(0) = f(a+x+y) - f(a+y) - f(a+x) + f(a) = \Delta^2 f_a(x)(y).$$

Since $f \in C^2(U,W)$, the function $t \mapsto f(a+x+ty)$ is $C^1$ (in fact $C^
2$) on $[0,1]$, and by the chain rule
$$g'(t) = df_{a+x+ty}(y) - df_{a+ty}(y).$$

Applying the fundamental theorem of calculus to $g$ on $[0,1]$,
$$\Delta^2 f_a(x)(y) = g(1) - g(0) = \int_0^1 g'(t)\,dt = \int_0^1 \bigl[df_{a+x
+ty}(y) - df_{a+ty}(y)\bigr]\,dt. \tag{1}$$

Now fix $t \in [0,1]$ and define $h(s) = df_{a+sx+ty}(y)$ for $s \in [0,1]$. Sin
ce $f \in C^2$, the map $s \mapsto df_{a+sx+ty}$ is $C^1$, and by the chain rule
$$h'(s) = d^2 f_{a+sx+ty}(x)(y).$$

Applying the fundamental theorem of calculus to $h$ on $[0,1]$,
$$df_{a+x+ty}(y) - df_{a+ty}(y) = h(1) - h(0) = \int_0^1 h'(s)\,ds = \int_0^1 d^
2 f_{a+sx+ty}(x)(y)\,ds. \tag{2}$$

Substituting (2) into (1),
$$\Delta^2 f_a(x)(y) = \int_0^1 \int_0^1 d^2 f_{a+sx+ty}(x)(y)\,ds\,dt = \left(\
int_0^1 \int_0^1 d^2 f_{a+sx+ty}\,ds\,dt\right)(x)(y).$$

This completes the proof. $\blacksquare$
