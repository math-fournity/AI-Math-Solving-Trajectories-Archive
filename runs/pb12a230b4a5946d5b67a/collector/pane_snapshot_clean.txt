## Proof

Let $P = \sum_{x_i > 0} x_i \ge 0$ and $Q = -\sum_{x_i < 0} x_i \ge 0$. Then
$$\sum_{i=1}^n |x_i| = P + Q, \qquad \left|\sum_{i=1}^n x_i\right| = |P - Q|.$$

The given condition becomes
$$P + Q = 19 + |P - Q|.$$

Using $P + Q - |P - Q| = 2\min(P,Q)$, we obtain
$$2\min(P,Q) = 19 \implies \min(P,Q) = \tfrac{19}{2} = 9.5.$$

Hence both $P \ge 9.5$ and $Q \ge 9.5$; in particular both positive and negative
 terms must exist.

**Lower bound.** Every positive term satisfies $x_i < 1$, so if there are $k$ po
sitive terms, their sum satisfies $P < k$. Since $P \ge 9.5$, we need $k > 9.5$,
 i.e. $k \ge 10$. At least one negative term is also required (to make $Q \ge 9.
5$). Therefore
$$n \ge k + 1 \ge 10 + 1 = 11.$$

**Construction achieving $n = 11$.** Take
$$x_1 = x_2 = \cdots = x_{10} = 0.95, \qquad x_{11} = -9.5.$$
Each $x_i < 1$. Then $P = 10 \times 0.95 = 9.5$, $Q = 9.5$, so
$$\sum |x_i| = 9.5 + 9.5 = 19, \qquad \sum x_i = 9.5 - 9.5 = 0, \qquad 19 = 19 +
 0. \checkmark$$

Thus the minimum value of $n$ is $\boxed{11}$.
