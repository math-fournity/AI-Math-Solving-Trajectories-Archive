### The Invariant

Consider the symmetric group $S_4$. Assign to each stone a group element:
$$E = (1\,2\,3\,4), \qquad S = (1\,2), \qquad R = S \cdot E^{-1} = (1\,2)(1\,4\,
3\,2) = (1\,4\,3).$$

For a necklace with stones $w_1, w_2, \ldots, w_n$ (read left to right), define:
$$\Phi(w_1 w_2 \cdots w_n) = g(w_1)\, g(w_2) \cdots g(w_n) \in S_4,$$
where $g(\text{ruby}) = R$, $g(\text{emerald}) = E$, $g(\text{sapphire}) = S$.

### The Invariant Is Preserved by Every Operation

Each operation replaces a consecutive block with another block of equal group-pr
oduct. We verify all four relations:

**(1°) $RR \to ES$:** Need $R^2 = ES$.
$$R^2 = (1\,4\,3)^2 = (1\,3\,4), \qquad ES = (1\,2\,3\,4)(1\,2) = (1\,3\,4). \qu

**(2°) $EEE \to SR$:** Need $E^3 = SR$.
$$E^3 = (1\,2\,3\,4)^3 = (1\,4\,3\,2), \qquad SR = (1\,2)(1\,4\,3) = (1\,4\,3\,2

**(3°) $SS \to \emptyset$:** Need $S^2 = 1$.
$$S^2 = (1\,2)^2 = 1. \quad \checkmark$$

**(4°) $RES \to \emptyset$:** Need $RES = 1$. Computing step by step:
$$(1\,2\,3\,4)(1\,2) = (1\,3\,4), \quad \text{then} \quad (1\,4\,3)(1\,3\,4) = 1
