## Proof

A Moore graph of diameter 2 is a $k$-regular graph on $n = k^2+1$ vertices with
diameter 2 and girth 5. It is strongly regular with parameters $(n, k, \lambda,
\mu) = (k^2+1, k, 0, 1)$.

**Eigenvalues.** For a strongly regular graph with parameters $(n,k,\lambda,\mu)
$, the two non-principal eigenvalues are roots of
$$x^2 - (\lambda - \mu)x - (k-\mu) = 0.$$
Substituting $\lambda=0,\mu=1$:
$$x^2 + x - (k-1) = 0 \implies x = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{4k-3}}{2}.$$
Set $D = 4k-3$ and $s = \sqrt{D}$.

**Multiplicities.** The multiplicity of the eigenvalue $r_1 = \frac{-1+s}{2}$ is
$$f = \frac{1}{2}\!\left[(n-1) - \frac{2k+(n-1)(\lambda-\mu)}{s}\right].$$
With $n-1 = k^2$ and $\lambda-\mu = -1$:
$$2k + (n-1)(\lambda-\mu) = 2k - k^2 = -k(k-2),$$
so
$$f = \frac{1}{2}\!\left[k^2 + \frac{k(k-2)}{s}\right].$$
For $f$ (and the companion multiplicity $g = n-1-f$) to be a non-negative intege
r, we need $\frac{k(k-2)}{s}$ to be an integer (so that $f$ is rational; then pa
rity and non-negativity are checked separately).

**Case 1: $k(k-2)=0$, i.e., $k=0$ or $k=2$.**
- $k=0$: $n=1$, a single vertex with diameter $0 \neq 2$. **Rejected.**
- $k=2$: $n=5$, the 5-cycle $C_5$, which has diameter 2 and girth 5. Here $f = \
frac{1}{2}[4+0]=2$, $g=2$. **Valid.** ✓

**Case 2: $k(k-2)\neq 0$.** Then $s=\sqrt{4k-3}$ must be a positive integer (oth
erwise $\frac{k(k-2)}{s}$ is irrational). Write $s = 2t+1$ (odd, since $4k-3 \eq
uiv 1\pmod 4$), giving
$$k = \frac{s^2+3}{4} = t^2+t+1, \qquad k-2 = t^2+t-1.$$
The integrality condition $(2t+1)\mid k(k-2)$ becomes
$$(2t+1)\mid (t^2+t+1)(t^2+t-1) = (t^2+t)^2 - 1 = t^4+2t^3+t^2-1.$$
Evaluating the polynomial remainder at $t=-\tfrac{1}{2}$:
$$\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^4 + 2\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^3 + \left(-\tfrac{1
}{2}\right)^2 - 1 = \tfrac{1}{16}-\tfrac{1}{4}+\tfrac{1}{4}-1 = -\tfrac{15}{16}.
$$
