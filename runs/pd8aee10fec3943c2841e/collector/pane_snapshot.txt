## Derivation of Menger's Theorem (Theorem 6.1) from Lemma A.1

**Theorem 6.1 (Menger's Theorem — vertex version, S–T form).** Let $G$ be a grap
h and let $S, T$ be two disjoint subsets of $V(G)$. Then the maximum number of v
ertex-disjoint $S$–$T$ paths in $G$ equals the minimum number of vertices whose
deletion separates $S$ from $T$ in $G$.

(Here an *$S$–$T$ path* is a path whose first vertex lies in $S$, whose last ver
tex lies in $T$, and whose internal vertices lie in $V(G)\setminus(S\cup T)$; a
set $W\subseteq V(G)\setminus(S\cup T)$ *separates $S$ from $T$* if every $S$–$T
$ path meets $W$.)

**Proof.**

Let $p$ denote the maximum number of vertex-disjoint $S$–$T$ paths and $q$ the m
inimum size of a vertex set separating $S$ from $T$.

**Easy inequality $p\le q$.** Any set $W$ separating $S$ from $T$ must meet ever
y $S$–$T$ path. Since the $p$ witness paths are vertex-disjoint, $W$ must contai
n at least one distinct vertex from each of them, so $|W|\ge p$. Taking the mini
mum over all such $W$ gives $q\ge p$.

**Reverse inequality $p\ge q$ via Lemma A.1.** We reduce to the two-terminal set
ting of the lemma by an auxiliary construction.

*Construction of $G^{+}$.* Form $G^{+}$ from $G$ by adding two new vertices $x$
and $y$, joining $x$ to every vertex of $S$ and $y$ to every vertex of $T$, and
adding no other edges. Because $S$ and $T$ are disjoint and $x,y$ are fresh vert
ices, $x$ and $y$ are **non-adjacent** in $G^{+}$. Hence Lemma A.1 applies to th
e pair $(x,y)$ in $G^{+}$.

In Lemma A.1 the "vertices whose deletion destroys all $(x,y)$-paths" are unders
tood to be vertices *other than $x$ and $y$ themselves* (otherwise the statement
 would be trivialized by deleting $x$). Thus Lemma A.1 gives:

$$\max\{\text{internally-disjoint }(x,y)\text{-paths in }G^{+}\}\;=\;\min\{\text
{size of an }(x,y)\text{-separator in }G^{+}\},$$

where an $(x,y)$-separator is a subset of $V(G^{+})\setminus\{x,y\}=V(G)$ meetin
g every $(x,y)$-path.

**Claim 1.** *The maximum number of internally-disjoint $(x,y)$-paths in $G^{+}$
 equals $p$.*

Every $(x,y)$-path in $G^{+}$ has the form $x\,s\,\cdots\,t\,y$ with $s\in S$, $
t\in T$, and the middle segment $s\cdots t$ an $S$–$T$ path in $G$. Two internal
ly-disjoint $(x,y)$-paths share no vertex except $x$ and $y$; in particular thei
r internal vertices — all of which lie in $V(G)$ — are distinct, so the correspo
nding $S$–$T$ paths in $G$ are vertex-disjoint. Conversely, any family of vertex
-disjoint $S$–$T$ paths in $G$ yields, by prepending $x$ and appending $y$, a fa
mily of internally-disjoint $(x,y)$-paths in $G^{+}$. The two maxima therefore c
oincide. $\square$

**Claim 2.** *The minimum size of an $(x,y)$-separator in $G^{+}$ equals $q$.*

- ($\le$) If $W\subseteq V(G)$ separates $S$ from $T$ in $G$, then $W$ meets the
 middle segment of every $(x,y)$-path $x\,s\cdots t\,y$, so $W$ is an $(x,y)$-se
parator in $G^{+}$. Hence $\min(G^{+})\le q$.
- ($\ge$) Let $U$ be a minimum $(x,y)$-separator in $G^{+}$. By the convention a
bove, $U\subseteq V(G^{+})\setminus\{x,y\}=V(G)$. Since $U$ meets every $(x,y)$-
path, it meets every middle $S$–$T$ segment, so $U$ separates $S$ from $T$ in $G
$. Thus $q\le|U|=\min(G^{+})$.

Combining both directions, $\min(G^{+})=q$. $\square$

**Conclusion.** Applying Lemma A.1 to $x,y$ in $G^{+}$ and substituting Claims 1
 and 2:

$$p \;=\; \max\{\text{internally-disjoint }(x,y)\text{-paths}\} \;=\; \min\{(x,y
)\text{-separator}\} \;=\; q.$$

Hence $p=q$, which is Menger's Theorem. $\blacksquare$

**Corollary (k-connectivity characterization).** A graph $G$ is $k$-connected ($
\kappa(G)\ge k$) if and only if every two distinct vertices of $G$ are joined by
 at least $k$ internally-disjoint paths.

*Sketch.* $\kappa(G)\ge k$ means deleting any $<k$ vertices leaves $G$ connected
, i.e. for every non-adjacent pair $x,y$ no set of $<k$ vertices separates $x$ f
rom $y$. By Lemma A.1 this is equivalent to there being $\ge k$ internally-disjo
int $(x,y)$-paths for every non-adjacent pair; adjacent pairs are handled by not
ing $k\ge 2$ gives at least one edge plus $k-1$ further paths after a standard r
eduction, and $k=1$ is just connectivity. $\square$
