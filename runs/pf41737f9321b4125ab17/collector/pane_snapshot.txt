## Proof

**Setup.** Let $\Delta^1_\bullet = \Delta(-,[1])$ be the representable simplicia
l set on $[1]=\{0,1\}$, and $\partial\Delta^1_\bullet$ its boundary (the simplic
ial subset generated by the two vertices). The quotient $S^1_\bullet = \Delta^1_
\bullet/\partial\Delta^1_\bullet$ collapses the boundary to a single basepoint.

**Simplices of $\Delta^1$.** The $n$-simplices of $\Delta^1$ are order-preservin
g maps $\alpha:[n]\to[1]$. Such a map is determined by $\alpha^{-1}(0)=\{0,1,\do
ts,k\}$ for some $k\in\{-1,0,1,\dots,n\}$, where $k=-1$ gives the constant map t
o $1$ and $k=n$ gives the constant map to $0$. Denote these maps $\alpha_k$, so
$|\Delta^1_n|=n+2$.

**Degeneracy in $\Delta^1$.** An $n$-simplex $\alpha_k$ is degenerate (lies in t
he image of some degeneracy $s_j$) iff it factors through $\sigma^j:[n]\to[n-1]$
 for some $j$, which happens iff $\alpha_k(j)=\alpha_k(j+1)$, i.e., iff $j\neq k
$ (the switch doesn't occur between $j$ and $j+1$). Thus $\alpha_k$ is **nondege
nerate** iff $\alpha_k(j)\neq\alpha_k(j+1)$ for every $j\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}$. Sin
ce $\alpha_k$ switches value only at $j=k$, this requires $\{0,\dots,n-1\}=\{k\}
$, forcing $n=1$ and $k=0$.

Therefore the nondegenerate simplices of $\Delta^1$ are exactly:
- Two nondegenerate $0$-simplices: $\alpha_0$ (vertex $0$) and $\alpha_{-1}$ (ve
rtex $1$).
- One nondegenerate $1$-simplex: $\alpha_0=\mathrm{id}_{[1]}$ (sending $0\mapsto
0,\;1\mapsto1$).
- None in dimensions $\geq 2$.

**The boundary $\partial\Delta^1$.** This is the simplicial subset generated by
the two vertices $\alpha_0$ and $\alpha_{-1}$ (in dimension $0$) and all their d
egeneracies. In dimension $n$, $\partial\Delta^1_n=\{\alpha_{-1},\alpha_n\}$ (th
e two constant maps).

**The quotient $S^1_\bullet$.** Collapsing $\partial\Delta^1$ to a point identif
ies $\alpha_{-1}$ and $\alpha_n$ (and all their degeneracies) into a single base
point $*$. The $n$-simplices of $S^1$ are:
$$S^1_n = \{*\} \cup \{\bar\alpha_k : 0\leq k\leq n-1\},$$
where $\bar\alpha_k$ is the image of $\alpha_k$ (the non-constant maps), giving
$|S^1_n|=n+1$.

**Nondegenerate simplices of $S^1$.**

*Dimension 0:* $S^1_0=\{*\}$, a single $0$-simplex. It is nondegenerate (no dege
neracies exist in dimension $0$).

*Dimension 1:* $S^1_1=\{*,\,\bar\alpha_0\}$, where $\bar\alpha_0$ is the image o
f $\mathrm{id}_{[1]}$. The only degenerate $1$-simplex is $s_0(*)$, which corres
ponds to the constant map. Since $\bar\alpha_0$ is the image of the nondegenerat
e $1$-simplex $\mathrm{id}_{[1]}$ and is not the constant map (it is not $s_0(*)
$), $\bar\alpha_0$ is **nondegenerate**. Its faces are $d_0(\bar\alpha_0)=d_1(\b
ar\alpha_0)=*$ (both vertices are identified to the basepoint).

*Dimensions $\geq 2$:* Every $n$-simplex of $\Delta^1$ with $n\geq 2$ is degener
ate. The quotient map $q:\Delta^1\to S^1$ preserves degeneracy maps, so the imag
e of any degenerate simplex is degenerate. Hence $S^1$ has **no nondegenerate si
mplices** in dimensions $\geq 2$.

**Conclusion.** The simplicial set $S^1_\bullet=\Delta^1_\bullet/\partial\Delta^
1_\bullet$ has exactly **two nondegenerate simplices**:

1. **One nondegenerate $0$-simplex**: the basepoint $*$ (the image of the collap
sed boundary $\partial\Delta^1$).
2. **One nondegenerate $1$-simplex**: the image of the identity map $\mathrm{id}
_{[1]}:[1]\to[1]$, whose two faces are both the basepoint.

This is the standard minimal simplicial model of the circle: a single vertex wit
h a single nondegenerate edge forming a loop. Its geometric realization is $S^1$
, and its homology is $H_0(S^1)\cong\mathbb{Z}$, $H_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb{Z}$, conf
irming the result.
