## Proof

Let \(g(x) = x^3 - 2 \in \mathbb{Q}[x]\).

**Irreducibility.** By Eisenstein's criterion at the prime \(p = 2\) (since \(2
\mid 2\), \(2 \mid 0\), \(4 \nmid 2\)), the polynomial \(g(x) = x^3 - 2\) is irr
educible over \(\mathbb{Q}\).

**The extension.** Since \(g\) is irreducible, the quotient \(\mathbb{Q}[x]/(g)\
) is a field, and \(\mathbb{Q}[x]/(x^3-2) \cong \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})\), a deg
ree-3 extension of \(\mathbb{Q}\).

**The extension is not Galois.** A finite extension of \(\mathbb{Q}\) is Galois
if and only if it is both separable and normal. All extensions of \(\mathbb{Q}\)
 are separable (character 0), so it suffices to check normality.

The roots of \(x^3 - 2\) in \(\mathbb{C}\) are
\[
\sqrt[3]{2},\quad \omega\sqrt[3]{2},\quad \omega^2\sqrt[3]{2},
\]
where \(\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}\) is a primitive cube root of unity. The field \(\
mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})\) is a subfield of \(\mathbb{R}\), so it cannot contain t
he non-real roots \(\omega\sqrt[3]{2}\) and \(\omega^2\sqrt[3]{2}\). Therefore \
(x^3 - 2\) does not split completely over \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})\), meaning t
he extension is **not normal**, hence **not Galois**.

(Equivalently, a degree-3 Galois extension would have Galois group of order 3, b
ut \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})\) has only the trivial automorphism over \(\mathbb{
Q}\) — any \(\mathbb{Q}\)-automorphism must send \(\sqrt[3]{2}\) to a root of \(
x^3-2\) lying in the field, and the only such root is \(\sqrt[3]{2}\) itself — s
o \(|\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})/\mathbb{Q})| = 1 \neq 3 = [\math
bb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}):\mathbb{Q}]\), confirming the extension is not Galois.)

Thus \(g(x) = x^3 - 2\) is an irreducible polynomial over \(\mathbb{Q}\) for whi
ch \((\mathbb{Q}[x]/(g))/\mathbb{Q}\) is not Galois.
