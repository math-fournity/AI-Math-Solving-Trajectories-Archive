Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $\tilde{c}(w_1, w_2)$ is al
ways strictly less than 1 for all $w_1 \neq w_2$ in $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$.

**Answer: No.** $\tilde{c}(w_1, w_2)$ is **not** always strictly less than 1. In
 fact, $\tilde{c}(w_1, w_2) = 1$ whenever at least one of $w_1, w_2$ lies on $\p
artial\mathbb{D}$.

---

## Proof

**Setup and key facts.** For $a, b \in \mathbb{D}$, the pseudo-hyperbolic distan
ce $\rho(a,b) = \left|\frac{a-b}{1-\bar{b}a}\right|$ satisfies $\rho(a,b) < 1$,
since $|a-b|^2 < |1-\bar{b}a|^2$ is equivalent to $(1-|a|^2)(1-|b|^2) > 0$. Sinc
e every $f \in \tilde{A}(\mathbb{D})$ maps $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$ into $\mathbb
{D}$, each individual value in the supremum is $<1$, giving $\tilde{c}(w_1,w_2)
\leq 1$ always.

### Case 1: Both $w_1, w_2 \in \mathbb{D}$ — here $\tilde{c} < 1$.

By the Schwarz–Pick lemma, for any holomorphic $f: \mathbb{D} \to \mathbb{D}$:
$$\rho(f(w_1), f(w_2)) \leq \rho(w_1, w_2).$$
Since $w_1, w_2 \in \mathbb{D}$ and $w_1 \neq w_2$, we have $\rho(w_1, w_2) < 1$
, so $\tilde{c}(w_1, w_2) \leq \rho(w_1, w_2) < 1$.

(Equality is approached: for any automorphism $\phi$ of $\mathbb{D}$ and $r \in
(0,1)$, the function $f_r = r\phi$ lies in $\tilde{A}(\mathbb{D})$, and $\rho(f_
r(w_1), f_r(w_2)) \to \rho(\phi(w_1), \phi(w_2)) = \rho(w_1, w_2)$ as $r \to 1^-
$. So $\tilde{c}(w_1, w_2) = \rho(w_1, w_2)$.)

### Case 2: At least one of $w_1, w_2$ lies on $\partial\mathbb{D}$ — here $\til
de{c} = 1$.

**Explicit counterexample.** Take $w_1 = 0 \in \mathbb{D}$ and $w_2 = 1 \in \par
tial\mathbb{D}$.

For each $r \in (0,1)$, define $f_r(z) = rz$. This is a polynomial (hence in the
 disc algebra), and $|f_r(z)| = r|z| \leq r < 1$ on $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$, so
$f_r \in \tilde{A}(\mathbb{D})$.

Computing the pseudo-hyperbolic distance of the images:
$$\left|\frac{f_r(0) - f_r(1)}{1 - \overline{f_r(1)}\, f_r(0)}\right| = \left|\f
rac{0 - r}{1 - r \cdot 0}\right| = r \xrightarrow{r \to 1^-} 1.$$

Therefore $\tilde{c}(0, 1) \geq 1$. Combined with the universal upper bound $\ti
lde{c} \leq 1$, we get $\tilde{c}(0, 1) = 1$.

**General argument for any $w_1, w_2 \in \overline{\mathbb{D}}$ with at least on
e on $\partial\mathbb{D}$.** WLOG say $|w_2| = 1$. Take $f_r(z) = rz$ for $r \in
 (0,1)$, so $f_r \in \tilde{A}(\mathbb{D})$. Then $f_r(w_1) = rw_1$, $f_r(w_2) =
 rw_2$, and:
$$\rho(rw_1, rw_2) = \frac{r|w_1 - w_2|}{|1 - r^2 \overline{w_2}\, w_1|}.$$

As $r \to 1^-$, the numerator approaches $|w_1 - w_2|$ and the denominator appro
aches $|1 - \overline{w_2}\, w_1|$. Since $|w_2| = 1$:
$$1 - \overline{w_2}\, w_1 = \overline{w_2}(w_2 - w_1), \quad \text{so} \quad |1
 - \overline{w_2}\, w_1| = |w_2 - w_1| = |w_1 - w_2|.$$

Thus $\rho(rw_1, rw_2) \to \frac{|w_1 - w_2|}{|w_1 - w_2|} = 1$ (noting $w_1 \ne
q w_2$ so the denominator is nonzero).

Hence $\tilde{c}(w_1, w_2) \geq 1$, and combined with $\tilde{c}(w_1, w_2) \leq
1$, we conclude $\tilde{c}(w_1, w_2) = 1$.

### Conclusion

| Configuration of $w_1, w_2$ | $\tilde{c}(w_1, w_2)$ |
|---|---|
| Both in $\mathbb{D}$ | $= \rho(w_1, w_2) < 1$ |
| At least one on $\partial\mathbb{D}$ | $= 1$ |

The answer to the question is **No**: $\tilde{c}(w_1, w_2)$ is not always strict
ly less than 1. It equals 1 whenever at least one of the two points lies on the
boundary of the disc.
