## Solution by the Bubnov-Galerkin Method

The given integral equation is a Fredholm equation of the second kind:

$$\varphi(x) = x + \int_{-1}^{1} x\,t\,\varphi(t)\,dt$$

with kernel $K(x,t)=xt$ and free term $f(x)=x$.

### Step 1. Choose the coordinate system

Since the kernel $K(x,t)=xt$ is degenerate (separable) and both the free term $f
(x)=x$ and the kernel depend linearly on $x$, the natural one-dimensional subspa
ce is spanned by

$$u_1(x)=x.$$

We seek an approximate solution in the form

$$\varphi(x) = x + c_1\,u_1(x) = x + c_1 x = (1+c_1)\,x.$$

### Step 2. Apply the Galerkin orthogonality condition

The Bubnov-Galerkin method requires the residual

$$R(x)=\varphi(x)-x-\int_{-1}^{1}xt\,\varphi(t)\,dt$$

to be orthogonal to the chosen basis function $u_1(x)=x$ on $[-1,1]$:

$$\int_{-1}^{1} R(x)\,u_1(x)\,dx = 0.$$

Substituting $\varphi(x)=(1+c_1)x$:

$$R(x)=(1+c_1)x - x - \int_{-1}^{1}xt\,(1+c_1)t\,dt = c_1 x - x(1+c_1)\int_{-1}^
{1}t^2\,dt.$$

Since $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}t^2\,dt = \frac{2}{3}$,

$$R(x)=c_1 x - \tfrac{2}{3}(1+c_1)x = \Big[c_1-\tfrac{2}{3}(1+c_1)\Big]x.$$

### Step 3. Enforce orthogonality

$$\int_{-1}^{1}\Big[c_1-\tfrac{2}{3}(1+c_1)\Big]x\cdot x\,dx = 0$$

$$\Big[c_1-\tfrac{2}{3}(1+c_1)\Big]\int_{-1}^{1}x^2\,dx = 0.$$

Since $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}x^2\,dx=\frac{2}{3}\neq 0$, the bracket must va
nish:

$$c_1-\tfrac{2}{3}(1+c_1)=0$$

$$3c_1 = 2(1+c_1)$$

$$3c_1 = 2+2c_1$$

$$\boxed{c_1 = 2}.$$

### Step 4. Write the solution

$$\varphi(x) = (1+c_1)x = (1+2)x = 3x.$$

### Step 5. Verification

Substitute $\varphi(x)=3x$ back into the original equation:

$$\text{RHS}=x+\int_{-1}^{1}xt\cdot 3t\,dt = x+3x\int_{-1}^{1}t^2\,dt = x+3x\cdo
t\tfrac{2}{3}=x+2x=3x=\varphi(x).\ \checkmark$$

### Conclusion

Because the kernel $K(x,t)=xt$ is degenerate of rank one, the single basis funct
ion $u_1(x)=x$ already spans the entire range of the kernel operator. Therefore
the Bubnov-Galerkin approximation with this basis is **exact**, not merely appro
ximate. The unique solution of the integral equation is

$$\boxed{\varphi(x)=3x.}$$
