## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. If $A$ and $B$ are conformally equivalent domains in $\mathbb{C}
$ and $A$ is simply connected, then $B$ is also simply connected.

**Key observation.** A conformal equivalence between $A$ and $B$ is, by definiti
on, a bijection $f: A \to B$ that is holomorphic with holomorphic inverse $f^{-1
}: B \to A$. In particular, both $f$ and $f^{-1}$ are continuous (holomorphic fu
nctions are continuous, and biholomorphic maps have continuous inverses). Hence
$f$ is a **homeomorphism** between $A$ and $B$ (with the subspace topology inher
ited from $\mathbb{C} \cong \mathbb{R}^2$).

**Simple connectedness is a topological invariant.** Recall that a domain $\Omeg
a \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ is simply connected if and only if every closed curve $\
gamma: [0,1] \to \Omega$ is null-homotopic in $\Omega$, i.e., the fundamental gr
oup $\pi_1(\Omega)$ is trivial. The fundamental group is a homotopy invariant, a
nd in particular a homeomorphism invariant: if $h: X \to Y$ is a homeomorphism,
then the induced map
$$h_*: \pi_1(X, x_0) \to \pi_1(Y, h(x_0))$$
is a group isomorphism for every basepoint $x_0 \in X$.

**Applying this.** Since $f: A \to B$ is a homeomorphism, the induced homomorphi
sm
$$f_*: \pi_1(A, a_0) \to \pi_1(B, f(a_0))$$
is an isomorphism for any $a_0 \in A$. By hypothesis $A$ is simply connected, so
 $\pi_1(A, a_0)$ is the trivial group. Therefore $\pi_1(B, f(a_0))$ is also triv
ial, which means $B$ is simply connected.

**Conclusion.** Conformal equivalence implies homeomorphism, and simple connecte
dness is preserved under homeomorphism. Hence $B$ must be simply connected. $\bl
acksquare$
