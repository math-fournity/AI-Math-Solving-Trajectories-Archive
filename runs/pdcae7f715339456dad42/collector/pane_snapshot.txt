## Proof

Let $A=(a_{ij})$ be a $3\times 3$ matrix such that $AX=XA$ for every upper trian
gular $3\times 3$ matrix $X$.

**Step 1: $A$ is diagonal.**

For each $i\in\{1,2,3\}$, the matrix $E_{ii}$ (the matrix with $1$ in position $
(i,i)$ and $0$ elsewhere) is upper triangular, so $AE_{ii}=E_{ii}A$.

The product $AE_{ii}$ has only its $i$-th column nonzero, and that column equals
 the $i$-th column of $A$; explicitly $(AE_{ii})_{ji}=a_{ji}$ and all other entr
ies are $0$. The product $E_{ii}A$ has only its $i$-th row nonzero, and that row
 equals the $i$-th row of $A$; explicitly $(E_{ii}A)_{ij}=a_{ij}$ and all other
entries are $0$.

Equating entries:
- For $j\neq i$, position $(j,i)$: $a_{ji}=0$ (since $(E_{ii}A)_{ji}=0$). This k
ills the off-diagonal entries of column $i$.
- For $k\neq i$, position $(i,k)$: $0=a_{ik}$ (since $(AE_{ii})_{ik}=0$). This k
ills the off-diagonal entries of row $i$.

Applying this for $i=1,2,3$ forces every off-diagonal entry of $A$ to be $0$. He
nce $A=\operatorname{diag}(a,b,c)$ for some scalars $a,b,c$.

**Step 2: $a=b=c$.**

The matrix $E_{12}$ (with $1$ in position $(1,2)$) is upper triangular, so $AE_{
12}=E_{12}A$. Since $A$ is diagonal,
$$(AE_{12})_{12}=a\cdot 1=a, \qquad (E_{12}A)_{12}=1\cdot b=b,$$
and all other entries are $0$ on both sides. Equality gives $a=b$.

Similarly, $E_{23}$ is upper triangular and gives $b=c$, and $E_{13}$ gives $a=c
$. Thus $a=b=c$; call this common value $\lambda$. So $A=\lambda I$.

**Step 3: Converse.**

For any scalar $\lambda$, the matrix $\lambda I$ commutes with every matrix, hen
ce with every upper triangular matrix.

**Conclusion.** The matrices $A$ satisfying $AX=XA$ for all $3\times 3$ upper tr
iangular $X$ are exactly the scalar multiples of the identity:

$$\boxed{A=\lambda I_3,\quad \lambda\in\mathbb{R}\ (\text{or the base field}).}$
$
