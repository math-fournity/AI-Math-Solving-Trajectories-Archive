The Bateman-Horn Conjecture is a famous open problem in analytic number theory,
generalizing the Hardy-Littlewood prime tuple conjecture and the Bunyakovsky con
jecture. It is marked `@[category research open]` in the formalization, indicati
ng it is an acknowledged open research problem.

The conjecture asserts that for a finite collection of distinct irreducible non-
constant polynomials $f_1, \dots, f_k \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ with positive leading c
oefficients satisfying the Schinzel condition, the count of $n \leq x$ for which
 all $f_i(n)$ are simultaneously prime is asymptotic to

$$C(f_1,\dots,f_k) \cdot \frac{x}{(\log x)^k},$$

where $C$ is the Bateman-Horn constant given by the convergent Euler product

$$C = \frac{1}{D}\prod_{p \in \mathbb{P}} \left(1 - \frac{1}{p}\right)^{-k}\left
(1 - \frac{\omega_p}{p}\right), \qquad D = \prod_{i=1}^k \deg f_i.$$

**Why this cannot be proved here:**

1. **It is genuinely open.** No proof of the Bateman-Horn conjecture is known, e
ven for the single non-linear case $k=1$ (e.g., $f(x) = x^2+1$). The case $k=1$
with linear polynomials recovers Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic pro
gressions (proved), and the general linear case with $k \geq 2$ recovers the Har
dy-Littlewood prime tuple conjecture (still open except for bounded gaps results
 following Zhang–Maynard–Tao). The general polynomial case is far beyond current
 techniques.

2. **The key obstruction.** The conjecture requires showing that the prime value
s of polynomials are distributed according to the singular series $C$. This dema
nds understanding of primes in "thin" sequences $\{f(n)\}$, which is well beyond
 the reach of the circle method, sieve methods, or any existing analytic techniq
ue for general irreducible polynomials of degree $\geq 2$. Even proving infinite
ly many primes of the form $n^2+1$ is open.

3. **The Schinzel condition** (that for every prime $p$ there exists $n$ with $p
 \nmid \prod f_i(n)$) guarantees local compatibility and positivity of the Euler
 product, but it is purely a local obstruction-removal condition. It does not pr
ovide any global distribution information about primes, which is what the conjec
ture ultimately asserts.

4. **No partial result suffices.** The statement requires the full asymptotic $\
sim[atTop]$, i.e., the ratio of the two sides tending to $1$ as $x \to \infty$.
This is strictly stronger than what any known method produces. Sieve methods (Se
lberg, large sieve) give upper bounds of the correct order of magnitude, and the
 parity phenomenon blocks lower bounds of the expected size. No technique curren
tly overcomes this barrier for general polynomial sequences.

Since this is an open research conjecture with no known proof, and the formaliza
tion itself classifies it as `research open`, I cannot provide a proof.
