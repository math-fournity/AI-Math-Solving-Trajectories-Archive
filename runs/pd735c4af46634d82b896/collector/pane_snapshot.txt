## Proof

Let $f(x)=x^2+ax+b$. Since $A\neq\emptyset$, the discriminant satisfies $\Delta=
a^2-4b\ge 0$. Let the roots of $f(x)=0$ be $\alpha\le\beta$, so $A=[\alpha,\beta
]$, and on $A$ we have $f(x)\in\big[f_{\min},\,0\big]$ where $f_{\min}=b-\tfrac{
a^2}{4}\le 0$; outside $A$, $f(x)>0$.

**Reformulating $B$.** Setting $y=f(x)$, the condition $f(f(x))\le 3$ becomes $y
^2+ay+b-3\le 0$, i.e. $f(y)\le 3$. Its discriminant is $a^2-4(b-3)=a^2-4b+12\ge
12>0$, so it has two real roots
$$y_1\le y_2,\qquad y_1,y_2=\frac{-a\pm\sqrt{a^2-4b+12}}{2},$$
and $B=\{x\mid y_1\le f(x)\le y_2\}$.

**Determining $y_2$.** Since $f$ is continuous with $f(\alpha)=f(\beta)=0$ and $
f(x)\to 0^+$ as $x\to\alpha^-$ or $x\to\beta^+$:
- If $y_2>0$, then for $x$ slightly outside $[\alpha,\beta]$, $0<f(x)<y_2$ and $
f(x)\ge y_1$ (as $y_1\le 0$), so such $x\in B\setminus A$; contradiction.
- If $y_2<0$, then the boundary points $\alpha,\beta$ (where $f=0>y_2$) lie in $
A\setminus B$; contradiction.

Hence **$y_2=0$**. Since $y_2$ is a root of $y^2+ay+b-3=0$, this gives $b=3$.

With $b=3$, the equation $y^2+ay=0$ has roots $y=0$ and $y=-a$. For $y_2=0$ we n
eed $-a\le 0$, i.e. $a\ge 0$. Combined with $A\neq\emptyset$ ($a^2-12\ge 0$), we
 get $a\ge 2\sqrt 3$.

**Ensuring $A\subseteq B$.** Now $y_1=-a$, $y_2=0$, so $B=\{x\mid -a\le f(x)\le
0\}$. On $A$, $f(x)\in\big[3-\tfrac{a^2}{4},\,0\big]$, and we need this entire i
nterval to lie inside $[-a,0]$, i.e.
$$-a\le 3-\frac{a^2}{4}\iff a^2-4a-12\le 0\iff (a-6)(a+2)\le 0\iff -2\le a\le 6.
$$

**Ensuring $B\subseteq A$.** Outside $A$, $f(x)>0=y_2$, so $f(x)\notin[-a,0]$; h
ence no point outside $A$ belongs to $B$. ✓

**Combining** $a\ge 2\sqrt 3$ with $a\le 6$:
$$\boxed{a\in\big[2\sqrt 3,\;6\big]}.$$

**Endpoint check.** At $a=2\sqrt 3$: $f(x)=(x+\sqrt3)^2$, $A=\{-\sqrt3\}$, $B=\{
x\mid -2\sqrt3\le f(x)\le 0\}=\{-\sqrt3\}=A$. ✓
At $a=6$: $f(x)=x^2+6x+3$, $f_{\min}=-6=-a$, $B=\{x\mid -6\le f(x)\le 0\}=A$. ✓
