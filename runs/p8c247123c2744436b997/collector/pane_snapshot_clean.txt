Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $\subseteq_{ec}$ (the conti
guous/consecutive embedding relation) is a well-quasi-ordering on finite sequenc
es of finite trees.

**Answer: No, $\subseteq_{ec}$ is not a well-quasi-ordering on finite sequences
of finite trees.**

## Proof

**Interpretation.** We interpret $\subseteq_{ec}$ as the contiguous embedding re
lation: for sequences $X=(T_1,\dots,T_k)$ and $Y=(U_1,\dots,U_m)$, we write $X\s
ubseteq_{ec} Y$ if $X$ occurs as a **contiguous** (consecutive) subsequence of $
Y$ and each $T_j$ homeomorphically embeds into the corresponding tree in $Y$.

We construct an infinite antichain.

### Step 1: Two incomparable finite trees

Define:
- **$P$**: the rooted path on 4 vertices $v_1 \to v_2 \to v_3 \to v_4$ (root $v_
1$, depth 3, 4 nodes).
- **$Q$**: the complete rooted binary tree of depth 2 (root with two children, e
ach child having two children; 7 nodes, depth 2).

We claim $P$ and $Q$ are **incomparable** under the homeomorphic embedding $\pre
ceq$:

- **$P \not\preceq Q$**: $P$ is a chain of 4 nodes. Any homeomorphic embedding m
aps this chain to a chain of 4 distinct nodes in $Q$ (by injectivity and ancesto
r-preservation). But $Q$ has depth 2, so its longest chain has only 3 nodes. No
chain of 4 exists in $Q$.

- **$Q \not\preceq P$**: $Q$ has 7 nodes and $P$ has 4 nodes. A homeomorphic emb
edding requires an injective map from nodes of $Q$ to nodes of $P$, which is imp
ossible since $7 > 4$.

### Step 2: The antichain

For each $n \geq 1$, define the sequence:
$$S_n = (P,\, \underbrace{Q, Q, \dots, Q}_{n \text{ copies}},\, P)$$
of length $n+2$.

### Step 3: Verification that $\{S_n : n \geq 1\}$ is an antichain

Let $n < m$. We show $S_n \not\subseteq_{ec} S_m$.

Suppose for contradiction that $S_n \subseteq_{ec} S_m$. Then there exists a sta
rting index $i$ (with $1 \leq i \leq m-n+1$) such that $S_n[j] \preceq S_m[i+j-1
]$ for all $j = 1, \dots, n+2$.

In particular:
- The first element gives $P = S_n[1] \preceq S_m[i]$.
- The last element gives $P = S_n[n+2] \preceq S_m[i+n+1]$.

Since $P$ and $Q$ are incomparable, $P \preceq T$ implies $T = P$ (the only tree
s appearing in our sequences are $P$ and $Q$, and $P \not\preceq Q$). Therefore
$S_m[i] = P$ and $S_m[i+n+1] = P$.

Now, in $S_m = (P, Q, \dots, Q, P)$, the tree $P$ appears **only** at positions
$1$ and $m+2$. So we need $i \in \{1, m+2\}$.

- **$i = m+2$**: Requires $i \leq m-n+1$, i.e., $m+2 \leq m-n+1$, giving $n \leq
 -1$. Impossible.

- **$i = 1$**: Then $i + n + 1 = n + 2$. We need $S_m[n+2] = P$. Since $n < m$,
we have $n + 2 \leq m + 1$, so position $n+2$ falls in the range $\{2, \dots, m+
1\}$ where $S_m$ contains $Q$. Thus $S_m[n+2] = Q \neq P$, and $P \not\preceq Q$
. **Contradiction.**

Both cases lead to contradiction, so $S_n \not\subseteq_{ec} S_m$.

For $n > m$: $|S_n| = n+2 > m+2 = |S_m|$, so $S_n$ cannot be a contiguous subseq
uence of the shorter $S_m$. Thus $S_n \not\subseteq_{ec} S_m$.

### Conclusion

The set $\{S_n : n \geq 1\}$ is an infinite set of finite sequences of finite tr
ees in which no two distinct elements are comparable under $\subseteq_{ec}$. Thi
s infinite antichain proves that $\subseteq_{ec}$ is **not** a well-quasi-orderi
ng on finite sequences of finite trees. $\blacksquare$
