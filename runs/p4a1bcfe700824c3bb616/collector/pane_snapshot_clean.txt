## Proof

Let $p$ be a prime. Define
$$
N = 1 + p + p^2 + \cdots + p^{p-1} = \frac{p^{p}-1}{p-1}.
$$
We will show that some prime divisor $q$ of $N$ satisfies the requirement that $
n^{p}\not\equiv p \pmod q$ for all $n\in\mathbb Z$.

**Step 1. Basic properties of $N$.** Since $N\equiv 1\pmod p$, we have $p\nmid N
$, so every prime divisor $q$ of $N$ satisfies $q\neq p$. Also
$$
N = \sum_{k=0}^{p-1}p^{k}\equiv \sum_{k=0}^{p-1}1 = p \pmod{p-1},
$$
so $\gcd(N,p-1)=\gcd(p,p-1)=1$. Hence no prime divisor of $N$ divides $p-1$.

**Step 2. The order of $p$ modulo any prime divisor $q$ of $N$ is exactly $p$.**
 Let $q\mid N$ be prime. Since $N\mid p^{p}-1$ (as $p^{p}-1=(p-1)N$), we have $p
^{p}\equiv 1\pmod q$, so $\operatorname{ord}_{q}(p)\mid p$. Thus the order is $1
$ or $p$. It equals $1$ iff $q\mid p-1$, which is impossible by Step 1. Therefor
e $\operatorname{ord}_{q}(p)=p$, and consequently $p\mid q-1$, i.e. $q\equiv 1\p
mod p$.

**Step 3. A criterion for $p$ to be a $p$-th power mod $q$.** Suppose $n^{p}\equ
iv p\pmod q$ for some $n$. Since $q\neq p$, $n\not\equiv 0\pmod q$ (otherwise $p
\equiv 0\pmod q$, forcing $q=p$). Raising to the $p$-th power again,
$$
n^{p^{2}}\equiv p^{p}\equiv 1\pmod q,
$$
so $\operatorname{ord}_{q}(n)\mid p^{2}$, i.e. the order is $1$, $p$, or $p^{2}$
.

- If $\operatorname{ord}_{q}(n)=1$, then $n\equiv 1$, so $p\equiv n^{p}\equiv 1\
pmod q$, contradicting $\operatorname{ord}_{q}(p)=p$.
- If $\operatorname{ord}_{q}(n)=p$, then $n^{p}\equiv 1$, so $p\equiv 1\pmod q$,
 again a contradiction.
- Therefore $\operatorname{ord}_{q}(n)=p^{2}$, which forces $p^{2}\mid q-1$, i.e
. $q\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}$.

So: **if $q\mid N$ is prime and $q\not\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}$, then $n^{p}\equiv p
\pmod q$ has no solution.**

**Step 4. Existence of such a $q$.** Suppose, for contradiction, that every prim
e divisor $q$ of $N$ satisfies $q\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}$. Writing $N=\prod q_{i}^{
e_{i}}$, each factor $q_{i}^{e_{i}}\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}$, so
$$
N\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}.
$$
But directly
$$
N = 1 + p + p^{2}+\cdots+p^{p-1}\equiv 1+p\pmod{p^{2}},
$$
which is $\not\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}$ since $p\not\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2}}$. This is a
 contradiction.

Hence $N$ has at least one prime divisor $q$ with $q\not\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}$. B
y Step 3, for this prime $q$ there is no integer $n$ with $n^{p}\equiv p\pmod q$
.

Therefore there exists a prime $q$ such that $n^{p}\not\equiv p\pmod q$ for all
$n\in\mathbb Z$. $\blacksquare$
