## Proof

We apply the ratio test to the series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n$ where $a_n = \dfra
c{(2n)!}{n!\,(\alpha n)^n}$.

**Compute the ratio.** For $\alpha > 0$ (the series clearly diverges for $\alpha
 \le 0$ since terms are non-negative and don't vanish),

$$\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = \frac{(2n+2)!}{(n+1)!\,(\alpha(n+1))^{n+1}} \cdot \frac{
n!\,(\alpha n)^n}{(2n)!}.$$

Simplifying factorials: $\dfrac{(2n+2)!}{(2n)!} = (2n+2)(2n+1)$ and $\dfrac{n!}{
(n+1)!} = \dfrac{1}{n+1}$, so

$$\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = \frac{(2n+2)(2n+1)}{n+1} \cdot \frac{(\alpha n)^n}{\alph
a(n+1)^{n+1}} = \frac{2(2n+1)}{\alpha(n+1)}\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^n.$$

**Take the limit.** As $n \to \infty$:
- $\dfrac{2(2n+1)}{n+1} \to 4$,
- $\left(\dfrac{n}{n+1}\right)^n = \left(1 - \dfrac{1}{n+1}\right)^n \to e^{-1}$
.

Hence

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = \frac{4}{e\,\alpha}.$$

**Apply the ratio test.** The series:
- **converges** when $\dfrac{4}{e\alpha} < 1$, i.e. $\alpha > \dfrac{4}{e}$;
- **diverges** when $\dfrac{4}{e\alpha} > 1$, i.e. $\alpha < \dfrac{4}{e}$ (term
s grow geometrically).

**Boundary case $\alpha = \dfrac{4}{e}$.** The ratio test is inconclusive (limit
 equals 1), so we examine $a_n$ directly via Stirling's approximation $n! \sim \
sqrt{2\pi n}\,(n/e)^n$:

$$a_n \sim \frac{\sqrt{4\pi n}\,(2n/e)^{2n}}{\sqrt{2\pi n}\,(n/e)^n \cdot (\alph
a n)^n} = \sqrt{2}\cdot\frac{4^n\,n^{2n}\,e^{-2n}}{n^{2n}\,\alpha^n\,e^{-n}} = \
sqrt{2}\left(\frac{4}{e\alpha}\right)^n.$$

At $\alpha = \dfrac{4}{e}$, this gives $a_n \to \sqrt{2} \neq 0$. Since the gene
ral term does not tend to zero, the series **diverges**.

**Conclusion.** The series converges if and only if $\alpha > \dfrac{4}{e}$. The
refore the infimum of all $\alpha$ for which the series converges is

$$\boxed{\alpha = \dfrac{4}{e}}.$$
