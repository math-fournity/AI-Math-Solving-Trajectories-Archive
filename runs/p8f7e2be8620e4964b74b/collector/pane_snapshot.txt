## Definition of a Colimit

Let \( F : \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{C} \) be a functor, where \(\mathcal{J}\) is
 a (small) index category and \(\mathcal{C}\) is a category.

### Cocone

A **cocone** on \(F\) consists of:
- an object \(C \in \operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal{C})\) (the *apex*), and
- a morphism \(\lambda_j : F(j) \to C\) for every object \(j \in \operatorname{O
b}(\mathcal{J})\),

such that for every morphism \(u : i \to j\) in \(\mathcal{J}\), the following t
riangle commutes:

\[
\lambda_j \circ F(u) \;=\; \lambda_i .
\]

Equivalently, the family \(\{\lambda_j\}_{j \in \mathcal{J}}\) is a **cocone** i
f it forms a natural transformation from \(F\) to the constant functor \(\Delta_
C : \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{C}\) sending every object to \(C\) and every morphi
sm to \(\operatorname{id}_C\).

### Colimit

A **colimit** of \(F\) is a **universal cocone**. Concretely, it is a cocone

\[
\bigl(L,\; \{\iota_j : F(j) \to L\}_{j \in \mathcal{J}}\bigr)
\]

with the following universal property:

> For every cocone \(\bigl(C,\; \{\lambda_j : F(j) \to C\}_{j \in \mathcal{J}}\b
igr)\) on \(F\), there exists a **unique** morphism \(h : L \to C\) in \(\mathca
l{C}\) such that for every \(j \in \mathcal{J}\),
>
> \[
> h \circ \iota_j \;=\; \lambda_j .
> \]

In other words, every cocone on \(F\) factors uniquely through the colimiting co
cone.

### Categorical formulation

Let \(\operatorname{Cocone}(F)\) denote the category whose objects are cocones o
n \(F\) and whose morphisms are morphisms \(h : C \to C'\) in \(\mathcal{C}\) co
mpatible with the legs (i.e., \(h \circ \lambda_j = \lambda'_j\) for all \(j\)).
 Then a colimit of \(F\) is an **initial object** of \(\operatorname{Cocone}(F)\
).

Equivalently, writing \(\Delta : \mathcal{C} \to [\mathcal{J}, \mathcal{C}]\) fo
r the diagonal/constant functor, the colimit of \(F\) is a representation of the
 functor

\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{[\mathcal{J},\mathcal{C}]}(F, \Delta(-)) : \mathcal{C}^{\mat
hrm{op}} \to \mathbf{Set},
\]

i.e., an object \(L \in \mathcal{C}\) together with a natural isomorphism

\[
\mathcal{C}(L, -) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Hom}_{[\mathcal{J},\mathcal{C}]}(F, \D
elta(-)).
\]

This expresses the universal property as a natural bijection: cocones on \(F\) w
ith apex \(C\) correspond bijectively (and naturally in \(C\)) to morphisms \(L
\to C\).

### Uniqueness

Colimits, when they exist, are unique up to unique isomorphism: if \((L, \{\iota
_j\})\) and \((L', \{\iota'_j\})\) are both colimits of \(F\), the universal pro
perty yields unique morphisms \(f : L \to L'\) and \(g : L' \to L\) with \(f \ci
rc \iota_j = \iota'_j\) and \(g \circ \iota'_j = \iota_j\); then \(g \circ f\) a
nd \(\operatorname{id}_L\) both satisfy the defining equation for the endomorphi
sm of \(L\) induced by its own colimiting cocone, so by uniqueness \(g \circ f =
 \operatorname{id}_L\), and symmetrically \(f \circ g = \operatorname{id}_{L'}\)
. Hence \(f\) and \(g\) are inverse isomorphisms.

### Notation

The colimit is commonly denoted

\[
\varinjlim_{\mathcal{J}} F \quad \text{or} \quad \operatorname{colim} F.
\]

Special cases: when \(\mathcal{J}\) is discrete, the colimit is the **coproduct*
* \(\coprod_{j} F(j)\); when \(\mathcal{J}\) is the walking span \(i \leftarrow
k \rightarrow j\), the colimit is the **pushout**; when \(\mathcal{J}\) is the p
arallel-pair category, the colimit is the **coequalizer**; when \(\mathcal{J}\)
is a directed poset, the colimit is the **directed/filtered colimit**.
