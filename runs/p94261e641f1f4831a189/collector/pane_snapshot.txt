## Solution

**The statement is TRUE.** Below is the proof.

---

**Claim.** If $f, g : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$ are entire and $|f(z)| \le |g(z
)|$ for all $z \in \mathbb{C}$, then there exists a constant $C \in \mathbb{C}$
such that $f(z) = Cg(z)$ for all $z$.

**Proof.**

The difficulty (compared to the standard "no-zeros" version) is that $g$ may hav
e zeros, so $f/g$ is not a priori defined everywhere. We first show that the zer
os of $g$ are removable singularities of $f/g$.

**Step 1. Zeros of $g$ are removable for $f/g$.**

Let $z_0 \in \mathbb{C}$ be a zero of $g$ of order $m \ge 1$. Write
$$g(z) = (z - z_0)^m \,\widetilde g(z), \qquad \widetilde g(z_0) \ne 0,$$
with $\widetilde g$ holomorphic near $z_0$. Since $\widetilde g$ is continuous a
nd nonzero at $z_0$, there is a neighborhood $U$ of $z_0$ and a constant $\mu >
0$ with $|\widetilde g(z)| \ge \mu$ on $U$. The hypothesis $|f(z)| \le |g(z)|$ g
ives, on $U$,
$$|f(z)| \le |z - z_0|^m\,|\widetilde g(z)| \le M\,|z - z_0|^m$$
for some $M > 0$ (using that $|\widetilde g|$ is bounded on a small disk).

Suppose $f$ had a zero of order $k < m$ at $z_0$ (with $k = 0$ meaning $f(z_0) \
ne 0$). Then $f(z) = (z-z_0)^k \widehat f(z)$ with $\widehat f(z_0) \ne 0$, and
$$\frac{|f(z)|}{|z-z_0|^m} = |z-z_0|^{k-m}\,|\widehat f(z)| \to +\infty \quad (z
 \to z_0),$$
contradicting $|f(z)|/|z-z_0|^m \le M$. Hence $f$ vanishes at $z_0$ to order $\g
e m$: $f(z) = (z-z_0)^m \widehat f(z)$ with $\widehat f$ holomorphic near $z_0$.
 Therefore
$$\frac{f(z)}{g(z)} = \frac{\widehat f(z)}{\widetilde g(z)}$$
extends holomorphically across $z_0$. Since $z_0$ was an arbitrary zero of $g$,
the quotient
$$h(z) := \frac{f(z)}{g(z)}$$
extends to an **entire** function (every zero of $g$ is a removable singularity)
.

**Step 2. $h$ is bounded, hence constant.**

For every $z$ with $g(z) \ne 0$,
$$|h(z)| = \frac{|f(z)|}{|g(z)|} \le 1.$$
The set $\{z : g(z) \ne 0\}$ is the complement of the discrete zero set of $g$,
hence is dense in $\mathbb{C}$. Since $h$ is entire (in particular continuous),
the inequality $|h(z)| \le 1$ extends by continuity to **all** $z \in \mathbb{C}
$:
$$|h(z)| \le 1 \quad \text{for every } z \in \mathbb{C}.$$

By **Liouville's theorem**, a bounded entire function is constant. Thus $h \equi
v C$ for some constant $C \in \mathbb{C}$ with $|C| \le 1$. Consequently
$$f(z) = C\,g(z) \quad \text{for all } z \in \mathbb{C}. \qquad \blacksquare$$

---

**Remark.** The constant satisfies $|C| \le 1$. The hypothesis that $g$ is allow
ed to have zeros is handled by the order-comparison argument in Step 1, which fo
rces $f$ to vanish at least as deeply as $g$ at each zero of $g$; this is exactl
y what makes every zero of $g$ a removable singularity of $f/g$, reducing the pr
oblem to the classical Liouville argument.
