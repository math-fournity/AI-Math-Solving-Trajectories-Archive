# Problem

In a remote mining district, four independent extraction companies—Alpha, Beta, Gamma, and Delta—compete for exclusive territory rights. To determine the regional leader, each company must enter a one-on-one efficiency audit against every other company exactly once.

The regulatory board issues credits based on the outcome of these audits:
- If one company clearly outperforms the other, the winner receives 3 credits and the loser receives 0 credits.
- If the companies are found to be equally efficient, the audit is declared a parity event, and both companies receive 1 credit each.

At the conclusion of all audits, the total credits earned by each company are recorded as $a, b, c$, and $d$. Upon final tally, it is observed that there are no ties in the total standings, such that $a > b > c > d$.

How many distinct possible sets of final scores $(a, b, c, d)$ can exist for these four companies?
