## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle I = \int_{|z|=1} \frac{1-\cos z}{(e^z-1)\sin z}\,dz$
using the residue theorem.

### Step 1: Locate the singularities inside $|z|=1$

The integrand $f(z) = \dfrac{1-\cos z}{(e^z-1)\sin z}$ is singular where the den
ominator vanishes:

- $e^z - 1 = 0 \implies z = 2k\pi i,\; k\in\mathbb{Z}$. The nearest nonzero solu
tions satisfy $|z| = 2\pi > 1$, so **only $z=0$** lies inside $|z|=1$.
- $\sin z = 0 \implies z = k\pi,\; k\in\mathbb{Z}$. The nearest nonzero solution
s satisfy $|z| = \pi > 1$, so **only $z=0$** lies inside $|z|=1$.

Hence $z = 0$ is the **only** singularity inside the contour.

### Step 2: Determine the nature of the singularity at $z=0$

Expand each factor in a Taylor series around $z=0$:

$$1 - \cos z = \frac{z^2}{2} - \frac{z^4}{24} + O(z^6) \quad\text{(zero of order
 2)}$$

$$e^z - 1 = z + \frac{z^2}{2} + \frac{z^3}{6} + O(z^4) \quad\text{(zero of order
 1)}$$

$$\sin z = z - \frac{z^3}{6} + O(z^5) \quad\text{(zero of order 1)}$$

The denominator:
$$(e^z-1)\sin z = \left(z + \tfrac{z^2}{2} + \cdots\right)\left(z - \tfrac{z^3}{
6} + \cdots\right) = z^2 + \tfrac{z^3}{2} + O(z^4) \quad\text{(zero of order 2)}
$$

Both numerator and denominator vanish to **order 2** at $z=0$, so:

$$\lim_{z\to 0} \frac{1-\cos z}{(e^z-1)\sin z} = \lim_{z\to 0}\frac{z^2/2 + O(z^
4)}{z^2 + O(z^3)} = \frac{1}{2}.$$

The limit exists and is finite, so **$z=0$ is a removable singularity**.

### Step 3: Compute the residue

Since $z=0$ is a removable singularity, the Laurent expansion of $f$ around $z=0
$ contains **no principal part**, in particular no $z^{-1}$ term. Therefore:

$$\operatorname{Res}(f,\, 0) = 0.$$

### Step 4: Apply the residue theorem

By the Cauchy residue theorem:

$$I = 2\pi i \sum_{\text{poles inside}} \operatorname{Res}(f, z_k) = 2\pi i \cdo
t \operatorname{Res}(f, 0) = 2\pi i \cdot 0 = 0.$$

### Result

$$\boxed{\int_{|z|=1} \frac{1-\cos z}{(e^z-1)\sin z}\,dz = 0}$$
