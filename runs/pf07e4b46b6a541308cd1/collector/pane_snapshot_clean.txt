**Answer: Yes.**

**Construction.** Let $\mathcal{T}$ be the standard (Euclidean) topology on $\ma
thbb{R}$. Define a bijection $f \colon \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ by swapping tw
o points:
$$
f(x) = \begin{cases} 1 & x = 0, \\ 0 & x = 1, \\ x & \text{otherwise.} \end{case
s}
$$
This is clearly a bijection (it is its own inverse). Now define a new topology
$$
\mathcal{T}' := \{\, f(U) : U \in \mathcal{T} \,\}.
$$
It is immediate that $\mathcal{T}'$ is a topology on $\mathbb{R}$ (images under
a bijection preserve arbitrary unions and finite intersections), and by construc
tion $f \colon (\mathbb{R}, \mathcal{T}) \to (\mathbb{R}, \mathcal{T}')$ is a ho
meomorphism, since for every $V \subseteq \mathbb{R}$,
$$
V \in \mathcal{T}' \iff f^{-1}(V) \in \mathcal{T}.
$$

**The two topologies are different.** It remains to show $\mathcal{T}' \neq \mat
hcal{T}$. Consider the open interval $(\tfrac12, \tfrac32) \in \mathcal{T}$. Its

$$
f\bigl((\tfrac12, \tfrac32)\bigr) = f\bigl((\tfrac12, 1)\bigr) \cup f(\{1\}) \cu
p f\bigl((1, \tfrac32)\bigr) = (\tfrac12, 1) \cup \{0\} \cup (1, \tfrac32).
$$
This set belongs to $\mathcal{T}'$ (as the image of an open set), but it does **
not** belong to $\mathcal{T}$, because the point $0$ is isolated in it: there is
 no open interval around $0$ contained in $(\tfrac12, 1) \cup \{0\} \cup (1, \tf
rac32)$. Hence $\mathcal{T}' \neq \mathcal{T}$.

(Equivalently, $f$ is not continuous as a map $(\mathbb{R}, \mathcal{T}) \to (\m
athbb{R}, \mathcal{T})$, since $f^{-1}\bigl((\tfrac12, \tfrac32)\bigr) = \{0\} \
cup (\tfrac12, 1) \cup (1, \tfrac32)$ is not Euclidean-open; so $f$ is not a sel
f-homeomorphism of $(\mathbb{R}, \mathcal{T})$, forcing the transported topology
 to differ from the original.)

**Conclusion.** The spaces $(\mathbb{R}, \mathcal{T})$ and $(\mathbb{R}, \mathca
l{T}')$ are homeomorphic (via $f$) yet $\mathcal{T} \neq \mathcal{T}'$. Therefor
e two topological spaces on the real numbers can indeed be homeomorphic while ha
ving different topologies. $\blacksquare$
