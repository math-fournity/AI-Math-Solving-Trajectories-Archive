Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $|A+B| \preceq |A|+|B|$ (Lo
ewner order) holds for all complex matrices $A, B$, where $|A| = \sqrt{A^\dagger
 A}$.

**Answer: No.** The inequality does not hold in general. I'll provide an explici
t counterexample.

---

## Counterexample

Take the $2 \times 2$ complex matrices:

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B = \begin{pmatrix} 0
 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

### Step 1: Compute $|A|$

$A$ is an orthogonal projection, so $A^\dagger A = A^2 = A$. Since $A$ is alread
y positive semidefinite:

$$|A| = \sqrt{A} = A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

### Step 2: Compute $|B|$

$$B^\dagger = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B^\dagger B =
 \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end
{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

This is already PSD, so:

$$|B| = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

### Step 3: Compute $|A| + |B|$

$$|A| + |B| = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = I.$$

### Step 4: Compute $|A+B|$

$$A + B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

$$(A+B)^\dagger (A+B) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatr
ix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ has eigenvalues $2$ (e
igenvector $(1,1)^T$) and $0$ (eigenvector $(1,-1)^T$). Its spectral decompositi
on is:

$$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatr
ix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Taking the square root (replace eigenvalues $2, 0$ with $\sqrt{2}, 0$):

$$|A+B| = \sqrt{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
 = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatr
ix} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \\[4pt] \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & \frac{1
}{\sqrt{2}} \end{pmatrix}.$$

### Step 5: Check the Loewner order

For $|A+B| \preceq |A|+|B|$ to hold, the difference $|A|+|B| - |A+B|$ must be po
sitive semidefinite:

$$|A|+|B| - |A+B| = I - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pm
atrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \\[4pt] -\
frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & 1 - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \end{pmatrix}.$$

The eigenvalues of this matrix are:

$$\lambda = \left(1 - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right) \pm \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2},$$

giving:

$$\lambda_1 = 1, \qquad \lambda_2 = 1 - \sqrt{2} \approx -0.414.$$

Since $\lambda_2 = 1 - \sqrt{2} < 0$, the matrix $|A|+|B| - |A+B|$ is **not** po
sitive semidefinite.

### Conclusion

The difference $|A| + |B| - |A+B|$ has a negative eigenvalue, so $|A+B| \not\pre
ceq |A| + |B|$ in the Loewner order. The inequality **fails** for this pair of m
atrices.

**Remark.** This contrasts sharply with the scalar case, where $|a+b| \leq |a|+|
b|$ is the ordinary triangle inequality. The failure arises because the matrix m
odulus $|A| = \sqrt{A^\dagger A}$ involves a *nonlinear* square root that does n
ot commute with addition. While the weaker *norm* inequality $\|A+B\| \leq \|A\|
 + \|B\|$ (for any unitarily invariant norm) does hold, the stronger *Loewner or
der* statement $|A+B| \preceq |A|+|B|$ is false in general.
