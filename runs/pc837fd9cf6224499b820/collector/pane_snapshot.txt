## Proof

**Setup and the Künneth formula.** Let $X, Y$ be finite CW cubical complexes wit
h torsion-free integral homology $H_*(X,\mathbb{Z})$ and $H_*(Y,\mathbb{Z})$. Th
e classical Künneth short exact sequence for singular (equivalently, cubical) ho
mology reads

$$0 \longrightarrow \bigoplus_{h+k=p} H_h(X,\mathbb{Z})\otimes H_k(Y,\mathbb{Z})
 \longrightarrow H_p(X\times Y,\mathbb{Z}) \longrightarrow \bigoplus_{h+k=p-1}\m
athrm{Tor}\bigl(H_h(X,\mathbb{Z}),\,H_k(Y,\mathbb{Z})\bigr) \longrightarrow 0.$$

Since both $H_*(X,\mathbb{Z})$ and $H_*(Y,\mathbb{Z})$ are torsion-free, every $
\mathrm{Tor}$ group vanishes, and the sequence collapses to an isomorphism

$$H_p(X\times Y,\mathbb{Z}) \;\cong\; \bigoplus_{h+k=p} H_h(X,\mathbb{Z})\otimes
 H_k(Y,\mathbb{Z}). \tag{1}$$

**Choice of bases.** Torsion-freeness makes each $H_h(X,\mathbb{Z})$ and $H_k(Y,
\mathbb{Z})$ a free abelian group of finite rank. Choose $\mathbb{Z}$-bases $\{R
_i^{(h)}\}_i$ for $H_h(X,\mathbb{Z})$ and $\{S_j^{(k)}\}_j$ for $H_k(Y,\mathbb{Z
})$, represented by integral cycles $R_i^{(h)}\in Z_h(X,\mathbb{Z})$ and $S_j^{(
k)}\in Z_k(Y,\mathbb{Z})$. Under the isomorphism (1), the tensor products $R_i^{
(h)}\otimes S_j^{(k)}$ correspond to the homology classes of the **product curre
nts** $R_i^{(h)}\times S_j^{(k)}$ in $X\times Y$. Concretely, the product curren
t is the $(h+k)$-dimensional rectifiable integral current obtained from the Cart
esian product of the representing cycles; the boundary formula

$$\partial\bigl(R_i^{(h)}\times S_j^{(k)}\bigr) = (\partial R_i^{(h)})\times S_j
^{(k)} + (-1)^{h}\,R_i^{(h)}\times(\partial S_j^{(k)}) = 0$$

shows each product is itself a cycle. The family

$$\bigl\{\,R_i^{(h)}\times S_j^{(k)} \;:\; h+k=p,\; i,j \,\bigr\}$$

is therefore a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis of $H_p(X\times Y,\mathbb{Z})$.

**Decomposition of $T$.** Let $T\in Z_p(X\times Y,\mathbb{Z})$ be a rectifiable
integral $p$-cycle. Its homology class $[T]\in H_p(X\times Y,\mathbb{Z})$ expand
s uniquely in this basis:

$$[T] \;=\; \sum_{\substack{h+k=p \\ i,j}} a_{ij}^{hk}\,\bigl[R_i^{(h)}\times S_
j^{(k)}\bigr], \qquad a_{ij}^{hk}\in\mathbb{Z}. \tag{2}$$

Lifting (2) from homology to the current level: the difference

$$T \;-\; \sum_{\substack{h+k=p \\ i,j}} a_{ij}^{hk}\,\bigl(R_i^{(h)}\times S_j^
{(k)}\bigr)$$

is a rectifiable integral $p$-cycle whose homology class is zero, hence it is a
boundary. There exists a $(p+1)$-dimensional rectifiable integral current $\Sigm
a$ in $X\times Y$ such that

$$\boxed{\;T \;=\; \sum_{\substack{h+k=p \\ i,j}} a_{ij}^{hk}\,\bigl(R_i^{(h)}\t
imes S_j^{(k)}\bigr) \;+\; \partial\Sigma\;}. \tag{3}$$

**The coefficients $a_{ij}^{hk}$.** They are **uniquely determined integers**. U
niqueness follows directly from the fact that $\{R_i^{(h)}\times S_j^{(k)}\}$ is
 a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis of the free abelian group $H_p(X\times Y,\mathbb{Z})$: two
 different sets of coefficients would give two different expansions of $[T]$, co
ntradicting basis uniqueness. The torsion-free hypothesis is essential here — we
re there torsion, the Künneth $\mathrm{Tor}$ term would obstruct such a clean ba
sis expansion and the coefficients would not be uniquely recoverable from $[T]$
alone.

**The boundary term $\partial\Sigma$.** Several things can be said:

1. **It is exact**: $\partial\Sigma$ represents the zero class in $H_p(X\times Y
,\mathbb{Z})$. It is precisely the part of $T$ that carries no homological infor
mation — all of the homology of $T$ is captured by the product-cycle sum.

2. **It is uniquely determined as a current**: once the coefficients $a_{ij}^{hk
}$ are fixed (which they are, uniquely), $\partial\Sigma = T - \sum a_{ij}^{hk}(
R_i^{(h)}\times S_j^{(k)})$ is completely determined by $T$.

3. **The primitive $\Sigma$ is not unique**: any two choices $\Sigma, \Sigma'$ s
atisfying (3) differ by a $(p+1)$-cycle: $\Sigma - \Sigma' \in Z_{p+1}(X\times Y
,\mathbb{Z})$. Thus $\Sigma$ is determined only up to addition of an integral $(
p+1)$-cycle.

4. **It is rectifiable and integral**: since $T$ and each $R_i^{(h)}\times S_j^{
(k)}$ are rectifiable integral currents, so is their difference, and hence $\Sig
ma$ can be chosen rectifiable and integral (the isoperimetric theorem for integr
al currents in cubical complexes guarantees a rectifiable integral filling of an
y null-homologous integral cycle).

**Summary.** Under the torsion-free hypothesis, the Künneth $\mathrm{Tor}$ obstr
uction vanishes, the product cycles $R_i^{(h)}\times S_j^{(k)}$ form a $\mathbb{
Z}$-basis of $H_p(X\times Y,\mathbb{Z})$, and every rectifiable integral $p$-cyc
le $T$ admits the unique decomposition (3): a canonical "homological" part — a $
\mathbb{Z}$-linear combination of product basis cycles with **uniquely determine
d integer coefficients** $a_{ij}^{hk}$ — plus an **exact** boundary term $\parti
al\Sigma$ that is uniquely determined as a current (though its primitive $\Sigma
