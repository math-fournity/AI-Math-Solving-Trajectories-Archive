We apply **Rouché's theorem**. Recall: if $g$ and $h$ are analytic inside and on
 a simple closed contour $\gamma$, and $|g(z) - h(z)| < |h(z)|$ on $\gamma$, the
n $g$ and $h$ have the same number of zeros (counted with multiplicity) inside $
\gamma$.

Set the contour $\gamma = \{z : |z| = 1\}$, and take
$$g(z) = f(z), \qquad h(z) = z.$$

Both are analytic inside and on the unit disc: $f$ by hypothesis, and $z$ trivia
lly. On the boundary $|z|=1$ we have $|h(z)| = |z| = 1$, and the hypothesis give
s
$$|g(z) - h(z)| = |f(z) - z| < |z| = |h(z)|.$$

Thus Rouché's theorem applies, and $f(z)$ has the same number of zeros inside th
e unit disc as $z$.

The function $z$ has exactly **one** zero inside $\{z:|z|<1\}$, namely $z=0$, wi
th multiplicity $1$.

Therefore $f(z)$ has exactly **one** zero inside the unit disc.
