# Eigenvalues of $y'' + \lambda y = 0$, $y(0)=0$, $y(1)=0$

## Proof

We seek nontrivial solutions $y\not\equiv 0$ of

$$
y''+\lambda y = 0,\qquad y(0)=0,\quad y(1)=0.
$$

We split into cases by the sign of $\lambda$.

**Case $\lambda < 0$.** Write $\lambda = -\mu^{2}$ with $\mu>0$. The general sol
ution is
$$
y(x)=A\cosh(\mu x)+B\sinh(\mu x).
$$
The condition $y(0)=0$ gives $A=0$, so $y(x)=B\sinh(\mu x)$. The condition $y(1)
=0$ gives $B\sinh\mu=0$. Since $\mu>0$, $\sinh\mu\neq 0$, hence $B=0$. Only the
trivial solution exists — no negative eigenvalues.

**Case $\lambda = 0$.** The equation reduces to $y''=0$, so $y(x)=Ax+B$. From $y
(0)=0$: $B=0$; from $y(1)=0$: $A=0$. Only the trivial solution — $\lambda=0$ is
not an eigenvalue.

**Case $\lambda > 0$.** Write $\lambda = \mu^{2}$ with $\mu>0$. The general solu
tion is
$$
y(x)=A\cos(\mu x)+B\sin(\mu x).
$$
From $y(0)=0$: $A\cos 0 = A = 0$, so $y(x)=B\sin(\mu x)$. From $y(1)=0$:
$$
B\sin\mu = 0.
$$
For a nontrivial solution we need $B\neq 0$, which forces
$$
\sin\mu = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mu = n\pi,\qquad n=1,2,3,\dots
$$
(We take $n\ge 1$ since $\mu>0$; $n=0$ would give $\mu=0$, already excluded.) Th
erefore the eigenvalues are
$$
\lambda_n = \mu_n^{2} = n^{2}\pi^{2},\qquad n=1,2,3,\dots
$$
with corresponding eigenfunctions $y_n(x)=\sin(n\pi x)$.

## The first four positive eigenvalues

Taking $n=1,2,3,4$:

$$
\boxed{\lambda_1 = \pi^{2},\quad \lambda_2 = 4\pi^{2},\quad \lambda_3 = 9\pi^{2}
,\quad \lambda_4 = 16\pi^{2}.}
$$

Numerically (for reference): $\pi^{2}\approx 9.8696$, $4\pi^{2}\approx 39.4784$,
 $9\pi^{2}\approx 88.8264$, $16\pi^{2}\approx 157.9137$.
