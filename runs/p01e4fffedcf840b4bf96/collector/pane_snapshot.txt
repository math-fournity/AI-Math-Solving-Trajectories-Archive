## Proof

**Yes**, $\sum b_n$ converges and $\tau = \sum b_n$.

**Setup.** Since $b_n \le 0$, set $a_n = -b_n \ge 0$ and $f(x) = -g(x) = \sum_{n
=0}^\infty a_n x^n$. Then $f$ has radius of convergence $1$, $a_n \ge 0$, and
$$\lim_{x \to 1^-} f(x) = -\tau =: S \quad (\text{finite}).$$
It suffices to show $\sum a_n = S$; then $\sum b_n = -S = \tau$.

**Step 1: The partial sums are bounded above by $S$.**

Let $s_N = \sum_{n=0}^N a_n$. For any $N \ge 0$ and any $x \in (0,1)$, since $a_
n \ge 0$ and $x^n \ge 0$,
$$f(x) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n x^n \ge \sum_{n=0}^N a_n x^n \ge x^N \sum_{n=0}^N
 a_n = x^N\, s_N.$$
Hence $s_N \le f(x)\, x^{-N}$ for all $x \in (0,1)$. Holding $N$ fixed and letti
ng $x \to 1^-$,
$$s_N \le \lim_{x \to 1^-} f(x) = S.$$
This holds for every $N$, so $(s_N)$ is bounded above by $S$.

**Step 2: The series $\sum a_n$ converges.**

Since $a_n \ge 0$, the partial sums $s_N$ are non-decreasing. By Step 1 they are
 bounded above by $S$, so they converge to a finite limit:
$$L := \lim_{N \to \infty} s_N \le S.$$

**Step 3: $L = S$ via Abel's theorem.**

The series $\sum a_n$ converges (to $L$) by Step 2. By **Abel's theorem** (the A
belian direction, which requires only convergence, no sign condition),
$$\lim_{x \to 1^-} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 1^-} \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n x^n = L.$$
But we are given $\lim_{x \to 1^-} f(x) = S$. Therefore $L = S$.

**Conclusion.** $\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n = S = -\tau$, so
$$\sum_{n=0}^\infty b_n = -\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n = -S = \tau.$$

Thus $\sum b_n$ converges and equals $\tau$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The sign condition $b_n \le 0$ (equivalently $a_n \ge 0$) is the ess
ential Tauberian hypothesis: it makes the partial sums monotone, so boundedness
(from the Abel limit) forces convergence. Without a sign condition the conclusio
n fails — e.g. $b_n = (-1)^n$ gives $g(x) = 1/(1+x) \to 1/2$ as $x \to 1^-$, yet
 $\sum (-1)^n$ does not converge.
