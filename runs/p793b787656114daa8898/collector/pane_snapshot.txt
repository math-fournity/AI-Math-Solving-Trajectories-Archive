## Proof

Let $p$ and $q$ be two consecutive prime numbers, so $n = pq$ with $\gcd(p,q)=1$
 and $p \neq q$.

**Sum of divisors.** Since $p$ and $q$ are distinct primes, the divisors of $n =
 pq$ are $1, p, q, pq$, so:

$$s(n) = (1+p)(1+q) = 1 + p + q + pq = n + 1 + (p+q).$$

**Euler's totient.** Since $n = pq$ with $p, q$ distinct primes:

$$p(n) = \varphi(n) = (p-1)(q-1) = pq - p - q + 1 = n + 1 - (p+q).$$

**The product.** Let $S = p + q$. Then:

$$s(n)\,p(n) = \bigl(n + 1 + S\bigr)\bigl(n + 1 - S\bigr) = (n+1)^2 - S^2.$$

Expanding:

$$\boxed{s(n)\,p(n) = n^2 + 2n + 1 - (p+q)^2.}$$

**Verification.**
- $p=2, q=3, n=6$: $s(6)=12$, $\varphi(6)=2$, product $= 24$. Formula: $49 - 25
= 24$. ✓
- $p=3, q=5, n=15$: $s(15)=24$, $\varphi(15)=8$, product $= 192$. Formula: $256
- 64 = 192$. ✓
- $p=5, q=7, n=35$: $s(35)=48$, $\varphi(35)=24$, product $= 1152$. Formula: $12
96 - 144 = 1152$. ✓

This is a polynomial of degree 2 in $n$ (with the constant term $1-(p+q)^2$ dete
rmined by the prime pair whose product is $n$).
