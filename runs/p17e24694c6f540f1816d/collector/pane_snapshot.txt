> If there is a finite projective plane of order $n$, then must $n$ be a prime p

This is the **prime power conjecture** for finite projective planes — one of the

The state of knowledge is:
- **Prime power orders always work**: For every prime power $q = p^k$, a project
ive plane of order $q$ exists (e.g., the Desarguesian plane $\mathrm{PG}(2, q)$
over $\mathbb{F}_q$). This is the `prime_power_is_projplane_order` variant, mark
ed *solved*.

ve plane of non-prime-power order has ever been constructed.

ressible as a sum of two squares. This rules out $n = 6$ (since $6 \equiv 2 \pmo
