We need to show that for every natural number $n \ge 2$,
$$\frac{1}{n+1}\left(1+\frac13+\cdots+\frac1{2n-1}\right)>\frac1n\left(\frac12+\
frac14+\cdots+\frac1{2n}\right).$$

**Setup.** Let
$$A=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac1{2k-1},\qquad B=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac1{2k}.$$
The desired inequality is $\dfrac{A}{n+1}>\dfrac{B}{n}$, equivalently $nA>(n+1)B
$, i.e.
$$n(A-B)>B. \tag{$\star$}$$

Now
$$A-B=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac1{2k-1}-\frac1{2k}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac1{(2
k-1)(2k)},$$
so
$$n(A-B)-B=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{n}{(2k-1)(2k)}-\frac1{2k}\right)=\sum_{k=1}
^{n}\frac{1}{2k}\left(\frac{n}{2k-1}-1\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{n-2k+1}{2k(2k-
1)}.$$

Thus $(\star)$ is equivalent to
$$S:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{n-2k+1}{2k(2k-1)}>0. \tag{$\star\star$}$$

**Pairing argument.** Pair the term indexed $k$ with the term indexed $n+1-k$. T
heir numerators are negatives of each other:
$$(n-2k+1)\quad\text{and}\quad n-2(n+1-k)+1=-(n-2k+1).$$
Hence the pair contributes
$$\frac{n-2k+1}{2k(2k-1)}+\frac{-(n-2k+1)}{2(n+1-k)(2n+1-2k)}=(n-2k+1)\left(\fra
c1{2k(2k-1)}-\frac1{2(n+1-k)(2n+1-2k)}\right). \tag{$\dagger$}$$

Consider a pair with $k<n+1-k$, i.e. $k<\frac{n+1}{2}$. Then $n-2k+1>0$. Also $k
<n+1-k$ gives $2k<2(n+1-k)$ and $2k-1<2n+1-2k$, so
$$2k(2k-1)<2(n+1-k)(2n+1-2k),$$
which makes the bracketed difference in $(\dagger)$ strictly positive. Therefore
 every such pair is **strictly positive**.

- **If $n$ is even**, $n=2m$: the pairs $(k,\,n+1-k)=(k,\,2m+1-k)$ for $k=1,\dot
s,m$ are all distinct and cover every index $1,\dots,2m$. Each pair is strictly
positive, so $S>0$.
- **If $n$ is odd**, $n=2m+1$: the pairs $(k,\,2m+2-k)$ for $k=1,\dots,m$ cover
all indices except the middle index $k=m+1$. The middle term has numerator $n-2(
m+1)+1=2m+1-2m-2+1=0$, so it contributes $0$. Every other pair is strictly posit
ive, so again $S>0$.

In both cases $S>0$ for $n\ge 2$ (at $n=1$ the sole term has numerator $0$, givi
ng equality, which is why the hypothesis $n\ge2$ is needed).

**Conclusion.** Since $S=n(A-B)-B>0$, we have $n(A-B)>B$, hence $nA>(n+1)B$, hen
ce
$$\frac{1}{n+1}\left(1+\frac13+\cdots+\frac1{2n-1}\right)>\frac1n\left(\frac12+\
frac14+\cdots+\frac1{2n}\right)$$
for every natural number $n\ge 2$. $\blacksquare$
