Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $(C^0[a,b], \|\cdot\|_1)$ a
nd $(C^0[0,1], \|\cdot\|_1)$ are isometrically isomorphic, where $\|f\|_1 = \int
_a^b |f(t)|\,dt$ (resp. $\int_0^1 |f(s)|\,ds$).

**Answer: Yes, they are isometrically isomorphic.**

**Proof.**

Define $T: C^0[a,b] \to C^0[0,1]$ by
$$Tf(s) = (b-a)\, f\!\big(a + (b-a)s\big), \qquad s \in [0,1].$$

**$T$ is linear.** For scalars $\alpha,\beta$ and $f,g \in C^0[a,b]$:
$$T(\alpha f + \beta g)(s) = (b-a)\big[\alpha f(a{+}(b{-}a)s) + \beta g(a{+}(b{-
}a)s)\big] = \alpha\, Tf(s) + \beta\, Tg(s).$$

**$T$ is injective.** If $Tf = 0$, then $f(a+(b-a)s)=0$ for all $s\in[0,1]$. Sin
ce $a+(b-a)s$ ranges over all of $[a,b]$, we get $f\equiv 0$.

**$T$ is surjective.** Given $g \in C^0[0,1]$, set $f(t) = \frac{1}{b-a}\,g\!\bi
g(\frac{t-a}{b-a}\big)$ for $t \in [a,b]$. Then $f \in C^0[a,b]$ and
$$Tf(s) = (b-a)\cdot\tfrac{1}{b-a}\, g\!\Big(\tfrac{a+(b-a)s-a}{b-a}\Big) = g(s)
.$$

**$T$ is an isometry.** Using the substitution $t = a + (b-a)s$, so $dt = (b-a)\
,ds$:
$$\|Tf\|_1 = \int_0^1 (b-a)\,|f(a+(b-a)s)|\,ds = \int_a^b (b-a)\,|f(t)|\,\frac{d
t}{b-a} = \int_a^b |f(t)|\,dt = \|f\|_1.$$

Thus $T$ is a linear bijective isometry, i.e., an isometric isomorphism between
$(C^0[a,b],\|\cdot\|_1)$ and $(C^0[0,1],\|\cdot\|_1)$. $\blacksquare$

The key point is that the scaling factor $(b-a)$ compensates exactly for the cha
nge of variables in the integral, making the norms match regardless of the lengt
h of $[a,b]$.
