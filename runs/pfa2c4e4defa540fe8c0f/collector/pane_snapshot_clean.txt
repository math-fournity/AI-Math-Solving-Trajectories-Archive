## Proof

We are given the drifted Brownian motion

$$X_t = \mu_x\, t + \sigma_x\, W_t^x,$$

where $\mu_x, \sigma_x$ are constants and $W_t^x$ is a standard Brownian motion
(so $W_t^x \sim \mathcal{N}(0,t)$, hence $\mathbb{E}[W_t^x]=0$).

We want $\mathbb{E}[X_t\, dt]$.

**Step 1 — Linearity of expectation.** Since $dt$ is a deterministic (non-random
) increment, it factors out of the expectation:

$$\mathbb{E}[X_t\, dt] = \mathbb{E}[X_t]\, dt.$$

**Step 2 — Compute $\mathbb{E}[X_t]$.** Using linearity of expectation on the de
finition of $X_t$:

$$\mathbb{E}[X_t] = \mathbb{E}[\mu_x\, t + \sigma_x\, W_t^x] = \mu_x\, t + \sigm
a_x\, \mathbb{E}[W_t^x].$$

Since $W_t^x$ is a standard Brownian motion, $\mathbb{E}[W_t^x] = 0$. Therefore

$$\mathbb{E}[X_t] = \mu_x\, t.$$

**Step 3 — Substitute back.**

$$\mathbb{E}[X_t\, dt] = \mathbb{E}[X_t]\, dt = \mu_x\, t\, dt.$$

**Step 4 — Integral form (if interpreted over $[0,T]$).** If the expression is u
nderstood as the integrated quantity $\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^T X_t\, dt\right]
$, then by Fubini's theorem (justified since $\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^T |X_t|\,
 dt\right] < \infty$):

$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^T X_t\, dt\right] = \int_0^T \mathbb{E}[X_t]\, dt = \
int_0^T \mu_x\, t\, dt = \mu_x\,\frac{T^2}{2}.$$

**Result.**

$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}[X_t\, dt] = \mu_x\, t\, dt}$$

and, in integrated form over $[0,T]$,

$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^T X_t\, dt\right] = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mu_x\, T^2.
}$$

The key stochastic-calculus facts used: (i) $dt$ is deterministic so it passes t
hrough the expectation; (ii) the Brownian motion has zero mean, $\mathbb{E}[W_t^
x]=0$, so only the drift $\mu_x t$ contributes to the expectation; (iii) Fubini'
s theorem allows exchanging expectation and integration.
