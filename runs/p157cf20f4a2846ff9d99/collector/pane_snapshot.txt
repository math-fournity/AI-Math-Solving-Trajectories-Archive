## Key Tool: The Artin–Schreier Theorem

**Theorem (Artin–Schreier, 1927).** *Let $F$ be a field with absolute Galois gro
up $G_F = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{F}/F)$. If $G_F$ is finite, then exactly one of the
following holds:*
1. *$F$ is algebraically closed and $G_F = \{1\}$; or*
2. *$F$ is real closed and $G_F \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, with $\bar{F} = F
(i)$ and $[\bar{F}:F]=2$.*

*In particular, there are no finite absolute Galois groups other than the trivia
l group and $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.*

## Applying the Theorem to Subfields of $\mathbb{R}$

Let $F \subsetneq \mathbb{R}$ be a proper subfield. Since $F \subseteq \mathbb{R
}$, the field $F$ is **formally real** (it admits an ordering, namely the restri
ction of the usual order on $\mathbb{R}$). A formally real field is never algebr
aically closed (the polynomial $x^2+1$ has no root in any ordered field). Theref
ore case (1) of the Artin–Schreier theorem is **excluded** for every subfield of
 $\mathbb{R}$.

Consequently, the only way $G_F$ can be finite is via case (2): **$F$ must be re
al closed**, in which case $G_F \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.

So the question reduces to: **does a real closed proper subfield of $\mathbb{R}$
 exist?**

## Constructing a Real Closed Proper Subfield of $\mathbb{R}$

Consider the field of **real algebraic numbers**:
$$F = \mathbb{R} \cap \bar{\mathbb{Q}} = \{\alpha \in \mathbb{R} : \alpha \text{
 is algebraic over } \mathbb{Q}\}.$$

**$F$ is a proper subfield of $\mathbb{R}$:** $F$ is countable (the set of all a
lgebraic numbers is countable), while $\mathbb{R}$ is uncountable. Hence $F \sub
setneq \mathbb{R}$.

**$F$ is real closed.** We verify the two defining properties of a real closed f
ield:

- *Every positive element is a square:* If $a \in F$ with $a > 0$, then $\sqrt{a
} \in \mathbb{R}$ and $\sqrt{a}$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}(a) \subseteq \bar
{\mathbb{Q}}$, so $\sqrt{a} \in \mathbb{R} \cap \bar{\mathbb{Q}} = F$.

- *Every odd-degree polynomial over $F$ has a root in $F$:* Let $f \in F[x]$ hav
e odd degree. Since $F \subset \mathbb{R}$, we may view $f$ as a real polynomial
 of odd degree, which has a real root $r$ by the Intermediate Value Theorem. Thi
s $r$ is algebraic over $F$ (it is a root of $f$), and $F$ consists of elements
algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$, so $r$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$. Thus $r \in
\mathbb{R} \cap \bar{\mathbb{Q}} = F$.

Therefore $F$ is real closed. (Equivalently, $F$ is the real closure of the orde
red field $(\mathbb{Q}, <)$.)

## Conclusion

Since $F$ is real closed, the Artin–Schreier theorem gives:
$$\bar{F} = F(i), \quad [\bar{F}:F] = 2, \quad G_F = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{F}/F) \co
ng \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.$$

This is a **finite** group (of order 2), and $F$ is a **proper** subfield of $\m
athbb{R}$.

**Answer:** Yes. The absolute Galois group of a proper subfield of $\mathbb{R}$
can be finite. Specifically, for any real closed proper subfield $F \subsetneq \
mathbb{R}$ (such as the field of real algebraic numbers $\mathbb{R}\cap\bar{\mat
hbb{Q}}$), the absolute Galois group is $G_F \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. More
over, by the Artin–Schreier theorem, $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is the *only* fini
te absolute Galois group that can arise for a subfield of $\mathbb{R}$ (the triv
ial group is excluded because every subfield of $\mathbb{R}$ is formally real, h
ence not algebraically closed).
