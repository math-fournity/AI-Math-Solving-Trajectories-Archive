# Winding Number of a Space-Filling Curve

## Setup

Let $\gamma: [0,T] \to [0,1]\times[0,1] \subset \mathbb{R}^2 \cong \mathbb{C}$ b
e a continuous, surjective, **closed** curve ($\gamma(0)=\gamma(T)$), and let $a
 \in \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus [0,1]^2$ be a point outside the image.

The winding number of $\gamma$ about $a$ is
$$n(\gamma,a) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma \frac{dz}{z-a}.$$

## Claim

$$n(\gamma,a) = 0.$$

## Proof

**Step 1: $a$ is uniformly separated from the image.** Since $\gamma$ is surject
ive, $\operatorname{Im}(\gamma) = [0,1]^2$, which is compact. As $a \notin [0,1]
^2$, there exists $\varepsilon > 0$ with
$$|z - a| \geq \varepsilon \quad \text{for all } z \in [0,1]^2.$$

**Step 2: Construct a simply connected open neighborhood of the image avoiding $
a$.** The square $[0,1]^2$ is convex, hence simply connected. Define
$$U = \bigl\{z \in \mathbb{C} : \operatorname{dist}(z,\,[0,1]^2) < \varepsilon/2
\bigr\}.$$
Then $U$ is open, $[0,1]^2 \subset U$, and $a \notin U$ (by the choice of $\vare
psilon$). Moreover, $U$ is simply connected: it is a convex thickening of a conv
ex set, hence convex, hence simply connected.

**Step 3: $\frac{1}{z-a}$ is exact on $U$.** Since $U$ is simply connected and $
a \notin U$, the translated set $U - a$ is simply connected and does not contain
 $0$. Therefore a holomorphic branch of the logarithm exists on $U - a$; equival
ently, the function
$$F(z) = \operatorname{Log}(z - a)$$
is a well-defined holomorphic antiderivative of $\frac{1}{z-a}$ on $U$, i.e.,
$$\frac{dF}{dz} = \frac{1}{z-a} \quad \text{on } U.$$

**Step 4: Evaluate the integral.** Since $\operatorname{Im}(\gamma) \subset U$ a
nd $\frac{1}{z-a} = F'(z)$ on $U$, the fundamental theorem of calculus for conto
ur integrals gives
$$\oint_\gamma \frac{dz}{z-a} = F(\gamma(T)) - F(\gamma(0)).$$
Because $\gamma$ is a **closed** curve, $\gamma(T) = \gamma(0)$, so
$$\oint_\gamma \frac{dz}{z-a} = 0.$$

**Step 5: Conclude.**
$$n(\gamma,a) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\cdot 0 = 0. \qquad \blacksquare$$

## Remark

The surjectivity of $\gamma$ (i.e., that $\gamma$ is a Peano-type space-filling
curve) plays no essential role. The only relevant facts are:

1. $\operatorname{Im}(\gamma) = [0,1]^2$ is **simply connected** (convex), and
2. $a \notin [0,1]^2$.

For *any* closed curve whose image lies in a simply connected region $U$ with $a
 \notin U$, the form $\frac{dz}{z-a}$ is exact on $U$, forcing the winding numbe
r about $a$ to vanish. The space-filling nature of $\gamma$ is a red herring; th
e topology of the image (simply connected, missing $a$) alone determines the ans
wer.
