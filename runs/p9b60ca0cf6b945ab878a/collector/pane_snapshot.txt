## Proof

**Setup and key observation.**

We are given:
- $(X, \tau)$ is a normal topological space.
- $D \subseteq X$ is closed, with $|D| = |\mathbb{R}| = \mathfrak{c}$, and the i
nduced topology on $D$ is **discrete**.

Let $S \subseteq D$ be a countable subset. We determine the number of continuous
 functions $f: S \to [0,1]$.

**Step 1: The induced topology on $S$ is discrete.**

Since $D$ carries the discrete topology, every subset of $D$ is open in $D$. The
 induced topology on $S$ (as a subspace of $D$) is therefore also discrete: for
any $A \subseteq S$, we have $A = S \cap A$ where $A$ is open in $D$, so $A$ is
open in $S$.

**Step 2: Every function $f: S \to [0,1]$ is continuous.**

In the discrete topology on $S$, every subset of $S$ is open. For any open set $
U \subseteq [0,1]$, the preimage $f^{-1}(U) \subseteq S$ is automatically open.
Hence **every** function from $S$ to $[0,1]$ is continuous.

**Step 3: Count the functions.**

The number of continuous functions $f: S \to [0,1]$ equals the total number of f
unctions $S \to [0,1]$, which is:
$$|[0,1]|^{|S|} = \mathfrak{c}^{\aleph_0}$$
(for $S$ countably infinite; for finite $S$ with $|S|=n$, we get $\mathfrak{c}^n
 = \mathfrak{c}$ as well).

Compute $\mathfrak{c}^{\aleph_0}$:
$$\mathfrak{c}^{\aleph_0} = (2^{\aleph_0})^{\aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph_0 \cdot \aleph
_0} = 2^{\aleph_0} = \mathfrak{c}.$$

**Step 4: The normality and closedness conditions are not needed here.**

The hypotheses that $X$ is normal and $D$ is closed in $X$ would be essential fo
r questions about *extending* continuous functions from $D$ to $X$ (e.g., via th
e Tietze extension theorem). However, the question asks only about continuous fu
nctions *on* a countable subset of $D$, whose continuity is determined entirely
by the (discrete) induced topology on that subset. Thus these hypotheses do not
affect the count.

**Conclusion.**

The number of continuous functions from a countable subset of $D$ to $[0,1]$ is:

$$\boxed{\mathfrak{c} = 2^{\aleph_0}}$$
