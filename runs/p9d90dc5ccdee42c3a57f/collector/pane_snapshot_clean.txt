## Solution

We work on $C[0,1]$ with the supremum norm $\|x\|_\infty = \max_{t\in[0,1]}|x(t)
|$. The functional is
$$l(x) = x(0) + \int_0^1 (2t-1)\,x(t)\,dt.$$

### Step 1. Upper bound

For any $x\in C[0,1]$ with $\|x\|_\infty\le 1$:
$$|l(x)|\le |x(0)| + \int_0^1|2t-1|\,|x(t)|\,dt \le 1 + \int_0^1|2t-1|\,dt.$$
Computing:
$$\int_0^1|2t-1|\,dt = \int_0^{1/2}(1-2t)\,dt + \int_{1/2}^1(2t-1)\,dt = \tfrac1
4 + \tfrac14 = \tfrac12.$$
Hence $\|l\|\le 1+\tfrac12 = \tfrac32$.

### Step 2. Lower bound via approximating functions

Define continuous functions $x_n\in C[0,1]$ with $\|x_n\|_\infty\le 1$ by:
- $x_n(0)=1$, then linearly decrease to $-1$ on $[0,\,1/n]$;
- $x_n=-1$ on $[1/n,\,1/2-1/n]$;
- linearly increase from $-1$ to $+1$ on $[1/2-1/n,\,1/2+1/n]$;
- $x_n=+1$ on $[1/2+1/n,\,1]$.

(For $n$ large enough that $1/n < 1/2$.) Then $x_n(0)=1$, and $x_n(t)\to -\opera
torname{sgn}(2t-1)$ pointwise for $t\in(0,1)\setminus\{1/2\}$. By dominated conv
ergence (since $|x_n|\le 1$ and $|2t-1|$ is integrable):
$$\int_0^1(2t-1)\,x_n(t)\,dt \;\longrightarrow\; \int_0^{1/2}(2t-1)(-1)\,dt + \i
nt_{1/2}^1(2t-1)(+1)\,dt = \tfrac14+\tfrac14 = \tfrac12.$$
Therefore
$$l(x_n) = x_n(0) + \int_0^1(2t-1)\,x_n(t)\,dt \;\longrightarrow\; 1 + \tfrac12
= \tfrac32.$$
Since $\|x_n\|_\infty\le 1$, this shows $\|l\|\ge \tfrac32$.

### Step 3. Conclusion

Combining both bounds:
$$\boxed{\|l\| = \dfrac{3}{2}}.$$

**Remark.** This agrees with the Riesz representation: $l$ corresponds to the si
gned measure $\mu = \delta_0 + (2t-1)\,dt$. Since $\delta_0$ (supported on $\{0\
}$) and $(2t-1)\,dt$ (which gives zero mass to $\{0\}$) are mutually singular, t
he total variation is $|\mu|=|\delta_0|+|(2t-1)\,dt|=1+\tfrac12=\tfrac32$.
