The condition $\int_0^{2\pi} f(t)\,dt=0$ gives $c_0=0$, so
$$f(t)=\sum_{n\ne 0} c_n\, e^{int}.$$
Since $f$ is real-valued, $c_{-n}=\overline{c_n}$. Termwise differentiation (jus

**Step 2: Parseval and the integral consequence.** The pointwise hypothesis $|f(

$$\int_0^{2\pi}|f(t)|^2\,dt \;\ge\; \int_0^{2\pi}|f''(t)|^2\,dt.$$
By Parseval's identity,
$$2\pi\sum_{n\ne 0}|c_n|^2 \;\ge\; 2\pi\sum_{n\ne 0} n^4|c_n|^2.$$
For every $n\ne 0$ we have $|n|\ge 1$, hence $n^4\ge 1$, so $\sum n^4|c_n|^2 \ge
 \sum |c_n|^2$. Combining the two inequalities forces
$$\sum_{n\ne 0} n^4|c_n|^2 = \sum_{n\ne 0}|c_n|^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sum
_{n\ne 0}(n^4-1)|c_n|^2=0.$$
Each summand is non-negative, and $n^4-1=0$ only for $n=\pm 1$. Therefore $c_n=0
