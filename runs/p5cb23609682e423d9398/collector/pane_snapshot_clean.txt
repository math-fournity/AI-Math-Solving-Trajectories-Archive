## Analysis of the System

**The system:** $f_i(x) = (x_i - 1)(x_{n-i+1}^2 + x_{n-i+1} + 3) = 0$, for $i =
1, \dots, n$.

**Key observation 1 — the second factor is always positive.** The quadratic $u^2
 + u + 3$ has discriminant $1 - 12 = -11 < 0$ and positive leading coefficient,
so $u^2 + u + 3 > 0$ for all real $u$. Therefore $f_i = 0$ forces $x_i = 1$. The
 **unique real root** is $x^* = (1, 1, \dots, 1)$.

**Key observation 2 — Jacobian structure.**
$$\frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x_i} = x_{n-i+1}^2 + x_{n-i+1} + 3, \qquad \frac{
\partial f_i}{\partial x_{n-i+1}} = (x_i - 1)(2x_{n-i+1} + 1)$$
All other partials are zero. So $J$ is a **diagonal-plus-anti-diagonal** matrix:
 each row has at most 2 nonzeros, at columns $i$ and $n-i+1$.

**Key observation 3 — block structure.** Rows $i$ and $n-i+1$ involve exactly co
lumns $i$ and $n-i+1$. Under the permutation $(x_1, x_n, x_2, x_{n-1}, \dots)$,
the Jacobian becomes **block diagonal** with $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ blocks of siz
e $2 \times 2$ (and one $1 \times 1$ block if $n$ is odd). Solving $J\Delta = -f
$ reduces to $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ independent $2 \times 2$ solves — **$O(n)$ pe
r Newton step** if the structure is exploited, vs. $O(n^3)$ for dense LU.

**Key observation 4 — symmetry reduction.** Starting at $x^{(0)} = \mathbf{0}$ (
all components equal), the system is invariant under the involution $x_i \leftri
ghtarrow x_{n-i+1}$. By uniqueness of the Newton step, all components remain equ
al: $x_i^{(k)} = t_k$ for all $i$. The problem **reduces to a scalar Newton iter
ation** on:
$$g(t) = (t-1)(t^2+t+3) = t^3 + 2t - 3, \qquad g'(t) = 3t^2 + 2$$

## Part (b): NOVAT (Newton One Variable at a Time)

NOVAT cycles through $i = 1, \dots, n$, performing a 1D Newton step on $f_i$ wit
h respect to $x_i$ (holding others fixed):

$$x_i \leftarrow x_i - \frac{f_i}{\partial f_i / \partial x_i} = x_i - \frac{(x_
i - 1)\,h(x_{n-i+1})}{h(x_{n-i+1})} = x_i - (x_i - 1) = 1$$

where $h(u) = u^2 + u + 3 > 0$. **Each 1D step sets $x_i$ to exactly 1, regardle
ss of the current value of $x_{n-i+1}$.**

**Result: NOVAT converges in exactly 1 sweep** ($n$ individual 1D Newton steps),
 with **exact convergence** (machine precision), not merely $< 10^{-5}$.

**Cost:** $O(n)$ total — one pass through the variables, each step $O(1)$.

**Maximum $n$ for < 1 minute:** $O(n)$ with tiny constant; $n$ can reach **tens
of millions** or more. NOVAT is dramatically superior to full Newton on this pro
blem because the equations are *separable* in $x_i$ — the factor $h(x_{n-i+1})$
cancels exactly in the Newton ratio.

## Part (d): Broyden's Method

Broyden's "good" method with Sherman-Morrison update maintains $B_k \approx J_k^
{-1}$ directly:

$$x_{k+1} = x_k - B_k f(x_k), \qquad B_{k+1} = B_k + \frac{(\Delta x - B_k \Delt
a f)\Delta x^T B_k}{\Delta x^T B_k \Delta f}$$

where $\Delta x = x_{k+1} - x_k$, $\Delta f = f(x_{k+1}) - f(x_k)$.

**Cost per iteration:** $O(n^2)$ (matrix-vector products and rank-1 update), vs.
 $O(n^3)$ for Newton's solve.

**Convergence:** Superlinear (rate $\approx 1.618$ in the 1D reduction, like the
 secant method). By the symmetry reduction, the effective 1D iteration is the se
cant method on $g(t) = t^3 + 2t - 3$:

## Part (f): Squared Factor — $(x_i - 1)^2(x_{n-i+1}^2 + x_{n-i+1} + 3) = 0$

The root is still $x^* = (1, \dots, 1)$, but now it is a **root of multiplicity
2** in each variable. This fundamentally changes convergence behavior.

### Newton's Method

The scalar reduction gives $g(t) = (t-1)^2(t^2+t+3)$, $g'(t) = (t-1)(4t^2+t+5)$.

The Newton iteration satisfies:
$$t_{k+1} - 1 = (t_k - 1) \cdot \frac{3t_k^2 + 2}{4t_k^2 + t_k + 5}$$

Near $t = 1$: $\frac{3+2}{4+1+5} = \frac{5}{10} = \frac{1}{2}$. So **$e_{k+1} \a
pprox \frac{1}{2} e_k$** — linear convergence with rate $1/2$ (the hallmark of a
 double root).

**Iterations needed:** $(1/2)^k < 10^{-5} \Rightarrow k \geq 17$. The graph is f
lat at $y \approx 17$ for all $n$.

### NOVAT

The 1D Newton step on $f_i$ w.r.t. $x_i$:
$$x_i \leftarrow x_i - \frac{(x_i-1)^2 h}{2(x_i-1)h} = x_i - \frac{x_i - 1}{2} =
 \frac{x_i + 1}{2}$$

Each step **halves the distance to 1**. After $k$ sweeps: $x_i = 1 - (1/2)^k$. N
eed $k \geq 17$ sweeps. Each sweep is $O(n)$, total $17n = O(n)$.

**NOVAT no longer converges in 1 sweep** — it needs 17 sweeps, but each is still

### Finite-Difference Newton

Same linear convergence as Newton (~17 iterations), $O(n^3)$ per iteration (dens

### Broyden's Method

At a double root, Broyden also converges **linearly** with rate $(m-1)/m = 1/2$,

The **critical difference** from part (a) is the **loss of quadratic convergence

- Newton's method degrades from **quadratic** (5 iterations) to **linear with ra

All methods suffer equally from the multiplicity — the convergence rate $(m-1)/m
