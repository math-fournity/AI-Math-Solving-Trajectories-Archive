**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

We construct such a polynomial explicitly.

**Step 1: A Galois extension $K/\mathbb{Q}$ with $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q
}) \cong S_3$.**

Consider the irreducible cubic $f(x) = x^3 - x - 1 \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$. It is irr
educible over $\mathbb{Q}$ (no rational roots, since $\pm 1$ are not roots). Its
 discriminant is
$$\Delta = -4(-1)^3 - 27(-1)^2 = 4 - 27 = -23,$$
which is not a square in $\mathbb{Q}$. Therefore the Galois group of $f$ is $S_3
$, and the splitting field $K$ of $f$ satisfies $[K:\mathbb{Q}] = |S_3| = 6$.

Concretely, if $\alpha$ is a root of $f$ and $\beta = \sqrt{-23}$, then $K = \ma
thbb{Q}(\alpha, \beta)$, with $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}] = 3$ and $[\mathb
b{Q}(\beta):\mathbb{Q}] = 2$. Since $\gcd(3,2)=1$, we get $[K:\mathbb{Q}] = 6$.

**Step 2: Extracting a degree 6 irreducible polynomial with Galois group $S_3$.*
*

Since $\text{char}(\mathbb{Q}) = 0$, the extension $K/\mathbb{Q}$ is separable,
and by the Primitive Element Theorem there exists $\theta \in K$ with $\mathbb{Q
}(\theta) = K$. For instance, $\theta = \alpha + \beta$ is a primitive element (
by the theorem, $\alpha + c\beta$ is primitive for all but finitely many $c \in
\mathbb{Q}$; $c = 1$ works here).

Let $g(x) \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ be the minimal polynomial of $\theta$ over $\mathbb
{Q}$. Then:
- $g$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$,
- $\deg g = [\mathbb{Q}(\theta):\mathbb{Q}] = [K:\mathbb{Q}] = 6$.

**Step 3: The Galois group of $g$ is $S_3$.**

The roots of $g$ are the conjugates $\sigma(\theta)$ for $\sigma \in \operatorna
me{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$. Since $\theta$ is a primitive element, the map $\sigma \
mapsto \sigma(\theta)$ is injective: if $\sigma(\theta) = \tau(\theta)$, then $\
sigma^{-1}\tau$ fixes $\theta$, hence fixes $\mathbb{Q}(\theta) = K$, so $\sigma
 = \tau$. Thus $g$ has exactly 6 distinct roots, all lying in $K$.

Since $K$ is a Galois extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ containing one root $\theta$ of
$g$, it contains all roots of $g$ (they are $\sigma(\theta)$ for $\sigma \in \op
eratorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$). Therefore the splitting field of $g$ is contain
ed in $K$. Conversely, the splitting field contains $\theta$, hence contains $\m
athbb{Q}(\theta) = K$. So the splitting field of $g$ is exactly $K$, and
$$\operatorname{Gal}(g) = \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_3.$$

**Step 4: The action is the regular representation.**

The 6 roots of $g$ are $\{\sigma(\theta) : \sigma \in S_3\}$, and $S_3$ acts on
them by $\tau \cdot \sigma(\theta) = (\tau\sigma)(\theta)$, which is the left-re
gular action of $S_3$ on itself. This action is transitive (consistent with $g$
being irreducible of degree 6), and the point stabilizer is trivial (order $6/6
= 1$), as expected.

**Conclusion.** The minimal polynomial $g(x)$ of any primitive element $\theta$
of the splitting field of $x^3 - x - 1$ is a degree 6 irreducible polynomial in
$\mathbb{Q}[x]$ whose Galois group is isomorphic to $S_3$. Therefore, such a pol
ynomial exists. $\blacksquare$
