## Proof

Let $S = \{s_1, s_2, \ldots, s_n\}$ with $n = |S|$. We seek the arithmetic mean
of $D_A$ over all non-empty subsets $A \subseteq S$:

$$M = \frac{1}{2^n - 1} \sum_{\substack{A \subseteq S \\ A \neq \varnothing}} D_
A = \frac{1}{2^n - 1} \sum_{\substack{A \subseteq S \\ A \neq \varnothing}} \fra
c{1}{|A|} \sum_{a \in A} a.$$

**Step 1: Swap the order of summation.**

$$\sum_{\substack{A \subseteq S \\ A \neq \varnothing}} \frac{1}{|A|} \sum_{a \i
n A} a = \sum_{a \in S} a \cdot \left( \sum_{\substack{A \subseteq S \\ A \ni a}
} \frac{1}{|A|} \right).$$

**Step 2: Evaluate the inner sum for a fixed element $a \in S$.**

For a fixed $a$, the subsets $A$ containing $a$ with $|A| = k$ are formed by cho
osing $k-1$ additional elements from the remaining $n-1$ elements, giving $\bino
m{n-1}{k-1}$ subsets. Thus:

$$\sum_{\substack{A \subseteq S \\ A \ni a}} \frac{1}{|A|} = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \fra
c{1}{k}\binom{n-1}{k-1}.$$

**Step 3: Simplify using a binomial identity.**

Setting $j = k-1$ (so $k = j+1$, $j$ from $0$ to $n-1$):

$$\frac{1}{j+1}\binom{n-1}{j} = \frac{(n-1)!}{(j+1)!\,(n-1-j)!} = \frac{1}{n}\cd
ot\frac{n!}{(j+1)!\,(n-1-j)!} = \frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{j+1}.$$

Therefore:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{1}{k}\binom{n-1}{k-1} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom
{n}{j+1} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\binom{n}{m} = \frac{1}{n}(2^n - 1).$$

**Step 4: Combine.**

$$\sum_{\substack{A \subseteq S \\ A \neq \varnothing}} D_A = \sum_{a \in S} a \
cdot \frac{2^n - 1}{n} = \frac{2^n - 1}{n}\sum_{a \in S} a.$$

**Step 5: Compute the arithmetic mean.**

$$M = \frac{1}{2^n - 1} \cdot \frac{2^n - 1}{n}\sum_{a \in S} a = \frac{1}{n}\su
m_{a \in S} a = D_S.$$

 $1, 2, 3, \tfrac{3}{2}, 2, \tfrac{5}{2}, 2$, summing to $14$. The mean is $14/7
 = 2 = \tfrac{1+2+3}{3} = D_S$. ✓
