## Solution

The surface is the paraboloid $z = x^2 + y^2$ restricted to the first-octant pat
ch $x>0,\, y>0,\, z<r^2$, i.e. the parameter domain is the open quarter disk $D_
r = \{(x,y): x>0,\, y>0,\, x^2+y^2<r^2\}$.

### Gaussian curvature of $z = f(x,y)$

For a graph $z=f(x,y)$, the first and second fundamental forms give

$$K = \frac{f_{xx}f_{yy}-f_{xy}^2}{(1+f_x^2+f_y^2)^2}, \qquad dA = \sqrt{1+f_x^2
+f_y^2}\;dx\,dy.$$

With $f(x,y)=x^2+y^2$: $f_x=2x,\; f_y=2y,\; f_{xx}=f_{yy}=2,\; f_{xy}=0$, so

$$K = \frac{4}{(1+4x^2+4y^2)^2}, \qquad dA = \sqrt{1+4x^2+4y^2}\;dx\,dy.$$

Therefore

$$K\,dA = \frac{4}{(1+4x^2+4y^2)^{3/2}}\;dx\,dy.$$

### Setting up the integral

$$\int_{M_r} K\,dA = \iint_{D_r} \frac{4}{(1+4x^2+4y^2)^{3/2}}\;dx\,dy.$$

Switch to polar coordinates $x=\rho\cos\theta,\; y=\rho\sin\theta$, with $\rho\i
n(0,r)$, $\theta\in(0,\pi/2)$, and $x^2+y^2=\rho^2$:

$$\int_{M_r} K\,dA = \int_0^{\pi/2}\!\!\int_0^r \frac{4\rho}{(1+4\rho^2)^{3/2}}\
;d\rho\,d\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}\int_0^r \frac{4\rho}{(1+4\rho^2)^{3/2}}\;d\rho.$
$

### Evaluating the radial integral

Let $u = 1+4\rho^2$, $du = 8\rho\,d\rho$, so $4\rho\,d\rho = \tfrac{1}{2}du$:

$$\int_0^r \frac{4\rho}{(1+4\rho^2)^{3/2}}\;d\rho = \frac{1}{2}\int_1^{1+4r^2} u
^{-3/2}\,du = \frac{1}{2}\left[-2u^{-1/2}\right]_1^{1+4r^2} = 1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt
{1+4r^2}}.$$

### Result

$$\boxed{\int_{M_r} K\,dA = \frac{\pi}{2}\left(1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+4r^2}}\right
)}.$$

### The limit

As $r\to\infty$, $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+4r^2}}\to 0$, hence

$$\boxed{\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{M_r} K\,dA = \frac{\pi}{2}}.$$

**Interpretation.** The full paraboloid $z=x^2+y^2$ has total Gaussian curvature
 $-\pi$ (it is conformally a disk; the curvature integral over the entire surfac
e equals $-\pi$ via $K\,dA = -d\Omega$ type reasoning). Our patch is one quarter
 of the full paraboloid (first quadrant), and the curvature is *positive* here (
the paraboloid is convex), so the quarter contributes $+\pi/2$ in the limit — co
nsistent with the computation.
