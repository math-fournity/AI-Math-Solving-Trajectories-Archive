Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every finite commutative ri
ng with unity has a single element $g$ such that every element is $f(g)$ for som
e $f \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$.

## Answer: **No**

## Proof

We exhibit a counterexample: $R = \mathbb{F}_2 \times \mathbb{F}_2 \times \mathb
b{F}_2$.

This is a finite commutative ring with unity $(1,1,1)$, having $|R| = 8$ element
s and characteristic $2$.

**Claim:** There is no $g \in R$ such that $\{f(g) : f \in \mathbb{Z}[x]\} = R$.

**Proof of claim.** Let $g = (g_1, g_2, g_3) \in R$ be arbitrary. Since each $g_
i \in \mathbb{F}_2 = \{0, 1\}$, and there are 3 coordinates but only 2 possible
values, by the pigeonhole principle at least two coordinates are equal. Without
loss of generality, say $g_i = g_j$ for some $i \neq j$.

Since $R$ has characteristic $2$, the evaluation map $\mathbb{Z}[x] \to R$ facto
rs through $\mathbb{F}_2[x]$. Thus:
$$\mathbb{Z}[g] = \{f(g) : f \in \mathbb{Z}[x]\} = \{f(g) : f \in \mathbb{F}_2[x
]\}.$$

For any $f \in \mathbb{F}_2[x]$, the $i$-th and $j$-th coordinates of $f(g)$ are
 $f(g_i)$ and $f(g_j)$ respectively. Since $g_i = g_j$, we have $f(g_i) = f(g_j)
$ for all $f$. Therefore every element of $\mathbb{Z}[g]$ has equal $i$-th and $
j$-th coordinates:
$$\mathbb{Z}[g] \subseteq \{r \in R : r_i = r_j\}.$$

The set $\{r \in R : r_i = r_j\}$ has exactly $4$ elements (two free coordinates
, each in $\mathbb{F}_2$), while $|R| = 8$. Hence $\mathbb{Z}[g] \neq R$.

Since $g$ was arbitrary, no single element generates $R$ as a $\mathbb{Z}$-algeb
ra. $\square$

**Generalization.** The same argument works for $\mathbb{F}_p^m$ whenever $m > p
$: by pigeonhole, any $g = (g_1, \ldots, g_m) \in \mathbb{F}_p^m$ must have two
equal coordinates, forcing $\mathbb{Z}[g]$ into a proper subring. This shows the
 phenomenon is not isolated.

Therefore, not every finite commutative ring with unity admits a single element
whose integer-coefficient polynomial evaluations cover the entire ring.
