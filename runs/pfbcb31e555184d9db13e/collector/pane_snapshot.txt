## Solution

**Convention.** We use $\hat f(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,e^{-i\omega
 x}\,dx$, with inverse $f(x)=\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\int\hat f(\omega)\,e^{i\omega x}\,d
\omega$. Two standard facts we use:
- Gaussian: $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-ax^2}e^{-i\omega x}dx=\sqr
t{\pi/a}\;e^{-\omega^2/(4a)}$ $\;(a>0)$.
- Derivative/multiplication: $\widehat{x\,f(x)}(\omega)=i\,\tfrac{d}{d\omega}\ha
t f(\omega)$, and $\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\int\omega\hat F(\omega)e^{i\omega x}d\omega=-
i\,F'(x)$.

### (a) Fourier transforms of $f$ and $g$

**For $g(x)=e^{-x^2}$:** apply the Gaussian formula with $a=1$:
$$\hat g(\omega)=\sqrt{\pi}\;e^{-\omega^2/4}.$$

**For $f(x)=x\,e^{-x^2/2}$:** let $h(x)=e^{-x^2/2}$, so $f(x)=x\,h(x)$. First,
$$\hat h(\omega)=\sqrt{\pi/(1/2)}\;e^{-\omega^2/(4\cdot 1/2)}=\sqrt{2\pi}\;e^{-\
omega^2/2}.$$
Then using $\widehat{x\,h} = i\,\tfrac{d}{d\omega}\hat h$:
$$\hat f(\omega)=i\,\frac{d}{d\omega}\!\left[\sqrt{2\pi}\,e^{-\omega^2/2}\right]
=i\sqrt{2\pi}\,(-\omega)\,e^{-\omega^2/2}=-i\sqrt{2\pi}\,\omega\,e^{-\omega^2/2}
.$$

$$\boxed{\hat f(\omega)=-i\sqrt{2\pi}\,\omega\,e^{-\omega^2/2},\qquad \hat g(\om
ega)=\sqrt{\pi}\,e^{-\omega^2/4}.}$$

### (b) Fourier transform of $f*g$

By the convolution theorem ($\widehat{f*g}=\hat f\cdot\hat g$):
$$\widehat{f*g}(\omega)=\left(-i\sqrt{2\pi}\,\omega\,e^{-\omega^2/2}\right)\!\le
ft(\sqrt{\pi}\,e^{-\omega^2/4}\right)=-i\sqrt{2}\,\pi\,\omega\,e^{-3\omega^2/4}.
$$

$$\boxed{\widehat{f*g}(\omega)=-i\sqrt{2}\,\pi\,\omega\,e^{-3\omega^2/4}.}$$

### (c) What is $f*g$?

We invert $\widehat{f*g}$. Let $F(x)$ be the inverse FT of $e^{-3\omega^2/4}$ (a
 Gaussian with $a=3/4$):
$$F(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{3/4}}\;e^{-x^2/(4\cdot 3/4)}=\frac{1}{2\pi
}\cdot\frac{2\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{3}}\,e^{-x^2/3}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3\pi}}\,e^{-x^2/3}
.$$

Using $\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\int\omega\hat F(\omega)e^{i\omega x}d\omega=-i\,F'(x)$, t
he inverse FT of $\omega\,e^{-3\omega^2/4}$ is
$$-i\,F'(x)=-i\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{3\pi}}\!\left(-\frac{2x}{3}\right)e^{-x^2/3}=\
frac{2ix}{3\sqrt{3\pi}}\,e^{-x^2/3}.$$

Multiplying by the prefactor $-i\sqrt{2}\,\pi$:
$$(f*g)(x)=(-i\sqrt{2}\,\pi)\cdot\frac{2ix}{3\sqrt{3\pi}}\,e^{-x^2/3}=(-i)(i)\,\
frac{2\sqrt{2}\,\pi\,x}{3\sqrt{3\pi}}\,e^{-x^2/3}=\frac{2x}{3}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{
3}}\,e^{-x^2/3}.$$

**Verification by direct convolution.** $f*g(x)=\int t\,e^{-t^2/2}\,e^{-(x-t)^2}
\,dt$. The exponent combines as
$$-\frac{t^2}{2}-(x-t)^2=-\tfrac{3}{2}\bigl(t-\tfrac{2x}{3}\bigr)^2-\frac{x^2}{3
}.$$
With $u=t-\tfrac{2x}{3}$:
$$f*g(x)=e^{-x^2/3}\int\!\bigl(u+\tfrac{2x}{3}\bigr)e^{-3u^2/2}\,du=e^{-x^2/3}\!
\left[0+\tfrac{2x}{3}\sqrt{\tfrac{2\pi}{3}}\right]=\frac{2x}{3}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}
{3}}\,e^{-x^2/3},$$
confirming the inversion. (The odd integrand $u\,e^{-3u^2/2}$ vanishes; the Gaus
sian integral gives $\sqrt{2\pi/3}$.)

$$\boxed{(f*g)(x)=\dfrac{2x}{3}\sqrt{\dfrac{2\pi}{3}}\;e^{-x^2/3}.}$$
