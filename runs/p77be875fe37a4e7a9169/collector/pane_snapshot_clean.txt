### The open conjecture

**`infinitely_many_wilson_primes`**: This asserts `Set.Infinite {p : ℕ | IsWilso
nPrime p}`.

This is an **open research problem**. A Wilson prime is a prime `p` with `p^2 ∣
(p-1)! + 1`. By Wilson's theorem, every prime `p` satisfies `p ∣ (p-1)! + 1`, so
 the Wilson-prime condition is the stronger requirement that the quotient `((p-1
)!+1)/p` be divisible by `p` again.

Heuristic considerations: under the standard random-model heuristic, for a prime
 `p` the value `((p-1)!+1)/p mod p` is treated as uniformly distributed in `{0,…
,p-1}`, giving a probability `≈ 1/p` that `p` is Wilson. The expected count of W
ilson primes up to `x` is then

$$\sum_{p \le x} \frac{1}{p} \sim \log\log x,$$

which diverges, suggesting infinitely many — but only logarithmically slowly, co
nsistent with the extreme scarcity observed (only `5, 13, 563` known below `5×10
^8`, per Costa–Gerbicz–Harvey). No unconditional proof of infinitude (or even of
 a fourth Wilson prime) is known. The heuristic gives the conjectured asymptotic
 count

$$\#\{p \le x : p \text{ Wilson}\} \sim \log\log x,$$

but this remains unproved.
