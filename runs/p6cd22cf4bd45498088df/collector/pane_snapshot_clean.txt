## Proof

The statement is **false**.

**Key observation.** For any $n \times n$ real matrix $A$, the powers $\{A^p : p
 \geq 1\}$ all lie in the subalgebra $\mathbb{R}[A] \subseteq M_n(\mathbb{R})$ c
onsisting of all polynomials in $A$. By the Cayley–Hamilton theorem, $A$ satisfi
es its own characteristic polynomial, which has degree $n$. Consequently the min
imal polynomial $m_A(x)$ of $A$ has degree $d \leq n$, and

$$\mathbb{R}[A] = \operatorname{span}\{I,\, A,\, A^2,\, \dots,\, A^{d-1}\},$$

so $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{R}[A] = d \leq n$.

Since every $A^p$ (with $p \geq 1$) is an element of $\mathbb{R}[A]$, we have

$$\operatorname{span}\{A^p : p \geq 1\} \subseteq \mathbb{R}[A],$$

and therefore

$$\dim\bigl(\operatorname{span}\{A^p : p \geq 1\}\bigr) \leq \dim \mathbb{R}[A]
\leq n.$$

On the other hand, $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} M_n(\mathbb{R}) = n^2$. For every $n \geq
2$ we have $n^2 > n$, so

$$\dim\bigl(\operatorname{span}\{A^p : p \geq 1\}\bigr) \leq n < n^2 = \dim M_n(
\mathbb{R}).$$

A proper subspace can never span the whole space. Hence for **no** $n \geq 2$ do
es there exist a matrix $A$ with the required property. The statement is false.
$\blacksquare$
