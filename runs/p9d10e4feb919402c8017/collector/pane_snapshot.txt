## Proof

Place the regular 2012-gon inscribed in a circle, with the technician at vertex
0. The cables run from vertex 0 to vertices 2, 3, …, 2009 (skipping the two adja
cent vertices 1 and 2011). This triangulates the polygon into triangles

$$(0,\, i,\, i+1), \quad i = 1, 2, \dots, 2010,$$

giving 2010 triangles total.

**Inscribed-angle computation.** Vertex $k$ lies at central angle $2\pi k/2012$.
 By the inscribed angle theorem, each angle of triangle $(0, i, i{+}1)$ equals h
alf the central angle of the opposite arc:

- **At vertex 0** (opposite side $i$–$i{+}1$, one polygon edge): $\;\theta_0 = \
pi/2012$.
- **At vertex $i$** (opposite arc $0$–$i{+}1$ not containing $i$, length $2011-i
$ edges): $\;\theta_i = \pi(2011-i)/2012$.
- **At vertex $i{+}1$** (opposite arc $0$–$i$ not containing $i{+}1$, length $i$
 edges): $\;\theta_{i+1} = \pi i/2012$.

Check: $\pi/2012 + \pi(2011-i)/2012 + \pi i/2012 = \pi$. ✓

**Obtuseness.** Since $\theta_0 = \pi/2012 < \pi/2$ always, only $\theta_i$ or $
\theta_{i+1}$ can be obtuse, and since they sum to $\pi(2011)/2012 < \pi$, at mo
st one is obtuse per triangle.

- $\theta_i > \pi/2 \iff 2011 - i > 1006 \iff i \le 1004$.
- $\theta_{i+1} > \pi/2 \iff i > 1006 \iff i \ge 1007$.
- $i = 1005$: $\theta_i = \pi\cdot1006/2012 = \pi/2$ (right angle).
- $i = 1006$: $\theta_{i+1} = \pi\cdot1006/2012 = \pi/2$ (right angle).

**Count.**

- $i = 1, \dots, 1004$: obtuse at vertex $i$ → **1004** triangles.
- $i = 1007, \dots, 2010$: obtuse at vertex $i{+}1$ → $2010 - 1007 + 1 = $ **100
4** triangles.

Total obtuse triangles $= 1004 + 1004 = \boxed{2008}$.
