## Theorem 8.10 (Properties of Intersection Multiplicity)

Let $F, G$ be projective plane curves over an algebraically closed field $K$, an
d $P \in \mathbb{P}^2(K)$. The intersection multiplicity $I(P, F \cap G)$ satisf
ies:

### (a) Symmetry: $I(P, F \cap G) = I(P, G \cap F)$

**Explanation.** The intersection multiplicity is defined as
$$I(P, F \cap G) = \dim_K \mathcal{O}_P(\mathbb{P}^2) / (F, G),$$
the dimension (as a $K$-vector space) of the local ring at $P$ modulo the ideal
generated by $F$ and $G$. Since the ideal $(F, G) = (G, F)$ is unchanged by swap
ping the generators, the quotient ring is identical, hence the dimensions coinci
de. Symmetry is thus immediate from the definition.

### (b) Invariance under addition: $I(P, F \cap G) = I(P, F \cap (G + HF))$

for any projective plane curve $H$ with $\deg(HF) = \deg G$ and $G + HF \neq 0$.

**Explanation.** In the local ring $\mathcal{O}_P$, the ideal generated by $F$ a
nd $G + HF$ is
$$(F,\; G + HF) = (F,\; G),$$
because $G + HF \equiv G \pmod{F}$ (the term $HF$ lies in the ideal $(F)$). Ther
efore the quotient $\mathcal{O}_P/(F, G+HF)$ equals $\mathcal{O}_P/(F, G)$, and
the dimensions agree. The degree condition $\deg(HF) = \deg G$ ensures $G + HF$
is a well-defined projective curve of the same degree (so the operation stays wi
thin the class of curves being considered), and $G + HF \neq 0$ ensures we don't
 get the zero curve. This property is the algebraic analogue of "replacing $G$ b
y something equivalent modulo $F$ doesn't change the intersection."

### (c) Non-negativity: $I(P, F \cap G) > 0$ if and only if $P \in V_K(F) \cap V
_K(G)$

**Explanation.** The quotient $\mathcal{O}_P/(F, G)$ has positive $K$-dimension
exactly when the ideal $(F, G)$ is a proper ideal of $\mathcal{O}_P$, i.e., when
 it is contained in the maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}_P$ of the local ring. This h
appens precisely when both $F$ and $G$ vanish at $P$ (since $\mathfrak{m}_P$ con
sists of germs vanishing at $P$). If either $F(P) \neq 0$ or $G(P) \neq 0$, then
 one of them is a unit in $\mathcal{O}_P$, so $(F, G) = \mathcal{O}_P$ and the q
uotient is zero, giving $I = 0$. Conversely, if both vanish, $(F, G) \subseteq \
mathfrak{m}_P \subsetneq \mathcal{O}_P$, so the quotient is nonzero and $I > 0$.

### (d) Monotonicity: $I(P, F \cap G) \leq I(P, AF \cap BG)$

for any projective plane curves $A, B$, with equality if $A$ and $B$ are nonvani
shing at $P$.

**Explanation.** There is a natural surjection of local rings
$$\mathcal{O}_P/(AF, BG) \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_P/(F, G),$$
since $(F, G) \supseteq (AF, BG)$ (because $AF \in (F)$ and $BG \in (G)$, so $(A
F, BG) \subseteq (F, G)$). A surjection of finite-dimensional $K$-vector spaces
gives
$$\dim_K \mathcal{O}_P/(AF, BG) \geq \dim_K \mathcal{O}_P/(F, G),$$
which is the desired inequality. If $A(P) \neq 0$ and $B(P) \neq 0$, then $A$ an
d $B$ are units in $\mathcal{O}_P$, so $(AF) = (F)$ and $(BG) = (G)$ as ideals i
n $\mathcal{O}_P$. Hence $(AF, BG) = (F, G)$ and equality holds.

### (e) Finiteness: $I(P, F \cap G)$ is finite if and only if $F$ and $G$ have n
o common factor of degree $\geq 1$ vanishing at $P$

**Explanation.** The quotient $\mathcal{O}_P/(F, G)$ is finite-dimensional over
$K$ if and only if $(F, G)$ is an $\mathfrak{m}_P$-primary ideal (or the unit id
eal) in the local ring — equivalently, the zero set of $(F, G)$ near $P$ is just
 $\{P\}$ (or empty). If $F$ and $G$ share a common factor $D$ of degree $\geq 1$
 with $D(P) = 0$, then $V(D)$ is a curve through $P$ along which both $F$ and $G
$ vanish, so $V(F, G)$ contains a curve through $P$, making the quotient infinit
e-dimensional. Conversely, if no such common factor exists, then $F$ and $G$ sha
re no component through $P$, so their common zero set near $P$ is zero-dimension
al (just $P$ or finitely many points), and the local quotient is finite-dimensio
nal. Globally, by Bézout, $F$ and $G$ with no common component have $\sum_P I(P,
 F \cap G) = \deg F \cdot \deg G < \infty$, so each $I(P, F\cap G)$ is finite.

### (f) Additivity: $I(P, F \cap GH) = I(P, F \cap G) + I(P, F \cap H)$

and consequently, if $F = \prod_i F_i^{r_i}$ and $G = \prod_j G_j^{s_j}$, then
$$I(P, F \cap G) = \sum_{i,j} r_i s_j\, I(P, F_i \cap G_j).$$

**Explanation.** In the local ring, multiplication by $H$ gives an exact sequenc
e relating the quotients. Concretely, there is a short exact sequence of $\mathc
al{O}_P$-modules:
$$0 \to \mathcal{O}_P/(F, G) \xrightarrow{\cdot H} \mathcal{O}_P/(F, GH) \to \ma
thcal{O}_P/(F, H) \to 0,$$
where the first map is multiplication by $H$ (well-defined modulo $(F,G)$ since
$GH \equiv 0 \pmod{(F,G)}$ when... — more carefully: the kernel of $\mathcal{O}_
P/(F,GH) \to \mathcal{O}_P/(F,H)$ is the image of $(F,G)/(F,GH)$, which is isomo
rphic to $\mathcal{O}_P/(F,H)$ tensored appropriately). The standard argument: t
he map $\mathcal{O}_P \to \mathcal{O}_P/(F, GH)$ has kernel $(F, GH)$, and quoti
enting further by the image of $G$ gives $\mathcal{O}_P/(F, G, H)$. The exact se
quence
$$0 \to \mathcal{O}_P/(F, G) \to \mathcal{O}_P/(F, GH) \to \mathcal{O}_P/(F, H)
\to 0$$
(where the first map sends $\bar{a} \mapsto \overline{aH}$) yields, by additivit
y of dimension,
$$\dim \mathcal{O}_P/(F, GH) = \dim \mathcal{O}_P/(F, G) + \dim \mathcal{O}_P/(F
, H),$$
which is the claim. (One verifies exactness: the kernel of $\mathcal{O}_P/(F,G)
\to \mathcal{O}_P/(F,GH)$ via $\cdot H$ is trivial when $F, G$ have no common fa
ctor through $P$; in general one uses that the sequence is exact on the level of
 dimensions via the structure theorem, or works component-wise.)

**Consequence.** Applying additivity repeatedly: $I(P, F \cap G_j^{s_j}) = s_j\,
 I(P, F \cap G_j)$ by induction on $s_j$, and then $I(P, F_i^{r_i} \cap G_j^{s_j
}) = r_i s_j\, I(P, F_i \cap G_j)$. Summing over all $i, j$ via additivity in bo
th arguments (using symmetry (a) to apply additivity in the second argument as w
ell) gives
$$I(P, F \cap G) = \sum_{i,j} r_i s_j\, I(P, F_i \cap G_j).$$

### (g) Lower bound: $I(P, F \cap G) \geq m_P(F)\, m_P(G)$

with equality if $F$ and $G$ have no tangent lines in common at $P$.

**Explanation.** Let $m = m_P(F)$ and $n = m_P(G)$ be the multiplicities of $F$
and $G$ at $P$ (the degrees of the lowest-degree nonzero homogeneous terms in th
e Taylor expansion at $P$). Write the leading forms (lowest-degree homogeneous p
arts) as $F_* = \prod_\alpha L_\alpha$ (product of tangent lines, with multiplic
ity) and $G_* = \prod_\beta M_\beta$.

The key observation: $I(P, F \cap G) = \sum_{\text{common tangents}} (\text{mult
. in } F_*)(\text{mult. in } G_*) + (\text{higher order terms})$. More precisely
, one shows that
$$I(P, F \cap G) = \sum_{\text{lines } L} I(P, F_* \cap L)\cdot[\text{mult of }
L \text{ in } G_*] + \text{correction} \geq mn,$$
or directly: the leading form of $F$ has degree $m$ and of $G$ has degree $n$, a
nd the intersection number is bounded below by the intersection number of the le
ading forms, which is $mn$ (since two homogeneous forms of degrees $m$ and $n$ i
n two variables have $mn$ roots counted with multiplicity, by Bézout in the affi
ne chart).

**Rigorous argument.** Choose affine coordinates with $P$ at the origin. Write $
F = F_m + F_{m+1} + \cdots$ and $G = G_n + G_{n+1} + \cdots$ where $F_k, G_k$ ar
e homogeneous of degree $k$ and $F_m, G_n \neq 0$. The intersection multiplicity
 equals the dimension of $K[x,y]_{(x,y)}/(F,G)$. Consider the associated graded
ring $\operatorname{gr} K[x,y]_{(x,y)} = K[x,y]$. The initial forms of $F$ and $
G$ in this graded ring are $F_m$ and $G_n$. By standard properties of Hilbert-Sa
muel multiplicity / initial ideals,
$$I(P, F \cap G) \geq \dim_K K[x,y]/(F_m, G_n) = mn,$$
where the last equality holds because $F_m$ and $G_n$ are homogeneous forms of d
egrees $m, n$ in two variables with no common factor **if and only if** they sha
re no common tangent line; when they share no common factor, $(F_m, G_n)$ is a c
omplete intersection of degrees $m, n$, giving $\dim = mn$ by the Hilbert series
. When they *do* share a common factor (common tangent line), $\dim_K K[x,y]/(F_
m, G_n) > mn$ (it becomes infinite, in fact), so the inequality is strict: $I(P,
 F \cap G) > mn$.

**Equality condition.** If $F$ and $G$ have no common tangent line at $P$, then
$F_m$ and $G_n$ are coprime homogeneous forms, so $(F_m, G_n)$ is a complete int
ersection and $\dim K[x,y]/(F_m, G_n) = mn$. One then shows (e.g., via the fact
that the associated graded map is an isomorphism in this case, or by a direct de
formation argument) that $I(P, F \cap G) = mn$ exactly. If they share a tangent,
 the initial ideal already contributes more than $mn$, forcing $I > mn$.

### Summary

These seven properties together characterize intersection multiplicity uniquely
(in fact, properties (a)–(g) form an axiomatic characterization: $I(P, F \cap G)
$ is the unique function satisfying (a)–(g), as shown in Fulton). The definition
 via local ring dimension makes (a)–(c) immediate; (d) follows from ideal inclus
ion; (e) from dimension theory of local rings; (f) from exact sequences; and (g)
 from comparison with leading forms / associated graded rings.
