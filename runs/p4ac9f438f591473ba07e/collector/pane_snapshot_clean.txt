No. $A$ is not necessarily compact.

**Claim.** The operator $A$ is compact if and only if $\alpha_n \to 0$. Since $\
{\alpha_n\}$ is only assumed bounded, compactness need not hold.

**Proof of the claim.**

($\Leftarrow$) Suppose $\alpha_n \to 0$. Define the finite-rank operators
$$A_N x = \sum_{n=1}^{N} \alpha_n \langle x, u_n\rangle u_n.$$
Each $A_N$ has finite rank (its range is $\operatorname{span}\{u_1,\dots,u_N\}$)
, hence is compact. For any $x$ with $\|x\|\le 1$, by Parseval $\sum_n |\langle
x,u_n\rangle|^2 \le 1$, so
$$\|(A - A_N)x\|^2 = \sum_{n>N} \alpha_n^2 |\langle x,u_n\rangle|^2 \le \Big(\su
p_{n>N} \alpha_n^2\Big)\sum_{n>N}|\langle x,u_n\rangle|^2 \le \sup_{n>N}\alpha_n
^2 \xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{} 0.$$
Thus $\|A - A_N\| \le \sup_{n>N}|\alpha_n| \to 0$. Being the norm-limit of compa
ct operators, $A$ is compact.

($\Rightarrow$) Suppose $A$ is compact. The vectors $u_n$ satisfy $\|u_n\|=1$ an
d $u_n \rightharpoonup 0$ weakly (since $\langle u_n, y\rangle \to 0$ for every
$y$ by Parseval/Bessel). A compact operator maps weakly convergent sequences to
norm-convergent sequences, so $Au_n \to 0$ in norm. But $Au_n = \alpha_n u_n$, h
ence $\|Au_n\| = |\alpha_n| \to 0$. So $\alpha_n \to 0$.

**Counterexample to the original question.** Take $\alpha_n = 1$ for every $n$.
Then $Ax = \sum_n \langle x,u_n\rangle u_n = x$ for all $x\in X$ (Parseval), i.e
. $A = I$, the identity. The sequence $\{\alpha_n\}=\{1\}$ is bounded, yet $I$ i
s not compact on the infinite-dimensional space $X$: the bounded sequence $\{u_n
\}$ has no norm-convergent subsequence since $\|u_m - u_n\|^2 = 2$ for $m\neq n$
.

Therefore $A$ is **not** necessarily compact; it is compact precisely when $\alp
ha_n \to 0$.
