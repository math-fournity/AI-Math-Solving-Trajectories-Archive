## Solution

**Claim.** The spectral radius formula holds for normal elements in a $C^*$-alge
bra. Concretely, if $A$ is a $C^*$-algebra and $a\in A$ is normal (i.e. $a^*a=aa
^*$), then
$$
\|a\| \;=\; r(a) \;:=\; \sup\{|\lambda|:\lambda\in\sigma(a)\}.
$$

We prove this below.

---

### Preliminaries

We work in a unital $C^*$-algebra $A$ (if $A$ is non-unital, we pass to the unit
ization; the norm and spectrum of $a$ are unchanged). Recall two standard facts.

**Fact 1 (Gelfand–Beurling spectral radius formula).** In any unital Banach alge
bra, for every element $b$,
$$
r(b)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|b^{n}\|^{1/n}=\inf_{n\ge 1}\|b^{n}\|^{1/n}.
$$
In particular, the limit exists and equals the infimum, so $r(b)$ can be compute
d along any subsequence of $\{n\}$.

**Fact 2 ($C^*$-identity).** For every $b\in A$,
$$
\|b^*b\|=\|b\|^2.
$$
Consequently, for a self-adjoint element $h=h^*$, applying the $C^*$-identity to
 $b=h$ gives $\|h^2\|=\|h^*h\|=\|h\|^2$, and by induction $\|h^{2^k}\|=\|h\|^{2^
k}$ for all $k\ge 0$.

---

### Key lemma: $\|a^{2^k}\|=\|a\|^{2^k}$ for normal $a$

It suffices to prove $\|a^2\|=\|a\|^2$; the general case then follows by inducti
on, since a power of a normal element is again normal (see below).

Compute using the $C^*$-identity and normality:
$$
\|a^2\|^2 \;=\; \|(a^2)^*\,a^2\| \;=\; \|a^{*2}\,a^2\|.
$$

Because $a$ is normal ($a^*a=aa^*$), we may commute $a^*$ past $a$:
$$
a^{*2}\,a^2 \;=\; a^*(a^*a)\,a \;=\; a^*(aa^*)\,a \;=\; (a^*a)(a^*a) \;=\; (a^*a
)^2.
$$

The element $h:=a^*a$ is self-adjoint, so by Fact 2, $\|h^2\|=\|h\|^2$. Therefor
e
$$
\|a^2\|^2 \;=\; \|(a^*a)^2\| \;=\; \|a^*a\|^2 \;=\; (\|a\|^2)^2 \;=\; \|a\|^4,
$$
where the last equality uses the $C^*$-identity $\|a^*a\|=\|a\|^2$. Taking squar
e roots (norms are non-negative) yields
$$
\|a^2\| \;=\; \|a\|^2.
$$

**Induction.** Suppose $\|a^{2^k}\|=\|a\|^{2^k}$ for some $k\ge 0$. The element
$a^{2^k}$ is normal: indeed $a^{2^k}$ commutes with $a$, and by normality of $a$
 one checks $(a^{2^k})^* = (a^*)^{2^k}$ commutes with $a^{2^k}$ (both are polyno
mials in $a$ and $a^*$, which commute pairwise). Applying the base step with $a$
 replaced by the normal element $a^{2^k}$,
$$
\|a^{2^{k+1}}\| \;=\; \|(a^{2^k})^2\| \;=\; \|a^{2^k}\|^2 \;=\; \|a\|^{2^{k+1}}.
$$
Thus $\|a^{2^k}\|=\|a\|^{2^k}$ for all $k\ge 0$.

---

### Conclusion: $r(a)=\|a\|$

By the Gelfand–Beurling formula (Fact 1), the spectral radius may be computed al
ong the subsequence $n=2^k$:
$$
r(a) \;=\; \lim_{k\to\infty}\|a^{2^k}\|^{1/2^k}.
$$

Substituting the identity from the key lemma,
$$
r(a) \;=\; \lim_{k\to\infty}\bigl(\|a\|^{2^k}\bigr)^{1/2^k} \;=\; \lim_{k\to\inf
ty}\|a\| \;=\; \|a\|.
$$

Therefore
$$
\boxed{\,r(a)=\|a\|\,}
$$
for every normal element $a$ in a $C^*$-algebra.

---

### Remark (sharpness / necessity of normality)

The equality $r(a)=\|a\|$ is characteristic of normality in the following sense:
 it always holds that $r(a)\le\|a\|$, and the proof above shows that normality f
orces the powers $\|a^n\|^{1/n}$ to be *constant* (equal to $\|a\|$) rather than
 merely converging down to $r(a)$. For a non-normal element the inequality can b
e strict: e.g. in $M_2(\mathbb C)$, the nilpotent matrix $N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}$ has $r(N)=0$ but $\|N\|=1$. Thus normality is precisely the co
ndition under which the spectral radius *realizes* the norm, i.e. the spectral r
adius formula $\|a\|=r(a)$ holds.
