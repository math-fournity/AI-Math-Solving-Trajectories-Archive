## Proof

**Classical Abel's Theorem.** If $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n$ converges to $S$, the
n
$$\lim_{y \to 1^-} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n\, y^n = S.$$

**Setup.** We are given:
1. $f(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n x^n$ converges for $|x| < 1$ (so the radius o
f convergence is $\geq 1$, hence the series converges absolutely for $|x|<1$).
2. $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n a_n$ converges.

**Reduction to the standard form.** Define
$$b_n := (-1)^n a_n, \qquad n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$$

By hypothesis (2), $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} b_n$ converges. Consider the power serie
s
$$g(y) := \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} b_n\, y^n = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n a_n\, y^n.$
$

For $|y| < 1$, we have $g(y) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n (-y)^n = f(-y)$, and sinc
e $|-y| = |y| < 1$, this converges (absolutely). So $g$ is well-defined on $(-1,
1)$ and equals $f(-y)$ there.

**Applying Abel's theorem.** Since $\sum b_n$ converges, Abel's theorem gives:
$$\lim_{y \to 1^-} g(y) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} b_n = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n a
_n.$$

**Translating back.** Substituting $g(y) = f(-y)$ and setting $x = -y$:
- As $y \to 1^-$, we have $x = -y \to -1^+$ (i.e., $x$ approaches $-1$ from the
right, from within the interval $(-1, 1)$, which is the part of the real line in
side the disk of convergence).

Therefore:
$$\lim_{x \to -1^+} f(x) = \lim_{y \to 1^-} f(-y) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n a
_n.$$

**Interpretation of the limit.** The function $f(x) = \sum a_n x^n$ is defined (
by the power series) only for $|x| < 1$. On the real line, this is the interval
$(-1, 1)$, so $-1$ is a boundary point of the domain. The notation $\lim_{x \to
-1} f(x)$ is therefore naturally interpreted as the one-sided limit $\lim_{x \to
 -1^+} f(x)$—the only direction from which $f$ is defined. A two-sided limit is
neither meaningful nor required here, since $f$ need not be defined for $x < -1$
 (the series may diverge there).

**Conclusion.** The application of Abel's theorem is **valid**. The substitution
 $b_n = (-1)^n a_n$, $y = -x$ reduces the problem at the endpoint $x = -1$ to th
e standard Abel's theorem at $y = 1$, yielding
$$\lim_{x \to -1} f(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n a_n,$$
where the limit is understood as the radial (one-sided) limit from within the di
sk of convergence. $\blacksquare$
