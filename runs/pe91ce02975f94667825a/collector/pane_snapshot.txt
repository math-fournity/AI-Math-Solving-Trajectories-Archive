## Solution

**Step 1: Simplify the derivative.**

Let $u = x + y$. When differentiating with respect to $y$ (holding $x$ fixed), w
e have $\frac{\partial}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial}{\partial u}$. Therefore:

$$\frac{\partial^n}{\partial y^n}\, e^{-(x+y)^2} = \frac{d^n}{du^n}\, e^{-u^2}\B
igg|_{u=x+y}.$$

**Step 2: Substitute in the integral.**

Setting $u = x + y$ (so $dx = du$, and the limits $x \in (-\infty,\infty)$ map t
o $u \in (-\infty,\infty)$):

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\partial^n}{\partial y^n}\, e^{-(x+y)^2}\, dx =
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{d^n}{du^n}\, e^{-u^2}\, du.$$

The right-hand side is **independent of $y$**, so the limit as $y \to 0$ equals
this same value.

**Step 3: Evaluate the integral for $n \geq 1$.**

Every derivative of $e^{-u^2}$ has the form $P(u)\,e^{-u^2}$ for some polynomial
 $P$, hence vanishes at $u = \pm\infty$. By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus,
 for $n \geq 1$:

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{d^n}{du^n}\, e^{-u^2}\, du = \left[\frac{d^{n-1}
}{du^{n-1}}\, e^{-u^2}\right]_{-\infty}^{\infty} = 0 - 0 = 0.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\lim_{y \to 0} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\partial^n}{\partial y^n}\, e^{-(
x+y)^2}\, dx = \boxed{0}.$$
