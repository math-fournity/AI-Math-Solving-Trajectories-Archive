# Problem

An architect is designing a high-tech security complex consisting of three server wings. The total energy consumption of the complex, measured in kilowatt-hours, is represented by a six-digit perfect square integer $N$. 

The digital display for the complex's power grid is divided into three separate two-digit sections representing the load on each wing. These sections are derived from the six-digit value $N$ such that the first two digits represent the load on the Alpha Wing, the middle two digits represent the load on the Beta Wing, and the last two digits represent the load on the Gamma Wing.

The monitoring system provides the following specifications regarding the power distribution:
1. The energy load on the Beta Wing is exactly equal to the load on the Alpha Wing.
2. The load on the Gamma Wing is higher than the load on the Beta Wing, and the difference between these two loads is equal to a perfect square integer (which may be zero).

Calculate the sum of all possible six-digit square numbers $N$ that satisfy these power grid requirements.
