## Analysis of each candidate

### 1. $f(x) = x$ — WORKS (Bertrand's Postulate)

**Bertrand's Postulate** (proved by Chebyshev, 1850): For every real $x > 1$, th
ere exists a prime $p$ with $x < p < 2x$.

Taking $a = 1$ and $b = 1$: for all $x > 1$, there is a prime between $x$ and $x
 + 1 \cdot x = 2x$. ✓

### 2. $f(x) = \log(x)$ — DOES NOT WORK (Westzynthius's Theorem)

Let $p_n$ denote the $n$-th prime and $g_n = p_{n+1} - p_n$ the $n$-th prime gap
.

**Westzynthius's Theorem (1931):**
$$\limsup_{n \to \infty} \frac{g_n}{\ln p_n} = \infty$$

This means: for any constant $b > 0$, there exist infinitely many $n$ with $g_n
> b \ln p_n$. Setting $x = p_n$, there is no prime in $(x, \, x + b\ln x)$ for i
nfinitely many arbitrarily large $x$.

Therefore **no** constants $a, b$ can make $f(x) = \log(x)$ satisfy the conditio
n. ✗

### 3. $f(x) = \log\log(x)$ — DOES NOT WORK

Since $\log\log(x) = o(\log(x))$ as $x \to \infty$, for any $b > 0$ we have $b\l
og\log(x) < b\log(x)$ for all sufficiently large $x$. Since there exist prime ga
ps exceeding $b\log(x)$ (by Westzynthius), there certainly exist gaps exceeding
$b\log\log(x)$. ✗

### 4. $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$ — WORKS

We show there exist constants $a, b$ such that for all $x > a$, there is a prime
 in $(x, \, x + b\sqrt{x})$.

**Key ingredients:**

**(a) Prime Number Theorem:** $\pi(x) \sim \frac{x}{\ln x}$, so the average prim
e gap near $x$ is $\sim \ln x$.

**(b) Primes in short intervals (Baker–Harman–Pintz, 2001):** For all sufficient
ly large $x$, there exists a prime in $(x, \, x + x^{0.525})$. This unconditiona
lly proves that $f(x) = x^{0.525}$ works.

**(c) Under the Riemann Hypothesis:** The maximal prime gap satisfies $G(x) = O(
\sqrt{x}\,\ln x)$, so $f(x) = \sqrt{x}\,\ln x$ works, and more generally $f(x) =
 x^{1/2 + \varepsilon}$ works for any $\varepsilon > 0$.

**(d) Under Cramér's Conjecture:** The maximal prime gap satisfies $G(x) = O((\l
n x)^2)$. Since $(\ln x)^2 = o(\sqrt{x})$, this gives $G(x) = o(\sqrt{x})$, mean
ing there exists $b > 0$ and $a > 0$ such that for all $x > a$, $G(x) < b\sqrt{x
}$, i.e., there is always a prime in $(x, \, x + b\sqrt{x})$.

**The critical comparison:** The maximal prime gap grows at most as $O(x^{0.525}
)$ unconditionally, $O(\sqrt{x}\ln x)$ under RH, and $O((\ln x)^2)$ under Cramér
's conjecture. In all cases, the maximal gap is $o(\sqrt{x} \cdot (\ln x)^k)$ fo
r appropriate $k$, and under Cramér's conjecture it is $o(\sqrt{x})$ outright. S
ince $\sqrt{x}$ dominates $(\ln x)^2$ by an enormous margin:

$$\frac{(\ln x)^2}{\sqrt{x}} \to 0 \quad \text{as } x \to \infty$$

there exists a constant $b$ such that $b\sqrt{x} > (\ln x)^2$ for all large $x$,
 guaranteeing a prime in every interval $(x, x + b\sqrt{x})$.

The function $\sqrt{x}$ sits at the natural threshold: it is far larger than the
 conjectured maximal gap $(\ln x)^2$, yet far smaller than $x$. The PNT heuristi
c confirms this — the expected number of primes in $(x, x + b\sqrt{x})$ is $\fra
c{b\sqrt{x}}{\ln x} \to \infty$, and the fluctuations in prime counting (governe
d by the zeros of the zeta function) are of order $\sqrt{x}\,\mathrm{polylog}(x)
$, which is dominated by $b\sqrt{x}$ for sufficiently large $b$.
