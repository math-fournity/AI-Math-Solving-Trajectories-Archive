## Answer

**Yes — every star compact subset of $\mathbb{R}$ is necessarily closed.**

### Proof

Let $X\subseteq\mathbb{R}$ be star compact. We use the sequential characterizati
on of closedness given in the problem: it suffices to show that whenever a seque
nce in $X$ converges (in $\mathbb{R}$), its limit lies in $X$.

Suppose, for contradiction, that $X$ is **not** closed. Then there exist distinc
t points $y_k\in X$ with $y_k\to p$ for some $p\notin X$.

**Constructing a subsequence with disjoint neighborhoods.** Since $y_k\to p$ and
 the $y_k$ are distinct, pass to a subsequence (still denoted $y_k$) such that $
|y_{k+1}-p|<\tfrac12|y_k-p|$ for all $k$. Then for $j>k$,
$$|y_k-y_j|\ge |y_k-p|-|y_j-p|>|y_k-p|-\tfrac12|y_k-p|=\tfrac12|y_k-p|>0,$$
so the $y_k$ are pairwise separated. Hence we may choose **pairwise disjoint** o
pen intervals $U_k$ with $y_k\in U_k$ and $p\notin U_k$.

**Constructing the open cover.** A convergent sequence of distinct terms has exa
ctly one accumulation point, namely its limit. Therefore the only accumulation p
oint of $\{y_k\}$ is $p$, and since $p\notin X$, **no point of $X$ is an accumul
ation point of $\{y_k\}$**. Define
$$U_0:=\mathbb{R}\setminus\bigl(\{p\}\cup\{y_k:k\ge 1\}\bigr).$$
The set $\{p\}\cup\{y_k\}$ is closed (it is a convergent sequence together with
its limit), so $U_0$ is open. Moreover $U_0\cap\{y_k\}=\varnothing$ and, because
 $p\notin X$, every point of $X\setminus\{y_k\}$ lies in $U_0$.

Now $\mathcal{U}=\{U_0\}\cup\{U_k:k\ge 1\}$ is an open cover of $X$: $U_0$ cover
s $X\setminus\{y_k\}$ and $U_k\ni y_k$.

**Deriving the contradiction.** By star compactness, there is a **finite** $F\su
bseteq X$ with $X=\operatorname{st}(F,\mathcal{U})$.

Fix $k$. Where can $y_k$ be covered? We have $y_k\in U_k$, $y_k\notin U_0$ (sinc
e $U_0\cap\{y_k\}=\varnothing$), and $y_k\notin U_j$ for $j\neq k$ (the $U_k$ ar
e pairwise disjoint). Thus
$$y_k\in\operatorname{st}(F,\mathcal{U})\iff U_k\cap F\neq\varnothing.$$

Since the $U_k$ are pairwise disjoint, each point of the finite set $F$ belongs
to **at most one** $U_k$. Consequently $U_k\cap F\neq\varnothing$ for at most $|
F|$ values of $k$. For all the remaining (infinitely many) indices $k$, we have
$y_k\notin\operatorname{st}(F,\mathcal{U})$, contradicting $X=\operatorname{st}(
F,\mathcal{U})$.

The contradiction shows that no such sequence can exist; hence every convergent
sequence of points of $X$ has its limit in $X$. By the given sequential characte
rization, $X$ is closed. $\blacksquare$
