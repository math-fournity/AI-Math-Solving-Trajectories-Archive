## Proof

We are given the general bounds for diagonal Ramsey numbers, valid for all $a \g
eq 3$:

$$R(a, a) > 2^{a/2}, \qquad R(a, a) < 4^{a}.$$

We apply both bounds with $a = 10$.

**Lower bound.** Substituting $a = 10$ into $R(a,a) > 2^{a/2}$:

$$R(10, 10) > 2^{10/2} = 2^{5} = 32.$$

So $R(10, 10) > 32$, i.e. $R(10, 10) \geq 33$ (since Ramsey numbers are integers
).

**Upper bound.** Substituting $a = 10$ into $R(a,a) < 4^{a}$:

$$R(10, 10) < 4^{10} = (2^{2})^{10} = 2^{20} = 1{,}048{,}576.$$

So $R(10, 10) < 1{,}048{,}576$, i.e. $R(10, 10) \leq 1{,}048{,}575$.

**Combined result.** Putting the two bounds together:

$$\boxed{32 < R(10, 10) < 1{,}048{,}576},$$

or equivalently, since $R(10,10)$ is a positive integer,

$$33 \leq R(10, 10) \leq 1{,}048{,}575.$$

**Remark.** The lower bound $R(a,a) > 2^{a/2}$ comes from the probabilistic meth
od (Erdős, 1947): a random 2-coloring of the edges of $K_n$ with $n \leq 2^{a/2}
$ has a positive probability of containing no monochromatic $K_a$, proving $R(a,
a) > 2^{a/2}$. The upper bound $R(a,a) < 4^a$ comes from the classical Erdős–Sze
keres recursion $R(a,b) \leq R(a-1,b) + R(a,b-1)$ together with the induction es
timate $R(a,a) \leq \binom{2a-2}{a-1} < 4^{a-1} < 4^a$. For $a = 10$ these yield
 the bounds above, though the true value of $R(10,10)$ is known to lie in a much
 narrower range from more refined arguments; here we report only what the two st
ated general bounds give.
