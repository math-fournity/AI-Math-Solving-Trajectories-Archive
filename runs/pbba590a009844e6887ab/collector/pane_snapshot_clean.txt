## Proof

Let $f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n z^n$. Since the radius of convergence is str
ictly greater than $1$, the series converges absolutely and uniformly on the clo
sed unit disk $\overline{D} = \{z : |z| \leq 1\}$. In particular, $f$ is continu
ous on $\overline{D}$ and analytic in a neighborhood of it, so $f$ is bounded an
d the series representation is valid on $|z|=1$.

**Key tool — Parseval's identity.** We compute the mean-square value of $f$ on t
he unit circle. Setting $z = e^{i\theta}$,

$$f(e^{i\theta}) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n e^{in\theta}, \qquad \overline{f(e^{i
\theta})} = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty} \overline{a_m}\, e^{-im\theta},$$

where both series converge uniformly in $\theta$ (absolute convergence on $|z|=1
$). Hence we may multiply them term-by-term and integrate term-by-term:

$$|f(e^{i\theta})|^2 = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty} a_n \overline{a_m}
\, e^{i(n-m)\theta}.$$

Averaging over $\theta \in [0,2\pi]$,

$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} |f(e^{i\theta})|^2\,d\theta = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\
sum_{m=0}^{\infty} a_n \overline{a_m}\,\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} e^{i(n-m)\the
ta}\,d\theta.$$

The orthogonality relation

$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} e^{i(n-m)\theta}\,d\theta = \begin{cases} 1 & n =
m, \\ 0 & n \neq m, \end{cases}$$

kills all off-diagonal terms, leaving

$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} |f(e^{i\theta})|^2\,d\theta = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
|a_n|^2. \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Applying the hypothesis.** By assumption $|f(z)| \leq 1$ for every $z$ with $|
z| \leq 1$; in particular $|f(e^{i\theta})| \leq 1$ for all $\theta \in [0,2\pi]
$. Therefore $|f(e^{i\theta})|^2 \leq 1$ pointwise, and integrating this inequal
ity,

$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} |f(e^{i\theta})|^2\,d\theta \;\leq\; \frac{1}{2\pi
}\int_0^{2\pi} 1\,d\theta \;=\; 1.$$

Combining with $(\ast)$,

$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} |a_n|^2 = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} |f(e^{i\theta})|^2\,
d\theta \leq 1.$$

This completes the proof. $\blacksquare$
