## Setting Up the Framework

**Directions as points on a circle.** Each line has a direction (angle mod 180°)
. Represent each direction as a point on a circle of circumference 180. The cond
itions become:
- No two points coincide (no parallel lines).
- No two points are antipodal, i.e., separated by exactly 90 (no perpendicular l
ines).
- No three lines concurrent is a genericity condition on *positions*, not direct
ions — it ensures every triple of lines forms a proper triangle (which we use to
 count C(n,3) total triangles).

**Triangle angles from arcs.** For three lines with directions θ₁ < θ₂ < θ₃ (mod
 180°), the three arcs between consecutive directions on the circle are d₁ = θ₂−
θ₁, d₂ = θ₃−θ₂, d₃ = 180°−(θ₃−θ₁), with d₁+d₂+d₃ = 180°. The angles of the trian
gle formed by these three lines are exactly d₁, d₂, d₃.

**Acute vs. obtuse criterion.** The triangle is:
- **Acute** iff all three arcs < 90° (all angles < 90°).
- **Obtuse** iff one arc > 90° (exactly one, since they sum to 180°).
- **Right** iff one arc = 90° — excluded by the no-perpendicular condition.

So a triple is **obtuse** iff all three points lie in some open semicircle (arc
of length < 90°), equivalently iff one arc exceeds 90°.

## Counting Obtuse Triangles

For each point Pᵢ, let kᵢ = number of other points in the open semicircle of len
gth 90° clockwise from Pᵢ.

**Each obtuse triple is counted exactly once** in Σᵢ C(kᵢ, 2): an obtuse triple
has a unique arc > 90°; the point at the start of the complementary two small ar
cs has both other points in its clockwise semicircle, and no other point does. (
Verified with small cases: n=3 equilateral gives 0 obtuse; n=5 equally spaced gi
ves 5 obtuse out of 10.)

**Key identity:** Σᵢ kᵢ = C(n, 2). For each unordered pair {Pᵢ, Pⱼ}, exactly one
 clockwise distance is < 90° (since the two directions sum to 180° and neither e
quals 90° by the no-perpendicular condition). So each pair contributes exactly 1
 to the sum.

## Minimizing Obtuse Triangles (Maximizing Acute)

We minimize Σᵢ C(kᵢ, 2) subject to Σᵢ kᵢ = C(n,2), where n = 4031.

Since f(x) = x(x−1)/2 is **convex**, by Jensen's inequality:
$$\sum_i \binom{k_i}{2} \geq n \cdot \binom{\bar{k}}{2}, \quad \bar{k} = \frac{\
binom{n}{2}}{n} = \frac{n-1}{2}.$$

For n = 4031 (odd), (n−1)/2 = 2015 is an integer, so equality holds when **all k
ᵢ = 2015**.

**This is achievable:** Place 4031 points equally spaced on the circle. Spacing
= 180°/4031. Each open semicircle of length 90° contains ⌊90°/(180°/4031)⌋ = ⌊40
31/2⌋ = 2015 points. Since 4031 is odd, 4031/2 = 2015.5 is not an integer, so no
 two points are antipodal (no perpendicular lines). ✓

## Computing the Answer

$$\text{Max acute} = \binom{4031}{3} - 4031\binom{2015}{2}$$

$$= 4031\left(\frac{4030 \cdot 4029}{6} - \frac{2015 \cdot 2014}{2}\right)$$

Using 4030 = 2·2015:

$$= 4031 \cdot 2015\left(\frac{4029}{3} - 1007\right) = 4031 \cdot 2015 \cdot (1
343 - 1007) = 4031 \cdot 2015 \cdot 336$$

Computing:
- 4031 × 2015 = 8,122,465
- 8,122,465 × 336 = **2,729,148,240**

## Verification of the Configuration

Equally spaced directions at 0°, 180°/4031, 2·180°/4031, ..., 4030·180°/4031:
