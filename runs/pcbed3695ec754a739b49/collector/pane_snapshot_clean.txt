## Solution

**Setup.** Let the edge vectors of pentagon $ABCDE$ (traversed counterclockwise)
 be $v_1=\overrightarrow{AB}, v_2=\overrightarrow{BC}, v_3=\overrightarrow{CD},
v_4=\overrightarrow{DE}, v_5=\overrightarrow{EA}$, all unit vectors with $v_1+\c
dots+v_5=0$.

The right angles give $v_1\perp v_2$ (at $B$) and $v_4\perp v_5$ (at $E$).

**Exterior angles.** Let the exterior (turning) angles be $e_A,e_B,e_C,e_D,e_E$,
 summing to $360°$. Since the interior angles at $B$ and $E$ are $90°$, we have
$e_B=e_E=90°$, hence
$$e_A+e_C+e_D = 180°.$$

Set $s = e_C+e_D$, so $e_A = 180°-s$. Choose the direction of $AB$ as the positi
ve $x$-axis. Then the edge directions are:
$$d_1=0,\quad d_2=90°,\quad d_3=90°+e_C,\quad d_4=90°+s,\quad d_5=180°+s,$$
giving
$$v_1=(1,0),\; v_2=(0,1),\; v_3=(-\sin e_C,\cos e_C),\; v_4=(-\sin s,\cos s),\;
v_5=(-\cos s,-\sin s).$$

**Closure equations.** From $v_1+\cdots+v_5=0$:
$$1-\sin e_C-\sin s-\cos s = 0, \qquad 1+\cos e_C+\cos s-\sin s = 0.$$
So
$$\sin e_C = 1-\sin s-\cos s, \qquad \cos e_C = -1-\cos s+\sin s.$$

**Determining $s$.** Imposing $\sin^2 e_C+\cos^2 e_C=1$:
$$(1-\sin s-\cos s)^2+(-1-\cos s+\sin s)^2=1.$$
Expanding (with $a=\sin s, b=\cos s$):
$$(1-a-b)^2+(a-b-1)^2 = 4-4a = 1,$$
so $\sin s = \tfrac{3}{4}$, hence $\cos s = \pm\tfrac{\sqrt 7}{4}$.

For convexity we need $e_C\in(0°,180°)$, so $\sin e_C>0$. Checking:
- $\cos s=+\tfrac{\sqrt7}{4}$: $\sin e_C = 1-\tfrac34-\tfrac{\sqrt7}{4}=\tfrac{1
-\sqrt7}{4}<0$. ✗
- $\cos s=-\tfrac{\sqrt7}{4}$: $\sin e_C = \tfrac{1+\sqrt7}{4}>0$, $\cos e_C=\tf
rac{\sqrt7-1}{4}>0$. ✓

Thus $\sin s=\tfrac34$, $\cos s=-\tfrac{\sqrt7}{4}$, $\sin e_C=\tfrac{1+\sqrt7}{
4}$, $\cos e_C=\tfrac{\sqrt7-1}{4}$. (One checks $e_C=e_D=s/2$, reflecting the s
ymmetry between $B$ and $E$.)

**Area via the shoelace formula.** Place $A=(0,0)$:
$$B=(1,0),\; C=(1,1),\; D=(1-\sin e_C,\,1+\cos e_C),\; E=(1-\sin e_C-\sin s,\,1+
\cos e_C+\cos s).$$

The signed area is $\tfrac12\sum(x_i y_{i+1}-x_{i+1}y_i)$. The nonzero contribut
ions are:
- $B\to C$: $1$,
- $C\to D$: $\cos e_C+\sin e_C$,
- $D\to E$: $\cos s-\sin e_C\cos s+\sin s+\sin s\cos e_C = \cos s+\sin s+\sin(s-
e_C)=\cos s+\sin s+\sin e_D$.

Since $e_C=e_D$, the total is
$$S = 1+\cos e_C+2\sin e_C+\cos s+\sin s.$$

Substituting:
$$S = 1+\frac{\sqrt7-1}{4}+\frac{1+\sqrt7}{2}-\frac{\sqrt7}{4}+\frac{3}{4}=\frac
{8+2\sqrt7}{4}=\frac{4+\sqrt7}{2}.$$

Therefore
$$\text{Area} = \frac{S}{2}=\frac{4+\sqrt7}{4}.$$

**Numerical check.** $\sqrt7\approx2.6458$, giving area $\approx1.6614\text{ km}
^2$. Direct coordinate computation with $e_C\approx65.7°$, $s\approx131.4°$ conf
irms the vertices close up and yield the same area.

$$\boxed{\dfrac{4+\sqrt7}{4}\text{ square kilometers}}$$
