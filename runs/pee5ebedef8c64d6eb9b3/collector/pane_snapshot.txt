Looking at this problem, I need to evaluate:

$$\frac{1}{\pi^2} \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{(\ln x)^2}{\sqrt{x}(1-x)^2}\,dx$$

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains no solutions, hints, or le
aked answers. Proceeding.

---

## Proof

**Step 1: Split and symmetrize via $x \mapsto 1/x$.**

Split the integral at $x=1$:

$$I = \int_0^1 \frac{(\ln x)^2\, x^{-1/2}}{(1-x)^2}\,dx + \int_1^\infty \frac{(\
ln x)^2\, x^{-1/2}}{(1-x)^2}\,dx.$$

In the second integral, substitute $x = 1/u$, $dx = -du/u^2$:

$$\int_1^\infty \frac{(\ln x)^2\, x^{-1/2}}{(1-x)^2}\,dx = \int_0^1 \frac{(\ln u
)^2\, u^{1/2}}{(1-u)^2}\,du.$$

(Here $\ln(1/u) = -\ln u$ so $(\ln u)^2$ is unchanged, and $(1-1/u)^2 = (u-1)^2/
u^2 = (1-u)^2/u^2$.)

Combining:

$$I = \int_0^1 \frac{(\ln x)^2\,(x^{-1/2}+x^{1/2})}{(1-x)^2}\,dx = \int_0^1 \fra
c{(\ln x)^2\,(1+x)}{x^{1/2}(1-x)^2}\,dx.$$

**Step 2: Series expansion on $(0,1)$.**

For $|x|<1$:

$$\frac{1+x}{(1-x)^2} = \frac{1}{(1-x)^2}+\frac{x}{(1-x)^2} = \sum_{n=0}^\infty
(n+1)x^n + \sum_{n=1}^\infty n\,x^n = \sum_{n=0}^\infty (2n+1)\,x^n.$$

Since all terms are non-negative on $(0,1)$, by monotone convergence:

$$I = \sum_{n=0}^\infty (2n+1)\int_0^1 (\ln x)^2\, x^{n-1/2}\,dx.$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the inner integral.**

Using the standard result $\int_0^1 (\ln x)^2\, x^a\,dx = \frac{2}{(a+1)^3}$ for
 $a>-1$, with $a = n-\tfrac12$:

$$\int_0^1 (\ln x)^2\, x^{n-1/2}\,dx = \frac{2}{(n+\tfrac12)^3}.$$

**Step 4: Sum the series.**

Since $2n+1 = 2(n+\tfrac12)$:

$$I = \sum_{n=0}^\infty (2n+1)\cdot\frac{2}{(n+\tfrac12)^3} = \sum_{n=0}^\infty
\frac{4(n+\tfrac12)}{(n+\tfrac12)^3} = 4\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{(n+\tfrac12)^
2}.$$

Now $(n+\tfrac12) = \tfrac12(2n+1)$, so:

$$\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{(n+\tfrac12)^2} = 4\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{(2n+1
)^2} = 4\cdot\frac{\pi^2}{8} = \frac{\pi^2}{2},$$

using the well-known identity $\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{(2n+1)^2} = \frac{\pi^
2}{8}$ (obtained from $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{6}$ minus th
e even terms $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{(2n)^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{24}$).

Therefore:

$$I = 4 \cdot \frac{\pi^2}{2} = 2\pi^2.$$

**Step 5: Final answer.**

$$\frac{1}{\pi^2}\,I = \frac{2\pi^2}{\pi^2} = \boxed{2}.$$
