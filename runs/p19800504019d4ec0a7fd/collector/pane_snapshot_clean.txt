## Proof

We compute
$$L = \lim_{x\to 0}\frac{(\ln(1+x_2)-x_2)(1-\cos x_3)\tan x_1}{\|x\|^4},\qquad x
\in\mathbb R^3.$$

**Step 1: Taylor expansions near 0.** For small $|u|$:

$$\ln(1+u)-u = -\frac{u^2}{2}+\frac{u^3}{3}-\cdots,\qquad 1-\cos u = \frac{u^2}{
2}-\frac{u^4}{24}+\cdots,\qquad \tan u = u+\frac{u^3}{3}+\cdots.$$

Hence there exist constants $C_1,C_2,C_3>0$ and $\delta>0$ such that for all $|u
|<\delta$:

$$|\ln(1+u)-u|\le C_1\,u^2,\qquad |1-\cos u|\le C_2\,u^2,\qquad |\tan u|\le C_3\
,|u|.$$

**Step 2: Bound the numerator.** For $\|x\|<\delta$ (so that each $|x_i|<\delta$
), using $|x_1|\le\|x\|$, $x_2^2\le\|x\|^2$, $x_3^2\le\|x\|^2$:

$$\bigl|(\ln(1+x_2)-x_2)(1-\cos x_3)\tan x_1\bigr|\le C_1x_2^2\cdot C_2x_3^2\cdo
t C_3|x_1|\le C\,|x_1|\,x_2^2\,x_3^2\le C\,\|x\|\cdot\|x\|^2\cdot\|x\|^2=C\,\|x\
|^5,$$

where $C=C_1C_2C_3$.

**Step 3: Bound the ratio.** Therefore, for $0<\|x\|<\delta$,

$$\left|\frac{(\ln(1+x_2)-x_2)(1-\cos x_3)\tan x_1}{\|x\|^4}\right|\le\frac{C\,\
|x\|^5}{\|x\|^4}=C\,\|x\|.$$

**Step 4: Take the limit.** As $x\to 0$, $\|x\|\to 0$, so $C\|x\|\to 0$. By the
squeeze theorem,

$$\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{(\ln(1+x_2)-x_2)(1-\cos x_3)\tan x_1}{\|x\|^4}=0.$$

**Remark on path-independence.** The bound $C\|x\|$ is uniform — it depends only
 on $\|x\|$, not on the direction of approach. Hence the limit is the same along
 every path to the origin, confirming the limit exists and equals $0$.

$$\boxed{\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{(\ln(1+x_2)-x_2)(1-\cos x_3)\tan x_1}{\|x\|^4}=0}$$
