# Problem

A specialized logistics company uses a dual-key encryption system for its high-security vaults. Each vault requires two digital keys, represented by two-digit integers $x$ and $y$, where $x \leq y$. To ensure security, the system requires that the set of all digits used across both keys must contain at least three distinct numerical values.

One afternoon, a technician is tasked with entering the keys to a specific vault. However, the technician suffers a specific form of digital transposition error: for both keys, they enter the digits in the reverse order (for example, if a key were $42$, they would input $24$). 

Upon performing the system’s authentication calculation—which involves multiplying the two keys together—the technician discovers a mathematical anomaly: the product of the two reversed keys is exactly equal to the product of the two original intended keys.

Given these constraints, how many possible pairs of original keys $\{x, y\}$ could the technician have been attempting to use?
