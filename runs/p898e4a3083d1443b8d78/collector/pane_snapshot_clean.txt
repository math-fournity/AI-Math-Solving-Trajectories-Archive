Claim: Yes, the quotient map $p: D \to D/E$ is a closed function. The proof does
not require $D$ to be Hausdorff, nor does it require the finer Noetherian
topology on $D \times D$.

Proof

Let $F \subseteq D$ be a closed set. We must show $p(F)$ is closed in the
quotient topology on $D/E$. By definition of the quotient topology, $p(F)$ is
closed in $D/E$ if and only if $p^{-1}(p(F))$ is closed in $D$.

Step 1: Express the preimage. We compute: $$p^{-1}(p(F)) = {x \in D : \exists, y
\in F \text{ with } (x,y) \in E} = \pi_1!\big(E \cap (D \times F)\big),$$ where
$\pi_1: D \times D \to D$ is the first projection.

Step 2: $E \cap (D \times F)$ is closed in the product topology. Since $E$ is a
closed equivalence relation, $E$ is closed in $D \times D$ under the product
topology. Since $F$ is closed in $D$, the set $D \times F$ is closed in $D
\times D$ under the product topology. Therefore $E \cap (D \times F)$ is closed
in the product topology.

Step 3: The projection $\pi_1: D \times D \to D$ is a closed map. This is the
key step, and it relies on $D$ being Noetherian.

Since $D$ is Noetherian, $D$ is quasi-compact (every open cover admits a finite
subcover). Let $C \subseteq D \times D$ be closed (in the product topology), and
let $x \notin \pi_1(C)$. Then ${x} \times D \cap C = \emptyset$, so ${x} \times
D \subseteq U := (D \times D) \setminus C$, which is open.

For each $y \in D$, the point $(x, y) \in U$, so there exist open neighborhoods
$V_y \ni x$ in $D$ and $W_y \ni y$ in $D$ such that $V_y \times W_y \subseteq
U$. The family ${W_y}{y \in D}$ is an open cover of $D$. By quasi-compactness of
$D$, there exist $y_1, \ldots, y_n \in D$ such that $D = \bigcup{i=1}^n
W_{y_i}$.

Set $V = \bigcap_{i=1}^n V_{y_i}$, which is an open neighborhood of $x$ in $D$.
Then: $$V \times D = V \times \Big(\bigcup_{i=1}^n W_{y_i}\Big) = \bigcup_{i=1}
^n (V \times W_{y_i}) \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^n (V_{y_i} \times W_{y_i})
\subseteq U = (D \times D) \setminus C.$$ Hence $V \cap \pi_1(C) = \emptyset$,
proving that $D \setminus \pi_1(C)$ is open, so $\pi_1(C)$ is closed.

Step 4: Conclude. Applying Step 3 to the closed set $C = E \cap (D \times F)$
from Step 2: $$p^{-1}(p(F)) = \pi_1!\big(E \cap (D \times F)\big) \text{ is
closed in } D.$$ Therefore $p(F)$ is closed in $D/E$. Since $F$ was an arbitrary
closed subset of $D$, the map $p$ is closed. $\blacksquare$

Remarks on the hypotheses

  • Non-Hausdorff is harmless. The classical result that closed equivalence
  relations on compact Hausdorff spaces yield closed quotient maps uses
  Hausdorffness to identify compact subsets with closed subsets. Here,
  Noetherianity directly provides quasi-compactness of $D$, which is all the
  tube lemma requires. The Hausdorff property plays no role.
  • The finer Noetherian topology is not needed. The product of two Noetherian
  spaces is Noetherian (a standard result), so $D \times D$ with the product
  topology is already Noetherian. The existence of a finer Noetherian topology
  does not affect the argument, since the proof operates entirely within the
  product topology, where $E$ is closed by the definition of "closed equivalence
  relation." The finer topology condition is consistent with—but redundant for—
  the conclusion.
