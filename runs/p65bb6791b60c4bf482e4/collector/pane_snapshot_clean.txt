**Answer: Yes, this is true.**

## Proof

**Setup and notation.** We are given:
- $N \trianglelefteq G$ and $G/N$ is abelian.
- $G/N = \langle Nh_1 \rangle \times \cdots \times \langle Nh_r \rangle$, where
$|\langle Nh_i \rangle| = \beta_i$ for each $i$. (The problem writes $Nh_i^{\bet
a_i}$ for the cyclic subgroup generated by the coset $Nh_i$, of order $\beta_i$;
 the indices $r$ and $e$ refer to the same number.)

**Key observation.** Since $G/N$ is the direct product of the cyclic groups $\la
ngle Nh_i \rangle$ (each of order $\beta_i$), every element of $G/N$ admits a un
ique representation
$$
(Nh_1)^{\gamma_1}(Nh_2)^{\gamma_2}\cdots(Nh_r)^{\gamma_r}, \qquad 0 \le \gamma_i
 < \beta_i.
$$

**Step 1: Coset decomposition.** Let $g \in G$ be arbitrary. Its coset $Ng \in G
/N$ is an element of the direct product, so there exist unique integers $\gamma_
1, \ldots, \gamma_r$ with $0 \le \gamma_i < \beta_i$ such that
$$
Ng = (Nh_1)^{\gamma_1}(Nh_2)^{\gamma_2}\cdots(Nh_r)^{\gamma_r}.
$$

**Step 2: Simplify the right-hand side.** By the definition of the quotient grou
p operation, the product of cosets is the coset of the product:
$$
(Nh_1)^{\gamma_1}(Nh_2)^{\gamma_2}\cdots(Nh_r)^{\gamma_r} = N\!\left(h_1^{\gamma
_1} h_2^{\gamma_2}\cdots h_r^{\gamma_r}\right).
$$
This holds **regardless of whether $G$ is abelian** — the quotient group operati
on is always well-defined since $N$ is normal, and powers of cosets equal cosets
 of powers.

**Step 3: Lift to $G$.** We now have
$$
Ng = N\!\left(h_1^{\gamma_1} h_2^{\gamma_2}\cdots h_r^{\gamma_r}\right).
$$
Two elements of $G$ lie in the same left coset of $N$ if and only if they differ
 by left multiplication by an element of $N$. Therefore there exists $n' \in N$
such that
$$
g = n'\, h_1^{\gamma_1} h_2^{\gamma_2}\cdots h_r^{\gamma_r}.
$$

**Step 4: Verify the exponent bound.** From Step 1, each $\gamma_i$ satisfies $0
 \le \gamma_i < \beta_i$, hence $\gamma_i \le \beta_i$ as required. $\square$

**Remark on the hypothesis about $N$.** The condition that every $n \in N$ can b

y $\{g_1,\ldots,g_k\}$) is not needed for this conclusion. The result follows pu
rely from the direct product decomposition of $G/N$ together with the normality
of $N$. The generation hypothesis on $N$ may be relevant for other purposes (e.g
., presenting $G$ via generators and relations), but it plays no role in establi
shing the existence of the decomposition $g = n' h_1^{\gamma_1}\cdots h_r^{\gamm
a_r}$.
