## Proof

We wish to evaluate
$$L = \lim_{N\to\infty} N^2 \sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)^{N\ln N}.$$

All summands are non-negative, so it suffices to extract a single term that alre
ady forces divergence.

**Lower bound from the last term.** Take $k = N-1$. Then
$$\left(\frac{N-1}{N}\right)^{N\ln N} = \left(1-\frac{1}{N}\right)^{N\ln N} = \e
xp\!\Big(N\ln N\cdot \ln\!\big(1-\tfrac{1}{N}\big)\Big).$$

Using the standard inequality $\ln(1-x) \ge -\dfrac{x}{1-x}$ for $x\in(0,1)$, wi
th $x = 1/N$:
$$\ln\!\left(1-\frac{1}{N}\right) \ge -\frac{1/N}{1-1/N} = -\frac{1}{N-1}.$$

Hence
$$\left(1-\frac{1}{N}\right)^{N\ln N} \ge \exp\!\left(-\frac{N\ln N}{N-1}\right)
 = N^{-N/(N-1)} = \frac{1}{N}\cdot N^{-1/(N-1)} = \frac{1}{N}\,e^{-\ln N/(N-1)}.
$$

Since every term in the sum is non-negative,
$$N^2\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)^{N\ln N} \ge N^2\left(\frac{N-1}{N
}\right)^{N\ln N} \ge N\, e^{-\ln N/(N-1)}.$$

As $N\to\infty$, $\dfrac{\ln N}{N-1}\to 0$, so $e^{-\ln N/(N-1)}\to 1$, and ther
efore
$$N\, e^{-\ln N/(N-1)} \longrightarrow +\infty.$$

**Upper bound (to confirm the rate, optional).** For completeness, set $k = N-j$
 ($j=1,\dots,N-1$). Then
$$\left(1-\frac{j}{N}\right)^{N\ln N} = N^{-j}\exp\!\left(-\frac{j^2\ln N}{2N} +
 O\!\left(\frac{j^3\ln N}{N^2}\right)\right) \le N^{-j}.$$

Thus
$$N^2 S \le N^2\sum_{j=1}^{N-1} N^{-j} < N^2\cdot\frac{1/N}{1-1/N} = \frac{N}{1-
1/N} = \frac{N^2}{N-1} \sim N.$$

Combining the lower and upper bounds,
$$N\,e^{-\ln N/(N-1)} \;\le\; N^2 S \;\le\; \frac{N^2}{N-1},$$
and both bounds tend to $+\infty$ (in fact both are $\sim N$). A sharper expansi
on of the $j=1$ and $j=2$ terms gives
$$N^2 S = N - \tfrac{1}{2}\ln N + 1 + o(1),$$
but the leading behavior $N^2 S \sim N$ already suffices.

Therefore the limit diverges to $+\infty$:

$$\boxed{\lim_{N\to\infty}N^2\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)^{N\ln N}=+
\infty.}$$
