## Solution

**Answer: No.** A minimal prime of a Cohen-Macaulay module $M$ need not be a min
imal prime of $R$. Here is a counterexample.

### Counterexample

Let $k$ be a field and set
$$R = k[[x,y,z]]/(xy).$$
This is a local ring (with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m} = (x,y,z)/(xy)$) of dimen
sion
$$\dim R = \dim k[[x,y,z]] - \operatorname{ht}(xy) = 3 - 1 = 2 > 0.$$
The minimal primes of $R$ are $(x)/(xy)$ and $(y)/(xy)$, corresponding to the mi
nimal primes $(x)$ and $(y)$ over $(xy)$ in $k[[x,y,z]]$.

Now set
$$M = R/(x,z).$$
Then
$$M \cong k[[x,y,z]]/(x,\, z,\, xy) = k[[x,y,z]]/(x,\, z) \cong k[[y]],$$
which is a 1-dimensional regular local ring, hence **Cohen-Macaulay**.

### The minimal prime of $M$ is not a minimal prime of $R$

The annihilator of $M$ in $R$ is $\operatorname{Ann}_R(M) = (x,z)/(xy)$. The uni
que minimal prime of $M$ is therefore
$$P = (x,\, z)/(xy),$$
the unique minimal prime of $R$ lying over $(x,z)$.

However, $P$ is **not** a minimal prime of $R$: it properly contains $(x)/(xy)$,
 which is a minimal prime of $R$.

### Verification that $\dim R_P > 0$

Localize $R$ at $P = (x,z)/(xy)$. In the local ring $k[[x,y,z]]_{(x,z)}$, the el
ement $y \notin (x,z)$ becomes a unit. Since $xy = 0$ in $R$ and $y$ is a unit i
n $R_P$, we get $x = 0$ in $R_P$. Thus:
$$R_P \cong k[[x,y,z]]_{(x,z)}/(x) \cong k[[y,z]]_{(z)}.$$
The ring $k[[y,z]]_{(z)}$ is a 1-dimensional local domain (its maximal ideal is
generated by $z$, and $(0) \subsetneq (z)$ is the chain of primes), so
$$\dim R_P = 1 > 0.$$

On the other hand, since $P$ is the unique minimal prime of $M$, we have $\dim M
_P = 0$.

### Conclusion

We have exhibited a local ring $(R, \mathfrak{m})$ of dimension $d = 2 > 0$ and
a finitely generated Cohen-Macaulay $R$-module $M$ (namely $M = R/(x,z) \cong k[
[y]]$) with a minimal prime $P = (x,z)/(xy)$ of $M$ such that $\dim M_P = 0$ but
 $\dim R_P = 1 \neq 0$. Therefore $P$ is **not** a minimal prime of $R$.

The answer to the question is **no**.
