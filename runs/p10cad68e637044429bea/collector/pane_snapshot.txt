**Counterexample.**

rangle$, where $F_2 = \langle a, b \rangle$ is the free group of rank 2. This is
 finitely presented (hence finitely generated).

Let $L = \{e\} \times \langle\!\langle b \rangle\!\rangle_{F_2}$, where $\langle
\!\langle b \rangle\!\rangle_{F_2}$ is the normal closure of $b$ in $F_2$. Then:

- **$L \trianglelefteq D$**: $\langle\!\langle b \rangle\!\rangle_{F_2} \triangl
elefteq F_2$ and $\langle c \rangle$ is central in $D$.
- **$L$ is not finitely generated**: $\langle\!\langle b \rangle\!\rangle_{F_2}
= \ker(F_2 \to \mathbb{Z},\; a \mapsto 1,\; b \mapsto 0)$, which by the Nielsen–
Schreier theorem is free of countably infinite rank, freely generated by $\{a^n
b a^{-n} : n \in \mathbb{Z}\}$.

Set $h_1 = ca$, $h_2 = c^2 a$. Then $h_1 \neq h_2$ (since $c$ has infinite order
), and $h_1^{-1}h_2 = a^{-1}c^{-1} \cdot c^2 a = c$ (using $[c,a]=1$), which lie

le b \rangle\!\rangle_{F_2})$, and $C_{F_2}(\langle\!\langle b \rangle\!\rangle_
{F_2}) = \{e\}$ because in a free group the centralizer of any nontrivial elemen
t is cyclic, and $\langle b \rangle \cap \langle aba^{-1} \rangle = \{e\}$ (they
 are distinct free generators).

The associated subgroups are $A = \langle h_1, L \rangle = \langle ca, L \rangle
$ and $B = \langle h_2, L \rangle = \langle c^2a, L \rangle$, with isomorphism $
\phi: A \to B$ sending $ca \mapsto c^2a$ and fixing $L$. This is well-defined be
cause conjugation by $ca$ and by $c^2a$ on $L$ coincide—both act as conjugation
by $a$ on $\langle\!\langle b \rangle\!\rangle_{F_2}$, since $c$ is central and
cancels.

**$A$ is finitely generated.** $A = \langle ca,\; b \rangle$: the element $b \in
 L$, and every generator $a^n b a^{-n}$ of $L$ is obtained as $(ca)^n \, b \, (c
a)^{-n}$ (the $c$-components cancel in conjugation). So $ca$ and $b$ generate al

**$H$ is finitely presented.** Since $D$ is finitely presented and $A = \langle
ca, b \rangle$ is finitely generated, the HNN extension admits the finite presen
tation:

$$H = \bigl\langle c, a, b, t \;\big|\; [c,a],\; [c,b],\; t^{-1}(ca)\,t = c^2 a,
\; t^{-1}b\,t = b \bigr\rrangle.$$

The infinitely many relations $t^{-1}lt = l$ for $l \in L$ are all consequences
of the two HNN relations above: every $l \in L$ is a product of conjugates $(ca)
^n b (ca)^{-n}$, and
$$t^{-1}(ca)^n b (ca)^{-n} t = (c^2a)^n \, b \, (c^2a)^{-n} = a^n b a^{-n} = (ca
)^n b (ca)^{-n},$$
where the $c$-components cancel since $c$ is central and $b \in F_2$. Thus $t$ f

nitely generated, $L \trianglelefteq D$ is normal, $h_1 \neq h_2$ are distinct e
