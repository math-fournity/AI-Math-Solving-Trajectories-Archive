## Proof

**Step 1: Identify the structure group.**

A general $S^1$-bundle (fiber bundle with fiber $S^1$) has structure group $\mat
hrm{Homeo}(S^1)$. The orientation-preserving subgroup $\mathrm{Homeo}^+(S^1)$ de
formation retracts onto $SO(2) \cong S^1$ (via the evaluation fibration $\mathrm
{Homeo}^+(S^1) \to S^1$, $h \mapsto h(1)$, with contractible fiber). Since $\mat
hrm{Homeo}(S^1) \cong \mathrm{Homeo}^+(S^1) \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/2$, we have a hom
otopy equivalence $\mathrm{Homeo}(S^1) \simeq O(2)$. The same holds for $\mathrm
{Diff}(S^1)$. Thus $S^1$-bundles over $B$ are classified by $[B, BO(2)]$.

**Step 2: Analyze the Postnikov tower of $BO(2)$.**

Since $O(2)$ has two components each homeomorphic to $S^1$:
$$\pi_1(BO(2)) = \pi_0(O(2)) = \mathbb{Z}/2, \quad \pi_2(BO(2)) = \pi_1(O(2)) =
\mathbb{Z}, \quad \pi_k(BO(2)) = 0 \text{ for } k \geq 3.$$

So $BO(2)$ is a 2-stage Postnikov system. The action of $\pi_1 = \mathbb{Z}/2$ o
n $\pi_2 = \mathbb{Z}$ is by $-1$ (conjugation by a reflection in $O(2)$ sends a
 rotation to its inverse). The $k$-invariant lives in $H^3(K(\mathbb{Z}/2,1);\,
\mathbb{Z}_{-1})$, but for a **2-dimensional** domain $B$, the pullback of the $
k$-invariant lands in $H^3(B;\, \mathbb{Z}_{w_1}) = 0$, so it presents no obstru
ction.

**Step 3: Classification over $\mathbb{RP}^2$.**

By obstruction theory, for the 2-dimensional CW complex $\mathbb{RP}^2$:
$$[\mathbb{RP}^2, BO(2)] \;\cong\; \bigsqcup_{w_1 \,\in\, H^1(\mathbb{RP}^2;\,\m
athbb{Z}/2)} H^2(\mathbb{RP}^2;\, \mathbb{Z}_{w_1}),$$
where $\mathbb{Z}_{w_1}$ denotes the local coefficient system on which the gener
ator $t$ of $\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2) = \mathbb{Z}/2$ acts as $(-1)^{w_1}$.

We have $H^1(\mathbb{RP}^2; \mathbb{Z}/2) = \mathbb{Z}/2$, giving two cases:

**Case $w_1 = 0$ (orientable bundles):** $\mathbb{Z}_{w_1} = \mathbb{Z}$ (trivia
l action). By the universal coefficient theorem:
$$H^2(\mathbb{RP}^2; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathrm{Ext}(H_1(\mathbb{RP}^2), \mathbb{
Z}) \oplus \mathrm{Hom}(H_2(\mathbb{RP}^2), \mathbb{Z}) = \mathrm{Ext}(\mathbb{Z
}/2, \mathbb{Z}) \oplus 0 = \mathbb{Z}/2.$$
This gives **2 orientable** $S^1$-bundles (the trivial bundle and the one with E
uler class $1$, e.g., the unit tangent bundle of $\mathbb{RP}^2$).

**Case $w_1 \neq 0$ (non-orientable bundles):** $\mathbb{Z}_{w_1} = \mathbb{Z}_a
$ where $t$ acts as $-1$. Compute $H^2(\mathbb{RP}^2; \mathbb{Z}_a)$ via the cel
lular cochain complex. The universal cover $S^2$ has chain groups $C_k = \mathbb
{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/2]$ with boundary maps $d_1 = 1 - t$ and $d_2 = 1 + t$ (verified:
 $d_1 \circ d_2 = (1-t)(1+t) = 0$). Dualizing with coefficients in $\mathbb{Z}_a
$ (where $t \mapsto -1$):
$$\delta^0 = (1-t)^* = 1-(-1) = 2, \qquad \delta^1 = (1+t)^* = 1+(-1) = 0.$$
The cochain complex is $\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z} \xrightarro
w{\times 0} \mathbb{Z}$, giving:
$$H^2(\mathbb{RP}^2;\, \mathbb{Z}_a) = \mathbb{Z}/\mathrm{im}(\times 0) = \mathb
b{Z}.$$
This gives **countably infinitely many non-orientable** $S^1$-bundles, indexed b
y $e \in \mathbb{Z}$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

The total set of isomorphism classes of $S^1$-bundles over $\mathbb{RP}^2$ is:
$$\mathbb{Z}/2 \;\sqcup\; \mathbb{Z},$$
which is **countably infinite**. There are 2 orientable bundles and countably in
finitely many non-orientable bundles.

**The claim that there are only two $S^1$-bundles over $\mathbb{RP}^2$ is false.
** That count of two applies only to *oriented* (equivalently, principal) $S^1$-
bundles, which are classified by $H^2(\mathbb{RP}^2; \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}/2$
. Once non-orientable $S^1$-bundles are included — which they must be for a comp
lete classification of $S^1$-bundles — the twisted Euler class $e \in H^2(\mathb
b{RP}^2; \mathbb{Z}_a) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ contributes infinitely many additional
bundles.
