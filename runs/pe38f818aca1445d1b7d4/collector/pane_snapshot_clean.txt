## Solution

**Setup.** Let $P_k$ denote the space of polynomials of degree $\leq k$ (dimensi
on $k+1$). The operator $L$ is

$$L = \sum_{j=0}^{n} \frac{D^j}{j!},$$

and the shift operator $T$ satisfies $T(p(x)) = p(x+1)$. By Taylor's theorem (wh
ich is exact on polynomials),

$$T = e^D = \sum_{j=0}^{\infty} \frac{D^j}{j!}.$$

**Key observation.** On $P_k$, every $D^j$ with $j > k$ vanishes identically, si
nce differentiating a polynomial of degree $\leq k$ more than $k$ times yields z
ero. Therefore, when restricted to $P_k$:

$$T\big|_{P_k} = \sum_{j=0}^{k} \frac{D^j}{j!}.$$

Since $n \geq k$, we likewise split $L$:

$$L\big|_{P_k} = \sum_{j=0}^{n} \frac{D^j}{j!} = \sum_{j=0}^{k} \frac{D^j}{j!} +
 \sum_{j=k+1}^{n} \frac{D^j}{j!} = \sum_{j=0}^{k} \frac{D^j}{j!},$$

where the second sum vanishes because $D^j = 0$ on $P_k$ for all $j > k$.

**Conclusion.** We have shown

$$(L - T)\big|_{P_k} = L\big|_{P_k} - T\big|_{P_k} = \sum_{j=0}^{k} \frac{D^j}{j
!} - \sum_{j=0}^{k} \frac{D^j}{j!} = 0.$$

Thus $L - T$ is the zero operator on $P_k$, so every polynomial in $P_k$ lies in
 its kernel:

$$\ker(L - T) = P_k.$$

The dimension of $P_k$ is $k + 1$.

**Verification (small case).** Take $n=2,\, k=1$. Then $L = \tfrac{1}{2}D^2 + D
+ I$. For $p(x)=ax+b$: $Lp = 0 + a + (ax+b) = ax+a+b$, and $Tp = a(x+1)+b = ax+a
+b$. So $(L-T)p = 0$ for all $p \in P_1$, confirming $\dim\ker(L-T) = 2 = k+1$.
✓

$$\boxed{\dim \ker(L - T) = k+1}$$
