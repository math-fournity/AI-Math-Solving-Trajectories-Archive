## Proof

We determine all pairs $(f,g):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ satisfying
$$g(f(x+y))=f(x)+(2x+y)\,g(y)\qquad(\star)$$
for all real $x,y$.

**Step 1: Two key substitutions.**

Setting $x=0$ in $(\star)$: $g(f(y))=f(0)+y\,g(y)$.
Setting $y=0$ in $(\star)$: $g(f(x))=f(x)+2x\,g(0)$.

Since both express $g(f(t))$, equating them (with $a:=f(0)$, $b:=g(0)$):
$$f(t)=a+t\bigl(g(t)-2b\bigr)\qquad\text{for all }t. \tag{1}$$

**Step 2: Reduce to an equation for $g$.**

Using $g(f(s))=a+s\,g(s)$ with $s=x+y$, the left side of $(\star)$ becomes $a+(x
+y)g(x+y)$. Using (1) for $f(x)$, the right side becomes $a+x\bigl(g(x)-2b\bigr)
+(2x+y)g(y)$. Canceling $a$:
$$(x+y)\,g(x+y)=x\,g(x)-2bx+(2x+y)\,g(y). \tag{2}$$

Define $h(t):=t\,g(t)$ (so $h(0)=0$). Then (2) reads
$$h(x+y)=h(x)+h(y)+2x\bigl(g(y)-b\bigr). \tag{3}$$

**Step 3: $g$ is linear.**

Fix $t$ and view (3) as a function of $x$: the difference $h(x+t)-h(x)=h(t)+2x(g
(t)-b)$ is **affine in $x$**. Compute $h(x+t_1+t_2)$ two ways (adding $t_2$ then
 $t_1$, vs. $t_1$ then $t_2$); equating the results gives
$$t_1\bigl(g(t_2)-b\bigr)=t_2\bigl(g(t_1)-b\bigr).$$

For $t_1,t_2\neq 0$ this says $\dfrac{g(t)-b}{t}$ is constant for $t\neq 0$; cal
l it $k$. Hence $g(t)=b+kt$ for $t\neq 0$, and since $g(0)=b$, this holds for **
all** $t$:
$$g(t)=kt+b. \tag{4}$$

(If $g$ were constant, the same conclusion holds with $k=0$.)

**Step 4: Determine $f$ and the constants.**

From (1) and (4): $f(t)=a+t(kt+b-2b)=kt^{2}-bt+a$.

Now substitute $g(t)=kt+b$ and $f(t)=kt^{2}-bt+a$ directly into $(\star)$. With
$s:=x+y$:

- LHS: $g(f(s))=k\bigl(ks^{2}-bs+a\bigr)+b=k^{2}s^{2}-kbs+ka+b$.
- RHS: $f(x)+(2x+y)g(y)=kx^{2}-bx+a+(2x+y)(ky+b)=ks^{2}+bs+a$.

Equating for all $s$:
$$k^{2}s^{2}-kbs+ka+b=ks^{2}+bs+a.$$

Comparing coefficients of $s^{2},s^{1},s^{0}$:

| power | equation | consequence |
|---|---|---|
| $s^{2}$ | $k^{2}=k$ | $k\in\{0,1\}$ |
| $s^{1}$ | $-kb=b$ | $b(k+1)=0$ |
| $s^{0}$ | $ka+b=a$ | $a(k-1)=-b$ |

**Case $k=0$:** $b(0+1)=0\Rightarrow b=0$; then $a(0-1)=-b=0\Rightarrow a=0$. So
 $f\equiv 0,\;g\equiv 0$.

**Case $k=1$:** $b(2)=0\Rightarrow b=0$; then $a(0)=-b=0$, automatically satisfi
ed, so $a$ is free. With $C:=a$: $g(t)=t,\; f(t)=t^{2}+C$.

**Step 5: Verification.**

- $(f,g)=(0,0)$: both sides of $(\star)$ are $0$. ✓
- $f(x)=x^{2}+C,\;g(x)=x$: LHS $=g(f(x+y))=(x+y)^{2}+C$; RHS $=x^{2}+C+(2x+y)y=x
^{2}+C+2xy+y^{2}=(x+y)^{2}+C$. ✓

Both families satisfy $(\star)$, and Steps 1–4 show no other solutions exist.

---

**Conclusion.** The pairs $(f,g)$ satisfying the equation are exactly:

$$\boxed{(f,g)\equiv(0,0)\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=x^{2}+C,\;g(x)=x\text{ for som
e }C\in\mathbb{R}.}$$
