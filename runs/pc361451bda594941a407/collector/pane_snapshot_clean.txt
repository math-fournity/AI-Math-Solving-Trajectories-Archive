**Claim.** Let $X$ be a complete Hausdorff locally convex topological vector spa
ce and $M$ a dense linear subspace of $X$. If $M$ is quasi-complete, then $M=X$.

**Proof.** Suppose, for a contradiction, that $M\subsetneq X$. Pick $x\in X\setm
inus M$. Since $M$ is a linear subspace, $x\neq 0$.

Because $X$ is Hausdorff and locally convex, the continuous seminorms separate p
oints; hence there exists a continuous seminorm $p$ on $X$ with $p(x)>0$. Replac
ing $p$ by $p/p(x)$, we may assume $p(x)=1$, so that $p(x/2)=\tfrac12<1$.

Define
$$B \;=\; \{\,m\in M : p(m)\le 1\,\}\;=\;M\cap\{\,y\in X:p(y)\le 1\,\}.$$

* $B$ is **closed in $M$**: $\{y\in X:p(y)\le 1\}$ is closed in $X$ (continuity
of $p$), and $B$ is its intersection with $M$.

* $B$ is **bounded in $M$** (equivalently, in $X$): given any neighborhood $U$ o
f $0$, by continuity of $p$ there is $\varepsilon>0$ with $\{y:p(y)<\varepsilon\
}\subseteq U$; then $B\subseteq \{y:p(y)\le 1\}\subseteq \tfrac1\varepsilon\{y:p
(y)<\varepsilon\}\subseteq \tfrac1\varepsilon U$.

By quasi-completeness of $M$, the bounded closed subset $B$ is **complete** (in
the uniformity induced from $X$, which coincides with that induced from $M$). Si
nce $X$ is Hausdorff, every complete subset of $X$ is closed; therefore $B$ is *
*closed in $X$**.

Now use density of $M$. Since $M$ is dense in $X$ and $x/2\in X$, there is a net
 $(m_\alpha)$ in $M$ with $m_\alpha\to x/2$ in $X$. By continuity of $p$,
$$p(m_\alpha)\longrightarrow p(x/2)=\tfrac12<1.$$
Hence there exists $\alpha_0$ such that $p(m_\alpha)\le 1$ for all $\alpha\ge\al
pha_0$; i.e., $m_\alpha\in B$ eventually. Thus $x/2\in\overline{B}^{\,X}$. But $
B$ is closed in $X$, so $x/2\in B\subseteq M$. Since $M$ is a linear subspace, $
x=2\cdot(x/2)\in M$ — contradicting the choice $x\notin M$.

The contradiction shows that no point of $X$ lies outside $M$; hence $M=X$. $\bl
acksquare$

---

**Remark.** The argument shows more generally that every quasi-complete linear s
ubspace of a Hausdorff locally convex space is closed: a dense quasi-complete su
bspace must therefore coincide with the ambient space. The two ingredients are (
i) continuous seminorms provide bounded closed "unit balls" inside the subspace,
 which quasi-completeness forces to be complete, hence closed in the ambient Hau
sdorff space; and (ii) density lets us push any ambient point into the closure o
f such a ball, which is the ball itself.
