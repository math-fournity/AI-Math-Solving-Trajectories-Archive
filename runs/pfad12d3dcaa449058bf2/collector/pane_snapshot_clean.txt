Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $a+b\sqrt{-3}$ and $a-b\sqr
t{-3}$ are cubes in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-3}]$ given $\gcd(a,b)=1$ and $a^2+3b^2=c^
3$.

**Answer: Yes, both are cubes in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-3}]$.**

## Proof

Let $\omega = \frac{-1+\sqrt{-3}}{2}$, so $\sqrt{-3}=1+2\omega$ and $\mathbb{Z}[
\omega]$ (the Eisenstein integers) is a UFD. Note $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-3}]=\{m+n\o
mega\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]: n\equiv 0\pmod{2}\}$ is the subring of index 2.

**Step 1: Setup in $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$.** Write $\alpha = a+b\sqrt{-3}=(a+b)+2b
\omega$ and $\bar\alpha = a-b\sqrt{-3}=(a-b)-2b\omega$. Then $N(\alpha)=\alpha\b
ar\alpha=a^2+3b^2=c^3$.

**Step 2: $c$ is odd and $3\nmid c$.** Since $\gcd(a,b)=1$, at most one is even.
 If both odd: $c^3\equiv 4\pmod 8$, impossible. So exactly one is even, giving $
c^3\equiv 1$ or $3\pmod 4$, hence $c$ odd. If $3\mid c$: then $27\mid a^2+3b^2$,
 forcing $3\mid a$; writing $a=3a'$ gives $9\mid 3a'^2+b^2$, so $b^2\equiv -3a'^
2\pmod 9\in\{0,6\}$, but $b^2\pmod 9\in\{0,1,4,7\}$ forces $3\mid b$, contradict
ing $\gcd(a,b)=1$. So $3\nmid c$.

**Step 3: $\gcd(\alpha,\bar\alpha)=1$ in $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$.** Any common prim
e divisor $\pi$ divides $\alpha+\bar\alpha=2a$ and $\alpha-\bar\alpha=2b\sqrt{-3
}$. Since $2$ is inert (prime) in $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ and $\sqrt{-3}$ is the pr
ime above $3$:
- $\pi\mid 2$: requires $2\mid\alpha$, i.e., $a\equiv b\pmod 2$ — excluded since
 $c$ is odd.
- $\pi\mid\sqrt{-3}$: requires $3\mid N(\alpha)=c^3$ — excluded since $3\nmid c$
.
- $\pi$ above $p\neq 2,3$: then $p\mid a$ and $p\mid b$, contradicting $\gcd(a,b
)=1$.

**Step 4: $\alpha = u\beta^3$ with $u=\pm 1$.** Since $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ is a
UFD and $\gcd(\alpha,\bar\alpha)=1$ with $\alpha\bar\alpha=c^3$, we get $\alpha=
u\beta^3$ for a unit $u\in\{\pm1,\pm\omega,\pm\omega^2\}$ and $\beta=p+q\omega\i
n\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$. Since $N(\beta)^3=c^3$ and $c$ is odd, $2\nmid\beta$, so n
ot both $p,q$ are even.

Compute $\beta^3 = (p^3-3pq^2+q^3)+3pq(p-q)\omega$. Denote $A=p^3-3pq^2+q^3$, $B
=3pq(p-q)$.

For $u=\omega$: $\omega\beta^3=-B+(A-B)\omega$, with $\omega$-coefficient $A-B=p
^3-3p^2q+q^3\equiv p+pq+q\pmod 2$, which equals $1$ unless both $p,q$ are even.
Since not both are even, this coefficient is **odd**, so $\omega\beta^3\notin\ma
thbb{Z}[\sqrt{-3}]$. The same parity computation applies to $u\in\{-\omega,\omeg
a^2,-\omega^2\}$ (each gives $\omega$-coefficient $\equiv p+pq+q\pmod 2$).

Since $\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-3}]$ (its $\omega$-coefficient $2b$ is even),
we must have $u\in\{1,-1\}$.

**Step 5: $\alpha$ is a cube in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-3}]$.** With $u=\pm 1$, $\alp
ha=(\pm\beta)^3$ in $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$. The three cube roots of $\alpha$ in $\
mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ are $\{\pm\beta,\pm\beta\omega,\pm\beta\omega^2\}$, with $\om
ega$-coefficients $\{q,\ p{-}q,\ {-}p\}$ (up to sign). At least one is even:
- $q$ even: $\pm\beta\in\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-3}]$. ✓
- $q$ odd, $p$ even: $\pm\beta\omega^2$ has coefficient $-p$ (even). ✓
- $q$ odd, $p$ odd: $\pm\beta\omega$ has coefficient $p-q$ (even). ✓

So some cube root of $\alpha$ lies in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-3}]$, making $\alpha$ a
 cube there.

**Step 6: $\bar\alpha$ is also a cube.** Replacing $b$ by $-b$ preserves $\gcd(a
,b)=1$ and $a^2+3b^2=c^3$, so the same argument applies to $a-b\sqrt{-3}$.

**Verification with example:** $a=10,b=9$: $100+243=343=7^3$, $\gcd(10,9)=1$, an
d $10+9\sqrt{-3}=(-2+\sqrt{-3})^3$. ✓
