**Answer: Yes.** Every Banach space is a complete topological vector space (in t
he sense that every Cauchy *net* converges).

The subtlety is that "Banach" only asserts sequential completeness (every Cauchy
 *sequence* converges in norm), while the TVS notion of completeness demanded he
re requires every Cauchy *net* to converge. In a metric space these two notions
coincide, and the norm topology is metrizable. Here is the direct argument.

---

**Proof.**

Let $(V, \|\cdot\|)$ be a Banach space, equipped with the norm topology $\mathca
l{T}$. The neighborhoods of $0$ are generated by the balls $B(0,\varepsilon)=\{v
:\|v\|<\varepsilon\}$, so a net $(v_\alpha)_{\alpha\in I}$ is Cauchy in the TVS
sense iff

$$\forall\,\varepsilon>0\;\exists\,\alpha_0\in I\;\forall\,\alpha,\beta\geq\alph
a_0:\;\|v_\alpha-v_\beta\|<\varepsilon.$$

Let $(v_\alpha)$ be such a Cauchy net. We construct a convergent subsequence-ind
exed cofinal subnet and use it to extract a limit.

**Step 1: Extract a Cauchy sequence.** For each $n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}$, by th
e Cauchy property applied with $\varepsilon=1/n$, there exists $\beta_n\in I$ su
ch that

$$\alpha,\beta\geq\beta_n\;\Longrightarrow\;\|v_\alpha-v_\beta\|<\tfrac{1}{n}.$$

Using that $I$ is directed, replace $\beta_n$ inductively by an upper bound of $
\{\beta_1,\dots,\beta_n\}$ so that the sequence $(\beta_n)$ is **increasing** (i
.e. $\beta_1\leq\beta_2\leq\cdots$). Set

$$x_n := v_{\beta_n}.$$

For $m,n\geq N$, both $\beta_m,\beta_n\geq\beta_N$, hence

$$\|x_m-x_n\|=\|v_{\beta_m}-v_{\beta_n}\|<\tfrac{1}{N}.$$

Thus $(x_n)$ is a Cauchy **sequence** in $V$.

**Step 2: Use Banach completeness.** Since $V$ is Banach, $(x_n)$ converges: the
re exists $x\in V$ with $x_n\to x$, i.e. $\|x_n-x\|\to 0$.

**Step 3: Show the original net converges to $x$.** Let $\varepsilon>0$. Choose
$N$ with $1/N<\varepsilon/2$. Since $x_n\to x$, pick $n\geq N$ with $\|x_n-x\|<\
varepsilon/2$. Now for any $\alpha\geq\beta_n$ (note $\beta_n\geq\beta_N$, so $1
/n\leq 1/N$):

$$\|v_\alpha-x_n\|=\|v_\alpha-v_{\beta_n}\|<\tfrac{1}{n}\leq\tfrac{1}{N}<\tfrac{
\varepsilon}{2}.$$

Therefore, for all $\alpha\geq\beta_n$,

$$\|v_\alpha-x\|\leq\|v_\alpha-x_n\|+\|x_n-x\|<\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}+\tfrac{\va
repsilon}{2}=\varepsilon.$$

Since $\varepsilon>0$ was arbitrary, $v_\alpha\to x$ in the norm topology.

**Conclusion.** Every Cauchy net in $V$ converges, so $(V,\mathcal{T})$ is a com
plete topological vector space. The essential reason this works is that the norm
 topology is **metrizable**, and in any metrizable uniform space sequential comp
leteness implies net/filter completeness: a Cauchy net always contains a cofinal
 Cauchy sequence that determines its limit. $\blacksquare$
