**Proof.**

We construct an explicit arithmetic progression and verify the required properti
es using modular arithmetic.

**Key observations.**

*Squares modulo 4.* For any integer $a$, we have $a^2 \equiv 0$ or $1 \pmod{4}$.
 Hence for any integers $a, b$:
$$a^2 + b^2 \equiv 0,\, 1,\, \text{or } 2 \pmod{4}.$$
In particular, **no integer congruent to $3 \pmod{4}$ is a sum of two squares**
(of any integers, hence not of natural numbers).

*Cubes modulo 9.* For any integer $a$, reducing $a$ modulo $9$ and cubing gives:
$$a^3 \equiv 0,\, 1,\, \text{or } 8 \pmod{9}$$
(since $0^3\!=\!0,\; (\pm1)^3\!=\!\pm1,\; (\pm2)^3\!=\!\pm8,\; (\pm3)^3\!=\!0,\;
 (\pm4)^3\!\equiv\!\pm1 \pmod{9}$). Therefore the possible residues of $a^3 + b^
3$ modulo $9$ are:
$$\{0+0,\; 0+1,\; 0+8,\; 1+1,\; 1+8,\; 8+8\} = \{0,\, 1,\, 2,\, 7,\, 8\} \pmod{9
}.$$
In particular, **no integer congruent to $3 \pmod{9}$ is a sum of two cubes** (o
f any integers, hence not of natural numbers).

**Construction.**

Since $\gcd(4, 9) = 1$, by the Chinese Remainder Theorem the system
$$r \equiv 3 \pmod{4}, \qquad r \equiv 3 \pmod{9}$$
has the solution $r \equiv 3 \pmod{36}$.

Define the arithmetic progression
$$a_n = 36n + 3, \qquad n = 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$$
i.e., $3,\; 39,\; 75,\; 111,\; 147,\; \ldots$ with common difference $d = 36$.

**Verification.**

- **Infinite and non-constant:** The common difference is $36 > 0$, so the progr
ession is infinite and strictly increasing. ✓

- **Natural numbers:** Every term $a_n = 36n + 3 \geq 3$ is a positive integer.
✓

- **Not a sum of two squares:** For every $n$,
$$a_n = 36n + 3 \equiv 3 \pmod{4},$$
so $a_n$ cannot be expressed as $x^2 + y^2$ for any integers $x, y$, and in part
icular not for natural numbers $x, y$. ✓

- **Not a sum of two cubes:** For every $n$,
$$a_n = 36n + 3 \equiv 3 \pmod{9},$$
and since $3 \notin \{0, 1, 2, 7, 8\}$, $a_n$ cannot be expressed as $x^3 + y^3$
 for any integers $x, y$, and in particular not for natural numbers $x, y$. ✓

**Conclusion.** The arithmetic progression $\{36n + 3 : n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots\}$ i
s an infinite, non-constant arithmetic progression of natural numbers, every ter
m of which is neither a sum of two squares nor a sum of two cubes of natural num
bers. $\blacksquare$
