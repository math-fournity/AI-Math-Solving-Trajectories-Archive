## Solution

**Setup.** Let $t = \frac{2}{x} - 1$ (so $x = \frac{2}{t+1}$, and $x \in (0,2)$
gives $t > 0$), and let $s = 3 - y$ (so $y = 3 - s$, and $y \in (0,3)$ gives $s
\in (0,3)$).

**Rewriting the constraint.** We compute:
$$x(2-x) = \frac{2}{t+1}\cdot\frac{2t}{t+1} = \frac{4t}{(t+1)^2}, \qquad \frac{8
}{x(2-x)} = \frac{2(t+1)^2}{t} = 2t + 4 + \frac{2}{t}.$$

The constraint $\frac{8}{x(2-x)} + \frac{1}{3-y} = 10$ becomes:
$$2t + 4 + \frac{2}{t} + \frac{1}{s} = 10 \implies \frac{1}{s} = 6 - 2t - \frac{
2}{t} =: q,$$
where $q > \frac{1}{3}$ (since $s < 3$) and $q > 0$ (since $s > 0$). The feasibl
e region requires $t + \frac{1}{t} < \frac{17}{6}$.

**Rewriting the objective.** We have $\frac{12}{x} = 6(t+1) = 6t+6$, and $y = 3
- \frac{1}{q}$, so:
$$F(t) = 6t + 6 - \frac{1}{4}\!\left(3 - \frac{1}{q}\right)^{\!2} = 6t + \frac{1
5}{4} + \frac{3}{2q} - \frac{1}{4q^2}, \qquad q = 6 - 2t - \frac{2}{t}.$$

**Finding the critical point.** Since $\frac{dq}{dt} = -2 + \frac{2}{t^2}$:
$$F'(t) = 6 + \left(-\frac{3}{2q^2} + \frac{1}{2q^3}\right)\!\left(-2 + \frac{2}
{t^2}\right).$$

**Testing $t = \frac{1}{2}$:** Then $q = 6 - 1 - 4 = 1$, and $\frac{dq}{dt}\big|
_{t=1/2} = -2 + 8 = 6$, so:
$$F'\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right) = 6 + \left(-\tfrac{3}{2} + \tfrac{1}{2}\right)(
6) = 6 + (-1)(6) = 0. \checkmark$$

**Confirming it is a minimum.** With $\frac{d^2q}{dt^2} = -\frac{4}{t^3}$, at $t
=\frac{1}{2}$, $q=1$:
$$F''\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right) = \left(\frac{3}{q^3} - \frac{3}{2q^4}\right)\!
\left(\frac{dq}{dt}\right)^{\!2} + \left(-\frac{3}{2q^2}+\frac{1}{2q^3}\right)\!
\frac{d^2q}{dt^2} = \frac{3}{2}\cdot 36 + (-1)(-32) = 54 + 32 = 86 > 0.$$

So $t = \frac{1}{2}$ is a local minimum.

**Boundary behavior.** At the domain boundary $q = \frac{1}{3}$ (i.e., $s=3$, $y
=0$), the objective becomes $6t + 6$. Solving $t + \frac{1}{t} = \frac{17}{6}$ g
ives $t \approx 0.413$ or $t \approx 2.420$, yielding objectives $\approx 8.48$
and $\approx 20.5$, both exceeding the interior value. Since $F$ is continuous o
n the feasible interval and has a unique critical point (a local min) with large
r values at both endpoints, $t = \frac{1}{2}$ gives the **global minimum**.

**Verification.** At $t = \frac{1}{2}$: $x = \frac{2}{3/2} = \frac{4}{3}$, $q =
1$, $s = 1$, $y = 2$.

- Constraint: $\frac{8}{(4/3)(2/3)} + \frac{1}{1} = 9 + 1 = 10$. ✓
- Objective: $\frac{12}{4/3} - \frac{2^2}{4} = 9 - 1 = 8$. ✓

The minimum value of $\dfrac{12}{x} - \dfrac{y^2}{4}$ is $\boxed{8}$.
