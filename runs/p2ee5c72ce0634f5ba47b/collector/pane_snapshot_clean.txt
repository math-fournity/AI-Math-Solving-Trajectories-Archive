**Counterexample.** Define
$$M = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1+i \\ 1+i & 1+2i \end{pmatrix}.$$

**Step 2: $\operatorname{Re}(M)$ is positive definite.** We have
$$\operatorname{Re}(M) = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} =: H.$$
$H$ is real symmetric with $\det(H) = 3\cdot 1 - 1\cdot 1 = 2 > 0$ and $\operato
rname{tr}(H) = 4 > 0$, so both eigenvalues $2 \pm \sqrt{2}$ are positive. Hence
$H \succ 0$.

$$\det(M - \lambda I) = (3-\lambda)(1+2i-\lambda) - (1+i)^2.$$
Since $(1+i)^2 = 2i$ and $(3-\lambda)(1+2i-\lambda) = \lambda^2 - (4+2i)\lambda

$$\det(M - \lambda I) = \lambda^2 - (4+2i)\lambda + (3+4i).$$
The discriminant is
$$\Delta = (4+2i)^2 - 4(3+4i) = (16 + 16i - 4) - (12 + 16i) = 0.$$
Thus $M$ has a single eigenvalue $\lambda = \frac{4+2i}{2} = 2+i$ with algebraic

part, and is not diagonalizable. Therefore, a complex symmetric matrix with posi
