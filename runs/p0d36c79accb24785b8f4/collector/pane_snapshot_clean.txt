## Solution

We seek all natural numbers $n$ such that $7 \mid 2^n - 1$, equivalently
$$2^n \equiv 1 \pmod{7}.$$

**Step 1: Compute the order of $2$ modulo $7$.**

Since $\gcd(2,7)=1$, the powers of $2$ cycle modulo $7$. Direct computation:
- $2^1 \equiv 2 \pmod{7}$
- $2^2 \equiv 4 \pmod{7}$
- $2^3 = 8 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$

Thus the multiplicative order of $2$ modulo $7$ is $\operatorname{ord}_7(2) = 3$
.

**Step 2: Characterize all $n$ with $2^n \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$.**

A standard fact: if $g$ has order $d$ modulo $m$, then $g^n \equiv 1 \pmod{m}$ i
f and only if $d \mid n$.

*Proof of the fact.* Write $n = qd + r$ with $0 \le r < d$. Then
$$g^n = g^{qd+r} = (g^d)^q \cdot g^r \equiv 1^q \cdot g^r = g^r \pmod{m}.$$
So $g^n \equiv 1 \pmod{m} \iff g^r \equiv 1 \pmod{m}$. By minimality of the orde
r $d$, this happens only when $r = 0$, i.e. $d \mid n$. ∎

Applying this with $g = 2$, $d = 3$, $m = 7$:
$$2^n \equiv 1 \pmod{7} \iff 3 \mid n.$$

**Step 3: Verify.**

For $n = 3k$: $2^{3k} - 1 = 8^k - 1 = (8-1)(8^{k-1} + 8^{k-2} + \cdots + 1) = 7
\cdot (\cdots)$, divisible by $7$. ✓

For $n$ not divisible by $3$, say $n = 3k+1$ or $n = 3k+2$: then $2^n \equiv 2$
or $4 \pmod{7}$, neither equal to $1$, so $7 \nmid 2^n - 1$. ✓

**Conclusion.**

The natural numbers $n$ for which $2^n - 1$ is divisible by $7$ are exactly the
positive multiples of $3$:
$$\boxed{n \in \{3, 6, 9, 12, \dots\} = \{n \in \mathbb{N} : 3 \mid n\}}.$$
