### 1. Variational (self-adjoint) formulation

Write the eigenvalue problem as
$$\mathcal{L}X = \lambda X, \qquad \mathcal{L} = -\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}, \qquad 0
<x<L,$$
with
$$X'(0)=0 \quad\text{(essential / geometric BC)},\qquad hX(L)+X'(L)=0 \quad\text
{(natural BC)}.$$

$\mathcal{L}$ is formally self-adjoint. For any smooth $X$ satisfying the essent
ial condition $X'(0)=0$, integrate by parts:
$$\langle \mathcal{L}X,X\rangle = \int_{0}^{L}(-X'')X\,dx = \bigl[-X'X\bigr]_{0}
^{L} + \int_{0}^{L}(X')^{2}\,dx.$$

The boundary term: at $x=0$, $-X'(0)X(0)=0$; at $x=L$, $-X'(L)X(L)$. Using the n
atural BC $X'(L)=-hX(L)$,
$$-X'(L)X(L) = -(-hX(L))\,X(L) = h\,[X(L)]^{2}.$$

Hence
$$\langle \mathcal{L}X,X\rangle = \int_{0}^{L}[X'(x)]^{2}\,dx + h\,[X(L)]^{2}.$$

### 2. Rayleigh quotient

The **Rayleigh quotient** for this problem is therefore
$$\boxed{\;R[X] \;=\; \frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}\bigl[X'(x)\bigr]^{2}\,dx \
;+\; h\,\bigl[X(L)\bigr]^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}\bigl[X(x)\bigr]^{2}\,dx}
\;}$$

defined for every admissible trial function with $X'(0)=0$ (and $X\not\equiv 0$)
. The natural BC $hX(L)+X'(L)=0$ is **built into** $R$ through the boundary term
 $h[X(L)]^{2}$; it need not be imposed on the trial functions. By the min–max pr
inciple,
$$\lambda_{1} = \min_{X'(0)=0,\;X\neq 0} R[X],$$
so any admissible trial gives an **upper bound** $\lambda_{1}\le R[X]$.

### 3. Trial-function estimate of $\lambda_{1}$

Use the one-parameter polynomial family (automatically satisfying $X'(0)=0$):
$$X_{a}(x) = 1 + a\,x^{2}, \qquad X_{a}'(x)=2ax,\quad X_{a}'(0)=0\;\checkmark.$$

Compute the three ingredients:

- $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}[X_{a}']^{2}\,dx = \int_{0}^{L}4a^{2}x^{2}\,dx = \fr
ac{4a^{2}L^{3}}{3}.$
- $X_{a}(L)=1+aL^{2}$, so $h[X_{a}(L)]^{2}=h(1+aL^{2})^{2}$.
- $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}X_{a}^{2}\,dx = \int_{0}^{L}(1+2ax^{2}+a^{2}x^{4})\,
dx = L+\frac{2aL^{3}}{3}+\frac{a^{2}L^{5}}{5}.$

Substituting into $R$:
$$R(a) = \frac{\dfrac{4a^{2}L^{3}}{3} + h(1+aL^{2})^{2}}{L+\dfrac{2aL^{3}}{3}+\d
frac{a^{2}L^{5}}{5}}.$$

Introduce the dimensionless parameter $t=aL^{2}$ and $\alpha = hL$ (so $\alpha>0
$):
$$\boxed{\;R(t) \;=\; \frac{\dfrac{4}{3}\,t^{2} \;+\; \dfrac{\alpha}{L^{2}}\,(1+
t)^{2}\,L^{2}\Big/\,L^{2}}{1+\dfrac{2t}{3}+\dfrac{t^{2}}{5}} \;=\; \frac{\dfrac{
4}{3}t^{2}+\dfrac{h}{L}(1+t)^{2}}{1+\dfrac{2t}{3}+\dfrac{t^{2}}{5}}\;}$$

i.e. with $\alpha=h/L$,
$$R(t)=\frac{\alpha+2\alpha t+\bigl(\tfrac{4}{3}+\alpha\bigr)t^{2}}{1+\tfrac{2}{
3}t+\tfrac{1}{5}t^{2}}.$$

**Crude bound (constant trial, $t=0$):** $R(0)=\alpha=h/L$, giving
$$\lambda_{1}\le \frac{h}{L}.$$

**Optimal one-parameter bound:** minimize $R(t)$. Setting $dR/dt=0$ (i.e. $N'D-N
D'=0$ with $N$ the numerator, $D$ the denominator) yields, after simplification
(the $t^{3}$ terms cancel),
$$\frac{4\alpha}{3}+\Bigl(\frac{8}{3}+\frac{8\alpha}{5}\Bigr)t+\Bigl(\frac{8}{9}
+\frac{4\alpha}{15}\Bigr)t^{2}=0.$$

This quadratic in $t$ has two real roots; the one giving the smaller $R$ is $t^{
*}$, and the estimate is
$$\boxed{\;\lambda_{1}\;\approx\;R(t^{*})\;=\;\frac{\alpha+2\alpha t^{*}+\bigl(\
tfrac{4}{3}+\alpha\bigr)(t^{*})^{2}}{1+\tfrac{2}{3}t^{*}+\tfrac{1}{5}(t^{*})^{2}
},\qquad \alpha=\frac{h}{L},\;}$$
where $t^{*}$ is the minimizing root of
$$\Bigl(\frac{8}{9}+\frac{4\alpha}{15}\Bigr)t^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{8}{3}+\frac{8\alph
a}{5}\Bigr)t+\frac{4\alpha}{3}=0.$$

Because $R$ is stationary (not just evaluated) at $t^{*}$, this is the **Rayleig
h–Ritz** estimate from the trial space $\{1+ax^{2}\}$, and it is a rigorous uppe
r bound:
$$\lambda_{1}\le R(t^{*})\le R(0)=\frac{h}{L}.$$

### 4. Consistency check

The exact eigenvalues satisfy $X=\cos(\sqrt{\lambda}\,x)$ (from $X'(0)=0$), with
 the transcendental equation $\sqrt{\lambda}\sin(\sqrt{\lambda}\,L)=h\cos(\sqrt{
\lambda}\,L)$, i.e. $\sqrt{\lambda}\tan(\sqrt{\lambda}\,L)=h$. For small $hL$ th
e first root is $\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}L\approx hL$, giving $\lambda_{1}\approx h^{2
}$… in fact $\lambda_{1}\approx h/L$ for small $hL$ (since $\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}L\
tan(\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}L)=hL$ and for small argument $\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}L\cdot\sq
rt{\lambda_{1}}L\approx hL$, so $\lambda_{1}L^{2}\approx hL$, i.e. $\lambda_{1}\
approx h/L$) — matching the crude trial bound $R(0)=h/L$, and the optimized $R(t
^{*})$ improves on it. The Rayleigh quotient and the variational structure are t
herefore consistent with the exact spectral problem.
