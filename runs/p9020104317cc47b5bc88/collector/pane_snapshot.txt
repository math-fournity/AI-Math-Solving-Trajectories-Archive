## Exterior Derivative Product Rule

**Claim.** For a $p$-form $\omega$ and a $q$-form $\eta$ on a smooth manifold $M
$,
$$d(\omega \wedge \eta) = (d\omega)\wedge \eta + (-1)^p\,\omega \wedge (d\eta).$
$

---

### Setup and conventions

Let $M$ be a smooth $n$-manifold with local coordinates $x^1,\dots,x^n$. A $p$-f
orm is written
$$\omega = \frac{1}{p!}\,\omega_{i_1\dots i_p}\,dx^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{i
_p},$$
with $\omega_{i_1\dots i_p}$ totally antisymmetric; summation over repeated indi
ces is understood. The exterior derivative is
$$d\omega = \frac{1}{p!}\,\partial_j\omega_{i_1\dots i_p}\,dx^j\wedge dx^{i_1}\w
edge\cdots\wedge dx^{i_p},$$
and it satisfies $d(dx^i)=0$ and $d(f)=\partial_j f\,dx^j$ for functions. The we
dge product obeys graded commutativity
$$dx^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{i_p}\wedge dx^{j_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{j_q}
\;=\; (-1)^{pq}\,dx^{j_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{j_q}\wedge dx^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\
wedge dx^{i_p},$$
i.e. swapping a $p$-block past a $q$-block costs a sign $(-1)^{pq}$.

---

### Proof

Write both forms in components (using the $1/p!$ normalization so the antisymmet
ry is built into the coefficients):
$$\omega = \frac{1}{p!}\,\omega_{I}\,dx^I,\qquad \eta = \frac{1}{q!}\,\eta_{J}\,
dx^J,$$
where $I=(i_1,\dots,i_p)$, $J=(j_1,\dots,j_q)$ are multi-indices and $dx^I = dx^
{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{i_p}$.

**Step 1 — Compute the left-hand side.** Using linearity of $d$ and the Leibniz
rule for $d$ on a product of functions times the fact that $d(dx^K)=0$ for any m
ulti-index $K$ (since $d^2=0$ on coordinate functions), we apply $d$ to the sing
le tensor-product-like term $\omega_I\eta_J\,dx^I\wedge dx^J$:
$$d(\omega_I\,\eta_J\,dx^I\wedge dx^J) = d(\omega_I\eta_J)\wedge dx^I\wedge dx^J
.$$
By the ordinary product rule for functions, $d(\omega_I\eta_J) = (d\omega_I)\,\e
ta_J + \omega_I\,(d\eta_J)$, so
$$d(\omega\wedge\eta) = \frac{1}{p!\,q!}\Big[(d\omega_I)\,\eta_J + \omega_I\,(d\
eta_J)\Big]\wedge dx^I\wedge dx^J.$$
Since $d\omega_I = \partial_k\omega_I\,dx^k$ and $d\eta_J = \partial_k\eta_J\,dx
^k$, this splits into two terms:
$$d(\omega\wedge\eta) = \underbrace{\frac{1}{p!\,q!}\,\partial_k\omega_I\,\eta_J
\,dx^k\wedge dx^I\wedge dx^J}_{T_1} + \underbrace{\frac{1}{p!\,q!}\,\omega_I\,\p
artial_k\eta_J\,dx^k\wedge dx^I\wedge dx^J}_{T_2}.$$

**Step 2 — Identify $T_1$.** The first term is exactly
$$T_1 = \frac{1}{p!\,q!}\,\partial_k\omega_I\,\eta_J\,dx^k\wedge dx^I\wedge dx^J
 = (d\omega)\wedge\eta,$$
since $d\omega = \frac{1}{p!}\partial_k\omega_I\,dx^k\wedge dx^I$ and wedging wi
th $\eta = \frac{1}{q!}\eta_J\,dx^J$ gives precisely $T_1$.

**Step 3 — Reduce $T_2$ to $(-1)^p\omega\wedge d\eta$.** In $T_2$ the 1-form $dx
^k$ sits to the **left** of the $p$-block $dx^I$. To match the order in $\omega\
wedge d\eta$ — where $d\eta = \frac{1}{q!}\partial_k\eta_J\,dx^k\wedge dx^J$ pla
ces $dx^k$ to the **right** of $dx^I$ (because $\omega\wedge d\eta = \frac{1}{p!
q!}\omega_I\partial_k\eta_J\,dx^I\wedge dx^k\wedge dx^J$) — we must move $dx^k$
past the $p$ basis 1-forms $dx^{i_1},\dots,dx^{i_p}$. Each swap of adjacent 1-fo
rms contributes a factor $-1$, so moving $dx^k$ across the $p$-block gives
$$dx^k\wedge dx^I = (-1)^p\,dx^I\wedge dx^k.$$
Substituting,
$$T_2 = \frac{1}{p!\,q!}\,\omega_I\,\partial_k\eta_J\,(-1)^p\,dx^I\wedge dx^k\we
dge dx^J = (-1)^p\,\omega\wedge(d\eta).$$

**Step 4 — Combine.**
$$d(\omega\wedge\eta) = T_1 + T_2 = (d\omega)\wedge\eta + (-1)^p\,\omega\wedge(d
\eta). \qquad\blacksquare$$

---

### Consistency checks

- **$p=0$:** $\omega=f$ a function. The formula gives $d(f\eta)=df\wedge\eta + f
\,d\eta$, the ordinary Leibniz rule — correct.
- **$q=0$:** $\eta=g$ a function. Gives $d(\omega\,g)=d\omega\,g + (-1)^p\omega\
wedge dg$, i.e. $d(\omega g) = (d\omega)g + (-1)^p\omega\,dg$ — the signed Leibn
iz rule for a form times a function — correct.
- **$d^2=0$ consequence:** Applying $d$ to both sides and using $d^2=0$ on the l
eft, one recovers the graded Jacobi-type identity, consistent with the sign $(-1
)^p$.
- **Degree check:** $d\omega$ is a $(p+1)$-form and $d\eta$ a $(q+1)$-form; both
 sides are $(p+q+1)$-forms — degrees match.

The sign $(-1)^p$ arises solely from commuting the single $dx^k$ (carrying the d
erivative of $\eta$) past the $p$ basis 1-forms of $\omega$, which is the entire
 content of the graded Leibniz rule.

### PROOF COMPLETE
