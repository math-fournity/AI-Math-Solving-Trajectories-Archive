## Proof

**The answer is yes: $\dim X \le n$.**

In fact, the result holds for *every* metric space — completeness is not needed.
 We work with the covering dimension $\dim$ (which coincides with the small and
large inductive dimensions for all metric spaces).

### Ingredients

**(1) Stone's theorem.** Every open cover of a metric space has a $\sigma$-discr
ete open refinement (a countable union of discrete families).

**(2) Discrete union lemma.** If $\{U_\alpha\}_{\alpha\in A}$ is a discrete fami
ly in $X$ (every point has a neighborhood meeting at most one $U_\alpha$) with $
\dim U_\alpha \le n$ for all $\alpha$, then $\dim\!\bigl(\bigcup_\alpha U_\alpha
\bigr) \le n$.

*Proof of the lemma.* Set $W = \bigcup_\alpha U_\alpha$ and let $\mathcal{G}$ be
 an open cover of $W$. For each $\alpha$, the family $\{G \cap U_\alpha : G \in
\mathcal{G}\}$ is an open cover of $U_\alpha$, so it admits a refinement $\mathc
al{H}_\alpha$ of order $\le n{+}1$ with each member open in $U_\alpha$. Since $\
{U_\alpha\}$ is discrete, each $U_\alpha$ is **clopen** in $W$: every point of $
W \setminus U_\alpha$ has a neighborhood missing $U_\alpha$, so $W \setminus U_\
alpha$ is open. Hence every $H \in \mathcal{H}_\alpha$ (open in $U_\alpha$, whic
h is open in $W$) is open in $W$. The union $\mathcal{H} = \bigcup_\alpha \mathc
al{H}_\alpha$ is an open refinement of $\mathcal{G}$ covering $W$. Its order is
$\le n{+}1$: any $x \in W$ lies in a *unique* $U_\alpha$, so $x$ belongs only to
 members of $\mathcal{H}_\alpha$, which has order $\le n{+}1$. Thus $\dim W \le
n$. $\square$

**(3) Perfect normality.** In a metric space, every open set is $F_\sigma$.

**(4) Countable sum theorem.** In a normal space, if $X = \bigcup_{i=1}^\infty F
_i$ with each $F_i$ closed and $\dim F_i \le n$, then $\dim X \le n$.

**(5) Hereditariness.** For metric spaces, $\dim A \le \dim X$ for every $A \sub
seteq X$.

### Main argument

Consider the open cover $\mathcal{B} = \{B_{\epsilon/2}(x) : x \in X\}$ of $X$.
By Stone's theorem **(1)**, $\mathcal{B}$ has a $\sigma$-discrete open refinemen
t
$$\mathcal{V} = \bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty} \mathcal{V}_k,$$
where each $\mathcal{V}_k$ is a discrete family of open sets.

**Each member has dimension $\le n$.** Every $V \in \mathcal{V}$ is contained in
 some $B_{\epsilon/2}(x) \subseteq B_\epsilon(x)$, so by hereditariness **(5)**:
$$\dim V \le \dim B_\epsilon(x) \le n.$$

**Each layer has dimension $\le n$.** Set $W_k = \bigcup_{V \in \mathcal{V}_k} V
$. Since $\mathcal{V}_k$ is discrete and each member has $\dim \le n$, the discr
ete union lemma **(2)** gives $\dim W_k \le n$. Each $W_k$ is open in $X$ (union
 of open sets), and $X = \bigcup_{k=1}^\infty W_k$.

**Reduce to a countable union of closed sets.** By perfect normality **(3)**, ea
ch open $W_k$ is $F_\sigma$: there exist closed sets $F_{k,j} \subseteq W_k$ wit
h $W_k = \bigcup_{j=1}^\infty F_{k,j}$. By hereditariness **(5)**, $\dim F_{k,j}
 \le \dim W_k \le n$.

**Apply the countable sum theorem.** We have
$$X = \bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty} F_{k,j},$$
a countable union of closed sets, each of dimension $\le n$. Since $X$ is a metr
ic space (hence normal), the countable sum theorem **(4)** yields
$$\dim X \le n. \qquad \blacksquare$$

### Remark

The completeness hypothesis is not used in the proof; the conclusion $\dim X \le
 n$ holds for every metric space satisfying the local dimension condition. The k
ey tool that replaces separability is Stone's theorem ($\sigma$-discrete refinem
ents in metric spaces), which allows us to decompose the uncountable cover into
countably many discrete layers, each of controlled dimension, and then apply the
 countable sum theorem.
