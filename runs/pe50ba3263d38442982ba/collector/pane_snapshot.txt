## Answer

The statement is **false** in both directions.

### Direction 1: Self-adjoint does **not** imply bounded.

**Counterexample (Position operator).** Let $H = L^2(\mathbb{R})$ and define
$$
(Qf)(x) = x\,f(x), \qquad D(Q) = \{f \in L^2(\mathbb{R}) : xf \in L^2(\mathbb{R}
)\}.
$$
The domain $D(Q)$ is dense (it contains $C_c(\mathbb{R})$). $Q$ is symmetric: fo
r $f,g \in D(Q)$,
$$
\langle Qf, g\rangle = \int x f(x)\overline{g(x)}\,dx = \langle f, Qg\rangle.
$$
One checks that $D(Q^*) = D(Q)$ and $Q^* = Q$, so $Q$ is **self-adjoint**. Yet $
Q$ is **unbounded**: taking $f_n = \mathbf{1}_{[n,n+1]}$, we have $\|f_n\|=1$ bu
t $\|Qf_n\|^2 = \int_n^{n+1} x^2\,dx \to \infty$.

Hence a self-adjoint operator need not be bounded. ∎

### Direction 2: Bounded does **not** imply self-adjoint.

**Counterexample (Bounded symmetric operator with dense, proper domain).** Let $
H = L^2[0,1]$ and set $D(T) = C[0,1]$, which is dense in $H$. Define $T: D(T) \t
o H$ by $(Tf)(x) = f(x)$, i.e. the restriction of the identity to $C[0,1]$.

- **Bounded:** $\|Tf\|_{L^2} = \|f\|_{L^2}$, so $\|T\|=1$.
- **Symmetric:** $\langle Tf, g\rangle = \langle f, Tg\rangle$ for $f,g \in C[0,
1]$.
- **Not self-adjoint:** Since $T$ is bounded, every $y \in L^2[0,1]$ yields a bo
unded functional $x \mapsto \langle Tx, y\rangle = \langle x, y\rangle$ on $D(T)
$, so $D(T^*) = L^2[0,1]$ and $T^* = I$ (the identity on all of $L^2[0,1]$). But
 $D(T) = C[0,1] \subsetneq L^2[0,1] = D(T^*)$, so $T \neq T^*$.

Thus a bounded symmetric operator with dense domain need not be self-adjoint — i
t is self-adjoint only when its domain is the **entire** Hilbert space. ∎

### Conclusion

Both implications fail:
- There exist unbounded self-adjoint operators (e.g. the position operator $Q$).
- There exist bounded symmetric operators with dense domain that are not self-ad
joint (e.g. the identity restricted to $C[0,1] \subset L^2[0,1]$).

Therefore the statement

> "A symmetric operator $T$ with a dense domain is self-adjoint if and only if i
t is bounded"

is **false**.
