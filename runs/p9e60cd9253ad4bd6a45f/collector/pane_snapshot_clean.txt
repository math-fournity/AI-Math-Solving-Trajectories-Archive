## Solution

**Yes, such a function exists.** We exhibit one explicitly and verify both prope
rties.

Let $\phi = \dfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$ (the golden ratio, satisfying $\phi^2 = \phi
+ 1$). Define

$$f(n) = \lfloor (n+1)\phi \rfloor - 1, \qquad n \in \mathbb{N}.$$

### Key Lemma

**For every positive integer $k$,**
$$\big\lfloor \lfloor k\phi \rfloor \cdot \phi \big\rfloor = \lfloor k\phi \rflo

). Then

$$\lfloor k\phi\rfloor \cdot \phi = (k\phi - \theta)\,\phi = k\phi^2 - \theta\ph
i = k(\phi+1) - \theta\phi = \lfloor k\phi\rfloor + \theta + k - \theta\phi = \l
floor k\phi\rfloor + k + \theta(1-\phi).$$

$$\big\lfloor \lfloor k\phi\rfloor \cdot \phi \big\rfloor = \lfloor k\phi\rfloor
 + k - 1. \qquad \blacksquare$$

$$f(1) = \lfloor 2\phi \rfloor - 1 = \lfloor 1+\sqrt{5} \rfloor - 1 = \lfloor 3.

### Verification that $f$ is strictly increasing

For each $n$, $(n+2)\phi - (n+1)\phi = \phi > 1$, so $\lfloor (n+2)\phi\rfloor -

### Conclusion

The function $f(n) = \lfloor (n+1)\phi \rfloor - 1$ maps $\mathbb{N}$ to $\mathb
