## Solution

**Yes, it is true.** The statement holds for any vector space $V$ (finite or inf
inite dimensional) over any field $F$, provided the functionals $\varphi_1, \ldo
ts, \varphi_k$ are linearly independent.

### Proof

Define the linear map
$$\Phi: V \to F^k, \qquad \Phi(x) = \bigl(\varphi_1(x), \ldots, \varphi_k(x)\big
r).$$

We claim that $\Phi$ is **surjective**. Once this is established, the result fol
lows immediately: let $e_1, \ldots, e_k$ be the standard basis of $F^k$, and cho
ose $x_j \in V$ with $\Phi(x_j) = e_j$. Then $\varphi_i(x_j) = \delta_{ij}$ as r
equired.

**Surjectivity of $\Phi$.** Suppose for contradiction that $\operatorname{im}(\P
hi) \subsetneq F^k$. Since $F^k$ is **finite-dimensional**, every proper subspac
e has a nontrivial annihilator: there exists a nonzero linear functional $\ell \
in (F^k)^*$ that vanishes on $\operatorname{im}(\Phi)$.

Every linear functional on $F^k$ has the form $\ell(a_1, \ldots, a_k) = \sum_{i=
1}^k c_i\, a_i$ for some scalars $c_1, \ldots, c_k \in F$, not all zero. The con
dition $\ell|_{\operatorname{im}(\Phi)} = 0$ means that for every $x \in V$:
$$0 = \ell\bigl(\Phi(x)\bigr) = \sum_{i=1}^k c_i\, \varphi_i(x).$$

Since this holds for all $x \in V$, we obtain
$$\sum_{i=1}^k c_i\, \varphi_i = 0 \quad \text{in } V^*.$$

But the $\varphi_i$ are linearly independent, so $c_1 = \cdots = c_k = 0$, contr
adicting the choice of $\ell$ as nonzero.

Therefore $\operatorname{im}(\Phi) = F^k$, i.e., $\Phi$ is surjective. ∎

### Remark on the role of infinite dimension

The argument uses **only** the finite-dimensionality of the codomain $F^k$ (to g
uarantee that a proper subspace has a nontrivial annihilator). The dimension of
$V$ plays no role: the proof works identically whether $\dim V$ is finite or inf
inite. The hypothesis $\dim V = \infty$ is included in the problem to test wheth
er one might suspect a failure in infinite dimensions — but no such failure occu
rs for **finitely many** linearly independent functionals.

(The situation would be different for *infinitely many* functionals: the evaluat
ion map $V \to F^I$ need not be surjective onto the full product, and algebraic
reflexivity $V \cong V^{**}$ fails when $\dim V = \infty$. But that is not what
is asked here.)
