**Answer: No.**

**Counterexample: the even shift.**

Let $m=2$ and define $X \subset \Sigma_2 = \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}$ to be the set o
f all bi-infinite sequences in which every two consecutive occurrences of the sy
mbol $1$ are separated by an **even** number of $0$'s (sequences with at most on
e $1$ are included automatically). Equivalently, $X$ is the subshift obtained by
 forbidding the infinite family of words
$$\mathcal{F} = \{\,1\,0^{2k+1}\,1 : k \geq 0\,\}.$$

**$X$ is a non-empty, compact, closed, $T$-invariant subset of $\Sigma_2$.** It
is non-empty (the all-$0$ sequence and the sequence $\ldots 0\,0\,1\,0\,0\,1\,0\
,0\,1\ldots$ both belong). It is $T$-invariant because the defining property (pa
rity of gaps between $1$'s) is shift-invariant. It is closed: if $x^{(n)} \to x$
 in $X$ and $x$ contained a forbidden block $1\,0^{2k+1}\,1$, then for large eno
ugh $n$ the sequence $x^{(n)}$ would also contain that same finite block, contra
dicting $x^{(n)} \in X$. Compactness follows from being a closed subset of the c
ompact space $\Sigma_2$.

**$X$ is not a subshift of finite type.** Suppose for contradiction that $X$ is
an SFT of order $n$, i.e., $X$ is characterized by forbidding a finite set $\mat
hcal{F}_n$ of words of length $n$. Then a bi-infinite sequence belongs to $X$ if
 and only if none of its length-$n$ subwords lies in $\mathcal{F}_n$.

The word $w = 1\,0^{2n+1}\,1$ is **forbidden** (it has an odd number of $0$'s be
tween two $1$'s), so $w$ must contain some word $f \in \mathcal{F}_n$ as a subwo
rd. The length-$n$ subwords of $w$ are exactly:
- $1\,0^{n-1}$ (the leftmost block),
- $0^{n}$ (interior blocks),
- $0^{n-1}\,1$ (the rightmost block).

We claim **each** of these appears as a subword of some **allowed** word (a word
 occurring in $X$), and hence none of them can belong to $\mathcal{F}_n$:

- $1\,0^{n-1}$ is a subword of $1\,0^{2n}\,1$, which is allowed (even gap). ✓
- $0^{n}$ is a subword of $1\,0^{2n}\,1$, which is allowed. ✓
- $0^{n-1}\,1$ is a subword of $1\,0^{2n}\,1$, which is allowed. ✓

Therefore every length-$n$ subword of $w$ occurs in some allowed word, so none o
f them is in $\mathcal{F}_n$. But then $w$ contains no forbidden block of length
 $n$, contradicting the assumption that $w$ is forbidden by $\mathcal{F}_n$.

This contradiction shows that $X$ cannot be an SFT of any order $n$.

**Conclusion.** The even shift $X$ is a non-empty, compact, closed, $T$-invarian
t subset of $\Sigma_2$ on which $T|_X$ is **not** a subshift of finite type. Hen
ce the answer to the question is **no**: $T|_A$ is not necessarily a subshift of
 finite type.
