## Proof

Let triangle $ABC$ have circumcircle $\Gamma$, and let $P, Q$ be two points on $
\Gamma$. Denote by $\ell_P$ and $\ell_Q$ the Simson lines of $P$ and $Q$ respect
ively.

**Claim.** The angle that the Simson line $\ell_P$ makes with side $BC$ equals $
90^\circ - \angle PCA$ (as a directed angle).

*Proof of claim.* Let $D_P$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $P$ to $BC$, a
nd $E_P$ the foot from $P$ to $CA$. By definition of the Simson line, $\ell_P$ i
s the line $D_PE_P$. Since
$$\angle PD_PC = 90^\circ \quad (PD_P \perp BC), \qquad \angle PE_PC = 90^\circ
\quad (PE_P \perp CA),$$
the four points $P, D_P, C, E_P$ are concyclic, lying on the circle with diamete
r $PC$.

The angle between $\ell_P$ and $BC$ is the directed angle $\angle(E_PD_P,\, D_PC
)$. By the inscribed-angle theorem for directed angles applied to the cyclic qua
drilateral $P D_P C E_P$,
$$\angle(E_PD_P,\, D_PC) = \angle(E_PP,\, PC) = \angle E_PPC.$$
In the right triangle $PE_PC$ (right at $E_P$),
$$\angle E_PPC = 90^\circ - \angle PCE_P = 90^\circ - \angle PCA.$$
This proves the claim. $\square$

By the same argument applied to $Q$, the angle that $\ell_Q$ makes with $BC$ equ
als $90^\circ - \angle QCA$.

**Angle between the two Simson lines.** Subtracting the two directions (both mea
sured from the same reference line $BC$), the angle between $\ell_P$ and $\ell_Q
$ is
$$\bigl|(90^\circ - \angle PCA) - (90^\circ - \angle QCA)\bigr| = \bigl|\angle Q
CA - \angle PCA\bigr| = \angle PCQ.$$
(With directed angles the equality $\angle(\ell_P,\ell_Q) = \angle PCQ$ holds ex
actly, without absolute values.)

**Conclusion.** The angle $\angle PCQ$ is an inscribed angle in $\Gamma$ subtend
ing the arc $PQ$ (the arc not containing $C$). By the inscribed-angle theorem,
$$\angle PCQ = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\widehat{PQ}.$$
Hence

> **The angle between the Simson lines of two points $P$ and $Q$ on the circumci
rcle equals one half of the arc $PQ$ between them.**

The result is independent of the auxiliary vertex $C$ used above, since every in
scribed angle subtending the same arc $PQ$ has the same measure (up to the usual
 orientation/supplement convention); the (unoriented) angle between the two line
s corresponds to half the measure of the arc $PQ$.
