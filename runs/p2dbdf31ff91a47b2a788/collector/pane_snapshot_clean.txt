## Solution

We evaluate the integral

$$I(\zeta) = \int_0^{\pi} e^{i\zeta\cos x}\,dx, \qquad \zeta > 0.$$

**Step 1: Split into real and imaginary parts.**

Writing $e^{i\zeta\cos x} = \cos(\zeta\cos x) + i\sin(\zeta\cos x)$,

$$I(\zeta) = \int_0^{\pi}\cos(\zeta\cos x)\,dx + i\int_0^{\pi}\sin(\zeta\cos x)\
,dx.$$

**Step 2: The imaginary part vanishes by symmetry.**

Substitute $x \mapsto \pi - x$ in the sine integral. Since $\cos(\pi - x) = -\co
s x$ and $dx \to -dx$ with limits swapping back to $[0,\pi]$,

$$\int_0^{\pi}\sin(\zeta\cos x)\,dx = \int_0^{\pi}\sin(\zeta\cos(\pi-x))\,dx = \
int_0^{\pi}\sin(-\zeta\cos x)\,dx = -\int_0^{\pi}\sin(\zeta\cos x)\,dx.$$

Hence $\int_0^{\pi}\sin(\zeta\cos x)\,dx = 0$.

**Step 3: Identify the real part with the Bessel function $J_0$.**

The Bessel function of the first kind of order zero has the standard integral re
presentation (Poisson/Bessel):

$$J_0(\zeta) = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi}\cos(\zeta\cos x)\,dx.$$

This representation follows from the generating-function expansion $e^{i\zeta\co
s x} = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} i^n J_n(\zeta) e^{inx}$ together with the ortho
gonality $\int_0^{\pi} e^{inx}\,dx = 0$ for $n\neq 0$ (odd $n$ give zero by the
sine symmetry above; even nonzero $n$ give $\frac{e^{in\pi}-1}{in}=0$), leaving
only the $n=0$ term:

$$\int_0^{\pi} e^{i\zeta\cos x}\,dx = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} i^n J_n(\zeta)\i
nt_0^{\pi}e^{inx}\,dx = J_0(\zeta)\cdot\pi.$$

**Step 4: Combine.**

$$I(\zeta) = \int_0^{\pi}\cos(\zeta\cos x)\,dx + i\cdot 0 = \pi J_0(\zeta).$$

**Result.**

$$\boxed{\int_0^{\pi} e^{i\zeta\cos x}\,dx = \pi\, J_0(\zeta)}$$

where $J_0$ is the Bessel function of the first kind of order zero.
