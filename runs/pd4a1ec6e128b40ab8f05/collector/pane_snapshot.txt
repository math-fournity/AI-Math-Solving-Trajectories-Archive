## Proof

We find all functions $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ satisfying
$$f(2x+f(y))+f(f(y))=4x+8y \quad \text{for all } x,y\in\mathbb{R}. \tag{P(x,y)}$
$

**Step 1: $f$ is determined by a single parameter, and is linear.**

Fix $y\in\mathbb{R}$. As $x$ ranges over $\mathbb{R}$, the quantity $t:=2x+f(y)$
 ranges over all of $\mathbb{R}$. Substituting $x=\tfrac{t-f(y)}{2}$ into $(P)$:
$$f(t)+f(f(y))=4\cdot\frac{t-f(y)}{2}+8y=2t-2f(y)+8y.$$
Hence
$$f(t)=2t+\underbrace{\bigl(-2f(y)+8y-f(f(y))\bigr)}_{=:c_y} \quad \text{for all
 } t\in\mathbb{R}. \tag{1}$$

Since the left side $f(t)$ is independent of $y$, the constant $c_y=-2f(y)+8y-f(
f(y))$ must be **independent of $y$**; call it $c$. Therefore
$$f(t)=2t+c \quad \text{for all } t\in\mathbb{R}. \tag{2}$$

**Step 2: Determine $c$.**

Using (2): $f(y)=2y+c$ and $f(f(y))=f(2y+c)=2(2y+c)+c=4y+3c$. Substituting into
the definition of $c$:
$$c=-2f(y)+8y-f(f(y))=-2(2y+c)+8y-(4y+3c)=(-4y+8y-4y)+(-2c-3c)=-5c.$$
Thus $6c=0$, so $c=0$.

**Step 3: Verification.**

With $c=0$, we have $f(t)=2t$. Check:
$$f(2x+f(y))+f(f(y))=f(2x+2y)+f(2y)=2(2x+2y)+2(2y)=4x+4y+4y=4x+8y.\ \checkmark$$

**Conclusion.** The unique solution is $\boxed{f(x)=2x}$.
