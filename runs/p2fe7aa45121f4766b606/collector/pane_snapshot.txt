## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. If $f''$ exists on $(0,1)$, then $f''$ is Lebesgue measurable.

**Key tool:** A classical theorem of Baire states that the derivative of any fun
ction (continuous or not) is of **Baire class 1**, i.e., it is the pointwise lim
it of a sequence of continuous functions. Baire class 1 functions are Borel meas
urable, and Borel measurability implies Lebesgue measurability.

We apply this to $g = f'$.

---

### Setup

Since $f$ has a continuous derivative, $f' : [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}$ is continuous
. Suppose $f''$ exists on $(0,1)$ (one-sided derivatives at the endpoints are ir
relevant to the measurability question, so we work on the open interval).

### Construction of the approximating sequence

For each integer $n \geq 1$, define $g_n : (0,1) \to \mathbb{R}$ by

$$
g_n(x) = \begin{cases} n\bigl(f'(x + \tfrac{1}{n}) - f'(x)\bigr) & \text{if } x
+ \tfrac{1}{n} \leq 1, \\[4pt] n\bigl(f'(1) - f'(1 - \tfrac{1}{n})\bigr) & \text
{if } x + \tfrac{1}{n} > 1. \end{cases}
$$

**Each $g_n$ is continuous on $(0,1)$.** On the interval $(0,\, 1 - \tfrac{1}{n}
]$, $g_n$ is a difference of the continuous function $f'$, hence continuous. On
$(1 - \tfrac{1}{n},\, 1)$, $g_n$ is constant, hence continuous. At the junction
$x_0 = 1 - \tfrac{1}{n}$, both pieces agree:

$$
\lim_{x \to x_0^-} g_n(x) = n\bigl(f'(1) - f'(1 - \tfrac{1}{n})\bigr) = g_n(x_0)
,
$$

so $g_n$ is continuous at $x_0$ as well. Thus $g_n \in C\bigl((0,1)\bigr)$.

### Pointwise convergence to $f''$

Fix any $x \in (0,1)$. For all sufficiently large $n$ (namely $n > \frac{1}{1-x}
$), we have $x + \frac{1}{n} < 1$, so

$$
g_n(x) = \frac{f'\!\bigl(x + \tfrac{1}{n}\bigr) - f'(x)}{1/n}.
$$

Since $f''(x)$ exists, this difference quotient converges to $f''(x)$:

$$
\lim_{n \to \infty} g_n(x) = f''(x).
$$

(The finitely many initial terms where $x + \frac{1}{n} \geq 1$ take the constan
t fallback value and do not affect the limit.)

### Conclusion

$f''$ is the pointwise limit of the sequence $(g_n)$ of continuous functions on
$(0,1)$. Therefore:

1. **$f''$ is Baire class 1** (by definition: pointwise limit of continuous func
tions).
2. **Baire class 1 $\subseteq$ Borel measurable.** Every Baire class 1 function
is Borel measurable, since it is $\limsup$ of Borel (in fact continuous) functio
ns, and $\limsup$ of measurable functions is measurable.
3. **Borel measurable $\subseteq$ Lebesgue measurable.** Every Borel set is Lebe
sgue measurable, so every Borel measurable function is Lebesgue measurable.

Hence $f''$ is Lebesgue measurable. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The hypothesis that $f'$ is *continuous* is actually stronger than n
eeded. The Baire theorem applies to the derivative of *any* function: if $g$ is
differentiable (with $g$ not necessarily continuous), then $g'$ is Baire class 1
. Here we only used continuity of $f'$ to ensure each $g_n$ is continuous; even
without it, one can show $g'$ is Baire class 1 by a similar (slightly more caref
ul) argument. The codomain constraint $f:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ plays no role in the arg
ument.
