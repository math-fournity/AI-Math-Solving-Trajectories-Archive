## Proof

**The statement is TRUE.**

**Key fact.** For any separated morphism of schemes $f: Y \to X$, every section
$s: X \to Y$ (a morphism satisfying $f \circ s = \mathrm{id}_X$) is a closed imm
ersion.

**Proof of the key fact.** Let $f: Y \to X$ be separated and let $s: X \to Y$ be
 a section. Consider the two morphisms over $X$:
$$\mathrm{id}_Y,\; s \circ f : Y \longrightarrow Y.$$
Both are $X$-morphisms since $f \circ \mathrm{id}_Y = f$ and $f \circ (s \circ f
) = (f \circ s) \circ f = f$.

Form the fiber product of the diagonal and the graph:
$$E := Y \times_{(Y \times_X Y)} Y,$$
where the two maps $Y \to Y \times_X Y$ are:
- the diagonal $\Delta_f = (\mathrm{id}_Y, \mathrm{id}_Y)$, and
- the map $(\mathrm{id}_Y, s \circ f)$.

Since $f$ is **separated**, the diagonal $\Delta_f: Y \to Y \times_X Y$ is a clo
sed immersion. Closed immersions are stable under base change, so the projection
 $E \to Y$ is a closed immersion.

The scheme $E$ is precisely the equalizer (locus of agreement) of $\mathrm{id}_Y
$ and $s \circ f$, i.e.,
$$E = \{ y \in Y : y = s(f(y)) \} = s(X).$$

The section $s: X \to Y$ factors as
$$X \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} E \xhookrightarrow{\;\;} Y,$$
where the first map is an isomorphism (with inverse $f|_E: E \to X$, since for $
e \in E$ we have $e = s(f(e))$, so $f|_E \circ s = \mathrm{id}_X$ and $s \circ f
|_E = \mathrm{id}_E$), and the second is the closed immersion $E \hookrightarrow
 Y$ from above.

Therefore $s: X \to Y$ is a closed immersion. $\square$

**Application to the problem.** A vector bundle $\pi: E \to X$ over a scheme $X$
 is locally of the form $\mathbb{A}^n_U \to U$ for an open cover $\{U\}$ of $X$.
 In particular, $\pi$ is an **affine** morphism, and every affine morphism is **
separated**. Hence the hypothesis "the projection of the vector bundle is a sepa
rated morphism" is satisfied (indeed automatically for any vector bundle over a
scheme).

By the key fact above, any section $s: X \to E$ of $\pi$ is a closed immersion,
i.e., it induces a closed embedding of $X$ into $E$.

**Conclusion.** The statement is **true**: any section of a vector bundle over a
 scheme whose projection is separated (which is always the case) induces a close
d embedding.
