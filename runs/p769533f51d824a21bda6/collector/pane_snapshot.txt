# Fundamental Group of $\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus \mathbb{Z}^3_{>0}$

## Setup and notation

Let $S = \mathbb{Z}^3_{>0} = \{(a,b,c) \in \mathbb{Z}^3 : a,b,c \geq 1\} \subset
 \mathbb{R}^3$, and let $X = \mathbb{R}^3 \setminus S$.

**Claim:** $\pi_1(X) = 0$ (the trivial group), i.e., $X$ is simply connected.

## Key observations

**Observation 1: $S$ is closed and discrete in $\mathbb{R}^3$.**

$S$ is a subset of the integer lattice $\mathbb{Z}^3$, which is discrete (every
point is isolated, with nearest neighbor at distance $1$) and closed in $\mathbb
{R}^3$ (its complement is a union of open unit cubes). Hence $S$ is closed and d
iscrete. In particular, $S$ is **locally finite**: every bounded subset of $\mat
hbb{R}^3$ meets $S$ in only finitely many points.

**Observation 2: An open ball in $\mathbb{R}^n$ ($n \geq 3$) minus finitely many
 interior points is simply connected.**

Let $B$ be an open ball in $\mathbb{R}^n$ and let $\{p_1,\dots,p_k\} \subset B$.
 The space $B \setminus \{p_1,\dots,p_k\}$ deformation retracts onto a wedge $\b
igvee_{i=1}^{k} S^{n-1}$ of $k$ copies of $S^{n-1}$: surround each $p_i$ by a sm
all $(n{-}1)$-sphere, connect these spheres to a common basepoint via disjoint a
rcs, and retract the complement onto the union of spheres and arcs. For $n \geq
3$, each $S^{n-1}$ is simply connected (since $n{-}1 \geq 2$), and by the Seifer
t–van Kampen theorem, a wedge of simply connected spaces is simply connected (th
e pairwise intersections are contractible neighborhoods of the wedge point). Hen
ce
$$\pi_1\!\left(B \setminus \{p_1,\dots,p_k\}\right) = 0.$$

## Proof of the claim

Let $\gamma : [0,1] \to X$ be a loop based at some point $x_0 \in X$. The image
$\gamma([0,1])$ is compact, hence bounded. Choose $R > 0$ such that $\gamma([0,1
]) \subset B(0, R)$ and $x_0 \in B(0, R)$.

By local finiteness of $S$, the set
$$S_R \;=\; S \cap \overline{B(0,\, R+1)}$$
is **finite**, say $S_R = \{s_1, \dots, s_k\}$.

Now define the open set
$$U \;=\; B(0,\, R+1) \setminus S_R \;\subset\; \mathbb{R}^3.$$

Since $S_R \supseteq S \cap B(0, R+1)$, every point of $S$ inside $B(0,R+1)$ is
removed from $U$, so
$$U \;=\; B(0, R+1) \setminus (S \cap B(0,R+1)) \;\subset\; \mathbb{R}^3 \setmin
us S \;=\; X.$$

The loop $\gamma$ has image in $B(0,R) \subset B(0,R+1)$ and avoids all of $S$,
hence avoids $S_R$ in particular. Therefore $\gamma$ is a loop in $U$.

By **Observation 2** (with $n=3$), $U = B(0,R+1) \setminus S_R$ is simply connec
ted. Thus $\gamma$ is null-homotopic **in $U$**. Since $U \subset X$, the same n
ull-homotopy witnesses that $\gamma$ is null-homotopic **in $X$**.

Since $\gamma$ was an arbitrary loop in $X$, we conclude
$$\pi_1(X) \;=\; \pi_1\!\left(\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus \mathbb{Z}^3_{>0}\right) \;
=\; 0.$$

## Conclusion

The fundamental group is **trivial**:

$$\boxed{\pi_1\!\left(\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus \mathbb{Z}^3_{>0}\right) \;=\; 0.}$
$

The essential reason is that $\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus \{\text{point}\}$ is alread
y simply connected (deformation retracting onto $S^2$), and any loop in $X$ is c
ompact hence confined to a bounded region where only finitely many points of the
 discrete set $S$ are relevant—reducing the problem to the finite-removal case,
which is simply connected in dimension $\geq 3$.
