The statement is **true**. Here is the justification.

---

**Theorem.** Let $u \in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}^k)$, $1 \le p < \
infty$. Then for almost every $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$, the slice $v_x := u(x,\cdot)
$ belongs to $W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^k)$, and its weak gradient satisfies
$$\nabla v_x(y) = \nabla_y u(x,y) \quad \text{for a.e. } y \in \mathbb{R}^k,$$
where $\nabla_y u$ denotes the weak gradient of $u$ with respect to the $y$-vari
ables.

---

**Proof.**

Write $z = (x,y) \in \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}^k$. Since $u \in W^{1,p}(\ma
thbb{R}^{n+k})$, we have $u \in L^p(\mathbb{R}^{n+k})$ and $\partial_{z_i} u \in
 L^p(\mathbb{R}^{n+k})$ for every $i = 1, \dots, n+k$.

**Step 1 (Slices are in $L^p$).** By Fubini–Tonelli applied to $|u|^p \in L^1(\m
athbb{R}^{n+k})$,
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \|u(x,\cdot)\|_{L^p(\mathbb{R}^k)}^p \,dx = \|u\|_{L^p(\ma
thbb{R}^{n+k})}^p < \infty.$$
Hence $u(x,\cdot) \in L^p(\mathbb{R}^k)$ for a.e. $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$. Likewise
, for each $j = 1,\dots,k$, applying Fubini–Tonelli to $|\partial_{y_j} u|^p$ gi
ves $(\partial_{y_j} u)(x,\cdot) \in L^p(\mathbb{R}^k)$ for a.e. $x$. Intersecti
ng the finitely many full-measure sets, there is a set $E \subset \mathbb{R}^n$
of full measure such that for every $x \in E$:
$$u(x,\cdot) \in L^p(\mathbb{R}^k), \qquad (\partial_{y_j} u)(x,\cdot) \in L^p(\
mathbb{R}^k), \quad j = 1,\dots,k.$$

**Step 2 (Weak gradient of the slice).** Fix $j \in \{1,\dots,k\}$. Let $\varphi
 \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^k)$ and $\psi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$. Then $\P
si(x,y) := \psi(x)\varphi(y) \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^{n+k})$ is an admissible
test function for the weak derivative $\partial_{y_j} u$, so
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+k}} u(x,y)\,\partial_{y_j}\!\big[\psi(x)\varphi(y)\big]\,d
x\,dy = -\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+k}} (\partial_{y_j} u)(x,y)\,\psi(x)\varphi(y)\,dx\
,dy.$$
Since $\partial_{y_j}[\psi(x)\varphi(y)] = \psi(x)\,\partial_{y_j}\varphi(y)$, e
xpanding both sides via Fubini (justified because $u(x,\cdot), (\partial_{y_j}u)
(x,\cdot) \in L^p$ and $\varphi, \partial_{y_j}\varphi \in L^{p'}(\mathbb{R}^k)$
 with $p' = p/(p-1)$, so the inner integrals are finite a.e.) yields
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \psi(x)\!\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^k} u(x,y)\,\partial_{y_j}\
varphi(y)\,dy\right] dx = -\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \psi(x)\!\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^k
} (\partial_{y_j} u)(x,y)\,\varphi(y)\,dy\right] dx.$$
Define
$$F_\varphi(x) := \int_{\mathbb{R}^k} u(x,y)\,\partial_{y_j}\varphi(y)\,dy + \in
t_{\mathbb{R}^k} (\partial_{y_j} u)(x,y)\,\varphi(y)\,dy.$$
The identity above says $\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \psi(x) F_\varphi(x)\,dx = 0$ for e
very $\psi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$. By the fundamental lemma of the calcul
us of variations, $F_\varphi(x) = 0$ for a.e. $x$ (the null set $N_\varphi$ may
a priori depend on $\varphi$).

**Step 3 (Removing the dependence on $\varphi$).** The space $C_c^\infty(\mathbb
{R}^k)$ is separable; let $\{\varphi_m\}_{m \ge 1}$ be a countable dense subset
(dense in the $W^{1,p'}$-topology on compact sets, or simply in the usual $C_c^\
infty$ LF-topology). Set $N := \bigcup_m N_{\varphi_m}$; this is still a null se
t. For $x \notin N$, the weak-derivative identity
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}^k} u(x,y)\,\partial_{y_j}\varphi_m(y)\,dy = -\int_{\mathbb{R}
^k} (\partial_{y_j} u)(x,y)\,\varphi_m(y)\,dy$$
holds for every $m$. For an arbitrary $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^k)$, ap
proximate $\varphi$ and $\partial_{y_j}\varphi$ in $L^{p'}$ by a subsequence of
$\{\varphi_m\}$ (using a common compact support). Since $u(x,\cdot), (\partial_{
y_j}u)(x,\cdot) \in L^p(\mathbb{R}^k)$ for $x \in E$, both sides of the identity
 are continuous functionals of $(\varphi, \partial_{y_j}\varphi) \in L^{p'} \tim
es L^{p'}$, so the identity passes to the limit and holds for all $\varphi \in C
_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^k)$.

**Step 4 (Conclusion).** For every $x \in E \setminus N$ (a set of full measure
in $\mathbb{R}^n$) and every $j = 1,\dots,k$,
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}^k} u(x,y)\,\partial_{y_j}\varphi(y)\,dy = -\int_{\mathbb{R}^k
} (\partial_{y_j} u)(x,y)\,\varphi(y)\,dy \qquad \forall\,\varphi \in C_c^\infty
(\mathbb{R}^k).$$
This is precisely the definition of $(\partial_{y_j} u)(x,\cdot)$ being the weak
 $\partial_{y_j}$-derivative of $u(x,\cdot)$. Since $u(x,\cdot) \in L^p(\mathbb{
R}^k)$ and each $(\partial_{y_j} u)(x,\cdot) \in L^p(\mathbb{R}^k)$, we conclude
 $v_x = u(x,\cdot) \in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^k)$ with
$$\nabla v_x(y) = \nabla_y u(x,y) \quad \text{for a.e. } y.$$

Moreover, integrating the pointwise identity $\|\nabla v_x\|_{L^p}^p = \|\nabla_
y u(x,\cdot)\|_{L^p}^p$ over $x$ and applying Fubini gives
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \|\nabla v_x\|_{L^p(\mathbb{R}^k)}^p\,dx = \|\nabla_y u\|_
{L^p(\mathbb{R}^{n+k})}^p \le \|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+k})}^p < \infty,$$
confirming that the slices' $W^{1,p}$-norms are $p$-integrable in $x$. $\blacksq
uare$

---

**Remark.** The result extends to $p = \infty$ by a duality/weak-* argument, and
 the same proof shows the analogous statement for $W^{1,p}(\Omega_1 \times \Omeg
a_2)$ with product domains. The key ingredients are: (i) Fubini–Tonelli to obtai
n $L^p$ slices, and (ii) testing the weak derivative identity against product te
st functions $\psi(x)\varphi(y)$ and using separability of $C_c^\infty$ to make
the exceptional set independent of $\varphi$.
