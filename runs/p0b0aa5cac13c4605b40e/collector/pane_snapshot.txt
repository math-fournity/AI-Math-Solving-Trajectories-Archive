## Proof

**Setup.** View $S^3 \subset \mathbb{R}^4$ and take the equator $S^2 = S^3 \cap
\{x_4 = 0\}$. This $S^2$ separates $S^3$ into two closed hemispheres
$$H_+ = S^3 \cap \{x_4 \ge 0\}, \qquad H_- = S^3 \cap \{x_4 \le 0\},$$
each homeomorphic to $D^3$, with common boundary $S^2$.

**Step 1: Identify each hemisphere after quotienting.** The space $X$ is obtaine
d from $S^3$ by the equivalence relation $x \sim -x$ for $x \in S^2$ (the equato
r). On each hemisphere $H_\pm \cong D^3$, the only identification is antipodal i
dentification on the boundary $\partial D^3 = S^2$. The quotient
$$D^3 / (x \sim -x \text{ for } x \in \partial D^3)$$
is precisely $\mathbb{R}P^3$ (the standard model of real projective 3-space). He
nce
$$X = \mathbb{R}P^3 \cup_{\mathbb{R}P^2} \mathbb{R}P^3,$$
two copies of $\mathbb{R}P^3$ glued along their common $\mathbb{R}P^2 = S^2/(x\s
im -x)$.

**Step 2: Apply van Kampen's theorem.** Let $U$ and $V$ be small open thickening
s of the two copies of $\mathbb{R}P^3$ in $X$, so that $U \simeq \mathbb{R}P^3$,
 $V \simeq \mathbb{R}P^3$, and $U \cap V \simeq \mathbb{R}P^2$. By van Kampen,
$$\pi_1(X) \cong \pi_1(U) *_{\pi_1(U\cap V)} \pi_1(V).$$

**Step 3: Compute the constituent groups and maps.**
- $\pi_1(\mathbb{R}P^3) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, generated by the image of
 a path in $D^3$ joining a boundary point to its antipode.
- $\pi_1(\mathbb{R}P^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.
- The inclusion $\mathbb{R}P^2 \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}P^3$ is the inclusion o
f the $2$-skeleton into the standard CW structure of $\mathbb{R}P^3$ (which has
one cell in each dimension $0,1,2,3$). Attaching the $3$-cell affects $\pi_2$ bu
t not $\pi_1$, so this inclusion induces an **isomorphism** $\pi_1(\mathbb{R}P^2
) \xrightarrow{\sim} \pi_1(\mathbb{R}P^3)$.

**Step 4: Compute the amalgamated product.** Writing
$$\pi_1(U) = \langle a \mid a^2 = 1\rangle, \quad \pi_1(V) = \langle b \mid b^2
= 1\rangle, \quad \pi_1(U\cap V) = \langle c \mid c^2 = 1\rangle,$$
both inclusion-induced maps send $c \mapsto a$ and $c \mapsto b$ (each an isomor
phism). The amalgamated free product therefore imposes $a = b$, giving
$$\pi_1(X) \cong \langle a \mid a^2 = 1\rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.$$

**Conclusion.** The fundamental group of $X$ is
$$\boxed{\pi_1(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.}$$
