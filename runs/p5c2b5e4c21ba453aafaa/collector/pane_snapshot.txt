Looking at this problem, I need to find the capacity of a non-stationary Gaussia
n channel where the signal fades as $1/i$, using Fano's inequality.

## Proof

**Channel model:** $Y_i = \frac{1}{i}X_i + Z_i$, where $Z_i \sim N(0,1)$ i.i.d.
Power constraint: $\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2(w) \leq 2$ for each codeword $w
 \in \{1,\ldots,2^{nR}\}$.

### Converse (Upper Bound): $C \leq 0$

Suppose a sequence of $(2^{nR}, n)$ codes achieves $P_e^{(n)} \to 0$. By **Fano'
s inequality**:

$$H(W \mid Y^n) \leq 1 + nR\, P_e^{(n)}$$

Therefore:
$$I(W; Y^n) = H(W) - H(W \mid Y^n) \geq nR - 1 - nR\,P_e^{(n)}$$

For $P_e^{(n)} \to 0$, for sufficiently large $n$: $I(W; Y^n) \geq nR - 1$.

By the **data processing inequality** ($W \to X^n \to Y^n$ forms a Markov chain)
:
$$I(W; Y^n) \leq I(X^n; Y^n)$$

**Bounding $I(X^n; Y^n)$:** Since the noise is independent and Gaussian:
$$I(X^n; Y^n) = h(Y^n) - h(Z^n) = h(Y^n) - \frac{n}{2}\log(2\pi e)$$

By the maximum entropy bound (with equality when $Y_i$ are independent):
$$h(Y^n) \leq \frac{1}{2}\log\left((2\pi e)^n \prod_{i=1}^n \operatorname{Var}(Y
_i)\right)$$

where $\operatorname{Var}(Y_i) = 1 + \frac{1}{i^2}\mathbb{E}[X_i^2]$. Therefore:
$$I(X^n; Y^n) \leq \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^n \log\left(1 + \frac{P_i}{i^2}\right)$
$
where $P_i = \mathbb{E}[X_i^2]$.

**Power constraint on $\{P_i\}$:** Averaging the per-codeword constraint $\sum_i
 x_i^2(w) \leq 2n$ over $W$ (uniformly distributed):
$$\sum_{i=1}^n P_i = \frac{1}{2^{nR}}\sum_{w=1}^{2^{nR}} \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2(w) \
leq 2n$$

**Maximizing via water-filling:** We maximize $\sum_{i=1}^n \log(1 + P_i/i^2)$ s
ubject to $\sum P_i \leq 2n$, $P_i \geq 0$. Viewing this as $n$ parallel Gaussia
n channels with noise variance $N_i = i^2$, the optimal solution is water-fillin
g:

$$P_i^* = (\mu - i^2)^+, \quad i = 1, \ldots, n$$

where $\mu$ is the water level. Let $k$ be the number of active channels ($i^2 <
 \mu$). The power constraint gives:

$$k\mu - \sum_{i=1}^k i^2 = 2n \implies k\mu - \frac{k(k+1)(2k+1)}{6} = 2n$$

The resulting mutual information is:
$$I^* = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^k \log\!\left(\frac{\mu}{i^2}\right) = \frac{1}{2}
\Big[k\log\mu - 2\log(k!)\Big]$$

**Asymptotic analysis:** For large $n$, from the power constraint with $\mu \app
rox k^2$:

$$\frac{2k^3}{3} \approx 2n \implies k \approx (3n)^{1/3}, \quad \mu \approx (3n
)^{2/3}$$

Applying Stirling's approximation $\log(k!) \approx k\log k - k$:

$$I^* \approx \frac{1}{2}\Big[k\cdot 2\log k - 2(k\log k - k)\Big] = \frac{1}{2}
\Big[2k\log k - 2k\log k + 2k\Big] = k \approx (3n)^{1/3}$$

**Combining the bounds:**
$$nR \leq I(W; Y^n) \leq I(X^n; Y^n) \leq (3n)^{1/3} + o\!\left(n^{1/3}\right)$$

$$R \leq \frac{(3n)^{1/3}}{n} = \frac{3^{1/3}}{n^{2/3}} \xrightarrow{n \to \inft
y} 0$$

Since this must hold for every $n$ with $P_e^{(n)} \to 0$, any achievable rate m
ust satisfy $R = 0$. Hence $C \leq 0$.

### Achievability: $C \geq 0$

Trivially, $C \geq 0$ (one can always send a single message with zero error).

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{C = 0}$$

The capacity is **zero**. The physical intuition is that the channel gain $1/i$
decays too rapidly: the total mutual information $\sum_{i=1}^n \log(1 + P_i/i^2)
$ grows only as $O(n^{1/3})$ under optimal water-filling, which is sublinear in
$n$. The fading $1/i^2$ in received energy is too severe to support any positive
 communication rate.
