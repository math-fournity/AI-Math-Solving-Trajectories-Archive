$$S = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{d(n) + \sum_{m=1}^{\nu_2(n)}(m-3)d(n/2^m)}{n}$$

## Simplifying the numerator

Write $n = 2^a k$ with $k$ odd, so $a = \nu_2(n)$. Since $\gcd(2^a, k)=1$:
- $d(n) = (a+1)\,d(k)$
- $d(n/2^m) = (a-m+1)\,d(k)$ for $1 \le m \le a$.

The numerator becomes $f(n) = d(k)\!\left[(a+1) + \sum_{m=1}^{a}(m-3)(a-m+1)\rig
ht]$.

Computing the inner sum (expanding $(m-3)(a-m+1) = (a+1)(m-3) - m^2 + 3m$ and su
mming):

$$\sum_{m=1}^{a}(m-3)(a-m+1) = \frac{a(a+1)(a-7)}{6}$$

So $f(n) = d(k)\,(a+1)\!\left[1 + \frac{a(a-7)}{6}\right] = d(k)\cdot\frac{(a+1)
(a-1)(a-6)}{6}$.

**Verification:** $a{=}0$: $f=d(k)=d(n)$ ✓ (empty sum). $a{=}1$: $f = d(k)\cdot\
frac{2\cdot0\cdot(-5)}{6}=0$ ✓ (matches $2d(k)-2d(k)=0$). $a{=}2$: $f = d(k)\cdo
t\frac{3\cdot1\cdot(-4)}{6}=-2d(k)$ ✓ (matches $3d(k)-4d(k)-d(k)$).

## Dirichlet series approach

Setting $g(a) = \frac{(a+1)(a-1)(a-6)}{6}$ and $x = 2^{-s}$, the generating func
tion is:

$$G(x) = \sum_{a=0}^\infty g(a)\,x^a = \frac{1}{6}\left[\frac{x(1{+}4x{+}x^2)}{(
1{-}x)^4} - \frac{6x(1{+}x)}{(1{-}x)^3} - \frac{x}{(1{-}x)^2} + \frac{6}{1{-}x}\
right]$$

Combining over $(1-x)^4$, the numerator simplifies to $6 - 24x + 24x^2 = 6(1-2x)
^2$, giving:

$$G(x) = \frac{(1-2x)^2}{(1-x)^4}$$

**Checked coefficients:** $g(0)=1$, $g(1)=0$, $g(2)=-2$, $g(3)=-4$ all match.

The full Dirichlet series factors as:
$$F(s) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{f(n)}{n^s} = G(2^{-s})\cdot\!\sum_{k\text{ odd}
}\frac{d(k)}{k^s}$$

Since $\sum_{k\text{ odd}} \frac{d(k)}{k^s} = \zeta(s)^2(1-2^{-s})^2$ (removing
the 2-Euler factor from $\zeta(s)^2$):

$$F(s) = \frac{(1-2^{1-s})^2}{(1-2^{-s})^4}\cdot\zeta(s)^2\cdot(1-2^{-s})^2 = \f
rac{(1-2^{1-s})^2}{(1-2^{-s})^2}\,\zeta(s)^2$$

## Taking the limit $s \to 1^+$

With $s = 1+\varepsilon$:
- $1 - 2^{1-s} = 1 - 2^{-\varepsilon} \approx \varepsilon\ln 2$
- $1 - 2^{-s} \to 1 - \tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{1}{2}$
- $\zeta(s) \approx \frac{1}{\varepsilon}$

Therefore:
$$S = \lim_{s\to 1^+} F(s) = \frac{(\varepsilon\ln 2)^2}{(1/2)^2}\cdot\frac{1}{\
varepsilon^2} = 4(\ln 2)^2 = (2\ln 2)^2 = (\ln 4)^2$$

## Result

$S = (\ln 4)^2 = (\ln m)^n$ with $m = 4$ and $n = 2$.

$$1000n + m = 1000(2) + 4 = \boxed{2004}$$
