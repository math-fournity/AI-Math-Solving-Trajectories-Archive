## Solution

We evaluate
$$S = \sum_{r=0}^{2019} \sum_{k=0}^{r} (-1)^k (k+1)(k+2) \binom{2021}{r-k}.$$

**Step 1: Recognize the inner sum as a convolution.**

Let $n = 2021$. Define two sequences:
- $a_k = (-1)^k (k+1)(k+2)$ for $k \ge 0$,
- $b_m = \binom{n}{m}$ for $m \ge 0$.

The inner sum is exactly the convolution
$$\sum_{k=0}^{r} a_k\, b_{r-k} = (a * b)_r.$$

**Step 2: Compute the generating functions.**

The generating function of $(a_k)$ is
$$A(x) = \sum_{k\ge 0} (-1)^k (k+1)(k+2)\, x^k.$$

Using the standard identity $\sum_{k\ge 0}(k+1)(k+2)\,x^k = \dfrac{2}{(1-x)^3}$
and substituting $x \mapsto -x$:
$$A(x) = \frac{2}{(1+x)^3}.$$

The generating function of $(b_m)$ is
$$B(x) = \sum_{m\ge 0}\binom{n}{m}x^m = (1+x)^n = (1+x)^{2021}.$$

**Step 3: Multiply the generating functions.**

The convolution has generating function
$$A(x)\,B(x) = \frac{2}{(1+x)^3}\cdot(1+x)^{2021} = 2\,(1+x)^{2018}.$$

Extracting the coefficient of $x^r$:
$$(a*b)_r = 2\binom{2018}{r}.$$

**Step 4: Sum over $r$.**

Therefore
$$S = \sum_{r=0}^{2019} 2\binom{2018}{r}.$$

Since $\binom{2018}{r} = 0$ for $r > 2018$, the term at $r = 2019$ vanishes, giv
ing
$$S = 2\sum_{r=0}^{2018}\binom{2018}{r} = 2 \cdot 2^{2018} = 2^{2019}.$$

**Final answer:**

$$\boxed{2^{2019}}$$
