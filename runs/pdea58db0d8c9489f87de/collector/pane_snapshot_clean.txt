## Proof

We evaluate
$$I = \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-2x} - e^{-x}}{x}\, dx.$$

**Method: Frullani's integral.**

Recall the Frullani formula: if $f$ is continuous on $[0,\infty)$, the limit $f(
\infty) = \lim_{x\to\infty} f(x)$ exists (finite), and the integral converges, t
hen for $a, b > 0$,
$$\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{f(ax) - f(bx)}{x}\, dx = \bigl(f(\infty) - f(0)\bigr)\
ln\frac{b}{a}.$$

Take $f(t) = e^{-t}$, which is continuous on $[0,\infty)$ with $f(0) = 1$ and $f
(\infty) = \lim_{t\to\infty} e^{-t} = 0$. Set $a = 2$, $b = 1$, so that $f(ax) =
 e^{-2x}$ and $f(bx) = e^{-x}$. The hypotheses of Frullani's theorem are satisfi
ed (the integrand decays exponentially at infinity and the singularity at $0$ is
 removable since $e^{-2x}-e^{-x} \sim -x$ as $x\to 0$). Therefore
$$I = \bigl(f(\infty) - f(0)\bigr)\ln\frac{b}{a} = (0 - 1)\ln\frac{1}{2} = (-1)(
-\ln 2) = \ln 2.$$

**Verification via differentiation under the integral sign.** For a self-contain
ed check, define
$$F(s) = \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-sx} - e^{-x}}{x}\, dx, \qquad s > 0.$$
Then $F(1) = 0$ and our target is $F(2)$. Differentiating under the integral sig
n (justified by dominated convergence, since $\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\frac{e
^{-sx}-e^{-x}}{x} = -e^{-sx}$ is dominated by $e^{-sx}$ on compact $s$-intervals
):
$$F'(s) = \int_{0}^{\infty} -e^{-sx}\, dx = -\frac{1}{s}.$$
Integrating from $s=1$ to $s=2$:
$$F(2) - F(1) = \int_{1}^{2} F'(s)\, ds = \int_{1}^{2} -\frac{1}{s}\, ds = -\ln
2.$$
Since $F(1) = 0$, we obtain $F(2) = -\ln 2 \cdot (-1)$... let me redo the sign:
$F(2) = F(1) + \int_1^2 F'(s)\,ds = 0 + (-\ln 2) = -\ln 2$.

Wait — this gives $-\ln 2$, contradicting the Frullani result. Let me recheck th
e sign of $F'(s)$:
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\frac{e^{-sx}-e^{-x}}{x} = \frac{-x\, e^{-sx}}{x} =
 -e^{-sx},$$
so $F'(s) = -\int_0^\infty e^{-sx}dx = -1/s$, and $F(2) = -\ln 2$.

But Frullani gave $+\ln 2$. The discrepancy is a sign convention in the Frullani
 formula. Re-examining: the standard Frullani formula is
$$\int_0^\infty \frac{f(ax)-f(bx)}{x}\,dx = \bigl(f(\infty)-f(0)\bigr)\ln\frac{a
}{b}.$$
With $\ln(a/b) = \ln 2$: $(0-1)\ln 2 = -\ln 2$. This now agrees with the differe
ntiation method.

So the correct value is $-\ln 2$.

**Conclusion.** Both methods agree:
$$\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-2x} - e^{-x}}{x}\, dx = -\ln 2.$$
