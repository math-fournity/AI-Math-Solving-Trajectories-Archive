## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** If $\Gamma \subseteq B(0,1) \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is a convex b
ody, then $S(\Gamma) \leq S(\partial B(0,1)) = \omega_n$, where $\omega_n$ denot
es the surface area of the unit sphere $S^{n-1}$.

The proof rests on **Cauchy's surface area formula**, which I state and justify
below.

---

### Cauchy's Surface Area Formula

**Theorem (Cauchy).** *For any convex body $K \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ ($n \geq 2$)
,*
$$S(K) = \frac{1}{\kappa_{n-1}} \int_{S^{n-1}} V_{n-1}\!\bigl(K \,\big|\, u^\per
p\bigr)\, d\sigma(u),$$
*where $\kappa_{n-1}$ is the $(n{-}1)$-dimensional volume of the unit ball in $\
mathbb{R}^{n-1}$, $K|u^\perp$ denotes the orthogonal projection of $K$ onto the
hyperplane $u^\perp$, and $d\sigma$ is surface measure on $S^{n-1}$.*

**Justification of the formula.** Assume first that $\partial K$ is $C^2$. For a
 direction $u \in S^{n-1}$, the projection $K|u^\perp$ is covered twice by $\par
tial K$ (once from each side), so

$$V_{n-1}(K|u^\perp) = \frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial K} |\langle \nu(x),\, u\rangle|
\, dS(x),$$

where $\nu(x)$ is the outward unit normal at $x \in \partial K$. Integrating ove
r $u \in S^{n-1}$ and exchanging the order of integration:

$$\int_{S^{n-1}} V_{n-1}(K|u^\perp)\,d\sigma(u) = \frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial K}\!
\left(\int_{S^{n-1}} |\langle \nu(x),u\rangle|\,d\sigma(u)\right) dS(x).$$

The inner integral is independent of $\nu(x)$ by rotational symmetry of $S^{n-1}
$. Using the standard slicing identity $\int_{S^{n-1}} f(u_1)\,d\sigma = \omega_
{n-1}\int_{-1}^{1} f(t)(1-t^2)^{(n-3)/2}\,dt$ with $f(t)=|t|$:

$$\int_{S^{n-1}} |u_1|\,d\sigma(u) = 2\omega_{n-1}\int_0^1 t(1-t^2)^{(n-3)/2}\,d
t = 2\omega_{n-1}\cdot\frac{1}{n-1} = 2\kappa_{n-1},$$

since $\omega_{n-1} = (n-1)\kappa_{n-1}$. Therefore

$$\int_{S^{n-1}} V_{n-1}(K|u^\perp)\,d\sigma(u) = \frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial K} 2
\kappa_{n-1}\,dS(x) = \kappa_{n-1}\, S(K),$$

which gives the formula. For general convex bodies, the result follows by approx
imation with smooth convex bodies (e.g., via Steiner symmetrization or mollifica
tion of the support function). $\square$

---

### Main Proof

Since $\Gamma$ is convex and $\Gamma \subseteq B(0,1)$, for every $u \in S^{n-1}
$ the orthogonal projection satisfies

$$\Gamma\,|\,u^\perp \;\subseteq\; B(0,1)\,|\,u^\perp \;=\; B_{n-1}(0,1) \;\subs
et\; u^\perp \cong \mathbb{R}^{n-1},$$

where $B_{n-1}(0,1)$ is the closed unit ball in the hyperplane $u^\perp$. The pr
ojection of a convex body is convex, and $(n{-}1)$-dimensional volume is monoton
e under inclusion, so

$$V_{n-1}(\Gamma\,|\,u^\perp) \;\leq\; V_{n-1}\!\bigl(B_{n-1}(0,1)\bigr) \;=\; \
kappa_{n-1} \qquad \text{for all } u \in S^{n-1}.$$

Applying Cauchy's formula:

$$S(\Gamma) = \frac{1}{\kappa_{n-1}}\int_{S^{n-1}} V_{n-1}(\Gamma\,|\,u^\perp)\,
d\sigma(u) \;\leq\; \frac{1}{\kappa_{n-1}}\int_{S^{n-1}} \kappa_{n-1}\,d\sigma(u
) = \int_{S^{n-1}} d\sigma(u) = \omega_n = S\!\bigl(\partial B(0,1)\bigr).$$

Hence

$$\boxed{S(\Gamma) \;\leq\; S\!\bigl(\partial B(0,1)\bigr).}$$

**Equality** holds when $\Gamma = B(0,1)$ (and only then, up to translation with
in the sphere—though containment in $B(0,1)$ forces $\Gamma = B(0,1)$ for equali
ty, since any strict containment reduces at least one projection). $\blacksquare
$
