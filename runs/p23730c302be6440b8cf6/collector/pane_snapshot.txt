## Answer

**No**, it is not true in general. A zero divisor in a finitely generated $k$-al
gebra need not generate an ideal of height zero.

## Counterexample

Let
$$A = k[x,y]\big/(x^{2},\,xy),$$
a finitely generated $k$-algebra (a quotient of a polynomial ring). Denote the i
mages of $x,y$ in $A$ by $\bar x, \bar y$.

### Step 1. $\bar y$ is a zero divisor.

In $A$ we have $\bar x\cdot\bar y = \overline{xy}=0$. Moreover $\bar x\neq 0$ in
 $A$: if $x\in(x^{2},xy)$, then $x = a\,x^{2}+b\,xy$ in $k[x,y]$, which is impos
sible by degree considerations (the left side has degree $1$, the right side has
 degree $\geq 2$). Hence $\bar y$ is a zero divisor, witnessed by the nonzero el
ement $\bar x$.

### Step 2. Primary decomposition and the primes of $A$.

We have
$$(x^{2},xy)=x(x,y)=(x)\cap(x^{2},y).$$

Indeed, $(x^{2},xy)\subseteq(x)$ and $(x^{2},xy)\subseteq(x^{2},y)$ (since $xy=x
\cdot y\in(x^{2},y)$), giving "$\subseteq$"; and any element of $(x)\cap(x^{2},y
)$, being divisible by $x$ and of the form $a\,x^{2}+b\,y$, forces $x\mid b$ (as
 $\gcd(x,y)=1$), so it lies in $x(x,y)=(x^{2},xy)$, giving "$\supseteq$."

Here $(x)$ is prime, and $(x^{2},y)$ is $(x,y)$-primary since $k[x,y]/(x^{2},y)\
cong k[x]/(x^{2})$ is local Artinian. Thus the associated primes of $A$ are
$$\operatorname{Ass}(A)=\{(x)/(x^{2},xy),\;(x,y)/(x^{2},xy)\},$$
with $(x)/(x^{2},xy)$ **minimal** and $(x,y)/(x^{2},xy)$ **embedded**.

The prime ideals of $A$ correspond to primes of $k[x,y]$ containing $(x^{2},xy)$
. A prime $P\supseteq(x^{2},xy)=(x)\cap(x^{2},y)$ must contain $(x)$ or $(x^{2},
y)$ (primality). Containing $(x^{2},y)$ forces $P\supseteq\operatorname{rad}(x^{
2},y)=(x,y)$, hence $P=(x,y)$ (maximal). Containing $(x)$ gives $P=(x)$ or $P=(x
,f(y))$ for irreducible $f\in k[y]$ (the maximal ideals above $(x)$). So the pri
mes of $A$ are:
- $(x)/(x^{2},xy)$ — the unique minimal prime, **height $0$**;
- $(x,f(y))/(x^{2},xy)$ for irreducible $f$ — each of **height $1$** (chain $(x)
/(x^{2},xy)\subsetneq(x,f(y))/(x^{2},xy)$, and $k[x,y]$ has dimension $2$, so no
thing lies strictly between).

### Step 3. $\operatorname{ht}(\bar y)=1$.

The primes of $A$ containing $\bar y$ correspond to primes $P\supseteq(x^{2},xy)
$ with $y\in P$. From the list above:
- $y\notin(x)$, so $(x)/(x^{2},xy)$ does not contain $\bar y$;
- $y\in(x,f(y))$ iff $f(y)\in(y)$ iff $f=y$ (up to units), giving $P=(x,y)$.

So the **only** prime of $A$ containing $\bar y$ is $(x,y)/(x^{2},xy)$, which ha
s height $1$. Therefore
$$\operatorname{ht}\big((\bar y)\big)=\min\{\operatorname{ht}(Q):Q\supseteq(\bar
 y)\}=\operatorname{ht}\big((x,y)/(x^{2},xy)\big)=1\neq 0.$$

### Conclusion

$\bar y$ is a zero divisor in the finitely generated $k$-algebra $A=k[x,y]/(x^{2
},xy)$, yet $\operatorname{ht}((\bar y))=1>0$. The statement is **false**.

The underlying reason: in a Noetherian ring the set of zero divisors is the unio
n of *all* associated primes, including embedded ones. An element lying in an em
bedded prime but in no minimal prime is a zero divisor whose principal ideal has
 positive height.
