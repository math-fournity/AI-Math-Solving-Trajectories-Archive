## Proof

**Setup.** $S_n$ is the square centered at $n+\tfrac{1}{2}$ with side length 1,
so its vertices are $n \pm \tfrac{i}{2}$ and $(n+1) \pm \tfrac{i}{2}$. The four
edges are:
- **Top/Bottom:** $w = x \pm \tfrac{i}{2}$, $x \in [n, n+1]$
- **Left/Right:** $w = n + iy$ and $w = (n+1)+iy$, $y \in [-\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac
{1}{2}]$

The integrand is $g(w) = \frac{f(w)}{w-z} = \frac{1}{w\,\tan(\pi w)\,(w-z)}$.

---

**Step 1: Top and bottom edges — $O(n^{-2})$.**

On the top edge, $w = x + \tfrac{i}{2}$. Using $\tan(\pi(x+\tfrac{i}{2})) = \fra
c{\sin(2\pi x)+i\sinh\pi}{\cos(2\pi x)+\cosh\pi}$:

$$|\tan(\pi w)|^2 = \frac{\sin^2(2\pi x)+\sinh^2\pi}{(\cos(2\pi x)+\cosh\pi)^2}
\geq \frac{\sinh^2\pi}{(\cosh\pi+1)^2} = \tanh^2\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right) >
0.$$

By conjugate symmetry the same bound holds on the bottom edge. For $n > 2|z|$: $
|w| \geq n$ and $|w-z| \geq n-|z| \geq n/2$, so

$$\left|\frac{f(w)}{w-z}\right| \leq \frac{1}{n \cdot \tanh(\pi/2) \cdot n/2} =
\frac{2}{n^2 \tanh(\pi/2)}.$$

Each horizontal edge has length 1, giving $|I_{\text{top}}| + |I_{\text{bottom}}
| \leq \frac{4}{n^2 \tanh(\pi/2)} = O(n^{-2})$.

---

**Step 2: Left and right edges — $O(n^{-3})$ (principal value).**

On the left edge, $w = n+iy$. Since $\tan(\pi(n+iy)) = i\tanh(\pi y)$:

$$I_{\text{left}} = \int_{-1/2}^{1/2} \frac{dy}{(n+iy)\,\tanh(\pi y)\,(n+iy-z)},
$$

interpreted as a principal value (the pole at $y=0$ corresponds to $w=n$ on the
contour). Substituting $u = \pi y$ and expanding for large $n$:

$$\frac{1}{(n+\tfrac{iu}{\pi})(n+\tfrac{iu}{\pi}-z)} = \frac{1}{n(n-z)} - \frac{
iu(2n-z)}{\pi\, n^2(n-z)^2} + O(n^{-3}).$$

Therefore:

$$I_{\text{left}} = \frac{1}{\pi}\,\text{PV}\!\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \frac{1}{\ta
nh u}\left[\frac{1}{n(n-z)} - \frac{iu(2n-z)}{\pi n^2(n-z)^2} + \cdots\right] du
.$$

- **Leading term:** $\frac{1}{\pi n(n-z)}\,\text{PV}\!\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \fra
c{du}{\tanh u} = 0$, since $1/\tanh u$ is **odd**.
- **Next term:** $-\frac{i(2n-z)}{\pi^2 n^2(n-z)^2}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \frac{u
}{\tanh u}\,du$. Here $u/\tanh u$ is **even** and bounded (with limit 1 at $u=0$
), so the integral is a finite constant $C$. This term is $O(n^{-3})$.

Thus $I_{\text{left}} = O(n^{-3})$. Replacing $n$ by $n+1$ gives $I_{\text{right
}} = O(n^{-3})$ identically.

---

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

Combining all four edges:

$$\oint_{\partial S_n} \frac{f(w)}{w-z}\,dw = \underbrace{O(n^{-2})}_{\text{top+
bottom}} + \underbrace{O(n^{-3})}_{\text{left+right}} = O(n^{-2}) \longrightarro
w 0.$$

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} \oint_{\partial S_n} \frac{f(w)}{w-z}\,dw = 0}$$
