## Proof

**Answer: Yes, always.**

Let $f \in K[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ be a nonzero polynomial. The non-zero locus of $f
$ is the principal open set
$$D(f) = \{p \in \mathbf{A}^n : f(p) \neq 0\}.$$
Its coordinate ring is the localization
$$\mathcal{O}(D(f)) = K[x_1, \dots, x_n, 1/f] = K[x_1, \dots, x_n]_f.$$

We construct a closed affine subvariety of $\mathbf{A}^{n+1}$ whose coordinate r
ing is isomorphic to this. Introduce a new indeterminate $t$ (the $(n+1)$-st coo
rdinate), and consider the polynomial
$$g = f \cdot t - 1 \in K[x_1, \dots, x_n, t].$$

Define the closed subvariety
$$V = V(g) \subseteq \mathbf{A}^{n+1}.$$

Its coordinate ring is
$$\mathcal{O}(V) = K[x_1, \dots, x_n, t] / (f \cdot t - 1).$$

**Claim.** There is a $K$-algebra isomorphism
$$K[x_1, \dots, x_n, t]/(f \cdot t - 1) \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; K[x_1, \dots,
 x_n, 1/f].$$

*Proof of claim.* Define the $K$-algebra homomorphism
$$\varphi: K[x_1, \dots, x_n, t] \to K[x_1, \dots, x_n, 1/f]$$
by $\varphi(x_i) = x_i$ for $i = 1, \dots, n$ and $\varphi(t) = 1/f$.

- **Surjectivity:** The target $K[x_1, \dots, x_n, 1/f]$ is generated as a $K$-a
lgebra by $x_1, \dots, x_n$ and $1/f$. Each $x_i$ is the image of $x_i$, and $1/
f$ is the image of $t$. Hence $\varphi$ is surjective.

- **The kernel is $(f \cdot t - 1)$:** Clearly $f \cdot t - 1 \in \ker\varphi$ s
ince $\varphi(f \cdot t - 1) = f \cdot (1/f) - 1 = 0$, so $(f \cdot t - 1) \subs
eteq \ker\varphi$.

  For the reverse inclusion, take any $h \in \ker\varphi$. View $h$ as a polynom
ial in $t$ with coefficients in $K[x_1, \dots, x_n]$:
  $$h = \sum_{j=0}^{d} a_j \, t^j, \quad a_j \in K[x_1, \dots, x_n].$$
  In the quotient ring $R = K[x_1, \dots, x_n, t]/(f \cdot t - 1)$, the relation
 $f \cdot t = 1$ holds, so $t$ is invertible with $t^{-1} = f$, i.e., $t^j = f^{
-j}$ in $R$. Substituting $\varphi$:
  $$0 = \varphi(h) = \sum_{j=0}^{d} a_j \, f^{-j} = \frac{\sum_{j=0}^{d} a_j \,
f^{d-j}}{f^d}.$$
  Hence $\sum_{j=0}^{d} a_j \, f^{d-j} = 0$ in $K[x_1, \dots, x_n]$. Now in $R$:
  $$h = \sum_{j=0}^{d} a_j \, t^j = \sum_{j=0}^{d} a_j \, t^j \cdot \frac{f^d \c
dot t^d}{f^d \cdot t^d} = \frac{\sum_{j=0}^{d} a_j \, f^{d-j} \cdot (f \cdot t)^
j}{f^d \cdot t^d}.$$
  Since $f \cdot t = 1$ in $R$, this gives $h = \frac{\sum_{j=0}^{d} a_j \, f^{d
-j}}{f^d \cdot t^d} = 0$ in $R$.

  More directly: in $R$, $t^j = f^{-j}$, so $h = \sum a_j f^{-j}$, and multiplyi
ng by $f^d$: $f^d h = \sum a_j f^{d-j} = 0$. Since $f$ is a unit in $R$ (with in
verse $t$), $f^d$ is a unit, so $h = 0$ in $R$.

  Thus $\ker\varphi \subseteq (f \cdot t - 1)$, and we conclude $\ker\varphi = (
f \cdot t - 1)$.

By the first isomorphism theorem,
$$K[x_1, \dots, x_n, t]/(f \cdot t - 1) \cong K[x_1, \dots, x_n, 1/f].$$

**Geometric interpretation.** The projection $\pi: \mathbf{A}^{n+1} \to \mathbf{
A}^n$ onto the first $n$ coordinates restricts to an isomorphism $V(f \cdot t -
1) \xrightarrow{\sim} D(f)$. The inverse map sends $p \in D(f)$ to $(p, 1/f(p))
\in V$. Thus $V$ is a closed model of the open affine variety $D(f)$ inside $\ma
thbf{A}^{n+1}$.

**Conclusion.** For every nonzero polynomial $f \in K[x_1, \dots, x_n]$, the clo
sed subvariety $V(f \cdot t - 1) \subseteq \mathbf{A}^{n+1}$ has coordinate ring
 isomorphic to $K[x_1, \dots, x_n, 1/f]$, the coordinate ring of $D(f)$. Hence t
he answer is **yes**, it is always possible. $\blacksquare$
