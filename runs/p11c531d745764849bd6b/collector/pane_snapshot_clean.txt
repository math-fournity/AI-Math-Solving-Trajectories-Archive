## Proof

Let $Q(K)$ denote the fundamental quandle (knot quandle) of $K$. Recall that a *
*coloring** of a diagram of $K$ by a quandle $X$ is a quandle homomorphism $Q(K)
 \to X$. A **trivial coloring** is a constant homomorphism (sending every elemen
t to a single element of $X$); such a map is always a quandle homomorphism becau
se every quandle is idempotent ($x \triangleright x = x$).

The hypothesis is: for **every** quandle $X$, every homomorphism $Q(K) \to X$ is
 constant.

---

**Step 1: $Q(K)$ is the one-element (trivial) quandle.**

Take $X = Q(K)$ itself. The identity map $\mathrm{id}: Q(K) \to Q(K)$ is a quand
le homomorphism. By the hypothesis, it must be constant. But a constant identity
 map means $Q(K)$ has exactly one element. Hence $Q(K)$ is the trivial quandle $
\{x\}$ with $x \triangleright x = x$.

---

**Step 2: The knot group $\pi_1(S^3 \setminus K)$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}$.
**

The **associated group** $\mathrm{Gr}(Q)$ of a quandle $Q$ is the group with gen
erators $[a]$ for each $a \in Q$ and relations
$$[a \triangleright b] = [b]^{-1}[a][b] \quad \text{for all } a, b \in Q.$$

A standard result (going back to Joyce and Matveev) is that the associated group
 of the knot quandle recovers the knot group:
$$\mathrm{Gr}(Q(K)) \cong \pi_1(S^3 \setminus K).$$
(This is because the Wirtinger presentation of the knot group is exactly the ass
ociated-group presentation of the knot quandle: arcs are generators, and crossin
g relations are the quandle relations rewritten as conjugation relations.)

Since $Q(K) = \{x\}$ is a single element with $x \triangleright x = x$, the asso
ciated group has one generator $[x]$ and the single relation
$$[x \triangleright x] = [x]^{-1}[x][x], \quad \text{i.e., } [x] = [x],$$
which is vacuous. Therefore
$$\mathrm{Gr}(Q(K)) \cong \langle [x] \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z},$$
and so $\pi_1(S^3 \setminus K) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.

---

**Step 3: $K$ is the unknot.**

By **Dehn's lemma** (Papakyriakopoulos, 1957), a knot in $S^3$ whose complement
has **abelian** fundamental group is the unknot. (Equivalently: a nontrivial kno
t always has a nonabelian knot group.) Since $\mathbb{Z}$ is abelian and $\pi_1(
S^3 \setminus K) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, we conclude that $K$ is the unknot.

*(Alternative route for Step 3:* By the **Joyce–Matveev theorem**, the knot quan
dle is a complete invariant of knots up to mirror image. Since $Q(K)$ is the one
-element quandle, which is exactly $Q(\text{unknot})$ — the free quandle on one
generator collapses to a single element by idempotence — we have $Q(K) \cong Q(\
text{unknot})$, and the unknot is its own mirror image, so $K$ is the unknot. *)
*

---

**Conclusion.** If a knot $K$ admits only trivial colorings by every quandle, th
en $K$ is necessarily the unknot. $\blacksquare$
