# Solution

**Yes.** Given any bounded semigroup $S \subseteq M_d(\mathbb{C})$, one can cons
truct a norm on $\mathbb{C}^d$ whose induced operator norm is $\le 1$ on every e
lement of $S$.

---

## Proof

Let $S \subseteq M_d(\mathbb{C})$ be a bounded semigroup. Fix any norm $\|\cdot\
|$ on $\mathbb{C}^d$ (say the Euclidean norm). Boundedness means there exists $M
>0$ with $\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le M$ for all $A\in S$.

**Step 1: Adjoin the identity.** Set $\widetilde{S} = S \cup \{I_d\}$. This is s
till a semigroup: $S\cdot S \subseteq S$, $I\cdot S = S\cdot I = S$, $I\cdot I =
 I$. It remains bounded since $\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \max(M,1)$ for all $A \in
 \widetilde{S}$.

**Step 2: Define the new norm.** For $x \in \mathbb{C}^d$, set
$$
\|x\|' \;=\; \sup_{A \in \widetilde{S}} \|Ax\|.
$$

This is finite: $\|Ax\| \le \max(M,1)\,\|x\|$, so $\|x\|' \le \max(M,1)\,\|x\| <
 \infty$.

We verify the norm axioms:

- *Positive definiteness:* $\|x\|' \ge 0$ is clear. Since $I_d \in \widetilde{S}
$, we have $\|x\|' \ge \|x\|$, so $\|x\|' = 0 \Rightarrow x = 0$.
- *Homogeneity:* $\|\lambda x\|' = \sup_{A} \|A(\lambda x)\| = |\lambda|\sup_{A}
\|Ax\| = |\lambda|\,\|x\|'$.
- *Triangle inequality:* $\|x+y\|' = \sup_A \|A(x+y)\| \le \sup_A(\|Ax\|+\|Ay\|)
 \le \|x\|' + \|y\|'$.

**Step 3: Every $A \in S$ is a contraction.** For $A \in S$ and $x \in \mathbb{C
}^d$:
$$
\|Ax\|' = \sup_{B \in \widetilde{S}} \|B(Ax)\| = \sup_{B \in \widetilde{S}} \|(B
A)x\|.
$$
Since $\widetilde{S}$ is a semigroup and $A \in \widetilde{S}$, every product $B
A$ lies in $\widetilde{S}$. Therefore
$$
\sup_{B \in \widetilde{S}} \|(BA)x\| \;\le\; \sup_{C \in \widetilde{S}} \|Cx\| \
;=\; \|x\|'.
$$
Thus $\|Ax\|' \le \|x\|'$ for all $A \in S$ and all $x$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.** The operator norm induced by $\|\cdot\|'$ is
$$
\|A\|'_{\mathrm{op}} = \sup_{\|x\|'=1} \|Ax\|' \le 1 \quad \text{for every } A \
in S. \qquad \blacksquare
$$

---

**Remark (equivalence).** The converse is immediate: if such a norm exists, $S$
is bounded (by $1$ in that norm, hence bounded in any norm by finite-dimensional
 norm equivalence). Thus a semigroup of $d\times d$ complex matrices is bounded
*if and only if* there exists a norm on $\mathbb{C}^d$ making every element a co
ntraction. This is the finite-dimensional instance of the Rota–Strang / joint-sp
ectral-radius characterization: a bounded semigroup has joint spectral radius $\
le 1$, and the sup-over-semigroup construction above is the explicit witness.
