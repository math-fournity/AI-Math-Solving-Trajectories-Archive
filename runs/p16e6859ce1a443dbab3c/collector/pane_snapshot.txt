# Center of the Polynomial Ring $R[x,y]$ over a Non-Commutative Ring

**Claim.** Let $R$ be a (possibly non-commutative) ring and let $R[x,y]$ be the
polynomial ring in two **central** indeterminates $x, y$ (i.e. $x$ and $y$ commu
te with each other and with every element of $R$). Then
$$Z(R[x,y]) = Z(R)[x,y],$$
where $Z(\cdot)$ denotes the center of a ring.

---

## Proof

**Setup.** Recall that the center of a ring $S$ is
$$Z(S) = \{s \in S : sa = as \text{ for all } a \in S\}.$$
Elements of $R[x,y]$ are finite sums $\sum_{i,j} a_{ij}\,x^i y^j$ with $a_{ij}\i
n R$, and the monomials $\{x^i y^j\}$ form a basis of $R[x,y]$ both as a left $R
$-module and as a right $R$-module (coefficient comparison is valid: if $\sum a_
{ij}x^i y^j = 0$, then every $a_{ij}=0$). This follows from the iterative constr
uction $R[x,y] = R[x][y]$, where at each step the indeterminate is central and a
lgebraically independent over $R$.

### Inclusion $Z(R)[x,y] \subseteq Z(R[x,y])$

Let $c \in Z(R)$, so $cr = rc$ for all $r \in R$. Since $x$ and $y$ are central
indeterminates, $x$ and $y$ commute with every element of $R[x,y]$. Hence any mo
nomial $c\,x^i y^j$ with $c \in Z(R)$ commutes with every element of $R[x,y]$:

- It commutes with every $r \in R$ because $c \in Z(R)$ and $x,y$ are central.
- It commutes with $x$ and $y$ because they are central.

By linearity, every finite sum $\sum c_{ij} x^i y^j$ with $c_{ij}\in Z(R)$ lies
in $Z(R[x,y])$. Thus
$$Z(R)[x,y] \subseteq Z(R[x,y]).$$

### Inclusion $Z(R[x,y]) \subseteq Z(R)[x,y]$

Let $f = \sum_{i,j} a_{ij}\,x^i y^j \in Z(R[x,y])$. Then $f$ commutes with every
 element of $R[x,y]$, in particular with every constant polynomial $r \in R \sub
seteq R[x,y]$. So for all $r \in R$:
$$r\,f = f\,r,$$
i.e.,
$$\sum_{i,j} r\,a_{ij}\,x^i y^j = \sum_{i,j} a_{ij}\,r\,x^i y^j.$$

By the uniqueness of the coefficient representation (the monomials $x^i y^j$ are
 linearly independent over $R$), we may compare coefficients monomial-by-monomia
l to obtain
$$r\,a_{ij} = a_{ij}\,r \quad \text{for all } r \in R \text{ and all } i,j.$$

This means each coefficient $a_{ij}$ lies in $Z(R)$. Therefore
$$f = \sum_{i,j} a_{ij}\,x^i y^j \in Z(R)[x,y],$$
which gives $Z(R[x,y]) \subseteq Z(R)[x,y]$.

### Conclusion

Both inclusions hold, so
$$\boxed{Z(R[x,y]) = Z(R)[x,y].}$$

That is, the center of the polynomial ring $R[x,y]$ is the polynomial ring over
the center of $R$, in the same indeterminates $x$ and $y$.
