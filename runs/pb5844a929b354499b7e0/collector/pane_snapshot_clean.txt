Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest $r$ such that for any 2023

**Key Observation: Central Symmetry of Subset Sums.**

Let $V = \sum_{i=1}^n v_i$ (with $n = 2023$). For any subset $S$, its complement
 $S^c$ gives subset sum $w_{S^c} = V - w_S$. So the set of all subset sums is sy
mmetric about the point $c = V/2$.

**Lemma: The minimal enclosing disc of a centrally symmetric set is centered at
the center of symmetry.**

*Proof.* Let $A$ be symmetric about $c$. Suppose the minimal enclosing disc is $
D(p, r)$ with $p \neq c$. By symmetry, $D(2c - p, r)$ also encloses $A$. Every p
oint of $A$ lies in both discs. The intersection of two radius-$r$ discs whose c
enters are distance $d = 2\|p - c\|$ apart is contained in $D(c, \sqrt{r^2 - \|p
-c\|^2})$, which has strictly smaller radius. This contradicts minimality of $r$
. $\square$

**Computing the required radius.**

By the lemma, the optimal center is $p = V/2$, and the required radius for a giv
en configuration is:

$$R = \max_S \left\|w_S - \frac{V}{2}\right\| = \frac{1}{2}\max_S \|w_S - w_{S^c
}\| = \frac{1}{2}\max_{\epsilon \in \{-1,+1\}^n} \left\|\sum_{i=1}^n \epsilon_i
v_i\right\|.$$

**Upper bound.** By the triangle inequality:

$$\left\|\sum_{i=1}^n \epsilon_i v_i\right\| \leq \sum_{i=1}^n \|\epsilon_i v_i\
| = \sum_{i=1}^n \|v_i\| = n.$$

So $R \leq \frac{n}{2}$ for every configuration.

**Lower bound (tightness).** Take all $v_i = u$ for a fixed unit vector $u$. The
 subset sums are $\{0, u, 2u, \ldots, nu\}$, lying on the segment $[0, nu]$. The

valently, choosing all $\epsilon_i = +1$ gives $\|\sum \epsilon_i v_i\| = n$, so
 $R = \frac{n}{2}$.

**Conclusion.** The supremum of $R$ over all configurations is exactly $\frac{n}
{2}$, achieved when all vectors are equal. Therefore the smallest $r$ that works
 for all configurations is:

$$r = \frac{2023}{2}.$$
