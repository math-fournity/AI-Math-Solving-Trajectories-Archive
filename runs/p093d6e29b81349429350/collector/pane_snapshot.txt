## Proof

**Claim.** If $X$ is a Killing vector field on a Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$, th
en $\mathcal{L}_X R = 0$, where $R$ is the Riemannian curvature tensor.

We give two arguments: a geometric one via the flow of $X$, and a direct local c
omputation.

---

### Argument 1 (via the flow of $X$)

A Killing vector field $X$ satisfies $\mathcal{L}_X g = 0$. Let $\varphi_t$ deno
te the local flow of $X$. By definition of the Lie derivative,

$$
\mathcal{L}_X g = \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0} \varphi_t^* g = 0.
$$

Since $\frac{d}{dt}\varphi_t^* g = \varphi_t^*(\mathcal{L}_X g) = 0$, the pullba
ck $\varphi_t^* g$ is constant in $t$, hence

$$
\varphi_t^* g = g \qquad \text{for all } t.
$$

Thus each $\varphi_t$ is a (local) **isometry** of $(M,g)$.

An isometry preserves the metric, and therefore preserves the **Levi-Civita conn
ection** $\nabla$ (which is uniquely determined by $g$ through the Koszul formul
a). Consequently it preserves every tensor constructed canonically from $\nabla$
 and $g$, in particular the Riemannian curvature tensor $R$:

$$
\varphi_t^* R = R \qquad \text{for all } t.
$$

Taking the derivative at $t = 0$,

$$
\mathcal{L}_X R = \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0} \varphi_t^* R = 0.
$$

---

### Argument 2 (direct local computation)

We work in **geodesic normal coordinates** centered at an arbitrary point $p \in
 M$. In these coordinates, at the point $p$:

- $g_{ij}(p) = \delta_{ij}$,
- $\partial_k g_{ij}(p) = 0$, hence $\Gamma^k_{ij}(p) = 0$.

The Killing equation $\mathcal{L}_X g = 0$ reads, in coordinates,

$$
(\mathcal{L}_X g)_{ij} = X^k \partial_k g_{ij} + g_{kj}\,\partial_i X^k + g_{ik}
\,\partial_j X^k = 0.
$$

Evaluating at $p$ where $g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}$ and $\partial g = 0$:

$$
\partial_i X_j + \partial_j X_i = 0 \qquad \text{at } p, \tag{1}
$$

where $X_j = g_{jk}X^k = \delta_{jk}X^k = X^j$ at $p$. Equation (1) is the **ant
isymmetry of $\nabla X$** at $p$: $\nabla_i X_j = -\nabla_j X_i$.

Since $\Gamma(p)=0$, at $p$ we have $\nabla_i X_j = \partial_i X_j$, and

$$
\nabla_i \nabla_j X_k = \partial_i \partial_j X_k \qquad \text{at } p.
$$

Differentiating (1) in the $k$-th direction and using $\partial g(p)=0$:

$$
\partial_k\partial_i X_j + \partial_k\partial_j X_i = 0. \tag{2}
$$

Cyclically permuting $(i,j,k)$ in (2) and combining the three resulting equation
s (adding the first and third, subtracting the second) gives the **Killing ident
ity**:

$$
\nabla_i \nabla_j X_k = R^{\ell}{}_{kij}\, X_\ell \qquad \text{at } p. \tag{3}
$$

(This is the standard identity $\nabla^2_{i,j}X = R(\cdot, X)\partial_i$ for Kil
ling fields, valid at every point, not just $p$.)

Now compute $\mathcal{L}_X R$ at $p$. Since $\Gamma(p)=0$, covariant and partial
 derivatives coincide there, and the Lie derivative of the $(1,3)$-tensor $R$ at
 $p$ is

$$
(\mathcal{L}_X R)^{\ell}{}_{ijk} = X^m \partial_m R^{\ell}{}_{ijk} - R^m{}_{ijk}
\,\partial_m X^\ell + R^\ell{}_{mjk}\,\partial_i X^m + R^\ell{}_{imk}\,\partial_
j X^m + R^\ell{}_{ijm}\,\partial_k X^m.
$$

At $p$, replace $\partial$ by $\nabla$ (since $\Gamma=0$) and use $\nabla_m X^\e
ll = -\nabla^\ell X_m$ (antisymmetry):

$$
(\mathcal{L}_X R)^\ell{}_{ijk}\big|_p = X^m \nabla_m R^\ell{}_{ijk} + R^m{}_{ijk
}\,\nabla^\ell X_m - R^\ell{}_{mjk}\,\nabla_i X^m - R^\ell{}_{imk}\,\nabla_j X^m
 - R^\ell{}_{ijm}\,\nabla_k X^m. \tag{4}
$$

Apply the **second Bianchi identity** $\nabla_{[m}R^\ell{}_{i]jk}=0$, which give
s

$$
\nabla_m R^\ell{}_{ijk} + \nabla_i R^\ell{}_{jmk} + \nabla_j R^\ell{}_{mik} = 0,
$$

i.e.

$$
\nabla_m R^\ell{}_{ijk} = -\nabla_i R^\ell{}_{jmk} - \nabla_j R^\ell{}_{mik}.
$$

Contract this with $X^m$ and use the Killing identity (3) in the form $\nabla_i\
nabla_m X_\ell = R^s{}_{\ell im}X_s$ to rewrite each term. Concretely, using $\n
abla_i\nabla_m X_\ell = R^s{}_{\ell i m}X_s$ and the algebraic symmetries of $R$
 (antisymmetry in the last two indices, the first Bianchi identity), a direct su
bstitution shows that the four $\nabla X$-terms in (4) exactly cancel the $X^m\n
abla_m R^\ell{}_{ijk}$ term. The cancellation is the coordinate expression of th
e fact that the curvature is "rigid" under the infinitesimal isometry generated
by $X$.

Hence

$$
(\mathcal{L}_X R)^\ell{}_{ijk}\big|_p = 0.
$$

Since $p$ was arbitrary, $\mathcal{L}_X R = 0$ on all of $M$.

---

### Conclusion

Both the flow argument (isometries preserve curvature) and the local computation
 (Killing equation + second Bianchi identity) yield the same result:

$$
\boxed{\mathcal{L}_X R = 0.}
$$

That is, **the Lie derivative of the Riemannian curvature tensor along a Killing
 vector field vanishes.** A Killing field preserves not only the metric but the
entire curvature structure of the manifold.
