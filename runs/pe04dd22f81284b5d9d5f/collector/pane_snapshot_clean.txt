## Solution

**The function.** We have $f = \chi_{(0,1/2)} - \chi_{(-1/2,0)}$, extended 1-per
iodically. On $[-1/2, 1/2]$, $f$ is odd: $f(x)=+1$ on $(0,1/2)$, $f(x)=-1$ on $(
-1/2,0)$, and $f=0$ at the jump points.

**Fourier coefficients.** With period 1, the Fourier coefficients are
$$\hat{f}(k) = \int_{-1/2}^{1/2} f(x)\, e^{-2\pi i k x}\, dx.$$

Since $f$ is odd, $\hat{f}(0)=0$. For $k\neq 0$, the even part ($\cos$) of the e
xponential times the odd $f$ integrates to zero, leaving
$$\hat{f}(k) = -i\int_{-1/2}^{1/2} f(x)\sin(2\pi k x)\,dx = -2i\int_0^{1/2}\sin(
2\pi k x)\,dx = \frac{i}{\pi k}\bigl[(-1)^k - 1\bigr].$$

Thus $\hat{f}(k)=0$ for even $k$, and $\hat{f}(k) = \frac{-2i}{\pi k}$ for odd $
k$.

**Partial sum.** The symmetric partial sum $S_n f(x)=\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\hat{f}(k)e^
{2\pi i kx}$. Pairing $\pm k$ for odd $k>0$:
$$\frac{-2i}{\pi k}e^{2\pi i kx} + \frac{2i}{\pi k}e^{-2\pi i kx} = \frac{-2i}{\
pi k}\cdot 2i\sin(2\pi kx) = \frac{4}{\pi k}\sin(2\pi kx).$$

Therefore
$$\boxed{S_n f(x) = \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{\substack{k=1\\k\text{ odd}}}^{n}\frac{\s
in(2\pi k x)}{k}.}$$

**Evaluation at $x_n = \frac{1}{4n}$.** Substituting:
$$S_n f(x_n) = \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{\substack{k=1\\k\text{ odd}}}^{n}\frac{1}{k}\s
in\!\left(\frac{\pi k}{2n}\right).$$

Writing $k=2j-1$ for $j=1,\dots,m$ with $m=\lfloor(n+1)/2\rfloor$, and setting $
t_j = \frac{2j-1}{2n}=\frac{j-1/2}{n}$ (midpoints of intervals of width $\Delta
t = 1/n$), we get $k = 2n\,t_j$ and:
$$S_n f(x_n) = \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\sin(\pi t_j)}{2n\,t_j} = \frac{
2}{\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\sin(\pi t_j)}{t_j}\,\Delta t.$$

As $n\to\infty$, $t_j$ ranges over $(0,\,1/2)$ (since the largest odd $k\leq n$
gives $t\to 1/2$), and this is a Riemann sum:
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} S_n f(x_n) = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^{1/2}\frac{\sin(\pi t)}{t}\
,dt.$$

Substituting $v = \pi t$ ($dv = \pi\,dt$, $t = v/\pi$):
$$\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^{1/2}\frac{\sin(\pi t)}{t}\,dt = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi/
2}\frac{\sin v}{v}\,dv.$$

**Result.**
$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} S_n f\!\left(\tfrac{1}{4n}\right) = \frac{2}{\pi}\int
_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\sin t}{t}\,dt = \frac{2}{\pi}\,\operatorname{Si}\!\left(\frac{\
pi}{2}\right) \approx 0.872.}$$

This is a manifestation of the Gibbs phenomenon: $x_n=1/(4n)$ lies at half the d
istance to the first overshoot peak (which occurs at $x\approx 1/(2n)$ and yield
s $\frac{2}{\pi}\operatorname{Si}(\pi)\approx 1.179$), so the value sits between
 the midpoint value $0$ at the jump and the classical overshoot.
