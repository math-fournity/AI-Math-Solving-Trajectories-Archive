## Proof

**Yes**, there exists a countable separating family for the space of probability
 measures on a Polish space $(E, \mathcal{A})$.

---

### Setup

Since $E$ is Polish, it is separable and metrizable. Let $d$ be a compatible com
plete metric and let $\{x_n : n \in \mathbb{N}\}$ be a countable dense subset of
 $E$. The Borel $\sigma$-algebra is $\mathcal{A} = \mathcal{B}(E)$.

### Constructing the countable family

Consider the countable collection of open balls

$$\mathcal{B} = \{B(x_n, q) : n \in \mathbb{N},\; q \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}\}.$$

**Claim 1.** $\mathcal{B}$ is a countable base for the topology of $E$.

*Proof.* $\mathcal{B}$ is countable (product of two countable sets). To verify i
t is a base: let $U \subseteq E$ be open and $x \in U$. Pick $r > 0$ with $B(x,r
) \subseteq U$. Choose $q \in \mathbb{Q}$ with $0 < q < r/2$. By density, pick $
n$ with $d(x, x_n) < q$. Then $x \in B(x_n, q)$, and for every $y \in B(x_n, q)$
:

$$d(x, y) \le d(x, x_n) + d(x_n, y) < q + q = 2q < r,$$

so $B(x_n, q) \subseteq B(x, r) \subseteq U$. Hence $\mathcal{B}$ is a base. $\s
quare$

Now define the family of all finite intersections:

$$\mathcal{P} = \left\{\bigcap_{i=1}^{k} B(x_{n_i}, q_i) : k \ge 1,\; n_i \in \m
athbb{N},\; q_i \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}\right\}.$$

**Claim 2.** $\mathcal{P}$ is countable.

*Proof.* For each fixed $k$, the $k$-fold intersections range over a countable p
roduct of countable sets, hence are countable. Taking the union over $k \ge 1$ (
countably many) preserves countability. $\square$

**Claim 3.** $\mathcal{P}$ is a $\pi$-system (closed under finite intersections)
.

*Proof.* If $A = \bigcap_{i=1}^{k} B(x_{n_i}, q_i)$ and $A' = \bigcap_{j=1}^{m}
B(x_{m_j}, r_j)$ are in $\mathcal{P}$, then

$$A \cap A' = \bigcap_{i=1}^{k} B(x_{n_i}, q_i) \;\cap\; \bigcap_{j=1}^{m} B(x_{
m_j}, r_j),$$

which is again a finite intersection of elements of $\mathcal{B}$, hence belongs
 to $\mathcal{P}$. $\square$

**Claim 4.** $\sigma(\mathcal{P}) = \mathcal{A}$.

*Proof.* Since $\mathcal{B} \subseteq \mathcal{P}$, we have $\sigma(\mathcal{B})
 \subseteq \sigma(\mathcal{P})$. By Claim 1, $\mathcal{B}$ is a base, so every o
pen set is a union of elements of $\mathcal{B}$; thus $\sigma(\mathcal{B}) = \ma
thcal{B}(E) = \mathcal{A}$. Conversely, every element of $\mathcal{P}$ is a fini
te intersection of open sets, hence Borel, so $\sigma(\mathcal{P}) \subseteq \ma
thcal{A}$. Therefore $\sigma(\mathcal{P}) = \mathcal{A}$. $\square$

### Separating property

**Claim 5.** $\mathcal{P}$ is separating: if two probability measures $\mu, \nu$
 on $(E, \mathcal{A})$ satisfy $\mu(A) = \nu(A)$ for all $A \in \mathcal{P}$, th
en $\mu = \nu$.

*Proof.* Define

$$\mathcal{L} = \{A \in \mathcal{A} : \mu(A) = \nu(A)\}.$$

We verify $\mathcal{L}$ is a $\lambda$-system (Dynkin system):

1. **$E \in \mathcal{L}$:** $\mu(E) = 1 = \nu(E)$.
2. **Closed under complements:** If $A \in \mathcal{L}$, then $\mu(A^c) = 1 - \m
u(A) = 1 - \nu(A) = \nu(A^c)$, so $A^c \in \mathcal{L}$.
3. **Closed under countable disjoint unions:** If $\{A_k\} \subseteq \mathcal{L}
$ are pairwise disjoint, then by countable additivity,
$$\mu\!\left(\bigcup_k A_k\right) = \sum_k \mu(A_k) = \sum_k \nu(A_k) = \nu\!\le
ft(\bigcup_k A_k\right),$$
so $\bigcup_k A_k \in \mathcal{L}$.

By assumption $\mathcal{P} \subseteq \mathcal{L}$, and by Claim 3, $\mathcal{P}$
 is a $\pi$-system. **Dynkin's $\pi$-$\lambda$ theorem** gives

$$\sigma(\mathcal{P}) \subseteq \mathcal{L}.$$

By Claim 4, $\sigma(\mathcal{P}) = \mathcal{A}$, so $\mathcal{A} \subseteq \math
cal{L}$, i.e., $\mu(A) = \nu(A)$ for every $A \in \mathcal{A}$. Hence $\mu = \nu
$. $\square$

### Conclusion

The family $\mathcal{P}$ — the collection of all finite intersections of rationa
l-radius balls centered at a countable dense set — is a **countable** $\pi$-syst
em generating $\mathcal{A}$, and by the $\pi$-$\lambda$ theorem it **separates**
 probability measures on $(E, \mathcal{A})$.

Therefore, **yes**, a countable separating family for the probability measures o
n a Polish space $(E, \mathcal{A})$ exists. $\blacksquare$
