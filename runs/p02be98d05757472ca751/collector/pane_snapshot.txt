## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** Every point of $(M, \omega)$ admits a coordinate chart in which
 $\omega$ becomes the standard volume form.

### Construction

Let $a \in M$ and choose any coordinate chart $(V, \varphi = (x_1, \dots, x_n))$
 centered near $a$, with $V \subset M$ open. Since $\omega$ is a volume form, on
 $V$ it has the form
$$
\omega = f\, dx_1 \wedge dx_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx_n
$$
for a unique smooth function $f \in C^\infty(V)$ with $f$ **nowhere vanishing**
(nowhere zero because $\omega$ is nonvanishing as an $n$-form).

Define a new first coordinate by integrating $f$ along the $x_1$-direction:
$$
y_1(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n) \;=\; \int_{x_1^{0}}^{x_1} f\!\left(t,\, x_2,\, \dots,
\, x_n\right)\, dt,
$$
where $x_1^0$ is the $x_1$-coordinate of $a$. Set
$$
y_i = x_i, \qquad i = 2, \dots, n.
$$
Let $g : V \to \mathbb{R}^n$ be the map $g = (y_1, y_2, \dots, y_n)$.

### $g$ is a local diffeomorphism

The Jacobian matrix of $g$ with respect to $(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ is
$$
Dg = \begin{pmatrix}
\frac{\partial y_1}{\partial x_1} & \frac{\partial y_1}{\partial x_2} & \cdots &
 \frac{\partial y_1}{\partial x_n} \\
0 & 1 & & 0 \\
\vdots & & \ddots & \\
0 & 0 & & 1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det(Dg) = \frac{\partial y_1}{\partial x_1} = f(x_1, \dots, x_n) \neq 0
$$
everywhere on $V$, since $f$ is nowhere vanishing. By the inverse function theor
em, $g$ is a local diffeomorphism at every point of $V$. Shrinking $V$ to a smal
ler open neighborhood $U \ni a$ if necessary, $g|_U : U \to g(U) \subset \mathbb
{R}^n$ is a diffeomorphism onto the open set $g(U)$.

### Pullback computation

Let $\omega_0 = du_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge du_n$ be the standard volume form on $
\mathbb{R}^n$. Then
$$
g^*\omega_0 = dy_1 \wedge dy_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge dy_n.
$$
Since $y_i = x_i$ for $i \geq 2$, we have $dy_i = dx_i$ for $i \geq 2$. For $y_1
$,
$$
dy_1 = \frac{\partial y_1}{\partial x_1}\, dx_1 + \sum_{i=2}^{n} \frac{\partial
y_1}{\partial x_i}\, dx_i = f\, dx_1 + \sum_{i=2}^{n} \frac{\partial y_1}{\parti
al x_i}\, dx_i.
$$
Wedge with $dx_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx_n$:
$$
dy_1 \wedge dx_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx_n
= f\, dx_1 \wedge dx_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx_n
+ \sum_{i=2}^{n} \frac{\partial y_1}{\partial x_i}\, \underbrace{dx_i \wedge dx_
2 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx_n}_{= 0 \text{ (repeated } dx_i)}.
$$
Every term in the sum vanishes because $dx_i$ appears twice. Therefore
$$
g^*\omega_0 = dy_1 \wedge dy_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge dy_n = f\, dx_1 \wedge \cdot
s \wedge dx_n = \omega.
$$

### Conclusion

For every $a \in M$, taking $U$ to be a sufficiently small open neighborhood of
$a$ on which the above map $g$ is a diffeomorphism onto the open set $g(U) \subs
et \mathbb{R}^n$, we have
$$
\omega|_U = g^*\omega_0.
$$
Hence the answer is **yes**: every point admits local coordinates in which the g
iven volume form coincides with the standard volume form on $\mathbb{R}^n$.
