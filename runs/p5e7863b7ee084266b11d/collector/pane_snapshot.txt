## Proof

We work on $C[0,1]$, the space of continuous real-valued functions on $[0,1]$. F
or $1 \le r < \infty$ the $r$-norm is
$$\|f\|_r = \left(\int_0^1 |f(x)|^r\,dx\right)^{1/r},$$
and $\|f\|_\infty = \sup_{x\in[0,1]} |f(x)|$.

Two norms $\|\cdot\|_p, \|\cdot\|_q$ are equivalent iff there exist $c, C > 0$ w
ith
$$c\,\|f\|_p \le \|f\|_q \le C\,\|f\|_p \quad \text{for all } f \in C[0,1].$$

**Claim.** The $p$-norm and $q$-norm on $C[0,1]$ are equivalent **if and only if
 $p = q$** (where $p,q \in [1,\infty]$).

---

### The "if" direction

If $p = q$ the two norms are identical, hence trivially equivalent.

---

### The "only if" direction

Assume $p \ne q$. We treat two cases.

**Case 1: $q = \infty$ and $p < \infty$ (or symmetrically $p = \infty$, $q < \in
fty$).**

For each $n \ge 1$ define the tent function
$$f_n(x) = \max\bigl(0,\; 1 - n|x - \tfrac12|\bigr).$$
This is continuous, $\|f_n\|_\infty = 1$, and
$$\|f_n\|_p^p = 2\int_0^{1/n}(1-nt)^p\,dt = \frac{2}{n(p+1)},$$
so $\|f_n\|_p = \left(\frac{2}{n(p+1)}\right)^{1/p} \to 0$ as $n\to\infty$.

If the norms were equivalent we would have $\|f_n\|_\infty \le C\|f_n\|_p$, i.e.
 $1 \le C\left(\frac{2}{n(p+1)}\right)^{1/p}$, which fails for large $n$. Contra
diction. So no finite $p$ is equivalent to $\infty$.

**Case 2: $1 \le p < q < \infty$.**

Use the same tent functions $f_n$. We compute
$$\|f_n\|_q = \left(\frac{2}{n(q+1)}\right)^{1/q}, \qquad \|f_n\|_p = \left(\fra
c{2}{n(p+1)}\right)^{1/p}.$$

Their ratio is
$$\frac{\|f_n\|_q}{\|f_n\|_p}
= \left(\frac{2}{q+1}\right)^{1/q}\!\left(\frac{p+1}{2}\right)^{1/p}\! \cdot n^{
\,1/p - 1/q}.$$

Since $p < q$, the exponent $\frac1p - \frac1q > 0$, so $n^{1/p-1/q} \to \infty$
 as $n\to\infty$. Hence
$$\frac{\|f_n\|_q}{\|f_n\|_p} \to \infty.$$

If the norms were equivalent we would have $\|f_n\|_q \le C\|f_n\|_p$ for some f
ixed $C$ and all $n$, contradicting the ratio tending to infinity.

The case $q < p$ is identical with the roles of $p$ and $q$ swapped (the ratio $
\|f_n\|_p/\|f_n\|_q \to \infty$).

---

### Conclusion

In every case with $p \ne q$ we exhibited a sequence $(f_n) \subset C[0,1]$ with
 $\|f_n\|_q/\|f_n\|_p \to \infty$ (or $\to 0$), ruling out equivalence. Therefor
e the $p$-norm and $q$-norm on $C[0,1]$ are equivalent **if and only if $p = q$*
*.
