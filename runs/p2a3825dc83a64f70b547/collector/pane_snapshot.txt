## Proof

**Setup (Stone duality).** Recall that Stone duality gives a contravariant equiv
alence between Boolean algebras and Boolean spaces (compact, Hausdorff, zero-dim
ensional topological spaces). Concretely, to a Boolean algebra $B$ one associate
s its Stone space $S(B)$, whose

- **points** are the ultrafilters of $B$,
- **clopen sets** are the sets $\widehat{a}=\{\mathcal{U}\in S(B):a\in\mathcal{U
}\}$ for $a\in B$,
- and the clopen sets form a base for the topology.

The correspondence $a\mapsto\widehat{a}$ is a Boolean isomorphism $B\cong\operat
orname{Clop}(S(B))$, and inclusion of clopen sets matches the order of $B$:
$$\widehat{a}\subseteq\widehat{b}\iff a\le b.$$

**Translating the local-base condition.** Let $x\in S(B)$ be a point, correspond
ing to an ultrafilter $\mathcal{U}\subseteq B$. Since the clopen sets form a bas
e, a local base at $x$ may be taken to consist of clopen neighborhoods. A clopen
 set $\widehat{a}$ contains $x$ iff $a\in\mathcal{U}$.

Thus a **countable local base at $x$** is a countable family $\{a_n\}_{n\in\math
bb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}$ such that every clopen neighborhood $\widehat{c}$ of
 $x$ (i.e. every $c\in\mathcal{U}$) contains some $\widehat{a_n}$:
$$\forall c\in\mathcal{U},\ \exists n:\ \widehat{a_n}\subseteq\widehat{c}.$$
By the order-isomorphism above, $\widehat{a_n}\subseteq\widehat{c}\iff a_n\le c$
. Hence the local-base condition becomes:

$$\text{There is a countable }\{a_n\}\subseteq\mathcal{U}\text{ such that }\fora
ll c\in\mathcal{U},\ \exists n:\ a_n\le c.\tag{$\ast$}$$

This is precisely the statement that $\mathcal{U}$ (viewed as a directed set und
er $\le$) has a **countable cofinal subset**.

**Equivalence with countable generation.** Given such a cofinal $\{a_n\}$, set $
b_n=a_1\wedge\cdots\wedge a_n\in\mathcal{U}$. Then $(b_n)$ is decreasing ($b_{n+
1}\le b_n$) and still cofinal: for any $c\in\mathcal{U}$, some $a_n\le c$, hence
 $b_n\le a_n\le c$. Therefore
$$\mathcal{U}=\{c\in B:\exists n,\ b_n\le c\},$$
i.e. $\mathcal{U}$ is the filter generated by the countable set $\{b_n\}$. Conve
rsely, if $\mathcal{U}$ is generated by a countable set $\{s_n\}$, then $b_n=s_1
\wedge\cdots\wedge s_n$ gives a decreasing cofinal sequence in $\mathcal{U}$, yi
elding $(\ast)$.

So $(\ast)$ is equivalent to: **$\mathcal{U}$ is countably generated** (equivale
ntly, generated by a countable decreasing sequence).

**Conclusion.** The first countability axiom says "every point has a countable l
ocal base." Under Stone duality, points are ultrafilters and local bases corresp
ond to cofinal subsets. Therefore the algebraic dual of the first countability a
xiom for Boolean spaces is:

> **Every ultrafilter of the Boolean algebra is countably generated** (equivalen
tly, every ultrafilter contains a countable decreasing cofinal sequence, i.e. ha
s a countable cofinal subset).

A Boolean algebra satisfying this condition is sometimes called a **$\sigma$-com
plete in the ultrafilter sense** — more precisely, the condition is that every u
ltrafilter of $B$ is countably generated. This is the exact algebraic dual of fi
rst countability of the Stone space $S(B)$.
