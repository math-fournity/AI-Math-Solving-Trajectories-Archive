## Solution

**The statement is FALSE.**

### Counterexample

Let $X \sim N(0,1)$ be a standard normal random variable, and define
$$X_n = -X \quad \text{for all } n \geq 1.$$

**Verification of the hypotheses:**

1. **$X_n \to X$ in distribution.** Since the standard normal distribution is sy
mmetric about $0$, the random variable $-X$ has the same distribution as $X$. Th
us each $X_n$ has the same law as $X$, and trivially $X_n \xrightarrow{d} X$.

2. **$\{X_n^2\}$ is uniformly integrable.** We have $X_n^2 = X^2$ for every $n$,
 so the family $\{X_n^2\}$ is the singleton $\{X^2\}$. Since $X^2$ is integrable
 ($E[X^2] = 1 < \infty$), any family consisting of a single integrable random va
riable is uniformly integrable. Concretely,
$$\sup_n E\!\left[X_n^2 \mathbf{1}_{\{|X_n| > M\}}\right] = E\!\left[X^2 \mathbf
{1}_{\{|X| > M\}}\right] \xrightarrow{M \to \infty} 0$$
by the dominated convergence theorem (applied to the single integrable random va
riable $X^2$).

**Failure of $L^2$ convergence:**

Compute
$$E\!\left[(X_n - X)^2\right] = E\!\left[(-X - X)^2\right] = E\!\left[4X^2\right
] = 4E[X^2] = 4 \neq 0 \quad \text{for all } n.$$

Hence $X_n$ does **not** converge to $X$ in $L^2$.

### Why the statement fails (intuition)

Convergence in distribution is a property of the **marginal** laws of $X_n$ and
$X$; it carries no information about the **joint** distribution of $(X_n, X)$. T
he uniform integrability of $\{X_n^2\}$ controls the tails of each $X_n$ individ
ually, ensuring that moments converge ($E[X_n^2] \to E[X^2]$), but it also says
nothing about the coupling between $X_n$ and $X$.

In contrast, $L^2$ convergence $E[(X_n - X)^2] \to 0$ depends crucially on the j
oint law of $(X_n, X)$. The counterexample exploits this gap: $X_n$ and $X$ have
 identical marginal distributions (so convergence in distribution holds), yet th
ey are perfectly *anti-correlated*, making $X_n - X = -2X$ a non-degenerate rand
om variable whose $L^2$ norm is bounded away from $0$.

**Remark.** The correct strengthening is: if $X_n \to X$ in **probability** (rat
her than merely in distribution) and $\{X_n^2\}$ is uniformly integrable, then $
X_n \to X$ in $L^2$. Convergence in probability provides the necessary control o
ver the joint distribution, and combined with UI (which controls the tails) yiel
ds $L^2$ convergence via the Vitali convergence theorem.
