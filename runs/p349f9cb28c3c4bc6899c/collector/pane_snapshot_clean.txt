The statement is **false**. Conjugation by a non-isometric isomorphism preserves
 the *algebraic* structure (invertibility, spectrum) but does **not** preserve t
he operator norm in general.

**Counterexample.**

Let $X = Y = \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$ with its standard Hilbert norm. Define the boun
ded invertible operator
$$I = \operatorname{diag}(2, 1, 1, 1, \dots), \qquad I(e_1)=2e_1,\; I(e_k)=e_k\;
(k\ge 2).$$
Then $I$ is an isomorphism of $\ell^2$ onto itself with $\|I\|=2$ and $\|I^{-1}\
|=1/2$, so $I$ is **not** an isometry.

Define the rank-one operator $T\in B(Y)$ by
$$T(e_1)=e_2, \qquad T(e_k)=0\;\;(k\ge 2).$$
$T$ is a partial isometry, so $\|T\|_{B(Y)}=1$.

Now compute the conjugate $ITI^{-1}\in B(X)$. Since $I^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}(
1/2,1,1,\dots)$:
- $ITI^{-1}(e_1) = I\bigl(T(\tfrac12 e_1)\bigr) = I(\tfrac12 e_2) = \tfrac12 e_2
$,
- $ITI^{-1}(e_k) = I(T(e_k)) = 0$ for $k\ge 2$.

Thus $ITI^{-1} = \tfrac12\, T$, and consequently
$$\|ITI^{-1}\|_{B(X)} = \tfrac12\|T\| = \tfrac12 \;\neq\; 1 = \|T\|_{B(Y)}.$$

**Justification.** The operator norm is *not* an algebraic invariant; it depends
 on the specific norm of the ambient space. Conjugation $T\mapsto ITI^{-1}$ only
 preserves properties that are invariant under Banach-algebra isomorphism (spect
rum, invertibility, spectral radius), but the norm itself is preserved precisely
 when $I$ is an isometry. In general one only has the bound
$$\|I^{-1}\|^{-1}\,\|T\| \;\le\; \|ITI^{-1}\| \;\le\; \|I\|\,\|T\|,$$
and both inequalities can be strict, as the example above shows. Hence the claim
ed equality fails for non-isometric isomorphisms.
