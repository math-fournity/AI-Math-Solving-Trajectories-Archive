## Solution

We evaluate
$$L = \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\left(\int_0^x e^{t^2}\,dt\right)^{2}}{\int_0^x
e^{2t^2}\,dt}.$$

**Step 1: Set up notation and confirm the indeterminate form.**

Let $F(x) = \int_0^x e^{t^2}\,dt$ and $G(x) = \int_0^x e^{2t^2}\,dt$. Since the
integrands are positive and growing, both $F(x) \to \infty$ and $G(x) \to \infty
$ as $x \to \infty$. Hence $F(x)^2 \to \infty$, and the quotient is of the form
$\infty/\infty$, so L'Hôpital's rule applies.

**Step 2: Apply L'Hôpital's rule once.**

Differentiating the numerator and denominator with respect to $x$:
$$\frac{d}{dx}\bigl[F(x)^2\bigr] = 2\,F(x)\,F'(x) = 2\,F(x)\,e^{x^2},$$
$$G'(x) = e^{2x^2}.$$

Thus
$$L = \lim_{x\to\infty} \frac{2\,F(x)\,e^{x^2}}{e^{2x^2}} = \lim_{x\to\infty} \f
rac{2\,F(x)}{e^{x^2}}.$$

The new quotient $\frac{2F(x)}{e^{x^2}}$ is again $\infty/\infty$. Differentiati

Therefore
$$L = \lim_{x\to\infty} \frac{2\,e^{x^2}}{2x\,e^{x^2}} = \lim_{x\to\infty} \frac
{1}{x} = 0.$$

**Step 4: Verification via asymptotic expansion (sanity check).**

By integration by parts, for large $x$:
$$\int_0^x e^{t^2}\,dt \sim \frac{e^{x^2}}{2x}, \qquad \int_0^x e^{2t^2}\,dt \si

$$\frac{F(x)^2}{G(x)} \sim \frac{e^{2x^2}/(4x^2)}{e^{2x^2}/(4x)} = \frac{1}{x} \

confirming the result.

$$\boxed{\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\left(\int_0^x e^{t^2}\,dt\right)^{2}}{\int_0
^x e^{2t^2}\,dt} = 0}$$
