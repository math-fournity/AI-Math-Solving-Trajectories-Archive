## Proof

Let $h(t) = \dfrac{|t|}{\sqrt{1+t^2}}$. We first show $h$ is Lipschitz on $\math
bb{R}$ with constant $1$.

For $t \neq 0$, $h$ is differentiable. By symmetry ($h$ is even) it suffices to
consider $t>0$, where
$$
h(t) = \frac{t}{\sqrt{1+t^2}}, \qquad h'(t) = \frac{(1+t^2)^{1/2} - t\cdot t(1+t
^2)^{-1/2}}{1+t^2} = \frac{1}{(1+t^2)^{3/2}}.
$$
Hence $0 < h'(t) \le 1$ for all $t>0$, and by evenness $|h'(t)| \le 1$ for all $
t\neq 0$. At $t=0$ the one-sided derivatives are $\pm 1$, so $h$ is Lipschitz wi
th constant $1$:
$$
|h(s)-h(t)| \le |s-t| \quad \forall\, s,t\in\mathbb{R}.
$$

Now write
$$
f(y) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k^2}\, h(y-q_k).
$$

**Pointwise convergence.** Since $0 \le h(t) < 1$ for all $t$, each term satisfi
es $0 \le \frac{1}{k^2}h(y-q_k) \le \frac{1}{k^2}$. Because $\sum_{k=1}^\infty \
frac{1}{k^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}<\infty$, the Weierstrass $M$-test gives uniform (
hence pointwise) convergence of $f$ on $\mathbb{R}$.

**Lipschitz estimate.** For any $y_1, y_2 \in \mathbb{R}$ and any $N\ge 1$, the
partial sum $f_N(y)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{k^2}h(y-q_k)$ satisfies
$$
|f_N(y_1)-f_N(y_2)| \le \sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{k^2}\,|h(y_1-q_k)-h(y_2-q_k)| \le
 \sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{k^2}\,|y_1-y_2| \le \frac{\pi^2}{6}|y_1-y_2|.
$$
Passing to the limit $N\to\infty$ (using pointwise convergence of $f_N$ to $f$)
preserves the inequality, so
$$
|f(y_1)-f(y_2)| \le \frac{\pi^2}{6}\,|y_1-y_2| \qquad \forall\, y_1,y_2\in\mathb
b{R}.
$$

**Conclusion.** The function $f$ is Lipschitz with respect to $y$, with Lipschit
z constant at most $\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{1}{k^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}$. This hol
ds for *every* choice of the rational sequence $(q_k)$, since the argument uses
only the uniform bound on the Lipschitz constants of the summands and the summab
ility of $\frac{1}{k^2}$.
