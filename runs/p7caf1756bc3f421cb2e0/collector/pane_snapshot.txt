## Solution

We solve the boundary-value problem for the wave equation on the half-line with
a non-homogeneous Neumann condition and zero initial data.

**Problem.**
$$u_{tt} = a^2 u_{xx},\quad 0<x,\,t<+\infty,$$
$$u_x(0,t)=\nu(t),\quad 0<t<+\infty,$$
$$u(x,0)=u_t(x,0)=0,\quad 0<x<+\infty.$$

---

### Step 1. General solution via d'Alembert

The general solution of $u_{tt}=a^2 u_{xx}$ is
$$u(x,t)=F(x-at)+G(x+at),$$
where $F$ and $G$ are determined by the initial and boundary data.

### Step 2. Apply the initial conditions ($x>0$)

From $u(x,0)=F(x)+G(x)=0$ for $x>0$:
$$G(x)=-F(x),\qquad x>0.$$

From $u_t(x,0)=-aF'(x)+aG'(x)=0$ for $x>0$:
$$G'(x)=F'(x),\qquad x>0.$$

Substituting $G=-F$ gives $-F'(x)=F'(x)$, hence $F'(x)=0$ for $x>0$. Therefore
$$F(x)=C,\quad G(x)=-C,\qquad x>0,$$
for some constant $C$.

**Consequence.** Whenever $x-at>0$ (i.e. $t<x/a$), both arguments $x-at$ and $x+
at$ are positive, so
$$u(x,t)=F(x-at)+G(x+at)=C+(-C)=0,\qquad t\le x/a.$$

### Step 3. Determine $F$ on negative arguments from the boundary condition

The boundary condition is $u_x(0,t)=\nu(t)$. Computing:
$$u_x(x,t)=F'(x-at)+G'(x+at).$$

At $x=0$: $u_x(0,t)=F'(-at)+G'(at)$. Since $at>0$, we have $G'(at)=-F'(at)=0$. T

$$F'(-at)=\nu(t),\qquad t>0.$$

Setting $s=-at<0$ (so $t=-s/a$):
$$F'(s)=\nu\!\left(-\frac{s}{a}\right),\qquad s<0.$$

Integrating from $0$ (using $F(0)=C$ for continuity):
$$F(s)=C+\int_0^s \nu\!\left(-\frac{\tau}{a}\right)d\tau,\qquad s<0.$$

Substituting $\sigma=-\tau/a$ ($d\tau=-a\,d\sigma$; limits $\tau:0\to s$ become
$\sigma:0\to -s/a$):
$$F(s)=C-a\int_0^{-s/a}\nu(\sigma)\,d\sigma,\qquad s<0.$$

### Step 4. Assemble the solution for $t>x/a$

When $t>x/a$, we have $x-at<0$ and $x+at>0$, so:
$$u(x,t)=F(x-at)+G(x+at)=\left[C-a\int_0^{(at-x)/a}\nu(\sigma)\,d\sigma\right]+(
-C),$$
$$u(x,t)=-a\int_0^{\,t-x/a}\nu(\sigma)\,d\sigma,\qquad t>x/a.$$

### Step 5. Final answer

$$\boxed{u(x,t)=\begin{cases} 0, & t\le \dfrac{x}{a},\\[6pt] -\,a\displaystyle\i
nt_0^{\,t-x/a}\nu(\sigma)\,d\sigma, & t>\dfrac{x}{a}. \end{cases}}$$

tegral is interpreted as $0$ when $t-x/a\le 0$.

We solve the boundary-value problem for the wave equation on the half-line with
a Neumann boundary condition and zero initial data.

**General solution.** The general solution of $u_{tt}=a^2 u_{xx}$ is
$$u(x,t)=F(x-at)+G(x+at)$$
for arbitrary (sufficiently smooth) functions $F$ and $G$.

**Applying the initial conditions** (for $x>0$):

From $u(x,0)=F(x)+G(x)=0$ we get $G(x)=-F(x)$ for $x>0$.

From $u_t(x,0)=-aF'(x)+aG'(x)=0$ we get $G'(x)=F'(x)$ for $x>0$. Combining with
$G=-F$ gives $F'(x)=0$ for $x>0$, so $F$ is constant on $(0,\infty)$. We normali
ze this constant to $0$ (so that $u\to 0$ as $x\to\infty$), giving
$$F(x)=0,\qquad G(x)=0,\qquad x>0.$$

**Applying the boundary condition** $u_x(0,t)=\nu(t)$:

$$u_x(0,t)=F'(-at)+G'(at)=\nu(t).$$

Since $at>0$, we have $G'(at)=0$, so
$$F'(-at)=\nu(t),\qquad t>0.$$

Setting $s=-at<0$ (so $t=-s/a$):
$$F'(s)=\nu\!\left(-\frac{s}{a}\right),\qquad s<0.$$

Integrating from $0$ to $s<0$ and using $F(0)=0$ (continuity with $F\equiv 0$ on
 $x>0$):
$$F(s)=\int_0^s \nu\!\left(-\frac{\xi}{a}\right)d\xi.$$

Substituting $\eta=-\xi/a$ ($d\xi=-a\,d\eta$; when $\xi=0$, $\eta=0$; when $\xi=
s$, $\eta=-s/a>0$):
$$F(s)=-a\int_0^{-s/a}\nu(\eta)\,d\eta,\qquad s<0.$$

**Assembling the solution.** Since $x+at>0$ always (for $x,t>0$), we have $G(x+a
t)=0$, so $u(x,t)=F(x-at)$.

- **Case 1: $x-at\geq 0$ (i.e., $x\geq at$).** Then $F(x-at)=0$, so
$$u(x,t)=0.$$

- **Case 2: $x-at<0$ (i.e., $0\leq x<at$).** Then
$$u(x,t)=F(x-at)=-a\int_0^{-(x-at)/a}\nu(\eta)\,d\eta=-a\int_0^{t-x/a}\nu(\eta)\
,d\eta.$$

**The solution is**
$$\boxed{u(x,t)=\begin{cases} -a\displaystyle\int_0^{\,t-x/a}\nu(\tau)\,d\tau, &
 0\leq x<at,\\[6pt] 0, & x\geq at. \end{cases}}$$

**Verification.**

*Boundary condition.* For $x=0$, $t>0$: we are in Case 2, so
$$u_x(x,t)=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[-a\int_0^{t-x/a}\nu(\tau)\,d\tau\rig
ht]=-a\cdot\nu(t-x/a)\cdot\left(-\frac{1}{a}\right)=\nu(t-x/a).$$
At $x=0$: $u_x(0,t)=\nu(t)$. ✓

*Initial displacement.* For $t=0$, $x>0$: $x\geq 0=a\cdot 0$, so Case 1 gives $u
(x,0)=0$. ✓

*Initial velocity.* For $t=0$, $x>0$: Case 1 gives $u\equiv 0$ in a neighborhood
, so $u_t(x,0)=0$. ✓

*Wave equation (in Case 2).* Let $\phi=t-x/a$. Then $u=-a\int_0^\phi\nu\,d\tau$,
 so
$$u_t=-a\,\nu(\phi),\quad u_{tt}=-a\,\nu'(\phi),\quad u_x=\nu(\phi),\quad u_{xx}
=-\frac{1}{a}\nu'(\phi).$$
Hence $a^2 u_{xx}=-a\,\nu'(\phi)=u_{tt}$. ✓ In Case 1, $u\equiv 0$ trivially sat
isfies the equation. The solution is continuous across the characteristic $x=at$
 (both pieces give $0$ there), and the derivatives match as well.

All conditions are satisfied.
