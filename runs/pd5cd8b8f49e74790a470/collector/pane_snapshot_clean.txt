## Proof

Let $\Pi(\mathbb{N})$ denote the set of all partitions of $\mathbb{N}$. We prove
 that $|\Pi(\mathbb{N})| = 2^{\aleph_0}$, the cardinality of the continuum.

### Upper bound: $|\Pi(\mathbb{N})| \leq 2^{\aleph_0}$

Every partition $P$ of $\mathbb{N}$ induces an equivalence relation $\sim_P$ on
$\mathbb{N}$, defined by $a \sim_P b$ if and only if $a$ and $b$ lie in the same
 block of $P$. Conversely, every equivalence relation on $\mathbb{N}$ determines
 a unique partition (its equivalence classes). This gives a **bijection** betwee
n partitions of $\mathbb{N}$ and equivalence relations on $\mathbb{N}$.

An equivalence relation on $\mathbb{N}$ is a subset of $\mathbb{N} \times \mathb
b{N}$. Since $|\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}| = \aleph_0$, the number of subsets
of $\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}$ is $2^{\aleph_0}$. Therefore:

$$|\Pi(\mathbb{N})| \leq 2^{\aleph_0}.$$

### Lower bound: $|\Pi(\mathbb{N})| \geq 2^{\aleph_0}$

We construct an injection from a family of size $2^{\aleph_0}$ into $\Pi(\mathbb
{N})$.

For each subset $S \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ with $0 \in S$ and $S \neq \mathbb{N}$,
 define the two-block partition:

$$P_S = \{S,\ \mathbb{N} \setminus S\}.$$

This is a valid partition: both blocks are non-empty ($S \neq \emptyset$ since $
0 \in S$; $\mathbb{N}\setminus S \neq \emptyset$ since $S \neq \mathbb{N}$), the
y are disjoint, and their union is $\mathbb{N}$.

**Injectivity:** Suppose $P_S = P_{S'}$, i.e., $\{S, \mathbb{N}\setminus S\} = \
{S', \mathbb{N}\setminus S'\}$. Then either $S = S'$ or $S = \mathbb{N}\setminus
 S'$. In the latter case, $0 \in S$ but $0 \notin \mathbb{N}\setminus S'$ (since
 $0 \in S'$), a contradiction. Hence $S = S'$, proving injectivity.

The number of subsets $S \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ with $0 \in S$ is $|\mathcal{P}(\
mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\})| = 2^{\aleph_0}$. Excluding the single case $S = \mathb
b{N}$ still leaves $2^{\aleph_0}$ subsets. Thus we have exhibited $2^{\aleph_0}$
 distinct partitions, giving:

$$|\Pi(\mathbb{N})| \geq 2^{\aleph_0}.$$

### Conclusion

Combining both bounds via the Cantor–Schröder–Bernstein theorem:

$$|\Pi(\mathbb{N})| = 2^{\aleph_0}.$$

The cardinality of the set of all partitions of $\mathbb{N}$ is $2^{\aleph_0}$,
the cardinality of the continuum.
