## Setup and Geometry

The first square is $[0,1]^2$. A quarter-circle arc of radius $1$ is inscribed i
n it, centered at a corner, joining the two adjacent corners. By the symmetries
of the square (which fix the center $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$), the final distance i
s independent of which corner is chosen and of the coiling direction. I pick the
 arc centered at $(0,0)$, from $P_0=(1,0)$ to $P_1=(0,1)$.

**Key observations.** Each arc is a $90^\circ$ (quarter-circle) arc, so:
- The chord from the start to the end of arc $k$ has length $r_k\sqrt{2}$, where
 $r_k = q^{k-1}$ is the radius.
- The chord direction rotates by exactly $90^\circ$ at each step (the curve turn
s $90^\circ$ per arc).
- Continuity forces the end of arc $k$ to be the start of arc $k{+}1$.

Setting $v_k = P_k - P_{k-1}$, the chord vectors form a geometrically-scaled, ro
tating sequence:
$$v_k = q^{k-1}\, w_k, \qquad w_1=(-1,1),\; w_2=(-1,-1),\; w_3=(1,-1),\; w_4=(1,
1),\;\ldots$$
with period $4$. (One verifies: $v_1=(-1,1)$ gives $P_1=(0,1)$✓; arc $2$ centere
d at $(0,1-q)$ joins $(0,1)$ to $(-q,1-q)$, i.e. $v_2=q(-1,-1)$✓, with the $q\ti
mes q$ square $[-q,0]\times[1-q,1]$ tiling against the first square — the natura
l Fibonacci construction.)

## Summing the Series

Since $0<q<1$, the series converges absolutely:
$$P_q = P_0 + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} q^{k-1} w_k = (1,0) + \frac{w_1 + q\,w_2 + q^2
 w_3 + q^3 w_4}{1-q^4}.$$

Compute the numerator:
$$w_1 + q w_2 + q^2 w_3 + q^3 w_4 = (-1{-}q{+}q^2{+}q^3,\; 1{-}q{-}q^2{+}q^3).$$
- $x$: $-(1+q)+q^2(1+q)=(1+q)(q^2-1)=-(1-q)(1+q)^2$.
- $y$: $(1-q)-q^2(1-q)=(1-q)(1-q^2)=(1-q)^2(1+q)$.

Dividing by $1-q^4=(1-q)(1+q)(1+q^2)$:
$$\sum v_k = \left(\frac{-(1+q)}{1+q^2},\;\frac{1-q}{1+q^2}\right).$$

Therefore, letting $A=\dfrac{1-q}{1+q^2}$,
$$P_q = (1,0)+(-qA,\, A) = \bigl(-qA,\; A\bigr) = \left(\frac{-q(1-q)}{1+q^2},\;
\frac{1-q}{1+q^2}\right).$$

## Distance to the Center

The center of the initial square is $C=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$.
$$d^2 = \left(-qA-\tfrac12\right)^2 + \left(A-\tfrac12\right)^2 = (qA+\tfrac12)^
2 + (A-\tfrac12)^2.$$
Expanding:
$$d^2 = q^2 A^2 + qA + \tfrac14 + A^2 - A + \tfrac14 = (1+q^2)A^2 + (q-1)A + \tf
rac12.$$

Now substitute $A=(1-q)/(1+q^2)$:
$$(1+q^2)A^2 = \frac{(1-q)^2}{1+q^2}, \qquad (q-1)A = -\frac{(1-q)^2}{1+q^2}.$$

These **cancel exactly**:
$$(1+q^2)A^2 + (q-1)A = 0.$$

Hence
$$d^2 = \frac12 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad d = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}.$$

## Result

The distance from $P_q$ to the center of the initial $1\times 1$ square is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}},$$

remarkably **independent of $q$** for all $0<q<1$.
