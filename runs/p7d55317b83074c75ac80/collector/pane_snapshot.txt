## Proof

Let **f(x) = x² − 2**. We determine for which values of *m* the equation f(f(f(x
))) = m has exactly 8 distinct real solutions.

### Step 1. Chebyshev substitution on [−2, 2]

For x ∈ [−2, 2], write x = 2 cos θ with θ ∈ [0, π]. Then

f(2 cos θ) = 4 cos²θ − 2 = 2 cos(2θ).

Iterating, f⁽ⁿ⁾(2 cos θ) = 2 cos(2ⁿ θ), so in particular

f(f(f(x))) = 2 cos(8θ),   where x = 2 cos θ, θ ∈ [0, π].

### Step 2. No solutions outside [−2, 2] when m ≤ 2

For |x| > 2, f(x) = x² − 2 > 2. On (2, ∞), f is strictly increasing and maps (2,
 ∞) into (2, ∞), so f(f(f(x))) > 2 for all |x| > 2. Hence for m ≤ 2, every solut
ion lies in [−2, 2].

### Step 3. Counting solutions via cos(8θ) = m/2

With θ ∈ [0, π], we have 8θ ∈ [0, 8π], covering **4 full periods** of cosine.

- **|m| < 2 (i.e. m/2 ∈ (−1, 1)):** cos(8θ) = m/2 has exactly 2 solutions per pe
riod, and since m/2 ≠ ±1 none coincide with period endpoints. Thus 4 × 2 = **8 d
istinct** values of θ, giving 8 distinct values x = 2 cos θ. Combined with Step
2 (no exterior solutions), the total is **exactly 8**.

- **m = 2:** cos(8θ) = 1 ⟹ 8θ = 2kπ, k = 0,1,…,4 (within [0, 8π] with θ ∈ [0,π])
, giving θ = 0, π/4, π/2, 3π/4, π → **5 solutions**. (One checks x > 2 and x < −
2 yield no additional solutions.) Total = 5 ≠ 8.

- **m = −2:** cos(8θ) = −1 ⟹ 8θ = π, 3π, 5π, 7π → θ = π/8, 3π/8, 5π/8, 7π/8 → **
4 solutions**. Total = 4 ≠ 8.

- **m > 2:** No interior solutions (cos(8θ) = m/2 > 1 impossible); exterior give
s only 2 solutions (one with x > 2, one with x < −2). Total = 2 ≠ 8.

- **m < −2:** Range of f∘f∘f is [−2, ∞), so no solutions. Total = 0 ≠ 8.

### Step 4. Conclusion

The equation f(f(f(x))) = m has **exactly 8 distinct real solutions precisely fo
r m ∈ (−2, 2)**. The greatest integer in this open interval is

**m = 1.**
