## Solution

**Setup.** Model the 36 canisters as cells of a $4\times 9$ grid: rows = element
al groups $\{1,2,3,4\}$, columns = pressure levels $\{1,\dots,9\}$. Cell $(g,\el
l)$ reacts with $(g',\ell')$ iff $g=g'$ or $\ell=\ell'$.

Aris selects a set $A$ of 18 cells; Boris gets $B$ (the complement, also 18). Th
e 18 phases define an **online bipartite matching game** on the *reaction graph*
 $G_R$ (bipartite between $A$ and $B$, edge = same row or same column): Aris rev
eals vertices of $A$ one at a time (adversarial order), Boris must irrevocably a
ssign each to an unused vertex of $B$; a "stability unit" is scored per edge use
d. Aris minimizes, Boris maximizes.

---

**Step 1. Boris achieves the maximum matching, regardless of order.**

Fix the partition $A,B$. Let $M^*$ be a maximum matching in $G_R$, of size $m$.
Boris's strategy: precompute $M^*$; when Aris reveals $a\in A$, if $a$ is matche
d in $M^*$ to some $b\in B$, respond with $b$; otherwise respond with any leftov
er canister.

The key observation: $b$ is still available when $a$ is revealed, because $b$ is
 only ever played when its $M^*$-partner arrives, and that partner is $a$ itself
, arriving now for the first time. Hence Boris completes all $m$ edges of $M^*$,
 scoring exactly $m = |M^*|$ reactions. The order is irrelevant.

So the game value for a fixed $A$ is exactly $\nu(G_R)$, the maximum matching si
ze. Aris chooses $A$ to minimize this, so the answer is
$$\min_{|A|=18}\nu(G_R(A)) = 36 - \max_{|A|=18}\alpha(G_R(A)),$$
where $\alpha$ is the maximum independent set (using König: $\nu = |A|+|B|-\alph
a = 36-\alpha$ in a bipartite graph).

---

**Step 2. Reformulating the independent set.**

An independent set $I$ in $G_R$ (bipartite, edges between $A$ and $B$) splits as
 $X\subseteq A$, $Y\subseteq B$ with **no reaction** between any $x\in X$, $y\in
 Y$. "No reaction" means $g(x)\neq g(y)$ **and** $\ell(x)\neq\ell(y)$, which is
equivalent to
$$\text{Groups}(X)\cap\text{Groups}(Y)=\varnothing \quad\text{and}\quad \text{Le
vels}(X)\cap\text{Levels}(Y)=\varnothing.$$

For fixed disjoint group-sets $G_X\perp G_Y$ and disjoint level-sets $L_X\perp L
_Y$, the best choice is $X=A\cap(G_X\times L_X)$, $Y=B\cap(G_Y\times L_Y)$. Let
$P=G_X\times L_X$, $Q=G_Y\times L_Y$ (disjoint rectangles, $|P|=ab$, $|Q|=cd$ wh
ere $a=|G_X|,b=|L_X|,c=|G_Y|,d=|L_Y|$, with $a+c\le 4$, $b+d\le 9$). Then
$$\alpha(A)\ge |A\cap P|+|B\cap Q|,\quad\text{and in fact }\alpha(A)=\max_{P,Q}\
bigl[|A\cap P|+|B\cap Q|\bigr].$$

---

**Step 3. Optimizing over $A$.**

For fixed rectangles $P,Q$ (with $P\cap Q=\varnothing$), optimizing over $A$ wit
h $|A|=18$:
$$\max_{|A|=18}\bigl[|A\cap P|+|B\cap Q|\bigr]=\min(|P|,18)+\min(|Q|,18).$$
(If $|P|,|Q|\le 18$: take $A\supseteq P$, $A\cap Q=\varnothing$, value $|P|+|Q|$
. If $|P|>18$: take $A\subseteq P$, value $18+|Q|$. Symmetric for $|Q|>18$. Both
 $>18$ is impossible since $P,Q$ are disjoint in a 36-cell grid.)

Therefore
$$\max_{|A|=18}\alpha(A)=\max_{\substack{a+c\le 4,\;b+d\le 9\\a,b,c,d\ge 0}}\Big
l[\min(ab,18)+\min(cd,18)\Bigr].$$

Since adding unused groups/levels only helps, take $a+c=4$, $b+d=9$ (i.e. $c=4-a
$, $d=9-b$) and enumerate $a\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}$:

| $a$ | expression $\min(3b{+}\cdot,18)+\min(\cdot,18)$ | max |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | $0+\min(4(9{-}b),18)$ | 18 |
| 1 | $b+\min(3(9{-}b),18)$, max at $b=3$ | **21** |
| 2 | $2b+\min(2(9{-}b),18)=18$ | 18 |
| 3 | $3b+\min(9{-}b,18)$, max at $b=6$ | **21** |
| 4 | $\min(4b,18)+0$ | 18 |

The maximum is $\mathbf{21}$, attained at $(a,b,c,d)=(3,6,1,3)$ (or symmetricall
y $(1,3,3,6)$).

---

**Step 4. The extremal configuration.**

Take $G_X=\{1,2,3\}$, $L_X=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$, so $P=G_X\times L_X$ has $3\cdot 6=
18$ cells. Set $A=P$ (Aris's 18 canisters). Then $B$ is the complement. With $G_
Y=\{4\}$, $L_Y=\{7,8,9\}$, $Q=G_Y\times L_Y$ has $1\cdot 3=3$ cells, all lying i
n $B$ (since $P\cap Q=\varnothing$). Thus
$$\alpha(A)\ge |A\cap P|+|B\cap Q|=18+3=21,$$
matching the upper bound, so $\alpha(A)=21$ and $\nu(G_R)=36-21=15$.

To confirm $\nu=15$ directly: $B\setminus\{(4,7),(4,8),(4,9)\}$ is a vertex cove
r of size 15 (any reaction edge $(g,\ell)\in A$–$(g',\ell')\in B$ with $(g',\ell
')\in\{(4,7),(4,8),(4,9)\}$ would require $g=4$—impossible since $g\in\{1,2,3\}$
—or $\ell=\ell'\in\{7,8,9\}$—impossible since $\ell\in\{1,\dots,6\}$). By König,
 $\nu\le 15$; combined with $\nu=36-21=15$, equality holds.

---

**Conclusion.** For every choice of $A$, Boris guarantees at least $36-21=15$ st
ability units (via the $M^*$-following strategy), and Aris can choose the $3\tim
es 6$ block configuration to hold Boris to exactly 15. The maximum number of sta
bility units Boris can guarantee is

$$\boxed{15}.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
