**Answer: The statement is FALSE.**

## Proof

We exhibit an explicit counterexample: the polynomial algebra $A = k[x]$ over a
field $k$, which is an associative (commutative) algebra that is **infinite-dime
nsional** over $k$.

Suppose, for contradiction, that $A$ admits a faithful matrix representation, i.
e., an injective $k$-algebra homomorphism
$$\varphi: k[x] \hookrightarrow M_n(k)$$
for some finite $n$.

Any $k$-algebra homomorphism from $k[x]$ is completely determined by the image o
f $x$. Let $T := \varphi(x) \in M_n(k)$. Then for every polynomial $p \in k[x]$,
$$\varphi(p) = p(T).$$

By the **Cayley–Hamilton theorem**, every matrix satisfies its own characteristi
c polynomial: if $\chi_T(\lambda) = \det(\lambda I - T)$ is the characteristic p
olynomial of $T$ (a monic polynomial of degree $n$ in $k[\lambda]$), then
$$\chi_T(T) = 0.$$

Therefore
$$\varphi(\chi_T) = \chi_T(T) = 0.$$

But $\chi_T$ is a **nonzero** polynomial (it is monic of degree $n \geq 1$), so
$\ker\varphi \neq \{0\}$. This contradicts the assumption that $\varphi$ is fait
hful (injective).

Hence $k[x]$ admits **no** faithful finite-dimensional matrix representation, an
d the statement "All associative algebras have a faithful matrix representation"
 is **false**. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on the boundary.** The obstruction is purely dimensional: every *finite
-dimensional* associative algebra (with or without identity) *does* admit a fait
hful matrix representation — for unital algebras via the left regular representa
tion $a \mapsto (x \mapsto ax)$ on $A$ itself (injective since $a \cdot 1 = a$),
 and for non-unital algebras by passing to the unitization $A \oplus k$ and rest
ricting the regular representation. The statement fails precisely for infinite-d
imensional algebras, since $\dim_k M_n(k) = n^2 < \infty$ cannot contain an inje
ctive image of an infinite-dimensional space.
