## Proof

We compute $\displaystyle \oint_{|z|=2} \tan(z)\,dz$ using the residue theorem.

**Step 1: Identify the singularities of $\tan(z)$.**

Since $\tan(z) = \dfrac{\sin z}{\cos z}$, the singularities occur where $\cos z
= 0$, i.e. at
$$z_n = \frac{\pi}{2} + n\pi, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z}.$$
At each such point $\sin(z_n) = \pm 1 \neq 0$, so every $z_n$ is a simple pole o
f $\tan(z)$.

**Step 2: Determine which poles lie inside $|z| = 2$.**

We need $|z_n| < 2$. Using $\pi/2 \approx 1.5708$:

- $n = 0$: $z_0 = \pi/2 \approx 1.5708$, $|z_0| \approx 1.5708 < 2$. ✓ Inside.
- $n = -1$: $z_{-1} = -\pi/2 \approx -1.5708$, $|z_{-1}| \approx 1.5708 < 2$. ✓
Inside.
- $n = 1$: $z_1 = \pi/2 + \pi = 3\pi/2 \approx 4.7124$, $|z_1| \approx 4.7124 >
2$. ✗ Outside.
- $n = -2$: $z_{-2} = -3\pi/2 \approx -4.7124$, $|z_{-2}| \approx 4.7124 > 2$. ✗
 Outside.

All other poles have $|z_n| \geq 3\pi/2 > 2$, so they lie strictly outside the c
ontour. Hence exactly two poles lie inside $|z| = 2$: $z_0 = \pi/2$ and $z_{-1}
= -\pi/2$.

**Step 3: Compute the residue at each pole.**

Each pole is simple. For a quotient $f(z)/g(z)$ with a simple zero of $g$ at $z_
0$ and $f(z_0) \neq 0$,
$$\operatorname{Res}\!\left(\frac{f}{g},\, z_0\right) = \frac{f(z_0)}{g'(z_0)}.$
$
Here $f(z) = \sin z$ and $g(z) = \cos z$, with $g'(z) = -\sin z$. Thus at every
pole $z_n$,
$$\operatorname{Res}(\tan,\, z_n) = \frac{\sin(z_n)}{-\sin(z_n)} = -1.$$

In particular:
$$\operatorname{Res}(\tan,\, \pi/2) = -1, \qquad \operatorname{Res}(\tan,\, -\pi
/2) = -1.$$

**Step 4: Apply the residue theorem.**

The contour $|z| = 2$ is a positively oriented simple closed curve enclosing bot
h poles, and $\tan(z)$ is analytic on and inside the contour except at those two
 poles. By the residue theorem,
$$\oint_{|z|=2} \tan(z)\,dz = 2\pi i \sum \operatorname{Res}(\tan,\, z_k) = 2\pi
 i \bigl((-1) + (-1)\bigr) = 2\pi i \cdot (-2).$$

Therefore,
$$\boxed{\oint_{|z|=2} \tan(z)\,dz = -4\pi i.}$$
