## Problem

A bank balance is modeled by Brownian motion with positive drift. Calculate the
expected time until it first becomes negative.

## Setup

Let the balance be
$$X_t = x_0 + \mu t + \sigma W_t, \qquad x_0 > 0,\ \mu > 0,\ \sigma > 0,$$
where $W_t$ is standard Brownian motion. Define the first passage (ruin) time
$$T = \inf\{t \ge 0 : X_t < 0\} = \inf\{t \ge 0 : X_t = 0\},$$
with the convention $T = \infty$ if $X_t$ never reaches $0$. By continuity of pa
ths, hitting $0$ and becoming negative coincide.

## Key observation: ruin is not certain

Because the drift $\mu > 0$ points *away* from the barrier at $0$, there is a st
rictly positive probability that the balance never becomes negative. We compute
this probability exactly.

Consider the exponential
$$M_t := \exp\!\Big(-\tfrac{2\mu}{\sigma^2} X_t\Big).$$
Writing $X_t = x_0 + \mu t + \sigma W_t$,
$$M_t = e^{-2\mu x_0/\sigma^2}\,\exp\!\Big(-\tfrac{2\mu}{\sigma}W_t - \tfrac{2\m
u^2}{\sigma^2}t\Big).$$
Since $\exp(\theta W_t - \tfrac12\theta^2 t)$ is a martingale with $\theta = -2\
mu/\sigma$ (note $\tfrac12\theta^2 = 2\mu^2/\sigma^2$), $M_t$ is a non-negative
martingale with $M_0 = e^{-2\mu x_0/\sigma^2}$.

Apply optional stopping at $T \wedge n$:
$$\mathbb{E}\big[M_{T\wedge n}\big] = e^{-2\mu x_0/\sigma^2}.$$

Let $n \to \infty$. On $\{T < \infty\}$, continuity gives $X_T = 0$, so $M_T = 1
$. On $\{T = \infty\}$, the strong law of large numbers for Brownian motion give
s $X_t/t \to \mu > 0$, hence $X_t \to +\infty$ and $M_t \to 0$. By Fatou's lemma
 (justified since $M_{T\wedge n} \le 1$ on $\{T<\infty\}$ eventually and $\to 0$
 on $\{T=\infty\}$, with bounded contributions),
$$e^{-2\mu x_0/\sigma^2} = \mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{1}_{\{T<\infty\}}\big] = \math
bb{P}(T < \infty).$$

Therefore
$$\mathbb{P}(T = \infty) = 1 - e^{-2\mu x_0/\sigma^2} > 0.$$

## Conclusion: the expected time is infinite

Since $T = \infty$ with strictly positive probability $1 - e^{-2\mu x_0/\sigma^2
} > 0$,
$$\mathbb{E}[T] = \infty.$$

A non-negative random variable that equals $+\infty$ with positive probability h
as infinite expectation. Thus **the expected time until the balance first become
s negative is infinite** — intuitively, with positive drift the process may simp
ly drift to $+\infty$ and never be ruined, and that event has positive mass, whi
ch is enough to make the mean infinite.

## Remark (conditional expectation)

For completeness, the *conditional* first-passage time, given that ruin occurs,
is finite and equals the mean of an inverse Gaussian. The defective density of $
T$ is
$$f(t) = \frac{x_0}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi t^3}}\exp\!\Big(-\frac{(x_0+\mu t)^2}{2\sig
ma^2 t}\Big),\qquad t>0,$$
with total mass $\mathbb{P}(T<\infty)=e^{-2\mu x_0/\sigma^2}$. Dividing by this
mass gives the proper density
$$g(t)=\frac{x_0}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi t^3}}\exp\!\Big(-\frac{(x_0-\mu t)^2}{2\sigma
^2 t}\Big),$$
i.e. $T\mid\{T<\infty\}\sim \mathrm{IG}\!\big(\tfrac{x_0}{\mu},\,\tfrac{x_0^2}{\
sigma^2}\big)$, with conditional mean
$$\mathbb{E}[T\mid T<\infty] = \frac{x_0}{\mu}.$$

But the quantity asked for — the unconditional expected time — is

$$\boxed{\;\mathbb{E}[T] = \infty\;}$$

because the positive drift makes ruin uncertain: $\mathbb{P}(T=\infty)=1-e^{-2\m
u x_0/\sigma^2}>0$.
