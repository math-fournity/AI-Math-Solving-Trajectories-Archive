The statement is **true**.

## Proof

Let $f \in \mathbb{C}(X)^\times$ be a nonzero rational function. We factor $f$ o
ver $\mathbb{C}$ as

$$f(X) = c\,\prod_{i=1}^{n} (X - a_i)^{m_i},$$

where $c \in \mathbb{C}^\times$, the $a_i \in \mathbb{C}$ are the distinct finit
e zeros and poles of $f$, and $m_i \in \mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$ is the order of
 $f$ at $a_i$ (positive for a zero, negative for a pole). By unique factorizatio
n in $\mathbb{C}[X]$, this factorization is unique up to reordering, and every r
ational function has this form.

**Orders at finite points.** For any $v \in \mathbb{C}$, the local ring $\mathca
l{O}_v \subset \mathbb{C}(X)$ has uniformizer $X - v$. Reading the factorization
 above, a uniformizer $X-v$ divides $f$ exactly $\sum_{i: a_i = v} m_i$ times. H
ence

$$\operatorname{ord}_v(f) = \begin{cases} m_i & \text{if } v = a_i \text{ for so
me } i,\\ 0 & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$

So the contribution of all finite points is $\sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i$.

**Order at infinity.** The point $\infty \in \mathbb{CP}^1$ has local uniformize
r $t = 1/X$. Substituting $X = 1/t$,

$$f(1/t) = c\,\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\tfrac{1}{t} - a_i\right)^{m_i} = c\,\prod_{i
=1}^{n} t^{-m_i}(1 - a_i t)^{m_i} = c\, t^{-\sum_i m_i}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1 - a_i t
)^{m_i}.$$

Each factor $(1 - a_i t)^{m_i}$ is a unit in $\mathcal{O}_\infty = \mathbb{C}[t]
_{(t)}$ (it evaluates to $1$ at $t = 0$, i.e. is nonzero at $\infty$), and $c \i
n \mathbb{C}^\times$ is also a unit. Therefore the order of $f$ at $\infty$ is t
he exponent of the uniformizer $t$:

$$\operatorname{ord}_\infty(f) = -\sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i.$$

**Summing over all of $\mathbb{CP}^1$.** The points of $\mathbb{CP}^1$ are $\mat
hbb{C} \cup \{\infty\}$, and $\operatorname{ord}_v(f) = 0$ for all but finitely
many $v$ (only the $a_i$ and $\infty$ can contribute). Thus

$$\sum_{v \in \mathbb{CP}^1} \operatorname{ord}_v(f) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \operatorn
ame{ord}_{a_i}(f) + \operatorname{ord}_\infty(f) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i + \left(-\
sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i\right) = 0.$$

Hence

$$\boxed{\sum_{v \in \mathbb{CP}^1} \operatorname{ord}_v(f) = 0.}$$

**Remark.** This is the genus-$0$ instance of the general fact that on a compact
 Riemann surface (or smooth projective curve) the divisor of any nonzero meromor
phic/rational function has degree $0$ — equivalently, the number of zeros equals
 the number of poles counted with multiplicity. The proof above is the explicit
computation for $\mathbb{CP}^1$: the order at infinity exactly cancels the total
 order at the finite points, because $\operatorname{ord}_\infty(f) = -\deg f$ wh
ere $\deg f$ is the degree of $f$ viewed as a map $\mathbb{CP}^1 \to \mathbb{CP}
^1$.
