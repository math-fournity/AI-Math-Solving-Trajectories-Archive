## Solution

We need the smallest positive integer $n$ such that
$$n\sin(1) > 5\cos(1)+1,$$
where the argument $1$ is in radians.

### Step 1. Rigorous bounds for $\sin(1)$ and $\cos(1)$

Using the Taylor series (alternating series with strictly decreasing term magnit
udes, so the truncation error is bounded by the first omitted term):

$$\sin(1) = 1 - \tfrac{1}{6} + \tfrac{1}{120} - \tfrac{1}{5040} + \tfrac{1}{3628
80} - \cdots$$

Summing through the $\tfrac{1}{362880}$ term:
$$\sin(1) \approx 0.841471, \qquad |\text{error}| < \tfrac{1}{3628800} < 3\times
 10^{-7}.$$

$$\cos(1) = 1 - \tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{24} - \tfrac{1}{720} + \tfrac{1}{40320}
 - \cdots$$

Summing through the $\tfrac{1}{40320}$ term:
$$\cos(1) \approx 0.540303, \qquad |\text{error}| < \tfrac{1}{3628800} < 3\times
 10^{-7}.$$

### Step 2. Evaluate the threshold

$$5\cos(1)+1 \approx 5(0.540303)+1 = 3.701515.$$

### Step 3. Check $n=4$

$$4\sin(1) \approx 4(0.841471) = 3.365884.$$

The gap is
$$3.701515 - 3.365884 = 0.335631,$$

which far exceeds the combined rounding error (well under $10^{-5}$). Therefore
$$4\sin(1) < 5\cos(1)+1,$$
so $n=4$ does **not** trigger transmission.

### Step 4. Check $n=5$

$$5\sin(1) \approx 5(0.841471) = 4.207355.$$

The gap is
$$4.207355 - 3.701515 = 0.505840,$$

again far exceeding any rounding error. Therefore
$$5\sin(1) > 5\cos(1)+1,$$
so $n=5$ **does** trigger transmission.

### Conclusion

Since $n=4$ fails and $n=5$ satisfies the strict inequality, and the inequality
is monotone increasing in $n$ (as $\sin(1)>0$), the smallest positive integer $n
$ for which the probe triggers its first data transmission is

$$\boxed{5}.$$
