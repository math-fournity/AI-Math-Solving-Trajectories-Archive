$$(x+y)^n\bigl(x^2 - (2-\epsilon)\,xy + y^2\bigr), \quad \epsilon \in (0,1).$$

This is a Pólya-type positivity problem. I'll prove it by direct coefficient com
putation.

**Proof.**

Let $\epsilon\in(0,1)$ and define the homogeneous polynomial of degree $n+2$:
$$P_n(x,y)=(x+y)^n\bigl(x^2-(2-\epsilon)\,xy+y^2\bigr).$$

We show that for all sufficiently large integers $n$, every coefficient of $P_n$
 is strictly positive.

**Step 1 — Coefficient formula.**

Since $(x+y)^n=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}x^k y^{n-k}$, the coefficient of $x^j y
^{n+2-j}$ ($0\le j\le n+2$) is
$$c_j=\binom{n}{j-2}-(2-\epsilon)\binom{n}{j-1}+\binom{n}{j},$$
with $\binom{n}{k}=0$ for $k<0$ or $k>n$.

**Step 2 — Boundary coefficients.**

- $c_0=\binom{n}{0}=1>0$.
- $c_1=-(2-\epsilon)\cdot 1+\binom{n}{1}=n-2+\epsilon>0$ for $n\ge 2$.
- $c_{n+1}=\binom{n}{n-1}-(2-\epsilon)\cdot 1=n-2+\epsilon>0$ for $n\ge 2$.
- $c_{n+2}=\binom{n}{n}=1>0$.

**Step 3 — Interior coefficients ($2\le j\le n$).**

All three binomial coefficients are nonzero, so factoring out $\binom{n}{j-1}>0$
:
$$c_j=\binom{n}{j-1}\!\left[\frac{j-1}{\,n-j+2\,}-(2-\epsilon)+\frac{n-j+1}{\,j\
,}\right].$$

Thus $c_j>0$ iff
$$L_j:=\frac{j-1}{n-j+2}+\frac{n-j+1}{j}>2-\epsilon.\tag{$\star$}$$

**Step 4 — Minimizing $L_j$ over $j$.**

Set $m=\frac{n+2}{2}$ and $s=j-m$, so $j=m+s$, $\;n-j+2=m-s$, $\;j-1=m+s-1$, $\;
n-j+1=m-s-1$. Then
$$L(s)=\frac{m+s-1}{m-s}+\frac{m-s-1}{m+s}=\frac{2(m^2+s^2-m)}{m^2-s^2},$$
valid for $|s|<m$ (guaranteed by $2\le j\le n$).

**Claim:** $L(s)\ge L(0)=2-\frac{2}{m}$, with equality iff $s=0$.

*Proof of claim.* Compute the difference:
$$L(s)-L(0)=\frac{2(m^2+s^2-m)}{m^2-s^2}-\frac{2(m-1)}{m}=\frac{2(2m-1)\,s^2}{m\
,(m^2-s^2)}.$$

Since $m\ge 1$ (for $n\ge 0$) we have $2m-1>0$, and $m^2-s^2>0$ in the valid ran
ge, so $L(s)-L(0)\ge 0$, with equality iff $s=0$. $\square$

So the **minimum** of $L_j$ over all interior $j$ is $L(0)=2-\frac{2}{m}$, attai
ned at $j=\frac{n+2}{2}$ (when this is an integer, i.e., $n$ even; for $n$ odd,
$s$ is a half-integer and $L(s)>L(0)$ strictly).

**Step 5 — Choosing $n$ large enough.**

We need $L(s)>2-\epsilon$ for all valid $s$. By Step 4 it suffices to require
$$L(0)=2-\frac{2}{m}>2-\epsilon \iff \frac{2}{m}<\epsilon \iff m>\frac{2}{\epsil
on}\iff \frac{n+2}{2}>\frac{2}{\epsilon}\iff n>\frac{4}{\epsilon}-2.$$

For any integer $n>\frac{4}{\epsilon}-2$, we have $L(s)\ge L(0)>2-\epsilon$ for
every valid $s$, hence $c_j>0$ for all $2\le j\le n$. Together with Step 2, all
coefficients $c_0,c_1,\dots,c_{n+2}$ are strictly positive.

**Verification (example).** Take $\epsilon=1$, so the threshold is $n>2$. For $n
=3$:
$$(x+y)^3(x^2-xy+y^2)=x^5+2x^4y+x^3y^2+x^2y^3+2xy^4+y^5,$$
and indeed every coefficient is positive. ✓

**Conclusion.** For every $\epsilon\in(0,1)$, the polynomial $(x+y)^n\bigl(x^2-(
2-\epsilon)\,xy+y^2\bigr)$ has all strictly positive coefficients for every inte
ger
$$n>\frac{4}{\epsilon}-2.$$
In particular, this holds for all $n$ sufficiently large. $\blacksquare$
