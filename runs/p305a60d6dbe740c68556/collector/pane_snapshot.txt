## Proof

We are given that $\bar{f}(y) = f(Ty)$ for some linear map $T$, and the first-or
der (gradient) relation:

$$\nabla \bar{f}(y) = T^{T}\,\nabla f(Ty).$$

The Hessian $\nabla^2 \bar{f}(y)$ is the Jacobian of the gradient, i.e., the der
ivative of $\nabla \bar{f}(y)$ with respect to $y$:

$$\nabla^2 \bar{f}(y) = \frac{\partial}{\partial y}\,\nabla \bar{f}(y) = \frac{\
partial}{\partial y}\Big[T^{T}\,\nabla f(Ty)\Big].$$

Since $T^{T}$ is a constant matrix (independent of $y$), it factors out of the d
ifferentiation:

$$\nabla^2 \bar{f}(y) = T^{T}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\big[\nabla f(Ty)\big]
.$$

It remains to compute $\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\big[\nabla f(Ty)\big]$. Defin
e the vector-valued map $g(y) = \nabla f(Ty)$. By the chain rule, the Jacobian o
f $g$ with respect to $y$ is the Jacobian of $\nabla f$ evaluated at the point $
Ty$, multiplied by the Jacobian of the inner map $y \mapsto Ty$.

- The Jacobian of $\nabla f$ at the point $x = Ty$ is the Hessian of $f$ at $x$,
 namely $\nabla^2 f(Ty)$.
- The Jacobian of the linear map $y \mapsto Ty$ is the constant matrix $T$.

Therefore, by the chain rule:

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\big[\nabla f(Ty)\big] = \nabla^2 f(Ty)\,T.$$

Substituting back:

$$\boxed{\nabla^2 \bar{f}(y) = T^{T}\,\nabla^2 f(Ty)\,T.}$$

**Consistency check.** When $f$ is twice continuously differentiable, $\nabla^2
f(Ty)$ is symmetric, and

$$\big(\nabla^2 \bar{f}(y)\big)^{T} = T^{T}\,\big(\nabla^2 f(Ty)\big)^{T}\,T = T
^{T}\,\nabla^2 f(Ty)\,T = \nabla^2 \bar{f}(y),$$

so the derived Hessian is symmetric, as required for the Hessian of a $C^2$ scal
ar function. This confirms the result.
