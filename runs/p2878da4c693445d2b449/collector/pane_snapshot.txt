## Definition of a Regular Convex Polyhedron

Let $P$ be a convex $m$-dimensional polyhedron in $\mathbb{R}^k$.

### Flags

A **flag** of $P$ is a maximal chain of mutually incident faces, containing exac
tly one face of each dimension $0, 1, \dots, m$. Formally, a flag is a tuple
$$\Phi = (F_0, F_1, \dots, F_m)$$
where:
- $F_i$ is an $i$-dimensional face of $P$ for each $i = 0, 1, \dots, m$,
- $F_0 \subsetneq F_1 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq F_m = P$,
- $F_0$ is a vertex, $F_1$ is an edge containing $F_0$, $F_2$ is a 2-face contai
ning $F_1$, and so on, with $F_{m-1}$ a facet and $F_m = P$ itself.

Equivalently, a flag selects one vertex, one edge through that vertex, one 2-fac
e through that edge, and so forth up to the entire polyhedron. The set of all fl
ags of $P$ is denoted $\mathcal{F}(P)$.

### Rotational Symmetry Group

The **rotational symmetry group** $G(P)$ of $P$ is the group of all isometries o
f $\mathbb{R}^k$ that map $P$ onto itself:
$$G(P) = \{\, g : \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}^k \mid g \text{ is an isometry and
 } g(P) = P \,\}.$$

When $k > m$, every symmetry of the $m$-dimensional polyhedron $P$ — including r
eflections within the $m$-dimensional affine subspace containing $P$ — can be re
alized as a rotation (orientation-preserving isometry) in the ambient $k$-dimens
ional space. This is because an $m$-dimensional reflection extends to a $k$-dime
nsional rotation by rotating in the 2-plane spanned by the reflection normal and
 an extra direction perpendicular to the $m$-flat. Thus, when $k > m$, $G(P)$ co
nsists entirely of rotations of $\mathbb{R}^k$ and coincides with the full symme
try group of $P$.

### Regularity

The polyhedron $P$ is **regular** if and only if $G(P)$ acts **transitively** on
 the set of flags $\mathcal{F}(P)$:

$$\forall\, \Phi, \Phi' \in \mathcal{F}(P),\ \ \exists\, g \in G(P) \text{ such
that } g(F_i) = F'_i \text{ for all } i = 0, 1, \dots, m.$$

In other words, for any two flags $\Phi = (F_0, \dots, F_m)$ and $\Phi' = (F'_0,
 \dots, F'_m)$, there is a symmetry $g \in G(P)$ that maps each face $F_i$ of th
e first flag to the corresponding face $F'_i$ of the second.

### Meaning of the Condition

Flag-transitivity is the strongest possible homogeneity condition on a polyhedro
n: it says that every local incidence configuration — one face of each dimension
, all nested — is geometrically indistinguishable from every other. The polyhedr
on "looks the same" when viewed from any flag. This single condition implies the
 more familiar consequences of regularity:

- **Vertex-transitivity**: $G(P)$ acts transitively on vertices (project any fla
g to its $F_0$).
- **Face-transitivity in every dimension**: $G(P)$ acts transitively on $i$-face
s for each $i$ (project any flag to its $F_i$).
- **Uniformity of vertex figures**: the vertex figure at every vertex is congrue
nt (it is determined by the flag up to symmetry).
- **Recursive regularity**: every facet $F_{m-1}$ is itself a regular $(m-1)$-po
lyhedron, and every vertex figure is a regular $(m-1)$-polyhedron.

Conversely, for convex polytopes, these weaker conditions together imply flag-tr
ansitivity, so the definition is robust: regularity = flag-transitivity of the s
ymmetry group.
