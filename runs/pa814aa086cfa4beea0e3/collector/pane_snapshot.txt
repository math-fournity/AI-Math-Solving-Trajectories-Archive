Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $F/F_0$ can contain an unco
untable injective (equivalently, divisible) $\mathbb{Z}$-submodule, where $F$ is
 free abelian and $F_0$ is countable.

**Answer: The statement is TRUE.**

## Proof

**Key facts.** Over $\mathbb{Z}$, injective modules coincide with divisible grou
ps. The maximal divisible subgroup $d(G)$ of any abelian group $G$ exists (it is
 the sum of all divisible subgroups, which is again divisible), $G = d(G) \oplus
 R$ for some reduced subgroup $R$, and every injective submodule of $G$ is conta
ined in $d(G)$. Moreover, $d(A \oplus B) = d(A) \oplus d(B)$: the inclusion $\su
pseteq$ is clear; for $\subseteq$, the projection of $d(A\oplus B)$ onto $A$ is
a homomorphic image of a divisible group, hence divisible, so lies in $d(A)$, an
d similarly for $B$.

**Step 1: $F_0$ lies in a countable-rank direct summand of $F$.**

Write $F \cong \mathbb{Z}^{(\kappa)}$ with basis $\{e_i\}_{i \in I}$. Since $F_0
$ is countable, it is generated by countably many elements $\{g_n\}_{n \in \omeg
a}$. Each $g_n$ is a finite $\mathbb{Z}$-linear combination of basis elements, s
o the set
$$S = \{i \in I : e_i \text{ appears with nonzero coefficient in some } g_n\}$$
is countable. Let $F_S = \bigoplus_{i \in S} \mathbb{Z}\, e_i$ and $F_{S^c} = \b
igoplus_{i \in I \setminus S} \mathbb{Z}\, e_i$. Then $F = F_S \oplus F_{S^c}$ a
nd $F_0 \subseteq F_S$.

**Step 2: Decompose the quotient.**

Since $F_0 \subseteq F_S$, by the isomorphism theorem:
$$F/F_0 = (F_S \oplus F_{S^c})/F_0 \cong (F_S / F_0) \oplus F_{S^c}.$$

**Step 3: Analyze the maximal divisible subgroup.**

- $F_S$ is a countable free abelian group (countable rank), and $F_0$ is a subgr
oup, so $F_S / F_0$ is countable. Hence $d(F_S/F_0)$ is countable.
- $F_{S^c}$ is free abelian, and free abelian groups are **reduced** (they conta
in no nonzero divisible subgroup: if $x \neq 0$ in a free abelian group and $n \
cdot y = x$, then $y$ must also lie in the group, but iterating shows the rank w
ould have to be infinite in a single direction, which is impossible in a free ab
elian group — equivalently, $\mathbb{Z}$ is reduced and direct sums of reduced g
roups are reduced). So $d(F_{S^c}) = 0$.

Using $d(A \oplus B) = d(A) \oplus d(B)$:
$$d(F/F_0) = d(F_S/F_0) \oplus d(F_{S^c}) = d(F_S/F_0) \oplus 0 = d(F_S/F_0),$$
which is **countable**.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Every injective (= divisible) submodule of $F/F_0$ is contained in $d(F/F_0)$, w
hich is countable. Therefore $F/F_0$ contains no uncountable injective submodule
. $\blacksquare$
