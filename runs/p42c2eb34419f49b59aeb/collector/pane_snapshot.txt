**Answer: Yes, F is always real-closed.**

## Proof

Let σ : K → K be the given involution (order-2 field automorphism), and set G =
{id, σ} ⊆ Aut(K). By hypothesis |G| = 2 and the fixed field is F = K^G, which is
 a proper subfield of K.

### Step 1. [K : F] = 2 (Artin's fixed-field theorem)

We use **Artin's theorem on fixed fields of finite automorphism groups**: *If G
is a finite group of automorphisms of a field L and K = L^G is its fixed field,
then [L : K] = |G|; in particular L/K is a finite Galois extension with Gal(L/K)
 = G.*

This theorem requires **no a priori hypothesis** that L/K be algebraic — finiten
ess of the automorphism group alone forces [L : K] = |G|. The proof uses Dedekin
d's lemma (distinct automorphisms are linearly independent over L) to build |G|
linearly independent elements of L over K, giving [L : K] ≥ |G|, and the minimal
 polynomial argument (each x ∈ L has orbit {g·x : g ∈ G} ⊆ L, so x is algebraic
over K of degree ≤ |G|) to get [L : K] ≤ |G|.

Applying this with L = K and G = {id, σ}:

$$[K : F] = |G| = 2, \qquad \operatorname{Gal}(K/F) = G.$$

So **K/F is automatically a finite (degree-2) Galois extension**, even though th
e problem never assumed K/F algebraic. This is the key point.

### Step 2. Apply the Artin–Schreier theorem

The **Artin–Schreier theorem** (1927) states: *Let Ω be an algebraically closed
field and k ⊊ Ω a proper subfield with [Ω : k] < ∞. Then [Ω : k] = 2, k is real-
closed, and Ω = k(i) with i² = −1.*

We verify the hypotheses:
- Ω = K is algebraically closed (given).
- k = F is a proper subfield of K (given).
- [K : F] = 2 < ∞ (Step 1).

Hence the Artin–Schreier theorem applies directly, yielding that **F is real-clo
sed** (and K = F(i)).

### Why the hypotheses matter

The subtle point of the problem is that one is *not* told K/F is algebraic or fi
nite. A naive reading might worry that the fixed field of an involution of an al
gebraically closed field could be "too large" (transcendental) and fail to be re
al-closed. Artin's fixed-field theorem removes this worry: any finite group of a
utomorphisms has a fixed field over which the original field is finite Galois of
 degree equal to the group order. Combined with K being algebraically closed, th
e classical Artin–Schreier situation is forced.

### Conclusion

For every algebraically closed field K of characteristic zero equipped with an o
rder-2 automorphism whose fixed field F is proper, F is real-closed.
