## Setting Up the Tiling

The triangular tiling is formed by three families of parallel lines (the edges o
f the triangles), all with perpendicular spacing $d = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ (the h

- **Family A**: horizontal lines $y = k\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
- **Family B**: lines at 60° ($x - \frac{y}{\sqrt{3}} = i$)
- **Family C**: lines at 120° ($x + \frac{y}{\sqrt{3}} = n$)

**Key property**: Every intersection of two lines from different families is als
o on a line from the third family — so every intersection point is a *triple poi
nt* (3 lines meeting). The cells of this arrangement are exactly the equilateral
 triangular tiles.

## Counting Formula

For a segment crossing this arrangement, let:
- $C$ = total number of lines crossed (from all three families)
- $V$ = number of triple points (vertices) the segment passes through

Each transversal line crossing (not at a vertex) adds 1 new tile. At a triple po
int, 3 lines are crossed simultaneously but only 1 new tile is entered (instead
of 3), losing 2. Therefore:

$$N = 1 + C - 2V$$
