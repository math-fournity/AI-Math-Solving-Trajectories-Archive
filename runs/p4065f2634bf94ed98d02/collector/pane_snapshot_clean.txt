\mathfrak{p}$ with $s a^n = 0$ in $R$.

Since $a$ is a non-zero divisor, $a^n$ is also a non-zero divisor (if $a^n b =
0$, then $a(a^{n-1}b)=0$ gives $a^{n-1}b = 0$ by the non-zero divisor property,
and iterating yields $b = 0$). Therefore $s a^n = 0$ implies $s = 0$. But $s
\notin \mathfrak{p}$ while $0 \in \mathfrak{p}$ (since $\mathfrak{p}$ is an
ideal), so $s \neq 0$ — contradiction.

Hence $a \notin \mathfrak{p}$ for every minimal prime $\mathfrak{p}$. $\square$

Step 2: The dimension inequality.

Consider any strict chain of prime ideals in $R/(a)$: $$\bar{\mathfrak{p}}_0
\subsetneq \bar{\mathfrak{p}}_1 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq \bar{\mathfrak{p}}
_n$$ of length $n$. By the correspondence theorem, this lifts to a chain of
primes in $R$: $$\mathfrak{p}_0 \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_1 \subsetneq \cdots
\subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_n$$ with $a \in \mathfrak{p}_0$.

By Step 1, $a$ is not in any minimal prime of $R$, so $\mathfrak{p}_0$ (which
contains $a$) is not a minimal prime. By definition of minimality, there exists
a prime $\mathfrak{q} \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_0$. This extends our chain to: $$
\mathfrak{q} \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_0 \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_1 \subsetneq
\cdots \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_n$$ which is a chain of length $n+1$ in $R$.
Since $\dim R = d$, we have $n + 1 \leq d$, hence $n \leq d - 1$.

Taking the supremum over all chains in $R/(a)$: $$\dim R/(a) \leq d - 1. \qquad
\blacksquare$$

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Remark. The classical Krull Principal Ideal Theorem bounds the height of minimal
primes over a principal ideal and requires Noetherianity. The argument above is
more elementary: it only uses that a non-zero divisor avoids all minimal primes
(which holds in any commutative ring) and that any non-minimal prime can be
extended downward. The "reduced" hypothesis ensures that non-zero divisors are
exactly the elements avoiding all minimal primes, but only the forward direction
(non-zero divisor $\Rightarrow$ avoids minimal primes) is needed, which holds
unconditionally.
