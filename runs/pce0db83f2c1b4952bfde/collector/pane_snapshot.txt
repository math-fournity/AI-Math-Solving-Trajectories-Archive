## Proof

Let $A = A(k)$ be the $k$-algebra generated by $x, y$ with the defining relation
 $yx - xy = 1$ (the first Weyl algebra). We assume $\operatorname{char} k = p >
0$.

**Normal form.** Using $yx = xy + 1$, every element of $A$ can be rewritten uniq
uely as a finite $k$-linear combination
$$\sum_{i,j \ge 0} a_{ij}\, x^i y^j, \qquad a_{ij} \in k,$$
so that $\{x^i y^j : i, j \ge 0\}$ is a $k$-basis of $A$.

**Commutator identities.** From $[y, x] = yx - xy = 1$, a straightforward induct
ion (the commutator with $y$ acts as a derivation) gives
$$[y, x^n] = n\, x^{n-1}, \qquad [x, y^n] = -n\, y^{n-1} \quad (n \ge 1).$$

**$x^p$ and $y^p$ are central.** Setting $n = p$ and using $\operatorname{char}
k = p$:
$$[y, x^p] = p\, x^{p-1} = 0, \qquad [x, y^p] = -p\, y^{p-1} = 0.$$
Since $x^p$ commutes with both $x$ (trivially) and $y$, it is central; likewise
$y^p$ is central. Hence the subalgebra $k[x^p, y^p]$ is contained in the center
$Z(A)$.

**Algebraic independence.** The monomials $(x^p)^a (y^p)^b = x^{pa} y^{pb}$ are
distinct elements of the basis $\{x^i y^j\}$, so $x^p$ and $y^p$ are algebraical
ly independent over $k$. Thus $k[x^p, y^p]$ is a polynomial ring in two variable
s.

**The center is exactly $k[x^p, y^p]$.** Let $z = \sum_{i,j} a_{ij} x^i y^j \in
Z(A)$. Then $[z, x] = 0$ and $[z, y] = 0$.

Computing $[z, x]$: since $[x^i, x] = 0$ and $[y^j, x] = -[x, y^j] = j\, y^{j-1}
$,
$$0 = [z, x] = \sum_{i,j} a_{ij}\, x^i\, [y^j, x] = \sum_{i,j\ge 1} a_{ij}\, j\,
 x^i y^{j-1}.$$
By the linear independence of the basis, $j\, a_{ij} = 0$ for all $i$ and all $j
 \ge 1$. In characteristic $p$, this forces $a_{ij} = 0$ whenever $p \nmid j$.

Computing $[z, y]$: since $[x^i, y] = -i\, x^{i-1}$ and $[y^j, y] = 0$,
$$0 = [z, y] = \sum_{i,j} a_{ij}\, [x^i, y]\, y^j = \sum_{i\ge 1, j} a_{ij}\, (-
i)\, x^{i-1} y^j.$$
Again by linear independence, $i\, a_{ij} = 0$ for all $j$ and all $i \ge 1$, fo
rcing $a_{ij} = 0$ whenever $p \nmid i$.

Combining both constraints, $a_{ij} = 0$ unless $p \mid i$ and $p \mid j$. Writi
ng $i = pa$, $j = pb$,
$$z = \sum_{a,b \ge 0} a_{pa,\,pb}\, (x^p)^a (y^p)^b \in k[x^p, y^p].$$

Therefore $Z(A) = k[x^p, y^p]$, the polynomial algebra in two independent centra
l variables $x^p$ and $y^p$.
