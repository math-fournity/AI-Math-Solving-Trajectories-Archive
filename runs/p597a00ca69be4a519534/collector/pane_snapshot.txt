## Solution

**The statement is false.** I provide a counterexample using a finite-rank (henc

### Counterexample

Let $H = \mathbb{C}^2$ and let $A: \mathbb{C}^2 \to \mathbb{C}^2$ be the operato
r represented (in the standard basis) by the matrix

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Since $H$ is finite-dimensional, $A$ is a bounded operator of finite rank, and t
herefore **compact**.

**Computation of $|A|$.** We have

$$A^* = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad A^*A = \begin{pmatr
ix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

The matrix $A^*A$ is already positive and diagonal, so

$$|A| = (A^*A)^{1/2} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

**Evaluating the quadratic forms.** For $x = (x_1, x_2) \in \mathbb{C}^2$:

$$\langle x, Ax\rangle = \overline{x_1}\, x_2, \qquad \langle x, |A|x\rangle = |
x_2|^2.$$

**Two problems immediately arise:**

**(1) The inequality is not even well-defined for general $x$.** The quantity $\
langle x, Ax\rangle = \overline{x_1}\,x_2$ is in general **complex** (e.g., $x =
 (1, i)$ gives $\langle x, Ax\rangle = i$), whereas $\langle x, |A|x\rangle = |x
_2|^2$ is always real. The relation "$\leq$" between a complex number and a real
 number is meaningless. The Loewner order $A \leq B$ is only defined when both $
A$ and $B$ are **self-adjoint**, and $A$ here is not self-adjoint.

**(2) Even restricting to $x$ for which $\langle x, Ax\rangle$ is real, the ineq
uality fails.** Take $x = (2, 1)$. Then

$$\langle x, Ax\rangle = \overline{2}\cdot 1 = 2 \in \mathbb{R}, \qquad \langle
x, |A|x\rangle = |1|^2 = 1.$$

Thus $\langle x, Ax\rangle = 2 > 1 = \langle x, |A|x\rangle$, **violating** the
claimed inequality.

### Conclusion

The proposed inequality $A \leq |A|$ does **not** hold for all compact operators
 $A$ on a complex Hilbert space. The operator $A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 &
0 \end{pmatrix}$ on $\mathbb{C}^2$ is a compact (finite-rank) operator for which
:

- the inequality is not even well-defined for general vectors (since $A$ is not
self-adjoint and $\langle x, Ax\rangle$ can be non-real), and
- even on vectors where $\langle x, Ax\rangle$ is real, the inequality is violat
ed (e.g., $x = (2,1)$).

**Remark.** If one additionally requires $A$ to be **self-adjoint**, then the in
equality *does* hold: by the spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators
, $A = \sum_n \lambda_n \langle \,\cdot\,, e_n\rangle e_n$ with $\lambda_n \in \
mathbb{R}$, and $|A| = \sum_n |\lambda_n|\,\langle\,\cdot\,,e_n\rangle e_n$, so
$\langle x, Ax\rangle = \sum_n \lambda_n |x_n|^2 \leq \sum_n |\lambda_n|\,|x_n|^
2 = \langle x, |A|x\rangle$ since $\lambda_n \leq |\lambda_n|$ for real $\lambda
_n$. But the problem as stated imposes no self-adjointness hypothesis, and the g
eneral claim is false.
