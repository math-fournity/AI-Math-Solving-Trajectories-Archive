## Definition of Γ-Convergence

Let $X$ be a topological space (typically a metric or first-countable space, so
that sequential characterizations suffice), and let $G_n : X \to \overline{\math
bb{R}} = \mathbb{R} \cup \{+\infty\}$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$, be a sequence of (ext
ended-valued) energy functionals. We say that $\{G_n\}$ **Γ-converges** to a lim
iting functional $G_\infty : X \to \overline{\mathbb{R}}$, written

$$G_n \xrightarrow{\Gamma} G_\infty,$$

if and only if the following two conditions hold at **every** point $x \in X$:

---

### (i) Lim inf inequality (lower bound)

For every $x \in X$ and for every sequence $\{x_n\} \subset X$ with $x_n \to x$,

$$G_\infty(x) \le \liminf_{n \to \infty} G_n(x_n).$$

This says: $G_\infty$ is a **lower bound** on the asymptotic energy of any seque
nce converging to $x$. No matter how one approaches $x$, the energies $G_n(x_n)$
 cannot drop below $G_\infty(x)$ in the limit inferior. Equivalently, $G_\infty$
 is the largest lower semicontinuous functional consistent with the asymptotic l
ower behavior of $\{G_n\}$.

---

### (ii) Lim sup inequality (recovery sequence / upper bound)

For every $x \in X$, there **exists** a sequence $\{x_n\} \subset X$ with $x_n \
to x$ such that

$$G_\infty(x) \ge \limsup_{n \to \infty} G_n(x_n).$$

This says: the lower bound in (i) is **sharp**. For every $x$ one can find a "re
covery sequence" converging to $x$ whose energies asymptotically attain (do not
exceed) $G_\infty(x)$. Combined with (i), for this recovery sequence we in fact
get

$$G_\infty(x) = \lim_{n \to \infty} G_n(x_n).$$

---

### Why these two properties uniquely characterize Γ-convergence

The two inequalities are complementary and together pin down $G_\infty$ uniquely
:

- **(i) alone** only gives a lower bound: it says $G_\infty$ cannot be larger th
an the asymptotic infimum of energies along sequences converging to $x$. Any fun
ctional smaller than the true Γ-limit would also satisfy (i), so (i) by itself d
oes not determine $G_\infty$.
- **(ii) alone** only gives an upper bound: it says $G_\infty$ cannot be smaller
 than the value attainable by some recovery sequence. Any functional larger than
 the true Γ-limit would also satisfy (ii), so (ii) by itself does not determine
$G_\infty$.
- **Together**, (i) forces $G_\infty$ to be **at most** the lim inf over all app
roaching sequences, while (ii) forces $G_\infty$ to be **at least** the lim sup
achieved by the best (recovery) sequence. Since the lim sup of a particular sequ
ence is always $\le$ the lim inf over all sequences, the two inequalities squeez
e $G_\infty$ to the unique value

$$G_\infty(x) = \min_{\{x_n : x_n \to x\}} \liminf_{n \to \infty} G_n(x_n) = \in
f_{\{x_n : x_n \to x\}} \liminf_{n \to \infty} G_n(x_n),$$

where the infimum is over all sequences converging to $x$, and (ii) guarantees t
his infimum is attained by a recovery sequence. Hence $G_\infty$ is uniquely det
ermined by the pair of conditions.

---

### Compact form

Equivalently, $\Gamma$-convergence $G_n \xrightarrow{\Gamma} G_\infty$ is the st
atement that for every $x \in X$:

$$G_\infty(x) = \inf_{\{x_n\} : x_n \to x} \liminf_{n \to \infty} G_n(x_n),$$

with the infimum **realized** by a recovery sequence (condition (ii)), and the i
nequality $G_\infty(x) \le \liminf_n G_n(x_n)$ holding for **all** converging se
quences (condition (i)).

---

### Key consequence (fundamental theorem of Γ-convergence)

When $X$ is a metric space and $\{G_n\}$ is **equicoercive**, Γ-convergence plus
 equicoercivity implies convergence of minimizers:

$$\min_X G_\infty = \lim_{n \to \infty} \inf_X G_n,$$

and every cluster point of a sequence of (near-)minimizers of $G_n$ is a minimiz
er of $G_\infty$. This is the central reason Γ-convergence is the natural notion
 of convergence for variational problems: it preserves minimizers in the limit.
