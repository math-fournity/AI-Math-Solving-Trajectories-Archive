## Proof

**Claim.** Let $P \in \mathbb{R}[X_1,\dots,X_n]$ be a non-constant polynomial. T
hen the Lebesgue measure of $P^{-1}(\{0\})$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ is zero.

We proceed by induction on $n$, the number of variables.

**Base case ($n = 1$).** A non-constant univariate polynomial $P \in \mathbb{R}[
X]$ of degree $d \geq 1$ has at most $d$ real roots. A finite set has Lebesgue m
easure zero. $\checkmark$

**Inductive step.** Assume the result holds for every non-constant polynomial in
 $n - 1$ variables, where $n \geq 2$. Let $P \in \mathbb{R}[X_1,\dots,X_n]$ be n
on-constant. Since $P$ is non-constant, it depends on at least one variable; rel
abeling variables if necessary, assume $P$ depends on $X_n$. Write

$$P(X_1,\dots,X_n) = \sum_{k=0}^{d} P_k(X_1,\dots,X_{n-1})\, X_n^k,$$

where $d \geq 1$, each $P_k \in \mathbb{R}[X_1,\dots,X_{n-1}]$, and $P_d$ is not
 the zero polynomial.

Since $P$ is continuous, $P^{-1}(\{0\})$ is closed and hence Lebesgue measurable
. We apply the **Tonelli/Fubini theorem** to the characteristic function $\mathb
f{1}_{P^{-1}(\{0\})}$ (which is non-negative and measurable):

$$\lambda_n\bigl(P^{-1}(\{0\})\bigr) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}} \lambda_1\!\bigl(
\{t \in \mathbb{R} : P(a, t) = 0\}\bigr)\, d\lambda_{n-1}(a),$$

where $a = (x_1,\dots,x_{n-1})$ and $\lambda_k$ denotes $k$-dimensional Lebesgue
 measure. For each $a$, define the **fiber**

$$F(a) := \{t \in \mathbb{R} : P(a, t) = 0\}.$$

We analyze the integrand $\lambda_1(F(a))$ by splitting into two cases based on
the leading coefficient $P_d$.

**Case A: $P_d$ is a non-zero constant.** Then for **every** $a \in \mathbb{R}^{
n-1}$, the univariate polynomial $t \mapsto P(a, t)$ has degree exactly $d \geq
1$, so $F(a)$ is a finite set (at most $d$ elements). Thus $\lambda_1(F(a)) = 0$
 for all $a$, and the integral is $0$.

**Case B: $P_d$ is non-constant.** By the induction hypothesis, the zero set

$$Z_d := \{a \in \mathbb{R}^{n-1} : P_d(a) = 0\}$$

has $\lambda_{n-1}$-measure zero. Now:

- For $a \notin Z_d$: $P_d(a) \neq 0$, so $t \mapsto P(a,t)$ is a univariate pol
ynomial of degree $d \geq 1$, hence $F(a)$ is finite and $\lambda_1(F(a)) = 0$.
- For $a \in Z_d$: the fiber $F(a) \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ may be large (possibly
all of $\mathbb{R}$), so $\lambda_1(F(a))$ may be infinite. However, $Z_d$ has $
\lambda_{n-1}$-measure zero.

Applying Tonelli's theorem:

$$\lambda_n\bigl(P^{-1}(\{0\})\bigr) = \int_{Z_d} \lambda_1(F(a))\, d\lambda_{n-
1}(a) \;+\; \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\setminus Z_d} 0\, d\lambda_{n-1}(a).$$

The second integral is $0$. The first integral vanishes because it is an integra
l over a set of measure zero: in Lebesgue integration, $\int_E f\, d\mu = 0$ whe
never $\mu(E) = 0$, **regardless** of the values of $f$ (even if $f = +\infty$ o
n $E$). Hence

$$\lambda_n\bigl(P^{-1}(\{0\})\bigr) = 0 + 0 = 0.$$

**Conclusion.** In both cases, $\lambda_n(P^{-1}(\{0\})) = 0$. This completes th
e inductive step.

By induction, for every $n \geq 1$ and every non-constant polynomial $P \in \mat
hbb{R}[X_1,\dots,X_n]$, the Lebesgue measure of $P^{-1}(\{0\})$ is zero. $\black
square$
