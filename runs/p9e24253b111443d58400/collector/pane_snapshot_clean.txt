# Degree of a Continuous Map $f: S^n \to S^n$

## Definition

Let $f: S^n \to S^n$ be a continuous map of the $n$-sphere to itself. Recall tha
t the $n$-th singular homology group of $S^n$ with integer coefficients is

$$H_n(S^n;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}.$$

The map $f$ induces a homomorphism on homology

$$f_* : H_n(S^n;\mathbb{Z}) \longrightarrow H_n(S^n;\mathbb{Z}).$$

Since $H_n(S^n;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, every endomorphism of this group i
s of the form $k \mapsto d \cdot k$ for a unique integer $d$. Concretely, if we
choose a generator $[S^n]$ of $H_n(S^n;\mathbb{Z})$ — represented by the fundame
ntal class of $S^n$ — then

$$f_*([S^n]) = d \cdot [S^n]$$

for a uniquely determined integer $d \in \mathbb{Z}$.

**Definition.** The integer $d$ satisfying $f_*([S^n]) = d\,[S^n]$ is called the
 **degree** of $f$, denoted $\deg(f) = d$.

The definition is independent of the choice of generator: replacing $[S^n]$ by $
-[S^n]$ changes the sign of both sides, leaving $d$ unchanged.

## Computation via the Induced Homomorphism

The degree is *defined* through the induced homomorphism $f_*$ in homology, so "
computation" amounts to determining the integer $d$ such that $f_*$ is multiplic
ation by $d$. Several equivalent characterizations and computational methods fol
low from this definition:

### 1. Direct homological computation

Fix the standard CW-structure on $S^n$ with one $0$-cell and one $n$-cell, so th
at the cellular chain complex has $\mathbb{Z}$ in degree $n$ and degree $0$, and
 is zero elsewhere. The cellular boundary maps are all zero. The cellular homolo
gy group $H_n^{\text{CW}}(S^n)$ is then $\mathbb{Z}$, generated by the $n$-cell
$e^n$.

The cellular approximation theorem lets us replace $f$ by a cellular map (homoto
pic to $f$), which maps the $n$-skeleton to the $n$-skeleton. The induced map on
 the cellular chain group $C_n(S^n) = \mathbb{Z}\langle e^n \rangle$ is multipli
cation by an integer $d$, and this $d$ is exactly $\deg(f)$. To compute it expli
citly one attaches an $n$-disk $D^n$ to $S^n$ along the attaching map and examin
es how $f$ restricted to the boundary of the cell wraps around the target cell;
the local degree formula (see below) gives $d$.

### 2. Local degree formula

Suppose $f: S^n \to S^n$ is a continuous map and $y \in S^n$ is a regular value
(in the smooth case) or, more generally, a point such that $f^{-1}(y) = \{x_1, \
dots, x_k\}$ is finite and $f$ is a local homeomorphism near each $x_i$. Then

$$\deg(f) = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \deg_{x_i}(f),$$

where each **local degree** $\deg_{x_i}(f) \in \{+1, -1\}$ is determined by whet
her $f$ preserves or reverses local orientation near $x_i$. This formula is deri
ved from the homological definition as follows:

- Excision identifies $H_n(S^n, S^n \setminus \{x_i\}) \cong H_n(U_i, U_i \setmi
nus \{x_i\}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ for a small neighborhood $U_i$ of $x_i$, and simi
larly $H_n(S^n, S^n \setminus \{y\}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
- The map $f$ induces a homomorphism between these relative groups, which is mul
tiplication by $\pm 1$ (since $f$ is a local homeomorphism).
- The long exact sequence of the pair $(S^n, S^n \setminus f^{-1}(y))$ and the n
aturality of $f_*$ show that $f_*([S^n]) = \sum_i \deg_{x_i}(f) \cdot [S^n]$.

Thus the global degree — the integer by which $f_*$ multiplies the fundamental c
lass — equals the sum of local degrees.

### 3. Homotopy invariance

A key consequence of the homological definition: if $f \simeq g$ (homotopic), th
en $f_* = g_*$ (homotopy invariance of singular homology), hence $\deg(f) = \deg
(g)$. This makes the degree a **homotopy invariant**, so to compute $\deg(f)$ on
e may replace $f$ by any homotopic map that is easier to analyze — e.g., a smoot
h map, a cellular map, or a simplicial map.

### 4. Standard examples

- **Identity map** $\operatorname{id}: S^n \to S^n$: $\operatorname{id}_* = \ope
ratorname{id}$ on $H_n$, so $\deg(\operatorname{id}) = 1$.
- **Antipodal map** $a(x) = -x$: This is a composition of $n+1$ reflections (one
 in each coordinate), each of degree $-1$, so $\deg(a) = (-1)^{n+1}$. Equivalent
ly, $a_*$ sends $[S^n] \mapsto (-1)^{n+1}[S^n]$.
- **Constant map** $c: S^n \to S^n$: $c_*$ factors through $H_n(\text{pt}) = 0$,
 so $\deg(c) = 0$.
- **Suspension / power maps**: The map $z \mapsto z^k$ on $S^1 \subset \mathbb{C
}$ has degree $k$, since it wraps the circle $k$ times around itself and $f_*$ m
ultiplies the generator of $H_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ by $k$.

### 5. Multiplicativity

From functoriality of the induced homomorphism, $(g \circ f)_* = g_* \circ f_*$,
 so

$$\deg(g \circ f) = \deg(g)\cdot \deg(f).$$

This is immediate: if $f_*([S^n]) = d_f [S^n]$ and $g_*([S^n]) = d_g [S^n]$, the
n $(g\circ f)_*([S^n]) = g_*(d_f [S^n]) = d_f d_g [S^n]$.

## Summary

The **degree** of a continuous map $f: S^n \to S^n$ is the unique integer $\deg(
f) \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that the induced homomorphism on the top homology group

$$f_*: H_n(S^n;\mathbb{Z}) \to H_n(S^n;\mathbb{Z})$$

acts as multiplication by $\deg(f)$, i.e., $f_*([S^n]) = \deg(f)\,[S^n]$, where
$[S^n]$ is the fundamental class generating $H_n(S^n;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z
}$. It is computed by determining this multiplier — either directly via cellular
/simplicial approximation, via the local degree formula at regular values, or by
 exploiting homotopy invariance to replace $f$ with a simpler homotopic map. The
 degree is a homotopy invariant, multiplicative under composition, and classifie
s maps $S^n \to S^n$ up to homotopy (by the Hopf theorem: two maps $S^n \to S^n$
 are homotopic if and only if they have the same degree).
