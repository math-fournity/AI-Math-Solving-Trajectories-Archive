**Proof.**

Let $L$ be an NP-complete problem, and suppose $L$ can be solved in polynomial t
ime, i.e., $L \in P$. We show that $P = NP$.

**Step 1: $P \subseteq NP$.**
Every problem in $P$ is trivially in $NP$, since a deterministic polynomial-time
 algorithm is a special case of a nondeterministic polynomial-time algorithm (th
e nondeterministic machine simply makes no nondeterministic choices). Hence $P \
subseteq NP$.

**Step 2: $NP \subseteq P$.**
Let $A$ be any language in $NP$. Since $L$ is NP-complete, by definition $L \in
NP$ and every language in $NP$ is polynomial-time (many-one) reducible to $L$. T

ery input $x$:
$$x \in A \iff f(x) \in L.$$

We construct a polynomial-time algorithm for $A$:
1. On input $x$, compute $f(x)$. This takes polynomial time in $|x|$.
2. Test whether $f(x) \in L$. Since $L \in P$, this takes polynomial time in $|f
(x)|$.

Since $f$ is computable in polynomial time, $|f(x)|$ is bounded by a polynomial
in $|x|$. Therefore testing $f(x) \in L$ also takes time polynomial in $|x|$. Th
e composition of two polynomial-time procedures is itself polynomial-time. Hence
 $A \in P$.

Since $A$ was arbitrary, $NP \subseteq P$.

**Conclusion.** Combining Steps 1 and 2, $P = NP$. Therefore the statement is **
TRUE**: if any NP-complete problem can be solved in polynomial time, then $P = N
