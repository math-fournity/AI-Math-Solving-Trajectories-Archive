## Proof

**The statement is true.** We prove that for the Gram matrix $A$ of a basis with
 $\langle e_i, e_j\rangle \le 0$ for $i\ne j$, the implication $Ax\ge 0 \Rightar
row x\ge 0$ holds.

### Step 1. $A$ is symmetric positive definite.

Since $e_1,\dots,e_n$ is a basis, the vectors are linearly independent. For any
$x\ne 0$,
$$x^\top A x = \sum_{i,j} x_i x_j \langle e_i,e_j\rangle = \Big\langle \sum_i x_
i e_i,\; \sum_j x_j e_j\Big\rangle = \Big\|\sum_i x_i e_i\Big\|^2 > 0,$$
by linear independence. Hence $A$ is positive definite, so all eigenvalues $\lam
bda_1,\dots,\lambda_n$ are strictly positive. Let $\lambda_{\max}$ and $\lambda_
{\min}>0$ denote the largest and smallest eigenvalues.

### Step 2. $A^{-1}\ge 0$ entrywise.

Define $B = \lambda_{\max} I - A$. We check that $B\ge 0$ entrywise:
- **Off-diagonal** ($i\ne j$): $b_{ij} = -a_{ij} = -\langle e_i,e_j\rangle \ge 0
$ by hypothesis.
- **Diagonal** ($i=j$): $b_{ii} = \lambda_{\max} - a_{ii}$. Since $a_{ii} = \lan
gle e_i,e_i\rangle > 0$ and $a_{ii}\le \lambda_{\max}$ (the Rayleigh quotient $a
_{ii}=e_i^\top A e_i / e_i^\top e_i \le \lambda_{\max}$), we get $b_{ii}\ge 0$.

So $B$ is entrywise non-negative. The eigenvalues of $B$ are $\lambda_{\max}-\la
mbda_k$, so its spectral radius is
$$\rho(B) = \lambda_{\max} - \lambda_{\min} < \lambda_{\max},$$
since $\lambda_{\min}>0$.

Now write $A = \lambda_{\max} I - B = \lambda_{\max}\big(I - \tfrac{1}{\lambda_{
\max}}B\big)$, so
$$A^{-1} = \frac{1}{\lambda_{\max}}\Big(I - \tfrac{1}{\lambda_{\max}}B\Big)^{-1}
 = \frac{1}{\lambda_{\max}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big(\tfrac{1}{\lambda_{\max}}B\Bi
g)^{k}.$$

The Neumann series converges because $\rho\!\big(\tfrac{1}{\lambda_{\max}}B\big)
 = \rho(B)/\lambda_{\max} < 1$. Since $B\ge 0$ entrywise, every power $B^k\ge 0$
 entrywise, and therefore each partial sum is entrywise non-negative. We conclud
e
$$A^{-1}\ge 0 \quad\text{(entrywise)}.$$

### Step 3. Conclusion.

Suppose $Ax\ge 0$. Then
$$x = A^{-1}(Ax).$$
The matrix $A^{-1}$ is entrywise non-negative (Step 2) and the vector $Ax$ is en
trywise non-negative (hypothesis). The product of an entrywise non-negative matr
ix with an entrywise non-negative vector is entrywise non-negative, hence
$$x\ge 0.$$

Therefore the implication $Ax\ge 0 \Rightarrow x\ge 0$ is **true**. $\blacksquar
e$
