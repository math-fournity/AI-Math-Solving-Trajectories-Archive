The answer is **1/4**. This is Cauchy's surface area formula. Here is the proof.

## Proof

Let $K \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ be a convex body with surface area $S$. For a unit
vector $u \in S^2$, let $A(u)$ denote the area of the orthogonal projection of $
K$ onto a plane with normal $u$. We average $A(u)$ over $u$ uniformly distribute
d on the unit sphere $S^2$.

### Step 1. Polyhedral case

First assume $K$ is a convex polyhedron with faces $F_1,\dots,F_n$, face areas $
a_1,\dots,a_n$, and outward unit normals $n_1,\dots,n_n$.

**Claim:** $A(u) = \tfrac12 \sum_{i=1}^n a_i\,|n_i\cdot u|$.

*Justification.* The orthogonal projection of $K$ onto a plane $\Pi$ with normal
 $u$ is a convex polygon. The faces with $n_i\cdot u \ge 0$ (the "front" faces r
elative to direction $u$) project onto $\Pi$, and these projections tile the pro
jected region exactly once (they meet only along edges, since $K$ is convex). Th
e area of the projection of face $F_i$ is $a_i\,(n_i\cdot u)$ when $n_i\cdot u\g
e 0$. Hence
$$A(u) = \sum_{i:\, n_i\cdot u \ge 0} a_i\,(n_i\cdot u).$$
Since $K$ is a closed surface, its vector area vanishes: $\sum_i a_i n_i = 0$, s
o
$$\sum_{i:\, n_i\cdot u \ge 0} a_i\,(n_i\cdot u) = -\sum_{i:\, n_i\cdot u < 0} a
_i\,(n_i\cdot u) = \tfrac12\sum_i a_i\,|n_i\cdot u|.$$
This proves the claim.

### Step 2. Averaging

Take $u$ uniform on $S^2$. By linearity and the claim,
$$\mathbb{E}[A(u)] = \tfrac12 \sum_{i=1}^n a_i\,\mathbb{E}\big[|n_i\cdot u|\big]
.$$

For $u$ uniform on $S^2$, the coordinate $u_3$ is uniformly distributed on $[-1,
1]$ (with density $\tfrac12$), so
$$\mathbb{E}\big[|n_i\cdot u|\big] = \mathbb{E}[|u_3|] = 2\cdot\tfrac12\int_0^1
t\,dt = \tfrac12.$$

Therefore
$$\mathbb{E}[A(u)] = \tfrac12 \sum_i a_i \cdot \tfrac12 = \tfrac14 \sum_i a_i =
\frac{S}{4}.$$

### Step 3. General convex bodies

Every convex body is the Hausdorff limit of a sequence of convex polyhedra $\{K_
m\}$. Both the surface area and the projection area $A(u)$ (for each fixed $u$)
are continuous under such convergence, and the averages are uniformly bounded. P
assing the identity $\mathbb{E}[A_{K_m}(u)] = S(K_m)/4$ to the limit gives
$$\mathbb{E}[A(u)] = \frac{S}{4}$$
for the original convex body $K$.

### Conclusion

The average area of the orthogonal projection of a convex body onto a plane, tak
en over all plane orientations uniformly, equals **one quarter** of the surface
area of the body. Expressed as a fraction of the surface area, the answer is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{4}}.$$
