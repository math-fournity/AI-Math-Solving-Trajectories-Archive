## Proof

The answer is **no**. Convergence in $\tau$ does not necessarily imply convergen
ce in $\tau'$, even when both topologies are Hausdorff.

**Counterexample.** Let $X = \mathbb{R}$. Define
- $\tau$ = the standard (Euclidean) topology on $\mathbb{R}$,
- $\tau'$ = the discrete topology on $\mathbb{R}$ (every subset is open).

Both $(\mathbb{R}, \tau)$ and $(\mathbb{R}, \tau')$ are Hausdorff:
- $(\mathbb{R}, \tau)$ is Hausdorff since distinct points can be separated by di
sjoint open intervals.
- $(\mathbb{R}, \tau')$ is Hausdorff since for any $a \neq b$, the singletons $\
{a\}$ and $\{b\}$ are disjoint open sets separating them.

Now consider the sequence $x_n = \tfrac{1}{n}$ and the point $x = 0$.

**Convergence in $\tau$:** For any $\tau$-open neighborhood $U$ of $0$, there ex
ists $\varepsilon > 0$ with $(-\varepsilon, \varepsilon) \subseteq U$. Choosing
$N > 1/\varepsilon$, we have $|x_n - 0| = \tfrac{1}{n} < \varepsilon$ for all $n
 \geq N$, so $x_n \in U$ for all $n \geq N$. Hence $x_n \to 0$ in $\tau$.

**Non-convergence in $\tau'$:** The singleton $\{0\}$ is open in the discrete to
pology $\tau'$. For $x_n \to 0$ in $\tau'$, we would need $x_n \in \{0\}$ for al
l sufficiently large $n$, i.e., $x_n = 0$ eventually. But $x_n = \tfrac{1}{n} \n
eq 0$ for every $n \geq 1$. Therefore $x_n \not\to 0$ in $\tau'$.

Thus we have two Hausdorff topologies on the same underlying set, with a sequenc
e converging to $x$ in $\tau$ but not in $\tau'$. The implication fails in gener
al. $\blacksquare$
