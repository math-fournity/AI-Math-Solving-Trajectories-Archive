## Proof

**Claim:** The infinite product space $X = \prod_{i=1}^{\infty} \mathbb{N}_n$ is
 compact (in the product topology).

**Setup.** Each factor $\mathbb{N}_n$ is a finite set (of cardinality $n$) equip
ped with the discrete topology. A finite discrete space is compact: any open cov
er is finite (since the space itself is a finite union of singleton open sets),
so a finite subcover exists immediately. Thus every factor $\mathbb{N}_n$ is a c
ompact topological space.

**Key theorem (Tychonoff).** Let $\{X_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in A}$ be any family (fi
nite or infinite, countable or uncountable) of compact topological spaces. Then
the product $\prod_{\alpha \in A} X_\alpha$, endowed with the product topology,
is compact.

**Application.** Here the index set is $A = \mathbb{N} = \{1, 2, 3, \ldots\}$, a
nd each factor $X_i = \mathbb{N}_n$ is compact (finite discrete). All hypotheses
 of Tychonoff's theorem are satisfied, so

$$X = \prod_{i=1}^{\infty} \mathbb{N}_n$$

is compact in the product topology.

**Why the finiteness of each factor matters but its infinitude does not.** Compa
ctness of the product depends only on the compactness of *each* factor, not on t
he number of factors. Each $\mathbb{N}_n$ being finite guarantees it is compact
(finite discrete ⇒ compact). The fact that there are infinitely many factors is
exactly the situation Tychonoff's theorem is designed to handle — it asserts com
pactness of *arbitrary* products of compact spaces, including countably infinite
 ones.

**Remark on the topology.** This conclusion holds for the **product topology** (
the standard topology on a product space, generated by cylinder sets that restri
ct only finitely many coordinates). If one instead used the **box topology** (wh
ere basic opens restrict *every* coordinate), the product would generally fail t
o be compact — for instance, the box product $\prod_{i=1}^\infty \{0,1\}$ is dis
crete and infinite, hence not compact. But the standard meaning of "product spac
e" is the product topology, under which Tychonoff's theorem applies.

**Conclusion.** The infinite product $\prod_{i=1}^{\infty} \mathbb{N}_n$, with e
ach factor finite discrete (hence compact) and equipped with the product topolog
y, is compact by Tychonoff's theorem.
