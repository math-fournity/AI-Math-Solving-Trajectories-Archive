## Proof: Homology of a sphere with $n$ handles via Mayer–Vietoris

Let $\Sigma_n$ denote the closed orientable surface of genus $n$ (a sphere with
$n$ handles). We prove by induction on $n$ that

$$H_k(\Sigma_n) \cong \begin{cases} \mathbb{Z} & k=0,\\ \mathbb{Z}^{\,2n} & k=1,
\\ \mathbb{Z} & k=2,\\ 0 & k\ge 3. \end{cases}$$

### Base case

$\Sigma_0 = S^2$, with $H_0\cong\mathbb{Z}$, $H_1=0=\mathbb{Z}^{0}$, $H_2\cong\m
athbb{Z}$, and $H_k=0$ for $k\ge 3$. The formula holds.

### Inductive setup: connected-sum decomposition

For $n\ge 1$, write $\Sigma_n = \Sigma_{n-1}\# T^2$ (connected sum with a torus)
. Concretely, remove the interior of an embedded closed disk $D_M\subset \Sigma_
{n-1}$ and $D_T\subset T^2$, and glue the resulting boundary circles. Set

$$A = \Sigma_{n-1}\setminus \operatorname{int}(D_M), \qquad B = T^2\setminus \op
eratorname{int}(D_T), \qquad A\cap B = S^1.$$

Then $\Sigma_n = A\cup B$, an open cover suitable for Mayer–Vietoris.

### Homology of the pieces

The standard CW structure of $\Sigma_g$ has one $0$-cell, $2g$ one-cells $a_1,b_
1,\dots,a_g,b_g$, and one $2$-cell attached along the word $\prod_{i=1}^g [a_i,b
_i]$. Removing an open disk in the interior of the $2$-cell leaves a space that
deformation-retracts onto the $1$-skeleton, i.e. a wedge of $2g$ circles. Hence

$$H_*(A):\quad H_0\cong\mathbb{Z},\; H_1\cong\mathbb{Z}^{\,2(n-1)},\; H_k=0\;(k\
ge 2),$$

$$H_*(B):\quad H_0\cong\mathbb{Z},\; H_1\cong\mathbb{Z}^{\,2},\; H_k=0\;(k\ge 2)
,$$

$$H_*(S^1):\quad H_0\cong\mathbb{Z},\; H_1\cong\mathbb{Z},\; H_k=0\;(k\ge 2).$$

### The Mayer–Vietoris sequence

The long exact sequence (reduced/unreduced, with the usual sign convention) read
s, for each $k$,

$$\cdots \to H_k(S^1)\xrightarrow{\;\partial\;} H_k(A)\oplus H_k(B)\xrightarrow{
\;\phi\;} H_k(\Sigma_n)\xrightarrow{\;\delta\;} H_{k-1}(S^1)\to\cdots$$

**Degrees $k\ge 3$.** Both $H_k(A)$, $H_k(B)$, and $H_{k-1}(S^1)$ vanish, so $H_
k(\Sigma_n)=0$.

**Degree $k=2$.** Since $H_2(A)=H_2(B)=0$, the relevant segment is

$$0 \to H_2(\Sigma_n)\xrightarrow{\;\delta\;} H_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb{Z}\xrightarro
w{\;\partial\;} H_1(A)\oplus H_1(B)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{\,2n}.$$

We claim $\partial = 0$. The map $\partial$ is $(i_{A*},-i_{B*})$, where $i_A,i_
B$ are the inclusions of the boundary circle into $A$ and $B$. Under the deforma
tion retractions above, the boundary circle is exactly the attaching curve of th
e $2$-cell, which represents $\prod_i[a_i,b_i]$ in $\pi_1$ and hence $0$ in $H_1
$ (abelianization kills every commutator). Thus both $i_{A*}$ and $i_{B*}$ are t
he zero map, so $\partial=0$.

Consequently $\ker\partial=\mathbb{Z}=\operatorname{im}\delta$, so $\delta$ is *
*surjective**; and $\ker\delta=\operatorname{im}(0\to H_2(\Sigma_n))=0$, so $\de
lta$ is **injective**. Therefore

$$H_2(\Sigma_n)\cong\mathbb{Z}.$$

**Degree $k=1$.** The segment is

$$H_1(S^1)\xrightarrow{\;\partial=0\;} H_1(A)\oplus H_1(B)\xrightarrow{\;\phi\;}
 H_1(\Sigma_n)\xrightarrow{\;\delta\;} H_0(S^1)\xrightarrow{\;\psi\;} H_0(A)\opl
us H_0(B).$$

Since $\partial=0$, we have $\ker\phi=\operatorname{im}\partial=0$, so $\phi$ is
 injective. The map $\psi(1)=(1,-1)$ is injective, so $\ker\psi=0=\operatorname{
im}\,\delta$; hence $\delta:H_1(\Sigma_n)\to H_0(S^1)$ is the zero map, which ma
kes $\phi$ **surjective**. Being both injective and surjective,

$$H_1(\Sigma_n)\cong H_1(A)\oplus H_1(B)\cong \mathbb{Z}^{\,2(n-1)}\oplus\mathbb
{Z}^{\,2}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{\,2n}.$$

**Degree $k=0$.** Exactness at $H_0(A)\oplus H_0(B)\cong\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{
Z}$ gives

$$H_0(\Sigma_n)\cong (\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z})/\langle(1,-1)\rangle\cong\math
bb{Z},$$

confirming $\Sigma_n$ is path-connected.

### Conclusion

By induction the homology groups of a sphere with $n$ handles are

$$\boxed{H_k(\Sigma_n)\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z} & k=0,\\[2pt]\mathbb{Z}^{\,2n
} & k=1,\\[2pt]\mathbb{Z} & k=2,\\[2pt]0 & k\ge 3.\end{cases}}$$

The Mayer–Vietoris argument closes the induction: the only nontrivial input is t
hat the boundary circle of the removed disk bounds the $2$-cell whose attaching
word is a product of commutators, hence is null-homologous — forcing the connect
ing map $\partial$ to vanish and splitting the sequence cleanly.
