## Solution

**Answer:** The only positive integer relatively prime to all terms of the seque
nce is $\boxed{1}$.

### Proof

Define $a_n = 2^n + 3^n + 6^n - 1$ for $n \geq 1$.

**Key observation.** Since $6 = 2 \cdot 3$, we have $6^n = 2^n \cdot 3^n$. Setti
ng $x = 2^n$, $y = 3^n$:

$$a_n = x + y + xy - 1 = (x+1)(y+1) - 2.$$

Thus

$$a_n \equiv 0 \pmod{p} \iff (2^n+1)(3^n+1) \equiv 2 \pmod{p}.$$

**Claim:** Every prime $p$ divides at least one term $a_n$.

**Case $p = 2$:** $a_1 = 2 + 3 + 6 - 1 = 10$, which is even. ✓

**Case $p = 3$:** $a_2 = 4 + 9 + 36 - 1 = 48$, which is divisible by $3$. ✓

**Case $p = 5$:** $a_1 = 10$, which is divisible by $5$. ✓

**Case $p \geq 7$:** Take $n = p - 2 \geq 5 \geq 1$. Since $\gcd(2,p) = \gcd(3,p
) = 1$, Fermat's little theorem gives $2^{p-1} \equiv 1$ and $3^{p-1} \equiv 1 \
pmod{p}$, hence

$$2^{p-2} \equiv 2^{-1} \pmod{p}, \qquad 3^{p-2} \equiv 3^{-1} \pmod{p}.$$

Therefore

$$(2^{p-2}+1)(3^{p-2}+1) \equiv \left(\tfrac{1}{2}+1\right)\left(\tfrac{1}{3}+1\
right) = \tfrac{3}{2}\cdot\tfrac{4}{3} = 2 \pmod{p}.$$

By the key observation, $a_{p-2} \equiv 0 \pmod{p}$. ✓

**Conclusion.** Every prime $p$ divides some term of the sequence. Let $m$ be a
positive integer relatively prime to every $a_n$. If $m > 1$, then $m$ has some
prime divisor $p$, and since $p \mid a_n$ for some $n$, we would have $\gcd(m, a
_n) \geq p > 1$, a contradiction. Hence $m = 1$.

Conversely, $m = 1$ is trivially relatively prime to every term. ∎
