## Proof

Let $M$ be a topological manifold (Hausdorff, second-countable, locally Euclidea
n of dimension $n$). Recall the relevant definitions.

**Charts and atlases.** A *chart* on $M$ is a pair $(U,\varphi)$ where $U\subset
eq M$ is open and $\varphi:U\to\varphi(U)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n$ is a homeomorphi
sm onto an open subset. An *atlas* $\mathcal{A}=\{(U_\alpha,\varphi_\alpha)\}_{\
alpha\in A}$ is a collection of charts whose domains cover $M$.

**Transition maps.** For two charts $(U_\alpha,\varphi_\alpha),(U_\beta,\varphi_
\beta)$ with $U_\alpha\cap U_\beta\neq\varnothing$, the *transition map* (overla
p map) is
$$\tau_{\beta\alpha}:=\varphi_\beta\circ\varphi_\alpha^{-1}:\varphi_\alpha(U_\al
pha\cap U_\beta)\longrightarrow\varphi_\beta(U_\alpha\cap U_\beta),$$
a homeomorphism between open subsets of $\mathbb{R}^n$.

**$C^r$ atlas ($r\in\{k,\infty,\omega\}$).** The atlas $\mathcal{A}$ is of class
 $C^r$ if every transition map $\tau_{\beta\alpha}$ is a $C^r$ diffeomorphism (i
.e. $C^r$ with $C^r$ inverse). A *$C^r$ manifold* is a topological manifold toge
ther with a maximal $C^r$ atlas.

---

### The statement to prove

If $\mathcal{A}$ is a $C^r$ atlas on $M$, then the maximal atlas $\overline{\mat
hcal{A}}$ generated by $\mathcal{A}$ is itself a $C^r$ atlas: every pair of over
lapping charts in $\overline{\mathcal{A}}$ has a $C^r$ transition map. This is w
hat it means for the $C^r$ structure to be *well-defined* and for a $C^r$ manifo
ld to "have an atlas whose overlapping [maps] are of class $C^r$."

---

### Construction of the maximal atlas

Define
$$\overline{\mathcal{A}}:=\bigl\{(V,\psi)\text{ chart on }M : (V,\psi)\text{ is
}C^r\text{-compatible with every }(U_\alpha,\varphi_\alpha)\in\mathcal{A}\bigr\}
,$$
where "$C^r$-compatible" means: for every $\alpha$ with $V\cap U_\alpha\neq\varn
othing$, the map
$$\varphi_\alpha\circ\psi^{-1}:\psi(V\cap U_\alpha)\to\varphi_\alpha(V\cap U_\al
pha)$$
is a $C^r$ diffeomorphism. Clearly $\mathcal{A}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{A}}$
(each chart is compatible with the $C^r$ atlas it belongs to, since the transiti
on maps $\tau_{\beta\alpha}$ are $C^r$ by hypothesis and inverses are $C^r$ as w
ell). So $\overline{\mathcal{A}}$ covers $M$.

---

### Key lemma: pairwise compatibility in $\overline{\mathcal{A}}$

**Claim.** Any two charts $(V,\psi),(W,\chi)\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}$ are $C^r$
-compatible, i.e.
$$\chi\circ\psi^{-1}:\psi(V\cap W)\longrightarrow\chi(V\cap W)$$
is a $C^r$ diffeomorphism.

**Proof.** It suffices to show $\chi\circ\psi^{-1}$ is $C^r$ (the same argument
with $\psi,\chi$ swapped gives that the inverse $\psi\circ\chi^{-1}$ is $C^r$).

Let $p\in V\cap W$. Since $\mathcal{A}$ covers $M$, choose $\alpha$ with $p\in U
_\alpha$. On the open neighborhood $\psi(V\cap W\cap U_\alpha)$ of $\psi(p)$ we
may factor
$$\chi\circ\psi^{-1}=\underbrace{\bigl(\chi\circ\varphi_\alpha^{-1}\bigr)}_{(\st
ar)}\circ\underbrace{\bigl(\varphi_\alpha\circ\psi^{-1}\bigr)}_{(\star\star)}.$$

- $(\star\star)=\varphi_\alpha\circ\psi^{-1}$ is $C^r$ because $(V,\psi)\in\over
line{\mathcal{A}}$, hence $C^r$-compatible with $(U_\alpha,\varphi_\alpha)$.
- $(\star)=\chi\circ\varphi_\alpha^{-1}$ is $C^r$ because $(W,\chi)\in\overline{
\mathcal{A}}$, hence $C^r$-compatible with $(U_\alpha,\varphi_\alpha)$.

By the **chain rule** (valid for $C^k$, $C^\infty$, and $C^\omega$ — see below),
 the composition of two $C^r$ maps is $C^r$. Therefore $\chi\circ\psi^{-1}$ is $
C^r$ on $\psi(V\cap W\cap U_\alpha)$.

Now vary $p$ over all of $V\cap W$. The corresponding open sets $\{\psi(V\cap W\
cap U_\alpha)\}_{\alpha}$ form an open cover of $\psi(V\cap W)$. Since $\chi\cir
c\psi^{-1}$ is $C^r$ on each member of an open cover, it is $C^r$ on all of $\ps
i(V\cap W)$. $\square$

---

### Why the chain rule applies in all three classes

- **$C^k$ ($1\le k<\infty$):** the classical chain rule gives $D(g\circ f)=Dg\cd
ot Df$; if $f,g$ are $C^k$ then $Df,Dg$ are $C^{k-1}$, so $D(g\circ f)$ is $C^{k
-1}$, hence $g\circ f$ is $C^k$.
- **$C^\infty$:** smooth maps are closed under composition, since the chain rule
 holds at every order of differentiation.
- **$C^\omega$ (real-analytic):** if $f$ is analytic near $x_0$ and $g$ is analy
tic near $f(x_0)$, then $g\circ f$ admits a convergent power-series expansion ne
ar $x_0$ (substitution of the power series of $f$ into that of $g$ yields a conv
ergent series). Hence the composition is analytic.

In every case the inverse of a $C^r$ diffeomorphism is $C^r$ (inverse function t
heorem, which holds in all three classes), so the transition maps are genuine $C
^r$ diffeomorphisms.

---

### Conclusion

Starting from any $C^r$ atlas $\mathcal{A}$, the maximal atlas $\overline{\mathc
al{A}}$ it generates is a $C^r$ atlas: by the lemma, every pair of overlapping c
harts in $\overline{\mathcal{A}}$ has a $C^r$ transition map. Thus a $C^k$ (resp
. $C^\infty$, $C^\omega$) manifold — defined as a manifold carrying a maximal $C
^r$ atlas — genuinely possesses an atlas whose overlap maps are all of class $C^
k$ (resp. $C^\infty$, $C^\omega$). The argument relies only on (i) the covering
property of the original atlas and (ii) closure of the class $C^r$ under composi
tion and inversion, which is exactly what the chain rule and inverse function th
eorem provide in each of the three regularity classes.
