Looking at this problem, I need to find all two-digit numbers $\overline{ab}$ su
ch that $3^{x+y} = 3^x + 3^y + \overline{ab}$ has a solution in natural numbers.

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $u = 3^x$ and $v = 3^y$. Then $3^{x+y} = uv$, and the equation be
comes:
$$uv = u + v + \overline{ab}$$

Rearranging:
$$\overline{ab} = uv - u - v = (u-1)(v-1) - 1$$

So $\overline{ab} + 1 = (u-1)(v-1)$, where $u = 3^x$, $v = 3^y$ with $x, y \in \
mathbb{N}$ (positive integers).

**Possible values.** With $x, y \geq 1$, the values of $u-1$ and $v-1$ are:
$$\{2,\ 8,\ 26,\ 80,\ 242,\ \ldots\} \quad (\text{from } 3^1-1,\ 3^2-1,\ 3^3-1,\
 3^4-1,\ 3^5-1,\ldots)$$

**Range constraint.** Since $\overline{ab}$ is a two-digit number, $10 \leq \ove
rline{ab} \leq 99$, so:
$$11 \leq (u-1)(v-1) \leq 100$$

**Enumerating products.** I compute all products of pairs from $\{2, 8, 26, 80,
242, \ldots\}$ that fall in $[11, 100]$:

| Product | $\overline{ab}$ | Factorization | $(x,y)$ |
|---------|-----------------|---------------|---------|
| $2 \times 8 = 16$ | $15$ | $u=3, v=9$ | $(1,2)$ |
| $2 \times 26 = 52$ | $51$ | $u=3, v=27$ | $(1,3)$ |
| $8 \times 8 = 64$ | $63$ | $u=9, v=9$ | $(2,2)$ |

All other products fall outside the range:
- $2 \times 2 = 4$ → too small ($\overline{ab} = 3$, one digit)
- $2 \times 80 = 160$ → too large
- $8 \times 26 = 208$ → too large
- $26 \times 26 = 676$ → too large
- All larger products exceed 100.

**Verification.**
- $\overline{ab} = 15$: $3^{1+2} = 27$ and $3^1 + 3^2 + 15 = 3 + 9 + 15 = 27$ ✓
- $\overline{ab} = 51$: $3^{1+3} = 81$ and $3^1 + 3^3 + 51 = 3 + 27 + 51 = 81$ ✓
- $\overline{ab} = 63$: $3^{2+2} = 81$ and $3^2 + 3^2 + 63 = 9 + 9 + 63 = 81$ ✓

**Sum.** The two-digit numbers are $15$, $51$, and $63$:
$$15 + 51 + 63 = \boxed{129}$$
