## Proof

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a function $f:\mathbb{R}^+\to\math
bb{R}^+$ satisfying

$$
(f(x))^2 \geq f(x+y)\bigl(f(x)+y\bigr) \qquad \text{for all } x,y\in\mathbb{R}^+
. \tag{$\star$}
$$

**Step 1: A halving relation.** Substitute $y = f(x)>0$ into $(\star)$:

$$
f(x)^2 \geq f\bigl(x+f(x)\bigr)\bigl(f(x)+f(x)\bigr) = 2\,f(x)\,f\bigl(x+f(x)\bi
gr).
$$

Since $f(x)>0$, we may divide by $2f(x)$ to obtain

$$
f\bigl(x+f(x)\bigr) \leq \tfrac{1}{2}\,f(x) \qquad \text{for every } x>0. \tag{1
}
$$

**Step 2: An iterative sequence.** Fix any $x_0>0$ and define

$$
x_{n+1} = x_n + f(x_n), \qquad n=0,1,2,\dots
$$

Each $x_n>0$, and by (1),

$$
f(x_{n+1}) \leq \tfrac{1}{2}f(x_n) \leq \cdots \leq \frac{f(x_0)}{2^{n+1}}. \tag
{2}
$$

In particular $f(x_n)\to 0$. The sequence $(x_n)$ is strictly increasing (since
$f(x_n)>0$), and

$$
x_{n+1}-x_n = f(x_n) \leq \frac{f(x_0)}{2^{n+1}},
$$

so $(x_n)$ is bounded above by $x_0 + 2f(x_0)$ and converges to a finite limit

$$
L = \lim_{n\to\infty} x_n \leq x_0 + 2f(x_0), \qquad L>0.
$$

Since the sequence is strictly increasing, $x_n < L$ for every $n$.

**Step 3: Relating $L-x_n$ to $f(x_n)$.** We have

$$
L - x_n = \sum_{k=n}^{\infty}(x_{k+1}-x_k) = \sum_{k=n}^{\infty} f(x_k) \leq \su
m_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{f(x_0)}{2^{k+1}} = \frac{f(x_0)}{2^n} \leq 2\,f(x_n)\cdot\
frac{f(x_0)/2^n}{2\,f(x_n)}.
$$

More directly, using $f(x_k)\leq f(x_n)/2^{k-n}$ (from (2) applied iteratively s
tarting at $n$):

$$
L - x_n = \sum_{k=n}^{\infty} f(x_k) \leq f(x_n)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^j}
 = 2\,f(x_n). \tag{3}
$$

**Step 4: The contradiction.** Apply $(\star)$ with $x = x_n$ and $y = L - x_n >
 0$ (so that $x+y = L$):

$$
f(x_n)^2 \geq f(L)\bigl(f(x_n) + (L-x_n)\bigr).
$$

Since $f(x_n) + (L-x_n) > f(x_n) > 0$, we obtain

$$
f(L) \leq \frac{f(x_n)^2}{f(x_n)+(L-x_n)} < \frac{f(x_n)^2}{f(x_n)} = f(x_n).
$$

Thus $0 < f(L) < f(x_n)$ for every $n$. But by (2), $f(x_n)\to 0$, so

$$
f(L) \leq \lim_{n\to\infty} f(x_n) = 0,
$$

which contradicts $f(L)\in\mathbb{R}^+$ (i.e., $f(L)>0$).

Therefore no such function $f:\mathbb{R}^+\to\mathbb{R}^+$ exists. $\blacksquare
$
