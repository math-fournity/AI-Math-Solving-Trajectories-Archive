## Proof

**Yes, such a local ring exists.** Let

$$O = \mathbb{R}[x]_{(x)},$$

the localization of the polynomial ring $\mathbb{R}[x]$ at the prime ideal $(x)$
. We verify the three required properties.

**$O$ is a local ring.** The ideal $(x) \subset \mathbb{R}[x]$ is prime (since $
\mathbb{R}[x]/(x) \cong \mathbb{R}$ is a domain), so localizing at $S = \mathbb{
R}[x] \setminus (x)$ yields a ring whose unique maximal ideal is $(x)\,\mathbb{R
}[x]_{(x)}$. Hence $O$ is local (in fact a DVR).

**$\mathbb{R} \subsetneq O$.** The natural map $\mathbb{R} \hookrightarrow \math
bb{R}[x] \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}[x]_{(x)}$ embeds $\mathbb{R}$ as a subring.
The element $x = x/1 \in O$ is not a real constant, so the containment is proper
: $\mathbb{R} \subsetneq O$.

**$\mathbb{C} \nsubseteq O$.** Every element of $O$ is a fraction $f/g$ with $f,
 g \in \mathbb{R}[x]$ and $g(0) \neq 0$, so $O$ embeds into the rational functio
n field $\mathbb{R}(x)$. Suppose, for contradiction, that $\mathbb{C}$ were a su
bring of $O$ (extending the given copy of $\mathbb{R}$). Then there would exist
an element $\alpha \in O \subseteq \mathbb{R}(x)$ with $\alpha^2 = -1$. Write $\
alpha = p(x)/q(x)$ with $p, q \in \mathbb{R}[x]$, $q \neq 0$. Then

$$p(x)^2 + q(x)^2 = 0 \quad \text{in } \mathbb{R}[x].$$

But for real polynomials, $p(x)^2 + q(x)^2 \equiv 0$ forces $p \equiv q \equiv 0
$: if either polynomial is nonzero, its highest-degree term produces a leading t
erm in $p^2 + q^2$ with strictly positive coefficient, which cannot be cancelled
. This contradicts $q \neq 0$.

Therefore $-1$ is not a square in $\mathbb{R}(x)$, hence not in $O$, so $\mathbb
{C}$ cannot be a subring of $O$.

**Conclusion.** $O = \mathbb{R}[x]_{(x)}$ is a local ring with $\mathbb{R} \subs
etneq O$ and $\mathbb{C} \nsubseteq O$. $\blacksquare$
