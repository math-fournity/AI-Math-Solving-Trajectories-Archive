Looking at this problem, I need to find all analytic functions on the disc $D =
\{z : |z-1| < 1\}$ with $f(1)=1$ and $f(z) = f(z^2)$.

## Setup

Since $f$ is analytic on $D$ (which contains $z=1$), I expand $f$ in a power ser
ies about $z=1$. Let $w = z - 1$, so $z = 1+w$ with $|w|<1$:

$$f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n w^n, \quad a_0 = f(1) = 1.$$

Now $z^2 = (1+w)^2 = 1 + 2w + w^2$, so $z^2 - 1 = 2w + w^2$. The functional equa
tion $f(z) = f(z^2)$ becomes:

$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n w^n = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n (2w+w^2)^n.$$

This holds on an open neighborhood of $w=0$ (since $z=1$ maps to $z^2=1$, both i
n $D$, and by continuity there's a neighborhood where both $z, z^2 \in D$). So I
 can compare power series coefficients.

## Expanding the right-hand side

Using $(2w+w^2)^n = w^n(2+w)^n = \sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}2^{n-k}w^{n+k}$, the
coefficient of $w^m$ on the right is:

$$\sum_{\substack{n+k=m\\0\le k\le n}} a_n \binom{n}{k}2^{n-k} = \sum_{n=\lceil
m/2\rceil}^{m} a_n \binom{n}{m-n}2^{2n-m}.$$

Equating coefficients of $w^m$:

$$a_m = \sum_{n=\lceil m/2\rceil}^{m} a_n \binom{n}{m-n}2^{2n-m}.$$

The $n=m$ term is $a_m\binom{m}{0}2^0 = a_m$, which cancels, yielding:

$$0 = \sum_{n=\lceil m/2\rceil}^{m-1} a_n \binom{n}{m-n}2^{2n-m}. \quad (\star)$
$

## Induction: all $a_n = 0$ for $n \geq 1$

**Base case ($m=2$):** The sum in $(\star)$ runs over $n=1$ only: $a_1\binom{1}{
1}2^0 = a_1 = 0$.

**Inductive step:** Assume $a_1 = a_2 = \cdots = a_{m-2} = 0$ for some $m \geq 3
$. In $(\star)$, all terms with $n \leq m-2$ vanish by hypothesis. The only surv
iving term is $n = m-1$:

$$0 = a_{m-1}\binom{m-1}{1}2^{2(m-1)-m} = a_{m-1}(m-1)\cdot 2^{m-2}.$$

Since $m \geq 3$, we have $(m-1)\cdot 2^{m-2} \neq 0$, so $a_{m-1} = 0$.

By induction, $a_n = 0$ for all $n \geq 1$.

## Conclusion

The power series collapses to $f(z) = a_0 = 1$.

**Verification:** $f(z) = 1$ is analytic on $D$, satisfies $f(1) = 1$, and $f(z)
 = 1 = f(z^2)$ for all $z$. ✓

The unique analytic function on $\{z : |z-1|<1\}$ satisfying $f(1)=1$ and $f(z)=
f(z^2)$ is:

$$\boxed{f(z) = 1}.$$
