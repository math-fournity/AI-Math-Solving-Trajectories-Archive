## Proof

**Yes**, the equations always have at least one non-trivial common root.

**Setup.** Let $f_1, \dots, f_m \in \mathbb{C}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ be the given homo
geneous polynomials. The condition "each variable appears in every polynomial" e
nsures each $f_i$ is non-zero and of positive degree $d_i \geq 1$. Set $R = \mat
hbb{C}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ and $I = (f_1,\dots,f_m)$.

**Step 1: $I$ is a proper ideal.** Each $f_i$ is homogeneous of positive degree,
 so every element of $I$ is a sum of homogeneous elements of positive degree (pl
us zero). In particular, $1 \notin I$, so $I \neq R$. Equivalently, $I \subseteq
 \mathfrak{m} := (x_1,\dots,x_n)$.

**Step 2: Bounding the height of $I$.** By **Krull's Height Theorem** (the gener
alized principal ideal theorem), in a Noetherian ring, every minimal prime $\mat
hfrak{p}$ over an ideal generated by $m$ elements satisfies $\operatorname{ht}(\
mathfrak{p}) \leq m$. Since $I$ is generated by $m$ elements:
$$\operatorname{ht}(I) \leq m.$$

**Step 3: Lower bound on the dimension of the variety.** Since $R$ is a polynomi
al ring in $n$ variables, $\dim R = n$. For a proper ideal $I$ in $R$:
$$\dim(R/I) = n - \operatorname{ht}(I) \geq n - m.$$
Since $m < n$, we have $m \leq n-1$, so:
$$\dim(R/I) \geq n - (n-1) = 1.$$

**Step 4: Extracting a non-trivial root.** Let $V = V(I) \subset \mathbb{A}^n(\m
athbb{C})$ be the affine variety cut out by $f_1 = \cdots = f_m = 0$. Its dimens
ion equals $\dim(R/I) \geq 1$. A variety of positive dimension over the algebrai
cally closed field $\mathbb{C}$ contains infinitely many points.

Since each $f_i$ is homogeneous, $V$ is a **cone**: $a \in V \implies \lambda a
\in V$ for all $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$. The origin lies in $V$ (as $f_i(0)=0$ f
or each $f_i$ of positive degree). But $V$ has infinitely many points, so there
exists $a \in V$ with $a \neq 0$.

This $a = (a_1,\dots,a_n) \neq (0,\dots,0)$ is a non-trivial common root of all
$m$ equations. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The condition "each variable appears in every polynomial" serves to
guarantee each $f_i$ is a non-zero homogeneous polynomial of positive degree. Th
e conclusion in fact holds for *any* $m < n$ non-zero homogeneous polynomials of
 positive degree — the all-variables condition is sufficient but not necessary.
