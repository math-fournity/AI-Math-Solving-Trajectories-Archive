## Proof of Hindman's Theorem (Erdős 532, 2-colour case)

We prove the right-hand side of the stated equivalence: for every 2-colouring $c
:\mathbb N\to\{0,1\}$ there exists an infinite set $A\subseteq\mathbb N$ and a c
olour $\kappa\in\{0,1\}$ such that every nonempty finite subset $S\subseteq A$ s
atisfies $c\!\left(\sum_{n\in S}n\right)=\kappa$. The proof uses the Stone–Čech
compactification of $(\mathbb N,+)$ and idempotent ultrafilters, in four steps.

---

### Step 1. The semigroup $\beta\mathbb N$

Let $\beta\mathbb N$ be the set of all ultrafilters on $\mathbb N$ with the Ston
e topology (basic clopen sets $\widehat A=\{p\in\beta\mathbb N:A\in p\}$, $A\sub
seteq\mathbb N$); this is a compact Hausdorff space. Define addition by
$$A\in p+q \iff \{n\in\mathbb N : A-n\in p\}\in q,\qquad A-n=\{m\in\mathbb N:m+n
\in A\}.$$
This extends ordinary addition (principal ultrafilters satisfy $p_a+p_b=p_{a+b}$
), is associative, and for each fixed $p$ the left-translation $L_p:q\mapsto p+q
$ is continuous, since $L_p^{-1}(\widehat A)=\widehat B$ where $B=\{n:A-n\in p\}
$ is a fixed subset of $\mathbb N$. Hence $(\beta\mathbb N,+)$ is a compact Haus
dorff semigroup with continuous left translations.

### Step 2. A non-principal idempotent ultrafilter (Ellis's theorem)

The set $\beta^*\mathbb N=\beta\mathbb N\setminus\{\text{principal ultrafilters}
\}$ of non-principal ultrafilters is a **closed subsemigroup**: it is closed (pr
incipal ultrafilters form the open set $\bigcup_n\widehat{\{n\}}$), and it is cl
osed under $+$, since for non-principal $p,q$ and any finite $F\subseteq\mathbb
N$, the set $F-n$ is finite hence not in $p$, so $\{n:F-n\in p\}=\varnothing\not
in q$, giving $F\notin p+q$.

$\beta^*\mathbb N$ is nonempty (free ultrafilters exist by the ultrafilter lemma
) and is a compact Hausdorff semigroup with continuous left translations. By **E
llis's idempotent theorem** (proved below) it contains an idempotent $p$ with $p
+p=p$. Being non-principal, every member of $p$ is infinite.

*Proof of Ellis's theorem.* Let $S$ be such a semigroup and pick $a\in S$. Set $
T=\overline{\{a,a^2,a^3,\dots\}}$. By continuity of left translations, $a^k\cdot
 T\subseteq\overline{\{a^{k+n}\}}\subseteq T$, and for $s=\lim_i a^{n_i}\in T$ o
ne has $s\cdot T=\lim_i a^{n_i}\cdot T\subseteq T$; so $T$ is a closed subsemigr
oup. By Zorn's lemma (intersections of chains of nonempty compact closed subsemi
groups are nonempty closed subsemigroups) $T$ contains a minimal nonempty closed
 subsemigroup $M$. Pick $m\in M$. Then $m\cdot M$ is a closed subsemigroup of $M
$ (closed as a continuous image of compact $M$; a subsemigroup since $(m a)(m b)
=m(am)b\in mM$ because $am\in M$). By minimality $mM=M$, so some $e\in M$ satisf
ies $me=m$. The set $E=\{t\in M:mt=m\}$ is a nonempty closed subsemigroup of $M$
 (if $ma=m,mb=m$ then $m(ab)=(ma)b=mb=m$), so $E=M$ by minimality; in particular
 $mm=m$. $\square$

### Step 3. Extracting the monochromatic set from $p$

Let $c:\mathbb N\to\{0,1\}$ and $C_0=c^{-1}(0),\,C_1=c^{-1}(1)$. Since $p$ is an
 ultrafilter, exactly one of $C_0,C_1$ lies in $p$; call it $C$ with colour $\ka
ppa$. We build a strictly increasing sequence $a_1<a_2<\cdots$ and a decreasing
sequence $A_0\supseteq A_1\supseteq\cdots$ with $A_0=C$, $A_k\in p$, and for eve
ry $k\ge1$:
1. $a_k\in A_{k-1}$,
2. $A_k+a_k\subseteq A_{k-1}$ (i.e. $A_k\subseteq A_{k-1}-a_k$),
3. $A_k\in p$.

*Construction.* Given $A_{k-1}\in p$: since $p+p=p$ and $A_{k-1}\in p$, we have
$A_{k-1}\in p+p$, i.e. $\{n:A_{k-1}-n\in p\}\in p$. Intersecting with $A_{k-1}\i
n p$ gives
$$D_k:=A_{k-1}\cap\{n:A_{k-1}-n\in p\}\in p.$$
Since $p$ is non-principal, $D_k$ is infinite, so choose $a_k\in D_k$ with $a_k>
a_{k-1}$. Then $a_k\in A_{k-1}$ and $A_{k-1}-a_k\in p$; set
$$A_k:=A_{k-1}\cap(A_{k-1}-a_k)\in p.$$
Then $A_k\subseteq A_{k-1}$ and $A_k+a_k\subseteq A_{k-1}$, as required.

### Step 4. All finite subset sums lie in $C$

Let $A=\{a_1,a_2,\dots\}$, which is infinite since the $a_k$ are strictly increa
sing. We claim: for every nonempty finite set of indices $1\le i_1<\cdots<i_m$,
the sum $\sigma=a_{i_1}+\cdots+a_{i_m}$ belongs to $A_{i_1-1}$ (with $A_0=C$), h
ence to $C$.

Induction on $m$.

- **$m=1$:** $\sigma=a_{i_1}\in A_{i_1-1}$ by (i).

- **$m\ge2$:** Let $\tau=a_{i_1}+\cdots+a_{i_{m-1}}$. By the induction hypothesi
s $\tau\in A_{i_1-1}$. Since the $A_k$ are decreasing and $i_1-1\le i_m-1$, we h
ave $\tau\in A_{i_m-1}$. Also $a_{i_m}\in A_{i_m-1}$ by (i). Hence
$$\sigma=\tau+a_{i_m}\in A_{i_m-1}+a_{i_m}\subseteq A_{i_m-2}\quad\text{by (ii)}
.$$
Since $i_m\ge i_1+1$, we have $i_m-2\ge i_1-1$, so $A_{i_m-2}\subseteq A_{i_1-1}
$. Thus $\sigma\in A_{i_1-1}\subseteq A_0=C$.

Therefore every nonempty finite subset sum of $A$ lies in $C$, i.e. $c\!\left(\s
um_{n\in S}n\right)=\kappa$ for all nonempty finite $S\subseteq A$.

---

### Conclusion

We have shown that for every 2-colouring $c:\mathbb N\to\mathrm{Fin}\,2$ there e
xist an infinite set $A\subseteq\mathbb N$ and a colour $\kappa\in\mathrm{Fin}\,
2$ such that every nonempty finite subset $S\subseteq A$ satisfies $c\!\left(\su
m_{n\in S}n\right)=\kappa$. This is precisely the right-hand side of the stated
equivalence
$$\textit{answer}(\mathrm{True})\;\longleftrightarrow\;\bigl[\forall\,c:\mathbb
N\to\mathrm{Fin}\,2,\;\exists A\subseteq\mathbb N\text{ infinite},\;\exists\,\ka
ppa,\;\forall S\text{ nonempty finite}\subseteq A,\;c(\textstyle\sum_{n\in S}n)=
\kappa\bigr].$$
The left-hand side $\textit{answer}(\mathrm{True})$ asserts that the conjecture'
s answer is "True", which is exactly the content of the theorem just proved; hen
ce the equivalence holds. $\blacksquare$
