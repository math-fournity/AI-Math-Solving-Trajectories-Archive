## Proof: Existence and Unpleteness of the Weak Solution

**Setting.** Let $D \subset \mathbb{R}^N$ be a bounded open nonempty domain, $k_
1, k_2 > 0$ strictly positive constants, $\Omega \subset D$ a measurable subset,
 and $f \in H^{-1}(D)$. Define the coefficient
$$
k(x) := k_1\,\chi_\Omega(x) + k_2\,(1-\chi_\Omega(x)), \qquad x \in D.
$$
We seek $y \in H^1_0(D)$ such that for all $\phi \in H^1_0(D)$,
$$
\int_D k(x)\,\nabla y \cdot \nabla \phi\, dx = \langle f, \phi \rangle. \tag{$\s
tar$}
$$

---

### Step 1. The coefficient $k$ is uniformly elliptic and bounded

Since $k_1, k_2 > 0$, set
$$
k_{\min} := \min\{k_1, k_2\} > 0, \qquad k_{\max} := \max\{k_1, k_2\} = \alpha >
 0.
$$
For a.e. $x \in D$, the value $k(x)$ is either $k_1$ (when $x \in \Omega$) or $k
_2$ (when $x \notin \Omega$). Hence
$$
k_{\min} \le k(x) \le k_{\max} = \alpha \quad \text{a.e. in } D. \tag{1}
$$
In particular, $k \in L^\infty(D)$ with $\|k\|_{L^\infty(D)} = \alpha$.

---

### Step 2. Define the bilinear form and the linear functional

Define
$$
a(u, v) := \int_D k(x)\,\nabla u \cdot \nabla v\, dx, \qquad u, v \in H^1_0(D),
$$
and
$$
\ell(v) := \langle f, v \rangle, \qquad v \in H^1_0(D).
$$

**$\ell$ is a bounded linear functional on $H^1_0(D)$.** This is immediate since
 $f \in H^{-1}(D) = (H^1_0(D))'$ by hypothesis, so
$$
|\ell(v)| = |\langle f, v\rangle| \le \|f\|_{H^{-1}(D)}\,\|v\|_{H^1_0(D)}. \tag{
2}
$$

---

### Step 3. Boundedness of $a$

For $u, v \in H^1_0(D)$, using (1) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality:
$$
|a(u,v)| \le \int_D k(x)\,|\nabla u|\,|\nabla v|\,dx \le \alpha \int_D |\nabla u
|\,|\nabla v|\,dx \le \alpha\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^2(D)}\,\|\nabla v\|_{L^2(D)}.
$$
On $H^1_0(D)$, the norm $\|v\|_{H^1_0} := \|\nabla v\|_{L^2(D)}$ (equivalent to
the full $H^1$ norm by the Poincaré inequality) gives
$$
|a(u,v)| \le \alpha\,\|u\|_{H^1_0(D)}\,\|v\|_{H^1_0(D)}. \tag{3}
$$

---

### Step 4. Coercivity of $a$

For $u \in H^1_0(D)$, using the lower bound in (1):
$$
a(u, u) = \int_D k(x)\,|\nabla u|^2\,dx \ge k_{\min}\int_D |\nabla u|^2\,dx = k_
{\min}\,\|u\|^2_{H^1_0(D)}. \tag{4}
$$
Here we use the fact that $\|\nabla u\|_{L^2(D)}$ is a norm on $H^1_0(D)$ equiva
lent to the standard $H^1$ norm (Poincaré inequality), so coercivity holds with
constant $k_{\min} > 0$.

---

### Step 5. Application of the Lax–Milgram theorem

We have verified:
- $H^1_0(D)$ is a Hilbert space (closed subspace of $H^1(D)$, since $D$ is open
and bounded),
- $a : H^1_0(D) \times H^1_0(D) \to \mathbb{R}$ is a **bounded** bilinear form b
y (3),
- $a$ is **coercive** by (4) with coercivity constant $k_{\min} > 0$,
- $\ell : H^1_0(D) \to \mathbb{R}$ is a **bounded** linear functional by (2).

By the **Lax–Milgram theorem**, there exists a **unique** $y \in H^1_0(D)$ such
that
$$
a(y, \phi) = \ell(\phi) \quad \text{for all } \phi \in H^1_0(D),
$$
which is precisely $(\star)$.

### Step 6. Energy estimate

Setting $\phi = y$ in $(\star)$ and using coercivity (4) and the bound (2):
$$
k_{\min}\,\|y\|^2_{H^1_0(D)} \le a(y,y) = \langle f, y\rangle \le \|f\|_{H^{-1}(
D)}\,\|y\|_{H^1_0(D)}.
$$
Dividing by $\|y\|_{H^1_0(D)}$ (if $y \ne 0$; if $f=0$ then $y=0$ and the estima
te is trivial):
$$
\boxed{\|y\|_{H^1_0(D)} \le \frac{1}{k_{\min}}\,\|f\|_{H^{-1}(D)} = \frac{1}{\mi
n\{k_1,k_2\}}\,\|f\|_{H^{-1}(D)}.} \tag{5}
$$

This estimate is **independent of the choice of measurable subset $\Omega$**, de
pending only on $k_1$, $k_2$, and $f$.

### Step 7. Continuity of the solution map $\chi_\Omega \mapsto y$

The estimate (5) shows that the solution operator
$$
S: L^\infty(D;\{k_1,k_2\}) \to H^1_0(D), \qquad k \mapsto y(k)
$$
is uniformly bounded. Moreover, if $k^{(1)}, k^{(2)}$ are two admissible coeffic
ients (corresponding to two measurable subsets $\Omega_1, \Omega_2$) with soluti
ons $y_1, y_2$, then subtracting the two variational equations and testing with
$\phi = y_1 - y_2$:
$$
\int_D k^{(1)}\,|\nabla(y_1 - y_2)|^2\,dx = \int_D (k^{(2)} - k^{(1)})\,\nabla y
_2 \cdot \nabla(y_1 - y_2)\,dx.
$$
Using coercivity of $k^{(1)}$ and Cauchy–Schwarz:
$$
k_{\min}\,\|y_1 - y_2\|^2_{H^1_0} \le \|k^{(1)} - k^{(2)}\|_{L^\infty}\,\|\nabla
 y_2\|_{L^2}\,\|\nabla(y_1-y_2)\|_{L^2}.
$$
Since $\|k^{(1)} - k^{(2)}\|_{L^\infty} \le |k_1 - k_2|$ and $\|\nabla y_2\|_{L^
2} \le \frac{1}{k_{\min}}\|f\|_{H^{-1}}$:
$$
\|y_1 - y_2\|_{H^1_0} \le \frac{|k_1 - k_2|}{k_{\min}^2}\,\|f\|_{H^{-1}}.
$$
This shows the solution map is Lipschitz-continuous (in the uniform bound sense)
, though the sharper statement involves convergence in measure of $\chi_{\Omega_
n} \to \chi_\Omega$ implying $y_n \to y$ in $H^1_0(D)$ (by dominated convergence
, since $k^{(n)} \to k$ in $L^1(D)$ and the uniform bound allows extraction of w
eakly convergent subsequences, with the limit identified uniquely).
