## Proof

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains only the problem to be sol
ved, with no solution, sketch, or answer embedded. Proceeding.

---

Let
$$A = \Bigl\{r\in\mathbb{R}:\exists\,C>0,\ \exists\,\tfrac{p_n}{q_n}\in\mathbb{Q
},\ q_n\to\infty,\ \bigl|r-\tfrac{p_n}{q_n}\bigr|<\tfrac{C}{q_n^{3}}\ \forall n\
Bigr\}.$$

We show $\lambda(A)=0$ (Lebesgue measure).

**Step 1. Reduction to a countable union.** For each integer $m\ge 1$ define
$$A_m=\Bigl\{r:\bigl|r-\tfrac{p}{q}\bigr|<\tfrac{m}{q^{3}}\text{ for infinitely
many rationals }\tfrac{p}{q}\text{ with }q\to\infty\Bigr\}.$$
If $r\in A$ with constant $C$, pick $m\ge\lceil C\rceil$; then $r\in A_m$. Hence
$$A\subseteq\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}A_m.$$
It suffices to show $\lambda(A_m)=0$ for every $m$.

**Step 2. Limsup formulation.** For fixed $m$ and $q\ge 1$ set
$$E_{q}^{(m)}=\bigcup_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}\Bigl(\tfrac{p}{q}-\tfrac{m}{q^{3}},\ \tfr
ac{p}{q}+\tfrac{m}{q^{3}}\Bigr).$$
Then
$$A_m=\bigcap_{Q=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{q\ge Q}E_{q}^{(m)}=\limsup_{q\to\infty}E_{q
}^{(m)}.$$
Indeed, $r\in A_m$ iff $r\in E_{q}^{(m)}$ for infinitely many $q$ (equivalently
for all $Q$, some $q\ge Q$), which is the limsup.

**Step 3. Measure estimate on bounded intervals.** Fix $R>0$ and consider $E_{q}
^{(m)}\cap[-R,R]$. Its measure is bounded by the sum of the lengths of the contr
ibuting intervals:
$$\lambda\bigl(E_{q}^{(m)}\cap[-R,R]\bigr)\le\sum_{\substack{p\in\mathbb{Z}\\|p/
q|\le R+1}}\frac{2m}{q^{3}}.$$
(The condition $|p/q|\le R+1$ suffices, since an interval centered at $p/q$ with
 $|p/q|>R+1$ cannot meet $[-R,R]$ once $m/q^{3}<1$, which holds for $q$ large; f
or small $q$ the bound $|p/q|\le R+1$ still captures all relevant $p$ up to a ha
rmless enlargement, and using $R+1$ only increases the count.)

The number of integers $p$ with $|p/q|\le R+1$ is at most $2(R+1)q+1\le (2R+3)q$
. Therefore
$$\lambda\bigl(E_{q}^{(m)}\cap[-R,R]\bigr)\le (2R+3)q\cdot\frac{2m}{q^{3}}=\frac
{2m(2R+3)}{q^{2}}.$$

**Step 4. Borel–Cantelli.** Summing,
$$\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\lambda\bigl(E_{q}^{(m)}\cap[-R,R]\bigr)\le 2m(2R+3)\sum_{q
=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{q^{2}}<\infty.$$
By the Borel–Cantelli lemma,
$$\lambda\Bigl(\limsup_{q\to\infty}\bigl(E_{q}^{(m)}\cap[-R,R]\bigr)\Bigr)=0.$$
But $\limsup_{q}(E_{q}^{(m)}\cap[-R,R])\supseteq A_m\cap[-R,R]$, so
$$\lambda\bigl(A_m\cap[-R,R]\bigr)=0.$$

**Step 5. Conclusion.** Since $R>0$ was arbitrary,
$$\lambda(A_m)=\lambda\Bigl(\bigcup_{R=1}^{\infty}(A_m\cap[-R,R])\Bigr)=0.$$
Finally,
$$\lambda(A)\le\lambda\Bigl(\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}A_m\Bigr)\le\sum_{m=1}^{\infty
}\lambda(A_m)=0.$$

Hence the Lebesgue measure of $A$ is zero. $\blacksquare$
