## Proof

**Setup.** We are given an open set $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ and a function $f:
\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ such that there exists a point $p_0 \in U$ with
$$f(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{f^{(n)}(p_0)}{n!}(x - p_0)^n \quad \text{for
all } x \in U.$$
We must show that $f$ is analytic on $U$, i.e., for every $q \in U$ there exists
 $\varepsilon > 0$ such that $f$ equals its Taylor series at $q$ on $(q - \varep
silon, q + \varepsilon)$.

Set $a_n := \frac{f^{(n)}(p_0)}{n!}$ and let $R \in [0, \infty]$ be the radius o
f convergence of the power series $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n (x - p_0)^n$.

---

**Step 1: $U \subseteq (p_0 - R,\, p_0 + R)$.**

By the definition of radius of convergence, the series converges (absolutely) fo
r $|x - p_0| < R$ and diverges for $|x - p_0| > R$.

- If some $x_0 \in U$ satisfies $|x_0 - p_0| > R$, the series diverges at $x_0$,
 contradicting $f(x_0) = \sum a_n (x_0 - p_0)^n$.
- If some $x_0 \in U$ satisfies $|x_0 - p_0| = R$: since $U$ is open, there is $
\delta > 0$ with $(x_0 - \delta, x_0 + \delta) \subseteq U$. This interval conta
ins points $y$ with $|y - p_0| > R$, where the series diverges — again a contrad
iction.

Hence every $x \in U$ satisfies $|x - p_0| < R$, i.e., $U \subseteq (p_0 - R, p_
0 + R)$.

---

**Step 2: Re-centering the power series at an arbitrary $q \in U$.**

Let $q \in U$, so $|q - p_0| < R$. Set $r := R - |q - p_0| > 0$. For any $x$ wit
h $|x - q| < r$,
$$|x - p_0| \leq |x - q| + |q - p_0| < r + |q - p_0| = R,$$
so $x$ lies in the open interval of convergence and
$$f(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n (x - p_0)^n = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n\bigl((x -
 q) + (q - p_0)\bigr)^n = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n \sum_{m=0}^{n} \binom{n}{m}(q
- p_0)^{n-m}(x - q)^m.$$

**Justification of exchanging summations.** Pick $\rho$ with $|q - p_0| < \rho <
 R$ and restrict to $|x - q| < R - \rho$. Then $|q - p_0| + |x - q| < \rho$, and
$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \sum_{m=0}^{n} |a_n| \binom{n}{m} |q - p_0|^{n-m} |x - q|^
m = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} |a_n|\bigl(|q - p_0| + |x - q|\bigr)^n \leq \sum_{n=0}^{
\infty} |a_n| \rho^n < \infty,$$
since $\rho < R$. Absolute convergence justifies (by Fubini/Tonelli for series)
exchanging the order of summation:
$$f(x) = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty} \underbrace{\left(\sum_{n=m}^{\infty} \binom{n}{m}
a_n (q - p_0)^{n-m}\right)}_{=:\, b_m} (x - q)^m = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty} b_m (x -
q)^m.$$

This is a power series in $(x - q)$ converging on $|x - q| < r > 0$.

---

**Step 3: The re-centered series is the Taylor series of $f$ at $q$.**

Within the radius of convergence, a power series may be differentiated term by t
erm. Differentiating $f(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n (x - p_0)^n$ a total of $m$
 times and evaluating at $x = q$:
$$f^{(m)}(q) = \sum_{n=m}^{\infty} \frac{n!}{(n-m)!}\, a_n\, (q - p_0)^{n-m} = m
! \sum_{n=m}^{\infty} \binom{n}{m} a_n (q - p_0)^{n-m} = m!\, b_m.$$
Hence $b_m = \frac{f^{(m)}(q)}{m!}$, and the re-centered series is exactly the T
aylor series of $f$ at $q$.

---

**Conclusion.** For every $q \in U$, taking $\varepsilon := r = R - |q - p_0| >
0$, we have shown that for all $x$ with $|x - q| < \varepsilon$,
$$f(x) = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty} \frac{f^{(m)}(q)}{m!}(x - q)^m.$$
This is precisely the definition of $f$ being (real-)analytic on $U$. $\blacksqu
are$
