Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum number of pairs (among 1985
integers) whose product is a perfect square.

## Solution

**Encoding via parity vectors.** Each integer has the form $p_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots
 p_9^{\alpha_9}$. Define its parity vector $v \in \mathbb{F}_2^9$ by $v_i = \alp
ha_i \pmod{2}$. The product of two integers is a perfect square if and only if t
heir parity vectors are equal (since the product has each exponent even iff the
two parity vectors sum to $\mathbf{0}$ in $\mathbb{F}_2^9$, i.e., they are ident
ical).

**Reduction to a distribution problem.** There are $|\mathbb{F}_2^9| = 2^9 = 512
$ possible parity vectors. Partition the 1985 integers into 512 classes accordin
g to their parity vector. If class $i$ has size $n_i$ (with $\sum n_i = 1985$),
the number of good pairs is
$$\sum_{i=1}^{512} \binom{n_i}{2}.$$
Every vector in $\mathbb{F}_2^9$ is realizable (e.g., $\prod_{v_i=1} p_i$), so w
e may choose any multiset of vectors we wish. Thus the problem becomes: **distri
bute 1985 items into 512 boxes to minimize $\sum \binom{n_i}{2}$.**

**Minimizing by equal distribution.** Since $f(n)=\binom{n}{2}=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$
 is convex, the sum is minimized when the $n_i$ are as equal as possible. Dividi
ng:
$$1985 = 512 \cdot 3 + 449.$$
So 449 classes receive 4 elements and $512 - 449 = 63$ classes receive 3 element
s. Verification:
$$449 \cdot 4 + 63 \cdot 3 = 1796 + 189 = 1985. \checkmark$$

**Computing the minimum.** The minimum number of pairs is
$$449\cdot\binom{4}{2} + 63\cdot\binom{3}{2} = 449\cdot 6 + 63\cdot 3 = 2694 + 1
89 = 2883.$$

**Achievability.** Assign 4 integers to each of 449 distinct parity vectors and
3 integers to each of the remaining 63 distinct parity vectors (using distinct a
ctual integers within each class, e.g., $p_i^0, p_i^2, p_i^4, \ldots$ to keep pa
rity fixed while varying the integer). This realizes exactly 2883 good pairs, an
d no arrangement can do better by the convexity argument above.
