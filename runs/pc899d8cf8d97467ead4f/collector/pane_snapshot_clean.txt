## Solution

**The statement is TRUE.**

**Theorem.** Every finitely presented group with deficiency $\geq 1$ is infinite
.

**Proof.**

Let $G$ be a finitely presented group with $\operatorname{def}(G) \geq 1$. By de
finition of deficiency, there exists a finite presentation
$$P = \langle x_1, \dots, x_n \mid r_1, \dots, r_m \rangle$$
with $n - m \geq 1$, i.e., $n \geq m + 1$.

**Step 1: Abelianization.** Consider the abelianization $G_{\mathrm{ab}} = G/[G,
G]$. This is a finitely generated abelian group obtained from the same presentat
ion by abelianizing the relators. Concretely, each relator $r_i$ maps to a linea
r relation $\sum_{j=1}^n \epsilon_{ij}\, x_j = 0$ in the free abelian group $\ma
thbb{Z}^n$, where $\epsilon_{ij}$ is the exponent sum of $x_j$ in $r_i$. Thus
$$G_{\mathrm{ab}} \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}^n \big/ \operatorname{im}(M),$$
where $M : \mathbb{Z}^m \to \mathbb{Z}^n$ is the $n \times m$ integer matrix $(\
epsilon_{ij})$.

**Step 2: Rank bound.** The image $\operatorname{im}(M) \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^n$
is generated by $m$ vectors, so
$$\operatorname{rank}(\operatorname{im}(M)) \;\leq\; m.$$
By the structure theorem for finitely generated abelian groups,
$$G_{\mathrm{ab}} \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}^{\,n - \operatorname{rank}(M)} \oplus T,$
$
where $T$ is a finite abelian group. Therefore the free rank of $G_{\mathrm{ab}}
$ satisfies
$$\operatorname{rank}(G_{\mathrm{ab}}) \;=\; n - \operatorname{rank}(M) \;\geq\;
 n - m \;\geq\; 1.$$

**Step 3: Conclusion.** Since $\operatorname{rank}(G_{\mathrm{ab}}) \geq 1$, the
 abelianization $G_{\mathrm{ab}}$ is infinite.

Now suppose for contradiction that $G$ is finite. Then every quotient of $G$ is
finite, in particular $G_{\mathrm{ab}} = G/[G,G]$ is finite. This contradicts th
e fact that $G_{\mathrm{ab}}$ is infinite.

Therefore $G$ must be infinite. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The argument shows more: a finitely presented group with deficiency
$\geq k$ has abelianization of free rank $\geq k$, hence $H_1(G;\mathbb{Z})$ has
 rank $\geq k$. The case $k=1$ already forces infiniteness.
