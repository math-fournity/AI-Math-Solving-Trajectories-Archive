Let $z = e^{2\pi i t}$. As $t$ ranges over $[0,1]$, the point $z$ traverses the
unit circle $|z|=1$ once counterclockwise. We have

$$
dz = 2\pi i\, e^{2\pi i t}\, dt = 2\pi i\, z\, dt,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
dt = \frac{dz}{2\pi i\, z}.
$$

Substituting $e^{2\pi i t} = z$ and the expression for $dt$,

$$
\int_0^1 \frac{e^{e^{2\pi it}}}{e^{2\pi it}}\, dt
= \oint_{|z|=1} \frac{e^{z}}{z}\cdot \frac{dz}{2\pi i\, z}
= \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=1} \frac{e^{z}}{z^{2}}\, dz.
$$

The integrand $\frac{e^z}{z^2}$ has a single isolated singularity inside $|z|=1$
, namely a pole of order $2$ at $z=0$. Apply Cauchy's integral formula in deriva
tive form with $f(z) = e^z$ (entire):

$$
\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=1} \frac{f(z)}{z^{2}}\, dz = f'(0).
$$

Since $f'(z) = e^z$, we get $f'(0) = e^{0} = 1$.

Therefore,

$$
\int_0^1 \frac{e^{e^{2\pi it}}}{e^{2\pi it}}\, dt = 1.
$$
