Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether all maximal subfields of a
finite central division algebra have the same dimension over the center.

**Answer: Yes, $\dim_Z(F)$ is constant for all maximal subfields $F$, and equals
 the degree $n$ of $D$ (where $[D:Z]=n^2$).**

## Proof

Let $D$ be a finite-dimensional central division algebra over its center $Z$. By
 the structure theory of central simple algebras, $[D:Z] = n^2$ for some positiv
e integer $n$ (the *degree* or *index* of $D$).

### Step 1: Characterizing maximal subfields via the centralizer

For any subfield $F$ of $D$ containing $Z$, let $C_D(F) = \{x \in D : xf = fx \t
ext{ for all } f \in F\}$ be the centralizer of $F$ in $D$.

**Claim:** $F$ is a maximal subfield of $D$ if and only if $C_D(F) = F$.

*Proof of claim.*

$(\Leftarrow)$: Suppose $C_D(F) = F$ and let $G \supseteq F$ be a subfield of $D
$. Since $G$ is commutative, every element of $G$ commutes with every element of
 $F$, so $G \subseteq C_D(F) = F$. Hence $G = F$, and $F$ is maximal.

$(\Rightarrow)$: Suppose $F$ is maximal. Since $F$ is commutative, $F \subseteq
C_D(F)$. The centralizer $C_D(F)$ is a subdivision ring of $D$ in which $F$ lies
 in the center. If $C_D(F) \neq F$, pick $x \in C_D(F) \setminus F$. Then $F(x)$
 — the subring generated by $F$ and $x$ — is commutative (since $x$ commutes wit
h all of $F$ and $F$ is a field), is a domain, hence a field (finite-dimensional
 over $F$), and strictly contains $F$. This contradicts the maximality of $F$. $
\square$

### Step 2: Applying the Double Centralizer Theorem

$D$ is a central simple algebra over $Z$, and $F$ (being a field containing $Z$)
 is a simple subalgebra. The **Double Centralizer Theorem** states:

> If $A$ is a central simple algebra over $Z$ and $B$ is a simple subalgebra, th
en $C_A(B)$ is simple, $C_A(C_A(B)) = B$, and
> $$\dim_Z(B) \cdot \dim_Z(C_A(B)) = \dim_Z(A).$$

Applying this with $A = D$ and $B = F$:

$$[F:Z] \cdot [C_D(F):Z] = [D:Z] = n^2.$$

### Step 3: Conclusion

If $F$ is a **maximal** subfield, then by Step 1, $C_D(F) = F$, so $[C_D(F):Z] =
 [F:Z]$. Substituting into the identity from Step 2:

$$[F:Z]^2 = n^2,$$

which gives

$$[F:Z] = n.$$

This value depends only on $D$ (through its degree $n$), not on the particular c
hoice of maximal subfield $F$. Therefore $\dim_Z(F) = n$ for **every** maximal s
ubfield $F$ of $D$.

### Remark on existence

Maximal subfields always exist: $D$ is finite-dimensional over $Z$, so any ascen
ding chain of subfields (ordered by inclusion) terminates, and a maximal element
 is a maximal subfield. Each such maximal subfield has dimension exactly $n = \s
qrt{[D:Z]}$ over $Z$.

$\blacksquare$
