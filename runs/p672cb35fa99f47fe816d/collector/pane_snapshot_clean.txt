## Proof

**Setup.** Let $f(\theta) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty x_n e^{in\theta}$. Since $\sum |x_
n|^2 \le \sum n|x_n|^2 \le 1$, we have $x \in \ell^2$, so $f \in H^2 \subset L^2
(\mathbb{T}) \subset L^1(\mathbb{T})$, and the integral is well-defined.

**Upper bound via duality.** The $L^1$ norm admits the dual characterization:
$$\|f\|_1 = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}|f(\theta)|\,d\theta = \sup_{\|g\|_\infty
 \le 1}\left|\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(\theta)\overline{g(\theta)}\,d\theta\
right|,$$
where the supremum is over $g \in L^\infty(\mathbb{T})$.

Expanding using Fourier coefficients $\hat{g}(n) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} g
(\theta)e^{-in\theta}\,d\theta$:
$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f\,\bar{g} = \sum_{n=1}^\infty x_n\,\overline{\hat
{g}(n)}.$$

**Applying Cauchy–Schwarz** with weights $n$ and $1/n$:
$$\left|\sum_{n=1}^\infty x_n\,\overline{\hat{g}(n)}\right| \le \left(\sum_{n=1}
^\infty n|x_n|^2\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{|\hat{g}(n)|^2}{n}\ri
ght)^{1/2} \le \left(\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{|\hat{g}(n)|^2}{n}\right)^{1/2}.$$

**Bounding the Fourier sum.** Since $1/n \le 1$ for all $n \ge 1$:
$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{|\hat{g}(n)|^2}{n} \le \sum_{n=1}^\infty |\hat{g}(n)|^
2 \le \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}|\hat{g}(n)|^2 = \|g\|_2^2 \le \|g\|_\infty^2 \le

where the second-to-last step uses Parseval's identity and the last step uses $\
|g\|_2 \le \|g\|_\infty$ (on a probability space).

Combining: $\|f\|_1 \le 1$.

**Achieving the bound.** Take $x_1 = 1$ and $x_n = 0$ for $n \ge 2$. Then:
- $\sum_{n=1}^\infty n|x_n|^2 = 1 \cdot |1|^2 = 1 \le 1$. ✓
- $f(\theta) = e^{i\theta}$, so $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}|e^{i\theta}|\,d\the

**Equality analysis.** Equality in the chain requires: (i) $\sum n|x_n|^2 = 1$,
(ii) $|\hat{g}(n)|^2/n = |\hat{g}(n)|^2$ for all nonzero terms (so $\hat{g}(n) =
 0$ for $n \ge 2$), (iii) $\|g\|_\infty = 1$ with $\|g\|_2 = 1$, and (iv) Cauchy

## Conclusion

The maximum value is $\boxed{1}$.
