$$L_n:=\bigl|z_1^n+z_2^n+\cdots+z_k^n\bigr|^{1/n}.$$

**Claim.** For every $z_1,\dots,z_k\in\mathbb C$,
$$\limsup_{n\to\infty} L_n \;=\; M,\qquad M:=\max_{1\le i\le k}|z_i|.$$
Consequently, *whenever the limit exists*, it equals $M$; in particular $L_n\to
0$ forces $M=0$, i.e. $z_1=\cdots=z_k=0$.

---

**Upper bound: $\limsup L_n\le M$.**
$$|z_1^n+\cdots+z_k^n|\le\sum_i|z_i|^n\le k\,M^n,$$
so $L_n\le k^{1/n}M\to M$. Thus $\limsup L_n\le M$.

---

**Lower bound: $\limsup L_n\ge M$.** If $M=0$ there is nothing to prove, so assu
me $M>0$.

Let $I=\{i:|z_i|=M\}$ and write $z_i=M\,u_i$ for $i\in I$, with $|u_i|=1$. The r
emaining terms satisfy $|z_i|\le M'$ for some $M'<M$. Then
$$z_1^n+\cdots+z_k^n \;=\; M^n\!\sum_{i\in I}u_i^n \;+\; R_n,\qquad |R_n|\le (k-
|I|)(M')^n=o(M^n).$$
Setting $S_n:=\sum_{i\in I}u_i^n$, we get
$$L_n \;=\; M\cdot\bigl|S_n+R_n/M^n\bigr|^{1/n} \;=\; M\cdot\bigl|S_n+o(1)\bigr|
^{1/n}.$$

Group the indices in $I$ by the value of $u_i=e^{i\theta}$. Suppose the distinct

$$|S_n|^2=\sum_{a,b}c_a c_b\,(v_a/v_b)^n=\sum_{j=1}^r c_j^2+\sum_{a\ne b}c_a c_b

For $a\ne b$, $v_a/v_b=e^{i\varphi}$ with $\varphi\not\equiv 0\pmod{2\pi}$, so i
ts Cesàro mean vanishes: $\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N(v_a/v_b)^n\to 0$. Therefore
$$\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N|S_n|^2 \;\longrightarrow\; \sum_{j=1}^r c_j^2 \;\ge\; 1.$$

Thus $\limsup L_n\ge M\cdot 1=M$.
