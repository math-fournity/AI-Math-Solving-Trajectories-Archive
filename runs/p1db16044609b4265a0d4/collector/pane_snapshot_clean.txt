## Solution

**Part 1: Mean value of $\ln r$ on the circle $(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=R^2$.**

Let $c = \sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ be the distance from the origin to the center. Parametr
ize the circle by
$$x = a + R\cos t,\quad y = b + R\sin t,\quad t\in[0,2\pi].$$
Then
$$r^2 = x^2+y^2 = c^2 + R^2 + 2R(a\cos t + b\sin t) = c^2+R^2+2Rc\cos(t-\varphi)
$$
for a suitable phase $\varphi$. By periodicity the shift is irrelevant, so the m
ean value is
$$\overline{\ln r} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\ln r\,dt = \frac{1}{4\pi}\int
_0^{2\pi}\!\ln\!\big(c^2+R^2+2Rc\cos t\big)\,dt.$$

**Standard integral.** For $A>|B|$,
$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\ln(A+B\cos t)\,dt = \ln\!\frac{A+\sqrt{A^2-B^2}}
{2}.$$
*Proof.* Differentiate the left side w.r.t. $A$: $\frac{d}{dA}=\int_0^{2\pi}\!\f
rac{dt}{A+B\cos t}=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{A^2-B^2}}$ (standard). Integrating gives $2
\pi\ln\!\big(A+\sqrt{A^2-B^2}\big)+C(B)$. As $A\to\infty$ both sides $\sim 2\pi\
ln A$, forcing $C=-2\pi\ln 2$. $\square$

Apply with $A=c^2+R^2$, $B=2Rc$. Then $\sqrt{A^2-B^2}=\sqrt{(c^2+R^2)^2-4R^2c^2}
=|c^2-R^2|$.

- **If $c>R$** (origin outside the circle): $|c^2-R^2|=c^2-R^2$, so $\frac{A+\sq
rt{A^2-B^2}}{2}=\frac{2c^2}{2}=c^2$, giving $\overline{\ln r}=\frac12\ln c^2=\ln
 c$.
- **If $c<R$** (origin inside the circle): $|c^2-R^2|=R^2-c^2$, so $\frac{A+\sqr
t{A^2-B^2}}{2}=\frac{2R^2}{2}=R^2$, giving $\overline{\ln r}=\frac12\ln R^2=\ln
R$.
- **If $c=R$**: both formulas give $\ln R=\ln c$.

$$\boxed{\overline{\ln r}=\ln\!\big(\max\{c,R\}\big),\qquad c=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}.}$$

This agrees with the mean-value property of the harmonic function $\ln r$ (in 2D
): when the singularity at the origin lies outside the circle, the mean equals t
he value at the center, $\ln c$; when it lies inside, the mean is $\ln R$.

---

**Part 2: Mean value of $1/r$ on the sphere $(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2+(z-c)^2=R^2$.**

Let $d=\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}$. Place the center on the $z$-axis; a point on the sph
ere is
$$x=R\sin\theta\cos\phi,\ y=R\sin\theta\sin\phi,\ z=d+R\cos\theta,$$
so $r^2=R^2+d^2+2dR\cos\theta$. The surface mean is
$$\overline{1/r}=\frac{1}{4\pi R^2}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{\pi}\!\frac{R^2\sin\
theta\,d\theta\,d\phi}{\sqrt{R^2+d^2+2dR\cos\theta}}=\frac12\int_0^{\pi}\!\frac{
\sin\theta\,d\theta}{\sqrt{R^2+d^2+2dR\cos\theta}}.$$
With $u=\cos\theta$:
$$\overline{1/r}=\frac12\int_{-1}^{1}\!\frac{du}{\sqrt{R^2+d^2+2dRu}}=\frac12\cd
ot\frac{1}{dR}\Big[\sqrt{R^2+d^2+2dRu}\Big]_{-1}^{1}=\frac{|R+d|-|R-d|}{2dR}.$$

- **If $d>R$**: $|R+d|-|R-d|=(R+d)-(d-R)=2R$, so $\overline{1/r}=\frac{2R}{2dR}=
\frac1d$.
- **If $d<R$**: $|R+d|-|R-d|=(R+d)-(R-d)=2d$, so $\overline{1/r}=\frac{2d}{2dR}=
\frac1R$.
- **If $d=R$**: both give $1/R=1/d$.

$$\boxed{\overline{1/r}=\frac{1}{\max\{d,R\}},\qquad d=\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}.}$$

Again this matches the mean-value property of the harmonic function $1/r$ (in 3D
): outside the sphere the mean equals the value at the center $1/d$; inside it e
quals $1/R$.

---

**Summary.** In both dimensions the answer is governed by whether the origin (th
e singularity of the harmonic function) lies outside or inside the surface:

| Function | Surface | Origin outside | Origin inside |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\ln r$ (2D) | circle, center dist. $c$, radius $R$ | $\ln c$ | $\ln R$ |
| $1/r$ (3D) | sphere, center dist. $d$, radius $R$ | $1/d$ | $1/R$ |
