## Solution

We compute $\psi\!\left(\frac{7}{8}\right) - \psi\!\left(\frac{3}{8}\right) - \s
qrt{2}\bigl[\pi + 2\ln(\sqrt{2}-1)\bigr]$ using **Gauss's digamma theorem**:

$$\psi\!\left(\frac{p}{q}\right) = -\gamma - \ln(2q) - \frac{\pi}{2}\cot\!\left(
\frac{\pi p}{q}\right) + 2\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor(q-1)/2\rfloor}\cos\!\left(\frac{2\
pi n p}{q}\right)\ln\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi n}{q}\right).$$

### Setting up the difference

For $q=8$, the sum runs over $n=1,2,3$ with
$$\sin\frac{\pi}{8}=\tfrac{\sqrt{2-\sqrt2}}{2},\quad \sin\frac{\pi}{4}=\tfrac{\s
qrt2}{2},\quad \sin\frac{3\pi}{8}=\tfrac{\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}}{2}.$$

Taking $\psi(7/8)-\psi(3/8)$, the $-\gamma-\ln 16$ terms cancel, leaving two par
ts.

**Cotangent part.** Since $\cot(7\pi/8)=-\cot(\pi/8)$ and $\cot(3\pi/8)=\tan(\pi
/8)$,

$$-\frac{\pi}{2}\bigl[\cot(7\pi/8)-\cot(3\pi/8)\bigr]=\frac{\pi}{2}\bigl[\cot(\p
i/8)+\tan(\pi/8)\bigr]=\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{\sin(\pi/4)}=\frac{\pi}{2}\cdo
t 2\sqrt2=\pi\sqrt2.$$

**Logarithmic sum part.** Since $\cos(7\pi n/4)=\cos(\pi n/4)$, we need $\cos(\p
i n/4)-\cos(3\pi n/4)$:

| $n$ | $\cos(\pi n/4)-\cos(3\pi n/4)$ |
|---|---|
| 1 | $\sqrt2$ |
| 2 | $0$ |
| 3 | $-\sqrt2$ |

So the sum contributes
$$2\sqrt2\bigl[\ln\sin(\pi/8)-\ln\sin(3\pi/8)\bigr]=2\sqrt2\ln\frac{\sin(\pi/8)}
{\sin(3\pi/8)}.$$

### Simplifying the sine ratio

$$\frac{\sin(\pi/8)}{\sin(3\pi/8)}=\sqrt{\frac{2-\sqrt2}{2+\sqrt2}}=\sqrt{\frac{
(2-\sqrt2)^2}{2}}=\frac{2-\sqrt2}{\sqrt2}=\sqrt2-1.$$

Hence the logarithmic part equals $2\sqrt2\ln(\sqrt2-1)$.

### Combining

$$\psi\!\left(\frac{7}{8}\right)-\psi\!\left(\frac{3}{8}\right)=\pi\sqrt2+2\sqrt
2\ln(\sqrt2-1)=\sqrt2\bigl[\pi+2\ln(\sqrt2-1)\bigr].$$

Therefore

$$\psi\!\left(\frac{7}{8}\right)-\psi\!\left(\frac{3}{8}\right)-\sqrt2\bigl[\pi+
2\ln(\sqrt2-1)\bigr]=\sqrt2\bigl[\pi+2\ln(\sqrt2-1)\bigr]-\sqrt2\bigl[\pi+2\ln(\
sqrt2-1)\bigr]=\boxed{0}.$$
