## Proof

Let $S = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$. The given expectation is
$$U(k) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[S^k\right].$$

**Step 1: Distribution of $S$.**

Since $X_1, \ldots, X_n$ are independent $N(0,\sigma^2)$ random variables, their
 sum is Gaussian with
$$\mathbb{E}[S] = \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}[X_i] = 0, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(S)
 = \sum_{i=1}^n \operatorname{Var}(X_i) = n\sigma^2.$$
Hence $S \sim N(0,\, n\sigma^2)$.

**Step 2: Fourth moment of a centered Gaussian.**

For $Z \sim N(0, v)$, the moments are $\mathbb{E}[Z^{2m}] = (2m-1)!!\, v^m$. In
particular,
$$\mathbb{E}[Z^4] = 3!!\, v^2 = 3v^2.$$

**Step 3: Apply to $S$.**

With $v = n\sigma^2$,
$$U(4) = \mathbb{E}[S^4] = 3(n\sigma^2)^2 = 3n^2\sigma^4.$$

**Verification via direct expansion (independent check).**

Expanding $S^4 = \left(\sum_i X_i\right)^4$ and taking expectations, using indep
endence, $\mathbb{E}[X_i]=0$, and $\mathbb{E}[X_i^2]=\sigma^2$, $\mathbb{E}[X_i^
4]=3\sigma^4$:

- Terms $\mathbb{E}[X_i^4]$: there are $n$ of them, each $3\sigma^4$, contributi
ng $3n\sigma^4$.
- Terms $\mathbb{E}[X_i^2 X_j^2]$ with $i\neq j$: there are $\binom{n}{2}\cdot 6
 = 3n(n-1)$ of them (the multinomial coefficient $\frac{4!}{2!\,2!}=6$), each eq
ual to $\sigma^2\cdot\sigma^2 = \sigma^4$, contributing $3n(n-1)\sigma^4$.
- All other terms contain an odd power of some $X_i$ and vanish by independence
and zero mean.

Total:
$$U(4) = 3n\sigma^4 + 3n(n-1)\sigma^4 = 3n^2\sigma^4.$$

Both methods agree.

$$\boxed{U(4) = 3\,n^2\,\sigma^4}$$
