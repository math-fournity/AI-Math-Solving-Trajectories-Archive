## Proof

**Claim.** The maximum number of points in $\mathbb{R}^n$ with all pairwise dist
ances equal to $1$ is $n+1$.

---

### Upper bound: at most $n+1$ points

Suppose $X = \{p_1, p_2, \dots, p_m\} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ satisfies $\|p_i -
 p_j\| = 1$ for all $i \neq j$. We show $m \le n+1$.

Set $v_i = p_i - p_m$ for $i = 1, \dots, m-1$. These are $m-1$ vectors in $\math
bb{R}^n$. We compute their Gram matrix. For each $i$:
$$\|v_i\|^2 = \|p_i - p_m\|^2 = 1.$$
For $i \neq j$ (both in $\{1,\dots,m-1\}$):
$$\|v_i - v_j\|^2 = \|p_i - p_j\|^2 = 1.$$
By the polarization identity,
$$v_i \cdot v_j = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\|v_i\|^2 + \|v_j\|^2 - \|v_i - v_j\|^2\bigr
) = \tfrac{1}{2}(1 + 1 - 1) = \tfrac{1}{2}.$$

So the Gram matrix $G = (v_i \cdot v_j)_{1 \le i,j \le m-1}$ has diagonal entrie
s $1$ and off-diagonal entries $\tfrac{1}{2}$. We can write
$$G = \tfrac{1}{2}\,I_{m-1} + \tfrac{1}{2}\,J_{m-1},$$
where $J_{m-1}$ is the $(m-1)\times(m-1)$ all-ones matrix. The eigenvalues of $J
_{m-1}$ are $m-1$ (once, for the all-ones eigenvector $\mathbf{1}$) and $0$ (wit
h multiplicity $m-2$, for vectors orthogonal to $\mathbf{1}$). Hence the eigenva
lues of $G$ are:
- $\tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{2}(m-1) = \tfrac{m}{2}$ (once),
- $\tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 0 = \tfrac{1}{2}$ (with multiplicity $m-2$).

All eigenvalues are strictly positive, so $G$ is positive definite and in partic
ular has full rank $m-1$. Since $G$ is the Gram matrix of $m-1$ vectors in $\mat
hbb{R}^n$, its rank cannot exceed $n$. Therefore
$$m - 1 \le n \quad\Longrightarrow\quad m \le n + 1.$$

---

### Lower bound: $n+1$ points are achievable

The vertices of a regular $n$-simplex provide an explicit construction. Place th
e $n+1$ vertices as the standard basis vectors $e_1, e_2, \dots, e_{n+1}$ in $\m
athbb{R}^{n+1}$, then project onto the hyperplane $H = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^{n+1}
: x_1 + \cdots + x_{n+1} = 1\}$, which is an $n$-dimensional affine subspace (is
ometric to $\mathbb{R}^n$). For $i \neq j$,
$$\|e_i - e_j\| = \sqrt{2},$$
so all pairwise distances are equal. Scaling by $1/\sqrt{2}$ makes every pairwis
e distance exactly $1$. This gives $n+1$ points in an $n$-dimensional space with
 all mutual distances equal to $1$.

---

### Conclusion

The upper bound shows $m \le n+1$, and the regular simplex construction shows $m
 = n+1$ is attained. Therefore the maximum number of elements in such a subset $
X \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is

$$\boxed{n+1}.$$
