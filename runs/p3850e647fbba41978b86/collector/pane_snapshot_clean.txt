## Proof

We use the Stirling-type asymptotic expansion of the central binomial coefficien
t. From
$$\log n! = \tfrac12\log(2\pi n)+n\log(n/e)+\tfrac{1}{12n}-\tfrac{1}{360n^3}+\cd
ots,$$
we obtain (the $1/n^2$ term cancels in the difference):
$$\log\binom{2n}{n}=2n\log 2-\tfrac12\log(\pi n)-\tfrac{1}{8n}+\tfrac{1}{192n^3}
+\cdots.$$
Hence
$$\frac{4^n}{\binom{2n}{n}}=\sqrt{\pi n}\,\exp\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{8n}-\tfrac{1}{192
n^3}+\cdots\Big).$$
Expanding the exponential:
$$\exp\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{8n}-\tfrac{1}{192n^3}\Big)=1+\tfrac{1}{8n}+\tfrac{1}{128n
^2}+\Big(\tfrac{1}{3072}-\tfrac{1}{192}\Big)\tfrac{1}{n^3}+\cdots=1+\tfrac{1}{8n
}+\tfrac{1}{128n^2}-\tfrac{5}{1024n^3}+\cdots,$$
so
$$\frac{4^n}{\binom{2n}{n}}=\sqrt{\pi n}\Big(1+\tfrac{1}{8n}+\tfrac{1}{128n^2}-\
tfrac{5}{1024n^3}+\cdots\Big).\tag{1}$$

Next, factor the square root:
$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{4n+c}}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt n}\Big(1+\tfrac{c}{4n}\Big)^{-1/2}=\frac
{1}{2\sqrt n}\Big(1-\tfrac{c}{8n}+\tfrac{3c^2}{128n^2}-\tfrac{5c^3}{1024n^3}+\cd
ots\Big).\tag{2}$$

Multiplying (1) and (2):
$$D_n(c)=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}\Big(1+\tfrac{1}{8n}+\tfrac{1}{128n^2}-\cdots\Big)\Bi
g(1-\tfrac{c}{8n}+\tfrac{3c^2}{128n^2}-\cdots\Big).$$

The coefficient of $1/n$ in the product is
$$\frac{1}{8}-\frac{c}{8}=\frac{1-c}{8}.$$

For fastest convergence we annihilate the leading error term, setting this coeff
icient to zero:
$$\frac{1-c}{8}=0\implies c=1.$$

With $c=1$, the $1/n$ term vanishes. The next term (coefficient of $1/n^2$) is
$$\tfrac{1}{128}+\tfrac{3}{128}+\tfrac{1}{8}\big(-\tfrac{1}{8}\big)=\tfrac{4}{12
8}-\tfrac{1}{64}=\tfrac{1}{32}-\tfrac{1}{64}=\tfrac{1}{64}\neq 0,$$
so the error is $O(1/n^2)$, which is the best achievable order: the single free
parameter $c$ can kill at most one error term, and killing the $1/n$ term (rathe
r than a higher-order one) yields the fastest overall rate.

Therefore
$$\boxed{c=1}$$
gives $D_n(1)=\dfrac{4^n}{\binom{2n}{n}\sqrt{4n+1}}=\dfrac{\sqrt\pi}{2}\Big(1+\d
