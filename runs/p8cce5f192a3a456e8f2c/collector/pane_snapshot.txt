## Solution

The statement is **FALSE**. The relative bicommutant is always a W*-algebra, but
 it need **not** equal the ultraweak closure of $A$.

### Counterexample

Let $\mathcal{M} = L^\infty([0,1])$, an abelian W*-algebra (acting on $L^2([0,1]
)$ by multiplication). Let $A = \mathbb{C}\cdot 1 \subseteq \mathcal{M}$, the sc
alar multiples of the unit. This is a unital *-subalgebra of $\mathcal{M}$.

**Step 1: Compute the relative commutant $A'$.**

$$A' = \{x \in \mathcal{M} : xa = ax \text{ for all } a \in A\}.$$

Since $A$ consists of scalars, every element of $\mathcal{M}$ commutes with ever
y element of $A$. Therefore $A' = \mathcal{M}$.

**Step 2: Compute the relative bicommutant $A''$.**

$$A'' = \{m \in \mathcal{M} : mx = xm \text{ for all } x \in A'\} = \{m \in \mat
hcal{M} : mx = xm \text{ for all } x \in \mathcal{M}\} = Z(\mathcal{M}).$$

Since $\mathcal{M} = L^\infty([0,1])$ is abelian, $Z(\mathcal{M}) = \mathcal{M}$
. Thus

$$A'' = \mathcal{M} = L^\infty([0,1]).$$

**Step 3: Compute the ultraweak closure of $A$.**

$A = \mathbb{C}\cdot 1$ is a one-dimensional (hence finite-dimensional) *-subalg
ebra. Finite-dimensional subspaces are closed in any Hausdorff locally convex to
pology, and the ultraweak topology on $\mathcal{M}$ is Hausdorff. Therefore the
ultraweak closure of $A$ is $A$ itself:

$$\overline{A}^{\text{ultraweak}} = \mathbb{C}\cdot 1.$$

**Step 4: Compare.**

$$A'' = L^\infty([0,1]) \neq \mathbb{C}\cdot 1 = \overline{A}^{\text{ultraweak}}
.$$

The two sets are distinct (e.g., the characteristic function $\mathbf{1}_{[0,1/2
]} \in A''$ but is not a scalar), so the claimed equality fails.

### Remark on the first clause

The first clause of the statement ("$A''$ is a W*-algebra") is in fact always tr
ue: $A''$ is an intersection of kernels of the ultraweakly continuous maps $m \m
apsto mx - xm$ (left/right multiplication by $x \in \mathcal{M}$ being ultraweak
ly continuous), hence is ultraweakly closed; it is a unital *-subalgebra; and an
 ultraweakly closed unital *-subalgebra of a W*-algebra is a W*-algebra. However
, the statement is a **conjunction**, and the second clause is false, so the ent
ire statement is false.

### Why the classical bicommutant theorem does not apply

Von Neumann's bicommutant theorem states that for a unital *-subalgebra $A \subs
eteq B(H)$, the bicommutant $A''$ **in $B(H)$** equals the weak/strong operator
closure of $A$. The crucial point is that the commutant is taken in the **full**
 algebra $B(H)$, which contains enough operators to separate points. When the co
mmutant is taken relative to a proper W*-subalgebra $\mathcal{M} \subsetneq B(H)
$, the commutant $A'_{\mathcal{M}} = A'_{B(H)} \cap \mathcal{M}$ can be strictly
 **larger** than $A'_{B(H)}$ (as in the example above, where $A'_{\mathcal{M}} =
 \mathcal{M}$ is much larger than $A'_{B(H)}$), making the bicommutant $A''_{\ma
thcal{M}}$ strictly larger than the ultraweak closure of $A$.
