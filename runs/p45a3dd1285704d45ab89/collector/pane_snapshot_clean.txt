## Solution

We evaluate
$$L = \lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^\infty \frac{e^{\sin(\ln x)}}{1+\sqrt{n}\,x^{42}}\
,dx.$$

**Key observation.** Since $e^{\sin(\ln x)} \le e$ for all $x>0$, we can bound t
he integrand uniformly and reduce the problem to a scaling computation.

**Step 1: Uniform bound.** For every $n\ge 1$ and every $x>0$,
$$0 \le \frac{e^{\sin(\ln x)}}{1+\sqrt{n}\,x^{42}} \le \frac{e}{1+\sqrt{n}\,x^{4
2}}.$$
Hence
$$0 \le \int_0^\infty \frac{e^{\sin(\ln x)}}{1+\sqrt{n}\,x^{42}}\,dx \le e\int_0
^\infty \frac{dx}{1+\sqrt{n}\,x^{42}}.$$

**Step 2: Scaling the comparison integral.** In $\int_0^\infty \frac{dx}{1+\sqrt
{n}\,x^{42}}$, substitute
$$x = n^{-1/84}\,t,\qquad dx = n^{-1/84}\,dt.$$
Then $\sqrt{n}\,x^{42} = n^{1/2}\cdot n^{-42/84}\,t^{42} = n^{1/2}\cdot n^{-1/2}
\,t^{42}=t^{42}$, so
$$\int_0^\infty \frac{dx}{1+\sqrt{n}\,x^{42}} = n^{-1/84}\int_0^\infty \frac{dt}
{1+t^{42}}.$$

**Step 3: The comparison integral is finite.** Since $42>1$, the integral
$$C := \int_0^\infty \frac{dt}{1+t^{42}}$$
converges (indeed $C = \frac{\pi/42}{\sin(\pi/42)}$ by the standard beta-integra
l formula, a finite positive constant).

**Step 4: Squeeze.** Combining Steps 1–3,
$$0 \le \int_0^\infty \frac{e^{\sin(\ln x)}}{1+\sqrt{n}\,x^{42}}\,dx \le e\,C\,n
^{-1/84}.$$
Since $n^{-1/84}\to 0$ as $n\to\infty$, by the squeeze theorem
$$L = 0.$$
