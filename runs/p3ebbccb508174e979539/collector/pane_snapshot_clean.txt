**Proof.**

Let $R = \prod_{n=1}^{\infty} \mathbb{F}_2$, the direct product of countably man

multiplication. This is a commutative ring with identity $1 = (1,1,1,\ldots)$.

**$R$ is infinite.** The elements $e_k \in R$ (with $1$ in the $k$-th coordinate
 and $0$ elsewhere) are pairwise distinct for $k = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$, so $R$ cont
ains infinitely many elements (in fact $|R| = 2^{\aleph_0}$).

**The polynomial.** Consider $f(t) = t^2 - t \in R[t]$, a polynomial of degree $
2$.

**Every element of $R$ is a root.** Let $a = (a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots) \in R$ be a
rbitrary, with each $a_i \in \{0, 1\}$. Then
$$f(a) = a^2 - a = (a_1^2 - a_1,\; a_2^2 - a_2,\; a_3^2 - a_3,\; \ldots).$$
In $\mathbb{F}_2$ we have $0^2 - 0 = 0$ and $1^2 - 1 = 0$, so $a_i^2 - a_i = 0$
for every $i$. Hence $f(a) = 0$.

**Conclusion.** Since $R$ is infinite and *every* element of $R$ is a root of th

ots in $R$. Therefore there exists a ring in which a finite degree polynomial ha
