## Semiclassical Principal Symbol of $h^2\Delta - 1$

### Setup and conventions

Let $D_{x_j} = \frac{1}{i}\partial_{x_j}$, so that $\partial_{x_j} = iD_{x_j}$ a
nd $\partial_{x_j}^2 = -D_{x_j}^2$. With $\Delta = -\sum_j \partial_{x_j}^2$, we
 obtain

$$\Delta = \sum_j D_{x_j}^2 = |D_x|^2.$$

In semiclassical quantization, a symbol $p(x,\xi;h) = p_0(x,\xi) + h\,p_1(x,\xi)
 + \cdots$ is quantized via $\operatorname{Op}_h(p)$, where the covariable $\xi$
 is the symbol of $hD_x$. The **semiclassical principal symbol** $\sigma_h(P)$ i
s the leading term $p_0(x,\xi)$ in the $h$-expansion of the full symbol.

### Computing the full symbol

Rewrite the operator in the semiclassical variables:

$$h^2\Delta = \sum_j (hD_{x_j})^2 = \operatorname{Op}_h\!\bigl(|\xi|^2\bigr),$$

since each $hD_{x_j}$ contributes the symbol $\xi_j$, and squaring gives $|\xi|^
2 = \sum_j \xi_j^2$. The constant $-1$ is the quantization of the symbol $-1$ (a
 function of $x,\xi$ constant in both). Therefore

$$h^2\Delta - 1 \;=\; \operatorname{Op}_h\!\bigl(|\xi|^2\bigr) - \operatorname{O
p}_h(1) \;=\; \operatorname{Op}_h\!\bigl(|\xi|^2 - 1\bigr).$$

The full symbol is $p(x,\xi;h) = |\xi|^2 - 1$, which is **independent of $h$** —
 there is no sub-leading $h$-term. Hence the $h$-expansion is just $p_0 = |\xi|^
2 - 1$ with $p_1 = p_2 = \cdots = 0$.

### Why the principal symbol is $|\xi|^2 - 1$, not $|\xi|^2$

The crucial point is what "principal" means in the **semiclassical** calculus ve
rsus the classical one.

- **Classical principal symbol** keeps only the highest *derivative order*. For
$\Delta$ (order $2$), this gives $|\xi|^2$; the zeroth-order term $-1$ is droppe
d because it is lower differential order.

- **Semiclassical principal symbol** keeps only the leading *power of $h$*. Both
 $h^2\Delta$ and the constant $-1$ are of order $h^0$ in the semiclassical expan
sion (multiplying $\Delta$ by $h^2$ cancels the two powers of $h$ that $hD_x$ ca
rries, leaving $|\xi|^2$ at $h^0$; the constant $-1$ is manifestly $h^0$). They
are therefore **the same $h$-order**, and the principal symbol must retain *both
*:

$$\sigma_h(h^2\Delta - 1) \;=\; |\xi|^2 \;-\; 1.$$

Dropping $-1$ would discard a term of the same semiclassical order as the kineti
c term, which is exactly the term that produces the characteristic variety $|\xi
|^2 = 1$ and hence the propagating modes of the semiclassical operator. Keeping
only $|\xi|^2$ would give the wrong characteristic set (it would never vanish fo
r real $\xi$, falsely suggesting the operator is elliptic).

### Answer

$$\boxed{\;\sigma_h(h^2\Delta - 1) \;=\; |\xi|^2 - 1\;}$$

The $-1$ survives in the semiclassical principal symbol because, after the $h^2$
 prefactor is absorbed into the quantization of $hD_x$, the potential term $-1$
and the kinetic term $|\xi|^2$ are of identical order $h^0$; the semiclassical p
rincipal symbol retains all $h^0$-order contributions, yielding $|\xi|^2 - 1$ ra
ther than $|\xi|^2$.
