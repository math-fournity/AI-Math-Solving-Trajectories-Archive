## Proof

**Claim:** $A_1 = A_2$.

Let $U := A_1 \cup B_1 = A_2 \cup B_2$. Both pairs $(A_i, B_i)$ partition $U$ in
to two disjoint open sets.

**Step 1: $A_1 \subseteq A_2$.**

Since $A_1 \subseteq U = A_2 \cup B_2$, we can write
$$A_1 = (A_1 \cap A_2) \cup (A_1 \cap B_2).$$
The sets $A_1 \cap A_2$ and $A_1 \cap B_2$ are open in the subspace topology of
$A_1$ (since $A_2, B_2$ are open in the Hilbert space), they are disjoint (since
 $A_2 \cap B_2 = \emptyset$), and their union is $A_1$.

Since $A_1$ is connected, one of these two sets must be empty. By condition (3),
 $0 \in A_1 \cap A_2$, so $A_1 \cap A_2 \neq \emptyset$. Therefore $A_1 \cap B_2
 = \emptyset$, which gives $A_1 \subseteq A_2$.

**Step 2: $A_2 \subseteq A_1$.**

By the symmetric argument: $A_2 \subseteq U = A_1 \cup B_1$, so
$$A_2 = (A_2 \cap A_1) \cup (A_2 \cap B_1).$$
These are disjoint open sets in $A_2$ whose union is $A_2$. Since $A_2$ is conne
cted, one is empty. Since $0 \in A_2 \cap A_1 \neq \emptyset$, we get $A_2 \cap
B_1 = \emptyset$, hence $A_2 \subseteq A_1$.

**Conclusion.** From Steps 1 and 2, $A_1 \subseteq A_2$ and $A_2 \subseteq A_1$,
 so $A_1 = A_2$. $\blacksquare$
