## Proof

**Yes**, $(f(y_n))$ necessarily converges to the same limit $q$.

**Key idea: interleave the two sequences.**

Since $i(x_n) \to p$ and $i(y_n) \to p$ in $X'$, define the interleaved sequence
 $(z_n)$ in $X$ by
$$z_{2n} = x_n, \qquad z_{2n+1} = y_n.$$

**Claim.** $i(z_n) \to p$ in $X'$.

*Proof of claim.* Let $\varepsilon > 0$. Since $i(x_n) \to p$, there exists $N_1
$ such that $d_{X'}(i(x_n), p) < \varepsilon$ for all $n \ge N_1$. Since $i(y_n)
 \to p$, there exists $N_2$ such that $d_{X'}(i(y_n), p) < \varepsilon$ for all
$n \ge N_2$. Set $N = \max(2N_1, 2N_2+1)$. For any $k \ge N$: if $k = 2n$ is eve
n, then $n \ge N_1$, so $d_{X'}(i(z_k), p) = d_{X'}(i(x_n), p) < \varepsilon$; i
f $k = 2n+1$ is odd, then $n \ge N_2$, so $d_{X'}(i(z_k), p) = d_{X'}(i(y_n), p)
 < \varepsilon$. Hence $i(z_n) \to p$. $\square$

By the hypothesis of the problem (convergence of $i(\cdot)$-images in $X'$ force
s convergence of $f(\cdot)$-images in $Y$), the sequence $f(z_n)$ converges to s
ome point $q' \in Y$.

Now $f(z_n)$ is the interleaving of the two subsequences $f(z_{2n}) = f(x_n)$ an
d $f(z_{2n+1}) = f(y_n)$. A basic fact about convergent sequences in metric spac
es: **a sequence converges to a limit if and only if both its even-indexed and o
dd-indexed subsequences converge to that same limit.** Therefore
$$f(x_n) \to q' \quad \text{and} \quad f(y_n) \to q'.$$

But the hypothesis also gives $f(x_n) \to q$. Since $Y$ is a metric space (hence
 Hausdorff), limits are unique, so $q' = q$.

Consequently,
$$f(y_n) \to q.$$

---

**Remark (the hypothesis is in fact automatic).** The assumed implication "$i(x_
n)$ converges in $X' \Rightarrow f(x_n)$ converges in $Y$" is not an extra condi
tion: it always holds in this setup. Indeed, if $i(x_n)$ converges in $X'$ then
it is Cauchy; since $i$ is an isometry, $(x_n)$ is Cauchy in $X$; since $f$ is a
n isometry, $f(x_n)$ is Cauchy in $Y$; and since $Y$ is complete, $f(x_n)$ conve
rges. So the argument above shows that the isometry $f : X \to Y$ extends to a w
ell-defined (indeed isometric) map $\bar f : X' \to Y$ sending $p = \lim i(x_n)$
 to $q = \lim f(x_n)$, with the limit independent of the representing sequence —
 exactly the uniqueness part of the universal property of the completion.
