## Proof

Let $a_n$ denote the number of permutations of $[n]$ whose cycle lengths are all
 distinct. The exponential generating function for a single $k$-cycle is $\frac{
(k-1)!}{k!}x^k = \frac{x^k}{k}$; since each cycle length is used at most once, t
he EGF is

$$p(x)=\prod_{k\ge 1}\left(1+\frac{x^k}{k}\right)=\sum_{n\ge 0}\frac{a_n}{n!}\,x
^n.$$

We determine the growth of $p(x)$ as $x\to 1^-$ in two steps: (i) find the limit
 of $a_n/n!$, then (ii) apply an Abelian theorem.

### Step 1. $\displaystyle \frac{a_n}{n!}\to e^{-\gamma}$ as $n\to\infty$.

For a uniform random permutation of $[n]$, let $C_k^{(n)}$ be the number of $k$-
cycles. A classical result (via the Chinese restaurant process / EGF) states tha
t for every fixed $m$, the vector $(C_1^{(n)},\dots,C_m^{(n)})$ converges jointl
y as $n\to\infty$ to independent Poisson random variables $Z_1,\dots,Z_m$ with $
\mathbb{E}Z_k=1/k$. Moreover the falling-factorial moments are exact: $\mathbb{E
}[(C_k^{(n)})_r]=1/k^r$ whenever $rk\le n$ (and $0$ otherwise).

The event "all cycle lengths are distinct" is $\{C_k^{(n)}\le 1\ \forall k\}$, w
hose probability is exactly $a_n/n!$.

**Truncation.** Fix $K$. Since $\{C_k\le 1\ \forall k\le K\}$ is a continuity se
t for the limiting joint distribution,

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\Pr\bigl(C_k^{(n)}\le 1\ \forall k\le K\bigr)=\prod_{k=1}^{K}
\Pr(Z_k\le 1)=\prod_{k=1}^{K}e^{-1/k}\Bigl(1+\frac1k\Bigr).$$

For the tail, using $\Pr(C_k^{(n)}\ge 2)\le \tfrac12\mathbb{E}[(C_k^{(n)})_2]=\t
frac{1}{2k^2}$ (valid for $2k\le n$; for $2k>n$ one has $C_k^{(n)}\le 1$ automat
ically),

$$\Pr\bigl(\exists\, k>K:\ C_k^{(n)}\ge 2\bigr)\le \sum_{k>K}\frac{1}{2k^2}\xrig
htarrow\to\infty]{}0,$$

uniformly in $n$ (for $n$ large). Combining,

$$\prod_{k=1}^{K}e^{-1/k}\Bigl(1+\frac1k\Bigr)-\sum_{k>K}\frac{1}{2k^2}\ \le\ \l
iminf_{n}\frac{a_n}{n!}\ \le\ \limsup_{n}\frac{a_n}{n!}\ \le\ \prod_{k=1}^{K}e^{
-1/k}\Bigl(1+\frac1k\Bigr).$$

Letting $K\to\infty$, both bounds converge to $\prod_{k\ge 1}e^{-1/k}(1+1/k)$. I
ts logarithm is the telescoping sum

$$\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Bigl[\log\Bigl(1+\frac1k\Bigr)-\frac1k\Bigr]=\log(N+1)-H_N\ \xr
ightarrow[N\to\infty]{}\ -\gamma,$$

since $H_N=\log N+\gamma+o(1)$. Hence

$$\frac{a_n}{n!}\longrightarrow e^{-\gamma}.$$

### Step 2. Abelian theorem.

Set $c_n=a_n/n!$. We have $c_n\to e^{-\gamma}$ and $0\le c_n\le 1$. Write

$$p(x)=\sum_{n\ge 0}c_n x^n=\sum_{n\ge 0}(c_n-e^{-\gamma})x^n+\frac{e^{-\gamma}}
{1-x}.$$

For any $\varepsilon>0$, choose $N$ so that $|c_n-e^{-\gamma}|<\varepsilon$ for
$n>N$. Then

$$(1-x)\Bigl|\sum_{n\ge 0}(c_n-e^{-\gamma})x^n\Bigr|\le (1-x)\sum_{n\le N}|c_n-e
^{-\gamma}|\,x^n+\varepsilon\ \xrightarrow[x\to 1^-]{}\ \varepsilon.$$

Since $\varepsilon>0$ is arbitrary, $(1-x)\sum(c_n-e^{-\gamma})x^n\to 0$, and th
erefore

$$p(x)\sim \frac{e^{-\gamma}}{1-x}\qquad (x\to 1^-).$$

Because $-\log x\sim 1-x$ as $x\to 1^-$, this is equivalently

$$\boxed{\,p(x)\sim \dfrac{e^{-\gamma}}{-\log x}\sim \dfrac{e^{-\gamma}}{1-x}\,,
\qquad x\to 1^-\,,}$$

where $\gamma=0.5771\ldots$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant. In particular $\lo
g p(x)=\log\frac{1}{1-x}-\gamma+o(1)$.
