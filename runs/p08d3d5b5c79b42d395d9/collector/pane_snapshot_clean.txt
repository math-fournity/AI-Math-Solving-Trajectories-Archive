# Fourier-Bessel Series Expansion on the Unit Disk

## Setup

Let $f$ be a function defined on the unit disk $\mathbb{D} = \{(r,\theta) : 0 \l
e r \le 1,\ 0 \le \theta < 2\pi\}$, expressed in polar coordinates $(r,\theta)$.
 We seek an expansion of $f$ in terms of the eigenfunctions of the Laplacian on
the disk with Dirichlet boundary conditions $f(1,\theta)=0$ (the standard settin
g for the Fourier–Bessel series).

The Laplacian in polar coordinates is
$$
\Delta f = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial r^2} + \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial f}{\par
tial r} + \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial \theta^2}.
$$

## Separation of Variables

We look for eigenfunctions $\Phi(r,\theta)$ satisfying $\Delta \Phi + \lambda \P
hi = 0$ with $\Phi(1,\theta)=0$ and $\Phi$ regular at $r=0$. Set $\Phi(r,\theta)
 = R(r)\Theta(\theta)$.

**Angular part.** Periodicity $\Theta(\theta+2\pi)=\Theta(\theta)$ forces
$$
\Theta_m(\theta) = e^{im\theta}, \qquad m \in \mathbb{Z},
$$
with separation constant $m^2$.

**Radial part.** The radial equation becomes
$$
r^2 R'' + r R' + (\lambda r^2 - m^2)R = 0.
$$
This is Bessel's equation of order $m$. Regularity at $r=0$ selects the Bessel f
unction of the first kind $J_m(\sqrt{\lambda}\,r)$. The boundary condition $R(1)
=0$ requires $J_m(\sqrt{\lambda})=0$, so $\sqrt{\lambda}$ must be a positive zer
o of $J_m$. Denoting the positive zeros of $J_m$ by
$$
0 < j_{m,1} < j_{m,2} < j_{m,3} < \cdots,
$$
we obtain eigenvalues $\lambda_{m,n} = j_{m,n}^2$ and radial eigenfunctions $J_m
(j_{m,n}\,r)$.

## Orthogonality

The eigenfunctions $\Phi_{m,n}(r,\theta) = J_m(j_{m,n}\,r)\,e^{im\theta}$ are or
thogonal on the disk with respect to the area measure $r\,dr\,d\theta$:

- **Angular orthogonality:** $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} e^{im\theta}\,e^{-im'\t
heta}\,d\theta = 2\pi\,\delta_{mm'}.$
- **Radial orthogonality:** From the Sturm–Liouville form of Bessel's equation,
for fixed $m$,
$$
\int_0^1 J_m(j_{m,n}\,r)\,J_m(j_{m,n'}\,r)\,r\,dr = \frac{1}{2}\bigl[J_{m+1}(j_{
m,n})\bigr]^2 \delta_{nn'}.
$$

Combining these, the full orthogonality on the disk is
$$
\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^{2\pi} \Phi_{m,n}\,\overline{\Phi_{m',n'}}\;r\,d\theta\,dr
= \pi\,\bigl[J_{m+1}(j_{m,n})\bigr]^2\,\delta_{mm'}\,\delta_{nn'}.
$$

## The Expansion

Assuming $f\in L^2(\mathbb{D})$ (with the Dirichlet boundary condition $f(1,\the
ta)=0$ for pointwise convergence at the boundary), we expand
$$
\boxed{\;f(r,\theta) \;=\; \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} c_{m,n}\
,J_m\!\bigl(j_{m,n}\,r\bigr)\,e^{im\theta}\;}
$$
where the Fourier–Bessel coefficients are obtained by projection:
$$
\boxed{\;c_{m,n} \;=\; \frac{1}{\pi\,\bigl[J_{m+1}(j_{m,n})\bigr]^2}\int_0^1\!\!
\int_0^{2\pi} f(r,\theta)\,J_m\!\bigl(j_{m,n}\,r\bigr)\,e^{-im\theta}\;r\,d\thet
a\,dr.\;}
$$

## Real (Trigonometric) Form

Equivalently, using $e^{im\theta}=\cos(m\theta)+i\sin(m\theta)$ and pairing $\pm
 m$ terms, one writes the expansion in real form. For $m=0$:
$$
f(r,\theta) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_{0,n}\,J_0(j_{0,n}\,r),
$$
and for $m\ge 1$:
$$
+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Bigl[a_{m,n}\cos(m\theta)+b_{m,n}\sin(m\
theta)\Bigr]J_m(j_{m,n}\,r),
$$
with
$$
a_{m,n} = \frac{2}{\pi[J_{m+1}(j_{m,n})]^2}\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^{2\pi} f(r,\theta)
\,J_m(j_{m,n}\,r)\cos(m\theta)\,r\,d\theta\,dr,
$$
$$
b_{m,n} = \frac{2}{\pi[J_{m+1}(j_{m,n})]^2}\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^{2\pi} f(r,\theta)
\,J_m(j_{m,n}\,r)\sin(m\theta)\,r\,d\theta\,dr,
$$
and
$$
a_{0,n} = \frac{1}{\pi[J_{1}(j_{0,n})]^2}\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^{2\pi} f(r,\theta)\,
J_0(j_{0,n}\,r)\,r\,d\theta\,dr.
$$

## Convergence

The functions $\{\Phi_{m,n}\}$ form a complete orthogonal system in $L^2(\mathbb
{D})$ (completeness follows from the spectral theorem for the self-adjoint Diric
hlet Laplacian on the disk). Therefore:

- For $f\in L^2(\mathbb{D})$, the series converges to $f$ in the $L^2$-norm.
- If $f$ is sufficiently smooth (e.g., $C^2$ on the closed disk and vanishing ap
propriately at the boundary), the series converges pointwise/uniformly to $f$ in
 the interior, with the usual Gibbs-type behavior at the boundary $r=1$.

## Summary

The Fourier–Bessel series of $f$ on the unit disk is the double expansion
$$
f(r,\theta) = \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} c_{m,n}\,J_m(j_{m,n}\
,r)\,e^{im\theta},
$$
with coefficients
$$
c_{m,n} = \frac{1}{\pi\,[J_{m+1}(j_{m,n})]^2}\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^{2\pi} f(r,\thet
a)\,J_m(j_{m,n}\,r)\,e^{-im\theta}\,r\,d\theta\,dr,
$$
where $j_{m,n}$ is the $n$-th positive zero of the Bessel function $J_m$. The an
gular factor $e^{im\theta}$ provides the Fourier series in $\theta$, and the rad
ial factor $J_m(j_{m,n}\,r)$ provides the Bessel series in $r$ — together yieldi
ng the complete spectral decomposition of $f$ on the disk.
