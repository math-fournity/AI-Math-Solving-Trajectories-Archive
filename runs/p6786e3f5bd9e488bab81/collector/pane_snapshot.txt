Looking at this problem, I need to construct two irreducible continuous parametr
izations of the same subset of ℝ², both rectifiable, with different lengths.

**Key idea:** Use a "T"-shaped image with branches of *unequal* lengths. A T-sha
pe has three branches meeting at a junction. Any irreducible parametrization mus
t start at one branch-tip and end at another, forcing the third branch to be tra
versed twice (out and back). By choosing which branch is traversed twice, we get
 different total lengths.

---

## Construction

Let the common image be the "T"-shaped set

$$S = \bigl([-3,1]\times\{0\}\bigr) \;\cup\; \bigl(\{0\}\times[0,1]\bigr),$$

i.e., a horizontal segment from $(-3,0)$ to $(1,0)$ and a vertical segment from
$(0,0)$ to $(0,1)$, meeting at the junction $(0,0)$. The three branches have len
gths $3$ (left), $1$ (right), and $1$ (up).

### Parametrization $f:[0,6]\to\mathbb{R}^2$

$$f(t)=\begin{cases}(t-3,\; 0), & t\in[0,4],\\(5-t,\; 0), & t\in[4,5],\\(0,\; t-
5), & t\in[5,6].\end{cases}$$

This traces: $(-3,0)\to(1,0)\to(0,0)\to(0,1)$.

- Segment $[0,4]$: full horizontal sweep, length $4$.
- Segment $[4,5]$: backtrack from $(1,0)$ to $(0,0)$ (retracing the right branch
), length $1$.
- Segment $[5,6]$: vertical branch from $(0,0)$ to $(0,1)$, length $1$.

**Length of $f$** $= 4+1+1 = 6$.

### Parametrization $g:[0,8]\to\mathbb{R}^2$

$$g(t)=\begin{cases}(1-t,\; 0), & t\in[0,4],\\(t-7,\; 0), & t\in[4,7],\\(0,\; t-
7), & t\in[7,8].\end{cases}$$

This traces: $(1,0)\to(-3,0)\to(0,0)\to(0,1)$.

- Segment $[0,4]$: full horizontal sweep (right to left), length $4$.
- Segment $[4,7]$: backtrack from $(-3,0)$ to $(0,0)$ (retracing the left branch
), length $3$.
- Segment $[7,8]$: vertical branch from $(0,0)$ to $(0,1)$, length $1$.

**Length of $g$** $= 4+3+1 = 8$.

---

## Verification

**Same image.** Both $f$ and $g$ traverse the entire horizontal segment $[-3,1]\
times\{0\}$ (in their first piece) and the entire vertical segment $\{0\}\times[
0,1]$ (in their last piece). Hence $f([0,6])=g([0,8])=S$.

**Continuity.** At each break point the pieces agree: $f(4)=(1,0)$ from both sid
es; $f(5)=(0,0)$ from both sides. Same for $g$ at $t=4$ and $t=7$. So $f$ and $g
$ are continuous.

**Rectifiable.** Both are piecewise linear (polygonal), hence rectifiable.

**Irreducibility of $f$.** Suppose $[c,d]\subseteq[0,6]$ with $f([c,d])=S$. The
full horizontal segment $[-3,1]\times\{0\}$ is attained only during $[0,4]$ (aft
er $t=4$, $f$ stays in $[0,1]\times\{0\}\cup\{0\}\times[0,1]$, which does not in
clude $(-3,0)$). So $[c,d]\supseteq[0,4]$, giving $c\le 0$ and $d\ge 4$, hence $
c=0$. The full vertical segment $\{0\}\times[0,1]$ is attained only during $[5,6
]$ (before $t=5$, $f$ has zero second coordinate). So $[c,d]\supseteq[5,6]$, giv
ing $d\ge 6$, hence $d=6$. Therefore $[c,d]=[0,6]$, and $f$ is irreducible.

**Irreducibility of $g$.** Suppose $[c,d]\subseteq[0,8]$ with $g([c,d])=S$. The
full horizontal $[-3,1]\times\{0\}$ is attained only during $[0,4]$ (after $t=4$
, $g$ stays in $[-3,0]\times\{0\}\cup\{0\}\times[0,1]$, missing $(1,0)$). So $c=
0$ and $d\ge 4$. The full vertical $\{0\}\times[0,1]$ is attained only during $[
7,8]$. So $d\ge 8$, hence $d=8$. Therefore $[c,d]=[0,8]$, and $g$ is irreducible
.

**Different lengths.** $L(f)=6\ne 8=L(g)$. $\square$

---

**Remark.** The mechanism is transparent: the T-shape has three branches of leng
ths $3,1,1$. An irreducible parametrization must start at one branch-tip and end
 at another, forcing the remaining branch to be traversed twice. In $f$, the rig
ht branch (length $1$) is doubled; in $g$, the left branch (length $3$) is doubl
ed. The unequal branch lengths yield different totals.
