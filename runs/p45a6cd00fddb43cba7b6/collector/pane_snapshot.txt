## Analysis

The statement claims that the coordinatewise-convergence characterization of net
 convergence holds in **both** the product topology and the box topology. I'll s
how it is true for the product topology but **false** for the box topology, maki
ng the overall statement **false**.

---

## Proof that the statement is FALSE

### Part A: The characterization holds in the product topology (true)

**($\Rightarrow$)** Each projection $p_i : \prod_j X_j \to X_i$ is continuous (t
he preimage of open $U_i \subseteq X_i$ is $\prod_j V_j$ with $V_i = U_i$, $V_j
= X_j$ for $j \neq i$, which is product-open). Continuous maps preserve net conv
ergence, so $x_\alpha \to x$ implies $p_i(x_\alpha) \to p_i(x)$ for every $i$.

**($\Leftarrow$)** Let $U$ be a product-neighborhood of $x$. It contains a basic
 open set $B = \prod_i U_i$ with $U_i = X_i$ for all $i \notin F$, where $F \sub
seteq I$ is **finite**, and $x_i \in U_i$ for $i \in F$. For each $i \in F$, sin
ce $p_i(x_\alpha) \to p_i(x)$, there is $\alpha_i \in \Lambda$ with $p_i(x_\alph
a) \in U_i$ for all $\alpha \geq \alpha_i$. Since $\Lambda$ is directed and $F$
is finite, there exists $\alpha_0 \geq \alpha_i$ for all $i \in F$. Then for all
 $\alpha \geq \alpha_0$ and all $i \in F$, $p_i(x_\alpha) \in U_i$; for $i \noti
n F$, $U_i = X_i$ so the condition is automatic. Hence $x_\alpha \in B \subseteq
 U$ for all $\alpha \geq \alpha_0$. So $x_\alpha \to x$.

The key point: **finiteness of $F$** lets us take an upper bound of finitely man
y $\alpha_i$ in the directed set.

---

### Part B: The characterization FAILS in the box topology (false)

**($\Rightarrow$)** Still holds: projections remain continuous in the box topolo
gy (the same preimage argument works, since $\prod_j V_j$ with $V_j = X_j$ for a
ll but one $j$ is box-open). So forward direction is fine.

**($\Leftarrow$) fails.** In the box topology, a basic neighborhood of $x$ is $U
 = \prod_{i \in I} U_i$ where **every** $U_i$ is a proper open neighborhood of $
x_i$ in $X_i$ (no finiteness restriction). Coordinatewise convergence gives, for
 each $i$, an index $\alpha_i$ with $p_i(x_\alpha) \in U_i$ for $\alpha \geq \al
pha_i$. To conclude $x_\alpha \in U$ eventually, we need a **single** $\alpha_0$
 with $\alpha_0 \geq \alpha_i$ for **all** $i \in I$ simultaneously. When $I$ is
 infinite, this requires an upper bound for an infinite family $\{\alpha_i : i \
in I\}$ in the directed set $\Lambda$ — which a directed set need not provide.

#### Explicit counterexample

Let $I = \mathbb{N}$. For each $i \in \mathbb{N}$, let $X_i = \{0, 1\}$ with the
 discrete topology. Take the directed set $\Lambda = \mathbb{N}$ with the usual
order. Define the net $(x_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ in $X = \prod_{i \in \mathbb{N}
} X_i$ by

$$
x_n(i) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } i \leq n, \\ 0 & \text{if } i > n. \end{ca
ses}
$$

Let $x = (1, 1, 1, \dots)$, i.e., $x(i) = 1$ for all $i$.

**Coordinatewise convergence holds.** Fix $i \in \mathbb{N}$. For all $n \geq i$
, $x_n(i) = 1 = x(i)$. So $p_i(x_n) \to p_i(x)$ in $X_i$ for every $i$.

**But $x_n \not\to x$ in the box topology.** Consider the box-open neighborhood

$$
U = \prod_{i \in \mathbb{N}} \{1\}
$$

of $x$. Each factor $\{1\}$ is open in the discrete topology, so $U$ is box-open
, and $x \in U$. However, for every $n$, we have $x_n(n+1) = 0 \notin \{1\}$, so
 $x_n \notin U$ for all $n$. Thus no tail of the net lies in $U$, and $x_n \not\
to x$ in the box topology.

This gives coordinatewise convergence **without** box-topology convergence, cont
radicting the "$\Leftarrow$" direction for the box topology.

---

### Conclusion

The characterization "$x_\alpha \to x$ iff $p_i(x_\alpha) \to p_i(x)$ for every
$i$" is **valid in the product topology** but **invalid in the box topology** (t
he reverse implication fails when the index set $I$ is infinite). Since the stat
ement asserts validity in **both** topologies, the statement is **false**.
