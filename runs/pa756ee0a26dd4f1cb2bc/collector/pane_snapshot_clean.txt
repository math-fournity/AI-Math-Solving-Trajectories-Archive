## Proof

Let the five-digit integer be $N = \overline{abcde}$, where $a \in \{1,\dots,9\}
$ and $b,c,d,e \in \{0,\dots,9\}$. Its reverse is $N' = \overline{edcba}$.

**Step 1: Compute the difference.**

$$N - N' = (10000a + 1000b + 100c + 10d + e) - (10000e + 1000d + 100c + 10b + a)
$$

$$= 9999a + 990b - 990d - 9999e = 9999(a-e) + 990(b-d)$$

$$= 99\bigl(101(a-e) + 10(b-d)\bigr).$$

So every such difference is a multiple of $99 = 9 \times 11 = 3^2 \times 11$. He
nce both $3$ and $11$ always divide the difference (whenever it is nonzero).

**Step 2: Show no prime larger than $11$ always divides the difference.**

We prove the greatest common divisor of all nonzero differences is exactly $99$,
 by exhibiting two differences whose $m$-values are coprime.

Let $m = 101(a-e) + 10(b-d)$, so $N - N' = 99m$.

- Choose $a = e = 1,\ b = 1,\ d = 0$ (and any $c$): then $m = 101\cdot 0 + 10\cd
ot 1 = 10$.
  Concretely $N = 11001$, $N' = 10011$, $N - N' = 990 = 99 \times 10$.

- Choose $a = 2,\ e = 1,\ b = d = 0$ (and any $c$): then $m = 101\cdot 1 + 10\cd
ot 0 = 101$.
  Concretely $N = 20001$, $N' = 10002$, $N - N' = 9999 = 99 \times 101$.

Since $\gcd(10, 101) = 1$, the gcd of all achievable $m$-values is $1$, and ther
efore

$$\gcd\{\,N - N' : N \neq N'\,\} = 99 \cdot 1 = 99 = 3^2 \times 11.$$

**Step 3: Conclude.**

The primes that divide *every* nonzero difference are exactly the prime factors
of $99$, namely $3$ and $11$. The largest among them is $11$.

$$\boxed{11}$$
