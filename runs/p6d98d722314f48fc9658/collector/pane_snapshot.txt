## Proof

We show no prime $p$ and integer $a$ satisfy the equation.

### Step 1: $p$ is odd and $a$ is even

Since $4 \mid 1998!$, we have $1998! \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$.

**$p = 2$ is impossible:** Then $p^{1998} \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$, so LHS $\equiv 0 \
pmod{4}$. The RHS $= a^{2016}+1$ is $\equiv 1$ (if $a$ even) or $\equiv 2$ (if $
a$ odd) mod $4$ — never $0$. Contradiction.

**$p$ odd forces $a$ even:** Then $p^{1998} \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$, so LHS $\equiv 1
 \pmod{4}$. RHS $\equiv 1 \pmod{4}$ requires $a$ even (if $a$ odd, RHS $\equiv 2
$). ✓

### Step 2: 2-adic valuation forces $p \geq 2^{1989}-1$

Compute $v_2(1998!) = \lfloor 1998/2\rfloor + \lfloor 1998/4\rfloor + \cdots = 9
99+499+249+124+62+31+15+7+3+1 = 1990$.

Since $a$ is even, $v_2(a^{2016}) \geq 2016 > 1990$. From $a^{2016} - 1998! = p^
{1998}-1$:

$$v_2(p^{1998}-1) = v_2(a^{2016}-1998!) = \min(2016, 1990) = 1990.$$

By the Lifting the Exponent Lemma (odd $p$, even exponent $1998 = 2\cdot 999$):

$$v_2(p^{1998}-1) = v_2(p-1)+v_2(p+1)+v_2(1998)-1 = v_2(p-1)+v_2(p+1).$$

Since $p$ is odd, $p-1$ and $p+1$ are consecutive even numbers: one has $v_2=1$,
 the other $v_2\geq 2$. For the sum to be $1990$, the larger has $v_2 = 1989$, s
o $2^{1989}\mid(p-1)$ or $2^{1989}\mid(p+1)$. Therefore:

$$p \geq 2^{1989}-1 \geq 2^{1988}.$$

### Step 3: Size comparison — contradiction

Rewrite: $a^{2016} - p^{1998} = 1998! - 1$.

Note $a \neq 0$ (else $1998!+p^{1998}=1$, impossible). Set $X = a^{112} = |a|^{1
12} > 0$ and $Y = p^{111} > 0$. Since $2016 = 18\times 112$ and $1998 = 18\times
 111$:

$$X^{18} - Y^{18} = 1998! - 1.$$

Since $1998! > 1$, we get $X > Y$. If $X \neq Y$, then $X \geq Y+1$ (integers),
and:

$$1998! - 1 = (X-Y)\bigl(X^{17}+X^{16}Y+\cdots+Y^{17}\bigr) \geq 1 \cdot Y^{17}
= p^{1887}.$$

**Upper bound on $1998!$:** $1998! < 1998^{1998} < 2048^{1998} = 2^{11 \cdot 199
8} = 2^{21{,}978}$.

**Lower bound on $p^{1887}$:** $p^{1887} \geq (2^{1988})^{1887} = 2^{1988 \times
 1887} = 2^{3{,}751{,}356}$.

Since $3{,}751{,}356 \gg 21{,}978$:

$$p^{1887} \geq 2^{3{,}751{,}356} > 2^{21{,}978} > 1998! > 1998!-1,$$

contradicting $1998!-1 \geq p^{1887}$.

Therefore $X = Y$, i.e., $a^{112} = p^{111}$, giving $a^{2016} = p^{1998}$ and t
hus $1998! = 1$ — a contradiction.

### Conclusion

No prime $p$ and integer $a$ satisfy $1998! + p^{1998} = a^{2016} + 1$. The solu
tion set is **empty**. $\blacksquare$
