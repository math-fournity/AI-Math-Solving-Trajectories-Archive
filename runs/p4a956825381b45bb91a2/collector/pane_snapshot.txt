## Solution

**Setting up the prism geometry.**

The equilateral triangular base has side 4 cm and height $2\sqrt{3}$ cm. The pis
ton is parallel to one lateral face (corresponding to one side of the triangle),
 starting at the opposite vertex ($w = 2\sqrt{3}$) and moving toward the face ($
w \to 0$). The prism extends 3 cm along its axis.

At distance $y$ from the lateral face, the triangle's cross-section has width $4
\!\left(1 - \frac{y}{2\sqrt{3}}\right)$, so the piston-face area is:
$$A(y) = 3 \cdot 4\!\left(1 - \frac{y}{2\sqrt{3}}\right) = 12\!\left(1 - \frac{y
}{2\sqrt{3}}\right)$$

The volume between the face and the piston at width $w$:
$$V_{\text{prism}}(w) = \int_0^w 12\!\left(1 - \frac{y}{2\sqrt{3}}\right)dy = 12
w - \sqrt{3}\,w^2$$

*Check:* at $w = 2\sqrt{3}$, $V = 12\cdot 2\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{3}\cdot 12 = 12\sqrt
{3}$, which equals the full prism volume $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\cdot 16 \cdot 3 = 1
2\sqrt{3}$. ✓

**Rate of volume displaced.**

$$\frac{dV_{\text{prism}}}{dw} = 12 - 2\sqrt{3}\,w$$

At $w = 2\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2}$:
$$\frac{dV_{\text{prism}}}{dw} = 12 - 2\sqrt{3}(2\sqrt{3}-\sqrt{2}) = 12 - 12 +
2\sqrt{6} = 2\sqrt{6}$$

Since $w$ is decreasing at $1$ cm/s ($\frac{dw}{dt} = -1$), the rate of water di
splaced into the tank:
$$\frac{dV}{dt} = -\frac{dV_{\text{prism}}}{dt} = -\frac{dV_{\text{prism}}}{dw}\
cdot\frac{dw}{dt} = -(2\sqrt{6})(-1) = 2\sqrt{6}\ \text{cm}^3/\text{s}$$

**Finding the current water height $h$ in the pyramid.**

The total volume displaced from the start ($w=2\sqrt{3}$) to the current positio
n:

$$V_{\text{prism}}(2\sqrt{3}-\sqrt{2}) = 12(2\sqrt{3}-\sqrt{2}) - \sqrt{3}(2\sqr
t{3}-\sqrt{2})^2$$

Computing $(2\sqrt{3}-\sqrt{2})^2 = 12 - 4\sqrt{6} + 2 = 14 - 4\sqrt{6}$:

$$= 24\sqrt{3} - 12\sqrt{2} - \sqrt{3}(14-4\sqrt{6}) = 24\sqrt{3} - 12\sqrt{2} -
 14\sqrt{3} + 12\sqrt{2} = 10\sqrt{3}$$

So the displaced volume entering the pyramid:
$$\Delta V = 12\sqrt{3} - 10\sqrt{3} = 2\sqrt{3}\ \text{cm}^3$$

**Pyramid volume as a function of water height.**

The inverted square pyramid has height $3\sqrt{3}$ and top side 4. At height $h$
 from the apex, the square cross-section has side $s(h) = 4\cdot\frac{h}{3\sqrt{
3}} = \frac{4h}{3\sqrt{3}}$.

$$V_{\text{pyr}}(h) = \int_0^h \left(\frac{4t}{3\sqrt{3}}\right)^2 dt = \frac{16
}{27}\cdot\frac{h^3}{3} = \frac{16h^3}{81}$$

Setting $V_{\text{pyr}}(h) = 2\sqrt{3}$:
$$\frac{16h^3}{81} = 2\sqrt{3} \implies h^3 = \frac{81\sqrt{3}}{8} \implies h =
\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}$$

*Check:* $\left(\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^3 = \frac{27\cdot 3\sqrt{3}}{8} = \fr
ac{81\sqrt{3}}{8}$. ✓

**Computing $d = \frac{dh}{dt}$.**

$$\frac{dV_{\text{pyr}}}{dh} = \frac{16h^2}{27} = \frac{16}{27}\cdot\frac{27}{4}
 = 4$$

By the chain rule, the inflow rate equals the pyramid's volume growth rate:
$$\frac{dV_{\text{pyr}}}{dt} = \frac{dV_{\text{pyr}}}{dh}\cdot\frac{dh}{dt} = 4d
 = 2\sqrt{6}$$

$$\boxed{d = \frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}\ \text{cm/s}}$$
