Let $f(x,y) = (x^2+y^2)^{-3/4}$, defined and non-negative on $\mathbb{R}^2\setmi
nus\{0\}$. Let $D = \{(x,y): x^2+y^2\le 1\}$ be the closed unit disk and $S_c =
[-c,c]\times[-c,c]$. The region of integration is $R_c = D\setminus S_c$.

**Step 1: $f$ is integrable over $D$.**

Switch to polar coordinates: $x = r\cos\theta$, $y = r\sin\theta$, $x^2+y^2 = r^
2$, $dA = r\,dr\,d\theta$. Then
$$
(x^2+y^2)^{3/4} = (r^2)^{3/4} = r^{3/2},
$$
so
$$
f\,dA = r^{-3/2}\cdot r\,dr\,d\theta = r^{-1/2}\,dr\,d\theta.
$$
The integral over the full disk is
$$
\iint_D f\,dA = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^1 r^{-1/2}\,dr\,d\theta
= 2\pi\cdot\Big[2r^{1/2}\Big]_0^1
= 2\pi\cdot 2 = 4\pi < \infty.
$$
Hence $f\in L^1(D)$; the singularity at the origin is integrable.

**Step 2: The limit equals the integral over $D$.**

As $c\searrow 0$, the squares $S_c$ shrink monotonically: $S_{c_2}\subsetneq S_{
c_1}$ for $0<c_2<c_1$. Consequently the regions $R_c = D\setminus S_c$ increase
monotonically:
$$
R_c \uparrow \bigcup_{c>0} R_c = D\setminus\{0\}.
$$
Since $f\ge 0$ and $f\in L^1(D)$, the monotone convergence theorem gives
$$
\lim_{c\to 0^+}\iint_{R_c} f\,dA
= \iint_{D\setminus\{0\}} f\,dA
= \iint_D f\,dA,
$$
where the last equality uses that $\{0\}$ has Lebesgue measure zero.

**Step 3: Value.**

By the computation in Step 1,
$$
\lim_{c\to 0}\iint_R \frac{1}{(x^2+y^2)^{3/4}}\,dA = 4\pi.
$$
