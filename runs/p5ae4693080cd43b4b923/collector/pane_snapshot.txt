## Solution

We evaluate
$$L = \lim_{n \to \infty} \int_0^{\pi/2 - \epsilon} \frac{(\sin x)^n}{1 - \sin x
}\,dx,$$
where $\epsilon > 0$ is a fixed small constant.

**Key observation.** On the closed interval $[0,\, \pi/2 - \epsilon]$, the funct
ion $\sin x$ is increasing, so
$$\sin x \le \sin\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2} - \epsilon\right) = \cos\epsilon \quad \
text{for all } x \in [0,\, \pi/2-\epsilon].$$
Since $0 < \epsilon < \pi/2$, we have $0 < \cos\epsilon < 1$. Denote $r := \cos\
epsilon \in (0,1)$.

**Bounding the integrand.** For every $x \in [0, \pi/2 - \epsilon]$ and every $n
 \ge 0$:
$$0 \le \frac{(\sin x)^n}{1 - \sin x} \le \frac{r^n}{1 - r},$$
because:
- $(\sin x)^n \le r^n$ (since $0 \le \sin x \le r$),
- $1 - \sin x \ge 1 - r > 0$ (since $\sin x \le r$).

**Integrating the bound.** The interval has length $\pi/2 - \epsilon$, so
$$0 \le \int_0^{\pi/2-\epsilon} \frac{(\sin x)^n}{1-\sin x}\,dx \le \frac{r^n}{1
-r}\cdot\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\epsilon\right).$$

**Taking the limit.** Since $0 < r < 1$, we have $r^n \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$.
The factor $\frac{1}{1-r}\bigl(\frac{\pi}{2}-\epsilon\bigr)$ is a finite constan
t (independent of $n$). Therefore, by the squeeze theorem:
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} \int_0^{\pi/2-\epsilon} \frac{(\sin x)^n}{1-\sin x}\,dx = 0.
$$

**Remark on why the cutoff matters.** Without the cutoff (i.e., integrating to $
\pi/2$), the integral would diverge for every $n$: near $x = \pi/2$, writing $t
= \pi/2 - x \to 0^+$, we have $1 - \sin x = 1 - \cos t \sim t^2/2$ while $(\sin
x)^n \to 1$, so the integrand behaves like $2/t^2$, which is not integrable. The
 cutoff at $\pi/2 - \epsilon$ keeps $\sin x$ bounded away from $1$, making both
the integral finite and the limit well-defined.

$$\boxed{L = 0}$$
