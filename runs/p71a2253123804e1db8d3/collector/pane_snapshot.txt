## Solution

**Setup.** We want the maximum number of regions formed by 10 circles and 10 lin
es in the plane, assuming general position (every pair of circles meets in 2 dis
tinct points, every pair of lines meets in 1 point, every circle–line pair meets
 in 2 points, and no three curves pass through a common point).

**Compactification to the sphere.** We compactify the plane to the Riemann spher
e by adding a point at infinity $P$. Under this compactification:
- Each line becomes a circle passing through $P$.
- Each (original) circle remains a circle not passing through $P$.

All 10 lines pass through $P$, so $P$ is a vertex of the arrangement graph on th
e sphere. The faces of the spherical arrangement graph correspond exactly to the
 regions of the plane (since $P$ is a vertex, no face contains it).

**Counting vertices.** The vertices are all intersection points:

| Type | Count |
|------|-------|
| Point at infinity $P$ | $1$ |
| Line–line (finite) | $\binom{10}{2} = 45$ |
| Circle–circle | $2\binom{10}{2} = 90$ |
| Circle–line | $10 \cdot 10 \cdot 2 = 200$ |

$$V = 1 + 45 + 90 + 200 = 336.$$

**Counting edges.** Each curve is divided into edges by the vertices lying on it
.

- **Each line** (a circle through $P$ on the sphere) passes through: $P$, plus $
9$ other line-intersection points, plus $2 \times 10 = 20$ circle-intersection p
oints $= 30$ vertices, giving $30$ edges. Over 10 lines: $10 \times 30 = 300$.
- **Each circle** passes through: $2 \times 9 = 18$ circle-intersection points,
plus $2 \times 10 = 20$ line-intersection points $= 38$ vertices, giving $38$ ed
ges. Over 10 circles: $10 \times 38 = 380$.

$$E = 300 + 380 = 680.$$

**Connectedness.** The graph is connected: all 10 lines share vertex $P$, and ev
ery circle intersects every line, so every component connects to the line-subgra
ph through $P$.

**Applying Euler's formula** $V - E + F = 2$ on the sphere:

$$F = 2 - V + E = 2 - 336 + 680 = 346.$$

**General formula (cross-check).** For $m$ circles and $n \ge 1$ lines, the same
 computation gives:
$$F = 1 + \frac{n(n+1)}{2} + m(m-1) + 2mn.$$
Substituting $m = n = 10$:
$$F = 1 + \frac{10 \cdot 11}{2} + 10 \cdot 9 + 2 \cdot 10 \cdot 10 = 1 + 55 + 90
 + 200 = 346.$$

**Verification against known special cases:**
- $m=0, n=10$ (lines only): $F = 1 + 55 + 0 + 0 = 56 = \frac{10 \cdot 11}{2}+1$.
 ✓
- $m=1, n=1$: $F = 1 + 1 + 0 + 2 = 4$. ✓ (A line through a circle gives 4 region
s.)
- $m=2, n=1$: $F = 1 + 1 + 2 + 4 = 8$. ✓ (Verified by direct geometric count.)
- $m=10, n=0$ (circles only, no $P$ needed): $F = 2 + m(m-1) = 92 = 10^2 - 10 +
2$. ✓

**General position is achievable.** The conditions (no parallel lines, no triple
 concurrence, every circle–circle and circle–line pair transversal, no intersect
ion point shared by 3+ curves) are finitely many algebraic avoidance conditions
forming an open dense subset of the configuration space. Hence they are simultan
eously satisfiable, and the count above is the maximum.

**Answer.** The maximum number of regions is $\boxed{346}$.
