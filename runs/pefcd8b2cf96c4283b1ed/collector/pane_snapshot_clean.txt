## Proof

The answer is **YES**, the inequality holds for all such $u, v$.

### Step 1: $u^T A u > 0$

Since $A$ has all positive entries and $u \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^n$ is non-zero
, every term $a_{ij} u_i u_j \geq 0$ in $u^T A u = \sum_{i,j} a_{ij} u_i u_j$, a
nd at least one term is strictly positive (if $u_k > 0$ then $a_{kk} u_k^2 > 0$)
. Hence $u^T A u > 0$, so $u$ lies in the **positive cone** of the form $B(x,y)
= x^T A y$.

Since $v$ is not parallel to $u$, and the zero vector is parallel to every vecto
r, we have $v \neq 0$.

### Step 2: Reduction to standard form

Since $A$ is symmetric with signature $(1, n-1)$, there exists an invertible mat
rix $P$ such that $A = P^T J P$ where $J = \operatorname{diag}(1, -1, \ldots, -1
)$. Set $u' = Pu$, $v' = Pv$. Then $u^T A v = (u')^T J v'$, $u^T A u = (u')^T J
u'$, $v^T A v = (v')^T J v'$. Write $u' = (u_1', \bar{u}')$, $v' = (v_1', \bar{v
}')$ with $\bar{u}', \bar{v}' \in \mathbb{R}^{n-1}$.

### Step 3: Case $v^T A v < 0$

Then $(u^T A u)(v^T A v) < 0$ (since $u^T A u > 0$) while $(u^T A v)^2 \geq 0$,
so the inequality holds strictly. ✓

### Step 4: Case $v^T A v = 0$, $v \neq 0$

We have $(v_1')^2 = \|\bar{v}'\|^2$ with $\|\bar{v}'\| > 0$ (since $v' \neq 0$),
 and $(u_1')^2 > \|\bar{u}'\|^2$ (positive cone). Then:

$$|u_1' v_1'| = |u_1'| \cdot \|\bar{v}'\| > \|\bar{u}'\| \cdot \|\bar{v}'\| \geq
 |\bar{u}' \cdot \bar{v}'|$$

where the strict inequality uses $|u_1'| > \|\bar{u}'\|$ and the last is Euclide
an Cauchy-Schwarz. By the reverse triangle inequality:

$$|u^T A v| = |u_1' v_1' - \bar{u}' \cdot \bar{v}'| \geq |u_1' v_1'| - |\bar{u}'
 \cdot \bar{v}'| > 0.$$

So $(u^T A v)^2 > 0 = (u^T A u)(v^T A v)$. ✓

### Step 5: Case $v^T A v > 0$ — Reverse Cauchy-Schwarz

Both $u', v'$ lie in the positive cone $\{x : x_1^2 > \|\bar{x}\|^2\}$, which ha
s two connected components: $C_+ = \{x_1 > \|\bar{x}\|\}$ and $C_- = \{x_1 < -\|
\bar{x}\|\}$, both open convex cones.

**Showing $D := (u^T A v)^2 - (u^T A u)(v^T A v) \geq 0$:** Consider $g(t) = (u'
 + t v')^T J (u' + t v') = u^T A u + 2t(u^T A v) + t^2(v^T A v)$, a quadratic in
 $t$ with positive leading coefficient $v^T A v > 0$.

- If $u', v'$ are in the **same** component (say $C_+$): for $t \geq 0$, $u' + t
v' \in C_+$ (convex cone). As $t \to -\infty$, $u' + tv' \to -\infty \cdot v' \i
n C_-$. By continuity, the path crosses from $C_+$ to $C_-$, hence crosses the n
ull cone $\{x : x^T J x = 0\}$. So $g(t_0) = 0$ for some $t_0$.
- If $u', v'$ are in **different** components: at $t = 0$ we are in one componen
t; as $t \to +\infty$ or $t \to -\infty$ we approach the other component. Again
the path crosses the null cone, so $g(t_0) = 0$ for some $t_0$.

Since $g$ has a real root, its discriminant is non-negative: $4(u^T A v)^2 - 4(u
^T A u)(v^T A v) \geq 0$, i.e., $D \geq 0$.

**Showing $D = 0$ iff $u, v$ proportional:** $D = 0$ means $g$ has a double root
 $t_0 = -(u^T A v)/(v^T A v)$. Set $w = u' + t_0 v'$. Then $w^T J w = 0$ (root c
ondition) and $w^T J v' = 0$ (derivative condition $g'(t_0) = 2w^T J v' = 0$).

In standard coordinates: $w_1 v_1' = \bar{w} \cdot \bar{v}'$ and $w_1^2 = \|\bar
{w}\|^2$, with $v_1'^2 > \|\bar{v}'\|^2$.

If $w \neq 0$: then $w_1 \neq 0$ (since $w_1^2 = \|\bar{w}\|^2$ and $w \neq 0$).
 By Cauchy-Schwarz:
$$|w_1|\,|v_1'| = |\bar{w} \cdot \bar{v}'| \leq \|\bar{w}\|\,\|\bar{v}'\| = |w_1
|\,\|\bar{v}'\|,$$
giving $|v_1'| \leq \|\bar{v}'\|$, contradicting $v_1'^2 > \|\bar{v}'\|^2$.

Therefore $w = 0$, i.e., $u' = -t_0 v'$, so $u$ and $v$ are proportional (since
$P$ is invertible). Conversely, if $v = \lambda u$, direct substitution gives $D
 = 0$.

Hence for $v$ **not parallel** to $u$: $D > 0$, i.e., $(u^T A v)^2 > (u^T A u)(v
^T A v)$. ✓

### Conclusion

All three cases yield strict inequality. For $n = 1$, no $v \in \mathbb{R}$ not
parallel to a non-zero $u$ exists, so the statement holds vacuously. For $n \geq
 2$, the analysis above covers all cases. Therefore:

**The inequality $(u^T A v)^2 > (u^T A u)(v^T A v)$ holds for every non-zero $u
\in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^n$ and every $v \in \mathbb{R}^n$ not parallel to $u$.**
