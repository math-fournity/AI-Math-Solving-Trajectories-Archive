**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $(z_n)$ be a sequence of complex numbers all lying in a sector of
 angular width less than $\pi$. By rotating the coordinate system, we may assume
 without loss of generality that the sector is
$$S = \{re^{i\theta} : r \ge 0,\; |\theta| \le \alpha\}, \quad \text{where } \al
pha < \frac{\pi}{2}.$$
This is valid because a sector of angular width $< \pi$ can always be centered a
t angle $0$ with half-width $\alpha < \pi/2$.

**Hypothesis.** There exist two non-collinear directions $\phi_1, \phi_2$ (i.e.,
 $\sin(\phi_1 - \phi_2) \ne 0$) such that the series
$$\sum_{n} \operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\phi_1} z_n) \quad \text{and} \quad \sum_{n} \
operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\phi_2} z_n)$$
both converge. Denote $a_n = \operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\phi_1} z_n)$ and $b_n = \op
eratorname{Re}(e^{-i\phi_2} z_n)$.

**Step 1: Recovering $\operatorname{Re}(z_n)$ as a linear combination.** Writing
 $z_n = x_n + iy_n$, the projections are:
$$a_n = x_n \cos\phi_1 + y_n \sin\phi_1, \qquad b_n = x_n \cos\phi_2 + y_n \sin\
phi_2.$$
Since $\sin(\phi_1 - \phi_2) \ne 0$, this linear system is invertible, giving:
$$x_n = \frac{a_n \sin\phi_2 - b_n \sin\phi_1}{\sin(\phi_2 - \phi_1)}.$$
Thus $x_n = \operatorname{Re}(z_n)$ is a fixed linear combination of $a_n$ and $
b_n$. Since $\sum a_n$ and $\sum b_n$ converge, it follows that
$$\sum_{n} \operatorname{Re}(z_n) = \sum_{n} x_n \quad \text{converges.}$$

**Step 2: Using the sector condition to bound $|z_n|$.** Since each $z_n$ lies i
n the sector $S$, we have $z_n = |z_n| e^{i\theta_n}$ with $|\theta_n| \le \alph
a < \pi/2$. Therefore:
$$\operatorname{Re}(z_n) = |z_n| \cos\theta_n \ge |z_n| \cos\alpha > 0 \quad \te
xt{for all } z_n \ne 0.$$
This gives the key inequality:
$$|z_n| \le \frac{\operatorname{Re}(z_n)}{\cos\alpha}.$$

**Step 3: Convergence of $\sum |z_n|$.** Since $\operatorname{Re}(z_n) \ge 0$ fo
r all $n$, the series $\sum \operatorname{Re}(z_n)$ is a convergent series of no
n-negative terms, hence $\sum \operatorname{Re}(z_n) < \infty$. By the inequalit
y from Step 2:
$$\sum_{n} |z_n| \le \frac{1}{\cos\alpha} \sum_{n} \operatorname{Re}(z_n) < \inf
ty.$$
Therefore $\sum |z_n|$ converges. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The sector condition (angular width $< \pi$) is essential: it guaran
tees that $\operatorname{Re}(z_n) \ge 0$ after rotation, making the real-part pr
ojection comparable to the modulus. Without this, convergence of projections alo
ng two directions would only yield convergence of $\sum z_n$ (not $\sum |z_n|$),
 since the non-negativity needed for the comparison would fail.
