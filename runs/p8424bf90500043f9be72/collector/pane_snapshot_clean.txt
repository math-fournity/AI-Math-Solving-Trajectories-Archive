**Theorem.** Every finite distributive lattice of length $n$ can be embedded int
o a boolean lattice of rank $n$.

**Proof.**

We use **Birkhoff's representation theorem** (fundamental theorem of finite dist
ributive lattices): every finite distributive lattice $L$ is isomorphic to the l
attice $D(J(L))$ of down-sets (order ideals) of the poset $J(L)$ of its join-irr
educible elements, ordered by inclusion. Under this isomorphism, join in $L$ cor
responds to union and meet in $L$ corresponds to intersection of down-sets.

**Step 1: The length of $L$ equals $|J(L)|$.**

The lattice $D(P)$ of down-sets of a finite poset $P$ has a longest chain obtain
ed by taking any linear extension $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_m$ of $P$ (which exists fo
r every finite poset) and forming
$$\emptyset \subset \{a_1\} \subset \{a_1, a_2\} \subset \cdots \subset \{a_1, \
dots, a_m\} = P.$$
Each successive set is a down-set (since $a_i$ is minimal among the remaining el
ements in the linear extension), giving a chain of $m+1$ elements, i.e., of leng
th $m = |P|$. No chain can be longer since each step adds at least one element a
nd there are only $|P|$ elements total. Hence
$$\operatorname{length}(D(P)) = |P|.$$
By Birkhoff's theorem $L \cong D(J(L))$, so
$$\operatorname{length}(L) = |J(L)| = n.$$

**Step 2: The embedding.**

Every down-set of $J(L)$ is, in particular, a subset of $J(L)$. Therefore
$$D(J(L)) \subseteq 2^{J(L)},$$
and the inclusion map $\iota: D(J(L)) \hookrightarrow 2^{J(L)}$ is a lattice emb
edding: for down-sets $S, T \in D(J(L))$,
$$\iota(S \vee T) = \iota(S \cup T) = S \cup T = \iota(S) \cup \iota(T) = \iota(
S) \vee \iota(T),$$
$$\iota(S \wedge T) = \iota(S \cap T) = S \cap T = \iota(S) \cap \iota(T) = \iot
a(S) \wedge \iota(T),$$
and $\iota$ is order-preserving and injective. Composing with the Birkhoff isomo
rphism $L \xrightarrow{\sim} D(J(L))$ yields a lattice embedding
$$L \hookrightarrow 2^{J(L)}.$$

**Step 3: The target has rank $n$.**

The boolean lattice $2^{J(L)}$ is the powerset of a set of size $|J(L)| = n$, so
 it is the boolean lattice of rank $n$.

**Conclusion.** Composing the Birkhoff isomorphism $L \cong D(J(L))$ with the in
clusion $D(J(L)) \hookrightarrow 2^{J(L)}$, we obtain a lattice embedding of $L$
 into the boolean lattice of rank $n = \operatorname{length}(L)$. $\blacksquare$
