Since $S$ is uncountable, $|S|$ is an infinite cardinal. For any infinite cardin

S \to S \times \{0, 1\}$. Define:

$$T = h^{-1}(S \times \{0\}), \qquad U = h^{-1}(S \times \{1\}).$$

Then $S = T \sqcup U$ (disjoint union) with $|T| = |U| = |S|$.

Define $\varphi: P(T) \to u(S)$ by:

$$\varphi(A) = A \cup U.$$

**$\varphi(A) \in u(S)$:** Since $U \subseteq \varphi(A)$, we have $|\varphi(A)|

$$(A_1 \cup U) \cap T = A_1, \qquad (A_2 \cup U) \cap T = A_2.$$

Therefore $A_1 = A_2$. ✓

$$f(B) = \varphi(g(B)) = g(B) \cup U.$$

Since $g$ is a bijection and $\varphi$ is an injection, $f = \varphi \circ g$ is
