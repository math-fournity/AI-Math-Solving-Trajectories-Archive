# Problem

In a remote industrial facility, a chemical engineer is optimizing a three-stage reaction process involving three reagents: Alpha ($a$), Beta ($b$), and Gamma ($c$), all of which must be present in positive quantities.

The total "Efficiency Potential" of the system is calculated as the sum of the quantities of the three reagents: $a+b+c$. However, this potential is limited by a "Dissipation Factor" caused by the physical containers. The dissipation for the first two stages is governed by the expression $(4a^2+2b^2+1)$, while the dissipation for the final stage is governed by the expression $(4c^2+3)$.

The "Net Output" of the entire operation is defined as the Efficiency Potential divided by the product of these two dissipation factors.

Calculate the maximum possible Net Output that can be achieved by adjusting the positive quantities of $a$, $b$, and $c$.
