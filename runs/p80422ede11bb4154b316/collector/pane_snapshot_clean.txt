## Proof

We use the convention that convolution corresponds to pointwise multiplication o
f Fourier transforms, so

$$\hat g(\xi) = \hat\omega(\xi)\,\hat f(\xi) = \sqrt{|\xi|}\,\hat f(\xi).$$

Recall that $g \in H^s(\mathbb{R})$ iff $(1+|\xi|^2)^{s/2}\hat g \in L^2(\mathbb
{R})$, i.e.

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} (1+|\xi|^2)^s\,|\hat g(\xi)|^2\,d\xi < \infty.$$

Substituting $\hat g(\xi)=\sqrt{|\xi|}\,\hat f(\xi)$ gives the membership condit
ion

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} \underbrace{(1+|\xi|^2)^s\,|\xi|}_{=:\,m_s(\xi)}\,|\hat f(\x
i)|^2\,d\xi < \infty. \tag{1}$$

Since $f\in L^2$, Parseval gives $\hat f\in L^2$, i.e. $\int|\hat f|^2<\infty$.
We seek the largest $s$ such that (1) holds **for every** $f\in L^2$.

**Key observation.** Condition (1) holds for every $\hat f\in L^2$ iff the multi
plier $m_s(\xi)=(1+|\xi|^2)^s|\xi|$ is essentially bounded. Indeed:

- If $m_s$ is bounded, then (1) $\le \|m_s\|_\infty\|\hat f\|_2^2<\infty$.
- If $m_s$ is unbounded, choose $\hat f$ supported in a region where $m_s$ is la
rge (e.g. an $L^2$-normalized bump near a point where $m_s\to\infty$); then (1)
diverges while $\|\hat f\|_2<\infty$.

**Boundedness of $m_s$.** We have $m_s(\xi)=(1+|\xi|^2)^s|\xi|$.

- As $|\xi|\to 0$: $m_s(\xi)\sim |\xi|\to 0$, so no singularity at the origin fo
r any $s$.
- As $|\xi|\to\infty$: $m_s(\xi)\sim |\xi|^{2s+1}$.

Thus $m_s$ is bounded on $\mathbb{R}$ iff $2s+1\le 0$, i.e. $s\le -\tfrac12$.

**Sharpness at $s=-\tfrac12$.** At $s=-\tfrac12$,
$$m_{-1/2}(\xi)=\frac{|\xi|}{\sqrt{1+|\xi|^2}}\le 1,$$
which is bounded (tending to $1$ as $|\xi|\to\infty$ and to $0$ as $|\xi|\to 0$)
. Hence $g\in H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{R})$ for every $f\in L^2$.

For any $s>-\tfrac12$, $m_s(\xi)\sim|\xi|^{2s+1}\to\infty$, so $m_s$ is unbounde
d and there exist $f\in L^2$ with $g\notin H^s$.

Therefore the largest Sobolev exponent guaranteed for $g=\omega*f$ with $f\in L^
2$ is

$$\boxed{s=-\tfrac12}.$$
