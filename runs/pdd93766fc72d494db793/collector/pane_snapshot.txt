## Proof

**Step 1: Series expansion of the kernel.**

For $x > y > 0$:
$$\frac{1}{e^x - e^y} = \frac{e^{-x}}{1-e^{y-x}} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} e^{-(k+1)
x+ky}.$$

By the symmetry $x\leftrightarrow y$, the integral over $\{y>x\}$ equals the int
egral over $\{x>y\}$ (since both $x^n-y^n$ and $e^x-e^y$ change sign, their rati
o is symmetric). Therefore:
$$I_n := \int_0^\infty\!\!\int_0^\infty \frac{x^n-y^n}{e^x-e^y}\,dx\,dy = 2\sum_
{k=0}^{\infty} A_k,$$
where
$$A_k = \int_0^\infty\!\!\int_0^x (x^n - y^n)\,e^{-(k+1)x+ky}\,dy\,dx.$$

**Step 2: Compute $A_k$ via the substitution $y=xt$.**

$$A_k = \int_0^\infty e^{-(k+1)x}\,x^{n+1}\!\int_0^1 (1-t^n)\,e^{kxt}\,dt\,dx =
\int_0^1 (1-t^n)\!\int_0^\infty x^{n+1}e^{-(k+1-kt)x}\,dx\,dt.$$

The inner integral is $(n+1)!/(k+1-kt)^{n+2}$, so
$$A_k = (n+1)!\int_0^1 \frac{1-t^n}{(k+1-kt)^{n+2}}\,dt.$$

**Case $k=0$:** $A_0 = (n+1)!\int_0^1(1-t^n)\,dt = (n+1)!\cdot\frac{n}{n+1} = n\
cdot n!.$

**Case $k\geq 1$:** Substitute $u = k+1-kt$, $t=(k+1-u)/k$, $dt = -du/k$:
$$A_k = \frac{(n+1)!}{k}\int_1^{k+1}\frac{1-\left(\frac{k+1-u}{k}\right)^n}{u^{n
+2}}\,du = \frac{(n+1)!}{k}\int_1^{k+1}\frac{du}{u^{n+2}} - \frac{(n+1)!}{k^{n+1
}}\int_1^{k+1}\frac{(k+1-u)^n}{u^{n+2}}\,du.$$

The first integral: $\displaystyle\int_1^{k+1}u^{-(n+2)}\,du = \frac{1}{n+1}\!\l
eft(1-\frac{1}{(k+1)^{n+1}}\right)$, giving $\dfrac{n!}{k}\!\left(1-\dfrac{1}{(k
+1)^{n+1}}\right)$.

For the second integral, set $p=(k+1-u)/u$ (so $u=(k+1)/(1+p)$, $du=-(k+1)/(1+p)
^2\,dp$):
$$\int_1^{k+1}\frac{(k+1-u)^n}{u^{n+2}}\,du = \frac{1}{k+1}\int_0^k p^n\,dp = \f
rac{k^{n+1}}{(n+1)(k+1)}.$$

This gives $\dfrac{(n+1)!}{k^{n+1}}\cdot\dfrac{k^{n+1}}{(n+1)(k+1)} = \dfrac{n!}
{k+1}$.

Combining:
$$A_k = \frac{n!}{k}\!\left(1-\frac{1}{(k+1)^{n+1}}\right) - \frac{n!}{k+1} = \f
rac{n!}{k(k+1)}\!\left(1-\frac{1}{(k+1)^n}\right), \quad k\geq 1.$$

(Verified: $k=1,n=0$ and $k=1,n=1$ agree with direct computation.)

**Step 3: Assemble and take the limit.**

$$\frac{I_n}{n!} = 2\left[n + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k(k+1)}\!\left(1-\frac
{1}{(k+1)^n}\right)\right],$$

so
$$\frac{I_n}{n!} - 2n = 2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k(k+1)}\!\left(1-\frac{1}{(
k+1)^n}\right).$$

Each summand is nonnegative and bounded by $\frac{1}{k(k+1)}$, and $\sum_{k=1}^{
\infty}\frac{1}{k(k+1)}=1<\infty$. Since $\frac{1}{(k+1)^n}\to 0$ for each fixed
 $k\geq 1$, the **Dominated Convergence Theorem** (for series) gives:

$$L = 2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k(k+1)} = 2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{
k}-\frac{1}{k+1}\right) = 2\cdot 1 = \boxed{2}.$$
