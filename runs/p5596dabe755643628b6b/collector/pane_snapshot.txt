## Proof

**Interpretation.** A Borel probability measure $\sigma$ on $\mathbb{T}$ is *sym
metric* if it is invariant under complex conjugation, i.e., $\sigma = \bar{\sigm
a}$ where $\bar{\sigma} := c_*\sigma$ is the pushforward of $\sigma$ under the m
ap $c(z) = \bar{z}$. Equivalently, $\sigma(A) = \sigma(\overline{A})$ for every
Borel set $A \subseteq \mathbb{T}$, where $\overline{A} = \{\bar{z} : z \in A\}$
.

Recall $\hat{\sigma}(n) = \int_{\mathbb{T}} z^n\,d\sigma(z)$.

---

**Key fact (uniqueness of Fourier–Stieltjes coefficients).** A finite Borel meas
ure on $\mathbb{T}$ is uniquely determined by its Fourier coefficients $\{\hat{\
sigma}(n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}$.

*Proof of the key fact.* If $\mu, \nu$ are finite Borel measures with $\hat{\mu}
(n) = \hat{\nu}(n)$ for all $n$, then $\int p\,d\mu = \int p\,d\nu$ for every tr
igonometric polynomial $p(z) = \sum_{|n|\le N} a_n z^n$. The trigonometric polyn
omials form a self-adjoint subalgebra of $C(\mathbb{T})$ that separates points a
nd contains the constants, so by the Stone–Weierstrass theorem they are dense in
 $C(\mathbb{T})$. By continuity of the integrals, $\int f\,d\mu = \int f\,d\nu$
for all $f \in C(\mathbb{T})$. The uniqueness part of the Riesz–Markov represent
ation theorem then gives $\mu = \nu$. $\square$

---

**($\Rightarrow$) Symmetry implies $\hat{\sigma}(n) = \hat{\sigma}(-n)$.**

Suppose $\sigma = \bar{\sigma}$. For any $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, using the change of
 variables $w = \bar{z}$ (which preserves $\sigma$ by symmetry):

$$\hat{\sigma}(n) = \int_{\mathbb{T}} z^n\,d\sigma(z) = \int_{\mathbb{T}} \bar{w
}^{\,n}\,d\sigma(w) = \int_{\mathbb{T}} w^{-n}\,d\sigma(w) = \hat{\sigma}(-n).$$

---

**($\Leftarrow$) The condition $\hat{\sigma}(n) = \hat{\sigma}(-n)$ for all $n$
implies symmetry.**

Compute the Fourier coefficients of $\bar{\sigma} = c_*\sigma$:

$$\widehat{\bar{\sigma}}(n) = \int_{\mathbb{T}} z^n\,d\bar{\sigma}(z) = \int_{\m
athbb{T}} \bar{z}^{\,n}\,d\sigma(z) = \int_{\mathbb{T}} z^{-n}\,d\sigma(z) = \ha
t{\sigma}(-n).$$

The hypothesis $\hat{\sigma}(n) = \hat{\sigma}(-n)$ therefore gives $\widehat{\b
ar{\sigma}}(n) = \hat{\sigma}(n)$ for every $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. By the key fact
above, $\bar{\sigma} = \sigma$, i.e., $\sigma$ is symmetric.

---

**Conclusion.** Both directions hold, so a Borel probability measure $\sigma$ on
 $\mathbb{T}$ is symmetric **if and only if** $\hat{\sigma}(n) = \hat{\sigma}(-n
)$ for every integer $n$.
