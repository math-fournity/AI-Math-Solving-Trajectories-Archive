Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a curve β:[0,1]→V̄ with β(0)
,β(1)∈∂V and β(t)∈V for t∈(0,1) can exist when V is regular open in ℝ² and V̄ is
not path-connected.

**Answer: Yes, such a curve can exist.**

## Proof

We provide an explicit example.

**Construction.** Let
$$V = B\big((0,0),\,1\big) \;\cup\; B\big((3,0),\,1\big),$$
the union of two disjoint open unit balls in ℝ², centered at (0,0) and (3,0). De
note them $B_1$ and $B_2$.

**V is regular open.** The two closed balls $\overline{B_1}$ and $\overline{B_2}
$ are disjoint (the distance between centers is 3, exceeding the sum of radii 2)
. Therefore:
$$\overline{V} = \overline{B_1} \sqcup \overline{B_2} \quad\text{(disjoint union
)}.$$
Taking interiors and using that the two closed balls are separated by positive d
istance:
$$\operatorname{int}(\overline{V}) = \operatorname{int}(\overline{B_1}) \cup \op
eratorname{int}(\overline{B_2}) = B_1 \cup B_2 = V.$$
Hence $V = \operatorname{int}(\overline{V})$, so $V$ is regular open. ✓

**$\overline{V}$ is not path-connected.** We have $\overline{V} = \overline{B_1}
 \sqcup \overline{B_2}$, a union of two nonempty, disjoint, closed (hence clopen
 in $\overline{V}$) sets. This is a separation, so $\overline{V}$ is not connect
ed, and therefore not path-connected. ✓

**The curve exists.** Define $\beta:[0,1]\to\overline{V}$ by
$$\beta(t) = \big(\cos(\pi t),\;\tfrac{1}{2}\sin(\pi t)\big).$$

- **Endpoints on $\partial V$:** $\beta(0) = (1,0)$ and $\beta(1) = (-1,0)$, bot
h of which lie on $\partial B_1 \subseteq \partial V$. ✓
- **Interior in $V$:** For $t \in (0,1)$, we have $\sin(\pi t) \neq 0$, so
$$|\beta(t)|^2 = \cos^2(\pi t) + \tfrac{1}{4}\sin^2(\pi t) = 1 - \tfrac{3}{4}\si
n^2(\pi t) < 1.$$
Thus $\beta(t) \in B_1 \subseteq V$ for all $t \in (0,1)$. ✓
- **Continuity:** $\beta$ is a composition of continuous functions, with image e
ntirely in $\overline{B_1} \subseteq \overline{V}$. ✓

**Why $\overline{V}$ not being path-connected does not obstruct this.** The cond
ition that $\overline{V}$ is not path-connected only means that *some* pair of p
oints in $\overline{V}$ cannot be joined by a path in $\overline{V}$—specificall
y, any point in $\overline{B_1}$ cannot be joined to any point in $\overline{B_2
}$. It does not prevent two points *within the same path-component* of $\overlin
e{V}$ from being connected. Our curve $\beta$ lies entirely in the single path-c
omponent $\overline{B_1}$, connecting two boundary points of $V$ through the int
erior $B_1 \subseteq V$.

**Conclusion.** There can exist such a curve $\beta$ connecting two points on $\
partial V$ through $V$, even when $\overline{V}$ is not path-connected. $\blacks
quare$
