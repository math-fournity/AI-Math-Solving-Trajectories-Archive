## Proof

We count functions $f:\{1,2,\dots,n\}\to\{1,2,3,4,5\}$ with $|f(k+1)-f(k)|\ge 3$
 for all $k=1,\dots,n-1$.

**Allowed transitions.** For each value $v\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}$, the next value $w$
must satisfy $|w-v|\ge 3$:

| from $v$ | allowed next values |
|---|---|
| 1 | {4, 5} |
| 2 | {5} |
| 3 | {} |
| 4 | {1} |
| 5 | {1, 2} |

In particular, **no transition is possible out of 3**, so the value 3 can only o
ccur as $f(n)$ (the last term) or not at all. For $n\ge 2$, once we leave the fi
rst position, state 3 is unreachable; and if $f(1)=3$ then $f(2)$ cannot exist,
giving no valid function. Hence for $n\ge 2$, the value 3 never appears, and we
work with states $\{1,2,4,5\}$.

**Symmetry.** The transition structure is invariant under the involution $v\maps
to 6-v$ (which swaps $1\leftrightarrow 5$ and $2\leftrightarrow 4$). Thus the co
unt of length-$k$ sequences ending at $1$ equals the count ending at $5$, and si
milarly $2\leftrightarrow 4$. Writing these common counts as $a_k$ (for states $
1,5$) and $b_k$ (for states $2,4$), the count vector for $k\ge 2$ is
$$v_k = (a_k,\, b_k,\, 0,\, b_k,\, a_k)^T.$$

**Recurrence.** From the transition table, for $k\ge 2$:
- A sequence ending at $1$ (resp. $5$) at step $k+1$ must come from $4$ or $5$ (
resp. $1$ or $2$) at step $k$: contribution $b_k + a_k$.
- A sequence ending at $2$ (resp. $4$) at step $k+1$ must come from $5$ (resp. $
1$) at step $k$: contribution $a_k$.

Therefore
$$a_{k+1}=a_k+b_k,\qquad b_{k+1}=a_k.$$
Eliminating $b_k=a_{k-1}$ gives $a_{k+1}=a_k+a_{k-1}$, the **Fibonacci recurrenc
e**.

**Initial values.** With $v_1=(1,1,1,1,1)^T$:
$$v_2 = (2,1,0,1,2)^T \implies a_2=2,\; b_2=1;\qquad v_3=(3,2,0,2,3)^T \implies
a_3=3.$$

$$x_n = 2a_n + 2b_n = 2(a_n+b_n) = 2a_{n+1} = 2F_{n+2},\qquad n\ge 2.$$
