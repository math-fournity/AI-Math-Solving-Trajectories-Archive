Looking at this problem, I need to prove that if $A, P, Q$ are collinear (where
$P$ is the circumcenter of the medial triangle and $Q$ is the midpoint of $OI$),
 then $\frac{AO}{OD} - \frac{BC}{AT} = 4$.

**Proof.**

**Step 1: Identify $P$ as the nine-point center.**

The medial triangle $DEF$ is the image of $\triangle ABC$ under the homothety ce
ntered at the centroid $G$ with ratio $-\frac{1}{2}$. The circumcircle of $DEF$
is the nine-point circle, so $P$ is the nine-point center $N = \frac{O+H}{2}$, w
here $H$ is the orthocenter.

**Step 2: Coordinate setup.**

Place $O$ at the origin with circumradius $R$. Then $H = \vec{A}+\vec{B}+\vec{C}
$, so:
$$P = \frac{\vec{A}+\vec{B}+\vec{C}}{2}, \qquad Q = \frac{I}{2} = \frac{a\vec{A}
+b\vec{B}+c\vec{C}}{2(a+b+c)}$$
where $a=BC$, $b=CA$, $c=AB$. Set $A=(R,0)$, $B=(R\cos 2C,\, R\sin 2C)$, $C=(R\c
os 2B,\, -R\sin 2B)$.

**Step 3: Derive the collinearity condition.**

Since $A,P,Q$ are collinear, $(P-A)\times(Q-A)=0$. Computing:
$$P-A = \tfrac{-A+B+C}{2},\quad Q-A = \tfrac{-(a+2b+2c)A+bB+cC}{2(a+b+c)}.$$
Expanding the cross product using $[A\times B]=R^2\sin 2C$, $[A\times C]=-R^2\si
n 2B$, $[B\times C]=R^2\sin 2A$, and substituting $a=2R\sin A$ etc., the conditi
on becomes:
$$(\sin A+\sin B+2\sin C)\sin 2C - (\sin A+2\sin B+\sin C)\sin 2B + (\sin C-\sin
 B)\sin 2A = 0. \quad (\star)$$

**Step 4: Simplify via $u=\frac{\pi-A}{2}$, $v=\frac{B-C}{2}$.**

Set $B=u+v$, $C=u-v$. Then $\sin B+\sin C = 2\cos\frac{A}{2}\cos v$, $\sin C-\si
n B=-2\sin\frac{A}{2}\sin v$, $\sin 2C-\sin 2B=2\cos A\sin 2v$, $\sin 2B+\sin 2C
=2\sin A\cos 2v$.

Rewriting $(\star)$ as $(\sin A+\sin B+\sin C)(\sin 2C-\sin 2B)+(\sin C\,\sin 2C
-\sin B\,\sin 2B)+(\sin C-\sin B)\sin 2A=0$, and using $\sin^2\theta\cos\theta=\
frac{1}{4}(\cos\theta-\cos 3\theta)$ to handle the middle term, after substituti
on and factoring out $4\cos\frac{A}{2}\sin v$ (both nonzero: $\cos\frac{A}{2}\ne
q 0$ since $A$ is acute, and $\sin v\neq 0$ since $B\neq C$), condition $(\star)
$ reduces to:

$$(4\cos A - 1)\cos^2 v + 2\sin\tfrac{A}{2}\cos A\cos v + \sin^2\tfrac{A}{2}(1-2
\cos A) = 0. \quad (\star\star)$$

**Step 5: Solve the quadratic.**

This is quadratic in $\cos v$ with discriminant:
$$\Delta = 4\sin^2\tfrac{A}{2}\bigl[\cos^2 A - (4\cos A-1)(1-2\cos A)\bigr] = 4\
sin^2\tfrac{A}{2}(3\cos A-1)^2.$$

The two roots are:
$$\cos v = \frac{\sin\frac{A}{2}(2\cos A-1)}{4\cos A-1} \qquad \text{or} \qquad
\cos v = -\sin\tfrac{A}{2}.$$

The second root is **impossible**: since $|v|<\frac{\pi-A}{2}$, we have $\cos v
> \cos\frac{\pi-A}{2}=\sin\frac{A}{2}>0>-\sin\frac{A}{2}$. (When $4\cos A=1$, th
e equation is linear and yields only $\cos v=-\sin\frac{A}{2}$, which is impossi
ble—so this case cannot arise.)

Therefore:
$$\cos v = \frac{\sin\frac{A}{2}(2\cos A-1)}{4\cos A-1}.$$

**Step 6: Compute the target expression.**

Since $OD$ is the distance from the circumcenter to chord $BC$, we have $OD = R\
cos A$, so $\frac{AO}{OD}=\frac{R}{R\cos A}=\sec A$.

Since $T$ is the tangent point of the incircle on $AB$, we have $AT = s-a = R(\s
in B+\sin C-\sin A)$, so $\frac{BC}{AT}=\frac{2R\sin A}{R(\sin B+\sin C-\sin A)}
=\frac{2\sin A}{\sin B+\sin C-\sin A}$.

From the relation above:
$$\sin B+\sin C = 2\cos\tfrac{A}{2}\cos v = \frac{\sin A(2\cos A-1)}{4\cos A-1}.
$$

Hence:
$$\sin B+\sin C-\sin A = \frac{\sin A(2\cos A-1)-\sin A(4\cos A-1)}{4\cos A-1} =
 \frac{-2\sin A\cos A}{4\cos A-1}.$$

Therefore:
$$\frac{BC}{AT} = \frac{2\sin A}{\;\frac{-2\sin A\cos A}{4\cos A-1}\;} = \frac{1
-4\cos A}{\cos A} = \sec A - 4.$$

Finally:
$$\frac{AO}{OD} - \frac{BC}{AT} = \sec A - (\sec A - 4) = 4. \qquad \blacksquare
$$
