## Proof

The equation is $x = \tan x$. The function $\tan x$ has vertical asymptotes at $
a_k = \tfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$ for every integer $k$, and on each open interval $(
a_{k-1}, a_k) = \bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{2}+(k-1)\pi,\;\tfrac{\pi}{2}+k\pi\bigr)$ it is
 continuous and strictly increasing from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$.

**Existence and uniqueness of one root per interval.** On $(a_{k-1},a_k)$ the fu
nction $f(x) = \tan x - x$ is continuous and strictly increasing (since $f'(x) =
 \sec^2 x - 1 = \tan^2 x \geq 0$, and $>0$ except at isolated points). Moreover
$f \to -\infty$ as $x \to a_{k-1}^+$ and $f \to +\infty$ as $x \to a_k^-$. Hence
 $f$ has exactly one zero $r_k$ in each interval $(a_{k-1},a_k)$. Together with
$r_0 = 0$ (in the central interval), these give all roots. Ordering them increas
ingly, the positive roots are $r_1 < r_2 < r_3 < \cdots$, with $r_k \in (a_{k-1}
, a_k)$.

**Location of $r_k$ for large $k$.** Since $r_k \in (a_{k-1}, a_k)$ and $\tan r_
k = r_k$, for large $k$ the value $r_k$ is large and positive. The only way $\ta
n x$ can be large and positive inside $(a_{k-1},a_k)$ is when $x$ is close to th
e right asymptote $a_k$. Write
$$r_k = a_k - \varepsilon_k = \tfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi - \varepsilon_k, \qquad \var
epsilon_k \in (0,\pi),\;\varepsilon_k \to 0^+.$$
Then
$$\tan r_k = \tan\!\Bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{2}+k\pi-\varepsilon_k\Bigr) = \cot \varepsi
lon_k = \frac{\cos\varepsilon_k}{\sin\varepsilon_k}.$$
The equation $\tan r_k = r_k$ becomes
$$\cot \varepsilon_k = \tfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi - \varepsilon_k.$$
As $k\to\infty$ the right-hand side $\to +\infty$, so $\cot\varepsilon_k \to +\i
nfty$, forcing $\varepsilon_k \to 0^+$. Using $\cot\varepsilon_k \sim 1/\varepsi
lon_k$,
$$\varepsilon_k \sim \frac{1}{\tfrac{\pi}{2}+k\pi} \to 0.$$

**The limit of the spacing.** The consecutive positive roots are
$$r_{k+1} - r_k = \bigl(a_{k+1} - \varepsilon_{k+1}\bigr) - \bigl(a_k - \varepsi
lon_k\bigr) = (a_{k+1}-a_k) - (\varepsilon_{k+1}-\varepsilon_k) = \pi - (\vareps
ilon_{k+1}-\varepsilon_k).$$
Since $\varepsilon_k \to 0$, we have $\varepsilon_{k+1}-\varepsilon_k \to 0$. Th
erefore
$$\lim_{k\to\infty}(r_{k+1}-r_k) = \pi.$$

Identifying the ordered sequence $\{x_n\}$ with $\{r_k\}$ (the indexing shift is
 immaterial for the limit), we conclude
$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}(x_{n+1}-x_n) = \pi.}$$
