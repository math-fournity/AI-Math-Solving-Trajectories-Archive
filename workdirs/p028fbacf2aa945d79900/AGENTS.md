# Problem

In a specialized digital cryptography lab, security protocols are generated using a recursive "Tower Function" denoted as $T(a, b)$. For any two positive integers $a$ and $b$, the function is defined by the following rules:
1. $T(a, 1) = a$
2. For $b > 1$, $T(a, b) = a^{T(a, b-1)}$

A researcher is testing the stability of these protocols when passed through a modulo-$n$ filter. She is looking for a specific filter size $n$ that can distinguish between a tower of height 6 and a tower of height 7.

Specifically, she needs to find the smallest positive integer $n$ such that there exists at least one positive integer $a$ where the remainder of $T(a, 6)$ divided by $n$ is not equal to the remainder of $T(a, 7)$ divided by $n$.

Find this smallest positive integer $n$.
