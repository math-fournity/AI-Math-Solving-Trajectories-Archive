## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, the increasing sequence has a least upper bound in $D(\mathcal{H
})$.

**Setup.** Let $A_0 \leq A_1 \leq \cdots$ be an increasing sequence in $D(\mathc
al{H})$, where the order is the Löwner order ($A \leq B \iff B - A$ is positive)
. Each $A_n$ is positive, trace-class, and $\operatorname{tr}(A_n) \leq 1$.

**Step 1: The sequence is uniformly bounded in operator norm.**

For a positive trace-class operator $A_n$, the operator norm satisfies $\|A_n\|
\leq \operatorname{tr}(A_n) \leq 1$. (The norm of a positive operator equals its
 largest eigenvalue, which is at most the sum of all eigenvalues, i.e., the trac
e.) Thus $\|A_n\| \leq 1$ for all $n$.

**Step 2: Strong convergence to a positive operator (Vigier's theorem).**

The sequence $(A_n)$ is monotone increasing in the Löwner order and uniformly bo
unded in operator norm. By **Vigier's theorem**, there exists a self-adjoint ope
rator $A$ with $\|A\| \leq 1$ such that $A_n \to A$ in the strong operator topol
ogy. Since each $A_n \geq 0$ and the positive cone is closed under strong limits
 (for any vector $x$, $\langle Ax, x\rangle = \lim_n \langle A_n x, x\rangle \ge
q 0$), we have $A \geq 0$.

**Step 3: $A$ is trace-class with $\operatorname{tr}(A) \leq 1$ (lower semiconti
nuity of the trace).**

The trace is lower semicontinuous with respect to the weak (hence strong) operat
or topology on positive operators. To see this: for any finite-rank projection $
P$,
$$\operatorname{tr}(PA) = \lim_{n\to\infty} \operatorname{tr}(PA_n) \leq \liminf
_{n\to\infty} \operatorname{tr}(A_n),$$
since $P \leq I$ implies $\operatorname{tr}(PA_n) \leq \operatorname{tr}(A_n)$.
Taking the supremum over all finite-rank projections $P$ (which gives $\operator
name{tr}(A) = \sup_P \operatorname{tr}(PA)$ for positive $A$), we obtain:
$$\operatorname{tr}(A) \leq \liminf_{n\to\infty} \operatorname{tr}(A_n) \leq 1.$
$
Since $A \geq 0$ and $\operatorname{tr}(A) \leq 1 < \infty$, the operator $A$ is
 trace-class. Thus $A \in D(\mathcal{H})$.

**Step 4: $A$ is an upper bound of the sequence.**

Fix any $n$. For all $m \geq n$, we have $A_m - A_n \geq 0$. Since $A_m \to A$ s
trongly as $m \to \infty$, the operator $A - A_n = \text{sot-}\lim_{m\to\infty}(
A_m - A_n)$ is a strong limit of positive operators, hence positive. Therefore $
A_n \leq A$ for every $n$.

**Step 5: $A$ is the *least* upper bound.**

Suppose $B \in D(\mathcal{H})$ satisfies $A_n \leq B$ for all $n$. Then for ever
y vector $x$,
$$\langle A_n x, x\rangle \leq \langle Bx, x\rangle \quad \text{for all } n.$$
Taking $n \to \infty$ and using strong convergence ($A_n x \to Ax$):
$$\langle Ax, x\rangle = \lim_{n\to\infty} \langle A_n x, x\rangle \leq \langle
Bx, x\rangle.$$
Since this holds for all $x$, we conclude $A \leq B$. Thus $A$ is the least uppe
r bound.

**Conclusion.** The increasing sequence $(A_n)$ in $D(\mathcal{H})$ has a least
upper bound in $D(\mathcal{H})$, namely the strong limit $A = \text{sot-}\lim_n
A_n$, which is positive, trace-class, and satisfies $\operatorname{tr}(A) \leq 1
$. The key ingredients are: (1) Vigier's theorem providing strong convergence of
 bounded monotone sequences of self-adjoint operators, and (2) lower semicontinu
ity of the trace ensuring the limit remains in $D(\mathcal{H})$.
