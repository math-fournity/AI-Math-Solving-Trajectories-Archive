**Proof.**

We show that if $\mathbb{Z}^n$ is a quotient of $\mathbb{Z}^m$, then $n \le m$.

Suppose $\mathbb{Z}^n$ is a quotient of $\mathbb{Z}^m$. By definition, there exi
sts a surjective group homomorphism
$$\varphi : \mathbb{Z}^m \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{Z}^n.$$

Apply the functor $-\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}$. Since the tensor product i
s right-exact, the surjection $\varphi$ induces a surjective linear map of $\mat
hbb{Q}$-vector spaces
$$\varphi \otimes 1 : \mathbb{Z}^m \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q} \twoheadright
arrow \mathbb{Z}^n \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}.$$

Now $\mathbb{Z}^m \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q} \cong \mathbb{Q}^m$ and $\math
bb{Z}^n \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q} \cong \mathbb{Q}^n$ as $\mathbb{Q}$-vect
or spaces. Thus we have a surjective linear map
$$\mathbb{Q}^m \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{Q}^n.$$

A surjective linear map between finite-dimensional vector spaces cannot increase
 dimension beyond the source, so
$$\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}^n \le \dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}^m,$$
that is,
$$n \le m. \qquad \blacksquare$$

**Remark.** The same conclusion follows more concretely by representing $\varphi
$ as an $n \times m$ integer matrix $A$ (acting on column vectors). Surjectivity
 of $\varphi$ means the columns of $A$ generate $\mathbb{Z}^n$, which in particu
lar means they span $\mathbb{Q}^n$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Hence $\operatorname{rank}
_{\mathbb{Q}} A = n$, but $\operatorname{rank} A \le \min(m,n)$, so $n \le m$. T
he tensor-product argument above is the coordinate-free version of this rank arg
ument.

### PROOF COMPLETE
