## Proof

Let $L = Z \cup C$ where $Z = \{(0,0,z): z\in\mathbb{R}\}$ is the $z$-axis and $
C = \{(x,y,0): x^2+y^2=1\}$ is the unit circle in the $xy$-plane. Note $Z\cap C
= \emptyset$ (since points of $Z$ have $x=y=0$ while points of $C$ have $x^2+y^2
=1$), so $L$ is a disjoint union and

$$\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus L = (\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus Z)\setminus C.$$

**Step 1: Identify $\mathbb{R}^3\setminus Z$ with $S^1\times\mathbb{R}^2$.**

Use cylindrical coordinates $(\rho,\theta,z)$ with $\rho>0$, $\theta\in S^1$, $z
\in\mathbb{R}$. The map

$$(x,y,z)\longmapsto \bigl(\theta,\;(\ln\rho,\; z)\bigr),\qquad \rho=\sqrt{x^2+y
^2},\;\theta=\arg(x+iy),$$

is a homeomorphism $\mathbb{R}^3\setminus Z \xrightarrow{\;\cong\;} S^1\times\ma
thbb{R}^2$, since $(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\xrightarrow{(\rho,z)\mapsto(\ln\rh
o,z)}\mathbb{R}^2$ is a homeomorphism.

**Step 2: Locate $C$ inside $S^1\times\mathbb{R}^2$.**

The circle $C$ consists of points with $\rho=1$, $z=0$, and arbitrary $\theta$:

$$C = \{(\rho=1,\;\theta,\;z=0): \theta\in S^1\}.$$

Under the homeomorphism above, $\ln\rho = \ln 1 = 0$, so $C$ maps to

$$S^1\times\{(0,0)\}\;\subset\; S^1\times\mathbb{R}^2.$$

That is, $C$ corresponds to $S^1\times\{p\}$ where $p=(0,0)\in\mathbb{R}^2$.

**Step 3: Compute the complement.**

Therefore

$$\mathbb{R}^3\setminus L \;\cong\; (S^1\times\mathbb{R}^2)\setminus(S^1\times\{
p\}) \;=\; S^1\times(\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{p\}).$$

The punctured plane $\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{p\}$ deformation retracts onto $S^1$
 (radial projection onto a circle centered at $p$). Hence

$$\mathbb{R}^3\setminus L \;\simeq\; S^1\times S^1 \;=\; T^2,$$

the $2$-torus.

**Step 4: Fundamental group.**

The torus has $\pi_1(T^2)\cong\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}$, generated by the two
factor circles: the meridian $\mu$ (loop around the removed $z$-axis, i.e. the $
S^1$ factor) and the longitude $\lambda$ (loop around the removed circle $C$, i.
e. the $S^1$ retract of $\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{p\}$). These commute since $\pi_
1$ of a product is the product of $\pi_1$'s.

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\pi_1\bigl(\mathbb{R}^3\setminus L\bigr)\;\cong\;\mathbb{Z}\times\mathb
b{Z}\;=\;\langle\mu,\lambda\;|\;\mu\lambda=\lambda\mu\rangle.}$$

*Geometric check.* Compactifying $\mathbb{R}^3$ to $S^3$ by adding $\infty$, the
 $z$-axis closes up to a circle $K_1$ (through $\infty$) and $C=K_2$ is another
circle. $K_1$ pierces the disk bounded by $C$ exactly once (at the origin), so $
\operatorname{lk}(K_1,K_2)=1$: they form a Hopf link. The Hopf link complement i
n $S^3$ is the standard thickened torus $T^2\times(0,1)$, retracting to $T^2$, c
onfirming $\pi_1\cong\mathbb{Z}^2$. $\square$
