## Answer

**Yes.** For every Borel probability measure $\mu$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$, such a ran
dom vector $X:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^2$ exists. We construct it explicitly.

---

### Step 1. A measure-preserving map $[0,1]\to[0,1]^2$

Define $\varphi:[0,1]\to[0,1]^2$ as follows. For $\omega\in[0,1)$, write its bin
ary expansion $\omega=\sum_{n=1}^\infty \omega_n\,2^{-n}$ (choosing the expansio
n with infinitely many $0$'s for dyadic rationals). Set
$$
u=\sum_{k=1}^\infty \omega_{2k-1}\,2^{-k},\qquad v=\sum_{k=1}^\infty \omega_{2k}
\,2^{-k},
$$
and $\varphi(\omega)=(u,v)$. Define $\varphi(1)=(0,0)$ arbitrarily (the point $\
{1\}$ has measure $0$).

**Claim.** $\varphi$ pushes Lebesgue measure on $[0,1]$ to the product Lebesgue
measure on $[0,1]^2$.

*Proof of claim.* The binary digits $\{\omega_n\}$ of a uniform random variable
on $[0,1]$ are i.i.d. $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)$. The odd-indexed subfamily $\{\o
mega_{2k-1}\}$ and the even-indexed subfamily $\{\omega_{2k}\}$ are independent
(disjoint subfamilies of independent random variables). Hence $u$ (determined by
 odd digits) and $v$ (determined by even digits) are independent $\mathrm{Unifor
m}[0,1]$ variables. Therefore $(u,v)$ has the product Lebesgue distribution on $
[0,1]^2$. $\square$

---

### Step 2. Disintegration of $\mu$

Let $\mu_1$ be the first marginal of $\mu$:
$$
\mu_1(B)=\mu(B\times\mathbb{R}),\qquad B\subseteq\mathbb{R}\text{ Borel},
$$
with CDF $F_1(x)=\mu_1((-\infty,x])$.

Since $\mathbb{R}$ is a standard Borel space and $\mu$ is a probability measure
on $\mathbb{R}^2$, the **disintegration theorem** provides a regular conditional
 distribution: a measurable family $\{\mu_2(\cdot\mid x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}$ of
probability measures on $\mathbb{R}$ such that for every Borel $A\subseteq\mathb
b{R}^2$,
$$
\mu(A)=\int_{\mathbb{R}} \mu_2(A_x\mid x)\,d\mu_1(x),\qquad A_x=\{y:(x,y)\in A\}
.
$$
Let $F_2(y\mid x)=\mu_2((-\infty,y]\mid x)$, which is jointly measurable in $(x,
y)$ and a CDF in $y$ for each fixed $x$.

---

### Step 3. Construction of $X$

Given $\omega\in[0,1]$, let $(u,v)=\varphi(\omega)$. Define
$$
X_1(\omega)=\inf\{x\in\mathbb{R}:F_1(x)\ge u\},
$$
$$
X_2(\omega)=\inf\{y\in\mathbb{R}:F_2(y\mid X_1(\omega))\ge v\},
$$
and $X(\omega)=(X_1(\omega),X_2(\omega))$.

**Measurability.** $\varphi$ is measurable (digit extraction is Borel), $F_1^{-1
}$ is measurable (the quantile function is monotone), so $X_1$ is measurable. Th
e map $(x,v)\mapsto F_2^{-1}(v\mid x)=\inf\{y:F_2(y\mid x)\ge v\}$ is measurable
 (take the infimum over a countable dense subset of $\mathbb{R}$; $F_2$ is joint
ly measurable), so $X_2$ is measurable. Hence $X$ is measurable.

---

### Step 4. Verification

**Marginal of $X_1$.** Since $u$ is $\mathrm{Uniform}[0,1]$ (Step 1), the standa
rd quantile-transform argument gives $X_1=F_1^{-1}(u)\sim\mu_1$.

**Conditional distribution of $X_2$ given $X_1$.** By Step 1, $u$ and $v$ are in
dependent. Since $X_1$ is a function of $u$ alone, $v$ is independent of $X_1$.
Therefore, conditioned on $X_1=x$, the variable $v$ is still $\mathrm{Uniform}[0
,1]$, and
$$
X_2=F_2^{-1}(v\mid x)\sim\mu_2(\cdot\mid x)
$$
again by the quantile transform.

**Joint distribution.** For every Borel $A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^2$,
$$
\mathrm{Meas}\{\omega:X(\omega)\in A\}=P\big((X_1,X_2)\in A\big)=\int_{\mathbb{R
}}\mu_2(A_x\mid x)\,d\mu_1(x)=\mu(A),
$$
where the last equality is the disintegration identity from Step 2.

---

### Conclusion

For every Borel probability measure $\mu$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$, the map $X:[0,1]\to
\mathbb{R}^2$ constructed above is measurable and satisfies
$$
\mathrm{Meas}\{\omega\in[0,1]:X(\omega)\in A\}=\mu(A)
$$
for every Borel set $A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^2$. The construction uses only: (i) th
e measure-preserving digit-interleaving map $[0,1]\to[0,1]^2$, (ii) the existenc
e of regular conditional distributions (disintegration) for probability measures
 on standard Borel spaces, and (iii) the one-dimensional quantile transform. $\b
lacksquare$
