## Computation of $\displaystyle\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx$ via $f(z)=\d
frac{e^{iz}}{z}$

**Contour.** For $0<\epsilon<R$, let $\Gamma$ be the positively-oriented closed
contour in the upper half-plane consisting of, in order:
- the real segment $[-R,-\epsilon]$,
- the small semicircle $C_\epsilon:\ z=\epsilon e^{i\theta},\ \theta\in[\pi,0]$
(traversed clockwise, from $-\epsilon$ to $+\epsilon$),
- the real segment $[\epsilon,R]$,
- the large semicircle $C_R:\ z=Re^{i\theta},\ \theta\in[0,\pi]$ (traversed coun
terclockwise, from $R$ to $-R$).

The function $f(z)=e^{iz}/z$ is holomorphic on and inside $\Gamma$ (the only sin
gularity, at $z=0$, lies outside the indented region). By Cauchy's theorem,

$$\int_{-R}^{-\epsilon}\frac{e^{ix}}{x}\,dx+\int_{C_\epsilon}\frac{e^{iz}}{z}\,d
z+\int_{\epsilon}^{R}\frac{e^{ix}}{x}\,dx+\int_{C_R}\frac{e^{iz}}{z}\,dz=0.\tag{
1}$$

**Large arc $C_R$.** Parametrize $z=Re^{i\theta}$, $dz=iRe^{i\theta}\,d\theta$:

$$\int_{C_R}\frac{e^{iz}}{z}\,dz=i\int_0^\pi e^{iR\cos\theta-R\sin\theta}\,d\the
ta.$$

Its modulus satisfies

$$\left|\int_{C_R}\frac{e^{iz}}{z}\,dz\right|\le \int_0^\pi e^{-R\sin\theta}\,d\
theta = 2\int_0^{\pi/2}e^{-R\sin\theta}\,d\theta \le 2\int_0^{\pi/2}e^{-2R\theta
/\pi}\,d\theta=\frac{\pi}{R}(1-e^{-R})\xrightarrow[R\to\infty]{}0,$$

using $\sin\theta\ge 2\theta/\pi$ on $[0,\pi/2]$ (Jordan's lemma). Hence

$$\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{C_R}\frac{e^{iz}}{z}\,dz=0.\tag{2}$$

**Small arc $C_\epsilon$.** Parametrize $z=\epsilon e^{i\theta}$, $\theta\in[\pi
,0]$, $dz=i\epsilon e^{i\theta}\,d\theta$:

$$\int_{C_\epsilon}\frac{e^{iz}}{z}\,dz=i\int_\pi^0 e^{i\epsilon e^{i\theta}}\,d
\theta.$$

Since $e^{i\epsilon e^{i\theta}}\to 1$ uniformly in $\theta$ as $\epsilon\to 0$,

$$\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{C_\epsilon}\frac{e^{iz}}{z}\,dz=i\int_\pi^0 1\,d\the
ta=-i\pi.\tag{3}$$

**Real-axis segments.** In $\int_{-R}^{-\epsilon}\frac{e^{ix}}{x}\,dx$ substitut
e $x=-t$:

$$\int_{-R}^{-\epsilon}\frac{e^{ix}}{x}\,dx=-\int_{\epsilon}^{R}\frac{e^{-it}}{t
}\,dt.$$

Therefore the two real-axis contributions combine as

$$\int_{-R}^{-\epsilon}\frac{e^{ix}}{x}\,dx+\int_{\epsilon}^{R}\frac{e^{ix}}{x}\
,dx=\int_{\epsilon}^{R}\frac{e^{ix}-e^{-ix}}{x}\,dx=2i\int_{\epsilon}^{R}\frac{\
sin x}{x}\,dx.\tag{4}$$

**Taking the limit.** Let $\epsilon\to 0^+$ and $R\to\infty$ in (1), using (2),
(3), (4):

$$2i\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx-i\pi+0=0.$$

Solving,

$$\boxed{\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx=\frac{\pi}{2}.}$$
