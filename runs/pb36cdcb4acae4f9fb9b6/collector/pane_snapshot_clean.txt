## Multidimensional Fourier Sine Series on a Rectangular Box

Let $\mathbb{X} = [0, X_1] \times \cdots \times [0, X_D]$ with each $X_d > 0$, a
nd let $f \in L^2(\mathbb{X})$.

### The basis functions

For each coordinate $d \in \{1,\dots,D\}$, the one-dimensional sine functions
$$\phi_n^{(d)}(x_d) = \sin\!\left(\frac{n\,\pi\, x_d}{X_d}\right), \qquad n = 1,
 2, 3, \dots$$
form a complete orthogonal system in $L^2([0, X_d])$, with
$$\int_0^{X_d} \sin\!\left(\frac{m\pi x_d}{X_d}\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x_d
}{X_d}\right)dx_d = \frac{X_d}{2}\,\delta_{mn}.$$

Taking the tensor product over all $D$ coordinates, the **multidimensional sine
basis** on $\mathbb{X}$ is indexed by the multi-index $\mathbf{n} = (n_1, \dots,
 n_D) \in \mathbb{N}^D$ (where $\mathbb{N} = \{1,2,3,\dots\}$):

$$\Phi_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x}) = \prod_{d=1}^{D} \sin\!\left(\frac{n_d\,\pi\, x
_d}{X_d}\right), \qquad \mathbf{x} = (x_1,\dots,x_D) \in \mathbb{X}.$$

### Orthogonality

By Fubini's theorem and the one-dimensional orthogonality,

$$\langle \Phi_{\mathbf{m}},\, \Phi_{\mathbf{n}} \rangle_{\mathbb{X}} = \int_{\m
athbb{X}} \Phi_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{x})\,\Phi_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf
{x} = \prod_{d=1}^{D} \frac{X_d}{2}\,\delta_{m_d n_d} = \left(\prod_{d=1}^{D}\fr
ac{X_d}{2}\right)\delta_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}}.$$

So $\{\Phi_{\mathbf{n}}\}_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^D}$ is an **orthogonal** syst
em with squared norm

$$\|\Phi_{\mathbf{n}}\|^2 = \prod_{d=1}^{D}\frac{X_d}{2}.$$

### Completeness in $L^2(\mathbb{X})$

The system $\{\Phi_{\mathbf{n}}\}$ is **complete** in $L^2(\mathbb{X})$. This fo
llows from the fact that the one-dimensional sine system is complete in each $L^
2([0,X_d])$, and the tensor product of complete orthogonal systems on a product
domain yields a complete orthogonal system on the product. Concretely, finite li
near combinations of the $\Phi_{\mathbf{n}}$ are dense in $L^2(\mathbb{X})$ (the
y include all finite sums of products of one-dimensional sine functions, which a
re dense by the Stone–Weierstrass / completion argument applied coordinatewise).

### The Fourier sine series representation

Since $\{\Phi_{\mathbf{n}}\}$ is a complete orthogonal system in $L^2(\mathbb{X}
)$, every $f \in L^2(\mathbb{X})$ admits the expansion

$$\boxed{\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x}) \;\sim\; \sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^D} \
hat{f}_{\mathbf{n}}\;\prod_{d=1}^{D}\sin\!\left(\frac{n_d\,\pi\, x_d}{X_d}\right
),}$$

where the **Fourier sine coefficients** are

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \hat{f}_{\mathbf{n}} \;=\; \frac{\langle f,\,\Phi_{\mathb
f{n}}\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}}{\|\Phi_{\mathbf{n}}\|^2} \;=\; \frac{2^D}{\prod_{d=1}
^{D} X_d}\int_{\mathbb{X}} f(\mathbf{x})\,\prod_{d=1}^{D}\sin\!\left(\frac{n_d\,
\pi\, x_d}{X_d}\right)\,d\mathbf{x}.}$$

### Convergence

The series converges to $f$ in the $L^2(\mathbb{X})$ norm:

$$\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|f - \sum_{\max_d n_d \le N}\hat{f}_{\mathbf{n}}\,\Phi_
{\mathbf{n}}\right\|_{L^2(\mathbb{X})} = 0,$$

and Parseval's identity holds:

$$\|f\|^2_{L^2(\mathbb{X})} = \left(\prod_{d=1}^{D}\frac{X_d}{2}\right)\sum_{\ma
thbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^D} |\hat{f}_{\mathbf{n}}|^2.$$

This is the **multidimensional Fourier sine series representation** of $f$ on th
e rectangular domain $\mathbb{X} = [0,X_1]\times\cdots\times[0,X_D]$.
