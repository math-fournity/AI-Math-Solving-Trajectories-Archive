## Proof

**Setup.** Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n$ be i.i.d. with characteristic function $\
chi(t) = e^{-|t|^{1.9}}$. This is the characteristic function of a symmetric $\a
lpha$-stable distribution with index $\alpha = 1.9$ and scale parameter $c = 1$.
 (Such $\chi$ is a valid characteristic function precisely when $0 < \alpha \le
2$.)

Let $S_n = \sum_{k=1}^n X_k$. Since the $X_k$ are independent, the characteristi
c function of $S_n$ is
$$
\mathbb{E}[e^{itS_n}] = \chi(t)^n = e^{-n|t|^{1.9}}.
$$

**Characteristic function of $n^{-5/9}S_n$.** For $a > 0$, the characteristic fu
nction of $a S_n$ is $\mathbb{E}[e^{it\,a S_n}] = \chi(at)^n$. Setting $a = n^{-
5/9}$:
$$
\varphi_n(t) := \mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{it\,n^{-5/9}S_n}\right] = \exp\!\left(-n\le
ft|n^{-5/9}t\right|^{1.9}\right) = \exp\!\left(-n^{1 - \frac{5}{9}\cdot 1.9}|t|^
{1.9}\right).
$$

Compute the exponent of $n$:
$$
\frac{5}{9}\times 1.9 = \frac{5\times 1.9}{9} = \frac{9.5}{9} = \frac{19}{18}, \
qquad 1 - \frac{19}{18} = -\frac{1}{18}.
$$

Hence
$$
\varphi_n(t) = \exp\!\left(-n^{-1/18}|t|^{1.9}\right).
$$

**Taking the limit.** As $n \to \infty$, $n^{-1/18} \to 0$, so for every $t \in
\mathbb{R}$,
$$
\varphi_n(t) \longrightarrow e^{0} = 1.
$$

The constant function $1$ is the characteristic function of the Dirac measure $\
delta_0$ (the degenerate distribution at $0$). Since $\varphi_n(t) \to 1$ pointw
ise and $1$ is continuous at $t=0$ (hence a valid characteristic function), Lévy
's continuity theorem guarantees
$$
n^{-5/9}S_n \xrightarrow{d} \delta_0 \quad (n\to\infty).
$$

**Why $\delta_0$ and not a nontrivial stable law.** The natural (self-similar) n
ormalization for an $\alpha$-stable law is $n^{-1/\alpha}$. Indeed, $\mathbb{E}[
e^{it\,n^{-1/\alpha}S_n}] = e^{-n\cdot n^{-1}|t|^\alpha} = e^{-|t|^\alpha}$, so
$n^{-1/\alpha}S_n \stackrel{d}{=} X_1$ for every $n$. Here $1/\alpha = 1/1.9 = 1
0/19 \approx 0.5263$, while $5/9 \approx 0.5556$. Since
$$
\frac{5}{9} - \frac{10}{19} = \frac{95 - 90}{171} = \frac{5}{171} > 0,
$$
the exponent $5/9$ exceeds the critical value $1/\alpha$. We can write
$$
n^{-5/9}S_n = n^{-5/171}\cdot\!\left(n^{-10/19}S_n\right),
$$
where $n^{-10/19}S_n \stackrel{d}{=} X_1$ (non-degenerate, fixed law) and $n^{-5
/171}\to 0$. The product of a tight (fixed) random variable and a deterministic
factor tending to $0$ converges in distribution to $0$, confirming $\delta_0$.

---

**General case.** Replace $1.9$ by $\alpha$ (with $0 < \alpha \le 2$ so that $e^
{-|t|^\alpha}$ is a characteristic function) and $5/9$ by $\beta > 0$. Then
$$
\varphi_n(t) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{it\,n^{-\beta}S_n}\right] = \exp\!\left(-n^{
1-\alpha\beta}|t|^{\alpha}\right).
$$

Three regimes emerge, governed by the sign of $1 - \alpha\beta$:

1. **$\beta = 1/\alpha$ (critical scaling):** $1 - \alpha\beta = 0$, so $\varphi
_n(t) = e^{-|t|^\alpha}$ for all $n$. The law is *exactly stable*: $n^{-1/\alpha
}S_n \stackrel{d}{=} X_1$ for every $n$, and the weak limit is the same $\alpha$
-stable law with characteristic function $e^{-|t|^\alpha}$.

2. **$\beta > 1/\alpha$ (over-normalization):** $1 - \alpha\beta < 0$, so $n^{1-
\alpha\beta}\to 0$ and $\varphi_n(t)\to 1 = $ characteristic function of $\delta
_0$. The weak limit is $\delta_0$ (degenerate at $0$). This is the regime of the
 given problem, since $5/9 > 10/19 = 1/1.9$.

3. **$\beta < 1/\alpha$ (under-normalization):** $1 - \alpha\beta > 0$, so $n^{1
-\alpha\beta}\to\infty$ and $\varphi_n(t)\to 0$ for every $t\ne 0$ (with $\varph
i_n(0)=1$). The pointwise limit is the function that is $1$ at $t=0$ and $0$ els
ewhere, which is **not** continuous at $0$ and hence **not** a characteristic fu
nction of any probability measure. By Lévy's continuity theorem, $n^{-\beta}S_n$
 has **no weak limit** as a probability measure — the mass escapes to infinity (
tightness fails).

**Summary.** For the given problem, since $\alpha = 1.9$ and $\beta = 5/9$ satis
fy $\alpha\beta = 19/18 > 1$ (equivalently $\beta > 1/\alpha$), the weak limit o
f $n^{-5/9}S_n$ is the degenerate distribution $\delta_0$. In general, with char
acteristic function $e^{-|t|^\alpha}$ ($0<\alpha\le 2$) and normalization $n^{-\
beta}$: the limit is the $\alpha$-stable law when $\beta = 1/\alpha$; it is $\de
lta_0$ when $\beta > 1/\alpha$; and no weak limit (as a probability measure) exi
sts when $\beta < 1/\alpha$.
