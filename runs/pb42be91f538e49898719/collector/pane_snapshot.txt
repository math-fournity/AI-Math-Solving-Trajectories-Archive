## Proof

**Claim:** Such an element $x$ need not exist. We provide an explicit counterexa
mple.

**Counterexample.** Let $X = \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$, the space of square-summable s
equences, with dual $X^* \cong \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$. Let $(e_n)_{n=1}^\infty$ den
ote the standard orthonormal basis of $\ell^2$. Define functionals $f_n \in X^*$
 by:

$$f_1 = e_1, \qquad f_n = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_n - e_{n-1}) \quad \text{for } n
\geq 2.$$

Concretely, for $x = (x_k)_{k=1}^\infty \in \ell^2$:
$$f_1(x) = x_1, \qquad f_n(x) = \frac{x_n - x_{n-1}}{\sqrt{2}} \quad \text{for }
 n \geq 2.$$

**Step 1: Each $f_n$ has norm 1.** We have $\|f_1\| = \|e_1\|_2 = 1$. For $n \ge
q 2$, since $e_n$ and $e_{n-1}$ are orthonormal, $\|e_n - e_{n-1}\|_2 = \sqrt{2}
$, so $\|f_n\| = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \cdot \sqrt{2} = 1$.

**Step 2: The $(f_n)$ are linearly independent.** Suppose $\sum_{n=1}^N a_n f_n
= 0$ for some scalars $a_1, \ldots, a_N$. Expanding in the basis $(e_k)$, the co
efficient of $e_N$ (for $N \geq 2$) is $\frac{a_N}{\sqrt{2}}$, so $a_N = 0$. By
downward induction, $a_n = 0$ for all $n \geq 2$, and then $a_1 e_1 = 0$ gives $
a_1 = 0$. Hence the $f_n$ are linearly independent.

**Step 3: No $x \in \ell^2$ satisfies $f_n(x) > 0$ for all $n$.** Suppose for co
ntradiction that $x = (x_k) \in \ell^2$ with $f_n(x) > 0$ for all $n$. Then:

- $f_1(x) = x_1 > 0$,
- $f_n(x) = \frac{x_n - x_{n-1}}{\sqrt{2}} > 0$ for all $n \geq 2$, i.e., $x_n >
 x_{n-1}$.

By induction, $x_n > x_1 > 0$ for every $n \geq 2$, and $x_1 > 0$. Therefore $(x
_n)$ is bounded below by $x_1 > 0$, so $x_n \not\to 0$ as $n \to \infty$.

However, $x \in \ell^2$ implies $\sum_{k=1}^\infty x_k^2 < \infty$, which forces
 $x_k \to 0$ as $k \to \infty$. This is a contradiction.

**Conclusion.** There exists a (separable) Banach space $X$ and a sequence of li
nearly independent norm-one functionals $(f_n)$ in $X^*$ such that no $x \in X$
satisfies $f_n(x) \in (0, \infty)$ for all $n$. Therefore, such an element $x$ d
oes **not** necessarily exist.
