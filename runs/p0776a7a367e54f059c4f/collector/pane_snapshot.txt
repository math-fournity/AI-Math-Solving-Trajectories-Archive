## Proof

We prove there exists $K>0$ such that for all $x>K$, the number of primes in $(x
,2x]$ is strictly less than the number of primes in $[0,x]$.

**Notation.** Let $\pi(t)$ denote the prime-counting function. The number of pri
mes in $[0,x]$ is $\pi(x)$ (since primes are $\ge 2$), and the number of primes
in $(x,2x]$ is $\pi(2x)-\pi(x)$. We must show $\pi(2x)-\pi(x)<\pi(x)$, equivalen
tly
$$\pi(2x)<2\pi(x),$$
for all sufficiently large $x$.

**Explicit bounds used.** We use the following well-known explicit estimates of
Dusart (2010):

- (Lower bound) For all $t\ge 5393$,
$$\pi(t)\ge \frac{t}{\ln t - 1}.$$

- (Upper bound) For all $t\ge 60184$,
$$\pi(t)\le \frac{t}{\ln t - 1.1}.$$

**Main argument.** Set $K=60184$. Let $x\ge K$, so in particular $x\ge 60184\ge
5393$ and $2x\ge 120368\ge 60184$, and both bounds above apply.

Applying the upper bound to $\pi(2x)$:
$$\pi(2x)\le \frac{2x}{\ln(2x)-1.1}=\frac{2x}{\ln x+\ln 2-1.1}.$$

Applying the lower bound to $\pi(x)$ and multiplying by $2$:
$$2\pi(x)\ge \frac{2x}{\ln x - 1}.$$

It remains to compare the two denominators. Since
$$\ln 2 \approx 0.6931 > 0.1,$$
we have
$$\ln x+\ln 2 - 1.1 \;>\; \ln x - 1.$$

Both denominators are positive (for $x\ge 60184$, $\ln x\ge \ln 60184\approx 11.
0$, so $\ln x-1>0$). Therefore, since the numerators $2x$ are equal and positive
,
$$\frac{2x}{\ln x+\ln 2-1.1} \;<\; \frac{2x}{\ln x-1}.$$

Combining the chain of inequalities,
$$\pi(2x)\;\le\;\frac{2x}{\ln x+\ln 2-1.1}\;<\;\frac{2x}{\ln x-1}\;\le\;2\pi(x).
$$

Hence $\pi(2x)<2\pi(x)$, i.e.
$$\pi(2x)-\pi(x)<\pi(x),$$
which is exactly the statement that $(x,2x]$ contains fewer primes than $[0,x]$.

**Conclusion.** Taking $K=60184$, for every $x>K$ the interval $(x,2x]$ contains
 strictly fewer primes than $[0,x]$. $\blacksquare$
