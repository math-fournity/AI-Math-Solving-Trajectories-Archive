## Proof

**Step 1: Reduce to a lattice problem.**

Let $f(x) = \sum_{k=0}^n a_k x^k$ with $a_k \in \mathbb{Z}$. Then

$$\int_{-1}^1 x^n f(x)\,dx = \sum_{k=0}^n a_k \int_{-1}^1 x^{n+k}\,dx.$$

Since $\int_{-1}^1 x^m\,dx = \frac{2}{m+1}$ if $m$ is even and $0$ if $m$ is odd
, only terms with $n+k$ even survive. Setting $m = n+k+1$ (which is odd), with $
k \in \{0,\dots,n\}$ and $n+k$ even, the integral equals

$$\sum_{\substack{k=0,\dots,n \\ n+k \text{ even}}} a_k \cdot \frac{2}{n+k+1} =
\sum_{\substack{m \text{ odd} \\ n+1 \le m \le 2n+1}} a_{m-n-1} \cdot \frac{2}{m
}.$$

The set of achievable values is the $\mathbb{Z}$-span of $\left\{\frac{2}{m} : m
 \text{ odd},\; n+1 \le m \le 2n+1\right\}$.

**Step 2: Compute the smallest positive element.**

Let $L_n = \operatorname{lcm}\{m : m \text{ odd},\; n+1 \le m \le 2n+1\}$. Since
 $\frac{2}{m} = \frac{2}{L_n}\cdot\frac{L_n}{m}$ and $\gcd\!\left(\frac{L_n}{m_1
}, \dots, \frac{L_n}{m_r}\right) = 1$ (because $L_n$ is the lcm), the $\mathbb{Z
}$-span is exactly $\frac{2}{L_n}\mathbb{Z}$.

(Even if "degree $n$" requires $a_n \neq 0$: writing $d = L_n/(2n+1)$, $e = L_n/
L'$ where $L' = \operatorname{lcm}$ of the other odd $m$'s, one checks $\gcd(d,e
)=1$, so Bézout gives $\frac{2}{L_n}$ with $a_n \neq 0$.) Thus:

$$\alpha_n = \frac{2}{L_n}, \qquad \log \alpha_n = \log 2 - \log L_n.$$

**Step 3: Express $\log L_n$ via Chebyshev functions.**

$$\log L_n = \sum_{\substack{p \text{ odd prime},\; k \ge 1}} \log p \cdot \math
bf{1}\big[\exists\text{ odd } m \in [n+1,\,2n+1] \text{ with } p^k \mid m\big].$
$

Since $p$ is odd, $m$ odd with $p^k \mid m$ means $m = j\cdot p^k$ for odd $j$.
We need odd $j$ with $n+1 \le j\,p^k \le 2n+1$. I determine which $p^k$ contribu
te:

- **$p^k \le n/2$:** The odd multiples $p^k, 3p^k, 5p^k, \dots$ are spaced $2p^k
 \le n$ apart. The interval $[n+1, 2n+1]$ has length $n \ge 2p^k$, so at least o
ne odd multiple falls inside. ✓ **Contributes.**

- **$n/2 < p^k \le (2n+1)/3$:** Take $j=3$: then $3p^k > 3n/2 \ge n+1$ (for $n\g
e 2$) and $3p^k \le 2n+1$. So $3p^k \in [n+1, 2n+1]$. ✓ **Contributes.**

- **$(2n+1)/3 < p^k \le n$:** $j=1$ gives $p^k \le n < n+1$; $j=3$ gives $3p^k >
 2n+1$; $j\ge 5$ gives $jp^k > 5(2n+1)/3 > 2n+1$. ✗ **Does not contribute.**

- **$n < p^k \le 2n+1$:** $j=1$ gives $p^k \in [n+1, 2n+1]$. ✓ **Contributes.**

- **$p^k > 2n+1$:** ✗ **Does not contribute.**

Therefore:

$$\log L_n = \psi_{\text{odd}}\!\left(\tfrac{2n+1}{3}\right) + \psi_{\text{odd}}
(2n+1) - \psi_{\text{odd}}(n),$$

where $\psi_{\text{odd}}(x) = \sum_{\substack{p \text{ odd prime},\; k\ge 1 \\ p
^k \le x}} \log p = \psi(x) - \lfloor\log_2 x\rfloor\log 2$ and $\psi$ is the se
cond Chebyshev function.

**Step 4: Apply the Prime Number Theorem.**

By the PNT, $\psi(x) \sim x$. Since the $p=2$ contribution is $O(\log x)$, we ha
ve $\psi_{\text{odd}}(x) \sim x$. Therefore:

$$\log L_n \sim \frac{2n}{3} + (2n - n) = \frac{2n}{3} + n = \frac{5n}{3}.$$

**Step 5: Compute the limit.**

$$\frac{\log \alpha_n}{n} = \frac{\log 2 - \log L_n}{n} = \frac{\log 2}{n} - \fr
ac{\log L_n}{n} \;\longrightarrow\; 0 - \frac{5}{3} = -\frac{5}{3}.$$

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{\log \alpha_n}{n} = -\frac{5}{3}.}$$
