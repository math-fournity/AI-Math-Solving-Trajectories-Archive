## Proof

Let $v_1, v_2, \ldots, v_n$ be vectors in $\mathbb{R}^n$ (the edges of the paral
lelepiped), and let $A$ be the $n \times n$ matrix whose $j$-th column is $v_j$.

**Step 1: The volume is $|\det A|$.**

The $n$-dimensional parallelepiped spanned by $v_1, \ldots, v_n$ is the image of
 the unit cube $[0,1]^n$ under the linear map $x \mapsto Ax$. By the change-of-v
ariables formula, its volume equals $|\det A|$. (Equivalently, this is the stand
ard geometric interpretation of the determinant.)

**Step 2: Relate the Gram matrix to $A$.**

Define $b_{i,j} = v_i \cdot v_j$ and the Gram matrix $B = [b_{i,j}]$. Since the
columns of $A$ are $v_1, \ldots, v_n$, the $(i,j)$-entry of $A^T A$ is exactly t
he dot product of the $i$-th and $j$-th columns of $A$, i.e.,

$$(A^T A)_{i,j} = v_i \cdot v_j = b_{i,j}.$$

Hence $B = A^T A$.

**Step 3: Compute $\det B$.**

Using the multiplicative property of determinants and the fact that $\det(A^T) =
 \det A$:

$$\det B = \det(A^T A) = \det(A^T)\,\det(A) = (\det A)(\det A) = (\det A)^2.$$

**Step 4: Equivalence.**

Since $\det B = (\det A)^2 \geq 0$ (confirming $B$ is positive semidefinite, as
every Gram matrix is), we take the principal square root:

$$(\det B)^{1/2} = \big((\det A)^2\big)^{1/2} = |\det A|.$$

Therefore the two expressions are **identical**:

$$(\det[b_{i,j}])^{1/2} = |\det A|.$$

**Conclusion.** Yes, the volume of the $n$-dimensional parallelepiped is given b
y both expressions, and they are equivalent. The identity $\det[b_{i,j}] = (\det
 A)^2$ (i.e., $\det(A^T A) = (\det A)^2$) is the precise algebraic reason. Both
equal the volume; the Gram determinant form $(\det[b_{i,j}])^{1/2}$ has the adva
ntage of being intrinsic (coordinate-free, depending only on inner products), wh
ile $|\det A|$ depends on the choice of basis but is computationally direct.
