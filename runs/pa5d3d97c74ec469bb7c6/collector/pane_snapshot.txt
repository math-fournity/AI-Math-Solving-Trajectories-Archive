## Solution

We wish to compute
$$L = \lim_{r \to \infty} \frac{\Gamma(r\alpha)}{\Gamma((r+1)\alpha)}, \qquad 0
< \alpha < 1.$$

**Step 1: Reformulate.** Set $x = r\alpha$. Then $(r+1)\alpha = r\alpha + \alpha
 = x + \alpha$, and as $r \to \infty$ we have $x \to \infty$. The limit becomes
$$L = \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\Gamma(x)}{\Gamma(x+\alpha)}.$$

**Step 2: Apply the standard asymptotic for the ratio of Gamma functions.** A cl
assical consequence of Stirling's approximation states that for fixed $\beta > 0
$,
$$\frac{\Gamma(x+\beta)}{\Gamma(x)} \sim x^{\beta} \quad \text{as } x \to \infty
.$$
This follows from Stirling's formula $\Gamma(x) \sim \sqrt{2\pi/x}\,(x/e)^{x}$:
$$\frac{\Gamma(x+\beta)}{\Gamma(x)} \sim \sqrt{\frac{x}{x+\beta}} \cdot \frac{(x
+\beta)^{x+\beta}}{x^{x}} \cdot e^{-\beta} = \sqrt{\frac{x}{x+\beta}} \cdot (x+\
beta)^{\beta}\cdot\left(1+\frac{\beta}{x}\right)^{x} \cdot e^{-\beta}.$$
As $x\to\infty$: $\sqrt{x/(x+\beta)}\to 1$, $(x+\beta)^\beta \sim x^\beta$, and
$(1+\beta/x)^x \to e^\beta$, so the $e^{-\beta}$ cancels and we get $\sim x^\bet
a$.

**Step 3: Take the reciprocal.** With $\beta = \alpha > 0$,
$$\frac{\Gamma(x)}{\Gamma(x+\alpha)} \sim x^{-\alpha} = \frac{1}{x^{\alpha}}.$$

**Step 4: Take the limit.** Since $\alpha > 0$ and $x = r\alpha \to \infty$,
$$L = \lim_{x\to\infty} x^{-\alpha} = 0.$$

The condition $0 < \alpha < 1$ ensures $\alpha$ is a positive fixed constant (th
e result actually holds for any fixed $\alpha > 0$), so $x^\alpha \to \infty$ an
d its reciprocal tends to $0$.

$$\boxed{\lim_{r \to \infty} \frac{\Gamma(r\alpha)}{\Gamma((r+1)\alpha)} = 0.}$$
