# Semidirect Products of $C_p$ by $C_p$

## Setup

A semidirect product of $C_p$ by $C_p$ is a group $G$ containing a normal subgro
up $N \cong C_p$ and a subgroup $H \cong C_p$ such that $G = NH$ and $N \cap H =
 \{e\}$. Such a structure is determined (up to isomorphism) by a homomorphism
$$\varphi: H \to \operatorname{Aut}(N),$$
which encodes the conjugation action of $H$ on $N$. We write $G = N \rtimes_\var
phi H$.

## The automorphism group of $C_p$

Let $N = \langle a \rangle \cong C_p$. Any automorphism of $N$ is determined by
the image of the generator $a$, which must be another generator $a^k$ with $k \i
n \{1, 2, \dots, p-1\}$ (i.e., $k \not\equiv 0 \pmod{p}$). Composition of automo
rphisms corresponds to multiplication of the exponents modulo $p$, so
$$\operatorname{Aut}(C_p) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^\times \cong C_{p-1},$$
a cyclic group of order $p-1$.

## Classifying the homomorphisms $\varphi: C_p \to \operatorname{Aut}(C_p)$

Let $H = \langle b \rangle \cong C_p$. A homomorphism $\varphi: C_p \to C_{p-1}$
 is determined by the image $\varphi(b)$, which must be an element of $C_{p-1}$
whose order divides $|b| = p$.

The order of $\varphi(b)$ divides both $p$ (since $b^p = e$ forces $\varphi(b)^p
 = e$) and $p-1$ (since $\varphi(b) \in C_{p-1}$, whose order divides $p-1$). He
nce
$$\operatorname{ord}(\varphi(b)) \mid \gcd(p,\, p-1) = 1.$$

Therefore $\varphi(b) = e$, and $\varphi$ is the **trivial homomorphism**. This
is the unique homomorphism $C_p \to \operatorname{Aut}(C_p)$.

## Conclusion

Since the only action of $H \cong C_p$ on $N \cong C_p$ is the trivial one, the
semidirect product reduces to the direct product:
$$C_p \rtimes C_p \;\cong\; C_p \times C_p \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}_p \times \mathbb
{Z}_p.$$

This is the **elementary abelian group** of order $p^2$: it is abelian, every no
n-identity element has order $p$, and it is a 2-dimensional vector space over $\
mathbb{F}_p$.

**Uniqueness.** There is exactly one isomorphism type of semidirect product $C_p
 \rtimes C_p$, namely $C_p \times C_p$. The key reason is the coprimality $\gcd(
p, p-1) = 1$, which forces every action of a $p$-group on a cyclic group of orde
r $p$ to be trivial (since $\operatorname{Aut}(C_p)$ has order $p-1$, prime to $
p$).

## Remark (contrast with $p^2$-order groups generally)

Among all groups of order $p^2$, there are exactly two isomorphism types: the cy
clic group $C_{p^2}$ and the elementary abelian group $C_p \times C_p$. The cycl
ic group $C_{p^2}$ is **not** a semidirect product $C_p \rtimes C_p$, because it
s unique subgroup of order $p$ is characteristic (hence the only subgroup of ord
er $p$), so it cannot be written as $NH$ with $N \cap H = \{e\}$ and $|N|=|H|=p$
. Thus, among semidirect products of $C_p$ by $C_p$ specifically, only $C_p \tim
es C_p$ arises — and it is the unique such structure.
