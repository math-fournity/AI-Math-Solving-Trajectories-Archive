## Proof

**Setup.** Real projective plane $\mathbb{RP}^2$ is the quotient of the square $
[0,1]^2$ by the identifications
$$(0,y)\sim(1,1-y),\qquad (x,0)\sim(1-x,1),$$
i.e. opposite edges identified with a half-twist. Let $p$ be the image of the ce
nter of the square. Define
- $U_2$: a small open disk around $p$,
- $U_1$: the complement in $\mathbb{RP}^2$ of a slightly smaller closed disk $\o
verline{D}\subset U_2$ centered at $p$.

Then $U_1\cup U_2=\mathbb{RP}^2$ and $U_1\cap U_2$ is an open annulus, so Van Ka
mpen applies.

**$\pi_1(U_2)$.** $U_2$ is an open disk, hence contractible:
$$\pi_1(U_2)=1.$$

**$\pi_1(U_1)$.** $U_1$ is the square with a small open disk removed around the
center, with the boundary identifications on the outer square. The region betwee
n the inner circle (boundary of the removed disk) and the outer boundary deforma
tion retracts onto the outer boundary. After the quotient identifications, the o
uter boundary of the square becomes the $1$-skeleton of $\mathbb{RP}^2$, which i
s $\mathbb{RP}^1\cong S^1$. Hence
$$\pi_1(U_1)\cong\mathbb{Z},\qquad \pi_1(U_1)=\langle a\rangle,$$
where $a$ is the generator represented by a single traversal of the $1$-skeleton
 $\mathbb{RP}^1$.

**$\pi_1(U_1\cap U_2)$.** The intersection is an open annulus, deformation retra
cting onto $S^1$:
$$\pi_1(U_1\cap U_2)\cong\mathbb{Z},\qquad \pi_1(U_1\cap U_2)=\langle c\rangle,$
$
where $c$ is the loop going once around the inner circle (boundary of the excise
d disk), oriented as the boundary of $U_1$.

**The inclusion maps.**

- $i_2:\pi_1(U_1\cap U_2)\to\pi_1(U_2)$: since $U_2$ is contractible, $i_2(c)=1$
.

- $i_1:\pi_1(U_1\cap U_2)\to\pi_1(U_1)$: the loop $c$, being the boundary of the
 excised disk, is homotopic in $U_1$ to the boundary of the square. Traversing t
he boundary of the square once, under the twist identifications $(0,y)\sim(1,1-y
)$ and $(x,0)\sim(1-x,1)$, wraps **twice** around the $1$-skeleton $\mathbb{RP}^
1$ — this is exactly the degree-$2$ attaching map of the $2$-cell of $\mathbb{RP
}^2$. Concretely: the bottom edge $(x,0)$, $x:0\to1$, is identified with the top
 edge traversed in reverse, and the left edge is identified with the right edge
in reverse, so all four edges compose into a path that runs around $\mathbb{RP}^
1$ two times. Therefore
$$i_1(c)=a^{2}.$$

**Applying Van Kampen.** With $U_1\cap U_2$ path-connected,
$$\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2)\cong \pi_1(U_1)*\pi_1(U_2)\Big/\big\langle\!\big\langle\,
 i_1(c)\,i_2(c)^{-1}\,\big\rangle\!\big\rangle = \langle a\rangle * 1 \Big/ \big
\langle\!\big\langle\, a^{2}\,\big\rangle\!\big\rangle = \langle a\mid a^{2}=1\r
angle.$$

**Conclusion.**
$$\boxed{\;\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\;}$$

The fundamental group of the real projective plane is the cyclic group of order
$2$.
