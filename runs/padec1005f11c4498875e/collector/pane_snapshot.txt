## Proof

Let $M$ be a closed (compact, without boundary) $n$-dimensional manifold, and le
t $x \in M$.

**Step 1: Excision.** Choose an open neighborhood $U$ of $x$ in $M$ together wit
h a homeomorphism $\varphi: \mathbb{R}^n \to U$ sending $0 \mapsto x$ (a coordin
ate chart). Set $A = M - U$, which is closed in $M$ and disjoint from $x$, so $A
 \subset \operatorname{int}(M - x)$. By excision of $A$,

$$H_n(M, M - x) \cong H_n(M - A,\ (M - x) - A) = H_n(U,\ U - x).$$

**Step 2: Reduce to the local model.** Via the chart homeomorphism $\varphi$,

$$H_n(U, U - x) \cong H_n(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R}^n - 0).$$

**Step 3: Long exact sequence of the pair.** Consider the long exact sequence of
 the pair $(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R}^n - 0)$:

$$\cdots \to H_n(\mathbb{R}^n) \to H_n(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R}^n - 0) \to H_{n-
1}(\mathbb{R}^n - 0) \to H_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^n) \to \cdots$$

Since $\mathbb{R}^n$ is contractible, $H_n(\mathbb{R}^n) = 0$ and $H_{n-1}(\math
bb{R}^n) = 0$. Therefore the connecting homomorphism gives an isomorphism

$$H_n(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R}^n - 0) \cong H_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^n - 0).$$

**Step 4: Homotopy equivalence.** The punctured space $\mathbb{R}^n - 0$ deforma
tion retracts onto the unit sphere $S^{n-1}$ via the radial retraction $v \mapst
o v/\|v\|$. Hence

$$H_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^n - 0) \cong H_{n-1}(S^{n-1}).$$

**Step 5: Sphere homology.** For $n \geq 1$, $H_{n-1}(S^{n-1}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$
 (the top-dimensional homology of an $(n-1)$-sphere is $\mathbb{Z}$, generated b
y the fundamental class).

(For $n = 0$: a closed $0$-manifold is a finite set of points, $M - x$ is the re
maining points, and $H_0(M, M - x) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ by the long exact sequence
since $M$ is a disjoint union of points and removing one leaves the rest with re
duced homology contributing exactly one $\mathbb{Z}$ factor. The same conclusion
 holds.)

**Conclusion.** Combining the isomorphisms from Steps 1–5,

$$\boxed{H_n(M, M - x) \cong \mathbb{Z}.}$$

This is the **local homology group** of $M$ at $x$; its generator is the local o
rientation class of $M$ at $x$. The result is independent of the particular poin
t $x$ and reflects the fact that an $n$-manifold is locally modeled on $\mathbb{
R}^n$.
