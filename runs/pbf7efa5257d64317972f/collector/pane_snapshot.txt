# Derivation of the Geodesic Equation from Arc Length Minimization

## Setup

Let $(M, g)$ be a Riemannian manifold with local coordinates $\{x^i\}_{i=1}^{n}$
 and metric tensor $g_{ij}(x)$. Consider a curve $\gamma: [a,b] \to M$ parameter
ized by $t \mapsto x^i(t)$ connecting two fixed points $P = \gamma(a)$ and $Q =
\gamma(b)$.

The arc length functional is

$$S[\gamma] = \int_a^b ds = \int_a^b \sqrt{g_{ij}(x)\,\dot x^i \dot x^j}\, dt,$$

where $\dot x^i := dx^i/dt$ and we use the Einstein summation convention.

## Step 1: Reparameterize by arc length

A geodesic that minimizes arc length, when parameterized by arc length $s$ itsel
f, satisfies $g_{ij}\,\frac{dx^i}{ds}\frac{dx^j}{ds} = 1$ (constant speed $|\dot
\gamma|=1$). It is technically cleaner to work with the **energy functional**

$$E[\gamma] = \tfrac{1}{2}\int_a^b g_{ij}\,\dot x^i \dot x^j\, dt,$$

because $E$ is quadratic in $\dot x$ (no square root), and by the Cauchy–Schwarz
 inequality $\left(\int ds\right)^2 \le 2(b-a)\,E$ with equality iff $g_{ij}\dot
 x^i\dot x^j$ is constant. Hence **minimizers of $E$ with constant-speed paramet
erization coincide with minimizers of $S$**. We therefore derive the Euler–Lagra
nge equations of $E$.

## Step 2: Identify the Lagrangian

$$L(x, \dot x) = \tfrac{1}{2}\, g_{ij}(x)\,\dot x^i \dot x^j.$$

## Step 3: Compute the Euler–Lagrange equations

The Euler–Lagrange equations are

$$\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot x^k} - \frac{\partial L}{\partial
x^k} = 0, \qquad k = 1,\dots,n.$$

**Momentum term:**

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot x^k} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,g_{ij}\,\delta^i_k \dot x
^j + \tfrac{1}{2}\,g_{ij}\,\dot x^i \delta^j_k = \tfrac{1}{2}(g_{kj}\dot x^j + g
_{ik}\dot x^i) = g_{ki}\dot x^i,$$

using symmetry $g_{ik}=g_{ki}$. Taking the total time derivative:

$$\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot x^k} = \frac{\partial g_{ki}}{\par
tial x^j}\dot x^j \dot x^i + g_{ki}\,\ddot x^i.$$

**Force term:**

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial x^k} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partia
l x^k}\,\dot x^i \dot x^j.$$

## Step 4: Assemble the equation of motion

Substituting:

$$g_{ki}\,\ddot x^i + \frac{\partial g_{ki}}{\partial x^j}\dot x^j \dot x^i - \t
frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial x^k}\dot x^i \dot x^j = 0.$$

Rename dummy indices in the middle term ($i \leftrightarrow j$ is symmetric in t
he product $\dot x^i\dot x^j$, so we may write $\partial_j g_{ki}\,\dot x^i\dot
x^j$). Collecting the quadratic velocity terms:

$$g_{ki}\,\ddot x^i + \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(\partial_j g_{ki} + \partial_i g_{kj}
- \partial_k g_{ij}\right)\dot x^i \dot x^j = 0,$$

where $\partial_k := \partial/\partial x^k$. (We used symmetry of $\dot x^i\dot
x^j$ to symmetrize the middle term: $\partial_j g_{ki}\dot x^i\dot x^j = \tfrac{
1}{2}(\partial_j g_{ki}+\partial_i g_{kj})\dot x^i\dot x^j$.)

## Step 5: Define the Christoffel symbols

The **Christoffel symbols of the second kind** are defined by raising an index o
n the Levi-Civita connection coefficients:

$$\Gamma^l_{\ ij} := \tfrac{1}{2}\,g^{lk}\!\left(\partial_i g_{kj} + \partial_j
g_{ki} - \partial_k g_{ij}\right),$$

where $g^{lk}$ is the inverse metric ($g^{lk}g_{ki} = \delta^l_i$). The brackete
d expression above is exactly $g^{lk}\Gamma^l_{\ ij}$ contracted back, i.e.

$$\tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(\partial_j g_{ki} + \partial_i g_{kj} - \partial_k g_{ij}\
right) = g_{kl}\,\Gamma^l_{\ ij}.$$

## Step 6: Obtain the geodesic equation

Substituting into the equation of motion:

$$g_{ki}\,\ddot x^i + g_{kl}\,\Gamma^l_{\ ij}\,\dot x^i \dot x^j = 0.$$

Multiplying through by $g^{mk}$ (and using $g^{mk}g_{ki} = \delta^m_i$, $g^{mk}g
_{kl} = \delta^m_l$):

$$\boxed{\;\ddot x^m + \Gamma^m_{\ ij}\,\dot x^i \dot x^j = 0\;}$$

for $m = 1, \dots, n$.

## Step 7: Recover the arc-length (affine) form

The derivation used the energy functional, whose extremals automatically have co
nstant $g_{ij}\dot x^i\dot x^j$. For a curve parameterized by **affine parameter
** $\lambda$ (of which arc length $s$ is the canonical choice up to affine resca
ling $\lambda \mapsto a\lambda + b$), the parameter is affine and the equation a
bove holds as written. If instead one uses a general (non-affine) parameter $t$,
 the equation acquires an extra term:

$$\ddot x^m + \Gamma^m_{\ ij}\dot x^i\dot x^j = f(t)\,\dot x^m,$$

where $f(t) = \frac{d}{dt}\ln\!\sqrt{g_{ij}\dot x^i\dot x^j}$. Re-parameterizing
 by arc length $s$ (so $g_{ij}\frac{dx^i}{ds}\frac{dx^j}{ds}=1$, making $f\equiv
 0$) recovers the clean form

$$\frac{d^2 x^m}{ds^2} + \Gamma^m_{\ ij}\frac{dx^i}{ds}\frac{dx^j}{ds} = 0.$$

## Summary

Starting from the arc-length-minimization principle and passing through the equi
valent energy functional $E = \tfrac12\int g_{ij}\dot x^i\dot x^j\,dt$ (whose co
nstant-speed extremals coincide with length-minimizing geodesics), the Euler–Lag
range equations yield

$$g_{ki}\ddot x^i + \tfrac12(\partial_j g_{ki}+\partial_i g_{kj}-\partial_k g_{i
j})\dot x^i\dot x^j = 0.$$

Recognizing the bracketed coefficient as $g_{kl}\Gamma^l_{ij}$ with

$$\Gamma^l_{\ ij} = \tfrac12 g^{lk}(\partial_i g_{kj}+\partial_j g_{ki}-\partial
_k g_{ij}),$$

and contracting with the inverse metric gives the **geodesic equation in Christo
ffel-symbol form**:

$$\ddot x^m + \Gamma^m_{\ ij}\,\dot x^i \dot x^j = 0.$$

This is the equation whose solutions are the autoparallel curves of the Levi-Civ
ita connection — equivalently, the curves that locally minimize arc length betwe
en their endpoints when parameterized affinely.
