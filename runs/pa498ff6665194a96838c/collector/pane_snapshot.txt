Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a differential system with
functions $u$ and $v$ holomorphic in the punctured disk $D^*(0,a)$ can admit a n
on-trivial holomorphic solution.

## Answer: Yes

**Claim.** There exists a differential system on $D^*(0,a)$ (with coefficients h
olomorphic on $D^*(0,a)$) admitting a non-trivial solution $(u,v)$ with both $u$
 and $v$ holomorphic on $D^*(0,a)$.

### Construction and Proof

Consider the system
$$\begin{cases} u'(z) = v(z), \\[4pt] v'(z) = -\dfrac{u(z)}{z}, \end{cases} \tag
{$\star$}$$
defined on the punctured disk $D^*(0,a) = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : 0 < |z| < a\}$ fo
r any $a > 0$. The coefficients $1$ and $-1/z$ are holomorphic on $D^*(0,a)$, so
 $(\star)$ is a valid linear ODE system on the punctured disk with a regular sin
gular point at $z = 0$.

**Eliminating $v$.** From $u' = v$ we get $v' = u''$, and substituting into the
second equation gives the scalar ODE:
$$z\,u''(z) + u(z) = 0. \tag{$\star\star$}$$

**Power series ansatz.** We seek a solution holomorphic at $z=0$ of the form $u(
z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n z^n$. Computing:
$$u''(z) = \sum_{n=2}^{\infty} n(n-1)\,c_n\, z^{n-2}, \qquad z\,u''(z) = \sum_{n
=1}^{\infty} (n+1)n\, c_{n+1}\, z^n.$$

Substituting into $(\star\star)$:
$$z\,u'' + u = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigl[(n+1)n\,c_{n+1} + c_n\bigr] z^n = 0.$$

Equating coefficients:

- **$n = 0$:** $0 \cdot c_1 + c_0 = 0 \implies c_0 = 0$.
- **$n \geq 1$:** $(n+1)n\,c_{n+1} + c_n = 0 \implies c_{n+1} = -\dfrac{c_n}{n(n
+1)}$.

The coefficient $c_1$ is a free parameter. Setting $c_1 = 1$ and iterating the r
ecurrence:

$$c_n = \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n!\,(n-1)!}, \qquad n \geq 1.$$

**Verification by induction.** Base case: $c_1 = (-1)^0/(1!\cdot 0!) = 1$. ✓
Inductive step: $c_{n+1} = -c_n/(n(n+1)) = -\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n!(n-1)!\cdot n(n+
1)} = \frac{(-1)^n}{(n+1)!\,n!}$. ✓

**Convergence.** By the ratio test, $\left|\frac{c_{n+1} z^{n+1}}{c_n z^n}\right
| = \frac{|z|}{n(n+1)} \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$ for every $z \in \mathbb{C}$. He
nce the series converges absolutely for all $z$, so:

$$u(z) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n!\,(n-1)!}\, z^n$$

is an **entire function** (holomorphic on all of $\mathbb{C}$), and in particula
r holomorphic on $D^*(0,a)$.

**Recovering $v$.** Since $v = u'$:

$$v(z) = u'(z) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n-1}\, n}{n!\,(n-1)!}\, z^{n-1}
 = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!\,(n-1)!}\, z^{n-1} = \sum_{m=0}^
{\infty} \frac{(-1)^m}{(m!)^2}\, z^m.$$

This is also an entire function (ratio test: $|z|/(m+1)^2 \to 0$), hence holomor
phic on $D^*(0,a)$.

**Direct verification of the system.** We confirm both equations of $(\star)$:

1. $u'(z) = v(z)$: established above by construction. ✓

2. $v'(z) = -u(z)/z$:
$$v'(z) = \sum_{m=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^m\, m}{(m!)^2}\, z^{m-1} = \sum_{m=1}^{
\infty} \frac{(-1)^m}{m!\,(m-1)!}\, z^{m-1},$$
$$-\frac{u(z)}{z} = -\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n!\,(n-1)!}\, z^{n-1}
 = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n!\,(n-1)!}\, z^{n-1}.$$

Setting $m = n$, these are identical. ✓

**Non-triviality.** We have $u(z) = z - \frac{z^2}{2} + \frac{z^3}{12} - \cdots$
 and $v(z) = 1 - z + \frac{z^2}{4} - \cdots$. In particular, $v(0) = 1 \neq 0$ a
nd $u'(0) = 1 \neq 0$, so neither $u$ nor $v$ is identically zero. The solution
$(u, v)$ is non-trivial.

### Conclusion

The differential system $(\star)$ on $D^*(0,a)$ admits the non-trivial solution
$$u(z) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n!\,(n-1)!}\, z^n, \qquad v(z) =
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^m}{(m!)^2}\, z^m,$$
where both $u$ and $v$ are holomorphic on $D^*(0,a)$ (in fact, entire). Therefor
e, **yes**, such a differential system can have a non-trivial holomorphic soluti
on.
