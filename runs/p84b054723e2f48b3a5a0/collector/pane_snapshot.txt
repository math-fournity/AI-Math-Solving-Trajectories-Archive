## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, such a function exists.

**Construction.** Take $[a,b]=[0,1]$ and let $f = \mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Q}}$ be th
e Dirichlet function, i.e. the indicator of the rationals on $[0,1]$:
$$f(x) = \begin{cases} 1, & x \in \mathbb{Q} \cap [0,1], \\ 0, & x \in [0,1]\set
minus \mathbb{Q}. \end{cases}$$

**Step 1: $f$ is Lebesgue integrable.** Since $\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]$ is countable
, it has Lebesgue measure $0$. The function $f$ is a non-negative simple functio
n, measurable, and
$$\int_{[0,1]} f\,d\mu = 1 \cdot \mu(\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]) + 0 \cdot \mu([0,1]\se
tminus\mathbb{Q}) = 1\cdot 0 + 0\cdot 1 = 0.$$
Hence $f$ is Lebesgue integrable with $\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx = 0$.

**Step 2: $f$ is not Riemann integrable.** For any partition of $[0,1]$, every s
ubinterval contains both rational and irrational points (by density of $\mathbb{
Q}$ and $\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}$). Thus on every subinterval the supremum
 of $f$ is $1$ and the infimum is $0$. Therefore every upper sum equals $1$ and
every lower sum equals $0$. The upper and lower integrals disagree:
$$\overline{\int_0^1} f = 1 \ne 0 = \underline{\int_0^1} f,$$
so $f$ is not Riemann integrable.

**Step 3: There exists $c\in[0,1]$ with $f(c)\le \int_0^1 f(x)\,dx$.** The Lebes
gue integral is $0$. Choose any irrational point $c\in[0,1]$, e.g. $c = 1/\sqrt{
2}\in(0,1)$. Then $f(c)=0$, and
$$f(c) = 0 \le 0 = \int_0^1 f(x)\,dx.$$

Thus the Dirichlet function on $[0,1]$ is Lebesgue integrable, not Riemann integ
rable, and satisfies the required inequality at the irrational point $c=1/\sqrt{
2}$.
