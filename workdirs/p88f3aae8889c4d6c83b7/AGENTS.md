# Problem

In the competitive world of professional coffee roasting, a Master Roaster uses a specific rating system to evaluate the quality of small-batch bean shipments. Every shipment is assigned a quality score, which must be a positive integer $n$.

The Roaster refuses to work with "Perfect Batches," which are defined as shipments where the quality score $n$ is the cube of an integer. 

The industrial efficiency of the roasting process is determined by the relationship between the score and its "Bulk Tier." The Bulk Tier of a shipment is defined as the floor of the cube root of the score, denoted as $\lfloor \sqrt[3]{n} \rfloor$. According to the facility's strict protocols, a shipment is only considered "Processable" if the square of its quality score ($n^2$) is exactly divisible by the fifth power of its Bulk Tier.

Determine the largest possible integer quality score $n$ that represents a shipment that is not a "Perfect Batch" but is still "Processable."
