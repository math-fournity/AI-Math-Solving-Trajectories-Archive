Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the "finite fiber" locus is
 open in $\operatorname{Spec} A$, given the hypotheses.

**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

**Setup.** Since $A \subseteq B$ are both finitely generated $\mathbb{C}$-domain
s, $B$ is a finitely generated $A$-algebra (the $\mathbb{C}$-algebra generators
of $B$ also generate it over $A$). The inclusion $A \hookrightarrow B$ induces a
 morphism $\varphi: \operatorname{Spec} B \to \operatorname{Spec} A$ of finite t
ype between Noetherian schemes. Since $A$ and $B$ are domains and the map is inj
ective, $\varphi$ is dominant.

**Step 1: The generic fiber is finite (dimension 0).**

For a dominant morphism of finite type between irreducible schemes, the dimensio
n formula gives:
$$\dim B = \dim A + \dim\left(B \otimes_A K(A)\right),$$
where $K(A) = \operatorname{Frac}(A)$ is the function field and the generic fibe
r is $B \otimes_A K(A)$. Since $\dim A = \dim B$, the generic fiber has dimensio
n $0$. Being a finitely generated $K(A)$-algebra of dimension $0$, it is Artinia
n, hence has finitely many prime ideals. So the generic point $\eta \in \operato
rname{Spec} A$ has finite fiber.

**Step 2: The finite-fiber locus is open.**

By Chevalley's upper semicontinuity of fiber dimension (EGA IV, 13.1.3), for a f
inite-type morphism of Noetherian schemes, the function
$$\mathfrak{p} \mapsto \dim\!\left(B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p})\right)$$
is upper semicontinuous on $\operatorname{Spec} A$. Therefore the set
$$U := \left\{\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Spec} A : \dim\!\left(B \otimes_A \
kappa(\mathfrak{p})\right) = 0\right\}$$
is open (it is the complement of the closed set where the fiber dimension is $\g
eq 1$).

For a finitely generated $\kappa(\mathfrak{p})$-algebra, having dimension $0$ is
 equivalent to being Artinian, which is equivalent to having finitely many prime
 ideals. Moreover, the primes of $B$ lying over $\mathfrak{p}$ are in bijection
with the primes of $B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p})$. Thus:
$$U = \left\{\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Spec} A : \text{finitely many primes
 of } B \text{ lie over } \mathfrak{p}\right\}.$$

**Step 3: $U$ is non-empty and contains $P$.**

$U$ is non-empty because it contains the generic point (Step 1). By hypothesis,
$P$ has finitely many primes of $B$ lying over it, so $P \in U$.

**Step 4: Construct $f$.**

Since $U$ is open and $P \in U$, and basic open sets $D(g) = \{\mathfrak{p} : g
\notin \mathfrak{p}\}$ form a basis for the Zariski topology on $\operatorname{S
pec} A$, there exists $f \in A \setminus P$ such that $D(f) \subseteq U$.

**Step 5: Verify the conclusion.**

The primes of $A_f$ are exactly the primes of $A$ not containing $f$, i.e., the
elements of $D(f) \subseteq U$. For any such prime $\mathfrak{p}$, since $\mathf
rak{p} \in U$, the fiber $B \otimes_A \kappa(\mathfrak{p})$ is Artinian, hence h
as finitely many prime ideals. These prime ideals are in bijection with the prim
es $\mathfrak{q}$ of $B$ satisfying $\mathfrak{q} \cap A = \mathfrak{p}$. (Note:
 since $f \notin \mathfrak{p}$ and $\mathfrak{q} \cap A = \mathfrak{p}$, automat
ically $f \notin \mathfrak{q}$, so these primes also correspond to primes of $B_
f$ lying over $\mathfrak{p}$ in the localized extension $A_f \subseteq B_f$.)

Therefore every prime ideal of $A_f$ has finitely many prime ideals of $B$ lying
 over it. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The regularity of $A$ is not needed for this argument; the result ho
lds for any dominant finite-type extension $A \subseteq B$ of finitely generated
 $\mathbb{C}$-domains of equal Krull dimension.
