### Key Observation

The Gaussian kernel
$$\phi_\epsilon(x,y) = \frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2+y^2}{2\epsil
on}\right)$$
is the density of a 2D Gaussian with variance $\epsilon$ in each coordinate, hen
ce $\phi_\epsilon \to \delta(x)\delta(y)$ as $\epsilon\to 0$.

Differentiating $\phi_\epsilon$ with respect to $y$:
$$\frac{\partial \phi_\epsilon}{\partial y}(x,y) = \frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\cdot\f
rac{-y}{\epsilon}\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2+y^2}{2\epsilon}\right).$$

Therefore the integrand's weight is exactly
$$\frac{y}{\epsilon}\,\phi_\epsilon(x,y) = -\frac{\partial \phi_\epsilon}{\parti
al y}(x,y),$$
and the given expression becomes
$$I = \lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\iint_{\mathbb R^2} f(x,y)\left(-\frac{\partial \phi_\
epsilon}{\partial y}\right)dx\,dy.$$

### Integration by Parts

Since $f$ and $\nabla f$ are continuous and bounded, and $\phi_\epsilon$ is a Sc
hwartz function, we integrate by parts in $y$:
$$\iint f(x,y)\left(-\frac{\partial \phi_\epsilon}{\partial y}\right)dx\,dy = \i
int \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(x,y)\,\phi_\epsilon(x,y)\,dx\,dy,$$
where the boundary term $\bigl[f\,\phi_\epsilon\bigr]_{y=-\infty}^{y=+\infty}$ v
anishes because $\phi_\epsilon$ decays super-exponentially while $f$ is bounded.

Thus
$$I = \lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\iint_{\mathbb R^2} \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(x,y)
\,\phi_\epsilon(x,y)\,dx\,dy.$$

### Taking the Limit

The kernel $\phi_\epsilon$ is an approximate identity: $\phi_\epsilon \to \delta
_{(0,0)}$ as $\epsilon\to 0$. Since $\partial f/\partial y$ is continuous (hence
 continuous at the origin), the standard property of approximate identities give
s
$$\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\iint \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(x,y)\,\phi_\epsilon(x,
y)\,dx\,dy = \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(0,0).$$

### Verification via Direct Computation

Set $x=\sqrt\epsilon\,u,\; y=\sqrt\epsilon\,v$, so $dx\,dy = \epsilon\,du\,dv$.
Then
$$I = \lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt\epsilon}\iint f(\sqrt\epsilon\,u,\s
qrt\epsilon\,v)\,v\,e^{-(u^2+v^2)/2}\,du\,dv.$$

Taylor expand $f$ at the origin:
$$f(\sqrt\epsilon\,u,\sqrt\epsilon\,v) = f(0,0) + \sqrt\epsilon\,u\,f_x(0,0) + \
sqrt\epsilon\,v\,f_y(0,0) + O(\epsilon).$$

- **$f(0,0)$ term:** $\displaystyle\frac{f(0,0)}{2\pi\sqrt\epsilon}\iint v\,e^{-
(u^2+v^2)/2}\,du\,dv = 0$ (integrand odd in $v$).
- **$f_x$ term:** $\displaystyle\frac{f_x(0,0)}{2\pi}\iint uv\,e^{-(u^2+v^2)/2}\
,du\,dv = 0$ (odd in both variables).
- **$f_y$ term:** $\displaystyle\frac{f_y(0,0)}{2\pi}\iint v^2\,e^{-(u^2+v^2)/2}
\,du\,dv = \frac{f_y(0,0)}{2\pi}\cdot\sqrt{2\pi}\cdot\sqrt{2\pi} = f_y(0,0).$
- **$O(\epsilon)$ terms:** contribute $O(\sqrt\epsilon)\to 0$.

Both methods agree.

### Result

$$\boxed{I = \dfrac{\partial f}{\partial y}(0,0)}$$
