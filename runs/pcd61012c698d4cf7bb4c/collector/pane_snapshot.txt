# Proof

**Answer: No.** We cannot deduce the existence of such a $G'$. A counterexample
suffices.

## Setup

Let $M$ be a transitive countable model of $\mathrm{ZFC} + V=L$, and let $c \in
2^\omega$ be a Cohen real over $M$, so $G = c$ and $M[G] = M[c]$.

## The Counterexample

Define $A = c \upharpoonright [1,\infty)$, i.e., $A(n) = c(n+1)$ for all $n \in
\omega$. This is the "tail" of $c$ starting from the second bit.

**Claim 1: $A$ is Cohen generic over $M$.**

The shift map $\sigma : 2^\omega \to 2^\omega$, $\sigma(x)(n) = x(n+1)$, is cont
inuous. For any $a \in 2^\omega$, the fiber $\sigma^{-1}[\{a\}] = \{0^\smallfrow
n a,\; 1^\smallfrown a\}$ consists of exactly two points, hence is nowhere dense
. A standard result says: if $f : 2^\omega \to 2^\omega$ is continuous and nowhe
re constant (every fiber is nowhere dense), then $f(c)$ is Cohen over $M$ whenev
er $c$ is Cohen over $M$. Therefore $A = \sigma(c)$ is Cohen generic over $M = \
mathbb{L}$. ✓

**Claim 2: $M[A] \neq M[c]$.**

The Cohen forcing $2^{<\omega}$ factors as $\mathrm{Fn}(1,2) \times 2^{<\omega}$
 (the first bit × the remaining bits). Under this factorization, $c(0)$ is gener
ic over $M$ for $\mathrm{Fn}(1,2)$, and $A = c \upharpoonright [1,\infty)$ is ge
neric over $M$ for $2^{<\omega}$, and they are mutually generic. In particular,
$c(0)$ is generic over $M[A]$ for $\mathrm{Fn}(1,2)$, so $c(0) \notin M[A]$. Sin
ce $c(0) \in M[c]$, we have $M[A] \neq M[c]$. ✓

**Claim 3: $M[c] = M[A][c(0)]$.**

Since $c = c(0)^\smallfrown A$ (concatenation of the first bit with the tail), $
c$ is definable from $c(0)$ and $A$. Thus $c \in M[A][c(0)]$, giving $M[c] \subs
eteq M[A][c(0)]$. Conversely, $A \in M[c]$ and $c(0) \in M[c]$, so $M[A][c(0)] \
subseteq M[c]$. Hence equality. ✓

## Key Lemma: No Cohen real over $M[A]$ exists in $M[c]$

**Lemma.** Every real $r \in 2^\omega \cap M[A][c(0)]$ is not Cohen generic over
 $M[A]$.

*Proof.* The extension $M[A][c(0)]$ is obtained from $M[A]$ by the forcing $\mat
hrm{Fn}(1,2) = \{\emptyset, \{(0,0)\}, \{(0,1)\}\}$, which has exactly two atoms
. Let $\dot{r}$ be an $\mathrm{Fn}(1,2)$-name for a real $r \in 2^\omega$. For e
ach $n$, the value $\dot{r}(n)$ is decided by some condition. Since the only non
-trivial conditions are the two atoms, there exist reals $r_0, r_1 \in M[A]$ suc
h that:

$$r = \begin{cases} r_0 & \text{if } c(0) = 0, \\ r_1 & \text{if } c(0) = 1. \en
d{cases}$$

So $r$ is either $r_0$ or $r_1$, both of which are in $M[A]$.

- **If $r_0 = r_1$**: then $r = r_0 \in M[A]$. The set $D = \{s \in 2^{<\omega}
: s \not\subseteq r_0\}$ is dense open in $M[A]$ (for any $t$, some extension of
 $t$ is not a prefix of $r_0$). No initial segment of $r_0$ lies in $D$, so $r_0
$ does not meet $D$. Hence $r$ is not Cohen over $M[A]$.

- **If $r_0 \neq r_1$**: define $D_0 = \{s \in 2^{<\omega} : s \not\subseteq r_0
\}$ and $D_1 = \{s \in 2^{<\omega} : s \not\subseteq r_1\}$. Both are dense open
 in $M[A]$ (since $r_0, r_1 \in M[A]$). Their intersection $D = D_0 \cap D_1$ is
 dense open (in $2^{<\omega}$, finite intersections of dense open sets are dense
 open). Now $r_0$ does not meet $D_0$ (no initial segment of $r_0$ is in $D_0$),
 so $r_0$ does not meet $D$. Similarly $r_1$ does not meet $D_1$, so $r_1$ does
not meet $D$. Since $r \in \{r_0, r_1\}$, $r$ does not meet $D$. Hence $r$ is no
t Cohen over $M[A]$.

In both cases, $r$ is not Cohen generic over $M[A]$. $\square$

## Conclusion

Since $M[c] = M[A][c(0)]$ (Claim 3), every real in $M[c]$ lies in $M[A][c(0)]$ a
nd hence is not Cohen generic over $M[A]$ by the Lemma. Therefore, **there is no
 $G' \in M[c]$ that is Cohen generic over $M[A]$**, and consequently no $G'$ Coh
en generic over $M[A]$ with $M[A][G'] = M[G]$.

The obstruction is that the "quotient" from $M[A]$ to $M[G]$ is the finite forci
ng $\mathrm{Fn}(1,2)$ (a single bit), which is strictly smaller than Cohen forci
ng $2^{<\omega}$. A Cohen real requires $\aleph_0$ independent bits of genericit
y, but the extension $M[A][c(0)]$ over $M[A]$ contributes only a single bit — in
sufficient to support any Cohen real.

Thus the answer to the question is **No**: from the hypotheses alone, one cannot
 deduce the existence of such a $G'$.
