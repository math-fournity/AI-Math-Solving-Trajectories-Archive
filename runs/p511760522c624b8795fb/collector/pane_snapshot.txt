## Solution

**Setup.** Let $a_n$ denote the number of length-$n$ words over a 3-letter alpha
bet with no nontrivial border (no $k$ with $1\le k<n$ and prefix$_k$ = suffix$_k
$). We seek $v_3(a_{2023})$.

**Key structural lemma.** *If a word of length $n$ has shortest border of length
 $k$, then $k\le \lfloor n/2\rfloor$.*

*Proof.* Suppose the shortest border has length $k>n/2$, and set $p=n-k<n/2$. Si
nce prefix$_k$ = suffix$_k$ and these overlap in $2k-n>0$ positions, the word ha
s period $p$: $w[i]=w[i+p]$ for $1\le i\le n-p$. Then $w[1..n-2p]=w[2p+1..n]$, g
iving a border of length $n-2p$. Since $p<n/2$, we have $n-2p>0$ and $n-2p<n-p=k
$, contradicting minimality of $k$. $\square$

**Recurrence.** Count words of length $n$ by shortest border length $k$ (with $1
\le k\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor$). For shortest border exactly $k$: the prefix $w[1..
k]$ must be unbordered ($a_k$ choices), the suffix is forced ($w[n-k+1..n]=w[1..
k]$), and the middle $w[k+1..n-k]$ is free ($3^{n-2k}$ choices). No shorter bord
er can appear: a border of length $j<k$ would force $w[1..j]=w[k-j+1..k]$, i.e.
a border of the unbordered prefix—impossible. Hence

$$3^n = a_n + \sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor} a_k\cdot 3^{n-2k},\qquad a_n = 3^n
 - \sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor} a_k\cdot 3^{n-2k}.$$

**3-adic analysis.** Let $f(n)=v_3(a_n)$ and $g(k)=f(k)-2k$. The sum $S_n=\sum_k
 a_k 3^{n-2k}$ has
$$v_3(S_n)=\min_{1\le k\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\bigl(f(k)+n-2k\bigr)=n+\min_{k\le\
lfloor n/2\rfloor}g(k).$$

I claim $g$ is **strictly decreasing**. First note $f(n+1)-f(n)\le 1$ for all $n
$ (proved by induction: when $n$ is even the step equals $1$; when $n$ is odd it
 equals $f(\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1)-f(\lfloor n/2\rfloor)-1\le 0$). Then:
- $k$ even: $g(k+1)-g(k)=f(k+1)-f(k)-2=1-2=-1<0$.
- $k$ odd: $g(k+1)-g(k)=f(\tfrac{k+1}{2})-f(\tfrac{k-1}{2})-3\le 1-3=-2<0$.

So $g$ is strictly decreasing; the minimum over $k\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ is atta
ined **uniquely** at $k=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. Since $g(1)=-1<0$, we get $v_3(S_n)
=n+g(\lfloor n/2\rfloor)<n$, and therefore (the unique-min term fixes the valuat
ion, and $3^n$ has higher valuation):

$$f(n)=v_3(S_n)=f\!\left(\left\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rfloor\right)+n-2\left\l
floor\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rfloor = f\!\left(\left\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rfloor\
right)+(n\bmod 2).$$

**Solving the recurrence.** With $f(1)=v_3(3)=1$, unrolling gives
$$f(n)=\sum_{i\ge 0}\left(\left\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2^i}\right\rfloor\bmod 2\right)=
s_2(n),$$
the number of $1$-bits in the binary expansion of $n$ (popcount). Verification:
$f(1),\dots,f(13)=1,1,2,1,2,2,3,1,2,2,3,2,3$, matching $s_2(1),\dots,s_2(13)$.

**Applying to $n=2023$.** Converting to binary:
$$2023 = 1024+512+256+128+64+32+4+2+1 = (11111100111)_2.$$
The binary representation $11111100111$ contains **9** ones (six leading ones, t
wo zeros, three trailing ones: $6+3=9$).

Therefore
$$v_3(a_{2023})=s_2(2023)=9,$$

and the largest integer $m$ such that $3^m\mid a_{2023}$ is

$$\boxed{m=9}.$$
