## Proof

### 1. Geometric data of Galileo spacetime

Galileo spacetime is a 4‑manifold $M$ equipped with:

- a **temporal metric** $t_{\mu\nu}$ of rank 1 (a degenerate "metric" giving abs
olute time),
- a **spatial metric** $h^{\mu\nu}$ of rank 3, positive definite on spatial dire
ctions,
- the compatibility $t_{\mu\nu}h^{\nu\rho}=0$,
- a **Cartan connection** $\nabla$: the unique torsion‑free connection satisfyin
g
  $$\nabla_\lambda t_{\mu\nu}=0,\qquad \nabla_\lambda h^{\mu\nu}=0,$$
  together with the Newton–Cartan curvature conditions
  $$R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\,t_\rho=0,\qquad R^i{}_{jkl}=0,\qquad R^i{}_{0j0}=R^
j{}_{0i0},$$
  the last being the integrability condition that makes the gravitational field
*conservative* (locally a gradient).

Choose adapted coordinates $(x^0=t,\,x^i)$ in which the absolute time function i
s $T=x^0$, so
$$t_{\mu\nu}=\delta^0_\mu\delta^0_\nu,\qquad h^{ij}=\delta^{ij},\qquad h^{0\mu}=
0.$$

### 2. Vanishing of $\Gamma^0_{\lambda\mu}$ and $\Gamma^i_{jk}$

From $\nabla_\lambda t_{\mu\nu}=0$ and $\partial_\lambda t_{\mu\nu}=0$:
$$-\Gamma^\rho_{\lambda\mu}t_{\rho\nu}-\Gamma^\rho_{\lambda\nu}t_{\rho\mu}=0
\;\Longrightarrow\; -\Gamma^0_{\lambda\mu}\delta^0_\nu-\Gamma^0_{\lambda\nu}\del
ta^0_\mu=0.$$
Setting $\nu=0$ gives
$$\boxed{\Gamma^0_{\lambda\mu}=0\quad\text{for all }\lambda,\mu.}\tag{1}$$

From $\nabla_\lambda h^{\mu\nu}=0$ with $\mu=i,\nu=j,\lambda=k$ all spatial, and
 $\partial_k h^{ij}=0$:
$$\Gamma^i_{kj}+\Gamma^j_{ki}=0\quad\text{(antisymmetry in }i,j\text{)}.\tag{2}$
$
Torsion‑freeness gives $\Gamma^i_{kj}=\Gamma^i_{jk}$ (symmetry in $j,k$). Combin
ing (2) with this symmetry and cycling indices:
$$\Gamma^i_{jk}=-\Gamma^j_{ik}=-\Gamma^j_{ki}=\Gamma^k_{ji}=\Gamma^k_{ij}=-\Gamm
a^i_{kj}=-\Gamma^i_{jk},$$
hence
$$\boxed{\Gamma^i_{jk}=0.}\tag{3}$$

The only possibly non‑vanishing component is $\Gamma^i_{00}$. The curvature symm
etry $R^i{}_{0j0}=R^j{}_{0i0}$ is precisely $\partial_j\Gamma^i_{00}=\partial_i\
Gamma^j_{00}$, so locally there exists a gravitational potential $\Phi$ with
$$\Gamma^i_{00}=h^{ij}\partial_j\Phi=\partial^i\Phi.\tag{4}$$

### 3. The geodesic equation

Let $\gamma^\mu(\lambda)$ be an affinely parametrized geodesic:
$$\frac{d^2x^\mu}{d\lambda^2}+\Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho}\frac{dx^\nu}{d\lambda}\frac{d
x^\rho}{d\lambda}=0.\tag{5}$$

**Time component ($\mu=0$).** Using (1), $\Gamma^0_{\nu\rho}=0$, so
$$\frac{d^2t}{d\lambda^2}=0\;\Longrightarrow\; t(\lambda)=a\lambda+b,\qquad a,b=
\text{const}.\tag{6}$$

**Spatial components ($\mu=i$).** By (3) only $\Gamma^i_{00}$ survives:
$$\frac{d^2x^i}{d\lambda^2}+\Gamma^i_{00}\Big(\frac{dt}{d\lambda}\Big)^2=0.\tag{
7}$$

### 4. Timelike geodesics: free fall ($a\neq 0$)

When $a=dt/d\lambda\neq 0$, reparametrize the curve by absolute time $t$. Since
$dt/d\lambda=a$ is constant,
$$\frac{d^2x^i}{d\lambda^2}=a^2\frac{d^2x^i}{dt^2},$$
and (7) reduces to
$$\frac{d^2x^i}{dt^2}=-\Gamma^i_{00}\stackrel{(4)}{=}-\partial^i\Phi.\tag{8}$$

This is **exactly Newton's equation for a particle moving freely in a gravitatio
nal potential** $\Phi$:
$$\ddot{\mathbf{x}}=-\nabla\Phi.$$

Conversely, every solution of (8), parametrized by $t$, satisfies (5) with $\lam
bda=t$. Therefore:

> **The worldlines of free‑falling particles are precisely the geodesics of the
Cartan connection with $dt/d\lambda\neq 0$, parametrized (up to an affine rescal
ing) by absolute time.**

The curvature condition $R^i{}_{0j0}=R^j{}_{0i0}$ guarantees the field is a grad
ient, i.e. that gravity is conservative — this is what distinguishes *Newtonian*
 gravity from an arbitrary force field and is built into the very definition of
the Cartan connection.

### 5. Spacelike geodesics: the other geodesics ($a=0$)

When $a=dt/d\lambda=0$, equation (6) gives $t=b=\text{const}$: the curve lies en
tirely within a single hypersurface of simultaneity $\Sigma_b$. Equation (7) bec
omes
$$\frac{d^2x^i}{d\lambda^2}=0,\tag{9}$$
since $\Gamma^i_{jk}=0$ by (3). Thus the curve is a **straight line in the spati
al slice $\Sigma_b$**, affinely parametrized.

These are *spatial* geodesics: their tangent vector $v^\mu$ satisfies $t_\mu v^\
mu=0$, so they are tangent to the instantaneous space, not to time evolution.

### 6. Physical interpretation

| Geodesic type | Condition | Equation | Physical meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Timelike** ($dt/d\lambda\neq 0$) | parametrized by absolute time $t$ | $\ddo
t{\mathbf{x}}=-\nabla\Phi$ | Worldlines of **free‑falling massive particles** (N
ewton's law). |
| **Spacelike** ($dt/d\lambda=0$) | $t=\text{const}$, lies in a slice $\Sigma_t$
 | $\ddot{\mathbf{x}}=0$ (straight line) | **Straight lines of instantaneous Euc
lidean space**. |

The spacelike geodesics do **not** represent the motion of any physical particle
: a particle worldline must advance in absolute time ($dt/d\lambda\neq 0$), and
$dt/d\lambda=0$ would correspond to "infinite velocity," which is unphysical in
Newtonian mechanics. Rather, they encode the **spatial geometry** of each instan
taneous slice — the Euclidean straight lines along which a rigid rod can be laid
, or along which one sights a target. They reflect the fact that each surface of
 simultaneity, equipped with the spatial metric $h^{ij}$ and the flat spatial co
nnection ($\Gamma^i_{jk}=0$), is ordinary flat Euclidean 3‑space, whose geodesic
s are straight lines.

In summary, the Cartan connection unifies two pieces of Newtonian physics in a s
ingle geometric object:

- its **timelike** geodesics reproduce the dynamics of free fall (Newton's gravi
tational law, encoded in $\Gamma^i_{00}=\partial^i\Phi$);
- its **spacelike** geodesics reproduce the kinematics of instantaneous space (E
uclidean straight lines, encoded in $\Gamma^i_{jk}=0$).

The split is enforced by the degeneracy of the temporal metric: $\Gamma^0_{\lamb
da\mu}=0$ forces every geodesic's time coordinate to be affine in the parameter,
 cleanly separating "particles that fall" from "lines that lie in an instant."
