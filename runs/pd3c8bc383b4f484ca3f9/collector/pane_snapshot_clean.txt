## Proof

Let $\Omega$ be a region (simply or multiply connected) with piecewise smooth bo
undary $\Gamma$, and let $u$ be harmonic on $\Omega$, so that $u$ has continuous
 first- and second-order partial derivatives in $\Omega$ and

$$
u_{xx} + u_{yy} = 0 \quad \text{on } \Omega.
$$

We wish to show that

$$
\int_{\Gamma} \frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\, ds = 0.
$$

**Key identity.** The normal derivative of $u$ along $\Gamma$ is

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial n} = \nabla u \cdot \mathbf{n},
$$

where $\mathbf{n}$ is the outward-pointing unit normal vector to $\Gamma$ and $\
nabla u = (u_x, u_y)$ is the gradient of $u$. Thus the boundary integral can be
rewritten as

$$
\int_{\Gamma} \frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\, ds = \int_{\Gamma} \nabla u \cdot
\mathbf{n}\, ds.
$$

**Applying the divergence theorem (Green's theorem in the plane).** The divergen
ce theorem states that for any continuously differentiable vector field $\mathbf
{F}$ defined on a region $\Omega$ with boundary $\Gamma$,

$$
\int_{\Gamma} \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n}\, ds = \iint_{\Omega} \nabla \cdot \ma
thbf{F}\, dA.
$$

We apply this with $\mathbf{F} = \nabla u = (u_x, u_y)$. Since $u$ has continuou
s second-order partial derivatives on $\Omega$, the vector field $\nabla u$ is c
ontinuously differentiable, and the divergence theorem applies. We obtain

$$
\int_{\Gamma} \nabla u \cdot \mathbf{n}\, ds = \iint_{\Omega} \nabla \cdot (\nab
la u)\, dA = \iint_{\Omega} (u_{xx} + u_{yy})\, dA.
$$

**Using harmonicity.** Since $u$ is harmonic on $\Omega$, we have $u_{xx} + u_{y
y} = 0$ at every point of $\Omega$. Therefore,

$$
\iint_{\Omega} (u_{xx} + u_{yy})\, dA = \iint_{\Omega} 0\, dA = 0.
$$

**Conclusion.** Chaining the equalities together,

$$
\int_{\Gamma} \frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\, ds = \int_{\Gamma} \nabla u \cdot
\mathbf{n}\, ds = \iint_{\Omega} (u_{xx} + u_{yy})\, dA = \iint_{\Omega} 0\, dA
= 0.
$$

Hence the normal derivative of any harmonic function $u$ on $\Omega$ satisfies t
he compatibility condition

$$
\boxed{\int_{\Gamma} \frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\, ds = 0.}
$$

**Remark on multiply connected regions.** The divergence theorem holds for simpl
y and multiply connected regions with piecewise smooth boundaries (the boundary
$\Gamma$ may consist of several disjoint closed curves, all oriented so that $\m
athbf{n}$ is the outward normal). The argument above applies unchanged, since th
e divergence theorem only requires that $\Omega$ be a bounded region with piecew
ise smooth boundary and that $\mathbf{F} = \nabla u$ be $C^1$ on $\Omega$—both o
f which are guaranteed by the hypotheses. $\blacksquare$
