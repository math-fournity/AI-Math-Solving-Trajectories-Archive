## Proof

We determine the cardinality of
$$S = \{(x,y,z)\mid x^2+y^2+z^2 = 2^{2018},\; xyz\in\mathbb Z\},$$
where $x,y,z$ range over the reals (the condition $xyz\in\mathbb Z$ would be vac
uous if $x,y,z\in\mathbb Z$, so the natural reading is $x,y,z\in\mathbb R$).

**Upper bound.** Clearly $S\subseteq\mathbb R^3$, so $|S|\le|\mathbb R^3| = 2^{\
aleph_0}$ (the cardinality of the continuum).

**Lower bound.** Consider the subset of $S$ obtained by setting $x=0$:
$$T = \{(0,y,z)\mid y^2+z^2 = 2^{2018}\}.$$
For every $(0,y,z)\in T$ we have $xyz = 0\cdot y\cdot z = 0\in\mathbb Z$, and $0
^2+y^2+z^2 = 2^{2018}$, so $T\subseteq S$.

But $T$ is a circle in the plane $x=0$: it is $\{(0,y,z): y^2+z^2 = (2^{1009})^2
\}$, a circle of radius $2^{1009}>0$. A (non‑degenerate) circle in $\mathbb R^2$
 has the cardinality of the continuum, i.e. $|T|=2^{\aleph_0}$.

Hence $|S|\ge|T| = 2^{\aleph_0}$.

**Conclusion.** Combining the two bounds,
$$2^{\aleph_0}\le|S|\le 2^{\aleph_0},$$
so
$$|S| = 2^{\aleph_0},$$
the cardinality of the continuum.

(The specific value $2^{2018}$ only guarantees the circle is non‑degenerate; any
 positive right‑hand side would give the same cardinality, since $xyz=0\in\mathb
b Z$ already forces an uncountable family of solutions, while $S\subseteq\mathbb
 R^3$ caps the size at the continuum.)
