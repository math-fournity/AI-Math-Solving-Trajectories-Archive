## Proof

**Yes**, such a Schwartz function exists. We exhibit an explicit example.

**Setup.** Take $M = N = 1$ and $\varphi(x) = e^{-x^2}$, the Gaussian. This is a
 Schwartz function: every semi-norm $\|\varphi\|_{\alpha,\beta}$ is finite, sinc
e $e^{-x^2}$ decays faster than any polynomial, and each derivative $D^n e^{-x^2
}$ is $e^{-x^2}$ times a polynomial (hence also rapidly decaying).

---

### Part 1: $\|\varphi\|_{0,n} \to \infty$ as $n \to \infty$

Recall that $D^n e^{-x^2} = (-1)^n H_n(x)\, e^{-x^2}$, where $H_n$ is the $n$-th
 (physicists') Hermite polynomial. The values at the origin are classical:

$$H_{2k}(0) = (-1)^k \frac{(2k)!}{k!}, \qquad H_{2k+1}(0) = 0.$$

Therefore:

$$\|\varphi\|_{0,\,2k} \;=\; \sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}} \bigl|D^{2k}e^{-x^2}\bigr| \;
\geq\; \bigl|D^{2k}e^{-x^2}\bigr|_{x=0} \;=\; \frac{(2k)!}{k!}.$$

By Stirling's approximation, $m! \sim \sqrt{2\pi m}\,(m/e)^m$, so:

$$\frac{(2k)!}{k!} \;\sim\; \frac{\sqrt{4\pi k}\,(2k/e)^{2k}}{\sqrt{2\pi k}\,(k/
e)^{k}} \;=\; \sqrt{2}\;\frac{4^k\, k^{2k}\, e^{-2k}}{k^k\, e^{-k}} \;=\; \sqrt{
2}\left(\frac{4k}{e}\right)^k \;\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\; \infty.$$

Hence $\|\varphi\|_{0,n} \to \infty$ as $n\to\infty$ (along the even subsequence
, which suffices).

---

### Part 2: $\|\varphi\|_{n,0} \to \infty$ as $n \to \infty$

We have $\|\varphi\|_{n,0} = \sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}} |x^n e^{-x^2}|$. For $x > 0$,
 set $f(x) = x^n e^{-x^2}$. Then:

$$f'(x) = \bigl(nx^{n-1} - 2x^{n+1}\bigr)e^{-x^2} = 0 \implies x = \sqrt{n/2}.$$

At this critical point:

$$f\!\left(\sqrt{n/2}\right) = \left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{n/2} e^{-n/2} = \left(\
frac{n}{2e}\right)^{n/2}.$$

Since $n/(2e) \to \infty$, we have $\left(\frac{n}{2e}\right)^{n/2} \to \infty$.
 Therefore:

$$\|\varphi\|_{n,0} \;\geq\; \left(\frac{n}{2e}\right)^{n/2} \;\xrightarrow{n\to
\infty}\; \infty.$$

---

### Conclusion

The Gaussian $\varphi(x) = e^{-x^2}$ is a Schwartz function for which:

- $\|\varphi\|_{0,\,\beta} \to \infty$ as $|\beta| \to \infty$ (the $n$-th deriv
ative grows without bound), and
- $\|\varphi\|_{\alpha,\,0} \to \infty$ as $|\alpha| \to \infty$ (the polynomial
-weighted sup grows without bound).

This is consistent with the definition of the Schwartz space: membership require
s each *individual* semi-norm to be finite, but places no constraint on how the
collection of semi-norms behaves as the indices grow. The finiteness of each $\|
\varphi\|_{\alpha,\beta}$ and the unboundedness of the family $\{\|\varphi\|_{\a
lpha,\beta}\}$ as $|\alpha|,|\beta|\to\infty$ are not in tension — the former is
 a pointwise condition, the latter is an asymptotic condition on an infinite fam
ily.
