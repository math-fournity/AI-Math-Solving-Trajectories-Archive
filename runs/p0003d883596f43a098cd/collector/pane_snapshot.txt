Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a quasi-compact morphism $f
: X \to Y$ allows us to find an open neighborhood $V$ of $y$ with $f^{-1}(V) \su
bseteq U$, given that $U$ contains the preimage of all generizations of $y$.

**The statement is TRUE.** Here is the proof.

---

**Setup.** Let $Z = X \setminus U$, which is closed in $X$. We seek an open $V \
ni y$ with $f^{-1}(V) \cap Z = \emptyset$. It suffices to show $y \notin \overli
ne{f(Z)}$, for then $V = Y \setminus \overline{f(Z)}$ is an open neighborhood of
 $y$ disjoint from $f(Z)$, giving $f^{-1}(V) \cap Z = \emptyset$.

**Step 1: Reduction to $Y$ affine.** Let $W = \operatorname{Spec} A$ be an affin
e open neighborhood of $y$. Every generization $y'$ of $y$ (meaning $y \in \over
line{\{y'\}}$) lies in $W$: any open set containing $y$ must intersect $\{y'\}$,
 hence contain $y'$. Replace $Y$ by $W$, $X$ by $f^{-1}(W)$, $U$ by $U \cap f^{-
1}(W)$. The restricted morphism $f': f^{-1}(W) \to W$ remains quasi-compact, the
 hypothesis is preserved (generizations of $y$ in $W$ coincide with those in $Y$
), and any $V'$ found in the reduced setting is open in $Y$ with $f^{-1}(V') \su
bseteq U$.

**Step 2: Affine setup.** Now $Y = \operatorname{Spec} A$ and $X = f^{-1}(Y)$ is
 quasi-compact (since $f$ is quasi-compact and $Y$ is affine). Cover $X$ by fini
tely many affine opens $X_i = \operatorname{Spec} B_i$, $i = 1, \ldots, n$, with
 ring maps $\phi_i: A \to B_i$. Each $Z \cap X_i$ is closed in $\operatorname{Sp
ec} B_i$, so $Z \cap X_i = V(I_i)$ for some ideal $I_i \subseteq B_i$.

Since $Z \subseteq X = \bigcup X_i$, we have $f(Z) = \bigcup_i f(Z \cap X_i)$, a
nd because the union is finite:
$$\overline{f(Z)} = \bigcup_{i=1}^n \overline{f(Z \cap X_i)}.$$

**Step 3: Computing $\overline{f(V(I_i))}$.** Let $S_i = \{g \in A : \phi_i(g) \
text{ is a unit in } B_i/I_i\}$ and $J_i = \langle S_i \rangle$ the ideal it gen
erates.

*Claim:* $\overline{f(V(I_i))} = V(J_i)$.

*Proof of claim.* A prime $\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Spec} A$ lies in $\ove
rline{f(V(I_i))}$ iff every basic open $D(g)$ with $g \notin \mathfrak{p}$ meets
 $f(V(I_i))$, iff for every $g \notin \mathfrak{p}$ there exists a prime $\mathf
rak{q} \supseteq I_i$ in $B_i$ with $\phi_i(g) \notin \mathfrak{q}$. Such $\math
frak{q}$ exists iff $I_i + (\phi_i(g)) \neq B_i$, iff $\phi_i(g)$ is not a unit
in $B_i/I_i$. So $\mathfrak{p} \in \overline{f(V(I_i))}$ iff for all $g \notin \
mathfrak{p}$, $g \notin S_i$, equivalently $S_i \subseteq \mathfrak{p}$, i.e., $
\mathfrak{p} \in V(J_i)$. $\square$

**Step 4: Relating $J_i$ and $\phi_i^{-1}(I_i)$.** Since $f(V(I_i)) \subseteq V(
\phi_i^{-1}(I_i))$ (if $\mathfrak{q} \supseteq I_i$ then $\phi_i^{-1}(\mathfrak{
q}) \supseteq \phi_i^{-1}(I_i)$), we get $V(J_i) = \overline{f(V(I_i))} \subsete
q V(\phi_i^{-1}(I_i))$, hence:
$$\phi_i^{-1}(I_i) \subseteq \sqrt{J_i}.$$

**Step 5: Using the hypothesis.** Let $\mathfrak{p}_y$ be the prime of $A$ corre
sponding to $y$. The hypothesis says: for all $x \in Z$, $f(x)$ is not a generiz
ation of $y$, i.e., $\mathfrak{p}_{f(x)} \not\subseteq \mathfrak{p}_y$.

For $x \in Z \cap X_i$ corresponding to $\mathfrak{q} \supseteq I_i$: $\phi_i^{-
1}(\mathfrak{q}) \not\subseteq \mathfrak{p}_y$. So no prime $\mathfrak{q} \supse
teq I_i$ in $B_i$ satisfies $\phi_i^{-1}(\mathfrak{q}) \subseteq \mathfrak{p}_y$
. Primes of $B_i$ with $\phi_i^{-1}(\mathfrak{q}) \subseteq \mathfrak{p}_y$ corr
espond to primes of $B_i \otimes_A A_{\mathfrak{p}_y}$. Thus no prime of $B_i \o
times_A A_{\mathfrak{p}_y}$ contains $I_i$, meaning $I_i \cdot (B_i \otimes_A A_
{\mathfrak{p}_y}) = B_i \otimes_A A_{\mathfrak{p}_y}$.

So $1 = \sum_j a_j \cdot (c_j/s_j)$ with $a_j \in I_i$, $c_j \in B_i$, $s_j \in
A \setminus \mathfrak{p}_y$. Setting $s = \prod_j s_j \in A \setminus \mathfrak{
p}_y$, we get $s = \sum_j a_j c_j' \in I_i \cap A = \phi_i^{-1}(I_i)$. Therefore
:
$$\phi_i^{-1}(I_i) \not\subseteq \mathfrak{p}_y \quad \text{for each } i.$$

**Step 6: Constructing the separating element.** For each $i$, pick $s_i \in \ph
i_i^{-1}(I_i) \setminus \mathfrak{p}_y$. By Step 4, $s_i \in \sqrt{J_i}$, so $s_
i^{n_i} \in J_i$ for some $n_i \geq 1$. Since $\mathfrak{p}_y$ is prime and $s_i
 \notin \mathfrak{p}_y$, we have $s_i^{n_i} \notin \mathfrak{p}_y$.

Set $g = \prod_{i=1}^n s_i^{n_i}$. Then:
- $g \notin \mathfrak{p}_y$ (prime ideal, each factor avoids it).
- $g \in \prod_{i=1}^n J_i \subseteq \bigcap_{i=1}^n J_i$ (each $J_i$ is an idea
l, so the product is contained in every factor).

**Step 7: Conclusion.** Since $g \in \bigcap_i J_i \setminus \mathfrak{p}_y$, we
 have $\bigcap_i J_i \not\subseteq \mathfrak{p}_y$, so:
$$y \notin V\!\left(\bigcap_i J_i\right) = \bigcup_i V(J_i) = \overline{f(Z)}.$$

Taking $V = Y \setminus \overline{f(Z)}$ gives an open neighborhood of $y$ with
$V \cap f(Z) = \emptyset$, hence $f^{-1}(V) \cap Z = \emptyset$, i.e., $f^{-1}(V
) \subseteq U$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on the role of quasi-compactness.** The finiteness of the affine cover
$\{X_i\}$ is essential: it allows the finite product $g = \prod s_i^{n_i}$ to li
e in $\bigcap J_i$. Without quasi-compactness, $X$ could require infinitely many
 affine opens, and no finite product would suffice.
