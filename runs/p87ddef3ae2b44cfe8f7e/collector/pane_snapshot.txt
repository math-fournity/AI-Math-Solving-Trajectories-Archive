# Solution

Let $f(x) = \psi(x) - \log x + \dfrac{1}{2x}$, so the series is $\displaystyle S
(\alpha) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} f(\alpha n)$.

## Step 1: Asymptotic expansion of $f(x)$

The digamma function has the well-known Stirling-type asymptotic expansion as $x
 \to +\infty$:

$$\psi(x) = \log x - \frac{1}{2x} - \frac{1}{12x^2} + \frac{1}{120x^4} - \cdots$
$

Therefore:

$$f(x) = \psi(x) - \log x + \frac{1}{2x} = -\frac{1}{12x^2} + O(x^{-4}) \quad \t
ext{as } x \to +\infty.$$

## Step 2: Quadratic bound

From the asymptotic expansion, there exist constants $x_0 > 0$ and $C > 0$ such
that for all $x \geq x_0$:

$$|f(x)| \leq \frac{C}{x^2}.$$

## Step 3: Convergence of the series for each fixed $\alpha$

For any fixed $\alpha > 0$, since $f(\alpha n) = O(1/n^2)$ as $n \to \infty$, th
e series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} f(\alpha n)$ converges absolutely by comparison wi
th $\sum 1/n^2$.

## Step 4: Evaluating the limit

For $\alpha \geq x_0$, every argument $\alpha n \geq \alpha \geq x_0$ (since $n
\geq 1$), so the bound from Step 2 applies to every term:

$$\left|S(\alpha)\right| = \left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} f(\alpha n)\right| \leq \su
m_{n=1}^{\infty} |f(\alpha n)| \leq \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{C}{\alpha^2 n^2} =
 \frac{C}{\alpha^2}\cdot\zeta(2) = \frac{C\pi^2}{6\alpha^2}.$$

As $\alpha \to +\infty$, the right-hand side converges to $0$. By the squeeze th
eorem:

$$\lim_{\alpha \to +\infty} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\psi(\alpha n) - \log(\alph
a n) + \frac{1}{2\alpha n}\right) = 0.$$

**Remark.** The leading-order behavior is $S(\alpha) \sim -\dfrac{\zeta(2)}{12\a
lpha^2} = -\dfrac{\pi^2}{72\alpha^2}$, confirming the limit is $0$ from the nega
tive side.
