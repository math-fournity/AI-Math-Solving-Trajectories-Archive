Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $f(n) \geq c \cdot \frac{n}
{\log n}$ for some positive constant $c$ and all sufficiently large even $n$, wh
ere $f(n)$ counts Goldbach representations.

**Answer: No, such a constant does not exist.**

## Proof

**Step 1: Sieve upper bound for Goldbach representations.**

A classical application of Selberg's sieve (see Halberstam & Richert, *Sieve Met
hods*) yields the following: for even $n \geq 4$,

$$f(n) \ll \frac{n}{(\log n)^2} \prod_{\substack{p \mid n \\ p > 2}} \frac{p-1}{
p-2},$$

where the implied constant is absolute. This is the *upper bound* part of the Ha
rdy–Littlewood Goldbach conjecture, and unlike the conjectured asymptotic, it is
 a proven theorem.

**Step 2: Bounding the singular series product.**

Let $q_1 < q_2 < \cdots < q_k$ be the odd prime divisors of $n$. Since $\prod_{i
=1}^k q_i \leq n$, taking logarithms gives $\sum_{i=1}^k \log q_i \leq \log n$.

Now:
$$\log \prod_{i=1}^k \frac{q_i - 1}{q_i - 2} = \sum_{i=1}^k \log\!\left(1 + \fra
c{1}{q_i - 2}\right) \leq \sum_{i=1}^k \frac{1}{q_i - 2} \leq 2\sum_{i=1}^k \fra
c{1}{q_i}.$$

To maximize $\sum 1/q_i$ subject to $\prod q_i \leq n$, we choose the smallest o
dd primes $3, 5, 7, \ldots, p_k$. The primorial satisfies $\prod_{p \leq x} p =
e^{(1+o(1))\,x}$ by the Prime Number Theorem, so the constraint $\prod q_i \leq
n$ gives $p_k \leq (1+o(1))\log n$, i.e., $k \leq (1+o(1))\frac{\log n}{\log\log
 n}$.

By **Mertens' theorem**, $\sum_{p \leq x} \frac{1}{p} = \log\log x + B + o(1)$ f
or a constant $B$. With $x = p_k \sim \log n$:

$$\sum_{i=1}^k \frac{1}{q_i} \leq \sum_{\substack{p \leq \log n \\ p > 2}} \frac
{1}{p} = \log\log\log n + O(1).$$

Therefore:
$$\prod_{\substack{p \mid n \\ p > 2}} \frac{p-1}{p-2} \leq e^{2(\log\log\log n
+ O(1))} \ll (\log\log n)^2.$$

**Step 3: Combining.**

From Steps 1 and 2:
$$f(n) \ll \frac{n \,(\log\log n)^2}{(\log n)^2}.$$

Dividing by $\frac{n}{\log n}$:
$$\frac{f(n)}{n/\log n} \ll \frac{(\log\log n)^2}{\log n} \longrightarrow 0 \qua
d \text{as } n \to \infty.$$

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Since $f(n) = o\!\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)$, for **any** constant $c > 0$, t
here exists $N$ such that for all even $n > N$:
$$f(n) < c \cdot \frac{n}{\log n}.$$

Hence no positive constant $c$ satisfies $f(n) \geq c \cdot \frac{n}{\log n}$ fo
r all sufficiently large even $n$. $\blacksquare$
