## Answer

**No.** Local compactness at a point does **not** imply that every open neighbor
hood of that point is compact.

### Proof

Recall the definition: a topological space $X$ is **locally compact at $x$** if
there exists an open neighborhood $U$ of $x$ such that $\overline{U}$ (the closu
re of $U$) is compact.

The claim "every open neighborhood of $x$ is compact" is a strictly stronger sta
tement, and it fails in general.

**Counterexample.** Let $X = \mathbb{R}$ with the standard (Euclidean) topology,
 and take $x = 0$.

- **$\mathbb{R}$ is locally compact at $0$.** Take $U = (-1, 1)$, which is an op
en neighborhood of $0$. Its closure is $\overline{U} = [-1, 1]$, which is closed
 and bounded in $\mathbb{R}$, hence compact by the Heine–Borel theorem. So $\mat
hbb{R}$ is locally compact at $0$.

- **Not every open neighborhood of $0$ is compact.** The entire space $\mathbb{R
}$ is itself an open neighborhood of $0$ (since $\mathbb{R}$ is open in itself a
nd contains $0$). But $\mathbb{R}$ is not compact: the open cover $\{(-n, n) : n
 \in \mathbb{N}\}$ has no finite subcover.

Therefore, local compactness at a point only guarantees the existence of **some*
* open neighborhood with compact closure; it does **not** require that every ope
n neighborhood be compact. $\blacksquare$
