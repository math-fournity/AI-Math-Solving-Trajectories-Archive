## Solution

We compute $\lim_{\lambda \to \infty} T_{\frac{\sin(\lambda x)}{x}}$ in $\mathca
l{D}'(\mathbb{R})$, i.e., for every test function $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathb

$$\lim_{\lambda \to \infty} \left\langle T_{\frac{\sin(\lambda x)}{x}},\, \varph
i \right\rangle = \lim_{\lambda \to \infty} \int_{\mathbb{R}} \frac{\sin(\lambda
 x)}{x}\,\varphi(x)\,dx.$$

**Decomposition.** Write $\varphi(x) = \varphi(0) + \bigl(\varphi(x)-\varphi(0)\
bigr)$, so that

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} \frac{\sin(\lambda x)}{x}\,\varphi(x)\,dx = \varphi(0)\int_{
\mathbb{R}} \frac{\sin(\lambda x)}{x}\,dx \;+\; \int_{\mathbb{R}} \frac{\sin(\la
mbda x)}{x}\bigl(\varphi(x)-\varphi(0)\bigr)\,dx.$$

**First term.** The classical Dirichlet integral gives, for every $\lambda > 0$,

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} \frac{\sin(\lambda x)}{x}\,dx = \pi \cdot \operatorname{sgn}
(\lambda) = \pi \qquad (\lambda>0).$$

Hence the first term equals $\pi\,\varphi(0)$ for all $\lambda > 0$.

**Second term.** Define

$$\psi(x) := \frac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(0)}{x}, \quad x \neq 0; \qquad \psi(0) :=
\varphi'(0).$$

Since $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$, the function $\psi$ is $C^\infty$ (T
aylor expansion of $\varphi$ at $0$ shows $\psi$ is smooth at the origin) and co
mpactly supported; in particular $\psi \in L^1(\mathbb{R})$. The second term bec
omes

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} \sin(\lambda x)\,\psi(x)\,dx.$$

By the **Riemann–Lebesgue lemma**, for any $\psi \in L^1(\mathbb{R})$,

$$\lim_{\lambda \to \infty} \int_{\mathbb{R}} \sin(\lambda x)\,\psi(x)\,dx = 0.$
$

**Conclusion.** Combining both terms,

$$\lim_{\lambda \to \infty} \int_{\mathbb{R}} \frac{\sin(\lambda x)}{x}\,\varphi
(x)\,dx = \pi\,\varphi(0) + 0 = \pi\,\varphi(0) = \langle \pi\,\delta_0,\, \varp
hi\rangle.$$

Since this holds for every $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$, we obtain

$$\boxed{\lim_{\lambda \to \infty} T_{\frac{\sin(\lambda x)}{x}} = \pi\,\delta_0
 \quad \text{in } \mathcal{D}'(\mathbb{R}).}$$
