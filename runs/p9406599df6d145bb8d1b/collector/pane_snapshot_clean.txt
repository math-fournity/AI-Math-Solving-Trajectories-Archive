## Proof

Let $A$ be an integral domain with quotient field $K$. For each maximal ideal $m
$ of $A$, the localization $A_m$ is the subring of $K$ consisting of elements $a
/s$ with $a \in A$ and $s \in A \setminus m$.

d, with no solution, sketch, or embedded answer. Proceeding.

---

**Inclusion $A \subseteq \bigcap_m A_m$.** For every maximal ideal $m$, every $a
 \in A$ equals $a/1 \in A_m$ (since $1 \notin m$). Hence $A \subseteq A_m$ for a
ll $m$, so $A \subseteq \bigcap_m A_m$.

**Inclusion $\bigcap_m A_m \subseteq A$.** Let $x \in \bigcap_m A_m \subseteq K$
. Define the *ideal of denominators*

$$I := \{\, s \in A : sx \in A \,\}.$$

We verify $I$ is an ideal of $A$:

- $0 \in I$ since $0 \cdot x = 0 \in A$.
- If $s_1, s_2 \in I$, then $s_1 x, s_2 x \in A$, so $(s_1 - s_2)x \in A$, givin
g $s_1 - s_2 \in I$.
- If $s \in I$ and $r \in A$, then $(rs)x = r(sx) \in A$, giving $rs \in I$.

Since $x \in K$, write $x = a/b$ with $a, b \in A$, $b \neq 0$. Then $b \cdot x
= a \in A$, so $b \in I$ and $I \neq (0)$; in particular $I$ is nonzero.

Now fix any maximal ideal $m$. Since $x \in A_m$, we may write $x = a_m / s_m$ w
ith $a_m \in A$ and $s_m \in A \setminus m$. Then $s_m \cdot x = a_m \in A$, so
$s_m \in I$. But $s_m \notin m$, which means

$$I \not\subseteq m.$$

This holds for **every** maximal ideal $m$ of $A$.

Recall that every proper ideal of a commutative ring with identity is contained
in some maximal ideal (by Zorn's lemma). Since $I$ is not contained in any maxim
al ideal, $I$ cannot be a proper ideal. Therefore

$$I = A.$$

In particular $1 \in I$, so $1 \cdot x = x \in A$.

Since $x \in \bigcap_m A_m$ was arbitrary, $\bigcap_m A_m \subseteq A$.

---

Combining both inclusions, $\bigcap_{m \text{ maximal}} A_m = A$. $\blacksquare$
