Let $g(x) = \dfrac{1-x}{1+x}$. First observe that $g$ is an **involution**:

$$
g(g(x)) = \frac{1 - \frac{1-x}{1+x}}{1 + \frac{1-x}{1+x}} = \frac{(1+x)-(1-x)}{(
1+x)+(1-x)} = \frac{2x}{2} = x.
$$

The given equation is

$$
f(x)^2\, f(g(x)) = 64x. \tag{1}
$$

**Substitute $x \mapsto g(x)$** (valid since $g$ maps $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0,\p
m1\}$ to itself):

$$
f(g(x))^2\, f(g(g(x))) = 64\,g(x) \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; f(g(x))^2\, f(x) = 64\
,g(x). \tag{2}
$$

From (1), $\;f(g(x)) = \dfrac{64x}{f(x)^2}$. Insert this into (2):

$$
\left(\frac{64x}{f(x)^2}\right)^{\!2} f(x) = 64\,g(x)
\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;
\frac{4096\,x^2}{f(x)^3} = 64\,g(x)
\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;
f(x)^3 = \frac{64\,x^2}{g(x)}.
$$

Since $g(x)=\dfrac{1-x}{1+x}$,

$$
\boxed{\,f(x)^3 = \frac{64\,x^2(1+x)}{1-x}\,}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
f(x) = \sqrt[3]{\frac{64\,x^2(1+x)}{1-x}}.
$$

**Verification.** Compute $f(g(x))^3$ using $1+g(x)=\dfrac{2}{1+x}$ and $1-g(x)=
\dfrac{2x}{1+x}$:

$$
f(g(x))^3 = \frac{64\,g(x)^2\,(1+g(x))}{1-g(x)}
= 64\cdot\frac{(1-x)^2}{(1+x)^2}\cdot\frac{2}{1+x}\cdot\frac{1+x}{2x}
= \frac{64\,(1-x)^2}{x\,(1+x)^2}.
$$

Now cube both sides of (1) and substitute:

$$
\bigl[f(x)^2 f(g(x))\bigr]^3 = f(x)^6\, f(g(x))^3
= \left(\frac{64\,x^2(1+x)}{1-x}\right)^{\!2}\!\cdot\frac{64\,(1-x)^2}{x\,(1+x)^
2}
= 4096\cdot 64\cdot x^3 = (64x)^3.
$$

Taking cube roots gives $f(x)^2 f(g(x)) = 64x$, confirming (1). ∎
