Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the countable intersection
must be nontrivial, given that each $A_n$ has fixed finite codimension $m$ and e
very finite intersection is infinite-dimensional.

**Answer: No.**

**Proof.**

It suffices to give a counterexample with $m = 1$ (since $m \geq 1$ is arbitrary
, a counterexample for one value refutes the universal claim).

Let $H$ be an infinite-dimensional separable complex Hilbert space, and let $\{e
_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ be an orthonormal basis for $H$. Define
$$A_n := e_n^{\perp} = \{x \in H : \langle x, e_n \rangle = 0\}, \quad n \geq 1.
$$

Each $A_n$ is a closed subspace (the kernel of the continuous linear functional
$x \mapsto \langle x, e_n\rangle$), and
$$A_n^{\perp} = \overline{\operatorname{span}}\{e_n\} = \operatorname{span}\{e_n
\},$$
so $\dim(A_n^{\perp}) = 1 = m$ for every $n$.

**Finite intersections are infinite-dimensional.** For any finite set of indices
 $F = \{n_1, \ldots, n_k\}$,
$$\bigcap_{n \in F} A_n = \{x \in H : \langle x, e_{n_j}\rangle = 0,\ j=1,\ldots
,k\} = \{e_{n_1}, \ldots, e_{n_k}\}^{\perp}.$$
This is the orthogonal complement of a $k$-dimensional subspace, hence has codim
ension $k$ in $H$. Since $H$ is infinite-dimensional, $\{e_{n_1},\ldots,e_{n_k}\
}^{\perp}$ is infinite-dimensional. ✓

**The full intersection is $\{0\}$.** We have
$$\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n = \{x \in H : \langle x, e_n\rangle = 0 \text{ for
all } n \geq 1\}.$$
Since $\{e_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ is an orthonormal basis, any $x \in H$ satisfies
$\|x\|^2 = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} |\langle x, e_n\rangle|^2$ (Parseval's identity).
 If $\langle x, e_n\rangle = 0$ for all $n$, then $\|x\|^2 = 0$, so $x = 0$. The
refore
$$\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n = \{0\}.$$

Thus we have a sequence of closed subspaces, each of codimension $m = 1$, such t
hat every finite intersection is infinite-dimensional, yet the countable interse
ction is trivial. The proposed conclusion fails.
