## Solution

**Setup.** A cube has 6 faces. The adjacency graph of faces is the octahedron gr
aph $K_{2,2,2}$: three pairs of opposite faces, where each face is adjacent to t
he four faces not opposite to it. We count proper colorings of $K_{2,2,2}$ with
8 colours, **up to rotation** of the cube, using Burnside's lemma over the rotat
ion group (order 24).

**Chromatic polynomial of $K_{2,2,2}$.** In a proper coloring, the only pairs al
lowed to share a colour are the three opposite pairs. Each colour class is an in
dependent set, hence contained in one of the three 2-element parts. Classifying
by how many opposite pairs are monochromatic:

$$P(k) = (k)_6 + 3(k)_5 + 3(k)_4 + (k)_3.$$

At $k=8$: $(8)_3=336,\ (8)_4=1680,\ (8)_5=6720,\ (8)_6=20160$, so
$$P(8)=20160+20160+5040+336=45696.$$

**Burnside over the 24 rotations.** Conjugacy classes acting on faces:

1. **Identity** (1 element): $45696$.
2. **90° face-axis** (6 elements): fixes 2 opposite faces, 4-cycle on the 4 side
 faces. The 4 side faces are cyclically adjacent, yet a fixed coloring forces al
l four equal — impossible. Contribution $0$.
3. **180° face-axis** (3 elements): fixes top/bottom (opposite, non-adjacent), s
waps the two opposite side-pairs. Let colors be $t,b$ (axis faces) and $n,e$ (th
e two side-pairs). Constraints: $t,b\notin\{n,e\}$, $n\ne e$, and $t,b$ mutually
 free. Count: $8\cdot7\cdot6\cdot6=2016$ each. Contribution $3\cdot2016=6048$.
4. **120° vertex-axis** (8 elements): two 3-cycles. The three faces meeting at a
 vertex are pairwise adjacent, yet fixed coloring forces them equal — impossible
. Contribution $0$.
5. **180° edge-axis** (6 elements): three 2-cycles. Each 2-cycle swaps two **adj
acent** faces (e.g. right↔front), forcing them equal while adjacent — impossible
. Contribution $0$.

**Total:**
$$N=\frac{1}{24}\bigl(45696+6048\bigr)=\frac{51744}{24}=2156.$$

**Factorization.** $2156=2^2\cdot 7^2\cdot 11$. The two-digit divisors are $11,1
4,22,28,44,49,77,98$. The largest is
$$98 = 2\cdot 7^2,\qquad 2156=98\cdot 22.$$

The largest two-digit factor of $N$ is $\boxed{98}$.
