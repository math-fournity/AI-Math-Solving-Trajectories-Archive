**Answer: No.** Here is a counterexample.

Take $k = 1$ (which is odd), so the exponent is $2k+1 = 3$. Let

$$M = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\frac{2\pi}{3} & -\sin\frac{2\pi}{3} \\[4pt] \sin\frac
{2\pi}{3} & \cos\frac{2\pi}{3} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -\tfrac{1}{2} & -
\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} \\[4pt] \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} & -\tfrac{1}{2} \end{pmatrix}, \qqua
d N = I_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Both are real $2\times 2$ matrices.

**Computing $M^3$ and $N^3$.** The matrix $M$ is the rotation matrix through ang
le $2\pi/3$, so $M^3$ is the rotation through $3\cdot 2\pi/3 = 2\pi$, i.e. $M^3
= I_2$. Trivially $N^3 = I_2$. Hence

$$M^{2k+1} = M^3 = I_2 = N^3 = N^{2k+1}.$$

**Characteristic polynomials.** The eigenvalues of $M$ are $e^{\pm 2\pi i/3}$, s
o

$$\chi_M(x) = \bigl(x - e^{2\pi i/3}\bigr)\bigl(x - e^{-2\pi i/3}\bigr) = x^2 -
2\cos\tfrac{2\pi}{3}\,x + 1 = x^2 + x + 1.$$

The characteristic polynomial of $N = I_2$ is

$$\chi_N(x) = (x-1)^2 = x^2 - 2x + 1.$$

Since $\chi_M(x) = x^2+x+1 \neq x^2-2x+1 = \chi_N(x)$, the two matrices have **d
ifferent** characteristic polynomials.

**Conclusion.** We have exhibited two $n\times n$ matrices ($n=2$) with $M^{2k+1
}=N^{2k+1}$ for an odd integer $k$ (namely $k=1$), yet with distinct characteris
tic polynomials. Therefore the answer to the question is **no** — $M$ and $N$ ne
ed not have the same characteristic polynomial.
