Let $m = \min\{n \ge 0 : a_n \ne 0\}$ be the order of vanishing of $F$ at the or
igin (with $m = \infty$ if $F \equiv 0$). I claim

$$\sup\left\{\delta > 0 : \exists\,\epsilon>0,\ \int_{-\epsilon}^{\epsilon}|F(x)
|^{-\delta}\,dx<\infty\right\} \;=\; \frac{1}{m},$$

with the conventions $1/0 = +\infty$ (so $F(0)\ne 0$ gives $+\infty$) and $1/\in
fty = 0$ (so $F\equiv 0$ gives $0$).

---

**Proof.**

Write $F(x) = x^m G(x)$ where $G(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} a_{m+k}\,x^k$ (when $m
<\infty$). Then $G$ is a power series converging in a neighborhood of $0$ with $
G(0) = a_m \ne 0$. By continuity of $G$, there exist $\epsilon, c, C > 0$ such t
hat for all $|x| \le \epsilon$,

$$c \;\le\; |G(x)| \;\le\; C.$$

Consequently, for $0 < |x| \le \epsilon$,

$$c\,|x|^m \;\le\; |F(x)| \;\le\; C\,|x|^m,$$

and raising to the power $-\delta > 0$ reverses the inequalities:

$$C^{-\delta}\,|x|^{-m\delta} \;\le\; |F(x)|^{-\delta} \;\le\; c^{-\delta}\,|x|^
{-m\delta}.$$

The integral $\int_{-\epsilon}^{\epsilon}|x|^{-m\delta}\,dx$ (improper at $0$ wh
en $m\delta \ge 1$) converges if and only if $m\delta < 1$, i.e. $\delta < 1/m$.

- **Upper bound.** If $m\delta \ge 1$, then $|F(x)|^{-\delta} \ge C^{-\delta}|x|
^{-m\delta}$, and $\int_{-\epsilon}^{\epsilon}|x|^{-m\delta}\,dx = +\infty$, so
by comparison $\int_{-\epsilon}^{\epsilon}|F(x)|^{-\delta}\,dx = +\infty$ for ev
ery $\epsilon>0$. Hence no $\delta \ge 1/m$ belongs to the set.

- **Lower bound.** If $\delta < 1/m$, then $m\delta < 1$, so $\int_{-\epsilon}^{
\epsilon}|x|^{-m\delta}\,dx < \infty$, and by comparison $|F(x)|^{-\delta} \le c
^{-\delta}|x|^{-m\delta}$ gives $\int_{-\epsilon}^{\epsilon}|F(x)|^{-\delta}\,dx
 < \infty$. Thus every $\delta \in (0, 1/m)$ belongs to the set.

Therefore the set equals $(0,\,1/m)$, and its supremum is $1/m$.

**Edge cases.** When $m = 0$ (i.e. $F(0) = a_0 \ne 0$), $|F(x)|$ is bounded away
 from $0$ near the origin, so $|F(x)|^{-\delta}$ is bounded and integrable for *
*every** $\delta > 0$; the set is $(0,\infty)$ and the supremum is $+\infty = 1/
0$, consistent with the formula. When $F \equiv 0$ ($m = \infty$), $|F(x)|^{-\de
lta} \equiv +\infty$ for every $\delta > 0$, so the set is empty and its supremu
m is $0 = 1/\infty$, again consistent. $\blacksquare$
