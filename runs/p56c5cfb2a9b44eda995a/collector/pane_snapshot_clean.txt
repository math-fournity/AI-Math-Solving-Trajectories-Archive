## Solution

We evaluate $\int_C \frac{2z^2 - 5z + 2}{\sin z}\,dz$ where $C$ is the unit circ
le $|z|=1$ oriented counterclockwise, using the **Residue Theorem**.

### Step 1: Identify singularities inside $C$

The integrand $f(z) = \frac{2z^2 - 5z + 2}{\sin z}$ has singularities where $\si
n z = 0$, i.e., at $z = n\pi$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

Inside the unit circle $|z| < 1$: we need $|n\pi| < 1$, i.e., $|n| < 1/\pi \appr
ox 0.318$. The only integer satisfying this is $n = 0$, giving the single singul
arity $z = 0$.

### Step 2: Classify the singularity at $z = 0$

At $z = 0$:
- $\sin z = 0$, and $\sin z$ has a simple zero at $z = 0$ (since $\sin' z = \cos
 z$ and $\cos 0 = 1 \neq 0$).
- The numerator $2z^2 - 5z + 2$ evaluates to $2(0)^2 - 5(0) + 2 = 2 \neq 0$.

Therefore $z = 0$ is a **simple pole** of $f(z)$.

### Step 3: Compute the residue at $z = 0$

For a simple pole where the denominator has a simple zero, the residue is:

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z) = \frac{2z^2 - 5z + 2}{\frac{d}{dz}(\sin z)}\big
g|_{z=0} = \frac{2z^2 - 5z + 2}{\cos z}\bigg|_{z=0} = \frac{2}{1} = 2$$

**Verification via limit:** Since $\sin z \sim z$ as $z \to 0$,

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z) = \lim_{z \to 0} z \cdot \frac{2z^2 - 5z + 2}{\s
in z} = \lim_{z \to 0} \frac{z \cdot 2}{z} = 2. \checkmark$$

### Step 4: Apply the Residue Theorem

By the Residue Theorem, with the only singularity inside $C$ being $z = 0$:

$$\int_C \frac{2z^2 - 5z + 2}{\sin z}\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}_{z=0
} f(z) = 2\pi i \cdot 2 = \boxed{4\pi i}$$
