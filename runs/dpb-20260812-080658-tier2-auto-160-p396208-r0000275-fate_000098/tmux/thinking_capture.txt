Let $\pi : G \to G/N$ denote the canonical projection homomorphism, so $\pi(g) =
gN$ for each $g \in G$. The kernel of $\pi$ is exactly $N$: $$\ker \pi = {g \in
G : gN = N} = N.$$

Since $G/N$ is abelian, the group operation in the quotient is commutative. For
any $a, b \in G$, the cosets $aN$ and $bN$ therefore satisfy $$aN \cdot bN = bN
\cdot aN.$$

Computing each side using the definition of multiplication in $G/N$:

  • Left side: $aN \cdot bN = (ab)N$.
  • Right side: $bN \cdot aN = (ba)N$.

Thus $(ab)N = (ba)N$. Two cosets are equal precisely when their representatives
differ by an element of $N$, i.e. $(ab)(ba)^{-1} \in N$. Since $(ba)^{-1} =
a^{-1}b^{-1}$, this gives $$(ab)(a^{-1}b^{-1}) \in N,$$ that is, $$aba^{-1}
b^{-1} \in N.$$

Equivalently, using the homomorphism $\pi$: since $G/N$ is abelian, $$
\pi(aba^{-1}b^{-1}) = \pi(a)\pi(b)\pi(a)^{-1}\pi(b)^{-1} = e_{G/N},$$ so
$aba^{-1}b^{-1} \in \ker \pi = N$.

This holds for all $a, b \in G$, completing the proof. $\blacksquare$
