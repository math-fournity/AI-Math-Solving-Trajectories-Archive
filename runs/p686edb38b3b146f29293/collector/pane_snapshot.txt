## Solution

We seek all real $c$ such that $\cosh x \le e^{cx^2}$ for all real $x$, where $\
cosh x = \frac{e^x+e^{-x}}{2}$.

**Reduction.** At $x=0$ both sides equal $1$, so the inequality is tight there.
For $x\ne 0$, taking logarithms (both sides positive) gives the equivalent condi
tion
$$\ln(\cosh x) \le c\,x^2 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c \ge \frac{\ln(\cosh x)
}{x^2}.$$
Thus the inequality holds for all real $x$ iff
$$c \ge \sup_{x\ne 0}\, f(x), \qquad f(x):=\frac{\ln(\cosh x)}{x^2}.$$

**Behavior of $f$.** Since $\cosh$ is even, $f$ is even; it suffices to study $x
>0$.

- *Limit at $0$:* Using $\cosh x = 1+\frac{x^2}{2}+\frac{x^4}{24}+O(x^6)$ and $\
ln(1+u)=u-\frac{u^2}{2}+\cdots$ with $u=\frac{x^2}{2}+\frac{x^4}{24}+\cdots$,
$$\ln(\cosh x)=\frac{x^2}{2}-\frac{x^4}{12}+O(x^6),\qquad f(x)=\frac12-\frac{x^2
}{12}+O(x^4)\to\frac12.$$

- *Limit at $\infty$:* $\cosh x\sim \frac{e^x}{2}$, so $\ln(\cosh x)\sim x-\ln 2
$, giving $f(x)\sim \frac1x-\frac{\ln 2}{x^2}\to 0$.

**Monotonicity.** Compute
$$f'(x)=\frac{x\tanh x-2\ln(\cosh x)}{x^3}=\frac{g(x)}{x^3},\qquad g(x):=x\tanh
x-2\ln(\cosh x).$$
We have $g(0)=0$. Differentiate:
$$g'(x)=\tanh x+x\operatorname{sech}^2 x-2\tanh x=x\operatorname{sech}^2 x-\tanh
 x,\qquad g'(0)=0.$$
Differentiate again:
$$g''(x)=\operatorname{sech}^2 x-2x\operatorname{sech}^2 x\tanh x-\operatorname{
sech}^2 x=-2x\,\operatorname{sech}^2 x\,\tanh x.$$
For $x>0$ we have $\tanh x>0$ and $\operatorname{sech}^2 x>0$, so $g''(x)<0$. He
nce $g'$ is strictly decreasing on $(0,\infty)$; with $g'(0)=0$ this gives $g'(x
)<0$ for $x>0$. Hence $g$ is strictly decreasing on $(0,\infty)$; with $g(0)=0$
this gives $g(x)<0$ for $x>0$. Therefore
$$f'(x)=\frac{g(x)}{x^3}<0\quad\text{for }x>0.$$
So $f$ is **strictly decreasing** on $(0,\infty)$.

**Conclusion.** Since $f$ is strictly decreasing on $(0,\infty)$,
$$\sup_{x\ne 0} f(x)=\lim_{x\to 0^+} f(x)=\frac12.$$
The inequality $\cosh x\le e^{cx^2}$ holds for all real $x$ if and only if $c\ge
 \tfrac12$.

**Verification of the endpoint.** For $c=\tfrac12$ we need $\ln(\cosh x)\le \tfr
ac{x^2}{2}$. This follows from $f(x)\le \tfrac12$ just established (with equalit
y only at $x=0$). Equivalently, $\cosh x\le e^{x^2/2}$, a standard Gaussian-vs-h
yperbolic bound. For any $c>\tfrac12$ the inequality is strict for $x\ne0$; for
$c<\tfrac12$, taking $x\to0$ gives $f(x)\to\tfrac12>c$, violating the inequality
 for small nonzero $x$.

$$\boxed{\,c\in\bigl[\tfrac12,\,\infty\bigr)\,}$$
