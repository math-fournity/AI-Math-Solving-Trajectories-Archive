## Solution

**Claim.** If $V$ is a vector space over $\mathbb{F}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}
$ with $\dim V=\alpha$ (an infinite cardinal), then
$$\dim V^{*}=|\mathbb{F}|^{\alpha}=2^{\alpha}.$$

---

### Step 1. Identify $V^{*}$ with $\mathbb{F}^{B}$

Let $B$ be a basis of $V$, so $|B|=\alpha$. A linear functional $\varphi\in V^{*
}$ is completely determined by its values on $B$, and every function $B\to\mathb
b{F}$ extends uniquely to a linear functional. Hence, as $\mathbb{F}$-vector spa
ces,
$$V^{*}\cong \mathbb{F}^{B}:=\prod_{b\in B}\mathbb{F}.$$
So $\dim V^{*}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\mathbb{F}^{B}$.

### Step 2. Cardinality of $V^{*}$ (upper bound on the dimension)

As a set,
$$|V^{*}|=|\mathbb{F}|^{|B|}=|\mathbb{F}|^{\alpha}.$$
Since $\mathbb{F}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}$, we have $|\mathbb{F}|=2^{\aleph_
{0}}$, and therefore
$$|V^{*}|=(2^{\aleph_{0}})^{\alpha}=2^{\aleph_{0}\cdot\alpha}=2^{\alpha},$$
using $\aleph_{0}\cdot\alpha=\alpha$ for infinite $\alpha$. Since the dimension
of a vector space never exceeds its cardinality,
$$\dim V^{*}\le |V^{*}|=2^{\alpha}.$$

### Step 3. Lower bound: a linearly independent family of size $2^{\alpha}$

We use the **Fichtenholz–Kantorovich–Hausdorff theorem**: for every infinite set
 $B$ of cardinality $\alpha$, there exists an *independent family* $\{A_{\xi}:\x
i<2^{\alpha}\}$ of subsets of $B$, meaning that for every finite choice of disti
nct indices $\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m},\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{n}$,
$$A_{\xi_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap A_{\xi_{m}}\cap(B\setminus A_{\eta_{1}})\cap\cdots\c
ap(B\setminus A_{\eta_{n}})\neq\varnothing.$$

For each $\xi<2^{\alpha}$, let $\chi_{A_{\xi}}\in\{0,1\}^{B}\subseteq\mathbb{F}^
{B}$ be the characteristic function of $A_{\xi}$, viewed as an element of $V^{*}
\cong\mathbb{F}^{B}$.

**These $2^{\alpha}$ functionals are linearly independent over $\mathbb{F}$.** I
ndeed, suppose
$$c_{1}\chi_{A_{\xi_{1}}}+\cdots+c_{n}\chi_{A_{\xi_{n}}}=0\qquad(c_{i}\in\mathbb
{F},\ \xi_{i}\ \text{distinct}).$$
Fix any $i_{0}\in\{1,\dots,n\}$. By independence of the family, there exists $b\
in B$ with $b\in A_{\xi_{i_{0}}}$ and $b\notin A_{\xi_{i}}$ for all $i\neq i_{0}
$. Evaluating the linear combination at $b$ gives
$$c_{i_{0}}\cdot 1+\sum_{i\neq i_{0}}c_{i}\cdot 0=c_{i_{0}}=0.$$
Since $i_{0}$ was arbitrary, all $c_{i}=0$. ∎

Thus $V^{*}$ contains $2^{\alpha}$ linearly independent elements, so
$$\dim V^{*}\ge 2^{\alpha}.$$

### Step 4. Conclusion

Combining the upper and lower bounds,
$$2^{\alpha}\le \dim V^{*}\le 2^{\alpha},$$
hence
$$\boxed{\dim V^{*}=|\mathbb{F}|^{\alpha}=2^{\alpha}.}$$
