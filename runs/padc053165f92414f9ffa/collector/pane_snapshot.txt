## Setup and Definitions

### 1. Small Category

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a **small category** (its objects and morphisms form sets,
not proper classes).

### 2. Presheaves

A **presheaf** on $\mathcal{C}$ is a functor
$$F : \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{Set}.$$
The category of presheaves is the functor category
$$\mathbf{PSh}(\mathcal{C}) = \mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}}.$$
For an object $C \in \mathcal{C}$, we write $F(C)$ for the set of *sections over
 $C$*, and for a morphism $f : C' \to C$, the restriction map is $F(f) : F(C) \t
o F(C')$, often denoted $s \mapsto s|_{C'}$.

### 3. Grothendieck Topology (Sieve Formulation)

A **Grothendieck topology** $J$ on $\mathcal{C}$ assigns to each object $C \in \
mathcal{C}$ a collection $J(C)$ of **covering sieves** (subfunctors $S \hookrigh
tarrow \mathrm{y}C$, where $\mathrm{y}C = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(-, C)$ is t
he representable presheaf) satisfying three axioms:

**(T1) Maximality.** The maximal sieve $\mathrm{y}C$ (all morphisms into $C$) is
 in $J(C)$.

**(T2) Stability (pullback).** If $S \in J(C)$ and $f : C' \to C$ is any morphis
m, then the pullback sieve
$$f^*(S) = \{g : D \to C' \mid f \circ g \in S\}$$
belongs to $J(C')$.

**(T3) Transitivity.** If $S \in J(C)$ and $R \hookrightarrow \mathrm{y}C$ is a
sieve such that for every morphism $f : C' \to C$ in $S$, the pullback $f^*(R) \
in J(C')$, then $R \in J(C)$.

The pair $(\mathcal{C}, J)$ is called a **site**.

**Equivalent formulations** (each determines the same topology):
- **Coverage form**: assign to each $C$ a family of covering families $\{f_i : C
_i \to C\}_{i \in I}$ satisfying a base-change axiom (each $f_i$ pulls back each
 covering family of $C$ to a covering family of $C_i \times_C C_j$-type refineme
nts) and a transitivity axiom.
- **Lawvere–Tierney topology**: an endomorphism $j : \Omega \to \Omega$ of the s
ubobject classifier in $\mathbf{PSh}(\mathcal{C})$ satisfying $j \circ \top = \t
op$, $j \circ j = j$, and $j \circ \wedge = \wedge \circ (j \times j)$.

### 4. Sheaves on a Site

A presheaf $F \in \mathbf{PSh}(\mathcal{C})$ is a **sheaf** on $(\mathcal{C}, J)
$ if for every $C \in \mathcal{C}$ and every covering sieve $S \in J(C)$, the ca
nonical map
$$F(C) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{PSh}(\mathcal{C})}(S, F)$$
is a **bijection**. Concretely, given a compatible family of sections $\{s_f \in
 F(\mathrm{dom}\, f)\}_{f \in S}$ (compatible meaning $s_{f \circ g} = s_f|_D$ f
or every composable $g : D \to \mathrm{dom}\, f$), there exists a **unique** sec
tion $s \in F(C)$ with $s|_{\mathrm{dom}\, f} = s_f$ for all $f \in S$.

Equivalently (in coverage form): for every covering family $\{f_i : C_i \to C\}$
, the diagram
$$F(C) \to \prod_i F(C_i) \rightrightarrows \prod_{i,j} F(C_i \times_C C_j)$$
is an equalizer.

A presheaf failing this condition is merely a presheaf; every presheaf has a **s
heafification** $F \mapsto F^{\#}$, constructed by the plus construction applied
 twice ($F \mapsto F^+ \mapsto F^{++} = F^{\#}$), which is left adjoint to the i
nclusion of sheaves into presheaves.

### 5. The Category of Sheaves

The full subcategory of sheaves is denoted
$$\mathbf{Sh}(\mathcal{C}, J) \hookrightarrow \mathbf{PSh}(\mathcal{C}).$$

**Key properties:**
- The inclusion $i : \mathbf{Sh}(\mathcal{C}, J) \hookrightarrow \mathbf{PSh}(\m
athcal{C})$ is full and faithful.
- Sheafification $(-)^{\#} : \mathbf{PSh}(\mathcal{C}) \to \mathbf{Sh}(\mathcal{
C}, J)$ is left adjoint to $i$: $(-)^{\#} \dashv i$.
- This adjunction is a **left exact localization** (the reflector $(-)^{\#}$ pre
serves finite limits), since the class of local isomorphisms is stable under fin
ite limits.

### 6. Definition of Grothendieck Topos

> **Definition.** A **Grothendieck topos** is a category equivalent to $\mathbf{
Sh}(\mathcal{C}, J)$ for some small category $\mathcal{C}$ equipped with a Groth
endieck topology $J$.

### 7. Characterizing Categorical Properties

The category $\mathbf{Sh}(\mathcal{C}, J)$ satisfies:

**(G1) Finite limits.** $\mathbf{Sh}(\mathcal{C}, J)$ is complete and cocomplete
; limits are computed pointwise as in $\mathbf{PSh}(\mathcal{C})$ (since $i$ pre
serves limits and reflects them). Finite limits exist because $\mathbf{Set}$ has
 them and they are computed objectwise, and sheaves are stable under finite limi
ts.

**(G2) Cartesian closed.** For sheaves $F, G$, the internal hom is $[F, G]_{\mat
hrm{sh}} = (i \Rightarrow \mathrm{Hom})(F,G)^{\#}$, i.e., the sheafification of
the presheaf internal hom. The exponential $G^F$ exists for all sheaves $F, G$.

**(G3) Subobject classifier.** The subobject classifier is $\Omega_J$, the sheaf
ification of the presheaf subobject classifier $\Omega$ (where $\Omega(C) = \{\t
ext{sieves on } C\}$) with respect to $J$. Explicitly,
$$\Omega_J(C) = \{\text{$J$-closed sieves on } C\},$$
where a sieve $S$ on $C$ is $J$-closed if for all $f : C' \to C$, $f^*(S) \in J(
C') \iff f^*(\mathrm{y}C') \in J(C')$ (i.e., $f^*(S)$ is covering iff the maxima
l sieve is). The monomorphism $\top : 1 \to \Omega_J$ picks out the maximal $J$-
closed sieve at each $C$.

**(G4) Generator.** The set of sheaves $\{\mathrm{y}C^{\#} \mid C \in \mathrm{Ob
}(\mathcal{C})\}$ (sheafifications of representables) forms a **separating set**
 (a set of generators): a family of jointly surjective morphisms into a sheaf is
 epimorphic. Since $\mathcal{C}$ is small, this is a set.

**(G5) Exactness of colimits.** Colimits are computed by sheafifying the pointwi
se presheaf colimit. Filtered colimits and finite limits commute (inherited from
 $\mathbf{Set}$ via sheafification, which is left exact). More generally, $\math
bf{Sh}(\mathcal{C}, J)$ is a **Grothendieck abelian-like exactness context**: it
 is a $\infty$-topos base in the 1-categorical sense of being a left exact local
ization of a presheaf category.

### 8. Equivalence with Giraud's Theorem

The definition above is equivalent to the **Giraud characterization**: a categor
y $\mathcal{E}$ is a Grothendieck topos iff

1. $\mathcal{E}$ has finite limits;
2. $\mathcal{E}$ has all small coproducts, and they are disjoint and universal (
pullback-stable);
3. Every equivalence relation in $\mathcal{E}$ is effective (kernel pair of its
quotient) and universal;
4. $\mathcal{E}$ has a small separating set;
5. $\mathcal{E}$ is locally small.

The equivalence is established by:
- **(Site $\Rightarrow$ Giraud):** $\mathbf{Sh}(\mathcal{C}, J)$ satisfies (1)–(
5) by the properties above.
- **(Giraud $\Rightarrow$ Site):** Given $\mathcal{E}$ satisfying Giraud's axiom
s, take the full subcategory $\mathcal{C}$ generated by the separating set under
 finite limits (this is small), equip it with the **canonical topology** (the la
rgest topology for which all representables are sheaves), and show $\mathcal{E}
\simeq \mathbf{Sh}(\mathcal{C}, J_{\mathrm{can}})$ via the Yoneda-plus-sheafific
ation functor $X \mapsto \mathrm{Hom}(-, X)^{\#}$.

### 9. Summary Construction

$$\boxed{\text{A Grothendieck topos is a category equivalent to } \mathbf{Sh}(\m
athcal{C}, J) \text{ for a site } (\mathcal{C}, J), \mathcal{C} \text{ small.}}$
$

The construction proceeds as:
1. Start with a small category $\mathcal{C}$.
2. Form the presheaf category $\mathbf{PSh}(\mathcal{C}) = \mathbf{Set}^{\mathca
l{C}^{\mathrm{op}}}$ — itself a Grothendieck topos (the *trivial* topology, wher
e only maximal sieves cover).
3. Specify a Grothendieck topology $J$ (sieves satisfying (T1)–(T3), or equivale
ntly a Lawvere–Tierney topology $j$).
4. Define sheaves as those presheaves $F$ for which $F(C) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(S,
F)$ for all covering sieves $S \in J(C)$.
5. The full subcategory $\mathbf{Sh}(\mathcal{C}, J)$, with sheafification as le
ft exact reflector, is the Grothendieck topos associated to the site.

This subcategory inherits finite limits, is cartesian closed, has a subobject cl
assifier $\Omega_J$ (closed sieves), and has a small separating set — making it
a left exact localization of a presheaf category, which is precisely the Giraud
characterization of a Grothendieck topos.
