$$\frac{k(1+\alpha)}{n-k+1+(n-k)\alpha} = \frac{kc}{(n-k)c + 1}$$

$$S_n = \sum_{k=1}^n \ln\!\left(\frac{kc}{(n-k)c+1}\right) = \ln P_n, \qquad P_n
 = \prod_{k=1}^n \frac{kc}{(n-k)c+1}.$$

**Computing the product.** The numerator is:

$$\prod_{k=1}^n kc = c^n \cdot n!$$

For the denominator, substitute $j = n-k$ (so $j$ ranges from $0$ to $n-1$):

$$\prod_{k=1}^n\bigl((n-k)c+1\bigr) = \prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(jc+1) = \prod_{j=0}^{n-1
}c\!\left(j+\tfrac{1}{c}\right) = c^n\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(j+\frac{1}{c}\right)
$$

Therefore:

$$P_n = \frac{c^n \cdot n!}{c^n\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(j+\frac{1}{c}\right)} = \f
rac{n!}{\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(j+\frac{1}{c}\right)}$$

$$P_n = \frac{\Gamma(n+1)\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{1}{c}\right)}{\Gamma\!\left(n+\fr

$$\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma\!\left(n+\frac{1}{c}\right)} \sim n^{\,1-\frac{1}{c}

$$P_n \sim \Gamma\!\left(\frac{1}{c}\right) \cdot n^{\,1-\frac{1}{c}}$$
