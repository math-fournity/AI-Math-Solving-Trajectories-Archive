**Problem.** Find all $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ with $f(f(x) + yz) = x + f(
y)f(z)$ for all $x, y, z \in \mathbb{R}$.

**Answer.** The unique solution is $f(x) = x$.

---

### Proof

Let $P(x,y,z)$ denote the assertion $f(f(x)+yz)=x+f(y)f(z)$.

**Step 1: Surjectivity.** Fix any $y,z$. As $x$ ranges over $\mathbb{R}$, the ri
ght-hand side $x+f(y)f(z)$ ranges over all of $\mathbb{R}$. Since the left-hand
side is a value of $f$, $f$ is surjective.

**Step 2: A zero and a multiplicative-type law.** Since $f$ is surjective, pick
$a$ with $f(a)=0$. Applying $P(a,y,z)$:
$$f(yz)=a+f(y)f(z) \qquad \text{for all } y,z. \tag{1}$$

**Step 3: $a=0$ and $f(1)=1$.** Set $y=1$ in (1): $f(z)=f(1)f(z)+a$, i.e. $(1-f(
1))f(z)=a$ for all $z$. If $f(1)\neq 1$, then $f$ is constant, contradicting sur
jectivity. Hence $f(1)=1$, and then $a=0$. In particular $f(0)=0$ (since $a$ is
the unique preimage of $0$ once we know injectivity below; more directly, (1) wi
th $y=z=0$ gives $f(0)=f(0)^2$, and with $y=0$: $f(0)=f(0)f(z)$, forcing $f(0)=0
$ as $f$ is not identically zero).

**Step 4: Injectivity and involution.** Rewrite the original equation using (1):
 $f(y)f(z)=f(yz)$, so
$$f(f(x)+yz)=x+f(yz).$$
Since $yz$ ranges over all of $\mathbb{R}$ as $y,z$ vary, for every $t\in\mathbb
{R}$:
$$f(f(x)+t)=x+f(t). \tag{2}$$
Setting $t=0$ (and $f(0)=0$): $f(f(x))=x$. Thus if $f(x_1)=f(x_2)$, applying $f$
 gives $x_1=x_2$; $f$ is injective, hence bijective.

**Step 5: Additivity.** In (2), since $f$ is bijective write $u=f(x)$ (so $x=f(u
)$, using $f(f(x))=x$):
$$f(u+t)=f(u)+f(t) \qquad \text{for all } u,t. \tag{3}$$
So $f$ is additive. Combined with (1) (now $f(yz)=f(y)f(z)$), $f$ is a field end
omorphism of $\mathbb{R}$ with $f(1)=1$.

**Step 6: $f$ is the identity.** For any $x\ge 0$, write $x=s^2$; then $f(x)=f(s
)^2\ge 0$. So $f$ preserves order. An additive, order-preserving map with $f(1)=
1$ fixes every rational, and for any real $x$ and any rationals $p\le x\le q$ we
 have $p=f(p)\le f(x)\le f(q)=q$; squeezing gives $f(x)=x$.

**Verification.** $f(x)=x$: $f(f(x)+yz)=f(x+yz)=x+yz=x+y\cdot z=x+f(y)f(z)$. ✓

No other function satisfies the equation.
