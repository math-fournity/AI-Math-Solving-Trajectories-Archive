Yes, such a subsequence exists.

**Proof.** Let $C := \sup_n \|g_n\|_{L^2(0,1)} < \infty$. Since the Lebesgue mea
sure of $(0,1)$ is $1$, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives
$$\|g_n\|_{L^1} = \int_0^1 |g_n|\cdot 1 \le \|g_n\|_{L^2}\,\|1\|_{L^2} = \|g_n\|
_{L^2} \le C,$$
so $(g_n)$ is uniformly bounded in $L^1(0,1)$.

We claim $(g_n)$ is uniformly integrable. Indeed, for every measurable $E\subset
eq(0,1)$,
$$\int_E |g_n| \le \|g_n\|_{L^2}\,|E|^{1/2} \le C\,|E|^{1/2},$$
uniformly in $n$. Hence $\sup_n \int_E |g_n| \to 0$ as $|E|\to 0$, which is unif
orm integrability (the "absolute continuity" condition; the boundedness part is
already established).

By the **Dunford–Pettis theorem**, a bounded subset of $L^1(\mu)$ (with $\mu$ $\
sigma$-finite, here finite) is relatively weakly compact if and only if it is un
iformly integrable. Therefore the set $\{g_n : n\ge 1\}$ is relatively weakly co
mpact in $L^1(0,1)$.

Relative weak compactness means its weak closure is weakly compact, so every seq
uence in it admits a weakly convergent subsequence (the weak topology on a weakl
y compact set is compact; in the sequentially reasonable setting of $L^1$ over a
 $\sigma$-finite measure, weak compactness yields sequential weak compactness —
the Eberlein–Šmulian theorem applies since $L^1$ is a Banach space). Concretely,
 there exist $g\in L^1(0,1)$ and a subsequence $(g_{n_k})$ such that
$$\int_0^1 g_{n_k}\,\varphi \to \int_0^1 g\,\varphi \qquad \forall\,\varphi\in L
^\infty(0,1),$$
i.e. $g_{n_k} \rightharpoonup g$ weakly in $L^1(0,1)$.

Thus the answer is **yes**: uniform boundedness in $L^2$ on a finite measure spa
ce forces uniform integrability in $L^1$, which by Dunford–Pettis gives relative
 weak compactness in $L^1$, hence a weakly convergent subsequence.
