## Answer: TRUE (for real line bundles, the standard interpretation of "orientab
le line bundle")

### Proof

Let $B$ be a paracompact base space and let $L \to B$ be a **real line bundle**
(a rank-1 real vector bundle). We show that $L$ is orientable if and only if $L$
 is trivial. In particular, every orientable line bundle is trivial.

**Step 1: Structure group of a real line bundle.**
A real line bundle has structure group
$$\mathrm{GL}(1,\mathbb{R}) = \mathbb{R}^{\times} = \mathbb{R}_{>0} \cup \mathbb
{R}_{<0},$$
acting on the fiber $\mathbb{R}$ by scalar multiplication. The bundle is determi
ned (up to isomorphism) by a Čech 1-cocycle $\{g_{\alpha\beta}: U_\alpha \cap U_
\beta \to \mathbb{R}^{\times}\}$ of transition functions.

**Step 2: Orientability = reduction to the positive component.**
By definition, a rank-$n$ real vector bundle is *orientable* if its structure gr
oup can be reduced from $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})$ to the identity component $\
mathrm{GL}^+(n,\mathbb{R})$ (positive determinant). For $n=1$,
$$\mathrm{GL}^+(1,\mathbb{R}) = \mathbb{R}_{>0}.$$
Thus $L$ is orientable iff every transition function $g_{\alpha\beta}$ can be ch
osen to take values in $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$, i.e., iff the structure group reduces
to $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$.

Equivalently, $L$ is orientable iff its first Stiefel–Whitney class vanishes:
$$w_1(L) = 0 \in H^1(B;\mathbb{Z}/2).$$
The class $w_1(L)$ is precisely the obstruction to reducing the structure group
to $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$: it records the sign (mod 2) of the transition functions.

**Step 3: The positive structure group is contractible.**
The group $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ is contractible: the map
$$H(t,x) = x^{1-t} \quad (t \in [0,1],\; x \in \mathbb{R}_{>0})$$
is a deformation retraction of $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ onto $\{1\}$. (Concretely, $H(0
,x)=x$, $H(1,x)=1$, and $H$ is continuous.)

Because $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ is contractible, its classifying space $B(\mathbb{R}_{
>0})$ is contractible. Therefore there is, up to isomorphism, **exactly one** $\
mathbb{R}_{>0}$-bundle over any base $B$: the trivial one. Concretely, given tra
nsition functions $g_{\alpha\beta}: U_\alpha\cap U_\beta \to \mathbb{R}_{>0}$, d
efine $h_\alpha: U_\alpha \to \mathbb{R}_{>0}$ by
$$h_\alpha(x) = \exp\!\Big(\int_{x_0}^{x} \text{(local primitive)}\Big) \quad \t
ext{or more directly,}$$
using the logarithm: $\log g_{\alpha\beta}$ is a real-valued Čech 1-cocycle, hen
ce (since the sheaf of continuous real-valued functions is fine/soft on a paraco
mpact space) a coboundary. So there exist continuous $f_\alpha: U_\alpha \to \ma
thbb{R}$ with
$$\log g_{\alpha\beta} = f_\alpha - f_\beta.$$
Setting $h_\alpha = e^{f_\alpha}: U_\alpha \to \mathbb{R}_{>0}$, we obtain
$$g_{\alpha\beta} = \frac{h_\alpha}{h_\beta}.$$
This is exactly the cocycle condition for the **trivial** bundle: the local triv
ializations $\varphi_\alpha: L|_{U_\alpha} \to U_\alpha \times \mathbb{R}$ can b
e re-scaled by $h_\alpha$ to give a single global trivialization. Hence $L \cong
 B \times \mathbb{R}$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**
Combining Steps 2 and 3: $L$ orientable $\Rightarrow$ structure group reduces to
 $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ $\Rightarrow$ $L$ is trivial (Step 3). The converse is immedi
ate: a trivial bundle is orientable. Therefore, for real line bundles,
$$\text{orientable} \iff \text{trivial}.$$
In particular, **every orientable line bundle is a trivial bundle**, so the stat
ement is **true**.

**Remark on the converse direction (trivial $\Rightarrow$ orientable).** A trivi
al bundle $B \times \mathbb{R}$ admits the global frame $(x \mapsto (x,1))$, whi
ch gives a global orientation, so it is orientable. This confirms the equivalenc
e is genuine.

**Remark on classification.** Real line bundles over a paracompact $B$ are class
ified by $H^1(B;\mathbb{Z}/2)$ via the map $L \mapsto w_1(L)$. The trivial bundl
e corresponds to $0 \in H^1(B;\mathbb{Z}/2)$. Since $w_1(L)=0$ is *both* the ori
entability condition and the triviality condition, orientable line bundles are e
xactly the trivial ones — a single, clean characterization.
