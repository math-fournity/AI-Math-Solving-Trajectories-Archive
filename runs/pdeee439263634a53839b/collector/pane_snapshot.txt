## Proof

Let $S_n = \operatorname{sgn}(X_n) \in \{+1,-1\}$. Since the $X_n$ are independe
nt, continuous, and symmetric about $0$ (mean $0$ Gaussian), the random variable
s $S_1, S_2, \dots$ are i.i.d. with
$$\mathbb{P}(S_n = +1) = \mathbb{P}(S_n = -1) = \tfrac{1}{2}.$$

The event in question is
$$A_n := \{X_n X_{n+1} > 0\} = \{S_n = S_{n+1}\},$$
and we want $\mathbb{P}(\limsup_{n\to\infty} A_n) = \mathbb{P}(A_n \text{ infini
tely often})$.

**Key reduction.** Consider the subsequence of odd indices and define
$$T_k := \mathbf{1}_{A_{2k-1}} = \mathbf{1}_{\{S_{2k-1} = S_{2k}\}}, \qquad k =
1, 2, \dots$$

Each $T_k$ depends only on the pair $(S_{2k-1}, S_{2k})$. For distinct $k$, thes
e pairs are **disjoint** collections of the independent variables $\{S_n\}$, hen
ce $\{T_k\}_{k\ge 1}$ are **independent**. Moreover,
$$\mathbb{P}(T_k = 1) = \mathbb{P}(S_{2k-1} = S_{2k}) = \tfrac{1}{2},$$
so $\{T_k\}$ are i.i.d. $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)$.

**Applying Borel–Cantelli.** Since
$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \mathbb{P}(T_k = 1) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \tfrac{1}{2} = \
infty,$$
the second Borel–Cantelli lemma (applicable because the $T_k$ are independent) g
ives
$$\mathbb{P}(T_k = 1 \text{ infinitely often}) = 1.$$

**Conclusion.** The event $\{T_k = 1 \text{ i.o.}\}$ is exactly $\{A_{2k-1} \tex
t{ i.o.}\}$, which is contained in $\{A_n \text{ i.o.}\} = \limsup_{n\to\infty}
A_n$. Therefore
$$\mathbb{P}\!\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty} \{X_n X_{n+1} > 0\}\right) \ge \mathbb{
P}(T_k = 1 \text{ i.o.}) = 1.$$

Since probabilities are at most $1$,
$$\boxed{\mathbb{P}\!\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty} \{X_n X_{n+1} > 0\}\right) = 1}.
$$
