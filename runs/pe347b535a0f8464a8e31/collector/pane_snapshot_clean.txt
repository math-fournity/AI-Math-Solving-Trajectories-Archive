Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a linear injective open ope
rator A: X → Y between Banach spaces must be bounded.

**Answer: Yes, A is necessarily bounded.**

**Proof:**

**Step 1: A is surjective.**

Since A: X → Y is open, it maps every open subset of X to an open subset of Y. T
aking U = X (which is open in itself), the image A(X) is open in Y. But A(X) is
a linear subspace of the normed space Y, and the only open linear subspace of a
normed space is the whole space.

*Proof of this fact:* Suppose M ⊆ Y is an open subspace. Then M contains an open
 ball B_Y(0, r) for some r > 0. For any nonzero y ∈ Y, the vector ry/(2‖y‖) has
norm r/2 < r, hence lies in B_Y(0, r) ⊆ M. Since M is a linear subspace, y = (2‖
y‖/r)·(ry/(2‖y‖)) ∈ M. Thus M = Y.

Therefore A(X) = Y, i.e., A is surjective.

**Step 2: A is bijective.**

A is injective (given) and surjective (Step 1), hence a bijection.

**Step 3: A⁻¹ is continuous.**

Since A is an open bijection, its inverse A⁻¹: Y → X is continuous: for any open
 set U ⊆ X, the preimage (A⁻¹)⁻¹(U) = A(U) is open in Y by the openness of A. Th
is is precisely the continuity of A⁻¹.

**Step 4: A is bounded.**

Now A⁻¹: Y → X is a continuous (bounded) linear bijection between Banach spaces.
 By the **Bounded Inverse Theorem** (a corollary of the Open Mapping Theorem), t
he inverse of A⁻¹ — which is A itself — is also continuous, hence bounded.

**Conclusion:** A is necessarily bounded. ∎

The key insight is that openness of a linear map into a normed space forces surj
ectivity (since the image is an open subspace, and the only open subspace is the
 whole space). Combined with injectivity, this gives bijectivity, and the Bounde
d Inverse Theorem closes the argument.
