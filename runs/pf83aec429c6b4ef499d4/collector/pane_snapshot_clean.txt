## Proof

Let us work over an arbitrary field (the argument is purely formal, so it applie
s in particular over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$). Write composition as $P\circ
 P$ for the polynomial $x\mapsto P(P(x))$, etc.

**Step 1. Reduce the two hypotheses to a single composition identity.**

From the first hypothesis,
$$P\circ P \;=\; Q\circ Q \;=:\; F.$$
Since $P$ has positive degree, $F = P\circ P$ also has positive degree; in parti
cular $\deg F \geq 1$.

From the second hypothesis,
$$P\circ P\circ P \;=\; Q\circ Q\circ Q.$$
Associating on the right and using $F = P\circ P = Q\circ Q$,
$$P\circ(P\circ P) \;=\; P\circ F, \qquad Q\circ(Q\circ Q) \;=\; Q\circ F.$$
Hence the second hypothesis is exactly
$$P\circ F \;=\; Q\circ F. \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Step 2. Conclude $P = Q$ from $(\ast)$ by a degree argument.**

Set $R := P - Q$. Equation $(\ast)$ says
$$R\circ F \;=\; (P-Q)\circ F \;=\; P\circ F - Q\circ F \;=\; 0,$$
i.e. $R(F(x))$ is the zero polynomial.

Suppose, for contradiction, that $R\neq 0$. Then $R$ is a nonzero polynomial; le
t $d = \deg R \geq 0$ and let $a\neq 0$ be its leading coefficient, so
$$R(t) = a\,t^{d} + (\text{terms of degree} < d).$$
Substituting $t = F(x)$, the term $a\,t^{d}$ becomes $a\,(F(x))^{d}$. Since $\de
g F = e \geq 1$ with leading coefficient $b\neq 0$, the polynomial $(F(x))^{d}$
has degree $de$ and leading coefficient $b^{d}\neq 0$. Every other term of $R(F(
x))$ has degree at most $(d-1)e < de$. Therefore
$$\deg(R\circ F) = de \geq e \geq 1,$$
and in particular $R\circ F$ is a **nonzero** polynomial.

This contradicts $R\circ F = 0$. Hence $R = 0$, i.e.
$$P(x) = Q(x).$$

**Conclusion.** The two identities $P(P(x))=Q(Q(x))$ and $P(P(P(x)))=Q(Q(Q(x)))$
 together force $P=Q$. The key point is that the first identity identifies the c
ommon iterate $F=P\circ P=Q\circ Q$ of positive degree, and the second identity
then reads $P\circ F = Q\circ F$; composing the nonzero polynomial $P-Q$ with a
non-constant polynomial cannot vanish, so $P-Q=0$.
