## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle I = \int_{|z|=1} e^{-1/z}\sin(1/z)\,dz$, where $C$ is
 the unit circle traversed once counterclockwise.

**Singularities.** The integrand $f(z)=e^{-1/z}\sin(1/z)$ has a single singulari
ty at $z=0$, which lies inside $|z|=1$. Since $e^{-1/z}$ and $\sin(1/z)$ both ha
ve essential singularities at $z=0$, so does $f$. By the residue theorem,

$$I = 2\pi i \,\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z).$$

**Computing the residue.** Set $w = 1/z$. Then $f(z) = e^{-w}\sin w$, and the La
urent coefficient of $z^{-1}$ in $f(z)$ equals the Taylor coefficient of $w^{1}$
 in $e^{-w}\sin w$ (viewed as a power series in $w$).

Use the identity $\sin w = \operatorname{Im}(e^{iw})$, so

$$e^{-w}\sin w = \operatorname{Im}\!\bigl(e^{-w}e^{iw}\bigr) = \operatorname{Im}
\!\bigl(e^{(i-1)w}\bigr).$$

Expanding,

$$e^{(i-1)w} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(i-1)^{n}}{n!}\,w^{n},$$

hence

$$e^{-w}\sin w = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{\operatorname{Im}\!\bigl((i-1)^{n}\bi
gr)}{n!}\,w^{n}.$$

The coefficient of $w^{1}$ is

$$\frac{\operatorname{Im}(i-1)}{1!} = \frac{1}{1} = 1.$$

Therefore

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z) = 1.$$

**Result.**

$$I = 2\pi i \cdot 1 = \boxed{2\pi i}.$$
