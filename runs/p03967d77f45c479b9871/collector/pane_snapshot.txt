## Weak Formulation

**Function spaces.** The Dirichlet condition $u(0)=0$ is essential; the Neumann-
type condition $u'(1)=1$ is natural. Set
$$V = \{v\in H^1(0,1): v(0)=0\}.$$

Multiply $-\epsilon u''+u'=1$ by a test function $v\in V$ and integrate over $(0
,1)$:
$$\int_0^1 (-\epsilon u''+u')v\,dx = \int_0^1 v\,dx.$$

Integrate the diffusion term by parts:
$$-\epsilon\int_0^1 u''v\,dx = -\epsilon\big[u'v\big]_0^1 + \epsilon\int_0^1 u'v
'\,dx = -\epsilon u'(1)v(1)+\epsilon u'(0)\underbrace{v(0)}_{=0}+\epsilon\int_0^
1 u'v'\,dx.$$

Using $u'(1)=1$, the boundary term becomes $-\epsilon v(1)$. Hence the **weak fo
rmulation** reads:

> Find $u\in V$ such that for all $v\in V$,
> $$\boxed{\;\epsilon\int_0^1 u'v'\,dx + \int_0^1 u'v\,dx = \int_0^1 v\,dx + \ep
silon\,v(1).\;}$$

Define the bilinear form and linear functional
$$a(u,v)=\epsilon\int_0^1 u'v'\,dx+\int_0^1 u'v\,dx,\qquad \ell(v)=\int_0^1 v\,d
x+\epsilon\,v(1).$$

## Galerkin Finite Element Approximation

Let $0=x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_N=1$ be a mesh with $h=\max_i(x_i-x_{i-1})$. Let $V_h\su
bset V$ be the space of continuous piecewise-affine ($\mathbb{P}_1$) functions v
anishing at $x=0$:
$$V_h=\{v_h\in C^0([0,1]): v_h|_{[x_{i-1},x_i]}\in\mathbb{P}_1,\ v_h(0)=0\}=\ope
ratorname{span}\{\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_N\},$$
where $\varphi_i$ is the hat function centered at $x_i$ (the basis node $\varphi
_0$ at $x_0=0$ is excluded).

The **Galerkin–FEM scheme** reads:

> Find $u_h\in V_h$ such that for all $v_h\in V_h$,
> $$\boxed{\;a(u_h,v_h)=\ell(v_h),\quad\text{i.e.}\quad \epsilon\int_0^1 u_h'v_h
'\,dx+\int_0^1 u_h'v_h\,dx=\int_0^1 v_h\,dx+\epsilon\,v_h(1).\;}$$

Writing $u_h=\sum_{j=1}^N U_j\varphi_j$, this yields the linear system $A\mathbf
{U}=\mathbf{b}$ with
$$A_{ij}=\epsilon\int_0^1 \varphi_i'\varphi_j'\,dx+\int_0^1 \varphi_i'\varphi_j\
,dx,\qquad b_i=\int_0^1\varphi_i\,dx+\epsilon\,\varphi_i(1)=\int_0^1\varphi_i\,d
x+\epsilon\,\delta_{iN}.$$

## Stability of the Scheme

We verify stability via **coercivity** of $a(\cdot,\cdot)$ on $V$ (and hence on
$V_h\subset V$).

For any $v\in V$, using $v(0)=0$,
$$\int_0^1 v'v\,dx=\tfrac12\big[v^2\big]_0^1=\tfrac12\,v(1)^2\ge 0.$$

Therefore
$$a(v,v)=\epsilon\int_0^1 (v')^2\,dx+\int_0^1 v'v\,dx=\epsilon\|v'\|_{L^2}^2+\tf
rac12 v(1)^2\ge \epsilon\|v'\|_{L^2}^2.$$

Since every $v\in V$ satisfies $v(0)=0$, the Poincaré–Friedrichs inequality give
s a constant $C_P>0$ (independent of $v$, here $C_P=2/\pi$ for the interval) wit
h
$$\|v\|_{L^2(0,1)}\le C_P\|v'\|_{L^2(0,1)}.$$

Consequently, with $\|v\|_{H^1}^2=\|v\|_{L^2}^2+\|v'\|_{L^2}^2\le (1+C_P^2)\|v'\
|_{L^2}^2$,
$$a(v,v)\ge \epsilon\|v'\|_{L^2}^2\ge \frac{\epsilon}{1+C_P^2}\,\|v\|_{H^1}^2.$$

So $a$ is **coercive** on $V$ with constant $\alpha=\dfrac{\epsilon}{1+C_P^2}>0$
. Continuity of $a$ on $V\times V$ is immediate (each term bounded by $\epsilon\
|u'\|\|v'\|+\|u'\|\|v\|\le(\epsilon+1)\|u\|_{H^1}\|v\|_{H^1}$, using $\|v\|_{L^\
infty}\le\|v\|_{H^1}$). The functional $\ell$ is continuous on $V$ since $|\ell(
v)|\le\|v\|_{L^2}+\epsilon|v(1)|\le(1+\epsilon)\|v\|_{H^1}$.

By the **Lax–Milgram theorem**, the weak problem admits a unique solution $u\in
V$. Since $V_h\subset V$ is a closed subspace and $a$ is coercive on $V_h$ with
the same constant $\alpha$, the Galerkin problem also admits a unique solution $
u_h\in V_h$, and **Céa's lemma** gives the quasi-optimal error estimate
$$\|u-u_h\|_{H^1}\le \frac{M}{\alpha}\inf_{v_h\in V_h}\|u-v_h\|_{H^1}=\frac{(1+\
epsilon)(1+C_P^2)}{\epsilon}\inf_{v_h\in V_h}\|u-v_h\|_{H^1}.$$

In particular, taking $v_h=0$ yields the **stability bound**
$$\|u_h\|_{H^1}\le \frac{1}{\alpha}\|\ell\|_{V'}\le \frac{(1+\epsilon)(1+C_P^2)}
{\epsilon},$$
i.e. the discrete solution is bounded independently of the mesh and of the data,
 with a constant depending only on $\epsilon$ and the domain. The scheme is ther
efore **stable**.

## Explanation of the Result

The stability has a precise structural reason. The bilinear form is the sum of a
 **symmetric, positive diffusion part** $\epsilon\int u'v'$ and a **skew-symmetr
ic convection part** $\int u'v$. Although the convection operator $v\mapsto v'$
is not self-adjoint and is not coercive on its own, its contribution to the ener
gy is

$$\int_0^1 v'v\,dx=\tfrac12 v(1)^2\ge 0,$$

which is **non-negative** precisely because the homogeneous Dirichlet condition
is imposed at the **inflow endpoint** $x=0$ (the direction of the convective fie
ld $b\equiv+1$ points from $0$ to $1$). The outflow boundary $x=1$ carries only
the natural condition, contributing the non-negative term $\tfrac12 v(1)^2$ rath
er than a destabilizing term. Thus:

- the diffusion term supplies $\epsilon\|v'\|^2\ge 0$;
- the convection term supplies $\tfrac12 v(1)^2\ge 0$ thanks to the inflow Diric
hlet condition;
- together they make $a(v,v)\ge \epsilon\|v'\|^2$, which is coercive on $V$ by P
oincaré.

This is the **energy stability** typical of convection–diffusion equations with
the Dirichlet condition placed on the inflow boundary: the convection does not d
estroy coercivity, so the standard Galerkin method (no upwinding needed) is stab
le. The price is that the coercivity constant $\alpha\sim\epsilon$ degenerates a
s $\epsilon\to 0$ (the singularly perturbed regime), so the error constant $M/\a
lpha\sim 1/\epsilon$ blows up and boundary layers of width $O(\epsilon)$ at $x=1
$ are not resolved unless $h\lesssim\epsilon$; this is the well-known limitation
 of plain Galerkin for convection-dominated problems, but it does not affect the
 **stability** of the scheme, which holds for every $\epsilon>0$.
