## Proof

**Step 1: $[G,G]$ is arcwise connected.**

Since $G$ is a connected Lie group, it is a connected manifold, hence locally pa
th-connected, hence path-connected.

Every element of $[G,G]$ is a finite product of commutators $[x,y] = xyx^{-1}y^{
-1}$ with $x, y \in G$. For any commutator $[x,y]$, since $G$ is path-connected,
 there exist continuous paths $\alpha, \beta: [0,1] \to G$ with $\alpha(0) = \be
ta(0) = e$, $\alpha(1) = x$, $\beta(1) = y$. The path
$$\gamma(t) = [\alpha(t), \beta(t)] = \alpha(t)\beta(t)\alpha(t)^{-1}\beta(t)^{-
1}$$
is continuous, lies in $[G,G]$ (each value is a commutator), and connects $e$ to
 $[x,y]$.

For a product $g = c_1 c_2 \cdots c_n$ of commutators, with paths $\gamma_i$ fro
m $e$ to $c_i$ in $[G,G]$, the product path $t \mapsto \gamma_1(t)\gamma_2(t)\cd
ots\gamma_n(t)$ is continuous, lies in $[G,G]$ (since $[G,G]$ is a subgroup), an
d connects $e$ to $g$. Hence $[G,G]$ is arcwise connected.

**Step 2: $[G,G]$ is a Lie subgroup (Yamabe's theorem).**

By **Yamabe's theorem** (1950): an arcwise connected subgroup of a Lie group is
a (unique) immersed Lie subgroup. Thus $[G,G]$ is an immersed Lie subgroup of $G
$ with some Lie algebra $\mathfrak{h} \subseteq \mathfrak{g}$, and the inclusion
 $[G,G] \hookrightarrow G$ is an immersion.

**Step 3: $[G,G] \subseteq H$, where $H$ is the connected Lie subgroup with Lie
algebra $[\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}]$.**

By the Lie subgroup–Lie subalgebra correspondence (Chevalley), there exists a un
ique connected immersed Lie subgroup $H$ of $G$ with Lie algebra $[\mathfrak{g},
 \mathfrak{g}]$. Since $[\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}]$ is an ideal of $\mathfrak{
g}$ (it is a characteristic ideal, preserved by all derivations $\mathrm{ad}(X)$
), $H$ is normal in $G$ (for connected $G$, a connected immersed Lie subgroup is
 normal iff its Lie algebra is an ideal).

The quotient $G/H$ is a connected Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}/[\mat
hfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]$, which is abelian. A connected Lie group with abelian Li
e algebra is abelian (the image of $\exp$ is an abelian open neighborhood of $e$
 that generates $G$). Therefore $G/H$ is abelian, which gives $[G,G] \subseteq H
$.

This implies $\mathfrak{h} \subseteq [\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}]$: for any $X \
in \mathfrak{h}$, we have $\exp(tX) \in [G,G] \subseteq H$ for all $t$, so $X \i
n \mathrm{Lie}(H) = [\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}]$.

**Step 4: $[\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}] \subseteq \mathfrak{h}$ via the commutat
or limit formula.**

We use the commutator limit formula. For $X, Y \in \mathfrak{g}$ and $t > 0$:

$$\exp(t[X,Y]) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left(\exp\!\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{t}\,X}{n}\ri
ght)\exp\!\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{t}\,Y}{n}\right)\exp\!\left(-\tfrac{\sqrt{t}\,X}{n}
\right)\exp\!\left(-\tfrac{\sqrt{t}\,Y}{n}\right)\right)^{n^2}$$

This follows from the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula:
$$\log\!\big(\exp(sX)\exp(sY)\exp(-sX)\exp(-sY)\big) = s^2[X,Y] + O(s^3),$$
so setting $s = \sqrt{t}/n$ and raising to the $n^2$-th power yields $\exp\!\big
(t[X,Y] + O(1/n)\big) \to \exp(t[X,Y])$ as $n \to \infty$.

Each factor $\exp(\sqrt{t}\,X/n)\exp(\sqrt{t}\,Y/n)\exp(-\sqrt{t}\,X/n)\exp(-\sq
rt{t}\,Y/n)$ is a commutator in $G$, hence lies in $[G,G]$. The $n^2$-th power i
s a product of elements of $[G,G]$, hence also in $[G,G]$. So $\exp(t[X,Y])$ lie

Now we use the local structure of immersed submanifolds. Since $[G,G]$ is an imm
ersed submanifold of $G$, it is **locally embedded**: there exists a neighborhoo
d $V$ of $e$ in $G$ such that $[G,G] \cap V$ is an embedded submanifold of $V$,
and in particular $[G,G] \cap V$ is **closed in $V$**.

For $t > 0$ sufficiently small, $\exp(t[X,Y]) \in V$. The approximating sequence
 lies in $[G,G]$ and converges to $\exp(t[X,Y]) \in V$; for large enough $n$, th
e terms lie in $V \cap [G,G]$, which is closed in $V$. Therefore the limit $\exp
(t[X,Y]) \in [G,G]$.

For $t < 0$: $\exp(t[X,Y]) = \big(\exp(|t|[X,Y])\big)^{-1} \in [G,G]$, since $[G
,G]$ is a group. For $t = 0$: trivially $e \in [G,G]$.

Thus $\exp(t[X,Y]) \in [G,G]$ for all $t$ near $0$, which means $[X,Y] \in \math
frak{h}$ (the Lie algebra of $[G,G]$). Since $[\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}]$ is s
panned (as a vector space) by elements of the form $[X,Y]$ with $X, Y \in \mathf
rak{g}$, we conclude:

$$[\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}] \subseteq \mathfrak{h}.$$

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 3 and 4:
$$\mathfrak{h} \subseteq [\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}] \quad \text{and} \quad [\m
athfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}] \subseteq \mathfrak{h},$$
so $\mathfrak{h} = [\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}]$.

Therefore $[G,G]$ is a Lie subgroup of $G$ (immersed) with Lie algebra $[\mathfr
ak{g}, \mathfrak{g}]$, and the inclusion map $[G,G] \hookrightarrow G$ is an imm
ersion. $\blacksquare$
