## Proof

**Setup.** Place the warehouse wall along a line. The fenced region is a rectang
le with one side parallel to the warehouse wall. Let $L$ be the length of the si
de parallel to the warehouse and $W$ the length of each perpendicular side. The
warehouse wall is 250 m long, so:

- If $L \le 250$: the entire side of length $L$ lies along the warehouse wall an
d needs no fence. Fencing used: $L + 2W$.
- If $L > 250$: only 250 m of that side is covered by the warehouse wall; the re
maining $L - 250$ must be fenced. Fencing used: $(L-250) + L + 2W = 2L + 2W - 25
0$.

In both cases the enclosed area is $A = LW$.

**Case 1: $L \le 250$.** The constraint $L + 2W = 1000$ gives $W = \frac{1000-L}
{2}$, so
$$A = \frac{L(1000-L)}{2}.$$
This is a downward parabola maximized at $L = 500$, which violates $L \le 250$.
Thus the maximum in this region is at the boundary $L = 250$:
$$A = 250 \cdot \frac{750}{2} = 250 \cdot 375 = 93{,}750 \text{ m}^2.$$

**Case 2: $L > 250$.** The constraint $2L + 2W - 250 = 1000$ gives $L + W = 625$
, i.e. $W = 625 - L$, so
$$A = L(625 - L).$$
This is a downward parabola maximized at $L = 625/2 = 312.5$, giving $W = 312.5$
. Check feasibility: $L = 312.5 > 250$ ✓ and $W = 312.5 > 0$ ✓.

**Fencing verification.** The rectangle is $312.5 \times 312.5$. The side along
the warehouse is 312.5 m, of which 250 m is against the wall (free) and 62.5 m m
ust be fenced. Total fencing:
$$(312.5 - 250) + 312.5 + 2(312.5) = 62.5 + 312.5 + 625 = 1000 \text{ m}. \check
mark$$

**Maximum area:**
$$A = 312.5^2 = \frac{625^2}{4} = \frac{390625}{4} = 97{,}656.25 \text{ m}^2.$$

Since $97{,}656.25 > 93{,}750$, the global maximum occurs in Case 2.

**The largest area that can be enclosed is $\boxed{97{,}656.25 \text{ m}^2}$ (i.
e., $\frac{390625}{4}$ m²), achieved by a $312.5 \text{ m} \times 312.5 \text{ m
}$ rectangle.**
