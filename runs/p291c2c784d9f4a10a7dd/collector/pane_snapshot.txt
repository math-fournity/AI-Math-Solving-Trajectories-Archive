## Solution

We want $\mathbb{E}[Y_{n+1} - Y_n \mid S_n = x]$, where $Y_n = f(S_n)$.

**Step 1: Express the increment in terms of the walk.**

$$Y_{n+1} - Y_n = f(S_{n+1}) - f(S_n).$$

Given $S_n = x$, the recurrence $S_{n+1} = S_n + X_{n+1} = x + X_{n+1}$, where $
X_{n+1}$ is uniform on $B(0,1)$ and independent of $S_n$ (the $X_i$ are i.i.d.).
 Hence

$$\mathbb{E}[Y_{n+1} - Y_n \mid S_n = x] = \mathbb{E}\big[f(x + X_{n+1})\big] -
f(x).$$

**Step 2: Evaluate the expectation as a ball average.**

Since $X_{n+1} \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(B(0,1))$,

$$\mathbb{E}\big[f(x + X_{n+1})\big] = \frac{1}{|B(0,1)|}\int_{B(0,1)} f(x + y)\
,dy = \frac{1}{|B(x,1)|}\int_{B(x,1)} f(z)\,dz,$$

using the change of variables $z = x + y$ and $|B(x,1)| = |B(0,1)|$. This is pre
cisely the **ball mean value** (average of $f$ over the unit ball centered at $x
$).

**Step 3: Apply the superharmonic mean-value inequality.**

A function $f$ is **superharmonic** on $\mathbb{R}^d$ iff it is lower semicontin
uous and for every ball $\overline{B(x,r)} \subset \mathbb{R}^d$,

$$f(x) \;\geq\; \frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)} f(z)\,dz.$$

That is, the value at the center dominates the average over any ball centered th
ere. Applying this with $r = 1$:

$$\frac{1}{|B(x,1)|}\int_{B(x,1)} f(z)\,dz \;\leq\; f(x).$$

**Step 4: Combine.**

$$\mathbb{E}[Y_{n+1} - Y_n \mid S_n = x] = \frac{1}{|B(x,1)|}\int_{B(x,1)} f(z)\
,dz - f(x) \;\leq\; 0.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}[Y_{n+1} - Y_n \mid S_n = x] \;=\; \frac{1}{|B(0,1)|}\int_{B(
0,1)} f(x+y)\,dy \;-\; f(x) \;\leq\; 0.}$$

The expected increment is non-positive: the process $Y_n = f(S_n)$ is a **superm
artingale**. This is exactly the probabilistic characterization of superharmonic
ity — a function is superharmonic if and only if its composition with a random w
alk (with symmetric, bounded increments) is a supermartingale. The ball-average
inequality defining superharmonicity is precisely the statement that the conditi
onal drift of $f(S_n)$ is $\leq 0$.
