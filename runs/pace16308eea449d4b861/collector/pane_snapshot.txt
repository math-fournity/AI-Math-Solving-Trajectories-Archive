## Proof

We show that $B$ must be positive semidefinite.

**Step 1: $B$ must be symmetric.**

The hypothesis is that $A^t B A \geq 0$ for every symmetric matrix $A$. Since po
sitive semidefinite implies symmetry (by assumption), $A^t B A$ is symmetric for
 every symmetric $A$. Taking $A = I$ (which is symmetric), we get $B = I^t B I =
 B \geq 0$, so in particular $B$ is symmetric. Explicitly: $B = B^t$.

**Step 2: $B$ is positive semidefinite.**

Since $B$ is symmetric, it remains to show $v^t B v \geq 0$ for every vector $v$
.

For any vector $v$, the matrix $A = vv^t$ is symmetric (and positive semidefinit
e). By hypothesis, $A^t B A \geq 0$. Using $A^t = A = vv^t$:

$$A^t B A = vv^t B\, vv^t = (v^t B v)\, vv^t.$$

Since $v^t B v$ is a scalar and $vv^t$ is positive semidefinite, the product $(v
^t B v)\,vv^t$ is positive semidefinite if and only if $v^t B v \geq 0$.

(Justification of "only if": if $v^t B v < 0$, then $(v^t B v)\,vv^t$ has the ne
gative eigenvalue $v^t B v$ with eigenvector $v$, contradicting positive semidef
initeness.)

Since this holds for every $v$, we conclude $v^t B v \geq 0$ for all $v$, i.e.,
$B$ is positive semidefinite.

**Conclusion.** Yes, $B$ must be positive semidefinite. The condition $A^t B A \
geq 0$ for all symmetric $A$ forces $B$ to be symmetric (via $A = I$) and then f
orces $v^t B v \geq 0$ for all $v$ (via $A = vv^t$), which is exactly positive s
emidefiniteness.
