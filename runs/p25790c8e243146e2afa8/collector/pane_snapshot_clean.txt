## Proof

**Answer: No.** In general, such a sequence of curves need not exist.

**Counterexample.**

Let $X = [0,1]$ with the standard metric $d(x,y)=|x-y|$. For each $n\in\mathbb{N
}$, let
$$X_n = \left\{0,\;\tfrac{1}{n},\;\tfrac{2}{n},\;\dots,\;1\right\}$$
with the metric $d_n$ induced from $[0,1]$ (i.e., $d_n(a,b)=|a-b|$).

**Claim 1: $X_n \to X$ in the Gromov–Hausdorff metric.**

Since $X_n \subset X = [0,1]$, we may embed both in the common ambient space $[0
,1]$. The Hausdorff distance $d_H(X_n, [0,1])$ equals $1/(2n)$: every point of $
[0,1]$ lies within distance $1/(2n)$ of some point $k/n \in X_n$ (the worst case
 being the midpoint between consecutive grid points), and $X_n \subset [0,1]$ tr
ivially. Since $d_{GH}(X_n, X) \leq d_H(X_n, X)$ when both sit in a common space
, we get $d_{GH}(X_n, X) \leq \frac{1}{2n} \to 0$. $\checkmark$

**Claim 2: The curve $\gamma(t)=t$ satisfies $d(\gamma(1),\gamma(0))=1$.**

This is immediate: $d(\gamma(1),\gamma(0))=|1-0|=1$.

**Claim 3: Every continuous curve $\gamma_n : I \to X_n$ is constant, so $d_n(\g
amma_n(1),\gamma_n(0))=0$.**

$X_n$ is a **finite** metric space. In any finite metric space, each singleton $
\{x\}$ is open (take a ball of radius smaller than the minimum distance from $x$
 to any other point), so $X_n$ carries the **discrete topology**. The only conne
cted subsets of a discrete space are singletons.

Since $I=[0,1]$ is connected and $\gamma_n$ is continuous, the image $\gamma_n(I
)$ is a connected subset of $X_n$, hence a single point. Therefore $\gamma_n$ is
 constant, and
$$d_n(\gamma_n(1),\gamma_n(0)) = 0 \quad \text{for every } n.$$

**Conclusion.**

For **any** sequence of curves $\gamma_n : I \to X_n$,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} d_n(\gamma_n(1),\gamma_n(0)) = 0 \neq 1 = d(\gamma(1),\gamma
(0)).$$

Thus no such sequence exists. The obstruction is topological: GH convergence pre
serves metric information (distances between approximating points converge corre
ctly), but it does **not** preserve path-connectivity or any topological propert
y that would guarantee the existence of non-constant curves in the approximating
 spaces. Finite (hence totally disconnected) spaces can approximate connected sp
aces in the GH sense, yet admit no non-constant continuous curves at all. $\blac
ksquare$
