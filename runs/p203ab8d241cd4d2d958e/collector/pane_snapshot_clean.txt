### Setup: a good cover of $S^1$

Parametrize $S^1 \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ by the angle $\theta \in \mathbb{R}/2\pi\
mathbb{Z}$. Choose two open arcs

$$U = S^1 \setminus \{(1,0)\}, \qquad V = S^1 \setminus \{(-1,0)\}.$$

Each of $U, V$ is an open arc diffeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}$, hence contractible.
 Their intersection is

$$U \cap V = S^1 \setminus \{(1,0),(-1,0)\},$$

the disjoint union of two open arcs (the upper and lower semicircles), each diff
eomorphic to $\mathbb{R}$. Thus $U \cap V$ has two contractible connected compon
ents $W_1, W_2$.

### Cohomology of the pieces

Since $U$, $V$, $W_1$, $W_2$ are all contractible:

| Space | $H^0$ | $H^1$ |
|-------|-------|-------|
| $U$ | $\mathbb{R}$ | $0$ |
| $V$ | $\mathbb{R}$ | $0$ |
| $U\cap V = W_1\sqcup W_2$ | $\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}$ | $0$ |

### Mayer–Vietoris sequence

The Mayer–Vietoris long exact sequence for de Rham cohomology of the cover $\{U,
V\}$ reads

$$0 \to H^0(S^1) \xrightarrow{\;\alpha\;} H^0(U)\oplus H^0(V) \xrightarrow{\;\be
ta\;} H^0(U\cap V) \xrightarrow{\;\delta\;} H^1(S^1) \xrightarrow{\;\gamma\;} H^
1(U)\oplus H^1(V) \to \cdots$$

Substituting the computed groups:

$$0 \to H^0(S^1) \xrightarrow{\;\alpha\;} \mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R} \xrightarro
w{\;\beta\;} \mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R} \xrightarrow{\;\delta\;} H^1(S^1) \xrigh
tarrow{\;\gamma\;} 0 \to 0.$$

### Identifying the maps

- **$H^0(S^1) \cong \mathbb{R}$** since $S^1$ is connected. The map $\alpha$ sen
ds a constant $c$ to $(c,c)$, the diagonal embedding. So $\ker\beta = \operatorn
ame{im}\alpha = \{(c,c):c\in\mathbb{R}\}$.

- The map $\beta: \mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}$ is th
e difference of restrictions: a pair of constants $(a,b)\in H^0(U)\oplus H^0(V)$
 is sent to $(a-b,\, a-b)\in H^0(W_1)\oplus H^0(W_2)$, since a constant on $U$ (
resp. $V$) restricts to the **same** constant on both components $W_1, W_2$. Hen
ce
$$\beta(a,b) = (a-b,\; a-b), \qquad \operatorname{im}\beta = \{(c,c):c\in\mathbb
{R}\}\cong\mathbb{R}.$$

- Since $H^1(U)\oplus H^1(V) = 0$, the map $\gamma$ is the zero map, so $\delta$
 is **surjective**.

### Computing $H^1(S^1)$

By exactness at $H^0(U\cap V)$:
$$\ker\delta = \operatorname{im}\beta \cong \mathbb{R}.$$

By exactness at $H^1(S^1)$ and surjectivity of $\delta$:
$$H^1(S^1) \cong H^0(U\cap V)\,/\,\operatorname{im}\beta = (\mathbb{R}\oplus\mat
hbb{R})\,/\,\{(c,c):c\in\mathbb{R}\} \cong \mathbb{R}.$$

### Explicit generator

A concrete representative of the generator is the 1-form
$$\omega = \frac{-y\,dx + x\,dy}{x^2+y^2}\bigg|_{S^1} = d\theta,$$
where $\theta$ is the angular coordinate. It is closed (locally exact, $d\omega
= 0$) but **not exact** on $S^1$: if $\omega = df$ for a globally defined smooth
 $f$ on $S^1$, then
$$\int_{S^1}\omega = \int_0^{2\pi}d\theta = 2\pi \stackrel{?}{=} \int_{S^1}df =
0,$$
a contradiction (Stokes' theorem on the closed manifold $S^1$). Hence $[\omega]\
neq 0$, and since $\dim H^1_{dR}(S^1) = 1$, the class $[\omega]$ generates $H^1_
{dR}(S^1)$.

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{H^1_{dR}(S^1)\;\cong\;\mathbb{R}}.$$
