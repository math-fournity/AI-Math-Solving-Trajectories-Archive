$y=-r$ (where $r$ is the inradius). The tangent point of the incircle with $BC$
is $T=(0,-r)$, with $BT=s-b$ and $TC=s-c$ (where $s$ is the semi-perimeter). Thu
s:
$$B=(-(s-b),\,-r),\quad C=(s-c,\,-r),\quad M=\left(\tfrac{b-c}{2},\,-r\right).$$

**Step 1: The equidistance condition.** We have $|JM|^2 = \frac{(b-c)^2}{4}+r^2$
. The distance from the incenter to vertex $A$ is $|JA|=r/\sin(\alpha/2)$, so $|
JA|^2=r^2/\sin^2(\alpha/2)$. Setting $|JA|=|JM|$:

$$\frac{(b-c)^2}{4}+r^2=\frac{r^2}{\sin^2(\alpha/2)} \implies \frac{(b-c)^2}{4}=
r^2\cot^2(\alpha/2).$$

Since $r=(s-a)\tan(\alpha/2)$, we get $r\cot(\alpha/2)=s-a$, hence $|b-c|=2(s-a)
=b+c-a$. This gives two cases: **$a=2b$** or **$a=2c$**. By symmetry (swapping $
B\leftrightarrow C$), we may assume $a=2b$ (which forces $c>b$).

**Step 2: Parametrization.** Set $t=c/b\in(1,3)$ and $u=\tan(\alpha/2)>0$. By th
e sine rule, $\sin\beta=\sin\alpha/2$, so $\beta=\arcsin(\sin\alpha/2)$ (the obt
use branch yields no valid triangle). Then:
$$t=\frac{c}{b}=\frac{\sin\gamma}{\sin\beta}=\frac{\sin(\alpha+\beta)}{\sin\beta
}=2\cos\beta+\cos\alpha.$$

**Step 3: Direction of $\overrightarrow{JA}$.** The angle bisector from $A$ meet
s $BC$ at $D$ with $BD=\frac{ac}{b+c}$. Computing $D_x=-(s-b)+\frac{ac}{b+c}=-\f
rac{b(t-1)^2}{2(1+t)}$. Since $A,J,D$ are collinear (in that order), the directi
on from $J$ to $A$ is:
$$\overrightarrow{JA}\propto\left(\frac{b(t-1)^2}{2(1+t)},\; r\right), \quad r=\
frac{b(t-1)}{2}\,u.$$

The angle of $\overrightarrow{JA}$ from the positive $x$-axis is $\theta_A=\arct
an\!\left(\frac{u(1+t)}{t-1}\right)$.

**Step 4: Direction of $\overrightarrow{JM}$.** Since $\overrightarrow{JM}=\left
(\frac{b(1-t)}{2},\,-r\right)$ (both components negative), its angle from the po
sitive $x$-axis is $\theta_M=\pi+\arctan(u)=\pi+\alpha/2$.

**Step 5: Computing $\angle AJM$.** We have $\angle AJM=\theta_M-\theta_A$. Usin
g $\arctan A-\arctan B=\arctan\frac{A-B}{1+AB}$ (valid since $1+AB>0$):

$$\angle AJM=\pi+\arctan(u)-\arctang\!\left(\frac{u(1+t)}{t-1}\right)=\pi-\arcta
n\!\left(\frac{2u}{t-1+u^2(1+t)}\right).$$

**Step 6: Key simplification.** Express $t$ in terms of $u$: since $\sin\alpha=\
frac{2u}{1+u^2}$, $\cos\alpha=\frac{1-u^2}{1+u^2}$, and $\cos\beta=\frac{\sqrt{u
^4+u^2+1}}{1+u^2}$, we get:
$$t=\frac{2\sqrt{u^4+u^2+1}+1-u^2}{1+u^2}.$$

Setting $w=\sqrt{u^4+u^2+1}$, a direct computation gives:
$$t-1=\frac{2(w-u^2)}{1+u^2},\quad 1+t=\frac{2(1+w)}{1+u^2},$$
$$t-1+u^2(1+t)=\frac{2(w-u^2)+2u^2(1+w)}{1+u^2}=\frac{2w(1+u^2)}{1+u^2}=2w.$$

Therefore:
$$\boxed{\angle AJM=\pi-\arctan\!\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt{u^4+u^2+1}}\right).}$$

**Step 7: Optimization.** To minimize $\angle AJM$, we maximize $g(u)=\arctan\!\
left(\frac{u}{\sqrt{u^4+u^2+1}}\right)$. Since $\arctan$ is increasing, it suffi
ces to maximize $h(u)=\frac{u}{\sqrt{u^4+u^2+1}}$, equivalently $h(u)^2=\frac{u^
2}{u^4+u^2+1}$. Setting $v=u^2>0$ and differentiating $\frac{v}{v^2+v+1}$:

$$\frac{d}{dv}\frac{v}{v^2+v+1}=\frac{1-v^2}{(v^2+v+1)^2},$$

which vanishes at $v=1$ (i.e., $u=1$, $\alpha=\pi/2$), changes sign from $+$ to
$-$, confirming a maximum. At $u=1$: $h=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, so $g=\arctan\frac{
1}{\sqrt{3}}=\frac{\pi}{6}$.

**Step 8: Verification.** At $\alpha=\pi/2$, $a=2b$: $\sin\beta=\frac{1}{2}$ giv
es $\beta=\pi/6$, $\gamma=\pi/3$, and $t=\sqrt{3}\in(1,3)$. The triangle inequal
ities are satisfied ($2b < b+\sqrt{3}b$, etc.), so this is a valid $30$-$60$-$90
$ triangle.

**Conclusion.** The smallest possible value of $\angle AJM$ is:

$$\pi-\frac{\pi}{6}=\boxed{\dfrac{5\pi}{6}}.$$
