## Solution

We solve the integral equation
$$g(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{t} e^{u-t} g(u)\,du + f'(t)$$
by recognizing the integral term as a convolution and applying the Fourier trans
form.

---

### Step 1. Rewrite the integral as a convolution

Set $v = t - u$ (so $u = t - v$, $du = -dv$). The limits $u \in (-\infty, t]$ be
come $v \in [\infty, 0)$, hence
$$\int_{-\infty}^{t} e^{u-t} g(u)\,du = \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-v} g(t - v)\,dv =
(k * g)(t),$$
where the kernel is $k(v) = e^{-v}\mathbf{1}_{v \ge 0}$.

The equation becomes
$$g = k * g + f'.$$

### Step 2. Apply the Fourier transform

Using the convention $\hat{\phi}(\xi) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \phi(t)\,e^{-i\x
i t}\,dt$, the convolution turns into a product:
$$\hat{g}(\xi) = \hat{k}(\xi)\,\hat{g}(\xi) + \widehat{f'}(\xi).$$

Compute each factor:
- $\hat{k}(\xi) = \displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-t}e^{-i\xi t}\,dt = \int_{0
}^{\infty} e^{-(1+i\xi)t}\,dt = \frac{1}{1+i\xi}$.
- $\widehat{f'}(\xi) = i\xi\,\hat{f}(\xi)$.

Substituting:
$$\hat{g}(\xi) = \frac{1}{1+i\xi}\,\hat{g}(\xi) + i\xi\,\hat{f}(\xi).$$

### Step 3. Solve for $\hat{g}$

Rearrange:
$$\hat{g}(\xi)\left(1 - \frac{1}{1+i\xi}\right) = i\xi\,\hat{f}(\xi).$$

Since
$$1 - \frac{1}{1+i\xi} = \frac{i\xi}{1+i\xi},$$
we get
$$\hat{g}(\xi)\cdot\frac{i\xi}{1+i\xi} = i\xi\,\hat{f}(\xi).$$

For $\xi \neq 0$, divide both sides by $i\xi$:
$$\frac{\hat{g}(\xi)}{1+i\xi} = \hat{f}(\xi)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \hat{g}(\x
i) = (1+i\xi)\,\hat{f}(\xi) = \hat{f}(\xi) + i\xi\,\hat{f}(\xi) = \hat{f}(\xi) +
 \widehat{f'}(\xi).$$

At $\xi = 0$ the equation reduces to $0 = 0$, so it imposes no constraint; the v
alue $\hat{g}(0) = \hat{f}(0) + \widehat{f'}(0) = \hat{f}(0)$ is consistent. Inv
erting the Fourier transform:
$$\boxed{\,g(t) = f(t) + f'(t)\,}.$$

Since $f \in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$ and $f' \in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$ are both given, $
g = f + f' \in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$, as required.

### Step 4. Direct verification

Substitute $g = f + f'$ back into the equation. The integral term splits:
$$\int_{-\infty}^{t} e^{u-t}\bigl(f(u)+f'(u)\bigr)\,du = \underbrace{\int_{-\inf
ty}^{t} e^{u-t}f(u)\,du}_{A} + \underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{t} e^{u-t}f'(u)\,du}_
{B}.$$

Integrate $B$ by parts with $U = e^{u-t}$, $dV = f'(u)\,du$:
$$B = \Bigl[e^{u-t}f(u)\Bigr]_{-\infty}^{t} - \int_{-\infty}^{t} e^{u-t}f(u)\,du
 = f(t) - A.$$

(The boundary term at $u = t$ gives $e^{0}f(t) = f(t)$; at $u \to -\infty$ it va
nishes because $e^{u-t}\to 0$ exponentially while $f$ is bounded — which follows
 from $f' \in L^{1}$ implying $f$ is Lipschitz/bounded, combined with $f \in L^{
1}$.)

Therefore
$$A + B = A + (f(t) - A) = f(t),$$
and the right-hand side of the original equation is
$$f(t) + f'(t) = g(t). \quad\checkmark$$

The function $g(t) = f(t) + f'(t)$ satisfies the integral equation and belongs t
o $L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$.
