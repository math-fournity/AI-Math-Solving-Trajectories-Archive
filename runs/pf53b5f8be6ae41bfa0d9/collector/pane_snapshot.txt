## Solution

We evaluate
$$I = \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\sin^3 x}{x}\,dx.$$

**Step 1: Linearize $\sin^3 x$.**

Using the standard trigonometric identity
$$\sin^3 x = \frac{3\sin x - \sin 3x}{4},$$
we obtain
$$I = \frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{3\sin x - \sin 3x}{x}\,dx = \frac{1}{4}
\left(3\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx - \int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin 3x}{x
}\,dx\right).$$

**Step 2: Use the Dirichlet integral.**

The classical Dirichlet integral gives, for every $a > 0$,
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(ax)}{x}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{2}.$$
This follows from the substitution $u = ax$ (which leaves the integral invariant
 in form) together with the well-known value $\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin u}{u}\
,du = \frac{\pi}{2}$, itself obtainable, for instance, by introducing a damping
factor $e^{-tx}$ and differentiating under the integral sign:
$$F(t) = \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-tx}\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx,\qquad F'(t) = -\int_{0}^
{\infty} e^{-tx}\sin x\,dx = -\frac{1}{1+t^2},$$
so $F(t) = \frac{\pi}{2} - \arctan t$, and taking $t \to 0^{+}$ yields $F(0) = \
frac{\pi}{2}$.

Applying this with $a = 1$ and $a = 3$:
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{2}, \qquad \int_{0}^{\infty
}\frac{\sin 3x}{x}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{2}.$$

(The second equality is confirmed directly by the substitution $u = 3x$: $\int_{
0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin 3x}{x}dx = \int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin u}{u}du = \frac{\pi
}{2}$.)

**Step 3: Combine.**

Substituting back,
$$I = \frac{1}{4}\left(3\cdot\frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{\pi}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{4}\
left(\frac{3\pi - \pi}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{4}\cdot\pi = \frac{\pi}{4}.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin^3 x}{x}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{4}.}$$
