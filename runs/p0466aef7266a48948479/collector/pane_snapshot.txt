## Solution

**Setup.** We use Stokes' theorem to convert the surface integral into a line in
tegral over the boundary curve.

The surface $S$ is the upper half of the ellipsoid $\frac{x^2}{4}+\frac{y^2}{9}+
z^2=1$, so its boundary $C$ is the ellipse in the plane $z=0$:
$$\frac{x^2}{4}+\frac{y^2}{9}=1.$$

**Stokes' theorem.** With the outward (upward) normal on $S$, the induced orient
ation on $C$ is counterclockwise when viewed from above, and
$$\iint_S \operatorname{curl}\mathbf{F}\cdot \mathbf{n}\,dS = \oint_C \mathbf{F}
\cdot d\mathbf{r}.$$

**Parameterize the boundary.** Let
$$x=2\cos t,\quad y=3\sin t,\quad z=0,\qquad 0\le t\le 2\pi.$$
Then $dx=-2\sin t\,dt$, $dy=3\cos t\,dt$, $dz=0$.

**Evaluate $\mathbf{F}$ on $C$.** Since $\mathbf{F}=\langle xy,\, yz,\, zx\rangl
e$ and $z=0$ on $C$:
$$\mathbf{F}\big|_C = \langle xy,\, 0,\, 0\rangle = \langle 6\cos t\sin t,\, 0,\
, 0\rangle.$$

**Compute the line integral.**
$$\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = (6\cos t\sin t)(-2\sin t\,dt) + 0 + 0 = -12\cos
t\,\sin^2 t\,dt.$$

Therefore
$$\oint_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = -12\int_0^{2\pi}\cos t\,\sin^2 t\,dt.$$

Substituting $u=\sin t$, $du=\cos t\,dt$: as $t$ runs from $0$ to $2\pi$, $u=\si
n t$ returns to $0$, so
$$\int_0^{2\pi}\cos t\,\sin^2 t\,dt = \int_0^{0} u^2\,du = 0.$$

(Equivalently, the integrand is odd about $t=\pi$.)

**Result.**
$$\iint_S \operatorname{curl}\mathbf{F}\cdot \mathbf{n}\,dS = 0.$$
