## Proof

**Governing problem.**
$$u_{tt}=a^{2}\!\left(u_{rr}+\frac1r u_r\right),\quad 0<r<r_0,\ t>0,$$
$$u(r_0,t)=0,\qquad u(r,0)=\phi(r),\quad u_t(r,0)=\psi(r).$$
We also require $u$ finite at $r=0$ (regularity at the origin).

### 1. Separation of variables

Set $u(r,t)=R(r)T(t)$. Substitution gives
$$R\,T''=a^{2}\!\left(R''+\frac1r R'\right)T
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\frac{T''}{a^{2}T}=\frac{R''+\frac1r R'}{R}=-\lambda,$$
with a separation constant $-\lambda$ (the sign is chosen so that $T$ is oscilla
tory, consistent with a wave equation). This yields the two ODEs

$$\boxed{R''+\frac1r R'+\lambda R=0,\quad R(r_0)=0,\ R\text{ finite at }0}\tag{1
}$$
$$\boxed{T''+a^{2}\lambda T=0.}\tag{2}$$

### 2. The radial (Sturm–Liouville) problem

Write (1) in self-adjoint form: $(rR')'+\lambda rR=0$ on $(0,r_0)$, with weight
$w(r)=r$.

- **$\lambda=0$:** $R=C_1\ln r+C_2$. Finiteness at $0$ forces $C_1=0$; then $R(r
_0)=0$ forces $C_2=0$. Trivial.
- **$\lambda<0$:** writing $\lambda=-\nu^{2}$ gives modified Bessel functions $I
_0,K_0$; finiteness at $0$ kills $K_0$, and $I_0(\nu r_0)=0$ has no positive sol
ution since $I_0>0$. Trivial.
- **$\lambda>0$:** set $\mu=\sqrt{\lambda}\,r$. Equation (1) becomes Bessel's eq
uation of order $0$,
  $$\mu^{2}R_{\mu\mu}+\mu R_\mu+\mu^{2}R=0,$$
  with general solution $R=C_1 J_0(\sqrt{\lambda}\,r)+C_2 Y_0(\sqrt{\lambda}\,r)
$. Since $Y_0(r)\to-\infty$ as $r\to0^{+}$, regularity at the origin forces $C_2
=0$. Thus $R(r)=J_0(\sqrt{\lambda}\,r)$.

The boundary condition $R(r_0)=0$ requires
$$J_0(\sqrt{\lambda}\,r_0)=0\;\Longrightarrow\;\sqrt{\lambda}\,r_0=\mu_n,$$
where $\mu_1<\mu_2<\cdots$ are the positive zeros of $J_0$. Hence the eigenvalue
s and eigenfunctions are
$$\lambda_n=\left(\frac{\mu_n}{r_0}\right)^{\!2},\qquad R_n(r)=J_0\!\left(\frac{
\mu_n r}{r_0}\right),\qquad n=1,2,\dots$$

### 3. Orthogonality of the eigenfunctions

For $R_n,R_m$ satisfying $(rR_n')'+\lambda_n rR_n=0$ and $(rR_m')'+\lambda_m rR_
m=0$, multiply the first by $R_m$, the second by $R_n$, subtract, and integrate:
$$\int_0^{r_0}\!\bigl[R_m(rR_n')'-R_n(rR_m')'\bigr]dr+(\lambda_n-\lambda_m)\int_
0^{r_0}rR_nR_m\,dr=0.$$
The first integral equals $\bigl[r(R_mR_n'-R_nR_m')\bigr]_0^{r_0}$. At $r=r_0$ i
t vanishes because $R_n(r_0)=R_m(r_0)=0$; at $r=0$ it vanishes because $R_n,R_m$
 are bounded and the prefactor $r\to0$. Therefore
$$(\lambda_n-\lambda_m)\int_0^{r_0}rR_nR_m\,dr=0,$$
so the family $\{R_n\}$ is orthogonal on $[0,r_0]$ with weight $r$.

For the norm, set $x=\mu_n r/r_0$:
$$\int_0^{r_0}r\,J_0^2\!\left(\frac{\mu_n r}{r_0}\right)dr
=\frac{r_0^2}{\mu_n^2}\int_0^{\mu_n}x\,J_0^2(x)\,dx.$$
Using the standard identity $\displaystyle\int_0^{\mu}xJ_\nu^2(x)\,dx=\frac{\mu^
2}{2}\bigl[J_\nu^2(\mu)-J_{\nu-1}(\mu)J_{\nu+1}(\mu)\bigr]$ with $\nu=0$ and $J_
{-1}=-J_1$,
$$\int_0^{\mu_n}xJ_0^2(x)\,dx=\frac{\mu_n^2}{2}\bigl[J_0^2(\mu_n)+J_1^2(\mu_n)\b
igr]=\frac{\mu_n^2}{2}J_1^2(\mu_n),$$
since $J_0(\mu_n)=0$. Hence
$$\boxed{\int_0^{r_0}r\,J_0\!\left(\frac{\mu_n r}{r_0}\right)J_0\!\left(\frac{\m
u_m r}{r_0}\right)dr=\frac{r_0^2}{2}J_1^2(\mu_n)\,\delta_{nm}.}\tag{3}$$

### 4. Time equation

For each $\lambda_n=(\mu_n/r_0)^2$, equation (2) gives
$$T_n(t)=A_n\cos\!\left(\frac{a\mu_n}{r_0}t\right)+B_n\sin\!\left(\frac{a\mu_n}{
r_0}t\right).$$

### 5. Superposition

By linearity and completeness of $\{R_n\}$ in $L^2_r(0,r_0)$,
$$u(r,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[A_n\cos\!\left(\frac{a\mu_n}{r_0}t\right)+B_n\
sin\!\left(\frac{a\mu_n}{r_0}t\right)\right]J_0\!\left(\frac{\mu_n r}{r_0}\right
).\tag{4}$$

### 6. Determining $A_n$, $B_n$ from initial data

**From $u(r,0)=\phi(r)$:** setting $t=0$ in (4),
$$\phi(r)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_n J_0\!\left(\frac{\mu_n r}{r_0}\right).$$
This is a Fourier–Bessel series. Multiplying by $rJ_0(\mu_m r/r_0)$, integrating
 over $[0,r_0]$, and using (3):
$$\int_0^{r_0}r\,\phi(r)J_0\!\left(\frac{\mu_m r}{r_0}\right)dr=A_m\,\frac{r_0^2
}{2}J_1^2(\mu_m),$$
so
$$\boxed{A_n=\frac{2}{r_0^2 J_1^2(\mu_n)}\int_0^{r_0}r\,\phi(r)J_0\!\left(\frac{
\mu_n r}{r_0}\right)dr.}\tag{5}$$

**From $u_t(r,0)=\psi(r)$:** differentiating (4) in $t$ and setting $t=0$,
$$\psi(r)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}B_n\frac{a\mu_n}{r_0}J_0\!\left(\frac{\mu_n r}{r_0}
\right).$$
Expanding $\psi$ in the same orthogonal basis and using (3),
$$\int_0^{r_0}r\,\psi(r)J_0\!\left(\frac{\mu_n r}{r_0}\right)dr=B_n\frac{a\mu_n}
{r_0}\cdot\frac{r_0^2}{2}J_1^2(\mu_n)=B_n\frac{a\mu_n r_0}{2}J_1^2(\mu_n),$$
giving
$$\boxed{B_n=\frac{2}{a\,\mu_n\,r_0\,J_1^2(\mu_n)}\int_0^{r_0}r\,\psi(r)J_0\!\le
ft(\frac{\mu_n r}{r_0}\right)dr.}\tag{6}$$

### 7. Verification

Each term of (4) solves the PDE: $J_0(\mu_n r/r_0)$ satisfies the radial equatio
n with eigenvalue $\lambda_n$ (Step 2), and the trigonometric factor satisfies $
T''=-a^2\lambda_n T$ (Step 4), so $u_{tt}=a^2(u_{rr}+\frac1r u_r)$ termwise. The
 boundary condition $u(r_0,t)=0$ holds because every $J_0(\mu_n)=0$. The initial
 conditions hold by construction of $A_n,B_n$ via (5),(6) and the orthogonality
(3). Regularity at $r=0$ holds since each $J_0$ is finite there. Convergence of
the series for sufficiently smooth $\phi,\psi$ follows from standard Sturm–Liouv
ille/Fourier–Bessel theory.

---

Formulas (4), (5), (6) are precisely the stated solution, with $\mu_n$ the posit
ive roots of $J_0(\mu)=0$. This completes the derivation. $\blacksquare$
