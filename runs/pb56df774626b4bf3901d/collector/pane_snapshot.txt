## Solution

**Setup.** Place $A$ at the origin. Let $S$ have vertices $A=(0,0)$, $B=(1,0)$,
$C=(1,1)$, $D=(0,1)$.

**The three squares via half-planes.** Using the rotation by $30°$ and inverting
 the coordinate maps:

$$S = \{0 \le x \le 1,\; 0 \le y \le 1\}$$

$$S^+ = \{0 \le \sqrt3\,x - y \le 2,\; 0 \le x + \sqrt3\,y \le 2\}$$

$$S^- = \{0 \le \sqrt3\,x + y \le 2,\; 0 \le -x + \sqrt3\,y \le 2\}$$

**Symmetry.** Reflection across $y=x$ fixes $S$ and swaps $S^+ \leftrightarrow S
^-$, so $|S\cap S^+|=|S\cap S^-|$.

The six edges emanating from $A$ point at angles $-30°, 0°, 30°, 60°, 90°, 120°$
 (each $30°$ apart). The wedge of each square at $A$: $S$ spans $0°$–$90°$, $S^+
$ spans $-30°$–$60°$, $S^-$ spans $30°$–$120°$.

---

**Triple intersection.** The overlap of all three wedges is $30°$–$60°$. The far
 edges that close off this region are $\sqrt3\,x+y=2$ (far edge of $S^-$) and $x
+\sqrt3\,y=2$ (far edge of $S^+$), meeting at $P=(\sqrt3-1,\;\sqrt3-1)$. The $30
°$ ray exits at $B^-=(\sqrt3/2,\,1/2)$ and the $60°$ ray exits at $D^+=(1/2,\,\s
qrt3/2)$. The other two far edges ($\sqrt3\,x-y=2$ and $-x+\sqrt3\,y=2$) lie out
side the cone for $x<\sqrt3$ and $x<1$ respectively, so they don't cut the regio
n.

Thus $S\cap S^+\cap S^-$ is the quadrilateral $A,\;B^-,\;P,\;D^+$. By the **shoe
lace formula**:

$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}\Big[(\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2})(\sqrt3{-}1) + (\sqrt3{-}1)\c
dot\tfrac{\sqrt3-1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot(-(\sqrt3{-}1))\Big] = \frac{(\sqrt3-1
)^2}{2} = 2-\sqrt3.$$

**Key observation:** $S^+\cap S^-$ is convex, contains $A$, and near $A$ equals
the $30°$–$60°$ wedge. Any point outside this cone would force a segment from $A
$ through a non-wedge direction, contradicting the local structure. So $S^+\cap
S^-$ lies in the cone $30°$–$60°$, bounded by the same two far edges. Hence

$$S^+\cap S^- = S\cap S^+\cap S^- \subseteq S, \qquad |S^+\cap S^-| = 2-\sqrt3.$
$

(All four vertices lie in $[0,1]^2$, confirming the inclusion.)

---

**Pairwise intersection $|S\cap S^+|$.** In $S$ we have $0\le y$, and for $x\in[
0,1]$ the lower bounds $-x/\sqrt3$ and $\sqrt3\,x-2$ are both $\le 0$, so the up
per bound is $\min(\sqrt3\,x,\;(2-x)/\sqrt3,\;1)$. Comparing these: $\sqrt3\,x =
 (2-x)/\sqrt3$ at $x=\tfrac12$; both are $\le 1$ for $x\le \tfrac12$ and $x\ge 2
-\sqrt3$ respectively. One checks:

$$|S\cap S^+| = \int_0^{1/2}\!\sqrt3\,x\,dx + \int_{1/2}^{1}\!\frac{2-x}{\sqrt3}
\,dx = \frac{\sqrt3}{8} + \frac{5}{8\sqrt3} = \frac{\sqrt3}{8}+\frac{5\sqrt3}{24
} = \frac{\sqrt3}{3}.$$

By symmetry $|S\cap S^-| = \sqrt3/3$.

---

**Inclusion–exclusion for "exactly two."** Let $b$ = area covered by exactly two
 squares, $c$ = area covered by all three. Then

$$b = \bigl(|S\cap S^+|+|S\cap S^-|+|S^+\cap S^-|\bigr) - 3c.$$

Substituting:

$$b = \left(\frac{\sqrt3}{3}+\frac{\sqrt3}{3}+(2-\sqrt3)\right) - 3(2-\sqrt3) =
\left(2-\frac{\sqrt3}{3}\right) - (6-3\sqrt3) = -4 + \frac{8\sqrt3}{3}.$$

**Numerical check:** $b \approx 4.619 - 4 = 0.619 > 0$; exactly-one $\approx 0.9
59$; exactly-three $\approx 0.268$; total $\approx 1.845$. All non-negative and
consistent.
