## Proof

**Setup.** Let $f: O(n) \to O(n)$ be defined by $f(M) = M^3$. We compute the tan
gent map $df_I: T_I O(n) \to T_I O(n)$.

**Tangent space at the identity.** The orthogonal group $O(n) = \{M \in \mathrm{
GL}_n(\mathbb{R}) : M^T M = I\}$ is a smooth submanifold of $M_n(\mathbb{R})$. I
ts tangent space at the identity is the space of skew-symmetric matrices:
$$T_I O(n) = \{A \in M_n(\mathbb{R}) : A + A^T = 0\}.$$
This is obtained by differentiating the defining relation: take a smooth curve $
\gamma(t)$ in $O(n)$ with $\gamma(0)=I$, differentiate $\gamma(t)^T \gamma(t) =
I$ at $t=0$, giving $\gamma'(0)^T + \gamma'(0) = 0$.

**$f$ is well-defined on $O(n)$.** For $M \in O(n)$, $(M^3)^T M^3 = (M^T)^3 M^3
= (M^T M)^3 = I$, so $M^3 \in O(n)$.

**Computing the tangent map.** Let $A \in T_I O(n)$ be arbitrary, and let $\gamm
a: (-\varepsilon, \varepsilon) \to O(n)$ be any smooth curve with $\gamma(0) = I
$ and $\gamma'(0) = A$. By the chain rule (equivalently, the product rule for ma
trix-valued functions),
$$df_I(A) = \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0} f(\gamma(t)) = \left.\frac{d}{dt}\ri
ght|_{t=0} \gamma(t)^3.$$
Applying the product rule to $\gamma(t)\cdot\gamma(t)\cdot\gamma(t)$:
$$\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0} \gamma(t)^3 = \gamma'(0)\,\gamma(0)^2 + \gamma
(0)\,\gamma'(0)\,\gamma(0) + \gamma(0)^2\,\gamma'(0).$$
Using $\gamma(0) = I$:
$$df_I(A) = A\cdot I + I\cdot A\cdot I + I\cdot A = A + A + A = 3A.$$

**The image lies in $T_I O(n)$.** Since $A$ is skew-symmetric, $(3A)^T + 3A = 3(
A^T + A) = 0$, so $3A \in T_I O(n)$, as required for the tangent map to be well-
defined as a map $T_I O(n) \to T_I O(n)$.

**Conclusion.** The tangent map of $f(M) = M^3$ at the identity is scalar multip
lication by $3$ on the tangent space:
$$\boxed{df_I(A) = 3A, \quad \text{for all } A \in T_I O(n).}$$
Equivalently, $df_I = 3\,\mathrm{id}_{T_I O(n)}$.
