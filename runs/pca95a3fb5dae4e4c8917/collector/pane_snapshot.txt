## Solution

**Contour:** $|z - i| = 1$ is the circle centered at $z_0 = i$ with radius $R =
1$.

**Step 1: Find the singularities.**

The integrand $f(z) = \frac{z^3}{e^z + i}$ has singularities where the denominat
or vanishes:

$$e^z + i = 0 \implies e^z = -i.$$

Since $-i = e^{i(-\pi/2 + 2\pi k)}$ for every integer $k$, the solutions are

$$z_k = i\!\left(2\pi k - \frac{\pi}{2}\right), \qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

These are all purely imaginary points on the imaginary axis, with imaginary part
s $y_k = 2\pi k - \frac{\pi}{2}$.

**Step 2: Determine which singularities lie inside $|z - i| = 1$.**

The distance from each singularity $z_k$ to the center $i$ is

$$|z_k - i| = \left|\,i\!\left(2\pi k - \tfrac{\pi}{2}\right) - i\,\right| = \le
ft|\,2\pi k - \tfrac{\pi}{2} - 1\,\right|.$$

Evaluating for successive integers $k$:

| $k$ | $2\pi k - \frac{\pi}{2} - 1$ | $\|z_k - i\|$ | Inside? |
|---|---|---|---|
| $0$ | $-\frac{\pi}{2} - 1 \approx -2.571$ | $\approx 2.571$ | No |
| $1$ | $\frac{3\pi}{2} - 1 \approx 3.712$ | $\approx 3.712$ | No |
| $-1$ | $-\frac{5\pi}{2} - 1 \approx -8.854$ | $\approx 8.854$ | No |

For $|k| \geq 1$ the distance only grows, and for $k = 0$ it is already $\frac{\
pi}{2} + 1 > 1$. The function $g(k) = |2\pi k - \frac{\pi}{2} - 1|$ is minimized
 over integers at $k = 0$, giving $\frac{\pi}{2} + 1 \approx 2.571 > 1$.

**No singularity $z_k$ lies inside the disk $|z - i| \leq 1$.**

**Step 3: Apply Cauchy's Integral Theorem.**

Since $e^z + i \neq 0$ for all $z$ in the closed disk $\overline{D} = \{z : |z -
 i| \leq 1\}$ (the nearest zero is at distance $\frac{\pi}{2} + 1 > 1$), the int
egrand $f(z) = \frac{z^3}{e^z + i}$ is holomorphic on an open set containing $\o
verline{D}$. The numerator $z^3$ is entire and the denominator is nonvanishing a
nd analytic on $\overline{D}$, so $f$ is analytic there.

By **Cauchy's Integral Theorem**, the integral of a function holomorphic on and
inside a simple closed contour vanishes:

$$\oint_{|z-i|=1} \frac{z^3}{e^z + i}\, dz = 0.$$
