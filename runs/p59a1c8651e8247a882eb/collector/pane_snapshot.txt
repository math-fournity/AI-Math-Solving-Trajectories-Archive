## Setup and Win-Count Formulas

Let $M$ be the set of malware (saboteur) servers with $|M| = m$, and $N$ the non
-malware servers with $|N| = 100 - m$. Servers have distinct speeds; rank them $
S_1 \succ S_2 \succ \cdots \succ S_{100}$ (fastest to slowest).

**Win count of a malware server** ranked $k$-th fastest *within* $M$ ($k=1$ is f
astest in $M$):
- Beats all $100-m$ non-malware servers (rule 1: malware dominates non-malware r
egardless of speed).
- Beats the $m - k$ malware servers slower than it (rule 2: both malware → faste
r wins).
- Total: $(100 - m) + (m - k) = 100 - k$.

So malware win counts are $\{99, 98, 97, \ldots, 100-m\}$ (for $k = 1, \ldots, m
$).

**Win count of a non-malware server** ranked $j$-th fastest *within* $N$ ($j=1$
is fastest in $N$):
- Loses to all $m$ malware servers (rule 1).
- Beats the $100 - m - j$ non-malware servers slower than it (rule 2: neither ma
lware → faster wins).
- Total: $100 - m - j$.

So non-malware win counts are $\{99-m, 98-m, \ldots, 0\}$ (for $j = 1, \ldots, 1
00-m$).

**Key gap:** Every malware server has win count $\geq 100 - m$, while every non-
malware server has win count $\leq 99 - m$. Thus the top $m$ servers by win coun
t are exactly the malware servers.

## The Condition

Let $T = \max\bigl(w(S_1), w(S_2), w(S_3)\bigr)$ where $S_1, S_2, S_3$ are the t
hree fastest servers overall. The audit requires **exactly three** servers with
win count strictly greater than $T$.

The descending win counts are: $99, 98, 97, 96, 95, \ldots$ (malware servers fir
st, then non-malware). For exactly three servers to exceed $T$, we need the 3rd-
highest count ($97$) to exceed $T$, and the 4th-highest ($96$) to not exceed $T$

$$96 \leq T < 97 \implies T = 96.$$
