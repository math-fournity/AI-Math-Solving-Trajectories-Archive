## Proof

We claim that $T_t \to 0$ in the **weak operator topology** (WOT) on $L^2(\mathb
b{R})$, and that no convergence occurs in the strong operator topology (SOT) or
the operator norm topology.

Recall $T_t\varphi(x)=\varphi(x+t)$ is a unitary operator on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$.

### Step 1. WOT convergence to 0

We must show that for every $f,g\in L^2(\mathbb{R})$,
$$
\langle T_t f, g\rangle = \int_{\mathbb{R}} f(x+t)\,\overline{g(x)}\,dx \xrighta
rrow[t\to\infty]{} 0.
$$

Let $C_c(\mathbb{R})$ denote the compactly supported continuous (or simply $L^2$
) functions, which are dense in $L^2(\mathbb{R})$. Given $\varepsilon>0$, pick $
f_0,g_0\in C_c(\mathbb{R})$ with $\|f-f_0\|_2<\varepsilon$, $\|g-g_0\|_2<\vareps
ilon$. Then
$$
|\langle T_t f,g\rangle| \le |\langle T_t f_0,g_0\rangle| + |\langle T_t(f-f_0),
g_0\rangle| + |\langle T_t f, g-g_0\rangle|.
$$
Since $T_t$ is unitary, the last two terms are bounded by $\|f-f_0\|_2\|g_0\|_2$
 and $\|f\|_2\|g-g_0\|_2$, both $O(\varepsilon)$.

For the first term: $f_0$ is supported in some $[-R,R]$ and $g_0$ in some $[-S,S
]$. Then $f_0(x+t)$ is supported in $[-R-t,R-t]$, which for $t>R+S$ is disjoint
from $[-S,S]$. Hence $\langle T_t f_0,g_0\rangle=0$ for all large $t$. Thus $\li
msup_{t\to\infty}|\langle T_t f,g\rangle|\le C\varepsilon$, and since $\varepsil
on$ is arbitrary, the limit is $0$.

Therefore $T_t\to 0$ in WOT.

### Step 2. No convergence in SOT or norm topology

Suppose $T_t\to T$ in SOT for some bounded operator $T$. Then $\|T_t f - Tf\|_2\
to 0$ for every $f\in L^2$. SOT convergence implies WOT convergence, so by Step
1, $T=0$. But $\|T_t f\|_2=\|f\|_2$ (unitarity), so SOT convergence to $0$ would
 require $\|f\|_2=0$ for all $f$, a contradiction. Hence there is no SOT limit.

Likewise, $\|T_t - T_s\|=\|T_{t-s}-I\|=2$ for $t\ne s$ (translation by a nonzero
 amount differs from the identity in norm by $2$, e.g. tested on a function supp
orted far from its translate), so $\{T_t\}$ is not Cauchy in operator norm; no n
orm convergence.

### Conclusion

The translation operators $T_t$ converge, as $t\to\infty$, to the **zero operato
r** $T=0$, and this convergence holds in the **weak operator topology** (and fai
ls in the strong operator topology and in the operator norm topology).
