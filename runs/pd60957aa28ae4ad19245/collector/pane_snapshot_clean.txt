## Evaluation of $\displaystyle I = \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\ln x}{1+x^2}\,dx$

**Key idea.** The integrand has a symmetry under the inversion $x \mapsto 1/x$,
which the substitution below exploits.

**Substitution.** Let $x = \dfrac{1}{t}$, so that $dx = -\dfrac{1}{t^2}\,dt$. Th
e limits transform as
$$x = 0^+ \Rightarrow t = +\infty, \qquad x = +\infty \Rightarrow t = 0^+.$$

Also $\ln x = \ln(1/t) = -\ln t$ and $1 + x^2 = 1 + \dfrac{1}{t^2} = \dfrac{1+t^
2}{t^2}$.

**Transforming the integral.**
$$
I = \int_{\infty}^{0} \frac{-\ln t}{\,\dfrac{1+t^2}{t^2}\,}\left(-\frac{1}{t^2}\
,dt\right)
  = \int_{\infty}^{0} \frac{(-\ln t)\,t^2}{1+t^2}\cdot\left(-\frac{1}{t^2}\right
)dt
  = \int_{\infty}^{0} \frac{\ln t}{1+t^2}\,dt
  = -\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\ln t}{1+t^2}\,dt.
$$

Since the variable of integration is a dummy variable, the last integral is exac
tly $I$:
$$
I = -I.
$$

**Convergence justification (so the manipulation is valid).** Near $x = 0^+$, $\
ln x$ is integrable against the bounded factor $\frac{1}{1+x^2}$ since $\int_0^1
 |\ln x|\,dx = 1 < \infty$. Near $x = +\infty$, $\dfrac{\ln x}{1+x^2} \sim \dfra
c{\ln x}{x^2}$, and $\int_1^\infty \frac{\ln x}{x^2}\,dx = 1 < \infty$. Hence $I
$ converges (absolutely, in fact, since $\int_0^\infty \frac{|\ln x|}{1+x^2}\,dx
 = 2\int_1^\infty \frac{\ln x}{1+x^2}\,dx < \infty$), so the substitution is leg
itimate.

**Conclusion.** From $I = -I$ we obtain $2I = 0$, and therefore
$$
\boxed{\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\ln x}{1+x^2}\,dx = 0.}
$$
