## Proof

We compute $\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_E \sin^n(x)\,dx$ where $E$ is a Lebesgue measu
rable set of finite measure.

**Step 1: Pointwise limit.** Consider the behavior of $\sin^n(x)$ as $n\to\infty
$.

- If $|\sin x| < 1$, then $|\sin x|^n \to 0$, so $\sin^n(x) \to 0$.
- If $|\sin x| = 1$, i.e., $x = \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$ for some $k \in \mathbb{Z}
$, then $\sin^n(x) = (\pm 1)^n$, which oscillates and does not converge in gener
al.

Let $A = \{x \in \mathbb{R} : |\sin x| = 1\} = \{\frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi : k \in \m
athbb{Z}\}$. This is a countable set, hence $m(A) = 0$.

Therefore $\sin^n(x) \to 0$ for almost every $x \in \mathbb{R}$ (in particular,
for a.e. $x \in E$).

**Step 2: Dominating function.** For all $n$ and all $x$, $|\sin^n(x)| \le 1$. S
ince $m(E) < \infty$, the constant function $g(x) = 1$ is integrable over $E$, i
.e., $g \in L^1(E)$, with $g \ge |\sin^n(x)|$ pointwise.

**Step 3: Apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem.** We have:
- $\sin^n(x) \to 0$ a.e. on $E$,
- $|\sin^n(x)| \le 1 \in L^1(E)$ (since $m(E) < \infty$).

By the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem,

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_E \sin^n(x)\,dx = \int_E \lim_{n\to\infty}\sin^n(x)\,dx
= \int_E 0\,dx = 0.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_E \sin^n(x)\,dx = 0.}$$
