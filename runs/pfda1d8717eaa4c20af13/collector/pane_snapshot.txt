## Proof

**Claim.** There exists a continuous map $h: X \to \mathbb{R}$ with $p \circ h =
 f$ if and only if the induced homomorphism

$$f_* : \pi_1(X) \to \pi_1(S^1)$$

is the **trivial** homomorphism, i.e. $f_*([\gamma]) = 0$ for every $[\gamma] \i
n \pi_1(X)$.

---

### Necessity ($\Rightarrow$)

Suppose a lift $h: X \to \mathbb{R}$ exists with $p \circ h = f$. Then for any l
oop $\gamma$ in $X$ based at $x_0$, the loop $f \circ \gamma$ in $S^1$ lifts to
the path $h \circ \gamma$ in $\mathbb{R}$. Since $\mathbb{R}$ is simply connecte
d, every loop in $\mathbb{R}$ is null-homotopic, so $h \circ \gamma$ is null-hom
otopic. Projecting via $p$ gives that $f \circ \gamma = p \circ (h \circ \gamma)
$ is null-homotopic in $S^1$. Hence

$$f_*([\gamma]) = [f \circ \gamma] = 0 \in \pi_1(S^1).$$

Since $[\gamma]$ was arbitrary, $f_*$ is trivial. $\square$

### Sufficiency ($\Leftarrow$)

This is the **lifting criterion** for covering spaces. We verify its hypotheses.

**Setup.** Fix a basepoint $x_0 \in X$ and choose $r_0 \in \mathbb{R}$ with $p(r
_0) = f(x_0)$. Define $h$ as follows. For any $x \in X$, choose a path $\alpha$
from $x_0$ to $x$. The path $f \circ \alpha$ in $S^1$ starting at $f(x_0)$ has a
 unique lift $\widetilde{f \circ \alpha}$ in $\mathbb{R}$ starting at $r_0$ (uni
que path lifting). Set

$$h(x) = \widetilde{f \circ \alpha}(1).$$

**Well-definedness.** Suppose $\alpha, \beta$ are two paths from $x_0$ to $x$. T
hen $\alpha \cdot \beta^{-1}$ is a loop at $x_0$, and by hypothesis

$$f_*([\alpha \cdot \beta^{-1}]) = 0 \in \pi_1(S^1),$$

so $f \circ (\alpha \cdot \beta^{-1})$ is null-homotopic in $S^1$. The lift of $
f \circ (\alpha \cdot \beta^{-1})$ starting at $r_0$ is therefore a **loop** in
$\mathbb{R}$ (a null-homotopic loop in the base lifts to a closed path). This li
ft equals $\widetilde{f \circ \alpha} \cdot \widetilde{f \circ \beta}^{-1}$, so
its being closed means

$$\widetilde{f \circ \alpha}(1) = \widetilde{f \circ \beta}(1).$$

Hence $h(x)$ is independent of the choice of path. $\square$

**Continuity.** Let $x \in X$ and let $U$ be an evenly-covered neighborhood of $
f(x)$ in $S^1$, so $p^{-1}(U) = \bigsqcup_j V_j$ with each $V_j \ni r$ mapped ho
meomorphically onto $U$ by $p$. Let $V_{j_0}$ be the sheet containing $h(x)$, an
d let $\varphi = p|_{V_{j_0}}^{-1}: U \to V_{j_0}$.

Since $f$ is continuous, $f^{-1}(U)$ is an open neighborhood of $x$. Since $X$ i
s path-connected and locally path-connected is **not** assumed, we use the follo
wing standard argument: $X$ is path-connected, and for any $y \in f^{-1}(U)$, ch
oose a path $\alpha$ from $x_0$ to $x$ and then a path $\delta$ from $x$ to $y$
lying in $f^{-1}(U)$ (this requires $X$ locally path-connected; the lifting crit
erion theorem requires $X$ path-connected and locally path-connected).

*Remark on hypotheses.* The standard lifting criterion (Hatcher, Prop. 1.33; Mun
kres, Lemma 54.1) requires $X$ to be **path-connected and locally path-connected
**. The problem states only path-connected. Under the stated hypotheses alone, t
he necessity direction holds unconditionally, and the sufficiency direction hold
s provided $X$ is also locally path-connected (which is the standard hypothesis
for the lifting criterion). With this standard assumption, the continuity argume
nt proceeds as above: for $y$ in the path-component of $f^{-1}(U)$ containing $x
$, concatenating a path to $x$ with a path in $f^{-1}(U)$ to $y$ shows $h(y) = \
varphi(f(y))$, which is continuous. Thus $h$ is continuous on a neighborhood of
$x$, hence continuous everywhere.

**Verification $p \circ h = f$.** By construction, $h(x) = \widetilde{f \circ \a
lpha}(1)$ and $p(\widetilde{f \circ \alpha}(1)) = f \circ \alpha(1) = f(x)$. $\s
quare$

---

### Conclusion

The necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a continuous lift $h
: X \to \mathbb{R}$ with $p \circ h = f$ is:

$$\boxed{f_* : \pi_1(X) \to \pi_1(S^1) \text{ is the trivial homomorphism, i.e.,
 } f_*(\pi_1(X)) = \{0\}.}$$

Equivalently, the image of the induced map on fundamental groups is trivial: eve
ry loop in $X$ maps under $f$ to a null-homotopic loop in $S^1$.

(Under the standard lifting criterion, $X$ is also assumed locally path-connecte
d; with only path-connectedness the necessity direction is unconditional while s
ufficiency requires the additional local path-connectedness hypothesis.)
