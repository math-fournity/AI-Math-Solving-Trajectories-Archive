## Proof

**Reformulation.** An embedding $\Sigma_n \hookrightarrow O(N)$ is a faithful or
thogonal representation of $\Sigma_n$ of dimension $N$. Since any real represent
ation of a finite group can be made orthogonal (by averaging any inner product o
ver the group), this is equivalent to asking for the minimal dimension $d(\Sigma
_n)$ of a faithful real representation of $\Sigma_n$. The answer is then $n_{\ma
x} = \max\{n : d(\Sigma_n) \leq N\}$.

**Claim:** $d(\Sigma_n) = n - 1$ for all $n \geq 2$.

### Upper bound: $d(\Sigma_n) \leq n - 1$

The **standard representation** of $\Sigma_n$ is the action on
$$V = \left\{(x_1, \ldots, x_n) \in \mathbb{R}^n : \sum_{i=1}^n x_i = 0\right\}$
$
by permuting coordinates. This is an $(n-1)$-dimensional real representation pre
serving the standard inner product, hence giving a homomorphism $\rho: \Sigma_n
\to O(n-1)$.

**Faithfulness:** If $\sigma \in \ker(\rho)$, then $P_\sigma v = v$ for all $v \
in V$. Since every permutation also fixes the vector $(1,1,\ldots,1) \in V^\perp
$, the matrix $P_\sigma$ fixes all of $\mathbb{R}^n$, so $P_\sigma = I$ and $\si
gma = e$. Thus $\rho$ is faithful for $n \geq 2$.

This gives $d(\Sigma_n) \leq n-1$, so $\Sigma_{N+1} \hookrightarrow O(N)$.

### Lower bound: $d(\Sigma_n) \geq n - 1$

By Maschke's theorem, every real representation of $\Sigma_n$ is a direct sum of
 irreducible representations. A representation is faithful iff the intersection
of the kernels of its irreducible summands is trivial. All irreducible represent
ations of $\Sigma_n$ are defined over $\mathbb{R}$ (in fact over $\mathbb{Q}$),
so real and complex irreducibles coincide.

We use the following **classical fact** (proved via the hook-length formula): *F
or $n \geq 7$, the only irreducible representations of $\Sigma_n$ of dimension l
ess than $n-1$ are the trivial representation (dimension 1, kernel $\Sigma_n$) a
nd the sign representation (dimension 1, kernel $A_n$).*

**Verification of the classical fact.** Irreducible representations of $\Sigma_n
$ correspond to partitions $\lambda \vdash n$, with dimension $f^\lambda = n!/\p
rod_{c \in \lambda} h(c)$. The four "small" partitions and their dimensions are:
- $(n)$: trivial, $f = 1$
- $(1^n)$: sign, $f = 1$
- $(n-1,1)$: standard, $f = n-1$
- $(2,1^{n-2})$: standard $\otimes$ sign, $f = n-1$

For any other partition, $f^\lambda \geq n-1$ when $n \geq 7$. For instance:
- Hook $(n-k, 1^k)$ with $2 \leq k \leq n-3$: $f = \binom{n-1}{k} \geq \binom{n-
1}{2} = \frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2} \geq n-1$ for $n \geq 4$.
- Non-hook partitions have even larger dimensions (e.g., $(n-2,2)$ has dimension
 $\frac{n(n-3)}{2} \geq 14 > 6$ for $n = 7$).

One checks directly that for $n \geq 7$, every non-hook partition also yields $f
^\lambda \geq n-1$.

**Consequence for $n \geq 7$:** Any faithful representation must include at leas
t one irreducible summand of dimension $\geq n-1$, because the only summands of
dimension $< n-1$ are trivial (kernel $\Sigma_n$) and sign (kernel $A_n$), and t
heir kernels intersect in $A_n \neq \{e\}$ for $n \geq 3$. Hence $d(\Sigma_n) \g
eq n-1$.

**Small cases ($n = 2, 3, 4, 5, 6$):** Direct verification using the irreducible
 character tables:
- $n=2$: $\Sigma_2 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$; $d = 1 = n-1$ (sign representation).
- $n=3$: $\Sigma_3$ is non-abelian, so no 1-dimensional representation is faithf
ul; $d = 2 = n-1$.
- $n=4$: Irrep dimensions are $1, 1, 2, 3, 3$. The 2-dimensional irrep (partitio
n $(2,2)$) has kernel $V_4$ (Klein four-group). Combined with sign (kernel $A_4$
), the intersection is $V_4 \cap A_4 = V_4 \neq \{e\}$. So $d = 3 = n-1$.
- $n=5$: Irrep dimensions are $1, 1, 4, 4, 5, 5, 6$. Smallest non-linear is $4 =
 n-1$; $d = 4$.
- $n=6$: Irrep dimensions are $1, 1, 5, 5, 5, 5, 9, 9, 10, 10, 16$. Smallest non
-linear is $5 = n-1$; $d = 5$.

In every case, $d(\Sigma_n) = n - 1$.
