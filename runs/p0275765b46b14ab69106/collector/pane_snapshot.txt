# Proof

**Question.** On a Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$ with Levi-Civita connection $\nab
la$, does $R\equiv 0$ imply that $\nabla$ is locally trivial, i.e. that the Chri
stoffel symbols vanish?

**Answer.** Yes — but in the only meaningful sense available: $R\equiv 0$ is *eq
uivalent* to the existence, around every point, of local coordinates in which th
e Christoffel symbols vanish identically. (Christoffel symbols are not tensorial
, so asking whether they vanish without specifying coordinates is ill-posed; the
 correct statement is the coordinate-existence one above.)

We prove the nontrivial direction $R\equiv 0 \Rightarrow$ locally trivial. The c
onverse is immediate and is given at the end.

---

## Setup and notation

Fix $p\in M$, $\dim M = n$. Let $U$ be a *normal neighborhood* of $p$: a geodesi
cally convex open set on which $\exp_p$ is a diffeomorphism from a star-shaped d
omain in $T_pM$ onto $U$. In particular $U$ is contractible and any two points a
re joined by a unique geodesic in $U$.

Let $\{e_i\}$ be a local orthonormal frame on $U$ with dual coframe $\{\theta^i\
}$. The connection $1$-forms $\omega^i{}_j$ are defined by
$$\nabla e_i = \omega^i{}_j \otimes e_j, \qquad \omega_{ij} := g(e_i,e_j)\text{
component} = -\omega_{ji} \text{ (metric compatibility)}.$$
The structure equations of the Levi-Civita connection (torsion-free $T=0$, curva
ture $\Omega$) are
$$d\theta^i = -\omega^i{}_j \wedge \theta^j \qquad\text{(first structure equatio
n, }T=0\text{)}, \tag{1}$$
$$d\omega^i{}_j = -\omega^i{}_k \wedge \omega^k{}_j + \Omega^i{}_j, \qquad \Omeg
a^i{}_j = \tfrac12 R^i{}_{jkl}\,\theta^k\wedge\theta^l. \tag{2}$$

---

## Step 1 — Parallel transport is path-independent on $U$ when $R=0$

Choose an orthonormal basis $\{v_i\}$ of $T_pM$. For $q\in U$, parallel-translat
e $\{v_i\}$ along the unique radial geodesic $\gamma_{pq}$ from $p$ to $q$; this
 gives a linear isometry $P_q : T_pM \to T_qM$ and a candidate frame $e_i(q) :=
P_q(v_i)$.

We claim $P_q$ agrees with parallel transport along **any** piecewise-smooth pat
h in $U$ from $p$ to $q$, so that $e_i$ is a well-defined smooth field depending
 only on the endpoint $q$.

*Justification.* Curvature is the infinitesimal generator of holonomy: parallel
transport around the boundary of an infinitesimal parallelogram spanned by $X,Y$
 is, to second order,
$$\operatorname{Id} - R(X,Y)\,ds\,dt + o(ds\,dt).$$
Concretely, by the Ambrose–Singer holonomy theorem, the Lie algebra of the holon
omy group at $p$ is spanned by the endomorphisms $P_\gamma^{-1} R_q(X,Y) P_\gamm
a$ for all $q$ and all loops $\gamma$. When $R\equiv 0$ on $U$, every such gener
ator is zero, so the holonomy group of $U$ is trivial: transport around any clos
ed loop in $U$ is the identity. Hence transport from $p$ to $q$ depends only on
the endpoints, not on the path. (Equivalently one subdivides any two paths into
a chain of small loops; each small loop contributes identity because its holonom
y is $\exp(\int R) = \operatorname{Id}$ when $R=0$.) $\square$

Thus $e_i(q) = P_q(v_i)$ is a smooth, well-defined orthonormal frame on all of $
U$.

---

## Step 2 — The frame is parallel, hence $\omega^i{}_j \equiv 0$

For any vector field $X$ and any $q\in U$, take a curve $\alpha$ through $q$ wit
h $\dot\alpha(0)=X_q$. Along $\alpha$, the fields $e_i$ are obtained by parallel
 transport (Step 1), so $\frac{D e_i}{dt}\big|_q = 0$. Therefore
$$\nabla_X e_i = 0 \quad\text{for all }X,\text{ hence } \omega^i{}_j = 0 \text{
on } U. \tag{3}$$

---

## Step 3 — The dual coframe is closed

Substituting (3) into the first structure equation (1):
$$d\theta^i = -\omega^i{}_j\wedge\theta^j = 0 \quad\text{on } U. \tag{4}$$
Each $\theta^i$ is a closed $1$-form.

---

## Step 4 — Closed $\Rightarrow$ exact on the contractible set $U$; obtain coord
inates

Since $U$ is geodesically convex, it is contractible. By the Poincaré lemma, eve
ry closed $1$-form on $U$ is exact: there exist smooth functions $x^1,\dots,x^n$
 on $U$ with
$$\theta^i = dx^i, \qquad i=1,\dots,n. \tag{5}$$
These functions form a coordinate system because
$$dx^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^n = \theta^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^n \neq 0$$
(the $\theta^i$ are linearly independent, being dual to the frame $e_i$). So $(U
, x^1,\dots,x^n)$ is a coordinate chart.

---

## Step 5 — The metric is Euclidean and the Christoffel symbols vanish

In the frame $\{e_i\}$, the metric is $g = \sum_i \theta^i\otimes\theta^i$. Usin
g (5),
$$g = \sum_{i=1}^n (dx^i)^2, \qquad g_{ij} = \delta_{ij} \text{ (constant)}.$$
The Christoffel symbols of $g$ in these coordinates are
$$\Gamma^k_{ij} = \tfrac12 g^{k\ell}\bigl(\partial_i g_{j\ell} + \partial_j g_{i
\ell} - \partial_\ell g_{ij}\bigr) = 0,$$
since every $g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}$ is constant. Equivalently and more directly: th
e coordinate vector fields satisfy $\partial/\partial x^i = e_i$ (because $dx^i(
e_j)=\delta^i_j$), and $e_i$ is parallel by Step 2, so
$$\nabla_{\partial_j}\partial_i = \nabla_{\partial_j} e_i = 0 = \Gamma^k_{ij}\,\
partial_k \;\Longrightarrow\; \Gamma^k_{ij}=0.$$

Thus on a neighborhood of every point there exist coordinates in which **all** C
hristoffel symbols vanish identically. The connection is locally trivial.

---

## Converse (completeness)

If there exist coordinates on $U$ with $\Gamma^k_{ij}\equiv 0$, then the coordin
ate expression of curvature
$$R^\ell{}_{ijk} = \partial_i\Gamma^\ell_{jk} - \partial_j\Gamma^\ell_{ik} + \Ga
mma^\ell_{im}\Gamma^m_{jk} - \Gamma^\ell_{jm}\Gamma^m_{ik}$$
gives $R\equiv 0$ on $U$. Covering $M$ by such charts gives $R\equiv 0$ on $M$.

---

## Conclusion

For the Levi-Civita connection of a Riemannian manifold,
$$\boxed{R\equiv 0 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \text{every point has a neighborhood
with coordinates in which } \Gamma^k_{ij}\equiv 0.}$$
The forward direction was proved above (parallel-transport frame $\Rightarrow$ c
losed coframe $\Rightarrow$ Euclidean coordinates $\Rightarrow$ vanishing Christ
offel symbols); the reverse direction is the immediate coordinate formula for $R
$ in terms of $\Gamma$.

So **yes**: vanishing curvature makes $\nabla$ locally trivial, in the precise s
ense that the Christoffel symbols can be made to vanish in suitable local coordi
nates. The essential mechanism is that $R=0$ kills holonomy, making parallel tra
nsport path-independent, which produces a parallel frame whose dual coframe is c
losed and hence (locally) exact — yielding flat Euclidean coordinates.
