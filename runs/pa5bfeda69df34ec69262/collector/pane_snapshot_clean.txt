## Solution

**Setup.** In a round-robin tournament with $N$ teams, the total number of games
 is $\binom{N}{2} = \frac{N(N-1)}{2}$. Each game (whether a draw or decisive) co
ntributes exactly 2 points total to the scoreboard, so the total points distribu
ted is always $N(N-1)$, regardless of the number of draws.

**Key observation.** If all $\binom{N}{2}$ games are draws, every team earns $1
\times (N-1) = N-1$ points. All teams have the same score — only **1** distinct
score value — which violates the condition that at least three teams have differ
ent point totals.

**One decisive game suffices.** Replace exactly one draw with a decisive game: t
eam $A$ beats team $B$, and all remaining $\binom{N}{2}-1$ games are draws.

Computing each team's score:
- **Team $A$**: $2$ (win over $B$) $+\; (N-2)\times 1$ (draws with the other $N-
2$ teams) $= N$ points.
- **Team $B$**: $0$ (loss to $A$) $+\; (N-2)\times 1$ (draws with the other $N-2
$ teams) $= N-2$ points.
- **Every other team** (there are $N-2 \ge 1$ such teams since $N \ge 3$): $(N-1
)\times 1 = N-1$ points.

The three scores $N-2,\; N-1,\; N$ are pairwise distinct. Taking teams $B$, any
third team $C$, and $A$ gives three teams with different point totals. ✓

**One decisive game is necessary.** With zero decisive games (all draws), every
team has score $N-1$, yielding only one distinct score. This fails the condition
. Hence at least one decisive game is required.

**Conclusion.** The minimum number of decisive games needed is $1$, so the maxim
um number of draws is:

$$D(N) = \binom{N}{2} - 1 = \frac{N(N-1)}{2} - 1.$$

**Computing the answer:**

$$D(10) = \binom{10}{2} - 1 = 45 - 1 = 44,$$

$$D(11) = \binom{11}{2} - 1 = 55 - 1 = 54,$$

$$D(10) + D(11) = 44 + 54 = \boxed{98}.$$
