## Proof

We determine the number of $\mathbb R$-module structures on the abelian group $(
\mathbb Q,+)$.

**Step 1: Identify the relevant endomorphism ring.**

Giving $(\mathbb Q,+)$ the structure of a (unital) $\mathbb R$-module is equival
ent to giving a unital ring homomorphism
$$\varphi:\mathbb R\longrightarrow \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Q),$$
where the module action is $r\cdot q=\varphi(r)(q)$.

**Step 2: Compute $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Q)$.**

Let $f\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Q)$, i.e. $f:\mathbb Q\to\mathbb
 Q$ is additive. Set $a=f(1)\in\mathbb Q$. For $n\in\mathbb Z$, $f(n)=n\,a$. For
 $m\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$,
$$a=f(1)=f\!\bigl(m\cdot\tfrac1m\bigr)=m\,f\!\bigl(\tfrac1m\bigr),\quad\text{so
}f\!\bigl(\tfrac1m\bigr)=\frac{a}{m}.$$
Hence for $\frac{p}{q}\in\mathbb Q$,
$$f\!\bigl(\tfrac{p}{q}\bigr)=p\,f\!\bigl(\tfrac1q\bigr)=\frac{p}{q}\,a.$$
So $f$ is multiplication by $a=f(1)$, and composition of such maps is multiplica
tion of the corresponding rationals. Thus
$$\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Q)\cong \mathbb Q$$
as rings (with $1\leftrightarrow 1$).

**Step 3: Count unital ring homomorphisms $\mathbb R\to\mathbb Q$.**

A unital homomorphism $\varphi:\mathbb R\to\mathbb Q$ has $\varphi(1_{\mathbb R}
)=1_{\mathbb Q}$. Since $\mathbb R$ is a field, $\ker\varphi$ is an ideal of $\m
athbb R$, hence either $0$ or $\mathbb R$. Because $\varphi(1)=1\neq 0$, we have
 $\ker\varphi\neq\mathbb R$, so $\ker\varphi=0$ and $\varphi$ is **injective**.

But $\mathbb R$ is uncountable while $\mathbb Q$ is countable; no injective map
$\mathbb R\hookrightarrow\mathbb Q$ can exist. Contradiction.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Equivalently, since $\mathbb R$ is a field, every $\mathbb R$-module is an $\mat
hbb R$-vector space. Any nonzero vector space over the uncountable field $\mathb
b R$ has cardinality at least $|\mathbb R|$ (if $\dim\geq 1$, then $|V|\geq|\mat
hbb R|$). But $\mathbb Q$ is countable and nonzero, so it cannot carry any nontr
ivial $\mathbb R$-vector-space structure. The unital module axiom $1_{\mathbb R}
\cdot q=q$ rules out the zero action as well (it would force $q=0$ for all $q$).

Therefore there is no unital $\mathbb R$-module structure on $\mathbb Q$.
