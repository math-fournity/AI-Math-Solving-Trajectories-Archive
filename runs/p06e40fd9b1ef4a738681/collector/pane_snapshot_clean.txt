## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, there exists a bijection $h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ that can be
 expressed as a pointwise product $h(x)=f(x)\,g(x)$ for some functions $f,g:\mat
hbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$.

**Construction.** Let $h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ be *any* bijection; for concre
teness take the identity $h(x)=x$. Define
$$f(x)=h(x),\qquad g(x)=1\;\;\text{(the constant function $1$)}.$$
Both $f$ and $g$ are functions from $\mathbb{R}$ to $\mathbb{R}$ (no continuity
or bijectivity is required of them). Their pointwise product is
$$(fg)(x)=f(x)\,g(x)=h(x)\cdot 1=h(x).$$
Since $h$ is a bijection, $fg=h$ is a bijection. $\blacksquare$

**Remark (the product of two bijections need not be a bijection).** If one stren
gthens the hypothesis to require *both* $f$ and $g$ to be bijections, the answer
 to "is $fg$ necessarily a bijection" becomes **no**: taking $f(x)=g(x)=x$ (both
 bijections) gives $(fg)(x)=x^{2}$, which is not injective (e.g. $(fg)(1)=(fg)(-
1)=1$) and not surjective onto $\mathbb{R}$ (it never takes negative values). So
 the freedom to choose non-bijective $f,g$ is essential to the existence result
above.
