## Fourier Transform of δ′(t)

**Convention.** Use the Fourier transform convention

$$\widehat{f}(\omega) = \mathcal{F}\{f\}(\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(t)\
,e^{-i\omega t}\,dt.$$

**Goal.** Compute $\widehat{\delta'}(\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \delta'(t
)\,e^{-i\omega t}\,dt$.

**Key identity (distributional derivative).** For any distribution $T$ and any s
mooth test function $\varphi$ of compact support (or Schwartz class), the deriva
tive $T'$ is defined by duality:

$$\langle T', \varphi\rangle = -\langle T, \varphi'\rangle.$$

This is the rigorous definition of the derivative of a distribution, and it is e
xactly what makes sense of "$\delta'(t)$" since $\delta$ is not a function but a
 distribution.

**Apply to the delta.** Take $T = \delta$ and $\varphi(t) = e^{-i\omega t}$ (tre
ated as a test function; the computation is valid since $\delta$ has compact sup
port, so we may evaluate against any smooth function). Then

$$\langle \delta', e^{-i\omega\,\cdot}\rangle = -\langle \delta, \tfrac{d}{dt}e^
{-i\omega t}\rangle.$$

Compute the derivative of the test function:

$$\frac{d}{dt}e^{-i\omega t} = -i\omega\, e^{-i\omega t}.$$

Substitute:

$$\langle \delta', e^{-i\omega\,\cdot}\rangle = -\langle \delta, -i\omega\, e^{-
i\omega\,\cdot}\rangle = i\omega\,\langle \delta, e^{-i\omega\,\cdot}\rangle.$$

By the defining property of the delta distribution, $\langle \delta, \varphi\ran
gle = \varphi(0)$, so

$$\langle \delta, e^{-i\omega\,\cdot}\rangle = e^{-i\omega\cdot 0} = 1.$$

Therefore

$$\widehat{\delta'}(\omega) = \langle \delta', e^{-i\omega\,\cdot}\rangle = i\om
ega \cdot 1 = i\omega.$$

**Result.**

$$\boxed{\mathcal{F}\{\delta'(t)\}(\omega) = i\omega.}$$

**Consistency check.** This agrees with the general Fourier property $\mathcal{F
}\{f'\}(\omega) = i\omega\,\widehat{f}(\omega)$, combined with $\widehat{\delta}
(\omega) = 1$:

$$\mathcal{F}\{\delta'\}(\omega) = i\omega\,\widehat{\delta}(\omega) = i\omega \
cdot 1 = i\omega. \checkmark$$

(\omega)=\int f(t)e^{+i\omega t}dt$, the sign flips and the result is $-i\omega$
; under the unitary convention with $1/\sqrt{2\pi}$ factors the magnitude is unc

\omega|$ and a phase factor $\pm i$" is convention-independent.
