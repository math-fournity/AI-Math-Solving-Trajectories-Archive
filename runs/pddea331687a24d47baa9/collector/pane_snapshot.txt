## Proof

**Setup.** There are three beaches; call them $A, B, C$. Each day the person vis
its a non-empty subset of $\{A,B,C\}$, and the constraint "does not go to the sa
me beach on two consecutive days" means **no beach visited on one day is visited
 again the next day** — i.e., the subsets chosen on consecutive days are **disjo
int**.

**Available daily choices.** The non-empty subsets of $\{A,B,C\}$ are:
- Singletons: $\{A\}, \{B\}, \{C\}$ — 3 of them.
- Pairs: $\{A,B\}, \{A,C\}, \{B,C\}$ — 3 of them.
- The triple: $\{A,B,C\}$ — 1 of them.

**The triple is unusable.** $\{A,B,C\}$ is disjoint from no non-empty subset, so
 it has no valid predecessor *and* no valid successor. It cannot appear at any p
osition in a 5-day schedule (position 1 needs a valid successor; position 5 need
s a valid predecessor; middle positions need both). Hence we work only with the
**6 subsets** consisting of 3 singletons and 3 pairs.

**Compatibility (disjointness) structure.** Label the singletons $a,b,c$ and the
 pairs $ab, ac, bc$.
- A singleton is disjoint from the **other two singletons** and the **one pair n
ot containing it**: e.g., $a \sim b,\; a \sim c,\; a \sim bc$.
- A pair is disjoint from **only the singleton it does not contain**: e.g., $ab
\sim c$.

By the $S_3$-symmetry permuting the beaches, singletons form one symmetry class
and pairs form another. Let
$$s_k = \text{# valid schedules of length } k \text{ ending at a singleton}, \qq
uad p_k = \text{# valid schedules of length } k \text{ ending at a pair}.$$

**Recurrence.** From a schedule ending at a specific singleton, there are 2 sing
leton continuations and 1 pair continuation. From a schedule ending at a specifi
c pair, there is 1 singleton continuation and 0 pair continuations. Aggregating
over the symmetry classes:
$$s_{k+1} = 2\,s_k + p_k, \qquad p_{k+1} = s_k.$$

**Base case.** $s_1 = 3$, $p_1 = 3$ (each of the 6 usable subsets starts a lengt
h-1 schedule).

**Iteration to $k=5$:**

| $k$ | $s_k$ | $p_k$ | Total $s_k+p_k$ |
|-----|-------|-------|-----------------|
| 1 | 3 | 3 | 6 |
| 2 | $2\cdot3+3=9$ | $3$ | 12 |
| 3 | $2\cdot9+3=21$ | $9$ | 30 |
| 4 | $2\cdot21+9=51$ | $21$ | 72 |
| 5 | $2\cdot51+21=123$ | $51$ | **174** |

**Verification via full transfer matrix.** Ordering states as $(a,b,c,ab,ac,bc)$
, the disjointness transfer matrix $M$ (entry $M_{ij}=1$ if state $i$ may be fol
lowed by state $j$) has row sums $(3,3,3,1,1,1)$. With $v_1=(1,1,1,1,1,1)$ and $
v_{k+1}=v_k M$, the totals $\sum v_k$ are $6, 12, 30, 72, 174$, confirming the r
ecurrence computation independently:
$$v_2=(3,3,3,1,1,1),\; v_3=(7,7,7,3,3,3),\; v_4=(17,17,17,7,7,7),\; v_5=(41,41,4
1,17,17,17),$$
and $\sum v_5 = 3\cdot41 + 3\cdot17 = 123 + 51 = 174.$

**Answer.** The number of different ways the person's five-day schedule can look
 is

$$\boxed{174}.$$
