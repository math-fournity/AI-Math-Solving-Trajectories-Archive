Yes, such a continuous map $h$ exists. In fact, the identity map works.

**Proof.**

We are given that $p \circ f = p$. The desired condition on $h$ is
$$h \circ p = p \circ f.$$
Substituting the given relation $p \circ f = p$ into the right-hand side, this b
ecomes
$$h \circ p = p.$$

Take $h = \operatorname{id}_Y$, the identity map on $Y$. Then
$$(\operatorname{id}_Y \circ p)(x) = \operatorname{id}_Y(p(x)) = p(x) \quad \tex
t{for all } x \in X,$$
so $\operatorname{id}_Y \circ p = p = p \circ f$, as required. The identity map
is continuous (the preimage of any open set $U \subseteq Y$ under $\operatorname
{id}_Y$ is $U$ itself, which is open), so $h = \operatorname{id}_Y$ is a continu
ous map $Y \to Y$ satisfying $h \circ p = p \circ f$.

**Remark.** The hypothesis that $f$ is continuous is not actually needed for the
 existence of $h$; only the relation $p \circ f = p$ matters, and it forces $h =
 \operatorname{id}_Y$ to be a valid (continuous) choice. The continuity of $f$ w
ould be relevant only if one wanted to *characterize* all such $f$ via descent t
hrough $p$, but for the existence question as stated, the identity suffices.
