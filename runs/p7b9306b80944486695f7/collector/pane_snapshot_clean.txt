## Proof

**Claim:** A countable group can have an uncountable number of distinct subgroup
s. Specifically, there exists a countable group with exactly $2^{\aleph_0}$ dist
inct subgroups, which is the maximum possible.

### Upper Bound

Let $G$ be a countable group. Every subgroup of $G$ is a subset of $G$, so the s
et of subgroups is a subset of $\mathcal{P}(G)$. Since $G$ is countable, $|\math
cal{P}(G)| = 2^{\aleph_0}$. Therefore $G$ has **at most** $2^{\aleph_0}$ subgrou
ps.

$$G = \bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty} \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},$$
the direct sum of countably many copies of $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.

**$G$ is countable:** Every element of $G$ has finite support, i.e., is a finite
 binary sequence. The set of all finite binary sequences is $\bigcup_{k=0}^{\inf

**$G$ is a vector space over $\mathbb{F}_2$:** Since every element has order div
iding 2, $G$ carries a natural $\mathbb{F}_2$-vector space structure. The subgro
ups of $G$ are exactly the $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear subspaces. Let $\{e_n : n \in \
mathbb{N}\}$ be the standard basis, where $e_n$ has a 1 in position $n$ and 0 el
sewhere.

**Constructing $2^{\aleph_0}$ distinct subgroups:** For each subset $S \subseteq

$$V_S = \operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{F}_2}\{e_n : n \in S\} = \left\{\sum_{n \in

Each $V_S$ is a subgroup (subspace) of $G$.

**Distinctness:** Suppose $S \neq T$. Without loss of generality, there exists $

T$ consists of finite $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear combinations of $\{e_m : m \in T\}$.

**Counting:** The map $S \mapsto V_S$ is an injection from $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{

eph_0}$, the group $G$ has at least $2^{\aleph_0}$ distinct subgroups.
