## Proof

 origin and use complex coordinates. Set $A = s\,e^{i\alpha}$, $B = s\,e^{i\beta

**Orientation choice.** Since $\triangle ADP$ and $\triangle BCP$ are equilatera
l, $D$ is obtained from $A$ by rotating $\pm 60°$ about $P$, and $C$ from $B$ by
 rotating $\pm 60°$ about $P$. Write $D = s\,e^{i(\alpha + \varepsilon_1 \cdot 6
0°)}$ and $C = s\,e^{i(\beta + \varepsilon_2 \cdot 60°)}$ with $\varepsilon_1, \
varepsilon_2 \in \{+1,-1\}$.

The chord lengths are:
$$AB = 2s\left|\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}\right|, \qquad CD = 2s\left|\sin\frac{\varp
hi + (\varepsilon_2 - \varepsilon_1)\cdot 60°}{2}\right|.$$

- If $\varepsilon_1 = \varepsilon_2$, then $CD = AB$, contradicting $CD = 13 \ne
q 11 = AB$.
- So $\varepsilon_1 \neq \varepsilon_2$. Take $\varepsilon_1 = -1,\; \varepsilon
_2 = +1$ (the other choice is symmetric).

Set $\alpha = 0$ (WLOG). Then:
$$A = s,\quad B = s\,e^{i\varphi},\quad D = s\,e^{-i60°},\quad C = s\,e^{i(\varp
hi+60°)}.$$

The angles around $P$ are $A{:}0°$, $B{:}\varphi$, $C{:}\varphi+60°$, $D{:}300°$
. For $0 < \varphi < 240°$ these are in counterclockwise order $A,B,C,D$, giving
 a convex quadrilateral with $P$ inside.

**Setting up equations.** Let $u = \varphi/2$. Then:
$$AB = 2s\sin u = 11, \tag{I}$$
$$CD = 2s\sin(120° - u) = 13. \tag{II}$$

**Solving.** Divide (II) by (I):
$$\frac{\sin(120° - u)}{\sin u} = \frac{13}{11}.$$

Expand $\sin(120° - u) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cos u + \frac{1}{2}\sin u$:
$$\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cot u + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{13}{11} \implies \frac{\sqrt{3}
}{2}\cot u = \frac{15}{22} \implies \cot u = \frac{5\sqrt{3}}{11}.$$

So $\tan u = \frac{11}{5\sqrt{3}}$, and:
$$\sin u = \frac{\tan u}{\sqrt{1+\tan^2 u}} = \frac{11/(5\sqrt{3})}{\sqrt{1 + \f
rac{121}{75}}} = \frac{11/(5\sqrt{3})}{\sqrt{196/75}} = \frac{11/(5\sqrt{3})}{14
/(5\sqrt{3})} = \frac{11}{14}.$$

**Finding $s$.** From (I):
$$2s \cdot \frac{11}{14} = 11 \implies \frac{11s}{7} = 11 \implies s = 7.$$

**Verification.** With $\sin u = 11/14$:
$$\cos u = \sqrt{1 - \frac{121}{196}} = \frac{5\sqrt{3}}{14},$$
$$\sin(120° - u) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cdot\frac{5\sqrt{3}}{14} + \frac{1}{2}\cdo

$$CD = 2(7)\cdot\frac{13}{14} = 13.\;\checkmark$$

Also $u = \arcsin(11/14) \approx 51.8°$, so $\varphi \approx 103.6°$, giving ang
