## Solution

Let $X_1, X_2, \dots$ be i.i.d. random variables with common density $f$, so tha
t
$$I_n = P\!\left(X_1^2 + X_2^2 + \cdots + X_n^2 \le r^2\right).$$

Set $Y_i = X_i^2 \ge 0$ and $S_n = Y_1 + \cdots + Y_n$. We must show $P(S_n \le
r^2) \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$.

**Step 1: $E[Y_i] > 0$.** Since $f$ is continuous, $f \ge 0$, and $\int f = 1$,
the function $f$ cannot be supported on $\{0\}$ (a continuous function vanishing
 off $\{0\}$ is identically $0$, contradicting $\int f = 1$). Hence $x^2 f(x) >
0$ on a set of positive measure, giving
$$E[Y_i] = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x^2 f(x)\,dx > 0,$$
possibly $+\infty$.

**Step 2: $S_n \to \infty$ almost surely.**

- *If $E[Y_i] < \infty$:* By the strong law of large numbers, $\frac{S_n}{n} \to
 E[Y_i] = \sigma^2 > 0$ a.s., so $S_n \to +\infty$ a.s.

- *If $E[Y_i] = +\infty$:* For non-negative i.i.d. random variables with infinit
e mean, the strong law gives $\frac{S_n}{n} \to +\infty$ a.s. (Indeed, for any $
M>0$, the truncated variables $Y_i \wedge M$ have finite mean $\mu_M \to \infty$
, and $\frac{1}{n}\sum(Y_i \wedge M) \to \mu_M$ a.s.; since $S_n/n \ge \frac{1}{
n}\sum(Y_i \wedge M)$, letting $M \to \infty$ yields $S_n/n \to \infty$ a.s.) He
nce $S_n \to +\infty$ a.s.

**Step 3: Conclusion.** Since $S_n \to +\infty$ a.s., for any fixed $r \ge 0$ th
e event $\{S_n \le r^2\}$ decreases (in the sense of pointwise convergence of in
dicators to $0$) to the empty set. By dominated convergence (or monotone converg
ence applied to the indicators' complements),
$$I_n = P(S_n \le r^2) = E\!\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{S_n \le r^2\}}\right] \longright
arrow 0.$$

Therefore,
$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} I_n = 0.}$$
