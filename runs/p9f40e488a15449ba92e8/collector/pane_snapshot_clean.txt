Yes, $f^*$ is necessarily injective. Here is the justification.

**Key ingredients.** (1) The Universal Coefficient Theorem for cohomology with f
ield coefficients, and (2) the elementary linear-algebra fact that over a field,
 the dual of a surjective linear map is injective.

---

**Step 1: UCT with field coefficients.**

Let $C_*(X;\mathbb{k}) = C_*(X;\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{k}$ be the singular cha
in complex with coefficients in $\mathbb{k}$, and $H^i(X;\mathbb{k}) = H^i(\oper
atorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{k}}(C_*(X;\mathbb{k}),\mathbb{k}))$.

Since $\mathbb{k}$ is a field, every $\mathbb{k}$-module is free, so the $\opera
torname{Ext}$-term in the UCT vanishes. The UCT therefore gives a **natural isom
orphism**

$$\Phi_X : H^i(X;\mathbb{k}) \;\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\; \operatorname{Hom}_{\ma
thbb{k}}\!\bigl(H_i(X;\mathbb{k}),\,\mathbb{k}\bigr).$$

Naturality means: for any continuous $f:X\to Y$, the following diagram commutes:

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
H^i(Y;\mathbb{k}) & \xrightarrow{\;f^*\;} & H^i(X;\mathbb{k}) \\[4pt]
{\scriptstyle\Phi_Y}\downarrow\;\cong & & \cong\;\downarrow{\scriptstyle\Phi_X}
\\[4pt]
\operatorname{Hom}(H_i(Y;\mathbb{k}),\mathbb{k}) & \xrightarrow{\;\operatorname{
Hom}(f_*,\,\mathbb{k})\;} & \operatorname{Hom}(H_i(X;\mathbb{k}),\mathbb{k})
\end{array}
$$

where the bottom map is precomposition: $\operatorname{Hom}(f_*,\mathbb{k})(\var
phi) = \varphi\circ f_*$.

**Step 2: Reduce to linear algebra.**

By the commutativity of the diagram, $f^*$ is injective if and only if $\operato
rname{Hom}(f_*,\mathbb{k})$ is injective.

**Step 3: Dual of a surjection is an injection (over a field).**

Let $V = H_i(X;\mathbb{k})$ and $W = H_i(Y;\mathbb{k})$, both finite-dimensional
 $\mathbb{k}$-vector spaces (singular homology of a space is finitely generated
in each degree when the space is of finite type; in general one argues with the
splitting that a field provides). The map $f_*:V\to W$ is surjective by hypothes
is.

**Claim.** If $g:V\to W$ is a surjective linear map of $\mathbb{k}$-vector space
s, then $g^*:W^*\to V^*$, $g^*(\varphi)=\varphi\circ g$, is injective.

*Proof of claim.* Suppose $g^*(\varphi)=0$, i.e. $\varphi\circ g = 0$. Since $g$
 is surjective, $g(V)=W$, so $\varphi$ vanishes on all of $W$, hence $\varphi=0$
. Thus $\ker g^* = 0$. $\square$

(Equivalently: choose a splitting $W\hookrightarrow V$ of the surjection $g$; th
en $g^*$ has a left inverse, namely restriction along this section, confirming i
njectivity. Over a field such a splitting always exists.)

**Step 4: Conclude.**

Applying the claim to $g = f_*$, the dual map $\operatorname{Hom}(f_*,\mathbb{k}
)$ is injective. By the naturality of the UCT isomorphism (Step 1), $f^*$ is ide
ntified with this dual map, hence $f^*$ is injective.

---

**Answer:** Yes. Surjectivity of $f_*:H_i(X;\mathbb{k})\to H_i(Y;\mathbb{k})$ fo
rces injectivity of $f^*:H^i(Y;\mathbb{k})\to H^i(X;\mathbb{k})$, because over a
 field the UCT identifies $f^*$ with the dual (precomposition) of $f_*$, and the
 dual of a surjective linear map over a field is always injective.
