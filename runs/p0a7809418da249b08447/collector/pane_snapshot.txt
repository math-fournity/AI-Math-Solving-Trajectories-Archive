## Proof

We maximize the Burg entropy $BE(y)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\log y(x)\,dx$ over positiv
e functions $y$ on $[-\pi,\pi]$ subject to the Fourier moment constraints
$$\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} y(x)\,e^{-inx}\,dx = r_n,\qquad |n|\le N. \tag{C}$$
Throughout we assume the data $\{r_n\}_{|n|\le N}$ are consistent, i.e. the Herm
itian Toeplitz matrix $T_{N+1}:=(r_{n-m})_{0\le n,m\le N}$ is positive definite
(so the feasible set is nonempty).

---

### Step 1. Variational (Euler–Lagrange) condition

Introduce Lagrange multipliers $\lambda_n\in\mathbb C$ ($|n|\le N$) and form
$$\mathcal L[y]=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\log y(x)\,dx-\sum_{|n|\le N}\lambda_n\!\left(\
int_{-\pi}^{\pi}y(x)e^{-inx}\,dx-r_n\right).$$
Stationarity $\delta\mathcal L/\delta y=0$ gives, pointwise,
$$\frac{1}{y(x)}=\sum_{|n|\le N}\lambda_n\,e^{-inx}=:T(x). \tag{1}$$
Hence any interior maximizer must be of the form
$$y^*(x)=\frac{1}{T(x)},\qquad T(x)=\sum_{|n|\le N}\lambda_n e^{-inx}. \tag{2}$$

### Step 2. $T$ is a strictly positive trigonometric polynomial

Since $y^*$ is real, $T$ is real, forcing $\lambda_{-n}=\overline{\lambda_n}$. S
ince $y^*>0$, we need $T(x)>0$ for all $x\in[-\pi,\pi]$. Thus $T$ is a **strictl
y positive trigonometric polynomial of degree $\le N$**.

### Step 3. Fejér–Riesz factorization

By the Fejér–Riesz theorem, every strictly positive trigonometric polynomial of
degree $N$ admits a unique (up to a unimodular phase) factorization
$$T(x)=\bigl|P(e^{ix})\bigr|^2,\qquad P(z)=a_0+a_1 z+\cdots+a_N z^N, \tag{3}$$
where $P$ is **minimum-phase**: all its zeros satisfy $|z_k|>1$. Consequently
$$\boxed{\;y^*(x)=\dfrac{1}{\bigl|P(e^{ix})\bigr|^2}\;} \tag{4}$$
with $P$ a minimum-phase polynomial of degree $N$.

### Step 4. The coefficients of $P$ are fixed by the constraints (Yule–Walker)

Write $P(z)=a_0 A(z)$ with $A(z)=1+\alpha_1 z+\cdots+\alpha_N z^N$ monic, and se
t $\sigma^2:=1/|a_0|^2$, so that $y^*(x)=\sigma^2/|A(e^{ix})|^2$. Substituting (
4) into (C) and using the contour representation $z=e^{ix}$, $dx=dz/(iz)$, with
$A$ minimum-phase (zeros outside the unit disk) so that $1/A$ is analytic inside
 $|z|<1$, a residue computation yields the **Yule–Walker equations**
$$\sum_{k=0}^{N}\alpha_k\,r_{n-k}=\sigma^2\,\delta_{n0},\qquad n=0,1,\dots,N,\qu
ad \alpha_0=1. \tag{5}$$
Equivalently, with the Toeplitz matrix $T_{N+1}=(r_{n-m})_{n,m=0}^{N}$ and $\bol
dsymbol\alpha=(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_N)^T$,
$$T_{N+1}\begin{pmatrix}1\\\boldsymbol\alpha\end{pmatrix}=\sigma^2\,\mathbf e_0,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad \sigma^2=\frac{1}{(T_{N+1}^{-1})_{00}}. \tag{6}$$
Since $T_{N+1}\succ 0$, (5)–(6) have a unique solution; hence $P$ (and thus $y^*
$) is uniquely determined by the data.

### Step 5. Concavity ⟹ the stationary point is the unique global maximizer

The map $y\mapsto\log y$ is strictly concave, so $BE$ is strictly concave on the
 convex cone of positive functions. The constraint set $\{y>0:\,y\text{ satisfie
s (C)}\}$ is convex (affine moments intersected with a convex cone). Therefore $
BE$ has **at most one** maximizer, and the stationary point (4)–(6) we construct
ed is necessarily the **unique global maximizer**.

### Step 6. Maximum value of the Burg entropy

At the optimum, using $T=|P(e^{ix})|^2$ and $P(z)=a_N\prod_{k=1}^{N}(z-z_k)$ wit
h $|z_k|>1$,
$$BE^*=-\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\log T(x)\,dx=-\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\log|P(e^{ix})|^2\,dx.$
$
By Jensen's formula, for $|z_k|>1$,
$$\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\log|e^{ix}-z_k|\,dx=2\pi\log|z_k|,$$
so
$$\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\log|P(e^{ix})|\,dx=2\pi\log|a_N|+2\pi\sum_{k=1}^{N}\log|z_k|
=2\pi\log|a_0|,$$
since $|a_0|=|P(0)|=|a_N|\prod_k|z_k|$. Hence
$$BE^*=-4\pi\log|a_0|=2\pi\log\sigma^2=-2\pi\log\bigl(T_{N+1}^{-1}\bigr)_{00}. \
tag{7}$$

---

### Conclusion

The unique maximizer of the Burg entropy under the moment constraints $\int y\,e
^{-inx}=r_n$, $|n|\le N$, is
$$\boxed{\,y^*(x)=\frac{1}{\bigl|P(e^{ix})\bigr|^2}=\frac{\sigma^2}{\bigl|1+\alp
ha_1 e^{ix}+\cdots+\alpha_N e^{iNx}\bigr|^2}\,}$$
where the minimum-phase polynomial $P$ (equivalently the coefficients $\alpha_1,
\dots,\alpha_N$ and $\sigma^2$) is determined by the Yule–Walker system
$$\sum_{k=0}^{N}\alpha_k\,r_{n-k}=\sigma^2\delta_{n0},\quad n=0,\dots,N,\quad \a
lpha_0=1,$$
i.e. $\sigma^2=1/(T_{N+1}^{-1})_{00}$ with $T_{N+1}=(r_{n-m})_{n,m=0}^{N}$. The
maximum value is
$$\boxed{\,BE_{\max}=2\pi\log\sigma^2=-2\pi\log\bigl(T_{N+1}^{-1}\bigr)_{00}\,}.
$$
