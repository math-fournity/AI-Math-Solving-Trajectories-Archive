## Solution

**Setup.** Place the circle with center $O=(0,0)$ and radius $r$. Let $P=(0,r)$
be the point of tangency, so the tangent line is $\ell: y=r$ (horizontal).

**Parametrize $R$.** A variable point on the circle is $R=(r\cos\theta,\, r\sin\
theta)$.

**Find $Q$.** Since $\ell$ is the horizontal line $y=r$, the perpendicular from
$R$ to $\ell$ is vertical, giving $Q=(r\cos\theta,\, r)$.

**Compute the area.** The base $PQ$ lies along $y=r$ with length
$$|PQ| = |r\cos\theta - 0| = r|\cos\theta|.$$
The height from $R$ to the line $PQ$ (i.e., to $y=r$) is
$$h = |r\sin\theta - r| = r(1-\sin\theta),$$
which is non-negative since $\sin\theta \le 1$. Thus
$$A(\theta) = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot r|\cos\theta|\cdot r(1-\sin\theta) = \frac{r^2}{
2}\,|\cos\theta|\,(1-\sin\theta).$$

**Maximize.** By symmetry ($|\cos\theta|$ is even), it suffices to maximize
$$g(\theta) = \cos\theta\,(1-\sin\theta), \qquad \theta\in[-\tfrac{\pi}{2},\tfra
c{\pi}{2}].$$
Differentiate:
$$g'(\theta) = -\sin\theta(1-\sin\theta) - \cos^2\theta = 2\sin^2\theta - \sin\t
heta - 1.$$
Setting $g'(\theta)=0$ with $u=\sin\theta$:
$$2u^2 - u - 1 = 0 \implies u = \frac{1\pm 3}{4} \implies u=1 \text{ or } u=-\tf
rac{1}{2}.$$

- $u=1$ ($\theta=\pi/2$): $g=0$ (minimum).
- $u=-\tfrac{1}{2}$ ($\theta=-\pi/6$): $\cos\theta=\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, so $g =
 \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cdot\tfrac{3}{2} = \tfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{4}$.

At the boundaries $\theta=\pm\pi/2$, $g=0$. Hence the maximum of $g$ is $\tfrac{
3\sqrt{3}}{4}$, attained at $\sin\theta=-\tfrac{1}{2}$ (i.e., $R$ at the point w
here the radius makes angle $-\pi/6$ with $OP$).

**Result.**
$$\boxed{A_{\max} = \frac{r^2}{2}\cdot\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4} = \frac{3\sqrt{3}\,r^2
}{8}}.$$
