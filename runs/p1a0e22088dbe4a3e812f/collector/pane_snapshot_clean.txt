**Answer: No.** I'll construct a counterexample.

## Counterexample

Let $G = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} = \{0, 1\}$ with addition modulo 2, equipped wit
h the discrete topology. This is an Abelian (Hausdorff) topological group.

Let $V = \{0\}$.

**$V$ is a valid open connected neighborhood of zero:**
- **Open:** $\{0\}$ is open in the discrete topology.
- **Connected:** Any singleton is connected.
- **Neighborhood of zero:** $0 \in V$.

**No open convex $W$ with $0 \in W \subseteq V$ exists:**

Since $V = \{0\}$, the only open set $W$ satisfying $0 \in W \subseteq V$ is $W
= \{0\}$ itself.

Now test convexity of $W = \{0\}$. Take $x = 1 \in G$. Then:
$$2x = 2 \cdot 1 = 0 \in \{0\} = W + W,$$
since $W + W = \{0 + 0\} = \{0\}$.

But $x = 1 \notin W = \{0\}$.

Therefore the implication "$2x \in W + W \implies x \in W$" **fails** for $x = 1
$, so $W$ is not convex.

Since $W = \{0\}$ is the only candidate and it is not convex, **no open convex n
eighborhood of zero contained in $V$ exists**.

## Key Observation

The failure is caused by **2-torsion**: the element $1 \in \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z
}$ satisfies $2 \cdot 1 = 0$, so its double lands in $W + W$ (which always conta
ins $0$) while the element itself lies outside $V$. Any group with nontrivial 2-
torsion elements outside the connected component of zero will exhibit this obstr
uction, since the doubling map sends such elements to $0 \in W + W$ for every ne
ighborhood $W$ of $0$.
