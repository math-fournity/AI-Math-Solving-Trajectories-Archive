## Counterexample

Let $n \geq 2$. Take $U = \mathbb{C}^n$ and let
$$K = \partial B(0,1) = \{z \in \mathbb{C}^n : \|z\| = 1\},$$
the unit sphere. This is a closed and bounded subset of $\mathbb{C}^n$, hence co
mpact, and $K \subset U$.

**$U \setminus K$ is disconnected.** We have
$$\mathbb{C}^n \setminus K = B(0,1) \;\cup\; \{z : \|z\| > 1\},$$
the disjoint union of the open unit ball $B(0,1)$ and the exterior region $\{z :
 \|z\| > 1\}$. Both are nonempty open sets, so $U \setminus K$ has exactly two c
onnected components.

**Define a holomorphic function on $U \setminus K$.** Set
$$f(z) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } \|z\| < 1, \\ 1 & \text{if } \|z\| > 1. \e
nd{cases}$$
Since $f$ is constant (hence holomorphic) on each connected component of the ope
n set $U \setminus K$, it is holomorphic on all of $U \setminus K$.

**$f$ does not extend holomorphically to $U$.** Suppose, for contradiction, that
 there exists a holomorphic function $F: \mathbb{C}^n \to \mathbb{C}$ with $F|_{
U \setminus K} = f$. Then $F = 0$ on the open ball $B(0,1)$. By the identity the
orem (applied in $\mathbb{C}^n$, $n \geq 2$), a holomorphic function that vanish
es on a nonempty open set must vanish identically on all of $\mathbb{C}^n$. Henc
e $F \equiv 0$, which contradicts $F = 1$ on $\{z : \|z\| > 1\}$.

Therefore no holomorphic extension of $f$ to $U$ exists, showing that the Hartog
s extension conclusion fails when $U \setminus K$ is disconnected. $\blacksquare
