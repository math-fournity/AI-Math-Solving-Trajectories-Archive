Looking at this problem, I need to evaluate:
$$L = \lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{\Gamma(1/x)}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{|\sin t|}{t}\,dt$$

I'll determine the asymptotic behavior of both the denominator and the numerator
 as $x \to \infty$.

## Step 1: Asymptotics of $\Gamma(1/x)$

As $x \to \infty$, we have $s = 1/x \to 0^+$. The Gamma function has a simple po
le at $s=0$. Using $\Gamma(s) = \Gamma(s+1)/s$ and the expansion $\Gamma(s+1) =
1 - \gamma s + O(s^2)$ (where $\gamma$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant):

$$\Gamma(s) = \frac{1}{s} - \gamma + O(s) \quad \text{as } s \to 0^+.$$

Substituting $s = 1/x$:

$$\Gamma\!\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) = x - \gamma + O\!\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) \s
im x \quad \text{as } x \to \infty.$$

## Step 2: Asymptotics of $\int_0^x \frac{|\sin t|}{t}\,dt$

The function $|\sin t|$ is periodic with period $\pi$, and its mean value over o
ne period is:

$$\frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi |\sin t|\,dt = \frac{1}{\pi}\cdot 2 = \frac{2}{\pi}.$$

Write $|\sin t| = \frac{2}{\pi} + g(t)$, where $g(t) = |\sin t| - \frac{2}{\pi}$
 is periodic with period $\pi$ and mean zero. Split the integral at $t=1$ (the i
ntegrand is bounded near $0$ since $|\sin t|/t \to 1$):

$$\int_0^x \frac{|\sin t|}{t}\,dt = \underbrace{\int_0^1 \frac{|\sin t|}{t}\,dt}
_{=:\,A\text{ (finite constant)}} + \frac{2}{\pi}\int_1^x \frac{dt}{t} + \int_1^
x \frac{g(t)}{t}\,dt.$$

The middle term is $\frac{2}{\pi}\ln x$. For the last term: since $g$ is periodi
c with mean zero and $1/t$ is positive and decreasing to $0$, by **Dirichlet's t
est for improper integrals**, $\int_1^\infty \frac{g(t)}{t}\,dt$ converges. Deno
ting its value by $B$:

$$\int_1^x \frac{g(t)}{t}\,dt = B + o(1) \quad \text{as } x \to \infty.$$

Therefore:

$$\int_0^x \frac{|\sin t|}{t}\,dt = \frac{2}{\pi}\ln x + (A + B) + o(1) = \frac{
2}{\pi}\ln x + O(1).$$

## Step 3: The Limit

Combining both asymptotics:

$$L = \lim_{x\to\infty} \frac{\frac{2}{\pi}\ln x + O(1)}{x - \gamma + O(1/x)} =
\lim_{x\to\infty} \frac{\frac{2}{\pi}\ln x + O(1)}{x\left(1 + O(1/x)\right)}.$$

Since $\frac{\ln x}{x} \to 0$ and $\frac{1}{x} \to 0$ as $x \to \infty$:

$$L = \lim_{x\to\infty}\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\frac{\ln x}{x} + O\!\left(\frac{
1}{x}\right)\right) = 0.$$

## Conclusion

The denominator $\Gamma(1/x)$ grows **linearly** in $x$ (like $x$), while the nu
merator $\int_0^x \frac{|\sin t|}{t}\,dt$ grows only **logarithmically** (like $
\frac{2}{\pi}\ln x$). The logarithmic growth is dominated by the linear growth,
so:

$$\boxed{0}$$
