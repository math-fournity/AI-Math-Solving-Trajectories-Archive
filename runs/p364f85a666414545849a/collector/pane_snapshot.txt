# Proof

**Claim.** For any finite extension $K/L$ of nonarchimedean local fields, the re
sidue field $\kappa$ of $K$ is naturally a (finite) extension of the residue fie
ld $\lambda$ of $L$.

## Setup

Let $L$ be a nonarchimedean local field: a complete discretely valued field with
 finite residue field. Denote by:
- $\mathcal{O}_L$ the valuation ring of $L$, $\mathfrak{m}_L$ its maximal ideal,
 $\lambda = \mathcal{O}_L/\mathfrak{m}_L$ its residue field;
- $v_L$ the normalized discrete valuation on $L$.

Let $K/L$ be a finite extension. Then $K$ is itself a nonarchimedean local field
 (completeness is preserved under finite extensions of complete discretely value
d fields). Denote the corresponding objects for $K$ by $\mathcal{O}_K$, $\mathfr
ak{m}_K$, $\kappa = \mathcal{O}_K/\mathfrak{m}_K$, and $v_K$.

## The valuation on $K$ extends the valuation on $L$

Since $L$ is complete with respect to a discrete valuation, the valuation $v_L$
extends *uniquely* to a discrete valuation $v_K$ on $K$. Concretely, if $e = e(K
/L)$ is the ramification index, then for every $x \in L$,
$$v_K(x) = e \cdot v_L(x).$$

The valuation ring $\mathcal{O}_K = \{x \in K : v_K(x) \geq 0\}$ is the integral
 closure of $\mathcal{O}_L$ in $K$, and in particular $\mathcal{O}_L \subseteq \
mathcal{O}_K$.

## The induced map on residue fields

Consider the maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}_K = \{x \in K : v_K(x) > 0\}$. For an e
lement $x \in \mathcal{O}_L$, we have:
$$x \in \mathfrak{m}_K \iff v_K(x) > 0 \iff e \cdot v_L(x) > 0 \iff v_L(x) > 0 \
iff x \in \mathfrak{m}_L.$$

Therefore:
$$\mathfrak{m}_K \cap \mathcal{O}_L = \mathfrak{m}_L.$$

The inclusion $\mathcal{O}_L \hookrightarrow \mathcal{O}_K$ thus descends to an
injective ring homomorphism on the quotients:
$$\iota : \lambda = \mathcal{O}_L/\mathfrak{m}_L \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_K/\
mathfrak{m}_K = \kappa.$$

Since $\lambda$ is a field, any nonzero ring homomorphism from $\lambda$ is inje
ctive (the kernel is a proper ideal, hence $\{0\}$). Explicitly, if $\bar{a} \in
 \lambda$ is nonzero, then $a \in \mathcal{O}_L \setminus \mathfrak{m}_L$, so $v
_L(a) = 0$, hence $v_K(a) = 0$, so $a \in \mathcal{O}_K \setminus \mathfrak{m}_K
$, meaning $\iota(\bar{a}) \neq 0$.

Thus $\iota$ is a field embedding, making $\kappa$ a field extension of $\lambda
$.

## Finiteness

The fundamental identity for finite extensions of complete discretely valued fie
lds states:
$$[K : L] = e \cdot f,$$
where $e = e(K/L)$ is the ramification index and $f = [\kappa : \lambda]$ is the
 residue degree. Since $[K:L] < \infty$ and $e \geq 1$, we get $f = [\kappa:\lam
bda] \leq [K:L] < \infty$.

In particular, since $\lambda$ is finite (as $L$ is a local field), $\kappa$ is
also finite, with $|\kappa| = |\lambda|^f$.

## Conclusion

For any finite extension $K/L$ of nonarchimedean local fields, the inclusion of
valuation rings $\mathcal{O}_L \subseteq \mathcal{O}_K$ together with the identi
ty $\mathfrak{m}_K \cap \mathcal{O}_L = \mathfrak{m}_L$ induces a canonical fiel
d embedding $\lambda \hookrightarrow \kappa$. Hence $\kappa/\lambda$ is always a
 (finite) field extension, with degree $f$ satisfying $ef = [K:L]$.
