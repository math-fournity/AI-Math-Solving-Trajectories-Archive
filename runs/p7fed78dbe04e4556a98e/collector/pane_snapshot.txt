## Determination of $I(\mathbb{Z}^n)$

**Claim.** $I(\mathbb{Z}^n) = \{0\} \subset \mathbb{C}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ for every
 $n \ge 1$.

Recall $I(\mathbb{Z}^n) = \{f \in \mathbb{C}[x_1,\dots,x_n] : f(a_1,\dots,a_n)=0
 \text{ for all } (a_1,\dots,a_n)\in\mathbb{Z}^n\}$.

**Proof by induction on $n$.**

*Base case $n=1$.* Let $f \in \mathbb{C}[x]$ vanish on every integer. A nonzero
polynomial in one variable over $\mathbb{C}$ has at most $\deg f$ roots, hence o
nly finitely many. Since $\mathbb{Z}$ is infinite, $f$ must be the zero polynomi
al. So $I(\mathbb{Z})=\{0\}$.

*Inductive step.* Assume $I(\mathbb{Z}^{n-1})=\{0\}$. Let $f \in \mathbb{C}[x_1,
\dots,x_n]$ vanish on $\mathbb{Z}^n$. View $f$ as a polynomial in $x_1$ with coe
fficients in $\mathbb{C}[x_2,\dots,x_n]$:
$$f = \sum_{i=0}^{d} g_i(x_2,\dots,x_n)\,x_1^i, \qquad g_i \in \mathbb{C}[x_2,\d
ots,x_n].$$

Fix any point $(a_2,\dots,a_n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}$. Then the one-variable polyno
mial
$$h(x_1) := f(x_1,a_2,\dots,a_n) = \sum_{i=0}^{d} g_i(a_2,\dots,a_n)\,x_1^i \in
\mathbb{C}[x_1]$$
vanishes on every $a_1\in\mathbb{Z}$, hence on infinitely many points. By the ba
se case, $h\equiv 0$, so every coefficient satisfies
$$g_i(a_2,\dots,a_n)=0.$$

Since $(a_2,\dots,a_n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}$ was arbitrary, each $g_i$ vanishes on
 all of $\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}$, i.e. $g_i \in I(\mathbb{Z}^{n-1})$. By the inductive
 hypothesis, $g_i = 0$ for every $i$. Therefore $f=0$.

**Conclusion.** $I(\mathbb{Z}^n) = \{0\}$.
