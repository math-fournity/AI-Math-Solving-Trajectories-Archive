# Fundamental Group of a Klein Bottle with Two Points Removed

## Answer

The fundamental group is the **free group of rank 3**, denoted $F_3 \cong \mathb
b{Z} * \mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z}$.

## Proof

### Step 1: Setup and Euler characteristic

The Klein bottle $K$ is a closed non-orientable surface of (crosscap) genus $k =
 2$, so its Euler characteristic is
$$\chi(K) = 2 - k = 2 - 2 = 0.$$

Removing a point from a closed surface is homotopy equivalent to removing an ope
n disk (replace the puncture by a small open disk neighborhood and retract). Rem
oving two points $p_1, p_2$ from $K$ thus yields a space homotopy equivalent to
$K$ with two open disks removed, which is a compact surface $S$ with two boundar
y components. Each removed open disk decreases the Euler characteristic by 1, so
$$\chi(S) = \chi(K) - 2 = 0 - 2 = -2.$$

### Step 2: The punctured surface retracts onto a graph

A compact connected surface with nonempty boundary deformation retracts onto a 1
-dimensional spine (a graph). This is a standard fact: triangulate the surface a
nd collapse a maximal tree in the 1-skeleton, then collapse all 2-simplices onto
 their remaining boundary edges. Since $S$ has boundary, this collapse is valid
and produces a connected graph $\Gamma$ with
$$\chi(\Gamma) = \chi(S) = -2.$$

A connected graph with one vertex and $r$ edges (a wedge of $r$ circles) has $\c
hi = 1 - r$. Equating:
$$1 - r = -2 \implies r = 3.$$

Therefore $S \simeq \bigvee_{3} S^1$, and
$$\pi_1(K \setminus \{p_1, p_2\}) \cong \pi_1(S) \cong \pi_1\!\left(\bigvee_{3}
S^1\right) \cong F_3.$$

### Step 3: Verification via presentation

For independent confirmation, we use the standard presentation. The Klein bottle
 has
$$\pi_1(K) = \langle a, b \mid a\,b\,a^{-1}\,b = 1 \rangle,$$
where the single relator $r = aba^{-1}b$ encodes the boundary word of the fundam
ental polygon.

When we remove two points (equivalently, two open disks), we introduce two bound
ary loops $c_1, c_2$. The fundamental group of the surface with boundary is obta
ined by replacing the relation $r = 1$ with $r = c_1 c_2$ (the boundary loops, w
ith appropriate orientations, multiply to give the original relator):
$$\pi_1(K \setminus \{p_1,p_2\}) = \langle a, b, c_1, c_2 \mid aba^{-1}b = c_1 c
_2 \rangle.$$

This presentation has $4$ generators and $1$ relation. We eliminate one generato
r using the relation:
$$c_2 = c_1^{-1}\, a\, b\, a^{-1}\, b,$$
leaving
$$\pi_1(K \setminus \{p_1,p_2\}) \cong \langle a, b, c_1 \rangle \cong F_3,$$
a free group on three generators with no remaining relations. This confirms the
Euler characteristic computation.

### Conclusion

Both the Euler characteristic / deformation retract argument and the direct pres
entation computation agree:

$$\boxed{\pi_1(K \setminus \{p_1, p_2\}) \cong F_3 \cong \mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z}
 * \mathbb{Z}.}$$
