## Proof

Let $F = (x+y)^2 + 4(y+z)^2 + (z+x)^2$ and $G = xyz(x+y+z) = 9$.

**Step 1: Find critical points via Lagrange multipliers.**

Computing partial derivatives:

$$\frac{\partial F}{\partial x} = 2(2x+y+z),\quad \frac{\partial F}{\partial y}
= 2x+10y+8z,\quad \frac{\partial F}{\partial z} = 2x+8y+10z$$

$$\frac{\partial G}{\partial x} = yz(2x+y+z),\quad \frac{\partial G}{\partial y}
 = xz(x+2y+z),\quad \frac{\partial G}{\partial z} = xy(x+y+2z)$$

Setting $\nabla F = \lambda \nabla G$:

From the first equation: $2(2x+y+z) = \lambda\, yz(2x+y+z)$. Since $2x+y+z > 0$,
 we get $\lambda = \tfrac{2}{yz}$.

Substituting into the second equation: $2x+10y+8z = \frac{2x(x+2y+z)}{y}$, which
 simplifies to
$$5y^2 + 4yz = x^2 + xy + xz \quad \text{...(A)}$$

Substituting into the third equation: $2x+8y+10z = \frac{2x(x+y+2z)}{z}$, which
simplifies to
$$5z^2 + 4yz = x^2 + xy + xz \quad \text{...(B)}$$

Subtracting (B) from (A): $5(y^2 - z^2) = 0$, so $y = z$ (since both are positiv
e).

With $y = z$, equation (A) gives $9y^2 = x^2 + 2xy$, i.e., $x^2 + 2xy - 9y^2 = 0
$, so
$$x = y(-1 + \sqrt{10})$$
(taking the positive root).

**Step 2: Compute the value.**

Setting $y = z = 1$ and $x = \sqrt{10}-1$:

- **Constraint check:** $xyz(x+y+z) = (\sqrt{10}-1)(1)(1)(\sqrt{10}-1+1+1) = (\s
qrt{10}-1)(\sqrt{10}+1) = 10 - 1 = 9$. ✓

- **Objective:** $F = (\sqrt{10}-1+1)^2 + 4(1+1)^2 + (1+\sqrt{10}-1)^2 = (\sqrt{
10})^2 + 4\cdot 4 + (\sqrt{10})^2 = 10 + 16 + 10 = 36$.

**Step 3: Verify this is the global minimum.**

The feasible set $S = \{(x,y,z) \in (0,\infty)^3 : xyz(x+y+z)=9\}$ is a smooth s
urface.

- **As $\|(x,y,z)\| \to \infty$ on $S$:** If (say) $x \to \infty$, then $F \geq
(x+y)^2 + (z+x)^2 \geq 2x^2 \to \infty$. Similarly for $y$ or $z$.

- **As any variable $\to 0^+$:** Consider $x \to 0^+$. The constraint becomes $y
z(y+z) = 9$, and $F \to 5y^2 + 8yz + 5z^2$. This expression is symmetric in $y,z
$, and by Lagrange multipliers its minimum subject to $yz(y+z)=9$ occurs at $y =
 z = (9/2)^{1/3}$, giving $18(9/2)^{2/3} \approx 49.05 > 36$. The cases $y \to 0
^+$ and $z \to 0^+$ similarly yield values exceeding 36 (each reduces to a two-v
ariable problem whose minimum is strictly greater than 36).

Since $F$ is continuous, tends to $+\infty$ at infinity, and exceeds 36 on every
 boundary approach, the **unique** interior critical point (established in Step
1) achieves the global minimum.

**The minimum value is $\boxed{36}$.**
