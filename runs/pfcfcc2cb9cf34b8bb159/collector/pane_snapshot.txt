## Modified Bessel Equation: Leading Asymptotics as $x \to +\infty$

### The Equation

The modified Bessel equation of order $\nu$ is:

$$x^2 y'' + x y' - (x^2 + \nu^2)\,y = 0, \qquad x > 0.$$

Dividing by $x^2$:

$$y'' + \frac{1}{x}\,y' - \left(1 + \frac{\nu^2}{x^2}\right)y = 0. \tag{1}$$

### Step 1: Remove the First-Derivative Term

Set $y = x^{-1/2}\,u(x)$. Computing:

$$y' = x^{-1/2}u' - \tfrac{1}{2}x^{-3/2}u, \qquad y'' = x^{-1/2}u'' - x^{-3/2}u'
 + \tfrac{3}{4}x^{-5/2}u.$$

Substituting into (1) and multiplying through by $x^{1/2}$, the $x^{-3/2}u'$ ter
ms cancel, giving:

$$u'' - \left(1 + \frac{\nu^2 - \tfrac{1}{4}}{x^2}\right)u = 0. \tag{2}$$

Define $\mu = \nu^2 - \tfrac{1}{4}$ and $Q(x) = 1 + \mu/x^2$, so (2) reads $u''
- Q(x)\,u = 0$.

### Step 2: WKB (Liouville–Green) Approximation

For large $x$, $Q(x) \to 1$, so the leading exponential behavior is $e^{\pm x}$.
 We refine this via the WKB ansatz. Set $u = Q^{-1/4}\exp\!\bigl(\pm\!\int^x \!\
sqrt{Q}\,dt\bigr)$. Then:

**Amplitude factor.** $Q(x)^{-1/4} = \bigl(1 + \mu/x^2\bigr)^{-1/4} \to 1$ as $x
\to\infty$.

**Phase integral.**

$$\int^x \sqrt{Q(t)}\,dt = \int^x \sqrt{1 + \frac{\mu}{t^2}}\,dt = \int^x \left(
1 + \frac{\mu}{2t^2} + O(t^{-4})\right)dt = x - \frac{\mu}{2x} + O(x^{-3}).$$

Since $\mu/(2x) \to 0$, the correction in the exponent vanishes, and:

$$u(x) \sim e^{\pm x}\quad\text{as }x\to+\infty.$$

### Step 3: Recover $y$ and Identify the Two Solutions

Since $y = x^{-1/2}u$:

$$\boxed{y(x) \sim \frac{e^{\pm x}}{\sqrt{x}}\quad\text{as }x\to+\infty.}$$

There are two linearly independent solutions, distinguished by the sign of the e

- **Growing solution** $I_\nu(x)$ (modified Bessel function of the first kind):
$$I_\nu(x) \sim \frac{e^{x}}{\sqrt{2\pi\,x}}, \qquad x\to+\infty.$$

- **Decaying solution** $K_\nu(x)$ (modified Bessel function of the second kind)

$$K_\nu(x) \sim \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2x}}\,e^{-x}, \qquad x\to+\infty.$$

The constants $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}$ and $\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}$ are fixed by ma
