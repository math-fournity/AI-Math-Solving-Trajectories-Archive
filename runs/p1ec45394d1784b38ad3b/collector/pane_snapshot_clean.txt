Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the strict inequality holds
 for all square-free $n \mid m$ with $n < m$, all $x \geq 0$, and all $z$ with $
z^2 \equiv 1 \pmod{m}$.

## Analysis

Set $a = \pi(z)^* = z^* \bmod n$, and write $z^* = a + nk$ where $0 \leq a < n$
and $0 \leq k < m/n$. The inequality becomes:
$$\left\lfloor\frac{x - a}{n}\right\rfloor > \left\lfloor\frac{x - a - nk}{m}\ri
ght\rfloor$$

## Counterexample

Take $n = 2$, $m = 6$. Both are square-free, $n \mid m$, and $n < m$.

Consider $z = 1 \in \mathbb{Z}/6$. Then $z^2 = 1 \equiv 1 \pmod{6}$, so $z$ is v
alid.

- $z^* = 1$ (least non-negative residue of $1$ mod $6$).
- $\pi(z) = 1 \bmod 2 = 1$, so $\pi(z)^* = 1$.

Now take $x = 0$:

- **LHS:** $\left\lfloor\frac{0 - 1}{2}\right\rfloor = \left\lfloor -\frac{1}{2}
\right\rfloor = -1$
- **RHS:** $\left\lfloor\frac{0 - 1}{6}\right\rfloor = \left\lfloor -\frac{1}{6}
\right\rfloor = -1$

The strict inequality $-1 > -1$ is **false**.

## General observation

For any square-free $n \geq 2$ and $m > n$ with $n \mid m$, the element $z = 1$
always satisfies $z^2 \equiv 1 \pmod{m}$, with $z^* = 1$ and $\pi(z)^* = 1$. At
$x = 0$:

$$\left\lfloor\frac{-1}{n}\right\rfloor = -1 \quad \text{and} \quad \left\lfloor
\frac{-1}{m}\right\rfloor = -1$$

since $n \geq 2$ and $m \geq 2$ both give floor $-1$. The strict inequality fail
s universally.

## Answer

**False**
