## Galois Structure of Q(ζ_{p²}) for p = 5 and p = 7

### Setup

Let ζ_{p²} denote a primitive p²-th root of unity. The cyclotomic field K = Q(ζ_
{p²}) is the splitting field of x^{p²} − 1 over Q, hence K/Q is a Galois extensi
on. Its degree is

[K : Q] = φ(p²) = p² − p = p(p − 1).

The Galois group is canonically isomorphic to the group of units modulo p²:

G := Gal(Q(ζ_{p²})/Q) ≅ (Z/p²Z)^×,

where an integer a (coprime to p²) acts by σ_a : ζ_{p²} ↦ ζ_{p²}^a.

### Cyclicity of the Galois Group

A classical result in number theory states that (Z/nZ)^× is cyclic if and only i
f n ∈ {1, 2, 4, p^k, 2p^k} for an odd prime p and k ≥ 1. Since p² falls into thi
s class for any odd prime p, **G is cyclic of order p(p−1)**.

Because gcd(p, p−1) = 1, the cyclic group G of order p(p−1) decomposes as an int
ernal direct product of its Sylow subgroups:

G ≅ Z_p × Z_{p−1}.

### The Tower Structure

There is a natural reduction homomorphism

ρ : (Z/p²Z)^× → (Z/pZ)^×,   a mod p² ↦ a mod p,

which is surjective with kernel ker(ρ) = {1 + kp mod p² : k = 0, 1, …, p−1}, a c
yclic group of order p. Under the Galois correspondence, this kernel is the subg
roup fixing Q(ζ_p) ⊂ Q(ζ_{p²}), yielding the tower

Q ⊊ Q(ζ_p) ⊊ Q(ζ_{p²}),

with [Q(ζ_p) : Q] = p−1 and [Q(ζ_{p²}) : Q(ζ_p)] = p. The extension Q(ζ_{p²})/Q(
ζ_p) is totally ramified at p, while Q(ζ_p)/Q is unramified at p.

### Counting Intermediate Fields

By the Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory, intermediate fields E with Q ⊆ E ⊆
Q(ζ_{p²}) are in bijection with subgroups of G. Since G is cyclic of order n = p
(p−1), it has exactly **one subgroup for each divisor of n**, so the number of i
ntermediate fields equals d(n), the number of positive divisors of n.

---

### Case p = 5

Here n = 5 · 4 = 20 = 2² · 5. The divisors of 20 are

1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20,

so **d(20) = 6**. There are **6 intermediate fields** (including Q and Q(ζ_{25})
 themselves; 4 strictly between).

The Galois group is G ≅ Z_{20} ≅ Z_4 × Z_5. The six subgroups and their fixed fi
elds are:

| Subgroup order | Fixed field | Degree over Q |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| 20 | Q | 1 |
| 10 | Q(√5) (unique quadratic subfield) | 2 |
| 5 | Q(ζ_5) (the (p−1)-part; fixed field of the p-Sylow) | 4 |
| 4 | Unique subfield of degree 5 (fixed field of the (p−1)-Sylow) | 5 |
| 2 | Fixed field of the unique index-10 subgroup | 10 |
| 1 | Q(ζ_{25}) | 20 |

The unique quadratic subfield is Q(√5), inherited from Q(ζ_5) (since the quadrat
ic subfield of Q(ζ_p) is Q(√{(−1)^{(p−1)/2} p}) = Q(√5) for p = 5).

The unique subfield of degree 5 is the fixed field of the subgroup of order 4 =
p−1 (the complementary factor to the p-Sylow). This is a degree-p extension of Q
, totally ramified at p.

### Case p = 7

Here n = 7 · 6 = 42 = 2 · 3 · 7. The divisors of 42 are

1, 2, 3, 6, 7, 14, 21, 42,

so **d(42) = 8**. There are **8 intermediate fields** (including Q and Q(ζ_{49})
 themselves; 6 strictly between).

The Galois group is G ≅ Z_{42} ≅ Z_6 × Z_7. The eight subgroups and their fixed
fields are:

| Subgroup order | Fixed field | Degree over Q |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| 42 | Q | 1 |
| 21 | Q(√{−7}) (unique quadratic subfield) | 2 |
| 14 | Unique subfield of degree 3 | 3 |
| 7 | Q(ζ_7) (fixed field of the p-Sylow) | 6 |
| 6 | Unique subfield of degree 7 (fixed field of the (p−1)-Sylow) | 7 |
| 3 | Fixed field of the index-14 subgroup | 14 |
| 2 | Fixed field of the index-21 subgroup | 21 |
| 1 | Q(ζ_{49}) | 20... → 42 |

The unique quadratic subfield is Q(√{−7}), since (−1)^{(7−1)/2} · 7 = −7.

The unique subfield of degree 7 is the fixed field of the subgroup of order 6 =
p−1; it is a totally ramified degree-7 extension of Q at the prime 7.

### Summary

- **p = 5**: G ≅ Z_{20}, cyclic of order 20. The number of intermediate fields i
s **6** (the number of divisors of 20).
- **p = 7**: G ≅ Z_{42}, cyclic of order 42. The number of intermediate fields i
s **8** (the number of divisors of 42).

In both cases the key structural features are: (1) the Galois group is cyclic (s
ince (Z/p²Z)^× is cyclic for odd p), so there is exactly one subgroup per diviso
r — hence the count is purely arithmetic; (2) the group splits as Z_p × Z_{p−1},
 giving the tower Q ⊊ Q(ζ_p) ⊊ Q(ζ_{p²}) with a degree-p totally ramified layer
on top of the degree-(p−1) cyclotomic layer; (3) the unique quadratic subfield i
s Q(√{(−1)^{(p−1)/2} p}), inherited from Q(ζ_p).
