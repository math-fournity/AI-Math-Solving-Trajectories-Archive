## Proof

Let $F_p = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$. We determine $|SO_2(F_p)|$ where

$$SO_2(F_p) = \{A \in M_2(F_p) : A^T A = I,\ \det A = 1\}.$$

### Step 1. Parametrize $SO_2(F_p)$

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$. The condition $A^T A = I
$ says the columns $(a,c)^T$ and $(b,d)^T$ are orthonormal:

$$a^2+c^2=1,\quad b^2+d^2=1,\quad ab+cd=0.$$

Given a unit vector $(a,c)$, the orthogonal complement is 1-dimensional, spanned
 by $(-c,a)$ (which also has norm $a^2+c^2=1$). So $(b,d) = \pm(-c,a)$. The dete
rminant is $ad - bc$; for $(b,d)=(-c,a)$ we get $a^2+c^2 = 1$, and for $(b,d)=(c
,-a)$ we get $-(a^2+c^2) = -1$. Since $\det A = 1$, we must take $(b,d)=(-c,a)$.
 Hence

$$SO_2(F_p) = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a & -b \\ b & a \end{pmatrix} : a,b \in F_
p,\ a^2+b^2=1 \right\}.$$

This bijection is valid over $F_p$ for every prime $p$ (including $p=2$, where $
-b=b$). Therefore

$$|SO_2(F_p)| = N := \#\{(a,b)\in F_p^2 : a^2+b^2=1\}.$$

### Step 2. Count $N$ for odd $p$

Let $\chi$ be the Legendre symbol on $F_p$, extended by $\chi(0)=0$. For each $a
$, the number of $b$ with $b^2 = 1-a^2$ equals $1 + \chi(1-a^2)$ (giving $1, 2,
0$ according as $1-a^2$ is $0$, a nonzero square, or a nonsquare). Thus

$$N = \sum_{a\in F_p}\bigl(1+\chi(1-a^2)\bigr) = p + S, \qquad S := \sum_{a\in F
_p}\chi(1-a^2).$$

**Compute $S$.** Since $\chi$ is multiplicative, $\chi(1-a^2)=\chi(1-a)\chi(1+a)
$. Substituting $u=1-a$ (so $1+a = 2-u$), then $u = 2t$ (a bijection since $p$ i
s odd):

$$S = \sum_{u\in F_p}\chi(u)\chi(2-u) = \sum_{t\in F_p}\chi(2t)\chi(2-2t) = \sum
_{t\in F_p}\chi(t)\chi(1-t) =: J(\chi,\chi),$$

the Jacobi sum, using $\chi(2)^2=1$.

**Evaluate $J(\chi,\chi)=-\chi(-1)$.** Let $g=\sum_{x}\chi(x)\zeta^x$ be the Gau
ss sum ($\zeta=e^{2\pi i/p}$). Then

$$g^2 = \sum_{s}\zeta^s\sum_x \chi(x(s-x)).$$

For $s\neq 0$, substituting $x=st$ gives $\sum_x\chi(x(s-x)) = \chi(s^2)\sum_t\c
hi(t(1-t)) = J(\chi,\chi)$. For $s=0$, $\sum_x\chi(-x^2)=\chi(-1)\sum_x\chi(x^2)
=\chi(-1)(p-1)$. Hence

$$g^2 = \chi(-1)(p-1) + J(\chi,\chi)\sum_{s\neq 0}\zeta^s = \chi(-1)(p-1) - J(\c
hi,\chi).$$

By Gauss's theorem $g^2 = \chi(-1)\,p$. Equating:

$$\chi(-1)\,p = \chi(-1)(p-1) - J(\chi,\chi) \implies J(\chi,\chi) = -\chi(-1).$
$

Therefore $S = -\chi(-1)$ and

$$\boxed{N = p - \chi(-1)}.$$

Since $\chi(-1)=1$ when $p\equiv 1\pmod 4$ and $\chi(-1)=-1$ when $p\equiv 3\pmo
d 4$:

- $p\equiv 1\pmod 4$: $|SO_2(F_p)| = p-1$.
- $p\equiv 3\pmod 4$: $|SO_2(F_p)| = p+1$.

### Step 3. The case $p=2$

In $F_2$, $x^2=x$, so $a^2+b^2=1$ becomes $a+b=1$ with $a,b\in\{0,1\}$, giving $
(a,b)\in\{(1,0),(0,1)\}$. Thus $|SO_2(F_2)|=2$.

### Verification

- $p=3$ ($\equiv 3\!\pmod 4$): expect $4$. Solutions of $a^2+b^2=1$ over $F_3$:
$(0,\pm1),(\pm1,0)$ — $4$. ✓
- $p=5$ ($\equiv 1\!\pmod 4$): expect $4$. Squares mod $5$: $\{0,1,4\}$; $a^2+b^
2=1$ needs $\{a^2,b^2\}=\{0,1\}$, giving $4$ solutions. ✓
- $p=2$: expect $2$. ✓

### Answer

$$|SO_2(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})| = \begin{cases} 2 & p = 2,\\ p-1 & p\equiv 1 \p
mod 4,\\ p+1 & p\equiv 3 \pmod 4. \end{cases}$$

Equivalently, for odd $p$: $|SO_2(F_p)| = p - \left(\tfrac{-1}{p}\right)$.
