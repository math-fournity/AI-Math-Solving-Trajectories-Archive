# Solution

## Setup

Let $f(t) = t + \frac{1}{t}$. The constraint $|t| \geq 1$ (i.e., $t \notin (-1,1
)$, $t \neq 0$) gives:
- $t \geq 1 \Rightarrow f(t) \geq 2$ (AM-GM, equality at $t=1$),
- $t \leq -1 \Rightarrow f(t) \leq -2$ (equality at $t=-1$).

The equation $f(x)+f(y)+f(z)=0$ requires three values from $(-\infty,-2]\cup[2,\
infty)$ summing to zero. The only feasible sign patterns are:
- **Case 1:** two $\geq 2$, one $\leq -2$,
- **Case 2:** one $\geq 2$, two $\leq -2$.

## Case 1: Two positive, one negative — yields $S < 0$

Let $f(x)=a\geq 2$, $f(y)=b\geq 2$, $f(z)=c\leq -2$, with $c=-(a+b)$. To maximiz
e $S=x+y+z$, take $x=\frac{a+\sqrt{a^2-4}}{2}$, $y=\frac{b+\sqrt{b^2-4}}{2}$ (th
e $+$ roots, giving $x,y\geq 1$). For $z\leq -1$ with $c < -2$ (since $a+b\geq 4
$), the $+$ root lands in $(-1,0)$ — **forbidden** — so we must take $z=\frac{c-
\sqrt{c^2-4}}{2}\leq -1$. Then:

$$S = \frac{a+b+c}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{a^2-4}+\sqrt{b^2-4}-\sqrt{(a+b)^2-4}}{2} = \
frac{\sqrt{a^2-4}+\sqrt{b^2-4}-\sqrt{(a+b)^2-4}}{2}.$$

This is $< 0$ iff $\sqrt{a^2-4}+\sqrt{b^2-4} < \sqrt{(a+b)^2-4}$. Squaring (both
 sides non-negative) reduces to $a^2+b^2+ab > 3$, which holds since $a,b\geq 2$
gives $a^2+b^2+ab\geq 12$. So **$S<0$ in Case 1**.

## Case 2: One positive, two negative — the maximizing case

Let $f(x)=a\geq 2$, $f(y)=b\leq -2$, $f(z)=c\leq -2$, with $a=-(b+c)\geq 4$. Set
 $b=-p,\, c=-q$ with $p,q\geq 2$, so $a=p+q$. Taking maximizing roots ($x=\frac{
a+\sqrt{a^2-4}}{2}\geq 1$; $y=\frac{b-\sqrt{b^2-4}}{2}\leq -1$, $z=\frac{c-\sqrt
{c^2-4}}{2}\leq -1$):

$$S = \frac{\sqrt{(p+q)^2-4}-\sqrt{p^2-4}-\sqrt{q^2-4}}{2} =: \frac{g(p,q)}{2}.$
$

### Maximizing $g(p,q)$ over $p,q\geq 2$

Define $h(t)=\frac{t}{\sqrt{t^2-4}}$ for $t>2$. Since $h(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-4/t
^2}}$ and $4/t^2$ is decreasing, $h$ is **strictly decreasing** for $t>2$.

The partial derivatives are:
$$\frac{\partial g}{\partial p} = h(p+q)-h(p), \qquad \frac{\partial g}{\partial
 q} = h(p+q)-h(q).$$

Since $p+q > p$ and $p+q > q$ (as $q,p\geq 2>0$), and $h$ is strictly decreasing
, both partials are **strictly negative** for all $p,q > 2$. Therefore $g$ is st
rictly decreasing in each variable, and the **maximum is on the boundary** $p=2,
\, q=2$:

$$g(2,2) = \sqrt{16-4} - 0 - 0 = \sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3}, \qquad S = \frac{2\sqrt{
3}}{2} = \sqrt{3}.$$

## Verification of the extremal point

At $p=q=2$: $b=c=-2$, $a=4$, giving
$$x = \frac{4+\sqrt{12}}{2} = 2+\sqrt{3}, \qquad y = z = \frac{-2-0}{2} = -1.$$

**Constraint check:** $|x|=2+\sqrt{3}>1$ ✓, $|y|=|z|=1\notin(-1,1)$ ✓.

**Equation check:**
$$\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{y}+\frac{1}{z}+x+y+z = (2-\sqrt{3})+(-1)+(-1)+(2+\sqrt{3}
)+(-1)+(-1) = 4-4 = 0.\ \checkmark$$

**Value:** $x+y+z = (2+\sqrt{3})-1-1 = \sqrt{3}$.

## Conclusion

Case 1 always gives $S<0$. Case 2 gives $S\leq\sqrt{3}$, with equality uniquely
at $(x,y,z)=(2+\sqrt{3},\,-1,\,-1)$ (and permutations). The maximum possible val
ue is:

$$\boxed{\sqrt{3}}$$
