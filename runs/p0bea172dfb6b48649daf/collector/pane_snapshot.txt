Looking at this problem, I need to analyze the group $K$ generated by positive d
iagonal matrices and permutation matrices in $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbf{R})$, and de
termine whether it can contain a subgroup $H$ where every element has non-negati
ve entries.

**Answer: Yes.** In fact, $K$ is the unique maximal subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\
mathbf{R})$ with the property that every element has non-negative entries — so $
K$ not only can contain such subgroups, it contains *all* of them.

---

## Proof

### Step 1: Structure of $K$

Let $D_+ = \{\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\dots,d_n) : d_i > 0\}$ be the group of dia
gonal matrices with positive entries, and $P_n \cong S_n$ the group of permutati
on matrices. Conjugation by a permutation matrix $\sigma$ permutes diagonal entr
ies:
$$\sigma \operatorname{diag}(d_1,\dots,d_n) \sigma^{-1} = \operatorname{diag}(d_
{\sigma^{-1}(1)},\dots,d_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}),$$
so $D_+$ is normal in $K = \langle D_+, P_n \rangle$. Therefore
$$K = D_+ \rtimes P_n,$$
and every element of $K$ is a **monomial matrix with positive entries** (exactly
 one positive entry per row and per column, zeros elsewhere). In particular, eve
ry element of $K$ already has non-negative entries.

### Step 2: Any subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbf{R})$ with all non-negative en
tries is contained in $K$

Let $H \leq \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbf{R})$ be a subgroup in which every element has
non-negative entries. We show $H \leq K$.

Take any $A \in H$. Then $A^{-1} \in H$ as well, so **both** $A = (a_{ij})$ and
$B = A^{-1} = (b_{ij})$ have non-negative entries. Since $AB = I$:

- **Off-diagonal** ($i \neq j$): $\sum_k a_{ik} b_{kj} = 0$. All summands are $\
geq 0$, so $a_{ik} b_{kj} = 0$ for every $k$.
- **Diagonal** ($i = j$): $\sum_k a_{ik} b_{ki} = 1$.

**Define** $S_i = \{k : a_{ik} > 0\}$, the support of row $i$ of $A$.

**Claim 1:** For $k \in S_i$, the only possibly nonzero entry in row $k$ of $B$
is $b_{ki}$.

*Proof:* If $k \in S_i$ then $a_{ik} > 0$. For any $j \neq i$, the off-diagonal
condition gives $a_{ik} b_{kj} = 0$, so $b_{kj} = 0$. Thus all entries $b_{kj}$
with $j \neq i$ vanish. Since $B$ is invertible, row $k$ is not identically zero
, so $b_{ki} > 0$. $\square$

**Claim 2:** The sets $S_1, \dots, S_n$ are pairwise disjoint.

*Proof:* Suppose $k \in S_i \cap S_{i'}$ with $i \neq i'$. By Claim 1 applied to
 $k \in S_i$, the only nonzero entry in row $k$ of $B$ is $b_{ki}$, so $b_{ki'}
= 0$. But by Claim 1 applied to $k \in S_{i'}$, we get $b_{ki'} > 0$. Contradict
ion. $\square$

**Claim 3:** Each $S_i$ is a singleton, so $A$ is monomial.

*Proof:* Each $S_i$ is non-empty (since $A$ is invertible, no row is zero). The
$n$ sets $S_1, \dots, S_n$ are pairwise disjoint subsets of $\{1, \dots, n\}$, a
ll non-empty. By the pigeonhole principle, each has exactly one element. So each
 row of $A$ has exactly one positive entry, and $A$ is a monomial matrix with po
sitive entries. $\square$

Therefore $A \in K$, and since $A$ was arbitrary, $H \leq K$.

### Step 3: Conclusion

We have shown:

1. **Every element of $K$ has non-negative entries** (Step 1), so $K$ itself — a
nd every subgroup of $K$ — satisfies the required property. Thus $K$ **can** con
tain such subgroups.

2. **Conversely**, any subgroup $H \leq \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbf{R})$ with all non-
negative entries must satisfy $H \leq K$ (Step 2). So $K$ is the **maximal** sub
group of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbf{R})$ with this property.

For $n \geq 3$, there are many nontrivial examples of such subgroups contained i
n $K$:
- $K$ itself,
- $D_+$ (positive diagonal matrices),
- $P_n \cong S_n$ (permutation matrices),
- $D_+ \rtimes A_n$ (where $A_n$ is the alternating group),
- and any other subgroup of the monomial group.

The key insight is that the non-negativity condition on **both** $A$ and $A^{-1}
$ forces each row to have exactly one positive entry, which is exactly the monom
ial structure of $K$. The condition $n \geq 3$ ensures the group structure is ri
ch enough to make the question non-vacuous (for $n \geq 3$, $S_n$ is non-abelian
 and $K$ has interesting proper subgroups), though the core argument holds for a
ll $n \geq 1$.
