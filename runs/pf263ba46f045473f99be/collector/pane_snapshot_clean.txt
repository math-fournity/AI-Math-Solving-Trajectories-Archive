## Distribution of the First Passage Time $T_x$

**Setup.** Let $\{W(t)\}_{t \geq 0}$ be a standard Brownian motion with $W(0)=0$
, and for $x>0$ define the first passage (hitting) time
$$T_x := \inf\{t \geq 0 : W(t) = x\}.$$

We claim $T_x$ has the **Lévy distribution** with density
$$\boxed{\,f_{T_x}(t) = \dfrac{x}{\sqrt{2\pi\, t^{3}}}\, e^{-x^{2}/(2t)}, \qquad
 t>0,\,}$$
and $P(T_x=\infty)=0$ (i.e. the level $x$ is hit almost surely).

---

### Step 1 — Reduce to the running maximum

The event $\{T_x \leq t\}$ is exactly the event that the running maximum reaches
 $x$ by time $t$:
$$\{T_x \leq t\} = \Bigl\{\max_{0\le s\le t} W(s) \geq x\Bigr\}.$$
So the CDF of $T_x$ is
$$F_{T_x}(t) := P(T_x \leq t) = P\!\Bigl(\max_{0\le s\le t} W(s) \geq x\Bigr).$$

### Step 2 — Reflection principle

By the **reflection principle**, for $x>0$,
$$P\!\Bigl(\max_{0\le s\le t} W(s) \geq x\Bigr) = 2\,P(W(t)\ge x).$$

*Sketch of the argument:* On the event $\{M_t \geq x\}$ where $M_t = \max_{0\le
s\le t}W(s)$, let $\tau = T_x$ be the first hitting time of $x$. Reflecting the
path after $\tau$ via $\widetilde W(s) = W(s)$ for $s\le\tau$ and $\widetilde W(
s) = 2x - W(s)$ for $s>\tau$ gives another Brownian path, and this reflection is
 a bijection between
$$\{M_t \geq x,\; W(t) \geq x\} \quad\text{and}\quad \{M_t \geq x,\; W(t) < x\}.
$$
Hence $P(M_t\ge x,\, W(t)<x)=P(M_t\ge x,\, W(t)\ge x)$, and adding $P(W(t)\ge x)
$ (which equals $P(M_t\ge x,\,W(t)\ge x)$ since $\{W(t)\ge x\}\subseteq\{M_t\ge
x\}$) yields $P(M_t\ge x)=2P(W(t)\ge x)$.

Since $W(t)\sim N(0,t)$,
$$P(W(t)\ge x) = 1 - \Phi\!\Bigl(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Bigr),$$
where $\Phi$ is the standard normal CDF. Therefore
$$F_{T_x}(t) = 2\Bigl[1 - \Phi\!\Bigl(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Bigr)\Bigr], \qquad t>0
. \tag{1}$$

### Step 3 — Differentiate to get the density

Differentiating (1) with respect to $t$, using $\phi(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{
-z^{2}/2}$ and $\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(x\,t^{-1/2}\bigr) = -\frac{x}{2}\,t^{-3/2}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{T_x}(t) &= \frac{d}{dt}\,2\Bigl[1-\Phi\!\Bigl(\frac{x}{\sqrt t}\Bigr)\Bigr] \
\
&= -2\,\phi\!\Bigl(\frac{x}{\sqrt t}\Bigr)\cdot\Bigl(-\frac{x}{2}\,t^{-3/2}\Bigr
) \\
&= \frac{x}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,t^{-3/2}\,e^{-x^{2}/(2t)} \\
&= \frac{x}{\sqrt{2\pi\,t^{3}}}\,e^{-x^{2}/(2t)}, \qquad t>0.
\end{aligned}
$$

### Step 4 — It is a proper probability density

We verify $\int_0^\infty f_{T_x}(t)\,dt = 1$. Substitute $u = \dfrac{x^{2}}{2t}$
, so $t = \dfrac{x^{2}}{2u}$, $dt = -\dfrac{x^{2}}{2u^{2}}\,du$, and $t^{-3/2} =
 \Bigl(\dfrac{x^{2}}{2u}\Bigr)^{-3/2} = \dfrac{(2u)^{3/2}}{x^{3}}$. Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^\infty \frac{x}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,t^{-3/2}\,e^{-x^{2}/(2t)}\,dt
&= \int_0^\infty \frac{x}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\cdot\frac{(2u)^{3/2}}{x^{3}}\cdot e^{-u}\
cdot\frac{x^{2}}{2u^{2}}\,du \\
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\cdot\frac{2^{3/2}}{2}\int_0^\infty u^{-1/2}\,e^{-u}\,du
 \\
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\Gamma\!\Bigl(\tfrac12\Bigr) \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}
\cdot\sqrt{\pi} \;=\; 1.
\end{aligned}
$$

### Step 5 — The level is hit almost surely

Taking $t\to\infty$ in (1):
$$P(T_x < \infty) = \lim_{t\to\infty} F_{T_x}(t) = 2\bigl[1-\Phi(0)\bigr] = 2\cd
ot\tfrac12 = 1.$$
So $T_x<\infty$ a.s. (Brownian motion is recurrent).

### Step 6 — Identification as the Lévy distribution

The density $f_{T_x}(t)=\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{2\pi t^{3}}}\,e^{-x^{2}/(2t)}$ is precis
ely the **Lévy distribution** $\mathrm{Levy}(c=x^{2})$, i.e. a stable law of ind
ex $\alpha=\tfrac12$. Equivalently, $T_x \stackrel{d}{=} \dfrac{x^{2}}{Z^{2}}$ w
here $Z\sim N(0,1)$, since if $Z\sim N(0,1)$ then $x^{2}/Z^{2}$ has exactly this
 density (verified by the change of variables $t=x^{2}/z^{2}$).

A useful consequence: $T_x$ is **self-similar** in $x$, namely $T_x \stackrel{d}
{=} x^{2}\,T_1$ (scaling property of Brownian motion). Its mean is infinite: $E[
T_x]=\infty$ (the tail of $f_{T_x}$ is $\sim t^{-3/2}$, so the integral $\int t\
,f_{T_x}(t)\,dt$ diverges), even though the hitting time is finite a.s.

---

**Summary.** For a standard Brownian motion starting at $0$ and $x>0$, the first
 passage time $T_x=\inf\{t\ge0:W(t)=x\}$ is almost surely finite and has the **L
évy distribution** with density

$$f_{T_x}(t)=\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{2\pi\,t^{3}}}\,\exp\!\Bigl(-\dfrac{x^{2}}{2t}\Bigr)
,\quad t>0,$$

equivalently $F_{T_x}(t)=2\bigl[1-\Phi(x/\sqrt{t})\bigr]$ for $t>0$. The key too
ls are the reflection principle and the identity $\{T_x\le t\}=\{\max_{0\le s\le
 t}W(s)\ge x\}$.
