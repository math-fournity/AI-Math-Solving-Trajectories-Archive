## Proof

Let $x$ be a peacock with digits $a_9 a_8 \cdots a_0$ (so $x = \sum a_i \cdot 10
^i$), and $y = 2x$ also a peacock with digits $b_9 \cdots b_0$. Set up the doubl
ing with carries $c_0, c_1, \ldots, c_{10}$ where $c_0 = 0$ (carry in to positio
n 0) and:
$$2a_i + c_i = b_i + 10c_{i+1}, \quad i = 0, 1, \ldots, 9.$$

**Step 1: Carry constraints.** Since $x$ is 10-digit with leading digit $\le 4$
(as $2x$ must stay 10-digit), $c_{10} = 0$. Each $c_i \in \{0,1\}$. Summing all
equations:
$$2\sum a_i + \sum_{i=0}^{9} c_i = \sum b_i + 10\sum_{i=0}^{9} c_{i+1}.$$
Using $\sum a_i = \sum b_i = 45$, $c_0 = c_{10} = 0$, and letting $S = c_1 + \cd
ots + c_9$:
$$90 + S = 45 + 10S \implies S = 5.$$
So exactly 5 of $c_1, \ldots, c_9$ equal 1.

**Step 2: Carry determined by digit size.** Since $2a_i + c_i \ge 10 \iff a_i \g
e 5$, we get $c_{i+1} = 1 \iff a_i \in \{5,6,7,8,9\}$. Call digits $\ge 5$ "high
" and digits $\le 4$ "low." Since $c_{10}=0$, $a_9$ is low (leading digit $\in \
{1,2,3,4\}$). Among $a_0, \ldots, a_8$: exactly 5 high, 4 low. Together with $a_
9$ low, all of $\{0,1,2,3,4\}$ and all of $\{5,6,7,8,9\}$ appear in $x$.

**Step 3: Parity of $b_i$.** From the equation, $b_i \equiv c_i \pmod{2}$. So $b
_i$ is even iff $c_i = 0$. Since $c_0 = 0$ and exactly 5 of $c_1,\ldots,c_9$ are
 1: exactly 5 even and 5 odd $b_i$'s — consistent with $y$ being pandigital.

**Step 4: Type classification.** Each position $i$ has a type determined by $(c_
i, c_{i+1})$:
- **Type A** $(0,0)$: $a_i$ low, $b_i = 2a_i$ (even)
- **Type B** $(1,0)$: $a_i$ low, $b_i = 2a_i+1$ (odd)
- **Type C** $(0,1)$: $a_i$ high, $b_i = 2a_i-10$ (even)
- **Type D** $(1,1)$: $a_i$ high, $b_i = 2a_i-9$ (odd)

Let $n_A, n_B, n_C, n_D$ be the counts. From the carry structure: $n_B = n_C$ (0
→1 transitions = 1→0 transitions) and $n_A = n_D$ (both equal $5 - n_C$). Settin
g $k = n_B = n_C$ (number of runs of 1s in the carry sequence), we get $n_A = n_
D = 5-k$.

**Step 5: Reduced digits.** Define $r_i = a_i \bmod 5 \in \{0,1,2,3,4\}$. Then:
- Type A or C ($c_i=0$): $b_i = 2r_i$ (even)
- Type B or D ($c_i=1$): $b_i = 2r_i+1$ (odd)

For $x$ pandigital: $r$-values at low positions (A,B) $= \{0,1,2,3,4\}$, and at
high positions (C,D) $= \{0,1,2,3,4\}$ — **automatic** from assigning distinct l
ow/high digits.

For $y$ pandigital: $r$-values at $c_i=0$ positions (A,C) $= \{0,1,2,3,4\}$, and
 at $c_i=1$ positions (B,D) $= \{0,1,2,3,4\}$ — **the real constraint**.

**Step 6: Counting valid $r$-assignments.** The constraints form a 2×2 structure
. Choose $S_A \subseteq \{0,1,2,3,4\}$ with $|S_A| = 5-k$ (the $r$-values for ty
pe A positions); then $S_C = \overline{S_A}$ (type C), $S_B = \overline{S_A}$ (t
ype B), $S_D = \overline{S_C} = S_A$ (type D). The column constraints $A \cup C
= \{0,\ldots,4\}$ and $B \cup D = \{0,\ldots,4\}$ are automatically satisfied si
nce $S_A \cap S_C = \emptyset$ and $S_A \cup S_C = \{0,\ldots,4\}$.

The number of choices: $\binom{5}{5-k}$ for $S_A$, then $(5-k)! \cdot k! \cdot k
! \cdot (5-k)!$ for permuting within each type. Total:
$$\binom{5}{k} \cdot (5-k)! \cdot k! \cdot k! \cdot (5-k)! = 5! \cdot k! \cdot (
5-k)! = 120 \cdot k! \cdot (5-k)!$$

**Step 7: Leading digit constraint.** We need $a_9 \neq 0$, i.e., $r_9 \neq 0$.
By symmetry (each value in $\{0,1,2,3,4\}$ is equally likely at position 9, veri
fied by direct computation for both type A and type B cases), exactly $\frac{1}{
5}$ of assignments have $r_9 = 0$. So valid assignments:
$$\frac{4}{5} \cdot 120 \cdot k! \cdot (5-k)! = 96 \cdot k! \cdot (5-k)!$$

**Step 8: Counting carry sequences.** The carry sequence $c_0, \ldots, c_{10}$ w

($\binom{5}{k}$ ways). Total: $\binom{5}{k}\binom{4}{k-1}$.

**Step 9: Final sum.** The total count is:
$$\sum_{k=1}^{5} \binom{5}{k}\binom{4}{k-1} \cdot 96 \cdot k! \cdot (5-k)!$$
