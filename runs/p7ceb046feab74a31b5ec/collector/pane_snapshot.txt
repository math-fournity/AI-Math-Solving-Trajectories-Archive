# Solution

## Setup

Let $X \sim \text{Binomial}(n, p)$ with $n = 10^{14}$ (100 trillion) and $p = 0.
1$. We seek $P(X \geq 5 \times 10^{13})$ (50 trillion or more successes).

The expected value is $\mu = np = 10^{13}$, so we are asking for the probability
 that $X$ reaches **5 times its mean**.

## Chernoff Bound

For $X \sim \text{Binomial}(n,p)$, the Chernoff bound gives:

$$P(X \geq k) \leq \exp\!\bigl(-n\, D(k/n \,\|\, p)\bigr)$$

where $D(a \| p) = a\ln\!\tfrac{a}{p} + (1-a)\ln\!\tfrac{1-a}{1-p}$ is the KL di
vergence (binary relative entropy).

Here $a = k/n = 0.5$ and $p = 0.1$, so:

$$D(0.5 \| 0.1) = 0.5\ln\!\frac{0.5}{0.1} + 0.5\ln\!\frac{0.5}{0.9}$$

$$= 0.5\ln 5 + 0.5\ln\!\frac{5}{9} = 0.5\bigl(2\ln 5 - \ln 9\bigr)$$

$$= 0.5\bigl(2 \times 1.60944 - 2.19722\bigr) = 0.5 \times 1.02165 \approx 0.510
83$$

## The Bound

$$P(X \geq 5\times10^{13}) \leq \exp\!\bigl(-10^{14} \times 0.51083\bigr) = \exp
\!\bigl(-5.1083 \times 10^{13}\bigr)$$

## Interpretation

The quantity $e^{-5.1083 \times 10^{13}}$ is a number whose decimal representati
on has approximately $2.218 \times 10^{13}$ zeros after the decimal point before
 the first nonzero digit. This is not merely "small" — it is **super-astronomica
lly small**, far beyond any physically meaningful probability.

For perspective: the number of atoms in the observable universe is $\sim 10^{80}
$, and $e^{-5 \times 10^{13}} \ll 10^{-10^{13}}$, which is smaller than $1/10^{8
0}$ raised to the trillionth power.

## Lower Bound (Order Confirmation)

By Cramér's large deviation theorem, the exact probability satisfies:

$$P(X \geq 5\times10^{13}) = \exp\!\bigl(-n\,D(0.5\|0.1) + O(\ln n)\bigr)$$

so the exponential rate $-5.1083 \times 10^{13}$ is **tight** — the true probabi
lity differs from the Chernoff bound only by a polynomial factor in $n$, which i
s utterly negligible at this scale.

## Conclusion

The probability of obtaining 50 trillion or more successes is:

$$\boxed{P(X \geq 5\times10^{13}) \approx e^{-5.1083 \times 10^{13}} \approx 0}$
$

This is effectively **zero** — bounded above by $e^{-5.1083 \times 10^{13}}$, a
number so small that it has over 22 trillion leading zeros in its decimal expans
ion. The event is impossible for all practical purposes; achieving 5× the expect
ed number of successes in a $10^{14}$-trial binomial with $p=0.1$ requires a dev
iation of roughly $1.33 \times 10^{7}$ standard deviations above the mean.
