**Answer: Yes.** Every bounded sequence in $E$ has a convergent subsequence.

**Proof.**

Let us call the given property $(\star)$:

> $(\star)$ For every bounded sequence $(x_n)$ in $E$ and every $\varepsilon>0$,
 there exist $i\neq j$ with $d(x_i,x_j)<\varepsilon$.

We first establish an equivalent reformulation.

**Lemma.** Property $(\star)$ holds if and only if every bounded subset of $E$ i
s totally bounded.

*Proof of Lemma.*

$(\Rightarrow)$ Suppose, for contradiction, that $B\subseteq E$ is bounded but n
ot totally bounded. Then there exists $\varepsilon>0$ such that $B$ cannot be co
vered by finitely many open $\varepsilon$-balls. We construct a sequence $(x_n)$
 in $B$ inductively: pick $x_1\in B$; having chosen $x_1,\dots,x_n$ with $d(x_i,
x_j)\ge\varepsilon$ for all $i\neq j$, the finite union $\bigcup_{k=1}^n B(x_k,\
varepsilon)$ does not cover $B$ (otherwise $B$ would admit a finite $\varepsilon
$-net), so choose
$$x_{n+1}\in B\setminus\bigcup_{k=1}^n B(x_k,\varepsilon).$$
Then $d(x_{n+1},x_k)\ge\varepsilon$ for all $k\le n$, and the construction conti
nues indefinitely. The resulting sequence is bounded (it lies in $B$) but satisf
ies $d(x_i,x_j)\ge\varepsilon$ for all $i\neq j$. Taking $\varepsilon'=\varepsil
on/2$ in $(\star)$ yields a contradiction. Hence $B$ is totally bounded.

$(\Leftarrow)$ Suppose every bounded subset is totally bounded. Let $(x_n)$ be a
 bounded sequence with range $R=\{x_n:n\ge1\}$, and let $\varepsilon>0$. Since $
R$ is bounded, it is totally bounded, so $R\subseteq\bigcup_{k=1}^m B(c_k,\varep
silon/2)$ for some centers $c_1,\dots,c_m$. By the pigeonhole principle, two dis
tinct terms $x_i,x_j$ ($i\neq j$) lie in the same ball $B(c_k,\varepsilon/2)$, g
iving $d(x_i,x_j)<\varepsilon$. Thus $(\star)$ holds. $\square$

**Main argument.** Let $(x_n)$ be an arbitrary bounded sequence in $E$, with ran
ge $R$. By the Lemma, $R$ is totally bounded. We claim $\overline{R}$ is compact
.

- *$\overline{R}$ is totally bounded:* Given $\varepsilon>0$, total boundedness
of $R$ gives a finite $\varepsilon/2$-net $\{c_1,\dots,c_m\}\subseteq R$. For an
y $y\in\overline{R}$, there exists $x\in R$ with $d(y,x)<\varepsilon/2$, and som
e $c_k$ with $d(x,c_k)<\varepsilon/2$; hence $d(y,c_k)<\varepsilon$. So $\{c_1,\
dots,c_m\}$ is a finite $\varepsilon$-net for $\overline{R}$.
- *$\overline{R}$ is complete:* $E$ is complete and $\overline{R}$ is closed in
$E$.

A complete, totally bounded metric space is compact. Therefore $\overline{R}$ is
 compact.

Since $(x_n)$ is a sequence in the compact set $\overline{R}$, it admits a conve
rgent subsequence (compactness in metric spaces is equivalent to sequential comp
actness). $\square$

**Conclusion.** Under property $(\star)$, every bounded sequence in $E$ possesse
s a convergent subsequence. In fact, $(\star)$ is equivalent to the assertion th
at every closed bounded subset of $E$ is compact — i.e., $E$ has the **Heine–Bor
el property** — and the desired conclusion follows immediately.
