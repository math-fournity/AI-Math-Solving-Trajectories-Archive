## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, such a chain exists. We prove that $G$ is solvable using the giv
en condition (without invoking Burnside's theorem).

### Step 1: The Sylow $q$-subgroup is normal in $G$.

Let $n_q$ denote the number of Sylow $q$-subgroups of $G$. By the Sylow theorems
:
- $n_q \mid p^n$, so $n_q = p^k$ for some $0 \le k \le n$.
- $n_q \equiv 1 \pmod{q}$, so $p^k \equiv 1 \pmod{q}$.

Let $d = \operatorname{ord}_q(p)$ be the order of $[p]$ in $\mathbb{Z}_q^\times$
, i.e., the smallest positive integer with $p^d \equiv 1 \pmod{q}$. By hypothesi
s, $d > n$.

If $k > 0$, then $p^k \equiv 1 \pmod{q}$ implies $d \mid k$, so $k \ge d > n$. B
ut $k \le n$ — contradiction. Therefore $k = 0$, giving $n_q = p^0 = 1$.

Thus the unique Sylow $q$-subgroup $Q$ is **normal** in $G$.

### Step 2: Both $Q$ and $G/Q$ are solvable.

**$Q$ is solvable:** $Q$ is a $q$-group of order $q^m$. Every finite $p$-group i
s solvable. Concretely, the center $Z(Q)$ is nontrivial (a standard result for f
inite $p$-groups), so $Q$ has a central (hence normal) subgroup of order $q$. Qu
otienting by it and proceeding inductively yields a chain
$$1 = Q_0 \trianglelefteq Q_1 \trianglelefteq \cdots \trianglelefteq Q_m = Q$$
with $|Q_i / Q_{i-1}| = q$ for each $i$.

**$G/Q$ is solvable:** $|G/Q| = p^n$, so $G/Q$ is a $p$-group. By the same argum
ent, $G/Q$ has a chain
$$1 = \bar{P}_0 \trianglelefteq \bar{P}_1 \trianglelefteq \cdots \trianglelefteq
 \bar{P}_n = G/Q$$
with $|\bar{P}_i / \bar{P}_{i-1}| = p$ for each $i$.

### Step 3: $G$ is solvable (extension of solvable by solvable).

Since $Q \trianglelefteq G$ with both $Q$ and $G/Q$ solvable, $G$ is solvable. W
e construct the desired chain explicitly:

Let $\pi: G \to G/Q$ be the canonical projection. Lift the series of $G/Q$: defi
ne $P_i = \pi^{-1}(\bar{P}_i)$ for $0 \le i \le n$. Then:
$$Q = P_0 \trianglelefteq P_1 \trianglelefteq \cdots \trianglelefteq P_n = G$$
with $P_i / P_{i-1} \cong \bar{P}_i / \bar{P}_{i-1}$ of order $p$ for each $i$ (
normality is preserved under preimages of normal subgroups).

Now concatenate the series for $Q$ from Step 2:
$$1 = Q_0 \trianglelefteq Q_1 \trianglelefteq \cdots \trianglelefteq Q_m = Q = P
_0 \trianglelefteq P_1 \trianglelefteq \cdots \trianglelefteq P_n = G$$

This is a chain of subgroups of $G$ where:
- Each subgroup is normal in the next (the $Q_i$ are normal in $Q_{i+1}$ since t
he quotients are central/abelian; the $P_i$ are normal in $P_{i+1}$ by construct
ion).
- Each quotient has prime order: $|Q_i/Q_{i-1}| = q$ and $|P_i/P_{i-1}| = p$.

### Conclusion

The chain
$$1 = Q_0 \trianglelefteq Q_1 \trianglelefteq \cdots \trianglelefteq Q_m = Q = P
_0 \trianglelefteq P_1 \trianglelefteq \cdots \trianglelefteq P_n = G$$
is a subnormal series for $G$ in which every successive quotient has prime order
 ($q$ or $p$). Therefore, **yes**, such a chain exists. $\blacksquare$
