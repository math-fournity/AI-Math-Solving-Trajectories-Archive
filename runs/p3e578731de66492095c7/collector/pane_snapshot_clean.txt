## Proof

**Step 1: $M$ is symmetric.**

Using $A^T = -A$ (skew-symmetry):

$$M^T = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -A^T \\ A^T & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 &
A \\ -A & 0 \end{pmatrix} = M.$$

So $M$ is symmetric (real symmetric when $A$ is real; complex symmetric when $A$
 is complex). In the real case, the signature is well-defined since the eigenval
ues are real.

**Step 2: $M$ and $-M$ are similar.**

Let

$$R = \begin{pmatrix} I_n & 0 \\ 0 & -I_n \end{pmatrix},$$

which is invertible with $R^{-1} = R$. Compute:

$$R\,M = \begin{pmatrix} I_n & 0 \\ 0 & -I_n \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & A
\\ -A & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & A \\ A & 0 \end{pmatrix},$$

and then

$$R\,M\,R^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & A \\ A & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} I_n
 & 0 \\ 0 & -I_n \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -A \\ A & 0 \end{pmatrix} =
 -M.$$

Therefore $M$ and $-M$ are similar (over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, since $R$
 is invertible over both fields).

**Step 3: The spectrum of $M$ is symmetric about $0$.**

Similar matrices share the same characteristic polynomial, hence the same eigenv
alues with the same algebraic multiplicities. Since $M \sim -M$,

$$\operatorname{spec}(M) = \operatorname{spec}(-M) = -\operatorname{spec}(M).$$

Thus every eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $M$ is paired with $-\lambda$ having the **sa
me multiplicity**.

**Step 4: Conclusion (real case).**

When $A$ is real, $M$ is real symmetric, so all eigenvalues are real. The involu
tion $\lambda \mapsto -\lambda$ is a bijection between the positive eigenvalues
and the negative eigenvalues of $M$ that preserves multiplicities. Eigenvalue $0
$ is fixed by this map. Hence

$$\#\{\text{positive eigenvalues of } M\} = \#\{\text{negative eigenvalues of }
M\},$$

so $\operatorname{sig}(M) = 0$.

**Step 5: Complex case.**

When $A$ is complex skew-symmetric, $M$ is complex symmetric. The similarity $R\
,M\,R^{-1} = -M$ still holds over $\mathbb{C}$, so the eigenvalues of $M$ still
come in pairs $\{\lambda, -\lambda\}$ with equal multiplicities. Moreover, over
$\mathbb{C}$ the Sylvester signature of a symmetric bilinear form trivializes (s
ince $-1 = i^2$ is a square, every nonsingular symmetric form is congruent to th

n, the signature is zero.
