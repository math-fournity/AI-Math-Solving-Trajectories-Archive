## Proof

**Claim.** Let \(F:\mathcal{J}\to\mathcal{C}\) be a functor and \(N\) an object
of \(\mathcal{C}\). Whenever the colimit \(\varinjlim F\) exists in \(\mathcal{C
}\), there is a natural isomorphism of sets

\[
\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathcal{C}}\!\bigl(\varinjlim F,\; N\bigr)\;\cong\;\varprojlim_{X
\in\mathcal{J}}\,\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathcal{C}}\!\bigl(F(X),\; N\bigr),
\]

where the limit on the right is taken in \(\mathbf{Set}\) over the functor

\[
\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathcal{C}}(F(-),\,N):\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Set},\q
quad X\;\mapsto\;\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathcal{C}}(F(X),N).
\]

In words: **the covariant hom-functor \(\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathcal{C}}(-,N)\) carrie
s colimits in \(\mathcal{C}\) to limits in \(\mathbf{Set}\).** This is the state
ment that \(\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathcal{C}}(-,N)\) is **limit-preserving** (equivalen
tly, sends colimits to limits), i.e. it is a **right adjoint** in each variable.

---

### Setup

Let \((L,\{\iota_X:L\to F(X)\}_{X\in\mathcal{J}})\) be a colimit cocone of \(F\)
, so \(L=\varinjlim F\). For every morphism \(\alpha:X\to Y\) in \(\mathcal{J}\)
 we have the compatibility \(F(\alpha)\circ\iota_X=\iota_Y\)... wait — for a **c
o**cone the arrows go \(F(X)\to L\); let me fix the direction. A colimit cocone
consists of maps \(\iota_X:F(X)\to L\) with \(\iota_Y\circ F(\alpha)=\iota_X\) f
or every \(\alpha:X\to Y\) in \(\mathcal{J}\).

Applying \(\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathcal{C}}(-,N)\) (contravariant in its first argumen
t) to each \(\iota_X\) gives a map

\[
\iota_X^{*}:\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathcal{C}}(L,N)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathcal
{C}}(F(X),N),\qquad f\mapsto f\circ\iota_X.
\]

The cocone compatibility \(\iota_Y\circ F(\alpha)=\iota_X\) translates, after ap
plying \((-)^{*}\), into

\[
F(\alpha)^{*}\circ\iota_Y^{*}=\iota_X^{*},
\]

i.e. the family \(\{\iota_X^{*}\}_{X}\) is a **cone** over the diagram \(X\mapst
o\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathcal{C}}(F(X),N)\) (a functor \(\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\
mathbf{Set}\)). By the universal property of the limit, this cone factors unique
ly through a map

\[
\Phi:\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathcal{C}}(L,N)\longrightarrow\varprojlim_{X}\mathrm{Mor}_{
\mathcal{C}}(F(X),N),
\]

defined by \(\Phi(f)=(f\circ\iota_X)_{X\in\mathcal{J}}\).

---

### \(\Phi\) is bijective

**Injectivity.** Suppose \(\Phi(f)=\Phi(g)\), i.e. \(f\circ\iota_X=g\circ\iota_X
\) for all \(X\). Then \(f\) and \(g\) agree on every leg of the colimit cocone.
 By the universal property of the colimit, two maps out of \(L\) that agree on t
he entire cocone are equal: \(f=g\).

**Surjectivity.** Let \((h_X\in\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathcal{C}}(F(X),N))_{X\in\mathcal
{J}}\) be an element of the limit, i.e. a compatible family: for every \(\alpha:
X\to Y\) in \(\mathcal{J}\),

\[
h_Y\circ F(\alpha)=h_X.
\]

This compatibility is exactly the condition that the family \(\{h_X:F(X)\to N\}\
) is a **cocone** over \(F\) with vertex \(N\). By the universal property of the
 colimit \(L=\varinjlim F\), there exists a unique map \(h:L\to N\) with \(h\cir
c\iota_X=h_X\) for all \(X\). Then \(\Phi(h)=(h_X)_{X}\), so \((h_X)\) is in the
 image of \(\Phi\).

Hence \(\Phi\) is a bijection.

---

### Naturality in \(N\)

For a morphism \(u:N\to N'\) in \(\mathcal{C}\), post-composition gives a commut
ative square

\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathcal{C}}(L,N) & \xrightarrow{\Phi_N} & \varprojlim_{X}\mathrm{
Mor}_{\mathcal{C}}(F(X),N)\\[4pt]
{\scriptstyle u\circ-}\downarrow & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle u\circ-}\\[4pt]
\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathcal{C}}(L,N') & \xrightarrow{\Phi_{N'}} & \varprojlim_{X}\mat
hrm{Mor}_{\mathcal{C}}(F(X),N')
\end{array}
\]

since \((u\circ h)\circ\iota_X=u\circ(h\circ\iota_X)\) componentwise. Thus \(\Ph
i\) is natural in \(N\).

---

### Conclusion

The relationship is the canonical natural isomorphism

\[
\boxed{\;\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathcal{C}}\!\bigl(\varinjlim_{\mathcal{J}} F,\; N\bigr)
\;\cong\;\varprojlim_{X\in\mathcal{J}}\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathcal{C}}\!\bigl(F(X),\;
N\bigr)\;}
\]

exhibiting the fact that the representable functor \(\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathcal{C}}(
-,N):\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}\) **sends colimits in \(\mathcal{C
}\) to limits in \(\mathbf{Set}\)**. This is the dual of the more familiar state
ment that \(\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathcal{C}}(M,-)\) preserves limits, and both are ins
tances of the general principle that **left/right adjoints preserve colimits/lim
its** — here \(\mathrm{Mor}_{\mathcal{C}}(-,N)\) being a right adjoint (its left
 adjoint is \(-\otimes N\) when \(\mathcal{C}\) is suitably closed) preserves li
mits, dually sending colimits to limits.
