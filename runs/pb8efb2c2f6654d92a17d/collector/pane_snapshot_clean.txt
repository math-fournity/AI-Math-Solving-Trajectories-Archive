## Proof

**Reformulation.** For convex regions $M,N$ (regular polygons are convex),

$$K(M,N)=\Bigl\{\tfrac{A+B}{2}:A\in M,\,B\in N\Bigr\}=\tfrac12\,(M\oplus N),$$

where $M\oplus N=\{A+B:A\in M,B\in N\}$ is the Minkowski sum. Scaling by $\tfrac
12$ preserves regularity, so $K(M,N)$ is a regular polygonal area **iff** $M\opl
us N$ is a regular polygonal area.

**Minkowski sum of convex polygons.** For a convex polygon $P$, list its outward
 edge-normal directions $\theta_1<\cdots<\theta_r$ (cyclically) with correspondi
ng edge lengths $\ell_1,\dots,\ell_r$. For convex polygons $P,Q$:

- the edge-normal set of $P\oplus Q$ is the **union** of the two normal sets (me
rged and sorted);
- at a normal direction belonging to both $P$ and $Q$, the edge length of $P\opl
us Q$ is $\ell_P+\ell_Q$; at a direction belonging to only one, it is that polyg
on's edge length.

A polygon is regular with $L$ sides iff its $L$ edge normals are equally spaced
(gaps $2\pi/L$) **and** all $L$ edge lengths are equal.

**Setup.** Let $M$ be a regular $n$-gon with side length $s$ and edge-normal dir
ections
$$\mathcal{N}_M=\bigl\{\alpha+\tfrac{2\pi k}{n}:k=0,\dots,n-1\bigr\},$$
and $N$ a regular $m$-gon with side length $t$ and
$$\mathcal{N}_N=\bigl\{\beta+\tfrac{2\pi j}{m}:j=0,\dots,m-1\bigr\}.$$
Every edge of $M$ has length $s$; every edge of $N$ has length $t$.

For $M\oplus N$ to be regular we need:

1. **Equal spacing:** $\mathcal{U}:=\mathcal{N}_M\cup\mathcal{N}_N$ is equally s
paced, say $\mathcal{U}=\{\gamma+2\pi i/L\}$ with $L=|\mathcal{U}|$.
2. **Equal lengths:** every edge of $M\oplus N$ has the same length.

**Edge-length analysis.** An edge of $M\oplus N$ at direction $\theta\in\mathcal
 U$ has length
$$L(\theta)=s\cdot\mathbf 1_{\theta\in\mathcal N_M}+t\cdot\mathbf 1_{\theta\in\m
athcal N_N}\in\{s,\;t,\;s+t\}.$$
For all to be equal:

- If directions of all three types occur, then $s=t=s+t$, forcing $s=t=0$ (degen
erate).
- If only $\{s,\,s+t\}$ occur (i.e. $\mathcal N_N\subseteq\mathcal N_M$), then $
s=s+t$, so $t=0$ (degenerate); similarly for $\{t,\,s+t\}$.
- If only $\{s,\,t\}$ occur (directions **disjoint**), then $s=t$.
- If only $\{s+t\}$ occurs, then $\mathcal N_M=\mathcal N_N$ (all directions com
mon).

So non-degenerate regularity requires exactly one of:
- **(A)** $\mathcal N_M=\mathcal N_N$ (all normals common), or
- **(B)** $\mathcal N_M\cap\mathcal N_N=\varnothing$ and $s=t$.

**Spacing analysis.**

*Case (A):* $\mathcal N_M=\mathcal N_N$ forces $n=m$ and $\alpha\equiv\beta\pmod
{2\pi/n}$. Then $|\mathcal U|=n$, equally spaced. All edge lengths equal $s+t$.
So $M\oplus N$ is a regular $n$-gon, and $K(M,N)$ is a regular $n$-gon. ✓

*Case (B):* disjoint, so $L=|\mathcal U|=n+m$, equally spaced with gap $2\pi/(n+
m)$. Since $\mathcal N_M$ (spacing $2\pi/n$) is a subset, $2\pi/n$ must be an in
teger multiple of $2\pi/(n+m)$, i.e. $n\mid(n+m)$, i.e. $n\mid m$. Symmetrically
 $m\mid n$. Hence $n=m$, and $L=2n$.

With $n=m$, $\mathcal N_M$ occupies every other point of the $2n$-set; disjointn
ess forces $\mathcal N_N$ to be the complementary every-other-point set, i.e.
$$\beta-\alpha\equiv \tfrac{\pi}{n}\pmod{\tfrac{2\pi}{n}}.$$
Together with $s=t$, all $2n$ edge lengths equal $s$ and all gaps equal $\pi/n$.
 So $M\oplus N$ is a regular $2n$-gon, and $K(M,N)$ is a regular $2n$-gon. ✓

**No other cases.** Any partial overlap (some common, some not) produces edge le
ngths of two or three distinct values among $\{s,t,s+t\}$, all equal only degene
rately. Any $n\neq m$ with disjoint normals requires $n\mid m$ and $m\mid n$, im
possible. Any $n\neq m$ with overlap forces one normal set inside the other, giv
ing lengths $\{s,s+t\}$ or $\{t,s+t\}$, degenerate. Hence no further solutions e
xist.

**Geometric restatement.** "Same orientation" means corresponding sides are para
llel (rotation angles equal modulo $2\pi/n$); "rotated by $\pi/n$" means $N$'s v
ertices point toward $M$'s side midpoints (the dual orientation).

### Conclusion

For regular polygonal areas $M,N$ (with $n\ge 3$), $K(M,N)$ is again a regular p
olygonal area **if and only if** $M$ and $N$ are regular $n$-gons for the **same
** $n$, and one of the following holds:

1. **Same orientation** (corresponding sides parallel; $N$ is a translate of $M$
 up to rotation by a multiple of $2\pi/n$). Then $K(M,N)$ is a **regular $n$-gon
** (side lengths of $M,N$ arbitrary).

2. **Dual orientation with equal sides**: $M$ and $N$ have the **same side lengt
h** and $N$ is **rotated by $\pi/n$** relative to $M$. Then $K(M,N)$ is a **regu
lar $2n$-gon**.

(Examples: two parallel equilateral triangles $\to$ equilateral triangle; two eq
ual equilateral triangles rotated $60^\circ$ $\to$ regular hexagon; two parallel
 squares $\to$ square; two equal squares rotated $45^\circ$ $\to$ regular octago
n.)
