## Proof

Let $z = \epsilon e^{i\theta}$. The integrand is
$$\left| i z \cdot \frac{e^{i\alpha}\,e^{i\alpha z}}{(1+z)^4 - 1}\right|.$$

**Step 1: Cancel the factor $z$.** Expanding,
$$(1+z)^4 - 1 = 4z + 6z^2 + 4z^3 + z^4 = z\bigl(4 + 6z + 4z^2 + z^3\bigr).$$
For every $\epsilon > 0$ and $\theta \in [0,\pi]$, we have $z = \epsilon e^{i\th
eta} \neq 0$, so we may cancel $z$:
$$\frac{i z\, e^{i\alpha} e^{i\alpha z}}{(1+z)^4-1} = \frac{i\, e^{i\alpha}\, e^
{i\alpha z}}{4 + 6z + 4z^2 + z^3}.$$

**Step 2: Compute the modulus.** Since $z = \epsilon(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)$,
$$i\alpha z = i\alpha\epsilon\cos\theta - \alpha\epsilon\sin\theta,$$
so $\left|e^{i\alpha z}\right| = e^{-\alpha\epsilon\sin\theta}$, and $|i\,e^{i\a
lpha}| = 1$. Hence the integrand equals
$$f_\epsilon(\theta) := \frac{e^{-\alpha\epsilon\sin\theta}}{\left|4 + 6\epsilon
 e^{i\theta} + 4\epsilon^2 e^{2i\theta} + \epsilon^3 e^{3i\theta}\right|}.$$

**Step 3: Uniform convergence to $1/4$.** For the denominator, by the reverse tr
iangle inequality,
$$\left|4 + 6\epsilon e^{i\theta} + 4\epsilon^2 e^{2i\theta} + \epsilon^3 e^{3i\
theta}\right| \ge 4 - 6\epsilon - 4\epsilon^2 - \epsilon^3.$$
For $\epsilon \le 1/11$, this is $\ge 4 - 11\epsilon \ge 3 > 0$, so the denomina
tor is bounded away from zero. Moreover,
$$\left|4 + 6\epsilon e^{i\theta} + 4\epsilon^2 e^{2i\theta} + \epsilon^3 e^{3i\
theta}\right| \to 4 \quad\text{uniformly as } \epsilon\to 0^+,$$
since the error is at most $6\epsilon + 4\epsilon^2 + \epsilon^3 \to 0$.

For the numerator, $0 \le \sin\theta \le 1$ on $[0,\pi]$, so
$$e^{-\alpha\epsilon\sin\theta} \to 1 \quad\text{uniformly},$$
with error at most $|\alpha|\epsilon$ (using $|e^u - 1| \le |u|e^{|u|}$ for smal
l $|u|$, or simply $|e^{-\alpha\epsilon\sin\theta}-1|\le |\alpha|\epsilon\, e^{|
\alpha|\epsilon}$).

Therefore $f_\epsilon(\theta) \to \frac{1}{4}$ **uniformly** on $[0,\pi]$ as $\e
psilon \to 0^+$.

**Step 4: Exchange limit and integral.** Uniform convergence permits passing the
 limit inside the integral:
$$\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^+}\int_0^\pi f_\epsilon(\theta)\,d\theta = \int_0^\pi \lim
_{\epsilon\to 0^+} f_\epsilon(\theta)\,d\theta = \int_0^\pi \frac{1}{4}\,d\theta
 = \frac{\pi}{4}.$$

$$\boxed{\dfrac{\pi}{4}}$$
