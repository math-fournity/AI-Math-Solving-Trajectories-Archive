## Proof

Let $A$ be a commutative reduced ring, and let $S$ be the multiplicative set of
all non-zerodivisors of $A$. We show that the Krull dimension of $Q(A) := S^{-1}
A$ (the total ring of fractions of $A$) is **0**.

**Step 1: Identify $S$.** Since $A$ is reduced, an element $a \in A$ is a zerodi
visor if and only if $a$ lies in some minimal prime of $A$.

*Proof.* If $a$ is a zerodivisor, then $ab = 0$ for some $b \neq 0$. Since $A$ i
s reduced, $\{0\} = \bigcap_{\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Min}(A)} \mathfrak{p
}$, so $b \notin \mathfrak{p}_0$ for some minimal prime $\mathfrak{p}_0$. From $
ab = 0 \in \mathfrak{p}_0$ and $\mathfrak{p}_0$ prime, we get $a \in \mathfrak{p
}_0$. Conversely, if $a \in \mathfrak{p}_0$ for a minimal prime $\mathfrak{p}_0$
, localize at $\mathfrak{p}_0$: the ring $A_{\mathfrak{p}_0}$ has nilradical $\m
athfrak{p}_0 A_{\mathfrak{p}_0} = 0$ (since $A$ reduced $\Rightarrow$ $A_{\mathf
rak{p}_0}$ reduced and $0$-dimensional $\Rightarrow$ a field), so $a/1 = 0$ in $
A_{\mathfrak{p}_0}$, meaning $sa = 0$ for some $s \notin \mathfrak{p}_0$, hence
$s \neq 0$ and $a$ is a zerodivisor. $\square$

Thus $S = A \setminus \bigcup_{\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Min}(A)} \mathfrak
{p}$.

**Step 2: Primes of $S^{-1}A$.** The prime ideals of $S^{-1}A$ are in bijection
with the primes $\mathfrak{q}$ of $A$ satisfying $\mathfrak{q} \cap S = \emptyse
t$, i.e., $\mathfrak{q} \subseteq \bigcup_{\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Min}(A
)} \mathfrak{p}$.

**Step 3: Such primes are exactly the minimal primes.** If $\mathfrak{q} \subset
eq \bigcup_{\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Min}(A)} \mathfrak{p}$, then by **pri
me avoidance** (applied to the prime $\mathfrak{q}$ and the collection of minima
l primes), $\mathfrak{q} \subseteq \mathfrak{p}_0$ for some minimal prime $\math
frak{p}_0$. Since $\mathfrak{p}_0$ is minimal and $\mathfrak{q}$ is prime (hence
 contains a minimal prime), we get $\mathfrak{q} = \mathfrak{p}_0$.

Conversely, every minimal prime $\mathfrak{p}$ satisfies $\mathfrak{p} \cap S =
\emptyset$ by Step 1, so it survives in $S^{-1}A$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.** The prime ideals of $S^{-1}A$ correspond exactly to the
minimal primes of $A$. Each such prime $\mathfrak{p} \cdot S^{-1}A$ is both mini
mal and maximal in $S^{-1}A$ (there is no prime strictly above it, since the onl
y primes are the minimal ones). Hence every prime is maximal, and the Krull dime
nsion of $S^{-1}A$ is

$$\dim(S^{-1}A) = 0.$$
