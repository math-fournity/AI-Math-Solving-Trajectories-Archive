**Theorem.** Let $H$ inject into $G$ and $G'$ via inclusions $\varphi: H \hookri
ghtarrow G$ and $\psi: H \hookrightarrow G'$. Then the canonical homomorphisms $
G \to G *_H G'$ and $G' \to G *_H G'$ are injective.

**Proof.** We prove the normal form theorem for amalgamated products, which dire
ctly implies injectivity.

**Setup.** Choose a set $T$ of left coset representatives for $H$ in $G$ (with $
1 \in T$ representing $H$), and similarly $T'$ for $H$ in $G'$. Note that $T \ca
p H = \{1\}$ and $T' \cap H = \{1\}$.

**Definition (Normal form).** A *normal form* is a tuple $(t_1, \dots, t_n, h)$
where $h \in H$, each $t_i \in (T \setminus \{1\}) \cup (T' \setminus \{1\})$, a
nd consecutive $t_i$ come from different factors (one from $T\setminus\{1\}$, th
e next from $T'\setminus\{1\}$, or vice versa). The case $n = 0$ gives the form
$(h)$. The *basepoint* is $(1)$ (i.e., $n=0$, $h=1$).

**Claim (Normal Form Theorem).** Every element $a \in G *_H G'$ has a unique rep
resentation as a normal form $(t_1, \dots, t_n, h)$, meaning $a = t_1 t_2 \cdots
 t_n \cdot h$ in $G *_H G'$.

*Existence.* Every element of $G *_H G'$ is represented by a word in $G \cup G'$
. Each $g \in G$ decomposes as $g = t \cdot h_0$ ($t \in T$, $h_0 \in H$) and ea
ch $g' \in G'$ as $g' = t' \cdot h_0'$ ($t' \in T'$, $h_0' \in H$). Using the am
algamation relations $\varphi(h) = \psi(h)$, we move all $H$-elements to the rig
ht, combine adjacent factors from the same group, and drop identity coset repres
entatives. This reduction terminates, yielding a normal form. $\square$

*Uniqueness.* We define a left action of $G *_H G'$ on the set $\Omega$ of all n
ormal forms. It suffices to define commuting actions of $G$ and $G'$ on $\Omega$
 that agree on $H$.

**Action of $g \in G$ on $(t_1, \dots, t_n, h) \in \Omega$.** Write $g = t_g \,
h_g$ with $t_g \in T$, $h_g \in H$. The product $g \cdot t_1 \cdots t_n \, h = t
_g \, (h_g \, t_1) \cdots t_n \, h$ is re-normalized by the following *absorptio
n process*:

**Step 1 (Absorb $h_g$ into $t_1$).** The element $h_g \in H$ lies in whichever
group $t_1$ belongs to (via the appropriate inclusion). Compute $h_g \, t_1$ in
that group and decompose it as $h_g \, t_1 = t_1' \, h_1'$ with $t_1' \in T^{(\c
dot)}$, $h_1' \in H$. Since $t_1 \notin H$ (as $t_1 \neq 1$ is a coset represent
ative) and $h_g \in H$, we have $h_g t_1 \in H$ iff $t_1 \in H$, which is false.
 So $t_1' \neq 1$.

**Step 2 (Handle the prefix).**
- If $t_g = 1$: set prefix $= [t_1']$, carry $h_{\mathrm{curr}} = h_1'$.
- If $t_g \neq 1$ and $t_1 \in T'\setminus\{1\}$ (different group from $G$): set
 prefix $= [t_g, t_1']$ (different groups, valid), carry $h_{\mathrm{curr}} = h_
1'$.
- If $t_g \neq 1$ and $t_1 \in T\setminus\{1\}$ (same group $G$): combine $t_g \
, t_1' = t'' \, h''$ (coset decomposition in $G$). If $t'' \neq 1$: set prefix $
= [t'']$, carry $h_{\mathrm{curr}} = h'' h_1'$. If $t'' = 1$: set prefix $= []$,
 carry $h_{\mathrm{curr}} = h'' h_1'$.

**Step 3 (Absorb into remaining factors).** For $i = 2, \dots, n$: compute $h_{\
mathrm{curr}} \, t_i$ in the group of $t_i$, decompose as $t_i' \, h_i'$ with $t
_i' \neq 1$ (same argument: $t_i \notin H$). Append $t_i'$ to the prefix. Set $h
_{\mathrm{curr}} = h_i'$.

The alternation of the original form guarantees that each newly appended $t_i'$
comes from a different group than the last prefix element, so no further combini
ng is needed beyond Step 2.

**Step 4 (Finalize).** The resulting normal form is $(\text{prefix}, \, h_{\math
rm{curr}} \cdot h)$.

The action of $G'$ is defined analogously (swapping the roles of $G, T$ and $G',
 T'$). For $n = 0$ (form $(h_0)$), the action of $g = t_g h_g$ gives $(t_g, \, h
_g h_0)$ if $t_g \neq 1$, or $(h_g h_0)$ if $t_g = 1$.

**Compatibility on $H$.** If $h_0 \in H$ acts as an element of $G$, then $h_0 =
1 \cdot h_0$ (coset decomposition in $G$, so $t_g = 1$). If $h_0$ acts as an ele
ment of $G'$, likewise $h_0 = 1 \cdot h_0$ (so $t_g = 1$). In both cases, Step 2
 gives prefix $= [t_1']$ and $h_{\mathrm{curr}} = h_1'$, where $t_1'$ and $h_1'$
 are determined by absorbing $h_0$ into $t_1$. This absorption computes $h_0 \,
t_1$ in the group to which $t_1$ belongs, using the inclusion $H \hookrightarrow
$ (that group) — which is the same inclusion regardless of whether $h_0$ entered
 via $G$ or $G'$. Hence the two actions agree on $H$, and we obtain a well-defin
ed action of $G *_H G'$ on $\Omega$. $\square$

**Faithfulness at the basepoint.** Let $a = t_1 t_2 \cdots t_n h$ be a non-ident
ity normal form (so $n \geq 1$ or $h \neq 1$). We compute $a \cdot (1)$ (the bas
epoint) by applying factors from right to left (left action: $(ab)\cdot x = a \c
dot (b \cdot x)$):

- $h \cdot (1) = (h)$.
- $t_n \cdot (h)$: since $n=0$ case applies to $(h)$ and $t_n = t_n \cdot 1$, we
 get $(t_n, h)$ (as $t_n \neq 1$).
- $t_{n-1} \cdot (t_n, h)$: $t_{n-1}$ is from a different group than $t_n$, so i
t is prepended: $(t_{n-1}, t_n, h)$.
- Continuing: $t_1 \cdot (t_2, \dots, t_n, h) = (t_1, t_2, \dots, t_n, h)$.

(At each step, the acting $t_i$ is from a different group than the first element
 of the current form, so $t_g = t_i \neq 1$ and we are in the "prepend" case wit
h no absorption combining needed, since $h_g = 1$.)

The result is $(t_1, \dots, t_n, h) \neq (1)$, since $n \geq 1$ or $h \neq 1$. T
herefore $a$ does not fix the basepoint, so $a \neq 1$ in $G *_H G'$. This prove
s uniqueness of normal forms. $\square$

**Injectivity of $G \to G *_H G'$.** Let $g \in G$, $g \neq 1$. Write $g = t \,
h$ with $t \in T$, $h \in H$.

- If $t \neq 1$: the normal form of the image of $g$ is $(t, h)$, which is non-t
rivial ($n = 1 \geq 1$).
- If $t = 1$: then $g = h \in H$, $h \neq 1$. The normal form is $(h)$ (i.e., $n
 = 0$, $h \neq 1$), which is non-trivial by faithfulness: $h \cdot (1) = (h) \ne
q (1)$.

In both cases, the image of $g$ in $G *_H G'$ is non-trivial. Hence the canonica
l map $G \to G *_H G'$ is injective. By symmetry, $G' \to G *_H G'$ is also inje
ctive. $\blacksquare$

---

**Answer:** Yes, the canonical map $G \to G *_H G'$ is always injective. This is
 the *embedding theorem* for amalgamated free products: both factor groups embed
 into their amalgamated product over a common subgroup.
