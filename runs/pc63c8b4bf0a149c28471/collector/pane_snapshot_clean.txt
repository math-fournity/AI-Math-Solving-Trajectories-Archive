## Proof

We compute
$$L := \liminf_{n\to\infty} \frac{p_n}{p_n+p_{n+1}}.$$

**Step 1. Rewrite the ratio.** Since $p_n>0$,
$$\frac{p_n}{p_n+p_{n+1}} = \frac{1}{1+\dfrac{p_{n+1}}{p_n}}.$$
So it suffices to understand the behavior of $p_{n+1}/p_n$.

**Step 2. Apply the Prime Number Theorem.** The PNT gives $p_n \sim n\log n$, i.
e.
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{p_n}{n\log n}=1.$$
Consequently,
$$\frac{p_{n+1}}{p_n} = \frac{p_{n+1}}{(n+1)\log(n+1)}\cdot\frac{(n+1)\log(n+1)}
{n\log n}\cdot\frac{n\log n}{p_n}.$$
The first and third factors tend to $1$ by the PNT. For the middle factor,
$$\frac{(n+1)\log(n+1)}{n\log n} = \left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)\cdot\frac{\log(n+1
)}{\log n}\longrightarrow 1\cdot 1 = 1,$$
since $\log(n+1)-\log n = \log(1+1/n)\to 0$. Hence
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{p_{n+1}}{p_n}=1.$$

**Step 3. Conclude.** The function $x\mapsto \frac{1}{1+x}$ is continuous at $x=
1$, so
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{p_n}{p_n+p_{n+1}} = \frac{1}{1+1} = \frac{1}{2}.$$
Since the full limit exists and equals $\frac12$, the $\liminf$ equals the same
value:
$$\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{p_n}{p_n+p_{n+1}} = \frac{1}{2}.$$
