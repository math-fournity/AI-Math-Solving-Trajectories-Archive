## Solution

**Problem.** Solve
$$u_t = a^2 \frac{1}{r}(r\,u_r)_r, \quad 0 \le r < b,\; t>0,$$
with $\lim_{r\to 0}|u|<\infty$, $u(b,t)=\varphi(t)$, $u(r,0)=0$.

---

### Step 1. Reduce to homogeneous boundary conditions

Set
$$u(r,t) = v(r,t) + \varphi(t).$$
Since $\varphi(t)$ is independent of $r$, we have $\frac{1}{r}(r\,\varphi_r)_r =
 0$. Substituting:

$$v_t + \varphi'(t) = a^2 \frac{1}{r}(r\,v_r)_r,$$

so $v$ satisfies the **nonhomogeneous PDE with homogeneous BCs**:

$$v_t = a^2 \frac{1}{r}(r\,v_r)_r - \varphi'(t), \quad v(b,t)=0, \quad v \text{
bounded at } r=0, \quad v(r,0) = -\varphi(0).$$

---

### Step 2. Eigenfunction expansion

The associated homogeneous problem $w_t = a^2 \frac{1}{r}(r\,w_r)_r$ with $w(b,t
)=0$, $w$ bounded at $r=0$, has eigenfunctions $J_0\!\left(\frac{\mu_n r}{b}\rig
ht)$, where $\mu_n$ ($n=1,2,\dots$) are the positive zeros of $J_0$ (i.e., $J_0(
\mu_n)=0$), with eigenvalues

$$\omega_n = \frac{a^2 \mu_n^2}{b^2}.$$

These eigenfunctions are orthogonal on $[0,b]$ with weight $r$, and

$$\int_0^b r\, J_0^2\!\left(\tfrac{\mu_n r}{b}\right) dr = \frac{b^2}{2}\,J_1^2(
\mu_n).$$

Expand:
$$v(r,t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} T_n(t)\, J_0\!\left(\frac{\mu_n r}{b}\right).$$

We also expand the constant function $1$ (independent of $r$) in this basis. Usi
ng $\frac{d}{dx}[xJ_1(x)]=xJ_0(x)$:

$$\int_0^b r\, J_0\!\left(\tfrac{\mu_n r}{b}\right) dr = \frac{b^2}{\mu_n^2}\int
_0^{\mu_n} x\,J_0(x)\,dx = \frac{b^2}{\mu_n^2}\bigl[xJ_1(x)\bigr]_0^{\mu_n} = \f
rac{b^2\,J_1(\mu_n)}{\mu_n}.$$

Hence the Fourier–Bessel coefficients of $f\equiv 1$ are

$$c_n = \frac{b^2 J_1(\mu_n)/\mu_n}{b^2 J_1^2(\mu_n)/2} = \frac{2}{\mu_n\, J_1(\
mu_n)},$$

giving the expansion (valid for $0 \le r < b$):

$$1 = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2}{\mu_n\, J_1(\mu_n)}\, J_0\!\left(\frac{\mu_n
r}{b}\right). \tag{$\ast$}$$

---

### Step 3. ODEs for the coefficients

Substituting the expansion of $v$ and of $\varphi'(t)\cdot 1 = \varphi'(t)\sum c
_n J_0(\mu_n r/b)$ into the PDE for $v$, and using $\frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}\!\le
ft(r\frac{d}{dr}J_0(\mu_n r/b)\right) = -\frac{\mu_n^2}{b^2}J_0(\mu_n r/b)$, we
equate coefficients:

$$T_n'(t) + \omega_n\, T_n(t) = -\frac{2\,\varphi'(t)}{\mu_n\, J_1(\mu_n)}.$$

The initial condition $v(r,0)=-\varphi(0)$ gives, via $(\ast)$:

$$T_n(0) = -\frac{2\,\varphi(0)}{\mu_n\, J_1(\mu_n)}.$$

Solving the first-order linear ODE:

$$T_n(t) = -\frac{2}{\mu_n\, J_1(\mu_n)}\left[\varphi(0)\,e^{-\omega_n t} + \int
_0^t e^{-\omega_n(t-\tau)}\,\varphi'(\tau)\,d\tau\right].$$

---

### Step 4. Simplify via integration by parts

Integrate by parts: with $f=e^{-\omega_n(t-\tau)}$, $g'=\varphi'(\tau)$:

$$\int_0^t e^{-\omega_n(t-\tau)}\varphi'(\tau)\,d\tau = \varphi(t) - e^{-\omega_
n t}\varphi(0) - \omega_n\int_0^t e^{-\omega_n(t-\tau)}\varphi(\tau)\,d\tau.$$

Therefore the bracket becomes:

$$\varphi(0)\,e^{-\omega_n t} + \varphi(t) - e^{-\omega_n t}\varphi(0) - \omega_
n\int_0^t e^{-\omega_n(t-\tau)}\varphi(\tau)\,d\tau = \varphi(t) - \omega_n\int_
0^t e^{-\omega_n(t-\tau)}\varphi(\tau)\,d\tau,$$

and

$$T_n(t) = -\frac{2\,\varphi(t)}{\mu_n\, J_1(\mu_n)} + \frac{2\,\omega_n}{\mu_n\
, J_1(\mu_n)}\int_0^t e^{-\omega_n(t-\tau)}\varphi(\tau)\,d\tau.$$

---

### Step 5. Recover $u$ and use the expansion $(\ast)$

Since $u = v + \varphi(t)$, write $\varphi(t) = \varphi(t)\cdot 1 = \varphi(t)\s
um_n c_n J_0(\mu_n r/b)$ (for $r<b$) using $(\ast)$:

$$u(r,t) = \varphi(t)\sum_n \frac{2}{\mu_n J_1(\mu_n)} J_0\!\left(\frac{\mu_n r}
{b}\right) + \sum_n T_n(t)\, J_0\!\left(\frac{\mu_n r}{b}\right) = \sum_n \left[
\frac{2\varphi(t)}{\mu_n J_1(\mu_n)} + T_n(t)\right] J_0\!\left(\frac{\mu_n r}{b
}\right).$$

The $\varphi(t)$ terms cancel:

$$\frac{2\varphi(t)}{\mu_n J_1(\mu_n)} + T_n(t) = \frac{2\,\omega_n}{\mu_n\, J_1
(\mu_n)}\int_0^t e^{-\omega_n(t-\tau)}\varphi(\tau)\,d\tau.$$

Since $\omega_n = a^2\mu_n^2/b^2$, we get $\frac{2\omega_n}{\mu_n J_1(\mu_n)} =
\frac{2a^2\mu_n}{b^2\,J_1(\mu_n)}$.

---

### Final Answer

For $0 \le r < b$ and $t > 0$:

$$\boxed{u(r,t) = \frac{2a^2}{b^2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\mu_n}{J_1(\mu_n)}\,
 J_0\!\left(\frac{\mu_n\, r}{b}\right) \int_0^t e^{-\frac{a^2 \mu_n^2}{b^2}(t-\t
au)}\,\varphi(\tau)\,d\tau,}$$

where $\mu_1 < \mu_2 < \cdots$ are the positive zeros of the Bessel function $J_
0$, and $J_1$ is the Bessel function of the first kind of order $1$.
