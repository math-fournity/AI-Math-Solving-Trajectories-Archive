## Solution

**Answer:** No nonzero compactly supported smooth function $\varphi$ has this pr
operty. (The identically zero function works trivially, but no nontrivial one do
es.)

---

**Proof.** Write the integral as
$$
I(x)=\int_{\mathbb R}\varphi(t)\,e^{itx}e^{tx}\,dt=\int_{\mathbb R}\varphi(t)\,e
^{(1+i)xt}\,dt.
$$
This is the bilateral Laplace transform $F(s)=\int\varphi(t)e^{st}dt$ evaluated
along the ray $s=(1+i)x$, $x\in\mathbb R$. Since $\varphi$ is compactly supporte
d, $F$ is an entire function, and $I(x)=F((1+i)x)$.

Let $\varphi\not\equiv 0$ be smooth and compactly supported, with $\operatorname
{supp}\varphi\subseteq[a,b]$, $a<b$ (the support has nonempty interior, since a
smooth function supported at a single point is identically zero). Let $m_b$ be t
he order of vanishing of $\varphi$ at $b$ (i.e. $\varphi^{(j)}(b)=0$ for $j<m_b$
, $\varphi^{(m_b)}(b)\neq 0$), and $m_a$ the order of vanishing at $a$.

**Behavior as $x\to+\infty$.** Here $\Re s=x\to+\infty$, so $e^{xt}$ is maximize
d on $[a,b]$ at $t=b$. By repeated integration by parts (or Watson-type lemma),
the integral is dominated by the contribution near $t=b$. Setting $u=b-t$:
$$
I(x)=(-1)^{m_b}e^{(1+i)xb}\cdot\frac{\varphi^{(m_b)}(b)}{m!}\int_0^{b-a}u^{m_b}e
^{-(1+i)xu}\,du\;+\;\text{lower order},
$$
and for $x\to+\infty$,
$$
\int_0^\infty u^{m_b}e^{-(1+i)xu}\,du=\frac{m_b!}{\big((1+i)x\big)^{m_b+1}}.
$$
Hence
$$
|I(x)|\sim \frac{|\varphi^{(m_b)}(b)|}{|1+i|^{m_b+1}}\cdot\frac{e^{xb}}{x^{m_b+1
}}=\frac{|\varphi^{(m_b)}(b)|}{2^{(m_b+1)/2}}\cdot\frac{e^{xb}}{x^{m_b+1}}.
$$

**Behavior as $x\to-\infty$.** Now $\Re s=x\to-\infty$, so $e^{xt}$ is maximized
 at $t=a$. A symmetric computation near $t=a$ (with $u=t-a$) gives
$$
|I(x)|\sim \frac{|\varphi^{(m_a)}(a)|}{2^{(m_a+1)/2}}\cdot\frac{e^{xa}}{|x|^{m_a
+1}},\qquad x\to-\infty.
$$

**Conclusion by cases.** Since $a<b$ and $\varphi\not\equiv 0$:

- If $b>0$: the asymptotic for $x\to+\infty$ gives $|I(x)|\sim C\,e^{xb}/x^{m_b+
1}\to\infty$. Unbounded.
- If $a<0$: the asymptotic for $x\to-\infty$ gives $|I(x)|\sim C'\,e^{xa}/|x|^{m
_a+1}\to\infty$ (since $xa\to+\infty$). Unbounded.
- The only way to avoid both is $b\le 0$ **and** $a\ge 0$, i.e. $a\ge 0\ge b$, w
hich forces $a=b=0$ — but then the support is $\{0\}$, forcing $\varphi\equiv 0$
.

Therefore every nonzero compactly supported smooth $\varphi$ yields an unbounded
 $I(x)$. No nontrivial such function exists. $\blacksquare$
