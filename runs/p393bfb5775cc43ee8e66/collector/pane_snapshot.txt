## Solution

**Problem.** Evaluate $\int_C \frac{f(z)}{z^3}\,dz$, where $f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\in
fty}k^4\!\left(\frac{z}{4}\right)^k$ and $C$ is the positively oriented circle $
|z|=\pi$.

**Answer-leak check.** The problem statement contains only the problem; no solut
ion, hint, or answer value is embedded. Proceeding.

---

**Step 1. Radius of convergence.** The series $f(z)=\sum_{k\ge 0} k^4(z/4)^k$ ha
s coefficients $a_k = k^4/4^k$. By the ratio test,
$$
\lim_{k\to\infty}\left|\frac{a_{k+1}}{a_k}\right|=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{(k+1)^4
}{4\,k^4}=\frac14,
$$
so the radius of convergence is $R=4$. Since $\pi < 4$, the circle $C:|z|=\pi$ a
nd its interior lie strictly inside the disk of analyticity of $f$. Hence $f$ is
 analytic on and inside $C$.

**Step 2. Apply Cauchy's integral formula.** Since $f$ is analytic inside $C$ an
d $0$ lies in the interior, Cauchy's differentiation formula with $n=2$ and $a=0
$ gives
$$
\oint_C \frac{f(z)}{(z-0)^{2+1}}\,dz = \frac{2\pi i}{2!}\,f''(0)=\pi i\,f''(0).
$$

**Step 3. Compute $f''(0)$.** Writing $f(z)=\sum_{k\ge 0}\frac{k^4}{4^k}z^k$, te
rmwise differentiation (valid inside the radius of convergence) yields
$$
f''(z)=\sum_{k\ge 2}\frac{k^4}{4^k}\,k(k-1)\,z^{k-2}.
$$
Evaluating at $z=0$ keeps only the $k=2$ term:
$$
f''(0)=\frac{2^4}{4^2}\cdot 2\cdot 1 = \frac{16}{16}\cdot 2 = 2.
$$

**Step 4. Conclusion.**
$$
\int_C \frac{f(z)}{z^3}\,dz = \pi i \cdot f''(0) = \pi i \cdot 2 = 2\pi i.
$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_C \frac{f(z)}{z^{3}}\,dz = 2\pi i.}$$
