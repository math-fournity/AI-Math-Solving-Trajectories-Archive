**Claim.** Yes. If $G$ is a locally compact Hausdorff group and $H\subseteq G$ i
s a closed subgroup with positive (left) Haar measure, then $H$ is open.

**Proof.** Let $\mu$ be a left Haar measure on $G$. Assume $\mu(H)>0$.

*Step 1: extract a compact subset of $H$ with positive measure.* Haar measure on
 a locally compact group is inner regular on sets of finite measure (and on $\si
gma$-finite Borel sets). Since $\mu(H)>0$, there exists a compact set $K\subsete
q H$ with $\mu(K)>0$. (Compact sets always have finite Haar measure, so $\mu(K)<
\infty$.)

*Step 2: Steinhaus lemma.* We use the following standard fact.

> **Lemma (Steinhaus).** Let $G$ be a locally compact group with left Haar measu
re $\mu$, and let $A\subseteq G$ be a Borel set with $0<\mu(A)<\infty$. Then $AA
^{-1}$ contains an open neighborhood of the identity.

*Proof of the lemma.* By inner regularity pick compact $K\subseteq A$ with $\mu(
K)>0$. Choose a symmetric open neighborhood $V_0$ of $e$ with compact closure. T
hen $V_0K$ is compact (hence has finite measure) and contains $K$. As $V$ ranges
 over symmetric open neighborhoods of $e$ with $\overline V\subseteq V_0$, the s
ets $VK$ decrease to $K$ (because $K$ is compact: $\bigcap_V VK = K$). By contin
uity from above, $\mu(VK)\downarrow\mu(K)$. Hence we may choose such a $V$ with
$$\mu(VK)<2\mu(K).$$
Now take any $g\in V$. Since $g\in V$, we have $gK\subseteq VK$. By left-invaria
nce, $\mu(gK)=\mu(K)$. If $gK\cap K=\varnothing$, then
$$\mu(VK)\ge\mu(gK\cup K)=\mu(gK)+\mu(K)=2\mu(K),$$
contradicting the choice of $V$. Therefore $gK\cap K\neq\varnothing$ for every $
g\in V$: there exist $k_1,k_2\in K$ with $gk_1=k_2$, i.e. $g=k_2k_1^{-1}\in KK^{
-1}\subseteq AA^{-1}$. Thus $V\subseteq AA^{-1}$, proving the lemma. $\square$

*Step 3: conclude $H$ is open.* Apply the lemma to $A=K$ (which has $0<\mu(K)<\i
nfty$). We obtain that $KK^{-1}$ contains an open neighborhood $V$ of $e$. Since
 $K\subseteq H$ and $H$ is a subgroup,
$$KK^{-1}\subseteq HH^{-1}=H.$$
Hence $V\subseteq H$: $H$ contains an open neighborhood of the identity. For any
 $h\in H$, the left translate $hV$ is an open neighborhood of $h$ contained in $
H$ (using that $H$ is a subgroup). Thus every point of $H$ is an interior point,
 so $H$ is open. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The hypotheses are all used: local compactness gives the compact $K$
 via inner regularity and the neighborhoods with compact closure; the Hausdorff
property ensures compact sets are closed (used implicitly in the decreasing-inte
rsection argument $\bigcap VK=K$); closedness of $H$ is automatic for a subgroup
 of positive measure but is stated for clarity; and positivity of $\mu(H)$ is es
sential — a measure-zero closed subgroup (e.g. $\{0\}\subset\mathbb R$, or $\mat
hbb Z\subset\mathbb R$) need not be open.
