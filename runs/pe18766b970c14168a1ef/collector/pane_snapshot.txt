## Proof

We determine the cardinality of a Hamel basis of $\mathbb{R}$ viewed as a vector
 space over $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Step 1: The basis is infinite.**

$\mathbb{R}$ is uncountable, while every finite-dimensional vector space over th
e countable field $\mathbb{Q}$ is countable (a finite product of countable sets)
. Hence $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}$ is infinite. Let $B$ be a Hamel basis and
let $\kappa = |B|$.

**Step 2: Upper bound — $\kappa \le |\mathbb{R}| = 2^{\aleph_0}$.**

Every real number is a *finite* $\mathbb{Q}$-linear combination of elements of $
B$. Thus

$$\mathbb{R} = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} \left\{ \sum_{i=1}^{n} q_i b_i : q_i \in \
mathbb{Q},\; b_i \in B \right\}.$$

For each fixed $n$, the set of such linear combinations has cardinality at most
$|\mathbb{Q}|^n \cdot |B|^n = \aleph_0^n \cdot \kappa^n = \kappa$ (using $\kappa
$ infinite). The countable union over $n$ gives $|\mathbb{R}| \le \aleph_0 \cdot
 \kappa = \kappa$. Since also $\kappa \le |\mathbb{R}|$ (the basis injects into
$\mathbb{R}$), we get

$$\kappa = |\mathbb{R}| = 2^{\aleph_0}.$$

s cleanly to establish *both* inequalities rigorously.

**Clean argument.**

*Lower bound:* $B \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, so $\kappa = |B| \le |\mathbb{R}| = 2^{
\aleph_0}$.

*Upper bound:* Every element of $\mathbb{R}$ is a finite $\mathbb{Q}$-linear com
bination of basis elements. The set of all finite subsets of $B$ has cardinality
 $|[B]^{<\omega}| = \kappa$ (for infinite $\kappa$). For each finite subset $F \
subseteq B$ of size $n$, the set of $\mathbb{Q}$-linear combinations supported o
n $F$ has cardinality $|\mathbb{Q}|^n = \aleph_0$. Hence

$$|\mathbb{R}| \le \kappa \cdot \aleph_0 = \kappa.$$

Combining: $2^{\aleph_0} = |\mathbb{R}| \le \kappa \le |\mathbb{R}| = 2^{\aleph_
0}$, so

$$\boxed{\kappa = 2^{\aleph_0}}.$$

**Step 3: Existence of the basis (Zorn's lemma).**

The collection of $\mathbb{Q}$-linearly independent subsets of $\mathbb{R}$, ord
ered by inclusion, is nonempty (e.g. $\{1\}$) and every chain has an upper bound
 (its union, which is linearly independent since any finite dependence would occ
ur within one element of the chain). By Zorn's lemma a maximal element $B$ exist
s, and maximality is equivalent to $B$ spanning $\mathbb{R}$, so $B$ is a Hamel
basis. The cardinality computation above applies to any such basis.

**Conclusion.** Every Hamel basis of $\mathbb{R}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ has cardinal
ity $2^{\aleph_0}$, the cardinality of the continuum.
