*Proof.* Call the right side Σ. Σ is a σ-algebra: it contains ∅; it's closed und

hbb{N})$, collect all the $A_{kn}$ into one countable family $\{B_m\}$, and $\bi
gcup_k E_k \in \sigma(\bar{B}_m : m\in\mathbb{N}) \subseteq \Sigma$). Since Σ co

### Step 2: Free ultrafilters on countable sets are not countably generated

### Step 3: Atoms of σ($\bar{A}_n : n \in \mathbb{N}$) are large or principal

For a countable family $\{A_n\}$, the atoms of $\sigma(\bar{A}_n : n\in\mathbb{N

- If $F$ is inconsistent, the atom is empty.
- If $F$ contains a finite set, it extends to at most one (principal) ultrafilte

- If $F$ is free and consistent, then by Step 2, $F$ is **not** an ultrafilter (
it's countably generated), so it has $2^{\aleph_0}$ ultrafilter extensions. The
atom has cardinality $2^{\aleph_0}$.

### Step 4: Singletons of free ultrafilters are not in σ(clopen)

Let $p \in \Gamma^* = \beta\Gamma \setminus \Gamma$ be a free ultrafilter. By St

The singleton $\{p\}$ is closed in βΓ (Hausdorff), hence $\{p\} \in \text{Borel}
(\beta\Gamma)$. But $\{p\} \notin \sigma(\text{clopen})$ by Step 4. Therefore:

$$\sigma(\text{clopen sets of } \beta\Gamma) \neq \text{Borel}(\beta\Gamma).$$

The σ-algebra generated by clopen sets is strictly smaller than the Borel σ-alge
bra. $\blacksquare$
