## Derivation of the Surface Gravity Formula

**Setup.** Let $\Sigma$ be a Killing horizon for the Killing vector $\chi^\mu$,
meaning $\chi^\mu\chi_\mu=0$ on $\Sigma$ and $\chi^\mu$ is normal to $\Sigma$. T
he surface gravity $\kappa$ is defined on $\Sigma$ by

$$\chi^\mu\nabla_\mu\chi^\nu=-\kappa\,\chi^\nu. \tag{def}$$

Killing's equation gives $\nabla_{(\mu}\chi_{\nu)}=0$, i.e. $\nabla_\mu\chi_\nu=
-\nabla_\nu\chi_\mu$ (the field tensor $F_{\mu\nu}:=\nabla_\mu\chi_\nu$ is antis
ymmetric).

### Step 1 — The fundamental identity

Using Killing antisymmetry $\nabla_\mu\chi_\nu=-\nabla_\nu\chi_\mu$:

$$\chi^\mu\nabla_\mu\chi_\nu=-\chi^\mu\nabla_\nu\chi_\mu=-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla_\nu
(\chi^\mu\chi_\mu)=-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla_\nu(\chi\!\cdot\!\chi).$$

Raising $\nu$:

$$\boxed{\;\chi^\mu\nabla_\mu\chi^\nu=-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla^\nu(\chi\!\cdot\!\chi)
\;} \tag{1}$$

This holds **everywhere**, not just on $\Sigma$.

### Step 2 — Gradient of the norm

Comparing (1) with the defining relation (def) on $\Sigma$:

$$-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla^\nu(\chi\!\cdot\!\chi)=-\kappa\,\chi^\nu,$$

$$\boxed{\;\nabla^\nu(\chi\!\cdot\!\chi)=2\kappa\,\chi^\nu\quad\text{on }\Sigma\

or with lowered index: $\nabla_\mu(\chi\!\cdot\!\chi)=2\kappa\,\chi_\mu$.

### Step 3 — Hypersurface orthogonality $\Rightarrow$ vanishing twist

Equation (2) shows $\chi_\mu=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\nabla_\mu(\chi\!\cdot\!\chi)$ on

$$\chi_{[\alpha}\nabla_\beta\chi_{\gamma]}=0 \quad\text{on }\Sigma. \tag{3}$$

Equivalently, the twist $\omega_\mu:=\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\,\chi^\nu\n
abla^\alpha\chi^\beta$ vanishes on $\Sigma$.
