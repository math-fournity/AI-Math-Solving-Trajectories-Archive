## Proof

**Answer:** The only such function is the constant function $f \equiv 1$.

**Verification that $f \equiv 1$ works.** It is entire (hence analytic on $B(0,1
)$), satisfies $f(0)=1$, and $|f(z)|=1\geq 1$ for all $z$. So it is admissible.

**Uniqueness.** Let $f$ satisfy the hypotheses. Since $|f(z)|\geq 1>0$ for every
 $|z|<1$, the function $f$ has **no zeros** in the open unit disk $D:=B(0,1)$. T
herefore the reciprocal
$$
g(z):=\frac{1}{f(z)}
$$
is well defined and analytic on $D$. Moreover, for every $z\in D$,
$$
|g(z)|=\frac{1}{|f(z)|}\leq 1,
$$
and at the origin
$$
g(0)=\frac{1}{f(0)}=\frac{1}{1}=1,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad |g(0)|=1.
$$

Thus $g$ is analytic on the connected open set $D$, satisfies $|g(z)|\leq 1$ on
$D$, and **attains the value of its maximum modulus at the interior point** $z=0
$:
$$
|g(0)|=1=\sup_{z\in D}|g(z)|.
$$

By the **maximum modulus principle**, an analytic function on a connected open s
et that attains its maximum modulus at an interior point must be constant. Hence
 $g\equiv g(0)=1$ on $D$, and consequently
$$
f(z)=\frac{1}{g(z)}\equiv 1\qquad\text{for all }|z|<1.
$$

The problem only constrains $f$ on $B(0,1)$ (analyticity, $f(0)=1$, and the modu
lus bound are all conditions inside the disk); the values of $f$ outside the dis
k are unrestricted. Hence the full set of solutions is

$$
\boxed{\,f\equiv 1\text{ on }B(0,1)\,}
$$

with $f$ arbitrary on $\mathbb{C}\setminus B(0,1)$.
