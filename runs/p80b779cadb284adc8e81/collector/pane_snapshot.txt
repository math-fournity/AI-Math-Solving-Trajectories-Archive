## Singular Points of the Steiner Quartic Surface

Let $F = T_0^2 T_1^2 + T_1^2 T_2^2 + T_0^2 T_2^2 - T_0 T_1 T_2 T_3 \in K[T_0,T_1
,T_2,T_3]$, and let $X = V(F) \subset \mathbb{P}^3(K)$.

A point $P = [T_0:T_1:T_2:T_3] \in X$ is singular if and only if all four partia
l derivatives vanish at $P$:
$$\frac{\partial F}{\partial T_0} = 2T_0T_1^2 + 2T_0T_2^2 - T_1T_2T_3,$$
$$\frac{\partial F}{\partial T_1} = 2T_1T_0^2 + 2T_1T_2^2 - T_0T_2T_3,$$
$$\frac{\partial F}{\partial T_2} = 2T_2T_1^2 + 2T_2T_0^2 - T_0T_1T_3,$$
$$\frac{\partial F}{\partial T_3} = -T_0T_1T_2.$$

### Step 1. The condition $\partial F/\partial T_3 = 0$

From $\partial F/\partial T_3 = -T_0 T_1 T_2 = 0$, at least one of $T_0, T_1, T_
2$ must be zero.

### Step 2. Case $T_0 = 0$

The remaining equations become:
$$\partial F/\partial T_0 = -T_1 T_2 T_3 = 0,$$
$$\partial F/\partial T_1 = 2T_1 T_2^2 = 0,$$
$$\partial F/\partial T_2 = 2T_2 T_1^2 = 0.$$

From $\partial F/\partial T_1 = 0$: either $T_1 = 0$ or $T_2 = 0$.

- **If $T_1 = 0$**: the point is $[0:0:T_2:T_3]$, and since $F = 0$ identically
on $\{T_0 = T_1 = 0\}$, every point on the line $L_1 = \{T_0 = T_1 = 0\}$ is sin
gular.
- **If $T_2 = 0$** (with $T_1 \neq 0$): the point is $[0:T_1:0:T_3]$, and $F = 0
$ identically on $\{T_0 = T_2 = 0\}$, so every point on the line $L_2 = \{T_0 =
T_2 = 0\}$ is singular.

### Step 3. Case $T_1 = 0$, $T_0 \neq 0$

The equations become:
$$\partial F/\partial T_0 = 2T_0 T_2^2 = 0 \implies T_2 = 0,$$
$$\partial F/\partial T_2 = 2T_2 T_0^2 = 0 \quad (\text{consistent}).$$

So $T_2 = 0$, giving points $[T_0:0:0:T_3]$ on the line $L_3 = \{T_1 = T_2 = 0\}
$. Again $F = 0$ identically on this line, so every point of $L_3$ is singular.

### Step 4. Case $T_2 = 0$, $T_0, T_1 \neq 0$

Then $\partial F/\partial T_0 = 2T_0 T_1^2 = 0$ forces $T_1 = 0$, contradicting
$T_1 \neq 0$. No new singular points.

### Step 5. All three variables nonzero is impossible

If $T_0 T_1 T_2 \neq 0$, then $\partial F/\partial T_3 = -T_0 T_1 T_2 \neq 0$, s
o no singular point arises.

### Verification

Each of the three lines lies on $X$: on $\{T_0 = T_1 = 0\}$ we have $F = 0 + 0 +
 0 - 0 = 0$, and similarly for the other two by the symmetry of $F$ in $T_0, T_1
, T_2$. On each line all four partial derivatives vanish identically (verified b
y direct substitution above), confirming every point is singular.

### Conclusion

The singular locus of the Steiner quartic surface $V(T_0^2 T_1^2 + T_1^2 T_2^2 +
 T_0^2 T_2^2 - T_0 T_1 T_2 T_3)$ is the union of three projective lines:

$$\operatorname{Sing}(X) = \{T_0 = T_1 = 0\} \;\cup\; \{T_0 = T_2 = 0\} \;\cup\;
 \{T_1 = T_2 = 0\}.$$

These three lines are concurrent, all passing through the point $[0:0:0:1]$.
