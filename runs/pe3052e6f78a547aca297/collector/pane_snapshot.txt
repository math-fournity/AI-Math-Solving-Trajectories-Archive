# Solution

**Answer: Yes, $F'$ is a triangulated functor.**

## Proof

Let $Q: \mathbb K(\mathcal A) \to \mathbb D(\mathcal A)$ denote the localization
 functor. By hypothesis $F = F' \circ Q$, where $F'$ is the unique factorization
 guaranteed by the universal property of the localization (as an ordinary functo
r). We must show $F'$ is in fact triangulated.

**Step 1: $F$ annihilates acyclic complexes.**

Recall that $\mathbb D(\mathcal A)$ is the Verdier quotient $\mathbb K(\mathcal
A)/\mathbb K_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathcal A)$, where $\mathbb K_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathcal
 A)$ is the full subcategory of acyclic complexes. This is a thick triangulated
subcategory of $\mathbb K(\mathcal A)$.

Let $X \in \mathbb K_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathcal A)$ be an acyclic complex. Then the
morphism $X \to 0$ is a quasi-isomorphism (both source and target have zero coho
mology). By hypothesis, $F$ sends quasi-isomorphisms to isomorphisms, so $F(X) \
to F(0) \cong 0$ is an isomorphism in $\mathbb E$. Hence $F(X) \cong 0$; i.e., $
F$ annihilates every object of $\mathbb K_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathcal A)$.

**Step 2: Universal property of the Verdier quotient (triangulated version).**

The Verdier quotient $\mathbb K(\mathcal A)/\mathbb K_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathcal A)$
 enjoys the following universal property in the category of triangulated categor
ies and triangulated functors:

> If $G: \mathbb K(\mathcal A) \to \mathbb E$ is a **triangulated** functor that
 annihilates $\mathbb K_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathcal A)$ (sends every object of $\math
bb K_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathcal A)$ to an object isomorphic to $0$), then there exis
ts a **unique triangulated** functor $\bar G: \mathbb D(\mathcal A) \to \mathbb
E$ such that $G = \bar G \circ Q$.

This is a standard property of the Verdier quotient: the quotient functor $Q$ is
 triangulated, and the factorization through a Verdier quotient by a triangulate
d subcategory, when the original functor is triangulated and annihilates the sub
category, is itself triangulated. Concretely, the translation functor on $\mathb
b D(\mathcal A)$ is induced from that on $\mathbb K(\mathcal A)$, and a distingu
ished triangle in $\mathbb D(\mathcal A)$ is, by definition, the image under $Q$
 of a distinguished triangle in $\mathbb K(\mathcal A)$; since $F$ is triangulat
ed, $F$ sends such triangles to distinguished triangles in $\mathbb E$, and thes
e descend to $\bar G$.

**Step 3: Identification of $F'$ with the triangulated factorization.**

Applying Step 2 to $G = F$ (which is triangulated by hypothesis and annihilates
$\mathbb K_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathcal A)$ by Step 1), we obtain a unique **triangula
ted** functor $\bar F: \mathbb D(\mathcal A) \to \mathbb E$ with $F = \bar F \ci
rc Q$.

But the universal property of the localization (as an ordinary category) also gi
ves a unique ordinary functor $F': \mathbb D(\mathcal A) \to \mathbb E$ with $F
= F' \circ Q$. By uniqueness of the factorization as an ordinary functor, we mus
t have $F' = \bar F$. Since $\bar F$ is triangulated, $F'$ is triangulated. $\bl
acksquare$
