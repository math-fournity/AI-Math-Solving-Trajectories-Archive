## Interpretation

A "morphism" from the complex numbers to the invertible matrices is a **group ho
momorphism** from the additive group \((\mathbb C,+)\) to the multiplicative gro
up \((\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb C),\cdot)\), i.e. a map \(f:\mathbb C\to\mathrm{GL}_
n(\mathbb C)\) with
\[
f(z+w)=f(z)f(w)\quad\text{for all }z,w\in\mathbb C,
\]
and "continuous" means continuous as a map \(\mathbb C\cong\mathbb R^2\to M_n(\m
athbb C)\).

**Answer.** The continuous homomorphisms are exactly the maps
\[
\boxed{\,f(z)=\exp\!\bigl(zA+\bar z\,B\bigr)\,}
\]
where \(A,B\in M_n(\mathbb C)\) are **commuting** matrices (\(AB=BA\)). Equivale
ntly, writing \(z=x+iy\),
\[
f(x+iy)=\exp(xA_1+iyA_2),\qquad A_1,A_2\in M_n(\mathbb C),\ A_1A_2=A_2A_1.
\]
(The two parametrizations are related by \(A_1=A+B,\ A_2=A-B\), or \(A=\tfrac12(
A_1+A_2),\ B=\tfrac12(A_1-A_2)\).)

---

## Proof

### Step 1. A lemma on continuous one-parameter subgroups

**Lemma.** If \(\gamma:\mathbb R\to\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb C)\) is continuous and
satisfies \(\gamma(s+t)=\gamma(s)\gamma(t)\), then there exists a unique \(X\in
M_n(\mathbb C)\) with \(\gamma(t)=\exp(tX)\) for all \(t\).

*Proof of lemma.* Clearly \(\gamma(0)=I\). By continuity at \(0\), there is \(\v
arepsilon>0\) so that \(\|\gamma(t)-I\|<1\) for \(|t|<\varepsilon\); on this bal
l the matrix logarithm (given by the convergent power series \(\log(I+H)=H-\tfra
c12H^2+\cdots\)) is a well-defined analytic inverse of \(\exp\). Set \(L(t)=\log
\gamma(t)\) for \(|t|<\varepsilon\).

For \(|s|,|t|,|s+t|<\varepsilon\) we have \(\gamma(s)\gamma(t)=\gamma(s+t)\). Th
e elements \(\gamma(s),\gamma(t)\) commute (since \(\gamma(s)\gamma(t)=\gamma(s+
t)=\gamma(t)\gamma(s)\)), hence their logarithms \(L(s),L(t)\) commute, and beca
use \(\exp\) is a local diffeomorphism with \(\exp(X+Y)=\exp X\exp Y\) for commu
ting \(X,Y\),
\[
L(s+t)=L(s)+L(t)\qquad(|s|,|t|,|s+t|<\varepsilon).
\]
Thus \(L\) is a continuous additive map near \(0\), so \(L(t)=tX\) with \(X=L'(0
)\) (e.g. \(X=\lim_{k\to\infty}k\,L(1/k)\)). Hence \(\gamma(t)=\exp(tX)\) for \(
|t|<\varepsilon\). For arbitrary \(t\), pick \(m\) with \(|t/m|<\varepsilon\); t
hen
\[
\gamma(t)=\gamma(t/m)^m=\exp\!\bigl((t/m)X\bigr)^m=\exp(tX). \qquad\square
\]

### Step 2. Reduction to two one-parameter subgroups

Let \(f:\mathbb C\to\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb C)\) be a continuous homomorphism. Def
ine
\[
\gamma_1(t)=f(t),\qquad \gamma_2(t)=f(it),\qquad t\in\mathbb R.
\]
Both are continuous one-parameter subgroups of \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb C)\) (con
tinuity of \(f\) restricted to the real and imaginary axes; the homomorphism pro
perty is inherited). By the Lemma, there exist \(A_1,A_2\in M_n(\mathbb C)\) wit
h
\[
f(t)=\exp(tA_1),\qquad f(it)=\exp(tA_2),\qquad t\in\mathbb R.
\]

### Step 3. The generators commute

For all \(x,y\in\mathbb R\),
\[
f(x)\,f(iy)=f(x+iy)=f(iy+x)=f(iy)\,f(x),
\]
i.e.
\[
\exp(xA_1)\exp(iyA_2)=\exp(iyA_2)\exp(xA_1)\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb R.
\]
Differentiating with respect to \(x\) and then \(y\) and evaluating at \((0,0)\)
 gives
\[
A_1\,(iA_2)=(iA_2)\,A_1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad A_1A_2=A_2A_1.
\]

### Step 4. Closed form

Since \(A_1,A_2\) commute, the matrices \(xA_1\) and \(iyA_2\) commute for all \
(x,y\), so
\[
f(x+iy)=f(x)f(iy)=\exp(xA_1)\exp(iyA_2)=\exp(xA_1+iyA_2).
\]
Writing \(z=x+iy\) and reparametrizing with
\[
A=\tfrac12(A_1+A_2),\qquad B=\tfrac12(A_1-A_2),
\]
(so that \(A,B\) still commute, since \(A_1,A_2\) do), and using
\[
xA_1+iyA_2=\tfrac{z+\bar z}{2}A_1+\tfrac{z-\bar z}{2}A_2=zA+\bar z\,B,
\]
we obtain
\[
f(z)=\exp(zA+\bar z\,B),\qquad A,B\in M_n(\mathbb C),\ AB=BA.
\]

### Step 5. Converse (every such map is a continuous homomorphism)

Let \(A,B\in M_n(\mathbb C)\) with \(AB=BA\), and define \(f(z)=\exp(zA+\bar z\,
B)\). Then:

* **Well-defined into \(\mathrm{GL}_n\):** an exponential of any matrix is inver
tible, so \(f(z)\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb C)\).
* **Homomorphism:** for \(z,w\in\mathbb C\), the matrices \(zA+\bar z B\) and \(
wA+\bar w B\) are both linear combinations of the commuting pair \(A,B\), hence
commute. Therefore
\[
f(z+w)=\exp\!\bigl((z+w)A+(\bar z+\bar w)B\bigr)=\exp(zA+\bar z B)\exp(wA+\bar w
 B)=f(z)f(w).
\]
* **Continuous:** \(z\mapsto zA+\bar z B\) is continuous (real-linear) and the m
atrix exponential is continuous (analytic).

### Step 6. Uniqueness of the parametrization

The map \(f\) determines \(A_1,A_2\) (hence \(A,B\)) uniquely: \(A_1=\left.\frac
{d}{dt}\right|_{0}f(t)\) and \(A_2=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}f(it)\). So dist
inct commuting pairs \((A,B)\) give distinct homomorphisms.

---

## Conclusion

The continuous group homomorphisms \((\mathbb C,+)\to(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb C),\
cdot)\) are precisely
\[
\boxed{\,f(z)=\exp\!\bigl(zA+\bar z\,B\bigr),\quad A,B\in M_n(\mathbb C),\ AB=BA
\,}.
\]
Equivalently, \(f(x+iy)=\exp(xA_1+iyA_2)\) with \(A_1,A_2\in M_n(\mathbb C)\) co
mmuting. This is a bijective parametrization by ordered pairs of commuting compl
ex \(n\times n\) matrices.
