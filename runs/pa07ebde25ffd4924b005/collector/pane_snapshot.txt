## Solution

**Setup.** Place the three centers (each circle has radius 1, mutually externall
y tangent, so center-to-center distance is 2) at the vertices of an equilateral
triangle of side 2:

$$A=(0,0),\quad B=(2,0),\quad C=(1,\sqrt 3).$$

**The three common external tangents.** For two equal-radius circles, the common
 external tangent is parallel to the line joining the centers, shifted by one ra
dius (distance 1) toward the side *away from the third circle*.

- Tangent to $A,B$ (away from $C$, which is above): $\;y=-1$.
- Tangent to $A,C$ (away from $B$): the line $AC$ is $\sqrt 3\,x-y=0$; $B$ lies
on the $+$ side, so shift outward by 1: $\;\sqrt 3\,x-y=-2$.
- Tangent to $B,C$ (away from $A$): the line $BC$ is $\sqrt 3\,x+y=2\sqrt 3$; $A
$ lies on the $-$ side, so shift outward by 1: $\;\sqrt 3\,x+y=2\sqrt 3+2$.

**Vertices of $\triangle XYZ$.**

- $L_1\cap L_2$: $y=-1,\ \sqrt 3\,x+1=-2\Rightarrow x=-\sqrt 3$, giving $(-\sqrt
 3,-1)$.
- $L_1\cap L_3$: $y=-1,\ \sqrt 3\,x-1=2\sqrt 3+2\Rightarrow x=2+\sqrt 3$, giving
 $(2+\sqrt 3,-1)$.
- $L_2\cap L_3$: adding the two equations gives $2\sqrt 3\,x=2\sqrt 3\Rightarrow
 x=1$, then $y=\sqrt 3+2$, giving $(1,2+\sqrt 3)$.

**Side lengths.** The base side (between the first two points) has length

$$\bigl(2+\sqrt 3\bigr)-\bigl(-\sqrt 3\bigr)=2+2\sqrt 3.$$

Check another side, between $(-\sqrt 3,-1)$ and $(1,2+\sqrt 3)$:

$$\Delta x=1+\sqrt 3,\quad \Delta y=3+\sqrt 3,$$
$$\Delta x^2+\Delta y^2=(1+\sqrt 3)^2+(3+\sqrt 3)^2=(4+2\sqrt 3)+(12+6\sqrt 3)=1
6+8\sqrt 3,$$
$$(2+2\sqrt 3)^2=4+8\sqrt 3+12=16+8\sqrt 3.\ \checkmark$$

So all three sides are equal, confirming $\triangle XYZ$ is equilateral.

**Result.** Each side of $\triangle XYZ$ has length

$$\boxed{2+2\sqrt 3}.$$
