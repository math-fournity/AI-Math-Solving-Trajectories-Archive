## Proof

We wish to compute
$$\lim_{x\to\infty} \frac{\Pi(x)}{x}, \qquad \Pi(x) = \#\{p^k \le x : p \text{ p
rime},\ k\in\mathbb{N}\}.$$

**Decomposition by exponent.** Every prime power $p^k \le x$ has a unique expone
nt $k\ge 1$ with $p \le x^{1/k}$. Hence
$$\Pi(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \pi\!\left(x^{1/k}\right),$$
where $\pi(y)=\#\{p\le y\}$ is the prime-counting function. The sum is effective
ly finite: for $k > \log_2 x$ we have $x^{1/k}<2$, so $\pi(x^{1/k})=0$.

**The $k=1$ term.** By the Prime Number Theorem, $\pi(x)\sim x/\ln x$, so
$$\frac{\pi(x)}{x} \sim \frac{1}{\ln x} \to 0.$$

**The $k\ge 2$ terms.** For $k\ge 2$ we have $x^{1/k}\le \sqrt{x}$, and there ar
e at most $\lfloor \log_2 x\rfloor$ such terms. Using the trivial bound $\pi(y)\
le y$,
$$\sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor\log_2 x\rfloor} \pi\!\left(x^{1/k}\right) \le \sum_{k=2}^{
\lfloor\log_2 x\rfloor} x^{1/k} \le \lfloor\log_2 x\rfloor \cdot \sqrt{x} = O\!\
left(\sqrt{x}\,\ln x\right).$$

Therefore
$$\frac{\Pi(x)}{x} = \frac{\pi(x)}{x} + \frac{O(\sqrt{x}\ln x)}{x} = O\!\left(\f
rac{1}{\ln x}\right) + O\!\left(\frac{\ln x}{\sqrt{x}}\right) \xrightarrow{x\to\
infty} 0.$$

**Conclusion.** Both contributions vanish asymptotically: the primes themselves
have density $0$ (by PNT), and the higher prime powers contribute only $O(\sqrt{
x}\ln x)$, which is $o(x)$. Hence

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\Pi(x)}{x}=0.}$$
