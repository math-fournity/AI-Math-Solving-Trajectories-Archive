## Solution

Consider the change of variables
$$u = e^x + y, \qquad v = x.$$

The Jacobian of the map $(x,y) \mapsto (v,u) = (x,\, e^x+y)$ is
$$\frac{\partial(v,u)}{\partial(x,y)} = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ e^x & 1 \end{vm
atrix} = 1.$$

Since the Jacobian determinant equals $1$, this transformation is area-preservin
g.

Under this transformation, the region
$$A = \{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^2 : x^2 + (e^x+y)^2 \le 1\}$$
becomes
$$A' = \{(v,u)\in\mathbb{R}^2 : v^2 + u^2 \le 1\},$$
which is the closed unit disk in the $(v,u)$-plane.

The area of the unit disk is $\pi$. Since the transformation preserves area,

$$\operatorname{Area}(A) = \operatorname{Area}(A') = \pi.$$

**Cross-check via direct integration.** For each $x\in[-1,1]$, the constraint $(
e^x+y)^2 \le 1-x^2$ gives
$$y \in \bigl[-e^x-\sqrt{1-x^2},\; -e^x+\sqrt{1-x^2}\bigr],$$
so the vertical width is $2\sqrt{1-x^2}$ (the $e^x$ terms cancel). Hence
$$\operatorname{Area}(A)=\int_{-1}^{1}2\sqrt{1-x^2}\,dx = 2\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}=\p
i,$$
since $\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{1-x^2}\,dx$ is the area of a semicircle of radius $1$.

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Area}(A)=\pi}$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
