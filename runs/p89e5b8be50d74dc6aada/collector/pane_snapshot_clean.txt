## Solution

We solve the initial-boundary value problem

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\alpha u+D\nabla^{2}u,\qquad x\in\Omega\subset\mat
hbb{R}^{n},\;t>0,
$$

with $u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)$ and either homogeneous Dirichlet ($u|_{\partial\Omega}=0$
) or homogeneous Neumann ($\partial u/\partial n|_{\partial\Omega}=0$) boundary
conditions. Here $\alpha$ is a constant and $D>0$.

### Step 1. Reduction to the Laplacian eigenvalue problem

The equation is linear with constant coefficients in $t$, so we apply separation
 of variables. Set $u(x,t)=X(x)\,T(t)$. Substituting:

$$
X\,T'=\alpha X T+D\,T\,\nabla^{2}X.
$$

Dividing by $XT$:

$$
\frac{T'}{T}=\alpha+D\,\frac{\nabla^{2}X}{X}.
$$

The left side depends only on $t$ and the right side only on $x$, so both equal
a constant, which we call $\lambda$. This gives two equations:

$$
T'=\lambda\,T, \tag{1}
$$

$$
\nabla^{2}X=\frac{\lambda-\alpha}{D}\,X. \tag{2}
$$

### Step 2. The spatial eigenvalue problem

Rewrite (2) as

$$
-\nabla^{2}X=\kappa\,X,\qquad \kappa:=-\frac{\lambda-\alpha}{D}=\frac{\alpha-\la
mbda}{D}. \tag{3}
$$

Equation (3) together with the homogeneous boundary condition (Dirichlet: $X|_{\
partial\Omega}=0$, or Neumann: $\partial X/\partial n|_{\partial\Omega}=0$) is t
he **eigenvalue problem for the (negative) Laplacian** on the bounded domain $\O
mega$.

By the spectral theorem for self-adjoint elliptic operators on bounded domains w
ith these boundary conditions:

- There exists a **discrete sequence of eigenvalues** $0\le\kappa_{0}\le\kappa_{
1}\le\kappa_{2}\le\cdots$ with $\kappa_{k}\to\infty$.
- There is a corresponding set of **eigenfunctions** $\{\varphi_{k}(x)\}_{k=0}^{
\infty}$ that form a **complete orthonormal basis** of $L^{2}(\Omega)$ (with res
pect to the standard inner product $\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\Omega}f\,g\,dx$).

**Dirichlet case:** All eigenvalues are strictly positive, $\kappa_{k}>0$ for ev
ery $k\ge 0$ (indexing from $0$).

**Neumann case:** The smallest eigenvalue is $\kappa_{0}=0$, with constant eigen
function $\varphi_{0}(x)=|\Omega|^{-1/2}$; all higher eigenvalues satisfy $\kapp
a_{k}>0$ for $k\ge 1$.

Each eigenfunction satisfies the same boundary condition imposed on $u$.

### Step 3. The temporal factor

From (1), $T(t)=e^{\lambda t}$. From the relation $\lambda=\alpha-D\kappa$ in (3
), each eigenvalue $\kappa_{k}$ yields the temporal factor

$$
T_{k}(t)=e^{(\alpha-D\kappa_{k})\,t}. \tag{4}
$$

### Step 4. General solution by superposition

Since the PDE is linear and the eigenfunctions are complete, the **general solut
ion** is the eigenfunction expansion

$$
\boxed{\displaystyle u(x,t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}\,\varphi_{k}(x)\,e^{(\alpha
-D\kappa_{k})\,t}}, \tag{5}
$$

where $\{\varphi_{k}\}$ are the orthonormal eigenfunctions of $-\nabla^{2}$ on $
\Omega$ with the prescribed homogeneous boundary condition, and $\{\kappa_{k}\}$
 are the corresponding eigenvalues.

### Step 5. Determining the coefficients from the initial condition

Setting $t=0$ in (5) and using $u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)$:

$$
u_{0}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}\,\varphi_{k}(x).
$$

By orthonormality $\bigl(\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{j}\varphi_{k}\,dx=\delta_{jk}\big
r)$, the coefficients are

$$
\boxed{\displaystyle c_{k}=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}(x)\,\varphi_{k}(x)\,dx},\qquad k=0
,1,2,\dots \tag{6}
$$

(If the eigenfunctions are not normalized, replace (6) by $c_{k}=\frac{\int_{\Om
ega}u_{0}\varphi_{k}\,dx}{\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{k}^{2}\,dx}$.)

### Step 6. Verification

**PDE check.** Each term $u_{k}=c_{k}\varphi_{k}e^{(\alpha-D\kappa_{k})t}$ satis
fies the PDE:

$$
\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial t}=(\alpha-D\kappa_{k})\,u_{k},\qquad \alpha u_{k
}+D\nabla^{2}u_{k}=\alpha u_{k}+D(-\kappa_{k})u_{k}=(\alpha-D\kappa_{k})\,u_{k}.
\;\checkmark
$$

**Boundary conditions.** Each $\varphi_{k}$ satisfies the homogeneous boundary c
ondition, hence so does every partial sum and (under suitable regularity of $u_{
0}$) the full series. $\checkmark$

**Initial condition.** By completeness of $\{\varphi_{k}\}$ in $L^{2}(\Omega)$,
the series (5) at $t=0$ converges to $u_{0}$ in the $L^{2}$ sense (and pointwise
/classically if $u_{0}$ is sufficiently smooth and satisfies compatibility condi
tions at the boundary). $\checkmark$

### Step 7. Qualitative behavior

The exponential factor $e^{(\alpha-D\kappa_{k})t}$ for each mode is governed by
the sign of $\alpha-D\kappa_{k}$:

- **Neumann case.** The zeroth mode ($\kappa_{0}=0$) evolves as $e^{\alpha t}$:
it grows if $\alpha>0$, is constant if $\alpha=0$, and decays if $\alpha<0$. Hig
her modes ($\kappa_{k}>0$) decay whenever $D\kappa_{k}>\alpha$, i.e., for all su
fficiently large $k$. The long-time behavior is dominated by the zeroth (constan
t) mode $c_{0}\,|\Omega|^{-1/2}\,e^{\alpha t}$.

- **Dirichlet case.** Every $\kappa_{k}>0$. Modes with $\kappa_{k}<\alpha/D$ gro
w, modes with $\kappa_{k}>\alpha/D$ decay, and the mode with $\kappa_{k}=\alpha/
D$ (if it exists) is stationary. The long-time behavior is dominated by the eige
nmode with the smallest $\kappa_{k}$, i.e., the principal eigenvalue $\kappa_{0}
$.

### Summary

The general solution of $\partial u/\partial t=\alpha u+D\nabla^{2}u$ on a bound
ed domain $\Omega$ with homogeneous Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions and
 initial data $u_{0}$ is

$$
u(x,t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\Omega}u_{0}(\xi)\,\varphi_{k}(\xi)\,d\xi\
right)\varphi_{k}(x)\,e^{(\alpha-D\kappa_{k})\,t},
$$

where $\{-\nabla^{2}\varphi_{k}=\kappa_{k}\varphi_{k}\}$ is the complete orthono
rmal eigenbasis of the Laplacian on $\Omega$ subject to the same homogeneous bou
ndary condition, with discrete spectrum $0\le\kappa_{0}\le\kappa_{1}\le\cdots\to
\infty$ ($\kappa_{0}=0$ only in the Neumann case; $\kappa_{0}>0$ in the Dirichle
t case).
