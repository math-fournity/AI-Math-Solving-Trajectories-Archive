Looking at this problem, I need to estimate N = the number of 679 HMMT February
2017 contestants who scored at or above the median in at least one of the three
individual tests (General, Theme, Guts).

## Setting Up the Model

**Key identity:** N = 679 − (contestants below median on *all* tests they took).

So I need to estimate the fraction of contestants who are below median on every
test they take.

## Estimating Per-Test "At/Above Median" Rates

HMMT February General and Theme tests have **no partial credit** — each problem
is worth 10 points (0 or 10), so scores take values in {0, 10, 20, ..., 100}, on
ly 11 distinct values. With ~679 contestants, each score bucket averages ~62 peo
ple.

The median score falls within a large bucket. "At or above the median score" inc
ludes *everyone tied at the median*, so the at/above group is substantially larg
er than half. Estimating the median bucket at ~80 people (scores cluster at low
values on hard tests), the at/above fraction is approximately:

- General: ~55% at/above (45% below)
- Theme: ~55% at/above (45% below)
- Guts: ~53% at/above (47% below) — finer scoring (36 problems, varying point va
lues) means smaller tie effects

Average P(below median on one test) ≈ 0.457, corresponding to z ≈ −0.114.

## Estimating Correlations Between Tests

Using a latent ability model (score = ability + noise), I estimate:
- ρ(General, Theme) ≈ 0.75 (both individual math tests, similar skills)
- ρ(General, Guts) ≈ 0.65 (Guts is team-based, noisier)
- ρ(Theme, Guts) ≈ 0.65

## Computing P(Below Median on All Three)

Using the trivariate normal orthant probability formula:

P(all ≤ 0) = 1/8 + (1/(4π))(arcsin ρ₁₂ + arcsin ρ₁₃ + arcsin ρ₂₃)
= 1/8 + (1/(4π))(arcsin 0.75 + arcsin 0.65 + arcsin 0.65)
= 0.125 + (0.848 + 0.708 + 0.708)/(4π)
= 0.125 + 2.264/12.566
≈ 0.305

Adjusting for the tie effect (shifting threshold to z = −0.114):

P(all below) ≈ 0.305 − 0.114 × 0.447 ≈ 0.305 − 0.051 ≈ 0.254

(The derivative dP/da ≈ 3·φ(0)·Φ₂(0,0;ρ) ≈ 3 × 0.399 × 0.373 ≈ 0.447.)

## Participation Adjustment

Not all 679 contestants take all three tests. Estimating ~95% take all three, wi
th the rest taking fewer (reducing their probability of being in N):

- 645 take all 3: 645 × 0.746 = 481
- 25 take 2 tests: P(in N) = 1 − Φ₂(z,z;ρ) ≈ 1 − 0.326 = 0.674 → 25 × 0.674 = 17
- 9 take 1 test: P(in N) ≈ 0.55 → 9 × 0.55 = 5

**N ≈ 481 + 17 + 5 = 503**

## Sensitivity Check

- If correlation is higher (ρ ≈ 0.80): P(all below) ≈ 0.295, N ≈ 465
- If correlation is lower (ρ ≈ 0.60): P(all below) ≈ 0.228, N ≈ 510
- If no tie effect (partial credit, P(at/above) = 51%): N ≈ 472

My estimates consistently fall in the range **470–510**, with the central estima
te around **490–500**.

## Estimate

Accounting for the no-partial-credit tie effect (which pushes N up), moderate-to
-high correlation between tests (which pushes N down), and near-universal partic
ipation:

**N ≈ 490**
