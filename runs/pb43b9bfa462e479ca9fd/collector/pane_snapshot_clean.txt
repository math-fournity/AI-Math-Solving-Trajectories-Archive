Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $A^k B^l = B^l A^k$ for all
 $k,l > 1$ (i.e., $k,l \geq 2$) implies $AB = BA$, given $A, B$ invertible.

**Answer: Yes, $AB = BA$.**

**Proof.**

Throughout, $A$ and $B$ are invertible, so all powers $A^k, B^l$ are invertible
and we may freely cancel.

**Step 1: $A^2$ commutes with $B$.**

From the hypothesis with $(k,l)=(2,2)$ and $(k,l)=(2,3)$:
$$A^2 B^2 = B^2 A^2, \qquad A^2 B^3 = B^3 A^2.$$

Compute $A^2 B^3$ two ways. First:
$$A^2 B^3 = (A^2 B^2)\,B = (B^2 A^2)\,B = B^2(A^2 B).$$
Second, using $B^3 A^2 = B\cdot B^2 A^2 = B\cdot A^2 B^2$:
$$A^2 B^3 = B^3 A^2 = B\cdot A^2 B^2 = B\,A^2\,B^2.$$

Equating: $B^2(A^2 B) = B\,A^2\,B^2$. Left-multiply by $B^{-1}$ and right-multip
ly by $B^{-1}$:
$$B\,A^2 = A^2\,B. \qquad\checkmark$$

**Step 2: $B^2$ commutes with $A$.**

From the hypothesis with $(k,l)=(3,2)$: $A^3 B^2 = B^2 A^3$.

Compute $A^3 B^2$ two ways. First:
$$A^3 B^2 = A\,(A^2 B^2) = A\,(B^2 A^2).$$
Second:
$$A^3 B^2 = B^2 A^3 = B^2\,A\,A^2.$$

Equating: $A\,B^2\,A^2 = B^2\,A\,A^2$. Right-multiply by $A^{-2}$:
$$A\,B^2 = B^2\,A. \qquad\checkmark$$

**Step 3: Derive $AB = BA$ using $(k,l)=(3,3)$.**

From the hypothesis: $A^3 B^3 = B^3 A^3$.

Compute the left side using Step 1 ($A^2$ commutes with $B$, hence with $B^3$):
$$A^3 B^3 = A\cdot A^2\cdot B^3 = A\cdot B^3\cdot A^2.$$

Compute the right side using Step 2 ($B^2$ commutes with $A$):
$$B^3 A^3 = B\cdot B^2\cdot A\cdot A^2 = B\cdot A\cdot B^2\cdot A^2.$$

Equating and right-multiplying by $A^{-2}$:
$$A\,B^3 = B\,A\,B^2.$$

Rewrite as $(AB)\,B^2 = (BA)\,B^2$. Right-multiply by $B^{-2}$:
$$AB = BA. \qquad\blacksquare$$
