## Solution

We evaluate $I = \displaystyle\int_\gamma (e^{z^2} + \overline{z})\,dz$ where $\
gamma$ is the positively oriented unit circle $|z|=1$.

### Step 1: Split the integral

By linearity,
$$I = \int_\gamma e^{z^2}\,dz + \int_\gamma \overline{z}\,dz.$$

### Step 2: The $e^{z^2}$ term

The function $f(z) = e^{z^2}$ is entire (holomorphic on all of $\mathbb{C}$). Si
nce $\gamma$ is a closed contour and $e^{z^2}$ is holomorphic on and inside $\ga
mma$, Cauchy's theorem gives
$$\int_\gamma e^{z^2}\,dz = 0.$$

### Step 3: The $\overline{z}$ term

On the unit circle $|z|=1$, we have $z\overline{z} = |z|^2 = 1$, hence
$$\overline{z} = \frac{1}{z}.$$

Therefore
$$\int_\gamma \overline{z}\,dz = \int_\gamma \frac{1}{z}\,dz.$$

The function $g(z) = 1/z$ has a simple pole at $z=0$ with residue $1$, and $z=0$
 lies inside the unit circle. By the residue theorem (equivalently, Cauchy's int
egral formula with $f \equiv 1$),
$$\int_\gamma \frac{1}{z}\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{1}{z}
 = 2\pi i \cdot 1 = 2\pi i.$$

### Step 4: Combine

$$I = 0 + 2\pi i = 2\pi i.$$

---

### Verification

Parameterize $\gamma$ by $z(\theta) = e^{i\theta}$, $\theta \in [0,2\pi]$, so $d
z = ie^{i\theta}\,d\theta$ and $\overline{z} = e^{-i\theta}$.

$$\int_\gamma \overline{z}\,dz = \int_0^{2\pi} e^{-i\theta}\,ie^{i\theta}\,d\the
ta = \int_0^{2\pi} i\,d\theta = 2\pi i. \checkmark$$

The $e^{z^2}$ integral vanishes by Cauchy's theorem as confirmed above.

### Answer

$$\boxed{\int_\gamma (e^{z^2} + \overline{z})\,dz = 2\pi i}$$
