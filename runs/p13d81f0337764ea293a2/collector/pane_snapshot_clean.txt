## Solution

We determine the values of $\alpha$ for which $\displaystyle\sum_{n\geqslant 1}
n^{\alpha} x^{n}(1-x)$ converges uniformly on $[0,1]$.

### Pointwise limit

For $x\in[0,1)$, the factor $x^n$ decays geometrically, so $\sum n^\alpha x^n$ c
onverges absolutely for every $\alpha\in\mathbb{R}$, and
$$f(x)=(1-x)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^{\alpha} x^{n},\qquad x\in[0,1).$$
At $x=1$ every term equals $0$, so $f(1)=0$. Thus the pointwise limit exists on
all of $[0,1]$ for every $\alpha$.

### Uniform convergence for $\alpha<0$

Write $\beta=-\alpha>0$, so $n^{\alpha}=n^{-\beta}$. The tail of the series is
$$R_N(x)=\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty} n^{-\beta} x^{n}(1-x).$$
For every $n\geqslant N+1$ we have $n^{-\beta}\leqslant (N+1)^{-\beta}$, hence
$$R_N(x)\leqslant (N+1)^{-\beta}(1-x)\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}x^{n}=(N+1)^{-\beta}(1
-x)\cdot\frac{x^{N+1}}{1-x}=(N+1)^{-\beta}x^{N+1}\leqslant (N+1)^{-\beta}.$$
The right–hand side is independent of $x$ and tends to $0$ as $N\to\infty$ (sinc
e $\beta>0$). Therefore
$$\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|R_N(x)|\leqslant (N+1)^{-\beta}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}0,$$
proving **uniform convergence on $[0,1]$ for every $\alpha<0$**.

### Failure of uniform convergence for $\alpha=0$

Here $n^\alpha=1$, and the partial sums are
$$S_N(x)=(1-x)\sum_{n=1}^{N}x^{n}=(1-x)\cdot\frac{x(1-x^{N})}{1-x}=x(1-x^{N})=x-
x^{N+1},\qquad S_N(1)=0.$$
The pointwise limit is $f(x)=x$ for $x\in[0,1)$ and $f(1)=0$. The remainder is
$$|S_N(x)-f(x)|=|{-x^{N+1}}|=x^{N+1}\xrightarrow[x\to 1^-]{}1,$$
so $\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|S_N(x)-f(x)|=1$ for every $N$. The convergence is **not uni
form**.

### Failure of uniform convergence for $\alpha>0$

We show the pointwise limit $f$ is unbounded on $[0,1)$. Fix $x\in(0,1)$ and set
 $M=\lfloor 1/(1-x)\rfloor$. For $1\leqslant n\leqslant M$,
$$x^{n}\geqslant x^{M}\geqslant x^{1/(1-x)}\xrightarrow[x\to 1^-]{}e^{-1}>0,$$
so for $x$ close enough to $1$, $x^n\geqslant c$ (some $c>0$) for all $1\leqslan
t n\leqslant M$. Then
$$f(x)=(1-x)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{\alpha}x^{n}\geqslant (1-x)\,c\sum_{n=1}^{M}n^
{\alpha}\geqslant c\,(1-x)\cdot\frac{M^{\alpha+1}}{\alpha+1}\sim \frac{c}{\alpha
+1}(1-x)^{-\alpha}\xrightarrow[x\to 1^-]{}+\infty,$$
since $\alpha>0$. Hence $f$ is unbounded near $1$, while $f(1)=0$.

Each partial sum $S_N$ is continuous on the compact interval $[0,1]$, hence boun
ded. A uniform limit of bounded functions is bounded; since $f$ is unbounded, th
e convergence **cannot be uniform**.

### Conclusion

The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n\geqslant 1} n^{\alpha} x^{n}(1-x)$ converges un
iformly on $[0,1]$ **if and only if $\alpha<0$**.
