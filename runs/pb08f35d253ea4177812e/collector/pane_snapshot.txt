## Proof

**Key observation:** Since $|\sin(\theta)| \leq 1$ for all real $\theta$, we can
 dominate the integrand:

$$\left|\frac{\sin(\sqrt{x^4+n^4})}{x^4+n^4}\right| \leq \frac{1}{x^4+n^4}.$$

Therefore:

$$|I_n| \leq \int_{\mathbb{R}} \frac{dx}{x^4+n^4}.$$

**Evaluating the dominating integral.** Substitute $x = nu$, so $dx = n\,du$:

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} \frac{dx}{x^4+n^4} = \int_{\mathbb{R}} \frac{n\,du}{n^4(u^4+
1)} = \frac{1}{n^3}\int_{\mathbb{R}} \frac{du}{u^4+1}.$$

The integral $\int_{\mathbb{R}} \frac{du}{u^4+1}$ is a standard convergent integ
ral. By the residue theorem (or the known formula for $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\f
rac{du}{u^4+a^4}$), with poles at $e^{i\pi/4}$ and $e^{i3\pi/4}$ in the upper ha
lf-plane:

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} \frac{du}{u^4+1} = \frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}}.$$

(This is a finite positive constant, independent of $n$.)

**Combining:**

$$|I_n| \leq \frac{1}{n^3}\cdot\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}\,n^3}.
$$

**Taking the limit:**

$$0 \leq |I_n| \leq \frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}\,n^3} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.$$

By the squeeze theorem, $|I_n| \to 0$, and hence $I_n \to 0$.

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} I_n = 0.}$$
