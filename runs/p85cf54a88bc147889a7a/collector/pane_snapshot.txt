# Coproducts: Definition and Verification

## 1. Definition of the Coproduct

Recall that a **product** of a family $\{X_i\}_{i \in I}$ in a category $\mathca
l{C}$ is an object $P$ together with morphisms $\pi_i : P \to X_i$ (projections)
 satisfying the universal property: for any object $Y$ equipped with morphisms $
f_i : Y \to X_i$, there exists a unique morphism $f : Y \to P$ with $\pi_i \circ
 f = f_i$ for all $i$.

**Reversing the arrows**, we obtain the definition of the **coproduct**:

> A **coproduct** of the family $\{X_i\}_{i \in I}$ in a category $\mathcal{C}$
is an object $S$ together with morphisms $\iota_i : X_i \to S$ (called **injecti
ons** or **coprojections**) satisfying the following universal property:
>
> For any object $Y$ equipped with morphisms $f_i : X_i \to Y$ ($i \in I$), ther
e exists a **unique** morphism $f : S \to Y$ such that $f \circ \iota_i = f_i$ f
or all $i \in I$.

We write $S = \coprod_{i \in I} X_i$ (or $\prod_{i \in I}^* X_i$ in the problem'
s notation). Schematically:

$$\begin{array}{ccc} X_i & \xrightarrow{\iota_i} & S = \coprod X_i \\ & \searrow
{f_i} \quad\swarrow{\exists!\, f} & \\ & Y & \end{array}$$

**Uniqueness up to unique isomorphism.** If $(S, \{\iota_i\})$ and $(S', \{\iota
_i'\})$ are both coproducts, then by the universal property of $S$ applied to $\
{\iota_i'\}$, there is a unique $u : S \to S'$ with $u \circ \iota_i = \iota_i'$
, and by the universal property of $S'$ applied to $\{\iota_i\}$, there is a uni
que $v : S' \to S$ with $v \circ \iota_i' = \iota_i$. Then $v \circ u \circ \iot
a_i = \iota_i$ for all $i$, so by the uniqueness part of the universal property
of $S$ (applied to $Y = S$ and $f_i = \iota_i$), $v \circ u = \mathrm{id}_S$. Si
milarly $u \circ v = \mathrm{id}_{S'}$. Hence $u$ is an isomorphism.

## 2. Direct Sum of Abelian Groups is the Coproduct in **Ab**

Let $\{A_i\}_{i \in I}$ be a family of abelian groups. Define the **direct sum**

$$\bigoplus_{i \in I} A_i = \left\{ (a_i)_{i \in I} \;\middle|\; a_i \in A_i,\;
a_i = 0 \text{ for all but finitely many } i \right\},$$

with componentwise addition. (When $I$ is finite, this coincides with the direct
 product $\prod A_i$.)

**Injections.** For each $j \in I$, define $\iota_j : A_j \to \bigoplus A_i$ by

$$(\iota_j(a))_i = \begin{cases} a & i = j, \\ 0 & i \neq j. \end{cases}$$

This is clearly a group homomorphism.

**Universal property.** Let $B$ be an abelian group and let $f_i : A_i \to B$ be
 homomorphisms for each $i$. Define

$$f : \bigoplus_{i \in I} A_i \to B, \qquad f\big((a_i)_{i \in I}\big) = \sum_{i
 \in I} f_i(a_i).$$

This sum is **finite** because $a_i = 0$ for all but finitely many $i$, so the d
efinition makes sense. We verify:

- **Homomorphism.** For $a = (a_i)$, $a' = (a_i')$, we have $f(a + a') = \sum f_
i(a_i + a_i') = \sum f_i(a_i) + \sum f_i(a_i') = f(a) + f(a')$.

- **Commutativity with injections.** $f(\iota_j(a)) = f_j(a) + \sum_{i \neq j} f
_i(0) = f_j(a)$. ✓

- **Uniqueness.** Suppose $g : \bigoplus A_i \to B$ also satisfies $g \circ \iot
a_j = f_j$ for all $j$. Any element $a = (a_i) \in \bigoplus A_i$ has $a_i = 0$
for all but finitely many $i$, and we can write $a = \sum_{i \in I} \iota_i(a_i)
$ (finite sum). Then

$$g(a) = g\!\left(\sum_i \iota_i(a_i)\right) = \sum_i g(\iota_i(a_i)) = \sum_i f
_i(a_i) = f(a).$$

Hence $g = f$, proving uniqueness. ∎

**Remark (modules).** The identical argument works in $R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$ (t
he category of left modules over a ring $R$) and $\mathbf{Mod}\text{-}R$ (right
modules): the direct sum $\bigoplus M_i$ of $R$-modules, defined as the submodul
e of $\prod M_i$ consisting of tuples with finitely many nonzero entries, with t
he same injections $\iota_j$, satisfies the coproduct universal property. The ma
p $f((m_i)) = \sum f_i(m_i)$ is $R$-linear because each $f_i$ is, and the sum is
 finite. Uniqueness follows by the same finite-support decomposition $a = \sum \
iota_i(a_i)$.

## 3. Free Product of Groups is the Coproduct in **Grp**

Let $\{G_i\}_{i \in I}$ be a family of groups. The **free product** $*_{i \in I}
\, G_i$ is the group whose elements are **reduced words**

$$w = g_{i_1} g_{i_2} \cdots g_{i_n}, \qquad g_{i_k} \in G_{i_k} \setminus \{e\}
,\quad i_k \neq i_{k+1},$$

(i.e., adjacent letters come from distinct groups, and no letter is the identity
), together with the empty word (the identity). Multiplication is by **concatena
tion followed by reduction**: combine adjacent letters from the same group using
 that group's operation, and delete identities, until a reduced word is obtained
.

**Injections.** For each $j \in I$, define $\iota_j : G_j \to *_{i} G_i$ by $\io
ta_j(g) = $ the one-letter word $g$ (for $g \neq e$) and $\iota_j(e) = $ the emp
ty word. This is a homomorphism: $\iota_j(gh)$ is the reduced form of the word $
g\, h$, which (since both are from $G_j$) is the single letter $gh$; this equals
 $\iota_j(g) \cdot \iota_j(h)$.

**Universal property.** Let $H$ be a group and let $f_i : G_i \to H$ be homomorp
hisms. Define

$$f : *_{i \in I} G_i \to H, \qquad f(g_{i_1} g_{i_2} \cdots g_{i_n}) = f_{i_1}(
g_{i_1})\, f_{i_2}(g_{i_2}) \cdots f_{i_n}(g_{i_n}),$$

and $f(\text{empty word}) = e_H$.

- **Well-defined homomorphism.** We must check that $f$ respects the reduction r
ules. If two adjacent letters $g, g'$ belong to the same group $G_j$, then in th
e free product they reduce to the single letter $gg' \in G_j$, and

$$f_j(g)\, f_j(g') = f_j(gg')$$

since $f_j$ is a homomorphism. So the value of $f$ is unchanged by combining adj
acent same-group letters. Similarly, deleting an identity letter ($g = e$) does
not change the value since $f_j(e) = e_H$. Hence $f$ is well-defined, and it is
a homomorphism because concatenation of reduced words maps to multiplication in
$H$ (with reduction handled by the above compatibility).

- **Commutativity with injections.** $f(\iota_j(g)) = f_j(g)$. ✓

- **Uniqueness.** Let $g : *_{i} G_i \to H$ be any homomorphism with $g \circ \i
ota_j = f_j$ for all $j$. Every reduced word $w = g_{i_1} \cdots g_{i_n}$ equals
 the product $\iota_{i_1}(g_{i_1}) \cdots \iota_{i_n}(g_{i_n})$ in the free prod
uct. Therefore

$$g(w) = g\!\left(\prod_k \iota_{i_k}(g_{i_k})\right) = \prod_k g(\iota_{i_k}(g_
{i_k})) = \prod_k f_{i_k}(g_{i_k}) = f(w).$$

So $g = f$, establishing uniqueness. ∎

## 4. Summary

| Category | Coproduct | Key feature enabling the proof |
|---|---|---|
| $\mathbf{Ab}$, $R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$ | Direct sum $\bigoplus A_i$ | Finite s
upport $\Rightarrow$ finite sum $\sum f_i(a_i)$ is well-defined |
| $\mathbf{Grp}$ | Free product $*_{i}\, G_i$ | Reduced words $\Rightarrow$ ever
y element is a finite product of injections |

In both cases, the **finiteness** built into the construction (finite support fo
r direct sums, finite words for free products) is exactly what makes the induced
 map $f$ well-defined, and the decomposition of every element into images of inj
ections is what gives uniqueness. This is the precise sense in which these class
ical constructions realize the arrow-reversed (coproduct) universal property.
