Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a continuous linear surject

**Answer: No.** Here is the proof.

---

**Counterexample.** Let $V = c_{00}$, the space of finitely supported sequences
over $\mathbb{R}$ (or $\mathbb{C}$). This is infinite dimensional. Define:
$$\|x\|_1 = \|x\|_\infty = \max_n |x_n|, \qquad \|x\|_2 = \|x\|_2 = \left(\sum_n
 |x_n|^2\right)^{1/2}.$$

**Step 1: The norm inequality holds.** For any $x \in c_{00}$,
$$\|x\|_\infty = \max_n |x_n| \leq \left(\sum_n |x_n|^2\right)^{1/2} = \|x\|_2,$
$
so $\|\cdot\|_1 \leq \|\cdot\|_2$. ✓

**Step 2: Identify the completions.** The completion of $c_{00}$ under $\|\cdot\
|_\infty$ is $c_0$, the space of sequences converging to zero. The completion of
 $c_{00}$ under $\|\cdot\|_2$ is $\ell^2$.

**Step 3: Key properties.**
- $\ell^2$ is **reflexive** (as is $\ell^p$ for any $1 < p < \infty$).
- $c_0$ is **not reflexive**: $c_0^* \cong \ell^1$ and $c_0^{**} \cong \ell^\inf
ty \supsetneq c_0$.

**Step 4: Quotients of reflexive spaces are reflexive.** Suppose $T: X \to Y$ is
 a continuous linear surjection between Banach spaces. Then $Y \cong X/\ker T$.
If $X$ is reflexive, then $X/\ker T$ is reflexive.

*Proof of this fact:* $(X/M)^* \cong M^\perp \subseteq X^*$. Since $X$ is reflex
ive, $X^*$ is reflexive. Closed subspaces of reflexive spaces are reflexive, so
$M^\perp$ is reflexive. A Banach space is reflexive iff its dual is reflexive, s
o $X/M$ is reflexive. ∎

**Step 5: Conclusion.** If there existed a continuous linear surjection $T: \ell
^2 \to c_0$, then $c_0 \cong \ell^2 / \ker T$ would be a quotient of a reflexive
 space, hence reflexive. But $c_0$ is not reflexive. **Contradiction.**

Therefore, no continuous linear surjection from $\hat{V}_2 = \ell^2$ to $\hat{V}
_1 = c_0$ exists, even though $\|\cdot\|_1 \leq \|\cdot\|_2$.
