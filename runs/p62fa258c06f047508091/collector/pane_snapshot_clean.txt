## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. There exists a constant $C>0$ such that $|T(x,y)|\le C\|x\|_X\|y
\|_Y$ for all $x\in X$, $y\in Y$.

**Setup.** For each fixed $x\in X$, the map $y\mapsto T(x,y)$ is a continuous li
near functional on $Y$ (separate continuity in the second argument). Hence there
 is a unique $S(x)\in Y^*$ with
$$T(x,y)=\langle S(x),y\rangle,\qquad y\in Y.$$
Linearity of $T$ in the first argument gives $S:X\to Y^*$ linear. Separate conti
nuity in the first argument says: for each fixed $y\in Y$, the map $x\mapsto T(x
,y)=\langle S(x),y\rangle$ is continuous, i.e. $x\mapsto \langle S(x),y\rangle$
is continuous for every $y$.

**Goal.** Show $S$ is bounded: $\sup_{\|x\|\le 1}\|S(x)\|_{Y^*}<\infty$. Then $C
:=\|S\|$ gives $|T(x,y)|=|\langle S(x),y\rangle|\le \|S\|\,\|x\|\,\|y\|$.

**Applying the Uniform Boundedness Principle.** For each $x$ with $\|x\|\le 1$ a
nd each $y\in Y$,
$$|\langle S(x),y\rangle|=|T(x,y)|.$$
Fix $y\in Y$. The map $x\mapsto \langle S(x),y\rangle$ is continuous, so
$$\sup_{\|x\|\le 1}|\langle S(x),y\rangle|<\infty\quad\text{for every }y\in Y.$$
Equivalently, the family of bounded linear functionals $\{\,\widehat{x}\circ S:\
|x\|\le 1\,\}$ on $Y^*$ — or more directly, the family $\{S(x):\|x\|\le 1\}\subs
eteq Y^*$ — is pointwise bounded on $Y$:
$$\sup_{\|x\|\le 1}|\langle S(x),y\rangle|<\infty,\qquad \forall\,y\in Y.$$
Since $Y$ is a Banach space, the **Uniform Boundedness Principle** (Banach–Stein
haus) applied to the family $\{S(x):\|x\|\le 1\}\subset Y^*$ yields
$$\sup_{\|x\|\le 1}\|S(x)\|_{Y^*}<\infty.$$
Set $C:=\sup_{\|x\|\le 1}\|S(x)\|_{Y^*}<\infty$. Then for all $x\neq 0$, $y\in Y
$,
$$|T(x,y)|=|\langle S(x),y\rangle|=\|x\|\cdot\bigl|\langle S(x/\|x\|),y\rangle\b
igr|\le \|x\|\cdot\|S(x/\|x\|)\|_{Y^*}\cdot\|y\|\le C\,\|x\|\,\|y\|.$$
The case $x=0$ is trivial.

**Remark on completeness.** Completeness of $Y$ is essential: the UBP requires t
he domain ($Y$) to be complete. Completeness of $X$ is not actually needed for t
his argument — only that $Y$ is Banach. (Symmetrically, one may instead fix $x$
and apply UBP on $X$ using completeness of $X$; either completeness hypothesis s
uffices for the conclusion.) For non-complete normed spaces a separately continu

Thus a separately continuous bilinear functional on Banach spaces is automatical
