# Fields Finitely Generated as ℤ-algebras Are Always Finite

**Claim.** If a field $K$ is finitely generated as a $\mathbb{Z}$-algebra, then
$K$ is a finite field. In particular, such fields are always finite.

**Proof.** Suppose $K$ is a field and a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}$-algebra,
 so $K \cong \mathbb{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_n]/\mathfrak{m}$ for some maximal ideal $\ma
thfrak{m}$. Let $\varphi:\mathbb{Z}\to K$ be the structure map. Its kernel is a
prime ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$, hence either $(0)$ or $(p)$ for a prime $p$.

**Case 1: $\ker\varphi=(p)$.** Then $K$ is a finitely generated $\mathbb{F}_p$-a
lgebra and a field. By Zariski's lemma (the general Nullstellensatz form: a fiel
d that is a finitely generated algebra over a field $k$ is a finite algebraic ex
tension of $k$), $K$ is a finite extension of $\mathbb{F}_p$. Hence $K$ is a fin
ite field. ✓

**Case 2: $\ker\varphi=(0)$.** Then $\mathbb{Z}$ embeds into $K$. Since $K$ is a
 field, it contains the fraction field of $\mathbb{Z}$, namely $\mathbb{Q}\subse
teq K$.

Write $K=\mathbb{Z}[a_1,\dots,a_n]$. Since $K\supseteq\mathbb{Q}$ and $K$ is a f
initely generated $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra (generated by the same $a_i$) that is a f
ield, Zariski's lemma gives that $K$ is a finite algebraic extension of $\mathbb
{Q}$, i.e. a number field. Thus each $a_i$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$.

Each $a_i$ satisfies a monic polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}$; clearing denominators
, there exists a nonzero integer $N$ such that every $a_i$ is integral over $R:=
\mathbb{Z}[1/N]$. Consequently $K=\mathbb{Z}[a_1,\dots,a_n]=R[a_1,\dots,a_n]$ is
 a finitely generated $R$-module, hence **integral over $R$**.

Now $K$ is a field and $R\subseteq K$ with $K$ integral over $R$. A standard res
ult on integral extensions states: if $B$ is integral over $A$ and $B$ is a fiel
d, then $A$ is a field. (For any nonzero $a\in A$, its inverse $a^{-1}\in B$ sat
isfies a monic polynomial $a^{-m}+c_{m-1}a^{-(m-1)}+\dots+c_0=0$ with $c_i\in A$
; multiplying by $a^{m-1}$ gives $a^{-1}=-(c_{m-1}+\dots+c_0 a^{m-1})\in A$.)

Therefore $R=\mathbb{Z}[1/N]$ would have to be a field. But $\mathbb{Z}[1/N]$ is
 **not** a field: any prime $q\nmid N$ is not invertible in $\mathbb{Z}[1/N]$ (i
ts inverse $1/q$ cannot be written with denominator a power of $N$). This is a c
ontradiction.

Hence Case 2 is impossible.

**Conclusion.** Only Case 1 occurs, and there $K$ is a finite field. Therefore e
very field finitely generated as a $\mathbb{Z}$-algebra is finite (indeed, exact
ly the finite fields $\mathbb{F}_{p^r}$). $\blacksquare$
