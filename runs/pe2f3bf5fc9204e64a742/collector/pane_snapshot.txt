## Solution

Let $\mu$ be the probability measure induced by $F$, so $d\mu = f\,dx$ and $\mu(
(a,b])=F(b)-F(a)$. Since $F$ is continuous, $\mu$ has no atoms, hence
$$F(s+\varepsilon)-F(s-\varepsilon)=\mu\big((s-\varepsilon,s+\varepsilon)\big)=\
int_{s-\varepsilon}^{s+\varepsilon}f(t)\,dt.$$

**Identifying $S_1$.** By definition,
$$S_1=\{s:\forall\varepsilon>0,\ \mu((s-\varepsilon,s+\varepsilon))>0\},$$
which is exactly the **support** of $\mu$ — the set of points every open neighbo
urhood of which has positive $\mu$-measure. In particular $S_1$ is closed.

---

### Inclusion $S_1\subseteq S_2$ (always true)

Let $s\in S_1$. For every $\varepsilon>0$,
$$\int_{s-\varepsilon}^{s+\varepsilon}f(t)\,dt=\mu((s-\varepsilon,s+\varepsilon)
)>0.$$
A non-negative function with positive integral must be strictly positive on a se
t of positive Lebesgue measure inside $(s-\varepsilon,s+\varepsilon)$; in partic
ular $\{f>0\}\cap(s-\varepsilon,s+\varepsilon)\neq\varnothing$. Since this holds
 for every $\varepsilon>0$, every neighbourhood of $s$ meets $\{f>0\}$, so $s\in
\overline{\{f>0\}}=S_2$. Hence
$$S_1\subseteq S_2.$$

---

### The reverse inclusion $S_2\subseteq S_1$ can fail

The obstruction is that a density is only determined up to null sets, so the poi
ntwise set $\{f>0\}$ may contain spurious points lying outside $\operatorname{su
pp}(\mu)$.

**Counterexample.** Let $F$ be the uniform distribution on $[0,1]$:
$$F(s)=\begin{cases}0,&s<0,\\ s,&0\le s\le 1,\\ 1,&s\ge 1.\end{cases}$$
$F$ is continuous and non-decreasing, with density $f_0=\mathbf 1_{[0,1]}$. Defi
ne another version of the density by
$$f(x)=\mathbf 1_{[0,1]}(x)+\mathbf 1_{\{2\}}(x).$$
Since $f$ and $f_0$ differ only at the single point $x=2$, $\int_{\mathbb R}f=\i
nt_{\mathbb R}f_0=1$, so $f$ is a legitimate associated density for the *same* $
F$.

Now
$$\{f>0\}=[0,1]\cup\{2\},\qquad S_2=\overline{\{f>0\}}=[0,1]\cup\{2\}.$$
But $2\notin S_1$: for any $0<\varepsilon<1$,
$$F(2+\varepsilon)=1=F(2-\varepsilon),$$
so $F(2+\varepsilon)>F(2-\varepsilon)$ fails. Thus $S_1=[0,1]$ while $S_2=[0,1]\
cup\{2\}$, and
$$S_1\ne S_2.$$

---

### Conclusion

The two sets are **not equal in general**. The universally valid relation is
$$S_1\subseteq S_2,$$
i.e. the support of the measure is always contained in the closure of $\{f>0\}$.
 The reverse inclusion (and hence equality) requires additional regularity on th
e chosen version of $f$ — for instance, equality does hold when $f$ is continuou
s (so that $\{f>0\}$ is open and every point of $\overline{\{f>0\}}$ has neighbo
urhoods whose intersection with $\{f>0\}$ contains a non-trivial open interval o
f positive $f$-integral). Without such assumptions, the pointwise positivity set
 of an arbitrary version of the density can strictly overshoot the true support
of $F$.
