Looking at this problem, I need to determine which values of k (1 to 50) allow p
ainting all 100 circular stones black.

## Setting Up the Framework

**The move:** Choose k consecutive stones with first and last white; paint both
black. The two endpoints are at circular distance d = k − 1. The middle stones a
re irrelevant. So each move = pick two white stones at distance d, paint both bl
ack.

**Key reduction:** We need 50 moves (each removes 2 white stones). The 50 moves
define 50 pairs, each at distance d, forming a **perfect matching** of the 100 s
tones. Conversely, any perfect matching of distance-d pairs can be executed in a
ny order — when we process a pair, neither stone has been touched yet, so both a
re white. Thus:

> k works ⟺ a perfect matching exists in the graph on ℤ₁₀₀ where i ~ i+d (mod 10
0).

## Analyzing the Distance-d Graph

For d = k−1 ∈ {1,…,49}, each vertex connects to i+d and i−d (mod 100), giving a
**2-regular graph** = union of cycles. The number of cycles is gcd(d, 100), each
 of length 100/gcd(d, 100).

A perfect matching exists ⟺ every cycle has **even length** ⟺ 100/gcd(d,100) is
even ⟺ gcd(d, 100) **divides 50**.

The divisors of 100 are {1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20, 25, 50, 100}. Those dividing 50: {1
, 2, 5, 10, 25, 50}. Those **not** dividing 50: {4, 20, 100}.

## Identifying Excluded k

For d ∈ {1,…,49}:
- **gcd = 100:** impossible (d < 100).
- **gcd = 4:** d divisible by 4 but not 5.
- **gcd = 20:** d divisible by 20 but not 25 → d ∈ {20, 40}.

Combining: for d ∈ {1,…,49}, the condition "4 | d" captures both cases (if 5 ∤ d
 then gcd = 4; if 5 | d then 20 | d and 25 ∤ d so gcd = 20; and 25 | d with 4 |
d would force 100 | d, impossible here).

So **k ≥ 2 fails ⟺ 4 | (k−1) ⟺ k ≡ 1 (mod 4)**.

The excluded values (k ∈ {2,…,50}, k ≡ 1 mod 4):
$$\{5, 9, 13, 17, 21, 25, 29, 33, 37, 41, 45, 49\} \quad (12 \text{ values})$$

## Handling k = 1

For k = 1: each move paints a single white stone black (first = last = that ston
e). 100 moves trivially paint all stones black. **k = 1 ∈ S.** (Note: k = 1 ≡ 1
mod 4, but it's special since d = 0, not covered by the graph analysis.)

## Computing the Sum

Sum of all k from 1 to 50: $\frac{50 \cdot 51}{2} = 1275$.

Excluded sum (arithmetic progression, 12 terms, first 5, last 49, difference 4):
$$\frac{12 \cdot (5 + 49)}{2} = 12 \cdot 27 = 324$$

**Sum of S = 1275 − 324 = 951.**

## Answer

$$S = \{1\} \cup \{k \in \{2,\dots,50\} : k \not\equiv 1 \pmod{4}\}$$

$$\sum_{k \in S} k = \boxed{951}$$
