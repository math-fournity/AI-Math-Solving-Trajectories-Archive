## Proof

**Answer: Yes, the marginal distribution of $(X_1, \ldots, X_k)$ converges weakl
y to $N(0, I_k)$ as $d \to \infty$.**

### Setup: Gaussian representation

Let $Z = (Z_1, \ldots, Z_d)$ where $Z_i \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} N(0,1)$.
The random vector $Z/\|Z\|$ is uniformly distributed on the unit sphere $S^{d-1}
(1)$ (by rotational invariance of the Gaussian measure). Therefore

$$X = \sqrt{d}\,\frac{Z}{\|Z\|}$$

is uniformly distributed on the sphere of radius $\sqrt{d}$, matching the given
distribution. Componentwise,

$$X_i = \sqrt{d}\,\frac{Z_i}{\|Z\|} = \frac{Z_i}{\sqrt{S_d}}, \qquad S_d := \fra
c{\|Z\|^2}{d} = \frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d} Z_j^2.$$

### Step 1: $S_d \to 1$ almost surely

Write $S_d = \frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{k} Z_j^2 + \frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=k+1}^{d} Z_j^2
$.

- The first term: $\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{k} Z_j^2 \to 0$ a.s., since the numera
tor is a fixed finite random variable (for fixed $k$) and the denominator $d \to
 \infty$.
- The second term: $\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=k+1}^{d} Z_j^2 = \frac{d-k}{d}\cdot\frac{
1}{d-k}\sum_{j=k+1}^{d} Z_j^2$. By the Strong Law of Large Numbers, $\frac{1}{d-
k}\sum_{j=k+1}^{d} Z_j^2 \to \mathbb{E}[Z_1^2] = 1$ a.s., and $\frac{d-k}{d} \to
 1$. Hence this term $\to 1$ a.s.

Combining: $S_d \to 1$ a.s., and therefore $\frac{1}{\sqrt{S_d}} \to 1$ a.s.

### Step 2: Apply Slutsky's theorem (multivariate)

We have the decomposition

$$(X_1, \ldots, X_k) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{S_d}}\,(Z_1, \ldots, Z_k).$$

- The vector $(Z_1, \ldots, Z_k) \sim N(0, I_k)$ for every $d$ (its distribution
 does not depend on $d$), so trivially $(Z_1, \ldots, Z_k) \xrightarrow{d} (Z_1,
 \ldots, Z_k) \sim N(0, I_k)$.
- The scalar $\frac{1}{\sqrt{S_d}} \to 1$ a.s., hence in probability.

By **Slutsky's theorem** (multivariate form): if $V_d \xrightarrow{d} V$ and $a_
d \xrightarrow{p} c$ (a constant), then $a_d \cdot V_d \xrightarrow{d} c \cdot V
$. Applying this with $V_d = (Z_1, \ldots, Z_k)$, $a_d = 1/\sqrt{S_d}$, and $c =
 1$:

$$(X_1, \ldots, X_k) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{S_d}}(Z_1, \ldots, Z_k) \xrightarrow{d} 1
\cdot (Z_1, \ldots, Z_k) \sim N(0, I_k).$$

### Conclusion

For every fixed $k \ge 2$, the marginal distribution of $(X_1, \ldots, X_k)$ con
verges weakly to $N(0, I_k)$ as $d \to \infty$. The key mechanism is that the no
rmalization factor $\|Z\|/\sqrt{d} \to 1$ concentrates, so the first $k$ coordin
ates—each asymptotically $N(0,1)$—become asymptotically independent, with the sp
herical constraint $\sum_{i=1}^{k} X_i^2 \le d$ becoming negligible for fixed $k
$.
