## Proof

**Step 1: Factor the curve.**

We factor $x^3 + y^3 + 3xy - 1$. Since $x^3 + y^3 = (x+y)(x^2 - xy + y^2)$, we t
est the factor $(x+y-1)$ (noting that when $x+y=1$, $x^3+y^3+3xy = (1-3xy)+3xy =
 1$):

$$x^3 + y^3 + 3xy - 1 = (x+y-1)(x^2 - xy + y^2 + x + y + 1).$$

Verification by expansion confirms this. So the curve is the union of:
- the line $L: x + y = 1$, and
- the conic $Q: x^2 - xy + y^2 + x + y + 1 = 0$.

**Step 2: The conic $Q$ is a single point.**

Set $s = x+y$, $p = xy$. Then $x^2 - xy + y^2 = s^2 - 3p$, so $Q$ becomes $s^2 -
 3p + s + 1 = 0$, i.e. $p = \frac{s^2+s+1}{3}$. For real $x,y$ with sum $s$ and
product $p$, we need $s^2 - 4p \geq 0$:

$$s^2 \geq \frac{4(s^2+s+1)}{3} \implies 3s^2 \geq 4s^2 + 4s + 4 \implies 0 \geq
 s^2 + 4s + 4 = (s+2)^2.$$

Hence $s = -2$ and $s^2 - 4p = 0$, giving $x = y = -1$. So $Q$ is the single poi
nt $P = (-1,-1)$, which is not on $L$ (since $-1+(-1) = -2 \neq 1$).

**Step 3: The only equilateral triangle on the curve.**

The curve is $L \cup \{P\}$. Three distinct points forming an equilateral triang
le cannot all lie on the line $L$ (collinear). So exactly two vertices, say $A,
B$, lie on $L$, and the third is $P$.

For $\triangle PAB$ equilateral with $A, B \in L$: since $PA = PB$, point $P$ li
es on the perpendicular bisector of $AB$, so the midpoint $M$ of $AB$ is the foo
t of the perpendicular from $P$ to $L$.

The line through $P = (-1,-1)$ perpendicular to $x+y=1$ has direction $(1,1)$: $
(-1+t, -1+t)$. Substituting into $x+y=1$: $2t - 2 = 1$, so $t = 3/2$ and $M = (1
/2, 1/2)$.

The distance from $P$ to $L$ is the height of the equilateral triangle:
$$h = \frac{|{-1-1-1}|}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}.$$

For an equilateral triangle with side $a$, the height is $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}a$,
so:
$$\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,a = \frac{3}{\sqrt{2}} \implies a = \frac{6}{\sqrt{6}} = \
sqrt{6}.$$

The direction along $L$ is $(1,-1)/\sqrt{2}$, so:
$$A = M + \tfrac{\sqrt{6}}{2}\cdot\tfrac{(1,-1)}{\sqrt{2}} = \left(\tfrac{1+\sqr
t{3}}{2},\, \tfrac{1-\sqrt{3}}{2}\right),$$
$$B = M - \tfrac{\sqrt{6}}{2}\cdot\tfrac{(1,-1)}{\sqrt{2}} = \left(\tfrac{1-\sqr
t{3}}{2},\, \tfrac{1+\sqrt{3}}{2}\right).$$

Both lie on $L$ (hence on the curve) and are distinct.

**Verification.** $PA^2 = \left(\frac{3+\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{3-\s
qrt{3}}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{12+6\sqrt{3}+12-6\sqrt{3}}{4} = 6$, and $AB^2 = (\sq
rt{3})^2 + (\sqrt{3})^2 = 6$. So $PA = PB = AB = \sqrt{6}$. ✓

**Uniqueness.** Given the curve $L \cup \{P\}$, any equilateral triangle must us
e $P$ and two points on $L$. The foot $M$ of the perpendicular from $P$ to $L$ i
s uniquely determined, the side length is forced by the height, and $A, B$ are s
ymmetric about $M$ along $L$. Thus the triangle is unique (up to swapping $A \le
ftrightarrow B$).
