We evaluate
$$I = \int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\cos x - \sin x}{x^{1/2}}\,dx.$$

### Step 1. Rewrite the numerator as a real part

Observe that
$$(1+i)e^{ix} = (1+i)(\cos x + i\sin x) = (\cos x - \sin x) + i(\sin x + \cos x)
,$$
so
$$\cos x - \sin x = \operatorname{Re}\!\big((1+i)e^{ix}\big).$$
Hence
$$I = \operatorname{Re}\!\left((1+i)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{ix}}{x^{1/2}}\,dx\
right).$$

### Step 2. Evaluate $\displaystyle J(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}e^{ix}\,dx$ for
 $0<\operatorname{Re}(s)<1$

Consider the contour $C$ in the first quadrant consisting of:
- $\gamma_1$: the real segment from $\varepsilon$ to $R$,
- $\gamma_2$: the quarter-circle arc $z=Re^{i\theta}$, $0\le\theta\le\pi/2$,
- $\gamma_3$: the imaginary segment from $iR$ down to $i\varepsilon$,
- $\gamma_4$: the quarter-circle arc $z=\varepsilon e^{i\theta}$, $0\le\theta\le
\pi/2$ (traversed inward).

Let $f(z)=z^{s-1}e^{iz}$, where the branch of $z^{s-1}$ is taken with $\arg z\in
[0,\pi/2]$. By Cauchy's theorem,
$$\oint_C f(z)\,dz = 0.$$

**On $\gamma_1$:** $z=x$, $dz=dx$, giving $\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon}^{R}x^
{s-1}e^{ix}\,dx\to J(s)$ as $\varepsilon\to0$, $R\to\infty$.

**On $\gamma_3$:** $z=iy$, $dz=i\,dy$, $z^{s-1}=(iy)^{s-1}=e^{i\pi(s-1)/2}y^{s-1
}$, $e^{iz}=e^{-y}$. Traversing from $iR$ to $i\varepsilon$ (i.e. $y$ from $R$ t
o $\varepsilon$):
$$\int_{\gamma_3} f\,dz = \int_{R}^{\varepsilon}e^{i\pi(s-1)/2}y^{s-1}e^{-y}\,i\
,dy = -i\,e^{i\pi(s-1)/2}\int_{\varepsilon}^{R}y^{s-1}e^{-y}\,dy \to -i\,e^{i\pi
(s-1)/2}\,\Gamma(s).$$

**On $\gamma_2$ (large arc):** $|z^{s-1}e^{iz}|=R^{\operatorname{Re}(s)-1}e^{-R\
sin\theta}$. For $0<\operatorname{Re}(s)<1$, Jordan's lemma gives $\int_{\gamma_
2}\to0$ as $R\to\infty$.

**On $\gamma_4$ (small arc):** $|z^{s-1}e^{iz}|=\varepsilon^{\operatorname{Re}(s
)-1}e^{-\varepsilon\sin\theta}$, and the arc length is $\sim\varepsilon$, so the
 integral is $O(\varepsilon^{\operatorname{Re}(s)})\to0$ as $\varepsilon\to0$ (s
ince $\operatorname{Re}(s)>0$).

Assembling:
$$J(s) - i\,e^{i\pi(s-1)/2}\,\Gamma(s) = 0.$$
Since $e^{i\pi(s-1)/2}=e^{i\pi s/2}\,e^{-i\pi/2}=-i\,e^{i\pi s/2}$, we get $i\,e
^{i\pi(s-1)/2}=i(-i)e^{i\pi s/2}=e^{i\pi s/2}$. Therefore
$$\boxed{J(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}e^{ix}\,dx = e^{i\pi s/2}\,\Gamma(s),\qqua
d 0<\operatorname{Re}(s)<1.}$$

### Step 3. Specialize to $s=1/2$

With $s=\tfrac12$ and $\Gamma(\tfrac12)=\sqrt{\pi}$:
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{ix}}{x^{1/2}}\,dx = e^{i\pi/4}\sqrt{\pi} = \frac{1+i
}{\sqrt{2}}\,\sqrt{\pi}.$$

Taking real and imaginary parts recovers the classical Fresnel-type integrals:
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\cos x}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}},\qquad \in
t_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin x}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}.$$

### Step 4. Combine

$$I = \operatorname{Re}\!\left((1+i)\cdot e^{i\pi/4}\sqrt{\pi}\right) = \operato
rname{Re}\!\left((1+i)\cdot\frac{1+i}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot\sqrt{\pi}\right) = \operato
rname{Re}\!\left(\frac{(1+i)^2}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\pi}\right).$$

Since $(1+i)^2 = 2i$,
$$I = \operatorname{Re}\!\left(\frac{2i}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\pi}\right) = \operatorn
ame{Re}\!\big(i\sqrt{2\pi}\big) = 0.$$

Equivalently, directly from the Fresnel values:
$$I = \int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\cos x}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx - \int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin
 x}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}} - \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}} = 0.$$

### Result

$$\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\cos x - \sin x}{x^{1/2}}\,dx = 0.$$
