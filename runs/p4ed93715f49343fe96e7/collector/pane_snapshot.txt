## Proof

**Claim.** The set $S_r = \{A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n} : \operatorname{rank}(
A) \le r\}$ has $mn$-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero whenever $r < \min\{m, n\
}$.

---

**Step 1. Partition by exact rank.**

Write $S_r = \bigcup_{k=0}^{r} M_k$ where $M_k = \{A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}
 : \operatorname{rank}(A) = k\}$. It suffices to show each $M_k$ ($0 \le k \le r
$) has measure zero, since a finite union of measure-zero sets has measure zero.

**Step 2. $M_k$ is a smooth submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ of dimensio
n $k(m+n-k)$.**

Fix $A_0 \in M_k$. Since $\operatorname{rank}(A_0) = k$, there exist $k$ linearl
y independent rows and $k$ linearly independent columns; after permuting rows an
d columns (a diffeomorphism of $\mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$), write $A_0$ in block
form

$$A_0 = \begin{pmatrix} A_{11} & A_{12} \\ A_{21} & A_{22} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
 A_{11} \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times k} \text{ invertible},$$

with row partition $(k,\, m-k)$ and column partition $(k,\, n-k)$.

For any $A$ in a neighborhood of $A_0$ with $A_{11}$ still invertible, perform b
lock elimination:

$$\begin{pmatrix} I & 0 \\ -A_{21}A_{11}^{-1} & I \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}
A_{11} & A_{12} \\ A_{21} & A_{22} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} A_{11} & A_{1
2} \\ 0 & A_{22} - A_{21}A_{11}^{-1}A_{12} \end{pmatrix}.$$

The left factor is invertible, so

$$\operatorname{rank}(A) = \operatorname{rank}(A_{11}) + \operatorname{rank}\!\b
igl(A_{22} - A_{21}A_{11}^{-1}A_{12}\bigr) = k + \operatorname{rank}(\text{Schur
 complement}).$$

Hence $\operatorname{rank}(A) = k$ if and only if the Schur complement vanishes:

$$A_{22} = A_{21}\, A_{11}^{-1}\, A_{12}.$$

The right-hand side $f(A_{11}, A_{12}, A_{21}) := A_{21}A_{11}^{-1}A_{12}$ is a
smooth function of the free blocks $(A_{11}, A_{12}, A_{21})$, which range over
an open subset of

$$\mathbb{R}^{k \times k} \times \mathbb{R}^{k \times (n-k)} \times \mathbb{R}^{
(m-k) \times k},$$

a space of dimension $k^2 + k(n-k) + (m-k)k = k(m+n-k)$.

Thus, near $A_0$, the set $M_k$ is the **graph** of the smooth map $f$:

$$M_k \cap U = \bigl\{(A_{11}, A_{12}, A_{21},\, f(A_{11},A_{12},A_{21})) : (A_{
11}, A_{12}, A_{21}) \in V\bigr\},$$

which is a smooth submanifold of dimension $k(m+n-k)$. Since $A_0$ was arbitrary
, $M_k$ is globally a smooth submanifold of dimension $k(m+n-k)$.

**Step 3. The dimension of $M_k$ is strictly less than $mn$.**

We must verify $k(m+n-k) < mn$ for all $0 \le k \le r < \min\{m,n\}$.

Compute:

$$mn - k(m+n-k) = mn - km - kn + k^2 = (m-k)(n-k).$$

Since $k \le r < \min\{m, n\}$, we have $m - k \ge 1$ and $n - k \ge 1$, so $(m-
k)(n-k) \ge 1 > 0$. Therefore

$$k(m+n-k) = mn - (m-k)(n-k) < mn.$$

**Step 4. Lower-dimensional submanifolds have measure zero.**

A standard result in measure theory: any smooth submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^{mn}$
 of dimension $d < mn$ has $mn$-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero. (Proof sketch
: cover the submanifold by countably many coordinate charts; in each chart it is
 the graph of a smooth — hence locally Lipschitz — function from $\mathbb{R}^d$
to $\mathbb{R}^{mn-d}$; a Lipschitz image of a bounded subset of $\mathbb{R}^d$
in $\mathbb{R}^{mn}$ has $mn$-dimensional measure zero when $d < mn$; countable
subadditivity finishes the argument.)

Applying this to each $M_k$ ($0 \le k \le r$): each $M_k$ has measure zero.

**Step 5. Conclusion.**

$$S_r = \bigcup_{k=0}^{r} M_k$$

is a finite union of measure-zero sets, hence has $mn$-dimensional Lebesgue meas
ure **zero**. $\blacksquare$

---

The measure of the set of $m \times n$ real matrices with rank $\le r$ (where $r
 < \min\{m, n\}$) is **$0$**.
