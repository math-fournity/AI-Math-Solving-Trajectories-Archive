## Answer

**No, in general $B_Y$ is not weakly compact in $Y$.** A counterexample suffices
.

---

### Counterexample

Let $E = \ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})$ over $\mathbb{R}$, with standard basis $\{e_{n}\}
_{n\ge 1}$. Recall two classical facts:

1. **Schur property of $\ell^{1}$**: every weakly convergent sequence in $\ell^{
1}$ is norm-convergent. Consequently, a subset of $\ell^{1}$ is weakly compact i
ff it is norm compact.
2. **Kakutani's theorem**: a Banach space is reflexive iff its closed unit ball
is weakly compact. Since $\ell^{1}$ is not reflexive, $B_{\ell^{1}}$ is **not**
weakly compact.

Define
$$
a_{n} := \frac{e_{n}}{n}, \qquad K := \{a_{n}: n\ge 1\}\cup\{0\}\subseteq \ell^{
1}.
$$

**Step 1. $K$ is weakly compact in $E$.**
Since $\|a_{n}\|_{1} = \tfrac{1}{n}\to 0$, the sequence $(a_{n})$ converges to $
0$ in norm, so $K$ is a norm-compact set (a convergent sequence together with it
s limit). By the Schur property, norm-compact sets in $\ell^{1}$ are weakly comp
act. Hence $K$ is weakly compact in $E$.

**Step 2. The closed linear span of $K$ is all of $\ell^{1}$.**
Since $e_{n} = n\,a_{n}\in\operatorname{span}(K)$ for every $n$, the (algebraic)
 span of $K$ contains every basis vector $e_{n}$, hence is norm-dense in $\ell^{
1}$. Therefore
$$
Y := \overline{\operatorname{span}}(K) = \ell^{1}.
$$

**Step 3. $B_{Y}$ is not weakly compact.**
With $Y=\ell^{1}$, the unit ball $B_{Y}=B_{\ell^{1}}$ is not weakly compact by K
akutani's theorem, because $\ell^{1}$ is not reflexive.

---

### Remark on the interpretation of "$\operatorname{span}$"

Even if one reads $Y=\operatorname{span}(K)$ as the *algebraic* span (the incomp
lete normed space $c_{00}$ of finitely supported sequences with the $\ell^{1}$-n
orm), the conclusion is the same. Indeed $Y^{*}\cong \ell^{\infty}=(\ell^{1})^{*
}$, so the weak topology $\sigma(Y,Y^{*})$ is the restriction of $\sigma(\ell^{1
},\ell^{\infty})$ to $Y$. The unit ball $B_{Y}=\{y\in c_{00}:\|y\|_{1}\le 1\}$ i
s norm-dense in $B_{\ell^{1}}$, hence weakly dense in $B_{\ell^{1}}$. If $B_{Y}$
 were weakly compact, it would be weakly closed (Hausdorff), forcing $B_{Y}=B_{\
ell^{1}}$, a contradiction since e.g. $(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{4},\tfrac{1}{8},\
dots)\in B_{\ell^{1}}\setminus c_{00}$. Thus $B_{Y}$ is not weakly compact in th
is reading either.

---

### Conclusion

Weak compactness of $K$ does **not** propagate to the unit ball of its span. The
 closed convex hull of a weakly compact set is weakly compact (Kreĭn's theorem),
 but the closed *linear* span can be a non-reflexive Banach space, whose unit ba
ll fails to be weakly compact.
