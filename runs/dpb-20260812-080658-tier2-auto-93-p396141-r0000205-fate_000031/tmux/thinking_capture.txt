Proof

Let $G$ be a finite group and $\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(G)$ such that

  • $\sigma(g)=g \iff g=1$ (fixed-point-free), and
  • $\sigma^{2}=\operatorname{id}_{G}$ (order dividing $2$; combined with fixed-
  point-free, $\sigma\neq\operatorname{id}$, so $\sigma$ has order exactly $2$).

Step 1. The map $\phi\colon G\to G$, $\phi(g)=g^{-1}\sigma(g)$, is injective.

Suppose $\phi(g)=\phi(h)$, i.e. $g^{-1}\sigma(g)=h^{-1}\sigma(h)$. Then $$
hg^{-1}=\sigma(h),\sigma(g)^{-1}=\sigma(hg^{-1}). $$ So $hg^{-1}$ is a fixed
point of $\sigma$. By the fixed-point-free hypothesis, $hg^{-1}=1$, hence $h=g$.
Thus $\phi$ is injective.

Since $G$ is finite, $\phi$ is also surjective: every $x\in G$ can be written as
$$ x=g^{-1}\sigma(g)\quad\text{for some }g\in G. \tag{$\ast$} $$

Step 2. $\sigma$ acts as inversion: $\sigma(x)=x^{-1}$ for every $x\in G$.

Given $x\in G$, write $x=g^{-1}\sigma(g)$ as in $(\ast)$. Applying $\sigma$ and
using that it is a homomorphism, $$
\sigma(x)=\sigma(g^{-1}\sigma(g))=\sigma(g)^{-1},\sigma^{2}(g)=\sigma(g)^{-1}
g=\bigl(g^{-1}\sigma(g)\bigr)^{-1}=x^{-1}. $$

Step 3. $G$ is abelian.

Since $\sigma$ is an automorphism, it is a homomorphism, so for all $x,y\in G$,
$$ \sigma(xy)=\sigma(x),\sigma(y). $$ Using Step 2 on both sides, $$ (xy)^{-1}
=x^{-1}y^{-1}. $$ But in any group $(xy)^{-1}=y^{-1}x^{-1}$. Hence $$ x^{-1}
y^{-1}=y^{-1}x^{-1}\quad\text{for all }x,y\in G. $$ Taking inverses of both
sides gives $yx=xy$ for all $x,y\in G$. Therefore $G$ is abelian. $\blacksquare$
