Looking at this problem, I need to find all possible edge lengths of a regular t
etrahedron whose orthogonal projection onto a plane yields a convex kite $A'B'C'
D'$ (since $A'B'=A'D'$ and $C'B'=C'D'$) with area 4.

## Setup

Place the regular tetrahedron (edge $2\sqrt{2}$) at:
$$A=(1,1,1),\ B=(1,-1,-1),\ C=(-1,1,-1),\ D=(-1,-1,1)$$

Orthogonal projection onto a plane with unit normal $\hat{n}=(n_1,n_2,n_3)$ send
s $P$ to $P' = P - (P\cdot\hat{n})\hat{n}$. Since projection preserves lengths p
erpendicular to $\hat{n}$:
$$|P'Q'|^2 = |P-Q|^2 - ((P-Q)\cdot\hat{n})^2$$

## Deriving the constraints

**Condition $A'B'=A'D'$:** Since $|AB|=|AD|$, we need $((A-B)\cdot\hat{n})^2 = (
(A-D)\cdot\hat{n})^2$. With $A-B=(0,2,2)$, $A-D=(2,2,0)$:
$$(n_2+n_3)^2 = (n_1+n_2)^2 \implies n_2+n_3 = \pm(n_1+n_2)$$

**Condition $C'B'=C'D'$:** With $C-B=(-2,2,0)$, $C-D=(0,2,-2)$:
$$(n_2-n_1)^2 = (n_2-n_3)^2 \implies n_2-n_1 = \pm(n_2-n_3)$$

## Combining the four cases

Checking all sign combinations, three yield degenerate projections (along a vert
ex direction or edge midpoint direction, collapsing a vertex or pair). The only
non-degenerate family is:

$$n_1 = n_3, \quad \text{i.e., } \hat{n} = (t, u, t), \quad 2t^2 + u^2 = 1$$

## Computing the projected kite

The dot products with $\hat{n}$: $A\cdot\hat{n}=2t+u$, $B\cdot\hat{n}=-u$, $C\cd
ot\hat{n}=-2t+u$, $D\cdot\hat{n}=-u$.

Key observation: $B\cdot\hat{n} = D\cdot\hat{n} = -u$, and $(B-D)\cdot\hat{n} =
0$, so $|B'D'| = |BD| = 2\sqrt{2}$ (unchanged by projection).

Computing $A'-C'$ and using $u^2 = 1-2t^2$:
$$A'-C' = (2u^2, -4tu, 2u^2), \quad |A'C'|^2 = 8u^2, \quad |A'C'| = 2\sqrt{2}\,|
u|$$

Both $A'$ and $C'$ lie on the perpendicular bisector of $B'D'$ (verified by dot
product with $B'-D'=(2,0,-2)$ equaling zero), confirming the kite structure. The
 diagonals are perpendicular, so:

$$\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2}|A'C'|\cdot|B'D'| = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 2\sqrt{2}\,|u|
\cdot 2\sqrt{2} = 4|u|$$

## Convexity condition

Writing $A'-\text{mid} = (u-2t)\vec{v}$ and $C'-\text{mid} = -(u+2t)\vec{v}$ for
 direction $\vec{v}=(u,-2t,u)$, the quadrilateral is convex iff $A'$ and $C'$ ar
e on **opposite** sides of line $B'D'$:
$$(u-2t)(u+2t) > 0 \implies u^2 - 4t^2 > 0 \implies 3u^2 - 2 > 0 \implies |u| >
\sqrt{2/3}$$

At $|u|=\sqrt{2/3}$: one vertex lands on $B'D'$ (degenerate). At $|u|=1$ ($t=0$)
: projection onto the $xz$-plane gives a square (convex). So $|u| \in (\sqrt{2/3
},\, 1]$.

## Scaling to area 4

For edge length $s$, scale factor $\lambda = s/(2\sqrt{2})$, area scales by $\la
mbda^2 = s^2/8$:
$$\text{Area} = 4|u|\cdot\frac{s^2}{8} = \frac{|u|\,s^2}{2} = 4 \implies s^2 = \
frac{8}{|u|}$$

As $|u|$ ranges over $(\sqrt{2/3},\, 1]$:
- $|u|=1$: $s^2 = 8$ (included)
- $|u|\to\sqrt{2/3}^+$: $s^2 \to 8/\sqrt{2/3} = 4\sqrt{6}$ (excluded)

So $s^2 \in [8,\, 4\sqrt{6})$, giving $s \in [2\sqrt{2},\, 2\sqrt[4]{6})$.

## Result

$$a = 2\sqrt{2}, \quad b = 2\sqrt[4]{6}, \quad b^4 = 2^4 \cdot 6 = \boxed{96}$$
