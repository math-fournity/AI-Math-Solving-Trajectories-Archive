## Character of the Standard (Permutation) Representation of $S_3$ on $\mathbb{R
}^3$

**Setup.** The standard representation of $S_3$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$ is the permuta
tion representation: each $\sigma \in S_3$ acts by permuting the coordinates,
$$\sigma \cdot (x_1, x_2, x_3) = (x_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}, x_{\sigma^{-1}(2)}, x_{\si
gma^{-1}(3)}),$$
equivalently, $\sigma$ permutes the standard basis vectors $e_1, e_2, e_3$.

**Character via fixed points.** The matrix of $\sigma$ in the standard basis has
 a $1$ in position $(i, \sigma(i))$, so its trace equals the number of fixed poi
nts of $\sigma$:
$$\chi(\sigma) = |\{i \in \{1,2,3\} : \sigma(i) = i\}|.$$

**Conjugacy classes of $S_3$.** There are three classes:

| Class | Representative | Size | Cycle type | Fixed points | $\chi$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| identity | $e$ | 1 | $1^3$ | 3 | **3** |
| transpositions | $(12)$ | 3 | $2\,1$ | 1 | **1** |
| 3-cycles | $(123)$ | 2 | $3$ | 0 | **0** |

**Verification of class-function property.** The character depends only on cycle
 type (number of fixed points), so it is constant on each conjugacy class, as re
quired.

**Decomposition (consistency check).** The permutation representation splits as
$$\mathbb{R}^3 = \mathbf{1} \oplus V,$$
where $\mathbf{1}$ is the trivial line $\langle(1,1,1)\rangle$ (character $(1,1,
1)$) and $V = \{x : x_1+x_2+x_3=0\}$ is the 2-dimensional irreducible standard r
epresentation. Subtracting the trivial character:
$$(3,1,0) - (1,1,1) = (2, 0, -1),$$
which is indeed the irreducible 2-dimensional character of $S_3$ (verifying $\la
ngle \chi_{\text{perm}}, \chi_{\text{perm}}\rangle = \tfrac{1}{6}(3^2\cdot1 + 1^
2\cdot3 + 0^2\cdot2) = \tfrac{1}{6}(9+3) = 2$, confirming the representation is
the sum of exactly two irreducibles).

**Answer.** The character of the standard (permutation) representation of $S_3$
on $\mathbb{R}^3$, expressed on the conjugacy classes, is:

$$\boxed{\chi(e) = 3, \quad \chi(\text{transposition}) = 1, \quad \chi(\text{3-c
ycle}) = 0.}$$

Equivalently, as a class vector $(\chi(C_1), \chi(C_2), \chi(C_3)) = (3,\, 1,\,
0)$.
