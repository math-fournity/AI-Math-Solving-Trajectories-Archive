## Proof

**Step 1: Identify the tangent lines.**

For P = (a, a²) on y = x², the line ℓ(P) through P with slope 2a has equation:
$$y - a^2 = 2a(x-a) \implies y = 2ax - a^2.$$
Since dy/dx = 2x = 2a at x = a, this is precisely the **tangent line** to the pa
rabola y = x² at P.

**Step 2: Compute the vertices of Δ.**

The tangent lines ℓᵢ: y = 2aᵢx − aᵢ² and ℓⱼ: y = 2aⱼx − aⱼ² intersect at:
$$2a_i x - a_i^2 = 2a_j x - a_j^2 \implies x = \frac{a_i+a_j}{2}, \quad y = a_i
a_j.$$
So the three vertices of Δ are:
$$Q_{12}=\left(\tfrac{a_1+a_2}{2},\, a_1 a_2\right),\quad Q_{23}=\left(\tfrac{a_
2+a_3}{2},\, a_2 a_3\right),\quad Q_{31}=\left(\tfrac{a_3+a_1}{2},\, a_3 a_1\rig
ht).$$

Note that side Q₁₂Q₃₁ lies on ℓ₁, side Q₁₂Q₂₃ lies on ℓ₂, and side Q₂₃Q₃₁ lies o
n ℓ₃. Therefore the **interior angle at each vertex equals the angle between the
 corresponding pair of tangent lines**.

**Step 3: Equilateral condition.**

Let φᵢ = arctan(2aᵢ) be the direction angle of ℓᵢ. Ordering φ₁ < φ₂ < φ₃, the in
terior angles of Δ are:
$$\varphi_2 - \varphi_1, \quad \varphi_3 - \varphi_2, \quad 180° - (\varphi_3 -
\varphi_1).$$
For Δ to be equilateral, all three equal 60°, giving:
$$\varphi_2 - \varphi_1 = 60°,\quad \varphi_3 - \varphi_2 = 60°,\quad \varphi_3
- \varphi_1 = 120°.$$
Thus the three directions are **equally spaced by 60°**: there exists α such tha
t (after reordering)
$$\varphi_1 = \alpha,\quad \varphi_2 = \alpha + 60°,\quad \varphi_3 = \alpha + 1
20°.$$
Since 2aᵢ = tan(φᵢ), we have $a_i = \frac{1}{2}\tan\varphi_i$.

**Step 4: Compute the center.**

For an equilateral triangle, all centers (centroid, circumcenter, etc.) coincide
. Using the centroid:
$$G = \left(\frac{s}{3},\, \frac{p}{3}\right), \quad s = a_1+a_2+a_3,\quad p = a
_1 a_2 + a_2 a_3 + a_3 a_1.$$

Set t = tan α. Then:
$$a_1 = \tfrac{t}{2},\quad a_2 = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{t+\sqrt{3}}{1-t\sqrt{3}}
,\quad a_3 = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{t-\sqrt{3}}{1+t\sqrt{3}}.$$

**Sum S = tan α + tan(α+60°) + tan(α+120°):**

$$\tan(\alpha+60°)+\tan(\alpha+120°) = \frac{8t}{1-3t^2},$$
$$S = t + \frac{8t}{1-3t^2} = \frac{t(9-3t^2)}{1-3t^2} = \frac{3t(3-t^2)}{1-3t^2
} = 3\tan(3\alpha).$$

So $s = S/2 = \frac{3}{2}\tan(3\alpha)$, and the x-coordinate of the center is:
$$\frac{s}{3} = \frac{1}{2}\tan(3\alpha).$$

**Sum of pairwise products P = tan α·tan(α+60°) + tan(α+60°)·tan(α+120°) + tan(α
+120°)·tan α:**

$$\tan\alpha\cdot\tan(\alpha+60°) + \tan(\alpha+120°)\cdot\tan\alpha = \frac{8t^
2}{1-3t^2},$$
$$\tan(\alpha+60°)\cdot\tan(\alpha+120°) = \frac{t^2-3}{1-3t^2},$$
$$P = \frac{8t^2 + t^2 - 3}{1-3t^2} = \frac{9t^2-3}{1-3t^2} = \frac{3(3t^2-1)}{1
-3t^2} = -3.$$

This is **constant**, independent of α! Therefore:
$$p = \frac{P}{4} = -\frac{3}{4}, \quad \text{y-coordinate of center} = \frac{p}
{3} = -\frac{1}{4}.$$

**Step 5: The locus.**

The center is:
$$G = \left(\frac{1}{2}\tan(3\alpha),\; -\frac{1}{4}\right).$$

As α varies over all values (excluding the degenerate cases α = 30° + 60°n where
 one tangent point recedes to infinity), $\tan(3\alpha)$ ranges over **all real
numbers**. Therefore the x-coordinate takes all real values, while the y-coordin
ate is constantly −1/4.

**Verification:** For α = 0°: a₁ = 0, a₂ = √3/2, a₃ = −√3/2. The vertices are (√
3/4, 0), (0, −3/4), (−√3/4, 0). All side lengths squared equal 3/4 ✓, and the ce
ntroid is (0, −1/4) ✓.

## Conclusion

The locus of the center of Δ is the horizontal line:

$$\boxed{y = -\frac{1}{4}}$$
