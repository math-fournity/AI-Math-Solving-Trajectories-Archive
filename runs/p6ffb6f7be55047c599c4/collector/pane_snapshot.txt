## Proof

 the $n$ bandits where each vertex has out-degree exactly $2$ (each bandit aims
at two others). Fix a calling order (a permutation). Process the bandits in this
 order: when a bandit is called and is still alive, they shoot at their two targ
ets, killing any target that is currently alive.

Let $A$ denote the set of bandits who are **alive when called** (the "shooters")
, and let $D = V \setminus A$ be those already dead when called. Let $N^+(A)$ de
note the set of all out-neighbors of $A$ in $G$ (the union of targets of members
 of $A$).

---

**Lemma 1 (Structure of $A$).** *For the set $A$ arising from any order:*
1. *The induced subgraph $G[A]$ is a DAG.*
2. *$V \setminus A \subseteq N^+(A)$.*
3. *The number of victims equals $|N^+(A)|$.*

*Proof of Lemma 1.*
(1): Suppose $u, v \in A$ and $u \to v$ in $G$ with $u$ called before $v$. When
$u$ is called, $v$ is still alive (since $v \in A$ means $v$ is alive at $v$'s o
wn turn, which is later). So $u$ shoots and kills $v$, contradicting $v \in A$.
Hence every edge within $A$ points from a later-called bandit to an earlier-call
ed one, so $G[A]$ is acyclic.

(2): If $w \in D$, then $w$ was dead when called, so some alive shooter killed $
w$ before $w$'s turn; that shooter is in $A$ and targets $w$, giving $w \in N^+(
A)$.

(3): Every victim is shot by an alive shooter, hence lies in $N^+(A)$. Conversel
y, take any $w \in N^+(A)$ and let $u \in A$ be the **first** bandit in the orde
r who targets $w$. When $u$ is called, $w$ is still alive (no earlier $A$-member
 targets $w$, and $D$-members never shoot), so $u$ kills $w$. Thus $w$ is a vict
im. So victims $= |N^+(A)|$. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (Key inequality).** *For any two orders with associated shooter sets $
A$ and $B$ (both satisfying the conditions of Lemma 1),*
$$|N^+(A)| \le 3\,|N^+(B)|.$$

*Proof of Lemma 2.* Let $k_B = |N^+(B)|$ and $\ell = |V \setminus B|$. By Lemma
1(2), $V \setminus B \subseteq N^+(B)$, so $\ell \le k_B$.

We bound $|N^+(A)|$ by splitting its targets into those in $B$ and those in $V \
setminus B$.

**Targets in $V \setminus B$:** $|N^+(A) \cap (V \setminus B)| \le |V \setminus
B| = \ell$.

**Targets in $B$:** A vertex of $B$ targeted by $A$ is targeted either by some m
ember of $A \cap B$, or only by members of $A \setminus B$.

- *Targeted by $A \cap B$:* Since $A \cap B \subseteq B$, such a vertex lies in
$N^+(B) \cap B$, i.e., it is a non-source of the DAG $G[B]$. Now
$$|N^+(B) \cap B| = k_B - |N^+(B) \setminus B| \le k_B - \ell,$$
because $N^+(B) \setminus B \supseteq V \setminus B$ (by Lemma 1(2)) has size $\
ge \ell$. So this contributes at most $k_B - \ell$.

- *Targeted only by $A \setminus B$:* Each vertex of $A \setminus B$ has out-deg

 number of edges from $A \setminus B$ into $B$ is at most $2\ell$, hence at most
 $2\ell$ distinct targets.

Combining:
$$|N^+(A) \cap B| \le (k_B - \ell) + 2\ell = k_B + \ell.$$

Therefore
$$|N^+(A)| \le \ell + (k_B + \ell) = k_B + 2\ell \le k_B + 2k_B = 3k_B = 3\,|N^+
(B)|. \qquad\square$$
