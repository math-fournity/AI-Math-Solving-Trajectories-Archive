## Distributional Derivative of the Heaviside Function

**Setup.** The Heaviside function is
$$H(t) = \begin{cases} 1, & t > 0, \\ 0, & t < 0, \end{cases}$$
with $H(0)$ left unspecified (its value at a single point is irrelevant for dist
ribution theory, since distributions act on test functions and are insensitive t
o changes on measure-zero sets).

A **test function** $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ is smooth with compact
support. The Heaviside function defines a regular distribution $T_H$ by
$$\langle T_H, \varphi \rangle := \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} H(t)\,\varphi(t)\,dt =
 \int_0^\infty \varphi(t)\,dt.$$

The **distributional derivative** $T_H'$ is defined by the rule
$$\langle T_H', \varphi \rangle := -\langle T_H, \varphi' \rangle$$
for every test function $\varphi$.

**Computation.** Using the definition,
$$\langle T_H', \varphi \rangle = -\int_0^\infty \varphi'(t)\,dt.$$

Since $\varphi$ has compact support, there exists $R > 0$ such that $\varphi(t)
= 0$ for all $t \ge R$. By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus applied on $[0, R
]$,
$$\int_0^\infty \varphi'(t)\,dt = \int_0^R \varphi'(t)\,dt = \varphi(R) - \varph
i(0) = 0 - \varphi(0) = -\varphi(0).$$

Therefore
$$\langle T_H', \varphi \rangle = -\bigl(-\varphi(0)\bigr) = \varphi(0).$$

**Identification with the Dirac delta.** The **Dirac delta distribution** $\delt
a$ is defined by
$$\langle \delta, \varphi \rangle := \varphi(0).$$

Comparing the two expressions, we obtain
$$\langle T_H', \varphi \rangle = \varphi(0) = \langle \delta, \varphi \rangle$$
for every test function $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$.

**Conclusion.** Two distributions are equal precisely when they agree on every t
est function. Hence
$$\boxed{H' = \delta}$$

in the sense of distributions. The distributional derivative of the Heaviside fu
nction is the Dirac delta distribution concentrated at the origin.

**Remark (consistency check).** This result is consistent with the classical pic
ture: $H$ is constant (hence classically with derivative $0$) on each of the ope
n half-lines $(-\infty,0)$ and $(0,\infty)$, and the entire "jump" of size $1$ a
t the discontinuity $t=0$ is captured by the delta mass. More generally, for a p
iecewise-smooth function with a jump of size $a$ at $t_0$, the distributional de
rivative picks up an additional term $a\,\delta_{t_0}$; here $a = 1$ and $t_0 =
0$.
