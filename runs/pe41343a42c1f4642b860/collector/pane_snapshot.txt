## Proof

Let $c$ denote the Banach space of all convergent (real or complex) sequences $x
 = (x_0, x_1, x_2, \ldots)$ equipped with the supremum norm $\|x\|_\infty = \sup
_{n \ge 0} |x_n|$, and let $c_0$ be the closed subspace of sequences converging
to $0$. For $x \in c$ write $L(x) = \lim_{n\to\infty} x_n$. The operator in ques
tion is
$$P x = \bigl(x_0 - L(x),\; x_1 - L(x),\; x_2 - L(x),\; \ldots\bigr).$$

**Step 1. $P$ is well-defined and maps $c$ into $c_0$.** Since $x_n \to L(x)$, w
e have $x_n - L(x) \to 0$, so $Px \in c_0$. Linearity of $P$ follows from linear
ity of the limit functional $L: c \to \mathbb{K}$.

**Step 2. Upper bound $\|P\| \le 2$.** For any $x \in c$ with $\|x\|_\infty \le
1$ we have $|x_n| \le 1$ for all $n$, and since $x_n \to L(x)$, also $|L(x)| \le
 \sup_n |x_n| \le 1$. Hence for every $n$,
$$|x_n - L(x)| \le |x_n| + |L(x)| \le 1 + 1 = 2,$$
so $\|Px\|_\infty = \sup_n |x_n - L(x)| \le 2$. Taking the supremum over $\|x\|_
\infty \le 1$ gives $\|P\| \le 2$.

**Step 3. The bound is attained.** Consider the convergent sequence
$$x = (1,\, -1,\, -1,\, -1,\, \ldots), \qquad L(x) = -1.$$
We have $\|x\|_\infty = 1$, and
$$Px = \bigl(1-(-1),\, -1-(-1),\, -1-(-1),\, \ldots\bigr) = (2,\, 0,\, 0,\, \ldo
ts),$$
so $\|Px\|_\infty = 2$.

Therefore $\|P\| = \sup_{\|x\|_\infty \le 1} \|Px\|_\infty \ge 2$.

**Conclusion.** Combining Steps 2 and 3,
$$\boxed{\|P\| = 2}.$$
