## Proof: The Kolmogorov Forward and Backward Equations Hold Simultaneously

### Setup

Let $X_t$ be a one-dimensional diffusion process governed by the Itô SDE

$$dX_t = b(X_t,t)\,dt + \sigma(X_t,t)\,dW_t, \qquad a(x,t):=\sigma(x,t)^2,$$

with $b\in C^{2,1}$, $\sigma\in C^{2,1}$, and $a(x,t)$ **uniformly elliptic** ($
a\ge a_0>0$). Let

$$p(x,t\mid y,s),\qquad t>s,$$

denote the transition density (the fundamental solution of the parabolic operato
r associated with the diffusion).

---

### The Two Equations

**Kolmogorov Backward Equation (KBE).** This is an equation in the *backward* va
riables $(y,s)$, with $(x,t)$ held fixed:

$$-\frac{\partial p}{\partial s}(x,t\mid y,s)=b(y,s)\,\frac{\partial p}{\partial
 y}(x,t\mid y,s)+\frac{1}{2}\,a(y,s)\,\frac{\partial^{2}p}{\partial y^{2}}(x,t\m
id y,s). \tag{B}$$

**Kolmogorov Forward Equation (KFE / Fokker–Planck).** This is an equation in th
e *forward* variables $(x,t)$, with $(y,s)$ held fixed:

$$\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}(x,t\mid y,s)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\bigl[b
(x,t)\,p\bigr]+\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\bigl[a(x,t)\,p\b
igr]. \tag{F}$$

---

### Derivation of the Backward Equation

Fix a terminal time $T$ and a smooth test function $g$. Define

$$u(y,s)=\mathbb{E}\bigl[g(X_T)\mid X_s=y\bigr]=\int g(x)\,p(x,T\mid y,s)\,dx.$$

By the Markov property, for $h>0$,

$$u(y,s)=\mathbb{E}\bigl[u(X_{s+h},\,s+h)\mid X_s=y\bigr].$$

Applying Itô's formula to $u(X_{s+h},s+h)$ over $[s,s+h]$ and letting $h\to 0$ y
ields

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial s}+b(y,s)\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}+\frac{1}{2}a
(y,s)\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial y^{2}}=0. \tag{$\ast$}$$

Since $u(y,s)=\int g(x)\,p(x,T\mid y,s)\,dx$ and ($\ast$) holds for **every** sm
ooth $g$, we may "strip off the integral" (by the arbitrariness of $g$) to obtai
n (B) for $p(x,T\mid y,s)$, hence for $p(x,t\mid y,s)$ at any $t>s$.

---

### Derivation of the Forward Equation

Let $\rho_0(y)$ be any smooth initial density and define

$$\rho(x,t)=\int p(x,t\mid y,s)\,\rho_0(y)\,dy.$$

By the Chapman–Kolmogorov equation, $\rho(\cdot,t)$ is the probability density o
f $X_t$ when $X_s\sim\rho_0$. Conservation of total probability gives

$$\frac{d}{dt}\int\rho(x,t)\,dx=0.$$

The probability current (Fokker–Planck flux) is

$$J(x,t)=b(x,t)\,\rho(x,t)-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\bigl[a(x,t)\,\
rho(x,t)\bigr],$$

and the continuity equation $\partial_t\rho+\partial_x J=0$ (derived from the It
ô SDE via Itô's formula applied to test functions and integration by parts, i.e.
 the weak form of the dynamics) gives

$$\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\bigl[b\,\rho\bigr
]+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\bigl[a\,\rho\bigr]. \tag{$\ast\
ast$}$$

Since $\rho(x,t)=\int p(x,t\mid y,s)\rho_0(y)\,dy$ and ($\ast\ast$) holds for **
every** smooth $\rho_0$, the arbitrariness of $\rho_0$ allows us to strip off th
e integral, yielding (F) for $p(x,t\mid y,s)$.

---

### Why There Is No Conflict: The Two Equations Live in Disjoint Variables

The crucial observation is:

- **(B)** is a PDE in the variables $(y,s)$; the pair $(x,t)$ enters only as a *
*parameter**.
- **(F)** is a PDE in the variables $(x,t)$; the pair $(y,s)$ enters only as a *
*parameter**.

These are **disjoint** sets of independent variables. There is no differential o
perator acting on the same variable in both equations, so the two equations cann
ot contradict each other any more than $\partial f/\partial x = g$ and $\partial
 f/\partial y = h$ can contradict each other for a function $f(x,y)$.

Formally, (B) and (F) are **adjoint** equations: the forward operator $L_t^{*}$
(acting on $x$) is the formal $L^2$-adjoint of the backward generator $L_s$ (act
ing on $y$). The transition kernel $p$ is precisely the object that intertwines
the two: it is the Green's function of $L_s$ in $(y,s)$ and simultaneously the G
reen's function of $L_t^{*}$ in $(x,t)$.

---

### Consistency Check via Adjointness

For any two smooth, compactly supported test functions $f,g$ and $t>s$:

$$\int f(x)\,\bigl(L_t^{*}p\bigr)(x,t\mid y,s)\,dx \;=\; \bigl(L_s\,(\cdot\mapst
o \textstyle\int f(x)p(x,t\mid\cdot,s)dx)\bigr)(y,s),$$

which is exactly the statement that the semigroup $P_{s,t}g(y)=\int g(x)p(x,t\mi
d y,s)\,dx$ satisfies $\partial_s P_{s,t}g = -L_s P_{s,t}g$ (backward) and $\par
tial_t P_{s,t}^{*}f = L_t^{*}P_{s,t}^{*}f$ (forward) simultaneously. The duality
 $\langle P_{s,t}g, f\rangle = \langle g, P_{s,t}^{*}f\rangle$ is the rigorous b
ridge confirming both equations describe the **same** kernel from the two dual p
erspectives.

---

### Existence and Simultaneous Validity

Under the standing hypotheses ($b,\sigma\in C^{2,1}$, uniform ellipticity of $a$
), the classical theory of one-dimensional parabolic PDEs (Friedman, *Partial Di
fferential Equations of Parabolic Type*; Ladyženskaja–Solonnikov–Ural'ceva) guar
antees:

1. **Existence and uniqueness** of a fundamental solution $p(x,t\mid y,s)$ that
is $C^{2,1}$ in each set of variables separately.
2. $p$ satisfies **(B)** in $(y,s)$ for each fixed $(x,t)$.
3. $p$ satisfies **(F)** in $(x,t)$ for each fixed $(y,s)$.
4. $p$ satisfies the Chapman–Kolmogorov semigroup identity.

Hence (B) and (F) hold **simultaneously** for the same transition density.

---

### Conclusion

**Yes, the Kolmogorov forward and backward equations can and do simultaneously h
old for a diffusion process** (with sufficiently smooth, uniformly elliptic coef
ficients). They are not competing constraints on the same variables: the backwar
d equation governs the dependence on the *initial* state $(y,s)$, while the forw
ard (Fokker–Planck) equation governs the dependence on the *terminal* state $(x,
t)$. They are mutually adjoint descriptions of the single transition kernel $p(x
,t\mid y,s)$, and classical parabolic PDE theory guarantees a unique smooth kern
el satisfying both at once.
