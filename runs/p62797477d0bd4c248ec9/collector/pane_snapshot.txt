Looking at this problem, I need to count 5-term increasing arithmetic progressio
ns (APs) from $S_n = \{-6n, \ldots, -1, 1, \ldots, 6n\}$ (integers from $-6n$ to
 $6n$ excluding 0), with no term equal to 0.

## Setup

A 5-term increasing AP is $a, a+d, a+2d, a+3d, a+4d$ with $d \geq 1$. Constraint
s:
- **Range**: $-6n \leq a$ and $a + 4d \leq 6n$.
- **Nonzero**: None of the five terms equals 0.

## Step 1: Total APs (ignoring the zero constraint)

For fixed $d$, $a$ ranges from $-6n$ to $6n - 4d$, giving $12n - 4d + 1$ values.
 This requires $d \leq 3n$.

$$\text{Total} = \sum_{d=1}^{3n}(12n - 4d + 1) = 3n(12n+1) - 4\cdot\frac{3n(3n+1
)}{2} = 3n\bigl[(12n+1)-2(3n+1)\bigr] = 3n(6n-1).$$

## Step 2: Subtract APs containing 0

A term is zero when $a + kd = 0$, i.e., $a = -kd$, for some $k \in \{0,1,2,3,4\}
$.

**Key observation**: At most one term can be zero (if two terms $a+kd$ and $a+jd
$ were both zero with $k \neq j$, then $(k-j)d=0$, forcing $d=0$, contradiction)
. So inclusion-exclusion has no overlaps — we simply sum.

For each $k$, the valid $d$ values satisfy $kd \leq 6n$ and $(4-k)d \leq 6n$, i.
e., $d \leq \frac{6n}{\max(k,\,4-k)}$:

| $k$ | $\max(k,4-k)$ | $N_k$ |
|-----|---------------|-------|
| 0 | 4 | $\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor$ |
| 1 | 3 | $2n$ |
| 2 | 2 | $3n$ |
| 3 | 3 | $2n$ |
| 4 | 4 | $\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor$ |

$$\text{Subtracted} = 2\left\lfloor \tfrac{3n}{2}\right\rfloor + 7n.$$

## Step 3: Formula

$$f(n) = 3n(6n-1) - 2\left\lfloor \tfrac{3n}{2}\right\rfloor - 7n.$$

## Step 4: Verification with $n=1$

$S_1 = \{-6,\ldots,-1,1,\ldots,6\}$. Formula: $f(1) = 3(5) - 2(1) - 7 = 15 - 9 =
 6$.

Manual enumeration: $d=1$ gives 4 valid APs, $d=2$ gives 2, $d=3$ gives 0. Total
 = 6. ✓

## Step 5: Compute $f(5)$ and $f(6)$

**$n=5$:**
$$f(5) = 3\cdot5\cdot29 - 2\left\lfloor\tfrac{15}{2}\right\rfloor - 35 = 435 - 1
4 - 35 = 386.$$

**$n=6$:**
$$f(6) = 3\cdot6\cdot35 - 2\left\lfloor\tfrac{18}{2}\right\rfloor - 42 = 630 - 1
8 - 42 = 570.$$

## Result

$$f(5) + f(6) = 386 + 570 = \boxed{956}.$$
