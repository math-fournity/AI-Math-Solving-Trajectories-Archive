## Proof

Let $R = \mathbb{Q}[X,Y]/(X^{20}, Y^{20})$. We show $R$ is a local ring by ident
ifying its unique maximal ideal.

**Step 1: Reduce to maximal ideals of $\mathbb{Q}[X,Y]$ containing $(X^{20}, Y^{
20})$.**

By the correspondence (lattice) theorem for quotient rings, the maximal ideals o
f $R$ are in bijection with the maximal ideals $\mathfrak{m} \subset \mathbb{Q}[
X,Y]$ such that $(X^{20}, Y^{20}) \subseteq \mathfrak{m}$.

**Step 2: Any such maximal ideal must contain $(X, Y)$.**

Let $\mathfrak{m}$ be a maximal ideal of $\mathbb{Q}[X,Y]$ with $(X^{20}, Y^{20}
) \subseteq \mathfrak{m}$. Then $X^{20} \in \mathfrak{m}$ and $Y^{20} \in \mathf
rak{m}$. Since $\mathfrak{m}$ is maximal, it is prime. In a prime ideal, $f^{20}
 \in \mathfrak{m}$ implies $f \in \mathfrak{m}$. Hence $X \in \mathfrak{m}$ and
$Y \in \mathfrak{m}$, so $(X, Y) \subseteq \mathfrak{m}$.

**Step 3: $(X, Y)$ is itself maximal, so it is the only possibility.**

The ideal $(X, Y) \subset \mathbb{Q}[X,Y]$ is maximal because
$$\mathbb{Q}[X,Y]/(X,Y) \cong \mathbb{Q},$$
which is a field. Therefore any maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ containing $(X,Y)$
must equal $(X,Y)$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 2 and 3, the only maximal ideal of $\mathbb{Q}[X,Y]$ containing
$(X^{20}, Y^{20})$ is $(X, Y)$. By the correspondence theorem, the only maximal
ideal of $R$ is
$$\mathfrak{m}_R = (X, Y)/(X^{20}, Y^{20}).$$

Since $R$ has exactly one maximal ideal, $R$ is a local ring. $\blacksquare$
