## Solution

**Claim.** Let $X$ be the Cantor space $X = \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$ (the countable
 product of the two-point discrete space), and let $p = (0,0,0,\ldots) \in X$ be
 the all-zeros point. Define the equivalence relation $\sim$ on $X$ by

$$x \sim y \iff x = y = p \;\text{ or }\; x, y \neq p,$$

i.e., the partition $X = \{p\} \sqcup (X \setminus \{p\})$. Then the quotient sp
ace $X/{\sim}$ is homeomorphic to the Sierpiński space.

---

### $X$ is a Boolean space

The Cantor space $X = \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$ is:

- **Compact:** the product of compact (finite discrete) spaces, by Tychonoff's t
heorem.
- **Hausdorff:** the product of Hausdorff spaces.
- **Totally disconnected:** the product of totally disconnected spaces (each fac
tor $\{0,1\}$ is discrete, hence totally disconnected), and a product of totally
 disconnected spaces is totally disconnected.

Hence $X$ is a compact, Hausdorff, totally disconnected space — a Boolean (Stone
) space.

### The quotient map

Let $q: X \to \{0,1\}$ be the map

$$q(x) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } x = p, \\ 1 & \text{if } x \neq p. \end{ca
ses}$$

This is constant on each equivalence class and injective on the quotient, so it
induces a bijection $\bar{q}: X/{\sim} \to \{0,1\}$. We equip $\{0,1\}$ with the
 quotient topology: $U \subseteq \{0,1\}$ is open iff $q^{-1}(U)$ is open in $X$
.

### Computing the quotient topology

- $q^{-1}(\emptyset) = \emptyset$, which is open. ✓
- $q^{-1}(\{0,1\}) = X$, which is open. ✓
- $q^{-1}(\{1\}) = X \setminus \{p\}$. Since $X$ is Hausdorff, $\{p\}$ is closed
, so $X \setminus \{p\}$ is **open**. Hence $\{1\}$ is open in the quotient. ✓
- $q^{-1}(\{0\}) = \{p\}$. The point $p$ is **not open** in $X$: the Cantor spac
e has no isolated points (every basic open cylinder set contains infinitely many
 points), so no singleton is open. Hence $\{0\}$ is **not open** in the quotient
. ✓

Therefore the quotient topology on $\{0,1\}$ is exactly

$$\big\{\emptyset,\; \{1\},\; \{0,1\}\big\},$$

which is the topology of the Sierpiński space.

### Conclusion

The Cantor space $X = \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$ is a Boolean space, and the quotient
 of $X$ by the partition $\{p\} \sqcup (X \setminus \{p\})$ (where $p$ is any po
int of $X$) is homeomorphic to the Sierpiński space $\{0,1\}$ with topology $\{\
emptyset, \{1\}, \{0,1\}\}$.
