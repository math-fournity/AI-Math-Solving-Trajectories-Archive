## Proof

**Setup.** Let $G$ be the (orientation-preserving) rotational symmetry group of
a cube inscribed in the unit sphere $S$. We have $|G| = 24$ (the group is isomor
phic to $S_4$, acting on the four long diagonals of the cube). A *pole* is a poi
nt $p \in S$ fixed by some non-identity element $g \in G$, i.e. $g \cdot p = p$.
 Every non-identity rotation of the cube is a rotation about some axis through t
he origin; the two points where that axis meets $S$ are exactly the poles of tha
t rotation. So finding $P$ amounts to listing all rotation axes of the cube.

---

**Step 1: Classification of rotation axes.**

A cube has three families of rotational symmetry axes:

1. **Face axes** — through the centers of opposite faces. There are $3$ such axe
s (one per pair of opposite faces). The non-identity rotations about each are by
 $90°$, $180°$, $270°$. Each axis meets $S$ in $2$ antipodal points (the face-ce
nter directions).
   Poles contributed: $3 \times 2 = 6$.

2. **Vertex axes** — through opposite vertices. There are $4$ such axes (one per
 pair of opposite vertices). The non-identity rotations about each are by $120°$
, $240°$. Each axis meets $S$ in $2$ points.
   Poles contributed: $4 \times 2 = 8$.

3. **Edge axes** — through the midpoints of opposite edges. There are $6$ such a
xes (one per pair of opposite edges). The only non-identity rotation about each
is by $180°$. Each axis meets $S$ in $2$ points.
   Poles contributed: $6 \times 2 = 12$.

These three families are disjoint (a face-center direction, a vertex direction,
and an edge-midpoint direction are geometrically distinct), and they exhaust all
 non-identity rotations since
$$\underbrace{3 \times 3}_{\text{face}} + \underbrace{4 \times 2}_{\text{vertex}
} + \underbrace{6 \times 1}_{\text{edge}} = 9 + 8 + 6 = 23 = |G| - 1.$$

Hence
$$\boxed{P = P_f \cup P_v \cup P_e,\quad |P| = 6 + 8 + 12 = 26,}$$
where $P_f, P_v, P_e$ are the face-center, vertex, and edge-midpoint poles respe
ctively.

---

**Step 2: Orbits of $P$ under $G$.**

- **$P_f$ is a single orbit.** $G$ acts transitively on the $6$ faces of the cub
e (any face can be rotated to any other), so all $6$ face-center poles lie in on
e orbit.

- **$P_v$ is a single orbit.** $G$ acts transitively on the $8$ vertices, so all
 $8$ vertex poles lie in one orbit.

- **$P_e$ is a single orbit.** $G$ acts transitively on the $12$ edges, so all $
12$ edge-midpoint poles lie in one orbit.

No element of $G$ maps a face-center pole to a vertex pole or an edge-midpoint p
ole, since these have distinct stabilizer orders (computed below), and conjugate
 points have conjugate (hence equal-order) stabilizers. Therefore the orbit part
ition of $P$ is exactly
$$P = \mathcal{O}_f \sqcup \mathcal{O}_v \sqcup \mathcal{O}_e,\qquad |\mathcal{O
}_f|=6,\; |\mathcal{O}_v|=8,\; |\mathcal{O}_e|=12.$$

---

**Step 3: Stabilizers.**

By the orbit–stabilizer theorem, $|\text{Stab}(p)| = |G|/|\mathcal{O}(p)| = 24/|
\mathcal{O}(p)|$.

- **Face-center pole $p \in \mathcal{O}_f$:** $|\text{Stab}(p)| = 24/6 = 4$. Con
cretely, the stabilizer is the cyclic group of rotations about the face axis thr
ough $p$:
$$\text{Stab}(p) \cong C_4 = \{e,\; R_{90°},\; R_{180°},\; R_{270°}\}.$$

- **Vertex pole $p \in \mathcal{O}_v$:** $|\text{Stab}(p)| = 24/8 = 3$. The stab
ilizer is the cyclic group of rotations about the vertex axis through $p$:
$$\text{Stab}(p) \cong C_3 = \{e,\; R_{120°},\; R_{240°}\}.$$

- **Edge-midpoint pole $p \in \mathcal{O}_e$:** $|\text{Stab}(p)| = 24/12 = 2$.
The stabilizer is the cyclic group of order $2$:
$$\text{Stab}(p) \cong C_2 = \{e,\; R_{180°}\}.$$

**Verification.** The orbit–stabilizer counts check:
$$6 \times 4 = 24, \qquad 8 \times 3 = 24, \qquad 12 \times 2 = 24,$$
each equal to $|G| = 24$. ∎

---

### Summary

| Orbit | Description | Size | Stabilizer |
|-------|-------------|------|------------|
| $\mathcal{O}_f$ | face-center poles | $6$ | $C_4$ (order $4$) |
| $\mathcal{O}_v$ | vertex poles | $8$ | $C_3$ (order $3$) |
| $\mathcal{O}_e$ | edge-midpoint poles | $12$ | $C_2$ (order $2$) |

The set of poles is $P = \mathcal{O}_f \sqcup \mathcal{O}_v \sqcup \mathcal{O}_e
$ with $|P| = 26$, partitioned into three orbits with stabilizers $C_4$, $C_3$,
and $C_2$ respectively.
