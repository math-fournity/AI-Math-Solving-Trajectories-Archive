## Setup

We study
$$\phi'' + \lambda\,\phi = \varepsilon\,\phi^3, \qquad \phi(0)=\phi(L)=0,$$
with the amplitude normalization
$$\int_0^L \phi^2\,dx = a^2.$$

Because the problem is **nonlinear**, the amplitude $a$ is not a free scaling fa
ctor; it enters the eigenvalue relation. We use a regular perturbation (Lindsted
t-type) expansion in $\varepsilon$, treating $a$ (hence the amplitude of the lea
ding mode) as a fixed parameter.

---

## Unperturbed problem ($\varepsilon=0$)

$$\phi_0'' + \lambda_0\,\phi_0 = 0,\qquad \phi_0(0)=\phi_0(L)=0$$
gives
$$\lambda_n^{(0)} = \Bigl(\frac{n\pi}{L}\Bigr)^2,\qquad \phi_n^{(0)}(x)=\sin\fra
c{n\pi x}{L}.$$

For the first eigenvalue ($n=1$):
$$\lambda_0 = \Bigl(\frac{\pi}{L}\Bigr)^2,\qquad \phi_0(x)=A\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}.
$$

The normalization fixes $A$:
$$\int_0^L \phi_0^2\,dx = A^2\int_0^L \sin^2\frac{\pi x}{L}\,dx = A^2\cdot\frac{
L}{2}=a^2 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; A^2=\frac{2a^2}{L}.$$

---

## Perturbation expansion

Set
$$\phi = \phi_0 + \varepsilon\,\phi_1 + O(\varepsilon^2),\qquad \lambda = \lambd
a_0 + \varepsilon\,\mu_1 + O(\varepsilon^2).$$

Substituting into $\phi''+\lambda\phi=\varepsilon\phi^3$ and collecting orders:

- **$O(1)$:** $\;\phi_0''+\lambda_0\phi_0=0$ — satisfied.
- **$O(\varepsilon)$:**
$$\phi_1'' + \lambda_0\,\phi_1 = \phi_0^3 - \mu_1\,\phi_0, \qquad \phi_1(0)=\phi
_1(L)=0. \tag{$\ast$}$$

---

## Solvability (Fredholm) condition

The homogeneous operator $L_0 = \tfrac{d^2}{dx^2}+\lambda_0$ with Dirichlet boun
dary conditions has a one-dimensional kernel spanned by $\sin(\pi x/L)$. For $(\
ast)$ to have a solution, the RHS must be $L^2$-orthogonal to this kernel:

$$\int_0^L \bigl(\phi_0^3 - \mu_1\,\phi_0\bigr)\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}\,dx = 0.$$

Hence
$$\mu_1 = \frac{\displaystyle\int_0^L \phi_0^3\,\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}\,dx}{\displa
ystyle\int_0^L \phi_0\,\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}\,dx}.$$

With $\phi_0 = A\sin(\pi x/L)$:

**Denominator:**
$$\int_0^L A\sin^2\frac{\pi x}{L}\,dx = A\cdot\frac{L}{2}.$$

**Numerator:**
$$\int_0^L A^3\sin^4\frac{\pi x}{L}\,dx = A^3\cdot\frac{3L}{8},$$
using $\displaystyle\int_0^L\sin^4\frac{\pi x}{L}\,dx = \frac{3L}{8}$ (from $\si
n^4\theta=\tfrac{3}{8}-\tfrac{1}{2}\cos 2\theta+\tfrac{1}{8}\cos 4\theta$).

Therefore
$$\mu_1 = \frac{A^3\cdot 3L/8}{A\cdot L/2} = \frac{3}{4}\,A^2 = \frac{3}{4}\cdot
\frac{2a^2}{L} = \frac{3a^2}{2L}.$$

---

## Perturbed first eigenvalue

$$\boxed{\;\lambda_1 = \Bigl(\frac{\pi}{L}\Bigr)^2 + \varepsilon\,\frac{3a^2}{2L
} + O(\varepsilon^2)\;}$$

The eigenvalue **shift is proportional to the square of the amplitude** $a^2$ —
a hallmark of nonlinearity absent in the linear theory.

---

## Sketch of $a$ as a function of $\lambda$

Solving the leading-order relation for $a$:
$$\lambda - \lambda_0 = \frac{3\varepsilon}{2L}\,a^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a
 = \sqrt{\frac{2L}{3\varepsilon}\,(\lambda-\lambda_0)},\qquad \lambda_0=\Bigl(\f
rac{\pi}{L}\Bigr)^2.$$

This is a **square-root (parabolic) bifurcation curve** in the $(\lambda,\,a)$ p
lane:

```
        a
        ^
        |        ε > 0  (supercritical, bends right)
        |       .'
        |     .'
        |   .
        | .'
        |'_______ λ₀=(π/L)² ______> λ
        |
        |  ε < 0  (subcritical, bends left)
        |  '.
        |    '.
        |      '.
```

**Description of the sketch:**

- The **trivial branch** $a=0$ exists for all $\lambda$ (the linear eigenvalue p
roblem always has the zero solution).
- **Nontrivial solutions** ($a\neq 0$) bifurcate from the trivial branch at the
unperturbed eigenvalue $\lambda = \lambda_0 = (\pi/L)^2$.
- The branch is a **parabola opening along the $\lambda$-axis**:
  - **$\varepsilon > 0$ (hardening nonlinearity):** the branch exists for $\lamb
da > \lambda_0$ and bends to the **right** (supercritical). The eigenvalue incre
ases with amplitude.
  - **$\varepsilon < 0$ (softening nonlinearity):** the branch exists for $\lamb
da < \lambda_0$ and bends to the **left** (subcritical). The eigenvalue decrease
s with amplitude.
- At the bifurcation point $(\lambda_0, 0)$ the tangent is **vertical** ($da/d\l
ambda \to \infty$), and the curve grows like $\sqrt{|\lambda-\lambda_0|}$.

This is the classic **pitchfork-type amplitude–eigenvalue relation** for a nonli
near Sturm–Liouville problem with a cubic perturbation, where — unlike the linea
r case — the eigenvalue is no longer amplitude-independent but shifts quadratica
lly in $a$.
