## Solution

On the contour $\gamma$, we have $|z| = 2$ (constant), since $\gamma$ is the cir
cle of radius $2$ centered at the origin. Therefore:

$$\int_\gamma \frac{|z|\,e^z}{z^2}\,dz = \int_\gamma \frac{2\,e^z}{z^2}\,dz = 2\
int_\gamma \frac{e^z}{z^2}\,dz.$$

The integrand $\frac{e^z}{z^2}$ has a pole of order $2$ at $z = 0$, which lies i
nside $\gamma$. By **Cauchy's integral formula for derivatives** (with $n = 1$):

$$\oint_\gamma \frac{f(z)}{z^{n+1}}\,dz = \frac{2\pi i}{n!}\,f^{(n)}(0),$$

where $f(z) = e^z$ (entire). Taking $n = 1$:

$$\int_\gamma \frac{e^z}{z^2}\,dz = \frac{2\pi i}{1!}\,f'(0) = 2\pi i \cdot e^0
= 2\pi i.$$

Therefore:

$$\int_\gamma \frac{|z|\,e^z}{z^2}\,dz = 2 \cdot 2\pi i = \boxed{4\pi i}.$$
