## Proof

We seek the smallest group (by order) that is not isomorphic to a cyclic group,
a direct product of cyclic groups, or a semidirect product of cyclic groups.

**Key observation.** Cyclic groups and direct products of cyclic groups are spec
ial (degenerate) cases of semidirect products of cyclic groups (a direct product
 is a semidirect product with trivial action; a cyclic group $C_{mn}$ with $\gcd
(m,n)=1$ is $C_m \rtimes C_n$ trivially, and trivially a product of one cyclic g
roup). So it suffices to find the smallest group that is **not** a semidirect pr
oduct $C_a \rtimes C_b$ of two cyclic groups (including the degenerate cases $a=
1$ or $b=1$).

### Step 1. All groups of order $\le 7$ are semidirect products of cyclic groups
.

- **Order 1–5, 7:** Every group of prime order is cyclic; order 4 gives $C_4$ (c
yclic) and $C_2 \times C_2$ (direct product of cyclics). All are semidirect prod
ucts of cyclic groups.
- **Order 6:** The two groups are $C_6 \cong C_2 \times C_3$ (direct product of
cyclics) and $S_3 \cong C_3 \rtimes C_2$ (the nontrivial action is inversion, si
nce $\operatorname{Aut}(C_3)\cong C_2$). Both are semidirect products of cyclic
groups.

Hence no group of order $\le 7$ qualifies.

### Step 2. The quaternion group $Q_8$ is not a semidirect product of cyclic gro
ups.

Recall
$$Q_8 = \{\pm 1, \pm i, \pm j, \pm k\},\quad i^2=j^2=k^2=ijk=-1.$$
Its subgroups are:
$$\{1\},\ \{1,-1\},\ \langle i\rangle,\ \langle j\rangle,\ \langle k\rangle,\ Q_
8,$$
with $\langle i\rangle \cong \langle j\rangle \cong \langle k\rangle \cong C_4$
and $\{1,-1\}\cong C_2$. Crucially, **every** subgroup of $Q_8$ is normal (it is
 a Hamiltonian group), and the unique element of order 2, namely $-1$, lies in *
*every** nontrivial subgroup.

Suppose for contradiction that $Q_8 \cong C_a \rtimes C_b$ with $a b = 8$ and $a
,b \ge 1$. The nontrivial factorizations of 8 are $(a,b) \in \{(4,2),(2,4)\}$ (t
he cases $(8,1),(1,8)$ give cyclic groups, but $Q_8$ is not cyclic). In an (inte
rnal) semidirect product $N \rtimes H$ we require
$$N \trianglelefteq Q_8,\qquad N \cap H = \{1\},\qquad NH = Q_8.$$

- **Case $(a,b)=(4,2)$:** $N$ is a normal cyclic subgroup of order 4, so $N \in
\{\langle i\rangle,\langle j\rangle,\langle k\rangle\}$, and $H$ is a cyclic sub
group of order 2, so $H = \{1,-1\}$. But $\{1,-1\} \subset N$ for every order-4
subgroup $N$, so $N \cap H = H \neq \{1\}$. Contradiction.
- **Case $(a,b)=(2,4)$:** $N = \{1,-1\}$ (the unique normal subgroup of order 2)
 and $H$ is a cyclic subgroup of order 4, so $H \in \{\langle i\rangle,\langle j
\rangle,\langle k\rangle\}$. Again $\{1,-1\} \subset H$, so $N \cap H = N \neq \
{1\}$. Contradiction.

Both cases are impossible. Therefore $Q_8$ is **not** a semidirect product of tw
o cyclic groups. Since $Q_8$ is non-abelian, it is neither cyclic nor a direct p
roduct of cyclic groups either.

### Step 3. $Q_8$ is also not an iterated semidirect product of cyclic groups.

(For completeness, under a broader reading.) An iterated semidirect product of c
yclic groups admits a normal series with cyclic quotients in which every extensi

$$1 \trianglelefteq \{1,-1\} \trianglelefteq \langle i\rangle \trianglelefteq Q_
8$$
with cyclic quotients $C_2, C_2, C_2$. But the step $\{1,-1\} \trianglelefteq \l
angle i\rangle$ does **not** split: $\langle i\rangle \cong C_4$ has a unique su
bgroup of order 2, so there is no complement to $\{1,-1\}$ in $\langle i\rangle$
 (a complement would make $\langle i\rangle \cong C_2 \times C_2$, which is fals
e). Hence $Q_8$ is not an iterated semidirect product of cyclic groups either.

Every group of order $\le 7$ is a (semi)direct product of cyclic groups, while $
