## Derivation of the Hilbert Transform Pair

### Setup and conventions

Let $f(x) = f_R(x) + i\,f_I(x)$ be a complex-valued function of the real variabl
e $x$, with $f_R, f_I$ real. Adopt the Fourier-transform convention

$$
G(\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} g(x)\,e^{-i\omega x}\,dx,
\qquad
g(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} G(\omega)\,e^{i\omega x}\,d\omega.
$$

Write $F(\omega) = F_R(\omega) + i\,F_I(\omega)$ for the transform of $f$.

**Analytic-signal hypothesis.** We assume $f$ is an *analytic signal*, i.e. its
spectrum is one-sided:

$$
F(\omega) = 0 \quad \text{for } \omega < 0. \tag{1}
$$

This is the condition that ties $f_R$ and $f_I$ together; without it the two par
ts are independent.

### Step 1 — Relate $F_R$ and $F_I$ in the frequency domain

Since $f_R = \tfrac{1}{2}(f + f^*)$ and $f_I = \tfrac{1}{2i}(f - f^*)$, and usin
g $\mathcal{F}\{f^*\}(\omega) = F^*(-\omega)$,

$$
F_R(\omega) = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[F(\omega) + F^*(-\omega)\bigr], \qquad
F_I(\omega) = \tfrac{1}{2i}\bigl[F(\omega) - F^*(-\omega)\bigr]. \tag{2}
$$

For $\omega > 0$, (1) gives $F(-\omega)=0$, so
$$
F_R(\omega) = \tfrac{1}{2}F(\omega), \qquad
F_I(\omega) = \tfrac{1}{2i}F(\omega) = -i\,\tfrac{1}{2}F(\omega) = -i\,F_R(\omeg
a). \tag{3}
$$

For $\omega < 0$, (1) gives $F(\omega)=0$, and using $F_R(-\omega)=F_R^*(\omega)
$ (reality of $f_R$) together with (2),
$$
F_R(\omega) = \tfrac{1}{2}F^*(-\omega) = F_R^*(-\omega), \qquad
F_I(\omega) = -\tfrac{1}{2i}F^*(-\omega) = i\,\tfrac{1}{2}F^*(-\omega) = i\,F_R(
\omega). \tag{4}
$$

Combining (3) and (4) into a single formula valid for all $\omega$:

$$
\boxed{\,F_I(\omega) = -i\,\operatorname{sgn}(\omega)\,F_R(\omega)\,}. \tag{5}
$$

(At $\omega=0$ the value is immaterial; $\operatorname{sgn}(0)=0$ is consistent
with the half-value convention.)

### Step 2 — Identify the Hilbert-transform frequency response

Equation (5) says that $f_I$ is obtained from $f_R$ by a linear filter whose fre
quency response is

$$
\widehat{H}(\omega) = -i\,\operatorname{sgn}(\omega). \tag{6}
$$

This is precisely the **Hilbert transform** $\mathcal{H}$. By definition,

$$
f_I = \mathcal{H}\{f_R\}, \qquad \widehat{H}(\omega) = -i\,\operatorname{sgn}(\o
mega). \tag{7}
$$

### Step 3 — Convert the frequency-domain filter to a principal-value integral

The impulse response $h(x)$ of the filter is the inverse Fourier transform of (6
):

$$
h(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \bigl[-i\,\operatorname{sgn}(\omega
)\bigr]\,e^{i\omega x}\,d\omega. \tag{8}
$$

A standard distributional identity (obtained, e.g., by differentiating the signu
m distribution or by contour regularization of $\int e^{i\omega x}/\omega$) give
s

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \operatorname{sgn}(\omega)\,e^{i\omega x}\,d\omega = \fr
ac{2i}{x}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
h(x) = \frac{1}{\pi x}\quad(\text{as a principal-value distribution}). \tag{9}
$$

Equivalently, $\mathcal{F}\{\operatorname{P.V.}\tfrac{1}{\pi x}\}(\omega) = -i\,
\operatorname{sgn}(\omega)$.

Filtering is convolution, so

$$
\mathcal{H}\{g\}(x) = (g * h)(x) = \operatorname{P.V.}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} g(
t)\,\frac{1}{\pi(x-t)}\,dt
= \frac{1}{\pi}\,\operatorname{P.V.}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{g(t)}{x-t}\,
dt. \tag{10}
$$

The principal value is required because $1/(x-t)$ is singular at $t=x$.

### Step 4 — Write the forward pair

Applying (10) to (7):

$$
\boxed{\,f_I(x) = \frac{1}{\pi}\,\operatorname{P.V.}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \f
rac{f_R(t)}{x-t}\,dt\,}. \tag{11}
$$

### Step 5 — Derive the inverse pair

The Hilbert transform is idempotent up to a sign: applying it twice multiplies t
he spectrum by $(-i\,\operatorname{sgn}(\omega))^2 = -1$, so

$$
\mathcal{H}^2 = -\mathcal{I}. \tag{12}
$$

Applying $\mathcal{H}$ to both sides of $f_I = \mathcal{H}\{f_R\}$ and using (12
):

$$
\mathcal{H}\{f_I\} = \mathcal{H}^2\{f_R\} = -f_R
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f_R = -\mathcal{H}\{f_I\}. \tag{13}
$$

Using (10) in (13):

$$
\boxed{\,f_R(x) = -\frac{1}{\pi}\,\operatorname{P.V.}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \
frac{f_I(t)}{x-t}\,dt
= \frac{1}{\pi}\,\operatorname{P.V.}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{f_I(t)}{t-x}
\,dt\,}. \tag{14}
$$

### The Hilbert transform pair

Collecting (11) and (14), the real and imaginary parts of an analytic signal $f(
x)=f_R(x)+i f_I(x)$ are related by the **Hilbert transform pair**

$$
\boxed{\;
\begin{aligned}
f_I(x) &= \frac{1}{\pi}\,\operatorname{P.V.}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{f_R(
t)}{x-t}\,dt, \\[6pt]
f_R(x) &= \frac{1}{\pi}\,\operatorname{P.V.}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{f_I(
t)}{t-x}\,dt
= -\frac{1}{\pi}\,\operatorname{P.V.}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{f_I(t)}{x-t
}\,dt.
\end{aligned}
\;}
$$

Equivalently, in operator form: $f_I = \mathcal{H}\{f_R\}$ and $f_R = -\mathcal{
H}\{f_I\}$, with $\mathcal{H}^2 = -\mathcal{I}$.

### Remarks on the derivation's logic

- The **only** structural assumption is the one-sided spectrum (1); it is what m
akes $f_R$ and $f_I$ dependent. With a two-sided spectrum the two components are
 independent and no such pair exists.
- The frequency-domain relation $F_I = -i\,\operatorname{sgn}(\omega)\,F_R$ is t
he *definition* of the Hilbert transform as a $-90^{\circ}$ phase shifter; the p
rincipal-value integral (10) is its time-domain representation, obtained via the
 distributional kernel $1/(\pi x)$.
- The inverse relation follows algebraically from $\widehat{H}(\omega)^2 = -1$,
requiring no additional assumptions.
- The same pair appears as the **Kramers–Kronig relations** when $x$ is interpre
ted as frequency and the one-sidedness comes from causality rather than analytic
ity; the mathematics is identical.
