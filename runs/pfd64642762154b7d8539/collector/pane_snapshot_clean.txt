Looking at this problem, I need to compute the limit of an integral over a regio

## Setup and Symmetry

The region $D_a$ requires $\frac{x^2}{2} \le y \le 2x^2$ (so $y > 0$ away from o
rigin) and $a \le \sqrt{x^2+y^2} \le 1/a$. The integrand $\frac{|x|}{y(1+y^2)} \
ge 0$, and the domain is symmetric in $x$. By monotone convergence (non-negative
 integrand, expanding domain), the limit equals the integral over the full parab
olic region $D = \{(x,y): x^2/2 \le y \le 2x^2\}$, provided it converges.

By symmetry in $x$:
$$I = 2\int_0^\infty \int_{x^2/2}^{2x^2} \frac{x}{y(1+y^2)}\,dy\,dx$$

## Inner Integral (over $y$)

Using partial fractions $\frac{1}{y(1+y^2)} = \frac{1}{y} - \frac{y}{1+y^2}$:

$$\int_{x^2/2}^{2x^2} \frac{dy}{y(1+y^2)} = \left[\ln\frac{y}{\sqrt{1+y^2}}\righ
t]_{x^2/2}^{2x^2} = \ln\frac{4\sqrt{1+x^4/4}}{\sqrt{1+4x^4}}$$

## Substitution $u = x^2$

With $u = x^2$, $x\,dx = du/2$:

$$I = \int_0^\infty \ln\frac{4\sqrt{1+u^2/4}}{\sqrt{1+4u^2}}\,du = \int_0^\infty
 \left[\ln 4 + \tfrac{1}{2}\ln(1+u^2/4) - \tfrac{1}{2}\ln(1+4u^2)\right]du$$

**Convergence check:** As $u\to\infty$, the bracket $\to \ln 4 + (\ln u - \ln 2)
 - (\ln u + \ln 2) = 0$, with decay $\sim 15/(8u^2)$. Near $u=0$, the integrand
$\approx \ln 4$ (bounded). So the integral converges.

## Simplification

Rewrite: $\frac{1}{2}\ln(1+u^2/4) = \frac{1}{2}\ln(u^2+4) - \ln 2$ and $\frac{1}
{2}\ln(1+4u^2) = \frac{1}{2}\ln(4u^2+1)$. Collecting:

$$I = \int_0^\infty \left[\ln 2 + \tfrac{1}{2}\ln(u^2+4) - \tfrac{1}{2}\ln(4u^2+
1)\right]du = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^\infty \ln\frac{4(u^2+4)}{4u^2+1}\,du$$

Dividing numerator and denominator by 4:

$$I = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^\infty \ln\frac{u^2+4}{u^2+1/4}\,du = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^
\infty \ln\frac{u^2+2^2}{u^2+(1/2)^2}\,du$$

## Applying the Standard Formula

**Lemma:** For $a, b > 0$, $\displaystyle\int_0^\infty \ln\frac{u^2+a^2}{u^2+b^2
}\,du = \pi(a-b)$.

*Proof:* Let $F(a) = \int_0^\infty \ln\frac{u^2+a^2}{u^2+b^2}\,du$. Differentiat
ing under the integral:
$$F'(a) = \int_0^\infty \frac{2a}{u^2+a^2}\,du = 2a\cdot\frac{\pi}{2a} = \pi$$
So $F(a) = \pi a + C$. Setting $a = b$ gives $F(b)=0$, hence $C = -\pi b$ and $F
(a) = \pi(a-b)$. $\square$

Applying with $a = 2$, $b = 1/2$:

$$I = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\pi\!\left(2 - \frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\pi\cd
ot\frac{3}{2} = \frac{3\pi}{4}$$

## Conclusion

$$\lim_{a\to 0^+}\int_{D_a}\frac{|x|}{y(1+y^2)}\,dx\,dy = \boxed{\dfrac{3\pi}{4}
}$$
