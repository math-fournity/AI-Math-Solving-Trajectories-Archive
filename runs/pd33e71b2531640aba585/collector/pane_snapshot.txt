## Proof

We show that the integral **can** be zero by exhibiting a regular function $f$ a
nd a vector field $v$ for which it vanishes.

**Setup.** Let $f(x) = e^{-|x|^2}$. This is a smooth ($C^\infty$) function on $\
mathbb{R}^d$, and $f(x) > 0$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$, so $f$ is regular a
nd $|f(x)| = f(x)$. Hence
$$
\frac{f(x)}{|f(x)|} = 1 \quad \text{for all } x \in \mathbb{R}^d.
$$

Let $v \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^d; \mathbb{R}^d)$ be any smooth, compactly supp
orted vector field (for instance, $v(x) = x\,\varphi(x)$ where $\varphi$ is a st
andard bump function).

**Computation.** With this choice the integral becomes
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}^d} \frac{f(x)}{|f(x)|}\,\operatorname{div}(v)(x)\,dx \;=\; \int
_{\mathbb{R}^d} \operatorname{div}(v)(x)\,dx.
$$

Since $v$ has compact support, there exists $R > 0$ such that $\operatorname{sup
p}(v) \subset B_R(0)$. Applying the divergence theorem on the ball $B_R(0)$,
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}^d} \operatorname{div}(v)(x)\,dx \;=\; \int_{B_R} \operatorname{
div}(v)\,dx \;=\; \int_{\partial B_R} v \cdot n\, dS \;=\; 0,
$$
because $v \equiv 0$ on $\partial B_R$ (the support of $v$ lies strictly inside
$B_R$).

**Conclusion.** The integral equals $0$ for this choice of $f$ and $v$. Therefor
e, **yes, the integral can be zero.**

More generally, whenever $f$ has constant sign on $\mathbb{R}^d$ (so that $f/|f|
 \equiv \pm 1$) and $v$ is a smooth vector field decaying sufficiently fast at i
nfinity, the integrand reduces to $\pm\operatorname{div}(v)$, and the divergence
 theorem forces the integral to vanish.
