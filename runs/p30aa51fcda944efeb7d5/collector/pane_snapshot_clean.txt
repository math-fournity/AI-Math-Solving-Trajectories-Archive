## Proof (by counterexample)

$$\varphi(t)=\operatorname{sgn}(t)\,|t|^{c},\qquad \varphi(0)=0.$$

* $\varphi$ is a homeomorphism: its inverse is $\varphi^{-1}(s)=\operatorname{sg
n}(s)|s|^{1/c}$, and both are continuous.
* $\varphi$ is multiplicative:
$$\varphi(st)=\operatorname{sgn}(st)|st|^{c}=\operatorname{sgn}(s)\operatorname{
sgn}(t)|s|^{c}|t|^{c}=\varphi(s)\varphi(t).$$
* $\varphi(1)=1$.
* $\varphi$ is **not** additive: $\varphi(1)+\varphi(1)=2$, but $\varphi(2)=2^{c
}\neq 2$ since $c\neq 1$.

Now define $p:C(K)\to C(K)$ by composition:
$$p(f)=\varphi\circ f,\qquad p(f)(x)=\varphi(f(x)).$$

Since $\varphi$ is a homeomorphism, $p$ is a bijection on $C(K)$ (with inverse $
p^{-1}(g)=\varphi^{-1}\circ g$). It preserves multiplication:
$$p(fg)(x)=\varphi(f(x)g(x))=\varphi(f(x))\,\varphi(g(x))=(p(f)\,p(g))(x),$$
so $p(fg)=p(f)\,p(g)$. Also $p(\mathbf{1})=\mathbf{1}$ since $\varphi(1)=1$.

Yet $p$ does **not** preserve addition. For the constant function $\mathbf{1}$:
$$p(\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{1})(x)=\varphi(2)=2^{c},\qquad (p(\mathbf{1})+p(\mathbf{1
}))(x)=1+1=2.$$
Since $c\neq 1$, $2^{c}\neq 2$, so $p(\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{1})\neq p(\mathbf{1})+p
(\mathbf{1})$.

Thus $p$ is a multiplicative bijection (even unital) that is not a ring isomorph
ism.
