## Proof

**Claim.** Yes — $h$ is an isomorphism of $L$-structures.

Let $|N| = n$ (a natural number, since $N$ is finite). We prove that $h$ is bije
ctive and preserves all $L$-structure; bijectivity together with structure-prese
rvation is exactly the definition of an isomorphism.

### Step 1. $h$ is injective.

Suppose $a, b \in N$ with $a \neq b$. Then $N \models (a \neq b)$, i.e. $N \mode
ls \varphi(a,b)$ where $\varphi(x,y) \equiv (x \neq y)$. By elementarity of $h$,
$$M \models \varphi(h(a), h(b)),$$
so $h(a) \neq h(b)$. Hence $h$ is injective, and $|h(N)| = |N| = n$.

### Step 2. $M$ has exactly $n$ elements.

Consider the first-order $L$-sentence
$$\sigma_n \;:=\; \exists x_1 \cdots \exists x_n \Bigl(\bigwedge_{1 \le i < j \l
e n} x_i \neq x_j \;\wedge\; \forall y \bigvee_{i=1}^{n} y = x_i\Bigr),$$
asserting "there are exactly $n$ elements." Since $|N| = n$, we have $N \models
\sigma_n$. By elementarity (applied to the sentence $\sigma_n$, i.e. the empty-t
uple case),
$$M \models \sigma_n.$$
Therefore $|M| = n$.

### Step 3. $h$ is surjective.

From Steps 1 and 2, $h \colon N \to M$ is an injective map between two finite se
ts of the same cardinality $n$. Hence $h$ is bijective.

### Step 4. $h$ preserves all $L$-structure.

An elementary embedding preserves the truth of **all** first-order $L$-formulas,
 in particular all atomic ones. Concretely:

- **Constants.** For each constant symbol $c \in L$, the atomic formula $c = x$
gives
  $$N \models (c^N = a) \iff M \models (c^M = h(a))$$
  for $a = c^N$, yielding $h(c^N) = c^M$.

- **Functions.** For each function symbol $f \in L$ of arity $k$ and every $\bar
 a \in N^k$, the atomic formula $f(\bar x) = y$ applied to $(\bar a, f^N(\bar a)
)$ gives
  $$h\bigl(f^N(\bar a)\bigr) = f^M\bigl(h(\bar a)\bigr).$$

- **Relations.** For each relation symbol $R \in L$ of arity $k$ and every $\bar
 a \in N^k$,
  $$\bar a \in R^N \iff h(\bar a) \in R^M,$$
  directly from elementarity applied to the atomic formula $R(\bar x)$.

Thus $h$ preserves constants, functions, and relations.

### Conclusion.

$h \colon N \to M$ is a bijection (Step 3) that preserves all $L$-structure (Ste
p 4). Therefore $h$ is an isomorphism of $L$-structures. $\blacksquare$
