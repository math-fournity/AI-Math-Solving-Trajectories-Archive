## Proof

**Claim:** The cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_r(X)$ can always be represented with
coefficients in $F_p$.

**Key Fact.** The cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_r(X)$ has integer coefficients, i.
e., $\Phi_r(X) \in \mathbb{Z}[X]$.

This is a standard result. The $r$-th cyclotomic polynomial is defined as
$$\Phi_r(X) = \prod_{\substack{1 \le k \le r \\ \gcd(k,r)=1}} \left(X - e^{2\pi
i k/r}\right).$$
Since $\Phi_r(X)$ divides $X^r - 1$ in $\mathbb{Z}[X]$ (it is a product of certa
in factors of $X^r - 1 = \prod_{d \mid r} \Phi_d(X)$), and $X^r - 1$ is monic wi
th integer coefficients, by Gauss's Lemma $\Phi_r(X)$ is monic with integer coef
ficients.

**Reduction modulo $p$.** There is a natural ring homomorphism (reduction modulo
 $p$):
$$\rho_p : \mathbb{Z}[X] \longrightarrow F_p[X], \qquad \sum a_i X^i \mapsto \su
m \bar{a}_i X^i,$$
where $\bar{a}_i$ denotes $a_i \bmod p$.

Applying $\rho_p$ to $\Phi_r(X) \in \mathbb{Z}[X]$ yields a well-defined polynom
ial $\bar{\Phi}_r(X) := \rho_p(\Phi_r(X)) \in F_p[X]$.

**Conclusion.** Since $\Phi_r(X) \in \mathbb{Z}[X]$ for every $r \ge 1$, and the
 reduction map $\rho_p : \mathbb{Z}[X] \to F_p[X]$ is defined for every prime $p
$, the polynomial $\Phi_r(X)$ can **always** be represented with coefficients in
 $F_p$. The representation is simply $\bar{\Phi}_r(X) \in F_p[X]$, obtained by r
educing each integer coefficient of $\Phi_r(X)$ modulo $p$.

**Remark on factorization (not required for the claim, but clarifying).** The be
havior of $\bar{\Phi}_r(X)$ in $F_p[X]$ depends on the relationship between $r$
and $p$:

- If $\gcd(r, p) = 1$, then $\bar{\Phi}_r(X)$ is separable over $F_p$ and factor
s into $\varphi(r)/d$ distinct irreducible polynomials of degree $d$, where $d =
 \operatorname{ord}_r(p)$ is the multiplicative order of $p$ modulo $r$.

- If $p \mid r$, write $r = p^k \cdot m$ with $\gcd(m, p) = 1$ and $k \ge 1$. Th
en $\bar{\Phi}_r(X) = \bar{\Phi}_m(X)^{\varphi(p^k)}$ in $F_p[X]$, so $\bar{\Phi
}_r(X)$ is a power of a separable polynomial.

In both cases, $\bar{\Phi}_r(X)$ is a well-defined element of $F_p[X]$, confirmi
ng that the representation always exists. $\blacksquare$
