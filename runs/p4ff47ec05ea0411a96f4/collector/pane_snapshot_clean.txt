Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether, for any domain $R$ with qu
otient field $K$ and any finite algebraic extension $L/K$, there exists a $K$-ba
sis of $L$ consisting of elements integral over $R$.

**Answer: Yes, such a basis always exists.**

## Proof

**Step 1: Reduction to integral generators.**

Since $L/K$ is a finite algebraic extension, there exist elements $\alpha_1, \do
ts, \alpha_m \in L$ (each algebraic over $K$) such that $L = K(\alpha_1, \dots,
\alpha_m)$.

For each $\alpha_i$, since it is algebraic over $K$, it satisfies a monic polyno
mial over $K$:
$$\alpha_i^n + c_{n-1}^{(i)}\,\alpha_i^{n-1} + \cdots + c_0^{(i)} = 0, \qquad c_
j^{(i)} \in K.$$

Write each $c_j^{(i)} = a_j^{(i)}/b_j^{(i)}$ with $a_j^{(i)}, b_j^{(i)} \in R$,
$b_j^{(i)} \neq 0$. Set
$$d_i = \prod_{j=0}^{n-1} b_j^{(i)} \in R \setminus \{0\}, \qquad \beta_i = d_i\
,\alpha_i.$$

Since $d_i \in K^\times$, we have $K(\beta_i) = K(\alpha_i)$, so $L = K(\beta_1,
 \dots, \beta_m)$.

**Claim:** Each $\beta_i$ is integral over $R$.

Substituting $\alpha_i = \beta_i / d_i$ into the minimal polynomial and multiply
ing through by $d_i^n$:
$$\beta_i^n + \bigl(c_{n-1}^{(i)}\,d_i\bigr)\,\beta_i^{n-1} + \bigl(c_{n-2}^{(i)
}\,d_i^2\bigr)\,\beta_i^{n-2} + \cdots + \bigl(c_0^{(i)}\,d_i^n\bigr) = 0.$$

The coefficient of $\beta_i^{n-k}$ is $c_{n-k}^{(i)}\,d_i^k = \frac{a_{n-k}^{(i)
}}{b_{n-k}^{(i)}}\,d_i^k$. Since $b_{n-k}^{(i)}$ divides $d_i$ (it is one of the
 factors in the product), we have $d_i^k / b_{n-k}^{(i)} \in R$, so the coeffici
ent lies in $R$. Thus $\beta_i$ satisfies a monic polynomial over $R$, i.e., $\b
eta_i$ is integral over $R$. ✓

**Step 2: The integral elements form a ring.**

A standard result in commutative algebra: the set of elements of $L$ that are in
tegral over $R$ forms a subring of $L$ (the *integral closure* of $R$ in $L$).

*Proof sketch:* If $a, b$ are integral over $R$, then $R[a]$ and $R[a][b]$ are f
initely generated $R$-modules. Since $a \pm b, ab \in R[a][b]$ and $R[a][b]$ is
a finite $R$-module, every element of $R[a][b]$ is integral over $R$. Hence $a \
pm b$ and $ab$ are integral over $R$. ✓

**Step 3: Constructing the basis.**

Since each $\beta_i$ is integral over $R$ and the integral elements form a ring,
 every monomial
$$\beta_1^{e_1}\,\beta_2^{e_2}\,\cdots\,\beta_m^{e_m}, \qquad e_i \geq 0,$$
is integral over $R$.

The set of all such monomials spans $L$ as a $K$-vector space, because $L = K(\b
eta_1, \dots, \beta_m)$: every element of $L$ is a $K$-linear combination of fin
itely many such monomials.

Since $[L:K] = n < \infty$, the $K$-vector space $L$ is finite-dimensional. From
 the (possibly infinite) spanning set of monomials — each integral over $R$ — we
 extract a finite $K$-basis $\{v_1, \dots, v_n\}$ by standard linear algebra (e.
g., greedily selecting linearly independent elements).

Each $v_j$ is one of the monomials $\beta_1^{e_1}\cdots\beta_m^{e_m}$, hence int
egral over $R$. ✓

**Conclusion.** For any domain $R$ with quotient field $K$, and any finite algeb
raic extension $L/K$, there exists a $K$-basis of $L$ such that every basis elem
ent is integral over $R$. The key ingredients are: (1) clearing denominators con
verts any algebraic generator into an integral one without changing the field it
 generates, and (2) the integral closure is a subring, so all monomials in integ
ral generators remain integral, allowing us to extract a basis from among them.
