## Proof

**Setup.** Let $U = \{z \in \mathbb{R}^2 : |z| = 1\}$ be the unit circle and $D
= \{z : |z| \le 1\}$ the closed unit disk. Let $V \subseteq U$ be the set of poi
nts whose angle from the positive $X$-axis is rational. Since the rationals are
dense in $\mathbb{R}$, $V$ is **dense in $U$**. Also $V$ is countable, so $V \su
bsetneq U$.

---

**Step 1. The closure of $\operatorname{conv}(V)$ is $D$.**

Since $V \subseteq U \subseteq D$ and $D$ is convex, $\operatorname{conv}(V) \su
bseteq D$, hence $\overline{\operatorname{conv}(V)} \subseteq D$.

Conversely, $V$ is dense in $U$, so $U \subseteq \overline{V} \subseteq \overlin
e{\operatorname{conv}(V)}$. Taking convex hull of both sides and using that the
closed convex hull of a set equals the closure of its convex hull,
$$
D = \operatorname{conv}(U) = \operatorname{conv}\!\big(\overline{V}\big) \subset
eq \overline{\operatorname{conv}(V)}.
$$
Thus $\overline{\operatorname{conv}(V)} = D$.

---

**Step 2. The interior of $\operatorname{conv}(V)$ is the open unit disk.**

$V$ contains three non-collinear points (indeed infinitely many), so $\operatorn
ame{conv}(V)$ has nonempty interior. A standard fact for convex sets with nonemp
ty interior is
$$
\operatorname{int}\!\big(\operatorname{conv}(V)\big) = \operatorname{int}\!\big(
\overline{\operatorname{conv}(V)}\big) = \operatorname{int}(D),
$$
which is the open unit disk. Hence **every point of the open disk lies in $\oper
atorname{conv}(V)$**.

---

**Step 3. $\operatorname{conv}(V) \cap U = V$ (strict convexity).**

The closed unit disk $D$ is **strictly convex**: its boundary $U$ contains no no
ntrivial line segment. Equivalently, if $x, y \in U$ and $(1-t)x + ty \in U$ for
 some $t \in (0,1)$, then $x = y$. By induction, any finite convex combination $
\sum_i \lambda_i v_i$ (with $v_i \in U$, $\lambda_i > 0$, $\sum \lambda_i = 1$)
that lies on $U$ forces $v_1 = \cdots = v_k$.

Now take $p \in \operatorname{conv}(V) \cap U$. Write $p = \sum_i \lambda_i v_i$
 with $v_i \in V \subseteq U$. Since $p \in U$, strict convexity forces all $v_i
$ equal, so $p = v_1 \in V$. The reverse inclusion $V \subseteq \operatorname{co
nv}(V) \cap U$ is trivial. Therefore
$$
\operatorname{conv}(V) \cap U = V.
$$

---

**Step 4. Determine $\operatorname{conv}(V)$ and its boundary.**

Combining Steps 2 and 3:
$$
\operatorname{conv}(V) = \operatorname{int}(D) \cup V = \{\,z : |z| < 1\,\} \cup
 V.
$$
Its closure is $D$ and its interior is the open disk, so
$$
H = \partial\!\big(\operatorname{conv}(V)\big) = \overline{\operatorname{conv}(V
)} \setminus \operatorname{int}\!\big(\operatorname{conv}(V)\big) = D \setminus
\operatorname{int}(D) = U.
$$

---

**Conclusion.** $H = U$. Moreover $H \neq V$, since $V$ is countable (hence a pr
oper subset) while $U$ is uncountable. Explicitly, any point of $U$ whose angle
is irrational — for instance $(\cos 1, \sin 1)$, since $1$ is irrational — lies
in $U \setminus V$.

$$
\boxed{H = U, \quad H \neq V.}
$$
