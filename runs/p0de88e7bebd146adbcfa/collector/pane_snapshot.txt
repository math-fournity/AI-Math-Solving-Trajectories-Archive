## Proof

We want to compute the variance of the Itô integral

$$I_t = \int_0^t (B_s^2 + s)\, dB_s.$$

**Step 1: Apply Itô isometry.**

For any square-integrable adapted integrand $f_s$, the Itô isometry gives

$$\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\int_0^t f_s\, dB_s\right) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_
0^t f_s^2\, ds\right].$$

Since $B_s^2 + s$ is adapted and square-integrable on $[0,t]$, we have

$$\operatorname{Var}(I_t) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^t (B_s^2 + s)^2\, ds\right]
 = \int_0^t \mathbb{E}\!\left[(B_s^2 + s)^2\right] ds.$$

**Step 2: Compute the integrand $\mathbb{E}[(B_s^2 + s)^2]$.**

Expand:

$$(B_s^2 + s)^2 = B_s^4 + 2s\,B_s^2 + s^2.$$

Since $B_s \sim \mathcal{N}(0, s)$, the moments of a centered Gaussian give

$$\mathbb{E}[B_s^2] = s, \qquad \mathbb{E}[B_s^4] = 3s^2.$$

Therefore

$$\mathbb{E}[(B_s^2 + s)^2] = 3s^2 + 2s \cdot s + s^2 = 3s^2 + 2s^2 + s^2 = 6s^2
.$$

**Step 3: Integrate.**

$$\operatorname{Var}(I_t) = \int_0^t 6s^2\, ds = 6 \cdot \frac{t^3}{3} = 2t^3.$$

**Result:**

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\int_0^t (B_s^2 + s)\, dB_s\right) = 2t^3.}$$
