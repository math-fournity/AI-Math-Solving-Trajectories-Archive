## Solution

We want the residue at $z=0$ of
$$f(z) = \frac{z^{n-1}}{\sin^n z}, \qquad n \in \mathbb{N}.$$

**Key observation.** Since $\sin z$ has a simple zero at $z=0$, we factor out $z
$ from each copy of $\sin z$:
$$\sin z = z \cdot \frac{\sin z}{z}, \qquad \text{where } \frac{\sin z}{z} = 1 -
 \frac{z^2}{6} + \frac{z^4}{120} - \cdots$$
is analytic at $z=0$ with value $1$.

Substituting,
$$f(z) = \frac{z^{n-1}}{z^n \left(\frac{\sin z}{z}\right)^n} = \frac{1}{z}\left(
\frac{z}{\sin z}\right)^n.$$

**Analyticity of the factor.** The function
$$g(z) := \left(\frac{z}{\sin z}\right)^n$$
has a removable singularity at $z=0$ (since $\sin z / z \to 1$, so $z/\sin z \to
 1$), and after removal it is analytic in a neighborhood of $0$ with
$$g(0) = 1^n = 1.$$

**Extracting the residue.** The Laurent expansion of $f$ at $z=0$ is therefore
$$f(z) = \frac{g(z)}{z} = \frac{g(0)}{z} + g'(0) + \frac{g''(0)}{2}\,z + \cdots$
$

The residue — the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ — is exactly $g(0)$.

**Verification with small $n$.**
- $n=1$: $\dfrac{1}{\sin z} = \dfrac{1}{z}\left(1 + \dfrac{z^2}{6}+\cdots\right)
$, residue $= 1$. ✓
- $n=2$: $\dfrac{z}{\sin^2 z} = \dfrac{1}{z}\left(\dfrac{z}{\sin z}\right)^2 = \
dfrac{1}{z}\left(1 + \dfrac{z^2}{3}+\cdots\right)$, residue $= 1$. ✓

Both agree with $g(0)=1$.

**Conclusion.** For every $n \in \mathbb{N}$,

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{z^{n-1}}{\sin^n z} = \left(\frac{z}{\sin z}\righ
t)^n\bigg|_{z=0} = 1.$$

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{z^{n-1}}{\sin^n z} = 1}$$
