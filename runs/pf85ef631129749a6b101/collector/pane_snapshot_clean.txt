Looking at this problem, I need to find a configuration of 5 points in 3D where
four spheres are pairwise externally tangent and all internally tangent to the f
ifth, yet the center of the largest sphere cannot be uniquely identified.

## Setting Up the Equations

Let the five points be $P_1, \ldots, P_5$ with pairwise distances $d_{ij}$. If $
P_i$ is the center of the largest sphere (radius $R_i$), the other four spheres
have radii $r_j = R_i - d_{ij}$, and pairwise external tangency gives:

$$d_{jk} = r_j + r_k = 2R_i - d_{ij} - d_{ik} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad d_{jk}
 + d_{ij} + d_{ik} = 2R_i$$

for all pairs $\{j,k\}$ not containing $i$. This sum must be **constant** over a
ll 6 such pairs.

## Two Ambiguous Points Force the Structure

Suppose points $P_1$ and $P_2$ are both valid centers of the largest sphere. Com
paring the equation $d_{23}+d_{12}+d_{13} = S_1$ (from $i=1$) with $d_{13}+d_{12
}+d_{23} = S_2$ (from $i=2$) gives $S_1 = S_2 = S$.

Cross-referencing all equations from both $i=1$ and $i=2$ yields:
- $d_{13} = d_{23}$, $d_{14} = d_{24}$, $d_{15} = d_{25}$ (points 1,2 equidistan
t from 3,4,5)
- $d_{14} = d_{13}$, $d_{15} = d_{13}$ (from constancy within $i=1$)
- $d_{34} = d_{35} = d_{45} = d_{12} = a$

So all cross-distances $d_{1j} = d_{2j} = \ell$ for $j \in \{3,4,5\}$, and $d_{1
2} = d_{34} = d_{35} = d_{45} = a$.

## The 3D Embedding Uniquely Determines $\ell/a$

Points $P_3, P_4, P_5$ form an equilateral triangle of side $a$. Points $P_1, P_
2$ are each equidistant ($\ell$) from all three vertices, so they lie on the per
pendicular axis through the circumcenter (circumradius $a/\sqrt{3}$). Since $d_{
12} = a$, they are at heights $\pm a/2$:

$$\ell = \sqrt{\frac{a^2}{3} + \frac{a^2}{4}} = a\sqrt{\frac{7}{12}}$$

This is a **triangular bipyramid**, and $\ell/a$ is uniquely fixed by the 3D con
straint.

## No Other Point Can Be the Largest

For $P_3$ to be the largest, the pair $\{4,5\}$ gives $d_{45}+d_{34}+d_{35} = 3a
$, while $\{1,2\}$ gives $d_{12}+d_{31}+d_{32} = a + 2\ell$. Equality requires $
\ell = a$, but $\ell = a\sqrt{7/12} \neq a$. So only $P_1, P_2$ are ambiguous —
exactly two possibilities, confirming "impossible to determine which."

## Computing the Ratio

With $P_1$ as the largest sphere (radius $R$), the four small spheres are at $P_
2, P_3, P_4, P_5$:

- $r_2 = R - a$, $\quad r_3 = r_4 = r_5 = R - \ell$
- From $d_{34} = r_3 + r_4 = 2(R-\ell) = a$: $\quad R = \frac{a}{2} + \ell$

So:
$$R = a\!\left(\frac{1}{2} + \sqrt{\frac{7}{12}}\right), \qquad r_2 = a\!\left(\
sqrt{\frac{7}{12}} - \frac{1}{2}\right), \qquad r_3 = r_4 = r_5 = \frac{a}{2}$$

Since $\sqrt{7/12} \approx 0.764$, we have $r_2 \approx 0.264a < 0.5a = r_3$, so
 the smallest radius is $r_2$.

The ratio of greatest to smallest:

$$\frac{R}{r_2} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} + \sqrt{\frac{7}{12}}}{\sqrt{\frac{7}{12}} -
 \frac{1}{2}}$$

Setting $t = \sqrt{7/12} = \sqrt{21}/6$ and rationalizing:

$$\frac{t + \frac{1}{2}}{t - \frac{1}{2}} = \frac{(t+\frac{1}{2})^2}{t^2 - \frac
{1}{4}} = \frac{t^2 + t + \frac{1}{4}}{t^2 - \frac{1}{4}} = \frac{\frac{7}{12} +
 \frac{\sqrt{21}}{6} + \frac{1}{4}}{\frac{7}{12} - \frac{1}{4}} = \frac{\frac{5}
{6} + \frac{\sqrt{21}}{6}}{\frac{1}{3}} = \frac{5 + \sqrt{21}}{2}$$

## Verification

- **Pairwise external tangency**: $d_{34} = a = \frac{a}{2} + \frac{a}{2} = r_3
+ r_4$ ✓; $d_{23} = \ell = (\ell - \frac{a}{2}) + \frac{a}{2} = r_2 + r_3$ ✓

- **3D realizability**: The bipyramid with equatorial side $a$ and apex separati
