## Solution

We determine the Pontryagin dual $\widehat{\mathbb{R}_{>0}^*}$, i.e., the group
of continuous homomorphisms $\chi: \mathbb{R}_{>0}^* \to \mathbb{T}$ (where $\ma
thbb{T} = \{z \in \mathbb{C}^* : |z|=1\}$), with pointwise multiplication.

**Step 1: Reduction to the dual of $\mathbb{R}$.**

The logarithm $\log: \mathbb{R}_{>0}^* \to \mathbb{R}$ is a group isomorphism (f
rom multiplicative to additive) and a homeomorphism. It induces a bijection betw
een characters:

- If $\chi \in \widehat{\mathbb{R}_{>0}^*}$, then $\widetilde{\chi}(t) := \chi(e
^t)$ is a continuous homomorphism $\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{T}$.
- Conversely, if $\widetilde{\chi} \in \widehat{\mathbb{R}}$, then $\chi(x) := \
widetilde{\chi}(\log x)$ is a continuous homomorphism $\mathbb{R}_{>0}^* \to \ma
thbb{T}$.

These constructions are inverse to each other and preserve the group operation,
so $\widehat{\mathbb{R}_{>0}^*} \cong \widehat{\mathbb{R}}$ as topological group
s.

**Step 2: Compute $\widehat{\mathbb{R}}$.**

Let $\widetilde{\chi}: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{T}$ be a continuous homomorphism.
We show $\widetilde{\chi}(t) = e^{2\pi i y t}$ for some $y \in \mathbb{R}$.

Since $\widetilde{\chi}$ is continuous and $\widetilde{\chi}(0) = 1$, there exis
ts $\delta > 0$ such that $\widetilde{\chi}(t)$ lies in a small arc around $1 \i
n \mathbb{T}$ for $|t| < \delta$. On this arc we choose a continuous branch of t
he argument with $\arg(1) = 0$, and set $\phi(t) = \arg(\widetilde{\chi}(t))$ fo
r $|t| < \delta$. Then $\phi$ is continuous, $\phi(0) = 0$, and $\widetilde{\chi
}(t) = e^{i\phi(t)}$.

For $s, t$ with $|s|, |t|, |s+t| < \delta$, the homomorphism property gives
$$e^{i\phi(s+t)} = \widetilde{\chi}(s+t) = \widetilde{\chi}(s)\widetilde{\chi}(t
) = e^{i(\phi(s)+\phi(t))},$$
so $\phi(s+t) - \phi(s) - \phi(t) \in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}$. By continuity this expres
sion is $0$ near the origin, hence $\phi$ is **additive** on $(-\delta, \delta)$
.

A continuous additive function on an interval extends uniquely to a continuous a
dditive function on all of $\mathbb{R}$ (by $\phi(t) = n\,\phi(t/n)$ for large $
n$), and every continuous additive function on $\mathbb{R}$ is of the form $\phi
(t) = at$ for $a = \phi(1) \in \mathbb{R}$. Therefore
$$\widetilde{\chi}(t) = e^{iat} \quad \text{for all } t \in \mathbb{R}.$$
Setting $y = a/(2\pi)$, we obtain $\widetilde{\chi}(t) = e^{2\pi i y t}$.

Conversely, every map $t \mapsto e^{2\pi i y t}$ is clearly a continuous homomor
phism $\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{T}$. The correspondence $y \leftrightarrow \wideti
lde{\chi}_y$ is a group isomorphism $\mathbb{R} \to \widehat{\mathbb{R}}$ since
$\widetilde{\chi}_y \cdot \widetilde{\chi}_{y'} = \widetilde{\chi}_{y+y'}$.

**Step 3: Conclude.**

Composing with the logarithm, every character of $\mathbb{R}_{>0}^*$ is of the f
orm
$$\chi_y(x) = e^{2\pi i y \log x} = x^{2\pi i y}, \quad y \in \mathbb{R},$$
and $\chi_y \cdot \chi_{y'} = \chi_{y+y'}$. The map $y \mapsto \chi_y$ is a topo
logical group isomorphism.

**Conclusion.** The dual group of $\mathbb{R}_{>0}^*$ is (isomorphic to) the add
itive group $\mathbb{R}$:
$$\widehat{\mathbb{R}_{>0}^*} \;\cong\; \mathbb{R}, \qquad \chi_y(x) = x^{2\pi i
 y},\;\; y \in \mathbb{R}.$$
