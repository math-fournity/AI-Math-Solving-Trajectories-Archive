## Solution

We find all primes $a,b,c$ and integers $k$ with $a^2+b^2+16c^2 = 9k^2+1$.

**Step 1: Reduction modulo 3.**

Since $9k^2\equiv 0\pmod 3$ and $16\equiv 1\pmod 3$, the equation gives
$$a^2+b^2+c^2 \equiv 1 \pmod 3.$$
A square is $0\pmod 3$ iff the base is divisible by $3$, and $1$ otherwise. The
sum of three values in $\{0,1\}$ equals $1\pmod 3$ only when the sum is exactly
$1$, i.e. **exactly one** of $a,b,c$ is not divisible by $3$. Since the only pri
me divisible by $3$ is $3$ itself, **exactly two of $a,b,c$ equal $3$**, and the
 third is a prime $\ne 3$.

**Step 2: The case $a=b=3$, $c\ne 3$ (the "odd one out" is $c$).**

The equation becomes $9+9+16c^2=9k^2+1$, i.e.
$$9k^2-16c^2=17,\qquad (3k-4c)(3k+4c)=17.$$
Since $17$ is prime and $3k+4c>3k-4c$ for $c>0$, the only positive factorization
 is
$$3k-4c=1,\quad 3k+4c=17 \implies 6k=18,\ 8c=16 \implies k=3,\ c=2.$$
(The factorization $(-1,-17)$ gives $c=-2$, not prime.) Thus $c=2$, $k=\pm 3$:
$$(a,b,c,k)=(3,3,2,\pm 3).$$
Check: $9+9+64=82=9\cdot 9+1$. ✓

**Step 3: The case $b=c=3$, $a\ne 3$ (the "odd one out" is $a$).**

The equation becomes $a^2+9+144=9k^2+1$, i.e.
$$9k^2-a^2=152,\qquad (3k-a)(3k+a)=152.$$
Both factors have the same parity; since $152$ is even, both are even. The even
factor pairs $(d,e)$ of $152=2^3\cdot 19$ with $d\le e$ and $de=152$ are:
- $(d,e)=(2,76)$: $3k=\tfrac{2+76}{2}=39\Rightarrow k=13$, $a=\tfrac{76-2}{2}=37
$.
- $(d,e)=(4,38)$: $3k=\tfrac{4+38}{2}=21\Rightarrow k=7$, $a=\tfrac{38-4}{2}=17$
.

(The pair $(8,19)$ has mismatched parity; $(1,152)$ is odd.) Both $17$ and $37$
are prime. Taking $k>0$ and then allowing $k<0$:
$$(a,b,c,k)=(17,3,3,\pm 7),\ (37,3,3,\pm 13).$$
Checks: $17^2+9+144=442=9\cdot 49+1$ ✓; $37^2+9+144=1522=9\cdot 169+1$ ✓.

**Step 4: The case $a=c=3$, $b\ne 3$ (the "odd one out" is $b$).**

By symmetry with Step 3 (the equation is symmetric in $a,b$), we get
$$(a,b,c,k)=(3,17,3,\pm 7),\ (3,37,3,\pm 13).$$
Checks: $9+17^2+144=442=9\cdot 49+1$ ✓; $9+37^2+144=1522=9\cdot 169+1$ ✓.

**Step 5: Completeness.**

The modulo-3 argument forces exactly two of $a,b,c$ to be $3$, leaving three sub
cases according to which variable is the exception. Each subcase reduces to a di
fference-of-squares factorization of a fixed integer ($17$ or $152$), whose comp
lete factor enumeration yields exactly the solutions above. No other cases exist
.

**All solutions $(a,b,c,k)$:**

$$\boxed{(3,3,2,\pm 3),\quad (3,17,3,\pm 7),\quad (17,3,3,\pm 7),\quad (3,37,3,\
pm 13),\quad (37,3,3,\pm 13).}$$
