Looking at this problem, I need to evaluate:
$$\lim_{x \to +\infty} \int_{0}^{\pi/2} x\, e^{-x^2 \cos t}\, dt.$$

 solutions, sketches, or formalization notes. Proceeding.

## Proof

**Step 1: Substitution to isolate the boundary.**

Let $u = \frac{\pi}{2} - t$, so $\cos t = \sin u$ and $dt = du$:
$$I(x) = \int_0^{\pi/2} x\, e^{-x^2 \sin u}\, du.$$

The integrand is largest near $u = 0$ (where $\sin u \approx 0$) and decays rapi
dly away from it.

**Step 2: Change of variable $s = x^2 \sin u$.**

Then $ds = x^2 \cos u\, du = x^2\sqrt{1 - s^2/x^4}\, du$, so $du = \frac{ds}{x^2
\sqrt{1 - s^2/x^4}}$. The limits become $s: 0 \to x^2$:
$$I(x) = \frac{1}{x}\int_0^{x^2} \frac{e^{-s}}{\sqrt{1 - s^2/x^4}}\, ds.$$

**Step 3: Upper bound — split into two regions.**

*Region 1:* $s \in [0,\, x^2/2]$. Here $s^2/x^4 \leq 1/4$, so $\sqrt{1 - s^2/x^4
} \geq \sqrt{3}/2$, giving
$$\frac{1}{x}\int_0^{x^2/2} \frac{e^{-s}}{\sqrt{1-s^2/x^4}}\, ds \;\leq\; \frac{
2}{x\sqrt{3}}\int_0^{\infty} e^{-s}\, ds \;=\; \frac{2}{x\sqrt{3}} \;\xrightarro
w{x\to\infty}\; 0.$$

*Region 2:* $s \in [x^2/2,\, x^2]$. Here $e^{-s} \leq e^{-x^2/2}$. For the remai
ning integral, substitute $s = x^2 v$:
$$\int_{x^2/2}^{x^2} \frac{ds}{\sqrt{1-s^2/x^4}} = x^2\int_{1/2}^{1}\frac{dv}{\s
qrt{1-v^2}} = x^2\Big[\arcsin v\Big]_{1/2}^{1} = x^2\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{
\pi}{6}\right) = \frac{\pi x^2}{3}.$$

Therefore
$$\text{Region 2} \;\leq\; \frac{e^{-x^2/2}}{x}\cdot\frac{\pi x^2}{3} = \frac{\p
i x}{3}\,e^{-x^2/2} \;\xrightarrow{x\to\infty}\; 0.$$

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Since the integrand $x\,e^{-x^2\cos t}$ is non-negative on $[0,\pi/2]$, we have
$I(x) \geq 0$. Combined with the upper bound $I(x) \leq \frac{2}{x\sqrt{3}} + \f
rac{\pi x}{3}e^{-x^2/2} \to 0$, by the squeeze theorem:

$$\lim_{x \to +\infty} \int_{0}^{\pi/2} x\, e^{-x^2 \cos t}\, dt = 0.$$

**Remark on the rate.** A more refined analysis (Laplace's method) shows $I(x) \
sim \frac{1}{x}$ as $x \to +\infty$, since the dominant contribution comes from
$u \approx 0$ where $\sin u \approx u$:
$$I(x) \approx \frac{1}{x}\int_0^{\infty} e^{-s}\, ds = \frac{1}{x} \to 0,$$
consistent with the rigorous bound above.
