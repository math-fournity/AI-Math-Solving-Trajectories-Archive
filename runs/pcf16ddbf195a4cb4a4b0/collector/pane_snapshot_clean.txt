## Proof

Let $A$ be a $9\times 9$ real matrix with $A^2 = I$.

**Step 1: The minimal polynomial divides $x^2 - 1$.**

Since $A^2 = I$, the matrix $A$ satisfies the polynomial $p(x) = x^2 - 1 = (x-1)
(x+1)$. Hence the minimal polynomial $m_A(x)$ divides $p(x)$.

**Step 2: $A$ is diagonalizable over $\mathbb{R}$.**

The polynomial $x^2 - 1 = (x-1)(x+1)$ splits over $\mathbb{R}$ into **distinct**
 linear factors. Since $m_A(x)$ divides a polynomial with distinct linear factor
s, $m_A(x)$ itself has distinct linear factors. By the standard criterion, $A$ i
s diagonalizable over $\mathbb{R}$.

**Step 3: The only possible eigenvalues are $1$ and $-1$.**

Any eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $A$ satisfies $\lambda^2 = 1$, so $\lambda \in \{1,
-1\}$.

**Step 4: Similarity classes are determined by the eigenvalue multiplicities.**

Since $A$ is diagonalizable, $A$ is similar to a diagonal matrix $\operatorname{
diag}(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{k},\underbrace{-1,\dots,-1}_{9-k})$ where $k$ is t
he multiplicity of the eigenvalue $1$. Two such diagonal matrices are similar if
 and only if they have the same eigenvalue multiplicities, i.e., the same value
of $k$.

**Step 5: Count the distinct classes.**

The multiplicity $k$ of eigenvalue $1$ can be any integer from $0$ to $9$ inclus
ive. Each value of $k$ gives a distinct similarity class. Therefore the number o
f distinct similarity classes is

$$|\{0, 1, 2, \dots, 9\}| = 10.$$
