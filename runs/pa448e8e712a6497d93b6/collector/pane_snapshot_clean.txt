## Solution

**Setup.** Let $A \in SL_2(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})$ with $p$ an odd prime, and s
uppose the characteristic polynomial
$$P_A(x) = x^2 - \operatorname{tr}(A)\,x + 1$$
has no roots in $\mathbb{F}_p = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$.

**Step 1: Eigenvalues live in $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}\setminus\mathbb{F}_p$.** Since $
P_A$ is an irreducible quadratic over $\mathbb{F}_p$, its roots $\lambda, \lambd
a'$ lie in the quadratic extension $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$ and are Galois conjugates:
 $\lambda' = \lambda^p$. By Vieta, $\lambda\lambda' = \det(A) = 1$, hence
$$\lambda^{p+1} = \lambda\cdot\lambda^p = 1.$$

**Step 2: Order of $A$ equals order of $\lambda$.** Since $P_A$ is separable (we
 are in characteristic $p \neq 2$, and irreducibility implies no repeated root),
 $A$ is diagonalizable over $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$, conjugate to $\operatorname{diag
}(\lambda, \lambda^p)$. Therefore
$$\operatorname{ord}(A) = \operatorname{ord}(\lambda).$$

**Step 3: The order divides $p+1$.** From $\lambda^{p+1}=1$ we get $\operatornam
e{ord}(\lambda)\mid(p+1)$.

**Step 4: The bound $p+1$ is attained.** The multiplicative group $\mathbb{F}_{p
^2}^*$ is cyclic of order $p^2-1=(p-1)(p+1)$, so it contains an element $\lambda
$ of order $p+1$. Such $\lambda$ is **not** in $\mathbb{F}_p^*$, since every ele
ment of $\mathbb{F}_p^*$ has order dividing $p-1$, and for odd $p\geq 3$ we have
 $\gcd(p-1,p+1)=2$, so $p+1\nmid p-1$ (as $p+1 > p-1$). Thus $\lambda\in\mathbb{
F}_{p^2}\setminus\mathbb{F}_p$, and its minimal polynomial over $\mathbb{F}_p$ i
s the irreducible quadratic
$$m_\lambda(x) = (x-\lambda)(x-\lambda^p) = x^2 - (\lambda+\lambda^p)x + \lambda
^{p+1} = x^2 - tx + 1,$$
with $t = \lambda+\lambda^p\in\mathbb{F}_p$ and constant term $1$. The companion
 matrix
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & t\end{pmatrix}$$
lies in $SL_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$, has characteristic polynomial $x^2 - tx + 1$ (irre
ducible, no roots in $\mathbb{F}_p$), and has order $\operatorname{ord}(\lambda)
 = p+1$.

**Conclusion.** The maximum order of an element in $SL_2(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
$ whose characteristic polynomial has no roots in $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ is

$$\boxed{p+1}.$$
