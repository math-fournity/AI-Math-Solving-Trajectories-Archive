Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every $2n \times 2n$ symmet
ric matrix with zero diagonal over a field $F$ of characteristic 2 can be writte
n as $X^t \Omega X$.

**Key observation:** In characteristic 2, a symmetric matrix with zero diagonal
is exactly an *alternating* matrix (one where $v^t A v = 0$ for all $v$), since
$v^t A v = \sum_{i,j} A_{ij} v_i v_j = \sum_i A_{ii} v_i^2 + \sum_{i<j} (A_{ij}+
A_{ji}) v_i v_j = 0 + \sum_{i<j} 2A_{ij} v_i v_j = 0$.

Here is the proof:

---

**Theorem.** Let $F$ be a field of characteristic 2, and let $A$ be a $2n \times
 2n$ symmetric matrix with zero diagonal over $F$. Then there exists a $2n \time
s 2n$ matrix $X$ over $F$ such that $A = X^t \Omega X$, where $\Omega = \begin{b
matrix} 0 & I_n \\ I_n & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.

**Proof.**

**Step 1: $X^t \Omega X$ is always alternating.** Since $\Omega^t = \Omega$ (sym
metric in char 2) and $\Omega$ has zero diagonal, for any $X$, the matrix $X^t \
Omega X$ is symmetric with zero diagonal: its $(i,i)$-entry is $\sum_{a=1}^{n}(X
_{ai}X_{n+a,i} + X_{n+a,i}X_{ai}) = 2\sum_a X_{ai}X_{n+a,i} = 0$ in characterist
ic 2. So the form $X^t \Omega X$ is always alternating — the constraint is neces
sary.

**Step 2: Every alternating form admits a symplectic basis (over any field, incl
uding char 2).** This is the classical symplectic basis theorem. The proof proce
eds by induction on $\dim V$: if $B \neq 0$, pick $u, v$ with $B(u,v) \neq 0$; r
escale so $B(u,v)=1$. Then $\operatorname{span}(u,v) \cap \operatorname{span}(u,
v)^\perp = \{0\}$: if $w = au+bv$ lies in both, then $B(w,u) = b\,B(v,u) = 0$ an
d $B(w,v) = a\,B(u,v) = 0$ give $a=b=0$ (using $B(u,v)\neq 0$). So $V = \operato
rname{span}(u,v) \oplus \operatorname{span}(u,v)^\perp$ and we restrict $B$ to t
he complement. This argument uses only $B(v,v)=0$ (alternating) and $B(u,v)\neq
0$, so it is valid in characteristic 2.

Consequently, if $A$ has rank $2r$, there exists an invertible $2n \times 2n$ ma
trix $P$ such that
$$A = P^t \begin{bmatrix} \Omega_r & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} P,$$
where $\Omega_r = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & I_r \\ I_r & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ is the $2r \
times 2r$ hyperbolic block (using $-1=1$ in char 2).

**Step 3: The canonical form $\begin{bmatrix} \Omega_r & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix
}$ is $X_0^t \Omega X_0$.** Define the $2n \times 2n$ matrix $X_0$ whose columns
 are:
$$e_1,\; e_{n+1},\; e_2,\; e_{n+2},\; \ldots,\; e_r,\; e_{n+r},\; \underbrace{0,
\ldots,0}_{2n-2r}.$$

Then $(X_0^t \Omega X_0)_{ij} = \Omega(\text{col}_i(X_0),\, \text{col}_j(X_0))$.
 For $i=2k{-}1, j=2\ell$ (columns $e_k, e_{n+\ell}$), this equals $\Omega_{k,n+\
ell} = \delta_{k\ell}$; for $i=2k, j=2\ell{-}1$, it equals $\delta_{k\ell}$; all
 other pairs give $0$. This is exactly $\Omega_r$ in the top-left $2r \times 2r$
 block and $0$ elsewhere:
$$X_0^t \Omega X_0 = \begin{bmatrix} \Omega_r & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}.$$

**Step 4: Combine.** Setting $X = X_0 P$ (a $2n \times 2n$ matrix over $F$), we
obtain:
$$X^t \Omega X = P^t X_0^t \Omega X_0 P = P^t \begin{bmatrix} \Omega_r & 0 \\ 0
& 0 \end{bmatrix} P = A.$$

This works for any rank $2r$ with $0 \le r \le n$ (the case $r=0$ gives $X=0$ an
d $A=0$). Since every alternating matrix has even rank, every $2n \times 2n$ sym
metric zero-diagonal matrix is covered. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** The matrix $X$ is generally *not* invertible when $A$ is degenerate
(rank $< 2n$); this is essential, since $X^t \Omega X$ with invertible $X$ would
 be non-degenerate. The freedom to use singular $X$ is what makes the degenerate
 case work.
