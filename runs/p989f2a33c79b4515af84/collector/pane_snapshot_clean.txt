Looking at this problem, I need to find the side length x of three congruent equ
ilateral triangles arranged inside a larger equilateral triangle of side 6, with
 a central triangle of side 1 between them.

## Setup

Place the large equilateral triangle with vertices A=(0,0), B=(6,0), C=(3, 3√3).
 Its centroid is G=(3, √3).

By the 3-fold symmetry of the problem, the three congruent triangles are arrange
d symmetrically, each near one side of the big triangle. They point **downward**
 (inverted relative to the big triangle), with each apex on a side of the big tr
iangle at the side's midpoint.

**Bottom triangle** (apex on AB): apex at (3, 0), top vertices at (3−x/2, x√3/2)
 and (3+x/2, x√3/2).

The other two triangles are obtained by 120° and 240° rotations about G.

## Vertex-on-another-triangle condition

The bottom triangle's top-right vertex P = (3+x/2, x√3/2) must lie on another sm
all triangle. By symmetry, it lies on a side of the BC triangle.

The BC triangle (120° rotation) has apex (9/2, 3√3/2) and a side from this apex
to its top-left vertex ((9−x)/2, √3(3−x)/2). Parametrizing this side:

$$Q(t) = \left(\tfrac{9}{2}, \tfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) + t\left(-\tfrac{x}{2},
\, -\tfrac{x\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) = \left(\tfrac{9-tx}{2},\, \tfrac{\sqrt{3}(3-tx)
}{2}\right)$$

Setting Q(t) = P gives both coordinates yielding t = (3−x)/x, which lies in [0,1
] when 3/2 ≤ x ≤ 3. ✓

## The central triangle

The central triangle is the gap bounded by the **top sides** (sides opposite the
 apexes) of the three small triangles. These three top sides are:

- **Bottom**: horizontal segment at height x√3/2, from (3−x/2, x√3/2) to (3+x/2,
 x√3/2).
- **BC triangle**: from ((9−x)/2, √3(3−x)/2) to (9/2−x, 3√3/2), with slope −√3.
- **CA triangle**: from (3/2+x, 3√3/2) to ((3+x)/2, √3(3−x)/2), with slope +√3.

**Finding the vertices of the central triangle** (intersections of these three l
ines):

- Bottom ∩ CA line: y = x√3/2 and y = √3·X − x√3 → X = 3x/2. Vertex: **(3x/2, x√
3/2)**.
- Bottom ∩ BC line: y = x√3/2 and y = √3(6−x) − √3·X → X = 6−3x/2. Vertex: **(6−
3x/2, x√3/2)**.
- BC ∩ CA line: √3(6−x) − √3·X = √3·X − x√3 → X = 3, y = √3(3−x). Vertex: **(3,
√3(3−x))**.

**Bottom side length**: (6−3x/2) − 3x/2 = **6 − 3x**.

**Left side length**: from (3x/2, x√3/2) to (3, √3(3−x)):
$$\sqrt{\left(\tfrac{3(2-x)}{2}\right)^2 + \left(\tfrac{3\sqrt{3}(2-x)}{2}\right
)^2} = \sqrt{\tfrac{9(2-x)^2 + 27(2-x)^2}{4}} = \tfrac{6(2-x)}{2} = 6-3x \checkm
ark$$

So the central triangle is equilateral with side **6 − 3x**.

## Solving

Setting the central triangle's side to 1:

$$6 - 3x = 1 \implies x = \frac{5}{3}$$

- **Valid range**: 3/2 ≤ 5/3 ≤ 3. ✓
