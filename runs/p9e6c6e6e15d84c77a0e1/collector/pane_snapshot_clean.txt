## Proof

**Setup.** Place the origin $O$ at the center. Let the sensors have polar coordi
nates
$$A = (1, \alpha),\quad B = (\sqrt{2}, \beta),\quad C = (\sqrt{5}, \gamma).$$

The signed area of $\triangle ABC$ is given by the standard polar formula:
$$\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2}\big|r_1 r_2\sin(\beta-\alpha) + r_2 r_3\sin(\gamma-
\beta) + r_3 r_1\sin(\alpha-\gamma)\big|.$$

Substituting $r_1=1,\, r_2=\sqrt{2},\, r_3=\sqrt{5}$, and setting $u=\beta-\alph
a$, $v=\gamma-\beta$ (so $\alpha-\gamma = -(u+v)$):

$$f(u,v) = \sqrt{2}\sin u + \sqrt{10}\sin v - \sqrt{5}\sin(u+v), \qquad \text{Ar
ea} = \tfrac{1}{2}|f|.$$

**Critical points.** Setting $\partial f/\partial u = \partial f/\partial v = 0$
:

$$\sqrt{2}\cos u = \sqrt{5}\cos(u+v), \qquad \sqrt{10}\cos v = \sqrt{5}\cos(u+v)
.$$

Dividing gives $\cos u = \sqrt{5}\cos v$, and substituting back gives $\cos(u+v)
 = \sqrt{2}\cos v$. Let $c = \cos v$. Then $\cos u = \sqrt{5}\,c$ and $\cos(u+v)
 = \sqrt{2}\,c$.

Expanding $\cos(u+v) = \cos u\cos v - \sin u\sin v$:

$$\sqrt{2}\,c = \sqrt{5}\,c^2 - \sin u\sin v \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \sin u\sin
v = \sqrt{5}\,c^2 - \sqrt{2}\,c.$$

Squaring and using $\sin^2 u = 1-5c^2$, $\sin^2 v = 1-c^2$:

$$(1-5c^2)(1-c^2) = (\sqrt{5}\,c^2 - \sqrt{2}\,c)^2.$$

Expanding both sides:

$$1 - 6c^2 + 5c^4 = 5c^4 - 2\sqrt{10}\,c^3 + 2c^2,$$

which simplifies to the cubic:

$$2\sqrt{10}\,c^3 - 8c^2 + 1 = 0.$$

**Solving the cubic.** Testing $c = -1/\sqrt{10}$:

$$2\sqrt{10}\cdot\!\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{10}}\right)^{\!3} - 8\cdot\frac{1}{10}
+ 1 = -\frac{2}{10} - \frac{8}{10} + 1 = 0.\;\checkmark$$

The cubic has three real roots ($c \approx -0.316,\; 0.437,\; 1.144$). Feasibili
ty requires $|\sqrt{5}\,c|\le 1$, i.e., $|c|\le 1/\sqrt{5}\approx 0.447$, ruling
 out $c\approx 1.144$.

**Evaluating at $c = -1/\sqrt{10}$.** Then:

$$\cos v = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{10}},\quad \cos u = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\quad \cos(u+
v) = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}},$$

$$\sin v = \frac{3}{\sqrt{10}},\quad \sin u = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\quad \sin(u+v)
 = -\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}.$$

(The signs are determined by $\sin u\sin v = \sqrt{5}\,c^2 - \sqrt{2}\,c = \frac
{3\sqrt{5}}{10} > 0$, so both sines share sign; choosing both positive gives the
 maximum.)

Therefore:

$$f = \sqrt{2}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} + \sqrt{10}\cdot\frac{3}{\sqrt{10}} - \sqr
t{5}\cdot\!\left(-\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\right) = 1 + 3 + 2 = 6.$$

The other feasible critical point ($c\approx 0.437$) yields $|f|\approx 0.79$, w
hich is smaller. Since $f$ is continuous on the compact torus $(u,v)\in\mathbb{T
}^2$, the global maximum of $|f|$ is $6$.

**Geometric verification.** Set $\alpha=0$. Then $u = 3\pi/4$ and $u+v$ is in th
e third quadrant with $\cos(u+v)=-1/\sqrt{5}$, $\sin(u+v)=-2/\sqrt{5}$, giving:

$$A=(1,0),\quad B=\sqrt{2}\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right
)=(-1,1),\quad C=\sqrt{5}\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}},-\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\right
)=(-1,-2).$$

$$\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2}\big|\det[(-2,1),\,(-2,-2)]\big| = \tfrac{1}{2}|4+2|
 = 3.\;\checkmark$$

**Conclusion.** The greatest possible area of triangle $ABC$ is

$$\boxed{3}.$$
