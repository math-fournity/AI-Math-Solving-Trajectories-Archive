**Proof.**

$$\int_{0}^{\pi} g(x)\,H(\cos x)\,dx \;=\; \int_{-1}^{1} \frac{g(\arccos t)}{\sq

$$f(t) \;=\; \frac{g(\arccos t)}{\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}, \qquad t\in(-1,1).$$

Because $g$ is continuous on $[0,\pi]$ and $\arccos$ is continuous, $g(\arccos t

{-1/2}dt=\pi<\infty$). Thus $f\in L^{1}(-1,1)$, and the hypothesis reads

$$\int_{-1}^{1} f(t)\,H(t)\,dt = 0 \qquad\text{for every polynomial } H.$$

By the Weierstrass approximation theorem, polynomials are dense in $C[-1,1]$ und

y}\int_{-1}^{1}|f|\,dt \;\longrightarrow\; 0.$$

Hence $\int_{-1}^{1} f(t)\,\varphi(t)\,dt = 0$ for every $\varphi\in C[-1,1]$.

By the fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations (a continuous linear func

The map $\arccos:(-1,1)\to(0,\pi)$ is a smooth diffeomorphism with nowhere-vanis
