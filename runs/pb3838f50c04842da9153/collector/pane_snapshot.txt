## Proof

We decompose $X = V(t_1^2 - t_2 t_3,\; t_1 t_3 - t_1) \subset \mathbb{A}_k^3$.

Let $I = (f_1, f_2)$ where $f_1 = t_1^2 - t_2 t_3$ and $f_2 = t_1(t_3 - 1)$. A p
oint $p \in X$ satisfies $f_2(p) = 0$, i.e. $t_1(p)\,(t_3(p)-1) = 0$. Since $k$
is a field (hence an integral domain), this forces $t_1 = 0$ or $t_3 = 1$. There
fore
$$X = \bigl(X \cap V(t_1)\bigr) \;\cup\; \bigl(X \cap V(t_3-1)\bigr).$$

**First piece:** $X \cap V(t_1) = V(t_1,\; t_1^2 - t_2 t_3,\; t_1(t_3-1)) = V(t_
1,\; t_2 t_3)$. Over a field, $V(t_2 t_3) = V(t_2) \cup V(t_3)$, so
$$X \cap V(t_1) = V(t_1, t_2) \;\cup\; V(t_1, t_3).$$

**Second piece:** $X \cap V(t_3-1) = V(t_3-1,\; t_1^2 - t_2 t_3,\; t_1(t_3-1)) =
 V(t_3-1,\; t_1^2 - t_2) = V(t_3-1,\; t_2 - t_1^2)$.

Hence
$$X = V(t_1, t_2) \;\cup\; V(t_1, t_3) \;\cup\; V(t_3-1,\; t_2 - t_1^2).$$

**Irreducibility.** Each piece is defined by a prime ideal, hence is irreducible
:

- $V(t_1, t_2)$: coordinate ring $k[t_3]$, a domain. This is the $t_3$-axis.
- $V(t_1, t_3)$: coordinate ring $k[t_2]$, a domain. This is the $t_2$-axis.
- $V(t_3-1,\, t_2-t_1^2)$: coordinate ring $k[t_1]$, a domain. This is the parab
ola $t_2 = t_1^2$ in the plane $t_3 = 1$.

**Maximality (no containment among them).** The three components are pairwise in
comparable:

- $V(t_1, t_2)$ contains points $(0,0,t_3)$ with $t_3$ free; not contained in $V
(t_3-1, t_2-t_1^2)$ (which forces $t_3=1$), nor in $V(t_1,t_3)$ (which forces $t
_3=0$).
- $V(t_1, t_3)$ contains points $(0,t_2,0)$ with $t_2$ free; not contained in $V
(t_3-1,\dots)$ (forces $t_3=1$), nor in $V(t_1,t_2)$ (forces $t_2=0$).
- $V(t_3-1, t_2-t_1^2)$ contains points $(t_1, t_1^2, 1)$ with $t_1$ free; not c
ontained in $V(t_1,t_2)$ (forces $t_1=0$), nor in $V(t_1,t_3)$ (forces $t_1=0$).

Thus none is contained in another, so each is maximal among irreducible subsets
of $X$.

**Conclusion.** The irreducible components of $X$ are
$$\boxed{\,V(t_1, t_2),\quad V(t_1, t_3),\quad V(t_3-1,\; t_2 - t_1^2)\,}$$
i.e. the $t_3$-axis, the $t_2$-axis, and the parabola $\{(t_1, t_1^2, 1) : t_1 \
in k\}$.
