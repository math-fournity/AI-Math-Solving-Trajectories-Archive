## Proof

**Answer: Yes, the statement is true.**

Let $Z = \phi^{-1}(0)$. We prove that $\lambda(\phi^{-1}(-\delta,\delta)) \to 0$
 as $\delta \to 0^+$, which immediately yields the desired claim.

### Step 1. $Z$ has Lebesgue measure zero.

Since $\phi \in C^1(\overline{\Omega})$ and $\nabla\phi(x)\neq 0$ for every $x\i
n Z$, the Implicit Function Theorem applies at each point of $Z$: for every $x_0
\in Z$ there exist a neighborhood $U_{x_0}\subset\mathbb{R}^N$, an index $i\in\{
1,\dots,N\}$ (with $\partial_i\phi(x_0)\neq 0$), and a $C^1$ function $g$ of the
 remaining $N-1$ variables such that
$$
Z\cap U_{x_0} = \bigl\{(y',g(y')) : y'\in V_{x_0}\bigr\},
$$
i.e., $Z\cap U_{x_0}$ is the graph of a $C^1$ (hence continuous) function over a
 bounded open set $V_{x_0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}$.

A graph of a continuous function over a bounded subset of $\mathbb{R}^{N-1}$ has
 $N$-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero (cover the domain by countably many cubes
 and use uniform continuity to bound the graph's thickness). Hence $\lambda(Z\ca
p U_{x_0})=0$.

Since $Z$ is a closed subset of the compact set $\overline{\Omega}$, it is compa
ct. The open cover $\{U_{x_0}\}_{x_0\in Z}$ admits a finite subcover $U_{x_1},\d
ots,U_{x_m}$. Therefore
$$
\lambda(Z)\le \sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda(Z\cap U_{x_j})=0.
$$

### Step 2. Continuity of measure from above.

For $\delta>0$ define $E_\delta = \phi^{-1}((-\delta,\delta))$. Each $E_\delta$
is open (hence Lebesgue measurable) and bounded (contained in $\overline{\Omega}
$), so $\lambda(E_\delta)\le\lambda(\Omega)<\infty$.

The family $\{E_\delta\}_{\delta>0}$ is monotone decreasing in $\delta$, and
$$
\bigcap_{\delta>0} E_\delta = \{x\in\Omega : |\phi(x)|<\delta\ \text{for all }\d
elta>0\} = \phi^{-1}(0)=Z.
$$

By the continuity of Lebesgue measure from above (valid since $\lambda(E_\delta)
<\infty$),
$$
\lim_{\delta\to 0^+}\lambda(E_\delta)=\lambda(Z)=0.
$$

### Step 3. Conclusion.

Given $\varepsilon>0$, since $\lambda(E_\delta)\to 0$, there exists $\delta=\del
ta(\varepsilon)>0$ such that
$$
\lambda\bigl(\phi^{-1}(-\delta,\delta)\bigr)=\lambda(E_\delta)<\varepsilon.
$$

 connected are not needed for the conclusion; the essential ingredients are: $\p
