## Solution

We compute the residue of $f(z) = \tan^2 z$ at the points $z_k = \frac{\pi}{2} +
 \pi k$, $k \in \mathbb{Z}$.

**Step 1: Identify the nature of the singularity.**

The function $\tan z = \frac{\sin z}{\cos z}$ has simple poles where $\cos z = 0
$, i.e. at $z_k = \frac{\pi}{2}+\pi k$. Since $\cos z$ has simple zeros at these
 points (because $-\sin z_k = -(-1)^k \neq 0$), $\tan z$ has **simple poles** at
 $z_k$, and therefore $\tan^2 z$ has **poles of order 2** at $z_k$.

**Step 2: Laurent expansion near $z_k$.**

Set $w = z - z_k$. Using the $\pi$-periodicity of $\tan$:

$$\tan(z_k + w) = \tan\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}+w\right) = -\cot w.$$

The Laurent series of $\cot w$ about $w=0$ is

$$\cot w = \frac{1}{w} - \frac{w}{3} - \frac{w^3}{45} - \cdots$$

Hence

$$\tan z = -\cot w = -\frac{1}{w} + \frac{w}{3} + \frac{w^3}{45} + \cdots$$

Squaring:

$$\tan^2 z = \cot^2 w = \left(\frac{1}{w} - \frac{w}{3} - \cdots\right)^2 = \fra
c{1}{w^2} - \frac{2}{3} + \frac{w^2}{9} + \cdots$$

**Step 3: Read off the residue.**

The residue is the coefficient of $\frac{1}{w} = \frac{1}{z-z_k}$ in the Laurent
 expansion. From the expansion above, the $\frac{1}{w}$ term is **absent** (the
series jumps from $\frac{1}{w^2}$ directly to the constant term $-\frac{2}{3}$).

**Step 4: Verification via the order-2 pole formula.**

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_k} \tan^2 z = \lim_{z\to z_k}\frac{d}{dz}\Big[(z-z_k)^
2\tan^2 z\Big] = \lim_{w\to 0}\frac{d}{dw}\Big[w^2\cot^2 w\Big].$$

Since $w^2\cot^2 w = 1 - \frac{2}{3}w^2 + \cdots$, its derivative is $-\frac{4}{
3}w + \cdots$, which tends to $0$ as $w\to 0$.

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_k}\tan^2 z = 0, \qquad z_k = \tfrac{\pi}{2}+\pi
 k,\ k\in\mathbb{Z}.}$$
