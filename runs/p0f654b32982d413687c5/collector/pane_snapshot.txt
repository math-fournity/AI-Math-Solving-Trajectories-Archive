## Proof

Factor the cubic $n^3 - 6n^2 + 23n - 18$. Testing $n=1$: $1 - 6 + 23 - 18 = 0$,
so $(n-1)$ is a factor:

$$n^3 - 6n^2 + 23n - 18 = (n-1)(n^2 - 5n + 18).$$

Thus
$$f(n) = \frac{n(n-1)(n^2-5n+18)}{24} + 1.$$

Note that $n^2 - 5n + 18 = (n-2)(n-3) + 12$, so

$$f(n) = \frac{n(n-1)\big[(n-2)(n-3)+12\big]}{24} + 1 = \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)}{
24} + \frac{n(n-1)}{2} + 1 = \binom{n}{4} + \binom{n}{2} + 1.$$

**Step 2: Test small values $n \geq 6$.**

| $n$ | $\binom{n}{4}$ | $\binom{n}{2}$ | $f(n)$ |
|-----|-------|-------|--------|
| 6 | 15 | 15 | 31 |
| 7 | 35 | 21 | 57 |
| 8 | 70 | 28 | 99 |
| 9 | 126 | 36 | 163 |
| 10 | 210 | 45 | **256** |

**Step 3: Verify $f(10) = 256 = 2^8$.**

Directly from the original formula:
$$f(10) = \frac{1}{24}\cdot 10 \cdot (1000 - 600 + 230 - 18) + 1 = \frac{10 \cdo

Since $10 \geq 6$ and $f(10) = 2^8$ is a power of 2, the statement **"$f(n)$ is
never a power of 2 for $n \geq 6$" is false**.

No, it is **not** true that $f(n)$ is never a power of 2 for $n \geq 6$. A count
