**Proof.** Fix $r > 0$ and consider the closed disk $\overline{D} = \{z \in \mat
hbb{C} : |z| \le r\}$, which is compact.

The exponential $e^z = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{z^k}{k!}$ is entire and has **n
o zeros** anywhere, since $e^z \cdot e^{-z} = 1$ for all $z$. In particular $e^z
 \ne 0$ on $\overline{D}$.

Because $|e^z|$ is continuous and strictly positive on the compact set $\overlin
e{D}$, it attains a positive minimum:
$$m := \min_{|z| \le r} |e^z| > 0.$$

The partial sums
$$P_n(z) = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \frac{z^k}{k!}$$
converge to $e^z$ **uniformly on compact subsets** of $\mathbb{C}$ (this is the
standard uniform convergence of the exponential series on bounded sets). Hence o
n $\overline{D}$, $P_n \to e^z$ uniformly, and there exists an integer $n_0$ suc
h that for all $n \ge n_0$ and all $z \in \overline{D}$,
$$|P_n(z) - e^z| < \frac{m}{2}.$$

By the reverse triangle inequality, for every $n \ge n_0$ and every $z \in \over
line{D}$,
$$|P_n(z)| \ge |e^z| - |P_n(z) - e^z| > m - \frac{m}{2} = \frac{m}{2} > 0.$$

Thus $P_n(z) \ne 0$ for all $z$ with $|z| \le r$ and all $n \ge n_0$; equivalent
ly, every zero of $P_n$ lies strictly outside the disk $|z| \le r$.

Since $r > 0$ was arbitrary, the claim follows. $\blacksquare$
