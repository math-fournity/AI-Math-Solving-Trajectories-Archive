## Proof

We disprove WOWII Conjecture 36 by exhibiting an explicit counterexample: the pa
th graph $P_7$ on 7 vertices.

**Construction.** Let $G = P_7$ with vertex set $V = \{0,1,2,3,4,5,6\}$ and edge
 set $E = \{\{i,i+1\} : 0 \le i \le 5\}$.

**Claim 1: $G$ is connected.** Every vertex $i$ is connected to vertex $0$ via t
he path $i \to i{-}1 \to \cdots \to 1 \to 0$.

**Claim 2: $\operatorname{diam}(G) = 6$.** The graph distance between vertices $
i$ and $j$ is $|i-j|$ (the unique shortest path along the line). The maximum is
$|0-6|=6$.

**Claim 3: $\operatorname{rad}(G) = 3$.** The eccentricity of vertex $i$ is $\ma
x(i,\, 6-i)$. This is minimized at $i=3$, giving $\operatorname{ecc}(3)=\max(3,3
)=3$. Hence $\operatorname{rad}(G)=3$.

**Claim 4: $\operatorname{dp}(G) = 1$.** A diametrical pair is a pair $\{u,v\}$
with $d(u,v)=\operatorname{diam}(G)=6$. The only such pair is $\{0,6\}$. Thus $\
operatorname{dp}(G)=1 > 0$.

**Claim 5: $\operatorname{path}(G) = \lfloor \overline{d}(G) \rfloor = 2$, where
 $\overline{d}(G)$ is the average distance.** We count pairs at each distance:

| Distance $d$ | Number of pairs | Contribution $d \times \#$ |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 1 | 6 | 6 |
| 2 | 5 | 10 |
| 3 | 4 | 12 |
| 4 | 3 | 12 |
| 5 | 2 | 10 |
| 6 | 1 | 6 |

Total pairs: $6+5+4+3+2+1 = 21 = \binom{7}{2}$. ✓

Sum of all distances: $6+10+12+12+10+6 = 56$.

Average distance: $\overline{d}(G) = \dfrac{56}{21} = \dfrac{8}{3} \approx 2.667
$.

Therefore $\operatorname{path}(G) = \left\lfloor \dfrac{8}{3} \right\rfloor = 2$
.

**Verification of the counterexample.** The conjecture asserts:
$$\operatorname{path}(G) \ge \frac{2 \cdot \operatorname{rad}(G)}{\operatorname{
dp}(G)}.$$

For $G = P_7$:
$$\frac{2 \cdot \operatorname{rad}(G)}{\operatorname{dp}(G)} = \frac{2 \times 3}
{1} = 6, \qquad \operatorname{path}(G) = 2.$$

The conjecture would require $2 \ge 6$, which is absurd.

**Conclusion.** The universal statement
$$\forall\, G \text{ (connected, dp}>0\text{)}: \quad \frac{2\,\operatorname{rad
}(G)}{\operatorname{dp}(G)} \le \operatorname{path}(G)$$
is **false**, since $P_7$ is a connected graph with $\operatorname{dp}(P_7)=1>0$
 and $\frac{2\cdot 3}{1} = 6 > 2 = \operatorname{path}(P_7)$.

Hence $\operatorname{answer}(\mathrm{False})$ holds: the conjecture is disproved
. $\blacksquare$
