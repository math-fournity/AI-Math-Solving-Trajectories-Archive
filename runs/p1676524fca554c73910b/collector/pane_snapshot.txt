## Theorem

A loxodromic linear fractional transformation (Möbius transformation) cannot map
 a disk on the Riemann sphere to itself.

## Proof

Suppose for contradiction that $T$ is a loxodromic Möbius transformation with $T
(D)=D$ for some disk $D$ on the Riemann sphere. By conjugating $T$ — which prese
rves the loxodromic classification, since the multiplier $\lambda$ is a conjugac
y invariant of the normal form $w\mapsto\lambda w$ — we may assume $D$ is the un
it disk $\Delta=\{z:|z|<1\}$.

Since $T(\Delta)=\Delta$, the map $T$ is an automorphism of $\Delta$, hence has
the form
$$T(z)=e^{i\theta}\frac{z-a}{1-\bar a\,z},\qquad |a|<1,\ \theta\in\mathbb R.$$
In particular $T$ preserves the unit circle $\partial\Delta$.

---

**Key property (inversion symmetry).** For every $z$,
$$T(1/\bar z)=1\big/\overline{T(z)}.$$

*Verification:* $\displaystyle T(1/\bar z)=e^{i\theta}\frac{1/\bar z-a}{1-\bar a
/\bar z}=e^{i\theta}\frac{1-a\bar z}{\bar z-\bar a}$, and $\displaystyle \frac1{
\overline{T(z)}}=\frac1{e^{-i\theta}(\bar z-\bar a)/(1-a\bar z)}=e^{i\theta}\fra
c{1-a\bar z}{\bar z-\bar a}$. $\checkmark$

**Consequence.** If $z_0$ is a fixed point of $T$, then so is $1/\bar z_0$:
$$T(1/\bar z_0)=1\big/\overline{T(z_0)}=1/\bar z_0.$$

---

**Fixed-point analysis.** Assume $T\ne\mathrm{id}$.

**Case 1 — One fixed point (parabolic).** Then $T$ is parabolic, not loxodromic.
 Contradiction.

**Case 2 — Two distinct fixed points $z_1,z_2$.** The set $\{z_1,z_2\}$ is invar
iant under $z\mapsto 1/\bar z$. Note both fixed points cannot lie strictly insid
e $\Delta$: if $z_1$ is inside then $1/\bar z_1$ is an outside fixed point, and
if $z_2\neq 1/\bar z_1$ were also inside then $1/\bar z_2$ would be a *fourth* f
ixed point — impossible. Likewise both cannot be outside. Hence exactly two subc
ases.

*Subcase 2a — Both fixed points on the unit circle* ($|z_1|=|z_2|=1$, so $1/\bar
 z_i=z_i$).

Write $z=e^{i\alpha},\ z_1=e^{i\beta},\ z_2=e^{i\gamma}$. Then
$$\frac{z-z_1}{z-z_2}=e^{i(\beta-\gamma)/2}\cdot\frac{\sin\!\bigl(\tfrac{\alpha-
\beta}{2}\bigr)}{\sin\!\bigl(\tfrac{\alpha-\gamma}{2}\bigr)},$$
which lies on the line through the origin of argument $(\beta-\gamma)/2$. Since
$T(z)$ is also on the unit circle, the ratio $(T(z)-z_1)/(T(z)-z_2)$ lies on the
 *same* line. The defining relation
$$\frac{T(z)-z_1}{T(z)-z_2}=\lambda\,\frac{z-z_1}{z-z_2}$$
then forces $\lambda\in\mathbb R$.

To determine the sign: conjugate by $\psi(z)=(z-z_1)/(z-z_2)$, sending $z_1\maps
to0$, $z_2\mapsto\infty$. The unit circle becomes a line through the origin and
$\Delta$ becomes one of the two half-planes it bounds; $T$ becomes $w\mapsto\lam
bda w$. If $\lambda>0$ each half-plane is preserved; if $\lambda<0$ the two half
-planes are swapped (since $w\mapsto -w$ reflects through the origin). Because $
T$ preserves $\Delta$, we must have $\lambda>0$, and $\lambda\neq1$ since $T\neq
\mathrm{id}$. Thus
$$\lambda\in\mathbb R^+\setminus\{1\},$$
so $T$ is **hyperbolic**, not loxodromic. Contradiction.

*Subcase 2b — One fixed point inside, one outside.* Say $|z_1|<1$; then $z_2=1/\
bar z_1$ with $|z_2|>1$. Conjugate by a disk automorphism $\phi$ with $\phi(z_1)
=0$. Then $S=\phi\circ T\circ\phi^{-1}$ preserves $\Delta$ and fixes $0$, so $S(
w)=e^{i\varphi}w$ for some $\varphi\in\mathbb R$. The multiplier of $S$ is $e^{i
\varphi}$, with $|e^{i\varphi}|=1$. Since the multiplier is a conjugacy invarian
t, the multiplier $\lambda$ of $T$ satisfies $|\lambda|=1$. Thus $T$ is **ellipt
ic**, not loxodromic. Contradiction.

---

In every possible case $T$ is parabolic, hyperbolic, or elliptic — never loxodro
mic. This contradicts the assumption that $T$ is loxodromic. $\blacksquare$

**Conclusion:** No. A linear fractional transformation that maps a disk on the R
iemann sphere to itself cannot be loxodromic. The automorphisms of a disk are ex
clusively elliptic, hyperbolic, or parabolic; the loxodromic type — which requir
es a non-trivial rotational component ($\arg\lambda\neq0,\pi$ with $|\lambda|\ne
q1$, or equivalently a non-real-negative multiplier off the unit circle) — is in
compatible with preserving a disk, because the inversion symmetry $z\leftrightar
row 1/\bar z$ forces the fixed points onto the boundary (giving real-positive $\
lambda$) or into an interior–exterior pair (giving $|\lambda|=1$).
