## Proof

Since $f$ is holomorphic on the unit disk $\mathbb{D}$, continuous on $\overline
{\mathbb{D}}$, and $|f(z)|=1$ for all $|z|=1$, the **maximum modulus principle**
 gives
$$|f(z)| \le 1 \quad \text{for all } z \in \mathbb{D}.$$

By hypothesis, $f$ has **no zeros** in $\mathbb{D}$. Therefore $g(z) := 1/f(z)$
is also holomorphic on $\mathbb{D}$ and continuous on $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$. O
n the boundary $|z|=1$ we have $|g(z)| = 1/|f(z)| = 1$, so applying the maximum
modulus principle to $g$ yields
$$|g(z)| \le 1 \quad \text{for all } z \in \mathbb{D},$$
that is,
$$|f(z)| \ge 1 \quad \text{for all } z \in \mathbb{D}.$$

Combining the two inequalities,
$$|f(z)| = 1 \quad \text{for all } z \in \mathbb{D}.$$

A holomorphic function whose modulus is constant on a domain must itself be cons
tant (this follows, for instance, from the open mapping theorem: a nonconstant h
olomorphic function maps open sets to open sets, which cannot be contained in th
e unit circle). Hence $f$ is constant on $\mathbb{D}$, and by continuity, consta
nt on $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$.

Using the given value $f(-1)=1$, we conclude
$$f(z) \equiv 1.$$
