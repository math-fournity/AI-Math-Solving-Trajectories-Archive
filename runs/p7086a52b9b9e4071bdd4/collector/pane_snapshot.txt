## Proof

Let $A, B, C$ denote the angles of $\triangle ABC$ at the respective vertices. S
ince $\triangle ABC$ is non-isosceles, $A \neq B$; assume WLOG that $B > A$ (the
 case $A > B$ follows by symmetry). Set $u = B - A > 0$ and $\varphi = u/2$.

**Step 1: Configuration of $D$.** The tangent at $C$ to $\omega$ meets line $AB$
 at $D$, which lies beyond $B$. By the tangent-chord angle theorem:
$$\angle DCB = A, \qquad \angle DCA = B.$$
Since $D$ is beyond $B$, $\angle DBC = \pi - B$, so in $\triangle DCB$:
$$\angle CDB = \pi - A - (\pi - B) = B - A = u.$$

**Step 2: Coordinates.** Place $D$ at the origin with the bisector of $\angle CD
B$ as the positive $x$-axis. Then ray $DC$ makes angle $+\varphi$ and ray $DB$ (
line $AB$) makes angle $-\varphi$ with the $x$-axis. Set $DC = 1$. Then:
$$C = (\cos\varphi,\, \sin\varphi), \quad B = DB(\cos\varphi,\, -\sin\varphi), \
quad A = DA(\cos\varphi,\, -\sin\varphi).$$
The line $KL$ is the $x$-axis. By the law of sines in $\triangle DCB$ and $\tria
ngle DCA$:
$$DB = \frac{\sin A}{\sin B}, \qquad DA = \frac{\sin B}{\sin A}.$$
Denote $s_A = \sin A$, $s_B = \sin B$, $S = s_A^2 + s_B^2$.

**Step 3: Computing $AK/BL$.** By the angle bisector theorem in $\triangle DCA$
(bisector of $\angle CDA = \angle CDB$ meets $CA$ at $K$):
$$AK = \frac{AC \cdot DA}{DA + DC}.$$
In $\triangle DCB$ (bisector meets $CB$ at $L$):
$$BL = \frac{BC \cdot DB}{DB + DC}.$$
By the power of a point, $DA \cdot DB = DC^2 = 1$. Then:
$$\frac{DB + DC}{DA + DC} = \frac{s_A/s_B + 1}{s_B/s_A + 1} = \frac{s_A}{s_B}, \
qquad \frac{DA}{DB} = \frac{s_B^2}{s_A^2}.$$
By the law of sines in $\triangle ABC$, $AC/BC = s_B/s_A$. Therefore:
$$\frac{AK}{BL} = \frac{AC}{BC} \cdot \frac{DA}{DB} \cdot \frac{DB+DC}{DA+DC} =
\frac{s_B}{s_A} \cdot \frac{s_B^2}{s_A^2} \cdot \frac{s_A}{s_B} = \frac{s_B^2}{s
_A^2} = \frac{DA}{DB}.$$

**Step 4: Locating $M$.** Since $M$ is on segment $AB$ at distance $DM$ from $D$
, with $AM = DA - DM$ and $BM = DM - DB$:
$$\frac{DA - DM}{DM - DB} = \frac{DA}{DB} \implies DM = \frac{2 \cdot DA \cdot D
B}{DA + DB} = \frac{2\, s_A\, s_B}{S}.$$
So $M = \frac{2s_A s_B}{S}(\cos\varphi,\, -\sin\varphi)$.

**Step 5: Locating $Q$.** The point $Q$ is the foot of the perpendicular from $M
$ to line $DC$ (unit direction $\hat{u} = (\cos\varphi, \sin\varphi)$):
$$DQ = M \cdot \hat{u} = DM(\cos^2\varphi - \sin^2\varphi) = DM\cos(2\varphi) =
DM\cos u.$$
So $Q = \frac{2s_A s_B \cos u}{S}(\cos\varphi,\, \sin\varphi)$, and $\overrighta
rrow{QC}$ is along line $DC$, at angle $\varphi$ to the $x$-axis.

**Step 6: Locating $P$.** The perpendicular from $M$ to $KL$ (the $x$-axis) is t
he vertical line $x = DM\cos\varphi$. Its intersection with line $AC$ gives $P =
 (1-t)A + tC$ where:
$$t = \frac{DM - DA}{1 - DA} = \frac{s_B(s_A + s_B)}{S}.$$
Computing coordinates:
$$y_P = \sin\varphi\bigl[-DA + t(DA+1)\bigr] = \sin\varphi \cdot \frac{2s_B^2}{S
}, \qquad x_P = \frac{2s_A s_B \cos\varphi}{S}.$$
Hence:
$$\overrightarrow{QP} = P - Q = \frac{2s_B}{S}\bigl(s_A \cos\varphi\,(1 - \cos u
),\;\sin\varphi\,(s_B - s_A\cos u)\bigr).$$

**Step 7: Key simplification.** Using $\sin B = \sin(A+u) = \sin A\cos u + \cos
A\sin u$:
$$s_B - s_A\cos u = \cos A\sin u = 2\cos A\sin\varphi\cos\varphi.$$
Also $1 - \cos u = 2\sin^2\varphi$. Substituting:
$$\overrightarrow{QP} = \frac{2s_B}{S}\bigl(2s_A\cos\varphi\sin^2\varphi,\;2\cos
 A\sin^2\varphi\cos\varphi\bigr) = \frac{4s_B\sin^2\varphi\cos\varphi}{S}\bigl(\
sin A,\;\cos A\bigr).$$
The direction of $\overrightarrow{QP}$ is $(\sin A, \cos A)$, which makes angle
$\pi/2 - A$ with the positive $x$-axis.

**Step 8: Conclusion.** The ray $QC$ is along line $DC$, at angle $\varphi = (B-
A)/2$ to the $x$-axis. The ray $QP$ is at angle $\pi/2 - A$ to the $x$-axis. The
refore:
$$\angle CQP = \left(\frac{\pi}{2} - A\right) - \frac{B - A}{2} = \frac{\pi - 2A
 - B + A}{2} = \frac{\pi - A - B}{2} = \frac{C}{2}.$$

The case $A > B$ follows by the identical argument with $A$ and $B$ interchanged
 (then $D$ lies beyond $A$, and the same computation yields $\angle CQP = C/2$).
 $\blacksquare$
