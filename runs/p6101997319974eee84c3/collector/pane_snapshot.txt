## Proof

**Claim.** $\mathbb{A}^n(k)$ and $\mathbb{A}^m(k)$ (with the Zariski topology, $
k$ algebraically closed) are homeomorphic if and only if $m = n$.

The direction "$m=n \Rightarrow$ homeomorphic" is trivial (they are the same spa
ce). We prove the converse using the **Krull dimension** as a topological invari
ant.

### The Krull dimension is a topological invariant

For any topological space $X$, define
$$\dim X = \sup\{\, d : \text{there exists a chain } Z_0 \subsetneq Z_1 \subsetn
eq \cdots \subsetneq Z_d \text{ of irreducible closed subsets of } X \,\}.$$
Here "irreducible" means $Z$ is nonempty and cannot be written as $Z = Z_1' \cup
 Z_2'$ with $Z_1', Z_2' \subsetneq Z$ proper closed subsets. Both "closed" and "
irreducible" are defined purely in terms of the topology, so $\dim X$ is invaria
nt under homeomorphism.

### Computing $\dim \mathbb{A}^n(k)$

Since $k$ is algebraically closed, the Nullstellensatz gives an inclusion-revers
ing bijection
$$\{\text{algebraic sets in } \mathbb{A}^n(k)\} \longleftrightarrow \{\text{radi
cal ideals of } k[x_1,\dots,x_n]\}, \qquad V \mapsto I(V),\quad I \mapsto V(I).$
$
Under this bijection, **irreducible** algebraic sets correspond to **prime** ide
als (an algebraic set $V$ is irreducible iff $I(V)$ is prime). Moreover the irre
ducible closed subsets of $\mathbb{A}^n(k)$ are exactly the irreducible algebrai
c sets (every Zariski-closed set is algebraic).

Therefore chains of irreducible closed subsets of $\mathbb{A}^n(k)$
$$Z_0 \subsetneq Z_1 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq Z_d$$
correspond bijectively (with reversed inclusion) to chains of prime ideals of $k
[x_1,\dots,x_n]$
$$\mathfrak{p}_d \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_{d-1} \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq \mat
hfrak{p}_0.$$
Hence
$$\dim \mathbb{A}^n(k) = \dim k[x_1,\dots,x_n].$$

### The Krull dimension of the polynomial ring

It is a standard result that $\dim k[x_1,\dots,x_n] = n$:

- **Lower bound.** The chain
$$(0) \subsetneq (x_1) \subsetneq (x_1,x_2) \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq (x_1,\d
ots,x_n)$$
consists of prime ideals (each quotient $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]/(x_1,\dots,x_i) \cong
k[x_{i+1},\dots,x_n]$ is a domain), so $\dim \geq n$.

- **Upper bound.** By Noether normalization (or by induction on $n$ using that $
k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ is integral over a polynomial subring in $\operatorname{tr.deg
}_k \operatorname{Frac}(k[x_1,\dots,x_n]) = n$ variables, and integral extension
s preserve Krull dimension), $\dim k[x_1,\dots,x_n] \leq n$.

Thus $\dim k[x_1,\dots,x_n] = n$, and consequently
$$\dim \mathbb{A}^n(k) = n.$$

### Conclusion

If $\mathbb{A}^n(k) \cong \mathbb{A}^m(k)$ as topological spaces, then by topolo
gical invariance of the Krull dimension,
$$n = \dim \mathbb{A}^n(k) = \dim \mathbb{A}^m(k) = m.$$

Therefore $\mathbb{A}^n(k)$ and $\mathbb{A}^m(k)$ are homeomorphic (in the Zaris
ki topology) **if and only if $m = n$**. $\blacksquare$
