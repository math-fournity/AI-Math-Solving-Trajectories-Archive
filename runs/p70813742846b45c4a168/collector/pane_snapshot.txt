Looking at this problem, I need to find the area of the region of "snug" points
— points that lie inside or on every triangle inscribed in circle ω (center O, r
adius 10) with orthocenter H (where OH = 6).

## Proof

**Setup.** Place O at the origin and H = (6, 0). For any triangle ABC inscribed
in ω with orthocenter H, the well-known relation **H = A + B + C** (vector sum w
ith O as origin) holds, with |A| = |B| = |C| = 10.

**Key reduction to half-planes.** Each side of triangle ABC is a chord of ω. The
 side BC is perpendicular to line AH (since H is the orthocenter, AH ⊥ BC). Let
**u** be the unit vector in direction AH. Then line BC has the form {**x** · **u
** = k} for some scalar k, and the half-plane containing vertex A is the interio
r side.

**Finding valid sides.** Since B, C lie on both the line {**x** · **u** = k} and
 ω, their midpoint is M = k**u**, and B + C = 2k**u**. Then A = H − 2k**u**, and
 the constraint |A| = 10 gives:

$$|H - 2k\mathbf{u}|^2 = 100 \implies k^2 - k(H \cdot \mathbf{u}) - 16 = 0$$

This yields two roots:
$$k_1(\mathbf{u}) = \frac{(H \cdot \mathbf{u}) + \sqrt{(H \cdot \mathbf{u})^2 +
64}}{2}, \qquad k_2(\mathbf{u}) = \frac{(H \cdot \mathbf{u}) - \sqrt{(H \cdot \m
athbf{u})^2 + 64}}{2}$$

One checks k₁ ∈ [2, 8] and k₂ ∈ [−8, −2], so both lines always intersect ω (sinc
e |k| < 10 = R), giving valid non-degenerate triangles.

**Half-plane directions.** For k₁: A · **u** = H · **u** − 2k₁ = −√((H·**u**)² +
 64) < 0 < k₁, so A lies in {**x** · **u** ≤ k₁}. For k₂: A · **u** = +√((H·**u*
*)² + 64) > 0 > k₂, so A lies in {**x** · **u** ≥ k₂}.

The snug region is the intersection of all these half-planes. Substituting **u**
 → −**u** in the k₂ constraint shows it equals the k₁ constraint, so:

$$S = \{\mathbf{x} : \mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{u} \le k_1(\mathbf{u}) \text{ for
all unit vectors } \mathbf{u}\}$$

**Computing the boundary (envelope).** Parametrize **u** = (cos θ, sin θ). Let f
(θ) = √(36 cos²θ + 64), so k₁(θ) = (6 cos θ + f)/2. The envelope of the family o
f lines x cos θ + y sin θ = k₁(θ) is found by solving simultaneously with the θ-
derivative. This gives:

$$x(\theta) = 3 + \frac{50\cos\theta}{f(\theta)}, \qquad y(\theta) = \frac{32\si
n\theta}{f(\theta)}$$

**Verification at key points:** θ = 0 gives (8, 0); θ = π gives (−2, 0); θ = π/2
 gives (3, 4); θ = 3π/2 gives (3, −4). These match direct triangle computations
(e.g., A = (10,0) gives side x = −2; A = (−10,0) gives side x = 8).

**Area computation.** Differentiating:
$$\dot{x} = \frac{-3200\sin\theta}{f^3}, \qquad \dot{y} = \frac{3200\cos\theta}{
f^3}$$

Then:
$$x\dot{y} - y\dot{x} = \frac{3200}{f^3}\left(3\cos\theta + \frac{50\cos^2\theta
 + 32\sin^2\theta}{f}\right)$$

Since 50 cos²θ + 32 sin²θ = 32 + 18 cos²θ = 32 + (f² − 64)/2 = f²/2, this simpli
fies to:

$$x\dot{y} - y\dot{x} = \frac{1600(6\cos\theta + f)}{f^3}$$

The area is:
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{2\pi} (x\dot{y} - y\dot{x})\,d\theta = 800\in
t_0^{2\pi} \frac{6\cos\theta + f}{f^3}\,d\theta$$

$$= 800\left[6\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{\cos\theta}{f^3}\,d\theta + \int_0^{2\pi}\frac{
1}{f^2}\,d\theta\right]$$

The first integral vanishes by symmetry (the integrand at θ and π − θ are negati
ves). For the second:

$$\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{36\cos^2\theta + 64}$$

Using cos²θ = (1 + cos 2θ)/2 and substituting φ = 2θ:

$$= \int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\varphi}{82 + 18\cos\varphi} = \frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{82^2 -
18^2}} = \frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{6400}} = \frac{2\pi}{80} = \frac{\pi}{40}$$

Therefore:
$$\text{Area} = 800 \cdot \frac{\pi}{40} = \boxed{20\pi}$$
