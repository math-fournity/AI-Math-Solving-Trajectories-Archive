# Galois Group of $x^4 - 4x^2 + 5$ over $\mathbb{Q}$

## Step 1: Find the roots

Substituting $y = x^2$, we get $y^2 - 4y + 5 = 0$, giving
$$y = \frac{4 \pm \sqrt{16-20}}{2} = 2 \pm i.$$
So the four roots are $\pm\alpha,\ \pm\beta$ where
$$\alpha = \sqrt{2+i}, \qquad \beta = \sqrt{2-i}.$$

## Step 2: Irreducibility over $\mathbb{Q}$

The polynomial has no rational roots. Testing factorization into two quadratics
over $\mathbb{Q}$: $(x^2+ax+b)(x^2-ax+c)$ requires $a(c-b)=0$, $bc=5$, $b+c-a^2=
-4$.

- If $a=0$: $b+c=-4,\ bc=5$ gives $t^2+4t+5=0$ with discriminant $-4<0$, no rati
onal solutions.
- If $c=b$: $b^2=5$, no rational solution.

So $f(x)=x^4-4x^2+5$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, and $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):
\mathbb{Q}]=4$.

## Step 3: Determine the splitting field

Note that $\alpha^2 = 2+i$, so $i = \alpha^2 - 2 \in \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$. Also
$$\alpha\beta = \sqrt{(2+i)(2-i)} = \sqrt{5},$$
so $\beta = \sqrt{5}/\alpha$. The splitting field is
$$K = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha, \beta) = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha, \sqrt{5}).$$

**Claim:** $\sqrt{5}\notin\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$, so $:\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)]=2$.

*Proof of claim:* If $\sqrt{5}\in\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$, then since $i\in\mathbb{Q}
(\alpha)$, we'd have $\mathbb{Q}(i,\sqrt{5})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$. Both h
ave degree $4$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, so $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)=\mathbb{Q}(i,\sqrt{5})$
, which is a Galois (biquadratic) extension. This would force $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha
)/\mathbb{Q}$ to be Galois, making $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ the splitting field, so
$\beta\in\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$. But $|\alpha|^2 = \sqrt{5}$ (as a complex number,
$|\sqrt{2+i}|^2 = |2+i| = \sqrt{5}$), and writing $\sqrt{2+i} = 5^{1/4}e^{i\thet
a/2}$ with $\cos\theta=2/\sqrt{5}$, we see $\sqrt{2+i}$ requires $5^{1/4}$, whic
h is not in $\mathbb{Q}(i,\sqrt{5})$. Contradiction. $\square$

Therefore $:\mathbb{Q}] = 4\cdot 2 = 8$, and $|G|=8$ where $G=\operatorname{Ga
l}(K/\mathbb{Q})$.

## Step 4: Identify the group structure

An automorphism $\sigma\in G$ is determined by $\sigma(\alpha)\in\{\pm\alpha,\pm
\beta\}$ and $\sigma(\sqrt{5})=\pm\sqrt{5}$, subject to $\sigma(\beta)=\sigma(\s
qrt{5})/\sigma(\alpha)$ being a root. One checks all $4\times 2=8$ combinations
are valid, confirming $|G|=8$.

Define:
- $u$: $\alpha\mapsto\beta,\ \sqrt{5}\mapsto -\sqrt{5}$ (hence $\beta\mapsto -\a
lpha,\ i\mapsto -i$).
- $t$: $\alpha\mapsto\alpha,\ \sqrt{5}\mapsto -\sqrt{5}$ (hence $\beta\mapsto -\
beta,\ i\mapsto i$).

**Order of $u$:**
$$u^2(\alpha)=u(\beta)=-\alpha,\quad u^3(\alpha)=-\beta,\quad u^4(\alpha)=\alpha
,$$
and $u^4(\sqrt{5})=\sqrt{5}$. So $u$ has **order 4**.

**Non-abelian:**
$$ut(\alpha)=u(\alpha)=\beta,\qquad tu(\alpha)=t(\beta)=-\beta.$$
So $ut\neq tu$; $G$ is non-abelian.

A non-abelian group of order $8$ containing an element of order $4$ is isomorphi
c to the **dihedral group $D_4$** (the only other order-$8$ group with an order-
$4$ element is $\mathbb{Z}/4\times\mathbb{Z}/2$, which is abelian).

## Conclusion

The Galois group of $x^4-4x^2+5$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is
$$\operatorname{Gal}(x^4-4x^2+5/\mathbb{Q})\ \cong\ D_4,$$
the dihedral group of order $8$. It acts on the four roots $\{\pm\alpha,\pm\beta
\}$ as the symmetry group of a square, generated by the $4$-cycle $u:\alpha\maps
to\beta\mapsto -\alpha\mapsto -\beta$ (rotation) and the reflection $t:\alpha\ma
psto\alpha,\ \beta\mapsto -\beta$.
