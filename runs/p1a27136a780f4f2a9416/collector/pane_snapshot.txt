## Proof

Fix a natural number $n$. We construct an explicit constant $C_n$.

**Case $n = 0$.** Then $f(t) = a_0$ is constant, so $\sup_{t \in [0,1]} |f(t)| =
 |a_0|$, and the inequality holds with $C_0 = 1$.

**Case $n \geq 1$.** Choose $n+1$ distinct points in $[0,1]$, for instance
$$t_k = \frac{k}{n}, \qquad k = 0, 1, \ldots, n.$$
These are distinct points in $[0,1]$.

By the Lagrange interpolation formula, every polynomial $f$ of degree at most $n
$ is uniquely determined by its values at these $n+1$ points, and
$$f(t) = \sum_{k=0}^{n} f(t_k)\, L_k(t),$$
where
$$L_k(t) = \prod_{\substack{j=0 \\ j \neq k}}^{n} \frac{t - t_j}{t_k - t_j}$$
is the $k$-th Lagrange basis polynomial, which has degree exactly $n$ and depend
s **only on the chosen points** $t_0, \ldots, t_n$ (hence only on $n$), not on $
f$.

Write each $L_k$ in the monomial basis:
$$L_k(t) = \sum_{i=0}^{n} \ell_{k,i}\, t^i,$$
where the coefficients $\ell_{k,i} \in \mathbb{R}$ are fixed real numbers determ
ined by $n$ and the chosen nodes. Substituting into the interpolation formula an
d collecting powers of $t$:
$$f(t) = \sum_{k=0}^{n} f(t_k) \sum_{i=0}^{n} \ell_{k,i}\, t^i = \sum_{i=0}^{n}
\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n} f(t_k)\, \ell_{k,i}\right) t^i.$$

Since the monomial basis $\{1, t, \ldots, t^n\}$ is linearly independent, the co
efficient of $t^i$ on the left, namely $a_i$, equals the coefficient on the righ
t:
$$a_i = \sum_{k=0}^{n} f(t_k)\, \ell_{k,i}.$$

Taking absolute values and applying the triangle inequality:
$$|a_i| \leq \sum_{k=0}^{n} |f(t_k)| \cdot |\ell_{k,i}| \leq \left(\sup_{t \in [
0,1]} |f(t)|\right) \sum_{k=0}^{n} |\ell_{k,i}|,$$
since each $t_k \in [0,1]$, so $|f(t_k)| \leq \sup_{t \in [0,1]} |f(t)|$.

Summing over $i = 0, 1, \ldots, n$:
$$\sum_{i=0}^{n} |a_i| \leq \left(\sup_{t \in [0,1]} |f(t)|\right) \sum_{i=0}^{n
} \sum_{k=0}^{n} |\ell_{k,i}|.$$

Now set
$$C_n \;=\; \sum_{i=0}^{n} \sum_{k=0}^{n} |\ell_{k,i}|.$$

This is a finite sum of finitely many fixed real numbers, each depending only on
 the chosen interpolation nodes $t_0, \ldots, t_n$, which in turn depend only on
 $n$. Therefore $C_n$ is a well-defined constant depending only on $n$, and we o
btain
$$|a_0| + |a_1| + \cdots + |a_n| \;\leq\; C_n \sup_{t \in [0,1]} |f(t)|,$$
as required. $\blacksquare$
