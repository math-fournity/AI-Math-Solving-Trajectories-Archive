## Proof

**Yes**, we can conclude that $a \geq 0$.

Let $a \in A$ be fixed, and suppose that $\pi(a) \geq 0$ for every cyclic repres
entation $\pi: A \to B(H)$.

**Step 1: $a$ is self-adjoint.**

For every cyclic representation $\pi$, the hypothesis $\pi(a) \geq 0$ means $\pi
(a)$ is a positive operator, hence self-adjoint: $\pi(a) = \pi(a)^* = \pi(a^*)$.
 Therefore $\pi(a - a^*) = 0$ for every cyclic representation $\pi$.

The **universal representation**
$$\Pi = \bigoplus_{\varphi \in S(A)} \pi_\varphi$$
where $S(A)$ is the set of all states on $A$ and $(\pi_\varphi, H_\varphi, \xi_\
varphi)$ is the GNS representation associated to $\varphi$, is a direct sum of c
yclic representations (each GNS representation is cyclic with cyclic vector $\xi
_\varphi$). A fundamental theorem of Gelfand–Naimark states that $\Pi$ is **fait
hful** (isometric). Since each summand $\pi_\varphi$ is cyclic, our hypothesis g
ives $\pi_\varphi(a - a^*) = 0$ for all $\varphi$, hence $\Pi(a - a^*) = 0$. By
faithfulness, $a - a^* = 0$, i.e., $a = a^*$.

**Step 2: $\varphi(a) \geq 0$ for every state $\varphi$.**

For any state $\varphi \in S(A)$, the GNS representation $(\pi_\varphi, H_\varph
i, \xi_\varphi)$ is cyclic, so by hypothesis $\pi_\varphi(a) \geq 0$. The state
is recovered as
$$\varphi(a) = \langle \pi_\varphi(a)\, \xi_\varphi,\, \xi_\varphi \rangle.$$
Since $\pi_\varphi(a) \geq 0$, this inner product is non-negative: $\varphi(a) \
geq 0$.

**Step 3: Positivity criterion.**

We use the standard theorem: *for a self-adjoint element $a \in A$, $a \geq 0$ i
f and only if $\varphi(a) \geq 0$ for every state $\varphi$ on $A$.*

*Proof of the criterion.* The forward direction is immediate since states are po
sitive linear functionals. For the converse, suppose $a = a^*$ and $\varphi(a) \
geq 0$ for all states $\varphi$, but $a \not\geq 0$. Then the spectrum $\sigma(a
)$ contains some $\lambda < 0$. By the functional calculus, $a$ has a non-trivia
l spectral projection corresponding to $(-\infty, 0)$. More concretely, since $a
$ is self-adjoint with $\sigma(a) \subseteq [\alpha, \beta]$ where $\alpha = \mi
n \sigma(a) < 0$, there exists a state $\psi$ with $\psi(a) = \alpha < 0$ (a sta
te attaining the minimum of the spectrum, which exists by considering the charac
ter on $C^*(a) \cong C(\sigma(a))$ corresponding to $\alpha$ and extending it to
 a state on $A$ via Hahn–Banach). This contradicts $\varphi(a) \geq 0$ for all s
tates. Hence $a \geq 0$.

**Conclusion.**

By Step 1, $a$ is self-adjoint. By Step 2, $\varphi(a) \geq 0$ for every state $
\varphi$. By the criterion in Step 3, $a \geq 0$ in $A$.
