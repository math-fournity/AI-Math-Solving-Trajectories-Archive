Looking at this problem, I need to find P-positions (losing positions for the pl
ayer to move) in this marble game, since Berta (second player) wins exactly when
 the starting position is a P-position.

**Legal moves from position $n$:** remove $k$ marbles where either $k$ is even w
ith $k \leq n/2$, or $k$ is odd with $n/2 \leq k \leq n$.

## Exploring Small Cases

Computing P/N positions for small $n$:

| $n$ | Moves lead to | Type |
|-----|---------------|------|
| 1 | $\to 0$ (win) | N |
| 2 | $\to 1$ (N) | **P** |
| 3 | $\to 0$ (win) | N |
| 4 | $\to 2$ (P) | N |
| 5 | $\to 2$ (P) or $\to 0$ | N |
| 6 | $\to 4,3,1$ (all N) | **P** |
| 7–13 | each can reach 2 or 6 (P) | N |
| 14 | $\to 12,10,8,7,5,3,1$ (all N) | **P** |

The P-positions are $0, 2, 6, 14, 30, 62, \ldots$ — these are $2^m - 2$ for $m \
geq 1$.

## Proof that P-positions are exactly $\{2^m - 2 : m \geq 1\}$

Let $P_m = 2^m - 2$. Note all $P_m$ for $m \geq 2$ are even.

### Part 1: Every move from $P_m$ ($m \geq 2$) leads to an N-position

Set $n = P_m = 2^m - 2$, so $n/2 = 2^{m-1} - 1$.

**Even moves** ($k$ even, $2 \leq k \leq 2^{m-1}-1$): The result is $n - k = 2^m
 - 2 - k$, which is even. For this to be a P-position $P_j = 2^j - 2$, we need $
k = 2^m - 2^j$. Since $k \leq 2^{m-1} - 2$, we need $2^j \geq 2^{m-1} + 2$, forc
ing $j \geq m$, which gives $k \leq 0$ — impossible. So no even move reaches a P
-position.

**Odd moves** ($k$ odd, $2^{m-1}-1 \leq k \leq 2^m-2$): The result $n - k$ is od
d, while all P-positions $P_j$ ($j \geq 2$) are even. So no odd move reaches a P
-position. ✓

### Part 2: Every N-position has a move to a P-position

**Case 1: $n$ is odd.** Choose odd $k = n - (2^j - 2)$ for suitable $j$. Since $
n$ is odd and $2^j - 2$ is even, $k$ is odd. We need $n/2 \leq k \leq n$, i.e.,
$2^j \leq n/2 + 2$ and $2^j \leq n+1$. Take $j$ maximal with $2^j \leq n/2 + 2$;
 then $2^j \geq (n/2+2)/2 = n/4+1$, giving $k = n - 2^j + 2 \leq 3n/4 + 1 \leq n
$ and $k \geq n/2$. This works for all odd $n \geq 1$.

**Case 2: $n$ is even and $n \neq 2^m - 2$.** We use an even move: $k = n - (2^j
 - 2)$, needing $k$ even (✓ since both $n$ and $2^j-2$ are even), $k \geq 2$, an
d $k \leq n/2$, i.e., $n/2 + 2 \leq 2^j \leq n$.

Let $2^s$ be the largest power of 2 with $2^s \leq n$. Then $n/2 < 2^s \leq n$.
Since $n$ and $2^s$ are integers, $2^s \geq \lceil n/2 \rceil + 1 = n/2 + 1$ (as
 $n$ is even). If $2^s = n/2 + 1$ exactly, then $n = 2(2^s - 1) = 2^{s+1} - 2$,
a P-position — contradicting our assumption. Therefore $2^s \geq n/2 + 2$, so $2
^s \in [n/2+2, n]$, and we can move to $P_s$. ✓

## Finding the Answer

The P-positions are $\{0, 2, 6, 14, 30, 62, 126, 254, 510, 1022, 2046, 4094, 819
