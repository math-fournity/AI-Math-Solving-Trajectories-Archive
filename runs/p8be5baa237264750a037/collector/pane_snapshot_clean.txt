**Answer: No.** In general the product is compact but not metrizable, so it is n
ot a compact *metric* space.

---

**Construction (counterexample).** Take $X_i = \{0,1\}$ with the discrete metric
 $d(a,b)=|a-b|$ for every $i\in[0,1]$. Each $X_i$ is a compact metric space (fin
ite, hence compact; discrete metric, hence metrizable). The product is
$$P \;=\; \prod_{i\in[0,1]} X_i \;=\; \{0,1\}^{[0,1]},$$
equipped with the product topology.

**Compactness.** By Tychonoff's theorem, an arbitrary product of compact topolog
ical spaces is compact in the product topology. Each $X_i=\{0,1\}$ is compact, s
o $P$ is compact. (No metrizability is needed for this direction.)

**Non-metrizability.** We show $P$ is not first-countable; since every metric sp
ace is first-countable, $P$ cannot be metrizable.

Fix a point $x=(x_i)_{i\in[0,1]}\in P$. Suppose, for contradiction, that $\{U_n\
}_{n\in\mathbb N}$ is a countable neighborhood base at $x$. In the product topol
ogy, every basic open neighborhood of $x$ restricts only *finitely many* coordin
ates. Hence for each $n$ there is a finite set $F_n\subseteq[0,1]$ such that $U_
n$ is determined by the coordinates in $F_n$ (more precisely, $U_n$ contains a b
asic open set $\pi_{F_n}^{-1}(\pi_{F_n}(x))$ and is contained in a cylinder set
over $F_n$; in particular membership in $U_n$ depends only on coordinates in $F_
n$).

Let $F=\bigcup_{n} F_n$. Then $F$ is countable, being a countable union of finit
e sets. Since $[0,1]$ is uncountable, there exists an index $j\in[0,1]\setminus
F$.

Consider the open neighborhood of $x$
$$V \;=\; \pi_j^{-1}(\{x_j\}) \;=\; \{y\in P : y_j = x_j\}.$$
This is open in the product topology (it is the preimage of the open singleton $
\{x_j\}$ under the projection $\pi_j$) and contains $x$.

Since $\{U_n\}$ is a neighborhood base, there must exist some $n$ with $U_n\subs
eteq V$. But $U_n$ depends only on coordinates in $F_n\subseteq F$, and $j\notin
 F_n$. So we may alter the $j$-th coordinate freely without leaving $U_n$: pick
$y\in P$ with $y_i=x_i$ for all $i\in F_n$ and $y_j\neq x_j$ (possible since $X_
j=\{0,1\}$ has two points and $j\notin F_n$). Then $y\in U_n$ but $y\notin V$. T
his contradicts $U_n\subseteq V$.

Therefore no countable neighborhood base exists at $x$; $P$ is not first-countab
le, hence not metrizable.

**Conclusion.** Although $\prod_{i\in[0,1]}X_i$ is compact by Tychonoff's theore
m, it is not in general a metric space: the uncountable product $\{0,1\}^{[0,1]}
$ is compact but not first-countable (hence not metrizable). Thus the answer to
the question is **no**.

(Remark: the only degenerate exception is when all but countably many factors ar
e singletons; then the product reduces to a countable product of compact metric
spaces, which is metrizable. But for a general family — and in particular for th
e family $X_i\equiv\{0,1\}$ — the product is not metrizable.)
