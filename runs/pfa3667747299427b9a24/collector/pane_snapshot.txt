**Answer: Yes, such a set exists.**

## Proof

The proof has three parts.

### Part 1. Every closed set in $\mathbb{R}^n$ is a $G_\delta$ set.

Let $F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n$ be closed. For each integer $k\ge 1$ set
$$U_k=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^n:\operatorname{dist}(x,F)<1/k\}.$$
The map $x\mapsto\operatorname{dist}(x,F)$ is continuous, so each $U_k$ is open.
 Since $F$ is closed, $x\in F\iff \operatorname{dist}(x,F)=0\iff x\in U_k\ \fora
ll k$, hence
$$F=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}U_k,$$
which exhibits $F$ as a countable intersection of open sets. ∎

### Part 2. There is a closed, nowhere-dense subset of $\mathbb{R}$ with positiv
e Lebesgue measure (Smith–Volterra–Cantor set).

**Construction.** Begin with $[0,1]$. At step $k\ge 1$ there are $2^{k-1}$ close
d intervals remaining; from the middle of each remove an open subinterval of len
gth $1/4^k$. The total length removed at step $k$ is
$$2^{k-1}\cdot\frac{1}{4^k}=\frac{1}{2^{k+1}},$$
so the total length removed over all steps is
$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}=\frac{1}{2}.$$
Let $C\subseteq[0,1]$ be what remains. Then:

- $C$ is closed, being the intersection of the nested closed sets obtained after
 each step.
- $\lambda_1(C)=1-\tfrac12=\tfrac12>0$.
- $C$ contains no interval: after step $k$ every remaining interval has length $
\le 2^{-k}\to 0$. Hence $\operatorname{int}(C)=\varnothing$, i.e. $C$ is nowhere
 dense. ∎

### Part 3. Lift to $\mathbb{R}^n$ by taking a product.

Set
$$K=\underbrace{C\times C\times\cdots\times C}_{n\text{ factors}}\subseteq[0,1]^
n\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n.$$

- **Closed:** $K$ is a finite product of closed sets, hence closed.
- **Positive measure:** by Fubini/Tonelli (or the definition of product measure)
,
  $$\lambda_n(K)=\lambda_1(C)^n=\left(\tfrac12\right)^n>0.$$
- **Empty interior:** $C$ is nowhere dense in $\mathbb{R}$, so $\operatorname{in
t}(\overline{C})=\operatorname{int}(C)=\varnothing$. In the product topology,
  $$\operatorname{int}\!\big(\overline{C^{\,n}}\big)=\operatorname{int}\!\big(\o
verline{C}^{\,n}\big)=\operatorname{int}(\overline{C})^n=\varnothing^n=\varnothi
ng,$$
  so $K$ is nowhere dense in $\mathbb{R}^n$, in particular $\operatorname{int}(K
)=\varnothing$.
- **$G_\delta$:** by Part 1, every closed subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$ is $G_\delta$
; $K$ is closed, so $K$ is $G_\delta$.

### Conclusion

$K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n$ is a $G_\delta$ set with $\lambda_n(K)=(1/2)^n>0$ and $
\operatorname{int}(K)=\varnothing$. Therefore a $G_\delta$ set in $\mathbb{R}^n$
 with positive Lebesgue measure and empty interior **does exist**. (For $n=1$ on
e simply takes $K=C$.)
