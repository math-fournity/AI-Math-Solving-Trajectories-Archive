## Solution

**Goal:** Find the smallest natural number $n$ with $\tau(\phi(n)) = 2017$, wher
e $\tau$ is the divisor-counting function and $\phi$ is Euler's totient.

### Step 1: 2017 is prime, so $\phi(n) = p^{2016}$

Since 2017 is prime, the equation $\tau(\phi(n)) = \prod (a_i + 1) = 2017$ force
s $\phi(n)$ to have a single prime-power form: $\phi(n) = p^{2016}$ for some pri
me $p$.

### Step 2: Odd $p$ is impossible

Suppose $p$ is odd and $\phi(n) = p^{2016}$. Write $n = \prod q_i^{e_i}$, so $\p
hi(n) = \prod q_i^{e_i-1}(q_i - 1) = p^{2016}$. Each factor $q_i^{e_i-1}(q_i-1)$
 must be a power of $p$.

- **If $q_i = p$:** the factor is $p^{e_i-1}(p-1)$. Since $p$ is odd, $p-1$ is e
ven, so this has a factor of 2, which cannot be a power of the odd prime $p$. Im
possible.
- **If $q_i \neq p$:** we need $e_i = 1$ and $q_i - 1 = p^{a_i}$, i.e., $q_i = p
^{a_i}+1$ prime. For $a_i \geq 1$, $p^{a_i}$ is odd, so $p^{a_i}+1$ is even and
$> 2$, hence **not prime**. For $a_i = 0$, $q_i = 2$, contributing $p^0 = 1$ to
the exponent — useless.

So no $n$ satisfies $\phi(n) = p^{2016}$ for odd $p$. **Only $p = 2$ is viable.*
*

### Step 3: For $p = 2$, minimize $n$ with $\phi(n) = 2^{2016}$

We need $\phi(n) = 2^{2016}$. The general structure:

- **Prime $q_i = 2$** with exponent $e$ in $n$: contributes $2^{e-1}$ to $\phi(n
)$ and $2^e$ to $n$.
- **Odd prime $q_i \neq 2$** with $e_i = 1$ and $q_i - 1 = 2^{a_i}$: i.e., $q_i
= 2^{a_i}+1$ must be a **Fermat prime** (so $a_i \in \{1,2,4,8,16,\ldots\}$). Co
ntributes $2^{a_i}$ to $\phi(n)$ and $2^{a_i}+1$ to $n$. Each such prime can be
used **at most once** (since $e_i = 1$ is forced).

The constraint is $e - 1 + \sum a_i = 2016$, i.e., $e = 2017 - \sum a_i$, giving
:

$$n = 2^{2017 - S} \cdot \prod (2^{a_i}+1), \quad S = \sum a_i.$$

### Step 4: Using Fermat primes always increases $n$

Compare against the baseline $n_0 = 2^{2017}$ (using no Fermat primes, $S = 0$).
 For any nonempty set of Fermat primes:

$$\prod (2^{a_i}+1) > \prod 2^{a_i} = 2^S,$$

so

$$n = 2^{2017-S} \cdot \prod(2^{a_i}+1) > 2^{2017-S} \cdot 2^S = 2^{2017} = n_0.
$$

Thus **every** use of Fermat primes makes $n$ strictly larger than $2^{2017}$.

### Step 5: Verification

For $n = 2^{2017}$:
- $\phi(2^{2017}) = 2^{2016}$
- $\tau(2^{2016}) = 2016 + 1 = 2017$ ✓

### Conclusion

The smallest natural number $n$ such that $\tau(\phi(n)) = 2017$ is:

$$\boxed{n = 2^{2017}}$$
