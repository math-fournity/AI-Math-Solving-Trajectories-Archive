**Claim.** Yes. If $\mu, \nu$ are finite positive Borel measures on $\mathbb{R}^
N$ and $\int f\,d\mu = \int f\,d\nu$ for every bounded continuous $f:\mathbb{R}^
N\to\mathbb{R}$, then $\mu=\nu$.

**Proof.**

**Step 1 — Total masses agree.** The constant function $f\equiv 1$ is bounded an
d continuous, so
$$\mu(\mathbb{R}^N)=\int 1\,d\mu=\int 1\,d\nu=\nu(\mathbb{R}^N)=:M<\infty.$$

**Step 2 — Agreement on closed sets.** Let $F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^N$ be closed. F
or $n\ge 1$ define
$$f_n(x)=\max\bigl(0,\;1-n\,\mathrm{dist}(x,F)\bigr),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^N.$$
Each $f_n$ is continuous (distance to a closed set is continuous), $0\le f_n\le
1$, and $f_n\downarrow \mathbf{1}_F$ pointwise: if $x\in F$ then $f_n(x)=1$ for
all $n$; if $x\notin F$ then $\mathrm{dist}(x,F)>0$ and $f_n(x)=0$ for all large
 $n$.

Since $\mu,\nu$ are finite and $|f_n|\le 1\in L^1$, the dominated convergence th
eorem gives
$$\mu(F)=\int \mathbf{1}_F\,d\mu=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int f_n\,d\mu=\lim_{n\to\inft
y}\int f_n\,d\nu=\int\mathbf{1}_F\,d\nu=\nu(F).$$
Hence $\mu(F)=\nu(F)$ for every closed $F$.

**Step 3 — Agreement on open sets.** Let $U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^N$ be open. Its c
omplement $F=\mathbb{R}^N\setminus U$ is closed, so by Steps 1 and 2,
$$\mu(U)=\mu(\mathbb{R}^N)-\mu(F)=M-\nu(F)=\nu(\mathbb{R}^N)-\nu(\mathbb{R}^N\se
tminus U)=\nu(U).$$

**Step 4 — Agreement on all Borel sets.** Every finite Borel measure on $\mathbb
{R}^N$ is outer regular: for every Borel set $E$,
$$\mu(E)=\inf\{\mu(U):E\subseteq U,\;U\text{ open}\},\qquad \nu(E)=\inf\{\nu(U):
E\subseteq U,\;U\text{ open}\}.$$
(This is the standard regularity theorem for finite Borel measures on a Polish s
pace; $\mathbb{R}^N$ is Polish.) By Step 3 the two families of numbers $\{\mu(U)
\}$ and $\{\nu(U)\}$ indexed by open $U\supseteq E$ are identical, so their infi
ma coincide:
$$\mu(E)=\inf_{U\supseteq E}\mu(U)=\inf_{U\supseteq E}\nu(U)=\nu(E).$$
Thus $\mu(E)=\nu(E)$ for every Borel set $E$, i.e. $\mu=\nu$. $\blacksquare$
