## Proof

Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion $f(x^2+f(y))=y+f(x)^2$.

**Injectivity.** If $f(a)=f(b)$, then $P(x,a)$ and $P(x,b)$ give $a+f(x)^2=b+f(x
)^2$, so $a=b$. Hence $f$ is injective.

**Surjectivity.** For fixed $x$, as $y$ ranges over $\mathbb{R}$, the right side
 $y+f(x)^2$ ranges over all of $\mathbb{R}$, so $f$ is surjective.

Let $a=f(0)$, and pick $c$ with $f(c)=0$.

**Key relation.** $P(0,y)$: $f(f(y))=y+a^2$. Applying $f$ and substituting $y\ma
psto f(y)$ gives $f(y+a^2)=f(y)+a^2$.

**Determining $c$.** $P(x,c)$: $f(x^2)=c+f(x)^2$. Setting $x=0$: $f(0)=c+f(0)^2$
, so $c=a-a^2$.

**Constraining $a$.** From $f(f(y))=y+a^2$ with $y=0$: $f(a)=a^2$. From $f(x^2)=
a-a^2+f(x)^2$ with $x=a$: $f(a^2)=a-a^2+a^4$. But $f(f(a))=a+a^2$, i.e., $f(a^2)
=a+a^2$. Equating: $a+a^2=a-a^2+a^4$, so $a^2(a^2-2)=0$. Thus $a=0$ or $a^2=2$.

**Ruling out $a^2=2$.** From $f(f(y))=y+a^2=y+2$ and $f(y+2)=f(y)+2$.

*Expression 1:* Using $f(t^2)=a-2+f(t)^2$ with $t=f(x)$:
$$f(f(x)^2)=a-2+f(f(x))^2=a-2+(x+2)^2=x^2+4x+a+2.$$

*Expression 2:* $P(x,0)$ gives $f(x^2+a)=f(x)^2$. Applying $f$ and using $f(f(z)
)=z+2$:
$$f(f(x)^2)=f(f(x^2+a))=(x^2+a)+2=x^2+a+2.$$

Equating: $x^2+4x+a+2=x^2+a+2$, forcing $4x=0$ for all $x$—contradiction. So $a^
2\neq 2$.

**Therefore $a=0$.** Then $c=0$, $f(0)=0$, $f(f(y))=y$ (involution), and $f(x^2)
=f(x)^2$.

**Additivity.** Substitute $u=f(y)$ (so $y=f(u)$) into $P(x,y)$:
$$f(x^2+u)=f(u)+f(x)^2=f(u)+f(x^2).$$
Since $x^2$ ranges over $[0,\infty)$, $f(t+u)=f(t)+f(u)$ for all $t\ge 0$, $u\in
\mathbb{R}$. For $s<0$, use $f((-s)+(s+u))=f(-s)+f(s+u)$, i.e., $f(u)=f(-s)+f(s+
u)$. From $P(x,-x)$-type symmetry: $f(x^2)=f((-x)^2)$ gives $f(x)^2=f(-x)^2$, an
d injectivity forces $f(-x)=-f(x)$ for $x\neq 0$ (with $f(0)=0$). Thus $f(-s)=-f
(s)$, yielding $f(s+u)=f(s)+f(u)$ for all $s,u$. So $f$ is additive.

**Multiplicativity.** Additivity gives $f((x+y)^2)=f(x^2)+2f(xy)+f(y^2)$. Also $
f((x+y)^2)=f(x+y)^2=(f(x)+f(y))^2=f(x)^2+2f(x)f(y)+f(y)^2=f(x^2)+2f(x)f(y)+f(y^2
)$. Hence $f(xy)=f(x)f(y)$.

**Conclusion.** $f$ is a ring endomorphism of $\mathbb{R}$ with $f(x^2)=f(x)^2\g
e 0$, so $f$ maps $[0,\infty)$ into $[0,\infty)$; combined with additivity, $f$
is order-preserving. An order-preserving additive map is $f(x)=kx$ for some $k\g
e 0$. The involution $f(f(y))=y$ forces $k^2=1$, so $k=1$.

**Verification.** $f(x)=x$: $f(x^2+f(y))=x^2+y=y+f(x)^2$. ✓

The unique solution is $\boxed{f(x)=x}$.
