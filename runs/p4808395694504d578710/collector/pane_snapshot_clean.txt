Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $D''$ being a masa in $A''$
 forces $D$ to be maximal abelian in $A$.

**Answer: No.** Here is a counterexample.

---

Let $H = L^2([0,1]) \otimes \mathbb{C}^2$ (separable) and $A = C([0,1], M_2(\mat
hbb{C}))$ acting on $H$ by multiplication. Define

$$D = \bigl\{\operatorname{diag}(g,h) : g,h\in C([0,1]),\; g(0)=h(0)\bigr\} \sub
seteq A.$$

**$D$ is a unital abelian C\*-subalgebra of $A$.** It is clearly abelian (all el
ements are diagonal). It is closed under the algebra operations and norm-cimited
: if $\operatorname{diag}(g_n,h_n) \to \operatorname{diag}(g,h)$ uniformly with
$g_n(0)=h_n(0)$, then $g(0)=h(0)$. The unit $\operatorname{diag}(1,1)=I_2$ belon
gs to $D$.

**$A'' = L^\infty([0,1])\,\bar\otimes\, M_2(\mathbb{C})$.** Since $A = C([0,1],M
_2)$ acts faithfully as multiplication operators, its double commutant is the al
gebra of all $L^\infty$ matrix-valued multiplication operators, i.e., $L^\infty(
[0,1])\,\bar\otimes\,M_2(\mathbb{C})$.

**$D'' = L^\infty([0,1])\,\bar\otimes\, D_2$** where $D_2$ is the diagonal $2\ti
mes 2$ matrices. Indeed, $D\subseteq L^\infty\,\bar\otimes\,D_2$ so $D''\subsete
q L^\infty\,\bar\otimes\,D_2$. For the reverse, note $D$ contains $\operatorname
{diag}(g,0)$ for every $g\in C([0,1])$ with $g(0)=0$. The set $\{g\in C([0,1]):g
(0)=0\}$ is strongly dense in $L^\infty([0,1])$ (the point condition is measure-
theoretically irrelevant), so $D''\supseteq L^\infty\otimes e_{11}$; similarly $
D''\supseteq L^\infty\otimes e_{22}$. Hence $D''=L^\infty\,\bar\otimes\,D_2$.

**$D''$ is a masa in $A''$.** Suppose $T\in L^\infty([0,1])\,\bar\otimes\,M_2(\m
athbb{C})$ commutes with every element of $L^\infty\,\bar\otimes\,D_2$. In parti
cular $T$ commutes with $L^\infty([0,1])\otimes I_2$; since $L^\infty([0,1])$ is
 a masa in $B(L^2([0,1]))$, $T$ must be a multiplication operator $M_F$ for some
 $F\in L^\infty([0,1],M_2)$. Commuting with constant diagonal matrices $e_{11},
e_{22}$ forces $F(t)$ to be diagonal a.e. Thus $T\in L^\infty\,\bar\otimes\,D_2
= D''$.

**$D$ is NOT maximal abelian in $A$.** Consider $x = \operatorname{diag}(1, t) \
in A = C([0,1],M_2)$. Since $x$ is diagonal, it commutes with every element of $
D$ (all of which are diagonal). However, $x\notin D$ because $g(0)=1\neq 0 = h(0
)$. Thus $x\in A$ commutes with all of $D$ but $x\notin D$, so $D$ is not maxima
l abelian in $A$.

---

**Conclusion.** The implication fails: $D''$ being a masa in $A''$ does not impl
y $D$ is maximal abelian in $A$. The obstruction is that $A\cap D''$ can be stri
ctly larger than $D$ — the weak closure "forgets" pointwise constraints (like $g
(0)=h(0)$) that are invisible at the von Neumann algebra level but visible in th
e C\*-algebra.
