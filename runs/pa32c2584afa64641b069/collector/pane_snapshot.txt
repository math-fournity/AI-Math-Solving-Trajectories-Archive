## Solution

**Problem.** Evaluate
$$L=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{x^2+x+1}{4}\right)^{n}\sin(nx)\,dx.
$$

**Step 1. Locate the maximum of the base.**

Set $f(x)=\dfrac{x^{2}+x+1}{4}$. Then
$$f'(x)=\frac{2x+1}{4}>0\qquad\text{for }x\in[0,1],$$
so $f$ is strictly increasing on $[0,1]$. Hence
$$\max_{x\in[0,1]}f(x)=f(1)=\frac{1+1+1}{4}=\frac{3}{4}<1,$$
and the minimum is $f(0)=\frac{1}{4}$.

**Step 2. Uniform bound on the integrand.**

For every $x\in[0,1]$ and every $n\ge 1$,
$$0\le f(x)\le \frac{3}{4}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 0\le f(x)^{n}\le \left(\frac
{3}{4}\right)^{n}.$$
Since $|\sin(nx)|\le 1$,
$$\left|f(x)^{n}\sin(nx)\right|\le \left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{n}\qquad\text{for a
ll }x\in[0,1].$$

**Step 3. Bound the integral and take the limit.**

Therefore
$$\left|\int_{0}^{1}f(x)^{n}\sin(nx)\,dx\right|\le \int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{3}{4}
\right)^{n}dx=\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0,$$
since $\frac{3}{4}<1$.

By the squeeze theorem,
$$L=0.$$

**Remark.** The key observation is that the base $\frac{x^{2}+x+1}{4}$ is strict
ly bounded above by $1$ on $[0,1]$ (its maximum $\frac{3}{4}$ is attained at the
 endpoint $x=1$). Consequently the factor $\bigl(\frac{x^{2}+x+1}{4}\bigr)^{n}$
decays to $0$ *uniformly* on $[0,1]$, and the bounded oscillation $\sin(nx)$ can
not compensate for this uniform exponential decay. No Laplace-type endpoint anal
ysis is needed here; a simple uniform estimate suffices.
