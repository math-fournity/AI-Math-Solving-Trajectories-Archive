## Proof

**Setup.** Let $ABCD$ be the cyclic quadrilateral with $AB=10$, $BC=8$, $CD=25$,
 $DA=12$. Place $B=(0,0)$ and $A=(10,0)$.

**Finding $\angle B$.** Using the diagonal $AC$ and the cyclic property $\angle
B + \angle D = 180^\circ$:
$$AC^2 = AB^2+BC^2-2(AB)(BC)\cos B = 164 - 160\cos B,$$
$$AC^2 = AD^2+CD^2-2(AD)(CD)\cos D = 769 + 600\cos B.$$
Equating: $164 - 160\cos B = 769 + 600\cos B$, giving $\cos B = -\tfrac{121}{152
}$ and $\sin B = \tfrac{\sqrt{8463}}{152}$.

So $C = (8\cos B, 8\sin B) = \left(-\tfrac{121}{19}, \tfrac{\sqrt{8463}}{19}\rig
ht)$.

**Finding $\angle A$ and $D$.** Similarly, using diagonal $BD$:
$$244 - 240\cos A = 689 + 400\cos A \implies \cos A = -\tfrac{89}{128},\quad \si
n A = \tfrac{\sqrt{8463}}{128}.$$
The direction from $A$ to $D$ is $(-\cos A, \sin A) = \left(\tfrac{89}{128}, \tf
rac{\sqrt{8463}}{128}\right)$, so
$$D = A + 12\left(\tfrac{89}{128}, \tfrac{\sqrt{8463}}{128}\right) = \left(\tfra
c{587}{32}, \tfrac{3\sqrt{8463}}{32}\right).$$

**The key triangle.** Let $P$ be the intersection of lines $AD$ and $BC$. Line $
BC$ has direction $(-121, \sqrt{8463})$; line $AD$ has direction $(89, \sqrt{846
3})$. Solving
$$\lambda(-121,\sqrt{8463}) = (10,0) + \mu(89,\sqrt{8463})$$
gives $\mu=\lambda$ from the $y$-component, and $-121\lambda = 10 + 89\lambda$,
so $\lambda = -\tfrac{1}{21}$. Thus
$$P = \left(\tfrac{121}{21}, -\tfrac{\sqrt{8463}}{21}\right).$$

**Side lengths of $\triangle ABP$:**
$$AB = 10,\quad BP = \frac{\sqrt{121^2+8463}}{21} = \frac{152}{21},\quad AP = \f
rac{\sqrt{89^2+8463}}{21} = \frac{128}{21}.$$

**Identifying $\omega$.** The circle $\omega$ is tangent to segments $DA$, $AB$,
 $BC$. In $\triangle ABP$: side $AB$ is the segment $AB$; the extension of $AP$
beyond $A$ runs toward $D$ (giving segment $DA$); the extension of $BP$ beyond $
B$ runs toward $C$ (giving segment $BC$). Therefore $\omega$ is the **excircle o
f $\triangle ABP$ opposite $P$**.

**Tangent points lie within the segments.** The $P$-excircle touches $AB$ at dis
tances $\tfrac{39}{7}$ from $A$ and $\tfrac{31}{7}$ from $B$ (both $< 10$ ✓); to
uches the extension of $AP$ beyond $A$ at distance $\tfrac{39}{7}\approx 5.57 <
12 = DA$ ✓; touches the extension of $BP$ beyond $B$ at distance $\tfrac{31}{7}\
approx 4.43 < 8 = BC$ ✓. So all three tangencies occur on the required segments.

**Computing the radius.** Semiperimeter:
$$s = \frac{10 + \frac{152}{21} + \frac{128}{21}}{2} = \frac{490/21}{2} = \frac{
35}{3},\qquad s - AB = \frac{35}{3}-10 = \frac{5}{3}.$$
Area of $\triangle ABP$ (base $AB=10$, height $|y_P| = \tfrac{\sqrt{8463}}{21}$)
:
$$[\triangle ABP] = \frac{1}{2}\cdot 10 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{8463}}{21} = \frac{5\s
qrt{8463}}{21}.$$
The $P$-exradius is
$$r = \frac{[\triangle ABP]}{s - AB} = \frac{5\sqrt{8463}/21}{5/3} = \frac{\sqrt
{8463}}{7}.$$

**Verification.** The excenter is $I_P = \left(\tfrac{31}{7}, \tfrac{\sqrt{8463}
}{7}\right)$. Its distance to line $AB$ ($y=0$) is $\tfrac{\sqrt{8463}}{7}$. Its
 distance to line $BC$ ($\sqrt{8463}\,x + 121\,y = 0$) is $\tfrac{\sqrt{8463}\cd
ot 152/7}{\sqrt{8463+121^2}} = \tfrac{\sqrt{8463}}{7}$. Its distance to line $AD

}{\sqrt{8463+89^2}} = \tfrac{\sqrt{8463}}{7}$. All three distances agree. ✓

$$\boxed{r = \dfrac{\sqrt{8463}}{7}}$$
