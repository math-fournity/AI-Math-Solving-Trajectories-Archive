## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. For any $\epsilon > 0$, there exists $n_0 \in \mathbb{N}$ such t
hat $P(|X| > x) < \dfrac{\epsilon}{x^t}$ for all real $x \geq n_0$.

**Key observation:** The tail probability $g(x) := P(|X| > x)$ is non-increasing
 in $x$, since for $x_1 \leq x_2$ we have $\{|X|>x_2\} \subseteq \{|X|>x_1\}$, h
ence $g(x_2) \leq g(x_1)$.

**Step 1: Relate the real-variable tail to the integer-variable tail.**

For any real $x \geq 1$, set $n = \lfloor x \rfloor$ (the greatest integer $\leq
 x$). Then $n \leq x$, so
$$\{|X| > x\} \subseteq \{|X| > n\},$$
which gives
$$P(|X| > x) \leq P(|X| > n). \tag{1}$$

**Step 2: Relate $x^t$ to $n^t$.**

Since $n \leq x < n+1$ and $n \geq 1$, we have $x < n+1 \leq 2n$, so
$$x^t < (2n)^t = 2^t \, n^t. \tag{2}$$

**Step 3: Combine.**

From (1) and (2):
$$x^t \, P(|X| > x) \leq x^t \, P(|X| > n) < 2^t \, n^t \, P(|X| > n). \tag{3}$$

**Step 4: Use the hypothesis.**

By hypothesis, $n^t \, P(|X|>n) \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$ (through integers). Giv
en $\epsilon > 0$, choose $N \in \mathbb{N}$ with $N \geq 1$ such that for all i
ntegers $n \geq N$:
$$n^t \, P(|X| > n) < \frac{\epsilon}{2^t}. \tag{4}$$

**Step 5: Conclude.**

Set $n_0 = N$. For any real $x \geq n_0$, let $n = \lfloor x \rfloor$. Since $x
\geq N$ and $N$ is a positive integer, $n = \lfloor x \rfloor \geq N$. Applying
(3) and (4):

$$x^t \, P(|X| > x) < 2^t \cdot \frac{\epsilon}{2^t} = \epsilon.$$

Dividing both sides by $x^t > 0$:

$$P(|X| > x) < \frac{\epsilon}{x^t}.$$

This holds for all real $x \geq n_0$. $\blacksquare$
