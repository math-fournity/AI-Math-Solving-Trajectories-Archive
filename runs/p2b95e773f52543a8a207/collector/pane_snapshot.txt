## Proof

Let $g(t) = \sum_{k=0}^{m} c_k t^k \in F[t]$. We compute both sides.

**Left-hand side.** By linearity,
$$
Bg(AB)A \;=\; B\!\left(\sum_{k=0}^{m} c_k (AB)^k\right)\!A \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{m}
c_k\, B(AB)^k A.
$$

**Key identity.** For every $k \ge 0$,
$$
B(AB)^k A \;=\; (BA)^{k+1}.
$$

*Proof of the identity.* For $k=0$: $B(AB)^0 A = BIA = BA = (BA)^1$. ✓

For $k \ge 1$, we use the standard factorization $(AB)^k = A(BA)^{k-1}B$, valid
for all $k \ge 1$ (proved by induction: the base case $k=1$ gives $AB = A\cdot I
\cdot B$; the inductive step gives $(AB)^{k+1} = (AB)\cdot A(BA)^{k-1}B = A(BA)^
kB$). Then
$$
B(AB)^k A \;=\; B\cdot A(BA)^{k-1}B \cdot A \;=\; (BA)(BA)^{k-1}(BA) \;=\; (BA)^
{k+1}. \quad\checkmark
$$

**Combining.** Substituting the identity into the expanded left-hand side,
$$
Bg(AB)A \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{m} c_k (BA)^{k+1} \;=\; (BA)\sum_{k=0}^{m} c_k (BA)^k
\;=\; (BA)\,g(BA) \;=\; BA\,g(BA).
$$

**Right-hand side** is exactly $BA\,g(BA)$.

Therefore
$$
Bg(AB)A \;=\; BA\,g(BA),
$$
so the statement holds for **all** $n\times n$ matrices $A, B$ over any field $F
$ and every polynomial $g(t)\in F[t]$. $\blacksquare$
