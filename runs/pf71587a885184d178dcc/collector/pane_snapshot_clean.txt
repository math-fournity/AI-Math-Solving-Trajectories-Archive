**Answer: Yes, $I(P)$ is invariant under the orthogonal group.**

**Proof.**

Identify the space of $k$-linear forms on $\mathbb{R}^n$ with the tensor space $
(\mathbb{R}^n)^{\otimes k}$ via the standard inner product (using the canonical
isomorphism $(\mathbb{R}^n)^* \cong \mathbb{R}^n$). Under this identification, t
he coefficient array $A = (a_{i_1,\dots,i_k})$ is a tensor in $(\mathbb{R}^n)^{\
otimes k}$, and

$$I(P) = \sum_{i_1,\dots,i_k} a_{i_1,\dots,i_k}^2 = \|A\|^2$$

is precisely the squared norm induced by the standard inner product on $(\mathbb
{R}^n)^{\otimes k}$ (which is the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product, the product inn
er product on the tensor factors).

Let $f \in O(n)$ with matrix $F$, so $F^T F = I_n$. Substituting $P_f(x_1,\dots,
x_k) = P(Fx_1,\dots,Fx_k)$ and expanding $(Fx_m)_{i_m} = \sum_{j_m} F_{i_m j_m}
x_{m,j_m}$, the coefficients of $P_f$ are

$$b_{j_1,\dots,j_k} = \sum_{i_1,\dots,i_k} a_{i_1,\dots,i_k}\, F_{i_1 j_1} \cdot
s F_{i_k j_k}.$$

In tensor notation this is $B = F^{\otimes k} A$, where $F^{\otimes k}$ acts on
each of the $k$ tensor factors. Now compute:

$$I(P_f) = \|B\|^2 = \langle F^{\otimes k} A,\; F^{\otimes k} A \rangle = \big\l
angle A,\; (F^{\otimes k})^* F^{\otimes k} A \big\rangle.$$

Since the inner product on the tensor product is the product of the factor inner
 products, the adjoint of $F^{\otimes k}$ is $(F^T)^{\otimes k}$. Hence

$$(F^{\otimes k})^* F^{\otimes k} = (F^T)^{\otimes k} F^{\otimes k} = (F^T F)^{\
otimes k} = I_n^{\otimes k} = \mathrm{Id}.$$

Therefore

$$I(P_f) = \langle A, A \rangle = \|A\|^2 = I(P).$$

This holds for every $f \in O(n)$, so $I(P)$ is invariant under the orthogonal g
roup. $\blacksquare$
