## Solution

**Claim:** The formula $x^2 + 11y^2$ correctly gives the field norm of $x + y\sq
rt{-11}$ for any $x, y \in \mathbb{Q}$. However, $x^2 + 11y^2$ is **not** the no
rm form of the ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_L = \mathbb{Z}\!\left[\frac{1+\sqrt
{-11}}{2}\right]$. The correct norm form on $\mathcal{O}_L$ is $x^2 + xy + 3y^2$
.

---

### Part 1: The field norm formula is correct

For $L = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-11})$, the nontrivial automorphism $\sigma$ sends $\s
qrt{-11} \mapsto -\sqrt{-11}$. For any $\alpha = x + y\sqrt{-11}$ with $x, y \in
 \mathbb{Q}$, the norm is

$$N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha) = \alpha \cdot \sigma(\alpha) = (x + y\sqrt{-11})(x -
 y\sqrt{-11}) = x^2 + 11y^2.$$

So as a **field norm formula**, $x^2 + 11y^2$ is correct. ✓

---

### Part 2: But $x^2 + 11y^2$ is NOT the norm form of $\mathcal{O}_L$

The ring of integers of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ for a squarefree $d$ with $d \equ
iv 1 \pmod{4}$ is $\mathbb{Z}\!\left[\frac{1+\sqrt{d}}{2}\right]$. Since $-11 \e
quiv 1 \pmod{4}$, we have

$$\mathcal{O}_L = \mathbb{Z}\!\left[\tfrac{1+\sqrt{-11}}{2}\right] \supsetneq \m
athbb{Z}[\sqrt{-11}].$$

This is a **strict** inclusion: the element $\omega = \frac{1+\sqrt{-11}}{2}$ is
 an algebraic integer (it satisfies $\omega^2 - \omega + 3 = 0$, since $\omega +
 \bar{\omega} = 1$ and $\omega\bar{\omega} = \frac{1+11}{4} = 3$), yet $\omega \
notin \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-11}]$ because its coordinates in the basis $\{1, \sqrt{-
11}\}$ are $\frac{1}{2}$ and $\frac{1}{2}$, which are not integers.

**Consequence:** The form $x^2 + 11y^2$ (with $x, y \in \mathbb{Z}$) only captur
es norms of elements in the **suborder** $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-11}] \subsetneq \mat
hcal{O}_L$, which has index $2$ in $\mathcal{O}_L$. It misses norms of algebraic
 integers like $\omega$.

**Explicit counterexample:** The element $\omega = \frac{1+\sqrt{-11}}{2} \in \m
athcal{O}_L$ has norm

$$N(\omega) = \frac{1 + 11}{4} = 3.$$

But $3$ **cannot** be represented as $x^2 + 11y^2$ with $x, y \in \mathbb{Z}$:
- If $y = 0$: $x^2 = 3$, no integer solution.
- If $|y| \geq 1$: $x^2 + 11y^2 \geq 11 > 3$.

So $N(\omega) = 3$ is a norm of an algebraic integer in $\mathcal{O}_L$ that is
**not** of the form $x^2 + 11y^2$ with $x, y \in \mathbb{Z}$.

---

### Part 3: The correct norm form on $\mathcal{O}_L$

Using the integral basis $\{1, \omega\}$ where $\omega = \frac{1+\sqrt{-11}}{2}$
, every element of $\mathcal{O}_L$ is uniquely $\alpha = a + b\omega$ with $a, b
 \in \mathbb{Z}$. Then

$$\alpha = a + b\cdot\frac{1+\sqrt{-11}}{2} = \left(a + \tfrac{b}{2}\right) + \t
frac{b}{2}\sqrt{-11},$$

$$N(\alpha) = \left(a + \tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2 + 11\left(\tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2 =
a^2 + ab + \tfrac{b^2}{4} + \tfrac{11b^2}{4} = a^2 + ab + 3b^2.$$

**Verification:** For $\omega$ itself ($a=0, b=1$): $0 + 0 + 3 = 3$ ✓. For $1$ (
$a=1, b=0$): $1$ ✓. For $\sqrt{-11} = -1 + 2\omega$ ($a=-1, b=2$): $1 - 2 + 12 =
 11$ ✓.

The norm form $x^2 + xy + 3y^2$ is a **positive-definite** binary quadratic form
 of discriminant $1 - 12 = -11$, which equals the field discriminant of $\mathbb
{Q}(\sqrt{-11})$. This is the correct reduced norm form representing norms of al
l algebraic integers in $\mathcal{O}_L$.

---

### Conclusion

The formula $N(x + y\sqrt{-11}) = x^2 + 11y^2$ is **correct as a field norm iden
tity** for $x, y \in \mathbb{Q}$. However, it is **not** the norm form of the ri
ng of integers $\mathcal{O}_L = \mathbb{Z}\!\left[\frac{1+\sqrt{-11}}{2}\right]$
, because $\mathcal{O}_L \neq \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-11}]$. The form $x^2 + 11y^2$ (w
ith $x, y \in \mathbb{Z}$) only represents norms of elements in the proper subor
der $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-11}]$ and fails to represent norms of algebraic integers
like $\omega = \frac{1+\sqrt{-11}}{2}$, whose norm $3$ is not expressible as $x^
2 + 11y^2$. The correct norm form on $\mathcal{O}_L$ is $x^2 + xy + 3y^2$.
