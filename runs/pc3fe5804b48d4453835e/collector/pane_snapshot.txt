## Analysis

This is **Erdős Problem 64**, a well-known open research problem. The question a
sks whether every finite graph with minimum degree at least 3 must contain a cyc
le whose length is a power of 2 (specifically $2^k$ for $k \geq 2$, i.e., length
s 4, 8, 16, 32, …). It is listed as open on erdosproblems.com and marked `catego
ry research open` in the formalization.

## What can be established

**Every graph with min degree ≥ 3 contains an even cycle.** A graph with min deg
ree ≥ 3 has a 2-connected subgraph with min degree ≥ 3. Any such subgraph contai
ns a theta graph (three internally disjoint paths between two vertices, with pat
h lengths $a, b, c$), yielding three cycles of lengths $a+b$, $a+c$, $b+c$. If a
ll three were odd, then $a+b$ and $a+c$ odd forces $b \equiv c \pmod{2}$, making
 $b+c$ even — contradiction. So at least one cycle is even.

**Bondy–Vince (1998):** Every graph with min degree ≥ 3 contains two cycles whos
e lengths differ by 1 or 2.

**Dirac:** A 2-connected graph with min degree $\delta$ has a cycle of length $\
geq 2\delta$, so min degree ≥ 3 gives a cycle of length ≥ 6.

## Why this is insufficient

These results only tell us the cycle-length set $L(G)$ is nonempty, contains an
even number, contains two near-consecutive lengths, and has maximum ≥ 6. A set l
ike $\{5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 13, 14, 15, 17, \ldots\}$ satisfies all these propert
ies yet avoids every power of 2. No known theorem forces $L(G)$ to be dense enou
gh to intersect $\{4, 8, 16, 32, \ldots\}$.

## Counterexample search

No counterexample is known. Avoiding all powers of 2 as cycle lengths requires a
voiding 4-cycles (girth ≥ 5), 8-cycles, 16-cycles, etc. Graphs with girth ≥ 9 an
d min degree 3 (e.g., certain cages) avoid 4- and 8-cycles but still appear to c
ontain 16-cycles. Constructing a graph avoiding *all* such lengths seems extreme
ly difficult and has not been achieved.
