## Proof

Let $C = \{B \in M_n(k) : BA = AB\}$ be the commutant (centralizer) of $A$ in $M
_n(k)$.

**Step 1: The minimal polynomial equals the characteristic polynomial.**

Let $p(x) = \chi_A(x)$ be the characteristic polynomial and $m(x) = \mu_A(x)$ th
e minimal polynomial. We always have $m \mid p$, and $m$ and $p$ share the same
irreducible factors over $k$. Since $p$ is irreducible over $k$ and $m$ is a non
-constant divisor of $p$, we must have $m = p$. In particular $\deg m = n$.

**Step 2: $A$ is cyclic (has a single invariant factor).**

The rational canonical form of $A$ is determined by its invariant factors $f_1 \
mid f_2 \mid \cdots \mid f_r$, where $f_r = m$ is the minimal polynomial and $\s
um \deg f_i = n$. Since $m = p$ has degree $n$, we have $\deg f_r = n = \sum \de
g f_i$, forcing $r = 1$. Thus $A$ has a single invariant factor, i.e., $A$ is cy
clic: there exists a vector $v$ such that $\{v, Av, \ldots, A^{n-1}v\}$ is a bas
is of $k^n$.

**Step 3: The centralizer of a cyclic matrix is $k[A]$.**

Let $B \in C$, so $BA = AB$. Let $v$ be a cyclic vector for $A$. Since $B$ commu
tes with $A$, for every $i \geq 0$:
$$B(A^i v) = A^i(Bv).$$
Since $\{v, Av, \ldots, A^{n-1}v\}$ is a basis, $B$ is completely determined by
$Bv$. Write $Bv = c_0 v + c_1 Av + \cdots + c_{n-1} A^{n-1}v$ for some $c_i \in
k$. Then for every basis vector $A^i v$:
$$B(A^i v) = A^i(Bv) = A^i\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} c_j A^j v\right) = \sum_{j=0}^{
n-1} c_j A^{i+j} v = \left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} c_j A^j\right)(A^i v).$$
Hence $B = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} c_j A^j \in k[A]$. Conversely, every element of $k[A
]$ commutes with $A$. Therefore:
$$C = k[A] = \{c_0 I + c_1 A + \cdots + c_{n-1}A^{n-1} : c_i \in k\}.$$

**Step 4: The dimension.**

Since $m = p$ has degree $n$, the powers $I, A, \ldots, A^{n-1}$ are linearly in
dependent over $k$ (any linear relation of degree $< n$ would give a polynomial
of degree $< n$ annihilating $A$, contradicting $\deg m = n$). Thus $\{I, A, \ld
ots, A^{n-1}\}$ is a basis of $C = k[A]$ over $k$, and:
$$\dim_k C = n.$$

**Remark on the Galois group.** The condition that the Galois group of $p$ over
$k$ is $S_n$ guarantees (in particular) that $p$ is irreducible, since $S_n$ act
s transitively on the roots. The irreducibility alone is what drives the argumen
t above; the full strength of the $S_n$ hypothesis is not needed for the dimensi
on computation. The answer is the same for any irreducible characteristic polyno
mial.

$$\boxed{\dim_k C = n}$$
