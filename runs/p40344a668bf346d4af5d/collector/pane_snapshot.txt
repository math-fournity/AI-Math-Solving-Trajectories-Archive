## Proof

Let $u$ be a real harmonic function on the unit disk $D$, so $\Delta u = u_{xx}
+ u_{yy} = 0$ on $D$. We are given $u(0)=0$ and that $u^2$ is also harmonic on $
D$.

Compute the Laplacian of $u^2$. By the product rule (or the identity $\Delta(uv)
 = u\,\Delta v + v\,\Delta u + 2\,\nabla u \cdot \nabla v$),

$$\Delta(u^2) = 2u\,\Delta u + 2\,|\nabla u|^2 = 2u\cdot 0 + 2(u_x^2 + u_y^2) =
2(u_x^2 + u_y^2).$$

Since $u^2$ is harmonic on $D$, we have $\Delta(u^2) = 0$ on $D$, hence

$$u_x^2 + u_y^2 = 0 \quad \text{on } D.$$

As a sum of squares of real-valued functions, this forces $u_x \equiv 0$ and $u_
y \equiv 0$ on $D$. Therefore $u$ is constant on the connected domain $D$. The c
ondition $u(0) = 0$ then forces $u \equiv 0$ on $D$.

Conversely, $u \equiv 0$ is harmonic, satisfies $u(0)=0$, and $u^2 \equiv 0$ is
harmonic. So it is a valid solution.

The unique real harmonic function on $D$ with $u(0)=0$ and $u^2$ harmonic is

$$\boxed{u \equiv 0}.$$
