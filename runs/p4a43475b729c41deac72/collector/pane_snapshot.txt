## Solution

**Setup.** We use the change of variables
$$u = x^2 - y^2, \qquad v = xy,$$
with density $\delta(x,y) = 2(x^2+y^2)$ (twice the squared distance from the ori
gin).

**Jacobian.** Compute
$$\frac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(x,y)} = \begin{vmatrix} 2x & -2y \\ y & x \end{v
matrix} = 2x\cdot x - (-2y)\cdot y = 2x^2 + 2y^2 = 2(x^2+y^2).$$

Hence
$$\left|\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)}\right| = \frac{1}{2(x^2+y^2)}.$$

**Region in the $uv$-plane.** In the first quadrant ($x>0, y>0$), the map $(x,y)
\mapsto(u,v)$ is one-to-one (since $x^2 = \frac{u+\sqrt{u^2+4v^2}}{2}>0$ and $y
= v/x>0$ are uniquely determined). The four bounding hyperbolas become:
- $x^2-y^2=1 \Rightarrow u=1$
- $x^2-y^2=6 \Rightarrow u=6$
- $xy=3 \Rightarrow v=3$
- $xy=8 \Rightarrow v=8$

So the region $R$ maps to the rectangle $S = [1,6]\times[3,8]$ in the $uv$-plane
.

**Transforming the integral.** The density cancels the Jacobian exactly:
$$m = \iint_R 2(x^2+y^2)\,dA = \iint_S 2(x^2+y^2)\cdot\frac{1}{2(x^2+y^2)}\,du\,
dv = \iint_S 1\,du\,dv.$$

**Evaluation.**
$$m = \int_{1}^{6}\int_{3}^{8} 1\,dv\,du = (6-1)(8-3) = 5\cdot 5 = 25.$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{25}$
