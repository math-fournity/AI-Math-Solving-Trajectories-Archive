## Proof

**Setup.** A circle of radius $r = 2$ is inscribed in a square, so the square ha
s side length $4$. Place the center of the circle at the origin $O = (0,0)$. The
 square has vertices at $(\pm 2, \pm 2)$, and the four points of tangency (where
 the circle touches the square's sides) are:

$$A = (2, 0),\quad B = (0, 2),\quad C = (-2, 0),\quad D = (0, -2).$$

**Identifying the chords.** The chords naturally arising from the square's geome
try are of two types:

1. **Diameters** created by the square's diagonals. The diagonal from $(2,2)$ to
 $(-2,-2)$ lies on the line $y = x$, which passes through $O$ and cuts the circl
e in a diameter.

2. **Chords connecting tangent points.** For instance, the chord $TU$ connecting
 $A = (2,0)$ and $B = (0,2)$ lies on the line $x + y = 2$.

Two chords connecting tangent points are either parallel (opposite pairs) or sha
re an endpoint (adjacent pairs), so they cannot properly intersect inside the ci
rcle. Therefore, one chord must be a diameter (from a diagonal) and the other a
chord connecting two tangent points.

**Finding the intersection.** Take the diameter along $y = x$ and the chord $TU$
 from $A(2,0)$ to $B(0,2)$ with equation $x + y = 2$.

Their intersection $P$:
$$y = x \quad \text{and} \quad x + y = 2 \implies 2x = 2 \implies x = 1,\; y = 1
.$$

So $P = (1, 1)$.

**Verifying P bisects TU.** The midpoint of $TU$ (from $A(2,0)$ to $B(0,2)$) is:
$$\left(\frac{2+0}{2},\; \frac{0+2}{2}\right) = (1, 1) = P. \quad \checkmark$$

**Perpendicularity check.** The direction of $TU$ is $(-1, 1)$, and the directio
n of $\overrightarrow{OP}$ is $(1, 1)$. Their dot product is $(-1)(1) + (1)(1) =
 0$, confirming $OP \perp TU$. This is consistent with the circle theorem: the l
ine from the center to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to the chord.

**Computing the distance.**
$$OP = \sqrt{1^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{2}.$$

**Generality by symmetry.** By the fourfold symmetry of the square, the same res
ult holds for every choice of diagonal–diameter and corresponding tangent-point
chord:

| Diameter | Chord $TU$ | Midpoint $P$ | $OP$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $y = x$ | $(2,0)$–$(0,2)$ | $(1,1)$ | $\sqrt{2}$ |
| $y = -x$ | $(0,2)$–$(-2,0)$ | $(-1,1)$ | $\sqrt{2}$ |
| $y = x$ | $(-2,0)$–$(0,-2)$ | $(-1,-1)$ | $\sqrt{2}$ |
| $y = -x$ | $(0,-2)$–$(2,0)$ | $(1,-1)$ | $\sqrt{2}$ |

In every case, $P$ bisects $TU$ and $OP = \sqrt{2}$.

---

**The distance from the point of intersection $P$ to the center of the circle is
 $\boxed{\sqrt{2}}$.**
