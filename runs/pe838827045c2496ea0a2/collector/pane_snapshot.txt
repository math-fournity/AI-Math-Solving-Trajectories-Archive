## Setup: Two-Dimensional Eigenfunctions of the Laplacian on a Disk

In polar coordinates, the Laplacian is ∇²u = (1/r)∂/∂r(r ∂u/∂r) + (1/r²)∂²u/∂θ².
 The eigenfunctions separate as Φ(r,θ) = R(r)Θ(θ), where Θ = e^{inθ} (n = 0, ±1,
 ±2, …) are the angular eigenfunctions and R(r) = J_n(kr) are Bessel functions s
atisfying the radial equation:

$$r^2 R'' + r R' + (k^2 r^2 - n^2) R = 0$$

The eigenvalue is −k². The specific eigenvalues k are determined by the boundary
 condition at r = a.

## Case (a): u(a, θ) = 0 (Homogeneous Dirichlet)

**Eigenfunctions.** The Dirichlet eigenfunctions are:

$$\Phi_{nm}^{(D)}(r,\theta) = J_n\!\left(\frac{\alpha_{nm}\, r}{a}\right) e^{in\
theta}, \quad n = 0,1,2,\ldots,\quad m = 1,2,3,\ldots$$

where α_{nm} is the m-th positive zero of J_n. These satisfy:

$$\nabla^2 \Phi_{nm}^{(D)} = -\lambda_{nm}\, \Phi_{nm}^{(D)}, \quad \lambda_{nm}
 = \left(\frac{\alpha_{nm}}{a}\right)^2 > 0$$

and Φ_{nm}^{(D)}(a,θ) = 0 by construction.

**Expansion.** Expand both u and Q:

$$u = \sum_{n,m} A_{nm}\, \Phi_{nm}^{(D)}, \qquad Q = \sum_{n,m} B_{nm}\, \Phi_{
nm}^{(D)}$$

where the coefficients B_{nm} are computed via orthogonality (with weight r):

$$B_{nm} = \frac{1}{N_{nm}} \int_0^a\!\int_0^{2\pi} Q(r,\theta)\, J_n\!\left(\fr
ac{\alpha_{nm} r}{a}\right) e^{-in\theta}\, r\, dr\, d\theta$$

with $N_{nm} = \frac{a^2}{2}\bigl[J_{n+1}(\alpha_{nm})\bigr]^2 \cdot 2\pi$ (for
n ≥ 0; the full normalization includes both e^{inθ} and e^{-inθ} modes).

**Solving.** Substituting into ∇²u = Q:

$$-\sum_{n,m} \lambda_{nm}\, A_{nm}\, \Phi_{nm}^{(D)} = \sum_{n,m} B_{nm}\, \Phi
_{nm}^{(D)}$$

By orthogonality, mode by mode:

$$\boxed{A_{nm} = -\frac{B_{nm}}{\lambda_{nm}} = -\frac{a^2\, B_{nm}}{\alpha_{nm

**Solvability.** Since every Dirichlet eigenvalue λ_{nm} > 0 (the Laplacian with

**Conclusion for (a):** A solution **always exists** and is **unique**. No solva
bility condition on Q is required.

## Case (b): ∂u/∂r(a, θ) = 0 (Homogeneous Neumann)

$$\mu_{nm} = \left(\frac{\beta_{nm}}{a}\right)^2, \quad \text{with } \mu_{00} =

$$-\sum_{n,m} \mu_{nm}\, A_{nm}\, \Phi_{nm}^{(N)} = \sum_{n,m} B_{nm}\, \Phi_{nm

## Case (d): ∂u/∂r(a, θ) = g(θ) (Non-homogeneous Neumann)

$$\int\!\!\int_D \nabla^2 u\, dA = \int\!\!\int_D Q\, dA$$

$$\oint_{\partial D} \frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\, ds = \int_0^{2\pi} g(\theta

$$\boxed{\int_0^a \!\int_0^{2\pi} Q(r,\theta)\, r\, dr\, d\theta = a \int_0^{2\p

$$\int\!\!\int_D Q\, dA = 2\pi a\, \bar{g}$$

**Conclusion for (d):** A solution exists **if and only if**:
