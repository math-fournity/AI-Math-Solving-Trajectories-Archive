## Answer

**Yes**, the functional $F(f) = \int_0^1 f(t)\,dt$ is $L^1$-weakly lower semicon
tinuous on $C([0,1],\mathbb{R})$. In fact it is **weakly continuous**.

### Proof

Equip $X = C([0,1],\mathbb{R})$ with the $L^1$-norm $\|f\|_1 = \int_0^1 |f(t)|\,
dt$.

**Step 1: Identify the dual.** Since $C([0,1])$ is dense in $L^1([0,1])$, every
continuous linear functional on $(X, \|\cdot\|_1)$ extends uniquely to a continu
ous linear functional on $L^1([0,1])$. By the duality $(L^1)^* \cong L^\infty$,
the dual of $(X, \|\cdot\|_1)$ is (isometrically) $L^\infty([0,1])$, with the pa
iring $\langle g, f\rangle = \int_0^1 f(t)g(t)\,dt$.

**Step 2: $F$ is a bounded linear functional.** $F$ is linear, and
$$|F(f)| = \left|\int_0^1 f(t)\,dt\right| \le \int_0^1 |f(t)|\,dt = \|f\|_1.$$
Moreover $F$ corresponds to the element $g \equiv 1 \in L^\infty([0,1])$, since
$F(f) = \int_0^1 f(t)\cdot 1\,dt$. Hence $F \in X^*$ with $\|F\|_{X^*} = 1$ (equ
ality is attained, e.g., by $f \equiv 1$).

**Step 3: Bounded linear functionals are weakly continuous.** Let $f_n \righthar
poonup f$ weakly in $X$, i.e. $\phi(f_n) \to \phi(f)$ for every $\phi \in X^*$.
Taking $\phi = F \in X^*$, we obtain
$$F(f_n) \to F(f).$$

**Step 4: Conclude.** Since $F(f_n) \to F(f)$, in particular
$$F(f) = \lim_{n\to\infty} F(f_n) = \liminf_{n\to\infty} F(f_n).$$
Thus $F(f) \le \liminf F(f_n)$ (with equality), proving $F$ is $L^1$-weakly lowe
r semicontinuous — indeed weakly continuous. $\blacksquare$
