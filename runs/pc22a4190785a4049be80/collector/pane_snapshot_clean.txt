## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $L = H_K^n$ must be Galois over $K$.

**Definitions and setup.** The Hilbert class field tower is defined recursively:
- $H_K^1$ is the Hilbert class field of $K$, i.e., the maximal unramified abelia
n extension of $K$.
- $H_K^{i+1}$ is the Hilbert class field of $H_K^i$, i.e., the maximal unramifie
d abelian extension of $H_K^i$.

Each step $H_K^{i+1}/H_K^i$ is unramified and abelian (hence Galois). By transit
ivity of unramifiedness, $H_K^i/K$ is unramified for every $i$. The tower is fin
ite means $H_K^n$ has class number 1, so $H_K^{n+1} = H_K^n$, and $L = H_K^n$ is
 the top.

**Key observation.** The Hilbert class field $H_F$ of a number field $F$ is char
acterized as the *maximal unramified abelian extension* of $F$. This characteriz
ation is intrinsic to $F$ and is preserved by any field isomorphism: if $\sigma:
 F \to F'$ is an isomorphism and $E/F$ is unramified abelian, then $\sigma(E)/F'
$ is unramified abelian.

**Induction.** We prove by induction on $i$ that $H_K^i/K$ is Galois for all $i
\geq 1$.

*Base case ($i=1$):* $H_K^1$ is the Hilbert class field of $K$, which is Galois
(indeed abelian) over $K$ by definition, with $\operatorname{Gal}(H_K^1/K) \cong
 \operatorname{Cl}(K)$.

*Inductive step:* Assume $H_K^i/K$ is Galois. We show $H_K^{i+1}/K$ is Galois. L
et $\sigma: H_K^{i+1} \hookrightarrow \overline{K}$ be any $K$-embedding. Since
$H_K^i/K$ is Galois, the restriction $\sigma|_{H_K^i}$ is a $K$-automorphism of
$H_K^i$, i.e., $\sigma(H_K^i) = H_K^i$.

Now consider $\sigma(H_K^{i+1})$. Since $H_K^{i+1}/H_K^i$ is unramified and abel
ian, and $\sigma$ fixes $K$ (hence preserves the primes of $K$ and the ramificat
ion structure), the extension $\sigma(H_K^{i+1})/\sigma(H_K^i) = \sigma(H_K^{i+1
})/H_K^i$ is also unramified and abelian.

Since $H_K^{i+1}$ is the *maximal* unramified abelian extension of $H_K^i$, we h
ave:
$$\sigma(H_K^{i+1}) \subseteq H_K^{i+1}.$$
Since $[\sigma(H_K^{i+1}) : H_K^i] = [H_K^{i+1} : H_K^i]$, equality of degrees f
orces:
$$\sigma(H_K^{i+1}) = H_K^{i+1}.$$

Thus every $K$-embedding of $H_K^{i+1}$ into $\overline{K}$ maps $H_K^{i+1}$ to
itself, which is precisely the condition that $H_K^{i+1}/K$ is Galois. $\checkma
rk$

**Conclusion.** By induction, $H_K^i/K$ is Galois for all $i$. In particular, $L
 = H_K^n$ is Galois over $K$.

**Remark.** The finiteness of the tower is not needed for the Galois property —
every stage of the tower is Galois over $K$. The finiteness assumption only ensu
res that $L$ is well-defined as the top of the tower (the stage where the class
number becomes 1).
