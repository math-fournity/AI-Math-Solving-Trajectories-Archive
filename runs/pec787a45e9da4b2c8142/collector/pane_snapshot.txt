Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether simple functions $Y_n \ge 0
$ can simultaneously approximate both $E(Y)$ and $E(F(Y))$, where $F$ is merely
continuous (not necessarily monotone or bounded).

**Answer: Yes, such simple functions always exist.**

## Proof

The key difficulty is that $F$ is only continuous — not monotone, so truncation
approximations $Y_n \le Y$ do not give $|F(Y_n)| \le |F(Y)|$, preventing a direc
t dominated convergence argument. We overcome this by handling the tail of $Y$ s
eparately, setting $Y_n = 0$ there (so $F(Y_n) = F(0)$, a fixed constant whose c
ontribution vanishes by Markov's inequality).

**Setup.** Since $Y \in L^1$ and $F(Y) \in L^1$, we have:
- $E[Y \cdot \mathbf{1}_{Y > M}] \to 0$ as $M \to \infty$ (absolute continuity o
f the integral),
- $E[|F(Y)| \cdot \mathbf{1}_{Y > M}] \to 0$ as $M \to \infty$,
- $P(Y > M) \le E[Y]/M \to 0$ as $M \to \infty$ (Markov's inequality), so $|F(0)
| \cdot P(Y > M) \to 0$ since $F(0)$ is a fixed real constant.

**Construction.** For each $n \ge 1$, choose $M_n > 0$ large enough that all thr
ee of the following hold:
$$E\!\left[Y \,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y > M_n\}}\right] < \tfrac{1}{n}, \qquad E\!\left[|
F(Y)|\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y > M_n\}}\right] < \tfrac{1}{n}, \qquad |F(0)|\,P(Y > M_n)
 < \tfrac{1}{n}.$$

Since $F$ is continuous on the compact interval $[0, M_n]$, it is uniformly cont
inuous there. Choose $\delta_n > 0$ such that $|x - y| < \delta_n$ and $x, y \in
 [0, M_n]$ imply $|F(x) - F(y)| < \frac{1}{n}$.

Partition $[0, M_n]$ into intervals $I_1, \ldots, I_{k_n}$ of width at most $\de
lta_n$. Let $a_j$ be the left endpoint of $I_j$.

Define the simple function:
$$Y_n(\omega) = \begin{cases} a_j & \text{if } Y(\omega) \in I_j \subseteq [0, M
_n], \\ 0 & \text{if } Y(\omega) > M_n. \end{cases}$$

Then $Y_n \ge 0$ and $Y_n$ is simple (it takes finitely many values: $a_1, \ldot
s, a_{k_n}, 0$).

**Convergence of $E(Y_n) \to E(Y)$.** On $\{Y \le M_n\}$, we have $|Y_n - Y| \le
 \delta_n$. On $\{Y > M_n\}$, we have $Y_n = 0$ so $|Y_n - Y| = Y$. Therefore:
$$|E[Y_n] - E[Y]| \le E|Y_n - Y| \le \delta_n \cdot P(Y \le M_n) + E\!\left[Y\,\
mathbf{1}_{\{Y > M_n\}}\right] < \delta_n + \tfrac{1}{n} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty
} 0.$$

**Convergence of $E(F(Y_n)) \to E(F(Y))$.** We split:
$$|E[F(Y_n)] - E[F(Y)]| \le E\!\left[|F(Y_n) - F(Y)|\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y \le M_n\}}
\right] + E\!\left[|F(Y_n) - F(Y)|\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y > M_n\}}\right].$$

- **On $\{Y \le M_n\}$:** Both $Y_n$ and $Y$ lie in the same interval $I_j \subs
eteq [0, M_n]$, so $|Y_n - Y| \le \delta_n$, hence $|F(Y_n) - F(Y)| < \frac{1}{n
}$ by uniform continuity. The first term is $< \frac{1}{n}$.

- **On $\{Y > M_n\}$:** $Y_n = 0$, so $|F(Y_n) - F(Y)| = |F(0) - F(Y)| \le |F(0)
| + |F(Y)|$. The second term is:
$$E\!\left[|F(0) - F(Y)|\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y > M_n\}}\right] \le |F(0)|\,P(Y > M_n)
 + E\!\left[|F(Y)|\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y > M_n\}}\right] < \tfrac{1}{n} + \tfrac{1}{n
} = \tfrac{2}{n}.$$

Combining:
$$|E[F(Y_n)] - E[F(Y)]| < \tfrac{1}{n} + \tfrac{2}{n} = \tfrac{3}{n} \xrightarro
w{n\to\infty} 0.$$

**Conclusion.** The simple functions $Y_n \ge 0$ constructed above satisfy both
$E(Y_n) \to E(Y)$ and $E(F(Y_n)) \to E(F(Y))$ simultaneously. The crucial idea i
s that on the tail $\{Y > M_n\}$ we set $Y_n = 0$, which makes the tail contribu
tion to $E|F(Y_n) - F(Y)|$ controlled by the fixed constant $|F(0)|$ (vanishing
via Markov) plus the integrability of $F(Y)$ — bypassing the lack of monotonicit
y of $F$.
