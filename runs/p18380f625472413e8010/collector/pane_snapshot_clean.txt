# Solution

## (a) Sketches of the covering spaces

Let $X = S^1 \vee S^2$ with wedge point $p$. Since $\pi_1(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}$ and
 $\pi_1(S^2) = 0$, the van Kampen theorem gives
$$\pi_1(X) \cong \mathbb{Z} * 0 \cong \mathbb{Z},$$
generated by the loop traversing $S^1$ once.

**Universal cover $\widetilde{X}$.** Since $\pi_1(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}$, the univer
sal cover is a regular cover with deck group $\mathbb{Z}$ (countably many sheets
). The universal cover of the $S^1$ factor is $\mathbb{R}\to S^1$. Since $S^2$ i
s simply connected, its preimage in any cover is a disjoint union of copies of $
S^2$, one per sheet. The wedge point $p$ lifts to every integer point on $\mathb
b{R}$, and at each such lift a single copy of $S^2$ is attached.

> **Picture:** A horizontal line $\mathbb{R}$ with a 2-sphere (drawn as a circle
/balloon) attached at each integer point $\ldots, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, \ldots$ The d
eck transformation $n\in\mathbb{Z}$ shifts the entire picture by $n$ units along
 the line.

**Connected 2-fold cover $Y$.** The connected 2-fold covers correspond to index-
2 subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}$. There is a unique one, namely $2\mathbb{Z}$, so the
re is a unique connected 2-fold cover. The 2-fold cover of the $S^1$ factor is $
S^1\to S^1$ (degree 2). The wedge point $p$ has two lifts $p_0, p_1$ on this cov
ering circle. The preimage of $S^2$ is two disjoint copies of $S^2$ (one per she
et), each attached at the corresponding lift of $p$.

> **Picture:** A circle with two 2-spheres (balloons) attached at two distinct p
oints $p_0$ and $p_1$ on the circle.

## (b) Homology of the connected 2-fold cover $Y$

The space $Y$ is a circle $S^1$ with a copy of $S^2$ attached at each of two dis
tinct points $p_0, p_1$.

**Homotopy type.** One of the two arcs of the circle from $p_0$ to $p_1$ can be
contracted to a point, yielding a wedge $S^1 \vee S^2 \vee S^2$. This contractio
n is a homotopy equivalence, so
$$Y \simeq S^1 \vee S^2 \vee S^2.$$

**Homology computation.** Give $Y$ a CW-structure: two 0-cells ($p_0, p_1$), two
 1-cells (the two arcs $a, b$ of the circle), and two 2-cells (the two spheres,
each attached at a single point). The cellular chain complex is
$$0 \longrightarrow \mathbb{Z}^2 \xrightarrow{\ \partial_2\ } \mathbb{Z}^2 \xrig
htarrow{\ \partial_1\ } \mathbb{Z}^2 \longrightarrow 0.$$

- **$\partial_1$:** Arc $a$ goes $p_0\to p_1$ and arc $b$ goes $p_1\to p_0$, so
$\partial_1(a) = p_1-p_0$, $\partial_1(b)=p_0-p_1$. Thus $\partial_1 = \begin{pm
atrix}-1 & 1\\ 1 & -1\end{pmatrix}$, $\operatorname{rank}(\partial_1)=1$, $\ker\
partial_1 = \langle(1,1)\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}$, $\operatorname{im}\partial_1=\l
angle(-1,1)\rangle$.

- **$\partial_2$:** Each 2-sphere is attached at a single point, so its cellular
 boundary is $0$. Hence $\partial_2 = 0$ and $\ker\partial_2 = \mathbb{Z}^2$.

Therefore:
$$H_0(Y) = \mathbb{Z}^2/\operatorname{im}\partial_1 \cong \mathbb{Z},$$
$$H_1(Y) = \ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2 = \mathbb{Z}/0 \cong \math
bb{Z},$$
$$H_2(Y) = \ker\partial_2/\operatorname{im}\partial_3 = \mathbb{Z}^2/0 \cong \ma
thbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z},$$
$$H_n(Y) = 0 \quad \text{for } n\geq 3.$$

**Summary.** The homology of the connected 2-fold cover of $S^1\vee S^2$ is:
$$\boxed{H_n(Y) = \begin{cases} \mathbb{Z} & n=0,\\ \mathbb{Z} & n=1,\\ \mathbb{
Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z} & n=2,\\ 0 & n\geq 3. \end{cases}}$$

This is consistent with the homotopy equivalence $Y\simeq S^1\vee S^2\vee S^2$,
whose homology is $\mathbb{Z}$ in degree 0, $\mathbb{Z}$ in degree 1 (one $S^1$)
, and $\mathbb{Z}^2$ in degree 2 (two $S^2$'s).
