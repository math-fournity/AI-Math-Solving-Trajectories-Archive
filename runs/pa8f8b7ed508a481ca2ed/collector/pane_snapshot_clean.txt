## Solution

**Goal:** Find the least positive integer $n$ such that $3^3 \cdot 5^5 \cdot 7^7
 \mid 149^n - 2^n$, then count its divisors.

**Key observation:** $149 - 2 = 147 = 3 \cdot 7^2$, so $149 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$ a
nd $149 \equiv 2 \pmod{7}$, but $149 \equiv 4 \pmod{5}$ (so $5 \nmid 149-2$).

---

### $p = 3$ part

Since $3 \mid 149 - 2$, $3 \nmid 149$, $3 \nmid 2$, by the **Lifting the Exponen
t Lemma (LTE)**:
$$v_3(149^n - 2^n) = v_3(149-2) + v_3(n) = 1 + v_3(n).$$
Requiring $\geq 3$: $v_3(n) \geq 2$, i.e. $\boxed{3^2 \mid n}$.

### $p = 7$ part

Since $7 \mid 149 - 2$, $7 \nmid 149$, $7 \nmid 2$, by LTE:
$$v_7(149^n - 2^n) = v_7(149-2) + v_7(n) = 2 + v_7(n).$$
Requiring $\geq 7$: $v_7(n) \geq 5$, i.e. $\boxed{7^5 \mid n}$.

### $p = 5$ part

Here $5 \nmid 149 - 2 = 147$, so LTE does not apply directly. Write
$$149^n - 2^n = 2^n\!\left(\left(\tfrac{149}{2}\right)^n - 1\right), \qquad r :=
 \tfrac{149}{2} \in \mathbb{Z}_5.$$
Since $5 \nmid 2$, $v_5(149^n - 2^n) = v_5(r^n - 1)$.

Compute $r \bmod 5$: $149 \equiv 4$, $2^{-1} \equiv 3 \pmod 5$, so $r \equiv 4\c
dot 3 = 12 \equiv 2 \pmod 5$.

The order of $2 \bmod 5$ is $4$ (since $2^1,2^2,2^3,2^4 \equiv 2,4,3,1$). So $\o
peratorname{ord}_5(r) = 4$, meaning $5 \mid r^n - 1 \iff 4 \mid n$.

Check the lift: $r \equiv 12 \pmod{25}$, so $r^4 \equiv 12^4 \pmod{25}$. Since $
12^2 = 144 \equiv 19 \pmod{25}$ and $19^2 = 361 \equiv 11 \pmod{25}$, we get $r^
4 \equiv 11 \not\equiv 1 \pmod{25}$. Hence $v_5(r^4 - 1) = 1$.

For $n = 4m$ (the only case with $5 \mid r^n-1$), apply LTE to $(r^4)^m - 1$ wit
h $p = 5$:
$$v_5(r^n - 1) = v_5\!\big((r^4)^m - 1\big) = v_5(r^4 - 1) + v_5(m) = 1 + v_5(m)
.$$
Requiring $\geq 5$: $v_5(m) \geq 4$, i.e. $5^4 \mid m$. Since $n = 4m$:
$$\boxed{2^2 \cdot 5^4 \mid n} \quad (\text{i.e. } 2500 \mid n).$$

---

### Combining

The three conditions are on coprime prime sets ($\{3\}$, $\{7\}$, $\{2,5\}$), so
 the least $n$ is
$$n = \operatorname{lcm}\!\big(3^2,\; 7^5,\; 2^2\cdot 5^4\big) = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 \
cdot 5^4 \cdot 7^5.$$

### Divisor count

$$d(n) = (2+1)(2+1)(4+1)(5+1) = 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 6 = \boxed{270}.$$
