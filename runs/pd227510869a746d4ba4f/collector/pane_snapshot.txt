Looking at this problem, I need to find four positive integers where every pair
shares a common factor, but no triple does.

## Setting up the structure

**Key observation:** The condition $\gcd(a_i, a_j, a_k) = 1$ for all triples mea
ns **no prime can divide three or more** of the four numbers. So every prime fac
tor divides **at most two** of $a_1, a_2, a_3, a_4$.

The condition $\gcd(a_i, a_j) > 1$ for all pairs means **each of the $\binom{4}{
2} = 6$ pairs must share at least one prime**. Since each prime divides at most
2 numbers (covering at most 1 pair), we need **at least 6 distinct primes**, eac
h assigned to exactly one pair.

**To minimize $a_4$, use exactly the 6 smallest primes** $\{2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13\}
$, each dividing exactly two of the four numbers, with no extra factors (any add
itional prime or higher power only increases values).

## The combinatorial optimization

Label the prime on pair $(i,j)$ as $p_{ij}$. Then:
$$a_1 = p_{12}p_{13}p_{14},\quad a_2 = p_{12}p_{23}p_{24},\quad a_3 = p_{13}p_{2
3}p_{34},\quad a_4 = p_{14}p_{24}p_{34}$$

This is an assignment of 6 primes to the 6 edges of $K_4$, where each vertex val
ue is the product of its 3 incident edge-primes. We minimize the maximum vertex
value.

**Structural fact:** The three pairs of **opposite edges** are $(12,34)$, $(13,2
4)$, $(14,23)$. For any assignment, the set of four vertex values depends only o
n how primes are **partitioned into opposite-edge pairs**. Specifically, if oppo
site-edge pairs get $(a,a')$, $(b,b')$, $(c,c')$, the four values are:
$$\{abc,\; ab'c',\; a'bc',\; a'b'c'\}$$
Note that $v_1 \cdot v_4 = v_2 \cdot v_3 = (aa')(bb')(cc') = 30030$ in all cases

## Verification of the optimal solution

## Minimality argument

1. **At least 6 primes are required** (one per pair, since each prime covers at
most one pair).
2. **The 6 smallest primes** $\{2,3,5,7,11,13\}$ give the smallest possible prod
ucts; any replacement with a larger prime strictly increases the geometric mean
$\sqrt{\prod p_i}$ and cannot reduce the maximum.
3. **Exhaustive enumeration** of all 15 partitions of these 6 primes into opposi
te-edge pairs shows the minimum achievable maximum is **231**.
4. **Extra primes or higher powers** only increase values without covering addit
ional pairs, so they cannot help.

Therefore, the smallest possible value of $a_4$ is $\boxed{231}$.
