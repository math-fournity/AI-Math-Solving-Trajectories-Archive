## Solution

**Key observation:** Three points P, P+d, P+2d (where d is the primitive directi
on) lie in the grid only if 2|d_i| ≤ 3 for each component, forcing |d_i| ≤ 1. Si
nce grid points on any line form a contiguous arithmetic progression (intermedia
te points between two grid points on a line are also in the grid), every colline
ar triple lies on a line with primitive direction in {-1,0,1}^3.

There are 26 non-zero such vectors, or **13 directions** up to sign, in three ty
pes:

### Type 1: Axis directions (1,0,0), (0,1,0), (0,0,1) — 3 directions

Each direction has 4×4 = 16 lines (fixing the other two coordinates). Each line
has 4 points, giving C(4,3) = 4 triples.

**Total: 3 × 16 × 4 = 192**

### Type 2: Face diagonals — 6 directions

For direction (1,1,0): lines lie in planes z = const (4 planes). In each plane,
lines are x−y = c:
- c = 0: 4 points → 4 triples
- c = ±1: 3 points each → 1 triple each
- c = ±2, ±3: fewer than 3 points

Per plane: 4 + 1 + 1 = 6 triples. Over 4 planes: **24 per direction**.

By symmetry, all 6 face diagonal directions give 24 each.

**Total: 6 × 24 = 144**

### Type 3: Space diagonals — 4 directions

For direction (1,1,1): lines parameterized by (c₁, c₂) = (x−y, x−z). A line has
n points where n = 3 − (max(0,c₁,c₂) − min(0,c₁,c₂)). For n ≥ 3, the spread of {
0, c₁, c₂} must be ≤ 1:

- (0,0): 4 points → C(4,3) = 4 triples (main diagonal)
- (1,0), (0,1), (1,1): 3 points each → 1 triple each
- (−1,0), (0,−1), (−1,−1): 3 points each → 1 triple each

**Total per direction: 4 + 6 = 10**

By symmetry, all 4 space diagonal directions give 10 each.

**Total: 4 × 10 = 40**
