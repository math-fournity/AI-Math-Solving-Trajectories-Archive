## Solution

**Decompose $f$ at the singularity.** Since $f$ is holomorphic on $\Omega = D(0,
2)\setminus\{\tfrac12\}$ and $\tfrac12$ is a simple pole with residue $1$, the p
rincipal part of the Laurent expansion at $z=\tfrac12$ is exactly $\dfrac{1}{z-1
/2}$. Define

$$g(z) = f(z) - \frac{1}{z-1/2}.$$

Then $g$ is holomorphic on all of $D(0,2)$ (the singularity at $\tfrac12$ is rem
ovable), so $g$ admits a Taylor expansion

$$g(z) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} a_k\, z^k$$

with radius of convergence $R \ge 2$.

**Compute the derivatives of the principal part.** Let $h(z) = \dfrac{1}{z-1/2}
= (z-\tfrac12)^{-1}$. Then

$$h^{(n)}(z) = (-1)^n\, n!\, (z-\tfrac12)^{-(n+1)}.$$

Evaluating at $z=0$:

$$h^{(n)}(0) = (-1)^n\, n!\, \left(-\tfrac12\right)^{-(n+1)} = (-1)^n\, n!\, (-1
)^{n+1}\, 2^{n+1} = (-1)^{2n+1}\, n!\, 2^{n+1} = -\,2^{n+1}\, n!.$$

**Combine.** Since $f = g + h$,

$$\frac{f^{(n)}(0)}{2^n\, n!} = \frac{g^{(n)}(0)}{2^n\, n!} + \frac{h^{(n)}(0)}{
2^n\, n!} = \frac{a_n}{2^n} \;-\; 2.$$

**Show $a_n/2^n \to 0$.** The radius of convergence of $g$ satisfies $R \ge 2$,
so by Cauchy–Hadamard,

$$\limsup_{n\to\infty} |a_n|^{1/n} = \frac{1}{R} \le \frac{1}{2}.$$

Hence

$$\limsup_{n\to\infty} \left|\frac{a_n}{2^n}\right|^{1/n} = \frac{1}{R}\cdot\fra
c{1}{2} \le \frac{1}{4} < 1.$$

By the root test, $\sum |a_n/2^n|$ converges, so in particular $a_n/2^n \to 0$.

**Conclusion.**

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{f^{(n)}(0)}{2^n\, n!} = 0 - 2 = \boxed{-2}.$$
