# Proof

**Claim:** Yes. The product measure $\otimes_n \mu_n$ is the unique probability
and right-invariant Haar measure on $\prod_n G_n$ equipped with the product $\si
gma$-algebra.

---

## Step 1. The product is a compact metrizable group.

Each $G_n$ is compact metrizable, hence second-countable. The countable product
$G := \prod_{n} G_n$ with componentwise multiplication $(g_n)(h_n) = (g_n h_n)$
and the product topology is:

- **Compact** by Tychonoff's theorem.
- **Metrizable** because a countable product of metrizable (equivalently, second
-countable) spaces is metrizable.

So $G$ is a compact metrizable (hence second-countable) topological group.

## Step 2. The product $\sigma$-algebra coincides with the Borel $\sigma$-algebr
a of $G$.

For a countable product of second-countable topological spaces, the product $\si
gma$-algebra (generated by coordinate projections) equals the Borel $\sigma$-alg
ebra of the product topology. This is standard: each coordinate projection is co
ntinuous (hence Borel measurable), so the product $\sigma$-algebra $\subseteq$ B
orel $\sigma$-algebra; conversely, a countable base for the product topology is
built from cylinder sets over basic opens, so every open set is in the product $
\sigma$-algebra, giving Borel $\sigma$-algebra $\subseteq$ product $\sigma$-alge
bra.

Henceforth we work with the Borel $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{B}(G)$, which is th
e product $\sigma$-algebra.

## Step 3. The product measure $\otimes_n \mu_n$ is a probability measure on $G$
.

Each $\mu_n$ is a probability measure, so $\mu := \otimes_n \mu_n$ is a probabil
ity measure on the product $\sigma$-algebra by the standard construction of prod
uct measures (Kolmogorov's extension theorem / Ionescu-Tulcea).

## Step 4. The product measure is right-invariant.

Fix $h = (h_n) \in G$. Right translation $R_h: G \to G$, $R_h(g) = gh$, is a hom
eomorphism (hence Borel measurable). We show $\mu(R_h^{-1}(B)) = \mu(B)$ for all
 $B \in \mathcal{B}(G)$.

**On cylinder sets:** A cylinder set has the form $C = \prod_n A_n$ with $A_n \i
n \mathcal{B}(G_n)$ and $A_n = G_n$ for all but finitely many $n$. Then:

$$R_h^{-1}(C) = \prod_n A_n h_n^{-1},$$

and since each $\mu_n$ is right-invariant, $\mu_n(A_n h_n^{-1}) = \mu_n(A_n)$. T
herefore:

$$\mu(R_h^{-1}(C)) = \prod_n \mu_n(A_n h_n^{-1}) = \prod_n \mu_n(A_n) = \mu(C).$
$

**Extension to all of $\mathcal{B}(G)$:** The cylinder sets of the form $\pi_k^{
-1}(A)$ (single-coordinate cylinders, $A \in \mathcal{B}(G_k)$) generate $\mathc
al{B}(G)$ and form a $\pi$-system. Define:

$$\mathcal{D} = \{B \in \mathcal{B}(G) : \mu(R_h^{-1}(B)) = \mu(B)\}.$$

One checks $\mathcal{D}$ is a $\lambda$-system (Dynkin system): it contains $G$,
 is closed under complementation (since $R_h^{-1}(G \setminus B) = G \setminus R
_h^{-1}(B)$ and $\mu$ is a probability), and closed under countable disjoint uni
ons. Since $\mathcal{D}$ contains the $\pi$-system of single-coordinate cylinder
s, Dynkin's $\pi$-$\lambda$ theorem gives $\mathcal{D} \supseteq \sigma(\text{cy
linders}) = \mathcal{B}(G)$.

Thus $\mu$ is right-invariant under every $h \in G$.

## Step 5. Uniqueness of probability right-invariant Haar measure on a compact g
roup.

On any compact topological group, there exists a unique regular Borel probabilit
y measure that is left-invariant (equivalently, right-invariant, since compact g
roups are unimodular). This is the classical Haar theorem: existence and uniquen
ess of Haar measure on locally compact groups, specialized to the compact case w
here the Haar measure is a probability and left-invariance coincides with right-
invariance.

Since $G$ is compact, its (right-invariant) Haar measure is the unique probabili
ty right-invariant Borel measure.

## Step 6. Conclusion.

We have shown:
- $\mu = \otimes_n \mu_n$ is a probability measure on $(G, \mathcal{B}(G))$ (Ste
p 3),
- $\mu$ is right-invariant (Step 4),
- the product $\sigma$-algebra is $\mathcal{B}(G)$ (Step 2),
- and any probability right-invariant Borel measure on the compact group $G$ is
unique (Step 5).

Therefore $\otimes_n \mu_n$ **is** the unique probability and right-invariant Ha
ar measure on $\prod_n G_n$ with the product $\sigma$-algebra. $\blacksquare$
