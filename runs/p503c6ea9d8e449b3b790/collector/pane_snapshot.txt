## Proof

**Claim.** Yes. If $X$ and $Y$ are random variables with the same characteristic
 function, then $X$ and $Y$ are identically distributed.

Let $\mu_X, \mu_Y$ denote the distributions of $X, Y$ on $\mathbb{R}$, with dist
ribution functions $F_X, F_Y$, and characteristic functions
$$\varphi_X(t) = \mathbb{E}[e^{itX}] = \int_{\mathbb{R}} e^{itx}\,d\mu_X(x), \qq
uad \varphi_Y(t) = \mathbb{E}[e^{itY}].$$
The hypothesis is $\varphi_X(t) = \varphi_Y(t)$ for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$.

### Step 1. The Lévy inversion formula

For any probability measure $\mu$ on $\mathbb{R}$ with characteristic function $
\varphi$, and any $a < b$, the Lévy inversion theorem gives
$$
\mu\big((a,b)\big) + \tfrac{1}{2}\mu(\{a\}) + \tfrac{1}{2}\mu(\{b\})
= \lim_{T \to \infty} \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-T}^{T} \frac{e^{-ita} - e^{-itb}}{it}
\,\varphi(t)\,dt,
$$
where the integrand at $t = 0$ is interpreted by continuity as $b - a$.

In particular, if $a$ and $b$ are **continuity points** of $\mu$ (i.e. $\mu(\{a\
}) = \mu(\{b\}) = 0$), then
$$
\mu\big((a,b)\big) = \lim_{T \to \infty} \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-T}^{T} \frac{e^{-i
ta} - e^{-itb}}{it}\,\varphi(t)\,dt. \tag{$\ast$}
$$

### Step 2. Equality of measures on intervals bounded by common continuity point
s

Let $a < b$ be continuity points of **both** $\mu_X$ and $\mu_Y$. Applying $(\as
t)$ to $\mu_X$ and to $\mu_Y$, the right-hand side depends only on $\varphi$ (an
d on $a, b$). Since $\varphi_X = \varphi_Y$, the two right-hand sides coincide,
hence
$$
\mu_X\big((a,b)\big) = \mu_Y\big((a,b)\big). \tag{1}
$$

### Step 3. The set of common continuity points is dense

A distribution function is monotone non-decreasing, so it has at most countably
many discontinuities. Thus the discontinuity sets $D_X, D_Y$ of $F_X, F_Y$ are e
ach at most countable, and so is $D := D_X \cup D_Y$. Its complement $C := \math
bb{R} \setminus D$ — the set of common continuity points — is dense in $\mathbb{
R}$.

### Step 4. Equality of distribution functions at all common continuity points

Let $x \in C$. Choose a sequence $a_n \in C$ with $a_n \to -\infty$ and $a_n < x
$ (possible since $C$ is dense and unbounded below). Then
$$
F_X(x) = \mu_X\big((-\infty, x]\big) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \mu_X\big((a_n, x)\bi
g),
$$
using that $x \in C_X$ so $\mu_X(\{x\}) = 0$, and $\mu_X((a_n, x]) = \mu_X((a_n,
 x))$. By (1), $\mu_X((a_n, x)) = \mu_Y((a_n, x))$ for every $n$, so
$$
F_X(x) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \mu_Y\big((a_n, x)\big) = F_Y(x).
$$
Thus
$$
F_X(x) = F_Y(x) \quad \text{for all } x \in C. \tag{2}
$$

### Step 5. Equality everywhere

$F_X$ and $F_Y$ are right-continuous, non-decreasing functions agreeing on the d
ense set $C$. For any $x \in \mathbb{R}$, take a sequence $x_n \in C$ with $x_n
\downarrow x$. By right-continuity and (2),
$$
F_X(x) = \lim_{n \to \infty} F_X(x_n) = \lim_{n \to \infty} F_Y(x_n) = F_Y(x).
$$
Hence $F_X \equiv F_Y$ on all of $\mathbb{R}$, i.e. $\mu_X = \mu_Y$.

### Conclusion

The characteristic function uniquely determines the distribution. Therefore $X$
and $Y$ are identically distributed. $\blacksquare$
