## Analysis of the Integrand

For fixed $x$:
- **$x \in [0,1)$**: $x^n \to 0$, so $\frac{x^{n-2}}{1+x^n} \sim x^{n-2} \to 0$
(for $n>2$).
- **$x = 1$**: value $= \frac{1}{2}\cos(n\pi) = \frac{(-1)^n}{2}$ (a single poin
t, measure zero).
- **$x > 1$**: $x^n \to \infty$, so $\frac{x^{n-2}}{1+x^n} \to \frac{1}{x^2}$, b
ut $\cos(n\pi x)$ oscillates rapidly.

This suggests splitting the integral at $x=1$ and using the Riemann–Lebesgue lem
ma for the tail.

## Proof

**Step 1: Split the integral.**

$$I_n = \int_0^1 \frac{x^{n-2}}{1+x^n}\cos(n\pi x)\,dx + \int_1^\infty \frac{x^{
n-2}}{1+x^n}\cos(n\pi x)\,dx =: A_n + B_n.$$

**Step 2: Show $A_n \to 0$.**

For $n \geq 3$ and $x \in [0,1]$, we have $\frac{x^{n-2}}{1+x^n} \leq x^{n-2}$.
Therefore

$$|A_n| \leq \int_0^1 \frac{x^{n-2}}{1+x^n}\,dx \leq \int_0^1 x^{n-2}\,dx = \fra
c{1}{n-1} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.$$

**Step 3: Decompose $B_n$.**

For $x > 0$, write the algebraic identity:

$$\frac{x^{n-2}}{1+x^n} = \frac{1}{x^2}\cdot\frac{x^n}{1+x^n} = \frac{1}{x^2} -
\frac{1}{x^2(1+x^n)}.$$

Hence

$$B_n = \underbrace{\int_1^\infty \frac{\cos(n\pi x)}{x^2}\,dx}_{=: C_n} \;-\; \
underbrace{\int_1^\infty \frac{\cos(n\pi x)}{x^2(1+x^n)}\,dx}_{=: D_n}.$$

**Step 4: Show $D_n \to 0$.**

For $x \geq 1$ and $n \geq 1$, $1 + x^n \geq x^n$, so $\frac{1}{x^2(1+x^n)} \leq
 \frac{1}{x^{n+2}}$. Thus

$$|D_n| \leq \int_1^\infty \frac{dx}{x^{n+2}} = \frac{1}{n+1} \xrightarrow{n\to\
infty} 0.$$

**Step 5: Show $C_n \to 0$ via the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma.**

The function $g(x) = \frac{1}{x^2}\mathbf{1}_{[1,\infty)}(x)$ belongs to $L^1(\m
athbb{R})$, since $\int_1^\infty x^{-2}\,dx = 1 < \infty$.

The Riemann–Lebesgue lemma (valid for $L^1$ functions on $\mathbb{R}$, hence als
o on $[1,\infty)$ by zero-extension) states that for any $f \in L^1$,

$$\int f(x)\cos(\omega x)\,dx \to 0 \quad \text{as } \omega \to \infty.$$

Applying this with $f = g$ and $\omega = n\pi \to \infty$:

$$C_n = \int_1^\infty \frac{\cos(n\pi x)}{x^2}\,dx \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.$$

*(Detailed justification for the half-line: For any $\varepsilon > 0$, choose $M
$ large enough that $\int_M^\infty x^{-2}\,dx < \varepsilon/2$. Then $|\int_M^\i
nfty \frac{\cos(n\pi x)}{x^2}dx| \leq \varepsilon/2$. On the finite interval $[1
,M]$, the classical Riemann–Lebesgue lemma gives $\int_1^M \frac{\cos(n\pi x)}{x
^2}dx \to 0$, so for $n$ large this is $< \varepsilon/2$. Combining, $|C_n| < \v
arepsilon$.)*

**Step 6: Combine.**

$$I_n = A_n + C_n - D_n,$$

where $A_n \to 0$, $C_n \to 0$, and $D_n \to 0$. Therefore

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} I_n = 0.$$
