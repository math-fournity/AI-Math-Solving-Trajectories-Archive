## Proof

**Claim.** Let $A$ be a PID and $a \in A$. The ideal $\displaystyle P := \bigcap
_{n \geq 1} a^{n}A$ is a prime ideal of $A$ if and only if $a$ is **not a unit**
 of $A$.

---

### Preliminaries

A PID is in particular an integral domain and a UFD. Recall that an ideal $P$ is
 prime iff $P$ is proper ($P \neq A$) and $A/P$ is an integral domain. In partic
ular, the zero ideal $(0)$ is prime in any integral domain, hence in any PID.

---

### Case 1: $a$ is a unit

If $a \in A^{\times}$, then $a^{n} \in A^{\times}$ for every $n \geq 1$, so $a^{
n}A = A$ for all $n$. Hence
$$P = \bigcap_{n \geq 1} A = A.$$
Since $A$ is not a proper ideal, it is **not** a prime ideal.

---

### Case 2: $a$ is not a unit

We show $P = (0)$, which is prime since $A$ is an integral domain.

**Sub-case 2a: $a = 0$.** Then $a^{n} = 0$ for all $n \geq 1$, so $a^{n}A = \{0\
}$ for all $n$, giving $P = \{0\} = (0)$. ✓

**Sub-case 2b: $a \neq 0$ and $a$ is a non-unit.** Since $A$ is a UFD and $a \ne
q 0$, we may write
$$a = u\, p_{1}^{e_{1}} \cdots p_{k}^{e_{k}}, \qquad u \in A^{\times},\ e_{i} \g
eq 1,$$
where the $p_{i}$ are pairwise non-associate primes. Because $a$ is a non-unit,
$k \geq 1$, i.e., $a$ has at least one prime factor.

The containment is clear: $(0) \subseteq a^{n}A$ for every $n$, so $(0) \subsete
q P$.

For the reverse inclusion, let $x \in P$. Then for every $n \geq 1$ there exists
 $b_{n} \in A$ with
$$x = a^{n} b_{n}.$$

Suppose for contradiction that $x \neq 0$. Since $A$ is a UFD, every nonzero ele
ment has a well-defined discrete valuation $v_{p}( \cdot )$ at each prime $p$. F
rom $x = a^{n} b_{n}$ we get, for every prime $p$ and every $n \geq 1$:
$$v_{p}(x) = n\, v_{p}(a) + v_{p}(b_{n}) \geq n\, v_{p}(a).$$

Pick any prime factor $p_{j}$ of $a$ (which exists since $k \geq 1$). Then $v_{p
_{j}}(a) = e_{j} \geq 1$, so
$$v_{p_{j}}(x) \geq n\, e_{j} \geq n \qquad \text{for all } n \geq 1.$$

But $v_{p_{j}}(x)$ is a fixed non-negative integer (finite, since $x \neq 0$), a
nd it cannot be $\geq n$ for arbitrarily large $n$. This is a contradiction.

Therefore $x = 0$, and we conclude $P = (0)$.

Since $A$ is an integral domain, $(0)$ is a prime ideal. ✓

---

### Conclusion

Combining both cases:

$$\bigcap_{n \geq 1} a^{n}A \text{ is a prime ideal} \iff a \notin A^{\times} \t
ext{ (i.e., } a \text{ is a non-unit).}$$

When $a$ is a non-unit, the intersection equals $(0)$, the zero ideal, which is
prime in the integral domain $A$. When $a$ is a unit, the intersection equals $A
$ itself, which is not a proper (hence not a prime) ideal.
