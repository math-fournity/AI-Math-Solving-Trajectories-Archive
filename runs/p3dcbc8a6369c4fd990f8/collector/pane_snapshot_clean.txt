Looking at this problem, I need to determine the correct dual pairing for $f \in
 L^2((0,1))$ with $f/x \in L^2((0,1))$ acting on $V = \{u \in H^1((0,1)) : u(0)=
0\}$.

**Answer: Option 2 is correct.** $\langle f, v\rangle_{V^*,V} = \int_0^1 fv\,dx$
.

---

## Proof

### Step 1: The Gelfand triple structure

The natural setting is the Gelfand (evolution) triple
$$V \hookrightarrow H \hookrightarrow V^*, \qquad H = L^2((0,1)),$$
where $V$ is densely and continuously embedded in $H$, and $H$ is identified wit
h its dual $H^* \cong H$ via the Riesz map. The **dual pairing** $\langle\cdot,\
cdot\rangle_{V^*,V}$ is *defined* to extend the $L^2$ inner product: for any $f
\in H = L^2((0,1))$ and $v \in V$,
$$\langle f, v\rangle_{V^*,V} = (f, v)_{L^2} = \int_0^1 f(x)\,v(x)\,dx.$$
This is the standard convention: the pivot space $H = L^2$ mediates the duality,
 and the dual pairing is the extension of the $L^2$ inner product from $V\times
V$ to $V^*\times V$.

### Step 2: $V \hookrightarrow L^2$ is continuous

We verify the embedding so that $L^2$ functions indeed define bounded functional
s on $V$.

For $v \in V$, since $v(0)=0$, we have $v(x) = \int_0^x v'(t)\,dt$. By Cauchy–Sc
hwarz,
$$|v(x)| \le \sqrt{x}\,\|v'\|_{L^2}.$$
Squaring and integrating:
$$\|v\|_{L^2}^2 = \int_0^1 |v(x)|^2\,dx \le \int_0^1 x\,\|v'\|_{L^2}^2\,dx = \tf
rac{1}{2}\|v'\|_{L^2}^2 \le \tfrac{1}{2}\|v\|_V^2.$$
Hence $\|v\|_{L^2} \le \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\|v\|_V$, confirming $V\hookrightarrow
L^2$ continuously. Therefore every $f\in L^2$ defines a bounded linear functiona
l $v\mapsto \int_0^1 fv\,dx$ on $V$, i.e., $f \in V^*$ with $\|f\|_{V^*}\le \fra
c{1}{\sqrt{2}}\|f\|_{L^2}$.

### Step 3: Why Option 1 is incorrect

Option 1, $\int_0^1 \frac{f}{x}\frac{v}{x}\,dx$, is indeed a bounded linear func
tional on $V$ (by Cauchy–Schwarz: $\|f/x\|_{L^2}\|v/x\|_{L^2}\le \|f/x\|_{L^2}\|
v\|_V$), so it defines *some* element of $V^*$. However, it is **not** the dual
pairing of $f$ with $v$.

The expression $\int \frac{f}{x}\frac{v}{x}\,dx$ is the $L^2$-pairing of $f/x$ w
ith $v/x$. It corresponds to the functional associated to $f/x$ acting on $v/x$
— not the functional associated to $f$ acting on $v$. Conflating these would amo
unt to identifying $f$ with $f/x$ as elements of $V^*$, which is incorrect: they
 are different functionals on $V$.

Concretely, take $f(x) = x$. Then $f \in L^2$ and $f/x = 1 \in L^2$.
- Option 2 gives $\int_0^1 x\,v\,dx$.
- Option 1 gives $\int_0^1 \frac{1}{x}\frac{v}{x}\,dx = \int_0^1 \frac{v}{x^2}\,
dx$.

These are clearly different functionals (e.g., test with $v(x)=x$: Option 2 give
s $\int_0^1 x^2\,dx = 1/3$, Option 1 gives $\int_0^1 1/x\,dx = +\infty$). Since
$f(x)=x$ is an $L^2$ function, its action on $V$ as a distribution is $\int xv\,
dx$, confirming Option 2.

### Step 4: Role of the condition $f/x \in L^2$

The condition $f/x\in L^2$ is a **sufficient condition** ensuring $f$ belongs to
 a subspace of $V^*$ with additional structure (for instance, it guarantees that
 the Riesz representative $u_f\in V$ satisfying $(u_f,v)_V = \int fv\,dx$ has ex
tra regularity, or that certain variational problems are well-posed). It does **
not** redefine the dual pairing. The dual pairing is determined by the Gelfand t
riple structure (the pivot space $L^2$), not by auxiliary integrability conditio
ns on $f$.

### Conclusion

The correct dual pairing is **Option 2**:
$$\boxed{\langle f, v\rangle_{V^*,V} = \int_0^1 f(x)\,v(x)\,dx.}$$

This follows from the standard Gelfand triple $V\hookrightarrow L^2\hookrightarr
ow V^*$, where the dual pairing extends the $L^2$ inner product. The condition $
f/x\in L^2$ provides additional regularity but does not alter the canonical dual
ity.
