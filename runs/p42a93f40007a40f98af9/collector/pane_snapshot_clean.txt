## Definition of Uniform Convergence for a Multivariable Map

Let $\vec{x} \in \mathbb{R}^m$, $\vec{y} \in \mathbb{R}^n$, and let $F(\vec{x},\
vec{y})$ be a map defined on a punctured neighborhood of $(\vec{a},\vec{b})$ wit
h values in $\mathbb{R}^p$.

**Definition (Uniform convergence as $\vec{y}\to\vec{b}$).** We say $F(\vec{x},\
vec{y})$ **converges uniformly** (with respect to $\vec{x}$) to a limit function
 $\Phi(\vec{x})$ as $\vec{y}\to\vec{b}$ if:

$$\forall\,\varepsilon>0,\;\exists\,\delta>0:\;0<\|\vec{y}-\vec{b}\|<\delta \;\L
ongrightarrow\; \|F(\vec{x},\vec{y})-\Phi(\vec{x})\|<\varepsilon \;\text{ for al
l }\vec{x}\text{ in some punctured neighborhood of }\vec{a}.$$

The key distinction from pointwise convergence is that $\delta$ depends **only**
 on $\varepsilon$, **not** on $\vec{x}$.

Symmetrically, $F(\vec{x},\vec{y})$ converges uniformly (with respect to $\vec{y
}$) to $\Psi(\vec{y})$ as $\vec{x}\to\vec{a}$ if the analogous condition holds w
ith the roles of $\vec{x}$ and $\vec{y}$ interchanged.

## Conditions for Equality of the Repeated Limits

**Theorem.** Let $F(\vec{x},\vec{y})$ be defined on $U = \bigl((\dot{B}_r(\vec{a
}))\times(\dot{B}_s(\vec{b}))\bigr)\setminus\{(\vec{a},\vec{b})\}$. Suppose:

1. **(Pointwise inner limits exist)** For every $\vec{x}\in\dot{B}_r(\vec{a})$,
the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{\vec{y}\to\vec{b}}F(\vec{x},\vec{y})=\varphi(\vec{
x})$ exists, and for every $\vec{y}\in\dot{B}_s(\vec{b})$, the limit $\displayst
yle\lim_{\vec{x}\to\vec{a}}F(\vec{x},\vec{y})=\psi(\vec{y})$ exists.

2. **(Uniform convergence of one inner limit)** One of the two inner limits conv
erges uniformly. Concretely, suppose $F(\vec{x},\vec{y})\to\varphi(\vec{x})$ **u
niformly** in $\vec{x}$ as $\vec{y}\to\vec{b}$.

3. **(Outer limit exists)** The outer limit $\displaystyle\lim_{\vec{x}\to\vec{a
}}\varphi(\vec{x})=L$ exists.

Then:

(a) The other outer limit also exists and equals $L$:
$$\lim_{\vec{y}\to\vec{b}}\psi(\vec{y})=L.$$

(b) Consequently the two repeated limits exist and are **equal**:
$$\lim_{\vec{x}\to\vec{a}}\lim_{\vec{y}\to\vec{b}}F(\vec{x},\vec{y}) \;=\; \lim_
{\vec{y}\to\vec{b}}\lim_{\vec{x}\to\vec{a}}F(\vec{x},\vec{y}) \;=\; L.$$

---

## Proof

**Step 1: Show $\lim_{\vec{y}\to\vec{b}}\psi(\vec{y})=L$.**

Let $\varepsilon>0$. By the uniform convergence of $F(\vec{x},\vec{y})\to\varphi
(\vec{x})$ as $\vec{y}\to\vec{b}$, there exists $\delta_1>0$ such that for all $
\vec{y}$ with $0<\|\vec{y}-\vec{b}\|<\delta_1$ and **all** $\vec{x}\in\dot{B}_r(
\vec{a})$:
$$\|F(\vec{x},\vec{y})-\varphi(\vec{x})\|<\varepsilon/3. \tag{1}$$

Fix any such $\vec{y}$ (with $0<\|\vec{y}-\vec{b}\|<\delta_1$). Since $\lim_{\ve
c{x}\to\vec{a}}\varphi(\vec{x})=L$, there exists $\delta_2>0$ such that $0<\|\ve
c{x}-\vec{a}\|<\delta_2$ implies $\|\varphi(\vec{x})-L\|<\varepsilon/3$. Pick on
e such $\vec{x}_0$ with $0<\|\vec{x}_0-\vec{a}\|<\min(\delta_2, r)$. Then:
$$\|\varphi(\vec{x}_0)-L\|<\varepsilon/3. \tag{2}$$

Since $\psi(\vec{y})=\lim_{\vec{x}\to\vec{a}}F(\vec{x},\vec{y})$, by taking the
limit $\vec{x}\to\vec{a}$ in inequality (1) (which holds for all $\vec{x}$ in th
e punctured ball, so the limit preserves the bound):
$$\|\psi(\vec{y})-\varphi(\vec{x}_0)\|\leq\varepsilon/3. \tag{3}$$
More carefully: from (1), $\|F(\vec{x},\vec{y})-\varphi(\vec{x})\|<\varepsilon/3
$ for all $\vec{x}$ near $\vec{a}$. Taking $\vec{x}\to\vec{a}$, the left side te
nds to $\|\psi(\vec{y})-L\|$ only if $\varphi(\vec{x})\to L$ simultaneously. Let
 us instead estimate directly:

For any $\vec{x}$ with $0<\|\vec{x}-\vec{a}\|<\min(\delta_2,r)$:
$$\|\psi(\vec{y})-L\|\leq\|\psi(\vec{y})-F(\vec{x},\vec{y})\|+\|F(\vec{x},\vec{y
})-\varphi(\vec{x})\|+\|\varphi(\vec{x})-L\|.$$

The second term is $<\varepsilon/3$ by (1), the third is $<\varepsilon/3$ by cho
ice of $\vec{x}$. For the first term: since $\psi(\vec{y})=\lim_{\vec{x}'\to\vec
{a}}F(\vec{x}',\vec{y})$, for fixed $\vec{y}$ and any $\eta>0$ there exists $\de
lta_3>0$ such that $0<\|\vec{x}'-\vec{a}\|<\delta_3$ implies $\|F(\vec{x}',\vec{
y})-\psi(\vec{y})\|<\eta$. Choose $\vec{x}$ satisfying both $0<\|\vec{x}-\vec{a}
\|<\min(\delta_2,\delta_3,r)$ and $\eta=\varepsilon/3$. Then:
$$\|\psi(\vec{y})-L\|<\varepsilon/3+\varepsilon/3+\varepsilon/3=\varepsilon.$$

This holds for every $\vec{y}$ with $0<\|\vec{y}-\vec{b}\|<\delta_1$. Therefore:
$$\lim_{\vec{y}\to\vec{b}}\psi(\vec{y})=L. \tag{4}$$

**Step 2: Conclude equality of the repeated limits.**

By assumption, $\displaystyle\lim_{\vec{x}\to\vec{a}}\varphi(\vec{x})=\lim_{\vec
{x}\to\vec{a}}\lim_{\vec{y}\to\vec{b}}F(\vec{x},\vec{y})=L$.

By Step 1, $\displaystyle\lim_{\vec{y}\to\vec{b}}\psi(\vec{y})=\lim_{\vec{y}\to\
vec{b}}\lim_{\vec{x}\to\vec{a}}F(\vec{x},\vec{y})=L$.

Hence both repeated limits exist and are equal to $L$. $\blacksquare$

## Summary of the Sufficient Conditions

The repeated limits $\displaystyle\lim_{\vec{x}\to\vec{a}}\lim_{\vec{y}\to\vec{b
}}F(\vec{x},\vec{y})$ and $\displaystyle\lim_{\vec{y}\to\vec{b}}\lim_{\vec{x}\to
\vec{a}}F(\vec{x},\vec{y})$ are equal provided:

1. Both **inner** (pointwise) limits exist in a punctured neighborhood of the re
spective limit point.
2. **At least one** of these inner limits converges **uniformly** with respect t
o the other variable.
3. The corresponding **outer** limit exists.

Under these three conditions, the other outer limit automatically exists, and th
e two repeated limits coincide. Uniform convergence is the essential hypothesis:
 it ensures the order of passing to the limit can be interchanged, because the r
ate of convergence no longer depends on the variable being held fixed.
