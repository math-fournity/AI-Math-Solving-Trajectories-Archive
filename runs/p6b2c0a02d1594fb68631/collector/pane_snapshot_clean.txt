# Solution

## Setup and Key Observations

We have a nested sequence $U_1 \subset U_2 \subset \cdots \subset X$ with $X = \
bigcup_i U_i$, where each inclusion-induced map $f_i: H_1(U_i) \to H_1(U_{i+1})$
 is **injective but not surjective**, and $H_1(X)$ is **not finitely generated**
.

**Fact 1 (New generator at each step).** Since $f_i$ is injective, $H_1(U_i)$ em
beds into $H_1(U_{i+1})$. Since $f_i$ is *not surjective*, $H_1(U_{i+1})$ strict
ly contains $\operatorname{im}(f_i) \cong H_1(U_i)$. Therefore $H_1(U_{i+1})$ ha
s at least one generator not in the image of $H_1(U_i)$ — a "new generator" — at
 **every** step $i$. This condition is automatically satisfied by the hypothesis
.

**Fact 2 (Finite chains give finitely generated $H_1$).** Suppose there are only
 finitely many subspaces, say $U_1 \subset \cdots \subset U_n = X$. Then $H_1(X)
 = H_1(U_n)$. Starting from $H_1(U_1)$ (finitely generated, as $U_1$ is a reason
able open subspace) and adding at least one but finitely many new generators at
each of the $n-1$ steps, $H_1(U_n)$ is built from finitely many generators. Thus
 $H_1(X)$ **would be finitely generated** — contradicting the hypothesis.

## The Minimum Number

Since:
- Each step contributes at least one new generator (by injective-but-not-surject
ive).
- Finitely many steps yield finitely many total generators, making $H_1(X)$ fini
tely generated — a contradiction.
- To make $H_1(X)$ **not** finitely generated, we need **infinitely many** new g
enerators to accumulate, hence **infinitely many steps**.

The smallest infinite cardinal is $\aleph_0$ (countably infinite). A countably i
nfinite chain $U_1 \subset U_2 \subset \cdots$ suffices: each step adds at least
 one new generator, and over countably many steps the generators accumulate with
out bound, yielding $H_1(X) = \varinjlim H_1(U_i)$ that is not finitely generate
d.

## Answer

The minimum number of subspaces required is **countably infinitely many**, i.e.,
 $\boxed{\aleph_0}$.
