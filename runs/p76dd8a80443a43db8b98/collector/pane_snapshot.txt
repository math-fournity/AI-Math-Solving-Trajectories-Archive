**Proof.**

The Borsuk–Ulam theorem states: for any continuous map $g : S^n \to \mathbb{R}^n
$, there exists a point $x \in S^n$ such that $g(x) = g(-x)$.

We are given $f : S^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$ with $f(-x) = -f(x)$ for all $x \in S^n$
, i.e., $f$ is an **odd** (antipodal) function.

**Regularity note.** The Borsuk–Ulam theorem requires $f$ to be continuous. The
problem statement says "function"; applying Borsuk–Ulam presupposes (and the sta
ndard formulation of this result assumes) that $f$ is continuous. We proceed und
er this standard assumption.

Apply the Borsuk–Ulam theorem to $f$. Then there exists a point $y \in S^n$ such
 that

$$f(y) = f(-y).$$

But by the antipodal property given in the hypothesis,

$$f(-y) = -f(y).$$

Combining these two equalities:

$$f(y) = -f(y),$$

hence

$$2f(y) = 0,$$

so

$$f(y) = 0.$$

Therefore there exists a point $y \in S^n$ such that $f(y) = 0$. $\blacksquare$
