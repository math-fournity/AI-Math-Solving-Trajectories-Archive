Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a single compact fiber of a
 holomorphic map between Hausdorff complex manifolds guarantees local properness
.

**Answer: No.**

**Counterexample.**

Let $X = S = \mathbb{C}$, and define $f: \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$ by $f(z) = z
 \cdot e^z$. This is an entire (holomorphic) function, and $\mathbb{C}$ is a Hau
sdorff complex manifold.

Take $s = 0$. Then:
$$f^{-1}(0) = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : z\, e^z = 0\} = \{0\},$$
since $e^z \neq 0$ for all $z \in \mathbb{C}$. This is a single point, hence **c
ompact** (and non-empty).

**Claim:** $f$ is not proper over any open neighborhood $U$ of $0$.

Let $U$ be any open neighborhood of $0$ in $\mathbb{C}$. Then $U$ contains a clo
sed disk $D_\varepsilon = \{w \in \mathbb{C} : |w| \le \varepsilon\}$ for some $
\varepsilon > 0$, which is compact.

Consider the preimage:
$$f^{-1}(D_\varepsilon) = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z\, e^z| \le \varepsilon\}.$$

This set is closed (as the preimage of a closed set under a continuous map). We
show it is **unbounded**, hence not compact (by Heine–Borel, compact subsets of
$\mathbb{C}$ are closed and bounded).

For $t > 0$ real, set $z = -t$. Then:
$$|f(-t)| = |-t \cdot e^{-t}| = t\, e^{-t} \xrightarrow{t \to +\infty} 0.$$

So there exists $T > 0$ such that $t\, e^{-t} \le \varepsilon$ for all $t \ge T$
. Therefore $\{-t : t \ge T\} \subset f^{-1}(D_\varepsilon)$, which is an unboun
ded subset.

Since $f^{-1}(D_\varepsilon)$ is closed and unbounded, it is **not compact**. As
 $D_\varepsilon$ is a compact subset of $U$, this shows $f|_{f^{-1}(U)}: f^{-1}(
U) \to U$ is not a proper map.

Since $U$ was an arbitrary open neighborhood of $s = 0$, there is **no** open ne
ighborhood $U$ of $s$ over which $f$ is proper, even though $f^{-1}(s) = \{0\}$
is compact. $\blacksquare$
