## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $F$ is necessarily complete.

**Setup.** Since $E \neq \{0\}$, choose $e_0 \in E$ with $\|e_0\| = 1$. By the H
ahn–Banach theorem, there exists a bounded linear functional $\varphi \in E^*$ (
the dual of $E$) such that
$$\varphi(e_0) = 1, \qquad \|\varphi\| = 1.$$
(Define $\varphi$ on $\operatorname{span}(e_0)$ by $\varphi(\lambda e_0) = \lamb
da$, which has norm $1$, and extend it to all of $E$ preserving the norm.)

**Goal.** Let $(f_n)_{n \geq 1}$ be a Cauchy sequence in $F$; we show it converg
es in $F$.

**Step 1: Lift the Cauchy sequence to $L(E,F)$.** For each $n$, define $T_n : E
\to F$ by
$$T_n(x) = \varphi(x)\, f_n, \qquad x \in E.$$
Each $T_n$ is linear (since $\varphi$ is linear) and bounded, with
$$\|T_n\| = \sup_{\|x\|\le 1} \|\varphi(x)\,f_n\| = \|\varphi\|\,\|f_n\| = \|f_n
\|.$$
So $T_n \in L(E,F)$.

**Step 2: $(T_n)$ is Cauchy in $L(E,F)$.** For any $n, m$,
$$\|T_n - T_m\| = \sup_{\|x\|\le 1} \|\varphi(x)(f_n - f_m)\| = \|\varphi\|\,\|f
_n - f_m\| = \|f_n - f_m\|.$$
Since $(f_n)$ is Cauchy in $F$, $(T_n)$ is Cauchy in $L(E,F)$.

**Step 3: Use completeness of $L(E,F)$.** By hypothesis $L(E,F)$ is complete, so
 there exists $T \in L(E,F)$ with $T_n \to T$ in the operator norm.

**Step 4: Pull back to $F$.** Set $f := T(e_0) \in F$. Then
$$\|f_n - f\| = \|\varphi(e_0)\,f_n - T(e_0)\| = \|T_n(e_0) - T(e_0)\| \le \|T_n
 - T\|\,\|e_0\| = \|T_n - T\| \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.$$
(We used $\varphi(e_0)=1$ so $T_n(e_0) = f_n$, and $\|e_0\|=1$.)

Thus $f_n \to f$ in $F$.

**Conclusion.** Every Cauchy sequence in $F$ converges, so $F$ is complete. $\bl
acksquare$
