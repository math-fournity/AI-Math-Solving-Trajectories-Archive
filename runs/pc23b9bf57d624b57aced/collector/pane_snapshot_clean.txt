## Solution

The contour $c$ is the square $\max\{|\operatorname{Re} z|, |\operatorname{Im} z
|\} = 1$ with positive (counterclockwise) orientation, with vertices $1+i,\ -1+i
,\ -1-i,\ 1-i$.

**Singularities of the integrand.** The integrand
$$f(z) = \frac{z}{\cos z - 1}$$
has poles where $\cos z = 1$, i.e. at $z = 2\pi k$ for $k \in \mathbb{Z}$. Withi
n the square $\{|\operatorname{Re} z| \le 1,\ |\operatorname{Im} z| \le 1\}$, th
e only such point is $z = 0$ (since $|2\pi k| \ge 2\pi > 1$ for $k \ne 0$).

**Nature of the singularity at $z = 0$.** Using the expansion $\cos z - 1 = -\df
rac{z^{2}}{2} + \dfrac{z^{4}}{24} - \cdots = -\dfrac{z^{2}}{2}\!\left(1 - \dfrac
{z^{2}}{12} + \cdots\right)$,
$$\frac{z}{\cos z - 1} = \frac{z}{-\dfrac{z^{2}}{2}\left(1 - \dfrac{z^{2}}{12} +
 \cdots\right)} = -\frac{2}{z}\left(1 + \frac{z^{2}}{12} + \cdots\right) = -\fra
c{2}{z} - \frac{z}{6} - \cdots$$
So $z = 0$ is a **simple pole** with residue
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z) = \lim_{z \to 0} z \cdot \frac{z}{\cos z - 1} =
\lim_{z \to 0} \frac{z^{2}}{\cos z - 1} = \lim_{z \to 0} \frac{z^{2}}{-z^{2}/2}
= -2.$$

**Applying the residue theorem.** The only singularity enclosed by $c$ is $z = 0
$, so
$$\int_c \frac{z\, dz}{\cos z - 1} = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z)
= 2\pi i \cdot (-2) = -4\pi i.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \int_c \frac{z\, dz}{\cos z - 1} = -4\pi i}$$
