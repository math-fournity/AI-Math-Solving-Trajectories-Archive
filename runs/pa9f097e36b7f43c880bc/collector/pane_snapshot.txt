**Claim.** Every convex function $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ (for any $n \g
eq 1$, in particular $n > 1$) is continuous, and hence Borel-measurable.

**Proof.**

We prove that $f$ is continuous at every point $x_0 \in \mathbb{R}^n$. The key i
s that $f$ is finite and convex on all of $\mathbb{R}^n$, which forces local bou
ndedness; convexity then upgrades boundedness to continuity.

**Step 1: $f$ is locally bounded above.**

Fix $x_0 \in \mathbb{R}^n$. Consider the $2^n$ vertices of the closed cube $C =
[x_0 - \mathbf{1}, x_0 + \mathbf{1}]^n$ (coordinatewise), i.e., the points $x_0
+ \sum_{i=1}^n s_i e_i$ with $s_i \in \{-1,+1\}$. Set
$$M := \max\bigl\{ f(x_0 + \textstyle\sum_i s_i e_i) : s_i \in \{-1,+1\}\bigr\}.
$$
Every point $y \in C$ is a convex combination of these $2^n$ vertices (each coor
dinate of $y$ is a convex combination of $\pm 1$, and the product structure give
s a joint convex combination). By convexity of $f$,
$$f(y) \leq M \qquad \text{for all } y \in C.$$
