# Proof

**Claim:** Yes. In the ring $\operatorname{End}(V)$ of all linear transformation
s of an infinite-dimensional vector space $V$ over a division ring $D$, there al
ways exists a surjective but not injective linear transformation.

---

**Proof.**

Let $V$ be an infinite-dimensional (left) vector space over a division ring $D$,
 and let $B$ be a basis of $V$. Since $B$ is infinite, it contains a countably i
nfinite subset. (In ZFC every infinite set admits a countably infinite subset.)
Enumerate this subset as $\{e_1, e_2, e_3, \dots\}$, and set $B_0 = B \setminus
\{e_1, e_2, e_3, \dots\}$.

Define a map $T : B \to V$ on the basis by

$$
T(e_1) = 0, \qquad T(e_n) = e_{n-1} \;\text{ for } n \ge 2, \qquad T(b) = b \;\t
ext{ for all } b \in B_0.
$$

Since $B$ is a basis, $T$ extends uniquely to a linear transformation $T \in \op
eratorname{End}(V)$.

**$T$ is not injective.** We have $T(e_1) = 0$ with $e_1 \neq 0$, so $\ker(T) \n
eq \{0\}$.

**$T$ is surjective.** The image of $T$ is the span of $T(B)$. Now

$$
T(B) = \{0\} \cup \{e_1, e_2, e_3, \dots\} \cup B_0 = \{0\} \cup B.
$$

Since $\operatorname{span}(\{0\} \cup B) = \operatorname{span}(B) = V$, we obtai
n $\operatorname{im}(T) = V$.

Thus $T$ is surjective but not injective. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The argument uses only that $B$ is infinite (to extract a countably
infinite subset) and the universal property of a basis. It is independent of the
 cardinality of $B$ and of the division ring $D$, so the conclusion holds for *e
very* infinite-dimensional vector space over *every* division ring.
