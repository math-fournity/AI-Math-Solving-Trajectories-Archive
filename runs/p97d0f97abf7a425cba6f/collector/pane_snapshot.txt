## Proof

**Yes, $q_1 = q_2$.**

**Key Lemma.** *If $\chi$ is a primitive Dirichlet character modulo $q$, then $q
$ is the smallest positive period of $\chi$ viewed as a function $\mathbb{Z}\to\
mathbb{C}$ (i.e. the least $d>0$ such that $\chi(n+d)=\chi(n)$ for every $n\in\m
athbb{Z}$).*

*Proof of Lemma.* A Dirichlet character mod $q$ is periodic mod $q$. Let $d$ be
its smallest positive period. The set of periods is a subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}$,
hence of the form $d\mathbb{Z}$; since $q$ is a period, $d\mid q$.

We claim $\chi$ is induced by a character mod $d$. By the standard characterizat
ion, a character $\chi$ mod $q$ factors through $(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{*}$ (
with $d\mid q$) if and only if
$$\chi(a)=1 \quad\text{for all } a\equiv 1\pmod d,\ \gcd(a,q)=1.$$
But periodicity mod $d$ gives $\chi(a)=\chi(1)=1$ for every $a\equiv 1\pmod d$ (
in particular for those coprime to $q$). Hence $\chi$ is induced by some Dirichl
et character mod $d$. (The inducing character is well-defined: every class in $(
\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{*}$ admits a representative coprime to $q$ by CRT, usin
g that the primes dividing $q$ but not $d$ can be avoided; and multiplicativity
follows from that of $\chi$.)

Since $\chi$ is **primitive**, its conductor equals $q$, so it cannot be induced
 by any character of modulus $<q$. Therefore $d=q$. $\square$

**Main argument.** Since $\chi_1(n)=\chi_2(n)$ for *all* integers $n$, the two f
unctions $\chi_1,\chi_2:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}$ are identical. Identical functi
ons have the same smallest positive period. By the Lemma, the smallest positive
period of $\chi_i$ is $q_i$. Hence
$$q_1 = q_2.$$

(Remark: one may also see the role of the zero-set. If $\chi_1=\chi_2$ everywher
e, then $\chi_1(n)\neq 0 \iff \chi_2(n)\neq 0$, so $\gcd(n,q_1)=1 \iff \gcd(n,q_
2)=1$, forcing $\operatorname{rad}(q_1)=\operatorname{rad}(q_2)$. But the period
 argument above is stronger and directly yields equality of the moduli, not just
 of the radicals.)
