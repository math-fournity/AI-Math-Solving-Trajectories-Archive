$t_k \le t_n$ and $t_i + t_j \ge t_1 + t_2$, so $t_k < t_i + t_j$; the other two
triangle inequalities are automatic (the smaller two sides are each less than
the largest plus a positive number).

We proceed by contradiction: assume $t_n \ge t_1 + t_2$ and show $(\sum t_i)
(\sum 1/t_i) \ge n^2 + 1$.

Step 1 (Three-variable lemma). If $a, b, c > 0$ with $c \ge a+b$, then $(a+b+c)
(1/a+1/b+1/c) \ge 10$, with equality iff $a=b,\ c = a+b$.

Proof of lemma. The expression is scale-invariant, so set $a+b=1$, $c \ge 1$, $a
= x$, $b = 1-x$ with $0 < x \le 1/2$. Then $$ (a+b+c)!\left(\tfrac{1}{a}+
\tfrac{1}{b}+\tfrac{1}{c}\right) = (1+c)!\left(\tfrac{1}{x(1-x)}+\tfrac{1}
{c}\right). $$ Since $x(1-x) \le 1/4$, we get $1/(x(1-x)) \ge 4$, so $$ \ge
(1+c)!\left(4+\tfrac{1}{c}\right) = 5 + 4c + \tfrac{1}{c}. $$ For $c \ge 1$: $
\frac{d}{dc}(5+4c+1/c) = 4 - 1/c^2 \ge 3 > 0$, so the minimum is at $c=1$,
giving $5+4+1 = 10$. Equality requires $x=1/2$ and $c=1$. $\square$

Step 2 (Splitting). Set $m = n - 3$ and split off the three extreme variables: $
$ A = t_1 + t_2 + t_n, \quad B = \tfrac{1}{t_1}+\tfrac{1}{t_2}+\tfrac{1}{t_n},
\quad S' = \sum_{i=3}^{n-1} t_i, \quad R' = \sum_{i=3}^{n-1} \tfrac{1}{t_i}. $$
(When $n=3$, $S'=R'=0$ and $m=0$.) Then $$ \left(\sum t_i\right)!\left(\sum
\tfrac{1}{t_i}\right) = (S'+A)(R'+B) = S'R' + S'B + AR' + AB. $$

Step 3 (Bounding the middle terms). By Cauchy–Schwarz, $S'R' \ge m^2$. By AM-GM,
$$ S'B + AR' \ge 2\sqrt{S'B \cdot AR'} = 2\sqrt{AB \cdot S'R'} \ge 2\sqrt{AB
\cdot m^2} = 2m\sqrt{AB}. $$ Therefore $$ (S'+A)(R'+B) \ge m^2 + 2m\sqrt{AB} +
AB = \bigl(m + \sqrt{AB}\bigr)^2. $$

Step 4 (Applying the lemma). Since $t_n \ge t_1 + t_2$, the lemma gives $AB =
(t_1+t_2+t_n)(1/t_1+1/t_2+1/t_n) \ge 10$, so $\sqrt{AB} \ge \sqrt{10}$. Hence $$
\left(\sum t_i\right)!\left(\sum \tfrac{1}{t_i}\right) \ge \bigl(n-3+
\sqrt{10}\bigr)^2. $$

Step 5 (Final comparison). We verify $(n-3+\sqrt{10})^2 \ge n^2+1$ for all $n
\ge 3$: $$ (n-3+\sqrt{10})^2 - (n^2+1) = 2(n-3)(\sqrt{10}-3). $$ Since $
\sqrt{10} > 3$ and $n-3 \ge 0$, this is $\ge 0$, with equality iff $n=3$.

Conclusion. Under the assumption $t_n \ge t_1 + t_2$, we obtain $(\sum t_i)(\sum
1/t_i) \ge n^2+1$, contradicting the hypothesis $n^2+1 > (\sum t_i)(\sum 1/
t_i)$. Therefore $t_n < t_1 + t_2$, and by Step 0, every triple $t_i, t_j, t_k$
($1 \le i < j < k \le n$) forms a triangle. $\blacksquare$
