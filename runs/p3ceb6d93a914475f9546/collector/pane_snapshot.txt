## Proof

Let $E$ be an $n$-dimensional complex vector space, $f \in L(E)$ an endomorphism
 with $k$ distinct eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_k$, and $F: L(E) \to L
(E)$ the linear map $F(g) = f \circ g - g \circ f$.

**Step 1: Triangularize $f$.** Over $\mathbb{C}$, $f$ admits an upper-triangular
 matrix in some basis $e_1, \dots, e_n$, with diagonal entries $\mu_1, \dots, \m
u_n$ (the eigenvalues of $f$ listed with algebraic multiplicity, each $\mu_i \in
 \{\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_k\}$). Write
$$f(e_j) = \sum_{a \le j} a_{aj}\, e_a, \qquad a_{jj} = \mu_j.$$

**Step 2: Action of $F$ on the standard basis of $L(E)$.** Let $E_{ij} \in L(E)$
 be the operator $E_{ij}(e_j) = e_i$, $E_{ij}(e_s)=0$ for $s \ne j$. Then
$$f E_{ij}(e_j) = f(e_i) = \sum_{a \le i} a_{ai}\, e_a \implies f E_{ij} = \sum_
{a \le i} a_{ai}\, E_{aj},$$
$$E_{ij} f(e_s) = E_{ij}\!\Big(\sum_{b \le s} a_{bs} e_b\Big) = a_{js}\, e_i \;\
;(j \le s) \implies E_{ij} f = \sum_{s \ge j} a_{js}\, E_{is}.$$
Hence
$$F(E_{ij}) = \sum_{a \le i} a_{ai}\, E_{aj} \;-\; \sum_{s \ge j} a_{js}\, E_{is
}.$$
The coefficient of $E_{ij}$ itself is $a_{ii} - a_{jj} = \mu_i - \mu_j$. The rem
aining terms are of two types:
- $E_{aj}$ with $a < i$ (strictly smaller row index),
- $E_{is}$ with $s > j$ (strictly larger column index).

**Step 3: $F$ is upper-triangular in a suitable order.** Order the basis $\{E_{i
j}\}$ by **row index descending, then column index ascending**. Under this order
:
- $E_{aj}$ with $a < i$ has smaller row index $\Rightarrow$ appears **after** $E
_{ij}$;
- $E_{is}$ with $s > j$ has the same row $i$ but larger column $\Rightarrow$ app
ears **after** $E_{ij}$.

Thus every off-diagonal term of $F(E_{ij})$ lies strictly above the diagonal in
this ordered basis. Therefore $F$ is upper-triangular, and its eigenvalues (with
 algebraic multiplicity) are exactly the diagonal entries
$$\boxed{\mu_i - \mu_j, \qquad 1 \le i, j \le n.}$$

**Step 4: Distinct eigenvalues of $F$.** Since each $\mu_i$ ranges over the set
$S = \{\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_k\}$ of distinct eigenvalues of $f$, the set of
 eigenvalues of $F$ is
$$\{\,\alpha - \beta : \alpha, \beta \in S\,\} = \{\,\lambda_i - \lambda_j : 1 \
le i, j \le k\,\}.$$
There are $k^2$ ordered pairs $(i,j)$, giving $k^2$ differences. In general posi
tion (no two pairwise differences coincide), these $k^2$ values are all distinct
. Hence the number of distinct eigenvalues of $F$ is

$$\boxed{k^2}.$$
