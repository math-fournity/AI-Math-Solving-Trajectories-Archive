## Proof

**Setup.** The integrand is $f(w) = \frac{\pi^2}{\sin^2\pi w}(w-N)$. The poles o
f $\frac{\pi^2}{\sin^2\pi w}$ occur where $\sin\pi w = 0$, i.e., at every intege
r $w = n$, $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. Since $C_r$ is centered at $N$ with radius $0 < r
 < 1$, the only integer inside $C_r$ is $N$ itself (the nearest integers $N\pm 1
$ are at distance $1 > r$). Thus the only singularity enclosed is at $w = N$.

**Local expansion at $w = N$.** Since $\sin\pi w = (-1)^N \sin\pi(w-N)$, we have
 $\sin^2\pi w = \sin^2\pi(w-N)$. Expanding around $w = N$ with $u = w - N$:

$$\sin\pi u = \pi u - \frac{(\pi u)^3}{3!} + \cdots = \pi u\left(1 - \frac{\pi^2
 u^2}{6} + \cdots\right)$$

$$\sin^2\pi u = \pi^2 u^2\left(1 - \frac{\pi^2 u^2}{3} + \cdots\right)$$

Therefore:

$$\frac{\pi^2}{\sin^2\pi w} = \frac{\pi^2}{\pi^2 u^2\left(1 - \frac{\pi^2 u^2}{3
} + \cdots\right)} = \frac{1}{u^2}\left(1 + \frac{\pi^2 u^2}{3} + \cdots\right)
= \frac{1}{(w-N)^2} + \frac{\pi^2}{3} + \cdots$$

**Forming the integrand.** Multiplying by $(w - N) = u$:

$$f(w) = \frac{\pi^2}{\sin^2\pi w}(w-N) = \frac{1}{w-N} + \frac{\pi^2}{3}(w-N) +
 \cdots$$

This is a Laurent series in which the coefficient of $\frac{1}{w-N}$ is $1$. Hen
ce:

$$\operatorname{Res}_{w=N} f(w) = 1.$$

**Applying the residue theorem.** Since $w = N$ is the only singularity inside $
C_r$:

$$\oint_{C_r}\frac{\pi^2}{\sin^2\pi w}(w-N)\,dw = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res
}_{w=N} f(w) = 2\pi i \cdot 1 = \boxed{2\pi i}.$$
