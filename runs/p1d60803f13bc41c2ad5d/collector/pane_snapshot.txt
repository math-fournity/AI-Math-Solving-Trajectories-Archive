## Answer

**No.** Two prime ideals can contain exactly the same non-zero, non-unit idempot
ents yet be distinct. Here is a counterexample.

### Counterexample

Let $R = \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$ (a commutative ring with identity). Its i
dempotents are exactly
$$\{(0,0),\;(1,0),\;(0,1),\;(1,1)\},$$
since $\mathbb{Z}$ is a domain, so each component idempotent is $0$ or $1$. The
non-zero, non-unit idempotents are therefore precisely $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$.

The prime ideals of $R = \mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}$ are exactly
$$P\times\mathbb{Z}\;\text{and}\;\mathbb{Z}\times Q,$$
where $P, Q$ range over prime ideals of $\mathbb{Z}$, i.e. $(p)$ for primes $p$
(and $(0)$).

Consider the two prime ideals
$$\mathfrak{p}_1 = (2)\times\mathbb{Z},\qquad \mathfrak{p}_2 = (3)\times\mathbb{
Z}.$$

Both are prime (the quotient $R/\mathfrak{p}_i \cong \mathbb{Z}/(p_i)$ is a doma
in).

**Idempotents contained in $\mathfrak{p}_1 = (2)\times\mathbb{Z}$:**
- $(0,1)\in\mathfrak{p}_1$ since $0\in(2)$ and $1\in\mathbb{Z}$. ✓
- $(1,0)\in\mathfrak{p}_1$ would require $1\in(2)$, which is false. ✗

So the non-zero, non-unit idempotents in $\mathfrak{p}_1$ are exactly $\{(0,1)\}
$.

**Idempotents contained in $\mathfrak{p}_2 = (3)\times\mathbb{Z}$:**
- $(0,1)\in\mathfrak{p}_2$ since $0\in(3)$. ✓
- $(1,0)\in\mathfrak{p}_2$ would require $1\in(3)$, false. ✗

So the non-zero, non-unit idempotents in $\mathfrak{p}_2$ are also exactly $\{(0
,1)\}$.

Yet $\mathfrak{p}_1\neq\mathfrak{p}_2$, since $(2,0)\in\mathfrak{p}_1\setminus\m
athfrak{p}_2$ (as $2\notin(3)$).

### Conclusion

The two distinct prime ideals $\mathfrak{p}_1=(2)\times\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathfra
k{p}_2=(3)\times\mathbb{Z}$ in $R=\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}$ contain exactly th
e same non-zero, non-unit idempotents (namely $(0,1)$), but are not equal. There
fore the answer to the question is **no**.
