## Proof

Place the origin at $E$. Let $\mathbf a,\mathbf b,\mathbf c,\mathbf d$ be the po
sition vectors of $A,B,C,D$ relative to $E$. Since $ABCD$ is a parallelogram, $D
=A+C-B$, hence

$$\mathbf d=\mathbf a+\mathbf c-\mathbf b. \tag{$\ast$}$$

Let $\mathbf o_1,\mathbf o_2$ be the centers of the two given circles (the one t
hrough $A,B$ and the one through $B,C$). Both circles have radius $R$ and both p
ass through $E\;(=\mathbf 0)$ and $B\;(=\mathbf b)$. Hence $\mathbf o_1,\mathbf
o_2$ both lie on the perpendicular bisector of $EB$ and satisfy $|\mathbf o_1|=|
\mathbf o_2|=R$.

The perpendicular bisector of $EB$ is the line $\{\mathbf z:\operatorname{Re}(\m
athbf z\,\overline{\mathbf b})=|\mathbf b|^2/2\}$, perpendicular to the directio
n $\mathbf b$. Its intersection with the circle $|\mathbf z|=R$ (centered at the
 origin) consists of two points symmetric about the line through the origin in d
irection $\mathbf b$; their midpoint is the foot of the perpendicular from the o
rigin, namely $\mathbf b/2$. Since the two given circles are distinct (they have
 a well-defined "other" intersection $E$), $\mathbf o_1\neq\mathbf o_2$ are exac
tly these two points, and therefore

$$\mathbf o_1+\mathbf o_2=\mathbf b. \tag{1}$$

From the fact that $A,B,E$ lie on the first circle and $C,B,E$ on the second:

$$|\mathbf a-\mathbf o_1|=R,\qquad |\mathbf o_1|=R,\qquad |\mathbf c-\mathbf o_2
|=R,\qquad |\mathbf o_2|=R. \tag{2}$$

Now define

$$\mathbf o_3:=\mathbf a-\mathbf o_1.$$

We claim the circle of radius $R$ centered at $\mathbf o_3$ passes through $E,A,
D$. Indeed, using $(\ast)$, $(1)$, and $(2)$:

* **Distance to $E$:** $\;|\mathbf o_3-\mathbf 0|=|\mathbf a-\mathbf o_1|=R.$
* **Distance to $A$:** $\;|\mathbf o_3-\mathbf a|=|-\mathbf o_1|=|\mathbf o_1|=R

* **Distance to $D$:**
$$|\mathbf o_3-\mathbf d|=|(\mathbf a-\mathbf o_1)-(\mathbf a+\mathbf c-\mathbf
b)|=|\mathbf b-\mathbf o_1-\mathbf c|=|(\mathbf o_1+\mathbf o_2)-\mathbf o_1-\ma
thbf c|=|\mathbf o_2-\mathbf c|=R.$$

Thus $E$, $A$, and $D$ all lie at distance $R$ from $\mathbf o_3$. Equivalently,
 the circle passing through $A$, $D$, and $E$ has center $\mathbf o_3$ and radiu
s $R$. $\blacksquare$
