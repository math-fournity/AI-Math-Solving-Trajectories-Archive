## Solution

**Step 1: Identify $C(\alpha)$.**

$C(\alpha)$ is the coefficient of $x^{1992}$ in $(1+x)^\alpha$, so by the genera
lized binomial theorem:
$$C(\alpha) = \binom{\alpha}{1992} = \frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)\cdots(\alpha-1991)}{
1992!}.$$

**Step 2: Compute $C(-y-1)$.**

Setting $\alpha = -y-1$:
$$C(-y-1) = \binom{-y-1}{1992} = \frac{(-y-1)(-y-2)\cdots(-y-1992)}{1992!}.$$

Each factor is $-(y+k)$ for $k=1,\dots,1992$, so there are $1992$ negative signs
. Since $1992$ is even:
$$C(-y-1) = \frac{(-1)^{1992}(y+1)(y+2)\cdots(y+1992)}{1992!} = \frac{1}{1992!}\
prod_{k=1}^{1992}(y+k).$$

**Step 3: Recognize the logarithmic derivative.**

The sum inside the integral is:
$$\sum_{k=1}^{1992}\frac{1}{y+k} = \frac{d}{dy}\ln\!\left(\prod_{k=1}^{1992}(y+k
)\right) = \frac{\frac{d}{dy}\prod_{k=1}^{1992}(y+k)}{\prod_{k=1}^{1992}(y+k)}.$
$

Therefore:
$$C(-y-1)\sum_{k=1}^{1992}\frac{1}{y+k} = \frac{1}{1992!}\prod_{k=1}^{1992}(y+k)
\cdot\frac{\frac{d}{dy}\prod_{k=1}^{1992}(y+k)}{\prod_{k=1}^{1992}(y+k)} = \frac
{1}{1992!}\frac{d}{dy}\prod_{k=1}^{1992}(y+k).$$

**Step 4: Evaluate the integral.**

$$I = \int_0^1 \frac{1}{1992!}\frac{d}{dy}\prod_{k=1}^{1992}(y+k)\,dy = \frac{1}
{1992!}\left[\prod_{k=1}^{1992}(y+k)\right]_{y=0}^{y=1}.$$

- At $y=1$: $\prod_{k=1}^{1992}(1+k) = 2\cdot3\cdots1993 = 1993!$
- At $y=0$: $\prod_{k=1}^{1992}k = 1\cdot2\cdots1992 = 1992!$

Thus:
$$I = \frac{1}{1992!}\bigl(1993! - 1992!\bigr) = \frac{1}{1992!}\bigl(1992!\cdot
 1993 - 1992!\bigr) = \frac{1992!(1993-1)}{1992!} = 1992.$$
