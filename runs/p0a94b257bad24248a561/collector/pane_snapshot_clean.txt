## Proof

Let $M$ be an oriented $m$-dimensional manifold. We explain how the orientation
furnishes a canonical bundle isomorphism between $\Lambda^m T^*M$ (the bundle of
 $m$-forms) and $|\Lambda|^m T^*M$ (the bundle of densities).

### 1. The two notions and their transformation laws

Fix $p \in M$ and an ordered basis $e = (e_1, \dots, e_m)$ of $T_p M$.

An **$m$-form** $\omega$ at $p$ is an alternating multilinear map $\omega_p : (T
_pM)^m \to \mathbb{R}$. It is determined by its value $\omega_p(e_1,\dots,e_m) \
in \mathbb{R}$, and under a change of basis $e' = eA$ (i.e. $e'_j = \sum_i A_{ij
} e_i$) it transforms by
$$
\omega_p(e'_1,\dots,e'_m) = \det(A)\,\omega_p(e_1,\dots,e_m). \tag{$\star$}
$$

A **density** $\mu$ at $p$ is a map $\mu_p : (T_pM)^m \to \mathbb{R}$ that is mu
ltilinear and alternating up to absolute value: it is determined by $\mu_p(e_1,\
dots,e_m) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ (in fact $\mu_p \ge 0$ on every basis when $\m
u$ is a genuine density, but more generally the density bundle is defined by the
 transformation law), and under $e' = eA$ it transforms by
$$
\mu_p(e'_1,\dots,e'_m) = |\det(A)|\,\mu_p(e_1,\dots,e_m). \tag{$\star\star$}
$$

The **only** difference between $(\star)$ and $(\star\star)$ is $\det(A)$ versus
 $|\det(A)|$.

### 2. What the orientation provides

An orientation on $M$ is a continuous choice of equivalence class of ordered bas
es of $T_pM$ at every point, where two bases are equivalent iff the change-of-ba
sis matrix has **positive** determinant. Call the chosen bases **positive**.

For a change of basis between two positive bases, $\det(A) > 0$, hence $|\det(A)
| = \det(A)$. Therefore, **restricted to positive bases, the transformation laws
 $(\star)$ and $(\star\star)$ coincide.** This is the entire mechanism by which
the orientation identifies the two bundles.

### 3. The canonical isomorphism (both directions)

**Densities $\to$ $m$-forms.** Given a density $\mu$, define an $m$-form $\Phi(\
mu)$ as follows. At each $p$, pick any positive ordered basis $e$ of $T_pM$ and
set
$$
\Phi(\mu)_p(e_1,\dots,e_m) := \mu_p(e_1,\dots,e_m).
$$
Extend $\Phi(\mu)_p$ to all ordered bases by the $m$-form law $(\star)$. We must
 check this is well-defined, i.e. independent of the choice of positive basis. I
f $e' = eA$ is another positive basis ($\det A > 0$), then
$$
\mu_p(e'_1,\dots,e'_m) = |\det A|\,\mu_p(e_1,\dots,e_m) = \det(A)\,\mu_p(e_1,\do
ts,e_m),
$$
which is exactly the value that the $m$-form extension assigns to $e'$. Hence $\
Phi(\mu)_p$ is well-defined, and it is alternating multilinear by construction.
Smoothness follows because locally an orientation admits a smooth positive frame
, on which $\Phi(\mu)$ agrees with the smooth density $\mu$.

**$m$-forms $\to$ densities.** Given an $m$-form $\omega$, define a density $\Ps
i(\omega)$ by
$$
\Psi(\omega)_p(e_1,\dots,e_m) := \omega_p(e_1,\dots,e_m)
$$
on every positive basis $e$, extended to all bases by the density law $(\star\st
ar)$. Well-definedness is the same argument: for $e' = eA$ positive,
$$
\omega_p(e'_1,\dots,e'_m) = \det(A)\,\omega_p(e_1,\dots,e_m) = |\det A|\,\omega_
p(e_1,\dots,e_m),
$$
matching $(\star\star)$. So $\Psi(\omega)$ is a well-defined density, varying sm
oothly by the same local-frame argument.

### 4. The two maps are inverse isomorphisms

- Start with a density $\mu$. Then $\Psi(\Phi(\mu))$ is a density agreeing with
$\mu$ on every positive basis; since two densities agreeing on one basis agree e
verywhere, $\Psi(\Phi(\mu)) = \mu$.
- Start with an $m$-form $\omega$. Then $\Phi(\Psi(\omega))$ is an $m$-form agre
eing with $\omega$ on every positive basis; since two $m$-forms agreeing on one
basis agree everywhere, $\Phi(\Psi(\omega)) = \omega$.

Both maps are $\mathbb{R}$-linear (the defining equations are linear) and fiberw
ise, so $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ are mutually inverse smooth bundle isomorphisms
$$
\Lambda^m T^*M \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; |\Lambda|^m T^*M.
$$

### 5. Canonicality

The construction used **no auxiliary choices**: at every step we evaluated on *p
ositive* bases, and the orientation itself is the only datum selecting which bas
es are positive. Any two oriented atlases defining the same orientation yield th
e same positive bases and hence the same isomorphism. Therefore the isomorphism
is **canonical** (natural in the oriented manifold $M$).

### Summary

The orientation splits ordered bases into positive and negative classes. On posi
tive bases the change-of-basis determinant is positive, so $|\det A| = \det A$ a
nd the transformation laws of $m$-forms and densities **coincine**. This lets us
 declare an $m$-form and a density to be **the same object when they agree on al
l positive bases**, producing a canonical, orientation-dependent, smooth bundle
isomorphism
$$
\Lambda^m T^*M \;\cong\; |\Lambda|^m T^*M.
$$
Concretely, a density $\mu$ is sent to the unique $m$-form equal to $\mu$ on pos
itive bases, and an $m$-form $\omega$ is sent to the unique density equal to $\o
mega$ on positive bases; these are inverse operations. Without an orientation th
e two bundles are only locally (not globally) isomorphic, since one cannot consi
stently choose the sign needed to turn $|\det A|$ into $\det A$.
