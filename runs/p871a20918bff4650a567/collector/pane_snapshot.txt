## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle S = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\zeta(2n+1)}{(2n+1)\,4^n}
$.

**Key tool.** The Taylor expansion of $\log\Gamma$ around $1$:

$$\log\Gamma(1+z) = -\gamma\, z + \sum_{k=2}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k\,\zeta(k)}{k}
\,z^k, \qquad |z|\le 1,\ z\ne -1.$$

Substituting $z\mapsto -z$ gives

$$\log\Gamma(1-z) = \gamma\, z + \sum_{k=2}^{\infty} \frac{\zeta(k)}{k}\,z^k.$$

**Isolate the odd zeta terms.** Subtracting:

$$\log\Gamma(1+z)-\log\Gamma(1-z) = -2\gamma\, z + \sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{\big
l[(-1)^k-1\bigr]\,\zeta(k)}{k}\,z^k.$$

For even $k$, $(-1)^k-1=0$; for odd $k=2n+1\ge 3$, $(-1)^k-1=-2$. Hence

$$\log\Gamma(1+z)-\log\Gamma(1-z) = -2\gamma\, z - 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ze
ta(2n+1)}{2n+1}\,z^{2n+1}.$$

Define $\displaystyle T(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta(2n+1)}{2n+1}\,z^{2n+1}
$. Then

$$T(z) = -\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\log\Gamma(1+z)-\log\Gamma(1-z)\bigr]-\gamma\, z.$$

**Extract the target sum.** Observe

$$T(z) = z\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta(2n+1)}{2n+1}\,z^{2n},$$

so setting $z^{2}=1/4$, i.e. $z=1/2$, gives $z^{2n}=4^{-n}$ and therefore

$$S = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta(2n+1)}{(2n+1)\,4^{n}} = \frac{T(1/2)}{1/2}=
2\,T(1/2).$$

**Evaluate at $z=1/2$.** Using $\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt{\pi}$ and $\Gamma(3/2)=\tfrac{
1}{2}\sqrt{\pi}$:

$$\log\Gamma(3/2)-\log\Gamma(1/2)=\log\frac{1}{2}=-\log 2.$$

Thus

$$T(1/2)=-\tfrac{1}{2}(-\log 2)-\frac{\gamma}{2}=\frac{\log 2-\gamma}{2},$$

and

$$S = 2\,T(1/2) = \log 2 - \gamma.$$

**Numerical check.** $\log 2 - \gamma \approx 0.69315 - 0.57722 = 0.11593$. Part
ial sums of the series: $\zeta(3)/12\approx 0.10017$, $+\,\zeta(5)/80\approx 0.0
1296$ ($0.11513$), $+\,\zeta(7)/448\approx 0.00225$ ($0.11538$), $+\,\zeta(9)/23
04\approx 0.000435$ ($0.11582$), converging to $0.11593$. ✓

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\zeta(2n+1)}{(2n+1)\,4^n} = \log 2
 - \gamma}$$
