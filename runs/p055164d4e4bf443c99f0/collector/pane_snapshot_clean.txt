## Solution

Let $\alpha = 5 + \sqrt{26}$ and $\beta = 5 - \sqrt{26}$.

**Key observations:**

- $\alpha + \beta = 10$, $\alpha\beta = 25 - 26 = -1$.
- So $\alpha, \beta$ are roots of $x^2 - 10x - 1 = 0$.
- $\sqrt{26} > 5$, so $\beta = 5 - \sqrt{26} < 0$; and $\sqrt{26} < 5.1$ (since
$5.1^2 = 26.01 > 26$), so $|\beta| = \sqrt{26} - 5 < 0.1 = 10^{-1}$.

**Integer sequence.** Define $S_n = \alpha^n + \beta^n$. From the recurrence rel
ation of the roots, $S_n = 10\,S_{n-1} + S_{n-2}$, with $S_0 = 2$, $S_1 = 10$. B
y induction, **$S_n$ is an integer for every $n$**.

**Isolating $\alpha^{1963}$.** Since $n = 1963$ is odd and $\beta < 0$, we have
$\beta^{1963} < 0$, hence
$$\alpha^{1963} = S_{1963} - \beta^{1963} = S_{1963} + |\beta|^{1963}.$$

**Bounding the fractional part.** Since $S_{1963}$ is a positive integer and $0
< |\beta| < 1$, the fractional part of $\alpha^{1963}$ is exactly $|\beta|^{1963
}$, and:
$$0 < |\beta|^{1963} < (10^{-1})^{1963} = 10^{-1963}.$$

**Conclusion.** The number $\alpha^{1963}$ equals the integer $S_{1963}$ plus a
positive quantity strictly less than $10^{-1963}$. In decimal form this is
$$\alpha^{1963} = S_{1963}.\underbrace{00\ldots0}_{\geq 1963 \text{ zeros}}\ldot
s$$
The fractional part is smaller than $10^{-1963}$, so the digit in the $k$-th pla
ce after the decimal point is $0$ for every $1 \le k \le 1963$.

Therefore, **the first 1963 digits after the decimal point of $(\sqrt{26}+5)^{19
63}$ are all $0$** — that is, $\underbrace{00\ldots0}_{1963}$.
