## Solution

Let $V = M_{n\times n}(\mathbb{R})$ (or any field) be the space of all $n\times
n$ matrices, with $\dim V = n^2$.

Define the map $\varphi: V \to \mathbb{R}$ by
$$\varphi(X) = \operatorname{tr}(PX).$$

**Linearity.** For $X, Y \in V$ and scalars $\alpha, \beta$,
$$\varphi(\alpha X + \beta Y) = \operatorname{tr}(P(\alpha X + \beta Y)) = \oper
atorname{tr}(\alpha PX + \beta PY) = \alpha\operatorname{tr}(PX) + \beta\operato
rname{tr}(PY) = \alpha\varphi(X) + \beta\varphi(Y).$$
So $\varphi$ is a linear functional on $V$.

**$\varphi$ is not the zero functional.** Since $P$ is invertible, take $X = P^{
-1}$. Then
$$\varphi(P^{-1}) = \operatorname{tr}(P \cdot P^{-1}) = \operatorname{tr}(I_n) =
 n \neq 0.$$
Hence $\varphi$ is a nonzero linear functional.

**Kernel dimension.** The space $L$ is exactly $\ker \varphi$:
$$L = \{X \in V : \operatorname{tr}(PX) = 0\} = \ker \varphi.$$
By the rank–nullity theorem,
$$\dim V = \dim \ker \varphi + \dim \operatorname{im} \varphi.$$
Since $\varphi$ is a nonzero linear functional, $\operatorname{im}\varphi = \mat
hbb{R}$, so $\dim \operatorname{im}\varphi = 1$. Therefore
$$\dim L = \dim \ker \varphi = n^2 - 1.$$

The dimension of the vector space $L$ is $\boxed{n^2 - 1}$.
