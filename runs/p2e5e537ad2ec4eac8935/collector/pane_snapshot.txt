## Solution

**Claim.** The least constant is $c = \dfrac{3}{32}$.

### Lower bound (tightness)

For the complete graph $K_n$: $f(K_n) = \binom{n}{3}$, $g(K_n) = \binom{n}{4}$.
Computing the ratio:

$$\frac{g(K_n)^3}{f(K_n)^4} = \frac{\binom{n}{4}^3}{\binom{n}{3}^4} = \frac{6^4}
{24^3}\cdot\frac{(n-3)^3}{n(n-1)(n-2)} = \frac{3}{32}\cdot\frac{(n-3)^3}{n(n-1)(
n-2)} \longrightarrow \frac{3}{32} \quad (n\to\infty).$$

So any valid $c$ must satisfy $c \ge \frac{3}{32}$.

### Upper bound

We prove $g(G)^3 \le \frac{3}{32}\,f(G)^4$ for every graph $G$.

**If $f(G)=0$**, then $G$ has no triangles, hence no $K_4$'s, so $g(G)=0$ and th
e inequality holds trivially.

**Assume $f(G) \ge 1$.** We use the following classical result:

> **Theorem (Kruskal–Katona, Lovász form, 1968).** *Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a famil
y of $r$-element subsets with $|\mathcal{F}| = \binom{x}{r}$ for real $x \ge r$.
 Then the upper shadow $\partial^+\mathcal{F} = \{S: |S|=r+1,\; \exists\, T\in\m
athcal{F},\; T\subset S\}$ satisfies $|\partial^+\mathcal{F}| \le \binom{x}{r+1}
$.*

**Applying the theorem.** Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the family of triangles of $G$, v
iewed as $3$-element subsets of $V(G)$. Then $|\mathcal{F}| = f(G)$. Since $\bin
om{3}{3}=1$ and $\binom{x}{3}$ is strictly increasing for $x\ge 2$, there is a u
nique $x \ge 3$ with $\binom{x}{3} = f(G)$.

Every $K_4$ in $G$ is a $4$-element subset $S\subseteq V(G)$ all of whose four $
3$-subsets are triangles, hence belong to $\mathcal{F}$. In particular $S \in \p
artial^+\mathcal{F}$. Therefore:

$$g(G) \le |\partial^+\mathcal{F}| \le \binom{x}{4}.$$

**Algebraic inequality.** We show $\binom{x}{4}^3 \le \frac{3}{32}\binom{x}{3}^4
$ for all $x \ge 3$:

$$\frac{\binom{x}{4}^3}{\binom{x}{3}^4} = \frac{\bigl(\frac{x(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)}{24
}\bigr)^3}{\bigl(\frac{x(x-1)(x-2)}{6}\bigr)^4} = \frac{6^4}{24^3}\cdot\frac{(x-
3)^3}{x(x-1)(x-2)} = \frac{3}{32}\cdot\frac{(x-3)^3}{x(x-1)(x-2)}.$$

It remains to show $\frac{(x-3)^3}{x(x-1)(x-2)} \le 1$ for $x \ge 3$. Setting $y
 = x-3 \ge 0$:

$$x(x-1)(x-2) - (x-3)^3 = (y+3)(y+2)(y+1) - y^3 = 6y^2 + 11y + 6 > 0 \quad \text
{for all } y \ge 0.$$

Hence $(x-3)^3 \le x(x-1)(x-2)$, giving $\binom{x}{4}^3 \le \frac{3}{32}\binom{x
}{3}^4$.

**Combining:**

$$g(G)^3 \le \binom{x}{4}^3 \le \frac{3}{32}\,\binom{x}{3}^4 = \frac{3}{32}\,f(G
)^4.$$

### Conclusion

The inequality $g(G)^3 \le \frac{3}{32}\,f(G)^4$ holds for every graph $G$, and
the constant $\frac{3}{32}$ is best possible (approached by $K_n$ as $n\to\infty
$).

$$\boxed{c = \frac{3}{32}}$$
