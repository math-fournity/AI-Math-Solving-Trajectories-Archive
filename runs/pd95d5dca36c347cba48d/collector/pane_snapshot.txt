A point $[x:y:z] \in \mathbb{P}^2$ lies in $U$ iff $x \neq 0$, $y \neq 0$, and
$z \neq 0$. On $U$ we may dehomogenize by setting $z = 1$, obtaining coordinates
$t = x/z$, $s = y/z$, both nonzero. This gives an isomorphism $$U ;
\xrightarrow{;\sim;}; \operatorname{Spec} k!\left[t,, t^{-1},, s,, s^{-1}\right]
;=; \mathbb{G}_{m,k}^2.$$ Concretely, $[x:y:z] \mapsto (x/z,, y/z)$ with inverse
$(t, s) \mapsto [t : s : 1]$.

Step 2: Compute $\operatorname{Cl}(U)$ via the exact sequence for class groups.

Since $U$ is smooth (open in $\mathbb{P}^2$), Weil divisors coincide with
Cartier divisors, so $\operatorname{Cl}(U) = \operatorname{Pic}(U)$. We compute
it using the standard exact sequence (Hartshorne, Algebraic Geometry, Prop.
II.6.5): for a normal variety $X$ and open subset $U = X \setminus (D_1 \cup
\cdots \cup D_n)$ where the $D_i$ are the codimension-1 irreducible components
of $X \setminus U$, $$\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n} \mathbb{Z}\cdot[D_i] ;\longrightarrow;
\operatorname{Cl}(X) ;\longrightarrow; \operatorname{Cl}(U) ;\longrightarrow; 0,
$$ where the left map sends each generator $[D_i]$ to the class of $D_i$ in $
\operatorname{Cl}(X)$.

Here $X = \mathbb{P}^2$, so $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{P}^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}$,
generated by the class of a line. Each $L_i$ is a line, hence $[L_i] \mapsto 1
\in \mathbb{Z}$. The map is $$\varphi : \mathbb{Z}^3 \to \mathbb{Z}, \qquad
(a_1, a_2, a_3) ;\mapsto; a_1 + a_2 + a_3.$$ This map is surjective (e.g., $
\varphi(1,0,0) = 1$). Therefore $$\operatorname{Cl}(U) ;\cong;
\operatorname{coker}(\varphi) ;=; \mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z} ;=; 0.$$
