## Proof

The boundary of the hexagonal circuit board is a regular hexagon with side lengt
h 2 cm, so the total perimeter is $L = 6 \times 2 = 12$ cm. The two sensors are
placed independently and uniformly at random along this boundary, which is a one
-dimensional curve of total length 12.

**Key observation:** For any fixed position of the first sensor $P$ on the bound
ary, the set of positions for the second sensor $Q$ that satisfy $|PQ| = 2$ (Euc
lidean distance through the air) is the intersection of the circle of radius 2 c
entered at $P$ with the boundary of the hexagon.

The boundary consists of six straight line segments. The intersection of a circl
e with a straight line segment is at most two points (a line can meet a circle i
n at most two points, and restricting to a segment can only reduce this). Theref
ore, across all six sides, the set of valid positions for $Q$ is a **finite set
of at most 12 points**.

A finite set of points has **measure zero** with respect to the one-dimensional
length measure on the boundary. Since the second sensor is placed uniformly (i.e
., according to normalized length measure on the boundary), the conditional prob
ability that $Q$ lands at distance exactly 2 from $P$ is

$$\Pr\bigl(|PQ|=2 \mid P\bigr) = \frac{0}{12} = 0$$

for every fixed $P$. Integrating over the uniform distribution of $P$,

$$\Pr\bigl(|PQ|=2\bigr) = \int_{\text{boundary}} \Pr\bigl(|PQ|=2 \mid P\bigr)\,\
frac{dP}{12} = \int_{\text{boundary}} 0 \cdot \frac{dP}{12} = 0.$$

**Why no arc of positive length can satisfy the condition:** A side of the hexag
on is a straight segment. For an entire sub-arc of a straight segment to lie on
a circle of radius 2 centered at $P$, that segment would have to be contained in
 the circle — but a straight line intersects a circle in at most two points, nev
er in an interval. Hence the valid set is always finite (never contains an inter
val), confirming measure zero.

Therefore, the probability that the two randomly placed sensors are at a straigh
t-line distance of exactly 2 centimeters is

$$\boxed{0}.$$
