## Proof

**Setup.** The 30 flight paths are rays from the beacon, i.e. 30 directions on a
 circle of circumference 360°. For two rays at directions α, β, the angle betwee
n them is δ(α,β) = min(|α−β|, 360°−|α−β|) ∈ [0°,180°]. A pair is *narrow* iff δ
< 90°. We minimize the number N of narrow pairs.

**Lower bound: N ≥ 98.**

Partition the circle into four fixed quadrants
Q₁=[0°,90°), Q₂=[90°,180°), Q₃=[180°,270°), Q₄=[270°,360°),
and let a,b,c,d be the numbers of rays falling in Q₁,Q₂,Q₃,Q₄, with a+b+c+d = 30
.

*Within-quadrant pairs are always narrow:* any two rays in the same Qᵢ lie in an
 arc of length 90° (half-open), so their angular difference is strictly less tha
n 90°, hence δ < 90°.

Therefore the number of narrow pairs is at least
$$\binom{a}{2}+\binom{b}{2}+\binom{c}{2}+\binom{d}{2}.$$
By convexity, this sum is minimized when a,b,c,d are as equal as possible. With
30 = 8+8+7+7,
$$\binom{8}{2}+\binom{8}{2}+\binom{7}{2}+\binom{7}{2} = 28+28+21+21 = 98.$$
Hence **N ≥ 98** for every configuration.

**Construction achieving N = 98.**

Place the 30 rays at exactly four directions: 0°, 90°, 180°, 270°, with counts 8
, 8, 7, 7 respectively (in any order).

- Two rays at the *same* direction: δ = 0° < 90° → narrow. Count: 28+28+21+21 =
98.
- Two rays at directions 90° apart (e.g. 0°&90°, 90°&180°, 180°&270°, 270°&0°):
δ = 90°, which is **not** strictly less than 90° → not narrow.
- Two rays at directions 180° apart (e.g. 0°&180°, 90°&270°): δ = 180° → not nar
row.

Thus the only narrow pairs are the within-direction pairs, giving exactly N = 98
.

**Optimality.** The lower bound N ≥ 98 holds for every arrangement of the 30 ray
s, and the four-direction construction attains 98. Therefore the smallest possib
le value is

$$\boxed{N = 98}.$$
