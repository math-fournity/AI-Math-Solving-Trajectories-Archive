**Answer: No**

**Proof.**

We show that for $n \geq 5$, it is impossible for every real $n \times n$ matrix
 to be orthogonally similar to a tridiagonal matrix via an element of $\mathrm{S
O}(n)$.

Consider the map
$$
\phi : \mathrm{SO}(n) \times \mathrm{Tridiag}(n) \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}^{n \
times n}, \qquad \phi(P, D) = P^{-1} D P = P^{\mathsf{T}} D P,
$$
where $\mathrm{Tridiag}(n)$ denotes the space of real tridiagonal $n \times n$ m
atrices. The image of $\phi$ is precisely the set of matrices that can be writte
n as $P^{-1} D P$ with $P \in \mathrm{SO}(n)$ and $D$ tridiagonal.

**Dimension count.** The dimension of the domain is
$$
\dim \mathrm{SO}(n) + \dim \mathrm{Tridiag}(n).
$$
We have $\dim \mathrm{SO}(n) = \frac{n(n-1)}{2}$, and a tridiagonal matrix is de
termined by its $n$ diagonal entries, $n-1$ super-diagonal entries, and $n-1$ su
b-diagonal entries, so $\dim \mathrm{Tridiag}(n) = 3n - 2$.

Thus the domain dimension is
$$
\frac{n(n-1)}{2} + (3n - 2) = \frac{n^2 - n + 6n - 4}{2} = \frac{n^2 + 5n - 4}{2
}.
$$

The target space $\mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ has dimension $n^2$.

**Comparison.** The domain dimension is strictly less than $n^2$ precisely when
$$
\frac{n^2 + 5n - 4}{2} < n^2 \iff n^2 + 5n - 4 < 2n^2 \iff n^2 - 5n + 4 > 0 \iff
 (n-1)(n-4) > 0.
$$
This inequality holds for all $n \geq 5$ (and also $n \leq 0$, which is irreleva
nt).

**Conclusion via Sard's theorem.** For $n \geq 5$, the map $\phi$ is a smooth ma
p from a manifold of dimension $\frac{n^2+5n-4}{2} < n^2$ into $\mathbb{R}^{n \t
imes n}$. By Sard's theorem, the set of regular values of $\phi$ has full measur
e, and since the domain dimension is strictly less than the target dimension, ev
ery point in the image is a critical value. Hence the image of $\phi$ has measur
e zero in $\mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$.

In particular, the image cannot be all of $\mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$. Therefore,
for $n \geq 5$, there exist real $n \times n$ matrices that **cannot** be writte
n as $P^{-1} D P$ with $P \in \mathrm{SO}(n)$ and $D$ tridiagonal.

Since the question asks whether **every** real square matrix (for general $n$) a
dmits such a decomposition, and the property fails for $n \geq 5$, the answer is
:

$$\boxed{\text{No}}$$
