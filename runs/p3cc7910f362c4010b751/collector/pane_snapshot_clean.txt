## Solution

We evaluate
$$L = \lim_{n \to \infty}\;\frac{1+ \frac{1}{2}+\cdots+\frac{1}{n}}{(\pi^n + e^n
)^{1/n}\,\ln{n}}.$$

### Step 1 — The numerator

Let $H_n = 1 + \tfrac{1}{2} + \cdots + \tfrac{1}{n}$ be the $n$-th harmonic numb
er. The classical asymptotic (obtained by comparing the sum with $\int_1^{n+1}\f
rac{dx}{x}$) gives

$$H_n = \ln n + \gamma + o(1),$$

where $\gamma$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant. In particular,

$$\frac{H_n}{\ln n} = 1 + \frac{\gamma + o(1)}{\ln n} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 1
.$$

### Step 2 — The factor $(\pi^n + e^n)^{1/n}$

Since $\pi > e$, we have $e/\pi < 1$. Factor out $\pi^n$:

$$(\pi^n + e^n)^{1/n} = \pi\left(1 + \left(\frac{e}{\pi}\right)^{\!n}\right)^{1/
n}.$$

As $n\to\infty$, $(e/\pi)^n \to 0$, so $1 + (e/\pi)^n \to 1$, and raising to the
 power $1/n \to 0$ gives

$$\left(1 + \left(\frac{e}{\pi}\right)^{\!n}\right)^{1/n} \longrightarrow 1^0 =
1.$$

Hence $(\pi^n + e^n)^{1/n} \to \pi$.

(More rigorously: $\pi^n \le \pi^n+e^n \le \pi^n(1+(e/\pi)^n) \le 2\pi^n$, so ta
king $n$-th roots, $\pi \le (\pi^n+e^n)^{1/n} \le \pi\cdot 2^{1/n} \to \pi$, and
 the squeeze theorem yields the limit $\pi$.)

### Step 3 — Combine

Rewrite the original expression as

$$L = \frac{H_n}{\ln n} \cdot \frac{1}{(\pi^n+e^n)^{1/n}}.$$

Both factors have limits:

$$\frac{H_n}{\ln n} \to 1, \qquad (\pi^n+e^n)^{1/n} \to \pi.$$

Therefore, by the product rule for limits,

$$L = 1 \cdot \frac{1}{\pi} = \frac{1}{\pi}.$$

### Answer

$$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{\pi}}$$
