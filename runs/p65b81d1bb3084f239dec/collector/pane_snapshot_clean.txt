# Fisher–Kolmogorov (Fisher-KPP) Travelling Wave Solution

## Answer-leak check
The problem statement contains no solution, answer, or proof sketch. Proceeding.

## Setup

The Fisher-KPP equation is
$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=k\,u(1-u)+D\,\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}
},\qquad k,D>0.$$

Seek a travelling wave $u(x,t)=U(z)$ with $z=x-ct$. Then
$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=-c\,U',\qquad \frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2
}}=U'',$$
so $U$ satisfies the ODE
$$\boxed{\,D\,U''+c\,U'+k\,U(1-U)=0\,}.\tag{1}$$

A physically realistic solution connects the two equilibria $U=1$ (unstable homo
geneous state) and $U=0$ (stable state) and satisfies
$$\lim_{z\to-\infty}U(z)=1,\qquad \lim_{z\to+\infty}U(z)=0,\qquad 0\le U(z)\le 1
,\qquad U'\le 0\;(\text{monotone}).\tag{2}$$

The sign of $c$ is fixed by (2): the wave moves so that the $U=1$ region invades
 the $U=0$ region, i.e. $c>0$.

---

## 1. Necessary condition on $c$ — linearization at the leading edge

Near $z\to+\infty$ we have $U\to0$, so (1) linearizes to
$$D\,U''+c\,U'+k\,U=0.\tag{3}$$
The characteristic equation is
$$D\lambda^{2}+c\,\lambda+k=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \lambda_{\pm}=\frac{-c\pm
\sqrt{c^{2}-4kD}}{2D}.\tag{4}$$

Two regimes:

- **$c^{2}<4kD$ ($c<2\sqrt{kD}$):** $\lambda_{\pm}$ are complex with $\Re\lambda
=-c/(2D)<0$. The general solution of (3) is $U\sim e^{-cz/(2D)}\!\bigl(A\cos\ome
ga z+B\sin\omega z\bigr)$, which **oscillates** about $0$ while decaying. Any os
cillation forces $U$ to take negative values, violating $0\le U\le1$. Hence **no
 physically realistic monotone wave exists for $c<2\sqrt{kD}$**.

- **$c^{2}\ge4kD$ ($c\ge2\sqrt{kD}$):** both roots are real and negative, $U\sim
 A\,e^{\lambda_{+}z}$ decays monotonically, consistent with (2).

Therefore a **necessary** condition is
$$\boxed{\,c\ge c_{\min}:=2\sqrt{kD}\,}.\tag{5}$$

## 2. Sufficiency — existence for every $c\ge2\sqrt{kD}$

Sufficiency is the content of the Kolmogorov–Petrovskii–Piskunov theorem (1937).
 Writing (1) as the phase system
$$U'=V,\qquad V'=-\frac{c}{D}V-\frac{k}{D}U(1-U),\tag{6}$$
the equilibria are $(U,V)=(0,0)$ and $(1,0)$. Their linearizations give:
- at $(1,0)$: eigenvalues $\frac{-c\pm\sqrt{c^{2}+4kD}}{2D}$ — one positive, one
 negative → saddle (for every $c>0$);
- at $(0,0)$: eigenvalues $\lambda_{\pm}$ from (4) — a stable node exactly when
$c\ge2\sqrt{kD}$.

For $c\ge2\sqrt{kD}$ the origin is a stable node, and the unstable manifold of t
he saddle at $(1,0)$ (the branch entering $0<U<1$, $V<0$) can be shown — by a sh
ooting/phase-plane argument using the invariant region $\{0\le U\le1,\;V\le0\}$
and the sign of $\frac{dV}{dU}=-\frac{c}{D}-\frac{k\,U(1-U)}{DV}$ — to connect t
o $(0,0)$ without leaving the strip $0\le U\le1$. This heteroclinic orbit is the
 travelling wave, and it is monotone ($V=U'\le0$). For $c<2\sqrt{kD}$ the origin
 is a spiral and the connecting orbit is forced out of the strip, confirming the
 obstruction from §1.

Thus (5) is both necessary and sufficient:
$$\boxed{\text{Physically realistic monotone travelling waves exist iff }c\ge2\s
qrt{kD}.}$$

The value $c_{\min}=2\sqrt{kD}$ is the **minimum wavespeed**; it is the speed se
lected by compact-support initial data.

---

## 3. An explicit closed-form solution at a special speed

Although the wave exists for a continuum of speeds, a closed-form solution is av
ailable at one particular $c$. Try
$$U(z)=\frac{1}{\bigl(1+A\,e^{bz}\bigr)^{2}},\qquad A,b>0.\tag{7}$$
(Any $A>0$ just shifts $z$, so set $A=1$ by translating the origin.) With $w=e^{
bz}$,
$$U'=-\frac{2b\,w}{(1+w)^{3}},\qquad U''=-\frac{2b^{2}w(1-2w)}{(1+w)^{4}},\qquad
 U(1-U)=\frac{w(2+w)}{(1+w)^{4}}.$$
Substituting into (1) and multiplying by $(1+w)^{4}/w$ yields two equations (con
stant term and coefficient of $w$):
$$-2Db^{2}-2bc+2k=0,\qquad 4Db^{2}-2bc+k=0.$$
Solving,
$$b^{2}=\frac{k}{6D}\;\Longrightarrow\; b=\sqrt{\frac{k}{6D}},\qquad c=\frac{5k/
6}{b}=\frac{5}{\sqrt{6}}\sqrt{kD}=\frac{5}{\sqrt6}\sqrt{kD}.$$
One checks $\frac{5}{\sqrt6}\approx2.041>2$, so this speed indeed satisfies $c\g
e2\sqrt{kD}$, as it must. The explicit wave is
$$\boxed{\,U(z)=\frac{1}{\bigl(1+e^{\sqrt{k/(6D)}\,z}\bigr)^{2}},\qquad c=\frac{
5}{\sqrt6}\sqrt{kD}=\frac{5\sqrt{6}}{6}\sqrt{kD}\,}.\tag{8}$$
It obeys $U(-\infty)=1$, $U(+\infty)=0$, $0\le U\le1$, $U'<0$ — a valid member o
f the family at the special speed $c=5\sqrt{kD/6}$.

---

## 4. Summary

Substituting $u(x,t)=U(z)$, $z=x-ct$, reduces the Fisher-KPP PDE to
$$D\,U''+c\,U'+k\,U(1-U)=0,\qquad U(-\infty)=1,\;U(+\infty)=0,\;0\le U\le1.$$

- **Wavespeed condition:** a physically realistic (monotone, $0\le U\le1$) trave
lling wave exists **if and only if**
$$\boxed{c\ge c_{\min}=2\sqrt{kD}}.$$
The bound comes from requiring the linearized decay at the leading edge $U\to0$
to be non-oscillatory (real characteristic roots $D\lambda^{2}+c\lambda+k=0$); s
ufficiency for every $c\ge2\sqrt{kD}$ is the KPP existence theorem. Compact-supp
ort initial data select the minimum speed $c=2\sqrt{kD}$.

- **Explicit solution** (at the special speed $c=5\sqrt{kD/6}\ge2\sqrt{kD}$):
$$\boxed{u(x,t)=\frac{1}{\Bigl(1+\exp\!\bigl[\sqrt{k/(6D)}\,(x-ct)\bigr]\Bigr)^{
2}},\qquad c=\frac{5}{\sqrt6}\sqrt{kD}.}$$
