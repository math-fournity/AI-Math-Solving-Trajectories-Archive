## Solution

We minimize $\int_0^1 f(x)^2\,dx$ subject to the two linear constraints $\langle
 f, 1\rangle = 5$ and $\langle f, x\rangle = 3$, where $\langle g, h\rangle = \i
nt_0^1 g(x)h(x)\,dx$ is the $L^2[0,1]$ inner product.

### Reduction to a finite-dimensional problem

Decompose any $f \in L^2[0,1]$ as $f = g + h$, where $g \in V := \operatorname{s
pan}\{1, x\}$ and $h \perp V$ (orthogonal projection onto $V$). Since both $1$ a
nd $x$ lie in $V$,

$$\langle f, 1\rangle = \langle g, 1\rangle, \qquad \langle f, x\rangle = \langl
e g, x\rangle.$$

So the constraints depend only on $g$, while

$$\|f\|^2 = \|g\|^2 + \|h\|^2 \geq \|g\|^2,$$

with equality iff $h = 0$. Hence the infimum is attained (in $L^2$, and by a con
tinuous function) at $f = g \in V$, i.e. $f(x) = a + bx$ for some $a, b$.

### Solving for $a, b$

The constraints become:
$$a + \tfrac{b}{2} = 5, \qquad \tfrac{a}{2} + \tfrac{b}{3} = 3.$$

From the first equation, $a = 5 - \tfrac{b}{2}$. Substituting into the second:
$$\tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(5 - \tfrac{b}{2}\right) + \tfrac{b}{3} = \tfrac{5}{2} - \t
frac{b}{4} + \tfrac{b}{3} = \tfrac{5}{2} + \tfrac{b}{12} = 3,$$
so $\tfrac{b}{12} = \tfrac{1}{2}$, giving $b = 6$, and then $a = 5 - 3 = 2$.

Thus the minimizer is $f(x) = 2 + 6x$.

**Verification of constraints:**
- $\int_0^1 (2+6x)\,dx = 2 + 3 = 5$. ✓
- $\int_0^1 x(2+6x)\,dx = \int_0^1 (2x+6x^2)\,dx = 1 + 2 = 3$. ✓

### Computing the infimum

$$I = \int_0^1 (2+6x)^2\,dx = \int_0^1 (4 + 24x + 36x^2)\,dx = 4 + 12 + 12 = 28.
$$

### Conclusion

The infimum is achieved by $f(x) = 2 + 6x \in S$, and

$$\boxed{I = 28}.$$
