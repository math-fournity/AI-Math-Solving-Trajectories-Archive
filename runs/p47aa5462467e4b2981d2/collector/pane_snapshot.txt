# KL Divergence: Laplace vs. Exponential

## Setup and Convention

The KL divergence "between distribution $f$ and distribution $g$" follows the st
andard convention
$$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(f \,\|\, g) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,\log\!\frac{f(x)}{
g(x)}\,dx,$$
with the Laplace density $f(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}e^{-|x|}$ (supported on all of $\math
bb{R}$) as the first argument $P$, and the exponential density $g(x)=e^{-x}$ (su
pported only on $[0,\infty)$) as the second argument $Q$.

## Key Observation: Support Mismatch

The exponential density satisfies $g(x)=0$ for all $x<0$, whereas the Laplace de
nsity satisfies
$$f(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}e^{x}>0 \quad\text{for all }x<0.$$
Hence $f$ assigns strictly positive probability to the entire negative half-line
:
$$\Pr_f(X<0)=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\tfrac{1}{2}e^{x}\,dx=\tfrac{1}{2}>0,$$
while $\Pr_g(X<0)=0$.

## Absolute Continuity Fails

A necessary condition for $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(f\|g)<\infty$ is that $f$ be **absolu
tely continuous** with respect to $g$ (denoted $f \ll g$): every set on which $g
$ vanishes must also have $f$-measure zero. Equivalently, the ratio $f(x)/g(x)$
must be well-defined (finite) $f$-almost everywhere.

Here the set $A=(-\infty,0)$ satisfies $g(A)=0$ yet $f(A)=\tfrac12>0$. Therefore
 $f\not\ll g$, and by the defining property of KL divergence,
$$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(f\,\|\,g)=+\infty.$$

## Explicit Integral Verification

Splitting the integral confirms this concretely. On the non-negative part,
$$\int_{0}^{\infty} f(x)\log\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}\,dx
=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tfrac12 e^{-x}\log\!\Big(\tfrac12\Big)\,dx
=-\frac{\log 2}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x}\,dx
=-\frac{\log 2}{2},$$
which is finite. On the negative part, however,
$$\int_{-\infty}^{0} f(x)\log\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}\,dx
=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\tfrac12 e^{x}\,\log\!\Big(\tfrac{\tfrac12 e^{x}}{0}\Big)\,dx
=+\infty,$$
since the integrand is $+\infty$ on a set of positive $f$-measure. Adding the tw
o pieces,
$$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(f\,\|\,g)=-\frac{\log 2}{2}+\infty=+\infty.$$

## Conclusion

The Kullback-Leibler divergence of the Laplace distribution $\tfrac12 e^{-|x|}$
relative to the exponential distribution $e^{-x}$ ($x\ge 0$) is **not finite**:
$$\boxed{\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}(f\,\|\,g)=+\infty\,}.$$
The divergence diverges because the Laplace law places positive mass ($\tfrac12$
) on $(-\infty,0)$, a region where the exponential density is identically zero,
violating the absolute-continuity requirement $f\ll g$ that is necessary for a f
inite KL divergence.

*(For completeness: the reverse direction is finite, $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(g\,\|\,f)=
\int_0^\infty e^{-x}\log\!\big(e^{-x}/\tfrac12 e^{-x}\big)\,dx=\log 2$, since $g
$ is supported on $[0,\infty)\subset\operatorname{supp}(f)$ and $g\ll f$. But th
e problem as stated, with $f$ first, yields $+\infty$.)*
