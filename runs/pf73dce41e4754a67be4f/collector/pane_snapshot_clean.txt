## Proof

**Setup.** A weight function on $[n]$ is a vector $w \in \mathbb{R}^n$. Two weig
ht functions are *equivalent*, $w \sim w'$, if they induce the same weak orderin
g on all subsets by total weight:
$$w \sim w' \iff \bigl[\forall\, S,T \subseteq [n]:\; w(S) \le w(T) \iff w'(S) \
le w'(T)\bigr],$$
where $w(S)=\sum_{i\in S} w_i$. The equivalence classes are the full-dimensional
 cones (regions) of the central hyperplane arrangement $\mathcal{A}_n$ in $\math
bb{R}^n$ with hyperplanes
$$H_v = \{w \in \mathbb{R}^n : w \cdot v = 0\}, \qquad v \in \{-1,0,1\}^n \setmi
nus \{0\},$$
since $w(S)=w(T) \iff w\cdot(\chi_S - \chi_T)=0$ and $\{\chi_S-\chi_T : S,T\subs
eteq[n]\} = \{-1,0,1\}^n$.

---

### Upper bound: $2^{O(n^2)}$

The arrangement $\mathcal{A}_n$ has $m = (3^n-1)/2$ distinct hyperplanes (identi
fying $v$ and $-v$). By the standard bound on central arrangements in $\mathbb{R
}^n$, the number of regions is
$$r(\mathcal{A}_n) \;\le\; 2\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{m-1}{k} \;\le\; 2n\cdot m^{n-
1} \;\le\; 2n\cdot 3^{n(n-1)} \;=\; 2^{O(n^2)}.$$

---

### Lower bound: $2^{\Omega(n^2)}$

Consider the sub-arrangement $\mathcal{A}_n' \subseteq \mathcal{A}_n$ using only
 vectors $v \in \{-1,1\}^n$, giving $2^{n-1}$ hyperplanes. Since adding hyperpla
nes can only increase the region count, $r(\mathcal{A}_n) \ge r(\mathcal{A}_n')$
.

The regions of $\mathcal{A}_n'$ are in bijection with **homogeneous threshold fu
nctions** on $n$ Boolean variables: each region yields a distinct sign pattern $
\operatorname{sign}(w\cdot v)$ for $v\in\{-1,1\}^n$.

**Zuev's theorem (1989):** The number of (affine) threshold functions on $n$ var
iables satisfies $T(n) = 2^{(1+o(1))\,n^2}$.

Each homogeneous threshold function $w$ gives rise to at most $2^n+1$ distinct a
ffine threshold functions by varying the bias $b$ (since there are $2^n$ input p
oints, at most $2^n+1$ bias values yield distinct functions). Hence
$$T_0(n) \;\ge\; \frac{T(n)}{2^n+1} \;=\; \frac{2^{(1+o(1))\,n^2}}{2^{O(n)}} \;=
\; 2^{\Omega(n^2)}.$$
Since $r(\mathcal{A}_n') = T_0(n)$, we obtain $r(\mathcal{A}_n) = 2^{\Omega(n^2)
}$.

---

### Combining

$$r(\mathcal{A}_n) = 2^{\Theta(n^2)}.$$

The number of non-equivalent weight functions on $[n]$ is $2^{\Theta(n^2)}$.

---

### Relation to the Frank–Tardos bound

The Frank–Tardos theorem guarantees that every equivalence class has an integer
representative $w\in\mathbb{Z}^n$ with $\|w\|_\infty \le 2^{O(n^3)}$. The total
count of such bounded integer vectors is
$$(2^{O(n^3)}+1)^n = 2^{O(n^4)},$$
which is the stated upper bound. However, this counts **all** bounded weight vec
tors, not equivalence classes. Since exponentially many (in $n^2$) bounded vecto
rs share the same equivalence class, the true number of *non-equivalent* weight
functions is far smaller:

$$2^{\Theta(n^2)} \;\ll\; 2^{O(n^4)}.$$

The gap arises because the weight magnitude $2^{O(n^3)}$ per coordinate is neces
sary to *represent* all classes with integers, but the number of classes itself
is governed by the geometry of the hyperplane arrangement, yielding only $2^{\Th
eta(n^2)}$.

---

### Conclusion

The optimal degree of the polynomial in the exponent is $\boxed{2}$.
