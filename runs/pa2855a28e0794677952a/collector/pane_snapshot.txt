## Proof

**Answer: $x \neq i$.**

**Step 1: Verify that $i$ satisfies the equation.**

We have $i^2 = -1$ and $\log(i^2) = \log(-1) = i\pi$ (principal branch). Hence
$$\frac{\log(i^2)}{i} = \frac{i\pi}{i} = \pi. \checkmark$$
Equivalently, $i^2 = -1 = e^{i\pi}$, so $i$ is a solution of $z^2 = e^{\pi z}$.

**Step 2: Identify what $x = -\frac{2W(\pi/2)}{\pi}$ actually is.**

Since $\pi/2 > 0$, the principal branch $W(\pi/2)$ is a **real positive** number
 (numerically $W(\pi/2) \approx 0.7429...$). Therefore
$$x = -\frac{2W(\pi/2)}{\pi} \approx -\frac{2 \times 0.7429}{3.14159} \approx -0
.4729\ldots$$
is a **real** (and negative) number.

**Step 3: Derive that this $x$ is the real solution of $\log(x^2)/x = \pi$.**

Setting $x = -t$ with $t > 0$, the equation $\log(x^2) = \pi x$ becomes $2\log t
 = -\pi t$, i.e. $t\,e^{\pi t/2} = 1$. Substituting $u = \pi t/2$ gives $u\,e^{u
} = \pi/2$, so $u = W(\pi/2)$, hence $t = \frac{2W(\pi/2)}{\pi}$ and $x = -\frac
{2W(\pi/2)}{\pi}$. This confirms $x$ is the **unique real** solution.

**Step 4: Conclude.**

The equation $\frac{\log(z^2)}{z} = \pi$, i.e. $z^2 = e^{\pi z}$, is transcenden
tal and possesses **multiple distinct** solutions in $\mathbb{C}$:
- $z = i$ (a purely imaginary solution), and
- $z = x = -\frac{2W(\pi/2)}{\pi}$ (the unique real solution, $\approx -0.473$).

Since $x$ is real while $i$ is purely imaginary and nonzero, they cannot be equa
l. Both satisfying the same equation merely shows the equation has more than one
 root; it does **not** imply the roots coincide.

$$\boxed{x \neq i}$$
