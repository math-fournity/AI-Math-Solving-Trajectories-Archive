## Plücker Embedding of the Grassmannian

**Setup.** Let $G(k,n)$ denote the Grassmannian parametrizing $k$-dimensional qu
otients of $\mathscr{O}^{\oplus n}$ (equivalently, $k$-dimensional subspaces of
an $n$-dimensional vector space $V$, depending on convention; the quotient and s
ubspace versions are dual and yield the same construction). We describe the Plüc
ker embedding
$$P: G(k,n) \longrightarrow \mathbb{P}^{\binom{n}{k}-1}.$$

---

### Construction

A point of $G(k,n)$ over a field $K$ is a surjection $q: V = K^n \twoheadrightar
row W$ with $\dim W = k$, or equivalently (choosing a splitting) a $k$-dimension
al subspace $U \subseteq V$. Fix the standard basis $e_1, \dots, e_n$ of $V$ and
 let $e_1^*, \dots, e_n^*$ be the dual basis.

Given a $k$-dimensional subspace $U \subseteq V$, choose a basis $u_1, \dots, u_
k$ of $U$ and write each $u_i$ in coordinates:
$$u_i = \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij}\, e_j, \qquad A = (a_{ij}) \in \mathrm{Mat}_{k \ti
mes n}(K).$$

The rows of $A$ form a basis of $U$. Since $U$ is $k$-dimensional, $A$ has rank
$k$.

**Definition of the Plücker coordinates.** For every multi-index $I = (i_1 < i_2
 < \cdots < i_k)$ with $1 \le i_1 < \cdots < i_k \le n$, define
$$p_I(U) := \det\!\bigl(A_{[k],\, I}\bigr) = \det \begin{pmatrix} a_{1,i_1} & \c
dots & a_{1,i_k} \\ \vdots & & \vdots \\ a_{k,i_1} & \cdots & a_{k,i_k}\end{pmat
rix},$$
the $k \times k$ minor of $A$ using columns indexed by $I$. There are $\binom{n}
{k}$ such minors, giving a vector
$$\bigl(p_I(U)\bigr)_{I} \in K^{\binom{n}{k}} \setminus \{0\}.$$

At least one minor is nonzero because $A$ has rank $k$, so this vector is not ze
ro. We set
$$P(U) := \bigl[p_I(U)\bigr]_{I} \in \mathbb{P}^{\binom{n}{k}-1}.$$

---

### Well-definedness

If we change the basis of $U$ by an invertible $k \times k$ matrix $B \in \mathr
m{GL}_k(K)$, the matrix $A$ becomes $BA$, and every minor transforms as
$$\det\bigl((BA)_{[k],\,I}\bigr) = \det(B)\cdot \det\bigl(A_{[k],\,I}\bigr).$$

Thus the entire vector $\bigl(p_I(U)\bigr)_I$ is scaled by the common factor $\d
et(B) \neq 0$, so the point $\bigl[p_I(U)\bigr]_I \in \mathbb{P}^{\binom{n}{k}-1
}$ is **independent** of the choice of basis. Hence $P$ is a well-defined map on
 $G(k,n)$.

---

### Functorial / sheaf-theoretic description

On $G(k,n)$ there is a universal (tautological) exact sequence of vector bundles
:
$$0 \longrightarrow S \longrightarrow V \otimes \mathscr{O}_{G} \longrightarrow
Q \longrightarrow 0,$$
where $S$ is the tautological subbundle (rank $n-k$) and $Q$ is the tautological
 quotient bundle (rank $k$). The Plücker line bundle is
$$\mathscr{O}_{G}(1) := \det Q = \bigwedge^{\,k} Q.$$

The map $V \otimes \mathscr{O}_G \to Q$ induces, by taking exterior powers, a su
rjection
$$\bigwedge^{\,k}(V \otimes \mathscr{O}_G) \cong \bigl(\bigwedge^{\,k} V\bigr) \
otimes \mathscr{O}_G \longrightarrow \bigwedge^{\,k} Q = \mathscr{O}_G(1).$$

This is a surjection of vector bundles, equivalently a global section
$$\varphi \in H^0\!\bigl(G,\, \mathscr{O}_G(1) \otimes \bigl(\bigwedge^{\,k} V\b
igr)^{\vee}\bigr) = \mathrm{Hom}\!\bigl(\bigwedge^{\,k} V,\, H^0(\mathscr{O}_G(1
))\bigr)^{\vee},$$
which dually gives a linear map
$$\bigwedge^{\,k} V \longrightarrow H^0\!\bigl(G,\,\mathscr{O}_G(1)\bigr).$$

Choosing a basis of $\bigwedge^k V$ (i.e., the basis $\{e_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \w
edge e_{i_k}\}$ indexed by $I$) identifies $\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^k V) \cong \mat
hbb{P}^{\binom{n}{k}-1}$, and the induced map on points is exactly
$$P: G(k,n) \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}\!\bigl(\bigwedge^{\,k} V\bigr) \cong \mat
hbb{P}^{\binom{n}{k}-1}, \qquad [q: V \twoheadrightarrow W] \mapsto \bigl[\bigwe
dge^{\,k} q\bigr],$$
where $\bigwedge^k q: \bigwedge^k V \to \bigwedge^k W \cong K$ is a nonzero line
ar functional on $\bigwedge^k V$, i.e., a point of $\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^k V^\ve
e) \cong \mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^k V)$.

---

### Injectivity

Suppose $P(U_1) = P(U_2)$, i.e., the Plücker coordinate vectors are proportional
: $p_I(U_1) = \lambda\, p_I(U_2)$ for all $I$ and some $\lambda \neq 0$. Pick a
multi-index $I_0$ with $p_{I_0}(U_2) \neq 0$. The columns of $A_2$ indexed by $I
_0$ form an invertible $k\times k$ matrix; after a change of basis (which scales
 all Plücker coordinates by $\det$), we may assume $A_2$ has the identity matrix
 in columns $I_0$. Then proportionality with $\lambda = 1$ forces $p_I(U_1) = p_
I(U_2)$ for all $I$, which determines every $k\times k$ minor of $A_1$ and $A_2$
 to be equal. By the Cauchy–Binet formula (or Laplace expansion), this forces $A
_1 = A_2$, hence $U_1 = U_2$. Therefore $P$ is **injective**.

---

### Image: the Plücker relations

The image $P(G(k,n))$ is a closed subvariety of $\mathbb{P}^{\binom{n}{k}-1}$, c
ut out by the **Plücker relations** (quadratic relations among the $p_I$). For $
k = 1$ or $k = n-1$ there are no relations and $P$ is surjective (giving $G(1,n)
 = \mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ and $G(n-1,n) = (\mathbb{P}^{n-1})^\vee$). For $2 \le k \le
 n-2$, the relations are:

For all multi-indices $I = (i_1 < \cdots < i_{k-1})$ and $J = (j_1 < \cdots < j_
{k+1})$,
$$\sum_{\ell=1}^{k+1} (-1)^{\ell}\, p_{I \cup \{j_\ell\}}\; p_{J \setminus \{j_\
ell\}} \;=\; 0,$$
where $p_{I'} = 0$ if $I'$ has repeated indices or is not strictly increasing (a
nd signs are adjusted accordingly). These generate the homogeneous ideal of $P(G
(k,n))$ in $\mathbb{P}^{\binom{n}{k}-1}$, so
$$G(k,n) \cong \mathrm{Proj}\, K\bigl[\,p_I \mid |I|=k\,\bigr]\big/\!\bigl(\text
{Plücker relations}\bigr).$$

---

### Summary

The **Plücker embedding** is the closed immersion
$$\boxed{P: G(k,n) \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}\!\Bigl(\bigwedge^{\,k} V\Bigr) \co
ng \mathbb{P}^{\binom{n}{k}-1}, \qquad \bigl[q: V \twoheadrightarrow W\bigr] \ma
psto \bigl[\bigwedge^{\,k} q\bigr],}$$
sending a $k$-dimensional quotient (or subspace) to the line $\bigwedge^k W \sub
seteq \bigwedge^k V$ determined by its $k$-th exterior power. In coordinates it
records all $\binom{n}{k}$ maximal minors of a $k \times n$ matrix representing
the quotient, modulo the common $\mathrm{GL}_k$-scaling. It is well-defined, inj
ective, and its image is the closed subvariety of $\mathbb{P}^{\binom{n}{k}-1}$
defined by the quadratic Plücker relations; the pullback of $\mathscr{O}(1)$ is
the tautological bundle $\det Q = \mathscr{O}_{G}(1)$.
