# Solution

**Answer: Yes.** The entire neighborhood (including the puncture) is biholomorph
ic to the unit disk, and this can be established using only the (elementary) **R
iemann removable singularity theorem**, without invoking the uniformization theo
rem.

---

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $X$ be a Riemann surface, $p \in X$, and $U \subset X$ an open ne
ighborhood of $p$ such that $U \setminus \{p\}$ is biholomorphic to the puncture
d unit disk $\mathbb{D}^* = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : 0 < |z| < 1\}$. Let

$$\varphi : \mathbb{D}^* \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} U \setminus \{p\}$$

be a biholomorphism. We construct a biholomorphic extension $\Phi : \mathbb{D} \
xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} U$.

---

**Step 1: $\varphi(z) \to p$ as $z \to 0$.**

Let $W \subset U$ be any open neighborhood of $p$ in $X$. Then $W \setminus \{p\
}$ is an open neighborhood of the puncture in $U \setminus \{p\}$. Since $\varph
i$ is a homeomorphism, $\varphi^{-1}(W \setminus \{p\})$ is an open neighborhood
 of the puncture $0$ in $\mathbb{D}^*$, hence contains $\{0 < |z| < \varepsilon\
}$ for some $\varepsilon > 0$. Therefore $\varphi(z) \in W$ for all $0 < |z| < \
varepsilon$. Since $W$ was arbitrary, $\varphi(z) \to p$ as $z \to 0$. $\checkma
rk$

---

**Step 2: Local extension via the removable singularity theorem.**

Choose a coordinate chart $(V, \psi)$ centered at $p$, i.e., $V \subset U$ is an
 open neighborhood of $p$ and $\psi : V \to \psi(V) \subset \mathbb{C}$ is a bih
olomorphism with $\psi(p) = 0$. Shrinking $V$ if necessary, assume $\psi(V)$ is
a bounded open disk around $0$.

By Step 1, there exists $\varepsilon > 0$ such that $\varphi(\{0 < |z| < \vareps
ilon\}) \subset V \setminus \{p\}$. Define

$$g := \psi \circ \varphi : \{0 < |z| < \varepsilon\} \longrightarrow \psi(V \se
tminus \{p\}) = \psi(V) \setminus \{0\}.$$

Then $g$ is holomorphic and **bounded** (since $\psi(V)$ is bounded). By the **R
iemann removable singularity theorem** *(a bounded holomorphic function on a pun
ctured disk extends holomorphically across the puncture)*, $g$ extends to a holo
morphic function

$$\tilde{g} : \{|z| < \varepsilon\} \to \mathbb{C}, \qquad \tilde{g}\big|_{\{0<|
z|<\varepsilon\}} = g.$$

By continuity and Step 1:

$$\tilde{g}(0) = \lim_{z \to 0} g(z) = \lim_{z \to 0} \psi(\varphi(z)) = \psi(p)
 = 0.$$

**Key remark on the removable singularity theorem.** Its standard proof is eleme
ntary: writing the Laurent expansion $g(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} a_n z^n$,
 Cauchy's estimate on $|z| = r$ gives $|a_n| \le M \, r^{-n}$ where $M = \sup|g|
$. For $n < 0$, $r^{-n} \to 0$ as $r \to 0$, forcing $a_n = 0$. No uniformizatio
n theorem is used.

---

**Step 3: The extension is a local biholomorphism at $0$.**

For $z \neq 0$ (with $|z| < \varepsilon$), we have $\tilde{g}(z) = g(z) = \psi(\
varphi(z)) \neq 0$, since $\varphi(z) \neq p$ and $\psi$ is injective with $\psi
(p) = 0$. Thus $\tilde{g}$ has an **isolated zero at $0$** and no other zeros ne
arby.

Suppose for contradiction that $\tilde{g}$ has a zero of order $m \geq 2$ at $0$
: $\tilde{g}(z) = z^m h(z)$ with $h(0) \neq 0$. Then for sufficiently small $w \
neq 0$ near $0$, the equation $\tilde{g}(z) = w$ has $m$ distinct solutions $z_1
, \dots, z_m$ near $0$, all nonzero. Each $z_i$ satisfies $\psi(\varphi(z_i)) =
w$, i.e., $\varphi(z_i) = \psi^{-1}(w)$. This gives $m \geq 2$ distinct preimage
s of the same point under $\varphi$, **contradicting the injectivity of $\varphi
$**.

Therefore $m = 1$, i.e., $\tilde{g}'(0) \neq 0$, and $\tilde{g}$ is a **local bi
holomorphism** at $0$.

---

**Step 4: Global biholomorphism $\mathbb{D} \to U$.**

Define $\Phi : \mathbb{D} \to U$ by

$$\Phi(z) = \begin{cases} \varphi(z) & \text{if } z \in \mathbb{D}^*, \\ p & \te
xt{if } z = 0. \end{cases}$

- **Holomorphicity:** On $\mathbb{D}^*$, $\Phi = \varphi$ is holomorphic. Near $
0$, in local coordinates, $\Phi = \psi^{-1} \circ \tilde{g}$, which is holomorph
ic (composition of holomorphic maps). So $\Phi$ is holomorphic on all of $\mathb
b{D}$.

- **Injectivity:** $\Phi$ is injective on $\mathbb{D}^*$ (since $\varphi$ is). A
lso $\Phi(0) = p$ while $\Phi(z) = \varphi(z) \in U \setminus \{p\}$ for $z \neq
 0$, so $\Phi(0) \neq \Phi(z)$ for $z \neq 0$. Hence $\Phi$ is injective on $\ma
thbb{D}$.

- **Surjectivity onto $U$:** $\Phi(\mathbb{D}) = \Phi(\mathbb{D}^*) \cup \{\Phi(
0)\} = (U \setminus \{p\}) \cup \{p\} = U$.

An injective holomorphic map between Riemann surfaces is a biholomorphism onto i
ts image (its derivative never vanishes, by the open mapping theorem / identity
theorem argument). Therefore

$$\Phi : \mathbb{D} \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} U$$

is a biholomorphism. $\blacksquare$

---

**Conclusion.** The puncture is a **removable singularity**: the complex structu
re extends uniquely across $p$, yielding $U \cong \mathbb{D}$. The only analytic
 tool required is the Riemann removable singularity theorem for bounded holomorp
hic functions on a punctured disk, whose proof (via Laurent series and Cauchy es
timates) is entirely elementary. **The uniformization theorem is not needed.**
