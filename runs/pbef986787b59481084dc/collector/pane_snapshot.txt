## Solution

**Yes, $\mu_f = \mu_g$.**

### Proof

Recall that the Schwartz space $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})$ contains $C_c^\infty(\m
athbb{R})$, the space of smooth compactly supported functions. Hence the hypothe
sis

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} h\,d\mu_f = \int_{\mathbb{R}} h\,d\mu_g \quad \text{for all
} h \in \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})$$

implies in particular

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} \varphi\,d\mu_f = \int_{\mathbb{R}} \varphi\,d\mu_g \quad \t
ext{for all } \varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}). \tag{1}$$

**Step 1: Extend to all of $C_c(\mathbb{R})$.**

The space $C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ is uniformly dense in $C_c(\mathbb{R})$: give
n $\psi \in C_c(\mathbb{R})$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, by mollification there exist
s $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ with $\|\psi - \varphi\|_\infty < \vareps
ilon$. Using (1) and the triangle inequality,

$$\left|\int \psi\,d\mu_f - \int \psi\,d\mu_g\right| \leq \left|\int (\psi - \va
rphi)\,d\mu_f\right| + \left|\int \varphi\,d\mu_f - \int \varphi\,d\mu_g\right|
+ \left|\int (\varphi - \psi)\,d\mu_g\right|.$$

The middle term vanishes by (1); the two outer terms are each bounded by $\varep
silon \cdot \mu_f(\mathbb{R}) = \varepsilon$ and $\varepsilon \cdot \mu_g(\mathb
b{R}) = \varepsilon$ respectively, since $\mu_f, \mu_g$ are probability measures
. Letting $\varepsilon \to 0$,

$$\int \psi\,d\mu_f = \int \psi\,d\mu_g \quad \text{for all } \psi \in C_c(\math
bb{R}). \tag{2}$$

**Step 2: Conclude $\mu_f = \mu_g$.**

A locally finite Borel measure on $\mathbb{R}$ is uniquely determined by its act
ion on $C_c(\mathbb{R})$ (this is the uniqueness part of the Riesz–Markov repres
entation theorem: the linear functional $L(\psi) = \int \psi\,d\mu$ on $C_c(\mat
hbb{R})$ determines $\mu$). Both $\mu_f$ and $\mu_g$ are finite (hence locally f
inite) Borel measures on $\mathbb{R}$, and (2) says they define the same positiv
e linear functional on $C_c(\mathbb{R})$. Therefore $\mu_f = \mu_g$.
