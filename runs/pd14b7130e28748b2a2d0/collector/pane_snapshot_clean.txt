The statement is TRUE.

Below is the justification.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Setup and definitions

Let $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ be open (the argument is local, so one
dimension suffices; the same proof works in $\mathbb{R}^d$ with partial
derivatives). Recall that $f \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ is weakly
differentiable if there exists $g \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ such that for
every test function $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$,

$$\int_\Omega f,\varphi' = -\int_\Omega g,\varphi. \tag{$\star$}$$

"Converges locally in $L^1$" means convergence in $L^1(K)$ for every compact $K
\Subset \Omega$; "locally Cauchy in $L^1$" means Cauchy in $L^1(K)$ for every
compact $K \Subset \Omega$.

We must prove:

│ $f$ is weakly differentiable $\iff$ there exist smooth $f_n$ with $f_n \to f$
│ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ and ${f_n'}$ Cauchy in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$; and in
│ that case $f' = \lim f_n'$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

($\Rightarrow$) Weakly differentiable $\Longrightarrow$ such a sequence exists

Assume $f$ is weakly differentiable with weak derivative $g \in
L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$. Let $\rho_\varepsilon$ be a standard mollifier and
set $$f_\varepsilon := \rho_\varepsilon * f, \qquad \varepsilon > 0.$$ On any
compact $K \Subset \Omega$, for $\varepsilon$ small enough the convolution is
well defined on a neighborhood of $K$, and standard mollification theory gives:

  1. $f_\varepsilon \in C^\infty$,
  2. $f_\varepsilon \to f$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$,
  3. $f_\varepsilon' = \rho_\varepsilon * g \to g$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}
  (\Omega)$.

(The identity $f_\varepsilon' = \rho_\varepsilon * g$ follows by differentiating
under the convolution and using the weak-derivative relation, or equivalently by
integrating by parts in the mollifier integral using $(\star)$.)

Since $f_\varepsilon' \to g$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$, the family
${f_\varepsilon'}$ is Cauchy in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$. Taking $\varepsilon = 1/n$
yields a sequence $f_n = f_{1/n}$ of smooth functions with $f_n \to f$ in
$L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ and ${f_n'}$ Cauchy (indeed convergent) in
$L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$, with $f_n' \to g = f'$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

($\Leftarrow$) Such a sequence exists $\Longrightarrow$ $f$ is weakly
differentiable

Assume $f_n \in C^\infty(\Omega)$ with $f_n \to f$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}
(\Omega)$ and ${f_n'}$ Cauchy in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$.

Step 1 — the derivatives converge. Since $L^1(K)$ is complete for every compact
$K \Subset \Omega$, the Cauchy condition on ${f_n'}$ gives a function $g \in
L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ with $$f_n' \to g \quad \text{in }
L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega).$$

Step 2 — pass to the limit in the integration-by-parts identity. Fix $\varphi
\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ and let $K := \operatorname{supp} \varphi \Subset
\Omega$. Since each $f_n$ is smooth, classical integration by parts gives $$
\int_\Omega f_n,\varphi' = -\int_\Omega f_n',\varphi. \tag{1}$$

Both sides depend only on values on $K$. We have $f_n \to f$ in $L^1(K)$ and
$f_n' \to g$ in $L^1(K)$, while $\varphi, \varphi' \in L^\infty(K)$. Hence by
the $L^1$–$L^\infty$ duality estimate, $$\int_\Omega f_n,\varphi' \to
\int_\Omega f,\varphi', \qquad \int_\Omega f_n',\varphi \to \int_\Omega g,
\varphi.$$

Passing to the limit in (1), $$\int_\Omega f,\varphi' = -\int_\Omega g,\varphi
\qquad \forall, \varphi \in C_c^\infty(\Omega).$$

This is exactly $(\star)$, so $f$ is weakly differentiable with weak derivative
$g = \lim f_n'$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Conclusion

Both directions hold, and in each direction the weak derivative is precisely the
local $L^1$-limit of $f_n'$. Therefore the statement is true: weak
differentiability of $f$ is equivalent to the existence of a smooth sequence
$f_n \to f$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ whose derivatives ${f_n'}$ are Cauchy in
$L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$, and the weak derivative equals $\lim f_n'$ in
$L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$.
