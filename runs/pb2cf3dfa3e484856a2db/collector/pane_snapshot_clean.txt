## Proof

Let $t = \sin^2 x$, so $\cos^2 x = 1 - t$ with $t \in [0,1]$. The identity becom
es
$$P(t) + P(1-t) = 1 \quad \text{for all } t \in [0,1].$$
Since both sides are polynomials in $t$, the identity holds for all $t \in \math
bb{R}$.

**Reduction to an odd function.** Define $Q(t) = P(t) - \tfrac{1}{2}$. Then
$$Q(t) + Q(1-t) = 0, \quad\text{i.e.,}\quad Q(1-t) = -Q(t).$$
Substituting $y = t - \tfrac{1}{2}$ and setting $F(y) = Q(y + \tfrac{1}{2}) = P(
y+\tfrac{1}{2}) - \tfrac{1}{2}$, the relation becomes
$$F(-y) = -F(y),$$
so $F$ is an **odd** polynomial. Hence $F(y) = y\cdot V(y^2)$ for some polynomia
l $V$, and
$$P(y+\tfrac{1}{2}) = \tfrac{1}{2} + y\,V(y^2).$$

**Degree check.** $\deg P = 2023$, and shifting does not change degree, so $\deg
 F = 2023$. Since $F(y)=y\,V(y^2)$, we have $\deg F = 1 + 2\deg V$, giving $\deg
 V = 1011$ (consistent, since $2023$ is odd).

**Structure of $F$.** Writing $F(y) = \tfrac{1}{2} + y\,V(y^2)$: the term $y\,V(
y^2)$ contains only **odd** powers of $y$ (namely $y^1, y^3, \dots, y^{2023}$),
plus the constant $\tfrac{1}{2}$. In particular, **every even-power coefficient
of $F$ is zero**, including the coefficient of $y^{2022}$.

**Sum of roots.** Let $y_1, \dots, y_{2023}$ be the roots of $F$ (counted with m
ultiplicity). By Vieta,
$$\sum_{i=1}^{2023} y_i = -\frac{[y^{2022}]\,F}{[y^{2023}]\,F} = -\frac{0}{[y^{2
023}]\,F} = 0.$$

The roots of $P$ are $t_i = y_i + \tfrac{1}{2}$ (since $P(t_i)=0 \iff F(t_i-\tfr
ac12)=0$). Therefore
$$\sum_{i=1}^{2023} t_i = \sum_{i=1}^{2023}\left(y_i + \tfrac{1}{2}\right) = \le
ft(\sum_{i=1}^{2023} y_i\right) + \frac{2023}{2} = 0 + \frac{2023}{2} = \frac{20
23}{2}.$$

**Final computation.** With $p = 2023$ and $q = 2$ (coprime, since $2023$ is odd
),
$$pq = 2023 \times 2 = \boxed{4046}.$$
