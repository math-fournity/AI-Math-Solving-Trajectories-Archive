Looking at this problem, I need to determine the probability that among a father
's $n > 4$ children, at least one child has an older brother, a younger brother,
 a sister, and an aunt.

**Interpretation.** The "aunt" is the father's sister. Since the problem involve
s only one parameter $n$ and mentions probabilities "at any age" (across generat
ions), the natural self-contained interpretation is: the father is himself one o
f $n$ children (of the grandfather). Thus the grandfather has $n$ children — one
 is the father (male, given), and the other $n-1$ are each independently male or
 female with probability $1/2$. The father independently has $n$ children, each
independently male or female with probability $1/2$, ordered by age (position $1
$ = oldest, position $n$ = youngest).

The four conditions split into two independent groups:
- **Aunt condition (A):** at least one of the father's $n-1$ siblings is female.
- **Children condition (C):** among the $n$ children, some child has an older br
other, a younger brother, and a sister.

These are independent (disjoint sets of people), so $P = P(A) \cdot P(C)$.

---

**Computing $P(A)$.** The father's $n-1$ siblings are all male with probability
$(1/2)^{n-1}$. So:
$$P(A) = 1 - \frac{1}{2^{n-1}} = \frac{2^{n-1}-1}{2^{n-1}}.$$

---

**Computing $P(C)$.** Label the children $1, \ldots, n$ by age. Let $F_M$ = posi
tion of the first (oldest) male, $L_M$ = position of the last (youngest) male. A
 child at position $i$ has an older brother and a younger brother if and only if
 $F_M < i < L_M$ (there is a male before and after them). Such a child has a sis
ter if and only if there is a female among the other $n-1$ children.

**Condition (C) fails** when no child in the range $(F_M, L_M)$ has a sister. I
enumerate all failure cases:

**Case 1: Range is empty ($L_M \le F_M + 1$).** No child has both an older and y
ounger brother. This occurs when:
- **0 males** (all $n$ female): $1$ sequence.
- **1 male** (at any of $n$ positions): $n$ sequences.
- **2 males, adjacent** (at positions $k, k+1$ for $k = 1, \ldots, n-1$; rest fe
male): $n - 1$ sequences.

**Case 2: Range is non-empty but no child in it has a sister.** For every child
$i$ in $(F_M, L_M)$, all other children must be male. If the range has $\geq 2$
children, then any two of them force each other to be male, making all $n$ child
ren male — this is the **all-male** case: $1$ sequence. If the range has exactly
 $1$ child $i_0$, then all other $n-1$ children are male, so $i_0$ is the only p
otentially non-male child. If $i_0$ is male, all $n$ are male (already counted).
 If $i_0$ is female, she is the unique female, positioned at $F_M + 1$ with $L_M
 = F_M + 2$. Positions before $F_M$ must be female (contradicting uniqueness unl
ess $F_M = 1$), and positions after $L_M = 3$ must be female (contradicting uniq
ueness unless $n = 3$). Since $n \geq 5$, **this sub-case is impossible**.

**Total failure count:** $1 + n + (n-1) + 1 = 2n + 1$ sequences out of $2^n$.

Therefore:
$$P(C) = 1 - \frac{2n+1}{2^n} = \frac{2^n - 2n - 1}{2^n}.$$

---

**Verification for $n = 5$.** There are $2^5 = 32$ sequences. Failure sequences:
 $2(5)+1 = 11$.
- All female: FFFFF (1)
- One male: 5 sequences
- Two adjacent males: MMFFF, FMMFF, FFMMF, FFFMM (4)
- All male: MMMMM (1)

Total: $11$. So $P(C) = 21/32$. Spot-checking a success case: MFMFF has $F_M=1,
L_M=3$, range $=\{2\}$; child 2 (F) has older brother at 1, younger brother at 3
, sister at 4. ✓

$P(A) = 15/16$, so $P = \frac{21}{32} \cdot \frac{15}{16} = \frac{315}{512}$. ✓

$$\boxed{P = \left(1 - \frac{2n+1}{2^n}\right)\!\left(1 - \frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\righ
