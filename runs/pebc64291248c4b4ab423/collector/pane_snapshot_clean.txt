## Solution

**Simplifying the equation.** Let $f' = \frac{df}{dx}$ and $f'' = \frac{d^2f}{dx
^2}$. The given equation is

$$\frac{d}{dx}\!\bigl(f' - f\bigr)\cdot f \;-\; f'\cdot\bigl(f' - f\bigr) \;=\;
0.$$

Since $\frac{d}{dx}(f' - f) = f'' - f'$, this becomes

$$(f'' - f')\,f \;-\; f'\,(f' - f) \;=\; 0.$$

Expanding:

$$f''f - f'f - (f')^2 + f'f \;=\; 0,$$

and the two middle terms cancel, leaving

$$\boxed{f\,f'' = (f')^2.}$$

**Solving $ff'' = (f')^2$.** On any interval where $f \neq 0$, divide both sides
 by $f\,f'$ (assuming $f'\neq 0$; the case $f'=0$ gives $f''=0$, i.e. constant $
f$, which is included below):

$$\frac{f''}{f'} = \frac{f'}{f}.$$

Both sides are total derivatives:

$$\frac{d}{dx}\ln|f'| = \frac{d}{dx}\ln|f|.$$

Integrating:

$$\ln|f'| = \ln|f| + c_1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f' = k\,f, \qquad k \in \math
bb{R}.$$

This first-order ODE has the general solution

$$f(x) = C\,e^{kx}, \qquad C,\,k \in \mathbb{R}.$$

**Verification.** For $f(x) = Ce^{kx}$:

- $f' = Ck\,e^{kx}$,
- $f'' = Ck^2\,e^{kx}$,

so

$$f\,f'' = Ce^{kx}\cdot Ck^2 e^{kx} = C^2 k^2 e^{2kx}, \qquad (f')^2 = (Ck\,e^{k
x})^2 = C^2 k^2 e^{2kx}.$$

Thus $ff'' = (f')^2$, confirming the solution. The case $C=0$ gives the trivial
solution $f\equiv 0$, and $k=0$ gives constant solutions $f\equiv C$, both cover
ed.

**Conclusion.** Yes, the differential equation admits solutions. Its general sol
ution is

$$f(x) = C\,e^{kx}, \qquad C,\,k \in \mathbb{R}\text{ arbitrary constants.}$$
