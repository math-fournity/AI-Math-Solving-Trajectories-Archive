## Proof

We prove that every proper ideal $I$ of a Noetherian ring $R$ admits a primary d
ecomposition $I = Q_1 \cap \cdots \cap Q_n$ with each $Q_i$ being $P_i$-primary
for some prime $P_i$.

**Definition.** An ideal $Q$ is *primary* if $Q \neq R$ and whenever $ab \in Q$
with $a \notin Q$, then $b^n \in Q$ for some $n \geq 1$. Equivalently, $\sqrt{Q}
$ is prime; setting $P = \sqrt{Q}$, we say $Q$ is $P$-primary.

**Definition.** An ideal is *irreducible* if it cannot be written as the interse
ction of two strictly larger ideals: $I = J \cap K \implies I = J$ or $I = K$.

The proof proceeds in two steps.

---

### Step 1: Every proper ideal is a finite intersection of irreducible ideals.

We argue by contradiction using the Noetherian (ascending chain) condition. Supp
ose there exists a proper ideal that is *not* a finite intersection of irreducib
le ideals. Let $\mathcal{S}$ be the set of all such ideals. Since $R$ is Noether
ian, $\mathcal{S}$ has a maximal element $I$ (by the maximal principle: every no
n-empty set of ideals in a Noetherian ring has a maximal element).

Since $I \in \mathcal{S}$, the ideal $I$ is not itself irreducible. Therefore $I
 = J \cap K$ for some ideals $J, K$ with $I \subsetneq J$ and $I \subsetneq K$ (
strict containment).

Now $J$ and $K$ are proper ideals strictly containing $I$. By the maximality of
$I$ in $\mathcal{S}$, neither $J$ nor $K$ belongs to $\mathcal{S}$, so each is a
 finite intersection of irreducible ideals:
$$J = J_1 \cap \cdots \cap J_s, \qquad K = K_1 \cap \cdots \cap K_t.$$
But then
$$I = J \cap K = J_1 \cap \cdots \cap J_s \cap K_1 \cap \cdots \cap K_t,$$
a finite intersection of irreducible ideals. This contradicts $I \in \mathcal{S}
$.

Hence $\mathcal{S} = \emptyset$; every proper ideal is a finite intersection of
irreducible ideals.

---

### Step 2: Every irreducible ideal is primary.

Let $Q$ be an irreducible ideal in $R$, and suppose $ab \in Q$ with $a \notin Q$
. We must show $b^n \in Q$ for some $n \geq 1$.

Consider the ascending chain of ideals
$$Q \subseteq (Q : b) \subseteq (Q : b^2) \subseteq (Q : b^3) \subseteq \cdots$$
where $(Q : b^k) = \{r \in R : r\, b^k \in Q\}$. Since $R$ is Noetherian, this c
hain stabilizes: there exists $m$ such that
$$(Q : b^m) = (Q : b^{m+1}) = \cdots.$$

**Claim.** $Q = (Q + (a)) \cap (Q + (b^m)).$

*Proof of Claim.* The inclusion $\subseteq$ is immediate since $Q \subseteq Q +
(a)$ and $Q \subseteq Q + (b^m)$. For the reverse inclusion, let $x \in (Q + (a)
) \cap (Q + (b^m))$. Then
$$x = q_1 + r_1 a = q_2 + r_2 b^m$$
for some $q_1, q_2 \in Q$ and $r_1, r_2 \in R$. We need to show $x \in Q$, i.e.,
 $r_1 a \in Q$ (since $q_1 \in Q$).

From $r_1 a = q_2 + r_2 b^m$, multiply both sides by $b$:
$$r_1 a b = q_2 b + r_2 b^{m+1}.$$
Since $ab \in Q$, we have $r_1 a b \in Q$, and $q_2 b \in Q$, so $r_2 b^{m+1} \i
n Q$. This means $r_2 \in (Q : b^{m+1}) = (Q : b^m)$ by the stabilization. Hence
 $r_2 b^m \in Q$, and therefore $x = q_2 + r_2 b^m \in Q$. This proves the claim
.

Since $Q$ is irreducible and $Q = (Q + (a)) \cap (Q + (b^m))$, we have either $Q
 = Q + (a)$ or $Q = Q + (b^m)$.

- If $Q = Q + (a)$, then $a \in Q$, contradicting our assumption $a \notin Q$.
- Therefore $Q = Q + (b^m)$, which gives $b^m \in Q$.

This shows $Q$ is primary. Moreover, $\sqrt{Q}$ is a prime ideal $P$ (a standard
 fact: the radical of a primary ideal is prime), so $Q$ is $P$-primary.

---

### Conclusion

Combining Steps 1 and 2: every proper ideal $I$ of the Noetherian ring $R$ is a
finite intersection of irreducible ideals, and each irreducible ideal is primary
. Therefore
$$I = Q_1 \cap Q_2 \cap \cdots \cap Q_n$$
where each $Q_i$ is $P_i$-primary for some prime ideal $P_i = \sqrt{Q_i}$. $\bla
cksquare$
