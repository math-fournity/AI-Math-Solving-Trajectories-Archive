**Definitions.**
- A Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$ is *locally symmetric* if $\nabla R = 0$, i.e.,
the Riemann curvature tensor is parallel.
- $M$ has *constant sectional curvature* if the sectional curvature $K(\sigma)$
is the same for every $2$-plane $\sigma \subset T_pM$ at every point $p \in M$.

**Counterexample: Complex projective space $\mathbb{CP}^n$ with the Fubini–Study
 metric, $n \geq 2$.**

$\mathbb{CP}^n$ has real dimension $2n \geq 4 > 2$, satisfying the dimension hyp
othesis.

1. **$\nabla R = 0$:** $\mathbb{CP}^n$ is a Riemannian symmetric space, being th
e homogeneous space $SU(n+1)/S(U(n)\times U(1))$ with the Fubini–Study metric in
duced by the Killing form. Every Riemannian symmetric space satisfies $\nabla R
= 0$ (the curvature tensor is invariant under the geodesic symmetries, which act
 as $-\mathrm{id}$ on tangent spaces, forcing the covariant derivative of $R$ to
 vanish). Hence $\mathbb{CP}^n$ is locally symmetric.

2. **Sectional curvature is not constant:** With the standard normalization (hol
omorphic sectional curvature $= 4$), the sectional curvature of $\mathbb{CP}^n$
satisfies
$$1 \leq K(\sigma) \leq 4$$
   for every $2$-plane $\sigma$. Specifically:
   - A *holomorphic* plane (spanned by $X$ and $JX$) has sectional curvature $K
= 4$.
   - A *totally real* plane (spanned by $X$ and $Y$ with $Y \perp X, JX$) has se
ctional curvature $K = 1$.

   Since $n \geq 2$, both types of $2$-planes exist at every point, so the secti
onal curvature takes two distinct values ($1$ and $4$) at the same point. Theref
ore $\mathbb{CP}^n$ does **not** have constant sectional curvature.

**Conclusion.** $\mathbb{CP}^n$ ($n \geq 2$) is a Riemannian manifold of dimensi
on $> 2$ with $\nabla R = 0$ but without constant sectional curvature. Thus $\na
bla R = 0$ does **not** imply constant sectional curvature.

The answer is **False**.
