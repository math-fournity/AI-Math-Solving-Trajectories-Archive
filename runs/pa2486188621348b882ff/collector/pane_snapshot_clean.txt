## Solution

**Notation.** Let $a=BC$, $b=CA$, $c=AB$ with opposite angles $A,B,C$. Place $C=
(0,0)$, $B=(a,0)$, $A=(b\cos C,\, b\sin C)$.

### Step 1: Determine angle $C$

- $D$ = foot of altitude from $A$ to $BC$ (the $x$-axis): $D=(b\cos C,\,0)$.
- $E$ = foot of altitude from $B$ to $AC$. Since $AC$ passes through the origin
with unit direction $(\cos C,\sin C)$, the projection of $B=(a,0)$ onto $AC$ is
$$E = a\cos C\,(\cos C,\sin C) = (a\cos^2 C,\; a\cos C\sin C).$$

Then
$$DE^2 = (b\cos C - a\cos^2 C)^2 + (a\cos C\sin C)^2 = \cos^2 C\big[(b-a\cos C)^
2 + a^2\sin^2 C\big]$$
$$= \cos^2 C\,(a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C) = \cos^2 C\cdot c^2.$$

Since the triangle is acute, $\cos C>0$, so $DE = c\cos C$. The condition $AB =
2DE$ gives
$$c = 2c\cos C \implies \cos C = \tfrac12 \implies \boxed{C = 60^\circ}.$$

Since $AC>BC$, we have $b>a$, hence $B>A$; with $A+B=120^\circ$ this yields $A<6
0^\circ<B$.

### Step 2: Compute $\angle AIO$

Apply the law of cosines in $\triangle AIO$ at vertex $I$, using:
- $OA = R$,
- $IA = \dfrac{r}{\sin(A/2)}$,
- $OI^2 = R^2 - 2Rr$ (Euler),

where $r = 4R\sin\frac A2\sin\frac B2\sin\frac C2$.

$$\cos\angle AIO = \frac{IA^2+OI^2-OA^2}{2\cdot IA\cdot OI} = \frac{r^2/\sin^2\f
rac A2 - 2Rr}{2\,(r/\sin\frac A2)\,OI} = \frac{R\big(2\sin\frac B2\sin\frac C2 -
 \sin\frac A2\big)}{OI}.$$

**Numerator.** With $C=60^\circ$ and $B=120^\circ-A$:
$$2\sin\tfrac B2\sin 30^\circ = \sin\tfrac B2 = \sin(60^\circ-\tfrac A2),$$
$$\sin(60^\circ-\tfrac A2)-\sin\tfrac A2 = 2\cos 30^\circ\sin(30^\circ-\tfrac A2
)=\sqrt3\,\sin(30^\circ-\tfrac A2).$$

**Denominator.** With $C=60^\circ$:
$$r = 2R\sin\tfrac A2\sin(60^\circ-\tfrac A2),$$
$$2Rr = 4R^2\sin\tfrac A2\sin(60^\circ-\tfrac A2)=2R^2\big[\cos(A-60^\circ)-\cos
 60^\circ\big]=2R^2\cos(A-60^\circ)-R^2,$$
$$OI^2 = R^2-2Rr = 2R^2\big(1-\cos(A-60^\circ)\big)=4R^2\sin^2(30^\circ-\tfrac A
2).$$

Since $A<60^\circ$, we have $30^\circ-\tfrac A2>0$, so $OI=2R\sin(30^\circ-\tfra
c A2)$.

### Step 3: Conclude

$$\cos\angle AIO = \frac{R\cdot\sqrt3\,\sin(30^\circ-\tfrac A2)}{2R\sin(30^\circ
-\tfrac A2)} = \frac{\sqrt3}{2}.$$

Since $0^\circ<\angle AIO<180^\circ$ and $O\neq I$ (as $b>a$ rules out the equil
ateral case),

$$\boxed{\angle AIO = 30^\circ}.$$
