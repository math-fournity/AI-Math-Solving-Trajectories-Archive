Looking at this problem, I need to solve a steady-state Stefan-type free boundar
y problem using domain perturbation.

## Setup

The strip is $-\infty < x < \infty$, $|\psi| < 1$, with boundary conditions $u(x
,\pm 1) = x$. The interface (where $u=0$) separates water ($u>0$, conductivity $
1+\varepsilon$) from ice ($u<0$, conductivity $1$). In steady state, the interfa
ce is stationary, so the Stefan condition reduces to **heat flux continuity** at
 the interface.

When $\varepsilon=0$: $u_0 = x$ everywhere, interface at $x=0$.

## Perturbation Expansion

Let the interface be $x = \delta(\psi) = \varepsilon\,\delta_1(\psi) + O(\vareps
ilon^2)$, and write:
$$u_w = x + \varepsilon\, v_w + O(\varepsilon^2) \quad (x > 0), \qquad u_i = x +
 \varepsilon\, v_i + O(\varepsilon^2) \quad (x < 0)$$

Since conductivity is constant in each phase, **both $v_w$ and $v_i$ are harmoni
c** in their respective domains.

**Boundary conditions on $\psi = \pm 1$:** Since $u(x,\pm 1) = x = u_0(x,\pm 1)$
, we get $v_w(x,\pm 1) = 0$ and $v_i(x,\pm 1) = 0$.

**Interface condition $u=0$ at $x = \varepsilon\,\delta_1(\psi)$:** Taylor expan
ding,
$$\varepsilon\,\delta_1(\psi) + \varepsilon\, v(0,\psi) = 0 \implies v_w(0,\psi)
 = v_i(0,\psi) = -\delta_1(\psi)$$

**Flux continuity** $(1+\varepsilon)\,\partial_n u_w = \partial_n u_i$ at the in
terface. The unit normal to $x=\delta(\psi)$ is $\hat{n} \approx (1, -\varepsilo
n\,\delta_1')$. Computing normal derivatives to $O(\varepsilon)$:
$$(1+\varepsilon)(1 + \varepsilon\,\partial_x v_w) = 1 + \varepsilon\,\partial_x
 v_i \implies \partial_x v_i(0,\psi) - \partial_x v_w(0,\psi) = 1$$

## Symmetry Reduction

The boundary data for $v_w$ (half-strip $x>0$) and $v_i$ (half-strip $x<0$) are
identical after reflection $x \to -x$. Therefore $v_i(x,\psi) = v_w(-x,\psi)$, g
iving $\partial_x v_i(0,\psi) = -\partial_x v_w(0,\psi)$. The flux condition bec
omes:

$$\partial_x v_w(0,\psi) = -\frac{1}{2}$$

## Solving the Overdetermined Problem for $v_w$

In the half-strip $x > 0$, $|\psi| < 1$: $v_w$ is harmonic, $v_w(x,\pm 1) = 0$,
$v_w(0,\psi) = -\delta_1(\psi)$, $\partial_x v_w(0,\psi) = -1/2$, and $v_w \to 0
$ as $x \to \infty$.

Expanding in eigenfunctions $\sin\!\bigl(\frac{n\pi(\psi+1)}{2}\bigr)$ that vani
sh at $\psi = \pm 1$:

$$v_w(x,\psi) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n\, e^{-n\pi x/2}\, \sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi
(\psi+1)}{2}\right)$$

The Neumann condition at $x=0$ gives:
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n\pi}{2}\, A_n\, \sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi(\psi+1)}{2}\
right) = \frac{1}{2}$$

Expanding the constant $1/2$ in this sine basis (with $\phi = (\psi+1)/2 \in (0,
1)$):

$$b_n = \int_0^1 \sin(n\pi\phi)\,d\phi = \frac{1-(-1)^n}{n\pi} = \begin{cases} \
frac{2}{n\pi} & n \text{ odd} \\ 0 & n \text{ even} \end{cases}$$

So $\frac{n\pi}{2}A_n = b_n$ gives $A_n = \frac{4}{n^2\pi^2}$ for $n$ odd, and $
A_n = 0$ for $n$ even.

## Computing $\delta_1(\psi)$

From $v_w(0,\psi) = -\delta_1(\psi)$:

$$\delta_1(\psi) = -\frac{4}{\pi^2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k}{(2k+1)^2}\c
os\!\left(\frac{(2k+1)\pi\psi}{2}\right)$$

where I used $\sin\!\bigl(\frac{(2k+1)\pi(\psi+1)}{2}\bigr) = (-1)^k \cos\!\bigl
(\frac{(2k+1)\pi\psi}{2}\bigr)$.

**Evaluating the series in closed form.** Let $S(\psi) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \fr
ac{(-1)^k}{(2k+1)^2}\cos\!\bigl(\frac{(2k+1)\pi\psi}{2}\bigr)$. Differentiating:

$$S'(\psi) = -\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k}{2k+1}\sin\!\left(\f
rac{(2k+1)\pi\psi}{2}\right)$$

Setting $\theta = \pi\psi/2$ and using the identity (derived via $\sum \frac{z^{
2k+1}}{2k+1} = \frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{1+z}{1-z}$ with $z = ie^{i\theta}$, taking $\
text{Im}$):

$$\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k}{2k+1}\sin((2k+1)\theta) = \frac{\pi}{4}\,\te
xt{sign}(\theta), \quad |\theta| < \frac{\pi}{2}$$

Therefore $S'(\psi) = -\frac{\pi^2}{8}\,\text{sign}(\psi)$, and integrating:

$$S(\psi) = S(0) - \frac{\pi^2}{8}|\psi|$$

At $\psi = 0$: $S(0) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k}{(2k+1)^2} = G$ (Catalan
's constant $\approx 0.9159$).

Thus:

$$\delta_1(\psi) = -\frac{4}{\pi^2}\left(G - \frac{\pi^2}{8}|\psi|\right) = \fra
c{|\psi|}{2} - \frac{4G}{\pi^2}$$

## Result

The interface position to first order in $\varepsilon$ is:

$$\boxed{x = \varepsilon\left(\frac{|\psi|}{2} - \frac{4G}{\pi^2}\right) + O(\va
repsilon^2)}$$

where $G = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k}{(2k+1)^2} \approx 0.9159$ is Catala
n's constant.

**Physical interpretation:** At the centerline ($\psi = 0$), the interface shift
s to $x \approx -0.371\,\varepsilon$ (into the ice region), while near the bound
aries ($\psi \to \pm 1$), it shifts to $x \approx +0.129\,\varepsilon$ (into the
 water region). The higher conductivity in water reduces the temperature gradien
t there; the interface curves to compensate, restoring flux continuity. (Near th
e corners $(0,\pm 1)$, corner boundary layers adjust the interface to pass throu
gh the points where $u=0$ on the boundary.)
