Looking at this problem, I need to partition $n$ points into collinear subsets s
o that at most $n^{3/2}$ aligned pairs (same $x$ or same $y$) cross between diff
erent subsets.

**Key idea:** Orient each point toward either its row (same $y$-coordinate) or i
ts column (same $x$-coordinate), choosing the side with more points. Points orie
nted toward their row form horizontal-line subsets; those oriented toward their
column form vertical-line subsets.

---

**Proof.**

For each row $R$ (set of points sharing a $y$-coordinate), let $r_R = |R|$; for
each column $C$ (same $x$-coordinate), let $c_C = |C|$. We have $\sum_R r_R = \s
um_C c_C = n$.

**Orientation rule.** For each point $p$ in row $R$ and column $C$:
- If $r_R \ge c_C$, assign $p$ to the row-subset $S_R$.
- If $c_C > r_R$, assign $p$ to the column-subset $T_C$.

Define $S_R = \{p \in R : r_R \ge c_{C(p)}\}$ and $T_C = \{p \in C : c_C > r_{R(
p)}\}$. The partition consists of all nonempty $S_R$ and $T_C$.

Each $S_R$ lies on the horizontal line $y = y_R$; each $T_C$ lies on the vertica
l line $x = x_C$. Every point goes to exactly one subset, so the subsets are dis
joint and cover $S$. This gives **part (a)**.

**Counting split pairs.** Let $a_R = |R \setminus S_R|$ (points of $R$ sent to c
olumns) and $b_C = |C \setminus T_C|$ (points of $C$ sent to rows).

*Row-aligned pairs in row $R$:* A pair is unsplit only if both points lie in $S_
R$. Split pairs number
$$\binom{r_R}{2} - \binom{r_R - a_R}{2} = \frac{a_R(2r_R - a_R - 1)}{2} \le a_R
\cdot r_R.$$

*Column-aligned pairs in column $C$:* Similarly, split pairs number
$$\binom{c_C}{2} - \binom{c_C - b_C}{2} \le b_C \cdot c_C.$$

**Total:**
$$\text{Split} \le \sum_R a_R\, r_R + \sum_C b_C\, c_C.$$

Rewriting per point: a point $p$ in row $R$, column $C$ contributes $r_R$ to $\s
um_R a_R r_R$ if $c_C > r_R$, and contributes $c_C$ to $\sum_C b_C c_C$ if $r_R
\ge c_C$. Exactly one condition holds, so each point contributes $\min(r_{R(p)},
 c_{C(p)})$:
$$\text{Split} \le \sum_{p \in S} \min\!\bigl(r_{R(p)},\, c_{C(p)}\bigr).$$

**Bounding the sum.** Since $\min(a,b) \le \sqrt{ab}$:
$$\sum_p \min(r_{R(p)}, c_{C(p)}) \le \sum_p \sqrt{r_{R(p)}\,c_{C(p)}} = \sum_R
\sqrt{r_R}\sum_{p \in R}\sqrt{c_{C(p)}}.$$

By Cauchy–Schwarz on the inner sum over the $r_R$ points in row $R$:
$$\sum_{p \in R}\sqrt{c_{C(p)}} \le \sqrt{r_R \cdot \sum_{p \in R} c_{C(p)}}.$$

Each column meets row $R$ in at most one point, so $\sum_{p \in R} c_{C(p)} = \s
um_{C:\,C\cap R\ne\emptyset} c_C \le \sum_C c_C = n$. Therefore:
$$\sum_R \sqrt{r_R}\cdot\sqrt{r_R \cdot n} = \sum_R r_R\,\sqrt{n} = n\sqrt{n} =
n^{3/2}.$$

Hence the number of aligned pairs crossing between subsets is at most $n^{3/2}$,
 establishing **part (b)**. $\blacksquare$
