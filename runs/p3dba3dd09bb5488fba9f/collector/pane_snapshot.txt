## Proof

We are given a Riemannian manifold $(M, g)$ of dimension $n$ with Ricci curvatur
e $R_{ij}$ satisfying
$$R_{ij} = \lambda\, g^{2}_{ij}, \qquad g^{2}_{ij} := g(\partial_i, \partial_j)\
,g(\partial_i, \partial_j) = (g_{ij})^{2}.$$

The **trace of the Ricci tensor** (the scalar curvature) is defined by contracti
on with the inverse metric:
$$R := g^{ij} R_{ij} = \lambda\, g^{ij}\, (g_{ij})^{2}.$$

To evaluate this contraction we work in **Riemann normal coordinates** centered
at an arbitrary point $p \in M$. In these coordinates the metric and its inverse
 take the canonical form at $p$:
$$g_{ij}(p) = \delta_{ij}, \qquad g^{ij}(p) = \delta^{ij}.$$

Substituting into the expression for the trace:
$$R(p) = \lambda \sum_{i,j=1}^{n} \delta^{ij}\,(\delta_{ij})^{2}.$$

Since $\delta^{ij}$ (and $\delta_{ij}$) vanish unless $i = j$, only the diagonal
 terms $i = j$ survive:
$$R(p) = \lambda \sum_{i=1}^{n} \delta^{ii}\,(\delta_{ii})^{2} = \lambda \sum_{i
=1}^{n} 1 \cdot 1^{2} = \lambda \sum_{i=1}^{n} 1 = \lambda\, n.$$

The point $p$ was arbitrary, so this holds at every point of $M$. Therefore the
trace of the Ricci curvature tensor is

$$\boxed{R = \mathrm{tr}(R_{ij}) = \lambda\, n.}$$
