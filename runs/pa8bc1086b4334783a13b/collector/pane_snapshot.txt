### Step 1: Symmetry of the Cantor function

{3}, 1]$, $\phi(x) = \tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{2}\,\phi(3x - 2)$; on $(\tfrac{1}{
3}, \tfrac{2}{3})$, $\phi(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}$.

The reflection $x \mapsto 1-x$ swaps the two pieces: $x \in [0, \tfrac{1}{3}] \i

$$\phi(1-x) = \tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{2}\,\phi(3(1-x)-2) = \tfrac{1}{2} + \tfra

1 - \tfrac{1}{2}$). By induction on the construction level, the identity holds e

### Step 2: Reduce the integral

Using the symmetry:
$$\int_0^1 \phi(1-x)\,d\phi(x) = \int_0^1 (1 - \phi(x))\,d\phi(x) = \int_0^1 1\,
d\phi(x) - \int_0^1 \phi(x)\,d\phi(x).$$

### Step 3: Evaluate each term

**First term.** Since $\phi$ is monotone increasing with $\phi(0) = 0$ and $\phi
(1) = 1$:
$$\int_0^1 1\,d\phi(x) = \phi(1) - \phi(0) = 1.$$

**Second term.** We use the standard identity: for any continuous function $f$ o

$$\int_a^b f\,df = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(f(b)^2 - f(a)^2\bigr).$$

*Proof of the identity:* Since $f$ is continuous, the quadratic variation term v
anishes, so $d(f^2) = 2f\,df$ in the Stieltjes sense. Integrating: $\int_a^b d(f
^2) = f(b)^2 - f(a)^2 = 2\int_a^b f\,df$, giving the result. (Equivalently, this

c{1}{2}f(a)^2$, since the boundary term $f(b)f(b) - f(a)f(a)$ splits symmetrical

The Cantor function $\phi$ is continuous and monotone (hence of bounded variatio
n), with $\phi(0) = 0$, $\phi(1) = 1$. Therefore:
$$\int_0^1 \phi(x)\,d\phi(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\phi(1)^2 - \phi(0)^2\bigr) = \

### Step 4: Combine

$$\int_0^1 \phi(1-x)\,d\phi(x) = 1 - \tfrac{1}{2} = \boxed{\tfrac{1}{2}}.$$
