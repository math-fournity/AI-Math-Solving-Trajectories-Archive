We evaluate the integral

\[
I(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-iw(x-x_0)}\,dw
\]

and show that, in the sense of distributions, \(I(x)=\delta(x-x_0)\).

## Step 1. Regularization

The integral \(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-iw(x-x_0)}\,dw\) does not converge as
an ordinary Lebesgue integral. We therefore define it as the limit of a family o
f regularized integrals. Introduce a Gaussian damping \(e^{-\varepsilon w^{2}/2}
\) with \(\varepsilon>0\), and set

\[
I_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-iw(x-x_0)}\,e^{-\var
epsilon w^{2}/2}\,dw.
\]

The Dirac delta is then recovered as the distributional limit

\[
\delta(x-x_0)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}I_{\varepsilon}(x).
\]

## Step 2. Closed-form evaluation of the regularized integral

Let \(a=x-x_0\). Completing the square in the exponent,

\[
-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}w^{2}-iaw
=-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\!\left(w+\frac{ia}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}
-\frac{a^{2}}{2\varepsilon}.
\]

Hence

\[
I_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\,e^{-a^{2}/(2\varepsilon)}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left(w+\frac{ia}{\varepsilon}\r
ight)^{2}}\,dw.
\]

Shifting the contour back to the real axis (justified since the integrand is ent
ire and decays rapidly), and using \(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\varepsilon u^{2
}/2}\,du=\sqrt{2\pi/\varepsilon}\),

\[
I_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\,e^{-a^{2}/(2\varepsilon)}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\v
arepsilon}}
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\varepsilon}}\,e^{-(x-x_0)^{2}/(2\varepsilon)}.
\]

## Step 3. Distributional limit

Define

\[
g_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\varepsilon}}\,e^{-(x-x_0)^{2}/(2\varepsil
on)}.
\]

This is a Gaussian probability density with mean \(x_0\) and variance \(\varepsi
lon\). We verify the two defining properties of an approximation to the identity
.

**(a) Unit mass.** Substituting \(u=(x-x_0)/\sqrt{\varepsilon}\),

\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\varepsilon}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-(x-x_0)^{2}/(2\varep
silon)}\,dx
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-u^{2}/2}\,du=1.
\]

**(b) Concentration at \(x_0\).** For any \(\delta>0\),

\[
\int_{|x-x_0|>\delta}g_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx
=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\delta/\sqrt{\varepsilon}}^{\infty}e^{-u^{2}/2}\,du\
;\xrightarrow{\;\varepsilon\to 0^{+}\;}\;0,
\]

since the lower limit tends to \(+\infty\) while the Gaussian tail decays super-
exponentially.

## Step 4. Action on test functions

Let \(\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\). Using the substitution \(x=x_0+\s
qrt{\varepsilon}\,u\) and the continuity of \(\varphi\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}I_{\varepsilon}(x)\,\varphi(x)
\,dx
&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g_{\varepsilon}(x)\,\varphi(
x)\,dx\\
&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-u^{
2}/2}\,\varphi\!\left(x_0+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,u\right)du\\
&=\varphi(x_0)\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-u^{2}/2}\,du\
\
&=\varphi(x_0).
\end{aligned}
\]

The dominated convergence theorem applies because \(\varphi\) is bounded and \(e
^{-u^{2}/2}\) is integrable. Since

\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(x-x_0)\,\varphi(x)\,dx=\varphi(x_0),
\]

we conclude, in the sense of distributions (i.e., equality tested against every
smooth compactly supported \(\varphi\)),

\[
\boxed{\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-iw(x-x_0)}\,dw=\delta(x-x_0)\;
}
\]

## Conclusion

The inverse Fourier transform of \(f(w)=\frac{1}{2\pi}e^{iwx_0}\) is the Dirac d
elta distribution centered at \(x_0\):

\[
\mathcal{F}^{-1}\!\left[\tfrac{1}{2\pi}e^{iwx_0}\right](x)=\delta(x-x_0).
\]

Equivalently, this confirms the Fourier representation of the delta function: a
pure complex exponential in frequency space localizes entirely at the single poi
nt \(x=x_0\) in physical space, with total unit mass — the defining behavior of
\(\delta(x-x_0)\).
