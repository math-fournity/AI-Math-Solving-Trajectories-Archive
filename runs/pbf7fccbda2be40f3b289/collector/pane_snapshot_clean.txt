cumcenter.

## Setting Up the Cevian Structure

Let a = BC, b = CA, c = AB, and σ = (a+b+c)/2. Define the tangent lengths u = σ−
a, v = σ−b, w = σ−c (so u = r·cot(A/2), etc.).

Parametrize the cevian points:
- A₁ on BC: BA₁ = m, A₁C = n
- B₁ on CA: CB₁ = p, B₁A = q
- C₁ on AB: AC₁ = s, C₁B = t

The equilibrium condition AB₁ − AC₁ = CA₁ − CB₁ = BC₁ − BA₁ becomes q − s = n −
p = t − m = λ.

Solving the system (with m+n=a, p+q=b, s+t=c), I get:
- BA₁ = v − λ/2, A₁C = w + λ/2
- CB₁ = w − λ/2, B₁A = u + λ/2
- AC₁ = u − λ/2, C₁B = v + λ/2

**Key observation:** Each cevian point is the incircle tangent point shifted by
λ/2 along its side (cyclically toward the next vertex).

## Computing IA₁

The tangent point T_A on BC is the foot of the perpendicular from I to BC, so IT
_A = r. Since A₁ is displaced from T_A by λ/2 *along* BC (perpendicular to IT_A)
:

$$IA_1^2 = r^2 + (\lambda/2)^2 = r^2 + \lambda^2/4$$

Setting IA₁ = 11 and r = 7: λ²/4 = 121 − 49 = 72, so λ = 12√2.

Define **ρ = IA₁ = √(r² + λ²/4) = 11**.

## The Incenter of Each Small Triangle

For triangle AB₁C₁ (at vertex A, with angle A):
- AB₁ = r·cot(A/2) + λ/2, AC₁ = r·cot(A/2) − λ/2
- By the law of cosines: B₁C₁ = cos(A/2)·√(4r² + λ²) = 2ρ·cos(A/2)

Computing the inradius r_A of triangle AB₁C₁:

$$\text{Area} = \sin\alpha\cos\alpha\cdot\frac{r^2\cos^2\alpha - (\rho^2 - r^2)\
sin^2\alpha}{\sin\alpha}, \quad s_A = r\cot\alpha + \rho\cos\alpha$$

where α = A/2. After simplification:

$$r_A = \frac{r^2 - \rho^2\sin^2\alpha}{r + \rho\sin\alpha} = \frac{(r - \rho\si
n\alpha)(r+\rho\sin\alpha)}{r+\rho\sin\alpha} = r - \rho\sin\alpha$$

Since I_A lies on the angle bisector from A: AI_A = r_A/sin(A/2) = r/sin(A/2) −
ρ = AI − ρ.

**This means I_A is on segment AI at distance exactly ρ from I (toward A).**

By identical arguments:
- I_B is on segment BI at distance ρ from I
- I_C is on segment CI at distance ρ from I

## The Circumcircle of I_A I_B I_C

All three points I_A, I_B, I_C lie at distance **ρ from I**. Therefore they all
lie on the circle of radius ρ centered at I.

Since I is equidistant from all three vertices of triangle I_A I_B I_C, **I is t
he circumcenter** of this triangle. Therefore:
- R = ρ = 11
- d = 0 (the circumcenter coincides with I)

## Result

$$R^2 - d^2 = \rho^2 - 0 = IA_1^2 = 11^2 = \boxed{121}$$
