## Proof

Let $O$ be the center of circle $k$ with radius $R$. Set coordinates with $O$ at
 the origin, so $A = (-R, 0)$, $B = (R, 0)$, and $AB$ is the diameter along the
$x$-axis.

---

### Part 1: The locus is contained in the open disk of radius $R$.

Any triangle in the family has one vertex $P$ on segment $AB$ (hence inside or o
n $k$) and two vertices $Q, R'$ on $k$. The triangle $PQR'$ is the convex hull o
f points in the closed disk of radius $R$, so it lies inside this closed disk. T
he incenter lies in the **interior** of the triangle. The only points of the tri
angle that can lie on circle $k$ are the vertices $Q$, $R'$ (and $P$ if $P = A$
or $B$). Since the incenter is an interior point, it cannot coincide with a vert
ex, so it lies strictly inside the open disk of radius $R$.

---

### Part 2: The locus contains every point of the open disk.

**Step 2a: $P = O$ fills the punctured disk.**

Since $O$ is the midpoint of $AB$, we may take $P = O$. Let $Q = (R\cos\alpha,\,
 R\sin\alpha)$ and $R' = (R\cos\beta,\, R\sin\beta)$ be on $k$, with $\alpha \ne
q \beta$. Set
$$u = \frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}, \qquad v = \frac{\alpha - \beta}{2}, \qquad v \in
(0,\pi),\; v \neq \frac{\pi}{2}$$
(where $v = \pi/2$ would make $O, Q, R'$ collinear, giving a degenerate triangle
).

The side lengths of $\triangle OQR'$ are:
- $QR' = 2R\sin v$ (opposite $O$),
- $OR' = R$ (opposite $Q$),
- $OQ = R$ (opposite $R'$).

The incenter is the weighted average:
$$I = \frac{QR'\cdot O + R\cdot Q + R\cdot R'}{QR' + R + R} = \frac{R(Q + R')}{2
R\sin v + 2R} = \frac{Q + R'}{2(1 + \sin v)}.$$

Using $Q + R' = (2R\cos u\cos v,\; 2R\sin u\cos v)$:
$$I = \frac{R\cos u\cos v}{1+\sin v}\,(\cos u,\;\sin u) \cdot \frac{1}{\cos u}\c
dot\cos u$$

More precisely:
$$I = \left(\frac{R\cos u\cos v}{1+\sin v},\; \frac{R\sin u\cos v}{1+\sin v}\rig
ht).$$

The distance from $O$ is:
$$|OI| = \frac{R\,|\cos v|}{1 + \sin v} = R\sqrt{\frac{1 - \sin v}{1 + \sin v}}.
$$

As $v$ ranges over $(0, \pi/2) \cup (\pi/2, \pi)$, the function $\sqrt{(1-\sin v
)/(1+\sin v)}$ is continuous and takes all values in $(0, 1)$:
- $v \to 0^+$ or $v \to \pi^-$: $|OI| \to R$,
- $v \to \pi/2$: $|OI| \to 0$.

The direction of $I$ from $O$ is $(\cos u, \sin u)$ (when $\cos v > 0$) or $(-\c
os u, -\sin u)$ (when $\cos v < 0$); since $u$ ranges over $[0, 2\pi)$, all dire
ctions are covered.

**Explicitly**, for any target point $X$ with $0 < |OX| = d < R$ in direction $\
theta$: set $u = \theta$ and choose $v \in (0, \pi/2)$ satisfying
$$\sin v = \frac{1 - (d/R)^2}{1 + (d/R)^2} \in (0,1),$$
which gives $|OI| = d$ and the correct direction. Thus every point of the punctu
red open disk $\{X : 0 < |OX| < R\}$ is achieved.

**Step 2b: $O$ itself is achieved.**

Take the equilateral triangle inscribed in $k$ with one vertex at $B = (R, 0)$ (
which lies on $AB$) and the other two vertices at $(R\cos 120°, R\sin 120°)$ and
 $(R\cos 240°, R\sin 240°)$. The incenter of an equilateral triangle coincides w
ith its circumcenter, which is $O$.

---

### Conclusion

- Every incenter lies in the open disk of radius $R$ centered at $O$ (Part 1).
- Every point of this open disk is realized as an incenter (Part 2: the puncture
d disk via $P = O$, and the center $O$ via the equilateral triangle with $P = B$
).

Therefore, the locus of the centers of the inscribed circles is **the interior o
f circle $k$** (the open disk bounded by $k$).
