## Proof

Let $F \colon \mathbb{F}_{2^n} \to \mathbb{F}_{2^n}$ be an $(n,n)$-function. Eve
ry such function admits a **unique univariate polynomial representation**

$$F(x) = \sum_{i=0}^{2^n - 1} a_i\, x^i, \qquad a_i \in \mathbb{F}_{2^n},$$

where uniqueness is guaranteed because a polynomial of degree $< 2^n$ over $\mat
hbb{F}_{2^n}$ that vanishes on all of $\mathbb{F}_{2^n}$ must be the zero polyno
mial (degree bound $< 2^n$ vs. $2^n$ roots).

**Goal.** Show that the algebraic degree of $F$ equals

$$\deg(F) = \max\{\,w_2(i) : a_i \neq 0,\; 0 \le i \le 2^n - 1\,\},$$

where $w_2(i)$ is the **(binary) 2-weight** of $i$, i.e. the number of $1$'s in
the binary expansion $i = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} i_j\, 2^j$.

---

### Step 1 — From univariate to multivariate

Fix an $\mathbb{F}_2$-basis $\{\beta_1, \dots, \beta_n\}$ of $\mathbb{F}_{2^n}$
and write $x = \sum_{k=1}^{n} x_k \beta_k$ with $x_k \in \mathbb{F}_2$. Expandin
g $F(x)$ in this basis gives

$$F(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} F_k(x_1, \dots, x_n)\, \beta_k,$$

where each $F_k \colon \mathbb{F}_2^n \to \mathbb{F}_2$ is a Boolean function (a
 coordinate function of $F$). By definition,

$$\deg(F) = \max_{1 \le k \le n} \deg(F_k),$$

with $\deg(F_k)$ the algebraic degree of the multivariate polynomial representat
ion of $F_k$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$.

---

### Step 2 — The multivariate degree of the monomial $x^i$

Write $i = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} i_j\, 2^j$ in binary, so $w_2(i) = \sum_{j} i_j$. Th
en

$$x^i = x^{\sum_j i_j 2^j} = \prod_{j\,:\, i_j = 1} x^{2^j}.$$

By the Frobenius automorphism and $x_k \in \mathbb{F}_2$ (so $x_k^{2^j} = x_k$),

$$x^{2^j} = \Big(\sum_{k=1}^{n} x_k \beta_k\Big)^{2^j} = \sum_{k=1}^{n} x_k\, \b
eta_k^{2^j} =: L_j(x_1, \dots, x_n),$$

which is an $\mathbb{F}_2$-**linear form** in $x_1, \dots, x_n$. Therefore

$$x^i = \prod_{j\,:\, i_j = 1} L_j(x_1, \dots, x_n),$$

a product of exactly $w_2(i)$ linear forms, so $\deg_{\text{mv}}(x^i) \le w_2(i)
$.

---

### Step 3 — The linear forms $L_j$ are linearly independent

The forms $L_0, \dots, L_{n-1}$ correspond to the $n$ Frobenius-conjugate bases
$\{\beta_k^{2^j}\}_{k=1}^{n}$. Each $\{\beta_k^{2^j}\}_k$ is a basis of $\mathbb
{F}_{2^n}/\mathbb{F}_2$ (Frobenius maps bases to bases). The $n$ linear forms $L
_j$ are pairwise distinct and in fact linearly independent over $\mathbb{F}_2$ (
they are the $n$ conjugate traces $\mathrm{Tr}(\beta_k x)$-type forms; equivalen
tly their coefficient vectors are related by Frobenius and span an $n$-dimension
al space). Hence:

> **A product of linearly independent linear forms has algebraic degree equal to
 the number of factors.**

(If a product of $d$ linearly independent linear forms had degree $< d$, its top
-degree homogeneous part would vanish, forcing a nontrivial linear dependence —
contradiction.)

Consequently

$$\deg_{\text{mv}}(x^i) = w_2(i).$$

---

### Step 4 — Degree of $F$ from the univariate coefficients

Write $F(x) = \sum_i a_i x^i$. Each term $a_i x^i$, expanded multivariately and
projected onto the basis $\{\beta_k\}$, contributes to each coordinate function
$F_k$ a polynomial whose degree is at most $w_2(i)$, and whose **top-degree (deg
ree-$w_2(i)$) homogeneous part** is a projection of

$$a_i \cdot \prod_{j\,:\, i_j = 1} L_j.$$

Since $a_i \neq 0$ and the map $a \mapsto (a \cdot g \text{ projected onto } \{\
beta_k\})$ is surjective for any nonzero multivariate polynomial $g$, at least o
ne coordinate function receives a **nonzero** degree-$w_2(i)$ contribution from
the term $a_i x^i$.

Now set $d = \max\{w_2(i) : a_i \neq 0\}$.

- **Upper bound.** Every term $a_i x^i$ has multivariate degree $\le w_2(i) \le
d$, so every $F_k$ has degree $\le d$, giving $\deg(F) \le d$.
- **Lower bound.** Pick $i^*$ with $a_{i^*} \neq 0$ and $w_2(i^*) = d$. Its degr
ee-$d$ homogeneous part is nonzero and, by the surjectivity argument above, surv
ives in at least one coordinate function $F_k$. No other term $a_i x^i$ with $w_
2(i) < d$ can contribute at degree $d$, and terms with $w_2(i) = d$ can only add
 (not cancel the nonzero part, since at least one coordinate retains a nonzero d
egree-$d$ component). Hence some $F_k$ has degree exactly $d$, giving $\deg(F) \
ge d$.

Combining both bounds:

$$\boxed{\deg(F) = \max\{\,w_2(i) : a_i \neq 0,\; 0 \le i \le 2^n - 1\,\}.}$$

---

### Summary

The algebraic degree of an $(n,n)$-function $F$ given in univariate form $F(x) =
 \sum_{i=0}^{2^n-1} a_i x^i$ over $\mathbb{F}_{2^n}$ is obtained by:

1. computing the **2-weight** $w_2(i)$ (number of $1$-bits in the binary expansi
on) of every exponent $i$ with $a_i \neq 0$,
2. taking the **maximum** of these 2-weights.

The reason is that each univariate monomial $x^i$ factors — via the Frobenius id
entity $x^{2^j} = \sum_k x_k \beta_k^{2^j}$ — into a product of exactly $w_2(i)$
 linearly independent $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear forms, hence has multivariate algebr
aic degree exactly $w_2(i)$; and the highest 2-weight present among the nonzero
coefficients determines the top-degree homogeneous part that cannot be cancelled
 across all coordinate functions.
