## Solution

**Assumption on the contour.** The problem does not specify $C_r$ explicitly. Th
e standard convention in this setting is that $C_r$ is the positively oriented c
ircle $|z|=r$. Under this convention the limit is computed as follows.

---

The integrand is
$$f(z)=\frac{e^{2iz}}{z(z^2-1)}=\frac{e^{2iz}}{z(z-1)(z+1)},$$
which has isolated singularities (simple poles) at $z=0,\;1,\;-1$.

**Residue at $z=0$.** Since $z=0$ is a simple pole,
$$\operatorname{Res}(f,0)=\lim_{z\to 0}\,z\,f(z)=\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{e^{2iz}}{(z-
1)(z+1)}=\frac{e^{0}}{(-1)(1)}=-1.$$

**Applying the residue theorem.** For every $r$ with $0<r<1$, the circle $C_r=\{
z:|z|=r\}$ (positively oriented) encloses exactly one singularity, namely $z=0$;
 the poles at $z=\pm1$ lie outside since $|\pm1|=1>r$. Hence, by the residue the
orem,
$$\int_{C_r}\frac{e^{2iz}}{z(z^2-1)}\,dz=2\pi i\,\operatorname{Res}(f,0)=2\pi i\
cdot(-1)=-2\pi i.$$

This value is **independent of $r$** for all $0<r<1$. Therefore the limit as $r\
to 0$ exists and equals this constant value:
$$\boxed{\lim_{r\to 0}\int_{C_r}\frac{e^{2iz}}{z(z^2-1)}\,dz=-2\pi i.}$$
