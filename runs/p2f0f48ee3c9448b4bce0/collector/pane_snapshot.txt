**Proof.**

Let $G$ be a profinite group of order $lp^n$ (as a supernatural number), where $
l$ and $p$ are primes with $l < p$ and $n \geq 1$. Let $P$ be a $p$-Sylow subgro
up of $G$. We show $P \trianglelefteq G$.

**Step 1: Finite quotients.** For every open normal subgroup $N \trianglelefteq_
o G$, the quotient $G/N$ is a finite group with $|G/N| \mid lp^n$. The $p'$-part
 of $|G/N|$ divides $l$.

**Step 2: $PN/N$ is a $p$-Sylow of $G/N$.** We have $|P| = p^n$ (the $p$-part of
 $|G|$) and $|PN/N| = |P|/|P \cap N|$. Since $P \cap N \leq N$ and the $p$-part
of $|N| = |G|/|G/N|$ is $p^{n - v_p(|G/N|)}$, we get $v_p(|P \cap N|) \leq n - v
_p(|G/N|)$, so $|PN/N| \geq p^{v_p(|G/N|)}$. As $PN/N$ is a $p$-subgroup of $G/N
$, we also have $|PN/N| \leq p^{v_p(|G/N|)}$. Hence $|PN/N| = p^{v_p(|G/N|)}$, c
onfirming $PN/N$ is a $p$-Sylow of $G/N$.

**Step 3: $PN/N \trianglelefteq G/N$ for all open normal $N$.** By the finite Sy
low theorems, the number $n_p(G/N)$ of $p$-Sylow subgroups of $G/N$ satisfies $n
_p(G/N) \mid (p'\text{-part of }|G/N|)$ and $n_p(G/N) \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$. Since
the $p'$-part of $|G/N|$ divides $l$, we have $n_p(G/N) \mid l$. As $l$ is prime
, $n_p(G/N) \in \{1, l\}$. But $l < p$ and $l > 1$ imply $l \not\equiv 1 \pmod{p
}$, so $n_p(G/N) = 1$. Thus $G/N$ has a unique $p$-Sylow subgroup, which is norm
al. Since $PN/N$ is a $p$-Sylow of $G/N$, it is normal in $G/N$.

**Step 4: $P$ is normal in $G$.** For any $g \in G$ and any open normal $N$, sin
ce $PN/N \trianglelefteq G/N$:
$$gPg^{-1}N/N = g(PN/N)g^{-1} = PN/N.$$
So $gPg^{-1} \leq PN$ for every open normal $N$, hence $gPg^{-1} \leq \bigcap_{N
 \trianglelefteq_o G} PN$.

**Claim:** $\bigcap_{N \trianglelefteq_o G} PN = P$.

*Proof of claim.* The inclusion $P \subseteq \bigcap_N PN$ is immediate. For the
 reverse, let $x \in \bigcap_N PN$. For each open normal $N$, there exists $p_N
\in P$ with $p_N^{-1}x \in N$. The open normal subgroups form a neighborhood bas
is of $1$ in $G$, so as $N$ shrinks, $p_N^{-1}x \to 1$, i.e., $p_N \to x$. Since
 $P$ is compact (closed in the compact group $G$) and $p_N \in P$, the limit $x$
 lies in $P$. $\square$

Therefore $gPg^{-1} \leq P$. Since $|gPg^{-1}| = |P| = p^n$ (as supernatural num
bers) and $gPg^{-1} \leq P$, the index $[P : gPg^{-1}]$ is a supernatural number
 dividing $|P| = p^n$ (hence a power of $p$) with trivial $p$-part (since $|gPg^
{-1}| = |P|$). Thus $[P : gPg^{-1}] = 1$, giving $gPg^{-1} = P$.

Since this holds for all $g \in G$, we conclude $P \trianglelefteq G$. $\blacksq
uare$

 $G$ is itself finite and the result follows immediately from the classical Sylo
w theorem. The argument above handles the general case (including $n = \infty$)
by reducing to finite quotients and using compactness of $P$ to lift normality b
ack to $G$.
