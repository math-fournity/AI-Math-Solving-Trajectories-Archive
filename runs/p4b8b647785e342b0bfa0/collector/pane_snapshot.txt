## Proof

**Setup.** Let the 25 scientists be elements of a ground set $E$ with $|E|=25$.
Over $N$ days we have distinct subsets $S_1,\dots,S_N\subseteq E$ (condition 1),
 and each element of $E$ belongs to exactly 2 of the subsets (condition 2). We m
aximize $N$.

**Graph reformulation.** Regard the $N$ subsets as **vertices** and the 25 scien
tists as **edges**: scientist $e$ appears in exactly 2 subsets, so we connect th
ose two subset-vertices by an edge labeled $e$. This gives a multigraph $G$ on $
N$ vertices with 25 edges, **no loops** (a scientist is in 2 *distinct* subsets)
. The incident edge-set $I(v)$ of vertex $v$ is exactly the subset $S_v$ of scie
ntists successful on day $v$. Condition 1 says all $I(v)$ are distinct. The tota
l degree is $\sum_v \deg(v)=2\cdot 25=50$.

**Upper bound.** Partition vertices by degree:
- $a$ vertices of degree $0$, $b$ of degree $1$, $c$ of degree $\ge 2$.

*Two degree-0 vertices both have $I(v)=\emptyset$*, so $a\le 1$.

*No edge can have both endpoints of degree 1*: if edge $e$ connects degree-1 ver
tices $u,v$, then $I(u)=I(v)=\{e\}$, violating distinctness. Hence each degree-1
 vertex is the unique degree-1 endpoint of its edge, giving $b\le 25$.

The $c$ vertices of degree $\ge 2$ carry total degree $50-b$ (the $b$ degree-1 v
ertices contribute $b$, degree-0 contributes $0$). Since each has degree $\ge 2$
:
$$2c \le 50-b \implies c\le \left\lfloor\frac{50-b}{2}\right\rfloor.$$

Therefore
$$N=a+b+c\le 1+b+\left\lfloor\frac{50-b}{2}\right\rfloor.$$

This is maximized at $b=25$ (or $b=24$): $N\le 1+25+12=38$.

**Construction achieving $N=38$.** Take $b=25$, $c=12$, $a=1$:
- 1 isolated vertex: $I=\emptyset$.
- 25 degree-1 vertices, each on a distinct edge: incident sets are the 25 distin
ct singletons $\{e_1\},\dots,\{e_{25}\}$.
- 12 degree-$\ge 2$ vertices with total degree $50-25=25$: assign 11 vertices ex
actly 2 edges each and 1 vertex exactly 3 edges, partitioning the 25 edges into
11 pairs and 1 triple (e.g., $\{e_1,e_2\},\{e_3,e_4\},\dots,\{e_{21},e_{22}\},\{
e_{23},e_{24},e_{25}\}$).

Each edge has one degree-1 endpoint and one degree-$\ge 2$ endpoint, so the grap
h is well defined with no loops and no multi-edges between the same pair. The in
cident sets of the 12 degree-$\ge 2$ vertices are **disjoint** (each edge assign
ed to exactly one), hence pairwise distinct, and all differ from the singletons
(different sizes) and from $\emptyset$.

**Verification.**
- *Distinctness:* $\emptyset$; 25 singletons (distinct); 11 disjoint pairs (dist
inct, and disjoint from each other); 1 triple — all 38 sets are distinct. ✓
- *Each scientist in exactly 2 subsets:* every edge $e_i$ has exactly two endpoi
nts — its degree-1 vertex and its degree-$\ge 2$ vertex — so scientist $e_i$ app
ears in exactly those 2 days. ✓
- *Count:* $0+25+22+3=50=2\times 25$. ✓

**Conclusion.** The upper bound $N\le 38$ is tight, so the largest possible valu
e is

$$\boxed{N=38}.$$
