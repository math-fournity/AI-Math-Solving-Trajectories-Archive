## Proof

### Part (a): Two non-isomorphic connected 1-factorable cubic graphs of the same
 order

**Claim:** The complete bipartite graph $K_{3,3}$ and the triangular prism graph
 (both of order 6) are non-isomorphic, connected, cubic, and 1-factorable.

**Both are connected cubic graphs of order 6.**
- $K_{3,3}$ has bipartition $\{a_1,a_2,a_3\}$ and $\{b_1,b_2,b_3\}$, with 9 edge
s; every vertex has degree 3. It is connected.
- The **triangular prism** $TP$ has vertices $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$ with edges $\{12,
23,13\}$ (top triangle), $\{45,56,46\}$ (bottom triangle), and $\{14,25,36\}$ (t
hree "rungs"). Every vertex has degree 3; it is connected.

**They are non-isomorphic.** $K_{3,3}$ is bipartite (contains no odd cycle), whi
le $TP$ contains triangles ($123$ and $456$), so $TP$ is not bipartite. Hence th
ey are not isomorphic.

**Both are 1-factorable** (edge set partitions into three perfect matchings):

*$K_{3,3}$:* Since it is bipartite and 3-regular, König's edge-coloring theorem
guarantees a proper 3-edge-coloring, i.e., a 1-factorization. Explicitly:
$$M_1=\{a_1b_1, a_2b_2, a_3b_3\},\quad M_2=\{a_1b_2, a_2b_3, a_3b_1\},\quad M_3=
\{a_1b_3, a_2b_1, a_3b_2\}.$$

*Triangular prism:* An explicit 1-factorization:
$$M_1=\{14, 23, 56\},\quad M_2=\{25, 13, 46\},\quad M_3=\{36, 12, 45\}.$$
One checks each $M_i$ is a perfect matching and the three are disjoint and cover
 all 9 edges. $\checkmark$

### Part (b): Two non-isomorphic connected cubic graphs, each factoring into a 1
-factor and a Hamiltonian cycle

**Claim:** The same pair $K_{3,3}$ and the triangular prism $TP$ works. Each is
Hamiltonian; in a Hamiltonian cubic graph the Hamiltonian cycle uses 2 edges at
every vertex, so the remaining edges form a perfect matching (1-factor). Thus ea
ch factors as (Hamiltonian cycle) $\cup$ (1-factor).

*$K_{3,3}$:* The cycle
$$a_1\, b_1\, a_2\, b_2\, a_3\, b_3\, a_1$$
is Hamiltonian (all 6 vertices, edges $a_1b_1, b_1a_2, a_2b_2, b_2a_3, a_3b_3, b
_3a_1$ all exist). The remaining edges $\{a_1b_2, a_2b_3, a_3b_1\}$ form a perfe
ct matching. $\checkmark$

*Triangular prism $TP$:* The cycle
$$1\, 2\, 5\, 6\, 4\, 3\, 1$$
is Hamiltonian (edges $12, 25, 56, 64, 43, 31$ all exist). The remaining edges $
\{14, 23, 45\}$... let me recheck: edges used by the cycle are $\{12, 25, 56, 46
, 34, 13\}$. The full edge set is $\{12,23,13,45,56,46,14,25,36\}$. Remaining =
$\{23, 45, 14, 36\}$...

Let me recompute. Full edges: $12, 23, 13, 45, 56, 46, 14, 25, 36$ (9 edges). Cy
cle $1\text{-}2\text{-}5\text{-}6\text{-}4\text{-}3\text{-}1$ uses: $12, 25, 56,
 46, 34, 13$. But $34$ is not an edge — the edges of the bottom triangle are $45
, 56, 46$, and $34$ is not among them. Let me fix the cycle.

Use cycle $1\text{-}2\text{-}5\text{-}6\text{-}3\text{-}4\text{-}1$: edges $12,
25, 56, 36, 34, 14$. But $36$ is a rung (yes), $34$ — is $34$ an edge? No. The r
ungs are $14, 25, 36$. So $34$ is not an edge.

Use cycle $1\text{-}2\text{-}3\text{-}6\text{-}5\text{-}4\text{-}1$: edges $12,
23, 36, 65, 54, 41$. All exist: $12\checkmark, 23\checkmark, 36\checkmark, 56\ch
eckmark, 45\checkmark, 14\checkmark$. This is Hamiltonian! Remaining edges: $\{1
3, 46, 25\}$ — a perfect matching. $\checkmark$

So the triangular prism factors as the Hamiltonian cycle $1\text{-}2\text{-}3\te
xt{-}6\text{-}5\text{-}4\text{-}1$ together with the 1-factor $\{13, 46, 25\}$.

As shown in part (a), $K_{3,3}$ and $TP$ are non-isomorphic. Both are connected
cubic graphs, and each admits a factorization into a Hamiltonian cycle and a 1-f
actor. $\checkmark$

**Summary.** For both (a) and (b), the pair $\{K_{3,3},\text{ triangular prism}\
}$ — two non-isomorphic connected cubic graphs of order 6 — serves as the requir
