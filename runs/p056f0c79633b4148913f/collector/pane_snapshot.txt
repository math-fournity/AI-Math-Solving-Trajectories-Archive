## Proof that no such surface exists

It suffices to prove this for **smooth** $f$ (a subclass of continuous functions
), since ruling out all smooth functions rules out all continuous functions.

**Setup.** Let $f:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$ be smooth and let $S = \{(x,y,f(x,y
)) : (x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^2\}$ be its graph, equipped with the induced Riemannian
metric and geodesic distance $d$. Suppose for contradiction that every two disti
nct geodesic triangles on $S$ are non-congruent (i.e., have distinct unordered t
riples of side lengths).

**Geodesic circles.** Fix a point $P \in S$ (corresponding to $p \in \mathbb{R}^
2$). By the inverse function theorem, the exponential map $\exp_P : T_P S \to S$
 is a diffeomorphism near the origin. Hence for all sufficiently small $r > 0$,
the **geodesic circle**
$$C_r = \{Q \in S : d(P,Q) = r\}$$
is a simple closed curve, homeomorphic to $S^1$.

**The parameter space.** Consider the space of unordered pairs of distinct point
s on $C_r$:
$$\mathcal{P} = \bigl\{\{Q_1, Q_2\} : Q_1, Q_2 \in C_r,\; Q_1 \neq Q_2\bigr\}.$$
This is the interior of the symmetric square $\operatorname{Sym}^2(S^1)$, which
is a **Möbius band** — a 2-dimensional manifold.

Each pair $\{Q_1, Q_2\} \in \mathcal{P}$ determines a geodesic triangle $PQ_1Q_2
$ with side lengths $(r,\, r,\, d(Q_1,Q_2))$, i.e., an **isosceles** geodesic tr
iangle. Two such triangles $PQ_1Q_2$ and $PQ_1'Q_2'$ are congruent if and only i
f $d(Q_1,Q_2) = d(Q_1',Q_2')$.

**Removing degenerate pairs.** We must exclude pairs where $P, Q_1, Q_2$ are col
linear (in $\mathbb{R}^2$). The collinear pairs — where $q_1, q_2$ (the projecti
ons to $\mathbb{R}^2$) lie on a common line through $p$ — form a **1-dimensional
** subset of $\mathcal{P}$. Removing them yields a 2-dimensional open subset $\m
athcal{P}' \subseteq \mathcal{P}$.

**The key map.** Define
$$\psi : \mathcal{P}' \to \mathbb{R}, \qquad \psi(\{Q_1,Q_2\}) = d(Q_1,Q_2).$$
The geodesic distance on a smooth surface is continuous (standard result in Riem
annian geometry), so $\psi$ is continuous.

**Dimension-theoretic obstruction.** We claim $\psi$ **cannot be injective**.

Take any compact 2-dimensional subset $K \subset \mathcal{P}'$ (e.g., a closed d
isk smoothly embedded in $\mathcal{P}'$). If $\psi$ were injective, then $\psi|_
K : K \to \mathbb{R}$ would be a continuous injection from a compact space into
a Hausdorff space, hence a **homeomorphism** onto its image $\psi(K) \subset \ma
thbb{R}$.

The **Lebesgue covering dimension** is a topological invariant, so:
$$\dim(K) = \dim(\psi(K)) \leq \dim(\mathbb{R}) = 1.$$
But $K$ is a 2-dimensional disk, so $\dim(K) = 2$. This is a **contradiction**.
$\square$

**Conclusion.** Since $\psi$ is not injective, there exist two distinct pairs $\
{Q_1, Q_2\} \neq \{Q_1', Q_2'\}$ in $\mathcal{P}'$ with $d(Q_1, Q_2) = d(Q_1', Q
_2')$. The corresponding triangles $PQ_1Q_2$ and $PQ_1'Q_2'$ are:

- **Distinct:** Since $\{Q_1,Q_2\} \neq \{Q_1',Q_2'\}$ and all four points lie o
n $C_r$ (so none equals $P$), the vertex sets $\{P,Q_1,Q_2\}$ and $\{P,Q_1',Q_2'
\}$ are different.
- **Non-collinear:** By construction ($\mathcal{P}'$ excludes collinear triples)
.
- **Congruent:** Both are isosceles with identical side lengths $(r, r, d(Q_1,Q_
2))$.

This contradicts the assumed property. Therefore, **no smooth function** $f$ — a
nd hence **no continuous function** $f:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$ — can define a
 surface on which all geodesic triangles are pairwise non-congruent.

The answer is **No**.
