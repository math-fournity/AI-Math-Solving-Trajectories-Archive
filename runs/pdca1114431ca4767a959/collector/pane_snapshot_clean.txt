## Proof

**Claim:** It is **not** always true that $A'(x) = a(x)$. The equality $A'(x)=a(
x)$ holds only at the Lebesgue points of $a$ (hence almost everywhere), but it c
an fail at individual points because a locally integrable function is only deter
mined up to sets of measure zero.

---

### Counterexample

Define $a:\mathbb{R}_+\to\mathbb{R}$ by

$$
a(t) = \begin{cases} 1, & t = 1,\\ 0, & t \neq 1.\end{cases}
$$

This $a$ is locally integrable (it is bounded and measurable). Its indefinite in
tegral is

$$
A(x) = \int_0^x a(t)\,dt = 0 \quad\text{for every } x\ge 0,
$$

because modifying an integrable function at a single point (a null set) does not
 change its Lebesgue integral. Hence $A\equiv 0$, so $A$ is differentiable **eve
rywhere** with

$$
A'(x) = 0 \quad\text{for all } x\ge 0.
$$

Yet at $x=1$,

$$
a(1) = 1 \neq 0 = A'(1).
$$

Thus $A'(x)=a(x)$ fails at $x=1$, even though $A$ is differentiable there. So th
e statement "always $A'(x)=a(x)$" is **false**.

---

### What *is* true

The Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem states: for any locally integrable $a$, at
every **Lebesgue point** $x$ of $a$,

$$
A'(x) = \lim_{h\to 0}\frac{1}{h}\int_x^{x+h}a(t)\,dt = a(x).
$$

Since almost every point is a Lebesgue point, $A'(x)=a(x)$ holds **almost everyw
here**. The counterexample above is consistent with this: $x=1$ is not a Lebesgu
e point of $a$ (the average of $|a(t)-a(1)|=|a(t)-1|$ over any neighborhood of $
1$ is $\approx 1$, not tending to $0$), so the theorem does not guarantee equali
ty there.

The essential subtlety: $a$ as a locally integrable function is an equivalence c
lass modulo null sets. The integral $A$ "sees" only this equivalence class, so $
A'$ can only recover $a$ up to modification on a null set. Redefining $a$ on a n
ull set (as we did at $t=1$) leaves $A$ unchanged but breaks pointwise equality
at those points.

---

**Conclusion:** No. $A'(x)=a(x)$ holds almost everywhere (at Lebesgue points), b
ut not necessarily at *every* point where $A$ is differentiable, since $a$ may b
e redefined on a null set without affecting $A$.
