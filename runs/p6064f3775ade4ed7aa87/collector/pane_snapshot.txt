Looking at this problem, I need to determine how many $k$-simplices in a triangu
lation of $\sigma_k$ contain a given $(k-1)$-simplex that does not lie on the bo
undary $\partial\sigma_k$.

## Proof

Let $T$ be a triangulation of the $k$-simplex $\sigma_k$, and let $\tau$ be a $(
k-1)$-simplex of $T$ that is **not** contained in the boundary $\partial\sigma_k
$.

**Setup and the link.** The $k$-simplices of $T$ that contain $\tau$ as a face a
re in bijection with the vertices of the **link** of $\tau$ in $T$:
$$\operatorname{Lk}_T(\tau) = \{ \sigma \in T : \sigma \cap \tau = \emptyset,\;
\sigma \cup \tau \in T \}.$$
Specifically, each $k$-simplex containing $\tau$ has the form $\tau * \{v\}$ for
 a unique vertex $v \in \operatorname{Lk}_T(\tau)$, so the number of such $k$-si
mplices equals $|\operatorname{Lk}_T(\tau)|$, the number of vertices in the link
. Since $\tau$ is $(k-1)$-dimensional and $T$ is $k$-dimensional, the link of $\
tau$ is a $0$-dimensional simplicial complex (a finite set of points).

**Local structure at an interior point.** Since $\tau \not\subset \partial\sigma
_k$, there exists a point $p$ in the relative interior of $\tau$ that lies in th
e interior of $\sigma_k$. A sufficiently small open neighborhood $U$ of $p$ in $
|T| = \sigma_k$ is therefore homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^k$ (the interior of a $
k$-ball is a $k$-manifold).

**Product decomposition of the star.** The **star** of $\tau$ — the union of all
 simplices containing $\tau$ — admits a local product structure near $p$. A smal
l neighborhood of $p$ within the star decomposes as
$$U \cap \operatorname{St}(\tau) \;\cong\; \big(\text{open interval in } \operat
orname{relint}(\tau)\big) \times \operatorname{Cone}\big(\operatorname{Lk}_T(\ta
u)\big),$$
where $\operatorname{Cone}(\operatorname{Lk}_T(\tau))$ is the cone over the link
 (a $0$-dimensional complex of $n$ points, whose cone is a "star" of $n$ rays em
anating from the apex).

**Determining $n$.** The neighborhood $U$ of $p$ in $|T|$ is entirely covered by
 the star of $\tau$ (since every simplex near $p$ either contains $\tau$ or lies
 in $\tau$), so:
$$U \cong \mathbb{R}^{k-1} \times \operatorname{Cone}\big(\operatorname{Lk}_T(\t
au)\big).$$
For this to be homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^k = \mathbb{R}^{k-1} \times \mathbb{R
}$, we need
$$\operatorname{Cone}\big(\operatorname{Lk}_T(\tau)\big) \cong \mathbb{R}.$$

The cone over $n$ isolated points is a union of $n$ half-open rays sharing a com
mon apex. This space is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}$ (a line) **if and only if**
 $n = 2$:
- $n = 1$: a single ray (a half-line), not homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}$.
- $n = 2$: two rays joined at the apex, forming a line — homeomorphic to $\mathb
b{R}$. ✓
- $n \geq 3$: $n$ rays joined at a point, a "book" with $n$ pages — the apex is
a branch point, not locally Euclidean, so not homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}$.

**Conclusion.** The link of $\tau$ has exactly $n = 2$ vertices, so exactly **2*
* $k$-simplices of $T$ contain $\tau$.
