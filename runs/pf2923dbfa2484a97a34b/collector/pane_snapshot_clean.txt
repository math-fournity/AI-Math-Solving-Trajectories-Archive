## Evaluation of $\displaystyle I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\sin^2 x}{x^2}
\,dx$

### Step 1. Reduction via integration by parts

The integrand is bounded near the origin ($\sin^2 x / x^2 \to 1$) and decays as
$O(1/x^2)$ at infinity, so the integral converges absolutely; the principal valu
e coincides with the ordinary value.

Set $u = \sin^2 x$ and $dv = x^{-2}\,dx$, so $du = \sin(2x)\,dx$ and $v = -1/x$.
 Then

$$I = \left[-\frac{\sin^2 x}{x}\right]_{-\infty}^{\infty} + \int_{-\infty}^{\inf
ty} \frac{\sin(2x)}{x}\,dx.$$

The boundary term vanishes since $|\sin^2 x / x| \le 1/|x| \to 0$ as $x\to\pm\in
fty$. Hence

$$I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\sin(2x)}{x}\,dx. \tag{1}$$
