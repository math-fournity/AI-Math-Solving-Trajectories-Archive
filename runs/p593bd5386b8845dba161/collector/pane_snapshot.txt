Looking at this problem, I need to find the value of $x \geq 0$ that minimizes $
F(x) = \int_0^x 2^t(2^t-3)(x-t)\,dt$.

## Setting Up the Derivative

Let $g(t) = 2^t(2^t - 3) = 2^{2t} - 3\cdot 2^t$, so $F(x) = \int_0^x g(t)(x-t)\,
dt$.

By the Leibniz rule:
$$F'(x) = \int_0^x g(t)\,dt + g(x)(x-x)\cdot 1 = \int_0^x g(t)\,dt$$

Computing the integral:
$$F'(x) = \left[\frac{2^{2t}}{2\ln 2} - \frac{3\cdot 2^t}{\ln 2}\right]_0^x = \f
rac{2^{2x} - 6\cdot 2^x}{2\ln 2} - \frac{1 - 6}{2\ln 2} = \frac{2^{2x} - 6\cdot
2^x + 5}{2\ln 2}$$

## Finding Critical Points

Setting $F'(x) = 0$:
$$2^{2x} - 6\cdot 2^x + 5 = 0$$

Let $y = 2^x$ (with $y \geq 1$ since $x \geq 0$):
$$y^2 - 6y + 5 = 0 \implies (y-1)(y-5) = 0$$

So $y = 1 \Rightarrow x = 0$, or $y = 5 \Rightarrow x = \log_2 5$.

## Second Derivative Test

$$F''(x) = \frac{d}{dx}F'(x) = \frac{2\ln 2 \cdot 2^{2x} - 6\ln 2 \cdot 2^x}{2\l
n 2} = 2^{2x} - 3\cdot 2^x = 2^x(2^x - 3)$$

- At $x = 0$: $F''(0) = 1\cdot(1-3) = -2 < 0$ (local maximum — but this is the b
oundary)
- At $x = \log_2 5$: $2^x = 5$, so $F''(\log_2 5) = 5\cdot(5-3) = 10 > 0$ (local

## Confirming the Global Minimum

The sign of $F'(x)$ is determined by $(2^x - 1)(2^x - 5)$:

- On $[0, \log_2 5)$: $2^x \in [1, 5)$, so $(2^x-1) \geq 0$ and $(2^x - 5) < 0$,

- At $x = \log_2 5$: $F'(x) = 0$.
- On $(\log_2 5, \infty)$: both factors positive, $F'(x) > 0$ → **$F$ is increas
ing**.

Since $F$ decreases on $[0, \log_2 5]$ and increases on $[\log_2 5, \infty)$, th
