## Solution

We are asked to determine the average value of $F(k)$ as $k \to \infty$, where

$$F(k) = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\csc(\tfrac{1}{2})\sin(k+\tfrac{1}{2})-1\bigr).$$

The Cesàro (running) average is given as

$$\overline{F}(k)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{n=1}^{k}(k-n+1)\cos(n),$$

with the closed form

$$\overline{F}(k)=\frac{1}{4}\csc^{2}\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)\frac{\cos(1)-\co
s(k+1)}{k}-\frac{1}{2}.$$

### Step 1. Identify the relationship between $F$ and $\overline{F}$

Using the product‑to‑sum identity,
$$\sin(k+\tfrac12)=\sin k\cos\tfrac12+\cos k\sin\tfrac12,$$
so
$$\csc(\tfrac12)\sin(k+\tfrac12)=\csc(\tfrac12)\sin k\cos\tfrac12+\cos k.$$

A direct (and cleaner) route is to recognize that $F(k)$ is the oscillatory part
 of the partial sums of $\cos n$. Indeed, the standard identity for the partial
sum of cosines is
$$\sum_{n=1}^{k}\cos n=\frac{\sin(k+\tfrac12)-\sin(\tfrac12)}{2\sin(\tfrac12)}=\
frac{1}{2}\csc(\tfrac12)\sin(k+\tfrac12)-\frac{1}{2}.$$

Comparing with
$$F(k)=\frac{1}{2}\csc(\tfrac12)\sin(k+\tfrac12)-\frac{1}{2},$$
we obtain the key identification
$$F(k)=\sum_{n=1}^{k}\cos n.$$

### Step 2. Express $\overline{F}(k)$ in terms of $F$

The weighted average
$$\overline{F}(k)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{n=1}^{k}(k-n+1)\cos(n)$$
is the Cesàro mean of the partial sums $F(1),F(2),\dots,F(k)$:
$$\overline{F}(k)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{m=1}^{k}F(m).$$

(One verifies this by exchanging the order of summation: $\sum_{m=1}^{k}\sum_{n=
1}^{m}\cos n=\sum_{n=1}^{k}(k-n+1)\cos n$.)

### Step 3. Take the limit $k\to\infty$

From the closed form,
$$\overline{F}(k)=\frac{1}{4}\csc^{2}\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)\frac{\cos(1)-\co
s(k+1)}{k}-\frac{1}{2}.$$

Since $|\cos(1)-\cos(k+1)|\le 2$, the fraction $\dfrac{\cos(1)-\cos(k+1)}{k}\to
0$ as $k\to\infty$. Therefore

$$\lim_{k\to\infty}\overline{F}(k)=0-\frac{1}{2}=-\frac{1}{2}.$$

### Step 4. Consistency check via the oscillatory term

Equivalently, since $F(k)=\frac{1}{2}\csc(\tfrac12)\sin(k+\tfrac12)-\frac{1}{2}$
 and $\sin(k+\tfrac12)$ is bounded and oscillatory, its Cesàro mean tends to $0$
. Hence

$$\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{m=1}^{k}F(m)=\frac{1}{2}\csc(\tfrac12)\cdot
0-\frac{1}{2}=-\frac{1}{2},$$

confirming the result.

### Conclusion

The average value of $F(k)$ as $k\to\infty$ is

$$\boxed{-\dfrac{1}{2}}.$$
