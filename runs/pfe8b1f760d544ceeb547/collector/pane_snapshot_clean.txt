## Proof

The polynomial $f(t) = t^4 + t + 1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_2$ (given).
Since $\theta$ is a root, the quotient ring $\mathbb{F}_2[t]/(f(t))$ is a field,
 and

$$\mathbb{F}_2[\theta] \cong \mathbb{F}_2[t]/(t^4+t+1).$$

As a vector space over $\mathbb{F}_2$, this field has basis $\{1, \theta, \theta
^2, \theta^3\}$, so $[\mathbb{F}_2[\theta] : \mathbb{F}_2] = 4$ and $|\mathbb{F}
_2[\theta]| = 2^4 = 16$. Up to isomorphism there is a unique field with 16 eleme
nts, namely $\mathbb{F}_{16}$, so $\mathbb{F}_2[\theta] \cong \mathbb{F}_{16}$.

Now use the structure theory of finite fields. For any prime power $q$ and any $
n \geq 1$, the field $\mathbb{F}_{q^n}$ contains, for each divisor $d$ of $n$, a
 unique subfield isomorphic to $\mathbb{F}_{q^d}$, and these are *all* the subfi
elds of $\mathbb{F}_{q^n}$. Concretely,

$$\mathbb{F}_{q^d} = \{x \in \mathbb{F}_{q^n} : x^{q^d} = x\} \quad (\text{the f
ixed field of the Frobenius } x \mapsto x^{q^d}).$$

Here $q = 2$ and $n = 4$. The divisors of $4$ are $1, 2, 4$, giving subfields $\
mathbb{F}_2$, $\mathbb{F}_4$, and $\mathbb{F}_{16}$ itself. In particular, since
 $2 \mid 4$, there is a proper intermediate subfield

$$\mathbb{L} = \mathbb{F}_4 = \{x \in \mathbb{F}_{16} : x^4 = x\} \subsetneq \ma
thbb{F}_{16},$$

with $[\mathbb{L} : \mathbb{F}_2] = 2$ and $[\mathbb{F}_{16} : \mathbb{L}] = 2$,
 so the degrees multiply: $4 = 2 \cdot 2$, consistent with the tower $\mathbb{F}
_2 \subsetneq \mathbb{L} \subsetneq \mathbb{F}_2[\theta]$.

One can also see this directly: the multiplicative group $\mathbb{F}_{16}^{\time
s}$ is cyclic of order $15$. The element $\theta$ has order dividing $15$; since
 $f$ is irreducible of degree $4$, $\theta$ is not in any proper subfield, and i
n fact $\theta$ has order $15$ (a primitive element), so $\theta$ generates $\ma
thbb{F}_{16}^{\times}$. Then $\theta^5$ has order $15/\gcd(15,5) = 3$, and the e
lements $\{1, \theta^5, \theta^{10}\}$ form the unique subgroup of order $3$ in
$\mathbb{F}_{16}^{\times}$. Together with $0$ this gives a subfield of $4$ eleme
nts, namely $\mathbb{F}_4 = \{0, 1, \theta^5, \theta^{10}\}$, which is strictly
between $\mathbb{F}_2$ and $\mathbb{F}_2[\theta]$.

Therefore, **yes**, there exists an intermediate field $\mathbb{L}$ with $\mathb
b{F}_2 \subsetneq \mathbb{L} \subsetneq \mathbb{F}_2[\theta]$, namely $\mathbb{L
} \cong \mathbb{F}_4$.
