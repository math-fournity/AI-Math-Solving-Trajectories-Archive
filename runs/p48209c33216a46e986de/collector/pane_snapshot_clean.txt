# Solution

**Answer: No, it is not true in general.**

## Proof

The key observation is that the category ${\bf Ring}$ is **not balanced**: it ad
mits epimorphisms that are not surjective. This pathology propagates to ${\bf Sh
}(X, {\bf Ring})$.

### Counterexample

Take $X = \{*\}$, a single point. Then sheaves of rings on $X$ are equivalent to
 the category ${\bf Ring}$ itself: a sheaf $\mathscr{F}$ is determined by $\math
scr{F}(X) = R$ (with $\mathscr{F}(\emptyset) = 0$, the zero ring), and morphisms
 of sheaves correspond exactly to ring homomorphisms.

Consider the localization map $\phi: \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Q}$.

**Claim 1: $\phi$ is an epimorphism in ${\bf Ring}$ (hence in ${\bf Sh}(X, {\bf
Ring})$).**

Let $f, g: \mathbb{Q} \to R$ be two ring homomorphisms (unital) with $f \circ \p
hi = g \circ \phi$, i.e., $f|_{\mathbb{Z}} = g|_{\mathbb{Z}}$. For any $\frac{a}
{b} \in \mathbb{Q}$ with $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}$, $b \neq 0$:

Since $b \neq 0$ in $\mathbb{Z}$, $b$ is a unit in $\mathbb{Q}$, so $f(b)$ is a
unit in $R$ (unital homomorphisms preserve units). Then:
$$f\!\left(\tfrac{a}{b}\right) \cdot f(b) = f(a) = g(a) = g\!\left(\tfrac{a}{b}\
right) \cdot g(b) = g\!\left(\tfrac{a}{b}\right) \cdot f(b).$$

Multiplying both sides on the right by $f(b)^{-1}$ gives $f\!\left(\tfrac{a}{b}\
right) = g\!\left(\tfrac{a}{b}\right)$. Since $\frac{a}{b}$ was arbitrary, $f =
g$. $\checkmark$

**Claim 2: The sheafification of the image presheaf is not $\mathscr{G}$.**

The image presheaf of $\phi$ assigns to $X$ the set $\operatorname{im}(\phi) = \
phi(\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z} \subsetneq \mathbb{Q}$, and to $\emptyset$ the zero
 ring. On a point, every presheaf (satisfying the terminal-object condition on $
\emptyset$) is already a sheaf, so the sheafification of the image presheaf is $
\mathbb{Z}$ itself.

But $\mathscr{G}(X) = \mathbb{Q} \neq \mathbb{Z}$. $\checkmark$

### Conclusion

We have produced an epimorphism $\phi: \mathscr{F} \to \mathscr{G}$ in ${\bf Sh}
(X, {\bf Ring})$ such that $\mathscr{G}$ is **not** the sheafification of the im
age presheaf of $\phi$.

**Remark on why this fails for rings but not for abelian groups.** For sheaves o
f sets or sheaves of abelian groups, epimorphisms coincide with locally surjecti
ve maps, and the sheafification of the image presheaf equals the target sheaf. T
he failure here is specific to ${\bf Ring}$: the category ${\bf Ring}$ is not ab
elian (not even additive), and it admits non-surjective epimorphisms (such as lo
calizations). Since ${\bf Sh}(\{*\}, {\bf Ring}) \cong {\bf Ring}$, this non-bal
anced behavior is inherited by the sheaf category.
