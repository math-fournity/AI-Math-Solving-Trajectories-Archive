## Proof

**Setup.** Label the three countries $A$, $B$, $C$, each with 3 delegates. Seat
9 people at a round table with 9 seats. Since the desired condition depends only
 on the country assigned to each seat, we compute the probability over all count
ry-label assignments of 3 $A$'s, 3 $B$'s, 3 $C$'s to 9 fixed positions in a circ
le. Each delegate assignment corresponds to $(3!)^3$ country-label assignments,
so the probabilities coincide.

**Total arrangements:**
$$\binom{9}{3}\binom{6}{3}\binom{3}{3} = 84 \cdot 20 \cdot 1 = 1680.$$

**Key equivalence.** The condition "each delegate sits next to at least one dele
gate from another country" fails for a delegate $P$ of country $X$ exactly when
both neighbors of $P$ are also country $X$. Since there are only 3 delegates of
country $X$, this forces all three $X$'s to occupy three consecutive seats (a ru
n of length 3). Conversely, any run of three consecutive same-country seats has
its middle delegate flanked by two same-country neighbors, violating the conditi
on. Therefore:

> The condition holds **iff no country has all three of its delegates in consecu
tive seats.**

**Inclusion–Exclusion.** Let $E_X$ = event that all three delegates of country $
X$ are consecutive. We seek $1680 - |E_A \cup E_B \cup E_C|$.

**Computing $|E_A|$:** Choose the starting position of the 3-seat $A$-block in t
he circle: 9 choices. Fill the remaining 6 seats with 3 $B$'s and 3 $C$'s: $\bin
om{6}{3}=20$ choices. Since there is only one $A$-run (only 3 $A$'s), no overcou
nting.
$$|E_A| = 9 \times 20 = 180.$$
By symmetry, $|E_A|=|E_B|=|E_C|=180$.

**Computing $|E_A \cap E_B|$:** Fix the $A$-block at some position (9 choices).
The remaining 6 seats form a **path** (line) of 6 consecutive positions, since t
he $A$-block separates the two ends in the circle. We need 3 consecutive $B$'s w
ithin this line of 6: there are $6 - 3 + 1 = 4$ choices. The $C$'s fill the rest
.
$$|E_A \cap E_B| = 9 \times 4 = 36.$$
By symmetry, all pairwise intersections equal 36.

**Computing $|E_A \cap E_B \cap E_C|$:** All three countries form runs of 3. Fix
 the $A$-block (9 choices). The remaining line of 6 must split into a $B$-block
of 3 and a $C$-block of 3, each consecutive. Only the two end-halves work: $B$ t
akes the first 3 and $C$ the last 3, or vice versa — 2 choices. (Middle splits l
ike $B=\{4,5,6\}$ leave $C$ non-consecutive.)
$$|E_A \cap E_B \cap E_C| = 9 \times 2 = 18.$$

**Apply inclusion–exclusion:**
$$|E_A \cup E_B \cup E_C| = 3(180) - 3(36) + 18 = 540 - 108 + 18 = 450.$$

**Good arrangements:**
$$1680 - 450 = 1230.$$

**Probability:**
$$\frac{1230}{1680} = \frac{41}{56}, \qquad \gcd(41,56)=1.$$

So $m = 41$, $n = 56$, and

$$m + n = \boxed{97}.$$
