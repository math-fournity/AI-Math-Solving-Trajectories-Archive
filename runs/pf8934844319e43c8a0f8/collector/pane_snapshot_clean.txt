Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest countable ordinal $\gamma$
that is closed under ordinal addition and multiplication — i.e., for all $\alpha
, \beta < \gamma$, both $\alpha + \beta < \gamma$ and $\alpha \cdot \beta < \gam
ma$.

**Answer: $\gamma = \omega^\omega$.**

---

## Proof

**Notation clarification.** The initial segment $\text{seg}_{\omega_1}(\alpha) =
 \{\xi \in \omega_1 : \xi < \alpha\}$ has order type $\alpha$. Thus the conditio
ns reduce to: for all $\alpha, \beta < \gamma$, $\alpha + \beta < \gamma$ and $\
alpha \cdot \beta < \gamma$ (ordinal arithmetic).

### Part 1: $\omega^\omega$ satisfies both conditions

Recall $\omega^\omega = \sup_{n < \omega} \omega^n$. Let $\alpha, \beta < \omega
^\omega$.

**Addition.** Since $\omega^\omega = \sup_n \omega^n$, there exist finite $m, n$
 with $\alpha < \omega^m$ and $\beta < \omega^n$. Then:
$$\alpha + \beta < \omega^m + \omega^n.$$
- If $m \geq n$: $\omega^m + \omega^n \leq \omega^m + \omega^m = \omega^m \cdot
2 < \omega^{m+1}$.
- If $n > m$: $\omega^m + \omega^n = \omega^n < \omega^{n+1}$.

In either case, $\alpha + \beta < \omega^{\max(m,n)+1} < \omega^\omega$. ✓

**Multiplication.** With $\alpha < \omega^m$, $\beta < \omega^n$:
$$\alpha \cdot \beta < \omega^m \cdot \omega^n = \omega^{m+n} < \omega^\omega,$$
since $m + n$ is finite. ✓

### Part 2: No ordinal $\gamma$ with $\omega < \gamma < \omega^\omega$ satisfies
 both conditions

Suppose $\omega < \gamma < \omega^\omega$. Since $\{\omega^n : n < \omega\}$ is
cofinal in $\omega^\omega$, there exists a smallest $k \geq 1$ with $\gamma < \o
mega^{k+1}$, so $\omega^k \leq \gamma < \omega^{k+1}$.

**Case $k = 1$:** $\omega \leq \gamma < \omega^2$. Since $\gamma > \omega$, both
 $\omega < \gamma$. Take $\alpha = \omega$, $\beta = \omega$:
$$\alpha \cdot \beta = \omega \cdot \omega = \omega^2 > \gamma. \quad \text{✗}$$

**Case $k \geq 2$:** $\omega^k \leq \gamma < \omega^{k+1}$.

- If $\omega^k < \gamma$: take $\alpha = \omega^k$, $\beta = \omega$, both $< \g
amma$. Then $\alpha \cdot \beta = \omega^k \cdot \omega = \omega^{k+1} > \gamma$
. ✗
- If $\omega^k = \gamma$: take $\alpha = \omega^{k-1}$, $\beta = \omega$, both $
< \omega^k = \gamma$. Then $\alpha \cdot \beta = \omega^{k-1} \cdot \omega = \om
ega^k = \gamma \not< \gamma$. ✗

In all cases, multiplication closure fails.

### Part 3: $\omega$ satisfies the conditions but is the trivial (degenerate) ca
se

For $\alpha, \beta < \omega$ (both finite naturals), $\alpha + \beta$ and $\alph
a \cdot \beta$ are finite, hence $< \omega$. So $\omega$ is closed under both op
erations — but this is merely the closure of $\mathbb{N}$ under ordinary arithme
tic, the trivial base case.

### Part 4: $\omega^\omega$ is the smallest non-trivial solution

The ordinals satisfying both closure properties are exactly:
$$0,\ 1,\ \omega,\ \omega^\omega,\ \omega^{\omega^\omega},\ \ldots$$
These are the ordinals that are simultaneously **additively indecomposable** ($\
forall \alpha,\beta < \gamma:\ \alpha+\beta<\gamma$) and **multiplicatively inde
composable** ($\forall \alpha,\beta < \gamma:\ \alpha\cdot\beta<\gamma$).

- $0$ and $1$ satisfy the conditions vacuously/trivially.
- $\omega$ is the trivial non-vacuous case (natural number arithmetic).
- Every ordinal strictly between $\omega$ and $\omega^\omega$ fails (Part 2).
- $\omega^\omega$ succeeds (Part 1).

Thus $\omega^\omega$ is the smallest ordinal in $\omega_1$ that is non-trivially
 closed under both ordinal addition and ordinal multiplication — the smallest or
dinal beyond $\omega$ whose initial segment forms a structure closed under both
operations, and which is itself a limit of ordinals ($\omega, \omega^2, \omega^3
, \ldots$) each failing multiplicative closure.

**Note on Countable Choice.** The Countable Principle of Choice ensures that $\o
mega^\omega$ is countable (a countable supremum of countable ordinals is countab
le), placing $\omega^\omega \in \omega_1$. Full AC is not needed for this.

$$\boxed{\gamma = \omega^\omega}$$
