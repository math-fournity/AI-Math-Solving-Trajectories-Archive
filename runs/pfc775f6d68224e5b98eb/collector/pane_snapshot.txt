## Solution

**Setting up coordinates.** Since $AB=3$, $BC=4$, $CA=5$ and $AB^2+BC^2=9+16=25=
CA^2$, triangle $ABC$ is right-angled at $B$. Place
$$B=(0,0),\quad A=(3,0),\quad C=(0,4).$$

**Finding $D$.** The line $AC$ has direction $(-3,4)$, so a perpendicular direct
ion is $(4,3)$. The line through $A=(3,0)$ perpendicular to $AC$ is
$$(3+4t,\; 3t).$$
It meets line $BC$ (the $y$-axis, $x=0$) when $3+4t=0$, i.e. $t=-\tfrac{3}{4}$,
giving
$$D=\left(0,\,-\tfrac{9}{4}\right).$$

**Finding $E$.** The line through $C=(0,4)$ perpendicular to $AC$ is
$$(4s,\; 4+3s).$$
It meets line $AB$ (the $x$-axis, $y=0$) when $4+3s=0$, i.e. $s=-\tfrac{4}{3}$,
giving
$$E=\left(-\tfrac{16}{3},\; 0\right).$$

**Computing the area.** With $B=(0,0)$, $D=(0,-\tfrac{9}{4})$, $E=(-\tfrac{16}{3
},0)$, the segments $BD$ and $BE$ lie along the coordinate axes, so $\angle DBE
= 90°$. Thus
$$[BDE]=\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot BD\cdot BE=\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot \tfrac{9}{4}\cdot \tfrac{
16}{3}=\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 12=6.$$

The area of triangle $BDE$ is $\boxed{6}$.
