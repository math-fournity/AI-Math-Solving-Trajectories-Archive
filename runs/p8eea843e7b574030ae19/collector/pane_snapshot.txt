## Solution

We use the **Fredholm alternative** for the Dirichlet Laplacian on $\Omega=(0,\p
i)^2$.

**Step 1. Eigenstructure of the Dirichlet Laplacian.**

The operator $A=-\Delta$ on $L^2(\Omega)$ with domain $H_0^1(\Omega)\cap H^2(\Om
ega)$ has a complete orthonormal eigenbasis
$$\varphi_{mn}(x,y)=\tfrac{2}{\pi}\sin(mx)\sin(ny),\qquad m,n\ge 1,$$
with eigenvalues
$$\lambda_{mn}=m^2+n^2.$$

**Step 2. Reformulate the equation.**

The PDE $\Delta u+2u = x-\alpha$ is equivalent to
$$(2I-A)u = f,\qquad f(x,y)=x-\alpha,\qquad A=-\Delta.$$
The operator $2I-A$ has eigenvalues $2-\lambda_{mn}=2-(m^2+n^2)$. This equals $0
$ exactly when $m^2+n^2=2$, i.e. $(m,n)=(1,1)$. Hence
$$\ker(2I-A)=\operatorname{span}\{\sin x\,\sin y\},$$
and $2I-A$ is not invertible; it is Fredholm of index $0$.

**Step 3. Apply the Fredholm alternative.**

A (weak) solution $u\in H_0^1(\Omega)$ exists if and only if the right-hand side
 $f$ is orthogonal in $L^2(\Omega)$ to the kernel:
$$\langle f,\,\sin x\,\sin y\rangle_{L^2(\Omega)}=0.$$

(Indeed, the weak formulation reads: find $u\in H_0^1$ with
$$\int_\Omega \nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx\,dy-2\int_\Omega uv\,dx\,dy=-\int_\Omega
 fv\,dx\,dy\quad\forall v\in H_0^1.$$
The associated self-adjoint eigenproblem $-\Delta \varphi-2\varphi=\mu\varphi$ h
as $\mu=0$ for $(m,n)=(1,1)$, so the kernel of the bilinear form is $\operatorna
me{span}\{\sin x\sin y\}$, and the Fredholm alternative gives the same orthogona
lity condition.)

**Step 4. Compute the orthogonality condition.**

We need
$$\int_0^\pi\!\!\int_0^\pi (x-\alpha)\sin x\,\sin y\,dx\,dy=0.$$

The integral separates:
$$\left(\int_0^\pi (x-\alpha)\sin x\,dx\right)\!\left(\int_0^\pi \sin y\,dy\righ
t)=0.$$

Compute each factor:
$$\int_0^\pi \sin y\,dy = 2,$$
$$\int_0^\pi x\sin x\,dx = \bigl[-x\cos x\bigr]_0^\pi+\int_0^\pi\cos x\,dx = \pi
+0=\pi,$$
$$\int_0^\pi \sin x\,dx = 2,$$
so
$$\int_0^\pi (x-\alpha)\sin x\,dx = \pi - 2\alpha.$$

Therefore the orthogonality condition becomes
$$(\pi-2\alpha)\cdot 2 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \pi-2\alpha=0 \quad\Longrig
htarrow\quad \alpha=\frac{\pi}{2}.$$

When $\alpha=\pi/2$, the right-hand side $f(x,y)=x-\pi/2$ satisfies $\langle f,\
sin x\sin y\rangle=0$, so by the Fredholm alternative the equation $(2I-A)u=f$ a

$2\pi-4\alpha\neq 0$, and no weak solution exists.
