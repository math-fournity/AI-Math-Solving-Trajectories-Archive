## Solution

I need to find all $p > 1$ such that $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} f_n$ converges in $L^p
(\mathbb{R})$, where $f_n(x) = \frac{1}{1+n^2\sqrt{x}}\chi_{[e^{2n},\, 2e^{2n+2}
]}(x)$.

### Step 1: Bounded Overlap of Supports

Let $I_n = [e^{2n}, 2e^{2n+2}]$. A point $x$ belongs to $I_n$ if and only if:
$$\frac{\ln x - \ln 2 - 2}{2} \le n \le \frac{\ln x}{2}.$$
The range of admissible $n$ has length $1 + \frac{\ln 2}{2} \approx 1.347$, so *
*at most 2** of the intervals $I_n$ contain any given point $x$. Since $f_n \ge
0$, the partial sums $S_N = \sum_{n=1}^N f_n$ increase pointwise to $S = \sum_{n
=1}^\infty f_n$. By the Monotone Convergence Theorem, the series converges in $L
^p$ if and only if $S \in L^p$.

With bounded overlap (factor 2), we have the equivalence (up to constants):
$$\|S\|_p^p \asymp \sum_{n=1}^\infty \|f_n\|_p^p.$$
Specifically, $S(x) \le 2\max_n f_n(x)$ gives $\|S\|_p^p \le 2^p \sum_n \|f_n\|_
p^p$, and $S \ge f_n$ on $I_n$ gives $\|S\|_p^p \ge \max_n \|f_n\|_p^p$. Thus th
e series converges in $L^p$ iff $\sum_n \|f_n\|_p^p < \infty$.

### Step 2: Compute $\|f_n\|_p^p$

$$\|f_n\|_p^p = \int_{e^{2n}}^{2e^{2n+2}} \frac{dx}{(1+n^2\sqrt{x})^p}.$$

Substituting $u = \sqrt{x}$ ($dx = 2u\,du$), then $v = n^2 u$ ($du = dv/n^2$):

$$\|f_n\|_p^p = \frac{2}{n^4}\int_{n^2 e^n}^{n^2\sqrt{2}\,e^{n+1}} \frac{v}{(1+v
)^p}\,dv.$$

Since $n^2 e^n \to \infty$ exponentially, $v$ is large throughout the integratio
n range, so $\frac{v}{(1+v)^p} \approx v^{1-p}$.

**Case $p \neq 2$:**
$$\|f_n\|_p^p \approx \frac{2}{n^4}\cdot\frac{v^{2-p}}{2-p}\bigg|_{n^2 e^n}^{n^2
\sqrt{2}\,e^{n+1}} = \frac{2\,n^{2-2p}}{2-p}\Big[2^{(2-p)/2}e^{2-p}-1\Big]\,e^{n
(2-p)}.$$

**Case $p = 2$:**
$$\|f_n\|_2^2 = \frac{2}{n^4}\left[\ln(1+v)+\frac{1}{1+v}\right]_{n^2 e^n}^{n^2\
sqrt{2}\,e^{n+1}} \sim \frac{2\ln(\sqrt{2}\,e)}{n^4} = \frac{\ln 2 + 2}{n^4}.$$

### Step 3: Determine Convergence

**Subcase $1 < p < 2$ ($2-p > 0$):** Both $2^{(2-p)/2} > 1$ and $e^{2-p} > 1$, s
o the bracketed constant is positive. The factor $e^{n(2-p)}$ grows exponentiall
y, so $\|f_n\|_p^p \to \infty$. The series **diverges**.

**Subcase $p = 2$:** $\|f_n\|_2^2 \sim \frac{\ln 2 + 2}{n^4}$, so $\sum_n \|f_n\
|_2^2 \le C\sum_n n^{-4} < \infty$. The series **converges**.

**Subcase $p > 2$ ($2-p < 0$):** The prefactor $\frac{1}{2-p} < 0$ and the brack
et $2^{(2-p)/2}e^{2-p}-1 < 0$ (both factors $< 1$), so their product is positive
. The factor $e^{n(2-p)} = e^{-n(p-2)}$ decays exponentially, and $n^{2-2p} \to
0$. Thus $\|f_n\|_p^p$ decays exponentially and $\sum_n \|f_n\|_p^p < \infty$. T
he series **converges**.

### Conclusion

The series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} f_n$ converges in $L^p(\mathbb{R})$ if and only
if $\boxed{p \ge 2}$.
