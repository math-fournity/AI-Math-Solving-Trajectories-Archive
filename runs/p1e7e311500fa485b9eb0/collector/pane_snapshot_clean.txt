## Proof

Let $S$ be the bilateral shift on $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z})$, $(Sa)_n = a_{n-1}$. $S$
is unitary, with $S^* = S^{-1}$.

**Step 1. Diagonalize via the Fourier transform.**

Let $\mathcal{F} : \ell^2(\mathbb{Z}) \to L^2(\mathbb{T})$ be the unitary Fourie
r transform
$$
(\mathcal{F}a)(e^{i\theta}) = \sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}} a_n e^{in\theta}, \qquad \te
xt{(convergence in } L^2\text{)}.
$$
A direct computation on the dense subspace of finitely supported sequences shows
$$
\mathcal{F}(Sa)(e^{i\theta}) = \sum_n a_{n-1} e^{in\theta} = e^{i\theta}\sum_m a
_m e^{im\theta} = e^{i\theta}(\mathcal{F}a)(e^{i\theta}).
$$
Hence $\mathcal{F}S\mathcal{F}^{-1} = M_z$, the operator of multiplication by $z
 = e^{i\theta}$ on $L^2(\mathbb{T})$. By linearity,
$$
\mathcal{F}(I - S - S^2)\mathcal{F}^{-1} = M_{\,1 - z - z^2}.
$$
Since $\mathcal{F}$ is unitary, $\|I - S - S^2\| = \|M_{\,1 - z - z^2}\|$.

**Step 2. Norm of a multiplication operator.**

For $\varphi \in L^\infty(\mathbb{T})$, the multiplication operator $M_\varphi$
on $L^2(\mathbb{T})$ satisfies $\|M_\varphi\| = \|\varphi\|_{L^\infty}$ (the ess
ential supremum). The inequality $\le$ is immediate; for $\ge$, for any $\vareps
ilon>0$ the set $E_\varepsilon = \{|\varphi| \ge \|\varphi\|_\infty - \varepsilo
n\}$ has positive measure, and $\|M_\varphi\mathbf{1}_{E_\varepsilon}\|_2 \ge (\
|\varphi\|_\infty - \varepsilon)\|\mathbf{1}_{E_\varepsilon}\|_2$.

Here $\varphi(z) = 1 - z - z^2$ is continuous, so the essential supremum is the
ordinary maximum:
$$
\|I - S - S^2\| = \max_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)} |1 - e^{i\theta} - e^{2i\theta}|.
$$

**Step 3. Maximize $|1 - z - z^2|$ on $\mathbb{T}$.**

With $z = e^{i\theta}$, $|z|=1$, expand:
$$
|1 - z - z^2|^2 = (1 - z - z^2)(1 - \bar z - \bar z^2).
$$
Using $z\bar z = 1$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1 - z - z^2)(1 - \bar z - \bar z^2)
&= 1 - \bar z - \bar z^2 - z + z\bar z + z\bar z^2 - z^2 + z^2\bar z + z^2\bar z
^2 \\
&= 1 - \bar z - \bar z^2 - z + 1 + \bar z - z^2 + z + 1 \\
&= 3 - (z^2 + \bar z^2) \\
&= 3 - 2\cos(2\theta).
\end{aligned}
$$
(The cross terms $z\bar z^2 = \bar z$ and $z^2\bar z = z$ cancel the linear term
s $-z-\bar z$.)

Thus
$$
|1 - e^{i\theta} - e^{2i\theta}|^2 = 3 - 2\cos(2\theta).
$$
This ranges over $[3 - 2, 3 + 2] = [1, 5]$, attaining the maximum $5$ when $\cos
(2\theta) = -1$, i.e. $\theta = \pi/2$ (where $z = i$ and $1 - i - i^2 = 2 - i$,
 $|2-i|^2 = 5$).

Therefore
$$
\|I - S - S^2\| = \sqrt{5}.
$$
