## Definition of the Pfaffian

The Pfaffian of a $2n \times 2n$ skew-symmetric matrix $A$ is the polynomial

$$\operatorname{Pf}(A) = \frac{1}{2^n\, n!}\sum_{\sigma \in S_{2n}} \operatornam
e{sgn}(\sigma)\, A_{\sigma(1)\sigma(2)} \cdots A_{\sigma(2n-1)\,\sigma(2n)} = \s
um_{\text{perfect matchings } \pi} \operatorname{sgn}(\pi) \prod_{\{i,j\}\in\pi}
 A_{ij}.$$

Equivalently, the Pfaffian is the sum over all **perfect matchings** (pairings)
of the index set $\{1, \dots, 2n\}$. The fundamental identity is $\operatorname{
Pf}(A)^2 = \det(A)$.

When the entries $A_{ij}$ are differential forms (here, 2-forms), the Pfaffian i
s computed with the wedge product in place of ordinary multiplication, yielding
a form of degree $2n$ (a product of $n$ 2-forms).

## The 3-Dimensional Case

For our 3-manifold, the curvature 2-form matrix $\Omega$ is $3 \times 3$ — an **
odd-dimensional** skew-symmetric matrix.

**Key observation:** The Pfaffian is a sum over perfect matchings of the index s
et. A perfect matching of $\{1, 2, 3\}$ would require partitioning 3 elements in
to disjoint pairs, which is impossible since 3 is odd. The set of perfect matchi
ngs of a 3-element set is **empty**.

Therefore:

$$\operatorname{Pf}(\Omega) = \sum_{\pi \in \varnothing} (\cdots) = 0.$$

## Verification via the Determinant Identity

We can confirm this through the identity $\operatorname{Pf}(A)^2 = \det(A)$. For
 any $3 \times 3$ skew-symmetric matrix $A$ (with entries in any commutative rin
g):

$$\det(A) = \det(-A^T) = (-1)^3 \det(A) = -\det(A),$$

so $\det(A) = 0$. Hence $\operatorname{Pf}(A)^2 = 0$, which is consistent with $
\operatorname{Pf}(A) = 0$.

## Consistency with the Chern–Gauss–Bonnet Theorem

The Pfaffian of the curvature 2-form gives the **Euler form**:

$$e(M) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^n}\operatorname{Pf}(\Omega) \in \Omega^{2n}(M),$$

defined for a $2n$-dimensional manifold. The Chern–Gauss–Bonnet theorem states $
\int_M e(M) = \chi(M)$.

For a **compact** oriented 3-manifold (odd-dimensional), Poincaré duality gives
$\chi(M) = 0$. The vanishing of $\operatorname{Pf}(\Omega)$ is the local differe
ntial-geometric manifestation of this topological fact: the Euler form is identi
cally zero in odd dimensions because the Pfaffian of an odd-dimensional skew-sym
metric matrix vanishes.
