## Solution

We solve the wave equation $u_{tt}=c^{2}u_{xx}$ on the half-line $x>0$, $t>0$, w
ith
$$u(0,t)=f(t),\qquad \lim_{x\to\infty}u(x,t)=0,\qquad u(x,0)=0,\qquad u_t(x,0)=0
.$$

### 1. General solution of the wave equation

The d'Alembert form of the general solution is
$$u(x,t)=F(x-ct)+G(x+ct),$$
where $F$ and $G$ are arbitrary (sufficiently smooth) functions, determined by d
ata. Here $F(x-ct)$ is a right-traveling wave (propagating in $+x$) and $G(x+ct)
$ is a left-traveling wave (propagating in $-x$).

### 2. Applying the initial conditions (for $x>0$)

At $t=0$, for every $x>0$:
$$u(x,0)=F(x)+G(x)=0, \tag{1}$$
$$u_t(x,0)=-cF'(x)+cG'(x)=0. \tag{2}$$

From (1): $G(x)=-F(x)$ for $x>0$. Differentiating: $G'(x)=-F'(x)$ for $x>0$. Sub
stituting into (2):
$$-cF'(x)+c(-F'(x))=-2cF'(x)=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; F'(x)=0,\quad x>0.$$

Hence $F$ (and therefore $G=-F$) is constant on $(0,\infty)$. The decay conditio
n $\lim_{x\to\infty}u(x,t)=0$ forces this constant to be $0$:
$$F(x)=0,\qquad G(x)=0,\qquad x>0. \tag{3}$$

So both profiles vanish on the positive half-line; the only nontrivial informati
on must come from the boundary.

### 3. Applying the boundary condition

At $x=0$:
$$u(0,t)=F(-ct)+G(ct)=f(t).$$

Since $ct>0$ for $t>0$, (3) gives $G(ct)=0$, leaving
$$F(-ct)=f(t),\qquad t>0. \tag{4}$$

Setting $s=-ct<0$, i.e. $t=-s/c$, this determines $F$ on the negative half-line:
$$F(s)=f\!\left(-\frac{s}{c}\right),\qquad s<0. \tag{5}$$

### 4. Assembling the solution

For $x>0$, $t>0$ we always have $x+ct>0$, so by (3) $G(x+ct)=0$, and
$$u(x,t)=F(x-ct).$$

There are two cases:

- **$x-ct>0$ (i.e. $t<x/c$, the region ahead of the wavefront):** by (3), $F(x-c
t)=0$.
- **$x-ct<0$ (i.e. $t>x/c$, the region behind the wavefront):** by (5) with $s=x
-ct$,
  $$F(x-ct)=f\!\left(-\frac{x-ct}{c}\right)=f\!\left(t-\frac{x}{c}\right).$$

At the wavefront $t=x/c$ the value is $f(0)$; consistency with the initial data
requires $f(0)=0$ (otherwise a jump discontinuity propagates along $x=ct$). With
 $f(0)=0$ the solution is continuous across the front.

### 5. Verification

Let $u(x,t)=f(t-x/c)$ for $t\ge x/c$ and $u(x,t)=0$ for $t<x/c$.

- **PDE:** In the region $t>x/c$, $u=f(t-x/c)$, so $u_t=f'(t-x/c)$, $u_{tt}=f''(
t-x/c)$, $u_x=-\tfrac1c f'(t-x/c)$, $u_{xx}=\tfrac1{c^{2}}f''(t-x/c)$. Thus $u_{
tt}=f''=c^{2}\cdot\tfrac1{c^{2}}f''=c^{2}u_{xx}$. In $t<x/c$, $u\equiv0$ trivial
ly satisfies the PDE.
- **Boundary:** $u(0,t)=f(t-0)=f(t)$. ✓
- **Decay at infinity:** For fixed $t$, once $x>ct$ we have $t<x/c$, so $u=0$; h
ence $\lim_{x\to\infty}u(x,t)=0$. ✓
- **Initial displacement:** $u(x,0)=0$ since $t=0<x/c$ for every $x>0$. ✓
- **Initial velocity:** $u_t(x,0)=0$ since $u\equiv0$ in a neighborhood of $t=0$
 for each fixed $x>0$. ✓

All conditions are satisfied.

### 6. Final answer

$$\boxed{\,u(x,t)=\begin{cases} f\!\left(t-\dfrac{x}{c}\right), & t\ge \dfrac{x}
{c},\\[6pt] 0, & 0<t<\dfrac{x}{c},\end{cases}\,}$$

equivalently $u(x,t)=f(t-x/c)\,H\!\left(t-\frac{x}{c}\right)$, where $H$ is the
Heaviside step function. The boundary signal $f$ travels to the right with speed
 $c$; the point $(x,t)$ feels the boundary only after the travel time $x/c$ has
elapsed.
