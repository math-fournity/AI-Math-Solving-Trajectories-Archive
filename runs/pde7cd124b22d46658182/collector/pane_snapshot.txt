## Proof

**Claim:** A reduced ring with identity cannot have exactly 5 idempotent element
s.

**Step 1: Idempotents in a reduced ring are central.**

Let $R$ be a reduced ring (no nonzero nilpotents) with identity, and let $e \in
R$ be idempotent ($e^2 = e$). For any $r \in R$, consider the element $x = er(1-
e)$. Then
$$x^2 = er(1-e)\cdot er(1-e) = e\,r\,\underbrace{(1-e)e}_{=\,0}\,r(1-e) = 0.$$
Since $R$ is reduced, $x = 0$, so $er = ere$. Similarly, $y = (1-e)re$ satisfies
 $y^2 = (1-e)re\cdot(1-e)re = (1-e)r\,\underbrace{e(1-e)}_{=\,0}\,re = 0$, so $(
1-e)re = 0$, giving $re = ere$. Therefore $er = re$ for all $r$, i.e., $e$ is ce
ntral.

**Step 2: The idempotents form a Boolean algebra.**

Let $B = \{e \in R : e^2 = e\}$. Clearly $0, 1 \in B$. Since all idempotents are
 central, for $e, f \in B$:

- **Meet:** $ef$ is idempotent since $(ef)^2 = e^2 f^2 = ef$ (using commutativit
y from centrality).
- **Complement:** $1 - e$ is idempotent since $(1-e)^2 = 1 - 2e + e^2 = 1 - e$.
- **Join:** $e \vee f = e + f - ef$ is idempotent:
$$(e+f-ef)^2 = e^2 + f^2 + (ef)^2 + 2ef - 2e^2f - 2ef^2 = e + f + ef + 2ef - 2ef
 - 2ef = e + f - ef.$$

These operations satisfy the Boolean algebra axioms (distributivity follows from
 centrality; complementation is verified by $e(1-e) = 0$ and $e + (1-e) = 1$). T
hus $(B, \vee, \wedge, \complement, 0, 1)$ is a Boolean algebra.

**Step 3: A finite Boolean algebra has $2^n$ elements.**

A finite Boolean algebra is isomorphic to the power set $\mathcal{P}(S)$ of its
set $S$ of atoms (via the map $b \mapsto \{a \in S : a \leq b\}$), hence has $|B
| = 2^{|S|} = 2^n$ for some non-negative integer $n$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

If $R$ has exactly 5 idempotents, then $B$ is a finite Boolean algebra with $|B|
 = 5$. By Step 3, $|B| = 2^n$ for some $n \geq 0$. But $5$ is not a power of $2$
 ($2^0=1, 2^1=2, 2^2=4, 2^3=8, \ldots$). Contradiction.

Therefore, **a reduced ring with identity cannot have exactly 5 idempotent eleme
