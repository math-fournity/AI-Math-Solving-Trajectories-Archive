## Solution

We evaluate $\lim_{x\to\infty} x!\, e^{-x^3}$, where $x! = \Gamma(x+1)$ for real
 $x$.

**Stirling's approximation** gives, as $x \to \infty$:
$$x! \sim \sqrt{2\pi x}\left(\frac{x}{e}\right)^x = \sqrt{2\pi x}\, e^{x\ln x -
x}.$$

Therefore:
$$x!\, e^{-x^3} \sim \sqrt{2\pi x}\, e^{x\ln x - x - x^3} = \sqrt{2\pi x}\, e^{x
(\ln x - 1 - x^2)}.$$

**Analysis of the exponent.** Consider $g(x) = \ln x - 1 - x^2$. As $x \to \inft
y$:
- $\ln x$ grows logarithmically,
- $-x^2$ grows quadratically to $-\infty$,

so $g(x) \to -\infty$. Consequently:
$$x\, g(x) = x(\ln x - 1 - x^2) \to -\infty \quad\text{as } x\to\infty.$$

**Combining the factors.** We have:
$$x!\, e^{-x^3} \sim \sqrt{2\pi x}\, e^{x(\ln x - 1 - x^2)}.$$

The factor $\sqrt{2\pi x}$ grows like $x^{1/2}$ (polynomial), while $e^{x(\ln x
- 1 - x^2)} \to 0$ decays super-exponentially (since the exponent tends to $-\in
fty$ faster than any polynomial). Hence the exponential decay dominates:
$$\lim_{x\to\infty} \sqrt{2\pi x}\, e^{x(\ln x - 1 - x^2)} = 0.$$

**Rigorous justification via Stirling bounds.** Stirling's formula provides two-
sided bounds: for sufficiently large $x$,
$$c_1\sqrt{2\pi x}\left(\frac{x}{e}\right)^x \le x! \le c_2\sqrt{2\pi x}\left(\f
rac{x}{e}\right)^x$$
for constants $c_1, c_2 > 0$. Both bounds, when multiplied by $e^{-x^3}$, yield
$$c_i\sqrt{2\pi x}\, e^{x(\ln x - 1 - x^2)} \to 0,$$
by the argument above. By the squeeze theorem:
$$\boxed{\lim_{x\to\infty} x!\, e^{-x^3} = 0.}$$
