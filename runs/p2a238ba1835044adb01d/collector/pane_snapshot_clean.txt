## A Non-Normal Covering of the Figure Eight

Let the figure eight be the wedge of two circles, with fundamental group $F_2 =
\langle a, b\rangle$, where $a$ and $b$ are the two loop generators.

### Construction (a 3-sheeted covering)

Build a covering $p:\tilde{X}\to X$ with three sheets, labeled $1,2,3$. The lift
ed edges are specified by permutations of $\{1,2,3\}$:

- **$a$-edges** act as the transposition $(1\;2)$: an $a$-edge runs $1\to 2$ and
 $2\to 1$, and an $a$-loop sits at vertex $3$.
- **$b$-edges** act as the 3-cycle $(1\;2\;3)$: a $b$-edge runs $1\to 2$, $2\to
3$, $3\to 1$ (with $b^{-1}$-edges reversing these).

Explicitly the covering graph is:

| Vertex | $a$-edge out | $b$-edge out |
|--------|--------------|--------------|
| 1      | $\to 2$      | $\to 2$      |
| 2      | $\to 1$      | $\to 3$      |
| 3      | loop at 3    | $\to 1$      |

Each vertex has exactly one outgoing and one incoming edge of each label, so thi
s is a genuine 3-sheeted covering of the figure eight.

### Why it is a covering

At every vertex the local structure is identical to the base: one $a$-edge enter
ing/leaving and one $b$-edge entering/leaving. The projection $p$ maps all three
 vertices to the wedge point and maps each labeled edge to the corresponding loo
p in the base, so $p$ is a local homeomorphism. Hence $p:\tilde X\to X$ is a cov
ering map.

### Why the covering is non-normal

Choose vertex $1$ as basepoint of $\tilde X$. The subgroup of $F_2$ correspondin
g to this covering is
$$H = \{\, g\in F_2 : \text{the lift of } g \text{ starting at } 1 \text{ ends a
t } 1 \,\}.$$

A covering is normal (regular) iff $H\trianglelefteq F_2$, equivalently iff the
deck transformation group acts transitively on the fiber over the basepoint.

**Argument 1 (deck transformations are trivial).** A deck transformation is a pe
rmutation $\sigma$ of $\{1,2,3\}$ preserving the labeled edge structure. The $a$
-loop at vertex $3$ is unique, so $\sigma(3)=3$ and $\sigma$ permutes $\{1,2\}$.
 But the $b$-edges form the directed cycle $1\to 2\to 3\to 1$; since $\sigma(3)=
3$, the $b$-predecessor of $3$ (which is $2$) must map to the $b$-predecessor of
 $\sigma(3)=3$, i.e. $\sigma(2)=2$, forcing $\sigma(1)=1$. Thus the only deck tr
ansformation is the identity. The deck group does **not** act transitively on th
e 3-point fiber, so the covering is non-normal.

**Argument 2 (explicit conjugation witness).** We exhibit $g\in F_2$ and $h\in H
$ with $ghg^{-1}\notin H$, proving $H$ is not normal.

Lift a few words from vertex $1$:
- $ba$: $1 \xrightarrow{b} 2 \xrightarrow{a} 1$, ends at $1$. So $ba\in H$.
- $a$: $1 \xrightarrow{a} 2$, ends at $2\neq 1$. So $a\notin H$.

Now conjugate: take $g = a$ and $h = ba\in H$. Then
$$g h g^{-1} = a\,(ba)\,a^{-1} = aba^{-1}.$$
Lift $aba^{-1}$ from vertex $1$:
$$1 \xrightarrow{a} 2 \xrightarrow{b} 3 \xrightarrow{a^{-1}} 3 \quad(\text{since
 } a \text{ loops at } 3).$$
It ends at vertex $3\neq 1$, so $aba^{-1}\notin H$.

Therefore $aHa^{-1}\neq H$, i.e. $H$ is **not** a normal subgroup of $F_2$. The
corresponding covering $p:\tilde X\to X$ is consequently non-normal. $\blacksqua
