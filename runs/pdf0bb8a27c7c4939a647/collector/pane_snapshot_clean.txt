**Proof.** Since $A^{k}=0$ with $k>1$ and $A\neq 0$, let $m$ ($2\le m\le k$) be
the nilpotency index of $A$, i.e. $A^{m}=0$ and $A^{m-1}\neq 0$. Then
$$\{0\}\neq \operatorname{range}(A^{m-1})\subseteq \operatorname{ker}(A)\cap \op
eratorname{range}(A),$$
so in particular both $\operatorname{ker}(A)$ and $\operatorname{range}(A)$ are
nonzero subspaces.

Pick a nonzero vector $v\in \operatorname{range}(A)$, so $v=Ax_{0}$ for some $x_
{0}\in X$, and pick a nonzero vector $w\in \operatorname{ker}(A)$. By the Hahn–B
anach theorem there exists a bounded linear functional $f\in X^{*}$ with $f(v)=1

$$B:X\to X,\qquad B(x)=f(x)\,w.$$
Then $B$ is a bounded linear operator, and its range satisfies
$$\operatorname{range}(B)\subseteq \operatorname{span}\{w\}\subseteq \operatorna

$$BA(x_{0})=B(Ax_{0})=B(v)=f(v)\,w=w\neq 0,$$
hence $BA\neq 0$.

Thus $B$ satisfies $AB=0$ but $BA\neq 0$. $\blacksquare$

exists. The meaningful reading of "$A^{k}=0$ for some $k>1$" — i.e. $A$ a nonzer
