**Solution.**

Let \(P(x,y)\) denote the given equation
\[
f(x f(y))=f(xy^{2})-2x^{2}f(y)-f(x)-1.
\]

**Step 1. Value at 0.**
Set \(x=0\):
\[
f(0)=f(0)-0-f(0)-1=-1.
\]
Hence
\[
f(0)=-1. \tag{0}
\]

**Step 2. Relating \(f(-x)\) and \(f(x)\).**
Set \(y=0\) and use \(f(0)=-1\):
\[
f(-x)=f(0)-2x^{2}f(0)-f(x)-1=-1+2x^{2}-f(x)-1.
\]
Thus
\[
f(-x)=2x^{2}-f(x)-2. \tag{1}
\]

**Step 3. A quadratic identity for \(f\).**
Set \(x=1\):
\[
f(f(y))=f(y^{2})-2f(y)-f(1)-1. \tag{2}
\]
Set \(x=-1\):
\[
f(-f(y))=f(y^{2})-2f(y)-f(-1)-1. \tag{3}
\]
From (1) with \(x=1\), we have \(f(-1)=-f(1)\), so (3) becomes
\[
f(-f(y))=f(y^{2})-2f(y)+f(1)-1. \tag{3'}
\]
On the other hand, applying (1) with \(x=f(y)\) gives
\[
f(-f(y))=2f(y)^{2}-f(f(y))-2.
\]
Substituting (2) into this:
\[
f(-f(y))=2f(y)^{2}-f(y^{2})+2f(y)+f(1)-1. \tag{4}
\]
Comparing (3') and (4):
\[
f(y^{2})-2f(y)+f(1)-1=2f(y)^{2}-f(y^{2})+2f(y)+f(1)-1,
\]
so
\[
2f(y^{2})=2f(y)^{2}+4f(y),
\]
i.e.
\[
f(y^{2})=f(y)^{2}+2f(y). \tag{5}
\]

**Step 4. Determining \(f\).**
Apply (5) to \(y\) and to \(-y\):
\[
f(y^{2})=f(y)^{2}+2f(y)=f(-y)^{2}+2f(-y).
\]
Hence
\[
\bigl(f(y)-f(-y)\bigr)\bigl(f(y)+f(-y)+2\bigr)=0.
\]
By (1), \(f(y)+f(-y)=2y^{2}-2\), so the second factor equals \(2y^{2}\). Therefo
re
\[
2y^{2}\bigl(f(y)-f(-y)\bigr)=0.
\]
For every \(y\neq 0\), this forces \(f(y)=f(-y)\). Substituting back into (1):
\[
2f(y)=2y^{2}-2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(y)=y^{2}-1 \qquad (y\neq 0).
\]
Together with (0), this also gives \(f(0)=0^{2}-1=-1\). Hence for all \(x\in\mat
hbf R\),
\[
f(x)=x^{2}-1.
\]

**Step 5. Verification.**
Let \(f(x)=x^{2}-1\). Then
\[
f(xf(y))=(x(y^{2}-1))^{2}-1=x^{2}(y^{2}-1)^{2}-1,
\]
while
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(xy^{2})-2x^{2}f(y)-f(x)-1
&=(x^{2}y^{4}-1)-2x^{2}(y^{2}-1)-(x^{2}-1)-1\\
&=x^{2}y^{4}-2x^{2}y^{2}+x^{2}-1\\
&=x^{2}(y^{2}-1)^{2}-1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Both sides agree, so \(f(x)=x^{2}-1\) satisfies the equation.

Therefore the unique solution is
\[
\boxed{f(x)=x^{2}-1}.
\]
