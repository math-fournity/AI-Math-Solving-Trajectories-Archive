## Solution

We evaluate $I = \displaystyle\int_{D(0,2)}\frac{z^3}{z^5+2z-8}\,dz$ using the r
esidue theorem.

**Step 1: Locate the poles.**

The integrand $f(z)=\dfrac{z^3}{z^5+2z-8}$ has poles at the roots of $p(z)=z^5+2
z-8=0$.

**Step 2: All five roots lie inside $|z|<2$ (Rouché's theorem).**

On the circle $|z|=2$:
$$|z^5|=32,\qquad |2z-8|\le |2z|+|8|=4+8=12<32.$$
By Rouché's theorem, $p(z)=z^5+(2z-8)$ has the same number of zeros inside $|z|<
2$ as $z^5$, namely **5** (counting multiplicity). Since $p$ has degree 5, all r
oots lie inside $|z|<2$.

**No roots on the boundary:** If $|z|=2$ and $p(z)=0$, then $z^5=8-2z$, giving $
32=|z^5|=|8-2z|\le 12$, a contradiction. So the contour is free of singularities
.

**Step 3: Compute the sum of residues via the residue at infinity.**

Since $\deg(\text{numerator})=3$ and $\deg(\text{denominator})=5$, we have $f(z)
\sim \dfrac{1}{z^2}$ as $z\to\infty$. The Laurent expansion of $f$ at infinity h
as no $\frac{1}{z}$ term, so
$$\operatorname{Res}(f,\infty)=0.$$

The sum of all residues (including infinity) on the Riemann sphere is zero:
$$\sum_{k=1}^{5}\operatorname{Res}(f,z_k)+\operatorname{Res}(f,\infty)=0 \implie
s \sum_{k=1}^{5}\operatorname{Res}(f,z_k)=0.$$

**Step 4: Apply the residue theorem.**

All five poles lie inside $D(0,2)$, so
$$I = 2\pi i\sum_{k=1}^{5}\operatorname{Res}(f,z_k)=2\pi i\cdot 0=0.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{D(0,2)}\frac{z^3}{z^5+2z-8}\,dz=0}$$
