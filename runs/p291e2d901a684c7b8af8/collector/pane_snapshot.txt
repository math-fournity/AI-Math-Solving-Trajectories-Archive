## Proof

**Claim:** $H$ is dense in $C([0,1],\mathbb{R})$ with respect to the uniform nor
m.

**Step 1: $H$ is a subalgebra of $C([0,1],\mathbb{R})$.**

- *Closed under addition:* If $h_1 = \sum_{j=1}^n a_j e^{b_j x}$ and $h_2 = \sum
_{k=1}^m c_k e^{d_k x}$, then $h_1 + h_2 = \sum_{j=1}^n a_j e^{b_j x} + \sum_{k=
1}^m c_k e^{d_k x}$, which is again a finite linear combination of real exponent
ials, hence in $H$.

- *Closed under scalar multiplication:* For $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$, $\lambda h
 = \sum_{j=1}^n (\lambda a_j) e^{b_j x} \in H$.

- *Closed under multiplication:*
$$h_1 \cdot h_2 = \left(\sum_{j=1}^n a_j e^{b_j x}\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^m c_k
e^{d_k x}\right) = \sum_{j=1}^n \sum_{k=1}^m (a_j c_k)\, e^{(b_j + d_k)x},$$
which is a finite linear combination of exponentials $e^{(b_j+d_k)x}$ with real
coefficients $a_j c_k$ and real exponents $b_j + d_k$. Thus $h_1 h_2 \in H$.

Every element of $H$ is continuous on $[0,1]$, so $H \subseteq C([0,1],\mathbb{R
})$, and $H$ is a subalgebra.

**Step 2: $H$ contains the constant functions.**

For any $c \in \mathbb{R}$, take $n=1$, $a_1 = c$, $b_1 = 0$. Then $h(x) = c \cd
ot e^{0 \cdot x} = c$ for all $x \in [0,1]$. In particular, $H$ contains the con
stant function $1$ (and every constant).

**Step 3: $H$ separates points of $[0,1]$.**

Consider $h(x) = e^x \in H$ (take $n=1$, $a_1=1$, $b_1=1$). For any $x_1 \neq x_
2$ in $[0,1]$, we have $e^{x_1} \neq e^{x_2}$ since the exponential function is
strictly monotone. Hence $H$ separates points.

**Step 4: Apply the Stone–Weierstrass theorem.**

The (real) Stone–Weierstrass theorem states: *Let $X$ be a compact Hausdorff spa
ce. If $A$ is a subalgebra of $C(X,\mathbb{R})$ that contains the constant funct
ions and separates points, then $A$ is dense in $C(X,\mathbb{R})$ with respect t
o the uniform norm.*

Here $X = [0,1]$ is compact Hausdorff, and $H$ is a subalgebra of $C([0,1],\math
bb{R})$ that contains the constants and separates points. Therefore, by the Ston
e–Weierstrass theorem, $H$ is dense in $C([0,1],\mathbb{R})$ with respect to the
 uniform norm. $\blacksquare$
