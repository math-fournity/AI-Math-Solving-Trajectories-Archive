## Solution

By **Stokes' theorem**, $\int_X d\omega = \oint_{\partial X} \omega$.

### Identifying the surface and its boundary

The parametrization $X(r,\theta) = (r\cos\theta,\, r\sin\theta,\, 1-r^2)$ with $
(r,\theta)\in[0,1]\times[0,2\pi]$ describes the paraboloid
$$z = 1 - x^2 - y^2, \quad x^2+y^2 \le 1,$$
oriented so that $r$ increases outward and $\theta$ increases counterclockwise (
viewed from above). The boundary $\partial X$ is the curve at $r=1$:
$$\partial X:\; X(1,\theta) = (\cos\theta,\, \sin\theta,\, 0), \quad \theta\in[0
,2\pi],$$
i.e. the unit circle in the plane $z=0$, traversed counterclockwise.

### Restricting $\omega$ to the boundary

On $\partial X$: $x=\cos\theta$, $y=\sin\theta$, $z=0$, and
$$dx = -\sin\theta\,d\theta,\quad dy = \cos\theta\,d\theta,\quad dz = 0.$$

Substituting into
$$\omega = \bigl[e^{xz}+2y\,e^{x^2+y^2}\bigr]dx + \bigl[\ln(2+z)+3x\,e^{x^2+y^2}
\bigr]dy + 3x^2y\,dz:$$

- $e^{xz} = e^{0} = 1$,
- $e^{x^2+y^2} = e^{1} = e$ (since $x^2+y^2=1$),
- $\ln(2+z) = \ln 2$,
- the $dz$ component vanishes.

Hence
$$\omega\big|_{\partial X} = \bigl[1 + 2e\sin\theta\bigr](-\sin\theta\,d\theta)
+ \bigl[\ln 2 + 3e\cos\theta\bigr](\cos\theta\,d\theta).$$

Expanding:
$$\omega\big|_{\partial X} = \bigl[-\sin\theta - 2e\sin^2\theta + \ln 2\,\cos\th
eta + 3e\cos^2\theta\bigr]d\theta.$$

### Integrating over $[0,2\pi]$

Using the standard identities over a full period:
$$\int_0^{2\pi}\sin\theta\,d\theta = 0,\quad \int_0^{2\pi}\cos\theta\,d\theta =
0,\quad \int_0^{2\pi}\sin^2\theta\,d\theta = \pi,\quad \int_0^{2\pi}\cos^2\theta
\,d\theta = \pi.$$

Therefore
$$\oint_{\partial X}\omega = -2e\cdot\pi + 3e\cdot\pi = \pi e.$$

### Result

$$\boxed{\int_X d\omega = \pi e}$$
