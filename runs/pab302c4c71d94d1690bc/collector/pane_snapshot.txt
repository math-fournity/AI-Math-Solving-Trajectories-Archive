## Proof

**Goal.** Show that $h(z) = f(z) - \sum_{k=1}^n \frac{a_k}{z-z_k}$ admits an ant
iderivative on $U^*$, i.e., there exists an analytic function $H$ on $U^*$ with
$H' = h$.

**Key fact.** A continuous function on a domain has an analytic antiderivative i
f and only if its integral over every closed curve in the domain is zero. Thus i
t suffices to prove:

$$\int_\Gamma h(z)\,dz = 0 \quad \text{for every closed curve } \Gamma \subset U
^*. \tag{$\star$}$$

---

**Step 1. A residue formula for $f$.**

We claim that for every closed curve $\Gamma$ in $U^*$,

$$\int_\Gamma f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i \sum_{k=1}^n a_k\, n(\Gamma, z_k), \tag{1}$$

where $n(\Gamma, z_k)$ is the winding number of $\Gamma$ about $z_k$.

Fix a closed curve $\Gamma \subset U^*$. Since each $\gamma_k$ is a *small* circ
le centered at $z_k \in U$ and $U$ is open, we may assume each $\gamma_k$ (with
its interior) lies in $U$; in particular $n(\gamma_k, w) = 0$ for every $w \noti
n U$. Since $U$ is simply connected and $\Gamma \subset U$, we also have $n(\Gam
ma, w) = 0$ for every $w \notin U$.

Consider the cycle

$$\Gamma' \;=\; \Gamma \;-\; \sum_{k=1}^n n(\Gamma, z_k)\,\gamma_k \quad \text{i
n } U^*.$$

Compute its winding numbers about points outside $U^*$:

- For each puncture $z_k$:
$$n(\Gamma', z_k) = n(\Gamma, z_k) - \sum_j n(\Gamma, z_j)\,n(\gamma_j, z_k) = n
(\Gamma, z_k) - n(\Gamma, z_k)\cdot 1 = 0,$$
since $n(\gamma_j, z_k) = \delta_{jk}$ (the small circles are disjoint and each
winds once about its own center).

- For any $w \notin U$:
$$n(\Gamma', w) = n(\Gamma, w) - \sum_j n(\Gamma, z_j)\,n(\gamma_j, w) = 0 - 0 =
 0.$$

Thus $n(\Gamma', \zeta) = 0$ for **every** $\zeta \notin U^*$, i.e., $\Gamma'$ i
s homologous to zero in $U^*$. Since $f$ is analytic on $U^*$, Cauchy's theorem
(homology form) gives

$$0 = \int_{\Gamma'} f(z)\,dz = \int_\Gamma f(z)\,dz - \sum_{k=1}^n n(\Gamma, z_
k)\int_{\gamma_k} f(z)\,dz.$$

Using $\int_{\gamma_k} f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i\, a_k$ (by the definition of $a_k$), we
 obtain (1). $\square$

---

**Step 2. The integrals of $h$ over closed curves vanish.**

Let $\Gamma$ be any closed curve in $U^*$. Using (1) and the elementary identity
 $\int_\Gamma \frac{dz}{z - z_k} = 2\pi i\, n(\Gamma, z_k)$:

$$\int_\Gamma h(z)\,dz = \int_\Gamma f(z)\,dz - \sum_{k=1}^n a_k \int_\Gamma \fr
ac{dz}{z - z_k} = 2\pi i \sum_{k=1}^n a_k\, n(\Gamma, z_k) \;-\; \sum_{k=1}^n a_
k \cdot 2\pi i\, n(\Gamma, z_k) = 0.$$

This establishes $(\star)$.

---

**Step 3. Construction of $H$.**

Since $\int_\Gamma h(z)\,dz = 0$ for every closed curve $\Gamma$ in $U^*$, the f
unction

$$H(z) \;=\; \int_{z_0}^{z} h(\xi)\,d\xi$$

is well defined (independent of the path in $U^*$ from a fixed base point $z_0 \
in U^*$ to $z$), and satisfies $H' = h$ on $U^*$. Being locally the integral of
an analytic function, $H$ is analytic on $U^*$.

---

Therefore there exists an analytic function $H$ on $U^*$ such that $H' = h$. $\b
lacksquare$
