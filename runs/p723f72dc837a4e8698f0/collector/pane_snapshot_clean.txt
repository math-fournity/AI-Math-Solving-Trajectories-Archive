## Proof

**Setup.** For a set $\{x_1 > x_2 > \cdots > x_k > 0\}$ with $\sum_{\text{odd }
i} x_i = n$, introduce the *gap variables*:
$$d_i = x_i - x_{i+1} \quad (i < k), \qquad d_k = x_k,$$
so $d_i \geq 1$ and $x_j = \sum_{i=j}^{k} d_i$. The odd-indexed sum becomes:
$$n = \sum_{\substack{j \text{ odd}}} x_j = \sum_{\substack{j \text{ odd}}} \sum
_{i=j}^{k} d_i = \sum_{i=1}^{k} d_i \cdot \bigl|\{j \le i : j \text{ odd}\}\bigr
| = \sum_{i=1}^{k} d_i\, c_i,$$
where $c_i = \lceil i/2 \rceil$, i.e., $c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4,\dots = 1,1,2,2,3,3,\dot
s$

**Generating function.** Let $P(q) = \sum_{n \ge 0} p(n)\, q^n$. For fixed $k$,
since $d_i \ge 1$ contributes $c_i d_i$ to the sum:
$$\sum_{\substack{d_1,\dots,d_k \ge 1}} q^{\sum c_i d_i} = \prod_{i=1}^{k} \frac
{q^{c_i}}{1 - q^{c_i}}.$$
Summing over all $k \ge 0$ (with $k=0$ giving the empty set, contributing $1$):
$$P(q) = 1 + \sum_{k \ge 1} \prod_{i=1}^{k} \frac{q^{c_i}}{1 - q^{c_i}}.$$

Since $c_{2j-1} = c_{2j} = j$, the product for $k = 2m$ is $\prod_{j=1}^{m} \fra
c{q^{2j}}{(1-q^j)^2}$, and for $k = 2m+1$ it is that times $\frac{q^{m+1}}{1-q^{
m+1}}$. Setting $A_0 = 1$ and $A_m = \prod_{j=1}^{m} \frac{q^{2j}}{(1-q^j)^2}$:
$$P(q) = \underbrace{1 + \sum_{m \ge 1} A_m}_{\text{even } k} + \underbrace{\sum
_{m \ge 0} A_m \cdot \frac{q^{m+1}}{1-q^{m+1}}}_{\text{odd } k} = \sum_{m \ge 0}
 A_m \left(1 + \frac{q^{m+1}}{1-q^{m+1}}\right) = \sum_{m \ge 0} \frac{A_m}{1 -
q^{m+1}}.$$

**Key simplification.** Using $\frac{1}{1-q^{m+1}} = 1 + \frac{q^{m+1}}{1-q^{m+1
}}$ and the relation $A_{m+1} = A_m \cdot \frac{q^{2(m+1)}}{(1-q^{m+1})^2}$, whi
ch gives $A_m \cdot \frac{q^{m+1}}{1-q^{m+1}} = A_{m+1} \cdot \frac{1-q^{m+1}}{q
^{m+1}}$:

$$P(q) = \sum_{m \ge 0} A_m + \sum_{m \ge 1} A_m \cdot \frac{1-q^m}{q^m} = 1 + \
sum_{m \ge 1} A_m \cdot \frac{1}{q^m} = \sum_{m \ge 0} \frac{A_m}{q^m}.$$

Computing the exponent: $\frac{A_m}{q^m} = q^{-m} \prod_{j=1}^m \frac{q^{2j}}{(1
-q^j)^2} = q^{\,2\sum_{j=1}^m j \;-\; m} \prod_{j=1}^m \frac{1}{(1-q^j)^2} = q^{
m(m+1)-m} \prod_{j=1}^m \frac{1}{(1-q^j)^2} = q^{m^2} \prod_{j=1}^m \frac{1}{(1-
q^j)^2}.$

Therefore:
$$\boxed{P(q) = \sum_{m \ge 0} q^{m^2} \prod_{j=1}^{m} \frac{1}{(1-q^j)^2}.}$$

**Durfee square decomposition.** Every partition $\lambda = (\lambda_1 \ge \lamb
da_2 \ge \cdots)$ of $n$ has a unique **Durfee square** of size $m$: the largest
 $m$ with $\lambda_m \ge m$. The partition decomposes into three parts:

1. **The $m \times m$ square** (size $m^2$).
2. **To the right**: $\alpha = (\lambda_1 - m, \dots, \lambda_m - m)$, a partiti
on into at most $m$ parts.
3. **Below**: $\beta = (\lambda_{m+1}, \lambda_{m+2}, \dots)$, a partition with
largest part $\le m$ (since $\lambda_{m+1} \le m$ by maximality of $m$).

The generating function for partitions into at most $m$ parts is $\prod_{j=1}^{m
} \frac{1}{1-q^j}$ (equivalently, partitions with largest part $\le m$, by conju
gation). Thus the generating function for partitions with Durfee square of size
$m$ is:
$$q^{m^2} \cdot \prod_{j=1}^{m} \frac{1}{1-q^j} \cdot \prod_{j=1}^{m} \frac{1}{1
-q^j} = q^{m^2} \prod_{j=1}^{m} \frac{1}{(1-q^j)^2}.$$

Summing over all $m \ge 0$ (uniqueness of the Durfee square ensures each partiti
on is counted exactly once):
$$\prod_{j=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{1-q^j} = \sum_{m \ge 0} q^{m^2} \prod_{j=1}^{m}
\frac{1}{(1-q^j)^2} = P(q).$$

**Conclusion.** Since $P(q) = \sum_{n \ge 0} p(n)\, q^n = \prod_{j=1}^{\infty} \
frac{1}{1-q^j} = \sum_{n \ge 0} \mathrm{par}(n)\, q^n$, comparing coefficients g
ives $p(n) = \mathrm{par}(n)$ for every $n \ge 0$, and in particular for every p
ositive integer $n$. $\blacksquare$
