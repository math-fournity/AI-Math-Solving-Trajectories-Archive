Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether restricting a Cartesian dia
gram to preimages of a curve preserves the Cartesian property.

**Answer: Yes, the restricted diagram is Cartesian.**

**Proof.**

Since the original diagram is Cartesian, we have $A \cong B \times_D C$, with $\
alpha$ and $\beta$ the two projections.

**Step 1: Compute $W$ as a fiber product.**

By definition, $W = \beta^{-1}(Y)$ is the scheme-theoretic preimage, i.e., $W =
A \times_C Y$. Using $A \cong B \times_D C$ and the associativity of fiber produ
cts:
$$W = A \times_C Y \cong (B \times_D C) \times_C Y \cong B \times_D (C \times_C
Y) \cong B \times_D Y,$$
where the map $Y \to D$ in the fiber product $B \times_D Y$ is the composition $
Y \hookrightarrow C \xrightarrow{\delta} D$.

**Step 2: Verify that $Y \to D$ factors through $Z$.**

Since $\delta$ is finite (hence closed) and $Y$ is irreducible, $Z = \delta(Y)$
is an irreducible closed subset of $D$. Equip $Z$ with the reduced induced close
d subscheme structure. The map $\delta|_Y : Y \to D$ has set-theoretic image $Z$
. To factor scheme-theoretically through $Z \hookrightarrow D$, we need the como
rphism $\mathcal{O}_D \to \mathcal{O}_Y$ to annihilate the ideal sheaf $I_Z$ of
$Z$ in $D$.

The ideal $I_Z$ is the radical of the ideal $I_{\mathrm{sch}}$ of the scheme-the
oretic image of $Y \to D$. By definition, $\mathcal{O}_D \to \mathcal{O}_Y$ kill
s $I_{\mathrm{sch}}$. If $f \in I_Z = \sqrt{I_{\mathrm{sch}}}$, then $f^n \in I_
{\mathrm{sch}}$ for some $n \geq 1$, so $f^n$ maps to $0$ in $\mathcal{O}_Y$, ma
king the image of $f$ nilpotent in $\mathcal{O}_Y$. Since $Y$ is nonsingular, $\
mathcal{O}_Y$ is reduced, so $f$ maps to $0$. Thus $Y \to D$ factors uniquely as
 $Y \xrightarrow{\delta|_Y} Z \hookrightarrow D$.

**Step 3: Compute $X \times_Z Y$ as a fiber product.**

By definition, $X = \gamma^{-1}(Z) = B \times_D Z$. Using associativity of fiber
 products with the factorization $Y \to Z \to D$ from Step 2:
$$X \times_Z Y = (B \times_D Z) \times_Z Y \cong B \times_D (Z \times_Z Y) \cong
 B \times_D Y,$$
where the map $Y \to D$ is $Y \xrightarrow{\delta|_Y} Z \hookrightarrow D$, whic
h is exactly $Y \hookrightarrow C \xrightarrow{\delta} D$ — the same map as in S
tep 1.

**Step 4: Conclude.**

From Steps 1 and 3:
$$W \cong B \times_D Y \cong X \times_Z Y.$$

These isomorphisms are induced by the universal property of fiber products and a
re compatible with the projection maps: the map $W \to X$ corresponds to the pro
jection $B \times_D Y \to B \times_D Z$ (induced by $Y \to Z$), which is the res
triction of $\alpha$; the map $W \to Y$ is the projection $B \times_D Y \to Y$,
which is the restriction of $\beta$; and the maps $X \to Z$ and $Y \to Z$ are th
e restrictions of $\gamma$ and $\delta$ respectively. Therefore the restricted d
iagram

$$\begin{array}{ccc} W & \to^\alpha & X \\ \downarrow^\beta & & \downarrow^\gamm
a \\ Y & \to^\delta & Z \end{array}$$

is Cartesian. $\blacksquare$

ity of fiber products and the fact that $Y \to D$ factors through $Z$. The hypot

$Y$ is irreducible and nonsingular ensure that all objects are well-defined (fin
ite maps send curves to curves) and that the factorization through the reduced i
mage $Z$ holds (reducedness of $Y$ is the key property, guaranteed by nonsingula
