# Proof

**Definitions.** A Tychonoff space $X$ is *strongly zero-dimensional* if every f
inite functionally open cover of $X$ admits a finite disjoint open refinement th
at covers $X$. A Tychonoff space $X$ is *almost strongly zero-dimensional* if ev
ery finite functionally open cover of $X$ admits a finite disjoint open refineme
nt whose union is dense in $X$.

**Claim.** The closed unit interval $[0,1]$ is a metrizable, separable, Tychonof
f space that is almost strongly zero-dimensional but not strongly zero-dimension
al.

---

**$[0,1]$ is metrizable, separable, and Tychonoff.** With the Euclidean metric,
$[0,1]$ is a compact metric space. The set $\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]$ is countable an
d dense, so $[0,1]$ is separable. Every compact Hausdorff space is normal, hence
 Tychonoff.

---

**$[0,1]$ is almost strongly zero-dimensional.**

Let $\{U_1,\dots,U_n\}$ be a finite functionally open cover of $[0,1]$. Since fu
nctionally open sets are open, this is a finite open cover. Since $[0,1]$ is com
pact, the Lebesgue number lemma furnishes $\delta>0$ such that every subset of $
[0,1]$ of diameter $<\delta$ lies inside some $U_i$.

Pick $k\in\mathbb{N}$ with $1/k<\delta$ and set $t_j=j/k$ for $j=0,1,\dots,k$. E
ach closed interval $[t_{j-1},t_j]$ has diameter $1/k<\delta$, so there exists $
i(j)\in\{1,\dots,n\}$ with $[t_{j-1},t_j]\subseteq U_{i(j)}$.

Define subsets of $[0,1]$:
- $V_1=[0,\,t_1)$,
- $V_j=(t_{j-1},\,t_j)$ for $2\le j\le k-1$,
- $V_k=(t_{k-1},\,1]$.

Each $V_j$ is open in the subspace topology of $[0,1]$. Since $V_j\subseteq[t_{j
-1},t_j]\subseteq U_{i(j)}$, the family $\{V_1,\dots,V_k\}$ refines $\{U_1,\dots
,U_n\}$. The $V_j$ are pairwise disjoint (they are separated by the cut points $
t_1,\dots,t_{k-1}$). Their union is

$$\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}V_j=[0,1]\setminus\{t_1,t_2,\dots,t_{k-1}\}=[0,1]\setminus\{1
/k,\,2/k,\,\dots,\,(k-1)/k\},$$

which is $[0,1]$ with finitely many points removed, hence dense in $[0,1]$.

Thus every finite functionally open cover of $[0,1]$ admits a finite disjoint op
en refinement with dense union. Therefore $[0,1]$ is almost strongly zero-dimens
ional. $\checkmark$

---

**$[0,1]$ is not strongly zero-dimensional.**

Consider the functionally open cover $\mathcal{U}=\bigl\{[0,\,2/3),\;(1/3,\,1]\b
igr\}$ of $[0,1]$. (Both members are functionally open: $[0,2/3)=f^{-1}((-\infty
,2/3))$ and $(1/3,1]=f^{-1}((1/3,\infty))$ for $f(x)=x$.)

Suppose, for contradiction, that $[0,1]$ is strongly zero-dimensional. Then $\ma
thcal{U}$ admits a finite disjoint open refinement $\{W_1,\dots,W_m\}$ that **co
vers** $[0,1]$. Since $[0,1]$ is connected, the only representation of $[0,1]$ a
s a disjoint union of open sets is the trivial one: $m=1$ and $W_1=[0,1]$. But $
[0,1]\not\subseteq[0,2/3)$ and $[0,1]\not\subseteq(1/3,1]$, so $W_1$ is containe
d in no member of $\mathcal{U}$—contradicting the requirement that $\{W_1\}$ ref
ine $\mathcal{U}$.

Therefore $[0,1]$ is not strongly zero-dimensional. $\checkmark$

---

**Conclusion.** The space $[0,1]$ is metrizable, separable, and Tychonoff; it is
 almost strongly zero-dimensional (by the Lebesgue-number argument above) but no
t strongly zero-dimensional (by connectedness). Hence the answer to the question
 is **yes**: such a space exists.
