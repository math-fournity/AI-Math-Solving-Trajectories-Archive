Simplify the denominator:
$$\log_2 e^{n/11} = \frac{n}{11}\log_2 e = \frac{n}{11\ln 2}, \qquad \text{so }
\frac{1}{\log_2 e^{n/11}} = \frac{11\ln 2}{n}.$$

Substitute $j = k-9$, so $j$ runs from $1$ to $n$:
$$S_1 = \frac{11\ln 2}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n} e^{\frac{11j\ln 2}{n}} = \sum_{j=1}^{n}
\left(\ln 2 \cdot 2^{t_j}\right)\Delta t, \quad t_j = \frac{11j}{n},\ \Delta t =
 \frac{11}{n}.$$

nfty$:
$$S_1 \to \int_0^{11} \ln 2 \cdot 2^t\,dt = \ln 2\cdot\frac{2^t}{\ln 2}\bigg|_0^

**Second sum.** Let $S_2 = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \frac{58}{\pi\sqrt{(n-k)(n+k)}}$.

Since $\sqrt{(n-k)(n+k)} = \sqrt{n^2-k^2} = n\sqrt{1-(k/n)^2}$:
$$S_2 = \frac{58}{\pi n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-(k/n)^2}}.$$

This is a left Riemann sum for $g(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$ on $[0,1]$ with $
x_k = k/n$, $\Delta x = 1/n$. Although $g$ has an integrable singularity at $x=1

$$S_2 \to \frac{58}{\pi}\int_0^1 \frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} = \frac{58}{\pi}\cdot\f
rac{\pi}{2} = 29.$$

**Combining:**
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}(S_1 - S_2) = 2047 - 29 = \boxed{2018}.$$
