**Answer: Yes, $F/K$ always admits such a generator.**

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $p$ be a prime with $4 \mid (p-1)$, $K = \mathbb{Q}_p$, and $F/K$
 the unramified extension of degree 4. We recall that up to $K$-isomorphism, the
re is a **unique** unramified extension of $K$ of each degree. So it suffices to
 exhibit *some* integer $a$ such that $K(\sqrt[4]{a})/K$ is unramified of degree
 4.

**Step 1: Choosing $a$.** Since $p \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$, the group $\mathbb{F}_p^*
$ is cyclic of order $p - 1$ with $4 \mid (p-1)$. Let $g$ be a primitive root mo
dulo $p$ (an integer with $1 \le g \le p-1$). Set $a = g \in \mathbb{Z}$.

**Step 2: $x^4 - \bar{g}$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_p$.** The subgroup of
4th powers $(\mathbb{F}_p^*)^4 = \langle \bar{g}^4 \rangle$ has index 4 in $\mat
hbb{F}_p^* = \langle \bar{g} \rangle$, so $\mathbb{F}_p^*/(\mathbb{F}_p^*)^4 \co
ng \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$, and $\bar{g}$ has **order 4** in this quotient (sinc
e $\bar{g}^j \in (\mathbb{F}_p^*)^4 \iff 4 \mid j$).

Since $4 \mid (p-1)$, $\mathbb{F}_p$ contains a primitive 4th root of unity $\ze
ta_4 = \bar{g}^{(p-1)/4}$. Let $\alpha = \sqrt[4]{\bar{g}}$ in $\overline{\mathb
b{F}_p}$. Under Frobenius:
$$\alpha^p = \alpha \cdot \alpha^{p-1} = \alpha \cdot \bar{g}^{(p-1)/4} = \alpha
\,\zeta_4.$$
Iterating (using $\zeta_4^p = \zeta_4$ since $\zeta_4 \in \mathbb{F}_p$):
$$\alpha^{p^2} = \alpha\,\zeta_4^2, \quad \alpha^{p^3} = \alpha\,\zeta_4^3, \qua
d \alpha^{p^4} = \alpha\,\zeta_4^4 = \alpha.$$
The orbit $\{\alpha, \alpha\zeta_4, \alpha\zeta_4^2, \alpha\zeta_4^3\}$ has 4 di
stinct elements, so $[\mathbb{F}_p(\alpha) : \mathbb{F}_p] = 4$. Since $\alpha$
is a root of $x^4 - \bar{g}$ (degree 4), this polynomial is **irreducible** over
 $\mathbb{F}_p$.

**Step 3: $x^4 - g$ is irreducible over $K$.** Since $g$ is a primitive root mod
 $p$, we have $p \nmid g$, so $g \in \mathbb{Z}_p^*$. The polynomial $f(x) = x^4
 - g \in \mathbb{Z}_p[x]$ is monic, and its reduction $\bar{f}(x) = x^4 - \bar{g
}$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_p$ by Step 2. By the **Hensel irreducibility
criterion** (any factorization $f = gh$ in $\mathbb{Z}_p[x]$ with monic factors
would yield $\bar{f} = \bar{g}\bar{h}$, contradicting irreducibility of $\bar{f}
$), $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}_p$.

**Step 4: $K(\sqrt[4]{g})/K$ is unramified of degree 4.** Let $\beta = \sqrt[4]{
g}$, so $[K(\beta) : K] = 4$. The residue field of $K(\beta)$ contains $\mathbb{
F}_p(\bar{\beta})$, where $\bar{\beta} = \alpha = \sqrt[4]{\bar{g}}$, so the res
idue degree $f \geq [\mathbb{F}_p(\alpha) : \mathbb{F}_p] = 4$. Since $f \leq [K
(\beta):K] = 4$ and $ef = 4$ (where $e$ is the ramification index), we conclude
$f = 4$ and $e = 1$. Thus $K(\sqrt[4]{g})/K$ is **unramified** of degree 4.

**Step 5: Conclusion.** By the uniqueness of unramified extensions, $F \cong K(\
sqrt[4]{g})$ as extensions of $K$. Since $g \in \mathbb{Z}$, we have expressed $
F = K(\sqrt[4]{a})$ with $a = g \in \mathbb{Z}$.

**Remark.** The condition $p \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$ is essential: it ensures $\mathb
b{F}_p^*$ contains 4th roots of unity and that $\mathbb{F}_p^*/(\mathbb{F}_p^*)^
4$ has elements of order 4, allowing $x^4 - \bar{a}$ to be irreducible of degree
 4 over $\mathbb{F}_p$.
