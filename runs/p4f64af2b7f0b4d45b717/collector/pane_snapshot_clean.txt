## Proof

**Setup.** Since $f \geq 0$ is continuous with $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx
 = 1$, it is a probability density function. Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots$ be i.i.d. ra
ndom variables with density $f$. Then
$$I_n(r) = P\!\left(X_1^2 + X_2^2 + \cdots + X_n^2 \leq r^2\right).$$

Set $Y_i = X_i^2 \geq 0$ and $S_n = Y_1 + \cdots + Y_n$, so $I_n(r) = P(S_n \leq
 r^2)$.

**Claim:** $\lim_{n\to\infty} I_n(r) = 0$ for every fixed $r \geq 0$.

**Case $r = 0$.** Then $I_n(0) = P(S_n = 0) = P(X_1 = \cdots = X_n = 0) = \prod_
{i=1}^n P(X_i = 0) = 0$, since $X_i$ has a continuous density (so $P(X_i = 0) =
0$). Hence $I_n(0) = 0$ for all $n$.

**Case $r > 0$.** Fix any $M > 0$ and define $Y_i' = \min(Y_i, M)$, which are i.
i.d., bounded, and non-negative. Let $S_n' = \sum_{i=1}^n Y_i'$. Since $Y_i' \le
q Y_i$, we have $S_n' \leq S_n$, so
$$\{S_n \leq r^2\} \subseteq \{S_n' \leq r^2\},$$
hence $I_n(r) = P(S_n \leq r^2) \leq P(S_n' \leq r^2)$.

**Key step: $\mu_M := E[Y_1'] = E[\min(X_1^2, M)] > 0$.**

Since $f$ is continuous, non-negative, and integrates to $1$, $f$ is strictly po
sitive on some open interval $U$. Pick $x_0 \in U$.
- If $x_0 \neq 0$: then $\min(x_0^2, M) > 0$, and by continuity of both $f$ and
$x \mapsto \min(x^2, M)$, there is a neighborhood of $x_0$ where $f > 0$ and $\m
in(x^2, M) > 0$, so $\mu_M > 0$.
- If $x_0 = 0$: then $f > 0$ on some interval $(-\delta, \delta)$. On the subint
erval $(\delta/2, \delta)$, we have $f > 0$ and $\min(x^2, M) \geq (\delta/2)^2
> 0$, so $\mu_M > 0$.

In either case, $\mu_M > 0$.

**Applying the Strong Law of Large Numbers.** The $Y_i'$ are i.i.d. with finite
mean $\mu_M > 0$. By the SLLN,
$$\frac{S_n'}{n} \to \mu_M \quad \text{a.s.}$$
In particular, $P\!\left(\frac{S_n'}{n} < \frac{\mu_M}{2}\right) \to 0$ as $n \t
o \infty$.

Choose $N$ such that $N \mu_M / 2 > r^2$, i.e., $N > 2r^2/\mu_M$. For all $n \ge
q N$:
$$P(S_n' \leq r^2) \leq P\!\left(\frac{S_n'}{n} \leq \frac{r^2}{n}\right) \leq P
\!\left(\frac{S_n'}{n} < \frac{\mu_M}{2}\right) \to 0.$$

Therefore:
$$0 \leq I_n(r) = P(S_n \leq r^2) \leq P(S_n' \leq r^2) \to 0.$$

By the squeeze theorem, $\lim_{n \to \infty} I_n(r) = 0$.

**Conclusion.** For every fixed $r \geq 0$,
$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} I_n(r) = 0.}$$

The intuition is clear: $S_n = \sum X_i^2$ is a sum of non-negative i.i.d. rando
m variables with positive mean (at least when truncated, the mean is positive),
so $S_n$ grows linearly in $n$, while the threshold $r^2$ is fixed. The probabil
ity that $S_n$ stays below a fixed bound vanishes.
