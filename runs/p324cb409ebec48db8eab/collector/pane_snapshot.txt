## Proof

**Answer: Yes, it is always possible.**

**Setup and definitions.** Let $M$ be a smooth manifold of dimension $n$, $c: [0
,1] \to M$ a smooth curve, and $w \in T_{c(1)}M$ a fixed tangent vector. A *vect
or field along $c$* is a smooth map $V: [0,1] \to TM$ such that $V(t) \in T_{c(t
)}M$ for every $t \in [0,1]$ — equivalently, a smooth section of the pullback bu
ndle $c^*TM \to [0,1]$.

**Key fact.** The pullback bundle $c^*TM \to [0,1]$ is a smooth vector bundle of
 rank $n$ over the interval $[0,1]$. Since $[0,1]$ is contractible, every vector
 bundle over it is trivial. Concretely, there exists a smooth bundle isomorphism
 (a *trivialization*)
$$\Phi: c^*TM \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} [0,1] \times \mathbb{R}^n,$$
which restricts on each fiber to a linear isomorphism $\Phi_t: T_{c(t)}M \xright
arrow{\sim} \mathbb{R}^n$ depending smoothly on $t$.

*Brief justification of triviality:* Cover $[0,1]$ by finitely many intervals on
 each of which $c^*TM$ admits a local frame (possible by the local triviality of
 $TM$ and compactness of $[0,1]$). On overlaps, the transition functions are smo
oth maps into $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})$. Since each overlap interval is connec
ted and $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})$ deformation-retracts onto $\mathrm{O}(n)$, o
ne can iteratively modify frames using a partition of unity and Gram–Schmidt to
glue them into $n$ globally defined, linearly independent smooth sections — yiel
ding a global frame and hence a trivialization. (Equivalently, parallel transpor
t via any connection on $TM$ pulled back along $c$ gives an explicit smooth triv
ialization.)

**Construction of $V$.** Under the trivialization, the prescribed vector $w \in
T_{c(1)}M$ corresponds to a vector $v := \Phi_1(w) \in \mathbb{R}^n$. Define the
 smooth map
$$f: [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}^n, \qquad f(t) = t\,v.$$
This satisfies $f(0) = 0$ and $f(1) = v$. Now set
$$V(t) := \Phi_t^{-1}\!\big(f(t)\big) = \Phi_t^{-1}(t\,v) \in T_{c(t)}M.$$

**Verification.**
- *Smoothness:* $V$ is the composition of the smooth map $t \mapsto (t, tv) \in
[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}^n$ with the smooth inverse trivialization $\Phi^{-1}$, hen
ce $V$ is smooth.
- *It is a vector field along $c$:* By construction $V(t) \in T_{c(t)}M$ for all
 $t$.
- *Boundary condition at $0$:* $V(0) = \Phi_0^{-1}(0) = 0 \in T_{c(0)}M$. ✓
- *Boundary condition at $1$:* $V(1) = \Phi_1^{-1}(v) = \Phi_1^{-1}(\Phi_1(w)) =
 w \in T_{c(1)}M$. ✓

**Conclusion.** For any smooth manifold $M$, any smooth curve $c:[0,1]\to M$, an
d any fixed vector $w \in T_{c(1)}M$, the construction above produces a smooth v
ector field $V$ along $c$ with $V(0)=0$ and $V(1)=w$. The essential reason is th
at $c^*TM$ is a vector bundle over the contractible base $[0,1]$ and therefore t
rivial, reducing the problem to choosing a smooth $\mathbb{R}^n$-valued function
 with prescribed endpoints — which is always possible (e.g., linear interpolatio
n $f(t)=tv$).
