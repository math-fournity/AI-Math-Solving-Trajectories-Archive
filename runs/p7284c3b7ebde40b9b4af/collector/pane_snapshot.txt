## Proof

We study the series $\displaystyle S(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x\,e^{-nx}}{
e^x - 1}, \quad x \in (0,\infty).$

**Pointwise convergence.** For each fixed $x > 0$, we have $e^x - 1 > 0$ and $0
< e^{-x} < 1$, so $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} e^{-nx} = \frac{1}{1-e^{-x}}$ converges (
geometric series). Hence

$$S(x) = \frac{x}{e^x-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} e^{-nx} = \frac{x}{e^x-1}\cdot\frac{
1}{1-e^{-x}} = \frac{x\,e^x}{(e^x-1)^2},$$

so the series converges pointwise on all of $(0,\infty)$.

---

**Uniform convergence on $[\delta, +\infty)$ for every $\delta > 0$.**

Let $\delta > 0$. For $x \geq \delta$ and $n \geq 0$,

$$\left|\frac{x\,e^{-nx}}{e^x-1}\right| = \frac{x}{e^x-1}\,e^{-nx} \leq M_\delta
 \cdot e^{-n\delta},$$

where $M_\delta := \sup_{x \geq \delta} \frac{x}{e^x-1} < \infty$ (the function
$x/(e^x-1)$ is continuous on $[\delta,\infty)$ and tends to $0$ as $x\to+\infty$
, so it is bounded). Since $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} M_\delta\, e^{-n\delta} = M_\del
ta \cdot \frac{1}{1-e^{-\delta}} < \infty$, the **Weierstrass M-test** gives uni
form convergence on $[\delta,+\infty)$.

---

**Non-uniform convergence on $(0,+\infty)$.**

The tail of the series is

$$R_N(x) = \sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty} \frac{x\,e^{-nx}}{e^x-1} = \frac{x}{e^x-1}\cdot
\frac{e^{-(N+1)x}}{1-e^{-x}} = \frac{x\,e^{-Nx}}{(e^x-1)^2}.$$

(We used $(e^x-1)(1-e^{-x}) = (e^x-1)^2/e^x$.)

To test uniform convergence on $(0,\infty)$, evaluate at $x_N = 1/N$:

$$R_N\!\left(\tfrac{1}{N}\right) = \frac{\tfrac{1}{N}\,e^{-1}}{\bigl(e^{1/N}-1\b
igr)^2}.$$

Since $e^{1/N}-1 \sim \frac{1}{N}$ as $N\to\infty$,

$$R_N\!\left(\tfrac{1}{N}\right) \sim \frac{\tfrac{1}{N}\,e^{-1}}{\tfrac{1}{N^2}
} = \frac{N}{e} \xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{} +\infty.$$

Therefore

$$\sup_{x\in(0,\infty)} |R_N(x)| \geq R_N\!\left(\tfrac{1}{N}\right) \to +\infty
 \neq 0,$$

so the series does **not** converge uniformly on $(0,+\infty)$. The same argumen
t (taking $x_N = 1/N \to 0$) shows non-uniform convergence on any interval of th
e form $(0, a]$ with $a > 0$.

---

**Conclusion.** The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x\,e^{-nx}}{e^
x-1}$ converges uniformly on $[\delta,+\infty)$ for every $\delta>0$, but does n
ot converge uniformly on $(0,+\infty)$ (nor on any interval with $0$ as an endpo
int). The interval of uniform convergence is

$$\boxed{[\delta,\,+\infty) \text{ for every } \delta > 0.}$$
