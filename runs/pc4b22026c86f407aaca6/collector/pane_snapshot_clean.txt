## Proof

We are given that $a^n = a$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, with $a \neq 0$ and $a \
neq 1$.

**Step 1: The condition reduces to idempotence.**

Taking $n = 2$ gives $a^2 = a$. Conversely, if $a^2 = a$, then by induction $a^n
 = a$ for every $n \geq 1$: assuming $a^k = a$, we have $a^{k+1} = a^k \cdot a =
 a \cdot a = a^2 = a$. Hence the full hypothesis is equivalent to $a^2 = a$, i.e
., $a$ is idempotent. In particular $a(a - 1) = 0$.

**Step 2: The spectrum is contained in $\{0, 1\}$.**

The spectral mapping theorem for polynomials gives
$$\sigma(p(a)) = p(\sigma(a))$$
for any polynomial $p$. Applying this to $p(t) = t^2 - t$ and using $a^2 - a = 0
$ (whose spectrum is $\{0\}$):
$$p(\sigma(a)) = \sigma(0) = \{0\}.$$
Thus for every $\lambda \in \sigma(a)$, $\lambda^2 - \lambda = 0$, so $\lambda \
in \{0, 1\}$.

**Step 3: Both $0$ and $1$ belong to the spectrum.**

*$0 \in \sigma(a)$:* Suppose for contradiction that $a$ is invertible. Then from
 $a(a - 1) = 0$, multiplying on the left by $a^{-1}$ yields $a - 1 = 0$, i.e., $
a = 1$, contradicting the hypothesis $a \neq 1$. Hence $a$ is not invertible, so
 $0 \in \sigma(a)$.

*$1 \in \sigma(a)$:* Suppose for contradiction that $a - 1$ is invertible (equiv
alently, $1 \in \rho(a)$). Then from $a(a - 1) = 0$, multiplying on the right by
 $(a-1)^{-1}$ yields $a = 0$, contradicting the hypothesis $a \neq 0$. Hence $a
- 1$ is not invertible, so $1 \in \sigma(a)$.

**Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 2 and 3,
$$\boxed{\sigma(a) = \{0, 1\}}.$$
