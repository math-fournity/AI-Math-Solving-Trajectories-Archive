## Proof

**Model.** Identify each cell $(i,j)$ of the $n\times n$ table with the vector
$$v_{ij} \;=\; e_i + f_j \;\in\; \mathbb{F}_2^{\,2n},$$
where $e_1,\dots,e_n,f_1,\dots,f_n$ is the standard basis of $\mathbb{F}_2^{2n}$
.

A subset $T$ of cells is **even** (every row and every column contains an even n
umber of elements of $T$) if and only if
$$\sum_{(i,j)\in T} v_{ij} \;=\; \mathbf{0} \quad\text{in } \mathbb{F}_2^{2n},$$
because the $i$-th coordinate of the sum counts (mod 2) how many elements of $T$
 lie in row $i$, and similarly the $(n+j)$-th coordinate counts column $j$.

Therefore a subset $S$ of cells **contains a non-empty even subset** if and only
 if the vectors $\{v_{ij}:(i,j)\in S\}$ are **linearly dependent** over $\mathbb
{F}_2$.

So the problem asks for the smallest $k$ such that every $k$-element subset of c
ells is linearly dependent, i.e. $k = \operatorname{rank}\{v_{ij}\} + 1$.

---

**Rank computation.** Consider the span of all $n^2$ vectors $v_{ij}=e_i+f_j$.

- For any $i\neq k$: $\;v_{ij}+v_{kj} = e_i+e_k$, so all vectors $e_i+e_k$ lie i
n the span.
- For any $j\neq \ell$: $\;v_{ij}+v_{i\ell} = f_j+f_\ell$, so all vectors $f_j+f
_\ell$ lie in the span.

The vectors $\{e_i+e_k\}$ span the $(n{-}1)$-dimensional subspace $R=\{x\in\math
bb{F}_2^n:\sum x_i=0\}$, and $\{f_j+f_\ell\}$ span the $(n{-}1)$-dimensional sub
space $C=\{y\in\mathbb{F}_2^n:\sum y_j=0\}$. Hence the span contains $R\times C$
, which has dimension $2(n{-}1)=2n{-}2$.

Now $v_{11}=e_1+f_1$ has $\sum x_i = 1$ and $\sum y_j = 1$, so $v_{11}\notin R\t
imes C$. Thus the full span is
$$R\times C \;+\; \langle e_1+f_1\rangle,$$
which has dimension $(2n{-}2)+1 = 2n{-}1$. (Equivalently, the span is the hyperp
lane $\{(x,y): \sum x_i = \sum y_j\}$ in $\mathbb{F}_2^{2n}$.)

Hence $\operatorname{rank}\{v_{ij}:1\le i,j\le n\} = 2n-1$.

---

**Lower bound ($k\ge 2n$).** Since the rank is $2n{-}1$, there exist $2n{-}1$ li
nearly independent vectors among the cells. The corresponding $2n{-}1$ cells for
m a subset with **no** non-empty even subset (independence means no non-trivial
subset sums to $\mathbf{0}$). So $k>2n{-}1$, i.e. $k\ge 2n$.

**Upper bound ($k\le 2n$).** Any $2n$ cells give $2n$ vectors lying in a space o
f dimension $2n{-}1$, so they are linearly dependent. The dependence yields a no
n-empty sub-subset summing to $\mathbf{0}$, i.e. a non-empty even subset. So $k\
le 2n$.

---

**Conclusion.** The minimum $k$ is

$$\boxed{k = 2n}.$$
