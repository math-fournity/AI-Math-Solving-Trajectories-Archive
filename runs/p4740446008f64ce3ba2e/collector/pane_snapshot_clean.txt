## Proof

Let $S$ be a regular oriented surface with the stated property: there exists a c
onstant $c \in \mathbb{R}$ such that for every simple, closed, positively orient
ed curve $C$ in $S$,
$$\int_C k_g\, ds = c.$$

**Applying the Gauss–Bonnet theorem.** For any simple, closed, positively orient
ed curve $C$ bounding a region $R \subset S$, the local Gauss–Bonnet theorem giv
es
$$\int_C k_g\, ds + \iint_R K\, dA = 2\pi,$$
where $K$ is the Gaussian curvature of $S$. (The winding number is $1$ for a sim
ple positively oriented curve, contributing $2\pi$.)

Rearranging,
$$\iint_R K\, dA = 2\pi - \int_C k_g\, ds = 2\pi - c.$$

Since the right-hand side is independent of $C$, we conclude:

> $\iint_R K\, dA$ is the **same constant** for every region $R$ bounded by a si
mple closed positively oriented curve.

**Determining the constant.** Fix any point $p \in S$ and take a family of such
curves $C_\varepsilon$ bounding regions $R_\varepsilon$ that shrink to $\{p\}$ a
s $\varepsilon \to 0$ (e.g., small geodesic circles of radius $\varepsilon$). Si
nce $K$ is continuous and bounded on the compact closure of each small region,
$$\left|\iint_{R_\varepsilon} K\, dA\right| \le \sup_{R_\varepsilon}|K|\cdot \op
eratorname{Area}(R_\varepsilon) \xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0} 0.$$

But $\iint_{R_\varepsilon} K\, dA$ equals the constant $2\pi - c$ for every $\va
repsilon > 0$. Taking the limit,
$$2\pi - c = 0, \qquad\text{i.e.,}\quad c = 2\pi.$$

Hence
$$\iint_R K\, dA = 0 \quad \text{for every such region } R.$$

**Concluding $K \equiv 0$.** Suppose for contradiction that $K(p_0) \neq 0$ for
some $p_0 \in S$. By continuity, $K$ keeps a fixed sign in a neighborhood $U$ of
 $p_0$, and $|K| > 0$ on some smaller neighborhood. Choose a simple closed posit
ively oriented curve $C$ bounding a region $R \subset U$ on which $K$ is, say, s
trictly positive. Then
$$\iint_R K\, dA > 0,$$
contradicting $\iint_R K\, dA = 0$. The same argument applies if $K(p_0) < 0$. T
herefore $K(p) = 0$ for every $p \in S$.

**Verification.** Conversely, if $K \equiv 0$ on $S$, then Gauss–Bonnet gives $\
int_C k_g\, ds = 2\pi$ for every simple closed positively oriented curve $C$, wh
ich is indeed a constant independent of $C$. This confirms the conclusion is sha
rp.

### Conclusion

The Gaussian curvature of $S$ is **identically zero** ($K \equiv 0$); equivalent
ly, $S$ is a flat (developable) surface.
