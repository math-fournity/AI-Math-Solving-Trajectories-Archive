## Proof

**Goal:** For each positive integer $n$, show that $N = 10^{10^{10^n}} + 10^{10^
n} + 10^n - 1$ is not prime.

---

### Step 1: Classify $n$ by its 2-adic valuation.

Write $n = 2^{k-1} \cdot m$ where $m$ is a positive odd integer and $k \geq 1$.
Then:
$$n \equiv 2^{k-1} \pmod{2^k}.$$

Note that $n \geq 2^{k-1} \geq k$ for all $k \geq 1$, so $k \leq n$.

### Step 2: For each $k \geq 1$, exhibit a prime $p_k$ with $\operatorname{ord}_
{p_k}(10) = 2^k$.

By **Zsygmondy's theorem**, for coprime $a > b > 0$ and $n > 1$, the number $a^n
 - b^n$ has a primitive prime divisor (a prime dividing $a^n - b^n$ but not $a^j
 - b^j$ for any $j < n$), except when $(a,b,n) = (2,1,6)$ or $n = 2$ with $a + b
$ a power of $2$.

Apply this with $a = 10$, $b = 1$, $n = 2^k$:
- For $k = 1$: $n = 2$ and $a + b = 11$ is **not** a power of $2$, so a primitiv
e prime divisor exists.
- For $k \geq 2$: $n = 2^k \geq 4 \neq 6$ and $(a,b,n) \neq (2,1,6)$, so a primi
tive prime divisor exists.

A primitive prime divisor $p_k$ of $10^{2^k} - 1$ satisfies $\operatorname{ord}_
{p_k}(10) = 2^k$. In particular $p_k \neq 2, 5$ (since $\gcd(10, p_k) = 1$).

### Step 3: Show $p_k \mid N$.

Since $\operatorname{ord}_{p_k}(10) = 2^k$, we have $10^j \equiv 10^{j \bmod 2^k
} \pmod{p_k}$.

**(a)** $10^{10^n} \pmod{p_k}$: The exponent is $10^n = 2^n \cdot 5^n$. Since $k
 \leq n$, we have $2^k \mid 10^n$, so:
$$10^{10^n} \equiv 10^0 = 1 \pmod{p_k}.$$

**(b)** $10^{10^{10^n}} \pmod{p_k}$: The exponent is $10^{10^n} = 2^{10^n} \cdot
 5^{10^n}$. Since $k \leq n \leq 10^n$, we have $2^k \mid 10^{10^n}$, so:
$$10^{10^{10^n}} \equiv 1 \pmod{p_k}.$$

**(c)** $10^n \pmod{p_k}$: Since $n \equiv 2^{k-1} \pmod{2^k}$:
$$10^n \equiv 10^{2^{k-1}} \pmod{p_k}.$$

Because $\operatorname{ord}_{p_k}(10) = 2^k$, the element $10^{2^{k-1}}$ has ord
er $2$ in $(\mathbb{Z}/p_k\mathbb{Z})^*$. The unique element of order $2$ is $-1
$, so:
$$10^{2^{k-1}} \equiv -1 \pmod{p_k}.$$

**Combining (a), (b), (c):**
$$N = 10^{10^{10^n}} + 10^{10^n} + 10^n - 1 \equiv 1 + 1 + (-1) - 1 = 0 \pmod{p_
k}.$$

### Step 4: Verify $N$ is strictly larger than $p_k$ (so $N$ is composite, not e
qual to $p_k$).

Since $p_k \mid 10^{2^k} - 1$, we have $p_k \leq 10^{2^k} - 1$. Since $n \geq 2^
{k-1}$, we get $2^k \leq 2n$, hence:
$$p_k \leq 10^{2n} - 1.$$

Meanwhile, for all $n \geq 1$:
$$N \geq 10^{10^{10^n}} \geq 10^{10^{10}} > 10^{2n} > p_k.$$

Thus $1 < p_k < N$ and $p_k \mid N$, proving $N$ is **not prime**.

---

**Conclusion:** For every positive integer $n$, writing $n = 2^{k-1} \cdot m$ wi
th $m$ odd, the prime $p_k$ (a primitive prime divisor of $10^{2^k} - 1$, guaran
teed by Zsygmondy's theorem) divides $N = 10^{10^{10^n}} + 10^{10^n} + 10^n - 1$
, and $N > p_k$, so $N$ is composite. $\blacksquare$
