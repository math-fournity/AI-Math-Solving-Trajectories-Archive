## Proof

**Claim.** The short exact sequence $0 \to A \xrightarrow{f} B \xrightarrow{g} C
 \to 0$ of divisible abelian groups splits.

**Key fact (Baer's criterion).** An abelian group is divisible if and only if it
 is an injective object in the category $\mathsf{Ab}$ of abelian groups.

*Proof of the key fact (sketch, for completeness).* An abelian group $D$ is divi
sible iff for every $n\geq 1$ and every $d\in D$, the equation $nx=d$ is solvabl
e in $D$. By Baer's injectivity criterion, $D$ is injective iff every homomorphi
sm $I\to D$ from an ideal $I\leq \mathbb{Z}$ extends to $\mathbb{Z}$. Since ever
y ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$ is $n\mathbb{Z}$, such a homomorphism is determined by t
he image of $n$, say $d\in D$; divisibility gives $d'\in D$ with $nd'=d$, and th
en $1\mapsto d'$ defines the extension $\mathbb{Z}\to D$. Conversely, injectivit
y applied to the inclusion $n\mathbb{Z}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Z}$ and the map $n
\mapsto d$ yields divisibility. $\square$

**Main argument.** Since $A$ is divisible, $A$ is injective. Consider the short
exact sequence
$$0 \longrightarrow A \xrightarrow{\;f\;} B \xrightarrow{\;g\;} C \longrightarro
w 0.$$
By injectivity of $A$, the identity morphism $\mathrm{id}_A : A \to A$ can be ex
tended along the monomorphism $f:A\to B$. Concretely, there exists a homomorphis
m $r:B\to A$ such that the diagram
$$\begin{array}{ccc} A & \xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}_A} & A \\[2pt] {\scriptstyle f
}\downarrow & \nearrow {\scriptstyle r} \\[2pt] B & \end{array}$$
commutes, i.e.
$$r\circ f = \mathrm{id}_A.$$

Such an $r$ is a **retraction** of $f$. The existence of a retraction is precise
ly the splitting condition for the left-hand side of the short exact sequence. E
quivalently, $B\cong A\oplus C$ via the map $b\mapsto\bigl(r(b),\,g(b)\bigr)$, w
ith inverse $(a,c)\mapsto f(a)+s(c)$ where $s:C\to B$ is any set-theoretic secti
on of $g$ (and the retraction guarantees this is a homomorphism). More directly:
 $\ker r$ is a complement to $\mathrm{im}\,f$ in $B$, and $g|_{\ker r}:\ker r\to
 C$ is an isomorphism, giving the splitting $B=\mathrm{im}\,f\oplus\ker r\cong A
\oplus C$.

Therefore the sequence splits. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** Only the divisibility (injectivity) of $A$ is needed; the divisibili
ty of $B$ and $C$ is automatic from the sequence (a quotient of a divisible grou
p is divisible, and a subgroup of a divisible group need not be divisible in gen
eral, but here $A$ is assumed divisible) and is not required for the splitting a
rgument. The essential point is that injective objects make every short exact se
quence beginning with them split.
