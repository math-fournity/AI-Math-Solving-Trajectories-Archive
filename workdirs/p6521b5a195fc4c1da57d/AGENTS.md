# Problem

In a bustling coastal port, a harbor master manages a row of $n$ numbered docking slips, arranged sequentially from 1 to $n$. A cargo ship arrives and occupies exactly one slip. Following this, a local ferry captain must claim a block of two adjacent, unoccupied slips for his twin-hulled vessel. Once the ferry is docked, the harbor master returns to claim a block of three adjacent, unoccupied slips for a naval patrol group. Finally, the ferry captain must claim a block of four adjacent, unoccupied slips to accommodate his new freight barge.

The ferry captain is determined to secure both of his requested berthing areas (the block of two and the block of four), regardless of which specific slips the harbor master chooses to occupy during his two turns.

What is the smallest value of $n$ for which the ferry captain can guaranteed he will be able to complete both of his docking maneuvers?
