## Proof

Let $S_n = \xi_1 + \cdots + \xi_n$ (with $S_0 = 0$), so that
$$X_t = \sup\{n \ge 0 : S_n \le t\}.$$
The $\xi_i$ are i.i.d. with $\xi_i \sim \mathrm{Exp}(\lambda)$, hence
$$\mu := \mathbb{E}[\xi_i] = \frac{1}{\lambda}, \qquad 0 < \mu < \infty.$$

**Step 1. $X_t \to \infty$ almost surely as $t \to \infty$.**

Since $\xi_i \ge 0$ and $\mathbb{P}(\xi_i > 0) = 1$, the random walk $S_n$ is no
ndecreasing and $S_n \to +\infty$ a.s. (e.g. by the strong law of large numbers,
 $S_n/n \to \mu > 0$, so $S_n \to \infty$). Therefore for every sample path in a
 probability-one event, $S_n \le t$ is satisfied for arbitrarily large $n$ as $t
$ grows, which means $X_t \to \infty$ a.s.

**Step 2. Sandwiching $t$ between consecutive partial sums.**

By definition of $X_t$,
$$S_{X_t} \le t < S_{X_t + 1}.$$
(If $X_t = 0$ the left inequality reads $S_0 = 0 \le t$, which holds.) Dividing
through by $X_t$ (for $X_t \ge 1$, which eventually holds a.s. by Step 1):
$$\frac{S_{X_t}}{X_t} \le \frac{t}{X_t} < \frac{S_{X_t+1}}{X_t+1}\cdot\frac{X_t+
1}{X_t}.$$

**Step 3. Apply the strong law of large numbers.**

By the SLLN, on a probability-one event $\Omega_0$,
$$\frac{S_n}{n} \to \mu = \frac{1}{\lambda} \quad \text{as } n \to \infty.$$

On $\Omega_0$, since $X_t \to \infty$ as $t \to \infty$ (Step 1), we may substit
ute the random index $X_t$ (which is integer-valued and tends to infinity) into
the limit:
$$\frac{S_{X_t}}{X_t} \to \mu, \qquad \frac{S_{X_t+1}}{X_t+1} \to \mu \quad \tex
t{a.s.}$$

Also $\frac{X_t+1}{X_t} = 1 + \frac{1}{X_t} \to 1$ a.s. since $X_t \to \infty$.

**Step 4. Squeeze.**

Both the lower bound $\frac{S_{X_t}}{X_t}$ and the upper bound $\frac{S_{X_t+1}}
{X_t+1}\cdot\frac{X_t+1}{X_t}$ converge to $\mu \cdot 1 = \mu$ almost surely. By
 the squeeze theorem,
$$\frac{t}{X_t} \to \mu = \frac{1}{\lambda} \quad \text{a.s.}$$

Since the limit is the positive constant $\mu$, taking reciprocals (continuous o
n $(0,\infty)$) gives
$$\frac{X_t}{t} \to \frac{1}{\mu} = \lambda \quad \text{a.s.}$$

## Conclusion

The almost sure limit of $\dfrac{X_t}{t}$ as $t \to +\infty$ is

$$\boxed{\lambda}.$$
