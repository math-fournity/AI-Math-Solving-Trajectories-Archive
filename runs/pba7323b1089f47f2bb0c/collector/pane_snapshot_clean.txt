# Proof: Angle KLM in an Equilateral Triangle with Two Inscribed Circles

## Setup and Configuration

Consider an equilateral triangle $ABC$ with side length $s$. Two congruent circl
es are inscribed in the triangle, arranged symmetrically about the axis of symme
try. The left circle is tangent to sides $AB$ and $BC$; the right circle is tang
ent to sides $AC$ and $BC$; and the two circles are tangent to each other.

The three points of tangency are:
- **$K$**: where the left circle touches the base $BC$
- **$M$**: where the right circle touches the base $BC$
- **$L$**: where the two circles touch each other

We find $\angle KLM$.

## Coordinate Computation

Place the triangle with:
- $B = (0, 0)$, $C = (s, 0)$, $A = (s/2,\; s\sqrt{3}/2)$.

**Centers.** Since each angle of the equilateral triangle is $60°$, the angle bi
sector at $B$ makes $30°$ with $BC$. The center $O_1$ of the left circle lies on
 this bisector at distance $d$ from $B$:

$$O_1 = \left(\tfrac{d\sqrt{3}}{2},\; \tfrac{d}{2}\right), \qquad r = \tfrac{d}{
2}$$

(the radius equals the perpendicular distance from $O_1$ to side $BC$, which is
$d\sin 30° = d/2$).

By symmetry, the center of the right circle is:

$$O_2 = \left(s - \tfrac{d\sqrt{3}}{2},\; \tfrac{d}{2}\right), \qquad r = \tfrac
{d}{2}$$

**Tangency condition.** The distance between centers equals $2r$ (external tange
ncy):

$$\left|O_1 O_2\right| = s - d\sqrt{3} = 2r = d$$

$$\Longrightarrow\quad s = d(1 + \sqrt{3})\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d = \frac{s}
{1+\sqrt{3}}.$$

**Tangency points.**

- $K$ is the foot of the perpendicular from $O_1$ to $BC$:
$$K = \left(\tfrac{d\sqrt{3}}{2},\; 0\right)$$

- $M$ is the foot of the perpendicular from $O_2$ to $BC$:
$$M = \left(s - \tfrac{d\sqrt{3}}{2},\; 0\right)$$

- $L$ is the midpoint of $O_1 O_2$ (since $r_1 = r_2$):
$$L = \left(\tfrac{s}{2},\; \tfrac{d}{2}\right)$$

## Computing Angle KLM

Form the vectors from $L$:

$$\vec{LK} = K - L = \left(\tfrac{d\sqrt{3}}{2} - \tfrac{s}{2},\; -\tfrac{d}{2}\
right), \qquad \vec{LM} = M - L = \left(\tfrac{s}{2} - \tfrac{d\sqrt{3}}{2},\; -
\tfrac{d}{2}\right)$$

**Key simplification.** Using $d = \frac{s}{1+\sqrt{3}}$:

$$\frac{d\sqrt{3}}{2} - \frac{s}{2} = \frac{s}{2}\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{1+\sqrt
{3}} - 1\right) = \frac{s}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3} - 1 - \sqrt{3}}{1+\sqrt{3}} = \
frac{s}{2}\cdot\frac{-1}{1+\sqrt{3}} = -\frac{d}{2}$$

Therefore:

$$\vec{LK} = \left(-\tfrac{d}{2},\; -\tfrac{d}{2}\right), \qquad \vec{LM} = \lef
t(\tfrac{d}{2},\; -\tfrac{d}{2}\right)$$

**Dot product:**

$$\vec{LK}\cdot\vec{LM} = \left(-\tfrac{d}{2}\right)\!\left(\tfrac{d}{2}\right)
+ \left(-\tfrac{d}{2}\right)\!\left(-\tfrac{d}{2}\right) = -\tfrac{d^2}{4} + \tf
rac{d^2}{4} = 0$$

Since the dot product vanishes, the vectors are perpendicular:

$$\boxed{\angle KLM = 90°}$$

## Geometric Insight

The horizontal and vertical components of $\vec{LK}$ are both $-d/2$, so $LK$ de
scends at $45°$. Likewise $\vec{LM}$ has components $(d/2, -d/2)$, so $LM$ desce
nds at $45°$ in the opposite direction. The two directions differ by exactly $90
°$, confirming the result independently of the side length $s$.
