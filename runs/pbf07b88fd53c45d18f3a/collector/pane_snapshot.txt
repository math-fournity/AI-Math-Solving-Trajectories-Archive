## Solution

Let $\alpha = \sqrt[3]{3}$ and let $\omega$ be a primitive cube root of unity. R
ecall that
$$\omega = \frac{-1+\sqrt{-3}}{2}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad \sqrt{-3} = 2\omega+1,$
$
and therefore $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}) = \mathbb{Q}(\omega)$.

The field $K = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha, \sqrt{-3}) = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha, \omega)$ is th
e splitting field of $x^3 - 3$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Since $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\ma
thbb{Q}]=3$ and $[\mathbb{Q}(\omega):\mathbb{Q}]=2$ with $\gcd(3,2)=1$, we have
$[K:\mathbb{Q}]=6$, and $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})\cong S_3$.

**Determining $\sigma(\omega)$.** From $\sigma(\sqrt{-3}) = -\sqrt{-3}$ and $\sq
rt{-3}=2\omega+1$:
$$-\sqrt{-3} = -(2\omega+1) = 2\sigma(\omega)+1 \implies 2\sigma(\omega) = -2\om
ega-2 \implies \sigma(\omega) = -\omega-1 = \omega^2.$$

So $\sigma$ acts as:
$$\sigma(\alpha) = \omega\alpha, \qquad \sigma(\omega) = \omega^2.$$

**Computing $\sigma^2$.**
$$\sigma^2(\alpha) = \sigma(\omega\alpha) = \sigma(\omega)\,\sigma(\alpha) = \om
ega^2\cdot\omega\alpha = \omega^3\alpha = \alpha.$$
$$\sigma^2(\omega) = \sigma(\omega^2) = \bigl(\sigma(\omega)\bigr)^2 = (\omega^2
)^2 = \omega^4 = \omega.$$

Since $\sigma^2$ fixes both generators $\alpha$ and $\omega$, we have $\sigma^2
= \mathrm{id}$.

**Checking $\sigma \neq \mathrm{id}$.** Since $\sigma(\alpha)=\omega\alpha \neq
\alpha$ (as $\omega\neq 1$), $\sigma$ is not the identity.

Therefore $\sigma$ has order $\boxed{2}$.
