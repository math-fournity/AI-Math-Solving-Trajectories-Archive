Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum number of 1's in an invertib
le $n \times n$ matrix over a field $F$ with all entries in $\{0, 1\}$.

## Proof

**Upper Bound: at most $n^2 - n + 1$ ones.**

Suppose $A$ is an $n \times n$ 0-1 matrix with at most $n - 2$ zeros. Then at mo
st $n - 2$ rows contain a zero entry. The remaining at least $n - (n-2) = 2$ row
s consist entirely of 1's. These two all-ones rows are identical, so $A$ is sing
ular — contradiction.

Therefore every invertible 0-1 matrix has **at least $n - 1$ zeros**, i.e., at m
ost $n^2 - (n-1) = n^2 - n + 1$ ones.

**Construction achieving $n^2 - n + 1$ ones.**

Define $A$ to have $0$ in positions $(i,i)$ for $i = 1, \ldots, n-1$ and $1$ in
every other entry (including position $(n,n)$). This gives exactly $n-1$ zeros a
nd $n^2 - n + 1$ ones.

To verify invertibility, subtract row $n$ (which is all 1's) from each row $i =
1, \ldots, n-1$. Row $i$ of $A$ has a $0$ at position $i$ and $1$'s elsewhere, s
o row $i$ minus row $n$ yields a row with $-1$ at position $i$ and $0$ elsewhere
. The resulting matrix is block lower-triangular:

$$\begin{pmatrix} -I_{n-1} & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{1}^T & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

Its determinant is $\det(-I_{n-1}) \cdot 1 = (-1)^{n-1} \neq 0$ in **any** field
 $F$ (since $-1 \neq 0$ in every field).

Hence $A$ is invertible with $n^2 - n + 1$ ones, matching the upper bound.

**Verification on small cases:**
- $n=1$: $[1]$, one 1, $1^2-1+1=1$. ✓
- $n=2$: $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$, $\det = -1 \neq 0$, three 1's,
$4-2+1=3$. ✓
- $n=3$: $\begin{pmatrix}0&1&1\\1&0&1\\1&1&1\end{pmatrix}$, $\det = 1 \neq 0$, s
even 1's, $9-3+1=7$. ✓

The maximum number of 1's in an invertible $n \times n$ 0-1 matrix over any fiel
d $F$ is $\boxed{n^2 - n + 1}$.
