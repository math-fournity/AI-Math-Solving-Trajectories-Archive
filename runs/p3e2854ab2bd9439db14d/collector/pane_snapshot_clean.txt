## Solution

**Goal:** Find $\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 1}\bigl[P(s)-\ln\zeta(s)\bigr]$.

**Step 1: Separate the $n=1$ term from $P(s)$.**

Recall $\mu(1)=1$. Writing out the defining sum:

$$P(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu(n)\frac{\ln\zeta(sn)}{n}=\underbrace{\mu(1)\frac{\
ln\zeta(s\cdot 1)}{1}}_{=\,\ln\zeta(s)}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\mu(n)\frac{\ln\zeta(
sn)}{n}.$$

Hence

$$P(s)-\ln\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\mu(n)\frac{\ln\zeta(sn)}{n}.\tag{1}$$

**Step 2: Justify passing the limit $s\to 1$ inside the sum.**

For $s$ in a small neighborhood of $1$ (say $|s-1|<\tfrac12$, so $sn\ge \tfrac32
$ for $n\ge 2$), each $\zeta(sn)$ is finite and continuous. Moreover $\ln\zeta(s
n)$ decays exponentially in $n$: since $\zeta(x)-1=\sum_{k\ge2}k^{-x}\le 2^{-x}\
bigl(1+2^{x}\!\sum_{k\ge3}k^{-x}\bigr)$ and for $x\ge\tfrac32$ we have $\zeta(x)
-1\le C\,2^{-x}$, it follows that $|\ln\zeta(sn)|\le C'\,2^{-sn}$. With $|\mu(n)
|\le 1$ and the factor $1/n$,

$$\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left|\mu(n)\frac{\ln\zeta(sn)}{n}\right|\le C'\sum_{n=2}^{
\infty}\frac{2^{-sn}}{n},$$

which converges uniformly for $s$ near $1$ (bounded by the convergent series at
$s=\tfrac12$). By uniform convergence, we may interchange limit and summation:

$$\lim_{s\to 1}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\mu(n)\frac{\ln\zeta(sn)}{n}=\sum_{n=2}^{\inft
y}\mu(n)\frac{\ln\zeta(n)}{n}.\tag{2}$$

**Step 3: Identify the resulting sum using the given constant $M$.**

The problem states

$$M=\gamma+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\mu(n)\frac{\ln\zeta(n)}{n},$$

so

$$\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\mu(n)\frac{\ln\zeta(n)}{n}=M-\gamma.\tag{3}$$

**Step 4: Combine.**

From (1), (2), and (3):

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 1}\bigl[P(s)-\ln\zeta(s)\bigr]=M-\gamma.}$$
