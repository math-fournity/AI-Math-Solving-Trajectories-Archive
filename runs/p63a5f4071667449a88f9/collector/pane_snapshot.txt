**Answer: No, it is not true in general.**

## Proof

The key observation is that the test-function class $C_0(\Omega)$ — continuous f
unctions with **compact support strictly inside** $\Omega$ — does not contain th
e constant function $\mathbf{1}_\Omega$, since $\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{1}_\
Omega) = \Omega$ is not a compact subset of $\Omega$. Therefore weak-star conver
gence against $C_0(\Omega)$ (vague convergence) does **not** control total mass:
 measure can "escape to the boundary" $\partial\Omega$.

### Counterexample

Let $\Omega = (0,1) \subset \mathbb{R}$, a bounded open smooth domain. Define
$$\mu_n := \delta_{1/n}, \qquad \mu := 0 \text{ (the zero measure)}.$$
Both are non-negative finite measures on $\Omega$ (note $1/n \in (0,1)$ for all
$n \geq 1$).

**Claim:** $\mu_n \to \mu$ weak-star against $C_0(\Omega)$, yet $\mu_n(\Omega) \
not\to \mu(\Omega)$.

**Step 1: Weak-star convergence.** Let $f \in C_0((0,1))$. By definition, $K :=
\operatorname{supp}(f)$ is a compact subset of $(0,1)$. Since $K \subset (0,1)$
is compact, it is bounded away from the endpoints: there exists $\varepsilon > 0
$ with $K \subset [\varepsilon, 1-\varepsilon]$. Choose $N$ such that $1/N < \va
repsilon$. Then for all $n \geq N$, the point $1/n \in (0,\varepsilon) \subset (
0,1)\setminus K$, so $f(1/n) = 0$. Hence
$$\int_\Omega f\,d\mu_n = f(1/n) = 0 = \int_\Omega f\,d\mu \qquad \forall\, n \g
eq N.$$
Since $f$ was arbitrary, $\mu_n \to \mu = 0$ in the weak-star sense.

**Step 2: Total mass does not converge.**
$$\mu_n(\Omega) = \delta_{1/n}((0,1)) = 1 \quad \text{for all } n,$$
while
$$\mu(\Omega) = 0.$$
Therefore $\mu_n(\Omega) = 1 \not\to 0 = \mu(\Omega)$.

### Intuition

The Dirac masses $\delta_{1/n}$ slide toward the boundary point $0 \in \partial\
Omega$. Every $C_0(\Omega)$-test function vanishes near the boundary, so it cann
ot "see" this escaping mass. The mass $1$ is lost in the limit, even though the
measures converge weak-star. To guarantee convergence of total mass one would ne
ed test functions that extend continuously to $\overline{\Omega}$ (i.e., weak co
nvergence against $C(\overline{\Omega})$ or $C_b(\Omega)$), under which the abov
e escape is detected.
