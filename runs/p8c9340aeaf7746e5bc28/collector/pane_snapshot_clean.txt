## Solution

**Step 1: Identify the distribution as a mixture.**

Note that $\frac{6}{\pi^2}\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{6}{\pi^2}\cdot\frac{
\pi^2}{6}=1$, so $M_X(0)=1$ and this is a valid MGF.

The term $e^{t^2/(2n)}$ is the MGF of a $N(0,1/n)$ random variable (since for $Y
\sim N(0,\sigma^2)$, $M_Y(t)=e^{\sigma^2 t^2/2}$, here $\sigma^2=1/n$).

Therefore $X$ is a **mixture of normals**:
$$X \sim N(0, 1/n) \quad\text{with probability } p_n = \frac{6}{\pi^2 n^2}.$$

Equivalently, $X = Z/\sqrt{N}$ where $Z\sim N(0,1)$ and $N$ is independent with
$P(N=n)=\frac{6}{\pi^2 n^2}$.

**Step 2: The mixture CDF is continuous.**

The CDF of $X$ is
$$F_X(x)=\sum_{n\ge1} p_n \, \Phi(x\sqrt{n}),$$
where $\Phi$ is the standard normal CDF. Each $\Phi(x\sqrt n)$ is continuous in
$x$, and the series converges uniformly (since $0\le \Phi\le 1$ and $\sum p_n=1$
). A uniformly convergent series of continuous functions is continuous, so **$F_
X$ is continuous**.

**Step 3: Continuous CDF ⟹ no atoms.**

A continuous CDF means $P(X=x)=F_X(x)-F_X(x^-)=0$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$. In
 particular, $P(X=q)=0$ for every rational $q$.

**Step 4: Countable union of null events.**

Since $\mathbb{Q}$ is countable,
$$P(X\in\mathbb{Q})=\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Q}}P(X=q)=\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Q}}0=0.$$

**Answer: A) 0**
