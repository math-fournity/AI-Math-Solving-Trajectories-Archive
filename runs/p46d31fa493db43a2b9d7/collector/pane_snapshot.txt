Proof

The Brownian Bridge SDE. The Brownian bridge on $[0,1)$ is governed by the
linear SDE

$$dX_t = \frac{X_t}{t-1},dt + dW_t, \qquad X_0 = 0, \qquad 0 \le t < 1,$$

where $W_t$ is a standard Brownian motion. Equivalently, $a(t) = \frac{1}{t-1} =
-\frac{1}{1-t}$, $b(t)=0$, $c(t)=0$, $d(t)=1$.

Step 1: Solve the SDE explicitly. This is a linear (Itô) SDE with multiplicative
drift and additive diffusion. The fundamental solution of the ODE $\dot{\Phi}
(t)=a(t)\Phi(t)$ is

$$\Phi(t) = \exp!\Bigl(\int_0^t \frac{ds}{s-1}\Bigr) = \exp!\bigl(\ln(1-t)\bigr)
= 1-t, \qquad 0\le t<1.$$

Since $b\equiv 0$ and $X_0=0$, the variation-of-constants formula gives

$$\boxed{X_t = (1-t)\int_0^t \frac{1}{1-s},dW_s, \qquad 0\le t<1.}$$

Step 2: Compute the quadratic variation of $X$. Writing $M_t := \int_0^t
\frac{1}{1-s},dW_s$, we have $X_t=(1-t)M_t$ and

$$\langle M\rangle_t = \int_0^t \frac{ds}{(1-s)^2} = \frac{1}{1-t}-1.$$

Hence

$$\langle X\rangle_t = (1-t)^2,\langle M\rangle_t = (1-t)^2\Bigl(\frac{1}{1-
t}-1\Bigr) = (1-t)-(1-t)^2 = t(1-t).$$

In particular $X_t$ is a continuous Gaussian martingale (deterministic integrand
$\times$ deterministic factor) with

$$\mathbb{E}[X_t]=0, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(X_t)=\langle X\rangle_t = t(1-t).
$$

Step 3: $L^2$ convergence to $0$. As $t\to 1^{-}$,

$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[X_t^2\bigr] = t(1-t) \longrightarrow 0,$$

so $X_t \to 0$ in $L^2(\Omega)$.

Step 4: Almost sure convergence to $0$. Since $X_t$ is a continuous martingale
on $[0,1)$ and

$$\sup_{0\le t<1}\langle X\rangle_t = \sup_{0\le t<1} t(1-t) = \tfrac{1}{4} <
\infty,$$

the martingale convergence theorem guarantees that $X_t$ converges almost surely
as $t\to 1^{-}$. The $L^2$ limit already identified in Step 3 is $0$; an a.s.
limit, when it exists, must agree with the $L^2$ limit. Therefore

$$\lim_{t\to 1^{-}} X_t = 0 \quad \text{almost surely.}$$

Step 5: Consistency check. The result is exactly what the name "Brownian bridge"
prescribes: the process is pinned to $X_0=0$ at $t=0$ and to $X_1=0$ at $t=1$.
The explicit solution $X_t=(1-t)\int_0^t (1-s)^{-1},dW_s$ is the standard
representation of a Brownian bridge (equivalent in law to $W_t - tW_1$), and the
factor $(1-t)\to 0$ forces the bridge to return to the origin, while the
stochastic integral diverges only at rate $(1-t)^{-1/2}$ in $L^2$, which is
exactly compensated.

Conclusion.

$$\boxed{\lim_{t\to 1} X_t = 0 \quad \text{a.s. and in } L^2.}$$
