## Solution

**Modeling the weaving process.** The shuttle travels across the *width* of the
panel. Each pass advances the fabric by 1.5 mm in the *length* direction. So for
 a panel with width $W$ and length $L$:

- Number of passes $= L / 1.5$
- Time per pass $= 1/\text{rate}$
- **Total time** $= \dfrac{L}{1.5 \times \text{rate}}$

The constraint is that the chosen width $W$ must not exceed the gear's maximum a
llowable width. We choose gear and orientation to minimize $L/\text{rate}$.

**Gear data:**
| Gear | Rate (passes/min) | Max width (mm) |
|------|-------------------|----------------|
| Alpha | 46 | 180 |
| Beta | 108 | 77 |

---

**Panel 1: $120 \times 60$ mm.**

Two orientations: $(W,L) = (120, 60)$ or $(60, 120)$.

*Gear Alpha* (max width 180, both orientations valid):
- $(W,L)=(120,60)$: $T = \dfrac{60}{1.5 \times 46} = \dfrac{60}{69} = \dfrac{20}
{23}$
- $(W,L)=(60,120)$: $T = \dfrac{120}{69} = \dfrac{40}{23}$

Best for Alpha: $\dfrac{20}{23}$.

*Gear Beta* (max width 77; only $W=60$ fits since $120 > 77$):
- $(W,L)=(60,120)$: $T = \dfrac{120}{1.5 \times 108} = \dfrac{120}{162} = \dfrac
{20}{27}$

Compare: $\dfrac{20}{27} \approx 0.741 < 0.870 \approx \dfrac{20}{23}$.

$$T_1 = \dfrac{20}{27}$$

---

**Panel 2: $150 \times 50$ mm.**

Two orientations: $(W,L) = (150, 50)$ or $(50, 150)$.

*Gear Alpha* (max width 180, both orientations valid):
- $(W,L)=(150,50)$: $T = \dfrac{50}{1.5 \times 46} = \dfrac{50}{69}$
- $(W,L)=(50,150)$: $T = \dfrac{150}{69} = \dfrac{50}{23}$

Best for Alpha: $\dfrac{50}{69}$.

*Gear Beta* (max width 77; only $W=50$ fits since $150 > 77$):
- $(W,L)=(50,150)$: $T = \dfrac{150}{1.5 \times 108} = \dfrac{150}{162} = \dfrac
{25}{27}$

Compare: $\dfrac{50}{69} \approx 0.725 < 0.926 \approx \dfrac{25}{27}$.

$$T_2 = \dfrac{50}{69}$$

---

**Final computation.**

$$T_1 + T_2 = \dfrac{20}{27} + \dfrac{50}{69}$$

Since $27 = 3^3$ and $69 = 3 \times 23$, the LCM is $3^3 \times 23 = 621$.

$$\dfrac{20}{27} = \dfrac{20 \times 23}{621} = \dfrac{460}{621}, \qquad \dfrac{5
0}{69} = \dfrac{50 \times 9}{621} = \dfrac{450}{621}$$

$$T_1 + T_2 = \dfrac{460 + 450}{621} = \dfrac{910}{621}$$

$$621 \cdot (T_1 + T_2) = 621 \cdot \dfrac{910}{621} = \boxed{910}$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
