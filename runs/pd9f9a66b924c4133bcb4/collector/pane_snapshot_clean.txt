## Proof

Consider the function
$$f: \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}, \qquad f(z) = \overline{z}.$$

**Continuity.** The map $z = x+iy \mapsto \overline{z} = x - iy$ is continuous (
it is $\mathbb{R}$-linear in the coordinates $x,y$, and linear maps between fini
te-dimensional real spaces are continuous).

**$f$ has no primitive.** Suppose, for contradiction, that $f$ admits a primitiv

$F'(z) = f(z) = \overline{z}$ for all $z \in \mathbb{C}$.

A fundamental theorem of complex analysis states that the derivative of a holomo
rphic function is itself holomorphic. Hence $F'(z) = \overline{z}$ would have to

But $\overline{z}$ is **not** holomorphic. Indeed, writing $F'(z) = \overline{z}

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial v}{\partial y}, \qquad \frac{\p

where $u(x,y) = x$ and $v(x,y) = -y$. This gives
$$1 = -1 \quad\text{and}\quad 0 = 0,$$
and the first equation $1 = -1$ is a contradiction. Equivalently, $\frac{\partia
