**Answer: No.** The same set can carry manifold structures of different dimensio
ns.

**Counterexample.** Let $S = \mathbb{R}$ as a bare set (no topology prescribed).

*Structure 1 (1-dimensional).* Give $S$ the standard topology and the standard s
mooth atlas $\{(U=\mathbb{R},\ \varphi=\mathrm{id})\}$. This makes $S$ a smooth
manifold of dimension $1$.

*Structure 2 (2-dimensional).* Since $|\mathbb{R}| = |\mathbb{R}^2| = \mathfrak{
c}$, there exists a set-theoretic bijection $\Phi : S \to \mathbb{R}^2$. **Trans
port the standard 2-manifold structure of $\mathbb{R}^2$ back to $S$** via $\Phi
$:
- Topology: declare $U \subseteq S$ open iff $\Phi(U) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ is
 open (in the standard topology of $\mathbb{R}^2$).
- Atlas: for each standard chart $(V, \psi)$ of $\mathbb{R}^2$, take the chart $
\bigl(\Phi^{-1}(V),\ \psi \circ \Phi\bigr)$ on $S$.

Because $\Phi$ is a bijection, this topology is well defined and makes $S$ homeo
morphic to $\mathbb{R}^2$; the transported charts form a smooth atlas (transitio
n maps are exactly those of $\mathbb{R}^2$, hence smooth). Thus $S$ becomes a sm
ooth manifold of dimension $2$.

These two manifold structures on the *same set* $S$ have different dimensions ($
1$ vs. $2$), so they are certainly distinct. $\square$

**Remark on the alternative reading.** If one *fixes the topology first* and the
n asks whether two manifold structures on that fixed topological space must shar
e a dimension, the answer becomes **yes**: by **invariance of domain** (Brouwer,
 1912), $\mathbb{R}^n$ and $\mathbb{R}^m$ are homeomorphic only when $n = m$, so
 the local dimension of a topological manifold is determined by its topology. Th
e question, however, speaks of "a set" — a structureless carrier — so the transp
ort-of-structure construction above applies and the dimensions need not agree.
