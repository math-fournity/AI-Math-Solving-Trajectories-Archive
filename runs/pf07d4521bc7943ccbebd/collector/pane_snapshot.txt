**Proof.**

**Key Lemma.** For positive integers $a, b$: $2^a - 1 \mid 2^b - 1$ if and only
if $a \mid b$.

*Proof of Lemma.* If $a \mid b$, write $b = ma$; then $2^b - 1 = (2^a)^m - 1 = (
2^a - 1)((2^a)^{m-1} + \cdots + 1)$, so $2^a - 1 \mid 2^b - 1$. Conversely, if $
2^a - 1 \mid 2^b - 1$, let $d = \gcd(a,b)$. Then $\gcd(2^a - 1, 2^b - 1) = 2^d -
 1$. Since $2^a - 1 \mid 2^b - 1$, we get $2^a - 1 \mid 2^d - 1$, forcing $a \le
 d$. But $d \mid a$ gives $d \le a$, so $d = a$, hence $a \mid b$. $\square$

Applying the lemma with $a = n$ and $b = k(n-1)! + k^n$, the condition becomes:

$$n \mid k(n-1)! + k^n \quad \text{for all integers } k > n. \tag{$\star$}$$

**Step 1: Necessity — $n$ must be prime.**

Take $k = n + 1$ (which satisfies $k > n$ and $\gcd(k, n) = 1$). Since $k \equiv

$$k(n-1)! + k^n \equiv (n-1)! + 1^n = (n-1)! + 1 \pmod{n}.$$

Condition $(\star)$ requires $n \mid (n-1)! + 1$, i.e., $(n-1)! \equiv -1 \pmod{
n}$.

By **Wilson's Theorem**, $(n-1)! \equiv -1 \pmod{n}$ holds **if and only if** $n
$ is prime. Therefore $n$ must be prime.

$$k(p-1)! + k^p \equiv -k + k = 0 \pmod{p}. \quad ✓$$

Thus every prime $p$ satisfies condition $(\star)$, and by the lemma, satisfies
