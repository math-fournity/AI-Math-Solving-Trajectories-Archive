## Proof

We assume the family $\{T(t)\}_{t\ge 0}$ is strongly continuous (so that $t\maps
to T(t)f$ is continuous, hence measurable, for every $f\in C_0(\mathbb R^d)$); t
his is the natural hypothesis making $B$ and $C$ well-defined. The only quantita
tive assumption is
$$\int_0^\infty \|T(t)\|\,dt<\infty. \tag{H}$$

We address the three operators in turn.

### 1.  $B$ and $C$ are well-defined and $B=C$

For $f\in C_0(\mathbb R^d)$, the map $t\mapsto T(t)f\in C_0(\mathbb R^d)$ is con
tinuous by strong continuity, hence strongly measurable. Moreover
$$\int_0^\infty |T(t)f|_0\,dt\le |f|_0\int_0^\infty\|T(t)\|\,dt<\infty$$
by (H), so $t\mapsto T(t)f$ is Bochner integrable in $C_0(\mathbb R^d)$. Thus
$$Bf=\int_0^\infty T(t)f\,dt\in C_0(\mathbb R^d)$$
is well-defined, and $\|B\|\le\int_0^\infty\|T(t)\|\,dt$.

For $C$: fix $x\in\mathbb R^d$. The evaluation functional $\delta_x:g\mapsto g(x
)$ is a bounded linear functional on $C_0(\mathbb R^d)$. A bounded linear operat
or commutes with the Bochner integral, so
$$\delta_x(Bf)=\int_0^\infty \delta_x(T(t)f)\,dt=\int_0^\infty (T(t)f)(x)\,dt=Cf
(x).$$
Hence $(Bf)(x)=Cf(x)$ for every $x$, i.e. $Bf=Cf$ as functions. Since $Bf\in C_0
(\mathbb R^d)$, also $Cf\in C_0(\mathbb R^d)$ and the scalar integral defining $
Cf(x)$ converges for every $x$. Therefore
$$\boxed{B=C.}$$

### 2.  When $A$ is well-defined, $A=B$

Suppose $t\mapsto T(t)$ is Bochner integrable in $\bigl(\mathcal L(C_0(\mathbb R
^d)),\|\cdot\|\bigr)$, so that
$$A=\int_0^\infty T(t)\,dt\in\mathcal L(C_0(\mathbb R^d))$$
exists. For each $f\in C_0(\mathbb R^d)$, the map
$$L_f:\mathcal L(C_0(\mathbb R^d))\to C_0(\mathbb R^d),\qquad L_f(S)=Sf$$
is bounded linear ($\|L_f(S)\|_0\le\|S\|\,|f|_0$). Bounded linear maps commute w
ith Bochner integrals, hence
$$Af=L_f(A)=L_f\!\left(\int_0^\infty T(t)\,dt\right)=\int_0^\infty L_f(T(t))\,dt
=\int_0^\infty T(t)f\,dt=Bf.$$
Since this holds for every $f$, $A=B$. Combined with step 1,
$$\boxed{A=B=C\quad\text{whenever }A\text{ is well-defined.}}$$

### 3.  $A$ is not well-defined in general

The Bochner integral defining $A$ lives in $\mathcal L(C_0(\mathbb R^d))$ equipp
ed with the **operator norm** topology. Bochner integrability there requires $t\
mapsto T(t)$ to be *strongly measurable in operator norm* — equivalently (Pettis
's measurability theorem), essentially separably valued in operator norm plus we
ak measurability. Strong continuity of $t\mapsto T(t)$ (in the strong operator t
opology) does **not** imply this.

**Counterexample.** Let $S(t)$ be the translation semigroup on $C_0(\mathbb R)$,
$$(S(t)f)(x)=f(x+t),\qquad t\ge0,$$
which is strongly continuous and isometric ($\|S(t)\|=1$). It is a standard fact
 that
$$\|S(t)-S(s)\|=2\qquad(t\ne s)$$
on $C_0(\mathbb R)$: choosing $f$ with $\|f\|_\infty\le1$ supported near a singl
e point makes $\sup_x|f(x+t)-f(x+s)|$ arbitrarily close to $2$ when $t\ne s$. He
nce $\{S(t):t\in[0,1]\}$ is an uncountable set with pairwise operator-norm dista
nce $2$, so it is **not separable** in operator norm.

Set $T(t):=e^{-t}S(t)$. Then $\|T(t)\|=e^{-t}$ and $\int_0^\infty\|T(t)\|\,dt=1<
\infty$, so (H) holds, and $T(t)$ is strongly continuous. For $t,s$ close but di
stinct,
$$\|T(t)-T(s)\|=\|e^{-t}S(t)-e^{-s}S(s)\|\ge e^{-s}\|S(t)-S(s)\|-|e^{-t}-e^{-s}|
\,\|S(t)\|=2e^{-s}-|e^{-t}-e^{-s}|,$$
which is bounded away from $0$ on compact $t$-intervals. Thus $\{T(t):t\in[0,1]\
}$ is still not separably valued in operator norm, so $t\mapsto T(t)$ is **not**
 Bochner measurable in $\mathcal L(C_0(\mathbb R))$. Consequently the Bochner in
tegral
$$A=\int_0^\infty T(t)\,dt\quad\text{in }\mathcal L(C_0(\mathbb R))$$
**does not exist**, even though (H) is satisfied.

Meanwhile $B$ and $C$ are perfectly well-defined here:
$$Bf(x)=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}f(x+t)\,dt,$$
a bounded operator on $C_0(\mathbb R)$, and $B=C$ by step 1.

### Conclusion

- $B=C$ **always** (under strong continuity and (H)).
- $A=B=C$ **whenever $A$ is well-defined**; the identity follows because the bou
nded linear map $S\mapsto Sf$ commutes with Bochner integrals.
- However, $A$ is **not** well-defined in general: the Bochner integral in $\mat
hcal L(C_0(\mathbb R^d))$ requires norm-measurability (essential separability of
 the range in operator norm), which strong continuity does not provide. The fami
ly $T(t)=e^{-t}S(t)$ with $S$ the translation semigroup on $C_0(\mathbb R)$ sati
sfies (H) yet makes $A$ undefined.

Therefore the statement "$A=B=C$" is **not valid in general**: $B=C$ always hold
s, and $A$ coincides with them only under the additional hypothesis that $t\maps
to T(t)$ is Bochner integrable in the operator-norm topology (e.g. norm continui
ty of $t\mapsto T(t)$).
