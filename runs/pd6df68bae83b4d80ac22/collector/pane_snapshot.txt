## Proof

Let $\mathcal{U}=\operatorname{col}(A)$ and $\mathcal{V}=\operatorname{col}(B)$,
 both $n$-dimensional subspaces of $\mathbb{R}^m$ (so $m\ge n$), with $\mathcal{
U}\ne\mathcal{V}$. Denote by $P_A=AA^T$ and $P_B=BB^T$ the orthogonal projection
s onto $\mathcal{U}$ and $\mathcal{V}$.

**Step 1 — Reduction.** As $O$ ranges over all $m\times m$ orthogonal matrices,
the matrix $U:=OB$ ranges over *all* $m\times n$ matrices with orthonormal colum
ns. Indeed, given any such $U$, complete $B$ to an orthogonal matrix $\widehat B
=[B\mid B_\perp]$ and $U$ to $\widehat U=[U\mid U_\perp]$; then $O=\widehat U\wi
dehat B^T$ is orthogonal and $OB=U$. Hence the question becomes:

> Does there exist an $m\times n$ matrix $U$ with orthonormal columns such that
$A^TU$ is orthogonal?

**Step 2 — Characterization.** We claim
$$A^TU\text{ is orthogonal}\iff \operatorname{col}(U)=\mathcal{U}.$$

*($\Leftarrow$)* If $\operatorname{col}(U)=\mathcal{U}$, then $U=AQ$ for some or
thogonal $n\times n$ matrix $Q$ (both are orthonormal bases of the same space).
Thus $A^TU=A^TAQ=Q$, which is orthogonal.

*($\Rightarrow$)* Suppose $A^TU$ is orthogonal. Then $(A^TU)^T(A^TU)=I_n$, i.e.
$$U^TAA^TU=U^TP_AU=I_n=U^TU.$$
Hence $U^T(I-P_A)U=0$. Since $I-P_A=P_{\mathcal{U}^\perp}$ is an orthogonal proj
ection (symmetric and idempotent),
$$0=U^TP_{\mathcal{U}^\perp}U=(P_{\mathcal{U}^\perp}U)^T(P_{\mathcal{U}^\perp}U)
,$$
so $P_{\mathcal{U}^\perp}U=0$, meaning every column of $U$ lies in $\mathcal{U}$
. Since $U$ has $n$ orthonormal columns and $\dim\mathcal{U}=n$, we get $\operat
orname{col}(U)=\mathcal{U}$.

**Step 3 — Existence of $O$.** By Step 2 we need $U=OB$ with $\operatorname{col}
(U)=\mathcal{U}$, i.e. $O(\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{U}$. Such an orthogonal map alwa
ys exists between two subspaces of the same dimension: take $U=A$ (an orthonorma
l basis of $\mathcal{U}$), extend $B$ to an orthogonal $\widehat B=[B\mid B_\per
p]$ and $A$ to an orthogonal $\widehat U=[A\mid A_\perp]$, and set
$$O=\widehat U\,\widehat B^T.$$
Then $O$ is orthogonal and $OB=A$, giving
$$A^TOB=A^TA=I_n,$$
which is orthogonal.

**Conclusion.** Yes — such an orthogonal matrix $O$ always exists. In fact $O$ c
an be chosen so that $A^TOB=I_n$. The hypothesis $\mathcal{U}\ne\mathcal{V}$ onl
y forces $O\neq I$ in general; it does not obstruct existence, since an orthogon
al transformation can always rotate one $n$-dimensional subspace onto another.
