## Solution

We wish to evaluate
$$I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \operatorname{sech}\!\left(\frac{\pi s}{2}\right)
ds.$$

**Step 1 — Reduction.** Substitute $u = \frac{\pi s}{2}$, so $ds = \frac{2}{\pi}
\,du$:
$$I = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \operatorname{sech}(u)\,du.$$
It remains to compute $J := \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \operatorname{sech}(u)\,du$
by contour integration.

**Step 2 — Setup.** Let $f(z) = \operatorname{sech}(z) = \dfrac{1}{\cosh z} = \d
frac{2}{e^{z}+e^{-z}}$. Its poles occur where $\cosh z = 0$, i.e. $e^{2z} = -1$,
 giving
$$z_k = \frac{(2k+1)\,i\pi}{2}, \qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}.$$
All poles are simple and lie on the imaginary axis.

A key identity is the **anti-periodicity**:
$$\operatorname{sech}(z + i\pi) = \frac{1}{\cosh(z+i\pi)} = \frac{1}{-\cosh z} =
 -\operatorname{sech}(z).$$

**Step 3 — Contour.** Consider the positively oriented rectangle $\mathcal{C}_R$
 with vertices
$$-R,\quad R,\quad R+i\pi,\quad -R+i\pi.$$
Inside $\mathcal{C}_R$ the only pole of $f$ is at $z_0 = \dfrac{i\pi}{2}$.

By the residue theorem,
$$\oint_{\mathcal{C}_R} f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i\,\operatorname{Res}(f,\,z_0).$$

**Step 4 — Residue.** Since $z_0$ is a simple zero of $\cosh z$ and $\frac{d}{dz
}\cosh z = \sinh z$,
$$\operatorname{Res}(f,\,z_0) = \frac{1}{\sinh z_0} = \frac{1}{\sinh\!\left(\fra
c{i\pi}{2}\right)} = \frac{1}{i\sin(\pi/2)} = \frac{1}{i} = -i.$$
Hence
$$\oint_{\mathcal{C}_R} f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i\,(-i) = 2\pi.$$

**Step 5 — Parametrize the four sides.**

*Bottom* ($z = x$, $x: -R \to R$):
$$\int_{-R}^{R} \operatorname{sech}(x)\,dx \;\xrightarrow[R\to\infty]{}\; J.$$

*Top* ($z = x + i\pi$, $x: R \to -R$): using $\operatorname{sech}(x+i\pi) = -\op
eratorname{sech}(x)$,
$$\int_{R}^{-R} \operatorname{sech}(x+i\pi)\,dx = \int_{R}^{-R}\bigl(-\operatorn
ame{sech} x\bigr)\,dx = \int_{-R}^{R}\operatorname{sech}(x)\,dx \;\xrightarrow[R
\to\infty]{}\; J.$$

*Right* ($z = R + iy$, $y: 0 \to \pi$):
$$\left|\int_{0}^{\pi} \operatorname{sech}(R+iy)\,i\,dy\right| \le \int_{0}^{\pi
}\frac{2}{e^{R}-e^{-R}}\,dy \;\xrightarrow[R\to\infty]{}\; 0,$$
since $|\operatorname{sech}(R+iy)| = \dfrac{2}{|e^{R+iy}+e^{-R-iy}|} \le \dfrac{
2}{e^{R}-e^{-R}}$.

*Left* ($z = -R + iy$, $y: \pi \to 0$): by the same estimate with $R$ replaced b
y $-R$ (using $|e^{-R+iy}+e^{R-iy}| \ge e^{R}-e^{-R}$),
$$\left|\int_{\pi}^{0} \operatorname{sech}(-R+iy)\,i\,dy\right| \;\xrightarrow[R
\to\infty]{}\; 0.$$

**Step 6 — Take the limit.** Adding the four contributions and letting $R \to \i
nfty$:
$$2J = 2\pi \quad\Longrightarrow\quad J = \pi.$$

**Step 7 — Conclude.**
$$I = \frac{2}{\pi}\,J = \frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\pi = 2.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \operatorname{sech}\!\left(\frac{
\pi s}{2}\right) ds = 2}$$
