**Answer: Yes.** Every valuation ring of a field $K$ can be expressed as $R = \{
a \in K : v(a) \ge 0\}$ for a suitable (Krull) valuation $v: K \to \Gamma \cup \
{\infty\}$, where $\Gamma$ is a totally ordered abelian group.

**Construction and proof.**

Let $R \subseteq K$ be a valuation ring, i.e., an integral domain with fraction
field $K$ such that for every $x \in K^\times$, either $x \in R$ or $x^{-1} \in
R$.

**Step 1: Define the value group.** Let $U = R^\times$ be the group of units of
$R$. Since $U$ is a subgroup of $K^\times$, we may form the quotient group
$$\Gamma = K^\times / U.$$
We write the coset of $a \in K^\times$ as $\bar{a} = aU$, and use additive notat
ion, so $\bar{a} + \bar{b} = \overline{ab}$ and $-\bar{a} = \overline{a^{-1}}$.

**Step 2: Define the total order on $\Gamma$.** For $a, b \in K^\times$, set
$$\bar{a} \le \bar{b} \iff b/a \in R.$$
*Well-definedness:* If $\bar{a} = \bar{a'}$ and $\bar{b} = \bar{b'}$, then $a' =
 au$, $b' = bu'$ for units $u, u' \in U$. Hence $b'/a' = (b/a)(u'/u)$. Since $u'
/u \in U \subseteq R$ and $(u'/u)^{-1} \in U \subseteq R$, multiplication by $u'
/u$ preserves membership in $R$ (if $c \in R$ then $c \cdot (u'/u) \in R$; if $c
 \notin R$ then $c^{-1} \in R$, so $(c \cdot u'/u)^{-1} = (u/u') \cdot c^{-1} \i
n R$, meaning $c \cdot (u'/u) \notin R$). Thus $b'/a' \in R \iff b/a \in R$.

*Totality:* For any $a, b \in K^\times$, the element $b/a \in K^\times$ satisfie
s $b/a \in R$ or $(b/a)^{-1} = a/b \in R$ (valuation ring property). Hence $\bar
{a} \le \bar{b}$ or $\bar{b} \le \bar{a}$.

*Transitivity and compatibility with the group law:* If $\bar{a} \le \bar{b}$ an
d $\bar{b} \le \bar{c}$, then $b/a, c/b \in R$, so $c/a = (c/b)(b/a) \in R$, giv
ing $\bar{a} \le \bar{c}$. If $\bar{a} \le \bar{b}$, then for any $\bar{c}$, we
have $b/a \in R$, so $(bc)/(ac) = b/a \in R$, giving $\bar{a} + \bar{c} \le \bar
{b} + \bar{c}$. Thus $\Gamma$ is a totally ordered abelian group. The identity e
lement is $\bar{1} = U$, and $\bar{a} \ge 0$ iff $a \in R$.

**Step 3: Define the valuation.** Set $v: K \to \Gamma \cup \{\infty\}$ by
$$v(a) = \begin{cases} \bar{a} & \text{if } a \in K^\times, \\ \infty & \text{if
 } a = 0, \end{cases}$$
where $\infty$ is a formal element larger than every element of $\Gamma$, with $
\infty + \gamma = \gamma + \infty = \infty$ and $\gamma \le \infty$ for all $\ga
mma$.

*Multiplicativity:* $v(ab) = \overline{ab} = \bar{a} + \bar{b} = v(a) + v(b)$ fo
r $a, b \in K^\times$, and the cases involving $0$ are immediate.

*Ultrametric inequality:* For $a, b \in K^\times$ with $a + b \ne 0$, we must sh
ow $v(a+b) \ge \min(v(a), v(b))$. By symmetry assume $v(a) \le v(b)$, i.e., $b/a
 \in R$. Then
$$\frac{a+b}{a} = 1 + \frac{b}{a} \in R$$
since $1 \in R$ and $b/a \in R$ and $R$ is a ring. Hence $a/(a+b) \in R$ or $(a+
b)/a \in R$; we've shown $(a+b)/a \in R$, so $v(a+b) \ge v(a) = \min(v(a), v(b))
$. (If $a + b = 0$, then $v(a+b) = \infty \ge \min(v(a), v(b))$ trivially.)

Thus $v$ is a valuation on $K$.

**Step 4: Recover $R$.** By construction,
$$\{a \in K : v(a) \ge 0\} = \{a \in K^\times : \bar{a} \ge 0\} \cup \{0\} = \{a
 \in K^\times : a \in R\} \cup \{0\} = R.$$

**Conclusion.** Every valuation ring $R$ of $K$ is the nonnegative locus of the
canonical valuation $v: K^\times \to K^\times/R^\times$ constructed above. Hence
 the answer is **yes**, every valuation ring for $K$ arises from a valuation $v$
 on $K$ (with values in the totally ordered abelian group $\Gamma = K^\times/R^\
times$).
