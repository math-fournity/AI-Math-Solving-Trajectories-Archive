## Solution

**Step 1: Substitution $t = 1/x$.**

With $x = 1/t$, $dx = -dt/t^2$, and limits $x: 0^+\to 1$ becoming $t: \infty \to
 1$:

$$I = \int_1^{\infty} \sqrt{\frac{\{t\}}{1-\{t\}}}\cdot\frac{1}{1-1/t}\cdot\frac
{dt}{t^2} = \int_1^{\infty} \sqrt{\frac{\{t\}}{1-\{t\}}}\cdot\frac{dt}{t(t-1)}$$

**Step 2: Decompose into unit intervals.**

On $[n, n+1)$ for $n=1,2,3,\ldots$, we have $\{t\} = t-n$. Substituting $u = t-n
 \in [0,1)$:

$$I = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_0^1 \sqrt{\frac{u}{1-u}}\cdot\frac{du}{(n+u)(n+u-1
)}$$

**Step 3: Partial fractions and telescoping.**

Using $\frac{1}{(n+u)(n+u-1)} = \frac{1}{n+u-1}-\frac{1}{n+u}$:

$$I = \int_0^1 \sqrt{\frac{u}{1-u}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{n+u-1}-\fra
c{1}{n+u}\right)du$$

The partial sum telescopes:
$$\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\frac{1}{n+u-1}-\frac{1}{n+u}\right) = \frac{1}{u}-\frac{1
}{N+u}\;\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}\;\frac{1}{u}$$

**Justification of interchange:** Each summand $\frac{1}{n+u-1}-\frac{1}{n+u} >
0$ for $u\in(0,1)$, $n\geq 1$, and $\sqrt{u/(1-u)}\geq 0$. By Tonelli's theorem
(non-negative integrand), the interchange is valid.

**Step 4: Evaluate the resulting integral.**

$$I = \int_0^1 \sqrt{\frac{u}{1-u}}\cdot\frac{1}{u}\,du = \int_0^1 \frac{du}{\sq
rt{u(1-u)}}$$

This is the Beta function integral:
$$\int_0^1 u^{-1/2}(1-u)^{-1/2}\,du = B\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}\right)
= \frac{\Gamma(1/2)\,\Gamma(1/2)}{\Gamma(1)} = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}\cdot\sqrt{\pi}}{
1} = \pi$$

**Convergence check (original integral):** Near $x=1^-$, $\{1/x\}=\frac{1-x}{x}\
approx 1-x$, so the integrand $\sim \sqrt{1-x}/(1-x)=1/\sqrt{1-x}$, integrable.
Near $x=0$, the substitution shows convergence via the Beta integral above. ✓
