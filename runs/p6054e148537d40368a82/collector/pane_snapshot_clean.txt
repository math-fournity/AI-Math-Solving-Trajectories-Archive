Answer: No. A closed subgroup of a semisimple linear algebraic group need not be
semisimple.

Counterexample

Let $K$ be any field of characteristic $0$ (e.g. $K = \mathbb{Q}$). Take

$$S = \mathrm{SL}_{2,K} = \left{ \begin{pmatrix} a & b \ c & d \end{pmatrix} :
ad - bc = 1 \right},$$

the special linear group. This is a connected, simply connected, simple (hence
semisimple) linear algebraic group over $K$.

Define

$$H = \left{ \begin{pmatrix} t & 0 \ 0 & t^{-1} \end{pmatrix} : t \in \mathbb{G}
{m,K} \right} ;\subset; \mathrm{SL}{2,K}.$$

$H$ is a closed $K$-subgroup of $S$. The map $\mathbb{G}{m,K} \to \mathrm{SL}
{2,K}$, $t \mapsto \mathrm{diag}(t, t^{-1})$, is a morphism of algebraic groups
defined over $K$. Its image is $H$, and since the map is a closed immersion (it
is a group homomorphism with trivial kernel, given by a surjection of coordinate
rings onto a closed subvariety), $H$ is a closed $K$-subgroup of $S$.

$H \cong \mathbb{G}_{m,K}$ is a one-dimensional torus. The map $t \mapsto
\mathrm{diag}(t, t^{-1})$ is an isomorphism of algebraic groups $\mathbb{G}
_{m,K} \xrightarrow{;\sim;} H$.

$H$ is not semisimple. Recall that a linear algebraic group is semisimple if and
only if it is connected and its (solvable) radical is trivial, equivalently its
connected center is trivial and it has no nontrivial connected solvable normal
subgroup. The group $\mathbb{G}_{m,K}$ is:

  • connected (yes, $\mathbb{G}_m$ is connected), but
  • its radical is itself: $\mathbb{G}_m$ is abelian, hence solvable, so its
  radical $R(\mathbb{G}_m) = \mathbb{G}_m \neq {1}$.

Equivalently, the connected center of $\mathbb{G}_m$ is $\mathbb{G}_m$ itself,
which is positive-dimensional. Therefore $\mathbb{G}_m$ is a torus, not a
semisimple group.

Conclusion

We have exhibited a semisimple linear algebraic group $S = \mathrm{SL}{2,K}$
(over a field $K$ of characteristic $0$) and a closed $K$-subgroup $H \cong
\mathbb{G}{m,K}$ (a maximal torus) which is not semisimple. Hence the answer to
the question is no: a closed subgroup of a semisimple linear algebraic group
over a field of characteristic $0$ need not be semisimple.
