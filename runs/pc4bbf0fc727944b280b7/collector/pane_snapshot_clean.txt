## Analysis

### The Construction (Left Side)

The iterative construction produces $H = \bigcup G_i$ and $M = \bigcup M_i$ wher
e:
- $G_1 = G$, $M_1 = F[G]$
- $G_{i+1} = \operatorname{Aut}_F(M_i)$, $M_{i+1} = F[G_{i+1}] \oplus M_i$

**Left-simplicity of $M$:** Any nonzero left $F[H]$-submodule $N \subseteq M$ co
ntains a nonzero element in some $M_i$. Since $G_{i+1} = \operatorname{Aut}_F(M_
i)$, the group $G_{i+1}$ acts on $M_i$ as the **full** $F$-linear automorphism g
roup, so it acts transitively on nonzero vectors. Thus $N \cap M_i = M_i$, and b
y ascending through the chain, $N = M$.

**Left-faithfulness of $M$:** If $h \in H$ acts trivially on $M$, then $h \in G_
i$ for some $i$ and acts trivially on $M_i$. But $G_i \hookrightarrow G_{i+1} =
\operatorname{Aut}_F(M_i)$, so $h$ acts as an element of $\operatorname{Aut}_F(M
_i)$. The identity automorphism corresponds to $h = 1$.

So $M$ is indeed a faithful simple **left** $F[H]$-module, and $F[H]$ is primiti
ve. ✓

### The Fatal Flaw: The Bimodule Condition

For the argument to work, we need a **ring homomorphism** $F[G] \to \operatornam
e{End}_{F[H]}(M)$ via right multiplication. This requires:

1. $M$ must be an $(F[H], F[H])$-**bimodule**: the left and right actions must c
ommute, i.e., $(h \cdot m) \cdot a = h \cdot (m \cdot a)$ for all $h, a \in F[H]
$, $m \in M$.

2. Only then does right multiplication by $a$ define an $F[H]$-linear endomorphi
sm.

**This commutativity fails.** Consider the first step: $M_1 = F[G]$ has a natura
l right $G$-action (right multiplication). Now $G_2 = \operatorname{Aut}_F(M_1)$
 is the **full** $F$-linear automorphism group of $M_1$. Take $\phi \in G_2$ and
 $g \in G_1$. For the bimodule condition we need:
$$\phi(m \cdot g) = \phi(m) \cdot g \quad \text{for all } m \in M_1,$$
i.e., $\phi$ must be **right-$G$-equivariant**: $\phi \in \operatorname{Aut}_{F[
G]}(M_1)$. But $G_2 = \operatorname{Aut}_F(M_1) \supsetneq \operatorname{Aut}_{F
[G]}(M_1)$ in general. A generic $F$-linear automorphism does **not** commute wi
th right multiplication by $g$.

**This is the core contradiction in the construction**: left-simplicity requires
 $G_{i+1} = \operatorname{Aut}_F(M_i)$ (the *full* automorphism group, for trans
itivity on nonzero vectors), but the bimodule condition requires $G_{i+1} \subse
teq \operatorname{Aut}_{F[\text{right action}]}(M_i)$ (automorphisms respecting
the right action). These two requirements are incompatible — the full automorphi
sm group is far too large to respect any nontrivial right action.

### Attempting the Antipode/Involution Fails Too

One might try defining the right action via the group inverse: $m \cdot h := h^{
-1} \cdot m$. This always commutes with the left action (since left and right mu
ltiplication commute in a group). However, the map $\phi_h: m \mapsto h^{-1} \cd
ot m$ is **not** $F[H]$-linear:
$$\phi_h(k \cdot m) = h^{-1} k \cdot m \neq k h^{-1} \cdot m = k \cdot \phi_h(m)
$$
unless $h$ is central in $H$. Since $H$ is highly non-abelian (it contains full
general linear groups), this fails.

Moreover, this map is a ring **anti**-homomorphism $F[G] \to \operatorname{End}(
M)$ (since $(gh)^{-1} = h^{-1}g^{-1}$), not a homomorphism.

### Conclusion

**No, $M$ is not simple and faithful on both sides, and $F[G]$ does not embed in
to a division ring via this argument.**

The construction guarantees only that $M$ is a faithful simple **left** $F[H]$-m
odule. The right module structure either:

- **Does not exist as a bimodule**: the left action of $G_{i+1} = \operatorname{
Aut}_F(M_i)$ (full automorphism group) cannot commute with any natural right act
ion, so right multiplication does not yield $F[H]$-linear endomorphisms, and the
re is no ring homomorphism $F[G] \to \operatorname{End}_{F[H]}(M)$ at all; or

- **Exists but lacks the required properties**: even if some right action is def
ined, right-simplicity and right-faithfulness are not guaranteed by the construc
tion (the transitivity argument uses the left action of the full automorphism gr
oup, which has no right-side analogue).

The embedding argument therefore **collapses at the first step**: there is no ri
ng homomorphism $F[G] \to \operatorname{End}_{F[H]}(M)$ because $M$ is not a bim
odule. The conclusion that $F[G]$ embeds into a division ring **does not follow*
*.

This is consistent with known algebra: if $G$ contains an element $g$ of finite
order $n$ with $\operatorname{char}(F) \mid n$, then $(1 - g)(1 + g + \cdots + g
^{n-1}) = 0$ in $F[G]$, so $F[G]$ has zero-divisors and cannot embed into any di
vision ring — providing concrete counterexamples to the claimed conclusion.
