## Proof

Let $C_g$ be a smooth compact complex curve of genus $g$, and let $J = \operator
name{Jac}(C_g)$ be its Jacobian, a principally polarized abelian variety (ppav)
of dimension $g$, with theta divisor $\Theta$ defining the principal polarizatio
n. We wish to compute
$$\dim H^0\!\bigl(J,\,\mathcal{O}(\Theta)\bigr).$$

### Step 1. The Riemann–Roch theorem on an abelian variety

For any line bundle $L$ on an abelian variety $A$ of dimension $g$, the Riemann–
Roch theorem gives
$$\chi(A, L) \;=\; \sum_{i=0}^{g} (-1)^i\, h^i(A, L) \;=\; \frac{(L)^g}{g!},$$
where $(L)^g$ is the top self-intersection number (the degree of the polarizatio
n).

For the theta divisor $\Theta$ on the Jacobian, the polarization is principal, m
eaning the polarization type is $(1,1,\dots,1)$, so
$$(\Theta)^g \;=\; g!.$$
Hence
$$\chi\!\bigl(J,\,\mathcal{O}(\Theta)\bigr) \;=\; \frac{g!}{g!} \;=\; 1. \tag{1}
$$

### Step 2. Vanishing of higher cohomology

By Mumford's vanishing theorem (the abelian-variety analogue of Kodaira vanishin
g): if $L$ is an ample line bundle on an abelian variety $A$, then
$$H^i(A, L) \;=\; 0 \quad \text{for all } i > 0.$$

The theta divisor $\Theta$ is ample (it defines a positive definite polarization
), so applying this to $L = \mathcal{O}(\Theta)$:
$$h^i\!\bigl(J,\,\mathcal{O}(\Theta)\bigr) \;=\; 0, \qquad i = 1, 2, \dots, g. \
tag{2}$$

### Step 3. Conclusion

Combining (1) and (2):
$$1 \;=\; \chi\!\bigl(J,\,\mathcal{O}(\Theta)\bigr) \;=\; h^0\!\bigl(J,\,\mathca
l{O}(\Theta)\bigr) \;-\; \underbrace{h^1}_{=\,0} \;-\;\cdots\;-\; \underbrace{h^
g}_{=\,0} \;=\; h^0\!\bigl(J,\,\mathcal{O}(\Theta)\bigr).$$

Therefore
$$\boxed{\dim H^0\!\bigl(J,\,\mathcal{O}(\Theta)\bigr) \;=\; 1.}$$

### Geometric interpretation

The unique (up to scalar) non-zero section of $\mathcal{O}(\Theta)$ is the canon
ical section vanishing precisely along $\Theta$. The fact that $h^0 = 1$ reflect
s that the theta divisor of a Jacobian is **unique up to translation** — equival
ently, the Jacobian is an *indecomposable* principally polarized abelian variety
, so its polarization admits no non-trivial deformations within its linear syste
m. Any effective divisor linearly equivalent to $\Theta$ must equal $\Theta$ its
elf.
