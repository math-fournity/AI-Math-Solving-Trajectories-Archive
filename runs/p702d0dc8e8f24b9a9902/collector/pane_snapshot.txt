**Claim.** $\bigcap_{n} A_n = \varnothing$, hence its Lebesgue measure is $0$.

**Proof.** Write $r^2 = x^2 + y^2$. Each set is determined by a condition on $r^
2$:

$$A_n = \{(x,y) : r^2 \in I_n\}, \qquad I_n = \left(\tfrac{1}{2^{n+1}},\, \tfrac
{1}{2^n}\right).$$

The intervals $I_n$ are pairwise disjoint. Indeed, for consecutive indices,

$$I_n = \left(\tfrac{1}{2^{n+1}},\, \tfrac{1}{2^n}\right), \qquad I_{n+1} = \lef
t(\tfrac{1}{2^{n+2}},\, \tfrac{1}{2^{n+1}}\right),$$

so $I_n$ and $I_{n+1}$ share only the endpoint $\tfrac{1}{2^{n+1}}$, which belon
gs to neither open interval. Hence $I_n \cap I_{n+1} = \varnothing$, and more ge
nerally $I_n \cap I_m = \varnothing$ for all $n \neq m$.

Since $A_n$ is the preimage of $I_n$ under the continuous map $(x,y)\mapsto x^2+
y^2$, the disjointness of the $I_n$ implies the disjointness of the $A_n$:

$$A_n \cap A_m = \varnothing \quad \text{for all } n \neq m.$$

In particular, taking any two distinct indices (say $n=0$ and $n=1$),

$$\bigcap_{n} A_n \;\subseteq\; A_0 \cap A_1 = \varnothing.$$

Therefore $\bigcap_{n} A_n = \varnothing$, and the Lebesgue measure of the empty
 set is

$$\lambda\!\left(\bigcap_{n} A_n\right) = 0.$$
