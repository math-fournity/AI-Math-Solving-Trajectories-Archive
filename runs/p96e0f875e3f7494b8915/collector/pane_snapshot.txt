## Proof

Recall the standard definitions:
- $H^1(\Omega) = \{u \in L^2(\Omega) : \partial_i u \in L^2(\Omega),\; i=1,\dots
,n\}$, the Sobolev space with norm $\|u\|_{H^1}^2 = \|u\|_{L^2}^2 + \|\nabla u\|
_{L^2}^2$.
- $H^{-1}(\Omega) = (H^1_0(\Omega))^*$, the dual space of $H^1_0(\Omega)$ (the c
losure of $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ in $H^1(\Omega)$).

Let $u \in H^1(\Omega)$. We define the distributional Laplacian $\Delta u$ as a
distribution on $\Omega$: for every test function $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\Omega
)$,

$$\langle \Delta u, \varphi \rangle := \int_\Omega u\, \Delta\varphi\, dx.$$

We must show this distribution extends to a bounded linear functional on $H^1_0(
\Omega)$.

fty(\Omega)$ has compact support strictly inside $\Omega$, integrating by parts
once gives

$$\int_\Omega u\, \partial_i \varphi\, dx = -\int_\Omega \partial_i u\, \varphi\
, dx,$$

with no boundary term because $\varphi \equiv 0$ near $\partial\Omega$. This is
valid since $u \in H^1(\Omega)$ and $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\Omega) \subset H^1_
0(\Omega)$. Applying this twice (summing over $i$):

$$\langle \Delta u, \varphi \rangle = \int_\Omega u\, \Delta\varphi\, dx = -\int
_\Omega \nabla u \cdot \nabla \varphi\, dx.$$

**Step 2: Boundedness on $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ with the $H^1_0$-norm.** By the Ca
uchy–Schwarz inequality,

$$|\langle \Delta u, \varphi \rangle| = \left|\int_\Omega \nabla u \cdot \nabla
\varphi\, dx\right| \leq \|\nabla u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\, \|\nabla \varphi\|_{L^2(\O
mega)} \leq \|u\|_{H^1(\Omega)}\, \|\varphi\|_{H^1(\Omega)}.$$

By the Poincaré inequality (valid on bounded domains), $\|\varphi\|_{H^1_0} := \
|\nabla \varphi\|_{L^2}$ is an equivalent norm on $H^1_0(\Omega)$, so this gives

$$|\langle \Delta u, \varphi \rangle| \leq C\, \|u\|_{H^1(\Omega)}\, \|\varphi\|
_{H^1_0(\Omega)}.$$

\Omega)$, the linear functional $\varphi \mapsto \langle \Delta u, \varphi \rang
le$ extends uniquely to a bounded linear functional on $H^1_0(\Omega)$, with ope
rator norm bounded by $C\|u\|_{H^1(\Omega)}$.

Therefore $\Delta u \in (H^1_0(\Omega))^* = H^{-1}(\Omega)$, and

$$\|\Delta u\|_{H^{-1}} \leq C\,\|u\|_{H^1}.$$

lta u$ belongs to $H^{-1}(\Omega)$. The statement is **true**. $\blacksquare$
