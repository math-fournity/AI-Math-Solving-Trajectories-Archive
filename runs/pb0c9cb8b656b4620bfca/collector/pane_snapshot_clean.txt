$$\mathbb{Q} \setminus S = \{q \in \mathbb{Q} : q \leq 0 \text{ or } q \geq 1\}.

This set contains, for instance, every negative integer $-1, -2, -3, \dots$, so

 \mathbb{R}$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}$ iff every nonempty open interval intersect

 of $\mathbb{Q}$ such that $\mathbb{Q} \setminus S$ is infinite but not dense in
