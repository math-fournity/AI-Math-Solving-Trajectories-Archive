## Analysis

The integral is:
$$I = \int_{0}^{+\infty} e^{-A\sqrt{x^2+B}}\cos(Cx)\,\frac{x'}{\sqrt{x'^2-B}}\,d
x'$$

The factor $\frac{x'}{\sqrt{x'^2-B}}\,dx'$ is the Jacobian of the substitution $
x'^2 = x^2 + B$ (i.e., $x = \sqrt{x'^2 - B}$), under which $\sqrt{x^2+B} = x'$ a
nd $dx = \frac{x'}{\sqrt{x'^2-B}}\,dx'$. The limits transform as $x'=\sqrt{B} \l
eftrightarrow x=0$ and $x'\to\infty \leftrightarrow x\to\infty$. After this subs
titution, the integral becomes:

$$I = \int_{0}^{+\infty} e^{-A\sqrt{x^2+B}}\cos(Cx)\,dx$$

## Proof of the Upper Bound

**Step 1: Bound the cosine.** Since $|\cos(Cx)| \leq 1$ for all real $C$ and $x$
:

$$|I| \leq \int_{0}^{+\infty} e^{-A\sqrt{x^2+B}}\,dx$$

**Step 2: Bound the exponent.** For $x \geq 0$ and $B > 0$:

$$x^2 + B \geq x^2 \implies \sqrt{x^2+B} \geq x$$

Since $A > 0$, the exponential is decreasing:

$$e^{-A\sqrt{x^2+B}} \leq e^{-Ax}$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the resulting integral.**

$$|I| \leq \int_{0}^{+\infty} e^{-Ax}\,dx = \left[-\frac{1}{A}e^{-Ax}\right]_0^{
\infty} = \frac{1}{A}$$

**Step 4: Verify the bound is well-defined and tight.** The original integral co
nverges absolutely since $e^{-A\sqrt{x^2+B}} \leq e^{-Ax}$ and $\int_0^\infty e^
{-Ax}\,dx = \frac{1}{A} < \infty$. The bound is tight in the limit $B \to 0^+$,
$C \to 0$, where the integral approaches $\int_0^\infty e^{-Ax}\,dx = \frac{1}{A
}$.

**Exact value (for reference).** Using the known result $\int_0^\infty \frac{\co
s(cx)}{\sqrt{x^2+b^2}}e^{-a\sqrt{x^2+b^2}}\,dx = K_0\!\bigl(b\sqrt{a^2+c^2}\bigr
)$ and differentiating with respect to $a$ (using $K_0'(z) = -K_1(z)$):

$$I = \frac{A\sqrt{B}}{\sqrt{A^2+C^2}}\,K_1\!\Bigl(\sqrt{B(A^2+C^2)}\Bigr)$$

Since $K_1(z) > 0$ for $z > 0$, and using the asymptotic property $K_1(z) \leq \
frac{1}{z} + \frac{1}{2}$ (which confirms the integral is positive and bounded),
 the bound $\frac{1}{A}$ holds.

## Result

$$\boxed{|I| \leq \frac{1}{A}}$$
