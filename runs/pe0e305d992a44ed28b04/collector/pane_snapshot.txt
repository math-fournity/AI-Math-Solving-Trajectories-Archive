## Proof

**Setup.** Let $F = \mathbb{Q}$, let $\bar{F} = \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ be the algebra
ic closure of $\mathbb{Q}$ (the field of algebraic numbers), let $E = \mathbb{R}
$, and let $\alpha = \pi \in \mathbb{R}$, which is transcendental over $\mathbb{
Q}$ (by the Lindemann–Weierstrass theorem). Take $\sigma = \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{
Q}} : \mathbb{Q} \hookrightarrow \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, the natural inclusion.

**Claim.** There is no field monomorphism $\tau : \mathbb{Q}(\pi) \to \bar{\math
bb{Q}}$ extending $\sigma = \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{Q}}$.

**Key Lemma.** *Let $F$ be a field, $\alpha$ transcendental over $F$, and $\varp
hi : F(\alpha) \to K$ a field homomorphism extending a monomorphism $\sigma : F
\to K$. Then $\varphi(\alpha)$ is transcendental over $\sigma(F)$.*

*Proof of Lemma.* Suppose for contradiction that $\beta := \varphi(\alpha)$ is a
lgebraic over $\sigma(F)$. Then there exists a nonzero polynomial $p(x) = \sum_{
i=0}^{n} c_i x^i \in \sigma(F)[x]$ with $p(\beta) = 0$. Since $\sigma : F \to \s
igma(F)$ is an isomorphism, each $c_i = \sigma(a_i)$ for a unique $a_i \in F$, a
nd not all $a_i$ are zero. Set $q(x) = \sum_{i=0}^{n} a_i x^i \in F[x]$, which i
s nonzero. Since $\varphi$ extends $\sigma$, we have $\varphi(q(\alpha)) = \sum
\sigma(a_i)\,\varphi(\alpha)^i = \sum c_i \beta^i = p(\beta) = 0$. Since $\varph
i$ is injective (being a field homomorphism), $q(\alpha) = 0$. But $q$ is a nonz
ero polynomial over $F$ with $\alpha$ as a root, contradicting the transcendence
 of $\alpha$ over $F$. $\square$

**Applying the Lemma.** Suppose $\tau : \mathbb{Q}(\pi) \to \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ ex
tends $\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{Q}}$. By the lemma, $\tau(\pi)$ must be transcendent
al over $\sigma(\mathbb{Q}) = \mathbb{Q}$. But $\tau(\pi) \in \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$,
 and every element of $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$ by defin
ition of algebraic closure. This is a contradiction: no element of $\bar{\mathbb
{Q}}$ is transcendental over $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Conclusion.** No such extension $\tau$ exists. The example is:

$$F = \mathbb{Q},\quad \bar{F} = \bar{\mathbb{Q}},\quad E = \mathbb{R},\quad \al
pha = \pi \text{ (transcendental over } \mathbb{Q}\text{)},\quad \sigma = \mathr
m{id}_{\mathbb{Q}} : \mathbb{Q} \hookrightarrow \bar{\mathbb{Q}}.$$

The identity embedding $\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{Q}} : \mathbb{Q} \to \bar{\mathbb{Q
}}$ cannot be extended to any monomorphism $\mathbb{Q}(\pi) \to \bar{\mathbb{Q}}
$, because such an extension would require mapping $\pi$ to an element of $\bar{
\mathbb{Q}}$ transcendental over $\mathbb{Q}$, and no such element exists in the
 algebraic closure.
