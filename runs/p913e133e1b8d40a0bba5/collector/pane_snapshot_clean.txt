## Proof

Consider the map
$$\Phi:(0,\infty)\times\mathbb S^{n-1}\longrightarrow\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\},\
qquad (r,\omega)\mapsto r\omega.$$
This is a smooth diffeomorphism. We compute its Jacobian.

**Jacobian computation.** Fix $\omega_0\in\mathbb S^{n-1}$ and a local orthonorm
al chart $(u_1,\dots,u_{n-1})$ on $\mathbb S^{n-1}$ near $\omega_0$, so that $\o
mega=\omega(u)$ with $\partial_{u_i}\omega\perp\omega_0$ orthonormal at $\omega_
0$. The full parameterization is $x(r,u)=r\,\omega(u)$. Its partial derivatives
are
$$\partial_r x=\omega(u),\qquad \partial_{u_i}x=r\,\partial_{u_i}\omega.$$
At $\omega_0$ these $n$ vectors are mutually orthogonal: $\omega_0\perp\partial_
{u_i}\omega$ (tangent vs. normal to the sphere), and the $\partial_{u_i}\omega$
are orthonormal. Their lengths are $1$ and $r$ respectively, so the Jacobian det
erminant is
$$\left|\det D\Phi\right|=1\cdot r^{n-1}=r^{n-1}.$$
Hence the volume form transforms as
$$dx_1\cdots dx_n=r^{n-1}\,dr\,d\sigma(\omega),$$
where $d\sigma$ is the surface measure on $\mathbb S^{n-1}$. The change-of-varia
bles formula then gives, for every integrable $f$,
$$\int_{\mathbb R^n}f(x)\,dx=\int_0^\infty\!\int_{\mathbb S^{n-1}} f(r\omega)\,d
\sigma(\omega)\,r^{n-1}\,dr.$$

**The multiplicative constant is exactly $1$.** It is not $C(n)$ and not $C(n,\p
i)$.

- *Dependence on $n$?* The only $n$-dependence in the formula is the **exponent*
* $n-1$ in the Jacobian factor $r^{n-1}$ and the **dimension** of the sphere $\m
athbb S^{n-1}$ in the domain of integration. Neither is an overall multiplicativ
e constant in front of the right-hand side. So the constant does **not** depend
on $n$.

- *Dependence on $\pi$?* The number $\pi$ enters only when one *evaluates* the t
otal surface area
$$|\mathbb S^{n-1}|=\frac{2\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(n/2)},$$
but this is internal to the measure $d\sigma$ on $\mathbb S^{n-1}$; it is not a
prefactor multiplying the whole integral. The identity itself, as a statement re
lating $dx$ to $r^{n-1}dr\,d\sigma$, carries no $\pi$.

**Verification.** Take $f(x)=e^{-|x|^2}$, a Gaussian.
- LHS: $\int_{\mathbb R^n}e^{-|x|^2}dx=\pi^{n/2}$ (product of $n$ one-dimensiona
l Gaussians).
- RHS: $\int_0^\infty e^{-r^2}r^{n-1}dr\cdot|\mathbb S^{n-1}|=\tfrac12\Gamma(n/2
)\cdot\frac{2\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(n/2)}=\pi^{n/2}$.

LHS $=$ RHS with prefactor $1$, for every $n$. If a non-trivial constant $C(n,\p
i)$ were required, this equality would fail; it does not. This confirms the cons
tant is $1$.

**Conclusion.** The constant in the polar-coordinate identity is exactly $1$, in
dependent of both $n$ and $\pi$.
