## Answer

**Yes**, the equality still holds.

## Proof

The key observation is that since $Y_1, \dots, Y_n$ "depend on $X_1, \dots, X_n$
" — meaning each $Y_i$ is measurable with respect to $\sigma(X_1, \dots, X_n)$ —
 the sigma-algebra generated by the enlarged collection equals the original one:

$$\sigma(X_1, \dots, X_n, Y_1, \dots, Y_n) = \sigma(X_1, \dots, X_n).$$

Indeed, the inclusion "$\supseteq$" is trivial (the $X_i$ are among the generato
rs), and the inclusion "$\subseteq$" holds because each $Y_i$ is a measurable fu
nction of $X_1, \dots, X_n$, so $\sigma(Y_i) \subseteq \sigma(X_1, \dots, X_n)$
for every $i$.

Since conditional expectation depends only on the conditioning sigma-algebra, we
 immediately obtain

$$E\!\left(f(S_n + X_{n+1}) \mid X_1, \dots, X_n, Y_1, \dots, Y_n\right) = E\!\l
eft(f(S_n + X_{n+1}) \mid X_1, \dots, X_n\right).$$

By the hypothesis given in the problem, the right-hand side equals $f(S_n)$. Hen
ce

$$E\!\left(f(S_n + X_{n+1}) \mid X_1, \dots, X_n, Y_1, \dots, Y_n\right) = f(S_n
).$$

**Remark on the "independent of $X_{n+1}$" condition.** This condition is automa
tically satisfied: since each $Y_i$ is a measurable function of $X_1, \dots, X_n
$, and $(X_1, \dots, X_n)$ is independent of $X_{n+1}$ (by the i.i.d. assumption
), every $Y_i$ is independent of $X_{n+1}$. The condition is stated for emphasis
 but does not impose any additional restriction beyond what "depends on $X_1, \d
ots, X_n$" already entails. Even if one interpreted "depends on" more loosely (e
.g., $Y_i$ correlated with but not determined by $X_1,\dots,X_n$), the stated in
dependence from $X_{n+1}$ together with the tower property would still yield the
 same conclusion, since $S_n$ remains $\sigma(X_1,\dots,X_n)$-measurable and $X_
{n+1}$ remains independent of the enlarged sigma-algebra.
