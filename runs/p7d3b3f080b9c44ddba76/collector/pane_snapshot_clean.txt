Looking at this problem, I need to find the expected hyperbolic distance from th
e center of the Poincaré disk to a random geodesic (determined by two random ide
al boundary points), with curvature $-1$.

## Setup

The Poincaré disk has radius 1 (curvature $-1$). By rotational symmetry, fix one
 ideal point at $(1,0)$ and let the other be at angle $\theta$. The angular sepa
ration $\delta = \min(\theta, 2\pi - \theta)$ is uniform on $[0, \pi]$.

## Finding the geodesic

The geodesic through $(1,0)$ and $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ is an arc of a circl
e orthogonal to the unit circle. Let its center be $C = (a, b)$ with radius $r$.

**Orthogonality condition:** $|C|^2 = r^2 + 1$.

**Equidistance from $(1,0)$:** $(a-1)^2 + b^2 = r^2$.

Subtracting: $(a-1)^2 + b^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 1 \Rightarrow -2a + 1 = -1 \Rightarrow
 a = 1$.

**Equidistance from $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$:** $(1-\cos\theta)^2 + (b - \sin\
theta)^2 = b^2$, which gives:
$$b = \frac{1 - \cos\theta}{\sin\theta} = \tan(\theta/2)$$

So $C = (1, \tan(\theta/2))$, $r = |\tan(\theta/2)|$.

## Euclidean distance from center to geodesic

For $\theta \in (0, \pi)$, all quantities are positive. The closest point on the
 geodesic circle to the origin lies along line $OC$:

$$d_E = |OC| - r = \sec(\theta/2) - \tan(\theta/2) = \frac{1 - \sin(\theta/2)}{\
cos(\theta/2)}$$

The radial segment from $O$ to this closest point is perpendicular to the geodes
ic (since $O$, $P$, $C$ are collinear, $OP \perp$ tangent at $P$), and radial li
nes are geodesics in the Poincaré disk. So this is the perpendicular foot.

## Hyperbolic distance

The hyperbolic distance from the center to a point at Euclidean distance $\rho$
is $d_H = 2\,\text{artanh}(\rho) = \ln\!\left(\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}\right)$.

Computing the ratio with $d_E = \frac{1-\sin(\theta/2)}{\cos(\theta/2)}$ and usi
ng the half-angle substitution $t = \tan(\theta/4)$:

$$\frac{1 + d_E}{1 - d_E} = \frac{\cos(\theta/2) + 1 - \sin(\theta/2)}{\cos(\the
ta/2) - 1 + \sin(\theta/2)}$$

With $\sin(\theta/2) = \frac{2t}{1+t^2}$, $\cos(\theta/2) = \frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}$
:

- Numerator: $\frac{2(1-t)}{1+t^2}$
- Denominator: $\frac{2t(1-t)}{1+t^2}$

$$\frac{1+d_E}{1-d_E} = \frac{1}{t} = \cot(\theta/4)$$

Therefore:
$$d_H = \ln\!\left(\cot(\theta/4)\right)$$

**Verification:** At $\theta = \pi$ (antipodal points, diameter through center):
 $d_H = \ln(\cot(\pi/4)) = \ln(1) = 0$. ✓

## Computing the expectation

With $\theta$ uniform on $[0, \pi]$:

$$\mathbb{E}[d_H] = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi} \ln\!\left(\cot(\theta/4)\right)\,
d\theta$$

Substituting $u = \theta/4$:

$$\mathbb{E}[d_H] = \frac{4}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi/4} \ln(\cot u)\,du = -\frac{4}{\pi}
\int_0^{\pi/4} \ln(\tan u)\,du$$

## Evaluating the integral

Using the Fourier series:
$$\ln(\tan u) = -2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{\cos(2(2k+1)u)}{2k+1}$$

$$\int_0^{\pi/4} \ln(\tan u)\,du = -2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2k+1}\cdot\fra
c{\sin((2k+1)\pi/2)}{2(2k+1)} = -\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k}{(2k+1)^2} = -
G$$

where $G = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k}{(2k+1)^2} \approx 0.9159655941\ldot
s$ is **Catalan's constant**.

Therefore:
$$\int_0^{\pi/4} \ln(\cot u)\,du = G$$

## Result

$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}[d_H] = \frac{4G}{\pi}}$$

where $G$ is Catalan's constant. Numerically, $\frac{4G}{\pi} \approx 1.1662$.
