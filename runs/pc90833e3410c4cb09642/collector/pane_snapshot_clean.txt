## Proof

We are given the Lebesgue measure $\lambda$ on $(\mathbb{R}^2, \mathcal{A})$, an
d $A = X \times Y \in \mathcal{A}$ where $X \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ is non-measura
ble and $Y \in \{\emptyset, \{y\}\}$ for some $y \in \mathbb{R}$. We show that i
n both cases $\lambda(A) = 0$.

**Key fact (completeness of Lebesgue measure).** Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{R}
^n$ is *complete*: every subset of a $\lambda$-null set belongs to $\mathcal{A}$
 and has measure $0$. This follows from the definition of Lebesgue measure via o
uter measure — if $N$ is measurable with $\lambda(N)=0$ and $B \subseteq N$, the
n $\lambda^*(B) \le \lambda^*(N) = 0$, so $B$ is measurable with $\lambda(B)=0$.

**Case 1: $Y = \emptyset$.** Then $A = X \times \emptyset = \emptyset$. The empt
y set is measurable and $\lambda(\emptyset) = 0$.

**Case 2: $Y = \{y\}$ for some $y \in \mathbb{R}$.** Then $A = X \times \{y\}$.
Consider the horizontal line
$$L = \mathbb{R} \times \{y\} = \{(x, y) : x \in \mathbb{R}\}.$$
The set $L$ is closed in $\mathbb{R}^2$, hence $L \in \mathcal{A}$. Moreover, $L
$ is a $\lambda$-null set: covering $L$ by the rectangle $[-n, n] \times [y - \v
arepsilon, y + \varepsilon]$ for each $n$ and letting $\varepsilon \to 0$ shows
$\lambda(L) = 0$ (equivalently, $L$ is the graph of a constant function, which h
as zero $2$-dimensional measure).

Since $A = X \times \{y\} \subseteq \mathbb{R} \times \{y\} = L$ and $L$ is a nu
ll set, by completeness of Lebesgue measure, $A \in \mathcal{A}$ and
$$\lambda(A) = 0.$$

Note that the non-measurability of $X \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ is irrelevant here:
although $X$ is not Lebesgue measurable in $\mathbb{R}^1$, the product $X \times
 \{y\}$ sits inside a null set of $\mathbb{R}^2$, and completeness forces it to
be measurable with measure zero. This is consistent with the hypothesis $A \in \
mathcal{A}$.

**Conclusion.** In both cases ($Y = \emptyset$ and $Y = \{y\}$), the Lebesgue me
asure of $A = X \times Y$ is **identically zero**. The set $A$ is measurable (as
 a subset of a null set), and $\lambda(A) = 0$.
