## Proof

By the Artin–Wedderburn theorem, every finite-dimensional central simple algebra
 $A$ over a field $F$ is isomorphic to $M_n(D)$ for a unique integer $n \geq 1$
and a unique (up to isomorphism) central division algebra $D$ over $F$. Moreover
, the dimension satisfies

$$
\dim_F A = n^2 \cdot \dim_F D. \tag{1}
$$

Since $D$ is a central division algebra over $F$, its dimension over $F$ is a pe
rfect square: $\dim_F D = d^2$, where $d = \operatorname{ind}(D)$ is the index (
degree) of $D$. Substituting into (1) with $\dim_F A = 9$ gives

$$
n^2 d^2 = 9 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad (nd)^2 = 9 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad nd =
3.
$$

Since $n$ and $d$ are positive integers, the only factorizations of $3$ are:

**Case 1: $n = 1$, $d = 3$.** Then $\dim_F D = 9$, and $A \cong M_1(D) \cong D$,
 where $D$ is a central division algebra over $F$ of degree $3$ (a "ternary" or
degree-$3$ division algebra). This case occurs whenever $F$ admits a central div
ision algebra of degree $3$ (e.g., $F = \mathbb{Q}$, or any field with $\operato
rname{Br}(F)$ containing an element of order $3$).

**Case 2: $n = 3$, $d = 1$.** Then $\dim_F D = 1$, so $D = F$, and $A \cong M_3(
F)$. This is the split case, which always occurs (for instance, $A = M_3(F)$ its
elf).

No other factorizations are possible since $3$ is prime. Both cases are consiste
nt: the split algebra $M_3(F)$ realizes Case 2 over every field, and Case 1 is r
ealized precisely over those fields whose Brauer group contains an element of or
der $3$ (equivalently, of degree $3$).

**Conclusion.** The possible values are:

| $n$ | $D$ |
|-----|-----|
| $1$ | a central division algebra over $F$ with $\dim_F D = 9$ (degree $3$) |
| $3$ | $D = F$ (the split case) |

These are the only two possibilities, and they are mutually exclusive: $A$ is ei
ther a degree-$3$ central division algebra itself ($n=1$), or it splits as $M_3(
F)$ ($n=3$, $D=F$).
