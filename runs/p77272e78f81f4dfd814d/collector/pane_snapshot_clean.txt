Looking at this problem, I need to find the two smallest primes $p \geq 23$ such
 that $8^n + p$ is composite for every $n \geq 1$.

## Key Observation

Since $8 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$, we have $8^n \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$ for all $n \geq 1$.
 Therefore, if $p \equiv 6 \pmod{7}$ (i.e., $p \equiv -1 \pmod{7}$), then $7 \mi
d (8^n + p)$ for all $n$, and since $8^n + p \geq 8 + 23 = 31 > 7$, the number i
s always composite.

**$p = 29$:** $n=1$: $8 + 29 = 37$ (prime). **Fails.**

**$p = 31$:** $n=1$: $39 = 3\times13$ ✓; $n=2$: $95 = 5\times19$ ✓; $n=3$: $543

**$p = 37$:** $n=1$: $45 = 9\times5$ ✓; $n=2$: $64 + 37 = 101$ (prime, since $\s

**$p = 41$:** Since $41 = 5\times7 + 6$, we have $41 \equiv 6 \pmod{7}$. By the

## Finding the second smallest

**$p = 43$:** $n=2$: $64 + 43 = 107$ (prime). **Fails.**

**$p = 47$:** I establish a covering system over all $n$:

- **Even $n$:** $8 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$, so $8^n \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$ for even $n$.
Since $47 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$, we get $8^n + 47 \equiv 1+2 = 3 \equiv 0 \pmod{3}$

- **$n \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$:** Write $n = 4k+1$. Then $8^n = 8 \cdot (8^4)^k = 8 \
