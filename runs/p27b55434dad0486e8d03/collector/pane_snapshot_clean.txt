Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether there exists a critical (mi
nimal/irredundant) covering of ω where every member is an infinite set.

**Definitions.** A *covering* of ω is a family ℱ of proper subsets of ω with ⋃ℱ
= ω. A covering is *critical* (irredundant/minimal) if for every A ∈ ℱ, ⋃(ℱ \ {A
}) ≠ ω — equivalently, each member A has a *private element* (a witness) belongi
ng to A and to no other member of ℱ.

---

**Theorem.** *There exists a critical covering of ω consisting only of infinite
sets.*

**Proof.**

We construct one explicitly. Let

$$Q = \{2n+1 : n \in \omega\} = \{1, 3, 5, 7, \dots\}$$

be the set of odd natural numbers. For each $n \in \omega$, define

$$A_n = \{2n\} \cup Q.$$

Set $\mathcal{F} = \{A_n : n \in \omega\}$.

We verify that ℱ is a critical covering of ω consisting only of infinite sets.

**(1) Each Aₙ is an infinite proper subset of ω.**
Since $Q \subseteq A_n$ and $Q$ is infinite, $A_n$ is infinite. Since $2m \notin
 A_n$ for every $m \neq n$ (the element $2m$ is even, hence not in $Q$, and $2m
\neq 2n$), we have $A_n \neq \omega$.

**(2) ℱ covers ω.**
$$\bigcup_{n \in \omega} A_n \;=\; Q \;\cup\; \{2n : n \in \omega\} \;=\; Q \cup
 P \;=\; \omega,$$
where $P = \{0, 2, 4, 6, \dots\}$ is the set of even numbers.

**(3) ℱ is critical (irredundant).**
For each $n \in \omega$, the element $2n$ belongs to $A_n$ but to **no other** m
ember of ℱ:
- $2n \notin Q$, since $2n$ is even.
- $2n \notin A_m$ for $m \neq n$, since $A_m = \{2m\} \cup Q$ and $2n \neq 2m$.

Thus $2n$ is a private element (witness) for $A_n$, and

$$\bigcup\bigl(\mathcal{F} \setminus \{A_n\}\bigr) = \omega \setminus \{2n\} \ne
q \omega.$$

So removing any single member destroys the covering property.

**(4) All members are infinite.**
Each $A_n$ contains the infinite set $Q$.

**Conclusion.** ℱ = {Aₙ : n ∈ ω} is a critical covering of ω consisting only of
infinite sets. ∎

---

**Remark.** An even simpler example is the two-element partition ℱ = {P, Q} wher
e P is the evens and Q is the odds. This is trivially critical (every element is
 a private witness for its part) and both members are infinite. The countable co
nstruction above has the additional property that all pairwise intersections are
 infinite (Aₙ ∩ Aₘ = Q for n ≠ m), making it robust under stronger interpretatio
ns of "critical."
