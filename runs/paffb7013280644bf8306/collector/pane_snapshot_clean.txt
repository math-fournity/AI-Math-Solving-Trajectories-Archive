## Solution

Work in the affine chart $z \neq 0$, dehomogenizing by $Z = 1$. The point $p = [
-2:1:4]$ becomes $(a,b) = (-1/2,\, 1/4)$.

The dehomogenized curves are
$$f = x^2 - y, \qquad g = (x+1)^2 - y.$$

**Verification that $p$ lies on both:**
$$f(-1/2,1/4) = 1/4 - 1/4 = 0, \qquad g(-1/2,1/4) = (1/2)^2 - 1/4 = 0. \checkmar
k$$

**Translate to the origin:** set $u = x - a = x + 1/2$, $v = y - b = y - 1/4$, s
o $x = u - 1/2$, $y = v + 1/4$. Substituting:

$$f = (u-\tfrac12)^2 - (v+\tfrac14) = u^2 - u + \tfrac14 - v - \tfrac14 = u^2 -
u - v,$$

$$g = (u-\tfrac12+1)^2 - (v+\tfrac14) = (u+\tfrac12)^2 - v - \tfrac14 = u^2 + u
+ \tfrac14 - v - \tfrac14 = u^2 + u - v.$$

**Compute the local intersection multiplicity** $I_p(f,g) = \dim_k k[[u,v]]/(f,g
)$.

In the quotient ring, subtract:
$$g - f = (u^2 + u - v) - (u^2 - u - v) = 2u.$$

Since $\operatorname{char} k = 0$, we get $u \in (f,g)$, hence $u^2 \in (f,g)$.
From $f = u^2 - u - v$ and $u, u^2 \in (f,g)$, it follows that $v \in (f,g)$. Th
us $(u,v) \subseteq (f,g)$.

Conversely, both $f = u^2 - u - v$ and $g = u^2 + u - v$ lie in $(u,v)$ (they ha
ve zero constant term), so $(f,g) \subseteq (u,v)$.

Therefore $(f,g) = (u,v)$ in $k[[u,v]]$, and
$$I_p(f,g) = \dim_k k[[u,v]]/(u,v) = 1.$$

**Sanity check (transversality):** Both curves are smooth at $p$:
- $\nabla f = (2x,\,-z,\,-y)$ at $p = (-2,1,4)$ gives $(-4,-4,-1) \neq 0$.
- $\nabla g = (2(x+z),\,-z,\,2(x+z)-y)$ at $p$ gives $(4,-4,3) \neq 0$.

Two smooth curves meeting with intersection multiplicity $1$ are transverse, con
sistent with our computation.
