## Proof

ically around the table, and let $c_i$ denote the country of $p_i$ (indices mod
$n^2$). The left neighbor of $p_i$ is $p_{i-1}$ and the right neighbor is $p_{i+
1}$.

**Step 1: The country sequence is a de Bruijn sequence.**

The condition states: for any two distinct people $p_i, p_j$ from the same count
ry ($c_i = c_j$, $i \neq j$), their left neighbors are from different countries
($c_{i-1} \neq c_{j-1}$).

Since each country has exactly $n$ people and there are $n$ countries, for each
country $A$, the $n$ left-neighbor countries $\{c_{i-1} : c_i = A\}$ are $n$ dis
tinct values — hence a permutation of all $n$ countries. Therefore each ordered
pair $(B, A)$ appears exactly once among the consecutive pairs $(c_{i-1}, c_i)$.
 This means the cyclic sequence $(c_0, c_1, \ldots, c_{n^2-1})$ is a **de Bruijn
 sequence of order 2** over $n$ symbols: every ordered pair of countries appears
 exactly once as a consecutive pair.

**Step 2: Reformulating the objective.**

We want to maximize the number of indices $i$ with $c_{i-1} = c_{i+1}$. Setting
$A = c_{i-1}$ and $B = c_i$, the condition $c_{i+1} = A$ means the consecutive p
air $(A, B)$ is immediately followed by $(B, A)$ in the de Bruijn sequence.

- If $A = B$: this requires pair $(A,A)$ followed by $(A,A)$, impossible since e
ach pair appears once.
- If $A \neq B$: this is a **reversal** — the edge $(A,B)$ is immediately follow
ed by $(B,A)$ in the Eulerian circuit of the complete directed graph $K_n^*$ (wi
th self-loops) on $n$ vertices.

So our goal is to maximize the number of reversals in an Eulerian circuit of $K_
n^*$.

**Step 3: Upper bound.**

Each reversal uses both directed edges $(A,B)$ and $(B,A)$ for some unordered pa
ir $\{A,B\}$. Since each edge appears once, each unordered pair $\{A,B\}$ contri
butes **at most one** reversal. The $n$ self-loops $(A,A)$ can never participate
 in a reversal.

Suppose we have $r$ reversals, using $2r$ non-self-loop edges. The remaining $n(
n-1) - 2r$ non-self-loop edges are "singles." For each unordered pair $\{A,B\}$,
 either both edges form a reversal or both remain as singles (since a reversal c
onsumes both). Let $s = \binom{n}{2} - r$ be the number of non-reversal unordere
d pairs, so $2s$ non-self-loop edges remain.

**Contract each reversal pair** $(A,B),(B,A)$ into a single loop at vertex $A$.
The contracted graph has:
- Loops at each vertex (from self-loops and contracted reversals),
- Bidirectional edges from the $s$ non-reversal pairs.

For an Eulerian circuit to exist in the contracted graph, it must be **connected
** (all edges in one component). The loops contribute nothing to connectivity, s
o the $s$ non-reversal pairs must connect all $n$ vertices. This requires:

$$s \geq n - 1.$$

Therefore:
$$r = \binom{n}{2} - s \leq \binom{n}{2} - (n-1) = \frac{n(n-1)}{2} - (n-1) = \f
rac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2} = \binom{n-1}{2}.$$

**Step 4: Achieving the bound.**

Set $s = n - 1$ and choose the $s$ non-reversal pairs to form a **spanning tree*
* $T$ on the $n$ vertices. The remaining $\binom{n}{2} - (n-1) = \binom{n-1}{2}$
 unordered pairs each form a reversal.

**Degree check:** In the contracted graph, each vertex $A$ has:
- $r_A + 1$ loops (where $r_A$ = reversals based at $A$, plus the self-loop),
- Non-loop out-edges: $n - 1 - r_A$ (edges to non-tree neighbors, both direction
s present from tree edges).

Total out-degree at $A$: $(r_A + 1) + (n - 1 - r_A) = n$. By symmetry, in-degree
 $= n$ as well. ✓

**Connectivity:** The tree $T$ connects all $n$ vertices, so the contracted grap
h is connected. ✓

Since the contracted graph is connected with equal in/out degrees, an Eulerian c
ircuit exists. Expanding each contracted loop back into its reversal pair $(A,B)
,(B,A)$ yields a valid Eulerian circuit of $K_n^*$, i.e., a valid de Bruijn sequ
ence, with exactly $\binom{n-1}{2}$ reversals.

**Step 5: Verification for small cases.**
- $n = 2$: $\binom{1}{2} = 0$. The de Bruijn sequences $(1,1,2,2)$ and $(1,2,2,1
)$ both give 0 matches. ✓
- $n = 3$: $\binom{2}{2} = 1$. Using spanning tree $\{\{1,2\},\{1,3\}\}$ with re
versal on $\{2,3\}$, the sequence $(1,1,2,2,3,2,1,3,3)$ gives exactly 1 match (a

**Conclusion.** The maximum number of people whose left and right neighbors belo

$$\boxed{\dfrac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}}$$
