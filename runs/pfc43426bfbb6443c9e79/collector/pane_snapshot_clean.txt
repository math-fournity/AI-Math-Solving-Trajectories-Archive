## Locating the small circle

The circle of radius t is tangent to the x-axis (one leg), the hypotenuse x+y=a,

Since it's tangent to the x-axis, its center is (h, t) for some h.

**Tangency to hypotenuse:** The center lies on the origin side of x+y=a, so:
(a − h − t)/√2 = t → h = a − t(1+√2).

**Tangency to incircle (external):** Distance from (h, t) to (r, r) equals r + t

Compute h − r = a − t(1+√2) − r = r(2+√2) − t(1+√2) − r = (1+√2)(r − t).

And t − r = −(r − t).

So the distance squared is:
$$[(1+\sqrt{2})^2 + 1](r-t)^2 = (4+2\sqrt{2})(r-t)^2.$$

Setting distance = r + t:
$$\sqrt{4+2\sqrt{2}}\;(r - t) = r + t.$$

Let k = √(4+2√2). Then k(r−t) = r+t, giving:
$$t = r\cdot\frac{k-1}{k+1}, \qquad rt = r^2\cdot\frac{k-1}{k+1}.$$
