## Proof

up $G'$ is periodic (torsion) and abelian. Let $x \in G'$ and let $x^G$ denote t
he normal closure of $x$ in $G$.

**Key observation: the action factors through $G/G'$.** Since $G'$ is abelian, c
onjugation by any element of $G'$ acts trivially on $G'$. Therefore the conjugat
ion action of $G$ on $G'$ factors through $G/G'$. Concretely, for $g \in G$ and
$y \in G'$, the conjugate $g^{-1}yg$ depends only on the coset $gG'$.

**The normal closure is abelian.** Since $G' \trianglelefteq G$ and $x \in G'$,
every conjugate $g^{-1}xg$ lies in $G'$. Thus $x^G \leq G'$. Since $G'$ is abeli
an, $x^G$ is abelian.

**The exponent of $x^G$ equals the order of $x$.** Write $|x| = n$ (finite since
 $G'$ is periodic). I claim $\exp(x^G) = n$.

*Lower bound:* Since $x \in x^G$, we have $\exp(x^G) \geq |x| = n$.

*Upper bound:* Every element of $x^G$ is a finite product of conjugates of $x$ a
nd their inverses:
$$y = \prod_{i=1}^{k} (g_i^{-1} x g_i)^{\epsilon_i}, \qquad \epsilon_i \in \{+1,
 -1\}.$$
Since $x^G$ is abelian, raising $y$ to the $n$-th power distributes:
$$y^n = \prod_{i=1}^{k} (g_i^{-1} x g_i)^{n \epsilon_i}.$$
For each factor, $(g_i^{-1} x g_i)^n = g_i^{-1} x^n g_i = g_i^{-1} \cdot 1 \cdot
 g_i = 1$, using $|x| = n$. Hence $y^n = 1$ for every $y \in x^G$, giving $\exp(
x^G) \leq n$.

Combining both bounds: $\exp(x^G) = |x|$.

**$G'$ has finite exponent (so the maximum is well-defined).** Since $G$ is fini

athbb{Z}^r \times T$ with $T$ finite. The commutator subgroup $G'$ is generated

i < \infty$, then $n_i$ kills the entire $R$-submodule $R \cdot m_i$ (by the sam

1, \ldots, n_k) < \infty$.

$$\boxed{\exp(G') = \operatorname{lcm}\{\,|g| : g \in G'\,\}}$$
