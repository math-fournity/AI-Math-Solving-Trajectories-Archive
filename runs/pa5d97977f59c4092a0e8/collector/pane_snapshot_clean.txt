## Proof

Let $W_\nu$ be a one-form (a $(0,1)$ tensor) on a manifold with coordinates $\{x
^\mu\}$. Under a coordinate transformation $x^\mu \mapsto x^{\mu'}$, a one-form
transforms by the pullback rule

$$
W_{\nu'} \;=\; \frac{\partial x^\nu}{\partial x^{\nu'}}\, W_\nu .
$$

A $(0,2)$ tensor $T_{\mu\nu}$ would transform as

$$
T_{\mu'\nu'} \;=\; \frac{\partial x^\mu}{\partial x^{\mu'}}\frac{\partial x^\nu}
{\partial x^{\nu'}}\, T_{\mu\nu}.
$$

We now compute how the partial derivative $\partial_{\mu'} W_{\nu'}$ transforms
and check whether it matches this law.

### Computing $\partial_{\mu'} W_{\nu'}$

$$
\frac{\partial W_{\nu'}}{\partial x^{\mu'}}
\;=\;
\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu'}}
\left(
\frac{\partial x^\nu}{\partial x^{\nu'}}\, W_\nu
\right).
$$

Applying the product rule, and using the chain rule $\frac{\partial}{\partial x^
{\mu'}} = \frac{\partial x^\mu}{\partial x^{\mu'}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^\mu
}$ on the factor $W_\nu$:

$$
\frac{\partial W_{\nu'}}{\partial x^{\mu'}}
\;=\;
\frac{\partial x^\nu}{\partial x^{\nu'}}\,
\frac{\partial x^\mu}{\partial x^{\mu'}}\,
\frac{\partial W_\nu}{\partial x^\mu}
\;+\;
\frac{\partial^2 x^\nu}{\partial x^{\mu'}\,\partial x^{\nu'}}\, W_\nu .
$$

### Comparison with the $(0,2)$ tensor transformation law

The first term,

$$
\frac{\partial x^\mu}{\partial x^{\mu'}}\frac{\partial x^\nu}{\partial x^{\nu'}}
\,\frac{\partial W_\nu}{\partial x^\mu},
$$

is exactly what we would get if $\partial_\mu W_\nu$ were a genuine $(0,2)$ tens
or.

The second term,

$$
\boxed{\;\frac{\partial^2 x^\nu}{\partial x^{\mu'}\,\partial x^{\nu'}}\, W_\nu\;
}
$$

is an **extra inhomogeneous term**. It involves the **second partial derivatives
** of the old coordinates with respect to the new ones, weighted by the one-form
 components $W_\nu$.

### Why this spoils tensorial character

For $\partial_\mu W_\nu$ to be a $(0,2)$ tensor, the transformation would have t
o be purely homogeneous:

$$
(\partial_{\mu'} W_{\nu'}) \;\stackrel{?}{=}\; \frac{\partial x^\mu}{\partial x^
{\mu'}}\frac{\partial x^\nu}{\partial x^{\nu'}}\,(\partial_\mu W_\nu).
$$

This holds **only if** the extra term vanishes:

$$
\frac{\partial^2 x^\nu}{\partial x^{\mu'}\,\partial x^{\nu'}}\, W_\nu \;=\; 0
$$

for all one-forms $W_\nu$ and all coordinate transformations. This requires

$$
\frac{\partial^2 x^\nu}{\partial x^{\mu'}\,\partial x^{\nu'}} \;=\; 0
$$

for every $\nu, \mu', \nu'$, i.e., the coordinate transformation must be **globa
lly affine** (linear: $x^\nu = A^{\nu}{}_{\nu'}\, x^{\nu'} + b^\nu$).

For a **general** (nonlinear) coordinate transformation, the second derivatives
do not vanish. A concrete counterexample: take a one-form $W_\nu = \delta_\nu^{\
,0}$ (constant) on $\mathbb{R}^2$ with coordinates $(x^0, x^1)$, and consider th
e nonlinear change $x^0 = (x^{0'})^2$, $x^1 = x^{1'}$. Then

$$
\frac{\partial^2 x^0}{\partial (x^{0'})^2} = 2 \neq 0,
$$

so the extra term equals $2\,W_0 = 2 \neq 0$, while the homogeneous (tensor) par
t is zero (since $W_\nu$ is constant, $\partial_\mu W_\nu = 0$). Thus

$$
\partial_{\mu'} W_{\nu'} \;\neq\; \frac{\partial x^\mu}{\partial x^{\mu'}}\frac{
\partial x^\nu}{\partial x^{\nu'}}\,\partial_\mu W_\nu,
$$

confirming the failure of the tensor transformation law.

### Conclusion

The partial derivative of a one-form transforms as

$$
\partial_{\mu'} W_{\nu'}
\;=\;
\frac{\partial x^\mu}{\partial x^{\mu'}}\frac{\partial x^\nu}{\partial x^{\nu'}}
\,\partial_\mu W_\nu
\;+\;
\frac{\partial^2 x^\nu}{\partial x^{\mu'}\,\partial x^{\nu'}}\, W_\nu .
$$

The presence of the inhomogeneous second-derivative term — which is nonzero for
general (nonlinear) coordinate transformations — means $\partial_\mu W_\nu$ does
 **not** transform as a $(0,2)$ tensor. Therefore, the partial derivative of a t
ensor is not, in general, a tensor. This is precisely why the **covariant deriva
tive** $\nabla_\mu$ (with Christoffel correction terms designed to cancel the of
fending second-derivative piece) is introduced in order to obtain a derivative o
peration that does produce a genuine tensor.
