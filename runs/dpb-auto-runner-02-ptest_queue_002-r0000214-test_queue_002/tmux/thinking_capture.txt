Proof that for any prime p > 3, p² − 1 is divisible by 24.

We need to show 24 = 8 · 3 divides p² − 1 = (p−1)(p+1). Since gcd(8, 3) = 1, it
suffices to prove separately that 8 | (p²−1) and 3 | (p²−1).

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Part 1: 8 | (p² − 1).

Since p > 3 is prime, p is odd. Every odd integer can be written as p = 2k + 1
for some integer k ≥ 2. Then:

p² − 1 = (2k+1)² − 1 = 4k² + 4k = 4k(k+1).

Now k and k+1 are consecutive integers, so one of them is even. Therefore k(k+1)
is even, i.e., k(k+1) = 2m for some integer m. Substituting:

p² − 1 = 4 · 2m = 8m.

Hence 8 | (p² − 1). ∎

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Part 2: 3 | (p² − 1).

Since p > 3 is prime, p is not divisible by 3 (the only prime divisible by 3 is
3 itself). So p ≢ 0 (mod 3), which means p ≡ 1 (mod 3) or p ≡ 2 (mod 3).

  • Case p ≡ 1 (mod 3): p² − 1 ≡ 1² − 1 = 0 (mod 3).
  • Case p ≡ 2 (mod 3): p² − 1 ≡ 4 − 1 = 3 ≡ 0 (mod 3).

In both cases, p² − 1 ≡ 0 (mod 3), so 3 | (p² − 1). ∎

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Conclusion.

We have shown 8 | (p² − 1) and 3 | (p² − 1). Since gcd(8, 3) = 1, it follows
that 8 · 3 = 24 divides p² − 1.

Therefore, for every prime p > 3, p² − 1 is divisible by 24. ∎
