# Legendre–Fenchel Transform (Convex Conjugate)

## 1. Definition

Let $f: \mathbb{R}^{D} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be a convex function. Its **Legen
dre–Fenchel transform** (or **convex conjugate**) is the function

$$
f^{*}(y) \;:=\; \sup_{x \in \mathbb{R}^{D}} \bigl\{ \langle y, x \rangle - f(x)
\bigr\}, \qquad y \in \mathbb{R}^{D},
$$

where $\langle y, x \rangle = y^{\top} x$ is the standard Euclidean inner produc
t.

**Geometric meaning.** For each $y$, the value $f^{*}(y)$ is the smallest vertic
al intercept at the origin of an affine hyperplane with slope $y$ that lies ever
ywhere above the graph of $f$. Equivalently, the affine function $x \mapsto \lan
gle y, x\rangle - f^{*}(y)$ is the **tightest upper support** of $f$ with slope
$y$.

## 2. Basic properties (no differentiability needed)

**Convexity of $f^{*}$.** For any $y_{1}, y_{2}$ and $\lambda \in [0,1]$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{*}(\lambda y_{1} + (1-\lambda) y_{2})
&= \sup_{x}\bigl\{\langle \lambda y_{1} + (1-\lambda)y_{2},\, x\rangle - f(x)\bi
gr\} \\
&= \sup_{x}\bigl\{\lambda[\langle y_{1},x\rangle - f(x)] + (1-\lambda)[\langle y
_{2},x\rangle - f(x)]\bigr\} \\
&\le \lambda \sup_{x}[\langle y_{1},x\rangle - f(x)] + (1-\lambda)\sup_{x}[\lang
le y_{2},x\rangle - f(x)] \\
&= \lambda f^{*}(y_{1}) + (1-\lambda) f^{*}(y_{2}).
\end{aligned}
$$

So $f^{*}$ is **always convex**, regardless of whether $f$ is differentiable. (I
t is a supremum of affine functions of $y$.)

**Lower-semicontinuity and the biconjugate.** One always has $f^{**}(x) \le f(x)
$, where $f^{**} = (f^{*})^{*}$. The equality $f^{**} = f$ holds **iff** $f$ is
convex and lower-semicontinuous (Fenchel–Moreau theorem). Since we assume $f$ co
nvex and finite everywhere on $\mathbb{R}^{D}$, it is continuous, hence l.s.c.,
so

$$
f^{**} = f.
$$

## 3. Simplification for convex differentiable $f$

Now assume $f$ is **convex and differentiable** on $\mathbb{R}^{D}$.

### 3.1 First-order optimality

For fixed $y$, the function $\varphi_{y}(x) := \langle y, x\rangle - f(x)$ is co
ncave (since $-f$ is concave). Its supremum is attained (when finite) at any poi
nt $x^{*}$ satisfying the stationarity condition

$$
\nabla_{x}\,\varphi_{y}(x)\big|_{x=x^{*}} = 0 \;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\; y = \n
abla f(x^{*}).
$$

Because $f$ is convex and differentiable, $\nabla f$ is a **monotone** map. If $
\nabla f$ is invertible (e.g. $f$ strictly convex), the maximizer is unique:

$$
x^{*} = (\nabla f)^{-1}(y).
$$

### 3.2 Closed-form expression

Substituting the maximizer back into the definition:

$$
\boxed{\; f^{*}(y) \;=\; \bigl\langle y,\, x^{*}\bigr\rangle - f(x^{*}), \qquad
\text{where } y = \nabla f(x^{*}). \;}
$$

This is the **classical Legendre transform**. Thus:

> *For a convex differentiable function, the Legendre–Fenchel transform reduces
to the Legendre transform: invert the gradient map $\nabla f$, then evaluate $\l
angle y, x^{*}\rangle - f(x^{*})$.*

### 3.3 Inverse-gradient (duality) relations

From $y = \nabla f(x^{*})$ and $f^{*}(y) = \langle y, x^{*}\rangle - f(x^{*})$,
differentiating $f^{*}$ w.r.t. $y$ gives

$$
\nabla f^{*}(y) = x^{*} + \Bigl[\nabla_{y}\langle y, x^{*}(y)\rangle^{\text{via
}x^{*}}\Bigr] - \nabla f(x^{*})\cdot \frac{\partial x^{*}}{\partial y}.
$$

The last two terms cancel (since $\nabla f(x^{*}) = y$), leaving

$$
\boxed{\;\nabla f^{*}(y) = x^{*} = (\nabla f)^{-1}(y).\;}
$$

So the gradient maps are mutual inverses:

$$
\nabla f^{*} = (\nabla f)^{-1}, \qquad \nabla f = (\nabla f^{*})^{-1}.
$$

### 3.4 Second-order relation

When $f$ is twice differentiable with invertible Hessian $\nabla^{2} f(x^{*}) \s
ucc 0$, differentiating $\nabla f^{*}(y) = x^{*}$ and using $y = \nabla f(x^{*})
$:

$$
\nabla^{2} f^{*}(y) = \frac{\partial x^{*}}{\partial y} = \bigl[\nabla^{2} f(x^{
*})\bigr]^{-1}.
$$

The Hessians are **pointwise inverse** (evaluated at conjugate points).

## 4. Worked illustration: quadratic

Let $f(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}$. Then $\nabla f(x) = x$, so $x^{*} = y$ and

$$
f^{*}(y) = \langle y, y\rangle - \tfrac{1}{2}\|y\|^{2} = \tfrac{1}{2}\|y\|^{2} =
 f(y).
$$

The quadratic $\tfrac12\|x\|^{2}$ is **self-conjugate**, consistent with $\nabla
 f^{*} = (\nabla f)^{-1} = \mathrm{id}$.

## 5. Summary

| Setting | Formula |
|---|---|
| General convex $f$ | $f^{*}(y) = \sup_{x}\{\langle y,x\rangle - f(x)\}$ (alway
s convex) |
| Convex differentiable $f$ | $f^{*}(y) = \langle y, x^{*}\rangle - f(x^{*})$, w
ith $y = \nabla f(x^{*})$ |
| Gradient duality | $\nabla f^{*} = (\nabla f)^{-1}$ |
| Hessian duality | $\nabla^{2} f^{*}(y) = [\nabla^{2} f(x^{*})]^{-1}$ |
| Biconjugate | $f^{**} = f$ (convex + l.s.c.) |

The Legendre–Fenchel transform generalizes the classical Legendre transform: whe
n $f$ is convex and differentiable (with invertible gradient), the supremum is a
ttained at the unique point where the gradient equals $y$, and the transform col
lapses to the familiar Legendre formula with the inverse-gradient duality $\nabl
a f^{*} = (\nabla f)^{-1}$.
