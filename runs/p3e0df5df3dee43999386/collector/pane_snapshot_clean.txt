## Proof

**Answer: Yes, $T$ is necessarily bounded.**

Let $V$ be a Hilbert space with inner product $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$ and
 induced norm $\|\cdot\|$. Suppose $T: V \to V$ is linear and there exists a con
stant $C$ such that

$$|\langle T(v), w \rangle| \leq C\|v\|\|w\| \quad \text{for all } v, w \in V.$$

Fix an arbitrary $v \in V$. Consider the map $\varphi_v : V \to \mathbb{F}$ (whe
re $\mathbb{F}$ is the scalar field) defined by

$$\varphi_v(w) = \langle T(v), w \rangle.$$

By linearity of the inner product in its second argument (or first, depending on
 convention), $\varphi_v$ is a linear functional on $V$. The given hypothesis sh
ows that $\varphi_v$ is bounded, with

$$|\varphi_v(w)| = |\langle T(v), w \rangle| \leq C\|v\|\|w\|.$$

Hence the operator norm of $\varphi_v$ satisfies

$$\|\varphi_v\| = \sup_{\|w\| = 1} |\varphi_v(w)| \leq C\|v\|.$$

Now we invoke the **Riesz representation theorem**: every bounded linear functio
nal $f$ on a Hilbert space $V$ is of the form $f(w) = \langle u, w \rangle$ for
a unique $u \in V$, and moreover $\|f\| = \|u\|$.

Applying this to $\varphi_v$, the unique representing vector is precisely $u = T
(v)$, since $\varphi_v(w) = \langle T(v), w \rangle$ for all $w$. Therefore

$$\|T(v)\| = \|\varphi_v\| \leq C\|v\|.$$

Since $v \in V$ was arbitrary, we have shown

$$\|T(v)\| \leq C\|v\| \quad \text{for all } v \in V,$$

which is exactly the definition of $T$ being a bounded (continuous) linear opera
tor, with operator norm $\|T\| \leq C$.

**Remark on completeness.** The Riesz representation theorem requires $V$ to be
complete (i.e., a Hilbert space, not merely a pre-Hilbert space). This is precis
ely why the hypothesis that $V$ is a Hilbert space is essential. On an incomplet
e inner product space, a bounded linear functional need not be representable by
a vector in the space, and the argument above would not go through. Thus the com
pleteness of $V$ is the key ingredient that guarantees $T$ is bounded.
