**No, it is not possible.** Every finite field has order equal to a power of a p
rime. Here is the justification.

---

**Proof.**

Let $F$ be a finite field, and let $|F| = q$ denote its order (the number of its
 elements).

**Step 1: The characteristic is a prime $p$.**

Since $F$ is finite, the sequence of repeated sums of the multiplicative identit
y,
$$1,\quad 1+1,\quad 1+1+1,\quad \dots$$
cannot all be distinct. Hence there exist positive integers $m < n$ with $m\cdot
 1 = n\cdot 1$, so $(n-m)\cdot 1 = 0$. Let $p$ be the **smallest** positive inte
ger with $p\cdot 1 = 0$. This $p$ is the *characteristic* of $F$.

We claim $p$ is prime. Indeed, if $p = ab$ with $1 < a, b < p$, then
$$(a\cdot 1)(b\cdot 1) = (ab)\cdot 1 = p\cdot 1 = 0.$$
Since $F$ is a field (hence an integral domain), this forces $a\cdot 1 = 0$ or $
b\cdot 1 = 0$, contradicting the minimality of $p$. Therefore $p$ is prime.

**Step 2: $F$ is a vector space over its prime subfield $\mathbb{F}_p$.**

The prime subfield of $F$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{F}_p = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z
}$. Every element of $F$ is acted upon by $\mathbb{F}_p$ via scalar multiplicati
on (using the field operations of $F$), so $F$ carries the structure of a vector
 space over $\mathbb{F}_p$.

**Step 3: $q = p^n$ for some positive integer $n$.**

Since $F$ is finite, this vector space is finite-dimensional; let $n = \dim_{\ma
thbb{F}_p} F$. Choosing a basis $e_1, \dots, e_n$, every element of $F$ is uniqu
ely expressible as
$$a_1 e_1 + a_2 e_2 + \cdots + a_n e_n,\qquad a_i \in \mathbb{F}_p.$$
Each coefficient $a_i$ ranges independently over $p$ values, so the total number
 of elements is
$$|F| = p \cdot p \cdots p = p^n.$$

**Conclusion.**

The order of any finite field is $p^n$, a power of a prime. Therefore **no field
 can have an order that is not a power of a prime**. (Conversely, for every prim
e $p$ and every $n \geq 1$ there does exist a field of order $p^n$—the Galois fi
eld $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$—but the question only asks about the necessary direction,
 which we have established.) $\blacksquare$
