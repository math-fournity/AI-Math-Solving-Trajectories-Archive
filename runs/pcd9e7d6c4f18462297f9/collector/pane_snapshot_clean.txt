## Setup

The equation $a^2 + b^2 + c^2 = n(ab + bc + ca)$ is homogeneous, so scaling $(a,
b,c)$ preserves the ratio. Treating this as a quadratic in $a$:

$$a^2 - n(b+c)\,a + (b^2 + c^2 - nbc) = 0.$$

The discriminant is $\Delta = n^2(b+c)^2 - 4(b^2+c^2-nbc) = (n^2-4)(b^2+c^2) + 2
n(n+2)bc$.

For $a$ to be a positive integer, $\Delta$ must be a perfect square. The other r
oot $a' = n(b+c)-a$ satisfies $aa' = b^2+c^2-nbc$ (Vieta). This descent structur
