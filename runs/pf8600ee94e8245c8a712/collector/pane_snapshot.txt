## Solution

**Yes, such a non-zero functional exists.**

### Setup

Recall that by the Riesz representation theorem, every bounded linear functional
 $f \in (l^2)^*$ has the form
$$f(x) = \langle x, b \rangle = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n \overline{b_n}$$
for a unique $b = (b_n) \in l^2$. So the question reduces to: does there exist a
 **non-zero** $b \in l^2$ such that $\langle x, b\rangle = 0$ for every $x \in Y
$?

### Structure of $Y$

A sequence $x = (a_n) \in Y$ satisfies $a_2 = 2a_1$ and $a_4 = 4a_3$, with $a_1,
 a_3$, and all $a_n$ ($n \ge 5$) free. Thus every $x \in Y$ decomposes as
$$x = a_1(1,2,0,0,\dots) + a_3(0,0,1,4,0,\dots) + \sum_{n\ge 5} a_n\, e_n.$$

### Conditions on $b$

For $\langle x, b\rangle = 0$ to hold for **all** $x \in Y$:

- **Free $a_n$ for $n \ge 5$** forces $b_n = 0$ for all $n \ge 5$.
- **Free $a_1$** forces $b_1\cdot 1 + b_2\cdot 2 = 0$, i.e. $b_1 = -2b_2$.
- **Free $a_3$** forces $b_3\cdot 1 + b_4\cdot 4 = 0$, i.e. $b_3 = -4b_4$.

### Explicit construction

Choose $b_2 = 1$, $b_1 = -2$, and $b_3 = b_4 = 0$. Then
$$b = (-2,\, 1,\, 0,\, 0,\, 0,\, \dots) \in l^2, \qquad b \neq 0.$$

The corresponding functional is
$$f(x) = -2a_1 + a_2.$$

For any $x \in Y$ we have $a_2 = 2a_1$, so
$$f(x) = -2a_1 + 2a_1 = 0.$$

Hence $f(Y) = \{0\}$ with $f \neq 0$.

### Conceptual confirmation

$Y$ is the intersection of the kernels of the two continuous linear functionals
$g_1(x) = a_2 - 2a_1$ and $g_2(x) = a_4 - 4a_3$, so $Y$ is a **closed proper sub
space** of $l^2$ (e.g. $e_1 \notin Y$). By the Hahn–Banach theorem, any closed p
roper subspace admits a non-zero continuous linear functional vanishing on it. T
he explicit $f$ above is one such functional.
