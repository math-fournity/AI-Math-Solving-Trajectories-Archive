## Proof

**Step 1: The nonnegativity condition forces all matrices to be singular.**

Let $m = 2n+1$. Since $F$ is a linear subspace, $A \in F$ implies $-A \in F$. Th
erefore $\det(A) \geq 0$ and $\det(-A) \geq 0$. Since $m = 2n+1$ is odd:
$$\det(-A) = (-1)^m \det(A) = -\det(A).$$
Thus $\det(A) \geq 0$ and $-\det(A) \geq 0$, which forces $\det(A) = 0$ for all
$A \in F$.

The problem reduces to: **find the maximal dimension of a linear subspace of $M_
m(\mathbb{R})$ consisting entirely of singular matrices**, where $m = 2n+1$.

**Step 2: Upper bound via Flanders' theorem.**

I use the following classical result:

> **Flanders' Theorem (1966).** *Let $K$ be a field with $|K| \geq m$, and let $
V$ be a linear subspace of $M_m(K)$ in which every matrix has rank at most $r$.
Then $\dim V \leq m \cdot r$.*

This theorem holds over any sufficiently large field, in particular over $\mathb
b{R}$.

Applying this with $r = m-1$ (the rank bound for singular $m \times m$ matrices)
, we obtain:
$$\dim F \leq m(m-1) = (2n+1)(2n).$$

**Step 3: The bound is achieved.**

Fix a nonzero vector $v \in \mathbb{R}^m$ and define:
$$F_0 = \{A \in M_m(\mathbb{R}) : Av = 0\}.$$

This is a linear subspace. Every $A \in F_0$ has $v$ in its kernel, so $A$ is si
ngular and $\det(A) = 0 \geq 0$.

To compute the dimension: the condition $Av = 0$ means each row of $A$ (viewed a
s a linear functional) annihilates $v$. Each row therefore lies in the $(m-1)$-d
imensional subspace $v^\perp \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$. Since the $m$ rows can be
chosen independently, we get:
$$\dim F_0 = m(m-1) = (2n+1)(2n) = 2n(2n+1).$$

**Step 4: Verification of the scalar extension argument (confirming the bound ov
er $\mathbb{R}$).**

One might worry that over $\mathbb{R}$ there exist singular subspaces larger tha
n those over $\mathbb{C}$. However, if $V \subseteq M_m(\mathbb{R})$ is a real l
inear subspace with $\det|_V \equiv 0$, then $V_\mathbb{C} = V \otimes_\mathbb{R

 dimension with $\det|_{V_\mathbb{C}} \equiv 0$. Since Flanders' theorem also gi
ves the bound $m(m-1)$ over $\mathbb{C}$, we have $\dim_\mathbb{R} V = \dim_\mat
hbb{C} V_\mathbb{C} \leq m(m-1)$. Thus the real and complex bounds coincide.

**Conclusion.**

The upper bound from Flanders' theorem and the matching lower bound from the con
struction $F_0 = \{A : Av = 0\}$ together give:

$$\boxed{\dim F_{\max} = 2n(2n+1).}$$
