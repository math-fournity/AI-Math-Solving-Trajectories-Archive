## Proof

**Key Observation: No two diagonals can share a grid point.**

Each diagonal of a unit square is a line segment contained entirely within that
square (including its two endpoint corners). Two different unit squares have dis
joint interiors, so two diagonals in different squares can only intersect at sha
red boundary points—which are grid points (corners). Therefore, the condition "n
o two diagonals share any common point" is equivalent to **no two diagonals shar
e a grid point endpoint**.

**Modeling as a matching problem.** The 8×8 table has a 9×9 grid of corner point
s (81 points). Each diagonal uses 2 corner points (opposite corners of some unit
 square, differing by (±1, ±1)). We seek a maximum matching in the graph where v
ertices are grid points and edges connect points differing by (±1, ±1).

**Decomposition into two independent components.** A move of (±1, ±1) changes x+
y by 0 or ±2, preserving the parity of x+y. Thus the graph splits into:
- **Even component:** 41 points with x+y even.
- **Odd component:** 40 points with x+y odd.

No diagonal connects points across these components, so we optimize each indepen
dently.

**Even component: bipartite 25 vs 16.** Within the even component, a (±1, ±1) mo
ve flips the parity of both x and y. This gives a bipartition:
- Even-even points (x, y both even): 5×5 = 25 points.
- Odd-odd points (x, y both odd): 4×4 = 16 points.

Each diagonal in this component pairs one even-even with one odd-odd point, so *
*at most 16 diagonals**.

**Construction for 16:** Match each odd-odd point (2i+1, 2j+1), i,j ∈ {0,1,2,3},
 to the even-even point (2i, 2j). These differ by (1,1), forming a valid diagona
l, and the mapping is injective. This gives **16 diagonals** with all endpoints
distinct. ✓

**Odd component: bipartite 20 vs 20.** Within the odd component, the bipartition
 is:
- (even, odd) points: 5×4 = 20 points.
- (odd, even) points: 4×5 = 20 points.

Each diagonal pairs one of each, so **at most 20 diagonals**.

**Construction for 20:** Apply the coordinate transform u = (x+y−1)/2, v = (x−y+
1)/2, mapping the 40 odd-component points to integer (u,v) coordinates. The diag
onal adjacency becomes standard 4-connectivity (grid adjacency) in (u,v). The re
gion is:

| u | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| #points | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 8 | 6 | 4 | 2 |

For each fixed u, the v-values form a contiguous interval, and for each fixed v,
 the u-values form a contiguous interval (orthogonally convex region). The bipar
tition by (u+v) mod 2 is balanced: 20 + 20.

Match each vertex (u, v) with (u, v+1) **within each row** (vertical edges in th
e (u,v)-grid). Since each row has an even number of vertices (2, 4, 6, 8, 8, 6,
4, 2), this pairs all vertices within each row:

- u=0: (0,0)–(0,1) → 1 match
- u=1: (1,−1)–(1,0), (1,1)–(1,2) → 2 matches
- u=2: 3 matches; u=3: 4 matches; u=4: 4 matches; u=5: 3 matches; u=6: 2 matches
; u=7: 1 match.

Total: 1+2+3+4+4+3+2+1 = **20 matches**. Each vertical edge (u,v)–(u,v+1) corres
ponds to a diagonal between grid points (u+v, u−v+1) and (u+v+1, u−v), which dif
fer by (1,−1). All 40 endpoints are distinct. ✓

**No cross-component sharing.** Even-component diagonals have endpoints with x+y
 even; odd-component diagonals have endpoints with x+y odd. Since no grid point
has both parities, no endpoint is shared across components.

**Combining:** 16 (even) + 20 (odd) = **36 diagonals**, achieving the upper boun
d.

## Answer

The maximum number of diagonals is **36**.
