## Solution

**The equality does not hold in general.** It holds only under additional integr
ability conditions on $f$ (e.g. Novikov's condition). Below I prove (i) the left
-hand side is always $\le 1$, (ii) a concrete counterexample where strict inequa
lity occurs, and (iii) a standard sufficient condition for equality.

---

### 1. The stochastic exponential is a non-negative local martingale, hence a su
permartingale

Let $z$ be standard Brownian motion and let $f(\omega,s)$ be a predictable proce
ss with $\int_0^t f^2\,ds<\infty$ a.s. Define

$$Z_t \;=\; \exp\!\Bigg(\int_0^t f(\omega,s)\,dz(s)\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\int_0^t f(\o
mega,s)^2\,ds\Bigg).$$

This is the **Doléans–Dade exponential** $\mathcal{E}\!\big(\int_0^\cdot f\,dz\b
ig)_t$. By Itô's formula, $Z_t$ satisfies

$$dZ_t = Z_t\,f(\omega,t)\,dz(t), \qquad Z_0 = 1,$$

so $Z_t$ is a **non-negative local martingale**. A non-negative local martingale
 is a **supermartingale** (by Fatou's lemma applied to the localizing sequence).
 Therefore

$$\mathbb{E}[Z_t] \;\le\; \mathbb{E}[Z_0] \;=\; 1 \qquad \text{for all } t\ge 0.
$$

Equality $\mathbb{E}[Z_t]=1$ holds **if and only if** $Z_t$ is a *true* martinga
le (not merely a strict local martingale). So the displayed equality is **not au
tomatic**; it is a genuine condition on $f$.

---

### 2. Counterexample: strict inequality is possible

I exhibit an adapted $f$ for which $\mathbb{E}[Z_t]<1$.

**Setup.** Let $X=(X_t)_{t\ge 0}$ be a three-dimensional Bessel process $\mathrm
{BES}(3)$ starting from $x>0$, driven by a Brownian motion $z$:

$$dX_t = dz(t) + \frac{1}{X_t}\,dt, \qquad X_0 = x.$$

Set $f(\omega,s) := -\dfrac{1}{X_s(\omega)}$, which is a well-defined predictabl
e process (since $\mathrm{BES}(3)$ never hits $0$). Define

$$M_t := \frac{x}{X_t}.$$

**Itô computation.** Applying Itô's formula to $1/X_t$:

$$d\!\left(\frac{1}{X_t}\right) = -\frac{1}{X_t^2}\,dX_t + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac
{2}{X_t^3}\,d\langle X\rangle_t = -\frac{1}{X_t^2}\,dz(t) - \frac{1}{X_t^3}\,dt
+ \frac{1}{X_t^3}\,dt = -\frac{1}{X_t^2}\,dz(t).$$

Hence

$$dM_t = -\frac{x}{X_t^2}\,dz(t) = -\frac{M_t}{X_t}\,dz(t), \qquad M_0 = 1.$$

Solving this linear SDE gives exactly the Doléans–Dade exponential:

$$M_t = \exp\!\Bigg(\int_0^t \underbrace{\Big(-\frac{1}{X_s}\Big)}_{=:\,f(\omega
,s)}\,dz(s)\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\int_0^t \frac{1}{X_s^2}\,ds\Bigg) = Z_t.$$

**$M_t$ is a strict local martingale.** The process $M_t = x/X_t$ is a positive
local martingale (hence supermartingale). It is a classical result (see e.g. Rev
uz–Yor, *Continuous Martingales and Brownian Motion*, or the identity via Girsan
ov relating $\mathrm{BES}(3)$ to Brownian motion absorbed at $0$) that $M_t$ is
**not** a true martingale: for every $t>0$,

$$\mathbb{E}[M_t] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{x}{X_t}\right] < 1.$$

*Justification of strict inequality.* Under the measure $\mathbb{Q}$ with densit
y $M_t = x/X_t$ (well-defined as a probability measure since $M_t$ is a non-nega
tive local martingale with $M_0=1$, hence defines a sub-probability, and in fact
 a probability on each finite horizon because $\mathrm{BES}(3)$ does not explode
), Girsanov's theorem transforms $z$ into a $\mathbb{Q}$-Brownian motion with dr
ift, and $X$ becomes a $\mathrm{BES}(3)$ killed at $0$—i.e., a Brownian motion s
tarted at $x$ and absorbed at $0$. The mass lost under $\mathbb{Q}$ is exactly $
\mathbb{Q}(X_t = 0) = \mathbb{P}(\text{Brownian motion started at }x\text{ hits
}0\text{ by time }t) > 0$ for $t>0$. This lost mass means

$$\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}[M_t] = 1 - \mathbb{Q}(X_t=0) < 1.$$

Therefore, for this choice of $f$,

$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\exp\!\Big(\int_0^t f\,dz - \tfrac{1}{2}\int_0^t f^2\,ds\Big
)\right] = \mathbb{E}[M_t] < 1 \qquad (t>0),$$

and the proposed equality **fails**.

---

### 3. Sufficient condition for equality (Novikov)

The equality **does** hold under **Novikov's condition**:

$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\exp\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}\int_0^t f(\omega,s)^2\,ds\Big)\right
] < \infty.$$

Under this condition, $Z_t$ is a true martingale (Novikov, 1973), and consequent
ly $\mathbb{E}[Z_t]=1$ for all $t$. Other sufficient conditions exist (Kazamaki'
s criterion, Beneš's condition, etc.), but none is automatic for arbitrary adapt
ed $f$.

---

### Conclusion

The equality

$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\exp\!\Big(\int_0^t f(\omega,s)\,dz(s)-\frac{1}{2}\int_0^t f
(\omega,s)^2\,ds\Big)\right] = 1$$

**does not hold in general.** The stochastic exponential is always a non-negativ
e local martingale and hence a supermartingale, giving $\mathbb{E}[\cdot]\le 1$;
 strict inequality occurs for suitable adapted $f$ (e.g. $f=-1/X_s$ with $X$ a $
\mathrm{BES}(3)$ process, yielding a strict local martingale with $\mathbb{E}[Z_
t]<1$). Equality holds only under additional integrability hypotheses such as No
vikov's condition, which guarantee that the local martingale is in fact a true m
artingale.
