## Proof

Let $p_1, p_2, p_3, \ldots$ denote the primes in increasing order: $p_1=2, p_2=3
, p_3=5, \ldots$. The first $n$ odd primes are $p_2, p_3, \ldots, p_{n+1}$, so

$$b_n = \prod_{k=2}^{n+1} p_k.$$

Since the $p_k$ are distinct primes, $\varphi$ is multiplicative on this product
, giving

$$\varphi(b_n) = \prod_{k=2}^{n+1} (p_k - 1),$$

and therefore

$$\frac{\varphi(b_n)}{b_n} = \prod_{k=2}^{n+1}\frac{p_k-1}{p_k} = \prod_{k=2}^{n
+1}\left(1 - \frac{1}{p_k}\right).$$

**Relating to the full Euler product.** Including the prime $p_1=2$,

$$\prod_{k=1}^{n+1}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_k}\right) = \left(1-\frac{1}{2}\right)\pro
d_{k=2}^{n+1}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_k}\right) = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\varphi(b_n)}{
b_n},$$

so

$$\frac{\varphi(b_n)}{b_n} = 2\prod_{k=1}^{n+1}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_k}\right). \ta
g{1}$$

**Applying Mertens' theorem.** Mertens' third theorem states that as $x\to\infty
$,

$$\prod_{p\le x}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right) \sim \frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\ln x},$$

where $\gamma$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant. In particular,

$$\prod_{p\le x}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right) \to 0 \quad\text{as } x\to\infty.$$

Taking $x = p_{n+1}$ (the largest prime in $b_n$), the partial product $\prod_{k
=1}^{n+1}(1-1/p_k) = \prod_{p\le p_{n+1}}(1-1/p) \to 0$ as $n\to\infty$.

**Conclusion.** Substituting into (1),

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\varphi(b_n)}{b_n} = 2\cdot\lim_{n\to\infty}\prod_{k=1}
^{n+1}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_k}\right) = 2\cdot 0 = 0.$$

The limit is $\boxed{0}$.
