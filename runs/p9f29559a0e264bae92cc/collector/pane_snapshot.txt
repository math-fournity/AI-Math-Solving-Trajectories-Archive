## Solution

**The transformation.** Let $u = xy$ and $v = x^2 - y^2$. The bounding curves of
 $D$ become $u = 1$, $u = 3$, $v = 1$, $v = 4$, so $D$ maps to the rectangle
$$E = [1,3]\times[1,4]$$
in the $uv$-plane.

**Computing the Jacobian.** First compute $\dfrac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(x,y)}$
:
$$\frac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(x,y)} = \begin{vmatrix} u_x & u_y \\ v_x & v_y \
end{vmatrix} = \begin{vmatrix} y & x \\ 2x & -2y \end{vmatrix} = y(-2y) - x(2x)
= -2(x^2+y^2).$$

Hence
$$\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)} = \frac{1}{\partial(u,v)/\partial(x,y)} =
\frac{-1}{2(x^2+y^2)}, \qquad \left|\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)}\right| =
 \frac{1}{2(x^2+y^2)}.$$

**Expressing $x^2+y^2$ in terms of $u,v$.** Using
$$(x^2+y^2)^2 = (x^2-y^2)^2 + 4x^2y^2 = v^2 + 4u^2,$$
and since $D$ lies in the first quadrant (so $x^2+y^2 > 0$),
$$x^2+y^2 = \sqrt{v^2+4u^2}.$$

**Evaluating the integral.** By the change-of-variables formula,
$$\iint_D (x^2+y^2)\,dx\,dy = \iint_E (x^2+y^2)\left|\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\parti
al(u,v)}\right|du\,dv = \iint_E \sqrt{v^2+4u^2}\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt{v^2+4u^2}}\,
du\,dv = \iint_E \frac{1}{2}\,du\,dv.$$

The integrand has collapsed to the constant $\tfrac12$, so
$$\iint_D (x^2+y^2)\,dx\,dy = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\operatorname{Area}(E) = \frac{1}{
2}\cdot(3-1)(4-1) = \frac{1}{2}\cdot 2\cdot 3 = 3.$$

$$\boxed{\iint_D (x^2+y^2)\,dx\,dy = 3}$$
