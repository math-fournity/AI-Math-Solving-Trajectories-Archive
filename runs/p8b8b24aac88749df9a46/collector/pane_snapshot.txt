## Solution

We seek the maximum of $|z| = |\omega+2|^3\,|\omega-3|^2$ over $|\omega|=1$.

**Parametrize the unit circle.** Let $\omega = e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\
theta$, and set $c = \cos\theta \in [-1,1]$.

**Compute the squared moduli.**

$$|\omega+2|^2 = (\cos\theta+2)^2 + \sin^2\theta = 5 + 4\cos\theta = 5+4c,$$

$$|\omega-3|^2 = (\cos\theta-3)^2 + \sin^2\theta = 10 - 6\cos\theta = 10-6c.$$

Therefore

$$|z| = (5+4c)^{3/2}\,(10-6c) =: f(c), \qquad c\in[-1,1].$$

**Optimize.** Differentiate:

$$f'(c) = \tfrac{3}{2}(5+4c)^{1/2}\cdot 4\cdot(10-6c) + (5+4c)^{3/2}\cdot(-6)$$
$$= 6(5+4c)^{1/2}\bigl[(10-6c)-(5+4c)\bigr] = 30\,(5+4c)^{1/2}(1-2c).$$

Since $5+4c > 0$ on $[-1,1]$, the only interior critical point is $c = \tfrac{1}
{2}$.

**Evaluate at the critical point and endpoints:**

- $c = \tfrac{1}{2}$: $f = (5+2)^{3/2}(10-3) = 7^{3/2}\cdot 7 = 7^{5/2} = 49\sqr
t{7} \approx 129.64$.
- $c = 1$: $f = 9^{3/2}\cdot 4 = 27\cdot 4 = 108$.
- $c = -1$: $f = 1^{3/2}\cdot 16 = 16$.

The sign of $f'$: positive for $c<\tfrac12$, negative for $c>\tfrac12$, confirmi
ng $c=\tfrac12$ is the global maximum on $[-1,1]$.

**Verify attainability.** $c = \cos\theta = \tfrac12$ is achievable (e.g. $\thet
a = \pi/3$), with $\omega = \tfrac12 + \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i$ on the unit circle.

**Result.** The maximum possible magnitude of $z$ is

$$\boxed{49\sqrt{7}}.$$
