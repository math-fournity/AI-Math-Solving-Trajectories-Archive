## Solution

The Legendre differential equation is:

$$(1-x^2)y'' - 2xy' + l(l+1)y = 0$$

### Step 1: Identify the singularities

Rewriting in standard form by dividing through by $(1-x^2)$:

$$y'' - \frac{2x}{1-x^2}y' + \frac{l(l+1)}{1-x^2}y = 0$$

The coefficients $P(x) = -\frac{2x}{1-x^2}$ and $Q(x) = \frac{l(l+1)}{1-x^2}$ ar
e analytic except where $1-x^2 = 0$, i.e., at $x = \pm 1$. These are the singula
r points of the equation. The expansion point is $x_0 = 0$.

### Step 2: Radius of convergence via the nearest singularity

For a linear ODE with analytic coefficients in a neighborhood of $x_0$, every po
wer series solution centered at $x_0$ converges at least in the largest disk (in
 the complex plane) centered at $x_0$ that contains no singularity of the equati
on. The distance from $x_0 = 0$ to the nearest singularity $x = \pm 1$ is:

$$R = |0 - (\pm 1)| = 1$$

### Step 3: Verify by the ratio test

For $y_2$, the coefficient of $x^{2n}$ is:

$$a_n = \frac{(-1)^n}{(2n)!}\prod_{k=1}^{2n}\bigl(l+(-1)^k(k-1)\bigr)$$

The ratio of successive terms:

$$\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = \frac{-1}{(2n+2)(2n+1)}\prod_{k=2n+1}^{2n+2}\bigl(l+(-1)
^k(k-1)\bigr)$$

For $k = 2n+1$ (odd): $l - (2n) = l - 2n$; for $k = 2n+2$ (even): $l + (2n+1)$.
Hence

$$\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = \frac{-(l-2n)(l+2n+1)}{(2n+2)(2n+1)} = \frac{(2n-l)(2n+1
+l)}{(2n+2)(2n+1)}$$

Taking the limit:

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right| = \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{(2n
-l)(2n+1+l)}{(2n+2)(2n+1)} = 1$$

The ratio test for the series $\sum a_n x^{2n}$ gives convergence when

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\frac{a_{n+1}x^{2n+2}}{a_n x^{2n}}\right| = |x|^2 < 1 \
implies |x| < 1$$

An identical computation for $y_1$ (with terms $x^{2n+1}$) yields the same limit
 $|x|^2 < 1$, confirming $R = 1$.

### Step 4: Note on the special (terminating) case

When $l$ is a non-negative integer, one of the two series terminates (the produc
t $\prod_{k=1}^{2n}(l+(-1)^k(\cdot))$ vanishes for sufficiently large $n$), prod
ucing a Legendre polynomial — which is entire and has $R = \infty$. However, the
 **general** power series solution (for arbitrary $l$) has radius of convergence
 determined by the nearest singularity of the equation.

### Conclusion

The radius of convergence of the power series solution is:

$$\boxed{R = 1}$$
