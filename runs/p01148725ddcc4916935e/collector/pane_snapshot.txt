## Proof

Let $X = [0, \infty)$ and $f: X \to \mathbb{R}$, $f(x) = \frac{x}{1+x}$.

**Step 1: $f$ is bounded and continuous, and the evaluation map is an embedding.
**

$f$ is continuous (rational function, no poles on $[0,\infty)$), strictly increa
sing, with $f(0)=0$ and $\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=1$, so $f(X)=[0,1)$. Define the e
valuation map $e: X \to [0,1]$ by $e(x)=f(x)=\frac{x}{1+x}$. Then:
- $e$ is continuous and injective (since $f$ is strictly increasing).
- $e(X)=[0,1)$, and the inverse $e^{-1}(y)=\frac{y}{1-y}$ is continuous on $[0,1
)$.

Hence $e$ is a homeomorphism from $X$ onto $[0,1)$, i.e., an embedding into $[0,
1]$.

**Step 2: Construct the candidate compactification.**

Set $K = \overline{e(X)} = \overline{[0,1)} = [0,1] \subset [0,1]$, with the sub
space topology. Then $K$ is compact (closed in compact $[0,1]$) and Hausdorff. T
he map $e: X \to K$ is an embedding with dense image, so $K$ is a Hausdorff comp
actification of $X$.

**Step 3: $f$ extends continuously to $K$.**

Define $\tilde{f}: K=[0,1] \to \mathbb{R}$ by $\tilde{f}(y)=y$ (the identity). T
his is continuous, and for all $x \in X$:
$$\tilde{f}(e(x)) = e(x) = f(x).$$
So $\tilde{f}\circ e = f$, giving a continuous extension of $f$ to $K$.

**Step 4: Minimality — $K$ is the smallest Hausdorff compactification to which $
f$ extends.**

Let $\gamma X$ be any Hausdorff compactification of $X$ to which $f$ extends con
tinuously, with extension $\hat{f}: \gamma X \to \mathbb{R}$. We construct a fac
torization map $\phi: \gamma X \to K$.

Since $X$ is dense in $\gamma X$ and $\hat{f}$ is continuous, every point $p \in
 \gamma X$ is a limit of a net $(x_i) \subset X$, so $\hat{f}(p) = \lim \hat{f}(
x_i) = \lim f(x_i) \in \overline{f(X)} = \overline{[0,1)} = [0,1] = K$. Thus $\h
at{f}$ maps $\gamma X$ into $K=[0,1]$.

Set $\phi = \hat{f}: \gamma X \to K$. Then:
- $\phi$ is continuous (restriction of continuous $\hat{f}$).
- $\phi|_X = f = e$, so $\phi$ commutes with the embeddings of $X$ into $\gamma
X$ and $K$.
- $\phi(\gamma X) \supseteq \phi(X) = e(X) = [0,1)$, and $\phi(\gamma X)$ is com
pact (hence closed in $K$), so $\phi(\gamma X) \supseteq \overline{[0,1)} = [0,1
] = K$. Thus $\phi$ is surjective.

Therefore $\phi: \gamma X \to K$ is a continuous surjection fixing $X$, which me
ans $K \leq \gamma X$ in the partial order of Hausdorff compactifications. Since
 $\gamma X$ was arbitrary, **$K$ is the minimum** element among all Hausdorff co
mpactifications to which $f$ extends.

**Step 5: Identification.**

The compactification $K = [0,1]$ adds exactly one point to $X$ (the point $1$, c
orresponding to $x \to \infty$). Since $X = [0,\infty)$ is locally compact and n
on-compact, this is precisely the **one-point (Alexandroff) compactification** o
f $[0,\infty)$.

**Conclusion.** The minimal Hausdorff compactification $K$ of $[0,\infty)$ to wh
ich $f(x)=\frac{x}{1+x}$ extends continuously is the **one-point compactificatio
n**, homeomorphic to $[0,1]$. The embedding is $x \mapsto \frac{x}{1+x}$, and th
e extension sends the added point $\infty \mapsto 1$.
