## Block Structure

Let $p_k$ denote the $k$-th prime ($p_1=2, p_2=3, p_3=5, p_4=7, \ldots$) and $P_
k = p_1 p_2 \cdots p_k$ the $k$-th primorial.

**Claim:** The sequence enumerates all squarefree positive integers, each exactl
y once, organized in blocks:
$$x_{2^{k-1}+j} = p_k \cdot x_j, \quad j = 0, 1, \ldots, 2^{k-1}-1$$
for each $k \geq 1$. Block $B_k$ (positions $2^{k-1}$ to $2^k - 1$) consists of
all $2^{k-1}$ squarefree numbers whose **largest prime factor** is $p_k$.

**Verification of block pattern:**
- $B_1$: $\{2\} = \{2 \cdot x_0\}$ ✓
- $B_2$: $\{3, 6\} = \{3 \cdot x_0, 3 \cdot x_1\}$ ✓

## Proof of the Block Recurrence (by induction on $k$)

**Base case ($k=1$):** $x_0=1$, $p(1)=2=p_1$, $q(1)=1$, so $x_1 = 2 = p_1 \cdot
x_0$. ✓

**Inductive step:** Assume blocks $B_1, \ldots, B_k$ satisfy the recurrence. The
 last element of $B_k$ is $x_{2^k - 1} = p_k \cdot x_{2^{k-1}-1}$. By induction,
 $x_{2^{k-1}-1} = P_{k-1}$, so $x_{2^k-1} = P_k$.

**Transition:** $p(P_k) = p_{k+1}$, $q(P_k) = P_k$, so $x_{2^k} = P_k \cdot p_{k
+1}/P_k = p_{k+1} = p_{k+1} \cdot x_0$. ✓

**Within-block propagation:** Suppose $x_{2^k + j} = p_{k+1} \cdot x_j$ for some
 $j < 2^k - 1$. Set $m = x_j$ (squarefree, prime factors in $\{p_1,\ldots,p_k\}$
, and $m \neq P_k$ since $j < 2^k-1$).

Since $m \neq P_k$, some prime $p_i$ ($i \leq k$) does not divide $m$, so $p(m)
\leq p_k < p_{k+1}$. Therefore:
- $p(m)$ does not divide $m$ and $p(m) \neq p_{k+1}$, so $p(m) \nmid p_{k+1} \cd
ot m$
- All primes less than $p(m)$ divide $m$ (hence divide $p_{k+1} \cdot m$)

Thus $p(p_{k+1} \cdot m) = p(m)$ and $q(p_{k+1} \cdot m) = q(m)$, giving:
$$x_{2^k+j+1} = \frac{p_{k+1} \cdot m \cdot p(m)}{q(m)} = p_{k+1} \cdot \frac{m
\cdot p(m)}{q(m)} = p_{k+1} \cdot x_{j+1} \quad \checkmark$$

**Squarefree preservation:** If $x_n$ is squarefree, write $x_n = q(x_n) \cdot r
$ where $\gcd(r, q(x_n))=1$ and $p(x_n) \nmid r$. Then $x_{n+1} = r \cdot p(x_n)
$, which is squarefree since $r$ is squarefree (divisor of squarefree) and $p(x_
n) \nmid r$.

**Bijectivity:** Each block $B_k$ maps $\{x_0, \ldots, x_{2^{k-1}-1}\}$ bijectiv
ely to squarefree numbers with largest prime factor $p_k$ via $m \mapsto p_k \cd
ot m$. Since every squarefree number $>1$ has a unique largest prime factor, the
 sequence is a bijection onto all squarefree positive integers.
