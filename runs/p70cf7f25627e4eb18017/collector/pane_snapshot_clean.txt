## Proof

We compute the Lebesgue integral $I = \int_E \sin(x)\,d\lambda(x)$ where
$$E = [0,\pi]\cup[2\pi,3\pi]\cup\mathbb{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}.$$

**Step 1: Reduce to the interval part.**

The set $\mathbb{Q}$ of rationals is countable, hence $\lambda(\mathbb{Q})=0$. T
herefore $E$ differs from $F := [0,\pi]\cup[2\pi,3\pi]$ only on a null set:
$$E \triangle F \subseteq \mathbb{Q},\qquad \lambda(E\triangle F)=0.$$
Since $\sin$ is measurable and two measurable functions that agree a.e. have the
 same Lebesgue integral over any measurable set, and modifying the domain by a n
ull set does not change the integral,
$$\int_E \sin(x)\,d\lambda(x) = \int_F \sin(x)\,d\lambda(x).$$

**Step 2: Split over the two disjoint intervals.**

The intervals $[0,\pi]$ and $[2\pi,3\pi]$ are disjoint (since $\pi < 2\pi$). By
additivity of the Lebesgue integral over disjoint measurable sets,
$$\int_F \sin(x)\,d\lambda(x) = \int_{[0,\pi]}\sin(x)\,d\lambda(x) + \int_{[2\pi
,3\pi]}\sin(x)\,d\lambda(x).$$

**Step 3: Evaluate each integral.**

The function $\sin$ is continuous, hence Riemann integrable on each compact inte
rval, and its Lebesgue integral coincides with its Riemann integral.

$$\int_{[0,\pi]}\sin(x)\,d\lambda(x) = \int_0^\pi \sin(x)\,dx = \bigl[-\cos x\bi
gr]_0^\pi = -\cos\pi + \cos 0 = -(-1)+1 = 2.$$

$$\int_{[2\pi,3\pi]}\sin(x)\,d\lambda(x) = \int_{2\pi}^{3\pi}\sin(x)\,dx = \bigl
[-\cos x\bigr]_{2\pi}^{3\pi} = -\cos(3\pi)+\cos(2\pi).$$
Using $\cos(3\pi)=\cos(\pi+2\pi)=\cos\pi=-1$ and $\cos(2\pi)=1$:
$$\int_{[2\pi,3\pi]}\sin(x)\,d\lambda(x) = -(-1)+1 = 2.$$

**Step 4: Combine.**

$$I = 2 + 2 = 4.$$

**Conclusion.** The Lebesgue measure of $\mathbb{Q}$ is zero, so the rational po
ints in $E$ contribute nothing to the integral. The integral is determined entir
ely by the two intervals $[0,\pi]$ and $[2\pi,3\pi]$, on each of which $\sin$ ha
s integral $2$. Therefore

$$\boxed{\int_E \sin(x)\,d\lambda(x) = 4.}$$
