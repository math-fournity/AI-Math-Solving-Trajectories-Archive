# Self-Adjointness of $-\Delta$ on a Bounded Domain with Boundary Conditions

## Setup and the Core Issue

Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ be a bounded open set with smooth boundary $\p
artial\Omega$. Define the **minimal operator**

$$A_0 := -\Delta, \qquad D(A_0) := C_c^\infty(\Omega),$$

acting in the Hilbert space $\mathcal{H} = L^2(\Omega)$.

**Symmetry.** For $u, v \in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$, Green's second identity gives

$$\langle A_0 u, v\rangle - \langle u, A_0 v\rangle = \int_{\partial\Omega}\Big(
\overline{v}\,\tfrac{\partial u}{\partial n} - u\,\tfrac{\partial \overline{v}}{
\partial n}\Big)\,dS = 0,$$

since $u,v$ vanish in a neighborhood of $\partial\Omega$. Hence $A_0$ is **symme
tric** (Hermitian): $A_0 \subseteq A_0^*$.

**But $A_0$ is not self-adjoint.** It is not even closed. Its closure $\overline
{A_0}$ is a closed, symmetric, positive operator, but $D(\overline{A_0}) \subset
neq D(A_0^*)$. Concretely, the adjoint is the **maximal operator**

$$A_0^* = (\overline{A_0})^* = A_{\max}, \qquad D(A_{\max}) = \big\{u \in L^2(\O
mega) : \Delta u \in L^2(\Omega)\big\},$$

where $\Delta u$ is understood distributionally. By elliptic regularity on a smo
oth bounded domain, $D(A_{\max}) \subset H^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$, and elemen
ts of $D(A_{\max})$ possess well-defined boundary traces $\gamma_0 u = u|_{\part
ial\Omega}$ and $\gamma_1 u = \partial_n u|_{\partial\Omega}$ (in appropriate So
bolev trace spaces).

Since $D(\overline{A_0}) \subsetneq D(A_{\max})$ — for instance, a smooth functi
on nonzero on $\partial\Omega$ belongs to $D(A_{\max})$ but not to $D(\overline{
A_0})$ — we have $\overline{A_0} \subsetneq A_0^*$, so **$A_0$ is not self-adjoi
nt, nor essentially self-adjoint**.

## The Boundary Form and the Self-Adjointness Criterion

For $u, v \in D(A_{\max})$, Green's identity yields the **boundary (Green's) for
m**:

$$\boxed{\;\langle A_{\max}u, v\rangle - \langle u, A_{\max}v\rangle = \int_{\pa
rtial\Omega}\Big(\overline{v}\,\tfrac{\partial u}{\partial n} - u\,\tfrac{\parti
al \overline{v}}{\partial n}\Big)\,dS =: \omega(u,v).\;}$$

A subspace $D$ with $\overline{D(A_0)} \subseteq D \subseteq D(A_{\max})$ define
s a self-adjoint extension $A$ (with $D(A)=D$) **if and only if**

$$\omega(u, v) = 0 \quad \text{for all } u, v \in D. \tag{$\star$}$$

This is the abstract von Neumann / boundary-form criterion. Condition $(\star)$
is the **specific condition on the boundary**: the conormal boundary form must v
anish identically on the chosen domain.

## Concrete Boundary Conditions Realizing $(\star)$

The condition $(\star)$ is realized by imposing classical boundary conditions. E
ach of the following defines a self-adjoint extension of $-\Delta|_{C_c^\infty(\
Omega)}$:

### 1. Dirichlet boundary condition
$$D(A_D) = \big\{u \in H^2(\Omega) : u|_{\partial\Omega} = 0\big\} = H^2(\Omega)
 \cap H^1_0(\Omega).$$
*Verification of $(\star)$:* If $u|_{\partial\Omega} = v|_{\partial\Omega} = 0$,
 then both terms in the integrand of $\omega$ vanish, so $\omega(u,v)=0$. ∎

### 2. Neumann boundary condition
$$D(A_N) = \big\{u \in H^2(\Omega) : \tfrac{\partial u}{\partial n}\big|_{\parti
al\Omega} = 0\big\}.$$
*Verification of $(\star)$:* If $\partial_n u = \partial_n v = 0$ on $\partial\O
mega$, then $\omega(u,v)=0$. ∎

### 3. Robin boundary condition
$$D(A_R) = \big\{u \in H^2(\Omega) : \tfrac{\partial u}{\partial n} + \sigma\, u
 = 0 \text{ on } \partial\Omega\big\},$$
where $\sigma \in C(\partial\Omega; \mathbb{R})$ is a **real-valued** continuous
 function on the boundary.

*Verification of $(\star)$:* From $\partial_n u = -\sigma u$ and $\partial_n v =
 -\sigma v$,
$$\omega(u,v) = \int_{\partial\Omega}\big(\overline{v}(-\sigma u) - u(-\sigma\ov
erline{v})\big)\,dS = \int_{\partial\Omega}\big(-\sigma\,\overline{v}u + \sigma\
,u\overline{v}\big)\,dS = 0,$$
since $\sigma$ is real (so $\sigma\overline{v} = \overline{\sigma v}$) and the i
ntegrand is $\sigma(-\overline{v}u + u\overline{v}) = 0$ pointwise. ∎

The reality of $\sigma$ is essential: a complex $\sigma$ would break the cancell
ation and destroy self-adjointness.

## Why These Are (Essentially) All Self-Adjoint Extensions

By the theory of self-adjoint extensions of symmetric semi-bounded operators (Gr
ubb, Birman–Krein, Seeley), every self-adjoint extension of $\overline{A_0}$ is
parametrized by a **self-adjoint relation** in the boundary trace space. For the
 Laplacian on a smooth bounded domain, the trace map

$$\Gamma: D(A_{\max}) \to H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega) \times H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega
), \quad \Gamma u = (\gamma_0 u,\, \gamma_1 u) = (u|_{\partial\Omega},\, \partia
l_n u|_{\partial\Omega})$$

identifies the quotient $D(A_{\max})/D(\overline{A_0})$ with the trace space. Se
lf-adjoint extensions correspond to Lagrangian (maximal isotropic) subspaces for
 the symplectic form $\omega$, equivalently to graphs of self-adjoint operators
$\Theta$ from a subspace of $H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)$ to $H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omeg
a)$:

$$D(A_\Theta) = \big\{u \in D(A_{\max}) : \gamma_1 u = \Theta\, \gamma_0 u\big\}
.$$

- $\Theta = +\infty$ (i.e., $\gamma_0 u = 0$) gives **Dirichlet**.
- $\Theta = 0$ gives **Neumann**.
- $\Theta = -\sigma$ (multiplication by a real function) gives **Robin**.

More general pseudodifferential choices of $\Theta$ (self-adjoint operators on t
he boundary) give further self-adjoint extensions, but the three above are the c
anonical, most commonly used ones.

## Summary / Answer to the Question

The operator $-\Delta$ with domain $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ on a bounded smooth doma
in $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ is **symmetric but not self-adjoint** (and not
essentially self-adjoint). It becomes self-adjoint precisely when its domain is
enlarged from $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ to a domain $D \subset \{u \in L^2 : \Delta u
 \in L^2\}$ on which the **boundary Green's form vanishes**:

$$\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big(\overline{v}\,\tfrac{\partial u}{\partial n} - u\,\t
frac{\partial \overline{v}}{\partial n}\Big)\,dS = 0 \qquad \forall\, u,v \in D.
$$

The standard concrete boundary conditions guaranteeing this — and hence the self
-adjointness of $-\Delta$ — are:

| Condition | Boundary equation | Domain |
|---|---|---|
| **Dirichlet** | $u\big|_{\partial\Omega} = 0$ | $H^2(\Omega)\cap H^1_0(\Omega)
$ |
| **Neumann** | $\partial_n u\big|_{\partial\Omega} = 0$ | $\{u\in H^2(\Omega):
\partial_n u=0\}$ |
| **Robin** | $\partial_n u + \sigma\, u = 0$, $\sigma\in C(\partial\Omega;\math
bb{R})$ | $\{u\in H^2(\Omega): \partial_n u + \sigma u = 0\}$ |

**Example.** Take $\Omega = (0,1)^3$ (the unit cube, smooth except at corners; f
or a truly smooth example take the unit ball $B_1(0)$). With the **Dirichlet** c
ondition $u|_{\partial\Omega}=0$, the operator $-\Delta$ on $D(A_D) = H^2(\Omega
)\cap H^1_0(\Omega)$ is self-adjoint and positive on $L^2(\Omega)$, with discret
e spectrum $\{\lambda_k\}$ accumulating only at $+\infty$. The eigenfunctions fo
rm a complete orthonormal basis of $L^2(\Omega)$. This is the prototypical self-
adjoint realization, and it is a self-adjoint extension of the original $-\Delta
|_{C_c^\infty(\Omega)}$.

The essential point: **the boundary condition must be "real" and must annihilate
 the symplectic boundary form** — this is the precise sense in which conditions
on the boundary guarantee self-adjointness.
