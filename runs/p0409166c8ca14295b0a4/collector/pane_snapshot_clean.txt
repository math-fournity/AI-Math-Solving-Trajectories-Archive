## Proof

We are given that $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are distinct $n\times n$ real ma
trices satisfying
$$\mathbf{A}^3=\mathbf{B}^3, \qquad \mathbf{A}^2\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{B}^2\mathbf{A
}.$$

Consider the product $\mathbf{A}^2(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B})$:
$$\mathbf{A}^2(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B})=\mathbf{A}^3-\mathbf{A}^2\mathbf{B}.$$

Using the two given identities, $\mathbf{A}^3=\mathbf{B}^3$ and $\mathbf{A}^2\ma
thbf{B}=\mathbf{B}^2\mathbf{A}$, we substitute:
$$\mathbf{A}^3-\mathbf{A}^2\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{B}^3-\mathbf{B}^2\mathbf{A}=\mathb
f{B}^2(\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{A})=-\mathbf{B}^2(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B}).$$

Therefore
$$\mathbf{A}^2(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B})=-\mathbf{B}^2(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B}),$$
which rearranges to
$$(\mathbf{A}^2+\mathbf{B}^2)(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B})=\mathbf{0}.$$

Since $\mathbf{A}\neq\mathbf{B}$, the matrix $\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B}$ is **not**
the zero matrix, so it is a nonzero (right) annihilator of $\mathbf{A}^2+\mathbf
{B}^2$.

If $\mathbf{A}^2+\mathbf{B}^2$ were invertible, we could left-multiply by $(\mat
hbf{A}^2+\mathbf{B}^2)^{-1}$ to obtain $\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{0}$, i.e.
$\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{B}$, contradicting the hypothesis that $\mathbf{A}$ and $\ma
thbf{B}$ are different.

Hence $\mathbf{A}^2+\mathbf{B}^2$ cannot be invertible. $\blacksquare$
