## Proof

**Setup.** Consider a group extension
$$1 \longrightarrow A \stackrel{i}{\longrightarrow} G \stackrel{\pi}{\longrighta
rrow} Q \longrightarrow 1,$$
so $A \cong i(A) \trianglelefteq G$ and $Q \cong G/A$. For each coset of $A$ in
$G$ (i.e., for each $q \in Q$), choose a representative $u(q) \in G$ with $\pi(u
(q)) = q$. Thus
$$G = \bigsqcup_{q \in Q} A\, u(q).$$

Every element $g \in G$ admits a unique factorization $g = a\, u(q)$ with $a \in
 A$, $q \in Q$.

**Definition of the factor set.** For $q_1, q_2 \in Q$, the product $u(q_1)\,u(q
_2)$ lies in the coset over $q_1 q_2$, since
$$\pi\bigl(u(q_1)\,u(q_2)\bigr) = \pi(u(q_1))\,\pi(u(q_2)) = q_1 q_2.$$
Hence $u(q_1)\,u(q_2)$ and $u(q_1 q_2)$ are in the same left $A$-coset, so they
differ by a unique element of $A$. The **factor set** (2-cocycle) $f : Q \times
Q \to A$ is defined by
$$u(q_1)\,u(q_2) = f(q_1, q_2)\; u(q_1 q_2), \qquad f(q_1, q_2) \in A. \tag{$\as
t$}$$

**Complement hypothesis.** The chosen representatives $\{u(q) : q \in Q\}$ form
a **complement** to $A$ in $G$; that is, the set $H := \{u(q) : q \in Q\}$ is a
subgroup of $G$ with
$$G = A\,H, \qquad A \cap H = \{e_G\}.$$

**Key observation.** Since $H$ is a subgroup, it is closed under the group opera
tion of $G$. Therefore for every $q_1, q_2 \in Q$,
$$u(q_1)\,u(q_2) \in H.$$

Now both $u(q_1)\,u(q_2)$ and $u(q_1 q_2)$ are elements of $H$, and both lie in
the *same* left $A$-coset (the coset over $q_1 q_2$), as shown by the projection
 computation above. Their quotient
$$\bigl(u(q_1)\,u(q_2)\bigr)\,u(q_1 q_2)^{-1} = f(q_1, q_2)$$
is therefore an element of $A \cap H$. By the complement condition $A \cap H = \
{e_G\}$, we conclude
$$f(q_1, q_2) = e_G \qquad \text{for all } q_1, q_2 \in Q.$$

**Equivalently**, since $H$ is a subgroup containing exactly one element per $A$
-coset (the condition $A \cap H = \{e\}$ guarantees the $u(q)$ are pairwise in d
istinct cosets, and $|H| = |Q| = [G:A]$ guarantees they exhaust all cosets), the
 multiplication within $H$ must mirror the multiplication in $Q$:
$$u(q_1)\,u(q_2) = u(q_1 q_2).$$
Comparing with $(\ast)$ gives $f(q_1, q_2) = e_G$ identically.

**Conclusion.** The factor set $f$ measures the failure of the section $q \mapst
o u(q)$ to be a group homomorphism. When the representatives form a complement,
the section *is* a homomorphism (a splitting of the extension), so the obstructi
on vanishes: $f \equiv e_G$. Geometrically, a complement is precisely a *split*
of the extension, and a split extension has trivial cohomology class; the factor
 set, being a representative cocycle of that class, is identically the identity.
 $\blacksquare$
