Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether local finiteness of a Borel
 measure implies that the measure of right-neighborhoods shrinking to a point te
nds to zero.

**Answer: Yes, the limit holds for all $a \in \mathbb{R}$.**

**Proof.**

Fix $a \in \mathbb{R}$. By hypothesis, there exists an open set $U$ containing $
a$ with $\nu(U) < \infty$. Since $U$ is open and $a \in U$, there exists $\varep
silon > 0$ such that $(a - \varepsilon,\, a + \varepsilon) \subseteq U$. In part
icular, $(a,\, a+\varepsilon) \subseteq U$, so by monotonicity of measure,

$$\nu(a,\, a+\varepsilon) \leq \nu(U) < \infty. \tag{1}$$

Now let $\delta_n \downarrow 0$ be any sequence of positive reals converging to
$0$. For all sufficiently large $n$, we have $\delta_n < \varepsilon$, so $(a,\,
 a+\delta_n) \subseteq (a,\, a+\varepsilon)$, and thus $\nu(a,\, a+\delta_n) < \
infty$ by (1).

The sets $E_n := (a,\, a+\delta_n)$ form a decreasing sequence: $E_1 \supseteq E
_2 \supseteq \cdots$. Their intersection is empty:

$$\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} (a,\, a+\delta_n) = \varnothing,$$

since for any $x > a$, choosing $n$ large enough that $\delta_n < x - a$ gives $
x \notin (a,\, a+\delta_n)$.

Since $\nu(E_N) < \infty$ for some $N$ (any $N$ with $\delta_N < \varepsilon$),
we may apply **continuity from above**: for a decreasing sequence of measurable
sets $E_n \downarrow E$ with $\nu(E_N) < \infty$ for some $N$,

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \nu(E_n) = \nu\!\left(\bigcap_{n} E_n\right).$$

Therefore,

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \nu(a,\, a+\delta_n) = \nu(\varnothing) = 0.$$

Since this holds for **every** sequence $\delta_n \downarrow 0$, we conclude

$$\lim_{\delta \downarrow 0} \nu(a,\, a+\delta) = 0. \qquad \blacksquare$$

**Key idea:** The hypothesis guarantees that every point $a$ has a right-neighbo
rhood $(a, a+\varepsilon)$ of finite measure. This finiteness is exactly what is
 needed to apply continuity from above to the decreasing family $(a, a+\delta) \
downarrow \varnothing$, yielding the limit $0$.
