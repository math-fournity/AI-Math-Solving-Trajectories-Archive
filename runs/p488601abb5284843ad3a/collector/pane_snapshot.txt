# Best Uniform Approximation Error for Real Analytic Functions

## Statement

Let $f$ be real analytic on a closed interval $[a,b]$. The best uniform approxim
ation error
$$E_n(f) := \inf_{p \in \mathcal{P}_n} \|f - p\|_{\infty,[a,b]}$$
satisfies an upper bound of **geometric (exponential) decay**:
$$\boxed{E_n(f) \le C\,\rho^{\,n}, \qquad 0 < \rho < 1,\; C > 0,}$$
equivalently $E_n(f) \le C'\,\rho'^{-n}$ for some $\rho' > 1$.

---

## Proof

**Step 1 — Reduction to $[-1,1]$.**
By the affine change of variable $x = \tfrac{b-a}{2}t + \tfrac{b+a}{2}$, approxi
mation on $[a,b]$ by polynomials of degree $\le n$ in $x$ is equivalent to appro
ximation on $[-1,1]$ by polynomials of degree $\le n$ in $t$. The error scales b
y the fixed factor $\tfrac{b-a}{2}$, which is absorbed into the constant. Hence
it suffices to prove the result on $[-1,1]$.

**Step 2 — Analytic extension to a complex neighborhood.**
Since $f$ is real analytic on $[-1,1]$, for every $x_0 \in [-1,1]$ there exists
$r_{x_0} > 0$ such that $f$ admits a convergent power series expansion $\sum_{k\
ge 0} c_k(x_0)\,(x-x_0)^k$ with radius of convergence $\ge r_{x_0}$, defining a
holomorphic extension to the disk $D(x_0, r_{x_0}) \subset \mathbb{C}$. The coll
ection $\{D(x_0, r_{x_0}/2) : x_0 \in [-1,1]\}$ is an open cover of the compact
set $[-1,1]$; extract a finite subcover. On the union $U$ of the corresponding d
isks $D(x_j, r_{x_j})$, the local power series define a single holomorphic funct
ion (they agree on real overlaps and hence on complex overlaps by the identity t
heorem). Thus $f$ extends to a function holomorphic on an open set $U \supset [-
1,1]$.

**Step 3 — Containment in a Bernstein ellipse.**
The **Bernstein ellipse** $E_\rho$ ($\rho > 1$) is the image of the circle $|w|=
\rho$ under the Joukowski map $z = \tfrac{1}{2}(w + w^{-1})$; its interior is $\
{z = \tfrac12(w+w^{-1}) : |w| < \rho\}$, an open set containing $[-1,1]$ whose b
oundary shrinks to $[-1,1]$ as $\rho \downarrow 1$. Since $U$ is an open neighbo
rhood of $[-1,1]$, there exists $\rho_0 > 1$ such that the closed Bernstein elli
pse $\overline{E_{\rho_0}} \subset U$. Set $M := \max_{z \in \overline{E_{\rho_0
}}} |f(z)| < \infty$ (continuous on the compact boundary, holomorphic inside, so
 bounded by the maximum principle).

**Step 4 — Chebyshev expansion and coefficient decay.**
Write $z = \cos\theta$ and expand $f$ in a Chebyshev series $f(\cos\theta) = \tf
rac12 a_0 + \sum_{k\ge 1} a_k \cos(k\theta)$. The substitution $w = e^{i\theta}$
 gives $z = \tfrac12(w + w^{-1})$, so $f$ is holomorphic for $|w| < \rho_0$ as a
 function of $w$ (and $w^{-1}$). The Chebyshev coefficients are
$$a_k = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi f(\cos\theta)\cos(k\theta)\,d\theta = \frac{1}{\
pi i}\oint_{|w|=1} f\!\bigl(\tfrac12(w+w^{-1})\bigr)\,\frac{w^k + w^{-k}}{2}\,\f
rac{dw}{w}.$$
Deforming the contour to $|w| = r$ for any $1 < r < \rho_0$ and using $|f| \le M
$ there, the term $\tfrac12(w^k + w^{-k})/w$ contributes $O(r^k)$ from the $w^k$
 part and $O(r^{-k})$ from the $w^{-k}$ part; the dominant contribution is $O(r^
{-k})$. Optimizing or simply taking $r$ close to $\rho_0$ yields
$$|a_k| \le \frac{2M}{\rho_0^k}, \qquad k \ge 0.$$

**Step 5 — Truncation bound.**
The Chebyshev partial sum $S_n(z) = \tfrac12 a_0 + \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k T_k(z)$ is
 a polynomial of degree $\le n$, so
$$E_n(f) \le \|f - S_n\|_{\infty,[-1,1]} \le \sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty} |a_k| \le \fr
ac{2M}{\rho_0^{n+1}}\cdot\frac{1}{1 - 1/\rho_0} = \frac{2M}{\rho_0^{n+1}(1-\rho_
0^{-1})}.$$
Setting $C = \frac{2M}{\rho_0(1-\rho_0^{-1})}$ and $\rho = \rho_0^{-1} \in (0,1)
$,
$$E_n(f) \le C\,\rho^{\,n}.$$

**Step 6 — Conclusion.**
Reverting the affine change of variables only rescales $C$ by the fixed factor $
\tfrac{b-a}{2}$. Therefore, for $f$ real analytic on $[a,b]$, there exist consta
nts $C > 0$ and $0 < \rho < 1$ (determined by the size of the complex neighborho
od to which $f$ extends) such that
$$\inf_{p \in \mathcal{P}_n}\|f - p\|_\infty \;\le\; C\,\rho^{\,n} \qquad \text{
for all } n \ge 0.$$

This is the **geometric (exponential) decay** form of the upper bound, character
istic of real analytic functions. (The converse—Bernstein's theorem—also holds:
geometric decay of $E_n(f)$ implies analytic extendability, so this rate is shar
p for the analytic class.)
