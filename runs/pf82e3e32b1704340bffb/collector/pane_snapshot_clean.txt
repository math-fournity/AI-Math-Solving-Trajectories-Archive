## Proof

**Key observation.** Since $\zeta(m)\to 1$ as $m\to\infty$, the individual sums
$\sum \zeta(2n)$ and $\sum \zeta(2n+1)$ diverge, but the differences $\zeta(2n)-
\zeta(2n+1)\to 0$ rapidly, so the sum of differences converges. We work with the
 "regularized" quantities $\zeta(m)-1$.

### Step 1. A telescoping identity

For $n\ge 2$, $\zeta(n)-1=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}k^{-n}$. All terms are positive, so
 by Tonelli's theorem we may swap the order of summation:

$$\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\bigl(\zeta(n)-1\bigr)=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\sum_{n=2}^{\inft
y}\frac{1}{k^{n}}=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1/k^{2}}{1-1/k}=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\f
rac{1}{k(k-1)}.$$

Using partial fractions, $\frac{1}{k(k-1)}=\frac{1}{k-1}-\frac{1}{k}$, giving a
telescoping series:

$$\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k-1}-\frac{1}{k}\right)=1.\tag{1}$$

### Step 2. The even part

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl(\zeta(2n)-1\bigr)=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\inf
ty}\frac{1}{k^{2n}}=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1/k^{2}}{1-1/k^{2}}=\sum_{k=2}^{\in
fty}\frac{1}{k^{2}-1}.$$

Since $\frac{1}{k^{2}-1}=\frac{1}{(k-1)(k+1)}=\frac{1}{2}\!\left(\frac{1}{k-1}-\
frac{1}{k+1}\right)$, the partial sum telescopes:

$$\sum_{k=2}^{N}\frac{1}{k^{2}-1}=\frac{1}{2}\!\left[\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{j}
-\sum_{j=3}^{N+1}\frac{1}{j}\right]=\frac{1}{2}\!\left(1+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{N}
-\frac{1}{N+1}\right)\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}\frac{3}{4}.\tag{2}$$

### Step 3. The odd part

Splitting (1) into even and odd indices ($n=2,3,4,5,\dots$ corresponds to $2n$ w
ith $n\ge1$ and $2n+1$ with $n\ge1$):

$$\underbrace{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl(\zeta(2n)-1\bigr)}_{\text{even}}+\underbr
ace{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl(\zeta(2n+1)-1\bigr)}_{\text{odd}}=1.$$

Using (2):

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl(\zeta(2n+1)-1\bigr)=1-\frac{3}{4}=\frac{1}{4}.\tag{3}
$$

### Step 4. The sum of differences

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl[\zeta(2n)-\zeta(2n+1)\bigr]=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl[
(\zeta(2n)-1)-(\zeta(2n+1)-1)\bigr]$$

$$=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl(\zeta(2n)-1\bigr)-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl(\zeta(2n+
1)-1\bigr)=\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{4}=\boxed{\frac{1}{2}}.$$

Both series in the difference converge (each term is $O(2^{-2n})$), so the subtr
action is justified.

**Conclusion.** The sum of the differences between the positive even and odd int
eger values of the Riemann zeta function **does** equal $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
