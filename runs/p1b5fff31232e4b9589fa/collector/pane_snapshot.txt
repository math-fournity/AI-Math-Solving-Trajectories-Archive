## Proof

The statement is **false**. We provide an explicit counterexample.

**Setup.** Let $n = 3$ and $p = 7$. Then $n = 3 > 2$ and $n \mid (p-1)$ since $3
 \mid 6$. Consider the two polynomials
$$f(x) = x^3 - 1, \qquad g(x) = x^3 - 8.$$
Both have degree $3 = n > 2$.

**Common root mod $p$.** Reducing modulo $7$:
$$g(x) = x^3 - 8 \equiv x^3 - 1 \equiv f(x) \pmod{7}.$$
Thus $f$ and $g$ are identical modulo $7$, so they share every root they have in
 $\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z}$. Concretely, $x^3 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$ is solved by $x =
 1, 2, 4$ (since $1^3=1$, $2^3=8\equiv 1$, $4^3=64\equiv 1 \pmod{7}$). In partic
ular, $x = 1$ is a common root of $f$ and $g$ in $\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z}$.

**No common root in $\mathbb{C}$.** Over $\mathbb{C}$, the roots of $f(x) = x^3
- 1$ are the cube roots of unity:
$$1,\; \omega,\; \omega^2, \qquad \text{where } \omega = e^{2\pi i/3}.$$
The roots of $g(x) = x^3 - 8$ are:
$$2,\; 2\omega,\; 2\omega^2.$$
For a common root to exist, we would need $2\omega^j = \omega^k$ for some $j, k
\in \{0, 1, 2\}$, which gives $2 = \omega^{k-j}$. But $\omega^{k-j} \in \{1, \om
ega, \omega^2\}$, and none of these equals $2$ (they are all roots of unity, hen
ce have modulus $1$, while $|2| = 2$). Therefore $f$ and $g$ share **no** common
 root in $\mathbb{C}$.

**Conclusion.** We have exhibited two polynomials of degree $n = 3 > 2$, a prime
 $p = 7$ with $n \mid (p-1)$, such that $f$ and $g$ have a common root in $\math
bb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ but no common root in $\mathbb{C}$. The statement is **false*
*.

**Remark.** The condition $n \mid (p-1)$ ensures that $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$ (cyc
lic of order $p-1$) contains primitive $n$-th roots of unity, so that $x^n - 1$
splits completely mod $p$. However, this structural fact about $\mathbb{F}_p$ sa
ys nothing about whether two *arbitrary* degree-$n$ polynomials that coincide (o
r share a root) mod $p$ must share a root over $\mathbb{C}$. Reducing modulo $p$
 collapses distinct integers (here $1$ and $8$), and this collapsing can create
spurious common roots that do not lift to characteristic zero.
