## Solution

We evaluate
$$I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigl(\sin^{2} x + 2\tan x\bigr)\,\delta'(x)\,dx.$$

**Key identity.** For the derivative of the Dirac delta distribution, the defini
ng property is
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,\delta'(x)\,dx = -f'(0),$$
valid whenever $f$ is smooth in a neighborhood of $x=0$ (the support of $\delta'
$ is $\{0\}$, so only the behavior of $f$ near the origin matters).

**Regularity check.** Let $f(x) = \sin^{2} x + 2\tan x$. Near $x=0$:
- $\sin^{2} x$ is smooth everywhere.
- $\tan x = x + \tfrac{x^{3}}{3} + \cdots$ is analytic at $x=0$ (its nearest sin
gularities are at $x = \pm\tfrac{\pi}{2}$, away from the origin).

Hence $f$ is smooth in a neighborhood of $0$, and the identity applies. The sing
ularities of $\tan x$ at $\pm\pi/2, \pm3\pi/2,\ldots$ are irrelevant because $\d
elta'$ is supported only at $\{0\}$.

**Compute the derivative.**
$$f'(x) = 2\sin x\cos x + 2\sec^{2} x = \sin 2x + 2\sec^{2} x.$$

Evaluating at the origin:
$$f'(0) = \sin 0 + 2\sec^{2} 0 = 0 + 2\cdot 1 = 2.$$

**Apply the identity.**
$$I = -f'(0) = -2.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
