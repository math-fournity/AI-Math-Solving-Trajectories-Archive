# Solution

## Answer: 8 colors

## Proof

**Setup.** The $100 \times 100$ board has $101 \times 101$ grid points. Each of
the $10000$ cells is split by a diagonal into $2$ triangles, giving $20000$ tria
ngles total. Two triangles are *adjacent* (must receive different colors) if the
y share a side or a vertex. Note that sharing a side implies sharing the two end
points of that side, so the adjacency condition is equivalent to: **two triangle
s are adjacent iff they share at least one grid point (corner).**

---

### Lower Bound: 8 colors are necessary

Consider any interior grid point $P = (a, b)$ with $1 \le a, b \le 99$. Four cel
ls meet at $P$: cells $(a{-}1, b{-}1)$, $(a, b{-}1)$, $(a{-}1, b)$, and $(a, b)$
. In each cell, $P$ is a different corner (NE, NW, SE, SW respectively). For eac
h cell, we can choose the diagonal to pass through $P$ (each cell independently
selects its diagonal). When the diagonal of a cell passes through $P$, **both**
triangles of that cell have $P$ as a vertex.

If all four diagonals pass through $P$, then all $4 \times 2 = 8$ triangles inci
dent to $P$ share the vertex $P$. Any two of them share $P$, so they are pairwis
e adjacent, forming a **clique of size 8**. Therefore, at least **8 colors** are
 required.

---

### Upper Bound: 8 colors suffice

We construct an explicit 8-coloring valid for **any** diagonal arrangement.

**Coloring scheme.** There are $4 \times 2 = 8$ colors, indexed by pairs $(s, t)
$ with $s \in \{0,1,2,3\}$ and $t \in \{0,1\}$.

For each cell $(i, j)$ (with $0 \le i, j \le 99$):
- Assign $s = (i + 2j) \bmod 4$ to both triangles in the cell.
- Assign $t = 0$ to one triangle and $t = 1$ to the other (arbitrarily).

So each triangle in cell $(i,j)$ receives color $((i+2j) \bmod 4,\; t)$ where $t
 \in \{0,1\}$ distinguishes the two halves.

**Verification.** We must show that at every grid point $(a, b)$, all incident t
riangles have distinct colors. The cells incident to $(a,b)$ are a subset of $\{
(a{-}1,b{-}1),\; (a,b{-}1),\; (a{-}1,b),\; (a,b)\}$ (all four for interior point
s, fewer on boundaries). Their $s$-values are:

| Cell | $s = (i+2j) \bmod 4$ |
|------|----------------------|
| $(a{-}1, b{-}1)$ | $(a + 2b - 3) \bmod 4$ |
| $(a, b{-}1)$ | $(a + 2b - 2) \bmod 4$ |
| $(a{-}1, b)$ | $(a + 2b - 1) \bmod 4$ |
| $(a, b)$ | $(a + 2b) \bmod 4$ |

These are **four consecutive integers modulo 4**, hence pairwise distinct. There
fore:

1. **Triangles from different cells** at $(a,b)$ have different $s$-values, henc
e different colors — regardless of their $t$-values.
2. **Triangles from the same cell** at $(a,b)$ (at most two, present only when t
he diagonal passes through $(a,b)$) share the same $s$-value but have $t$-values
 $0$ and $1$, hence different colors.

Thus all triangles incident to any grid point $(a,b)$ receive **pairwise distinc
t colors**. Since two triangles sharing a side also share a vertex (an endpoint
of that side), the vertex condition automatically ensures the side condition. Th
e coloring is valid. $\checkmark$

---

### Conclusion

The lower bound of 8 (from a clique of size 8 at a worst-case interior vertex) m
atches the upper bound of 8 (from the explicit construction above). The smallest
 number of colors needed is $\boxed{8}$.
