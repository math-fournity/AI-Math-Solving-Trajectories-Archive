# IMO 1998 Problem 2 — Proof

**Setup.** Encode each rating as $r_{j,c}\in\{0,1\}$, where $j\in\{1,\dots,b\}$
is a judge and $c\in\{1,\dots,a\}$ is a contestant. For contestant $c$, let $p_c
$ be the number of judges rating "pass" and $q_c=b-p_c$ the number rating "fail"
.

**Counting total coincidences two ways.** For judges $i\neq j$, let $\operatorna
me{coinc}(i,j)$ be the number of contestants on which they agree. Summing over a
ll unordered pairs of judges and swapping the order of summation:

$$\sum_{1\le i<j\le b}\operatorname{coinc}(i,j) \;=\; \sum_{c=1}^{a}\Bigl[\binom
{p_c}{2}+\binom{q_c}{2}\Bigr],$$

since for a fixed contestant $c$, exactly $\binom{p_c}{2}$ pairs of judges both
pass and $\binom{q_c}{2}$ pairs both fail.

**Lower bound (per contestant).** Using $p_c+q_c=b$,

$$\binom{p_c}{2}+\binom{q_c}{2}=\frac{p_c^2+q_c^2-b}{2}.$$

The quantity $p_c^2+q_c^2=p_c^2+(b-p_c)^2=2p_c^2-2bp_c+b^2$ is a convex function
 of $p_c$, minimized at $p_c=b/2$. Since $b$ is **odd**, the closest integer val
ues are $p_c=\frac{b-1}{2}$ and $p_c=\frac{b+1}{2}$, both giving

$$p_c^2+q_c^2=\frac{(b-1)^2+(b+1)^2}{4}=\frac{b^2+1}{2}.$$

Hence for every contestant $c$,

$$\binom{p_c}{2}+\binom{q_c}{2}\;\ge\;\frac{\frac{b^2+1}{2}-b}{2}\;=\;\frac{b^2-
2b+1}{4}\;=\;\frac{(b-1)^2}{4}.$$

Summing over all $a$ contestants:

$$\sum_{1\le i<j\le b}\operatorname{coinc}(i,j)\;\ge\;a\cdot\frac{(b-1)^2}{4}.\t
ag{1}$$

**Upper bound (using the hypothesis).** By hypothesis every pair of judges coinc
ides on at most $k$ contestants, and there are $\binom{b}{2}=\frac{b(b-1)}{2}$ p
airs, so

$$\sum_{1\le i<j\le b}\operatorname{coinc}(i,j)\;\le\;k\cdot\frac{b(b-1)}{2}.\ta
g{2}$$

**Combining (1) and (2):**

$$a\cdot\frac{(b-1)^2}{4}\;\le\;k\cdot\frac{b(b-1)}{2}.$$

Since $b\ge 3$, we have $b-1>0$, so divide both sides by $\frac{b-1}{2}$:

$$a\cdot\frac{b-1}{2}\;\le\;k\cdot b,$$

which gives

$$\frac{k}{a}\;\ge\;\frac{b-1}{2b}.$$

This is exactly the desired inequality. $\blacksquare$
