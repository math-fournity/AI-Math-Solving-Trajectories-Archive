## Proof

**Yes.** For every integer $m \geq 2$, there exists an elliptic curve $E/\mathbb
{C}$ and a distortion map $\phi$ such that $\{T, \phi(T)\}$ is a basis of $E[m]$
 for some $T \in E[m]$.

### Construction

Consider the elliptic curve
$$E: y^2 = x^3 - x$$
over $\mathbb{C}$. This curve has $j$-invariant $1728$ and complex multiplicatio
n by the Gaussian integers $\mathbb{Z}[i]$. The CM endomorphism is:
$$\phi: E \to E, \qquad \phi(x, y) = (-x,\, iy).$$

**Verification that $\phi$ is an endomorphism.** If $y^2 = x^3 - x$, then $(iy)^
2 = -y^2 = -(x^3 - x) = -x^3 + x = (-x)^3 - (-x)$, so $\phi(x,y) \in E$. ✓

### Uniformization and the $m$-torsion

Over $\mathbb{C}$, we have $E(\mathbb{C}) \cong \mathbb{C}/\Lambda$ where, for t
his CM curve, the period lattice is (up to homothety) $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z} + \m
athbb{Z}\,i$. The endomorphism $\phi$ corresponds to multiplication by $i$ on $\
mathbb{C}/\Lambda$, which is well-defined because $i \cdot \Lambda = \Lambda$ (s
ince $i \cdot 1 = i \in \Lambda$ and $i \cdot i = -1 \in \Lambda$).

The $m$-torsion subgroup is:
$$E[m] = \tfrac{1}{m}\Lambda\,/\,\Lambda \;\cong\; (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^2.$$

A natural $\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$-basis of $E[m]$ is $\{T_1, T_2\}$ where
$$T_1 = \frac{1}{m} \bmod \Lambda, \qquad T_2 = \frac{i}{m} \bmod \Lambda.$$

### The distortion map sends $T$ to a linearly independent point

Take $T = T_1 = \frac{1}{m} \bmod \Lambda$, which has exact order $m$ (since $k
\cdot \frac{1}{m} \in \Lambda$ iff $m \mid k$). Applying the distortion map:
$$\phi(T) = [i]\!\left(\frac{1}{m}\right) = \frac{i}{m} \bmod \Lambda = T_2.$$

Therefore $\{T,\, \phi(T)\} = \{T_1, T_2\}$.

**Linear independence.** Suppose $aT + b\,\phi(T) = O$ for $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}/
m\mathbb{Z}$. This means
$$\frac{a + bi}{m} \in \Lambda = \mathbb{Z} + \mathbb{Z}\,i,$$
so $a + bi = m(c + di)$ for some $c, d \in \mathbb{Z}$, giving $a \equiv 0 \pmod
{m}$ and $b \equiv 0 \pmod{m}$. Hence $T$ and $\phi(T)$ are linearly independent
 over $\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$.

Since $|E[m]| = m^2$ and $\{T, \phi(T)\}$ generates a subgroup of order $m^2$, t
he set $\{T, \phi(T)\}$ is a **basis** of $E[m]$.

**Distortion property.** Since $\phi(T) = T_2 \notin \langle T_1 \rangle$ (as $T
_2$ is not a $\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$-scalar multiple of $T_1$), the map $\phi$
is indeed a distortion map for $T$.

### Why CM is necessary (and why this is the right approach)

A non-CM elliptic curve has $\operatorname{End}(E) = \mathbb{Z}$, so every endom
orphism is $[n]$ for some $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, and $\phi(T) = nT \in \langle T \r
angle$ always. Thus no distortion map can exist on a non-CM curve. The construct
ion above exploits the extra endomorphism furnished by complex multiplication: t
he CM element $i \in \mathbb{Z}[i] \setminus \mathbb{Z}$ provides the distortion
.

### Conclusion

For every integer $m \geq 2$, the CM elliptic curve $E: y^2 = x^3 - x$ with dist
ortion map $\phi(x,y) = (-x, iy)$ and the point $T = \frac{1}{m} \bmod \Lambda$
yield $\{T, \phi(T)\}$ as a basis of $E[m] \cong (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^2$. (F
or $m = 1$, $E[1] = \{O\}$ is trivial and the statement is vacuous; for $m \leq
0$, one reduces to $|m|$.)
