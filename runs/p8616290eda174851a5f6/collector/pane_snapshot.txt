## Proof

**Claim.** Yes, the Volterra operator $T: L^1([0,1]) \to C([0,1])$, $T(f)(x)=\in
t_0^x f(t)\,dt$, is weak‑strong continuous: whenever $f_n \rightharpoonup f$ wea
kly in $L^1([0,1])$, we have $\|T(f_n)-T(f)\|_\infty \to 0$.

---

**Step 1 — Pointwise convergence.** For each $x\in[0,1]$, the indicator $\mathbf
{1}_{[0,x]}$ belongs to $L^\infty([0,1]) = (L^1([0,1]))^*$. Hence weak convergen
ce $f_n\rightharpoonup f$ gives
$$
T(f_n)(x)-T(f)(x)=\int_0^1 \bigl(f_n(t)-f(t)\bigr)\mathbf{1}_{[0,x]}(t)\,dt \;\x
rightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\; 0.
$$
So $T(f_n)\to T(f)$ pointwise on $[0,1]$.

**Step 2 — Uniform integrability of $\{f_n\}$.** A weakly convergent sequence in
 $L^1$ is relatively weakly compact. By the **Dunford–Pettis theorem**, a subset
 of $L^1([0,1])$ is relatively weakly compact iff it is uniformly integrable (eq
ui‑integrable): there exists $M<\infty$ with $\sup_n\|f_n\|_1\le M$, and for eve
ry $\varepsilon>0$ there is $\delta>0$ such that
$$
|E|<\delta \;\Longrightarrow\; \sup_n\int_E |f_n(t)|\,dt < \varepsilon. \tag{UI}
$$

**Step 3 — Equicontinuity of $\{T(f_n)\}$.** For $0\le x<y\le 1$,
$$
\bigl|T(f_n)(y)-T(f_n)(x)\bigr|=\left|\int_x^y f_n(t)\,dt\right|\le \int_{[x,y]}
|f_n(t)|\,dt.
$$
The set $[x,y]$ has measure $|y-x|$. By (UI), choosing $\delta$ for a given $\va
repsilon$,
$$
|y-x|<\delta \;\Longrightarrow\; \sup_n\bigl|T(f_n)(y)-T(f_n)(x)\bigr|<\varepsil
on.
$$
Thus $\{T(f_n)\}_n$ is equicontinuous (the case $x>y$ is identical, and $x=y$ is
 trivial).

**Step 4 — Uniform boundedness of $\{T(f_n)\}$.** Using $\sup_n\|f_n\|_1\le M$ f
rom Step 2,
$$
\sup_n\|T(f_n)\|_\infty=\sup_n\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\left|\int_0^x f_n\right|\le \sup_
n\|f_n\|_1\le M<\infty.
$$

**Step 5 — Arzelà–Ascoli.** The family $\{T(f_n)\}\subset C([0,1])$ is uniformly
 bounded and equicontinuous, hence relatively compact in $(C([0,1]),\|\cdot\|_\i
nfty)$ by the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem.

**Step 6 — From pointwise to uniform.** Let $g_n:=T(f_n)$ and $g:=T(f)$. We have
 $g_n\to g$ pointwise (Step 1) and $\{g_n\}$ is relatively compact in $\|\cdot\|
_\infty$ (Step 5). Take any subsequence $\{g_{n_k}\}$; by relative compactness i
t has a further subsequence $\{g_{n_{k_j}}\}$ converging uniformly to some $\til
de g\in C([0,1])$. Uniform convergence implies pointwise convergence, so $\tilde
 g = g$ by the pointwise limit from Step 1. Hence *every* subsequence of $\{g_n\
}$ has a further subsequence converging uniformly to $g$, which forces
$$
\|T(f_n)-T(f)\|_\infty = \|g_n-g\|_\infty \xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{} 0.
$$

---

Therefore the Volterra operator $T:L^1([0,1])\to C([0,1])$ is weak‑strong contin
uous. $\blacksquare$
