## Solution

**Problem.** Evaluate $\displaystyle \oint_{C} \frac{\sin\pi z^2+\cos\pi z^2}{(z
-1)(z-2)}\,dz$ where $C$ is the circle $|z|=3$.

**Answer-leak check.** The problem statement contains no solutions, hints, or fo
rmalization notes. Proceeding.

---

### Analysis

Let $f(z) = \sin\pi z^2 + \cos\pi z^2$. This is an entire function (sum of entir
e functions).

The integrand is
$$g(z) = \frac{f(z)}{(z-1)(z-2)}.$$

The only singularities are at $z=1$ and $z=2$, both simple poles. Since $|1|=1<3
$ and $|2|=2<3$, both poles lie inside the contour $C: |z|=3$. No other singular
ities exist (the numerator is entire), so by the **Residue Theorem**:

$$\oint_C g(z)\,dz = 2\pi i\left(\operatorname{Res}_{z=1} g + \operatorname{Res}
_{z=2} g\right).$$

### Computing the residues

For a simple pole at $z=a$ of $\dfrac{f(z)}{(z-1)(z-2)}$:

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=a} g = \frac{f(a)}{a'-\text{other root}}.$$

**Residue at $z=1$:**
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=1} g = \frac{f(1)}{1-2} = \frac{\sin\pi + \cos\pi}{-1} =
 \frac{0 + (-1)}{-1} = \frac{-1}{-1} = 1.$$

**Residue at $z=2$:**
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=2} g = \frac{f(2)}{2-1} = \frac{\sin 4\pi + \cos 4\pi}{1
} = \frac{0 + 1}{1} = 1.$$

### Assembling the result

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=1} g + \operatorname{Res}_{z=2} g = 1 + 1 = 2.$$

Therefore

$$\oint_{C} \frac{\sin\pi z^2+\cos\pi z^2}{(z-1)(z-2)}\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot 2 = \b
oxed{4\pi i}.$$
