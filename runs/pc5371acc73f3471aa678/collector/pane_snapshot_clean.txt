## Proof

The "original question" refers to the standard setup: the cyclic permutation mat
rix $A$ and the diagonal matrix $B = \operatorname{diag}(1, W, W^2, \ldots, W^{n
-1})$, where $W$ is a primitive $n$-th root of unity. Let $H = \langle A, B \ran
gle \subset GL(n)$.

### Step 1: The Commutation Relation

Using 0-indexing, $A$ has entries $A_{i,\,i+1 \bmod n} = 1$, and $B_{jj} = W^j$.
 Computing:

$$(AB)_{i,\,i+1} = A_{i,\,i+1}\, B_{i+1,\,i+1} = W^{i+1}, \qquad (BA)_{i,\,i+1}
= B_{ii}\, A_{i,\,i+1} = W^{i}.$$

Therefore $AB = W \cdot BA$, equivalently:

$$ABA^{-1}B^{-1} = W \cdot I.$$

### Step 2: The Center

The scalar matrix $W \cdot I$ lies in $H$ (from the commutator above). Since $W$
 is a primitive $n$-th root of unity, $Z := \langle W \cdot I \rangle$ has order
 $n$.

**$Z$ is the full center of $H$.** Any central element must commute with both $A
$ and $B$. A diagonal element $\operatorname{diag}(W^{a_1}, \ldots, W^{a_n})$ co
mmutes with $A$ iff cyclic conjugation fixes it, i.e., all $a_i$ are equal — giv
ing exactly $Z$. A non-diagonal element $A^j D_0$ ($j \not\equiv 0$) fails to co
mmute with $B$ since $A^j B A^{-j}$ has exponent vector $v_0 - je \neq v_0$. So
$Z(H) = Z$, $|Z| = n$.

### Step 3: The Diagonal Subgroup $D$

Let $D = H \cap \{\text{diagonal matrices}\}$. This is generated by the conjugat
es $A^k B A^{-k}$ for $k = 0, \ldots, n-1$. The exponent vector of $A^k B A^{-k}
$ is the cyclic shift $v_k$ of $v_0 = (0, 1, 2, \ldots, n-1)$.

**Key identity:** $v_k = v_0 - k \cdot e$ where $e = (1, 1, \ldots, 1)$, since s
hifting $(0, 1, \ldots, n-1)$ cyclically by $k$ gives $(-k, 1-k, \ldots, n-1-k)
\pmod{n}$.

Therefore $D = \langle v_0, e \rangle$ in $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^n$.

**Injectivity:** The map $(k, l) \mapsto k\,e + l\,v_0$ sends to $(k,\; k+l,\; k
+2l,\; \ldots,\; k+(n-1)l)$. Setting this to zero: the first component gives $k
\equiv 0 \pmod{n}$, the second gives $l \equiv 0 \pmod{n}$. So the map is inject
ive and:

$$|D| = n^2.$$

### Step 4: Semidirect Product Structure

- **$D \trianglelefteq H$**: $D$ is generated by conjugates of $B$; conjugation
by $A$ permutes these cyclically, and conjugation by $B \in D$ preserves $D$ (as
 $D$ is abelian).
- **$D \cap \langle A \rangle = \{I\}$**: $A^k$ for $0 < k < n$ is a non-diagona
l permutation matrix.
- **$H = D \cdot \langle A \rangle$**: $H$ is generated by $D$ and $A$ (since $B
 \in D$).

Therefore $H = D \rtimes \langle A \rangle$ and:

$$|H| = |D| \cdot |\langle A \rangle| = n^2 \cdot n = n^3.$$

### Step 5: Parity Does Not Affect the Order

Note that $Z \leq D$ (since $W \cdot I$ corresponds to $e \in D$), and in the qu
otient $H/Z$, the images $\bar{A}, \bar{B}$ commute (since $[A,B] = W \cdot I \i
n Z$) with $\bar{A}^n = \bar{B}^n = 1$, giving $H/Z \cong (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z
})^2$. Thus:

$$|H| = |Z| \cdot |H/Z| = n \cdot n^2 = n^3.$$

This argument uses only that $W$ is a primitive $n$-th root of unity (so $W \cdo
t I$ has order $n$) and that $A^n = B^n = I$. **The parity of $n$ plays no role*
* — the order is $n^3$ for all $n \geq 2$, including even $n$.

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{|H| = n^3}$$

The group $H$ is a Heisenberg-type group of order $n^3$, isomorphic to $(\mathbb
{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^2 \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ with center $\langle W \cdot
I \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, regardless of whether $n$ is even or od
d.
