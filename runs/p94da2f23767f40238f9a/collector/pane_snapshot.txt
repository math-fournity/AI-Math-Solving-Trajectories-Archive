Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a finite group acting freel
y on a locally compact Hausdorff space always yields a quotient that can be cove
red by finitely many open sets admitting local cross-sections.

**Answer: No.**

## Counterexample

Let $G = \mathbb{Z}/2$ and $X = \bigsqcup_{n=2}^{\infty} S^n$ (topological disjo
int union of $n$-spheres). Since each $S^n$ is compact Hausdorff, $X$ is locally
 compact Hausdorff. Let $G$ act by the antipodal map $x \mapsto -x$ on each $S^n
$. This action is free ($x = -x$ has no solution on $S^n$ for $n \geq 1$). The q
uotient is
$$X/G = \bigsqcup_{n=2}^{\infty} \mathbb{R}P^n,$$
and $\pi: X \to X/G$ is a 2-fold covering map (any finite group acting freely on
 a Hausdorff space yields a covering map), hence admits local cross-sections.

## Key Lemma

**The 2-fold covering $p_n: S^n \to \mathbb{R}P^n$ cannot be trivialized over fe
wer than $n+1$ open sets.**

*Proof.* The covering $p_n$ is classified by the generator $\alpha_n \in H^1(\ma
thbb{R}P^n;\, \mathbb{Z}/2)$, with $p_n^*(\alpha_n) = 0$ since $H^1(S^n;\,\mathb
b{Z}/2) = 0$ for $n \geq 2$. The cohomology ring is
$$H^*(\mathbb{R}P^n;\, \mathbb{Z}/2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2[\alpha_n]/(\alpha_n^{n+1
}),$$
so $\alpha_n^j \neq 0$ for $0 \leq j \leq n$.

Suppose $\mathbb{R}P^n = V_1 \cup \cdots \cup V_k$ with each $V_i$ open and admi
tting a section $s_i: V_i \to S^n$. Since $p_n \circ s_i = \mathrm{id}_{V_i}$, w
e have $s_i^* \circ p_n^* = \mathrm{id}$ on cohomology, so $\alpha_n|_{V_i} = s_
i^*(p_n^*(\alpha_n)) = 0$ for each $i$.

By the standard cup-product argument for open covers: if a class $\beta \in H^1(
B; R)$ restricts to zero on each set of a $k$-fold open cover, then $\beta^k = 0
$ in $H^k(B; R)$. (This follows from the Čech–de Rham/Mayer–Vietoris comparison:
 the class $\beta$ is represented by a Čech 1-cocycle on the nerve of the cover,
 and since it vanishes on each vertex, $\beta^k$ vanishes on all $k$-simplices,
hence is zero.)

Applying this with $\beta = \alpha_n$: since $\alpha_n|_{V_i} = 0$ for all $i$,
we get $\alpha_n^k = 0$. But $\alpha_n^k \neq 0$ for $k \leq n$. Therefore $k \g
eq n+1$. $\square$

## Conclusion

Suppose for contradiction that $X/G = \bigsqcup_{n=2}^{\infty} \mathbb{R}P^n$ ad
mits a finite open cover $\{U_1, \ldots, U_k\}$ with each $U_j$ carrying a secti
on $s_j: U_j \to X$ of $\pi$.

For each $n \geq 2$, the intersections $U_j \cap \mathbb{R}P^n$ ($j = 1, \ldots,
 k$) form an open cover of $\mathbb{R}P^n$. The restriction $s_j|_{U_j \cap \mat
hbb{R}P^n}$ is a section of $p_n: S^n \to \mathbb{R}P^n$ over $U_j \cap \mathbb{
R}P^n$. By the Lemma, we need
$$k \geq n + 1 \quad \text{for every } n \geq 2.$$

Since $n$ is unbounded, no finite $k$ can satisfy this. Contradiction.

Therefore, **no finite open cover of $X/G$ by sets admitting local cross-section
s exists**. The answer to the question is **no**. $\blacksquare$
