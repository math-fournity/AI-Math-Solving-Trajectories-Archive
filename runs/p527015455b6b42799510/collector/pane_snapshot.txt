## Proof

### Setup and notation

Let $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} V = 2n$. The complex structure $J$ ($J^2 = -\mathrm{id}$)
 gives the splitting
$$V_{\mathbb{C}} = V^{1,0} \oplus V^{0,1}, \qquad \dim_{\mathbb{C}} V^{1,0} = n,
$$
where $V^{1,0}$ (resp. $V^{0,1}$) is the $+i$ (resp. $-i$) eigenspace of $J$. Du
ally, $(V_{\mathbb{C}})^* = (V^{1,0})^* \oplus (V^{0,1})^*$, with bases $\{e^j\}
$ and $\{\bar{e}^j\}$ respectively. The exterior algebra decomposes into bidegre
e:
$$\Lambda^*(V_{\mathbb{C}}) = \bigoplus_{p,q} \Lambda^{p,q}, \qquad \Lambda^{p,q
} = \Lambda^p(V^{1,0})^* \otimes \Lambda^q(V^{0,1})^*.$$

The Kähler form
$$\omega = \frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=1}^n e^j \wedge \bar{e}^j \in \Lambda^{1,1}$$
is a real $(1,1)$-form. The Lefschetz operator is $L(\alpha) = \omega \wedge \al
pha$.

### Step 1. $L$ preserves the bidegree difference $p - q$

Since $\omega \in \Lambda^{1,1}$, wedging by $\omega$ adds $(1,1)$ to the bidegr
ee:
$$\boxed{L\bigl(\Lambda^{p,q}\bigr) \subset \Lambda^{p+1,\,q+1}.}$$
In particular, $L$ **preserves** $d := p - q$. Therefore, with respect to the de
composition
$$\Lambda^*(V_{\mathbb{C}}) = \bigoplus_{d = -n}^{n} \mathcal{L}_d, \qquad \math
cal{L}_d := \bigoplus_{p - q = d} \Lambda^{p,q},$$
the operator $L$ is **block-diagonal**:
$$L = \bigoplus_{d=-n}^{n} L_d, \qquad L_d := L\big|_{\mathcal{L}_d} : \mathcal{
L}_d \to \mathcal{L}_d.$$

### Step 2. $L$ is nilpotent

Since $\omega \in \Lambda^2$ and $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} V = 2n$, we have $\omega^{n+
1} = 0$, hence
$$L^{n+1} = 0.$$
Each $L_d$ is nilpotent. The normal form of a nilpotent operator is a direct sum
 of Jordan blocks (all with eigenvalue $0$). We now identify the Jordan-block st
ructure via the $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$-representation.

### Step 3. The $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$-triple $\{L, \Lambda, H\}$

Define:
- $L = \omega \wedge (\cdot)$ (the Lefschetz operator, the **raising** operator)
.
- $\Lambda = $ the adjoint of $L$ with respect to the Hodge inner product (contr
action with $\omega$; the **lowering** operator).
- $H := [L, \Lambda]$, which acts on $\Lambda^{p,q}$ as
$$H\big|_{\Lambda^{p,q}} = (n - p - q)\cdot \mathrm{id}_{\Lambda^{p,q}}.$$

One verifies the $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$ commutation relations:
$$[H, L] = 2L, \qquad [H, \Lambda] = -2\Lambda, \qquad [L, \Lambda] = H.$$

Note that $H$ also preserves $d = p - q$ (since $n - p - q$ depends on $p+q$ and
 the block $\mathcal{L}_d$ is determined by $p - q$; within a fixed $d$, varying
 $p + q$ gives the weight grading). So each $\mathcal{L}_d$ is an $\mathfrak{sl}
(2)$-subrepresentation.

### Step 4. Primitive decomposition (Lefschetz decomposition)

A form $\alpha \in \Lambda^{p,q}$ is **primitive** if $\Lambda \alpha = 0$. Defi
ne the primitive space
$$P^{p,q} := \Lambda^{p,q} \cap \ker \Lambda \subset \Lambda^{p,q}.$$

By the representation theory of $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$ (each finite-dimensional irre
ducible representation is a highest-weight string generated by a primitive/lowes
t-weight vector), every $\alpha \in \Lambda^{p,q}$ admits the **Lefschetz decomp

$$\alpha = \sum_{r \ge 0} L^r \alpha_r, \qquad \alpha_r \in P^{p-r,\,q-r},$$
where the sum is over $r \ge \max(0,\, p+q-n)$ (so that $p - r, q - r \ge 0$ and
 the primitive space is nonzero). Equivalently,
$$\Lambda^{p,q} = \bigoplus_{r \ge \max(0,\,p+q-n)} L^r\bigl(P^{p-r,\,q-r}\bigr)
.$$

This decomposition is **direct** and $L$-invariant in the following sense: each
primitive element $\alpha_r \in P^{p-r,q-r}$ generates an $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$-str

$$\alpha_r,\; L\alpha_r,\; L^2\alpha_r,\; \dots,\; L^{m}\alpha_r,$$
where $m = n - (p - r) - (q - r) = n - p - q + 2r$ is the length determined by t
he $H$-weight (the string terminates when $H$-weight reaches its maximum, i.e.,
when $L^{m+1}\alpha_r = 0$ with $L^m \alpha_r \ne 0$).

Combining Steps 1–4, the **normal form of $L$** with respect to the bidegree dec

> **$L$ is block-diagonal with respect to the decomposition $\Lambda^*(V_{\mathb
