## Solution

**Setup.** The curve is $y = f(x) = 2x - 3x^3$, which passes through the origin,
 rises to a local maximum at $x = \tfrac{1}{3}$ (where $f' = 2-9x^2 = 0$) with v
alue $f(\tfrac{1}{3}) = \tfrac{5}{9}$, then descends through $(\sqrt{2/3},\,0)$.
 For $0 < c < \tfrac{5}{9}$, the line $y = c$ meets the curve at two first-quadr
ant points $x_1 < \tfrac{1}{3} < x_2$, both satisfying $f(x) = c$.

**The two regions.**
- Region 1 (between $y$-axis, line, and curve): $A_1 = \displaystyle\int_0^{x_1}
\bigl[c - f(x)\bigr]\,dx$.
- Region 2 (under curve, above line, between intersections): $A_2 = \displaystyl
e\int_{x_1}^{x_2}\bigl[f(x) - c\bigr]\,dx$.

**Computing the areas.** Since $\int f(x)\,dx = x^2 - \tfrac{3}{4}x^4$:

$$A_1 = c\,x_1 - x_1^2 + \tfrac{3}{4}x_1^4,$$

$$A_2 = \bigl(x_2^2 - \tfrac{3}{4}x_2^4\bigr) - \bigl(x_1^2 - \tfrac{3}{4}x_1^4\
bigr) - c(x_2 - x_1).$$

**Setting $A_1 = A_2$.** Expanding and cancelling the common terms $c\,x_1$, $-x
_1^2$, $+\tfrac{3}{4}x_1^4$ from both sides leaves:

$$0 = x_2^2 - \tfrac{3}{4}x_2^4 - c\,x_2 = x_2\!\left(x_2 - \tfrac{3}{4}x_2^3 -
c\right).$$

Since $x_2 \neq 0$:

$$c = x_2 - \tfrac{3}{4}x_2^3. \tag{1}$$

**Using the intersection condition.** Since $x_2$ lies on the curve, $c = 2x_2 -
 3x_2^3$. Equating with (1):

$$x_2 - \tfrac{3}{4}x_2^3 = 2x_2 - 3x_2^3 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \tfrac{9}{4}x_
2^3 - x_2 = 0 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; x_2\!\left(\tfrac{9}{4}x_2^2 - 1\right) =
0.$$

The positive solution is $x_2 = \tfrac{2}{3}$.

**Finding $c$.**

$$c = 2\cdot\tfrac{2}{3} - 3\cdot\!\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^3 = \tfrac{4}{3} -
\tfrac{8}{9} = \tfrac{4}{9}.$$

**Verification.** With $c = \tfrac{4}{9}$, the cubic $27x^3 - 18x + 4 = (3x-2)(9
x^2+6x-2) = 0$ gives $x_2 = \tfrac{2}{3}$ and $x_1 = \tfrac{\sqrt{3}-1}{3} \appr
ox 0.244$ (both in the first quadrant, with $x_1 < \tfrac{1}{3} < x_2$). A direc
t numerical evaluation of both integrals yields $A_1 = A_2 \approx 0.0514$, conf
irming equality.

$$\boxed{c = \dfrac{4}{9}}$$
