## Proof

**Setup.** Let $X$ be a complex algebraic variety and let $\pi: X_{\mathrm{an}}
\to X$ be the canonical morphism of locally ringed spaces arising from the analy
tification functor. On the level of topological spaces, $\pi$ is the identity (t
he $\mathbb{C}$-points of $X$ carry both the Zariski and the analytic topology);
 on structure sheaves, the morphism $\pi^\#: \mathcal{O}_X \to \pi_*\mathcal{O}_
{X_{\mathrm{an}}}$ sends every regular function to its associated holomorphic fu
nction. This is a morphism of sheaves of $\mathbb{C}$-algebras, and on every aff
ine open $U \subset X$ the map $\mathcal{O}_X(U) \to \mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{an}
}}(U_{\mathrm{an}})$ is injective (a regular function that is holomorphically ze
ro on a nonempty Euclidean-open subset is zero).

---

### Construction

Given an invertible sheaf $\mathscr{L}$ on $X$, define the **analytification** o
f $\mathscr{L}$ by pulling back along $\pi$:

$$\boxed{\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{an}} \;:=\; \pi^*\mathscr{L} \;=\; \pi^{-1}\mathsc
r{L}\,\otimes_{\pi^{-1}\mathcal{O}_X}\,\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{an}}.}$$

This is an $\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{an}}}$-module on $X_{\mathrm{an}}$.

**It is invertible.** Since $\mathscr{L}$ is locally free of rank $1$ over $\mat
hcal{O}_X$, there is an open cover $\{U_i\}$ of $X$ with $\mathscr{L}|_{U_i}\con
g\mathcal{O}_{U_i}$. The pullback $\pi^*$, being a tensor-product functor, prese
rves local freeness: on each $(U_i)_{\mathrm{an}}$,

$$\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{an}}\big|_{(U_i)_{\mathrm{an}}} \;=\; \pi^*(\mathscr{L}|_
{U_i}) \;\cong\; \pi^*\mathcal{O}_{U_i} \;=\; \mathcal{O}_{(U_i)_{\mathrm{an}},$
$

so $\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{an}}$ is locally free of rank $1$ over $\mathcal{O}_{X_
{\mathrm{an}}}$, i.e. an invertible sheaf on $X_{\mathrm{an}}$.

---

### Concrete description via transition functions

Choose trivializations $\varphi_i: \mathscr{L}|_{U_i}\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\math
cal{O}_{U_i}$. The transition functions $g_{ij}:=\varphi_i\circ\varphi_j^{-1}\in
\mathcal{O}_X(U_i\cap U_j)^*$ satisfy the Čech cocycle condition $g_{ij}\,g_{jk}
=g_{ik}$ on $U_i\cap U_j\cap U_k$. Applying the ring homomorphism $\mathcal{O}_X
(-)\to\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{an}}}((-)_{\mathrm{an}})$ yields holomorphic units
 $g_{ij}^{\mathrm{an}}\in\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{an}}}\bigl((U_i)_{\mathrm{an}}\
cap(U_j)_{\mathrm{an}}\bigr)^*$ that still satisfy the cocycle condition. The sh
eaf $\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{an}}$ is then obtained by gluing the free sheaves $\ma
thcal{O}_{(U_i)_{\mathrm{an}}}$ via the $g_{ij}^{\mathrm{an}}$:

$$\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{an}}\big|_{(U_i)_{\mathrm{an}}\cap(U_j)_{\mathrm{an}}} \;
\xrightarrow{\;\text{transition}\;}\; \mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{an}}\big|_{(U_i)_{\ma
thrm{an}}\cap(U_j)_{\mathrm{an}}}, \qquad s\mapsto g_{ij}^{\mathrm{an}}\cdot s.$
$

This is exactly the local description of $\pi^*\mathscr{L}$, confirming that the
 two definitions agree.

---

### Well-definedness up to unique isomorphism

The assignment $\mathscr{L}\mapsto\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{an}}=\pi^*\mathscr{L}$ is
 **functorial**: every morphism $\alpha:\mathscr{L}\to\mathscr{M}$ of $\mathcal{
O}_X$-modules induces a morphism $\pi^*\alpha:\pi^*\mathscr{L}\to\pi^*\mathscr{M
}$ of $\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{an}}}$-modules, with $\pi^*(\mathrm{id})=\mathrm{
id}$ and $\pi^*(\beta\circ\alpha)=\pi^*\beta\circ\pi^*\alpha$. In particular, ev
ery algebraic isomorphism $\alpha:\mathscr{L}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathscr{M}$ of i
nvertible sheaves induces an analytic isomorphism $\pi^*\alpha:\mathscr{L}_{\mat
hrm{an}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathscr{M}_{\mathrm{an}}$.

**Independence of choices.** Suppose the same $\mathscr{L}$ is described by two
systems of trivializations, yielding transition cocycles $\{g_{ij}\}$ (on cover
$\{U_i\}$) and $\{h_{\alpha\beta}\}$ (on cover $\{V_\alpha\}$). Passing to the c
ommon refinement $\{U_i\cap V_\alpha\}$, the two cocycles represent the same cla
ss in $H^1(X,\mathcal{O}_X^*)$, so there exist algebraic units $f_{i\alpha}\in\m
athcal{O}_X(U_i\cap V_\alpha)^*$ with

$$g_{ij}\big|_{U_i\cap U_j\cap V_\alpha\cap V_\beta} \;=\; f_{i\alpha}\;h_{\alph
a\beta}\;f_{j\beta}^{-1}.$$

After analytification the identical relation holds with $f_{i\alpha}^{\mathrm{an
}},\,g_{ij}^{\mathrm{an}},\,h_{\alpha\beta}^{\mathrm{an}}$, so the $f_{i\alpha}^
{\mathrm{an}}$ define an isomorphism between the two glued analytic sheaves. Thi
s isomorphism is precisely $\pi^*$ applied to the algebraic isomorphism between
the two presentations of $\mathscr{L}$.

**Uniqueness of the isomorphism.** Suppose two isomorphisms $\Phi,\Psi:\mathscr{
L}_{\mathrm{an}}^{(1)}\to\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{an}}^{(2)}$ both arise from the co
nstruction (i.e., both are $\pi^*$ of algebraic isomorphisms $\varphi,\psi:\math
scr{L}^{(1)}\to\mathscr{L}^{(2)}$). Then $\Phi=\pi^*\varphi$ and $\Psi=\pi^*\psi
$. If $\Phi=\Psi$ as analytic isomorphisms, we are done; to see that the isomorp
hism *induced by the algebraic data* is unique, note that $\varphi$ and $\psi$ d
iffer by an algebraic automorphism $\alpha=\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}$ of $\mathscr{L
}^{(2)}$, i.e. a global section $\alpha\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_X^*)$. The two an
alytic isomorphisms differ by $\pi^*\alpha\in\Gamma(X_{\mathrm{an}},\mathcal{O}_
{X_{\mathrm{an}}}^*)$. Crucially, the map

$$\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_X^*)\longrightarrow\Gamma(X_{\mathrm{an}},\mathcal{O}_{X_
{\mathrm{an}}}^*)$$

is **injective**: an algebraic global unit that is holomorphically equal to $1$
is algebraically equal to $1$ (its reciprocal is also a regular function, and a
regular function that is $1$ on a nonempty Euclidean-open set is $1$ everywhere)
. Hence $\pi^*\alpha=1$ implies $\alpha=1$, so $\varphi=\psi$ and consequently $
\Phi=\Psi$.

Therefore the isomorphism between any two realizations of $\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{
an}}$ is **unique**, and the analytification $\mathscr{L}\mapsto\mathscr{L}_{\ma
thrm{an}}$ is defined up to unique isomorphism. $\blacksquare$
