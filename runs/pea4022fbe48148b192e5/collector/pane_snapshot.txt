## Proof

**Step 1: The Euler line degenerates.**

For an equilateral triangle, all classical triangle centers coincide: the circum
center $O$, centroid $G$, orthocenter $H$, and nine-point center $N$ are the sam
e point. The Euler line is defined as the line through these centers; when they
all coincide, the Euler line degenerates to a single point. Therefore:

$$e = 0$$

**Step 2: The Fermat points coincide.**

The first Fermat point $F_+$ is the unique point inside the triangle where each
side subtends an angle of $120°$. For an equilateral triangle with side $s = 6$,
 by the $120°$ rotational symmetry, this point must be the center $O$ (the only
point fixed by the rotation). One can verify: placing vertices at $A = (2\sqrt{3
}, 0)$, $B = (-\sqrt{3}, 3)$, $C = (-\sqrt{3}, -3)$ with circumcenter $O = (0,0)
$, the angle $\angle BOC = 120°$ (since the central angle subtending each side o
f an equilateral triangle is $120°$). Thus $F_+ = O$.

The second Fermat point $F_-$ is the isogonal conjugate of $F_+$. For an equilat
eral triangle, the isogonal conjugate of the center $(1:1:1)$ in barycentric coo
rdinates is $(a^2:b^2:c^2) = (1:1:1)$, which is again the center. Thus:

$$F_+ = F_- = O$$

**Step 3: The Kiepert Hyperbola degenerates.**

The Kiepert hyperbola is the unique rectangular circumhyperbola passing through
$A$, $B$, $C$, $H$, $F_+$, and $F_-$. In barycentric coordinates, its equation i
s:

$$\sum_{\text{cyc}} (b^2 - c^2)\, yz = 0$$

For an equilateral triangle, $a = b = c$, so every coefficient $b^2 - c^2 = 0$.
The equation collapses to $0 = 0$ — the hyperbola is **completely degenerate**.
Geometrically, since $H$, $F_+$, and $F_-$ all coincide at $O$, we would need a
conic through $A$, $B$, $C$ with $O$ as a triple point. No non-degenerate conic
can have a triple point (a conic has degree 2, so the maximum multiplicity at an
y point is 2, and even that forces degeneracy). The Kiepert hyperbola therefore
has no well-defined tangent at $F_+ = F_- = O$.

**Step 4: $P_+$, $P_-$, and $d$ are undefined.**

Since the Kiepert hyperbola degenerates and $F_+ = F_-$, the tangent lines at th
ese points are not well-defined. Consequently, the intersection points $P_+$ and
 $P_-$ of these (non-existent) tangent lines with the circumcircle cannot be det
ermined, and $d$ is undefined.

**Step 5: The ratio $d/e$ is undefined.**

We have:
- $e = 0$ (Euler line degenerates to a point)
- $d$ is undefined (Kiepert hyperbola degenerates, tangent lines undefined)

Even if one attempts a limiting argument by considering triangles approaching eq
uilateral, the result depends on the direction of approach. For isosceles pertur
bations, the Kiepert hyperbola degenerates to the union of the symmetry axis and
 the base line ($xy = 0$ in suitable coordinates), giving $d = 0$ for all such t
riangles, so $d/e \to 0$. For non-isosceles perturbations, the Kiepert hyperbola
 remains non-degenerate and $d$ is generally nonzero, yielding a different limit
ing ratio. The limit is therefore direction-dependent and does not exist.

**Conclusion:** For an equilateral triangle with side length $s = 6$, both the E
uler line length $e = 0$ and the distance $d$ are degenerate (either zero or und
efined), producing the indeterminate form $d/e = 0/0$. The ratio $\frac{d}{e}$ i
s **undefined** for an equilateral triangle.
