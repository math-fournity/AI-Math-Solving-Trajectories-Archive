## Answer

**No.** A weakly Cauchy sequence in a non-complete inner product space need not
be bounded. The key obstruction is that the Uniform Boundedness Principle requir
es the domain to be *complete*, so it cannot be invoked here. We give an explici
t counterexample.

---

### Counterexample

Let $H = \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$ with its usual inner product, and let

$$V = c_{00} = \{ y = (y_1, y_2, \dots) : y_k = 0 \text{ for all but finitely ma
ny } k \} \subsetneq \ell^2.$$

Then $V$ is an inner product space (with the $\ell^2$ inner product) that is **n
ot complete**: for instance, the sequence of partial sums $\sum_{k=1}^{N} \frac{
1}{k} e_k$ is Cauchy in $V$ but converges in $\ell^2$ to an element not in $c_{0
0}$.

Define

$$x_n := n\, e_n \in V, \qquad n = 1, 2, \dots,$$

where $e_n$ is the $n$-th standard basis vector.

**The sequence is unbounded.** Indeed $\|x_n\| = n \to \infty$.

**The sequence is weakly Cauchy.** Let $y = (y_1, y_2, \dots) \in c_{00}$ be arb
itrary. Since $y$ has finite support, there exists $N$ such that $y_k = 0$ for a
ll $k > N$. Then for every $n > N$,

$$\langle x_n, y \rangle = \langle n\, e_n, y \rangle = n\, y_n = n \cdot 0 = 0.
$$

Thus the scalar sequence $\big(\langle x_n, y\rangle\big)_{n\ge 1}$ is **eventua
lly zero**, hence converges (to $0$). Since $y \in V$ was arbitrary, $x_n$ is we
akly Cauchy.

---

### Why the standard argument fails

In a *Hilbert* space (complete), the usual proof is: each $f_n := \langle x_n, \
cdot \rangle$ is a bounded linear functional, pointwise boundedness of $\{f_n(y)
\}$ follows from convergence, and the Uniform Boundedness Principle (which requi
res the domain $V$ to be a Banach space) gives $\sup_n \|f_n\| < \infty$; then $
\|x_n\| = \|f_n\|$ (by Cauchy–Schwarz with the test vector $x_n/\|x_n\|$) yields
 boundedness of $\{x_n\}$.

The counterexample above shows precisely where this breaks: $V = c_{00}$ is **no
t** a Banach space, so the Uniform Boundedness Principle does not apply, and ind
eed the conclusion fails — the family $\{f_n\}$ is pointwise bounded on $V$ yet
$\sup_n \|f_n\| = \sup_n \|x_n\| = \infty$.

---

### Conclusion

In a non-complete inner product space, a weakly Cauchy sequence need not be boun
ded. The completeness hypothesis is essential.
