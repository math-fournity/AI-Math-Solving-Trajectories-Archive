## Proof

**Setup.** Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the sheaf of holomorphic functions (equivalently
, continuous or smooth functions) on $\mathbb{C}$, where for each open set $U \s
ubseteq \mathbb{C}$, $\mathcal{F}(U)$ is the set (ring) of holomorphic functions
 on $U$, and the restriction map $\operatorname{res}_{U,V} : \mathcal{F}(U) \to
\mathcal{F}(V)$ for $V \subseteq U$ is the usual restriction of functions $f \ma
psto f|_V$.

The map $\varphi : \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{F}$ is defined on sections by
$$\varphi_U(f) = f^2 + 1, \qquad f \in \mathcal{F}(U).$$

**Goal.** Show that $\varphi$ commutes with restriction maps, i.e., for every pa
ir of open sets $V \subseteq U \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ and every $f \in \mathcal{F
}(U)$,
$$\operatorname{res}_{U,V}\!\big(\varphi_U(f)\big) = \varphi_V\!\big(\operatorna
me{res}_{U,V}(f)\big).$$

**Computation.** Let $f \in \mathcal{F}(U)$ and $V \subseteq U$ open.

*Left-hand side:*
$$\operatorname{res}_{U,V}\!\big(\varphi_U(f)\big) = \operatorname{res}_{U,V}(f^
2 + 1) = (f^2 + 1)\big|_V.$$

Since restriction of functions is **pointwise**, for every $z \in V$:
$$\big(f^2 + 1\big)\big|_V(z) = f(z)^2 + 1 = \big(f\big|_V\big)(z)^2 + 1.$$

Therefore:
$$\operatorname{res}_{U,V}(f^2 + 1) = \big(f\big|_V\big)^2 + 1.$$

*Right-hand side:*
$$\varphi_V\!\big(\operatorname{res}_{U,V}(f)\big) = \varphi_V(f\big|_V) = \big(
f\big|_V\big)^2 + 1.$$

**Conclusion.** Both sides equal $\big(f\big|_V\big)^2 + 1$, so
$$\operatorname{res}_{U,V}\!\big(\varphi_U(f)\big) = \varphi_V\!\big(\operatorna
me{res}_{U,V}(f)\big).$$

The key reason this works is that the restriction map $\operatorname{res}_{U,V}$
 is a **ring homomorphism** (it preserves both addition and multiplication of fu
nctions), and $\varphi$ is built from the ring operations (squaring and adding t
he constant $1$). Since restriction commutes with these algebraic operations, it
 commutes with $\varphi$.

Equivalently, the diagram

$$\begin{array}{ccc}
\mathcal{F}(U) & \xrightarrow{\varphi_U} & \mathcal{F}(U) \\
\downarrow{\scriptstyle \operatorname{res}_{U,V}} & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle \o
peratorname{res}_{U,V}} \\
\mathcal{F}(V) & \xrightarrow{\varphi_V} & \mathcal{F}(V)
\end{array}$$

commutes for all $V \subseteq U$.

**Answer: Yes**, the map $\varphi : f \mapsto f^2 + 1$ commutes with the restric
tion maps, and is therefore a well-defined morphism of sheaves on $\mathbb{C}$.
