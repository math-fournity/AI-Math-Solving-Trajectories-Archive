## Solution

**Setup.** Place $E$ at the origin. Let line $AB$ be the $x$-axis with $A=(-a,0)
$, $B=(b,0)$, $M=(m,0)$ where $0<m<b$. Let line $CD$ make angle $\theta$ with th
e $x$-axis, with $C=(c\cos\theta,\, c\sin\theta)$ and $D=(-d\cos\theta,\, -d\sin
\theta)$.

By the power of point $E$: $EA\cdot EB = EC\cdot ED$, i.e., $ab = cd$.

**Circle through $D, E, M$ and its tangent at $E$.** A circle through the origin
 has equation $x^2+y^2+ux+vy=0$. Passing through $M=(m,0)$ gives $u=-m$; passing
 through $D$ gives $v=(d+m\cos\theta)/\sin\theta$. The tangent at the origin is
$ux+vy=0$, i.e.,
$$y = \frac{m\sin\theta}{d+m\cos\theta}\,x \;=:\; kx.$$

**Finding $F$ (intersection with line $BC$).** Parametrize $BC$: $P = B + s(C-B)
$. Setting $y=kx$ and solving:
$$s = \frac{kb}{c(\sin\theta - k\cos\theta)+kb}.$$
Using $\sin\theta - k\cos\theta = \frac{d\sin\theta}{d+m\cos\theta}$ and $cd=ab$
:
$$s = \frac{mb\sin\theta}{cd\sin\theta+mb\sin\theta} = \frac{m}{a+m}.$$
So $F_x = \frac{ab+mc\cos\theta}{a+m}$, $\;F_y = \frac{mc\sin\theta}{a+m}$, givi
ng
$$|EF|^2 = \frac{a^2b^2+2abmc\cos\theta+m^2c^2}{(a+m)^2}.$$

**Finding $G$ (intersection with line $AC$).** Parametrize $AC$: $P = A + s'(C-A
)$. Setting $y=kx$:
$$s' = \frac{-ka}{c(\sin\theta-k\cos\theta)-ka} = \frac{-ma}{cd-ma} = \frac{-m}{
b-m}.$$
So $G_x = \frac{-(ab+mc\cos\theta)}{b-m}$, $\;G_y = \frac{-mc\sin\theta}{b-m}$,
giving
$$|EG|^2 = \frac{a^2b^2+2abmc\cos\theta+m^2c^2}{(b-m)^2}.$$

**Taking the ratio.** The numerators are identical, so
$$\frac{EG}{EF} = \frac{a+m}{b-m}.$$

**Expressing in terms of $t$.** Since $A=(-a,0)$, $M=(m,0)$, $B=(b,0)$:
$$\frac{AM}{AB} = \frac{a+m}{a+b} = t \implies a+m = t(a+b).$$
Then
$$b-m = (a+b)-(a+m) = (a+b)(1-t).$$
Therefore
$$\frac{EG}{EF} = \frac{t(a+b)}{(a+b)(1-t)} = \boxed{\dfrac{t}{1-t}}.$$
