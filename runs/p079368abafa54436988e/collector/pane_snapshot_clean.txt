## Answer

**Yes**, such an operator exists. Here is a complete proof.

---

**Construction.** Let $X = \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$ with standard orthonormal basis $
\{e_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$. Define $A: \ell^2 \to \ell^2$ by
$$A e_n = \tfrac{1}{n}\, e_{n+1}, \qquad n \ge 1.$$
Equivalently, $(Ax)_1 = 0$ and $(Ax)_{n+1} = \frac{x_n}{n}$ for $n \ge 1$.

**$A$ is nonzero.** $Ae_1 = e_2 \neq 0$, so $A \neq 0$.

**$A$ is compact.** For each $N \ge 1$, define the finite-rank operator $A_N$ by
$$A_N e_n = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{n} e_{n+1}, & n \le N, \\ 0, & n > N. \end{ca
ses}$$
Each $A_N$ has rank at most $N$. For any $x = (x_n) \in \ell^2$,
$$\|(A - A_N)x\|^2 = \sum_{n>N} \frac{|x_n|^2}{n^2} \le \frac{1}{(N+1)^2}\sum_{n
>N}|x_n|^2 \le \frac{\|x\|^2}{(N+1)^2}.$$
Hence $\|A - A_N\| \le \frac{1}{N+1} \to 0$. So $A$ is the norm-limit of finite-
rank operators and is therefore compact.

**$A$ has no nonzero eigenvalues.** Suppose $Ax = \lambda x$ with $\lambda \neq
0$. Comparing the first coordinate:
$$\lambda\, x_1 = (Ax)_1 = 0 \implies x_1 = 0.$$
Then the second coordinate gives $\lambda\, x_2 = (Ax)_2 = \frac{x_1}{1} = 0$, s
o $x_2 = 0$. Proceeding inductively, $\lambda\, x_{n+1} = \frac{x_n}{n}$ and $x_
n = 0$ forces $x_{n+1} = 0$. Thus $x = 0$, and $\lambda$ is not an eigenvalue.

**$\sigma(A) = \{0\}$.** Since $\ell^2$ is infinite-dimensional and $A$ is compa
ct, $A$ cannot be invertible (a compact operator on an infinite-dimensional Bana
ch space is never surjective), so $0 \in \sigma(A)$. By the Riesz–Schauder spect
ral theorem for compact operators, every nonzero point of the spectrum is an eig
envalue of finite multiplicity, and the nonzero eigenvalues form a (finite or co
untable) sequence accumulating only at $0$. Since $A$ has no nonzero eigenvalues
, $\sigma(A) \setminus \{0\} = \emptyset$. Therefore
$$\sigma(A) = \{0\}.$$

---

We have exhibited a nonzero compact operator on the Banach space $\ell^2$ whose
spectrum is $\{0\}$, so the answer is **yes**.
