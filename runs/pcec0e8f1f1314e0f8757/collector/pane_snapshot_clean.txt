**Claim.** The natural numbers $n$ for which $T_n T_{n+1} \cdots T_{n+15}$ is a
perfect square are exactly $n = 2$ and $n = 9$.

---

### Setup

Recall $T_k = \frac{k(k+1)}{2}$. The product is

$$P = \prod_{k=n}^{n+15} \frac{k(k+1)}{2} = \frac{1}{2^{16}} \prod_{k=n}^{n+15}
k \cdot \prod_{k=n}^{n+15}(k+1).$$

Splitting the two products:

$$\prod_{k=n}^{n+15} k = n\cdot(n+1)\cdots(n+15), \qquad \prod_{k=n}^{n+15}(k+1)
 = (n+1)(n+2)\cdots(n+16).$$

Writing $L = (n+1)(n+2)\cdots(n+15)$, we get

$$P = \frac{1}{2^{16}}\cdot n\cdot L \cdot L \cdot (n+16) = \frac{n(n+16)}{2^{16
}}\, L^2.$$

Since $L^2$ is already a perfect square, $P$ is a perfect square if and only if
$\dfrac{n(n+16)}{2^{16}}$ is a perfect square of a rational number.

---

### Reduction to a Diophantine condition

Let $d = \gcd(n,\, n+16) = \gcd(n, 16)$, so $d \in \{1,2,4,8,16\}$. Write

$$n = d\,s, \qquad n+16 = d\,t, \qquad \gcd(s,t)=1, \qquad t - s = \frac{16}{d}.
$$

Then

$$\frac{n(n+16)}{2^{16}} = \frac{d^2\, s\, t}{2^{16}}.$$

Since $d^2$ is a perfect square, we need $\dfrac{s\,t}{2^{16}}$ to be a perfect
square rational. Because $\gcd(s,t)=1$, the prime factorizations of $s$ and $t$
are disjoint, so:

- **$t$ must be a perfect square** (every prime exponent in $t$ is even).
- **$s \cdot 2^{16}$ must be a perfect square.** Since $2^{16} = (2^8)^2$ is alr
eady a perfect square, this is equivalent to **$s$ being a perfect square**.

> *Key observation:* $\gcd(s, t) = \gcd(s,\, s + 16/d) = \gcd(s,\, 16/d) = 1$, w
hich is automatically satisfied given $d = \gcd(n,16)$, so no extra constraint i
s needed.

Thus we need: **$s$ and $t$ are perfect squares, $\gcd(s,t)=1$, and $t - s = 16/
d$**.

Writing $s = b^2$, $t = a^2$ with $a > b \geq 0$, the condition becomes

$$(a-b)(a+b) = \frac{16}{d}.$$

---

### Case analysis

**Case $d = 1$** ($n$ odd): $t - s = 16$, so $(a-b)(a+b) = 16$.

| $a-b$ | $a+b$ | $a$ | $b$ | $s=b^2$ | $t=a^2$ | $\gcd(s,t)$ | $n=ds$ | Valid?
|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 16 | $17/2$ | — | — | — | — | — | No |
| 2 | 8 | 5 | 3 | 9 | 25 | 1 | **9** | **Yes** |
| 4 | 4 | 4 | 0 | 0 | 16 | — | 0 | No ($n>0$) |

**Solution: $n = 9$.**

**Case $d = 2$** ($n \equiv 2 \pmod{4}$): $t - s = 8$, so $(a-b)(a+b) = 8$.

| $a-b$ | $a+b$ | $a$ | $b$ | $s$ | $t$ | $\gcd$ | $n$ | Valid? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8 | $9/2$ | — | — | — | — | — | No |
| 2 | 4 | 3 | 1 | 1 | 9 | 1 | **2** | **Yes** |

**Solution: $n = 2$.**

**Case $d = 4$** ($n \equiv 4 \pmod{8}$): $t - s = 4$, so $(a-b)(a+b) = 4$.

| $a-b$ | $a+b$ | $a$ | $b$ | $s$ | $t$ | $n$ | Valid? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 | $5/2$ | — | — | — | — | No |
| 2 | 2 | 2 | 0 | 0 | 4 | 0 | No ($n>0$) |

**No solution.**

**Case $d = 8$** ($n \equiv 8 \pmod{16}$): $t - s = 2$, so $(a-b)(a+b) = 2$.

Only factor pair $(1,2)$ gives $a = 3/2$, not an integer. **No solution.**

**Case $d = 16$** ($16 \mid n$): $t - s = 1$, so $(a-b)(a+b) = 1$.

Only factor pair $(1,1)$ gives $a=1, b=0$, so $s=0, t=1$, $n=0$. **No positive s
olution.**

### Verification

**$n = 2$:** $P = \frac{2 \cdot 18}{2^{16}} \cdot L^2 = \frac{36}{2^{16}} \cdot
L^2 = \frac{9}{2^{16}} \cdot L^2 = \left(\frac{3\,L}{2^8}\right)^2$, where $L =
3\cdot 4\cdots 17 = 17!/2!$. Since $v_2(17!) = 8+4+2+1 = 15 \geq 8$, the quotien
t $3L/2^8$ is an integer. ✓

**$n = 9$:** $P = \frac{9 \cdot 25}{2^{16}} \cdot L^2 = \left(\frac{15\,L}{2^8}\
right)^2$, where $L = 10\cdot 11\cdots 24 = 24!/9!$. Since $v_2(24!) - v_2(9!) =
 (12+6+3+1)-(4+2+1) = 22-7 = 15 \geq 8$, the quotient $15L/2^8$ is an integer. ✓

---

### Conclusion

The only natural numbers $n$ for which $T_n\, T_{n+1} \cdots T_{n+15}$ is a perf
ect square are $\boxed{n = 2}$ and $\boxed{n = 9}$.
