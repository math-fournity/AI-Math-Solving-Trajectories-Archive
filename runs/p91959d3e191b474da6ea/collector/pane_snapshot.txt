# Proof of the Erdős–Mordell Inequality

**Setup.** Let $A,B,C\in\mathbb{R}^2$ form a triangle and let $P$ be an interior
 point. Denote the angles $\angle A = \alpha$, $\angle B = \beta$, $\angle C = \
gamma$ (with $\alpha+\beta+\gamma=\pi$). Let

$$d_a = PM = \operatorname{dist}(P, BC),\quad d_b = PL = \operatorname{dist}(P,
CA),\quad d_c = PN = \operatorname{dist}(P, AB).$$

We must show $PA+PB+PC \ge 2(d_a+d_b+d_c)$.

---

## Key Lemma

**Lemma.** $PA \ge \dfrac{d_b\sin\gamma + d_c\sin\beta}{\sin\alpha}$, and analog
ously for the other two vertices.

*Proof of Lemma.* Let $\alpha_1 = \angle PAB$ and $\alpha_2 = \angle PAC$, so $\
alpha_1+\alpha_2 = \alpha$ with $\alpha_1,\alpha_2>0$ (since $P$ is interior). I
n the right triangles $APN$ (right angle at $N\in AB$) and $APL$ (right angle at
 $L\in CA$):

$$d_c = PA\sin\alpha_1,\qquad d_b = PA\sin\alpha_2.$$

Therefore
$$\frac{d_b\sin\gamma + d_c\sin\beta}{\sin\alpha} = PA\cdot\frac{\sin\alpha_2\si
n\gamma + \sin\alpha_1\sin\beta}{\sin\alpha}.$$

It suffices to show $\sin\alpha \ge \sin\alpha_2\sin\gamma + \sin\alpha_1\sin\be
ta$.

Since $\beta = \pi - \alpha - \gamma = \pi - \alpha_1 - \alpha_2 - \gamma$, we h
ave $\sin\beta = \sin(\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\gamma)$. Expanding:

$$\sin\alpha_2\sin\gamma + \sin\alpha_1\sin\beta = \sin\alpha_2\sin\gamma + \sin
\alpha_1\bigl(\sin\alpha\cos\gamma + \cos\alpha\sin\gamma\bigr)$$

$$= \sin\alpha\,\sin\alpha_1\cos\gamma + \sin\gamma\bigl(\sin\alpha_2 + \sin\alp
ha_1\cos\alpha\bigr).$$

Now simplify the bracketed term using $\alpha = \alpha_1+\alpha_2$:

$$\sin\alpha_2 + \sin\alpha_1\cos\alpha = \sin\alpha_2 + \sin\alpha_1(\cos\alpha
_1\cos\alpha_2 - \sin\alpha_1\sin\alpha_2)$$

$$= \sin\alpha_2\cos^2\alpha_1 + \sin\alpha_1\cos\alpha_1\cos\alpha_2 = \cos\alp
ha_1\,\sin(\alpha_1+\alpha_2) = \cos\alpha_1\sin\alpha.$$

Substituting back:

$$\sin\alpha_2\sin\gamma + \sin\alpha_1\sin\beta = \sin\alpha\,\sin\alpha_1\cos\
gamma + \sin\gamma\cos\alpha_1\sin\alpha = \sin\alpha\,\sin(\alpha_1+\gamma).$$

Since $\alpha_1>0$ and $\gamma>0$ and $\alpha_1+\gamma < \alpha_1+\alpha_2+\beta
+\gamma = \pi$, we have $\sin(\alpha_1+\gamma)\le 1$. Hence

$$\sin\alpha_2\sin\gamma + \sin\alpha_1\sin\beta = \sin\alpha\,\sin(\alpha_1+\ga
mma) \le \sin\alpha. \qquad\checkmark$$

This proves the Lemma. $\square$

By the **same argument** applied at vertices $B$ and $C$ (with $\beta_1=\angle P
BA$, $\beta_2=\angle PBC$, $\beta_1+\beta_2=\beta$, and $\gamma_1=\angle PCB$, $
\gamma_2=\angle PCA$, $\gamma_1+\gamma_2=\gamma$):

$$PB \ge \frac{d_a\sin\gamma + d_c\sin\alpha}{\sin\beta},\qquad PC \ge \frac{d_a
\sin\beta + d_b\sin\alpha}{\sin\gamma}.$$

---

## Summation and AM–GM

Adding the three inequalities:

$$PA+PB+PC \ge \frac{d_b\sin\gamma+d_c\sin\beta}{\sin\alpha} + \frac{d_a\sin\gam
ma+d_c\sin\alpha}{\sin\beta} + \frac{d_a\sin\beta+d_b\sin\alpha}{\sin\gamma}.$$

Collecting the coefficient of each distance:

- **Coefficient of $d_a$:** $\;\dfrac{\sin\gamma}{\sin\beta} + \dfrac{\sin\beta}
{\sin\gamma} \ge 2$ by AM–GM.
- **Coefficient of $d_b$:** $\;\dfrac{\sin\gamma}{\sin\alpha} + \dfrac{\sin\alph
a}{\sin\gamma} \ge 2$ by AM–GM.
- **Coefficient of $d_c$:** $\;\dfrac{\sin\beta}{\sin\alpha} + \dfrac{\sin\alpha
}{\sin\beta} \ge 2$ by AM–GM.

Therefore

$$PA+PB+PC \ge 2\,d_a + 2\,d_b + 2\,d_c = 2\,(PM + PL + PN).$$

---

## Conclusion

Recalling $d_a = PM$, $d_b = PL$, $d_c = PN$, we have proved

$$\boxed{PA + PB + PC \ge 2\,(PM + PN + PL),}$$

which is the Erdős–Mordell inequality. $\blacksquare$

*(Equality holds iff the triangle is equilateral and $P$ is its center: AM–GM eq
uality forces $\sin\alpha=\sin\beta=\sin\gamma$, hence $\alpha=\beta=\gamma=\pi/
3$; the Lemma equality $\sin(\alpha_1+\gamma)=1$ then forces $\alpha_1=\pi/6$, a
nd similarly at each vertex, placing $P$ at the incenter/centroid.)*
