## Solution

We compute $\mathbb{E}^x[L_{T_a}^y]$ for Brownian motion $B_t$ starting at $x$,
with $y < x < a$, where $T_a = \inf\{s \ge 0 : B_s = a\}$, and $L_t^y$ denotes t
he **symmetric local time** at level $y$:
$$L_t^y = \lim_{\varepsilon \downarrow 0} \frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_0^t \mathbf
{1}_{\{|B_s - y|<\varepsilon\}}\,ds.$$

### Step 1. Occupation density and the Green function

The occupation density formula states that for every bounded measurable $f$,
$$\int_0^{T_a} f(B_s)\,ds = \int_{\mathbb{R}} f(z)\,L_{T_a}^z\,dz \quad \text{a.
s.}$$

Taking expectations and applying Fubini on both sides:
$$\mathbb{E}^x\!\left[\int_0^{T_a} f(B_s)\,ds\right] = \int_{\mathbb{R}} f(z)\,\
mathbb{E}^x[L_{T_a}^z]\,dz.$$

On the other hand, using the killed transition density $p_s^{(a)}(x,z)$ of Brown
ian motion on $(-\infty, a)$ (absorbed at $a$),
$$\mathbb{E}^x\!\left[\int_0^{T_a} f(B_s)\,ds\right] = \int_0^\infty\!\int_{\mat
hbb{R}} f(z)\,p_s^{(a)}(x,z)\,dz\,ds = \int_{\mathbb{R}} f(z)\,G(x,z)\,dz,$$
where $G(x,z) = \int_0^\infty p_s^{(a)}(x,z)\,ds$ is the Green function. Since t
his holds for all bounded $f$,
$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}^x[L_{T_a}^z] = G(x,z).}$$

### Step 2. Computing the Green function by the method of images

For Brownian motion killed at the single upper barrier $a$, the method of images
 gives, for $z < a$,
$$p_s^{(a)}(x,z) = p_s(x - z) - p_s(x + z - 2a), \qquad p_s(u) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{
2\pi s}}\,e^{-u^2/(2s)}.$$

Hence
$$G(x,z) = \int_0^\infty \bigl[p_s(x-z) - p_s(x+z-2a)\bigr]\,ds.$$

Each individual integral diverges, but the difference is integrable. Evaluating
the regularized integral $\int_0^\infty p_s(u)\,ds$ via the substitution $v = u^
2/(2s)$:
$$\int_0^\infty p_s(u)\,ds = \frac{|u|}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,\Gamma\!\left(-\tfrac12\ri
ght) = \frac{|u|}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\cdot(-2\sqrt{\pi}) = -|u|.$$

Therefore
$$G(x,z) = -|x - z| + |x + z - 2a| = |x + z - 2a| - |x - z|.$$

### Step 3. Specializing to $z = y$ with $y < x < a$

Since $y < x$: $|x - y| = x - y$.
Since $a > x$ and $a > y$: $x + y - 2a < 0$, so $|x + y - 2a| = 2a - x - y$.

Thus
$$G(x,y) = (2a - x - y) - (x - y) = 2a - 2x = 2(a - x).$$

### Step 4. Verification via the strong Markov property

As an independent check, decompose at $T_y$ (first hitting time of $y$):

- On $\{T_y \ge T_a\}$: the path never reaches $y$ before absorption at $a$, so
$L_{T_a}^y = 0$.
- On $\{T_y < T_a\}$: by the strong Markov property at $T_y$ (noting $L_{T_y}^y
= 0$ since local time is continuous and starts at $0$),
$$\mathbb{E}^x[L_{T_a}^y] = \mathbb{P}^x(T_y < T_a)\cdot \mathbb{E}^y[L_{T_a}^y]
.$$

The gambler's ruin probability for $y < x < a$ is
$$\mathbb{P}^x(T_y < T_a) = \frac{a - x}{a - y}.$$

From Step 3 with starting point $y$ (so $\max(y,y)=y$): $\mathbb{E}^y[L_{T_a}^y]
 = G(y,y) = 2(a - y)$.

Therefore
$$\mathbb{E}^x[L_{T_a}^y] = \frac{a - x}{a - y}\cdot 2(a - y) = 2(a - x).$$

The $y$-dependence cancels exactly: as $y \to -\infty$, the probability of ever
reaching $y$ shrinks like $(a-x)/(a-y)$, while the conditional expected local ti
me at $y$ (once reached) grows like $2(a-y)$, and the two effects balance perfec
tly.

### Result

$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}^x\!\left[L_{T_a}^y\right] = 2(a - x)}$$
