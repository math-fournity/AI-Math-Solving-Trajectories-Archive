## Proof

**Claim.** Yes. For every bounded linear operator $T: X \to Y$ between Banach sp
aces, there exists a sequence $(x_n)$ in the unit ball of $X$ such that $\|Tx_n\
| \to \|T\|$.

**Proof.**

Let $X, Y$ be Banach spaces and let $T: X \to Y$ be a bounded linear operator. T
he operator norm is defined by

$$\|T\| = \sup\{\|Tx\| : x \in X,\ \|x\| \le 1\}.$$

Denote $S = \{\|Tx\| : x \in X,\ \|x\| \le 1\} \subseteq [0, \infty)$. By defini
tion of the supremum as the *least upper bound* of $S$:

1. **Upper bound property:** $\|Tx\| \le \|T\|$ for every $x$ with $\|x\| \le 1$
.
2. **Least upper bound property:** For every $\varepsilon > 0$, the number $\|T\
| - \varepsilon$ is *not* an upper bound of $S$. Hence there exists $x_\varepsil
on \in X$ with $\|x_\varepsilon\| \le 1$ such that
$$\|Tx_\varepsilon\| > \|T\| - \varepsilon.$$

Now apply this with $\varepsilon = \tfrac{1}{n}$ for each $n \in \mathbb{N}$. We
 obtain a sequence $(x_n)_{n=1}^\infty$ in $X$ with $\|x_n\| \le 1$ satisfying

$$\|T\| - \frac{1}{n} < \|Tx_n\| \le \|T\| \qquad \text{for all } n.$$

Therefore

$$\bigl|\|Tx_n\| - \|T\|\bigr| < \frac{1}{n} \xrightarrow[n \to \infty]{} 0,$$

which gives $\|Tx_n\| \to \|T\|$.

**Remark.** The supremum need not be *attained* — that is, there may be no vecto
r $x$ with $\|x\| \le 1$ and $\|Tx\| = \|T\|$ (consider, e.g., multiplication by
 $t$ on $C[0,1]$, or appropriate integral/diagonal operators on $\ell^2$). The a
rgument above shows that even when the norm is not attained, it is always the li
mit of a sequence of values $\|Tx_n\|$ with $\|x_n\| \le 1$. Completeness of $X$
 and $Y$ (the Banach property) is not actually required for this result; it hold
s for any bounded linear operator between normed spaces, since it relies only on
 the definition of the supremum.
