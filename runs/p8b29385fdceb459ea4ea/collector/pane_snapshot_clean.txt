**Answer: Yes, $g \in L^2(X)$.**

**Proof.** Define $T:L^2(X)\to L^1(X)$ by $T(f)=f\overline{g}$. By hypothesis $T
$ is defined on all of $L^2$, and it is clearly linear. We first show $T$ is bou
nded via the Closed Graph Theorem.

*Graph is closed.* Suppose $f_n\to f$ in $L^2$ and $Tf_n=f_n\overline{g}\to h$ i
n $L^1$. Passing to a subsequence, $f_{n_k}\to f$ a.e., hence $f_{n_k}\overline{
g}\to f\overline{g}$ a.e. Since $f_{n_k}\overline{g}\to h$ in $L^1$, a further s
ubsequence converges to $h$ a.e. Thus $h=f\overline{g}$ a.e., proving the graph
is closed.

Since $L^2$ and $L^1$ are Banach spaces, the Closed Graph Theorem gives a consta
nt $C$ with
$$
\|f\overline{g}\|_1 \;=\; \int_X |f\overline{g}|\,d\mu \;\le\; C\|f\|_2 \qquad\t
ext{for all } f\in L^2. \tag{1}
$$

*Truncation test.* For $n\ge 1$ let $A_n=\{|g|\le n\}$, so $A_n\nearrow X$ (usin
g $g\in L^\infty$, in fact $A_n=X$ for $n\ge\|g\|_\infty$). Let $E\in\mathcal M$
 with $\mu(E)<\infty$, and set
$$
f \;=\; \overline{g}\,\mathbf 1_{E\cap A_n}.
$$
Then $|f|^2=|g|^2\mathbf 1_{E\cap A_n}\le n^2\mathbf 1_E$, so $\int|f|^2\le n^2\
mu(E)<\infty$; hence $f\in L^2$. Applying (1) to this $f$:
$$
\int_{E\cap A_n}|g|^2\,d\mu \;=\; \|f\overline{g}\|_1 \;\le\; C\|f\|_2 \;=\; C\B
igl(\int_{E\cap A_n}|g|^2\,d\mu\Bigr)^{1/2}.
$$
If the integral is positive, dividing both sides by its square root yields
$$
\Bigl(\int_{E\cap A_n}|g|^2\,d\mu\Bigr)^{1/2}\le C;
$$
if it is zero the same bound holds trivially. Therefore
$$
\int_{E\cap A_n}|g|^2\,d\mu \;\le\; C^2 \qquad\text{for all } E\text{ with }\mu(
E)<\infty,\;\text{all }n. \tag{2}
$$

*Removing the finite-measure restriction.* For the bounded nonnegative function
$h=|g|^2\mathbf 1_{A_n}$, one has
$$
\int_X h\,d\mu \;=\; \sup_{\mu(E)<\infty}\int_E h\,d\mu.
$$
Indeed, $\int h=\sup\{\int s:0\le s\le h,\;s\text{ simple}\}$, and for each simp
le $s$, $\int s=\sup_{\mu(E)<\infty}\int_E s$ (take $E$ enlarging to capture eac
h level set of $s$; sets of infinite measure are approximated from within by fin
ite-measure subsets). Taking the supremum over $E$ in (2) gives
$$
\int_{A_n}|g|^2\,d\mu \;\le\; C^2 \qquad\text{for all }n. \tag{3}
$$

*Conclusion.* Since $|g|^2\mathbf 1_{A_n}\nearrow|g|^2$, the Monotone Convergenc
e Theorem and (3) give
$$
\int_X|g|^2\,d\mu \;=\; \lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{A_n}|g|^2\,d\mu \;\le\; C^2 \;<\;
\infty.
$$
Hence $g\in L^2(X)$. $\blacksquare$
