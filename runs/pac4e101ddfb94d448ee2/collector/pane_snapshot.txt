# Proof

**Claim:** In an idempotent-complete $\mathsf{Ab}$-enriched category, every idem
potent gives rise to a biproduct decomposition.

**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an idempotent-complete $\mathsf{Ab}$-enriched category, and
 let $e: X \to X$ be an idempotent, so $e \circ e = e$.

### Step 1: $1 - e$ is also an idempotent

Since $\mathcal{C}$ is $\mathsf{Ab}$-enriched, each hom-set is an abelian group
and composition is bilinear. Thus $1_X - e$ is a well-defined endomorphism of $X
$, and:

$$(1 - e)^2 = 1 - 2e + e^2 = 1 - 2e + e = 1 - e.$$

So $1 - e$ is an idempotent.

### Step 2: Both idempotents split

Since $\mathcal{C}$ is idempotent-complete, every idempotent splits. Thus:

- $e$ splits: there exist an object $Y$, morphisms $r: X \to Y$ and $s: Y \to X$
 with
$$s \circ r = e, \qquad r \circ s = \mathrm{id}_Y.$$

- $1 - e$ splits: there exist an object $Z$, morphisms $r': X \to Z$ and $s': Z
\to X$ with
$$s' \circ r' = 1 - e, \qquad r' \circ s' = \mathrm{id}_Z.$$

### Step 3: Verify the biproduct axioms

Set the injections $i_Y = s: Y \to X$, $i_Z = s': Z \to X$ and the projections $
p_Y = r: X \to Y$, $p_Z = r': X \to Z$. We verify the five biproduct conditions.

**(a)** $p_Y \circ i_Y = r \circ s = \mathrm{id}_Y$. ✓

**(b)** $p_Z \circ i_Z = r' \circ s' = \mathrm{id}_Z$. ✓

**(c)** $p_Y \circ i_Z = r \circ s' = 0$. To see this, compute:
$$r \circ s' \circ r' = r \circ (1 - e) = r - r \circ e = r - r \circ s \circ r
= r - (r \circ s) \circ r = r - \mathrm{id}_Y \circ r = r - r = 0.$$
Since $r'$ is a split epimorphism (with section $s'$), it is in particular an ep
imorphism, so $(r \circ s') \circ r' = 0$ implies $r \circ s' = 0$. ✓

**(d)** $p_Z \circ i_Y = r' \circ s = 0$. By the symmetric argument:
$$r' \circ s \circ r = r' \circ e = r' - r' \circ (1 - e) = r' - r' \circ s' \ci
rc r' = r' - (r' \circ s') \circ r' = r' - \mathrm{id}_Z \circ r' = r' - r' = 0.
$$
Since $r$ is a split epimorphism (with section $s$), $r' \circ s = 0$. ✓

**(e)** $i_Y \circ p_Y + i_Z \circ p_Z = s \circ r + s' \circ r' = e + (1 - e) =
 \mathrm{id}_X$. ✓

### Conclusion

All five biproduct axioms are satisfied, so $X \cong Y \oplus Z$ is a biproduct
decomposition, where $Y$ is the splitting of $e$ and $Z$ is the splitting of $1
- e$. The crucial ingredients are:

1. **$\mathsf{Ab}$-enrichment** provides the additive structure needed to form $
1 - e$ and verify it is an idempotent.
2. **Idempotent-completeness** ensures both $e$ and $1 - e$ split.

Therefore, every idempotent in an idempotent-complete $\mathsf{Ab}$-enriched cat
egory gives rise to a biproduct decomposition. $\blacksquare$
