## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle I = \oint_C \frac{z^2+1}{e^{z/10}-1}\,dz$, where $C$
is the unit circle $|z|=1$ traversed counterclockwise.

**Identify the singularities.** The integrand $f(z) = \frac{z^2+1}{e^{z/10}-1}$
is singular where the denominator vanishes:
$$e^{z/10} = 1 \iff z/10 = 2\pi i k \iff z = 20\pi i k,\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}.$$

**Determine which lie inside $C$.** For $k=0$: $z=0$, and $|0|=0<1$ — inside. Fo
r $k\neq 0$: $|z| = 20\pi|k| \geq 20\pi \approx 62.83 > 1$ — outside. So the onl
y singularity enclosed by $C$ is $z=0$.

**Classify the singularity at $z=0$.** Expand the denominator near $z=0$:
$$e^{z/10}-1 = \frac{z}{10} + \frac{z^2}{200} + \cdots = \frac{z}{10}\left(1 + \
frac{z}{20} + \cdots\right).$$
Since the leading term is $\frac{z}{10}\neq 0$ at first order, $z=0$ is a **simp
le pole**. The numerator $z^2+1$ is analytic and nonzero at $z=0$ (value $1$), c
onfirming the pole is simple.

**Compute the residue.** For a simple pole,
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z) = \lim_{z\to 0} z\cdot\frac{z^2+1}{e^{z/10}-1} =
 \lim_{z\to 0}\frac{z^2+1}{\dfrac{e^{z/10}-1}{z}}.$$
Using $\displaystyle\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{e^{z/10}-1}{z} = \frac{1}{10}$, we get
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z) = \frac{0^2+1}{1/10} = \frac{1}{1/10} = 10.$$

**Apply the Residue Theorem.** With exactly one pole inside $C$,
$$I = 2\pi i \sum \operatorname{Res} = 2\pi i \cdot 10 = 20\pi i.$$

### Result

$$\boxed{\oint_C \frac{z^2+1}{e^{z/10}-1}\,dz = 20\pi i}$$
