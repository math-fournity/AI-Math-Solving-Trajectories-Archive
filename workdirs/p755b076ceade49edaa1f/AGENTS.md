# Problem

A high-tech manufacturing firm has developed a modular drone-core shaped like a perfect cube. To ensure proper sensor calibration, each of the six flat docking faces must be coated with a specialized electromagnetic film. The engineering team has a palette of exactly six distinct chemical isotopes available for these films.

The assembly process is subject to two strict technical constraints:
1. Every individual docking face must be coated with exactly one isotope.
2. To prevent interference, no two faces that share a common edge can be coated with the same isotope.

The drones are designed to operate in zero-gravity environments where they can rotate freely along any axis. Consequently, two drone-cores are considered to have identical "interference signatures" if one can be rotated in three-dimensional space so that the isotopes on its top, bottom, front, back, left, and right faces match those of the other drone exactly.

Based on these parameters, how many distinct interference signatures can be produced using selections from the six available isotopes?
