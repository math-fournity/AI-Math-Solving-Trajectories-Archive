Let $V=\mathbb C^n$ and let $W:=\operatorname{span}\{A,B\}\subset\mathfrak{gl}(V
)$. The hypothesis says that **every** $Z\in W$ is nilpotent (since $Z=sA+tB$ fo
r some $s,t$, and every such linear combination is nilpotent). In particular $A$
 and $B$ are nilpotent.

The proof has three steps.

### Step 1. A trace-vanishing lemma (polarization)

**Lemma 1.** For every $k\ge 1$ and every $X_1,\dots,X_k\in W$,
$$\operatorname{tr}(X_1X_2\cdots X_k)=0.$$

*Proof.* Fix $k$ and consider the multilinear form
$$f(Z_1,\dots,Z_k):=\operatorname{tr}(Z_1Z_2\cdots Z_k)\quad (Z_i\in W).$$
For any $Z\in W$, the matrix $Z$ is nilpotent, hence $Z^k$ is nilpotent, hence
$$f(Z,Z,\dots,Z)=\operatorname{tr}(Z^k)=0.$$
Since $W$ is a complex vector space (characteristic $0$), the standard polarizat
ion identity for multilinear forms applies:
$$f(X_1,\dots,X_k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{S\subseteq\{1,\dots,k\}}(-1)^{k-|S|}\,f\!\l
eft(\sum_{i\in S}X_i,\dots,\sum_{i\in S}X_i\right).$$
Each vector $Z_S:=\sum_{i\in S}X_i$ lies in $W$, so $f(Z_S,\dots,Z_S)=\operatorn
ame{tr}(Z_S^k)=0$. Therefore every summand vanishes and $f(X_1,\dots,X_k)=0$. $\
square$

### Step 2. The Lie algebra generated by $A,B$ consists of nilpotent endomorphis
ms

Let $L:=\operatorname{Lie}(A,B)\subset\mathfrak{gl}(V)$ be the Lie subalgebra ge
nerated by $A$ and $B$.

**Lemma 2.** Every $X\in L$ is a nilpotent endomorphism of $V$.

*Proof.* Every iterated commutator of $A,B$ (such as $[A,B]$, $[A,[A,B]]$, etc.)
 is, by expansion, a linear combination of **words** in the letters $A,B$. Hence
 every $X\in L$ is a linear combination of words: $X=\sum_i c_i\,w_i$.

For any $m\ge 1$,
$$X^m=\Big(\sum_i c_i w_i\Big)^m=\sum_{i_1,\dots,i_m}c_{i_1}\cdots c_{i_m}\,w_{i
_1}\cdots w_{i_m}.$$
Each product $w_{i_1}\cdots w_{i_m}$ is again a word in $A,B$, i.e. a product of
 elements of $W$. By Lemma 1,
$$\operatorname{tr}(w_{i_1}\cdots w_{i_m})=0.$$
Thus $\operatorname{tr}(X^m)=0$ for every $m\ge 1$.

Over $\mathbb C$, vanishing of $\operatorname{tr}(X),\operatorname{tr}(X^2),\dot
s,\operatorname{tr}(X^n)$ forces all eigenvalues of $X$ to be $0$ (Newton's iden
tities), so $X$ is nilpotent. $\square$

### Step 3. Apply Engel's theorem

We have a finite-dimensional Lie subalgebra $L\subset\mathfrak{gl}(V)$ such that
 every element of $L$ is a nilpotent endomorphism. By **Engel's theorem** (linea
r form), there exists a basis of $V$ in which every element of $L$ is represente
d by a **strictly upper-triangular** matrix.

Since $A,B\in L$, both $A$ and $B$ are strictly upper triangular in this common
basis. In particular they are simultaneously triangularizable.

Therefore, **yes**: $A$ and $B$ are simultaneously triangularizable. $\blacksqua
