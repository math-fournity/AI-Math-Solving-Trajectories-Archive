## Proof

We want to compute
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial \Sigma}\,\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\Sigma^{1/2} S^{-1}
 \Sigma^{1/2}\right),$$
where $\Sigma, S \succ 0$ are symmetric positive definite matrices (so $S^{-1}$
is symmetric, and $\Sigma^{1/2}$ denotes the unique symmetric positive definite
square root of $\Sigma$).

**Step 1: Simplify using the cyclic property of the trace.**

For any conformable matrices, $\operatorname{tr}(ABC) = \operatorname{tr}(CAB)$.
 Applying this with $A = \Sigma^{1/2}$, $B = S^{-1}$, $C = \Sigma^{1/2}$:
$$\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\Sigma^{1/2} S^{-1} \Sigma^{1/2}\right) = \operatorna
me{tr}\!\left(\Sigma^{1/2}\Sigma^{1/2} S^{-1}\right) = \operatorname{tr}\!\left(
\Sigma\, S^{-1}\right).$$

Using cyclicity once more, $\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma S^{-1}) = \operatorname{tr}
(S^{-1}\Sigma)$. Hence
$$f(\Sigma) := \operatorname{tr}\!\left(\Sigma^{1/2} S^{-1} \Sigma^{1/2}\right)
= \operatorname{tr}(S^{-1}\Sigma).$$

**Step 2: Compute the differential.**

Since $S$ is constant with respect to $\Sigma$,
$$df = \operatorname{tr}\!\left(S^{-1}\, d\Sigma\right).$$

**Step 3: Identify the gradient.**

The matrix derivative (gradient) $G = \dfrac{\partial f}{\partial \Sigma}$ is de
fined by the relation
$$df = \operatorname{tr}\!\left(G^{\mathsf T}\, d\Sigma\right)$$
for all symmetric perturbations $d\Sigma$. Comparing with Step 2,
$$G^{\mathsf T} = S^{-1} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad G = (S^{-1})^{\mathsf T}.$$

Since $S$ is symmetric positive definite, $S^{-1}$ is also symmetric, so $(S^{-1
})^{\mathsf T} = S^{-1}$.

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\dfrac{\partial}{\partial \Sigma}\,\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\Sigma^{1/2
} S^{-1} \Sigma^{1/2}\right) = S^{-1}.}$$
