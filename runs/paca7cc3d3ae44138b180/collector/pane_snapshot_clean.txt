Let $a=x+y,\ b=y+z,\ c=z+x$. Since $x+y+z=0$, we have $a+b+c=2(x+y+z)=0$, and $(

$$P(a)^3+P(b)^3+P(c)^3=3P(abc)\quad\text{for all }a,b,c\in\mathbb R,\ a+b+c=0. \
tag{1}$$

We use the classical identity: if $a+b+c=0$, then $a^3+b^3+c^3=3abc$.

**Step 1. Reduction to odd polynomials or constants.**

Set $(a,b,c)=(t,-t,0)$ in (1):
$$P(t)^3+P(-t)^3+P(0)^3=3P(0),$$
so $P(t)^3+P(-t)^3=K$ is constant, where $K=3P(0)-P(0)^3$.

Decompose $P(t)=E(t)+O(t)$ with $E$ even and $O$ odd. Then
$$P(t)^3+P(-t)^3=(E+O)^3+(E-O)^3=2E(E^2+3O^2).$$
So $2E\,(E^2+3O^2)=K$ in $\mathbb R[t]$.

- If $K\neq 0$: $\mathbb R[t]$ is an integral domain, so $\deg E+\deg(E^2+3O^2)=
0$; both factors are constant. Hence $E$ is constant and $E^2+3O^2$ constant for
ces $O^2$ constant, so $O\equiv 0$ (being odd). Thus $P$ is constant.
- If $K=0$: $E(E^2+3O^2)\equiv 0$. Either $E\equiv 0$ (so $P=O$ is **odd**), or
$E^2+3O^2\equiv 0$, which (evaluating at real $t$) forces $E\equiv O\equiv 0$, i
.e. $P\equiv 0$ (constant).

**Conclusion:** Every solution is either constant, or an odd polynomial.

**Step 2. Constant solutions.**

**Step 3. Odd polynomial solutions must be linear.**

Now $P$ is odd. Set $(a,b,c)=(t,s,-(t+s))$ in (1); using $P$ odd:
$$P(t)^3+P(s)^3-P(t+s)^3=-3P\bigl(ts(t+s)\bigr)\quad\text{for all }t,s. \tag{2}$
$

Suppose $\deg P=n$ with leading coefficient $\alpha\ne 0$ (and $n$ odd, $\ge 1$)
. Compare the degree-$3n$ homogeneous parts of (2):

- LHS top part: $\alpha^3\bigl(t^{3n}+s^{3n}-(t+s)^{3n}\bigr)$.
- RHS top part: $-3\alpha\,\bigl(ts(t+s)\bigr)^n=-3\alpha\,t^n s^n(t+s)^n$.

Equating and dividing by $\alpha\ne 0$:
$$\alpha^2\bigl(t^{3n}+s^{3n}-(t+s)^{3n}\bigr)=-3\,t^n s^n(t+s)^n. \tag{3}$$

The polynomial $t^{3n}+s^{3n}-(t+s)^{3n}$ contains the monomial $-3n\,t^{3n-1}s$

or **$n\ge 3$** we have $3n-1>2n$, so $t^{3n-1}s$ does **not** appear on the RHS
, yet its coefficient $-3n\alpha^2\ne 0$ on the LHS — a contradiction.

Hence $n=1$.
