## Solution

Suppose $f$ has a pole of order $k+1$ at $z_0$. Then in a punctured neighborhood
 of $z_0$ we can write

$$f(z) = \frac{g(z)}{(z-z_0)^{k+1}},$$

where $g$ is analytic at $z_0$ and $g(z_0) \neq 0$.

Taking the logarithmic derivative:

$$\frac{f'(z)}{f(z)} = \frac{d}{dz}\log f(z) = \frac{d}{dz}\left[\log g(z) - (k+
1)\log(z-z_0)\right] = \frac{g'(z)}{g(z)} - \frac{k+1}{z-z_0}.$$

Since $g(z_0)\neq 0$, the term $g'/g$ is analytic at $z_0$ and contributes no re
sidue. The only contribution to the residue comes from the simple pole term $-\f
rac{k+1}{z-z_0}$.

Therefore

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_0}\frac{f'}{f} = -(k+1).$$

$$\boxed{-(k+1)}$$
