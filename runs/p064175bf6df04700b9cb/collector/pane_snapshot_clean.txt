## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. Under the given hypotheses, every eigenvalue of $A\!\restriction
_V$ has strictly negative real part.

**Key facts used.**

1. Since $0$ is a *simple* eigenvalue of $A$ (algebraic multiplicity $1$), its g
eometric multiplicity is also $1$, so the eigenspace is exactly $\ker A = \opera
torname{span}(v)$.

2. $AV\subseteq V$ makes $V$ an $A$-invariant subspace. Choosing a basis of $V$
and extending to a basis of $\mathbb{C}^n$, the matrix of $A$ is block upper-tri
angular:
$$A \sim \begin{pmatrix} A\!\restriction_V & * \\ 0 & B \end{pmatrix},$$
hence
$$\chi_A(t)=\chi_{A\!\restriction_V}(t)\cdot\chi_B(t).$$
In particular, the eigenvalues of $A\!\restriction_V$ (with multiplicity) form a
 sub-multiset of the eigenvalues of $A$.

**Argument.**

Suppose, for contradiction, that $0$ is an eigenvalue of $A\!\restriction_V$. Th
en there exists $w\in V$, $w\neq 0$, with $Aw=0$, i.e. $w\in\ker A$. By Fact 1,
$\ker A=\operatorname{span}(v)$, so $w=cv$ for some $c\neq 0$. This gives $v\in
V$, contradicting the hypothesis $v\notin V$.

Therefore $0$ is **not** an eigenvalue of $A\!\restriction_V$.

By Fact 2, every eigenvalue of $A\!\restriction_V$ is an eigenvalue of $A$. The
eigenvalues of $A$ are precisely $\{0\}\cup\{\lambda_2,\dots,\lambda_n\}$ where
each $\lambda_i$ has $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_i)<0$. Since $0$ is excluded fro
m the spectrum of $A\!\restriction_V$, every eigenvalue of $A\!\restriction_V$ m
ust be one of the $\lambda_i$, each of which has strictly negative real part.

**Conclusion.** All eigenvalues of $A$ restricted to $V$ have strictly negative
real part. $\blacksquare$
