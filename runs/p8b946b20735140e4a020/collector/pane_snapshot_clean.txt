Looking at this problem, I need to find the number of distinct real values of $x
$ satisfying $(x^4 - 5)^5 = 5 - \frac{24}{x^4}$.

## Setup

Note $x \neq 0$ (since $x^4$ appears in a denominator). Let $t = x^4$, so $t > 0
$. The equation becomes:

$$(t-5)^5 = 5 - \frac{24}{t}$$

Multiplying both sides by $t$:

$$t(t-5)^5 = 5t - 24 \implies t(t-5)^5 - 5t + 24 = 0$$

## Substitution $u = t - 5$

Setting $t = u + 5$:

$$(u+5)u^5 - 5(u+5) + 24 = 0$$
$$u^6 + 5u^5 - 5u - 25 + 24 = 0$$
$$u^6 + 5u^5 - 5u - 1 = 0$$

## Factoring

**Testing $u = 1$:** $1 + 5 - 5 - 1 = 0$ ✓. So $(u-1)$ is a factor. Synthetic di
vision yields:

$$(u-1)(u^5 + 6u^4 + 6u^3 + 6u^2 + 6u + 1) = 0$$

**Testing $u = -1$ in the quintic:** $-1 + 6 - 6 + 6 - 6 + 1 = 0$ ✓. So $(u+1)$
is a factor:

$$(u-1)(u+1)(u^4 + 5u^3 + u^2 + 5u + 1) = 0$$

## The palindromic quartic

$u^4 + 5u^3 + u^2 + 5u + 1$ has palindromic coefficients $(1, 5, 1, 5, 1)$. Divi
ding by $u^2$ and substituting $v = u + \frac{1}{u}$:

$$\left(v^2 - 2\right) + 5v + 1 = 0 \implies v^2 + 5v - 1 = 0$$

$$v = \frac{-5 \pm \sqrt{29}}{2}$$

Let $v_1 = \frac{-5+\sqrt{29}}{2} \approx 0.19$ and $v_2 = \frac{-5-\sqrt{29}}{2
} \approx -5.19$.

For each $v$, solving $u^2 - vu + 1 = 0$ requires $v^2 - 4 \geq 0$, i.e., $|v| \
geq 2$.

- **$v_1 \approx 0.19$:** $|v_1| < 2$ → **no real roots** (2 complex roots).
- **$v_2 \approx -5.19$:** $|v_2| > 2$ → **two real roots**:
$$u = \frac{v_2 \pm \sqrt{v_2^2 - 4}}{2}$$

## Counting real roots of $u$

Total real roots: $u = 1$, $u = -1$, and 2 from $v_2$ → **4 real roots**. (The 2
 complex roots from $v_1$ account for the full degree 6.)

## Checking $t > 0$ (i.e., $u > -5$) for each root

1. **$u = 1$:** $t = 6 > 0$ ✓
2. **$u = -1$:** $t = 4 > 0$ ✓
3. **$u_1 = \frac{v_2 + \sqrt{v_2^2-4}}{2} \approx -0.20$:** $t \approx 4.80 > 0

4. **$u_2 = \frac{v_2 - \sqrt{v_2^2-4}}{2} \approx -4.99$:** Need to verify $u_2

For $u_2 > -5$, we need $v_2 - \sqrt{v_2^2 - 4} > -10$, i.e., $v_2 + 10 > \sqrt{
v_2^2-4}$. Since $v_2 + 10 \approx 4.81 > 0$, squaring:

$$(v_2+10)^2 > v_2^2 - 4 \implies 20v_2 + 100 > -4 \implies v_2 > -5.2$$

Now $v_2 = \frac{-5-\sqrt{29}}{2}$. Since $\sqrt{29} < 5.4$, we get $v_2 > \frac
{-5-5.4}{2} = -5.2$ ✓

So $t_2 = u_2 + 5 > 0$ ✓ (approximately $0.008$).

## Converting back to $x$

Each valid $t > 0$ gives $x^4 = t$, yielding exactly **two** real values $x = \p
m\, t^{1/4}$.

The four $t$-values ($6, 4, \approx 4.80, \approx 0.008$) are all distinct (comi
ng from distinct roots of the degree-6 polynomial), so all eight $x$-values are
distinct.
