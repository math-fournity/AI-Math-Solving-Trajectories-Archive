The proof proceeds in two parts: an upper bound via a specific $Y$, and a lower

### Part 1 (Upper Bound): $\inf_Y \sup_X P(X > Y) \leq \frac{1}{2}$

Take $Y \sim \text{Uniform}(0, 2)$. Then $E[Y] = 1$ and $Y \geq 0$. Define $h(x)
 = P(Y < x)$. For $x \geq 0$:

$$h(x) = \begin{cases} x/2 & 0 \leq x \leq 2 \\ 1 & x \geq 2 \end{cases}$$

This function is **concave on $[0, \infty)$** (slopes $1/2 \to 0$ are non-increa
sing).

For any $X \geq 0$ with $E[X] = 1$, since $X$ and $Y$ are independent:

$$P(X > Y) = E_X[P(Y < X)] = E_X[h(X)].$$

Since $h$ is concave on $[0,\infty)$ and $X \geq 0$, by **Jensen's inequality**:

$$E[h(X)] \leq h(E[X]) = h(1) = \frac{1}{2}.$$

Equality is achieved by $X \equiv 1$ (constant). Therefore $\sup_X P(X > Y) = \f
rac{1}{2}$, which gives:

$$\sup_X \inf_Y P(X > Y) \leq \inf_Y \sup_X P(X > Y) \leq \frac{1}{2},$$

where the first inequality is weak duality ($\sup \inf \leq \inf \sup$).

### Part 2 (Lower Bound): $\sup_X \inf_Y P(X > Y) \geq \frac{1}{2}$

Take $X \sim \text{Uniform}(0, 2)$. Then $E[X] = 1$ and $X \geq 0$. We show $P(X

Since $P(X > y) = \max\!\left(1 - \frac{y}{2},\, 0\right)$ for $y \geq 0$:

$$P(X > Y) = E_Y\!\left[\left(1 - \frac{Y}{2}\right) \mathbf{1}_{\{Y \leq 2\}}\r

Using $E[Y] = E[Y\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y \leq 2\}}] + E[Y\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y > 2\}}] = 1

$$P(X > Y) = P(Y \leq 2) - \frac{1}{2}\left(1 - E[Y\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y > 2\}}]\rig

$$P(X > Y) \geq \frac{1}{2} - P(Y > 2) + \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2\, P(Y > 2) = \frac{

This holds for **every** admissible $Y$, so $\inf_Y P(X > Y) \geq \frac{1}{2}$,

$$\sup_X \inf_Y P(X > Y) \geq \frac{1}{2}.$$

### Conclusion

Combining both parts:

$$\sup_X \inf_Y P(X > Y) = \frac{1}{2}.$$

The value is achieved by $X \sim \text{Uniform}(0,2)$ (for the lower bound) and
$Y \sim \text{Uniform}(0,2)$ (for the upper bound), with $E[X] = E[Y] = 1$.
