Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $\mu(T^{-n}[0,\tfrac{1}{2})
) \to \tfrac{1}{2}$ for every absolutely continuous probability measure $\mu$ on
 $[0,1]$, where $T(x) = 2x \bmod 1$.

**Answer: Yes, it is true in general.**

## Proof

Let $f \in L^1[0,1]$ be the density of $\mu$ (so $f \geq 0$, $\int_0^1 f = 1$),
and let $A_n := T^{-n}[0,\tfrac{1}{2})$.

**Step 1: Structure of $A_n$.**

Since $T^n(x) = 2^n x \bmod 1$, we have
$$A_n = \bigl\{x \in [0,1) : \{2^n x\} \in [0,\tfrac{1}{2})\bigr\} = \bigcup_{k=
0}^{2^n-1}\Bigl[\frac{k}{2^n},\,\frac{k}{2^n}+\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\Bigr).$$
This is a union of $2^n$ intervals, each of length $2^{-(n+1)}$, totaling Lebesg
ue measure $\tfrac{1}{2}$.

**Step 2: Reduction to a weak-convergence statement.**

Define the complementary set $B_n := T^{-n}[\tfrac{1}{2},1) = [0,1)\setminus A_n
$, and set
$$g_n(x) := \mathbf{1}_{A_n}(x) - \mathbf{1}_{B_n}(x) = 2\,\mathbf{1}_{A_n}(x) -
 1.$$
This is the $(n{+}1)$-th **Rademacher function**: on each dyadic interval $[k/2^
n,(k{+}1)/2^n)$ it equals $+1$ on the left half and $-1$ on the right half. Sinc
e $A_n \cup B_n = [0,1)$ and $A_n \cap B_n = \emptyset$:
$$\mu(A_n) - \tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\mu(A_n)-\mu(B_n)\bigr) = \tfrac{1
}{2}\int_0^1 f(x)\,g_n(x)\,dx.$$
Thus it suffices to prove:

> **Claim.** $\displaystyle\int_0^1 f(x)\,g_n(x)\,dx \to 0$ for every $f \in L^1
[0,1]$.

**Step 3: Key property of $g_n$.**

*For any dyadic interval $J$ of length $2^{-N}$ with $N \leq n$, $\int_J g_n(x)\
,dx = 0$.*

*Proof.* Write $J = [k/2^N, (k{+}1)/2^N)$. On $J$, the function $g_n$ alternates
 between $+1$ and $-1$ on consecutive sub-intervals of length $2^{-(n+1)}$. Ther
e are exactly $2^{n-N}$ such $+1$-pieces and $2^{n-N}$ such $-1$-pieces, so the
integral vanishes. $\square$

**Step 4: Density of dyadic step functions.**

Dyadic step functions — finite linear combinations $s = \sum_j c_j\,\mathbf{1}_{
J_j}$ where each $J_j$ is a dyadic interval of some common length $2^{-N}$ — are
 dense in $L^1[0,1]$. (This follows, e.g., from the martingale convergence theor
em: the conditional expectations $E[f\mid\mathcal{F}_N]$, where $\mathcal{F}_N$
is the $\sigma$-algebra generated by dyadic intervals of length $2^{-N}$, are ex
actly such step functions and converge to $f$ in $L^1$.)

**Step 5: Proof of the Claim.**

Let $\varepsilon > 0$. By Step 4, choose $N$ and a dyadic step function $s$ (con
stant on intervals of length $2^{-N}$) with $\|f - s\|_{L^1} < \varepsilon$. For
 all $n \geq N$:
$$\int_0^1 f\,g_n = \underbrace{\int_0^1 (f-s)\,g_n}_{\text{(I)}} + \underbrace{
\int_0^1 s\,g_n}_{\text{(II)}}.$$

- **(I):** Since $|g_n| \equiv 1$, $|\text{(I)}| \leq \|f-s\|_{L^1}\cdot\|g_n\|_
{L^\infty} < \varepsilon$.
- **(II):** Writing $s = \sum_j c_j\,\mathbf{1}_{J_j}$ with each $J_j$ of length
 $2^{-N}$, Step 3 gives $\int_{J_j} g_n = 0$ for every $j$ (since $n \geq N$). H
ence $\text{(II)} = \sum_j c_j\int_{J_j} g_n = 0$.

Therefore $\bigl|\int_0^1 f\,g_n\bigr| < \varepsilon$ for all $n \geq N$. Since
$\varepsilon$ was arbitrary, the Claim holds.

**Step 6: Conclusion.**

From Step 2 and the Claim:
$$\mu(A_n) = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\int_0^1 f(x)\,g_n(x)\,dx \;\longrightarro
w\; \frac{1}{2} + 0 = \frac{1}{2}.$$

This holds for **every** absolutely continuous probability measure $\mu$ on $[0,
1]$, since the only hypothesis used is $f \in L^1[0,1]$. $\blacksquare$
