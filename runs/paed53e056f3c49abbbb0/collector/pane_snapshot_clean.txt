## Solution

**Claim.** The function $f(x) = x^2$ (with $x_k = 0$, $d_k = 1$) is a valid exam
ple: for every $t > 0$, the Armijo criterion with $\alpha = 1$ is **not** satisf
ied.

**Verification.**

The Armijo criterion (with $\alpha = 1$) reads:
$$f(x_k + t d_k) \leq f(x_k) + t\,\nabla f(x_k)^{T} d_k.$$

Take $f(x) = x^2$, $x_k = 0$, $d_k = 1$. Then:
- $f(x_k) = f(0) = 0$,
- $\nabla f(x) = 2x$, so $\nabla f(x_k) = \nabla f(0) = 0$,
- $f(x_k + t d_k) = f(t) = t^2$,
- Right-hand side: $f(x_k) + t\,\nabla f(x_k)^{T} d_k = 0 + t \cdot 0 \cdot 1 =
0$.

The criterion becomes:
$$t^2 \leq 0.$$

For every $t > 0$, we have $t^2 > 0$, so the inequality $t^2 \leq 0$ is **false*
*. Hence the Armijo criterion fails for every $t > 0$.

**Why this happens.** With $\alpha = 1$, the Armijo condition reduces to the fir
st-order linear (tangent-plane) upper bound:
$$f(x_k + t d_k) \leq f(x_k) + t\,\nabla f(x_k)^{T} d_k.$$
This is exactly the statement that $f$ lies *below* its first-order Taylor appro
ximation at $x_k$ along the ray $x_k + t d_k$. For a **strictly convex** twice-d
ifferentiable function, the Taylor remainder is
$$f(x_k + t d_k) = f(x_k) + t\,\nabla f(x_k)^{T} d_k + \tfrac{t^2}{2}\,d_k^{T}\n
abla^2 f(x_k)\,d_k + o(t^2),$$
and when $\nabla^2 f(x_k)$ is positive definite and $d_k \neq 0$, the quadratic
term $\tfrac{t^2}{2}d_k^{T}\nabla^2 f(x_k)d_k > 0$ for all $t > 0$, making the f
unction lie strictly *above* its tangent line. Thus the Armijo condition with $\
alpha = 1$ is violated for every $t > 0$.

For $f(x) = x^2$ this is exact (no remainder): the gap is precisely $t^2 d_k^2 =
 t^2 > 0$.
