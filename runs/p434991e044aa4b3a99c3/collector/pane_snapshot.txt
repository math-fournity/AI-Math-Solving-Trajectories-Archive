## Proof

Let \( U \) be an open region in the complex plane, and let \( \mathcal{M} \) be
 the collection of meromorphic functions on \( U \). We must show that \( \mathc
al{M} \) is closed under addition, subtraction, multiplication, and (nonzero) di
vision.

### Setup and key facts

Recall that a function \( f: U \to \widehat{\mathbb{C}} \) is **meromorphic** on
 \( U \) if it is holomorphic on \( U \setminus P_f \), where \( P_f \subset U \
) is a discrete set of poles. Equivalently, around every point \( a \in U \), th
ere is a neighborhood \( V \subset U \) of \( a \) and a holomorphic function \(
 g \) on \( V \) with \( g \not\equiv 0 \) on any connected component of \( V \)
, together with a nonnegative integer \( m \), such that

\[
f(z) = \frac{h(z)}{(z-a)^m}, \qquad z \in V \setminus \{a\},
\]

where \( h \) is holomorphic on \( V \) with \( h(a) \neq 0 \) when \( m > 0 \).
 In other words, locally every meromorphic function is a ratio of two holomorphi
c functions with the denominator not identically zero.

A crucial and standard fact we use throughout:

> **Fact (local representation).** A function is meromorphic on \( U \) if and o
nly if it is locally a quotient \( g/h \) of holomorphic functions with \( h \)
not identically zero on each connected component of the neighborhood.

We also use:

> **Fact (discreteness of zeros).** The zero set of a holomorphic function that
is not identically zero on a connected open set is discrete.

---

### Addition and subtraction

Let \( f, g \in \mathcal{M} \). We show \( f \pm g \in \mathcal{M} \).

Fix any point \( a \in U \). Since \( f \) and \( g \) are meromorphic, there ex
ist a neighborhood \( V \subset U \) of \( a \), holomorphic functions \( F, G,
H, K \) on \( V \) (with \( H, K \) not identically zero on each component of \(
 V \)) such that

\[
f = \frac{F}{H}, \qquad g = \frac{G}{K} \quad \text{on } V \setminus (\{H=0\} \c
up \{K=0\}).
\]

(Here we shrink \( V \) so that both local representations hold simultaneously.)
 Then

\[
f \pm g = \frac{F K \pm G H}{H K} \quad \text{on } V \setminus (\{H=0\} \cup \{K
=0\}).
\]

The numerator \( FK \pm GH \) and the denominator \( HK \) are holomorphic on \(
 V \). The denominator \( HK \) is not identically zero on any connected compone
nt of \( V \) (since \( H \) and \( K \) are each not identically zero, and a pr
oduct of two non-identically-zero holomorphic functions on a connected domain is
 not identically zero). Therefore, by the local representation fact, \( f \pm g
\) is meromorphic on \( V \). Since \( a \) was arbitrary, \( f \pm g \) is mero
morphic on all of \( U \).

---

### Multiplication

With the same local setup, on \( V \setminus (\{H=0\} \cup \{K=0\}) \):

\[
f \cdot g = \frac{F G}{H K}.
\]

Again \( FG \) and \( HK \) are holomorphic on \( V \), and \( HK \) is not iden
tically zero on any connected component of \( V \). By the local representation
fact, \( fg \) is meromorphic on \( V \), and hence on \( U \).

---

### Division

Let \( f, g \in \mathcal{M} \) with \( g \not\equiv 0 \) (i.e., \( g \) is not t
he zero element of \( \mathcal{M} \), meaning \( g \) is not identically zero on
 any connected component of \( U \)). We show \( f/g \in \mathcal{M} \).

Fix \( a \in U \) and choose a connected neighborhood \( V \subset U \) of \( a
\) on which both \( f = F/H \) and \( g = G/K \) admit local representations wit
h \( F, G, H, K \) holomorphic on \( V \), \( H, K \) not identically zero on \(
 V \). Since \( g \not\equiv 0 \) on \( U \), and \( V \) is connected, \( g \)
is not identically zero on \( V \), which means \( G \) is not identically zero
on \( V \).

On \( V \setminus (\{H=0\} \cup \{K=0\} \cup \{G=0\}) \):

\[
\frac{f}{g} = \frac{F/H}{G/K} = \frac{F K}{G H}.
\]

The numerator \( FK \) and denominator \( GH \) are holomorphic on \( V \). We m
ust check the denominator \( GH \) is not identically zero on \( V \). Since \(
V \) is connected and \( G \) is not identically zero on \( V \), and \( H \) is
 not identically zero on \( V \), their product \( GH \) is not identically zero
 on \( V \) (a product of two non-identically-zero holomorphic functions on a co
nnected domain is not identically zero). By the local representation fact, \( f/
g \) is meromorphic on \( V \), and hence on \( U \).

---

### Why the "discrete set of poles" condition is preserved

For completeness, we verify the pole sets remain discrete. For \( f \pm g \) and
 \( fg \), the only possible poles occur where \( H = 0 \) or \( K = 0 \), i.e.,
 in \( P_f \cup P_g \), which is a union of two discrete sets in \( U \) and hen
ce discrete (a discrete subset of an open set in \( \mathbb{C} \) has no accumul
ation point in \( U \), and a finite union of such sets has the same property).
For \( f/g \), possible poles occur where \( H = 0 \) (poles of \( f \)) or \( G
 = 0 \) (zeros of \( g \)); the zero set of the non-identically-zero holomorphic
 \( G \) is discrete, so again we get a discrete set. Thus in every case the res
ulting function is holomorphic off a discrete set, confirming meromorphicity.

---

### Conclusion

We have shown that for any \( f, g \in \mathcal{M} \):

- \( f + g \in \mathcal{M} \) and \( f - g \in \mathcal{M} \) (addition/subtract
ion),
- \( f \cdot g \in \mathcal{M} \) (multiplication),
- \( f / g \in \mathcal{M} \) whenever \( g \not\equiv 0 \) (division).

The key mechanism in each case is the **local quotient representation**: meromor
phic functions are locally ratios of holomorphic functions, and the four arithme
tic operations on such ratios again produce ratios of holomorphic functions (wit
h a non-identically-zero denominator on each connected component). This local pr
operty, combined with the discreteness of zeros of non-identically-zero holomorp
hic functions, guarantees that the result is again meromorphic on all of \( U \)
.

Therefore \( \mathcal{M} \) is closed under addition, subtraction, multiplicatio
n, and division (by nonzero elements), making it a **field** (the field of merom
orphic functions on \( U \)).
