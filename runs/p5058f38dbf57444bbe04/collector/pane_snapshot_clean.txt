**Answer: Yes, such $N$ exists.**

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $X$ be a smooth complex projective variety of dimension $n$, $L$
an ample divisor. A nontrivial holomorphic map $u: \Sigma \to X$ is non-constant
, so since $L$ is ample, $d := \deg(u^*L) \geq 1$. We must show: for each $M \ge
q 1$, there exists $N$ such that generic smooth $D \in |NL|$ contains no image o
f a holomorphic map from a curve of genus $g < M$ and degree $d$ with $1 \leq d
< M$.

**Step 1: Moduli spaces are of finite type.** For each pair $(g, d)$ with $0 \le
q g < M$ and $1 \leq d < M$, the moduli space $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,0}(X, d
)$ of stable maps from genus-$g$ curves to $X$ of $L$-degree $d$ is a proper Del
igne–Mumford stack of finite type (since $X$ is projective). If non-empty, set
$$C_{g,d} := \dim \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,0}(X, d) < \infty.$$
If empty, there is nothing to exclude for this $(g,d)$.

**Step 2: Uniform surjectivity of the restriction map.** Over $\overline{\mathca
l{M}}_{g,0}(X,d)$ we have the universal curve $\pi: \mathcal{C} \to \overline{\m
athcal{M}}$ and evaluation map $\mathrm{ev}: \mathcal{C} \to X$. The line bundle
 $\mathrm{ev}^*L$ restricted to each fiber of $\pi$ has degree $d \geq 1$, hence
 is $\pi$-ample. By relative Serre vanishing (EGA III, 4.1.5), there exists $N_0
$ (depending on $g, d, X, L$) such that for all $N \geq N_0$:
- $R^1\pi_* \mathrm{ev}^*\mathcal{O}(NL) = 0$, so $\pi_*\mathrm{ev}^*\mathcal{O}
(NL)$ is locally free of rank $Nd + 1 - g$ (by Riemann–Roch on each fiber).
- The natural map $H^0(X, \mathcal{O}(NL)) \otimes \mathcal{O}_{\overline{\mathc
al{M}}} \to \pi_*\mathrm{ev}^*\mathcal{O}(NL)$ is surjective (by Castelnuovo–Mum
ford regularity applied uniformly over the bounded family).

Taking the maximum over the finitely many pairs $(g,d)$, we obtain a single $N_0
$ such that for all $N \geq N_0$ and all $[u] \in \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,0}(X
,d)$, the pullback map
$$u^*: H^0(X, \mathcal{O}(NL)) \longrightarrow H^0(\Sigma,\, u^*\mathcal{O}(NL))
 \cong H^0(\Sigma, \mathcal{O}_\Sigma(Nd))$$
is **surjective**, with target dimension $Nd + 1 - g$.

**Step 3: Incidence variety dimension count.** For each $(g,d)$, define the inci
dence variety:
$$\mathcal{I}_{g,d} = \bigl\{([u], [s]) \in \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,0}(X,d) \t
imes |NL| : u(\Sigma) \subseteq V(s)\bigr\}.$$
The fiber of the projection $p_1: \mathcal{I}_{g,d} \to \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{
g,0}(X,d)$ over $[u]$ is
$$\mathbb{P}\bigl(\ker(u^*: H^0(X,\mathcal{O}(NL)) \to H^0(\Sigma, \mathcal{O}_\
Sigma(Nd)))\bigr).$$
By Step 2, for $N \geq N_0$ this kernel has dimension $h^0(X, \mathcal{O}(NL)) -
 (Nd + 1 - g)$, so the fiber has dimension
$$h^0(X, \mathcal{O}(NL)) - Nd + g - 2.$$
Therefore:
$$\dim \mathcal{I}_{g,d} = C_{g,d} + h^0(X, \mathcal{O}(NL)) - Nd + g - 2.$$

**Step 4: The projection to $|NL|$ is not surjective for $N$ large.** The projec
tion $p_2: \mathcal{I}_{g,d} \to |NL|$ has image equal to the locus of divisors
in $|NL|$ containing the image of some genus-$g$, degree-$d$ holomorphic map. Th
is image is a proper subset of $|NL|$ provided:
$$\dim \mathcal{I}_{g,d} < \dim |NL| = h^0(X, \mathcal{O}(NL)) - 1.$$
Substituting:
$$C_{g,d} + h^0(X, \mathcal{O}(NL)) - Nd + g - 2 < h^0(X, \mathcal{O}(NL)) - 1,$
$
which simplifies to:
$$C_{g,d} < Nd - g + 1.$$
Since $d \geq 1$ and $g \leq M - 1$, it suffices to take $N > C_{g,d} + M - 1$.

**Step 5: Choosing $N$.** Set
$$N = \max\!\Bigl(N_0,\; \max_{\substack{0 \leq g < M \\ 1 \leq d < M}} \bigl(C_
{g,d} + M\bigr)\Bigr),$$
where the inner maximum is taken over pairs for which $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g
,0}(X,d)$ is non-empty (a finite set). Then for every such $(g,d)$, the bad locu
s $p_2(\mathcal{I}_{g,d}) \subsetneq |NL|$ is a **proper closed subset**.

**Step 6: Genericity and smoothness.** The union
$$\mathcal{B} = \bigcup_{\substack{0 \leq g < M \\ 1 \leq d < M}} p_2(\mathcal{I
}_{g,d})$$
is a finite union of proper closed subsets of $|NL|$, hence is itself a proper c
losed subset. Furthermore, for $N \geq N_0$, $NL$ is very ample (by Serre's theo
rem), so by the Bertini theorem the locus of singular divisors in $|NL|$ is also
 a proper closed subset $\mathcal{S} \subsetneq |NL|$.

Therefore, the complement $|NL| \setminus (\mathcal{B} \cup \mathcal{S})$ is non
-empty and Zariski-open. Any $D$ in this complement is **smooth** and contains *
*no nontrivial holomorphic map** of genus $< M$ and degree $< M$.

**Conclusion.** For every positive integer $M$, there exists a positive integer
$N$ such that a generic smooth hypersurface $D \in |NL|$ admits no nontrivial ho
lomorphic maps of both degree and genus less than $M$ into $D$. $\blacksquare$
