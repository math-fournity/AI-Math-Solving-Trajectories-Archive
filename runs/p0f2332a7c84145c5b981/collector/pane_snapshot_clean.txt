Looking at this problem, I need to evaluate:
$$\lim_{x \to \infty} \sum_{n=0}^\infty (-1)^{n} \frac{x |x|^{2n}}{2n+1}$$

## Step 1: Simplify the general term

As $x \to \infty$, we have $x > 0$, so $|x| = x$ and $|x|^{2n} = x^{2n}$. Theref
ore:
$$x \cdot |x|^{2n} = x \cdot x^{2n} = x^{2n+1}$$

The series becomes:
$$S(x) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty (-1)^{n} \frac{x^{2n+1}}{2n+1}$$

## Step 2: Identify the series

Recall the Maclaurin series for $\arctan(x)$:
$$\arctan(x) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty (-1)^{n} \frac{x^{2n+1}}{2n+1}, \quad |x| \leq
1$$

This is derived from integrating the geometric series:
$$\frac{1}{1+t^2} = \sum_{n=0}^\infty (-1)^n t^{2n}, \quad |t| < 1$$

Integrating term-by-term from $0$ to $x$:
$$\arctan(x) = \int_0^x \frac{dt}{1+t^2} = \sum_{n=0}^\infty (-1)^n \frac{x^{2n+
1}}{2n+1}$$

The series converges for $|x| \leq 1$ (at $x = \pm 1$ by the alternating series
test; for $|x| < 1$ by absolute convergence).

## Step 3: Analytic continuation and the limit

The series $S(x)$ defines $\arctan(x)$ on $[-1, 1]$. The function $\arctan(x)$ e
xtends analytically to all of $\mathbb{R}$ via the integral representation:
$$\arctan(x) = \int_0^x \frac{dt}{1+t^2}$$

For $x > 0$, using the identity $\arctan(x) + \arctan(1/x) = \pi/2$:
$$\arctan(x) = \frac{\pi}{2} - \arctan\!\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)$$

where for $x > 1$, the series for $\arctan(1/x)$ **does** converge (since $|1/x|
 < 1$):
$$\arctan\!\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty (-1)^n \frac{x^{-(2n+1)}
}{2n+1}$$

## Step 4: Evaluate the limit

The series $S(x) = \arctan(x)$ represents the function $\arctan$ (its analytic c
ontinuation beyond the radius of convergence). Taking the limit:
$$\lim_{x \to \infty} \arctan(x) = \lim_{x \to \infty} \left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \ar
ctan\!\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\right) = \frac{\pi}{2} - \arctan(0) = \frac{\pi}{
2} - 0 = \frac{\pi}{2}$$

## Conclusion

The series is the Maclaurin expansion of $\arctan(x)$, and the limit of $\arctan
(x)$ as $x \to \infty$ is:

$$\boxed{\dfrac{\pi}{2}}$$
