Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether fiber multiplicity is local

**Answer: Yes**

**Proof.**

**Step 1: Dimension and structure of $V$.**

Since $\pi: V \to \mathbb{A}^1_{\mathbb{C}}$ is flat and surjective, dimension t
heory for flat morphisms gives $\dim V = \dim \mathbb{A}^1 = 1$. Since $\pi$ is
quasifinite, every fiber $\pi^{-1}(p)$ is a zero-dimensional scheme of finite ty
pe over $\mathbb{C}$, hence an artinian $\mathbb{C}$-scheme (a finite set of poi
nts with multiplicities). The *multiplicity* of the fiber is $\operatorname{leng
th}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{O}_{\pi^{-1}(p)})$, i.e., the sum of local multiplicit
ies over all points in the fiber.

**Step 2: The analytic restriction $\pi|_{U \cap V}$ is flat.**

Flatness is a local property. The analytic space $V^{\mathrm{an}}$ associated to
 $V$ carries a flat holomorphic map $\pi^{\mathrm{an}}: V^{\mathrm{an}} \to (\ma
thbb{A}^1)^{\mathrm{an}}$. Restricting to the open analytic subset $U \cap V^{\m
athrm{an}}$ preserves flatness. So $\pi|_{U \cap V}: U \cap V \to \mathbb{A}^1$
is a flat, quasifinite holomorphic map.

**Step 3: The condition on components ensures properness over a neighborhood of
$0$.**

Let $C_1, \dots, C_n$ be the irreducible components of the analytic set $U \cap
V$. By hypothesis, every $C_i$ passes through $(0,0)$. Each $C_i$ is a one-dimen
sional analytic set (an analytic curve) since $\dim V = 1$.

Since $\pi$ is quasifinite, the restriction $\pi|_{C_i}: C_i \to \mathbb{A}^1$ i
s quasifinite: the fiber over any point is zero-dimensional. As $C_i$ is an irre
ducible curve through $(0,0)$ with $\pi(0,0) = 0$, the map $\pi|_{C_i}$ is a non
-constant holomorphic map from a curve to a curve, hence a branched covering loc
ally. By the local properness theorem for finite maps between analytic curves (o
r by the Remmert–Stein / local finiteness theorem), there exist:
- a neighborhood $W_i \subset U \cap C_i$ of $(0,0)$ in $C_i$, and
- a disk $D_i \subset \mathbb{A}^1$ around $0$,

such that $\pi|_{W_i}: W_i \to D_i$ is **finite** (proper with finite fibers).

Set $U' = \bigcap_{i=1}^n D_i$ (a disk around $0$) and $W = \bigcup_i W_i$.

**Key claim:** For $U'$ sufficiently small, $U \cap \pi^{-1}(p) = W \cap \pi^{-1
}(p)$ for all $p \in U'$.

*Proof of claim.* Every component $C_i$ of $U \cap V$ passes through $(0,0)$, an
d $\pi|_{C_i}$ is finite over $D_i$. For $p \in U' \subset D_i$, all points of $
C_i \cap \pi^{-1}(p)$ lie in $W_i$ (since $\pi|_{W_i}: W_i \to D_i$ is finite an
d $W_i$ is a neighborhood of $(0,0)$ in $C_i$; any preimage of $p$ in $C_i$ that
 is near $(0,0)$ lies in $W_i$, and for $p$ close to $0$, all preimages in $C_i$
 are near $(0,0)$ because $\pi|_{C_i}$ is continuous and $(\pi|_{C_i})^{-1}(0) \
cap C_i$ is a finite set near $(0,0)$). Since $U$ is a neighborhood of $(0,0)$ a
nd all components pass through $(0,0)$, shrinking $U'$ ensures all preimages of
$p$ in $U \cap V$ lie in $W$. $\square$

**Step 4: Finite flat implies locally constant fiber length.**

The map $\pi|_W: W \to U'$ is finite (a finite union of finite maps) and flat (r
estriction of the flat map $\pi$). For a finite flat morphism $f: X \to S$ of co
mplex spaces, the direct image $f_*\mathcal{O}_X$ is a locally free $\mathcal{O}
_S$-module (finite flat = locally free). Its rank at $p \in S$ equals:

$$\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{O}_{S,p}}(f_*\mathcal{O}_X)_p = \operatorname{le
ngth}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{O}_{f^{-1}(p)}) = \text{multiplicity of } f^{-1}(p).
$$

Since the rank of a locally free sheaf is locally constant (on a connected base,
 which a disk is), the multiplicity of $f^{-1}(p) = W \cap \pi^{-1}(p)$ is const
ant for all $p \in U'$.

By the claim in Step 3, $U \cap \pi^{-1}(p) = W \cap \pi^{-1}(p)$ for $p \in U'$
, so the multiplicity of $U \cap \pi^{-1}(p)$ is constant for all $p \in U'$.

**Conclusion.** There exists an analytic neighborhood $U'$ of $(0)$ in $\mathbb{
A}^1_{\mathbb{C}}$ such that the multiplicity of $U \cap \pi^{-1}(p)$ is constan
t for all $p \in U'$. The answer is **Yes**. $\square$
