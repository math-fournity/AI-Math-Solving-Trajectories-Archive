# Problem

In the competitive world of urban planning, a developer is designing a $5 \times 5$ grid of residential plots. To ensure a diverse neighborhood, the developer assigns each of the 25 plots a unique "prestige rating" using every integer from $1$ to $25$ exactly once.

The city’s zoning laws focus on "mini-blocks," defined as any $2 \times 2$ cluster of four adjacent plots within the larger grid. The city council wants to guarantee a minimum level of collective prestige for these clusters, regardless of how the developer distributes the individual ratings across the grid.

What is the largest natural number $N$ such that every possible arrangement of the 25 distinct ratings must contain at least one $2 \times 2$ mini-block whose four ratings sum to at least $N$?
