## Proof

Let $n$ be the (finite) number of diagonal blocks, and set
$$
D \;:=\;\begin{pmatrix} M & & \\ & \ddots & \\ & & M \end{pmatrix} \;\in\; \math
cal{L}(E^{\oplus n}),
\qquad E^{\oplus n} = \underbrace{E\oplus\cdots\oplus E}_{n}.
$$
Since $M\ge 0$, the block-diagonal operator $D=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n} M$ is also po
sitive. We claim
$$
D^{\,t} \;=\; \bigoplus_{k=1}^{n} M^{\,t}, \qquad \forall\, t\in(0,\infty).
$$

---

### Spectral resolution of $D$

By the spectral theorem, the positive operator $M$ admits a projection-valued sp
ectral family $\{E(\lambda)\}_{\lambda\ge 0}$ on $E$ with
$$
M \;=\; \int_{[0,\infty)} \lambda\, dE(\lambda).
$$

Define on $E^{\oplus n}$ the family
$$
\mathcal{E}(\lambda) \;:=\; \bigoplus_{k=1}^{n} E(\lambda) \;=\; \begin{pmatrix}
 E(\lambda) & & \\ & \ddots & \\ & & E(\lambda) \end{pmatrix}.
$$
Each $\mathcal{E}(\lambda)$ is an orthogonal projection on $E^{\oplus n}$; the f
amily is increasing, right-continuous, with $\mathcal{E}(0^-)=0$ and $\mathcal{E
}(\lambda)\to I_{E^{\oplus n}}$ as $\lambda\to\infty$. Moreover,
$$
\int_{[0,\infty)} \lambda\, d\mathcal{E}(\lambda) \;=\; \bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}\int_
{[0,\infty)}\lambda\, dE(\lambda) \;=\; \bigoplus_{k=1}^{n} M \;=\; D,
$$
so $\{\mathcal{E}(\lambda)\}$ is precisely the spectral family of $D$.

---

### Functional calculus commutes with direct sums

For any bounded Borel function $f$ on $[0,\infty)$, the functional calculus give
s
$$
f(D) \;=\; \int_{[0,\infty)} f(\lambda)\, d\mathcal{E}(\lambda).
$$
Because $\mathcal{E}(\lambda)=\bigoplus_{k} E(\lambda)$ acts componentwise, the
spectral integral decomposes componentwise: for every $x=(x_1,\dots,x_n)\in E^{\
oplus n}$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\bigl[f(D)\bigr]x
&= \int f(\lambda)\, d\mathcal{E}(\lambda)\,(x_1,\dots,x_n) \\
&= \int f(\lambda)\, d\bigl(E(\lambda)x_1,\dots,E(\lambda)x_n\bigr) \\
&= \Bigl(\int f(\lambda)\,dE(\lambda)\,x_1,\;\dots,\;\int f(\lambda)\,dE(\lambda
)\,x_n\Bigr) \\
&= \bigl(f(M)x_1,\dots,f(M)x_n\bigr) \\
&= \Bigl(\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n} f(M)\Bigr)x.
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence
$$
f(D) \;=\; \bigoplus_{k=1}^{n} f(M). \tag{$\ast$}
$$

---

### Applying $f(\lambda)=\lambda^{t}$

For $t\in(0,\infty)$, the function $f(\lambda)=\lambda^{t}$ is continuous (hence
 Borel) on $[0,\infty)$, and by definition
$$
M^{t} = \int_{[0,\infty)} \lambda^{t}\, dE(\lambda), \qquad D^{t} = \int_{[0,\in
fty)} \lambda^{t}\, d\mathcal{E}(\lambda).
$$
Applying $(\ast)$ with $f(\lambda)=\lambda^{t}$:
$$
D^{t} \;=\; \bigoplus_{k=1}^{n} M^{t} \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} M^{t} & & \\ & \ddot
s & \\ & & M^{t} \end{pmatrix}.
$$

---

### Remark: consistency with the $t=\tfrac12$ case

The given identity for $t=\frac12$ is the special case $f(\lambda)=\lambda^{1/2}
$ of $(\ast)$. One may also recover the full result from this special case witho
ut directly invoking the spectral theorem:

- **Integer powers**: $D^{m}=(\bigoplus M)^{m}=\bigoplus M^{m}$ by direct block
multiplication.
- **Dyadic exponents $t=m/2^{r}$**: By induction on $r$, using $(\bigoplus A)^{1
/2}=\bigoplus A^{1/2}$ (the given fact) at each step, one gets $D^{1/2^{r}}=\big
oplus M^{1/2^{r}}$, and then $D^{m/2^{r}}=(D^{1/2^{r}})^{m}=(\bigoplus M^{1/2^{r
}})^{m}=\bigoplus M^{m/2^{r}}$.
- **General $t$**: The map $t\mapsto A^{t}$ is norm-continuous (for bounded $M$;
 strongly continuous in the unbounded case) on $(0,\infty)$, and the dyadic rati
onals $\{m/2^{r}\}$ are dense in $(0,\infty)$. Passing to the limit extends the
identity to all $t>0$.

Both arguments confirm the same conclusion.

---

**Conclusion.** For every $t\in(0,\infty)$,
$$
\boxed{\;\begin{pmatrix} M^{t} & & \\ & \ddots & \\ & & M^{t} \end{pmatrix} \;=\
; \begin{pmatrix} M & & \\ & \ddots & \\ & & M \end{pmatrix}^{\!t}\;}
$$
The equality holds.
