Proof

Answer: Yes. There exists a finite group acting on $S^3$ by real-analytic (hence
smooth) diffeomorphisms that leaves invariant a knotted circle.

Setup. Identify $S^3 = {(z_1,z_2)\in\mathbb{C}^2 : |z_1|^2+|z_2|^2=1}$. Let
$T={(|z_1|=|z_2|=1/\sqrt{2})}\subset S^3$ be the Clifford torus. For coprime
integers $p,q\ge 2$, the $(p,q)$-torus knot is $$K_{p,q} = \left{\tfrac{1}
{\sqrt{2}}\bigl(e^{ipt},, e^{iqt}\bigr) : t\in[0,2\pi)\right}\subset T\subset
S^3.$$ Taking $(p,q)=(2,3)$ gives the trefoil, which is knotted (its knot group
is non-abelian, or equivalently its Alexander polynomial $t^2-t+1$ is
nontrivial).

The group action. Define $\rho:\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}\to\operatorname{Diff}
(S^3)$ by $$\rho(\bar 1)(z_1,z_2) = \bigl(e^{2\pi i/q},z_1,; z_2\bigr).$$ This
is a real-analytic diffeomorphism of $S^3$ of order $q$, so $\rho$ is a finite
group action by analytic (hence smooth) diffeomorphisms.

Invariance of $K_{p,q}$. Take a point $x=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e^{ipt},e^{iqt})\in
K_{p,q}$. Its image is $$\rho(\bar 1)(x)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl(e^{2\pi i/
q},e^{ipt},, e^{iqt}\bigr).$$ Since $\gcd(p,q)=1$, $p$ is invertible modulo $q$;
choose $k\in\mathbb{Z}$ with $pk\equiv 1\pmod{q}$. Set $t'=t+\tfrac{2\pi k}{q}$.
Then $$e^{iqt'}=e^{iqt},e^{2\pi i k}=e^{iqt},\qquad e^{ipt'}=e^{ipt},e^{2\pi i
pk/q}=e^{ipt},e^{2\pi i/q}.$$ Therefore $$\rho(\bar 1)(x)=\tfrac{1}
{\sqrt{2}}\bigl(e^{ipt'},, e^{iqt'}\bigr)\in K_{p,q}.$$ So $\rho(\bar 1)
(K_{p,q})\subseteq K_{p,q}$, and since $\rho(\bar 1)$ is a diffeomorphism
(injective) and $K_{p,q}$ is compact (a circle), $\rho(\bar 1)
(K_{p,q})=K_{p,q}$. Thus $K_{p,q}$ is invariant under the entire $\mathbb{Z}/
q\mathbb{Z}$-action.

Conclusion. With $(p,q)=(2,3)$, $K_{2,3}$ is the trefoil (a knotted circle), and
the cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ acts on $S^3$ by real-analytic
diffeomorphisms preserving $K_{2,3}$ setwise. Hence such a finite group action
exists. $\blacksquare$
