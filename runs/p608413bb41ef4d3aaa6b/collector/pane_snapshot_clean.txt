## Solution

Define
$$S_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{k}{n}\sin\!\left(\frac{k\pi}{n}\right).$$

**Step 1. Identify $S_n$ as a Riemann sum.** Rewrite
$$S_n=n\cdot\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{k}{n}\sin\!\left(\frac{k\pi}{n}\rig
ht).$$
The inner average
$$I_n:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{k}{n}\sin\!\left(\frac{k\pi}{n}\right)$$
is a Riemann sum for $f(x)=x\sin(\pi x)$ on $[0,1]$ with the partition $\{k/n\}_
{k=0}^{n}$. Since $f$ is continuous, $I_n\to I:=\int_0^1 x\sin(\pi x)\,dx$.

**Step 2. Evaluate the integral.** Integrating by parts with $u=x$, $dv=\sin(\pi
 x)\,dx$:
$$I=\left[-\frac{x\cos(\pi x)}{\pi}\right]_0^1+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^1\cos(\pi x)\
,dx=\frac{1}{\pi}+\frac{1}{\pi^2}\Big[\sin(\pi x)\Big]_0^1=\frac{1}{\pi}.$$

**Step 3. Asymptotics of $S_n$.** Since $f$ is $C^\infty$, the Riemann-sum error
 satisfies $I_n-I=O(1/n)$. Hence
$$S_n=n\,I_n=n\cdot\frac{1}{\pi}+n\cdot O(1/n)=\frac{n}{\pi}+O(1)=\frac{n}{\pi}\
Big(1+O(1/n)\Big).$$
Inverting,
$$\frac{1}{S_n}=\frac{\pi}{n}\Big(1+O(1/n)\Big)^{-1}=\frac{\pi}{n}+O(1/n^2).$$

**Step 4. Evaluate the limit.** Therefore
$$1+\frac{1}{S_n}=1+\frac{\pi}{n}+O(1/n^2),$$
and
$$\left(1+\frac{1}{S_n}\right)^n=\exp\!\left(n\log\!\left(1+\frac{\pi}{n}+O(1/n^
2)\right)\right).$$
Using $\log(1+t)=t+O(t^2)$ for $t\to0$ with $t=\frac{\pi}{n}+O(1/n^2)$:
$$n\log\!\left(1+\frac{\pi}{n}+O(1/n^2)\right)=n\left(\frac{\pi}{n}+O(1/n^2)\rig
ht)=\pi+O(1/n)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\pi.$$
By continuity of $\exp$,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(1+\frac{1}{S_n}\right)^n=e^{\pi}.$$
