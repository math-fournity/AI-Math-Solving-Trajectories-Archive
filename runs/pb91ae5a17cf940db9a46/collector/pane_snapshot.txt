## Proof

We show that such a polynomial **always exists**.

Let
$$D=\sup\{[L:k]\mid L/k\text{ finite extension}\}\in\{2,3,\dots\}\cup\{\infty\}.
$$
The value is $\ge 2$ because $k$ is not algebraically closed (some nontrivial fi
nite extension exists).

### Case 1: $D\ge n$ (including $D=\infty$).

Choose a finite extension $L/k$ with $d=[L:k]\ge n$, and a $k$-basis $e_1,\dots,
e_d$ of $L$. Consider the norm
$$N_{L/k}:L\to k,\qquad N_{L/k}(x)=\det\nolimits_k(m_x),$$
where $m_x$ is the $k$-linear endomorphism "multiplication by $x$". Writing $x=\
sum_{i=1}^d X_i e_i$, the map $N_{L/k}$ is a homogeneous polynomial $F\in k[X_1,
\dots,X_d]$ of degree $d$. Since the norm is multiplicative and $N(1)=1$, we hav
e
$$N_{L/k}(x)=0\iff x=0.$$

Restrict to the $n$-dimensional subspace $W=\operatorname{span}_k\{e_1,\dots,e_n
\}\subseteq L$ by setting
$$f(X_1,\dots,X_n):=F(X_1,\dots,X_n,0,\dots,0)=N_{L/k}(X_1e_1+\cdots+X_ne_n).$$
Then for $(a_1,\dots,a_n)\in k^n$,
$$f(a_1,\dots,a_n)=0\iff a_1e_1+\cdots+a_ne_n=0\iff a_1=\cdots=a_n=0,$$
using the $k$-linear independence of $e_1,\dots,e_n$. Hence $Z(f)=\{0\}$.

### Case 2: $D<n$.

Then $D<\infty$. Pick $L/k$ finite with $[L:k]=D$ maximal. Such $L$ admits no pr
oper finite extension, so every algebraic extension of $L$ is trivial; thus $L=k
^{\mathrm{alg}}$ is algebraically closed and $[k^{\mathrm{alg}}:k]=D<\infty$. By
 the **Artin–Schreier theorem**, $k$ is **real closed** (and in fact $D=2$).

A real closed field is formally real, i.e. $-1\notin\sum_i k^2$. Equivalently (s
tandard equivalence): for every $m$ and every $a_1,\dots,a_m\in k$,
$$a_1^2+\cdots+a_m^2=0\implies a_1=\cdots=a_m=0.$$
(Indeed, if some $a_j\neq 0$ then $-1=\sum_{i\neq j}(a_i/a_j)^2$, contradiction.
)

Therefore
$$f(X_1,\dots,X_n)=X_1^2+\cdots+X_n^2$$
satisfies $Z(f)=\{0\}$ in $k^n$.

### Conclusion

In both cases there exists $f\in k[X_1,\dots,X_n]$ with $Z(f)=\{0\}$. Hence the
answer is **yes** for every field $k$ that is not algebraically closed and every
 $n>1$.
