## Proof

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$. The equation $A^2 = A$ g
ives four polynomial equations:

$$a^2 + bc = a, \qquad b(a+d) = b, \qquad c(a+d) = c, \qquad bc + d^2 = d.$$

Equations (2) and (3) factor as $b(a+d-1)=0$ and $c(a+d-1)=0$.

**Case 1: $a+d \neq 1$.** Then $b=0$ and $c=0$. Equations (1) and (4) become $a^
2=a$ and $d^2=d$, so $a,d \in \{0,1\}$. The constraint $a+d\neq 1$ leaves $(a,d)
=(0,0)$ or $(1,1)$, giving exactly the two matrices $0$ and $I$.

**Case 2: $a+d = 1$.** Setting $d = 1-a$, equation (4) becomes $bc + (1-a)^2 = 1
-a$, i.e. $bc + a^2 - a = 0$, which is identical to equation (1). Thus this case
 is the single condition
$$a + d = 1, \qquad bc = a(1-a).$$

Hence
$$V = \{0\} \;\cup\; \{I\} \;\cup\; W, \qquad W := \{(a,b,c,d) : a+d=1,\; bc = a
(1-a)\}.$$

The points $0$ and $I$ do not satisfy $a+d=1$ (they have traces $0$ and $2$), so
 they are disjoint from $W$.

**$W$ is irreducible.** Eliminating $d=1-a$, we work in $\mathbb{A}^3$ with coor
dinates $(a,b,c)$ and the single equation $bc - a(1-a) = 0$. View $f = bc - a +
a^2$ as a polynomial in $b$ over $F[a,c]$: it is linear in $b$ with leading coef
ficient $c$ and constant term $a^2 - a = a(a-1)$. Since $\gcd(c,\, a(a-1)) = 1$
in $F[a,c]$, the polynomial is irreducible in $F[a,b,c]$. (A linear polynomial i
n $b$ is reducible only if its two coefficients share a nonunit common factor.)
Therefore $W$ is an irreducible hypersurface of dimension $2$.

**The points are irreducible components.** Since $F$ is infinite, the Zariski to
pology on $\mathbb{A}^4$ has the property that a nonzero polynomial does not van
ish on all of $\mathbb{A}^4$; in particular each singleton $\{0\}$ and $\{I\}$ i
s a proper closed subset not contained in the closed set $W$. Being zero-dimensi
onal, each is trivially irreducible, and neither is contained in the other. They
 are therefore maximal irreducible subsets of $V$, i.e. irreducible components.

No component is contained in another: $\{0\}, \{I\}$ are $0$-dimensional while $
W$ is $2$-dimensional, and the two points are distinct.

**Conclusion.** The irreducible components of $V$ are:

1. the singleton $\{0\}$ (the zero matrix),
2. the singleton $\{I\}$ (the identity matrix),
3. the two-dimensional irreducible surface $W = \{A \in M_2(F) : \operatorname{t
r}(A) = 1,\; \det(A) = 0\}$, equivalently $\{A : A^2=A,\; \operatorname{tr}(A)=1
\}$.
