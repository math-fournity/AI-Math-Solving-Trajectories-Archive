## Proof

We want
$$
L = \lim_{\lambda\to\infty} e^{-it\sqrt{\lambda}}\cdot e^{\lambda\left(e^{it/\sq
rt{\lambda}}-1\right)}.
$$

Combine the exponents:
$$
L = \lim_{\lambda\to\infty} \exp\!\Big(\underbrace{-it\sqrt{\lambda}+\lambda\big
(e^{it/\sqrt{\lambda}}-1\big)}_{=:A_\lambda}\Big).
$$

It suffices to find $\lim_{\lambda\to\infty} A_\lambda$, since $\exp$ is continu
ous.

Set $u := \dfrac{t}{\sqrt{\lambda}}$, so $u\to 0$ as $\lambda\to\infty$, and $\l
ambda = \dfrac{t^2}{u^2}$, $\sqrt{\lambda}=\dfrac{t}{u}$ (taking $u$ with the sa
me sign as $t$; the algebra is sign-independent). Then
$$
A_\lambda = -it\cdot\frac{t}{u} + \frac{t^2}{u^2}\big(e^{iu}-1\big)
= \frac{t^2}{u^2}\big(e^{iu}-1-iu\big).
$$

Now use the Taylor expansion of $e^{iu}$ around $u=0$:
$$
e^{iu} = 1 + iu + \frac{(iu)^2}{2} + O(u^3) = 1 + iu - \frac{u^2}{2} + O(u^3).
$$
Hence
$$
e^{iu}-1-iu = -\frac{u^2}{2} + O(u^3).
$$
Therefore
$$
A_\lambda = \frac{t^2}{u^2}\left(-\frac{u^2}{2}+O(u^3)\right) = -\frac{t^2}{2}+O
(u)\;\xrightarrow[u\to 0]{}\;-\frac{t^2}{2}.
$$

Since $u\to 0$ as $\lambda\to\infty$,
$$
\lim_{\lambda\to\infty} A_\lambda = -\frac{t^2}{2}.
$$

By continuity of the exponential,
$$
L = \exp\!\left(-\frac{t^2}{2}\right) = e^{-t^2/2}.
$$
