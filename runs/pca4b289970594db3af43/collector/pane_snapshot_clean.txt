## Solution

**Reformulation in complex numbers.** Identify each point $(x,y)$ with $z = x +
iy \in \mathbb{C}$. The two replacement rules become:

- $(x-y,\, x+y) \;\longleftrightarrow\; (x+iy)(1+i) = z(1+i)$
- $(x+y,\, y-x) \;\longleftrightarrow\; (x+iy)(1-i) = z(1-i)$

(Verified by direct expansion: $z(1+i)=x+iy+ix+i^{2}y=(x-y)+i(x+y)$; $z(1-i)=x+i
y-ix-i^{2}y=(x+y)+i(y-x)$.)

So **each step replaces every point $z$ by $\{z(1+i),\; z(1-i)\}$**.

**Behavior of the origin.** $z=0$ maps to $\{0,0\}=\{0\}$; it is a fixed point p
resent at every stage.

**Behavior of $z=2$.** After $n$ steps, every descendant of $2$ has the form

$$2\,(1+i)^{a}(1-i)^{b}, \qquad a+b=n,\; a,b\ge 0.$$

Writing $1+i=\sqrt{2}\,e^{i\pi/4}$ and $1-i=\sqrt{2}\,e^{-i\pi/4}$:

$$2\,(1+i)^{a}(1-i)^{b} = 2\,(\sqrt{2})^{n}\, e^{\,i\pi(a-b)/4}.$$

Since $a+b=n$, we have $a-b = n-2b$ with $b=0,1,\dots,n$. **All non-zero points
share the same modulus** $2(\sqrt{2})^{n}$, so distinctness depends only on the
**angle** $\pi(n-2b)/4$, i.e. on $(n-2b)\bmod 8$.

**Counting distinct angles.** Two indices $b_1,b_2$ yield the same point iff

$$n-2b_1 \equiv n-2b_2 \pmod{8} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; 2(b_1-b_2)\equiv 0 \pmod
{8} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; b_1\equiv b_2 \pmod{4}.$$

So the number of distinct non-zero points equals the number of distinct residues
 of $b \bmod 4$ for $b\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}$:

| $n$ | residues of $b\bmod 4$ hit | distinct non-zero points |
|-----|---------------------------|--------------------------|
| 0   | $\{0\}$                   | 1 |
| 1   | $\{0,1\}$                 | 2 |
| 2   | $\{0,1,2\}$               | 3 |
| $\ge 3$ | $\{0,1,2,3\}$         | 4 |

**Verification on small cases.**

- $n=0$: $\{0,2\}$ → 2 points. Formula: $1+1=2$. ✓
- $n=1$: $\{0,\,2(1+i),\,2(1-i)\}=\{0,(2,2),(2,-2)\}$ → 3 points. Formula: $2+1=
3$. ✓
- $n=2$: descendants of $2$ are $4i,\,4,\,-4i$ → $\{0,(0,4),(4,0),(0,-4)\}$ → 4
points. Formula: $3+1=4$. ✓
- $n=3$: descendants are $-4+4i,\,4+4i,\,4-4i,\,-4-4i$ → 4 non-zero + origin = 5
. Formula: $4+1=5$. ✓
- $n=4$: descendants are $-8,\,8i,\,8,\,-8i$ → 4 non-zero + origin = 5. Formula:
 $4+1=5$. ✓

For all $n\ge 3$ the count stabilizes at **4 non-zero points + 1 origin = 5**.

**Applying to $n=14$.** Since $14\ge 3$, the final set contains $4$ non-zero anc
hor points plus the origin.

$$\boxed{5}$$
