## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, there exist vertex-transitive graphs in which the multiplicity o
f the largest eigenvalue $\mu_1$ is even.

We construct an explicit family of examples.

### Background facts

**Fact 1.** Every vertex-transitive graph is regular. (The automorphism group ac
ts transitively on vertices, so every vertex has the same degree.)

**Fact 2.** A connected regular graph has its largest eigenvalue equal to the co
mmon degree $k$, and by the Perron–Frobenius theorem this largest eigenvalue has
 **multiplicity exactly 1** (the all-ones vector spans the eigenspace, and conne
ctivity forces simplicity).

**Fact 3.** A disconnected vertex-transitive graph is a disjoint union of isomor
phic connected vertex-transitive graphs. Indeed, the automorphism group acts tra
nsitively on all vertices, so every connected component is isomorphic to every o
ther, and each component is itself vertex-transitive.

**Fact 4.** The spectrum of a disjoint union $mG$ (i.e., $m$ copies of $G$) is t
he spectrum of $G$ with every eigenvalue's multiplicity multiplied by $m$.

### Construction

Let $G$ be any connected vertex-transitive graph (for concreteness, take $G = K_
3$, the complete graph on 3 vertices, which is clearly vertex-transitive). Let $
m = 2$ and form the disjoint union $H = 2G = G \sqcup G$.

**$H$ is vertex-transitive.** The automorphism group of $H$ is the wreath produc
t $\operatorname{Aut}(G) \wr S_2 = (\operatorname{Aut}(G) \times \operatorname{A
ut}(G)) \rtimes S_2$, where $S_2$ swaps the two copies and each $\operatorname{A
ut}(G)$ factor acts within its copy. Given any two vertices $u, v$ of $H$:
- If they lie in the same copy, transitivity of $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ sends $u
$ to $v$.
- If they lie in different copies, first apply the $S_2$ swap to move $u$'s copy
 to $v$'s copy, then use transitivity of $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ within that cop
y.

Hence $\operatorname{Aut}(H)$ acts transitively on $V(H)$, so $H$ is vertex-tran
sitive.

**The largest eigenvalue of $H$ has even multiplicity.** Since $G$ is connected
and regular (Fact 1), its largest eigenvalue $\mu_1$ has multiplicity $1$ (Fact
2). By Fact 4, the largest eigenvalue of $H = 2G$ is the same value $\mu_1$, now
 with multiplicity $2 \times 1 = 2$, which is **even**.

### Concrete instance

Take $G = K_3$. Its eigenvalues are $2$ (multiplicity $1$) and $-1$ (multiplicit
y $2$). The graph $H = 2K_3$ (two disjoint triangles) is vertex-transitive with
eigenvalues $2$ (multiplicity $2$) and $-1$ (multiplicity $4$). The largest eige
nvalue is $\mu_1 = 2$ with multiplicity $2$, which is even.

### Generalization

More generally, the disjoint union $mG$ of $m$ copies of any connected vertex-tr
ansitive graph $G$ is vertex-transitive, and the multiplicity of its largest eig
envalue is $m$. Thus **every** even positive integer $m = 2, 4, 6, \ldots$ is re
alized as the multiplicity of $\mu_1$ for some vertex-transitive graph. (For odd
 $m$, the multiplicity is odd; in particular $m=1$ recovers the connected case.)

### Conclusion

There do exist vertex-transitive graphs where the multiplicity of the largest ei
genvalue $\mu_1$ is even. The disjoint union of two (or any even number of) copi
es of a connected vertex-transitive graph provides an infinite family of example
s, with $2K_3$ being the smallest concrete instance.
