## Proof

**Yes**, the geodesic $\gamma : [0,1] \to S$ can be lifted to a geodesic $\tilde
\gamma : [0,1] \to \mathbb{H}$. The justification combines the path-lifting prop
erty of covering spaces with the fact that the quotient map is a local isometry.

---

### Setup

Let $\pi : \mathbb{H} \to S = \mathbb{H}/\Gamma$ be the canonical projection. A
Fuchsian group $\Gamma$ acts on $\mathbb{H}$ properly discontinuously by isometr
ies. Assuming $\Gamma$ acts freely (torsion-free, the standard setting so that $
S$ is a hyperbolic surface rather than an orbifold), $\pi$ is a **covering map**
 and a **local isometry**: every point $x \in \mathbb{H}$ has a neighborhood $U$
 such that $\pi|_U : U \to \pi(U)$ is an isometry onto an open set of $S$.

### Step 1 — Path lifting

Pick any point $\tilde p \in \mathbb{H}$ with $\pi(\tilde p) = \gamma(0)$. By th
e **path-lifting lemma** for covering spaces, there exists a unique continuous l
ift
$$\tilde\gamma : [0,1] \to \mathbb{H}, \qquad \tilde\gamma(0) = \tilde p, \qquad
 \pi \circ \tilde\gamma = \gamma.$$

### Step 2 — The lift is locally a geodesic

Cover $[0,1]$ by subintervals $I_j = (t_j, t_{j+1})$ small enough that $\gamma(I
_j)$ lies inside an evenly covered open set $V_j \subset S$ on which $\pi$ admit
s an isometric inverse section $\sigma_j : V_j \to \mathbb{H}$ with $\sigma_j(\g
amma(t_j)) = \tilde\gamma(t_j)$.

On $I_j$ the lift agrees with this section:
$$\tilde\gamma|_{I_j} = \sigma_j \circ \gamma|_{I_j}.$$

Since $\sigma_j$ is an **isometry**, it preserves the Levi-Civita connection and
 hence the geodesic equation. The curve $\gamma|_{I_j}$ is a geodesic in $S$ (be
ing a restriction of a geodesic), so $\tilde\gamma|_{I_j} = \sigma_j \circ \gamm
a|_{I_j}$ is a geodesic in $\mathbb{H}$.

### Step 3 — Locally geodesic $\Rightarrow$ geodesic

The geodesic equation $\nabla_{\dot{\tilde\gamma}}\dot{\tilde\gamma} = 0$ is a *
*local** condition: it holds at every $t \in [0,1]$ if and only if it holds on a
n open interval around every $t$. Since $\tilde\gamma$ is a geodesic on each $I_
j$, and the $I_j$ cover $[0,1]$, the equation holds at every point. Therefore $\
tilde\gamma$ is a geodesic on all of $[0,1]$.

(Equivalently, in the hyperbolic plane a curve is a geodesic iff it is locally a
 segment of a hyperbolic line; being locally a geodesic forces $\tilde\gamma$ to
 lie on a single complete geodesic, hence to be a geodesic globally.)

### Conclusion

For every choice of lift of the initial point $\tilde p \in \pi^{-1}(\gamma(0))$
, there is a unique lift $\tilde\gamma : [0,1] \to \mathbb{H}$ of $\gamma$, and
this lift is a geodesic of $\mathbb{H}$. The two ingredients are:

1. **Covering-space path lifting** guarantees existence and uniqueness of the co
ntinuous lift.
2. **The quotient map is a local isometry**, so it pulls back the geodesic equat
ion, making the lift locally — and hence globally — a geodesic.
