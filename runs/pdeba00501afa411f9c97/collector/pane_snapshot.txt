## Solution

**Answer:** $\boxed{n^{n/2}}$

**Proof.**

Let $P_1,\dots,P_n$ be points in the closed unit disc $\overline{D}=\{z:|z|\le 1
\}$, and let
$$\Phi(P_1,\dots,P_n)=\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}|P_iP_j|.$$
We prove $\max \Phi = n^{n/2}$, attained by the vertices of a regular $n$-gon in
scribed in the unit circle.

---

**Step 1. At the maximum, every point lies on the boundary $|z|=1$.**

The function $\Phi$ is continuous on the compact set $\overline{D}^n$, so it att
ains its maximum. The maximum is positive (e.g. a regular $n$-gon gives a positi
ve value), so at a maximizer all points are distinct.

Fix all points except $P_i$ and consider
$$g(P)=\prod_{j\ne i}|P-P_j|,\qquad \log g(P)=\sum_{j\ne i}\log|P-P_j|.$$
Each $\log|P-P_j|$ is harmonic in $\mathbb C\setminus\{P_j\}$, so $\log g$ is ha
rmonic in the open disc minus the (finitely many) interior points $P_j$. At thos
e points $g=0$, the global minimum. Hence any interior maximizer of $g$ not equa
l to some $P_j$ would be an interior maximum of a harmonic function, forcing it
to be constant — impossible since $g\to 0$ near each $P_j$. Therefore the maximu
m of $g$ over $\overline D$ is attained on $\partial D$.

Applying this to each coordinate at a global maximizer: if some $P_i$ were inter
ior, replacing it by a boundary maximizer of $g$ would strictly increase $\Phi$,
 a contradiction. Thus every $P_i$ lies on $|z|=1$.

---

**Step 2. Among points on the unit circle, $\Phi\le n^{n/2}$, with equality for
the regular $n$-gon.**

Write $z_k=e^{i\theta_k}\in\mathbb C$, $|z_k|=1$. Form the Vandermonde matrix $V
=(z_j^{\,i})$ with $i=0,\dots,n-1$ (rows) and $j=1,\dots,n$ (columns). Then
$$\det V=\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_j-z_i),\qquad |\det V|=\prod_{i<j}|z_j-z_i|=\Ph
i.$$
The squared norm of row $i$ is
$$\sum_{j=1}^{n}|z_j|^{2i}=n\qquad(\text{since }|z_j|=1).$$
By **Hadamard's determinant inequality**,
$$\Phi=|\det V|\le\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\|{\rm row\ }i\|=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\sqrt n=n^{
n/2}.$$

**Equality condition.** Equality in Hadamard holds iff the rows are mutually ort
hogonal, i.e.
$$\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_j^{\,i}\,\overline{z_j^{\,k}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_j^{\,i-k}=0\quad
\text{for all }0\le i\ne k\le n-1,$$
equivalently $\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_j^{\,m}=0$ for $m=1,\dots,n-1$. This says $z_1,\do
ts,z_n$ are the roots of $z^n-c=0$ for some $|c|=1$, i.e. the vertices of a regu
lar $n$-gon. For such a configuration, $\Phi=n^{n/2}$ (also confirmed directly:
$\prod_{j\ne k}|z_k-z_j|=n$ for each $k$, so $\Phi^2=n^n$).

---

**Conclusion.** Steps 1 and 2 together give
$$\max_{P_1,\dots,P_n\in\overline D}\prod_{i<j}|P_iP_j|=n^{n/2},$$
attained uniquely (up to rotation) by the regular $n$-gon inscribed in the unit
circle.

$$\boxed{n^{n/2}}$$
