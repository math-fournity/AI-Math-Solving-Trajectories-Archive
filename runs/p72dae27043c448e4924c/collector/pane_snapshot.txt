Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $\|Tx\| = \|Sx\|$ for all $
x \in X$ (which is what $\sup_{x \in X} |\|Tx\| - \|Sx\|| = 0$ means) guarantees
 the existence of a surjective isometry $P$ on $Y$ with $PT = S$.

**Answer: No.** The condition does not imply $[T] = [S]$ in general. Here is a c
ounterexample and complete proof.

---

## Counterexample

Let $X = (\mathbb{R}^2, \|\cdot\|_\infty)$ and $Y = (\mathbb{R}^3, \|\cdot\|_\in
fty)$. Define:
$$T(x_1, x_2) = (x_1,\, x_2,\, 0), \qquad S(x_1, x_2) = (x_1,\, x_2,\, x_2).$$

Both $T, S \in L(X, Y)$ are linear and injective.

**Step 1: $\|Tx\|_\infty = \|Sx\|_\infty$ for all $x \in X$.**

$$\|Tx\|_\infty = \max(|x_1|, |x_2|, 0) = \max(|x_1|, |x_2|),$$
$$\|Sx\|_\infty = \max(|x_1|, |x_2|, |x_2|) = \max(|x_1|, |x_2|).$$

Hence $\sup_{x \in X} |\|Tx\| - \|Sx\|| = 0$.

**Step 2: Any isometry $P$ on $Y$ satisfying $PT = S$ must be linear.**

If $PT = S$, then $P(T(0)) = S(0) = 0$, so $P(0) = 0$. Since $Y$ is a real norme
d space and $P: Y \to Y$ is a surjective isometry (any isometric embedding $Y \t
o Y$ is surjective in finite dimensions) with $P(0) = 0$, the Mazur–Ulam theorem
 implies $P$ is **linear**.

**Step 3: Surjective linear isometries of $(\mathbb{R}^n, \|\cdot\|_\infty)$ are
 exactly signed permutation matrices.**

Let $P$ be a surjective linear isometry of $\ell_\infty^n$. Denote $u_i = Pe_i$
(the $i$-th column). Then $\|u_i\|_\infty = 1$ for each $i$, and for $i \neq j$:
$$\|u_i + u_j\|_\infty = \|e_i + e_j\|_\infty = 1, \qquad \|u_i - u_j\|_\infty =
 \|e_i - e_j\|_\infty = 1.$$

For each coordinate $k$, $|u_{i,k} + u_{j,k}| \leq 1$ and $|u_{i,k} - u_{j,k}| \
leq 1$, which gives $|u_{i,k}|^2 + |u_{j,k}|^2 \leq 1$ (by the parallelogram ide
ntity $|a+b|^2 + |a-b|^2 = 2|a|^2 + 2|b|^2$ and $\max(|a+b|^2, |a-b|^2) \leq 1$)
.

Since $\|u_i\|_\infty = 1$, there exists $k_i$ with $|u_{i,k_i}| = 1$, which for
ces $u_{j,k_i} = 0$ for all $j \neq i$.

**Claim:** Each $u_i$ has exactly one coordinate with absolute value $1$. Suppos
e $u_i$ has absolute value $1$ at coordinates $k_1, \ldots, k_m$ with $m \geq 2$
. Then every $u_j$ ($j \neq i$) vanishes at all of $k_1, \ldots, k_m$, so the $n
-1$ vectors $\{u_j : j \neq i\}$ live in an $(n-m)$-dimensional coordinate subsp
ace. By induction on dimension, fitting $n-1$ isometric columns requires $n - m
\geq n - 1$, i.e., $m \leq 1$. Contradiction.

Since each $u_i$ has exactly one coordinate with $|u_{i,k}| = 1$ and these coord
inates must be distinct (if $u_i$ and $u_j$ both have $|u_{\cdot, k}| = 1$, then
 $u_{j,k} = 0$ from the constraint, contradiction), $P$ is a signed permutation
matrix. $\square$

**Step 4: No signed permutation matrix $P$ satisfies $PT = S$.**

If $P$ is a signed permutation matrix, then $P(x_1, x_2, 0)$ has **at most two n
onzero coordinates** (it permutes and signs the entries $(x_1, x_2, 0)$, one of
which is zero).

However, $S(1, 1) = (1, 1, 1)$ has **three nonzero coordinates**. Therefore $P(T
(1,1)) \neq S(1,1)$ for every signed permutation matrix $P$.

Since by Step 2 any isometry $P$ with $PT = S$ must be linear, and by Step 3 eve
ry surjective linear isometry of $Y$ is a signed permutation matrix, **no isomet
ry $P$ on $Y$ satisfies $PT = S$**.

---

## Conclusion

We have exhibited finite-dimensional real normed spaces $X, Y$ and operators $T,
 S \in L(X, Y)$ such that:
- $\sup_{x \in X} |\|Tx\| - \|Sx\|| = 0$ (i.e., $\|Tx\| = \|Sx\|$ for all $x$),
yet
- $[T] \neq [S]$ (no surjective isometry $P$ on $Y$ satisfies $PT = S$).

The condition $\sup_{x \in X} |\|Tx\| - \|Sx\|| = 0$ does **not** imply $[T] = [
S]$.

The essential obstruction is that $\|Tx\| = \|Sx\|$ for all $x$ only guarantees
a linear isometry between the subspaces $T(X)$ and $S(X)$ of $Y$, and this isome
try need not extend to a surjective isometry of the entire space $Y$ — extension
 depends on the specific geometry of the norm on $Y$.
