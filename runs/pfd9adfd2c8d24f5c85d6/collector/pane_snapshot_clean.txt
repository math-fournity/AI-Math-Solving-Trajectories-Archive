Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether elements of the matrix idea
l $M_n(I)$ are quasi-regular when $I$ is an ideal of $R$ filled with quasi-regul
ar elements.

**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

**Convention.** An element $a$ of a unital ring is *quasi-regular* if $1 - a$ is
 a unit (equivalently, $a$ is both left and right quasi-regular under the circle
 operation $x \circ y = x + y - xy$). The proof below works identically under th
e convention $x \circ y = x + y + xy$ (replace $I_n - C$ by $I_n + C$ throughout
).

**Setup.** Let $I$ be a two-sided ideal of $R$ such that every element of $I$ is
 quasi-regular. We prove by induction on $n$ that for every $C = (c_{ij}) \in M_
n(I)$, the matrix $I_n - C$ is invertible in $M_n(R)$, which is equivalent to $C
$ being quasi-regular.

**Base case ($n = 1$).** Here $C = (c_{11})$ with $c_{11} \in I$. Since $I$ is q
uasi-regular, $1 - c_{11}$ is a unit in $R$, so $I_1 - C = (1 - c_{11})$ is inve
rtible. ✓

**Inductive step.** Assume the claim holds for $n - 1$. Let $C = (c_{ij}) \in M_
n(I)$ and write
$$I_n - C = \begin{pmatrix} 1 - c_{11} & -v \\ -w & I_{n-1} - D \end{pmatrix},$$
where $v = (c_{12}, \dots, c_{1n})$, $w = (c_{21}, \dots, c_{n1})^\top$, and $D
= (c_{ij})_{2 \le i,j \le n} \in M_{n-1}(I)$.

By the inductive hypothesis, $I_{n-1} - D$ is invertible. Observe that
$$(I_{n-1} - D)^{-1} = I_{n-1} + D(I_{n-1}-D)^{-1},$$
and since $D \in M_{n-1}(I)$ and $I$ is a two-sided ideal, $D(I_{n-1}-D)^{-1} \i
n M_{n-1}(I)$. Thus $(I_{n-1}-D)^{-1} = I_{n-1} + E$ for some $E \in M_{n-1}(I)$
.

The **Schur complement** of the block $I_{n-1} - D$ is
$$S \;=\; (1 - c_{11}) - v\,(I_{n-1}-D)^{-1}\,w.$$

Since $v$ has entries in $I$ and $I$ is a two-sided ideal, the scalar $v\,(I_{n-
1}-D)^{-1}\,w$ lies in $I$. Denoting it by $j \in I$, we get
$$S = 1 - (c_{11} + j), \qquad c_{11} + j \in I.$$

Since $I$ is quasi-regular, $S = 1 - (c_{11}+j)$ is a unit in $R$.

**Block inversion (valid over non-commutative rings).** For a block matrix $M =
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ with $D$ inver
tible, $M$ is invertible if and only if the Schur complement $A - BD^{-1}C$ is i
nvertible; the inverse is
$$M^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} S^{-1} & -S^{-1}BD^{-1} \\ -D^{-1}CS^{-1} & D^{-1} +
D^{-1}CS^{-1}BD^{-1} \end{pmatrix}, \quad S = A - BD^{-1}C,$$
as verified by direct multiplication (no commutativity needed).

Applying this with $A = 1-c_{11}$, $B = -v$, $C = -w$, $D = I_{n-1}-D$: both $D$
 and the Schur complement $S$ are invertible, so $I_n - C$ is invertible. ✓

This closes the induction.

**Conclusion.** Every $C \in M_n(I)$ satisfies: $I_n - C$ is invertible in $M_n(
R)$, hence $C$ is quasi-regular. Therefore all elements of the matrix ideal $M_n
(I)$ are quasi-regular. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** This also follows from the standard identity $J(M_n(R)) = M_n(J(R))$
 for the Jacobson radical: since $I \subseteq J(R)$ (as $J(R)$ is the largest qu
asi-regular ideal), we get $M_n(I) \subseteq M_n(J(R)) = J(M_n(R))$, and the Jac
obson radical is quasi-regular. The inductive proof above is self-contained and
avoids invoking this theorem.
