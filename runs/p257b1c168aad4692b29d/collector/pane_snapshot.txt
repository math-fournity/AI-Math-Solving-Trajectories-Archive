# Solution

## Setting up the 1-form

Let
$$\omega = \underbrace{\frac{-y}{x^2+y^2}\,dx + \frac{x}{x^2+y^2}\,dy}_{\omega_1
} + \underbrace{\ln(z^4+z^2+1)\,dz}_{\omega_2}$$

with $P = \frac{-y}{x^2+y^2}$, $Q = \frac{x}{x^2+y^2}$, $R = \ln(z^4+z^2+1)$.

## The curve is closed

The curve $\gamma$ is the intersection of the cone $x^2+y^2=z^2$ with the plane
$x+y+10z=10$. The plane does not pass through the apex (origin) of the cone, and
 the plane's angle with the $z$-axis satisfies $\cos\alpha = \frac{10}{\sqrt{102
}} \approx 0.99$, so $\alpha \approx 8° < 45°$ (the cone's half-angle). Hence th
e intersection is an **ellipse** — a closed curve.

## The form $\omega$ is closed

Computing partial derivatives:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = \frac{-(x^2+y^2)+2y^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2} = \frac{y^
2-x^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2}, \qquad \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} = \frac{(x^2+y^2)-2x^2
}{(x^2+y^2)^2} = \frac{y^2-x^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2}$$

So $\frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}$. Also $\frac{
\partial P}{\partial z} = \frac{\partial R}{\partial x} = 0$ and $\frac{\partial
 Q}{\partial z} = \frac{\partial R}{\partial y} = 0$. Thus $\omega$ is **closed*
* on $\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus \{z\text{-axis}\}$.

## The $\omega_2$ part vanishes

Since $R$ depends only on $z$, the form $\omega_2 = \ln(z^4+z^2+1)\,dz = dF(z)$
where $F(z) = \int_0^z \ln(t^4+t^2+1)\,dt$ is a single-valued smooth function (t
he polynomial $t^4+t^2+1$ has discriminant $<0$ in $t^2$ and is always positive)
. Over the **closed** curve $\gamma$:

$$\oint_\gamma \omega_2 = \oint_\gamma dF = 0.$$

## The $\omega_1$ part: the angle form

The form $\omega_1 = \frac{-y\,dx + x\,dy}{x^2+y^2} = d\theta$ is the standard *
*angle form** (exterior derivative of the polar angle $\theta$). For any closed
curve not touching the $z$-axis:

$$\oint_\gamma \omega_1 = 2\pi \cdot n$$

where $n$ is the **winding number** of $\gamma$ around the $z$-axis.

**The curve never touches the $z$-axis:** At $z=0$ the cone forces $x=y=0$, but
the plane gives $0=10$, a contradiction. So $x^2+y^2 = z^2 > 0$ on $\gamma$.

**The curve winds around the $z$-axis once:** Projecting onto the $xy$-plane via
 $z = \frac{10-x-y}{10}$ and $x^2+y^2=z^2$:

$$100(x^2+y^2) = (10-x-y)^2 \implies 99x^2 + 99y^2 - 2xy + 20x + 20y - 100 = 0.$
$

The quadratic part $99x^2 - 2xy + 99y^2$ is positive definite (discriminant $4 -
 4\cdot 99\cdot 99 < 0$), so this is an ellipse. Evaluating at the origin: $-100
 < 0$, which is inside the ellipse (interior = where the expression is negative)
. Hence the origin lies inside the projected ellipse, confirming the curve winds
 around the $z$-axis exactly once.

**Orientation:** The curve is oriented **clockwise when viewed from above** (loo
king down the $z$-axis). By convention, counterclockwise viewed from above gives
 winding number $+1$; clockwise gives winding number $-1$.

Therefore:
$$\oint_\gamma \omega_1 = 2\pi \cdot (-1) = -2\pi.$$

## Final result

$$\int_\gamma \omega = \oint_\gamma \omega_1 + \oint_\gamma \omega_2 = -2\pi + 0
 = \boxed{-2\pi}$$
