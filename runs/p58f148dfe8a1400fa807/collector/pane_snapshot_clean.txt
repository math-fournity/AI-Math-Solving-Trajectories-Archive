## Transformation Law for Contravariant Basis Vectors

### Setup and Definitions

Consider a point $P$ in an $n$-dimensional space described by two curvilinear co
ordinate systems $u^i$ (with $i = 1, \dots, n$) and $u'^k$ (with $k = 1, \dots,
n$). The two systems are related by smooth, invertible functions:
$$u'^k = u'^k(u^1, u^2, \dots, u^n), \qquad u^i = u^i(u'^1, u'^2, \dots, u'^n).$
$

**Covariant basis vectors** (tangent to coordinate curves):
$$e_i = \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u^i}, \qquad e'_j = \frac{\partial \
mathbf{r}}{\partial u'^j}.$$

**Contravariant basis vectors** (reciprocal/gradients of coordinate functions):
$$e^i = \nabla u^i, \qquad e'^k = \nabla u'^k.$$

These two bases satisfy the duality (biorthogonality) relation:
$$e^i \cdot e_j = \delta^i_j, \qquad e'^k \cdot e'_l = \delta^k_l.$$

### Derivation

We derive the transformation law for the contravariant basis vectors $e'^k$ in t
erms of $e^i$.

**Step 1: Express $e'^k$ as a gradient.**

By definition, the contravariant basis vector in the primed system is the gradie
nt of the coordinate function $u'^k$:
$$e'^k = \nabla u'^k.$$

**Step 2: Apply the chain rule.**

Since $u'^k$ is a function of the unprimed coordinates $u^1, u^2, \dots, u^n$, i
.e., $u'^k = u'^k(u^1, \dots, u^n)$, the gradient of a composite function decomp
oses via the chain rule:
$$\nabla u'^k = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{\partial u'^k}{\partial u^i}\, \nabla u^i.$
$

**Step 3: Substitute the definition of $e^i$.**

Recognizing that $\nabla u^i = e^i$, we obtain:
$$\boxed{e'^k = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{\partial u'^k}{\partial u^i}\, e^i.}$$

This is the **transformation law for contravariant basis vectors**: the primed c
ontravariant basis $e'^k$ is obtained from the unprimed contravariant basis $e^i
$ by contraction with the Jacobian matrix $\dfrac{\partial u'^k}{\partial u^i}$.

### Verification via Duality

To confirm consistency, we check that the duality relation $e'^k \cdot e'_l = \d
elta^k_l$ is preserved. We also need the transformation law for the covariant ba
sis vectors, obtained from the chain rule on $e'_j = \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{
\partial u'^j}$:
$$e'_j = \sum_{l=1}^{n} \frac{\partial u^l}{\partial u'^j}\, e_l.$$

Now compute:
$$e'^k \cdot e'_j = \left(\sum_i \frac{\partial u'^k}{\partial u^i}\, e^i\right)
 \cdot \left(\sum_l \frac{\partial u^l}{\partial u'^j}\, e_l\right) = \sum_{i,l}
 \frac{\partial u'^k}{\partial u^i}\, \frac{\partial u^l}{\partial u'^j}\, \unde
rbrace{(e^i \cdot e_l)}_{\delta^i_l}.$$

Summing over $l$ (using $\delta^i_l$):
$$e'^k \cdot e'_j = \sum_i \frac{\partial u'^k}{\partial u^i}\, \frac{\partial u
^i}{\partial u'^j} = \frac{\partial u'^k}{\partial u'^j} = \delta^k_j.$$

The last equality holds because $u'^k$ and $u'^j$ are independent coordinates. T
hus the duality relation is preserved, confirming the transformation law is corr
ect.

### Summary of the Dual Structure

| Quantity | Transformation Law | Matrix |
|---|---|---|
| Covariant basis $e_i \to e'_j$ | $e'_j = \dfrac{\partial u^i}{\partial u'^j}\,
 e_i$ | Inverse Jacobian |
| Contravariant basis $e^i \to e'^k$ | $e'^k = \dfrac{\partial u'^k}{\partial u^
i}\, e^i$ | Jacobian |

The contravariant basis vectors transform with the **Jacobian** $\frac{\partial
u'^k}{\partial u^i}$ (the same matrix that governs the transformation of covaria
nt *components*), while the covariant basis vectors transform with the **inverse
 Jacobian** $\frac{\partial u^i}{\partial u'^j}$ (the same matrix that governs t
he transformation of contravariant *components*). This is the fundamental co/con
tra duality: basis vectors and components of the opposite variance transform ide
ntically, ensuring that geometric objects (vectors, tensors) remain invariant un
der coordinate change.
