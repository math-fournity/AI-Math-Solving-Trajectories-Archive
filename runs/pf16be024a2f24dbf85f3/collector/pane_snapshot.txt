Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest set of weights (positive ma
sses) that can be partitioned into 4, 5, and 6 equal-mass piles.

## Setup

Let T be the total mass. For equal division into 4, 5, and 6 piles, T must be di
visible by lcm(4,5,6) = 60. Write T = 60k. The pile masses are:
- 4 piles: 15k each
- 5 piles: 12k each
- 6 piles: 10k each

Each weight goes entirely into one pile (weights cannot be split). We need at le
ast 6 weights (one per pile for 6-way division). We seek the minimum n.

## Lower Bound: n ≤ 9 is impossible

For 6-way division with n weights, any single-weight pile has mass 10k. Any weig
ht in a multi-weight pile has mass < 10k. So **every weight has mass ≤ 10k**.

For 5-way division, a single-weight pile would need mass 12k. But 12k > 10k ≥ ev
ery weight, so **no weight can form a single-weight pile in the 5-way division**
.

Now check: can we partition n ≤ 9 weights into 5 groups with no singletons? The
minimum group sizes for 5 groups from n weights, with each group having ≥ 2 weig
hts, requires n ≥ 10. Since n ≤ 9 < 10, **every 5-way partition of at most 9 wei
ghts must contain at least one singleton** — contradiction. So n ≥ 10.

## n = 10 is impossible

For 10 weights into 5 groups with no singletons, the only partition is 2+2+2+2+2
 (five pairs, each summing to 12k).

For 10 weights into 6 groups, the possible distributions are 5+1+1+1+1+1, 4+2+1+
1+1+1, 3+3+1+1+1+1, 3+2+2+1+1+1, or 2+2+2+2+1+1. In each case, the singletons ha
ve mass 10k and the remaining weights form groups summing to 10k.

**Key parity argument:** In the 5-way pairing (pairs sum to 12k), each singleton
 of mass 10k from the 6-way partition must pair with a weight of mass 2k. Tracin
g the chain of complementary pairs through both matchings:

Starting from 10k: → (5-pile partner) 2k → (6-pile partner) 8k → (5-pile partner
) 4k → (6-pile partner) 6k → (5-pile partner) 6k → (6-pile partner) 4k → (5-pile
 partner) 8k → (6-pile partner) 2k → (5-pile partner) 10k

This forces the multiset {10k, 10k, 2k, 2k, 8k, 8k, 4k, 4k, 6k, 6k} — **all weig
hts are even multiples of k**. The same all-even result holds for every other 6-
way partition type (the singletons are 10k = even, and the 2k partners are even,
 propagating evenness through all complementary pairs).

Since all weights are even multiples of k, every pile sum is an even multiple of
 k. But the 4-way target is 15k, which is an **odd** multiple of k. This is impo
ssible. So n ≥ 11.

## n = 11 works (explicit construction)

Take T = 60 (k=1) with 11 weights:
**{10, 1, 9, 1, 9, 3, 7, 3, 7, 5, 5}** — sum = 60 ✓

**6 piles of 10:**

**5 piles of 12:**

**4 piles of 15:**
- {10, 5}, {9, 5, 1}, {9, 3, 3}, {7, 7, 1} — each sums to 15 ✓

All 11 weights are used in each partition, and every pile has the correct mass.

## Conclusion

The minimum number of weights is **11**.
