## Proof

**Setup.** Let $G = (V, E)$ be a directed graph. We wish to characterize when th
e vertex set $V$ can be partitioned into vertex-disjoint directed circuits (each
 vertex lies in exactly one circuit, and each circuit passes through each of its
 vertices exactly once).

Following the hint, construct the bipartite graph $G' = (X, Y, E')$ where:
- $X = \{x_v : v \in V\}$ is a copy of $V$,
- $Y = \{y_v : v \in V\}$ is another copy of $V$,
- For each directed edge $(v_1, v_2) \in E$, we add the edge $(x_{v_1}, y_{v_2})
$ to $E'$.

Note $|X| = |Y| = |V|$.

**Restatement in terms of $G'$.** We claim:

> *$G$ has a partition of $V$ into vertex-disjoint directed circuits if and only
 if $G'$ has a perfect matching.*

**Proof of the claim.**

$(\Leftarrow)$ Suppose $G'$ has a perfect matching $M \subseteq E'$. Since $|X|
= |Y|$ and $M$ matches every vertex of $X \cup Y$, $M$ is a bijection $\sigma: V
 \to V$ where $x_v$ is matched to $y_{\sigma(v)}$, and the map $v \mapsto \sigma
(v)$ is a bijection. By construction of $E'$, the edge $(x_v, y_{\sigma(v)}) \in
 M$ corresponds to the directed edge $(v, \sigma(v)) \in E$.

Since $\sigma$ is a bijection on $V$:
- Every $v \in V$ has exactly one **outgoing** edge $(v, \sigma(v))$ (from the m
atching of $x_v$).
- Every $v \in V$ has exactly one **incoming** edge $(\sigma^{-1}(v), v)$ (from
the matching of $y_v$).

Thus the selected edges form a subgraph of $G$ in which every vertex has in-degr
ee $1$ and out-degree $1$. Such a directed graph is exactly a disjoint union of
directed cycles, and since every vertex is incident to exactly one outgoing and
one incoming edge, every vertex is covered. Hence $V$ is partitioned into vertex
-disjoint directed circuits.

$(\Rightarrow)$ Conversely, suppose $V$ is partitioned into vertex-disjoint dire
cted circuits $C_1, \dots, C_k$. In each circuit, every vertex has exactly one s
uccessor (outgoing edge within the circuit) and one predecessor (incoming edge w
ithin the circuit). Define $M = \{(x_v, y_{w}) : v \in V,\ w \text{ is the succe
ssor of } v \text{ in its circuit}\}$. Then:
- Every $x_v \in X$ is matched (each $v$ has a unique successor).
- Every $y_w \in Y$ is matched (each $w$ has a unique predecessor, whose success
or is $w$).
- No two edges in $M$ share an endpoint (each vertex has a unique successor and
unique predecessor).

So $M$ is a perfect matching of $G'$. $\square$

**Applying Hall's Theorem.** By Hall's Marriage Theorem, $G'$ has a perfect matc
hing if and only if for every subset $S \subseteq X$,
$$|N_{G'}(S)| \geq |S|,$$
where $N_{G'}(S) = \{y \in Y : \exists\, x \in S,\ (x,y) \in E'\}$ is the neighb
orhood of $S$ in $G'$.

Translating back to $G$: a subset $S \subseteq X$ corresponds to $A \subseteq V$
 via $S = \{x_v : v \in A\}$. Then
$$N_{G'}(S) = \{y_w : \exists\, v \in A,\ (v, w) \in E\} = \{y_w : w \in N^+(A)\
},$$
where $N^+(A) = \{w \in V : \exists\, v \in A,\ (v,w) \in E\}$ is the **out-neig
hborhood** of $A$ in $G$. Since the map $w \mapsto y_w$ is a bijection, $|N_{G'}
(S)| = |N^+(A)|$ and $|S| = |A|$.

**Conclusion.** The necessary and sufficient condition is:

> **$G$ admits a partition of $V$ into vertex-disjoint directed circuits if and
only if for every subset $A \subseteq V$,**
> $$|N^+(A)| \geq |A|,$$
> **where $N^+(A)$ is the set of out-neighbors of $A$ in $G$.**

Equivalently (via the bipartite reformulation), $G'$ has a perfect matching, i.e
., Hall's condition holds in $G'$: for every $S \subseteq X$, $|N_{G'}(S)| \geq
|S|$.

**Interpretation.** The condition $|N^+(A)| \geq |A|$ for all $A \subseteq V$ sa
ys that no subset of vertices "contracts" its out-neighborhood — every set of ve
rtices collectively reaches at least as many distinct vertices as it contains. T
his is the directed-graph analogue of Hall's condition, and via the bipartite "d
oubling" construction $G'$, it reduces exactly to the classical Hall condition f
or a perfect matching.
