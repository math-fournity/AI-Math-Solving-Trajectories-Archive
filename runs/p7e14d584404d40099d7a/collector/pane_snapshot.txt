## Proof

Let the five unit hyperspheres have centers $C_1, \dots, C_5$ in $\mathbb{R}^4$.
 Since each pair is externally tangent, every pair of centers is at distance $2$
 (sum of unit radii). Thus the five centers form a **regular $4$-simplex** with
edge length $a = 2$.

**Circumradius of the regular $4$-simplex.** For a regular $n$-simplex with $n+1
$ vertices and edge length $a$, the circumradius is
$$R = a\sqrt{\frac{n}{2(n+1)}}.$$
For $n=4$, $a=2$:
$$R = 2\sqrt{\frac{4}{2\cdot 5}} = 2\sqrt{\frac{2}{5}} = \sqrt{\frac{8}{5}}.$$

**Locating the large sphere's center.** The large hypersphere of radius $r$ is i
nternally tangent to each unit sphere, so its center $O$ satisfies
$$|OC_i| = r - 1 \quad \text{for all } i = 1,\dots,5.$$
Hence $O$ is equidistant from all five vertices $C_i$. Five points in general po
sition in $\mathbb{R}^4$ determine a unique circumsphere, so $O$ is the circumce
nter of the simplex and
$$r - 1 = R = \sqrt{\frac{8}{5}}.$$

**Uniqueness check.** The five centers are affinely independent (a nondegenerate
 $4$-simplex), so the circumcenter is the unique point equidistant from all five
. No other center can achieve internal tangency to all five unit spheres simulta
neously.

Therefore
$$r = 1 + \sqrt{\frac{8}{5}} = 1 + \frac{2\sqrt{10}}{5} = \frac{5 + 2\sqrt{10}}{
5}.$$
