Looking at this problem, I need to find the largest N such that a 6×N table exis
ts where every column is a permutation of {1,...,6}, any two columns agree in at
 least one row, and any two columns differ in at least one row (i.e., are distin
ct).

This is the problem of finding the maximum **intersecting family of permutations
** of [6].

---

## Proof

**Answer: N = 120.**

### Lower bound: N ≥ 120

Consider the family $\mathcal{F}$ of all permutations $\pi$ of $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$
 satisfying $\pi(1) = 1$, i.e., all permutations that place the value $1$ in row
 $1$. There are $5! = 120$ such permutations.

- **Each column is a permutation of $\{1,\ldots,6\}$**: by construction.
- **Any two columns agree in at least one row**: any two permutations in $\mathc
al{F}$ both have value $1$ in row $1$, so they agree in row $1$.
- **Any two columns differ in at least one row**: the permutations are distinct,
 so they differ in at least one position.

Arranging these 120 permutations as columns gives a valid $6 \times 120$ table,
so $N \geq 120$.

### Upper bound: N ≤ 120

Consider the cyclic group $C_6 = \langle c \rangle$ where $c = (1\ 2\ 3\ 4\ 5\ 6
)$ is the full cycle, acting on permutations by **right composition**: $\pi \map
sto \pi \circ c^k$ for $k = 0, 1, \ldots, 5$.

**Claim:** For any permutation $\pi$, the six permutations $\pi \circ c^0, \pi \
circ c^1, \ldots, \pi \circ c^5$ are pairwise **non-intersecting** (they disagre
e in every row).

*Proof of claim:* Take $\pi \circ c^i$ and $\pi \circ c^j$ with $i \not\equiv j
\pmod{6}$. They agree in row $r$ iff $(\pi \circ c^i)(r) = (\pi \circ c^j)(r)$,
i.e., $\pi(c^i(r)) = \pi(c^j(r))$. Since $\pi$ is injective, this requires $c^i(
r) = c^j(r)$, i.e., $c^{i-j}(r) = r$. But $c$ is a $6$-cycle, so $c^{i-j}$ has *
*no fixed point** when $i - j \not\equiv 0 \pmod{6}$. Therefore $\pi \circ c^i$
and $\pi \circ c^j$ disagree in every row. $\square$

This action partitions all $6! = 720$ permutations into orbits of size $6$, givi
ng $720/6 = 120$ orbits. Each orbit consists of 6 mutually non-intersecting perm
utations.

Now let $\mathcal{F}$ be any intersecting family (any two members agree in at le
ast one row). Since all 6 permutations in a single orbit are pairwise non-inters
ecting, $\mathcal{F}$ can contain **at most one** permutation from each orbit. T
herefore:

$$|\mathcal{F}| \leq 120.$$

### Conclusion

The lower bound and upper bound match:

$$\boxed{N = 120}$$
