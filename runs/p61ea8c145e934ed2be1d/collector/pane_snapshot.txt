## Complex and Exact Sequence in an Abelian Category

### Setup

Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an **abelian category**. Recall that an abelian category is
 an additive category (enriched over abelian groups, with a zero object, finite
biproducts) in which:

- every morphism $f: A \to B$ has a **kernel** $\ker f \to A$ and a **cokernel**
 $B \to \operatorname{coker} f$;
- every monomorphism is a kernel of some morphism, and every epimorphism is a co
kernel of some morphism (equivalently, every morphism $f$ factors as
$$A \xrightarrow{\;e\;} I \xrightarrow{\;m\;} B$$
where $e = \operatorname{coim} f$ is a cokernel (epimorphism) and $m = \operator
name{im} f$ is a kernel (monomorphism), and the canonical map $\bar f: \operator
name{coim} f \to \operatorname{im} f$ is an isomorphism).

Because $\mathcal{A}$ is additive, the **zero morphism** $0_{AB}: A \to B$ (the
unique morphism factoring through the zero object) is well-defined for every pai
r of objects.

### Definition (Complex)

A **(cochain) complex** in $\mathcal{A}$ is a sequence of objects and morphisms

$$\cdots \longrightarrow A^{n-1} \xrightarrow{\;d^{\,n-1}\;} A^{n} \xrightarrow{
\;d^{\,n}\;} A^{n+1} \xrightarrow{\;d^{\,n+1}\;} A^{n+2} \longrightarrow \cdots$
$

indexed over $\mathbb{Z}$ (or a finite interval), such that the **composing-to-z
ero** condition holds:

$$d^{\,n} \circ d^{\,n-1} = 0_{A^{n-1},\, A^{n+1}} \qquad \text{for all } n.$$

Equivalently, in arrow-free language: for every $n$, the composite of two consec
utive differentials is the zero morphism.

A **chain complex** is the same notion with the indexing reversed (lower indices
, $d_n: A_n \to A_{n-1}$, $d_{n-1}\circ d_n = 0$). The two notions are formally
identical; we use cochain notation below.

### Definition (Exact Sequence)

A sequence

$$\cdots \longrightarrow A^{n-1} \xrightarrow{\;f^{\,n-1}\;} A^{n} \xrightarrow{
\;f^{\,n}\;} A^{n+1} \longrightarrow \cdots$$

is **exact at $A^n$** if

$$\operatorname{im} f^{\,n-1} = \ker f^{\,n},$$

where both sides are subobjects of $A^n$ (i.e., monomorphism classes into $A^n$)
, and equality is equality of subobjects.

The sequence is an **exact sequence** if it is exact at every object $A^n$ appea
ring in it.

### Exactness in Terms of Kernels and Cokernels

Because $\mathcal{A}$ is abelian, the image and coimage of every morphism exist,
 and we may reformulate exactness purely in terms of kernels and cokernels. The
key observation is:

$$\operatorname{im} f = \ker(\operatorname{coker} f).$$

This identity holds in any abelian category: $\operatorname{coker} f: B \to \ope
ratorname{coker} f$ is the cokernel of $f: A \to B$, and $\ker(\operatorname{cok
er} f) \to B$ is precisely the image monomorphism of $f$ (this is one of the equ
ivalent axioms of an abelian category — every monomorphism is a kernel, and the
image is by definition the kernel of the cokernel).

Applying this to $f = f^{\,n-1}: A^{n-1} \to A^n$, we obtain

$$\operatorname{im} f^{\,n-1} = \ker\!\bigl(\operatorname{coker} f^{\,n-1}\bigr)
 \quad \text{(as subobjects of } A^n\text{)}.$$

Hence the exactness condition $\operatorname{im} f^{\,n-1} = \ker f^{\,n}$ is eq
uivalent to

$$\boxed{\;\ker\!\bigl(\operatorname{coker} f^{\,n-1}\bigr) \;=\; \ker f^{\,n}\;
}$$

as subobjects of $A^n$.

**Dual formulation.** Dually, since $\operatorname{coim} f = \operatorname{coker
}(\ker f)$ and $\operatorname{coim} f \cong \operatorname{im} f$ (the abelian-ca
tegory axiom), one may also express exactness via the cokernel side. The conditi
on $\operatorname{im} f^{\,n-1} = \ker f^{\,n}$ is equivalent to requiring that
the induced morphism

$$A^{n-1} \xrightarrow{\;f^{\,n-1}\;} A^n \xrightarrow{\;\operatorname{coker} f^
{\,n-1}\;} \operatorname{coker} f^{\,n-1}$$

factors $\ker f^{\,n}$ trivially, i.e., that the canonical map

$$\operatorname{coker} f^{\,n-1} \;\longrightarrow\; \operatorname{coker}\!\bigl
(\ker f^{\,n}\bigr) \;=\; \operatorname{coim} f^{\,n}$$

is an isomorphism. Concretely, exactness at $A^n$ is equivalent to each of the f
ollowing (all equivalent in an abelian category):

1. **Image–kernel equality:** $\operatorname{im} f^{\,n-1} = \ker f^{\n}$ (subob
ject equality in $A^n$).
2. **Kernel–of–cokernel form:** $\ker(\operatorname{coker} f^{\,n-1}) = \ker f^{
\,n}$.
3. **Factorization / quotient form:** the canonical morphism $\operatorname{coke
r} f^{\,n-1} \to \operatorname{coim} f^{\,n}$ is an isomorphism; equivalently $\
operatorname{coker} f^{\,n-1} \cong \operatorname{coker}(\ker f^{\,n})$.
4. **Kernel–cokernel exactness of the factorization:** writing $f^{\,n-1} = m \c
irc e$ with $e: A^{n-1} \twoheadrightarrow \operatorname{im} f^{\,n-1}$ (cokerne
l of $\ker f^{\,n-1}$) and $m: \operatorname{im} f^{\,n-1} \hookrightarrow A^n$
(kernel of $\operatorname{coker} f^{\,n-1}$), the condition is that $m$ coincide
s (as a subobject) with $\ker f^{\,n}$.

### Special Cases

- **Short exact sequence:** $0 \to A \xrightarrow{f} B \xrightarrow{g} C \to 0$
is exact iff $f$ is a kernel of $g$ (i.e., $\ker g = \operatorname{im} f$, and $
f$ is monic) and $g$ is a cokernel of $f$ (i.e., $\operatorname{coker} f = \oper
atorname{coim} g$, and $g$ is epic). In kernel/cokernel language: $\ker g = \ker
(\operatorname{coker} f)$ and $\operatorname{coker} f = \operatorname{coker}(\ke
r g)$.
- **Long exact sequence:** a complex that is exact at every term, equivalently s
atisfying condition (2) at every position.

### Summary

In an abelian category $\mathcal{A}$:

- A **complex** is a family $\{A^n, d^n\}$ with $d^n \circ d^{n-1} = 0$ for all
$n$; the condition $d^n \circ d^{n-1} = 0$ is equivalent to $\operatorname{im} d
^{n-1} \subseteq \ker d^n$ (as subobjects), i.e., $\ker(\operatorname{coker} d^{
n-1}) \leq \ker d^n$.
- The complex is **exact at $A^n$** precisely when this inclusion is an equality
:
$$\ker\!\bigl(\operatorname{coker} d^{\,n-1}\bigr) = \ker d^{\,n},$$
expressing exactness purely in terms of kernels and cokernels, with no reference
 to elements.
