## Solution

We evaluate
$$I=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin^{2}x}{x^{2}}\,e^{ix}\,dx.$$

**Fourier-transform setup.** Use the convention
$$\widehat{f}(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\,e^{-i\omega x}\,dx,\qquad f(x
)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(\omega)\,e^{i\omega x}\,d\ome
ga.$$
With this convention the convolution theorem reads
$$\widehat{fg}(\omega)=\frac{1}{2\pi}(\widehat{f}*\widehat{g})(\omega).$$

The target integral is $I=\widehat{h}(-1)$, where $h(x)=\dfrac{\sin^{2}x}{x^{2}}
=\left(\dfrac{\sin x}{x}\right)^{2}$.

**Transform of the sinc function.** Let $g(x)=\dfrac{\sin x}{x}$. Its well-known
 transform is
$$\widehat{g}(\omega)=\pi\,\mathbf{1}_{[-1,1]}(\omega),$$
i.e. $\pi$ on $(-1,1)$, $\pi/2$ at the endpoints, and $0$ outside. (This follows
 from inverting the rectangular pulse: $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\pi\,e^{i\ome
ga x}d\omega=\frac{\sin x}{x}$.)

**Convolution of two rectangles.** Since $h=g^{2}$,
$$\widehat{h}(\omega)=\frac{1}{2\pi}(\widehat{g}*\widehat{g})(\omega)=\frac{1}{2
\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\pi\mathbf{1}_{[-1,1]}(t)\;\pi\mathbf{1}_{[-1,1]}(\om
ega-t)\,dt.$$
The integrand is $\pi^{2}$ exactly on the overlap of $[-1,1]$ and $[\omega-1,\om
ega+1]$. The overlap length is
$$\max\bigl(0,\;2-|\omega|\bigr),$$
so
$$(\widehat{g}*\widehat{g})(\omega)=\pi^{2}(2-|\omega|)\quad\text{for }|\omega|\
le 2,\qquad 0\quad\text{otherwise}.$$
Hence
$$\widehat{h}(\omega)=\frac{\pi}{2}\,(2-|\omega|)\quad\text{for }|\omega|\le 2,\
qquad 0\quad\text{otherwise}.$$

**Evaluate at $\omega=-1$.** Since $h$ is real and even, $\widehat{h}$ is even,
so
$$I=\widehat{h}(-1)=\widehat{h}(1)=\frac{\pi}{2}\,(2-|1|)=\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot 1=\
frac{\pi}{2}.$$

**Consistency check.** $\widehat{h}(0)=\dfrac{\pi}{2}\cdot 2=\pi$, which equals
the classical value $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin^{2}x}{x^{2}}
\,dx=\pi$. ✓

Therefore
$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin^{2}x}{x^{2}}\,e^{ix}\,dx
=\frac{\pi}{2}}.$$
