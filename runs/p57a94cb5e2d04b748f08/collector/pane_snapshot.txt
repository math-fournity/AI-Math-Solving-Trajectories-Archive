The implication is **false**. Here is a counterexample.

**Counterexample.** Let $G = \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$, the Klein four-g
roup $V_4 = \{e, a, b, c\}$ with $a^2 = b^2 = c^2 = e$ and $ab = c$, $bc = a$, $
ca = b$.

**Step 1: $|G| = 4 \geq 3$.** The hypothesis on order is satisfied.

**Step 2: $Z(G) = G \neq \{e\}$.** Since $V_4$ is abelian, every element commute
s with every other, so $Z(G) = G$, which is nontrivial (it has order 4).

**Step 3: $\operatorname{Aut}(G) \cong S_3$.** An automorphism of $V_4$ is deter
mined by where it sends the three non-identity elements $a, b, c$. Since automor
phisms preserve the group structure, the images of $a, b, c$ must again be three
 distinct non-identity elements whose pairwise products give the third — which i
s exactly the structure of $V_4$. Hence any permutation of $\{a, b, c\}$ extends
 uniquely to an automorphism, and conversely every automorphism permutes $\{a, b
, c\}$. Therefore
$$\operatorname{Aut}(V_4) \cong S_3.$$

**Step 4: $Z(\operatorname{Aut}(G)) = Z(S_3) = \{e\}$.** The symmetric group $S_
3$ has trivial center: any non-identity element is either a transposition (which
 fails to commute with a 3-cycle) or a 3-cycle (which fails to commute with a tr
ansposition). Thus $Z(S_3) = \{e\}$.

**Conclusion.** We have constructed a group $G = V_4$ with $|G| = 4 \geq 3$ such
 that
$$Z(\operatorname{Aut}(G)) = \{e\} \quad\text{but}\quad Z(G) = G \neq \{e\}.$$
Therefore, the triviality of $Z(\operatorname{Aut}(G))$ does **not** imply the t
riviality of $Z(G)$.
