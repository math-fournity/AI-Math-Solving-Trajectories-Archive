# Expected Value of $\int_0^t \sin(s)\, dB_s$

## Problem

Calculate $\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^t \sin(s)\, dB_s\right]$, where $B_s$ is sta
ndard Brownian motion.

## Proof

The integrand $f(s,\omega) = \sin(s)$ is **deterministic** (it does not depend o
n $\omega$), bounded (since $|\sin(s)|\le 1$), and measurable. Hence it belongs
to the class $\mathcal{V}$ of admissible integrands for the Itô integral on $[0,
t]$:

$$
\int_0^t \mathbb{E}\!\left[\sin^2(s)\right] ds = \int_0^t \sin^2(s)\, ds < \inft
y.
$$

So $I_t := \int_0^t \sin(s)\, dB_s$ is a well-defined Itô integral.

### Key property: the Itô integral is a martingale starting at $0$

A fundamental theorem of stochastic calculus states that for any $f \in \mathcal
{V}$, the process

$$
M_t = \int_0^t f(s,\omega)\, dB_s
$$

is a **martingale** with respect to the natural filtration $(\mathcal{F}_t)$ of
the Brownian motion, and $M_0 = 0$.

In particular, for every $t \ge 0$,

$$
\mathbb{E}[M_t] = \mathbb{E}[M_0] = 0.
$$

### Applying it here

With $f(s) = \sin(s)$, we obtain

$$
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^t \sin(s)\, dB_s\right] = \mathbb{E}[M_t] = \mathbb{E}[
M_0] = 0.
$$

### Direct verification (optional)

One can also see this directly from the construction of the Itô integral. For a
simple process approximation $f_n$ converging to $\sin$ in $L^2([0,t])$, the dis
crete Itô sum is

$$
I_t^{(n)} = \sum_{j} f_n(s_j)\,\bigl(B_{s_{j+1}} - B_{s_j}\bigr).
$$

The increments $B_{s_{j+1}} - B_{s_j}$ are independent of $\mathcal{F}_{s_j}$ an
d have mean $0$, while $f_n(s_j)$ is $\mathcal{F}_{s_j}$-measurable (here even d
eterministic). Therefore

$$
\mathbb{E}\!\left[I_t^{(n)}\right] = \sum_j f_n(s_j)\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[B_{s_{j+
1}} - B_{s_j}\right] = 0.
$$

Since $I_t^{(n)} \to I_t$ in $L^2(\Omega)$, we have $\mathbb{E}[I_t^{(n)}] \to \
mathbb{E}[I_t]$, giving $\mathbb{E}[I_t] = 0$.

## Result

$$
\boxed{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^t \sin(s)\, dB_s\right] = 0.}
$$
