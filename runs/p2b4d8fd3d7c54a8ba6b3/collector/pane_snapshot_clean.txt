## Proof

We are given
$$v(t,r) = u_0\!\big((r-r_0)e^t + r_0\big)\,e^t, \qquad r_0 = \int_0^1 r\,u_0(r)
\,dr,$$
with $u_0:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^+$, compactly supported in $[0,1]$, $\int_0^1
u_0 = 1$, and the two properties
$$\int_0^1 v(t,r)\,dr = 1, \qquad \lim_{t\to\infty}\int_0^1 v(t,r)\,G(r)\,dr = G
(r_0) \text{ for every test function } G.$$

The second property is precisely the statement that $v(t,\cdot)$ converges to th
e Dirac delta distribution $\delta_{r_0}$ concentrated at $r_0$ in the sense of
distributions (weak-$*$ convergence of measures). We verify that this is consist
ent with the explicit form of $v$ and then determine the limit.

**Step 1: Mass preservation.** Make the change of variable $s = (r-r_0)e^t + r_0
$, so $r = r_0 + (s-r_0)e^{-t}$ and $dr = e^{-t}\,ds$. Then
$$\int_0^1 v(t,r)\,dr = \int_{r_0+(0-r_0)e^t}^{r_0+(1-r_0)e^t} u_0(s)\,e^t\,e^{-
t}\,ds = \int_{r_0-r_0 e^t}^{r_0+(1-r_0)e^t} u_0(s)\,ds.$$
For $t$ large enough the integration interval contains $[0,1]$ (since $r_0\in(0,
1)$ for a positive density on $[0,1]$, the lower endpoint $\to -\infty$ and the
upper $\to +\infty$), and since $\operatorname{supp} u_0\subseteq[0,1]$,
$$\int_0^1 v(t,r)\,dr = \int_0^1 u_0(s)\,ds = 1.$$

**Step 2: Weak convergence to $\delta_{r_0}$.** For any test function $G$, the s
ame substitution gives
$$\int_0^1 v(t,r)\,G(r)\,dr = \int_{r_0-r_0 e^t}^{r_0+(1-r_0)e^t} u_0(s)\,G\!\bi
g(r_0 + (s-r_0)e^{-t}\big)\,ds.$$
As $t\to\infty$, the argument of $G$ tends to $r_0$ uniformly on the support of
$u_0\subseteq[0,1]$, and $G$ is continuous, so $G(r_0+(s-r_0)e^{-t})\to G(r_0)$
uniformly. Hence
$$\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_0^1 v(t,r)\,G(r)\,dr = G(r_0)\int_0^1 u_0(s)\,ds = G(r_0
).$$
This confirms $v(t,\cdot)\rightharpoonup \delta_{r_0}$ as measures.

**Step 3: Pointwise behavior.** Fix $r\neq r_0$. The argument of $u_0$ in $v$ is
$$\varphi_t(r) = (r-r_0)e^t + r_0.$$
If $r>r_0$, then $\varphi_t(r)\to +\infty$; if $r<r_0$, then $\varphi_t(r)\to -\
infty$. Since $\operatorname{supp}u_0\subseteq[0,1]$, for all sufficiently large
 $t$ we have $\varphi_t(r)\notin[0,1]$, hence $u_0(\varphi_t(r))=0$, and therefo
re
$$v(t,r) = 0 \quad\text{for all large }t, \qquad r\neq r_0.$$
At $r=r_0$, $\varphi_t(r_0)=r_0$, so $v(t,r_0)=u_0(r_0)\,e^t\to +\infty$ (since
$u_0(r_0)>0$ for a positive density whose center of mass is $r_0\in(0,1)$).

Thus $v(t,\cdot)$ concentrates all its unit mass at the single point $r_0$: it v
anishes pointwise off $r_0$ and diverges at $r_0$, while integrating to $1$ and
testing against any $G$ to give $G(r_0)$.

**Conclusion.** The limit of $v(t,r)$ as $t\to+\infty$ is the Dirac delta distri
bution concentrated at the center of mass $r_0$:

$$\boxed{\lim_{t\to+\infty} v(t,r) = \delta(r-r_0).}$$
