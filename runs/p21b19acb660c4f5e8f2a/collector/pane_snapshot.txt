## Solution

We evaluate $I(s) = \int_{0}^{\infty} J_{0}(x)\, x^{s-1}\, dx$ for $0 < s < 1$.

### Step 1. Convergence

Near $x=0$: $J_0(x) \to 1$, so the integrand behaves like $x^{s-1}$, integrable
for $s>0$. Near $x=\infty$: $J_0(x) \sim \sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\pi x}}\cos\!\bigl(x-\t
frac{\pi}{4}\bigr)$, so $|J_0(x)x^{s-1}|\sim x^{s-3/2}$; the integral converges
(conditionally, by the Dirichlet test) for $s<\tfrac{3}{2}$. Hence $I(s)$ is wel
l-defined for $0<s<1$.

### Step 2. Integral representation of $J_0$

Use the Schläfli/Poisson representation
$$J_0(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\cos\!\bigl(x\cos\theta\bigr)\,d\theta.$$
By the substitution $\theta\mapsto\pi-\theta$, $\cos(x\cos(\pi-\theta))=\cos(-x\
cos\theta)=\cos(x\cos\theta)$, so the integrand is symmetric about $\theta=\pi/2
$, giving
$$J_0(x)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos\!\bigl(x\cos\theta\bigr)\,d\theta.$$

### Step 3. Interchange of integrals

Insert a regularizing factor $e^{-\varepsilon x}$ ($\varepsilon>0$) to justify F
ubini, then let $\varepsilon\to 0^+$ at the end (justified by dominated/Abelian
convergence). For the regularized integral:
$$I_\varepsilon(s)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\!\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-\varepsi
lon x}\cos(x\cos\theta)\,x^{s-1}\,dx\,d\theta.$$
The inner integral is a standard Mellin–Laplace transform. Taking $\varepsilon\t
o 0^+$ and using
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos(ax)\,x^{s-1}\,dx=\Gamma(s)\,a^{-s}\cos\!\Bigl(\frac{\pi
s}{2}\Bigr),\qquad 0<s<1,\ a>0,$$
with $a=\cos\theta>0$ on $(0,\pi/2)$, we obtain
$$I(s)=\frac{2\,\Gamma(s)\cos(\pi s/2)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos^{-s}\theta\,d\t
heta.$$

### Step 4. The beta integral

$$\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos^{-s}\theta\,d\theta=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sin^{0}\theta\,\cos^
{-s}\theta\,d\theta=\frac{1}{2}\,B\!\Bigl(\frac12,\frac{1-s}{2}\Bigr)=\frac{\sqr
t{\pi}\,\Gamma\!\bigl(\frac{1-s}{2}\bigr)}{2\,\Gamma\!\bigl(1-\frac{s}{2}\bigr)}
.$$

Substituting:
$$I(s)=\frac{2\,\Gamma(s)\cos(\pi s/2)}{\pi}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma\!\bigl
(\frac{1-s}{2}\bigr)}{2\,\Gamma\!\bigl(1-\frac{s}{2}\bigr)}=\frac{\Gamma(s)\cos(
\pi s/2)\,\Gamma\!\bigl(\frac{1-s}{2}\bigr)}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma\!\bigl(1-\frac{s
}{2}\bigr)}.$$

### Step 5. Simplification via duplication and reflection

**Duplication formula** ($z=s$):
$$\Gamma(s)=\frac{2^{s-1}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\Gamma\!\Bigl(\frac{s}{2}\Bigr)\Gamma\!\
Bigl(\frac{s+1}{2}\Bigr).$$

**Reflection formula** with $z=\frac{s+1}{2}$ (so $1-z=\frac{1-s}{2}$):
$$\Gamma\!\Bigl(\frac{s+1}{2}\Bigr)\Gamma\!\Bigl(\frac{1-s}{2}\Bigr)=\frac{\pi}{
\sin\!\bigl(\frac{\pi(s+1)}{2}\bigr)}=\frac{\pi}{\cos(\pi s/2)}.$$

Substitute both into $I(s)$:
$$I(s)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma\!\bigl(1-\frac{s}{2}\bigr)}\cdot\frac{2^{s-1}
}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\Gamma\!\Bigl(\frac{s}{2}\Bigr)\cdot\underbrace{\Gamma\!\Bigl(\fr
ac{s+1}{2}\Bigr)\Gamma\!\Bigl(\frac{1-s}{2}\Bigr)\cos(\pi s/2)}_{=\,\pi}$$
$$=\frac{2^{s-1}\,\Gamma\!\bigl(\frac{s}{2}\bigr)\,\pi}{\pi\,\Gamma\!\bigl(1-\fr
ac{s}{2}\bigr)}=\frac{2^{s-1}\,\Gamma\!\bigl(\frac{s}{2}\bigr)}{\Gamma\!\bigl(1-
\frac{s}{2}\bigr)}.$$

### Result

$$\boxed{\int_{0}^{\infty} J_{0}(x)\,x^{s-1}\,dx=\frac{2^{s-1}\,\Gamma\!\bigl(\t
frac{s}{2}\bigr)}{\Gamma\!\bigl(1-\tfrac{s}{2}\bigr)},\qquad 0<s<1.}$$

**Consistency check.** At $s=\tfrac12$: $\displaystyle I=\frac{2^{-1/2}\Gamma(1/
4)}{\Gamma(3/4)}$. Using $\Gamma(1/4)\Gamma(3/4)=\pi\sqrt{2}$, this equals $\dfr
ac{\Gamma(1/4)^2}{2\pi}$, the known value of $\int_0^\infty J_0(x)\,x^{-1/2}dx$.
