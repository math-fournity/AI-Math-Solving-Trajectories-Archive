## Solution

The Toeplitz operator $T_\phi$ on the Hardy space $H^2(S^1) = L_2^+(S^1)$ is def
ined by $T_\phi f = P(\phi f)$, where $P: L^2(S^1) \to H^2(S^1)$ is the orthogon
al projection onto the closed span of $\{e^{ikx}\}_{k \ge 0}$.

**Symbol.** Here $\phi(x) = e^{2ix}$, which is continuous and nowhere vanishing
on $S^1$, so $T_{e^{2ix}}$ is Fredholm (by the standard criterion for Toeplitz o
perators with continuous nonvanishing symbol).

**Direct computation of kernel and cokernel.** The set $\{e^{ikx}\}_{k=0}^{\inft
y}$ is an orthonormal basis of $H^2(S^1)$. For each basis element:

$$T_{e^{2ix}}\, e^{ikx} = P\!\left(e^{2ix}\cdot e^{ikx}\right) = P\!\left(e^{i(k
+2)x}\right).$$

Since $k \ge 0$, we have $k+2 \ge 2 > 0$, so $e^{i(k+2)x}$ already lies in $H^2(
S^1)$ and the projection acts as the identity:

$$T_{e^{2ix}}\, e^{ikx} = e^{i(k+2)x}, \qquad k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$$

Thus $T_{e^{2ix}}$ is the forward shift by 2, mapping the basis $\{e^{0}, e^{ix}
, e^{2ix}, \ldots\}$ onto $\{e^{2ix}, e^{3ix}, e^{4ix}, \ldots\}$.

**Kernel.** If $f = \sum_{k\ge 0} a_k e^{ikx} \in H^2$ and $T_{e^{2ix}}f = 0$, t
hen $\sum_{k\ge 0} a_k e^{i(k+2)x} = 0$, which forces $a_k = 0$ for all $k$. Hen
ce

$$\dim \ker T_{e^{2ix}} = 0.$$

**Range / cokernel.** The range of $T_{e^{2ix}}$ is the closed span of $\{e^{2ix
}, e^{3ix}, e^{4ix}, \ldots\}$, which is the subspace of $H^2(S^1)$ orthogonal t
o both $e^{0}=1$ and $e^{ix}$. Therefore the range has codimension 2, and

$$\dim \operatorname{coker} T_{e^{2ix}} = 2.$$

**Fredholm index.**

$$\operatorname{ind}\!\left(T_{e^{2ix}}\right) = \dim \ker T_{e^{2ix}} - \dim \o
peratorname{coker} T_{e^{2ix}} = 0 - 2 = -2.$$

This agrees with the general formula $\operatorname{ind}(T_\phi) = -\operatornam
e{wind}(\phi)$, since $e^{2ix}$ winds twice counterclockwise around the origin,
giving $\operatorname{wind}(e^{2ix}) = 2$.
