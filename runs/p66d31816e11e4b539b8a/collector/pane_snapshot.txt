# Why φ is a morphism and extends to Φ: E → C

## Part 1 — φ: E − {O} → C is a morphism

Recall that a map from a variety to projective space **Pⁿ** is a morphism on an
open set **U** if it can be written as **[f₀ : … : fₙ]** with the **fᵢ** regular
 on **U** and having no common zero on **U**.

On **E − {O}**, the functions **x** and **y** are regular: by hypothesis their o
nly poles are at **O** (since **(x)∞ = 2(O)** and **(y)∞ = 3(O)**). Hence on **E
 − {O}** the triple **[x : y : 1]** consists of regular functions with no common
 zero (the third coordinate is the constant 1). Therefore

$$P \longmapsto [x(P) : y(P) : 1]$$

is a morphism **E − {O} → P²**.

It remains to check the image lands in **C**. The function field **k(E)** is gen
erated by **x, y** subject to the Weierstrass relation

$$y^2 + a_1 xy + a_3 y = x^3 + a_2 x^2 + a_4 x + a_6,$$

i.e. **f(x, y) = 0** in **k(E)**. So for every **P ∈ E − {O}**,

$$f(x(P), y(P)) = 0,$$

which means **[x(P) : y(P) : 1]** lies on the affine piece **Z ≠ 0** of the proj
ective closure **C** of **V(f)**. Hence **φ: E − {O} → C** is a morphism.

## Part 2 — φ extends to a morphism Φ: E → C

The key fact is the standard theorem:

> **A rational map from a nonsingular (smooth) curve to a complete (projective)
variety extends uniquely to a morphism defined on the whole curve.**

This is the valuative criterion of properness applied to the discrete valuation
ring **𝒪_{E,O}**. Concretely:

**E** is nonsingular (an elliptic curve is smooth by definition), so **𝒪_{E,O}**
 is a DVR with uniformizer **t**, and **C ⊂ P²** is projective, hence complete.
The rational map **φ: E − {O} ⇢ C** is a rational map from the smooth curve **E*
* to the complete variety **C**; by the theorem it extends to a morphism **Φ: E
→ C**.

### Explicit verification at O

We can also see the extension concretely. Near **O**, choose a uniformizer **t**
 with **ord_O(t) = 1**. Then

$$\operatorname{ord}_O(x) = -2, \qquad \operatorname{ord}_O(y) = -3, \qquad \ope
ratorname{ord}_O(1) = 0.$$

To clear the poles, multiply all three homogeneous coordinates by **t³** (the po
wer needed to make every coordinate regular):

$$[x : y : 1] = [x\,t^3 : y\,t^3 : t^3].$$

Now

$$\operatorname{ord}_O(x\,t^3) = -2+3 = 1,\quad \operatorname{ord}_O(y\,t^3) = -
3+3 = 0,\quad \operatorname{ord}_O(t^3) = 3.$$

All three are **≥ 0**, so they are regular at **O**, and **y t³** has order **0*
*, hence is **nonzero** at **O**. Therefore the representation **[x t³ : y t³ :
t³]** is well-defined and non-vanishing at **O**, and

$$\Phi(O) = [0 : (y\,t^3)(O) : 0] = [0 : 1 : 0],$$

the unique point at infinity of the Weierstrass model **C** — which is a smooth
point of **C**. Thus **Φ** is a well-defined morphism on all of **E**, agreeing
with **φ** on **E − {O}** and sending **O ↦ [0:1:0]**.

## Summary

- **φ is a morphism** because **x, y** are regular on **E − {O}** (their poles a
re only at **O**), so **[x:y:1]** gives a regular map into **P²** landing in **C
** by the Weierstrass equation.
- **φ extends to Φ: E → C** because **E** is a smooth curve and **C** is project
ive (complete), so the valuative criterion of properness forces the rational map
 to extend across the single missing point **O**; concretely, rescaling by **t³*
* produces regular coordinates at **O** with image **[0:1:0]**.
