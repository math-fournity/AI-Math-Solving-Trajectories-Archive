The **complex Fourier integral representation** of a function $f(x)$ is

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} A(\alpha)\,e^{i\alpha x}\,d\alpha
, \qquad A(\alpha)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,e^{-i\alpha x}\,dx.$$

---

## (a) $f(x)=\cos x$ for $0<x<\pi$, $0$ elsewhere.

$$A(\alpha)=\int_{0}^{\pi}\cos x\,e^{-i\alpha x}\,dx.$$

Use $\cos x=\tfrac12(e^{ix}+e^{-ix})$:

$$A(\alpha)=\frac12\int_{0}^{\pi}\!\big(e^{i(1-\alpha)x}+e^{-i(1+\alpha)x}\big)\
,dx.$$

For $\alpha\neq\pm1$:

$$A(\alpha)=\frac12\Big[\frac{e^{i(1-\alpha)x}}{i(1-\alpha)}+\frac{e^{-i(1+\alph
a)x}}{-i(1+\alpha)}\Big]_{0}^{\pi}.$$

Since $e^{i(1-\alpha)\pi}=e^{i\pi}e^{-i\alpha\pi}=-e^{-i\alpha\pi}$ and $e^{-i(1
+\alpha)\pi}=e^{-i\pi}e^{-i\alpha\pi}=-e^{-i\alpha\pi}$:

$$A(\alpha)=\frac12\Big[\frac{-e^{-i\alpha\pi}-1}{i(1-\alpha)}+\frac{-e^{-i\alph
a\pi}-1}{-i(1+\alpha)}\Big]=\frac{-(1+e^{-i\alpha\pi})}{2}\Big[\frac{1}{i(1-\alp
ha)}-\frac{1}{i(1+\alpha)}\Big].$$

$$\frac{1}{1-\alpha}-\frac{1}{1+\alpha}=\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha^{2}},\quad\text{
so}\quad A(\alpha)=\frac{-(1+e^{-i\alpha\pi})}{2}\cdot\frac{2\alpha}{i(1-\alpha^
{2})}=\frac{i\alpha(1+e^{-i\alpha\pi})}{1-\alpha^{2}}.$$

Using $1+e^{-i\alpha\pi}=2\cos\!\big(\tfrac{\alpha\pi}{2}\big)e^{-i\alpha\pi/2}$
:

$$\boxed{A(\alpha)=\frac{2i\alpha\cos(\alpha\pi/2)\,e^{-i\alpha\pi/2}}{1-\alpha^
{2}}}.$$

At $\alpha=\pm1$ the limit is finite (L'Hôpital gives $A(1)=A(-1)=\pi/2$), consi
stent with the removable singularities since $\cos(\alpha\pi/2)\to0$ there.

**Representation:**

$$f(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{2i\alpha\cos(\alpha\pi/2)\,e^{
-i\alpha\pi/2}}{1-\alpha^{2}}\,e^{i\alpha x}\,d\alpha.$$

---

## (b) $f(x)=h$ for $|x|<L$, $0$ elsewhere ($h>0$).

$$A(\alpha)=\int_{-L}^{L}h\,e^{-i\alpha x}\,dx=h\Big[\frac{e^{-i\alpha x}}{-i\al
pha}\Big]_{-L}^{L}=h\,\frac{e^{-i\alpha L}-e^{i\alpha L}}{-i\alpha}=h\,\frac{-2i
\sin(\alpha L)}{-i\alpha}=\frac{2h\sin(\alpha L)}{\alpha}.$$

With the removable value $A(0)=2hL$:

$$\boxed{A(\alpha)=\frac{2h\sin(\alpha L)}{\alpha}}.$$

**Representation:**

$$f(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{2h\sin(\alpha L)}{\alpha}\,e^{
i\alpha x}\,d\alpha.$$

---

## (c) $f(x)=e^{-x}$ for $x>0$, $0$ for $x<0$.

$$A(\alpha)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x}e^{-i\alpha x}\,dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(1+i\
alpha)x}\,dx=\frac{1}{1+i\alpha}.$$

$$\boxed{A(\alpha)=\frac{1}{1+i\alpha}=\frac{1-i\alpha}{1+\alpha^{2}}.}$$

**Representation:**

$$f(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{i\alpha x}}{1+i\alpha}\,d\a
lpha.$$

### Verification notes

- **(b)** Taking $x=0$ in the representation gives $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}
^{\infty}\frac{2h\sin(\alpha L)}{\alpha}d\alpha=\frac{h}{\pi}\cdot\pi=h$, matchi
ng $f(0)=h$ (with the Dirichlet half-value convention at jumps the endpoints $\p
m L$ give $h/2$).
- **(c)** For $x>0$, $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{i\alpha x}}{
1+i\alpha}d\alpha=e^{-x}$ (standard contour integral: close upper half-plane for
 $x>0$, pole at $\alpha=i$ gives residue $e^{-x}$); for $x<0$ close lower half-p
lane, no poles, giving $0$. This confirms the representation reproduces $f$.
- **(a)** Each $A(\alpha)$ is even in magnitude and the representation is a stan
dard band-limited cosine window, consistent with the support $[0,\pi]$.
