## Proof

Let $F$ be the field of fractions of $R$. We prove that **yes**, such a free $R$
-module $N$ exists, under the standard hypothesis that $K/F$ is **finite separab
le** (of degree $n$). The argument uses the non-degeneracy of the trace form.

### Step 1: $K = F \cdot B$

For any $x \in K$, $x$ is algebraic over $F$ with minimal polynomial $x^m + a_{m
-1}x^{m-1} + \cdots + a_0 = 0$, $a_i \in F$. Choose $r \in R \setminus \{0\}$ cl
earing all denominators (so $ra_i \in R$). Multiplying through by $r^m$:

$$(rx)^m + (ra_{m-1})(rx)^{m-1} + \cdots + r^m a_0 = 0,$$

so $rx$ is integral over $R$, hence $rx \in B$, and $x = (rx)/r \in F \cdot B$.

### Step 2: Choose an $F$-basis of $K$ from $B$

Since $K = F \cdot B$ and $\dim_F K = n$, we can select $e_1, \dots, e_n \in B$
forming an $F$-basis of $K$.

### Step 3: Construct the dual basis via the trace form

Since $K/F$ is **separable**, the trace form $\langle x, y \rangle := \operatorn
ame{Tr}_{K/F}(xy)$ is a **non-degenerate** symmetric $F$-bilinear form on $K$. L
et $e_1^*, \dots, e_n^* \in K$ be the dual basis, defined by:

$$\operatorname{Tr}_{K/F}(e_i \, e_j^*) = \delta_{ij}.$$

### Step 4: Define $N$ and prove $B \subseteq N$

Set $N := R\,e_1^* \oplus \cdots \oplus R\,e_n^*$, a **free $R$-module of rank $
n$**.

Take any $b \in B$ and write $b = \sum_{i=1}^n a_i\, e_i^*$ with $a_i \in F$. Ap
plying the trace pairing with $e_j$:

$$a_j = \operatorname{Tr}_{K/F}(b \, e_j).$$

Now we show each $a_j \in R$:

1. **$b\, e_j \in B$:** Since $b \in B$ and $e_j \in B$, and $B$ (the integral c
losure) is a subring of $K$, the product $be_j \in B$, so $be_j$ is integral ove
r $R$.

2. **$\operatorname{Tr}_{K/F}(be_j)$ is integral over $R$:** Since $K/F$ is sepa
rable, the minimal polynomial of $be_j$ over $F$ has distinct roots $\alpha_1, \
dots, \alpha_d$ (in a normal closure). Each $\alpha_k$ satisfies the same monic
polynomial over $R$ (obtained by clearing denominators in the minimal polynomial
 over $F$), so each $\alpha_k$ is integral over $R$. The trace is:

$$\operatorname{Tr}_{K/F}(be_j) =  : F(be_j)] \sum_{k=1}^d \alpha_k,$$

which is a sum of elements integral over $R$, hence integral over $R$.

3. **$\operatorname{Tr}_{K/F}(be_j) \in R$:** The trace lies in $F$ (by definiti
on, $\operatorname{Tr}_{K/F}: K \to F$). Since $R$ is **integrally closed** in $
F$, any element of $F$ that is integral over $R$ must belong to $R$. Therefore $
\operatorname{Tr}_{K/F}(be_j) \in R$.

Thus $a_j \in R$ for every $j$, so $b \in N$.

### Conclusion

Since $b \in B$ was arbitrary, we have:

$$B \subseteq N = \bigoplus_{i=1}^{n} R\, e_i^*,$$

where $N$ is a **free $R$-module of rank $n = :F]$**.

**The answer is yes.** The three essential hypotheses are:
- **$R$ integrally closed**: ensures trace values lying in $F$ and integral over
 $R$ actually belong to $R$.
- **$K/F$ separable**: ensures the trace form is non-degenerate, which is needed
 to construct the dual basis $\{e_i^*\}$.
- **$K/F$ finite**: ensures a finite basis and a well-defined trace map $K \to F
$.

Without separability, the trace form can be degenerate (e.g., in purely insepara
ble extensions the trace may vanish identically), and the dual basis argument fa
ils.
