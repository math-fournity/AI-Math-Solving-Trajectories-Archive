## Proof

**Step 1: The two given points coincide.**

In projective space, $[i,-1,i] = i\cdot[1,i,1]$, since $i\cdot 1 = i$, $i\cdot i
 = -1$, $i\cdot 1 = i$. Thus the two "points" $[1,i,1]$ and $[i,-1,i]$ are the s
ame point $P=[1,i,1]$. The problem therefore asks for real conics singular at th
e single complex point $P$.

**Step 2: Set up the space of real conics.**

A conic in the real projective plane $\mathbb{RP}^2$ is a quadratic form with re
al coefficients
$$F(x,y,z)=ax^2+by^2+cz^2+dxy+exz+fyz,\qquad a,b,c,d,e,f\in\mathbb{R},$$
forming a $6$-dimensional real vector space.

**Step 3: Singularity condition.**

A conic is singular at $P$ iff $F_x(P)=F_y(P)=F_z(P)=0$. (By Euler's relation $x
F_x+yF_y+zF_z=2F$, vanishing of all three partials at $P$ automatically forces $
F(P)=0$.) The partials are
$$F_x=2ax+dy+ez,\quad F_y=2by+dx+fz,\quad F_z=2cz+ex+fy.$$

**Step 4: Evaluate at $P=(1,i,1)$.**

$$F_x(P)=2a+di+e=0,\quad F_y(P)=2bi+d+fi=0,\quad F_z(P)=2c+e+fi=0.$$

Splitting each complex equation into real and imaginary parts gives six real lin
ear equations:
$$\underbrace{2a+e=0}_{(1)},\;\underbrace{d=0}_{(2)},\;\underbrace{d=0}_{(3)},\;
\underbrace{2b+f=0}_{(4)},\;\underbrace{2c+e=0}_{(5)},\;\underbrace{f=0}_{(6)}.$
$

Equations (2) and (3) are identical, so there are **5 independent** constraints
on the 6 real parameters.

**Step 5: Solve.**

From (6): $f=0$. From (4): $2b=0\Rightarrow b=0$. From (2): $d=0$. From (1): $e=
-2a$. From (5): $2c+e=0\Rightarrow c=-e/2=a$.

Thus every solution has the form
$$F=a\bigl(x^2+z^2-2xz\bigr)=a(x-z)^2,$$
with a single free real parameter $a$.

**Step 6: Verification.**

The resulting conic is the double line $(x-z)^2=0$. It is singular along the ent
ire line $x=z$. The point $P=[1,i,1]$ lies on this line since $1-1=0$, so $F$ is
 indeed singular at $P$. The solution space is the 1-dimensional span $\{\,a(x-z
)^2 : a\in\mathbb{R}\,\}$.

**Conclusion.**

The vector space of real conics singular at $[1,i,1]$ and $[i,-1,i]$ (the same p
oint) has dimension

$$\boxed{1}.$$
