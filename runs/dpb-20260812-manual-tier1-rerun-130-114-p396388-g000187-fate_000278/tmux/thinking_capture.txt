For any basic open subgroup $N_L$ of $G$ (with $L/K$ finite Galois), the
quotient $G/N_L \cong \operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ is finite. The image of $g$ in
this finite group has some finite order $m_L \geq 1$. Then: $$\varphi^{-1}(N_L)
= {n \in \mathbb{Z} : g^n \in N_L} = m_L,\mathbb{Z},$$ which is open in the
profinite topology on $\mathbb{Z}$. Hence $\varphi$ is continuous.

Since $\mathbb{Z}$ is dense in its profinite completion $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ and
$G$ is Hausdorff and compact, $\varphi$ extends uniquely to a continuous
homomorphism: $$\hat{\varphi}: \hat{\mathbb{Z}} \longrightarrow G, \qquad
\hat{\varphi}(1) = g.$$

Step 2: The image of $\hat{\varphi}$ is the closure of $\langle g \rangle$.

The image $\hat{\varphi}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})$ is compact (continuous image of a
compact set), hence closed in the Hausdorff space $G$. It contains $
\varphi(\mathbb{Z}) = \langle g \rangle$. Since $\mathbb{Z}$ is dense in $
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ and $\hat{\varphi}$ is continuous: $$\hat{\varphi}
(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}) = \hat{\varphi}!\left(\overline{\mathbb{Z}}\right) \subseteq
\overline{\hat{\varphi}(\mathbb{Z})} = \overline{\langle g \rangle}.$$
Conversely, $\hat{\varphi}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})$ is a closed set containing $
\langle g \rangle$, so $\overline{\langle g \rangle} \subseteq \hat{\varphi}
(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})$. Therefore: $$\overline{\langle g \rangle} = \hat{\varphi}
(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}) \cong \hat{\mathbb{Z}} \big/ H, \qquad H :=
\ker(\hat{\varphi}).$$

Step 3: If $g$ has infinite order, then $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}/H$ is infinite.

Since $g$ has infinite order, $\varphi: \mathbb{Z} \to G$ is injective, so $H
\cap \mathbb{Z} = \ker(\varphi) = {0}$.

Suppose for contradiction that $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}/H$ is finite. Then $H$ has
finite index in $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$, hence $H$ is an open subgroup of $
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$. Every open subgroup of $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is of the form
$m\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ for some integer $m \geq 1$ (since $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is
procyclic and its finite quotients are cyclic). But then: $$H \cap \mathbb{Z} =
m\hat{\mathbb{Z}} \cap \mathbb{Z} = m\mathbb{Z} \neq {0},$$ contradicting $H
\cap \mathbb{Z} = {0}$. Hence $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}/H$ is infinite.

Step 4: An infinite quotient of $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is uncountable.

By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, $\hat{\mathbb{Z}} \cong \prod_p \mathbb{Z}_p$
(product over all primes $p$). Every closed subgroup $H$ of $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$
decomposes as $H = \prod_p H_p$, where each $H_p$ is a closed subgroup of $
\mathbb{Z}_p$. The closed subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}_p$ are ${0}$ and
$p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_p$ for $k \geq 0$.

Thus $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}/H \cong \prod_p \mathbb{Z}_p / H_p$, where each factor is
either:

  • $\mathbb{Z}_p$ (if $H_p = {0}$) — uncountable, or
  • $\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$ (if $H_p = p^k\mathbb{Z}_p$, $k \geq 1$) —
  finite, or
  • ${0}$ (if $H_p = \mathbb{Z}_p$, i.e., $k=0$) — trivial.

The quotient $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}/H$ is finite only if all but finitely many
factors are trivial and the remaining factors are finite. Since $
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}/H$ is infinite (Step 3), either:

  1. Some $H_p = {0}$, giving a factor $\mathbb{Z}_p$ which is uncountable, or
  2. Infinitely many primes $p$ have $H_p \neq \mathbb{Z}_p$ (so $H_p =
  p^{k_p}\mathbb{Z}_p$ with $k_p \geq 1$), giving an infinite product $\prod_p
  \mathbb{Z}/p^{k_p}\mathbb{Z}$ of finite sets with at least two elements — this
  product has cardinality $\geq 2^{\aleph_0}$, hence is uncountable.

In both cases, $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}/H$ is uncountable.

Step 5: Conclusion.

We have shown:

  • $\overline{\langle g \rangle} \cong \hat{\mathbb{Z}}/H$ is uncountable
  (Steps 2–4),
  • $\langle g \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}$ is countable (since $g$ has infinite
  order).

Therefore $\langle g \rangle \subsetneq \overline{\langle g \rangle}$, so $
\langle g \rangle$ is not closed. $\checkmark$

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Combining both directions, $\langle g \rangle$ is closed in $G(K)$ if and only
if $g$ is torsion. $\blacksquare$
