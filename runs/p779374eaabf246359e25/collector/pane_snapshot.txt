## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. The space $\{g|_C : g \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})\}$ is dense in
$L^p(\mu)$ for every $1 \le p < \infty$.

The argument uses three standard facts, combined in a chain of density:

$$\{g|_C : g \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})\} \;\overset{\text{dense in}}{\longright
arrow}\; C(C) \;\overset{\text{dense in}}{\longrightarrow}\; L^p(\mu).$$

---

**Step 1: $C(C)$ is dense in $L^p(\mu)$.**

The Cantor measure $\mu$ is a finite Borel measure ($\mu(C)=1$) on the compact m
etric space $C$. For any finite Borel measure on a compact metric space, continu
ous functions are dense in $L^p(\mu)$ for $1 \le p < \infty$. This follows from
Lusin's theorem combined with the regularity of $\mu$: simple functions are dens
e in $L^p(\mu)$, and by Lusin's theorem each characteristic function $\mathbf{1}
_E$ of a Borel set $E$ can be approximated in $L^p(\mu)$ by a continuous functio
n (since $\mu$ is regular, we can approximate $E$ from inside/outside by compact
/open sets and use Urysohn-type functions). Hence $C(C)$ is dense in $L^p(\mu)$.

---

**Step 2: $\{g|_C : g \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})\}$ is dense in $C(C)$ in the su
p-norm (hence in $L^p(\mu)$).**

Let $f \in C(C)$. Since $C \subset [0,1]$ is closed, the **Tietze extension theo
rem** provides a continuous extension $\tilde{f} \in C([0,1])$ with $\tilde{f}|_
C = f$. Multiplying by a continuous bump function $\psi \in C_c(\mathbb{R})$ wit
h $\psi \equiv 1$ on $[0,1]$, we obtain $F = \psi \cdot \tilde{f} \in C_c(\mathb
b{R})$ with $F|_C = f$.

Now let $\varphi_\varepsilon$ be a standard smooth mollifier (nonneg­ative, $C^\
infty$, supported in $(-\varepsilon, \varepsilon)$, $\int \varphi_\varepsilon =
1$). Define
$$F_\varepsilon := F * \varphi_\varepsilon.$$
Then:
- $F_\varepsilon \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ (convolution with a smooth function i
s smooth),
- $F_\varepsilon$ has compact support (the support of $F * \varphi_\varepsilon$
is contained in $\operatorname{supp}(F) + [-\varepsilon, \varepsilon]$, which is
 compact),
- $F_\varepsilon \to F$ **uniformly** on $\mathbb{R}$ as $\varepsilon \to 0$ (st
andard property: mollification of a uniformly continuous function converges unif
ormly).

Therefore $F_\varepsilon|_C \to f$ uniformly on $C$. Since $\mu(C) = 1 < \infty$
, uniform convergence implies $L^p(\mu)$-convergence:
$$\|F_\varepsilon|_C - f\|_{L^p(\mu)} \le \|F_\varepsilon - F\|_{\sup,\, C} \cdo
t \mu(C)^{1/p} \longrightarrow 0.$$

Thus every $f \in C(C)$ is an $L^p(\mu)$-limit of restrictions of $C_c^\infty(\m
athbb{R})$ functions, so $\{g|_C : g \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})\}$ is dense in $
C(C)$ in the $L^p(\mu)$-norm.

---

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

Since $\{g|_C : g \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})\}$ is dense in $C(C)$ (Step 2) and
$C(C)$ is dense in $L^p(\mu)$ (Step 1), by transitivity of density, $\{g|_C : g
\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})\}$ is dense in $L^p(\mu)$ for every $1 \le p < \infty
$.

---

**Remark.** The argument uses only that $C$ is a compact (hence closed) subset o
f $\mathbb{R}$ and that $\mu$ is a finite Borel measure. The total disconnectedn
ess of $C$ and the singularity of $\mu$ with respect to Lebesgue measure play no
 obstructing role: the Tietze extension theorem and mollification operate on the
 ambient space $\mathbb{R}$, and restriction to $C$ preserves the uniform (hence
 $L^p$) approximation.
