## Integration by Parts for Continuous Local Martingales

**Theorem.** Let $M, N \in \mathfrak{M}_{c,loc}$ be continuous local martingales
 on a filtered probability space $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, (\mathcal{F}_t)_{t\ge 0}
, \mathbb{P})$. Then for all $t \ge 0$,

$$M_t N_t - M_0 N_0 = \int_0^t M_s\, dN_s + \int_0^t N_s\, dM_s + \langle M, N\r
angle_t,$$

where $\langle M, N\rangle$ denotes the quadratic covariation (predictable covar
iation) of $M$ and $N$, and the two stochastic integrals are Itô integrals (cont
inuous local martingales).

---

### Proof

**Step 1 — Reduction to bounded martingales.**

Since $M, N$ are continuous local martingales, there exist stopping times $\sigm
a_n \uparrow \infty$ (a common localizing sequence, e.g. $\sigma_n = \inf\{t : |
M_t| \ge n\} \wedge \inf\{t : |N_t| \ge n\}$) such that the stopped processes $M
^{\sigma_n}$ and $N^{\sigma_n}$ are bounded continuous martingales. If we prove
the formula for bounded continuous martingales, then applying it to $M^{\sigma_n
}, N^{\sigma_n}$ and letting $n \to \infty$ yields the result for $M, N$ (all te
rms converge pathwise on $\{t < \sigma_n\}$, and $\sigma_n \uparrow \infty$ a.s.
). Hence it suffices to prove the formula when $M, N$ are **bounded continuous m
artingales**.

**Step 2 — Itô's formula for $f(x,y) = xy$.**

The function $f(x,y) = xy$ is of class $C^2(\mathbb{R}^2)$ with
$$\partial_x f = y, \quad \partial_y f = x, \quad \partial_{xx} f = 0, \quad \pa
rtial_{yy} f = 0, \quad \partial_{xy} f = \partial_{yx} f = 1.$$

Applying the two-dimensional Itô formula to the continuous semimartingale $(M_t,
 N_t)$:

$$f(M_t, N_t) = f(M_0, N_0) + \int_0^t \partial_x f(M_s, N_s)\, dM_s + \int_0^t
\partial_y f(M_s, N_s)\, dN_s + \frac{1}{2}\int_0^t \partial_{xx} f\, d\langle M
\rangle_s + \frac{1}{2}\int_0^t \partial_{yy} f\, d\langle N\rangle_s + \int_0^t
 \partial_{xy} f\, d\langle M, N\rangle_s.$$

Substituting the partial derivatives:

$$M_t N_t = M_0 N_0 + \int_0^t N_s\, dM_s + \int_0^t M_s\, dN_s + 0 + 0 + \int_0
^t 1\, d\langle M, N\rangle_s.$$

The $\partial_{xx}$ and $\partial_{yy}$ terms vanish because $f$ is linear in ea
ch variable separately. The mixed term gives the covariation. Therefore:

$$M_t N_t - M_0 N_0 = \int_0^t M_s\, dN_s + \int_0^t N_s\, dM_s + \langle M, N\r
angle_t.$$

**Step 3 — Identification of the terms.**

- The stochastic integrals $\int_0^t M_s\, dN_s$ and $\int_0^t N_s\, dM_s$ are w
ell-defined Itô integrals: since $M, N$ are continuous local martingales, the in
tegrands $M$ and $N$ are locally bounded predictable processes, so the integrals
 exist as continuous local martingales starting at $0$.
- $\langle M, N\rangle_t = \int_0^t d\langle M, N\rangle_s$ is the (predictable,
 continuous, finite-variation) quadratic covariation process, the unique continu
ous adapted process of finite variation such that $MN - \langle M, N\rangle$ is
a continuous local martingale.

**Step 4 — Verification of the decomposition's structure.**

The right-hand side decomposes $M_t N_t - M_0 N_0$ into:
- a **local martingale part**: $\int_0^t M_s\, dN_s + \int_0^t N_s\, dM_s$ (sum
of two continuous local martingales),
- a **finite-variation predictable part**: $\langle M, N\rangle_t$.

This is precisely the Doob–Meyer / semimartingale decomposition of the continuou
s semimartingale $M_t N_t - M_0 N_0$. Indeed, the product $MN$ is a continuous s
emimartingale (product of semimartingales), and the formula provides its canonic
al decomposition into local martingale plus predictable finite-variation compens
ator. This is consistent with the defining property of $\langle M, N\rangle$: th
e process

$$M_t N_t - \langle M, N\rangle_t = M_0 N_0 + \int_0^t M_s\, dN_s + \int_0^t N_s
\, dM_s$$

is a continuous local martingale, confirming that $\langle M, N\rangle$ is the c
orrect compensator.

**Step 5 — Localization removes the boundedness assumption.**

For general $M, N \in \mathfrak{M}_{c,loc}$, let $\sigma_n$ be a localizing sequ
ence making both $M^{\sigma_n}$ and $N^{\sigma_n}$ bounded. The formula holds fo
r the stopped processes:

$$(M_t \wedge \text{stopped})(N_t \wedge \text{stopped}) - M_0 N_0 = \int_0^{t \
wedge \sigma_n} M_s\, dN_s + \int_0^{t \wedge \sigma_n} N_s\, dM_s + \langle M,
N\rangle_{t \wedge \sigma_n}.$$

Letting $n \to \infty$, by continuity of paths and a.s. convergence $\sigma_n \u
parrow \infty$, all terms converge pathwise, yielding the formula for all $t \ge
 0$ a.s.

---

### Conclusion

For all continuous local martingales $M, N \in \mathfrak{M}_{c,loc}$ and all $t
\ge 0$:

$$\boxed{M_t N_t - M_0 N_0 = \int_0^t M_s\, dN_s + \int_0^t N_s\, dM_s + \langle
 M, N\rangle_t.}$$

The first two terms are Itô stochastic integrals (continuous local martingales v
anishing at $0$), and $\langle M, N\rangle_t$ is the quadratic covariation (the
continuous predictable finite-variation process compensating the product into a
local martingale). This is the **Itô integration by parts formula** for continuo
us local martingales.
