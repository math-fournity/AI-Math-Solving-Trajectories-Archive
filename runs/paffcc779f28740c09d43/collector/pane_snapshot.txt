**Answer: Yes.** A deformation (mod $h^{n+1}$) of a Hopf algebra $A$ as a bialge
bra is automatically a Hopf algebra.

---

## Proof

Let $k$ be the base field. Set $R = k[h]/(h^{n+1})$, a local ring with maximal i
deal $\mathfrak{m} = (h)$ (nilpotent, since $h^{n+1}=0$) and residue field $R/\m
athfrak{m} \cong k$.

A deformation of $A$ as a bialgebra mod $h^{n+1}$ is, by definition, a bialgebra
 $B$ over $R$ that is free (or flat) as an $R$-module, together with an isomorph
ism of bialgebras
$$B \otimes_R k \;\cong\; A,$$
where $k = R/\mathfrak{m}$ is an $R$-algebra via $h \mapsto 0$.

We are given that $A$ is a Hopf algebra, i.e. $A$ admits an antipode $S_A : A \t
o A$. We must show $B$ admits an antipode $S_B : B \to B$.

### Step 1. The convolution algebra

For any bialgebra $B$ over a commutative ring $R$, the set $\operatorname{Hom}_R
(B, B)$ carries the structure of an associative $R$-algebra under **convolution*
*:
$$(f * g)(b) = \mu \circ (f \otimes g) \circ \Delta(b),$$
with unit $\eta \circ \varepsilon$ (where $\eta: R \to B$ is the unit and $\vare
psilon: B \to R$ is the counit). An antipode on $B$ is precisely a convolution i
nverse of $\operatorname{id}_B$ in this algebra:
$$S * \operatorname{id} = \eta \circ \varepsilon = \operatorname{id} * S.$$

### Step 2. Reduction modulo $\mathfrak{m}$

Since $B$ is free over $R$, reduction modulo $\mathfrak{m}$ gives a surjective a
lgebra homomorphism
$$\rho: \operatorname{Hom}_R(B, B) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}_k(B \otime
s_R k,\; B \otimes_R k) = \operatorname{Hom}_k(A, A),$$
which respects convolution (it is a morphism of convolution algebras, since $\De
lta$, $\mu$, $\varepsilon$, $\eta$ all reduce correctly). Its kernel is $\mathfr
ak{m}\operatorname{Hom}_R(B,B)$.

Under $\rho$, the element $\operatorname{id}_B \in \operatorname{Hom}_R(B,B)$ ma
ps to $\operatorname{id}_A \in \operatorname{Hom}_k(A,A)$.

### Step 3. Invertibility lifts over a local ring

Since $A$ is a Hopf algebra, $\operatorname{id}_A$ is invertible in $\operatorna
me{Hom}_k(A,A)$, with inverse $S_A$:
$$S_A * \operatorname{id}_A = \eta_A \varepsilon_A = \operatorname{id}_A * S_A.$
$

We now use the standard lifting lemma for associative algebras over a local ring
:

> **Lemma.** Let $M$ be an associative (unital) $R$-algebra over a local ring $(
R, \mathfrak{m})$. An element $x \in M$ is invertible if and only if its image $
\bar{x} \in M/\mathfrak{m}M$ is invertible.

*Proof of Lemma.* The "only if" direction is immediate. For "if": suppose $\bar{
x}$ is invertible, so there is $\bar{y}$ with $\bar{x}\bar{y} = \bar{1}$. Lift $
\bar{y}$ to $y \in M$. Then $xy = 1 - r$ for some $r \in \mathfrak{m}M$. Since $
\mathfrak{m}$ is nilpotent (in our case $\mathfrak{m}^{n+1} = 0$), $r$ is nilpot
ent, so $1 - r$ is invertible with inverse $1 + r + r^2 + \cdots + r^{n}$ (a fin
ite sum). Then $x \cdot \bigl(y(1-r)^{-1}\bigr) = 1$, giving a right inverse of
$x$. A symmetric argument gives a left inverse, and the two coincide. $\square$

### Step 4. Conclusion

Applying the Lemma with $M = \operatorname{Hom}_R(B, B)$ (convolution algebra) a
nd $x = \operatorname{id}_B$: since $\rho(\operatorname{id}_B) = \operatorname{i
d}_A$ is invertible in $M/\mathfrak{m}M = \operatorname{Hom}_k(A,A)$, we conclud
e that $\operatorname{id}_B$ is invertible in $M$.

That is, there exists $S_B \in \operatorname{Hom}_R(B, B)$ with
$$S_B * \operatorname{id}_B = \eta_B \varepsilon_B = \operatorname{id}_B * S_B.$
$

Hence $B$ is a Hopf algebra over $R = k[h]/(h^{n+1})$. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** The argument uses only that $R$ is a local ring with nilpotent maxim
al ideal and that $B$ is free (so that reduction is well-behaved). The same proo
f shows: *any bialgebra over an Artinian local ring whose special fiber is a Hop
f algebra is itself a Hopf algebra.* In particular, deformations of a Hopf algeb
ra as a bialgebra (to any finite order in $h$, or even over $k[[h]]$) are automa
tically Hopf — the antipode deforms uniquely and requires no separate obstructio
n/compatibility computation.
