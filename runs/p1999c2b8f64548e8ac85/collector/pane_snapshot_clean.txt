## Proof

**Setup and interpretation.** Let $P \in \mathbb{Q}[X]$ be irreducible of degree
 $n \geq 3$, $\mathbb{L}$ its splitting field, and $G = \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{L}/
\mathbb{Q})$ a solvable group. Since $G$ is solvable, it admits a composition se
ries with cyclic quotients of prime order:
$$\{1\} = G_r \trianglelefteq G_{r-1} \trianglelefteq \cdots \trianglelefteq G_1
 \trianglelefteq G_0 = G,$$
where each $G_i / G_{i+1}$ is cyclic of prime order $p_i$.

By the Galois correspondence, this subnormal series yields a tower of fields
$$\mathbb{Q} = \mathbb{K}_0 \subseteq \mathbb{K}_1 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq \m
athbb{K}_r = \mathbb{L},$$
where $\mathbb{K}_i = \mathbb{L}^{G_i}$ (the fixed field of $G_i$), and
$$[\mathbb{K}_{i+1} : \mathbb{K}_i] = |G_i / G_{i+1}| = p_i.$$

The total degree is
$$D = [\mathbb{K}_r : \mathbb{Q}] = \prod_{i=0}^{r-1} p_i = |G|.$$

**Key bound.** Since $P$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, $G$ acts transitively
 (and faithfully) on the $n$ roots $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n$ of $P$ in $\math
bb{L}$. Faithfulness holds because $\mathbb{L}$ is generated by these roots: any
 automorphism fixing all roots fixes $\mathbb{L}$ pointwise. Therefore $G$ embed
s into $S_n$:
$$G \hookrightarrow S_n.$$

**Conclusion.** Since $G$ is a subgroup of $S_n$,
$$D = |G| \leq |S_n| = n!.$$

Setting $f(n) = n!$ (which depends only on $n$), we have $D \leq f(n)$ for every
 irreducible polynomial of degree $n$ with solvable Galois group.

**Remark.** The bound $n!$ is in fact sharp in the sense that for $n \geq 3$, th
e symmetric group $S_n$ itself can be realized as a Galois group over $\mathbb{Q
}$ (by Hilbert's irreducibility theorem, e.g., the generic polynomial), though $
S_n$ is not solvable for $n \geq 5$. For solvable $G$, the maximal solvable subg
roup of $S_n$ has order strictly less than $n!$ for $n \geq 5$, but $n!$ remains
 a valid universal bound depending only on $n$. The essential point is that the
faithful permutation representation on $n$ elements constrains $|G|$ purely in t
erms of $n$, independent of the specific polynomial.
