Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum number of edges in a graph o
n 10 vertices where no two triangles share a vertex (all triangles are pairwise
vertex-disjoint).

## Proof

**Key Observation.** The condition "does not contain two triangles with a common
 vertex" means all triangles in the graph must be pairwise vertex-disjoint. In p
articular, a triangle-free graph (zero triangles) satisfies this condition vacuo
usly.

**Lower bound: $f(10) \geq 25$.**

The complete bipartite graph $K_{5,5}$ has 10 vertices and 25 edges, and is tria
ngle-free (bipartite graphs contain no odd cycles). Since it has no triangles at
 all, it trivially satisfies the condition. So $f(10) \geq 25$.

**Upper bound: $f(10) \leq 25$.**

Let $G$ be a graph on 10 vertices satisfying the condition. Let $t$ be the numbe
r of triangles in $G$. Since all triangles are vertex-disjoint, they use $3t$ ve
rtices. Let $R$ be the set of vertices not in any triangle, with $|R| = 10 - 3t$
. Note $t \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ since $3t \leq 10$.

We partition all edges of $G$ into four disjoint categories and bound each:

**(a) Edges within triangles:** Each triangle contributes exactly 3 edges, and t
he vertex sets are disjoint, so this gives exactly $3t$ edges.

**(b) Edges within $R$:** The induced subgraph $G[R]$ is triangle-free (any tria
ngle in $G[R]$ would mean those vertices are in a triangle, contradicting the de
finition of $R$). By Mantel's theorem, this contributes at most $\lfloor |R|^2/4
 \rfloor$ edges.

**(c) Edges between $R$ and the triangle vertices:** For each triangle $T_i$ and
 each vertex $r \in R$, vertex $r$ is adjacent to **at most 1** vertex of $T_i$.
 Indeed, if $r$ were adjacent to two vertices $a_1, a_2 \in T_i$, then $\{r, a_1
, a_2\}$ would form a triangle (since $(a_1, a_2)$ is an edge of $T_i$), sharing
 vertices $a_1, a_2$ with $T_i$ — a contradiction. Summing over all $t$ triangle
s, this gives at most $t|R|$ edges.

**(d) Edges between different triangles $T_i$ and $T_j$:** For any pair of trian
gles, each vertex of one triangle is adjacent to at most 1 vertex of the other (
by the same pair-constraint argument: if $b_k$ were adjacent to both $a_1$ and $
a_2$ in $T_i$, then $\{b_k, a_1, a_2\}$ is a triangle sharing vertices with $T_i
$). So the cross-edges form a matching of size at most 3 per pair. Over $\binom{
t}{2}$ pairs, this gives at most $3\binom{t}{2}$ edges.

**Combining:** Every edge belongs to exactly one category, so:
$$|E(G)| \leq 3t + \left\lfloor \frac{|R|^2}{4} \right\rfloor + t|R| + 3\binom{t
}{2}$$

Evaluating for each valid $t$ (with $|R| = 10 - 3t$):

| $t$ | $|R|$ | $3t$ | $\lfloor R^2/4\rfloor$ | $t|R|$ | $3\binom{t}{2}$ | **Tot
al** |
|-----|-------|------|------------------------|--------|-----------------|------
-----|
| 0   | 10    | 0    | 25                     | 0      | 0               | **25*
*    |
| 1   | 7     | 3    | 12                     | 7      | 0               | 22
     |
| 2   | 4     | 6    | 4                      | 8      | 3               | 21
     |
| 3   | 1     | 9    | 0                      | 3      | 9               | 21
     |

The maximum over all $t$ is **25**, achieved at $t = 0$.

**Conclusion.** Since $f(10) \geq 25$ (by $K_{5,5}$) and $f(10) \leq 25$ (by the
 upper bound), we conclude:

$$\boxed{f(10) = 25}$$
