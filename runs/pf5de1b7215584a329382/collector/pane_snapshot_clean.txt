## Proof

**Setup.** Let $\mathbb{T} = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ be the circle. The irrationa
l rotation is $f_\theta(x) = x + \theta \pmod{1}$ with $\theta \notin \mathbb{Q}
$. By induction on the given definition, $f^n_\theta(x) = x + n\theta \pmod{1}$
for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$ (where $\mathbb{N}$ includes $0$, as signaled by th
e explicit definition of $f^0_\theta$).

**All iterates are distinct.** Suppose $f^n_\theta = f^m_\theta$ for some $n, m
\in \mathbb{N}$ with $n \neq m$. Then $x + n\theta \equiv x + m\theta \pmod{1}$
for all $x$, so $(n - m)\theta \in \mathbb{Z}$. Since $n - m$ is a nonzero integ
er and $\theta$ is irrational, $(n-m)\theta$ is irrational, hence not an integer
 — contradiction. Therefore the map $n \mapsto f^n_\theta$ is injective, and $F
= \{f^n_\theta : n \in \mathbb{N}\}$ is in bijection with $\mathbb{N}$.

**Computing $F'$.** By definition,
$$F' = \{f_\theta \circ g : g \in F\} = \{f_\theta \circ f^n_\theta : n \in \mat
hbb{N}\}.$$
Since $f_\theta \circ f^n_\theta = f^{n+1}_\theta$ (by the recursive definition
$f^{n+1}_\theta = f^n_\theta \circ f_\theta$ and commutativity of addition on $\
mathbb{T}$, or directly: $(f_\theta \circ f^n_\theta)(x) = f^n_\theta(x) + \thet
a = x + n\theta + \theta = x + (n+1)\theta = f^{n+1}_\theta(x)$), we get
$$F' = \{f^{n+1}_\theta : n \in \mathbb{N}\} = \{f^k_\theta : k \in \mathbb{N},\
; k \geq 1\}.$$

**Comparing $F$ and $F'$.** We have
$$F = \{f^0_\theta, f^1_\theta, f^2_\theta, \ldots\}, \qquad F' = \{f^1_\theta,
f^2_\theta, f^3_\theta, \ldots\}.$$

Clearly $F' \subseteq F$. The question is whether $f^0_\theta \in F'$, i.e., whe
ther $f^0_\theta = f^k_\theta$ for some $k \geq 1$. This would require $k\theta
\in \mathbb{Z}$ for some positive integer $k$. But $\theta$ is irrational, so $k
\theta$ is irrational for every $k \geq 1$, hence $k\theta \notin \mathbb{Z}$. T
herefore $f^0_\theta \notin F'$.

**Conclusion.** The identity map $f^0_\theta = \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{T}}$ belongs
 to $F$ but not to $F'$. Hence
$$F' \subsetneq F, \qquad \text{so } F \neq F'.$$

The irrationality of $\theta$ is essential: it guarantees that no positive itera
te of $f_\theta$ returns to the identity, so the "shift by one" in passing from
$F$ to $F'$ permanently loses the element $f^0_\theta$ with no compensation.
