## Proof

We compute \(E[B_t \mid B_t^2]\) for a standard Brownian motion \(B_t\) at time
\(t \geq 0\).

**Key observation (symmetry).** For every \(t \geq 0\), the random variable \(B_
t\) is Gaussian with mean \(0\) and variance \(t\), hence its distribution is sy
mmetric about the origin:

\[
B_t \stackrel{d}{=} -B_t.
\]

Moreover, the \(\sigma\)-algebra \(\sigma(B_t^2)\) is **invariant** under the si
gn flip \(B_t \mapsto -B_t\), since \((-B_t)^2 = B_t^2\). In other words, every
event measurable with respect to \(B_t^2\) is unchanged when \(B_t\) is replaced
 by \(-B_t\).

**Formal argument.** Let \(X = B_t\) and \(Y = B_t^2\). Consider the transformat
ion \(\varphi(X) = -X\). This transformation leaves \(Y = X^2\) unchanged, so it
 preserves the \(\sigma\)-algebra \(\sigma(Y)\). Therefore, for any bounded meas
urable test function \(g\),

\[
E\bigl[X\, g(Y)\bigr] = E\bigl[(-X)\, g(Y)\bigr] = -E\bigl[X\, g(Y)\bigr],
\]

where the first equality uses the fact that \((X,Y) \stackrel{d}{=} (-X, Y)\) (b
oth sides have the same joint law, since \(X\) is symmetric and \(Y\) depends on
ly on \(|X|\)). It follows that

\[
E\bigl[X\, g(Y)\bigr] = 0
\]

for every bounded measurable \(g\). By the defining characterization of conditio
nal expectation, this means

\[
E[B_t \mid B_t^2] = 0 \quad \text{a.s.}
\]

**Intuition.** Knowing the value of \(B_t^2\) tells us \(|B_t|\) but gives no in
formation about the sign of \(B_t\). Since the sign is equally likely to be \(+\
) or \(-\) (by the symmetry of the Gaussian law about \(0\)), the conditional me
an of \(B_t\) given \(B_t^2\) is \(0\).

**Verification via density (optional).** The joint density of \((B_t, B_t^2)\) c
an be derived from \(B_t \sim N(0,t)\). Conditioning on \(B_t^2 = s\) (with \(s
\geq 0\)), the conditional distribution of \(B_t\) puts equal mass \(\tfrac{1}{2
}\) at \(+\sqrt{s}\) and \(-\sqrt{s}\):

\[
\mathcal{L}(B_t \mid B_t^2 = s) = \tfrac{1}{2}\delta_{+\sqrt{s}} + \tfrac{1}{2}\
delta_{-\sqrt{s}},
\]

so

\[
E[B_t \mid B_t^2 = s] = \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{s} + \tfrac{1}{2}(-\sqrt{s}) = 0.
\]

This holds for every \(s \geq 0\), confirming

\[
\boxed{E[B_t \mid B_t^2] = 0 \quad \text{a.s.}}
\]
