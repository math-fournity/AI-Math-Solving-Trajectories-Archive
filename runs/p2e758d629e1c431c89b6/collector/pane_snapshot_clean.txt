**Answer: No.** Weak-star convergence does not imply convergence of total variat
ion norms.

## Counterexample

Let $X = [0,1]$. Since $X$ is compact, $C_0(X) = C(X)$.

Define the sequence of regular signed Borel measures
$$\mu_n := \delta_0 - \delta_{1/n}, \qquad n \ge 1,$$
where $\delta_a$ denotes the Dirac point mass at $a$.

### Step 1. Weak-star convergence $\mu_n \to 0$

Set $\mu := 0$. For any $f \in C([0,1])$,
$$\int_{[0,1]} f \, d\mu_n = f(0) - f(1/n).$$
By continuity of $f$ at $0$, we have $f(1/n) \to f(0)$, hence
$$f(0) - f(1/n) \to 0 = \int_{[0,1]} f \, d\mu.$$
Therefore $\mu_n \to \mu = 0$ in the weak-star topology.

### Step 2. Total variation norms do not converge

The measures $\delta_0$ and $\delta_{1/n}$ are mutually singular (concentrated o
n disjoint singletons), so the total variation measure of $\mu_n$ is
$$|\mu_n| = |\delta_0 - \delta_{1/n}| = \delta_0 + \delta_{1/n},$$
and consequently
$$|\mu_n|(X) = |\mu_n|([0,1]) = 1 + 1 = 2 \qquad \text{for every } n.$$

On the other hand, the limit measure is $\mu = 0$, so
$$|\mu|(X) = 0.$$

Thus
$$|\mu_n|(X) = 2 \;\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\; 2 \neq 0 = |\mu|(X).$$

### Conclusion

We have exhibited a sequence of regular signed Borel measures $\mu_n = \delta_0
- \delta_{1/n}$ on $[0,1]$ that converges weak-star to $\mu = 0$, yet $|\mu_n|(X
) = 2$ for all $n$ while $|\mu|(X) = 0$. Hence weak-star convergence does **not*
* imply convergence of total variation norms.

(Intuitively: weak-star convergence only controls the pairing with continuous fu
nctions, so it cannot detect the "cancellation" between two nearby point masses
of opposite sign that the total variation norm counts with full weight.)
