## Proof

**Step 1: A generating function for harmonic numbers.**

It is well known (and follows from differentiating $-\ln(1-x) = \sum_{k=1}^\inft
y \frac{x^k}{k}$ and multiplying by $\frac{1}{1-x} = \sum_{m=0}^\infty x^m$) tha
t for $|x| < 1$:

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty H_n\, x^n = -\frac{\ln(1-x)}{1-x}.$$

**Step 2: Integrate to introduce the factor $\frac{1}{n+1}$.**

Integrating term-by-term from $0$ to $x$ (valid for $|x|<1$):

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{H_n}{n+1}\, x^{n+1} = \int_0^x \frac{-\ln(1-t)}{1-t}\,
 dt.$$

For the right-hand side, substitute $u = 1-t$, $du = -dt$:

$$\int_0^x \frac{-\ln(1-t)}{1-t}\, dt = \int_{1}^{1-x} \frac{\ln u}{u}\, du = \l
eft[\frac{(\ln u)^2}{2}\right]_{1}^{1-x} = \frac{\bigl(\ln(1-x)\bigr)^2}{2}.$$

Therefore, for $|x| < 1$ (and by continuity, extendable to $x \in [-1, 1)$):

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{H_n}{n+1}\, x^{n+1} = \frac{\bigl(\ln(1-x)\bigr)^2}{2}
. \tag{$\star$}$$

**Step 3: Evaluate at $x = -1$.**

The series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{H_n}{n+1}(-1)^{n+1}$ converges by the altern
ating series test, since $H_n/(n+1)$ is positive and eventually decreasing to $0
$ (as $H_n \sim \ln n$, so $H_n/(n+1) \to 0$). The right-hand side of $(\star)$,
 namely $\frac{(\ln(1-x))^2}{2}$, is continuous at $x = -1$ with value $\frac{(\
ln 2)^2}{2}$.

By **Abel's theorem** (the power series converges at the boundary point $x=-1$ t
o the continuous limit of its sum function):

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{H_n}{n+1}\,(-1)^{n+1} = \frac{(\ln 2)^2}{2}.$$

**Step 4: Multiply by 2.**

$$S = 2\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n+1}\, H_n = 2 \cdot \frac{(\ln 2)^2}
{2} = (\ln 2)^2.$$
