## Proof

**Yes**, such a continuous function exists.

**Construction.** Since $\Omega$ is open, its complement $F := \mathbb{R}^N \set
minus \Omega$ is closed. Define
$$g(x) := d(x, F) = \inf_{y \in F} \|x - y\|.$$

**Claim 1: $g$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}^N$.**

For any $x, x' \in \mathbb{R}^N$ and any $y \in F$,
$$\|x - y\| \le \|x - x'\| + \|x' - y\|.$$
Taking the infimum over $y \in F$ on the right gives $g(x) \le \|x - x'\| + g(x'
)$, so $g(x) - g(x') \le \|x - x'\|$. By symmetry, $|g(x) - g(x')| \le \|x - x'\
|$, proving $g$ is Lipschitz (hence continuous).

**Claim 2: $g(x) = 0 \iff x \in F$.**

If $x \in F$, then $x \in F$ gives $g(x) \le \|x - x\| = 0$, so $g(x) = 0$.

Conversely, if $g(x) = 0$, there exists a sequence $(y_n) \subset F$ with $\|x -
 y_n\| \to 0$, i.e., $y_n \to x$. Since $F$ is closed, $x \in F$.

**Claim 3: $\Omega = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^N : g(x) > 0\}$.**

For any $x \in \mathbb{R}^N$:
$$x \in \Omega \iff x \notin F \iff g(x) \neq 0 \iff g(x) > 0,$$
where the last equivalence uses $g(x) \ge 0$ always (distance is non-negative),
and Claim 2.

Thus $g = d(\cdot, \mathbb{R}^N \setminus \Omega)$ is a continuous function $\ma
thbb{R}^N \to \mathbb{R}$ with $\Omega = \{x : g(x) > 0\}$. $\blacksquare$
