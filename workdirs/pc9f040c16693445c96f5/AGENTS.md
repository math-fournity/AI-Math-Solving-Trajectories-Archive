# Problem

A specialized circular irrigation zone is monitored by five sensor stations positioned at points $A, B, C, D$, and $E$ along its perimeter. To optimize water distribution, researchers have mapped the straight-line distances between these stations. The distance between station $A$ and station $B$ is exactly 14 decameters, while the distance from station $B$ to station $E$ is 10 decameters.

The layout of the remaining stations is highly symmetrical: the pipeline segments $BC$, $CD$, and $DE$ are all of equal length. During a survey of the land area covered by these sensors, surveyors determined that the total area of the convex pentagonal region $ABCDE$ is exactly three times the area of the triangular plot defined by stations $A, C$, and $D$.

Based on these specific geometric constraints, there are only two possible values for the radius of the circular irrigation zone. If the sum of these two possible radii is expressed as $\sqrt{n}$ for some positive integer $n$, what is the value of $n$?
