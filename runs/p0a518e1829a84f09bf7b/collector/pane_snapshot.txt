Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum number of Online beacons (ex
cluding the all-Offline state) achievable by flipping triangle toggles on a hexa
gonal tiling where 6 triangles meet at each beacon.

## Proof

**Setup and 3-coloring.**

The floor is a hexagonal tiling refined into triangles. Each hexagon is divided
into 6 triangles by connecting its center to its 6 vertices. The beacon set $V$
consists of two types of points: the original hexagonal tiling vertices $V_h$ an
d the hexagon centers $F_h$. Every triangle has exactly one center vertex and tw
o adjacent original vertices. At every beacon, exactly 6 triangles converge.

The hexagonal lattice is bipartite, so we 2-color $V_h$ with colors $A$ and $B$.
 Assign color $C$ to all centers in $F_h$. Since each triangle $(c, v_i, v_{i+1}
)$ has $c \in F_h$ (color $C$) and $v_i, v_{i+1} \in V_h$ adjacent (one color $A
$, one color $B$), **every triangle contains one vertex of each color**.

**Parity invariant.**

Let $n_A, n_B, n_C$ denote the number of Online beacons of each color. Flipping
any triangle toggles exactly one beacon of each color, so each of $n_A, n_B, n_C
$ changes parity simultaneously. Starting from all-Offline ($n_A \equiv n_B \equ
iv n_C \equiv 0$), after any sequence of flips:
$$n_A \equiv n_B \equiv n_C \pmod{2}.$$

**Weight 1 is impossible.** A single Online beacon of color $k$ gives $n_k = 1$
and the others $0$, violating $n_A \equiv n_B \equiv n_C$.

**Weight 2 is achievable.** Take two adjacent hexagons sharing an edge $(v_1, v_
2)$ with $v_1, v_2 \in V_h$. Let $c_1, c_2$ be their respective centers. The two
 triangles sharing this edge are:
- $T_1 = (c_1, v_1, v_2)$ in hexagon 1,
- $T_2 = (c_2, v_1, v_2)$ in hexagon 2.

Flip both toggles. The toggled vertices are $c_1, v_1, v_2, c_2, v_1, v_2$. Modu
lo 2, $v_1$ and $v_2$ each appear twice and cancel, leaving exactly $\{c_1, c_2\
}$ — two beacons of color $C$ Online. This configuration has $n_A = n_B = n_C =
0$, satisfying the invariant. **Weight 2 is achieved.**

**The parity invariant is the only constraint.** To confirm no other obstruction
 exists, I verify the cokernel of the toggle map $\varphi: \mathbb{F}_2^{\triang
le} \to \mathbb{F}_2^V$. A function $g: V \to \mathbb{F}_2$ lies in the cokernel
 iff $g(a)+g(b)+g(c)=0$ for every triangle $\{a,b,c\}$, i.e., $g(c_{\text{center
}}) = g(v_i)+g(v_{i+1})$.

From two adjacent triangles in one hexagon sharing edge $(c, v_2)$: $g(v_1)+g(v_
2) = g(c) = g(v_2)+g(v_3)$, so $g(v_1)=g(v_3)$. Since $v_1, v_3$ are same-color
vertices at graph-distance 2, and the distance-2 graph of each bipartite part of
 the hexagonal lattice is connected, $g$ is constant on each color class: $g_A,
g_B, g_C$ with $g_C = g_A + g_B$. This gives a **2-dimensional** cokernel, so th
e image of $\varphi$ has codimension exactly 2, with the sole constraints $n_A \
equiv n_B \equiv n_C \pmod{2}$.

**Conclusion.** Weight 0 is the excluded trivial state. Weight 1 is forbidden by
 the parity invariant. Weight 2 is achievable. Therefore:

$$L = \boxed{2}$$
