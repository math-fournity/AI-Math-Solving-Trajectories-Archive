Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the four given congruences
uniquely determine $(w,x,y,z)$ up to a natural symmetry.

**Identifying the symmetry.** The data consists of $e_1 = wy+xz \pmod{p+q}$, $e_
2 = wz+xy \pmod{p+q}$, $e_3 = wy \pmod{q}$, and $e_4 = xz \pmod{p}$. The map $\s
igma: (w,x,y,z) \mapsto (y,z,w,x)$ (swapping $w\leftrightarrow y$ and $x\leftrig
htarrow z$) preserves all four: $e_1\mapsto yw+zx=e_1$, $e_2\mapsto yx+zw=e_2$,
$e_3\mapsto yw=e_3$, $e_4\mapsto zx=e_4$. This is the only non-trivial permutati
on of variables that universally preserves the data (since $e_3$ is reduced mod
$q$ while $e_4$ is reduced mod $p\neq q$, they cannot be swapped).

**What the data determines.** From $e_1 \bmod (p+q)$ and $e_3 = wy \bmod q$, we
get $xz \bmod q = (e_1 \bmod q - e_3) \bmod q$. From $e_1 \bmod (p+q)$ and $e_4
= xz \bmod p$, we get $wy \bmod p = (e_1 \bmod p - e_4) \bmod p$. Since $\gcd(p,
q)=1$, the Chinese Remainder Theorem recovers $wy \bmod{pq}$ and $xz \bmod{pq}$.
 Because $0\le wy, xz < p^2 < pq$ (using $p<q$), we recover $wy$ and $xz$ **exac
tly**. The remaining information is just $B = wz+xy \pmod{p+q}$.

**Counterexample.** Take $p=5,\, q=7$ (coprime, $5<7<10$). Then $w,x,y,z \in \{0
,1,2,3,4\}$, $p+q=12$, $pq=35$, $p^2=25<35$.

**Solution 1:** $(w,x,y,z)=(1,1,4,4)$.
- $wy+xz = 4+4=8$, so $(wy+xz)\bmod 12 = 8$.
- $wz+xy = 4+4=8$, so $(wz+xy)\bmod 12 = 8$.
- $wy \bmod q = 4 \bmod 7 = 4$.
- $xz \bmod p = 4 \bmod 5 = 4$.

**Solution 2:** $(w,x,y,z)=(2,2,2,2)$.
- $wy+xz = 4+4=8$, so $(wy+xz)\bmod 12 = 8$.
- $wz+xy = 4+4=8$, so $(wz+xy)\bmod 12 = 8$.
- $wy \bmod q = 4 \bmod 7 = 4$.
- $xz \bmod p = 4 \bmod 5 = 4$.

Both solutions yield **identical** data: $(8, 8, 4, 4)$.

**Not related by symmetry.** The symmetry $\sigma$ maps $(1,1,4,4)\mapsto(4,4,1,
1)$, which is not $(2,2,2,2)$. No permutation of the variables can transform $(1
,1,4,4)$ into $(2,2,2,2)$, since permutations preserve the multiset of values $\
{1,1,4,4\} \neq \{2,2,2,2\}$.

**Why this happens.** Both solutions have $wy = xz = 4$ (recovered exactly via C
RT). The value $4$ admits three factorizations with factors $< 5$: $(1,4)$, $(2,
2)$, $(4,1)$. Choosing the same factorization for both $wy$ and $xz$ always give
s $B = 8 \equiv 8 \pmod{12}$, producing three symmetry-orbits: $\{(1,1,4,4),(4,4
,1,1)\}$, $\{(2,2,2,2)\}$, and $\{(1,1,4,4)\text{'s swap}\}$. The constraint $B
\bmod 12$ cannot distinguish the orbit of $(1,1,4,4)$ from the fixed-point orbit
 of $(2,2,2,2)$.

**Conclusion.** The four congruences do **not** uniquely determine $w,x,y,z$ up
to the symmetry $(w,x,y,z)\mapsto(y,z,w,x)$. The data determines $wy$ and $xz$ e
xactly, but multiple non-symmetry-related factorizations of these products can y
ield the same value of $(wz+xy)\bmod(p+q)$, as demonstrated by the explicit coun
terexample above.
