## Proof

We wish to evaluate $\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{g(n)}{10^n}$, where $g(n)$ is the l
ast $n$ digits of $(10^n-1)^{10^n-1}$, i.e., $g(n) = (10^n-1)^{10^n-1} \bmod 10^
n$.

**Key observation.** Since $10^n - 1 \equiv -1 \pmod{10^n}$, we have
$$(10^n-1)^{10^n-1} \equiv (-1)^{10^n-1} \pmod{10^n}.$$

**Parity of the exponent.** $10^n$ is even for all $n \geq 1$, so $10^n - 1$ is
**odd**. Therefore
$$(-1)^{10^n-1} = -1 \equiv 10^n - 1 \pmod{10^n}.$$

**Conclusion.** Thus $g(n) = 10^n - 1$ for every $n \geq 1$, and
$$\frac{g(n)}{10^n} = \frac{10^n - 1}{10^n} = 1 - \frac{1}{10^n} \xrightarrow{n\
to\infty} 1.$$

**Verification for small cases.**
- $n=1$: $9^9 = 387420489$, last digit $= 9 = 10^1-1$. ✓
- $n=2$: $99 \equiv -1 \pmod{100}$, $99^{99}\equiv(-1)^{99}=-1\equiv 99\pmod{100
}$, so $g(2)=99=10^2-1$. ✓
- $n=3$: $999\equiv -1\pmod{1000}$, $999^{999}\equiv(-1)^{999}=-1\equiv 999\pmod
{1000}$, so $g(3)=999=10^3-1$. ✓

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{g(n)}{10^n} = 1}$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
