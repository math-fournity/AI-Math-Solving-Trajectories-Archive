## Proof

We use Cauchy's coefficient estimate. Since $f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n z^n$ is
entire (the bound $|f(z)|\le Me^{|z|}$ forces the power series to converge every
where), for any $R>0$,

$$
|a_n|=\left|\frac{f^{(n)}(0)}{n!}\right|\le \frac{\max_{|z|=R}|f(z)|}{R^n}\le \f
rac{Me^{R}}{R^n}.
$$

This holds for every $R>0$, so we minimize the right-hand side over $R$. Let $g(
R)=\frac{Me^R}{R^n}$. Then

$$
\frac{g'(R)}{g(R)}=1-\frac{n}{R},
$$

which vanishes at $R=n$. Since $g(R)\to\infty$ as $R\to 0^+$ and as $R\to\infty$
, the unique critical point $R=n$ is the global minimum. Substituting,

$$
|a_n|\le \frac{Me^n}{n^n}\qquad(n\ge 1).
$$

Therefore

$$
\left|\frac{n^n}{3^n}\,a_n\right|\le \frac{n^n}{3^n}\cdot\frac{Me^n}{n^n}=M\left
(\frac{e}{3}\right)^{\!n}.
$$

Since $e<3$, we have $\frac{e}{3}<1$, and consequently

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty} M\left(\frac{e}{3}\right)^{\!n}=0.
$$

By the squeeze theorem,

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n^n}{3^n}\,a_n=0.
$$
