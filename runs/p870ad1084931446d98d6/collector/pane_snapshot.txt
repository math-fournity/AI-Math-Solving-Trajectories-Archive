## Proof

**Setup.** Let $\mathcal{F}$ denote the field of sets of integers of period four
, i.e., the collection of all $S \subseteq \mathbb{Z}$ satisfying $S = S + 4$ (e
quivalently, $n \in S \iff n+4 \in S$). This is a Boolean algebra under the usua
l set operations (union, intersection, complement relative to $\mathbb{Z}$), wit
h $\mathbf{0} = \emptyset$ and $\mathbf{1} = \mathbb{Z}$.

**The atoms.** Define the four residue classes modulo 4:
$$C_r = \{n \in \mathbb{Z} : n \equiv r \pmod 4\} = 4\mathbb{Z} + r, \quad r \in
 \{0,1,2,3\}.$$

Each $C_r$ belongs to $\mathcal{F}$ since $C_r + 4 = C_r$. I claim these four se
ts are exactly the atoms of $\mathcal{F}$.

*Each $C_r$ is an atom.* $C_r$ is non-empty, so it is nonzero in $\mathcal{F}$.
Suppose $T \in \mathcal{F}$ with $T \subseteq C_r$ and $T \neq \emptyset$. Pick
any $n \in T$. Since $T = T+4$, we have $n + 4k \in T$ for every $k \in \mathbb{
Z}$. But $\{n + 4k : k \in \mathbb{Z}\} = C_r$ (since $n \equiv r \pmod 4$). Hen
ce $C_r \subseteq T$, so $T = C_r$. Therefore $C_r$ has no proper nonempty subse
t within $\mathcal{F}$; it is an atom.

*There are no other atoms.* Let $A \in \mathcal{F}$ be any atom. Since $A \neq \
emptyset$, pick $n \in A$. By periodicity, $C_{n \bmod 4} \subseteq A$. But $C_{
n \bmod 4}$ is itself a nonzero element of $\mathcal{F}$ contained in $A$, and $
A$ is an atom, so $A = C_{n \bmod 4}$. Thus every atom is one of the $C_r$.

**Every element is a join of atoms.** Let $S \in \mathcal{F}$, $S \neq \emptyset
$. Define
$$I = \{r \in \{0,1,2,3\} : C_r \subseteq S\}.$$

I claim $S = \bigcup_{r \in I} C_r$.

*($\supseteq$)* Each $C_r$ with $r \in I$ is contained in $S$ by definition, so
the union is contained in $S$.

*($\subseteq$)* Let $n \in S$ and let $r = n \bmod 4$. By periodicity ($S = S+4$
), every element of $C_r$ has the form $n + 4k$ and lies in $S$. Hence $C_r \sub
seteq S$, i.e., $r \in I$. Therefore $n \in C_r \subseteq \bigcup_{r \in I} C_r$
.

So $S = \bigcup_{r \in I} C_r$, a finite join of atoms. (When $S = \emptyset$, $
I = \emptyset$ and the empty join gives $\emptyset = S$.)

**Conclusion.** The atoms of the Boolean algebra of period-four sets of integers
 are the four residue classes $C_0, C_1, C_2, C_3$ modulo 4. Every element of th
e algebra is uniquely expressible as the join of the atoms it contains, namely $
S = \bigcup\{C_r : C_r \subseteq S\}$. $\blacksquare$
