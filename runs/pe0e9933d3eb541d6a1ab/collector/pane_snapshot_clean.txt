## Solution

Convert to polar coordinates: $x = r\cos\theta$, $y = r\sin\theta$, $x^2+y^2 = r
^2$, $\sqrt{x^2+y^2}=r$, $dA = r\,dr\,d\theta$.

**Region.** The constraint $x \le x^2+y^2 \le 2x$ becomes
$$r\cos\theta \le r^2 \le 2r\cos\theta.$$
For $r>0$, divide by $r$:
$$\cos\theta \le r \le 2\cos\theta.$$
This requires $\cos\theta \ge 0$, i.e. $\theta\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$. At the endpoin
ts $\cos\theta=0$ the annulus collapses to $r=0$, contributing nothing.

**Integral.**
$$\iint_D \sqrt{x^2+y^2}\,dA = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\int_{\cos\theta}^{2\cos\the
ta} r\cdot r\,dr\,d\theta = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\int_{\cos\theta}^{2\cos\theta}
 r^2\,dr\,d\theta.$$

Inner integral:
$$\int_{\cos\theta}^{2\cos\theta} r^2\,dr = \frac{r^3}{3}\Bigg|_{\cos\theta}^{2\
cos\theta} = \frac{8\cos^3\theta - \cos^3\theta}{3} = \frac{7\cos^3\theta}{3}.$$

Outer integral:
$$\frac{7}{3}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos^3\theta\,d\theta.$$

By evenness ($\cos^3\theta$ is even):
$$= \frac{14}{3}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos^3\theta\,d\theta.$$

Using the standard value $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos^3\theta\,d\theta = \
frac{2}{3}$ (via $\cos^3\theta = \frac{1}{4}(3\cos\theta+\cos 3\theta)$, or the
Wallis reduction):

$$= \frac{14}{3}\cdot\frac{2}{3} = \frac{28}{9}.$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{\dfrac{28}{9}}$

### PROOF COMPLETE
