Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every non-Voronoi-relevant
lattice vector has a strictly shorter Voronoi relevant vector.

## Proof

**Key Claim:** The shortest nonzero vector of any lattice $L$ is always Voronoi
relevant.

**Proof of Claim.** Let $v^*$ be a shortest nonzero vector of $L$. The Voronoi c
ell is
$$\mathcal{V} = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : \langle x, \mu \rangle \leq \|\mu\|^2/2 \
text{ for all } \mu \in L \setminus \{0\}\}.$$

Consider the point $p = v^*/2$, which lies on the hyperplane $H_{v^*} = \{x : \l
angle x, v^* \rangle = \|v^*\|^2/2\}$.

**Step 1: $p \in \mathcal{V}$.** For any $\mu \in L \setminus \{0\}$, we need $\
langle v^*/2, \mu \rangle \leq \|\mu\|^2/2$, i.e., $\langle v^*, \mu \rangle \le
q \|\mu\|^2$. By Cauchy–Schwarz, $\langle v^*, \mu \rangle \leq \|v^*\|\|\mu\| \
leq \|\mu\|^2$ since $\|v^*\| \leq \|\mu\|$ (as $v^*$ is shortest). ✓

**Step 2: $p$ is in the relative interior of $H_{v^*} \cap \mathcal{V}$.** We sh
ow every constraint other than the one from $v^*$ is strict at $p$. For any $\mu
 \in L \setminus \{0, v^*\}$:

- If $\|\mu\| > \|v^*\|$: $\langle v^*, \mu \rangle \leq \|v^*\|\|\mu\| < \|\mu\
|^2$. ✓ (strict)
- If $\|\mu\| = \|v^*\|$, $\mu \neq v^*$: $\langle v^*, \mu \rangle \leq \|v^*\|
^2 = \|\mu\|^2$, with equality iff $\mu = v^*$ (Cauchy–Schwarz equality). Since
$\mu \neq v^*$, the inequality is strict. ✓

Since the only active constraint at $p$ is from $v^*$ itself, $p$ lies in the re
lative interior of $H_{v^*} \cap \mathcal{V}$. Therefore $H_{v^*} \cap \mathcal{
V}$ contains a neighborhood of $p$ in $H_{v^*}$, so it is $(n-1)$-dimensional. H
ence $v^*$ is Voronoi relevant. $\square$

**Main Theorem.** If $\lambda \in L \setminus \{0\}$ is not Voronoi relevant, th
en there exists a Voronoi relevant vector $v$ with $\|v\| < \|\lambda\|$.

**Proof.** Since $\lambda$ is not Voronoi relevant, $\lambda \neq 0$ (the hyperp
lane for $v = 0$ is all of $\mathbb{R}^n$, which intersects $\mathcal{V}$ in an
$n$-dimensional set, not $(n-1)$-dimensional). Let $v^*$ be a shortest nonzero v
ector of $L$. By the Claim, $v^*$ is Voronoi relevant. Since $v^*$ is the shorte
st nonzero vector and $\lambda \neq 0$ is not the shortest (because the shortest
 nonzero vector is relevant, but $\lambda$ is not), we have
$$\|v^*\| < \|\lambda\|.$$
Taking $v = v^*$ gives a Voronoi relevant vector with $\|v\| < \|\lambda\|$. $\s
quare$

**Answer: Yes.** Every non-Voronoi-relevant (nonzero) lattice vector is strictly
 longer than the shortest nonzero lattice vector, which is always Voronoi releva
nt.
