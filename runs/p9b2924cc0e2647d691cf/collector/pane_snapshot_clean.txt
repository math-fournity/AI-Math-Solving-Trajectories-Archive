## Proof

**Claim:** The series converges if and only if $a > 2$.

**Key idea:** Group subsets by their maximum element. For each $m \geq 0$, let $
\mathcal{F}_m = \{A \in \mathcal{F} : \max A = m\}$. There are exactly $2^m$ suc
h subsets (each element of $\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}$ is independently in or out, and
$m$ must be in). Then

$$S = \sum_{A \in \mathcal{F}} \frac{1}{\sum_{k \in A} a^k} = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty
} \sum_{A \in \mathcal{F}_m} \frac{1}{\sum_{k \in A} a^k}.$$

---

### Part 1: Convergence for $a > 2$

For any $A \in \mathcal{F}_m$, we have $\sum_{k \in A} a^k \geq a^m$ (since $m \
in A$). Therefore

$$\sum_{A \in \mathcal{F}_m} \frac{1}{\sum_{k \in A} a^k} \leq \frac{2^m}{a^m} =
 \left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^m.$$

Since $a > 2$, we have $2/a < 1$, so

$$S \leq \sum_{m=0}^{\infty} \left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^m = \frac{1}{1 - 2/a} < \i
nfty.$$

---

### Part 2: Divergence for $a = 2$

For $A \in \mathcal{F}_m$, we have $\sum_{k \in A} 2^k \leq \sum_{k=0}^{m} 2^k =
 2^{m+1} - 1$. Hence

$$\sum_{A \in \mathcal{F}_m} \frac{1}{\sum_{k \in A} 2^k} \geq \frac{2^m}{2^{m+1
} - 1} \xrightarrow{m \to \infty} \frac{1}{2} > 0.$$

Since the summands (grouped contributions) are bounded below by a positive const
ant for large $m$, the series $S = \sum_m \sum_{A \in \mathcal{F}_m} \cdots$ div
erges.

---

### Part 3: Divergence for $1 < a < 2$

For $A \in \mathcal{F}_m$, we have $\sum_{k \in A} a^k \leq \sum_{k=0}^{m} a^k =
 \frac{a^{m+1} - 1}{a - 1} \leq \frac{a^{m+1}}{a-1}$. Hence

$$\sum_{A \in \mathcal{F}_m} \frac{1}{\sum_{k \in A} a^k} \geq 2^m \cdot \frac{a
-1}{a^{m+1}} = \frac{a-1}{a} \left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^m.$$

Since $a < 2$, we have $2/a > 1$, so $\sum_m (2/a)^m = \infty$, and thus $S$ div
erges.

---

### Part 4: Divergence for $a = 1$

When $a = 1$, $\sum_{k \in A} 1^k = |A|$. The series becomes $\sum_{A \in \mathc
al{F}} \frac{1}{|A|}$. For each $n \geq 1$, there are infinitely many subsets of
 size $n$ (e.g., $\{0,1,\ldots,n-2,j\}$ for each $j \geq n-1$), so the sum over
subsets of size $n$ alone is infinite. The series diverges.

---

### Part 5: Divergence for $0 < a < 1$

For the singleton $A = \{n\}$, the term is $\frac{1}{a^n}$. Since $0 < a < 1$, $
a^n \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$, so $\frac{1}{a^n} \to \infty$. The general term do
es not tend to zero, so the series diverges.

---

### Conclusion

The series converges if and only if $a > 2$.
