## Solution

We view $\mathbb{H}$ as a left $\mathbb{C}$-vector space via the standard embedd
ing $\mathbb{C}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}$, $a+bi\mapsto a+bi$ (with $i$ the quat
ernion $i$), where the $\mathbb{C}$-action is left multiplication. Then $\operat
orname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{H}$ denotes the algebra of $\mathbb{C}$-linear e
ndomorphisms of this space.

**Step 1: $\mathbb{H}$ as a $\mathbb{C}$-vector space has dimension 2.**

Every quaternion admits a unique decomposition
$$q = (a+bi) + (c+di)j = z + wj,\qquad z=a+bi,\; w=c+di\in\mathbb{C},$$
since $(c+di)j = cj + d(ij) = cj + dk$. Thus $\mathbb{H} = \mathbb{C}\cdot 1 \op
lus \mathbb{C}\cdot j$.

Both summands are $\mathbb{C}$-submodules under left multiplication: for $\lambd
a\in\mathbb{C}$, $\lambda\cdot 1 = \lambda\in\mathbb{C}$ and $\lambda\cdot j = \
lambda j\in\mathbb{C}j$. They intersect trivially because an element of $\mathbb
{C}\cdot 1$ has only $1,i$-components while a nonzero element of $\mathbb{C}\cdo
t j$ has a $j$-component. Hence
$$\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{H} = 2,$$
with $\mathbb{C}$-basis $\{1, j\}$.

**Step 2: Compute $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{H}$.**

A $\mathbb{C}$-linear endomorphism is determined by the images of the two basis
vectors $1$ and $j$, each an arbitrary element of $\mathbb{H}$. Choosing a $\mat
hbb{C}$-basis identifies
$$\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{H} \cong M_{2}(\mathbb{C}).$$
Therefore
$$\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{H} = \dim_{\mathbb{C}}
 M_{2}(\mathbb{C}) = 4.$$

**Step 3: Convert to real dimension.**

Since $\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C} = 2$, a complex vector space of complex dimen
sion $n$ has real dimension $2n$. Thus
$$\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{H} = 2\cdot 4 = 8.$$

**Answer:** The dimension of $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{H}$ as a re
al vector space is $\boxed{8}$.
