## Problem

Find $y(x)$ making $\displaystyle J[y]=\int_0^1\!\bigl(x^2+y'^2\bigr)\,dx$ stati
onary, subject to $\displaystyle\int_0^1 y^2\,dx=2$, $y(0)=0$, $y(1)=0$.

## Proof

This is an **isoperimetric problem** (a constrained variational problem with an
integral constraint). The standard necessary condition is that the augmented fun
ctional, with a Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$ for the integral constraint, be st
ationary:

$$\widetilde{J}[y]=\int_0^1\!\bigl(x^2+y'^2+\lambda\,y^2\bigr)\,dx.$$

The integrand is $F(x,y,y')=x^2+y'^2+\lambda y^2$. The Euler–Lagrange equation i
s

$$\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}-\frac{d}{dx}\frac{\partial F}{\partial y'}=0
\;\Longrightarrow\; 2\lambda y-\frac{d}{dx}(2y')=0
\;\Longrightarrow\; y''=\lambda y. \tag{1}$$

(The term $x^2$ is independent of $y$ and $y'$, so it drops out of the E–L equat
ion; it contributes only the constant $\tfrac13$ to $J$ and does not affect the
extremals.)

We now solve (1) under the boundary conditions $y(0)=0$, $y(1)=0$, and the const
raint $\int_0^1 y^2\,dx=2$ (which forces $y\not\equiv 0$).

**Case $\lambda>0$** ($\lambda=\mu^2$, $\mu>0$): $y=A\cosh(\mu x)+B\sinh(\mu x)$
. From $y(0)=0$: $A=0$. From $y(1)=0$: $B\sinh\mu=0$, and since $\sinh\mu\neq0$,
 $B=0$. Hence $y\equiv0$, contradicting the constraint. No solution.

**Case $\lambda=0$**: $y=Ax+B$; the two boundary conditions give $A=B=0$, again
$y\equiv0$. No solution.

**Case $\lambda<0$** (write $\lambda=-\mu^2$, $\mu>0$): equation (1) becomes $y'
'+\mu^2 y=0$, with general solution

$$y(x)=A\cos(\mu x)+B\sin(\mu x).$$

From $y(0)=0$: $A=0$, so $y=B\sin(\mu x)$. From $y(1)=0$: $B\sin\mu=0$. For a **
nontrivial** solution we need $\sin\mu=0$, i.e.

$$\mu=n\pi,\qquad n=1,2,3,\dots$$

Thus $y(x)=B\sin(n\pi x)$ and $\lambda=-n^2\pi^2$.

**Applying the constraint.** Using $\displaystyle\int_0^1\sin^2(n\pi x)\,dx=\tfr
ac12$,

$$\int_0^1 y^2\,dx=B^2\int_0^1\sin^2(n\pi x)\,dx=\frac{B^2}{2}=2
\;\Longrightarrow\; B^2=4\;\Longrightarrow\; B=\pm2.$$

**Verification.** For $y(x)=\pm2\sin(n\pi x)$:
- $y(0)=0$, $y(1)=\pm2\sin(n\pi)=0$. ✓
- $\int_0^1 y^2\,dx=4\cdot\tfrac12=2$. ✓
- $y'=\pm2n\pi\cos(n\pi x)$, $y''=\mp2n^2\pi^2\sin(n\pi x)=-n^2\pi^2\,y=\lambda
y$ with $\lambda=-n^2\pi^2$, satisfying the E–L equation. ✓

## Result

The stationary functions form a countable family, indexed by $n=1,2,3,\dots$, wi
th the corresponding Lagrange multiplier $\lambda_n=-n^2\pi^2$:

$$\boxed{\,y(x)=\pm\,2\sin(n\pi x),\qquad n=1,2,3,\ldots\,}$$

Each member satisfies the boundary conditions and the isoperimetric constraint,
and makes the augmented functional stationary.
