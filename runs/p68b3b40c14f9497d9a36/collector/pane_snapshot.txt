## Proof

Let $F_n = 2^{2^n}+1$ denote the $n$-th Fermat number. The two given numbers are
$$A = 2^{2^{22}}+1 = F_{22}, \qquad B = 2^{2^{222}}+1 = F_{222}.$$

**Key identity.** For every $n \ge 1$,
$$F_n - 2 = 2^{2^n}-1 = (2^{2^{n-1}}-1)(2^{2^{n-1}}+1) = (2^{2^{n-1}}-1)\,F_{n-1
}.$$
Iterating this factorization down to $2^{2^0}-1 = 2^1-1 = 1$ gives
$$F_n - 2 = \prod_{k=0}^{n-1} F_k. \tag{1}$$

**Consequence.** For any $m < n$, the factor $F_m$ appears in the product (1), s
o $F_m \mid F_n - 2$, i.e.
$$F_n \equiv 2 \pmod{F_m}.$$
Hence any common divisor $d$ of $F_m$ and $F_n$ must also divide $2$. But every
Fermat number is odd (it is one more than a power of $2$), so $d \mid \gcd(F_m,2
)=1$. Therefore
$$\gcd(F_m, F_n) = 1 \qquad \text{for all } m \ne n. \tag{2}$$

**Applying to the problem.** Here $m=22 < n=222$, so by (2),
$$\gcd\!\left(2^{2^{22}}+1,\; 2^{2^{222}}+1\right) = \gcd(F_{22},F_{222}) = 1.$$

The greatest common divisor is

$$\boxed{1}$$
