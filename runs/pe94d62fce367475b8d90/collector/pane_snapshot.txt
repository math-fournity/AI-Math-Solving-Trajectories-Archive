## Proof

**Setup.** Since $g$ is differentiable at $0$ with $g(0)=0$ and $g'(0)=1$, we ha
ve
$$g(u) = u + u\,\varepsilon(u), \qquad \varepsilon(u)\to 0 \text{ as } u\to 0.$$
In particular $g(u)=u+o(u)$.

**Step 1 — the inner integral.** Define
$$F(t) := \int_0^t g(s^2)\,ds.$$
For small $|s|$, $s^2$ is small, so $g(s^2)=s^2+o(s^2)$. Hence
$$F(t)=\int_0^t \bigl(s^2+o(s^2)\bigr)\,ds = \frac{t^3}{3}+o(t^3). \tag{1}$$

More precisely, writing $g(s^2)=s^2+s^2\varepsilon(s^2)$ with $\varepsilon(s^2)\
to 0$:
$$F(t)=\frac{t^3}{3}+R(t),\qquad R(t):=\int_0^t s^2\varepsilon(s^2)\,ds,$$
and $R(t)=o(t^3)$.

**Step 2 — the outer integral.** Set
$$I(x):=\int_{x^3}^{x^2} F(t)\,dt = \int_{x^3}^{x^2}\!\left(\frac{t^3}{3}+R(t)\r
ight)dt.$$

The main term:
$$\int_{x^3}^{x^2}\frac{t^3}{3}\,dt = \frac{1}{12}\Bigl[t^4\Bigr]_{x^3}^{x^2}=\f
rac{x^8}{12}-\frac{x^{12}}{12}=\frac{x^8}{12}+o(x^8). \tag{2}$$

The remainder term. For $|x|$ small, every $t$ between $x^3$ and $x^2$ satisfies
 $|t|\le |x|^2$ (since $|x^3|\le x^2$ for $|x|\le 1$). Given $\eta>0$, for $|t|$
 small enough $|R(t)|\le \eta\,|t|^3\le \eta\,|x|^6$. Therefore
$$\left|\int_{x^3}^{x^2} R(t)\,dt\right|\le \eta\,|x|^6\cdot\bigl|x^2-x^3\bigr|\
le \eta\,|x|^6\cdot 2x^2 = 2\eta\,|x|^8$$
for $|x|$ small (using $|x^2-x^3|\le 2x^2$). Since $\eta>0$ is arbitrary,
$$\int_{x^3}^{x^2} R(t)\,dt = o(x^8). \tag{3}$$

Combining (1), (2), (3):
$$I(x)=\frac{x^8}{12}+o(x^8). \tag{4}$$

**Step 3 — the sine and the limit.** Since $I(x)=x^8/12+o(x^8)\to 0$ and $\sin u
 = u+o(u)$ as $u\to 0$,
$$\sin(I(x)) = I(x)+o(I(x)) = \frac{x^8}{12}+o(x^8).$$

Therefore
$$\frac{\sin(I(x))}{x^8}=\frac{1}{12}+o(1)\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{1}{12}\qquad(
x\to 0).$$

**Conclusion.** The limit converges, and its value is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{12}}.$$
