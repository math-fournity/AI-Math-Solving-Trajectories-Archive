## Claim

Yes, $P(T = Y) = 1$.

## Proof

Recall two standard facts:

1. **Almost-sure convergence implies convergence in probability.** If $X_n \to Y
$ a.s., then $X_n \to Y$ in probability.
2. **Limits in probability are unique up to null sets.** If $X_n \to T$ and $X_n
 \to Y$ both in probability, then $P(T = Y) = 1$.

We use both. Since $X_n \to Y$ a.s. by hypothesis, fact (1) gives $X_n \to Y$ in
 probability. Combined with the hypothesis $X_n \to T$ in probability, fact (2)
yields $P(T = Y) = 1$.

For completeness, we verify fact (2) directly. For any $\varepsilon > 0$ and any
 $n$,

$$|T - Y| \le |T - X_n| + |X_n - Y|,$$

so by the union bound,

$$P(|T - Y| > \varepsilon) \le P\!\left(|T - X_n| > \tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\right
) + P\!\left(|X_n - Y| > \tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\right).$$

Both terms on the right tend to $0$ as $n \to \infty$ because $X_n \to T$ and $X
_n \to Y$ in probability. The left-hand side does not depend on $n$, hence

$$P(|T - Y| > \varepsilon) = 0 \quad \text{for every } \varepsilon > 0.$$

Therefore

$$P(T \ne Y) = P\!\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty} \left\{|T - Y| > \tfrac{1}{k}\rig
ht\}\right) \le \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} P\!\left(|T - Y| > \tfrac{1}{k}\right) = 0,$
$

so $P(T = Y) = 1$. $\blacksquare$
