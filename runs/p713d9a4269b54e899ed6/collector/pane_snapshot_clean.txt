## Proof

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains only the problem; no solut
ion, sketch, or answer is embedded. Proceeding.

**Case 1: $a = b = 0$.** The equation reads $h(x,y) = 0\cdot h_x + 0\cdot h_y =
0$ for every $(x,y)$, so $h \equiv 0$ immediately.

**Case 2: $(a,b) \neq (0,0)$.** Assume $h$ is $C^1$ (so that the partial derivat
ives in the equation are defined and the chain rule below applies). Fix an arbit
rary point $(x_0,y_0)\in\mathbb{R}^2$ and consider the line through it in the di
rection $(a,b)$, parametrized by
$$(x(t),y(t)) = (x_0 + at,\; y_0 + bt), \qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.$$
Define $g(t) = h(x(t),y(t))$. By the chain rule,
$$g'(t) = a\,\frac{\partial h}{\partial x}(x(t),y(t)) + b\,\frac{\partial h}{\pa
rtial y}(x(t),y(t)).$$
The given identity $h = a\,h_x + b\,h_y$ applied at the point $(x(t),y(t))$ yiel
ds
$$g'(t) = h(x(t),y(t)) = g(t).$$
Hence $g$ satisfies the ODE $g'(t)=g(t)$, whose general solution is
$$g(t) = g(0)\,e^{t}.$$

Since $|h|\le M$ on all of $\mathbb{R}^2$, we have $|g(t)|\le M$ for every $t\in
\mathbb{R}$. But
$$|g(t)| = |g(0)|\,e^{t}.$$
If $g(0)\neq 0$, then $|g(t)| = |g(0)|e^{t}\to\infty$ as $t\to+\infty$, contradi
cting the bound $|g(t)|\le M$. Therefore $g(0)=0$.

Recalling $g(0)=h(x_0,y_0)$, we conclude $h(x_0,y_0)=0$. Since $(x_0,y_0)$ was a
rbitrary, $h\equiv 0$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$.

In both cases $h$ is identically zero. $\blacksquare$
