**Interpretation.** "No two elements of the open cover contain all three points
$a,b,c$" means: there is no pair of distinct elements $U,V$ of the cover with $\
{a,b,c\}\subseteq U\cup V$. (The weaker reading "at most one element contains al
l three" is trivially satisfied by the one-element cover $\{Y\}$, so the meaning
ful reading is the union one.)

**Answer: Yes, always.**

**Proof.**

*Lemma.* In a Hausdorff space, any three distinct points $a,b,c$ admit pairwise
disjoint open neighborhoods $U_a,U_b,U_c$ with $a\in U_a,\ b\in U_b,\ c\in U_c$.

*Proof of lemma.* By Hausdorffness, choose disjoint open pairs separating each p
air of points:
- $W_a\ni a,\ W_b\ni b$ with $W_a\cap W_b=\varnothing$;
- $V_a\ni a,\ V_c\ni c$ with $V_a\cap V_c=\varnothing$;
- $X_b\ni b,\ X_c\ni c$ with $X_b\cap X_c=\varnothing$.

Set
$$U_a=W_a\cap V_a,\qquad U_b=W_b\cap X_b,\qquad U_c=V_c\cap X_c.$$
Then $a\in U_a,\ b\in U_b,\ c\in U_c$. Moreover $b,c\notin U_a$ (since $U_a\subs
eteq W_a$ excludes $b\in W_b$, and $U_a\subseteq V_a$ excludes $c\in V_c$); simi
larly $a,c\notin U_b$ and $a,b\notin U_c$. Hence $U_a,U_b,U_c$ are pairwise disj
oint and each contains exactly one of the three points. $\square$

*Construction of the cover.* For every $y\in Y\setminus\{a,b,c\}$, the point $y$
 is distinct from each of $a,b,c$. By Hausdorffness, for each $p\in\{a,b,c\}$ th
ere are disjoint open sets separating $y$ and $p$; let $N_p^{(y)}\ni y$ be the o
ne excluding $p$. Set
$$V_y \;=\; N_a^{(y)}\cap N_b^{(y)}\cap N_c^{(y)}.$$
Then $V_y$ is an open neighborhood of $y$ with $V_y\cap\{a,b,c\}=\varnothing$.

Now define
$$\mathcal U \;=\; \{U_a,\,U_b,\,U_c\}\ \cup\ \{\,V_y : y\in Y\setminus\{a,b,c\}
\,\}.$$

*It is an open cover.* The points $a,b,c$ are covered by $U_a,U_b,U_c$ respectiv
ely, and every other point $y$ is covered by $V_y$.

*Each element contains at most one of $\{a,b,c\}$.* By the lemma, $U_a$ contains
 $a$ but not $b,c$; $U_b$ contains $b$ but not $a,c$; $U_c$ contains $c$ but not
 $a,b$; and each $V_y$ contains none of $a,b,c$.

*No two elements together contain all three.* Take any two distinct $U,V\in\math
cal U$. Each contains at most one of $\{a,b,c\}$, so $U\cup V$ contains at most
two of them. Therefore $\{a,b,c\}\not\subseteq U\cup V$.

Thus $\mathcal U$ is an open cover of $Y$ in which no two elements contain all t
hree points $a,b,c$. Since the construction works for every Hausdorff space $Y$
and every triple of distinct points, the answer is **yes, it is always possible*
*. $\blacksquare$
