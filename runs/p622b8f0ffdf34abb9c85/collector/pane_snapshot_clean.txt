## Proof

---

**Key observation.** Since $\angle ABC = \angle ADC = 90°$ and $ABCD$ is convex,
 the points $B$ and $D$ lie on opposite sides of diagonal $AC$, and both lie on
the circle with diameter $AC$ (Thales' theorem). Hence $ABCD$ is **cyclic** with
 circumdiameter $AC$.

**Coordinate setup.** Place the circumcircle at the origin with $AC$ as a diamet
er along the $x$-axis:
$$A = (-1,0),\quad C = (1,0).$$

Let $\alpha = \angle BAC$ and $\beta = \angle DAC$, with $\alpha,\beta \in (0,\p
i/2)$. Since $AB = 2\cos\alpha$ and $AD = 2\cos\beta$ (chord lengths in the unit
 circumcircle), the condition $AB > AD$ gives $\cos\alpha > \cos\beta$, i.e., **
$\alpha < \beta$**.

With $B$ on the upper semicircle and $D$ on the lower (consistent with convexity
):
$$B = (\cos 2\alpha,\;\sin 2\alpha),\qquad D = (\cos 2\beta,\;-\sin 2\beta).$$

---

**Point $P$.** Since $AP = AD$ and $P \in AB$, we have $\frac{AP}{AB} = \frac{\c
os\beta}{\cos\alpha}$, giving
$$P = A + \tfrac{\cos\beta}{\cos\alpha}(B-A) = \bigl(2\cos\alpha\cos\beta - 1,\;
 2\sin\alpha\cos\beta\bigr).$$

**Point $S$** (foot of perpendicular from $D$ to $AC$, the $x$-axis):
$$S = (\cos 2\beta,\, 0) = (2\cos^2\beta - 1,\; 0).$$

**Point $Q = PD \cap BC$.** The direction of $PD$ is $(\cos\beta - \cos\alpha,\;
 -(\sin\alpha+\sin\beta))$ and the direction of $BC$ is $(\sin\alpha,\; -\cos\al
pha)$. Solving
$$P + t(\cos\beta-\cos\alpha,\;-(\sin\alpha+\sin\beta)) = B + u(\sin\alpha,\;-\c
os\alpha)$$
yields, after simplification using sum-to-product identities,
$$\boxed{Q = \bigl(1 - 2\sin\alpha\sin\beta,\; 2\sin\beta\cos\alpha\bigr).}$$

**Point $R$.** Since $QR \perp AC$ (the $x$-axis), $R$ shares its $x$-coordinate
 with $Q$. Intersecting the vertical line $x = 1 - 2\sin\alpha\sin\beta$ with li
ne $AB$ (direction $(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)$) gives
$$R = \left(1 - 2\sin\alpha\sin\beta,\;\frac{2\sin\alpha(1-\sin\alpha\sin\beta)}
{\cos\alpha}\right).$$

---

**Computing $\tan\angle PSQ$.** At $S$:
$$\vec{SP} = 2\cos\beta\,(\cos\alpha - \cos\beta,\;\sin\alpha),\qquad \vec{SQ} =
 2\sin\beta\,(\sin\beta - \sin\alpha,\;\cos\alpha).$$

Cross product (determinant):
$$\vec{SP}\times\vec{SQ} = 4\cos\beta\sin\beta\bigl[(\cos\alpha-\cos\beta)\cos\a
lpha - \sin\alpha(\sin\beta-\sin\alpha)\bigr] = 4\cos\beta\sin\beta\bigl[1 - \co
s(\alpha-\beta)\bigr].$$

Dot product:
$$\vec{SP}\cdot\vec{SQ} = 4\cos\beta\sin\beta\bigl[(\cos\alpha-\cos\beta)(\sin\b
eta-\sin\alpha)+\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\bigr] = 4\cos\beta\sin\beta\bigl[\sin(\alph
a+\beta)-\sin\beta\cos\beta\bigr].$$

Therefore:
$$\tan\angle PSQ = \frac{1 - \cos(\alpha-\beta)}{\sin(\alpha+\beta) - \sin\beta\
cos\beta}. \tag{1}$$

---

**Computing $\tan\angle RCP$.** At $C$:
$$\vec{CR} = 2\sin\alpha\!\left(-\sin\beta,\;\frac{1-\sin\alpha\sin\beta}{\cos\a
lpha}\right),\qquad \vec{CP} = 2\,(\cos\alpha\cos\beta - 1,\;\sin\alpha\cos\beta
).$$

Cross product:
$$\vec{CR}\times\vec{CP} = \frac{4\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha}\bigl[1 - \cos(\alpha-\
beta)\bigr].$$

Dot product:
$$\vec{CR}\cdot\vec{CP} = \frac{4\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha}\bigl[\sin(\alpha+\beta)
 - \sin\beta\cos\beta\bigr].$$

Therefore:
$$\tan\angle RCP = \frac{1 - \cos(\alpha-\beta)}{\sin(\alpha+\beta) - \sin\beta\
cos\beta}. \tag{2}$$

---

**Conclusion.** From (1) and (2):
$$\tan\angle PSQ = \tan\angle RCP.$$

It remains to confirm both angles lie in the same interval $(0,\pi/2)$:

- **Numerator:** $1 - \cos(\alpha-\beta) = 1 - \cos(\beta-\alpha) > 0$ since $\b
eta \neq \alpha$.
- **Denominator:** $\sin(\alpha+\beta) - \sin\beta\cos\beta = \sin\alpha\cos\bet
a + \sin\beta(\cos\alpha - \cos\beta) > 0$, since each term is positive (using $
\alpha < \beta \Rightarrow \cos\alpha > \cos\beta$).

Both cross products and dot products are positive, so both $\angle PSQ$ and $\an
gle RCP$ are in $(0, \pi/2)$. Equal tangents in this range yield equal angles:

$$\boxed{\angle PSQ = \angle RCP.}\qquad\blacksquare$$
