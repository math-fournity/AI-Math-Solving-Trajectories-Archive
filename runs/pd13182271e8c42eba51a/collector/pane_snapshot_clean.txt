**Proof.** Let $(y_n)$ be a Cauchy sequence in $F$. We show it converges in $F$.

Since $E \neq \{0\}$, pick $e_0 \in E$ with $e_0 \neq 0$ and normalize so $\|e_0
\| = 1$. Define a linear functional $\varphi$ on $\operatorname{span}\{e_0\}$ by
 $\varphi(\alpha e_0) = \alpha$. This has norm $1$ on that one-dimensional subsp
ace. By the Hahn–Banach theorem (which requires no completeness of $E$), $\varph
i$ extends to a bounded linear functional on all of $E$, still denoted $\varphi$
, with $\|\varphi\| = 1$ and $\varphi(e_0) = 1$.

For each $n$, define $T_n : E \to F$ by
$$T_n(x) = \varphi(x)\, y_n.$$
Each $T_n$ is linear and bounded, with $\|T_n\| \le \|\varphi\|\,\|y_n\| = \|y_n
\|$, so $T_n \in L(E,F)$.

For $n, m$,
$$\|T_n - T_m\| = \sup_{\|x\|\le 1} \|\varphi(x)(y_n - y_m)\| \le \|\varphi\|\,\
|y_n - y_m\| = \|y_n - y_m\|.$$
Since $(y_n)$ is Cauchy, $(T_n)$ is Cauchy in $L(E,F)$. By hypothesis $L(E,F)$ i
s complete, so $T_n \to T$ for some $T \in L(E,F)$.

In particular, $T_n(e_0) \to T(e_0)$ in $F$. But $T_n(e_0) = \varphi(e_0) y_n =
y_n$. Hence $y_n \to T(e_0) \in F$.

Every Cauchy sequence in $F$ converges, so $F$ is complete. $\square$
