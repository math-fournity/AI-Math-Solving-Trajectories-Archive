## Proof

We prove that the family of products
$$\mathcal{B}=\{\,\varphi_{n,m,p}(x,y,z):=\sin(nx)\sin(my)\sin(pz)\;:\;n,m,p\in\
mathbb{N}\,\}$$
forms a complete orthogonal system in $L^2\bigl([0,\pi]^3\bigr)$, and derive the
 stated coefficient formula.

---

### Step 1. One-dimensional orthogonality

For $k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}$,
$$I_{k\ell}:=\int_0^\pi \sin(kx)\sin(\ell x)\,dx.$$
Using the product-to-sum identity
$$\sin(kx)\sin(\ell x)=\tfrac12\bigl[\cos((k-\ell)x)-\cos((k+\ell)x)\bigr],$$
we obtain, for $k\neq\ell$,
$$I_{k\ell}=\tfrac12\Bigl[\tfrac{\sin((k-\ell)x)}{k-\ell}-\tfrac{\sin((k+\ell)x)
}{k+\ell}\Bigr]_0^\pi=0,$$
since $\sin(j\pi)=0$ for every integer $j$. For $k=\ell$,
$$I_{kk}=\int_0^\pi \sin^2(kx)\,dx=\int_0^\pi \tfrac{1-\cos(2kx)}{2}\,dx=\tfrac{
\pi}{2}.$$
Hence
$$\int_0^\pi \sin(kx)\sin(\ell x)\,dx=\tfrac{\pi}{2}\,\delta_{k\ell}. \tag{1}$$

---

### Step 2. Three-dimensional orthogonality

By Fubini's theorem and (1),
$$\begin{aligned}
\langle\varphi_{n,m,p},\varphi_{n',m',p'}\rangle
&=\int_0^\pi\!\!\int_0^\pi\!\!\int_0^\pi
\sin(nx)\sin(my)\sin(pz)\,
\sin(n'x)\sin(m'y)\sin(p'z)\,dz\,dy\,dx\\
&=\Bigl(\int_0^\pi\sin(nx)\sin(n'x)\,dx\Bigr)
\Bigl(\int_0^\pi\sin(my)\sin(m'y)\,dy\Bigr)
\Bigl(\int_0^\pi\sin(pz)\sin(p'z)\,dz\Bigr)\\
&=\Bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\delta_{nn'}\Bigr)\Bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\delta_{mm'}\Bigr)\B
igl(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\delta_{pp'}\Bigr)\\
&=\tfrac{\pi^3}{8}\,\delta_{nn'}\delta_{mm'}\delta_{pp'}. \tag{2}
\end{aligned}$$
Thus $\mathcal{B}$ is an **orthogonal** system in $L^2\bigl([0,\pi]^3\bigr)$, wi
th each $\varphi_{n,m,p}$ having squared norm $\|\varphi_{n,m,p}\|^2=\pi^3/8$.

---

### Step 3. Derivation of the coefficients

Assume $f\in L^2\bigl([0,\pi]^3\bigr)$ admits the expansion
$$f(x,y,z)\sim\sum_{n,m,p\ge1}c_{n,m,p}\,\varphi_{n,m,p}(x,y,z).$$
Formally taking the inner product of both sides with $\varphi_{n,m,p}$ and using
 orthogonality (2),
$$\langle f,\varphi_{n,m,p}\rangle
=\sum_{n',m',p'\ge1}c_{n',m',p'}\,\langle\varphi_{n',m',p'},\varphi_{n,m,p}\rang
le
=c_{n,m,p}\cdot\tfrac{\pi^3}{8}.$$
Therefore
$$c_{n,m,p}=\frac{8}{\pi^3}\,\langle f,\varphi_{n,m,p}\rangle
=\frac{8}{\pi^3}\int_0^\pi\!\!\int_0^\pi\!\!\int_0^\pi f(x,y,z)\,\sin(nx)\sin(my
)\sin(pz)\,dz\,dy\,dx, \tag{3}$$
which is exactly the stated formula.

---

### Step 4. Completeness of the system $\mathcal{B}$

It remains to justify that the expansion is legitimate, i.e. that $\mathcal{B}$
is **complete** (a basis) in $L^2\bigl([0,\pi]^3\bigr)$.

**One-dimensional completeness.** It is a classical result that $\{\sin(nx):n\ge
1\}$ is complete in $L^2[0,\pi]$. One standard proof: extend $g\in L^2[0,\pi]$ o
ddly to $[-\pi,\pi]$ and then $2\pi$-periodically; the ordinary (full) Fourier s
eries of this odd extension contains only sine terms, and converges to $g$ in $L
^2[0,\pi]$. Equivalently, the exponentials $\{e^{ikx}:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}$ are comp
lete in $L^2[-\pi,\pi]$, and restricting to odd functions selects exactly the si
ne modes.

**Tensor-product completeness.** Let $f\in L^2\bigl([0,\pi]^3\bigr)$ satisfy
$$\langle f,\varphi_{n,m,p}\rangle=0\qquad\text{for all }n,m,p\ge1. \tag{4}$$
We show $f=0$ a.e.

Define, for a.e. fixed $(x,y)\in[0,\pi]^2$,
$$F_{x,y}(z):=f(x,y,z).$$
For each $p\ge1$, condition (4) gives
$$0=\int_0^\pi\!\!\int_0^\pi\sin(nx)\sin(my)\Bigl[\int_0^\pi f(x,y,z)\sin(pz)\,d
z\Bigr]dy\,dx.$$
Set
$$g_p(x,y):=\int_0^\pi f(x,y,z)\sin(pz)\,dz.$$
Then for all $n,m\ge1$,
$$\int_0^\pi\!\!\int_0^\pi g_p(x,y)\sin(nx)\sin(my)\,dy\,dx=0. \tag{5}$$
Fixing $p$, apply the one-dimensional completeness in $y$: for a.e. fixed $x$, (
5) and Fubini imply
$$\int_0^\pi g_p(x,y)\sin(my)\,dy=0\quad\forall m\ge1\;\Longrightarrow\;g_p(x,y)
=0\;\text{for a.e. }y.$$
Hence $g_p\equiv0$ a.e. on $[0,\pi]^2$ for every $p\ge1$:
$$\int_0^\pi f(x,y,z)\sin(pz)\,dz=0\quad\forall p\ge1,\;\text{for a.e. }(x,y). \
tag{6}$$
Now apply one-dimensional completeness in $z$ to (6): for a.e. fixed $(x,y)$, $f
(x,y,\cdot)$ is orthogonal to every $\sin(pz)$, so $f(x,y,z)=0$ for a.e. $z$. Th
erefore $f=0$ a.e. on $[0,\pi]^3$.

This proves $\mathcal{B}^\perp=\{0\}$, i.e. $\mathcal{B}$ is **complete** in $L^
2\bigl([0,\pi]^3\bigr)$.

---

### Step 5. Convergence

Since $\mathcal{B}$ is a complete orthogonal system in the separable Hilbert spa
ce $L^2\bigl([0,\pi]^3\bigr)$, every $f\in L^2\bigl([0,\pi]^3\bigr)$ — in partic
ular every well-behaved (e.g. piecewise $C^1$, or $L^2$) function on the box — h
as the expansion
$$f(x,y,z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}c_{n,m,p}\si
n(nx)\sin(my)\sin(pz)$$
with convergence in the $L^2$ sense, and the coefficients are uniquely given by
(3):
$$\boxed{\;c_{n,m,p}=\frac{8}{\pi^3}\int_0^\pi\!\!\int_0^\pi\!\!\int_0^\pi f(x,y
,z)\,\sin(nx)\sin(my)\sin(pz)\,dz\,dy\,dx.\;}$$

For well-behaved $f$ (e.g. piecewise smooth), standard Fourier theory further up
grades the $L^2$ convergence to pointwise convergence at points of continuity an
d to the midpoint value at jump discontinuities, so the symbol $\sim$ may be rep
laced by $=$ at points of continuity. $\blacksquare$
