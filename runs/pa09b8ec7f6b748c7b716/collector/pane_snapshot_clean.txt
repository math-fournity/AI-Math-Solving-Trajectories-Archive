## Solution

We find the 2001st derivative of $f(x) = \dfrac{x^{5}}{x^{6}+1}$ at $x=0$.

**Step 1: Geometric series expansion.**

For $|x|<1$,
$$\frac{1}{1+x^{6}} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}x^{6n}.$$

Multiplying by $x^{5}$:
$$f(x) = \frac{x^{5}}{x^{6}+1} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}x^{6n+5}.$$

**Step 2: Read off the Taylor coefficient at $x^{2001}$.**

The Taylor series of $f$ about $0$ is $f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{f^{(k)}(0)
}{k!}x^{k}$. Comparing with the expansion above, the only nonzero coefficients o
ccur at exponents of the form $6n+5$ ($n=0,1,2,\dots$), i.e. $k\in\{5,11,17,23,\
dots\}$, with coefficient $(-1)^{n}$.

We need the coefficient at $k=2001$. Solving $6n+5=2001$ gives $6n=1996$, so $n=
\dfrac{1996}{6}=\dfrac{998}{3}$, which is **not an integer**.

Therefore the coefficient of $x^{2001}$ in the Taylor expansion is $0$:
$$\frac{f^{(2001)}(0)}{2001!}=0.$$

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

$$f^{(2001)}(0)=0.$$
