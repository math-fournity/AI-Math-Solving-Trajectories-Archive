## Proof

**Setup and key observation.** Let $S_n$ denote the set of roots of $f^n(x) = 0$
. Since $f^n$ is a polynomial of degree $2^n$, the set $S_n$ has $2^n$ elements
(counting multiplicity). The roots of $f^{n+1}(x) = 0$ are precisely the preimag
es under $f$ of the elements of $S_n$.

For any real $r$, the equation $f(x) = r$ becomes $cx^2 - cx - r = 0$ with discr
iminant $c^2 + 4cr$. This has real solutions if and only if $r \geq -c/4$.

**Equivalence reformulation.** The condition "all roots of $f^n(x)=0$ are real f
or every $n$" is equivalent to:

$$\text{For all } n \geq 1: \text{ every element of } S_n \text{ is } \geq -\fra
c{c}{4}. \tag{$\star$}$$

*Justification:* The roots of $f^{n+1}(x)=0$ are real iff every $r \in S_n$ sati
sfies $r \geq -c/4$. Since $S_1 = \{0, 1\}$ and both are $\geq -c/4$ (as $c > 0$
), condition $(\star)$ guarantees all $S_n$ consist of real numbers $\geq -c/4$,
 which in turn guarantees each subsequent $S_{n+1}$ is real.

**Tracking the extremal roots.** Define $M_n = \max(S_n)$ and $m_n = \min(S_n)$.

The preimages of $r$ under $f$ are:
$$x = \frac{1 \pm \sqrt{1 + 4r/c}}{2}.$$

The smaller preimage $\phi(r) = \frac{1 - \sqrt{1+4r/c}}{2}$ is **strictly decre
asing** in $r$, and the larger preimage $\psi(r) = \frac{1 + \sqrt{1+4r/c}}{2}$
is **strictly increasing** in $r$.

Therefore:
- The **largest** element of $S_{n+1}$ is $\psi(M_n)$ (larger preimage of the la
rgest element of $S_n$).
- The **smallest** element of $S_{n+1}$ is $\phi(M_n)$ (smaller preimage of the
largest element of $S_n$).

This gives the recurrence:
$$M_{n+1} = \frac{1 + \sqrt{1 + 4M_n/c}}{2}, \quad M_1 = 1. \tag{1}$$

Moreover, $m_{n+1} = \phi(M_n) = \frac{1 - \sqrt{1+4M_n/c}}{2} = 1 - M_{n+1}$, s
o:

$$m_n = 1 - M_n \quad \text{for all } n \geq 2. \tag{2}$$

**Converting the condition.** Condition $(\star)$ for $n \geq 2$ becomes $m_n \g
eq -c/4$, i.e., $1 - M_n \geq -c/4$, i.e.:

$$M_n \leq 1 + \frac{c}{4} \quad \text{for all } n \geq 2. \tag{3}$$

**Analyzing the recurrence.** Let $g(x) = \frac{1 + \sqrt{1+4x/c}}{2}$. Then $M_
{n+1} = g(M_n)$.

- $g$ is strictly increasing and continuous.
- $g(1) = \frac{1+\sqrt{1+4/c}}{2} > 1 = M_1$ for all $c > 0$, so $M_2 > M_1$.
- By induction (since $g$ is increasing), $M_n$ is **strictly increasing**.

Fixed points of $g$: $L = \frac{1+\sqrt{1+4L/c}}{2}$ leads to $L^2 - L = L/c$, s
o $L = 0$ or $L = 1 + 1/c$. Since $M_n$ is increasing from $M_1 = 1 > 0$, we hav
e $M_n \nearrow 1 + 1/c$.

Convergence is confirmed since $g'(1+1/c) = \frac{1}{c+2} < 1$ (the fixed point
is attractive).

**The critical condition.** Since $M_n$ is strictly increasing and converges to
$1 + 1/c$:

- If $1 + 1/c \leq 1 + c/4$, then $M_n < 1 + 1/c \leq 1 + c/4$ for all $n$, so c
ondition (3) holds. ✓
- If $1 + 1/c > 1 + c/4$, then for sufficiently large $n$, $M_n > 1 + c/4$, and
condition (3) fails. ✗

Solving $1 + 1/c \leq 1 + c/4$:
$$\frac{1}{c} \leq \frac{c}{4} \implies 4 \leq c^2 \implies c \geq 2.$$

**Verification at $c = 2$.** Here $1 + 1/c = 3/2 = 1 + c/4$. The sequence $M_n$
is strictly increasing toward $3/2$ but never reaches it, so $M_n < 3/2$ for all
 finite $n$, giving $m_n = 1 - M_n > -1/2 = -c/4$ for all $n$. All roots remain
real. ✓

**Verification that $c < 2$ fails.** Take $c = 1.9$: $-c/4 = -0.475$, and $1 + 1
/c \approx 1.526 > 1.475 = 1 + c/4$. Computing: $M_2 \approx 1.381$, $M_3 \appro
x 1.488$, so $m_3 \approx -0.488 < -0.475 = -c/4$. Thus $S_4$ contains complex r
oots — the condition fails at $n = 4$. ✗

**Conclusion.** The smallest value of $c$ for which all roots of $f^n(x) = 0$ ar
e real for every positive integer $n$ is:

$$\boxed{c = 2}$$
