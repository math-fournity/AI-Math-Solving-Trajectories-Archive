# Transformation of Lorentz Generators under Parity and Time-Reversal

## Setup

The Lorentz group generators are the antisymmetric tensor operators $M^{\rho\sig
ma}$ ($\rho,\sigma = 0,1,2,3$), from which the rotation and boost generators are
 defined as

$$J_i = \tfrac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ijk}\,M^{jk}, \qquad K_i = M^{0i}.$$

Under a Lorentz transformation $\Lambda$, the adjoint action on the generators i
s the tensor transformation law

$$\Lambda\, M^{\rho\sigma}\,\Lambda^{-1} \;=\; \Lambda^{\rho}{}_{\alpha}\,\Lambd
a^{\sigma}{}_{\beta}\,M^{\alpha\beta}\,\sigma_{\mathcal{O}}, \tag{1}$$

where $\sigma_{\mathcal{O}} = +1$ for a **unitary** operation (parity) and $\sig
ma_{\mathcal{O}} = -1$ for an **anti-unitary** operation (time reversal). The ex
tra sign for time reversal arises because the quantum operators $M^{\rho\sigma}$
 carry an explicit factor of $i$ in their definition, and an anti-unitary operat
or complex-conjugates: $\Theta\,(i)\,\Theta^{-1} = -i$.

The discrete transformations are
$$\Lambda_P = \mathrm{diag}(+1,-1,-1,-1) \quad\text{(parity)},\qquad \Lambda_T =
 \mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1) \quad\text{(time reversal)}.$$

---

## Parity ($\Lambda_P$, unitary, $\sigma = +1$)

**Rotations $J_i = \frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ijk}M^{jk}$.** Both indices of $M^{jk}$
are spatial, so each picks up a factor $(-1)$ from $\Lambda_P$:

$$\Lambda_P\, M^{jk}\,\Lambda_P^{-1} = (-1)(-1)\,M^{jk} = +M^{jk}.$$

Contracting with $\epsilon_{ijk}$:

$$\boxed{\;\Lambda_P\, J_i\,\Lambda_P^{-1} = +J_i\;} \qquad\text{(rotations are
even under parity).}$$

**Boosts $K_i = M^{0i}$.** The index $0$ is temporal (factor $+1$) and the index
 $i$ is spatial (factor $-1$):

$$\Lambda_P\, M^{0i}\,\Lambda_P^{-1} = (+1)(-1)\,M^{0i} = -M^{0i}.$$

$$\boxed{\;\Lambda_P\, K_i\,\Lambda_P^{-1} = -K_i\;} \qquad\text{(boosts are odd
 under parity).}$$

---

## Time Reversal ($\Lambda_T$, anti-unitary, $\sigma = -1$)

The anti-unitary sign $\sigma = -1$ contributes an overall minus sign on top of
the tensor-index factors.

**Rotations $J_i = \frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ijk}M^{jk}$.** Both indices are spatial,
 each picking up $+1$ from $\Lambda_T$, giving a tensor factor of $(+1)$. Combin
ed with the anti-unitary sign:

$$\Lambda_T\, M^{jk}\,\Lambda_T^{-1} = (+1)(+1)\,(-1)\,M^{jk} = -M^{jk}.$$

$$\boxed{\;\Lambda_T\, J_i\,\Lambda_T^{-1} = -J_i\;} \qquad\text{(rotations are
odd under time reversal).}$$

**Boosts $K_i = M^{0i}$.** The index $0$ picks up $(-1)$ and the index $i$ picks
 up $(+1)$, giving a tensor factor of $(-1)$. Combined with the anti-unitary sig
n:

$$\Lambda_T\, M^{0i}\,\Lambda_T^{-1} = (-1)(+1)\,(-1)\,M^{0i} = +M^{0i}.$$

$$\boxed{\;\Lambda_T\, K_i\,\Lambda_T^{-1} = +K_i\;} \qquad\text{(boosts are eve
n under time reversal).}$$

---

## Consistency Check: The Algebra Is Preserved

The Lorentz algebra is
$$[J_i,J_j]=i\epsilon_{ijk}J_k,\quad [J_i,K_j]=i\epsilon_{ijk}K_k,\quad [K_i,K_j
]=-i\epsilon_{ijk}J_k.$$

**Under parity** ($J_i\to J_i,\;K_i\to -K_i$, $i$ unchanged):
- $[J_i,J_j]\to[J_i,J_j]=i\epsilon_{ijk}J_k$ ✓
- $[J_i,K_j]\to[J_i,-K_j]=-i\epsilon_{ijk}K_k=i\epsilon_{ijk}(-K_k)$ ✓
- $[K_i,K_j]\to[-K_i,-K_j]=[K_i,K_j]=-i\epsilon_{ijk}J_k$ ✓

**Under time reversal** ($J_i\to -J_i,\;K_i\to K_i$, and $i\to -i$ by anti-unita
rity):
- $[J_i,J_j]\to[-J_i,-J_j]=[J_i,J_j]$; RHS: $(-i)\epsilon_{ijk}(-J_k)=i\epsilon_
{ijk}J_k$ ✓
- $[J_i,K_j]\to[-J_i,K_j]=-[J_i,K_j]=-i\epsilon_{ijk}K_k$; RHS: $(-i)\epsilon_{i
jk}K_k=-i\epsilon_{ijk}K_k$ ✓
- $[K_i,K_j]\to[K_i,K_j]=-i\epsilon_{ijk}J_k$; RHS: $-(-i)\epsilon_{ijk}(-J_k)=-
i\epsilon_{ijk}J_k$ ✓

All three commutation relations are preserved in both cases.

## Physical Interpretation

The result can be understood through the relativistic angular-momentum tensor $M
^{\mu\nu}=x^\mu p^\nu - x^\nu p^\mu$, so $K_i = M^{0i} = t\,p_i - x_i\,E$:

- **$J_i \sim \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{p}$ (angular momentum, a pseudovector):**
Under parity $\mathbf{r}\to-\mathbf{r},\,\mathbf{p}\to-\mathbf{p}$, so $\mathbf{
r}\times\mathbf{p}\to(+)\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{p}$ (even). Under time reversal
$\mathbf{p}\to-\mathbf{p},\,\mathbf{r}\to\mathbf{r}$, so $\mathbf{r}\times\mathb
f{p}\to(-)\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{p}$ (odd).
- **$K_i \sim t\,p_i - x_i\,E$ (boost generator):** Under parity $t\to t,\,p_i\t
o-p_i,\,x_i\to-x_i,\,E\to E$, giving $t(-p_i)-(-x_i)E = -(tp_i-x_iE)$ (odd). Und
er time reversal $t\to-t,\,p_i\to-p_i,\,x_i\to x_i,\,E\to E$, giving $(-t)(-p_i)
-x_iE = tp_i-x_iE$ (even).

This matches the algebraic derivation exactly.

## Summary

| Generator | Parity $\Lambda_P$ (unitary) | Time reversal $\Lambda_T$ (anti-uni
tary) |
|-----------|------------------------------|------------------------------------
------|
| $J_i$ (rotation) | $+J_i$ (even) | $-J_i$ (odd) |
| $K_i$ (boost) | $-K_i$ (odd) | $+K_i$ (even) |

The key distinction between the two discrete symmetries is that parity is unitar
y (only tensor-index signs matter) while time reversal is anti-unitary (an addit
ional overall minus sign from $i\to -i$ flips the parity of each generator's tra
nsformation. This is why rotations, even under parity, become odd under time rev
ersal, and boosts, odd under parity, become even under time reversal.
