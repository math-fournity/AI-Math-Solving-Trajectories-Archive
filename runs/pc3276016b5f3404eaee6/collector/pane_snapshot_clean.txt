## Problem Interpretation

The problem asks for the product obtained by taking the first 90 counting number
s, raising each to its own power, and multiplying:

$$P \;=\; \prod_{n=1}^{90} n^{\,n} \;=\; 1^1\cdot 2^2\cdot 3^3\cdots 90^{90}.$$

This is the **hyperfactorial** $H(90)$. (The phrase "90-digit resulting number"
in the statement is a misnomer: as shown below, the product has **7124 digits**,
 not 90. I will give the exact closed form and the exact digit count.)

## Closed Form

Use the identity relating the hyperfactorial to factorials. Observe

$$\prod_{n=1}^{N} n! \;=\; \prod_{n=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{n} k \;=\; \prod_{k=1}^{N
} k^{\,N+1-k},$$

since each $k$ appears in $k!, (k+1)!, \dots, N!$, i.e. exactly $N+1-k$ times. T
herefore

$$\prod_{k=1}^{N} k^{k} \;=\; \frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N} k^{\,N+1}}{\prod_{k=1}^{N} k
^{\,N+1-k}} \;=\; \frac{(N!)^{\,N+1}}{\prod_{n=1}^{N} n!}.$$

Setting $N=90$:

$$\boxed{\,P \;=\; H(90) \;=\; \prod_{n=1}^{90} n^{n} \;=\; \frac{(90!)^{\,91}}{
1!\cdot 2!\cdot 3!\cdots 90!}\,}.$$

**Verification of the identity on small cases.**
- $N=2$: $H(2)=1^1\cdot2^2=4$; formula gives $(2!)^3/(1!\cdot2!)=8/2=4$. ✓
- $N=3$: $H(3)=1\cdot4\cdot27=108$; formula gives $(3!)^4/(1!\cdot2!\cdot3!)=129
6/12=108$. ✓

So the closed form is correct.

## Number of Digits

The number of decimal digits of $P$ is $\lfloor \log_{10} P\rfloor + 1$, where

$$\log_{10} P \;=\; \sum_{n=1}^{90} n\log_{10} n.$$

Evaluating the sum term by term (using $\log_{10}$ to five decimals):

$$\log_{10} P \;\approx\; 7123.445 \quad (\text{error} \le 5\times10^{-6}\cdot\t
frac{90\cdot91}{2}\approx 0.021).$$

The fractional part $0.445$ is safely away from $0$ and $1$, so

$$\lfloor \log_{10} P\rfloor = 7123, \qquad \text{digits}(P) = 7123+1 = \boxed{7
124}.$$

## Conclusion

The resulting number is the hyperfactorial

$$P \;=\; \prod_{n=1}^{90} n^{n} \;=\; \frac{(90!)^{91}}{\prod_{k=1}^{90} k!},$$

an integer with exactly **7124 decimal digits**. (The descriptor "90-digit" in t
