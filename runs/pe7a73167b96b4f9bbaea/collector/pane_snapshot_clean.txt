# Solution

## Setup: Generating Function

Let $f(n)$ denote the number of ordered compositions of $n$ into prime parts. It
s ordinary generating function is

$$F(z)=\sum_{n\ge 0} f(n)\,z^n=\frac{1}{1-P(z)},\qquad P(z):=\sum_{p\text{ prime
}} z^{p}=z^{2}+z^{3}+z^{5}+z^{7}+\cdots$$

(The term $1$ accounts for the empty composition of $0$; the geometric-series id
entity $\sum_{k\ge0}P(z)^k$ enumerates sequences of primes summing to $n$.)

## Locating the Dominant Singularity

Since $P(0)=0$ and $P(z)\to\infty$ as $z\to1^{-}$ (the sum of prime reciprocals
diverges), there is a unique $\rho\in(0,1)$ with

$$P(\rho)=\sum_{p\text{ prime}}\rho^{p}=1.$$

**Uniqueness on $|z|=\rho$.** Suppose $P(z)=1$ with $|z|=\rho$. Then

$$1=|P(z)|\le\sum_{p}|z|^{p}=\sum_{p}\rho^{p}=P(\rho)=1,$$

so equality holds in the triangle inequality, forcing all $z^{p}$ to share the s
ame argument. In particular $z^{2}$ and $z^{3}$ have the same argument, so $z^{3
}/z^{2}=z$ is a positive real; combined with $|z|=\rho<1$ this gives $z=\rho$. H
ence $\rho$ is the **unique** singularity of $F$ on the circle $|z|=\rho$.

## Nature of the Singularity

Near $z=\rho$,

$$1-P(z)=-P'(\rho)(z-\rho)+O\!\big((z-\rho)^{2}\big),\qquad P'(\rho)=\sum_{p\tex
t{ prime}}p\,\rho^{p-1}>0.$$

So $z=\rho$ is a simple pole of $F$ with residue

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=\rho}F(z)=\frac{1}{-P'(\rho)\cdot(-1)}\cdot\frac{1}{\rho
^{-1}}\;=\;\\frac{1}{\rho\,P'(\rho)}.$$

More explicitly, $F(z)=\dfrac{1}{\rho\,P'(\rho)}\cdot\dfrac{1}{1-z/\rho}+O(1)$ n
ear $\rho$.

## Singularity Analysis

All other singularities of $F$ lie on $|z|>\rho$ (since $\rho$ is the unique roo
t of $P(z)=1$ of minimal modulus). Writing $F(z)=\dfrac{A}{1-z/\rho}+G(z)$ with
$A=\dfrac{1}{\rho P'(\rho)}$ and $G$ analytic in $|z|<R$ for some $R>\rho$, a st
andard transfer (Pringsheim / Darboux) gives

$$f(n)=A\,\rho^{-n}+O(R^{-n})\quad(n\to\infty).$$

Since $A=\dfrac{1}{\rho P'(\rho)}=\dfrac{1}{\sum_{p}p\,\rho^{p}}$, we obtain

$$\boxed{\,f(x)\;\sim\;\dfrac{\rho^{-x}}{\displaystyle\sum_{p\text{ prime}} p\,\
rho^{p}}\,,\qquad\text{where }\rho\in(0,1)\text{ is the unique solution of }\sum
_{p\text{ prime}}\rho^{p}=1.\,}$$

## Sanity Check (numerical)

Solving $\rho^{2}+\rho^{3}+\rho^{5}+\rho^{7}+\rho^{11}+\cdots=1$ gives $\rho\app
rox 0.677$ (so $\rho^{-1}\approx 1.477$). The constant $C=1/\sum_{p}p\rho^{p}\ap
prox 1/2.48\approx 0.40$. Thus $f(x)\approx 0.40\cdot 1.477^{\,x}$, an exponenti
al growth rate governed entirely by the prime-composition pole $\rho$.
