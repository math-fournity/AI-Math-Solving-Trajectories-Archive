**Answer: No, $T$ is not necessarily continuous.**

First, observe that the condition "$T^{-1}(\{y\})$ is closed for every $y\in Y$"
 is equivalent to "$\ker T$ is closed." Indeed, $T^{-1}(\{0\})=\ker T$, and for
any $y\in Y$, if $T^{-1}(\{y\})\neq\varnothing$ then for any $x_0$ with $T(x_0)=
y$ we have $T^{-1}(\{y\})=x_0+\ker T$, which is closed iff $\ker T$ is closed. S
o the fiber condition adds nothing beyond "$\ker T$ closed."

We now give a counterexample showing that a linear map with closed graph and clo
sed kernel between normed (non-Banach) spaces need not be continuous.

**Counterexample.** Let
$$X=\bigl(C([0,1]),\,\|\cdot\|_{2}\bigr),\qquad Y=\bigl(C([0,1]),\,\|\cdot\|_{\i
nfty}\bigr),$$
and let $T:X\to Y$ be the identity map $T(f)=f$. Note $\|\cdot\|_2$ is a genuine
 norm on $C([0,1])$ (since a continuous function with zero $L^2$-norm vanishes i
dentically), and $X$ is **not** complete.

*Linearity.* $T$ is plainly linear.

*Fibers are closed.* $\ker T=\{0\}$, which is closed in $X$; hence every fiber $
T^{-1}(\{y\})$ is either empty or a singleton, both closed.

*Graph is closed.* Suppose $(f_n,f_n)\to(f,g)$ in $X\times Y$, i.e. $\|f_n-f\|_2
\to 0$ and $\|f_n-g\|_\infty\to 0$. Uniform convergence implies $\|f_n-g\|_2\to
0$. By uniqueness of $L^2$-limits, $f=g$ a.e.; since both are continuous, $f=g$
everywhere. Thus $(f,g)=(f,f)$ lies in the graph of $T$, so the graph is closed.

*$T$ is not continuous.* For $n\ge 1$ define the triangular "tent" function
$$f_n(x)=\max\bigl(0,\; n-n^2|x-\tfrac12|\bigr).$$
Then $f_n\in C([0,1])$, $\|f_n\|_\infty=n$, and $\|f_n\|_2^2=2\int_0^{1/2}(n-n^2
 x)^2\,dx=\frac{2n}{3}$, so $\|f_n\|_2=\sqrt{2n/3}$. Hence
$$\frac{\|T f_n\|_\infty}{\|f_n\|_2}=\frac{n}{\sqrt{2n/3}}=\sqrt{\tfrac{3n}{2}}\
xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\infty.$$
So $T$ is unbounded, hence discontinuous.

**Conclusion.** $T$ is linear, has closed graph, and has all fibers closed, yet
is not continuous. Therefore $T$ is **not** necessarily continuous. (The closed
graph theorem's completeness hypothesis on the domain cannot be dropped, and the
 closed-kernel/fiber condition does not compensate for it.)
