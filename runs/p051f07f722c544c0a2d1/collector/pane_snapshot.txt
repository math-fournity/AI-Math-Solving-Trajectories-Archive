## Solution

**Answer: No.** Completeness of $H^2(\mathbb{D})$ under $\|\cdot\|_X$, together
with continuity of point evaluations at $0$ and $1/2$, does **not** force $\|\cd
ot\|_X$ to be equivalent to the Hardy norm $\|\cdot\|_H$. We construct an explic
it counterexample.

---

### Setup

Let $H = H^2(\mathbb{D})$ with its usual Hardy Hilbert norm $\|\cdot\|_H$. For $
a \in \mathbb{D}$, the point evaluation $\operatorname{ev}_a(f) = f(a)$ is a bou
nded linear functional on $H$, represented (via Riesz) by the reproducing kernel
 $k_a(z) = \frac{1}{1-\bar a\, z} \in H$. In particular
$$k_0(z) = 1, \qquad k_{1/2}(z) = \frac{1}{1 - z/2},$$
which are linearly independent.

Define the closed subspace
$$M := \ker(\operatorname{ev}_0) \cap \ker(\operatorname{ev}_{1/2}) \subseteq H.
$$
Since $\operatorname{ev}_0, \operatorname{ev}_{1/2}$ are linearly independent bo
unded functionals, $M$ is closed of codimension $2$, and
$$M^\perp = \operatorname{span}\{k_0,\, k_{1/2}\}, \qquad H = M \oplus M^\perp \
quad (\text{Hardy orthogonal direct sum}).$$
Every $f \in H$ decomposes uniquely as $f = m + p$ with $m \in M$, $p \in M^\per
p$.

### A non-equivalent complete norm on $M$

$M$ is an infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space (closed subspace of $H$),
 hence linearly isometric to $\ell^2$. In particular $M$ has Hamel dimension $\m
athfrak{c}$, the same as $\ell^1$. Choose any **linear bijection** $S : M \to \e
ll^1$ (which exists by equality of Hamel dimensions) and define
$$\|m\|_X := \|S m\|_{\ell^1}, \qquad m \in M.$$
Because $S$ is a linear bijection and $\ell^1$ is complete, $(M, \|\cdot\|_X)$ i
s a Banach space (isometric to $\ell^1$).

**Claim.** $\|\cdot\|_X$ is **not** equivalent to $\|\cdot\|_H$ on $M$.

*Proof.* If they were equivalent, the identity map would be a Banach-space isomo
rphism between $(M, \|\cdot\|_H)$ and $(M, \|\cdot\|_X)$. But $(M, \|\cdot\|_H)
\cong \ell^2$ (Hilbert, reflexive) while $(M, \|\cdot\|_X) \cong \ell^1$ (non-re
flexive). Since $\ell^2$ and $\ell^1$ are not isomorphic as Banach spaces (e.g.
one is reflexive and the other is not), this is a contradiction. $\square$

### Extending to all of $H^2$

Define, for $f = m + p \in M \oplus M^\perp$,
$$\|f\|_X := \|m\|_X + \|p\|_H.$$

**$\|\cdot\|_X$ is a complete norm on $H^2$.** It is clearly a norm. For complet
eness: $(M, \|\cdot\|_X)$ is Banach (isometric to $\ell^1$) and $(M^\perp, \|\cd
ot\|_H)$ is Banach (finite-dimensional, $\dim = 2$). The $\ell^1$-sum of two Ban
ach spaces is Banach, so $(H^2, \|\cdot\|_X)$ is complete.

**Point evaluations at $0$ and $1/2$ are $\|\cdot\|_X$-continuous.** For $a \in
\{0, 1/2\}$ and $f = m + p$,
$$\operatorname{ev}_a(f) = \operatorname{ev}_a(m) + \operatorname{ev}_a(p) = 0 +
 \operatorname{ev}_a(p) = \operatorname{ev}_a(p),$$
since $m \in M \subseteq \ker(\operatorname{ev}_a)$. As $\operatorname{ev}_a$ is
 $\|\cdot\|_H$-continuous and $M^\perp$ is finite-dimensional,
$$|\operatorname{ev}_a(f)| = |\operatorname{ev}_a(p)| \le \|\operatorname{ev}_a\
|_{H^*}\, \|p\|_H \le \|\operatorname{ev}_a\|_{H^*}\, \|f\|_X.$$
Hence $\operatorname{ev}_0$ and $\operatorname{ev}_{1/2}$ are bounded with respe
ct to $\|\cdot\|_X$.

**$\|\cdot\|_X$ is not equivalent to $\|\cdot\|_H$.** Restricting to $M$ (i.e. t
aking $p = 0$), $\|\cdot\|_X$ and $\|\cdot\|_H$ are not equivalent on $M$ by the
 claim above. Therefore no global constants $c, C > 0$ can satisfy $c\|f\|_H \le
 \|f\|_X \le C\|f\|_H$ for all $f \in H^2$.

---

### Why the closed graph theorem does not save equivalence

One might try to apply the closed graph theorem to the identity $I : (H^2, \|\cd
ot\|_X) \to (H^2, \|\cdot\|_H)$. For the graph to be closed one needs: $f_n \to
0$ in $\|\cdot\|_X$ and $f_n \to g$ in $\|\cdot\|_H$ implies $g = 0$. The $\|\cd
ot\|_X$-convergence gives $f_n(0) \to 0$ and $f_n(1/2) \to 0$; the $\|\cdot\|_H$
-convergence gives $f_n(0) \to g(0)$ and $f_n(1/2) \to g(1/2)$. Thus $g(0) = g(1
/2) = 0$, i.e. $g \in M$ — but this only pins down $g$ at **two** points, which
does not force $g = 0$. The graph is therefore not closed in general, and the cl
osed graph theorem cannot yield equivalence. This is exactly the gap our counter
example exploits: the two point evaluations only control the $2$-dimensional com
plement $M^\perp$, leaving the infinite-dimensional piece $M$ free to carry a ge
nuinely different complete norm.

---

### Conclusion

There exists a complete norm $\|\cdot\|_X$ on $H^2(\mathbb{D})$ for which the po
int evaluations at $z = 0$ and $z = 1/2$ are continuous, yet which is **not** eq
uivalent to the Hardy norm $\|\cdot\|_H$. Therefore the answer is **no**.
