# Stokes Lines for $y'' = z^3 y$ as $z \to \infty$

## Setup

The equation is
$$\frac{d^2 y}{dz^2} = z^3 y, \qquad \text{i.e. } y'' - Q(z)\,y = 0, \quad Q(z)
= z^3.$$

The **turning point** is where $Q(z) = 0$, namely $z = 0$. Stokes lines are curv
es emanating from the turning point along which the two WKB solutions have **equ
al exponential magnitude**, i.e. where the dominant/subdominant roles of the two
 solutions interchange. Equivalently, they are the rays from the turning point o
n which
$$\operatorname{Re}\!\int_{0}^{z} \sqrt{Q(t)}\,dt = 0.$$

## WKB solutions

For large $|z|$ the two formal (WKB) solutions are
$$y_{\pm}(z) \sim Q(z)^{-1/4}\exp\!\left(\pm\int^{z}\sqrt{Q(t)}\,dt\right) = z^{
-3/4}\exp\!\left(\pm\tfrac{2}{5}\,z^{5/2}\right),$$
since
$$\int_{0}^{z}\sqrt{t^{3}}\,dt = \int_{0}^{z}t^{3/2}\,dt = \tfrac{2}{5}\,z^{5/2}
.$$

The exponential factor controlling growth/decay is therefore $\tfrac{2}{5}\,z^{5
/2}$.

## Stokes-line condition

A Stokes line is where the two exponents $\pm\tfrac{2}{5}z^{5/2}$ are purely ima
ginary (so that $|e^{+\cdots}| = |e^{-\cdots}| = 1$ and neither solution dominat
es). Hence
$$\operatorname{Re}\!\bigl(z^{5/2}\bigr) = 0.$$

Write $z = r\,e^{i\theta}$ with $r>0$. Then
$$z^{5/2} = r^{5/2}\,e^{\,i\,5\theta/2}, \qquad \operatorname{Re}\!\bigl(z^{5/2}
\bigr) = r^{5/2}\cos\!\bigl(\tfrac{5\theta}{2}\bigr).$$

The condition $\cos(5\theta/2) = 0$ gives
$$\frac{5\theta}{2} = \frac{\pi}{2} + n\pi,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z} \qquad\Longright
arrow\qquad \theta = \frac{\pi}{5} + \frac{2n\pi}{5}.$$

## The five Stokes lines

Taking $n = 0,1,2,3,4$ (mod $2\pi$) yields five distinct rays from the origin:
$$\boxed{\;\theta = \frac{\pi}{5},\ \frac{3\pi}{5},\ \pi,\ \frac{7\pi}{5},\ \fra
c{9\pi}{5}\;}$$

$$\arg z = \frac{\pi}{5} + \frac{2k\pi}{5}, \qquad k = 0,1,2,3,4.$$

These are five equally spaced rays, separated by angles of $2\pi/5$, emanating f
