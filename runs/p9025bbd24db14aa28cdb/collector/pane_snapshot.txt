## Solution

**Setup.** We work in $F_{q^2}$, with Frobenius $x \mapsto x^q$ the nontrivial a
utomorphism of $F_{q^2}/F_q$. The equation is
$$\alpha + \alpha^q + \beta\beta^q = 0.$$
Recognize the two standard maps:
- **Trace:** $\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha) = \alpha + \alpha^q \in F_q$,
- **Norm:** $N(\beta) = \beta\beta^q \in F_q$.

So the equation becomes $\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha) + N(\beta) = 0$, i.e. $\opera
torname{Tr}(\alpha) = -N(\beta)$, with both sides in $F_q$.

**Fiber sizes.**

*Trace.* The map $\operatorname{Tr}: F_{q^2} \to F_q$ is a surjective $F_q$-line
ar map from a 2-dimensional $F_q$-vector space onto a 1-dimensional one. Its ker
nel has $F_q$-dimension 1, hence size $q$. Therefore every fiber has size $q$:
$$|\{\alpha \in F_{q^2} : \operatorname{Tr}(\alpha) = t\}| = q \quad \text{for e
ach } t \in F_q.$$

*Norm.* The map $N: F_{q^2} \to F_q$ satisfies $N(0)=0$ (unique preimage 0). On
$F_{q^2}^*$, $N$ is a surjective group homomorphism onto $F_q^*$ with kernel $\k
er N = \{x : x^{q+1}=1\}$, the group of $(q+1)$-th roots of unity, of order $q+1
$. Thus every nonzero value has exactly $q+1$ preimages in $F_{q^2}^*$, and $0$
has exactly $1$ preimage. In all cases:
$$|\{\beta \in F_{q^2} : N(\beta) = n\}| = q+1 \quad \text{for each } n \in F_q^
*, \qquad |\{\beta : N(\beta)=0\}| = 1.$$

**Counting.** For each value $n \in F_q$ of the norm, the trace must equal $t =
-n$, which fixes a unique trace value. The choices of $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are i
ndependent once $(t,n)$ is fixed with $t=-n$. Hence
$$\#\{(\alpha,\beta)\} = \sum_{n \in F_q} |\{\alpha:\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha)=-n
\}|\cdot|\{\beta:N(\beta)=n\}|.$$

Splitting the $n=0$ term from the $n\neq 0$ terms:
- $n=0$: $|\{\alpha:\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha)=0\}|=q$, $|\{\beta:N(\beta)=0\}|=1
$, contributing $q\cdot 1 = q$.
- $n\neq 0$ (there are $q-1$ such values): each contributes $q\cdot(q+1)$, contr
ibuting $(q-1)\cdot q(q+1)$.

Total:
$$q + (q-1)\,q(q+1) = q + q(q-1)(q+1) = q + q(q^2-1) = q + q^3 - q = q^3.$$

**Verification.** Cross-check: if instead we naively assumed every norm fiber ha
d size $q+1$ (including $n=0$), we'd get $q\cdot q(q+1)=q^2(q+1)$, which overcou
nts by $q^2(q+1)-q^3 = q^2$, exactly the $q\cdot q$ discrepancy from the $n=0$ f
iber ($q+1$ assumed vs. $1$ actual, times $q$ trace choices, times $1$ value). T
he correct count is $q^3$.

As a sanity check on magnitude: the ambient space $F_{q^2}^2$ has $q^4$ pairs; t
he single scalar equation over $F_q$ should cut the count down by roughly a fact
or of $q$, giving $\sim q^3$. Consistent.

**Answer:** The number of solutions is $\boxed{q^3}$.
