## Proof

We prove that $\lim_{x\to\infty} \frac{\pi(x)}{x} = 0$.

### Step 1: Bounding the Chebyshev theta function

Define $\theta(x) = \sum_{p \le x} \ln p$, the sum ranging over primes $p \le x$
.

Consider the central binomial coefficient $\binom{2n}{n} = \frac{(2n)!}{(n!)^2}$
. Every prime $p$ with $n < p \le 2n$ divides $(2n)!$ but does not divide $(n!)^
2$ (since $p > n$ implies $p \nmid n!$, hence $p \nmid (n!)^2$). Therefore each
such prime divides $\binom{2n}{n}$, giving:

$$\prod_{n < p \le 2n} p \;\Big|\; \binom{2n}{n}.$$

Since $\binom{2n}{n} \le \sum_{k=0}^{2n}\binom{2n}{k} = 2^{2n} = 4^n$, we obtain
:

$$\prod_{n < p \le 2n} p \le 4^n.$$

Taking logarithms:

$$\theta(2n) - \theta(n) = \sum_{n < p \le 2n} \ln p \le n \ln 4. \qquad (\ast)$
$

### Step 2: $\theta(x) = O(x)$

Set $n = 2^k$ in $(\ast)$:

$$\theta(2^{k+1}) - \theta(2^k) \le 2^k \ln 4.$$

Telescoping from $k=0$ to $K-1$ (noting $\theta(1)=0$):

$$\theta(2^K) \le (2^K - 1)\ln 4 < 2^K \ln 4.$$

For general $x \ge 2$, choose $K$ with $2^K \le x < 2^{K+1}$. Then:

$$\theta(x) \le \theta(2^{K+1}) < 2^{K+1}\ln 4 \le 2x\ln 4. \qquad (\ast\ast)$$

### Step 3: Relating $\pi(x)$ to $\theta(x)$

For $x \ge 4$, split the sum defining $\theta$:

$$\theta(x) = \sum_{p \le x} \ln p \ge \sum_{\sqrt{x} < p \le x} \ln p \ge \sum_
{\sqrt{x} < p \le x} \ln\sqrt{x} = \bigl(\pi(x) - \pi(\sqrt{x})\bigr)\cdot\tfrac
{1}{2}\ln x,$$

where the second inequality uses $\ln p \ge \ln\sqrt{x}$ for $p > \sqrt{x}$. Rea
rranging and applying $(\ast\ast)$:

$$\pi(x) - \pi(\sqrt{x}) \le \frac{2\,\theta(x)}{\ln x} \le \frac{4x\ln 4}{\ln x
}.$$

Since $\pi(\sqrt{x}) \le \sqrt{x}$ (there are at most $\sqrt{x}$ integers up to
$\sqrt{x}$):

$$\pi(x) \le \frac{4x\ln 4}{\ln x} + \sqrt{x}.$$

Dividing by $x$:

$$\frac{\pi(x)}{x} \le \frac{4\ln 4}{\ln x} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}.$$

### Step 4: Taking the limit

As $x \to \infty$:
- $\dfrac{4\ln 4}{\ln x} \to 0$,
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}} \to 0$.

Hence $\dfrac{\pi(x)}{x} \le \dfrac{4\ln 4}{\ln x} + \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}} \to 0$.

Since $\dfrac{\pi(x)}{x} \ge 0$ for all $x > 0$, the **Squeeze Theorem** yields:

$$\boxed{\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\pi(x)}{x} = 0.}$$
