## Setup and Explicit Computation

The domain $\Omega$ is an L-shaped domain with a reentrant corner at the origin
of interior angle $3\pi/2$ (the angular range near the origin is $\theta \in (\p
i/2, 2\pi)$). The origin lies on $\partial\Omega$, not in $\Omega$.

Using the convention $\nabla \times \psi = (\partial_2 \psi,\, -\partial_1 \psi)
$ for a scalar $\psi$ (consistent with the problem's definition $\nabla \times v
 = \partial_1 v_2 - \partial_2 v_1$), I compute the partial derivatives in polar
 coordinates.

With $\psi = r^{2/3}\cos(\tfrac{2}{3}\theta - \tfrac{\pi}{3})$:
$$\partial_r \psi = \tfrac{2}{3}r^{-1/3}\cos(\tfrac{2}{3}\theta - \tfrac{\pi}{3}

tfrac{\pi}{3}).$$

Converting to Cartesian:
$$\partial_1 \psi = \partial_r\psi\cos\theta - \tfrac{1}{r}\partial_\theta\psi\s
in\theta = \tfrac{2}{3}r^{-1/3}\cos(\tfrac{1}{3}\theta + \tfrac{\pi}{3}),$$
$$\partial_2 \psi = \partial_r\psi\sin\theta + \tfrac{1}{r}\partial_\theta\psi\c
os\theta = \tfrac{2}{3}r^{-1/3}\sin(\tfrac{1}{3}\theta + \tfrac{\pi}{3}).$$

Therefore:
$$v = \left(\tfrac{2}{3}r^{-1/3}\sin(\tfrac{1}{3}\theta+\tfrac{\pi}{3}),\; -\tfr
ac{2}{3}r^{-1/3}\cos(\tfrac{1}{3}\theta+\tfrac{\pi}{3})\right).$$

The magnitude is $|v| = \tfrac{2}{3}r^{-1/3}$, so $|v|^2 = \tfrac{4}{9}r^{-2/3}$

## 1. $v \in L^2(\Omega)$

$$\int_\Omega |v|^2\,dx = \frac{4}{9}\int_\Omega r^{-2/3}\,dx \le C\int_0^{1/\sq
rt{2}} r^{-2/3}\cdot r\,dr = C\int_0^{1/\sqrt{2}} r^{1/3}\,dr = C\cdot\tfrac{3}{
4}r^{4/3}\Big|_0^{1/\sqrt{2}} < \infty.$$

The integral converges since $1/3 > -1$. ✓

## 2. $\operatorname{div} v = 0$ and $v \in H(\mathrm{div};\,\Omega)$

Since $v = \nabla \times \psi = (\partial_2\psi,\, -\partial_1\psi)$:
$$\operatorname{div} v = \partial_1(\partial_2\psi) + \partial_2(-\partial_1\psi
) = \partial_1\partial_2\psi - \partial_2\partial_1\psi = 0.$$

This holds pointwise in $\Omega$ (where $\psi$ is smooth, since the origin is on
 $\partial\Omega$). Thus $\operatorname{div} v = 0 \in L^2(\Omega)$, giving $v \
in H(\mathrm{div};\,\Omega)$. ✓

## 3. $v \in H(\mathrm{curl};\,\Omega)$

$$\nabla \times v = \partial_1 v_2 - \partial_2 v_1 = -\partial_1(\partial_1\psi
) - \partial_2(\partial_2\psi) = -\Delta\psi.$$

The function $\psi = r^{2/3}\cos(\tfrac{2}{3}\theta - \tfrac{\pi}{3})$ is of the
 form $r^\alpha\cos(\alpha\theta + \varphi)$ with $\alpha = 2/3$. Such functions
 are harmonic: in polar coordinates,
$$\Delta\psi = \left[\alpha(\alpha-1) + \alpha - \alpha^2\right]r^{\alpha-2}\cos
(\alpha\theta+\varphi) = 0.$$

Since the origin is on $\partial\Omega$ (not in $\Omega$), $\psi$ is smooth and
harmonic throughout $\Omega$. Therefore:
$$\nabla \times v = -\Delta\psi = 0 \in L^2(\Omega),$$

so $v \in H(\mathrm{curl};\,\Omega)$. ✓

## 4. $v \notin H^1(\Omega;\,\mathbb{R}^2)$

The derivatives of $v$ scale as $r^{-4/3}$ (one power of $r$ stronger singularit
y than $v$ itself). Explicitly, for $v_1 = \tfrac{2}{3}r^{-1/3}\sin(\tfrac{1}{3}

Thus $|\nabla v_1|^2 = \tfrac{4}{81}r^{-8/3}$, and similarly $|\nabla v_2|^2 = \

$$|\nabla v|^2 = \tfrac{8}{81}\,r^{-8/3}.$$

The $L^2$ norm of $\nabla v$ over a neighborhood of the reentrant corner:
$$\int_\Omega |\nabla v|^2\,dx \ge c\int_0^{\varepsilon}\int_{\pi/2}^{2\pi} r^{-
8/3}\cdot r\,d\theta\,dr = c'\int_0^{\varepsilon} r^{-5/3}\,dr = \infty,$$

since $-5/3 < -1$, the integral diverges. Therefore $\nabla v \notin L^2(\Omega;
\,\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})$, and $v \notin H^1(\Omega;\,\mathbb{R}^2)$. ✓
