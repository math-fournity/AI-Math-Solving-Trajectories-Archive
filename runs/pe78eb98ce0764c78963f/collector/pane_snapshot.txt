Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a dense ℝ-subalgebra 𝒜 of C
(X,ℝ) must contain a function f with f(x)=0, f(y)=1 for any distinct x,y ∈ X.

**Answer: Yes.** Here is the proof.

---

**Proof.**

Let $X$ be a compact subset of $\mathbb{R}$ and let $\mathcal{A}$ be an $\mathbb
{R}$-subalgebra of $\mathscr{C}(X,\mathbb{R})$ that is dense in $\mathscr{C}(X,\
mathbb{R})$ (with respect to the uniform norm). Let $x, y \in X$ be distinct poi
nts. We show there exists $f \in \mathcal{A}$ with $f(x)=0$ and $f(y)=1$.

**Key object.** Consider the joint evaluation map
$$\Phi: \mathcal{A} \to \mathbb{R}^2, \qquad \Phi(f) = (f(x),\, f(y)).$$
Since $\mathcal{A}$ is an $\mathbb{R}$-vector space and $\Phi$ is linear, the im
age
$$V := \Phi(\mathcal{A}) = \{(f(x), f(y)) : f \in \mathcal{A}\}$$
is a **linear subspace** of $\mathbb{R}^2$.

**Step 1: $V$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}^2$.**

Let $(a, b) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ be arbitrary. Since $X \subset \mathbb{R}$ and $x
\neq y$, the function
$$h(t) = a + \frac{b - a}{y - x}(t - x), \qquad t \in X,$$
is continuous on $X$ (it is the restriction of an affine function) and satisfies
 $h(x) = a$, $h(y) = b$.

Since $\mathcal{A}$ is dense in $\mathscr{C}(X,\mathbb{R})$, there exists a sequ
ence $\{f_n\} \subset \mathcal{A}$ with $\|f_n - h\|_\infty \to 0$. In particula
r,
$$f_n(x) \to h(x) = a \quad \text{and} \quad f_n(y) \to h(y) = b.$$
Thus $\Phi(f_n) = (f_n(x), f_n(y)) \to (a, b)$, so $(a,b) \in \overline{V}$. Sin
ce $(a,b)$ was arbitrary, $V$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}^2$.

**Step 2: $V = \mathbb{R}^2$.**

Every linear subspace of a finite-dimensional normed space is closed. Since $\ma
thbb{R}^2$ is finite-dimensional, $V$ is a closed linear subspace of $\mathbb{R}
^2$. Being both dense and closed, $V = \mathbb{R}^2$.

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

Since $V = \mathbb{R}^2$, the point $(0, 1) \in V$. By definition of $V$, there
exists $f \in \mathcal{A}$ such that $\Phi(f) = (0, 1)$, i.e.,
$$f(x) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad f(y) = 1.$$

**Remark.** The argument uses only that $\mathcal{A}$ is a dense **linear subspa
ce** of $\mathscr{C}(X,\mathbb{R})$; the multiplicative closure (subalgebra prop
erty) is not needed. The essential ingredients are: (i) the joint evaluation ima
ge $V$ is a linear subspace of $\mathbb{R}^2$, (ii) density of $\mathcal{A}$ for
ces $V$ to be dense, and (iii) finite-dimensionality of $\mathbb{R}^2$ forces ev
ery subspace to be closed, so $V = \mathbb{R}^2$.

Therefore, for every compact $X \subset \mathbb{R}$ and every dense $\mathbb{R}$
-subalgebra $\mathcal{A}$ of $\mathscr{C}(X,\mathbb{R})$, and for every pair of
distinct points $x, y \in X$, there exists $f \in \mathcal{A}$ with $f(x) = 0$ a
nd $f(y) = 1$. $\blacksquare$
