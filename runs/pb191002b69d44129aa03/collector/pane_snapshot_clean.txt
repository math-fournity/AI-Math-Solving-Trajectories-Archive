## Stability Analysis of $\ddot{x} = (x-\lambda)(x^2-\lambda)$

### Setup: Conservative Structure

The system $\ddot{x} = f(x)$ with $f(x) = (x-\lambda)(x^2-\lambda)$ is conservat
ive (no damping). Define the potential

$$V(x) = -\int f(x)\,dx = -\frac{x^4}{4} + \frac{\lambda x^3}{3} + \frac{\lambda
 x^2}{2} - \lambda^2 x + C,$$

so that $\ddot{x} = -V'(x)$. The conserved energy is $E = \frac{1}{2}\dot{x}^2 +
 V(x)$.

Equilibria occur where $f(x_0) = 0$, i.e., $x_0 = \lambda$ or $x_0 = \pm\sqrt{\l
ambda}$ (the latter only for $\lambda \geq 0$). Since the system is conservative
:

- If $V''(x_0) = -f'(x_0) > 0$ (i.e., $f'(x_0) < 0$): $V$ has a local minimum →
**center** (Lyapunov stable, not asymptotically stable).
- If $V''(x_0) = -f'(x_0) < 0$ (i.e., $f'(x_0) > 0$): $V$ has a local maximum →
**saddle** (unstable).
- If $f'(x_0) = 0$: **degenerate**, requires higher-order analysis.

### Linearization

$$f'(x) = (x^2 - \lambda) + 2x(x-\lambda) = 3x^2 - 2\lambda x - \lambda.$$

---

### Equilibrium $x_0 = \lambda$

$$f'(\lambda) = 3\lambda^2 - 2\lambda^2 - \lambda = \lambda^2 - \lambda = \lambd
a(\lambda - 1).$$

- **$\lambda < 0$ or $\lambda > 1$**: $f'(\lambda) > 0$ → **saddle (unstable)**.
- **$0 < \lambda < 1$**: $f'(\lambda) < 0$ → **center (stable)**.
- **$\lambda = 0$ and $\lambda = 1$**: degenerate (analyzed below).

### Equilibrium $x_0 = \sqrt{\lambda}$ (exists for $\lambda \geq 0$)

$$f'(\sqrt{\lambda}) = 3\lambda - 2\lambda\sqrt{\lambda} - \lambda = 2\lambda(1
- \sqrt{\lambda}).$$

- **$0 < \lambda < 1$**: $\sqrt{\lambda} < 1$, so $f' > 0$ → **saddle (unstable)
**.
- **$\lambda > 1$**: $\sqrt{\lambda} > 1$, so $f' < 0$ → **center (stable)**.
- **$\lambda = 0$**: degenerate (merges with $x_0 = \lambda = 0$).
- **$\lambda = 1$**: degenerate (merges with $x_0 = \lambda = 1$).

### Equilibrium $x_0 = -\sqrt{\lambda}$ (exists for $\lambda \geq 0$)

$$f'(-\sqrt{\lambda}) = 3\lambda + 2\lambda\sqrt{\lambda} - \lambda = 2\lambda(1
 + \sqrt{\lambda}).$$

For all $\lambda > 0$: both $2\lambda > 0$ and $1 + \sqrt{\lambda} > 0$, so $f'
> 0$ → **saddle (unstable)** for all $\lambda > 0$.

At $\lambda = 0$: degenerate (merges with $x_0 = 0$).

---

### Degenerate Cases

**$\lambda = 0$:** The system becomes $\ddot{x} = x^3$, with a single equilibriu
m $x_0 = 0$ (triple root). The potential is $V(x) = -x^4/4$, which has a **globa
l maximum** at $x = 0$. The energy $E = \frac{1}{2}\dot{x}^2 - x^4/4$:
- For $E > 0$: $\dot{x}^2 = 2E + x^4/2 > 0$ for all $x$; trajectories escape to
$\pm\infty$.
- For $E < 0$: motion is confined to $|x| \geq (4|E|)^{1/4}$, i.e., the particle
 is repelled from the origin.
- For $E = 0$: $\dot{x} = \pm x^2/\sqrt{2}$; the separatrix, trajectories approa
ch $x = 0$ asymptotically but never cross.

The origin is a **cusp** — **unstable**.

**$\lambda = 1$:** The system becomes $\ddot{x} = (x-1)^2(x+1)$. Equilibria at $
x_0 = 1$ (double root) and $x_0 = -1$.

- At $x_0 = -1$: $f'(-1) = 3 + 2 - 1 = 4 > 0$ → **saddle (unstable)**.
- At $x_0 = 1$: $f'(1) = 0$, $f''(1) = 6(1) - 2 = 4 > 0$. Near $x = 1$: $f(x) \a
pprox 2(x-1)^2 \geq 0$, so the acceleration is **always non-negative**. A partic
le slightly above $x = 1$ with zero velocity accelerates away (to the right); a
particle slightly below accelerates toward $x = 1$ but only approaches it asympt
otically. The potential has an **inflection point** at $x = 1$: $V(x) \approx V(
1) - \frac{2}{3}(x-1)^3$. This is a **semi-stable (degenerate) equilibrium — Lya
punov unstable**, since arbitrarily close initial conditions above $x = 1$ diver
ge.

---

### Bifurcation Summary

| $\lambda$ range | Equilibria | Stability pattern |
|---|---|---|
| $\lambda < 0$ | $x = \lambda$ only | saddle |
| $\lambda = 0$ | $x = 0$ (triple) | cusp (unstable) |
| $0 < \lambda < 1$ | $-\sqrt{\lambda} < \lambda < \sqrt{\lambda}$ | saddle, **c
enter**, saddle |
| $\lambda = 1$ | $x = -1$, $x = 1$ (double) | saddle, semi-stable (unstable) |
| $\lambda > 1$ | $-\sqrt{\lambda} < \sqrt{\lambda} < \lambda$ | saddle, **cente
r**, saddle |

**Bifurcation at $\lambda = 0$ (pitchfork-type):** As $\lambda$ increases throug
h $0$, the single saddle equilibrium $x = \lambda$ splits into three equilibria:
 the saddle at $x = \lambda$ becomes a **center**, and two new saddles appear at
 $x = \pm\sqrt{\lambda}$. This is a supercritical-pitchfork-like bifurcation in
the conservative setting (the symmetry $x \to -x$ of $f$ at $\lambda = 0$ enable
s the pitchfork structure).

**Bifurcation at $\lambda = 1$ (saddle-center collision / exchange of stability)
:** As $\lambda$ increases through $1$, the center at $x = \lambda$ and the sadd
le at $x = \sqrt{\lambda}$ (which satisfy $\lambda < \sqrt{\lambda}$ for $\lambd
a < 1$) coalesce at $x = 1$ and then separate with **exchanged roles** ($\sqrt{\
lambda} < \lambda$ for $\lambda > 1$): $x = \sqrt{\lambda}$ becomes the center a
nd $x = \lambda$ becomes the saddle. The equilibrium at $x = -\sqrt{\lambda}$ re
mains a saddle throughout.

### Conclusion

The system exhibits two bifurcations as $\lambda$ varies:

1. **At $\lambda = 0$**: A pitchfork-type bifurcation where one unstable equilib
rium splits into a stable center flanked by two unstable saddles.

2. **At $\lambda = 1$**: A saddle-center collision where the stable center and a
n adjacent unstable saddle merge into a degenerate (semi-stable, Lyapunov unstab
le) equilibrium, then re-emerge with exchanged stability roles.

For all $\lambda > 0$, $\lambda \neq 1$, the phase portrait consists of a single
 stable center sandwiched between two unstable saddles — the hallmark of a doubl
e-well potential in a conservative system. The equilibrium at $x = -\sqrt{\lambd
a}$ is always a saddle for $\lambda > 0$.
