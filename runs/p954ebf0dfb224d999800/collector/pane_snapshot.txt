## Derivation of the Sokhotsky–Plemelj Formulas

**Setup.** Let $S$ be a closed Jordan curve with continuous curvature, positivel
y oriented (counterclockwise), bounding the interior domain $D^+$ and exterior d
omain $D^-$. Let $f \in C^2(S)$ and define

$$F(z) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_S \frac{f(t)}{t-z}\,dt, \qquad z \notin S.$$

Fix $t_0 \in S$. We seek the limits $F^+(t_0) = \lim_{z\to t_0,\, z\in D^+} F(z)
$ and $F^-(t_0) = \lim_{z\to t_0,\, z\in D^-} F(z)$.

---

### Step 1. Decomposition

Write $f(t) = f(t_0) + \bigl(f(t)-f(t_0)\bigr)$ and split:

$$F(z) = \frac{f(t_0)}{2\pi i}\int_S \frac{dt}{t-z} \;+\; \underbrace{\frac{1}{2
\pi i}\int_S \frac{f(t)-f(t_0)}{t-z}\,dt}_{=:\,\Psi(z)}.$$

**Evaluation of the constant-density integral.** By the Cauchy integral formula
applied to $g\equiv 1$:

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_S \frac{dt}{t-z} = \begin{cases} 1, & z\in D^+,\\[4pt] 0,
 & z\in D^-. \end{cases}$$

(For $z\in D^-$ the integrand is analytic inside $S$, so the integral vanishes b
y Cauchy's theorem.)

Hence:

$$F(z) = \begin{cases} f(t_0) + \Psi(z), & z\in D^+,\\[4pt] \Psi(z), & z\in D^-.
 \end{cases} \tag{1}$$

---

### Step 2. The limit of $\Psi(z)$ as $z\to t_0$

Since $f\in C^2(S)$, the function

$$\phi(t) := \frac{f(t)-f(t_0)}{t-t_0},\quad t\neq t_0; \qquad \phi(t_0):=f'(t_0
)$$

is well-defined and **continuous** on $S$ (the singularity at $t_0$ is removable
). Then $f(t)-f(t_0)=\phi(t)(t-t_0)$, and:

$$\Psi(z) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_S \frac{\phi(t)(t-t_0)}{t-z}\,dt.$$

Using $\frac{t-t_0}{t-z} = 1 + \frac{z-t_0}{t-z}$:

$$\Psi(z) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_S \phi(t)\,dt \;+\; \frac{z-t_0}{2\pi i}\int_S
\frac{\phi(t)}{t-z}\,dt. \tag{2}$$

**Claim.** $\displaystyle\lim_{z\to t_0}\Psi(z) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_S \frac{f
(t)-f(t_0)}{t-t_0}\,dt$, with the same limit from both sides.

*Proof of Claim.* From (2), the first term is the constant $\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int
_S \phi(t)\,dt = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_S \frac{f(t)-f(t_0)}{t-t_0}\,dt$. We must
show the second term vanishes as $z\to t_0$.

Since $\phi$ is continuous on the compact set $S$, $|\phi|\le M$. Parameterize $
S$ by arc length $t=t(s)$, $s\in[0,L]$, with $t_0=t(s_0)$. Because $S$ has conti
nuous curvature, near $s_0$:

$$t(s)-t_0 = (s-s_0)\,\tau_0 + O\bigl((s-s_0)^2\bigr),$$

where $\tau_0$ is the unit tangent at $t_0$. Let $z = t_0 + \delta\, n_0 + O(\de
lta^2)$ approach $t_0$ along the inward normal $n_0$ (the general approach reduc
es to this by rotation/translation). Then for $|s-s_0|$ small:

$$|t(s)-z| \ge c_1\sqrt{(s-s_0)^2+\delta^2}$$

for some constant $c_1>0$ (the normal component $\delta$ prevents $t(s)-z$ from
vanishing). Split the integral over $|s-s_0|\le\epsilon$ and its complement:

- **Near part:** $\displaystyle\left|\int_{|s-s_0|\le\epsilon}\frac{\phi(t(s))\,
t'(s)}{t(s)-z}\,ds\right| \le \frac{M}{c_1}\int_{-\epsilon}^{\epsilon}\frac{ds}{
\sqrt{s^2+\delta^2}} = \frac{2M}{c_1}\log\frac{\epsilon+\sqrt{\epsilon^2+\delta^
2}}{|\delta|} = O\bigl(|\log|\delta||\bigr).$

- **Far part:** For $|z-t_0|<\epsilon/2$ and $|s-s_0|>\epsilon$, we have $|t(s)-
z|\ge c_2\epsilon$, so the far integral is $O(1/\epsilon)$, bounded for fixed $\
epsilon$.

Combining, $\left|\int_S \frac{\phi(t)}{t-z}\,dt\right| = O\bigl(|\log|z-t_0||\b
igr)$ as $z\to t_0$. Therefore the second term in (2) is bounded by

$$|z-t_0|\cdot O\bigl(|\log|z-t_0||\bigr) \;\longrightarrow\; 0 \quad\text{as }
z\to t_0.$$

This establishes the Claim. $\square$

### Step 3. The principal value $\displaystyle\text{P.V.}\int_S \frac{dt}{t-t_0}
 = i\pi$

Since $f(t)-f(t_0) = O(|t-t_0|)$, the integral $\int_S \frac{f(t)-f(t_0)}{t-t_0}
\,dt$ is a **proper** (convergent) integral. We relate it to the principal value
:

$$\text{P.V.}\int_S \frac{f(t)}{t-t_0}\,dt = \int_S \frac{f(t)-f(t_0)}{t-t_0}\,d
t \;+\; f(t_0)\underbrace{\text{P.V.}\int_S \frac{dt}{t-t_0}}_{=:\,I_0}. \tag{3}
$$

**Computation of $I_0$.** Observe $\frac{t'(s)}{t(s)-t_0} = \frac{d}{ds}\log\big
l(t(s)-t_0\bigr)$. Since $S$ is closed, $t(0)=t(L)$, so:

$$I_0 = \lim_{\epsilon\to 0^+}\left[\log\bigl(t(s)-t_0\bigr)\right]_{0}^{s_0-\ep
silon} + \left[\log\bigl(t(s)-t_0\bigr)\right]_{s_0+\epsilon}^{L}$$

$$= \lim_{\epsilon\to 0^+}\Bigl[\log\bigl(t(s_0-\epsilon)-t_0\bigr) - \log\bigl(
t(s_0+\epsilon)-t_0\bigr)\Bigr],$$

where the boundary terms at $s=0$ and $s=L$ cancel because $t(0)=t(L)$. Now:

$$t(s_0+\epsilon)-t_0 = \epsilon\,\tau_0 + O(\epsilon^2), \qquad t(s_0-\epsilon)
-t_0 = -\epsilon\,\tau_0 + O(\epsilon^2).$$

Both have modulus $\sim\epsilon$, so $\log|t(s_0-\epsilon)-t_0| - \log|t(s_0+\ep
silon)-t_0| \to 0$. The arguments satisfy:

$$\arg\bigl(t(s_0+\epsilon)-t_0\bigr) \to \arg(\tau_0), \qquad \arg\bigl(t(s_0-\
epsilon)-t_0\bigr) \to \arg(-\tau_0) = \arg(\tau_0)+\pi.$$

Therefore:

$$I_0 = i\bigl[(\arg\tau_0 + \pi) - \arg\tau_0\bigr] = i\pi. \tag{4}$$

### Step 4. Assemble the formulas

From the Claim (Step 2) and equation (3)+(4):

$$\lim_{z\to t_0}\Psi(z) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\text{P.V.}\int_S \frac{f(t)}{t-t_0}\
,dt \;-\; \frac{f(t_0)}{2\pi i}\cdot i\pi = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\text{P.V.}\int_S \f
rac{f(t)}{t-t_0}\,dt \;-\; \frac{1}{2}f(t_0).$$

Substituting into (1):

**From $D^+$:**
$$F^+(t_0) = f(t_0) + \left[\frac{1}{2\pi i}\text{P.V.}\int_S \frac{f(t)}{t-t_0}
\,dt - \frac{1}{2}f(t_0)\right] = \boxed{\frac{1}{2}f(t_0) + \frac{1}{2\pi i}\,\
text{P.V.}\!\int_S \frac{f(t)}{t-t_0}\,dt.}$$

**From $D^-$:**
$$F^-(t_0) = 0 + \left[\frac{1}{2\pi i}\text{P.V.}\int_S \frac{f(t)}{t-t_0}\,dt
- \frac{1}{2}f(t_0)\right] = \boxed{-\frac{1}{2}f(t_0) + \frac{1}{2\pi i}\,\text
{P.V.}\!\int_S \frac{f(t)}{t-t_0}\,dt.}$$

### Summary (Sokhotsky–Plemelj Formulas)

For a closed smooth ($C^1$ curvature) Jordan curve $S$ with $f\in C^2(S)$ and $t
_0\in S$:

$$\boxed{F^+(t_0) = +\frac{1}{2}\,f(t_0) + \frac{1}{2\pi i}\,\text{P.V.}\!\int_S
 \frac{f(t)}{t-t_0}\,dt,}$$

$$\boxed{F^-(t_0) = -\frac{1}{2}\,f(t_0) + \frac{1}{2\pi i}\,\text{P.V.}\!\int_S
 \frac{f(t)}{t-t_0}\,dt,}$$

where the principal value is taken as $\text{P.V.}\int_S = \lim_{\epsilon\to 0^+
}\int_{S\setminus S_\epsilon}$ with $S_\epsilon$ the arc of $S$ within arclength
 $\epsilon$ of $t_0$.

**Key ingredients of the proof:**
1. **Decomposition** into a constant-density part (evaluated by Cauchy's formula
/theorem) and a vanishing-density part $\Psi(z)$.
2. **Continuity of $\Psi$ at $t_0$**: the factor $f(t)-f(t_0)=O(|t-t_0|)$ cancel
s the singularity, making $\phi(t)=\frac{f(t)-f(t_0)}{t-t_0}$ continuous; the re
sidual term $|z-t_0|\cdot O(|\log|z-t_0||)\to 0$.
3. **Evaluation of $I_0 = \text{P.V.}\int_S \frac{dt}{t-t_0}=i\pi$** via $\frac{
d}{ds}\log(t(s)-t_0)$ and the argument jump of $\pi$ across $t_0$ on the closed
curve.
