## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. For any prime $p$ (including $p \neq 2$), there exists a Lie gro
up $G$ with $p$-torsion in $\pi_1(G)$.

**Construction.** For any integer $n \geq 2$, consider the projective special un
itary group
$$\mathrm{PSU}(n) = \mathrm{SU}(n) / Z(\mathrm{SU}(n)),$$
where the center of $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ is
$$Z(\mathrm{SU}(n)) = \{\,\zeta I_n : \zeta^n = 1\,\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{
Z}.$$

**Step 1: $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ is simply connected.**

We proceed by induction on $n$.

- **Base case:** $\mathrm{SU}(1) = \{1\}$ is a point, hence simply connected.

- **Inductive step:** For $n \geq 2$, there is a fiber bundle
$$\mathrm{SU}(n-1) \hookrightarrow \mathrm{SU}(n) \xrightarrow{\;\varphi\;} S^{2
n-1},$$
where $\varphi$ sends a matrix to its first column (a unit vector in $\mathbb{C}
^n$, i.e., an element of $S^{2n-1}$). The long exact sequence in homotopy gives:
$$\cdots \to \pi_1(\mathrm{SU}(n-1)) \to \pi_1(\mathrm{SU}(n)) \to \pi_1(S^{2n-1
}) \to \cdots$$

Since $2n - 1 \geq 3$ for $n \geq 2$, the sphere $S^{2n-1}$ is simply connected,
 so $\pi_1(S^{2n-1}) = 0$. Thus the map $\pi_1(\mathrm{SU}(n-1)) \to \pi_1(\math
rm{SU}(n))$ is surjective. By the induction hypothesis $\pi_1(\mathrm{SU}(n-1))
= 0$, so $\pi_1(\mathrm{SU}(n)) = 0$.

Hence $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ is simply connected for all $n \geq 1$.

**Step 2: Compute $\pi_1(\mathrm{PSU}(n))$.**

Since $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ is simply connected and the quotient map
$$q: \mathrm{SU}(n) \to \mathrm{PSU}(n) = \mathrm{SU}(n)/Z(\mathrm{SU}(n))$$
is a covering map with deck group $Z(\mathrm{SU}(n)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z
}$, the group $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ is the universal cover of $\mathrm{PSU}(n)$. The
fundamental group is the group of deck transformations:
$$\pi_1(\mathrm{PSU}(n)) \cong Z(\mathrm{SU}(n)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}.$$

**Step 3: Extract $p$-torsion for $p \neq 2$.**

For any prime $p$, set $n = p$. Then:
$$\pi_1(\mathrm{PSU}(p)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}.$$

This is a cyclic group of order $p$, hence is entirely $p$-torsion. In particula
r:

- For $p = 3$: $\mathrm{PSU}(3)$ is a compact, connected Lie group with $\pi_1(\
mathrm{PSU}(3)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$, which is $3$-torsion, and $3 \neq
 2$.

- For $p = 5$: $\mathrm{PSU}(5)$ has $\pi_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}$, which
 is $5$-torsion, and $5 \neq 2$.

More generally, for **every** prime $p \neq 2$, the Lie group $\mathrm{PSU}(p)$
provides an explicit example of $p$-torsion in the fundamental group.

**Conclusion.** A Lie group $G$ can indeed have $p$-torsion in $\pi_1(G)$ for a
prime $p \neq 2$. The groups $\mathrm{PSU}(p)$ for odd primes $p$ furnish concre
te counterexamples to any claim that only $2$-torsion can occur. $\blacksquare$
