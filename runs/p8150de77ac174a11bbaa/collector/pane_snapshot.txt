## Proof

Place $C$ at the origin. Let $A = \mathbf{a}$, $B = \mathbf{b}$, so the midpoint
s are $A' = \mathbf{b}/2$ and $B' = \mathbf{a}/2$.

Let $\omega_1$ (through $A, A'$) and $\omega_2$ (through $B, B'$) be the two cir
cles, each with an arc of angular measure $2\theta$ on the side of $C$. We show
$C$ lies on their radical axis, i.e., $\operatorname{pow}_{\omega_1}(C)=\operato
rname{pow}_{\omega_2}(C)$.

**Power formula.** For a circle through $\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}$ with chord $\mat
hbf{d}=\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{q}$, midpoint $M=(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q})/2$, and cente
r $\mathbf{o}=M+t\,R(\mathbf{d})$ ($R$ = 90° CCW rotation), since $r^2=|\mathbf{
d}|^2/4+t^2|\mathbf{d}|^2$:

$$\operatorname{pow}(O)=|\mathbf{o}|^2-r^2=|M|^2-\tfrac{|\mathbf{d}|^2}{4}+2t\,M
\cdot R(\mathbf{d}),$$

using $\mathbf{u}\cdot R(\mathbf{v})=-[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}]$ (2D cross product
).

**Circle $\omega_1$** ($\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{a},\;\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{b}/2$): $M_1=
\mathbf{a}/2+\mathbf{b}/4$, $\mathbf{d}_1=\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}/2$. Direct compu
tation:

$$|M_1|^2-\frac{|\mathbf{d}_1|^2}{4}=\frac{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}}{2},\qquad
M_1\cdot R(\mathbf{d}_1)=\frac{[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]}{2}.$$

So $\operatorname{pow}_{\omega_1}(C)=\frac{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}}{2}+t_1\,[\
mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]$.

**Circle $\omega_2$** ($\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{b},\;\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{a}/2$): $M_2=
\mathbf{b}/2+\mathbf{a}/4$, $\mathbf{d}_2=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}/2$. By symmetry
($\mathbf{a}\leftrightarrow\mathbf{b}$):

$$|M_2|^2-\frac{|\mathbf{d}_2|^2}{4}=\frac{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}}{2},\qquad
M_2\cdot R(\mathbf{d}_2)=-\frac{[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]}{2}.$$

So $\operatorname{pow}_{\omega_2}(C)=\frac{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}}{2}-t_2\,[\
mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]$.

**Angular measure gives $|t|$.** Since $r=|\mathbf{d}|/(2\sin\theta)$, we get $t
^2=\cot^2\theta/4$, so $t=\pm\frac{\cot\theta}{2}$.

**Lemma (sign from "towards $C$").** *For an arc of angular measure $2\theta$ on
 a chord, built towards a point $P$, the signed distance from the center to the
chord (positive towards $P$) equals $-r\cos\theta$.*

*Proof.* If $2\theta<180°$ (minor arc towards $P$): center is opposite $P$ at di
stance $r\cos\theta>0$; signed distance towards $P$ is $-r\cos\theta$. If $2\the
ta>180°$ (major arc towards $P$): center is on the same side as $P$ at distance
$r|\cos\theta|=-r\cos\theta$ (since $\cos\theta<0$); signed distance towards $P$
 is $-r\cos\theta$. Both cases agree. $\square$

**Determining $t_1, t_2$.** The signed distance from $C=\mathbf{0}$ to line $AA'
$, positive to the left of $\mathbf{d}_1=\overrightarrow{A'A}$, is

$$s_C^{(1)}=\frac{[\mathbf{d}_1,\,-\mathbf{a}]}{|\mathbf{d}_1|}=\frac{-[\mathbf{
a},\mathbf{b}]/2}{|\mathbf{d}_1|},\qquad \operatorname{sgn}(s_C^{(1)})=-\operato
rname{sgn}[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}].$$

The signed distance from $O_1$ to line $AA'$ (same convention) is $t_1|\mathbf{d
}_1|$. By the lemma (towards $C$):

$$t_1|\mathbf{d}_1|\cdot\operatorname{sgn}(s_C^{(1)})=-r_1\cos\theta=-\frac{|\ma
thbf{d}_1|\cos\theta}{2\sin\theta},$$

giving $t_1=\dfrac{\cot\theta}{2\,\operatorname{sgn}[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]}$.

For $\omega_2$, the signed distance from $C$ to line $BB'$, positive to the left
 of $\mathbf{d}_2=\overrightarrow{B'B}$, is

$$s_C^{(2)}=\frac{[\mathbf{d}_2,\,-\mathbf{b}]}{|\mathbf{d}_2|}=\frac{[\mathbf{a
},\mathbf{b}]/2}{|\mathbf{d}_2|},\qquad \operatorname{sgn}(s_C^{(2)})=+\operator
name{sgn}[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}].$$

This yields $t_2=\dfrac{-\cot\theta}{2\,\operatorname{sgn}[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}
]}$.

**Conclusion.** We have $t_1=-t_2$, therefore

$$\operatorname{pow}_{\omega_1}(C)=\frac{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}}{2}+t_1[\math
bf{a},\mathbf{b}]=\frac{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}}{2}-t_2[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]
=\operatorname{pow}_{\omega_2}(C).$$

Since $C$ has equal power with respect to $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$, it lies on
their radical axis, which is precisely the common chord of the two circles. $\bl
acksquare$
