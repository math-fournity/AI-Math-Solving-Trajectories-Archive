## Proof

We start from the line element in coordinates $(t', r')$:
$$
ds^2 = g'_{\mu\nu}\,dx'^\mu dx'^\nu = U\,(dt')^2 - V\,(dr')^2,
$$
with $U=U(t',r')$, $V=V(t',r')$. We seek new coordinates $(t'',r'')$ such that
$$
ds^2 = (dt'')^2 - (dr'')^2 = \eta_{\mu\nu}\,dx''^\mu dx''^\nu.
$$

### The natural rescaling

Define the rescaled coordinates implicitly through their differentials:
$$
\boxed{\;dt'' = \sqrt{U}\,dt', \qquad dr'' = \sqrt{V}\,dr'\;}
$$
i.e.
$$
t''(t',r') = \int \sqrt{U(t',r')}\,dt', \qquad r''(t',r') = \int \sqrt{V(t',r')}
\,dr'.
$$

Substituting immediately gives
$$
ds^2 = U\,(dt')^2 - V\,(dr')^2 = (dt'')^2 - (dr'')^2 = \eta_{\mu\nu}\,dx''^\mu d
x''^\nu,
$$
so $g''_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(1,-1)$, as required.

### Integrability condition (when is this a legitimate coordinate change?)

For $t''$ and $r''$ to be well-defined single-valued functions of $(t',r')$, the
 one-forms $\sqrt{U}\,dt'$ and $\sqrt{V}\,dr'$ must be **exact**. By the Poincar
é lemma this requires
$$
\frac{\partial \sqrt{U}}{\partial r'} = 0 \quad\text{and}\quad \frac{\partial \s
qrt{V}}{\partial t'} = 0,
$$
i.e. **$U$ depends only on $t'$ and $V$ depends only on $r'$**. Under this condi
tion the rescaling above is a genuine coordinate transformation, the Jacobian is
 $\partial(t'',r'')/\partial(t',r') = \mathrm{diag}(\sqrt{U},\sqrt{V})$ (non-deg
enerate since $U,V>0$), and the metric is brought exactly to Minkowski form $\et
a_{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)$.

### Remark on the general case

If $U$ and $V$ genuinely depend on both variables, a *separable* rescaling $t''=
t''(t')$, $r''=r''(r')$ cannot flatten the metric in general. In two dimensions
every Lorentzian metric is **conformally flat**, so one can always reach $ds^2=\
Omega^2\big((dT)^2-(dX)^2\big)$ for some conformal factor $\Omega$; reaching exa
ctly $\eta$ (i.e. $\Omega=1$) is possible **if and only if the Gaussian curvatur
e of $g'$ vanishes**:
$$
K = -\frac{1}{2\sqrt{UV}}\!\left[\partial_{t'}\!\left(\frac{\partial_{t'}V}{\sqr
t{UV}}\right) - \partial_{r'}\!\left(\frac{\partial_{r'}U}{\sqrt{UV}}\right)\rig
ht] = 0.
$$
When $K=0$, the required $(t'',r'')$ are obtained by solving the eikonal equatio
ns $g'^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\xi\,\partial_\nu\xi=0$ for two independent null coor
dinates and then passing to their sum/difference. The simple rescaling above is
the special (and most common) case where the diagonal factors separate.

### Conclusion

The rescaling that transforms $g'_{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{diag}(U,-V)$ into the Minkows
ki metric is
$$
t'' = \int\!\sqrt{U}\,dt', \qquad r'' = \int\!\sqrt{V}\,dr',
$$
(valid as a coordinate change when $U=U(t')$, $V=V(r')$, and more generally when
ever the 2D metric is flat), yielding
$$
g''_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(1,-1) = \eta_{\mu\nu}.
$$
