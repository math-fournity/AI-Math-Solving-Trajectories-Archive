## Proof

We show that $K = 1$ works for every $p > 1$, and that it is the smallest possib
le constant.

### Reduction to one variable

The case $a = b$ gives $0 \leq 0$, trivially true for any $K \geq 0$. By symmetr
y of $|a-b|$ and $|a^p - b^p|$ under swapping $a,b$, we may assume $a > b > 0$.
Set $t = a/b > 1$. Then

$$|a-b|^p = b^p(t-1)^p, \qquad |a^p - b^p| = b^p(t^p - 1),$$

so the inequality $|a-b|^p \leq K\,|a^p - b^p|$ is equivalent to

$$(t-1)^p \leq K\,(t^p - 1), \qquad t > 1. \tag{$\ast$}$$

### Key inequality: $(t-1)^p < t^p - 1$ for $t > 1$, $p > 1$

Define $h(t) = t^p - (t-1)^p - 1$ for $t \geq 1$. We have $h(1) = 1 - 0 - 1 = 0$
, and

$$h'(t) = p\,t^{p-1} - p\,(t-1)^{p-1} = p\bigl[t^{p-1} - (t-1)^{p-1}\bigr].$$

Since $p > 1$, the function $x \mapsto x^{p-1}$ is strictly increasing on $(0,\i
nfty)$. For $t > 1$ we have $t > t - 1 > 0$, hence $t^{p-1} > (t-1)^{p-1}$, so $
h'(t) > 0$. Therefore $h$ is strictly increasing on $[1,\infty)$ with $h(1) = 0$
, giving $h(t) > 0$ for all $t > 1$, i.e.,

$$(t-1)^p < t^p - 1 \quad \text{for all } t > 1,\; p > 1. \tag{$\ast\ast$}$$

### $K = 1$ is valid

Inequality $(\ast\ast)$ shows that $(\ast)$ holds with $K = 1$ (strictly for $t
> 1$, and with equality at $t = 1$). Translating back, for all $a, b > 0$ and $p
 > 1$:

$$|a - b|^p \leq |a^p - b^p|.$$

### $K = 1$ is optimal (smallest valid constant)

Consider the ratio $g(t) = \dfrac{(t-1)^p}{t^p - 1}$ for $t > 1$. As $t \to \inf
ty$,

$$g(t) = \frac{(t-1)^p}{t^p - 1} = \frac{t^p(1 - 1/t)^p}{t^p(1 - 1/t^p)} \to \fr
ac{1}{1} = 1.$$

For any $K < 1$, choosing $t$ sufficiently large makes $g(t) > K$, so $(\ast)$ f
ails. Hence no $K < 1$ works, and $K = 1$ is the infimum of all valid constants
— and it is itself valid.

### Conclusion

There exists a constant $K$ (in fact independent of $p$) such that $|a-b|^p \leq
 K\,|a^p - b^p|$ for all $a, b > 0$ and all $p > 1$, namely

$$\boxed{K = 1}.$$

This is the smallest such constant.
