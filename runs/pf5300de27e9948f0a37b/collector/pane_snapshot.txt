## Proof

**Claim.** The function $u(\vec{x}, \vec{y})$ satisfies the symmetry of mixed pa
rtial derivatives: for all indices $i, j \in \{1,2,3\}$,
$$\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x_i\, \partial y_j} = \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partia
l y_j\, \partial x_i}.$$

**Setup.** Assume $\rho \in C^2(\mathbb{R}^3)$ with sufficient decay so that all
 integrals and differentiations below are valid (interchanging differentiation a
nd integration is justified by dominated convergence). Write the line parameteri
zation as
$$\vec{z}(t) = t\,\vec{x} + (1-t)\,\vec{y}, \qquad z_k(t) = t\,x_k + (1-t)\,y_k.
$$
The partial derivatives of the parameterization are
$$\frac{\partial z_k}{\partial x_i} = t\,\delta_{ki}, \qquad \frac{\partial z_k}
{\partial y_j} = (1-t)\,\delta_{kj}.$$

**First partials.** By the chain rule applied under the integral,
$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_i} = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \sum_{k=1}^{3} \frac
{\partial \rho}{\partial z_k}(\vec{z}(t))\,\frac{\partial z_k}{\partial x_i}\,dt
 = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} t\,\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial z_i}(\vec{z}(t))\,dt
,$$
$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial y_j} = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} (1-t)\,\frac{\partia
l \rho}{\partial z_j}(\vec{z}(t))\,dt.$$

**Mixed second partials.** Differentiating once more, again by the chain rule un
der the integral:
$$\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y_j\,\partial x_i} = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} t \s
um_{k=1}^{3} \frac{\partial^2 \rho}{\partial z_k\,\partial z_i}(\vec{z}(t))\,\fr
ac{\partial z_k}{\partial y_j}\,dt = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} t(1-t)\,\frac{\part
ial^2 \rho}{\partial z_j\,\partial z_i}(\vec{z}(t))\,dt,$$
$$\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x_i\,\partial y_j} = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} (1-t
) \sum_{k=1}^{3} \frac{\partial^2 \rho}{\partial z_k\,\partial z_j}(\vec{z}(t))\
,\frac{\partial z_k}{\partial x_i}\,dt = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} (1-t)\,t\,\frac
{\partial^2 \rho}{\partial z_i\,\partial z_j}(\vec{z}(t))\,dt.$$

**Conclusion.** Since $\rho \in C^2$, Clairaut's theorem gives $\frac{\partial^2
 \rho}{\partial z_i\,\partial z_j} = \frac{\partial^2 \rho}{\partial z_j\,\parti
al z_i}$ pointwise. The two integrands are therefore identical ($t(1-t)$ is symm
etric in the two differentiations), so
$$\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x_i\,\partial y_j} = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} t(1-
t)\,\frac{\partial^2 \rho}{\partial z_i\,\partial z_j}(\vec{z}(t))\,dt = \int_{-
\infty}^{\infty} t(1-t)\,\frac{\partial^2 \rho}{\partial z_j\,\partial z_i}(\vec
{z}(t))\,dt = \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y_j\,\partial x_i}.$$

Thus $u$ satisfies **equality (commutativity) of mixed partial derivatives** in
the $\vec{x}$ and $\vec{y}$ variables:
$$\boxed{\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x_i\,\partial y_j} = \frac{\partial^2 u}{\
partial y_j\,\partial x_i} \quad \text{for all } i,j.}$$
