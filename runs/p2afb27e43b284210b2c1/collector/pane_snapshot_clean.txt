## Proof

The automorphism group of the finite field $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ (over its prime su
bfield $\mathbb{F}_p$) is the Galois group $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/
\mathbb{F}_p)$. Every automorphism of $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ fixes $\mathbb{F}_p$ po
intwise (since it must fix $0$ and $1$, hence all of $\mathbb{F}_p$), so the ful
l automorphism group equals this Galois group.

It is well known that $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p)$ is cyc
lic of order $n$, generated by the Frobenius automorphism $\varphi \colon x \map
sto x^p$. The powers $\varphi^0, \varphi^1, \dots, \varphi^{n-1}$ are all distin
ct (since $\varphi^k = \mathrm{id}$ would require $x^{p^k} = x$ for all $x$, i.e
. $\mathbb{F}_{p^n} \subseteq \mathbb{F}_{p^k}$, which forces $n \mid k$), and t
here are no other automorphisms. Hence

$$\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{3^{100}}) \cong C_{100},$$

a cyclic group of order $100$.

A cyclic group of order $n$ has exactly one subgroup of order $d$ for each posit
ive divisor $d$ of $n$, and these are all the subgroups. Therefore the number of
 distinct subgroups equals the number of positive divisors of $n$.

For $n = 100 = 2^2 \cdot 5^2$, the number of positive divisors is

$$(2+1)(2+1) = 9.$$

Indeed, the divisors are $1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20, 25, 50, 100$, giving nine distinct
 subgroups of $C_{100}$.

Therefore, the number of distinct subgroups of $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{3
^{100}})$ is

$$\boxed{9}.$$
