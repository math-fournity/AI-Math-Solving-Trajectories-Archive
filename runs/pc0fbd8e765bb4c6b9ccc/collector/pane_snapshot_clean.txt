## Proof

**Convention.** For $\alpha \in \rho(A)$ we write $R_A(\alpha) := (A - \alpha I)
^{-1}$, a bounded operator on the domain $D(A)$ extended to the whole space (sin
ce $\alpha\in\rho(A)$ means $A-\alpha I : D(A)\to X$ is bijective with bounded i
nverse). The resolvent is continuous in $\alpha$ in the operator norm: if $\alph
a_n\to\alpha$ then $R_A(\alpha_n)\to R_A(\alpha)$, because
$$R_A(\alpha_n)=R_A(\alpha)\bigl(I-(\alpha_n-\alpha)R_A(\alpha)\bigr)^{-1}\to R_
A(\alpha).$$

---

### 1. The first resolvent identity

Let $\alpha_0,\alpha_1\in\rho(A)$. The scalar identity
$$(A-\alpha_1 I)-(A-\alpha_0 I)=(\alpha_0-\alpha_1)I$$
holds on $D(A)$. Insert $R_A(\alpha_0)$ on the left and $R_A(\alpha_1)$ on the r
ight (both are defined on all of $X$, and $R_A(\alpha_1)$ maps $X$ into $D(A)$,
so the composition is legitimate):

$$R_A(\alpha_0)\bigl[(A-\alpha_1 I)-(A-\alpha_0 I)\bigr]R_A(\alpha_1)=(\alpha_0-
\alpha_1)\,R_A(\alpha_0)R_A(\alpha_1).$$

Expanding the left-hand side and using $R_A(\alpha_0)(A-\alpha_0 I)=I$ and $(A-\
alpha_1 I)R_A(\alpha_1)=I$:

$$\underbrace{R_A(\alpha_0)(A-\alpha_1 I)R_A(\alpha_1)}_{=\,R_A(\alpha_0)\cdot I
\,=\,R_A(\alpha_0)}\;-\;\underbrace{R_A(\alpha_0)(A-\alpha_0 I)R_A(\alpha_1)}_{=
\,I\cdot R_A(\alpha_1)\,=\,R_A(\alpha_1)}=R_A(\alpha_0)-R_A(\alpha_1).$$

Hence
$$\boxed{\,R_A(\alpha_0)-R_A(\alpha_1)=(\alpha_0-\alpha_1)\,R_A(\alpha_0)R_A(\al
pha_1)\,}.\tag{$\ast$}$$

**Commutativity.** The right-hand side of $(\ast)$ is symmetric under swapping $
\alpha_0\leftrightarrow\alpha_1$ up to the overall sign, which is exactly compen
sated by the sign change of the left-hand side. Concretely, applying $(\ast)$ wi
th the roles interchanged gives
$$R_A(\alpha_1)-R_A(\alpha_0)=(\alpha_1-\alpha_0)\,R_A(\alpha_1)R_A(\alpha_0),$$
i.e. $R_A(\alpha_0)-R_A(\alpha_1)=(\alpha_0-\alpha_1)\,R_A(\alpha_1)R_A(\alpha_0
)$. Comparing with $(\ast)$ and dividing by $\alpha_0-\alpha_1\neq 0$ (the case
$\alpha_0=\alpha_1$ is trivial) yields
$$R_A(\alpha_0)R_A(\alpha_1)=R_A(\alpha_1)R_A(\alpha_0).$$
Thus the two displayed products in the statement coincide, completing the first
part.

---

### 2. First derivative $\dfrac{d}{d\alpha}R_A(\alpha)=R_A(\alpha)^2$

Set $\alpha_0=\alpha+h$, $\alpha_1=\alpha$ in $(\ast)$:
$$R_A(\alpha+h)-R_A(\alpha)=h\,R_A(\alpha+h)R_A(\alpha).$$
Dividing by $h$ and using norm-continuity of the resolvent (so $R_A(\alpha+h)\to
 R_A(\alpha)$ as $h\to 0$):
$$\frac{d}{d\alpha}R_A(\alpha)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{R_A(\alpha+h)-R_A(\alpha)}{h}=
\lim_{h\to 0}R_A(\alpha+h)R_A(\alpha)=R_A(\alpha)^2.\tag{$\ast\ast$}$$

---

### 3. Higher derivatives $\dfrac{d^n}{d\alpha^n}R_A(\alpha)=n!\,R_A(\alpha)^{n+
1}$

We proceed by induction. The base case $n=1$ is $(\ast\ast)$. Suppose
$$\frac{d^{\,n-1}}{d\alpha^{\,n-1}}R_A(\alpha)=(n-1)!\,R_A(\alpha)^{n}.$$
Differentiating the right-hand side (justified because $R_A(\alpha)$ is norm-con
tinuous and the product rule holds for the operator-norm topology) and using $(\
ast\ast)$ together with the commutativity already established:
$$\frac{d^{\,n}}{d\alpha^{\,n}}R_A(\alpha)=(n-1)!\cdot n\,R_A(\alpha)^{n-1}\cdot
\frac{d}{d\alpha}R_A(\alpha)=(n-1)!\cdot n\,R_A(\alpha)^{n-1}\cdot R_A(\alpha)^2
=n!\,R_A(\alpha)^{n+1}.$$
This closes the induction.

---

### 4. Derivative of powers $\dfrac{d}{d\alpha}R_A(\alpha)^n=n\,R_A(\alpha)^{n+1
}$

Since the resolvents at the same point commute, the usual product rule applies:
$$\frac{d}{d\alpha}R_A(\alpha)^n=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}R_A(\alpha)^{k}\Bigl(\frac{d}{d
\alpha}R_A(\alpha)\Bigr)R_A(\alpha)^{\,n-1-k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}R_A(\alpha)^{k}\,R
_A(\alpha)^2\,R_A(\alpha)^{\,n-1-k}.$$
Every summand equals $R_A(\alpha)^{n+1}$ (powers of a single operator commute),
and there are $n$ of them, so
$$\frac{d}{d\alpha}R_A(\alpha)^n=n\,R_A(\alpha)^{n+1}.$$

---

All four claims are proved. $\blacksquare$
