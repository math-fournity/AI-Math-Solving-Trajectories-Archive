## Proof

Let $\alpha = a^{x/2}$, $\beta = b^{x/2}$, $\gamma = c^{x/2}$, $\delta = d^{x/2}
$. Since $a, b, c, d > 0$, these are all positive reals. Note that $\sqrt{3a^x}
= \sqrt{3}\cdot a^{x/2} = \sqrt{3}\,\alpha$, and similarly for the others. The e
quation to solve becomes:

$$\sqrt{\alpha^2+\beta^2+\gamma^2}+\sqrt{\beta^2+\gamma^2+\delta^2}+\sqrt{\gamma
^2+\delta^2+\alpha^2}+\sqrt{\delta^2+\alpha^2+\beta^2}=\sqrt{3}\,(\alpha+\beta+\
gamma+\delta). \quad (\star)$$

### Key Inequality

By the **Cauchy–Schwarz inequality** (equivalently, QM–AM), for any three positi
ve reals $p, q, r$:

$$(p+q+r)^2 \le 3(p^2+q^2+r^2),$$

so

$$\sqrt{p^2+q^2+r^2}\ge \frac{p+q+r}{\sqrt{3}},$$

with **equality if and only if $p = q = r$**.

### Applying to Each Term

Applying this to each of the four terms on the left-hand side of $(\star)$:

$$\sqrt{\alpha^2+\beta^2+\gamma^2}\ge\frac{\alpha+\beta+\gamma}{\sqrt{3}},\qquad
 \sqrt{\beta^2+\gamma^2+\delta^2}\ge\frac{\beta+\gamma+\delta}{\sqrt{3}},$$
$$\sqrt{\gamma^2+\delta^2+\alpha^2}\ge\frac{\gamma+\delta+\alpha}{\sqrt{3}},\qqu
ad \sqrt{\delta^2+\alpha^2+\beta^2}\ge\frac{\delta+\alpha+\beta}{\sqrt{3}}.$$

Summing all four inequalities:

$$\text{LHS}\ge\frac{(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)+(\beta+\gamma+\delta)+(\gamma+\delta+
\alpha)+(\delta+\alpha+\beta)}{\sqrt{3}}=\frac{3(\alpha+\beta+\gamma+\delta)}{\s
qrt{3}}=\sqrt{3}\,(\alpha+\beta+\gamma+\delta)=\text{RHS}.$$

### Equality Condition

Thus $\text{LHS}\ge\text{RHS}$ **always**, and equality holds if and only if all
 four equality conditions are satisfied simultaneously:

$$\alpha=\beta=\gamma,\quad \beta=\gamma=\delta,\quad \gamma=\delta=\alpha,\quad
 \delta=\alpha=\beta.$$

These together force $\alpha=\beta=\gamma=\delta$, i.e.,

$$a^{x/2}=b^{x/2}=c^{x/2}=d^{x/2}.$$

### Determining $x$

The problem specifies **four distinct** colonies with positive constants $a, b,
c, d$. Since the colonies are distinct, at least two of $a, b, c, d$ differ.

- **If $x\ne 0$:** The function $t\mapsto t^{x/2}$ is injective on $(0,\infty)$,
 so distinct inputs yield distinct outputs. Thus $a^{x/2}=b^{x/2}=c^{x/2}=d^{x/2
}$ is **impossible** when the $a,b,c,d$ are not all equal. The inequality is str
ict; the two indices are **not** equal.

- **If $x=0$:** Then $a^0=b^0=c^0=d^0=1$ for all positive $a,b,c,d$, so $\alpha=
\beta=\gamma=\delta=1$ automatically. Verification:
  - LHS $= \sqrt{1+1+1}+\sqrt{1+1+1}+\sqrt{1+1+1}+\sqrt{1+1+1}=4\sqrt{3}$.
  - RHS $= \sqrt{3\cdot1}+\sqrt{3\cdot1}+\sqrt{3\cdot1}+\sqrt{3\cdot1}=4\sqrt{3}

  The indices are exactly equal. ✓
