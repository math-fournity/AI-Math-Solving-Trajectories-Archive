## Proof

We identify $S^1$ with $[0,1)=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ equipped with Lebesgue meas
ure. The operator is
$$(Tf)(x)=\tfrac12\bigl(f(\tfrac{x}{2})+f(\tfrac{x+1}{2})\bigr),$$
all arguments mod $1$. This is the Perron–Frobenius (transfer) operator for the
doubling map $\sigma(x)=2x\bmod 1$.

**Claim.** $\sigma(T)=\overline{\mathbb D}=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|\le 1\}$.

---

### Step 1 — $\|T\|=1$, hence $\sigma(T)\subseteq\overline{\mathbb D}$

For $f\in L^1$,
$$\|Tf\|_1\le\tfrac12\int_0^1|f(x/2)|\,dx+\tfrac12\int_0^1|f((x+1)/2)|\,dx.$$
Substituting $u=x/2$ and $v=(x+1)/2$:
$$\int_0^1|f(x/2)|\,dx=2\!\int_0^{1/2}|f|,\qquad \int_0^1|f((x+1)/2)|\,dx=2\!\in
t_{1/2}^1|f|,$$
so $\|Tf\|_1\le\|f\|_1$, i.e. $\|T\|\le1$. Since $T\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1$, we get
$\|T\|=1$.

---

### Step 2 — Action on Fourier modes

For $f_n(x)=e^{2\pi i n x}$:
$$Tf_n(x)=\tfrac12 e^{\pi i n x}\bigl(1+(-1)^n\bigr)=\begin{cases} f_{n/2}(x)&n\
text{ even},\\0&n\text{ odd}.\end{cases}$$
Equivalently, for Fourier coefficients $\hat f(n)=\int_0^1 f(x)\,e^{-2\pi i n x}
\,dx$:
$$\widehat{Tf}(n)=\hat f(2n).\qquad(\star)$$

---

### Step 3 — The open unit disk lies in the point spectrum

**$\lambda=0$:** Any $f$ with $\hat f(n)=0$ for all even $n$ satisfies $\widehat
{Tf}(n)=\hat f(2n)=0$ for every $n$, so $Tf=0$. E.g. $f(x)=e^{2\pi i x}$.

**$0<|\lambda|<1$:** Fix such $\lambda$ and set
$$f_\lambda(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\lambda^j\,e^{2\pi i\,2^j x}.$$
The terms are orthogonal in $L^2$ and $\sum|\lambda|^{2j}<\infty$, so the series
 converges in $L^2$, hence in $L^1$ (finite measure). For the partial sums $f_N=
\sum_{j=0}^N\lambda^j e^{2\pi i 2^j x}$:
- $j=0$: $T(e^{2\pi i x})=0$ (frequency $1$ is odd);
- $j\ge1$: $T(e^{2\pi i 2^j x})=e^{2\pi i 2^{j-1}x}$.

Hence
$$Tf_N=\sum_{j=1}^N\lambda^j e^{2\pi i 2^{j-1}x}=\lambda\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\lambda^
j e^{2\pi i 2^j x}=\lambda f_N-\lambda^{N+1}e^{2\pi i 2^N x}.$$
Since $\|\lambda^{N+1}e^{2\pi i 2^N x}\|_1=|\lambda|^{N+1}\to0$ and $f_N\to f_\l
ambda$ in $L^1$, continuity of $T$ gives
$$Tf_\lambda=\lambda\, f_\lambda.$$
Moreover $f_\lambda\neq0$ since $\hat f_\lambda(1)=1$. So every $\lambda$ with $
0<|\lambda|<1$ is an eigenvalue.

**$\lambda=1$:** $T\mathbf1=\mathbf1$.

---

### Step 4 — No eigenvalues on $|\lambda|=1$ except $\lambda=1$

Suppose $Tf=\lambda f$ with $|\lambda|=1$, $\lambda\neq1$. By $(\star)$, $\hat f
(2n)=\lambda\hat f(n)$, so iterating: $|\hat f(n)|=|\hat f(2^k n)|$ for all $k\g
e0$. By the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma ($f\in L^1\Rightarrow\hat f\in c_0$), $\hat f
(2^k n)\to0$, hence $\hat f(n)=0$ for all $n\neq0$. For $n=0$: $\hat f(0)=\lambd
a\hat f(0)$ and $\lambda\neq1$ forces $\hat f(0)=0$. Thus $f=0$, contradiction.

*(For completeness: the boundary points $|\lambda|=1,\lambda\neq1$ belong to the
 continuous spectrum. Indeed, the adjoint $T^*$ is the Koopman operator $U g=g\c
irc\sigma$ on $L^\infty\subset L^2$; since the doubling map is mixing, $U$ has n
o $L^2$-eigenvalues on the unit circle except $1$, so $\ker(T^*-\bar\lambda I)=\
{0\}$, making $\overline{\operatorname{ran}(T-\lambda I)}=L^1$ while $T-\lambda
I$ is not surjective.)*

---

### Step 5 — Conclusion

The point spectrum contains the open disk $\mathbb D=\{|\lambda|<1\}$. Since the
 spectrum of a bounded operator on a complex Banach space is **closed** and $\ma
thbb D\subseteq\sigma(T)$, we have $\overline{\mathbb D}\subseteq\sigma(T)$. Com
bined with Step 1:

$$\boxed{\sigma(T)=\overline{\mathbb D}=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|\le 1\}.}$$

The point spectrum is $\sigma_p(T)=\{z:|z|<1\}\cup\{1\}$; the remaining boundary
 points $\{z:|z|=1,\,z\neq1\}$ form the continuous spectrum.
