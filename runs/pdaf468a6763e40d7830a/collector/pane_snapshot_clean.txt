**Proof.**

Identify $V$ with $F^n$ via the basis $\{e_1,\dots,e_n\}$, so that $P=\{0,1\}^n\
subseteq F^n$ and $W$ is an $m$-dimensional subspace of $F^n$. We must show $|W\
cap\{0,1\}^n|\le 2^m$.

**Lemma.** For any $m$-dimensional subspace $W\subseteq F^n$, there exist $m$ co
ordinate indices $i_1<\dots<i_m$ such that the coordinate projection
$$\pi:W\longrightarrow F^m,\qquad \pi(x)=(x_{i_1},\dots,x_{i_m})$$
is injective.

*Proof of Lemma.* Since $\dim W=m$, choose a basis of $W$ and form the $m\times
n$ matrix $A$ whose rows are that basis; then $W=\operatorname{rowspan}(A)$ and
$\operatorname{rank}A=m$. By the equality of row rank and column rank, $A$ has $
m$ linearly independent columns, say columns $i_1<\dots<i_m$. The restriction of
 $\pi$ to $W$ is injective iff the $m\times m$ submatrix $A_{[:,\{i_1,\dots,i_m\
}]}$ has trivial kernel on $W$, equivalently is invertible. Concretely: if $x\in
 W$ with $\pi(x)=0$, write $x=A^{\!\top}c$ for some $c\in F^m$ (identifying $W$
with the row space); then $x_{i_j}=0$ for all $j$ means $c^{\!\top}A_{[:,\{i_1,\
dots,i_m\}]}=0$, and since this submatrix is invertible, $c=0$, hence $x=0$. Thu
s $\ker(\pi|_W)=\{0\}$. $\square$

Now apply the lemma to obtain indices $i_1,\dots,i_m$ with $\pi:W\hookrightarrow
 F^m$ injective. Restrict $\pi$ to $S:=W\cap\{0,1\}^n$. For every $x\in S$, each
 coordinate $x_{i_j}\in\{0,1\}$, so $\pi(x)\in\{0,1\}^m$. Hence
$$\pi(S)\subseteq\{0,1\}^m,$$
and since $\pi$ is injective on $W$ (hence on $S$),
$$|S|=|\pi(S)|\le|\{0,1\}^m|=2^m.$$

Therefore $|W\cap P|\le 2^m$, as claimed. $\blacksquare$
