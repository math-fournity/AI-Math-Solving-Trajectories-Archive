## Solution

**The system:** $\dot x = f(x,\mu) = (x+\mu)(\mu + 2x - x^2)$.

### Step 1: Find all equilibrium branches

Setting $f=0$:

**Branch 1:** $x + \mu = 0 \implies x = -\mu$ (a straight line, exists for all $
\mu$).

**Branches 2 & 3:** $\mu + 2x - x^2 = 0 \implies x^2 - 2x - \mu = 0 \implies x =
 1 \pm \sqrt{1+\mu}$, existing for $\mu \geq -1$.

### Step 2: Find bifurcation points (where branches meet)

- **At $\mu = -1$:** Branch 2 and 3 coalesce at $x = 1$. Branch 1 also gives $x
= -(-1) = 1$. All three branches meet at $(\mu, x) = (-1, 1)$.

- **At $\mu = 0$:** Branch 1 gives $x = 0$. Branch 3 gives $x = 1 - \sqrt{1} = 0
$. Branches 1 and 3 cross at $(\mu, x) = (0, 0)$. (Branch 2 gives $x=2 \neq 0$,
so it's not involved.)

No other intersections exist (solving $x=-\mu$ with branch 2 yields only $\mu=-1
$, already found).

### Step 3: Stability analysis

Compute $f_x = (\mu + 2x - x^2) + (x+\mu)(2 - 2x)$.

**Branch 1** ($x = -\mu$): The second factor $\mu + 2x - x^2$ is not zero in gen
eral, but $(x+\mu)=0$, so:
$$f_x\big|_{x=-\mu} = (\mu - 2\mu - \mu^2) + 0 = -\mu(1+\mu)$$
- Stable ($f_x < 0$) when $\mu < -1$ or $\mu > 0$.
- Unstable ($f_x > 0$) when $-1 < \mu < 0$.

**Branch 2** ($x = 1 + \sqrt{1+\mu}$, let $s = \sqrt{1+\mu} > 0$): Here $\mu + 2
x - x^2 = 0$, so:
$$f_x = (x+\mu)\cdot 2(1-x) = (1+s+s^2-1)\cdot(-2s) = s(s+1)(-2s) = -2s^2(s+1) <
 0$$
**Always stable** for $\mu > -1$.

**Branch 3** ($x = 1 - \sqrt{1+\mu}$, let $s = \sqrt{1+\mu} > 0$):
$$f_x = (x+\mu)\cdot 2(1-x) = (s^2 - s)\cdot 2s = 2s^2(s-1)$$
- Stable ($f_x < 0$) when $0 < s < 1$, i.e., $-1 < \mu < 0$.
- Unstable ($f_x > 0$) when $s > 1$, i.e., $\mu > 0$.

### Step 4: Classify the bifurcations

**At $\mu = -1$ (point $x=1$): Supercritical Pitchfork**

Set $\epsilon = \mu + 1$, $y = x - 1$. Then:
$$\dot y = (y + \epsilon)(\epsilon - y^2) = -y^3 + \epsilon y + \underbrace{(\ep
silon^2 - \epsilon y^2)}_{\text{higher order}}$$

The leading-order normal form is $\dot y = \epsilon y - y^3$, which is the **sup
ercritical pitchfork** normal form. The behavior confirms this:

| Region | Branch 1 | Branch 2 | Branch 3 |
|--------|----------|----------|----------|
| $\mu < -1$ | stable | — | — |
| $\mu > -1$ (near $-1$) | **unstable** | **stable** (born) | **stable** (born)
|

One branch (Branch 1) loses stability while two new stable branches (2, 3) emerg
e — the hallmark of a **supercritical pitchfork**.

**At $\mu = 0$ (point $x=0$): Transcritical**

Branches 1 and 3 cross transversally (slopes $-1$ and $-1/2$ respectively) and *
*exchange stability**:

| Region | Branch 1 | Branch 3 |
|--------|----------|----------|
| $\mu < 0$ (near $0$) | unstable | stable |
| $\mu > 0$ (near $0$) | **stable** | **unstable** |

Two branches cross and swap stability — this is a **transcritical bifurcation**.
