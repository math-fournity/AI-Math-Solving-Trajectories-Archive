**Answer: Yes, such a group exists.**

## Proof

Let $p \geq 5$ be a prime and let $G = \mathrm{UT}(4, p)$, the group of $4 \time
s 4$ upper unitriangular matrices over $\mathbb{F}_p$. Elements of $G$ are matri
ces of the form $I + N$ where $N$ is strictly upper triangular with entries in $
\mathbb{F}_p$.

### G is finite and nonabelian

$|G| = p^6$ since there are 6 free entries above the diagonal. $G$ is nonabelian
: setting $x_1 = I + E_{12}$, $x_2 = I + E_{23}$, we compute $[x_1, x_2] = I + E
_{13} \neq I$.

### G is directly indecomposable

The center of $G$ is $Z(G) = \{I + cE_{14} : c \in \mathbb{F}_p\} \cong \mathbb{
Z}/p$, which has order $p$. Suppose for contradiction that $G = H \times K$ with
 $H, K$ nontrivial. Then $Z(G) = Z(H) \times Z(K)$. Since $H$ and $K$ are nontri
vial $p$-groups, each has nontrivial center, so $|Z(H)| \geq p$ and $|Z(K)| \geq
 p$. This gives $|Z(G)| \geq p^2 > p$, contradicting $|Z(G)| = p$.

### The Frattini subgroup and d(G) = 3

For $p \geq 5$, the exponent of $G$ is $p$. Indeed, for any $g = I + N \in G$ wi
th $N^4 = 0$:
$$(I+N)^p = \sum_{k=0}^{p}\binom{p}{k}N^k = I + pN + \binom{p}{2}N^2 + \binom{p}
{3}N^3 = I,$$
since $p \mid \binom{p}{k}$ for $1 \leq k \leq p-1$ and $N^k = 0$ for $k \geq 4$
. Thus $G^p = \{I\}$.

The Frattini subgroup is $\Phi(G) = G'G^p = G'$. The commutator subgroup is:
$$G' = \langle E_{13},\, E_{24},\, E_{14} \rangle \cong (\mathbb{Z}/p)^3,$$
generated by $[x_1, x_2] = E_{13}$, $[x_2, x_3] = E_{24}$ (where $x_3 = I + E_{3
4}$), and $[[x_1, x_2], x_3] = E_{14}$. So $|G'| = p^3$ and:
$$G/\Phi(G) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/p)^3, \qquad d(G) = \dim_{\mathbb{F}_p}(G/\Phi(G))
 = 3.$$

### Every minimal generating set has size 3

By the **Burnside Basis Theorem**, for a finite $p$-group $G$, a subset $S \subs
eteq G$ is a minimal generating set if and only if the image of $S$ in $G/\Phi(G
)$ is a basis of the $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector space $G/\Phi(G)$. Since $\dim_{\math
bb{F}_p}(G/\Phi(G)) = 3$, every minimal generating set of $G$ has exactly 3 elem
ents.

### Verification of the size bounds

Let $S$ be any minimal generating set. Then $|S| = 3$ and $|G| = p^6 \geq 5^6 =
15625$, so:
$$3 \leq |S| = 3 \leq 15624 = |G| - 1. \quad \checkmark$$

A concrete minimal generating set is $S = \{x_1, x_2, x_3\} = \{I + E_{12},\; I
+ E_{23},\; I + E_{34}\}$, whose images form the standard basis of $G/\Phi(G) \c
ong (\mathbb{F}_p)^3$.

### Conclusion

$G = \mathrm{UT}(4, p)$ for any prime $p \geq 5$ is a finite nonabelian group th
at is directly indecomposable (not a direct product of nonabelian or abelian gro
ups), and every minimal generating set $S$ satisfies $3 \leq |S| \leq |G| - 1$.
Therefore, such a group **exists**. $\blacksquare$
