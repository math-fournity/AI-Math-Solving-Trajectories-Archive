# Solution

## Setup and Key Invariant

We have 10 racks, initially each holding 10 batteries (100 total). At the end, t
he supervisor takes the 3 largest racks; the technician takes the remaining 7. S
o the supervisor's haul = 100 − (sum of the 7 smallest racks). The supervisor wa
nts to minimize the bottom-7 sum; the technician wants to maximize it.

**Invariant (lower bound on every rack):** Every rack always holds at least 2 ba
tteries.

*Proof by induction.* Initially every rack has 10 ≥ 2. Consider any round. An **
unselected** rack keeps its value (≥ 2 by induction). A **selected** rack has so
me value $a_i$; the supervisor removes $b_i$ with $1 \le b_i \le a_i - 1$, leavi
ng remainder $r_i = a_i - b_i \ge 1$. After the derangement, this rack receives
a bin containing $b_j \ge 1$ batteries, so its new value is $r_i + b_j \ge 1 + 1
 = 2$. ∎

**Consequence:** The 7 smallest racks always sum to at least $7 \times 2 = 14$,
so the supervisor can never take more than $100 - 14 = 86$.

## Supervisor's Strategy Achieving 86

The supervisor runs 7 rounds, maintaining the invariant that after round $k$ ($1
 \le k \le 7$) the state is:

$$\text{one rack at } 10 + 8k, \quad k \text{ racks at } 2, \quad 9 - k \text{ r
acks at } 10.$$

**Base case (Round 1).** All racks are at 10. Select any 4 racks. Remove 9 from
rack 1 (leaving 1), and 1 from each of racks 2, 3, 4 (leaving 9 each). Bins: $(9
, 1, 1, 1)$. Remainders: $(1, 9, 9, 9)$.

The technician must apply a derangement. The bin of size 9 cannot return to rack
 1, so it must go to one of racks 2, 3, 4 — all of which have remainder 9. Which
ever rack receives it attains $9 + 9 = 18$. Rack 1 (remainder 1) receives a size
-1 bin, attaining $1 + 1 = 2$. The remaining two racks receive size-1 bins, atta
ining $9 + 1 = 10$.

**The technician has no meaningful choice**: all three candidate racks are ident
ical (remainder 9), so every derangement produces the same multiset $\{18, 2, 10
, 10\}$.

State after round 1: one rack at 18, one at 2, eight at 10. ✓

**Inductive step (Round $k$, $2 \le k \le 7$).** The state has one rack at $10 +
 8(k-1)$, $k-1$ racks at 2, and $10 - k$ racks at 10 (so $9 - (k-1) = 10-k$ rack
s at 10, which is $\ge 3$ for $k \le 7$).

Select the big rack (value $10 + 8(k-1)$) and three 10-racks. Remove $9 + 8(k-1)
$ from the big rack (leaving 1), and 1 from each 10-rack (leaving 9 each). Bins:
 $(9 + 8(k-1),\; 1,\; 1,\; 1)$. Remainders: $(1, 9, 9, 9)$.

The technician must derange. The large bin (size $9 + 8(k-1)$) cannot return to
the big rack, so it goes to one of the three 9-remainder racks, creating value $
9 + 9 + 8(k-1) = 10 + 8k$. The big rack's origin (remainder 1) receives a size-1
 bin → value 2. The other two receive size-1 bins → value 10.

Again, all three 9-remainder racks are identical, so the technician's choice is
irrelevant. The state becomes: one rack at $10 + 8k$, $k$ racks at 2, $9 - k$ ra
cks at 10. ✓

**After Round 7:** The state is

$$(66,\; 10,\; 10,\; 2,\; 2,\; 2,\; 2,\; 2,\; 2,\; 2).$$

Only 2 racks remain at 10, which is fewer than the 3 needed to continue. The sup
ervisor stops and takes the top 3 racks: $66 + 10 + 10 = 86$.

## Verification

- **Total check:** $66 + 2 \times 7 + 10 \times 2 = 66 + 14 + 20 = 100$. ✓
- **Rounds 1–7 are feasible:** After round $k$, there are $9 - k$ racks at 10. F
or round $k+1$ we need 3 ten-racks, so we need $9 - k \ge 3$, i.e., $k \le 6$. R
ound 7 uses the last 3 ten-racks (at $k=6$, there are 3 ten-racks). After round
7, only 2 ten-racks remain, so the process naturally terminates. ✓
- **Technician cannot interfere:** In every round, the three non-big selected ra
cks all have value 10 and remainder 9, making them indistinguishable. The derang
ement constraint forces the large bin onto one of them, and the technician canno
t redirect it back to its origin or to a low-remainder rack. The technician's on
ly freedom is choosing *which* identical rack receives the large bin, which has
no effect on the multiset of values. ✓

## Conclusion

- **Upper bound:** By the invariant that every rack always holds ≥ 2 batteries,
the 7 smallest racks sum to ≥ 14, so the supervisor can take at most $100 - 14 =
 86$.
- **Lower bound:** The supervisor's explicit 7-round strategy forces the state $
(66, 10, 10, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2)$ regardless of the technician's choices, yield
ing exactly 86.

The maximum number of batteries the supervisor can guarantee is $\boxed{86}$.
