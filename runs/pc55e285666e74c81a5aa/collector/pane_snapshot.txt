## Analysis

**Interior Sphere Condition (ISC) at $p \in \partial\Omega$**: There exists an o
pen ball $B$ with $p \in \partial B$ and $B \subseteq \Omega$.

**Key idea**: Use the largest inscribed ball (the inball).

**Proof that the answer is True:**

Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ be bounded, open, and convex.

**Step 1: The inradius is achieved.**

Define $f(x) = \operatorname{dist}(x, \partial\Omega)$ for $x \in \Omega$. This
is a continuous function. Since $\Omega$ is bounded, $\overline{\Omega}$ is comp
act. Extending $f$ to $\overline{\Omega}$ (where $f = 0$ on $\partial\Omega$), $
f$ is continuous on the compact set $\overline{\Omega}$, so it attains its maxim
um. Since $f > 0$ on $\Omega$ and $f = 0$ on $\partial\Omega$, the maximum is at
tained at some interior point $x_0 \in \Omega$. Let $R = f(x_0) = \max_{x \in \O
mega} \operatorname{dist}(x, \partial\Omega) > 0$.

**Step 2: The inball is contained in $\Omega$.**

By definition, $R = \operatorname{dist}(x_0, \partial\Omega) = \inf_{q \in \part
ial\Omega} \|x_0 - q\|$. Since $\Omega$ is bounded, $\partial\Omega$ is compact,
 so this infimum is achieved: there exists $p \in \partial\Omega$ with $\|x_0 -
p\| = R$.

The open ball $B(x_0, R)$ does not intersect $\partial\Omega$ (every point of $\
partial\Omega$ is at distance $\geq R$ from $x_0$). Since $B(x_0, R)$ is connect
ed, contains $x_0 \in \Omega$, and is disjoint from $\partial\Omega$, it lies en
tirely in $\Omega$:
$$B(x_0, R) \subseteq \Omega.$$

**Step 3: ISC holds at $p$.**

We have $p \in \partial\Omega$ with $\|x_0 - p\| = R$, so $p \in \partial B(x_0,
 R)$. The ball $B(x_0, R)$ is open, contained in $\Omega$, and has $p$ on its bo
undary. This is exactly the interior sphere condition at $p$.

**Remark:** Convexity is not even required — the argument works for any bounded
open set. The inball always provides an interior sphere at the boundary point wh
ere it touches $\partial\Omega$.

## Answer

**True.** Every bounded, open, and convex subset of Euclidean space satisfies th
e interior sphere condition at some point on its boundary. The point is the boun
dary point closest to the center of the maximal inscribed ball (inball), and the
 inball itself serves as the required interior sphere.
