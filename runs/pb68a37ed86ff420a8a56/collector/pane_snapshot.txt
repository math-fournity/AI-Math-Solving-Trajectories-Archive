## Proof

The primes not exceeding 23 are
$$2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23,$$
which are **9** primes. Every element $n \in M$ can be written uniquely as
$$n = 2^{a_1}\, 3^{a_2}\, 5^{a_3}\, \cdots 23^{a_9}.$$

**Parity vector.** Associate to each $n$ its *parity vector*
$$v(n) = (a_1 \bmod 2,\; a_2 \bmod 2,\; \ldots,\; a_9 \bmod 2) \in (\mathbb{Z}/2
\mathbb{Z})^9.$$
There are $2^9 = 512$ possible parity vectors. Two numbers $a, b$ have $ab$ a pe
rfect square if and only if $v(a) = v(b)$.

---

**Step 1 — Form many disjoint pairs whose product is a perfect square.**

Distribute the 1985 elements of $M$ among the 512 parity classes. If class $i$ c
ontains $n_i$ elements, we can form $\lfloor n_i / 2 \rfloor$ disjoint pairs wit
hin that class. The total number of such pairs is
$$P = \sum_{i=1}^{512} \left\lfloor \frac{n_i}{2} \right\rfloor.$$
Since $\lfloor n_i/2 \rfloor \ge (n_i - 1)/2$ and at most 512 classes are non-em
pty (each wasting at most one unpaired element),
$$P \;\ge\; \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{512}(n_i - 1) \;=\; \frac{1985 - 512}{2} \;=\
; \frac{1473}{2} \;=\; 736.5.$$
Since $P$ is an integer, $P \ge 737$.

These 737 pairs are mutually disjoint (each pair lies within a single parity cla
ss, and pairs from different classes share no elements). For each pair $(a_j, b_
j)$, the product $a_j b_j$ is a perfect square, so write
$$a_j b_j = s_j^2$$
for some positive integer $s_j$. Each $s_j$ has all prime divisors $\le 23$, hen
ce admits its own parity vector $v(s_j) \in (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^9$.

---

**Step 2 — Find two pairs whose square-roots share a parity vector.**

We have 737 values $s_1, s_2, \ldots, s_{737}$, each carrying one of 512 possibl
e parity vectors. Since
$$737 > 512,$$
by the Pigeonhole Principle there exist two distinct indices $j \neq k$ with $v(
s_j) = v(s_k)$. This means $s_j s_k$ is a perfect square; write $s_j s_k = t^2$
for some positive integer $t$.

---

**Step 3 — Conclude.**

The four numbers $a_j, b_j, a_k, b_k$ are distinct elements of $M$ (the pairs ar
e disjoint). Their product is
$$a_j \, b_j \, a_k \, b_k \;=\; s_j^2 \cdot s_k^2 \;=\; (s_j s_k)^2 \;=\; (t^2)
^2 \;=\; t^4,$$
which is the fourth power of the integer $t$.

Therefore $M$ contains a subset of 4 distinct elements whose product is a fourth
 power. $\blacksquare$
