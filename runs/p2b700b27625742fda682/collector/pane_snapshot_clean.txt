## Proof

Let $H$ be a Hilbert space with orthonormal basis $(e_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}$ (we ta
ke $\mathbb N=\{1,2,\dots\}$; the argument is identical for any convention). The
 operator $F$ has matrix entries
$$a_{kn}:=\langle e_k,Fe_n\rangle=\phi(n,k),$$
so $F$ is represented on $\ell^2(\mathbb N)$ by the matrix $(\phi(n,k))_{k,n}$.

### General estimate (Schur test)

**Theorem (Schur).** Suppose there exist positive weights $(p_n)_{n\ge1}$, $p_n>
0$, and constants $C_1,C_2\ge0$ such that
$$\sup_{n\ge1}\frac{1}{p_n}\sum_{k\ge1}|\phi(n,k)|\,p_k\le C_1,\qquad \sup_{k\ge
1}\frac{1}{p_k}\sum_{n\ge1}|\phi(n,k)|\,p_n\le C_2.$$
Then $F$ is bounded and
$$\boxed{\;\|F\|\le\sqrt{C_1C_2}.\;}$$

*Proof.* For $x=(x_n)\in\ell^2$ with $\|x\|_2=1$, set $y_n=p_n|x_n|$ and $z_n=p_
n^{-1}|x_n|$, so that $\sum y_n z_n=\sum|x_n|^2=1$. Then by Cauchy–Schwarz appli
ed twice,
$$|\langle e_k,Fx\rangle|^2\le\Big(\sum_n|\phi(n,k)|\,p_n\,|x_n|\Big)^2\le\Big(\
sum_n|\phi(n,k)|\,p_n\,z_n\Big)\Big(\sum_n|\phi(n,k)|\,p_n\,y_n\Big).$$
Summing over $k$ and using the two Schur bounds gives $\|Fx\|^2\le C_1C_2\,\|x\|
^2$. $\square$

(When $\sum_{n,k}|\phi(n,k)|^2<\infty$ one also has the cruder Hilbert–Schmidt b
ound $\|F\|\le(\sum_{n,k}|\phi(n,k)|^2)^{1/2}$, but this diverges for the kernel
 below, so the Schur test is the relevant tool.)

### Application: $\phi(n,k)=\dfrac{1}{n+k}$

Take the weight $p_n=n^{-1/2}$. Consider the row sum
$$S(n):=\frac{1}{p_n}\sum_{k\ge1}\phi(n,k)\,p_k=\sqrt n\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{1}{(n+k
)\sqrt k}.$$
The function $f(x)=x^{-1/2}(n+x)^{-1}$ is positive and decreasing on $(0,\infty)
$, hence
$$\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{1}{\sqrt k\,(n+k)}\le\int_0^\infty\frac{dx}{\sqrt x\,(n+x)}.
$$
With the substitution $x=nt$,
$$\int_0^\infty\frac{dx}{\sqrt x\,(n+x)}=n^{-1/2}\int_0^\infty\frac{t^{-1/2}}{1+
t}\,dt=n^{-1/2}\,\pi,$$
since $\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}/(1+t)\,dt=\pi$ (Beta integral $B(\tfrac12,\tfrac12
)=\pi$). Therefore
$$S(n)\le\sqrt n\cdot\frac{\pi}{\sqrt n}=\pi,\qquad\text{so }C_1\le\pi.$$
By symmetry of the kernel $\phi(n,k)=1/(n+k)$ in $n,k$, the column sum gives the
 identical bound $C_2\le\pi$.

Applying the Schur test,
$$\boxed{\;\|F\|\le\sqrt{\pi\cdot\pi}=\pi.\;}$$

This is sharp: the matrix $(1/(n+k))_{n,k\ge1}$ is a (shifted) Hilbert matrix, w
hose norm is known to equal exactly $\pi$ (e.g. via the Mellin transform / Rosen
blum's spectral analysis of generalized Hilbert matrices $1/(n+k+\alpha)$, which
 have norm $\pi$ for $\alpha\ge\tfrac12$; here $\alpha=2$ after reindexing). Thu
s the Schur estimate captures the true norm.
