## Proof

**Setup.** The standard CW-structure on $\mathbb{RP}^2$ consists of:
- one $0$-cell $e^0$,
- one $1$-cell $e^1$, attached via the constant map $S^0 \to e^0$ (both endpoint
s map to the single $0$-cell), so the $1$-skeleton is $\mathbb{RP}^1 \cong S^1$,
- one $2$-cell $e^2$, attached via the map $f: S^1 \to \mathbb{RP}^1 = S^1$ of d
egree $2$ (the doubling map $z \mapsto z^2$).

The $2$-fold covering $p: S^2 \to \mathbb{RP}^2$ induces a CW-structure on $S^2$
 by taking, for each open cell $e^k$ of $\mathbb{RP}^2$, the connected component
s of $p^{-1}(e^k)$ as the open $k$-cells of $S^2$.

---

**$0$-cells.** Since $p$ is a $2$-fold cover and $e^0$ is a point,
$$p^{-1}(e^0) = \{v_0,\, v_1\},$$
two antipodal points on $S^2$. These are the two $0$-cells.

**$1$-cells.** The open $1$-cell $e^1$ is an open interval, hence simply connect
ed. The covering is trivial over it, so $p^{-1}(e^1)$ is the disjoint union of t
wo open intervals, each mapping homeomorphically onto $e^1$. These give two $1$-
cells $e^1_0, e^1_1$.

To find their attaching maps, lift the attaching map of $e^1$ in $\mathbb{RP}^2$
 (which sends both endpoints of $S^0$ to $e^0$). Each lift sends the two endpoin
ts to the two points in $p^{-1}(e^0) = \{v_0, v_1\}$. Concretely, each $1$-cell
connects $v_0$ to $v_1$. The two $1$-cells together form a circle $S^1$ — the eq
uator of $S^2$ — subdivided into two arcs by the two vertices $v_0, v_1$.

**$2$-cells.** The open $2$-cell $e^2$ is an open disk, hence simply connected.
Again the covering is trivial over it, so $p^{-1}(e^2)$ is the disjoint union of
 two open $2$-disks, each mapping homeomorphically onto $e^2$. These give two $2
$-cells $e^2_0, e^2_1$ — the northern and southern hemispheres.

**Attaching maps of the $2$-cells.** The attaching map of $e^2$ in $\mathbb{RP}^
2$ is $f: S^1 \to \mathbb{RP}^1 = S^1$, $\deg(f) = 2$. The $1$-skeleton of $S^2$
 is the equator $S^1$, and the restriction of $p$ to this equator is the $2$-fol
d cover $g: S^1 \to S^1$, $\deg(g) = 2$. Each lift $\tilde{f}: S^1 \to S^1$ of $
f$ through $g$ satisfies $f = g \circ \tilde{f}$, so
$$\deg(f) = \deg(g)\cdot\deg(\tilde{f}) \implies 2 = 2\cdot\deg(\tilde{f}) \impl
ies \deg(\tilde{f}) = 1.$$
Thus each $2$-cell attaches to the $1$-skeleton via a degree-$1$ map $S^1 \to S^
1$, i.e., a homeomorphism onto the equator. Each hemisphere caps off one side of
 the equatorial circle.

---

**Verification.** The Euler characteristic is
$$\chi(S^2) = 2 - 2 + 2 = 2, \quad \checkmark$$
matching $\chi(S^2) = 2$. The $1$-skeleton is $S^1$ (a circle with $2$ vertices
and $2$ edges), and gluing two disks along the identity map on their common boun
dary $S^1$ yields $S^2$. $\checkmark$

---

**Conclusion.** The induced CW-structure on $S^2$ has **two cells in each dimens
ion** $0, 1, 2$:

- **Two $0$-cells**: antipodal points $v_0, v_1$ on the equator.
- **Two $1$-cells**: the two semicircular arcs of the equator, each connecting $
v_0$ to $v_1$. The $1$-skeleton is a circle $S^1$.
- **Two $2$-cells**: the northern and southern hemispheres, each attached to the
 equator via the identity (degree-$1$) map $S^1 \to S^1$.

This is the cell decomposition of $S^2$ as two disks glued along their common bo
undary circle, where the boundary circle carries the subdivision into $2$ vertic
es and $2$ edges.
