Looking at this problem, I need to find all positive integers $n$ where $R(n) =
\frac{2n}{\phi(n)}$ is an integer, then sum their reciprocals.

## Setting Up the Divisibility Condition

Since $\frac{n}{\phi(n)} = \prod_{p \mid n} \frac{p}{p-1}$, we have $R(n) = 2\pr
od_{p \in P} \frac{p}{p-1}$, where $P$ is the set of distinct prime factors of $
n$. For $R(n)$ to be an integer:

$$\prod_{p \in P}(p-1) \;\Big|\; 2\prod_{p \in P} p$$

Taking $q$-adic valuations for each prime $q$:

$$\sum_{p \in P} v_q(p-1) \leq v_q(2) + [q \in P]$$

## Analyzing the Constraints

**For $q = 2$:** $\sum_{p \in P} v_2(p-1) \leq 1 + [2 \in P]$. Every odd prime c
ontributes $\geq 1$, so:
- If $2 \notin P$: at most **1 odd prime** with $v_2(p-1)=1$.
- If $2 \in P$: at most **2 odd primes** (sum of $v_2(p-1) \leq 2$).

**For odd $q \notin P$:** No $p \in P$ may have $q \mid (p-1)$ — so $p-1$ values
 have no odd prime factors outside $P$.

**For odd $q \in P$:** At most one other prime $p \in P$ satisfies $q \mid (p-1)
$, with exact valuation 1.

## Enumerating All Valid Prime Sets

**Case $2 \notin P$:** At most 1 odd prime $p$ with $v_2(p-1)=1$, and $p-1$ has
no odd prime factors (else they'd be outside $P$). So $p-1 = 2^k$ with $k=1$, gi
ving $p=3$.
- $P = \emptyset$: $n=1$, $R(1)=2$ ✓
- $P = \{3\}$: $n=3^b$, $R=3$ ✓

**Case $2 \in P$, one odd prime $p$:** Need $p-1 = 2^k$ (no odd factors outside
$P$) with $v_2(p-1)=k \leq 2$.
- $k=1 \Rightarrow p=3$: $P=\{2,3\}$, $R=6$ ✓
- $k=2 \Rightarrow p=5$: $P=\{2,5\}$, $R=5$ ✓
- ($P=\{2\}$ alone: $n=2^a$, $R=4$ ✓)

**Case $2 \in P$, two odd primes $p_1 < p_2$:** Both need $v_2(p_i-1)=1$ (so $p_
i \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$). Since $p_1-1$ has no odd factor outside $P$ and $p_2 > p_
1$, we get $p_1-1=2$, so $p_1=3$. Then $p_2-1 = 2\cdot 3^b$ (only odd factor all
owed is 3), and the $q=3$ constraint gives $b \leq 1$, so $b=1$, $p_2=7$.
- $P=\{2,3,7\}$: $n=2^a 3^b 7^c$, $R=7$ ✓

**Three odd primes impossible** (violates $q=2$ constraint). No other sets work
(e.g., $\{2,7\}$ fails since $3\mid 6$ but $3\notin P$; $\{2,3,5\}$ fails since
$v_2(4)=2$ makes the sum 3).

## Computing the Sum

The six disjoint families and their reciprocal sums:

| Family | Sum |
|--------|-----|
| $n=1$ | $1$ |
| $n=2^a,\; a\geq 1$ | $\sum_{a\geq 1} 2^{-a} = 1$ |
| $n=3^b,\; b\geq 1$ | $\sum_{b\geq 1} 3^{-b} = \frac{1}{2}$ |
| $n=2^a 3^b,\; a,b\geq 1$ | $1 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2}$ |
| $n=2^a 5^c,\; a,c\geq 1$ | $1 \cdot \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{4}$ |
| $n=2^a 3^b 7^c,\; a,b,c\geq 1$ | $1 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{6} = \fra
c{1}{12}$ |

**Total:**
$$1 + 1 + \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{12} = 3 + \frac{3+1
}{12} = 3 + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{10}{3}$$

## Verification

Spot-checking excluded cases: $n=14=2\cdot 7$: $\phi=6$, $R=14/3$ (not integer ✓
). $n=30=2\cdot 3\cdot 5$: $\phi=8$, $R=15/2$ (not integer ✓). $n=42=2\cdot 3\cd
ot 7$: $\phi=12$, $R=7$ (integer ✓).

The fraction $\frac{10}{3}$ is irreducible ($\gcd(10,3)=1$), so $a=10$, $b=3$.
