## Solution

Consider the PDE $xU_x + yU_y = 0$ with $U = x$ on $x^2+y^2=1$.

**Method of characteristics.** The characteristic system is
$$\frac{dx}{x} = \frac{dy}{y} = \frac{dU}{0}.$$

From $dU = 0$: $U$ is constant along each characteristic curve. From $\frac{dx}{
x}=\frac{dy}{y}$: $\ln|x| = \ln|y| + c$, i.e. $y/x = \text{const}$. So the chara
cteristics are **rays from the origin** (lines $y = kx$, including the coordinat
e axes).

**Determining the solution.** Every point $(x,y)\neq(0,0)$ lies on a unique ray
from the origin. This ray meets the unit circle at the normalized point $\left(\
tfrac{x}{r},\tfrac{y}{r}\right)$ where $r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$. Since $U$ is constant
 along the ray and equals $x$ on the unit circle,
$$U(x,y) = \frac{x}{r} = \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}, \qquad (x,y)\neq(0,0).$$

**Verification.**
$$U_x = \frac{y^2}{(x^2+y^2)^{3/2}}, \quad U_y = \frac{-xy}{(x^2+y^2)^{3/2}},$$
$$xU_x + yU_y = \frac{xy^2 - xy^2}{(x^2+y^2)^{3/2}} = 0.\ \checkmark$$
On $x^2+y^2=1$: $U = x/1 = x$. $\checkmark$

**Uniqueness on $\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}$.** Each ray from the origin int
ersects the unit circle at exactly one point, so the boundary value fixes $U$ un
iquely on every ray, hence on all of $\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}$.

**Boundedness.** $|U(x,y)| = \frac{|x|}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}} \leq 1$, so the solution
 is bounded.

**Behavior at the origin.** All characteristics meet at the origin, so the bound
ary data on the circle does **not** determine $U(0,0)$. Moreover $U = x/\sqrt{x^
2+y^2}$ has no limit as $(x,y)\to(0,0)$ (it depends on direction), so no continu
ous extension to the origin exists. Hence there is **no** unique solution on all
 of $\mathbb{R}^2$.

**Evaluating the statements:**

1. *Unique solution for all $\mathbb{R}^2$* — **False**: the origin is a charact
eristic singularity; $U(0,0)$ is undetermined and no continuous extension exists
.
2. *Bounded solution exists in $\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}$* — **True**: $U
= x/\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$ is the unique solution and satisfies $|U|\leq 1$.
3. *Unbounded solution exists in $\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}$* — **False**:
the unique solution is bounded.
4. *Unique solution in $\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}$ but unbounded* — **False
**: the solution is unique but **bounded**, not unbounded.

**The correct statement is (2).** The unique solution $U(x,y) = \dfrac{x}{\sqrt{
x^2+y^2}}$ on $\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}$ is bounded (by $1$), and it satis
fies both the PDE and the boundary condition $U(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)=\cos\thet
a$.
