# Proof

**Claim.** The statement "$A$ is nilpotent if and only if $A^n = 0$" **holds tru
e** over a division ring $F$.

---

**Notation and conventions.** We view $F^n$ as a *right* $F$-vector space (colum
n vectors); left multiplication by $A \in M_n(F)$ is a right $F$-linear endomorp
hism. The dimension theory of finite-dimensional vector spaces over a division r
ing is valid: every subspace is free of a well-defined rank, and $\dim_F(F^n) =
n$.

---

**($\Leftarrow$)** If $A^n = 0$, then $A$ is nilpotent by definition. ∎

---

**($\Rightarrow$)** Suppose $A$ is nilpotent, so $A^k = 0$ for some $k \geq 1$.
Set $V = F^n$ and consider the descending chain of right $F$-subspaces
$$V = A^0 V \supseteq A^1 V \supseteq A^2 V \supseteq \cdots \supseteq A^k V = 0
.$$

**Lemma (stabilization forces vanishing).** If $A^i V = A^{i+1} V$ for some $i$,
 then $A^i = 0$.

*Proof of lemma.* Let $W = A^i V$. The equality $A^i V = A^{i+1} V = A(A^i V)$ r
eads $W = AW$. Iterating, $W = A^j W$ for every $j \geq 0$. Taking $j = k$ (so t
hat $A^k = 0$): $W = A^k W = 0$. Hence $A^i V = 0$, i.e., $A^i = 0$. ∎

Now examine the first $n+1$ terms $A^0 V \supseteq A^1 V \supseteq \cdots \supse
teq A^n V$ (a chain of $n$ inclusions, with $\dim(A^0 V) = n$).

- **Case 1: every inclusion is strict.** Then the dimension drops by at least $1
$ at each of the $n$ steps, giving $\dim(A^n V) \leq n - n = 0$. Thus $A^n V = 0
$, i.e., $A^n = 0$.

- **Case 2: some inclusion $A^i V = A^{i+1} V$ is non-strict with $0 \leq i \leq
 n-1$.** By the lemma, $A^i = 0$. Since $n \geq i$, we have $A^n = A^{n-i} \cdot
 A^i = A^{n-i} \cdot 0 = 0$.

In both cases $A^n = 0$. ∎

---

**Remark on where commutativity is not needed.** The classical proof over commut
ative fields often invokes the Cayley–Hamilton theorem or the minimal polynomial
, both of which rely on commutativity (determinants are not well-behaved over no
n-commutative rings). The argument above avoids this entirely: it uses only (a)
the definition of nilpotence, (b) the dimension theory of vector spaces over div
ision rings (which is valid since $F$ is a division ring, hence $F^n$ is a free
module of unique rank $n$), and (c) the elementary stabilization lemma. No commu
tativity of $F$ is required at any step.

---

**Conclusion.** For $A \in M_n(F)$ over a division ring $F$,
$$A \text{ is nilpotent} \iff A^n = 0.$$
The statement holds true.
