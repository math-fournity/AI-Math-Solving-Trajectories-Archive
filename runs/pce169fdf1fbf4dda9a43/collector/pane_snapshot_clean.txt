Looking at this problem, I need to find the largest positive integer $n < 2012$
such that for every prime $p \mid n$, $(p^2 - 1) \mid n$.

## Analysis

**Step 1: Establish the base structure.**

If any odd prime $p \mid n$, then $p^2 - 1$ is even, so $2 \mid n$. Then $2 \mid
 n$ forces $2^2 - 1 = 3 \mid n$. Then $3 \mid n$ forces $3^2 - 1 = 8 \mid n$. So
 for any $n > 1$, we must have $24 \mid n$ (since $8 \mid n$ and $3 \mid n$). Th
e case $n = 1$ is vacuously true but trivially small.

**Step 2: Identify closed prime sets.**

The set of prime divisors $S$ of $n$ must be **closed**: for each $p \in S$, all
 prime factors of $p^2 - 1$ must also lie in $S$, with sufficient exponents.

Computing $p^2 - 1$ for small primes:
| $p$ | $p^2 - 1$ | factorization |
|-----|-----------|---------------|
| 2 | 3 | $3$ |
| 3 | 8 | $2^3$ |
| 5 | 24 | $2^3 \cdot 3$ |
| 7 | 48 | $2^4 \cdot 3$ |
| 11 | 120 | $2^3 \cdot 3 \cdot 5$ |

**Step 3: Enumerate closed sets and their minimal values.**

- $\{2, 3, 5, 7, 13\}$: Min $= 2^4 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 \cdot 13 = 21840 > 20

large.**

**Step 5: Select the overall maximum.**

Comparing: $1536$, $\mathbf{1920}$, $1344$, $1680$, $1320$.

## Verification

$n = 1920 = 2^7 \cdot 3 \cdot 5$. Prime divisors: $\{2, 3, 5\}$.

- $p = 2$: $p^2 - 1 = 3$. Is $3 \mid 1920$? $1920 / 3 = 640$. ✓
- $p = 3$: $p^2 - 1 = 8$. Is $8 \mid 1920$? $1920 / 8 = 240$. ✓
- $p = 5$: $p^2 - 1 = 24$. Is $24 \mid 1920$? $1920 / 24 = 80$. ✓

All conditions satisfied, and $1920 < 2012$. No larger value exists among all cl

## Proof

**Claim:** The largest $n < 2012$ satisfying the Prime Stability protocol is $n
= 1920$.

**Proof.** For $n > 1$, any odd prime divisor $p$ of $n$ forces $2 \mid n$ (sinc
e $p^2 - 1$ is even), which forces $3 \mid n$ (since $2^2 - 1 = 3$), which force
s $8 \mid n$ (since $3^2 - 1 = 8$). Thus $24 \mid n$ for all valid $n > 1$.

The prime divisor set $S$ of $n$ must be closed under the map $p \mapsto \{\text
{prime factors of } p^2 - 1\}$, with exponents satisfying $v_q(n) \geq v_q(p^2 -
 1)$ for all $p \mid n$ and all primes $q$.

We enumerate all closed sets $S$ whose minimal valid product is $< 2012$:

1. $S = \{2,3\}$: minimal $24$, maximum $2^9 \cdot 3 = 1536$.
2. $S = \{2,3,5\}$: minimal $120$, maximum $2^7 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 = 1920$.
3. $S = \{2,3,7\}$: minimal $336$, maximum $2^6 \cdot 3 \cdot 7 = 1344$.
4. $S = \{2,3,5,7\}$: minimal $1680$, maximum $1680$.
5. $S = \{2,3,5,11\}$: minimal $1320$, maximum $1320$.

Every closed set containing 5 or more primes (e.g., $\{2,3,5,7,11\}$ with minimu
m $18480$, or $\{2,3,5,7,13\}$ with minimum $21840$) has minimal product exceedi
ng $2012$. Closed sets involving primes $\geq 17$ (e.g., $\{2,3,5,19\}$ with min
imum $6840$, $\{2,3,5,31\}$ with minimum $29760$) also exceed $2012$.

Among all viable sets, the maximum is $1920 = 2^7 \cdot 3 \cdot 5$. Direct verif
ication confirms:
- $2 \mid 1920 \Rightarrow 3 \mid 1920$ ✓
- $3 \mid 1920 \Rightarrow 8 \mid 1920$ ✓
- $5 \mid 1920 \Rightarrow 24 \mid 1920$ ✓

Therefore, the largest possible integer $n < 2012$ satisfying the stability requ
irement is $\boxed{1920}$.
