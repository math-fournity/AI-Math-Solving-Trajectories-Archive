## Solution

We evaluate

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=r} f(z)\,g(z)\,dz,\qquad r>0.$$

**Setup.** Since $f:\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{C}$ is analytic with a si
mple pole of order $1$ at $0$ with residue $a_1$, near $z=0$ we have the Laurent
 expansion

$$f(z)=\frac{a_1}{z}+a_0+a_{-1}z+a_{-2}z^2+\cdots$$

(the residue is the coefficient of $z^{-1}$). Meanwhile $g:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{
C}$ is entire, so it has a Taylor expansion about $0$,

$$g(z)=g(0)+g'(0)\,z+\tfrac{1}{2}g''(0)\,z^2+\cdots,$$

with $g(0)\neq 0$.

**Product.** The product $h(z):=f(z)g(z)$ is analytic on $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0
\}$ (the only singularity of $f$ is at $0$, and $g$ is entire, so no new singula
rities are introduced; in particular $g$ has no poles that could cancel anything
). Near $z=0$,

$$h(z)=f(z)g(z)=\left(\frac{a_1}{z}+a_0+a_{-1}z+\cdots\right)\!\left(g(0)+g'(0)z
+\tfrac{1}{2}g''(0)z^2+\cdots\right).$$

The coefficient of $z^{-1}$ in this product (the residue of $h$ at $0$) receives
 contributions only from the term $\frac{a_1}{z}$ of $f$ multiplied by the const
ant term $g(0)$ of $g$:

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\bigl(f(z)g(z)\bigr)=a_1\cdot g(0).$$

All other terms of $f$ (which carry non-negative powers of $z$) multiply non-neg
ative powers of $g$ and thus contribute only to non-negative powers of $z$ in th
e product, never to $z^{-1}$.

**Applying the residue theorem.** The circle $|z|=r$ (positively oriented) enclo
ses the unique singularity $z=0$ of $h$. By the residue theorem,

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=r} f(z)\,g(z)\,dz=\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\bigl(f(z)
g(z)\bigr)=a_1\,g(0).$$

This holds for every $r>0$ since $0$ is the only singularity and it lies inside
every such circle.

**Result.**

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=r} f(z)\,g(z)\,dz \;=\; a_1\,g(0
).}$$
