## Proof

Let $V \subset [0,1]$ be a Vitali set, constructed by selecting exactly one repr
esentative from each equivalence class of the relation on $[0,1]$ defined by $x
\sim y \iff x - y \in \mathbb{Q}$.

**Claim.** The outer measure of $V$ satisfies $m^*(V) > 0$. Moreover $m^*(V) \le
 1$, so $m^*(V) \in (0, 1]$.

### Setup

Let $\{q_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ be an enumeration of $\mathbb{Q} \cap [-1, 1]$. For
 each $n$ define the translate
$$V_n := V + q_n = \{v + q_n : v \in V\}.$$

**Pairwise disjointness.** Suppose $V_m \cap V_n \neq \emptyset$ for $m \neq n$.
 Then there exist $u, v \in V$ with $u + q_m = v + q_n$, hence $u - v = q_n - q_
m \in \mathbb{Q}$. By definition of $V$, two elements of $V$ that differ by a ra
tional must be the *same* representative, so $u = v$. Then $q_m = q_n$, contradi
cting $m \neq n$. Thus the $\{V_n\}$ are pairwise disjoint.

**Covering property.** Every $x \in [0,1]$ belongs to some equivalence class, so
 $x = v + q$ for some $v \in V$ and $q \in \mathbb{Q}$. Since $v, x \in [0,1]$,
we have $q = x - v \in [-1,1]$, so $q = q_n$ for some $n$. Hence $x \in V_n$. Th
erefore
$$[0,1] \subseteq \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} V_n.$$

**Containment.** Since $V \subseteq [0,1]$ and $q_n \in [-1,1]$, each $V_n \subs
eteq [-1, 2]$, so
$$\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} V_n \subseteq [-1, 2].$$

### Lower bound: $m^*(V) > 0$

Suppose for contradiction that $m^*(V) = 0$. Outer measure is translation-invari
ant, so $m^*(V_n) = m^*(V) = 0$ for every $n$. By countable subadditivity of out
er measure,
$$m^*\!\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} V_n\right) \le \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} m^*(V_n)
= 0.$$

But $[0,1] \subseteq \bigcup_n V_n$, so by monotonicity
$$1 = m^*([0,1]) \le m^*\!\left(\bigcup_n V_n\right) \le 0,$$
a contradiction. Hence $m^*(V) > 0$.

### Upper bound: $m^*(V) \le 1$

Since $V \subseteq [0,1]$ and outer measure is monotone,
$$m^*(V) \le m^*([0,1]) = 1.$$

### Why no single value is forced

The argument above pins down only the *sign*: $m^*(V) > 0$. The exact value is *
*not** determined by the construction, because outer measure is merely subadditi
ve (not additive on disjoint non-measurable sets), so the disjointness of the $\
{V_n\}$ and the containment $\bigcup_n V_n \subseteq [-1,2]$ (a set of measure $
3$) yield only
$$\sum_n m^*(V_n) \;\not\le\; 3$$
in general — subadditivity goes the wrong way for an upper bound on the sum. Thu
s no contradiction forces $m^*(V) = 0$, and no equality forces a specific positi
ve value. Indeed, by choosing representatives appropriately one can realize Vita
li sets with outer measure equal to any prescribed value in $(0, 1]$.

### Conclusion

The outer measure of a Vitali set $V \subset [0,1]$ is **strictly positive** and
 at most $1$:
$$\boxed{\,m^*(V) \in (0,\, 1]\,}.$$
The only universally forced fact is $m^*(V) > 0$; the precise value depends on t
he choice of representatives and can be any number in $(0, 1]$.
