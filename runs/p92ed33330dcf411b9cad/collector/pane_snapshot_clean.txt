## Proof

A transitive $G$-set is a $G$-set on which $G$ acts transitively. We classify al
l transitive $G$-sets up to $G$-set isomorphism (equivalence).

**Step 1: Every transitive $G$-set is isomorphic to some coset $G$-set $G/H$.**

Let $Y$ be a transitive $G$-set. Pick any point $y \in Y$ and let $H = \operator
name{Stab}_G(y) = \{g \in G : g \cdot y = y\}$, which is a subgroup of $G$. Defi
ne
$$\varphi: G/H \to Y, \qquad gH \mapsto g \cdot y.$$
This is well-defined: if $gH = g'H$, then $g' = gh$ for some $h \in H$, so $g' \
cdot y = gh \cdot y = g \cdot (h \cdot y) = g \cdot y$. It is $G$-equivariant si
nce $\varphi(g_0 \cdot gH) = \varphi(g_0 g H) = g_0 g \cdot y = g_0 \cdot \varph
i(gH)$. It is surjective by transitivity of the action, and injective because $g
 \cdot y = g' \cdot y$ implies $g^{-1}g' \in H$, i.e. $gH = g'H$. Hence $Y \cong
 G/H$ as $G$-sets.

**Step 2: $G/H \cong G/K$ as $G$-sets if and only if $H$ and $K$ are conjugate i
n $G$.**

$(\Leftarrow)$ If $K = g_0 H g_0^{-1}$, the map $gH \mapsto g g_0^{-1} K$ is a $
G$-set isomorphism $G/H \to G/K$.

$(\Rightarrow)$ Suppose $\varphi: G/H \to G/K$ is a $G$-set isomorphism. Let $\v
arphi(H) = g_0 K$. Then for all $h \in H$:
$$\varphi(h \cdot H) = h \cdot \varphi(H) = h g_0 K,$$
but also $h \cdot H = H$, so $\varphi(h \cdot H) = \varphi(H) = g_0 K$. Thus $h
g_0 K = g_0 K$, giving $g_0^{-1} h g_0 \in K$ for all $h \in H$, i.e. $H \subset
eq g_0 K g_0^{-1}$. By symmetry (considering $\varphi^{-1}$), $K \subseteq g_0^{
-1} H g_0$, so $H = g_0 K g_0^{-1}$. Hence $H$ and $K$ are conjugate.

**Step 3: Count.**

By Steps 1 and 2, equivalence classes of transitive $G$-sets are in bijection wi
th conjugacy classes of subgroups of $G$. Since there are $n$ conjugacy classes
of subgroups of $G$, the number of transitive $G$-sets up to equivalence is

$$\boxed{n}.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
