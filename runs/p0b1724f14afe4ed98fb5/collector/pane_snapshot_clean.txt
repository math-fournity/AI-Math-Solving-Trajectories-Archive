**Proof.**

Define $d_n = x_n - x_{n-1}$ for $n \ge 1$. Then
$$x_n - x_{n-2} = (x_n - x_{n-1}) + (x_{n-1} - x_{n-2}) = d_n + d_{n-1}.$$
By hypothesis, setting $\varepsilon_n := x_n - x_{n-2}$, we have $\varepsilon_n
\to 0$, and the identity above reads
$$d_n + d_{n-1} = \varepsilon_n, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad d_n = -d_{n-1} + \varep
silon_n. \tag{1}$$

We solve the recurrence (1) explicitly. Iterating,
$$d_n = (-1)^n d_0 + \sum_{k=1}^{n} (-1)^{\,n-k}\,\varepsilon_k.$$
(Verified directly: for $n=1$, $d_1 = -d_0+\varepsilon_1$; for $n=2$, $d_2 = d_0
 - \varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2$; the pattern continues by induction using (1).
)

Since $(-1)^{n-k} = (-1)^n(-1)^k$, this becomes
$$d_n = (-1)^n\!\left(d_0 + \sum_{k=1}^{n} (-1)^k \varepsilon_k\right).$$

Dividing by $n$,
$$\frac{d_n}{n} = \frac{(-1)^n d_0}{n} + \frac{(-1)^n}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n} (-1)^k \
varepsilon_k. \tag{2}$$

The first term of (2) satisfies $\left|\frac{(-1)^n d_0}{n}\right| = \frac{|d_0|
}{n} \to 0$.

For the second term, observe that $\varepsilon_k \to 0$ implies $(-1)^k \varepsi
lon_k \to 0$ as well. By **Cesàro's theorem** (if a sequence converges to $L$, i
ts arithmetic means converge to $L$), applied to the null sequence $a_k := (-1)^
k \varepsilon_k$,
$$\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n} (-1)^k \varepsilon_k \;\longrightarrow\; 0.$$
Consequently
$$\left|\frac{(-1)^n}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n} (-1)^k \varepsilon_k\right| = \frac{1}{n}
\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n} (-1)^k \varepsilon_k\right| \longrightarrow 0.$$

Both terms in (2) tend to $0$, so $\displaystyle\frac{d_n}{n} \to 0$. Recalling
that $d_n = x_n - x_{n-1}$,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{x_n - x_{n-1}}{n} = 0. \qquad\blacksquare$$
