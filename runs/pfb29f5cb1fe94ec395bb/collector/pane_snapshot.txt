## Solution

**Coordinate system.** Place the side-5 equilateral triangle on a triangular lat
tice with grid points $(a,b)$ where $a,b \geq 0$ and $a+b \leq 5$. There are $\b
inom{7}{2}=21$ grid points.

**Upward triangles.** An upward triangle of side $s$ with bottom-left vertex at
$(a,b)$ has vertices $(a,b)$, $(a+s,b)$, $(a,b+s)$. It lies inside the field iff
 $a+b+s\leq 5$. The number of upward triangles of side $s$ is the number of pair
s $(a,b)$ with $a,b\geq 0$, $a+b\leq 5-s$, which is $\binom{7-s}{2}$.

| $s$ | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|-----|---|---|---|---|---|
| count | 15 | 10 | 6 | 3 | 1 |

Total upward triangles: $N = 15+10+6+3+1 = 35$.

Total pairs: $\binom{35}{2} = 595$.

**Key lemma: two distinct upward triangles share at most one vertex.** Each of t
he three edges of an upward triangle lies in a distinct lattice direction (horiz
ontal, $60°$, $120°$). If two upward triangles shared two vertices, they would s
hare the edge between them, which has a fixed direction. For each lattice direct
ion, a given segment is the edge of exactly one upward triangle (it's the bottom
 edge / left edge / right edge of a unique triangle). Hence no two distinct upwa
rd triangles share an edge, so they share at most one vertex.

**Counting pairs sharing exactly one vertex.** For each grid point $P=(x,y)$, le
t $f(P)$ = number of upward triangles having $P$ as a vertex. A triangle $(a,b,s
)$ contains $P$ in one of three roles:

- **Bottom-left vertex** $(a,b)=(x,y)$: $s$ ranges $1$ to $5-x-y$ → $5-x-y$ tria
ngles (if $x+y\leq 4$).
- **Bottom-right vertex** $(a+s,b)=(x,y)$: $s$ ranges $1$ to $x$ → $x$ triangles
.
- **Top vertex** $(a,b+s)=(x,y)$: $s$ ranges $1$ to $y$ → $y$ triangles.

So $f(x,y) = \max(5-x-y,\,0)+x+y = 5$ for every grid point (since $x+y\leq 5$).

Since no pair shares more than one vertex, the number of pairs sharing exactly o
ne vertex equals:

$$\sum_{P}\binom{f(P)}{2} = 21\cdot\binom{5}{2} = 21\cdot 10 = 210.$$

**Probability.** $\dfrac{210}{595}$. Simplify: $\gcd(210,595)=35$, giving $\dfra
c{6}{17}$.

Thus $m=6$, $n=17$, and $m+n = \boxed{23}$.
