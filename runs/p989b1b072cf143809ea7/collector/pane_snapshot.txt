Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every IA-automorphism of $F
_n$ has a fixed point outside the commutator subgroup.

**Answer: No** (for $n \geq 2$).

## Proof

Let $F_n = \langle x_1, \dots, x_n \rangle$ with $n \geq 2$. Consider the elemen
t
$$g = [x_1, x_2] = x_1 x_2 x_1^{-1} x_2^{-1} \in [F_n, F_n] \setminus \{1\}.$$

This is non-trivial since $F_n$ is free of rank $\geq 2$.

**The automorphism.** Define $\varphi = \mathrm{Inn}_g \in \mathrm{Aut}(F_n)$ by
 $\varphi(w) = gwg^{-1}$. Since every inner automorphism acts trivially on the a
belianization $F_n/[F_n,F_n] \cong \mathbb{Z}^n$, we have $\varphi \in \mathrm{I
A}_n$.

**Fixed points of $\varphi$.** An element $w \in F_n$ is fixed by $\varphi$ if a
nd only if $gwg^{-1} = w$, i.e., $gw = wg$, i.e., $w$ commutes with $g$.

**Centralizers in free groups are cyclic.** By the classical theorem (Magnus–Kar
rass–Solitar, or Lyndon–Schützenberger): in a free group, the centralizer of any
 non-identity element is infinite cyclic. Concretely, there exists $h \in F_n$ a
nd $k \geq 1$ such that
$$C_{F_n}(g) = \langle h \rangle, \qquad g = h^k.$$

**All fixed points lie in $[F_n, F_n]$.** Since $g = h^k \in [F_n, F_n]$, consid
er the image $\bar{h}$ of $h$ in $F_n/[F_n, F_n] \cong \mathbb{Z}^n$. We have $k
 \cdot \bar{h} = \bar{g} = 0$ in $\mathbb{Z}^n$. Since $\mathbb{Z}^n$ is torsion
-free, $\bar{h} = 0$, so $h \in [F_n, F_n]$.

Therefore every fixed point of $\varphi$ lies in $\langle h \rangle \subseteq [F
_n, F_n]$, and $\varphi$ has **no** fixed point in $F_n \setminus [F_n, F_n]$.

**Non-triviality.** The automorphism $\varphi = \mathrm{Inn}_g$ is non-trivial b
ecause $g \neq 1$ and the center of $F_n$ ($n \geq 2$) is trivial, so $g$ does n
ot commute with every element.

**Conclusion.** For every $n \geq 2$, the IA-automorphism $\mathrm{Inn}_{[x_1, x
_2]}$ provides a counterexample: it has no fixed point in $F_n \setminus [F_n, F
_n]$.

(For $n = 1$, $\mathrm{IA}_1$ is trivial since $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}) \to \ma
thrm{GL}(1,\mathbb{Z})$ is an isomorphism, so the statement holds vacuously.) $\
blacksquare$
