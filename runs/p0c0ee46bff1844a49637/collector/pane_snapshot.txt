## Proof

Let $\mathbb{H}^2$ denote the hyperbolic plane and let $\partial_\infty\mathbb{H
}^2$ be its boundary at infinity (the set of ideal points, i.e. equivalence clas
ses of asymptotic geodesic rays). Set $X = \mathbb{H}^2 \cup \partial_\infty\mat
hbb{H}^2$. We topologize $X$ by taking as a subbasis the open subsets of $\mathb
b{H}^2$ together with the sets $U_P$, where $P$ ranges over all open half-planes
 of $\mathbb{H}^2$.

**Step 1. Description of $U_P$.** An open half-plane $P$ is one of the two compo
nents of $\mathbb{H}^2 \setminus \gamma$, where $\gamma$ is a complete geodesic.
 The geodesic $\gamma$ has two distinct ideal endpoints $a, b \in \partial_\inft
y\mathbb{H}^2$, and $\gamma \cup \{a,b\}$ separates the closed disk $\overline{\
mathbb{H}^2} = \mathbb{H}^2 \cup \partial_\infty\mathbb{H}^2$ into two closed re
gions sharing the chord $\gamma \cup \{a,b\}$. The set $U_P$ is
$$U_P \;=\; P \;\cup\; A_P,$$
where $A_P \subset \partial_\infty\mathbb{H}^2$ is the open arc of $\partial_\in
fty\mathbb{H}^2$ lying on the same side of $\gamma$ as $P$ (the arc from $a$ to
$b$ not containing the ideal points of the opposite half-plane). In particular
$$U_P \cap \partial_\infty\mathbb{H}^2 \;=\; A_P,$$
an open arc of the boundary.

**Step 2. The subspace topology on $\partial_\infty\mathbb{H}^2$ is generated by
 open arcs.** By definition of the subspace topology, a subset $V \subseteq \par
tial_\infty\mathbb{H}^2$ is open iff $V = W \cap \partial_\infty\mathbb{H}^2$ fo
r some open $W \subseteq X$. The only subbasic open sets of $X$ that meet $\part
ial_\infty\mathbb{H}^2$ are the $U_P$ (the open subsets of $\mathbb{H}^2$ are di
sjoint from the boundary). Hence the subspace topology is generated by the sets
$A_P = U_P \cap \partial_\infty\mathbb{H}^2$, i.e. by open arcs of $\partial_\in
fty\mathbb{H}^2$.

**Step 3. Every open arc of $\partial_\infty\mathbb{H}^2$ is realized.** We use
the standard fact that in $\mathbb{H}^2$, for any two distinct ideal points $p \
neq q$ in $\partial_\infty\mathbb{H}^2$ there is a unique complete geodesic $\ga
mma_{pq}$ with ideal endpoints $p$ and $q$. The two half-planes bounded by $\gam
ma_{pq}$ yield the two open arcs of $\partial_\infty\mathbb{H}^2$ with endpoints
 $p, q$. Therefore *every* open arc of $\partial_\infty\mathbb{H}^2$ (between an
y two distinct boundary points) occurs as some $A_P$.

**Step 4. Comparison with the standard topology on $S^1$.** The collection of al
l open arcs of a circle is a basis for the standard topology of $S^1$: every ope
n set in $S^1$ is a union of open arcs, and open arcs are themselves open in $S^
1$. Conversely, by Step 2 and Step 3, the open sets of $\partial_\infty\mathbb{H
}^2$ in the subspace topology are exactly the unions of open arcs. Hence the sub
space topology on $\partial_\infty\mathbb{H}^2$ coincides with the topology gene
rated by all open arcs, which is precisely the standard topology of the circle.

**Step 5. Conclusion.** Choose any homeomorphism of sets $\varphi : \partial_\in
fty\mathbb{H}^2 \to S^1$ (both sets have the cardinality of the continuum, and s
uch a bijection exists). More concretely, in any model — e.g. the upper half-pla
ne model where $\partial_\infty\mathbb{H}^2 = \mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty\}$ — the
identification with $S^1$ is explicit. Under this identification the open arcs o
f $\partial_\infty\mathbb{H}^2$ correspond exactly to the open arcs of $S^1$, an
d since both topologies are generated by their open arcs (Steps 2–4), $\varphi$
pulls open sets to open sets in both directions. Thus $\varphi$ is a homeomorphi
sm.

Therefore $\partial_\infty\mathbb{H}^2$, endowed with the subspace topology indu
ced by the open sets of $\mathbb{H}^2$ and the sets $U_P$, is homeomorphic to $S
^1$.
