## Upper Bound: $n \leq 14$

**Pair-counting bound.** Each elevator stopping at 6 floors covers $\binom{6}{2}
 = 15$ pairs. With 7 elevators, the total pair-coverage capacity is $7 \times 15
 = 105$. Since every pair of floors must be covered at least once:
$$\binom{n}{2} \leq 105 \implies n(n-1) \leq 210 \implies n \leq 15.$$

**Ruling out $n = 15$ via incidence counting.** Suppose $n = 15$. For any floor
$f$, let $d_f$ be the number of elevators stopping at $f$. Each such elevator pa
irs $f$ with 5 other floors, so $f$ can be paired with at most $5\,d_f$ distinct
 other floors. Since $f$ must be paired with all other 14 floors:
$$5\,d_f \geq 14 \implies d_f \geq 3 \quad (\text{since } 5 \cdot 2 = 10 < 14).$
$
Summing over all 15 floors: total floor-elevator incidences $\geq 15 \times 3 =
45$. But the actual total is $7 \times 6 = 42 < 45$. **Contradiction.** So $n =
15$ is impossible, giving $n \leq 14$.

## Construction Achieving $n = 14$

I use the **Fano plane** (the projective plane of order 2), which has:
- 7 points and 7 lines,
- each line contains exactly 3 points,
- each point lies on exactly 3 lines,
- every two points lie on exactly one common line.

**Construction.** Replace each Fano point $p$ with two copies $p_1, p_2$, yieldi
ng 14 floors total. For each Fano line $\ell = \{p, q, r\}$, create an elevator
stopping at the 6 floors $\{p_1, p_2, q_1, q_2, r_1, r_2\}$. This gives 7 elevat
ors, each stopping at exactly 6 floors.

**Verification of pair coverage.** Take any two floors $a, b$ among the 14:

- **Case 1: $a, b$ are copies of the same Fano point** (say $p_1, p_2$). The Fan

 $p \neq q$). In the Fano plane, every two distinct points lie on exactly one co

Every pair of floors is covered by at least one elevator, and we have exactly 7
