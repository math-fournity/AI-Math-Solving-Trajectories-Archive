Proof

We are given that $H$ is a subgroup of $G$ satisfying $g^{-1} h g \in H$ for all
$g \in G$ and all $h \in H$. This is precisely the condition that $H$ is a
normal subgroup of $G$. We show that for every $g \in G$, the left coset $gH$
equals the right coset $Hg$.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 1: $gH \subseteq Hg$.

Let $x \in gH$. Then $x = gh$ for some $h \in H$. We must show $x \in Hg$, i.e.,
$x = h' g$ for some $h' \in H$.

Consider the element $g h g^{-1}$. By the given condition applied to $g^{-1} \in
G$ and $h \in H$ (note the condition holds for all $g \in G$, so in particular
for $g^{-1}$), we have $$(g^{-1})^{-1}, h, (g^{-1}) = g h g^{-1} \in H.$$

Set $h' = g h g^{-1} \in H$. Then $$h' g = (g h g^{-1}) g = g h (g^{-1} g) = g h
e = g h = x.$$

Thus $x = h' g \in Hg$, proving $gH \subseteq Hg$.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 2: $Hg \subseteq gH$.

Let $y \in Hg$. Then $y = h g$ for some $h \in H$. We must show $y \in gH$,
i.e., $y = g h''$ for some $h'' \in H$.

By the given condition (applied to $g \in G$ and $h \in H$), we have $$g^{-1} h
g \in H.$$

Set $h'' = g^{-1} h g \in H$. Then $$g h'' = g(g^{-1} h g) = (g g^{-1}) h g = e,
h g = h g = y.$$

Thus $y = g h'' \in gH$, proving $Hg \subseteq gH$.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Conclusion.

From Step 1 and Step 2, $gH \subseteq Hg$ and $Hg \subseteq gH$, so $$gH = Hg$$
for every $g \in G$. $\blacksquare$
