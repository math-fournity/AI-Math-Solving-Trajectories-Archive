Yes. The subgroup $GL_n^+(\mathbb{R}) = \{A \in GL_n(\mathbb{R}) : \det A > 0\}$
 has index $2$ in $GL_n(\mathbb{R})$.

**Proof.**

Consider the determinant homomorphism
$$\det : GL_n(\mathbb{R}) \to \mathbb{R}^{\times} = \mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}.$$
This is a surjective group homomorphism (every nonzero real number is the determ
inant of some invertible matrix, e.g. $\operatorname{diag}(t,1,\dots,1)$ for $t
\neq 0$).

The multiplicative group $\mathbb{R}^{\times}$ has a subgroup $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$
of index $2$: the cosets are $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ and $\mathbb{R}_{<0}$, since ever
y nonzero real is either positive or negative, and $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ is closed u
nder multiplication while $\mathbb{R}_{<0} = (-1)\cdot\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ is its si
ngle nontrivial coset.

Taking the preimage under $\det$,
$$GL_n^+(\mathbb{R}) := \det^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>0}) = \{A \in GL_n(\mathbb{R}) :
\det A > 0\}.$$
Since $\det$ is a surjective homomorphism, the preimage of a subgroup of index $
k$ is a subgroup of index $k$. Concretely, the two cosets of $GL_n^+(\mathbb{R})
$ in $GL_n(\mathbb{R})$ are:
- $GL_n^+(\mathbb{R})$ itself (matrices with positive determinant), and
- $\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,\dots,1)\cdot GL_n^+(\mathbb{R})$ (matrices with neg
ative determinant).

Every invertible real matrix has either positive or negative determinant, so the
se two cosets partition $GL_n(\mathbb{R})$, confirming
$$[GL_n(\mathbb{R}) : GL_n^+(\mathbb{R})] = 2.$$

Thus $GL_n^+(\mathbb{R})$ is a proper subgroup of finite index (index $2$) in $G
L_n(\mathbb{R})$. $\blacksquare$
