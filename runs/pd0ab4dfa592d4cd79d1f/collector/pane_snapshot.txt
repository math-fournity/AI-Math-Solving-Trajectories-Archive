## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. If the ideal $I = (p_1, \dots, p_k) \subset \mathbb{F}[x_1,\dots
,x_n]$ is proper, then the ideal $I^e = (p_1,\dots,p_k) \subset \mathbb{K}[x_1,\
dots,x_n]$ generated by the same polynomials over the algebraic closure is also
proper.

We prove this using faithful flatness of the field extension.

---

**Step 1: $\mathbb{K}$ is a free $\mathbb{F}$-module.**

Every field extension $\mathbb{F} \subseteq \mathbb{K}$ gives $\mathbb{K}$ the s
tructure of an $\mathbb{F}$-vector space. By the axiom of choice, every vector s
pace admits a basis, so $\mathbb{K}$ is a **free** $\mathbb{F}$-module.

**Step 2: $\mathbb{K}[x_1,\dots,x_n] \cong \mathbb{K} \otimes_{\mathbb{F}} \math
bb{F}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$.**

This is the standard base-change isomorphism for polynomial rings: extending sca
lars on the coefficient field is exactly tensoring with $\mathbb{K}$ over $\math
bb{F}$. Concretely, the map
$$\mathbb{K} \otimes_{\mathbb{F}} \mathbb{F}[x_1,\dots,x_n] \to \mathbb{K}[x_1,\
dots,x_n], \qquad a \otimes f \mapsto a \cdot f$$
is an isomorphism of $\mathbb{K}$-algebras (and of $\mathbb{F}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$-m
odules).

**Step 3: $\mathbb{K}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ is a **free** $\mathbb{F}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$-
module.**

Let $\{e_j\}_{j \in J}$ be an $\mathbb{F}$-basis of $\mathbb{K}$. Then as an $\m
athbb{F}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$-module,
$$\mathbb{K}[x_1,\dots,x_n] \cong \mathbb{K} \otimes_{\mathbb{F}} \mathbb{F}[x_1
,\dots,x_n] \cong \bigoplus_{j \in J} \mathbb{F}[x_1,\dots,x_n],$$
with the isomorphism sending $e_j \otimes f \mapsto f$ in the $j$-th summand. He
nce $\mathbb{K}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ is a **free** $\mathbb{F}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$-module
.

**Step 4: Free modules are faithfully flat.**

A free module over a ring $R$ is flat (tensor products distribute over direct su
ms, and $R$ itself is flat). It is **faithfully** flat because $R \neq 0$ and a
direct sum of copies of $R$ is zero only when there are zero summands; in genera
l, for a free module $F$, $F \otimes_R M = 0 \iff M = 0$ (since a nonzero elemen
t of $M$ tensored with a basis element of $F$ is nonzero). This characterizes fa
ithful flatness.

Therefore the ring homomorphism $\varphi: \mathbb{F}[x_1,\dots,x_n] \hookrightar
row \mathbb{K}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ is **faithfully flat**.

**Step 5: Faithful flatness preserves properness of ideals.**

This is the fundamental property of faithfully flat extensions. We verify it dir
ectly. Let $R = \mathbb{F}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$, $S = \mathbb{K}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$, and
 $I = (p_1,\dots,p_k) \subset R$. The extended ideal is $IS = I^e \subset S$.

- ($\Leftarrow$) If $I = R$, then clearly $IS = S$.
- ($\Rightarrow$) Suppose $IS = S$. Then $1 \in IS$, i.e., $S/IS = 0$. By faithf
ul flatness, $S/IS \cong S \otimes_R (R/I)$, so $S \otimes_R (R/I) = 0$. Faithfu
l flatness gives $R/I = 0$, hence $I = R$.

Contrapositively: $I \neq R \implies IS \neq S$.

**Step 6: Conclusion.**

By hypothesis, $I = (p_1,\dots,p_k)$ is proper in $\mathbb{F}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$, i
.e., $I \neq \mathbb{F}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$. By Step 5 applied to the faithfully fla
t extension of Step 4, the extended ideal $I^e = (p_1,\dots,p_k) \subset \mathbb
{K}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ satisfies $I^e \neq \mathbb{K}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$. Hence $I^e$
is proper. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** This argument uses only that $\mathbb{K}$ is a field extension of $\
mathbb{F}$ (any extension, not necessarily algebraic or a closure). The algebrai
c-closure hypothesis is more than sufficient; it is relevant for the *converse d
irection* (weak Nullstellensatz: over an algebraically closed field, a proper id
eal has a common zero), but for the question asked — preservation of properness
under base change — faithful flatness of *any* field extension suffices.
