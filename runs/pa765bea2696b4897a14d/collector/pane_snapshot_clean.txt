## Proof

Place the unit cube as $C = [0,1]^3$ with the space diagonal from $(0,0,0)$ to $
(1,1,1)$ as the rotation axis. Let $R$ be rotation by $\pi/3$ about this axis, a
nd $C' = R(C)$.

### Cross-Section Decomposition

Parameterize the diagonal by $(t,t,t)$ for $t \in [0,1]$. The plane $\Pi_t: x+y+
z = 3t$ is perpendicular to the diagonal. Since $R$ fixes the axis pointwise, $R
$ maps each $\Pi_t$ to itself, so:
$$\operatorname{Area}\bigl((C\cap C')\cap\Pi_t\bigr) = \operatorname{Area}\bigl(
(C\cap\Pi_t)\cap R(C\cap\Pi_t)\bigr).$$

### Cross-Sections of $C$

The section $C\cap\Pi_t$ is:
- **Equilateral triangle** with vertices $(3t,0,0),(0,3t,0),(0,0,3t)$ for $t\in[
0,\tfrac13]$;
- **Equiangular hexagon** with alternating side lengths $a\sqrt2$ and $(1{-}a)\s
qrt2$ (where $a=3t{-}1$) for $t\in[\tfrac13,\tfrac23]$;
- **Equilateral triangle** with vertices $(1,1,3t{-}2),(1,3t{-}2,1),(3t{-}2,1,1)
$ for $t\in[\tfrac23,1]$.

### Half-Plane Representation

The section $C\cap\Pi_t$ is cut out by 6 face-constraints with outward normals (
in $\Pi_t$) at angles $30°,90°,150°,210°,270°,330°$. The distances from the cent
er $(t,t,t)$ to these 6 constraints are:
$$(1{-}t)\sqrt{3/2},\ t\sqrt{3/2},\ (1{-}t)\sqrt{3/2},\ t\sqrt{3/2},\ (1{-}t)\sq
rt{3/2},\ t\sqrt{3/2}.$$

(For $t\in[0,\tfrac13]$, only the three at $90°,210°,330°$ with distance $t\sqrt
{3/2}$ are active — yielding the triangle; similarly near $t=1$.)

### Effect of Rotation by $\pi/3$

The 6 normal directions are equally spaced at $60°$ intervals, so rotation by $6
0°$ permutes them cyclically (shifting by one position). This **swaps** the alte
rnating distances. The rotated section $R(C\cap\Pi_t)$ has distances:
$$t\sqrt{3/2},\ (1{-}t)\sqrt{3/2},\ t\sqrt{3/2},\ (1{-}t)\sqrt{3/2},\ t\sqrt{3/2
},\ (1{-}t)\sqrt{3/2}.$$

### The Intersection Is a Regular Hexagon

At each of the 6 directions, the intersection takes the more restrictive (smalle
r) distance:
$$\min\bigl(t,\,1{-}t\bigr)\sqrt{3/2} \quad\text{at all 6 directions.}$$

This is a **regular hexagon** with inradius $r = \min(t,1{-}t)\sqrt{3/2}$.

*Triangular check ($t\in[0,\tfrac13]$):* The triangle's 3 active half-planes (at
 $90°,210°,330°$, distance $t\sqrt{3/2}$) and the rotated triangle's 3 active ha
lf-planes (at $150°,270°,30°$, distance $t\sqrt{3/2}$) combine into 6 half-plane
s all at distance $t\sqrt{3/2}$ — a regular hexagon. ✓ (Same near $t=1$ with $t\
to 1{-}t$.)

### Cross-Sectional Area

A regular hexagon with inradius $r$ has area $2\sqrt{3}\,r^2$, so:
$$\operatorname{Area}(t) = 2\sqrt{3}\cdot\tfrac{3}{2}\min(t,1{-}t)^2 = 3\sqrt{3}
\,\min(t,1{-}t)^2.$$

*Verification at $t=\tfrac12$:* $3\sqrt3\cdot\tfrac14 = \tfrac{3\sqrt3}{4}$, mat
ching the regular hexagon (side $1/\sqrt2$) of the cube's mid-section, which is
invariant under $60°$ rotation. ✓

*Continuity at $t=\tfrac13$:* triangle gives $3\sqrt3\cdot\tfrac19=\tfrac{\sqrt3
}{3}$; hexagon gives the same. ✓

### Volume (Cavalieri's Principle)

$$V = \int_0^1 3\sqrt{3}\,\min(t,1{-}t)^2\,dt = 2\cdot 3\sqrt{3}\int_0^{1/2} t^2
\,dt = 6\sqrt{3}\cdot\frac{1}{24} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}.$$

### Result

The volume of the intersection $C\cap C'$ is $\boxed{\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}}$.
