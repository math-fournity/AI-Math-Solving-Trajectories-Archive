## Evaluation of $\int_0^\infty \frac{\cos x}{x^2+1}\,dx$

### Setup

Consider the complex function
$$f(z) = \frac{e^{iz}}{z^2+1},$$
which is meromorphic on $\mathbb{C}$ with simple poles at $z = \pm i$ (the zeros
 of $z^2+1 = (z-i)(z+i)$).

Let $\Gamma_R$ be the closed contour consisting of:
- the real segment $[-R, R]$, and
- the upper semicircular arc $C_R = \{Re^{i\theta} : 0 \le \theta \le \pi\}$,

oriented counterclockwise. For $R > 1$, the pole $z = i$ lies inside $\Gamma_R$,
 while $z = -i$ lies outside.

### Residue computation

The residue of $f$ at $z = i$:
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=i} f(z) = \lim_{z \to i} (z-i)\frac{e^{iz}}{(z-i)(z+i)}
= \frac{e^{i\cdot i}}{i+i} = \frac{e^{-1}}{2i}.$$

By the residue theorem:
$$\oint_{\Gamma_R} f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot \frac{e^{-1}}{2i} = \frac{\pi}{e}.$$

### The arc integral vanishes

We show $\int_{C_R} f(z)\,dz \to 0$ as $R \to \infty$. On $C_R$, write $z = Re^{
i\theta}$ with $\theta \in [0,\pi]$, so $|e^{iz}| = e^{-R\sin\theta}$. Then
$$\left|\int_{C_R} \frac{e^{iz}}{z^2+1}\,dz\right| \le \int_0^\pi \frac{e^{-R\si
n\theta}}{|R^2e^{2i\theta}+1|}\,R\,d\theta \le \int_0^\pi \frac{R\,e^{-R\sin\the
ta}}{R^2-1}\,d\theta.$$

Using Jordan's inequality $\sin\theta \ge \frac{2\theta}{\pi}$ for $\theta \in [
0,\pi/2]$ (and symmetry about $\pi/2$):
$$\int_0^\pi e^{-R\sin\theta}\,d\theta = 2\int_0^{\pi/2} e^{-R\sin\theta}\,d\the
ta \le 2\int_0^{\pi/2} e^{-2R\theta/\pi}\,d\theta = 2\cdot\frac{\pi}{2R}\bigl(1
- e^{-R}\bigr) = \frac{\pi}{R}(1-e^{-R}).$$

Therefore
$$\left|\int_{C_R} f(z)\,dz\right| \le \frac{R}{R^2-1}\cdot\frac{\pi}{R}(1-e^{-R
}) = \frac{\pi(1-e^{-R})}{R^2-1} \xrightarrow{R\to\infty} 0.$$

### Taking the limit

Decomposing the contour integral:
$$\int_{-R}^{R} \frac{e^{ix}}{x^2+1}\,dx + \int_{C_R} f(z)\,dz = \frac{\pi}{e}.$
$

Letting $R \to \infty$:
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{ix}}{x^2+1}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{e}.$$

Taking real parts (and noting $\operatorname{Re}(e^{ix}) = \cos x$):
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\cos x}{x^2+1}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{e}.$$

### Reducing to $[0,\infty)$

The integrand $\frac{\cos x}{x^2+1}$ is even (since $\cos(-x) = \cos x$ and $(-x
)^2 = x^2$), so
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\cos x}{x^2+1}\,dx = 2\int_0^{\infty} \frac{\cos
 x}{x^2+1}\,dx.$$

Hence
$$\int_0^\infty \frac{\cos x}{x^2+1}\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\pi}{e} = \frac
{\pi}{2e}.$$

### Result

$$\boxed{\int_0^\infty \frac{\cos x}{x^2+1}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{2e}}$$
