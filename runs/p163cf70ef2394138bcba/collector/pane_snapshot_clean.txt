## The j-invariant and the cross-ratio of the four branch points

### 1. Setup: the elliptic curve as a double cover of ℙ¹

Every elliptic curve $E$ (over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $
\neq 2$) admits a degree-2 map $\pi: E \to \mathbb{P}^1$ given by the hyperellip
tic / quotient-by-$[-1]$ involution. By the Riemann–Hurwitz formula this cover i
s branched at exactly four points $p_1, p_2, p_3, p_4 \in \mathbb{P}^1$. The cro
ss-ratio

$$
\lambda \;=\; [p_1,p_2;p_3,p_4] \;=\; \frac{(p_3-p_1)(p_4-p_2)}{(p_3-p_2)(p_4-p_
1)}
$$

is the fundamental invariant of an unordered quadruple of points on $\mathbb{P}^
1$, since $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ acts triply transitively: we may send three of the br
anch points to $\{0,1,\infty\}$, and the fourth lands at $\lambda$. The curve is
 then presented in **Legendre form**

$$
E_\lambda:\quad y^2 \;=\; x(x-1)(x-\lambda).
$$

### 2. Computing the j-invariant of $E_\lambda$

Write $x(x-1)(x-\lambda) = x^3 - (1+\lambda)x^2 + \lambda x$. To pass to short W
eierstrass form $y^2 = u^3 + Au + B$, substitute $x = u + \tfrac{1+\lambda}{3}$
(eliminating the $x^2$ term). A direct calculation gives

$$
A \;=\; -\frac{1-\lambda+\lambda^2}{3}, \qquad
B \;=\; -\frac{(1+\lambda)(2\lambda-1)(\lambda-2)}{27}.
$$

For $y^2 = u^3 + Au + B$ the classical formula is

$$
j \;=\; \frac{1728\cdot 4A^3}{4A^3 + 27B^2}.
$$

Now compute the two ingredients.

**Numerator.** $\;4A^3 = -\dfrac{4(1-\lambda+\lambda^2)^3}{27}$.

**Denominator.** Set $g = \lambda^2 - \lambda + 1$ and $f = (1+\lambda)(2\lambda
-1)(\lambda-2) = 2\lambda^3 - 3\lambda^2 - 3\lambda + 2$. Then

$$
4A^3 + 27B^2 \;=\; \frac{-4g^3 + f^2}{27}.
$$

Expanding:

$$
g^3 = \lambda^6 - 3\lambda^5 + 6\lambda^4 - 7\lambda^3 + 6\lambda^2 - 3\lambda +
 1,
$$

$$
f^2 = 4\lambda^6 - 12\lambda^5 - 3\lambda^4 + 26\lambda^3 - 3\lambda^2 - 12\lamb
da + 4.
$$

Subtracting,

$$
f^2 - 4g^3 \;=\; -27\lambda^4 + 54\lambda^3 - 27\lambda^2 \;=\; -27\,\lambda^2(\
lambda-1)^2 \;=\; -27\,\lambda^2(1-\lambda)^2.
$$

Hence

$$
4A^3 + 27B^2 \;=\; -\lambda^2(1-\lambda)^2.
$$

**Assembling:**

$$
j(\lambda) \;=\; \frac{1728\cdot\bigl(-\tfrac{4(1-\lambda+\lambda^2)^3}{27}\bigr
)}{-\lambda^2(1-\lambda)^2}
\;=\; \frac{1728\cdot 4}{27}\cdot\frac{(1-\lambda+\lambda^2)^3}{\lambda^2(1-\lam
bda)^2}.
$$

Since $\tfrac{1728\cdot 4}{27} = 256$, we obtain the **Legendre formula**

$$
\boxed{\;j \;=\; 256\,\frac{(1-\lambda+\lambda^2)^3}{\lambda^2(1-\lambda)^2}\;}
$$

where $\lambda$ is the cross-ratio of the four branch points (after normalizing
three of them to $0,1,\infty$).

### 3. Why this relationship is significant for classification

**(a) The six cross-ratio values and the anharmonic group.** The four branch poi
nts are *unordered*, while the cross-ratio depends on an ordering. Permuting the
 four points acts on $\lambda$ through the **anharmonic group** $S_3 \subset \ma
thrm{PGL}_2$, generated by $\lambda \mapsto 1/\lambda$ and $\lambda \mapsto 1-\l
ambda$, producing the six values

$$
\lambda,\quad \frac{1}{\lambda},\quad 1-\lambda,\quad \frac{1}{1-\lambda},\quad
\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1},\quad \frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}.
$$

A direct check shows that $j(\lambda)$ as defined above is invariant under both
generators $\lambda\mapsto 1/\lambda$ and $\lambda\mapsto 1-\lambda$:

- $1 - \tfrac{1}{\lambda} + \tfrac{1}{\lambda^2} = \tfrac{\lambda^2-\lambda+1}{\
lambda^2}$, so the numerator picks up a factor $\lambda^{-6}$ and the denominato

/\lambda)=j(\lambda)$;
- $1-(1-\lambda)+(1-\lambda)^2 = 1-\lambda+\lambda^2$, and $(1-\lambda)^2(1-(1-\
lambda))^2 = (1-\lambda)^2\lambda^2$, so $j(1-\lambda)=j(\lambda)$.

Thus $j$ is constant on each $S_3$-orbit: **it depends only on the unordered set
 of four branch points**, i.e. only on the isomorphism class of the double cover

**(b) $j$ is a complete invariant.** Conversely, the map $\lambda \mapsto j(\lam
bda)$ realizes the quotient $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus\{0,1,\infty\} \to \mathbb{A}

$$
E_\lambda \;\cong\; E_{\lambda'} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad j(\lambda)=j(\lam
bda').
$$

So the j-invariant is a **complete invariant** of elliptic curves: the moduli st

**(c) Geometric meaning.** The formula makes explicit how the single complex num
