Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the process $Y_n(t) = \frac
{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor n^a t\rfloor} X_i$ converges in distribution to
 the zero process when $0 < a < 1/2$.

**Answer: Yes, the convergence holds.**

**Proof.**

Let $S_k = \sum_{i=1}^k X_i$ and set $m_n = \lfloor n^a \rfloor$. Since $a > 0$,
 we have $m_n \to \infty$ as $n \to \infty$.

**Step 1: Rewrite the process.** Define the Donsker-scaled process
$$\widetilde{W}_n(t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{m_n}} S_{\lfloor n^a t \rfloor}.$$
Then we can write
$$Y_n(t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} S_{\lfloor n^a t \rfloor} = \frac{\sqrt{m_n}}{\sqr
t{n}} \cdot \widetilde{W}_n(t) = c_n \,\widetilde{W}_n(t),$$
where $c_n = \frac{\sqrt{m_n}}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{\sqrt{\lfloor n^a\rfloor}}{\sqr
t{n}}$.

Since $0 < a < \frac{1}{2}$, we have $a < 1$, so
$$c_n = \frac{\sqrt{\lfloor n^a\rfloor}}{\sqrt{n}} \sim \frac{n^{a/2}}{n^{1/2}}
= n^{(a-1)/2} \to 0 \quad \text{as } n\to\infty.$$

**Step 2: $\widetilde{W}_n$ converges in distribution to Brownian motion.** By D
onsker's invariance principle (which requires only $\mathbb{E}[X_i]=0$ and $\ope
ratorname{Var}(X_i)=1$), for any sequence of integers $m\to\infty$,
$$\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}} S_{\lfloor mt\rfloor}\right)_t \xrightarrow{d} (B(t))
_t \quad \text{in } D[0,\infty),$$
where $B$ is a standard Brownian motion. Applying this with $m = m_n = \lfloor n
^a\rfloor \to \infty$:
$$\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_n}} S_{\lfloor m_n t\rfloor}\right)_t \xrightarrow{d} (
B(t))_t.$$

It remains to note that $\widetilde{W}_n(t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{m_n}} S_{\lfloor n^
a t\rfloor}$ differs from $\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_n}} S_{\lfloor m_n t\rfloor}$ only b
y a sum of at most $K = O(T+1)$ terms (since $|n^a - m_n| < 1$ implies $|\lfloor
 n^a t\rfloor - \lfloor m_n t\rfloor| \leq T+1$ for $t \in [0,T]$). Specifically
,
$$\sup_{0\le t\le T}\left|\widetilde{W}_n(t) - \frac{1}{\sqrt{m_n}} S_{\lfloor m
_n t\rfloor}\right| \leq \frac{1}{\sqrt{m_n}}\sum_{j=1}^{K}|X_{i_j(n)}|$$
for some bounded number of indices. By Chebyshev's inequality, this supremum con
verges to $0$ in probability (variance $\leq K/m_n \to 0$). By Slutsky's theorem
,
$$\widetilde{W}_n \xrightarrow{d} B \quad \text{in } D[0,T] \text{ for every } T
>0,$$
hence in $D[0,\infty)$.

**Step 3: Apply the continuous mapping theorem.** The scalar multiplication map
$$\Phi: D[0,T]\times \mathbb{R} \to D[0,T], \quad (x, c)\mapsto c\,x$$
is continuous in the product of the Skorokhod $J_1$ topology and the Euclidean t
opology. (Indeed, if $x_n\to x$ in $J_1$ via time changes $\lambda_n$ and $c_n\t
o c$, then $\sup_{t}|c_n x_n(\lambda_n(t))-cx(t)|\le |c_n|\sup_t|x_n(\lambda_n(t
))-x(t)|+|c_n-c|\sup_t|x(t)|\to 0$.)

Since $\widetilde{W}_n \Rightarrow B$ and $c_n \to 0$ (deterministic, hence $c_n
 \Rightarrow 0$), we have $(\widetilde{W}_n, c_n) \Rightarrow (B, 0)$. By the co
ntinuous mapping theorem:
$$Y_n = c_n\,\widetilde{W}_n = \Phi(\widetilde{W}_n, c_n) \xrightarrow{d} \Phi(B
, 0) = 0\cdot B = \mathbf{0},$$
where $\mathbf{0}$ denotes the zero process.

This holds in $D[0,T]$ for every $T>0$, and therefore in $D[0,\infty)$.

**Conclusion.** For $0 < a < \frac{1}{2}$, the convergence
$$\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n^a t} X_i\right)_t \xrightarrow{d} 0$$
holds. The key mechanism is that the Donsker scaling produces a Brownian motion
of order $n^{a/2}$, while the prefactor $1/\sqrt{n}$ shrinks it by $n^{(a-1)/2}\
to 0$ (since $a<1$), and in fact the condition $a<1$ suffices for the prefactor
to vanish; the sharper bound $a<1/2$ ensures the variance $n^{a-1}t\to 0$ at eac
h fixed $t$ at a rate faster than $n^{-1/2}$, but the process-level convergence
to zero holds for all $a<1$ by the argument above.
