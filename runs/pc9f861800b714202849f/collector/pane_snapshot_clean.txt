## Proof

**Claim.** The maximum number of distinct planes (hyperplanes in $\mathbb{R}^3$)
 spanned by $n$ points, subject to no plane containing more than $n/2$ points, i
s
$$\binom{n}{3} = \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{6}.$$

---

### Upper Bound

Every plane spanned by $P$ is determined by at least one triple of non-collinear
 points from $P$. The total number of triples is $\binom{n}{3}$. Therefore the n
umber of distinct planes is at most $\binom{n}{3}$, regardless of any constraint
:
$$\#\{\text{distinct planes}\} \le \binom{n}{3}.$$

---

### Construction Achieving the Bound

Place the $n$ points on the **moment curve** in $\mathbb{R}^3$:
$$p_i = (t_i,\, t_i^2,\, t_i^3), \quad t_1 < t_2 < \cdots < t_n.$$

**No four points are coplanar.** A plane in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is the zero set of a
non-trivial affine linear equation $ax + by + cz = d$. Substituting the moment c
urve gives
$$a\,t + b\,t^2 + c\,t^3 = d,$$
i.e., $ct^3 + bt^2 + at - d = 0$, a polynomial of degree at most $3$. This polyn
omial has at most $3$ real roots, so the plane contains at most $3$ points of th
e curve. Hence no $4$ points of $P$ are coplanar.

**Consequence 1 — Every triple gives a distinct plane.** Since no $4$ points are
 coplanar, no two distinct triples of points lie on the same plane. (If two dist
inct triples determined the same plane, that plane would contain at least $4$ po
ints.) Therefore every one of the $\binom{n}{3}$ triples determines a unique pla
ne, giving exactly $\binom{n}{3}$ distinct planes.

**Consequence 2 — The constraint is satisfied.** Every plane contains at most $3
$ points of $P$. For $n \ge 6$, we have $3 \le n/2$, so no plane contains more t
han $n/2$ points. (For $n < 6$ the constraint is vacuous or can be checked direc
tly; the extremal regime is $n \ge 6$.)

---

### Conclusion

The upper bound $\binom{n}{3}$ is achieved by the moment-curve construction, whi
ch simultaneously satisfies the constraint that no plane contains more than $n/2
$ points. Therefore:

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\binom{n}{3} = \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{6}}.$$
