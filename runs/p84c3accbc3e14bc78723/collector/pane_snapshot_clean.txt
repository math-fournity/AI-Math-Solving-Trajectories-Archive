## Setup: Pairwise Relations

Subtracting any two equations, say the first from the second:
$$-y^3 + x^3 = (z+w+x) - (y+z+w) = x - y$$
$$(x-y)(x^2+xy+y^2) = (x-y)$$
$$(x-y)(x^2+xy+y^2-1)=0$$

So for every pair $(a,b)$ from $\{x,y,z,w\}$: either $a = b$, or $a^2+ab+b^2 = 1
$.

## Case Analysis by Partition Structure

**Case 1: All four equal** ($x=y=z=w=a$). Then $-a^3 = 3a$, so $a(a^2+3)=0$, giv
ing $a=0$. **1 solution.**

**Case 2: Exactly three equal** ($x=y=z=a$, $w=b\neq a$). The equations give:
$$a^3 + 2a + b = 0, \quad b^3 + 3a = 0, \quad a^2+ab+b^2=1$$

From $b^3 = -3a$: $a = -b^3/3$. Substituting into $a^2+ab+b^2=1$ yields $b^6 - 3
b^4 + 9b^2 - 9 = 0$, i.e., with $v = b^2$:
$$v^3 - 3v^2 + 9v - 9 = 0$$

This cubic has $f'(v) = 3(v^2-2v+3) = 3((v-1)^2+2) > 0$, so it is strictly incre
asing with exactly one real root $v_0 \in (1,2)$ (since $f(1)=-2, f(2)=5$). This
 gives $b = \pm\sqrt{v_0}$ (2 choices), with $a$ determined and $a \neq b$ (sinc
e $a=b$ would force $a=0$, contradicting $b\neq 0$).

The odd variable can be in any of 4 positions. **$4 \times 2 = 8$ solutions.**

**Case 3a: Two pairs** ($x=y=a$, $z=w=b$, $a \neq b$). Equations give:
$$a^3+a+2b=0, \quad b^3+2a+b=0, \quad a^2+ab+b^2=1$$

From the constraint $a^2+ab+b^2=1$ with $b = -(a^3+a)/2$, setting $t = a^2$ yiel
ds $t^3+3t-4=0$, which factors as $(t-1)(t^2+t+4)=0$. Since $t^2+t+4$ has negati
ve discriminant, $t=1$, so $a = \pm 1$ and $b = \mp 1$.

There are 3 ways to partition 4 elements into 2 pairs, and 2 assignments of valu
es. **$3 \times 2 = 6$ solutions.**

**Case 3b: Two equal, other two distinct** ($x=y=a$, $z=b$, $w=c$, all distinct)
. From pairs $(x,z)$ and $(x,w)$: $a^2+ab+b^2 = a^2+ac+c^2$, giving $(b-c)(a+b+c
)=0$. Since $b \neq c$: $a+b+c=0$.

Equation 1 gives $-a^3 = a+b+c = 0$, so $a = 0$. Then $b^2 = 1$ and $c = -b$, gi
ving $(b,c) = (1,-1)$ or $(-1,1)$.

Verification: $(0,0,1,-1)$: Eq3 gives $-1 = -1+0+0 = -1$ ✓, Eq4 gives $1 = 0+0+1
 = 1$ ✓.

The equal pair can be chosen in $\binom{4}{2}=6$ ways, with 2 orderings for the
remaining. **$6 \times 2 = 12$ solutions.**

**Case 4: All four distinct.** From pairs $(x,y)$ and $(x,z)$: $(y-z)(x+y+z)=0$,
 so $x+y+z=0$. From pairs $(x,y)$ and $(x,w)$: $(y-w)(x+y+w)=0$, so $x+y+w=0$. T
hese give $z = w$, contradicting distinctness. **0 solutions.**
