## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. Every rearrangement of the series converges, and converges to th
e same limit $Lx$.

**Setup.** Fix $x \in H$ and let $v_k := \alpha_k \langle x, f_k \rangle f_k$. S
ince $\{f_k\}$ is an orthonormal system, the vectors $\{v_k\}$ are mutually orth
ogonal. We prove the series $\sum v_k$ converges unconditionally (i.e., every pe
rmutation converges to the same sum).

**Step 1: Square-summability of the norms.**

Since $\alpha_k \to 0$, the sequence $(\alpha_k)$ is bounded: there exists $M$ w
ith $|\alpha_k| \le M$ for all $k$. By Bessel's inequality applied to the orthon
ormal system $\{f_k\}$:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} |\langle x, f_k\rangle|^2 \le \|x\|^2 < \infty.$$

Therefore

$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \|v_k\|^2 = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \alpha_k^2\,|\langle x,f_k
\rangle|^2 \le M^2 \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\langle x,f_k\rangle|^2 \le M^2\|x\|^2 <
\infty. \tag{1}$$

**Step 2: The original series converges.**

For $m < n$, by orthogonality of the $v_k$:

$$\Bigl\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}v_k - \sum_{k=1}^{m}v_k\Bigr\|^2 = \Bigl\|\sum_{k=m+1}^{n
}v_k\Bigr\|^2 = \sum_{k=m+1}^{n}\|v_k\|^2 \xrightarrow[m,n\to\infty]{} 0$$

by (1). So the partial sums are Cauchy, hence $S := \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} v_k$ exi
sts in $H$ (and equals $Lx$ by the spectral theorem).

**Step 3: Every rearrangement converges to the same $S$.**

Let $\sigma:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}$ be any permutation, and set $T_n := \sum_{k
=1}^{n} v_{\sigma(k)}$. Let $A_n := \sigma(\{1,\dots,n\})$. Since $\sigma$ is a
bijection, $A_n$ has exactly $n$ elements, and

$$T_n - S = \sum_{k \in A_n} v_k - \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} v_k = -\sum_{k \notin A_n
} v_k.$$

(All terms indexed by $A_n$ cancel.) By orthogonality:

$$\|T_n - S\|^2 = \sum_{k \notin A_n}\|v_k\|^2. \tag{2}$$

Now let $\varepsilon > 0$. By (1), choose $N$ such that $\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\|
v_k\|^2 < \varepsilon^2$. Since $\{1,\dots,N\}$ is finite and $\sigma$ is a bije
ction, there exists $M$ such that $\{1,\dots,N\}\subseteq A_n$ for all $n \ge M$
. For such $n$, the complement $\mathbb{N}\setminus A_n \subseteq \{N+1, N+2, \d
ots\}$, so by (2):

$$\|T_n - S\|^2 = \sum_{k\notin A_n}\|v_k\|^2 \le \sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\|v_k\|^2
 < \varepsilon^2.$$

Hence $\|T_n - S\| < \varepsilon$ for all $n \ge M$, proving $T_n \to S = Lx$.

**Conclusion.** For every $x\in H$ and every permutation $\sigma$, the rearrange
d series $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{\sigma(k)}\langle x,f_{\sigma(k)}\rangle f_
{\sigma(k)}$ converges to $Lx$. The convergence is **unconditional**: no rearran
gement can change the sum or destroy convergence.

The essential reason is that the summands $\alpha_k\langle x,f_k\rangle f_k$ are
 *mutually orthogonal* and have *square-summable norms* (a consequence of Bessel
's inequality and boundedness of the eigenvalues). For orthogonal series in a Hi
lbert space, $\sum\|v_k\|^2<\infty$ guarantees unconditional convergence—a stric
tly weaker hypothesis than absolute convergence $\sum\|v_k\|<\infty$, which one
should not expect here (e.g., take $\alpha_k = 1/k$ and $x$ with $\langle x,f_k\
rangle = 1/k$, giving $\|v_k\| = 1/k^2$ square-summable but $\sum 1/k^2$ absolut
ely summable in this case; however for general data absolute summability of $\|v
_k\|$ need not hold, yet unconditional convergence still does).
