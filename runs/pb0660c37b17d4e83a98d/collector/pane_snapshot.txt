## Proof

**Factorization.** $2020 = 4 \cdot 5 \cdot 101$, with $4, 5, 101$ pairwise copri
me.

**CRT reduction.** For coprime $m_1, m_2$ with $n = m_1 m_2$, we claim $k$ is a
fan of $n$ iff $k \bmod m_1$ is a fan of $m_1$ and $k \bmod m_2$ is a fan of $m_
2$.

*Forward:* If $x^2+y^2+z^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{n}$ and $xyz \equiv k \pmod{n}$, reduc
ing mod $m_i$ gives the fan conditions for $m_i$.

*Backward:* Given solutions $(x_i, y_i, z_i)$ mod $m_i$ for $i=1,2$, use CRT to
find $x \equiv x_i, y \equiv y_i, z \equiv z_i \pmod{m_i}$. Then both congruence
s hold mod $m_1$ and mod $m_2$, hence mod $n$.

Therefore $f(n) = f(m_1)\,f(m_2)$, and by induction:
$$f(2020) = f(4)\cdot f(5)\cdot f(101).$$

**Computing $f(4)$.** Squares mod 4 are $\{0,1\}$. We need $x^2+y^2+z^2 \equiv 0
 \pmod 4$ with each term in $\{0,1\}$, so the sum (an integer in $\{0,1,2,3\}$)
must equal $0$. Thus $x^2\equiv y^2\equiv z^2\equiv 0\pmod 4$, meaning $x,y,z$ a
re all even. Then $xyz$ is divisible by $8$, so $xyz \equiv 0 \pmod 4$. Only $k=
0$ is a fan.

$$f(4) = 1.$$

---

**Computing $f(5)$.** Squares mod 5 are $\{0,1,4\}$. We need three values from $
\{0,1,4\}$ summing to $0 \bmod 5$. Checking all multisets:
- $\{0,0,0\}$: sum $0$. ✓
- $\{0,1,4\}$: sum $5\equiv 0$. ✓
- $\{1,1,1\}$: sum $3$. ✗
- $\{1,1,4\}$: sum $6\equiv 1$. ✗
- $\{1,4,4\}$: sum $9\equiv 4$. ✗
- $\{4,4,4\}$: sum $12\equiv 2$. ✗

In every valid case, at least one of $x,y,z$ is $\equiv 0 \pmod 5$, so $xyz \equ
iv 0 \pmod 5$. Only $k=0$ is a fan.

$$f(5) = 1.$$

**Computing $f(101)$.** Here $p = 101$ is prime with $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, so $\
left(\frac{-1}{p}\right) = 1$.

*$k = 0$ is a fan:* Take $x = y = z = 0$.

*Existence of an all-nonzero solution:* Set $x = 1$; we need $y^2 + z^2 \equiv -
1 \pmod{101}$ with $y, z \not\equiv 0$. The number of pairs $(y,z)$ with $y^2+z^
2 \equiv a \pmod p$ for $a \not\equiv 0$ is $p - \left(\frac{-1}{p}\right) = 101

nzero exists.

, 100) = 1$. Hence for any target $k \not\equiv 0$, choosing $t$ with $t^3 \equi
v k/k_0$ yields $xyz \equiv k$.

So every $k \in \{0, 1, \dots, 100\}$ is a fan:

$$f(101) = 101.$$
