## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. If $f: A \to B$ is a bijective holomorphic map between Riemann s
urfaces, then $f^{-1}: B \to A$ is holomorphic.

---

**Step 1: Reduction to the local question.**

Let $b \in B$ be arbitrary and set $a := f^{-1}(b) \in A$. To show $f^{-1}$ is h
olomorphic, it suffices to show it is holomorphic in a neighborhood of each $b \
in B$. Choose local coordinates $z$ on $A$ centered at $a$ (so $z(a) = 0$) and $
w$ on $B$ centered at $b$ (so $w(b) = 0$). In these coordinates, $f$ is represen
ted by a holomorphic function $g$ defined near $z = 0$ with $g(0) = 0$. We must
show $g'(0) \neq 0$, for then the holomorphic inverse function theorem gives tha
t $g^{-1}$ (hence $f^{-1}$) is holomorphic near $b$.

**Step 2: The local normal form.**

Let $m \geq 1$ be the order of vanishing of $g$ at $0$, i.e., $g(z) = z^m h(z)$
where $h$ is holomorphic near $0$ and $h(0) \neq 0$. By choosing a local holomor
phic $m$-th root of $h$ (which exists since $h(0) \neq 0$ and we work in a simpl
y connected neighborhood), there is a biholomorphic change of local coordinate $
\varphi$ with $\varphi(0) = 0$, $\varphi'(0) \neq 0$, such that

$$g(\varphi^{-1}(\zeta)) = \zeta^m$$

in a neighborhood of $\zeta = 0$.

**Step 3: Injectivity forces $m = 1$.**

Since $f$ is bijective, it is in particular injective, so $g$ is injective in a
neighborhood of $0$. Suppose for contradiction that $m \geq 2$. Then for any sma
ll $\varepsilon \neq 0$, the equation $\zeta^m = \varepsilon$ has $m$ distinct s
olutions $\zeta_1, \dots, \zeta_m$ near $0$. Pulling back via $\varphi^{-1}$, th
e equation $g(z) = \varepsilon$ has $m \geq 2$ distinct solutions near $z = 0$.
This contradicts the injectivity of $g$ (hence of $f$) near $a$.

Therefore $m = 1$, which means $g'(0) \neq 0$.

**Step 4: Apply the holomorphic inverse function theorem.**

Since $g$ is holomorphic near $0$ with $g(0) = 0$ and $g'(0) \neq 0$, the holomo
rphic inverse function theorem provides a neighborhood $V$ of $w = 0$ and a holo
morphic function $g^{-1}: V \to \mathbb{C}$ with $g^{-1}(0) = 0$ and $g(g^{-1}(w
)) = w$ for all $w \in V$. In terms of the Riemann surfaces, this means $f^{-1}$
 is holomorphic in a neighborhood of $b$.

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

Since $b \in B$ was arbitrary, $f^{-1}$ is holomorphic at every point of $B$. Th
e trivial case where $A$ (and hence $B$) is a single point is immediate. Therefo
re $f^{-1}: B \to A$ is holomorphic. $\blacksquare$
