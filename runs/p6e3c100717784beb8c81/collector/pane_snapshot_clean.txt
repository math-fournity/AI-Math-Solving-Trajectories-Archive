## Solution

**Setting up the agreement.** Let $g(x) = \dfrac{1}{1+2x^3}$. Substituting $x =
1/n$ (so $n = 1/x$):

$$g\!\left(\tfrac{1}{n}\right) = \frac{1}{1+2/n^3} = \frac{n^3}{n^3+2} = f\!\lef
t(\tfrac{1}{n}\right).$$

So $h(x) := f(x) - g(x)$ vanishes at $x = 1/n$ for every positive integer $n$, a
nd $1/n \to 0$.

**All derivatives of $h$ vanish at $0$.** Since $f$ is $C^\infty$ and $g$ is $C^
\infty$ near $0$, so is $h$. A standard result: if a $C^\infty$ function vanishe
s on a sequence of distinct points converging to an interior point $a$, then $h^
{(k)}(a)=0$ for all $k$. (Proof by induction: $h(a)=0$ by continuity; given $h^{
(k)}$ vanishes on a sequence $\to a$, Rolle's theorem yields $h^{(k+1)}$ vanishe
s on a sequence $\to a$, hence $h^{(k+1)}(a)=0$.)

Therefore $f^{(k)}(0) = g^{(k)}(0)$ for all $k$.

**Computing $g^{(4)}(0)$.** Expand $g$ as a power series about $0$:

$$g(x) = (1+2x^3)^{-1} = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty} (-1)^m\, 2^m\, x^{3m} = 1 - 2x^3 +
4x^6 - 8x^9 + \cdots$$

The powers that appear are $0, 3, 6, 9, \dots$ — all multiples of $3$. In partic
ular, the coefficient of $x^4$ is $0$. Since

$$g^{(4)}(0) = 4!\cdot [x^4]\,g(x) = 24 \cdot 0 = 0,$$

we conclude

$$\boxed{f^{(4)}(0) = 0}.$$
