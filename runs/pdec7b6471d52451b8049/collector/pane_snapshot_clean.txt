Proof

Claim. Yes. Such an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space exists; in fact the
property holds in every metric space (hence in every Hilbert space), so any
infinite-dimensional Hilbert space — for instance $X=\ell^{2}(\mathbb N)$ — is
an example.

Key fact. In any metric space, the range of a convergent sequence is relatively
compact (its closure is compact).

Proof of the key fact. Let $(M,d)$ be a metric space and let $(x_n)_{n\ge 1}$ be
a sequence in $M$ converging to $L\in M$. Set $S={x_n:n\ge 1}$. We show $
\overline S$ is compact by verifying sequential compactness (equivalent to
compactness in metric spaces).

Take any sequence $(y_k)$ in $\overline S$. We must extract a convergent
subsequence with limit in $\overline S$.

  • If infinitely many $y_k$ are equal to some fixed point $p\in\overline S$,
  the constant subsequence converges to $p\in\overline S$.
  • Otherwise, discarding repetitions, we may assume each $y_k\in S$, say
  $y_k=x_{n_k}$. For each $k$ pick $x_{m_k}$ with $d(x_{m_k},y_k)<1/k$ (possible
  since $y_k\in\overline S$). Then $d(x_{m_k},L)\le d(x_{m_k},y_k)+d(y_k,L)$.
    • If $(m_k)$ is unbounded, pass to a subsequence with $m_k\to\infty$; then
    $x_{m_k}\to L$ (since $x_n\to L$), and $d(y_k,L)\le
    d(y_k,x_{m_k})+d(x_{m_k},L)\to 0$, so $y_k\to L\in\overline S$.
    • If $(m_k)$ is bounded, some value $m_$ occurs infinitely often; the
    corresponding subsequence of $y_k$ converges to $x_{m_}\in
    S\subseteq\overline S$.

In every case a convergent subsequence with limit in $\overline S$ exists. Hence
$\overline S$ is compact, i.e. $S$ is relatively compact. ∎

Application. A Hilbert space is in particular a metric space (with $d(x,y)=|x-y|
$). Therefore every convergent sequence in any Hilbert space is relatively
compact. Taking $X=\ell^{2}(\mathbb N)$, which is infinite-dimensional (the
standard unit vectors $e_n$ are mutually orthogonal and linearly independent),
gives an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space satisfying the required property.

Conclusion. Such a space exists; $\ell^{2}(\mathbb N)$ is an explicit example.
The property is in fact automatic in every metric space, so the infinite-
dimensionality hypothesis is not an obstruction.
