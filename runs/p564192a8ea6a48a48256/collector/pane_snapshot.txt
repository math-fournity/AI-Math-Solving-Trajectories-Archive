## Proof

**Notation.** Each team plays \(n-1\) games. For team \(i\): \(w_i\) wins, \(\el
l_i\) losses, \(t_i\) ties, with \(w_i+\ell_i+t_i=n-1\), and points \(p_i=3w_i+t
_i\). Let \(A\) be the (unique) team with strictly the most points and strictly
the fewest wins. Write \(a=w_A,\ \ell=\ell_A\), so
\[
p_A=3a+t_A=3a+(n-1-a-\ell)=n-1+2a-\ell.
\]
Every other team has at least \(a+1\) wins and at most \(p_A-1\) points.

Let \(G=\frac{n(n-1)}2\) be the total number of games, \(T\) the number of tied
games, and \(W=G-T\) the number of decisive games (each contributing one win).

**Three bounds on \(T\).**

*Wins.* The total number of wins is \(W=G-T\). Team \(A\) owns \(a\) of them, an
d the other \(n-1\) teams own at least \((n-1)(a+1)\). Hence
\[
G-T-a\ge (n-1)(a+1)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad T\le G-a-(n-1)(a+1). \tag{1}
\]

*Points.* Total points equal \(3W+2T=3G-T\). Team \(A\) has \(p_A\); the others
have at most \((n-1)(p_A-1)\) in total. Thus
\[
3G-T-p_A\le (n-1)(p_A-1)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad T\ge 3G-n\,p_A+(n-1). \tag{2}
\]

*Ties involving \(A\).* The \(\,t_A=n-1-a-\ell\,\) games that \(A\) tied are dis
tinct tied games, so
\[
T\ge n-1-a-\ell. \tag{3}
\]

**Deriving \(a\ge \ell+2\).** Combining (2) \(\le\) (1):
\[
3G-n\,p_A+(n-1)\le G-a-(n-1)(a+1),
\]
\[
n\,p_A\ge 2G+(n-1)(a+2)+a=n(n-1)+na+2n-2=n(n+1+a)-2.
\]
So \(p_A\ge n+1+a-\tfrac2n\). For \(n\ge 3\) this lies strictly between \(n+a\)
and \(n+a+1\), hence (being an integer)
\[
p_A\ge n+a+1.
\]
But \(p_A=n-1+2a-\ell\le n-1+2a\), so \(n-1+2a-\ell\ge n+a+1\), i.e.
\[
a\ge \ell+2. \tag{4}
\]

**Deriving \(a\le \tfrac n2-2+\tfrac{\ell}{n-1}\).** Combining (3) with (1):
\[
n-1-a-\ell\le G-a-(n-1)(a+1)
\;\Longrightarrow\;
n-1-\ell\le (n-1)\Bigl(\tfrac n2-a-1\Bigr),
\]
\[
a\le \frac n2-2+\frac{\ell}{n-1}. \tag{5}
\]

**Lower bound \(n\ge 8\).** From (4) and (5),
\[
\ell+2\le \frac n2-2+\frac{\ell}{n-1}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\ell\cdot\frac{n-2}{n-1}\le \frac n2-4. \tag{6}
\]
For \(3\le n\le 7\), the right side is \(\le \tfrac72-4=-\tfrac12<0\), while the
 left side is \(\ge 0\) (since \(\ell\ge0\) and \(\tfrac{n-2}{n-1}>0\)): contrad
iction. For \(n\le 2\) the scenario is trivially impossible (a single game canno
t make one team simultaneously the unique points-leader and the unique win-minim
izer). Therefore
\[
n\ge 8.
\]

**Construction for \(n=8\).** Take teams \(A,T_0,T_1,\dots,T_6\).

- \(A\) beats \(T_0,T_1\) and ties \(T_2,T_3,T_4,T_5,T_6\).
- Among \(T_0,\dots,T_6\) play a *regular tournament* (no ties): \(T_i\) beats \
(T_j\) iff \((j-i)\bmod 7\in\{1,2,3\}\). Each \(T_i\) thus has exactly \(3\) win
s and \(3\) losses within this subtournament (a regular tournament on \(7\) vert
ices exists since \(7\) is odd, with out-degree \((7-1)/2=3\)).

Records and points:

| team | wins | losses | ties | points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| \(A\) | \(2\) | \(0\) | \(5\) | \(6+5=11\) |
| \(T_0,T_1\) | \(3\) | \(4\) | \(0\) | \(9\) |
| \(T_2,\dots,T_6\) | \(3\) | \(3\) | \(1\) | \(9+1=10\) |

Team \(A\) has \(11\) points — strictly more than every other team (\(10\) or \(
9\)) — and \(2\) wins — strictly fewer than every other team (each has \(3\)). T
hus \(A\) is simultaneously the unique points-leader and the unique team with th
e fewest wins.

**Conclusion.** The scenario is impossible for \(n\le 7\) and possible for \(n=8
\). The smallest value is

\[
\boxed{n=8}.
\]
