## Definition

Let $\cdots \to X_3 \xrightarrow{f_3} X_2 \xrightarrow{f_2} X_1 \xrightarrow{f_1
} X_0$ be an inverse system (cofiltered diagram) in the category **Top** of topo
logical spaces, indexed by the directed set $\mathbb{N}$ with the usual order (s
o a morphism $f_{ij}: X_i \to X_j$ exists whenever $i \geq j$, given by the comp
osite $f_{j+1}\circ\cdots\circ f_i$, and satisfying $f_{ii}=\mathrm{id}$, $f_{jk
}\circ f_{ij}=f_{ik}$ for $i\geq j\geq k$).

A **cone** over this system is a topological space $Y$ together with continuous
maps $\pi_i: Y \to X_i$ for every $i\in\mathbb{N}$ such that the diagrams commut
e:
$$f_{ij}\circ \pi_i = \pi_j \qquad \text{for all } i\geq j.$$

The **limit** (inverse limit, projective limit) of the system is a cone $(L,\,p_
i:L\to X_i)$ that is universal: for any cone $(Y,\pi_i)$ there exists a unique c
ontinuous map $u:Y\to L$ with $p_i\circ u = \pi_i$ for all $i$.

Equivalently, $L$ represents the functor that sends a space $Y$ to the set of al
l compatible families $(\pi_i)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}$ of maps $Y\to X_i$.

## Construction

Let $P = \prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}} X_i$ be the product space, equipped with the pro
duct topology, and let $\mathrm{pr}_i:P\to X_i$ be the canonical projections. De
fine
$$L \;=\; \bigl\{\, x=(x_i)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in P \;\big|\; f_{i+1}(x_{i+1})=x_i
 \text{ for all } i\in\mathbb{N}\,\bigr\}\;\subseteq\; P.$$

Equivalently, $L = \bigcap_{i\in\mathbb{N}} f_{i+1}^{-1}(\mathrm{pr}_i)$... more
 precisely, $L$ is the equalizer condition expressed as
$$L = \bigcap_{i\in\mathbb{N}} \bigl\{x\in P \mid f_{i+1}(\mathrm{pr}_{i+1}(x))
= \mathrm{pr}_i(x)\bigr\}.$$

**Topology on $L$.** We give $L$ the subspace topology inherited from $P$. Let $
p_i := \mathrm{pr}_i\big|_L : L\to X_i$ be the restriction of the projection.

## Verification

### (a) $L$ is a cone.

We must show each $p_i$ is continuous and that $f_{i+1}\circ p_{i+1}=p_i$ for al
l $i$.

- *Continuity:* $p_i$ is the composite of the inclusion $\iota:L\hookrightarrow
P$ (continuous by definition of subspace topology) and the projection $\mathrm{p
r}_i:P\to X_i$ (continuous by definition of product topology). Hence $p_i$ is co
ntinuous.

- *Compatibility:* For $x=(x_i)\in L$ we have by definition $f_{i+1}(x_{i+1})=x_
i$, i.e. $f_{i+1}(p_{i+1}(x))=p_i(x)$. Thus $f_{i+1}\circ p_{i+1}=p_i$. By induc
tion, $f_{ij}\circ p_i = p_j$ for all $i\geq j$. So $(L,p_i)$ is a cone.

### (b) $L$ is non-empty when each $X_i$ is non-empty (optional remark; not need
ed for the universal property).

*(This is only automatic under additional hypotheses such as compactness; we do
not require it for the universal property, which holds regardless.)*

### (c) Universality.

Let $(Y,\pi_i)$ be any cone, so $\pi_i:Y\to X_i$ are continuous and $f_{i+1}\cir
c\pi_{i+1}=\pi_i$ for all $i$. Define
$$u:Y\to P,\qquad u(y)=\bigl(\pi_i(y)\bigr)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}.$$

- *$u$ is well-defined as a map into $P$:* each component $\pi_i$ is a map $Y\to
 X_i$, so $u(y)\in\prod X_i=P$.

- *$u$ is continuous:* By the universal property of the product topology, a map
into $P$ is continuous iff every coordinate $\mathrm{pr}_i\circ u = \pi_i$ is co
ntinuous, which holds by assumption.

- *$u$ lands in $L$:* For $y\in Y$,
$$f_{i+1}\bigl(\mathrm{pr}_{i+1}(u(y))\bigr)=f_{i+1}(\pi_{i+1}(y))=\pi_i(y)=\mat
hrm{pr}_i(u(y)),$$
where the middle equality uses the cone compatibility of $(Y,\pi_i)$. Hence $u(y
)$ satisfies the defining equations of $L$, so $u(y)\in L$. Thus $u$ corestricts
 to a continuous map $u:Y\to L$ (continuous because $\iota:L\hookrightarrow P$ i
s an embedding, i.e. $L$ carries the subspace topology, and a map into a subspac
e is continuous iff the composite with the inclusion is continuous).

- *$p_i\circ u = \pi_i$:* $p_i(u(y)) = \mathrm{pr}_i(u(y)) = \pi_i(y)$ for all $
y\in Y$.

- *Uniqueness:* Suppose $v:Y\to L$ also satisfies $p_i\circ v=\pi_i$ for all $i$
. Then $\mathrm{pr}_i(\iota\circ v)=\pi_i=\mathrm{pr}_i(\iota\circ u)$ for every
 $i$. Two points of a product with equal coordinates are equal, so $\iota\circ v
=\iota\circ u$; since $\iota$ is injective, $v=u$.

### (d) Conclusion.

We have produced a cone $(L,p_i)$ with $L\subseteq\prod X_i$ carrying the subspa
ce topology, and shown it satisfies the universal property of the limit. Therefo
re the limit exists in **Top** and is realized by this explicit construction. $\
blacksquare$
