 an open 2-cell. Removing a single point from an open 2-cell yields a path-conne
cted space (a punctured plane or punctured disk). Since path-connectedness is a
topological invariant, $X \setminus \{0\}$ is path-connected.

**Step 2: There exists a simple polygonal arc from $p$ to $q$ in $X \setminus \{
0\}$.**

The set $X \setminus \{0\}$ is open (as $X \cap (\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\})$)
 and path-connected. A standard result states that any open, path-connected subs

 polygonal path. Any polygonal path can be simplified to a simple (injective) po
lygonal arc by removing loops. Thus there exists a simple polygonal arc $\gamma:
 [0,1] \to X \setminus \{0\}$ with $\gamma(0) = p$, $\gamma(1) = q$.
