## Solution

**Parametrization of the parabola.** Write the parabola as $\gamma(t)=(t,\,at^{2
})$. Its tangent vector is $\gamma'(t)=(1,2at)$, with length $R(t):=\sqrt{1+4a^{
2}t^{2}}$. The unit normal (oriented toward the concave side) is

$$\hat n(t)=\frac{(-2at,\;1)}{R(t)}.$$

**Construction of the offset curve.** The curve at constant signed distance $d$
along the common normal is

$$\boxed{\;\Gamma(t)=\gamma(t)+d\,\hat n(t)=\left(t-\frac{2atd}{\sqrt{1+4a^{2}t^
{2}}},\;\;at^{2}+\frac{d}{\sqrt{1+4a^{2}t^{2}}}\right),\qquad t\in\mathbb R.\;}$
$

The branch on the opposite side of the parabola is obtained by replacing $d$ wit
h $-d$.

---

**Verification of the orthogonality condition.** By construction the segment joi
ning $\gamma(t)$ and $\Gamma(t)$ is $d\,\hat n(t)$, which is orthogonal to $\gam
ma'(t)=(1,2at)$. It remains to show that the tangent of the offset curve is **pa
rallel** to the tangent of the parabola, so that the same segment is also orthog
onal to the offset's tangent.

Using $\dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{t}{R}=\dfrac{1}{R^{3}}$ and $\dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{1}{R
}=-\dfrac{4a^{2}t}{R^{3}}$:

$$\Gamma'_{x}=1-\frac{2ad}{R^{3}},\qquad \Gamma'_{y}=2at-\frac{4a^{2}td}{R^{3}}=
2at\!\left(1-\frac{2ad}{R^{3}}\right).$$

Hence

$$\Gamma'(t)=\left(1-\frac{2ad}{R^{3}}\right)(1,\,2at),$$

which is a scalar multiple of $\gamma'(t)=(1,2at)$. Therefore the connecting seg
ment $d\,\hat n(t)$ is orthogonal to **both** tangents. $\checkmark$

**Constant-distance check.** $|\Gamma(t)-\gamma(t)|=|d\,\hat n(t)|=|d|$ for ever
y $t$. $\checkmark$

---

**Implicit form (elimination of the parameter).** A point $(x,y)$ lies on the of
fset iff it is on the normal to the parabola at some foot point $(t,at^{2})$ at
distance $d$. The normal condition $(x,y)=(t,at^{2})+s(-2at,1)$ yields, after el
iminating $s$,

$$2a^{2}t^{3}+(1-2ay)\,t-x=0. \tag{N}$$

The distance condition $(x-t)^{2}+(y-at^{2})^{2}=d^{2}$, reduced via (N), gives

$$(2ay-1)\,t^{2}+3xt-2\bigl(x^{2}+y^{2}-d^{2}\bigr)=0. \tag{Q}$$

Eliminating $t$ between the cubic (N) and the quadratic (Q) produces the implici
t equation of the offset. Setting $S=x^{2}+y^{2}-d^{2}$, $m=2ay-1$, $N=m^{2}+12a
^{2}S$, $D=4a^{2}Sm+18a^{2}x^{2}-m^{3}$, the resultant is

$$m\,x^{2}N^{2}+3x^{2}ND-2SD^{2}=0.$$

After removing the extraneous degree‑$2$ factor introduced by the squaring inher
ent in the elimination, the genuine offset is an algebraic **sextic** (degree $6
$) in $(x,y)$—consistent with the classical result that the parallel curve of a
parabola is a sextic, of degree $8$ for a general conic but reduced by the parab
ola's tangency to the line at infinity.

---

**Consistency checks.**

- **Degenerate case $a\to 0$:** the parabola becomes the $x$-axis; the offset re
duces to $y=\pm d$, two parallel lines. $\checkmark$
- **Cusp / radius of curvature:** the offset tangent vanishes when $\Gamma'_{x}=
0$, i.e. $R^{3}=2ad$; at $t=0$ this gives $d=\frac{1}{2a}$, precisely the radius
 of curvature of $y=ax^{2}$ at its vertex. The inner branch develops a cusp at t
his critical distance, exactly as expected. $\checkmark$
- **Vertex point:** at $t=0$ the offset passes through $(0,d)$; the tangent ther
e is $(1-2ad,\,0)$, horizontal, and the connecting segment $(0,0)\to(0,d)$ is ve
rtical—orthogonal to both horizontal tangents. $\checkmark$

---

**Conclusion.** The curve at constant orthogonal distance $d$ from the parabola
$y=ax^{2}$ is the parallel (offset) curve

$$\boxed{\;x=t-\frac{2atd}{\sqrt{1+4a^{2}t^{2}}},\qquad y=at^{2}+\frac{d}{\sqrt{
1+4a^{2}t^{2}}}\;}$$

(with the opposite-side branch given by $d\mapsto -d$). The connecting segment b
etween corresponding points is normal to—hence orthogonal to the tangents of—bot
h curves, the inter-curve distance is identically $|d|$, and eliminating the par
ameter $t$ yields a degree‑$6$ algebraic equation in $(x,y)$.
