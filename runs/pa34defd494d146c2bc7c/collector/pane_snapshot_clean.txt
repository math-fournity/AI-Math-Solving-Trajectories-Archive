## Proof

**Problem.** Find the mathematical condition under which mixed second-order cova
riant derivatives commute: $\nabla_i \nabla_j = \nabla_j \nabla_i$ for $i \neq j
$ (using the semicolon notation $\partial_{;i} \equiv \nabla_i$).

---

### Setup

Let $\nabla$ be an affine connection with Christoffel symbols $\Gamma^{\lambda}_
{\mu\nu}$ on a manifold. We define:

- **Torsion tensor:** $T^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\nu} = \Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu} - \Gam
ma^{\lambda}_{\nu\mu}$
- **Riemann curvature tensor:** $R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu \Gamma
^{\rho}_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu \Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^{\rho}_{\m
u\lambda}\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^{\lambd
a}_{\mu\sigma}$

---

### Step 1: Commutator on a scalar field

For a scalar field $\phi$, the first covariant derivative reduces to a partial d
erivative: $\nabla_\mu \phi = \partial_\mu \phi$. The second covariant derivativ
e is:

$$\nabla_\nu \nabla_\mu \phi = \partial_\nu \partial_\mu \phi - \Gamma^{\lambda}
_{\nu\mu}\,\partial_\lambda \phi$$

Taking the commutator:

$$[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]\phi = \nabla_\mu \nabla_\nu \phi - \nabla_\nu \nabla_
\mu \phi = \left(\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\nu\mu} - \Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}\right)\pa
rtial_\lambda \phi = -T^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\nu}\,\nabla_\lambda \phi$$

**For scalars, commutativity requires $T^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\nu} = 0$ (torsion-free
).**

---

### Step 2: Commutator on a vector field (the Ricci identity)

For a contravariant vector field $V^{\rho}$, a direct computation gives the **Ri
cci identity**:

$$[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]V^{\rho} = R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\,V^{\sigma} - T^{
\lambda}{}_{\mu\nu}\,\nabla_\lambda V^{\rho}$$

For this to vanish for **all** vector fields $V^{\rho}$, both terms must vanish
independently (since $V^{\sigma}$ and $\nabla_\lambda V^{\rho}$ can be chosen in
dependently at a point):

1. $R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = 0$ — the Riemann curvature tensor vanishes (the
connection is **flat**).
2. $T^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\nu} = 0$ — the torsion tensor vanishes (the connection is
 **torsion-free**).

---

### Step 3: General tensor fields

For a general tensor field of type $(r,s)$, the commutator $[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_
\nu]$ produces terms involving $R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ contracted with each
index of the tensor, plus the torsion term $-T^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\nu}\nabla_\lambd
a$. The curvature terms arise from the non-commutativity of parallel transport a
round an infinitesimal loop, and the torsion term from the antisymmetric part of
 the connection. For the operator equality $\nabla_i \nabla_j = \nabla_j \nabla_
i$ to hold on **all** tensor fields, both conditions must hold simultaneously.

---

### Step 4: Geometric interpretation

- **$T^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\nu} = 0$** ensures the connection is symmetric: $\Gamma^
{\lambda}_{\mu\nu} = \Gamma^{\lambda}_{\nu\mu}$. This is the condition for the s
econd covariant derivative of a scalar to be symmetric.

- **$R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = 0$** ensures the connection is **flat** — paral
lel transport is path-independent, and there exist local coordinates in which $\
Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu} = 0$ everywhere (Riemann normal coordinates become glob
al). This is the **integrability condition** for the system $\nabla_\mu V^{\rho}
 = 0$ to have a full set of solutions.

When both hold, the connection is a **flat, torsion-free affine connection**, an
d covariant derivatives reduce to ordinary partial derivatives in suitable globa
l coordinates, which trivially commute.

---

### Special case: Levi-Civita connection

In standard general relativity, the connection is the **Levi-Civita connection**
, which is torsion-free by construction ($T^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\nu} \equiv 0$). In
this case, the Ricci identity reduces to:

$$[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]V^{\rho} = R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\,V^{\sigma}$$

and the **sole** condition for commutativity is:

$$\boxed{R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = 0}$$

i.e., **the Riemann curvature tensor must vanish** (the spacetime is flat).

---

### Conclusion

The mixed second-order covariant derivatives $\partial_{;i}\partial_{;j}$ and $\
partial_{;j}\partial_{;i}$ are equal ($i \neq j$) if and only if:

1. **The torsion tensor vanishes:** $T^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\nu} = 0$, and
2. **The Riemann curvature tensor vanishes:** $R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = 0$.

For the standard torsion-free (Levi-Civita) connection, condition (1) is automat
ic, and the **required condition reduces to the vanishing of the Riemann curvatu
re tensor**: $R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = 0$.
