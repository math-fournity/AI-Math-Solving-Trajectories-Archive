$$i_a: S \to S \times S, \qquad i_a(b) = (a, b).$$

We show $i_a$ is continuous. Recall that a map into a product space is continuou
s if and only if each of its coordinate projections is continuous (this is the u
niversal property / characteristic property of the product topology).

- The first coordinate of $i_a$ is the constant map $c_a: S \to S$, $c_a(b) = a$
. A constant map is always continuous, since the preimage of any open set $U \su
bseteq S$ is either $\emptyset$ (if $a \notin U$) or all of $S$ (if $a \in U$),
both open.
- The second coordinate of $i_a$ is the identity map $\mathrm{id}_S: S \to S$, $
b \mapsto b$, which is trivially continuous.

Since both coordinate maps are continuous, $i_a$ is continuous by the characteri
stic property of the product topology.

Now observe that
$$f_a = f \circ i_a,$$

f: S \times S \to S$ (continuous by hypothesis). A composition of continuous map
