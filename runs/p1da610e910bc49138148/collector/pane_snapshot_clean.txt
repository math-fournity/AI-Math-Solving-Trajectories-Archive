## Solution

**Setup.** Place $O=(0,0)$, $B=(b,0)$ on the positive $x$-axis, and $A=(a,c)$ in
 the first quadrant ($a,c>0$). The condition $AO=AB$ gives

$$a^2+c^2=(a-b)^2+c^2 \implies a^2=a^2-2ab+b^2 \implies b=2a.$$

So $B=(2a,0)$, and the area of $\triangle OAB$ is $\tfrac12\cdot 2a\cdot c = ac
= s$, hence $c=s/a$.

The triangle is bounded by:
- $OA$: $y=\frac{s}{a^2}x$ for $0\le x\le a$,
- $AB$: $y=\frac{s}{a^2}(2a-x)$ for $a\le x\le 2a$,
- $OB$: $y=0$.

**Maximum of $xy$.** On $OA$: $xy=\frac{s}{a^2}x^2$, maximized at $x=a$ giving $
xy=s$. On $AB$: $xy=\frac{s}{a^2}(2a-x)x$, maximized at $x=a$ giving $xy=s$. So
$\max xy = s$ at vertex $A$.

- **If $0<s\le 1$:** the entire triangle satisfies $xy\le 1$, so the regulated a
rea is $\boxed{s}$.

- **If $s>1$:** the hyperbola $xy=1$ (i.e., $y=1/x$) cuts through the triangle.
We compute the area where $xy>1$ and subtract from $s$.

**Intersection points.** With $OA$: $\frac{s}{a^2}x=\frac1x \Rightarrow x=\frac{
a}{\sqrt{s}}$. With $AB$: $\frac{s}{a^2}(2a-x)=\frac1x \Rightarrow sx^2-2asx+a^2
=0$, giving $x=a\!\left(1\pm\frac{\sqrt{s-1}}{\sqrt{s}}\right)$. Only $x_2=a\!\l
eft(1+\frac{\sqrt{s-1}}{\sqrt{s}}\right)$ lies in $[a,2a]$.

**Area where $xy>1$.** Setting $t=\frac{\sqrt{s-1}}{\sqrt{s}}$:

$$\mathcal{A}_{>1}=\int_{a/\sqrt{s}}^{a}\!\left[\frac{s}{a^2}x-\frac1x\right]dx+
\int_{a}^{a(1+t)}\!\left[\frac{s}{a^2}(2a-x)-\frac1x\right]dx.$$

**First integral:**

$$\left[\frac{s}{2a^2}x^2-\ln x\right]_{a/\sqrt{s}}^{a}=\frac{s}{2}-\ln a-\frac1
2+\ln a-\frac12\ln s=\frac{s-1}{2}-\frac12\ln s.$$

**Second integral:**

$$\left[\frac{s}{a^2}\!\left(2ax-\frac{x^2}{2}\right)-\ln x\right]_{a}^{a(1+t)}.
$$

At $x=a(1+t)$: $\frac{s}{a^2}\!\left(2a^2(1+t)-\frac{a^2(1+t)^2}{2}\right)=\frac
{s(3+2t-t^2)}{2}$, and $\ln(a(1+t))=\ln a+\ln(1+t)$.

At $x=a$: $\frac{s}{a^2}\cdot\frac{3a^2}{2}=\frac{3s}{2}$, and $\ln a$.

Subtracting:

$$\frac{s(2t-t^2)}{2}-\ln(1+t)=\frac{s\,t(2-t)}{2}-\ln(1+t).$$

Since $t=\frac{\sqrt{s-1}}{\sqrt{s}}$ and $t^2=\frac{s-1}{s}$:

$$\frac{s(2t-t^2)}{2}=\sqrt{s(s-1)}-\frac{s-1}{2},$$

$$\ln(1+t)=\ln\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{s}+\sqrt{s-1}}{\sqrt{s}}\right)=\ln(\sqrt{s}+\
sqrt{s-1})-\frac12\ln s.$$

So the second integral equals:

$$\sqrt{s(s-1)}-\frac{s-1}{2}-\ln(\sqrt{s}+\sqrt{s-1})+\frac12\ln s.$$

**Total area where $xy>1$:**

$$\left[\frac{s-1}{2}-\frac12\ln s\right]+\left[\sqrt{s(s-1)}-\frac{s-1}{2}-\ln(
\sqrt{s}+\sqrt{s-1})+\frac12\ln s\right]$$

$$=\sqrt{s(s-1)}-\ln\!\left(\sqrt{s}+\sqrt{s-1}\right).$$

(The $\frac{s-1}{2}$ and $\frac12\ln s$ terms cancel.)

**Regulated area ($xy\le 1$):**

$$\boxed{s-\sqrt{s(s-1)}+\ln\!\left(\sqrt{s}+\sqrt{s-1}\right)},\qquad s\ge 1.$$

**Verification.** At $s=1$: $\sqrt{0}-\ln(1)=0$, giving area $1=s$. ✓ As $s\to\i
nfty$: $\sqrt{s(s-1)}\approx s-\frac12$, $\ln(\sqrt{s}+\sqrt{s-1})\approx\ln(2\s
qrt{s})$, so area $\approx\frac12+\ln 2+\frac12\ln s$, growing slowly as the reg
ulated strip near the axes. ✓ The result is independent of $a$, confirming it de
pends only on $s$.

Note: $\ln(\sqrt{s}+\sqrt{s-1})=\operatorname{arcosh}(\sqrt{s})$, so the answer
can also be written $s-\sqrt{s(s-1)}+\operatorname{arcosh}(\sqrt{s})$.

### PROOF COMPLETE
