## Proof

Consider the functional
$$J(f) = \int_0^1 \bigl(f'(x)^2 - 4\pi^2 f(x)^2\bigr)\,dx, \qquad X = \{f \in C^
1[0,1] : f(0)=0=f(1)\}.$$

**Key observation: homogeneity.** For every $f \in X$ and every scalar $c \in \m
athbb{R}$, the function $cf$ again lies in $X$ (the boundary conditions are line
ar and homogeneous). Moreover,
$$J(cf) = \int_0^1 \bigl((cf')^2 - 4\pi^2(cf)^2\bigr)\,dx = c^2 \int_0^1 \bigl(f
'^2 - 4\pi^2 f^2\bigr)\,dx = c^2 J(f).$$
Thus $J$ is **quadratic** (positively homogeneous of degree $2$) on $X$.

**Exhibit a function with negative $J$.** Take $f_*(x) = \sin(\pi x)$. Then $f_*
 \in C^1[0,1]$, $f_*(0)=\sin 0 = 0$, $f_*(1)=\sin\pi = 0$, so $f_* \in X$. Its d
erivative is $f_*'(x) = \pi\cos(\pi x)$. Using $\int_0^1 \sin^2(\pi x)\,dx = \in
t_0^1 \cos^2(\pi x)\,dx = \tfrac12$,
$$\int_0^1 f_*'^2\,dx = \pi^2 \cdot \tfrac12 = \tfrac{\pi^2}{2}, \qquad \int_0^1
 f_*^2\,dx = \tfrac12.$$
Hence
$$J(f_*) = \frac{\pi^2}{2} - 4\pi^2 \cdot \frac12 = \frac{\pi^2 - 4\pi^2}{2} = -
\frac{3\pi^2}{2} < 0.$$

**Drive $J$ to $-\infty$.** For each integer $n \ge 1$ define $f_n = n\,f_* \in
X$. By homogeneity,
$$J(f_n) = n^2 J(f_*) = -\frac{3\pi^2}{2}\,n^2 \;\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\; -\
infty.$$

Therefore the set $\{J(f) : f \in X\}$ is **not bounded below**: for every $M >
0$ there exists $f \in X$ (namely $f = \sqrt{2M/(3\pi^2)}\,f_*$) with $J(f) < -M
$.

**Conclusion.** The infimum $\inf_{f\in X} J(f)$ does **not** exist as a finite
real number; in the extended-real sense it equals $-\infty$.
