## Solution

We solve
$$\nabla^2 u + k^2 u = 0,\quad 0<r<a,\qquad u(a,\theta)=f(\theta),$$
with $u$ bounded as $r\to 0$ and $2\pi$-periodic in $\theta$.

### Step 1. Separation of variables

Write $u(r,\theta)=R(r)\Theta(\theta)$. Substituting into $\nabla^2 u + k^2 u=0$
 (with $\nabla^2 = \partial_{rr}+\frac1r\partial_r+\frac1{r^2}\partial_{\theta\t
heta}$) and dividing by $R\Theta$:

$$\frac{r^2 R''+rR'}{R}+k^2 r^2 + \frac{\Theta''}{\Theta}=0.$$

Set $\Theta''/\Theta = -n^2$ (constant). Periodicity $\Theta(\theta+2\pi)=\Theta
(\theta)$ forces $n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$, giving

$$\Theta_n(\theta)=A_n\cos(n\theta)+B_n\sin(n\theta),\qquad n=0,1,2,\dots$$

(For $n=0$: $\Theta_0=A_0$, constant.)

### Step 2. Radial equation — Bessel's equation

The radial part becomes

$$r^2 R''+rR'+(k^2 r^2-n^2)R=0,$$

which is Bessel's equation of order $n$. Its general solution is

$$R(r)=C\,J_n(kr)+D\,Y_n(kr),$$

where $J_n,Y_n$ are Bessel functions of the first and second kind. Since $Y_n(kr
)\to -\infty$ as $r\to 0$, the boundedness condition at the origin forces $D=0$.
 Hence $R_n(r)=J_n(kr)$.

### Step 3. General solution

Superposing all separated solutions:

$$u(r,\theta)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}J_n(kr)\bigl[\alpha_n\cos(n\theta)+\beta_n\sin(
n\theta)\bigr].$$

### Step 4. Impose the boundary condition

At $r=a$:

$$f(\theta)=u(a,\theta)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}J_n(ka)\bigl[\alpha_n\cos(n\theta)+\b
eta_n\sin(n\theta)\bigr].$$

Assuming $J_n(ka)\neq 0$ for all $n\ge 0$ (i.e. $ka$ is not a zero of any $J_n$
— the non-resonance condition), we divide through and identify the Fourier coeff
icients of $f$:

$$\alpha_n=\frac{a_n}{J_n(ka)},\qquad \beta_n=\frac{b_n}{J_n(ka)},$$

where the Fourier coefficients of $f$ (extended $2\pi$-periodically) are

$$a_n=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(\varphi)\cos(n\varphi)\,d\varphi,\quad n\ge 0;
\qquad b_n=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(\varphi)\sin(n\varphi)\,d\varphi,\quad n\
ge 1.$$

### Step 5. Final formula

$$\boxed{\;u(r,\theta)=\frac{a_0}{2}\frac{J_0(kr)}{J_0(ka)}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\
frac{J_n(kr)}{J_n(ka)}\bigl[a_n\cos(n\theta)+b_n\sin(n\theta)\bigr]\;}$$

with $a_n,b_n$ the Fourier coefficients of $f$ as above.

Equivalently, using the Fourier series $f(\theta)=\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{n\ge1}(a_n
\cos n\theta+b_n\sin n\theta)$, the solution is obtained from $f$ by replacing e
ach Fourier mode $\cos(n\theta),\sin(n\theta)$ with $\frac{J_n(kr)}{J_n(ka)}\cos
(n\theta),\frac{J_n(kr)}{J_n(ka)}\sin(n\theta)$.

### Step 6. Verification

- **PDE:** Each term $J_n(kr)\cos(n\theta)$ satisfies $\nabla^2 u+k^2u=0$ by con
struction (Bessel's equation is exactly the radial part of the Helmholtz equatio
n). Linearity preserves this.
- **Boundary:** At $r=a$, $J_n(ka)/J_n(ka)=1$, so the series reduces to the Four
ier series of $f$.
- **Boundedness at origin:** $J_n(kr)$ is bounded (indeed $J_n(0)=\delta_{n0}$)
as $r\to 0$; no $Y_n$ terms present.
- **Periodicity:** Each $\cos(n\theta),\sin(n\theta)$ is $2\pi$-periodic.

### Caveat (resonance)

If $J_n(ka)=0$ for some $n$, then for that mode the boundary condition $J_n(ka)\
alpha_n = a_n$ either has no solution (if $a_n$ or $b_n\neq 0$) or leaves $\alph
a_n$ undetermined (if $a_n=b_n=0$). In the latter case one may add the homogeneo
us (eigenfunction) term $C\,J_n(kr)\cos(n\theta)$ (or $\sin$), so uniqueness fai
ls. Thus the formula above gives the unique bounded solution precisely when $ka$
 is not a zero of any $J_n$ ($n\ge0$); these exceptional values of $ka$ are the
Dirichlet eigenvalues of $-\nabla^2$ on the disk of radius $a$.
