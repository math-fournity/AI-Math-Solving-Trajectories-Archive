Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the contour integral of $\p
i \csc(\pi z)\frac{P(z)}{Q(z)}$ around the specified square vanishes as $N \to \
infty$.

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $C_N$ denote the square contour with vertices $\pm\left(N+\tfrac{
1}{2}\right) \pm i\left(N+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)$. We analyze $I_N = \oint_{C_N} \p
i \csc(\pi z)\,\frac{P(z)}{Q(z)}\,dz$.

Since $\deg Q = \deg P + 1$, there exist constants $c, R_0 > 0$ such that for $|
z| \geq R_0$:
$$\left|\frac{P(z)}{Q(z)}\right| \leq \frac{c}{|z|}.$$

On $C_N$, we have $|z| \geq N + \tfrac{1}{2}$, so $\left|\frac{P(z)}{Q(z)}\right
| = O(1/N)$ uniformly on $C_N$.

**Bounding $|\csc(\pi z)|$ on $C_N$.**

*Vertical sides* ($z = \pm(N+\tfrac{1}{2}) + iy$, $|y| \leq N+\tfrac{1}{2}$):

Using $\sin(\pi(N+\tfrac{1}{2})) = (-1)^N$ and $\cos(\pi(N+\tfrac{1}{2})) = 0$:
$$\sin(\pi z) = (-1)^N \cosh(\pi y), \quad\text{so}\quad |\csc(\pi z)| = \frac{1
}{\cosh(\pi y)} \leq 1.$$

*Horizontal sides* ($z = x \pm i(N+\tfrac{1}{2})$, $|x| \leq N+\tfrac{1}{2}$):

$$|\sin(\pi z)|^2 = \sin^2(\pi x)\cosh^2\!\big(\pi(N+\tfrac{1}{2})\big) + \cos^2
(\pi x)\sinh^2\!\big(\pi(N+\tfrac{1}{2})\big)$$
$$= \sin^2(\pi x) + \sinh^2\!\big(\pi(N+\tfrac{1}{2})\big) \geq \sinh^2\!\big(\p
i(N+\tfrac{1}{2})\big).$$

Since $\sinh(\pi(N+\tfrac{1}{2})) \sim \tfrac{1}{2}e^{\pi N}$, we get $|\csc(\pi
 z)| \leq \operatorname{csch}(\pi(N+\tfrac{1}{2})) = O(e^{-\pi N})$ on the horiz
ontal sides.

**Estimating the integral.**

*Horizontal sides:* The integrand is bounded by $\pi \cdot O(e^{-\pi N}) \cdot O
(1/N) = O(e^{-\pi N}/N)$. The side length is $O(N)$, so the contribution is $O(e
^{-\pi N}) \to 0$.

*Vertical sides:* Parametrizing by $y \in [-(N+\tfrac{1}{2}),\, N+\tfrac{1}{2}]$
, the contribution of one vertical side is bounded by:
$$\int_{-(N+1/2)}^{N+1/2} \pi \cdot \frac{1}{\cosh(\pi y)} \cdot O(1/N)\, dy = O
(1/N) \int_{-(N+1/2)}^{N+1/2} \operatorname{sech}(\pi y)\, dy.$$

Since $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \operatorname{sech}(\pi y)\, dy = 1$, the integra
l is bounded by a constant, giving a contribution of $O(1/N) \to 0$.

**Conclusion.** Combining all four sides:
$$|I_N| \leq O(e^{-\pi N}) + O(1/N) \to 0 \quad\text{as } N \to \infty.$$

Therefore, **the integral vanishes** as $N \to \infty$ through the positive inte
gers. $\blacksquare$
