## Proof

**Preliminaries.** Let $K = \mathbb{k}(X)$. Since $\operatorname{char}\mathbb{k}
\neq 2$ and $f(X)$ is square-free and nonconstant, $f(X)$ is not a square in $K$
: a square in $K=\mathbb{k}(X)$ that lies in $\mathbb{k}[X]$ must be a square in
 $\mathbb{k}[X]$, but a square-free nonconstant polynomial is not a square. Henc
e $T^2 - f(X)$ has no root in $K$ and, being of degree $2$, is irreducible over
$K$. Therefore $(Y^2-f(X))$ is a maximal ideal of $K[Y]$ and $\mathbb{F}=K[Y]/(Y
^2-f(X))$ is a field. The natural map $K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{F}$ is injective,
 so we identify $K=\mathbb{k}(x)\subseteq\mathbb{F}$, and $y$ denotes the image
of $Y$, giving $y^2=f(x)$. Note $f(x)\neq 0$ (as $f\neq 0$ in $\mathbb{k}[X]$),
so $y\neq 0$ and, since $\operatorname{char}\mathbb{k}\neq 2$, $y\neq -y$.

### (a) $x$ transcendental over $\mathbb{k}$; $y$ algebraic over $\mathbb{k}(x)$

The embedding $\mathbb{k}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{F}$ sends $X\mapsto x$. Since

By construction $y^2=f(x)$, so $y$ is a root of $T^2-f(x)\in\mathbb{k}(x)[T]$; h

Now $\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{k}(x)[y]$ (generated as a $K$-algebra by $y$, and $K=\ma
thbb{k}(x)$), so $\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{k}(x,y)$ is finitely generated over $\mathb

\mathbb{k}(x)$, the transcendence degree satisfies $\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathb
