## Setting Up the Count

Fix $S = A \cup B$ with $|S| = 999$. Each element of $S$ is assigned to one of t
hree regions: $A \setminus B$, $B \setminus A$, or $A \cap B$ — giving 3 choices
 per element, subject to the constraint on $\{1,2\}$.

The constraint requires $1 \in A \cap B$ and $2 \notin A \cap B$. Since $A \cap
B \subseteq S$, we need $1 \in S$.

**Case 1: $1 \in S, 2 \in S$.** Element 1: forced into $A \cap B$ (1 choice). El
ement 2: must avoid $A \cap B$, so 2 choices. Remaining 997 elements: 3 choices
each. Count: $2 \cdot 3^{997}$. Number of such $S$: $\binom{1995}{997}$.

**Case 2: $1 \in S, 2 \notin S$.** Element 1: forced into $A \cap B$ (1 choice).
 Element 2 absent — constraint auto-satisfied. Remaining 998 elements: 3 choices
 each. Count: $3^{998}$. Number of such $S$: $\binom{1995}{998}$.

**Cases 3, 4 ($1 \notin S$):** Impossible since $1 \in A \cap B \subseteq S$.
