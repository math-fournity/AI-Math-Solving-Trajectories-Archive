$$(a_1, b_1) \prec (a_2, b_2) \iff b_1 < b_2, \text{ or } (b_1 = b_2 \text{ and

### Step 1: $\prec$ is a linear (total) order

- $b_1 < b_2$: then $(a_1, b_1) \prec (a_2, b_2)$.

\prec (a_1,b_1)$).

Exactly one holds, so trichotomy is satisfied.

**Transitivity.** Suppose $(a_1, b_1) \prec (a_2, b_2) \prec (a_3, b_3)$.
- If $b_1 < b_2$ or $b_2 < b_3$: then $b_1 \leq b_2 \leq b_3$ with at least one

### Step 5: Apply Cantor's Isomorphism Theorem

**Cantor's Theorem (1895).** *Any two countable dense linear orders without endp
oints are order-isomorphic.*
