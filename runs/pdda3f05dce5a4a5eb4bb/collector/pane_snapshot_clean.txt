## Solution

**Step 1: Factor the expression.**

Let $f(a,b,c)=ab(a^2-b^2)+bc(b^2-c^2)+ca(c^2-a^2)=a^3b-ab^3+b^3c-bc^3+c^3a-ca^3$
.

Since $f$ is antisymmetric (swapping any two variables negates it) and vanishes
whenever two variables coincide, the cubic $(a-b)(b-c)(c-a)$ divides $f$. As bot
h sides are antisymmetric of degrees $4$ and $3$, the remaining factor is a symm
etric linear form $k(a+b+c)$. Comparing the coefficient of $a^3b$ (which is $+1$
 in $f$ and $-1$ in $(a-b)(b-c)(c-a)(a+b+c)$) gives $k=-1$, hence

$$f(a,b,c)=(a-b)(b-c)(a-c)(a+b+c).$$

**Step 2: Reduce to a two-parameter problem.**

The inequality is homogeneous of degree $4$, so normalize $a^2+b^2+c^2=1$. Write
 $s=a+b+c$ and set $a=\tfrac{s}{3}+x,\ b=\tfrac{s}{3}+y,\ c=\tfrac{s}{3}+z$ with
 $x+y+z=0$. Then
$$a^2+b^2+c^2=\tfrac{s^2}{3}+(x^2+y^2+z^2)=1,\qquad (a-b)(b-c)(a-c)=(x-y)(y-z)(x
-z),$$
so
$$|f|=|s|\cdot|(x-y)(y-z)(x-z)|,\qquad r^2:=x^2+y^2+z^2=1-\tfrac{s^2}{3},\ s^2\l
e 3.$$

**Step 3: Bound the Vandermonde product.**

With $x+y+z=0$ and $x^2+y^2+z^2=r^2$, parametrize
$$x=\rho\cos\theta,\quad y=\rho\cos\!\big(\theta+\tfrac{2\pi}{3}\big),\quad z=\r
ho\cos\!\big(\theta-\tfrac{2\pi}{3}\big),\qquad \rho^2=\tfrac{2r^2}{3}.$$
A direct computation gives
$$(x-y)(y-z)(x-z)=-3\sqrt{3}\,\rho^3\sin\theta\big(\sin^2\theta-\tfrac34\big).$$
Setting $u=\sin\theta\in[-1,1]$, the function $|u^3-\tfrac34 u|$ attains its max
imum at $u=\pm\tfrac12$ (where $g'(\tfrac12)=0$) with value $\tfrac14$ (the endp
oints $u=\pm1$ give the same value $\tfrac14$). Hence
$$\max |(x-y)(y-z)(x-z)|=\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4}\rho^3=\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4}\cdot r^3\
!\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^{3/2}=\frac{r^3}{\sqrt{2}}.$$

**Step 4: Optimize over $s$.**

Combining,
$$|f|\le \frac{|s|}{\sqrt{2}}\Big(1-\tfrac{s^2}{3}\Big)^{3/2}.$$
With $t=s^2\in[0,3]$, maximize $h(t)^2=\dfrac{t}{2}\big(1-\tfrac{t}{3}\big)^3$.
The derivative of $t(1-t/3)^3$ is $(1-t/3)^2\big(1-\tfrac{4t}{3}\big)$, which va
nishes at $t=\tfrac34$ (interior) — the unique maximum. Thus
$$h_{\max}^2=\frac12\cdot\frac34\Big(\frac34\Big)^3=\frac12\cdot\frac{81}{256}=\
frac{81}{512},\qquad h_{\max}=\frac{9}{\sqrt{512}}=\frac{9\sqrt{2}}{32}.$$

**Step 5: Equality is attained.**

Take $s^2=\tfrac34$ (so $s=\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, $r^2=\tfrac34$) and $\sin\theta=\
tfrac12$, giving $x=\tfrac{\sqrt6}{4},\ y=-\tfrac{\sqrt6}{4},\ z=0$, i.e.
$$a=\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt3}+\tfrac{\sqrt6}{4},\quad b=\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt3}-\tfrac{\sqrt
6}{4},\quad c=\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt3}.$$
One checks $a^2+b^2+c^2=1$ and $|f|=\tfrac{9\sqrt2}{32}$, so the bound is sharp.

$$\boxed{M=\dfrac{9\sqrt{2}}{32}}.$$
