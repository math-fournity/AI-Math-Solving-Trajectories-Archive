## Solution

Let $W_t = (W_t^{(1)}, W_t^{(2)})$ be the 2D Brownian motion starting at $(1,1)$
, with independent components. The x-axis is hit at the stopping time
$$T = \inf\{t \ge 0 : W_t^{(2)} = 0\}.$$
We seek $P\!\bigl(W_T^{(1)} > 0\bigr)$, i.e. the probability that the hitting po
int $(W_T^{(1)}, 0)$ lies on the positive half of the x-axis. (The origin has hi
t-probability $0$, so it does not affect the answer.)

**Poisson kernel for the upper half-plane.** The starting point $(1,1)$ lies in
the upper half-plane $\mathbb H = \{(x,y): y>0\}$. For Brownian motion in $\math
bb H$ starting at $(x_0,y_0)$, the harmonic measure (hitting distribution) on th
e boundary $\partial\mathbb H = \mathbb R$ has density given by the Poisson kern
el
$$p(x) = \frac{1}{\pi}\,\frac{y_0}{(x-x_0)^2 + y_0^2}, \qquad x\in\mathbb R.$$
This is a Cauchy density with location $x_0$ and scale $y_0$. (It is the unique
probability density that is harmonic-conjugate–consistent; equivalently it is th
e real part of the Cauchy–Poisson kernel $\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Im}\frac{1}
{z-z_0}$.)

**Verification via independence of components.** Since $W^{(1)}$ and $W^{(2)}$ a
re independent, $W^{(2)}$ starting at $1$ hits $0$ at a Lévy-distributed time $T
$ with density $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t^3}}e^{-1/(2t)}$, and $W_T^{(1)}\mid T=t \s
im N(1,t)$. Mixing,
$$f(x)=\int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-(x-1)^2/(2t)}}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\cdot\frac{e^{-1/(2t
)}}{\sqrt{2\pi t^3}}\,dt = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^\infty t^{-2}e^{-\frac{(x-1)^2+1
}{2t}}\,dt.$$
With $a=\frac{(x-1)^2+1}{2}$ and $u=a/t$,
$$\int_0^\infty t^{-2}e^{-a/t}\,dt = \frac{1}{a}\int_0^\infty e^{-u}\,du = \frac
{1}{a},$$
so $f(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi a}=\frac{1}{\pi\bigl((x-1)^2+1\bigr)}$, confirming the Ca
uchy$(1,1)$ law.

**Computing the probability.** With $(x_0,y_0)=(1,1)$,
$$P\!\bigl(W_T^{(1)}>0\bigr)=\int_0^\infty \frac{1}{\pi}\,\frac{dx}{(x-1)^2+1}=\
frac{1}{\pi}\Bigl[\arctan(x-1)\Bigr]_{0}^{\infty}.$$
Evaluating,
$$\arctan(\infty)-\arctan(-1)=\frac{\pi}{2}-\Bigl(-\frac{\pi}{4}\Bigr)=\frac{\pi
}{2}+\frac{\pi}{4}=\frac{3\pi}{4}.$$
Therefore
$$P\!\bigl(W_T^{(1)}>0\bigr)=\frac{1}{\pi}\cdot\frac{3\pi}{4}=\frac{3}{4}.$$

The probability that $W_t$ hits the positive half of the $x$-axis before the neg
ative part is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{3}{4}}.$$
