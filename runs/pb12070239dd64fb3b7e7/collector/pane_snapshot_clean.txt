**Setup.** Convert to polar coordinates with $dxdy = r\,dr\,d\theta$, $r\in[0,1]
$, $\theta\in[0,2\pi]$:

$$I_n = \int_0^{2\pi}d\theta\int_0^1 \frac{n}{r\cos\pi r + n^2 r^3}\,r\,dr = 2\p
i\int_0^1 \frac{n}{\cos\pi r + n^2 r^2}\,dr,$$

where for $r>0$ we cancelled a factor of $r$ (the point $r=0$ is measure-zero).

**Rescaling.** Substitute $u = nr$, i.e. $r = u/n$, $dr = du/n$. The limits beco
me $u\in[0,n]$:

$$I_n = 2\pi\int_0^n \frac{n}{\cos(\pi u/n) + u^2}\,\frac{du}{n} = 2\pi\int_0^n
\frac{du}{\cos(\pi u/n) + u^2}.$$

Extend the integrand to all of $[0,\infty)$ by setting $f_n(u) = \frac{1}{\cos(\
pi u/n)+u^2}\mathbf{1}_{[0,n]}(u)$.

**Pointwise limit.** For each fixed $u\geq 0$, once $n > u$ we have $\pi u/n \to
 0$, hence $\cos(\pi u/n)\to 1$, and

$$f_n(u) \longrightarrow \frac{1}{1+u^2}.$$

**Dominating function.** We produce an integrable bound valid for all large $n$.

- *On $[0,2]$:* For $n\geq 4$, $\pi u/n \leq \pi/2$, so $\cos(\pi u/n)\geq 0$, g
iving $\cos(\pi u/n)+u^2 \geq u^2 \geq 0$. More sharply, for $u\in[0,2]$ and $n\
geq 4$: $\cos(\pi u/n)\geq \cos(\pi/2)=0$ is too weak near $u=2$. Instead use $\
cos(\pi u/n)\geq \cos(\pi\cdot 2/4)=\cos(\pi/2)=0$; but we need a positive lower
 bound. For $n\geq 8$: $\pi u/n\leq \pi/4$, so $\cos(\pi u/n)\geq \tfrac{\sqrt2}
{2}\geq \tfrac12$. Thus $f_n(u)\leq \frac{1}{1/2 + u^2}\leq 2$.

- *On $[2,\infty)$:* For $u\geq 2$ and $u\leq n$, $u^2\geq 4$ while $|\cos(\pi u
/n)|\leq 1$, so $\cos(\pi u/n)+u^2 \geq u^2-1\geq \frac34 u^2$ (using $u^2-1\geq
 \frac34 u^2 \iff u^2\geq 4$). Hence $f_n(u)\leq \frac{4}{3u^2}$.

Define $g(u) = 2\cdot\mathbf{1}_{[0,2]}(u) + \tfrac{4}{3u^2}\mathbf{1}_{(2,\inft
y)}(u)$, which is integrable on $[0,\infty)$. Then $|f_n(u)|\leq g(u)$ for all $
n\geq 8$, and $g\in L^1([0,\infty))$.

**Applying Dominated Convergence.**

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} I_n = 2\pi\int_0^\infty \lim_{n\to\infty} f_n(u)\,du = 2\pi\
int_0^\infty \frac{du}{1+u^2} = 2\pi\cdot\frac{\pi}{2} = \pi^2.$$

---

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} I_n = \pi^2.}$$
