# The Weak-Star Dual of a Fréchet Space is Quasi-Complete

**Answer: Yes.** The weak-star dual $(E', \sigma(E', E))$ of a Fréchet space $E$
 is quasi-complete.

## Proof

Let $E$ be a Fréchet space (complete metrizable locally convex space) and let $E
'$ denote its topological dual. We equip $E'$ with the weak-star topology $\sigm
a(E', E)$, which is the topology of pointwise convergence on $E$. We show that e
very $\sigma(E', E)$-bounded and $\sigma(E', E)$-closed subset of $E'$ is $\sigm
a(E', E)$-complete.

### Step 1: Fréchet spaces are barreled

A Fréchet space is a complete metrizable topological vector space, hence a Baire
 space. Every Baire locally convex space is barreled (every closed, balanced, ab
sorbing, convex set — i.e., every barrel — has nonempty interior, hence is a nei
ghborhood of zero). Therefore $E$ is barreled.

### Step 2: Weak-star bounded sets are equicontinuous (Banach–Steinhaus)

A subset $B \subset E'$ is $\sigma(E', E)$-bounded if and only if for every $x \
in E$,
$$\sup_{f \in B} |f(x)| < \infty,$$
i.e., $B$ is pointwise bounded on $E$.

Since $E$ is barreled, the **Banach–Steinhaus theorem** (uniform boundedness pri
nciple for barreled spaces) applies: every pointwise bounded family of continuou
s linear functionals on a barreled space is **equicontinuous**.

Therefore every $\sigma(E', E)$-bounded subset of $E'$ is equicontinuous.

### Step 3: Equicontinuous sets are contained in polars of neighborhoods

A family $B \subset E'$ is equicontinuous if and only if there exists a neighbor
hood $U$ of zero in $E$ such that
$$B \subset U^\circ := \{f \in E' : |f(x)| \leq 1 \text{ for all } x \in U\}.$$

### Step 4: Polars of neighborhoods are weak-star compact (Alaoglu–Bourbaki)

By the **Alaoglu–Bourbaki theorem**, for any neighborhood $U$ of zero in a local
ly convex space $E$, the polar $U^\circ$ is $\sigma(E', E)$-compact.

### Step 5: Closed bounded sets are compact, hence complete

Let $B \subset E'$ be $\sigma(E', E)$-bounded and $\sigma(E', E)$-closed.

- By Steps 2 and 3, $B \subset U^\circ$ for some neighborhood $U$ of zero in $E$
.
- By Step 4, $U^\circ$ is $\sigma(E', E)$-compact.
- Since $B$ is $\sigma(E', E)$-closed and $B \subset U^\circ$, and a closed subs
et of a compact set is compact, $B$ is $\sigma(E', E)$-compact.
- The weak-star topology $\sigma(E', E)$ is Hausdorff (since $E$ separates point
s of $E'$: if $f \in E'$ and $f(x) = 0$ for all $x \in E$, then $f = 0$). In a H
ausdorff topological vector space (equivalently, a Hausdorff uniform space), eve
ry compact set is complete.

Therefore $B$ is $\sigma(E', E)$-complete.

### Conclusion

Every $\sigma(E', E)$-bounded and $\sigma(E', E)$-closed subset of $E'$ is $\sig
ma(E', E)$-complete. By definition, this means $(E', \sigma(E', E))$ is **quasi-
complete**.

**Remark.** The argument uses only that $E$ is barreled (for the Banach–Steinhau
s theorem) and that $E$ is locally convex (for Alaoglu–Bourbaki). Since every Fr
échet space is barreled and locally convex, the result applies. In fact, the wea
k-star dual of any barreled locally convex space is quasi-complete.
