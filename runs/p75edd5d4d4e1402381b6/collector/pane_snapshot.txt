**Claim.** The only function $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ satisfying
$$f\!\left(x^{2}+xy+f(y^{2})\right)=xf(y)+x^{2}+f(y^{2}) \tag{$\star$}$$
for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$ is $f(t)=t$.

---

### Step 1. Determine $f(0)$ and $f$ on $[0,\infty)$.

Set $x=0$ in $(\star)$:
$$f\!\big(f(y^{2})\big)=f(y^{2}) \quad\text{for all }y. \tag{1}$$

Set $y=0$ in $(\star)$, writing $c=f(0)$:
$$f(x^{2}+c)=cx+x^{2}+c \quad\text{for all }x. \tag{2}$$

Replacing $x$ by $-x$ in (2) gives $f(x^{2}+c)=-cx+x^{2}+c$. Comparing with (2):
$$cx=-cx \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2cx=0 \quad\text{for all }x,$$
so $c=0$, i.e. $\boxed{f(0)=0}$.

Then (2) becomes $f(x^{2})=x^{2}$ for all $x$. Since every $t\ge 0$ equals $x^{2
}$ for some $x$:
$$\boxed{f(t)=t \quad\text{for all }t\ge 0.} \tag{3}$$

### Step 2. Reduce the equation and conclude.

Using (3) with $t=y^{2}\ge 0$ gives $f(y^{2})=y^{2}$. Substituting into $(\star)
$:
$$f\!\left(x^{2}+xy+y^{2}\right)=xf(y)+x^{2}+y^{2}. \tag{4}$$

Now observe that $x^{2}+xy+y^{2}=\!\left(x+\tfrac{y}{2}\right)^{2}+\tfrac{3}{4}y
^{2}\ge 0$ for all real $x,y$. Hence by (3) the left-hand side of (4) is simply
$$f\!\left(x^{2}+xy+y^{2}\right)=x^{2}+xy+y^{2}.$$

So (4) becomes
$$x^{2}+xy+y^{2}=xf(y)+x^{2}+y^{2},$$
i.e.
$$x\big(y-f(y)\big)=0 \quad\text{for all }x,y.$$

Choosing any $x\ne 0$ (e.g. $x=1$) yields $f(y)=y$ for every $y\in\mathbb{R}$.

### Step 3. Verification.

For $f(t)=t$: LHS $=f(x^{2}+xy+y^{2})=x^{2}+xy+y^{2}$; RHS $=xy+x^{2}+y^{2}$. Th
ese are equal. ✓

---

Therefore the unique solution is $\,f(t)=t\,$ for all $t\in\mathbb{R}$.
