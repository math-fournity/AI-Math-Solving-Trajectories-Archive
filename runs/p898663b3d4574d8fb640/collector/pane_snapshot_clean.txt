**Solution.**

Note that $\cos(iz) = \cosh(z)$, so the integrand is $f(z) = \frac{e^{izt}}{\pi\
cosh(z)}$.

**Step 1: Identify the poles.**

$\cosh(z) = 0$ at $z_n = i\pi\!\left(n+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)$, $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.
 These are simple poles with $\sinh(z_n) = i(-1)^n$. The poles in the upper half
-plane are $z_n = i\pi(n+\frac{1}{2})$ for $n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$

**Step 2: Compute residues.**

$$\operatorname{Res}(f, z_n) = \frac{e^{iz_n t}}{\pi\sinh(z_n)} = \frac{e^{-\pi(
n+1/2)\,t}}{\pi\, i\,(-1)^n} = \frac{(-1)^n\, e^{-\pi(n+1/2)\,t}}{\pi\, i}.$$

**Step 3: Closed contour integral via the residue theorem.**

Let $C_R$ be the closed contour $[-R,R] \cup \Gamma_R$. For $R$ not equal to any
 $\pi(n+\frac{1}{2})$, the residue theorem gives:

$$\oint_{C_R} f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i \sum_{n=0}^{N(R)} \frac{(-1)^n\, e^{-\pi(n+1/2)\
,t}}{\pi\, i} = 2\sum_{n=0}^{N(R)} (-1)^n e^{-\pi(n+1/2)\,t},$$

where $N(R) = \lfloor R/\pi - 1/2 \rfloor$. As $R \to \infty$, this converges (g
eometric series, ratio $e^{-\pi t} < 1$):

$$\lim_{R\to\infty}\oint_{C_R} f\,dz = 2e^{-\pi t/2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^n e^
{-\pi n t} = \frac{2e^{-\pi t/2}}{1+e^{-\pi t}} = \frac{1}{\cosh(\pi t/2)} = \op
eratorname{sech}\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{2}\right).$$

**Step 4: Compute the real-line integral independently.**

We evaluate $I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{ixt}}{\pi\cosh(x)}\,dx$ using
a **rectangular contour** with vertices $-R,\, R,\, R+i\pi,\, -R+i\pi$.

- The only pole inside is $z = i\pi/2$, with residue $\frac{e^{-\pi t/2}}{\pi\,
i}$.
- Residue theorem: $\oint = 2\pi i \cdot \frac{e^{-\pi t/2}}{\pi i} = 2e^{-\pi t
/2}$.
- **Bottom** ($-R$ to $R$): $\to I$.
- **Top** ($R+i\pi$ to $-R+i\pi$): Using $\cosh(x+i\pi)=-\cosh(x)$ and $e^{i(x+i
\pi)t}=e^{ixt}e^{-\pi t}$, this gives $e^{-\pi t}\cdot I$.
- **Sides**: $|f| \leq \frac{2}{\pi}e^{-R}\cdot 1 \to 0$.

So $I(1+e^{-\pi t}) = 2e^{-\pi t/2}$, giving:

$$I = \frac{2e^{-\pi t/2}}{1+e^{-\pi t}} = \operatorname{sech}\!\left(\frac{\pi
t}{2}\right).$$

**Step 5: Conclude.**

Since $\oint_{C_R} = \int_{-R}^{R} f(x)\,dx + \int_{\Gamma_R} f(z)\,dz$, taking
$R\to\infty$:

$$\operatorname{sech}\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{2}\right) = \operatorname{sech}\!\left
(\frac{\pi t}{2}\right) + \lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\Gamma_R} \frac{e^{izt}}{\pi\co
sh(z)}\,dz.$$

Therefore:

$$\boxed{\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\Gamma_R}\frac{e^{izt}}{\pi\cos(iz)}\,dz = 0.}$$

**Remark on why the semicircle integral vanishes.** The exponential decay comes
from two complementary mechanisms on $\Gamma_R$ (where $z=Re^{i\theta}$): (1) Aw
ay from $\theta=\pi/2$, $|\cosh(z)|\geq |\sinh(R\cos\theta)|\sim \frac{1}{2}e^{R
|\cos\theta|}$ grows exponentially while $|e^{izt}|=e^{-Rt\sin\theta}\leq 1$, gi
ving exponential decay. (2) Near $\theta=\pi/2$, $|e^{izt}|=e^{-Rt\sin\theta}\ap
prox e^{-Rt}$ decays exponentially (since $t>0$), which dominates any growth of
$1/|\cosh(z)|$ near the poles on the imaginary axis. The factor $e^{-Rt}$ always
 wins because $t>0$.
