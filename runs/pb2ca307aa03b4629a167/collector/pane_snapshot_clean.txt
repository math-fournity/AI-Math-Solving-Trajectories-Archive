## Totally Geodesic Submanifolds of $\mathbb{R}P^n$

**Theorem.** The connected totally geodesic submanifolds of $\mathbb{R}P^n$ (wit
h its standard metric of constant sectional curvature $+1$) are exactly the proj
ective subspaces $\mathbb{R}P^k \subset \mathbb{R}P^n$ for $0 \le k \le n$.

---

### Proof

**Step 1: Setup.** Real projective space $\mathbb{R}P^n$ is the quotient $S^n/\{
\pm\mathrm{id}\}$, where $S^n \subset \mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ is the unit sphere with
its round metric and the antipodal map $a: x \mapsto -x$ acts freely and isometr
ically. The quotient projection
$$\pi: S^n \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}P^n$$
is a Riemannian covering map, hence a **local isometry**.

**Step 2: Reduction to $S^n$.** Let $N \subset \mathbb{R}P^n$ be a connected sub
manifold. Since $\pi$ is a local isometry, it preserves the Levi-Civita connecti
on, geodesics, and the second fundamental form. Concretely, if $\widetilde{N} :=
 \pi^{-1}(N) \subset S^n$, then at every point $\tilde{p} \in \widetilde{N}$ the
 second fundamental forms satisfy
$$\alpha_{S^n}^{\widetilde{N}}(\tilde{X}, \tilde{Y}) = d\pi_{\tilde{p}}^{-1}\!\b
igl(\alpha_{\mathbb{R}P^n}^{N}(\pi_*\tilde{X},\, \pi_*\tilde{Y})\bigr).$$
Therefore:
$$N \text{ is totally geodesic in } \mathbb{R}P^n \iff \widetilde{N} \text{ is t
otally geodesic in } S^n.$$

**Step 3: Totally geodesic submanifolds of $S^n$.** We recall the classical char
acterization. Let $M \subset S^n$ be a connected totally geodesic submanifold. T
ake $p \in M$ and let $T_p M \subset T_p S^n = p^\perp \subset \mathbb{R}^{n+1}$
. Set $V := \mathbb{R}p \oplus T_p M \subset \mathbb{R}^{n+1}$, a linear subspac
e of dimension $k+1$ where $k = \dim M$. The great sphere $S^k := V \cap S^n$ is
 totally geodesic in $S^n$. Both $M$ and $S^k$ are connected, have the same dime
nsion $k$, pass through $p$, and share the same tangent space $T_p M = T_p S^k$.
 Since both are totally geodesic, geodesics of $S^n$ starting at $p$ in directio
ns tangent to $T_p M$ stay in both $M$ and $S^k$. The exponential map is a diffe
omorphism near $p$, so $M$ and $S^k$ coincide in a neighborhood of $p$. By conne
ctedness they coincide globally:
$$M = S^k = V \cap S^n.$$
Conversely, every great sphere $V \cap S^n$ is totally geodesic (it is a fixed-p
oint set of the reflection across $V$, which is an isometry). Thus **the connect
ed totally geodesic submanifolds of $S^n$ are exactly the great spheres $S^k$, $
0 \le k \le n$.**

**Step 4: Imposing antipodal invariance.** Since $\widetilde{N} = \pi^{-1}(N)$ a
nd $N$ is a submanifold of the quotient, $\widetilde{N}$ is invariant under the
antipodal map: $a(\widetilde{N}) = \widetilde{N}$. By Step 2 and Step 3, $\widet
ilde{N}$ is a great sphere $S^k = V \cap S^n$ for some $(k+1)$-dimensional linea
r subspace $V \subset \mathbb{R}^{n+1}$. But any linear subspace $V$ satisfies $
-V = V$, so $a(S^k) = S^k$ automatically. Hence the antipodal invariance is **au
tomatically satisfied** for every great sphere.

**Step 5: Conclusion.** The image $\pi(S^k) = \pi(V \cap S^n)$ is the set of lin
es through the origin contained in $V$, i.e., the projective space $\mathbb{R}P^
k = \mathbb{P}(V) \subset \mathbb{R}P^n = \mathbb{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})$. This is
 a projective $k$-plane, and by Step 2 it is totally geodesic.

Conversely, every connected totally geodesic $N \subset \mathbb{R}P^n$ lifts to
a great sphere $\widetilde{N} = S^k$, so $N = \pi(S^k) = \mathbb{R}P^k$.

Therefore the connected totally geodesic submanifolds of $\mathbb{R}P^n$ are pre
cisely
$$\boxed{\mathbb{R}P^k \subset \mathbb{R}P^n, \quad 0 \le k \le n,}$$
i.e., the projective subspaces (projective $k$-planes), ranging from a single po
int ($k=0$) to the full space ($k=n$). $\blacksquare$
