## Proof

Let $q$ be the conjugate exponent to $p$, i.e., $\frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{q} = 1$.
 Since $1 < p < \infty$, we have $1 < q < \infty$.

**Step 1: Define a candidate linear functional.**

Define $T: L^q(\mu) \to \mathbb{R}$ (or $\mathbb{C}$) by
$$T(g) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \int_X f_n\, g\, d\mu, \qquad g \in L^q(\mu).$$
By hypothesis, this limit exists for every $g \in L^q(\mu)$, so $T$ is well-defi
ned.

**Step 2: $T$ is linear.**

For $g, h \in L^q(\mu)$ and scalars $\alpha, \beta$:
$$T(\alpha g + \beta h) = \lim_{n\to\infty} \int f_n(\alpha g + \beta h)\,d\mu =
 \alpha \lim_{n\to\infty}\int f_n g\,d\mu + \beta \lim_{n\to\infty}\int f_n h\,d
\mu = \alpha\, T(g) + \beta\, T(h),$$
using linearity of the integral and linearity of limits.

**Step 3: $T$ is bounded (hence $T \in (L^q)^*$).**

Since $(f_n)$ is bounded in $L^p(\mu)$, set $M := \sup_{n} \|f_n\|_p < \infty$.
By Hölder's inequality, for every $n$ and every $g \in L^q(\mu)$:
$$\left|\int f_n\, g\, d\mu\right| \leq \|f_n\|_p\, \|g\|_q \leq M\, \|g\|_q.$$
Taking the limit (the absolute value of the limit is at most the limit superior
of the absolute values):
$$|T(g)| = \left|\lim_{n\to\infty} \int f_n\, g\, d\mu\right| = \lim_{n\to\infty
}\left|\int f_n\, g\, d\mu\right| \leq M\, \|g\|_q.$$
Thus $T$ is a bounded linear functional on $L^q(\mu)$ with $\|T\| \leq M$.

**Step 4: Apply the Riesz representation theorem.**

Since $1 < q < \infty$ and $(X, M, \mu)$ is $\sigma$-finite, the Riesz represent
ation theorem for $L^p$-spaces gives an isometric isomorphism
$$(L^q(\mu))^* \cong L^p(\mu),$$
where every bounded linear functional on $L^q(\mu)$ is represented by a unique f
unction in $L^p(\mu)$. Concretely, there exists $f \in L^p(\mu)$ such that
$$T(g) = \int_X f\, g\, d\mu \qquad \text{for all } g \in L^q(\mu).$$

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

Combining the definition of $T$ with the representation from Step 4:
$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \int_X f_n\, g\, d\mu = T(g) = \int_X f\, g\, d\mu \qquad
\text{for every } g \in L^q(\mu).$$

This $f \in L^p(\mu)$ is the desired function. $\blacksquare$
