# USAMO 1999, Problem 1 — Proof

Let $S$ be the set of squares containing checkers, and let $k = |S|$. Let $E$ be
 the set of empty squares, so $|E| = n^2 - k$.

Model the board as a grid graph $G$ whose vertices are the $n^2$ squares and who
se edges join pairs of squares sharing a side. Define:

- $e(S)$ = number of edges with both endpoints in $S$,
- $e(S,E)$ = number of edges with one endpoint in $S$ and one in $E$,
- $b$ = number of sides of squares in $S$ that lie on the boundary of the board.

**Counting sides.** Every square of $S$ has 4 sides, giving $4k$ sides total. Ea
ch edge internal to $S$ accounts for 2 of these sides; each $S$–$E$ edge account
s for 1; each board-boundary side accounts for 1. Hence

$$4k = 2\,e(S) + e(S,E) + b. \tag{1}$$

**Lower bound on $e(S)$ (connectivity).** By condition (b), the subgraph induced
 by $S$ is connected. A connected graph on $k$ vertices has at least $k-1$ edges
, so

$$e(S) \ge k - 1. \tag{2}$$

**Lower bound on $e(S,E)$ (domination).** By condition (a), every empty square s
hares a side with at least one square of $S$, i.e. every vertex of $E$ is incide
nt to at least one $S$–$E$ edge. Since each $S$–$E$ edge is incident to exactly
one vertex of $E$, these contributing edges are distinct for distinct empty squa
res. Therefore

$$e(S,E) \ge |E| = n^2 - k. \tag{3}$$

**Combining.** Since $b \ge 0$, substituting (2) and (3) into (1):

$$4k = 2\,e(S) + e(S,E) + b \ge 2(k-1) + (n^2 - k) + 0 = k + n^2 - 2.$$

Rearranging,

$$3k \ge n^2 - 2, \qquad\text{so}\qquad k \ge \frac{n^2 - 2}{3}.$$

Thus at least $\dfrac{n^2-2}{3}$ checkers have been placed on the board. $\black
square$
