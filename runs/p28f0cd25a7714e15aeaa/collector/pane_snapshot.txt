## Proof

**Claim.** The ring $B \otimes_A K$ has only finitely many prime ideals.

**Step 1: $B \otimes_A K$ is a finitely generated $K$-module.**

The field $K = \operatorname{Frac}(A/\mathfrak{p})$ is an $A$-algebra via the co
mposition
$$A \longrightarrow A/\mathfrak{p} \hookrightarrow \operatorname{Frac}(A/\mathfr
ak{p}) = K.$$
Since $B$ is finitely generated as an $A$-module, write $B = \sum_{i=1}^{n} A\,
b_i$. Then every element of $B \otimes_A K$ has the form $\sum_j b^{(j)} \otimes
 k_j$ with $b^{(j)} = \sum_i a_{ij} b_i$, so
$$\sum_j b^{(j)} \otimes k_j = \sum_j \sum_i b_i \otimes a_{ij}\, k_j = \sum_i b
_i \otimes \Big(\sum_j a_{ij} k_j\Big) \in \sum_{i=1}^{n} K\,(b_i \otimes 1).$$
Hence $B \otimes_A K = \sum_{i=1}^{n} K\,(b_i \otimes 1)$ is finitely generated
as a $K$-module.

**Step 2: $B \otimes_A K$ is an Artinian ring.**

Since $K$ is a field, a finitely generated $K$-module is a finite-dimensional $K
$-vector space. Thus $B \otimes_A K$ is a finite-dimensional $K$-algebra. Any de
scending chain of ideals in $B \otimes_A K$ is in particular a descending chain
of $K$-subspaces, which must stabilize (dimensions are non-negative integers bou
nded by $\dim_K(B \otimes_A K)$). Therefore $B \otimes_A K$ is Artinian.

**Step 3: An Artinian ring has only finitely many prime ideals.**

This is a standard theorem of commutative algebra. Briefly: in an Artinian ring
$R$, every prime ideal is maximal (since $R/\mathfrak{q}$ is an Artinian domain,
 hence a field). Moreover $R$ has finitely many maximal ideals: the Jacobson rad
ical $J(R)$ is nilpotent, and $R/J(R)$ is a finite product of fields (a semisimp
le Artinian ring), $\prod_{i=1}^{m} K_i$, whose maximal ideals $\prod_{j \neq i}
 K_j$ correspond bijectively to the maximal ideals of $R$. Since every prime ide
al is maximal, the set of prime ideals equals the set of maximal ideals, which i
s finite.

**Conclusion.** Applying Step 3 to the Artinian ring $B \otimes_A K$, we conclud
e that $B \otimes_A K$ has only finitely many prime ideals. $\blacksquare$
