## Proof

**Claim.** The unique probability density (in the generalized-function sense) sa
tisfying $\mathbb{E}[|X|]=0$ and $\mathbb{E}[X^2]=0$ is the Dirac delta $\delta(
x)$, concentrated at $x=0$. No classical (Lebesgue-integrable) PDF can satisfy b
oth conditions.

---

### Necessity: the distribution must be concentrated at $0$

Let $\mu$ be any probability measure on $\mathbb{R}$ (we work at the level of th
e induced measure, which is the most general setting). The two conditions are

$$
\mathbb{E}[|X|] = \int_{\mathbb{R}} |x|\,d\mu(x) = 0, \qquad
\mathbb{E}[X^2] = \int_{\mathbb{R}} x^2\,d\mu(x) = 0.
$$

Since the integrands $|x|\ge 0$ and $x^2\ge 0$ are non-negative and $\mu$ is a p
ositive measure, a non-negative integrand integrating to $0$ must vanish $\mu$-a
lmost everywhere. From the first condition,

$$
|x| = 0 \quad \mu\text{-a.e.} \implies x = 0 \quad \mu\text{-a.e.}
$$

so $\mu$ is supported on the singleton $\{0\}$. Since $\mu$ is a probability mea
sure ($\mu(\mathbb{R})=1$) and $\mu(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})=0$, we must have $
\mu(\{0\})=1$, i.e.

$$
\mu = \delta_0,
$$

the Dirac measure at the origin. (The second condition $\mathbb{E}[X^2]=0$ gives
 the same conclusion via $x^2=0\Rightarrow x=0$, so it is consistent but redunda
nt once the first holds.)

### No classical PDF exists

Suppose $f:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)$ is a classical PDF, i.e. $f\in L^1(\mathbb{R
})$ with $\int f\,dx = 1$, and $\mathbb{E}[|X|]=\int |x|f(x)\,dx = 0$. Then $|x|
f(x)=0$ Lebesgue-a.e., so $f(x)=0$ for a.e. $x\neq 0$. The set $\{0\}$ has Lebes
gue measure $0$, hence $f=0$ a.e. on all of $\mathbb{R}$, giving $\int f\,dx = 0
 \neq 1$. Contradiction. Therefore **no ordinary (Lebesgue-density) PDF can sati
sfy the conditions**; the only solution is a singular distribution.

### The Dirac delta $\delta(x)$ is the solution

The Dirac delta $\delta(x)$ is the generalized function (Schwartz distribution)
whose action on any test function $\varphi$ is

$$
\langle \delta, \varphi\rangle = \varphi(0).
$$

Equivalently, it is the density of the Dirac measure $\delta_0$ with respect to
the (formal) Lebesgue measure. It is a legitimate probability density in the gen
eralized sense because

$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}} \delta(x)\,dx = \langle \delta, 1\rangle = 1.
$$

Its moments are

$$
\mathbb{E}[|X|] = \int_{\mathbb{R}} |x|\,\delta(x)\,dx = |0| = 0,
$$
$$
\mathbb{E}[X^2] = \int_{\mathbb{R}} x^2\,\delta(x)\,dx = 0^2 = 0.
$$

Both expected values are exactly $0$, as required.

### Uniqueness

We showed above that any probability measure with $\mathbb{E}[|X|]=0$ must equal
 $\delta_0$. Hence $\delta(x)$ is the **unique** probability density (generalize
d) satisfying both conditions.

---

**Answer.** The required PDF is the Dirac delta function

$$
\boxed{\,f(x) = \delta(x)\,}
$$

the generalized probability density concentrated entirely at $x=0$. It is the un
ique such density: any probability distribution with $\mathbb{E}[|X|]=0$ (equiva
lently $\mathbb{E}[X^2]=0$) must be supported on $\{0\}$, forcing it to be $\del
ta_0$. No classical Lebesgue-density PDF can achieve this, since a function vani
shing a.e. off $\{0\}$ vanishes a.e. everywhere and cannot integrate to $1$.
