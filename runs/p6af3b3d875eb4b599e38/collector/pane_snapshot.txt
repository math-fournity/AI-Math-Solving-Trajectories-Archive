## Solution

**Claim:** The stalks of the presheaf $\mathcal{F}(U) = \prod_{z \in U} I_z$ are
 **not** exactly $I_x$ in general. There is a natural *surjective* homomorphism
$\mathcal{F}_x \twoheadrightarrow I_x$, but it fails to be injective whenever $x
$ is a non-isolated point (with nontrivial $I_z$ nearby).

---

### Setup

Let $X$ be a topological space and $\{I_z\}_{z \in X}$ a family of abelian group
s. Define the presheaf (in fact a sheaf) $\mathcal{F}$ by
$$\mathcal{F}(U) = \prod_{z \in U} I_z, \qquad \rho^U_V : \prod_{z \in U} I_z \t
o \prod_{z \in V} I_z \text{ (natural projection, for } V \subseteq U\text{)}.$$
The stalk at $x$ is
$$\mathcal{F}_x = \varinjlim_{x \in U} \mathcal{F}(U) = \varinjlim_{x \in U} \pr
od_{z \in U} I_z,$$
i.e., the set of germs $[(s_z)_{z \in U}]$ where two sections agree on some neig
hborhood of $x$.

### The natural map $\varphi_x : \mathcal{F}_x \to I_x$

Define $\varphi_x\bigl([(s_z)_{z \in U}]\bigr) = s_x$.

**Well-defined:** If $(s_z)_{z\in U} \sim (t_z)_{z \in V}$, they agree on some o
pen $W \ni x$ with $W \subseteq U \cap V$; in particular $s_x = t_x$.

**Group homomorphism:** Clear, since the group operation is componentwise.

**Surjective:** Given $a \in I_x$, take $U = X$ and the section $s$ with $s_x =
a$ and $s_z = 0$ for $z \neq x$. Then $\varphi_x([s]) = a$. ∎

### Failure of injectivity (counterexample)

Take $X = \{x, y\}$ with the **indiscrete topology** $\tau = \{\emptyset, X\}$,
so the only open neighborhood of $x$ is $X$ itself. Set $I_x = 0$ and $I_y = \ma
thbb{Z}$.

Then
$$\mathcal{F}_x = \mathcal{F}(X) = I_x \times I_y = 0 \times \mathbb{Z} \cong \m
athbb{Z},$$
since there is no smaller neighborhood to quotient by. The map $\varphi_x : \mat
hbb{Z} \to 0$ is the zero map — surjective but **not injective** (its kernel is
all of $\mathbb{Z}$). Hence $\mathcal{F}_x \cong \mathbb{Z} \not\cong 0 = I_x$.

**Even for nice spaces:** Let $X = \mathbb{R}$ (usual topology), $I_z = \mathbb{
Z}$ for all $z$, and pick $x \in \mathbb{R}$. Define the section $s \in \mathcal
{F}(\mathbb{R})$ by
$$s_z = \begin{cases} 0 & z = x, \\ 1 & z \neq x. \end{cases}$$
Then $\varphi_x([s]) = s_x = 0$, so $[s] \in \ker\varphi_x$. But $[s] \neq 0$: a
ny neighborhood $W$ of $x$ contains some $y \neq x$ (since $x$ is not isolated),
 and $s_y = 1 \neq 0$, so $s$ does not vanish on any neighborhood of $x$. Thus $
\ker\varphi_x \neq 0$ and $\mathcal{F}_x \not\cong I_x$.

### When does $\mathcal{F}_x \cong I_x$ hold?

$\varphi_x$ is an isomorphism **iff $x$ is an isolated point** (assuming some $I
_z$ near $x$ is nonzero). If $\{x\}$ is open, then $\mathcal{F}(\{x\}) = I_x$ is
 cofinal in the neighborhood system, so $\mathcal{F}_x = I_x$ directly. Converse
ly, if $x$ is not isolated, every neighborhood $U \ni x$ contains some $y \neq x
$; choosing $I_y \neq 0$ and a section that is $0$ at $x$ but nonzero at $y$ pro
duces a nonzero element of $\ker\varphi_x$.

### Conclusion

The stalks of $\mathcal{F}$ are **not** exactly $I_x$ in general. The natural ev
aluation map $\varphi_x : \mathcal{F}_x \to I_x$ is always a **surjective** grou
p homomorphism, but it is **injective only when $x$ is isolated** (or all nearby
 groups vanish). For any non-isolated point with nontrivial neighboring groups,
$\mathcal{F}_x$ is strictly larger than $I_x$.
