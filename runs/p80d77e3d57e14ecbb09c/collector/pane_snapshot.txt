## Proof

We model the fleet as a graph $G = (V, E)$ where ships are vertices and high-ris
k pairs are edges, with $|E| = k$. A partition into Route A and Route B is a 2-c
oloring $f: V \to \{0,1\}$. An edge is **uncut** (same route) if both endpoints
receive the same color; it is **cut** if endpoints receive different colors. We
seek the smallest constant $C$ such that for every graph $G$ with $k$ edges, the
re exists a partition with at most $Ck$ uncut edges.

### Upper bound: $C \le \tfrac{1}{2}$

Assign each vertex independently and uniformly at random to one of the two route
s. For any fixed edge $e = \{u,v\}$, the probability that $u$ and $v$ receive th
e same color is

$$\Pr[f(u) = f(v)] = \tfrac{1}{2}.$$

By linearity of expectation, the expected number of uncut edges is

$$\mathbb{E}[\text{uncut}] = \sum_{e \in E} \Pr[e \text{ uncut}] = \tfrac{k}{2}.
$$

Since the expected number of uncut edges over all $2^{|V|}$ partitions equals $k
/2$, there must exist at least one partition achieving at most $k/2$ uncut edges
. Hence every graph admits a partition with uncut $\le \tfrac{1}{2} k$, giving $
C \le \tfrac{1}{2}$.

### Lower bound: $C \ge \tfrac{1}{2}$

We show that no constant strictly smaller than $\tfrac{1}{2}$ suffices, by exhib
iting a family of graphs where the ratio (minimum uncut edges)$/k$ approaches $\
tfrac{1}{2}$.

Consider the complete graph $K_n$ on $n$ vertices, with $k = \binom{n}{2} = \fra
c{n(n-1)}{2}$ edges. A partition into parts of sizes $a$ and $n-a$ cuts exactly
$a(n-a)$ edges (all edges between the two parts), so the number of uncut edges i
s

$$\text{uncut} = \binom{n}{2} - a(n-a).$$

The number of cut edges $a(n-a)$ is maximized when $a$ is as close to $n/2$ as p
ossible, i.e., the **maximum cut** of $K_n$ has size $\lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor$. Th
erefore the minimum number of uncut edges is

$$\text{min uncut} = \binom{n}{2} - \left\lfloor \tfrac{n^2}{4}\right\rfloor.$$

Take $n = 2m$ even. Then $k = m(2m-1)$, $\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor = m^2$, and

$$\text{min uncut} = m(2m-1) - m^2 = m^2 - m = m(m-1).$$

The ratio is

$$\frac{\text{min uncut}}{k} = \frac{m(m-1)}{m(2m-1)} = \frac{m-1}{2m-1} \xright
arrow{m \to \infty} \frac{1}{2}.$$

Thus for every $C < \tfrac{1}{2}$, choosing $m$ sufficiently large yields a grap
h $K_{2m}$ for which every partition leaves more than $Ck$ edges uncut. Therefor
e no $C < \tfrac{1}{2}$ can serve as a universal guarantee.

### Conclusion

The upper bound shows $C = \tfrac{1}{2}$ always suffices, and the lower bound sh
ows no smaller constant suffices. Therefore the smallest possible value is

$$\boxed{C = \tfrac{1}{2}}.$$
