## Proof

**Statement:** For points $x$ and $y$ on a Riemannian manifold $M$ (within the i
njectivity radius so that the logarithmic maps are well-defined), $P^{x\rightarr
ow y}(\log_x y) = -\log_y x$.

**The statement is TRUE.**

### Setup

Let $v = \log_x y \in T_x M$. By definition of the logarithmic map, $v$ is the u
nique tangent vector at $x$ such that $\exp_x(v) = y$ and $\|v\| = d(x,y)$. Defi
ne the geodesic
$$\gamma(t) = \exp_x(t\,v), \quad t \in [0,1].$$
Then $\gamma(0) = x$, $\gamma(1) = y$, and $\gamma'(0) = v = \log_x y$.

### Step 1: Parallel transport of $\log_x y$

Since $\gamma$ is a geodesic, its velocity field $\gamma'(t)$ is **parallel alon
g $\gamma$** (this is one of the equivalent characterizations of a geodesic: $\n
abla_{\gamma'}\gamma' = 0$). The parallel transport operator $P^{x \rightarrow y
}$ along $\gamma$ maps any parallel vector field $V(t)$ along $\gamma$ to its va
lue at the endpoint: $P^{x\rightarrow y}(V(0)) = V(1)$. Applying this to $V(t) =
 \gamma'(t)$:
$$P^{x\rightarrow y}(\log_x y) = P^{x\rightarrow y}(\gamma'(0)) = \gamma'(1).$$

### Step 2: Computing $\log_y x$

The reversed curve $\tilde{\gamma}(t) = \gamma(1 - t)$ for $t \in [0,1]$ is also
 a geodesic (the reparametrization of a geodesic by an affine change of paramete
r is again a geodesic), with:
- $\tilde{\gamma}(0) = \gamma(1) = y$,
- $\tilde{\gamma}(1) = \gamma(0) = x$,
- $\tilde{\gamma}'(0) = -\gamma'(1)$.

Since geodesics have constant speed, $\|\tilde{\gamma}'(0)\| = \|\gamma'(1)\| =
\|\gamma'(0)\| = \|v\| = d(x,y)$. Therefore $\tilde{\gamma}'(0)$ is the unique v
ector $w \in T_y M$ with $\exp_y(w) = x$ and $\|w\| = d(x,y)$, i.e.,
$$\log_y x = \tilde{\gamma}'(0) = -\gamma'(1).$$

### Step 3: Conclusion

Combining Steps 1 and 2:
$$P^{x\rightarrow y}(\log_x y) = \gamma'(1) = -(-\gamma'(1)) = -\log_y x.$$

$$\boxed{P^{x\rightarrow y}(\log_x y) = -\log_y x}$$

### Verification on a non-trivial example (sphere $S^2$)

On the unit sphere, let $x$ and $y$ be separated by angle $\theta$ along a great
 circle. Then $\log_x y$ is the tangent vector at $x$ of length $\theta$ along t
he great circle toward $y$. Parallel transporting this along the great circle to
 $y$ yields the tangent vector at $y$ of length $\theta$ pointing in the directi
on of continued travel along the great circle (i.e., away from $x$). Meanwhile,
$\log_y x$ is the tangent vector at $y$ of length $\theta$ pointing back toward
$x$ along the great circle. These are indeed negatives of each other, confirming
 the result.

### Key ingredients

1. **Definition of $\log$**: $\log_x y = \gamma'(0)$ where $\gamma$ is the minim
izing geodesic from $x$ to $y$.
2. **Geodesic velocity is parallel**: $\nabla_{\gamma'}\gamma' = 0$ implies $P^{
x\rightarrow y}(\gamma'(0)) = \gamma'(1)$.
3. **Reversal of geodesics**: The geodesic from $y$ to $x$ is $\gamma(1-t)$, giv
ing $\log_y x = -\gamma'(1)$.
