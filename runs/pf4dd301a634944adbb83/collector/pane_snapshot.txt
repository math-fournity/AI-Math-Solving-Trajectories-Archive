## Proof

We use the classical Fourier series identity. Consider the function $f(x) = \fra
c{\pi - x}{2}$ on the interval $(0, 2\pi)$, extended $2\pi$-periodically. We com
pute its Fourier sine coefficients:

$$b_n = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} \frac{\pi - x}{2}\sin(nx)\,dx = \frac{1}{2\pi
}\int_0^{2\pi}(\pi - x)\sin(nx)\,dx.$$

Splitting the integral:

$$\int_0^{2\pi}(\pi-x)\sin(nx)\,dx = \pi\underbrace{\int_0^{2\pi}\sin(nx)\,dx}_{
=\,0} - \int_0^{2\pi}x\sin(nx)\,dx.$$

For the remaining integral, integrate by parts:

$$\int_0^{2\pi}x\sin(nx)\,dx = \left[-\frac{x\cos(nx)}{n}\right]_0^{2\pi} + \int
_0^{2\pi}\frac{\cos(nx)}{n}\,dx = -\frac{2\pi\cos(2n\pi)}{n} + 0 = -\frac{2\pi}{
n}.$$

Therefore

$$b_n = \frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{2\pi}{n} = \frac{1}{n}.$$

Since $f(x) = \frac{\pi-x}{2}$ is piecewise smooth and continuous on $(0,2\pi)$,
 the Fourier series converges to $f(x)$ at every point of continuity:

$$\frac{\pi - x}{2} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(nx)}{n}, \qquad 0 < x < 2\pi
.$$

Setting $x = 1$ (which lies in $(0, 2\pi)$):

$$S = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin n}{n} = \frac{\pi - 1}{2}.$$

Finally,

$$2S + 1 = 2\cdot\frac{\pi - 1}{2} + 1 = (\pi - 1) + 1 = \pi.$$

$$\boxed{2S + 1 = \pi}$$
