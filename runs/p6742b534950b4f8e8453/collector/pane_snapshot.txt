## Solution

**Setup.** Work over $R = \mathbb{Z}$. Recall the tensor product of chain comple
xes:
$$(A \otimes B)_n = \bigoplus_{p+q=n} A_p \otimes B_q, \qquad \partial^{A\otimes
 B}_n = \sum_{p+q=n} \partial^A_p \otimes \mathrm{id} + (-1)^p\,\mathrm{id}\otim
es \partial^B_q.$$

---

### 1. $H_*(C)$ and $H_*(D)$

$C = D$ has $C_0 = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ and $C_p = 0$ for $p \neq 0$. All bou
ndary maps are zero (only one nonzero term). Hence $H_p(C) = C_p$:

$$H_0(C) = H_0(D) = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \qquad H_p(C) = H_p(D) = 0 \text{ fo
r } p \neq 0.$$

### 2. $H_*(C')$

$C'$ has $C'_0 = \mathbb{Z}$, $C'_1 = \mathbb{Z}$, $\partial'_1 = \times 2$, and
 $C'_p = 0$ otherwise.

- **Degree 1:** $\ker(\partial'_1) = \ker(\times 2 : \mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}) =
0$, $\mathrm{im}(\partial'_2) = 0$. So $H_1(C') = 0$.
- **Degree 0:** $\ker(\partial'_0) = \mathbb{Z}$ (since $C'_{-1}=0$), $\mathrm{i
m}(\partial'_1) = 2\mathbb{Z}$. So $H_0(C') = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.
- **Other degrees:** $H_p(C') = 0$.

$$H_0(C') = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \qquad H_p(C') = 0 \text{ for } p \neq 0.$$

**Key observation:** $H_*(C) \cong H_*(C') \cong H_*(D)$ — all three agree: $\ma
thbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ in degree 0, zero elsewhere.

### 3. $H_*(C \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} D)$

Since $C_p \neq 0$ only at $p=0$ and $D_q \neq 0$ only at $q=0$:
$$(C\otimes D)_n = C_0 \otimes D_0 \text{ if } n=0, \quad 0 \text{ otherwise.}$$
$$(C\otimes D)_0 = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \cong \
mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.$$

All boundary maps of $C$ and $D$ are zero, so all boundary maps of $C\otimes D$
are zero. Therefore:
$$H_0(C\otimes D) = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \qquad H_p(C\otimes D) = 0 \text{ fo
r } p \neq 0.$$

### 4. $H_*(C' \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} D)$

Since $D_q \neq 0$ only at $q=0$:
$$(C'\otimes D)_n = C'_n \otimes D_0 = C'_n \otimes \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.$$

- $(C'\otimes D)_0 = \mathbb{Z}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\
mathbb{Z}$.
- $(C'\otimes D)_1 = \mathbb{Z}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\
mathbb{Z}$.
- $(C'\otimes D)_n = 0$ for $n\neq 0,1$.

The boundary $\partial^{C'\otimes D}_1 = \partial'_1 \otimes \mathrm{id}_{D_0}$
(the $D$-part is zero). Under the identification $\mathbb{Z}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/2\
mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$:
$$a\otimes\bar{b} \;\longmapsto\; 2a\otimes\bar{b} = a\otimes\overline{2b} = a\o
times\bar{0} = 0.$$

**The boundary map $\partial^{C'\otimes D}_1$ is the zero map.** (Multiplication
 by 2 on $\mathbb{Z}$ becomes zero after tensoring with $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$
.)

Therefore:
- $H_1(C'\otimes D) = \ker(\partial_1)/\mathrm{im}(\partial_2) = \mathbb{Z}/2\ma
thbb{Z}/\,0 = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.
- $H_0(C'\otimes D) = \ker(\partial_0)/\mathrm{im}(\partial_1) = \mathbb{Z}/2\ma
thbb{Z}/\,0 = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.
- $H_p(C'\otimes D) = 0$ for $p\neq 0,1$.

$$H_0(C'\otimes D) = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \quad H_1(C'\otimes D) = \mathbb{Z}
/2\mathbb{Z}, \quad H_p(C'\otimes D) = 0 \text{ otherwise.}$$

### 5. Conclusion: homology of the tensor product is not determined by the homol
ogy of the factors

We have established:

| Complex | $H_0$ | $H_1$ | others |
|---|---|---|---|
| $C$ | $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ | $0$ | $0$ |
| $C'$ | $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ | $0$ | $0$ |
| $D$ | $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ | $0$ | $0$ |
| $C\otimes D$ | $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ | $0$ | $0$ |
| $C'\otimes D$ | $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ | $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ | $0$ |

The complexes $C$ and $C'$ have **identical homology** ($H_*(C) \cong H_*(C')$),
 and $D$ is the same in both tensor products. Yet:

$$H_*(C \otimes D) \;\not\cong\; H_*(C' \otimes D),$$

since $H_1(C'\otimes D) = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \neq 0 = H_1(C\otimes D)$.

**Therefore the homology of the tensor product of two chain complexes is not det
ermined by the homology groups of the individual complexes.** The chain-level st
ructure matters: the nontrivial boundary map $\partial'_1 = \times 2$ in $C'$ is
 invisible at the homology level (it produces the same $H_*$ as the zero-differe
ntial complex $C$), but it becomes visible after tensoring with $D = \mathbb{Z}/
2\mathbb{Z}$, because tensoring kills the injectivity of $\times 2$ (turning it
into the zero map) and thereby creates an extra homology class in degree 1.

This is precisely the phenomenon captured by the Künneth theorem's $\mathrm{Tor}
$ term: the short exact sequence
$$0 \to \bigoplus_p H_p(C)\otimes H_q(D) \to H_n(C\otimes D) \to \bigoplus_p \ma
thrm{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(H_p(C), H_q(D)) \to 0$$
includes $\mathrm{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/2\mathb
b{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, which is exactly the extra degree-1 class a
ppearing in $H_*(C'\otimes D)$ but absent from $H_*(C\otimes D)$. The $\mathrm{T
or}$ term depends on the *complex* (not just its homology), which is why two com
plexes with the same homology can yield different tensor-product homologies.
