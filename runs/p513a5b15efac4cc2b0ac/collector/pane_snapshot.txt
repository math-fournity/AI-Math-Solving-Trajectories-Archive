Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every additive, covariant f
unctor preserves direct sums.

**Answer: No.** Additive covariant functors always preserve *finite* direct sums
, but need not preserve *infinite* direct sums. Here is a complete proof with a
counterexample.

---

## Proof

### Part 1: Additive functors preserve finite direct sums

In an additive category, finite direct sums coincide with finite products (bipro
ducts). Let $F: \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}$ be an additive functor between addi
tive categories. Since $F$ is additive, it preserves the zero object ($F(0) \con
g 0$) and preserves addition on Hom-sets: $F(f + g) = F(f) + F(g)$.

Given a biproduct $A \oplus B$ with injections $i_A, i_B$ and projections $p_A,
p_B$ satisfying $p_A i_A = \text{id}_A$, $p_B i_B = \text{id}_B$, $p_A i_B = 0$,
 $p_B i_A = 0$, and $i_A p_A + i_B p_B = \text{id}_{A \oplus B}$, applying $F$ y
ields $F(p_A)F(i_A) = \text{id}_{F(A)}$, $F(p_B)F(i_B) = \text{id}_{F(B)}$, $F(p
_A)F(i_B) = 0$, $F(p_B)F(i_A) = 0$, and $F(i_A)F(p_A) + F(i_B)F(p_B) = \text{id}
_{F(A \oplus B)}$. These are exactly the biproduct equations, so $F(A \oplus B)
\cong F(A) \oplus F(B)$. By induction, $F$ preserves all finite direct sums.

### Part 2: A counterexample for infinite direct sums

Let $\mathcal{A} = \mathbf{Ab}$ (abelian groups). Let $M = \bigoplus_{k=0}^{\inf
ty} \mathbb{Z}$, the free abelian group on generators $\{e_k\}_{k \geq 0}$. Defi
ne
$$F: \mathbf{Ab} \to \mathbf{Ab}, \qquad F(A) = \text{Hom}(M, A).$$
This is **additive** (since $\text{Hom}(M, -)$ is additive in the second variabl
e: $\text{Hom}(M, f+g) = \text{Hom}(M,f) + \text{Hom}(M,g)$) and **covariant**.

Consider the family $\{N_i\}_{i \geq 0}$ with $N_i = \mathbb{Z}$ for all $i$. Th
e direct sum is $\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty} \mathbb{Z}$, which we identify with $M
$ itself.

There is a canonical homomorphism (always injective):
$$\Phi: \bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty} \text{Hom}(M, N_i) \longrightarrow \text{Hom}\!
\left(M,\, \bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty} N_i\right) = \text{Hom}(M, M).$$

**Claim:** $\Phi$ is not surjective.

Consider the identity map $\text{id}_M \in \text{Hom}(M, M)$. Suppose, for contr
adiction, that $\text{id}_M = \Phi\!\left(\sum_{i \in I_0} f_i\right)$ for some
**finite** subset $I_0 \subset \mathbb{N}$ and $f_i \in \text{Hom}(M, \mathbb{Z}
)$. Then for every $x \in M$:
$$\text{id}_M(x) = \sum_{i \in I_0} \iota_i\!\big(f_i(x)\big),$$
where $\iota_i: \mathbb{Z} \hookrightarrow \bigoplus_i \mathbb{Z}$ is the inclus
ion. This means the image of $\text{id}_M$ is contained in the finite sub-sum $\
bigoplus_{i \in I_0} \mathbb{Z}$.

But take $x = e_j$ for any $j \notin I_0$. Then $\text{id}_M(e_j) = e_j$, whose
support is $\{j\}$, which is **not** contained in $I_0$. This is a contradiction
.

Therefore $\text{id}_M \notin \operatorname{im}(\Phi)$, so $\Phi$ is not surject
ive, and
$$F\!\left(\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty} N_i\right) = \text{Hom}(M, M) \;\not\cong\;
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty} \text{Hom}(M, N_i) = \bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty} F(N_i).$$

### Conclusion

The functor $F = \text{Hom}(M, -)$ with $M = \bigoplus_{k \geq 0} \mathbb{Z}$ is
 additive and covariant, yet it fails to preserve the infinite direct sum $\bigo
plus_{i \geq 0} \mathbb{Z}$. Therefore, **not every additive covariant functor p
reserves direct sums**. Additive functors preserve finite direct sums automatica
lly, but preservation of infinite direct sums requires an additional condition (
e.g., the functor being a left adjoint, or the relevant module being finitely ge
nerated in the $\text{Hom}$ case).
