## Proof

**Reduction.** Since the condition $\sum_{a\in S} a \equiv 0 \pmod{p^k}$ and the
 parity of $|S|$ depend only on each $a \bmod p^k$, we may replace every element
 of $\mathcal{A}$ by its residue in $\{0,1,\dots,p^k-1\}$; the quantities $N_{\t
ext{even}}, N_{\text{odd}}$ are unchanged. Write $\mathcal{A}=\{a_1,\dots,a_n\}$
 with $n\ge p^k$ and $a_i\in\{0,\dots,p^k-1\}$.

**Trivial case.** If some $a_i=0$, then for every subset $S$ not containing $a_i
$, the pair $S$ and $S\cup\{a_i\}$ have equal sum modulo $p^k$ but opposite pari
ty, so they cancel in $N_{\text{even}}-N_{\text{odd}}$. Hence $D:=N_{\text{even}
}-N_{\text{odd}}=0$ and we are done. Thus assume all $a_i\neq 0$.

**Generating polynomial.** Set $P(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-x^{a_i})\in\mathbb{Z}[x]$
. Expanding, $P(x)=\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal{A}}(-1)^{|S|}x^{\sum_{a\in S}a}$. Le
t $Q(x)=P(x)\bmod (x^{p^k}-1)$, of degree $<p^k$. Then the constant coefficient
of $Q$ collects exactly the coefficients of $P$ at exponents $\equiv 0\pmod{p^k}
$:
$$[x^0]\,Q(x)=\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq\mathcal{A}\\\sum_S a\equiv 0\,(p^k)}}(-
1)^{|S|}=D.$$
So it suffices to prove $[x^0]\,Q(x)\equiv 0\pmod p$.

**Valuation in $\mathbb{F}_p[x]$.** Work in $\mathbb{F}_p[x]$, where by the Fres
hman's dream $x^{p^k}-1=(x-1)^{p^k}$. We compute the $(x-1)$-adic valuation of e
ach factor $1-x^{a_i}$.

Write $a_i=p^{t_i}m_i$ with $p\nmid m_i$ and $t_i=v_p(a_i)\ge 0$. Then
$$1-x^{a_i}=1-(x^{p^{t_i}})^{m_i}=(1-x^{p^{t_i}})\bigl(1+x^{p^{t_i}}+\cdots+x^{p
^{t_i}(m_i-1)}\bigr).$$
In $\mathbb{F}_p$, $1-x^{p^{t_i}}=-(x^{p^{t_i}}-1)=-(x-1)^{p^{t_i}}$, so $v_{(x-
1)}(1-x^{p^{t_i}})=p^{t_i}$. The second factor evaluates to $m_i\not\equiv 0\pmo
d p$ at $x=1$, hence is a unit at $(x-1)$. Therefore
$$v_{(x-1)}(1-x^{a_i})=p^{t_i}=p^{v_p(a_i)}\ge 1.$$

Summing over all factors,
$$v_{(x-1)}\bigl(P(x)\bigr)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p^{v_p(a_i)}\ge\sum_{i=1}^{n}1=n\ge p^
k.$$

**Conclusion.** Since $v_{(x-1)}(P)\ge p^k$, we have $P(x)\equiv 0\pmod{(x-1)^{p
^k}}$ in $\mathbb{F}_p[x]$. Because $Q\equiv P\pmod{x^{p^k}-1}=(x-1)^{p^k}$, it
follows that $Q(x)\equiv 0\pmod{(x-1)^{p^k}}$. But $\deg Q<p^k=\deg(x-1)^{p^k}$,
 so the only way $Q$ is divisible by $(x-1)^{p^k}$ is $Q\equiv 0$ in $\mathbb{F}
_p[x]$. In particular every coefficient of $Q$ vanishes modulo $p$, so
$$D=N_{\text{even}}-N_{\text{odd}}=[x^0]\,Q(x)\equiv 0\pmod p.$$

Hence $N_{\text{even}}\equiv N_{\text{odd}}\pmod p$. $\blacksquare$
