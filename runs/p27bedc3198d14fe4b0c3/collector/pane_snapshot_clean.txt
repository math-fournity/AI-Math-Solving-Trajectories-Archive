## Solution

We minimize $f(a) = \int_0^1 |e^{-x} - a|\,dx$ over $a \in \mathbb{R}$.

**Convexity and the critical point.** Since $|e^{-x}-a|$ is convex in $a$, so is
 $f(a)$. For $a \in (e^{-1}, 1)$ (the range of $e^{-x}$ on $[0,1]$), differentia
ting under the integral:

$$f'(a) = \int_0^1 \operatorname{sgn}(a - e^{-x})\,dx.$$

Now $a - e^{-x} > 0 \iff x > -\ln a$. Thus $\operatorname{sgn}=+1$ on $(-\ln a,\
, 1)$ of length $1+\ln a$, and $\operatorname{sgn}=-1$ on $(0,\, -\ln a)$ of len
gth $-\ln a$. Hence

$$f'(a) = (1+\ln a) - (-\ln a) = 1 + 2\ln a.$$

Setting $f'(a)=0$ gives $\ln a = -\tfrac12$, i.e. $a = e^{-1/2}$. Convexity guar
antees this is the global minimizer.

**Evaluating the minimum.** The crossover $e^{-x}=e^{-1/2}$ occurs at $x=\tfrac1
2$. Splitting:

$$\int_0^{1/2}(e^{-x}-e^{-1/2})\,dx = \bigl(1-e^{-1/2}\bigr) - \tfrac12 e^{-1/2}
 = 1 - \tfrac32 e^{-1/2},$$

$$\int_{1/2}^{1}(e^{-1/2}-e^{-x})\,dx = \tfrac12 e^{-1/2} - \bigl(e^{-1/2}-e^{-1
}\bigr) = e^{-1} - \tfrac12 e^{-1/2}.$$

Adding:

$$f(e^{-1/2}) = 1 - \tfrac32 e^{-1/2} + e^{-1} - \tfrac12 e^{-1/2} = 1 - 2e^{-1/
2} + e^{-1} = \bigl(1 - e^{-1/2}\bigr)^2.$$

**Minimum value:**

$$\boxed{\left(1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{e}}\right)^2}$$
