## Proof

**Claim.** The Arzela-Ascoli theorem holds: every equicontinuous sequence $\{f_n
\}$ of functions from a compact metric space $E$ to a compact subspace $K$ of a
normed space $X$ admits a subsequence that converges uniformly to a continuous f
unction $f:E\to K$. No separate boundedness hypothesis is required.

### Step 1. Compactness of $K$ supplies boundedness automatically.

$X$ is a normed space, hence a metric space, hence Hausdorff. A compact subset o

. Therefore $K$ is closed and bounded in $X$:

$$\exists\, M>0 \quad\text{such that}\quad \|y\|\le M \;\;\forall\,y\in K.$$

Since each $f_n$ maps $E$ into $K$,

$$\|f_n(x)\|\le M \qquad \forall\,n\in\mathbb{N},\;\forall\,x\in E.$$

Thus $\{f_n\}$ is **uniformly bounded** — the boundedness condition of the class
ical theorem is satisfied for free.

Equicontinuity of the family $\{f_n\}$ means: for every $\varepsilon>0$ there ex
ists $\delta>0$ such that $d_E(x,x')<\delta \Rightarrow \|f_n(x)-f_n(x')\|<\vare
psilon$ for all $n$ and all $x,x'\in E$. In particular each $f_n$ is continuous,
 so $f_n\in C(E,X)$.

### Step 3. Apply the classical Arzela-Ascoli theorem (Banach-space-valued versi

**Theorem (Arzela-Ascoli).** Let $E$ be a compact metric space and $X$ a Banach
space. A subset $\mathcal{F}\subset C(E,X)$ is relatively compact in the sup-nor
m topology if and only if $\mathcal{F}$ is equicontinuous and pointwise bounded.

Our sequence $\{f_n\}\subset C(E,X)$ is equicontinuous (given) and uniformly bou
nded (Step 1), hence pointwise bounded. By the theorem, the set $\{f_n:n\in\math
bb{N}\}$ is relatively compact in $\bigl(C(E,X),\|\cdot\|_\infty\bigr)$. Therefo

$$\|f_{n_k}-f\|_\infty \to 0 \qquad (k\to\infty).$$

### Step 4. The limit maps $E$ into $K$.

$K$ is compact in the Hausdorff space $X$, hence $K$ is **closed** in $X$. For e
ach $x\in E$, we have $f_{n_k}(x)\in K$ for every $k$, and uniform convergence i

$$f(x)=\lim_{k\to\infty} f_{n_k}(x).$$

Since $K$ is closed and $f_{n_k}(x)\in K$, the limit $f(x)\in K$. This holds for

The compactness of the codomain $K$ automatically guarantees:
