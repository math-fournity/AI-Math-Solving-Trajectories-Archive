**Step 1: The product group $G = \prod_n G_n$ is a compact metrizable (hence Hau

Each $G_n$ is compact and metrizable. By Tychonoff's theorem, the product $G$ is
 compact. A countable product of metrizable spaces is metrizable (e.g., via $d((
x_n),(y_n)) = \sum_n 2^{-n}\min(1, d_n(x_n,y_n))$). Coordinatewise multiplicatio
n makes $G$ a topological group. So $G$ is a compact metrizable group.

**Step 2: The product measure $\mu = \bigotimes_n \mu_n$ is a right-invariant pr
obability Borel measure on $G$.**

It is a probability measure (product of probability measures). It is defined on
the product $\sigma$-algebra, which coincides with the Borel $\sigma$-algebra of
 $G$ (countable product of second-countable spaces is second-countable, and cyli
nder sets generate the Borel $\sigma$-algebra). It is regular (product of regula
r Borel probability measures on compact metric spaces is regular).

For right-invariance, consider the $\pi$-system of cylinder sets $\mathcal{C} =
\{\prod_{n\in F} A_n \times \prod_{n\notin F} G_n : F \subset \mathbb{N} \text{
finite}, A_n \in \mathcal{B}(G_n)\}$. For $g = (g_n) \in G$ and a cylinder set $
C = \prod_{n\in F} A_n \times \prod_{n\notin F} G_n$, the right-translate is $C\
cdot g = \prod_{n\in F}(A_n \cdot g_n) \times \prod_{n\notin F} G_n$, so
$$\mu(C \cdot g) = \prod_{n\in F} \mu_n(A_n \cdot g_n) = \prod_{n\in F} \mu_n(A_
n) = \mu(C),$$
using right-invariance of each $\mu_n$. The measure $A \mapsto \mu(A \cdot g)$ a
grees with $\mu$ on the $\pi$-system $\mathcal{C}$, so by the $\pi$-$\lambda$ th
eorem (Dynkin), they agree on $\sigma(\mathcal{C}) = \mathcal{B}(G)$. Hence $\mu
$ is right-invariant.

**Step 3: A compact group admits a unique right-invariant regular Borel probabil
ity measure.**

This is the classical uniqueness theorem for Haar measure on compact groups. We
give the standard argument. Let $\mu, \nu$ be two right-invariant regular Borel
probability measures on a compact group $G$. We show $\int f\,d\mu = \int f\,d\n
u$ for every continuous $f: G \to \mathbb{R}$, which by the Riesz representation
 theorem gives $\mu = \nu$.

First, note that any right-invariant measure on a compact group is also left-inv
ariant. Indeed, the modular function $\Delta: G \to \mathbb{R}_{>0}$ is a contin
uous homomorphism; since $G$ is compact, $\Delta(G)$ is a compact subgroup of $\
mathbb{R}_{>0}$, hence $\Delta \equiv 1$, so $G$ is unimodular.

Now compute, using Fubini (valid since $G$ is compact and $f$ is continuous, so
all integrals are finite):
$$\int_G f\,d\mu = \int_G\left(\int_G f(xy)\,d\mu(x)\right)d\nu(y).$$
For fixed $y$, the map $x \mapsto xy$ is right-translation, so by right-invarian
ce of $\mu$, $\int_G f(xy)\,d\mu(x) = \int_G f\,d\mu$. Thus the expression equal
s $\int_G f\,d\mu$.

Switching the order of integration:
$$\int_G\int_G f(xy)\,d\mu(x)\,d\nu(y) = \int_G\int_G f(xy)\,d\nu(y)\,d\mu(x).$$
For fixed $x$, the map $y \mapsto xy$ is left-translation, so by left-invariance
 of $\nu$ (established above), $\int_G f(xy)\,d\nu(y) = \int_G f\,d\nu$. Thus th
e expression equals $\int_G f\,d\nu$.

Combining: $\int_G f\,d\mu = \int_G f\,d\nu$ for all continuous $f$, hence $\mu
= \nu$.

By Step 1, $G = \prod_n G_n$ is a compact group. By Step 3, $G$ has a unique rig

is such a measure. Therefore $\bigotimes_n \mu_n$ is the unique probability righ
t-invariant Haar measure on $\prod_n G_n$.

irst, it guarantees the modular function is trivial (unimodularity), which enter

vailable because each $G_n$ is compact) ensures the product measure is a probabi
