**Answer: Yes** for $1 \le p < \infty$ (and **No** for $p = \infty$).

---

## Proof for $1 \le p < \infty$

**Setup.** Assume $f_n \to f$ in measure on $\mathbb{R}$ and $\|f_n\|_{L^p} = \|
f\|_{L^p}$ for every $n$. We show $\|f_n - f\|_{L^p} \to 0$.

**Key inequality.** Since $t \mapsto t^p$ is convex for $p \ge 1$, the triangle-
inequality-type bound
$$|a - b|^p \le 2^{p-1}\bigl(|a|^p + |b|^p\bigr)$$
holds pointwise. Applying it with $a = f_n(x)$, $b = f(x)$ gives
$$|f_n - f|^p \le 2^{p-1}\bigl(|f_n|^p + |f|^p\bigr).$$

**Define the non-negative defect**
$$g_n := 2^{p-1}\bigl(|f_n|^p + |f|^p\bigr) - |f_n - f|^p \;\ge\; 0.$$

**Convergence in measure of $g_n$.** Convergence in measure is preserved under c
ontinuous maps, so from $f_n \to f$ in measure we get $|f_n - f|^p \to 0$ in mea
sure and $|f_n|^p \to |f|^p$ in measure. Hence
$$g_n \;\xrightarrow{\text{meas}}\; 2^{p-1}\bigl(|f|^p + |f|^p\bigr) - 0 \;=\; 2
^p|f|^p.$$

**Apply Fatou's lemma for convergence in measure.** The standard Fatou lemma ext
ends to convergence in measure: if $g_n \ge 0$ and $g_n \to g$ in measure, then
$\int g \le \liminf_n \int g_n$. (Proof: pass to a subsequence realizing the $\l
iminf$, then extract a further a.e.-convergent sub-subsequence and apply the cla
ssical Fatou lemma.) Applying it,
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} 2^p|f|^p \;\le\; \liminf_{n\to\infty} \int_{\mathbb{R}} g_n.
$$

**Compute the integrals.** Using $\|f_n\|_{L^p} = \|f\|_{L^p}$, i.e. $\int|f_n|^
p = \int|f|^p$:
$$\int g_n = 2^{p-1}\bigl(\|f_n\|_p^p + \|f\|_p^p\bigr) - \|f_n - f\|_p^p = 2^{p
-1}\cdot 2\|f\|_p^p - \|f_n - f\|_p^p = 2^p\|f\|_p^p - \|f_n - f\|_p^p.$$

**Combine.** Substituting,
$$2^p\|f\|_p^p \;\le\; \liminf_{n\to\infty}\Bigl[2^p\|f\|_p^p - \|f_n - f\|_p^p\
Bigr] = 2^p\|f\|_p^p - \limsup_{n\to\infty}\|f_n - f\|_p^p.$$

Rearranging,
$$\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|f_n - f\|_p^p \;\le\; 0.$$

Since $\|f_n - f\|_p^p \ge 0$, this forces $\|f_n - f\|_p^p \to 0$, i.e.
$$\|f_n - f\|_{L^p(\mathbb{R})} \to 0. \qquad\blacksquare$$

---

## Counterexample for $p = \infty$

The implication **fails** when $p = \infty$. Take on $\mathbb{R}$:
$$f = \mathbf{1}_{[0,1]}, \qquad f_n = \mathbf{1}_{[1/n,\,1]}.$$

- **Norms match:** $\|f_n\|_{L^\infty} = 1 = \|f\|_{L^\infty}$ for every $n$.
- **Convergence in measure:** $|f_n - f| = \mathbf{1}_{[0,\,1/n)}$, so for any $
\varepsilon > 0$,
  $$\mu\bigl(\{|f_n - f| > \varepsilon\}\bigr) = \tfrac{1}{n} \to 0.$$
- **No $L^\infty$ convergence:** $\|f_n - f\|_{L^\infty} = 1$ for every $n$, so
$\|f_n - f\|_{L^\infty} \not\to 0$.

Thus the equality of norms plus convergence in measure does **not** force $L^\in
fty$ convergence.

---

**Summary.** For $1 \le p < \infty$, convergence in measure together with $\|f_n
\|_{L^p} = \|f\|_{L^p}$ implies $f_n \to f$ in $L^p$. The result fails for $p =
\infty$.
