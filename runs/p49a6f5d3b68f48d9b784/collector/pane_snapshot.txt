# Solution: Gas Vibrations in a Circular Cylinder with Oscillating Lateral Wall

## Setup

Let $a$ be the speed of sound in the gas. We describe the motion via the **veloc
ity potential** $\Phi(r,\phi,z,t)$, so that the particle velocity is $\mathbf{v}
=\nabla\Phi$ and the pressure fluctuation is $p=-\rho_0\,\partial\Phi/\partial t
$. The potential satisfies the wave equation

$$\nabla^2\Phi = \frac{1}{a^2}\frac{\partial^2\Phi}{\partial t^2}.$$

**Boundary conditions.** The ends $z=0$ and $z=l$ are rigid (no normal velocity)
:

$$\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial z}\bigg|_{z=0}=0,\qquad \frac{\partial\Phi}{\part
ial z}\bigg|_{z=l}=0.$$

The lateral wall $r=r_0$ oscillates radially with velocity $f(z)\cos n\phi\cos\o
mega t$, so the gas velocity in the $r$-direction at the wall matches the wall v
elocity:

$$\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial r}\bigg|_{r=r_0}=f(z)\cos n\phi\cos\omega t,\qqua
d n>0\text{ integer}.$$

## Steady-State Reduction

Since the forcing is harmonic $\cos\omega t$ for all $t>0$ and no initial condit
ions are prescribed, we seek the **steady-state forced response**

$$\Phi(r,\phi,z,t)=\Psi(r,\phi,z)\cos\omega t.$$

Substituting into the wave equation gives the **Helmholtz equation**

$$\nabla^2\Psi + k^2\Psi = 0,\qquad k=\frac{\omega}{a},$$

with boundary conditions

$$\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial z}\bigg|_{z=0,l}=0,\qquad \frac{\partial\Psi}{\pa
rtial r}\bigg|_{r=r_0}=f(z)\cos n\phi.$$

## Separation of Variables

The forcing and geometry select the azimuthal mode $\cos n\phi$ (the $\sin n\phi
$ component vanishes by symmetry). The Neumann conditions in $z$ select the axia
l eigenfunctions $\cos(m\pi z/l)$, $m=0,1,2,\dots$. We therefore write

$$\Psi(r,\phi,z)=\cos n\phi\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}A_m\,R_m(r)\cos\frac{m\pi z}{l}.$$

Substituting into the Helmholtz equation, each radial function $R_m$ satisfies

$$R_m''+\frac{1}{r}R_m'+\left(\kappa_m^2-\frac{n^2}{r^2}\right)R_m=0,\qquad \kap
pa_m^2=k^2-\left(\frac{m\pi}{l}\right)^2.$$

This is **Bessel's equation of order $n$**. Regularity at $r=0$ (the axis is inc
luded in the domain) selects:

- **Propagating modes** ($\kappa_m^2>0$): $\;R_m(r)=J_n(\kappa_m r)$,
- **Evanescent modes** ($\kappa_m^2<0$): $\;\mu_m=\sqrt{(m\pi/l)^2-k^2}$, $\;R_m
(r)=I_n(\mu_m r)$ (modified Bessel function, regular at the origin).

## Determining the Coefficients

Expand the wall profile in the axial cosine series:

$$f(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}f_m\cos\frac{m\pi z}{l},\qquad f_0=\frac{1}{l}\int_0^l
 f(z)\,dz,\quad f_m=\frac{2}{l}\int_0^l f(z)\cos\frac{m\pi z}{l}\,dz\;(m\ge1).$$

Applying the wall condition $\partial\Psi/\partial r|_{r=r_0}=f(z)\cos n\phi$ an
d using orthogonality of $\cos(m\pi z/l)$:

$$A_m\,\kappa_m\,J_n'(\kappa_m r_0)=f_m \quad\Longrightarrow\quad A_m=\frac{f_m}
{\kappa_m\,J_n'(\kappa_m r_0)}$$

for propagating modes, and $A_m=f_m/[\mu_m\,I_n'(\mu_m r_0)]$ for evanescent mod
es.

## Solution

The velocity potential of the gas vibrations is

$$\boxed{\Phi(r,\phi,z,t)=\cos n\phi\cos\omega t\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{f_m\,J_
n(\kappa_m r)}{\kappa_m\,J_n'(\kappa_m r_0)}\cos\frac{m\pi z}{l}}$$

where $k=\omega/a$, $\kappa_m^2=k^2-(m\pi/l)^2$, and for modes with $\kappa_m^2<
0$ the ratio $J_n(\kappa_m r)/[\kappa_m J_n'(\kappa_m r_0)]$ is replaced by $I_n
(\mu_m r)/[\mu_m I_n'(\mu_m r_0)]$ with $\mu_m=\sqrt{(m\pi/l)^2-k^2}$.

The pressure and velocity fields follow as

$$p=-\rho_0\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}=\rho_0\omega\sin\omega t\cos n\phi\su
m_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{f_m\,J_n(\kappa_m r)}{\kappa_m\,J_n'(\kappa_m r_0)}\cos\fr
ac{m\pi z}{l},$$

$$\mathbf{v}=\nabla\Phi.$$

## Validity and Resonance

The solution is valid provided $\omega$ is **not a resonant frequency** of the c
ylinder, i.e., the denominators $\kappa_m J_n'(\kappa_m r_0)\neq 0$ for all prop
agating modes. The eigenfrequencies of the closed cylinder (with rigid walls) ar
e

$$\omega_{m,s}^2=a^2\!\left[\left(\frac{m\pi}{l}\right)^2+\left(\frac{\lambda_{n
,s}}{r_0}\right)^2\right],\qquad m=0,1,2,\dots$$

where $\lambda_{n,s}$ ($s=1,2,\dots$) are the positive zeros of $J_n'(\lambda)=0
$. At $\omega=\omega_{m,s}$ the corresponding denominator vanishes and the force
d response diverges (resonance), reflecting the absence of damping in the model.
