## Solution

**Setup.** Let $X_M = O_p(M^{-1/2})$, so by definition, for every $\delta > 0$ t
here exist $C_0, M_0$ such that
$$P\!\bigl(|X_M| > C_0\, M^{-1/2}\bigr) < \delta, \qquad M \ge M_0.$$
Set $z_M := \dfrac{X_M}{f\,u}$, where $u \sim N(0,1)$ and $f \neq 0$ is a consta
nt. The target is $\log|1 + z_M|$.

---

**Step 1 — $z_M = O_p(M^{-1/2})$.**

Since $u \sim N(0,1)$, we have $P(u = 0) = 0$, so $Y := 1/(fu)$ is a well-define
d, finite-valued random variable that does **not** depend on $M$. For any $\delt
a > 0$, choose $K_Y$ with $P(|Y| > K_Y) < \delta/2$. Then
$$P\!\bigl(|z_M| > C\,M^{-1/2}\bigr) \le P\!\bigl(|X_M| > C\,M^{-1/2}/K_Y\bigr)
+ P(|Y| > K_Y).$$
Pick $C$ large enough that the first term is $< \delta/2$ (using $X_M = O_p(M^{-
1/2})$). Hence $z_M = O_p(M^{-1/2})$, and in particular $z_M \xrightarrow{p} 0$.

---

**Step 2 — Taylor expansion on the likely event.**

On the event $\{|z_M| \le \tfrac12\}$, which has probability $\to 1$, we have $1
 + z_M \ge \tfrac12 > 0$, so
$$\log|1+z_M| = \log(1+z_M) = z_M - \tfrac{z_M^2}{2} + \tfrac{z_M^3}{3} - \cdots
.$$
For $|z_M| \le \tfrac12$, the remainder satisfies $\bigl|\log(1+z_M) - z_M\bigr|
 \le |z_M|^2$. Therefore
$$\log|1+z_M| = z_M + O_p(M^{-1}),$$
since $z_M^2 = O_p(M^{-1})$.

---

**Step 3 — Combining the rates.**

$$\log\left|1+\frac{O_p(M^{-1/2})}{fu}\right| = z_M + O_p(M^{-1}) = O_p(M^{-1/2}
) + O_p(M^{-1}) = O_p(M^{-1/2}).$$

The $O_p(M^{-1/2})$ term dominates the $O_p(M^{-1})$ remainder.

---

**Step 4 — Rigorous $O_p$ bound (handling the tail event).**

For any $\varepsilon > 0$:
$$P\!\bigl(|\log|1+z_M|| > C\,M^{-1/2}\bigr) \le \underbrace{P\!\bigl(2|z_M| > C
\,M^{-1/2},\; |z_M|\le\tfrac12\bigr)}_{\le\, P(|z_M| > C\,M^{-1/2}/2)} + \underb
race{P(|z_M| > \tfrac12)}_{\to\, 0}.$$
Choose $C$ so the first term is $< \varepsilon/2$ (possible since $z_M = O_p(M^{
-1/2})$); for $M$ large the second term is $< \varepsilon/2$. Thus
$$P\!\bigl(|\log|1+z_M|| > C\,M^{-1/2}\bigr) < \varepsilon,$$
confirming the $O_p(M^{-1/2})$ rate.

---

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{\log\left|1+\frac{O_p(M^{-1/2})}{fu}\right| = O_p\!\left(M^{-1/2}\right
)}$$

The key ingredients are: (1) $1/(fu)$ is a fixed-in-$M$, a.s.-finite random vari
able, so it preserves the $O_p(M^{-1/2})$ rate of $X_M$; (2) the Taylor expansio
n $\log(1+z) = z + O(z^2)$ converts the log to a linear leading term with a high
er-order remainder $O_p(M^{-1})$; (3) the dominant term is therefore $O_p(M^{-1/
2})$.
