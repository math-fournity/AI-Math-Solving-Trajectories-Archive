# Solution

## Setup and Decomposition

Let $\mathcal{S} \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ be a fixed subspace with $\dim \mathcal{S
} = r$, where $1 \le r < n$. We write $V \in GL_n(\mathbb{R})$ in block form as
$V = [A \mid B]$, where $A$ is the $n \times r$ matrix of the first $r$ columns
and $B$ is the $n \times (n-r)$ matrix of the remaining columns.

The defining conditions for $\mathcal{E}$ are:
1. **On $A$**: the columns $v_1, \dots, v_r$ form a basis of $\mathcal{S}$ (line
arly independent and spanning $\mathcal{S}$).
2. **On $V$**: $V$ is invertible.

Since the columns of $A$ already span $\mathcal{S}$, the full matrix $V = [A \mi
d B]$ is invertible if and only if the columns of $B$ are linearly independent m
odulo $\mathcal{S}$, i.e., their images under the quotient projection $\pi: \mat
hbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n/\mathcal{S}$ form a basis of the $(n-r)$-dimensional s
pace $\mathbb{R}^n/\mathcal{S}$.

**Crucially, conditions (1) and (2) are independent**: condition (1) constrains
only $A$, and condition (2) constrains only $B$ (through its projection). Theref
ore:

$$\mathcal{E} \cong \mathcal{F}_A \times \mathcal{F}_B,$$

where $\mathcal{F}_A = \{A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times r} : \text{columns of } A \t
ext{ form a basis of } \mathcal{S}\}$ and $\mathcal{F}_B = \{B \in \mathbb{R}^{n
 \times (n-r)} : \pi(\text{columns of } B) \text{ form a basis of } \mathbb{R}^n
/\mathcal{S}\}$.

## Connected Components of $\mathcal{F}_A$

Choose a basis $e_1, \dots, e_r$ of $\mathcal{S}$. Each column $v_j$ of $A$ lies
 in $\mathcal{S}$, so $v_j = \sum_{i=1}^r a_{ij} e_i$. This identifies $\mathcal
{F}_A$ with the set of $r \times r$ matrices $(a_{ij})$ that are invertible (sin
ce the columns are linearly independent iff the coordinate matrix is invertible)
. Thus:

$$\mathcal{F}_A \cong GL_r(\mathbb{R}).$$

Since $r \ge 1$, $GL_r(\mathbb{R})$ has **2 connected components**, distinguishe
d by the sign of the determinant: $\{M : \det M > 0\}$ and $\{M : \det M < 0\}$.

## Connected Components of $\mathcal{F}_B$

Choose a complement $\mathcal{T}$ to $\mathcal{S}$, so $\mathbb{R}^n = \mathcal{
S} \oplus \mathcal{T}$ with $\dim \mathcal{T} = n - r \ge 1$. Each column $v_j$
of $B$ (for $j = r+1, \dots, n$) decomposes uniquely as $v_j = s_j + t_j$ with $
s_j \in \mathcal{S}$ and $t_j \in \mathcal{T}$. The projection $\pi(v_j) = t_j$
(under the identification $\mathbb{R}^n/\mathcal{S} \cong \mathcal{T}$).

The condition on $B$ is that $t_{r+1}, \dots, t_n$ form a basis of $\mathcal{T}$
. Choosing a basis for $\mathcal{T}$, the matrix $(t_{r+1}, \dots, t_n)$ corresp
onds to an $(n-r) \times (n-r)$ matrix that must be invertible. The $\mathcal{S}
$-components $s_j$ are completely free. Therefore:

$$\mathcal{F}_B \cong \mathbb{R}^{r(n-r)} \times GL_{n-r}(\mathbb{R}).$$

The factor $\mathbb{R}^{r(n-r)}$ is connected. Since $n - r \ge 1$, $GL_{n-r}(\m
athbb{R})$ has **2 connected components** (again by the sign of the determinant)
. Hence $\mathcal{F}_B$ has **2 connected components**.

## Counting Components of $\mathcal{E}$

Since $\mathcal{E} \cong \mathcal{F}_A \times \mathcal{F}_B$ and the number of c
onnected components of a product is the product of the numbers of connected comp
onents:

$$\#\pi_0(\mathcal{E}) = \#\pi_0(\mathcal{F}_A) \times \#\pi_0(\mathcal{F}_B) =
2 \times 2 = 4.$$

## Verification (Sanity Check with $n=2, r=1$)

Let $\mathcal{S}$ be a line in $\mathbb{R}^2$. Then:
- $\mathcal{F}_A$: nonzero vectors in $\mathcal{S}$, i.e., $GL_1(\mathbb{R}) = \
mathbb{R}^*$, which has 2 components (positive and negative). ✓
- $\mathcal{F}_B$: vectors $v_2 = s + t$ with $s \in \mathcal{S}$ free and $t \i
n \mathcal{T}$ nonzero, giving $\mathbb{R}^1 \times \mathbb{R}^*$, which has 2 c
omponents. ✓
- Total: 4. ✓

## Answer

The number of connected components of $\mathcal{E}$ is $\boxed{4}$.
