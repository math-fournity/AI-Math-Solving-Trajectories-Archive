**Answer: Yes.** Here is the complete proof.

## Proof

We prove that any invertible map $f:\mathbb{R}^4\to\mathbb{R}^4$ sending lines t
o lines is affine, i.e., $f(x)=Ax+b$. The "preserving constancy of velocity" con
dition is automatically satisfied by affine maps and is redundant over $\mathbb{
R}$ (as shown at the end).

### Step 1: $f$ maps $k$-flats to $k$-flats

A plane $\Pi$ through point $p$ and line $\ell\ni p$ can be written as $\Pi=\big
cup_{q\in\ell}\overline{pq}$. Since $f$ is bijective and maps lines to lines:
$$f(\Pi)=\bigcup_{q\in\ell}f(\overline{pq})=\bigcup_{q'\in f(\ell)}\overline{f(p
)\,q'},$$
which is the plane through $f(p)$ and $f(\ell)$. By induction, $f$ maps every $k
$-dimensional affine subspace to a $k$-dimensional affine subspace.

### Step 2: $f$ preserves parallelism

Two lines $\ell_1,\ell_2$ are parallel iff they lie in a common plane and are di
sjoint. Since $f$ maps planes to planes (Step 1) and is bijective (disjoint sets
 map to disjoint sets), $f(\ell_1),f(\ell_2)$ lie in a common plane and are disj
oint — hence parallel. The same holds for parallel $k$-flats.

### Step 3: $f$ preserves parallelograms

A quadrilateral $a,b,c,d$ (with $a,c$ opposite) is a parallelogram iff $\overlin
e{ab}\parallel\overline{cd}$ and $\overline{ad}\parallel\overline{bc}$. Since $f
$ preserves lines and parallelism, $f$ maps parallelograms to parallelograms.

### Step 4: $f$ preserves midpoints

**Claim:** $b$ is the midpoint of $a,c$ iff there exist points $d,e$ (not on lin
e $ac$, with $d\neq e$) such that $adce$ is a parallelogram and $b$ is the inter
section of its diagonals $ac$ and $de$.

*Proof of claim:* If $b=(a+c)/2$, take any $d$ not on line $ac$ and set $e=a+c-d
$. Then $adce$ is a parallelogram (since $e-a=c-d$ so $\overline{ae}\parallel\ov
erline{dc}$, and $e-c=a-d$ so $\overline{ec}\parallel\overline{ad}$). The diagon
als $ac$ and $de$ intersect at $(a+c)/2=b$. Conversely, the diagonals of a paral
lelogram bisect each other, so their intersection is the midpoint of $ac$. $\squ
are$

Since $f$ preserves parallelograms, lines, and intersections (bijectivity), $f$
preserves midpoints.

### Step 5: Construction of the field automorphism $\sigma$

Fix a point $o\in\mathbb{R}^4$ and a point $e_1\neq o$. Since $f$ maps line $\ov
erline{oe_1}$ to line $\overline{f(o)f(e_1)}$, for each $t\in\mathbb{R}$ there i
s a unique $\sigma(t)\in\mathbb{R}$ with:
$$f\big(o+t(e_1-o)\big)=f(o)+\sigma(t)\big(f(e_1)-f(o)\big).$$
Setting $t=0$ gives $\sigma(0)=0$; $t=1$ gives $\sigma(1)=1$. Bijectivity of $f$
 gives bijectivity of $\sigma$.

### Step 6: $\sigma$ is a field automorphism

**Additivity $\sigma(t+s)=\sigma(t)+\sigma(s)$:** Let $p=o+t(e_1-o)$, $q=o+s(e_1
-o)$, $r=o+(t+s)(e_1-o)=p+q-o$. Then $o,p,r,q$ form a parallelogram ($r-p=q-o$,
$r-q=p-o$). Since $f$ preserves parallelograms:
$$f(r)=f(p)+f(q)-f(o)=f(o)+\big(\sigma(t)+\sigma(s)\big)\big(f(e_1)-f(o)\big).$$
But also $f(r)=f(o)+\sigma(t+s)(f(e_1)-f(o))$, so $\sigma(t+s)=\sigma(t)+\sigma(
s)$.

**Multiplicativity $\sigma(ts)=\sigma(t)\sigma(s)$:** Choose $e_2$ with $e_1-o$
and $e_2-o$ linearly independent. Let $p=o+t(e_1-o)$ on line $\overline{oe_1}$ a
nd $q=o+s(e_2-o)$ on line $\overline{oe_2}$. Draw line $\overline{e_1 q}$; draw
the line through $p$ parallel to $\overline{e_1 q}$; it meets line $\overline{oe
_2}$ at point $q'=o+ts(e_2-o)$ (by the intercept theorem / similar triangles: $\
frac{oq'}{oq}=\frac{op}{oe_1}=t$, so $q'=o+ts(e_2-o)$).

Since $f$ preserves lines, parallelism, and intersections, the same construction
 in the image yields $f(q')$ at parameter $\sigma(t)\cdot\sigma(s)$ on line $\ov
erline{f(o)f(e_2)}$. But by definition $f(q')$ is at parameter $\sigma(ts)$. The
refore $\sigma(ts)=\sigma(t)\sigma(s)$.

Thus $\sigma$ is a field automorphism of $\mathbb{R}$.

### Step 7: The only field automorphism of $\mathbb{R}$ is the identity

Let $\sigma$ be a field automorphism of $\mathbb{R}$. From $\sigma(0)=0$, $\sigm
a(1)=1$, and additivity/multiplicativity, $\sigma$ fixes $\mathbb{Q}$. For non-n
egativity: if $x\geq 0$, then $x=(\sqrt{x})^2$, so $\sigma(x)=\sigma(\sqrt{x})^2
\geq 0$. Hence $x\leq y\Rightarrow\sigma(x)\leq\sigma(y)$: $\sigma$ is order-pre
serving. An order-preserving bijection fixing the dense subset $\mathbb{Q}$ is t
he identity. Therefore $\sigma=\mathrm{id}$.

### Step 8: $f$ is affine

Set $b_0=f(o)$ and define $A:\mathbb{R}^4\to\mathbb{R}^4$ by $A(v)=f(o+v)-f(o)$.

- **Additivity:** $o+v+w$ is the fourth vertex of the parallelogram $o,\, o{+}v,
\, o{+}v{+}w,\, o{+}w$. Since $f$ preserves parallelograms: $f(o+v+w)=f(o+v)+f(o
+w)-f(o)$, so $A(v+w)=A(v)+A(w)$.
- **Homogeneity:** $f(o+tv)=f(o)+\sigma(t)(f(o+v)-f(o))=f(o)+t\,A(v)$ (since $\s
igma=\mathrm{id}$), so $A(tv)=t\,A(v)$.

Therefore $A$ is linear, and $f(x)=A(x-o)+f(o)=Ax+\big(f(o)-Ao\big)=Ax+b$ for $b
=f(o)-Ao$. Since $f$ is invertible, $A$ is invertible.

### Step 9: The velocity condition is automatically satisfied

If $\gamma(t)=x_0+tv$ traverses a line at constant velocity, then $f(\gamma(t))=
Ax_0+b+t(Av)$ traverses a line at constant velocity $Av$. So every affine map pr
eserves constancy of velocity — the condition is redundant given line-preservati
on and invertibility over $\mathbb{R}$.

---

**Conclusion:** Yes, $f$ is necessarily of the form $f(x)=Ax+b$ for some inverti
ble matrix $A$ and vector $b$. The key theorem is the Fundamental Theorem of Aff
ine Geometry: a bijective collineation of $\mathbb{R}^n$ ($n\geq 2$) is a semi-a
ffine map, and over $\mathbb{R}$ the only field automorphism is the identity, fo
rcing semi-affine = affine.
