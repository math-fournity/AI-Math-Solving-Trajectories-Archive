Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a second-countable, connect
ed, locally path-connected, semi-locally simply connected space can have uncount
able fundamental group.

**Answer: No.** Such a space does not exist. Every second-countable, connected,
locally path-connected, semi-locally simply connected topological space has coun
table fundamental group.

---

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $X$ be second-countable, connected, locally path-connected, and s
emi-locally simply connected. We show $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ is countable for any basep
oint $x_0$.

**Step 1: Construct a good countable basis.**

We claim $X$ admits a countable basis $\{B_i\}_{i \in \mathbb{N}}$ of path-conne
cted open sets, each satisfying: every loop in $B_i$ is null-homotopic in $X$ (i
.e., the inclusion $B_i \hookrightarrow X$ induces the trivial map on $\pi_1$).

*Proof of claim.* Since $X$ is second-countable, it has a countable basis $\{U_i
\}$. Since $X$ is locally path-connected, path components of open sets are open;
 thus the path components of each $U_i$ are open and path-connected. By second-c
ountability, each $U_i$ has at most countably many path components (disjoint ope
n sets in a second-countable space form a countable collection). The collection
of all path components of all $U_i$ is a countable basis of path-connected open
sets; call it $\{V_k\}$.

Since $X$ is semi-locally simply connected, every point $x$ has an open neighbor
hood $W_x$ in which every loop is null-homotopic in $X$. For any $V_k \subseteq
W_x$, every loop in $V_k$ is null-homotopic in $X$. The subcollection of $\{V_k\
}$ consisting of those $V_k$ contained in some $W_x$ is still a basis (for any $
x$ and open neighborhood $O$ of $x$, some $V_k$ satisfies $x \in V_k \subseteq W
_x \cap O$). Relabel this countable basis as $\{B_i\}$. $\square$

**Step 2: Key property of the basis.**

Since each $B_i$ is path-connected and $\pi_1$-trivial in $X$: any two paths in
$B_i$ with the same endpoints are homotopic in $X$ rel endpoints. (Their concate
nation is a loop in $B_i$, null-homotopic in $X$.)

**Step 3: Path components of intersections are countable.**

For any $i, j$, the intersection $B_i \cap B_j$ is open in $X$. Since $X$ is loc
ally path-connected, its path components are open; since $X$ is second-countable
, there are at most countably many. Enumerate them as $C_{ij,1}, C_{ij,2}, \ldot
s$

**Step 4: Encoding loops by finite descriptions.**

Let $\gamma : [0,1] \to X$ be a loop at $x_0$. By compactness of $[0,1]$ and the
 Lebesgue number lemma, there exists a partition $0 = t_0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_n =
 1$ such that $\gamma([t_{j-1}, t_j]) \subseteq B_{i_j}$ for some indices $i_1,
\ldots, i_n$.

For each $j = 1, \ldots, n-1$, the point $\gamma(t_j)$ lies in $B_{i_j} \cap B_{
i_{j+1}}$, hence in a unique path component $C_{i_j, i_{j+1},\, k_j}$.

Define the **description** of $\gamma$ (with respect to this covering) as:
$$\mathcal{D}(\gamma) = \bigl(n,\; (i_1, \ldots, i_n),\; (k_1, \ldots, k_{n-1})\
bigr).$$

**Step 5: The description determines the homotopy class.**

**Claim.** If two loops $\gamma, \gamma'$ at $x_0$ have the same description, th
en $\gamma \simeq \gamma'$ (homotopic rel basepoint in $X$).

*Proof.* Let $\gamma$ use partition $t_0, \ldots, t_n$ with basis elements $B_{i
_1}, \ldots, B_{i_n}$ and intermediate points $p_j = \gamma(t_j)$. Let $\gamma'$
 use partition $t'_0, \ldots, t'_n$ with the same basis elements and intermediat
e points $p'_j = \gamma'(t'_j)$. By hypothesis, $p_j$ and $p'_j$ lie in the same
 path component of $B_{i_j} \cap B_{i_{j+1}}$ for each $j = 1, \ldots, n-1$, and
 $p_0 = p'_0 = p_n = p'_n = x_0$.

For each $j = 1, \ldots, n-1$, choose a path $\alpha_j$ in $B_{i_j} \cap B_{i_{j
+1}}$ from $p_j$ to $p'_j$. Set $\alpha_0$ and $\alpha_n$ to be the constant pat
h at $x_0$.

For each $j = 1, \ldots, n$, define the path in $B_{i_j}$:
$$\beta_j = \alpha_{j-1} \cdot \gamma'\big|_{[t'_{j-1}, t'_j]} \cdot \alpha_j^{-
1},$$
which goes from $p_{j-1}$ to $p_j$. Both $\beta_j$ and $\gamma\big|_{[t_{j-1}, t
_j]}$ are paths in $B_{i_j}$ from $p_{j-1}$ to $p_j$. By Step 2, they are homoto
pic in $X$ rel endpoints.

Therefore:
$$\gamma = \gamma\big|_{[t_0,t_1]} \cdots \gamma\big|_{[t_{n-1},t_n]} \simeq \be
ta_1 \cdots \beta_n = \alpha_0 \cdot \gamma'\big|_{[t'_0,t'_1]} \cdot \alpha_1^{
-1} \cdot \alpha_1 \cdot \gamma'\big|_{[t'_1,t'_2]} \cdot \alpha_2^{-1} \cdots \
alpha_{n-1} \cdot \gamma'\big|_{[t'_{n-1},t'_n]} \cdot \alpha_n^{-1}.$$

The $\alpha_j^{-1} \cdot \alpha_j$ pairs cancel (each is null-homotopic), and $\
alpha_0, \alpha_n$ are constant. So the right side is homotopic to $\gamma'$. He
nce $\gamma \simeq \gamma'$. $\square$

**Step 6: Count the descriptions.**

The set of all possible descriptions is:
$$\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} \mathbb{N}^n \times \mathbb{N}^{n-1}.$$
Each $\mathbb{N}^n \times \mathbb{N}^{n-1} \cong \mathbb{N}^{2n-1}$ is countable
, and a countable union of countable sets is countable. So there are at most cou
ntably many descriptions.

**Step 7: Conclude.**

Every loop $\gamma$ at $x_0$ admits a description (Step 4), and the description
uniquely determines the homotopy class (Step 5). The map from descriptions to ho
motopy classes is therefore well-defined and surjective. Since there are countab
ly many descriptions:

$$|\pi_1(X, x_0)| \leq \aleph_0.$$

Hence $\pi_1(X)$ is countable, and in particular not uncountable. $\blacksquare$

---

**Conclusion.** No second-countable, connected, locally path-connected, semi-loc
ally simply connected topological space $X$ exists with $\pi_1(X)$ uncountable.
The combination of second-countability (providing a countable basis), local path
-connectedness (making path components of open sets open and countable), and sem
i-local simple connectedness (making basis elements $\pi_1$-trivial in $X$) forc
es the fundamental group to be countable, since every homotopy class is determin
ed by a finite description drawn from countable data.
