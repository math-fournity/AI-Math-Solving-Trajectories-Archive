## Proof

Let $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ be differentiable at $a = (a_1,\dots,a_n)$,
 and assume $a$ is a **regular point** of the level surface, i.e. $\nabla f(a) \
neq 0$, so that the tangent plane to the level surface is well-defined.

**The tangent plane (linearization) of $f$ at $a$.** The first-order Taylor appr
oximation of $f$ at $a$ is the affine function

$$z(x) = f(a) + \nabla f(a)\cdot (x - a) = f(a) + \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{\partial
f}{\partial x_i}(a)(x_i - a_i).$$

**The level surface of $z$.** We are told $a \in N_k$, i.e. $z(a) = k$. Since $z
(a) = f(a) + \nabla f(a)\cdot 0 = f(a)$, this forces $k = f(a)$. Therefore

$$N_k = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : z(x) = k\} = \{x : f(a) + \nabla f(a)\cdot(x-a) =
 f(a)\} = \{x : \nabla f(a)\cdot(x-a) = 0\}.$$

So $N_k$ is the hyperplane through $a$ with normal vector $\nabla f(a)$.

**The tangent plane to the level surface of $f$.** Consider the level surface

$$M_k = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : f(x) = k\}, \qquad k = f(a),$$

so that $a \in M_k$. Since $\nabla f(a) \neq 0$, $M_k$ is a smooth hypersurface
near $a$, and its tangent plane at $a$ is the set of all tangent vectors $v = x
- a$ satisfying $df_a(v) = 0$, i.e.

$$p = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : \nabla f(a)\cdot (x - a) = 0\}.$$

This is exactly the hyperplane through $a$ with normal $\nabla f(a)$.

**Comparison.** Both sets are

$$\{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : \nabla f(a)\cdot (x - a) = 0\},$$

the unique hyperplane passing through $a$ orthogonal to $\nabla f(a)$. Hence

$$N_k = p.$$

**Remark on the degenerate case.** If $\nabla f(a) = 0$, then $z(x) \equiv f(a)$
 is constant, so $N_k = \mathbb{R}^n$ (the whole domain), while $M_k$ need not b
e a smooth hypersurface at the critical point $a$, and its tangent plane is not
defined in the usual sense. Thus the identification is meaningful precisely at r
egular points, and there the two planes coincide.

**Conclusion.** At every regular point $a$ of $f$ (with $k = f(a)$), the level s
urface $N_k$ of the tangent plane $z$ of $f$ at $a$ is identical to the tangent
plane $p$ of the level surface $M_k$ of $f$ at $a$. Both are the hyperplane thro
ugh $a$ with normal $\nabla f(a)$.
