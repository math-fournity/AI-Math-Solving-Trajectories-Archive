## Proof of `Invariant_subspace_problem_finite_dimensional`

**Setup.** Let `H` be a complex normed vector space that is finite-dimensional o
ver `ℂ`, with `2 ≤ Module.rank ℂ H`, and let `T : H →L[ℂ] H` be a bounded linear
 operator.

**Goal.** Construct a non-trivial closed `T`-invariant subspace, i.e. exhibit a
`ClosedInvariantSubspace T`.

### Step 1: `T` has an eigenvalue

Since `H` is finite-dimensional over `ℂ`, the linear map `T` (equivalently `T.to
LinearMap`) has a characteristic polynomial `χ_T(z) = det(zI - T)` which is a po
lynomial with complex coefficients. By the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, every
 non-constant polynomial over `ℂ` has a root, so `χ_T` has a root `λ ∈ ℂ`. This
`λ` is an eigenvalue of `T`.

### Step 2: There exists a nonzero eigenvector

Since `λ` is an eigenvalue, `T - λI` is not invertible, so its kernel is nontriv
ial:

$$\ker(T - \lambda I) \neq \{0\}.$$

Choose `v ∈ ker(T - λI)` with `v ≠ 0`. Then `Tv = λv`.

### Step 3: Define `W = span_ℂ {v}`

Set `W := Submodule.span ℂ {v}`. This is a `ℂ`-submodule (linear subspace) of `H
`.

**`W` is `T`-invariant:** For any `w ∈ W`, write `w = αv` for some `α ∈ ℂ`. Then

$$Tw = T(αv) = α \cdot Tv = α \cdot λv = (αλ)v \in W.$$

In submodule language: `W.map T.toLinearMap ≤ W`, since `T` sends the generator
`v` to `λv ∈ W` and `T` is linear.

### Step 4: `W` is non-trivial

**`W ≠ ⊥` (i.e., `W ≠ {0}`):** Since `v ≠ 0` and `v ∈ W`, the subspace `W` conta
ins a nonzero vector, so `W ≠ ⊥`.

**`W ≠ ⊤` (i.e., `W ≠ H`):** The subspace `W = span_ℂ{v}` is 1-dimensional (it i
s spanned by a single nonzero vector). Since `2 ≤ Module.rank ℂ H`, we have `dim
_ℂ H ≥ 2 > 1 = dim_ℂ W`. A proper dimensional inequality gives `W ≠ ⊤`. Concrete
ly: if `W = H` then `dim W = dim H`, contradicting `1 < dim H`.
