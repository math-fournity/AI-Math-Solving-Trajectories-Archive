## Proof

Let $S_N = \sum_{i=1}^{N} X_i$, where $X_i \stackrel{\text{iid}}{\sim} \mathrm{U
niform}[0,1]$.

**Step 1: Almost sure limit of $S_N^{1/N}$.**

Write
$$S_N^{1/N} = \exp\!\left(\frac{1}{N}\log S_N\right).$$

Since $X_i \ge 0$ and not all zero (a.s.), $S_N > 0$ a.s. for all $N\ge 1$, so $
\log S_N$ is well-defined a.s.

By the Strong Law of Large Numbers,
$$\frac{S_N}{N} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N X_i \;\xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}}\; \mat
hbb{E}[X_1] = \frac{1}{2}.$$

Hence $S_N = N\cdot (S_N/N)$, and
$$\frac{1}{N}\log S_N = \frac{\log N}{N} + \frac{1}{N}\log\!\left(\frac{S_N}{N}\
right).$$

The first term satisfies $\frac{\log N}{N}\to 0$. For the second term, since $\f
rac{S_N}{N}\to \frac{1}{2}$ a.s. and $\log$ is continuous on $(0,\infty)$, we ha
ve $\log(S_N/N)\to \log(1/2)=-\log 2$ a.s., and therefore
$$\frac{1}{N}\log\!\left(\frac{S_N}{N}\right) \to 0 \quad\text{a.s.}$$

Thus
$$\frac{1}{N}\log S_N \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} 0,$$
and by continuity of $\exp$,
$$S_N^{1/N} = \exp\!\left(\frac{1}{N}\log S_N\right) \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} e
^0 = 1.$$

**Step 2: Interchanging limit and expectation.**

To conclude $\mathbb{E}\!\left[\lim_{N\to\infty} S_N^{1/N}\right] = 1$, it suffi
ces to justify that the limit and expectation can be exchanged. We exhibit a uni
form integrable bound on $\{S_N^{1/N}\}$.

Since $0 \le X_i \le 1$, we have $0 \le S_N \le N$, so $0 \le S_N^{1/N} \le N^{1
/N}$. The sequence $N^{1/N}$ is bounded (in fact $\sup_{N\ge 1} N^{1/N} = \sqrt{
2}$, attained at $N=2$, and $N^{1/N}\to 1$). Therefore
$$0 \le S_N^{1/N} \le \sup_{N\ge 1} N^{1/N} = \sqrt{2},$$
a deterministic constant. The family $\{S_N^{1/N}\}_{N\ge 1}$ is thus uniformly
bounded, hence uniformly integrable.

By the Dominated (Bounded) Convergence Theorem,
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\lim_{N\to\infty} S_N^{1/N}\right] = \lim_{N\to\infty}\mathb
b{E}\!\left[S_N^{1/N}\right] = \mathbb{E}[1] = 1.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\lim_{N\to\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_i\right)^{1/N}\right]
 = 1.$$
