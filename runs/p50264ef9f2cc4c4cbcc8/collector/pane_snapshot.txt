## Solution

We evaluate
$$I = \int_0^{2\pi} \sin(\cos z)\sinh(\sin z)\,dz$$
using Cauchy's Integral Formula.

**Step 1: Encode the integrand as an imaginary part.**

Recall that for real $a,b$,
$$\cos(a+ib) = \cos a\cosh b - i\sin a\sinh b.$$

Since $e^{iz} = \cos z + i\sin z$, we get
$$\cos(e^{iz}) = \cos(\cos z + i\sin z) = \cos(\cos z)\cosh(\sin z) - i\sin(\cos
 z)\sinh(\sin z).$$

Therefore
$$\sin(\cos z)\sinh(\sin z) = -\operatorname{Im}\!\big[\cos(e^{iz})\big],$$
and so
$$I = -\operatorname{Im}\!\left(\int_0^{2\pi}\cos(e^{iz})\,dz\right).$$

**Step 2: Convert to a contour integral.**

Set $w = e^{iz}$, so $dz = \dfrac{dw}{iw}$. As $z$ runs from $0$ to $2\pi$, $w$
traverses the unit circle $|w|=1$ once counterclockwise. Hence
$$\int_0^{2\pi}\cos(e^{iz})\,dz = \oint_{|w|=1}\cos(w)\,\frac{dw}{iw} = \frac{1}
{i}\oint_{|w|=1}\frac{\cos w}{w}\,dw.$$

**Step 3: Apply Cauchy's Integral Formula.**

The function $f(w)=\cos w$ is entire, so by Cauchy's Integral Formula at the poi
nt $w_0=0$ (which lies inside $|w|=1$):
$$\oint_{|w|=1}\frac{\cos w}{w}\,dw = 2\pi i\, f(0) = 2\pi i\cdot\cos(0) = 2\pi
i.$$

Thus
$$\int_0^{2\pi}\cos(e^{iz})\,dz = \frac{1}{i}\cdot 2\pi i = 2\pi,$$
which is purely **real**.

**Step 4: Conclude.**

Since $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\cos(e^{iz})\,dz = 2\pi \in \mathbb{R}$, its im
aginary part is $0$, and therefore
$$I = -\operatorname{Im}(2\pi) = 0.$$

$$\boxed{\int_0^{2\pi} \sin(\cos z)\sinh(\sin z)\,dz = 0}$$

**Verification (series check).** Expanding $\sin(\cos z)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{(-1)^
n\cos^{2n+1}z}{(2n+1)!}$ and $\sinh(\sin z)=\sum_{m\ge0}\frac{\sin^{2m+1}z}{(2m+
1)!}$, the integrand is a sum of terms $c_{n,m}\cos^{2n+1}z\,\sin^{2m+1}z$. Each
 such term is a product of odd-harmonic cosines and odd-harmonic sines, whose in
tegral over a full period $[0,2\pi]$ vanishes (every product reduces to sines of
 nonzero integer frequencies). Hence the integral is $0$, confirming the result.
