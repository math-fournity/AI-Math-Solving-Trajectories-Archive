## Proof

**The polynomial.** Let

$$p(x) = (x^2 - 1)(x^2 - 2)(x^2 - 3).$$

This is a degree 6 polynomial. Its six roots are $\pm 1, \pm\sqrt{2}, \pm\sqrt{3
}$, which are all distinct, so $p$ is squarefree.

---

**Genus of $Y$.** The compact Riemann surface $Y$ defined by $y^2 = p(x)$ is a h
yperelliptic curve. For a squarefree polynomial of degree $d$, the genus $g$ of
$y^2 = p(x)$ satisfies $g = \lfloor (d-1)/2 \rfloor$. With $d = 6$, we get $g =
2$. Concretely, $Y$ is a double cover of $\mathbb{P}^1$ branched over the 6 root
s of $p$ (and since $\deg p$ is even, there are two points at infinity, unbranch
ed), so by Riemann–Hurwitz:

$$2g(Y) - 2 = 2(2 \cdot 0 - 2) + 6 = -4 + 6 = 2 \implies g(Y) = 2.$$

---

**The map to a genus-1 surface.** Since $p(x)$ is an even function, we can write
 $p(x) = q(x^2)$ where

$$q(u) = (u - 1)(u - 2)(u - 3).$$

Define the affine curve $E_0$ by $y^2 = q(u) = (u-1)(u-2)(u-3)$, and let $E$ be
its smooth projective completion. Since $q$ is squarefree of degree 3, $E$ is a
smooth plane cubic, hence a Riemann surface of genus 1 (with the point at infini
ty serving as a rational base point, making $E$ an elliptic curve).

Define the map

$$\varphi : Y \longrightarrow E, \qquad (x, y) \longmapsto (u, y) = (x^2, y).$$

**Well-definedness on the affine part.** If $(x, y) \in Y$, then $y^2 = p(x) = q
(x^2)$, so setting $u = x^2$ gives $y^2 = q(u)$, meaning $(u, y) = (x^2, y) \in
E_0$. Thus $\varphi$ maps $Y_0$ into $E_0$.

**Extension to infinity.** The curve $Y$ (degree 6, even) has two points at infi
nity, $\infty_+$ and $\infty_-$, corresponding to $y \sim +x^3$ and $y \sim -x^3
$ as $x \to \infty$. The curve $E$ (degree 3) has a single point at infinity $\i
nfty_E$. Under $\varphi$, as $x \to \infty$ we have $u = x^2 \to \infty$ and $y
\sim \pm x^3 \sim \pm u^{3/2}$, which matches the behavior at $\infty_E$ on $E$
(since $y^2 \sim u^3$). Both $\infty_+$ and $\infty_-$ map to $\infty_E$, so $\v
arphi$ extends holomorphically to all of $Y$.

**Holomorphicity.** On the affine part, $\varphi$ is given by polynomial functio
ns $(x, y) \mapsto (x^2, y)$, hence holomorphic. At the points at infinity, the
extension is holomorphic by the local analysis above (the map on local rings is
well-defined and sends the local parameter to a holomorphic function). Thus $\va
rphi$ is a holomorphic map of compact Riemann surfaces.

**Nonconstant.** The map $\varphi$ is generically 2-to-1: for a generic point $(
u, y) \in E$ with $u \neq 0$, the preimage consists of $(x, y)$ and $(-x, y)$ wh
ere $x^2 = u$. Hence $\deg \varphi = 2$, so $\varphi$ is nonconstant.

---

**Conclusion.** The degree 6 polynomial

$$\boxed{p(x) = (x^2 - 1)(x^2 - 2)(x^2 - 3)}$$

is squarefree, so $Y : y^2 = p(x)$ is a smooth hyperelliptic curve of genus 2. T
he map $\varphi(x, y) = (x^2, y)$ defines a nonconstant holomorphic map of degre
e 2 from $Y$ to the elliptic curve $E : y^2 = (u-1)(u-2)(u-3)$ of genus 1. This
gives the required explicit example.
