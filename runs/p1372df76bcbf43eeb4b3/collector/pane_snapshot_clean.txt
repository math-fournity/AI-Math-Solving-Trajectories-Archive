## Proof

**Claim:** The statement is **true**. There cannot exist topologies $\tau, \tau'
$ on an infinite set $X$ such that the product topology $\tau \times \tau'$ on $
X \times X$ equals the cofinite topology on $X \times X$.

**Proof by contradiction.** Suppose such topologies $\tau, \tau'$ exist. Then th
e product topology equals the cofinite topology, so in particular:

> **(Containment condition)** Every open set in the product topology is either e
mpty or cofinite in $X \times X$.

Since every basic open set $U \times V$ (with $U \in \tau$, $V \in \tau'$) is op
en in the product topology, each such $U \times V$ must be empty or cofinite.

**Key observation.** For non-empty $U \subseteq X$ and non-empty $V \subseteq X$
:
$$X \times X \setminus (U \times V) = (X \setminus U) \times X \;\cup\; X \times
 (X \setminus V).$$
Since $X$ is infinite, the set $(X \setminus U) \times X$ is finite if and only
if $X \setminus U = \emptyset$, i.e., $U = X$. Similarly, $X \times (X \setminus
 V)$ is finite if and only if $V = X$. Therefore:

$$U \times V \text{ is cofinite} \iff U = X \text{ and } V = X.$$

**Deriving the contradiction.** Suppose $\tau$ contains a non-empty proper open
set $U$ (i.e., $U \neq \emptyset$, $U \neq X$). Since $X \in \tau'$, take $V = X
 \in \tau'$. Then $U \times X$ is a basic open set, non-empty, but not cofinite
(since $X \setminus U \neq \emptyset$ makes $(X \setminus U) \times X$ infinite)
. This violates the containment condition. Contradiction.

Therefore $\tau$ has no non-empty proper open sets, meaning $\tau = \{\emptyset,
 X\}$ (the indiscrete topology). By the symmetric argument (taking $U = X \in \t
au$ and any non-empty proper $V \in \tau'$), we likewise get $\tau' = \{\emptyse
t, X\}$.

**The product topology is then indiscrete.** With $\tau = \tau' = \{\emptyset, X
\}$, the only basic open sets are $\emptyset$ and $X \times X$, so:
$$\tau \times \tau' = \{\emptyset,\; X \times X\}.$$

**But the cofinite topology on $X \times X$ is strictly finer.** Since $X$ is in
finite, $X \times X$ is infinite, and there exist cofinite subsets of $X \times
X$ other than $\emptyset$ and $X \times X$ (for instance, $(X \times X) \setminu
s \{(a,b)\}$ for any $(a,b) \in X \times X$). These are open in the cofinite top
ology but not in the indiscrete topology.

Thus $\tau \times \tau' \neq$ cofinite topology, contradicting our assumption. $
\square$

**Conclusion:** No such pair of topologies $(\tau, \tau')$ exists on an infinite
 set $X$. The statement is **true**.
