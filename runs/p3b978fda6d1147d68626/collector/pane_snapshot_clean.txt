## Proof

**The statement is TRUE.** We prove that any linear operator $T:X\to X$ on a Ban
ach space $X$ that sends every weakly convergent sequence to a weakly convergent
 sequence must be continuous (bounded).

**Two preliminary facts.**

1. *Norm convergence implies weak convergence.* If $\|x_n-x\|\to 0$, then for ev
ery $f\in X^*$, $|f(x_n)-f(x)|\le \|f\|\,\|x_n-x\|\to 0$, so $x_n\rightharpoonup
 x$.

2. *Weakly convergent sequences are norm-bounded.* If $x_n\rightharpoonup x$, th
en $\{f(x_n)\}_n$ is bounded for each $f\in X^*$. By the Uniform Boundedness Pri
nciple, $\sup_n\|x_n\|<\infty$.

**Main argument.** Suppose, for contradiction, that $T$ is unbounded. Then there
 exists a sequence $(u_n)$ in $X$ with $\|u_n\|=1$ and $\|Tu_n\|\to\infty$. Set
$a_n:=\sqrt{\|Tu_n\|}$, so $a_n\to\infty$, and define
$$x_n:=\frac{u_n}{a_n}.$$
Then $\|x_n\|=\frac{1}{a_n}\to 0$, so $x_n\to 0$ in norm. By Fact 1, $x_n\righth
arpoonup 0$ weakly.

By the hypothesis on $T$, the sequence $(Tx_n)$ is weakly convergent, hence — by
 Fact 2 — norm-bounded. But
$$\|Tx_n\|=\frac{\|Tu_n\|}{a_n}=\frac{a_n^2}{a_n}=a_n\to\infty,$$
which contradicts the boundedness of $(Tx_n)$.

Therefore $T$ cannot be unbounded; i.e., $T$ is bounded and hence continuous. $\
blacksquare$
