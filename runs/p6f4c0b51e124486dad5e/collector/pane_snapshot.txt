## Solution

**Setup and notation.** Let $\varphi: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^{n+k}$ be a gl
obal parametrization of $M = \varphi(\mathbb{R}^n)$. The coordinate vector field
s are
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\bigg|_{\varphi(x)} = D\varphi(x)\cdot e_i = \fra
c{\partial \varphi}{\partial x_i}(x), \qquad i = 1,\dots,n,$$
where $e_i$ is the standard $i$-th basis vector of $\mathbb{R}^n$. The metric co
mponents are
$$g_{ij}(x) = \left\langle \frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial x_i}(x),\, \frac{\pa
rtial \varphi}{\partial x_j}(x) \right\rangle_{\varphi(x)}.$$

At $x = 0$, we are given that $\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\big|_{\varph
i(0)}\right\}$ is orthonormal, so
$$g_{ij}(0) = \delta_{ij}.$$

**Computing the differential $DL_a(\varphi(0))$.** The map $L_a: M \to M$ satisf
ies $L_a(\varphi(x)) = \varphi(x + a)$, i.e., $L_a \circ \varphi = \varphi \circ
 \tau_a$ where $\tau_a(x) = x + a$ is translation in $\mathbb{R}^n$. Differentia
ting at $x = 0$:

$$D(L_a \circ \varphi)(0) = D\varphi(a) \circ D\tau_a(0) = D\varphi(a) \circ \ma
thrm{Id} = D\varphi(a).$$

Applying this to the coordinate basis vector $\frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial x
_i}(0)$:

$$DL_a(\varphi(0)) \cdot \frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial x_i}(0) = \frac{\parti
al}{\partial x_i}\bigg|_{x=0} \varphi(x+a) = \frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial x_
i}(a) = \frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\bigg|_{\varphi(a)}.$$

So $DL_a(\varphi(0))$ maps each coordinate vector $\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}
\big|_{\varphi(0)} \mapsto \frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\big|_{\varphi(a)}$.

**Applying the isometry condition.** The inner product at $\varphi(a)$ is *defin
ed* so that $DL_a(\varphi(0))$ is a Euclidean isometry. A Euclidean isometry pre
serves inner products, so for all $i, j$:

$$\left\langle DL_a\!\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\bigg|_{\varphi(0)}\righ
t),\, DL_a\!\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\bigg|_{\varphi(0)}\right) \right
\rangle_{\varphi(a)} = \left\langle \frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\bigg|_{\varphi
(0)},\, \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\bigg|_{\varphi(0)} \right\rangle_{\varphi(
0)}.$$

Substituting the computed action of $DL_a$:

$$\left\langle \frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\bigg|_{\varphi(a)},\, \frac{\partia
l}{\partial x_j}\bigg|_{\varphi(a)} \right\rangle_{\varphi(a)} = \delta_{ij}.$$

The left-hand side is precisely $g_{ij}(a)$. Since $a \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is **arb
itrary**, we conclude:

$$\boxed{g_{ij}(x) = \delta_{ij} \quad \text{for all } x \in \mathbb{R}^n.}$$

**Interpretation.** The translation maps $L_a$ act as isometries of $M$ that are
 transitive on $M$ (since $L_a(\varphi(0)) = \varphi(a)$ reaches every point). T
he isometry condition forces the metric to be invariant under all these translat
ions, so the metric tensor is constant and equals its value at the origin, which
 is $\delta_{ij}$. The manifold $(M, \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)$ is therefore fl
at — isometric to Euclidean $\mathbb{R}^n$ — regardless of how $M$ is embedded i
n $\mathbb{R}^{n+k}$.
