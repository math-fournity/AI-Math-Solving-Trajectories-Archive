## Proof

**Step 1: Relate the partial sum to a binomial probability.**

By the binomial theorem, the full sum is
$$\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(a-1)^k = \bigl(1+(a-1)\bigr)^n = a^n.$$

Set $p = \frac{a-1}{a} \in (0,1)$, so $1-p = \frac{1}{a}$ and $1 - \frac{1}{a} =
 p$. Rewrite each summand:
$$\binom{n}{k}(a-1)^k = a^n\,\binom{n}{k}\left(\frac{a-1}{a}\right)^k\left(\frac
{1}{a}\right)^{n-k} = a^n\,\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k}.$$

Let $X_n \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(n,\,p)$. Then the partial sum (whether $\m
athbb{N}$ starts at $0$ or $1$ only changes the sum by the constant $1$, which i
s negligible) is
$$S_n := \sum_{k \le \lfloor np\rfloor}\binom{n}{k}(a-1)^k = a^n\,P\!\left(X_n \
le \lfloor np\rfloor\right).$$

**Step 2: Show $P(X_n \le \lfloor np\rfloor) \to \tfrac{1}{2}$.**

The random variable $X_n$ has mean $np$ and variance $np(1-p)$. By the De Moivre
–Laplace central limit theorem,
$$\frac{X_n - np}{\sqrt{np(1-p)}} \xrightarrow{d} N(0,1).$$

Applying the continuity correction at the mean:
$$P\!\left(X_n \le \lfloor np\rfloor\right) = P\!\left(\frac{X_n - np}{\sqrt{np(
1-p)}} \le \frac{\lfloor np\rfloor - np}{\sqrt{np(1-p)}}\right).$$

The right-hand side argument satisfies
$$\frac{-1}{\sqrt{np(1-p)}} \;\le\; \frac{\lfloor np\rfloor - np}{\sqrt{np(1-p)}
} \;\le\; 0,$$
so it tends to $0$ as $n\to\infty$. Hence, by convergence of the CDF at a contin
uity point of the limit,
$$P\!\left(X_n \le \lfloor np\rfloor\right) \;\longrightarrow\; \Phi(0) = \frac{
1}{2}.$$

**Step 3: Compute the limit.**

Since $P(X_n \le \lfloor np\rfloor) \to \frac{1}{2} \in (0,1)$, the quantity $\l
og_a\!\bigl(P(X_n \le \lfloor np\rfloor)\bigr)$ converges to the finite constant
 $\log_a(1/2) = -\log_a 2$. Therefore
$$\log_a S_n = \log_a\!\bigl(a^n \cdot P(X_n \le \lfloor np\rfloor)\bigr) = n +
\log_a\!\bigl(P(X_n \le \lfloor np\rfloor)\bigr),$$
and
$$\frac{\log_a S_n}{n} = 1 + \frac{\log_a\!\bigl(P(X_n \le \lfloor np\rfloor)\bi
gr)}{n} \;\longrightarrow\; 1 + 0 = 1.$$

**Verification with $a=2$:** Here $p=1/2$, and by symmetry $\sum_{k\le n/2}\bino
m{n}{k} \approx 2^{n-1}$, giving $\log_2(2^{n-1})/n = (n-1)/n \to 1$. ✓

---

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log_a\!\left(\sum_{k\le n(1-1/a)}\
binom{n}{k}(a-1)^k\right)}{n} = 1}$$
