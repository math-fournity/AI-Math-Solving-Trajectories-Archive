## Proof

We are given the Lagrangian

$$\mathcal{L}(r, z, x) = \tfrac{1}{2}|r|^2 - F(z), \qquad F(z) = \int_0^z f(y)\,
dy,$$

where $z = z(x)$ is a scalar field defined on a domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R
}^n$, $r = \nabla z$ is its gradient, and $x$ is the independent spatial variabl
e.

### Step 1. The action functional

The variational principle associates to $\mathcal{L}$ the action

$$J[z] = \int_\Omega \mathcal{L}(\nabla z(x),\, z(x),\, x)\, dx = \int_\Omega \l
eft[\tfrac{1}{2}|\nabla z|^2 - F(z)\right] dx.$$

### Step 2. The Euler–Lagrange equation

For a Lagrangian of the form $\mathcal{L}(\nabla z, z, x)$, the Euler–Lagrange e
quation is

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z} - \nabla \cdot \left(\frac{\partial \m
athcal{L}}{\partial r}\right) = 0,$$

where $r = \nabla z$ and the divergence $\nabla\cdot$ is taken with respect to $
x$.

### Step 3. Compute the partial derivatives

**Derivative with respect to $z$:**

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z} = \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\!\left[-
F(z)\right] = -F'(z) = -f(z),$$

by the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus applied to $F(z) = \int_0^z f(y)\,dy$.

**Derivative with respect to $r$:**

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial r} = \frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left[\
tfrac{1}{2}|r|^2\right] = r.$$

### Step 4. Substitute into the Euler–Lagrange equation

Substituting both partial derivatives:

$$-f(z) - \nabla \cdot r = 0.$$

Since $r = \nabla z$, we have $\nabla \cdot r = \nabla \cdot (\nabla z) = \Delta
 z$, where $\Delta$ is the Laplacian. Therefore:

$$-f(z) - \Delta z = 0,$$

which gives

$$\boxed{\Delta z + f(z) = 0.}$$

### Step 5. Interpretation

This is the **semilinear Poisson equation** (also called the nonlinear Poisson e
quation). It is a second-order elliptic PDE:

- When $f(z) = c$ (constant), it reduces to the classical Poisson equation $\Del
ta z = -c$.
- When $f(z) = z$, it becomes the Helmholtz equation $\Delta z + z = 0$.
- When $f(z) = \sin z$, it becomes the sine-Gordon / stationary sine-Gordon equa
tion $\Delta z + \sin z = 0$.

The equation expresses a balance between the "diffusive" term $\Delta z$ (from t
he kinetic part $\frac{1}{2}|r|^2$ of the Lagrangian) and the "source" term $f(z
)$ (from the potential part $-F(z)$).

### PROOF COMPLETE
