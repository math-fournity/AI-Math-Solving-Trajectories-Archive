## Setting Up the Reduction

The sum is $S = \sum_{k=2}^{p-3} \binom{p-1}{k} a^{k-2}$, i.e., the general term
 is $\binom{p-1}{k} a^{k-2}$.

**Case $a = 0$:** $S = \binom{p-1}{2} = \frac{(p-1)(p-2)}{2} \equiv \frac{(-1)(-
2)}{2} = 1 \pmod{p}$, so no prime $p \geq 5$ divides $S$. ✗

**Case $a \neq 0$:** Multiply by $a^2$:
$$a^2 S = \sum_{k=2}^{p-3} \binom{p-1}{k} a^k = (1+a)^{p-1} - 1 - (p-1)a - (p-1)
a^{p-2} - a^{p-1}$$

(since the full binomial sum is $(1+a)^{p-1}$, and we exclude $k=0,1,p-2,p-1$).

## Working Modulo $p$

For $a \neq 0, a \neq -1$ (so $\gcd(a,p)=\gcd(1+a,p)=1$, which holds since $|a|,

By Fermat's little theorem: $(1+a)^{p-1} \equiv 1$, $a^{p-1} \equiv 1$, $a^{p-2}

$$a^2 S \equiv 1 - 1 - (-1)a - (-1)a^{-1} - 1 = a + a^{-1} - 1 \pmod{p}$$

Since $\gcd(a^2, p) = 1$:
$$p \mid S \iff p \mid (a + a^{-1} - 1) \iff p \mid (a^2 - a + 1)$$
