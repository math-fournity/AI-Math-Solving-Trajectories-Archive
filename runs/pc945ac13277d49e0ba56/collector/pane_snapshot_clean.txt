## Solution

We wish to find the critical value of $n$ at which
$$L_n := \lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{x^x}{(\Gamma(x+1))^n}$$
transitions from diverging ($L_n=+\infty$) to converging ($L_n=0$).

### Stirling's approximation

For $x\to\infty$,
$$\Gamma(x+1) \sim \sqrt{2\pi x}\left(\frac{x}{e}\right)^x,$$
equivalently
$$\log\Gamma(x+1) = x\log x - x + \tfrac12\log(2\pi x) + o(1).$$

### Logarithm of the expression

Taking logarithms,
$$\log\frac{x^x}{(\Gamma(x+1))^n} = x\log x - n\log\Gamma(x+1).$$

Substituting Stirling,
$$\log\frac{x^x}{(\Gamma(x+1))^n} \sim x\log x - n\Big(x\log x - x + \tfrac12\lo
g(2\pi x)\Big).$$

Expanding and collecting terms in decreasing order of growth:
$$= (1-n)\,x\log x \;+\; nx \;-\; \tfrac{n}{2}\log(2\pi x).$$

The three terms have growth rates $x\log x \gg x \gg \log x$, so the **dominant
term** is $(1-n)\,x\log x$.

### Case analysis

**Case $n < 1$:** Then $1-n>0$, so $(1-n)\,x\log x \to +\infty$. The lower-order
 terms ($nx$ and $-\tfrac{n}{2}\log(2\pi x)$) cannot overcome a term of strictly
 larger growth order. Hence $\log L_n \to +\infty$, i.e. $\frac{x^x}{(\Gamma(x+1
))^n}\to+\infty$. **Diverges.**

**Case $n = 1$:** The dominant term vanishes. We get
$$\log\frac{x^x}{\Gamma(x+1)} \sim x - \tfrac12\log(2\pi x)\to +\infty,$$
so $\frac{x^x}{\Gamma(x+1)}\sim\frac{e^x}{\sqrt{2\pi x}}\to+\infty$. **Diverges.
**

**Case $n > 1$:** Then $1-n<0$, so $(1-n)\,x\log x\to-\infty$. Again the lower-o
rder terms cannot overcome the dominant term (which is of strictly larger growth
 order and tends to $-\infty$). Hence $\log L_n\to-\infty$, i.e. $\frac{x^x}{(\G
amma(x+1))^n}\to 0$. **Converges (to $0$).**

### Justifying that lower-order terms cannot overcome the dominant term

For rigor, note that for $n\ne 1$ the ratio of the second term to the first is
$$\frac{nx}{(1-n)\,x\log x} = \frac{n}{(1-n)\log x}\to 0,$$
and the ratio of the third term to the first is
$$\frac{-\tfrac{n}{2}\log(2\pi x)}{(1-n)\,x\log x}\to 0.$$
So the sign of the whole expression is eventually determined by $(1-n)\,x\log x$
, confirming the conclusions above.

### Conclusion

- For every $n \le 1$, the limit diverges to $+\infty$.
- For every $n > 1$, the limit converges to $0$.

The transition from diverging to converging occurs at the threshold

$$\boxed{n = 1}.$$
