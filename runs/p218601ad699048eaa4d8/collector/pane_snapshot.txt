## Solution

**Goal.** Evaluate $J = \displaystyle\int_0^{\infty}\frac{x}{x^4+1}\,dx$.

**Choice of contour.** A semicircle in the upper half-plane would enclose two po
les, but since the integrand $x/(x^4+1)$ is **odd**, the full-line integral vani
shes and that contour gives $0=0$ — useless for the half-line integral. Instead
we use a **sector (wedge) contour** of angle $\pi/2$, which exploits the rotatio
nal symmetry $z\mapsto iz$ of $z^4$.

Let $f(z)=\dfrac{z}{z^4+1}$, and let $\Gamma_R$ be the boundary of the sector $\
{z:0\le\arg z\le\pi/2,\ |z|\le R\}$, oriented counterclockwise. It consists of:
- $\gamma_1$: the real segment $[0,R]$,
- $\gamma_2$: the arc $z=Re^{i\theta}$, $0\le\theta\le\pi/2$,
- $\gamma_3$: the imaginary segment from $iR$ down to $0$.

**Poles.** The zeros of $z^4+1$ are $z_k=e^{i(2k+1)\pi/4}$, $k=0,1,2,3$. The onl
y one inside the sector $0<\arg z<\pi/2$ is
$$z_0=e^{i\pi/4}.$$

**Residue.** Since $z^4+1$ has simple zeros,
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_0} f(z)=\frac{z_0}{4z_0^3}=\frac{1}{4z_0^2}=\frac{1}{4
e^{i\pi/2}}=\frac{1}{4i}=-\frac{i}{4}.$$

**Estimate on the arc.** On $\gamma_2$, $|z^4+1|\ge |z|^4-1=R^4-1$, so
$$\left|\int_{\gamma_2}f(z)\,dz\right|\le \frac{R}{R^4-1}\cdot\frac{\pi R}{2}\si
m\frac{\pi}{2R^2}\xrightarrow[R\to\infty]{}0.$$

**Parametrize the two radial sides.**
- On $\gamma_1$: $z=x$, $x\in[0,R]$,
$$\int_{\gamma_1}f(z)\,dz=\int_0^R\frac{x}{x^4+1}\,dx\;\xrightarrow[R\to\infty]{
}\;J.$$
- On $\gamma_3$: $z=it$, $t$ runs from $R$ to $0$, $dz=i\,dt$, and $z^4=(it)^4=t
^4$, so
$$\int_{\gamma_3}f(z)\,dz=\int_R^0\frac{it}{t^4+1}\,i\,dt=\int_R^0\frac{-t}{t^4+
1}\,dt=\int_0^R\frac{t}{t^4+1}\,dt\;\xrightarrow[R\to\infty]{}\;J.$$

(The substitution $z\mapsto iz$ leaves $z^4$ invariant and maps the real ray ont
o the imaginary ray, which is exactly why the two radial contributions are equal
 — this is the key to extracting the half-line integral.)

**Apply the residue theorem and let $R\to\infty$.**
$$\int_{\gamma_1}f+\int_{\gamma_2}f+\int_{\gamma_3}f=2\pi i\,\operatorname{Res}_
{z=z_0}f(z).$$
Taking $R\to\infty$, the arc vanishes and
$$J+0+J=2\pi i\!\left(-\frac{i}{4}\right).$$
The right-hand side is
$$2\pi i\!\left(-\frac{i}{4}\right)=2\pi\cdot\frac{1}{4}=\frac{\pi}{2}$$
(since $i\cdot(-i)=1$). Hence
$$2J=\frac{\pi}{2},\qquad J=\frac{\pi}{4}.$$

**Verification (real-variable check).** With $u=x^2$, $du=2x\,dx$,
$$J=\frac12\int_0^\infty\frac{du}{u^2+1}=\frac12\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}=\frac{\pi}{4}
,$$
confirming the result.

## Answer

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_0^{\infty}\frac{x}{x^4+1}\,dx=\frac{\pi}{4}}$$
