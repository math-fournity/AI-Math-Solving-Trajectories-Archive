I'll solve this directly via the Fourier transform, deriving the Duhamel formula
 and giving the explicit kernel.

## Setup

Consider
$$u_{tt} - \Delta u = f, \quad (x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^n\times\mathbb{R}_+, \qquad u(
x,0)=u_t(x,0)=0.$$

Use the Fourier transform in the spatial variable (convention $\hat g(\xi)=\int_
{\mathbb{R}^n}g(x)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,dx$, $g(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^n}\int\hat g(\xi)e
^{ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi$).

## Step 1 — Reduce to a family of ODEs

Taking the Fourier transform in $x$ and using $\widehat{\Delta u}=-|\xi|^2\hat u
$:
$$\hat u_{tt}(\xi,t) + |\xi|^2\,\hat u(\xi,t) = \hat f(\xi,t), \qquad \hat u(\xi
,0)=\hat u_t(\xi,0)=0.$$

For each fixed $\xi$ this is the inhomogeneous harmonic-oscillator ODE. The homo
geneous solutions are $\cos(|\xi|t)$ and $\frac{\sin(|\xi|t)}{|\xi|}$; with zero
 initial data, Duhamel's principle for the ODE gives
$$\hat u(\xi,t) = \int_0^t \frac{\sin\!\big(|\xi|(t-s)\big)}{|\xi|}\,\hat f(\xi,
s)\,ds. \tag{1}$$

## Step 2 — Invert the Fourier transform

Apply the inverse Fourier transform to (1) and use Fubini (justified for $f$ suf
ficiently nice, and then by density/distributional extension):
$$u(x,t)=\int_0^t\left[\frac{1}{(2\pi)^n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\,\fra
c{\sin(|\xi|(t-s))}{|\xi|}\,\hat f(\xi,s)\,d\xi\right]ds.$$

The bracketed term is a convolution: defining the **fundamental kernel**
$$G_\tau(x)\;:=\;\mathcal{F}^{-1}\!\left[\frac{\sin(|\xi|\tau)}{|\xi|}\right]\!(
x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\,\frac{\sin(|\xi|\tau)}{|
\xi|}\,d\xi, \tag{2}$$
the convolution theorem yields the **Duhamel formula**
$$\boxed{\,u(x,t)=\int_0^t\big(G_{t-s}*f(\cdot,s)\big)(x)\,ds=\int_0^t\int_{\mat
hbb{R}^n}G_{t-s}(y)\,f(x-y,s)\,dy\,ds.\,} \tag{3}$$

Note $G_\tau$ is exactly the solution of $G_{\tau\tau}-\Delta G=0$ with data $G(
x,0)=0$, $G_\tau(x,0)=\delta(x)$, i.e. the retarded fundamental solution of the
wave equation.

## Step 3 — Explicit form of $G_\tau$

Evaluate (2) in polar coordinates $\xi=r\omega$, $r=|\xi|$, $\omega\in S^{n-1}$.
 Using
$$\int_{S^{n-1}}e^{ir\,|x|\cos\theta}\,d\sigma(\omega)=(2\pi)^{n/2}\,(r|x|)^{1-n
/2}\,J_{n/2-1}(r|x|),$$
one obtains
$$G_\tau(x)=\frac{|x|^{1-n/2}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_0^\infty \sin(r\tau)\,r^{n/2-1}
\,J_{n/2-1}(r|x|)\,dr. \tag{4}$$

The Weber–Schafheitlin integral (4) evaluates in closed form. The result depends
 on the parity of $n$.

**Odd $n=2m+1$, $m\ge 1$:** the Bessel integral collapses to a distribution supp
orted on the light cone $|x|=\tau$:
$$G_\tau(x)=\frac{1}{2(2\pi)^m}\left(\frac{1}{\tau}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}
\right)^{m-1}\!\frac{\delta(\tau-|x|)}{\tau}. \tag{5a}$$

**Even $n=2m$, $m\ge 1$:** the support is the full interior of the light cone:
$$G_\tau(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi^m}\left(\frac{1}{\tau}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\ri
ght)^{m-1}\!\frac{H(\tau-|x|)}{\sqrt{\tau^2-|x|^2}}. \tag{5b}$$

**$n=1$:** $G_\tau(x)=\tfrac12\,H(\tau-|x|)$.

These are verified by the Hadamard descent relation $G^{(n+2)}_\tau=\frac{1}{2\p
i\tau}\partial_\tau G^{(n)}_\tau$ and the base cases below.

### Base cases (directly from (4))

- **$n=1$:** $\displaystyle G_\tau(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{ix
\xi}\frac{\sin(|\xi|\tau)}{|\xi|}\,d\xi=\frac12\,H(\tau-|x|)$.

- **$n=2$:** $\displaystyle G_\tau(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^\infty\sin(r\tau)\,J_
0(r|x|)\,dr=\frac{H(\tau-|x|)}{2\pi\sqrt{\tau^2-|x|^2}}$.

- **$n=3$:** $\displaystyle G_\tau(x)=\frac{1}{4\pi\tau}\int_{|y|=\tau}e^{ix\cdo
t y}\,dS_y$ via (2) gives
  $$G_\tau(x)=\frac{\delta(\tau-|x|)}{4\pi\tau}.$$
  (Check: $\mathcal F\!\left[\frac{\delta(\tau-|x|)}{4\pi\tau}\right]=\frac{1}{4
\pi\tau}\cdot 4\pi\tau^2\frac{\sin(|\xi|\tau)}{|\xi|\tau}=\frac{\sin(|\xi|\tau)}
{|\xi|}$. ✓)

## Step 4 — Explicit Duhamel formula in low dimensions

Substituting the kernels into (3):

- **$n=1$:** $\displaystyle u(x,t)=\frac12\int_0^t\int_{x-(t-s)}^{x+(t-s)}f(y,s)
\,dy\,ds.$

- **$n=2$:** $\displaystyle u(x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^t\int_{|y|<t-s}\frac{f(x
-y,s)}{\sqrt{(t-s)^2-|y|^2}}\,dy\,ds.$

- **$n=3$ (Kirchhoff):** using $\frac{\delta((t-s)-|y|)}{4\pi(t-s)}$,
  $$u(x,t)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_0^t\frac{1}{t-s}\int_{|y|=t-s}f(x-y,s)\,dS_y\,ds=\
frac{1}{4\pi}\int_0^t(t-s)\int_{S^2}f\big(x-(t-s)\omega,s\big)\,d\omega\,ds.$$

## Verification

One checks directly that (3) solves the problem: differentiating under the integ
ral and using $G_0=0$, $\partial_\tau G_0=\delta$,
$$u_t(x,t)=\int_0^t(\partial_\tau G_{t-s}*f(\cdot,s))(x)\,ds,\qquad u_{tt}-\Delt
a u=(\partial_\tau^2 G_0*f(\cdot,t))(x)=f(x,t),$$
and $u(x,0)=u_t(x,0)=0$ since $G_0=0$ and the integral is over an interval of le
ngth $0$. This confirms (3) is the desired solution.

---

**Summary.** The Fourier transform reduces the PDE to the ODE $\hat u_{tt}+|\xi|
^2\hat u=\hat f$ whose Duhamel solution (1) inverts to the convolution formula (
3) with the explicit wave kernel $G_\tau$ given in closed form by (4)–(5), repro
ducing the classical d'Alembert ($n=1$), Poisson ($n=2$), and Kirchhoff ($n=3$)
formulas.
