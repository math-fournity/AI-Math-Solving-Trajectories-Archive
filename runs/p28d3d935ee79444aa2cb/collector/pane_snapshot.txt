## Norms of the Generalized Laguerre Polynomials

**Claim.** For $\alpha > -1$ and $n = 0,1,2,\dots$, the generalized Laguerre pol
ynomials
$$L_n^{(\alpha)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+\alpha}{\,n-k\,}\frac{(-1)^k}{k!}\,x^
k$$
satisfy
$$\boxed{\;\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigl[L_n^{(\alpha)}(x)\bigr]^{2}\,x^{\alpha}e^{-x}\
,dx \;=\; \frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)}{n!}\;}$$
i.e. the norm is $\bigl\|L_n^{(\alpha)}\bigr\|=\sqrt{\dfrac{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)}{
n!}}$.

(For integer $\alpha$, $\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)=(n+\alpha)!$, giving the common form
$\dfrac{(n+\alpha)!}{n!}$.)

---

### Proof

We use the **Rodrigues formula**
$$L_n^{(\alpha)}(x)=\frac{x^{-\alpha}e^{x}}{n!}\,\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\!\bigl(x^{
\,n+\alpha}e^{-x}\bigr). \tag{1}$$

**Step 1 — Rewrite the integrand.** Substituting (1) for one copy of $L_n^{(\alp
ha)}$,
$$\bigl[L_n^{(\alpha)}(x)\bigr]^{2}x^{\alpha}e^{-x}
= L_n^{(\alpha)}(x)\cdot\frac{x^{-\alpha}e^{x}}{n!}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\!\bigl(x
^{n+\alpha}e^{-x}\bigr)\cdot x^{\alpha}e^{-x}
= \frac{1}{n!}\,L_n^{(\alpha)}(x)\,\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\!\bigl(x^{n+\alpha}e^{-x
}\bigr).$$

Hence
$$I_n:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigl[L_n^{(\alpha)}(x)\bigr]^{2}x^{\alpha}e^{-x}\,dx
=\frac{1}{n!}\int_{0}^{\infty}L_n^{(\alpha)}(x)\,\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\!\bigl(x^{
n+\alpha}e^{-x}\bigr)\,dx. \tag{2}$$

**Step 2 — Integrate by parts $n$ times.** We transfer the $n$-th derivative fro
m $x^{n+\alpha}e^{-x}$ onto $L_n^{(\alpha)}$. The boundary terms all vanish:

- At $x\to\infty$: every derivative of $x^{n+\alpha}e^{-x}$ is $O(x^{n+\alpha}e^
{-x})\to 0$, while $L_n^{(\alpha)}$ and its derivatives are polynomials (sub-exp
onential).
- At $x\to 0^{+}$: $\frac{d^{j}}{dx^{j}}(x^{n+\alpha}e^{-x})\sim C\,x^{n+\alpha-
j}$ for $j\le n$, which vanishes since $n+\alpha-j\ge \alpha>-1$; and $\frac{d^{
n-j}}{dx^{n-j}}L_n^{(\alpha)}$ is bounded at $0$.

Each integration by parts contributes a factor $(-1)$, so after $n$ integrations
$$I_n=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}L_n^{(\alpha)}(x)\
;\cdot\; x^{n+\alpha}e^{-x}\,dx. \tag{3}$$

**Step 3 — Evaluate the $n$-th derivative of $L_n^{(\alpha)}$.** From the explic
it expansion, the highest-degree term is
$$\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\,x^{n}\quad\text{(coefficient of $x^{n}$ is $\tfrac{(-1)^{
n}}{n!}$)},$$
and all lower-degree terms vanish under $n$-fold differentiation. Therefore
$$\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}L_n^{(\alpha)}(x)=(-1)^{n}. \tag{4}$$

**Step 4 — Conclude.** Substituting (4) into (3),
$$I_n=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\cdot(-1)^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{n+\alpha}e^{-x}\,dx
=\frac{1}{n!}\,\Gamma(n+\alpha+1),$$
where the last integral is the defining integral of the Gamma function (converge
nt since $n+\alpha>-1$).

Thus
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigl[L_n^{(\alpha)}(x)\bigr]^{2}x^{\alpha}e^{-x}\,dx=\frac{\
Gamma(n+\alpha+1)}{n!},\qquad\text{and}\qquad \bigl\|L_n^{(\alpha)}\bigr\|=\sqrt
{\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)}{n!}}.\qquad\blacksquare$$
