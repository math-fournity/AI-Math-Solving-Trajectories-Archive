## Proof

Let $r = |x| > 0$. Write
$$\sqrt{\lambda+i\varepsilon} = \alpha + i\beta, \qquad \alpha = \sqrt{\lambda_\
varepsilon},\quad \beta = \frac{\varepsilon}{2\sqrt{\lambda_\varepsilon}},$$
where $\lambda_\varepsilon = \frac{\sqrt{\lambda^2+\varepsilon^2}+\lambda}{2} >
0$.

**Key observation:** the sign of $\beta$ equals the sign of $\varepsilon$.

---

### Expansion of the numerator

$$e^{i\sqrt{\lambda+i\varepsilon}\,r} = e^{i(\alpha+i\beta)r} = e^{-\beta r}\,e^
{i\alpha r}, \qquad e^{i\sqrt{\lambda}\,r} = e^{i\sqrt{\lambda}\,r}.$$

Therefore
$$\bigl|e^{i\sqrt{\lambda+i\varepsilon}\,r} - e^{i\sqrt{\lambda}\,r}\bigr|^2 = \
bigl|e^{-\beta r}\,e^{i\alpha r} - e^{i\sqrt{\lambda}\,r}\bigr|^2.$$

Expanding the squared modulus:
$$= e^{-2\beta r} + 1 - 2\,e^{-\beta r}\cos\!\bigl((\alpha-\sqrt{\lambda})\,r\bi
gr). \tag{$\ast$}$$

The target inequality is equivalent (after multiplying both sides by $r^2$) to s
howing that $(\ast)$ is bounded by a constant $C$ independent of $r$.

---

### Case $\varepsilon > 0$ (so $\beta > 0$): **the inequality holds.**

Since $\beta > 0$, we have $0 < e^{-\beta r} \le 1$ for all $r > 0$. Using $|\co
s(\cdot)|\le 1$:

$$(\ast) \;\le\; e^{-2\beta r} + 1 + 2\,e^{-\beta r} = \bigl(1+e^{-\beta r}\bigr
)^2 \;\le\; (1+1)^2 = 4.$$

Hence
$$\frac{\bigl|e^{i\sqrt{\lambda+i\varepsilon}\,|x|}-e^{i\sqrt{\lambda}\,|x|}\big
r|^2}{|x|^2} \;\le\; \frac{4}{|x|^2}, \qquad \forall\,|x|\neq 0.$$

The inequality holds with $C = 4$ (independent of $x$, depending only on the fix
ed parameters $\lambda,\varepsilon$).

---

### Case $\varepsilon < 0$ (so $\beta < 0$): **the inequality fails.**

Here $-\beta = |\beta| > 0$, so $e^{-\beta r} = e^{|\beta|r}\to\infty$ as $r\to\
infty$. Choose $r_n = \frac{2\pi n}{\alpha - \sqrt{\lambda}}$ (when $\alpha\neq\
sqrt{\lambda}$; if $\alpha=\sqrt{\lambda}$ any $r$ works) so that $\cos((\alpha-
\sqrt{\lambda})\,r_n)=1$. Then
$$(\ast)\big|_{r=r_n} = 1 + e^{2|\beta|r_n} - 2\,e^{|\beta|r_n} = \bigl(1 - e^{|
\beta|r_n}\bigr)^2 \;\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\; \infty.$$

So $(\ast)$ is unbounded and no finite constant $C$ can bound it. The inequality
 **does not hold**.

---

### Case $\varepsilon = 0$: **trivial.**

Then $\beta=0$, $\alpha=\sqrt{\lambda}$, and $(\ast) = 1+1-2\cos(0)=0$ for all $
r$. The inequality holds with $C=0$.

---

### Conclusion

The inequality
$$\frac{\bigl|e^{i\sqrt{\lambda+i\varepsilon}\,|x|}-e^{i\sqrt{\lambda}\,|x|}\big
r|^2}{|x|^2}\le\frac{C}{|x|^2}$$

holds for all $|x|\neq 0$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$ (with $\lambda>0$) **if and only if
$\varepsilon\ge 0$**. In the standard physical regime $\varepsilon>0$ (the $i\va
repsilon$-prescription of limiting absorption / scattering theory), it holds wit
h $C=4$.
