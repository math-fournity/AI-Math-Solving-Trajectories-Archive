## Proof

Let $g(x) = f(f(x))$. The equation becomes
$$f(x + g(y)) = y + g(x) \quad \text{for all } x, y \in \mathbb{Z}. \tag{1}$$

**Step 1: $f$ is surjective.** Setting $x = 0$ in (1) gives $f(g(y)) = y + g(0)$
. As $y$ ranges over $\mathbb{Z}$, the right side $y + g(0)$ ranges over all of
$\mathbb{Z}$, so $f$ is surjective. Consequently $g = f \circ f$ is also surject
ive.

**Step 2: A Cauchy-type relation.** Since $g$ is surjective, every $u \in \mathb
b{Z}$ can be written as $u = g(y)$ for some $y$. From $f(g(y)) = y + g(0)$ we ge
t $y = f(u) - g(0)$. Substituting into (1):
$$f(x + u) = \bigl(f(u) - g(0)\bigr) + g(x) = f(u) + g(x) - g(0). \tag{2}$$
Setting $u = 0$ in (2): $f(x) = f(0) + g(x) - g(0)$, i.e. $g(x) = f(x) - f(0) +
g(0)$. Substituting back into (2):
$$f(x + u) = f(x) + f(u) - f(0). \tag{3}$$

**Step 3: Form of $f$.** Let $h(x) = f(x) - f(0)$. Then (3) gives $h(x+u) = h(x)
 + h(u)$, so $h$ is additive on $\mathbb{Z}$. Hence $h(n) = kn$ for $k = h(1) \i
n \mathbb{Z}$, and
$$f(n) = kn + a, \qquad a := f(0).$$

**Step 4: Determine $k$ and $a$.** Compute $g(y) = f(f(y)) = f(ky + a) = k(ky+a)
+a = k^2 y + ka + a$. Check (1):
$$\text{LHS} = f(x + g(y)) = k\bigl(x + k^2 y + ka + a\bigr) + a = kx + k^3 y +
k^2 a + ka + a,$$
$$\text{RHS} = y + g(x) = y + kx + ka + a.$$
Equating and canceling $kx + ka + a$:
$$k^3 y + k^2 a = y \quad \text{for all } y \in \mathbb{Z}.$$
Comparing coefficients: $k^3 = 1$ and $k^2 a = 0$. Since $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, $k^
3 = 1$ forces $k = 1$, and then $a = 0$.

**Step 5: Verification.** With $f(n) = n$: $f(x + f(f(y))) = f(x + y) = x + y =
y + f(f(x))$. ✓

Therefore the unique solution is $f(x) = x$ for all $x \in \mathbb{Z}$.
