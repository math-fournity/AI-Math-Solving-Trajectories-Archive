## Solution

**Problem.** Find all surfaces $z = f(x, y)$, $f \in C^1$, such that the normal
line at every point intersects the $z$-axis.

---

### Setting up the condition

The surface $S: z = f(x,y)$ can be written as $F(x,y,z) = z - f(x,y) = 0$. At a
point $P_0 = (x_0, y_0, z_0)$ with $z_0 = f(x_0, y_0)$, the normal line has dire
ction $\nabla F = (-f_x,\, -f_y,\, 1)$, so it is parametrized as

$$
(x,\, y,\, z) = \bigl(x_0 - t\, f_x(x_0,y_0),\;\; y_0 - t\, f_y(x_0,y_0),\;\; z_
0 + t\bigr), \quad t \in \mathbb{R}.
$$

This line meets the $z$-axis ($x = 0,\, y = 0$) if and only if there exists $t$
with

$$
x_0 - t\, f_x = 0, \qquad y_0 - t\, f_y = 0.
$$

Eliminating $t$ (when at least one partial is nonzero) gives the **necessary and
 sufficient condition**

$$
\boxed{x\, f_y(x,y) - y\, f_x(x,y) = 0 \quad \text{for all } (x,y) \in \mathbb{R
}^2.} \tag{$\star$}
$$

(If both partials vanish at a point off the $z$-axis, the normal is a vertical l
ine through $(x_0,y_0) \neq (0,0)$, parallel to and distinct from the $z$-axis,
so it does not intersect—this is automatically excluded by the solutions we find
 below, since they have $h'(s) \neq 0$ for $s > 0$ wherever the gradient is nonz
ero; the degenerate case $h' \equiv 0$ gives a plane $z = \text{const}$, whose n
ormals are all vertical and intersect the $z$-axis only at points with $x = y =
0$—but a constant function trivially satisfies $(\star)$ and its normal at the o
rigin is the $z$-axis itself.)

### Solving the PDE

Condition $(\star)$ says that the directional derivative of $f$ along the rotati
onal vector field

$$
V = -y\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x} + x\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
$$

vanishes: $Vf = -y\,f_x + x\,f_y = 0$.

The flow of $V$ is rotation about the origin:

$$
\Phi_t(x, y) = (x\cos t - y\sin t,\; x\sin t + y\cos t).
$$

Since $Vf = 0$, the function $f$ is constant along every orbit of this flow, i.e
., $f$ is **invariant under all rotations** about the $z$-axis:

$$
f(x\cos t - y\sin t,\; x\sin t + y\cos t) = f(x, y) \quad \forall\, t.
$$

The orbits of rotation are exactly the circles $x^2 + y^2 = \text{const}$ (and t
he origin). Therefore $f$ depends only on $x^2 + y^2$:

$$
f(x, y) = h(x^2 + y^2)
$$

for some function $h$.

**Regularity.** Since $f \in C^1$ and the map $(x,y) \mapsto x^2 + y^2$ is smoot
h, we can recover $h$ by setting $h(s) = f(\sqrt{s}, 0)$ for $s \geq 0$. Then $h
$ is $C^1$ on $[0, \infty)$ (right-differentiable at $0$), and

$$
f_x = 2x\, h'(x^2+y^2), \qquad f_y = 2y\, h'(x^2+y^2),
$$

which are continuous, confirming $f \in C^1$.

### Verification

For $f(x,y) = h(x^2 + y^2)$:

$$
x\, f_y - y\, f_x = x \cdot 2y\, h' - y \cdot 2x\, h' = 0. \quad \checkmark
$$

The normal at $(x_0, y_0, h(x_0^2+y_0^2))$ is

$$
(x_0 - 2t\, x_0\, h',\; y_0 - 2t\, y_0\, h',\; z_0 + t).
$$

Setting $x = 0$ and $y = 0$: if $(x_0, y_0) \neq (0,0)$, take $t = \frac{1}{2h'(
x_0^2+y_0^2)}$ (when $h' \neq 0$); if $h' = 0$ the normal is vertical through $(
x_0, y_0)$, which intersects the $z$-axis only when $x_0 = y_0 = 0$. At the orig
in the normal is the $z$-axis itself. Thus every normal line intersects the $z$-
axis. $\checkmark$

### Conclusion

The surfaces are precisely the **surfaces of revolution about the $z$-axis**:

$$
\boxed{z = h\!\left(x^2 + y^2\right),}
$$

where $h : [0, \infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ is any $C^1$ function.
