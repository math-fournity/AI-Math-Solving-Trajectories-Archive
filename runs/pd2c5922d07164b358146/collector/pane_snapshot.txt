## Proof

**Claim.** Yes, such a manifold exists for every $n \geq 1$: a complex torus $\m
athbb{C}^n/\Lambda$ is a compact, parallelizable complex $n$-manifold admitting
$n$ globally defined holomorphic vector fields that form a basis of the holomorp
hic tangent space at every point.

---

### Construction

Let $\Lambda \subset \mathbb{C}^n$ be a lattice of rank $2n$ over $\mathbb{R}$,
i.e., $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}\lambda_1 + \cdots + \mathbb{Z}\lambda_{2n}$ where $\
lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_{2n}$ are $\mathbb{R}$-linearly independent vectors in
 $\mathbb{C}^n$. Define

$$X = \mathbb{C}^n / \Lambda.$$

**Compactness.** A fundamental domain for $\Lambda$ is the parallelotope $\{\sum
 t_i \lambda_i : 0 \le t_i \le 1\}$, which is compact. The quotient map $\mathbb
{C}^n \to X$ identifies opposite faces, so $X$ is the continuous image of a comp
act set, hence compact.

**Complex manifold structure.** The lattice $\Lambda$ acts on $\mathbb{C}^n$ by
translations $z \mapsto z + \lambda$, which are holomorphic, free, and properly
discontinuous. The quotient $X$ therefore inherits the structure of a compact co
mplex manifold of dimension $n$, with local charts induced from $\mathbb{C}^n$.

---

### Parallelizability

On $\mathbb{C}^n$ the standard coordinate vector fields

$$V_j = \frac{\partial}{\partial z_j}, \qquad j = 1, \ldots, n,$$

are holomorphic and form a global frame of $T\mathbb{C}^n$. The key observation
is that each $V_j$ is **invariant under translations**: for any $\lambda \in \La
mbda$,

$$(d\tau_\lambda)(V_j) = V_j,$$

where $\tau_\lambda(z) = z + \lambda$ is translation by $\lambda$. This is becau
se $V_j$ has constant coefficients in the global coordinate $z$, and translation
s do not change the coordinate expression of a constant vector field.

Therefore each $V_j$ descends to a well-defined holomorphic vector field $\widet
ilde{V}_j$ on the quotient $X = \mathbb{C}^n/\Lambda$. Concretely, if $\pi: \mat
hbb{C}^n \to X$ is the projection, then

$$d\pi(V_j(z)) = \widetilde{V}_j(\pi(z)),$$

and the right-hand side is independent of the choice of representative $z$ in th
e coset, precisely by translation-invariance.

Since $d\pi_z: T_z\mathbb{C}^n \to T_{\pi(z)}X$ is a linear isomorphism for ever
y $z$ (the quotient by a free action is a local diffeomorphism), and $\{V_1(z),
\ldots, V_n(z)\}$ is a basis of $T_z\mathbb{C}^n$, their images

$$\{\widetilde{V}_1(\pi(z)), \ldots, \widetilde{V}_n(\pi(z))\}$$

form a basis of $T_{\pi(z)}X$. As $z$ ranges over $\mathbb{C}^n$, $\pi(z)$ range
s over all of $X$, so:

> **The $n$ holomorphic vector fields $\widetilde{V}_1, \ldots, \widetilde{V}_n$
 form a basis of $T_pX$ at every point $p \in X$.**

This is precisely the definition of a **holomorphically parallelizable** manifol
d: the holomorphic tangent bundle $T^{1,0}X$ is holomorphically trivial, with gl
obal holomorphic frame $\widetilde{V}_1, \ldots, \widetilde{V}_n$.

---

### Summary

For every $n \geq 1$, the complex torus $X = \mathbb{C}^n/\Lambda$ (for any rank
-$2n$ lattice $\Lambda$) is:

- **compact** (quotient of a compact fundamental domain),
- **a complex manifold of dimension $n$** (quotient of $\mathbb{C}^n$ by holomor
phic, free, properly discontinuous translations),
- **holomorphically parallelizable**, with the $n$ descended coordinate vector f
ields $\widetilde{V}_1, \ldots, \widetilde{V}_n$ providing a global holomorphic
frame of the tangent bundle.

Hence such a manifold exists. $\blacksquare$
