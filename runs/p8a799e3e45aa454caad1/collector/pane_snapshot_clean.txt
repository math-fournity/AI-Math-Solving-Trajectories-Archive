## Solution

We are given a Bernoulli scheme with success probability $p$, and the tail bound

$$P\!\left(\left|\frac{S_n}{n}-p\right|\geq \epsilon\right)=e^{-\,n\epsilon^{2}/
4}.$$

We wish to determine

$$P\!\Big(\exists\, n_0\;\text{such that}\;A_n=\Big\{\Big|\frac{S_n}{n}-p\Big|\l
eq\epsilon\Big\}\;\text{occurs for every }n>n_0\Big).$$

---

### Step 1. Reformulate via the complement

The event "there exists $n_0$ such that $A_n$ occurs for all $n>n_0$" is the com
plement of "$A_n^c$ occurs infinitely often (i.o.)":

$$\Big\{\exists\,n_0:\forall\,n>n_0,\;A_n\Big\}^{c}=\Big\{A_n^{c}\;\text{i.o.}\B
ig\}=\Big\{\Big|\frac{S_n}{n}-p\Big|>\epsilon\;\text{i.o.}\Big\}.$$

Hence

$$P\!\Big(\exists\,n_0:\forall\,n>n_0,\;A_n\Big)=1-P\!\Big(A_n^{c}\;\text{i.o.}\
Big).$$

### Step 2. Bound $P(A_n^c)$

Since $A_n^c=\left\{\left|\frac{S_n}{n}-p\right|>\epsilon\right\}\subseteq\left\
{\left|\frac{S_n}{n}-p\right|\geq\epsilon\right\}$,

$$P(A_n^{c})\;\leq\;P\!\left(\left|\frac{S_n}{n}-p\right|\geq\epsilon\right)=e^{
-\,n\epsilon^{2}/4}.$$

### Step 3. Sum the bounds

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P(A_n^{c})\;\leq\;\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-\,n\epsilon^{2}/4}
.$$

The right-hand side is a geometric series with common ratio $r=e^{-\epsilon^{2}/
4}$. For any $\epsilon>0$, we have $0<r<1$, so the series converges:

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-\,n\epsilon^{2}/4}=\frac{e^{-\epsilon^{2}/4}}{1-e^{-\ep
silon^{2}/4}}<\infty.$$

Therefore $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P(A_n^{c})<\infty$.

### Step 4. Apply the Borel–Cantelli lemma

Since $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P(A_n^{c})<\infty$, the **first Borel–Cantelli lemma**
 gives

$$P\!\Big(A_n^{c}\;\text{i.o.}\Big)=0.$$

### Step 5. Conclude

$$P\!\Big(\exists\,n_0:\forall\,n>n_0,\;A_n\Big)=1-P\!\Big(A_n^{c}\;\text{i.o.}\
Big)=1-0=1.$$

---

**Answer:** The probability that there exists $n_0$ such that $A_n$ occurs for e
very $n>n_0$ is

$$\boxed{1}.$$

The key point is that the given bound $e^{-n\epsilon^2/4}$ decays geometrically
in $n$, so $\sum_n P(A_n^c)<\infty$, and Borel–Cantelli forces $A_n^c$ to occur
only finitely many times with probability one — equivalently, with probability o
ne there is some $n_0$ beyond which $|S_n/n - p|\leq\epsilon$ holds for all $n>n
_0$. This is precisely the strong law of large numbers (in probability-one form)
 established here via the exponential tail bound.
