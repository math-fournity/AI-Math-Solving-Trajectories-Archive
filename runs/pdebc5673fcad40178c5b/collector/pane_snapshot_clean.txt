## Proof (Counterexample)

The implication does **not** hold in general. We give a counterexample over a fi
eld (so submodules are subspaces).

Let $R = k$ be any field, and let $M = k^2$ as a $k$-vector space with standard
basis $e_1 = (1,0)$, $e_2 = (0,1)$. Set

$$K = \operatorname{span}(e_1), \qquad M_1 = \operatorname{span}(e_2), \qquad M_
2 = \operatorname{span}(e_1 + e_2).$$

**Check $M_1 \cap K = M_2 \cap K$:**
- $M_1 \cap K = \operatorname{span}(e_2) \cap \operatorname{span}(e_1) = \{0\}$,
 since $e_1, e_2$ are linearly independent.
- $M_2 \cap K = \operatorname{span}(e_1+e_2) \cap \operatorname{span}(e_1) = \{0
\}$, since $e_1 + e_2 \notin \operatorname{span}(e_1)$ and the two lines are dis
tinct.

Hence $M_1 \cap K = M_2 \cap K = \{0\}$.

**Check $M_1 + K = M_2 + K$:**
- $M_1 + K = \operatorname{span}(e_2) + \operatorname{span}(e_1) = \operatorname
{span}(e_1, e_2) = k^2 = M$.
- $M_2 + K = \operatorname{span}(e_1+e_2) + \operatorname{span}(e_1) = \operator
name{span}(e_1+e_2, e_1) = \operatorname{span}(e_1, e_2) = k^2 = M$,

since $(e_1+e_2) - e_1 = e_2$, so $e_2 \in M_2 + K$.

Hence $M_1 + K = M_2 + K = M$.

**Check $M_1 \neq M_2$:**
- $e_2 \in M_1$ but $e_2 \notin M_2 = \operatorname{span}(e_1+e_2)$ (since $e_2$
 is not a scalar multiple of $e_1+e_2$). Therefore $M_1 \neq M_2$.

**Conclusion.** We have exhibited submodules $M_1, M_2, K$ of $M = k^2$ with
$$M_1 \cap K = M_2 \cap K = \{0\}, \qquad M_1 + K = M_2 + K = M, \qquad M_1 \neq
 M_2.$$
Thus the conditions $M_1 \cap K = M_2 \cap K$ and $M_1 + K = M_2 + K$ do **not**
 imply $M_1 = M_2$ in general.

**Remark.** The submodule lattice is *modular*, and the cancellation property us
ed here is the *distributive* law. The counterexample is exactly the classical "
diamond" $M_3$ sublattice ($\{0\}, K, M_1, M_2, M$) inside $\operatorname{Sub}(k
^2)$, which is modular but not distributive. The implication $M_1 = M_2$ would h
old if the lattice of submodules of $M$ were distributive (e.g., if $M$ is a cyc
lic module over a PID, or more generally when the relevant sublattice is distrib
utive), but it fails for arbitrary modules.
