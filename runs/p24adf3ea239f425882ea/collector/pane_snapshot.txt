## Proof

Let $\mathcal{H}_n$ be the real vector space of $n\times n$ Hermitian matrices a
nd $\mathcal{P}_n \subset \mathcal{H}_n$ the open subset of positive definite ma
trices. We prove that $\log:\mathcal{P}_n\to\mathcal{H}_n$ is real-analytic.

### Step 1 — Contour integral representation

For $A\in\mathcal{P}_n$, all eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n$ lie in $(0,
\infty)$. By the holomorphic functional calculus, for any simple closed contour
$\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]$ enclosing $\{\lambda_1,\dots,\lamb
da_n\}$,

$$\log A \;=\; \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_\Gamma \log z\,(zI-A)^{-1}\,dz,$$

where $\log$ denotes the principal branch (cut along $(-\infty,0]$).

### Step 2 — A uniform contour near any base point

Fix $A_0\in\mathcal{P}_n$ with eigenvalues in $[\lambda_{\min},\lambda_{\max}]$,
 $0<\lambda_{\min}\le\lambda_{\max}$. Pick $\varepsilon>0$ with $\varepsilon<\la
mbda_{\min}/2$ and let $\Gamma$ be a contour enclosing the interval $[\lambda_{\
min}-\varepsilon,\,\lambda_{\max}+\varepsilon]\subset(0,\infty)$, lying entirely
 in the half-plane $\{z:\operatorname{Re}z>\lambda_{\min}/4\}$. Then $\Gamma$ av
oids the branch cut $(-\infty,0]$.

By continuity of eigenvalues of a Hermitian matrix as functions of its entries,
there is a neighborhood $U\subset\mathcal{P}_n$ of $A_0$ such that for every $A\
in U$, the spectrum of $A$ lies strictly inside $\Gamma$.

### Step 3 — Analyticity of the resolvent in $A$

For fixed $z\in\Gamma$, the resolvent $(zI-A)^{-1}$ exists for all $A\in U$ (sin
ce $z$ is not an eigenvalue of any $A\in U$). Its entries are

$$(zI-A)^{-1}_{ij}=\frac{C_{ji}(z,A)}{\det(zI-A)},$$

where $C_{ji}$ are cofactors — polynomials in the (real) independent parameters
of the Hermitian matrix $A$ and in $z$. The denominator $\det(zI-A)\neq 0$ on $U
\times\Gamma$. Hence each entry of $(zI-A)^{-1}$ is a **real-analytic** function
 of the real parameters of $A$ on $U$, for each $z\in\Gamma$.

### Step 4 — Analyticity of the contour integral

The integrand $F(A,z)=\log z\,(zI-A)^{-1}$ is:
- real-analytic in $A$ on $U$ for each $z\in\Gamma$ (Step 3),
- jointly continuous on $U\times\Gamma$,
- with $\Gamma$ compact.

On any compact sub-neighborhood $K\Subset U$, the power series of $(zI-A)^{-1}$
in $A$ converge uniformly for $(A,z)\in K\times\Gamma$ (the nearest singularity
in $z$ is bounded away from $\Gamma$). Multiplying by the bounded scalar $\log z
$ and integrating term-by-term over the compact contour $\Gamma$ preserves unifo
rm convergence. Therefore

$$A\;\longmapsto\;\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_\Gamma \log z\,(zI-A)^{-1}\,dz \;=\;\log
 A$$

is given by a **convergent power series** in the real parameters of $A$ on a nei
ghborhood of $A_0$, i.e., is real-analytic at $A_0$.

### Step 5 — Global conclusion

Since $A_0\in\mathcal{P}_n$ was arbitrary, $\log$ is real-analytic in a neighbor
hood of every point of $\mathcal{P}_n$. Hence $\log$ is **real-analytic on $\mat
hcal{P}_n$**.

n, positive definite matrices. The key points are that (i) the holomorphic funct
ional calculus expresses $\log A$ as a contour integral of the resolvent against
 $\log z$, (ii) the positivity of the spectrum allows a contour avoiding the bra
nch cut, uniformly in a neighborhood of each base point, and (iii) the resolvent
 is a rational (hence real-analytic) function of the matrix entries with nonvani
shing denominator, so the compact contour integral preserves real-analyticity. N

genvalue/eigenvector perturbation theory is not smooth.
