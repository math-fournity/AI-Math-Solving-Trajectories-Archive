## Proof

The proof proceeds in two steps: (1) establish pointwise convergence $f_n^{-1} \
to f^{-1}$ on all of $\mathbb{R}$, and (2) upgrade to uniform convergence on com
pact sets using equicontinuity.

### Step 1: Pointwise convergence $f_n^{-1}(y) \to f^{-1}(y)$ for all $y \in \ma
thbb{R}$

Fix $y \in \mathbb{R}$ and set $x = f^{-1}(y)$, so $f(x) = y$. Define $x_n = f_n
^{-1}(y)$, so $f_n(x_n) = y = f(x)$.

**Boundedness of $\{x_n\}$.** Since $f$ is strictly increasing and continuous, $
f(x-1) < f(x) = y < f(x+1)$. Set $\eta = \min\{y - f(x-1),\; f(x+1) - y\} > 0$.
Since $f_n \to f$ uniformly on $[x-1, x+1]$, there exists $N$ such that for all
$n \geq N$, $\sup_{t \in [x-1,x+1]} |f_n(t) - f(t)| < \eta/2$. In particular:
$$f_n(x-1) < f(x-1) + \eta/2 \leq y - \eta/2 < y, \qquad f_n(x+1) > f(x+1) - \et
a/2 \geq y + \eta/2 > y.$$
Since each $f_n$ is continuous and strictly increasing, $f_n^{-1}(y) \in (x-1, x
+1)$ for all $n \geq N$. Thus $\{x_n\}_{n \geq N}$ is bounded, hence $\{x_n\}$ i
s bounded overall.

**Convergence to $x$.** Suppose for contradiction that $x_n \not\to x$. Since $\
{x_n\}$ is bounded, there exists a subsequence $\{x_{n_k}\}$ converging to some
$x' \neq x$. Then:
$$\left|f_{n_k}(x_{n_k}) - f(x')\right| \leq \underbrace{\left|f_{n_k}(x_{n_k})
- f(x_{n_k})\right|}_{\to\, 0 \text{ (unif. conv. on compacts)}} + \underbrace{\
left|f(x_{n_k}) - f(x')\right|}_{\to\, 0 \text{ (continuity of } f\text{)}} \lon
grightarrow 0.$$
But $f_{n_k}(x_{n_k}) = y = f(x)$ for all $k$, so $f(x') = f(x)$. Since $f$ is i
njective and $x' \neq x$, this is a contradiction. Therefore $x_n \to x$, i.e.,
$f_n^{-1}(y) \to f^{-1}(y)$.

This holds for every $y \in \mathbb{R}$, so $f_n^{-1} \to f^{-1}$ pointwise on $
\mathbb{R}$.

### Step 2: Uniform convergence on compact sets via equicontinuity

Let $K \subset \mathbb{R}$ be compact. We are given that $\{f_n^{-1}\}$ is equic
ontinuous (on $\mathbb{R}$, hence on $K$). By Step 1, $f_n^{-1} \to f^{-1}$ poin
twise on $K$. We apply the classical **equicontinuity + pointwise convergence $\
Rightarrow$ uniform convergence** argument:

Let $\varepsilon > 0$. By equicontinuity on $K$, there exists $\delta > 0$ such
that for all $n$ and all $y, y' \in K$ with $|y - y'| < \delta$:
$$|f_n^{-1}(y) - f_n^{-1}(y')| < \frac{\varepsilon}{3}.$$
Taking $n \to \infty$, this also gives $|f^{-1}(y) - f^{-1}(y')| \leq \varepsilo
n/3$ (so $f^{-1}$ is uniformly continuous on $K$, as expected).

Since $K$ is compact, cover it by finitely many $\delta$-balls centered at $y_1,
 \dots, y_m \in K$. By pointwise convergence, for each $i$ there exists $N_i$ su
ch that $n \geq N_i$ implies $|f_n^{-1}(y_i) - f^{-1}(y_i)| < \varepsilon/3$. Se
t $N = \max_i N_i$.

For any $y \in K$, pick $y_i$ with $|y - y_i| < \delta$. Then for $n \geq N$:
$$|f_n^{-1}(y) - f^{-1}(y)| \leq \underbrace{|f_n^{-1}(y) - f_n^{-1}(y_i)|}_{<\,
 \varepsilon/3} + \underbrace{|f_n^{-1}(y_i) - f^{-1}(y_i)|}_{<\, \varepsilon/3}
 + \underbrace{|f^{-1}(y_i) - f^{-1}(y)|}_{\leq\, \varepsilon/3} < \varepsilon.$
$

This holds for all $y \in K$ and all $n \geq N$, so $f_n^{-1} \to f^{-1}$ unifor
mly on $K$.

### Conclusion

Since $K$ was an arbitrary compact subset of $\mathbb{R}$, we have shown $f_n^{-
1} \to f^{-1}$ uniformly on every compact set. In particular, this holds for eve
ry compact $K \subseteq \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$, which is precisely uniform
local convergence outside $\{0\}$.
