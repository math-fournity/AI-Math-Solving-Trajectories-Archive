## Answer

**The statement is true for Riemannian manifolds (positive-definite metric), but
 false in general for pseudo-Riemannian manifolds.** In general, $*\Omega = (-1)
^q$ where $q$ is the number of negative eigenvalues of the metric (the index of
the metric).

## Proof

Let $(M, g)$ be an $n$-dimensional oriented manifold with metric tensor $g$ of s
ignature $(p, q)$, $p + q = n$. Let $\{x^1, \dots, x^n\}$ be a positively orient
ed coordinate system, $g_{ij}$ the metric components, and $g = \det(g_{ij})$.

**Volume form.** The volume form is
$$\Omega = \sqrt{|g|}\, dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n.$$

**Hodge star definition.** The Hodge star $*$ is the unique linear map sending $
k$-forms to $(n-k)$-forms satisfying
$$\alpha \wedge *\beta = \langle \alpha, \beta \rangle_g \, \Omega$$
for all $k$-forms $\alpha, \beta$, where $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_g$ is the
 induced inner product on $k$-forms.

**Computing $*\Omega$.** Since $\Omega$ is an $n$-form, $*\Omega$ is a $0$-form
(a scalar function). Applying the defining relation with $\alpha = \beta = \Omeg
a$:
$$\Omega \wedge *\Omega = \langle \Omega, \Omega \rangle_g \, \Omega.$$

Since $*\Omega$ is a $0$-form, say $*\Omega = f$, we have $\Omega \wedge f = f\,
\Omega$, so
$$f\,\Omega = \langle \Omega, \Omega \rangle_g \, \Omega \implies f = \langle \O
mega, \Omega \rangle_g.$$

**Norm of the volume form.** The induced inner product on top-degree forms gives
$$\langle dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n,\; dx^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx^n \rang
le_g = \det(g^{ij}) = \frac{1}{\det(g_{ij})} = \frac{1}{g}.$$

Therefore
$$\langle \Omega, \Omega \rangle_g = |g| \cdot \frac{1}{g} = \frac{|g|}{g} = \op
eratorname{sgn}(g).$$

**Signature determines the sign.** For a metric of signature $(p, q)$, the deter
minant $g = \det(g_{ij})$ has sign $(-1)^q$ (the product of eigenvalue signs). H
ence
$$*\Omega = \operatorname{sgn}(g) = (-1)^q.$$

**Conclusion.**
- **Riemannian case** ($q = 0$, positive-definite): $*\Omega = (-1)^0 = 1$. ✓ Th
e statement holds.
- **Lorentzian case** ($q = 1$): $*\Omega = -1$. The statement fails.
- **General pseudo-Riemannian**: $*\Omega = (-1)^q$.

Thus the claim $*\Omega = 1$ is **true if and only if the metric is positive-def
inite** (Riemannian). For a general "metric tensor" allowing indefinite signatur
e, the correct identity is $*\Omega = (-1)^q$, and the statement as posed is not
 universally true.
