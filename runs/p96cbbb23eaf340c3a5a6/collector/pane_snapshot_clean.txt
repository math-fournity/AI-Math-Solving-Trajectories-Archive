## Proof

Let $\mathcal{H} = \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$ with standard orthonormal basis $\{e_n\}_
{n=0}^{\infty}$, and let $S$ be the unilateral shift, $S e_n = e_{n+1}$. We prov
e

$$d(S, \mathbb{K}(\mathcal{H})) = 1.$$

### Upper bound: $d(S, \mathbb{K}(\mathcal{H})) \leq 1$

$S$ is an isometry ($\|Sx\|=\|x\|$ for all $x$), so $\|S\|=1$. Taking $K=0 \in \
mathbb{K}(\mathcal{H})$ gives $\|S-0\|=1$, hence $d(S,\mathbb{K}(\mathcal{H}))\l
eq 1$.

### Lower bound: $d(S, \mathbb{K}(\mathcal{H})) \geq 1$

Let $K \in \mathbb{K}(\mathcal{H})$ be any compact operator. Consider the sequen
ce of unit vectors $\{e_n\}$. Since $\langle e_n, y\rangle \to 0$ for every $y \
in \mathcal{H}$, the sequence $\{e_n\}$ converges weakly to $0$.

A standard fact: **compact operators map weakly convergent sequences to norm-con
vergent sequences.** Hence $\|K e_n\| \to 0$.

Now compute
$$(S-K)e_n = S e_n - K e_n = e_{n+1} - K e_n.$$

Taking norms squared:
$$\|(S-K)e_n\|^2 = \|e_{n+1}\|^2 - 2\operatorname{Re}\langle e_{n+1}, K e_n\rang
le + \|K e_n\|^2.$$

Since $\|K e_n\|\to 0$ and $|\langle e_{n+1}, K e_n\rangle| \leq \|K e_n\| \to 0
$, we obtain

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} \|(S-K)e_n\|^2 = 1 - 0 + 0 = 1,$$

so $\lim_{n\to\infty}\|(S-K)e_n\| = 1$. Therefore

$$\|S-K\| \geq \sup_n \|(S-K)e_n\| \geq \limsup_{n\to\infty}\|(S-K)e_n\| = 1.$$

Since this holds for every compact $K$,

$$d(S,\mathbb{K}(\mathcal{H})) = \inf_{K\in\mathbb{K}(\mathcal{H})}\|S-K\| \geq
1.$$

### Conclusion

Combining both bounds,

$$\boxed{d(S, \mathbb{K}(\mathcal{H})) = 1}.$$

**Remark.** This agrees with the Calkin-algebra viewpoint: $S^*S = I$ and $SS^*
= I - P_{e_0}$ (with $P_{e_0}$ rank-one, hence compact), so the image $\pi(S)$ i
n the Calkin algebra $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})/\mathbb{K}(\mathcal{H})$ is unita
ry and thus has norm $1$; the essential norm $\|S\|_e = \|\pi(S)\| = 1$.
