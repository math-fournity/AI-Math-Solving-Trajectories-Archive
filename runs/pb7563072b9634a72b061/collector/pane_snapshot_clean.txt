## Solution

Let $U = |X|$ and $V = |Y|$. Since $X, Y \stackrel{\text{iid}}{\sim} N(0,1)$, th
e variables $U, V$ are iid half-normal with density
$$f(t) = \sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\pi}}\, e^{-t^2/2}, \qquad t \ge 0.$$

We want
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\max\{U,V\}}{\min\{U,V\}}\right].$$

**Symmetry reduction.** The ratio $\max/\min$ equals $U/V$ on $\{U \ge V\}$ and
$V/U$ on $\{V > U\}$. By exchangeability of $U,V$,
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\max\{U,V\}}{\min\{U,V\}}\right] = 2\int_{u \ge v \ge
0} \frac{u}{v}\, f(u)f(v)\, du\, dv.$$

**Change of variables.** Set $u = r v$ with $r \ge 1$, $v \ge 0$. The Jacobian i
s $\frac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(r,v)} = v$, so $du\,dv = v\,dr\,dv$. The integr
and becomes
$$\frac{u}{v}\,f(u)f(v) = r\cdot f(rv)\,f(v) = r\cdot \frac{2}{\pi}\, e^{-(r^2+1
)v^2/2}.$$

Hence
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\max\{U,V\}}{\min\{U,V\}}\right] = \frac{4}{\pi}\int_{
1}^{\infty} r\left(\int_{0}^{\infty} v\, e^{-(r^2+1)v^2/2}\, dv\right) dr.$$

**Inner integral.** Using $\int_{0}^{\infty} v\, e^{-a v^2/2}\, dv = \frac{1}{a}
$ for $a>0$,
$$\int_{0}^{\infty} v\, e^{-(r^2+1)v^2/2}\, dv = \frac{1}{r^2+1}.$$

**Outer integral.**
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\max\{U,V\}}{\min\{U,V\}}\right] = \frac{4}{\pi}\int_{
1}^{\infty} \frac{r}{r^2+1}\, dr = \frac{4}{\pi}\cdot \frac{1}{2}\Big[\ln(r^2+1)
\Big]_{1}^{\infty} = \frac{2}{\pi}\Big(\lim_{r\to\infty}\ln(r^2+1) - \ln 2\Big).
$$

Since $\ln(r^2+1) \to \infty$ as $r \to \infty$, the integral diverges.

**Conclusion.** The expectation diverges logarithmically; the expected value is
infinite.

$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\max\{|X|,|Y|\}}{\min\{|X|,|Y|\}}\right] = +\in
fty}$$
