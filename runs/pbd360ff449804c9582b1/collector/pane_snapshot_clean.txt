## Proof

Let $d = \dim X$ be the dimension of the affine variety $X$ over $k$.

**Upper bound: $m \le d$.**

Suppose $f: X \to \mathbb{A}_k^m$ is a dominant morphism. A fundamental theorem
of algebraic geometry states that for a dominant morphism $f: X \to Y$ of irredu
cible varieties, $\dim X \ge \dim Y$. Since $\dim \mathbb{A}_k^m = m$, we obtain
 $d \ge m$.

(If $X$ is reducible, take an irreducible component $X_0$ of maximal dimension $
d$; the restriction $f|_{X_0}$ is dominant onto some irreducible component of $\
overline{f(X)}$ of dimension $\le m$, and the same dimension inequality gives $d
 \ge m$.)

**Lower bound: $m = d$ is achievable.**

Since $X$ is affine, its coordinate ring $A = k[X]$ is a finitely generated $k$-
algebra with Krull dimension $d$. By the **Noether Normalization Lemma**, there
exist elements $f_1, \ldots, f_d \in A$ that are algebraically independent over
$k$, inducing an injective (integral) $k$-algebra homomorphism
$$
\varphi: k[t_1, \ldots, t_d] \hookrightarrow A, \qquad t_i \mapsto f_i.
$$
This injection corresponds to a morphism
$$
F: X \to \mathbb{A}_k^d, \qquad x \mapsto (f_1(x), \ldots, f_d(x)).
$$
The injectivity of $\varphi$ on the level of coordinate rings means precisely th
at $F$ is **dominant**: the comorphism $k[\mathbb{A}_k^d] \to k[X]$ is injective
, so the kernel (which defines $\overline{F(X)}$) is zero, hence $\overline{F(X)
} = \mathbb{A}_k^d$.

**Conclusion.** The upper bound gives $m \le d$, and Noether normalization gives
 a dominant morphism achieving $m = d$. Therefore the maximum value is:

$$\boxed{m = \dim X}$$
