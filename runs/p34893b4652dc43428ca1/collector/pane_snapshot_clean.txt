## Solution

We evaluate $S = \sum_{k=0}^{n} (-1)^k \binom{n}{k}(x-k)^n$.

**Key tool: the forward difference operator.** For any function $f$, define
$$\Delta f(t) = f(t+1) - f(t), \qquad \Delta^{n} f(t) = \sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}
\binom{n}{k}f(t+k).$$

**Fact.** If $p(t)$ is a polynomial of degree $n$ with leading coefficient $a_n$
, then $\Delta^n p$ is the constant $n!\,a_n$.

*Proof of fact.* $\Delta$ lowers degree by one and maps the leading term $a_m t^
m$ to $a_m\bigl((t+1)^m - t^m\bigr) = a_m\cdot m\, t^{m-1} + \text{lower}$. Iter
ating $n$ times, the leading coefficient $a_n$ becomes $a_n \cdot n(n-1)\cdots 1
 = n!\,a_n$, and all lower terms vanish. $\square$

**Apply to $p(t) = (x - t)^n$.** This is a polynomial of degree $n$ in $t$ with
leading coefficient $(-1)^n$. Hence
$$\Delta^n p(0) = \sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}(x-k)^n = n!\,(-1)^n.$$

**Relate to $S$.** Since $(-1)^{n-k} = (-1)^n(-1)^{-k} = (-1)^n(-1)^k$,
$$\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}(x-k)^n = (-1)^n\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^k\binom
{n}{k}(x-k)^n = (-1)^n S.$$

Combining with the fact above:
$$(-1)^n S = n!\,(-1)^n,$$

so dividing by $(-1)^n \neq 0$ gives

$$\boxed{S = n!}.$$

**Remark (independence of $x$).** The result is independent of $x$ precisely bec
ause the $n$-th finite difference of a degree-$n$ polynomial extracts only the l
eading coefficient, annihilating every lower-degree term—so the parameter $x$ (w
hich only appears in the lower-degree coefficients of $(x-t)^n$ as a polynomial
in $t$) drops out entirely.
