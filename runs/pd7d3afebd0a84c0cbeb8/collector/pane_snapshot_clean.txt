Looking at this problem, I need to find the smallest positive $h$ such that $a_{
n+h} \equiv a_n \pmod{1998}$ for all $n \geq 0$, where $a_0=20$, $a_1=100$, and
$a_{n+2} = 4a_{n+1} + 5a_n + 20$.

## Proof

**Step 1: Homogenize the recurrence.**

Set $b_n = a_n + \alpha$ where $8\alpha \equiv 20 \pmod{1998}$. Since $1998 = 2
\cdot 3^3 \cdot 37$, dividing by $\gcd(8,1998)=2$: $4\alpha \equiv 10 \pmod{999}
$, giving $\alpha \equiv 502 \pmod{999}$. Taking $\alpha = 502$ (check: $8 \cdot
 502 = 4016 = 2 \cdot 1998 + 20 \equiv 20$), we get:

$$b_{n+2} = a_{n+2}+502 = 4a_{n+1}+5a_n+522 = 4(b_{n+1}-502)+5(b_n-502)+522 = 4b
_{n+1}+5b_n - 3996$$

Since $3996 = 2 \cdot 1998 \equiv 0 \pmod{1998}$, we have $b_{n+2} \equiv 4b_{n+
1}+5b_n \pmod{1998}$, with $b_0 = 522$, $b_1 = 602$. Since $a_{n+h}\equiv a_n \i
ff b_{n+h}\equiv b_n$, both sequences share the same period mod 1998.

**Step 2: Factor via CRT.** $1998 = 2 \cdot 27 \cdot 37$. Compute the period mod
 each prime power.

**Mod 2:** $b_0=522\equiv 0$, $b_1=602\equiv 0$, and $b_{n+2}\equiv b_n \pmod{2}
$, so all $b_n\equiv 0\pmod{2}$. **Period = 1.**

**Mod 37:** The characteristic roots are $5$ and $-1$, with $5-(-1)=6$ invertibl
e mod 37, so $b_n = A\cdot 5^n + B\cdot(-1)^n \pmod{37}$. From $b_0\equiv 4$, $b
_1\equiv 10$: $A+B=4$, $5A-B=10$, giving $A\equiv 27$, $B\equiv 14 \pmod{37}$ (b
oth nonzero). Now $\operatorname{ord}_{37}(5)=36$ (since $5^{18}\equiv -1\pmod{3
7}$, verified by successive squaring) and $\operatorname{ord}_{37}(-1)=2$. Since
 both $A,B\neq 0$ and $5\not\equiv -1\pmod{37}$, the period is $\operatorname{lc
m}(36,2) = \mathbf{36}$.

**Mod 27:** Here $5-(-1)=6$ and $\gcd(6,27)=3\neq 1$, so the matrix $M=\begin{pm
atrix}0&1\\5&4\end{pmatrix}$ is **not** diagonalizable mod 27. Using $M^n = c_n
M + d_n I$ where $c_n = \frac{5^n-(-1)^n}{6}$, $d_n = \frac{5^n+5(-1)^n}{6}$ (in
tegers since $5^n\equiv (-1)^n\pmod{6}$):

$M^n \equiv I \pmod{27}$ requires $c_n\equiv 0$ and $d_n\equiv 1\pmod{27}$, i.e.
, $5^n\equiv (-1)^n\pmod{162}$ and $(-1)^n\equiv 1\pmod{27}$ (so $n$ even), then
 $5^n\equiv 1\pmod{162}$. Since $162=2\cdot 81$ and $5$ is a primitive root mod
$3^k$ with $\operatorname{ord}_{81}(5)=2\cdot 3^3=54$, the smallest such $n$ is
**54**.

To confirm the *sequence* period equals the matrix order: the condition $M^h\vec
{b}\equiv \vec{b}\pmod{27}$ yields (with $b_0\equiv 9$, $b_1\equiv 8$, $b_2\equi
v 23$) the system $8c_h+9d_h\equiv 9$ and $23c_h+8d_h\equiv 8\pmod{27}$. Setting
 $u=1-d_h$, this gives $c_h\equiv 18u$ and $c_h\equiv 25u\pmod{27}$, forcing $7u
\equiv 0\pmod{27}$, hence $u\equiv 0$ (since $\gcd(7,27)=1$). So $d_h\equiv 1$ a
nd $c_h\equiv 0$, meaning the sequence period equals the matrix order = **54**.

**Step 3: Combine via CRT.**

$$h = \operatorname{lcm}(1, 54, 36) = \operatorname{lcm}(2\cdot 3^3,\; 2^2\cdot
3^2) = 2^2\cdot 3^3 = \boxed{108}$$
