## Solution

We count the number of triangles formed by vertices of a regular 21-gon that con
tain the center $C$ in their interior.

**Total triangles.** There are $\binom{21}{3} = 1330$ triangles in total.

**Triangles NOT containing the center.** A triangle fails to contain $C$ if and
only if all three vertices lie strictly within some open semicircle (equivalentl
y, some open half-plane through $C$).

Since $n = 21$ is odd, from any fixed vertex $P_i$, the number of other vertices
 lying in the clockwise open semicircle of length $\pi$ starting at $P_i$ is exa
ctly $\frac{n-1}{2} = 10$.

**Key bijection.** For any triangle that does not contain $C$, there is a *uniqu
e* vertex among the three such that the other two vertices both lie in the clock
wise open semicircle of length 10 starting from that vertex. (This vertex is the
 "first" vertex of the triangle when we traverse clockwise — the one after which
 the arc containing all three vertices begins.) This gives a bijection between:
- triangles not containing $C$, and
- pairs $(P_i, \{P_a, P_b\})$ where $P_a, P_b$ are two of the 10 vertices in the
 clockwise open semicircle from $P_i$.

Therefore, the number of triangles not containing $C$ is:
$$21 \cdot \binom{10}{2} = 21 \cdot 45 = 945.$$

**Triangles containing the center.**
$$\binom{21}{3} - 21\cdot\binom{10}{2} = 1330 - 945 = \boxed{385}.$$

**Verification with small cases.**
- $n=3$: $\binom{3}{3} - 3\binom{1}{2} = 1 - 0 = 1$. The single triangle contain
s the center. ✓
- $n=5$: $\binom{5}{3} - 5\binom{2}{2} = 10 - 5 = 5$. Five of the ten pentagon t
riangles contain the center. ✓

Both check out, confirming the formula.

The number of sets of three docking ports forming a triangular shield that stric
tly contains the Core Reactor $C$ is $\boxed{385}$.
