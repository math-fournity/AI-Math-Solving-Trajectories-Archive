## Solution

**Goal.** Count isomorphism classes of $\mathbb{Z}[i]$-modules with exactly $5$
elements.

**Step 1. Structure of finite $\mathbb{Z}[i]$-modules.**

$\mathbb{Z}[i]$ is a Euclidean domain (hence a PID). By the structure theorem fo
r finitely generated modules over a PID, every finite $\mathbb{Z}[i]$-module dec
omposes as
$$M \cong \bigoplus_j \mathbb{Z}[i]/(\pi_j^{e_j}),$$
where each $\pi_j$ is an irreducible (prime) in $\mathbb{Z}[i]$. The order of a
summand $\mathbb{Z}[i]/(\pi^e)$ is $N(\pi)^e$, where $N(a+bi)=a^2+b^2$ is the no
rm. Hence
$$|M| = \prod_j N(\pi_j)^{e_j}.$$

**Step 2. Factor $5$ in $\mathbb{Z}[i]$.**

In $\mathbb{Z}[i]$, $5 = (2+i)(2-i)$. Both $2+i$ and $2-i$ are Gaussian primes s
ince $N(2+i)=N(2-i)=5$ is prime in $\mathbb{Z}$. They are **not associates** (th
e units of $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ are $\{\pm1,\pm i\}$, and none sends $2+i$ to $2-i$).
 These are the only primes (up to associates) lying above $5$.

**Step 3. Enumerate modules of order $5$.**

Since $5$ is prime, the only way to write $5 = \prod N(\pi_j)^{e_j}$ with each $
N(\pi_j) \geq 2$ is a single factor $N(\pi)=5$, $e=1$. Thus every $\mathbb{Z}[i]
$-module of order $5$ is cyclic:
$$M \cong \mathbb{Z}[i]/(\pi), \quad N(\pi)=5.$$
The only primes of norm $5$ (up to associates) are $\pi = 2+i$ and $\pi = 2-i$,
giving two candidates:
$$M_1 = \mathbb{Z}[i]/(2+i), \qquad M_2 = \mathbb{Z}[i]/(2-i).$$

**Step 4. Check whether $M_1 \cong M_2$ as $\mathbb{Z}[i]$-modules.**

Both quotients are isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}$ as abelian groups (sin
ce $\mathbb{Z}[i]/(a+bi)\cong \mathbb{Z}/(a^2+b^2)\mathbb{Z}$ when $\gcd(a,b)=1$
). The difference lies in **how $i$ acts**:

- In $\mathbb{Z}[i]/(2+i)$: the relation $2+i=0$ gives $i \equiv -2 \equiv 3 \pm
od{5}$. So $i$ acts as multiplication by $3$.
- In $\mathbb{Z}[i]/(2-i)$: the relation $2-i=0$ gives $i \equiv 2 \pmod{5}$. So
 $i$ acts as multiplication by $2$.

A $\mathbb{Z}[i]$-module isomorphism $\varphi: M_1 \to M_2$ is a group isomorphi
sm $\varphi(x)=cx$ (for some $c\in(\mathbb{Z}/5)^\times$) satisfying $\varphi(i\
cdot x) = i\cdot \varphi(x)$ for all $x$. This requires
$$c\cdot 3x = 2\cdot cx \pmod{5} \quad\text{for all }x,$$
i.e., $3c \equiv 2c \pmod{5}$, i.e., $c \equiv 0 \pmod{5}$. But $c$ must be a un
it, contradiction. Hence **no isomorphism exists**: $M_1 \not\cong M_2$.

**Step 5. Conclusion.**

The two modules $\mathbb{Z}[i]/(2+i)$ and $\mathbb{Z}[i]/(2-i)$ are the only $\m
athbb{Z}[i]$-modules of order $5$, and they are non-isomorphic. Therefore the nu
mber of isomorphism classes is

$$\boxed{2}.$$
