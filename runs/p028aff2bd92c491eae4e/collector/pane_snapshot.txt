Solution

Answer: No. A simply-connected $\mathbb{Z}_p$-homology $n$-sphere need not be
homotopy equivalent to $S^n$.

Setup and Key Observation

A $\mathbb{Z}p$-homology $n$-sphere $X$ satisfies $\widetilde{H}(X;\mathbb{Z}
p)\cong \widetilde{H}(S^n;\mathbb{Z}_p)$, i.e., $\widetilde{H}_n(X;\mathbb{Z}
_p)\cong\mathbb{Z}_p$ and $\widetilde{H}_i(X;\mathbb{Z}_p)=0$ for $i\neq n$.
This constrains only mod-$p$ homology; integral homology may carry torsion at
primes $q\neq p$, which is invisible to $\mathbb{Z}_p$-coefficients.

Note on $n=1$: If $X$ is simply-connected then $H_1(X;\mathbb{Z})=0$, hence
$H_1(X;\mathbb{Z}_p)=0$ by UCT, contradicting $\widetilde{H}_1(X;\mathbb{Z}
_p)\cong\mathbb{Z}_p$. So the hypothesis is vacuous for $n=1$. We work with
$n\geq 2$.

The Counterexample

Fix a prime $q\neq p$ and $n\geq 2$. Let $M(\mathbb{Z}/q,,n)$ be the Moore space
obtained by attaching an $(n+1)$-cell to $S^n$ via a degree-$q$ map $S^n\to
S^n$. Set

$$X ;=; S^n ;\vee; M(\mathbb{Z}/q,,n).$$

Claim: $X$ is a simply-connected $\mathbb{Z}_p$-homology $n$-sphere, but
$X\not\simeq S^n$.

Simply-connected. For $n\geq 2$, the Moore space $M(\mathbb{Z}/q,n)$ has trivial
$1$-skeleton (a point), so $\pi_1(M(\mathbb{Z}/q,n))=0$. Likewise $
\pi_1(S^n)=0$. By van Kampen, the wedge of simply-connected spaces is simply-
connected. ✓

Integral homology of $M(\mathbb{Z}/q,n)$. The cellular chain complex is $
\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{;\times q;}\mathbb{Z}$ in degrees $n+1\to n$, giving $
\widetilde{H}_n(M;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/q$ and $\widetilde{H}_i(M;
\mathbb{Z})=0$ for $i\neq n$.

Mod-$p$ homology of $M(\mathbb{Z}/q,n)$. Tensor the cellular complex with $
\mathbb{Z}_p$: we get $\mathbb{Z}_p\xrightarrow{;\times q;}\mathbb{Z}_p$. Since
$q\neq p$, $q$ is a unit in $\mathbb{Z}_p$, so this map is an isomorphism. Hence

$$\widetilde{H}_*!\big(M(\mathbb{Z}/q,n);,\mathbb{Z}_p\big)=0.$$

The Moore space is $\mathbb{Z}_p$-acyclic.

Mod-$p$ homology of $X$. By the wedge axiom for homology:

$$\widetilde{H}_i(X;\mathbb{Z}_p);\cong;\widetilde{H}_i(S^n;\mathbb{Z}_p);
\oplus;\widetilde{H}_i(M(\mathbb{Z}/q,n);\mathbb{Z}_p).$$

The second summand vanishes for all $i$, so $\widetilde{H}_n(X;\mathbb{Z}
_p)\cong\mathbb{Z}_p$ and $\widetilde{H}_i(X;\mathbb{Z}_p)=0$ for $i\neq n$.
Thus $X$ is a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-homology $n$-sphere. ✓

$X$ is not homotopy equivalent to $S^n$. The integral homology is

$$H_n(X;\mathbb{Z});\cong;H_n(S^n;\mathbb{Z})\oplus H_n(M(\mathbb{Z}/q,n);
\mathbb{Z});\cong;\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/q,$$

with $\widetilde{H}_i(X;\mathbb{Z})=0$ for $i\neq n$. Since $H_n(X;
\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/q\not\cong\mathbb{Z}\cong H_n(S^n;
\mathbb{Z})$, the spaces have different integral homology, so they cannot be
homotopy equivalent. ✓

Conclusion

The $\mathbb{Z}_p$-homology sphere condition detects only the mod-$p$ homology.
Torsion at primes $q\neq p$ is invisible to $\mathbb{Z}_p$-coefficients (since $
\mathbb{Z}/q^k\otimes\mathbb{Z}_p=0$ and $\operatorname{Tor}(\mathbb{Z}/q^k,
\mathbb{Z}p)=0$ for $q\neq p$), so it can appear in $H*(X;\mathbb{Z})$ without
affecting the $\mathbb{Z}_p$-homology. The space $X=S^n\vee M(\mathbb{Z}/q,n)$,
for any prime $q\neq p$ and $n\geq 2$, is a simply-connected $\mathbb{Z}_p$-
homology $n$-sphere that is not homotopy equivalent to $S^n$.
