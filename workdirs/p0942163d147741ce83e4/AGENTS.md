# Problem

In the city of Arithmos, there is a grand boulevard with exactly 100 consecutive streetlamps, numbered 1 through 100. One night, a city maintenance worker named Alfred is instructed to deactivate exactly ten of these lamps for repair.

After Alfred finishes his work, leaving 90 lamps illuminated, a lighting inspector named Phil arrives. Phil is looking for "linear sequences"—sets of illuminated lamps where the distance between the numbers of any two consecutive lamps in the sequence is constant (for example, lamps 5, 10, and 15 form a linear sequence of length 3).

No matter which ten lamps Alfred chooses to deactivate, Phil is guaranteed to find at least one linear sequence of a certain length among the remaining 90 lamps. What is the maximum length $L$ such that Phil is always guaranteed to find a linear sequence of at least $L$ illuminated lamps?
