**Theorem.** Every probability measure $\mu$ on $(\mathbb{R}, \mathcal{B})$ is t
he distribution (induced measure) of some random variable. That is, there exists
 a probability space $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ and a random variable $
X: \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ such that $\mu(B) = \mathbb{P}(X^{-1}(B))$ for every $
B \in \mathcal{B}$.

**Proof.**

Let $\mu$ be an arbitrary probability measure on $(\mathbb{R}, \mathcal{B})$. De
fine its cumulative distribution function
$$F(x) = \mu((-\infty, x]), \qquad x \in \mathbb{R}.$$
Then $F$ is non-decreasing, right-continuous, with $\lim_{x\to-\infty} F(x) = 0$
 and $\lim_{x\to+\infty} F(x) = 1$.

Take the canonical probability space $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P}) = ((0,1)
, \mathcal{B}((0,1)), \lambda)$, where $\lambda$ is Lebesgue measure on $(0,1)$.

Define the **generalized inverse** (quantile function) $Q: (0,1) \to \mathbb{R}$
 by
$$Q(u) = \inf\{x \in \mathbb{R} : F(x) \geq u\}, \qquad u \in (0,1).$$
Since $F$ is non-decreasing and right-continuous with range limits $0$ and $1$,
the set $\{x : F(x) \geq u\}$ is non-empty for every $u \in (0,1)$ (because $F(x
)\to 1$ as $x\to+\infty$) and bounded below for every $u \in (0,1)$ (because $F(
x)\to 0$ as $x\to-\infty$). Hence $Q(u)$ is a well-defined real number, and $Q$
is a measurable function (it is non-decreasing, hence Borel-measurable).

We claim $X = Q$ is the desired random variable, i.e., $\mu = \mathbb{P} \circ Q
^{-1}$.

**Key identity.** For every $u \in (0,1)$ and every $x \in \mathbb{R}$:
$$Q(u) \leq x \iff u \leq F(x).$$

*Proof of the identity.*
- ($\Rightarrow$): If $Q(u) \leq x$, then $\inf\{t : F(t)\geq u\} \leq x$. By ri
ght-continuity of $F$, for any $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $t \leq x + \varep
silon$ with $F(t) \geq u$. Since $F$ is non-decreasing, $F(x+\varepsilon) \geq u
$ for all $\varepsilon > 0$, and by right-continuity $F(x) \geq u$.
- ($\Leftarrow$): If $u \leq F(x)$, then $x \in \{t : F(t) \geq u\}$, so $Q(u) =
 \inf\{t : F(t) \geq u\} \leq x$.

**Conclusion.** Using the identity, for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$:
$$\mathbb{P}(X \leq x) = \lambda(\{u \in (0,1) : Q(u) \leq x\}) = \lambda(\{u \i
n (0,1) : u \leq F(x)\}) = \lambda((0, F(x)]) = F(x) = \mu((-\infty, x]).$$

Thus $X = Q$ and $\mu$ have the same CDF. Since a probability measure on $(\math
bb{R}, \mathcal{B})$ is uniquely determined by its CDF (this follows from the $\
pi$–$\lambda$ theorem: the half-lines $(-\infty, x]$ form a $\pi$-system generat
ing $\mathcal{B}$, and the measures agree on this $\pi$-system), we conclude
$$\mu(B) = \mathbb{P}(X^{-1}(B)) \quad \text{for all } B \in \mathcal{B}.$$

Therefore $\mu$ is the induced measure of the random variable $X = Q$ on the pro
bability space $((0,1), \mathcal{B}((0,1)), \lambda)$. $\blacksquare$
