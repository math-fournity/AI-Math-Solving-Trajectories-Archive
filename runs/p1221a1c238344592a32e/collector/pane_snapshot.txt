## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** It is sufficient to check smoothness at the closed points of $X
$.

**Key ingredients.**

1. **$X$ is quasi-compact.** The morphism $f: X \to Y = \operatorname{Spec}(R)$
is projective, hence factors as a closed immersion $X \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}
^n_Y$ followed by the projection $\mathbb{P}^n_Y \to Y$. Both are quasi-compact
(closed immersions are quasi-compact; $\mathbb{P}^n_Y$ is covered by finitely ma
ny affine opens), so $f$ is quasi-compact. Since $Y = \operatorname{Spec}(R)$ is
 affine (hence quasi-compact), $X$ is quasi-compact.

2. **The smooth locus is open.** Since $R$ is a DVR (noetherian) and $f$ is of f
inite type, $f$ is locally of finite presentation. For a morphism locally of fin
ite presentation, the set
$$U = \{x \in X : f \text{ is smooth at } x\}$$
is open in $X$ (EGA IV$_4$, 17.5.1).

3. **Every non-empty closed subset of a quasi-compact scheme contains a closed p
oint of the scheme.** This is a standard topological fact: if $Z$ is a non-empty
 closed subset of a quasi-compact scheme $X$, then $Z$ is quasi-compact (closed
in quasi-compact is quasi-compact) and sober (subspace of a sober space). A non-
empty quasi-compact sober space has a closed point $z$; since $Z$ is closed in $
X$, the point $z$ is closed in $Z$ implies $\{z\}$ is closed in $Z$, hence close
d in $X$ (closed-in-closed is closed). Thus $z$ is a closed point of $X$ lying i
n $Z$.

**The argument.**

Let $U \subseteq X$ be the smooth locus (open by ingredient 2) and let $Z = X \s
etminus U$ be the non-smooth locus (closed in $X$).

Suppose, for contradiction, that $f$ is smooth at every closed point of $X$ but
$f$ is not smooth everywhere. Then $Z \neq \emptyset$. By ingredient 1, $X$ is q
uasi-compact. By ingredient 3, the non-empty closed subset $Z \subseteq X$ conta
ins a closed point $z$ of $X$. But $z \in Z$ means $f$ is **not** smooth at $z$,
 contradicting the assumption that $f$ is smooth at all closed points.

Therefore $Z = \emptyset$, i.e., $U = X$, and $f$ is smooth everywhere.

**Remark on the role of the DVR.** The fact that $Y = \operatorname{Spec}(R)$ fo
r a DVR $R$ (which is *not* a Jacobson scheme) might raise the concern that clos
ed points of $X$ fail to "see" the generic fiber $X_\eta$. Indeed, since $f$ is
projective (hence proper), closed points of $X$ map to closed points of $Y$, so
all closed points of $X$ lie over the special point $s \in \operatorname{Spec}(R
)$, and the entire generic fiber $X_\eta$ consists of non-closed points. However
, the argument above is purely topological and does not rely on any Jacobson pro
perty: the non-smooth locus, being a *closed* subset of a quasi-compact scheme,
must contain a closed point if it is non-empty. This holds regardless of whether
 the closed points lie over the special or generic point of the base. The DVR hy
pothesis is therefore not essential for the conclusion; what matters is that $X$
 is quasi-compact and the smooth locus is open (both guaranteed by $f$ being pro
jective of finite type over an affine base).
