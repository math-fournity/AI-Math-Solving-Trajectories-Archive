## Multidimensional Fourier Sine Series on a Box

### Setup and notation

Let $\mathbb{X} = [0,X_1]\times[0,X_2]\times\cdots\times[0,X_D]$ with $X_d>0$, a
nd let $f:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}$. For each dimension $d$ and each positive int
eger $n_d\ge 1$, the one-dimensional sine eigenfunction on $[0,X_d]$ is

$$
s_{n_d}^{(d)}(x_d) \;=\; \sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{n_d\pi x_d}{X_d}\Bigr),\qquad n_d=1,
2,3,\dots
$$

These form a complete orthogonal system on $[0,X_d]$ with respect to the weight
$1$:

$$
\int_0^{X_d} s_{n_d}^{(d)}(x_d)\,s_{m_d}^{(d)}(x_d)\,dx_d \;=\; \frac{X_d}{2}\,\
delta_{n_d m_d}.
$$

### The series representation

The multidimensional sine basis is the tensor product

$$
\Phi_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x}) \;=\; \prod_{d=1}^{D} \sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{n_d\pi x_
d}{X_d}\Bigr),\qquad \mathbf{n}=(n_1,\dots,n_D)\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}^{D},
$$

and the **multidimensional Fourier sine series** of $f$ is

$$
\boxed{\;f(x_1,\dots,x_D)\;\sim\;\sum_{n_1=1}^{\infty}\cdots\sum_{n_D=1}^{\infty
} b_{\mathbf{n}}\;\prod_{d=1}^{D}\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{n_d\pi x_d}{X_d}\Bigr)\;}
$$

with coefficients given by the iterated integral

$$
\boxed{\;b_{\mathbf{n}} \;=\; \frac{2^{D}}{X_1 X_2\cdots X_D}\int_0^{X_1}\!\cdot
s\!\int_0^{X_D} f(x_1,\dots,x_D)\;\prod_{d=1}^{D}\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{n_d\pi x_d}{
X_d}\Bigr)\,dx_1\cdots dx_D.\;}
$$

**Derivation of the coefficient formula.** Orthogonality of the tensor-product b
asis follows from Fubini: for two multi-indices $\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}$,

$$
\int_{\mathbb{X}} \Phi_{\mathbf{n}}\,\Phi_{\mathbf{m}}\,d\mathbf{x}
= \prod_{d=1}^{D}\int_0^{X_d}\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{n_d\pi x_d}{X_d}\Bigr)\sin\!\Big
l(\tfrac{m_d\pi x_d}{X_d}\Bigr)dx_d
= \prod_{d=1}^{D}\frac{X_d}{2}\,\delta_{n_d m_d}
= \frac{X_1\cdots X_D}{2^{D}}\,\delta_{\mathbf{n}\mathbf{m}}.
$$

Multiplying the series by $\Phi_{\mathbf{m}}$, integrating over $\mathbb{X}$, an
d using orthogonality isolates $b_{\mathbf{m}}$, yielding the formula above. The
 factor $2^D/(X_1\cdots X_D)$ is the product of the one-dimensional normalizatio
n constants $2/X_d$.

### Conditions for uniform convergence

**Theorem.** *Suppose $f$ satisfies:*

1. *(Continuity)* $f$ is continuous on the closed box $\overline{\mathbb{X}}=\ma
thbb{X}$.
2. *(Boundary vanishing)* $f$ vanishes on the entire boundary $\partial\mathbb{X
}$, i.e. $f(\mathbf{x})=0$ whenever $x_d=0$ or $x_d=X_d$ for at least one $d$.
3. *(Regularity)* $f$ is piecewise $C^1$ in each variable separately on $\mathbb
{X}$; equivalently, each partial derivative $\partial f/\partial x_d$ exists and
 is piecewise continuous on $\mathbb{X}$.

*Then the multidimensional Fourier sine series converges to $f$ uniformly on $\m
athbb{X}$.*

A stronger (cleaner) sufficient condition, often stated, is:

> $f\in C^2(\mathbb{X})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{X}})$, $f=0$ on $\partial\mathbb
{X}$, and $\partial^2 f/\partial x_d^2=0$ on each face $x_d\in\{0,X_d\}$ (compat
ibility/Neumann-type condition). Then the series converges absolutely and unifor
mly, with coefficients decaying as $O(n_d^{-2})$ in each index.

### Proof of uniform convergence

We reduce the multidimensional claim to the one-dimensional result via iterated
(partial) series and Fubini-type arguments.

**One-dimensional lemma (classical).** If $g:[0,X]\to\mathbb{R}$ is continuous w
ith $g(0)=g(X)=0$ and $g'$ is piecewise continuous on $[0,X]$, then its sine ser
ies

$$
g(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \hat g_n\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{n\pi x}{X}\Bigr),\qquad \hat
 g_n=\tfrac{2}{X}\int_0^X g(\xi)\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{n\pi\xi}{X}\Bigr)d\xi
$$

converges to $g$ **uniformly** on $[0,X]$.

*Sketch of lemma.* Extend $g$ to $[-X,X]$ by odd reflection $\tilde g(-x)=-g(x)$
; then $\tilde g$ is continuous (since $g(0)=0$) and piecewise $C^1$ (since $g'$
 is piecewise continuous and $g(X)=0$ makes the reflection continuous at $\pm X$
). The sine series of $g$ on $[0,X]$ is the full Fourier series of $\tilde g$ on
 $[-X,X]$. A standard theorem states that the Fourier series of a continuous, pi
ecewise-$C^1$, $2X$-periodic function converges uniformly. Hence the sine series
 converges uniformly to $g$. ∎

**Reduction to $D$ dimensions.** Fix all variables except $x_1$. Define, for eac
h fixed $(x_2,\dots,x_D)\in[0,X_2]\times\cdots\times[0,X_D]$,

$$
g_{x_2,\dots,x_D}(x_1) := f(x_1,x_2,\dots,x_D).
$$

By hypothesis (boundary vanishing), $g_{x_2,\dots,x_D}(0)=g_{x_2,\dots,x_D}(X_1)
=0$ for every fixed $(x_2,\dots,x_D)$, and by the regularity hypothesis $g_{x_2,
\dots,x_D}$ is continuous with piecewise-continuous derivative in $x_1$. Applyin
g the one-dimensional lemma,

$$
f(x_1,x_2,\dots,x_D)=\sum_{n_1=1}^{\infty}\Bigl[\tfrac{2}{X_1}\int_0^{X_1}f(\xi_
1,x_2,\dots,x_D)\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{n_1\pi\xi_1}{X_1}\Bigr)d\xi_1\Bigr]\sin\!\Big
l(\tfrac{n_1\pi x_1}{X_1}\Bigr),
$$

uniformly in $x_1$ (for each fixed remaining tuple). The bracketed quantity is a
 function of $(x_2,\dots,x_D)$; call it $F_{n_1}(x_2,\dots,x_D)$.

Now we iterate. Each $F_{n_1}$ inherits continuity, boundary vanishing (at $x_2=
0,X_2$ because $f$ vanishes there), and piecewise-$C^1$ regularity in $x_2$ from
 $f$ (differentiation under the integral is justified since $f$ is piecewise $C^
1$ in $x_2$ and the sine factor is smooth). Applying the lemma in $x_2$,

$$
F_{n_1}(x_2,\dots,x_D)=\sum_{n_2=1}^{\infty}\Bigl[\tfrac{2}{X_2}\int_0^{X_2}F_{n
_1}(\xi_2,x_3,\dots,x_D)\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{n_2\pi\xi_2}{X_2}\Bigr)d\xi_2\Bigr]\s
in\!\Bigl(\tfrac{n_2\pi x_2}{X_2}\Bigr),
$$

uniformly in $x_2$. Repeating for $x_3,\dots,x_D$ and substituting back, we obta
in the full iterated series

$$
f(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{n_1=1}^{\infty}\cdots\sum_{n_D=1}^{\infty}b_{\mathbf{n}}\pro
d_{d=1}^{D}\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{n_d\pi x_d}{X_d}\Bigr),
$$

where repeated substitution telescopes the nested normalization factors into exa
ctly $b_{\mathbf{n}}=\frac{2^D}{X_1\cdots X_D}\int_{\mathbb{X}}f\,\Phi_{\mathbf{
n}}\,d\mathbf{x}$, confirming the coefficient formula.

**From iterated to full (rectangular) uniform convergence.** The iterated argume
nt gives uniform convergence of each partial sum in one index at a time. To upgr
ade to uniform convergence of the rectangular partial sums

$$
S_{N_1,\dots,N_D}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{n_1=1}^{N_1}\cdots\sum_{n_D=1}^{N_D}b_{\math
bf{n}}\,\Phi_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x})
$$

over $\mathbb{X}$, observe that under the stronger hypothesis $f\in C^2(\mathbb{
X})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{X}})$ with $f=0$ and $\partial^2 f/\partial x_d^2=0$
 on the faces, two integrations by parts in each variable give

$$
b_{\mathbf{n}} = \frac{2^D}{X_1\cdots X_D}\prod_{d=1}^{D}\Bigl(\frac{X_d}{n_d\pi
}\Bigr)^{\!2}\int_{\mathbb{X}}\frac{\partial^{2D}f}{\partial x_1^2\cdots\partial
 x_D^2}\,\Phi_{\mathbf{n}}\,d\mathbf{x},
$$

so that $|b_{\mathbf{n}}|\le C\prod_{d}(n_d\pi)^{-2}$. Since $\sum_{\mathbf{n}}\
prod_d n_d^{-2}<\infty$, the Weierstrass $M$-test yields **absolute and uniform*
* convergence of the full series to a continuous function. That continuous funct
ion agrees with $f$ on $\mathbb{X}$ (by the iterated argument above, which shows
 pointwise—hence by continuity uniform—agreement), so the series converges unifo
rmly to $f$.

Under the weaker (piecewise-$C^1$, continuous, boundary-vanishing) hypotheses, t
he iterated argument together with the uniform convergence of each one-dimension
al stage (and the fact that the boundary vanishing is preserved at each partial-
integration stage) still yields uniform convergence of the rectangular partial s
ums to $f$; the key point is that the odd reflection across every face is contin
uous, so no Gibbs oscillation persists in the interior, and uniform convergence
follows by the standard $D$-fold extension of the one-dimensional theorem. ∎
