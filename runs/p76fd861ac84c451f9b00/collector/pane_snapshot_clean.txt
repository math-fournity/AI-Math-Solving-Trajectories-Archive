# Solution

## Step 1: Simplify the function using binomial coefficients

The product $x(x-1)\cdots(x-k)$ contains $k+1$ consecutive factors, so:
$$x(x-1)\cdots(x-k) = (k+1)!\,\binom{x}{k+1}$$

Substituting:
$$f = 1 + \sum_{k=0}^{100}(-1)^{k+1}\binom{x}{k+1}$$

Setting $j = k+1$ (so $j$ runs from $1$ to $101$) and noting $1 = \binom{x}{0}$:
$$f = \sum_{j=0}^{101}(-1)^{j}\binom{x}{j}$$

## Step 2: Apply the alternating binomial sum identity

Using the well-known identity (proved by induction via Pascal's rule $\binom{x}{
n+1}=\binom{x-1}{n+1}+\binom{x-1}{n}$):
$$\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\binom{x}{j} = (-1)^{n}\binom{x-1}{n}$$

With $n = 101$:
$$f(x) = (-1)^{101}\binom{x-1}{101} = -\frac{(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-101)}{101!}$$

## Step 3: Find $S$ (sum of real roots of $f$)

The roots of $f$ are $x = 1, 2, 3, \ldots, 101$ (all simple roots).

$$S = 1 + 2 + \cdots + 101 = \frac{101 \cdot 102}{2} = 5151$$

## Step 4: Find $T$ (sum of real roots of $f'$)

Since $f$ has 101 simple real roots at $1, 2, \ldots, 101$, by Rolle's theorem $
f'$ has at least one root in each of the 100 intervals $(k, k+1)$, $k=1,\ldots,1
00$. Since $\deg(f') = 100$, these are **all** the roots, and all are real.

By Vieta's formulas, writing $f(x) = -\frac{1}{101!}\left[x^{101} - S\,x^{100} +
 \cdots\right]$:

$$f'(x) = -\frac{101}{101!}x^{100} + \frac{100\,S}{101!}x^{99} - \cdots$$

The sum of roots of $f'$ is:
$$T = -\frac{100\,S/101!}{-101/101!} = \frac{100\,S}{101} = \frac{100 \cdot 5151
}{101}$$

Since $5151 = 101 \times 51$:
$$T = 100 \times 51 = 5100$$

## Step 5: Compute $S - T$

$$S - T = 5151 - 5100 = \boxed{51}$$
