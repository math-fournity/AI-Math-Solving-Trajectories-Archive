**Interpretation.** "Eventually almost periodic with period $p$" means the perio

$$\lim_{t\to\infty}\bigl(f(t+p)-f(t)\bigr)=0.$$
So the hypotheses give us
$$\lim_{t\to\infty}\bigl(f(t+1)-f(t)\bigr)=0,\qquad \lim_{t\to\infty}\bigl(f(t+a

and $F(x):=\lim_{n\to\infty}f(x+n)$ exists for $x\in[0,1)$.

**Proof.**

**Step 1: $F$ is well-defined and measurable.** Each map $x\mapsto f(x+n)$ is co
ntinuous on $[0,1)$ (since $f$ is continuous), and $F$ is their pointwise limit,
 so $F$ is Borel measurable.

**Step 2: Derive a functional equation for $F$ from the period-$a$ condition.**
Fix $x\in[0,1)$ and set $t=x+n$, $n\to\infty$. By hypothesis,
$$f(x+n+a)-f(x+n)\to 0.$$

- **Case $x\in[0,1-a)$:** Then $x+a\in[a,1)\subset[0,1)$, so $x+n+a=(x+a)+n$ and
$$f(x+n+a)=f\bigl((x+a)+n\bigr)\to F(x+a).$$
Also $f(x+n)\to F(x)$. Hence $F(x+a)-F(x)=0$.

- **Case $x\in[1-a,1)$:** Then $x+a\in[1,1+a)$, so write $x+n+a=(x+a-1)+(n+1)$,
giving
$$f(x+n+a)=f\bigl((x+a-1)+(n+1)\bigr)\to F(x+a-1).$$
Hence $F(x+a-1)-F(x)=0$.

Combining both cases, with $T:[0,1)\to[0,1)$ the rotation $T(x)=x+a\pmod 1$:
$$\boxed{F(Tx)=F(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in[0,1).}$$

Here we have the stronger statement $F\circ T = F$ **everywhere**, so certainly
$F$ is $T$-invariant a.e. By ergodicity, $F$ is constant almost everywhere.
