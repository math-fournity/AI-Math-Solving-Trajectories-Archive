## Proof

We show there are **infinitely many** solutions in positive integers to $x^7 + y
^8 = z^9$, by constructing an infinite parametric family.

**Construction.** Set $a = 2$ and seek solutions of the form $x = 2^A,\; y = 2^B
,\; z = 2^C$ with $A, B, C$ positive integers. The equation becomes
$$2^{7A} + 2^{8B} = 2^{9C}.$$

Choose $7A = 8B$ so that the two left-hand terms share the same power of $2$. Th
en
$$2^{7A} + 2^{7A} = 2 \cdot 2^{7A} = 2^{7A+1},$$
so we need $7A + 1 = 9C$, i.e.
$$9C - 7A = 1. \tag{$\star$}$$

From $7A = 8B$, we need $A$ divisible by $8$ and $B$ divisible by $7$. Write $A
= 8t,\; B = 7t$ for a positive integer $t$. Substituting into ($\star$):
$$9C - 56t = 1 \implies 9C = 56t + 1.$$
We need $56t + 1 \equiv 0 \pmod{9}$. Since $56 \equiv 2 \pmod{9}$, this gives $2
t \equiv -1 \equiv 8 \pmod{9}$, i.e. $t \equiv 4 \pmod{9}$.

Thus $t = 4 + 9s$ for any non-negative integer $s$, yielding
$$A = 8(4+9s) = 32+72s, \quad B = 7(4+9s) = 28+63s, \quad C = \frac{56(4+9s)+1}{
9} = 25+56s.$$

**Verification.** For each $s \geq 0$, define
$$x = 2^{32+72s},\quad y = 2^{28+63s},\quad z = 2^{25+56s}.$$
Then
$$x^7 = 2^{224+504s}, \quad y^8 = 2^{224+504s},$$
$$x^7 + y^8 = 2 \cdot 2^{224+504s} = 2^{225+504s},$$
$$z^9 = 2^{9(25+56s)} = 2^{225+504s}. \quad \checkmark$$

All of $x, y, z$ are positive integers, and as $s$ ranges over $0, 1, 2, \dots$
the solutions are distinct (the exponents grow without bound).

**Conclusion.** The equation $x^7 + y^8 = z^9$ has **infinitely many** solutions
 in positive integers.
