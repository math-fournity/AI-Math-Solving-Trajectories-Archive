## Answer

**Yes — a closed subset of isolated points in a compact set is necessarily finit
e.**

### Proof

Let $X$ be a topological space, $K \subseteq X$ a compact set, and $S \subseteq
K$ a closed set such that every point of $S$ is an isolated point of $K$. We sho
w $S$ is finite.

**Step 1: $S$ is compact.**
Since $S$ is closed in $K$ (closed in $X$ and $S\subseteq K$ implies closed in $
K$) and $K$ is compact, $S$ is compact. Indeed, given any open cover $\{V_\alpha
\}$ of $S$ in $X$, the family $\{V_\alpha\}\cup\{X\setminus S\}$ is an open cove
r of $K$; by compactness of $K$ it has a finite subcover, which restricts to a f
inite subcover of $S$.

**Step 2: Each singleton $\{s\}$, $s\in S$, is open in $S$.**
Since $s$ is an isolated point of $K$, there exists an open set $U_s\subseteq X$
 with $U_s\cap K=\{s\}$. Because $S\subseteq K$, we have $U_s\cap S=\{s\}$, so $
\{s\}$ is open in the subspace topology of $S$.

**Step 3: $S$ is finite.**
By Step 2, the family $\bigl\{\{s\}:s\in S\bigr\}$ is an open cover of $S$ (each
 member is open in $S$, and their union is $S$). By Step 1, $S$ is compact, so t
his cover admits a finite subcover. A finite subcover of singletons is a finite
collection $\{s_1\},\dots,\{s_n\}$ whose union is $S$, giving $S=\{s_1,\dots,s_n
\}$. Hence $S$ is finite. $\blacksquare$

**Key ingredients.** (i) A closed subset of a compact set is compact (holds in a
ny topological space). (ii) Isolated points of $K$ yield open singletons in any
subset $S\subseteq K$ consisting of such points. (iii) A compact space whose sin
gletons are all open — i.e., a compact discrete space — must be finite (the sing
leton cover has no proper subcover).

**Remark on interpretations.** The same conclusion holds under the alternative r
eading where "isolated points" means isolated in $S$ (i.e., $S$ is discrete): th
en $S$ is a closed (hence compact) discrete subspace of $K$, and a compact discr
ete space is finite by the same singleton-cover argument.
