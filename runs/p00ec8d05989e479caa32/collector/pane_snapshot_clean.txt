## Proof

Let $f$ be holomorphic on $D(0,1)$ with $|f(z)|\le 1$ for all $z\in D(0,1)$, and
 suppose $f$ has a zero of order $4$ at $z=0$.

**Step 1: Factor out the zero.** Since $f$ has a zero of order $4$ at $0$, we ma
y write
$$f(z) = z^4\, g(z),$$
where $g$ is holomorphic on $D(0,1)$ (the singularity of $f(z)/z^4$ at $0$ is re
movable, and the value there is $g(0) = f^{(4)}(0)/4!$).

**Step 2: Bound $|g(0)|$ via the maximum modulus principle.** Fix any $r$ with $
0 < r < 1$. On the circle $|z| = r$, we have
$$|g(z)| = \frac{|f(z)|}{|z|^4} = \frac{|f(z)|}{r^4} \le \frac{1}{r^4},$$
since $|f(z)|\le 1$. By the maximum modulus principle applied to $g$ on the clos
ed disk $\overline{D(0,r)}$,
$$|g(0)| \le \frac{1}{r^4}.$$
This holds for every $r\in(0,1)$. Letting $r\to 1^-$ gives
$$|g(0)| \le 1.$$

**Step 3: Translate to $|f^{(4)}(0)|$.** Since $f^{(4)}(0) = 4!\, g(0) = 24\, g(
0)$, we obtain
$$|f^{(4)}(0)| \le 24.$$

**Step 4: Equality cases.** Equality $|g(0)|=1$ requires $|g(0)| = 1/r^4$ for a
sequence $r\to 1^-$, which forces $g$ to attain its maximum modulus at an interi
or point. By the maximum modulus principle, $g$ must be constant. Since $|g(0)|=
1$, this constant has modulus $1$, so $g(z) = e^{i\theta}$ for some $\theta\in\m
athbb{R}$. Therefore
$$f(z) = e^{i\theta}\, z^4.$$
Conversely, every function of this form is holomorphic on $D(0,1)$, satisfies $|
f(z)|=|z|^4\le 1$, has a zero of order $4$ at $0$, and $f^{(4)}(0) = 24\, e^{i\t
heta}$, so $|f^{(4)}(0)|=24$.

**Conclusion.** The maximum possible value of $|f^{(4)}(0)|$ is $\boxed{24}$, ac
hieved exactly by the functions $f(z) = e^{i\theta}z^4$ for $\theta\in\mathbb{R}
$.
