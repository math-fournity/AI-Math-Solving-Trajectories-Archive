## Answer

**Theorem.** Let $X$ be a locally compact Hausdorff space (the standard setting
in which the one-point compactification $X_\infty = X \cup \{\infty\}$ is Hausdo
rff). Then

$$
X_\infty \text{ is second countable} \iff X \text{ is second countable and } \si
gma\text{-compact}.
$$

---

### Proof

Recall the topology of $X_\infty$: open sets are (i) all open subsets of $X$, an
d (ii) all sets of the form $X_\infty \setminus C$ where $C \subseteq X$ is comp
act (hence closed, since $X$ is Hausdorff). When $X$ is locally compact Hausdorf
f, $X_\infty$ is compact Hausdorff.

#### ($\Longrightarrow$) Suppose $X_\infty$ is second countable.

1. **$X$ is second countable.** $X$ is a subspace of $X_\infty$, and second coun
tability is hereditary (intersect a countable base of $X_\infty$ with $X$). Henc
e $X$ is second countable.

2. **$X$ is $\sigma$-compact.** $X_\infty$ is compact Hausdorff and second count
able, so by Urysohn's metrization theorem $X_\infty$ is metrizable; let $d$ be a
 compatible metric. Since $X = X_\infty \setminus \{\infty\}$ is open in the com
pact metric space $X_\infty$, we can write
$$
X = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} \left\{x \in X_\infty : d(x,\infty) \ge \tfrac{1}{n}\
right\}.
$$
Each set $\{x : d(x,\infty) \ge 1/n\}$ is closed in the compact space $X_\infty$
, hence compact. Therefore $X$ is a countable union of compact sets, i.e. $\sigm
a$-compact.

#### ($\Longleftarrow$) Suppose $X$ is second countable and $\sigma$-compact.

1. **A countable base of precompact open sets.** Let $\{U_n\}_{n\ge 1}$ be a cou
ntable base for $X$. Since $X$ is locally compact Hausdorff, the relatively comp
act open sets form a base. Concretely, for every open $V \ni x$ there is an open
 $W$ with $x \in W \subseteq \overline{W} \subseteq V$ and $\overline{W}$ compac
t; picking $U_n$ with $x \in U_n \subseteq W$ gives $\overline{U_n} \subseteq \o
verline{W}$ compact. Hence
$$
\mathcal{B} := \{U_n : \overline{U_n} \text{ is compact}\}
$$
is a countable base for $X$ consisting of open sets with compact closure.

2. **An increasing $\sigma$-compact exhaustion.** Write $X = \bigcup_{n} K_n$ wi
th each $K_n$ compact, and replace $K_n$ by $K_1 \cup \cdots \cup K_n$ so that $
K_1 \subseteq K_2 \subseteq \cdots$.

3. **A countable base for $X_\infty$.** Define
$$
\mathcal{B}_\infty := \mathcal{B} \cup \{X_\infty \setminus K_n : n \ge 1\}.
$$
This is countable. We verify it is a base. Let $U$ be open in $X_\infty$ and $p
\in U$.

   - **If $p \in X$:** then $U \cap X$ is an open neighborhood of $p$ in $X$, so
 there exists $B \in \mathcal{B}$ with $p \in B \subseteq U \cap X \subseteq U$.

   - **If $p = \infty$:** then $U$ is a neighborhood of $\infty$, so $U = X_\inf
ty \setminus C$ for some compact $C \subseteq X$. Since $C \subseteq \bigcup_n K
_n$ with $K_n$ increasing and $C$ compact, there exists $N$ with $C \subseteq K_
N$. Then
   $$
   \infty \in X_\infty \setminus K_N \subseteq X_\infty \setminus C = U.
   $$

   In both cases we found a member of $\mathcal{B}_\infty$ containing $p$ and co
ntained in $U$. Hence $\mathcal{B}_\infty$ is a countable base, and $X_\infty$ i
s second countable. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark on the general (non-locally-compact) case.** If one defines the Alexand
roff extension $X_\infty$ for an arbitrary topological space (open neighborhoods
 of $\infty$ being complements of closed compact subsets of $X$), the space $X_\
infty$ is always compact but is Hausdorff only when $X$ is locally compact Hausd
orff. The second-countability criterion in full generality reads: $X_\infty$ is
second countable if and only if $X$ is second countable **and** the directed fam
ily of closed compact subsets of $X$ admits a countable cofinal subfamily. In th
e locally compact Hausdorff setting this cofinality condition is equivalent to $
\sigma$-compactness, recovering the theorem above.
