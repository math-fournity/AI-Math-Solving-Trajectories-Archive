## Proof

**Goal.** Evaluate $l = \displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\exp\!\b
igl(\cos^{n}x - x\bigr)\,dx$ using the Dominated Convergence Theorem (DCT).

---

**Step 1. Pointwise limit of the integrand.**

Set $f_n(x) = \exp(\cos^{n}x - x) = e^{\cos^{n}x}\,e^{-x}$ for $x\in[0,+\infty)$
.

For a.e. $x\ge 0$ we have $|\cos x|<1$, hence $\cos^{n}x\to 0$. The exceptional
set
$$E=\{x\ge 0:|\cos x|=1\}=\{k\pi:k=0,1,2,\dots\}$$
is countable, hence of Lebesgue measure zero. Therefore, for a.e. $x\ge 0$,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} f_n(x)=\exp(0-x)=e^{-x}.$$

---

**Step 2. Dominating function.**

Since $|\cos x|\le 1$, for every $n\ge 1$ we have $\cos^{n}x\le 1$ (when $n$ is
even, $\cos^{n}x\in[0,1]$; when $n$ is odd, $\cos^{n}x\in[-1,1]\le 1$). Thus
$$f_n(x)=e^{\cos^{n}x}\,e^{-x}\le e^{1}\,e^{-x}=e\,e^{-x}.$$
Clearly $f_n(x)\ge 0$, so $|f_n(x)|\le e\,e^{-x}$. The function $g(x)=e\,e^{-x}$
 is integrable on $[0,+\infty)$:
$$\int_{0}^{\infty} e\,e^{-x}\,dx = e\cdot 1 = e < \infty.$$

---

**Step 3. Apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem.**

We have verified:
- $f_n\to e^{-x}$ a.e. on $[0,+\infty)$,
- $|f_n|\le g$ with $g\in L^{1}([0,+\infty))$,
- each $f_n$ is measurable (continuous, in fact).

By the DCT,
$$l=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}f_n(x)\,dx=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lim_{n\to\i
nfty}f_n(x)\,dx=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-x}\,dx.$$

---

**Step 4. Evaluate.**

$$l=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-x}\,dx=\Bigl[-e^{-x}\Bigr]_{0}^{+\infty}=0-(-1)=1.$$

---

$$\boxed{l=1}$$
