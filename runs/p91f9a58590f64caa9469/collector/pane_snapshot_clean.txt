## Proof

**Model.** Suppose the conference lasts $t$ hours. For each teacher $i$ ($1 \le
i \le 11$), let $S_i \subseteq \{1,2,\dots,t\}$ be the set of hours in which tea
cher $i$ gives a presentation. In hour $h$, the presenters are $\{i : h \in S_i\
}$ and the observers are $\{i : h \notin S_i\}$.

**Reformulation as an antichain.** Teacher $i$ observes teacher $j$ if and only
if there exists an hour $h$ with $j$ presenting and $i$ observing, i.e., $h \in
S_j \setminus S_i$. The requirement that *every* teacher observes *every* other
teacher at least once is therefore:

$$\text{For all } i \ne j: \quad S_j \not\subseteq S_i.$$

Applying this with $i$ and $j$ swapped as well, we get: for all $i \ne j$, neith
er $S_i \subseteq S_j$ nor $S_j \subseteq S_i$. In other words, $\{S_1, \dots, S
_{11}\}$ is an **antichain** (a Sperner family) in the Boolean lattice $2^{[t]}$
.

Additional constraints:
- Each $S_i \ne \emptyset$ (each teacher must present at least once; otherwise $
S_i = \emptyset \subseteq S_j$ for all $j$, violating the condition).
- Each hour $h$ has at least one presenter: $\exists\, i$ with $h \in S_i$ (othe
rwise the hour is wasted and can be removed).

**Lower bound: $t \ge 6$.** By Sperner's theorem, the largest antichain in $2^{[
t]}$ has size $\binom{t}{\lfloor t/2\rfloor}$. We need an antichain of size $11$
, so we need $\binom{t}{\lfloor t/2\rfloor} \ge 11$.

| $t$ | $\binom{t}{\lfloor t/2\rfloor}$ |
|-----|-------------------------------|
| 4   | $\binom{4}{2} = 6$            |
| 5   | $\binom{5}{2} = 10$           |
| 6   | $\binom{6}{3} = 20$           |

For $t \le 5$, the maximum antichain size is at most $10 < 11$, so it is **impos
sible** to have $11$ mutually incomparable subsets. Hence $t \ge 6$.

**Upper bound: $t = 6$ suffices.** Take $11$ distinct $3$-element subsets of $\{
1,2,3,4,5,6\}$, one per teacher, for example:

$$\{1,2,3\},\; \{1,2,4\},\; \{1,2,5\},\; \{1,2,6\},\; \{1,3,4\},\; \{1,3,5\},\;
\{1,3,6\},\; \{1,4,5\},\; \{2,4,6\},\; \{3,5,6\},\; \{4,5,6\}.$$

Since all $11$ sets have the same cardinality $3$, no one is contained in anothe
r; they form an antichain. Every element $1$ through $6$ appears in at least one
 set (e.g., $1$ appears in the first $8$, $2$ in the first $4$ and the $9$th, $3
$ in several, $4,5,6$ each in several), so every hour has at least one presenter
. Moreover, each element belongs to at most $10$ of the $20$ three-element subse
ts of $[6]$, so no hour has all $11$ teachers presenting—there is always at leas
t one observer.

For any two teachers $i \ne j$: since $|S_i| = |S_j| = 3$ and $S_i \ne S_j$, nei
ther set contains the other, so $S_j \setminus S_i \ne \emptyset$. Thus there is
 an hour where $j$ presents and $i$ observes. This holds for every ordered pair,
 so every teacher observes every other teacher.

**Conclusion.** The minimum is $t = 6$.

### PROOF COMPLETE
