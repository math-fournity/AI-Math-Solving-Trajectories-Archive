## Proof

We work in the space of distributions $\mathcal{D}'(\mathbb{R})$, where a sequen
ce $\{T_n\}$ converges to $T$ (convergence in $\mathcal{D}'$, i.e. weak-* conver
gence) iff for every test function $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$,
$$\langle T_n, \varphi\rangle \to \langle T, \varphi\rangle.$$

Here each $\phi_n(x) = e^{-x^2/n^2}$ is a smooth, bounded function, hence define
s a regular distribution via
$$\langle \phi_n, \varphi\rangle = \int_{\mathbb{R}} e^{-x^2/n^2}\,\varphi(x)\,d
x.$$

**Claim:** $\phi_n \to \mathbf{1}$ in $\mathcal{D}'(\mathbb{R})$, where $\mathbf
{1}$ is the regular distribution associated to the constant function $1$:
$$\langle \mathbf{1}, \varphi\rangle = \int_{\mathbb{R}} \varphi(x)\,dx.$$

**Proof of the claim.** Fix an arbitrary $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$. L
et $\operatorname{supp}\varphi \subseteq [-R,R]$ for some $R>0$. Then
$$\langle \phi_n, \varphi\rangle = \int_{-R}^{R} e^{-x^2/n^2}\,\varphi(x)\,dx.$$

We apply the **Dominated Convergence Theorem** on the finite interval $[-R,R]$:

1. **Pointwise limit.** For every fixed $x \in [-R,R]$,
$$e^{-x^2/n^2} \xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{} e^{0} = 1,$$
   since $x^2/n^2 \to 0$. Hence the integrand converges pointwise:
$$e^{-x^2/n^2}\,\varphi(x) \to 1\cdot\varphi(x) = \varphi(x).$$

2. **Dominating function.** Since $x^2/n^2 \ge 0$, we have $0 < e^{-x^2/n^2} \le
 1$ for all $x$ and all $n$. Therefore
$$\bigl|e^{-x^2/n^2}\,\varphi(x)\bigr| \le |\varphi(x)|,$$
   and $|\varphi| \in L^1([-R,R])$ because $\varphi$ is continuous on a compact
set.

By dominated convergence,
$$\langle \phi_n, \varphi\rangle = \int_{-R}^{R} e^{-x^2/n^2}\,\varphi(x)\,dx \x
rightarrow[n\to\infty]{} \int_{-R}^{R} \varphi(x)\,dx = \int_{\mathbb{R}} \varph
i(x)\,dx = \langle \mathbf{1}, \varphi\rangle.$$

**Conclusion.** Since $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ was arbitrary, we hav
e shown
$$\forall\,\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}):\quad \langle \phi_n,\varphi\rangle
 \to \langle \mathbf{1},\varphi\rangle.$$
This is precisely the definition of convergence in $\mathcal{D}'(\mathbb{R})$. T
herefore
$$\boxed{\;\phi_n \to \mathbf{1} \text{ in } \mathcal{D}'(\mathbb{R})\;}$$
where $\mathbf{1}$ is the regular distribution induced by the constant function
$1$.

**Remark (analytical viewpoint).** The mechanism is that the Gaussian $e^{-x^2/n
^2}$ flattens out as $n$ grows: its "width" scales like $n$, so on any fixed com
pact support of a test function it becomes uniformly close to $1$. The compact s
upport of test functions is essential — it localizes the integration and supplie
s the $L^1$ dominating function $|\varphi|$, which is what makes dominated conve
rgence applicable. Without compact support (e.g. in $\mathcal{S}'$ with Schwartz
 test functions) the same conclusion still holds by a similar dominated-converge
nce argument using $|\varphi|\in L^1$, but the $\mathcal{D}'$ setting is the cle
anest: compact support gives boundedness of the integrand for free.
