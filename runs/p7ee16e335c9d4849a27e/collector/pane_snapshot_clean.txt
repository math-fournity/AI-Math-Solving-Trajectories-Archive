## Solution

**Claim:** $f(z)=\sqrt{z\sin z}$ can be defined as a single-valued analytic func
tion near the origin, and the radius of convergence of its Taylor series about $
z=0$ is $\pi$.

---

### Step 1. Factor the radicand

Write
$$z\sin z = z^2\cdot\frac{\sin z}{z} = z^2\,h(z),\qquad h(z):=\frac{\sin z}{z},\
; h(0):=1.$$
The function $h$ is **entire** (the removable singularity at $0$ is filled by $h
(0)=1$), and $h(0)=1\neq 0$.

### Step 2. Single-valued analytic square root near $0$

Since $h(0)=1>0$, there is a neighborhood of $0$ on which $h$ takes values in a
simply connected region avoiding $0$. On any simply connected domain where $h\ne
q 0$, a single-valued analytic branch of $\log h$ exists, and hence
$$\sqrt{h(z)}:=\exp\!\bigl(\tfrac12\log h(z)\bigr)$$
is a single-valued analytic function with $\sqrt{h(0)}=1$.

Choosing the branch of $\sqrt{z^2}$ given by $\sqrt{z^2}=z$ (valid near $0$), we
 obtain
$$f(z)=\sqrt{z\sin z}=z\,\sqrt{h(z)},$$
which is single-valued and analytic near $z=0$, with $f(0)=0$. The apparent bran
ch point at $0$ is **removable** because $z\sin z$ has a zero of *even* order ($
2$) there.

### Step 3. Maximal disk of analyticity

The singularities (branch points) of $f$ occur at the zeros of $z\sin z$ that ar
e zeros of **odd** order:

- $z=0$: zero of order $2$ (even) → removable, not a singularity.
- $z=n\pi,\; n\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$: here $\sin(n\pi)=0$, and
  $$(z\sin z)'\big|_{n\pi}=\sin(n\pi)+n\pi\cos(n\pi)=n\pi\,(-1)^n\neq 0,$$
  so each $n\pi\neq 0$ is a **simple** (order $1$, odd) zero of $z\sin z$, hence
 a genuine branch point of the square root.

These branch points are precisely at $z=\pm\pi,\pm 2\pi,\pm 3\pi,\dots$. The one
s nearest to the origin are $z=\pm\pi$, both at distance $\pi$.

### Step 4. Analyticity on $|z|<\pi$

On the open disk $D=\{z:|z|<\pi\}$:
- $D$ is simply connected.
- $h(z)=\sin z/z$ has no zeros in $D$: its zeros are at $n\pi,\;n\neq 0$, all ly
ing on or outside $|z|=\pi$ (the nearest, $\pm\pi$, are on the boundary).
- Hence $h\neq 0$ on $D$, so $\sqrt{h}$ is analytic on $D$, and $f(z)=z\sqrt{h(z
)}$ is analytic on all of $D$.

Since $z=\pi$ and $z=-\pi$ are branch points lying on the boundary $|z|=\pi$, th
e Taylor series of $f$ about $0$ cannot converge beyond radius $\pi$ (a power se
ries converges in a disk free of singularities, and these branch points are at d
istance exactly $\pi$).

### Conclusion

The function $f(z)=\sqrt{z\sin z}$ **can** be defined as a single-valued analyti
c function near the origin, and the radius of convergence of its power series ex
pansion about $z=0$ is

$$\boxed{\pi}.$$
