# Covariant Derivative of Contravariant Components of a Second-Order Tensor

## Setup and Goal

Let $T^{ij}$ be the contravariant components of a second-order tensor in general
 coordinates $x^i$. Under a coordinate transformation $x^i \to x'^i$, the compon
ents transform as

$$T'^{ij} = \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^p}\frac{\partial x'^j}{\partial x^q}
\,T^{pq}.$$

We seek an expression $T^{ij}_{\ :k}$ that (a) reduces to $\partial T^{ij}/\part
ial x^k$ in Cartesian coordinates (where $\Gamma \equiv 0$), and (b) transforms
as a genuine third-order tensor (two contravariant, one covariant):

$$T'^{ij}_{\ :k} = \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^p}\frac{\partial x'^j}{\parti
al x^q}\frac{\partial x^r}{\partial x'^k}\,T^{pq}_{\ :r}.$$

The ordinary partial derivative $\partial T^{ij}/\partial x^k$ fails condition (
b), because differentiating the transformation law produces extra second-derivat
ive terms. We identify and cancel these using Christoffel symbols.

---

## Key Identity

We need the transformation property of second derivatives of the coordinate func
tions. Differentiating the identity $\frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^p}\frac{\pa
rtial x^p}{\partial x'^j}=\delta^i_j$ and using the standard Christoffel transfo
rmation law, one obtains

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial^2 x'^i}{\partial x^p\,\partial x^r} = \frac{\partial x'^
i}{\partial x^m}\,\Gamma^{m}_{pr} \;-\; \Gamma'^{i}_{\alpha\beta}\,\frac{\partia
l x'^\alpha}{\partial x^p}\frac{\partial x'^\beta}{\partial x^r}.}$$

*(Derivation sketch: start from $\frac{\partial}{\partial x^r}\!\bigl(\frac{\par
tial x'^i}{\partial x^p}\frac{\partial x^p}{\partial x'^j}\bigr)=0$, expand $\fr
ac{\partial}{\partial x^r}\!\bigl(\frac{\partial x^p}{\partial x'^j}\bigr)$ via
the chain rule, and substitute the known identity $\frac{\partial^2 x^p}{\partia
l x'^j\partial x'^l}=\frac{\partial x^p}{\partial x'^\alpha}\Gamma'^\alpha_{jl}-
\frac{\partial x^c}{\partial x'^j}\frac{\partial x^d}{\partial x'^l}\Gamma^p_{cd
}$, which itself follows from the Christoffel transformation law.)*

---

## Direct Derivation

**Step 1 — Expand the partial derivative in the primed frame.**

$$\frac{\partial T'^{ij}}{\partial x'^k} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x'^k}\!\left
(\frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^p}\frac{\partial x'^j}{\partial x^q}\,T^{pq}\ri
ght).$$

Applying the product rule and $\frac{\partial}{\partial x'^k}=\frac{\partial x^r
}{\partial x'^k}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^r}$:

$$\frac{\partial T'^{ij}}{\partial x'^k} = \underbrace{\frac{\partial x'^i}{\par
tial x^p}\frac{\partial x'^j}{\partial x^q}\frac{\partial x^r}{\partial x'^k}\fr
ac{\partial T^{pq}}{\partial x^r}}_{\text{(I)}} + \underbrace{\frac{\partial^2 x
'^i}{\partial x^p\,\partial x^r}\frac{\partial x^r}{\partial x'^k}\frac{\partial
 x'^j}{\partial x^q}\,T^{pq}}_{\text{(II)}} + \underbrace{\frac{\partial x'^i}{\
partial x^p}\frac{\partial^2 x'^j}{\partial x^q\,\partial x^r}\frac{\partial x^r
}{\partial x'^k}\,T^{pq}}_{\text{(III)}}.$$

Terms (II) and (III) are the non-tensorial pieces that must be cancelled.

**Step 2 — Apply the key identity to terms (II) and (III).**

Contracting the identity with $\frac{\partial x^r}{\partial x'^k}$:

$$\frac{\partial^2 x'^i}{\partial x^p\,\partial x^r}\frac{\partial x^r}{\partial
 x'^k} = \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^m}\,\Gamma^{m}_{pr}\frac{\partial x^r}{
\partial x'^k} - \Gamma'^{i}_{\alpha k}\frac{\partial x'^\alpha}{\partial x^p},$
$

and similarly for the $j$ index. Substituting into (II):

$$\text{(II)} = \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^m}\frac{\partial x'^j}{\partial
x^q}\frac{\partial x^r}{\partial x'^k}\,\Gamma^{m}_{pr}\,T^{pq} \;-\; \Gamma'^{i
}_{\alpha k}\,T'^{\alpha j},$$

and into (III):

$$\text{(III)} = \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^p}\frac{\partial x'^j}{\partial
 x^m}\frac{\partial x^r}{\partial x'^k}\,\Gamma^{m}_{qr}\,T^{pq} \;-\; \Gamma'^{
j}_{\alpha k}\,T'^{i\alpha}.$$

**Step 3 — Move the $\Gamma'$ terms to the left and collect.**

$$\frac{\partial T'^{ij}}{\partial x'^k} + \Gamma'^{i}_{k\alpha}\,T'^{\alpha j}
+ \Gamma'^{j}_{k\alpha}\,T'^{i\alpha} = \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^p}\frac{
\partial x'^j}{\partial x^q}\frac{\partial x^r}{\partial x'^k}\left[\frac{\parti
al T^{pq}}{\partial x^r} + \Gamma^{p}_{r\alpha}\,T^{\alpha q} + \Gamma^{q}_{r\al
pha}\,T^{p\alpha}\right],$$

where in the last line we relabeled dummy indices ($m\to p,\;p\to\alpha$ in the
second term; $m\to q,\;q\to\alpha$ in the third) and used the symmetry $\Gamma^m
_{pr}=\Gamma^m_{rp}$.

**Step 4 — Read off the tensorial quantity.**

The left-hand side depends only on primed quantities; the right-hand side is the
 tensor transformation law applied to the bracketed expression. Therefore the br
acketed expression **is** a third-order tensor, and we define:

$$\boxed{T^{ij}_{\ :k} \;=\; \frac{\partial T^{ij}}{\partial x^k} \;+\; \Gamma^{
i}_{k\alpha}\,T^{\alpha j} \;+\; \Gamma^{j}_{k\alpha}\,T^{i\alpha}.}$$

It transforms as

$$T'^{ij}_{\ :k} = \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^p}\frac{\partial x'^j}{\parti
al x^q}\frac{\partial x^r}{\partial x'^k}\,T^{pq}_{\ :r},$$

confirming tensorial character.

---

## Verification via the Leibniz Rule

As an independent check, decompose $T^{ij}$ into a sum of outer products of cont
ravariant vectors, $T^{ij}=\sum A^i B^j$ (always possible). The known vector res
ult is $V^i_{\ :k}=\partial_k V^i+\Gamma^i_{k\alpha}V^\alpha$. Requiring the cov
ariant derivative to satisfy the Leibniz rule:

$$T^{ij}_{\ :k} = (A^i B^j)_{:k} = A^i_{:k}\,B^j + A^i\,B^j_{:k}$$

$$= \left(\frac{\partial A^i}{\partial x^k}+\Gamma^i_{k\alpha}A^\alpha\right)B^j
 + A^i\left(\frac{\partial B^j}{\partial x^k}+\Gamma^j_{k\alpha}B^\alpha\right)$
$

$$= \frac{\partial(A^i B^j)}{\partial x^k} + \Gamma^i_{k\alpha}\,A^\alpha B^j +
\Gamma^j_{k\alpha}\,A^i B^\alpha.$$

Summing over the decomposition recovers exactly

$$T^{ij}_{\ :k} = \frac{\partial T^{ij}}{\partial x^k} + \Gamma^i_{k\alpha}\,T^{
\alpha j} + \Gamma^j_{k\alpha}\,T^{i\alpha},$$

confirming the direct derivation.

---

## Result and Interpretation

$$\boxed{T^{ij}_{\ :k} = \frac{\partial T^{ij}}{\partial x^k} + \Gamma^{i}_{k\al
pha}\,T^{\alpha j} + \Gamma^{j}_{k\alpha}\,T^{i\alpha}}$$

Each **contravariant (upper) index** contributes one **positive** Christoffel co
rrection term: $+\Gamma^{\,\bullet}_{k\alpha}$ contracting the index with a dumm
y index on $T$. The correction for index $i$ is $\Gamma^i_{k\alpha}T^{\alpha j}$
 (dummy replaces $i$), and the correction for index $j$ is $\Gamma^j_{k\alpha}T^
{i\alpha}$ (dummy replaces $j$). In Cartesian coordinates $\Gamma\equiv 0$ and t
he expression reduces to the ordinary partial derivative, as required.
