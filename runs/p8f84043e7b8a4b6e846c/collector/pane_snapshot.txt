Looking at this problem, I need to find a circle with nonzero radius tangent to
three circles, each passing through the orthocenter H and two vertices of triang
le ABC (sides 13, 14, 15).

## Setup

Place B = (0,0), C = (14,0). Solving for A: x² + y² = 169 and (x−14)² + y² = 225
 gives A = (5, 12). The area is 84 (Heron's formula with s = 21).

**Orthocenter:** The altitude from A is x = 5 (perpendicular to BC). The altitud
e from B has slope 3/4 (perpendicular to AC with slope −4/3), giving y = (3/4)x.
 At x = 5: y = 15/4. So **H = (5, 15/4)**.

## The Three Circles

**Circle 1** (through A, H, B): Since A and H share x = 5, the perpendicular bis
ector of AH is y = 63/8. Solving equidistance from B and A gives center **O₁ = (
−2, 63/8)**, radius **R = 65/8**.

**Circle 2** (through B, H, C): Since B and C share y = 0, the perpendicular bis
ector is x = 7. Solving gives center **O₂ = (7, −33/8)**, radius **R = 65/8**.

**Circle 3** (through C, H, A): Center **O₃ = (12, 63/8)**, radius **R = 65/8**.

All three circles have the **same radius R = 65/8** and pass through H. The cent
ers form a triangle with sides 13, 14, 15 (congruent to ABC), and each center is
 at distance R from H.

## Inversion Approach

Invert about H with power k² = 2R = 65/4. Each circle (passing through H, with c
enter at distance R from H and radius R) maps to a **line at distance k²/(2R) =
1 from H**, perpendicular to the direction from H to that circle's center.

Setting (u, v) = (x − 5, y − 15/4), the three lines are:
- **L₁:** −56u + 33v = 65 (normal direction O₁ − H, scaled)
- **L₂:** 16u − 63v = 65
- **L₃:** 56u + 33v = 65

Each line is at distance 65/|normal| = 65/65 = 1 from the origin H. Since H sati
sfies all three inequalities (0 < 65), **H is the incenter** of the triangle for
med by these lines, with **inradius 1**.

## The Incircle Solution

The incircle of the line-triangle is centered at H with radius 1. Under inversio
n (k² = 65/4), a circle centered at the inversion center with radius ρ maps to a
 circle centered at the same point with radius k²/ρ.

The incircle (radius 1) maps to a circle centered at H with radius **65/4**.

**Verification of tangency:** Each original circle has center at distance R = 65
/8 from H and radius R = 65/8. The new circle has center H and radius 65/4 = 2R.
 The distance between centers is R = 65/8, and |2R − R| = R = 65/8, confirming *
*internal tangency** with all three circles. ✓

## Uniqueness of the Clean Solution

The other tangent circles correspond to excircles of the line-triangle. Computin
g the exradii (r_a = 16/5, r_b = 224/55, r_c = 224/99) and applying the inversio
n formula ρ' = k²·r_ex/|d² − r_ex²| yields fractions with large irreducible deno
minators (e.g., 11700/1921, 40040/6161) — none clean. The incircle solution is t
he natural nonzero-radius answer.

## Result

The radius is 65/4, which is irreducible (gcd(65, 4) = 1).
