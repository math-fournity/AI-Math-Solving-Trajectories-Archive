**Step 1: Behavior of $1/\Gamma(x)$ near $x=0$.**

The Gamma function has a simple pole at $x=0$ with residue 1:
$$\Gamma(x) = \frac{1}{x} - \gamma + O(x), \quad x\to 0$$
where $\gamma$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant. Inverting:
$$\frac{1}{\Gamma(x)} = x + \gamma\, x^2 + O(x^3) = x\bigl(1+O(x)\bigr).$$

**Step 2: Behavior of $I(T) := \int_0^T \frac{|\sin t|}{t}\,dt$ as $T\to\infty$.
**

Since $|\sin t|$ is $\pi$-periodic with mean value $\frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi \sin
 t\,dt = \frac{2}{\pi}$, we compare the integrand to $\frac{2/\pi}{t}$. Decompos
ing into periods and estimating the error (which is bounded per period after sum
ming via the standard technique), one obtains the classical asymptotic:
$$I(T) = \frac{2}{\pi}\ln T + C_0 + o(1), \quad T\to+\infty$$
for some constant $C_0$. The key point is the **logarithmic** growth with coeffi
cient $2/\pi$.

**Step 3: Handle both one-sided limits.**

*Case $x\to 0^+$:* Here $T=1/x\to+\infty$, so
$$I(x) := \int_0^{1/x}\frac{|\sin t|}{t}\,dt = \frac{2}{\pi}\ln\frac{1}{x} + C_0
 + o(1).$$

*Case $x\to 0^-$:* Write $x=-y$, $y\to 0^+$, so $1/x = -1/y$. Substituting $u=-t
$:
$$\int_0^{-1/y}\frac{|\sin t|}{t}\,dt = \int_0^{1/y}\frac{|\sin u|}{u}\,du = I(y

since $|\sin(-u)|=|\sin u|$ and the two sign changes from $1/t$ and $dt$ cancel.
 Thus $I(x)=I(|x|)$ for both sides, and in all cases
$$I(x) = \frac{2}{\pi}\ln\frac{1}{|x|} + C_0 + o(1).$$

$$\frac{I(x)}{\Gamma(x)} = \frac{1}{\Gamma(x)}\cdot I(x) = x\bigl(1+O(x)\bigr)\l
