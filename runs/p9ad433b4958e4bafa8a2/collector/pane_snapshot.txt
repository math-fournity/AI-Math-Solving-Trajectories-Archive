## Solution

We determine for which real values of $\alpha$ the function $f(x)=|x|^\alpha$ be
longs to the Sobolev space $H^1(B(0,1))$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$, where $B(0,1)=\{x\in
\mathbb{R}^2:|x|<1\}$.

Recall $f\in H^1(B(0,1))$ iff $f\in L^2(B)$ and each weak partial derivative $\p
artial_i f\in L^2(B)$.

---

### 1. The $L^2$ condition on $f$

Using polar coordinates ($dx = r\,dr\,d\theta$, $r=|x|$):

$$
\int_B |f|^2\,dx = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^1 r^{2\alpha}\,r\,dr\,d\theta = 2\pi\
int_0^1 r^{2\alpha+1}\,dr.
$$

This integral converges at $r=0$ iff $2\alpha+1>-1$, i.e.

$$
\boxed{\alpha>-1.}
$$

(At $r=1$ there is no issue since $B$ is the open unit ball and $r^{2\alpha+1}$
is bounded there.)

---

### 2. The $L^2$ condition on $\nabla f$

Away from the origin, $f$ is smooth and

$$
\partial_i f = \alpha\,|x|^{\alpha-2}x_i,\qquad |\nabla f| = |\alpha|\,|x|^{\alp
ha-1}.
$$

For $\alpha\neq 0$, this is the weak derivative as well (the singularity at $0$
is harmless in the distributional sense provided $f\in L^1_{\text{loc}}$, which
holds for $\alpha>-2$). Then

$$
\int_B |\nabla f|^2\,dx = \alpha^2\!\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^1 r^{2(\alpha-1)}\,r
\,dr\,d\theta = 2\pi\alpha^2\int_0^1 r^{2\alpha-1}\,dr.
$$

This converges at $r=0$ iff $2\alpha-1>-1$, i.e.

$$
\boxed{\alpha>0.}
$$

---

### 3. The boundary case $\alpha=0$

When $\alpha=0$, $f(x)=|x|^0\equiv 1$ (a constant, in $L^2(B)$), and $\nabla f\e
quiv 0\in L^2(B)$. Hence $f\in H^1(B(0,1))$ trivially. The formula $\alpha^2\int
_0^1 r^{2\alpha-1}dr$ gives the indeterminate $0\cdot\infty$ here, but the actua
l gradient is identically zero, so $\alpha=0$ is admissible.

---

### 4. Negative $\alpha$

For $\alpha<0$, $f$ is singular at the origin. Even when $-1<\alpha<0$ (so $f\in
 L^2$), the gradient condition requires $\alpha>0$, which fails. Thus no negativ
e $\alpha$ works.

---

### 5. Combining the conditions

- $f\in L^2$ requires $\alpha>-1$.
- $\nabla f\in L^2$ requires $\alpha>0$ (for $\alpha\neq 0$), and $\alpha=0$ is
verified directly.

The intersection is $\alpha>0$, together with the admissible point $\alpha=0$.

---

### Conclusion

The function $f(x)=|x|^\alpha$ belongs to $H^1(B(0,1))$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ if and
 only if

$$
\boxed{\alpha\ge 0.}
$$
