The statement is **false**. Here is a counterexample.

## Counterexample

Let $A = \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$, the space of square-summable sequences with the st
andard norm $\|x\| = \bigl(\sum_{n=1}^\infty |x_n|^2\bigr)^{1/2}$.

Let $B = c_{00}(\mathbb{N})$, the subspace of finitely supported sequences (thos
e $x = (x_n)$ with only finitely many nonzero terms).

**Claim 1: $B \subsetneq A$ and $B$ is a linear subspace.**
$B$ is clearly a linear subspace. It is proper: for instance, $x = (1/n)_{n \geq
 1}$ belongs to $\ell^2$ (since $\sum 1/n^2 < \infty$) but has infinitely many n
onzero terms, so $x \in A \setminus B$.

**Claim 2: $B$ is dense in $A$.**
For any $x = (x_n) \in \ell^2$ and any $\delta > 0$, define the truncation $x^{(
N)} = (x_1, \dots, x_N, 0, 0, \dots) \in c_{00}$. Then
$$\|x - x^{(N)}\|^2 = \sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty} |x_n|^2 \to 0 \quad \text{as } N \to
 \infty,$$
since the tail of a convergent series tends to $0$. Hence $x^{(N)} \to x$ in $\e
ll^2$, so $\overline{B} = A$.

**Claim 3: $d(a, B) = 0$ for every $a \in A$.**
Since $B$ is dense in $A$, for every $a \in A$ and every $\delta > 0$ there exis
ts $b \in B$ with $\|a - b\| < \delta$. Therefore
$$d(a, B) = \inf_{b \in B} \|a - b\| = 0.$$

**Conclusion.**
Take $\epsilon = \tfrac{1}{2}$. The statement would require an $a \in A$ with $\
|a\| = 1$ and $d(a, B) > 1 - \tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{1}{2}$. But by Claim 3, $d(a,
 B) = 0$ for **every** $a \in A$, so no such $a$ exists.

## Remark

The classical Riesz lemma requires the subspace to be **closed**. The closure hy
pothesis is essential: once $B$ is allowed to be non-closed, it may be dense (as
 above), in which case $d(a, B) = 0$ for all $a$, making the desired inequality
impossible for any $\epsilon < 1$.
