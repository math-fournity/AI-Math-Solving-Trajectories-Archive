## Solution

**Claim:** Yes. A Jordan measurable set $X$ is always integrable over any rectan
gle $R$ that contains it.

**Justification.**

Recall the relevant definitions and the key criterion (Lebesgue's theorem for Ri
emann integrability):

> A bounded function $f$ on a rectangle $R$ is Riemann integrable over $R$ if an
d only if the set of points at which $f$ is discontinuous has (Lebesgue) measure
 zero.

A bounded set $X \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is **Jordan measurable** precisely when
 its boundary $\partial X$ has Jordan content zero (equivalently, Lebesgue measu
re zero, since Jordan content zero implies Lebesgue measure zero).

Consider the characteristic function
$$
\chi_X(x) = \begin{cases} 1 & x \in X \\ 0 & x \notin X. \end{cases}
$$

We analyze the discontinuities of $\chi_X$ on an arbitrary rectangle $R$ with $X
 \subseteq R$:

- **On $\operatorname{int}(X)$:** $\chi_X \equiv 1$ in a neighborhood of every p
oint, so $\chi_X$ is continuous.
- **On $R \setminus \overline{X}$ (i.e., the exterior of $X$ within $R$):** $\ch
i_X \equiv 0$ in a neighborhood of every point, so $\chi_X$ is continuous.
- **On $\partial X$:** Every neighborhood of a boundary point contains both poin
ts in $X$ (where $\chi_X = 1$) and points not in $X$ (where $\chi_X = 0$), so $\
chi_X$ is discontinuous at every point of $\partial X$.

Therefore the set of discontinuities of $\chi_X$ on $R$ is exactly $\partial X$.

Since $X$ is Jordan measurable, $\partial X$ has Jordan content zero, hence Lebe
sgue measure zero. By Lebesgue's criterion, $\chi_X$ is Riemann integrable over
$R$.

Crucially, this argument is **independent of the choice of rectangle $R$** conta
ining $X$: enlarging $R$ only adds regions where $\chi_X = 0$ (continuous), so t
he discontinuity set remains $\partial X$ regardless of which containing rectang
le is chosen. Hence $X$ is integrable over **every** rectangle $R \supseteq X$.

$\blacksquare$
