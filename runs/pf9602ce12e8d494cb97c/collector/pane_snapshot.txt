**Theorem.** Let $X$ be a Hausdorff space determined by an upward filtered famil
y $\{K_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in A}$ of compact subsets. Let $Y$ be a metric space. T
hen the topology of uniform convergence on $\{K_\alpha\}$ on $C(X, Y)$ is weaker
 than (i.e., coarser than) the topology of uniform convergence on all compact su
bsets of $X$.

**Proof.**

Denote by $\mathcal{K}$ the family of all compact subsets of $X$. The topology o
f uniform convergence on a family $\mathcal{S}$ of subsets of $X$ is generated b
y basic neighborhoods of the form
$$N(f, S, \varepsilon) = \left\{g \in C(X,Y) : \sup_{x \in S} d(f(x), g(x)) < \v
arepsilon\right\}, \qquad f \in C(X,Y),\ S \in \mathcal{S},\ \varepsilon > 0.$$

Since each $K_\alpha$ is a compact subset of $X$ by hypothesis, we have $\{K_\al
pha\}_{\alpha \in A} \subseteq \mathcal{K}$. Therefore, every basic neighborhood
 $N(f, K_\alpha, \varepsilon)$ in the topology of uniform convergence on $\{K_\a
lpha\}$ is also a basic neighborhood in the topology of uniform convergence on $
\mathcal{K}$. Consequently, every open set in the $\{K_\alpha\}$-topology is ope
n in the $\mathcal{K}$-topology, i.e.,
$$\tau_{\{K_\alpha\}} \subseteq \tau_{\mathcal{K}}. \qquad \blacksquare$$
