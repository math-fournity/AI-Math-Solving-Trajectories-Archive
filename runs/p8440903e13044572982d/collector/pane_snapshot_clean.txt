## Analytic continuation of \(g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n}{2n+1}z^{2n+1
}\)

**Step 1. Identify the function inside the disk of convergence.**

The series has radius of convergence \(R=1\) (the nearest singularities of the s
um are at \(z=\pm i\), since the coefficient ratio gives \(\lim |a_{2n+3}/a_{2n+
1}|=1\)). For \(|z|<1\),

\[
g(z)=z-\frac{z^3}{3}+\frac{z^5}{5}-\cdots=\arctan(z),
\]

because the Taylor expansion of \(\arctan z\) about \(0\) is exactly \(\sum_{n\g
e 0}\frac{(-1)^n}{2n+1}z^{2n+1}\).

**Step 2. Closed form via logarithms.**

Recall that for \(|w|<1\), \(\arctan w=\frac{1}{2i}\log\frac{1+iw}{1-w}\) is *no
t* the right identity; the correct one is obtained from

\[
\arctan z=\frac{1}{2i}\bigl(\Log(1+iz)-\Log(1-iz)\bigr)=\frac{1}{2i}\Log\!\left(
\frac{1+iz}{1-iz}\right),
\]

where \(\Log\) denotes the principal branch of the logarithm (cut along \((-\inf
ty,0]\)). Indeed, differentiating the right–hand side gives

\[
\frac{d}{dz}\frac{1}{2i}\Log\!\left(\frac{1+iz}{1-iz}\right)
=\frac{1}{2i}\left(\frac{i}{1+iz}+\frac{i}{1-iz}\right)
=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{1+z^2}=\frac{1}{1+z^2},
\]

which equals \((\arctan z)'\). Since both sides vanish at \(z=0\), they coincide
 on the connected component containing \(0\), in particular on \(|z|<1\). Hence
this logarithmic expression is an analytic continuation of \(g\).

**Step 3. Determine the maximal slit domain.**

The expression \(\frac{1}{2i}\Log\!\left(\frac{1+iz}{1-iz}\right)\) is single-va
lued analytic wherever the argument \(\frac{1+iz}{1-iz}\) avoids the cut \((-\in
fty,0]\) of \(\Log\) *and* avoids \(0\) (which it never does, since numerator an
d denominator do not vanish simultaneously). Using the difference form

\[
G(z)=\frac{1}{2i}\bigl(\Log(1+iz)-\Log(1-iz)\bigr),
\]

\(G\) is analytic wherever both \(1+iz\) and \(1-iz\) avoid \((-\infty,0]\).

- \(1+iz\in(-\infty,0]\iff iz\in(-\infty,-1]\iff z\in\{iy:y\ge 1\}\) (the ray fr
om \(i\) to \(+i\infty\)).
- \(1-iz\in(-\infty,0]\iff -iz\in(-\infty,-1]\iff z\in\{iy:y\le -1\}\) (the ray
from \(-i\) to \(-i\infty\)).

Therefore \(G\) is analytic on

\[
\mathbb C\setminus\{z:\operatorname{Re}(z)=0,\ |\operatorname{Im}(z)|\ge 1\},
\]

i.e. the plane slit along the two imaginary-axis rays issuing from the branch po
ints \(\pm i\). This is the natural maximal slit domain for \(\arctan\), since \
(\pm i\) are precisely its branch points (where \(1\pm iz=0\)).

**Step 4. Consistency check on the stated domain.**

The branch points of \(\arctan\) are fixed at \(z=\pm i\); no choice of branch c
an relocate them. The points \(\pm i\) satisfy \(\operatorname{Im}(z)=\pm1\neq0\
), so they lie *inside* the set \(\mathbb C\setminus\{z:\operatorname{Im}(z)=0,\
 |\operatorname{Re}(z)|\ge1\}\). Consequently no single-valued analytic continua
tion of \(g\) can exist on that set as written: any such continuation would have
 to be analytic at the branch points \(\pm i\), which is impossible. The slit mu
st connect the branch points \(\pm i\) to infinity, which forces the cut to lie
on the **imaginary** axis, not the real axis. Thus the domain in the problem sta
tement should read \(\mathbb C\setminus\{z:\operatorname{Re}(z)=0,\ |\operatorna
me{Im}(z)|\ge1\}\) (the standard slit plane for \(\arctan\)); with that correcti
on the answer below is the required analytic extension.

**Conclusion.**

The analytic continuation of \(g\) is

\[
\boxed{\,g(z)=\arctan z=\frac{1}{2i}\,\Log\!\left(\frac{1+iz}{1-iz}\right)\,}
\]

with \(\Log\) the principal logarithm, single-valued and analytic on

\[
\mathbb C\setminus\{z:\operatorname{Re}(z)=0,\ |\operatorname{Im}(z)|\ge 1\}.
\]

On \(|z|<1\) it expands as \(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n}{2n+1}z^{2n+1}\), c
onfirming it extends \(g\).
