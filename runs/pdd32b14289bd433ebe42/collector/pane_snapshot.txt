**Setting up the bound via Cauchy–Schwarz (Engel/Titu form).**

The expression is homogeneous of degree 1, so we may analyze the ratio $\text{LH
S}/(a+b+c)$ directly.

By Titu's lemma (Cauchy–Schwarz in Engel form),

$$
\sum_{\text{cyc}} \frac{a^2}{\frac{b}{29}+\frac{c}{31}}
\;\ge\;
\frac{(a+b+c)^2}{\displaystyle\sum_{\text{cyc}}\!\left(\frac{b}{29}+\frac{c}{31}
\right)}.
$$

**Computing the denominator.** Cyclically summing,

$$
\sum_{\text{cyc}}\!\left(\frac{b}{29}+\frac{c}{31}\right)
= \frac{a+b+c}{29} + \frac{a+b+c}{31}
= (a+b+c)\!\left(\frac{1}{29}+\frac{1}{31}\right)
= (a+b+c)\cdot\frac{60}{899}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\text{LHS} \;\ge\; \frac{(a+b+c)^2}{(a+b+c)\cdot\frac{60}{899}} \;=\; (a+b+c)\cd
ot\frac{899}{60}.
$$

**Equality condition.** Equality in Titu's lemma holds iff

$$
\frac{a}{\frac{b}{29}+\frac{c}{31}} \;=\; \frac{b}{\frac{c}{29}+\frac{a}{31}} \;
=\; \frac{c}{\frac{a}{29}+\frac{b}{31}},
$$

which is satisfied when $a = b = c$. Hence the bound is **sharp**: the minimum o
f $\text{LHS}/(a+b+c)$ over all positive reals equals exactly $\dfrac{899}{60}$.

**Verification at $a=b=c=1$.** Each denominator is $\frac{1}{29}+\frac{1}{31}=\f
rac{60}{899}$, so each term is $\frac{899}{60}$, and the sum is $3\cdot\frac{899
}{60}=\frac{899}{60}\cdot(a+b+c)$. Confirmed.

**Determining $n$.** We have

$$
\frac{899}{60} = 14.98\overline{3}.
$$

- For $n = 14$: since $14 < \frac{899}{60}$, the inequality $\text{LHS} \ge 14(a

- For $n = 15$: since $15 > \frac{899}{60}$, taking $a=b=c$ gives $\text{LHS} =
\frac{899}{60}(a+b+c) < 15(a+b+c)$. ✗

Therefore the largest positive integer $n$ is

$$
\boxed{n = 14}.
$$
