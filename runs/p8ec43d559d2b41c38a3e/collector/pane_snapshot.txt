**Answer: Yes, the sequence converges strongly in $L^2$.**

## Proof

Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ be a bounded open Lipschitz domain, and let $\
{u_n\} \subset H^1(\Omega)$ satisfy:
1. $u_n \to u$ pointwise a.e. in $\Omega$,
2. $\{u_n\}$ is monotone (WLOG $u_n \leq u_{n+1}$ a.e.),
3. $\{u_n\}$ is bounded in $H^1(\Omega)$,
4. $u_n \rightharpoonup u$ weakly in $H^1(\Omega)$.

**Claim:** $u_n \to u$ strongly in $L^2(\Omega)$.

### Justification 1: Rellich–Kondrachov Compact Embedding

Since $\Omega$ is a bounded Lipschitz domain, the **Rellich–Kondrachov theorem**
 states that the embedding
$$H^1(\Omega) \hookrightarrow L^2(\Omega)$$
is **compact**.

A compact linear operator between Banach spaces maps weakly convergent sequences
 to strongly convergent sequences. Indeed, let $T: H^1(\Omega) \to L^2(\Omega)$
denote the embedding. If $u_n \rightharpoonup u$ weakly in $H^1$ but $Tu_n \not\
to Tu$ strongly in $L^2$, then there exists $\varepsilon > 0$ and a subsequence
$\{u_{n_k}\}$ with $\|Tu_{n_k} - Tu\|_{L^2} \geq \varepsilon$. But $\{u_{n_k}\}$
 is bounded in $H^1$, so by compactness of $T$, a further subsequence converges
strongly in $L^2$; its limit must be $Tu$ (since weak $H^1$ convergence implies
weak $L^2$ convergence to the same limit, and strong limits are unique). This co
ntradicts $\|Tu_{n_k} - Tu\|_{L^2} \geq \varepsilon$.

Therefore, $u_n \rightharpoonup u$ weakly in $H^1(\Omega)$ implies $u_n \to u$ s
trongly in $L^2(\Omega)$. $\square$

### Justification 2: Monotone Convergence (independent of weak $H^1$ convergence
)

This approach uses only conditions (1)–(3) and does not require the weak $H^1$ c
onvergence.

Set $v_n := u_n - u_1$. Since $\{u_n\}$ is monotonically increasing a.e., we hav
e $v_n \geq 0$ a.e. and $v_n \uparrow v := u - u_1$ a.e. Consequently, $v_n^2 \u
parrow v^2$ a.e. (as $v_n \geq 0$ and increasing). By the **Monotone Convergence
 Theorem**:
$$\int_\Omega v_n^2 \,dx \longrightarrow \int_\Omega v^2 \,dx.$$

Since $\{u_n\}$ is bounded in $H^1$, it is bounded in $L^2$, so $\sup_n \|v_n\|_
{L^2} < \infty$. The monotone convergence theorem then gives $v \in L^2(\Omega)$
, hence $u = v + u_1 \in L^2(\Omega)$.

Now compute:
$$\|u_n - u\|_{L^2}^2 = \int_\Omega (v_n - v)^2\,dx = \int_\Omega v_n^2\,dx - 2\
int_\Omega v_n v\,dx + \int_\Omega v^2\,dx.$$

Since $v_n \geq 0$, $v \geq 0$, and $v_n \uparrow v$, we have $v_n v \uparrow v^
2$ a.e., so by monotone convergence $\int v_n v \,dx \to \int v^2\,dx$. Combinin
g:
$$\|u_n - u\|_{L^2}^2 \longrightarrow \int v^2\,dx - 2\int v^2\,dx + \int v^2\,d
x = 0.$$

Hence $u_n \to u$ strongly in $L^2(\Omega)$. $\square$

### Consistency of the limits

The pointwise a.e. limit and the weak $H^1$ limit coincide: Justification 2 show
s $u_n \to u$ strongly in $L^2$ (using only the pointwise a.e. + monotone + $H^1
$-bounded conditions), hence $u_n \rightharpoonup u$ weakly in $L^2$. Since the
continuous embedding $H^1 \hookrightarrow L^2$ preserves weak convergence, condi
tion (4) gives $u_n \rightharpoonup u$ weakly in $L^2$ as well. By uniqueness of
 weak limits, the two limits agree.

### Conclusion

The sequence **converges strongly in $L^2(\Omega)$**. The result follows either
from the compactness of the Rellich–Kondrachov embedding $H^1(\Omega) \hookright
arrow L^2(\Omega)$ (using weak $H^1$ convergence) or from the Monotone Convergen
ce Theorem (using pointwise a.e. convergence, monotonicity, and $L^2$-boundednes
s). The given hypotheses are in fact redundant: either the weak $H^1$ convergenc
e alone, or the monotone pointwise a.e. convergence plus $H^1$-boundedness alone
, suffices.
