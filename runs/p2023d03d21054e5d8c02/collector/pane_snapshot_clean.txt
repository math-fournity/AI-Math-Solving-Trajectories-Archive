# Derivation of the Schwarzschild Metric in Eddington–Finkelstein Coordinates

## 1. Starting point: Schwarzschild metric

In geometrized units ($G = c = 1$) and Schwarzschild coordinates $(t, r, \theta,
 \phi)$, the line element is

$$
ds^2 = -\left(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\right) dt^2 + \left(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1}
dr^2 + r^2\,d\Omega^2,
\qquad d\Omega^2 = d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2.
$$

Define $f(r) = 1 - 2M/r$. The metric component $g_{rr} = f^{-1}$ diverges and $g
_{tt} = -f$ vanishes at $r = 2M$ (the Schwarzschild radius). This is a **coordin
ate singularity**, not a curvature singularity — one can verify that the Kretsch
mann scalar $R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} = 48M^2/r^6$ is perfectly
finite at $r = 2M$. The goal of Eddington–Finkelstein (EF) coordinates is to rem
ove this coordinate pathology.

## 2. Radial null geodesics and the tortoise coordinate

For radial light rays ($d\Omega = 0$), $ds^2 = 0$ gives

$$
-f\,dt^2 + f^{-1}dr^2 = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{dt}{dr} = \pm \frac{1}{f} =
\pm\frac{1}{1 - 2M/r}.
$$

Integrating defines the **tortoise coordinate**

$$
r_* = r + 2M\ln\left|\frac{r}{2M} - 1\right|,
\qquad \frac{dr_*}{dr} = \frac{1}{f(r)} = \left(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1}.
$$

Note $r_* \to -\infty$ as $r \to 2M$, so $r_*$ "stretches out" the horizon. In t
erms of $r_*$, the radial part of the metric becomes

$$
-f\,dt^2 + f^{-1}dr^2 = -f\,(dt^2 - dr_*^2) = -f\,(dt - dr_*)(dt + dr_*).
$$

This factorization suggests introducing null coordinates.

## 3. Ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates (advanced time)

Define the **advanced (ingoing) null coordinate**

$$
v = t + r_*, \qquad\text{so}\qquad t = v - r_*, \quad dt = dv - dr_* = dv - \fra
c{dr}{f}.
$$

Now substitute into the $(t,r)$ sector:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-f\,dt^2 + f^{-1}dr^2
&= -f\left(dv - \frac{dr}{f}\right)^2 + \frac{dr^2}{f} \\
&= -f\left(dv^2 - \frac{2\,dv\,dr}{f} + \frac{dr^2}{f^2}\right) + \frac{dr^2}{f}
 \\
&= -f\,dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr - \frac{dr^2}{f} + \frac{dr^2}{f} \\
&= -f\,dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr.
\end{aligned}
$$

The $dr^2$ terms cancel exactly. Therefore, in coordinates $(v, r, \theta, \phi)
$,

$$
\boxed{\;ds^2 = -\left(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\right)dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr + r^2\,d\Omega^2.
\;}
$$

**Regularity at the horizon.** The metric components $g_{vv} = -f$, $g_{vr} = g_
{rv} = 1$, $g_{\theta\theta} = r^2$, $g_{\phi\phi} = r^2\sin^2\theta$ are all sm
ooth and finite at $r = 2M$. The determinant $\det(g_{\mu\nu}) = -r^4\sin^2\thet
a$ (the cross term $2\,dv\,dr$ makes the $(v,r)$ block non-degenerate with deter
minant $-1$) is non-zero at $r = 2M$. Hence the horizon is a perfectly regular s
urface in these coordinates.

**Causal structure.** Radial null curves ($d\Omega = 0$, $ds^2 = 0$) satisfy

$$
-f\,dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; dv = 0 \quad\text{or}\quad \frac{dr
}{dv} = \frac{f}{2} = \frac{1}{2}\left(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\right).
$$

- $dv = 0$ ($v = \text{const}$) are **ingoing** null rays; they cross $r = 2M$ s
moothly and reach $r = 0$.
- $dr/dv = \tfrac{1}{2}(1 - 2M/r)$: for $r > 2M$ this is positive (outgoing), fo
r $r < 2M$ it is **negative** — even the "outgoing" family is dragged inward. Th
is reveals $r = 2M$ as a **one-way membrane** (future event horizon), with $r =
0$ as the true curvature singularity.

## 4. Outgoing Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates (retarded time)

Symmetrically, define the **retarded (outgoing) null coordinate**

$$
u = t - r_*, \qquad t = u + r_*, \quad dt = du + dr_* = du + \frac{dr}{f}.
$$

Substituting:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-f\,dt^2 + f^{-1}dr^2
&= -f\left(du + \frac{dr}{f}\right)^2 + \frac{dr^2}{f} \\
&= -f\,du^2 - 2\,du\,dr - \frac{dr^2}{f} + \frac{dr^2}{f} \\
&= -f\,du^2 - 2\,du\,dr.
\end{aligned}
$$

So in coordinates $(u, r, \theta, \phi)$,

$$
\boxed{\;ds^2 = -\left(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\right)du^2 - 2\,du\,dr + r^2\,d\Omega^2.
\;}
$$

This is also regular at $r = 2M$. Its radial null curves are $du = 0$ (outgoing,
 $u = \text{const}$) and $dr/du = -\tfrac{1}{2}(1 - 2M/r)$. The ingoing family $
du = 0$ escapes from $r < 2M$ to $r > 2M$; this version describes the **white ho
le** region (the time-reverse of the black hole), since it is related to the ing
oing form by $v \leftrightarrow u$, i.e. $t \to -t$.

## 5. Summary

Starting from the Schwarzschild metric, one introduces the tortoise coordinate $
r_*$ which absorbs the conformal factor $f^{-1}$, factorizes the radial sector a
s $-f(dt^2 - dr_*^2)$, and passes to the null coordinates $v = t + r_*$ (advance
d) or $u = t - r_*$ (retarded). The cross term $2\,dv\,dr$ (resp. $-2\,du\,dr$)
that appears is precisely what keeps the $(v,r)$ (resp. $(u,r)$) block non-degen
erate at $r = 2M$, eliminating the coordinate singularity while leaving the genu
ine curvature singularity at $r = 0$ intact. The two forms are:

$$
\boxed{\,ds^2 = -\left(1-\tfrac{2M}{r}\right)dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr + r^2 d\Omega^2\,}
\qquad\text{(ingoing EF, black hole)},
$$

$$
\boxed{\,ds^2 = -\left(1-\tfrac{2M}{r}\right)du^2 - 2\,du\,dr + r^2 d\Omega^2\,}
\qquad\text{(outgoing EF, white hole)}.
$$
