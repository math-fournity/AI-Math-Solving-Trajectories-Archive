## Proof

**Notation.** For a color $c$, let $e_c$ be the number of edges of color $c$, an
d let $p_c$ be the number of unordered vertex pairs that lie in the same monochr
omatic connected component of color $c$. The condition "every pair of vertices i
s connected by a monochromatic path" means every pair is counted in at least one
 $p_c$, hence
$$\sum_c p_c \;\ge\; \binom{100}{2}=4950. \tag{1}$$

---

### Upper bound: $n\le 1936$

A color using $e$ edges spans a subgraph whose components have sizes $s_1,\dots,
s_k$ with $\sum s_j=100$ and $\sum(s_j-1)\le e$. Since $\binom{x}{2}$ is convex,
 merging two components (spending one extra edge to connect them) strictly incre
ases $\sum\binom{s_j}{2}$. Thus $p_c=\sum\binom{s_j}{2}$ is maximized by a singl
e tree on $e_c+1$ vertices, giving
$$p_c\le \binom{e_c+1}{2}=\frac{e_c^2+e_c}{2}.$$
Summing and using $\sum_c e_c=2013$ and (1):
$$\frac{\sum_c e_c^2+2013}{2}\ge 4950 \;\Longrightarrow\; \sum_c e_c^2\ge 7887.
\tag{2}$$

With $n$ colors, $e_c\ge 1$ and $\sum e_c=2013$. For fixed $n$, the maximum of $
\sum e_c^2$ is attained by concentrating: $n-1$ colors with $1$ edge and one col
or with $2014-n$ edges, yielding
$$\sum_c e_c^2\le (n-1)+(2014-n)^2.$$
For (2) to be satisfiable we need $(n-1)+(2014-n)^2\ge 7887$. Setting $t=2014-n$
 this reads $t^2-t\ge 5874$. Since $76\cdot77=5852<5874\le 6006=77\cdot78$, we n
eed $t\ge 78$, i.e.
$$n\le 2014-78=1936.$$

---

### Construction achieving $n=1936$

Split the $100$ vertices into $U$ ($79$ vertices) and $W$ ($21$ vertices). Build
 $G$ with these edges:

1. **$W$ is a clique:** $\binom{21}{2}=210$ edges.
2. **Complete bipartite join $W$–$U$:** $21\cdot79=1659$ edges.
3. **Inside $U$:** a path $v_1v_2\cdots v_{79}$ ($78$ edges) plus $66$ chords $v
_iv_{i+2}$ for $i=1,\dots,66$.

Total: $210+1659+78+66=2013$ edges. The graph is connected (the path spans $U$,
and every $w\in W$ touches all of $U$).

**Coloring.** Give the $78$ path edges color $1$; give each of the remaining $19
35$ edges its own distinct color. So $n=1+1935=1936$.

**Every pair is monochromatically connected:**
- Two vertices of $U$: color $1$ (the path) connects them. ✓
- Two vertices of $W$: their joining edge is a distinct color. ✓
- One in $W$, one in $U$: their joining edge is a distinct color. ✓

**Distance condition.** Take $A=v_1$, $B=v_{79}$. Every edge inside $U$ connects
 vertices whose indices differ by $1$ (path) or $2$ (chord), so any walk from $v
_1$ to $v_{79}$ advances by at most $2$ per step; hence its length is at least $
\lceil 78/2\rceil=39\ge 3$. Thus $A$ and $B$ cannot be joined by one or two edge
s. ✓

So a valid graph and coloring with $n=1936$ colors exists.

---

### Conclusion

The upper bound $n\le 1936$ and the matching construction give

$$\boxed{n=1936}.$$
