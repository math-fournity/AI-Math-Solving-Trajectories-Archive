## Solution

**Problem.** Count the distinct normal subgroups $H \trianglelefteq F_2$ with $F
_2/H \cong V_4$.

---

**Step 1: Reduce to counting surjective homomorphisms modulo automorphisms.**

Let $F_2 = \langle a, b \rangle$. By the correspondence theorem, every normal su
bgroup $H$ with $F_2/H \cong V_4$ arises as the kernel of some surjective homomo
rphism $\varphi: F_2 \to V_4$. Two surjective homomorphisms $\varphi, \psi$ have
 the same kernel if and only if $\psi = \alpha \circ \varphi$ for some $\alpha \
in \operatorname{Aut}(V_4)$.

*Proof of the equivalence:* If $\ker\varphi = \ker\psi = H$, both factor through
 $F_2/H$ via isomorphisms $\bar\varphi, \bar\psi: F_2/H \to V_4$, so $\psi = \ba
r\psi \circ \bar\varphi^{-1} \circ \varphi$, and $\bar\psi \circ \bar\varphi^{-1
} \in \operatorname{Aut}(V_4)$. The converse is immediate since automorphisms pr
eserve kernels.

Therefore:
$$\#\{H \trianglelefteq F_2 : F_2/H \cong V_4\} = \frac{\#\{\text{surjective hom
omorphisms } F_2 \to V_4\}}{|\operatorname{Aut}(V_4)|}.$$

---

**Step 2: Count surjective homomorphisms $F_2 \to V_4$.**

Since $F_2$ is free on $\{a,b\}$, a homomorphism $\varphi: F_2 \to V_4$ is deter
mined by $(\varphi(a), \varphi(b)) \in V_4^2$, giving $|V_4|^2 = 16$ total homom
orphisms.

Write $V_4 = \{e, x, y, z\}$ with $x^2=y^2=z^2=e$ and $xy=z$. The image $\langle
 \varphi(a), \varphi(b)\rangle = V_4$ iff $\varphi(a), \varphi(b)$ are **two dis
tinct non-identity elements** (any two distinct elements of $\{x,y,z\}$ generate
 $V_4$, since their product gives the third).

Counting surjective pairs: $3$ choices for $\varphi(a) \in \{x,y,z\}$, then $2$
choices for $\varphi(b) \in \{x,y,z\}\setminus\{\varphi(a)\}$, giving $3 \times
2 = 6$ surjective homomorphisms.

*(Cross-check via inclusion–exclusion over the 4 proper subgroups $\{e\}, \{e,x\
}, \{e,y\}, \{e,z\}$: non-surjective homomorphisms $= (1+4+4+4) - 6\cdot1 + 4\cd
ot1 - 1 = 10$, so surjective $= 16 - 10 = 6$. ✓)*

---

**Step 3: Compute $|\operatorname{Aut}(V_4)|$.**

Every automorphism of $V_4$ permutes the three non-identity elements $\{x, y, z\
}$ (the only elements of order 2), and any permutation of $\{x,y,z\}$ extends to
 an automorphism (the relation $xy=z$ is preserved under any permutation, since
the product of any two distinct non-identity elements is the third). Hence $\ope
ratorname{Aut}(V_4) \cong S_3$, with $|\operatorname{Aut}(V_4)| = 6$.

---

**Step 4: Conclude.**

$$\#\{H \trianglelefteq F_2 : F_2/H \cong V_4\} = \frac{6}{6} = 1.$$

The unique such subgroup is the normal closure of $\{a^2,\, b^2,\, [a,b]\}$ in $
F_2$, i.e., the kernel of the map $a \mapsto x,\; b \mapsto y$. Every other surj
ective homomorphism $F_2 \to V_4$ differs from this one by an automorphism of $V
_4$, hence has the same kernel.

$$\boxed{1}$$
