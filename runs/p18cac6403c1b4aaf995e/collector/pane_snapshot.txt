**Answer: Yes.** The first coefficient $a_0$ is invertible in $R$, and this does
 not require $R$ to be perfect.

**Proof.**

Let $R$ be a commutative ring of characteristic $p$, and let $W(R)$ denote the r
ing of $p$-typical Witt vectors. Every element $x \in W(R)$ is written as a sequ
ence $x = (x_0, x_1, x_2, \ldots)$ with $x_i \in R$, and its Teichmüller expansi
on is
$$x = \sum_{n \ge 0} V^n([x_n]),$$
so the "first coefficient" in the Teichmüller expansion is $x_0$ (equivalently,
$[x_0]$ is the degree-$0$ Teichmüller term).

**Key fact.** The projection onto the $0$-th component
$$\pi_0 : W(R) \longrightarrow R, \qquad (x_0, x_1, x_2, \ldots) \longmapsto x_0
$$
is a ring homomorphism.

*Justification.* Recall the ghost map
$$w : W(R) \longrightarrow R^{\mathbb{N}}, \qquad (x_0, x_1, \ldots) \longmapsto
 (w_0, w_1, \ldots), \quad w_n = \sum_{i=0}^{n} p^i\, x_i^{p^{n-i}}.$$
The ghost map $w$ is a ring homomorphism (this is the defining property of the W
itt ring structure: addition and multiplication on $W(R)$ are the unique operati
ons making $w$ a ring homomorphism into the product ring $R^{\mathbb{N}}$). The
$0$-th ghost component is $w_0 = x_0$. Since the projection $\mathrm{pr}_0 : R^{
\mathbb{N}} \to R$ onto the first factor is a ring homomorphism, the composition
$$\pi_0 = \mathrm{pr}_0 \circ w : W(R) \to R$$
is a ring homomorphism, and $\pi_0(x) = x_0$. $\square$

**Conclusion.** Ring homomorphisms send units to units: if $x \in W(R)$ is inver
tible with inverse $y \in W(R)$, then
$$\pi_0(x)\,\pi_0(y) = \pi_0(xy) = \pi_0(1) = 1,$$
so $x_0 = \pi_0(x)$ is invertible in $R$ with inverse $\pi_0(y)$.

This argument uses only:
1. that $W(R)$ carries a ring structure (true for any commutative ring $R$ of ch
aracteristic $p$),
2. that the ghost map is a ring homomorphism (true for any such $R$),
3. that ring homomorphisms preserve units (categorical fact).

**Perfection of $R$ is never invoked.** Perfection is relevant for other propert
ies of $W(R)$ — e.g., that the Frobenius on $W(R)$ is an automorphism, or that t
he Teichmüller expansion converges uniquely / that $W(R)$ is a strict $p$-ring —
 but it is **not** needed for the projection $\pi_0$ to be a ring homomorphism.
That fact holds for all commutative rings of characteristic $p$.

Therefore, even when $R$ is not perfect, invertibility of $x \in W(R)$ implies i
nvertibility of the first Teichmüller coefficient $a_0 = x_0$ in $R$. $\blacksqu
