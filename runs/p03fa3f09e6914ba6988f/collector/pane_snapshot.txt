## Proof

Let $A \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ be a (round, unknotted) circle. We compute $\pi_1(\
mathbb{R}^3 \setminus A)$ using van Kampen's theorem.

### Step 1: Compactification

Identify $\mathbb{R}^3 = S^3 \setminus \{\infty\}$ via stereographic projection,
 with $\infty \notin A$. Then
$$\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus A = S^3 \setminus (A \cup \{\infty\}).$$
We first compute $\pi_1(S^3 \setminus A)$ by van Kampen, then show that removing
 the additional point $\infty$ does not change the fundamental group (again by v
an Kampen).

### Step 2: $\pi_1(S^3 \setminus A)$ via the two-disk decomposition

Since $A$ is unknotted, it bounds two embedded $2$-disks $D_1, D_2$ in $S^3$ wit
h
$$D_1 \cup D_2 = S^2 \subset S^3, \qquad D_1 \cap D_2 = \partial D_1 = \partial
D_2 = A.$$
(Geometrically, $A$ is the equator of a $2$-sphere $S^2 \subset S^3$, and $D_1,
D_2$ are the upper and lower hemispheres.)

Define open sets in $S^3 \setminus A$:
$$U = S^3 \setminus D_1, \qquad V = S^3 \setminus D_2.$$

- Each $D_i$ is a properly embedded closed disk in $S^3$, so $S^3 \setminus D_i$
 is an open $3$-ball. Hence $U$ and $V$ are each homeomorphic to an open $3$-bal
l, so **$\pi_1(U) = \pi_1(V) = 0$**.
- Since $A = \partial D_i \subset D_i$, we have $U \cap A = V \cap A = \emptyset
$, so $U, V \subset S^3 \setminus A$. ✓
- **Cover:** $U \cup V = S^3 \setminus (D_1 \cap D_2) = S^3 \setminus A$. ✓
- **Intersection:** $U \cap V = S^3 \setminus (D_1 \cup D_2) = S^3 \setminus S^2
$. The $2$-sphere $S^2$ separates $S^3$ into two open $3$-balls, so
$$U \cap V = B_+ \sqcup B_-$$
is the disjoint union of **two** open $3$-balls. In particular, $U \cap V$ has *
*two path-components**, each simply connected.

**Applying van Kampen (disconnected intersection).** When $U \cap V$ has $n$ pat
h-components $C_1, \dots, C_n$ and the inclusions $\pi_1(C_j) \to \pi_1(U)$, $\p
i_1(C_j) \to \pi_1(V)$ are all trivial, van Kampen's theorem gives
$$\pi_1(U \cup V) \cong \pi_1(U) * \pi_1(V) * F_{n-1},$$
where $F_{n-1}$ is the free group on $n - 1$ generators (one for each component
beyond the first; each generator is represented by a loop traveling from $C_1$ t
o $C_j$ through $U$ and returning through $V$).

Here $\pi_1(U) = \pi_1(V) = 0$, each $C_j$ is simply connected (so all inclusion
 maps are trivial), and $n = 2$. Therefore
$$\pi_1(S^3 \setminus A) \cong 0 * 0 * F_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}.$$

The generator is represented by a loop that passes from $B_+$ to $B_-$ through $
U = S^3 \setminus D_1$ and returns from $B_-$ to $B_+$ through $V = S^3 \setminu
s D_2$ — geometrically, a loop that threads through $A$ once (linking number $1$
).

### Step 3: $\pi_1(\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus A) = \pi_1(S^3 \setminus A)$ via a bal
l decomposition

It remains to show that removing the point $\infty$ from $S^3 \setminus A$ does
not change $\pi_1$. We again use van Kampen.

Choose $R > 0$ large enough that $A \subset B_R$ (the open ball of radius $R$) a
nd $\infty \notin \overline{B}_R$. Let $\varepsilon > 0$. Define open sets in $\
mathbb{R}^3 \setminus A$:
$$U' = B_{R+\varepsilon} \setminus A, \qquad V' = \mathbb{R}^3 \setminus \overli
ne{B}_R.$$

- **$V'$** is the exterior of the closed ball $\overline{B}_R$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$
; it deformation retracts onto the sphere $\partial \overline{B}_R \cong S^2$. S
o **$\pi_1(V') = 0$**.
- **$U' \cap V'$** $= (B_{R+\varepsilon} \setminus A) \cap (\mathbb{R}^3 \setmin
us \overline{B}_R) = \{R < |x| < R+\varepsilon\} \setminus A$. Since $A \subset
B_R$, this is just the spherical shell $\{R < |x| < R+\varepsilon\}$, which defo
rmation retracts onto $S^2$. So **$U' \cap V'$ is path-connected and simply conn
ected** ($\pi_1 = 0$).
- **Cover:** $U' \cup V' = (B_{R+\varepsilon} \setminus A) \cup (\mathbb{R}^3 \s
etminus \overline{B}_R) = \mathbb{R}^3 \setminus A$. ✓ (Every point of $\mathbb{
R}^3 \setminus A$ is either inside $B_{R+\varepsilon} \setminus A$ or outside $\
overline{B}_R$.)

By van Kampen (path-connected, simply connected intersection, so no amalgamation
 relations):
$$\pi_1(\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus A) = \pi_1(U') * \pi_1(V') = \pi_1(U') * 0 = \pi_
1(B_{R+\varepsilon} \setminus A).$$

Now relate $\pi_1(B_{R+\varepsilon} \setminus A)$ to $\pi_1(S^3 \setminus A)$ by
 one more van Kampen application. Decompose $S^3 \setminus A$ as:
$$S^3 \setminus A = \underbrace{(B_{R+\varepsilon} \setminus A)}_{U''} \cup \und
erbrace{(S^3 \setminus \overline{B}_{R+\varepsilon/2})}_{V''},$$
where $V'' = S^3 \setminus \overline{B}_{R+\varepsilon/2}$ is an open $3$-ball (
the cap containing $\infty$), so $\pi_1(V'') = 0$. The intersection
$$U'' \cap V'' = \{R+\varepsilon/2 < |x| < R+\varepsilon\} \setminus A = \{R+\va
repsilon/2 < |x| < R+\varepsilon\}$$
(since $A \subset B_{R+\varepsilon/2}$) is a spherical shell $\simeq S^2$, path-
connected and simply connected. By van Kampen:
$$\pi_1(S^3 \setminus A) = \pi_1(U'') * \pi_1(V'') = \pi_1(B_{R+\varepsilon} \se
tminus A) * 0 = \pi_1(B_{R+\varepsilon} \setminus A).$$

### Conclusion

Chaining the three van Kampen applications:
$$\pi_1(\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus A) \;=\; \pi_1(B_{R+\varepsilon} \setminus A) \;=
\; \pi_1(S^3 \setminus A) \;=\; \mathbb{Z}.$$

$$\boxed{\pi_1(\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus A) \cong \mathbb{Z}.}$$

The generator is the homotopy class of any loop linking $A$ once (passing throug
h the disk bounded by $A$ exactly once). A loop with linking number $k$ with $A$
 represents $k$ times the generator; loops of linking number $0$ are null-homoto
pic.
