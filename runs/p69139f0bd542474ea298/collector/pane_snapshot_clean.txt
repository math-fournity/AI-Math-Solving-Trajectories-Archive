## Answer

**No.** The Mackey irreducibility test is *not* valid over an arbitrary field of
 characteristic zero (or characteristic coprime to $|G|$). It can fail when the
field is not a splitting field for the group. Below I give a concrete counterexa
mple and explain exactly where the proof over $\mathbb{C}$ breaks down.

---

### The Mackey test (over $\mathbb{C}$)

Let $G$ be a finite group, $H \leq G$, and $(\rho, W)$ an irreducible complex re
presentation of $H$. The **Mackey irreducibility criterion** states:

> $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G(\rho)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{C}$ if and only if
> 1. $\rho$ is irreducible, and
> 2. for every $g \in G \setminus H$ (representing non-trivial $(H,H)$-double co
sets), the restricted representations $\rho\big|_{H \cap gHg^{-1}}$ and $\rho^g\
big|_{H \cap gHg^{-1}}$ are **disjoint** (share no irreducible constituent), whe
re $\rho^g(x) = \rho(g^{-1}xg)$.

### Why the proof works over $\mathbb{C}$

The proof uses two ingredients:

**(a) Mackey decomposition + Frobenius reciprocity** (valid over any field $k$ w
ith $\operatorname{char}(k) \nmid |G|$):
$$\operatorname{End}_{kG}(\operatorname{Ind}_H^G W) \;\cong\; \bigoplus_{g \in H
\backslash G/H} \operatorname{Hom}_{k(H \cap gHg^{-1})}\!\bigl(W,\; W^g\bigr) \q
quad (\text{as } k\text{-vector spaces}).$$

**(b) Schur's lemma over $\mathbb{C}$** (uses algebraic closure):
$$\operatorname{Ind}_H^G W \text{ irreducible over } \mathbb{C} \iff \dim_{\math
bb{C}} \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}G}(\operatorname{Ind} W) = 1.$$

Since $W$ irreducible over $\mathbb{C}$ gives $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} \operatorname{E
nd}_{\mathbb{C}H}(W) = 1$ (the $g = e$ term), we get:
$$\dim_{\mathbb{C}} \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}G}(\operatorname{Ind} W) = 1 +
 \sum_{g \neq e} \dim_{\mathbb{C}} \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}(H \cap gHg^{-1
})}(W, W^g).$$
So $\operatorname{Ind} W$ is irreducible $\iff$ every cross-term vanishes $\iff$
 the disjointness condition holds. $\checkmark$

### Where it breaks over a general field

Over an arbitrary field $k$, Schur's lemma only gives:

$$\operatorname{Ind}_H^G W \text{ irreducible over } k \iff \operatorname{End}_{
kG}(\operatorname{Ind} W) \text{ is a division algebra}.$$

The $g = e$ term is $\operatorname{End}_{kH}(W) = D$, a division algebra (possib
ly with $\dim_k D > 1$). If all cross-terms vanish, then $\operatorname{End}_{kG
}(\operatorname{Ind} W) \cong D$, a division algebra, so $\operatorname{Ind} W$
is irreducible. **The forward direction still works.**

But the **converse can fail**: if $\operatorname{End}_{kG}(\operatorname{Ind} W)
 = E$ is a division algebra, we have a vector-space decomposition $E \cong D \op
lus M$ where $M = \bigoplus_{g \neq e} \operatorname{Hom}(\cdots)$. A division a
lgebra $E$ can properly contain a subalgebra $D$ with $E = D \oplus M$ and $M \n
eq 0$ — for instance $\mathbb{H} \supset \mathbb{C}$, or $\mathbb{C} \supset \ma
thbb{R}$. So $M \neq 0$ (some cross-term nonzero, disjointness fails) does **not
** force $E$ to be non-division. The backward direction fails.

### Counterexample

Let:
- $G = \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} = \langle \sigma \rangle$,
- $H = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} = \langle \sigma^2 \rangle$ (the unique subgroup o
f order $2$),
- $k = \mathbb{R}$ (so $\operatorname{char}(k) = 0$, coprime to $|G| = 4$),
- $W = $ the **sign** representation of $H \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$: $\sigma^2 \mapst
o -1$.

**Step 1: $W$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{R}$.** It is $1$-dimensional. $\check
mark$

**Step 2: $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G W$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{R}$.**

Coset representatives of $H$ in $G$: $\{1, \sigma\}$. A basis for $\operatorname
{Ind}_H^G W$ is $\{v_1 = 1 \otimes w,\; v_\sigma = \sigma \otimes w\}$. The acti
on of $\sigma$ is:
$$\sigma \cdot v_1 = v_\sigma, \qquad \sigma \cdot v_\sigma = \sigma^2 \otimes w
 = 1 \otimes (\sigma^2 \cdot w) = -v_1.$$
So $\sigma$ acts by the matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$,
a rotation by $90°$. Its characteristic polynomial is $x^2 + 1$, which has **no
real roots**, so there is no $1$-dimensional invariant subspace. Since $\dim_{\m
athbb{R}} = 2$, the representation is **irreducible over $\mathbb{R}$**. $\check
mark$

(Over $\mathbb{C}$ it splits into two $1$-dimensional eigenspaces with eigenvalu
es $\pm i$; it is irreducible over $\mathbb{R}$ but **not absolutely irreducible
**. Indeed $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{R}G}(\operatorname{Ind} W) \cong \mathbb
{C}$, the commuting algebra of the rotation matrix.)

**Step 3: The Mackey test condition fails.**

The $(H,H)$-double cosets: since $H$ is normal in $G$ (index $2$), the double co
sets are just the cosets $\{H,\; \sigma H\}$, with representatives $g = e$ and $
g = \sigma$.

- **$g = e$:** $H \cap H = H$, $\operatorname{Hom}_H(W, W) = \operatorname{End}_
H(W) = \mathbb{R}$. (This is the "good" term.)
- **$g = \sigma$:** $H \cap \sigma H \sigma^{-1} = H$ (since $H \trianglelefteq
G$). The conjugate representation $W^\sigma$ satisfies:
$$W^\sigma(\sigma^2) = W(\sigma^{-1}\sigma^2\sigma) = W(\sigma^2) = -1,$$
so $W^\sigma = W$. Therefore:
$$\operatorname{Hom}_{H}\!\bigl(W,\; W^\sigma\bigr) = \operatorname{Hom}_H(W, W)
 = \operatorname{End}_H(W) = \mathbb{R} \neq 0.$$

The restrictions $\rho\big|_{H \cap \sigma H \sigma^{-1}}$ and $\rho^\sigma\big|
_{H \cap \sigma H \sigma^{-1}}$ are **the same** irreducible representation $W$,
 so they share a common constituent — they are **not disjoint**.

**Step 4: Contradiction.**

The Mackey test, applied over $k = \mathbb{R}$, predicts: since the disjointness
 condition fails, $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G W$ is **reducible**. But we showed in
Step 2 that $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G W$ is **irreducible** over $\mathbb{R}$. $\c
ontradiction$

The test gives the wrong answer.

### What goes wrong, explicitly

In this example:
$$\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{R}G}(\operatorname{Ind} W) \cong \mathbb{C} = \mat
hbb{R} \oplus \mathbb{R}\,i,$$
where $\mathbb{R} = \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{R}H}(W)$ (the $g = e$ term) and
$\mathbb{R}\,i$ is the nonzero cross-term ($g = \sigma$). The endomorphism algeb
ra $\mathbb{C}$ is a division algebra, so $\operatorname{Ind} W$ is irreducible
— but the cross-term is nonzero, so the disjointness test fails. The dimension-$
1$ argument that works over $\mathbb{C}$ (where $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} \operatorname
{End} = 1$ is the criterion) has no analogue here, because $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} \o
peratorname{End}_{\mathbb{R}G}(\operatorname{Ind} W) = 2 \neq 1$ even though the
 representation is irreducible.

### When the test *is* valid

The Mackey test remains valid over $k$ in either of the following strengthened f
orms:

1. **$k$ is a splitting field for $G$** (equivalently, for $H$ and all $H \cap g
Hg^{-1}$). Then every irreducible is absolutely irreducible, $\operatorname{End}
_{kH}(W) = k$ for irreducible $W$, and the dimension-$1$ argument goes through e
xactly as over $\mathbb{C}$.

2. **Replace "irreducible" with "absolutely irreducible" throughout.** Then:
$$\dim_k \operatorname{End}_{kG}(\operatorname{Ind} W) = \dim_k \operatorname{En
d}_{kH}(W) + \sum_{g \neq e} \dim_k \operatorname{Hom}_{k(H \cap gHg^{-1})}(W, W
^g).$$
$\operatorname{Ind} W$ absolutely irreducible $\iff$ $\dim_k \operatorname{End}_
{kG}(\operatorname{Ind} W) = 1$ $\iff$ $\dim_k \operatorname{End}_{kH}(W) = 1$ (
i.e., $W$ absolutely irreducible) **and** all cross-terms vanish (disjointness).
 This is an if-and-only-if over any $k$ with $\operatorname{char}(k) \nmid |G|$.

But the test as originally stated — with plain "irreducibility" and the disjoint
ness condition — is **not** valid over arbitrary fields of characteristic $0$ or
 coprime to $|G|$. The counterexample above ($G = \mathbb{Z}/4$, $H = \mathbb{Z}
/2$, $k = \mathbb{R}$, $W = $ sign representation) proves this.
