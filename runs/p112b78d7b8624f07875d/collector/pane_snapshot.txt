## Answer

A free homotopy of circular loops (maps $S^1 \to X$) corresponds to a **homotopy
 of ordinary (based) loops in which the basepoint is allowed to move along a pat
h in $X$**. Equivalently, two based loops are freely homotopic as circular loops
 if and only if they represent **conjugate elements** of the fundamental group $
\pi_1(X, x_0)$.

---

## Proof

**Setup and definitions.**

Let $X$ be a path-connected space with basepoint $x_0$. A *circular loop* is a c
ontinuous map $f: S^1 \to X$ with no basepoint condition. A *free homotopy* of c
ircular loops is a continuous map $H: S^1 \times [0,1] \to X$ with no constraint
 on where any point of $S^1$ is sent.

An *ordinary (based) loop* is a continuous map $\gamma: [0,1] \to X$ with $\gamm
a(0) = \gamma(1) = x_0$. A *based homotopy* of ordinary loops fixes the basepoin
t: $H(0,t) = H(1,t) = x_0$ for all $t$.

**Step 1: A free homotopy of circular loops induces a moving-basepoint homotopy
of ordinary loops.**

Identify $S^1 = [0,1]/(0\sim 1)$ and pick the marked point $s_0 = 0 \in S^1$. Gi
ven a free homotopy $H: S^1 \times [0,1] \to X$ with $H(\cdot, 0) = f_0$ and $H(
\cdot, 1) = f_1$, define the **basepoint path**

$$\alpha(t) := H(s_0, t), \quad t \in [0,1].$$

This is a continuous path in $X$ with $\alpha(0) = f_0(s_0)$ and $\alpha(1) = f_
1(s_0)$. At each time $t$, the map $\gamma_t(s) := H(s, t)$ is a loop based at $
\alpha(t)$, since $\gamma_t(0) = \gamma_t(1) = H(s_0, t) = \alpha(t)$. Thus the
free homotopy is exactly a one-parameter family of ordinary loops whose basepoin
t traces out the path $\alpha$.

**Step 2: The conjugacy relation.**

Suppose $f_0$ and $f_1$ both send $s_0$ to $x_0$ (so they are ordinary based loo
ps $\gamma_0, \gamma_1$ at $x_0$), and suppose they are freely homotopic via $H$
. Then $\alpha(t) = H(s_0,t)$ is a loop based at $x_0$ (since $\alpha(0) = \alph
a(1) = x_0$), i.e., $\alpha$ represents an element $[\alpha] \in \pi_1(X, x_0)$.

**Claim:** $[\gamma_1] = [\alpha]^{-1}\,[\gamma_0]\,[\alpha]$ in $\pi_1(X, x_0)$
.

*Proof of claim.* Define a based homotopy (fixing basepoint $x_0$) that "pulls t
he basepoint path back." Concretely, consider the loop $\gamma_0$ and the path $
\alpha$. The concatenation $\alpha \cdot \gamma_1 \cdot \alpha^{-1}$ (traverse $
\alpha$, then $\gamma_1$, then $\alpha$ reversed) is a based loop at $x_0$. The
free homotopy $H$ provides a continuous deformation: at time $t$, the loop $\gam
ma_t$ is based at $\alpha(t)$; pre-concatenating with the segment $\alpha|_{[0,t
]}$ (from $x_0$ to $\alpha(t)$) and post-concatenating with its reverse yields a
 based loop at $x_0$:

$$\widetilde{\gamma}_t := \alpha|_{[0,t]} \;\cdot\; \gamma_t \;\cdot\; \overline
{\alpha|_{[0,t]}}.$$

At $t = 0$: $\widetilde{\gamma}_0 = \gamma_0$ (the pre- and post-segments are co
nstant). At $t = 1$: $\widetilde{\gamma}_1 = \alpha \cdot \gamma_1 \cdot \alpha^
{-1}$. The family $\widetilde{\gamma}_t$ is a **based homotopy** (basepoint fixe
d at $x_0$ throughout), so

$$[\gamma_0] = [\alpha]\,[\gamma_1]\,[\alpha]^{-1} \quad\text{in } \pi_1(X, x_0)
,$$

i.e., $[\gamma_1] = [\alpha]^{-1}\,[\gamma_0]\,[\alpha]$. $\square$

**Step 3: Converse — conjugate loops are freely homotopic.**

Suppose $[\gamma_1] = [\alpha]^{-1}[\gamma_0][\alpha]$ for some loop $\alpha$ at
 $x_0$. Then $\gamma_0$ and $\alpha\cdot\gamma_1\cdot\alpha^{-1}$ are based-homo
topic, hence (a fortiori) freely homotopic. The loop $\alpha\cdot\gamma_1\cdot\a
lpha^{-1}$ is freely homotopic to $\gamma_1$: slide the basepoint along $\alpha$
 — at parameter $t$, use the loop $\alpha|_{[0,t]}\cdot\gamma_1\cdot\overline{\a
lpha|_{[0,t]}}$ based at $\alpha(t)$, which at $t=0$ gives $\gamma_1$ and at $t=
1$ gives $\alpha\cdot\gamma_1\cdot\alpha^{-1}$. By transitivity, $\gamma_0$ and
$\gamma_1$ are freely homotopic.

**Conclusion.**

A free homotopy of circular loops $S^1 \to X$ corresponds precisely to a **homot
opy of ordinary loops with moving basepoint** — a homotopy in which the basepoin
t is allowed to travel along an arbitrary path in $X$. The equivalence classes u
nder free homotopy are in bijection with the **conjugacy classes** of the fundam
ental group $\pi_1(X, x_0)$: two based loops are freely homotopic if and only if
 they are conjugate in $\pi_1(X, x_0)$.
