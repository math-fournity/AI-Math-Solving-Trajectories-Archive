## Proof

Let $a_n$ denote the $n$-th composite squarefree positive integer. We claim that
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{a_n} = +\infty,$$
i.e., the series **diverges**.

**Key observation.** A positive integer is *squarefree* if it is not divisible b
y the square of any prime. A *composite squarefree* number is a squarefree numbe
r that is neither $1$ nor prime.

**Constructing a subseries.** Consider numbers of the form $2p$, where $p$ range
s over all **odd** primes ($p = 3, 5, 7, 11, \dots$).

- Each $2p$ is squarefree: its prime factorization is $2 \cdot p$, with both pri
mes appearing to the first power, so no square divides it.
- Each $2p$ is composite (since $p \geq 3$, we have $2p \geq 6 > 2$, and $2p$ ha
s the nontrivial factorization $2 \times p$).
- The numbers $\{2p : p \text{ odd prime}\}$ are all distinct and each appears a
mong the composite squarefree numbers $\{a_n\}$.

Therefore
$$\sum_{\substack{p \text{ odd prime}}} \frac{1}{2p} \;\leq\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty
} \frac{1}{a_n}.$$

**Euler's theorem on prime reciprocals.** Euler proved that
$$\sum_{p \text{ prime}} \frac{1}{p} = +\infty.$$
Removing the single term $p = 2$ does not affect divergence, so
$$\sum_{\substack{p \text{ odd prime}}} \frac{1}{p} = +\infty.$$

Hence
$$\sum_{\substack{p \text{ odd prime}}} \frac{1}{2p} = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{\substac
k{p \text{ odd prime}}} \frac{1}{p} = +\infty.$$

Since a subseries of $\sum 1/a_n$ already diverges to $+\infty$, and every term
$1/a_n > 0$, the full series must also diverge:
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{a_n} = +\infty.$$

**Conclusion.** The sum of reciprocals of the composite squarefree numbers diver
ges; its value is $+\infty$.
