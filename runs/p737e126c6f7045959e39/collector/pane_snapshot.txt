## Solution

**Setup.** Let the incircle have center $O$ and radius $r$. Let the five sides $
AB, BC, CD, DE, EA$ be tangent to the circle at points $T_1, T_2, T_3, T_4, T_5$
 respectively. Denote the arcs (central angles) between consecutive tangent poin
ts by
$$\alpha_1 = \angle T_1OT_2,\ \alpha_2 = \angle T_2OT_3,\ \alpha_3 = \angle T_3O
T_4,\ \alpha_4 = \angle T_4OT_5,\ \alpha_5 = \angle T_5OT_1,$$
with $\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3+\alpha_4+\alpha_5 = 360°$.

**Key relation.** The angle at each vertex equals $180°$ minus the arc between i
ts two adjacent tangent points (the arc on the vertex's side). Therefore:
- $\angle A = 180° - \alpha_5 = 100° \Rightarrow \alpha_5 = 80°$,
- $\angle C = 180° - \alpha_2 = 100° \Rightarrow \alpha_2 = 80°$,
- $\angle E = 180° - \alpha_4 = 100° \Rightarrow \alpha_4 = 80°$.

Then $\alpha_1 + \alpha_3 = 360° - 240° = 120°$, and $\angle B + \angle D = 360°
 - 120° = 240°$, consistent with the pentagon angle sum $540°$.

**Vertices lie on a concentric circle.** Place $O$ at the origin and set $r=1$.
The tangent line at a point with polar angle $\phi$ is $x\cos\phi + y\sin\phi =
1$. The intersection of the two tangent lines at angles $\phi_1, \phi_2$ (with a
rc $\delta = |\phi_2-\phi_1|$ between them) lies at distance $\sec(\delta/2)$ fr
om $O$, in the direction of the arc's midpoint. Applying this:

| Vertex | Adjacent arc | Distance from $O$ | Direction |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|-----------|
| $A$ | $\alpha_5 = 80°$ | $\sec 40°$ | midpoint of arc $T_5T_1$ |
| $C$ | $\alpha_2 = 80°$ | $\sec 40°$ | midpoint of arc $T_2T_3$ |
| $E$ | $\alpha_4 = 80°$ | $\sec 40°$ | midpoint of arc $T_4T_5$ |

Since $\alpha_2 = \alpha_4 = \alpha_5 = 80°$, the three vertices $A, C, E$ all l
ie on the **same circle** centered at $O$ with radius $R = \sec 40°$.

**Computing $\angle ACE$.** Set $T_1$ at polar angle $0°$. Then:
- $T_5$ is at $360° - \alpha_5 = 280°$, so $A$ is at direction $280° + 40° = 320
°$.
- $T_4$ is at $280° - \alpha_4 = 200°$, so $E$ is at direction $200° + 40° = 240
°$.
- $T_2$ is at $\alpha_1$, $T_3$ at $\alpha_1 + 80°$, so $C$ is at direction $\al
pha_1 + 40°$.

Since $\alpha_1 + \alpha_3 = 120°$ with $\alpha_1, \alpha_3 > 0$, we have $\alph
a_1 \in (0°, 120°)$, so $C$'s direction $\alpha_1 + 40° \in (40°, 160°)$.

The central angle $\angle AOE$ (minor arc) between directions $320°$ and $240°$
is $80°$. The point $C$ (direction $40°$–$160°$) lies on the **major** arc $AE$,
 so the arc $AE$ **not containing** $C$ is the minor arc of $80°$.

By the inscribed angle theorem (since $A, C, E$ lie on the circle centered at $O
$):
$$\angle ACE = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot \angle AOE = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 80° = 40°.$$

**Verification (symmetric case $\alpha_1 = \alpha_3 = 60°$).** With $R = \sec 40
° \approx 1.3054$:
- $A = R(\cos 320°, \sin 320°) \approx (1.0,\ -0.839)$,
- $E = R(\cos 240°, \sin 240°) \approx (-0.653,\ -1.131)$,
- $C = R(\cos 100°, \sin 100°) \approx (-0.227,\ 1.286)$.

Then $\vec{CA}\cdot\vec{CE} / (|CA||CE|) \approx 0.7660 = \cos 40°$, confirming
$\angle ACE = 40°$.

$$\boxed{\angle ACE = 40°}$$
