Looking at this problem, I need to find the generating function for cyclic compo
sitions of *n* (compositions up to circular shift) and their asymptotic growth.

## Setup and Bijection with Binary Necklaces

**Cyclic composition.** A composition of *n* is a sequence $(a_1, \dots, a_k)$ o
f positive integers with $\sum a_i = n$. A *cyclic* composition is an equivalenc
e class under cyclic rotation $(a_1, \dots, a_k) \sim (a_2, \dots, a_k, a_1)$.

**Necklace model.** Place $n$ unit segments around a circle, creating $n$ gaps.
Marking a subset $S$ of gaps as "cuts" partitions the circle into arcs whose len
gths form a composition of $n$. Two markings yield the same cyclic composition i
ff they differ by a rotation. Thus cyclic compositions of $n$ correspond to **bi
nary necklaces of length $n$** (binary strings of length $n$ up to cyclic rotati
on), with one caveat:

- **0 marks** → 1 arc of length $n$ → composition $(n)$.
- **1 mark** → 1 arc of length $n$ → composition $(n)$ (same as above).
- **$k \geq 2$ marks** → $k$ arcs → a $k$-part composition, distinct for each ne
cklace.

So the composition $(n)$ is represented by **two** necklaces (all-zeros and the
single-one necklace) but should be counted once. All other necklaces (with $\geq
 2$ ones) map injectively to distinct cyclic compositions. Hence:

$$c(n) = N_2(n) - 1$$

where $N_2(n)$ is the number of binary necklaces of length $n$.

## Counting Binary Necklaces via Burnside's Lemma

The cyclic group $C_n$ acts on $\{0,1\}^n$ by rotation. By Burnside's lemma, the
 number of orbits is:

$$N_2(n) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{r=0}^{n-1} |\text{Fix}(r)|.$$

A rotation by $r$ positions fixes a binary string iff it is periodic with period
 $g = \gcd(n, r)$, so it is determined by its first $g$ bits: $|\text{Fix}(r)| =
 2^g$. Grouping rotations by $d = \gcd(n,r)$ (there are $\varphi(n/d)$ values of
 $r$ with $\gcd(n,r)=d$):

$$N_2(n) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{d \mid n} \varphi(d)\, 2^{n/d}.$$

**Verification.** $n=4$: $\frac{1}{4}(\varphi(1)\cdot 16 + \varphi(2)\cdot 4 + \
varphi(4)\cdot 2) = \frac{24}{4} = 6$, so $c(4)=5$. The cyclic compositions of 4
 are $(4), (1,3), (2,2), (1,1,2), (1,1,1,1)$. ✓

## The Explicit Formula

$$\boxed{c(n) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{d \mid n} \varphi(d)\, 2^{n/d} \;-\; 1.}$$

## Deriving the Generating Function

Let $C(x) = \sum_{n \geq 1} c(n)\, x^n$. Then:

$$C(x) = \sum_{n \geq 1} \frac{1}{n}\sum_{d \mid n} \varphi(d)\, 2^{n/d}\, x^n \
;-\; \sum_{n \geq 1} x^n.$$

**First sum.** Substitute $n = dm$ (so $d \mid n$, $m = n/d \geq 1$):

$$\sum_{n \geq 1} \frac{1}{n}\sum_{d \mid n} \varphi(d)\, 2^{n/d}\, x^n = \sum_{
d \geq 1} \frac{\varphi(d)}{d} \sum_{m \geq 1} \frac{(2x^d)^m}{m}.$$

Using $-\ln(1-u) = \sum_{m \geq 1} u^m/m$ for $|u|<1$:

$$= \sum_{d \geq 1} \frac{\varphi(d)}{d}\, \ln\frac{1}{1 - 2x^d}.$$

**Second sum.** $\sum_{n \geq 1} x^n = \frac{x}{1-x}$.

Therefore:

$$\boxed{C(x) = \sum_{d \geq 1} \frac{\varphi(d)}{d}\, \ln\frac{1}{1-2x^d} \;-\;
 \frac{x}{1-x}.}$$

**Coefficient check.** The $d=1$ term contributes $\ln\frac{1}{1-2x} = \sum_{m\g
eq 1} \frac{2^m}{m}x^m$. Collecting coefficients of $x^n$ from all $d \mid n$ an
d subtracting 1 (from $-x/(1-x)$):

- $n=1$: $2 - 1 = 1$ ✓
- $n=2$: $2 + 1 - 1 = 2$ ✓
- $n=3$: $\tfrac{8}{3} + \tfrac{4}{3} - 1 = 3$ ✓
- $n=4$: $4 + 1 + 1 - 1 = 5$ ✓
- $n=5$: $\tfrac{32}{5} + \tfrac{8}{5} - 1 = 7$ ✓

## Asymptotic Behavior

The **dominant singularity** of $C(x)$ is at $x = 1/2$, arising from the $d=1$ t
erm:

$$\frac{\varphi(1)}{1}\ln\frac{1}{1-2x} = \ln\frac{1}{1-2x}.$$

For all $d \geq 2$, the terms $\frac{\varphi(d)}{d}\ln\frac{1}{1-2x^d}$ are anal
ytic at $x = 1/2$ (since $2(1/2)^d = 2^{1-d} \leq 1/2 < 1$), and $x/(1-x)$ is an
alytic there as well. Thus, near $x = 1/2$:

$$C(x) = \ln\frac{1}{1-2x} + g(x), \qquad g(x) \text{ analytic at } x = \tfrac{1
}{2}.$$

The coefficient of $x^n$ in $\ln\frac{1}{1-2x}$ is $\frac{2^n}{n}$. Since $g(x)$
 has radius of convergence $> 1/2$ (its nearest singularity is at $x = 2^{-1/2}$
, from the $d=2$ term), its coefficients are $O(2^{n/2})$, and by standard singu
larity analysis:

$$c(n) = \frac{2^n}{n} + O\!\left(\frac{2^{n/2}}{n}\right).$$

This can also be seen directly from the explicit formula: the $d=1$ term gives $
\frac{2^n}{n}$, the $d=2$ term (when $2 \mid n$) gives $\frac{\varphi(2)\cdot 2^
{n/2}}{n} = \frac{2^{n/2}}{n}$, and all remaining divisor terms are $O(2^{n/3})$
, while the $-1$ is $O(1)$.

$$\boxed{c(n) \sim \frac{2^n}{n} \quad \text{as } n \to \infty.}$$

## Summary

- **Explicit formula:** $\displaystyle c(n) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{d \mid n} \varphi
(d)\, 2^{n/d} - 1.$

- **Generating function:** $\displaystyle C(x) = \sum_{d \geq 1} \frac{\varphi(d
)}{d}\,\ln\frac{1}{1-2x^d} - \frac{x}{1-x}.$

- **Asymptotics:** $\displaystyle c(n) \sim \frac{2^n}{n}$, with error $O\!\left
(\frac{2^{n/2}}{n}\right)$.

The key insight is the bijection between cyclic compositions of $n$ and binary n
ecklaces of length $n$ (minus one for the double-counting of the trivial composi
tion $(n)$), which brings in Burnside's lemma and the Euler totient function, an
d the fact that the dominant singularity at $x = 1/2$ comes solely from the $d=1
$ term, yielding the $2^n/n$ growth rate.
