## Proof

We prove the uniqueness of the conformal map $f: D \to G = \{|w|<1\}$ under the
stated normalization.

**Setup.** Suppose $f_1$ and $f_2$ are two univalent analytic functions, each co
nformally mapping $D$ onto $G$, and each having, in a neighborhood of $\infty$,
an expansion
$$f_j(z) = \frac{a_1^{(j)}}{z} + \frac{a_2^{(j)}}{z^2} + \cdots, \qquad a_1^{(j)
} > 0, \quad j = 1, 2.$$

The absence of a constant term means $f_1(\infty) = f_2(\infty) = 0$.

**Step 1: The two maps differ by a disc automorphism.** Since $f_1: D \to G$ is
a conformal bijection, $f_1^{-1}: G \to D$ is well defined and analytic. Set
$$\phi := f_2 \circ f_1^{-1}: G \to G.$$
Then $\phi$ is a conformal automorphism of the unit disc. Every automorphism of
$G$ has the form
$$\phi(w) = e^{i\theta}\,\frac{w - \alpha}{1 - \overline{\alpha}\,w}, \qquad |\a
lpha| < 1,\ \theta \in \mathbb{R}.$$

**Step 2: $\phi$ fixes the origin, hence is a rotation.** Since $f_1(\infty) = 0
$, we have $f_1^{-1}(0) = \infty$. Therefore
$$\phi(0) = f_2\bigl(f_1^{-1}(0)\bigr) = f_2(\infty) = 0.$$
The condition $\phi(0) = 0$ forces $\alpha = 0$, so
$$\phi(w) = e^{i\theta}\, w.$$

**Step 3: The normalization $a_1 > 0$ forces $\theta = 0$.** From $f_2 = \phi \c
irc f_1$ we obtain
$$f_2(z) = e^{i\theta}\, f_1(z).$$
Comparing the leading terms of the expansions at $\infty$:
$$\frac{a_1^{(2)}}{z} + \cdots = e^{i\theta}\left(\frac{a_1^{(1)}}{z} + \cdots\r
ight),$$
so $a_1^{(2)} = e^{i\theta}\, a_1^{(1)}$. By hypothesis both $a_1^{(1)} > 0$ and
 $a_1^{(2)} > 0$ are positive real numbers. Hence
$$e^{i\theta} = \frac{a_1^{(2)}}{a_1^{(1)}} > 0,$$
which (being a complex number of unit modulus and positive real) gives $e^{i\the
ta} = 1$, i.e., $\theta \equiv 0 \pmod{2\pi}$.

**Conclusion.** $\phi = \mathrm{id}_G$, and therefore $f_2 = f_1$. The conformal
 map satisfying the given normalization is unique. $\blacksquare$
