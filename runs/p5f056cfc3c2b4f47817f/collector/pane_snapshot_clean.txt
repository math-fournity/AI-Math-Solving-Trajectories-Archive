## Solution

**Setup.** Place the circumcircle as the unit circle. Let the vertices $A,B,C,D,
E$ appear in order at angles $\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta, \epsilon$, and defi
ne the arc gaps
$$a=\beta-\alpha,\ b=\gamma-\beta,\ c=\delta-\gamma,\ d=\epsilon-\delta,\ e=2\pi
-(\epsilon-\alpha),$$
so $a+b+c+d+e=2\pi$, all positive. The area of a cyclic polygon on the unit circ
le is
$$\text{Area}=\tfrac12\bigl(\sin a+\sin b+\sin c+\sin d+\sin e\bigr).$$

**Translating $AC\perp BD$.** The chord joining angles $\theta_1,\theta_2$ has d
irection angle $\tfrac{\theta_1+\theta_2}{2}+\tfrac\pi2$. Thus $AC\perp BD$ give
s
$$\tfrac{\alpha+\gamma}{2}-\tfrac{\beta+\delta}{2}=\tfrac\pi2\pmod\pi,\quad\text
{i.e.}\quad \alpha+\gamma-\beta-\delta=\pi\pmod{2\pi}.$$
Substituting $\beta=\alpha+a,\ \gamma=\alpha+a+b,\ \delta=\alpha+a+b+c$:
$$\alpha+\gamma-\beta-\delta=-a-c.$$
Since $0<a+c<2\pi$, the constraint is
$$\boxed{a+c=\pi}.$$

**Reducing the area.** With $c=\pi-a$ and $e=\pi-b-d$:
$$\text{Area}=\tfrac12\bigl[\sin a+\sin b+\sin(\pi-a)+\sin d+\sin(\pi-b-d)\bigr]
=\tfrac12\bigl[2\sin a+\sin b+\sin d+\sin(b+d)\bigr].$$
The variables $a$ and $(b,d)$ separate.

- **$a$-part:** $\max_{0<a<\pi}2\sin a=2$, attained at $a=\pi/2$.
- **$(b,d)$-part:** For fixed $s=b+d$, $\sin b+\sin d=2\sin(s/2)\cos((b-d)/2)$ i
s maximized at $b=d=s/2$, giving $2\sin(s/2)$. Set $t=s/2\in(0,\pi/2)$ and maxim
ize
$$g(t)=2\sin t+\sin 2t=2\sin t(1+\cos t).$$
$$g'(t)=2\bigl[\cos t+\cos 2t\bigr]=0\implies 2\cos^2 t+\cos t-1=0\implies \cos
t=\tfrac12,\ t=\tfrac\pi3.$$
Then $g(\pi/3)=2\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\cdot\tfrac32=\tfrac{3\sqrt3}{2}$.

**Combining.**
$$\text{Area}_{\max}=\tfrac12\Bigl[2+\tfrac{3\sqrt3}{2}\Bigr]=\frac{4+3\sqrt3}{4
}.$$

**Verification of the maximizer.** $a=\pi/2,\ b=d=\pi/3$ gives $c=\pi/2,\ e=\pi/
3$; all arcs positive, sum $=2\pi$, and $a+c=\pi$. The area equals $\tfrac12(1+\
tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}+1+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2})=\tfrac12(2+\tfrac{3\sqrt
3}{2})=\tfrac{4+3\sqrt3}{4}$. ✓

The maximum area of the pentagon is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{4+3\sqrt3}{4}}.$$
