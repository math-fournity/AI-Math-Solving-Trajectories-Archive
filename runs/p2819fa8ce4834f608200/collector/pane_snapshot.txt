Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a proper 3-coloring of the

## Key Structural Observation

A fundamental property of the icosahedron: **the 5 neighbors of any vertex form
a 5-cycle.**

This is because each vertex of the icosahedron is surrounded by 5 triangular fac
es. Consecutive neighbors (in cyclic order around the vertex) share a face with
the central vertex, hence are joined by an edge. So the neighborhood of every ve
rtex is a cycle of length 5.

## Proof of Impossibility

**Claim:** The icosahedron graph admits no proper 3-coloring at all. Therefore t
he number of balanced paintings is **0**.

**Proof:**

Pick any vertex $v$ and consider its 5 neighbors $w_0, w_1, w_2, w_3, w_4$, whic
h form a 5-cycle $w_0 w_1 w_2 w_3 w_4 w_0$.

**Step 1: The 5-cycle forces all three colors.** A 5-cycle is an odd cycle, so i
t cannot be properly colored with only 2 colors. Any proper 3-coloring of a 5-cy
cle must use all three colors. (If only 2 colors were used, we'd have a proper 2
-coloring of an odd cycle, which is impossible.)

**Step 2: The central vertex has no available color.** The vertex $v$ is adjacen
t to all five vertices $w_0, \ldots, w_4$. Since these five vertices collectivel
y use all three colors (by Step 1), the vertex $v$ is adjacent to at least one v
ertex of each color. Therefore no color can be assigned to $v$ without creating
a monochromatic edge.

This is a contradiction. Since $v$ was chosen arbitrarily, **no proper 3-colorin
g of the icosahedron graph exists.**

## Conclusion

Since there is no proper 3-coloring whatsoever, there are certainly no *balanced

$$\boxed{0}$$
