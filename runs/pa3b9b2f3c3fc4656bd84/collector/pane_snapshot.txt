Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the condition "every smooth
 $f: M \to \mathbb{R}^n$ has regular values forming an open set" forces $M$ to b
e compact, given $m > n$.

**Answer: Yes, $M$ must be compact.**

## Proof

### Forward direction: Compact $\Rightarrow$ regular values always open

Suppose $M$ is compact and $f: M \to \mathbb{R}^n$ is smooth. The set of critica
l points $C = \{p \in M : \operatorname{rank}(df_p) < n\}$ is closed in $M$, sin
ce the rank condition is equivalent to the vanishing of all $n \times n$ minors
of the Jacobian—a closed condition. Since $M$ is compact, $C$ is compact, so $f(
C)$ is compact in $\mathbb{R}^n$, hence closed. The set of regular values is $\m
athbb{R}^n \setminus f(C)$, which is open. $\checkmark$

### Reverse direction: Non-compact $\Rightarrow$ exists $f$ with non-open regula
r values

We prove the contrapositive. Suppose $M$ is non-compact. We construct a smooth $
f: M \to \mathbb{R}^n$ whose regular values are **not** open.

**Constructing the sequence and neighborhoods.** Since $M$ is a non-compact mani
fold (metrizable, hence non-sequentially-compact), there exists a sequence $\{p_
k\}_{k=1}^\infty$ in $M$ with no convergent subsequence (i.e., $p_k \to \infty$)
. Choose pairwise disjoint coordinate neighborhoods $U_k$ around each $p_k$ with
 pairwise disjoint closures (possible since $p_k$ eventually leaves every compac
t set).

**Constructing bump functions.** For each $k$, let $\psi_k: M \to [0,1]$ be a sm
ooth bump function with $\psi_k(p_k) = 1$, $d\psi_k(p_k) = 0$, and $\operatornam
e{supp}(\psi_k) \subset U_k$. Since the $U_k$ are pairwise disjoint, at most one
 $\psi_k$ is nonzero at any point, so the sum is locally finite and smooth.

**Defining $f_1: M \to \mathbb{R}$.** Set
$$f_1 = 2 - \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(2 - \frac{1}{k}\right)\psi_k.$$

Then:
- **Smooth:** locally finite sum of smooth functions. $\checkmark$
- **Value at $p_k$:** $f_1(p_k) = 2 - (2 - 1/k) = 1/k$. $\checkmark$
- **$p_k$ is critical:** $df_1(p_k) = -(2-1/k)\,d\psi_k(p_k) = 0$ since $\psi_k$
 has a maximum at $p_k$. $\checkmark$
- **$f_1 > 0$ everywhere:** On $U_k$, $f_1 = 2 - (2-1/k)\psi_k \geq 2 - (2-1/k)
= 1/k > 0$. Outside all $U_k$, $f_1 = 2$. $\checkmark$

**Defining $f: M \to \mathbb{R}^n$.** Set $f(p) = (f_1(p), 0, \ldots, 0)$.

**Case $n = 1$ (so $m \geq 2$):** Here $f = f_1$. The critical values include $\
{1/k : k \geq 1\}$, since each $p_k$ is critical with $f(p_k) = 1/k$. Since $f_1
 > 0$ everywhere, $0 \notin f(M)$, so $f^{-1}(0) = \emptyset$ and $0$ is a regul
ar value (vacuously). But $0 = \lim_{k\to\infty} 1/k$ is a limit of critical val
ues. Thus the set of critical values is not closed, and the set of regular value
s is not open.

**Case $n > 1$ (so $m > n \geq 2$):** Since $df = (df_1, 0, \ldots, 0)$ has rank
 at most $1 < n$, **every** point of $M$ is critical. The set of critical values
 is $f(M) = f_1(M) \times \{0\}^{n-1}$. The points $(1/k, 0, \ldots, 0) \in f(M)
$ converge to $(0, 0, \ldots, 0)$, but $(0,\ldots,0) \notin f(M)$ since $f_1 > 0
$. Hence $f(M)$ is not closed, and the set of regular values $\mathbb{R}^n \setm
inus f(M)$ is not open (it contains $(0,\ldots,0)$, which is a limit point of th
e complement).

### Conclusion

In both cases, non-compactness of $M$ allows us to construct a smooth $f: M \to
\mathbb{R}^n$ whose regular values do not form an open set. Combined with the fo
rward direction, the condition "every smooth $f: M \to \mathbb{R}^n$ has regular
 values forming an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$" holds **if and only if** $M$ i
s compact.

Therefore, **yes, $M$ must be a compact manifold.** $\blacksquare$
