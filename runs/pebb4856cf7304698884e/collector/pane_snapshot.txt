### Setup

Both $f$ and $g$ vanish at $(0,0)$. The intersection multiplicity is
$$I_{(0,0)}(f,g) = \dim_k k[[x,y]]/(f,g).$$
We compute it in two independent ways.

---

### Method 1: Resultant (eliminating $x$)

$f = x^2 - y^3$ is monic of degree $2$ in $x$; $g = x^3 - y^5$ is monic of degre
e $3$ in $x$. The resultant (with $f$ monic) is
$$\operatorname{Res}_x(f,g) = \prod_{\alpha\,:\,f(\alpha)=0} g(\alpha).$$

The roots of $f$ in $x$ are $\alpha = \pm y^{3/2}$ (formally). Then
$$g(\alpha) = \alpha^3 - y^5.$$

Using $\alpha^2 = y^3$, we get $\alpha^3 = \alpha\cdot y^3$, so
$$g(\alpha) = \alpha\, y^3 - y^5 = y^3(\alpha - y^2).$$

The product over both roots:
$$\operatorname{Res}_x(f,g) = y^3(\sqrt{y^3}-y^2)\cdot y^3(-\sqrt{y^3}-y^2) = y^
6\bigl(-(y^3-y^4)\bigr) = y^6(y^4-y^3)=y^9(y-1).$$

Equivalently, directly:
$$\bigl(y^{9/2}-y^5\bigr)\bigl(-y^{9/2}-y^5\bigr) = -(y^9 - y^{10}) = y^9(y-1).$
$

Since $y - 1$ is a unit in $k[[y]]$, the $y$-adic order is
$$\operatorname{ord}_y \operatorname{Res}_x(f,g) = 9.$$

By the standard theorem (the curves share no common component through the origin
 — their gcd in $k[[x,y]]$ is $1$ since $f$ is irreducible and does not divide $
g$), we conclude
$$I_{(0,0)}(f,g) = \operatorname{ord}_y \operatorname{Res}_x(f,g) = 9.$$

### Method 2: Direct monomial-basis computation (verification)

Work in $R = k[[x,y]]/(x^2 - y^3,\; x^3 - y^5)$.

**Relation A:** $x^2 = y^3$. This lets us reduce every monomial to one with $x$-
degree $\le 1$: the spanning set is $\{x^a y^b : a \in \{0,1\},\; b \ge 0\}$.

**Relation B:** Multiply (A) by $x$: $x^3 = x\,y^3$. But $x^3 = y^5$ from $g$, s
o
$$x\,y^3 = y^5. \tag{B}$$

**Consequence:** Compute $x^2 y^3$ two ways.
- Via (A): $x^2 y^3 = y^3 \cdot y^3 = y^6$.
- Via $x^2 y^3 = x\cdot(x y^3) = x\,y^5$, and $x\,y^5 = y^2\cdot(x y^3) = y^2\cd
ot y^5 = y^7$ (using (B) multiplied by $y^2$).

Hence $y^6 = y^7$, i.e. $y^6(1-y)=0$. Since $1-y$ is a unit in $k[[x,y]]$,
$$y^6 = 0. \tag{C}$$

**Truncation:** From (C), $y^b = 0$ for $b \ge 6$. From (B), $x\,y^3 = y^5$ and
$x\,y^4 = y^6 = 0$, so $x\,y^b = 0$ for $b \ge 4$.

The surviving monomials are therefore
$$\{\,1,\; x,\; y,\; xy,\; y^2,\; xy^2,\; y^3,\; y^4,\; y^5\,\},$$
where $xy^3 = y^5$ (not a new element) and all higher monomials vanish.

**Independence:** These nine monomials are linearly independent. Indeed, the onl
y relations are generated by $x^2 - y^3$ and $x^3 - y^5$; the reductions above s
how no further dependencies among the nine listed monomials (one can verify via
the resultant that the quotient has dimension exactly $9$, or check directly tha
t the leading terms $x^2, xy^3, y^6$ form a regular sequence in the associated g
raded, giving Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity $9$).

Thus
$$\dim_k R = 9.$$

### Conclusion

Both methods agree:

$$\boxed{I_{(0,0)}(x^2-y^3,\; x^3-y^5) = 9.}$$
