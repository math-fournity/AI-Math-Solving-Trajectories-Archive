**Answer: No.**

**Counterexample.** Let $E = \mathbb{R}$ with Lebesgue measure, and let $f \equi
v 1$. Then $f$ is measurable and nonnegative, with
$$\int_E f = m(\mathbb{R}) = +\infty,$$
so $f$ is **not** integrable ($\int_E |f| = +\infty$).

Now let $(E_n)$ be any ascending sequence of measurable sets with $\bigcup_{n=0}
^\infty E_n = E$. The functions $g_n := f \cdot \mathbf{1}_{E_n}$ are nonnegativ
e and measurable, and since $E_n \uparrow E$, we have $g_n \uparrow f \cdot \mat
hbf{1}_E = f$ pointwise. By the **Monotone Convergence Theorem**,
$$\int_{E_n} f = \int g_n \uparrow \int f = \int_E f = +\infty.$$
Hence $\int_{E_n} f \to \int_E f$ (in the extended-real sense, which is the stan
dard notion of convergence for nonnegative integrals) for **every** ascending se
quence $E_n \uparrow E$.

Yet $f$ is not integrable. Therefore the stated condition does **not** imply tha
t $f$ is integrable.

**Remark.** The key point is that for nonnegative measurable functions, the Mono
tone Convergence Theorem guarantees $\int_{E_n} f \uparrow \int_E f$ for *every*
 ascending sequence $E_n \uparrow E$, **automatically and unconditionally** — re
gardless of whether $\int_E f$ is finite or infinite. Thus the hypothesis is sat
isfied by *any* nonnegative measurable function, including those with infinite i
ntegral, which are precisely not integrable. The condition therefore carries no
force beyond measurability and nonnegativity (or more generally, beyond having a
 well-defined extended-real integral), and cannot force $\int_E |f| < \infty$.
