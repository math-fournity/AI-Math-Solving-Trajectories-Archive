## Solution

**Setup.** Let the equilateral triangle $ABC$ have side length $s$. Place coordi
nates with $B=(0,0)$, $C=(s,0)$, $A=(s/2,\, s\sqrt3/2)$.

Since $Q,R\in BC$ and $PQRS$ is a rectangle, side $QR$ lies along $BC$ (horizont
al), so $PQ$ and $RS$ are vertical. Thus $P$ is directly above $Q$ and $S$ is di
rectly above $R$.

**Parametrize.** Let $Q=(q,0)$, $R=(r,0)$ with $q<r$.

- $P\in AB$: line $AB$ has equation $y=\sqrt3\,x$, so $P=(q,\,\sqrt3\,q)$, requi
ring $0\le q\le s/2$.
- $S\in AC$: line $AC$ has equation $y=\sqrt3(s-x)$, so $S=(r,\,\sqrt3(s-r))$, r
equiring $s/2\le r\le s$.

For $PQRS$ to be a rectangle, $SP$ must be horizontal, so $P$ and $S$ share the
same $y$-coordinate:
$$\sqrt3\,q=\sqrt3(s-r)\;\Longrightarrow\; r=s-q.$$

**Dimensions.**
$$PQ=\sqrt3\,q,\qquad PS=QR=r-q=(s-q)-q=s-2q.$$

**Apply $PQ=\sqrt3\,PS$:**
$$\sqrt3\,q=\sqrt3(s-2q)\;\Longrightarrow\; q=s-2q\;\Longrightarrow\; 3q=s\;\Lon
grightarrow\; q=\frac{s}{3}.$$

Then $r=s-q=\frac{2s}{3}$, and
$$PQ=\frac{s\sqrt3}{3},\qquad PS=\frac{s}{3}.$$

**Apply the area condition.** The area of $PQRS$ is
$$PQ\cdot PS=\frac{s\sqrt3}{3}\cdot\frac{s}{3}=\frac{s^2\sqrt3}{9}=28\sqrt3.$$
$$\frac{s^2}{9}=28\;\Longrightarrow\; s^2=252\;\Longrightarrow\; s=6\sqrt7.$$

**Compute $PC$.** With $P=(s/3,\, s\sqrt3/3)$ and $C=(s,0)$:
$$PC^2=\left(s-\frac{s}{3}\right)^2+\left(\frac{s\sqrt3}{3}\right)^2=\frac{4s^2}
{9}+\frac{3s^2}{9}=\frac{7s^2}{9}.$$
$$PC=\frac{s\sqrt7}{3}=\frac{6\sqrt7\cdot\sqrt7}{3}=\frac{6\cdot 7}{3}=14.$$

$$\boxed{PC=14}$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
