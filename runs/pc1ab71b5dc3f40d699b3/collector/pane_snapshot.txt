## Asymptotic Approximation of Γ(n+1) for Large Positive n

We derive **Stirling's approximation**:

$$\Gamma(n+1) \sim \sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^n, \qquad n\to\infty.$$

### Setup

Using the integral representation, valid for $n>-1$ (so in particular for all la
rge positive $n$, integer or not):

$$\Gamma(n+1)=\int_0^\infty t^n e^{-t}\,dt.$$

Substitute $t = ns$, $dt = n\,ds$:

$$\Gamma(n+1) = n^{n+1}\int_0^\infty s^n e^{-ns}\,ds = n^{n+1}\int_0^\infty e^{\
,n(\ln s - s)}\,ds.$$

Define $\varphi(s) = \ln s - s$. The integral becomes

$$\Gamma(n+1) = n^{n+1}\int_0^\infty e^{\,n\,\varphi(s)}\,ds.$$

### Locating the maximum

$$\varphi'(s) = \frac{1}{s}-1 = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; s=1, \qquad \varphi(1) = -
1,$$
$$\varphi''(s) = -\frac{1}{s^2}, \qquad \varphi''(1) = -1 < 0.$$

So $\varphi$ has a unique **global maximum** at $s=1$ (since $\varphi(s)\to -\in
fty$ as $s\to 0^+$ and as $s\to\infty$). For large $n$ the integrand $e^{n\varph
i(s)}$ is sharply peaked near $s=1$, so the integral is dominated by a neighborh
ood of $s=1$.

### Laplace's method

Expand $\varphi$ around $s=1$. Set $s = 1+u$:

$$\varphi(1+u) = -1 + \tfrac12\varphi''(1)u^2 + O(u^3) = -1 - \tfrac12 u^2 + O(u
^3).$$

The cubic term is $\tfrac16\varphi'''(1)u^3 = \tfrac16\cdot 2\,u^3 = \tfrac13 u^
3$, but it contributes only lower-order corrections. To leading order,

$$\int_0^\infty e^{\,n\varphi(s)}\,ds \approx e^{-n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-
\tfrac{n}{2}u^2}\,du.$$

(Extending the lower limit from $u=-1$ to $-\infty$ introduces only an exponenti
ally small error, since the Gaussian decays as $e^{-nu^2/2}$.)

The Gaussian integral evaluates exactly:

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-\tfrac{n}{2}u^2}\,du = \sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{n}}.$$

Therefore

$$\int_0^\infty e^{\,n\varphi(s)}\,ds \sim e^{-n}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{n}}.$$

### Assembling

$$\Gamma(n+1) \sim n^{n+1}\cdot e^{-n}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{n}} = n^{n+1}\,e^{-n}\,\
frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\sqrt{n}} = \sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^n.$$

### Error estimate (sketch)

Keeping the cubic and quartic terms in the Taylor expansion and expanding the in
tegrand in powers of $n^{-1/2}$ yields the full Stirling series

$$\Gamma(n+1) \sim \sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^n\left(1+\frac{1}{12n}+
\frac{1}{288n^2}-\frac{139}{51840n^3}-\cdots\right),$$

an asymptotic (divergent) series in powers of $1/n$. The leading term already gi
ves relative error $O(1/n)$, since the first correction is $1+1/(12n)+O(n^{-2})$
.

### Conclusion

For large positive $n$ (not necessarily an integer),

$$\boxed{\;\Gamma(n+1)\sim \sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^n\;}$$

with the next correction being the factor $\bigl(1+\tfrac{1}{12n}+O(n^{-2})\bigr
)$.
