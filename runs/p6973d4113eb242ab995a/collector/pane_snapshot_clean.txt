## Proof

The operator $T: C[0,1] \to C[0,1]$ is given by
$$Tf(x) = \int_0^1 \frac{f(y)}{1+x^2+y^2}\,dy,$$
with the sup norm $\|f\|_\infty = \sup_{x\in[0,1]}|f(x)|$.

**Step 1: Upper bound.** For any $f \in C[0,1]$ and any $x \in [0,1]$,
$$|Tf(x)| \le \int_0^1 \frac{|f(y)|}{1+x^2+y^2}\,dy \le \|f\|_\infty \int_0^1 \f
rac{dy}{1+x^2+y^2}.$$
Hence
$$\|Tf\|_\infty \le \|f\|_\infty \cdot \sup_{x\in[0,1]} \int_0^1 \frac{dy}{1+x^2
+y^2},$$
so
$$\|T\| \le \sup_{x\in[0,1]} \int_0^1 \frac{dy}{1+x^2+y^2}.$$

**Step 2: Compute the integral.** For fixed $x$, with $a = \sqrt{1+x^2}$,
$$\int_0^1 \frac{dy}{1+x^2+y^2} = \int_0^1 \frac{dy}{a^2+y^2} = \frac{1}{a}\arct
an\!\left(\frac{y}{a}\right)\bigg|_0^1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}\arctan\!\left(\f
rac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}\right).$$
Define
$$g(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}\arctan\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}\right).$$

**Step 3: Maximize $g$ on $[0,1]$.** Set $t = \sqrt{1+x^2}$, so $t \in [1,\sqrt{
2}]$ and $t$ is increasing in $x$. Define
$$h(t) = \frac{1}{t}\arctan\!\left(\frac{1}{t}\right).$$
Differentiating:
$$h'(t) = -\frac{1}{t^2}\arctan\!\left(\frac{1}{t}\right) + \frac{1}{t}\cdot\fra
c{1}{1+1/t^2}\cdot\left(-\frac{1}{t^2}\right) = -\frac{1}{t^2}\arctan\!\left(\fr
ac{1}{t}\right) - \frac{1}{t(t^2+1)}.$$
Both terms are negative for $t > 0$, so $h'(t) < 0$. Thus $h$ is strictly decrea
sing in $t$, and since $t$ increases with $x$, $g(x) = h(\sqrt{1+x^2})$ is stric
tly decreasing in $x$. The maximum on $[0,1]$ is therefore attained at $x=0$:
$$g(0) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1}}\arctan\!\left(\frac{1}{1}\right) = \arctan(1) = \fra
c{\pi}{4}.$$

**Step 4: Lower bound (equality).** Take $f \equiv 1$, so $\|f\|_\infty = 1$. Th
en
$$Tf(0) = \int_0^1 \frac{dy}{1+0+y^2} = \int_0^1 \frac{dy}{1+y^2} = \arctan(1) -
 \arctan(0) = \frac{\pi}{4}.$$
Therefore $\|Tf\|_\infty \ge Tf(0) = \pi/4 = \|f\|_\infty \cdot \pi/4$, giving $
\|T\| \ge \pi/4$.

**Conclusion.** Combining the upper and lower bounds,
$$\boxed{\|T\| = \frac{\pi}{4}}.$$
