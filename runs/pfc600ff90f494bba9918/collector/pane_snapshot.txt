## Problem

Let $S$ be a hyperbolic surface and $p: M \to S$ be a covering map. Determine if
 there exists a metric on $M$ such that $M$ is a hyperbolic surface and $p$ is a
 local isometry.

## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** Such a metric always exists on $M$.

**Setup.** Let $g$ be the hyperbolic metric on $S$, i.e. a Riemannian metric of
constant Gaussian curvature $-1$. Let $p: M \to S$ be a covering map. We constru
ct a metric $\tilde g$ on $M$ by pulling back $g$ along $p$, and verify that $\t
ilde g$ is well defined, hyperbolic, and makes $p$ a local isometry.

**Construction (pullback metric).** For each point $x \in M$ and each pair of ta
ngent vectors $v, w \in T_x M$, define
$$
\tilde g_x(v, w) := g_{p(x)}\bigl(dp_x(v),\, dp_x(w)\bigr).
$$
Since $p$ is a covering map, the differential $dp_x : T_x M \to T_{p(x)} S$ is a
 linear isomorphism at every $x$ (covering maps are local diffeomorphisms). Henc
e $\tilde g_x$ is a positive-definite, symmetric bilinear form on $T_x M$, so $\
tilde g$ is a Riemannian metric on $M$. Smoothness of $\tilde g$ follows from sm
oothness of $g$ and of $p$ (a covering map of smooth manifolds is smooth, and lo
cally $p$ is a diffeomorphism, so in local charts $\tilde g$ is the pullback of
a smooth metric by a diffeomorphism).

**$p$ is a local isometry.** By construction, for every $x \in M$,
$$
\tilde g_x(v, w) = g_{p(x)}(dp_x\, v,\, dp_x\, w),
$$
which is exactly the statement that $dp_x : (T_x M, \tilde g_x) \to (T_{p(x)} S,
 g_{p(x)})$ is a linear isometry for every $x$. This is the infinitesimal charac
terization of a local isometry, so $p : (M, \tilde g) \to (S, g)$ is a local iso
metry.

**$M$ is hyperbolic.** A local isometry preserves all local Riemannian invariant
s, in particular the Gaussian curvature $K$. Concretely, if $U \subseteq M$ is a
n evenly covered neighborhood and $p|_U : U \to V$ is a diffeomorphism onto an o
pen set $V \subseteq S$, then $p|_U$ is an isometry between $(U, \tilde g)$ and
$(V, g)$. Isometries preserve curvature, so
$$
K_{\tilde g}\big|_U = K_g \circ p\big|_U.
$$
Since $g$ is hyperbolic, $K_g \equiv -1$ on $S$, and therefore $K_{\tilde g} \eq
uiv -1$ on $M$. Thus $\tilde g$ has constant curvature $-1$, which is precisely
the condition for $M$ to be a hyperbolic surface.

(Equivalently, one may observe that the pullback of a metric of constant section
al curvature $-1$ by a local diffeomorphism again has constant sectional curvatu
re $-1$, since the curvature tensor and the metric scale identically under pullb
ack, leaving the normalized sectional curvature unchanged.)

**Conclusion.** The pullback metric $\tilde g = p^* g$ is a well-defined Riemann
ian metric on $M$, it has constant Gaussian curvature $-1$ (so $M$ is a hyperbol
ic surface), and by construction $p : (M, \tilde g) \to (S, g)$ is a local isome
try. Therefore such a metric always exists.
