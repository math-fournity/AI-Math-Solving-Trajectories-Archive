## Proof

**Claim:** The sum equals the zero matrix.

**Key Tool — Schur Orthogonality (general form).** Let $\pi: G \to \mathrm{GL}(V
)$ and $\rho: G \to \mathrm{GL}(W)$ be irreducible representations over $\mathbb
{C}$ with $\pi \not\cong \rho$. Then for all indices $a,b,c,d$:
$$\sum_{g \in G} \pi(g)_{ab}\,\rho(g^{-1})_{cd} = 0.$$

*Derivation.* Consider the averaging map $\Phi: \mathrm{Hom}(W,V) \to \mathrm{Ho
m}(W,V)$ defined by
$$\Phi(A) = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g \in G} \pi(g)\, A\, \rho(g)^{-1}.$$
A direct substitution shows $\pi(h)\,\Phi(A) = \Phi(A)\,\rho(h)$ for all $h \in
G$, so $\Phi$ maps into $\mathrm{Hom}_G(W,V) = \{A : \pi(g)A = A\rho(g)\ \forall
 g\}$. Since $\Phi$ is a projection (it acts as the identity on $\mathrm{Hom}_G$
), its image is exactly $\mathrm{Hom}_G(W,V)$. By **Schur's Lemma**, since $\pi$
 and $\rho$ are distinct irreducibles, $\mathrm{Hom}_G(W,V) = \{0\}$. Therefore
$\Phi(A) = 0$ for every $A$.

Setting $A = E_{bc}$ (the matrix unit with $1$ in position $(b,c)$ and $0$ elsew
here) and reading off the $(a,d)$-entry yields exactly $\sum_g \pi(g)_{ab}\,\rho
(g^{-1})_{cd} = 0$. $\square$

**Applying to the sum.** Both $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$ have degree $d$, so each $\pi_
1(g)\,\pi_2(g^{-1})^\top$ is a well-defined $d \times d$ matrix. Computing the $
(i,j)$-entry of $S = \sum_{g} \pi_1(g)\,\pi_2(g^{-1})^\top$:

$$S_{ij} = \sum_{g \in G} \sum_{k=1}^{d} \pi_1(g)_{ik}\,\bigl(\pi_2(g^{-1})^\top
\bigr)_{kj} = \sum_{k=1}^{d} \sum_{g \in G} \pi_1(g)_{ik}\,\pi_2(g^{-1})_{jk},$$

since $(\pi_2(g^{-1})^\top)_{kj} = \pi_2(g^{-1})_{jk}$.

Now apply the orthogonality relation with $\pi = \pi_1$, $\rho = \pi_2$ (which a
re **distinct** irreducibles, so $\pi_1 \not\cong \pi_2$), and indices $a = i$,
$b = k$, $c = j$, $d = k$:

$$\sum_{g \in G} \pi_1(g)_{ik}\,\pi_2(g^{-1})_{jk} = 0 \qquad \text{for all } i,
 j, k.$$

Therefore:
$$S_{ij} = \sum_{k=1}^{d} 0 = 0 \qquad \text{for all } i, j,$$

which gives $S = 0$.

**Remark.** The "same degree" hypothesis ensures the matrix product is well-defi
ned; the vanishing itself relies only on $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$ being distinct irre
ducibles. The proof does not require unitarity—the orthogonality relation $\sum_
g \pi(g)_{ab}\,\rho(g^{-1})_{cd} = 0$ for distinct irreducibles is a direct cons
equence of Schur's Lemma via the averaging projection, valid for all irreducible
 complex representations of finite groups. $\blacksquare$
