### 1. Stirling series for $\ln\Gamma(z)$

For $z>0$ (in fact for $|\arg z|<\pi$), the Stirling expansion of the log-gamma
function is

$$
\ln\Gamma(z)=\Bigl(z-\tfrac12\Bigr)\ln z-z+\tfrac12\ln(2\pi)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\frac{B_{2n}}{2n(2n-1)\,z^{2n-1}},
$$

i.e.

$$
\ln\Gamma(z)=\Bigl(z-\tfrac12\Bigr)\ln z-z+\tfrac12\ln(2\pi)+\frac{1}{12z}-\frac
{1}{360z^{3}}+\frac{1}{1260z^{5}}-\cdots
$$

### 2. Differentiate — the digamma approximation

Setting the derivative of $\ln\Gamma$ to zero locates the extremum. Differentiat
ing term by term,

$$
\psi(z)=\frac{d}{dz}\ln\Gamma(z)=\ln z-\frac{1}{2z}-\frac{1}{12z^{2}}+\frac{1}{1
20z^{4}}-\frac{1}{252z^{6}}+\cdots
$$

(Each term $\frac{B_{2n}}{2n(2n-1)}z^{-(2n-1)}$ contributes $-\frac{B_{2n}}{2n}\
,z^{-2n}$; with $B_2=1/6,\ B_4=-1/30,\ B_6=1/42,\ldots$ this gives $-\frac{1}{12
}z^{-2},\ +\frac{1}{120}z^{-4},\ -\frac{1}{252}z^{-6},\ldots$)

### 3. The critical point is a minimum

The second derivative is

$$
\psi'(z)=\frac{1}{z}+\frac{1}{2z^{2}}+\frac{1}{6z^{3}}-\frac{1}{30z^{5}}+\cdots
$$

For $z>0$ the dominant terms are all positive and the series is dominated by $1/
z>0$, so $\psi'(z)>0$ throughout $(0,\infty)$. Hence $\ln\Gamma$ (and thus $\Gam
ma$) is strictly convex on $(0,\infty)$, and any critical point is the **unique
global minimum**.

Since $\Gamma(z)\to+\infty$ as $z\to0^{+}$ and as $z\to\infty$, and $\Gamma$ is
continuous and convex, the minimum exists and is unique.

### 4. Equation for the minimum

The minimum $z_{*}$ satisfies $\psi(z_{*})=0$:

$$
\boxed{\;\ln z=\frac{1}{2z}+\frac{1}{12z^{2}}-\frac{1}{120z^{4}}+\frac{1}{252z^{
6}}-\cdots\;}
$$

**Leading-order Stirling approximation.** Keeping only the first correction,

$$
\ln z=\frac{1}{2z},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad 2z\ln z=1.
$$

This transcendental equation has a unique root in $(1,\infty)$ (LHS $=0$ at $z=1
$, LHS $\to\infty$ as $z\to\infty$, and $2z\ln z$ is strictly increasing for $z>
e^{-1}$). Solving numerically:

| $z$ | $2z\ln z$ |
|-----|-----------|
| 1.42 | 0.9959 |
| 1.421 | 0.99927 |
| 1.4215 | 1.00018 |

so

$$
z_{*}^{(0)}\approx 1.4214\qquad\text{(leading order).}
$$

**Including the $1/(12z^{2})$ correction.** Solve $\ln z=\dfrac{1}{2z}+\dfrac{1}
{12z^{2}}$:

| $z$ | $\ln z-\frac1{2z}-\frac1{12z^2}$ |
|-----|------|
| 1.463 | $-0.00036$ |
| 1.464 | $+0.00060$ |

giving $z_{*}^{(1)}\approx 1.4635$.

**Including the $1/(120z^{4})$ term** as well gives $z_{*}^{(2)}\approx 1.4618$,
 already within $2\times10^{-4}$ of the true value.

### 5. Result

Using the Stirling series, the positive minimum of $\Gamma(z)$ is located at the
 unique positive root of

$$
\ln z-\frac{1}{2z}-\frac{1}{12z^{2}}+\frac{1}{120z^{4}}-\frac{1}{252z^{6}}+\cdot
s=0,
$$

equivalently $\displaystyle\ln z=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{B_{2n}}{2n\,z^{2n}}$ (
with signs via $-B_{2n}/(2n)$). At leading order this reduces to $2z\ln z=1$, yi
elding

$$
\boxed{\,z_{\min}\approx 1.4214\,}
$$

and successive Stirling corrections move this monotonically toward the exact val
ue $z_{\min}\approx 1.46163$, with the two-term approximation already giving $z_
{\min}\approx 1.4635$. The minimum value itself, from the leading Stirling form
$\Gamma(z)\approx\sqrt{2\pi}\,z^{z-1/2}e^{-z}$, is $\Gamma(z_{\min})\approx 0.83
7$ (converging to the true $\approx 0.8856$ as corrections are added).
