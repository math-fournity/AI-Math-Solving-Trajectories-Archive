## Solution

We determine the fundamental group of $X = (S^1 \times S^1)/(S^1 \times \{x\})$.

**Step 1: CW structure on the torus.**

Equip $T^2 = S^1 \times S^1$ with the standard CW structure, taking $x \in S^1$
as the basepoint (the unique 0-cell):

- **0-cell:** $e^0 = (x, x)$.
- **1-cells:** $e^1_a = S^1 \times \{x\} \setminus \{(x,x)\}$ (the "horizontal"
circle, first factor) and $e^1_b = \{x\} \times S^1 \setminus \{(x,x)\}$ (the "v
ertical" circle, second factor).
- **2-cell:** $e^2$, attached via the boundary word $a\, b\, a^{-1}\, b^{-1}$.

The subspace $A = S^1 \times \{x\} = e^0 \cup e^1_a$ is a **subcomplex** (it is
the union of the 0-cell and the 1-cell $e^1_a$), and $A$ is connected (it is a c
ircle).

**Step 2: Apply the quotient theorem for CW pairs.**

Since $(T^2, A)$ is a CW pair and $A$ is connected, the standard theorem (Hatche
r, *Algebraic Topology*, Prop. 1.26) gives:

$$\pi_1(T^2 / A) \cong \pi_1(T^2) / N$$

where $N$ is the normal subgroup generated by the image of the inclusion-induced
 homomorphism $\iota_* : \pi_1(A) \to \pi_1(T^2)$.

**Step 3: Compute the quotient group.**

We have $\pi_1(T^2) \cong \langle a, b \mid a b a^{-1} b^{-1} \rangle \cong \mat
hbb{Z}^2$, where $a$ and $b$ are the loops corresponding to the two 1-cells.

The subspace $A = S^1 \times \{x\}$ is the circle generating the class $a$, so t
he inclusion $\iota: A \hookrightarrow T^2$ sends the generator of $\pi_1(A) \co
ng \mathbb{Z}$ to $a \in \pi_1(T^2)$. Thus the image of $\iota_*$ is the cyclic
subgroup $\langle a \rangle$.

Since $\pi_1(T^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$ is abelian, every subgroup is normal, so $
N = \langle a \rangle$. Therefore:

$$\pi_1(T^2 / A) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2 / \langle a \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}.$$

**Step 4: Geometric confirmation via the quotient CW complex.**

Collapsing the subcomplex $A = e^0 \cup e^1_a$ to a single 0-cell, the quotient
$X = T^2/A$ inherits a CW structure:

- **0-cell:** the collapsed point $\ast$ (image of $A$).
- **1-cell:** the image of $e^1_b$, forming a loop at $\ast$ (call it $b$).
- **2-cell:** the image of $e^2$, attached via the image of the original attachi
ng map $a\, b\, a^{-1}\, b^{-1}$ under the quotient. Since $a$ is collapsed to t
he constant loop at $\ast$, the attaching word becomes $b\, b^{-1}$, which is nu
ll-homotopic.

A 2-cell attached via a null-homotopic map produces an $S^2$ summand. Hence:

$$X \cong S^1 \vee S^2.$$

Since $S^2$ is simply connected, by the Seifert–van Kampen theorem (or the wedge
 sum formula):

$$\pi_1(X) \cong \pi_1(S^1) * \pi_1(S^2) \cong \mathbb{Z} * \{1\} \cong \mathbb{
Z}.$$

**Conclusion.** Both the algebraic (quotient group) and geometric (CW quotient)
approaches agree:

$$\boxed{\pi_1\!\big((S^1 \times S^1)/(S^1 \times \{x\})\big) \cong \mathbb{Z}.}
$$
