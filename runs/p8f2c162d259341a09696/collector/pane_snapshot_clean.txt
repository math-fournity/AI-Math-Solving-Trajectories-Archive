**Claim: Yes.** The hypothesis implies that $\{\mathrm{id}, f, \ldots, f^p\}$ is
 linearly dependent in $\mathcal{L}(E)$.

Let $m_f$ be the minimal polynomial of $f$.

**Step 1 — Reformulation via cyclic subspaces.** For $x \in E$ let $Z(x,f) = \op
eratorname{span}\{x, f(x), f^2(x), \ldots\}$ be the $f$-cyclic subspace generate
d by $x$. We show the hypothesis is equivalent to
$$\dim Z(x,f) \le p \quad\text{for every } x \in E.$$

*($\Leftarrow$)* If $\dim Z(x,f) \le p$, then the $p+1$ vectors $x, f(x), \ldots
, f^p(x)$ all lie in a space of dimension $\le p$, hence are linearly dependent.

*($\Rightarrow$)* Suppose $x, f(x), \ldots, f^p(x)$ are linearly dependent. Set
$W = \operatorname{span}\{x, f(x), \ldots, f^{p-1}(x)\}$, so $\dim W \le p$. The
 dependence relation gives $f^p(x) \in W$, whence
$$f(W) = \operatorname{span}\{f(x), \ldots, f^p(x)\} \subseteq W,$$
so $W$ is $f$-invariant. Since $x \in W$ and $W$ is $f$-invariant, $Z(x,f) \subs
eteq W$, giving $\dim Z(x,f) \le p$.

**Step 2 — A vector attaining the minimal polynomial.** There exists $v \in E$ w
hose $f$-annihilator equals $m_f$, so that $\dim Z(v,f) = \deg m_f$.

This is the standard cyclic decomposition (rational canonical form): $E = Z(v_1,
f) \oplus \cdots \oplus Z(v_k,f)$ with invariant factors $m_1 \mid m_2 \mid \cdo
ts \mid m_k = m_f$. The generator $v_k$ of the last summand has annihilator $m_k
 = m_f$, hence $\dim Z(v_k,f) = \deg m_f$.

(Equivalently, without invoking the full structure theorem: by primary decomposi
tion $E = \bigoplus E_i$ with $m_f|_{E_i} = p_i^{e_i}$; on each primary componen
t one finds a vector with annihilator exactly $p_i^{e_i}$, and their sum has ann
ihilator $\operatorname{lcm}(p_i^{e_i}) = m_f$.)

**Step 3 — Bounding the minimal polynomial.** Applying the hypothesis (in the eq
uivalent form of Step 1) to the vector $v$ from Step 2:
$$\deg m_f = \dim Z(v,f) \le p.$$

**Step 4 — Conclusion.** Write $m_f(t) = c_0 + c_1 t + \cdots + c_d t^d$ with $d
 = \deg m_f \le p$ and not all $c_i$ zero. Then
$$c_0\,\mathrm{id} + c_1 f + \cdots + c_d f^d = 0.$$
Setting $a_i = c_i$ for $i \le d$ and $a_i = 0$ for $d < i \le p$ yields a nontr
ivial linear relation
$$a_0\,\mathrm{id} + a_1 f + \cdots + a_p f^p = 0.$$
Hence $\{\mathrm{id}, f, \ldots, f^p\}$ is linearly dependent in $\mathcal{L}(E)
$.

**Therefore the answer is yes.** $\blacksquare$
