## Solution

$$I = \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\log x}{x^2+2x+4}\,dx, \qquad J = \int_{0}^{\infty

**Step 1. Evaluate $J$.**

Complete the square: $x^2+2x+4 = (x+1)^2+3$, so
$$J = \int_0^\infty \frac{dx}{(x+1)^2+3} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left[\arctan\frac{
x+1}{\sqrt{3}}\right]_0^\infty = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi
}{6}\right) = \frac{\pi}{3\sqrt{3}}.$$

**Step 2. Relate $I$ to itself via the substitution $x = 4/t$.**

The constant term of the denominator is $4 = 2^2$, so use the reciprocal-type su
bstitution $x = 4/t$, $dx = -4/t^2\,dt$ (limits $0\to\infty$ become $\infty\to 0

$$\frac{1}{x^2+2x+4} = \frac{1}{16/t^2+8/t+4} = \frac{t^2}{4(t^2+2t+4)},$$

and the full integrand transforms as
$$\frac{\log x}{x^2+2x+4}\,dx \;\longrightarrow\; \frac{t^2}{4(t^2+2t+4)}\cdot\f
rac{4}{t^2}\,(\log 4 - \log t)\,dt = \frac{\log 4 - \log t}{t^2+2t+4}\,dt.$$

Therefore
$$I = \int_0^\infty \frac{\log 4 - \log t}{t^2+2t+4}\,dt = \log 4 \cdot J - I.$$

$$2I = \log 4 \cdot J = 2\log 2 \cdot J \implies I = \log 2 \cdot J = \log 2 \cd
ot \frac{\pi}{3\sqrt{3}} = \frac{\pi\ln 2}{\sqrt{27}}.$$

$$p + q = 2 + 27 = \boxed{29}.$$
