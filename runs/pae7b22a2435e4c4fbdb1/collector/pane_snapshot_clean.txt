Let $v(x) = \log|x|^2 = 2\log|x|$ and $u(x) = \varphi(x)$. Since $v$ has a logar
ithmic singularity at the origin, we excise a small ball $B_\varepsilon(0)$ and
apply Green's second identity on the domain $\Omega_\varepsilon = \mathbb{R}^2 \
setminus \overline{B_\varepsilon(0)}$:

$$\int_{\Omega_\varepsilon}\bigl(u\,\Delta v - v\,\Delta u\bigr)\,dx \;=\; \int_
{\partial\Omega_\varepsilon}\!\left(u\,\frac{\partial v}{\partial n} - v\,\frac{
\partial u}{\partial n}\right)dS.$$

**Interior contribution.** On $\Omega_\varepsilon$ (away from the origin) we hav
e $\Delta v = \Delta\log|x|^2 = 0$, so the left-hand side reduces to

$$-\int_{\Omega_\varepsilon} v\,\Delta\varphi\,dx.$$

**Outer boundary.** Since $\varphi\in C_c^\infty$, both $\varphi$ and $\nabla\va
rphi$ vanish outside a compact set, so the boundary at infinity contributes $0$.

**Inner boundary** $\partial B_\varepsilon$. The outward normal to $\Omega_\vare
psilon$ points toward the origin: $n = -x/|x|$. We compute

$$\nabla v = \frac{2x}{|x|^2}, \qquad \frac{\partial v}{\partial n} = \frac{2x}{
|x|^2}\cdot\Bigl(-\frac{x}{|x|}\Bigr) = -\frac{2}{|x|} = -\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\
 \text{on }|x|=\varepsilon.$$

The boundary integral splits into two terms:

1. $\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_\varepsilon}\varphi\,\frac{\partial v}{\partia
l n}\,dS = -\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\int_{\partial B_\varepsilon}\varphi\,dS = -\fr
ac{2}{\varepsilon}\cdot 2\pi\varepsilon\,\varphi(0) + O(\varepsilon) \;\longrigh
tarrow\; -4\pi\,\varphi(0).$

2. $\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_\varepsilon} v\,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partia
l n}\,dS = \int_{\partial B_\varepsilon}\log\varepsilon^2\cdot O(1)\,dS = 2\log\
varepsilon\cdot O(\varepsilon)\;\longrightarrow\; 0.$

Hence, as $\varepsilon\to 0$,

$$\int_{\partial\Omega_\varepsilon}\!\left(u\,\frac{\partial v}{\partial n} - v\
,\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\right)dS \;\longrightarrow\; -4\pi\,\varphi(0).$$

**Assembling.** Green's identity gives

$$-\int_{\Omega_\varepsilon}\log|x|^2\,\Delta\varphi\,dx \;=\; -4\pi\,\varphi(0)
 + o(1).$$

Since $\log|x|^2$ is locally integrable in $\mathbb{R}^2$ and $\Delta\varphi$ is
 smooth and compactly supported, the integral over $\Omega_\varepsilon$ converge
s to the integral over all of $\mathbb{R}^2$ as $\varepsilon\to 0$:

$$\boxed{\,I \;=\; \int_{\mathbb{R}^2}\Delta\varphi(x)\,\log|x|^2\,dx \;=\; 4\pi
\,\varphi(0)\,}.$$

This is consistent with the distributional identity $\Delta\log|x|^2 = 4\pi\,\de
lta_0$, which is precisely what the computation above establishes via Green's id
