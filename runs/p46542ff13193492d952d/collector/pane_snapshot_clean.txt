## Proof

For $z \in \mathbb{D}$, write $\frac{1+z}{z-1} = \frac{(1+z)(\bar z - 1)}{|z-1|^
2} = \frac{|z|^2 - 1 - 2i\,\text{Im}(z)}{|z-1|^2}$, so

$$\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\frac{1+z}{z-1}\right) = \frac{|z|^2 - 1}{|z-1|^2} <
0 \quad \text{for } |z|<1.$$

Thus $|f(z)| = \exp\!\left(\frac{|z|^2-1}{|z-1|^2}\right) < 1$ for all $z \in \m
athbb{D}$, and $f$ is a **bounded holomorphic** function on $\mathbb{D}$.

**Step 2: Boundary values of $f$.**

For $z = e^{i\theta}$, $\theta \in (0,2\pi)$:

$$\frac{1+e^{i\theta}}{e^{i\theta}-1} = \frac{2\cos(\theta/2)}{2i\sin(\theta/2)}
 = -i\cot(\theta/2).$$

So the a.e. boundary values of $f$ are $g(e^{i\theta}) = e^{-i\cot(\theta/2)}$,
which satisfies $|g| = 1$ a.e. on $\partial\mathbb{D}$.

As $\theta \to 0^+$, $\cot(\theta/2) \to +\infty$, so $g$ **oscillates infinitel
y**, passing through every value on the unit circle in any neighborhood of $\the
ta = 0$. In particular, $\operatorname{Re}(g(e^{i\theta})) = \cos(\cot(\theta/2)
)$ takes the values $+1$ and $-1$ at sequences $\theta_n, \theta_n' \to 0^+$ (na
mely where $\cot(\theta/2) = 2\pi k$ and $\cot(\theta/2) = (2k+1)\pi$ respective

**Step 3: Reduction to boundary $L^\infty$ approximation.**

Let $u$ be harmonic in $\mathbb{D}$ and continuous on $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$, w
ith boundary values $\phi = u|_{\partial\mathbb{D}} \in C(\partial\mathbb{D})$.
Then $h = f - u$ is bounded harmonic in $\mathbb{D}$. By the standard characteri
zation of bounded harmonic functions (Poisson integral of $L^\infty$ boundary da
ta):

$$\sup_{z \in \mathbb{D}} |h(z)| = \|h^*\|_{L^\infty(\partial\mathbb{D})},$$

where $h^* = g - \phi$ are the a.e. boundary values. Therefore:

$$\|f - u\|_{\mathbb{D}} = \|g - \phi\|_{L^\infty(\partial\mathbb{D})}.$$

**Step 4: Lower bound — $\|g - \phi\|_{L^\infty} \geq 1$ for every continuous $\
phi$.**

Let $a = \phi(1) \in \mathbb{C}$. Since $\phi$ is continuous, $\phi(e^{i\theta})
 \to a$ as $\theta \to 0^+$.

Take sequences $\theta_k = 2\arctan\!\bigl(\frac{1}{2\pi k}\bigr) \to 0^+$ and $
\theta_k' = 2\arctan\!\bigl(\frac{1}{(2k+1)\pi}\bigr) \to 0^+$. Then:

$$g(e^{i\theta_k}) = e^{-i \cdot 2\pi k} = 1, \qquad g(e^{i\theta_k'}) = e^{-i(2
k+1)\pi} = -1.$$

Since $g$ and $\phi$ are both continuous at $\theta_k, \theta_k' > 0$, the a.e.
bound $\|g-\phi\|_{L^\infty} \geq |g(e^{i\theta}) - \phi(e^{i\theta})|$ holds at
 these points. Thus:

$$\|g - \phi\|_{L^\infty} \geq |1 - \phi(e^{i\theta_k})| \quad \text{and} \quad
\|g - \phi\|_{L^\infty} \geq |-1 - \phi(e^{i\theta_k'})|.$$

Letting $k \to \infty$ (so $\theta_k, \theta_k' \to 0$ and $\phi(e^{i\theta_k}),
 \phi(e^{i\theta_k'}) \to a$):

$$\|g - \phi\|_{L^\infty} \geq |1 - a| \quad \text{and} \quad \|g - \phi\|_{L^\i
nfty} \geq |-1 - a|.$$

By the triangle inequality:

$$2 = |1 - (-1)| \leq |1 - a| + |a - (-1)| \leq 2\,\|g - \phi\|_{L^\infty}.$$

Therefore $\|g - \phi\|_{L^\infty} \geq 1$, and consequently $\|f - u\|_{\mathbb
{D}} \geq 1$ for every harmonic $u$ continuous on $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$.

**Step 5: The bound is sharp (infimum $= 1$).**

For any $\eta > 0$, define a continuous $\phi_\eta$ on $\partial\mathbb{D}$ as f
ollows (parametrizing by $\theta \in [0, 2\pi]$):

- $\phi_\eta(e^{i\theta}) = 0$ for $\theta \in [0, \eta/2] \cup [2\pi - \eta/2,
2\pi]$,
- $\phi_\eta(e^{i\theta}) = g(e^{i\theta})$ for $\theta \in [\eta, 2\pi - \eta]$

- On $[\eta/2, \eta]$: $\phi_\eta(e^{i\theta}) = t(\theta)\, g(e^{i\theta})$ whe
re $t$ increases continuously from $0$ to $1$,
- On $[2\pi - \eta, 2\pi - \eta/2]$: similarly with $t$ decreasing from $1$ to $
0$.

This $\phi_\eta$ is continuous on $\partial\mathbb{D}$ with $\phi_\eta(1) = 0$.
Then:

- On $(0, \eta/2)$: $|g - \phi_\eta| = |g| = 1$,
- On $[\eta/2, \eta]$: $|g - \phi_\eta| = |1 - t(\theta)| \cdot |g| = 1 - t(\the
ta) \leq 1$,
- On $[\eta, 2\pi - \eta]$: $|g - \phi_\eta| = 0$,
- On $[2\pi - \eta, 2\pi - \eta/2]$: $|g - \phi_\eta| \leq 1$,
- On $(2\pi - \eta/2, 2\pi)$: $|g - \phi_\eta| = 1$.

So $\|g - \phi_\eta\|_{L^\infty} = 1$. Taking $u_\eta = P[\phi_\eta]$ (the Poiss

\mathbb{D}}$, with $\|f - u_\eta\|_{\mathbb{D}} = 1$.

**Conclusion.**

$$\inf\bigl\{\|f - u\|_{\mathbb{D}} : u \text{ harmonic in } \mathbb{D},\; \text
{continuous on } \overline{\mathbb{D}}\bigr\} = 1 \neq 0.$$

The infimum is **not** zero. The function $f(z) = e^{(1+z)/(z-1)}$ **cannot** be
