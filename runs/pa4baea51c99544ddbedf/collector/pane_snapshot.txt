## Solution

**Setup.** We have 5 racks, each with 4 slots, giving 20 distinct drives and 20
distinct slots. The 4 drives originally in rack $i$ form "group $i$." The constr
aint requires that no new rack contains two drives from the same original group.

**Counting via a 0-1 matrix.** Let $M(i,j)$ = number of drives from original rac
k $i$ placed in new rack $j$. Since each new rack can hold **at most one** drive
 from each original group (else two same-group drives share a rack), every entry
 is 0 or 1. The constraints are:

- **Row sums = 4**: each original group's 4 drives must all be placed.
- **Column sums = 4**: each new rack has exactly 4 slots to fill.

Since $M$ is $5 \times 5$ with all entries in $\{0,1\}$ and every row/column sum
s to 4, each row has **exactly one** 0 and each column has **exactly one** 0. Th
e positions of the zeros therefore form a **permutation matrix**, corresponding
to a permutation $\sigma \in S_5$: $M(i,j)=0 \iff j = \sigma(i)$.

Equivalently, $M = J - P_\sigma$, where $J$ is the all-ones matrix and $P_\sigma
$ is the permutation matrix of $\sigma$.

**Counting arrangements for a fixed $\sigma$.** Given $\sigma$, the matrix $M$ i
s determined, telling us *how many* drives go from each original rack to each ne
w rack (always 0 or 1). We count in two independent stages:

**Stage 1 — Drive-to-rack assignment.** For each original rack $i$, its 4 distin
ct drives must be bijected onto the 4 new racks $\{1,\dots,5\}\setminus\{\sigma(
i)\}$, one drive each. This gives $4!$ choices per rack. These choices are indep
endent across racks (column sums are automatically satisfied: new rack $j$ recei
ves one drive from each $i \ne \sigma^{-1}(j)$, exactly 4 drives). Total: $(4!)^
5$.

**Stage 2 — Slot assignment within each rack.** Each new rack $j$ receives 4 dis
tinct drives, which must be arranged into its 4 distinct slots: $4!$ ways per ra
ck, independent. Total: $(4!)^5$.

Combined for a fixed $\sigma$: $(4!)^5 \times (4!)^5 = (4!)^{10}$.

**Summing over all $\sigma$.** The permutation $\sigma$ ranges over all $5!$ ele
ments of $S_5$, each giving a distinct, valid pattern. Therefore:

$$\text{Total} = 5! \times (4!)^{10}.$$

**Prime factorization.**

$$5! = 120 = 2^3 \cdot 3 \cdot 5, \qquad 4! = 24 = 2^3 \cdot 3.$$

$$(4!)^{10} = (2^3 \cdot 3)^{10} = 2^{30} \cdot 3^{10}.$$

$$\text{Total} = 2^3 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 2^{30} \cdot 3^{10} = 2^{33} \cdot 3^
{11} \cdot 5^{1}.$$

So $a = 33$, $b = 11$, $c = 1$.

$$a + b + c = 33 + 11 + 1 = \boxed{45}.$$
