## Solution

**Setup.** Let
- A = digit sum of 7^(7^(7^7))
- B = digit sum of A
- C = digit sum of B

We want the digit sum of C.

**Key fact.** For any positive integer n, (digit sum of n) ≡ n (mod 9). Iteratin
g, the digital root equals n mod 9 (with 9 replacing 0 when 9 | n).

**Step 1: Compute 7^(7^(7^7)) mod 9.**

The order of 7 modulo 9 is 3, since 7¹≡7, 7²≡4, 7³≡1 (mod 9).

So we need the exponent 7^(7^7) mod 3. Since 7 ≡ 1 (mod 3), we get 7^(7^7) ≡ 1^(
7^7) ≡ 1 (mod 3).

Therefore 7^(7^(7^7)) ≡ 7¹ ≡ **7 (mod 9)**.

Since 7 ≢ 0 (mod 9), the digital root is 7.

**Step 2: Bound the sizes.**

log₁₀(7^(7^(7^7))) = 7^(7^7)·log₁₀7 = 7^823543 · 0.8451 ≈ 6.96 × 10⁵.

So 7^(7^(7^7)) has roughly 696,000 digits, giving A ≤ 9·696,000 ≈ 6.26 × 10⁶ (at
 most 7 digits). Hence B ≤ 9·7 = 63 (at most 2 digits), and C ≤ 6+9 = 15.

**Step 3: Determine C.**

By the digit-sum congruence:
- A ≡ 7 (mod 9)
- B ≡ A ≡ 7 (mod 9)
- C ≡ B ≡ 7 (mod 9)

With B ≤ 63 and B ≡ 7 (mod 9), the possible values of B are:
{7, 16, 25, 34, 43, 52, 61}.

Computing C = digit sum of B for each:
- 7 → 7
- 16 → 1+6 = 7
- 25 → 2+5 = 7
- 34 → 3+4 = 7
- 43 → 4+3 = 7
- 52 → 5+2 = 7
- 61 → 6+1 = 7

In **every** case, C = 7.

**Step 4: Digit sum of C.**

Since C = 7 is a single digit, the sum of the digits of C is **7**.
