The bilinear form has matrix $A$ with entries $A_{ij} = 1 - \delta_{ij}$, i.e. $
A = J - I$, where $J$ is the $n \times n$ all-ones matrix and $I$ is the identit
y.

**Eigenvalues of $J$:** The all-ones matrix $J$ has:
- eigenvalue $n$ with eigenvector $\mathbf{1} = (1,1,\dots,1)$,
- eigenvalue $0$ with multiplicity $n-1$ (eigenspace $= \mathbf{1}^{\perp}$).

**Eigenvalues of $A = J - I$:** Subtracting $I$ shifts every eigenvalue down by
$1$:
- from $n$: eigenvalue $n - 1$, eigenvector $\mathbf{1}$,
- from $0$ (multiplicity $n-1$): eigenvalue $-1$, multiplicity $n-1$.

So the spectrum of $A$ is $\{n-1,\; \underbrace{-1,\dots,-1}_{n-1}\}$.

**For $n > 1$:** $n - 1 \geq 1 > 0$, so $n-1$ is strictly positive, and $-1$ is
strictly negative. No eigenvalue is zero. Hence in an orthogonal basis diagonali
zing the form:
- exactly **1** vector squares to a positive value,
- exactly $n-1$ vectors square to a negative value,
- **0** vectors square to zero.

**Verification (small cases):**
- $n=2$: $A = \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$, eigenvalues $1, -1$. Signat
ure $(1,1,0)$. ✓
- $n=3$: $A = \begin{pmatrix}0&1&1\\1&0&1\\1&1&0\end{pmatrix}$, eigenvalues $2,
-1, -1$. Signature $(1,2,0)$. ✓

---

**The signature for $n > 1$ is $(1,\; n-1,\; 0)$: one positive, $n-1$ negative,
and zero null directions.**
