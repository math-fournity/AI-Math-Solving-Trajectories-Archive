## Proof

We determine $I(a) = \int_0^\infty \frac{1-e^{-ax}}{xe^x}\,dx$ by differentiatin
g under the integral sign with respect to the parameter $a$.

**Step 1. Differentiate under the integral sign.**

For $a > -1$,
$$
I'(a) = \int_0^\infty \frac{\partial}{\partial a}\!\left(\frac{1-e^{-ax}}{xe^x}\
right) dx
      = \int_0^\infty \frac{xe^{-ax}}{xe^x}\,dx
      = \int_0^\infty e^{-(a+1)x}\,dx.
$$

**Justification of the interchange.** Near $x=0$, the integrand $\frac{1-e^{-ax}
}{xe^x}\to a$ (finite, since $1-e^{-ax}\sim ax$), so it is integrable. At $x\to\
infty$, $\left|\frac{1-e^{-ax}}{xe^x}\right|\le \frac{1}{xe^x}$, integrable. The
 differentiated integrand $e^{-(a+1)x}$ is integrable on $(0,\infty)$ for every
$a>-1$, and is continuous in $a$ and dominated locally uniformly in $a$ on any c
ompact subinterval of $(-1,\infty)$. Hence differentiation under the integral si
gn is valid for $a>-1$.

**Step 2. Evaluate $I'(a)$.**

For $a>-1$ (so that $a+1>0$),
$$
I'(a) = \int_0^\infty e^{-(a+1)x}\,dx
      = \left[-\frac{e^{-(a+1)x}}{a+1}\right]_0^\infty
      = \frac{1}{a+1}.
$$

**Step 3. Integrate to recover $I(a)$.**

$$
I(a) = \int \frac{da}{a+1} = \ln(a+1) + C.
$$

**Step 4. Determine the constant.**

At $a=0$,
$$
I(0) = \int_0^\infty \frac{1-e^{0}}{xe^x}\,dx = \int_0^\infty 0\,dx = 0.
$$
Thus $0 = \ln(1) + C = C$, giving $C=0$.

**Conclusion.**

$$
\boxed{I(a) = \ln(a+1)}, \qquad a > -1.
$$
