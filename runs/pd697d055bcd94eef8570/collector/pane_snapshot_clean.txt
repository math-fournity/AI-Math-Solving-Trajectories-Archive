Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a non-semisimple Lie algebr
a can possess an invariant inner product (a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear fo
rm $B$ satisfying the ad-invariance condition $B([X,Y],Z) = B(X,[Y,Z])$).

## Answer: **Yes**

## Proof

**Key distinction.** Semisimplicity of a Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ is equivalen
t to the non-degeneracy of its *Killing form* $\kappa(X,Y) = \mathrm{tr}(\mathrm
{ad}_X \circ \mathrm{ad}_Y)$ (Cartan's criterion). However, the question asks ab
out *an* invariant inner product — not specifically the Killing form. These are
different notions: the existence of *some* non-degenerate ad-invariant symmetric
 bilinear form is strictly weaker than semisimplicity.

**Example 1 (trivial): the 1-dimensional abelian algebra.**

Let $\mathfrak{g} = \mathbb{R}$ be the 1-dimensional abelian Lie algebra, so $[a
,b]=0$ for all $a,b$. Define $B(a,b) = ab$. This is a positive definite (hence n
on-degenerate) symmetric bilinear form. The ad-invariance condition $B([a,b],c)
= B(a,[b,c])$ reduces to $B(0,c) = B(a,0)$, i.e., $0 = 0$, which holds trivially
.

Yet $\mathfrak{g} = \mathbb{R}$ is **not semisimple**: it is abelian, so its sol
vable radical is $\mathfrak{g}$ itself, whereas a semisimple Lie algebra must ha
ve trivial radical. $\square$

**Example 2 (non-abelian, reductive): $\mathfrak{u}(1) \oplus \mathfrak{su}(2)$.
**

This Lie algebra is reductive but **not semisimple** because it has a non-trivia
l center $\mathfrak{u}(1) \cong \mathbb{R}$. (A semisimple Lie algebra has zero
center.)

Define $B = B_{\mathfrak{u}(1)} \oplus (-\kappa_{\mathfrak{su}(2)})$, where $B_{
\mathfrak{u}(1)}$ is any positive definite form on the abelian factor and $\kapp
a_{\mathfrak{su}(2)}$ is the Killing form of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ (which is negati
ve definite, so $-\kappa$ is positive definite). Then:

- $B$ is positive definite, hence non-degenerate.
- $B$ is ad-invariant: on the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ factor this follows from the ad
-invariance of the Killing form on any Lie algebra; on the $\mathfrak{u}(1)$ fac
tor it holds trivially (all brackets vanish); and cross-terms vanish because the
 two factors commute (direct sum of ideals).

Thus $\mathfrak{u}(1) \oplus \mathfrak{su}(2)$ is a non-semisimple Lie algebra w
ith a positive definite ad-invariant inner product. $\square$

**Example 3 (non-reductive, via double extension).**

One can even construct *non-reductive* (hence non-semisimple) examples. Using th
e Medina–Revoy double extension: start with the abelian algebra $\mathbb{R}^2$ w
ith standard inner product $B_0$, and the skew-symmetric derivation $D = \begin{
pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$. The resulting 4-dimensional Lie algebra $\tilde
{\mathfrak{g}} = \mathbb{R}e \oplus \mathbb{R}a_1 \oplus \mathbb{R}a_2 \oplus \m
athbb{R}f$ has brackets:
$$[e,a_1]=a_2,\quad [e,a_2]=-a_1,\quad [a_1,a_2]=f,\quad f\text{ central},$$
and admits the non-degenerate ad-invariant symmetric bilinear form:
$$\tilde{B}(a_i,a_j)=\delta_{ij},\quad \tilde{B}(e,f)=1,\quad \text{all other pa
irings }0.$$
This algebra has non-trivial center $\mathbb{R}f$ and is not even reductive, yet
 it carries a non-degenerate invariant inner product. $\square$

**Conclusion.** A non-semisimple Lie algebra **can** have an invariant inner pro
duct. The confusion to avoid is conflating "the Killing form is non-degenerate"
(which characterizes semisimplicity) with "there exists some non-degenerate ad-i
nvariant symmetric bilinear form" (which is a strictly weaker condition satisfie
d by many non-semisimple algebras, including all abelian algebras, all reductive
 algebras with center, and even certain non-reductive algebras).
