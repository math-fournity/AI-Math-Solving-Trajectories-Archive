Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $\mathbb{Q}_p^n \cong \math
bb{Q}_p^m$ (homeomorphic) forces $n = m$.

**Answer: No.** For all $n, m \geq 1$, the spaces $\mathbb{Q}_p^n$ and $\mathbb{
Q}_p^m$ are homeomorphic.

## Proof

**Step 1: $\mathbb{Z}_p^n$ is homeomorphic to the Cantor set $C$ for every $n \g
eq 1$.**

We use **Brouwer's characterization theorem** (1910): *Every nonempty, compact,
Hausdorff, perfect (no isolated points), totally disconnected, metrizable space
is homeomorphic to the Cantor set $C = \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$.*

We verify the hypotheses for $\mathbb{Z}_p^n$:

- **Compact:** $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is the closed unit ball $\{x \in \mathbb{Q}_p : |x
|_p \leq 1\}$, hence compact. A finite product of compact spaces is compact. ✓
- **Metrizable:** $\mathbb{Q}_p^n$ carries the $p$-adic metric (e.g., $d(x,y) =
\max_i |x_i - y_i|_p$), so $\mathbb{Z}_p^n$ is metrizable. ✓
- **Totally disconnected:** $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is totally disconnected (the $p$-adic
 topology has a basis of clopen balls). A finite product of totally disconnected
 spaces is totally disconnected (equivalently, the product of zero-dimensional s
paces is zero-dimensional). ✓
- **Perfect (no isolated points):** Every point $a \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ has neighbo
rhoods $a + p^k\mathbb{Z}_p$ (for $k \geq 1$), each containing points distinct f
rom $a$ (e.g., $a + p^k$). For the product $\mathbb{Z}_p^n$: a basic open set ar
ound any point is a product of open sets in each factor, each containing at leas
t two points, so the product contains other points. ✓

By Brouwer's theorem, $\mathbb{Z}_p^n \cong C$ for all $n \geq 1$. (In particula
r, $C^n \cong C$, which is also a direct consequence.)

**Step 2: $\mathbb{Q}_p^n$ is a countable disjoint union of clopen copies of $\m
athbb{Z}_p^n$.**

The subgroup $\mathbb{Z}_p^n \leq \mathbb{Q}_p^n$ is **clopen**: it is the close
d ball $\{x : \max_i |x_i|_p \leq 1\}$, which is closed; it is also open since $
\{x : \max_i |x_i|_p \leq 1\} = \{x : \max_i |x_i|_p < p\}$ (the $p$-adic absolu
te value takes values in $\{p^k : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$, so $\leq 1 \iff < p$).

The quotient $\mathbb{Q}_p^n / \mathbb{Z}_p^n \cong (\mathbb{Q}_p / \mathbb{Z}_p
)^n$ is **countable**: $\mathbb{Q}_p / \mathbb{Z}_p \cong \mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$
(the Prüfer $p$-group) is countable, since each element of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ has a
unique representative modulo $\mathbb{Z}_p$ given by its principal part $\sum_{k
=N}^{-1} a_k p^k$ (a finite sum), and there are countably many such.

The cosets $\{a + \mathbb{Z}_p^n : a \in \mathbb{Q}_p^n / \mathbb{Z}_p^n\}$ form
 a partition of $\mathbb{Q}_p^n$ into:
- **countably many** pieces (since the quotient is countably infinite),
- each **clopen** (translates of the clopen subgroup $\mathbb{Z}_p^n$),
- each **homeomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_p^n \cong C$** (via translation).

Therefore:
$$\mathbb{Q}_p^n \;\cong\; \bigsqcup_{\mathbb{N}} C \;\cong\; \mathbb{N} \times
C.$$

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

The homeomorphism type $\mathbb{N} \times C$ (a countably infinite disjoint unio
n of Cantor sets) is **independent of $n$**. Hence for all $n, m \geq 1$:
$$\mathbb{Q}_p^n \;\cong\; \mathbb{N} \times C \;\cong\; \mathbb{Q}_p^m.$$

So $\mathbb{Q}_p^n$ and $\mathbb{Q}_p^m$ are homeomorphic **regardless** of whet
her $n = m$. The answer to the question is **no**, $n = m$ is not forced.

**Remark.** This stands in sharp contrast with $\mathbb{R}^n$, where invariance
of domain forces $n = m$. The key difference is that $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is totally d
isconnected: all local and global topological invariants (covering dimension, Ca
ntor–Bendixson rank, one-point compactification, local structure) collapse to th
ose of the Cantor set, leaving no invariant to distinguish the "dimension." The
algebraic/group-theoretic dimension $n$ is simply not a topological invariant fo
r $p$-adic spaces.
