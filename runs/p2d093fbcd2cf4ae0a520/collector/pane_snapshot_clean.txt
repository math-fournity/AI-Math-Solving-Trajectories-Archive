# Evaluation of the Limit and Fréchet Differentiability

## Step 1: Bounding $|\operatorname{tr}(V^2)|$

We use two standard facts about the operator (spectral) norm $\|\cdot\|_{op}$ fo
r $n\times n$ real matrices:

1. **Trace bound:** For any $n\times n$ matrix $A$, $|\operatorname{tr}(A)|\le n
\,\|A\|_{op}$.
   (Reason: $\operatorname{tr}(A)=\sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i$ where $\lambda_i$ are
the eigenvalues, each satisfying $|\lambda_i|\le\|A\|_{op}$.)

2. **Submultiplicativity:** $\|AB\|_{op}\le\|A\|_{op}\|B\|_{op}$, so in particul
ar $\|V^2\|_{op}\le\|V\|_{op}^2$.

Combining these with $A=V^2$:
$$|\operatorname{tr}(V^2)|\;\le\; n\,\|V^2\|_{op}\;\le\; n\,\|V\|_{op}^2.$$

## Step 2: The Squeeze

For $V\neq 0$, dividing by $\|V\|_{op}>0$:
$$0\;\le\;\frac{|\operatorname{tr}(V^2)|}{\|V\|_{op}}\;\le\; n\,\|V\|_{op}.$$

As $V\to \mathbf{0}^{\,n\times n}$ (i.e. $\|V\|_{op}\to 0$), the right-hand side
 $n\|V\|_{op}\to 0$. By the squeeze theorem:

$$\boxed{\lim_{V\to \mathbf{0}^{\,n\times n}}\frac{|\operatorname{tr}(V^2)|}{\|V
\|_{op}}=0.}$$

## Step 3: Application — Fréchet Differentiability of $f(V)=\operatorname{tr}(V^
2)$ at the Origin

Consider $f:\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\to\mathbb{R}$, $f(V)=\operatorname{tr}(V^2)$,
 with the domain equipped with the operator norm. We test Fréchet differentiabil
ity at $V=0$.

- **Value at the point:** $f(0)=\operatorname{tr}(0)=0$.

- **Candidate derivative.** The directional (Gâteaux) derivative at $0$ in direc
tion $H$ is
$$\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{f(tH)-f(0)}{t}=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{t^2\operatorname{tr}(H^2)
}{t}=\lim_{t\to 0}t\,\operatorname{tr}(H^2)=0.$$
Since this vanishes for every $H$, the only candidate for the Fréchet derivative
 is the **zero linear map** $L\equiv 0$.

- **Fréchet condition.** $f$ is Fréchet differentiable at $0$ with derivative $L
$ iff
$$\lim_{V\to 0}\frac{|f(V)-f(0)-L(V)|}{\|V\|_{op}}=0.$$
Here $L(V)=0$ and $f(0)=0$, so the condition reduces exactly to
$$\lim_{V\to 0}\frac{|\operatorname{tr}(V^2)|}{\|V\|_{op}}=0,$$
which we established in Step 2.

## Conclusion

The limit equals **$0$**. Consequently, the function $f(V)=\operatorname{tr}(V^2
)$ is **Fréchet differentiable at $V=0$**, with Fréchet derivative $Df(0)=0$ (th
e zero linear map). The quadratic growth of $\operatorname{tr}(V^2)=O(\|V\|_{op}
^2)$ is exactly what makes the remainder $o(\|V\|_{op})$, satisfying the definit
ion of Fréchet differentiability.
