## Proof

We work in the quotient algebra $A/I_2$. Let $q: A \to A/I_2$ denote the quotien
t *-homomorphism, and write $\bar{x} = q(x)$ for the class of $x$.

**Step 1: Compute $\bar{a}_1^2$.**

Since $a_1^2 + a_2^2 = 1$, we have $a_1^2 = 1 - a_2^2$. Applying $q$:

$$\bar{a}_1^2 = \overline{a_1^2} = \overline{1 - a_2^2} = \bar{1} - \overline{a_
2^2}.$$

Since $a_2 \in I_2$, we have $\bar{a}_2 = 0$, hence $\overline{a_2^2} = \bar{a}_
2^2 = 0$. Therefore:

$$\bar{a}_1^2 = \bar{1} = 1_{A/I_2}.$$

**Step 2: $\bar{a}_1$ is positive in $A/I_2$.**

The quotient map $q$ is a surjective *-homomorphism, and surjective *-homomorphi
sms preserve positivity (a standard result: $q(x^*x) = q(x)^*q(x) \geq 0$, and e
very positive element is of the form $x^*x$). Since $a_1 \geq 0$ in $A$, we get
$\bar{a}_1 \geq 0$ in $A/I_2$.

**Step 3: A positive element whose square is the unit equals the unit.**

We have $\bar{a}_1 \geq 0$ and $\bar{a}_1^2 = 1_{A/I_2}$. Consider the spectrum
$\sigma(\bar{a}_1) \subseteq [0, \infty)$ (positivity). By the spectral mapping
theorem,

$$\sigma(\bar{a}_1^2) = \{\lambda^2 : \lambda \in \sigma(\bar{a}_1)\}.$$

Since $\bar{a}_1^2 = 1_{A/I_2}$, we have $\sigma(\bar{a}_1^2) = \{1\}$. Thus $\l
ambda^2 = 1$ for every $\lambda \in \sigma(\bar{a}_1)$. Combined with $\lambda \
geq 0$, this forces $\lambda = 1$ for all $\lambda \in \sigma(\bar{a}_1)$, i.e.,
 $\sigma(\bar{a}_1) = \{1\}$.

By the continuous functional calculus, $\bar{a}_1 = f(\bar{a}_1)$ where $f$ is t
he identity on $\sigma(\bar{a}_1) = \{1\}$. The constant function $1$ agrees wit
h the identity function on $\{1\}$, so:

$$\bar{a}_1 = 1 \cdot 1_{A/I_2} = 1_{A/I_2}.$$

**Conclusion.**

$\bar{a}_1 = \bar{1}$ in $A/I_2$, i.e., $a_1 - 1 \in I_2$. Therefore $a_1$ and $
1$ represent the same class in $A/I_2$. $\blacksquare$
