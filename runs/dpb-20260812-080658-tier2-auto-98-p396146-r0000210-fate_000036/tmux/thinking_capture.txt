Proof

We prove the equivalence. Throughout, $R$ denotes an integral domain
(commutative, with $1 \neq 0$).

Definitions recalled.

  • $R$ is a P.I.D. if every ideal of $R$ is principal.
  • $R$ is a U.F.D. if every nonzero nonunit element is a product of irreducible
  elements, uniquely up to order and associates.
  • $R$ is a Bezout domain if every finitely generated ideal of $R$ is principal
  (equivalently, for all $a,b\in R$, the ideal $(a,b)$ is principal, i.e., $
  \exists, x,y\in R$ with $ax+by=d$ where $(a,b)=(d)$).

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

($\Rightarrow$) P.I.D. $\Longrightarrow$ U.F.D. and Bezout domain.

Bezout. If $R$ is a P.I.D., every ideal is principal, so in particular every
finitely generated ideal is principal. Hence $R$ is a Bezout domain.

U.F.D. This is the classical theorem that every P.I.D. is a U.F.D. We recall the
argument for completeness.

Existence of factorization. In a P.I.D. every ideal is finitely generated
(indeed principal), so $R$ is Noetherian. Thus the ascending chain condition on
principal ideals holds: there is no infinite strictly ascending chain
$(a_1)\subsetneq(a_2)\subsetneq\cdots$. Given any nonzero nonunit $a$, if $a$ is
irreducible we are done; otherwise $a=bc$ with $b,c$ nonunits, giving
$(a)\subsetneq(b)$ and $(a)\subsetneq(c)$. Iterating, the ACC on principal
ideals forces termination, yielding a factorization into irreducibles.

Uniqueness. It suffices to show every irreducible is prime. Let $p$ be
irreducible in a P.I.D. Then $(p)$ is maximal among proper principal ideals;
since every ideal is principal, $(p)$ is maximal, hence prime. Therefore $p$ is
a prime element, and factorization is unique. ∎

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

($\Leftarrow$) U.F.D. and Bezout domain $\Longrightarrow$ P.I.D.

Assume $R$ is a U.F.D. and a Bezout domain. We show every ideal is principal.

Key lemma (ACC on principal ideals in a U.F.D.). In a U.F.D. there is no
infinite strictly ascending chain of principal ideals.

Proof of lemma. Suppose $(c_1)\subsetneq(c_2)\subsetneq(c_3)\subsetneq\cdots$.
The strict inclusion $(c_i)\subsetneq(c_{i+1})$ means $c_{i+1}\mid c_i$ and
$c_{i+1}\not\sim c_i$ (not associate), so $c_i = c_{i+1},u_i$ where $u_i$ is a
nonunit. Let $\ell(x)$ denote the number of irreducible factors of a nonzero
nonunit $x$, counted with multiplicity (well-defined by unique factorization).
Then $$\ell(c_i)=\ell(c_{i+1})+\ell(u_i)\geq \ell(c_{i+1})+1,$$ so $
\ell(c_1)>\ell(c_2)>\ell(c_3)>\cdots$ is a strictly decreasing sequence of
nonnegative integers — impossible to continue infinitely. ∎
