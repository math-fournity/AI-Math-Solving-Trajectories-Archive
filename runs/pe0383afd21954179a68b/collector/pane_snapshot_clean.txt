# Turán's Theorem

**Problem.** What is the maximum number of edges in an $r$-clique-free graph on
$n$ vertices?

## Answer

The maximum is achieved by the **Turán graph** $T(n, r-1)$: the complete $(r-1)$
-partite graph on $n$ vertices with part-sizes as equal as possible. Writing $n
= (r-1)q + s$ with $0 \le s < r-1$, the value is

$$\operatorname{ex}(n, K_r) \;=\; \frac{1}{2}\Bigl(n^2 - s\,(q+1)^2 - (r-1-s)\,q
^2\Bigr).$$

When $(r-1)\mid n$ this simplifies to $\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{r-1}\right)
\frac{n^2}{2}$.

---

## Proof

We prove by induction on $n$ that $\operatorname{ex}(n,K_r)=e(T(n,r-1))$ and tha
t $T(n,r-1)$ is the unique extremal graph.

**Base case** $n \le r-1$: The complete graph $K_n$ is $K_r$-free and equals $T(
n,r-1)$ (each vertex in its own part, remaining parts empty), with $\binom{n}{2}
$ edges. ✓

**Inductive step** ($n \ge r$). Let $G$ be a $K_r$-free graph on $n$ vertices wi
th $e(G)=\operatorname{ex}(n,K_r)$.

**Claim 1.** $G$ contains a clique $S=\{v_1,\dots,v_{r-1}\}$ of size $r-1$.

*Proof.* If $G$ were $K_{r-1}$-free, then by induction $e(G)\le e(T(n,r-2))$. Bu
t $T(n,r-1)$ is $(r-1)$-partite hence $K_r$-free, and $e(T(n,r-1))>e(T(n,r-2))$
(a complete multipartite graph gains edges when a part is split). This contradic
ts the maximality of $G$. $\square$

**Claim 2 (Edge bound).** $e(G)\le e(T(n,r-1))$.

*Proof.* Partition the edges of $G$ into three disjoint classes:

$$e(G) = e(G[S]) + e(S,\,V\setminus S) + e(G[V\setminus S]).$$

- **Edges within $S$:** $S$ is a clique, so $e(G[S])=\binom{r-1}{2}$.
- **Edges between $S$ and $V\setminus S$:** Every $w\notin S$ is adjacent to at
most $r-2$ vertices of $S$ (otherwise $S\cup\{w\}$ is a $K_r$). Hence $e(S,V\set
minus S)\le (r-2)(n-r+1)$.
- **Edges within $V\setminus S$:** $G[V\setminus S]$ is $K_r$-free, so by induct
ion $e(G[V\setminus S])\le e(T(n-r+1,\,r-1))$.

Therefore
$$e(G)\le \binom{r-1}{2}+(r-2)(n-r+1)+e(T(n-r+1,r-1)).$$

**Key identity.** We verify
$$\binom{r-1}{2}+(r-2)(n-r+1)+e(T(n-r+1,r-1))=e(T(n,r-1)).$$

Write $n=(r-1)q+s$, $0\le s<r-1$. Then $T(n,r-1)$ has $s$ parts of size $q+1$ an
d $r-1-s$ parts of size $q$, while $T(n-r+1,r-1)$ has $s$ parts of size $q$ and
$r-1-s$ parts of size $q-1$. A direct computation gives

$$e(T(n,r-1))-e(T(n-r+1,r-1))=\frac{(r-2)(2n-r+1)}{2},$$

and independently

$$\binom{r-1}{2}+(r-2)(n-r+1)=(r-2)\!\left(\frac{r-1}{2}+n-r+1\right)=\frac{(r-2
)(2n-r+1)}{2}.$$

These are equal, confirming the identity. Thus $e(G)\le e(T(n,r-1))$.

Since $T(n,r-1)$ is $(r-1)$-partite and therefore $K_r$-free, this bound is **ti
ght**: $\operatorname{ex}(n,K_r)=e(T(n,r-1))$. $\square$

**Claim 3 (Uniqueness).** $G=T(n,r-1)$.

*Proof sketch.* Equality forces: (i) $G[V\setminus S]=T(n-r+1,r-1)$ with parts $
B_1,\dots,B_{r-1}$ (by induction); (ii) every $w\notin S$ has exactly $r-2$ neig
hbors in $S$, i.e., $w$ is non-adjacent to exactly one $v_{\sigma(w)}\in S$.

Define $C_k=\{w\notin S:\sigma(w)=k\}$. If $u,w$ lie in different $B$-parts and
$\sigma(u)=\sigma(w)=k$, then $\{u,w\}\cup\{v_j:j\ne k\}$ is a $K_r$ (all pairwi
se adjacent), contradiction. So $\sigma$ is **injective across different $B$-par
ts**.

If some $B_i$ contained vertices with two distinct $\sigma$-values $a\ne b$, the
n every vertex in every other nonempty $B$-part ($j\ne i$) must avoid both $a$ a
nd $b$, requiring $r-2$ distinct values from only $r-3$ available — impossible b
y pigeonhole (when all parts are nonempty; when some are empty, all nonempty par
ts have size $1$ and the issue cannot arise). Hence **$\sigma$ is constant on ea
ch $B_i$** and is a bijection $\{B_1,\dots,B_{r-1}\}\to\{1,\dots,r-1\}$.

The sets $P_k=\{v_k\}\cup B_{\sigma^{-1}(k)}$ are then independent (since $v_k$
is non-adjacent to all of $B_{\sigma^{-1}(k)}$, and $B_{\sigma^{-1}(k)}$ is inde
pendent), and every pair of vertices in different $P_k$'s is adjacent. Thus $G$
is complete $(r-1)$-partite with part-sizes $1+|B_{\sigma^{-1}(k)}|$. Since the
$|B_i|$ differ by at most $1$ (Turán partition) and each gets $+1$, the sizes $|
P_k|$ differ by at most $1$ and sum to $n$. Therefore $G=T(n,r-1)$. $\square$
