## Setup

A "Data Loop" is any cycle in the multigraph whose vertices are hubs and whose e

picks 2 from the remaining 6, cable 3 picks 2 from the remaining 4:
$$\binom{8}{2}\binom{6}{2}\binom{4}{2} = 28 \cdot 15 \cdot 6 = 2520.$$
(Equivalently $\binom{8}{6}\cdot\frac{6!}{2!^3}=28\cdot90=2520$.) Since the loop

## Counting no-loop configurations

With 3 edges on 4 hub-vertices, the hub-multigraph is acyclic (a forest) iff the
re is no loop. A forest with 3 edges on 4 vertices has $4-3=1$ component, so it
is a **tree**. A loop exists iff:
- some cable has both ends in the same hub (self-loop), or
- two cables join the same pair of hubs (parallel edge / 2-cycle), or
- three cables form a triangle (3-cycle).

So no-loop ⟺ the hub-graph is a simple tree with 3 edges.

**Degree constraint.** Each hub has only 2 bays, so each hub can be incident to
at most 2 cables, i.e., every vertex has degree ≤ 2. A tree in which every verte
x has degree ≤ 2 is a **path** $P_4$.

**Number of labeled paths on 4 vertices.** By Cayley's formula there are $4^{4-2
}=16$ labeled trees on 4 vertices. Of these, 4 are stars $K_{1,3}$ (one per choi
ce of center, degree 3). The remaining $16-4=12$ are paths. (Equivalently, $4!/2
 = 12$ orderings of 4 vertices up to reversal.)

**Bay choices per path.** Fix a path, say $A\!-\!B\!-\!C\!-\!D$ with edges $AB,
BC, CD$ and degrees $1,2,2,1$:
- Hub $A$ (degree 1): choose 1 of its 2 bays for edge $AB$: $2$ ways.
- Hub $B$ (degree 2): assign its 2 bays to edges $AB$ and $BC$: $2!=2$ ways.
- Hub $C$ (degree 2): assign its 2 bays to edges $BC$ and $CD$: $2!=2$ ways.
- Hub $D$ (degree 1): choose 1 of its 2 bays for edge $CD$: $2$ ways.

This gives $2\cdot2\cdot2\cdot2 = 16$ bay assignments per path.

**Cable-to-edge assignment.** The 3 distinguishable cables must be assigned to t
he 3 distinct hub-edges of the path: $3! = 6$ ways.

**No-loop total:**
$$12 \text{ (paths)} \times 16 \text{ (bay choices)} \times 6 \text{ (cable labe
ls)} = 1152.$$
