## Proof

Let $S_N = \sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{X_n}{n}$ denote the partial sums. We show that $
\{S_N\}$ converges almost surely, hence
$$\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{X_n}{n}\text{ converges}\right)=1.$$

**Method 1: Kolmogorov's Two-Series Theorem.**

The random variables $Y_n = X_n/n$ are independent with
$$\mathbb{E}[Y_n]=0,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(Y_n)=\mathbb{E}[Y_n^2]=\frac{1}{n^
2}.$$

Since
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\operatorname{Var}(Y_n)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^2}=\f
rac{\pi^2}{6}<\infty$$
and $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[Y_n]=0$ converges (trivially), Kolmogorov's T
wo-Series Theorem implies that $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}Y_n=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{
X_n}{n}$ converges almost surely.

**Method 2: Martingale Convergence Theorem (independent verification).**

With respect to the natural filtration $\mathcal{F}_N=\sigma(X_1,\dots,X_N)$, th
e partial sums $S_N$ form a martingale:
$$\mathbb{E}[S_{N+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_N]=S_N+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{X_{N+1}}{N+1}
\right]=S_N.$$

Moreover,
$$\mathbb{E}[S_N^2]=\operatorname{Var}(S_N)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n^2}\le\frac{
\pi^2}{6},$$
so $\sup_{N}\mathbb{E}[S_N^2]<\infty$; the martingale is bounded in $L^2$. By th
e Martingale Convergence Theorem for $L^2$-bounded martingales, $S_N$ converges
almost surely (and in $L^2$) to a finite limit.

**Conclusion.** Both approaches yield that the series $\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{X_n}{n
}$ converges with probability $1$.

$$\boxed{\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{X_n}{n}\text{ converges}\right)=1}
$$
