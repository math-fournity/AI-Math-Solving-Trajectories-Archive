Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $m$ homogeneous polynomial
equations in $n$ variables ($n > m$) over $\mathbb{C}$ must always have a non-tr
ivial common root.

**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

Let $f_1, \ldots, f_m \in \mathbb{C}[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ be homogeneous polynomia
ls of positive degree, with $n > m$. Set $S = \mathbb{C}[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ and
let $S_+ = (x_1, \ldots, x_n)$ be the irrelevant (maximal homogeneous) ideal. No
te that $\dim S = n$ and $\operatorname{ht}(S_+) = n$.

Let $I = (f_1, \ldots, f_m) \subset S$. A non-trivial common root is a point $(a
_1, \ldots, a_n) \neq \mathbf{0}$ with $f_i(a_1, \ldots, a_n) = 0$ for all $i$,
which is equivalently a point $[a_1 : \cdots : a_n] \in V_+(I) \subset \mathbb{P
}^{n-1}_{\mathbb{C}}$. We prove $V_+(I) \neq \emptyset$.

**Projective Nullstellensatz.** For a homogeneous ideal $I \subset S$, $V_+(I) =
 \emptyset$ if and only if $\sqrt{I} \supseteq S_+$ (equivalently, $S_+^N \subse
teq I$ for some $N \geq 1$).

Suppose for contradiction that $V_+(I) = \emptyset$, so $S_+ \subseteq \sqrt{I}$
.

Since $S$ is Noetherian (Hilbert Basis Theorem), the radical $\sqrt{I}$ decompos
es as
$$\sqrt{I} = \mathfrak{p}_1 \cap \cdots \cap \mathfrak{p}_r$$
where $\mathfrak{p}_1, \ldots, \mathfrak{p}_r$ are the finitely many minimal pri
mes over $I$. The containment $S_+ \subseteq \sqrt{I} = \bigcap_{j=1}^r \mathfra
k{p}_j$ means that every element of $S_+$ lies in every $\mathfrak{p}_j$, i.e.,
$$S_+ \subseteq \mathfrak{p}_j \quad \text{for all } j = 1, \ldots, r.$$

**Krull's Height Theorem** (generalized principal ideal theorem): In a Noetheria
n ring, if an ideal $I$ is generated by $m$ elements, then every minimal prime $
\mathfrak{p}$ over $I$ satisfies $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}) \leq m$.

Applying this to $I = (f_1, \ldots, f_m)$, each minimal prime $\mathfrak{p}_j$ s
atisfies $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}_j) \leq m$.

On the other hand, $S_+ \subseteq \mathfrak{p}_j$ implies
$$\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}_j) \geq \operatorname{ht}(S_+) = n.$$

Combining the two inequalities:
$$n \leq \operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}_j) \leq m \quad \Longrightarrow \quad n
\leq m,$$
which contradicts the hypothesis $n > m$.

Therefore the assumption $V_+(I) = \emptyset$ is false, and $V_+(I) \neq \emptys
et$. Hence there exists a point $[a_1 : \cdots : a_n] \in \mathbb{P}^{n-1}_{\mat
hbb{C}}$ with $f_i(a_1, \ldots, a_n) = 0$ for all $i$ and $(a_1, \ldots, a_n) \n
eq \mathbf{0}$. This is the desired non-trivial common root. $\blacksquare$

**Remarks on the hypotheses.** The algebraic closedness of $\mathbb{C}$ is essen
tial: over $\mathbb{R}$, the single equation $x^2 + y^2 = 0$ ($m=1, n=2$) has on
ly the trivial root. The condition "$n > m$" is sharp: $n = m$ can fail (e.g., $
x_1 = 0, \ldots, x_n = 0$ has only the trivial root). The condition "each variab
le appears at least once" rules out inflating $n$ with dummy variables that don'
t genuinely participate, keeping the problem well-posed.
