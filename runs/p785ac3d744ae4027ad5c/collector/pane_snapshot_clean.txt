**Proof.**

Set up coordinates with $A$ at the origin. Let $\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}
$ be the position vectors of $B,C,D$, and let $k=\dfrac{AD}{BC}$.

**Locating $E$ and $F$ on line $AB$.** The order is $B,E,A,F$, so $E$ lies betwe
en $B$ and $A$, while $F$ lies beyond $A$.

- $\dfrac{AE}{EB}=k$ with $AE+EB=AB$ gives $AE=\dfrac{k}{k+1}\,AB$, hence
$$E=A+\frac{k}{k+1}(B-A)=\frac{k}{k+1}\,\mathbf{b}.$$

- $\dfrac{AF}{FB}=k$ with $FB=FA+AB$ gives $AF=\dfrac{k}{1-k}\,AB$, and $F$ is o
n the far side of $A$ from $B$, so
$$F=A+\frac{k}{1-k}(A-B)=-\frac{k}{1-k}\,\mathbf{b}.$$

**Locating $G$ and $H$ on line $CD$.** The order is $C,G,D,H$, so $G$ lies betwe
en $C$ and $D$, while $H$ lies beyond $D$.

- $\dfrac{DG}{GC}=k$ with $DG+GC=CD$ gives $DG=\dfrac{k}{k+1}\,CD$, hence
$$G=D+\frac{k}{k+1}(C-D)=\frac{1}{k+1}\,\mathbf{d}+\frac{k}{k+1}\,\mathbf{c}.$$

- $\dfrac{DH}{HC}=k$ with $HC=HD+DC$ gives $DH=\dfrac{k}{1-k}\,DC$, and $H$ is b
eyond $D$ from $C$, so
$$H=D+\frac{k}{1-k}(D-C)=\frac{1}{1-k}\,\mathbf{d}-\frac{k}{1-k}\,\mathbf{c}.$$

**Computing the vectors $F-H$ and $E-G$.**

$$F-H=-\frac{k}{1-k}\,\mathbf{b}-\frac{1}{1-k}\,\mathbf{d}+\frac{k}{1-k}\,\mathb
f{c}=\frac{1}{1-k}\bigl(k(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{b})-\mathbf{d}\bigr).$$

$$E-G=\frac{k}{k+1}\,\mathbf{b}-\frac{1}{k+1}\,\mathbf{d}-\frac{k}{k+1}\,\mathbf
{c}=\frac{1}{k+1}\bigl(-k(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{b})-\mathbf{d}\bigr).$$

**Taking the dot product.** Let $\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{b}$ and $\mathbf{
v}=\mathbf{d}$. Then

$$(F-H)\cdot(E-G)=\frac{1}{(1-k)(k+1)}\,(k\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v})\cdot(-k\mathbf{
u}-\mathbf{v}).$$

Expanding,

$$(k\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v})\cdot(-k\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v})=-k^{2}|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\
underbrace{-k\,\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}+k\,\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{u}}_{=\,0}+|\
mathbf{v}|^{2}=-k^{2}|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+|\mathbf{v}|^{2}.$$

**Using the defining ratio.** Now $|\mathbf{u}|=|\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{b}|=BC$ and
$|\mathbf{v}|=|\mathbf{d}|=AD$. Since $k=\dfrac{AD}{BC}=\dfrac{|\mathbf{v}|}{|\m
athbf{u}|}$, we have $|\mathbf{v}|^{2}=k^{2}|\mathbf{u}|^{2}$. Therefore

$$(F-H)\cdot(E-G)=\frac{-k^{2}|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+|\mathbf{v}|^{2}}{(1-k)(k+1)}=\fr
ac{-k^{2}|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+k^{2}|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}{(1-k)(k+1)}=0.$$

Since the dot product of the direction vectors of $FH$ and $EG$ is zero, the lin
es $FH$ and $EG$ are perpendicular. $\blacksquare$
