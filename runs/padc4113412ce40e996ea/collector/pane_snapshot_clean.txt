## Proof

**Setup.** An *alternating permutation* of $\{1, 2, \ldots, n\}$ is a permutatio
n $\sigma$ satisfying $\sigma(1) < \sigma(2) > \sigma(3) < \sigma(4) > \cdots$ (
up-down pattern). Let $A_n$ denote the number of such permutations. These are th
e **Euler zigzag numbers** $E_n$.

**Exponential generating function.** The classical result (André's theorem) give
s the EGF:

$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} E_n \frac{x^n}{n!} = \sec x + \tan x.$$

For **odd** $n = 2m+1$, the relevant EGF is $\tan x$ (the tangent numbers), and
for even $n$ it is $\sec x$. Since the problem restricts to odd $n$, we work wit
h

$$\tan x = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty} E_{2m+1} \frac{x^{2m+1}}{(2m+1)!}.$$

**Singularity analysis of $\tan x$.** Since $\tan x = \sin x / \cos x$, the sing
ularities (simple poles) occur at the zeros of $\cos x$, namely $x = \pm\frac{\p
i}{2}, \pm\frac{3\pi}{2}, \ldots$. The **dominant** singularities (closest to th
e origin) are at $x = \pm\frac{\pi}{2}$, both at distance $\rho = \frac{\pi}{2}$
.

**Local expansions near the dominant poles.**

*Near $x = \frac{\pi}{2}$:* Set $t = \frac{\pi}{2} - x \to 0^+$. Then
$$\tan x = \tan\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2} - t\right) = \cot t = \frac{1}{t} - \frac{
t}{3} - \frac{t^3}{45} - \cdots = \frac{1}{\frac{\pi}{2} - x} + O\!\left(\tfrac{
\pi}{2} - x\right).$$

*Near $x = -\frac{\pi}{2}$:* Set $t = \frac{\pi}{2} + x \to 0^+$. Then
$$\tan x = \tan\!\left(-\tfrac{\pi}{2} + t\right) = -\cot t = -\frac{1}{t} + \fr
ac{t}{3} + \cdots = -\frac{1}{\frac{\pi}{2} + x} + O\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2} + x\r
ight).$$

**Extracting coefficients via Darboux's method.** The function $\tan x$ is merom
orphic with simple poles. The coefficient $[x^n]\tan x$ receives contributions f
rom each pole. The dominant contribution comes from the two nearest poles at $\p
m\frac{\pi}{2}$; all other poles at $\pm\frac{3\pi}{2}, \ldots$ contribute terms
 smaller by a factor of at least $(1/3)^{n+1}$, hence are exponentially negligib
le.

From the pole at $x = \frac{\pi}{2}$ with residue $-1$ (i.e., leading term $\fra
c{1}{\pi/2 - x}$):

$$[x^n]\,\frac{1}{\frac{\pi}{2} - x} = \frac{1}{\pi/2}\cdot\frac{1}{(1 - \frac{2
x}{\pi})}\Bigg|_{[x^n]} = \left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{n+1}.$$

From the pole at $x = -\frac{\pi}{2}$ with leading term $-\frac{1}{\pi/2 + x}$:

$$[x^n]\,\left(-\frac{1}{\frac{\pi}{2} + x}\right) = -\frac{1}{\pi/2}\cdot\frac{
1}{(1+\frac{2x}{\pi})}\Bigg|_{[x^n]} = -\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{n+1}(-1)^n =
 \left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{n+1}(-1)^{n+1}.$$

**Combining the two dominant contributions:**

$$[x^n]\tan x \sim \left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{n+1}\bigl(1 + (-1)^{n+1}\bigr).$$

For **odd** $n$: $(-1)^{n+1} = (-1)^{\text{even}} = 1$, so

$$[x^n]\tan x \sim 2\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{n+1}.$$

(For even $n$ the leading terms cancel, consistent with the fact that $\tan x$ h
as only odd powers — the even-index coefficients come from $\sec x$ instead.)

**Final asymptotic.** Since $E_n / n! = [x^n](\sec x + \tan x)$ and for odd $n$
this equals $[x^n]\tan x$:

$$\boxed{E_n \sim 2\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{n+1} n! \quad \text{as } n \to \i
nfty \text{ through odd values.}}$$

**Numerical verification.** For $n = 9$: $E_9 = 7936$. The formula gives $2 \cdo
t (2/\pi)^{10} \cdot 9! = 2 \cdot 0.01094 \cdot 362880 \approx 7940$, agreeing t
o within $0.05\%$. For $n = 7$: $E_7 = 272$, and $2 \cdot (2/\pi)^8 \cdot 7! = 2
 \cdot 0.02700 \cdot 5040 \approx 272.2$, matching almost exactly. $\blacksquare
$
