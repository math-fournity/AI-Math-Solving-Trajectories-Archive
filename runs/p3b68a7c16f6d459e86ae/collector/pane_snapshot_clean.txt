## Proof

**Claim:** If a manifold $M$ is $\sigma$-compact, then $M$ possesses a countable
 dense subset.

**Setup.** Recall that a (topological) $n$-manifold is a Hausdorff space in whic
h every point has an open neighborhood homeomorphic to an open subset of $\mathb
b{R}^n$. Since $M$ is $\sigma$-compact, we may write
$$M = \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} K_i,$$
where each $K_i \subseteq M$ is compact.

**Step 1: Each compact $K_i$ is covered by finitely many separable open sets.**

Fix $i$. For every point $p \in K_i$, by the manifold property there exists an o
pen neighborhood $U_p$ of $p$ and a homeomorphism $\varphi_p : U_p \to V_p$ wher
e $V_p \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is open. The family $\{U_p : p \in K_i\}$ is an o
pen cover of $K_i$. By compactness of $K_i$, there is a finite subcover
$$K_i \subseteq U_{i,1} \cup U_{i,2} \cup \cdots \cup U_{i,m_i}.$$

Each $U_{i,j}$ is homeomorphic to an open subset $V_{i,j}$ of $\mathbb{R}^n$. Si
nce $\mathbb{R}^n$ is separable (it has the countable dense subset $\mathbb{Q}^n
$) and separability is inherited by open subspaces, $V_{i,j}$ is separable. Let
$E_{i,j} \subseteq V_{i,j}$ be a countable dense subset (for instance $E_{i,j} =
 V_{i,j} \cap \mathbb{Q}^n$). Setting $D_{i,j} = \varphi_{i,j}^{-1}(E_{i,j})$, w
e obtain a countable subset $D_{i,j} \subseteq U_{i,j}$ that is dense in $U_{i,j
}$ (homeomorphisms preserve density).

**Step 2: Build a countable dense subset of $M$.**

Define
$$D \;=\; \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{j=1}^{m_i} D_{i,j}.$$
This is a countable union of finite unions of countable sets, hence $D$ is **cou
ntable**.

**Step 3: $D$ is dense in $M$.**

Let $W \subseteq M$ be any nonempty open set. Since $M = \bigcup_i K_i$, we have
 $W = \bigcup_i (W \cap K_i)$, so $W \cap K_i \neq \varnothing$ for some $i$. Si
nce $K_i \subseteq \bigcup_{j=1}^{m_i} U_{i,j}$, there exists some $j$ with $W \
cap U_{i,j} \neq \varnothing$. Now $W \cap U_{i,j}$ is a nonempty open subset of
 $U_{i,j}$, and $D_{i,j}$ is dense in $U_{i,j}$, so
$$D_{i,j} \cap (W \cap U_{i,j}) \neq \varnothing.$$
In particular $D \cap W \neq \varnothing$.

Since $D$ meets every nonempty open subset of $M$, $D$ is dense in $M$.

**Conclusion.** The set $D$ is countable and dense in $M$. Therefore every $\sig
ma$-compact manifold admits a countable dense subset. $\blacksquare$
