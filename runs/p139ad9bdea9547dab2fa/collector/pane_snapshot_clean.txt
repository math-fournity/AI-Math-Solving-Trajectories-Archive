**Claim:** The limit equals $0$.

**Proof.**

Let $r_n = 1/n$, so $B_n = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^m : \|x\| \le r_n\}$. Throughout w
e assume the Gaussian measure $\mu$ is such that $\mu(B_n)>0$ for all sufficient
ly large $n$ (this holds, for instance, whenever the origin lies in the relative
 interior of the support of $\mu$ — in particular for every non‑degenerate Gauss
ian, and for any Gaussian whose mean is the origin). The quantities $\sup_{x\in
B_n}\|x\|^2 = r_n^2 = 1/n^2 > 0$ are strictly positive, so the displayed express
ion is well defined and non‑negative for all large $n$.

The key observation is a pointwise bound on $B_n$. For every $x \in B_n$ we have
 $\|x\| \le r_n$, hence
$$
\|x\|^4 = \|x\|^2 \cdot \|x\|^2 \le r_n^2 \cdot \|x\|^2 \le r_n^2 \cdot r_n^2 =
r_n^4.
$$
Integrating against $\mu$ over $B_n$,
$$
\int_{B_n} \|x\|^4 \, d\mu(x) \;\le\; r_n^4 \, \mu(B_n).
$$
Therefore
$$
0 \;\le\; \frac{1}{\mu(B_n)\,\sup_{x\in B_n}\|x\|^2}\int_{B_n}\|x\|^4\,d\mu(x)
\;\le\; \frac{r_n^4\,\mu(B_n)}{\mu(B_n)\,r_n^2}
\;=\; r_n^2
\;=\; \frac{1}{n^2}.
$$
Since $1/n^2 \to 0$ as $n\to\infty$, the squeeze theorem yields
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\mu(B_n)\sup_{x\in B_n}\|x\|^2}\int_{B_n}\|x\|^4\,d\mu
(x) \;=\; 0.
$$

**Remark.** The argument uses only the crude bound $\|x\|^4 \le (\sup_{B_n}\|x\|
^2)\|x\|^2$ together with $\|x\|^2 \le \sup_{B_n}\|x\|^2$; it is independent of
the dimension $m$, the covariance structure, and the mean of the Gaussian (provi
ded the expression is defined). The Gaussian hypothesis serves only to guarantee
 that $\mu(B_n)>0$ so that the ratio is meaningful; the actual value of the limi
t is forced entirely by the geometry of the shrinking ball.
