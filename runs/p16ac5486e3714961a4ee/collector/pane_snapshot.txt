## Proof

**Step 1: Identify the dominant singularity.**

The function $\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-z}}$ has a singularity at $z = 1$ (a branch point
 of type $(1-z)^{-1/2}$) and is analytic elsewhere in $|z| < 1$.

The function $F_5(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{z^{2^n}}{2^{5n}}$ is a lacunary
 series. At $z = 1$:
$$F_5(1) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^{5n}} = \frac{1}{1 - 2^{-5}} = \frac{3
2}{31} < \infty.$$

Since the coefficients $\frac{1}{2^{5n}}$ decay geometrically, $F_5(z)$ converge
s absolutely on $|z| \leq 1$, so $F_5$ is analytic at $z = 1$ with $F_5(1) = 32/
31$.

The product $G(z) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-z}} F_5(z)$ therefore has its dominant sing
ularity at $z = 1$, inherited entirely from $\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-z}}$.

**Step 2: Local behavior near $z = 1$.**

As $z \to 1^-$, since $F_5$ is continuous (indeed analytic) at $z = 1$:
$$G(z) = \frac{F_5(z)}{\sqrt{1-z}} \sim \frac{F_5(1)}{\sqrt{1-z}} = \frac{32/31}
{\sqrt{1-z}} = \frac{32}{31}(1-z)^{-1/2}.$$

**Step 3: Apply the transfer theorem (singularity analysis).**

By the standard transfer theorem (Flajolet–Odlyzko): if $f(z) \sim C(1-z)^{-\alp
ha}$ as $z \to 1$ with $\alpha \notin \{0, -1, -2, \ldots\}$, and $f$ is analyti
c in a $\Delta$-domain at $z=1$, then:
$$[z^N]\, f(z) \sim \frac{C}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\, N^{\alpha - 1}.$$

Here $C = 32/31$, $\alpha = 1/2$, and $\Gamma(1/2) = \sqrt{\pi}$.

The $\Delta$-domain analyticity condition is satisfied: $\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-z}}$ i
s analytic in a dented domain around $z=1$ (excluding the ray $[1, \infty)$), an
d $F_5(z)$ is analytic in a neighborhood of $z=1$, so their product is analytic
in the same $\Delta$-domain.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

$$[z^N]\, G(z) \sim \frac{32/31}{\Gamma(1/2)}\, N^{1/2 - 1} = \frac{32}{31\sqrt{
\pi}}\, N^{-1/2} = \frac{32}{31\sqrt{\pi N}}.$$

**Direct verification via convolution.** The coefficient can also be written exp
licitly:
$$g_N = [z^N]\, G(z) = \sum_{\substack{n \geq 0 \\ 2^n \leq N}} \frac{1}{2^{5n}}
 \cdot c_{N - 2^n},$$
where $c_m = \binom{2m}{m}/4^m \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi m}}$. For each fixed $n$,
 as $N \to \infty$, $c_{N-2^n} \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi N}}$. Since $\sum_{n=0}^{
\infty} 2^{-5n} = 32/31$ converges and the tail is controlled by geometric decay
, exchanging the limit with the sum gives:
$$g_N \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi N}} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^{5n}} = \frac{3
2}{31\sqrt{\pi N}},$$
confirming the singularity analysis result.

$$\boxed{[z^N]\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-z}} F_5(z)\right) \sim \frac{32}{31\sqrt{\p
