Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a finite group $G$ can have
 a normal subgroup $H$ with $|\operatorname{Aut} H| > |\operatorname{Aut} G|$.

**Answer: Yes.** Here is an explicit example.

---

## Construction

Let $G = \operatorname{AGL}(1, 16) = \mathbb{F}_{16} \rtimes \mathbb{F}_{16}^*$,
 the group of affine transformations $x \mapsto ax + b$ of the field $\mathbb{F}
_{16}$, where $a \in \mathbb{F}_{16}^*$, $b \in \mathbb{F}_{16}$. Let $H = \math
bb{F}_{16}$ (the translation subgroup), so $H \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^4$
and $H \trianglelefteq G$. We have $|G| = 16 \cdot 15 = 240$.

## Computing $|\operatorname{Aut} H|$

Since $H \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^4$, we have $\operatorname{Aut} H \cong
\operatorname{GL}(4, 2)$, so
$$|\operatorname{Aut} H| = \prod_{i=0}^{3}(2^4 - 2^i) = 15 \cdot 14 \cdot 12 \cd
ot 8 = 20160.$$

## Computing $|\operatorname{Aut} G|$

Set $K = G/H \cong \mathbb{F}_{16}^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/15\mathbb{Z}$, acting on $
H$ by field multiplication.

**Step 1: $H$ is the Fitting subgroup of $G$ (hence characteristic).**

$H$ is an abelian normal $2$-subgroup. The action of $K = \mathbb{F}_{16}^*$ on
$H = \mathbb{F}_{16}$ (additive) is **irreducible**: any $K$-invariant additive
subgroup $W \subseteq \mathbb{F}_{16}$ satisfies $a \cdot W \subseteq W$ for all
 $a \in \mathbb{F}_{16}^*$, making $W$ an ideal of the field $\mathbb{F}_{16}$,
so $W = 0$ or $W = \mathbb{F}_{16}$.

Since $H$ is the unique Sylow $2$-subgroup of $G$ (it is normal of order $16 = |
G|_2$), every nontrivial normal subgroup meets $H$ nontrivially, hence contains
$H$ (by irreducibility). The quotient $G/H \cong \mathbb{Z}/15\mathbb{Z}$ has no
rmal subgroups of orders $1, 3, 5, 15$, giving normal subgroups $H \leq N \leq G
$. None of $H \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_3$ or $H \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_5$ is nilpotent (th
e complement acts nontrivially on $H$), so $F(G) = H$. The Fitting subgroup is c
haracteristic, so every automorphism of $G$ restricts to an automorphism of $H$.

**Step 2: The restriction homomorphism.**

We obtain $\operatorname{res}: \operatorname{Aut}(G) \to \operatorname{Aut}(H) =
 \operatorname{GL}(4,2)$.

**Step 3: Kernel.** An automorphism fixing $H$ pointwise sends $k \in K$ to $(v_
k, k) \in H \rtimes K$, and the homomorphism condition gives $v_{kk'} = v_k + k
\cdot v_{k'}$, the $1$-cocycle condition. So $\ker(\operatorname{res}) = Z^1(K,
H)$.

Since $\gcd(|K|, |H|) = \gcd(15, 16) = 1$, we have $H^1(K, H) = 0$, so $Z^1(K, H
) = B^1(K, H)$. Since $K$ acts irreducibly, $C_H(K) = 0$, giving $|B^1(K,H)| = |
H/C_H(K)| = |H| = 16$.

**Step 4: Image.** The image consists of all $\alpha \in \operatorname{GL}(H)$ s
uch that $\alpha(k \cdot v) = \beta(k) \cdot \alpha(v)$ for some $\beta \in \ope
ratorname{Aut}(K)$ and all $k, v$. These are the **semilinear automorphisms** of
 $H = \mathbb{F}_{16}$ as an $\mathbb{F}_2]$-module:
$$\operatorname{\Gamma L}(1, 16) = \mathbb{F}_{16}^* \rtimes \operatorname{Gal}(
\mathbb{F}_{16}/\mathbb{F}_2).$$
Since $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{16}/\mathbb{F}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathb
b{Z}$ (the Frobenius $x \mapsto x^2$ has order $4$, as $2^4 \equiv 1 \pmod{15}$)
, we get
$$|\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{res})| = 15 \cdot 4 = 60.$$

**Step 5: Conclusion.**
$$|\operatorname{Aut} G| = |\ker(\operatorname{res})| \cdot |\operatorname{im}(\
operatorname{res})| = 16 \cdot 60 = 960.$$

## Final Comparison

$$|\operatorname{Aut} H| = 20160 > 960 = |\operatorname{Aut} G|.$$

The group $G = \operatorname{AGL}(1, 16)$ of order $240$, with normal subgroup $
H = (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^4$, satisfies $|\operatorname{Aut} H| > |\operatorn
ame{Aut} G|$.

The key mechanism: $K = \mathbb{F}_{16}^*$ acts irreducibly on $H$, making $H$ t
he Fitting subgroup (characteristic). The image of $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ in $\
operatorname{GL}(4,2)$ is only the semilinear group $\operatorname{\Gamma L}(1,1
6)$ of order $60$ — a tiny fraction of $|\operatorname{GL}(4,2)| = 20160$ — and
the kernel (of order $16$) cannot compensate, yielding $|\operatorname{Aut} G| =
 960 \ll 20160 = |\operatorname{Aut} H|$.
