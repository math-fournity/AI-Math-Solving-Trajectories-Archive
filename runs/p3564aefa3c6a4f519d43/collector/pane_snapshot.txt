Setup and interpretation. Since $f$ is finite and $Y$ is locally noetherian, $f$
is of finite presentation. The phrase "$f$ is flat at $y$" means that $f$ is
flat at every point of the fiber $f^{-1}(y)$, i.e., for every $x \in f^{-1}(y)$,
the local ring $\mathcal{O}{X,x}$ is flat as an $\mathcal{O}{Y,f(x)} =
\mathcal{O}_{Y,y}$-module. Since $f$ is finite, the fiber $f^{-1}(y)$ is a
finite set.

Step 1: The flat locus is open. Define the flat locus $$U = {x \in X :
\mathcal{O}{X,x} \text{ is flat over } \mathcal{O}{Y,f(x)}}.$$ By the openness
of the flat locus theorem (EGA IV$_4$, 11.1.1), for a morphism of finite
presentation over a locally noetherian base, $U$ is an open subset of $X$. Since
$f$ is finite and $Y$ is locally noetherian, $f$ is of finite presentation, so
this applies.

Step 2: The fiber over $y$ lies in $U$. By hypothesis, $f$ is flat at every $x
\in f^{-1}(y)$, so $f^{-1}(y) \subset U$.

Step 3: Use that finite morphisms are closed. Let $Z = X \setminus U$; this is
closed in $X$. A finite morphism is proper (in particular, a closed map), so
$f(Z)$ is a closed subset of $Y$.

Step 4: $y \notin f(Z)$. If $y \in f(Z)$, there would exist $z \in Z$ with $f(z)
= y$, i.e., $z \in f^{-1}(y)$. But $f^{-1}(y) \subset U$, contradicting $z \in Z
= X \setminus U$.

Step 5: Construct $V$. Set $$V = Y \setminus f(Z).$$ This is an open
neighborhood of $y$ in $Y$.

Step 6: $f^{-1}(V) \to V$ is flat. We claim $f^{-1}(V) \subset U$. Indeed, if $x
\in f^{-1}(V)$, then $f(x) \in V = Y \setminus f(Z)$, so $f(x) \notin f(Z)$. If
$x$ were in $Z$, then $f(x) \in f(Z)$, a contradiction. Hence $x \in U$.
Therefore $f^{-1}(V) \subset U$, which means that for every $x \in f^{-1}(V)$,
the morphism $f$ is flat at $x$. Equivalently, the induced morphism $f^{-1}(V)
\to V$ is flat. $\blacksquare$
