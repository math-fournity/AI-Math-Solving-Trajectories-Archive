## Transformation Law of Christoffel Symbols

### Setup

Let $\nabla$ be an affine connection on $M$. In $x$-coordinates the Christoffel
symbols are defined by
$$\nabla_{\partial_{x_j}}\partial_{x_k}=\sum_i \Gamma_{jk}^i\,\partial_{x_i},\qq
uad \partial_{x_i}:=\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_i},$$
and in $y$-coordinates by
$$\nabla_{\partial_{y_j}}\partial_{y_k}=\sum_i \overline{\Gamma}_{jk}^i\,\partia
l_{y_i},\qquad \partial_{y_i}:=\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y_i}.$$

By the chain rule, with Jacobian entries $J_{aj}:=\tfrac{\partial x_a}{\partial
y_j}$ and inverse $K_{ib}:=\tfrac{\partial y_i}{\partial x_b}$ (so $\sum_a J_{aj
}K_{da}=\delta_{dj}$),
$$\partial_{y_j}=\sum_a J_{aj}\,\partial_{x_a},\qquad \partial_{x_c}=\sum_i K_{i
c}\,\partial_{y_i}.$$

### Computation

Using $C^\infty$-linearity of $\nabla$ in the first slot and the Leibniz rule in
 the second:

$$\nabla_{\partial_{y_j}}\partial_{y_k}
=\sum_{a,b}J_{aj}\,\nabla_{\partial_{x_a}}\!\bigl(J_{bk}\,\partial_{x_b}\bigr)
=\underbrace{\sum_{a,b}J_{aj}\,\partial_{x_a}(J_{bk})\,\partial_{x_b}}_{(I)}
+\underbrace{\sum_{a,b}J_{aj}J_{bk}\sum_c\Gamma_{ab}^c\,\partial_{x_c}}_{(II)}.$
$

**Term (I).** Rewrite $\partial_{x_a}(J_{bk})=\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_a}\!\b
igl(\tfrac{\partial x_b}{\partial y_k}\bigr)$. Using $\partial_{x_a}=\sum_d K_{d
a}\,\partial_{y_d}$,
$$\partial_{x_a}(J_{bk})=\sum_d K_{da}\,\frac{\partial^2 x_b}{\partial y_d\,\par
tial y_k}.$$
Hence
$$(I)=\sum_{a,b,d}J_{aj}K_{da}\,\frac{\partial^2 x_b}{\partial y_d\,\partial y_k
}\,\partial_{x_b}
=\sum_{b,d}\delta_{dj}\,\frac{\partial^2 x_b}{\partial y_d\,\partial y_k}\,\part
ial_{x_b}
=\sum_b\frac{\partial^2 x_b}{\partial y_j\,\partial y_k}\,\partial_{x_b}.$$

**Term (II).** Substitute $\partial_{x_c}=\sum_i K_{ic}\,\partial_{y_i}$:
$$(II)=\sum_{a,b,c,i}J_{aj}J_{bk}K_{ic}\,\Gamma_{ab}^c\,\partial_{y_i}.$$

**Combine and convert to the $y$-basis.** Replace $\partial_{x_b}$ in (I) by $\s
um_i K_{ib}\,\partial_{y_i}$:
$$(I)=\sum_{b,i}K_{ib}\,\frac{\partial^2 x_b}{\partial y_j\,\partial y_k}\,\part
ial_{y_i}.$$

Therefore
$$\nabla_{\partial_{y_j}}\partial_{y_k}
=\sum_i\Biggl[\sum_{a,b,c}K_{ic}J_{aj}J_{bk}\,\Gamma_{ab}^c+\sum_b K_{ib}\,\frac
{\partial^2 x_b}{\partial y_j\,\partial y_k}\Biggr]\partial_{y_i}.$$

### Result

Comparing coefficients of $\partial_{y_i}$ with the definition of $\overline{\Ga
mma}_{jk}^i$ yields the **transformation law of the Christoffel symbols**:

$$\boxed{\;\overline{\Gamma}_{jk}^{\,i}\;=\;\sum_{a,b,c}\frac{\partial y_i}{\par
tial x_c}\,\frac{\partial x_a}{\partial y_j}\,\frac{\partial x_b}{\partial y_k}\
,\Gamma_{ab}^{\,c}\;+\;\sum_{b}\frac{\partial y_i}{\partial x_b}\,\frac{\partial
^2 x_b}{\partial y_j\,\partial y_k}\;}$$

### Remarks

- The **first (homogeneous) term** transforms exactly like the components of a $
(1,2)$-tensor: it is the "tensor-like" part of the transformation.
- The **second (inhomogeneous) term** involves *second* derivatives of the old c
oordinates with respect to the new ones. Its presence is what prevents the $\Gam
ma_{jk}^i$ from being the components of a tensor.
- Consequently, the Christoffel symbols of an affine connection are **not** tens
orial; they are the components of a *connection*, i.e. a non-tensorial geometric
 object. The genuinely tensorial object associated to a connection is the **diff
erence** $\Gamma_{jk}^i-\widetilde{\Gamma}_{jk}^i$ of two connections, whose inh
omogeneous terms cancel, leaving a genuine $(1,2)$-tensor (related to the torsio
n and the contorsion).
