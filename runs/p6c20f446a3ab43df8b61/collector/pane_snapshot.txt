## Proof

**Claim:** The induced homomorphism $H_1(p): H_1(E) \to H_1(B)$ is **not** injec
tive in general.

**Counterexample.** Let $B = K$ be the Klein bottle, and let $p: E \to K$ be the
 covering map corresponding to the subgroup $H = \langle b \rangle \leq \pi_1(K)
$, where we describe $\pi_1(K)$ below.

**Step 1: The fundamental group of $K$ and its abelianization.**

The Klein bottle has presentation
$$\pi_1(K) = \langle a, b \mid aba^{-1} = b^{-1} \rangle.$$
Abelianizing, the relation $aba^{-1} = b^{-1}$ becomes $b = -b$, i.e., $2b = 0$.
 Thus
$$H_1(K) \cong \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},$$
where $a \mapsto (1, \bar{0})$ and $b \mapsto (0, \bar{1})$. In particular, the
class of $b$ has **order 2** in $H_1(K)$.

**Step 2: The subgroup $H = \langle b \rangle$ and the covering space $E$.**

The subgroup $H = \langle b \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}$ is **normal** in $\pi_1(K)
$, since $aba^{-1} = b^{-1} \in H$ and $a^{-1}ba = b^{-1} \in H$. The quotient i
s $\pi_1(K)/H \cong \langle a \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}$, so $H$ defines an infin
ite-sheeted (normal) covering $p: E \to K$ with $\pi_1(E) \cong H \cong \mathbb{
Z}$.

Concretely, the universal cover of $K$ is $\mathbb{R}^2$, with deck transformati
ons $a: (x,y) \mapsto (x+1, -y)$ and $b: (x,y) \mapsto (x, y+1)$. The subgroup $
H = \langle b \rangle$ acts by integer translations in the $y$-direction, so
$$E = \mathbb{R}^2 / H \cong \mathbb{R} \times S^1.$$

**Step 3: Compute $H_1(E)$.**

Since $E \cong \mathbb{R} \times S^1$ and $\mathbb{R}$ is contractible,
$$H_1(E) \cong H_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z},$$
generated by the loop $\gamma$ going once around the $S^1$ factor (the $b$-direc
tion).

**Step 4: The induced map $H_1(p)$ is not injective.**

The generator $\gamma \in H_1(E) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ projects to the loop in $K$ r
epresenting $b \in \pi_1(K)$. Under abelianization, $b$ maps to $(0, \bar{1}) \i
n \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, which has **order 2**. Therefore
$$H_1(p)(\gamma) = (0, \bar{1}) \quad \text{and} \quad H_1(p)(2\gamma) = (0, \ba
r{0}).$$
Since $2\gamma \neq 0$ in $H_1(E) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ but $H_1(p)(2\gamma) = 0$, t
he kernel of $H_1(p)$ contains $2\mathbb{Z} \neq 0$.

**Conclusion.** The map $H_1(p): H_1(E) \to H_1(K)$ has nontrivial kernel ($\ker
 H_1(p) = 2\mathbb{Z}$), so it is **not injective**.

**Remark on the mechanism.** For any covering map, the induced map $\pi_1(p): \p
i_1(E) \to \pi_1(B)$ is injective. However, $H_1$ is the abelianization of $\pi_
1$, and abelianization is **not left exact**: an injective group homomorphism ne
ed not remain injective after abelianization. In this example, $b \in \pi_1(E) \
cong \mathbb{Z}$ is sent to $b \in \pi_1(K)$ injectively, but in $H_1(K)$ the el
ement $[b]$ becomes torsion (order 2), so the commutator $aba^{-1}b \in [\pi_1(K
), \pi_1(K)]$ kills $2[b]$ in homology.
