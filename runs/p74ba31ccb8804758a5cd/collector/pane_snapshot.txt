## Solution

The equation is $A^{10} - 3A^9 + 2A^8 = 0$, which factors as
$$A^8(A^2 - 3A + 2) = A^8(A-1)(A-2) = 0.$$

So the minimal polynomial $m_A(x)$ divides $x^8(x-1)(x-2)$.

**Eigenvalues.** The only possible eigenvalues are $0, 1, 2$.

**Jordan block size constraints.** For each eigenvalue $\lambda$, the size of th
e largest Jordan block $J_\lambda$ is bounded by the power of $(x-\lambda)$ in $
x^8(x-1)(x-2)$:
- $\lambda = 0$: largest block $\le 8$.
- $\lambda = 1$: largest block $\le 1$ (all blocks are $1\times 1$).
- $\lambda = 2$: largest block $\le 1$ (all blocks are $1\times 1$).

**Counting via Jordan normal form.** Two matrices in $M_3(\mathbb{C})$ are simil
ar iff they share the same Jordan normal form. So $|\mathcal{S}|$ equals the num
ber of valid Jordan forms.

Let $a, b, c$ be the algebraic multiplicities of $0, 1, 2$ respectively, with $a
+b+c=3$ and $a,b,c\ge 0$.

For eigenvalue $0$: the Jordan structure is a partition of $a$ into parts each $
\le 8$. Since $a \le 3 \le 8$, this is just the number $p(a)$ of all partitions
of $a$:
$$p(0)=1,\quad p(1)=1,\quad p(2)=2,\quad p(3)=3.$$

For eigenvalues $1$ and $2$: since all blocks are $1\times 1$, there is exactly
**one** Jordan structure for each (given the multiplicity).

**Summing over all $(a,b,c)$.** For each value of $a$, the number of valid $(b,c
)$ pairs with $b+c = 3-a$ is $3-a+1 = 4-a$. The contribution is $(4-a)\cdot p(a)
$:

| $a$ | $b+c$ | # of $(b,c)$ pairs | $p(a)$ | contribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 3 | 4 | 1 | 4 |
| 1 | 2 | 3 | 1 | 3 |
| 2 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| 3 | 0 | 1 | 3 | 3 |

**Total:**
$$|\mathcal{S}| = 4 + 3 + 4 + 3 = 14.$$

**Verification that each form is achievable.** Every partition counted above cor
responds to an actual Jordan form whose minimal polynomial divides $x^8(x-1)(x-2
)$: the block-size bounds are satisfied by construction, and $x^8$ annihilates a
ny $0$-block of size $\le 8$ (in particular $\le 3$), $(x-1)$ annihilates every
$1\times 1$ block for $\lambda=1$, and $(x-2)$ annihilates every $1\times 1$ blo
ck for $\lambda=2$. Hence all 14 forms are valid, and no two are similar to each
 other.

$$\boxed{14}$$
