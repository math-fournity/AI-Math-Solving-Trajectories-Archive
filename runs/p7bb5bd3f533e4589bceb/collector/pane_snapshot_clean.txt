## Proof

We work in a sufficiently saturated model $\mathbb{U} \models ACF_0$. The type $
x = x$ (in one variable) defines the whole field $\mathbb{U} = \mathbb{A}^1(\mat
hbb{U})$. We compute its Morley rank.

**Key fact (quantifier elimination for ACF).** $ACF$ admits quantifier eliminati
on in the language of rings. Consequently every definable subset of $\mathbb{A}^
1$ (in one variable, over any parameter set) is a Boolean combination of zero se
ts $\{x : p(x) = 0\}$ of univariate polynomials $p$. Each such zero set is finit
e, so every definable subset of $\mathbb{A}^1$ is either **finite** or **cofinit
e**.

**Step 1: $\mathrm{MR}(x = x) \geq 1$.**

By definition, $\mathrm{MR}(\varphi) \geq \alpha + 1$ if there are infinitely ma
ny pairwise inconsistent formulas $\psi_i$, each implying $\varphi$, with $\math
rm{MR}(\psi_i) \geq \alpha$.

Consider the formulas $\psi_n \equiv (x = n)$ for $n \in \mathbb{N} \subseteq \m
athbb{Q} \subseteq \mathbb{U}$. These are pairwise inconsistent, each implies $x
 = x$, and each defines a singleton, hence is algebraic, so $\mathrm{MR}(\psi_n)
 = 0 \geq 0$. Since there are infinitely many of them, $\mathrm{MR}(x = x) \geq
1$.

**Step 2: $\mathrm{MR}(x = x) \not\geq 2$.**

Suppose for contradiction that $\mathrm{MR}(x = x) \geq 2$. Then there exist inf
initely many pairwise inconsistent formulas $\chi_i$, each implying $x = x$, wit
h $\mathrm{MR}(\chi_i) \geq 1$.

A formula $\chi_i$ in one variable with $\mathrm{MR}(\chi_i) \geq 1$ cannot defi
ne a finite set (finite sets have Morley rank $0$). By the quantifier-eliminatio
n structure above, every definable subset of $\mathbb{A}^1$ is finite or cofinit
e; since $\chi_i$ is not finite, it must be **cofinite**.

But any two cofinite subsets of $\mathbb{A}^1$ have cofinite (hence nonempty, in
deed infinite) intersection. Therefore no two of the $\chi_i$ can be pairwise in
consistent — contradicting the requirement that they be pairwise inconsistent. (
In fact we cannot even find *two* disjoint cofinite definable sets, let alone in
finitely many.)

Hence $\mathrm{MR}(x = x) \not\geq 2$.

**Conclusion.** Combining Steps 1 and 2,

$$\mathrm{MR}(x = x) = 1.$$

This agrees with the general principle that in $ACF$ the Morley rank of a defina
ble set equals its Krull (Zariski) dimension: the affine line $\mathbb{A}^1$ has
 dimension $1$.
