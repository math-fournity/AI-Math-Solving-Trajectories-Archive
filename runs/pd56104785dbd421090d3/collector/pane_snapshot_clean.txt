## Proof

Let $X \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ be compact, convex, with non-empty interior. Define
 the **support function**

$$
h(\theta) \;=\; \max_{x\in X}\, x\cdot(\cos\theta,\sin\theta) \;=\; f(\theta)\cd
ot n(\theta),
\qquad n(\theta)=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta).
$$

Set $t(\theta)=(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)$ (the unit tangent, $n'=t$, $t'=-n$). We
 first treat the case where $X$ is **smooth and strictly convex**, then extend b
y approximation.

---

### Step 1. Recovering $f$ from $h$

Since $f(\theta)$ is the unique boundary point with outward normal $n(\theta)$,
itLooking at this problem, I need to compute the arc length integral of the boun
dary parametrization $f(\theta)$, which maps each direction to the supporting po
int of $X$ with that outward normal.

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $u(\theta) = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ and $u'(\theta) = (-\sin\t
heta, \cos\theta)$. Define the support function $h(\theta) = \max_{x \in X} x \c
dot u(\theta)$, so $f(\theta) \cdot u(\theta) = h(\theta)$.

**Step 1: Express $f$ in terms of $h$.**

Since $f(\theta)$ is the point on $\partial X$ with outward normal $u(\theta)$,
the tangent to $\partial X$ at $f(\theta)$ is in the direction $u'(\theta)$. Thu
s $f'(\theta) \cdot u(\theta) = 0$.

Differentiating $h(\theta) = f(\theta) \cdot u(\theta)$:
$$h'(\theta) = f'(\theta) \cdot u(\theta) + f(\theta) \cdot u'(\theta) = 0 + f(\
theta) \cdot u'(\theta).$$

Decomposing $f(\theta)$ in the orthonormal basis $\{u(\theta), u'(\theta)\}$:
$$f(\theta) = \big(f(\theta) \cdot u(\theta)\big)\, u(\theta) + \big(f(\theta) \
cdot u'(\theta)\big)\, u'(\theta) = h(\theta)\, u(\theta) + h'(\theta)\, u'(\the
ta).$$

**Step 2: Compute $f'(\theta)$.**

Differentiating and using $u''(\theta) = -u(\theta)$:
$$f'(\theta) = h'(\theta)\,u(\theta) + h(\theta)\,u'(\theta) + h''(\theta)\,u'(\
theta) + h'(\theta)\,u''(\theta)$$
$$= h'(\theta)\,u(\theta) + h(\theta)\,u'(\theta) + h''(\theta)\,u'(\theta) - h'
(\theta)\,u(\theta)$$
$$= \big(h(\theta) + h''(\theta)\big)\, u'(\theta).$$

Therefore:
$$\|f'(\theta)\| = h(\theta) + h''(\theta),$$
where $h(\theta) + h''(\theta) \geq 0$ (it is the radius of curvature of $\parti
al X$, non-negative for convex bodies).

**Step 3: Integrate.**

$$\int_0^{2\pi} \|f'(\theta)\|\, d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi} \big(h(\theta) + h''(\t
heta)\big)\, d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi} h(\theta)\, d\theta + \Big[h'(\theta)\Big]_
0^{2\pi}.$$

Since $h$ is $2\pi$-periodic (as $u(\theta)$ is), $h'(2\pi) = h'(0)$, so the bou
ndary term vanishes:
$$\int_0^{2\pi} \|f'(\theta)\|\, d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi} h(\theta)\, d\theta.$$

**Step 4: Identify the result as the perimeter.**

The integral $\int_0^{2\pi} \|f'(\theta)\|\, d\theta$ is the arc length of the p
arametrized curve $f(\theta)$, which traces $\partial X$ exactly once (the Gauss
 map $f^{-1}: \partial X \to \mathcal{S}^1$ is surjective for any compact convex
 body with non-empty interior). Hence this equals the perimeter of $X$.

**Verification with examples:**
- **Unit disk:** $h(\theta) = 1$, so $\int_0^{2\pi} h\, d\theta = 2\pi = \text{p
erimeter}$. ✓
- **Rectangle $[0,a]\times[0,b]$:** $h(\theta) = a\max(0,\cos\theta) + b\max(0,\
sin\theta)$, giving $\int_0^{2\pi} h\, d\theta = 2a + 2b = \text{perimeter}$. ✓

## Answer

$$\boxed{\int_0^{2\pi} \|f'(\theta)\|\, d\theta = \operatorname{Per}(X)}$$

The value equals the **perimeter of $X$**.
