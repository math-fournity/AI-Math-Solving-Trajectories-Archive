## Counterexample

Let $n = 1$, $T = 1$. Set $f \equiv 0$ and $g \equiv 1$ (constant functions, whi
ch are in $C^1(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})$). Let $x_p(s) = 0$ and $x(s) = 0$ for all
 $s \in [0,1]$ and all $p \geq 1$. Let $A_p = [0,\, 1/p]$.

**Verification of hypotheses:**
- $f, g \in C^1(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})$: yes, both are constant.
- $A_p \subset [0,1]$ and $\mu(A_p) = 1/p \to 0$: yes.
- $x_p, x \in C^1([0,1],\mathbb{R})$ and $x_p \to x$ uniformly: yes, trivially (
$x_p = x \equiv 0$).

**Evaluation of the conclusion:** Since $g(x_p(s)) - f(x_p(s)) = 1 - 0 = 1$ for
all $s$ and $p$,
$$\frac{1}{\mu(A_p)}\int_{A_p} |g(x_p(s)) - f(x_p(s))|\,ds = \frac{1}{1/p}\int_0
^{1/p} 1\,ds = p \cdot \frac{1}{p} = 1 \quad \text{for all } p.$$
This does **not** converge to $0$. Hence the conclusion does not follow from the
 stated hypotheses. $\square$

## Characterization: When the conclusion *does* hold

The conclusion holds for **all** sequences $(A_p)$ with $\mu(A_p)\to 0$ **if and
 only if** $g(x(s)) = f(x(s))$ for every $s \in [0,T]$.

**Sufficiency ($g \circ x = f \circ x \Rightarrow$ conclusion holds):**

Since $x_p \to x$ uniformly and $f, g$ are continuous, the compositions $g \circ
\, x_p \to g \circ\, x$ and $f \circ\, x_p \to f \circ\, x$ uniformly on $[0,T]$
. If $g(x(s)) = f(x(s))$ for all $s$, then
$$\|g(x_p(s)) - f(x_p(s))\| \;\leq\; \|g(x_p(s)) - g(x(s))\| + \|f(x(s)) - f(x_p
(s))\| \;\to\; 0$$
uniformly in $s$. Therefore, setting $M_p := \sup_{s \in [0,T]}\|g(x_p(s)) - f(x
_p(s))\|$, we have $M_p \to 0$, and
$$\frac{1}{\mu(A_p)}\int_{A_p}\|g(x_p(s)) - f(x_p(s))\|\,ds \;\leq\; M_p \;\to\;
 0,$$
regardless of the choice of $A_p$.

**Necessity (conclusion holds for all $(A_p)$ $\Rightarrow$ $g \circ x = f \circ
 x$):**

We prove the contrapositive. Suppose there exists $s_0 \in [0,T]$ with $h(s_0) >
 0$, where $h(s) := \|g(x(s)) - f(x(s))\|$ is continuous. By continuity, there e
xist $\delta > 0$ and $c > 0$ such that $h(s) \geq c$ on $I := [s_0 - \delta,\,
s_0 + \delta] \cap [0,T]$.

Define $h_p(s) := \|g(x_p(s)) - f(x_p(s))\|$. As shown above, $h_p \to h$ unifor
mly. Choose $A_p = [s_0,\, s_0 + \varepsilon_p] \cap [0,T]$ with $\varepsilon_p
\to 0$ and $\varepsilon_p \leq \delta$ (adjusting if $s_0 = T$ by using a left-n
eighborhood). Then $\mu(A_p) \to 0$ and we decompose:
$$\frac{1}{\mu(A_p)}\int_{A_p} h_p(s)\,ds = \underbrace{\frac{1}{\mu(A_p)}\int_{
A_p} h(s)\,ds}_{\text{(I)}} + \underbrace{\frac{1}{\mu(A_p)}\int_{A_p}\bigl(h_p(
s) - h(s)\bigr)\,ds}_{\text{(II)}}.$$

- **Term (II):** $|\text{(II)}| \leq \|h_p - h\|_\infty \to 0$.
- **Term (I):** By continuity of $h$ at $s_0$ (Lebesgue differentiation theorem
/ elementary continuity argument), $\frac{1}{\mu(A_p)}\int_{A_p} h(s)\,ds \to h(
s_0) > 0$.

Therefore the full expression converges to $h(s_0) > 0 \neq 0$, so the conclusio
n **fails** for this choice of $(A_p)$. $\square$

## Summary

The stated hypotheses are **insufficient** to guarantee $\frac{1}{\mu(A_p)}\int_
{A_p}\|g(x_p(s)) - f(x_p(s))\|\,ds \to 0$. The convergence holds for all such se
quences $(A_p)$ if and only if the additional condition $g \circ\, x = f \circ\,
 x$ on $[0,T]$ is satisfied. The counterexample above ($f \equiv 0$, $g \equiv 1
$, $x_p \equiv x \equiv 0$, $A_p = [0,1/p]$) demonstrates the failure concretely
, yielding a limit of $1$ rather than $0$.
