## The Dehn Function

Let \(G=\langle S\mid R\rangle\) be a finitely presented group. Write \(F(S)\) f
or the free group on \(S\), and let \(\pi:F(S)\to G\) be the quotient map, so th
at \(G\cong F(S)/\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle\), where \(\langle\!\langle
R\rangle\!\rangle\) is the normal closure of \(R\) in \(F(S)\).

### Van Kampen diagrams and area

Fix a word \(w\) in the generators \(S\cup S^{-1}\) with \(\pi(w)=1\in G\), i.e.
 \(w\in\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle\). A **van Kampen diagram** \(D\) for
\(w\) over the presentation \(\langle S\mid R\rangle\) is a finite, connected, s
imply connected, planar 2-dimensional CW-complex together with a labeling of eac
h oriented edge by a letter of \(S\cup S^{-1}\) (with orientation reversal inver
ting the label), such that:

1. Reading the labels around the boundary of each 2-cell (face), following the o
rientation, gives a word that is a cyclic conjugate of an element of \(R\cup R^{
-1}\cup\{1\}\).
2. Reading the labels around the boundary of the whole diagram (with the induced
 outer orientation) gives the word \(w\) (up to free reduction / insertion of tr
ivial subwords, depending on the convention).

The **area** of the diagram \(D\), denoted \(\operatorname{Area}(D)\), is the nu
mber of 2-cells (faces) it contains. Equivalently, it counts how many relator-ti
les are glued together to fill the loop labelled by \(w\).

The **area of the word** \(w\) is then

\[
\operatorname{Area}(w)\;=\;\min\bigl\{\operatorname{Area}(D)\;:\;D\text{ is a va
n Kampen diagram for }w\bigr\}.
\]

This minimum is finite because \(w\in\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle\), so \(
w\) can be expressed as a product of conjugates of elements of \(R\cup R^{-1}\);
 each such conjugate corresponds to one 2-cell, and the diagram is the geometric
 realization of that expression. Taking the minimum corresponds to choosing the
most efficient filling.

### Definition of the Dehn function

The **Dehn function** of \(G\) (with respect to the finite presentation \(\langl
e S\mid R\rangle\)) is

\[
\operatorname{Dehn}_{G}(n)\;=\;\max\bigl\{\,\operatorname{Area}(w)\;:\;w\text{ a
 word over }S\cup S^{-1},\;\ell(w)\le n,\;\pi(w)=1\,\bigr\},
\]

where \(\ell(w)\) is the free length of \(w\) (number of letters). In words: amo
ng all words of length at most \(n\) that represent the identity in \(G\), take
the one that is hardest to fill, and record the area of its most efficient filli
ng. That worst-case area is \(\operatorname{Dehn}_{G}(n)\).

### How it measures the difficulty of the word problem

The word problem for \(G\) asks: given a word \(w\) of length \(\le n\), decide
whether \(\pi(w)=1\). A positive certificate that \(\pi(w)=1\) is precisely a va
n Kampen diagram for \(w\); its area is the size of that certificate. Hence:

- \(\operatorname{Dehn}_{G}(n)\) is the **minimal worst-case size of a certifica
te** needed to prove that a word of length \(\le n\) is trivial.
- The time/space complexity of any "filling-based" algorithm for the word proble
m is bounded below by the growth rate of \(\operatorname{Dehn}_{G}\). Concretely
, a naive Dehn-reduction / diagram-search algorithm runs in time polynomial in \
(\operatorname{Dehn}_{G}(n)\) (one must enumerate diagrams of area up to \(\oper
atorname{Dehn}_{G}(n)\)), so the asymptotic growth of the Dehn function controls
 the algorithmic difficulty:
  - \(\operatorname{Dehn}_{G}(n)\preceq n\) (linear) — e.g. hyperbolic groups —
the word problem is solvable in linear time.
  - \(\operatorname{Dehn}_{G}(n)\preceq n^{2}\) — automatic groups, including vi
rtually abelian and many Coxeter groups.
  - \(\operatorname{Dehn}_{G}(n)\preceq n^{\alpha}\) for various \(\alpha\), or
\(\exp(n)\), or worse — correspondingly harder word problems.
  - \(\operatorname{Dehn}_{G}\) grows faster than every recursive function exact
ly when the word problem is **unsolvable** for that finitely presented group.

Here \(\preceq\) denotes the usual preorder: \(f\preceq g\) iff \(f(n)\le C\,g(C
n)+C\) for some constant \(C\) and all large \(n\). The Dehn function is well-de
fined up to this equivalence because changing the finite presentation only chang
es \(\operatorname{Dehn}_{G}\) by a \(\preceq\)-equivalence (Tietze transformati
ons modify area by at most a linear-in-\(n\) multiplicative factor and a polynom
ial distortion of length).

### Summary of the mechanism

A word \(w\) of length \(\le n\) representing the identity is a closed loop in t
he Cayley 2-complex of \(\langle S\mid R\rangle\). Reducing \(w\) to the identit
y means contracting this loop to a point inside the complex. Each 2-cell (each r
elator) is a "move" that fills in a piece of the loop. The **area** is the numbe
r of such moves needed in the most economical filling. The Dehn function takes t
he maximum such area over all loops of perimeter \(\le n\), and thereby quantifi
es, as a function of input length, how hard it is to certify triviality — i.e. h
ow hard the word problem is.
