# Problem

A master landscape architect is designing a private botanical garden on a vast, flat meadow. She plans to define various themed planting zones by installing three distinct, continuous decorative borders that can overlap or cross one another.

The first border is a perfectly circular stone path. The second border is a rectangular koi pond edge. The third border is a triangular hedge row. 

Any area of the meadow enclosed by these borders (or portions of them) is considered a distinct "region," and regions are separated from one another whenever a border segment lies between them. By strategically positioning the circle, the rectangle, and the triangle, what is the greatest number of disjoint regions the architect can create within the meadow?
