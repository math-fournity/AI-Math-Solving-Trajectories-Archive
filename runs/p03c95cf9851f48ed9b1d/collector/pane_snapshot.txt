## Setup and Framing

Each component $K_i = \{p_i\} \times S^1$ is a fiber of the projection $\pi: S^2
 \times S^1 \to S^2$. Since $[K_i] \in H_1(S^2 \times S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ is
the generator, $K_i$ admits no Seifert surface, so the natural framing is the **
product framing**: the longitude is $\lambda_i = \{p_i'\} \times S^1$ (push-off
in the $S^2$ direction) and the meridian is $\mu_i = \partial D^2_i \times \{\ma
thrm{pt}\}$ (boundary of a small disk $D^2_i \ni p_i$ in $S^2$).

We perform **0-surgery** (with respect to the product framing) on all $n$ compon
ents: the meridian of each glued-in solid torus maps to $\lambda_i$.

## Computing the Complement

 \Sigma_{0,n} \times S^1,$$
where $\Sigma_{0,n}$ is a 2-sphere with $n$ open disks removed (a genus-0 surfac

## Fundamental Group via van Kampen

**Complement:** $\pi_1(C) = \pi_1(\Sigma_{0,n}) \times \pi_1(S^1) = F_{n-1} \tim

**Boundary inclusions:** On each torus $T_i = \partial D^2_i \times S^1$:

**Gluing in solid tori:** Each $V_i = D^2 \times S^1$ is glued with its meridian

$$\boxed{\pi_1(M) = F_{n-1}}$$

(the free group on $n-1$ generators; trivial when $n=1$).

## Homology Verification

$$H_1(M) = \mathbb{Z}^{n-1}.$$

**For $H_2$:** The map $H_2(\bigsqcup T_i) = \mathbb{Z}^n \to H_2(C) = \mathbb{Z

$$H_2(M) = \mathbb{Z}^{n-1}.$$

This is consistent: $H_1(M) = H_2(M) = \mathbb{Z}^{n-1}$, matching the abelianiz

## Identifying the Manifold

**Key observations:**

1. **$M$ is not aspherical:** $H_2(M) = \mathbb{Z}^{n-1} \neq 0$ for $n \geq 2$,
 while $H_2(F_{n-1}) = 0$ (free groups have cohomological dimension 1). So $M$ i
s not a $K(F_{n-1}, 1)$.

2. **Sphere theorem:** Since $\pi_2(M) \neq 0$ (as $M$ is not aspherical and $\p
i_1$ is nontrivial for $n \geq 2$), the sphere theorem guarantees an essential e
mbedded 2-sphere, so $M$ decomposes as a nontrivial connected sum.

3. **Kneser–Milnor prime decomposition:** $M \cong P_1 \# \cdots \# P_k$ with $\
pi_1(M) = \pi_1(P_1) * \cdots * \pi_1(P_k)$ (free product).

4. **Grushko decomposition:** $F_{n-1}$ decomposes uniquely as a free product of
 $n-1$ copies of $\mathbb{Z}$ (its freely indecomposable factors). Each factor $
\mathbb{Z}$ must arise from a prime summand $P_i$ with $\pi_1(P_i) = \mathbb{Z}$
.

5. **Classification:** The only closed, orientable, prime 3-manifold with $\pi_1
 = \mathbb{Z}$ is $S^2 \times S^1$. (Lens spaces have finite $\pi_1$; $K(\pi,1)$
 manifolds with $\pi_1 = \mathbb{Z}$ don't exist in dimension 3 since $S^1$ has
cohomological dimension 1.)

6. **Poincaré conjecture (Perelman):** Any simply-connected prime summand is $S^
3$, which is trivial in the connected sum.

Therefore:

$$\boxed{M \cong \#\,(n-1)\,(S^2 \times S^1)}$$
