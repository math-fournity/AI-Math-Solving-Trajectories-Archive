### Step 1: Compute the area $S(a)$

$$S(a)=\int_0^1 \frac{a^{4}}{(a^{2}-x^{2})^{3/2}}\,dx = a^{4}\int_0^1 (a^{2}-x^{
2})^{-3/2}\,dx.$$

**Substitution:** $x=a\sin\theta$, $dx=a\cos\theta\,d\theta$. Limits: $x=0\Right
arrow\theta=0$; $x=1\Rightarrow\theta=\arcsin(1/a)$.

$$a^{2}-x^{2}=a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta,\qquad (a^{2}-x^{2})^{-3/2}=a^{-3}\sec^{3}\the

Therefore

$$\int (a^{2}-x^{2})^{-3/2}\,dx = \int a^{-3}\sec^{3}\theta\cdot a\cos\theta\,d\

Evaluating,

$$S(a)=a^{4}\cdot a^{-2}\Big[\tan\theta\Big]_0^{\arcsin(1/a)}=a^{2}\tan\!\big(\a

$$\boxed{S(a)=\frac{a^{2}}{\sqrt{a^{2}-1}}.}$$

### Step 2: Find the extremal value of $S(a)$ for $a>1$

Let $f(a)=\dfrac{a^{2}}{\sqrt{a^{2}-1}}=a^{2}(a^{2}-1)^{-1/2}$.

Setting $f'(a)=0$ with $a>1$ gives $a^{2}=2$, i.e. $a=\sqrt{2}$.
