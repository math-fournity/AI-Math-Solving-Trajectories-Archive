## Proof

**Setup and symmetry.** The two hyperbolas $xy=1$ and $xy=-1$ bound the region $
R=\{(x,y): |xy|\le 1\}$. An ellipse "enclosed by" them is an ellipse contained i
n $R$ and tangent to both hyperbolas. We maximize its area.

The region $R$ is invariant under the Klein four-group generated by $(x,y)\mapst
o(-x,-y)$ and $(x,y)\mapsto(y,x)$. By symmetrization, a maximizing ellipse may b
e taken invariant under this group, hence it is centered at the origin and symme
tric in $x,y$, with equation
$$a(x^2+y^2)+2bxy=1,\qquad a^2>b^2.$$

**Diagonalize.** Set $p=\frac{x+y}{\sqrt2},\ q=\frac{x-y}{\sqrt2}$ (a rotation,
so area is preserved and $x^2+y^2=p^2+q^2$, $xy=\frac{p^2-q^2}{2}$). The ellipse
 becomes
$$(a+b)\,p^2+(a-b)\,q^2=1,$$
with semi-axes $P=\frac1{\sqrt{a+b}}$, $Q=\frac1{\sqrt{a-b}}$, and area $\pi P Q
$.

**Tangency constraint.** Parametrize the boundary by $p=P\cos\theta,\ q=Q\sin\th
eta$. Then
$$xy=\tfrac12\bigl(P^2\cos^2\theta-Q^2\sin^2\theta\bigr),$$
which ranges over $\bigl[-\tfrac{Q^2}{2},\,\tfrac{P^2}{2}\bigr]$. For the ellips
e to lie in $R$ we need
$$\tfrac{P^2}{2}\le 1\quad\text{and}\quad \tfrac{Q^2}{2}\le 1,\qquad\text{i.e. }
 P^2\le 2,\ Q^2\le 2.$$
Tangency to both hyperbolas forces both inequalities to be equalities: $P^2=Q^2=
2$.

**Maximize.** The area $\pi P Q$ is maximized when $P$ and $Q$ are as large as p
ossible, i.e. $P=Q=\sqrt2$, giving
$$\text{Area}_{\max}=\pi\cdot\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt2=2\pi.$$

**Verification.** With $P=Q=\sqrt2$ the ellipse is $p^2+q^2=2$, i.e. $x^2+y^2=2$
, a circle of radius $\sqrt2$. On it, $xy\le\frac{x^2+y^2}{2}=1$ (AM–GM), with e
quality at $(\pm1,\pm1)$; likewise $xy\ge -1$, with equality at $(1,-1),(-1,1)$.
 So the circle is tangent to $xy=1$ at $(1,1),(-1,-1)$ and to $xy=-1$ at $(1,-1)
,(-1,1)$, and lies entirely in $R$. Its area is $2\pi$.

The largest area is $\boxed{2\pi}$.
