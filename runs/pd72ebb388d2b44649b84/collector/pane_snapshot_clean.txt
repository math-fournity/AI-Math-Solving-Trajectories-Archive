Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether uniform convergence on comp
act subsets, combined with $\int_\Omega f_k = 1$, forces $\int_\Omega f = 1$.

**Answer: No.** The key obstruction is that uniform convergence on compact subse
ts gives no control over what happens near the boundary $\partial\Omega$. Mass c
an "escape to the boundary," so the limit can have a different integral.

---

## Counterexample

Take $n = 1$ and $\Omega = (0,1) \subset \mathbb{R}$, which is a bounded domain.

Define
$$
f_k(x) = k \cdot \mathbf{1}_{(0,\,1/k]}(x), \qquad x \in (0,1).
$$

**Step 1: Each $f_k \in L^1(\Omega)$ and $\int_\Omega f_k = 1$.**

We have $f_k \geq 0$ and
$$
\int_0^1 f_k(x)\,dx = \int_0^{1/k} k\,dx = k \cdot \frac{1}{k} = 1.
$$

**Step 2: Identify the limit $f$.**

Let $K \subset (0,1)$ be compact. Since $K$ is compact in $\mathbb{R}$ and $K \s
ubset (0,1)$, it is closed in $\mathbb{R}$ and bounded away from the endpoints:
there exists $\epsilon > 0$ such that $K \subset [\epsilon,\, 1-\epsilon]$.

For all $k > 1/\epsilon$, we have $1/k < \epsilon$, so $(0, 1/k] \cap [\epsilon,
 1-\epsilon] = \emptyset$. Therefore
$$
f_k(x) = 0 \quad \text{for all } x \in K, \quad \text{for all } k > 1/\epsilon.
$$

Setting $f \equiv 0$, we get
$$
\sup_{x \in K} |f_k(x) - f(x)| = 0 \quad \text{for all } k > 1/\epsilon.
$$

Thus $f_k \to f \equiv 0$ uniformly on every compact subset of $\Omega$. Clearly
 $f \in L^1(\Omega)$.

**Step 3: The integral of $f$ is not $1$.**

$$
\int_\Omega f(x)\,dx = \int_0^1 0\,dx = 0 \neq 1.
$$

---

## Conclusion

All hypotheses are satisfied—$\Omega$ is bounded, $f \in L^1(\Omega)$, $f_k \to
f$ uniformly on compact subsets of $\Omega$, and $\int_\Omega f_k = 1$ for every
 $k$—yet $\int_\Omega f = 0 \neq 1$.

The conclusion **fails** because uniform convergence on compact subsets provides
 no control over the behavior of $f_k$ near $\partial\Omega$. In this example, t
he entire mass of $f_k$ concentrates in the shrinking region $(0, 1/k]$ adjacent
 to the boundary point $x = 0$ and vanishes in the limit. The sequence $\{f_k\}$
 is not uniformly integrable, so the limit and the integral cannot be interchang
ed.

Therefore, **we cannot conclude that $\int_\Omega f = 1$**.
