# Proof

**Claim:** If $H$ is a minor of $G$, then $g(H) \leq g(G)$, where $g(\cdot)$ den
otes the (orientable) genus — the minimum genus of an orientable surface into wh
ich the graph embeds.

**Answer: Yes, it is always true.**

## Setup

A graph $H$ is a minor of $G$ if $H$ can be obtained from $G$ by a sequence of t
hree operations:

1. **Edge deletion** ($G \mapsto G - e$),
2. **Vertex deletion** ($G \mapsto G - v$),
3. **Edge contraction** ($G \mapsto G / e$).

It suffices to show that each of these three operations, applied to a graph embe
dded on a surface of genus $g$, yields a graph that still embeds on a surface of
 genus $\leq g$. Then by induction on the length of the minor sequence, $g(H) \l
eq g(G)$.

## Step 1: Edge deletion preserves embeddability

Suppose $G$ embeds on a surface $S$ of genus $g$. Remove the curve corresponding
 to edge $e$ from the drawing. The remaining drawing is a valid embedding of $G
- e$ on $S$. Hence $g(G - e) \leq g$. $\checkmark$

## Step 2: Vertex deletion preserves embeddability

Suppose $G$ embeds on $S$ of genus $g$. Remove the point corresponding to vertex
 $v$ and all curves corresponding to edges incident to $v$. The remaining drawin
g is a valid embedding of $G - v$ on $S$. Hence $g(G - v) \leq g$. $\checkmark$

## Step 3: Edge contraction preserves embeddability (the key step)

Suppose $G$ embeds on a surface $S$ of genus $g$, and let $e = uv$ be an edge of
 $G$. We show $G / e$ embeds on $S$.

Consider the embedding of $G$ on $S$. The edge $e$ is drawn as a simple arc $\ga
mma$ connecting points $u$ and $v$ on $S$. Take a small closed regular neighborh
ood $N$ of the arc $\gamma \cup \{u, v\}$. Topologically, $N$ is a closed disk o
n $S$ (it is a thin "ribbon" along $\gamma$ thickened to include small disks aro
und $u$ and $v$).

The boundary $\partial N$ is a single simple closed curve. The edges of $G$ inci
dent to $u$ (other than $e$) cross $\partial N$ near the $u$-end, and the edges
incident to $v$ (other than $e$) cross $\partial N$ near the $v$-end. No other e
dges or vertices of $G$ lie in the interior of $N$ (for $N$ chosen small enough)
.

Now perform the following topological operation entirely within the disk $N$:

- **Merge** the two points $u$ and $v$ into a single point $w$ (the new vertex o
f $G/e$), by continuously "sliding" $v$ along $\gamma$ to coincide with $u$.
- **Redirect** every edge formerly incident to $v$ so that it now connects to $w
$, by sliding its endpoint along $\gamma$ from the $v$-side to the $u$-side.

Since all of this happens inside the disk $N$ (which is topologically trivial —
a disk in any surface looks locally like a disk in the plane), and since the edg
es incident to $u$ and the edges incident to $v$ entered $N$ through **disjoint
arcs** on $\partial N$ (they are separated because $e$ is a single arc, not a lo
op, so $u \neq v$ and the incident edges cluster on opposite sides), we can rout
e all of them to $w$ without any two edges crossing.

Concretely: parametrize $\partial N$ cyclically. The edges incident to $u$ cross
 $\partial N$ in a contiguous block of arcs, and the edges incident to $v$ cross
 $\partial N$ in another contiguous block, and these two blocks are separated (b
ecause $\gamma$ connects them on "opposite sides"). Inside the disk $N$, we can
draw non-crossing arcs from every boundary crossing point to the single interior
 point $w$. This is always possible in a disk — it is the classical fact that a
set of arcs from boundary points to a single interior point of a disk can be dra
wn without crossing (a "star" from $w$).

Therefore $G/e$ embeds on $S$ without crossings, and $g(G/e) \leq g$. $\checkmar
k$

## Conclusion

Each minor operation (edge deletion, vertex deletion, edge contraction) takes a
graph embeddable on a surface of genus $g$ to a graph still embeddable on a surf
ace of genus $\leq g$. Since $H$ is obtained from $G$ by a finite sequence of th
ese operations,

$$g(H) \leq g(G).$$

Hence the genus of the embedding surface of a minor never exceeds the genus of t
he embedding surface of the original graph. $\blacksquare$
