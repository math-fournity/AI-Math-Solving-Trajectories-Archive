## Proof

**Interpretation.** The space $X$ is $\mathbb{R}^3$ with the $z$-axis removed, i
.e., $X = \mathbb{R}^3 \setminus \{(0,0,z) : z \in \mathbb{R}\}$. The circle $C
= \{(x,y,0) : x^2+y^2=1\}$ lies in $X$ and encircles the removed $z$-axis. We at
tach a 2-cell $D^2$ to $X$ via the attaching map $f: S^1 \to X$, $f(e^{i\theta})
 = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta, 0)$, forming $W = X \cup_f D^2$.

### Step 1: Compute $\pi_1(X)$

**Claim:** $X$ deformation retracts onto the unit circle $S^1 \subset \{z=0\}$.

Define $H: X \times [0,1] \to X$ by:
$$H\big((x,y,z),\, t\big) = \Big((1-t)\,x + t\,\tfrac{x}{r},\;\; (1-t)\,y + t\,\
tfrac{y}{r},\;\; (1-t)\,z\Big), \quad r = \sqrt{x^2+y^2} > 0.$$

- **Well-defined:** Since $(x,y) \neq (0,0)$ in $X$, we have $r > 0$. The $(x,y)
$-component of $H$ is $(1-t)(x,y) + t\,\frac{(x,y)}{r}$, a convex combination of
 two nonzero vectors in the same direction, hence always nonzero. So $H$ stays i
n $X$.
- **$t=0$:** $H((x,y,z),0) = (x,y,z)$.
- **$t=1$:** $H((x,y,z),1) = (x/r,\, y/r,\, 0) \in S^1$.
- **Fixed on $S^1$:** If $(x,y,z) = (x/r, y/r, 0)$ (i.e., on $S^1$), then $H$ is
 constant.

Therefore $X \simeq S^1$, and $\pi_1(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by the loop
 $\gamma(\theta) = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta, 0)$ — which is exactly the attaching
 circle $C$.

### Step 2: Apply the Seifert–van Kampen theorem

By the standard result on attaching 2-cells (Hatcher, Proposition 1.26): if $W =
 X \cup_f e^2$ where $f: S^1 \to X$ is the attaching map and $X$ is path-connect
ed, then

$$\pi_1(W) \cong \pi_1(X) \big/ \langle\!\langle [f] \rangle\!\rangle,$$

where $\langle\!\langle [f] \rangle\!\rangle$ is the **normal closure** of $[f]$
 in $\pi_1(X)$.

**Derivation via van Kampen:** Write $W = U \cup V$ where $U$ is a neighborhood
of $X$ in $W$ (deformation retracting to $X$) and $V$ is the interior of the att
ached 2-cell (contractible). Then $U \cap V$ is an annular neighborhood of the a
ttaching circle, deformation retracting to $S^1$, so $\pi_1(U \cap V) \cong \mat
hbb{Z}$. The inclusion-induced maps are:

- $\pi_1(U \cap V) \to \pi_1(U) \cong \mathbb{Z}$: sends generator to $[f]$, the
 class of the attaching loop.
- $\pi_1(U \cap V) \to \pi_1(V) = \{1\}$: trivial.

Van Kampen's theorem gives the amalgamated product:
$$\pi_1(W) \cong \pi_1(U) *_{\pi_1(U\cap V)} \pi_1(V) = \mathbb{Z} *_{\mathbb{Z}
} \{1\} \cong \mathbb{Z} / \langle\!\langle [f] \rangle\!\rangle.$$

### Step 3: Compute the quotient

The attaching map $f$ is the loop $\gamma(\theta) = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta, 0)$
, which is the **generator** of $\pi_1(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ (as established in S
tep 1). The normal closure of the generator in $\mathbb{Z}$ is:

$$\langle\!\langle 1 \rangle\!\rangle = \mathbb{Z}.$$

Therefore:
$$\pi_1(W) \cong \mathbb{Z} / \mathbb{Z} \cong \{1\}.$$

### Step 4: Geometric verification

The $z$-axis pierces the attached disk $D^2$ at exactly one point (the origin, w
hich lies inside the unit circle). After attaching $D^2$, any loop in $W$ that w
inds around the former $z$-axis can be homotoped to the boundary circle $C = \pa
rtial D^2$, and then contracted across the 2-cell $D^2$. This confirms that the
generator of $\pi_1(X)$ is killed in $W$, making $W$ simply connected.

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{\pi_1(W) \cong \{1\} \text{ (the trivial group).}}$$
