## Solution

We find the resolvent kernel $R(x,t;\lambda)$ of the Volterra integral equation
with kernel $K(x,t)\equiv 1$.

### Resolvent via iterated kernels

For a Volterra integral equation, the resolvent kernel is given by the Neumann s
eries

$$R(x,t;\lambda)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{n-1}K_n(x,t),$$

where the iterated kernels $K_n$ are defined recursively by

$$K_1(x,t)=K(x,t),\qquad K_{n+1}(x,t)=\int_t^x K(x,s)\,K_n(s,t)\,ds.$$

### Computing the iterated kernels

With $K(x,t)\equiv 1$:

- $K_1(x,t)=1$.
- $K_2(x,t)=\displaystyle\int_t^x 1\cdot 1\,ds = x-t$.
- $K_3(x,t)=\displaystyle\int_t^x 1\cdot(s-t)\,ds = \frac{(x-t)^2}{2}$.
- $K_4(x,t)=\displaystyle\int_t^x 1\cdot\frac{(s-t)^2}{2}\,ds = \frac{(x-t)^3}{2
\cdot 3}=\frac{(x-t)^3}{3!}$.

**Induction.** Suppose $K_n(x,t)=\dfrac{(x-t)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}$. Then

$$K_{n+1}(x,t)=\int_t^x \frac{(s-t)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\,ds=\frac{(x-t)^n}{n!}.$$

So by induction, for all $n\ge 1$,

$$K_n(x,t)=\frac{(x-t)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}.$$

### Summing the series

Substituting into the Neumann series:

$$R(x,t;\lambda)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{n-1}\frac{(x-t)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}=\sum
_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{[\lambda(x-t)]^m}{m!}=e^{\lambda(x-t)}.$$

The series converges for all $\lambda$ and all $x,t$ (entire function), so the r
esolvent exists for every $\lambda$ — as expected for a Volterra equation, whose
 resolvent is an entire function of $\lambda$.

### Verification

The resolvent must satisfy the resolvent equation

$$R(x,t;\lambda)=K(x,t)+\lambda\int_t^x K(x,s)\,R(s,t;\lambda)\,ds.$$

With $K\equiv 1$ and $R=e^{\lambda(x-t)}$:

$$1+\lambda\int_t^x e^{\lambda(s-t)}\,ds=1+\lambda\cdot\frac{e^{\lambda(x-t)}-1}
{\lambda}=1+e^{\lambda(x-t)}-1=e^{\lambda(x-t)}=R(x,t;\lambda).\ \checkmark$$

### Result

$$\boxed{R(x,t;\lambda)=e^{\lambda(x-t)}}$$
