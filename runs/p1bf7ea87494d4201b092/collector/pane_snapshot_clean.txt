We solve the initial-value problem

$$u_{tt} - u_{txx} + u_{xxxx} = 0,\qquad u(x,0)=f(x),\quad u_t(x,0)=g(x),$$

on $\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty)$, assuming $f,g$ are sufficiently nice (e.g. Schw
artz) so that the Fourier transform in $x$ is justified.

### Step 1. Fourier transform in $x$

Let $\widehat{u}(k,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}u(x,t)e^{-ikx}\,dx$. Under this tra
nsform

$$\partial_x \leftrightarrow ik,\qquad \partial_{xx}\leftrightarrow -k^{2},\qqua
d \partial_{xxxx}\leftrightarrow k^{4},$$

so the PDE becomes, for each fixed $k\in\mathbb{R}$, an ODE in $t$:

$$\widehat{u}_{tt}+k^{2}\,\widehat{u}_{t}+k^{4}\,\widehat{u}=0,\qquad \widehat{u
}(k,0)=\widehat{f}(k),\quad \widehat{u}_{t}(k,0)=\widehat{g}(k). \tag{1}$$

### Step 2. Solve the ODE (1)

The characteristic equation is $r^{2}+k^{2}r+k^{4}=0$, with roots

$$r_{1,2}=\frac{-k^{2}\pm\sqrt{k^{4}-4k^{4}}}{2}=\frac{k^{2}}{2}\bigl(-1\pm i\sq
rt{3}\bigr)=\omega_{1,2}\,k^{2},$$

where

$$\omega_{1}=\frac{-1+i\sqrt{3}}{2}=e^{2\pi i/3},\qquad \omega_{2}=\frac{-1-i\sq
rt{3}}{2}=e^{-2\pi i/3}$$

are the two primitive cube roots of unity ($\omega_{1,2}^{2}+\omega_{1,2}+1=0$,
$\omega_{1}\omega_{2}=1$, $\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}=-1$).

Note $\operatorname{Re}\omega_{1}=\operatorname{Re}\omega_{2}=-\tfrac12<0$, so b
oth modes decay in $t$ for $k\neq0$.

For $k\neq0$ the general solution is $\widehat{u}=A\,e^{r_{1}t}+B\,e^{r_{2}t}$.
Imposing the initial data:

$$A+B=\widehat{f},\qquad r_{1}A+r_{2}B=\widehat{g}.$$

Since $r_{1}-r_{2}=(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})k^{2}=i\sqrt{3}\,k^{2}$,

$$A=\frac{\widehat{g}-\omega_{2}k^{2}\widehat{f}}{i\sqrt{3}\,k^{2}},\qquad B=\fr
ac{\omega_{1}k^{2}\widehat{f}-\widehat{g}}{i\sqrt{3}\,k^{2}}.$$

Hence

$$\widehat{u}(k,t)=\frac{1}{i\sqrt{3}\,k^{2}}\Bigl[\widehat{g}(k)\bigl(e^{\omega
_{1}k^{2}t}-e^{\omega_{2}k^{2}t}\bigr)+k^{2}\widehat{f}(k)\bigl(\omega_{1}e^{\om
ega_{2}k^{2}t}-\omega_{2}e^{\omega_{1}k^{2}t}\bigr)\Bigr]. \tag{2}$$

For $k=0$ equation (1) reduces to $\widehat{u}_{tt}=0$, giving the consistent li
mit

$$\widehat{u}(0,t)=\widehat{f}(0)+t\,\widehat{g}(0),$$

which is exactly the $k\to0$ limit of (2) (expand the exponentials to first orde
r).

### Step 3. Inverse transform — the solution

$$\boxed{\;u(x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{u}(k,t)\,e^{ikx}
\,dk\;}$$

with $\widehat{u}(k,t)$ given by (2) and $\widehat{f},\widehat{g}$ the Fourier t
ransforms of the data. Equivalently, using convolution notation with $F^{-1}$ de
noting the inverse Fourier transform,

$$u(x,t)=\bigl(K_{1}(\cdot,t)*g\bigr)(x)+\bigl(K_{2}(\cdot,t)*f\bigr)(x),$$

where the kernels are defined by

$$K_{1}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{\omega_{1}k^{2}t}-e^{
\omega_{2}k^{2}t}}{i\sqrt{3}\,k^{2}}\,e^{ikx}\,dk,$$

$$K_{2}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\omega_{1}e^{\omega_{2}k
^{2}t}-\omega_{2}e^{\omega_{1}k^{2}t}}{i\sqrt{3}}\,e^{ikx}\,dk.$$

### Step 4. Verification

* **PDE.** Each factor $e^{\omega_{j}k^{2}t}e^{ikx}$ satisfies the transformed e
quation $r^{2}+k^{2}r+k^{4}=0$ with $r=\omega_{j}k^{2}$, since $\omega_{j}^{2}+\
omega_{j}+1=0$ gives $(\omega_{j}k^{2})^{2}+k^{2}(\omega_{j}k^{2})+k^{4}=k^{4}(\
omega_{j}^{2}+\omega_{j}+1)=0$. By linearity so does $u$.
* **Initial data.** At $t=0$: $e^{0}-e^{0}=0$ kills the $\widehat{g}$ term, and
$\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}=i\sqrt{3}$ makes the $\widehat{f}$ term equal $\widehat{f
}$, recovering $u(x,0)=f(x)$. Differentiating (2) in $t$ and setting $t=0$: the
$\widehat{f}$ contribution is $\omega_{1}\omega_{2}k^{2}-\omega_{2}\omega_{1}k^{
2}=0$ (using $\omega_{1}\omega_{2}=1$), and the $\widehat{g}$ contribution is $(
\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})k^{2}/(i\sqrt{3}k^{2})=1$, recovering $u_{t}(x,0)=g(x)$.
* **Well-posedness.** Since $\operatorname{Re}\omega_{1,2}=-\tfrac12$, the facto
r $e^{\omega_{j}k^{2}t}$ decays like $e^{-k^{2}t/2}$; the integrals converge abs
olutely for $t>0$, giving a smooth solution that depends continuously on the dat
a.
