## Proof

Take the constant function $f(x) = 1$ for all $x \in [0,\infty)$. This is contin
uous on $[0,\infty)$.

For any integer $n \geq 1$, the integral becomes
$$I_n = \int_0^{\infty} \left(\frac{x^2}{1+x^2}\right)^n dx.$$

**Key observation.** For $x \geq 1$ we have $1 + x^2 \leq x^2 + x^2 = 2x^2$, so
$$\frac{x^2}{1+x^2} \geq \frac{x^2}{2x^2} = \frac{1}{2}.$$

Since $t \mapsto t^n$ is increasing on $[0,\infty)$, for all $x \geq 1$,
$$\left(\frac{x^2}{1+x^2}\right)^n \geq \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n.$$

Therefore
$$I_n = \int_0^{\infty} \left(\frac{x^2}{1+x^2}\right)^n dx \geq \int_1^{\infty}
 \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n dx = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n \cdot \infty = +\inf
ty.$$

Hence $I_n$ diverges for every integer $n \geq 1$.

**Remark on the underlying mechanism.** The weight $w(x) = x^2/(1+x^2)$ satisfie
s $w(x) \to 1$ as $x \to \infty$, and in fact $w(x) \geq 1/2$ for all $x \geq 1$
. Consequently $w(x)^n$ is bounded below by the positive constant $(1/2)^n$ on $
[1,\infty)$. Any nonnegative continuous $f$ with $\int_1^\infty f(x)\,dx = +\inf
ty$ therefore yields a divergent weighted integral for every $n \geq 1$. The cho
ice $f \equiv 1$ is the simplest instance.
