## Answer

**No.** Not every division algebra over $E$ is of the form $D \otimes_F E$ for a
 division algebra $D$ over $F$. Here is a counterexample.

---

### Counterexample

Let $F = \mathbb{C}$ (algebraically closed) and $E = \mathbb{C}(x,y)$, the ratio
nal function field in two independent variables.

**Step 1: $\operatorname{Br}(\mathbb{C}) = 0$.**

Since $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraically closed, every finite-dimensional central div
ision algebra over $\mathbb{C}$ is $\mathbb{C}$ itself (a classical consequence
of Wedderburn's structure theorem: any central simple algebra over an algebraica
lly closed field is a matrix algebra, hence the only central *division* algebra
is the field itself).

**Step 2: Construct a non-trivial division algebra over $E = \mathbb{C}(x,y)$.**

Consider the **quaternion algebra**
$$A = (x,\, y)_{\mathbb{C}(x,y)},$$
the $4$-dimensional $E$-algebra with $E$-basis $\{1, i, j, ij\}$ and relations
$$i^2 = x,\quad j^2 = y,\quad ij = -ji.$$

**Claim:** $A$ is a division algebra.

*Proof of claim.* The quaternion algebra $(a,b)_K$ over a field $K$ (with $\oper
atorname{char} K \neq 2$) is a division algebra if and only if $a$ is **not** a
norm from the quadratic extension $K(\sqrt{b})/K$.

Here $K = \mathbb{C}(x,y)$, $a = x$, $b = y$. The extension $L = K(\sqrt{y}) = \
mathbb{C}(x,y)(\sqrt{y})$ is a non-trivial quadratic extension (since $y$ is not
 a square in $\mathbb{C}(x,y)$).

We show $x \notin N_{L/K}(L^*)$. Consider the discrete valuation $v_x$ on $\math
bb{C}(x,y)$ associated to the prime divisor $x = 0$, so $v_x(x) = 1$ and $v_x(y)
 = 0$. The residue field is $\kappa = \mathbb{C}(y)$.

In the extension $L/K$, since $v_x(y) = 0$ and $y$ is not a square in the residu
e field $\mathbb{C}(y)$, the extension is **unramified** at $v_x$, with residue
degree $f = 2$ and ramification index $e = 1$.

For an unramified extension of discretely valued fields, the norm satisfies
$$v_x\!\bigl(N_{L/K}(\alpha)\bigr) = f \cdot w(\alpha) = 2\, w(\alpha),$$
where $w$ is the extension of $v_x$ to $L$. Since $w(\alpha) \in \mathbb{Z}$ for
 all $\alpha \in L^*$, every norm from $L$ has **even** $v_x$-valuation.

But $v_x(x) = 1$ is **odd**. Therefore $x \notin N_{L/K}(L^*)$, and $A = (x,y)$
is a division algebra. $\square$

**Step 3: $A$ is not of the form $D \otimes_{\mathbb{C}} \mathbb{C}(x,y)$.**

Suppose, for contradiction, that $A \cong D \otimes_{\mathbb{C}} \mathbb{C}(x,y)
$ for some central division algebra $D$ over $\mathbb{C}$. By Step 1, $D = \math
bb{C}$, so
$$A \cong \mathbb{C} \otimes_{\mathbb{C}} \mathbb{C}(x,y) \cong \mathbb{C}(x,y),
$$
which is a **commutative** field. But $A = (x,y)$ is **non-commutative** (since
$ij = -ji \neq ji$). Contradiction.

---

### Conceptual explanation

The scalar extension map on Brauer groups
$$\operatorname{Br}(F) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Br}(E), \qquad [D] \longmap
sto [D \otimes_F E]$$
is **not surjective in general**. Division algebras over $E$ that lie in the ima
ge of this map are precisely those "defined over $F$," and the counterexample ab
ove exploits the fact that $\operatorname{Br}(\mathbb{C}) = 0$ while $\operatorn
ame{Br}(\mathbb{C}(x,y)) \neq 0$, so the image is trivial but the target is not.
