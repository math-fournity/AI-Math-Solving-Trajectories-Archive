**Theorem.** If a topological space $X$ is both locally connected and second-cou
ntable, then $X$ admits a countable basis consisting of connected open sets.

**Proof.**

Since $X$ is second-countable, fix a countable basis $\mathcal{B} = \{U_n : n \i
n \mathbb{N}\}$. Index the nonempty members of $\mathcal{B}$; the empty set, if
present, plays no role and is ignored.

For every ordered pair $(n, m)$ of indices with $U_n \subseteq U_m$ and $U_n \ne
q \varnothing$, define

$$C_{n,m} \;=\; \bigcup\bigl\{\, W \subseteq X : W \text{ is open, connected, an
d } U_n \subseteq W \subseteq U_m \,\bigr\}.$$

If the family being unioned is nonempty, set $C_{n,m}$ to be this union; if it i
s empty, discard the pair $(n,m)$.

**Claim 1.** Each $C_{n,m}$ is well-defined, open, and connected.

*Openness* is immediate, since $C_{n,m}$ is a union of open sets. For *connected
ness*: every $W$ appearing in the union contains $U_n$, and $U_n \neq \varnothin
g$, so all these $W$ share the nonempty set $U_n$ in common. A union of connecte
d sets with nonempty total intersection is connected. Hence $C_{n,m}$ is connect
ed. Moreover $U_n \subseteq C_{n,m} \subseteq U_m$, so $C_{n,m}$ is one of the s
ets being unioned — the union is in fact the *largest* connected open set sandwi
ched between $U_n$ and $U_m$.

**Claim 2.** The collection $\mathcal{C} = \{C_{n,m}\}$ is countable.

It is indexed by a subcollection of $\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}$, hence countabl
e.

**Claim 3.** $\mathcal{C}$ is a basis for the topology of $X$.

Let $O \subseteq X$ be open and let $x \in O$. We must find $C_{n,m} \in \mathca
l{C}$ with $x \in C_{n,m} \subseteq O$.

Since $\mathcal{B}$ is a basis, there exists $m$ with $x \in U_m \subseteq O$.

Since $X$ is locally connected and $U_m$ is an open neighborhood of $x$, there e
xists a connected open set $W$ with $x \in W \subseteq U_m$.

Since $\mathcal{B}$ is a basis, there exists $n$ with $x \in U_n \subseteq W$.

Now $U_n \neq \varnothing$ (it contains $x$) and $U_n \subseteq W \subseteq U_m$
 with $W$ connected and open. Therefore $W$ is among the sets unioned in the def
inition of $C_{n,m}$, so the pair $(n,m)$ is *not* discarded and

$$x \in U_n \subseteq C_{n,m} \subseteq U_m \subseteq O.$$

Thus for every open $O$ and every $x \in O$ there is $C_{n,m} \in \mathcal{C}$ w
ith $x \in C_{n,m} \subseteq O$, which is exactly the basis criterion.

**Conclusion.** $\mathcal{C}$ is a countable family of connected open sets formi
ng a basis for $X$. Therefore local connectedness together with second-countabil
ity guarantees a countable basis of connected sets. $\blacksquare$
