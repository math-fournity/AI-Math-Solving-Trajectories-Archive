Yes. Every smooth manifold admits a complete distance metric compatible with its
 topology.

**Proof.**

Let $M$ be a smooth manifold. By the usual convention, $M$ is Hausdorff, second-
countable, and locally Euclidean. Second-countability makes $M$ $\sigma$-compact
, which is the hypothesis needed for the (strong) Whitney embedding theorem:

> **Whitney (proper form).** Every smooth $n$-manifold admits a proper smooth em
bedding $\varphi:M\hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}^{N}$ for some $N$.

Recall that *proper* means $\varphi^{-1}(K)$ is compact in $M$ whenever $K\subse
t\mathbb{R}^{N}$ is compact.

Pull back the Euclidean metric to obtain a Riemannian metric on $M$:
$$g \;=\; \varphi^{*}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}.$$
This is well defined because $\varphi$ is an immersion (being an embedding). Let
 $d$ be the Riemannian distance induced by $g$:
$$d(p,q)=\inf_{\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}\|\dot\gamma(t)\|_{g}\,dt,$$
the infimum being over smooth curves $\gamma:[0,1]\to M$ joining $p$ to $q$.

**Compatibility with the topology.** For any Riemannian metric on a smooth manif
old, the induced distance topology coincides with the original manifold topology
. Indeed, in a coordinate chart the metric tensor is comparable to the Euclidean
 metric on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ (its components are continuous and positive definite
), so $d$-balls and coordinate balls are comparable. Hence $d$ is compatible wit
h the topology of $M$.

**Completeness.** Let $\{p_{k}\}$ be a $d$-Cauchy sequence. Then $\{p_{k}\}$ is
$d$-bounded, so it is contained in some closed $d$-ball $\overline{B}_{d}(p_{0},
R)$. For any curve $\gamma$ joining $p$ to $q$ in $M$,
$$\|\dot\gamma\|_{g}=\|D\varphi\,\dot\gamma\|_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\ge 0,$$
and the Euclidean length of $\varphi\circ\gamma$ is at least the straight-line d
istance $|\varphi(p)-\varphi(q)|$. Therefore
$$d(p,q)\;\ge\;|\varphi(p)-\varphi(q)|_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}.$$
Consequently $\{\varphi(p_{k})\}$ is Euclidean-bounded, hence lies in some compa
ct Euclidean ball $K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}$. By properness of $\varphi$, the sequ
ence $\{p_{k}\}$ lies in the compact set $\varphi^{-1}(K)\subset M$. A Cauchy se
quence contained in a compact set has a convergent subsequence, and a Cauchy seq
uence with a convergent subsequence converges. Thus every $d$-Cauchy sequence co
nverges, so $(M,d)$ is complete.

**Conclusion.** The Riemannian distance $d$ induced by the pullback metric $\var
phi^{*}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is a complete distance metric on $M$ whose top
ology agrees with the given manifold topology. Therefore every smooth manifold a
dmits a complete distance metric compatible with its topology. $\blacksquare$
