## Proof

**Claim:** The limit is $0$.

**Step 1: Interpret the hypothesis.**

The condition "$|f(t)|$ is nonincreasing as $t \to \infty$" means there exists $
T \geq 0$ such that the map $t \mapsto |f(t)|$ is nonincreasing on $[T, \infty)$
.

**Step 2: Show the supremum is eventually constant.**

Since $f$ is absolutely continuous, $f$ is continuous, so $|f|$ is continuous. O
n the compact interval $[0, T]$, $|f|$ attains a maximum, so
$$M := \sup_{r \in [0,T]} |f(r)|^2 < \infty.$$

For any $t \geq T$:
$$\sup_{r \in [0,t]} |f(r)|^2 = \max\!\Big(\sup_{r \in [0,T]} |f(r)|^2,\; \sup_{
r \in [T,t]} |f(r)|^2\Big).$$

Since $|f|$ is nonincreasing on $[T,\infty)$, we have $|f(r)| \leq |f(T)|$ for a
ll $r \in [T,t]$, so
$$\sup_{r \in [T,t]} |f(r)|^2 = |f(T)|^2.$$

Since $T \in [0,T]$, we have $|f(T)|^2 \leq M$. Therefore:
$$\sup_{r \in [0,t]} |f(r)|^2 = M \quad \text{for all } t \geq T.$$

**Step 3: Compute the limit.**

For $t \geq T$:
$$\frac{1}{t}\sup_{r \in [0,t]} |f(r)|^2 = \frac{M}{t} \xrightarrow{t \to \infty
} 0.$$

**Conclusion:**
$$\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\sup_{r\in[0,t]}|f(r)|^2 = 0.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
