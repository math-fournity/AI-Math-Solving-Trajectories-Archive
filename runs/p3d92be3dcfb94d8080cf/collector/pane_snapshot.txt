## Solution

**Setup.** Place the cone with apex at $(0,0,h)$ and base the disk $x^2+y^2\le R
^2$ in the plane $z=0$. The total volume is $V=\tfrac13\pi R^2 h$.

**The cutting plane.** The plane is tangent to the base circle $x^2+y^2=R^2$ at
the point $(R,0,0)$; it contains the tangent line $\{(R,t,0):t\in\mathbb R\}$, a
nd it passes through the altitude midpoint $(0,0,h/2)$. A plane containing the l
ine $x=R,\,z=0$ has the form $x+kz=R$. Substituting $(0,0,h/2)$ gives $k=2R/h$,
so

$$\Pi:\quad x = R\!\left(1-\frac{2z}{h}\right).$$

**Cross-sections.** At height $z$ the cone has disk $x^2+y^2\le r(z)^2$ with $r(
z)=R(1-z/h)$. The plane cuts this disk at $x_0=R(1-2z/h)$.

The region $x\ge x_0$ (containing the apex) is the smaller part. For $z>2h/3$ we
 have $x_0<-r(z)$, so the entire disk lies in the $x\ge x_0$ side; for $0\le z\l
e 2h/3$ the cut lies inside the disk and produces a circular segment. The comple
mentary region $x\le x_0$ (containing the base center) exists only for $0\le z\l
e 2h/3$; it is easier to compute, then subtract from $V$.

**Volume of the $x\le x_0$ side.** With $u=z/h\in[0,2/3]$, $r=R(1-u)$, $x_0=R(1-
2u)$, set $\alpha=x_0/r=(1-2u)/(1-u)$. The area of the portion $x\le x_0$ of a d
isk of radius $r$ is

$$A_<(u)=r^2\!\left[\arccos(-\alpha)+\alpha\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}\right].$$

Hence $V_< = hR^2\!\displaystyle\int_0^{2/3}(1-u)^2\!\left[\arccos\!\left(\tfrac
{-(1-2u)}{1-u}\right)+\tfrac{1-2u}{1-u}\sqrt{1-\left(\tfrac{1-2u}{1-u}\right)^2}
\right]du$.

Substitute $t=\alpha=(1-2u)/(1-u)$, so $u=(t-1)/(t-2)$, $1-u=1/(2-t)$, $du=dt/(2
-t)^2$, with $t:1\to -1$:

$$V_< = hR^2\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\arccos(-t)+t\sqrt{1-t^2}}{(2-t)^4}\,dt = hR^2\,
I.$$

**Evaluating $I$.** Using $\arccos(-t)=\pi-\arccos(t)$:

$$I=\pi\underbrace{\int_{-1}^1\frac{dt}{(2-t)^4}}_{A}\;-\;\underbrace{\int_{-1}^
1\frac{\arccos t}{(2-t)^4}\,dt}_{B}\;+\;\underbrace{\int_{-1}^1\frac{t\sqrt{1-t^
2}}{(2-t)^4}\,dt}_{C}.$$

*Term $A$:* $\displaystyle A=\frac{1}{3}\Big[(2-t)^{-3}\Big]_{-1}^{1}=\frac{1}{3
}\!\left(1-\frac1{27}\right)=\frac{26}{81}.$

*Term $B$:* with $t=\cos\theta$, $B=\int_0^\pi\frac{\theta\sin\theta}{(2-\cos\th
eta)^4}\,d\theta$. Integrate by parts ($u=\theta$, $dv=\sin\theta(2-\cos\theta)^
{-4}d\theta$, $v=-\frac{1}{3}(2-\cos\theta)^{-3}$):

$$B=\Big[-\frac{\theta}{3(2-\cos\theta)^3}\Big]_0^\pi+\frac13\int_0^\pi\frac{d\t
heta}{(2-\cos\theta)^3}=-\frac{\pi}{81}+\frac13\cdot\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt3}=-\frac{\
pi}{81}+\frac{\pi}{6\sqrt3},$$

using the standard formula $\int_0^\pi\frac{d\theta}{(a-\cos\theta)^3}=\frac{\pi
(2a^2+1)}{2(a^2-1)^{5/2}}$, which at $a=2$ gives $\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt3}$.

*Term $C$:* integrate by parts with $u=\sqrt{1-t^2}$, $dv=\frac{t\,dt}{(2-t)^4}$
. The boundary term vanishes and

$$C=-\frac12\int_{-1}^1\frac{t\,dt}{(2-t)^2\sqrt{1-t^2}}+\frac23\int_{-1}^1\frac
{t\,dt}{(2-t)^3\sqrt{1-t^2}}.$$

With $t=\cos\theta$ these become $J_n=\int_0^\pi\frac{\cos\theta}{(2-\cos\theta)
^n}d\theta=2I_n-I_{n-1}$, where $I_1=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt3}$, $I_2=\frac{2\pi}{3\sqr
t3}$, $I_3=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt3}$. Thus $J_2=J_3=\frac{\pi}{3\sqrt3}$, giving

$$C=\frac{\pi}{3\sqrt3}\!\left(-\frac12+\frac23\right)=\frac{\pi}{18\sqrt3}.$$

**Combining:**

$$I=\pi\cdot\frac{26}{81}-\!\left(-\frac{\pi}{81}+\frac{\pi}{6\sqrt3}\right)+\fr
ac{\pi}{18\sqrt3}
=\frac{27\pi}{81}+\frac{\pi}{\sqrt3}\!\left(-\frac16+\frac1{18}\right)
=\frac{\pi}{3}-\frac{\pi}{9\sqrt3}
=\frac{\pi(9-\sqrt3)}{27}.$$

Therefore

$$V_< = hR^2\cdot\frac{\pi(9-\sqrt3)}{27}=\frac{9-\sqrt3}{9}\cdot\frac{\pi R^2 h
}{3}=\frac{9-\sqrt3}{9}\,V.$$

The smaller part is the complementary (apex) side:

$$\boxed{V_{\text{small}}=V-V_<=\frac{\sqrt3}{9}\,V.}$$
