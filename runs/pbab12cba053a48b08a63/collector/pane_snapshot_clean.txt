## Proof

**Claim:** A bijection can be established between the set of all infinite binary
 sequences $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$ and the ring of 2-adic integers $\mathbb{Z}_2$
.

**Definitions.**

- An *infinite binary sequence* is a sequence $(a_0, a_1, a_2, \ldots)$ with eac
h $a_n \in \{0,1\}$. The set of all such sequences is $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$.
- The *2-adic integers* $\mathbb{Z}_2$ are defined as the inverse limit
$$\mathbb{Z}_2 = \varprojlim_{n} \mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z},$$
i.e., the set of sequences $(x_1, x_2, x_3, \ldots)$ with $x_n \in \mathbb{Z}/2^
n\mathbb{Z}$ and $x_{n+1} \equiv x_n \pmod{2^n}$ for all $n \geq 1$.

**Construction of the map.**

Define $\Phi : \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}} \to \mathbb{Z}_2$ by
$$\Phi\big((a_0, a_1, a_2, \ldots)\big) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n\, 2^n.$$

This is a well-defined 2-adic integer: its image in $\mathbb{Z}/2^{N}\mathbb{Z}$
 is the partial sum $\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} a_n 2^n \pmod{2^N}$, and these images are
compatible under the reduction maps since truncating to fewer terms is consisten
t with reduction modulo a smaller power of $2$.

**Injectivity.** Suppose $\Phi((a_n)) = \Phi((b_n))$, i.e., $\sum a_n 2^n = \sum
 b_n 2^n$ in $\mathbb{Z}_2$. Reducing modulo $2^{N}$ for each $N$ gives
$$\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} a_n 2^n \equiv \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} b_n 2^n \pmod{2^N}.$$
We prove $a_n = b_n$ for all $n$ by induction. For $n = 0$: reducing modulo $2$
gives $a_0 \equiv b_0 \pmod{2}$, and since both are in $\{0,1\}$, we get $a_0 =
b_0$. Suppose $a_0 = b_0, \ldots, a_{k-1} = b_{k-1}$. Reducing modulo $2^{k+1}$
and cancelling the common lower terms yields $a_k 2^k \equiv b_k 2^k \pmod{2^{k+
1}}$, hence $a_k \equiv b_k \pmod{2}$, so $a_k = b_k$. By induction, all digits
agree, proving injectivity.

**Surjectivity.** Let $z \in \mathbb{Z}_2$. We construct a binary sequence $(a_n
)$ with $\Phi((a_n)) = z$. The image of $z$ in $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is a res
idue class mod $2$; set $a_0$ to its representative in $\{0,1\}$. The image of $
z$ in $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ is a residue class mod $4$; its representative in
 $\{0,1,2,3\}$ equals $a_0 + 2a_1$ for a unique $a_1 \in \{0,1\}$, which defines
 $a_1$. Inductively, suppose $a_0, \ldots, a_{k-1}$ have been determined. The im
age of $z$ in $\mathbb{Z}/2^{k+1}\mathbb{Z}$ is a residue class whose representa
tive in $\{0, 1, \ldots, 2^{k+1}-1\}$ can be written uniquely as $\sum_{n=0}^{k}
 a_n 2^n$ with $a_n \in \{0,1\}$; the already-determined lower digits fix $a_0,
\ldots, a_{k-1}$, and the remaining bit determines $a_k$. This produces a sequen
ce $(a_n) \in \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$, and by construction the image of $\Phi((a_n
))$ in every $\mathbb{Z}/2^N\mathbb{Z}$ agrees with that of $z$, so $\Phi((a_n))
 = z$ in $\mathbb{Z}_2$.

**Conclusion.** The map $\Phi$ is both injective and surjective, hence a bijecti
on. Therefore a one-to-one correspondence (bijection) **can** be established bet
ween the set of all infinite binary sequences and the set of 2-adic integers. In
 fact, the canonical binary expansion provides a natural such bijection.
