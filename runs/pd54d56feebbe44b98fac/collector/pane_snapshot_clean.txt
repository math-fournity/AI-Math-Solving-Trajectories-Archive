## Solution

Let $s = a+b$ and $p = ab$.

**From the first condition:**
$$(a+1)(b+1) = ab + a + b + 1 = p + s + 1 = 2 \implies p + s = 1 \implies p = 1
- s.$$

**From the second condition:**
$$(a^2+1)(b^2+1) = a^2b^2 + a^2 + b^2 + 1 = p^2 + (s^2 - 2p) + 1 = 32.$$

Substituting $p = 1-s$:
$$(1-s)^2 + s^2 - 2(1-s) + 1 = 32$$
$$1 - 2s + s^2 + s^2 - 2 + 2s + 1 = 32$$
$$2s^2 = 32 \implies s^2 = 16 \implies s = \pm 4.$$

This gives two cases:
- **Case 1:** $s = 4,\ p = -3$.
- **Case 2:** $s = -4,\ p = 5$.

Both are realizable since $a, b$ are roots of $t^2 - st + p = 0$ and complex val
ues are permitted (Case 1 has discriminant $28 > 0$; Case 2 has discriminant $-4
 < 0$, giving complex roots).

**Computing $(a^4+1)(b^4+1) = p^4 + (a^4+b^4) + 1$, where $a^4+b^4 = (s^2-2p)^2
- 2p^2$:**

**Case 1** ($s=4,\ p=-3$):
$$s^2 - 2p = 16 + 6 = 22,\quad a^4+b^4 = 22^2 - 2(9) = 484 - 18 = 466,$$
$$(a^4+1)(b^4+1) = 81 + 466 + 1 = 548.$$

**Case 2** ($s=-4,\ p=5$):
$$s^2 - 2p = 16 - 10 = 6,\quad a^4+b^4 = 36 - 2(25) = -14,$$
$$(a^4+1)(b^4+1) = 625 - 14 + 1 = 612.$$

**Sum of all possible values:**
$$548 + 612 = \boxed{1160}.$$
