## Proof

Let $C = \{e^{i\theta} : 0 < \theta < \pi/2\}$, the open arc of the unit circle
in the first quadrant. Its closure is $\bar{C} = \{e^{i\theta} : 0 \le \theta \l
e \pi/2\}$, and the set $\{s : |s|=1,\; s \notin \bar{C}\}$ is $\{e^{i\theta} :
\pi/2 < \theta < 2\pi\}$.

**Definition of the boundary function.** Define $f$ on the unit circle by
$$f(e^{i\theta}) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } \theta \in (0,\,\pi/2), \\ 0 & \
text{if } \theta \in (\pi/2,\,2\pi). \end{cases}$$
The function $f$ is bounded ($0 \le f \le 1$) and has exactly two discontinuitie
s, at $e^{i\cdot 0}=1$ and $e^{i\pi/2}=i$—precisely the endpoints of $C$, i.e.,
the points of $\bar{C}\setminus C$. At no point of $C$ or of $\{e^{i\theta}:\pi/
2<\theta<2\pi\}$ is $f$ discontinuous.

**Definition of $u$.** For $z = re^{i\varphi}$ with $0 \le r < 1$, set
$$u(re^{i\varphi}) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} P_r(\varphi - \theta)\, f(e^{i\
theta})\, d\theta = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{1-r^2}{1 - 2r\cos(\varphi
-\theta) + r^2}\, d\theta,$$
where $P_r(t) = \frac{1-r^2}{1-2r\cos t + r^2}$ is the Poisson kernel. This is t
he **Poisson integral** of $f$.

**Claim:** $u$ satisfies all the required properties.

**1. $u$ is harmonic on $\{|z|<1\}$.** For each fixed $\theta$, the Poisson kern
el $P_r(\varphi-\theta) = \operatorname{Re}\!\left(\frac{e^{i\theta}+re^{i\varph
i}}{e^{i\theta}-re^{i\varphi}}\right)$ is a harmonic function of $z = re^{i\varp
hi}$ in the open disk (it is the real part of an analytic function of $z$ for $|
z|<1$). Since $f$ is integrable on the circle, integrating against $f\,d\theta$
preserves harmonicity: $u$ is harmonic in $\{|z|<1\}$.

**2. $u$ is bounded.** The Poisson kernel satisfies $P_r \ge 0$ and $\frac{1}{2\
pi}\int_0^{2\pi} P_r(\varphi-\theta)\,d\theta = 1$ for all $r<1$. Since $0 \le f
 \le 1$,
$$0 \le u(re^{i\varphi}) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} P_r(\varphi-\theta)\, f(e
^{i\theta})\, d\theta \le \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} P_r(\varphi-\theta)\, d\th
eta = 1.$$
Hence $u$ is bounded (by $0$ and $1$).

**3. Boundary limits.** We use the classical theorem on Poisson integrals (see,
e.g., Rudin, *Real and Complex Analysis*, Theorem 11.16): *If $f \in L^1(\partia
l\mathbb{D})$ and $f$ is continuous at a point $\zeta_0 \in \partial\mathbb{D}$,
 then the Poisson integral $u(z) \to f(\zeta_0)$ as $z \to \zeta_0$ from inside
the disk (unrestricted approach).*

- If $s \in C$, then $s = e^{i\theta_0}$ with $\theta_0 \in (0,\pi/2)$, and $f$
is continuous at $s$ with $f(s)=1$. By the theorem, $\lim_{z\to s} u(z) = 1$.
- If $s \notin \bar{C}$ and $|s|=1$, then $s = e^{i\theta_0}$ with $\theta_0 \in
 (\pi/2, 2\pi)$, and $f$ is continuous at $s$ with $f(s)=0$. By the theorem, $\l
im_{z\to s} u(z) = 0$.

At the two endpoints $e^{i\cdot 0}=1$ and $e^{i\pi/2}=i$ (the points of $\bar{C}
\setminus C$), no boundary condition is required, and indeed $f$ is discontinuou
s there, so the limit need not exist—this is consistent with the problem stateme
nt, which imposes no requirement at those points.

**Conclusion.** The function
$$\boxed{u(re^{i\varphi}) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{1-r^2}{1-2r\cos(\
varphi-\theta)+r^2}\,d\theta}$$
is bounded, harmonic on $\{|z|<1\}$, satisfies $\lim_{z\to s}u(z)=1$ for $s\in C
$, and $\lim_{z\to s}u(z)=0$ for $s\notin\bar{C}$, $|s|=1$. $\blacksquare$
