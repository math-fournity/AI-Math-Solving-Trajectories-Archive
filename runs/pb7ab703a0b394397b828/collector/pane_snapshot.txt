## Solution

Consider the meromorphic function
$$f(z) = \frac{e^z - 1}{z^2(e^z - z)}.$$

**Poles of $f$.** The poles occur at $z=0$ and at the roots of $e^z - z = 0$ (i.
e., the $z_k$).

**Residue at $z = 0$.** Since $e^0 = 1 \neq 0$, the point $z=0$ is not a root of
 $e^z = z$. Expanding near $0$:
- $e^z - 1 = z + \tfrac{z^2}{2} + O(z^3)$,
- $e^z - z = 1 + \tfrac{z^2}{2} + O(z^3)$,

so
$$f(z) = \frac{z + z^2/2 + O(z^3)}{z^2\bigl(1 + z^2/2 + O(z^3)\bigr)} = \frac{1}
{z}\cdot\frac{1 + z/2 + O(z^2)}{1 + O(z^2)} = \frac{1}{z}\bigl(1 + z/2 + O(z^2)\
bigr).$$

Hence $\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z) = 1$.

**Residue at each root $z_k$.** Each $z_k$ is a simple zero of $e^z - z$, since
$(e^z - z)' = e^z - 1 = z_k - 1 \neq 0$ (as $z_k = 1$ would give $e = 1$, imposs
ible). Thus $z_k$ is a simple pole of $f$, with residue
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_k} f(z) = \frac{e^{z_k}-1}{z_k^2\,(e^z-z)'\big|_{z=z_k
}} = \frac{z_k - 1}{z_k^2\,(e^{z_k}-1)} = \frac{z_k-1}{z_k^2\,(z_k-1)} = \frac{1
}{z_k^2}.$$

**Applying the residue theorem.** For the square $C_N$ (centered at the origin,
side $2\pi N$), which encloses $z=0$ and all roots $z_k$ inside it:
$$\int_{C_N} f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i\left(1 + \sum_{\substack{k:\, z_k \in \operatorna
me{int}(C_N)}} \frac{1}{z_k^2}\right).$$

Taking $N \to \infty$ and using the given hypothesis $\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\in
fty}\int_{C_N} f(z)\,dz = 0$, every root is eventually enclosed, so
$$0 = 2\pi i\left(1 + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{z_k^2}\right).$$

Therefore
$$\boxed{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{z_k^2} = -1}.$$
