## Proof

**Key Observation.** The Cayley graph of the free group $F_2 = \langle a, b \ran
gle$ with respect to the generating set $S = \{a, b, a^{-1}, b^{-1}\}$ is a **4-
regular tree**. Each vertex (group element) has exactly 4 neighbors, and since $
F_2$ has no relations, there are no cycles. The random walk described is therefo
re a simple random walk on a 4-regular tree, starting at the root (the identity
element $1$).

Since the walk starts at $1$ and the first step always moves away (to one of $a,
 b, a^{-1}, b^{-1}$), the question asks: **what is the probability of ever retur
ning to the root after the first step?**

**Setup.** Let $d = 4$ be the degree of the tree. Define:
- $p$ = probability of ever reaching the root, starting from a vertex at distanc
e 1 from the root.
- $q$ = probability of ever reaching distance $k-1$, starting from a vertex at d
istance $k \geq 2$ from the root.

By the homogeneity of the tree away from the root, $q$ is the same for all $k \g
eq 2$.

**Computing $q$.** A vertex at distance $k \geq 2$ has 1 neighbor at distance $k
-1$ (its "parent") and $d - 1 = 3$ neighbors at distance $k+1$ (its "children").
 From such a vertex:
- With probability $\frac{1}{d}$, the walk moves to distance $k-1$ — success (re
ached $k-1$).
- With probability $\frac{d-1}{d}$, the walk moves to distance $k+1$. From there
, it must first reach distance $k$ (probability $q$), then reach distance $k-1$
from distance $k$ (probability $q$).

This gives the equation:
$$q = \frac{1}{d} + \frac{d-1}{d}\, q^2.$$

Rearranging: $(d-1)q^2 - d\,q + 1 = 0$, with solutions:
$$q = \frac{d \pm \sqrt{d^2 - 4(d-1)}}{2(d-1)} = \frac{d \pm (d-2)}{2(d-1)}.$$

The two roots are $q = 1$ and $q = \frac{1}{d-1}$. Since the walk on a $d$-regul
ar tree with $d \geq 3$ is **transient**, we select the smaller root:
$$q = \frac{1}{d-1}.$$

**Computing $p$.** A vertex at distance 1 from the root has 1 neighbor at distan
ce 0 (the root) and $d - 1 = 3$ neighbors at distance 2. From distance 1:
- With probability $\frac{1}{d}$, the walk moves to the root — success.
- With probability $\frac{d-1}{d}$, the walk moves to distance 2. From there, it
 must reach distance 1 (probability $q = \frac{1}{d-1}$), then reach the root fr
om distance 1 (probability $p$).

This gives:
$$p = \frac{1}{d} + \frac{d-1}{d} \cdot \frac{1}{d-1} \cdot p = \frac{1}{d} + \f
rac{p}{d}.$$

Solving: $p\left(1 - \frac{1}{d}\right) = \frac{1}{d}$, so $p \cdot \frac{d-1}{d
} = \frac{1}{d}$, yielding:
$$p = \frac{1}{d-1}.$$

**Return probability.** After the first step, the walk is at distance 1 from the
 root. The probability of ever returning to the root is exactly $p$:
$$\rho = p = \frac{1}{d-1}.$$

**Substituting $d = 4$:**
$$\boxed{\rho = \frac{1}{3}}.$$

**Verification.** For $d = 2$ (the integers $\mathbb{Z}$, a 2-regular tree), the
 formula gives $\rho = \frac{1}{2-1} = 1$, correctly recovering the recurrence o
f the 1D simple random walk. For $d = 3$, it gives $\rho = \frac{1}{2}$, the kno
wn return probability for the 3-regular tree. The transience for $d \geq 3$ is c
