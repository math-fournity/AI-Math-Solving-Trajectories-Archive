Looking at this problem, I need to show that $E\!\left[\int_0^\infty e^{-A_t}\,d
A_t\right] \le C$ for a universal constant, where $A_t$ is an increasing process
 of locally integrable variation with $A_0=0$, possibly purely discontinuous.

**Key idea.** The chain rule for functions of finite-variation processes, combin
ed with the elementary inequality $e^{-x}(1+x)\le 1$ for $x\ge 0$, gives a *poin
twise* bound $\int_0^\infty e^{-A_t}\,dA_t \le 1$, from which the result follows
 by taking expectations.

---

**Proof.**

Since $A$ is increasing (hence of finite variation on compacts), it admits the L
ebesgue decomposition $A = A^c + A^d$ into a continuous increasing part $A^c$ an
d a purely discontinuous part $A^d = \sum_{0<s} \Delta A_s$ (a sum of point mass
es at jump times). The integral is understood in the Lebesgue–Stieltjes sense:
$$\int_0^\infty e^{-A_t}\,dA_t = \int_0^\infty e^{-A_t}\,dA^c_t + \sum_{0<s\le\c
dot} e^{-A_s}\,\Delta A_s,$$
which is well defined as a non-negative extended random variable (monotone limit
 of integrals over $[0,n]$).

**Change of variables.** Apply the change-of-variables formula for right-continu
ous finite-variation functions to $F(x)=1-e^{-x}$ (with $F'(x)=e^{-x}$) and $A_0
=0$:
$$1 - e^{-A_t} = \int_0^t e^{-A_s}\,dA^c_s + \sum_{0<s\le t}\bigl(e^{-A_{s-}} -
e^{-A_s}\bigr). \tag{1}$$
Letting $t\to\infty$,
$$\int_0^\infty e^{-A_s}\,dA^c_s = \bigl(1 - e^{-A_\infty}\bigr) - \sum_{0<s}\bi
gl(e^{-A_{s-}} - e^{-A_s}\bigr). \tag{2}$$

**Combine.** Using (2),
$$\int_0^\infty e^{-A_t}\,dA_t = \bigl(1-e^{-A_\infty}\bigr) + \sum_{0<s}\Bigl[-
\bigl(e^{-A_{s-}}-e^{-A_s}\bigr) + e^{-A_s}\,\Delta A_s\Bigr].$$
Rewrite each jump term. Since $A_s = A_{s-}+\Delta A_s$,
$$-\bigl(e^{-A_{s-}}-e^{-A_s}\bigr) + e^{-A_s}\,\Delta A_s = e^{-A_{s-}}\Bigl[e^
{-\Delta A_s}(1+\Delta A_s) - 1\Bigr].$$
Hence
$$\int_0^\infty e^{-A_t}\,dA_t = \bigl(1-e^{-A_\infty}\bigr) + \sum_{0<s} e^{-A_
{s-}}\Bigl[e^{-\Delta A_s}(1+\Delta A_s) - 1\Bigr]. \tag{3}$$

**The inequality.** For every $x\ge 0$, the classical bound $1+x\le e^x$ gives $
e^{-x}(1+x)\le 1$, i.e.
$$e^{-x}(1+x)-1\le 0.$$
Since $\Delta A_s\ge 0$ and $e^{-A_{s-}}\ge 0$, every summand in (3) is non-posi
tive. Therefore
$$\int_0^\infty e^{-A_t}\,dA_t \;\le\; 1 - e^{-A_\infty} \;\le\; 1 \qquad\text{a
.s.}$$

**Take expectations.** The integrand $e^{-A_t}$ and the measure $dA_t$ are both
non-negative, so $\int_0^\infty e^{-A_t}\,dA_t\ge 0$; its expectation is a well-
defined element of $[0,\infty]$. The pointwise bound yields
$$E\!\left[\int_0^\infty e^{-A_t}\,dA_t\right] \;\le\; E\!\bigl[1-e^{-A_\infty}\
bigr] \;\le\; 1.$$

Thus $E\!\left[\int_0^\infty e^{-A_t}\,dA_t\right]$ is bounded above by the univ
ersal constant $\boxed{1}$, independent of the law of $A$ and valid whether $A$
is continuous, purely discontinuous, or a mixture. $\blacksquare$
