**Proof.**

Let $F$ be a finite field. Then $|F| = p^n$ for some prime $p$ (the characterist
ic of $F$) and some integer $n \ge 1$. The multiplicative group $F^{\times} = F
\setminus \{0\}$ is cyclic of order $m := p^n - 1$.

The element $0$ is a square ($0 = 0^2$), so the hypothesis "every element is a s
quare" concerns $F^{\times}$. An element $a \in F^{\times}$ is a square iff $a =
 b^2$ for some $b \in F^{\times}$.

Consider the squaring homomorphism
$$\varphi : F^{\times} \to F^{\times}, \qquad \varphi(b) = b^2.$$
Its image $\varphi(F^{\times})$ is the set of squares. Since $F^{\times}$ is cyc
lic of order $m$, generated by some $g$, we have
$$\varphi(F^{\times}) = \langle g^2 \rangle,$$
which is the subgroup of $F^{\times}$ of order $m / \gcd(2, m)$.

The hypothesis that every element of $F$ is a square says $\varphi(F^{\times}) =
 F^{\times}$, i.e., the squaring map is surjective. This holds iff
$$\frac{m}{\gcd(2, m)} = m \iff \gcd(2, m) = 1 \iff m \text{ is odd}.$$

Now $m = p^n - 1$ is odd iff $p^n$ is even iff $p = 2$ (since $p$ is prime, $p^n
$ is even exactly when $p = 2$).

Therefore $p = 2$, i.e., the characteristic of $F$ is $2$. $\blacksquare$
