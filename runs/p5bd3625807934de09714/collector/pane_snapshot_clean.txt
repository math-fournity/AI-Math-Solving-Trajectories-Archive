## Solution

Place $O$ at the origin. Let $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{w}$ be unit vector
s in the directions of $OA, OB, OC$ respectively. The given angles yield:

$$\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=\cos 60^\circ=\tfrac12,\qquad \mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf
{w}=\cos 90^\circ=0,\qquad \mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{u}=\cos 120^\circ=-\tfrac12.$$

Both planes $AOB$ and $AOC$ contain the line $OA$, so the dihedral angle $\theta
$ between them is the angle between their normal vectors:

$$\mathbf{n}_1 = \mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v},\qquad \mathbf{n}_2 = \mathbf{u}\tim
es\mathbf{w}.$$

Using the identity $(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b})\cdot(\mathbf{c}\times\mathbf{d}
)=(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{c})(\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{d})-(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf
{d})(\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{c})$:

$$\mathbf{n}_1\cdot\mathbf{n}_2=(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{u})(\mathbf{v}\cdot\math
bf{w})-(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{w})(\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{u})=(1)(0)-\left(-\tfr
ac12\right)\left(\tfrac12\right)=\tfrac14.$$

The magnitudes are:

$$|\mathbf{n}_1|^2=|\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}|^2=1-(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v})^
2=1-\tfrac14=\tfrac34,$$

$$|\mathbf{n}_2|^2=|\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{w}|^2=1-(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{w})^
2=1-\tfrac14=\tfrac34.$$

Hence $|\mathbf{n}_1||\mathbf{n}_2|=\tfrac34$, and

$$\cos\theta=\frac{\mathbf{n}_1\cdot\mathbf{n}_2}{|\mathbf{n}_1||\mathbf{n}_2|}=
\frac{1/4}{3/4}=\frac13.$$

Since $\cos\theta=\frac13>0$, $\theta$ is acute as required. Therefore

$$\cos^2\theta=\frac19,\qquad m=1,\ n=9,$$

with $\gcd(1,9)=1$. The answer is

$$100m+n=100(1)+9=\boxed{109}.$$
