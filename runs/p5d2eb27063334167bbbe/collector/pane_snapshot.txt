## Solution

The answer is **True**: such a character $\chi$ can always be realized by a repr
esentation over $\mathbb{R}$.

### Proof

Let $\chi$ be an irreducible complex character of $G$ that is real-valued ($\chi
(g)\in\mathbb{R}$ for all $g$), and let $V$ be a complex irreducible $\mathbb{C}
G$-module affording $\chi$, with $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} V = \chi(1)$.

**Frobenius–Schur indicator.** Recall the Frobenius–Schur indicator
$$\nu(\chi) = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g^2)\in\{1,0,-\}.$$
For an irreducible character, $\nu(\chi)$ classifies the representation into thr
ee types:
- $\nu(\chi)=1$ (real/orthogonal type): $\chi$ is the character of a representat
ion over $\mathbb{R}$.
- $\nu(\chi)=0$ (complex type): $\chi$ is **not** real-valued ($\chi\ne\bar\chi$
).
- $\nu(\chi)=-1$ (quaternionic/symplectic type): $\chi$ is real-valued but **not
** realizable over $\mathbb{R}$.

Since $\chi$ is real-valued, we are in either real type ($\nu=1$) or quaternioni
c type ($\nu=-1$). The character is realizable over $\mathbb{R}$ if and only if
$\nu(\chi)=1$.

**Quaternionic type forces even degree.** Suppose $\nu(\chi)=-1$. By the theory
of the Frobenius–Schur indicator, there exists a nonzero $G$-invariant **skew-sy
mmetric** bilinear form $B$ on $V$. Since $V$ is irreducible, Schur's-lemma-type
 argument shows any nonzero invariant bilinear form is **nondegenerate** (its ra
dical is a $G$-submodule, hence $0$ or $V$; if $B\ne 0$ the radical is not all o
f $V$).

A nondegenerate skew-symmetric bilinear form on a finite-dimensional vector spac
e exists only in **even** dimension (the representing matrix is invertible and s
kew-symmetric, and $\det(A)=\det(A^T)=\det(-A)=(-1)^n\det(A)$, so for invertibil
ity $(-1)^n=1$, i.e. $n$ even).

Therefore $\nu(\chi)=-1\Rightarrow \chi(1)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}V$ is even.

**Conclusion.** We are given $\chi(1)$ is **odd**. By the contrapositive of the
above, $\nu(\chi)\ne -1$. Since $\chi$ is real-valued, $\nu(\chi)\ne 0$. Hence $
\nu(\chi)=1$, i.e. $\chi$ is of real type and is the character of a representati
on over $\mathbb{R}$.

Thus an irreducible real character of odd degree is always realizable over $\mat
hbb{R}$.
