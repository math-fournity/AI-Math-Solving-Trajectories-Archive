**Proof.**

We are given $f(t) = \sum_{k} a_k e^{ikt}$ with $\sum_{k} |a_k| < \infty$ (the c
oefficients are in $\ell^1$). Here the sum is over $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, and $\mat
hbb{T} = \mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}$ is the torus (the unit circle, parametrized
by $t$).

Consider the partial sums
$$S_N(t) = \sum_{|k| \le N} a_k e^{ikt}.$$

**Step 1: Each $S_N$ is continuous.** Each $S_N$ is a finite linear combination
of the functions $e^{ikt}$, each of which is continuous in $t$. A finite sum of
continuous functions is continuous, so $S_N : \mathbb{T} \to \mathbb{C}$ is cont
inuous for every $N$.

**Step 2: The series converges uniformly.** For every $t \in \mathbb{T}$ and eve
ry $k$,
$$|a_k e^{ikt}| = |a_k| \cdot |e^{ikt}| = |a_k|,$$
since $|e^{ikt}| = 1$. Setting $M_k = |a_k|$, we have $\sum_k M_k = \sum_k |a_k|
 < \infty$. By the **Weierstrass $M$-test**, the series $\sum_k a_k e^{ikt}$ con
verges **absolutely and uniformly** on $\mathbb{T}$.

**Step 3: The uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous.** Since $S_N
\to f$ uniformly on $\mathbb{T}$ and each $S_N$ is continuous, the limit functio
n $f$ is continuous on $\mathbb{T}$.

**Conclusion.** The $\ell^1$ condition on the Fourier coefficients guarantees un
iform (indeed absolute) convergence of the Fourier series, and since each partia
l sum is continuous, the limit $f$ is continuous. Therefore $f$ is necessarily a
 continuous function. $\blacksquare$
