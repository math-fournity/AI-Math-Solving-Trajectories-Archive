## Answer

**No**, the proposed $m$ is not countably additive on $R(\mathbb{R}^n)$. Below i
s a counterexample in $n=1$.

---

### Setup

Let $C \subset [0,1]$ be the Smith–Volterra–Cantor (fat Cantor) set: a closed, n
owhere dense subset of $[0,1]$ with Lebesgue measure $\mu(C) = \tfrac{1}{2}$.

Its complement $[0,1]\setminus C$ is open and dense in $[0,1]$, and decomposes a
s a countable union of pairwise disjoint open intervals:
$$[0,1]\setminus C = \bigsqcup_{k=1}^{\infty} I_k, \qquad I_k = (a_k, b_k).$$

Each $I_k$ is a **regular open set** (every open interval satisfies $\overline{(
a,b)}^{\,\circ} = (a,b)$), and the $I_k$ are pairwise disjoint, so $I_i \wedge I
_j = I_i \cap I_j = \emptyset$ for $i\neq j$.

### The countable join

The join in $R(\mathbb{R})$ is
$$\bigvee_{k=1}^{\infty} I_k = \overline{\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty} I_k\right)
}^{\,\circ} = \overline{[0,1]\setminus C}^{\,\circ}.$$

Since $C$ is nowhere dense, $[0,1]\setminus C$ is dense in $[0,1]$, so $\overlin
e{[0,1]\setminus C} = [0,1]$, and thus
$$\bigvee_{k=1}^{\infty} I_k = [0,1]^{\circ} = (0,1).$$

### Computing $m$ on both sides

**Left side** (measure of the join):
$$m\!\left(\bigvee_{k=1}^{\infty} I_k\right) = m((0,1)) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mu\!\bi
g((0,1)^{\circ}\big) + \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mu\!\big(\overline{(0,1)}\big) = \tfrac{1}
{2}\cdot 1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 1 = 1.$$

**Right side** (sum of measures). For each $I_k = (a_k,b_k)$: $I_k^{\circ} = I_k
$ and $\overline{I_k} = [a_k, b_k]$, both of measure $b_k - a_k$, so
$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} m(I_k) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}(b_k-a_k)+\t
frac{1}{2}(b_k-a_k)\bigr) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(b_k-a_k) = \mu([0,1]\setminus C)
 = \tfrac{1}{2}.$$

### Contradiction

$$m\!\left(\bigvee_{k=1}^{\infty} I_k\right) = 1 \;\neq\; \tfrac{1}{2} = \sum_{k
=1}^{\infty} m(I_k).$$

### Why it fails

The join $\vee$ applies a closure, which "fills in" the nowhere dense set $C$ of
 **positive** measure $\tfrac{1}{2}$. The term $\tfrac{1}{2}\mu(\overline{U})$ i
n the definition of $m$ charges this filled-in region, so the measure of the joi
n strictly exceeds the sum of the measures of the disjoint parts. Countable addi
tivity is violated.
