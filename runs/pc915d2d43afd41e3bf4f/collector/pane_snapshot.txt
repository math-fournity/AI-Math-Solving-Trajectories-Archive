## Galois Group of $x^4 + 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$

**Irreducibility.** $f(x) = x^4 + 2$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ by Eisenst
ein's criterion at $p = 2$ (every non-leading coefficient is divisible by $2$, a
nd the constant term $2$ is not divisible by $4$).

**The roots.** Writing $\alpha = 2^{1/4}$ and $\zeta_8 = e^{i\pi/4} = \frac{1+i}
{\sqrt{2}}$, the four roots of $x^4 + 2 = 0$ (i.e. $x^4 = -2$) are
$$\gamma,\; i\gamma,\; -\gamma,\; -i\gamma, \qquad \text{where } \gamma := \alph
a\,\zeta_8 = 2^{1/4}e^{i\pi/4},$$
since $\gamma^4 = \alpha^4 \zeta_8^4 = 2 \cdot e^{i\pi} = -2$. Note $\gamma^2 =
\alpha^2 \zeta_8^2 = \sqrt{2}\cdot i = i\sqrt{2}$.

**The splitting field.** The splitting field is $K = \mathbb{Q}(\gamma, i\gamma,
 -\gamma, -i\gamma) = \mathbb{Q}(\gamma, i)$.

**Degree of $\mathbb{Q}(\gamma)/\mathbb{Q}$.** Since $f$ is irreducible, $[\math
bb{Q}(\gamma) : \mathbb{Q}] = 4$.

**Is $i \in \mathbb{Q}(\gamma)$?** Suppose $i \in \mathbb{Q}(\gamma)$. Then $\sq
rt{2} = \gamma^2 / i \in \mathbb{Q}(\gamma)$, so $\mathbb{Q}(i, \sqrt{2}) \subse
teq \mathbb{Q}(\gamma)$. Both fields have degree $4$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ (the exte
nsions $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ are independent quadratic exte
nsions), so $\mathbb{Q}(\gamma) = \mathbb{Q}(i,\sqrt{2})$. But then $\gamma \in
\mathbb{Q}(i,\sqrt{2})$, so $\gamma = u + vi$ with $u, v \in \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}
)$, and $\gamma^2 = u^2 - v^2 + 2uv\,i = i\sqrt{2}$ requires $u^2 = v^2$ and $2u
v = \sqrt{2}$, giving $u^2 = \sqrt{2}/2 = 1/\sqrt{2}$, i.e. $u = \pm\, 2^{-1/4}
\notin \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ — a contradiction. Hence $i \notin \mathbb{Q}(\gamm
a)$.

**Degree of $K/\mathbb{Q}$.** Since $i \notin \mathbb{Q}(\gamma)$ and $i$ is qua
dratic over $\mathbb{Q}$ (hence over $\mathbb{Q}(\gamma)$),
$$[K : \mathbb{Q}] = [K:\mathbb{Q}(\gamma)]\cdot[\mathbb{Q}(\gamma):\mathbb{Q}]
= 2 \cdot 4 = 8.$$
So $|G| = 8$ where $G = \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$.

**Structure of $G$.** $G$ acts faithfully on the four roots, so $G \hookrightarr
ow S_4$ as a transitive subgroup of order $8$. The only transitive subgroups of
$S_4$ of order $8$ are the Sylow $2$-subgroups, each isomorphic to the dihedral
group $D_4$. Hence $G \cong D_4$.

**Explicit generators confirming $D_4$.** Define:
- $\sigma \in G$ by $\sigma(\gamma) = i\gamma$, $\sigma(i) = i$. Then $\sigma$ c
ycles the roots as $\gamma \to i\gamma \to -\gamma \to -i\gamma \to \gamma$, so
$\sigma$ has order $4$.
- $\tau \in G$ (complex conjugation) by $\tau(\gamma) = \overline{\gamma} = -i\g
amma$, $\tau(i) = -i$. Then $\tau$ swaps $\gamma \leftrightarrow -i\gamma$ and $
i\gamma \leftrightarrow -\gamma$, so $\tau$ has order $2$.

One checks $\tau\sigma\tau(\gamma) = \tau\sigma(-i\gamma) = \tau(-i\cdot i\gamma
) = \tau(\gamma) = -i\gamma = \sigma^{-1}(\gamma)$, and $\tau\sigma\tau(i) = i =
 \sigma^{-1}(i)$, so
$$\tau\sigma\tau = \sigma^{-1}.$$
Together with $\sigma^4 = \tau^2 = 1$, this is a presentation of the dihedral gr
oup of order $8$.

---

**Conclusion.** The Galois group of $x^4 + 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is the dihedral
group of order $8$:
$$\operatorname{Gal}(x^4+2/\mathbb{Q}) \;\cong\; D_4 \;\cong\; \langle \sigma, \
tau \mid \sigma^4 = \tau^2 = 1,\; \tau\sigma\tau = \sigma^{-1}\rangle,$$
a group of order $8$.
