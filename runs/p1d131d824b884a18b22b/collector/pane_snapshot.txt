**Proof.**

**Step 1: The limit is constant (Hurwitz's theorem).**

Each $f_n$ is holomorphic and univalent (one-to-one) on $\mathbb{D}$. By Hurwitz
's theorem, if a sequence of univalent holomorphic functions on a domain converg
es locally uniformly, the limit is either univalent or constant. Since $f$ is gi
ven to be not one-to-one, $f$ must be constant. Let $f \equiv c$.

**Step 2: The preimages of any fixed point escape to the boundary.**

Since each $f_n$ maps $\mathbb{D}$ **onto** $\mathbb{A}$, for any $w \in \mathbb
{A}$ there exists $z_n \in \mathbb{D}$ with $f_n(z_n) = w$. Set $z_n = f_n^{-1}(
w)$.

Suppose $|z_n| \not\to 1$. Then some subsequence $z_{n_k}$ converges to a point
$z^* \in \overline{\mathbb{D}}$ with $|z^*| < 1$ (i.e., staying in a compact sub
set of $\mathbb{D}$). By locally uniform convergence:
$$w = f_{n_k}(z_{n_k}) \longrightarrow f(z^*) = c.$$
This forces $w = c$. Since $w \in \mathbb{A}$ was arbitrary (with $w \neq c$), t
his is a contradiction. Therefore $|z_n| \to 1$ for every $w \in \mathbb{A}$, $w
 \neq c$.

**Step 3: Hyperbolic distance argument to locate $c$.**

Each $f_n: \mathbb{D} \to \mathbb{A}$ is a conformal bijection, hence an isometr
y for the hyperbolic (Poincaré) metrics:
$$d_{\mathbb{A}}\big(f_n(0),\, w\big) = d_{\mathbb{D}}\big(0,\, f_n^{-1}(w)\big)
, \qquad \forall\, w \in \mathbb{A}.$$

Fix any $w \in \mathbb{A}$ with $w \neq c$.

- **Right-hand side:** By Step 2, $f_n^{-1}(w) \to \partial \mathbb{D}$, so $d_{
\mathbb{D}}(0, f_n^{-1}(w)) \to \infty$.

- **Left-hand side:** Since $f_n(0) \to c$, if $c \in \mathbb{A}$ (i.e., $\Re c
> 0$), then $d_{\mathbb{A}}(f_n(0), w) \to d_{\mathbb{A}}(c, w) < \infty$ (the h
yperbolic distance between two interior points of $\mathbb{A}$ is finite).

This gives $\infty = \text{finite}$, a contradiction. Therefore $c \notin \mathb
b{A}$, i.e., $\Re c \leq 0$.

**Step 4: Combining with the lower bound.**

Since $f_n(0) \in \mathbb{A}$ for every $n$, we have $\Re f_n(0) > 0$, and takin

Combining $\Re c \leq 0$ and $\Re c \geq 0$:
$$\Re f(0) = \Re c = 0.$$
