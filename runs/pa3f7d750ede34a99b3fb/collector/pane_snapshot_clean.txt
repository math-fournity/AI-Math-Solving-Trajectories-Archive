**Claim:** $f(z)$ has exactly **one** zero in the open unit disk $\{z:|z|<1\}$.

**Proof.**

We apply **Rouché's theorem**. Set $g(z) = z$, which is analytic on $\{z:|z|\leq
 1\}$.

**Step 1: Verify the Rouché condition on the boundary.**

For every $z$ with $|z|=1$, the hypothesis gives
$$|f(z)-z|<|z|=1=|g(z)|.$$
Thus $|f(z)-g(z)|<|g(z)|$ holds on the entire boundary $|z|=1$.

**Step 2: Verify neither function vanishes on the boundary.**

- $g(z)=z$ has its only zero at $z=0$, which lies in the interior, so $g(z)\neq
0$ on $|z|=1$.
- For $f$: by the reverse triangle inequality, on $|z|=1$,
$$|f(z)|\geq |z|-|f(z)-z|>1-1=0,$$
since $|f(z)-z|<1=|z|$. Hence $f(z)\neq 0$ on $|z|=1$.

**Step 3: Apply Rouché's theorem.**

Since $f$ and $g$ are analytic on a neighborhood of $\{|z|\leq 1\}$, both are no
nzero on $|z|=1$, and $|f-g|<|g|$ on $|z|=1$, Rouché's theorem asserts that $f$
and $g$ have the same number of zeros (counting multiplicity) inside $|z|<1$.

**Step 4: Count the zeros of $g$.**

$g(z)=z$ has exactly one zero (at $z=0$, of multiplicity $1$) inside the open un
it disk.

**Conclusion.**

Therefore $f(z)$ has exactly **one** zero within the open unit disk $\{z:|z|<1\}
$. $\blacksquare$
