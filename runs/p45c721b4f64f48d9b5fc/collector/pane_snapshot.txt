$$\phi_n(0) = 0 \in B(H_n).$$

Therefore:

$$P_n \phi_n(0) = P_n \cdot 0 = 0 \in B(H_n) \quad \text{for all } n.$$

The sequence ${P_n \phi_n(0)}_{n=1}^{\infty} = {0, 0, 0, \ldots}$ is constant,
hence norm convergent (to $0$). $\square$

The role of the nonunital hypothesis

The nonunital hypothesis is essential for understanding why no better (nonzero)
guarantee can be made. In the unital case, if $A$ has a unit $1_A$ and the
representations $\phi_n$ are unital (i.e., $\phi_n(1_A) = I_{H_n}$), then taking
$a_0 = 1_A$ gives:

$$P_n \phi_n(1_A) = P_n \cdot I_{H_n} = P_n,$$

and $|P_n| \in {0, 1}$ for any projection. If all $P_n$ are nonzero projections,
then $|P_n \phi_n(1_A)| = 1$ for all $n$, which converges. Thus in the unital
case, there is always a nonzero $a_0$ that works.

In the nonunital case, no such universal nonzero element exists. The following
counterexample demonstrates that $a_0 = 0$ may be the only solution.

\mathbb{R}$ vanishing at infinity (a standard nonunital C*-algebra).

  • Let ${x_n}_{n=1}^\infty$ be an enumeration of the rational numbers $
  \mathbb{Q}$ (which is dense in $\mathbb{R}$).
  • Define $\phi_n: C_0(\mathbb{R}) \to \mathbb{C}$ by $\phi_n(f) = f(x_n)$

  (representation on $\mathbb{C}$).

  \mathbb{C}$).

Then $P_n \phi_n(f) = f(x_n)$ and $|P_n \phi_n(f)| = |f(x_n)|$.

Claim: For any nonzero $f \in C_0(\mathbb{R})$, the sequence ${|f(x_n)|}$ does
not converge.

Proof of claim: Since $f$ is nonzero and continuous, there exists $x_0 \in
\mathbb{R}$ with $f(x_0) \neq 0$. By continuity, there is an open interval $U$

outside a sufficiently large compact set, giving $|f(x_n)| < |f(x_0)|/4$ along
another subsequence.

does not converge. $\square$
