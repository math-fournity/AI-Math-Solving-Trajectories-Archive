# Solution: Heat Equation with Nonconstant Thermal Properties

## Setup: Separation of Variables

Given the PDE
$$c(x)\rho(x)\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\!\left_
0(x)\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right],\qquad u(x,0)=f(x),$$
we substitute $u(x,t)=\phi(x)h(t)$. The hint gives
$$\frac{1}{h}\frac{dh}{dt}=\frac{1}{c(x)\rho(x)\,\phi}\frac{d}{dx}\!\left_0(x)
\frac{d\phi}{dx}\right]=-\lambda,$$
which separates into:

**Time equation:**
$$h'(t)=-\lambda\,h(t)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad h(t)=e^{-\lambda t}.$$

**Spatial (eigenvalue) equation:**
$$\boxed{\;\frac{d}{dx}\!\left_0(x)\frac{d\phi}{dx}\right]+\lambda\,c(x)\rho(x
)\,\phi(x)=0,\qquad 0<x<L.\;}$$

This is a **regular Sturm–Liouville problem** with
$$p(x)=K_0(x),\qquad q(x)=0,\qquad w(x)=c(x)\rho(x),$$
where we assume $K_0(x)>0$ and $c(x)\rho(x)>0$ on $[0,L]$.

---

## Orthogonality of Eigenfunctions

By Sturm–Liouville theory, the eigenvalues $\{\lambda_n\}$ are real, and the cor
responding eigenfunctions $\{\phi_n(x)\}$ are **orthogonal with respect to the w
eight $w(x)=c(x)\rho(x)$**:

$$\int_0^L c(x)\rho(x)\,\phi_n(x)\,\phi_m(x)\,dx=0,\qquad n\neq m.$$

Moreover, $\{\phi_n\}$ forms a **complete** orthogonal set on $[0,L]$ with weigh
t $c\rho$, so any (sufficiently regular) $f(x)$ can be expanded as
$$f(x)=\sum_{n} a_n\,\phi_n(x).$$

---

## General Solution

Superposing the separated solutions:
$$\boxed{\;u(x,t)=\sum_{n} a_n\,\phi_n(x)\,e^{-\lambda_n t}.\;}$$

**Determining the coefficients.** At $t=0$:
$$f(x)=\sum_n a_n\,\phi_n(x).$$
Multiply both sides by $c(x)\rho(x)\,\phi_m(x)$ and integrate over $[0,L]$. By o
rthogonality, all cross-terms vanish, leaving:

$$\boxed{\;a_n=\frac{\displaystyle\int_0^L c(x)\rho(x)\,f(x)\,\phi_n(x)\,dx}{\di
splaystyle\int_0^L c(x)\rho(x)\,\bigl[\phi_n(x)\bigr]^2\,dx}.\;}$$

This is the **orthogonality-based coefficient formula**, valid for both sets of
boundary conditions below (with the appropriate eigenfunctions).

---

## Energy Identity (used for both parts)

Multiply the eigenvalue equation by $\phi$ and integrate:
$$\int_0^L \phi\,\frac{d}{dx}\!\left_0\phi'\right]dx+\lambda\int_0^L c\rho\,\p
hi^2\,dx=0.$$

Integration by parts:
$$\Bigl[\phi\,K_0\,\phi'\Bigr]_0^L-\int_0^L K_0(\phi')^2\,dx+\lambda\int_0^L c\r
ho\,\phi^2\,dx=0.$$

Hence
$$\lambda=\frac{\displaystyle\int_0^L K_0(x)\bigl[\phi'(x)\bigr]^2 dx\;-\;\Bigl[
\phi\,K_0\,\phi'\Bigr]_0^L}{\displaystyle\int_0^L c(x)\rho(x)\,\bigl[\phi(x)\big
r]^2 dx}.$$

---

## Part (a): Dirichlet BCs $\;u(0,t)=0,\; u(L,t)=0$

The eigenfunctions satisfy $\phi_n(0)=0$ and $\phi_n(L)=0$.

**Eigenvalues are strictly positive.** With $\phi(0)=\phi(L)=0$, the boundary te
rm $\bigl[\phi\,K_0\,\phi'\bigr]_0^L$ vanishes, so
$$\lambda_n=\frac{\displaystyle\int_0^L K_0\bigl[\phi_n'\bigr]^2 dx}{\displaysty
le\int_0^L c\rho\,\phi_n^2\,dx}>0\qquad(\text{for all }n),$$
since the numerator is non-negative and equals zero only if $\phi_n'\equiv 0$, w
hich (combined with $\phi_n(0)=0$) gives $\phi_n\equiv 0$, a trivial solution. T
hus every eigenvalue is strictly positive.

**Solution:**
$$\boxed{\;u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n\,\phi_n(x)\,e^{-\lambda_n t},\qquad \l
ambda_n>0,\;}$$
with
$$a_n=\frac{\displaystyle\int_0^L c(x)\rho(x)\,f(x)\,\phi_n(x)\,dx}{\displaystyl
e\int_0^L c(x)\rho(x)\,\bigl[\phi_n(x)\bigr]^2\,dx},$$
and $\phi_n$ solving
$$\frac{d}{dx}\!\left_0(x)\frac{d\phi_n}{dx}\right]+\lambda_n\,c(x)\rho(x)\,\p
hi_n=0,\qquad \phi_n(0)=\phi_n(L)=0.$$

**Long-time limit.** Since every $\lambda_n>0$, each exponential $e^{-\lambda_n
t}\to 0$ as $t\to\infty$. Therefore
$$\boxed{\;\lim_{t\to\infty}u(x,t)=0.\;}$$

Physically: both ends are held at zero temperature, so all heat eventually drain
s out.

---

## Part (b): Neumann BCs $\;\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x}(0,t)=0,\;\dfrac{\part
ial u}{\partial x}(L,t)=0$

The eigenfunctions satisfy $\phi_n'(0)=0$ and $\phi_n'(L)=0$.

**Zero eigenvalue.** Take $\phi_0=\text{const}$ (say $\phi_0=1$). Then $\phi_0'=
0$, so
$$\frac{d}{dx}\!\left_0\cdot 0\right]=0,\qquad 0+\lambda\,c\rho\cdot 1=0\;\Lon
grightarrow\;\lambda_0=0.$$
Thus $\lambda_0=0$ is an eigenvalue with constant eigenfunction $\phi_0=1$. This
 is the **steady-state (equilibrium) mode**.

**Positive eigenvalues for $n\ge 1$.** With $\phi_n'(0)=\phi_n'(L)=0$, the bound
ary term $\bigl[\phi\,K_0\,\phi'\bigr]_0^L=0$ again vanishes, giving
$$\lambda_n=\frac{\displaystyle\int_0^L K_0\bigl[\phi_n'\bigr]^2 dx}{\displaysty
le\int_0^L c\rho\,\phi_n^2\,dx}\ge 0.$$
For $n\ge 1$, $\phi_n$ is not constant, so $\phi_n'\not\equiv 0$ and $\lambda_n>
0$.

**Solution:**
$$\boxed{\;u(x,t)=a_0+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n\,\phi_n(x)\,e^{-\lambda_n t},\qqua
d \lambda_0=0,\;\lambda_n>0\;(n\ge 1),\;}$$
where $\phi_0=1$ and for $n\ge 1$, $\phi_n$ solves
$$\frac{d}{dx}\!\left_0(x)\frac{d\phi_n}{dx}\right]+\lambda_n\,c(x)\rho(x)\,\p
hi_n=0,\qquad \phi_n'(0)=\phi_n'(L)=0.$$

**Coefficients** (same orthogonality formula, now including $n=0$):
$$a_n=\frac{\displaystyle\int_0^L c(x)\rho(x)\,f(x)\,\phi_n(x)\,dx}{\displaystyl
e\int_0^L c(x)\rho(x)\,\bigl[\phi_n(x)\bigr]^2\,dx},\qquad n=0,1,2,\dots$$

For $n=0$ (with $\phi_0=1$) this specializes to:
$$\boxed{\;a_0=\frac{\displaystyle\int_0^L c(x)\rho(x)\,f(x)\,dx}{\displaystyle\
int_0^L c(x)\rho(x)\,dx},\;}$$
which is the **weighted average of the initial temperature** $f(x)$ with weight
$c(x)\rho(x)$ (the heat capacity per unit length).

**Long-time limit.** Since $\lambda_n>0$ for all $n\ge 1$, every $e^{-\lambda_n
t}\to 0$ as $t\to\infty$. Only the $n=0$ term survives:
$$\boxed{\;\lim_{t\to\infty}u(x,t)=a_0=\frac{\displaystyle\int_0^L c(x)\rho(x)\,
f(x)\,dx}{\displaystyle\int_0^L c(x)\rho(x)\,dx}.\;}$$

Physically: with insulated (no-flux) boundaries, no heat escapes, so the rod rel
axes to a uniform temperature equal to the total heat energy divided by the tota
l heat capacity — exactly the weighted average above.

## Summary

| | **(a) Dirichlet** $u(0,t)=u(L,t)=0$ | **(b) Neumann** $u_x(0,t)=u_x(L,t)=0$
|
|---|---|---|
| Eigenvalue problem | $\bigl(K_0\phi_n'\bigr)'+\lambda_n c\rho\,\phi_n=0$, $\ph
i_n(0)=\phi_n(L)=0$ | $\bigl(K_0\phi_n'\bigr)'+\lambda_n c\rho\,\phi_n=0$, $\phi
_n'(0)=\phi_n'(L)=0$ |
| Eigenvalues | $\lambda_n>0$ for all $n$ | $\lambda_0=0$ (const. eigenfunction)
, $\lambda_n>0$ for $n\ge 1$ |
| Orthogonality weight | $w(x)=c(x)\rho(x)$ | $w(x)=c(x)\rho(x)$ |
| Solution | $u=\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n\phi_n e^{-\lambda_n t}$ | $u=a_0+\sum_{n=1
}^\infty a_n\phi_n e^{-\lambda_n t}$ |
| Coefficients | $a_n=\dfrac{\int_0^L c\rho\,f\,\phi_n\,dx}{\int_0^L c\rho\,\phi
_n^2\,dx}$ | same formula, $n=0,1,2,\dots$ |
| $\lim_{t\to\infty}u$ | $0$ | $a_0=\dfrac{\int_0^L c\rho\,f\,dx}{\int_0^L c\rho
\,dx}$ (weighted average) |

The key insight throughout is that the nonconstant coefficients $K_0(x)$, $c(x)$
, $\rho(x)$ enter the Sturm–Liouville framework naturally: $K_0$ plays the role
of $p(x)$ and $c\rho$ plays the role of the weight $w(x)$. The standard orthogon
ality, completeness, and eigenvalue-sign results of Sturm–Liouville theory then
apply unchanged, and the coefficient formula acquires the weight $c(x)\rho(x)$.
