**Answer: Yes, such a sequence exists for every prime p.**

**Construction.** We build a sequence of multiquadratic fields in which p splits
 completely, with degrees tending to infinity.

**Step 1: Infinitely many quadratic fields where p splits.**

*Case p odd.* A prime p (odd, p ∤ d, d squarefree) splits completely in Q(√d) if
 and only if d is a quadratic residue mod p, i.e. (d/p) = +1. By quadratic recip
rocity and Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions, there are i
nfinitely many primes q ≠ p with (q/p) = +1 (indeed, a positive density of prime
s satisfy this).

*Case p = 2.* The prime 2 splits completely in Q(√d) (d odd squarefree) if and o
nly if d ≡ 1 (mod 8). By Dirichlet's theorem, there are infinitely many primes q
 ≡ 1 (mod 8).

In either case, we obtain an infinite list of distinct primes q_1, q_2, q_3, … (
all different from p) such that **p splits completely in each Q(√q_i)**.

**Step 2: Linear disjointness.** Since q_1, q_2, … are distinct primes, they are
 independent in Q^×/(Q^×)^2: no product of any subset is a perfect square. By Ku
mmer theory (or a straightforward induction), each √q_{k+1} does not lie in Q(√q
_1, …, √q_k). Hence

$$[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q_1},\dots,\sqrt{q_n}):\mathbb{Q}] = 2^n.$$

**Step 3: Definition of K_n and verification.** Set

$$K_n = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q_1}, \sqrt{q_2}, \dots, \sqrt{q_n}).$$

- **Degree tends to infinity:** _n : Q] = 2^n → ∞.
- **p splits completely in each K_n:** Each Q(√q_i) is a Galois (quadratic) exte
nsion in which p splits completely (Step 1), and p is unramified in each (since
p ≠ q_i). The compositum K_n is Galois. The Frobenius element Frob_p is trivial
in Gal(Q(√q_i)/Q) for every i, hence trivial in Gal(K_n/Q) ≅ (Z/2Z)^n. Therefore
 p is unramified in K_n with trivial Frobenius, which means p splits completely
in O_{K_n}.

**Conclusion.** The sequence (K_n) satisfies _n : Q] = 2^n → ∞ and p splits co
mpletely in O_{K_n} for every n. Thus the answer is **yes** for every prime p.
