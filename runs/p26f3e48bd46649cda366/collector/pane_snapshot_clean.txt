## Proof

**Setup and reduction to $\mathbb{R}^n$.** Since $X$ is a *proper* neighborhood
retract of $S^n$, we have $X \subsetneq S^n$, so there exists a point $q \in S^n
 \setminus X$. The original retraction $r: U_0 \to X$ is defined on some open ne
ighborhood $U_0 \supset X$. Replacing $U_0$ by
$$U := U_0 \cap (S^n \setminus \{q\}),$$
we obtain an open set still containing $X$ (since $q \notin X$), on which the re
stricted retraction $r|_U: U \to X$ satisfies $r|_X = \mathrm{id}_X$. Moreover $
U \subset S^n \setminus \{q\} \cong \mathbb{R}^n$.

For $n \geq 2$, removing a single point from a connected $n$-manifold preserves
connectedness, so $U$ remains connected. For $n = 1$, if $U_0 = S^1$ then $U = S
^1 \setminus \{q\} \cong \mathbb{R}$ is connected; if $U_0 \neq S^1$ we may pick
 $q \notin U_0$ so $U = U_0$ is unchanged and connected by hypothesis. In all ca
ses ($n \geq 1$) we may assume:

> **($\star$)** $U$ is a connected open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$, hence a connec
ted, **non-compact** $n$-manifold.

---

**Step 1: $H_n(U) = 0$.** A connected non-compact $n$-manifold has trivial top-d
imensional homology. This is a standard consequence of Poincaré duality with com
pact supports:
$$H_n(U) \cong H_c^0(U).$$
An element of $H_c^0(U)$ is represented by a locally constant function $f: U \to
 \mathbb{Z}$ with compact support. Since $U$ is connected, $f$ is constant; if $
f \neq 0$ its support is all of $U$, which is non-compact—a contradiction. Hence
 $f = 0$ and $H_c^0(U) = 0$. Therefore
$$H_n(U) = 0. \tag{1}$$

---

**Step 2: The long exact sequence of the pair $(U, B_p)$.** Let $B_p \subset U$
be a small open ball around $p$; $B_p$ is contractible, so
$$H_k(B_p) = 0 \quad \text{for all } k \geq 1, \qquad H_0(B_p) \cong \mathbb{Z}.
 \tag{2}$$

The long exact sequence of the pair $(U, B_p)$ reads:
$$\cdots \to H_n(B_p) \to H_n(U) \to H_n(U, B_p) \xrightarrow{\partial} H_{n-1}(
B_p) \to H_{n-1}(U) \to \cdots$$

**Case $n \geq 2$:** By (2), $H_n(B_p) = 0$ and $H_{n-1}(B_p) = 0$. The exact se
quence collapses to
$$0 \to H_n(U) \to H_n(U, B_p) \to 0,$$
so $H_n(U, B_p) \cong H_n(U) = 0$ by (1).

**Case $n = 1$:** The relevant portion of the LES is
$$\underbrace{H_1(B_p)}_{=0} \to H_1(U) \to H_1(U, B_p) \xrightarrow{\partial} \
underbrace{H_0(B_p)}_{\cong\,\mathbb{Z}} \xrightarrow{i_*} H_0(U).$$
Since $U$ is connected and $B_p$ is a nonempty connected subset, the inclusion-i
nduced map $i_*: H_0(B_p) \xrightarrow{\sim} H_0(U) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ is an isom
orphism. Therefore $\ker i_* = 0$, so by exactness $\operatorname{im} \partial =
 0$, i.e., $\partial = 0$. This makes the map $H_1(U) \to H_1(U, B_p)$ **surject
ive**. Since $U$ is a connected open subset of $\mathbb{R}$ (hence an open inter
val, contractible), $H_1(U) = 0$ by (1). Surjectivity of $0 \to H_1(U, B_p)$ giv
es
$$H_1(U, B_p) = 0.$$

---

**Conclusion.** In all cases $n \geq 1$,
$$\boxed{H_n(U,\, B_p) = 0.}$$

The essential ingredients are: (i) properness of $X$ forces $U$ into $\mathbb{R}
^n$ (via excision of a point $q \notin X$), making $U$ a non-compact $n$-manifol
d with $H_n(U)=0$; (ii) contractibility of the ball $B_p$ trivializes the flanki
ng terms $H_n(B_p)$ and $H_{n-1}(B_p)$ in the LES of the pair, so that $H_n(U, B
_p) \cong H_n(U) = 0$ (with a minor adjustment via $H_0$ for $n=1$).
