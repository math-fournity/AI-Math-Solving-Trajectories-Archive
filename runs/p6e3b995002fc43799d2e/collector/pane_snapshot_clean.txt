## Solution

The behavior functor $B : \mathbf{Set} \to \mathbf{Set}$ is defined by
$$BX = 1 + \mathrm{Act} \times X,$$
where $1$ is a singleton set $\{\star\}$ and $\mathrm{Act}$ is a fixed set of ac
tions. A $B$-coalgebra is a set $X$ equipped with a map $c : X \to 1 + \mathrm{A
ct} \times X$. Each state $x \in X$ is thereby classified as either:

- **terminated**: $c(x) = \iota_1(\star)$, meaning the process stops, or
- **active**: $c(x) = \iota_2(a, x')$ with $a \in \mathrm{Act}$, $x' \in X$, mea
ning the process performs action $a$ and transitions to successor state $x'$.

---

### The final coalgebra $D$

Let $D$ be the set of **all finite or infinite sequences** (lists/streams) of ac
tions:
$$D \;=\; \mathrm{Act}^{*} \;\cup\; \mathrm{Act}^{\omega},$$
where $\mathrm{Act}^{*} = \bigcup_{n \geq 0} \mathrm{Act}^{n}$ is the set of fin
ite sequences (including the empty sequence $\varepsilon$) and $\mathrm{Act}^{\o
mega}$ is the set of countably infinite sequences.

The coalgebra structure $\zeta : D \to 1 + \mathrm{Act} \times D$ is defined by
$$\zeta(\varepsilon) = \iota_1(\star), \qquad \zeta(a \cdot s) = \iota_2(a, s),$
$
for $a \in \mathrm{Act}$ and $s \in D$. In words: the empty sequence is "termina
ted," and a non-empty sequence $a \cdot s$ decomposes into its first action $a$
and its tail $s$.

---

### Proof of finality

We show that $(D, \zeta)$ is terminal in the category of $B$-coalgebras. Let $(X
, c)$ be any $B$-coalgebra. We must produce a **unique** coalgebra homomorphism
$h : X \to D$, i.e. a unique map making the following diagram commute:

$$\begin{array}{ccc}
X & \xrightarrow{h} & D \\
{\scriptstyle c}\downarrow & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle \zeta} \\
1 + \mathrm{Act}\times X & \xrightarrow{\mathrm{id} + \mathrm{Act}\times h} & 1
+ \mathrm{Act}\times D
\end{array}$$

The commutativity condition is $\zeta \circ h = (\mathrm{id} + \mathrm{Act}\time
s h) \circ c$, which forces the **corecursive definition**:

- If $c(x) = \iota_1(\star)$, then $h(x) = \varepsilon$ (the empty sequence).
- If $c(x) = \iota_2(a, x')$, then $h(x) = a \cdot h(x')$ (prepend $a$ to the be
havior of $x'$).

**Existence.** Define $h(x)$ as the (possibly infinite) sequence of actions obta
ined by iterating $c$:
$$h(x) = a_0 \cdot a_1 \cdot a_2 \cdots$$
where $a_0, a_1, \ldots$ are the actions emitted along the trajectory $x = x_0 \
to x_1 \to x_2 \to \cdots$ determined by $c(x_i) = \iota_2(a_i, x_{i+1})$. If at
 some finite stage $n$ we reach $c(x_n) = \iota_1(\star)$, the sequence terminat
es after $n$ actions, giving a finite list $a_0 \cdots a_{n-1} \in \mathrm{Act}^
{*}$. If no termination is ever reached, the sequence is infinite, giving an ele
ment of $\mathrm{Act}^{\omega}$. In either case $h(x) \in D$, and by constructio
n $\zeta(h(x)) = (\mathrm{id} + \mathrm{Act}\times h)(c(x))$, so $h$ is a coalge
bra homomorphism.

**Uniqueness.** Suppose $h, h' : X \to D$ are both coalgebra homomorphisms. We s
how $h = h'$ by **coinduction**. Define the relation $R = \{(h(x), h'(x)) : x \i
n X\} \subseteq D \times D$. We claim $R$ is a $B$-bisimulation. Take $(h(x), h'
(x)) \in R$:
- If $c(x) = \iota_1(\star)$, then $h(x) = h'(x) = \varepsilon$, so both map und
er $\zeta$ to $\iota_1(\star)$ — trivially matched.
- If $c(x) = \iota_2(a, x')$, then $h(x) = a \cdot h(x')$ and $h'(x) = a \cdot h
'(x')$, so $\zeta(h(x)) = \iota_2(a, h(x'))$ and $\zeta(h'(x)) = \iota_2(a, h'(x
'))$ share the same action $a$, and $(h(x'), h'(x')) \in R$.

Thus $R$ is a bisimulation. Since the final coalgebra is the greatest fixed poin
t of $B$, the greatest bisimulation on $D$ is the identity (by Lambek's lemma, $
\zeta$ is an isomorphism, so bisimilarity on $D$ coincides with equality). There
fore $h(x) = h'(x)$ for all $x$, proving uniqueness.

---

### Structure summary

The final coalgebra $D = \mathrm{Act}^{*} \cup \mathrm{Act}^{\omega}$ is the set
 of **all finite and infinite execution traces** over $\mathrm{Act}$. Its struct
ure map $\zeta : D \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} 1 + \mathrm{Act} \times D$ is an **iso
morphism** (Lambek's lemma): every trace is either empty (terminated) or decompo
ses uniquely into a first action and a remaining trace. The unique homomorphism
$h : (X, c) \to (D, \zeta)$ sends each state to its **observable behavior** — th
e complete (finite or infinite) sequence of actions it produces — which is preci
sely the **behavioral semantics** that motivates calling $B$ the behavior functo
r.
