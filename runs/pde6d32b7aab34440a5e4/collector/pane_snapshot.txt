**Exploration.** Place A at the origin, B = (c, 0), C = (b cos A, b sin A). Solv
ing AP = AB with PB ∥ AC gives P = (−c cos 2A, −c sin 2A), and AQ = AC with CQ ∥

- **P is the reflection of B over the line ℓ_B through A perpendicular to AC.**
- **Q is the reflection of C over the line ℓ_C through A perpendicular to AB.**

(Indeed, reflection over ℓ_B fixes A so preserves distance to A, and the segment

This reflection structure is the key. Here is the proof.

**Proof.**

Place A at the origin. Let **u** = AB⃗ and **v** = AC⃗. Let O denote the circumcen
ter of △ABC.

**Step 1 — Reflection characterization.** Let ℓ_B be the line through A perpendi
cular to AC, and ℓ_C the line through A perpendicular to AB. As verified above:

- P = reflection of B over ℓ_B,
- Q = reflection of C over ℓ_C.

**Step 2 — Reflected points.** Define:
- O_B = reflection of O over ℓ_B, and C' = reflection of C over ℓ_B,
- O_C = reflection of O over ℓ_C, and B' = reflection of B over ℓ_C.

Since reflection over ℓ_B sends B ↦ P, O ↦ O_B, C ↦ C' and preserves angles:
$$\angle OPC = \angle O_B\, B\, C'.$$
Since reflection over ℓ_C sends C ↦ Q, O ↦ O_C, B ↦ B':
$$\angle OQB = \angle O_C\, C\, B'.$$

**Step 3 — Key identity: BO⃗_B = CO⃗_C and BC⃗' = CB⃗'.**

Let n̂_B be the unit vector along AC (normal to ℓ_B) and n̂_C the unit vector alon
g AB (normal to ℓ_C). Reflection over a line through the origin with unit normal
 n̂ sends X to X − 2(X·n̂)n̂, so:
$$O_B - O_C = 2\bigl[(O\cdot \hat{n}_C)\hat{n}_C - (O\cdot \hat{n}_B)\hat{n}_B\b
igr].$$

Since O is the circumcenter, it lies on the perpendicular bisector of AB, giving
 O · **u** = |**u**|²/2, i.e. O · n̂_C = |**u**|/2. Likewise O · n̂_B = |**v**|/2
from the perpendicular bisector of AC. Therefore:
$$O_B - O_C = 2\!\left[\frac{|\mathbf{u}|}{2}\hat{n}_C - \frac{|\mathbf{v}|}{2}\
hat{n}_B\right] = \mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v} = B - C,$$
using **u** = |**u**|n̂_C and **v** = |**v**|n̂_B. Hence **O_B − B = O_C − C**, i.
e. $\overrightarrow{BO_B} = \overrightarrow{CO_C}$.

For the other pair: since B lies along AB ⊥ ℓ_C through the origin, reflecting B
 over ℓ_C gives B' = −B; similarly C' = −C. Thus:
$$\overrightarrow{BC'} = C' - B = -C - B = B' - C = \overrightarrow{CB'}.$$

**Step 4 — Conclusion.** Since the two side vectors at B in △O_B B C' equal the
two side vectors at C in △O_C C B' (namely $\overrightarrow{BO_B} = \overrightar
row{CO_C}$ and $\overrightarrow{BC'} = \overrightarrow{CB'}$), the included angl
es are equal:
$$\angle O_B\, B\, C' = \angle O_C\, C\, B'.$$

By Step 2, $\angle OPC = \angle OQB$.

Since X lies on line CP and on line BQ, the directed angles satisfy $\angle OPX
= \angle OPC$ and $\angle OQX = \angle OQB$ (mod π). Therefore:
$$\angle OPX = \angle OQX \pmod{\pi}.$$

By the converse of the inscribed angle theorem, P and Q subtend equal directed a
ngles from segment OX, so O, P, X, Q are concyclic. Hence the circumcenter O of
△ABC lies on the circumcircle of △PXQ. $\blacksquare$
