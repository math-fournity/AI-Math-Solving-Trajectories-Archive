## Solution

We evaluate $I = \displaystyle\int_{|z|=1} \frac{dz}{\sin z}$, where the contour
 $|z|=1$ is traversed counterclockwise.

**Step 1: Locate the poles of $f(z) = 1/\sin z$ inside $|z| < 1$.**

The singularities of $f$ occur at the zeros of $\sin z$, which are $z = n\pi$ fo
r $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. We check which lie inside the unit disk:

- For $n \neq 0$: $|n\pi| = |n|\,\pi \geq \pi \approx 3.14159 > 1$, so these are
 all **outside** $|z|=1$.
- For $n = 0$: $z = 0$ lies **inside** $|z| < 1$.

Hence the only pole of $f$ enclosed by the contour is at $z = 0$.

**Step 2: Determine the order and residue at $z = 0$.**

Since $\sin z = z - \frac{z^3}{6} + \cdots = z\!\left(1 - \frac{z^2}{6} + \cdots
\right)$, the point $z = 0$ is a **simple zero** of $\sin z$. Therefore $f(z) =
1/\sin z$ has a **simple pole** at $z = 0$.

The residue at a simple pole of $1/g(z)$, where $g$ has a simple zero at $z_0$,
is

$$\operatorname{Res}\!\left(\frac{1}{g},\, z_0\right) = \frac{1}{g'(z_0)}.$$

With $g(z) = \sin z$ and $g'(z) = \cos z$:

$$\operatorname{Res}\!\left(\frac{1}{\sin z},\, 0\right) = \frac{1}{\cos 0} = \f
rac{1}{1} = 1.$$

**Step 3: Apply the residue theorem.**

By Cauchy's residue theorem,

$$I = 2\pi i \sum_{\text{poles inside}} \operatorname{Res}(f, z_k) = 2\pi i \cdo
t \operatorname{Res}\!\left(\frac{1}{\sin z},\, 0\right) = 2\pi i \cdot 1 = 2\pi
 i.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{|z|=1} \frac{dz}{\sin z} = 2\pi i}$$
