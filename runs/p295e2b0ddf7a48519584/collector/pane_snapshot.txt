# Solution

**Answer: Yes** (for bounded double complexes, the standard setting). The proof
uses the spectral sequence comparison theorem.

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $K^{\bullet,\bullet}$ and $L^{\bullet,\bullet}$ be bounded double
 complexes (e.g., first-quadrant) with commuting differentials $d$ (vertical, wi
thin each column $K^{p,\bullet}$) and $\delta$ (horizontal, between columns), sa
tisfying $d\delta + \delta d = 0$. The **total complex** $\operatorname{Tot}(K)^
\bullet$ has $(\operatorname{Tot}(K))^n = \bigoplus_{p+q=n} K^{p,q}$ with total
differential $D = d + \delta$ (with appropriate sign conventions ensuring $D^2 =
 0$). The map $f: K^{\bullet,\bullet} \to L^{\bullet,\bullet}$ preserves both $d
$ and $\delta$, hence induces a map $f^*: H_D(K^\bullet) \to H_D(L^\bullet)$ on
total cohomology.

**Step 1: The column filtration spectral sequence.** Filter the total complex by
 columns:
$$F^p \operatorname{Tot}(K)^n = \bigoplus_{i \geq p} K^{i,\,n-i}.$$
This is a bounded, decreasing filtration (bounded because the double complex is
bounded). The associated spectral sequence $\{E_r^{p,q}(K)\}$ has:
- $E_0^{p,q} = K^{p,q}$, with $d_0 = d$,
- $E_1^{p,q} = H_d^q(K^{p,\bullet})$, with $d_1$ induced by $\delta$,
- $E_2^{p,q} = H_\delta^p\, H_d^q(K)$,

and converges (by boundedness) to the total cohomology:
$$E_\infty^{p,q} \cong \operatorname{gr}^p\, H_D^{p+q}(\operatorname{Tot}(K)).$$

**Step 2: $f$ induces a morphism of spectral sequences.** Since $f$ preserves bo
th $d$ and $\delta$, it respects the column filtration and commutes with all the
 differentials $d_r$. Thus $f$ induces maps $f_r: E_r^{p,q}(K) \to E_r^{p,q}(L)$
 for every $r \geq 1$, satisfying $d_r \circ f_r = f_r \circ d_r$.

**Step 3: $f_1$ is an isomorphism by hypothesis.** By assumption, $f$ induces is
omorphisms $H_d(K^{p,\bullet}) \xrightarrow{\sim} H_d(L^{p,\bullet})$ for all $p
$. Since $E_1^{p,q}(K) = H_d^q(K^{p,\bullet})$, the map
$$f_1: E_1^{p,q}(K) \xrightarrow{\sim} E_1^{p,q}(L)$$
is an isomorphism for all $(p,q)$.

**Step 4: Propagation to all pages.** Since $f_1$ is an isomorphism and $d_1 \ci
rc f_1 = f_1 \circ d_1$, the induced map on homology
$$f_2 = H_{d_1}(f_1): E_2^{p,q}(K) \to E_2^{p,q}(L)$$
is an isomorphism. By induction on $r$: if $f_r$ is an isomorphism, then $f_{r+1
} = H_{d_r}(f_r)$ is an isomorphism. Hence $f_r$ is an isomorphism for all $r \g
eq 1$.

**Step 5: Convergence and conclusion.** By boundedness, the spectral sequence co
nverges: for each $(p,q)$, the groups $E_r^{p,q}$ stabilize for $r \gg 0$, givin
g $E_\infty^{p,q}$. Since each $f_r$ is an isomorphism, the limit map
$$f_\infty: E_\infty^{p,q}(K) \xrightarrow{\sim} E_\infty^{p,q}(L)$$
is an isomorphism for all $(p,q)$.

Now $E_\infty^{p,q} \cong \operatorname{gr}^p H_D^{p+q}$, so $f^*$ induces an is
omorphism on every graded piece of the filtration on $H_D^n$. The filtration on
$H_D^n$ is finite (bounded), say
$$0 = F^{s+1} \subseteq F^s \subseteq \cdots \subseteq F^t = H_D^n,$$
and $f^*$ maps each $F^i$ to $F^i$ with $F^i / F^{i+1} \xrightarrow{\sim} F^i /
F^{i+1}$. By induction on the filtration length (or equivalently, repeated appli
cation of the five lemma to the short exact sequences $0 \to F^{i+1} \to F^i \to
 F^i/F^{i+1} \to 0$), we conclude:
$$f^*: H_D^n(\operatorname{Tot}(K)) \xrightarrow{\sim} H_D^n(\operatorname{Tot}(
L))$$
is an isomorphism for every $n$.

**Remark on boundedness.** The boundedness assumption (or more generally, a conv
ergence condition ensuring the spectral sequence converges strongly to $H_D$) is
 essential. For unbounded double complexes, the spectral sequence may fail to co
nverge, and the implication can break down. In the standard setting of bounded (
e.g., first- or last-quadrant) double complexes—which is the natural context for
 this result—the answer is affirmative.

$\blacksquare$
