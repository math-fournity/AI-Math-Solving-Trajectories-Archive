## Proof

Convert to polar coordinates. Let $r^2 = x^2 + y^2$, so $dx\,dy = r\,dr\,d\theta
$:

$$I = \iint_{\mathbb{R}^2} \frac{\sin(x^2+y^2)}{(x^2+y^2+1)^\alpha}\,dx\,dy = \i
nt_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin(r^2)}{(r^2+1)^\alpha}\,r\,dr\,d\theta =
2\pi\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin(r^2)\,r}{(r^2+1)^\alpha}\,dr.$$

Substitute $u = r^2$, $du = 2r\,dr$:

$$I = \pi\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin u}{(u+1)^\alpha}\,du.$$

So convergence of $I$ is equivalent to convergence of $\displaystyle\int_0^\inft
y \frac{\sin u}{(u+1)^\alpha}\,du$.

**Near $u = 0$:** $\sin u/(u+1)^\alpha \sim u$, which is integrable on $[0,1]$ f
or every $\alpha\in\mathbb{R}$. No restriction from the origin.

**At infinity:** we study $\displaystyle\int_1^\infty \frac{\sin u}{(u+1)^\alpha
}\,du$, where the integrand behaves like $\sin u / u^\alpha$.

- **If $\alpha > 0$:** $g(u) = (u+1)^{-\alpha}$ is positive, decreasing, and $g(
u)\to 0$. Since $\int_1^T \sin u\,du$ is bounded (by $2$), the **Dirichlet test*
* gives convergence of $\int_1^\infty \frac{\sin u}{(u+1)^\alpha}\,du$.

- **If $\alpha = 0$:** $\int_1^T \sin u\,du = \cos 1 - \cos T$, which has no lim
it as $T\to\infty$. The integral **diverges**.

- **If $\alpha < 0$:** write $\alpha = -\beta$ with $\beta > 0$, so the integran
d is $(u+1)^\beta\sin u$, whose amplitude grows without bound. The partial integ
rals $\int_1^T (u+1)^\beta\sin u\,du$ oscillate with growing amplitude (integrat
ion by parts gives boundary terms of size $(T+1)^\beta\cos T$ that do not conver
ge). The integral **diverges**.

Combining the behavior at $0$ and at $\infty$: the integral converges if and onl
y if $\alpha > 0$.

(More precisely: for $\alpha > 1$ it converges absolutely, since $\int_1^\infty
|\sin u|/u^\alpha\,du \le \int_1^\infty u^{-\alpha}\,du < \infty$; for $0 < \alp
ha \le 1$ it converges conditionally by Dirichlet but not absolutely, since $|\s
in u|/u^\alpha$ has mean value $\sim \tfrac{2}{\pi}u^{-\alpha}$ whose integral d
iverges when $\alpha \le 1$.)

**Answer:** The double integral converges if and only if $\alpha > 0$.
