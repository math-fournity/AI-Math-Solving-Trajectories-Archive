**Problem.** Find the volume of the body defined by $0 \le z \le c\sin\!\bigl(\p
i\sqrt{x^2/a^2+y^2/b^2}\bigr)$, with $a,b,c>0$.

### Step 1. Identify the region of integration

Set $r = \sqrt{x^2/a^2 + y^2/b^2}$. The upper bound $c\sin(\pi r)$ is non-negati
ve only when $\sin(\pi r)\ge 0$, i.e. on the intervals $r\in[0,1]\cup[2,3]\cup[4
,5]\cup\cdots$. On each such interval the inequality carves out a separate "lobe
" of the body. The lobe at $r\in[0,1]$ is the unique bounded component containin
g the origin; the remaining lobes form an infinite sequence extending to $r\to\i
nfty$, and their total volume diverges (as will be shown). The **bounded body**
referred to in the problem is therefore the first lobe, $0\le r\le 1$.

### Step 2. Change to elliptical polar coordinates

Let $u=x/a,\; v=y/b$, so $dx\,dy = ab\,du\,dv$ and $r=\sqrt{u^2+v^2}$. Then swit
ch to polar: $u=\rho\cos\theta,\;v=\rho\sin\theta$, $du\,dv=\rho\,d\rho\,d\theta
$, with $\rho=r$. The volume of the first lobe is

$$V = abc\int_0^{2\pi}\!d\theta\int_0^1 \rho\sin(\pi\rho)\,d\rho = 2\pi\,abc\int
_0^1 \rho\sin(\pi\rho)\,d\rho.$$

### Step 3. Evaluate the radial integral

Integrate by parts with $u=\rho$, $dv=\sin(\pi\rho)\,d\rho$:

$$\int \rho\sin(\pi\rho)\,d\rho = -\frac{\rho\cos(\pi\rho)}{\pi}+\frac{\sin(\pi\
rho)}{\pi^2}.$$

Evaluating on $[0,1]$:

- At $\rho=1$: $-\dfrac{1\cdot\cos\pi}{\pi}+\dfrac{\sin\pi}{\pi^2} = \dfrac{1}{\
pi}+0=\dfrac{1}{\pi}$.
- At $\rho=0$: $-\dfrac{0\cdot\cos 0}{\pi}+\dfrac{\sin 0}{\pi^2}=0$.

Hence $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \rho\sin(\pi\rho)\,d\rho = \frac{1}{\pi}$.

### Step 4. Compute the volume

$$V = 2\pi\,abc\cdot\frac{1}{\pi} = 2abc.$$

### Step 5. Verify the remaining lobes give divergent total (confirming the firs
t lobe is intended)

For the $k$-th lobe ($\rho\in[2k,\,2k+1]$, $k=0,1,2,\dots$):

$$\int_{2k}^{2k+1}\rho\sin(\pi\rho)\,d\rho = \Bigl[-\tfrac{\rho\cos(\pi\rho)}{\p
i}+\tfrac{\sin(\pi\rho)}{\pi^2}\Bigr]_{2k}^{2k+1}.$$

Using $\cos((2k+1)\pi)=-1$, $\cos(2k\pi)=1$, $\sin(\cdot)=0$ at both endpoints:

$$= \frac{2k+1}{\pi}+\frac{2k}{\pi}=\frac{4k+1}{\pi}.$$

The total over all lobes would be $2\pi\,abc\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{4k+1}{\pi}=
2abc\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(4k+1)=\infty$, which is not a finite volume. Thus the pr
oblem's "body bounded by the surface" is the single bounded lobe at the origin,
and its volume is

$$\boxed{V = 2abc}.$$
