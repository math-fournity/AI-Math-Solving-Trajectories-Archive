# Maximal Order of an Abelian Subgroup of $S_n$

## Setup and notation

Let $H \leq S_n$ be an Abelian subgroup. Let $O_1, \dots, O_m$ be the orbits of
$H$ acting on $\{1, \dots, n\}$, with $|O_i| = k_i$ and $\sum_{i=1}^m k_i = n$.

## Step 1 — Transitive Abelian groups are regular

**Claim.** If an Abelian group $G$ acts transitively on a set $X$ with $|X| = k$
, then the action is regular, so $|G| = k$.

*Proof.* Fix $x \in X$. The stabilizer $G_x$ is a subgroup of $G$. Since $G$ is
Abelian, every subgroup is normal, so $G_x \trianglelefteq G$. For any $y \in X$
, by transitivity $y = g \cdot x$ for some $g \in G$, and then $G_y = g G_x g^{-
1} = G_x$. So $G_x$ stabilizes **every** point of $X$, meaning $G_x \leq \bigcap
_{y \in X} G_y = \ker(\text{action})$. But $G$ acts faithfully (as a subgroup of
 $\text{Sym}(X)$), so $G_x = \{e\}$. Hence $|G| = |X| = k$ by the orbit–stabiliz
er theorem. $\square$

## Step 2 — Upper bound via orbit decomposition

For each orbit $O_i$, let $\phi_i : H \to \text{Sym}(O_i) \cong S_{k_i}$ be the
restriction homomorphism, and let $A_i = \phi_i(H)$. Since $H$ is Abelian, $A_i$
 is Abelian; since $H$ acts transitively on $O_i$, so does $A_i$; by Step 1, $|A
_i| = k_i$.

The combined map $\phi = (\phi_1, \dots, \phi_m) : H \to \prod_{i=1}^m A_i$ is i
njective (an element fixing every orbit fixes every point, hence is the identity
). Therefore

$$|H| \leq \prod_{i=1}^m |A_i| = \prod_{i=1}^m k_i.$$

## Step 3 — The bound is achieved for every partition

Given any partition $n = k_1 + \dots + k_m$ with $k_i \geq 1$, partition $\{1, \
dots, n\}$ into disjoint blocks $B_i$ of size $k_i$, and let $\sigma_i$ be a $k_
i$-cycle on $B_i$ (fixing all other blocks). Set

$$H = \langle \sigma_1 \rangle \times \cdots \times \langle \sigma_m \rangle \co
ng C_{k_1} \times \cdots \times C_{k_m}.$$

Since the $\sigma_i$ have disjoint supports, they commute, so $H$ is Abelian, an
d $|H| = \prod k_i$. Thus the maximal order of an Abelian subgroup of $S_n$ equa
ls

$$M(n) := \max\Bigl\{\prod_{i=1}^m k_i : k_i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},\; \sum k_i
 = n\Bigr\}.$$

## Step 4 — Solving the integer product maximization

We maximize $\prod k_i$ over positive integers $k_i$ with $\sum k_i = n$.

**(a) No part equals 1 in an optimum.** If some $k_j = 1$ and some $k_\ell \geq
2$, replacing $(k_\ell, 1)$ by $(k_\ell + 1)$ keeps the sum fixed and changes th
e product from $k_\ell \cdot 1$ to $k_\ell + 1 > k_\ell$. (If all parts are 1, t
hen $n = 1$ and the product is 1.)

**(b) No part is $\geq 5$.** If $k_j \geq 5$, replace $k_j$ by $3 + (k_j - 3)$.
The product changes from $k_j$ to $3(k_j - 3) = 3k_j - 9$, and $3k_j - 9 > k_j \
iff 2k_j > 9 \iff k_j \geq 5$. So the product strictly increases—contradiction.

**(c) Parts equal to 4 can be split.** Replacing $4$ by $2 + 2$ gives product $2
 \cdot 2 = 4$, unchanged. So we may assume all parts are in $\{2, 3\}$.

**(d) At most two 2's.** Replacing $2 + 2 + 2$ (product $8$) by $3 + 3$ (product
 $9$) preserves the sum $6$ and increases the product. So an optimum has at most
 two parts equal to 2.

**Conclusion.** An optimal partition consists of $m$ threes and $r$ twos with $r
 \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ and $3m + 2r = n$. Solving:

- **$n \equiv 0 \pmod{3}$:** $r = 0$, $m = n/3$, product $= 3^{n/3}$.
- **$n \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$:** $r = 1$, $m = (n-2)/3$, product $= 2 \cdot 3^{(n-2)
/3}$.
- **$n \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$, $n \geq 4$:** $r = 2$, $m = (n-4)/3$, product $= 4 \c
dot 3^{(n-4)/3}$.
- **$n = 1$:** the only partition is $\{1\}$, product $= 1$.

## Verification of small cases

| $n$ | Optimal partition | $M(n)$ | Abelian subgroup |
|-----|-------------------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | $1$ | $1$ | $\{e\}$ |
| 2 | $2$ | $2$ | $\langle(12)\rangle \cong C_2$ |
| 3 | $3$ | $3$ | $\langle(123)\rangle \cong C_3$ |
| 4 | $2+2$ | $4$ | $\langle(12),(34)\rangle \cong C_2^2$ |
| 5 | $3+2$ | $6$ | $\langle(123),(45)\rangle \cong C_6$ |
| 6 | $3+3$ | $9$ | $\langle(123),(456)\rangle \cong C_3^2$ |
| 7 | $3+2+2$ | $12$ | $\langle(123),(45),(67)\rangle \cong C_3 \times C_2^2$ |

## Answer

The maximal order of an Abelian subgroup of $S_n$ is

$$\boxed{M(n) = \begin{cases} 3^{n/3} & \text{if } n \equiv 0 \pmod{3}, \\[4pt]
2 \cdot 3^{(n-2)/3} & \text{if } n \equiv 2 \pmod{3}, \\[4pt] 4 \cdot 3^{(n-4)/3
} & \text{if } n \equiv 1 \pmod{3},\ n \geq 4, \\[4pt] 1 & \text{if } n = 1. \en
d{cases}}$$

Equivalently (for $n \geq 2$), writing $n = 3q + r$ with $r \in \{0, 1, 2\}$:

$$M(n) = \begin{cases} 3^q & r = 0, \\ 4 \cdot 3^{q-1} & r = 1, \\ 2 \cdot 3^q &
 r = 2. \end{cases}$$

The extremal subgroup is a direct product of disjoint cyclic groups $C_{k_1} \ti
mes \cdots \times C_{k_m}$ where the $k_i$ are as many 3's as possible, with at
most two 2's (or one 4) to absorb the remainder.
