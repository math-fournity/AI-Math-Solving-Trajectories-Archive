## Proof

We verify that the level curves of $h(x,y) = \tfrac{y^2}{2} - \tfrac{x^2}{2} + \
tfrac{x^4}{4}$ give the complete global phase portrait of the system $\dot x = y
,\; \dot y = x - x^3$.

### Step 1. $h$ is a first integral (conserved along trajectories)

Differentiate along a solution:
$$\dot h = y\,\dot y - x\,\dot x + x^3\,\dot x = y(x-x^3) + (-x + x^3)\,y = y(x-
x^3) - y(x - x^3) = 0.$$
Hence $h$ is constant on every orbit. Every trajectory is therefore contained in
 a level set $\{h = c\}$.

### Step 2. Rewrite as kinetic + potential

Set $V(x) = -\tfrac{x^2}{2} + \tfrac{x^4}{4}$, so $h = \tfrac{y^2}{2} + V(x)$. T
hen:
- $V'(x) = -x + x^3 = x(x-1)(x+1)$, with critical points $x = 0, \pm 1$.
- $V''(x) = -1 + 3x^2$: $V''(0) = -1 < 0$ (local **maximum**), $V''(\pm 1) = 2 >
 0$ (local **minima**).
- $V(0) = 0$, $V(\pm 1) = -\tfrac{1}{4}$, and $V(x) \to +\infty$ as $|x|\to\inft
y$.

Thus $V$ has a "double-well" shape: two symmetric minima of value $-\tfrac14$ at
 $x=\pm 1$, and a local maximum of value $0$ at $x=0$.

### Step 3. Equilibria and their types

Equilibria satisfy $y=0$ and $x - x^3 = 0$, giving $(0,0), (1,0), (-1,0)$. The J
acobian is
$$J = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1-3x^2 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$
- At $(0,0)$: $J = \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$, eigenvalues $\pm 1$ →
**saddle**. Energy $h=0$.
- At $(\pm 1, 0)$: $J = \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-2&0\end{pmatrix}$, eigenvalues $\pm
 i\sqrt{2}$ → **centers** (confirmed nonlinear centers by the conserved $h$). En
ergy $h = -\tfrac14$.

### Step 4. Classification of level curves $h = c$

Since $h = \tfrac{y^2}{2} + V(x) \ge \min V = -\tfrac14$, level sets with $c < -
\tfrac14$ are **empty**.

**Case $c = -\tfrac14$:** Requires $y=0$ and $V(x)=-\tfrac14$, i.e. $x = \pm 1$.
 Two isolated points: the two centers.

**Case $-\tfrac14 < c < 0$:** The equation $y^2 = 2(c - V(x))$ requires $V(x) \l
e c < 0 = V(0)$. Since $V(0)=0 > c$, the region $\{V \le c\}$ does **not** conta
in $x=0$; it splits into two disjoint intervals, one inside each well around $x=
+1$ and one around $x=-1$. Each yields a **smooth closed curve** (a periodic orb
it) encircling the respective center. We obtain **two one-parameter families of
periodic orbits**, one in each well.

**Case $c = 0$:** $y^2 = -2V(x) = x^2 - \tfrac{x^4}{2} = \tfrac{x^2(2-x^2)}{2}$,
 defined for $|x| \le \sqrt{2}$. This is a **figure-eight (lemniscate)** passing
 through the saddle $(0,0)$ and the points $(\pm\sqrt{2}, 0)$. It consists of tw
o **homoclinic loops**, each connecting the saddle $(0,0)$ to itself, one enclos
ing $(1,0)$ and the other enclosing $(-1,0)$. This is the **separatrix**: it div
ides bounded motion inside the wells from the large outer orbits.

**Case $c > 0$:** Now $c > V(0) = 0$, so the region $\{V(x) \le c\}$ is a **sing
le connected interval** $[-x_*(c),\, x_*(c)]$ containing $x=0$. The level set is
 a single smooth closed curve encircling **all three equilibria**. These are **l
arge periodic orbits** that cross the separatrix region and surround both wells
and the saddle.

### Step 5. Why this is the *global* structure

Because $h$ is a first integral, every orbit lies within a single level set. Con
versely, away from the equilibria and the separatrix, each level set $\{h=c\}$ i
s a one-dimensional smooth manifold, hence coincides with a single orbit. Theref
ore the partition of the plane by level curves of $h$ is exactly the partition b
y orbits:

1. **Two centers** at $(\pm 1, 0)$ (energy $-\tfrac14$).
2. **Two families of periodic orbits** inside the wells (energy $-\tfrac14 < c <
 0$), each family accumulating on its center as $c \to -\tfrac14^+$ and on the s
eparatrix as $c \to 0^-$.
3. **A figure-eight separatrix** through the saddle $(0,0)$ (energy $0$), compos
ed of two homoclinic loops.
4. **One family of large periodic orbits** encircling all three equilibria (ener
gy $c > 0$), accumulating on the separatrix as $c \to 0^+$ and expanding to infi
nity as $c \to +\infty$.
5. **No orbits** with energy below $-\tfrac14$.

This is the complete global phase portrait: the topology of every orbit (equilib
rium / periodic / homoclinic) and the way orbits are organized globally is read
off entirely from the level curves of $h$. $\blacksquare$
