## Proof

The operator $T \in \mathcal{B}(\ell^2)$ acts by
$$T(x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4, \dots) = (-x_2, x_1, -x_4, x_3, \dots).$$

**Block decomposition.** Pairing coordinates as $(x_{2k-1}, x_{2k})$ for $k = 1,
 2, \dots$, the space $\ell^2$ decomposes as the orthogonal direct sum
$$\ell^2 = \bigoplus_{k=1}^{\infty} \mathbb{C}^2,$$
and $T$ acts block-diagonally:
$$T = \bigoplus_{k=1}^{\infty} A, \qquad A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \en
d{pmatrix}.$$

**Spectrum of the block.** The characteristic polynomial of $A$ is $\lambda^2 +
1$, so $\sigma(A) = \{i, -i\}$, with eigenvectors
$$A \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -i \end{pmatrix} = i \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -i \end{pmatr
ix}, \qquad A \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ i \end{pmatrix} = -i \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ i \
end{pmatrix}.$$

**$\{i,-i\} \subseteq \sigma_p(T)$ (point spectrum).** For each $k$, place the e
igenvector $(1,-i)^\top$ in the $k$-th block and $0$ elsewhere; the resulting ve
ctor $v^{(k)} \in \ell^2$ satisfies $T v^{(k)} = i\, v^{(k)}$. Hence $i$ is an e
igenvalue of $T$ (with infinite multiplicity). Likewise $-i$ is an eigenvalue. S
o $\{i, -i\} \subseteq \sigma_p(T) \subseteq \sigma(T)$.

**No other spectral values.** Let $\lambda \notin \{i, -i\}$. Then $A - \lambda
I_2$ is invertible, and since every block of $T$ equals $A$,
$$T - \lambda I = \bigoplus_{k=1}^{\infty} (A - \lambda I_2).$$
The inverse is
$$(T - \lambda I)^{-1} = \bigoplus_{k=1}^{\infty} (A - \lambda I_2)^{-1},$$
which is bounded on $\ell^2$ because
$$\big\|(T - \lambda I)^{-1}\big\| = \sup_{k} \big\|(A - \lambda I_2)^{-1}\big\|
 = \big\|(A - \lambda I_2)^{-1}\big\| < \infty,$$
the supremum being a single finite constant (all blocks identical). Thus $T - \l
ambda I$ is invertible in $\mathcal{B}(\ell^2)$, so $\lambda \notin \sigma(T)$.

**Conclusion.** Combining both inclusions,
$$\sigma(T) = \{i,\, -i\},$$
and the spectrum is purely point spectrum, with both $i$ and $-i$ being eigenval
ues of infinite multiplicity.
