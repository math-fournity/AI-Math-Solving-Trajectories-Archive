## Proof

Let $\partial G$ denote the circle $|z-2|=1$, oriented counterclockwise. Since $
f$ has a simple pole at $z_0 \in G$ and is holomorphic on a neighborhood of $\ov

**Step 1: No preimages on $\partial G$.** For $z \in \partial G$, $|f(z)|=1$. Si
nce $|a|>1$, we have $f(z)\neq a$ for all $z\in\partial G$. Thus $f^{-1}(a)\subs

**Step 2: Apply the argument principle to $f(z)-a$.** The function $f(z)-a$ is m
eromorphic on $G$ with exactly one simple pole (at $z_0$, the same as $f$) and n
o poles on $\partial G$. By the argument principle:

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\partial G}\frac{f'(z)}{f(z)-a}\,dz \;=\; Z_a - P$$

where $Z_a$ is the number of zeros of $f(z)-a$ in $G$ (counting multiplicity) an

**Step 3: The integral equals the winding number of $f(\partial G)$ around $a$,

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\partial G}\frac{f'(z)}{f(z)-a}\,dz \;=\; \frac{1}{2\pi
 i}\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{dw}{w-a} \;=\; \operatorname{Ind}_\Gamma(a).$$

Since $|f(z)|=1$ for $z\in\partial G$, the curve $\Gamma$ lies on the unit circl

bset\{|w|<1+\varepsilon\}$. By Cauchy's theorem:

$$\oint_\Gamma \frac{dw}{w-a}=0.$$

Hence $\operatorname{Ind}_\Gamma(a)=0$.

**Step 4: Conclude.** From Steps 2 and 3:

$$Z_a - 1 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad Z_a = 1.$$

So $f(z)=a$ has exactly one solution in $G$ counting multiplicity. Since the tot
