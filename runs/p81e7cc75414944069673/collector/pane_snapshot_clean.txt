Looking at this problem, I need to find all integer solutions to $(a^a)^n = b^b$
 with $a, b > 1$ integers and $n > 0$ integer.

## Setting Up the Structure

The equation is $a^{an} = b^b$. Comparing prime factorizations: if $a = \prod p_
i^{\alpha_i}$ and $b = \prod p_i^{\beta_i}$, then for each prime $p_i$:

$$\alpha_i \cdot an = \beta_i \cdot b$$

This forces $a$ and $b$ to share the same prime factors, with exponent vectors i
n constant ratio $r = \alpha_i/\beta_i = b/(an)$. Writing $r = u/v$ in lowest te
rms, we get $\alpha_i = u\gamma_i$, $\beta_i = v\gamma_i$, so:

$$a = d^u, \quad b = d^v \quad \text{for some integer } d > 1$$

Substituting into $b = r \cdot an$:

$$d^v = \frac{u}{v} \cdot d^u \cdot n \implies n = \frac{v}{u} \cdot d^{v-u}$$

## Determining Which $n$ Are Possible

**Case $v = u$ (i.e., $q = p$):** $n = 1$. Always achievable (e.g., $a = b = 2$)
. So $1 \in S$.

**Case $v > u$ (set $k = v - u \geq 1$):** $n = \frac{v}{u} \cdot d^k = \frac{u+
k}{u} \cdot d^k$. For $n$ to be a positive integer, need $u \mid k \cdot d^k$.

**Case $v < u$:** $n = \frac{v}{u \cdot d^{u-v}} < 1$ since $u > v \geq 1$ and $
d \geq 2$, so $u \cdot d^{u-v} \geq 2 \cdot 2 = 4 > v$. Impossible for positive
integer $n$.

**Showing all $n \geq 3$ are achievable ($k = 1$):** Take $u = n-1$, $d = n-1$,
$m = d/u = 1$. Then $n = (u+1) \cdot 1 = n$, valid when $d = n-1 > 1$, i.e., $n
\geq 3$. Concretely: $a = (n-1)^{n-1}$, $b = (n-1)^n$.

**Showing $n = 2$ is impossible:** For $v > u$, $n = \frac{v}{u} \cdot d^{v-u} \
geq \frac{u+1}{u} \cdot 2 > 2$ for all finite $u$. For $v = u$, $n = 1$. So $2 \
notin S$.

Therefore: $S = \{1\} \cup \{n \in \mathbb{Z} : n \geq 3\}$.

## Part (a): Computing $s_1$

$$S \cap [1, 100] = \{1, 3, 4, 5, \ldots, 100\}$$

$$s_1 = 1 + \sum_{i=3}^{100} i = 1 + \left(\frac{100 \cdot 101}{2} - 1 - 2\right
) = 1 + (5050 - 3) = 1 + 5047 = \boxed{5048}$$

## Part (b): Finding the Unique Pair for $n = 5$

We need $\frac{v}{u} \cdot d^{v-u} = 5$ with $v > u \geq 1$, $d \geq 2$. Setting
 $k = v - u \geq 1$:

$$(u+k) \cdot d^k = 5u \implies u = \frac{k \cdot d^k}{5 - d^k}$$

For $u > 0$, need $d^k < 5$. Since $d^k \geq 2$, we have $d^k \in \{2, 3, 4\}$:

| $d^k$ | $(d, k)$ | $u = \frac{k \cdot d^k}{5 - d^k}$ | Valid? |
|-------|-----------|-------------------------------------|--------|
| 2 | $(2, 1)$ | $2/3$ | No |
| 3 | $(3, 1)$ | $3/2$ | No |
| 4 | $(2, 2)$ | $8$ | Yes: $v = 10$, $(a,b) = (2^8, 2^{10}) = (256, 1024)$ |
| 4 | $(4, 1)$ | $4$ | Yes: $v = 5$, $(a,b) = (4^4, 4^5) = (256, 1024)$ |

Both valid cases yield the **same pair** $(a, b) = (256, 1024)$.

**Verification:** $(256^{256})^5 = 256^{1280} = (2^8)^{1280} = 2^{10240}$, and $
1024^{1024} = (2^{10})^{1024} = 2^{10240}$. ✓

$$s_2 = 256 + 1024 = \boxed{1280}$$

## Final Answer

$$s_1 + s_2 = 5048 + 1280 = \boxed{6328}$$
