Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether an infinite, binary, direct
ed, connected, non-rooted graph (where every vertex has in-degree 2 and out-degr
ee 1) is countable.

**Answer: Yes, the graph is countable.**

## Proof

**Key Principle:** Every connected, locally finite graph is countable.

**Setup.** Let $G = (V, E)$ be the graph in question. Each vertex has in-degree
2 and out-degree 1, so in the underlying undirected graph, every vertex has degr
ee at most $2 + 1 = 3$ (finite). The graph is connected (in the undirected sense
; strong connectivity would only be a stronger condition).

**BFS argument.** Pick any vertex $v_0 \in V$. Define the ball of radius $n$:
$$B_n = \{v \in V : d(v_0, v) \leq n\}$$
where $d$ is the undirected graph distance.

**Claim:** Each $B_n$ is finite.

*Proof by induction.* $B_0 = \{v_0\}$ is finite. For the inductive step, $B_{n+1
}$ consists of $B_n$ together with all neighbors of vertices in $B_n \setminus B
_{n-1}$. Since every vertex has at most 3 neighbors (finite degree), and $B_n \s
etminus B_{n-1}$ is finite by the inductive hypothesis, the set of new neighbors
 added is finite. Thus $B_{n+1}$ is finite. $\square$

More precisely, $|B_n| \leq 1 + 3 + 3\cdot 2 + 3 \cdot 2^2 + \cdots + 3 \cdot 2^
{n-1} \leq 3 \cdot 2^n - 2$, so the balls grow at most exponentially but remain
finite.

**Countability.** Since $G$ is connected, every vertex is at some finite distanc
e from $v_0$, so:
$$V = \bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty} B_n$$

This expresses $V$ as a countable union of finite sets, which is countable. $\sq
uare$

**Remark on existence.** Such a graph does exist. One construction: take a bi-in
finite spine $\{\ldots, v_{-2}, v_{-1}, v_0, v_1, v_2, \ldots\}$ with $f(v_i) =
v_{i+1}$. At each $v_i$, attach a full infinite binary tree (vertex set = all fi
nite binary strings, countable) whose root $r_i$ satisfies $f(r_i) = v_i$. Then:
- Each $v_i$ has in-neighbors $v_{i-1}$ and $r_i$ (in-degree 2), out-neighbor $v
_{i+1}$ (out-degree 1). ✓
- Each tree node has in-degree 2 (two children) and out-degree 1 (parent). ✓
- No root exists (every vertex has out-degree 1). ✓
- The graph is connected. ✓

The total vertex set is $\mathbb{Z} \times \{0,1\}^*$, a product of two countabl
e sets, hence countable — consistent with the theorem above.

**Conclusion.** Any connected graph in which every vertex has finite degree is n
ecessarily countable, as it is a countable union of finite balls. Since our grap
h has maximum degree 3 and is connected, it is countable.
