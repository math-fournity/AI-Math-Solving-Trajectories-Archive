## Setting up the gcd maximization

Let $d = \gcd(a,b,c)$, and write $a = dA$, $b = dB$, $c = dC$ with $\gcd(A,B,C)=
1$. The constraints become:
- $A < B \le C$, $\;C < 2A$, $\;A,B,C \ge 1$
- $d(A+B+C) = 1998$

To **maximize** $d$, I minimize $n = A+B+C$, subject to $n \mid 1998$ and the ex
istence of a valid triple with $\gcd(A,B,C)=1$.

## Finding achievable sums $n$

**Smallest possible $A$ is 2** (if $A=1$: $C < 2$ forces $C=1$, but $B > A = 1$
and $B \le C = 1$ is impossible).

| $A$ | Valid $(A,B,C)$ | Sums |
|-----|-----------------|------|
| 2 | $(2,3,3)$ | 8 |
| 3 | $(3,4,4),(3,4,5),(3,5,5)$ | 11, 12, 13 |
| 4 | $(4,5,5),\ldots,(4,7,7)$ | 14–18 |
| $\ge 3$ | — | all $\ge 11$ |

So achievable sums are $\{8\} \cup \{11, 12, 13, 14, \ldots\}$. **Sums 9 and 10
are impossible.**

## Selecting the largest $d$

$1998 = 2 \times 3^3 \times 37$. Divisors $\ge 8$: $9, 18, 27, 37, 54, \ldots$

- $n = 9$: **not achievable** (no triple sums to 9). So $d = 222$ is impossible.
- $n = 18$: achievable. This gives $d = 1998/18 = 111$. ✓

Since 18 is the smallest achievable divisor of 1998, **$d = 111$ is the maximum*
*.

Both triples have $\gcd = 1$:
