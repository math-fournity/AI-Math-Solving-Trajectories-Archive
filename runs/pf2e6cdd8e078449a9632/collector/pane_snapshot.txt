## Note on the problem statement

As literally written, Evelyn's statement (2) is *"n is not divisible by 27."* Wi
th that wording the problem is **underdetermined** — for example $n=202=2\cdot 1
01$ already satisfies all three people (Dirk: $202<100$ false, two primes true;
Evelyn: $9\nmid 202$ false, $27\nmid 202$ true; Franziska: $202\ne 91809$ false,
 $101\mid 202$ true), and so do $303, 404, 505, 707, 808,\dots$ So no unique $n$
 exists under the literal text.

The problem is the classic well-posed version in which Evelyn's (2) is **"n is d
ivisible by 27"** (the number $91809=3^2\cdot 101^2$ is then the designed trap).
 I solve that intended version, which yields a unique answer.

## Proof (intended version: Evelyn (2) = "$27\mid n$")

**Franziska.** $91809 = 9\cdot 101^{2}=3^{2}\cdot 101^{2}$, so $101\mid 91809$.
If $n=91809$ then both F(1) and F(2) are true, impossible. Hence F(1) is false a
nd F(2) is true:

$$101\mid n,\qquad\text{so } n\ge 101.$$

**Dirk.** Since $101\mid n$, we have $n\ge 101>99$, so $n$ has at least three de
cimal digits; D(1) ("fewer than three digits") is **false**. Therefore D(2) is *
*true**: exactly two distinct primes occur in the factorization of $n$. Because
$101\mid n$,

$$n = 101^{a}\cdot q^{b},\qquad a,b\ge 1,\ q\text{ prime},\ q\ne 101.$$

**Evelyn** (with E(2) = "$27\mid n$"). Since $27\mid n\Rightarrow 9\mid n$, the
two statements cannot both be false. "Both true" happens exactly when $27\mid n$
. For exactly one true statement we therefore need

$$9\mid n\quad\text{and}\quad 27\nmid n,\qquad\text{i.e. } v_{3}(n)=2.$$

Thus $3\mid n$, forcing $q=3$, and $3^{2}\Vert n$:

$$n = 101^{a}\cdot 3^{2},\qquad a\ge 1.$$

**Pin down $a$.** With $n\le 1\,000\,000$:

- $a=1:\ n=101\cdot 9 = 909$.
- $a=2:\ n=101^{2}\cdot 9 = 91809$, excluded (this is Franziska's F(1) value, al
ready ruled out).
- $a\ge 3:\ n=101^{3}\cdot 9 = 9\,272\,709>1\,000\,000$, excluded.

Hence the only remaining possibility is

$$\boxed{n=909}.$$

**Verification of $n=909=3^{2}\cdot 101$:**

- *Dirk:* $909<100$ is **false**; the prime factorization $3^{2}\cdot 101$ has e
xactly two distinct primes, **true**. ✓
- *Evelyn:* $9\mid 909$ is **true** ($909=9\cdot 101$); $27\mid 909$ is **false*
* ($909/27=33\tfrac{2}{3}$). ✓
- *Franziska:* $909=91809$ is **false**; $101\mid 909$ is **true**. ✓

Each of Dirk, Evelyn, and Franziska makes exactly one true and one false stateme
nt.

$$n = 909.$$
