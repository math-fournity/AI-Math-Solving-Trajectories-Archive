## Proof

**Setup and norm.** The space $st_0$ consists of all bounded statistically conve
rgent complex sequences. We equip it with the supremum norm $\|x\|_\infty = \sup
_{n} |x_n|$, which is the natural norm making $st_0$ a normed linear space (it i
s a subspace of $\ell_\infty$). The functional is $g(x) = \operatorname{st\text{
-}lim} x_n$, which is linear: statistical limits respect addition and scalar mul
tiplication (this follows directly from the density characterization and the fac
t that the union of two density-zero sets has density zero).

**Key claim:** $|g(x)| = |\operatorname{st\text{-}lim} x_n| \le \|x\|_\infty$ fo
r every $x \in st_0$.

*Proof of claim.* Let $L = \operatorname{st\text{-}lim} x_n$. By definition of s
tatistical convergence, for every $\varepsilon > 0$,
$$\delta\big(\{n \in \mathbb{N} : |x_n - L| \ge \varepsilon\}\big) = 0,$$
where $\delta(A) = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{1}{n}|A \cap \{1,\dots,n\}|$ denotes
the natural density. Since this set has density $0$, its complement
$$A_\varepsilon^c = \{n \in \mathbb{N} : |x_n - L| < \varepsilon\}$$
has density $1$, and in particular is infinite. Pick any $n \in A_\varepsilon^c$
. Then by the triangle inequality,
$$|L| \le |x_n| + |x_n - L| < |x_n| + \varepsilon \le \|x\|_\infty + \varepsilon
.$$
Since $\varepsilon > 0$ was arbitrary, we conclude $|L| \le \|x\|_\infty$. $\squ
are$

**Boundedness.** The claim shows $|g(x)| \le \|x\|_\infty$ for all $x \in st_0$,
 so $g$ is a bounded linear functional with operator norm $\|g\| \le 1$.

**Norm is exactly 1.** Consider the constant sequence $x = (1, 1, 1, \dots)$. Th
is is statistically convergent with $\operatorname{st\text{-}lim} x_n = 1$, and
$\|x\|_\infty = 1$. Hence $|g(x)| = 1 = \|x\|_\infty$, giving $\|g\| \ge 1$. Com
bined with the upper bound, $\|g\| = 1$.

**Continuity.** A linear functional on a normed space is bounded if and only if
it is continuous. Since $g$ is bounded, it is continuous. Concretely, if $x^{(k)
} \to x$ in $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ in $st_0$, then
$$|g(x^{(k)}) - g(x)| = |g(x^{(k)} - x)| \le \|g\|\,\|x^{(k)} - x\|_\infty = \|x
^{(k)} - x\|_\infty \to 0.$$

**Conclusion.** The linear functional $g(x) = \operatorname{st\text{-}lim} x_n$
is bounded (with $\|g\| = 1$) and continuous on $(st_0, \|\cdot\|_\infty)$.
