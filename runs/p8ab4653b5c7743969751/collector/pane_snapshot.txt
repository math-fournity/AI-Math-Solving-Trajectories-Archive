## Schematic Intersection of $Y_1 = V(T^n + U^n - 1)$ and $Y_2 = V(T+1)$ in $\ma
thbb{A}^2_k$

**Setup.** The schematic intersection is the fiber product $Z = Y_1 \times_X Y_2
$, corresponding to the sum of defining ideals:

$$Z = V(T^n + U^n - 1,\; T + 1) = \operatorname{Spec} k[T,U]\big/(T^n + U^n - 1,
\; T+1).$$

**Eliminate $T$.** The relation $T + 1 = 0$ gives $T = -1$. Substituting:

$$k[T,U]/(T^n + U^n - 1,\; T+1) \;\cong\; k[U]\big/\big((-1)^n + U^n - 1\big).$$

Set $h(U) = U^n + (-1)^n - 1$. The structure of $Z$ now bifurcates by the parity
 of $n$.

---

### Case 1: $n$ even ($n \geq 2$)

Then $(-1)^n = 1$, so $h(U) = U^n$. Thus

$$Z \cong \operatorname{Spec} k[U]/(U^n).$$

The ring $k[U]/(U^n)$ is local artinian with unique prime ideal $\bar{(U)}$, so:

- **Number of points:** exactly **1** (the point $U = 0$, i.e. $(T,U) = (-1, 0)$
).
- **Irreducible:** **yes** — a single point is trivially irreducible.
- **Reduced:** **no** — the class $\bar{U}$ satisfies $\bar{U}^{\,n} = 0$ but $\
bar{U} \neq 0$ (since $n \geq 2$), so $Z$ carries nilpotent thickenings. It is a
 **non-reduced fat point** of multiplicity $n$.

This holds for **every** field $k$, regardless of characteristic.

---

### Case 2: $n$ odd

Then $(-1)^n = -1$, so $h(U) = U^n - 2$. Thus

$$Z \cong \operatorname{Spec} k[U]/(U^n - 2).$$

**Subcase 2a: $\operatorname{char}(k) = 2$.** Then $2 = 0$, so $U^n - 2 = U^n$ a
nd $Z \cong \operatorname{Spec} k[U]/(U^n)$ — identical to Case 1. One point, ir
reducible, not reduced (for $n \geq 3$; for $n = 1$ it is $\operatorname{Spec} k
[U]/(U) \cong \operatorname{Spec} k$, which is reduced).

**Subcase 2b: $\operatorname{char}(k) \neq 2$.** The polynomial $U^n - 2$ is **s
eparable** (squarefree): its derivative $nU^{n-1}$ vanishes only at $U = 0$, and
 $0^n - 2 = -2 \neq 0$, so $\gcd(U^n - 2,\; nU^{n-1}) = 1$. Therefore:

- **Reduced:** **yes**.
- **Number of points:** equals the number of irreducible factors of $U^n - 2$ ov
er $k$.
- **Irreducible:** iff $U^n - 2$ is irreducible over $k$.

These depend on $k$. Two illustrative extremes:

- **$k$ algebraically closed, $\operatorname{char}(k) \nmid n$:** $U^n - 2$ spli
ts into $n$ distinct linear factors $\Rightarrow$ $Z$ has **$n$ points**, is red
uced, and is reducible for $n \geq 3$ (irreducible for $n = 1$).
- **$k = \mathbb{Q}$:** $U^n - 2$ is irreducible by **Eisenstein's criterion** a
t $p = 2$ (the prime $2$ divides every non-leading coefficient, and $4 \nmid 2$)
 $\Rightarrow$ $Z$ has **1 point**, is reduced, and is irreducible.

---

**Key takeaway.** For **$n$ even**, the schematic intersection is *always* a non
-reduced fat point supported at $(-1, 0)$, independent of the field — illustrati
ng how schematic intersection records the multiplicity $n$ that the set-theoreti
c intersection (a single point) discards. For **$n$ odd** with $\operatorname{ch
ar}(k) \neq 2$, the intersection is reduced and its geometry is governed by the
factorization of $U^n - 2$ over $k$.
