## Solution

We determine the radius of convergence using the Cauchy–Hadamard formula

$$R = \frac{1}{\limsup_{k\to\infty}\sqrt[k]{|a_k|}}, \qquad a_k = \frac{k^{2k+5}
\,\ln^{10} k\,\ln\ln k}{(k!)^2}.$$

**Step 1. Compute the $k$-th root of $|a_k|$.**

$$\sqrt[k]{|a_k|}
= \frac{\bigl(k^{2k+5}\bigr)^{1/k}\bigl(\ln^{10} k\bigr)^{1/k}(\ln\ln k)^{1/k}}{
\bigl((k!)^2\bigr)^{1/k}}
= \frac{k^{2+5/k}\cdot \ln^{10/k} k\cdot (\ln\ln k)^{1/k}}{(k!)^{2/k}}.$$

**Step 2. Take the limit of the sub-exponential factors.**

As $k\to\infty$, for any fixed power $\alpha$ and any $\beta>0$:

- $k^{5/k} = \exp\!\bigl(\tfrac{5\ln k}{k}\bigr)\to \exp(0)=1$,
- $\ln^{10/k} k = \exp\!\bigl(\tfrac{10\ln\ln k}{k}\bigr)\to 1$,
- $(\ln\ln k)^{1/k} = \exp\!\bigl(\tfrac{\ln\ln\ln k}{k}\bigr)\to 1$.

All logarithmic/polylogarithmic factors are therefore asymptotically negligible
compared with the dominant terms $k^2$ and $(k!)^{2/k}$.

**Step 3. Apply Stirling's formula to the factorial.**

Stirling's approximation gives $k! \sim \sqrt{2\pi k}\,(k/e)^k$, hence

$$(k!)^{1/k} \sim (2\pi k)^{1/(2k)}\cdot \frac{k}{e}.$$

Since $(2\pi k)^{1/(2k)} = \exp\!\bigl(\tfrac{\ln(2\pi k)}{2k}\bigr)\to 1$, we o
btain

$$(k!)^{1/k} \sim \frac{k}{e}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad (k!)^{2/k}\sim \frac{k^2}{e
^2}.$$

**Step 4. Combine.**

$$\lim_{k\to\infty}\sqrt[k]{|a_k|}
= \lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{k^2}{(k!)^{2/k}}
= \lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{k^2}{k^2/e^2}
= e^2.$$

Since the ordinary limit exists, the $\limsup$ equals the same value:

$$\limsup_{k\to\infty}\sqrt[k]{|a_k|} = e^2.$$

**Step 5. Conclude.**

$$R = \frac{1}{e^2} = e^{-2}.$$

The sub-exponential factors $k^{5/k}$, $\ln^{10/k} k$, and $(\ln\ln k)^{1/k}$ al
l tend to $1$, so they do not affect the radius; only the ratio of $k^2$ to $(k!
)^{2/k}\sim k^2/e^2$ matters, yielding the limit $e^2$.

$$\boxed{R = e^{-2}}$$
