## Solution

**Problem.** Evaluate $\displaystyle \int_{C} \frac{|z|\, e^{z}}{z^{2}} \, dz$,
where $C$ is the circle of radius $2$ (standard counterclockwise orientation).

### Answer-leak check

The problem statement contains no solutions, solution sketches, or formalization
 notes. Proceeding.

### Key observation: $|z|$ is constant on $C$

The contour $C$ is the circle $|z| = 2$, centered at the origin. At **every** po
int of $C$ we have $|z| = 2$, so the factor $|z|$ can be pulled out of the integ
ral as a constant:

$$
\int_{C} \frac{|z|\, e^{z}}{z^{2}} \, dz \;=\; 2 \int_{C} \frac{e^{z}}{z^{2}} \,
 dz.
$$

### Applying Cauchy's integral formula

The integrand $\dfrac{e^{z}}{z^{2}}$ has a single singularity: a pole of order $
2$ at $z = 0$, which lies inside the disk $|z| < 2$ bounded by $C$.

Cauchy's integral formula for derivatives states, for $f$ analytic inside and on
 $C$ and $a$ inside $C$:

$$
f^{(n)}(a) = \frac{n!}{2\pi i} \oint_{C} \frac{f(z)}{(z-a)^{n+1}}\, dz,
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
\oint_{C} \frac{f(z)}{(z-a)^{n+1}}\, dz = \frac{2\pi i}{n!}\, f^{(n)}(a).
$$

Take $f(z) = e^{z}$ (entire), $a = 0$, $n = 1$:

$$
\int_{C} \frac{e^{z}}{z^{2}} \, dz = \frac{2\pi i}{1!}\, f'(0) = 2\pi i \cdot e^
{0} = 2\pi i.
$$

### Final computation

$$
\int_{C} \frac{|z|\, e^{z}}{z^{2}} \, dz = 2 \cdot 2\pi i = \boxed{4\pi i}.
$$

### Verification via residues (cross-check)

The residue of $\dfrac{e^{z}}{z^{2}}$ at the double pole $z=0$ is

$$
\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{e^{z}}{z^{2}} = \lim_{z\to 0}\frac{d}{dz}\!\left[z
^{2}\cdot\frac{e^{z}}{z^{2}}\right] = \lim_{z\to 0}\frac{d}{dz}e^{z} = e^{0} = 1
.
$$

By the residue theorem, $\displaystyle \int_{C}\frac{e^{z}}{z^{2}}\,dz = 2\pi i\
cdot 1 = 2\pi i$, and multiplying by the constant $|z|=2$ gives $4\pi i$. Both m
ethods agree.
