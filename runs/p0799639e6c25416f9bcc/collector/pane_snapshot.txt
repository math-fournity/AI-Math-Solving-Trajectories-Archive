Proof

Answer: No. A non-measurable set in $\mathbb{R}^n$ need not contain any
Lebesgue-measurable subset of positive measure. We construct an explicit
counterexample (a Vitali-type set in $\mathbb{R}^n$).

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Construction

Define an equivalence relation on $\mathbb{R}^n$ by $$x \sim y \iff x - y \in
\mathbb{Q}^n.$$ This partitions $\mathbb{R}^n$ into cosets of $\mathbb{Q}^n$. By
the Axiom of Choice, select one representative from each equivalence class that
meets $[0,1]^n$, and let $V \subseteq [0,1]^n$ be the set of selected
representatives. Enumerate $\mathbb{Q}^n \cap [-1,1]^n = {q_1, q_2, q_3,
\dots}$.

Claim 1: $V$ is not Lebesgue-measurable.

The translates ${V + q_k}_{k \geq 1}$ are pairwise disjoint: if $(V+q_i) \cap
(V+q_j) \neq \emptyset$ with $v_i, v_j \in V$ and $v_i + q_i = v_j + q_j$, then
$v_i - v_j = q_j - q_i \in \mathbb{Q}^n$, so $v_i \sim v_j$; by construction
$v_i = v_j$, forcing $q_i = q_j$, contradiction.

Moreover, $$[0,1]^n \subseteq \bigcup_{k \geq 1} (V + q_k) \subseteq [-1,2]^n.$$
The first inclusion holds because every $x \in [0,1]^n$ belongs to some coset,
whose representative $v \in V$ satisfies $x - v \in \mathbb{Q}^n$ and $x - v \in
[-1,1]^n$, so $x \in V + q_k$ for some $k$.

Suppose $V$ were measurable with measure $m$. Then by countable additivity and
translation-invariance, $$m!\left(\bigcup_{k} (V+q_k)\right) = \sum_{k} m(V).$$
If $m(V) = 0$, the union has measure $0$, contradicting $[0,1]^n \subseteq
\bigcup_k (V+q_k)$ which has measure $1$. If $m(V) > 0$, the sum is $+\infty$,
contradicting $\bigcup_k (V+q_k) \subseteq [-1,2]^n$ which has finite measure
$3^n$. Hence $V$ is non-measurable.

Claim 2: $V$ contains no measurable subset of positive measure.

Suppose, for contradiction, that $M \subseteq V$ is Lebesgue-measurable with
$m(M) > 0$. For distinct $q_i, q_j$, the translates $M + q_i$ and $M + q_j$ are
disjoint (since $M + q_i \subseteq V + q_i$ and the $V + q_k$ are pairwise
disjoint). Each $M + q_k$ is measurable with $m(M + q_k) = m(M) > 0$, and $$
\bigcup_{k \geq 1} (M + q_k) \subseteq [-1,2]^n,$$ since $M \subseteq V
\subseteq [0,1]^n$ and $q_k \in [-1,1]^n$. Therefore $$m!\left(\bigcup_{k \geq
1} (M+q_k)\right) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} m(M) = +\infty,$$ yet this union is
contained in $[-1,2]^n$, which has finite measure $3^n$. Contradiction.

Thus no measurable subset of $V$ has positive measure; equivalently, the inner
measure of $V$ is $0$.

Conclusion

$V \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is a non-measurable set (Lebesgue) that contains no
measurable subset of positive measure. Therefore a non-measurable set in $
\mathbb{R}^n$ does not necessarily contain a positive-measure measurable subset.
