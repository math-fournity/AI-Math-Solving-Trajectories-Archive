## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, such a sequence can strongly converge to zero in $L^2(\Omega)$.

### Step 1: Upper bound analysis

On a bounded domain $\Omega$ with $|\Omega| < \infty$, for any $p > 2$ and $f \i
n L^p(\Omega)$, the standard embedding gives:

$$\|f\|_{L^2} \leq |\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{p}} \|f\|_{L^p}.$$

Setting $p = 2 + \frac{1}{k}$ and $\|f_k\|_{L^{2+1/k}} = 1$:

$$\|f_k\|_{L^2} \leq |\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2+1/k}} = |\Omega|^{\frac{
1}{2(2k+1)}}.$$

As $k \to \infty$, the exponent $\frac{1}{2(2k+1)} \to 0$, so the upper bound $|
\Omega|^{1/(2(2k+1))} \to 1$. This bound does **not** prevent convergence to zer
o — it is too weak to rule out the possibility.

### Step 2: Explicit construction

Let $\Omega$ be a bounded domain with $|\Omega| > 0$. Choose a sequence of measu

lon_k > 0$ and $\varepsilon_k \to 0$ (to be specified later). Define:

$$f_k = c_k \cdot \mathbf{1}_{E_k}, \quad \text{where } c_k = \varepsilon_k^{-\f
rac{k}{2k+1}}.$$

**Verification of the $L^{2+1/k}$ norm:**

$$\|f_k\|_{L^{2+1/k}}^{2+1/k} = c_k^{2+1/k} \cdot |E_k| = \varepsilon_k^{-\frac{
k(2+1/k)}{2k+1}} \cdot \varepsilon_k = \varepsilon_k^{-\frac{2k+1}{2k+1}} \cdot
\varepsilon_k = \varepsilon_k^{-1} \cdot \varepsilon_k = 1. \checkmark$$

**Computation of the $L^2$ norm:**

$$\|f_k\|_{L^2}^2 = c_k^2 \cdot |E_k| = \varepsilon_k^{-\frac{2k}{2k+1}} \cdot \
varepsilon_k = \varepsilon_k^{1 - \frac{2k}{2k+1}} = \varepsilon_k^{\frac{1}{2k+
1}}.$$

Therefore:

$$\|f_k\|_{L^2} = \varepsilon_k^{\frac{1}{2(2k+1)}}.$$

### Step 3: Choosing $\varepsilon_k$ to force convergence to zero

Set $\varepsilon_k = e^{-k^2}$. Then:

$$\|f_k\|_{L^2} = e^{-\frac{k^2}{2(2k+1)}}.$$

As $k \to \infty$:

$$\frac{k^2}{2(2k+1)} = \frac{k^2}{4k+2} \sim \frac{k}{4} \to \infty.$$

Hence $\|f_k\|_{L^2} = e^{-k^2/(2(2k+1))} \to 0$ as $k \to \infty$.

For sufficiently large $k$, $\varepsilon_k = e^{-k^2} \leq |\Omega|$, so the set
s $E_k$ of measure $\varepsilon_k$ can indeed be chosen inside $\Omega$.

### Step 4: Conclusion

The sequence $f_k = \varepsilon_k^{-k/(2k+1)} \mathbf{1}_{E_k}$ with $|E_k| = e^
{-k^2}$ satisfies:

1. $\|f_k\|_{L^{2+1/k}(\Omega)} = 1$ for all $k \geq 1$,
2. $\|f_k\|_{L^2(\Omega)} = e^{-k^2/(2(2k+1))} \to 0$ as $k \to \infty$.

Therefore $f_k \to 0$ strongly in $L^2(\Omega)$.

**Key insight:** The $L^{2+1/k}$ norm, being a higher-order norm than $L^2$, is
more sensitive to concentration (peaks on small sets). By concentrating the mass

norm remains fixed at 1 while the $L^2$ norm is driven to zero. The gap between
the exponents $2$ and $2+1/k$ — though shrinking to zero — is always positive, a
nd this gap is exactly what the concentration exploits.
