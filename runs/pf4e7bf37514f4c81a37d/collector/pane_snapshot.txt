## Setup and Model

Initially the lower hemisphere ($y < 0$) is painted. Each random rotation (unifo
rm on $SO(3)$, i.e., Haar measure) produces a new independently uniformly random
 hemisphere that gets painted. After $k$ rotations, $m = k+1$ hemispheres have b
een painted (the original plus $k$ random ones).

**Key observation:** A random rotation maps any fixed direction to a uniformly r
andom point on $S^2$, and successive rotations give independent uniform directio
ns. So after $k$ rotations, the painted region is the union of $m = k+1$ hemisph
eres, the first with fixed "down" normal $\hat{y}$ and the rest with iid uniform
 normals $d_1, \dots, d_k$ on $S^2$.

The **unpainted** region is the intersection of the complementary hemispheres:
$$U = \{P \in S^2 : P \cdot \hat{y} \geq 0\} \cap \bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{P \in S^2 :
 P \cdot d_i \leq 0\}$$

This is the intersection of $m$ closed hemispheres with normals $n_0 = \hat{y},
n_1 = -d_1, \dots, n_{k} = -d_k$. Since $-d_i$ is also uniform on $S^2$, by rota
tional symmetry the distribution of this intersection (up to rotation) is the sa
me as if all $m$ normals were iid uniform on $S^2$.

## Geometric Criterion

The intersection of $m$ hemispheres $\{P : P \cdot n_j \geq 0\}$ is **non-empty*
* if and only if all normals $n_1, \dots, n_m$ lie in some closed hemisphere, wh
ich happens if and only if the **origin is not in the interior** of the convex h
ull of $\{n_1, \dots, n_m\}$.

The sphere is fully painted when the intersection becomes **empty**, i.e., when
the origin enters the interior of the convex hull.

## Wendel's Theorem (1962)

**Theorem (Wendel):** If $n$ points are iid uniformly distributed on $S^{d-1} \s
ubset \mathbb{R}^d$, the probability they all lie in some closed hemisphere is:
$$P(n, d) = 2^{1-n}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\binom{n-1}{j}$$

**Proof sketch:** The $n$ points define $n$ hyperplanes through the origin (with
 normals $X_i$), partitioning $\mathbb{R}^d$ into regions. For points in general
 position (which holds a.s.), the number of regions is $2\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\binom{
n-1}{j}$ (a classical result on hyperplane arrangements). Each region correspond
s to a sign pattern $(\text{sgn}(u \cdot X_1), \dots, \text{sgn}(u \cdot X_n))$.
 The $(+,+, \dots,+)$ region is non-empty iff all points lie in one hemisphere.
By the symmetry of the iid uniform distribution under sign flips $(X_1,\dots,X_n
) \to (\varepsilon_1 X_1, \dots, \varepsilon_n X_n)$, each of the $2^n$ sign pat
terns is equally likely to be realizable. Since exactly $2\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\binom
{n-1}{j}$ are realizable:
$$P(\text{all in one hemisphere}) = \frac{2\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\binom{n-1}{j}}{2^n}
= 2^{1-n}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\binom{n-1}{j}. \qquad \square$$

## Application to $d = 3$ (the sphere $S^2$)

For $d = 3$:
$$P(m, 3) = 2^{1-m}\left[\binom{m-1}{0} + \binom{m-1}{1} + \binom{m-1}{2}\right]
 = 2^{1-m}\cdot\frac{m^2 - m + 2}{2} = \frac{m^2 - m + 2}{2^m}$$

Verification:
- $m=1$: $(1-1+2)/2 = 1$ ✓ (one hemisphere always non-empty)
- $m=2$: $(4-2+2)/4 = 1$ ✓ (two hemispheres on $S^2$ always intersect)
- $m=3$: $(9-3+2)/8 = 1$ ✓ (any 3 points on $S^2$ lie in a hemisphere)
- $m=4$: $(16-4+2)/16 = 7/8$ ✓ (probability $7/8$ the intersection is non-empty)

## Computing the Expected Value

Let $M$ = the number of hemispheres (including the initial) needed for full cove
rage. The number of rotations is $M - 1$.

For a non-negative integer random variable:
$$\mathbb{E}[M] = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty} \Pr(M > m) = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty} \Pr(\text
{intersection of } m \text{ hemispheres is non-empty})$$

- $m = 0$: intersection is the whole sphere → $P = 1$
- $m \geq 1$: $P = (m^2 - m + 2)/2^m$

$$\mathbb{E}[M] = 1 + \sum_{m=1}^{\infty} \frac{m^2 - m + 2}{2^m}$$

Using the standard generating function identities at $x = \tfrac{1}{2}$:
$$\sum_{m=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^m} = 2, \quad \sum_{m=0}^{\infty} \frac{m}{2^m}
 = 2, \quad \sum_{m=0}^{\infty} \frac{m^2}{2^m} = 6$$

$$\sum_{m=0}^{\infty} \frac{m^2 - m + 2}{2^m} = 6 - 2 + 2\cdot 2 = 8$$

Subtracting the $m = 0$ term ($= 2$):
$$\sum_{m=1}^{\infty} \frac{m^2 - m + 2}{2^m} = 8 - 2 = 6$$

$$\mathbb{E}[M] = 1 + 6 = 7$$

## Final Answer

The expected number of rotations is:
$$\boxed{6}$$
