## Proof

**Setup.** Let $M = \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0,0)\}$ and $\alpha = \frac{x\,dy
- y\,dx}{x^2+y^2}$. Let $\gamma$ be a smooth curve going once around the origin,
 with image $N$ a 1-dimensional submanifold of $M$, oriented counterclockwise.

**Step 1: $\alpha$ is closed on $M$.**

Computing $d\alpha$: write $\alpha = P\,dx + Q\,dy$ with $P = \frac{-y}{x^2+y^2}
$, $Q = \frac{x}{x^2+y^2}$. Then
$$\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} = \frac{(x^2+y^2) - x\cdot 2x}{(x^2+y^2)^2} = \f
rac{y^2 - x^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2}, \qquad \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = \frac{-(x^2+
y^2) + y\cdot 2y}{(x^2+y^2)^2} = \frac{y^2 - x^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2}.$$
Hence $d\alpha = \left(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partia
l y}\right)dx\wedge dy = 0$ on $M$.

**Step 2: The integral depends only on the homology class.**

Since $\alpha$ is closed, by Stokes' theorem, $\int_{\gamma_1} \alpha = \int_{\g
amma_2} \alpha$ whenever $\gamma_1, \gamma_2$ are homologous 1-cycles in $M$. Th
e space $M$ deformation retracts onto $S^1$, so $H_1(M;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb
{Z}$, generated by the unit circle traversed once counterclockwise.

**Step 3: Compute on the generator.**

Parametrize the unit circle $S^1$ by $\gamma_0(t) = (\cos t, \sin t)$, $t \in [0
,2\pi]$. Then $x^2+y^2=1$, $dx = -\sin t\,dt$, $dy = \cos t\,dt$, and
$$x\,dy - y\,dx = \cos t\cdot\cos t\,dt - \sin t\cdot(-\sin t\,dt) = (\cos^2 t +
 \sin^2 t)\,dt = dt.$$
Therefore
$$\int_{S^1} \alpha = \int_0^{2\pi} dt = 2\pi.$$

**Step 4: Reduce the general $N$ to the generator.**

Since $\gamma$ goes once around the origin and its image $N$ is a submanifold (h
ence $\gamma$ is an embedding of $S^1$ onto $N$), $N$ is a simple closed curve.
The Jordan curve theorem implies the origin lies in the bounded component of $\m
athbb{R}^2 \setminus N$, so $N$ represents the generator of $H_1(M)$ (with sign
determined by orientation). With counterclockwise orientation, $N$ is homologous
 to $S^1$ traversed once counterclockwise.

Since $\gamma$ parametrizes $N$ once with the given counterclockwise orientation
, $\int_N i^*\alpha = \int_\gamma \gamma^*\alpha$. By Steps 1 and 2, this equals
 the integral over the generator computed in Step 3.

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\int_N i^*\alpha = 2\pi.}$$
