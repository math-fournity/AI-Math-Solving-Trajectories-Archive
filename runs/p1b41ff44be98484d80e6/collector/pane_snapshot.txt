## Proof

**Claim:** The dominated convergence theorem (DCT) does **not** apply to random
variables outside $L^1$.

**Statement of DCT.** Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$ be a probability space. If
$(X_n)$ is a sequence of random variables such that
1. $X_n \to X$ almost surely, and
2. there exists $Y \in L^1(P)$ with $|X_n| \leq Y$ a.s. for all $n$,

then $X \in L^1$ and $\mathbb{E}[|X_n - X|] \to 0$; in particular $\mathbb{E}[X_
n] \to \mathbb{E}[X]$.

---

**Step 1: The domination hypothesis forces every $X_n$ into $L^1$.**

Suppose condition (2) holds, so $|X_n| \leq Y$ a.s. with $\mathbb{E}[Y] < \infty
$. Taking expectations,

$$\mathbb{E}[|X_n|] \leq \mathbb{E}[Y] < \infty,$$

so $X_n \in L^1$ for every $n$. Likewise, $|X| \leq Y$ a.s. (by a.s. convergence
 and continuity of the absolute value), giving $\mathbb{E}[|X|] \leq \mathbb{E}[
Y] < \infty$, so $X \in L^1$.

Hence **the hypothesis of DCT can never be satisfied when any $X_n \notin L^1$**
. The theorem is structurally inapplicable outside $L^1$: the existence of an in
tegrable dominator $Y$ is precisely the mechanism that places the entire sequenc
e in $L^1$.

**Step 2: The conclusion is meaningless outside $L^1$.**

The conclusion $\mathbb{E}[X_n] \to \mathbb{E}[X]$ requires $\mathbb{E}[X_n]$ an
d $\mathbb{E}[X]$ to be finite real numbers. If $X_n \notin L^1$, then $\mathbb{
E}[X_n]$ may be undefined (when $\mathbb{E}[X_n^+] = \mathbb{E}[X_n^-] = \infty$
) or infinite, so the statement of convergence has no content.

**Step 3: Counterexample — convergence without domination fails.**

On $\bigl([0,1], \mathcal{B}, \lambda\bigr)$, define $X_n(\omega) = \tfrac{1}{\o
mega}\mathbf{1}_{(1/n,\, 1]}(\omega)$. Then $X_n \to X := \tfrac{1}{\omega}\math
bf{1}_{(0,1]}$ pointwise (hence a.s.), yet

$$\mathbb{E}[X_n] = \int_{1/n}^{1} \frac{d\omega}{\omega} = \ln n \;\longrightar
row\; \infty, \qquad \mathbb{E}[X] = \int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\omega}{\omega} = \infty
.$$

Here $X \notin L^1$, no integrable dominator $Y$ exists (any $Y \geq \sup_n X_n
= X$ would have $\mathbb{E}[Y] = \infty$), and the expectations diverge. The DCT
 conclusion fails — confirming that without the $L^1$-forcing domination, the th
eorem provides no control.

---

**Conclusion.** The dominated convergence theorem cannot be applied to random va
riables outside $L^1$. Its domination condition $|X_n| \leq Y$, $Y \in L^1$, aut
omatically places every $X_n$ (and the limit $X$) in $L^1$, so the hypothesis is
 never satisfiable by non-$L^1$ random variables, and the conclusion is not even
 well-defined outside $L^1$.
