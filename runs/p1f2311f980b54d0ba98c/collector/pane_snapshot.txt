## Solution

**Key identity.** Let $f(x) = \dfrac{x^{2}\cos(x)}{1+\exp(x^{2}\sin(x))}$. I use
 the substitution $x \mapsto -x$ together with the symmetry of the integrand.

Since $\cos(-x)=\cos x$, $(-x)^2=x^2$, and $\sin(-x)=-\sin x$,

$$f(-x)=\frac{x^{2}\cos(x)}{1+\exp(-x^{2}\sin(x))}.$$

Setting $u=x^{2}\sin(x)$, the sum of the two denominators collapses via the stan
dard identity:

$$\frac{1}{1+e^{u}}+\frac{1}{1+e^{-u}}=\frac{1}{1+e^{u}}+\frac{e^{u}}{1+e^{u}}=1
.$$

Therefore

$$f(x)+f(-x)=x^{2}\cos(x)\left[\frac{1}{1+e^{u}}+\frac{1}{1+e^{-u}}\right]=x^{2}
\cos(x).$$

**Reducing the integral.** By symmetry of the interval $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$,

$$I=\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}f(x)\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\bigl[f(x)+f(-
x)\bigr]\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}x^{2}\cos(x)\,dx.$$

Since $x^{2}\cos(x)$ is even,

$$I=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}x^{2}\cos(x)\,dx.$$

**Computing the reduced integral.** Integrate by parts twice. With $u=x^{2}$, $d
v=\cos x\,dx$:

$$\int x^{2}\cos x\,dx = x^{2}\sin x - \int 2x\sin x\,dx.$$

For the remaining term, with $u=2x$, $dv=\sin x\,dx$:

$$\int 2x\sin x\,dx = -2x\cos x + \int 2\cos x\,dx = -2x\cos x + 2\sin x.$$

Hence

$$\int x^{2}\cos x\,dx = x^{2}\sin x + 2x\cos x - 2\sin x.$$

Evaluating on $[0,\pi/2]$:

- At $x=\pi/2$: $\;(\pi/2)^{2}\cdot 1 + 2(\pi/2)\cdot 0 - 2\cdot 1 = \dfrac{\pi^
{2}}{4}-2.$
- At $x=0$: $\;0+0-0=0.$

So

$$I = \frac{\pi^{2}}{4}-2 = \frac{\pi^{2}-8}{4}.$$

**Extracting $a,b,c$.** Comparing $\dfrac{\pi^{2}-8}{4}$ with $\dfrac{\pi^{a}-b}
{c}$ gives

$$a=2,\qquad b=8,\qquad c=4.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{a+b+c = 2+8+4 = 14}.$$
