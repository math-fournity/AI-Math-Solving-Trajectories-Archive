# Compactness of Uniformly Bounded Non-Decreasing Functions in the L² Metric

**Claim.** The set $\Phi = \{f:[a,b]\to[-M,M]\mid f \text{ non-decreasing}\}$ is
 compact in the metric $d(f,g)=\bigl(\int_a^b|f-g|^2\,dx\bigr)^{1/2}$.

Since $\Phi$ with this metric is a metric space, compactness is equivalent to se
quential compactness. We prove sequential compactness directly.

---

## Step 1. Setup and goal

Let $(f_n)_{n\ge 1}$ be an arbitrary sequence in $\Phi$. Each $f_n$ is non-decre
asing on $[a,b]$ and satisfies $|f_n(x)|\le M$ for all $x\in[a,b]$. We must prod
uce a subsequence $(f_{n_k})$ and a function $f\in\Phi$ with $d(f_{n_k},f)\to 0$
.

---

## Step 2. Helly's selection theorem (proved in-line)

Let $\{r_1,r_2,r_3,\dots\}$ be an enumeration of $\mathbb{Q}\cap[a,b]$.

**Diagonal extraction.** The sequence $(f_n(r_1))$ lies in $[-M,M]$, so by Bolza
no–Weierstrass it has a convergent subsequence. From that subsequence, extract a
 further subsequence converging at $r_2$, and so on. The diagonal subsequence $(
f_{n_k})$ converges at **every** rational $r_j$; denote $g(r_j)=\lim_{k\to\infty
}f_{n_k}(r_j)$.

**$g$ is non-decreasing on the rationals.** If $r_i<r_j$ with both rational, the
n $f_{n_k}(r_i)\le f_{n_k}(r_j)$ for every $k$, so passing to the limit gives $g
(r_i)\le g(r_j)$.

**Define the limit function.** For $x\in[a,b]$ set
$$f(x)=\sup\{g(r):r\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[a,b],\, r\le x\},\qquad f(a)=g(a)\text{ if
}a\in\mathbb{Q},\text{ else }f(a)=\inf_{r>a,\,r\in\mathbb{Q}}g(r).$$
Equivalently $f(x)=\inf\{g(r):r\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[a,b],\,r>x\}$, and the two defi
nitions agree except possibly at jump points; taking the right-continuous versio
n, $f$ is a well-defined non-decreasing function $[a,b]\to[-M,M]$, hence $f\in\P
hi$.

**Pointwise convergence at continuity points of $f$.** Let $x_0$ be a continuity
 point of $f$. For rationals $r<x_0<s$ with $r,s$ arbitrarily close to $x_0$, mo
notonicity gives
$$f_{n_k}(r)\le f_{n_k}(x_0)\le f_{n_k}(s).$$
Taking $k\to\infty$:
$$g(r)\le \liminf_k f_{n_k}(x_0)\le \limsup_k f_{n_k}(x_0)\le g(s).$$
By continuity of $f$ at $x_0$, both $g(r)$ (as $r\uparrow x_0$) and $g(s)$ (as $
s\downarrow x_0$) tend to $f(x_0)$. Therefore
$$f_{n_k}(x_0)\to f(x_0).$$

A monotone function has at most countably many discontinuities, so $f_{n_k}(x)\t
o f(x)$ for **all $x$ outside a countable (hence measure-zero) set** $D\subset[a
,b]$.

---

## Step 3. From a.e. pointwise convergence to $L^2$ convergence

We have $f_{n_k}(x)\to f(x)$ for every $x\in[a,b]\setminus D$ with $|D|=0$, and
both $f_{n_k}$ and $f$ take values in $[-M,M]$. Hence
$$|f_{n_k}(x)-f(x)|^2\le (2M)^2=4M^2\quad\text{for all }x\in[a,b],\text{ all }k.
$$
The constant $4M^2$ is integrable on the finite interval $[a,b]$. The functions
$|f_{n_k}-f|^2$ converge to $0$ a.e. and are dominated by $4M^2\in L^1([a,b])$.
By the **Dominated Convergence Theorem**,
$$\int_a^b|f_{n_k}(x)-f(x)|^2\,dx\;\longrightarrow\;0,$$
i.e. $d(f_{n_k},f)\to 0$.

---

## Step 4. Conclusion

We started with an arbitrary sequence in $\Phi$ and extracted a subsequence conv
erging in the metric $d$ to a limit $f\in\Phi$. Therefore $\Phi$ is **sequential
ly compact**, and since the topology is metrizable, sequentially compact $\iff$
compact.

$$\boxed{\Phi \text{ is compact in the } L^2\text{ metric } d.}$$

**Remark on the mechanism.** The two ingredients that make this work are: (i) **
uniform boundedness** $|f|\le M$, which provides the $L^1$-dominating function $
4M^2$ needed for dominated convergence, and (ii) **monotonicity**, which via Hel
ly's theorem converts a bounded sequence into an a.e.-pointwise-convergent subse
quence. Without monotonicity the unit ball of $L^2$ is only *weakly* compact (no
t strongly); without the uniform bound the monotone functions can escape to infi
nity. With both, strong $L^2$ compactness holds.
