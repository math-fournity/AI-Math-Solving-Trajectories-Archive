Looking at this problem, I need to find the tightest lower bound for the largest
 eigenvalue of a symmetric correlation matrix (PSD, unit diagonal, off-diagonal
in [-1,1]).

## Proof

Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ symmetric correlation matrix, so:
- $A$ is positive semidefinite (all eigenvalues $\lambda_i \geq 0$),
- $A_{ii} = 1$ for all $i$ (unit diagonal),
- $A_{ij} \in [-1, 1]$ for $i \neq j$.

**Step 1: Trace argument.** The trace equals the sum of eigenvalues:
$$\operatorname{tr}(A) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} A_{ii} = n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \lambda_i.$$

**Step 2: Average bound.** Since all eigenvalues are non-negative and sum to $n$
, the largest eigenvalue is at least the average:
$$\lambda_{\max} \geq \frac{\operatorname{tr}(A)}{n} = \frac{n}{n} = 1.$$

**Step 3: This is tighter than the trivial PSD bound** $\lambda_{\max} \geq 0$,
and also tighter than the Rayleigh-quotient diagonal bound $\lambda_{\max} \geq
\max_i A_{ii} = 1$ (which gives the same value but the trace/average argument is
 the structural reason).

**Step 4: Tightness.** The bound $\lambda_{\max} \geq 1$ cannot be improved. The
 identity matrix $I_n$ is a valid correlation matrix (symmetric, PSD, unit diago
nal, off-diagonal entries $0 \in [-1,1]$), and its largest eigenvalue is exactly
 $1$.

**Conclusion.** The tightest lower bound for the largest eigenvalue of such a co
rrelation matrix is:

$$\boxed{\lambda_{\max} \geq 1}$$

This bound is achieved by the identity matrix, so it is the best possible (tight
) lower bound.
