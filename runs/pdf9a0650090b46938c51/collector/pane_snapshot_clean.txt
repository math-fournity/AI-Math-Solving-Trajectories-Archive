Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $f(\mathbb{Q}) \subseteq g(
\mathbb{Q})$ forces $f = g \circ h$ for some $h \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$.

**Answer: Yes, such $h$ must always exist.**

**Proof.**

**Trivial cases.** If $g$ is constant, say $g \equiv c$, then $f(\mathbb{Q}) \su
bseteq \{c\}$ forces $f \equiv c$, and $f = g \circ h$ for any $h$. If $\deg g =
 1$, say $g(x)=ax+b$ with $a \neq 0$, then $g$ is a bijection on $\mathbb{Q}$, s
o the hypothesis is automatic, and $h(x) = (f(x)-b)/a \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ gives $
g \circ h = f$.

**Main case: $\deg g = d \geq 2$.** Consider
$$G(x,y) := g(y) - f(x) \in \mathbb{Q}[x,y],$$
viewed as a polynomial in $y$ over $\mathbb{Q}(x)$. Factor $G = p_1 \cdots p_k$
into irreducibles in $\mathbb{Q}[x,y]$ (equivalently in $\mathbb{Q}(x)[y]$ by Ga
uss's lemma, taking each $p_i$ primitive).

*No factor is independent of $y$:* if $p_i \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$, then $p_i$ divide
s every coefficient of $G$ as a polynomial in $y$; in particular $p_i$ divides t
he leading coefficient $a_d \neq 0$ (a constant), so $p_i$ is constant. Thus eac
h $p_i$ satisfies $\deg_y p_i \geq 1$.

**Claim 1: Some $p_i$ has $\deg_y p_i = 1$.**

Suppose for contradiction that every $p_i$ has $\deg_y p_i \geq 2$. For each $i$
, let $\ell_i(x) \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ be the leading coefficient of $p_i$ in $y$.
The set $B = \{a \in \mathbb{Q} : \ell_i(a) = 0 \text{ for some } i\}$ is finite
.

By **Hilbert's Irreducibility Theorem** (strong form: given finitely many irredu
cible $p_1,\ldots,p_k \in \mathbb{Q}[x,y]$, there are infinitely many $a \in \ma
thbb{Q}$ such that each $p_i(a,y)$ is irreducible in $\mathbb{Q}[y]$), there exi
sts $a \in \mathbb{Q} \setminus B$ such that $p_i(a, y)$ is irreducible in $\mat
hbb{Q}[y]$ for every $i$.

For this $a$, each $p_i(a,y)$ is irreducible of degree $\geq 2$, hence has **no
rational root**. Therefore
$$g(y) - f(a) = \prod_{i=1}^k p_i(a,y)$$
has no rational root, i.e., $f(a) \notin g(\mathbb{Q})$. This contradicts $f(\ma
thbb{Q}) \subseteq g(\mathbb{Q})$.

So some $p_i$ has $\deg_y p_i = 1$. Write $p_i(x,y) = \alpha(x)\,y + \beta(x)$ w
ith $\alpha \in \mathbb{Q}[x]\setminus\{0\}$, $\beta \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$. Setting
 $p_i = 0$ gives
$$r(x) := -\frac{\beta(x)}{\alpha(x)} \in \mathbb{Q}(x), \qquad g(r(x)) = f(x).$
$

**Claim 2: $r \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$.**

Write $r = p/q$ with $p, q \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$, $\gcd(p,q)=1$, $q$ monic. Suppose
 $q$ is non-constant, and let $\gamma \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ be a root of $q
$. Since $\gcd(p,q)=1$, we have $p(\gamma) \neq 0$.

From $g(p/q) = f$, multiplying by $q^d$ (where $g(t) = \sum_{j=0}^d a_j t^j$, $a
_d \neq 0$):
$$\sum_{j=0}^{d} a_j\, p(x)^j\, q(x)^{d-j} = q(x)^d\, f(x).$$
Evaluating at $x = \gamma$: every term with $j < d$ contains a positive power of
 $q(\gamma) = 0$ and vanishes, leaving
$$a_d\, p(\gamma)^d = 0.$$
Since $a_d \neq 0$ and $p(\gamma) \neq 0$, this is a contradiction. Therefore $q
$ is constant, so $r =: h \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$, and $g(h(x)) = f(x)$ for all $x$.
$\blacksquare$
