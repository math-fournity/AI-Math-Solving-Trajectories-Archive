## Example: The Cantor Set

Let me construct the **middle-thirds Cantor set** \(K \subset [0,1]\) and prove
it is a non-trivial compact perfect subset of \(\mathbb{R}\) with Lebesgue measu
re zero.

### Construction

Define a decreasing sequence of closed sets \(C_0 \supseteq C_1 \supseteq C_2 \s
upseteq \cdots\) as follows:

- \(C_0 = [0,1]\).
- \(C_1 = [0,\tfrac{1}{3}] \cup [\tfrac{2}{3}, 1]\) — remove the open middle thi
rd \((\tfrac{1}{3},\tfrac{2}{3})\).
- \(C_2 = [0,\tfrac{1}{9}] \cup [\tfrac{2}{9},\tfrac{1}{3}] \cup [\tfrac{2}{3},\
tfrac{7}{9}] \cup [\tfrac{8}{9},1]\) — remove the open middle third of each inte
rval in \(C_1\).
- In general, \(C_{n+1}\) is obtained from \(C_n\) by removing the open middle t
hird of each of the \(2^n\) closed intervals composing \(C_n\).

The **Cantor set** is
\[
K = \bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty} C_n.
\]

### Claim: \(K\) is compact

Each \(C_n\) is a finite union of closed intervals, hence closed. \(K\) is a cou
ntable intersection of closed sets, so \(K\) is closed. Since \(K \subseteq [0,1
]\), it is bounded. By the Heine–Borel theorem, \(K\) is **compact**.

### Claim: \(K\) has Lebesgue measure zero

At stage \(n\), \(C_n\) consists of \(2^n\) disjoint closed intervals, each of l
ength \(3^{-n}\). Therefore
\[
m(C_n) = 2^n \cdot 3^{-n} = \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^n.
\]
Since \(K \subseteq C_n\) for every \(n\),
\[
m(K) \le m(C_n) = \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^n \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.
\]
Hence \(m(K) = 0\).

### Claim: \(K\) is non-trivial (i.e., \(K \neq \emptyset\) and is uncountable)

**Nonempty:** The endpoints of every removed interval belong to \(K\) (e.g., \(0
, 1, \tfrac{1}{3}, \tfrac{2}{3}, \tfrac{1}{9}, \dots\)). More strongly, every po
int of the form
\[
x = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{a_k}{3^k}, \qquad a_k \in \{0, 2\},
\]
belongs to \(K\). This is the ternary expansion characterization: a number is in
 \(K\) iff it admits a ternary expansion using only the digits \(0\) and \(2\).

**Uncountable:** The map
\[
\varphi: \{0,2\}^{\mathbb{N}} \to K, \qquad (a_1, a_2, \dots) \mapsto \sum_{k=1}
^{\infty} \frac{a_k}{3^k}
\]
is a surjection (every point of \(K\) has such an expansion). The domain \(\{0,2
\}^{\mathbb{N}}\) has the cardinality of the continuum (\(2^{\aleph_0}\)), so \(
K\) is **uncountable**. In particular \(K\) is non-trivial.

### Claim: \(K\) is perfect (closed and has no isolated points)

We must show every point of \(K\) is a limit point of \(K\).

Let \(x \in K\) with ternary expansion \(x = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} a_k / 3^k\), \(
a_k \in \{0,2\}\). For each \(n \ge 1\), define a sequence of points \(x^{(n)} \
in K\) by modifying the tail of the expansion:
\[
x^{(n)} = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{a_k}{3^k} + \sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty} \frac{b_k^{(n)}
}{3^k},
\]
where we choose \(b_k^{(n)} \in \{0, 2\}\) so that \(x^{(n)} \neq x\) (e.g., fli
p the \((n+1)\)-st digit: set \(b_{n+1}^{(n)} = 2 - a_{n+1}\) and \(b_k^{(n)} =
a_k\) for \(k > n+1\)).

Then each \(x^{(n)} \in K\) (its ternary expansion uses only \(0\) and \(2\)), a
nd
\[
|x^{(n)} - x| = \frac{|b_{n+1}^{(n)} - a_{n+1}|}{3^{n+1}} = \frac{2}{3^{n+1}} \x
rightarrow{n\to\infty} 0.
\]
So \(x^{(n)} \to x\) with \(x^{(n)} \in K \setminus \{x\}\). Hence \(x\) is a li
mit point of \(K\).

Since \(x\) was arbitrary, **every point of \(K\) is a limit point**, so \(K\) h
as no isolated points. Combined with \(K\) being closed, \(K\) is **perfect**.

### Conclusion

The middle-thirds Cantor set \(K = \bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty} C_n\) is:
- **compact** (closed and bounded),
- **perfect** (closed, no isolated points),
- **non-trivial** (uncountable, in particular nonempty and not a single point),
- **Lebesgue measure zero** (\(m(K) \le (2/3)^n \to 0\)).

This provides the required example. \(\blacksquare\)
