Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the compactness property ho
lds for hypergraph coloring when edges may be infinite.

## Analysis

The statement is a compactness-type claim: if every finite subhypergraph is $n$-
colorable, must the whole hypergraph be $n$-colorable?

**Key distinction from the De Bruijn–Erdős theorem:** For graphs (edges of size
2) or hypergraphs with *finite* edges, the compactness theorem of propositional
logic applies directly — every constraint clause is finite, so finite satisfiabi
lity implies global satisfiability. But the problem places **no restriction on e
dge size**, so edges may be infinite. With infinite edges, the corresponding cla
uses are infinite, and compactness can fail.

## Proof that the statement is FALSE

**Counterexample (works for any $n > 1$):**

Let $V = \mathbb{N}$ and let $E = \{A \subseteq \mathbb{N} : A \text{ is infinit
e}\}$ — the collection of **all** infinite subsets of $\mathbb{N}$.

**Claim 1: Every finite subhypergraph $(V, E_0)$ is $n$-colorable.**

Let $E_0 = \{A_1, \ldots, A_k\} \subseteq E$, so each $A_i$ is infinite. For eac
h $i$, pick two distinct elements $a_i, b_i \in A_i$ such that all $2k$ chosen e
lements are pairwise distinct (possible by greedy selection, since each $A_i$ is
 infinite and removing finitely many elements from an infinite set leaves it non
empty). Define $c: \mathbb{N} \to \{1, \ldots, n\}$ by:
- $c(a_i) = 1$ and $c(b_i) = 2$ for each $i = 1, \ldots, k$,
- $c(v) = 1$ for all remaining $v \in \mathbb{N}$.

Then each $A_i$ contains $a_i$ (color 1) and $b_i$ (color 2), so no edge in $E_0
$ is monochromatic. Thus $(V, E_0)$ is properly $n$-colored. $\checkmark$

**Claim 2: $H = (\mathbb{N}, E)$ is not $n$-colorable.**

Let $c: \mathbb{N} \to \{1, \ldots, n\}$ be any coloring. Set $A_j = c^{-1}(j)$
for $j = 1, \ldots, n$, so $\mathbb{N} = A_1 \cup \cdots \cup A_n$ (disjoint uni
on). Since $\mathbb{N}$ is infinite and a finite union of finite sets is finite,
 **at least one** $A_j$ must be infinite. Then $A_j \in E$ (it is an infinite su
bset of $\mathbb{N}$), and $A_j$ is monochromatic — every vertex in $A_j$ has co
lor $j$. Therefore $c$ is **not** a proper coloring. $\checkmark$

**Conclusion:** The hypergraph $H = (\mathbb{N}, \{\text{all infinite subsets of
 } \mathbb{N}\})$ satisfies the hypothesis (every finite subhypergraph is $n$-co
lorable) but violates the conclusion ($H$ itself is not $n$-colorable), for ever
y $n > 1$.

The statement is **false**. $\blacksquare$
