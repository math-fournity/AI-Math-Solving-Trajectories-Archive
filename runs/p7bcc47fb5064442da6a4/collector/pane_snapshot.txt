## Solution

We prove the maximum value is $\dfrac{3}{2}$, attained at $x=y=z=1$.

**Step 1: AM–GM on each denominator.**

For $x,y,z>0$,
$$x^4+yz\ge 2\sqrt{x^4\cdot yz}=2x^2\sqrt{yz},$$
hence
$$\frac{x^2}{x^4+yz}\le \frac{1}{2\sqrt{yz}}.$$
Summing cyclically,
$$\sum_{\text{cyc}}\frac{x^2}{x^4+yz}\le \frac12\sum_{\text{cyc}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{
yz}}. \tag{1}$$

**Step 2: Reduce $\sum \frac1{\sqrt{yz}}\le 3$ under the constraint.**

Set $x=1/a^2,\ y=1/b^2,\ z=1/c^2$ with $a,b,c>0$. The constraint $x^2+y^2+z^2=3x
yz$ becomes
$$\frac{b^2c^2}{a^2}+\frac{a^2c^2}{b^2}+\frac{a^2b^2}{c^2}=3. \tag{2}$$
Also $\frac1{\sqrt{yz}}=bc$, so $\sum\frac1{\sqrt{yz}}=bc+ca+ab$.

Define
$$u=\frac{bc}{a},\quad v=\frac{ca}{b},\quad w=\frac{ab}{c}.$$
Then by (2), $u^2+v^2+w^2=3$. Moreover $vw=a^2,\ wu=b^2,\ uv=c^2$, so $a=\sqrt{v
w}$, etc., and
$$bc+ca+ab=u\sqrt{vw}+v\sqrt{wu}+w\sqrt{uv}=\sqrt{uvw}\,(\sqrt u+\sqrt v+\sqrt w
). \tag{3}$$

**Step 3: Bound the right-hand side of (3).**

From $u^2+v^2+w^2=3$:

- By AM–GM applied to $u^2,v^2,w^2$: $\dfrac{u^2+v^2+w^2}{3}\ge (u^2v^2w^2)^{1/3
}$, so $1\ge (uvw)^{2/3}$, i.e. $uvw\le 1$, hence $\sqrt{uvw}\le 1$.

- By Cauchy–Schwarz, $u+v+w\le\sqrt{3(u^2+v^2+w^2)}=3$, and again $(\sqrt u+\sqr
t v+\sqrt w)^2\le 3(u+v+w)\le 9$, so $\sqrt u+\sqrt v+\sqrt w\le 3$.

Therefore
$$bc+ca+ab=\sqrt{uvw}\,(\sqrt u+\sqrt v+\sqrt w)\le 1\cdot 3=3. \tag{4}$$

**Step 4: Conclude.**

Combining (1) and (4),
$$\sum_{\text{cyc}}\frac{x^2}{x^4+yz}\le \frac12\cdot 3=\frac32.$$

**Equality.** Equality in Step 1 requires $x^4=yz$ (and cyclically), i.e. $x^2=\
sqrt{yz}$. Equality in Step 3 requires $u^2=v^2=w^2$ and $u=v=w$, giving $u=v=w=
1$, which forces $a=b=c=1$, i.e. $x=y=z=1$. This satisfies the constraint ($3=3\
cdot1$), and yields the value $\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}=\frac32$.

Hence the maximum value is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{3}{2}}.$$
