## Answer

**Yes.** A bounded self-adjoint operator $A$ satisfying $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\la
ngle A x_n,x_n\rangle|<\infty$ for every orthonormal basis $\{x_n\}$ is necessar
ily in the trace class. In fact the converse also holds, so this is a characteri
zation.

---

### Proof

Let $A$ be a bounded self-adjoint operator on a separable Hilbert space $H$ and
assume
$$
\sum_{n}|\langle A x_n,x_n\rangle|<\infty \qquad\text{for every orthonormal basi
s }\{x_n\}.
\tag{$\ast$}
$$

**Jordan decomposition.** Write $A=A^{+}-A^{-}$ with $A^{+},A^{-}\ge 0$ and $A^{
+}A^{-}=0$, obtained from the spectral theorem:
$$
A^{+}=A\,P^{+},\qquad A^{-}=-A\,P^{-},
$$
where $P^{+}=\mathbf 1_{(0,\infty)}(A)$, $P^{-}=\mathbf 1_{(-\infty,0)}(A)$, $P^
{0}=\mathbf 1_{\{0\}}(A)$ are mutually orthogonal projections summing to $I$. No
te $|A|=A^{+}+A^{-}$.

**Choosing a well-adapted basis.** Decompose $H=\operatorname{ran}P^{+}\oplus\op
eratorname{ran}P^{0}\oplus\operatorname{ran}P^{-}$ and pick an orthonormal basis
 $\{e_n\}$ that respects this splitting: it is the union of orthonormal bases of
 each of the three subspaces.

For $e_n\in\operatorname{ran}P^{+}$ we have $A^{-}e_n=0$, hence $\langle A e_n,e
_n\rangle=\langle A^{+}e_n,e_n\rangle\ge 0$, so $|\langle A e_n,e_n\rangle|=\lan
gle A^{+}e_n,e_n\rangle$.

For $e_n\in\operatorname{ran}P^{-}$ we have $A^{+}e_n=0$, hence $\langle A e_n,e
_n\rangle=-\langle A^{-}e_n,e_n\rangle\le 0$, so $|\langle A e_n,e_n\rangle|=\la
ngle A^{-}e_n,e_n\rangle$.

For $e_n\in\operatorname{ran}P^{0}$ we have $\langle A e_n,e_n\rangle=0$.

Summing over the whole basis,
$$
\sum_n|\langle A e_n,e_n\rangle|
=\sum_{e_n\in\operatorname{ran}P^{+}}\langle A^{+}e_n,e_n\rangle
+\sum_{e_n\in\operatorname{ran}P^{-}}\langle A^{-}e_n,e_n\rangle
=\operatorname{tr}(A^{+})+\operatorname{tr}(A^{-}),
$$
where the traces of the positive operators $A^{\pm}$ are well defined (possibly
$+\infty$) and basis-independent.

By hypothesis $(\ast)$ applied to this particular basis, the left-hand side is f
inite, so
$$
\operatorname{tr}(A^{+})+\operatorname{tr}(A^{-})<\infty,
$$
hence both $\operatorname{tr}(A^{+})<\infty$ and $\operatorname{tr}(A^{-})<\inft
y$.

**Positive finite-trace operators are trace class.** A positive bounded operator
 $B$ with $\operatorname{tr}(B)=\sum_n\langle B f_n,f_n\rangle<\infty$ (for any/
every ONB) is trace class: its singular values are its eigenvalues $\lambda_1\ge
\lambda_2\ge\cdots\ge 0$, and $\sum_k\lambda_k=\operatorname{tr}(B)<\infty$, whi
ch is exactly $\|B\|_1<\infty$.

Thus $A^{+}$ and $A^{-}$ are both trace class, and therefore
$$
A=A^{+}-A^{-}
$$
is trace class. $\qquad\blacksquare$

---

**Converse (for completeness).** If $A$ is self-adjoint and trace class, then fo
r *any* ONB $\{e_n\}$ and any $x$,
$$
|\langle A x,x\rangle|=\bigl|\langle A^{+}x,x\rangle-\langle A^{-}x,x\rangle\big
r|\le \langle A^{+}x,x\rangle+\langle A^{-}x,x\rangle=\langle|A|x,x\rangle,
$$
so
$$
\sum_n|\langle A e_n,e_n\rangle|\le\sum_n\langle|A|e_n,e_n\rangle=\operatorname{
tr}(|A|)=\|A\|_1<\infty.
$$
Hence the condition is equivalent to $A$ being trace class.
