## Solution

Let $T = \text{TNYWR}$. The equation is
$$\left(z+\frac{T}{z}\right)^2 = 1,$$
so $z + \dfrac{T}{z} = \pm 1$. Multiplying by $z$ (note $z\neq 0$) gives two qua
dratics:
$$z^2 - z + T = 0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad z^2 + z + T = 0.$$

Let $w = \sqrt{1-4T}$. The four solutions are
$$z_1 = \frac{1+w}{2},\quad z_2 = \frac{1-w}{2},\quad z_3 = \frac{-1+w}{2},\quad
 z_4 = \frac{-1-w}{2}.$$

**Structure of the quadrilateral.** Observe
$$z_1 + z_4 = \frac{1+w}{2}+\frac{-1-w}{2}=0,\qquad z_2+z_3 = \frac{1-w}{2}+\fra
c{-1+w}{2}=0.$$
Thus $z_4 = -z_1$ and $z_3 = -z_2$: the four vertices are $\{z_1,\, z_2,\, -z_1,
\, -z_2\}$, symmetric about the origin. Hence they form a **parallelogram** cent
ered at the origin, with adjacent side vectors
$$\vec{u} = z_2 - z_1 = -w,\qquad \vec{v} = -z_1 - z_2 = -1.$$

**Area.** The area of a parallelogram in the complex plane with side vectors $u,
v$ is $\bigl|\operatorname{Im}(\overline{u}\,v)\bigr|$. Here
$$\overline{u}\,v = \overline{(-w)}\,(-1) = \overline{w},$$
so
$$\text{Area} = \bigl|\operatorname{Im}(\overline{w})\bigr| = \bigl|\operatornam
e{Im}(w)\bigr| = \bigl|\operatorname{Im}\!\bigl(\sqrt{1-4T}\bigr)\bigr|.$$

For real $T > \tfrac14$, we have $1-4T < 0$, so $\sqrt{1-4T} = i\sqrt{4T-1}$, gi
ving
$$\text{Area} = \sqrt{4T-1}.$$

**Verification.** With $T=\tfrac12$: $w=i$, vertices $(\pm\tfrac12,\pm\tfrac12)$
 form a unit square, area $1=\sqrt{4\cdot\tfrac12-1}$. ✓
With $T=1$: $w=i\sqrt3$, vertices form a $1\times\sqrt3$ rectangle, area $\sqrt3
=\sqrt{4\cdot1-1}$. ✓

$$\boxed{\text{Area} = \sqrt{4T-1}}$$
