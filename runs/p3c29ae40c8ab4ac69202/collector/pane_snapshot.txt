**Step 1: Simplify the convolution integral.**

Consider $\int_0^x g(x-t)\,dt$. Substituting $u = x - t$ (so $dt = -du$, with $t
=0 \Rightarrow u=x$ and $t=x \Rightarrow u=0$):

$$\int_0^x g(x-t)\,dt = \int_x^0 g(u)(-du) = \int_0^x g(u)\,du.$$

So the given equation becomes:

$$\int_a^x e^{f(t)}\,dt = \int_0^x g(u)\,du + 2x + 3.$$

**Step 2: Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$.**

By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus:

$$e^{f(x)} = g(x) + 2,$$

hence

$$g(x) = e^{f(x)} - 2.$$

**Step 3: Find the critical points of $g$.**

$$g'(x) = f'(x)\,e^{f(x)}.$$

Since $e^{f(x)} > 0$ for all real $x$, the critical points of $g$ are exactly th
e zeros of $f'(x)$:

$$f'(x) = 100\prod_{k=1}^{100}(x-k)^k = 0 \iff x \in \{1, 2, 3, \ldots, 100\}.$$

**Step 4: Determine which critical points are local extrema.**

A local extremum requires $g'(x)$ to **change sign** at the critical point. Sinc
e $e^{f(x)} > 0$, the sign of $g'(x)$ equals the sign of $f'(x) = 100\prod_{k=1}
^{100}(x-k)^k$.

Near $x = m$, all factors $(x-k)^k$ for $k \neq m$ are nonzero with a fixed sign
. The sign change of $f'(x)$ at $x = m$ is therefore determined solely by the fa
ctor $(x-m)^m$:

- **$m$ odd:** $(x-m)^m$ changes sign as $x$ crosses $m$ (odd power) $\Rightarro
w$ $f'(x)$ changes sign $\Rightarrow$ **local extremum**.
- **$m$ even:** $(x-m)^m$ does not change sign (even power) $\Rightarrow$ $f'(x)
$ does not change sign $\Rightarrow$ **not a local extremum** (inflection point)
.

**Step 5: Sum the values giving local extrema.**

The local extrema occur at $x = 1, 3, 5, \ldots, 99$ (the odd integers from 1 to
 99). This is an arithmetic sequence with 50 terms, first term 1, last term 99:

$$\text{Sum} = \frac{50}{2}(1 + 99) = 25 \times 100 = \boxed{2500}.$$
