## Proof

**Setup.** Let $(X, \prec)$ be a poset. Its barycentric subdivision $X'$ is the
set of all nonempty finite chains (strictly increasing sequences) of $X$, ordere
d by inclusion: for chains $\sigma, \tau \in X'$, we write $\sigma \leq \tau$ if
f $\sigma \subseteq \tau$ as subsets of $X$. The natural map $f: X' \to X$ sends
 each chain to its greatest element:
$$f(a_1 \prec a_2 \prec \cdots \prec a_n) = a_n.$$

---

**Claim 1: $f$ is monotone.**

Let $\sigma \leq \tau$ in $X'$, i.e., $\sigma \subseteq \tau$. Write $\sigma = (
a_1 \prec \cdots \prec a_n)$ and $\tau = (b_1 \prec \cdots \prec b_m)$, so $f(\s
igma) = a_n$ and $f(\tau) = b_m$. Since $\sigma \subseteq \tau$, every element o
f $\sigma$ is an element of $\tau$; in particular $a_n \in \tau$. Since $\tau$ i
s a chain and $b_m$ is its greatest element, we have $a_n \preceq b_m$, i.e.,
$$f(\sigma) \preceq f(\tau).$$
Hence $f$ is monotone. $\square$

---

**Claim 2: $f$ is not strictly monotone (in general).**

Recall $f$ is strictly monotone if $\sigma < \tau$ (i.e., $\sigma \subseteq \tau
$ and $\sigma \neq \tau$) implies $f(\sigma) \prec f(\tau)$ (strict inequality i
n $X$).

**Counterexample.** Let $X = \{a, b\}$ with the order $a \prec b$ (a two-element
 chain). Consider the chains:
- $\sigma = (b)$, the singleton chain consisting of $b$ alone, with $f(\sigma) =
 b$;
- $\tau = (a \prec b)$, the full chain, with $f(\tau) = b$.

Then $\sigma \subsetneq \tau$, so $\sigma < \tau$ in $X'$. However,
$$f(\sigma) = b = f(\tau),$$
so $f(\sigma) \not\prec f(\tau)$. Therefore $f$ is not strictly monotone. $\squa
re$

---

**Remark.** The failure of strict monotonicity is structural, not accidental: an
y chain $\tau$ of length $\geq 2$ shares its greatest element with its singleton
 subchain $(\max \tau)$, and the singleton is a proper subchain. Thus strict mon
otonicity fails for *every* poset that contains a chain of length $\geq 2$ (i.e.
, any poset that is not an antichain). For an antichain (where every chain has l
ength $1$), $X'$ is itself an antichain and the map is trivially strictly monoto
ne (vacuously, since there are no comparable distinct pairs). But in general, th
e answer is:

**The natural map $X' \to X$ is monotone, but not strictly monotone.**
