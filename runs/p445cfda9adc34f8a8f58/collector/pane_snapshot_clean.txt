## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** The converse is false. There exist $b \geq 2$ and $x \in [0,1)$
 such that $\operatorname{orb}(x)$ is dense in $[0,1)$ but $x$ is not normal wit
h respect to $b$.

We construct an explicit example with $b = 2$.

### Setup and key equivalence

For $b=2$, write the binary expansion $x = 0.d_1 d_2 d_3 \cdots$ (choosing the e
xpansion that is not eventually all $1$s). The map $T_2(x) = 2x \bmod 1$ shifts
the binary expansion left by one position, so $T_2^n(x) = 0.d_{n+1} d_{n+2} \cdo
ts$.

**Density criterion.** The orbit $\{T_2^n(x) : n \geq 0\}$ is dense in $[0,1)$ i
f and only if every finite binary string $w$ appears as a consecutive block $(d_
{k}, d_{k+1}, \ldots, d_{k+|w|-1})$ somewhere in the expansion of $x$.

*Proof of criterion.* The dyadic intervals $[j/2^m, (j+1)/2^m)$ form a basis for
 the topology on $[0,1)$. We have $T_2^n(x) \in [j/2^m, (j+1)/2^m)$ if and only
if the $m$-bit binary representation of $j$ appears starting at position $n+1$ i
n the expansion of $x$. Hence the orbit meets every dyadic interval (and thus ev
ery open interval) iff every finite binary string appears as a block. $\square$

### Construction

Let $s_1, s_2, s_3, \ldots$ be a fixed enumeration of all finite binary strings
(including the empty string, though it plays no role), each appearing at least o
nce. Define the binary expansion of $x$ as

$$
x = 0.\; s_1\; 0^{N_1}\; s_2\; 0^{N_2}\; s_3\; 0^{N_3}\; \cdots
$$

where the positive integers $N_i$ are chosen **inductively**. Suppose $N_1, \ldo
ts, N_{i-1}$ have been chosen. Let $L_i' = |s_1 0^{N_1} s_2 0^{N_2} \cdots s_i|$
 be the total length up to the end of $s_i$, and let $C_i$ be the total number o
f $1$s in $s_1 0^{N_1} \cdots s_i$. Choose $N_i$ large enough that:

1. $\dfrac{C_i}{L_i' + N_i} \le \dfrac{1}{i}$, and
2. $N_i \ge i \cdot |s_{i+1}|$.

(Both $s_i$ and $s_{i+1}$ are known at step $i$ since the enumeration is fixed,
so this is well-defined.) Set $L_i = L_i' + N_i$, the total length up to the end
 of the $i$-th padding block.

### The orbit is dense

Every finite binary string $w$ equals some $s_j$ in the enumeration, hence $w$ a
ppears as a consecutive block in the expansion of $x$. By the density criterion,
 $\operatorname{orb}(x)$ is dense in $[0,1)$.

### $x$ is not normal

Let $f(p) = \frac{\#\{1 \le k \le p : d_k = 1\}}{p}$ be the running frequency of
 the digit $1$ among the first $p$ digits. We show $f(p) \to 0$ as $p \to \infty
$.

**At the end of the $i$-th padding block** ($p = L_i$): By condition (1), $f(L_i
) = C_i / L_i \le 1/i$.

**Within $s_{i+1}$** ($p = L_i + q$, $1 \le q \le |s_{i+1}|$): The number of $1$
s is at most $C_i + |s_{i+1}|$, so

$$
f(p) \le \frac{C_i + |s_{i+1}|}{L_i + q} \le \frac{C_i}{L_i} + \frac{|s_{i+1}|}{
L_i} \le \frac{1}{i} + \frac{|s_{i+1}|}{N_i} \le \frac{1}{i} + \frac{1}{i} = \fr
ac{2}{i},
$$

using condition (1) for the first term and condition (2) ($N_i \ge i|s_{i+1}|$)
for the second.

**Within the $(i+1)$-th padding block**: Padding adds only $0$s, so the frequenc
y is non-increasing from its value at the end of $s_{i+1}$, hence $\le 2/i$.

Combining: for every position $p \ge L_i$, we have $f(p) \le 2/i$. Since $L_i \t
o \infty$ (as $N_i \ge 1$ for all $i$), this gives

$$
\lim_{p \to \infty} f(p) = 0.
$$

The frequency of the digit $1$ in the binary expansion of $x$ is $0 \neq \tfrac{
1}{2}$. By the characterization of normality (uniform distribution of $(T_2^n(x)
)$ implies each digit appears with frequency $1/2$), $x$ is **not normal** with
respect to base $2$.

### Well-definedness

The expansion contains arbitrarily long runs of $0$s (the padding blocks $0^{N_i
}$ with $N_i \to \infty$), so it is not eventually all $1$s; thus $x$ has a uniq
ue binary expansion. The expansion is not eventually periodic (it contains every
 finite binary string, including strings not compatible with any periodic sequen
ce), so $x$ is not a dyadic rational and the orbit is genuinely infinite.

### Conclusion

We have exhibited $b = 2$ and $x \in [0,1)$ such that $\operatorname{orb}(x)$ is
 dense in $[0,1)$ but $x$ is not normal with respect to $b = 2$. Therefore the c
onverse is **not** true: density of the orbit does not imply normality.
