**Theorem.** $H^1_0(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is dense in $H^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$. In fact, $H^
1_0(\mathbb{R}^n) = H^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$.

**Proof.**

Recall that $H^1_0(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is *defined* as the closure of $C_c^\infty(\ma
thbb{R}^n)$ in the $H^1$-norm:
$$\|u\|_{H^1}^2 = \|u\|_{L^2}^2 + \sum_{i=1}^n \|\partial_i u\|_{L^2}^2.$$
Thus it suffices to show that $C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is dense in $H^1(\mathb
b{R}^n)$. We establish this in two steps: **mollification** (to get smoothness)
and **smooth cutoff** (to get compact support).

---

**Step 1 (Mollification).** Let $\eta \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ be a standar
d mollifier: $\eta \ge 0$, $\operatorname{supp}\eta \subset B(0,1)$, $\int \eta
= 1$. Set $\eta_\varepsilon(x) = \varepsilon^{-n}\eta(x/\varepsilon)$. For $u \i
n H^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$, define $u_\varepsilon = u * \eta_\varepsilon$.

- $u_\varepsilon \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ (convolution with a smooth function
 is smooth).
- $\partial_i u_\varepsilon = (\partial_i u) * \eta_\varepsilon$, since weak dif
ferentiation commutes with convolution by a smooth compactly supported kernel.
- By Young's inequality, $\|u_\varepsilon\|_{L^2} \le \|\eta_\varepsilon\|_{L^1}
\|u\|_{L^2} = \|u\|_{L^2}$, and similarly $\|\partial_i u_\varepsilon\|_{L^2} \l
e \|\partial_i u\|_{L^2}$.
- Standard approximation property of mollifiers in $L^p$ ($1 \le p < \infty$): $
\|u_\varepsilon - u\|_{L^2} \to 0$ and $\|\partial_i u_\varepsilon - \partial_i
u\|_{L^2} \to 0$ as $\varepsilon \to 0$.

Hence $\|u_\varepsilon - u\|_{H^1} \to 0$, and $u_\varepsilon \in C^\infty(\math
bb{R}^n) \cap H^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$.

---

**Step 2 (Smooth cutoff).** Let $\phi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ with $\phi \
equiv 1$ on $B(0,1)$, $\phi \equiv 0$ outside $B(0,2)$, and $0 \le \phi \le 1$.
Set $\phi_R(x) = \phi(x/R)$, so $\phi_R \equiv 1$ on $B(0,R)$, $\operatorname{su
pp}\phi_R \subset B(0,2R)$, and $|\nabla \phi_R| \le C/R$ for a constant $C$ ind
ependent of $R$.

For $v \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n) \cap H^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$ (e.g., $v = u_\varepsi
lon$ from Step 1), set $v_R = v \cdot \phi_R$. Then $v_R \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{
R}^n)$. We show $\|v_R - v\|_{H^1} \to 0$ as $R \to \infty$.

*L² convergence of the function:* Since $\phi_R \to 1$ pointwise and $|v\phi_R -
 v| = |v|\,|\phi_R - 1| \le |v|$, dominated convergence gives
$$\|v_R - v\|_{L^2}^2 = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} |v|^2 |\phi_R - 1|^2 \,dx \to 0.$$

*L² convergence of the gradient:* We have $\nabla v_R = \phi_R \nabla v + v \nab
la \phi_R$, so
$$\|\nabla v_R - \nabla v\|_{L^2} \le \underbrace{\|(\phi_R - 1)\nabla v\|_{L^2}
}_{(I)} + \underbrace{\|v\,\nabla\phi_R\|_{L^2}}_{(II)}.$$

For $(I)$: $|(\phi_R - 1)\nabla v| \le |\nabla v|$ and $\phi_R \to 1$ pointwise,
 so by dominated convergence, $(I) \to 0$.

For $(II)$: Since $\operatorname{supp}(\nabla\phi_R) \subset B(0,2R)\setminus B(
0,R)$ and $|\nabla\phi_R| \le C/R$,
$$(II)^2 = \int |v|^2 |\nabla\phi_R|^2 \,dx \le \frac{C^2}{R^2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^
n} |v|^2\,dx = \frac{C^2}{R^2}\|v\|_{L^2}^2 \to 0.$$

Hence $\|v_R - v\|_{H^1} \to 0$.

---

**Combining the two steps.** Given $u \in H^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$ and $\delta > 0$:

1. Choose $\varepsilon > 0$ small enough that $\|u_\varepsilon - u\|_{H^1} < \de
lta/2$ (Step 1). Then $u_\varepsilon \in C^\infty \cap H^1$.
2. Choose $R > 0$ large enough that $\|(u_\varepsilon)_R - u_\varepsilon\|_{H^1}
 < \delta/2$ (Step 2). Then $(u_\varepsilon)_R \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$.
3. By the triangle inequality, $\|(u_\varepsilon)_R - u\|_{H^1} < \delta$.

Since $\delta > 0$ was arbitrary, $C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is dense in $H^1(\m
athbb{R}^n)$.

---

**Conclusion.** By definition, $H^1_0(\mathbb{R}^n) = \overline{C_c^\infty(\math
bb{R}^n)}^{\,\|\cdot\|_{H^1}}$. Since $C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is dense in $H^
1(\mathbb{R}^n)$, its $H^1$-closure equals all of $H^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$. Therefore
$$H^1_0(\mathbb{R}^n) = H^1(\mathbb{R}^n),$$
and in particular $H^1_0(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is dense in $H^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$. $\black
square$

a bounded domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^n$, $H^1_0(\Omega)$ is a *proper* cl
