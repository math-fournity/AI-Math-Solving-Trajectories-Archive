Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether there exists a polynomial i
n ℤ[x] that is irreducible over every finite field 𝔽_p. I'll address the non-tri
vial case (degree ≥ 2) and give an elementary proof that no such polynomial exis
ts.

---

**Theorem.** No polynomial f(x) ∈ ℤ[x] of degree n ≥ 2 is irreducible over every
 finite field 𝔽_p.

**Proof.**

**Step 1: f must be irreducible over ℚ.**

Suppose f is reducible over ℚ. By Gauss's lemma, f = g · h with g, h ∈ ℤ[x] both
 of positive degree. For any prime p not dividing the leading coefficients of g
and h, the reductions ḡ, ȟ ∈ 𝔽_p[x] retain positive degree, so f̄ = ḡ · ȟ is redu
cible in 𝔽_p[x]. Since infinitely many primes satisfy this condition, f cannot b
e irreducible over every 𝔽_p.

So assume f is irreducible over ℚ with deg f = n ≥ 2.

**Step 2: f has no integer root.**

Since f is irreducible over ℚ and deg f ≥ 2, f has no rational root, hence no in
teger root. Thus f(m) ≠ 0 for every m ∈ ℤ.

**Step 3: The set of primes dividing some value f(m) is infinite.**

For each integer m, f(m) is a nonzero integer. Define

S = { p prime : p divides f(m) for some m ∈ ℤ }.

Suppose for contradiction that S = {p₁, …, p_k} is finite. Then every f(m) has t
he form ±p₁^{a₁(m)} ··· p_k^{a_k(m)}.

Since deg f = n ≥ 2, we have |f(m)| → ∞ as m → +∞, and f is eventually monotone
(f′ has finitely many real roots, so f is monotone for all sufficiently large m)
. Thus there exists M₀ such that the values f(M₀), f(M₀+1), f(M₀+2), … are all d
istinct.

For m = M₀, M₀+1, …, M₀+N−1, we obtain N distinct integers, each with absolute v
alue at most C·(M₀+N)^n for some constant C > 0. Each is of the form ±p₁^{a₁} ··
· p_k^{a_k}.

The number of integers of the form ±p₁^{a₁} ··· p_k^{a_k} with absolute value ≤
X is at most

2·(⌊log₂ X⌋+1)^k = O((log X)^k).

Setting X = C·(M₀+N)^n, the count of such integers is O((log N)^k). But we need
N distinct such integers. For N sufficiently large,

N ≫ (log N)^k,

which is a contradiction. Therefore S is infinite.

**Step 4: f is reducible mod p for every p ∈ S.**

For each p ∈ S, there exists an integer m with p | f(m), so m mod p is a root of
 f̄ in 𝔽_p. Thus f̄ has a linear factor (x − m̄) in 𝔽_p[x]. Since deg f = n ≥ 2, th
is means f̄ is reducible over 𝔽_p.

Since S is infinite, f is reducible over 𝔽_p for infinitely many primes p, contr
adicting the assumption that f is irreducible over every 𝔽_p. ∎

**Remark.** Polynomials of degree 1 (e.g., f(x) = x) are trivially irreducible o
ver every 𝔽_p, since a degree-1 polynomial is irreducible over any field. The su
bstance of the problem is the degree ≥ 2 case: **no such polynomial exists**, as
 proved above.
