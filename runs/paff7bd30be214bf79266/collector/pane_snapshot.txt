## Solution

**Setup.** We have the complete graph $K_{10}$ on 10 vertices (no three collinea
r is irrelevant since we only use the combinatorial structure). The total number
 of edges is $\binom{10}{2}=45$. We choose 4 edges uniformly at random, so the s
ample space has size

$$\binom{45}{4} = \frac{45\cdot44\cdot43\cdot42}{24} = 148995.$$

**Counting favorable outcomes.** We want the number of 4-edge subsets containing
 at least one triangle (a 3-cycle on 3 vertices).

The number of triangles in $K_{10}$ is $\binom{10}{3}=120$. For each triangle (3
 edges), we choose 1 additional edge from the remaining $45-3=42$ edges, giving
$120\times 42 = 5040$ pairs (triangle, extra edge).

**Checking for overcounting.** We verify no 4-edge set is counted twice, i.e., n
o 4-edge set contains two distinct triangles. Two distinct triangles sharing $k$
 edges occupy $6-k$ edges total. They can share at most 1 edge (sharing 2 edges
forces the same triangle, since two edges determine all three vertices). So two
distinct triangles share at most 1 edge, requiring $\geq 5$ edges — impossible w
ithin 4 edges. Hence no overcounting.

Therefore the number of favorable 4-edge sets is exactly $5040$.

**Computing the probability.**

$$P = \frac{5040}{148995}.$$

Factor both:
- $5040 = 2^4\cdot 3^2\cdot 5\cdot 7$
- $148995 = 3^2\cdot 5\cdot 7\cdot 11\cdot 43$

So $\gcd(5040,148995)=3^2\cdot5\cdot7=315$, giving

$$P = \frac{5040/315}{148995/315} = \frac{16}{473}.$$

Since $473=11\cdot43$ is odd and $16=2^4$, we have $\gcd(16,473)=1$, so the frac
tion is in lowest terms.

Thus $m=16$, $n=473$, and

$$m+n = 16+473 = \boxed{489}.$$
