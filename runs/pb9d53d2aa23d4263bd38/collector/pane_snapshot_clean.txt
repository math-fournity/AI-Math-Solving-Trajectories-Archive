## Proof

**Setup.** Let $P_1, P_2, \ldots, P_n$ be the $n$ points, drawn i.i.d. uniformly
 in the hypersphere $H \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ of radius $r$. Let $x = P_1$ denote
 one of the chosen points, and let $y \in H$ be another point (fixed, not necess
arily among the chosen points). We seek
$$\Pr\bigl[x \text{ is the closest chosen point to } y\bigr].$$

**Key observation: exchangeability.** Since $P_1, \ldots, P_n$ are i.i.d. unifor
m on $H$, the joint distribution is invariant under any permutation of the label
s. For the fixed point $y$, define the events
$$A_i := \{\|P_i - y\| < \|P_j - y\| \text{ for all } j \neq i\}, \quad i = 1, \
ldots, n.$$
These are the events "$P_i$ is the (unique) closest chosen point to $y$."

**Ties have probability zero.** Since the points have continuous distributions,
$\Pr(\|P_i - y\| = \|P_j - y\|) = 0$ for $i \neq j$. Hence, almost surely, exact
ly one of $A_1, \ldots, A_n$ occurs:
$$\Pr\!\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^n A_i\right) = 1, \qquad \Pr(A_i \cap A_j) = 0 \;\;(i
 \neq j).$$

**Symmetry gives equal shares.** By exchangeability (the i.i.d. property), every
 permutation $\sigma$ preserves the joint law, so $\Pr(A_i) = \Pr(A_{\sigma(i)})
$ for all $i$ and all $\sigma$. Therefore
$$\Pr(A_1) = \Pr(A_2) = \cdots = \Pr(A_n).$$

Since the $A_i$ partition the sample space (up to null sets) into $n$ equally li
kely pieces:
$$\sum_{i=1}^n \Pr(A_i) = 1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Pr(A_i) = \frac{1}{n} \qu
ad\text{for each } i.$$

**Conclusion.** The chosen point $x = P_1$ is the closest chosen point to $y$ wi
th probability
$$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{n}}.$$

This result is independent of the dimension $d$, the radius $r$, and the specifi
c locations of $x$ and $y$ within $H$ — it relies solely on the i.i.d. (exchange
able) uniform distribution of the $n$ points.
