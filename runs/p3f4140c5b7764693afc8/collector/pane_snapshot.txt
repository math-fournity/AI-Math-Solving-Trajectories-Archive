$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x^6+1} = \Im\!\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\f

The poles satisfy $z^3 = i = e^{i\pi/2}$, giving
$$z_k = e^{i(\pi/2+2k\pi)/3},\qquad k=0,1,2.$$

- $z_0 = e^{i\pi/6} = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}+\tfrac{i}{2}$ — **upper** half-plane,
- $z_1 = e^{i5\pi/6} = -\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}+\tfrac{i}{2}$ — **upper** half-plane,
- $z_2 = e^{i3\pi/2} = -i$ — **lower** half-plane.

All three are simple poles.

**Step 3 — Close the contour in the upper half-plane.**

Let $C_R$ be the semicircle $\{Re^{i\theta}:0\le\theta\le\pi\}$. On $C_R$,
$$|f(z)|=\frac{1}{|z^3-i|}\le\frac{1}{R^3-1}\to 0,$$
so by Jordan's estimate $\int_{C_R}f(z)\,dz\to 0$ as $R\to\infty$ (since $|dz|=R

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x^3-i} = 2\pi i\sum_{\Im z_k>0}\operatorname{
Res}(f,z_k).$$

**Step 4 — Compute the residues.**

For a simple pole at $z_k$ with $z_k^3=i$,
$$\operatorname{Res}(f,z_k)=\frac{1}{(z^3-i)'\big|_{z=z_k}}=\frac{1}{3z_k^2}.$$

- At $z_0=e^{i\pi/6}$: $z_0^2=e^{i\pi/3}$, so
$$\operatorname{Res}(f,z_0)=\frac{1}{3}e^{-i\pi/3}=\frac{1}{3}\!\left(\tfrac12-\
tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i\right)=\tfrac16-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{6}i.$$

$$\operatorname{Res}(f,z_1)=\frac{1}{3}e^{i\pi/3}=\frac{1}{3}\!\left(\tfrac12+\t
frac{\sqrt3}{2}i\right)=\tfrac16+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{6}i.$$

$$\sum_{\Im z_k>0}\operatorname{Res}(f,z_k)=\left(\tfrac16-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{6}i\ri

Therefore
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x^3-i}=2\pi i\cdot\frac13=\frac{2\pi i}{3}.$$
