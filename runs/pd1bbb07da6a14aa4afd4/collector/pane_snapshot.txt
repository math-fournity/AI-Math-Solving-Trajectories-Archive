## Proof

**Construction of $L(p,q)$.** The lens space $L(p,q)$ is obtained by gluing two
solid tori $V_1 = S^1 \times D^2$ and $V_2 = S^1 \times D^2$ along their boundar
ies via a homeomorphism $h \colon \partial V_2 \to \partial V_1$. Set $L(p,q) =
V_1 \cup_h V_2$, with $T = \partial V_1 \cong \partial V_2$ the common torus.

**Coordinates on each torus.** On $\partial V_i = S^1 \times S^1$ let
- $\mu_i$ = the **meridian** (the $S^1$-factor of $\partial D^2$), which bounds
the disk $\{*\}\times D^2$ in $V_i$, hence is null-homotopic in $V_i$;
- $\lambda_i$ = the **longitude** (the $S^1$-factor of the core), which generate
s $\pi_1(V_i)$.

The gluing map $h$ for $L(p,q)$ sends the meridian of $V_2$ to the $(p,q)$-curve
 on $\partial V_1$:
$$\mu_2 \;\longmapsto\; p\,\lambda_1 + q\,\mu_1, \qquad \lambda_2 \;\longmapsto\
; r\,\lambda_1 + s\,\mu_1,$$
where $r,s$ are chosen so that $\begin{pmatrix} p & q \\ r & s \end{pmatrix} \in
 GL(2,\mathbb{Z})$ (i.e. $ps - qr = \pm 1$), guaranteeing $h$ is a torus homeomo
rphism. (Such $r,s$ exist because $\gcd(p,q)=1$.)

**Fundamental groups of the pieces.**
$$\pi_1(V_1) = \langle \lambda_1 \mid \mu_1 = 1 \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}, \qquad
 \pi_1(V_2) = \langle \lambda_2 \mid \mu_2 = 1 \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z},$$
since each solid torus deformation retracts onto its core circle, and the meridi
an bounds a disk.

The boundary torus has
$$\pi_1(T) = \langle \mu_1, \lambda_1 \mid [\mu_1,\lambda_1]=1 \rangle = \langle
 \mu_2, \lambda_2 \mid [\mu_2,\lambda_2]=1 \rangle,$$
the two presentations being related by the gluing matrix above.

**Inclusion-induced maps.**
- $i_{1*} \colon \pi_1(T) \to \pi_1(V_1)$: $\mu_1 \mapsto 1$, $\lambda_1 \mapsto
 \lambda_1$.
- $i_{2*} \colon \pi_1(T) \to \pi_1(V_2)$: $\mu_2 \mapsto 1$, $\lambda_2 \mapsto
 \lambda_2$.

**Applying Van Kampen.** Since $V_1, V_2, T$ are path-connected and $T = V_1 \ca
p V_2$, the Seifert–Van Kampen theorem gives
$$\pi_1(L(p,q)) \;=\; \pi_1(V_1) * \pi_1(V_2) \big/ \langle\!\langle\, i_{1*}(c)
\, i_{2*}(c)^{-1} : c \in \pi_1(T) \,\rangle\!\rangle.$$
Starting from generators $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$, it suffices to impose the relat
ions coming from $c = \mu_2$ and $c = \lambda_2$ (these generate $\pi_1(T)$).

*Relation from $c = \mu_2$.* Using the gluing, $\mu_2 = p\lambda_1 + q\mu_1$ in
$\partial V_1$-coordinates, so
$$i_{1*}(\mu_2) = i_{1*}(\mu_1^q \lambda_1^p) = \lambda_1^p, \qquad i_{2*}(\mu_2
) = 1.$$
The relation is
$$\lambda_1^p = 1. \tag{R1}$$

*Relation from $c = \lambda_2$.* Using $\lambda_2 = r\lambda_1 + s\mu_1$,
$$i_{1*}(\lambda_2) = i_{1*}(\mu_1^s \lambda_1^r) = \lambda_1^r, \qquad i_{2*}(\
lambda_2) = \lambda_2.$$
The relation is
$$\lambda_2 = \lambda_1^r. \tag{R2}$$

**The presentation.** Combining (R1) and (R2), the generator $\lambda_2$ is redu
ndant (expressed via $\lambda_1$), and we obtain
$$\boxed{\;\pi_1\bigl(L(p,q)\bigr) \;\cong\; \langle\, \lambda_1 \;\mid\; \lambd
a_1^p = 1 \,\rangle \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}.\;}$$

**Remarks.**
1. The parameter $q$ does **not** appear in the presentation: it enters only thr
ough the complementary longitude $\lambda_2 = r\lambda_1 + s\mu_1$, which is eli
minated by (R2). This recovers the classical fact that $\pi_1(L(p,q))$ depends o
nly on $p$.
2. The coprimality $\gcd(p,q)=1$ is precisely the condition that the gluing matr
ix lies in $GL(2,\mathbb{Z})$, i.e. that $h$ is a genuine homeomorphism of the t
orus; without it the construction would not yield a manifold.
3. For $p=1$, $\pi_1(L(1,q)) \cong \{1\}$, consistent with $L(1,q) \cong S^3$.
