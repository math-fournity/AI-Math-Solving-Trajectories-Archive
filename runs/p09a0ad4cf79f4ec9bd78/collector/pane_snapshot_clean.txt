## Bayesian Nash Equilibrium of the Symmetric All-Pay Auction

**Setup.** Two players, valuations $v_1, v_2 \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} U[0,
1]$. Each observes only his own valuation, then bids simultaneously. The higher
bid wins the object; **both pay their bids**. Payoff to player $i$: $v_i - b_i$
if he wins, $-b_i$ if he loses.

**Symmetric equilibrium ansatz.** We look for a symmetric BNE in which both play
ers use the same strictly increasing, differentiable bidding function $b:[0,1]\t
o\mathbb{R}_+$ with $b(0)=0$. Let $\phi = b^{-1}$ denote its inverse.

**Player 1's optimization.** Fix $v_1 = v$. Believing player 2 bids $b(v_2)$, pl
ayer 1 wins iff $b_1 > b(v_2)$, i.e. iff $v_2 < \phi(b_1)$. Since $v_2\sim U[0,1
]$, the win probability is $\phi(b_1)$. His expected payoff is

$$
U(b_1;v) \;=\; \phi(b_1)(v-b_1) + (1-\phi(b_1))(-b_1) \;=\; \phi(b_1)\,v \;-\; b
_1.
$$

**First-order condition.** Differentiating,
$$
\frac{\partial U}{\partial b_1} \;=\; \phi'(b_1)\,v \;-\; 1.
$$
In equilibrium player 1 optimally chooses $b_1 = b(v)$, at which point $\phi(b(v
)) = v$ and $\phi'(b(v)) = 1/b'(v)$. Substituting,
$$
\frac{v}{b'(v)} - 1 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad b'(v) = v.
$$

**Solving the ODE.** With the boundary condition $b(0)=0$ (a player with zero va
luation bids zero, since any positive bid yields strictly negative payoff),
$$
b(v) \;=\; \int_0^v t\,dt \;=\; \frac{v^2}{2}.
$$

**Second-order verification.** With $b(v)=v^2/2$, we have $\phi(b_1)=\sqrt{2b_1}
$ and
$$
U(b_1;v) = v\sqrt{2b_1} - b_1,\qquad
U'(b_1;v) = \frac{v}{\sqrt{2b_1}} - 1,\qquad
U''(b_1;v) = -\frac{v}{(2b_1)^{3/2}} < 0.
$$
So $U$ is strictly concave in $b_1$, and the unique maximizer satisfies $\sqrt{2
b_1}=v$, i.e. $b_1 = v^2/2 = b(v)$. This confirms that bidding $b(v)$ is a globa
l best response when the opponent uses $b(\cdot)$.

**Boundary behavior.** For $v\in[0,1]$, $b(v)=v^2/2\in[0,\tfrac12]$, so $\phi$ m
aps $[0,\tfrac12]$ onto $[0,1]$ as required; bids never exceed $\tfrac12 < 1$, s
o no player ever bids above the maximum possible valuation, and the inverse is w
ell-defined on the relevant range.

**Conclusion.** The unique symmetric Bayesian Nash equilibrium is for each playe
r $i$ to bid

$$
\boxed{\,b_i \;=\; b(v_i) \;=\; \dfrac{v_i^{\,2}}{2}\,}
$$

i.e. each player bids half the square of his own valuation. By symmetry and the
standard uniqueness argument for monotone equilibria in this class of auctions (
the ODE $b'(v)=v$ with $b(0)=0$ has a unique solution), this is the equilibrium
bidding strategy.
