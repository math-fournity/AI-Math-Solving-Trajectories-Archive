## Solution

Let $X_1, X_2, X_3, X_4 \stackrel{iid}{\sim} N(0,1)$, and define
$$Y = \frac{c(X_1 - X_2)}{\sqrt{X_3^2 + X_4^2}}.$$

**Step 1: Distribution of the numerator part.**

Since $X_1, X_2$ are independent standard normals,
$$X_1 - X_2 \sim N(0,\, 1+1) = N(0,2).$$
Therefore
$$Z := \frac{X_1 - X_2}{\sqrt{2}} \sim N(0,1),$$
and $Z$ is independent of $(X_3, X_4)$ (hence of $X_3^2+X_4^2$).

**Step 2: Distribution of the denominator part.**

Since $X_3, X_4 \stackrel{iid}{\sim} N(0,1)$,
$$W := X_3^2 + X_4^2 \sim \chi^2(2),$$
i.e. a chi-squared distribution with $2$ degrees of freedom, and $W$ is independ
ent of $Z$.

**Step 3: Rewrite $Y$ in terms of $Z$ and $W$.**

$$Y = \frac{c(X_1-X_2)}{\sqrt{W}} = \frac{c\sqrt{2}\, Z}{\sqrt{W}}.$$

**Step 4: Match the $t$-distribution form.**

A Student-$t$ random variable with $\nu$ degrees of freedom has the form
$$T_\nu = \frac{Z}{\sqrt{W/\nu}}, \qquad Z\sim N(0,1),\ W\sim\chi^2(\nu),\ Z\per
p W.$$

Here $W\sim\chi^2(2)$, so the natural candidate is $\nu = 2$:
$$T_2 = \frac{Z}{\sqrt{W/2}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}\, Z}{\sqrt{W}}.$$

Comparing with $Y = \dfrac{c\sqrt{2}\, Z}{\sqrt{W}}$, we need
$$c\sqrt{2} = \sqrt{2} \implies c = 1.$$

**Step 5: Verification.**

With $c=1$,
$$Y = \frac{X_1 - X_2}{\sqrt{X_3^2+X_4^2}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}\,Z}{\sqrt{W}} = \fra
c{Z}{\sqrt{W/2}} \sim t(2),$$
exactly the Student-$t$ distribution with $2$ degrees of freedom, since $Z\sim N
(0,1)$, $W\sim\chi^2(2)$, and $Z\perp W$.

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{c = 1}$$
