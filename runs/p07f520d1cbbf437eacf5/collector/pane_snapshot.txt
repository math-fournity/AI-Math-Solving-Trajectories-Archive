## Proof

The problem asks us to classify the time complexity of an algorithm whose **stat
ed running time is $N$ minutes and 30 seconds**, and to decide whether this is $
O(N)$ or $O(2^N)$.

**Key observation.** The running time is given to us explicitly and concretely:

$$T(N) = N \text{ minutes} + 30 \text{ seconds}.$$

Converting to a single unit (minutes),

$$T(N) = N + \tfrac{1}{2} \text{ minutes}.$$

This is an **explicit, closed-form expression** for the running time as a functi
on of $N$. We do not need to derive it from the algorithm's structure, the natur
e of boolean satisfiability, or the number of mathematicians — the time is hande
d to us directly.

**Applying the definition of Big-O.** By definition, $T(N) = O(g(N))$ if there e
xist constants $c > 0$ and $N_0$ such that $T(N) \le c \cdot g(N)$ for all $N \g
e N_0$.

- **For $g(N) = N$:** We have $T(N) = N + \tfrac{1}{2} \le \tfrac{3}{2} N$ for a
ll $N \ge 1$. Taking $c = \tfrac{3}{2}$ and $N_0 = 1$, the definition is satisfi
ed. Hence $T(N) = O(N)$. ✓

- **For $g(N) = 2^N$:** While it is *technically true* that $N + \tfrac{1}{2} =
O(2^N)$ (since $2^N$ grows faster), Big-O notation is about **characterizing the
 growth rate** of a function with a **tight** (or at least minimal reasonable) u
pper bound. The expression $N + \tfrac{1}{2}$ is *linear* in $N$; classifying it
 as $O(2^N)$ would be a grossly loose, misleading bound that fails to capture th
e actual growth. The natural, correct classification of a linear function is $O(
N)$, not $O(2^N)$.

**Why the framing is a distraction.** The references to "boolean satisfiability"
 (an NP-complete problem whose naive algorithms are $O(2^N)$) and "an infinite n
umber of mathematicians at a party" (a classic joke setup) are **red herrings**.
 The problem does **not** ask us to analyze the complexity of SAT in general, no
r to model the parallelism of infinitely many mathematicians. It tells us the al
gorithm's running time **outright**: $N$ minutes and 30 seconds. The complexity
classification applies to *that given running time*, which is linear.

The 30 seconds is a **constant additive term**, which is absorbed (dropped) in B
ig-O notation. Only the $N$ term survives, giving $O(N)$.

**Conclusion.** The running time $T(N) = N + \tfrac{1}{2}$ is linear in $N$, so
the correct classification is:

$$\boxed{O(N)}$$
