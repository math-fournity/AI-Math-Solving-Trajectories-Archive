## Solution

We solve the heat equation $u_t = 7u_{xx}$ on $0<x<\pi$ with homogeneous Neumann
 boundary conditions $u_x(0,t)=u_x(\pi,t)=0$ and initial data $u(x,0)=1-\sin x$.

**Step 1: Eigenfunction expansion.** Separation of variables with Neumann condit
ions gives eigenfunctions $\cos(nx)$, $n=0,1,2,\dots$, with eigenvalues $\lambda
_n=n^2$. The formal solution is

$$u(x,t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} A_n\, e^{-7n^2 t}\cos(nx).$$

At $t=0$: $\;1-\sin x = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} A_n \cos(nx)$.

**Step 2: Compute coefficients.** Using orthogonality on $[0,\pi]$:

- **$n=0$:** $\;A_0 = \dfrac{1}{\pi}\displaystyle\int_0^\pi (1-\sin x)\,dx = \df
rac{1}{\pi}\Big[x+\cos x\Big]_0^\pi = \dfrac{1}{\pi}(\pi-1-1)=\dfrac{\pi-2}{\pi}
.$

- **$n\geq 1$:** $\;A_n = \dfrac{2}{\pi}\displaystyle\int_0^\pi (1-\sin x)\cos(n
x)\,dx.$

  Since $\displaystyle\int_0^\pi \cos(nx)\,dx = 0$ for $n\geq 1$, we have $A_n =
 -\dfrac{2}{\pi}\displaystyle\int_0^\pi \sin x\cos(nx)\,dx.$

  Using $\sin x\cos(nx)=\tfrac12[\sin((n+1)x)+\sin((1-n)x)]$:

  **$n=1$:** $\;\displaystyle\int_0^\pi \sin x\cos x\,dx = \tfrac12\displaystyle
\int_0^\pi \sin 2x\,dx = 0,\;$ so $A_1=0$.

  **$n\geq 2$:**
  $$\int_0^\pi \sin x\cos(nx)\,dx = \frac{1-(-1)^{n+1}}{1-n^2}.$$
  Therefore
  $$A_n = -\frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\frac{1-(-1)^{n+1}}{1-n^2} = \frac{2\bigl(1-(-1)^{n
+1}\bigr)}{\pi(n^2-1)}.$$
  - If $n$ is odd ($n\geq 3$): $(-1)^{n+1}=1$, so $A_n=0$.
  - If $n$ is even ($n=2k$, $k\geq 1$): $(-1)^{n+1}=-1$, so $A_{2k}=\dfrac{4}{\p
i(4k^2-1)}$.

**Step 3: Verification of initial data.** The series becomes
$$u(x,0)=\frac{\pi-2}{\pi}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4}{\pi(4k^2-1)}\cos(2kx).$$
The partial fraction $\dfrac{4}{4k^2-1}=\dfrac{2}{2k-1}-\dfrac{2}{2k+1}$ telesco
pes, and one checks (e.g. at $x=0$) that $\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{4}{4k^2-1}=2$, giv
ing $(\pi-2)/\pi+2/\pi=1=\,1-\sin 0$. ✓ The cosine series of $1-\sin x$ on $[0,\
pi]$ with Neumann extension is exactly this even-reflected function, confirming
the expansion.

**Step 4: Formal solution.**

$$\boxed{\;u(x,t)=\frac{\pi-2}{\pi}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4}{\pi(4k^2-1)}\,e^
{-28k^2 t}\cos(2kx)\;}$$

This series converges uniformly for $t\geq t_0>0$ (with all derivatives), so $u$
 is $C^\infty$ for $t>0$, satisfies $u_t=7u_{xx}$, the Neumann conditions $u_x(0
,t)=u_x(\pi,t)=0$ (since $\frac{d}{dx}\cos(2kx)\big|_{x=0,\pi}=0$), and $u(x,0)=
1-\sin x$ by the Fourier–Neumann expansion above.
