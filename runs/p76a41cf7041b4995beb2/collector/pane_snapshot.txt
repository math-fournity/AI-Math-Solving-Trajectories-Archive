## Proof

Let $T \in \mathcal{B}(X)$ be a Fredholm operator on a Banach space $X$. By defi
nition, $T$ being Fredholm means:

1. $\ker T$ is finite-dimensional,
2. $\operatorname{ran} T$ is closed in $X$,
3. $\operatorname{coker} T := X / \operatorname{ran} T$ is finite-dimensional.

The **Fredholm index** of $T$ is

$$\operatorname{ind}(T) = \dim \ker T - \dim \operatorname{coker} T.$$

We are given:

- $\operatorname{ind}(T) = 0$,
- $\ker T = \{0\}$, i.e., $\dim \ker T = 0$.

Substituting into the index formula:

$$0 = \operatorname{ind}(T) = \dim \ker T - \dim \operatorname{coker} T = 0 - \d
im \operatorname{coker} T.$$

Hence

$$\dim \operatorname{coker} T = 0.$$

Since $\operatorname{ran} T$ is closed (a requirement of Fredholmness), the quot
ient $X / \operatorname{ran} T$ is a well-defined Banach space. A vector space q
uotient has dimension zero if and only if it is the trivial space:

$$X / \operatorname{ran} T = \{0\}.$$

This means every coset $x + \operatorname{ran} T$ equals $\operatorname{ran} T$
itself, i.e., every $x \in X$ belongs to $\operatorname{ran} T$. Therefore

$$\operatorname{ran} T = X,$$

so $T$ is surjective.

**Conclusion:** Yes. If a bounded operator $T$ on a Banach space $X$ is Fredholm
 with index zero and has trivial null space, then $T$ is surjective. In fact, un
der these hypotheses $T$ is bijective (injective by the trivial null space, surj
ective as shown above), and by the bounded inverse theorem (open mapping theorem
), $T$ is an isomorphism of $X$ onto itself.
