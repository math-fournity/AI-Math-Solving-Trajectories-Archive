## Setup

ordinates. The circle has radius $R = 100$ and center $(a, b)$. By the translati

**Key formula.** For a simple closed curve that crosses grid lines transversally
 (never at a vertex), the number of cells intersected equals the total number of
 grid-line crossings. As we traverse the curve, each crossing moves us into a ne
w cell; since the curve is closed we return to the starting cell, so distinct ce
lls visited = number of crossings. Thus:
$$N = V + H$$
where $V$ = total crossings of vertical lines, $H$ = total crossings of horizont

## Counting crossings

The circle crosses vertical line $x = k$ (for integer $k$) iff $|k - a| < R$, an
d each such line is crossed exactly **twice** (once entering, once exiting). So:
$$V = 2 \cdot \#\{k \in \mathbb{Z} : |k - a| < 100\}, \qquad H = 2 \cdot \#\{k \
in \mathbb{Z} : |k - b| < 100\}.$$

## Constraints on the center

**Non-tangency condition** ("does not touch any sides"): the circle must not be
tangent to any grid line. Tangency to $x = k$ occurs when $|k - a| = R = 100$ fo

sfying $|k-a| = 100$, giving tangency — **forbidden**. So $a \notin \mathbb{Z}$,

Reducing modulo 1: $a, b \in (0, 1)$.

**Non-tangency is then automatic:** for $a \in (0,1)$, the values $a \pm 100$ li

integer $k$ satisfies $|k - a| = 100$. ✓

**Non-vertex condition** ("does not pass through any vertices"): $(m-a)^2 + (n-b

## Computing $V$ and $H$

For $a \in (0, 1)$: the interval $(a - 100,\, a + 100)$ has $a - 100 \in (-100,
-99)$ and $a + 100 \in (100, 101)$.

- Integers strictly inside: from $-99$ (smallest integer $> a - 100$) to $100$ (
largest integer $< a + 100$).
- Count: $100 - (-99) + 1 = 200$ integers.

This count is **independent** of the specific value of $a \in (0,1)$.

Therefore:
$$V = 2 \times 200 = 400, \qquad H = 2 \times 200 = 400.$$
