Looking at this problem, I need to find the side length of a square inscribed in
 a 120° sector with two adjacent vertices on the arc and two vertices on the rad
ii.

## Setup

Place the sector's vertex at the origin $O$, with the bisector of the 120° angle
 along the positive $y$-axis. The two radii then point at angles $30°$ and $150°
$ from the positive $x$-axis.

By the symmetry of the sector about its bisector, the inscribed square must also
 be symmetric about the $y$-axis. Thus the two vertices on the radii are mirror
images across the $y$-axis, as are the two vertices on the arc.

Let the two vertices on the radii be:
$$A = \left(\tfrac{t\sqrt{3}}{2},\, \tfrac{t}{2}\right), \qquad B = \left(-\tfra
c{t\sqrt{3}}{2},\, \tfrac{t}{2}\right)$$
where $t$ is the distance from $O$ to each (they are equidistant by symmetry). V
ertex $A$ lies on the radius at $30°$ and $B$ on the radius at $150°$.

The edge $AB$ is horizontal with length:
$$s = |AB| = t\sqrt{3}.$$

The square extends **upward** (toward the arc) from edge $AB$. Since $AB$ is hor
izontal, the perpendicular edge has direction $(0, s)$, giving the other two ver
tices:
$$D = A + (0,\, s) = \left(\tfrac{t\sqrt{3}}{2},\, \tfrac{t}{2} + t\sqrt{3}\righ
t) = \left(\tfrac{t\sqrt{3}}{2},\, \tfrac{t(1+2\sqrt{3})}{2}\right),$$
$$C = B + (0,\, s) = \left(-\tfrac{t\sqrt{3}}{2},\, \tfrac{t(1+2\sqrt{3})}{2}\ri

## Applying the arc constraint

Both $C$ and $D$ lie on the arc of radius $R = 2 + \sqrt{19}$, so $|D|^2 = R^2$:

$$|D|^2 = \frac{3t^2}{4} + \frac{t^2(1+2\sqrt{3})^2}{4} = \frac{t^2\!\left[3 + 1
 + 4\sqrt{3} + 12\right]}{4} = \frac{t^2(16 + 4\sqrt{3})}{4} = t^2(4 + \sqrt{3})
.$$

Setting this equal to $R^2$:
$$t^2(4 + \sqrt{3}) = R^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t^2 = \frac{R^2}{4+\sqrt{3}}
.$$

By symmetry, $|C|^2 = |D|^2 = R^2$ is automatically satisfied. One can also veri
fy that $C$ and $D$ lie within the sector (their angles from the $x$-axis are ap
proximately $68.8°$ and $111.2°$, both within $[30°, 150°]$), and since the sect
or is convex, the entire square lies inside it.

## Computing the side length

Since $s = t\sqrt{3}$:
$$s^2 = 3t^2 = \frac{3R^2}{4+\sqrt{3}}.$$

With $R = 2 + \sqrt{19}$, we have $R^2 = (2+\sqrt{19})^2 = 23 + 4\sqrt{19}$, so:
$$s^2 = \frac{3(23 + 4\sqrt{19})}{4 + \sqrt{3}}.$$

Rationalizing the denominator by multiplying by $\frac{4-\sqrt{3}}{4-\sqrt{3}}$
(using $(4+\sqrt{3})(4-\sqrt{3}) = 13$):
$$s^2 = \frac{3(23 + 4\sqrt{19})(4 - \sqrt{3})}{13}.$$

Therefore:
$$\boxed{s = (2+\sqrt{19})\,\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{4+\sqrt{3}}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{3(23+4\s
