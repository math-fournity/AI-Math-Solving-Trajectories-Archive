## Proof

**Step 1: Identify the inner sum as a partial Taylor sum.**

The Taylor series of $f$ expanded about $t = a$ is
$$f(t) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!}(t-a)^n.$$

Setting $t - a = -a$ (i.e., $t = 0$):
$$f(0) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n \frac{a^n}{n!}\, f^{(n)}(a).$$

Since $f(0) = (b/b)^2 = 1$, the inner sum $S_{i-1} := \sum_{n=0}^{i-1}(-1)^n \fr
ac{a^n}{n!} f^{(n)}(a)$ is the $(i{-}1)$-th partial sum of a series converging t
o $1$. Therefore
$$1 - S_{i-1} = \sum_{n=i}^{\infty} (-1)^n \frac{a^n}{n!}\, f^{(n)}(a),$$
the tail of the Taylor series.

**Step 2: Exchange the order of summation.**

$$\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(1 - S_{i-1}) = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=i}^{\infty} c_n,
 \qquad c_n := (-1)^n \frac{a^n}{n!} f^{(n)}(a).$$

For fixed $n \geq 1$, the term $c_n$ appears in the inner sum for every $i$ with
 $1 \leq i \leq n$, i.e., exactly $n$ times. (The $n=0$ term never appears since
 $i \geq 1$.) Hence
$$\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(1 - S_{i-1}) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n\, c_n = \sum_{n=1}^{\
infty} (-1)^n \frac{a^n}{(n-1)!}\, f^{(n)}(a). \tag{$\star$}$$

**Step 3: Apply the Cauchy integral formula.**

Choose a contour $C$ encircling $a$ with radius $R$ satisfying $|a| < R < |a+b|$
 (so $C$ encloses both $a$ and $0$, avoids the pole at $-b$, and $|a|/|z-a| < 1$
 on $C$). By Cauchy's formula:
$$f^{(n)}(a) = \frac{n!}{2\pi i}\oint_C \frac{f(z)}{(z-a)^{n+1}}\,dz.$$

Substituting into $(\star)$:
$$\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-S_{i-1}) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_C f(z) \sum_{n=1}^{\inf
ty} \frac{n(-a)^n}{(z-a)^{n+1}}\,dz.$$

Set $w = -a/(z-a)$ with $|w|<1$ on $C$. Then
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n(-a)^n}{(z-a)^{n+1}} = \frac{1}{z-a}\sum_{n=1}^{\in
fty} n\,w^n = \frac{1}{z-a}\cdot\frac{w}{(1-w)^2}.$$

Since $1 - w = 1 + \frac{a}{z-a} = \frac{z}{z-a}$:
$$\frac{w}{(1-w)^2} = \frac{-a/(z-a)}{z^2/(z-a)^2} = \frac{-a(z-a)}{z^2}.$$

Therefore
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n(-a)^n}{(z-a)^{n+1}} = \frac{1}{z-a}\cdot\frac{-a(z
-a)}{z^2} = \frac{-a}{z^2}.$$

**Step 4: Evaluate the resulting integral.**

$$\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-S_{i-1}) = \frac{-a}{2\pi i}\oint_C \frac{f(z)}{z^2}\,dz
 = -a\cdot f'(0),$$
where the last equality uses the Cauchy integral formula $\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint
\frac{f(z)}{z^2}dz = f'(0)$ (valid since $C$ encloses $0$).

**Step 5: Compute $f'(0)$.**

$$f(t) = b^2(t+b)^{-2} \implies f'(t) = -2b^2(t+b)^{-3} \implies f'(0) = \frac{-
2b^2}{b^3} = -\frac{2}{b}.$$

**Conclusion:**

$$\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left[1-\sum_{n=0}^{i-1}(-1)^n \frac{a^n}{n!}\left.\frac{d^
n}{dt^n}f(t)\right|_{t=a}\right] = -a\cdot\left(-\frac{2}{b}\right) = \boxed{\df
rac{2a}{b}}.$$

 $-1\cdot(-1)=1=2(1)/2$. ✓ For $a=1, b=3$: $f'(0)=-2/3$, giving $2/3 = 2(1)/3$.
