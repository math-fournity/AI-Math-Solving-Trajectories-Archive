Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum number of lines (none throug
h $P$) such that every ray from $P$ hits at least $k$ lines.

## Reformulation

Each line $\ell$ not passing through $P$ blocks a set of directions: a ray from
$P$ in direction $\theta$ hits $\ell$ iff $\theta$ lies in an **open semicircle*
* $S_\ell$ of length $\pi$ on the circle of directions. (The two directions para
llel to $\ell$ do not hit it, making the semicircle open.) The problem becomes:
**$k$-fold cover the circle $S^1$ by open semicircles of length $\pi$, minimizin
g their count.**

## Lower Bound: $2k$ lines are insufficient

Suppose $n=2k$ lines suffice, blocking open semicircles $S_i=(a_i,\, a_i+\pi)$ f
or $i=1,\dots,2k$. For direction $\theta$, define the *uncovered count*
$$u(\theta)=|\{i:\theta\notin S_i\}|.$$
We need $u(\theta)\le 2k-k=k$ for every $\theta$ (so that coverage $\ge k$).

**Average of $u$.** Since each $S_i$ is open of length $\pi$, its complement has
 measure $\pi$, so
$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} u(\theta)\,d\theta=\sum_{i=1}^{2k}\frac{\pi}{2\pi}
=k.$$
With $u(\theta)\le k$ everywhere and average exactly $k$, we must have $u(\theta
)=k$ for almost every $\theta$.

**Why this is impossible.** The function $u$ is integer-valued and changes by $\
pm 1$ exactly at the boundary points $\theta=a_i$ and $\theta=a_i+\pi$ (where $\
theta$ enters/exits the closed semicircle complement of $S_i$). For $u$ to remai
n constant a.e., every entry must be paired with a simultaneous exit: each $a_i+
\pi$ must coincide with some $a_j$, i.e., the $a_i$ come in **antipodal pairs**
$\{a_i,\, a_i+\pi\}$.

Now consider $\theta = a_i+\pi$ (a shared boundary point of the pair $\{a_i, a_i
+\pi\}$). At this direction, $\theta$ lies on the boundary of both $S_i$ and $S_
j$ (where $a_j=a_i+\pi$), so **neither** line is hit. The closed semicircle $[\t
heta,\theta+\pi]=[a_j,\,a_j+\pi]$ has both $b_i=a_i+\pi$ and $b_j=a_j+\pi=a_i$ a
t its endpoints (both counted), and each of the remaining $k-1$ antipodal pairs
contributes exactly one interior point. Thus
$$u(\theta)=2+(k-1)=k+1>k,$$
giving coverage $2k-(k+1)=k-1<k$. **Contradiction.**

If the $a_i$ do *not* form antipodal pairs, then $u$ is not constant, so (since
its average is $k$) it exceeds $k$ somewhere — again a contradiction. Hence **$2
k$ lines never suffice**, and $n\ge 2k+1$.

## Upper Bound: $2k+1$ lines suffice

Place $2k+1$ lines $\ell_0,\dots,\ell_{2k}$ at distance $\varepsilon>0$ from $P$
, with outward normal directions $a_i=\frac{2\pi i}{2k+1}$. Each blocks the open
 semicircle $S_i=(a_i,\,a_i+\pi)$.

For any $\theta$, the uncovered count $u(\theta)$ equals the number of points $b
_i=a_i+\pi$ lying in the closed semicircle $[\theta,\theta+\pi]$ of length $\pi$
. The $b_i$ are equally spaced with spacing $\frac{2\pi}{2k+1}$. A closed interv
al of length $\pi$ contains at most
$$\left\lfloor\frac{\pi}{2\pi/(2k+1)}\right\rfloor+1=\left\lfloor\frac{2k+1}{2}\
right\rfloor+1=k+1$$
of these points. Therefore $u(\theta)\le k+1$ for every $\theta$, and the covera
ge is
$$(2k+1)-u(\theta)\ge (2k+1)-(k+1)=k.$$
So every ray from $P$ hits at least $k$ lines.

## Conclusion

The minimum number of lines is $\boxed{2k+1}$.
