# Proof

**Claim:** Yes. Every weak-star compact subset of $L^\infty$ is automatically bo
unded in the $L^\infty$-norm.

**Setup.** Let $(X, \mathcal{M}, \mu)$ be a measure space. The duality $(L^1)^*
\cong L^\infty$ holds (for any measure space, via the standard pairing $\langle
g, f\rangle = \int_X fg\,d\mu$ for $g \in L^\infty$, $f \in L^1$). The weak-star
 topology on $L^\infty$ is $\sigma(L^\infty, L^1)$, i.e., the coarsest topology
making every map $g \mapsto \int_X fg\,d\mu$ ($f \in L^1$) continuous.

**General theorem.** Let $X$ be a Banach space and $K \subseteq X^*$ be weak-* c
ompact. Then $K$ is norm-bounded, i.e., $\sup_{x^* \in K} \|x^*\|_{X^*} < \infty
$.

*Proof of the theorem.* For each $x \in X$, the evaluation map
$$\Phi_x : X^* \to \mathbb{F}, \qquad \Phi_x(x^*) = x^*(x)$$
is weak-* continuous by definition of the weak-* topology. Since $K$ is weak-* c
ompact and $\Phi_x$ is continuous, the image $\Phi_x(K) = \{x^*(x) : x^* \in K\}
$ is a compact — hence bounded — subset of $\mathbb{F}$. Therefore, for every $x
 \in X$,
$$\sup_{x^* \in K} |x^*(x)| < \infty. \tag{1}$$

Equation (1) says the family $\{x^*\}_{x^* \in K} \subseteq X^*$ is **pointwise
bounded** on $X$. Since each $x^*$ is a bounded (continuous) linear functional o
n the Banach space $X$, the **Uniform Boundedness Principle** (Banach–Steinhaus
theorem) applies: pointwise boundedness of a family of bounded linear operators
implies uniform boundedness of their norms. Hence
$$\sup_{x^* \in K} \|x^*\|_{X^*} < \infty. \qquad \square$$

**Application to $L^\infty$.** Set $X = L^1(X,\mu)$, so $X^* \cong L^\infty(X,\m
u)$. The weak-* topology $\sigma(X^*, X) = \sigma(L^\infty, L^1)$ is exactly the
 weak-star topology on $L^\infty$. Applying the theorem: any weak-* compact $K \
subseteq L^\infty$ satisfies
$$\sup_{g \in K} \|g\|_{L^\infty} = \sup_{g \in K} \|g\|_{(L^1)^*} < \infty.$$

**Conclusion.** A weak-star compact subset of $L^\infty$ is automatically bounde
d in the $L^\infty$-norm. The key mechanism is that weak-* compactness forces po
intwise boundedness against every $L^1$ function, and the Uniform Boundedness Pr
inciple upgrades this to uniform norm-boundedness.
