## Proof

**Yes**, it follows that $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{B}$ are elementarily equiva
lent.

**Key tool: Łoś's Theorem.** Let $I$ be the index set over which the non-princip
al ultrafilter $\mathcal{U}$ lives, so that $\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{U} = \mathcal{
A}^I/\mathcal{U}$ is the ultrapower of $\mathcal{A}$, and similarly $\mathcal{B}
/\mathcal{U} = \mathcal{B}^I/\mathcal{U}$. Łoś's theorem states that for every f
irst-order $\mathcal{L}$-sentence $\varphi$,

$$\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{U} \models \varphi \iff \{i \in I : \mathcal{A} \models \
varphi\} \in \mathcal{U}.$$

Since the truth value of a sentence $\varphi$ in the single structure $\mathcal{
A}$ is constant across all $i \in I$, the set $\{i \in I : \mathcal{A} \models \
varphi\}$ is either $I$ (if $\mathcal{A} \models \varphi$) or $\emptyset$ (if $\
mathcal{A} \not\models \varphi$). Because $\mathcal{U}$ is a (proper) ultrafilte
r, $I \in \mathcal{U}$ and $\emptyset \notin \mathcal{U}$. Therefore:

$$\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{U} \models \varphi \iff \mathcal{A} \models \varphi.$$

Since this holds for every $\mathcal{L}$-sentence $\varphi$, we conclude:

$$\mathcal{A} \equiv \mathcal{A}/\mathcal{U}.$$

By the identical argument applied to $\mathcal{B}$:

$$\mathcal{B} \equiv \mathcal{B}/\mathcal{U}.$$

**Isomorphism implies elementary equivalence.** The hypothesis is $\mathcal{A}/\
mathcal{U} \cong \mathcal{B}/\mathcal{U}$. Isomorphic structures satisfy exactly
 the same first-order sentences, so:

$$\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{U} \equiv \mathcal{B}/\mathcal{U}.$$

**Combining via transitivity.** Elementary equivalence is an equivalence relatio
n (reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity all follow immediately from its defin
ition as agreement on all sentences). Chaining the three relations:

$$\mathcal{A} \;\equiv\; \mathcal{A}/\mathcal{U} \;\equiv\; \mathcal{B}/\mathcal
{U} \;\equiv\; \mathcal{B}.$$

By transitivity, $\mathcal{A} \equiv \mathcal{B}$.

**Remark on the role of $\mathcal{U}$ being non-principal.** The argument above
uses only that $\mathcal{U}$ is a proper ultrafilter (so that $I \in \mathcal{U}
$ and $\emptyset \notin \mathcal{U}$). The non-principality hypothesis is not ne
eded for this particular conclusion; the result holds for any ultrafilter. The n
on-principality is typically assumed in ultraproduct contexts to ensure the ultr
apower is a genuine "limit" construction (e.g., for saturation properties via Ke
isler–Shelah), but elementary equivalence of the ultrapower with the original st
ructure follows from Łoś's theorem alone.

**Conclusion.** If $\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{U} \cong \mathcal{B}/\mathcal{U}$, then
 $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{B}$ are elementarily equivalent.
