## Solution

We solve the Dirichlet problem for Laplace's equation in the upper half-plane us
ing the Poisson integral formula.

**Poisson kernel.** For the upper half-plane $y > 0$, the Poisson kernel is
$$P(x,y) = \frac{1}{\pi}\frac{y}{x^2 + y^2},$$
and the bounded harmonic function with boundary data $u(x,0) = f(x)$ is
$$u(x,y) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} P(x-t,\, y)\, f(t)\, dt = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{
-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{y}{(x-t)^2 + y^2}\, f(t)\, dt.$$

**Applying the boundary data.** Here
$$f(t) = \begin{cases} 1, & -1 < t < 0, \\ 0, & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases}$$
so the integral collapses to the interval $[-1, 0]$:
$$u(x,y) = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{0} \frac{y}{(x-t)^2 + y^2}\, dt.$$

**Evaluating the integral.** Substitute $s = x - t$, $ds = -dt$. When $t = -1$,
$s = x+1$; when $t = 0$, $s = x$:
$$u(x,y) = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{x}^{x+1} \frac{y}{s^2 + y^2}\, ds = \frac{1}{\pi}\
left[\arctan\!\left(\frac{s}{y}\right)\right]_{s=x}^{s=x+1}.$$

Therefore
$$\boxed{\,u(x,y) = \frac{1}{\pi}\left[\arctan\!\left(\frac{x+1}{y}\right) - \ar
ctan\!\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)\right], \qquad y > 0.\,}$$

**Verification of the boundary conditions ($y \to 0^+$).** Using $\lim_{a\to 0^+
}\arctan(c/a) = \frac{\pi}{2}\operatorname{sgn}(c)$:

- **$0 < x < \infty$:** Both $x+1 > 0$ and $x > 0$, so both arctangents $\to \pi
/2$. Thus $u \to \frac{1}{\pi}(\pi/2 - \pi/2) = 0$. ✓
- **$-1 < x < 0$:** Here $x+1 > 0$ but $x < 0$, so $\arctan\frac{x+1}{y} \to \pi
/2$ and $\arctan\frac{x}{y} \to -\pi/2$. Thus $u \to \frac{1}{\pi}(\pi/2 - (-\pi
/2)) = 1$. ✓
- **$-\infty < x < -1$:** Both $x+1 < 0$ and $x < 0$, so both arctangents $\to -
\pi/2$. Thus $u \to 0$. ✓

**Harmonicity.** $u$ is the Poisson integral of an $L^\infty$ boundary function,
 hence harmonic in $y > 0$ (equivalently, each $\arctan$ term is the imaginary p
art of $\log(z - a)$, hence harmonic).

**Boundedness at infinity.** Since $|\arctan| \le \pi/2$, we have $|u| \le 1$ ev
erywhere. Moreover, as $|(x,y)| \to \infty$ with $y > 0$, both arguments $\frac{
x+1}{y}$ and $\frac{x}{y}$ behave identically to leading order, so their arctang
ents agree asymptotically and $u \to 0$. Hence $u$ is bounded at infinity. ✓

**Uniqueness.** By the maximum principle, a bounded harmonic function in the upp
er half-plane with prescribed continuous boundary data (away from the jump point
s $x = -1, 0$) is unique. Our solution satisfies all the given conditions, so it
 is the desired solution.
