## Solution

**Claim:** The smallest such $C$ is $C = 5^{15}$.

**Homogeneity check.** The left side has degree $2005$ in the $x_i$. The right s
ide has degree $5 + 16\cdot 125 = 5 + 2000 = 2005$. So the inequality is homogen

x_1=\cdots=x_5=1$.

**Lower bound on $C$.** Setting $x_1=\cdots=x_5=1$: LHS $= 5C$, RHS $= 1\cdot(5)
^{16}=5^{16}$. Hence $5C\ge 5^{16}$, i.e. $C\ge 5^{15}$. So $C=5^{15}$ is the on
ly candidate.

**Reduction by normalization.** We prove that $C=5^{15}$ works, i.e.
$$5^{15}\sum_{i=1}^5 x_i^{2005}\;\ge\;\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^5 x_i\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{i
=1}^5 x_i^{125}\Bigr)^{16}.$$
By homogeneity, normalize $\sum_{i=1}^5 x_i^{125}=5$. Then the inequality become
s
$$\sum_{i=1}^5 x_i^{2005}\;\ge\;5\prod_{i=1}^5 x_i.\qquad(\star)$$

**Substitution.** Let $u_i = x_i^{125/25}=x_i^5$... — more cleanly, set $u_i = x
_i^{5}$, so $x_i^{125}=u_i^{25}$ and $x_i^{2005}=u_i^{401}$, and $\prod x_i = \p
rod u_i^{1/5}=(\prod u_i)^{1/5}$. The normalization becomes
$$\sum_{i=1}^5 u_i^{25}=5,\qquad u_i>0,$$
and $(\star)$ becomes
$$\sum_{i=1}^5 u_i^{401}\;\ge\;5\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^5 u_i\Bigr)^{1/5}.\qquad(\star
\star)$$

**Two standard inequalities.**

1. *Power mean (or Jensen on $t\mapsto t^{401/25}$, convex).* Since $401>25$,
$$\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{5}\sum u_i^{401}\Bigr)^{1/401}\;\ge\;\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{5}\sum u_
i^{25}\Bigr)^{1/25}=1^{1/25}=1,$$
so $\sum u_i^{401}\ge 5$.

2. *AM–GM on $\{u_i^{25}\}$.* Since $\sum u_i^{25}=5$,
$$\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^5 u_i^{25}\Bigr)^{1/5}\le\frac{\sum u_i^{25}}{5}=1,$$
so $\prod u_i^{25}\le 1$, hence $\prod u_i\le 1$ and therefore $\bigl(\prod u_i\
bigr)^{1/5}\le 1$.

**Combining.** From (1) and (2),
$$\sum_{i=1}^5 u_i^{401}\;\ge\;5\;\ge\;5\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^5 u_i\Bigr)^{1/5},$$
which is exactly $(\star\star)$. Tracing back through the normalization and subs
titution gives the original inequality with $C=5^{15}$.

s=u_5$, and the normalization forces $u_i=1$, i.e. $x_i=1$. This is precisely wh
ere the lower bound $C\ge 5^{15}$ was attained, confirming sharpness.

Therefore the smallest real number $C$ is
