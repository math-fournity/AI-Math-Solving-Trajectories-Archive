## Proof

**Claim.** Every invertible real matrix $M$ admits a unique decomposition $M = O
P$ with $O$ orthogonal and $P$ symmetric positive-definite (the *polar decomposi
tion*).

---

### Existence

Let $M \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{R})$. Consider
$$S := M^{\mathsf{T}} M.$$

**$S$ is symmetric:** $S^{\mathsf{T}} = (M^{\mathsf{T}} M)^{\mathsf{T}} = M^{\ma
thsf{T}} (M^{\mathsf{T}})^{\mathsf{T}} = M^{\mathsf{T}} M = S$.

**$S$ is positive-definite:** For every nonzero $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$,
$$x^{\mathsf{T}} S x = x^{\mathsf{T}} M^{\mathsf{T}} M x = \|M x\|^2 > 0,$$
since $M$ is invertible, so $Mx \neq 0$ whenever $x \neq 0$.

Since $S$ is real symmetric, it is orthogonally diagonalizable: $S = Q \Lambda Q
^{\mathsf{T}}$ with $Q$ orthogonal and $\Lambda = \mathrm{diag}(\lambda_1, \dots
, \lambda_n)$, where every $\lambda_i > 0$ (by positive-definiteness). Define th
e **unique symmetric positive-definite square root**
$$P := S^{1/2} = Q\, \mathrm{diag}(\sqrt{\lambda_1}, \dots, \sqrt{\lambda_n})\,
Q^{\mathsf{T}}.$$
Then $P$ is symmetric (being $Q D Q^{\mathsf{T}}$ with $D$ diagonal) and positiv
e-definite (eigenvalues $\sqrt{\lambda_i} > 0$), and $P^2 = S = M^{\mathsf{T}} M
$.

Now set
$$O := M P^{-1}.$$
This is well-defined since $P$ is invertible. We verify $O$ is orthogonal:
$$O^{\mathsf{T}} O = (M P^{-1})^{\mathsf{T}} (M P^{-1}) = P^{-{\mathsf{T}}} M^{\
mathsf{T}} M\, P^{-1}.$$
Because $P$ is symmetric, $P^{-{\mathsf{T}}} = P^{-1}$. Using $M^{\mathsf{T}} M
= P^2$:
$$O^{\mathsf{T}} O = P^{-1} P^2 P^{-1} = P^{-1} P \cdot P P^{-1} = I.$$
Hence $O \in O(n)$, and $M = O P$ by construction. $\square$

---

### Uniqueness

Suppose $M = O_1 P_1 = O_2 P_2$ with each $O_i$ orthogonal and each $P_i$ symmet
ric positive-definite. Then
$$M^{\mathsf{T}} M = P_1^{\mathsf{T}} O_1^{\mathsf{T}} O_1 P_1 = P_1^2, \qquad M
^{\mathsf{T}} M = P_2^2.$$
So $P_1^2 = P_2^2$. A symmetric positive-definite matrix has a **unique** symmet
ric positive-definite square root (the eigenvalue-by-eigenvalue construction abo
ve is forced, since the $\sqrt{\lambda_i}$ are the unique positive roots). There
fore $P_1 = P_2$, and consequently
$$O_1 = M P_1^{-1} = M P_2^{-1} = O_2. \qquad \square$$

---

### Conclusion

Every invertible real matrix $M$ decomposes uniquely as $M = OP$, where $O$ is o
rthogonal and $P$ is symmetric positive-definite, with
$$P = (M^{\mathsf{T}} M)^{1/2}, \qquad O = M (M^{\mathsf{T}} M)^{-1/2}.$$
