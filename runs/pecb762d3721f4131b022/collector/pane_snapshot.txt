## Part (a): Green's function in the 60° wedge

**Setup.** The wedge is $W = \{(r,\theta): 0 < \theta < \pi/3,\; 0 < r < \infty\
}$ with Dirichlet data $G=0$ on both walls $\theta=0$ and $\theta=\pi/3$. The so
urce is at $x_0 = (r_0,\theta_0)$ with $0<\theta_0<\pi/3$. The free-space Green'
s function in 2D is
$$G_0(x,x_0) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|x - x_0|, \qquad \nabla^2 G_0 = \delta(x-x_0).$
$

**Image group.** Let $R_0$ denote reflection across $\theta=0$ (sending $\theta\
mapsto -\theta$) and $R_1$ reflection across $\theta=\pi/3$ (sending $\theta\map
sto 2\pi/3-\theta$). Their composition $R_1R_0$ is a rotation by $2\pi/3$. Since
 $(2\pi/3)\cdot 3 = 2\pi$, the group generated by $R_0,R_1$ is the dihedral grou
p $D_3$ of order 6. The orbit of $\theta_0$ consists of exactly six angles, whic
h we list with the parity (sign) of the group element — $+1$ for an even number
of reflections (rotations), $-1$ for an odd number:

| point | angle | sign |
|---|---|---|
| $p_1$ | $\theta_0$ | $+$ |
| $p_2$ | $-\theta_0$ | $-$ |
| $p_3$ | $2\pi/3 - \theta_0$ | $-$ |
| $p_4$ | $2\pi/3 + \theta_0$ | $+$ |
| $p_5$ | $4\pi/3 - \theta_0$ | $-$ |
| $p_6$ | $4\pi/3 + \theta_0$ | $+$ |

All six points have the same radial coordinate $r_0$.

**Image solution.** Define
$$\boxed{\;G(x,x_0) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{i=1}^{6} s_i\,\ln\!\bigl|x - p_i\bigr|
\;}$$
where $s_i = \pm 1$ is the sign in the table above, i.e.
$$G = \frac{1}{2\pi}\Big[\ln|x-p_1| - \ln|x-p_2| - \ln|x-p_3| + \ln|x-p_4| - \ln
|x-p_5| + \ln|x-p_6|\Big].$$

**Verification of the boundary conditions.**

*Wall $\theta=0$:* reflection across this wall sends $\phi\mapsto -\phi$. The si
x points pair up as mirror images with opposite signs:
- $p_1(\theta_0) \leftrightarrow p_2(-\theta_0)$, signs $(+,-)$;
- $p_3(2\pi/3-\theta_0) \leftrightarrow p_6(4\pi/3+\theta_0)$, since $-(2\pi/3-\
theta_0)\equiv 4\pi/3+\theta_0\pmod{2\pi}$, signs $(-,+)$;
- $p_5(4\pi/3-\theta_0) \leftrightarrow p_4(2\pi/3+\theta_0)$, since $-(4\pi/3-\
theta_0)\equiv 2\pi/3+\theta_0\pmod{2\pi}$, signs $(-,+)$.

Each pair contributes $\ln|x-p| - \ln|x-p'| = 0$ when $x$ lies on the mirror $\t
heta=0$ (the distances are equal). Hence $G=0$ on $\theta=0$.

*Wall $\theta=\pi/3$:* reflection across this wall sends $\phi\mapsto 2\pi/3-\ph
i$. The pairings are:
- $p_1(\theta_0)\leftrightarrow p_3(2\pi/3-\theta_0)$, signs $(+,-)$;
- $p_2(-\theta_0)\leftrightarrow p_4(2\pi/3+\theta_0)$, since $2\pi/3-(-\theta_0
)=2\pi/3+\theta_0$, signs $(-,+)$;
- $p_5(4\pi/3-\theta_0)\leftrightarrow p_6(4\pi/3+\theta_0)$, since $2\pi/3-(4\p
i/3-\theta_0)=-2\pi/3+\theta_0\equiv 4\pi/3+\theta_0$, signs $(-,+)$.

Again each pair cancels on the mirror, so $G=0$ on $\theta=\pi/3$.

**PDE.** Away from $x=p_1=x_0$ every term is harmonic, so $\nabla^2 G=0$ in $W\s
etminus\{x_0\}$. Near $x_0$ only the $p_1$ term is singular and contributes $\de
lta(x-x_0)$; all other image points lie outside $W$ (their angles are $-\theta_0
, 2\pi/3-\theta_0, \ldots$, none in $(0,\pi/3)$), so no spurious singularities a
ppear inside the wedge. Thus $\nabla^2 G = \delta(x-x_0)$ in $W$ with $G=0$ on $
\partial W$. $\square$

## Part (b): Which other wedge angles admit the method of images

Let the wedge angle be $\alpha$. The two wall reflections $R_0$ (across $\theta=
0$) and $R_1$ (across $\theta=\alpha$) generate a group whose rotation subgroup
is $\{R_1R_0\}^k$, a rotation by $2\alpha$ each time. The method of images produ
ces a **finite** set of image charges that tiles the plane without overlap preci
sely when this rotation has finite order, i.e.
$$2\alpha = \frac{2\pi}{n} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \alpha = \frac{\pi}{n},
 \qquad n = 1,2,3,\dots$$

When $\alpha=\pi/n$, the dihedral group $D_n$ has order $2n$, giving $2n-1$ imag
e charges (plus the original). The $2n$ copies of the wedge fill the plane exact
ly, every boundary point is shared by two wedges carrying opposite-sign charges,
 and the Dirichlet condition is satisfied automatically.

If $\alpha/\pi$ is irrational, the orbit of the source is infinite and dense — n
o finite image construction exists. If $\alpha/\pi = p/q$ is rational but not of
 the form $1/n$ (e.g. $\alpha=2\pi/5$), the rotation $(R_1R_0)^q$ is the identit
y but the $2q$ reflected copies of the wedge **overlap** (they cover the plane m
ore than once with conflicting signs), so the construction fails.

**Conclusion.** The method of images works for a wedge of angle $\alpha$ (with D
irichlet data on both walls) if and only if
$$\boxed{\;\alpha = \frac{\pi}{n}, \quad n\in\mathbb{Z}^+\;}$$
i.e. wedge angles $\pi, \pi/2, \pi/3, \pi/4, \pi/5, \ldots$ (180°, 90°, 60°, 45°
, 36°, …). The 60° wedge is the case $n=3$.

*(Remark: with mixed or Neumann conditions the same angle restriction $\alpha=\p
i/n$ applies; only the sign pattern of the images changes.)*
