No, in general $\|f\| \neq |\det(A)|$.

**Proof.**

Recall the standard operator norm of a linear map $f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^n
$ with matrix $A$ is

$$\|f\| = \sup_{x\neq 0}\frac{\|Ax\|}{\|x\|} = \sup_{\|x\|\le 1}\|Ax\| = \sigma_
{\max}(A),$$

the largest singular value of $A$. On the other hand, the determinant satisfies

$$|\det(A)| = \prod_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_i(A),$$

the product of all $n$ singular values.

These two quantities coincide only in very special cases (e.g. $n=1$, or when $A
$ is orthogonal up to a scalar so that all singular values are equal). In genera
l they differ.

**Counterexample.** Take $n=2$ and $A = 2I_2 = \begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&2\end{pmatr
ix}$, with $\Omega$ any bounded open subset of $\mathbb{R}^2$ (say the unit disk
). Then:

- $\|f\| = \sup_{\|x\|\le 1}\|2x\| = 2$.
- $|\det(A)| = |\det(2I_2)| = 4$.

So $\|f\| = 2 \neq 4 = |\det(A)|$.

More generally, for $A = cI_n$ with $c>0$ and $n\ge 2$: $\|f\| = c$ while $|\det
(A)| = c^n$, and these are equal only when $c=1$ or $c=0$ or $n=1$.

**Conceptual reason.** The operator norm $\|f\| = \sigma_{\max}(A)$ measures the
 *maximal directional stretching* factor of $f$, whereas $|\det(A)| = \prod_i \s
igma_i(A)$ measures the *volume scaling* factor (the product of all stretching f
actors along mutually orthogonal principal directions). The maximum of a set of
positive numbers and their product are fundamentally different quantities once $
n\ge 2$. The determinant captures global volume change; the norm captures the si
ngle worst-case distortion. They agree only in dimension $1$, where there is a s
ingle singular value so that "maximum" and "product" coincide.

Therefore $\|f\| = |\det(A)|$ is **false** in general.
