# Proof: A Principal Ideal Ring (PIR) has Krull dimension at most 1

**Interpretation.** "PIR" denotes a **Principal Ideal Ring**: a commutative ring
 with identity in which every ideal is principal. The qualifier "not necessarily
 an integral domain" is meaningful here, since a PIR that is a domain is a PID.
(A Prüfer *domain*, by contrast, is necessarily a domain and can have arbitrary
Krull dimension—e.g., valuation domains of rank > 1—so the question only makes s
ense for PIRs.)

---

**Step 1: A PIR is Noetherian.**

Every ideal of $R$ is principal, hence finitely generated. So $R$ is Noetherian.

**Step 2: Every prime ideal of $R$ has height at most 1.**

Let $P$ be any prime ideal of $R$. Since $R$ is a PIR, $P$ is principal: $P = (p
)$ for some $p \in R$. Since $P \neq R$, $p$ is not a unit, so $(p)$ is a proper
 ideal.

We claim $P$ is **minimal over** $(p)$. Indeed, $P = (p)$ is a prime ideal conta
ining $(p)$. If $Q$ is any prime ideal with $(p) \subseteq Q$, then $P = (p) \su
bseteq Q$, so $Q \supseteq P$. Therefore no prime ideal $Q$ satisfies $(p) \subs
eteq Q \subsetneq P$, which means $P$ is minimal over $(p)$.

By **Krull's Principal Ideal Theorem** (applicable since $R$ is Noetherian): if
$P$ is a prime ideal minimal over a principal ideal, then $\operatorname{ht}(P)
\leq 1$.

Therefore $\operatorname{ht}(P) \leq 1$ for every prime ideal $P$ of $R$.

**Step 3: The Krull dimension of $R$ is at most 1.**

The Krull dimension is:
$$\dim(R) = \sup\{\operatorname{ht}(P) : P \text{ is a prime ideal of } R\}.$$

Since $\operatorname{ht}(P) \leq 1$ for every prime $P$, we conclude $\dim(R) \l
eq 1$.

---

**Sharpness.** The bound is attained: $\mathbb{Z}$ is a PID (hence a PIR) with $
\dim(\mathbb{Z}) = 1$. A field (also a PIR) has dimension $0$. A PIR that is not
 a domain, such as $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, has dimension $0$ (it is Artinian).
 So PIRs have dimension $0$ or $1$, but never more. $\blacksquare$
