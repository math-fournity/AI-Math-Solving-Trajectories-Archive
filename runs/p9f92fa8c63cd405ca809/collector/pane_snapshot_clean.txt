**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.** The condition $\mathrm{d}\omega \wedge \omega = 0$ is precisely the F
robenius integrability condition for the codimension‑one distribution $D = \ker\
omega$ (note $\mathrm{d}\omega\wedge\omega = \omega\wedge\mathrm{d}\omega$ since
 $\mathrm{d}\omega$ is a 2‑form). Since $\omega$ is nowhere vanishing, $D$ is a
smooth hyperplane distribution on $M$.

By the Frobenius theorem, $D$ is integrable, so locally on a neighborhood $U$ of
 every point $p\in M$ there exist smooth functions $g$ and $f$ (with $f$ nowhere
 zero on $U$) such that
$$\omega\big|_U = f\,\mathrm{d}g.$$

Computing the exterior derivative:
$$\mathrm{d}\omega = \mathrm{d}(f\,\mathrm{d}g) = \mathrm{d}f\wedge\mathrm{d}g.$
$

Since $\omega = f\,\mathrm{d}g$, we have $\mathrm{d}g = \omega/f$, hence
$$\mathrm{d}\omega = \mathrm{d}f\wedge\frac{\omega}{f} = \frac{\mathrm{d}f}{f}\w
edge\omega = \mathrm{d}(\log|f|)\wedge\omega.$$

Set $\alpha := \mathrm{d}(\log|f|)$, which is a smooth 1‑form on $U$ (since $f$
is smooth and nowhere vanishing). Then
$$\mathrm{d}\omega = \alpha\wedge\omega.$$

The requested representation $\mathrm{d}\omega = \sum_i a_i\wedge\omega$ is achi
eved with a single term $a_1 = \alpha$. (Locally; patching over all of $M$ is un
necessary since the question asks for existence of such 1‑forms, and the local c
onstruction at each point suffices — or one may take the sum over a locally fini
te cover.)

Therefore $\mathrm{d}\omega\wedge\omega = 0$ does imply $\mathrm{d}\omega = \sum
_i a_i\wedge\omega$ for some 1‑forms $a_i$. $\blacksquare$
