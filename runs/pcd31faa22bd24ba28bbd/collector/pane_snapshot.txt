## Proof

Let $\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_n \geq 0$ be the eigenvalues of $A$. Since $A$ i
s symmetric positive semi-definite, it is orthogonally diagonalizable, and $(A+I
)^{-1}$ has eigenvalues $\frac{1}{1+\lambda_i}$. Hence

$$\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(\left((A+I)^{-1}\right)^2\right) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \fr
ac{1}{(1+\lambda_i)^2}.$$

The constraint $\operatorname{Tr}(A) \leq n$ becomes $\sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i \le
q n$.

**Step 1: Use the full trace budget.** The function $g(\lambda) = \frac{1}{(1+\l
ambda)^2}$ is strictly decreasing on $[0,\infty)$. Therefore, to minimize $\sum
g(\lambda_i)$, we should make the $\lambda_i$ as large as possible, i.e. we use
$\sum \lambda_i = n$ (any leftover budget only increases the sum). So the minimu
m is attained on the hyperplane $\sum \lambda_i = n$.

**Step 2: Convexity and Jensen.** The function $g(\lambda) = (1+\lambda)^{-2}$ h
as second derivative $g''(\lambda) = 6(1+\lambda)^{-4} > 0$ on $[0,\infty)$, so
$g$ is strictly convex. By Jensen's inequality, for $\lambda_i \geq 0$ with $\su
m \lambda_i = n$ (so the average is $\bar\lambda = 1$):

$$\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n g(\lambda_i) \;\geq\; g\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \lambda_i\right) = g(1) = \frac{1}{4}.$$

Multiplying by $n$:

$$\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{1}{(1+\lambda_i)^2} \;\geq\; \frac{n}{4}.$$

**Step 3: Conclusion.** Since $n \geq 1$, we have $\frac{n}{4} \geq \frac{1}{4}$
, and therefore

$$\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(\left((A+I)^{-1}\right)^2\right) \;\geq\; \frac{n}{4}
 \;\geq\; \frac{1}{4}.$$

**Sharpness.** The bound $\frac{n}{4}$ is attained when $\lambda_1 = \cdots = \l
ambda_n = 1$, i.e. $A = I$, which satisfies $\operatorname{Tr}(A) = n \leq n$. I
n that case $\operatorname{Tr}(((A+I)^{-1})^2) = n \cdot \frac{1}{4} = \frac{n}{
4}$.

Thus the answer is **yes**: $\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(\left((A+I)^{-1}\right)^2\
right) \geq \dfrac{1}{4}$, and the sharp lower bound is in fact $\dfrac{n}{4}$.
