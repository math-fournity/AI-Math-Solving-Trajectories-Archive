## Answer

**No**, unimodularity does not follow. Here is a counterexample.

Let $G = \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{Z}$ (product group, product topology) and $H
= \mathbb{R} \rtimes_{\phi} \mathbb{Z}$, where $\phi:\mathbb{Z}\to\operatorname{
Aut}(\mathbb{R})$ is $\phi(n)(x)=e^{an}x$ for some fixed $a\neq 0$. The multipli
cation in $H$ is
$$(x,n)\cdot(y,m)=(x+e^{an}y,\;n+m).$$
Both are locally compact groups ($\mathbb{Z}$ discrete).

**$G$ is unimodular.** $G$ is abelian, so its left Haar measure (Lebesgue $\time
s$ counting) is right-invariant: $\Delta_G\equiv 1$.

**$H$ is not unimodular.** The left Haar measure on $H$ is $\mu = c\sum_{n\in\ma
thbb{Z}} e^{-an}\,\lambda_n$ (where $\lambda_n$ is Lebesgue measure on fiber $\m
athbb{R}\times\{n\}$). One checks left-invariance: left translation by $(x_0,n_0
)$ sends fiber $m$ to fiber $n_0+m$ and scales Lebesgue measure by $e^{an_0}$, e
xactly compensated by the weight ratio $e^{-a(n_0+m)}/e^{-am}=e^{-an_0}$.

Right translation by $(x_0,n_0)$ sends $(y,m)\mapsto(y+e^{am}x_0,\,m+n_0)$, mapp
ing fiber $m$ to fiber $m+n_0$ while *preserving* Lebesgue measure (it's a trans
lation on each fiber). Hence
$$\mu(E\cdot(x_0,n_0))=c\sum_m e^{-am}\lambda_{m+n_0}(E\cap\text{fiber}_{m+n_0})
=e^{an_0}\,\mu(E).$$
Since $\mu(E\cdot g)=\Delta_H(g)^{-1}\mu(E)$, we get $\Delta_H(x_0,n_0)=e^{-an_0
}$, which is not identically $1$ for $a\neq 0$. So $H$ is **not** unimodular.

**$G$ and $H$ are locally isomorphic.** Both contain the open subgroup $\mathbb{
R}\times\{0\}\cong\mathbb{R}$. On this subgroup the operations coincide in both
groups:
$$(x,0)\cdot(y,0)=(x+e^{a\cdot 0}y,0)=(x+y,0),$$
and $(x,0)^{-1}=(-x,0)$ in both. Taking $V_G=V_H=(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\time
s\{0\}$ and $f(x,0)=(x,0)$ gives a local group isomorphism between neighborhoods
 of the identity.

**Conclusion.** $G$ is unimodular and locally isomorphic to $H$, yet $H$ is not
unimodular. The reason is that the modular function $\Delta:G\to\mathbb{R}^+$ is
 a continuous homomorphism determined only on the open subgroup $\langle V\rangl
e$ generated by a neighborhood of the identity. When this subgroup is proper (th
e group is disconnected), $\Delta$ can be nontrivial on the remaining cosets—exa
ctly what happens here, where $\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}$ is unimodular in both grou
ps but $H$ has nontrivial $\Delta$ on other cosets.
