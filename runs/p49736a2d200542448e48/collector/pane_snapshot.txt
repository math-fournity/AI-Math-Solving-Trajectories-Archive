**Answer: Yes.** A self-adjoint operator with bounded spectrum is necessarily bo
unded.

**Proof.**

Let $T$ be a self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space $H$, and suppose its spect
rum $\sigma(T)$ is bounded. We use the spectral theorem for (possibly unbounded)
 self-adjoint operators.

By the spectral theorem, there is a unique projection-valued measure $E$ on the
Borel $\sigma$-algebra of $\mathbb{R}$ such that
$$
T = \int_{\mathbb{R}} \lambda \, dE(\lambda),
$$
with domain
$$
D(T) = \Bigl\{x \in H : \int_{\mathbb{R}} \lambda^2 \, d\langle E(\lambda)x, x\r
angle < \infty\Bigr\},
$$
and for $x \in D(T)$,
$$
\|Tx\|^2 = \int_{\mathbb{R}} \lambda^2 \, d\langle E(\lambda)x, x\rangle.
$$
The spectrum satisfies $\sigma(T) = \operatorname{supp}(E)$, the support of the
spectral measure: the smallest closed set $S$ with $E(\mathbb{R} \setminus S) =
0$.

Since $\sigma(T)$ is bounded, there exists $M \geq 0$ with $\sigma(T) \subseteq
[-M, M]$. Hence $E(\mathbb{R} \setminus [-M,M]) = 0$, so the spectral measure is
 supported inside $[-M, M]$.

For **every** $x \in H$ (not just $x \in D(T)$),
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}} \lambda^2 \, d\langle E(\lambda)x, x\rangle
= \int_{[-M,M]} \lambda^2 \, d\langle E(\lambda)x, x\rangle
\leq M^2 \int_{[-M,M]} d\langle E(\lambda)x, x\rangle
= M^2 \langle E(\mathbb{R})x, x\rangle
= M^2 \|x\|^2 < \infty.
$$
Therefore $D(T) = H$: the operator is everywhere-defined. Moreover, for every $x
 \in H$,
$$
\|Tx\|^2 = \int_{\mathbb{R}} \lambda^2 \, d\langle E(\lambda)x, x\rangle \leq M^
2 \|x\|^2,
$$
so
$$
\|T\| = \sup_{\|x\|=1} \|Tx\| \leq M < \infty.
$$

Thus $T$ is a bounded operator (in fact $\|T\| = \sup\{|\lambda| : \lambda \in \
sigma(T)\}$, since the reverse inequality $\|T\| \geq \sup|\sigma(T)|$ always ho
lds for self-adjoint $T$).

**Remark (converse).** The converse also holds: an unbounded self-adjoint operat
or necessarily has unbounded spectrum. Indeed, if $\sigma(T) \subseteq [-M,M]$,
the argument above forces $\|T\| \leq M$. So for self-adjoint operators, *bounde
dness of the operator* and *boundedness of the spectrum* are equivalent. The key
 ingredient that makes this work is self-adjointness (more generally, normality)
: it is the spectral theorem that ties the operator norm to the spectral radius
via $\|T\| = r(T) = \sup|\sigma(T)|$. Without self-adjointness the implication f
ails — a non-normal operator can have bounded (even point) spectrum yet be unbou
nded.
