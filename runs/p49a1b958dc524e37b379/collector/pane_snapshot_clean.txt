## Setup and Interpretation

A **ticket** is a permutation $\pi$ of $\{1, 2, \ldots, 50\}$, placed in 50 cell
s (cell $i$ contains $\pi(i)$). The **winning permutation** $\sigma$ is also a p
ermutation of $\{1,\ldots,50\}$. A cell $i$ of ticket $\pi$ **matches** if $\pi(
i) = \sigma(i)$.

We seek the minimum number of tickets such that for **every** winning permutatio
n $\sigma$, at least one ticket $\pi$ in our collection satisfies $\pi(i) = \sig
ma(i)$ for some position $i$.

## Reformulation as Edge Covering in $K_{50,50}$

Model the problem on the complete bipartite graph $K_{50,50}$ with left vertices
 = positions $\{1,\ldots,50\}$ and right vertices = values $\{1,\ldots,50\}$. An
 edge $(i, j)$ represents "position $i$ holds value $j$."

- A permutation $\pi$ corresponds to a **perfect matching** (one edge per row, o
ne per column).
- The winning permutation $\sigma$ is also a perfect matching.
- Ticket $\pi$ matches $\sigma$ iff their perfect matchings **share at least one
 edge**.

**Goal:** Find the minimum set $S$ of perfect matchings such that every perfect
matching of $K_{50,50}$ shares at least one edge with some matching in $S$.

## Upper Bound: 50 Tickets Suffice

$K_{50,50}$ admits a **1-factorization** into 50 edge-disjoint perfect matchings
. Concretely, label both sides $0, 1, \ldots, 49$; the $k$-th matching ($k = 0,
\ldots, 49$) connects left $i$ to right $(i+k) \bmod 50$. These 50 matchings par
tition all $50 \times 50 = 2500$ edges.

Take these 50 matchings as our tickets. Since they cover **every edge** of $K_{5
0,50}$, any winning permutation $\sigma$ (which uses 50 edges) must use at least
 one covered edge. That edge belongs to some ticket $\pi_k$, so $\pi_k$ and $\si
gma$ agree at the corresponding position. **At least one cell matches.**

## Lower Bound: 49 Tickets Are Not Enough

Suppose we use $k \leq 49$ tickets (perfect matchings). Remove their edges from
$K_{50,50}$. Each perfect matching removes exactly one edge incident to each ver
tex, so after removing $k$ matchings, **every vertex has degree exactly $50 - k
\geq 1$**.

The remaining graph $G$ is $(50-k)$-**regular** and bipartite. I claim $G$ alway
s contains a perfect matching. By **Hall's Marriage Theorem**, it suffices to sh
ow: for every subset $S$ of left vertices, $|N(S)| \geq |S|$.

The number of edges leaving $S$ is $(50-k)|S|$. Each vertex in $N(S)$ has degree
 at most $50-k$ in $G$, so the number of edges entering $N(S)$ is at most $(50-k
)|N(S)|$. Therefore:
$$(50-k)|N(S)| \geq (50-k)|S| \implies |N(S)| \geq |S|.$$

Hall's condition holds, so $G$ has a **perfect matching** $\sigma^*$. This $\sig
ma^*$ uses only uncovered edges, meaning it shares no edge with any of our $k$ t
ickets — **no cell matches**.

In particular, when $k = 49$, each vertex has degree exactly 1, so $G$ is a 1-re
gular bipartite graph, which is itself a perfect matching — a winning permutatio
n that evades all 49 tickets.

## Conclusion

- **49 or fewer tickets** always leave a $(50-k)$-regular bipartite graph, which

- **50 tickets** via a 1-factorization cover every edge, forcing every winning p
ermutation to share at least one edge (cell match) with some ticket.
