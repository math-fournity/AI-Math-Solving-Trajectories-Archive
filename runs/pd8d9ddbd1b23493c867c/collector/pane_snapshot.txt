**Answer: No.** The union need not be uncountable.

**Counterexample.** Define, for each real number $t$,
$$E_t \;=\; \{\,q \in \mathbb{Q} : q < t\,\},$$
the set of all rationals strictly less than $t$. Each $E_t$ is a subset of $\mat
hbb{R}$.

**The chain is strictly increasing.** Let $s < r$. Clearly $E_s \subseteq E_r$,
since any rational $q < s$ also satisfies $q < r$. To see the inclusion is *prop
er*, use the density of $\mathbb{Q}$ in $\mathbb{R}$: there exists a rational $q
$ with $s < q < r$. Then $q \in E_r$ (as $q < r$) but $q \notin E_s$ (as $q \geq
 s$ is false only if... here $q > s$, so $q \not< s$, hence $q \notin E_s$). Thu
s $E_s \subsetneq E_r$.

**The union is countable.** Every $E_t$ is contained in $\mathbb{Q}$, so
$$\bigcup_{t \in \mathbb{R}} E_t \;\subseteq\; \mathbb{Q}.$$
Conversely, every rational $q$ belongs to $E_t$ for any $t > q$ (e.g. $t = q+1$)
, so $\mathbb{Q} \subseteq \bigcup_{t} E_t$. Hence
$$\bigcup_{t \in \mathbb{R}} E_t \;=\; \mathbb{Q},$$
which is countable.

Therefore the union of such a strictly increasing $\mathbb{R}$-indexed chain of
subsets of $\mathbb{R}$ need **not** be uncountable.
