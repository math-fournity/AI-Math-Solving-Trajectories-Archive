## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $I = J$.

**Key lemma.** For an $R$-module $M$, $M = 0$ if and only if $M_P = 0$ for every
 prime ideal $P$ of $R$.

*Proof of lemma.* The forward direction is immediate (localizing the zero module
 gives zero). For the converse, suppose $M \neq 0$ and pick $0 \neq x \in M$. It
s annihilator $\operatorname{ann}(x) = \{r \in R : rx = 0\}$ is a proper ideal o
f $R$ (since $1 \notin \operatorname{ann}(x)$). By Zorn's lemma, $\operatorname{
ann}(x)$ is contained in some maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$, which is in particul
ar prime. We claim $x/1 \neq 0$ in $M_{\mathfrak{m}}$. Indeed, $x/1 = 0$ in $M_{
\mathfrak{m}}$ would mean there exists $s \in R \setminus \mathfrak{m}$ with $sx
 = 0$, i.e. $s \in \operatorname{ann}(x) \subseteq \mathfrak{m}$—contradicting $
s \notin \mathfrak{m}$. Hence $M_{\mathfrak{m}} \neq 0$, so $M_P$ is not zero fo
r every prime $P$. $\square$

**Main proof.** Consider the quotient module
$$M := (I + J)/I.$$
This is an $R$-module. For any prime ideal $P$, localization is exact, so
$$M_P \cong (I_P + J_P)/I_P.$$
By hypothesis $I_P = J_P$ for all primes $P$, so $I_P + J_P = I_P$ and thus $M_P
 = 0$ for every prime $P$.

Applying the lemma, $M = 0$, i.e. $(I + J)/I = 0$, which means $I + J = I$, equi
valently $J \subseteq I$.

Repeating the symmetric argument with $M' := (I + J)/J$ gives $I \subseteq J$. T
herefore $I = J$. $\blacksquare$
