## Solution

**Claim:** The statement is **not** true in general. It holds if and only if $F_
1$ and $F_2$ are **closed** subspaces.

### Key fact

For any subspace $F$ of a Hilbert space $H$, the orthogonal complement $F^\bot$
is always a **closed** subspace, and

$$
(F^\bot)^\bot = \overline{F},
$$

the closure of $F$. In particular, $(F^\bot)^\bot = F$ if and only if $F$ is clo
sed.

### Proof that $F_1^\bot = F_2^\bot \implies F_1 = F_2$ when both are closed

Suppose $F_1, F_2$ are closed subspaces and $F_1^\bot = F_2^\bot$. Then

$$
F_1 = (F_1^\bot)^\bot = (F_2^\bot)^\bot = F_2,
$$

where the middle equality uses the hypothesis and the outer equalities use the f
act that closed subspaces satisfy $(F^\bot)^\bot = F$. ∎

### Counterexample showing the statement fails for non-closed subspaces

Let $H = \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$, the space of square-summable sequences. Define

- $F_2 = \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$ (the whole space, which is closed),
- $F_1 = c_{00}$, the subspace of sequences with only finitely many nonzero term
s.

**$F_1$ is a proper, non-closed, dense subspace of $\ell^2$.** It is proper sinc
e, e.g., $(1/n)_{n\geq 1} \in \ell^2 \setminus c_{00}$. It is dense because ever
y $\ell^2$ sequence is the $\ell^2$-limit of its truncations, which lie in $c_{0
0}$.

**Compute $F_1^\bot$:** Suppose $y = (y_n) \in \ell^2$ satisfies $\langle y, x\r
angle = 0$ for every $x \in c_{00}$. Taking $x = e_k$ (the $k$-th standard basis
 vector, which is in $c_{00}$) gives $y_k = \langle y, e_k\rangle = 0$ for every
 $k$. Hence $y = 0$, so

$$
F_1^\bot = \{0\}.
$$

**Compute $F_2^\bot$:** Since $F_2 = H$,

$$
F_2^\bot = H^\bot = \{0\}.
$$

**Therefore $F_1^\bot = F_2^\bot = \{0\}$, yet $F_1 \neq F_2$** (as $c_{00}$ is
a proper subspace of $\ell^2$). ∎

### Conclusion

The implication $F_1^\bot = F_2^\bot \Rightarrow F_1 = F_2$ is **false** in gene
ral. The equality of orthogonal complements only forces $\overline{F_1} = \overl
ine{F_2}$ (the closures coincide). The implication is valid precisely when both
subspaces are **closed**.
