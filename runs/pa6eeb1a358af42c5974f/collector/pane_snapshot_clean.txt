## Proof

**Claim:** The equality holds for every proper divisor $k$ of $|G|$.

Let $k$ be a proper divisor of $|G|$, so $1 \le k < |G|$.

**Inclusion $\subseteq$:** Let $H \le G$ with $|H| = k$. Since $k < |G|$, we hav
e $|H| < |G|$, so $H$ is a **proper** subgroup of $G$. By hypothesis, every prop
er subgroup of $G$ is cyclic, so $H = \langle h \rangle$ for some $h \in H$. Sin
ce $|H| = |\langle h \rangle| = |h| = k$, we have $h \in G$ with $|h| = k$, and
thus $H = \langle h \rangle \in \{\langle g \rangle \mid g \in G,\ |g| = k\}$.

**Inclusion $\supseteq$:** Let $g \in G$ with $|g| = k$. Then $\langle g \rangle
$ is a subgroup of $G$ with $|\langle g \rangle| = |g| = k$, so $\langle g \rang
le \in \{H \mid H \le G,\ |H| = k\}$.

Both inclusions hold, so for every proper divisor $k$ of $|G|$:
$$\{H \mid H \le G,\ |H| = k\} = \{\langle g \rangle \mid g \in G,\ |g| = k\}.$$

**Remark on the role of the hypothesis.** The forward inclusion is exactly where
 the hypothesis is used: any subgroup whose order is a *proper* divisor of $|G|$
 is automatically a proper subgroup, hence cyclic by assumption, hence generated
 by one of its elements of order $k$. The reverse inclusion requires no hypothes
is beyond the definition of $\langle g \rangle$. Note that the hypothesis "every
 proper subgroup is cyclic" is essential — without it, a subgroup of order $k$ c
ould be non-cyclic (e.g., $V_4 \le S_4$ of order 4), and no element of order 4 w
ould generate it.
