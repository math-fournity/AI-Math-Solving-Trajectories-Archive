## Proof

**Noting an inconsistency in the statement.** The problem says "$Z$ is the plane
 curve defined by $y = x^2$" yet writes $A(Z) = k[x,y]/(xy-1)$. These are incons
istent: the curve $y = x^2$ would give $k[x,y]/(y - x^2)$, not $k[x,y]/(xy-1)$.
However, the ring is stated *explicitly and unambiguously* as $k[x,y]/(xy-1)$, s
o we compute the dimension of that ring. (As shown at the end, the same argument
 with $y - x^2$ in place of $xy - 1$ gives the same answer, so the conclusion is
 robust to the inconsistency.)

---

**Goal.** Compute $\dim A := \sup\{\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak p : \mathfrak p \
in \operatorname{Spec} A\}$ for $A = k[x,y]/(xy-1)$, with $k$ algebraically clos
ed.

---

**Step 1. $(xy-1)$ is a prime ideal of $k[x,y]$.**

$k[x,y]$ is a UFD. View $xy - 1$ as a polynomial in $y$ over $k[x]$:
$$xy - 1 \in k[x][y].$$
It is linear in $y$ with leading coefficient $x \in k[x]^{\times}$ only up to no
n-units; more directly, suppose $xy - 1 = f \cdot g$ in $k[x,y]$. Treating both
sides as polynomials in $y$ over $k[x]$, the total degree in $y$ is $1$, so one
of $f, g$ has $y$-degree $0$, i.e. lies in $k[x]$. Say $f \in k[x]$. Then $f$ di
vides every coefficient of $xy - 1$ in $k[x]$, namely $f \mid x$ and $f \mid (-1
)$. From $f \mid (-1)$ we get $f \in k^{\times}$, a unit. Hence $xy - 1$ is irre
ducible in the UFD $k[x,y]$, therefore prime, so $(xy-1) \in \operatorname{Spec}
 k[x,y]$.

Consequently $A = k[x,y]/(xy-1)$ is an integral domain, and $(0) \in \operatorna
me{Spec} A$.

---

**Step 2. Height of $(xy-1)$ in $k[x,y]$.**

$k[x,y]$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra and a domain, so $\dim k[x,y] = 2$.
 By Krull's Principal Ideal Theorem, every minimal prime over a principal ideal
generated by a non-unit has height $\le 1$. Since $(xy-1)$ is itself prime and n
on-zero (so not the zero ideal, and $xy-1$ is a non-unit), it is the unique mini
mal prime over itself, giving $\operatorname{ht}(xy-1) \le 1$. It is not $0$ (as
 $xy - 1 \ne 0$ and $k[x,y]$ is a domain, so $(xy-1) \ne (0)$), hence $\operator
name{ht}(xy-1) = 1$.

---

**Step 3. Transfer heights to the quotient.**

$k[x,y]$ is a finitely generated algebra over a field, hence *catenary* (and eve
n universally catenary). For a catenary domain $R$ and a prime $\mathfrak q \sub
set R$, the height of $\mathfrak q/\mathfrak q$-cosets behaves as follows: under
 the order-preserving bijection
$$\{\mathfrak p \in \operatorname{Spec} k[x,y] : (xy-1) \subseteq \mathfrak p\}
\;\longleftrightarrow\; \operatorname{Spec} A, \qquad \mathfrak p \mapsto \mathf
rak p/(xy-1),$$
the height of $\mathfrak p/(xy-1)$ in $A$ equals $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)
 - \operatorname{ht}(xy-1) = \operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p) - 1$ (catenarity giv
es that every saturated chain from $(xy-1)$ to $\mathfrak p$ has the same length
 $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p) - \operatorname{ht}(xy-1)$, and removing the bo
ttom link yields a chain of that length from $(0)$ to $\mathfrak p/(xy-1)$ in $A
$).

---

**Step 4. Maximal primes of $A$.**

Because $k$ is algebraically closed, the maximal ideals of $k[x,y]$ are exactly
$(x - a,\, y - b)$ for $(a,b) \in k^2$ (Nullstellensatz). Such a maximal ideal c
ontains $(xy-1)$ iff $ab - 1 = 0$, i.e. $b = 1/a$ with $a \ne 0$. Each such maxi
mal ideal has height $2$ in $k[x,y]$ (it is maximal in a ring of dimension $2$).
 Hence the corresponding maximal ideals of $A$ have height
$$\operatorname{ht}\bigl((x-a,\,y-b)/(xy-1)\bigr) = 2 - 1 = 1.$$
These exist (e.g. $a = 1, b = 1$), so $A$ has prime ideals of height $1$.

The unique minimal prime of $A$ is $(0)$, of height $0$. There are no primes of
height $\ge 2$: any such prime would correspond to a prime $\mathfrak p \supsetn
eq (xy-1)$ in $k[x,y]$ with $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p) \ge 3$, impossible s
ince $\dim k[x,y] = 2$.

---

**Step 5. Supremum.**

$$\dim A \;=\; \sup_{\mathfrak q \in \operatorname{Spec} A} \operatorname{ht}(\m
athfrak q) \;=\; \sup\{0,\, 1\} \;=\; 1.$$

**Robustness to the statement's inconsistency.** Had the intended ring been $k[x

prime in the UFD $k[x,y]$, of height $1$ by the same Principal Ideal Theorem arg
