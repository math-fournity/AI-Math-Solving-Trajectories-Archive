## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. The continuous path $\gamma$ from $A$ to $B$ in $GL_n(\mathbb{R}
)$ can be chosen to be piecewise linear.

**Step 1: Both $A$ and $B$ lie in the identity component $GL_n^+(\mathbb{R})$.**

The determinant of $A$ equals the product of its eigenvalues (counted with algeb
raic multiplicity). Complex eigenvalues of a real matrix come in conjugate pairs
 $\lambda, \bar{\lambda}$, and $\lambda\bar{\lambda} = |\lambda|^2 > 0$. Real ei
genvalues of $A$ have positive real parts, hence are positive. Therefore every f
actor in the product is positive, giving $\det(A) > 0$.

Since $B = S^{-1}AS$, we have $\det(B) = \det(A) > 0$. Thus both $A, B \in GL_n^
+(\mathbb{R}) := \{M \in GL_n(\mathbb{R}) : \det(M) > 0\}$, the identity compone
nt of $GL_n(\mathbb{R})$.

**Step 2: $GL_n^+(\mathbb{R})$ is a connected open subset of $\mathbb{R}^{n^2}$.
**

Identifying $M_n(\mathbb{R}) \cong \mathbb{R}^{n^2}$, the set $GL_n^+(\mathbb{R}
)$ is open (the determinant is continuous and $(0,\infty)$ is open) and connecte
d (it is the identity component of $GL_n(\mathbb{R})$, which is a Lie group; equ
ivalently, every element of $GL_n^+(\mathbb{R})$ connects to $I$ via a continuou
s path).

**Step 3: Any two points in a connected open subset of $\mathbb{R}^m$ can be joi
ned by a piecewise linear path staying in the subset.**

Let $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$ be open and connected, and let $x, y \in U$. Sinc
e $U$ is open and connected in $\mathbb{R}^m$, it is path-connected, so there ex
ists a continuous path $\alpha:[0,1]\to U$ with $\alpha(0)=x$, $\alpha(1)=y$. Th
e image $K = \alpha([0,1])$ is compact. For each $p \in K$, openness of $U$ give
s an open ball $B_p \subseteq U$ with $p \in B_p$. By compactness, finitely many
 such balls $B_1, \dots, B_N$ cover $K$. By refining the cover along the path, w
e can find a partition $0 = t_0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_N = 1$ such that $\alpha([t_{
i-1}, t_i]) \subseteq B_i$ for each $i$. Set $p_i = \alpha(t_i)$, so $p_0 = x$ a
nd $p_N = y$. Since each $B_i$ is convex and contains both $p_{i-1}$ and $p_i$,
the straight line segment $\{(1-s)p_{i-1} + s\,p_i : s \in [0,1]\}$ lies entirel
y in $B_i \subseteq U$. Concatenating these segments yields a piecewise linear p
ath from $x$ to $y$ in $U$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Applying Step 3 to $U = GL_n^+(\mathbb{R}) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{n^2}$ with $x =
 A$ and $y = B$ (both in $U$ by Step 1, $U$ connected open by Step 2), we obtain
 a piecewise linear path $\gamma:[0,1]\to GL_n^+(\mathbb{R}) \subset GL_n(\mathb
b{R})$ with $\gamma(0) = A$ and $\gamma(1) = B$. $\blacksquare$
