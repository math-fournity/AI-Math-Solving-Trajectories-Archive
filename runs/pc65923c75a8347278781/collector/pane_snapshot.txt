Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $MK = 0$ when $K$ is a nil
ideal and $M$ is a minimal right ideal of a ring $R$ (possibly without identity,
 possibly non-commutative).

**Answer: Yes, $MK = 0$.**

**Proof.**

**Step 1: $MK$ is a right ideal contained in $M$.**

Since $M$ is a right ideal and $K \subseteq R$, we have $MK \subseteq MR \subset
eq M$. To see that $MK$ is itself a right ideal: it is clearly an additive subgr
oup, and for any $r \in R$,
$$(MK)R = M(KR) \subseteq MK,$$
since $K$ is a right ideal (so $KR \subseteq K$). Thus $MK$ is a right ideal of
$R$ contained in $M$.

**Step 2: By minimality, $MK = 0$ or $MK = M$.**

Since $M$ is a minimal right ideal and $MK \subseteq M$ is a right ideal, we mus
t have $MK = 0$ or $MK = M$.

**Step 3: Suppose for contradiction that $MK = M$.**

**Step 4: For any $m \in M$, the set $mK$ is a right ideal contained in $M$.**

Indeed, $mK = \{mk : k \in K\}$ is an additive subgroup (since $m(k_1+k_2) = mk_
1 + mk_2$ and $m(-k) = -mk$), and for any $r \in R$:
$$(mk)r = m(kr) \in mK,$$
since $kr \in K$ ($K$ is a right ideal). Also $mK \subseteq MR \subseteq M$.

**Step 5: There exists a nonzero $m \in M$ with $mK = M$.**

If $mK = 0$ for every $m \in M$, then $MK = 0$, contradicting $MK = M \neq 0$. S
o there exists $m \in M$ with $mK \neq 0$; such $m$ is necessarily nonzero. By m
inimality of $M$, the nonzero right ideal $mK \subseteq M$ must equal $M$.

**Step 6: Derive the contradiction.**

Since $mK = M$ and $m \in M$, there exists $k \in K$ such that
$$m = mk.$$
Iterating this relation:
$$m = mk = (mk)k = mk^2 = \cdots = mk^n \quad \text{for all } n \geq 1.$$
But $k \in K$ and $K$ is a nil ideal, so $k$ is nilpotent: there exists $n \geq
1$ with $k^n = 0$. Therefore
$$m = mk^n = m \cdot 0 = 0,$$
contradicting $m \neq 0$.

**Conclusion.** The assumption $MK = M$ is impossible, so $MK = 0$. $\blacksquar
e$

**Remark.** The argument uses only that $K$ is a nil right ideal (the left ideal
 property is not needed), and does not require $R$ to have an identity or be com
mutative.
