Looking at this problem, I need to model the ant's journey on a flipping die as
a Markov decision process where the ant chooses edges optimally and the die flip
s randomly.

## Setup

Label the cube's vertices by coordinates $(\pm1,\pm1,\pm1)$. The ant starts at $
v_1=(1,1,1)$ on the top face $T$ ($z=1$). The target is the body-diagonal opposi
te vertex $v_7=(-1,-1,-1)$ (farthest vertex, distance $\sqrt{3}$).

**Each second:** the ant moves along an edge to an adjacent vertex on the curren
t top face (choosing optimally), then the die flips to one of 4 adjacent faces (
each with probability $1/4$). If the ant's vertex lies on the new bottom face, i
t is crushed. Reaching $v_7$ counts as success even if subsequently crushed.

**State:** $(v, f)$ = (ant's vertex, top face), where $v$ must lie on face $f$.
Every cube vertex lies on either the top or bottom face, so the ant is always on
 the top face or crushed.

## Symmetry Reduction

The $S_3$ symmetry (permuting coordinates $x,y,z$) fixes $v_1$ and $v_7$, partit
ioning the 24 valid states into 6 orbits:

| Orbit | Representative | States | Description |
|-------|---------------|--------|-------------|
| $\alpha$ | $(v_1, T)$ | 3 | Start: ant at $v_1$, top face contains $v_1$ |
| $\beta$ | $(v_2, T)$ | 6 | Ant at distance-1 vertex, positive face |
| $\gamma$ | $(v_2, F)$ | 3 | Ant at distance-1 vertex, negative face |
| $\delta$ | $(v_3, T)$ | 3 | Ant at distance-2 vertex, positive face |
| $\varepsilon$ | $(v_3, F)$ | 6 | Ant at distance-2 vertex, negative face |
| target | $(v_7, B)$ | 3 | $p=1$ |

**Key observation for $\varepsilon$:** In every state in orbit $\varepsilon$, th
e ant is adjacent to $v_7$ on the top face. For example, at $(v_3, F)$: face $F$
 ($y=-1$) contains $v_2, v_3, v_6, v_7$ in a square, and $v_3$ is adjacent to $v
_7$. So the ant moves to $v_7$ and succeeds immediately: $p(\varepsilon) = 1$.

## Transition Equations

Computing transitions for each orbit (ant moves, then die flips to each of 4 adj
acent faces with prob $1/4$; crushed if on new bottom face):

**Orbit $\alpha$** (rep. $(v_1,T)$): Ant moves to $v_2$ or $v_4$ (both in $\beta
$). Either choice: 2 of 4 flips crush, 1 leads to $\gamma$, 1 leads to $\beta$.
$$p(\alpha) = \tfrac{1}{4}[p(\beta) + p(\gamma)]$$

**Orbit $\beta$** (rep. $(v_2,T)$): Ant moves to $v_1$ (orbit $\alpha$) or $v_3$
 (orbit $\varepsilon$).
- Move to $v_1$: 2 flips crush, 2 lead to $\alpha$ → $\frac{1}{2}p(\alpha)$
- Move to $v_3$: 2 flips crush, 2 lead to $\varepsilon$ → $\frac{1}{2}p(\varepsi
lon) = \frac{1}{2}$

$$p(\beta) = \max\!\left[\tfrac{1}{2}p(\alpha),\;\tfrac{1}{2}\right]$$

**Orbit $\gamma$** (rep. $(v_2,F)$): Ant moves to $v_3$ or $v_6$ (both lead to $
\delta$ or $\varepsilon$). Either choice gives:
$$p(\gamma) = \tfrac{1}{4}[p(\delta) + p(\varepsilon)] = \tfrac{1}{4}[p(\delta)+
1]$$

**Orbit $\delta$** (rep. $(v_3,T)$): Ant moves to $v_2$ or $v_4$ (both in $\beta
$). Either choice:
$$p(\delta) = \tfrac{1}{4}[p(\beta) + p(\gamma)]$$

## Solving the System

Since $p(\alpha) = \frac{1}{4}[p(\beta)+p(\gamma)] = p(\delta)$, let $p(\alpha)=
p(\delta)=a$, $p(\gamma)=c$.

Since $p(\alpha)\leq 1$, we have $\frac{1}{2}p(\alpha) \leq \frac{1}{2}$, so $p(
\beta) = \frac{1}{2}$.

Substituting:
$$c = \tfrac{1}{4}(a+1), \qquad a = \tfrac{1}{4}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+c\right)$$

$$a = \tfrac{1}{4}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{a+1}{4}\right) = \tfrac{1}{4}\!\le
ft(\tfrac{3}{4}+\tfrac{a}{4}\right) = \tfrac{3}{16}+\tfrac{a}{16}$$

$$\frac{15}{16}a = \frac{3}{16} \implies a = \frac{1}{5}$$

**Verification:** $c = \frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{6}{5}=\frac{3}{10}$, and $p(\alpha)
=\frac{1}{4}(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{10})=\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{4}{5}=\frac{1}{5}$.

## Result

The probability that the ant reaches the target is $p(\alpha) = \dfrac{1}{5}$.

This is already irreducible: $\frac{a}{b}=\frac{1}{5}$, so $a+b=1+5=\boxed{6}$.
