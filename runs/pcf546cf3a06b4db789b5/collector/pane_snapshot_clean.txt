## Proof

Let $c(y) = y(1-y) = y - y^2$, so $F(y) = \int_0^y \sqrt{x^4 + c(y)^2}\,dx$.

**Step 1: Compute $F'(y)$ via the Leibniz integral rule.**

$$F'(y) = \sqrt{y^4 + c^2}\bigg|_{x=y} + \int_0^y \frac{\partial}{\partial y}\sq
rt{x^4+c^2}\,dx = \sqrt{y^4+c^2} + c(1-2y)\int_0^y \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^4+c^2}},$$

since $c'(y) = 1-2y$ and $\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\sqrt{x^4+c^2} = \frac{c\cd
ot c'}{\sqrt{x^4+c^2}}$.

**Step 2: Show $F'(y) \geq 0$ on $[0,1]$.**

*Case 1: $y \in [0, \tfrac{1}{2}]$.* Here $c = y(1-y) \geq 0$ and $1-2y \geq 0$,
 so both terms in $F'(y)$ are non-negative. Thus $F'(y) \geq 0$.

*Case 2: $y \in [\tfrac{1}{2}, 1)$.* Here $c > 0$ and $1-2y < 0$, so the second
term is negative. We bound it from below. Since $x^4 \geq 0$, we have $\sqrt{x^4
+c^2} \geq c$, hence:

$$\int_0^y \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^4+c^2}} \leq \int_0^y \frac{dx}{c} = \frac{y}{c}.$$

Therefore:

$$c(1-2y)\int_0^y \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^4+c^2}} \geq c(1-2y)\cdot\frac{y}{c} = y(1-2
y).$$

Meanwhile, $\sqrt{y^4+c^2} \geq y^2$. So:

$$F'(y) \geq y^2 + y(1-2y) = y^2 + y - 2y^2 = y - y^2 = y(1-y) \geq 0.$$

*Case 3: $y = 1$.* Here $c = 0$, so $F'(1) = \sqrt{1+0} + 0 = 1 > 0$.

**Step 3: Conclude.**

$F'(y) \geq 0$ for all $y \in [0,1]$, so $F$ is non-decreasing on $[0,1]$. The m
aximum is attained at $y=1$:

$$F(1) = \int_0^1 \sqrt{x^4 + 0}\,dx = \int_0^1 x^2\,dx = \frac{1}{3}.$$

The maximum value is $\boxed{\dfrac{1}{3}}$.
