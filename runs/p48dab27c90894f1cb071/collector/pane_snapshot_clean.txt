## Solution

**Recursive definition.** Let $p_1(x)=(x-2)^2$ and $p_{k+1}(x)=(p_k(x)-2)^2$ for
 $k\ge 1$. This matches the nested structure in the problem.

**Tracking low-degree coefficients.** Write $p_k(x)=\cdots+B_kx^2+C_kx+D_k$ (ell
ipsis = higher-degree terms). Since $p_{k+1}=(p_k-2)^2=p_k^2-4p_k+4$, the low-de
gree coefficients satisfy:

- **Constant:** $D_{k+1}=D_k^2-4D_k+4=(D_k-2)^2$.
- **Linear:** $C_{k+1}=2D_kC_k-4C_k=C_k(2D_k-4)$.
- **Quadratic:** $B_{k+1}=2D_kB_k+C_k^2-4B_k$.

(The $x^2$ term of $p_k^2$ is $2D_kB_k+C_k^2$, since only the degree-$0,1,2$ ter
ms of $p_k$ can pair to give degree $2$.)

**Base case** ($p_1=x^2-4x+4$): $B_1=1,\ C_1=-4,\ D_1=4$.

**Solving $D_k$:** $D_1=4$, $D_2=(4-2)^2=4$, so $D_k=4$ for all $k\ge 1$.

**Solving $C_k$:** $C_{k+1}=C_k(2\cdot4-4)=4C_k$, with $C_1=-4$. Thus $C_k=-4^k$
.

**Solving $B_k$:** Substituting $D_k=4$ and $C_k^2=16^k$:
$$B_{k+1}=2\cdot4\cdot B_k+16^k-4B_k=4B_k+16^k.$$

This linear recurrence $B_{k+1}=4B_k+16^k$ has:
- Homogeneous solution: $A\cdot 4^k$.
- Particular solution: try $\alpha\cdot16^k$; then $16\alpha=4\alpha+1$, giving
$\alpha=\tfrac{1}{12}$.

So $B_k=A\cdot4^k+\tfrac{1}{12}\cdot16^k$. Using $B_1=1$:
$$4A+\frac{16}{12}=1 \implies 4A=-\frac{1}{3}\implies A=-\frac{1}{12}.$$

Therefore
$$B_k=\frac{16^k-4^k}{12}.$$

**Verification:** $B_1=\frac{16-4}{12}=1$ ✓; $B_2=\frac{256-16}{12}=20$ (from $p
_2=((x-2)^2-2)^2=(x^2-4x+2)^2=x^4-8x^3+20x^2-16x+4$) ✓; $B_3=\frac{4096-64}{12}=
336$ ✓.

The coefficient of $x^2$ in $p_k(x)$ is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{16^k-4^k}{12}}$$
