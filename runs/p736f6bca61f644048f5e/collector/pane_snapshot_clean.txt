## Proof

We evaluate
$$I = \int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\sin x}{x}\right)^{2}dx.$$

### Step 1. Reduce to a cosine integral

Using $\sin^{2}x = \tfrac{1}{2}(1-\cos 2x)$,
$$\left(\frac{\sin x}{x}\right)^{2} = \frac{1-\cos 2x}{2x^{2}}.$$
Hence
$$I = \frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos 2x}{x^{2}}\,dx.$$

### Step 2. A contour-integration lemma

**Lemma.** For $a>0$,
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos(ax)}{x^{2}}\,dx = \frac{\pi a}{2}.$$

*Proof of Lemma.* Consider
$$f(z) = \frac{1-e^{iaz}}{z^{2}}, \qquad a>0.$$
The numerator vanishes to first order at $z=0$ (since $1-e^{iaz}=-iaz+\cdots$),
so $f$ has a **simple pole** at $z=0$ with residue
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} f(z) = \lim_{z\to 0} z\cdot\frac{1-e^{iaz}}{z^{2}} =
\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{1-e^{iaz}}{z} = -ia.$$

Integrate $f$ over the closed contour $\Gamma$ consisting of (see figure):
- $\Gamma_{R}^{+}$: the upper semicircle $|z|=R$, $\operatorname{Im}z\ge 0$,
- $[-R,-\varepsilon]$: the real axis from $-R$ to $-\varepsilon$,
- $C_{\varepsilon}$: the upper semicircle $|z|=\varepsilon$, $\operatorname{Im}z
\ge 0$ (traversed clockwise),
- $[\varepsilon,R]$: the real axis from $\varepsilon$ to $R$.

The only singularity inside $\Gamma$ is at $z=0$, so by the residue theorem,
$$\oint_{\Gamma} f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i\cdot(-ia) = 2\pi a.$$

**Large arc $\Gamma_{R}^{+}$.** For $z=Re^{i\theta}$, $0\le\theta\le\pi$:
$$|e^{iaz}| = e^{-a R\sin\theta}\le 1 \quad(\text{since }a>0,\ \sin\theta\ge 0),
$$
so $|1-e^{iaz}|\le 2$ and
$$\left|\int_{\Gamma_{R}^{+}} f(z)\,dz\right| \le \frac{2}{R^{2}}\cdot\pi R = \f
rac{2\pi}{R}\xrightarrow[R\to\infty]{}0.$$

**Small arc $C_{\varepsilon}$.** Parametrize $z=\varepsilon e^{i\theta}$, $\thet
a$ from $\pi$ to $0$:
$$\int_{C_{\varepsilon}} \frac{1-e^{iaz}}{z^{2}}\,dz = \int_{\pi}^{0}\frac{1-e^{
ia\varepsilon e^{i\theta}}}{\varepsilon^{2}e^{2i\theta}}\,i\varepsilon e^{i\thet
a}\,d\theta = \frac{i}{\varepsilon}\int_{\pi}^{0}\bigl(1-e^{ia\varepsilon e^{i\t
heta}}\bigr)e^{-i\theta}\,d\theta.$$
Since $1-e^{ia\varepsilon e^{i\theta}} = -ia\varepsilon e^{i\theta} + O(\varepsi
lon^{2})$,
$$\int_{C_{\varepsilon}} f\,dz = \frac{i}{\varepsilon}\int_{\pi}^{0}\bigl(-ia\va
repsilon + O(\varepsilon^{2})\bigr)\,d\theta = i\int_{\pi}^{0}(-ia)\,d\theta + O
(\varepsilon) = a\int_{\pi}^{0}d\theta + O(\varepsilon) = -\pi a + O(\varepsilon
).$$
Thus $\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{C_{\varepsilon}} f\,dz = -\pi a$
.

**Real-axis pieces.** As $R\to\infty$ and $\varepsilon\to 0$,
$$\int_{\varepsilon}^{R}\frac{1-e^{iax}}{x^{2}}\,dx + \int_{-R}^{-\varepsilon}\f
rac{1-e^{iax}}{x^{2}}\,dx = \int_{\varepsilon}^{R}\frac{1-e^{iax}}{x^{2}}\,dx +
\int_{\varepsilon}^{R}\frac{1-e^{-iax}}{x^{2}}\,dx = 2\int_{\varepsilon}^{R}\fra
c{1-\cos(ax)}{x^{2}}\,dx.$$

**Assemble.** Taking the limits,
$$0 + 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos(ax)}{x^{2}}\,dx + (-\pi a) = 2\pi a,$$
giving
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos(ax)}{x^{2}}\,dx = \frac{\pi a}{2}. \qquad\blacks
quare$$

### Step 3. Apply the lemma

With $a=2$:
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos 2x}{x^{2}}\,dx = \frac{\pi\cdot 2}{2} = \pi.$$
Therefore
$$I = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\pi = \boxed{\dfrac{\pi}{2}}.$$

### Step 4. Verification (numerical sanity check)

The integrand $(\sin x/x)^{2}$ is non-negative, equals $1$ at $x=0$, and decays
like $1/x^{2}$, so a finite positive value is expected. The known value $\pi/2\a
pprox 1.5708$ is consistent with the area under the main lobe $[0,\pi]$ (where $
(\sin x/x)^{2}$ ranges from $1$ down to $0$, contributing roughly $1.45$) plus t
he oscillating tail contributing the remainder. ✓
