## Proof

**Setup.** Let the four roots of $x^4+px^3+qx^2+rx+s=0$ be the vertices of a squ
are in the complex plane. A square centered at $c\in\mathbb{C}$ with "radius" $a
\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}$ has vertices
$$c+a,\quad c+ai,\quad c-a,\quad c-ai.$$

**The polynomial.** Setting $u=x-c$, the polynomial with these roots is
$$(u-a)(u-ai)(u+a)(u+ai)=(u^2-a^2)(u^2+a^2)=u^4-a^4.$$
So the polynomial is $(x-c)^4-a^4$, giving
$$p=-4c,\quad q=6c^2,\quad r=-4c^3,\quad s=c^4-a^4.$$

**Forcing $c$ real.** Since $p=-4c$ is a (real) integer, $c$ must be real. Write
 $c=-p/4$ with $p\in\mathbb{Z}$. Then $q=6c^2=3p^2/8$ must be an integer, so $8\
mid p^2$, hence $4\mid p$. Set $p=4k$ ($k\in\mathbb{Z}$); then $c=-k$, $q=6k^2$,
 $r=4k^3$, and
$$s=k^4-a^4.$$

**Forcing $a^4$ real.** Since $k^4$ is real and $s$ is a real integer, $a^4=k^4-
s$ is real. Let $a=\rho e^{i\theta}$; then $a^4=\rho^4 e^{4i\theta}$ is real, so
 $4\theta=n\pi$ for some $n\in\mathbb{Z}$.

**Area in terms of $a^4$.** The side length of the square is $|a-ai|=|a|\sqrt{2}
$, so
$$\text{Area}=2|a|^2=2\rho^2.$$
Since $|a^4|=\rho^4$, we get $\rho^2=\sqrt{|a^4|}$, hence
$$\text{Area}=2\sqrt{|a^4|}.$$

Now $a^4=k^4-s$ is a **nonzero integer** (nonzero because $a\neq 0$). Let $t=a^4
\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$. Then
$$\text{Area}=2\sqrt{|t|},\qquad |t|\geq 1.$$

**Minimum.** The function $2\sqrt{|t|}$ over nonzero integers $t$ is minimized a
t $|t|=1$, giving
$$\text{Area}_{\min}=2.$$

**Achievability.** Take $k=0$ (so $c=0$, $p=q=r=0$) and $a=1$ (so $a^4=1$, $s=0-
1=-1$). The polynomial is
$$x^4-1=0,$$
with roots $1,-1,i,-i$ — a square centered at the origin with side length $\sqrt
{2}$ and area $2$. All coefficients $p=0,q=0,r=0,s=-1$ are integers. ✓
