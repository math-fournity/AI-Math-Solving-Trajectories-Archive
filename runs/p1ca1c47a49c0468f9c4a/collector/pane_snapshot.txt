The claim is **FALSE**. Here is a counterexample.

**Counterexample.** Let $X = \ell^2$, the Hilbert space of square-summable real
sequences with the usual norm $\|x\|_2 = \bigl(\sum_{n=1}^\infty |x_n|^2\bigr)^{
1/2}$. Let $x_i = e_i$, the $i$-th standard unit vector (the sequence with $1$ i
n position $i$ and $0$ elsewhere). The set $\{e_1, e_2, \dots\}$ is linearly ind
ependent. Set $\alpha_i = i$ for each $i \in \mathbb{N}$.

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a continuous (bounded) linear func
tional $f \in X^* = (\ell^2)^*$ with $f(e_i) = i$ for all $i$.

By the Riesz representation theorem, every $f \in (\ell^2)^*$ has the form
$$f(x) = \langle x, y\rangle = \sum_{n=1}^\infty x_n y_n$$
for a unique $y \in \ell^2$. In particular, $f(e_i) = y_i$ for each $i$. The req
uirement $f(e_i) = i$ forces $y_i = i$ for every $i$, i.e. $y = (1, 2, 3, \dots)
$.

But
$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty y_n^2 = \sum_{n=1}^\infty n^2 = +\infty,$$
so $y \notin \ell^2$. This contradicts $y \in \ell^2$.

Therefore no such $f \in X^*$ exists, and the claim is **false**.

**Remark on why the naive argument fails.** One might try to invoke Hahn–Banach:
 define a linear functional $g$ on $\operatorname{span}\{e_i\}$ (which is $c_{00
}$, the finitely supported sequences) by $g(e_i) = i$ and extend linearly. This
$g$ is well-defined on $c_{00}$ since $\{e_i\}$ is linearly independent. However
, $g$ is **unbounded** on this subspace: $\|e_i\|_2 = 1$ yet $|g(e_i)| = i \to \
infty$, so $\|g\| \geq i$ for all $i$, i.e. $\|g\| = \infty$. Hahn–Banach extend
s *bounded* linear functionals from a subspace to the whole space while preservi
ng the norm; it does not extend unbounded ones. Hence the extension to a continu
ous functional on all of $\ell^2$ is impossible, confirming the counterexample.
