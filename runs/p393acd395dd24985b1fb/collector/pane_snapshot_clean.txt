## Solution

**Step 1: Simplify the sum.**

Using $\cos^2(kx) = \frac{1+\cos(2kx)}{2}$:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{2n+1}\cos^2(kx) = \frac{2n+1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{2n+1}\co
s(2kx)$$

The equation $\sum \cos^2(kx) = n$ becomes:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{2n+1}\cos(2kx) = -1 \tag{$\star$}$$

**Step 2: Evaluate the cosine sum (for $\sin x \neq 0$).**

Using the arithmetic-progression formula with $m=2n+1$, $\theta=2x$:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{2n+1}\cos(2kx) = \frac{\sin((2n+1)x)\,\cos((2n+2)x)}{\sin x}$$

So ($\star$) becomes $\sin((2n+1)x)\cos((2n+2)x) = -\sin x$.

**Step 3: Reduce to a factored equation.**

Apply product-to-sum on the left:

$$\sin((2n+1)x)\cos((2n+2)x) = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\sin((4n+3)x)-\sin x\bigr]$$

So the equation is $\frac{1}{2}[\sin((4n+3)x)-\sin x] = -\sin x$, i.e.

$$\sin((4n+3)x)+\sin x = 0$$

Sum-to-product gives:

$$2\sin((2n+2)x)\,\cos((2n+1)x)=0 \tag{$\star\star$}$$

All steps from ($\star$) to ($\star\star$) are reversible algebraic identities v
alid for **all** $x$ (the only division, by $\sin x$, was in the sum formula). W
e must separately handle $\sin x = 0$.

**Step 4: Check $\sin x = 0$ (i.e. $x=0$ or $x=\pi$).**

At $x=0$: LHS of ($\star$) $= 2n+1 \neq -1$. At $x=\pi$: $\cos(2k\pi)=1$, LHS $=
 2n+1\neq -1$. So $x=0,\pi$ are **not** solutions of ($\star$), even though they
 satisfy ($\star\star$). They are extraneous and must be excluded.

**Step 5: Count solutions of ($\star\star$) in $[0,2\pi)$.**

*Case 1:* $\sin((2n+2)x)=0 \Rightarrow x = \frac{m\pi}{2n+2}$, $m\in\{0,1,\dots,
4n+3\}$: gives $4n+4$ values.

*Case 2:* $\cos((2n+1)x)=0 \Rightarrow x = \frac{(2m+1)\pi}{2(2n+1)}$, $m\in\{0,
1,\dots,4n+1\}$: gives $4n+2$ values.

*Overlap:* Both hold iff $(2n+2)x = a\pi$ and $(2n+1)x = \frac{\pi}{2}+b\pi$. Fr
om the first, $x=\frac{a\pi}{2(n+1)}$; substituting: $\frac{(2n+1)a}{n+1} = 1+2b
$. Since $\gcd(2n+1,n+1)=1$, we need $(n+1)\mid a$, say $a=(n+1)j$, giving $x =
\frac{j\pi}{2}$. In $[0,2\pi)$ with $j$ odd: $j\in\{1,3\}$, i.e. $x=\frac{\pi}{2
},\frac{3\pi}{2}$. **2 overlaps.**

Total solutions of ($\star\star$): $(4n+4)+(4n+2)-2 = 8n+4$.

**Step 6: Exclude extraneous solutions.**

Remove $x=0$ and $x=\pi$ (from Case 1, where $\sin x=0$):

$$\text{Number of solutions} = (8n+4)-2 = \boxed{8n+2}$$

**Verification ($n=1$):** Equation $\cos^2 x+\cos^2 2x+\cos^2 3x=1$ reduces to $
\sin 4x\cos 3x=0$, yielding $8+6-2=12$ roots of ($\star\star$); excluding $x=0,\
pi$ gives $10 = 8(1)+2$. Spot-checks: $x=\pi/4\Rightarrow \frac12+0+\frac12=1$ ✓
; $x=\pi/6\Rightarrow \frac34+\frac14+0=1$ ✓; $x=\pi/2\Rightarrow 0+1+0=1$ ✓.
