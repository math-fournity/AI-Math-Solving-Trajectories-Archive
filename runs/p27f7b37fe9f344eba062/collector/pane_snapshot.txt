## De Rham Cohomology of the m-Torus

**Setup.** Let $\mathbb{T}^m = (S^1)^m = \mathbb{R}^m/\mathbb{Z}^m$ be the $m$-d
imensional torus, equipped with the quotient smooth structure. Denote the standa
rd angular coordinates by $\theta_1,\dots,\theta_m \in \mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
$, so that $d\theta_1,\dots,d\theta_m$ are globally defined closed $1$-forms on
$\mathbb{T}^m$.

**Claim.** The de Rham cohomology of $\mathbb{T}^m$ is an exterior algebra on $m
$ generators of degree $1$:
$$
H^*_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{T}^m) \;\cong\; \Lambda^*(\mathbb{R}^m), \qquad H^k_{\
mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{T}^m) \;\cong\; \Lambda^k(\mathbb{R}^m),
$$
and consequently
$$
\dim H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{T}^m) \;=\; \binom{m}{k}, \qquad 0 \le k \le m,
$$
with $H^k = 0$ for $k > m$.

---

### Proof

**Step 1 — Künneth reduction.** Since $\mathbb{T}^m = \underbrace{S^1 \times \cd
ots \times S^1}_{m}$, we apply the Künneth formula for de Rham cohomology (valid
 without Tor terms over the field $\mathbb{R}$):
$$
H^*_{\mathrm{dR}}(X \times Y) \;\cong\; H^*_{\mathrm{dR}}(X) \otimes H^*_{\mathr
m{dR}}(Y),
$$
as graded algebras (with the graded tensor product / wedge structure). Iterating
,
$$
H^*_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{T}^m) \;\cong\; \bigotimes_{i=1}^{m} H^*_{\mathrm{dR}}
(S^1).
$$

**Step 2 — Cohomology of $S^1$.** The circle $S^1$ has
$$
H^0_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^1) \cong \mathbb{R}, \qquad H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^1) \cong \ma
thbb{R},
$$
with $H^1$ generated by the closed $1$-form $d\theta$ (which is not exact, since
 $\theta$ is not a global function). All higher groups vanish. Thus $H^*_{\mathr
m{dR}}(S^1) \cong \Lambda^*(\mathbb{R})$, an exterior algebra on one generator o
f degree $1$.

**Step 3 — Tensor product of exterior algebras is an exterior algebra.** A stand
ard algebraic fact: the graded tensor product of exterior algebras is again an e
xterior algebra:
$$
\Lambda^*(V_1) \otimes \cdots \otimes \Lambda^*(V_m) \;\cong\; \Lambda^*(V_1 \op
lus \cdots \oplus V_m),
$$
where each $V_i \cong \mathbb{R}$ sits in degree $1$. Combining with Steps 1–2,
$$
H^*_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{T}^m) \;\cong\; \Lambda^*(\underbrace{\mathbb{R}\oplus
\cdots\oplus\mathbb{R}}_{m}) \;=\; \Lambda^*(\mathbb{R}^m).
$$

**Step 4 — Explicit basis and dimension.** A basis of $H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb
{T}^m)$ is given by the cohomology classes of the forms
$$
d\theta_{i_1} \wedge d\theta_{i_2} \wedge \cdots \wedge d\theta_{i_k}, \qquad 1
\le i_1 < i_2 < \cdots < i_k \le m.
$$
These are closed (each $d\theta_i$ is closed, and $d(\alpha\wedge\beta) = d\alph
a\wedge\beta + (-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha\wedge d\beta$). They are linearly independe
nt in cohomology: this can be verified by integration against the dual cycles (t
he sub-tori obtained by fixing the complementary coordinates), using Stokes' the
orem — a nontrivial linear combination $\sum c_I\, d\theta_I$ that is exact woul
d integrate to zero against every dual cycle, forcing all $c_I = 0$. They span:
every $k$-form on $\mathbb{T}^m$ can be written (in coordinates) as $\sum_I f_I\
, d\theta_I$ with periodic coefficient functions $f_I$; integrating the closedne
ss condition $d\omega = 0$ and using the Fourier expansion of each $f_I$ on $\ma
thbb{T}^m$ shows that the only non-exact contributions come from the constant Fo
urier modes, i.e. precisely the span of $\{d\theta_I\}$.

The number of strictly increasing multi-indices $1 \le i_1 < \cdots < i_k \le m$
 is $\binom{m}{k}$, so
$$
\dim H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{T}^m) = \binom{m}{k}.
$$

**Step 5 — Summary of dimensions.**
$$
\dim H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{T}^m) =
\begin{cases}
\binom{m}{k}, & 0 \le k \le m,\\[4pt]
0, & k > m.
\end{cases}
$$
In particular $\dim H^0 = 1$ (connectedness), $\dim H^1 = m$ (rank of the fundam
ental group / first Betti number $b_1 = m$), $\dim H^m = 1$ (orientation class),
 and the Euler characteristic is
$$
\chi(\mathbb{T}^m) = \sum_{k=0}^{m} (-1)^k \binom{m}{k} = (1-1)^m = 0,
$$
consistent with $\mathbb{T}^m$ admitting a nowhere-vanishing vector field.

---

**Conclusion.** The de Rham cohomology of the $m$-torus is the exterior algebra
on $m$ degree-$1$ generators:
$$
\boxed{\,H^*_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{T}^m) \cong \Lambda^*(\mathbb{R}^m), \qquad \
dim H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{T}^m) = \binom{m}{k}\,}
$$
for $0 \le k \le m$, and $H^k = 0$ for $k > m$.
