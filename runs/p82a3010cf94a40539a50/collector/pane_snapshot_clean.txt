### Lower bound: $\chi(G) \ge n$

Consider the $n$ singleton subsets $\{1\}, \{2\}, \dots, \{n\}$. For any $i \neq
 j$, we have $\{i\} \cap \{j\} = \varnothing$, so these two vertices are adjacen
t. Hence the singletons form a clique of size $n$, i.e., a copy of $K_n$. Theref
ore
$$\chi(G) \ge \omega(G) \ge n.$$

### Upper bound: $\chi(G) \le n$

Define a coloring $c : V(G) \to [n]$ by
$$c(S) = \min S$$
for every non-empty subset $S \subseteq [n]$. This is well-defined since every n
on-empty subset of $[n]$ has a minimum element, and $c(S) \in S \subseteq [n]$,
so it uses at most $n$ colors.

We verify it is a proper coloring. Suppose $A$ and $B$ are adjacent, meaning $A
\cap B = \varnothing$. Then no element belongs to both $A$ and $B$. In particula
r $\min A \in A$ and $\min B \in B$, so $\min A \neq \min B$ (otherwise that com
mon value would lie in $A \cap B$). Thus $c(A) \neq c(B)$.

So $c$ is a proper $n$-coloring, giving $\chi(G) \le n$.

### Conclusion

Combining both bounds,
$$\chi(G) = n.$$
