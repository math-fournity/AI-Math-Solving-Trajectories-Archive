# Problem

In a remote desert, a security firm is contracted to protect a high-value facility containing exactly 101 sensors placed at distinct locations. A survey of the terrain confirms that no three sensors are positioned in a perfectly straight line.

To secure the perimeter, the firm must deploy a series of long, straight laser-fences. These lasers must be positioned such that no individual laser beam passes directly through any sensor. The security protocol requires that the configuration of lasers must be "locally isolating": for every possible pair of sensors $X$ and $Y$ in the facility, there must be at least one laser fence that physically separates sensor $X$ from sensor $Y$ (placing them on opposite sides of the beam).

What is the minimum number of laser fences $m$ that must be deployed to ensure that, regardless of the specific coordinates of the 101 sensors, this isolation requirement is always satisfied?
