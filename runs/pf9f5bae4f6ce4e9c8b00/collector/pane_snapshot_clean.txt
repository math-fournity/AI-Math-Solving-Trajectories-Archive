## Solution

We evaluate
$$L = \lim_{n\to\infty} n^{1/2}\int_0^\infty \left( \frac{2x}{1+x^2} \right)^n d
x.$$

**Step 1: Locate the maximum.** Let $f(x) = \frac{2x}{1+x^2}$. By AM–GM, $1+x^2
\ge 2x$, so $f(x) \le 1$, with equality iff $x=1$. Thus $f$ has a unique global
maximum $f(1)=1$ at the interior point $x_0=1$ of $(0,\infty)$, and $f(x)<1$ for
 all $x\ne 1$.

**Step 2: Apply Laplace's method.** Write the integrand as $e^{n\,h(x)}$ where $
h(x) = \ln f(x) = \ln(2x) - \ln(1+x^2)$. Then:
- $h(1) = 0$ (the maximum value),
- $h'(x) = \frac{1}{x} - \frac{2x}{1+x^2}$, so $h'(1) = 1 - 1 = 0$,
- $h''(x) = -\frac{1}{x^2} - \frac{2(1-x^2)}{(1+x^2)^2}$, so $h''(1) = -1 - 0 =
-1 < 0$.

Since $x_0=1$ is an interior, non-degenerate maximum of $h$ with $h(1)=0$ and $|
h''(1)|=1$, and $f(x)\to 0$ at both endpoints $0$ and $\infty$ (so the contribut
ion away from $x_0=1$ is exponentially suppressed), Laplace's method gives:

$$\int_0^\infty e^{n\,h(x)}\,dx \;\sim\; e^{n\,h(1)}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{n\,|h''(1)
|}} \;=\; \sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{n}}.$$

**Step 3: Justifying the conditions.** The maximum is unique and non-degenerate
($h''(1)=-1<0$). For any $\delta>0$, $\sup_{|x-1|\ge\delta} f(x) < 1$, so the ta
ils contribute $O(\rho^n)$ for some $\rho<1$, which is $o(n^{-k})$ for every $k$
—negligible compared to the $n^{-1/2}$ main term. Near $x=1$, the quadratic appr
oximation $h(x) = -\frac{1}{2}(x-1)^2 + O((x-1)^3)$ controls the integral, and t
he standard Gaussian integral $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-nt^2/2}\,dt = \sqrt{2
\pi/n}$ yields the leading term.

**Step 4: Conclude.** Therefore

$$L = \lim_{n\to\infty} n^{1/2} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{n}} = \sqrt{2\pi}.$$

$$\boxed{L = \sqrt{2\pi}}$$
