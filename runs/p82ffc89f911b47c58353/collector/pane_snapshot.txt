## Proof

We evaluate

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}\frac{p'(z)}{p(z)}\,dz.$$

**Setup.** Since $z_0$ is a simple zero of $p$ (multiplicity $1$), we can factor

$$p(z) = (z - z_0)\,q(z),$$

where $q$ is a polynomial (hence entire) with $q(z_0) \neq 0$.

**Compute the logarithmic derivative.** Differentiating,

$$p'(z) = q(z) + (z-z_0)\,q'(z),$$

so

$$\frac{p'(z)}{p(z)} = \frac{q(z) + (z-z_0)\,q'(z)}{(z-z_0)\,q(z)} = \frac{1}{z-
z_0} + \frac{q'(z)}{q(z)}.$$

**Apply the residue theorem.** The curve $\gamma$ is a simple closed $C^1$ curve
 surrounding $z_0$ and no other zero of $p$. The function $\frac{q'(z)}{q(z)}$ i
s holomorphic on and inside $\gamma$, since $q$ has no zeros enclosed by $\gamma
$ (all zeros of $q$ coincide with the remaining zeros of $p$, none of which lie
inside $\gamma$). Therefore

$$\int_{\gamma}\frac{q'(z)}{q(z)}\,dz = 0.$$

The only singularity of $\frac{p'(z)}{p(z)}$ inside $\gamma$ is the simple pole
at $z_0$ with residue $1$. By the residue theorem,

$$\int_{\gamma}\frac{p'(z)}{p(z)}\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}_{z=z_0}\
frac{p'(z)}{p(z)} = 2\pi i \cdot 1.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}\frac{p'(z)}{p(z)}\,dz = 1.$$

This is the local form of the **argument principle**: each zero of multiplicity
$m$ enclosed by $\gamma$ contributes $m$ to the integral, and here $m = 1$ with
exactly one zero enclosed.
