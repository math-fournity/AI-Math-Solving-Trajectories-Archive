# IMO 2023 Problem 1 — Solution

**Claim.** The composite integers $n > 1$ satisfying the property are exactly th
e prime powers $n = p^a$ with $p$ prime and $a \geq 2$.

---

## Prime powers satisfy the condition

Let $n = p^a$ with $p$ prime, $a \geq 2$. The divisors are $1, p, p^2, \ldots, p
^a$, so $d_i = p^{i-1}$. The condition becomes
$$p^{i-1} \mid p^i + p^{i+1} = p^i(1+p),$$
which holds since $p^{i-1} \mid p^i$. ✓

---

## No number with ≥ 2 distinct prime factors satisfies the condition

Suppose $n$ is composite with at least two distinct prime factors. Let $p < q$ b
e the two smallest prime factors of $n$, and let $p^a \| n$ ($a \geq 1$).

**Key observation.** Since $q$ is the second smallest prime factor, every diviso
r of $n$ strictly less than $q$ is a power of $p$ (any divisor involving a prime
 $\geq q$ is itself $\geq q$).

Let $j^*$ be the smallest positive integer with $p^{j^*} \geq q$. Since $p < q$,
 we have $p^1 < q$, so $j^* \geq 2$.

The divisors of $n$ less than $q$ are exactly $1, p, p^2, \ldots, p^{j^*-1}$. Eq
uivalently, the divisors of $n$ greater than $n/q$ are exactly
$$n,\; \frac{n}{p},\; \frac{n}{p^2},\; \ldots,\; \frac{n}{p^{j^*-1}}.$$

Therefore the largest $j^*+1$ divisors in decreasing order are:
$$d_k = n,\quad d_{k-1} = \tfrac{n}{p},\quad d_{k-2} = \tfrac{n}{p^2},\quad \ldo
ts,\quad d_{k-j^*+1} = \tfrac{n}{p^{j^*-1}},\quad d_{k-j^*} = \tfrac{n}{q}.$$

**Conditions among the $p$-power quotients (always satisfied).** For $2 \leq t \
leq j^*-1$, the condition at $i = k-t$ is:
$$\frac{n}{p^t} \;\Big|\; \frac{n}{p^{t-1}} + \frac{n}{p^{t-2}} = \frac{n(p+1)}{
p^{t-1}} = \frac{n}{p^t}\cdot p(p+1). \quad\checkmark$$

**The transition condition (fails).** At $i = k - j^*$:
$$d_{k-j^*} = \frac{n}{q}, \qquad d_{k-j^*+1} = \frac{n}{p^{j^*-1}}, \qquad d_{k
-j^*+2} = \frac{n}{p^{j^*-2}}.$$

The condition requires:
$$\frac{n}{q} \;\Big|\; \frac{n}{p^{j^*-1}} + \frac{n}{p^{j^*-2}} = \frac{n(p+1)
}{p^{j^*-1}}.$$

This is equivalent to requiring $\dfrac{q(p+1)}{p^{j^*-1}} \in \mathbb{Z}$, i.e.
,
$$p^{j^*-1} \mid q(p+1).$$

Since $\gcd(p, p+1) = 1$, we have $\gcd\!\big(p^{j^*-1},\, p+1\big) = 1$, so:
$$p^{j^*-1} \mid q.$$

But $j^* \geq 2$ gives $p^{j^*-1} \geq p \geq 2$, while $q$ is prime. The only w
ay $p^{j^*-1} \mid q$ is $p^{j^*-1} = q$, which forces $j^* - 1 = 1$ (so $j^* =
2$) and $q = p$—contradicting $p < q$.

**Contradiction.** So no composite $n$ with two or more distinct prime factors c
an satisfy the property. $\square$

---

## Conclusion

The composite integers $n > 1$ satisfying the divisor condition are precisely
$$\boxed{n = p^a, \quad p \text{ prime}, \quad a \geq 2.}$$
