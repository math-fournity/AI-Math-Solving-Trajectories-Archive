## Solution

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains no solutions, answer value
s, or proof sketches. Proceeding.

**Goal.** Find the probability that
$$|x|+|y|+|z|+|x+y+z|=|x+y|+|y+z|+|z+x|,$$
with $x,y,z\overset{\text{iid}}{\sim}\mathrm{Unif}[-1,1]$.

---

### Step 1. Reduce to a triangle inequality

Set $u=x+y,\;v=y+z,\;w=z+x$. Then $x=(u+w-v)/2,\;y=(u+v-w)/2,\;z=(v+w-u)/2,\;x+y
+z=(u+v+w)/2$. Multiplying the desired equality by $2$:

$$|u+w-v|+|u+v-w|+|v+w-u|+|u+v+w|=2\bigl(|u|+|v|+|w|\bigr).\tag{$*$}$$

This expression is symmetric in $u,v,w$ and invariant under $(u,v,w)\mapsto(-u,-
v,-w)$. I claim ($*$) holds **iff $|u|,|v|,|w|$ satisfy the triangle inequality*
* (the largest is at most the sum of the other two).

*Proof of claim.* By symmetry it suffices to check two sign patterns.

- **All same sign**, say $u,v,w\ge 0$. Then $|u+v+w|=u+v+w$. If the triangle ine
quality holds, all of $u+w-v,u+v-w,v+w-u$ are $\ge 0$, so the LHS $=2(u+v+w)=$ R
HS. If it fails, say $w>u+v$, then $|u+v-w|=w-u-v$ while the other two stay nonn
eg, giving LHS $=4w>2(u+v+w)=$ RHS.
- **Mixed sign**, say $u,v\ge 0,\;w\le 0$, put $w'=-w\ge 0$. A direct case check
 (split on whether $w'\le |u-v|$, $|u-v|\le w'\le u+v$, or $w'\ge u+v$) shows eq
uality holds exactly when $|u-v|\le w'\le u+v$, i.e. precisely when $u,v,w'$ for
m a triangle — equivalently $|u|,|v|,|w|$ form a triangle. Outside this range th
e LHS is strictly larger.

Hence in every case LHS $\ge$ RHS, with equality **iff** $|u|,|v|,|w|$ are the s
ides of a (possibly degenerate) triangle. ∎

So
$$P(\text{equilibrium})=P\bigl(|x+y|,\,|y+z|,\,|z+x|\text{ form a triangle}\bigr
).$$

---

### Step 2. Complement via three disjoint events

The triangle condition fails iff one side strictly exceeds the sum of the other
two. These three events are mutually exclusive (two simultaneous strict violatio
ns would give $p+q>p+q+2r$, impossible). By the $S_3$-symmetry in $(x,y,z)$:

$$P(\text{fail})=3\,P\bigl(|x+y|>|y+z|+|z+x|\bigr).$$

---

### Step 3. Compute $V=\operatorname{Vol}\{|x+y|>|y+z|+|z+x|\}\subset[-1,1]^3$

Use coordinates $s=x+y$, $d=(x-y)/2$ (so $x=s/2+d,\;y=s/2-d$), and keep $z$. The
n $|J|=1$, so $dx\,dy\,dz=ds\,dd\,dz$. Set $e=s/2+z$ (so $z=e-s/2$, $dz=de$, aga
in $|J|=1$). Then
$$y+z=e-d,\qquad z+x=e+d.$$

Using the identity $|e-d|+|e+d|=2\max(|e|,|d|)$, the condition becomes
$$|s|>2\max(|e|,|d|)\;\iff\;|d|<\tfrac{|s|}{2}\;\text{and}\;|e|<\tfrac{|s|}{2}.$
$

The domain ranges (for $s\ge 0$; the $s\le 0$ half is symmetric):
- $s\in[0,2]$, $\;d\in[-(1-s/2),\,1-s/2]$ (length $2-s$), $\;e\in[-1+s/2,\,1+s/2
]$ (length $2$).

Intersecting each with $|d|<s/2$, $|e|<s/2$:

| $s$ range | $d$-length | $e$-length | cross-section |
|---|---|---|---|
| $0\le s\le 1$ | $s$ | $s$ | $s^{2}$ |
| $1\le s\le 2$ | $2-s$ | $1$ | $2-s$ |

Therefore
$$V=2\Bigl[\int_0^1 s^2\,ds+\int_1^2(2-s)\,ds\Bigr]=2\Bigl[\tfrac13+\tfrac12\Big
r]=2\cdot\tfrac56=\tfrac53.$$

---

### Step 4. Assemble

$$P\bigl(|x+y|>|y+z|+|z+x|\bigr)=\frac{V}{8}=\frac{5/3}{8}=\frac{5}{24}.$$

$$P(\text{fail})=3\cdot\frac{5}{24}=\frac{5}{8},\qquad P(\text{equilibrium})=1-\
frac{5}{8}=\frac{3}{8}.$$

The irreducible fraction is $\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{3}{8}$, so
$$a+b=3+8=\boxed{11}.$$
