## Proof

**Answer: Yes, $\dim(G/H)$ is even.**

Let $\mathfrak g, \mathfrak h$ be the Lie algebras of $G, H$. Since $G$ is compa
ct, $\mathfrak g$ is a compact Lie algebra, and every Lie subalgebra of a compac
t Lie algebra is again of compact type (reductive with compact semisimple part).
 Hence $\mathfrak h$ is reductive.

**Step 1: $\mathfrak h$ contains a Cartan subalgebra of $\mathfrak g$.**

Let $\operatorname{rank}G = \operatorname{rank}H = r$, and let $\mathfrak t_{\ma
thfrak h}\subseteq\mathfrak h$ be a Cartan subalgebra of $\mathfrak h$, so $\dim
\mathfrak t_{\mathfrak h}=r$. In a compact Lie algebra every element is semisimp
le, so the elements of $\mathfrak t_{\mathfrak h}$ are semisimple in $\mathfrak
g$. Since $\mathfrak t_{\mathfrak h}$ is abelian, it is a toral subalgebra of $\
mathfrak g$ of dimension $r=\operatorname{rank}\mathfrak g$, hence a Cartan suba
lgebra of $\mathfrak g$. Denote it $\mathfrak t$.

**Step 2: Root decomposition of $\mathfrak h$ relative to $\mathfrak t$.**

Complexify. We have
$$\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}=\mathfrak t_{\mathbb C}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi}
(\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C})_{\alpha},$$
where $\Phi$ is the root system of $\mathfrak g$ and each $(\mathfrak g_{\mathbb
 C})_{\alpha}$ is one-dimensional over $\mathbb C$. Since $\mathfrak h$ is reduc
tive and contains $\mathfrak t$ as a Cartan subalgebra, $\mathfrak h_{\mathbb C}
$ decomposes as
$$\mathfrak h_{\mathbb C}=\mathfrak t_{\mathbb C}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Psi}
(\mathfrak h_{\mathbb C})_{\alpha},$$
where $\Psi\subseteq\Phi$ is a (closed) root subsystem, and each $(\mathfrak h_{
\mathbb C})_{\alpha}$ is a one-dimensional complex subspace of $(\mathfrak g_{\m
athbb C})_{\alpha}$. But $(\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C})_{\alpha}$ is itself one-dime
nsional, so $(\mathfrak h_{\mathbb C})_{\alpha}=(\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C})_{\alph
a}$ for every $\alpha\in\Psi$.

**Step 3: Compute the dimension.**

Passing back to the real compact form, each root space contributes $2$ real dime
nsions. Therefore
$$\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathfrak g = \dim\mathfrak t + 2|\Phi|,\qquad \dim_{\mathbb R
}\mathfrak h = \dim\mathfrak t + 2|\Psi|.$$
Since $\operatorname{rank}G=\operatorname{rank}H$, the $\dim\mathfrak t$ terms c
ancel:
$$\dim(G/H)=\dim\mathfrak g-\dim\mathfrak h = 2\bigl(|\Phi|-|\Psi|\bigr),$$
which is even.

Thus $\dim(G/H)$ is always even. $\blacksquare$
