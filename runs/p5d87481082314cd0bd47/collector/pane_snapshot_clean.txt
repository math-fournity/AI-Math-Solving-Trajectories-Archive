## Proof

**Setup.** Since $g:\mathbb{R}^m\to\mathbb{R}$ is continuous, non-negative, and
$\int_{\mathbb{R}^m} g(x)\,dx=1$, the function $g$ is a probability density func
tion. Let $Y_1,Y_2,\dots$ be i.i.d. $\mathbb{R}^m$-valued random vectors with de
nsity $g$. The notation $y_k^2$ in the constraint denotes $\|y_k\|^2$ (the squar
ed Euclidean norm), so

$$J_n(r) = \mathbb{P}\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|Y_k\|^2 \le r^2\right).$$

Set $X_k = \|Y_k\|^2 \ge 0$. Then $(X_k)$ are i.i.d. non-negative random variabl
es and $J_n(r)=\mathbb{P}(S_n\le r^2)$ where $S_n=\sum_{k=1}^n X_k$.

**The mean is positive.** Since $g$ is continuous with $\int g=1$, there exists
a non-empty open set $U$ on which $g>0$. On $U\setminus\{0\}$ (which is open and
 non-empty), $\|x\|^2>0$ and $g(x)>0$, so

$$\mu := \mathbb{E}[X_1]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^m}\|x\|^2 g(x)\,dx \ge \int_{U\setminu
s\{0\}}\|x\|^2 g(x)\,dx > 0,$$

where $\mu\in(0,+\infty]$ (the integral may diverge).

**The sum diverges almost surely.** We show $S_n\to\infty$ a.s. in both cases:

- *Finite mean* ($0<\mu<\infty$): By the Strong Law of Large Numbers, $S_n/n\to\
mu>0$ a.s., hence $S_n\to\infty$ a.s.

- *Infinite mean* ($\mu=+\infty$): For any $M>0$, set $X_k^{(M)}=X_k\,\mathbf{1}
_{\{X_k\le M\}}$. Then $\mathbb{E}[X_k^{(M)}]=\mu_M<\infty$ and $\mu_M\to\infty$
 as $M\to\infty$. By SLLN, $S_n^{(M)}/n\to\mu_M$ a.s. Since $S_n\ge S_n^{(M)}$,
we get $\liminf_{n\to\infty} S_n/n\ge\mu_M$ a.s. for every $M$. Letting $M\to\in
fty$ gives $S_n/n\to\infty$ a.s., so $S_n\to\infty$ a.s.

**Conclusion.** The events $\{S_n\le r^2\}$ are decreasing (since $S_n$ is non-d
ecreasing in $n$), so by continuity of probability from above,

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}J_n(r)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(S_n\le r^2)=\mathbb{P}\!\l
eft(\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\{S_n\le r^2\}\right)=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sup_{n\ge 1}S
_n\le r^2\right).$$

Since $S_n\to\infty$ a.s., we have $\sup_{n\ge 1}S_n=\infty$ a.s., hence

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}J_n(r)=0.$$
