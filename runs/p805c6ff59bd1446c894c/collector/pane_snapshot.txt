## Proof

**Setup.** Let $M = \{(X, Y) \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times k} \times \mathbb{R}^{n\tim
es k} : X^T Y = I_k\}$, and let $(X_0, Y_0) \in M$ be a point (so $X_0^T Y_0 = I
_k$). The ambient space has dimension $2nk$.

**The constraint map and its differential.** Define the smooth map
$$F: \mathbb{R}^{n\times k} \times \mathbb{R}^{n\times k} \to \mathbb{R}^{k\time
s k}, \qquad F(X,Y) = X^T Y.$$
Then $M = F^{-1}(I_k)$. The tangent space $T_{(X_0,Y_0)} M$ is the kernel of the
 differential
$$dF_{(X_0,Y_0)}(\dot X, \dot Y) = \dot X^T Y_0 + X_0^T \dot Y,$$
provided $I_k$ is a regular value of $F$ at $(X_0, Y_0)$, i.e., provided $dF_{(X
_0,Y_0)}$ is surjective.

**Surjectivity of the differential.** Since $X_0^T Y_0 = I_k$, the matrix $Y_0$
has full column rank $k$ (its $k$ columns are linearly independent in $\mathbb{R
}^n$). I claim the partial map $\dot X \mapsto \dot X^T Y_0$ alone is already su
rjective onto $\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}$.

Indeed, let $Y_0 = QR$ be a QR-type factorization where $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{n\tim
es k}$ has orthonormal columns and $R \in \mathbb{R}^{k\times k}$ is invertible
(possible since $Y_0$ has rank $k$). For any target $k\times k$ matrix $M$, set
$\dot X = Q\, M^T\, R^{-T}$. Then
$$\dot X^T Y_0 = (Q M^T R^{-T})^T (Q R) = R^{-1} M\, Q^T Q\, R = R^{-1} M\, R \c
dot \ldots$$

More directly: the $(i,j)$-entry of $\dot X^T Y_0$ is $\langle (\dot X)_{\cdot i
},\, (Y_0)_{\cdot j}\rangle$, the inner product of the $i$-th column of $\dot X$
 with the $j$-th column of $Y_0$. Since the columns of $Y_0$ are linearly indepe
ndent, we can choose each column $(\dot X)_{\cdot i} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ to realiz
e any desired values of $\langle (\dot X)_{\cdot i}, (Y_0)_{\cdot j}\rangle$ for
 $j=1,\dots,k$ (e.g., place $(\dot X)_{\cdot i}$ in the column span of $Y_0$ and
 solve a nonsingular $k\times k$ system). Thus $\dot X^T Y_0$ ranges over all of
 $\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}$, confirming surjectivity.

**Dimension of the tangent space.** Since $dF_{(X_0,Y_0)}: \mathbb{R}^{2nk} \to
\mathbb{R}^{k^2}$ is surjective, by the Regular Value Theorem $M$ is a smooth ma
nifold near $(X_0, Y_0)$ of dimension
$$\dim M = \dim(\text{ambient}) - \dim(\text{target}) = 2nk - k^2,$$
and the tangent space at $(X_0, Y_0)$ has the same dimension.

$$\boxed{\dim T_{(X_0,Y_0)} M = 2nk - k^2.}$$
