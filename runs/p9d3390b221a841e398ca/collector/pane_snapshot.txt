## Solution

We apply the **argument principle**:

$$I = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_C \frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}\,dz = N - P,$$

where $N$ = number of zeros of $f$ inside $C$ (counting multiplicity), and $P$ =
 number of poles of $f$ inside $C$ (counting order), with $C: |z|=\pi$.

Here
$$f(z) = \frac{1+z^2}{1-\cos 2\pi z}.$$

### Zeros of $f$ (zeros of the numerator, not canceled by the denominator)

$1+z^2=0 \Rightarrow z=\pm i$. Both satisfy $|\pm i|=1<\pi$, so both lie inside
$C$.

Check they are not zeros of the denominator: $1-\cos(2\pi i)=1-\cosh(2\pi)\neq 0
$. Hence $z=\pm i$ are genuine **simple zeros** of $f$.

$$N = 2.$$

### Poles of $f$ (zeros of the denominator, not canceled by the numerator)

$1-\cos 2\pi z = 0 \iff \cos 2\pi z = 1 \iff z=k,\; k\in\mathbb{Z}$.

Inside $|z|<\pi\approx 3.14159$: $k=0,\pm1,\pm2,\pm3$ — that is **7 integer poin
ts**.

None coincide with zeros of the numerator (which are $\pm i$, non-integers), so
all 7 are genuine poles.

**Order of each pole.** Near $z=k$, set $w=z-k$:
$$1-\cos 2\pi z = 1-\cos 2\pi w = 2\sin^2(\pi w) = 2\pi^2 w^2 + O(w^4).$$

So each zero of the denominator has order $2$, giving a **pole of order 2** at e
ach integer $k$.

$$P = 7\times 2 = 14.$$

### Result

$$I = N - P = 2 - 14 = \boxed{-12}.$$
