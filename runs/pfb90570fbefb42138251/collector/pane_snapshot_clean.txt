## Proof

Let $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ with $\det(A) = 1$. Let $\sigma_1, \dots, \s
igma_n > 0$ be the singular values of $A$.

**Key facts.** The Frobenius norm satisfies
$$\|A\|^2 = \operatorname{Tr}(A^T A) = \sum_{i=1}^n \sigma_i^2,$$
and the product of the singular values equals $|\det A|$:
$$\prod_{i=1}^n \sigma_i = |\det A| = 1.$$

**Applying AM–GM.** By the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality applied to the p
ositive numbers $\sigma_1^2, \dots, \sigma_n^2$:
$$\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \sigma_i^2 \;\ge\; \left(\prod_{i=1}^n \sigma_i^2\righ
t)^{1/n} = \left(\prod_{i=1}^n \sigma_i\right)^{2/n} = 1^{2/n} = 1.$$

Hence
$$\|A\|^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n \sigma_i^2 \;\ge\; n,$$
so $\|A\| \ge \sqrt{n}$ for every special linear matrix.

**Equality condition.** Equality in AM–GM holds if and only if all the $\sigma_i
^2$ are equal, i.e. $\sigma_1^2 = \cdots = \sigma_n^2$. Combined with $\prod \si
gma_i = 1$, this forces $\sigma_i = 1$ for all $i$. Therefore
$$A^T A = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1^2, \dots, \sigma_n^2) = I_n,$$
so $A$ is orthogonal. Together with $\det(A) = 1$, this means $A \in SO(n)$, i.e
. $A$ is special orthogonal.

**Attainability.** Conversely, every special orthogonal matrix $Q \in SO(n)$ sat
isfies $Q^T Q = I_n$, giving $\|Q\|^2 = \operatorname{Tr}(I_n) = n$, so $\|Q\| =
 \sqrt{n}$.

**Conclusion.** The minimum of $\|A\| = \sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}(A^T A)}$ over al
l matrices with $\det(A) = 1$ is exactly $\sqrt{n}$, and it is attained precisel
y by the special orthogonal matrices. The answer is **yes**.
