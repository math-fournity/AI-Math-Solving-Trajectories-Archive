The Homotopy Extension Property (HEP) for a pair of topological spaces $(X, A)$,
 where $A \subseteq X$, is defined as follows.

**Definition.** The pair $(X, A)$ has the *Homotopy Extension Property* if, for
every topological space $Y$, every continuous map $f : X \to Y$, and every homot
opy
$$F : A \times I \to Y, \qquad I = [0,1],$$
with $F(a, 0) = f(a)$ for all $a \in A$, there exists a homotopy
$$\widetilde{F} : X \times I \to Y$$
such that

1. $\widetilde{F}(x, 0) = f(x)$ for all $x \in X$ (the homotopy extends the init
ial map $f$), and
2. $\widetilde{F}(a, t) = F(a, t)$ for all $a \in A$ and all $t \in I$ (the homo
topy extends $F$ on $A$).

In words: any homotopy of a map $f|_A$ that starts at $f|_A$ can be extended to
a homotopy of $f$ on all of $X$, keeping the initial time slice fixed at $f$.

**Equivalent formulations.**

- **Retraction form.** $(X, A)$ has the HEP if and only if the inclusion $i_0 :
X \times \{0\} \cup A \times I \hookrightarrow X \times I$ admits a retraction
$$r : X \times I \to X \times \{0\} \cup A \times I.$$
Here $X \times \{0\} \cup A \times I$ is given the subspace topology from $X \ti
mes I$. This is the most commonly cited characterization and is independent of t
he choice of target space $Y$.

- **Cofibration form.** The inclusion map $j : A \hookrightarrow X$ is a *cofibr
ation* (in the Hurewicz sense) precisely when $(X, A)$ has the HEP. Thus "HEP pa
ir" and "Hurewicz cofibration" are synonymous.

- **Mapping property form.** The restriction map
$$\rho : \mathrm{Map}(X, Y) \to \mathrm{Map}(A, Y), \qquad \rho(g) = g|_A,$$
together with the evaluation-at-$0$ maps, has the following lifting property: ev
ery commutative square
$$\require{AMScd}
\begin{CD}
A @>{F}>> \mathrm{Map}(I, Y)\\
@V{j}VV @V{\mathrm{ev}_0}VV\\
X @>{f}>> Y
\end{CD}$$
admits a diagonal lift $\widetilde{F} : X \to \mathrm{Map}(I, Y)$ (with the comp
act-open topology on mapping spaces, under suitable hypotheses).

**Remark on the subspace $X \times \{0\} \cup A \times I$.** This subspace is th
e "mapping cylinder-like" region obtained by attaching the cylinder over $A$ to
$X$ at time $0$. The existence of a retraction $r$ onto it is what guarantees th
at any partial homotopy on $A$ can be pushed out to a homotopy on all of $X$.

**Standard example.** If $A$ is a subcomplex of a CW-complex $X$, then $(X, A)$
has the HEP. In particular, the pair $(X, \{x_0\})$ formed by a pointed CW-compl
ex has the HEP, which is the setting in which the HEP is most frequently applied
 in homotopy theory (e.g., in defining the cofiber sequence and in proving prope
rties of homotopy groups).
