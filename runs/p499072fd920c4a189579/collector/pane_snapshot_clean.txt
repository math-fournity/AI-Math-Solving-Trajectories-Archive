## Solution

We compute $E(W_6 \mid W_2, W_4)$ for a standard Brownian motion $W$.

**Key property.** Brownian motion has independent increments: for any disjoint t
ime intervals, the increments are independent. In particular, the increment $W_6
 - W_4$ over $[4,6]$ is independent of $\sigma(W_t : t \le 4)$, and hence indepe
ndent of $\sigma(W_2, W_4)$.

**Decomposition.** Write
$$W_6 = W_4 + (W_6 - W_4).$$

Taking conditional expectation with respect to $\sigma(W_2, W_4)$:
$$E(W_6 \mid W_2, W_4) = E\bigl(W_4 \mid W_2, W_4\bigr) + E\bigl(W_6 - W_4 \mid
W_2, W_4\bigr).$$

**First term.** Since $W_4$ is $\sigma(W_2, W_4)$-measurable,
$$E\bigl(W_4 \mid W_2, W_4\bigr) = W_4.$$

**Second term.** Since $W_6 - W_4$ is independent of $\sigma(W_2, W_4)$, the con
ditional expectation reduces to the unconditional expectation:
$$E\bigl(W_6 - W_4 \mid W_2, W_4\bigr) = E(W_6 - W_4) = 0,$$
because $W_6 - W_4 \sim N(0, 2)$ has mean $0$.

**Combining:**
$$E(W_6 \mid W_2, W_4) = W_4 + 0 = W_4.$$

### Verification (Gaussian projection viewpoint)

$(W_2, W_4, W_6)$ is jointly Gaussian with mean zero and covariances $\text{Cov}
(W_s, W_t) = \min(s,t)$. The conditional expectation of a Gaussian vector given
a linear sub-observation is the linear regression. Here the conditioning variabl
es are $W_2, W_4$, and we predict $W_6$.

The covariance matrix of $(W_2, W_4, W_6)$ is
$$\Sigma = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 & 4 \\ 2 & 4 & 6 \end{pmatrix}.$$

The conditional mean is $\Sigma_{13}\Sigma_{11}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix} W_2 \\ W_4\e
nd{pmatrix}$ where $\Sigma_{13} = (2, 4)$ and
$$\Sigma_{11} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \Sigma_{11}^
{-1} = \frac{1}{4}\begin{pmatrix} 4 & -2 \\ -2 & 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatri
x} 1 & -1/2 \\ -1/2 & 1/2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Then
$$\Sigma_{13}\Sigma_{11}^{-1} = (2, 4)\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1/2 \\ -1/2 & 1/2 \en
d{pmatrix} = (2\cdot 1 + 4\cdot(-1/2),\; 2\cdot(-1/2) + 4\cdot(1/2)) = (0, 1).$$

So
$$E(W_6 \mid W_2, W_4) = 0\cdot W_2 + 1\cdot W_4 = W_4,$$

confirming the result. (The coefficient on $W_2$ is $0$ because $W_4$ already ca
rries all the information about $W_2$ that is relevant to $W_6$: $W_4 = W_2 + (W
_4 - W_2)$, and $W_4 - W_2$ is independent of $W_2$.)
