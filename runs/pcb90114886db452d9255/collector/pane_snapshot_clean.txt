## Solution

We want to evaluate
$$l = \lim_{k\to\infty}\int_0^k \left(1-\frac{x}{k}\right)^k e^{x/3}\,dx.$$

**Step 1: Pointwise limit of the integrand.**

For each fixed $x\ge 0$, as $k\to\infty$ (so that eventually $k>x$),
$$\left(1-\frac{x}{k}\right)^k \xrightarrow{k\to\infty} e^{-x}.$$
Hence the integrand converges pointwise:
$$\left(1-\frac{x}{k}\right)^k e^{x/3}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty} e^{-x}\cdot e^{x/
3}=e^{-2x/3}.$$

**Step 2: A dominating function (Dominated Convergence).**

Extend the integrand to all of $[0,\infty)$ by defining
$$f_k(x)=\left(1-\frac{x}{k}\right)^k e^{x/3}\,\mathbf{1}_{[0,k]}(x).$$
For $x\in[0,k]$, using $\ln(1-t)\le -t$ for $t\in[0,1)$ with $t=x/k$:
$$k\ln\!\left(1-\frac{x}{k}\right)\le -x \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \left(1-\frac
{x}{k}\right)^k\le e^{-x}.$$
Therefore
$$0\le f_k(x)\le e^{-x}\cdot e^{x/3}=e^{-2x/3},\qquad x\ge 0.$$
The dominating function $g(x)=e^{-2x/3}$ is integrable on $[0,\infty)$:
$$\int_0^\infty e^{-2x/3}\,dx=\frac{3}{2}<\infty.$$

**Step 3: Apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem.**

Since $f_k\to e^{-2x/3}$ pointwise and $|f_k|\le g\in L^1([0,\infty))$, the DCT
gives
$$l=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_0^k\left(1-\frac{x}{k}\right)^k e^{x/3}\,dx=\lim_{k\to
\infty}\int_0^\infty f_k(x)\,dx=\int_0^\infty e^{-2x/3}\,dx.$$

**Step 4: Compute the limit integral.**
$$\int_0^\infty e^{-2x/3}\,dx=\left[-\frac{3}{2}e^{-2x/3}\right]_0^\infty=0-\lef
t(-\frac{3}{2}\right)=\frac{3}{2}.$$

**Conclusion.**
$$\boxed{l=\dfrac{3}{2}}.$$
