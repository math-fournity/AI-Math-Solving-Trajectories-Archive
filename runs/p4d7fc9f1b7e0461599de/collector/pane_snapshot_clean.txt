## Proof

The answer is **no**. We provide an explicit counterexample.

Let $p(n) = \left(n+1\right)\left(n+2\right)\left(n+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\left(n+\
tfrac{5}{2}\right)$.

This is a degree-4 polynomial with rational coefficients and rational roots $-1,
\,-2,\,-\tfrac{1}{2},\,-\tfrac{5}{2}$, all negative, so $p(n)\neq 0$ for every p
ositive integer $n$.

---

### Partial fraction decomposition

Since $p$ is monic with distinct roots $r_1=-1,\; r_2=-2,\; r_3=-\tfrac{1}{2},\;
 r_4=-\tfrac{5}{2}$, we have
$$\frac{1}{p(n)}=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\frac{A_i}{n-r_i}, \qquad A_i=\frac{1}{\prod_{j\n
eq i}(r_i-r_j)}.$$

Computing each $A_i$:

| $i$ | $r_i$ | $\prod_{j\neq i}(r_i-r_j)$ | $A_i$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $-1$ | $(1)(-\tfrac12)(\tfrac32)=-\tfrac34$ | $-\tfrac43$ |
| 2 | $-2$ | $(-1)(-\tfrac32)(\tfrac12)=\tfrac34$ | $\tfrac43$ |
| 3 | $-\tfrac12$ | $(\tfrac12)(\tfrac32)(2)=\tfrac32$ | $\tfrac23$ |
| 4 | $-\tfrac52$ | $(-\tfrac32)(-\tfrac12)(-2)=-\tfrac32$ | $-\tfrac23$ |

One verifies $\sum A_i=0$ and $\sum A_i r_i=0$ (automatic for degree $\ge 3$).

---

### Digamma preliminaries

Let $\psi(z)=\frac{d}{dz}\ln\Gamma(z)$. We use:
- $\psi(n)=H_{n-1}-\gamma$ for positive integers $n$,
- $\psi(n+\tfrac12)=-\gamma-2\ln 2+2\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{2k+1}$ for non-nega
tive integers $n$,
- $\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n+a}=\psi(N+1+a)-\psi(1+a)$, with $\psi(N+1+a)\sim\ln
N$.

Since $\sum A_i=0$ (degree $\ge 2$) and $\sum A_i r_i=0$ (degree $\ge 3$), the l
ogarithmically divergent parts cancel in both sums below, giving:

$$S_1:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p(n)}=-\sum_{i=1}^{4}A_i\,\psi(1-r_i),$$

$$S_2:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n}{p(n)}=-\sum_{i=1}^{4}A_i\,r_i\,\psi(1-r_i),$$

where the second formula uses $\frac{n}{n-r_i}=1+\frac{r_i}{n-r_i}$ and $\sum A_
i=0$.

The arguments $1-r_i$ are $2,\;3,\;\tfrac32,\;\tfrac72$, with values:

$$\psi(2)=1-\gamma,\quad \psi(3)=\tfrac32-\gamma,\quad \psi(\tfrac32)=2-\gamma-2
\ln 2,\quad \psi(\tfrac72)=\tfrac{46}{15}-\gamma-2\ln 2.$$

---

### Computing $S_1$

$$S_1=\tfrac43\psi(2)-\tfrac43\psi(3)-\tfrac23\psi(\tfrac32)+\tfrac23\psi(\tfrac
72).$$

- **$\gamma$-coefficient:** $\tfrac43-\tfrac43+\tfrac23-\tfrac23=0$. ✓
- **$\ln 2$-coefficient:** $0+0+\tfrac23\cdot 2-\tfrac23\cdot 2=0$. ✓
- **Rational part:** $\tfrac43(1)-\tfrac43(\tfrac32)-\tfrac23(2)+\tfrac23(\tfrac
{46}{15})=\tfrac43-2-\tfrac43+\tfrac{92}{45}=-2+\tfrac{92}{45}=\tfrac{2}{45}$.

$$\boxed{S_1=\frac{2}{45}\in\mathbb{Q}.}$$

---

### Computing $S_2$

$$S_2=-\tfrac43\psi(2)+\tfrac83\psi(3)+\tfrac13\psi(\tfrac32)-\tfrac53\psi(\tfra
c72).$$

- **$\gamma$-coefficient:** $\tfrac43-\tfrac83-\tfrac13+\tfrac53=0$. ✓
- **$\ln 2$-coefficient:** $0+0-\tfrac13\cdot 2+\tfrac53\cdot 2=-\tfrac23+\tfrac
{10}{3}=\tfrac83$.
- **Rational part:** $-\tfrac43(1)+\tfrac83(\tfrac32)+\tfrac13(2)-\tfrac53(\tfra
c{46}{15})=-\tfrac43+4+\tfrac23-\tfrac{46}{9}=-\tfrac{16}{9}$.

$$S_2=-\frac{16}{9}+\frac{8}{3}\ln 2.$$

---

### Irrationality of $S_2$

By the **Lindemann–Weierstrass theorem**: if $\alpha$ is a nonzero algebraic num
ber, then $e^{\alpha}$ is transcendental. If $\ln 2$ were algebraic and nonzero,
 then $e^{\ln 2}=2$ would be transcendental—a contradiction. Hence $\ln 2$ is tr
anscendental, so $\frac{8}{3}\ln 2$ is irrational, and $S_2=-\frac{16}{9}+\frac{
8}{3}\ln 2\notin\mathbb{Q}$.

---

### Conclusion

The polynomial $p(n)=(n+1)(n+2)(n+\tfrac12)(n+\tfrac52)$ satisfies all the hypot
heses—rational coefficients, rational roots, degree $\ge 3$—and $\sum_{n=1}^{\in
fty}\frac{1}{p(n)}=\frac{2}{45}\in\mathbb{Q}$, yet

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n}{p(n)}=-\frac{16}{9}+\frac{8}{3}\ln 2\notin\mathbb{
Q}.$$

Therefore, we **cannot** conclude that $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n}{p(n)}\in\mat
hbb{Q}$.
