# Problem

A high-security communications network consists of 2010 satellite stations positioned as the vertices of a convex polygon in orbit. Due to interference constraints, no three potential communication beams (the internal diagonals of the polygon) intersect at the same point in space.

A specialized signal packet must be routed to form a "Hamiltonian Data Loop." This loop must satisfy three conditions:
1. It must consist of exactly 2010 unique communication beams (diagonals of the polygon).
2. It must not use any of the 2010 direct perimeter links (edges) between adjacent stations.
3. It must visit every satellite station exactly once before returning to its starting station to close the circuit.

Engineers are concerned about signal degradation caused by "data collisions," which occur every time two communication beams in the loop physically cross each other in space. 

Based on the geometric constraints of the 2010 stations, what is the maximum possible number of signal crossings that can exist within such a closed data loop?
