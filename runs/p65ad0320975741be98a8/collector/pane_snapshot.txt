## Proof

**Setup.** Let the bank balance be modeled by
$$X(t) = x + \mu t + \sigma W(t), \qquad t \geq 0,$$
where $x > 0$ is the initial balance, $\mu > 0$ is the positive drift, $\sigma >
 0$ is the volatility, and $W(t)$ is a standard Brownian motion. Define the firs
t passage time
$$\tau = \inf\{t \geq 0 : X(t) \leq 0\}.$$
Since $X(t)$ has continuous sample paths, $\tau = \inf\{t \geq 0 : X(t) = 0\}$.

**Goal.** Compute $E[\tau]$.

---

**Step 1: Construct an exponential martingale.**

Consider $M(t) = \exp\!\bigl(-\lambda\, X(t)\bigr)$ for a constant $\lambda > 0$
 to be chosen. Since $X(t) = x + \mu t + \sigma W(t)$,

$$M(t) = \exp\!\Bigl(-\lambda x - \lambda\mu\, t - \lambda\sigma\, W(t)\Bigr).$$

Using $E[e^{-\lambda\sigma W(t)}] = e^{\frac{1}{2}\lambda^2\sigma^2 t}$,

$$E[M(t)] = \exp\!\Bigl(-\lambda x + \bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda^2\sigma^2 - \lamb
da\mu\bigr)\,t\Bigr).$$

Setting the drift to zero gives $\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda^2\sigma^2 - \lambda\mu = 0$
, i.e., $\lambda = \frac{2\mu}{\sigma^2}$. With this choice, $M(t) = \exp\!\bigl
(-\frac{2\mu}{\sigma^2}X(t)\bigr)$ is a martingale with $M(0) = e^{-2\mu x/\sigm
a^2}$.

---

**Step 2: Apply optional stopping to compute $P(\tau < \infty)$.**

Apply the optional stopping theorem to the bounded stopping time $\tau \wedge T$
:

$$E\bigl[M(\tau \wedge T)\bigr] = M(0) = e^{-2\mu x/\sigma^2}.$$

Split the expectation:

$$E\bigl[M(\tau \wedge T)\bigr] = E\bigl[M(\tau)\,\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau \leq T\}}\b
igr] + E\bigl[M(T)\,\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau > T\}}\bigr].$$

- On $\{\tau \leq T\}$: $X(\tau) = 0$, so $M(\tau) = e^{0} = 1$.
- On $\{\tau > T\}$: $X(T) \geq 0$ (the process hasn't hit zero yet), so $M(T) =
 e^{-2\mu X(T)/\sigma^2} \leq 1$.

Thus:
$$e^{-2\mu x/\sigma^2} = P(\tau \leq T) + E\bigl[M(T)\,\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau > T\}}
\bigr].$$

Now let $T \to \infty$:

- $P(\tau \leq T) \to P(\tau < \infty)$.
- On the event $\{\tau = \infty\}$, the strong law for Brownian motion with drif
t gives $\frac{X(t)}{t} \to \mu > 0$ a.s., hence $X(t) \to +\infty$ and $M(t) \t
o 0$. Since $0 \leq M(T)\,\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau > T\}} \leq 1$ and $M(T)\,\mathbf{1
}_{\{\tau > T\}} \to 0$ a.s., by dominated convergence the second term vanishes.

Therefore:
$$\boxed{P(\tau < \infty) = e^{-2\mu x/\sigma^2} < 1.}$$

---

**Step 3: Conclude $E[\tau] = \infty$.**

Since $\mu > 0$ and $x > 0$, we have $P(\tau = \infty) = 1 - e^{-2\mu x/\sigma^2
} > 0$.

For any $K > 0$:
$$E[\tau] \geq E\bigl[\tau\,\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau > K\}}\bigr] \geq K\, P(\tau > K)
 \geq K\, P(\tau = \infty).$$

Letting $K \to \infty$:
$$E[\tau] \geq \lim_{K\to\infty} K\, P(\tau = \infty) = +\infty,$$

since $P(\tau = \infty) > 0$ is a fixed positive constant.

---

**Conclusion.** The expected time until the balance first becomes negative is **
infinite**. The intuitive reason is that the positive drift $\mu > 0$ gives a st
rictly positive probability $1 - e^{-2\mu x/\sigma^2}$ that the balance never re
aches zero, and this atom of positive mass at $\tau = \infty$ forces the expecta
tion to diverge.
