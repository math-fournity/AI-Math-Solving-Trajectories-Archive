## Proof

**Claim.** There exists a topological manifold that is not a smooth manifold (i.
e., a topological manifold admitting no smooth structure).

**Answer: Yes.** We exhibit the **E₈ manifold** as an explicit example.

---

### Step 1 — The E₈ form

The E₈ root lattice yields an **even, positive definite, unimodular** symmetric
bilinear form $Q$ on $\mathbb{Z}^8$ (the Cartan matrix of the $E_8$ root system)
. This form has signature $8$.

**Key property:** $Q$ is **not diagonalizable over $\mathbb{Z}$**. Indeed, $Q$ i
s *even* (meaning $Q(x,x) \equiv 0 \pmod{2}$ for all $x \in \mathbb{Z}^8$), wher
eas any positive definite unimodular form diagonalizable over $\mathbb{Z}$ is is
omorphic to the identity matrix $I_8$, which is *odd* ($Q(e_i, e_i) = 1$). Hence
 $Q \not\cong I_8$.

---

### Step 2 — Realization as a topological manifold (Freedman, 1982)

**Freedman's Classification Theorem.** Every unimodular symmetric bilinear form
is realized as the intersection form of a simply-connected, closed, oriented **t
opological** 4-manifold. Moreover, for even forms, the manifold is unique up to
homeomorphism.

Applying this to the E₈ form: there exists a simply-connected, closed, oriented
topological 4-manifold $M$ (called the **E₈ manifold**) whose intersection form
$\lambda_M$ is the E₈ form $Q$.

By construction, $M$ is Hausdorff, second-countable, and locally homeomorphic to
 $\mathbb{R}^4$ — a genuine topological manifold.

---

### Step 3 — Obstruction to smoothability (Donaldson, 1983)

**Donaldson's Theorem.** Let $N$ be a **smooth**, simply-connected, closed, orie
nted 4-manifold whose intersection form is **positive definite**. Then the inter
section form of $N$ is **diagonalizable over $\mathbb{Z}$** (equivalently, isomo
rphic to $I_n$ for some $n$).

---

### Step 4 — Contradiction

Suppose, for contradiction, that $M$ admitted a smooth structure. Then $M$ would
 be:
- smooth ✓,
- simply-connected ✓ (by construction),
- closed and oriented ✓ (by construction),
- with positive definite intersection form $Q = \lambda_M$ ✓ (the E₈ form is pos
itive definite).

By Donaldson's theorem, $Q$ must be diagonalizable over $\mathbb{Z}$. But by Ste
p 1, the E₈ form is **not** diagonalizable over $\mathbb{Z}$. **Contradiction.**

Therefore, $M$ admits **no** smooth structure.

---

### Conclusion

The E₈ manifold $M$ is a topological manifold (a simply-connected, closed, orien
ted topological 4-manifold) that does not admit any smooth structure. Hence $M$
is a topological manifold that is not a smooth manifold.

**Remark.** In dimensions 1, 2, and 3, every topological manifold *does* admit a
 (essentially unique) smooth structure (Rado for dim 2, Moise for dim 3, element
ary for dim 1). Thus dimension 4 is the lowest dimension in which non-smoothable
 topological manifolds arise. Other examples exist in dimensions $\geq 5$ (e.g.,
 Kervaire's 10-dimensional non-smoothable manifold from 1960, and manifolds with
 non-vanishing Kirby–Siebenmann invariant), but the E₈ manifold is the most cele
brated instance.
