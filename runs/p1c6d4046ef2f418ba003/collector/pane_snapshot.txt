## Solution

**Claim.** The minimum number of days is **2**.

### Setup and notation

Snow White has 7 dwarfs and 2021 apples, exactly one poisoned. Each day she choo
ses, for each living dwarf, a salad (a subset of apples). At day's end, a dwarf
dies if and only if his salad contained the poisoned apple. Dead dwarfs cannot b
e used on subsequent days. Snow White may adapt her strategy based on all prior
outcomes.

Let $f(d,n)$ denote the maximum number of apples that can be **guaranteed** dist
inguishable using $d$ days and $n$ dwarfs.

### Recurrence

On the first day with $n$ dwarfs, Snow White assigns each apple to a subset $S\s
ubseteq[n]$ of dwarfs (those who will eat it). If the poisoned apple is assigned
 to subset $S$, then exactly the dwarfs in $S$ die, leaving $n-|S|$ dwarfs for t
he remaining $d-1$ days. The apples assigned to subset $S$ must therefore be dis
tinguishable using $d-1$ days and $n-|S|$ dwarfs, so at most $f(d-1,\,n-|S|)$ ap
ples can be assigned to $S$. Optimizing over the partition:

$$f(d,n)=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]} f(d-1,\,n-|S|)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\,f(d-1,i
),$$

where $i=n-|S|$ is the number of surviving dwarfs. The base case is $f(0,n)=1$ f
or all $n$ (with zero days, only a single candidate can be resolved).

### Closed form: $f(d,n)=(d+1)^n$

We prove $f(d,n)=(d+1)^n$ by induction on $d$.

- **Base:** $f(0,n)=1=(0+1)^n$. ✓
- **Step:** Assuming $f(d-1,i)=d^{\,i}$,

$$f(d,n)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\,d^{\,i}=(d+1)^n$$

by the binomial theorem. ✓

### Applying to $n=7$

| Days $d$ | Capacity $(d+1)^7$ |
|----------|-------------------|
| 1        | $2^7 = 128$       |
| 2        | $3^7 = 2187$      |

### Lower bound: 1 day is insufficient

With a single day, each apple is assigned to a subset of the 7 dwarfs. The obser
vation is precisely which dwarfs die — i.e., the subset assigned to the poisoned
 apple. Two apples assigned the same subset are indistinguishable, so at most $2
^7=128$ apples can be handled. Since $128 < 2021$, one day cannot suffice.

### Upper bound: 2 days suffice

Since $f(2,7)=3^7=2187\ge 2021$, two days suffice. Explicitly:

- **Day 1.** Partition the 2021 apples into groups $G_S$ indexed by subsets $S\s
ubseteq[7]$, placing at most $2^{7-|S|}$ apples in group $G_S$ (this is feasible
 since $\sum_S 2^{7-|S|}=3^7=2187\ge 2021$). Dwarf $j$ eats all apples in groups
 $G_S$ with $j\in S$.
- **Outcome of Day 1.** The dwarfs in some subset $S^\*$ die — precisely those w
hose salads contained the poisoned apple. The poisoned apple lies in $G_{S^\*}$,
 which has at most $2^{7-|S^\*|}=2^{k}$ apples, where $k=7-|S^\*|$ dwarfs surviv
e.
- **Day 2.** With $k$ surviving dwarfs and at most $2^k$ candidate apples, perfo
rm standard binary group testing: label candidates with distinct $k$-bit strings
; dwarf $j$ eats candidate $a$ iff bit $j$ of $a$'s label is 1. The set of dwarf
s dying on Day 2 reveals the exact label of the poisoned apple.

This identifies the poisoned apple in at most 2 days for every possible outcome.

### Conclusion

One day handles at most $128 < 2021$ apples, while two days handle up to $2187 \
ge 2021$ apples. Therefore the minimum number of days Snow White needs is

$$\boxed{2}.$$
