**Claim:** $\chi(M) = 0$.

**Proof.**

Assume $M$ is a compact smooth manifold (so that the Euler characteristic is def
ined and the Poincaré–Hopf theorem applies). The $S^1$-action $S^1 \times M \to
M$ is smooth and free.

**Step 1: Construct a nowhere-vanishing vector field.**

Let $\mathfrak{s}^1 \cong \mathbb{R}$ be the Lie algebra of $S^1$, with generato
r $\frac{d}{d\theta}$ corresponding to the standard parameterization $e^{i\theta
}$. The infinitesimal generator of the action is the vector field $X \in \Gamma(
TM)$ defined by
$$X(p) = \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0} \exp(t \tfrac{d}{d\theta}) \cdot p.$$

Since the action is **free**, no point $p \in M$ is fixed by any non-identity el
ement of $S^1$. In particular, the orbit map $S^1 \to M$, $g \mapsto g \cdot p$,
 is an injective immersion for every $p$, so its differential at the identity is
 nonzero. This means $X(p) \neq 0$ for all $p \in M$. Thus $X$ is a **nowhere-va
nishing** vector field on $M$.

**Step 2: Apply the Poincaré–Hopf theorem.**

The Poincaré–Hopf theorem states: if $M$ is a compact smooth manifold and $V$ is
 a vector field on $M$ with only isolated zeros, then
$$\chi(M) = \sum_{p:\, V(p)=0} \operatorname{ind}_p(V).$$

Since $X$ has **no** zeros, the sum on the right-hand side is empty, giving
$$\chi(M) = 0. \qquad \blacksquare$$

**Remark (alternative argument via fibrations).** The free $S^1$-action makes $M
 \to M/S^1$ a principal $S^1$-bundle, i.e., a fibration with fiber $S^1$. Since
$S^1$ is connected, the structure group acts on the fiber by maps homotopic to t
he identity, so $\pi_1(M/S^1)$ acts trivially on $H_*(S^1;\mathbb{Q})$. The mult
iplicativity of the Euler characteristic in such a fibration gives
$$\chi(M) = \chi(M/S^1)\cdot \chi(S^1) = \chi(M/S^1)\cdot 0 = 0,$$
confirming the result.
