## Proof

**Setup.** With the particle confined to the plane $\theta = \pi/2$, the line el
ement reduces to

$$
(ds)^2 = c^{2}A(r)\,(dt)^{2} - \frac{(dr)^{2}}{A(r)} - r^{2}(d\phi)^{2},
\qquad A(r) = 1 - \frac{2GM}{c^{2}r}.
$$

The motion is determined by the variational principle $\delta\!\int ds = 0$. Int
roduce an arbitrary parameter $\lambda$ along the curve and write dots for $d/d\
lambda$. Then

$$
\frac{ds}{d\lambda} = \sqrt{\,c^{2}A\,\dot t^{\,2} - \frac{\dot r^{\,2}}{A} - r^
{2}\dot\phi^{\,2}\,}.
$$

The Lagrangian is $L = ds/d\lambda$.

**Cyclic coordinates.** The metric coefficients depend on $r$ only; they are ind
ependent of $t$ and $\phi$. Hence $t$ and $\phi$ are cyclic (ignorable) coordina
tes, and the corresponding canonical momenta are conserved along the stationary
path.

**Momentum conjugate to $t$.**

$$
p_{t} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot t}
     = \frac{c^{2}A\,\dot t}{\sqrt{c^{2}A\,\dot t^{2} - \dot r^{2}/A - r^{2}\dot
\phi^{2}}}
     = c^{2}A\,\frac{dt}{ds}.
$$

Since $t$ is cyclic, $p_{t}$ is a constant of motion. This gives the **first fir
st integral (energy)**:

$$
\boxed{\;c^{2}\!\left(1 - \frac{2GM}{c^{2}r}\right)\frac{dt}{ds} = \text{const.}
\;}
$$

Physically this is the conserved energy per unit rest mass of the particle (the
time-translation Killing vector $\partial_{t}$ yields this conserved quantity).

**Momentum conjugate to $\phi$.**

$$
p_{\phi} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot\phi}
        = \frac{-r^{2}\dot\phi}{\sqrt{c^{2}A\,\dot t^{2} - \dot r^{2}/A - r^{2}\
dot\phi^{2}}}
        = -\,r^{2}\frac{d\phi}{ds}.
$$

Since $\phi$ is cyclic, $p_{\phi}$ is a constant of motion. This gives the **sec
ond first integral (angular momentum)**:

$$
\boxed{\;r^{2}\,\frac{d\phi}{ds} = \text{const.}\;}
$$

Physically this is the conserved angular momentum per unit rest mass (the axial
Killing vector $\partial_{\phi}$ yields this conserved quantity).

**Verification.** Both quantities arise directly from the existence of Killing v
ectors of the Schwarzschild metric: $\partial_{t}$ (stationarity) and $\partial_
{\phi}$ (axial symmetry). For any Killing vector $\xi^{\mu}$, the quantity $g_{\
mu\nu}\,\xi^{\mu}\,u^{\nu}$ (with $u^{\nu}=dx^{\nu}/ds$ the 4-velocity) is conse
rved along a geodesic. For $\xi = \partial_{t}$:

$$
g_{\mu\nu}\,(\partial_{t})^{\mu}\,u^{\nu} = g_{tt}\,\frac{dt}{ds} = c^{2}\!\left
(1-\frac{2GM}{c^{2}r}\right)\frac{dt}{ds},
$$

and for $\xi = \partial_{\phi}$:

$$
g_{\mu\nu}\,(\partial_{\phi})^{\mu}\,u^{\nu} = g_{\phi\phi}\,\frac{d\phi}{ds} =
-r^{2}\frac{d\phi}{ds}.
$$

These reproduce exactly the two first integrals above (up to the irrelevant over
all sign for the second), confirming the result.

**Conclusion.** The two first integrals of motion for a test particle in the Sch
warzschild metric confined to $\theta=\pi/2$ are:

1. **Energy:** $\displaystyle c^{2}\!\left(1-\frac{2GM}{c^{2}r}\right)\frac{dt}{
ds} = \text{const}$
2. **Angular momentum:** $\displaystyle r^{2}\,\frac{d\phi}{ds} = \text{const}$
