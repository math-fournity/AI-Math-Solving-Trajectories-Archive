## Proof

We wish to determine the number of zeros of $p(z) = z^7 + z(z-3)^3 + 1$ inside t
he open disk $B_1(3) = \{z : |z-3| < 1\}$, using Rouché's theorem.

**Setup.** Let $\gamma$ be the boundary circle $|z - 3| = 1$. We split
$$p(z) = \underbrace{z^7}_{f(z)} + \underbrace{\bigl(z(z-3)^3 + 1\bigr)}_{g(z)}.
$$
We will show $|f(z)| > |g(z)|$ for every $z$ on $\gamma$.

**Bounding $|f|$ from below.** For $z$ on $\gamma$, we have $|z - 3| = 1$, so $z
$ lies on the circle of radius $1$ centered at $3$. The point of this circle clo
sest to the origin is $z = 2$ and the farthest is $z = 4$; hence
$$|z| \in [2, 4], \qquad \text{so} \quad |f(z)| = |z|^7 \geq 2^7 = 128.$$

**Bounding $|g|$ from above.** Using $|z-3| = 1$ on $\gamma$,
$$|g(z)| = \bigl|z(z-3)^3 + 1\bigr| \leq |z|\,|z-3|^3 + 1 = |z|\cdot 1 + 1 \leq
4 + 1 = 5.$$

**Comparison.** On $\gamma$,
$$|f(z)| \geq 128 > 5 \geq |g(z)|.$$
Thus $|f| > |g|$ everywhere on the boundary.

**Applying Rouché's theorem.** Since $|f| > |g|$ on $\gamma$, Rouché's theorem g
ives that $f + g = p$ and $f = z^7$ have the same number of zeros (counted with
multiplicity) inside the region bounded by $\gamma$, namely $B_1(3)$.

**Counting zeros of $f(z) = z^7$ in $B_1(3)$.** The only zero of $z^7$ is at $z
= 0$, with multiplicity $7$. But $|0 - 3| = 3 > 1$, so $z = 0$ lies **outside**
$B_1(3)$. Therefore $z^7$ has **$0$** zeros inside $B_1(3)$.

**Conclusion.** By Rouché's theorem, $p(z)$ has the same number of zeros in $B_1
(3)$ as $z^7$, namely

$$\boxed{0}.$$
