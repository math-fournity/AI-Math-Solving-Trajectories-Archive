# Maximum Number of Regions from $n$ Hyperplanes in $\mathbb{R}^d$

## Answer

The maximum number of regions is

$$
R(n,d) \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{d}\binom{n}{k} \;=\; \binom{n}{0}+\binom{n}{1}+\cdots+\
binom{n}{d}.
$$

(With the convention $\binom{n}{k}=0$ for $k>n$, so the sum naturally truncates.
)

## Proof

### 1. Setup and the recurrence

Let $R(n,d)$ denote the maximum number of regions into which $n$ hyperplanes div
ide $\mathbb{R}^d$. We establish a recurrence by inserting hyperplanes one at a
time.

Suppose $n-1$ hyperplanes $H_1,\dots,H_{n-1}$ are already placed, and we add an
$n$-th hyperplane $H_n$. The new hyperplane $H_n$ is itself a copy of $\mathbb{R
}^{d-1}$. Each of the previous hyperplanes $H_i$ intersects $H_n$ in a $(d-2)$-d
imensional affine subspace (a hyperplane *within* $H_n \cong \mathbb{R}^{d-1}$),
 **provided $H_i$ is not parallel to $H_n$**. When the hyperplanes are in *gener
al position* (defined below), all $n-1$ intersections are genuine hyperplanes in
 $H_n$, and they are themselves in general position within $H_n$.

These $n-1$ hyperplanes inside $H_n \cong \mathbb{R}^{d-1}$ divide $H_n$ into so
me number of $(d-1)$-dimensional cells. Each such cell lies in the interior of a
 single region of the existing arrangement of $H_1,\dots,H_{n-1}$, and adding $H
_n$ splits that region into two. Therefore the number of **new** regions created
 equals the number of cells into which $H_n$ is divided, which is at most $R(n-1
,\,d-1)$.

This gives the recurrence (achieved in general position, and an upper bound alwa
ys):

$$
\boxed{R(n,d) \;=\; R(n-1,d) \;+\; R(n-1,\,d-1).}
$$

### 2. Base cases

- **$n=0$:** No hyperplanes, so all of $\mathbb{R}^d$ is one region: $R(0,d)=1$.
- **$d=0$:** $\mathbb{R}^0$ is a single point; any number of "hyperplanes" (whic
h are empty) leaves it as one region: $R(n,0)=1$.
- **$d=1$:** $n$ points (0-dimensional hyperplanes) on a line create $n+1$ inter
vals: $R(n,1)=n+1$.

### 3. Closed form by induction

**Claim.** $\displaystyle R(n,d)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}\binom{n}{k}$ for all $n\ge 0,\;
d\ge 0$.

**Base cases.**
- $R(0,d)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}\binom{0}{k}=\binom{0}{0}=1$. ✓
- $R(n,0)=\sum_{k=0}^{0}\binom{n}{k}=\binom{n}{0}=1$. ✓

**Inductive step.** Assume the formula holds for all smaller values (double indu
ction on $n$ and $d$). Using the recurrence and Pascal's identity $\binom{n-1}{k
}+\binom{n-1}{k-1}=\binom{n}{k}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
R(n,d) &= R(n-1,d)+R(n-1,d-1) \\
&= \sum_{k=0}^{d}\binom{n-1}{k} \;+\; \sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\binom{n-1}{k} \\
&= \binom{n-1}{0} + \sum_{k=1}^{d}\binom{n-1}{k} \;+\; \sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\binom{n-
1}{k} \\
&= \binom{n-1}{0} + \sum_{k=1}^{d}\binom{n-1}{k} \;+\; \sum_{j=1}^{d}\binom{n-1}
{j-1} \quad (j=k+1)\\
&= \binom{n-1}{0} + \sum_{k=1}^{d}\Big[\binom{n-1}{k}+\binom{n-1}{k-1}\Big] \\
&= \binom{n}{0} + \sum_{k=1}^{d}\binom{n}{k} \qquad\text{(Pascal's identity)}\\
&= \sum_{k=0}^{d}\binom{n}{k}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This completes the induction. ✓

### 4. The maximum is attained (general position)

A collection of $n$ hyperplanes in $\mathbb{R}^d$ is in **general position** if
every subset of $k\le d$ of them has intersection of dimension exactly $d-k$ (i.
e., their normal vectors are linearly independent), and every subset of $k>d$ ha
s empty intersection. Such configurations exist: for instance, take $n$ hyperpla
nes whose normal vectors are in general position in $\mathbb{R}^d$ (e.g., chosen
 generically).

Under general position:
- Each new hyperplane $H_n$ is not parallel to any previous $H_i$, so it is cut
by $n-1$ genuine hyperplanes inside $H_n\cong\mathbb{R}^{d-1}$.
- Those $n-1$ hyperplanes are themselves in general position within $H_n$ (a sub
set of $k\le d-1$ of them, together with the constraint of lying in $H_n$, has n
ormal vectors that are the projections of $k$ independent normals onto $H_n$'s d
irection, which remain independent).
- Hence $H_n$ is divided into exactly $R(n-1,d-1)$ cells, each splitting a disti
nct existing region.

So the recurrence's upper bound is **achieved**, confirming the formula gives th
e true maximum.

### 5. Conclusion

$$
\boxed{\,R(n,d)\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{d}\binom{n}{k}\,}
$$

is the maximum number of regions created by $n$ hyperplanes in $d$-dimensional s
pace. $\blacksquare$
