## Proof

**Conformal setup.** The Möbius transformation
$$\phi(z) = \frac{z-1}{z+1}$$
maps the right half-plane $P = \{z : \operatorname{Re} z > 0\}$ conformally onto
 the unit disk $\mathbb{D}$, with $\phi(1) = 0$. Its inverse is $\phi^{-1}(w) =
\frac{1+w}{1-w}$.

**Reduction to Schwarz lemma.** Given $f: P \to B_1(0) = \mathbb{D}$ holomorphic
 with $f(1)=0$, define
$$g = f \circ \phi^{-1} : \mathbb{D} \to \mathbb{D}.$$
Then $g$ is holomorphic, $g(\mathbb{D}) \subseteq \mathbb{D}$, and
$$g(0) = f(\phi^{-1}(0)) = f(1) = 0.$$
By the **Schwarz lemma**, $|g(w)| \le |w|$ for all $w \in \mathbb{D}$.

**Applying at the target point.** We have
$$\phi(2) = \frac{2-1}{2+1} = \frac{1}{3},$$
so
$$f(2) = g(\phi(2)) = g(1/3).$$
Therefore
$$|f(2)| = |g(1/3)| \le |1/3| = \frac{1}{3}.$$

**Sharpness.** The bound is attained by the family of functions
$$f_\theta(z) = e^{i\theta}\,\frac{z-1}{z+1}, \qquad \theta \in \mathbb{R}.$$
Indeed:
- $\frac{z-1}{z+1}$ maps $P$ onto $\mathbb{D}$ (standard Möbius map), and rotati
on by $e^{i\theta}$ preserves $\mathbb{D}$, so $f_\theta: P \to \mathbb{D}$.
- $f_\theta(1) = e^{i\theta}\cdot 0 = 0$.
- $f_\theta(2) = e^{i\theta}\cdot\frac{1}{3}$, giving $|f_\theta(2)| = \frac{1}{
3}$.

This corresponds to $g(w) = e^{i\theta}w$, the extremal functions of the Schwarz
 lemma.

**Conclusion.** The supremum of $|f(2)|$ over all holomorphic $f: P \to \mathbb{
D}$ with $f(1)=0$ is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{3}}.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
