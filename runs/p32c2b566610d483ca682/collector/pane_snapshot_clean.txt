## Solution

**Claim:** Yes. For almost every $y \in \mathbb{R}$, the map $x \mapsto f(x,y)$
is weakly differentiable on $\mathbb{R}^n$, and its weak partial derivative with
 respect to $x_i$ is $(\partial_{x_i} f)(\cdot, y)$.

**Proof.** Since $f \in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R})$, the weak
partial derivatives $\partial_{x_i} f \in L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})$ exist for
$i = 1, \dots, n$, and satisfy the defining relation
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}} f(x,y)\, \partial_{x_i}\Phi(x,y)\, dx\,dy = -\int_{\math
bb{R}^{n+1}} (\partial_{x_i} f)(x,y)\, \Phi(x,y)\, dx\,dy \tag{1}
$$
for every $\Phi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})$.

**Step 1 (Tensor-product test functions).** Fix $i \in \{1,\dots,n\}$, $\varphi
\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$, $\psi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$, and set $\Phi(
x,y) = \varphi(x)\psi(y)$. Then $\partial_{x_i}\Phi = (\partial_{x_i}\varphi)\,\
psi$, and (1) becomes
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}} \psi(y)\!\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} f(x,y)\,\partial_{x_i}\varp
hi(x)\,dx\right] dy
= -\int_{\mathbb{R}} \psi(y)\!\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} (\partial_{x_i}f)(x,y)\,
\varphi(x)\,dx\right] dy.
$$
Define
$$
g_{\varphi,i}(y) := \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} f(x,y)\,\partial_{x_i}\varphi(x)\,dx + \
int_{\mathbb{R}^n}(\partial_{x_i}f)(x,y)\,\varphi(x)\,dx.
$$
Then $\int_{\mathbb{R}} g_{\varphi,i}(y)\,\psi(y)\,dy = 0$ for all $\psi \in C_c
^\infty(\mathbb{R})$.

**Step 2 ($g_{\varphi,i} \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$, hence $g_{\varphi,i} = 0$ a.e.
).** By Fubini, $f \in L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})$ implies $f(\cdot, y) \in L^\i
nfty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ for a.e. $y$, with $\|f(\cdot,y)\|_\infty \le \|f\|_\infty$;
 likewise for $\partial_{x_i} f$. Since $\varphi, \partial_{x_i}\varphi \in L^1(
\mathbb{R}^n)$ (compact support, smooth), $g_{\varphi,i}$ is bounded, hence in $
L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})$. The fundamental lemma of the calculus of variat
ions gives $g_{\varphi,i}(y) = 0$ for a.e. $y$, i.e. there is a null set $N_{\va
rphi,i}$ with
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} f(x,y)\,\partial_{x_i}\varphi(x)\,dx = -\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}(
\partial_{x_i}f)(x,y)\,\varphi(x)\,dx, \qquad y \notin N_{\varphi,i}. \tag{2}
$$

**Step 3 (Removing the dependence on $\varphi$).** Let $\{\varphi_k\}_{k\ge1}$ b
e a countable set that is dense in $C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ in the usual test-
function topology (uniform convergence of all derivatives with supports in a com
mon compact). Set
$$
N := \bigcup_{k\ge1}\bigcup_{i=1}^{n} N_{\varphi_k,i} \;\cup\; \{y : f(\cdot,y)\
notin L^\infty_{\mathrm{loc}} \text{ or } (\partial_{x_i}f)(\cdot,y)\notin L^\in
fty_{\mathrm{loc}}\text{ for some } i\},
$$
a null set. For $y \notin N$, (2) holds for every $\varphi_k$ and every $i$.

Now take an arbitrary $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$. Choose $\varphi_{k
_j} \to \varphi$ in the test-function topology, so $\partial_{x_i}\varphi_{k_j}
\to \partial_{x_i}\varphi$ uniformly with all supports inside a common compact $
K \subset \mathbb{R}^n$. Since $f(\cdot,y) \in L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ and $(\pa
rtial_{x_i}f)(\cdot,y) \in L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ for $y \notin N$,
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} f(x,y)\,\partial_{x_i}\varphi_{k_j}(x)\,dx \to \int_{\mathbb
{R}^n} f(x,y)\,\partial_{x_i}\varphi(x)\,dx,
$$
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}(\partial_{x_i}f)(x,y)\,\varphi_{k_j}(x)\,dx \to \int_{\mathb
b{R}^n}(\partial_{x_i}f)(x,y)\,\varphi(x)\,dx.
$$
Passing to the limit in (2) for $\varphi_{k_j}$ yields (2) for $\varphi$. Thus f
or every $y \notin N$ and every $i$,
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} f(x,y)\,\partial_{x_i}\varphi(x)\,dx = -\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}(
\partial_{x_i}f)(x,y)\,\varphi(x)\,dx \qquad \forall\,\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\ma
thbb{R}^n). \tag{3}
$$

**Step 4 (Conclusion).** Equation (3) is precisely the statement that $x \mapsto
 f(x,y)$ is weakly differentiable on $\mathbb{R}^n$ with weak partial derivative
 $\partial_{x_i}(\cdot,y) = (\partial_{x_i}f)(\cdot,y)$, for every $i = 1,\dots,
n$. This holds for all $y$ outside the null set $N$, i.e. for almost every $y \i
n \mathbb{R}$.

Moreover, for such $y$, $f(\cdot,y) \in L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ and $\partial_{x
_i}f(\cdot,y) \in L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$, so in fact $f(\cdot,y) \in W^{1,\inft
y}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ for a.e. $y$. $\blacksquare$
