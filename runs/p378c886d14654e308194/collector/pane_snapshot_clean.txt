## Proof

**Polynomial encoding over $\mathbb{F}_2$.** For each $n \geq 0$, associate to $
S_n$ the polynomial
$$f_n(x) = \sum_{a \in S_n} x^a \in \mathbb{F}_2[x].$$

**Claim:** $f_{n+1}(x) = (1+x)\,f_n(x)$ for all $n \geq 0$.

*Proof of claim.* By definition, $a \in S_{n+1}$ iff exactly one of $a-1, a$ lie
s in $S_n$. Over $\mathbb{F}_2$, "exactly one" is addition mod 2, so the indicat
or of $a \in S_{n+1}$ is $\mathbf{1}_{a-1 \in S_n} + \mathbf{1}_{a \in S_n} \pmo
d{2}$. Since $S_n$ consists of positive integers, $0 \notin S_n$, so the $a=1$ b
oundary term $\mathbf{1}_{0 \in S_n}$ vanishes. Therefore:
$$f_{n+1}(x) = \sum_{a \geq 1}\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{a-1\in S_n}+\mathbf{1}_{a\in S_n
}\bigr)x^a = \sum_{b\geq 1}\mathbf{1}_{b\in S_n}\,x^{b+1} + f_n(x) = x\,f_n(x)+f
_n(x)=(1+x)\,f_n(x). \quad\checkmark$$

Iterating: $f_n(x) = (1+x)^n\, f_0(x)$ in $\mathbb{F}_2[x]$.

**Key identity (Freshman's dream).** For $N = 2^m$ ($m \geq 0$), the Frobenius e
ndomorphism in characteristic 2 gives
$$(1+x)^{2^m} = 1 + x^{2^m} \quad \text{in } \mathbb{F}_2[x].$$

**Consequence.** For $N = 2^m$:
$$f_N(x) = (1+x)^N f_0(x) = (1+x^N)\,f_0(x) = f_0(x) + x^N f_0(x).$$

The polynomial $f_0(x) + x^N f_0(x)$ encodes the **symmetric difference** $S_0 \
triangle \{N+a : a \in S_0\}$ (addition in $\mathbb{F}_2$ is XOR). When the two
sets $S_0$ and $\{N+a : a\in S_0\}$ are **disjoint**, the symmetric difference e
quals the union.

**Disjointness for large $N$.** Every element of $\{N+a : a \in S_0\}$ is at lea
st $N + \min(S_0)$, while every element of $S_0$ is at most $\max(S_0)$. These s
ets are disjoint whenever
$$N + \min(S_0) > \max(S_0), \quad\text{i.e.,}\quad N > \max(S_0) - \min(S_0).$$

**Conclusion.** Let $M = \max(S_0) - \min(S_0)$ (a fixed non-negative integer si
nce $S_0$ is finite). For every $N = 2^m$ with $2^m > M$, the sets $S_0$ and $\{
N+a:a\in S_0\}$ are disjoint, so:
$$S_N = S_0 \triangle \{N+a:a\in S_0\} = S_0 \cup \{N+a:a\in S_0\}.$$

There are infinitely many powers of 2 exceeding $M$, giving infinitely many vali
d $N$. $\blacksquare$
