## Proof

**Answer: Yes**, the kernel of $l_{H'}$ defines a projective hyperplane in $P(V)
$, although the map $l_{H'}: P(V) \to K$ is **not well-defined** as a function.
The key point is that the *zero set* (kernel) is well-defined even though the ma
p is not.

---

**Step 1: The map $l_{H'}: P(V) \to K$ is not well-defined.**

Recall that $P(V) = (V \setminus \{0\}) / \sim$, where $x \sim \lambda x$ for ev
ery $\lambda \in K^\times$. Thus a representative of a projective point $[x]$ is
 only defined up to a nonzero scalar.

For the proposed map to be well-defined, we would need
$$l_{H'}([\lambda x]) = l_{H'}([x]) \quad \text{for all } \lambda \in K^\times.$
$

But by the given definition,
$$l_{H'}([\lambda x]) = a_1(\lambda x_1) + \cdots + a_n(\lambda x_n) = \lambda\,
(a_1 x_1 + \cdots + a_n x_n) = \lambda\, l_{H'}([x]).$$

For this to equal $l_{H'}([x])$ for **every** $\lambda \in K^\times$, we would n
eed either $l_{H'}([x]) = 0$ for all $[x]$, or $\lambda = 1$ for all $\lambda \i
n K^\times$. Neither holds in general (e.g., take $K = \mathbb{R}$, $\lambda = 2
$, and any $[x]$ with $l_H(x) \neq 0$). Hence $l_{H'}$ is **not** a well-defined
 function $P(V) \to K$.

This is the standard fact that a linear form on $V$ descends to projective space
 only as a *section of a line bundle* (namely $\mathcal{O}(1)$), not as a $K$-va
lued function.

---

**Step 2: The kernel of $l_{H'}$ is nevertheless well-defined as a set.**

Although the value $l_{H'}([x]) \in K$ depends on the choice of representative,
the condition $l_{H'}([x]) = 0$ does **not**:
$$l_{H'}([\lambda x]) = \lambda\, l_{H'}([x]) = 0 \iff l_{H'}([x]) = 0,$$
since $\lambda \neq 0$. Therefore the subset
$$\ker(l_{H'}) := \big\{[x] \in P(V) : a_1 x_1 + \cdots + a_n x_n = 0\big\}$$
is independent of the choice of homogeneous representative and is a well-defined
 subset of $P(V)$.

---

**Step 3: This subset is exactly the projectivization of the hyperplane $H$.**

By definition, $H = \ker(l_H) = \{x \in V : a_1 x_1 + \cdots + a_n x_n = 0\}$, a
 linear subspace of $V$ of codimension $1$ (assuming the linear form $l_H$ is no
nzero, i.e., not all $a_i$ are zero, which is the non-degenerate case for a hype
rplane). Then
$$\ker(l_{H'}) = \big\{[x] \in P(V) : x \in H \setminus \{0\}\big\} = P(H).$$

Since $\dim H = n - 1$, the projective space $P(H)$ has projective dimension $n
- 2$, which is one less than $\dim P(V) = n - 1$. Hence $P(H)$ is a projective s
ubspace of $P(V)$ of codimension $1$, i.e., a **projective hyperplane**.

---

**Conclusion.**

The map $l_{H'}: P(V) \to K$ is not well-defined as a $K$-valued function (it is
 only well-defined up to a nonzero scalar, i.e., as a section of $\mathcal{O}(1)
$). However, its *kernel* — the vanishing locus $\{[x] : a_1 x_1 + \cdots + a_n
x_n = 0\}$ — is well-defined, because the vanishing condition is invariant under
 rescaling of homogeneous coordinates. This kernel equals $P(H) = P(\ker l_H)$,
the projectivization of the linear hyperplane $H \subset V$, and is therefore a
projective hyperplane in $P(V)$.

**So yes: the kernel of $l_{H'}$ defines a projective hyperplane in $P(V)$.**
