# Proof (CMO 1998, Problem 5)

**Answer-leak check.** The problem statement contains only the problem; no solut
ion, sketch, or answer is leaked. Proceeding.

---

Let $m$ be a positive integer and let $(a_n)$ be defined by $a_0=0,\ a_1=m,\ a_{
n+1}=m^2 a_n-a_{n-1}$.

We prove that the nonnegative-integer solutions $(a,b)$ with $a\le b$ of
$$\frac{a^2+b^2}{ab+1}=m^2 \qquad (\star)$$
are exactly the pairs $(a_n,a_{n+1})$ ($n\ge 0$) that are nonnegative with first
 coordinate $\le$ second.

---

### 1. Every $(a_n,a_{n+1})$ is a solution.

Equation $(\star)$ is equivalent to $a^2+b^2=m^2(ab+1)$.

*Base.* $n=0$: $(a_0^2+a_1^2)/(a_0a_1+1)=(0+m^2)/(0+1)=m^2$. ✓
*Base.* $n=1$: $a_2=m^2\cdot m-0=m^3$, and $(m^2+m^6)/(m\cdot m^3+1)=m^2(m^4+1)/
(m^4+1)=m^2$. ✓

*Inductive step.* Suppose $(a_{n-1},a_n)$ and $(a_n,a_{n+1})$ both satisfy $(\st
ar)$; in particular
$$a_n^2+a_{n+1}^2=m^2(a_n a_{n+1}+1). \tag{II}$$
Set $a_{n+2}=m^2 a_{n+1}-a_n$. From (II), $a_{n+1}^2-m^2=m^2 a_n a_{n+1}-a_n^2=a
_n(m^2 a_{n+1}-a_n)=a_n a_{n+2}$. Hence $a_n$ and $a_{n+2}$ are the two roots of
$$x^2-m^2 a_{n+1}\,x+(a_{n+1}^2-m^2)=0,$$
so in particular $a_{n+2}^2+a_{n+1}^2=m^2(a_{n+1}a_{n+2}+1)$. Thus $(a_{n+1},a_{
n+2})$ is a solution. By induction all $(a_n,a_{n+1})$ satisfy $(\star)$.

*Nonnegativity and ordering.* For $m=1$: the sequence is $0,1,1,0,-1,\dots$; the
 pairs with $a\le b$ and nonnegative entries are $(0,1)=(a_0,a_1)$ and $(1,1)=(a
_1,a_2)$. For $m\ge 2$: $a_0=0<a_1=m\le a_2=m^3$, and $a_{n+1}-a_n=(m^2-1)a_n-a_
{n-1}\ge 3a_n-a_{n-1}>0$ once $a_n\ge a_{n-1}\ge 0$; by induction $(a_n)$ is str
ictly increasing and nonnegative, so every $(a_n,a_{n+1})$ has $a_n\le a_{n+1}$
and is nonnegative.

---

### 2. Every solution is some $(a_n,a_{n+1})$.

Let $(a,b)$ be a solution of $(\star)$ with $0\le a\le b$.

**If $a=0$:** $b^2/1=m^2$, so $b=m$ and $(a,b)=(0,m)=(a_0,a_1)$.

**If $a>0$:** View $(\star)$ as a quadratic in $b$:
$$b^2-m^2 a\,b+(a^2-m^2)=0. \tag{Q}$$
The other root is $b'=m^2 a-b$, with $bb'=a^2-m^2$.

*Claim:* $a\ge m$. Suppose for contradiction $0<a<m$. Then $a^2-m^2<0$, so the t
wo roots of (Q) have opposite signs; the positive root is
$$b=\frac{m^2 a+\sqrt{D}}{2},\qquad D=a^2(m^4-4)+4m^2=a^2m^4+4(m^2-a^2).$$
Since $a<m$, we have $4(m^2-a^2)>0$, so $D>a^2m^4$, giving $b>m^2 a$. On the oth
er hand
$$\sqrt{D}=\sqrt{a^2m^4+4(m^2-a^2)}<am^2\sqrt{1+\tfrac{4}{a^2m^2}}<am^2+\tfrac{2
}{am},$$
so $b<\frac{m^2 a+am^2+2/(am)}{2}=m^2 a+\frac{1}{am}\le m^2 a+1$. Thus $m^2 a<b<
m^2 a+1$, an open interval containing no integer — contradiction. Hence $a\ge m$
.

*Descent.* With $a\ge m$ we have $a^2-m^2\ge 0$, and since $b>0$, $b'=(a^2-m^2)/
b\ge 0$. Also
$$b'=\frac{a^2-m^2}{b}<\frac{a^2}{b}\le a \qquad(\text{as } b\ge a),$$
so $0\le b'<a$. Being the other root of (Q), $b'$ is an integer and satisfies $b
'^2+a^2=m^2(ab'+1)$; i.e. $(b',a)$ is again a solution of $(\star)$ with $0\le b
'\le a$ and **strictly smaller** first coordinate.

Iterating, the first coordinate strictly decreases through nonnegative integers,
 so after finitely many steps it reaches $0$, yielding $(0,m)=(a_0,a_1)$.

*Climbing back.* Each descent step reverses as: from $(b',a)=(a_k,a_{k+1})$ we r
ecover
$$(a,b)=\bigl(a,\, m^2 a-b'\bigr)=(a_{k+1},\,m^2 a_{k+1}-a_k)=(a_{k+1},a_{k+2}).
$$
Starting from $(a_0,a_1)$ and climbing back the same number of steps, $(a,b)=(a_
n,a_{n+1})$ for some $n\ge 0$.

---

### 3. Conclusion.

We have shown $(a_n,a_{n+1})$ satisfies $(\star)$ for every $n\ge 0$ (Section 1)
, and conversely every nonnegative-integer solution $(a,b)$ with $a\le b$ equals
 $(a_n,a_{n+1})$ for some $n\ge 0$ (Section 2). Therefore such a pair is a solut
ion of $\dfrac{a^2+b^2}{ab+1}=m^2$ **if and only if** $(a,b)=(a_n,a_{n+1})$ for
some $n\ge 0$. $\blacksquare$
