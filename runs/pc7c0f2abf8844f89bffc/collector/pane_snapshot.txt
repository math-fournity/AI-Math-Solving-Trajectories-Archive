## Solution

We need the smallest prime $p$ such that $p + p^{-1} \equiv 25 \pmod{143}$, wher
e $p^{-1}$ is the modular inverse of $p$ modulo 143.

**Setup.** Since $143 = 11 \times 13$, $p$ must be coprime to 143 (so $p \neq 11
, 13$). Multiplying both sides of $p + p^{-1} \equiv 25$ by $p$:

$$p^2 - 25p + 1 \equiv 0 \pmod{143}.$$

By CRT this splits into:
- $p^2 - 25p + 1 \equiv 0 \pmod{11}$, i.e. $p^2 - 3p + 1 \equiv 0 \pmod{11}$.
- $p^2 - 25p + 1 \equiv 0 \pmod{13}$, i.e. $p^2 + p + 1 \equiv 0 \pmod{13}$.

**Mod 11:** Discriminant $9 - 4 = 5$. Since $4^2 = 16 \equiv 5 \pmod{11}$, the r
oots are $p \equiv (3 \pm 4)\cdot 2^{-1} \pmod{11}$, where $2^{-1}\equiv 6$. Thi
s gives $p \equiv 7\cdot6 = 42\equiv 9$ and $p\equiv (-1)\cdot6 = -6 \equiv 5 \p
mod{11}$.

**Mod 13:** Discriminant $1 - 4 = -3 \equiv 10$. Since $6^2 = 36 \equiv 10 \pmod
{13}$, the roots are $p \equiv (-1 \pm 6)\cdot 2^{-1} \pmod{13}$, where $2^{-1}\
equiv 7$. This gives $p\equiv 5\cdot7=35\equiv 9$ and $p\equiv (-7)\cdot7=-49\eq
uiv 3 \pmod{13}$.

**CRT combinations** (four residue classes mod 143):

| $p \bmod 11$ | $p \bmod 13$ | $p \bmod 143$ |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| 5 | 9 | 126 |
| 5 | 3 | 16 |
| 9 | 9 | 9 |
| 9 | 3 | 42 |

**Finding the smallest prime in each class:**

- $p \equiv 126$: $126$ (even), $269$. Check $269$: not divisible by $2,3,5,7,11
,13$ (and $\sqrt{269}\approx 16.4$). **269 is prime.**
- $p \equiv 42$: $42, 185, 328, 471, 614, 757$ — first prime is $757$.
- $p \equiv 9$: $9, 152, 295, 438, 581, 724, 867, 1010, 1153$ — first prime is $
1153$.
- $p \equiv 16$: $16, 159, 302, \ldots, 1303$ — first prime is $1303$.

The smallest prime among all classes is **$p = 269$**.

**Verification.** $269 \equiv 126 \pmod{143}$. Compute $126^{-1} \pmod{143}$:
- $126 \equiv 5 \pmod{11}$, and $5^{-1}\equiv 9\pmod{11}$.
- $126 \equiv 9 \pmod{13}$, and $9^{-1}\equiv 3\pmod{13}$.
- CRT: $q\equiv 9\pmod{11},\ q\equiv 3\pmod{13}$ gives $q\equiv 42\pmod{143}$.

So $p^{-1}\equiv 42\pmod{143}$, and
$$p + p^{-1} \equiv 126 + 42 = 168 \equiv 25 \pmod{143}. \checkmark$$

The smallest positive prime frequency is $\boxed{269}$.

### PROOF COMPLETE
