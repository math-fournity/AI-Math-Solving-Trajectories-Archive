**Answer: No.** Polynomials (in $z$) are **not** dense in $C(S^1)$ equipped with
 the supremum norm.

---

## Proof

Let $S^1 = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| = 1\}$, and let $\mathcal{P} \subset C(S^1)$
 denote the subalgebra of polynomials in $z$, i.e., functions of the form
$$p(z) = a_0 + a_1 z + a_2 z^2 + \cdots + a_n z^n, \qquad a_k \in \mathbb{C}.$$

We show $\mathcal{P}$ is **not** dense in $C(S^1)$ by exhibiting a continuous fu
nction that cannot be uniformly approximated by polynomials.

### The obstruction: $f(z) = \bar{z}$

Consider $f(z) = \bar{z} = 1/z$ on $S^1$ (using $|z|=1$). This is continuous on
$S^1$.

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a sequence of polynomials $\{p_n\}
 \subset \mathcal{P}$ with
$$\|p_n - f\|_\infty = \sup_{z \in S^1} |p_n(z) - \bar{z}| \to 0.$$

### Integrals of polynomials vanish

For any polynomial $p(z) = \sum_{k=0}^n a_k z^k$, parameterizing $S^1$ by $z = e
^{i\theta}$, $dz = ie^{i\theta}\,d\theta$:
$$\oint_{S^1} p(z)\,dz = \sum_{k=0}^n a_k \oint_{S^1} z^k\,dz = \sum_{k=0}^n a_k
 \int_0^{2\pi} e^{ik\theta}\, ie^{i\theta}\,d\theta = i\sum_{k=0}^n a_k \int_0^{
2\pi} e^{i(k+1)\theta}\,d\theta.$$

For every integer $k \ge 0$, $k+1 \ge 1$, so $\int_0^{2\pi} e^{i(k+1)\theta}\,d\
theta = 0$. Hence
$$\oint_{S^1} p(z)\,dz = 0 \qquad \text{for all } p \in \mathcal{P}.$$

(Equivalently, this is Cauchy's theorem: $p$ is holomorphic inside the disk, so
its contour integral over $S^1$ is zero.)

### Integral of $f(z)=\bar{z}$ is nonzero

$$\oint_{S^1} \bar{z}\,dz = \oint_{S^1} \frac{1}{z}\,dz = \int_0^{2\pi} e^{-i\th
eta}\, ie^{i\theta}\,d\theta = i\int_0^{2\pi} d\theta = 2\pi i \neq 0.$$

### Contradiction via uniform convergence

Since $p_n \to f$ uniformly on $S^1$ and $S^1$ has finite measure,
$$\oint_{S^1} p_n(z)\,dz \;\longrightarrow\; \oint_{S^1} f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i.$$

But every term on the left equals $0$, so the limit must be $0$. This gives $0 =
 2\pi i$, a contradiction.

### Conclusion

The continuous function $f(z) = \bar{z}$ on $S^1$ cannot be uniformly approximat
ed by polynomials in $z$. Therefore $\mathcal{P}$ is **not dense** in $C(S^1)$.

---

**Remark (Stone–Weierstrass perspective).** The complex Stone–Weierstrass theore
m requires the subalgebra to be *self-adjoint* (closed under conjugation). The a
lgebra $\mathcal{P}$ is not self-adjoint: $\overline{z} = z^{-1} \notin \mathcal
{P}$. Indeed, the *trigonometric polynomials* $\sum_{k=-n}^{n} c_k z^k$ (i.e., $
\mathcal{P} + \overline{\mathcal{P}}$) **are** self-adjoint and **are** dense in
 $C(S^1)$ — this is the classical Fourier/Stone–Weierstrass result. The failure
is precisely the absence of the negative-frequency terms.
