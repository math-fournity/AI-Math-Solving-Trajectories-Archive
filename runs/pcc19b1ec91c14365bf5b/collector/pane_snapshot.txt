Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $|\mathbb{G}^H(B) - \mathbb
{G}^L(B)| > 0$ must hold for every half-closed interval $B = (a, b]$ with $0 < \
mathbb{G}^L(B) < 1$, given the stated conditions on $\gamma$.

**Answer: No.** The assertion is **false**. Here is a counterexample.

---

### Counterexample

Let $\mathbb{G}^L$ be Lebesgue measure on $[0,1]$, and define
$$\gamma(r) = 2r, \quad r \in [0,1].$$
Set $\mathbb{G}^H(B) = \int_B \gamma(r)\,\mathbb{G}^L(dr) = \int_B 2r\,dr$.

**Verification of all hypotheses:**

1. **$\mathbb{G}^H$ is a probability measure:** $\mathbb{G}^H([0,1]) = \int_0^1
2r\,dr = 1$. ✓

2. **Same support:** $\mathbb{G}^L$ has support $[0,1]$. Since $\gamma(r) = 2r >
 0$ for all $r \in (0,1]$, every open interval intersecting $(0,1]$ has positive
 $\mathbb{G}^H$-measure, and every neighborhood of $0$ also has positive $\mathb
b{G}^H$-measure (it contains points $r > 0$). So $\operatorname{supp}(\mathbb{G}
^H) = [0,1] = \operatorname{supp}(\mathbb{G}^L)$. ✓

3. **$\gamma$ almost surely positive:** $\gamma(r) = 2r = 0$ only at $r = 0$, an
d $\mathbb{G}^L(\{0\}) = 0$. So $\gamma > 0$ a.s. ✓

4. **$\gamma$ finite:** $2r \leq 2$ on $[0,1]$. ✓

5. **$\gamma \neq 1$ a.s.:** $\gamma(r) = 1$ only at $r = 1/2$, and $\mathbb{G}^
L(\{1/2\}) = 0$. ✓

6. **$\gamma$ increasing:** $2r$ is strictly increasing. ✓

**The violating interval:**

Take $B = (1/4, 3/4]$. Then:
$$\mathbb{G}^L(B) = \tfrac{3}{4} - \tfrac{1}{4} = \tfrac{1}{2}, \quad \text{so }
 0 < \mathbb{G}^L(B) < 1. \quad ✓$$

$$\mathbb{G}^H(B) = \int_{1/4}^{3/4} 2r\,dr = \Big[r^2\Big]_{1/4}^{3/4} = \tfrac
{9}{16} - \tfrac{1}{16} = \tfrac{8}{16} = \tfrac{1}{2}.$$

Therefore:
$$|\mathbb{G}^H(B) - \mathbb{G}^L(B)| = \left|\tfrac{1}{2} - \tfrac{1}{2}\right|
 = 0.$$

---

### Why cancellation is possible

The key observation is that $\gamma - 1 = 2r - 1$ changes sign at $r = 1/2$: it
is negative on $(0, 1/2)$ and positive on $(1/2, 1)$. For an interval $B = (a, b
]$ straddling the crossing point $1/2$, the integral $\int_B (\gamma - 1)\,d\mat
hbb{G}^L$ receives negative contributions from the left part and positive contri
butions from the right part. These can cancel exactly.

More generally, for $\gamma(r) = 2r$ and $B = (a, b]$:
$$\int_a^b (2r - 1)\,dr = (b - a)(a + b - 1),$$
which equals zero whenever $a + b = 1$ (with $a < b$, so $a < 1/2 < b$). This gi
ves a whole family of intervals satisfying $0 < \mathbb{G}^L(B) < 1$ for which $
\mathbb{G}^H(B) = \mathbb{G}^L(B)$.

The monotonicity of $\gamma$ and the condition $\gamma \neq 1$ a.s. only guarant
ee that $\gamma - 1$ has a consistent sign on each side of a single crossing poi
nt. They do **not** prevent the positive and negative parts of $\gamma - 1$ from
 having equal total mass over a symmetric (or suitably chosen) interval that str
addles that crossing point.

---

### Conclusion

The assertion is **false**. The conditions on $\gamma$ (positive, finite, $\neq
1$ a.s., increasing) do not guarantee $|\mathbb{G}^H(B) - \mathbb{G}^L(B)| > 0$
for every half-closed interval $B = (a,b]$ with $0 < \mathbb{G}^L(B) < 1$. A cou
nterexample is $\mathbb{G}^L = $ Lebesgue measure on $[0,1]$, $\gamma(r) = 2r$,
and $B = (1/4, 3/4]$, for which $\mathbb{G}^H(B) = \mathbb{G}^L(B) = 1/2$.
