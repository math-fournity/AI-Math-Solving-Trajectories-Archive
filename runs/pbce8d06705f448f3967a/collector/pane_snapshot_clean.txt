**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

Let $\mathbb{D}=\{z:|z|<1\}$ and $\overline{\mathbb{D}}=\{z:|z|\le 1\}$. Since $
f$ is analytic in $\mathbb{D}$ and continuous on $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$, $f$ be
longs to the disk algebra $A(\mathbb{D})$. We construct an explicit sequence of
polynomials converging uniformly to $f$ on $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$ using the Fej
ér means of the Taylor series.

**Step 1: Taylor coefficients as Fourier coefficients.** Expand $f$ in its Taylo
r series
$$f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_k z^k,\qquad a_k=\frac{f^{(k)}(0)}{k!},$$
which converges uniformly on compact subsets of $\mathbb{D}$. The boundary funct
ion $g(\theta):=f(e^{i\theta})$ is continuous and $2\pi$-periodional on $[0,2\pi
]$. By uniform convergence of the Taylor series on circles $|z|=r<1$ and continu
ity of $f$ up to the boundary, a limiting argument ($r\to 1^-$, using $f$ contin
uous on $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$) shows
$$a_k=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}g(\theta)e^{-ik\theta}\,d\theta,\qquad k\ge 0,$
$
and $a_k=0$ for $k<0$. Thus $\{a_k\}_{k\ge 0}$ are precisely the non-negative Fo
urier coefficients of $g$.

**Step 2: Fejér means on the boundary.** Define the Fejér means (Cesàro means of
 order $n$)
$$\sigma_n(z):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left(1-\frac{k}{n+1}\right)a_k z^k.$$
Each $\sigma_n$ is a polynomial of degree $n$. On the boundary, $\sigma_n(e^{i\t
heta})$ is exactly the $n$-th Fejér mean of the Fourier series of the continuous
 function $g$. By **Fejér's theorem**, since $g$ is continuous and $2\pi$-period
ic,
$$\sigma_n(e^{i\theta})\longrightarrow g(\theta)=f(e^{i\theta})\quad\text{unifor
mly on }[0,2\pi].$$
Equivalently,
$$\max_{|z|=1}|\sigma_n(z)-f(z)|\longrightarrow 0.$$

**Step 3: Extend to the closed disk via the maximum principle.** For each $n$, t
he function $h_n(z):=\sigma_n(z)-f(z)$ is analytic in $\mathbb{D}$ (difference o
f a polynomial and an analytic function) and continuous on $\overline{\mathbb{D}
}$. By the **maximum modulus principle**,
$$\max_{z\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}}|h_n(z)|=\max_{|z|=1}|h_n(z)|.$$
Combining with Step 2,
$$\max_{z\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}}|\sigma_n(z)-f(z)|=\max_{|z|=1}|\sigma_n(z)-f(
z)|\longrightarrow 0.$$

Therefore $\{\sigma_n\}$ is a sequence of polynomials converging uniformly to $f
$ on the closed unit disk $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$.

**Remark.** This also follows from Mergelyan's theorem: $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$
is compact with connected complement $\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}$,
 and $f$ is continuous on $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$ and analytic in its interior,
so $f$ is uniformly approximable by polynomials on $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$. The
Fejér-means argument above gives a constructive proof specific to the disk.
