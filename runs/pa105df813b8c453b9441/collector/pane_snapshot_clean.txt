## Proof

We must show that for every polynomial $H$ of degree at most $3$ and every $T>0$
,

$$\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T} H(t)\,dt \;=\; \frac{1}{2}\!\left[H\!\left(-\tfrac{T
}{\sqrt{3}}\right)+H\!\left(\tfrac{T}{\sqrt{3}}\right)\right].$$

Both sides are **linear functionals** of $H$. Since every polynomial of degree a
t most $3$ is a linear combination of the monomials $\{1,\,t,\,t^{2},\,t^{3}\}$,
 it suffices to verify the identity for each of these four basis elements.

---

**Case $H(t)=1$.**

$$\text{LHS}=\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}1\,dt=\frac{1}{2T}\cdot 2T=1,$$
$$\text{RHS}=\tfrac{1}{2}(1+1)=1.\qquad\checkmark$$

**Case $H(t)=t$.**

$$\text{LHS}=\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}t\,dt=0 \quad(\text{odd integrand over a s
ymmetric interval}),$$
$$\text{RHS}=\tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(-\tfrac{T}{\sqrt{3}}+\tfrac{T}{\sqrt{3}}\right)
=0.\qquad\checkmark$$

**Case $H(t)=t^{2}$.**

$$\text{LHS}=\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}t^{2}\,dt=\frac{1}{2T}\cdot\frac{2T^{3}}{3
}=\frac{T^{2}}{3},$$
$$\text{RHS}=\tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(\tfrac{T^{2}}{3}+\tfrac{T^{2}}{3}\right)=\frac{
T^{2}}{3}.\qquad\checkmark$$

**Case $H(t)=t^{3}$.**

$$\text{LHS}=\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}t^{3}\,dt=0 \quad(\text{odd integrand over
 a symmetric interval}),$$
$$\text{RHS}=\tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(\!\left(-\tfrac{T}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^{\!3}+\!\le
ft(\tfrac{T}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^{\!3}\right)=\tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(-\tfrac{T^{3}}{3\
sqrt{3}}+\tfrac{T^{3}}{3\sqrt{3}}\right)=0.\qquad\checkmark$$

---

All four basis cases agree. By linearity, for any coefficients $a_{0},a_{1},a_{2
},a_{3}$,

$$\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}\!\bigl(a_{0}+a_{1}t+a_{2}t^{2}+a_{3}t^{3}\bigr)\,dt
=\frac{1}{2}\!\left[H\!\left(-\tfrac{T}{\sqrt{3}}\right)+H\!\left(\tfrac{T}{\sqr
t{3}}\right)\right].$$

That is, the average value of $H(t)$ over $[-T,T]$ equals the average of $H\!\le
ft(-\tfrac{T}{\sqrt{3}}\right)$ and $H\!\left(\tfrac{T}{\sqrt{3}}\right)$ for ev
ery polynomial $H$ of degree at most $3$ and every $T>0$. $\blacksquare$
