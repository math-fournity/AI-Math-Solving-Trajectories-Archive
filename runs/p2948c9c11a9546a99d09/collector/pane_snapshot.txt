### Setup

We seek $G_a(x,x_0)$ on $[0,L]$ satisfying

$$G_a''(x,x_0)=\delta(x-x_0),\qquad G_a'(0,x_0)=-c,\quad G_a'(L,x_0)=c,\quad c=\
tfrac12.$$

On each side of $x_0$ the equation is $G''=0$, so $G$ is piecewise linear:

$$G_a(x,x_0)=\begin{cases}A_1 x+B_1,&0\le x<x_0,\\ A_2 x+B_2,&x_0<x\le L.\end{ca
ses}$$

**Boundary conditions** give $A_1=-c=-\tfrac12$ and $A_2=c=\tfrac12$.

**Jump condition** $G'(x_0^+)-G'(x_0^-)=1$: $A_2-A_1=\tfrac12-(-\tfrac12)=1$. ✓
(This is precisely why $c=1/2$.)

**Continuity at $x_0$:** $A_1 x_0+B_1=A_2 x_0+B_2$, i.e. $-\frac{x_0}{2}+B_1=\fr
ac{x_0}{2}+B_2$, giving $B_1-B_2=x_0$.

One free parameter remains. Setting $B_2=g(x_0)$ (arbitrary function of the sour
ce point), we get $B_1=g(x_0)+x_0$, and

$$G_a(x,x_0)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{x_0-x}{2}+g(x_0),&x<x_0,\\[4pt]\dfrac{x-x_0}{2}
+g(x_0),&x>x_0.\end{cases}$$

---

### (a) All possible $G_a(x,x_0)$

The piecewise expression above is compactly written as

$$\boxed{G_a(x,x_0)=\tfrac12|x-x_0|+g(x_0),}$$

where $g(x_0)$ is an **arbitrary function** of $x_0\in[0,L]$.

*Verification.* $G_a'=\pm\tfrac12$ on each side (matching $-c,c$); the jump is $
1$; continuity holds; $G_a''=\delta(x-x_0)$ in the distributional sense. ∎

---

### (b) All symmetric $G_a(x,x_0)$

Symmetry requires $G_a(x,x_0)=G_a(x_0,x)$ for all $x,x_0$:

$$\tfrac12|x-x_0|+g(x_0)=\tfrac12|x_0-x|+g(x).$$

Since $|x-x_0|=|x_0-x|$, this forces $g(x_0)=g(x)$ for all $x,x_0$, i.e. $g$ is
a **constant** $K$.

$$\boxed{G_a^{\mathrm{sym}}(x,x_0)=\tfrac12|x-x_0|+K,\qquad K\in\mathbb{R}.}$$

---

### (c) All $G_a$ orthogonal to $\phi_h(x)$

The homogeneous solution of $u''=0$ with Neumann conditions is $\phi_h(x)=\text{
const}$; we take $\phi_h(x)=1$. Orthogonality means

$$\int_0^L G_a(x,x_0)\,dx=0\quad\forall\,x_0.$$

Compute

$$\int_0^L|x-x_0|\,dx=\int_0^{x_0}(x_0-x)\,dx+\int_{x_0}^L(x-x_0)\,dx=\frac{x_0^
2}{2}+\frac{(L-x_0)^2}{2}=\frac{x_0^2+(L-x_0)^2}{2}.$$

Thus

$$\frac12\cdot\frac{x_0^2+(L-x_0)^2}{2}+g(x_0)\,L=0\;\Longrightarrow\;g(x_0)=-\f
rac{x_0^2+(L-x_0)^2}{4L}.$$

$$\boxed{G_a^{\perp}(x,x_0)=\tfrac12|x-x_0|-\frac{x_0^2+(L-x_0)^2}{4L}.}$$

This is **unique** (no free parameter remains). Note it is *not* symmetric, so s
ymmetry and orthogonality are generally incompatible.

---

### (d) Solvability condition and solution via the Neumann function

**Solvability.** Integrate $u''=f$ over $[0,L]$:

$$u'(L)-u'(0)=\int_0^L f(x)\,dx\;\Longrightarrow\;\boxed{\gamma-\beta=\int_0^L f
(x)\,dx.}$$

This is the Fredholm alternative condition: a solution exists **iff** $\gamma-\b
eta=\int_0^L f$.

**Derivation of the solution.** Apply Green's second identity to $u(x)$ and $G_a
(x,x_0)$ (as a function of the first argument $x$):

$$\int_0^L\!\bigl(u\,G_a''-G_a\,u''\bigr)\,dx=\Bigl[u\,G_a'-G_a\,u'\Bigr]_0^L.$$

The left side is $u(x_0)-\int_0^L G_a(x,x_0)\,f(x)\,dx$. The right side, using $
G_a'(0)=-c$, $G_a'(L)=c$, $u'(0)=\beta$, $u'(L)=\gamma$, is

$$c\,u(L)-\gamma\,G_a(L,x_0)+c\,u(0)+\beta\,G_a(0,x_0)=c\bigl[u(0)+u(L)\bigr]+\b
eta\,G_a(0,x_0)-\gamma\,G_a(L,x_0).$$

Hence

$$u(x_0)=\int_0^L G_a(x,x_0)\,f(x)\,dx+\beta\,G_a(0,x_0)-\gamma\,G_a(L,x_0)+\tfr
ac12\bigl[u(0)+u(L)\bigr].$$

Swapping the names $x\leftrightarrow x_0$ and using the **symmetric** Neumann fu
nction $G_a(x,x_0)=\tfrac12|x-x_0|+K$ from part (b) (so $G_a(\xi,x)=G_a(x,\xi)$)
:

$$\boxed{u(x)=\int_0^L G_a(x,\xi)\,f(\xi)\,d\xi+\beta\,G_a(0,x)-\gamma\,G_a(L,x)
+K_0,}$$

where $K_0=\tfrac12[u(0)+u(L)]$ is an **arbitrary constant** reflecting the non-
uniqueness of the Neumann problem (solutions differ by an additive constant, i.e
. by $\phi_h$).

*Verification.*
- $u''(x)=\int_0^L \delta(x-\xi)f(\xi)\,d\xi+0+0=f(x)$, since $G_a(0,x)=\frac{x}
{2}+K$ and $G_a(L,x)=\frac{L-x}{2}+K$ are linear in $x$ on $[0,L]$.
- $u'(0)=-\frac12\int_0^L f+\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}=\beta$ using $\int_
0^L f=\gamma-\beta$. ✓
- $u'(L)=\frac12\int_0^L f+\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}=\gamma$ using the sa
me condition. ✓
