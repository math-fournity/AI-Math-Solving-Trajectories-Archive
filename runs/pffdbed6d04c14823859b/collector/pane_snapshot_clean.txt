## Proof

**Claim.** The only finite group with exactly two conjugacy classes is the cycli
c group $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.

**Proof.**

Let $G$ be a finite group with exactly two conjugacy classes. In any group, the
identity element $e$ forms a conjugacy class by itself: $\{e\}$, since $geg^{-1}
 = e$ for all $g \in G$.

Since $G$ has exactly two conjugacy classes and one of them is $\{e\}$, all the
remaining non-identity elements must constitute a single conjugacy class. Let $|
G| = n$. Then:

- The conjugacy class $\{e\}$ has size $1$.
- The other conjugacy class $G \setminus \{e\}$ has size $n - 1$.

By the orbit-stabilizer theorem (applied to the conjugation action of $G$ on its
elf), the size of every conjugacy class divides $|G| = n$. Therefore:

$$n - 1 \mid n.$$

Since $n - 1 \mid n$ and $n - 1 \mid (n-1)$, we obtain:

$$n - 1 \mid n - (n-1) = 1.$$

Hence $n - 1 = 1$, so $n = 2$.

Thus $|G| = 2$, and every group of order $2$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/2\math
bb{Z}$.

**Verification.** The group $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} = \{e, a\}$ is abelian, so e
very element is its own conjugacy class. This gives exactly two conjugacy classe
s: $\{e\}$ and $\{a\}$. $\checkmark$

Therefore, the unique finite group with exactly two conjugacy classes is $\boxed
{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}$.
