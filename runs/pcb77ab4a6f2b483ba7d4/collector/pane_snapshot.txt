## Proof

**Claim:** A non-zero holomorphic function $g$ on an annulus $A = \{z : r_1 < |z
| < r_2\}$ (centered at the origin, with $0 \le r_1 < r_2 \le \infty$) satisfyin
g $|g(z)| = |z|^b$ for some real $b$ exists **if and only if $b \in \mathbb{Z}$*
*. In particular, the answer is **yes**: such functions exist, and they are prec
isely $g(z) = c\, z^n$ (up to a unimodular constant) for $b = n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

---

### Existence ($b \in \mathbb{Z}$)

For any integer $n$, the function $g(z) = z^n$ is holomorphic and non-vanishing
on any annulus centered at the origin (for $n \ge 0$ it extends to all of $\math
bb{C}$; for $n < 0$ it is holomorphic on $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}$, which conta
ins the annulus). We have $|g(z)| = |z|^n$, so $b = n$ works. Thus such a $g$ ex
ists for every integer $b$.

---

### Necessity ($b$ must be an integer)

Suppose $g$ is holomorphic and non-vanishing on the annulus $A$ with $|g(z)| = |
z|^b$ for some real $b$.

**Step 1: Structure decomposition.** Since $g$ is holomorphic and non-vanishing
on the annulus $A$, by the standard structure theorem for non-vanishing holomorp
hic functions on an annulus, there exists an integer $k \in \mathbb{Z}$ (the win
ding number of $g$ around any circle $|z|=r$ in $A$) and a single-valued holomor
phic function $h$ on $A$ such that:
$$g(z) = z^k\, e^{h(z)}.$$

**Step 2: Reduce to a harmonic equation.** Taking absolute values:
$$|g(z)| = |z|^k \cdot e^{\operatorname{Re}(h(z))}.$$
The condition $|g(z)| = |z|^b$ yields:
$$e^{\operatorname{Re}(h(z))} = |z|^{b-k}, \qquad \text{hence} \qquad \operatorn
ame{Re}(h(z)) = (b-k)\ln|z|.$$

**Step 3: Compute the period of the harmonic conjugate.** Set $u(z) = (b-k)\ln|z
|$, which is harmonic on $A$. Since $h$ is single-valued and holomorphic, $h = u
 + iv$ where $v = \operatorname{Im}(h)$ is a **single-valued** harmonic conjugat
e of $u$ on $A$.

In polar coordinates $(r, \theta)$, we have $u = (b-k)\ln r$, whose harmonic con
jugate is $v = (b-k)\theta + C$. The change in $v$ upon traversing the circle $|
z| = r$ once counterclockwise is:
$$\Delta v = \oint_{|z|=r} \!\left(-\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\,dx + \frac{\p
artial u}{\partial x}\,dy\right) = \int_0^{2\pi}(b-k)\,d\theta = 2\pi(b-k).$$

**Step 4: Single-valuedness forces $b = k$.** For $v$ (and hence $h$) to be sing
le-valued on $A$, we require $\Delta v = 0$:
$$2\pi(b - k) = 0 \implies b = k.$$

Since $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, we conclude $b \in \mathbb{Z}$.

---

### Conclusion

A non-zero holomorphic function $g$ on an annulus centered at the origin satisfy
ing $|g(z)| = |z|^b$ exists **if and only if $b$ is an integer**. The answer to
the question is **yes**: for every $b = n \in \mathbb{Z}$, the function $g(z) =
z^n$ (on any annulus not containing the origin) provides an example; for non-int
eger $b$, no such function exists, because the harmonic conjugate of $(b-k)\ln|z
|$ has non-zero period $2\pi(b-k) \neq 0$ around the annulus, preventing the exi
stence of a single-valued holomorphic $h$ in the decomposition $g = z^k e^h$.
