## Proof

Let $B$ be a basis of $V$ with $|B| = \alpha$. Since $V$ is infinite-dimensional
, $\alpha$ is an infinite cardinal.

**Step 1: Identify $V^*$ with $\mathbb{F}^B$.**

Every linear functional $f: V \to \mathbb{F}$ is uniquely determined by its valu
es on $B$, and any function $g: B \to \mathbb{F}$ extends uniquely to a linear f
unctional (by linearity). Thus
$$V^* \cong \mathbb{F}^B = \prod_{b \in B} \mathbb{F}$$
as $\mathbb{F}$-vector spaces (with pointwise operations). So $\dim(V^*) = \dim_
{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{F}^B)$.

**Step 2: Compute $|V^*|$.**

$|V^*| = |\mathbb{F}|^{|B|} = |\mathbb{F}|^\alpha$. For $\mathbb{F} = \mathbb{R}

$$|V^*| = (2^{\aleph_0})^\alpha = 2^{\aleph_0 \cdot \alpha} = 2^\alpha,$$

**Step 3: Upper bound.**

$$|V^*| = \max(\dim(V^*),\, |\mathbb{F}|).$$
Since $\alpha \geq \aleph_0$, we have $2^\alpha \geq 2^{\aleph_0} = |\mathbb{F}|
$, so $|V^*| = 2^\alpha \geq |\mathbb{F}|$. This yields
$$\dim(V^*) \leq |V^*| = |\mathbb{F}|^\alpha = 2^\alpha.$$
