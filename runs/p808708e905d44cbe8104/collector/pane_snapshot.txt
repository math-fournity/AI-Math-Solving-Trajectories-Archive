**Answer: Yes, a finite subcollection exists.**

**Proof.**

Let $S_n(T)$ denote the set of all complete $n$-types of $T$. For a formula $\ph
i(\bar{x})$ in $n$ free variables, let
$$A_\phi = \{\Sigma \in S_n(T) : \phi \in \Sigma\}.$$

The hypothesis is that $\{A_{\phi_i}\}_{i \in I}$ covers $S_n(T)$, i.e., every c
omplete $n$-type contains at least one $\phi_i$.

**Step 1: Translate the covering condition into an inconsistency.**

The covering condition says: there is no complete $n$-type $\Sigma$ with $\phi_i
 \notin \Sigma$ for all $i \in I$. Equivalently, there is no complete $n$-type c
ontaining $\{\neg \phi_i : i \in I\}$.

By Lindenbaum's lemma (applied to the Lindenbaum–Tarski algebra of $n$-variable
formulas modulo $T$-provable equivalence), a set of formulas $\Gamma(\bar{x})$ i
s contained in some complete $n$-type of $T$ if and only if $\Gamma$ is *consist
ent with $T$*, meaning every finite subset of $T \cup \Gamma$ is satisfiable.

Since $\{\neg \phi_i : i \in I\}$ is contained in no complete $n$-type, it is **
inconsistent with $T$**.

**Step 2: Apply the Compactness Theorem.**

By the compactness theorem of first-order logic, the inconsistency of $T \cup \{
\neg \phi_i : i \in I\}$ is witnessed by a finite subset. That is, there exists
a finite $I' = \{i_1, \dots, i_k\} \subseteq I$ such that
$$T \cup \{\neg \phi_{i_1}, \dots, \neg \phi_{i_k}\}$$
is inconsistent. Equivalently,
$$T \vdash \forall \bar{x}\,\bigl(\phi_{i_1}(\bar{x}) \vee \cdots \vee \phi_{i_k
}(\bar{x})\bigr). \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Step 3: Verify the finite subcollection covers $S_n(T)$.**

Let $\Sigma \in S_n(T)$ be any complete $n$-type. Since $\Sigma$ is a complete t
ype of $T$, it contains every $T$-theorem; in particular, by $(\ast)$,
$$\phi_{i_1} \vee \cdots \vee \phi_{i_k} \in \Sigma.$$
Because $\Sigma$ is *complete* (maximal consistent), it must contain at least on
e disjunct: there exists $j \in \{1,\dots,k\}$ with $\phi_{i_j} \in \Sigma$, i.e
., $\Sigma \in A_{\phi_{i_j}}$.

Therefore
$$S_n(T) = \bigcup_{j=1}^{k} A_{\phi_{i_j}},$$
so the finite subcollection $\{\phi_{i_j}\}_{j=1}^{k}$ satisfies the same proper
ty as the original collection. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** This is precisely the topological compactness of the Stone space $S_
n(T)$: the sets $A_\phi$ are the basic clopen sets, and the argument above is th
e standard proof that $S_n(T)$ is compact via the compactness theorem. The compl
eteness of $T$ ensures $S_n(T) \neq \emptyset$ and that every $T$-consistent set
 of formulas extends to a complete $n$-type (so the Lindenbaum step is valid), b
ut compactness of the type space holds for arbitrary theories; the completeness
hypothesis here guarantees the types in question are genuine complete types of $
T$.
