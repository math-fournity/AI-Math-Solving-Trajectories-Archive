**Answer: No.** A subsequence converging almost surely does not imply convergenc
e in probability of the whole sequence. Below is a counterexample.

---

### Counterexample

Work on a probability space carrying a random variable $Z \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli
}(1/2)$, i.e. $\mathbb{P}(Z=0)=\mathbb{P}(Z=1)=1/2$.

Define the sequence $\{X_n\}_{n\ge 1}$ by
$$
X_n = \begin{cases}
0, & n \text{ even},\\[2pt]
Z, & n \text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
$$
Set $X \equiv 0$.

**The subsequence along even indices converges almost surely to $X$.** Let $n_k
= 2k$. Then $X_{n_k} = X_{2k} = 0$ for every $k$, deterministically. Hence $X_{n
_k} \to 0 = X$ almost surely (in fact surely).

**The whole sequence does not converge in probability to $X$.** Take any $\varep
silon \in (0,1)$. For every odd index $n = 2k-1$,
$$
\mathbb{P}\bigl(|X_n - X| > \varepsilon\bigr) = \mathbb{P}(|Z| > \varepsilon) =
\mathbb{P}(Z = 1) = \tfrac{1}{2}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty} \mathbb{P}\bigl(|X_n - X| > \varepsilon\bigr) \ge \tfrac{1}
{2} > 0,
$$
so $X_n \not\to_p X$.

---

### Why this is consistent with the standard theory

The correct implication relating subsequences and convergence in probability goe
s the other way:

- **(Standard fact)** $X_n \to_p X$ **iff** every subsequence $\{X_{n_k}\}$ has
a *further* subsequence $\{X_{n_{k_j}}\}$ that converges to $X$ almost surely.

This is a statement about *all* subsequences possessing a.s.-convergent further
subsequences, not about a *single* subsequence converging a.s. A single a.s.-con
vergent subsequence carries no information about the behavior of the terms outsi
de it; those terms may oscillate, as in the example above, where the odd-indexed
 terms are pinned to a non-degenerate random variable $Z$.

Thus almost-sure convergence of one subsequence is strictly weaker than converge
nce in probability of the full sequence.
