Looking at this problem, I need to find the angle $\angle GG_2G_1$ where $G_1$ a
nd $G_2$ are intersection points of chords in a circle containing both a regular
 pentagon and regular hexagon.

## Setup

Place the circle as the unit circle with $G$ at angle $0°$. Since $G, A_1, A_2,
A_3, A_4$ are consecutive vertices of a regular pentagon (spacing $72°$), and $G
, B_1, \ldots, B_5$ are consecutive vertices of a regular hexagon (spacing $60°$
):

| Point | Angle | Point | Angle |
|-------|-------|-------|-------|
| $G$ | $0°$ | $B_1$ | $60°$ |
| $A_1$ | $72°$ | $B_2$ | $120°$ |
| $A_2$ | $144°$ | $B_3$ | $180°$ |
| $A_3$ | $216°$ | $B_4$ | $240°$ |
| $A_4$ | $288°$ | $B_5$ | $300°$ |

$B_1$ at $60°$ lies on the shorter arc from $G(0°)$ to $A_1(72°)$. ✓

**Key coordinates** (let $c = \cos 36°$, $s = \sin 36°$, $h = \sqrt{3}/2 = \sin
60°$):
$$G=(1,0),\ B_3=(-1,0),\ B_1=\left(\tfrac12, h\right),\ B_5=\left(\tfrac12, -h\r
ight),\ A_2=(-c, s),\ A_3=(-c, -s).$$

## Finding $G_2$: intersection of $B_5A_3$ and $GB_3$

Line $GB_3$ is the $x$-axis. Line $B_5A_3$: parametrize as $\left(\frac12 - v(c+
\frac12),\ -h + v(h-s)\right)$. Setting $y=0$ gives $v = h/(h-s)$, so:
$$G_2 = \left(\frac{-(s+2hc)}{2(h-s)},\ 0\right).$$

## Finding $G_1$: intersection of $B_5B_3$ and $B_1A_2$

**Line $B_5B_3$**: $\left(\frac12 - \frac{3t}{2},\ -h+ht\right)$.

**Line $B_1A_2$**: $\left(\frac12 - u(c+\frac12),\ h+u(s-h)\right)$.

From the $x$-equation: $t = \frac{2u(c+1/2)}{3}$. Substituting into the $y$-equa
tion and solving yields (with $D = 2hc+4h-3s$):
$$u = \frac{6h}{D}, \qquad G_1 = \left(\frac{-10hc-2h-3s}{2D},\ \frac{h(2h(c-1)+
3s)}{D}\right).$$

## Computing $\tan(\angle GG_2G_1)$

Since $G_2$ lies on the $x$-axis and $G=(1,0)$ is to its right, the ray $G_2G$ p
oints in the $+x$ direction. The angle satisfies:
$$\tan(\angle GG_2G_1) = \frac{y_{G_1}}{x_{G_1}-x_{G_2}}.$$

Computing $x_{G_1} - x_{G_2}$ with common denominator $2D(h-s)$:

$$x_{G_1}-x_{G_2} = \frac{(-10hc-2h-3s)(h-s)+(s+2hc)D}{2D(h-s)}.$$

Expanding the numerator:
$$(-10hc-2h-3s)(h-s) + (s+2hc)(2hc+4h-3s)$$
$$= -10h^2c+10hcs-2h^2-hs+3s^2 + 2hcs+4hs-3s^2+4h^2c^2+8h^2c-6hcs$$
$$= 4h^2c^2-2h^2c+6hcs-2h^2+3hs = (2c+1)\cdot h\bigl[2h(c-1)+3s\bigr].$$

Therefore:
$$\tan(\angle GG_2G_1) = \frac{h[2h(c-1)+3s]/D}{(2c+1)\cdot h[2h(c-1)+3s]/\bigl(
2D(h-s)\bigr)} = \frac{2(h-s)}{2c+1}.$$

## Simplifying to $\tan 12°$

Substituting $h = \sin 60°$, $s = \sin 36°$, $c = \cos 36°$:

$$\tan(\angle GG_2G_1) = \frac{2(\sin 60° - \sin 36°)}{1 + 2\cos 36°}.$$

**Numerator** (sum-to-product): $\sin 60° - \sin 36° = 2\cos 48°\sin 12°$.

**Denominator identity**: Using product-to-sum, $2\cos 48°\cos 12° = \cos 36° +
\cos 60° = \cos 36° + \frac12$, so:
$$4\cos 48°\cos 12° = 2\cos 36° + 1 = 1 + 2\cos 36°. \quad (\star)$$

Therefore:
$$\tan(\angle GG_2G_1) = \frac{4\cos 48°\sin 12°}{1+2\cos 36°} = \frac{4\cos 48°
\sin 12°}{4\cos 48°\cos 12°} = \frac{\sin 12°}{\cos 12°} = \tan 12°,$$

where the denominator replacement uses identity $(\star)$.

Since $G_2 \approx (-3.58, 0)$, $G = (1,0)$, and $G_1 \approx (-1.69, 0.40)$, th
e angle at $G_2$ is acute, confirming:

$$\boxed{\angle GG_2G_1 = 12°}$$
