## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $X_\infty$ is $\mathcal{F}_\infty$-measurable.

**Step 1: Tail variables are measurable with respect to any earlier $\sigma$-alg
ebra.**

Fix an arbitrary index $n \geq 1$. For every $m \geq n$, the reverse filtration
property gives $\mathcal{F}_m \subseteq \mathcal{F}_n$. Since $X_m$ is $\mathcal
{F}_m$-measurable and $\mathcal{F}_m \subseteq \mathcal{F}_n$, it follows that $
X_m$ is also $\mathcal{F}_n$-measurable. Thus the entire tail $\{X_m : m \geq n\
}$ consists of $\mathcal{F}_n$-measurable functions.

**Step 2: The limit is $\mathcal{F}_n$-measurable for each fixed $n$.**

By hypothesis the pointwise limit $X_\infty = \lim_{m \to \infty} X_m$ exists in
 $\overline{\mathbb{R}}$. For the fixed $n$ from Step 1, we may write
$$X_\infty = \lim_{\substack{m \to \infty \\ m \geq n}} X_m.$$
Each $X_m$ ($m \geq n$) is $\mathcal{F}_n$-measurable, and the pointwise limit o
f a sequence of $\mathcal{F}_n$-measurable $\overline{\mathbb{R}}$-valued functi
ons is again $\mathcal{F}_n$-measurable (this is the standard closure of measura
bility under limits, proved via $\limsup$, which is built from countable suprema
/infima of measurable functions). Hence
$$X_\infty \text{ is } \mathcal{F}_n\text{-measurable}.$$
Since $n$ was arbitrary, $X_\infty$ is $\mathcal{F}_n$-measurable for **every**
$n \geq 1$.

**Step 3: Measurable with respect to every $\mathcal{F}_n$ implies measurable wi
th respect to the intersection.**

Let $B \in \mathcal{B}(\overline{\mathbb{R}})$. By Step 2, for every $n$,
$$(X_\infty)^{-1}(B) \in \mathcal{F}_n.$$
Therefore
$$(X_\infty)^{-1}(B) \in \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} \mathcal{F}_n = \mathcal{F}_\inf
ty.$$
Since this holds for every Borel set $B$, $X_\infty$ is $\mathcal{F}_\infty$-mea
surable.

**Conclusion.** The crucial point is the *reverse* nature of the filtration: $\m
athcal{F}_m \subseteq \mathcal{F}_n$ for $m \geq n$ makes every tail variable $X
_m$ automatically measurable with respect to all the *larger* earlier $\sigma$-a
lgebras $\mathcal{F}_n$. The limit, being a limit of such tail variables, inheri
ts $\mathcal{F}_n$-measurability for every $n$, and a function measurable with r
espect to each member of a family of $\sigma$-algebras is measurable with respec
t to their intersection. Hence
$$X_\infty : (\Omega, \mathcal{F}_\infty) \to (\overline{\mathbb{R}}, \mathcal{B
}(\overline{\mathbb{R}}))$$
is measurable. $\blacksquare$
