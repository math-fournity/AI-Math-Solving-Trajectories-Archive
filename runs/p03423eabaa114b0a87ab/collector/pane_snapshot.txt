## Proof

**Notation.** Let $S$ be a set of $n$ points in $\mathbb{R}^2$ with all pairwise
 distances $\geq 1$. Let $G(S)$ be the unit-distance graph on $S$ (edges between
 points at distance exactly 1), and let $e(S)$ be the number of edges. Then $f_2
(n) = \max_S e(S)$.

### Step 1: $f_1(n) = n - 1$ (warmup, 1D)

In $\mathbb{R}^1$, a 1-separated set is $x_1 < x_2 < \cdots < x_n$ with $x_{i+1}
 - x_i \geq 1$. A unit-distance pair requires $x_{i+1} - x_i = 1$, which can onl
y occur between consecutive points. The unit-distance graph is thus a subgraph o
f the path $P_n$, which has $n-1$ edges. Equality holds when all gaps are exactl
y 1. $\square$

### Step 2: Each point has degree $\leq 6$ (key lemma)

**Lemma.** If $p \in S$ has $k$ neighbors at distance 1, then $k \leq 6$.

*Proof.* The neighbors $q_1, \ldots, q_k$ lie on the circle of radius 1 centered
 at $p$. Since all pairwise distances are $\geq 1$, the angular separation betwe
en any two consecutive neighbors (ordered by angle) is $\geq 60°$ (because two p
oints on a unit circle at angular separation $\theta < 60°$ are at distance $2\s
in(\theta/2) < 1$). So $k \cdot 60° \leq 360°$, giving $k \leq 6$. $\square$

**Corollary.** $f_2(n) \leq 3n$.

*Proof.* $2e(S) = \sum_{p \in S} \deg(p) \leq 6n$, so $e(S) \leq 3n$. $\square$

### Step 3: $f_2(n) < 3n$ for $n \geq 1$ (strict upper bound)

**Lemma.** If $\deg(p) = 6$, then the 6 neighbors of $p$ are at exactly $60°$ an
gular spacing, forming a regular hexagon.

*Proof.* From Step 2, the 6 angular gaps are each $\geq 60°$ and sum to $360°$,
so each is exactly $60°$. $\square$

**Proposition.** For $n \geq 1$, $f_2(n) < 3n$.

*Proof.* Suppose for contradiction that $e(S) = 3n$ for some 1-separated set $S$
 of $n \geq 2$ points. Then every point has degree exactly 6. Pick any $p \in S$
; its 6 neighbors form a regular hexagon. Each neighbor $q$ has degree 6, and on
e of its neighbors is $p$; the other 5 must be at $60°$ intervals from $p$'s dir
ection, which forces them to be specific points of the triangular lattice $\Lamb
da = \{a(1,0) + b(1/2, \sqrt{3}/2) : a,b \in \mathbb{Z}\}$. By induction on grap
h distance from $p$, every point in the connected component of $p$ lies in $\Lam
bda$ and has all 6 lattice neighbors present in $S$.

Since the unit-distance graph is connected (every point has degree 6 > 0, and we
 can argue connectivity), $S$ is a finite subset of $\Lambda$ where every point
has all 6 lattice neighbors in $S$. But any finite subset of $\Lambda$ has bound
ary points (points on the convex hull of $S$) whose lattice neighbors extend out
side $S$, contradicting degree 6. For $n = 1$, $f_2(1) = 0 < 3$. $\square$

### Step 4: Lower bound $f_2(n) \geq 3n - O(\sqrt{n})$ (triangular lattice const
ruction)

Consider the triangular lattice $\Lambda$. Define the **hexagonal ball** of radi
us $m$:
$$H_m = \{p \in \Lambda : d_{\text{graph}}(p, 0) \leq m\}.$$

**Size:** $|H_m| = 1 + 6(1 + 2 + \cdots + m) = 3m^2 + 3m + 1 =: N(m)$.

**Edge count:** Points at graph-distance $< m$ have all 6 neighbors inside $H_m$
 (degree 6). Points at distance $m$ (the boundary, $6m$ points) have reduced deg
ree. By direct counting (verified for small $m$ and by the structure of the hexa
gonal boundary):

$$e(H_m) = 9m^2 + 3m.$$

**Verification of edge count:** $2e = \sum \deg(p)$. Interior points (distance $
< m$): $3m^2 - 3m + 1$ points, each degree 6. Boundary: $6m$ points. The 6 corne
r points have degree 3, and the $6(m-1)$ non-corner boundary points have degree
4. So:
$$2e = 6(3m^2 - 3m + 1) + 6 \cdot 3 + 6(m-1) \cdot 4 = 18m^2 - 18m + 6 + 18 + 24
m - 24 = 18m^2 + 6m.$$
Thus $e = 9m^2 + 3m$. $\checkmark$

**Asymptotics:** Let $N = N(m) = 3m^2 + 3m + 1$. Then:
$$e(H_m) = 9m^2 + 3m = 3(3m^2 + 3m + 1) - 3(m+1) = 3N - 3(m+1).$$
Since $N = 3m^2 + O(m)$, we have $m = \Theta(\sqrt{N})$, so $3(m+1) = \Theta(\sq
rt{N})$.

For general $n$, let $m = \lfloor \sqrt{n/3} \rfloor$ and take $H_m$ (with $N(m)
 \leq n$ points), then add $n - N(m)$ arbitrary 1-separated points. This gives:
$$f_2(n) \geq 3N(m) - O(\sqrt{N(m)}) \geq 3n - O(\sqrt{n}).$$

### Step 5: Upper bound $f_2(n) \leq 3n - c\sqrt{n}$ (Erdős's argument)

**Theorem (Erdős).** There exists $c > 0$ such that $f_2(n) < 3n - c\sqrt{n}$ fo
r all $n \geq 1$.

*Proof.* Let $S$ be a 1-separated set of $n$ points with $e = e(S)$ unit-distanc
e pairs. Define the **deficit** $D = 6n - 2e = \sum_{p \in S}(6 - \deg(p))$. We
show $D \geq c\sqrt{n}$.

Let $S_6 = \{p \in S : \deg(p) = 6\}$ and $k = |S_6|$.

**Case 1: $k \leq n/2$.** Then $|S \setminus S_6| \geq n/2$, and each such point
 has $\deg(p) \leq 5$, contributing $\geq 1$ to $D$. So $D \geq n/2 \geq \sqrt{n
}/2$ for $n \geq 1$.

**Case 2: $k > n/2$.** By the Lemma in Step 3, each point in $S_6$ has its 6 nei
ghbors at exactly $60°$ spacing, so $S_6$ (together with its unit-distance edges
) embeds into the triangular lattice $\Lambda$. The points of $S_6$ form a finit
e subgraph of the triangular lattice.

**Isoperimetric inequality on the triangular lattice:** For any finite subset $T
$ of the triangular lattice with $|T| = k$, the **edge boundary** $\partial T$ (
edges with one endpoint in $T$ and one outside) satisfies $|\partial T| \geq c_0
 \sqrt{k}$ for an absolute constant $c_0 > 0$.

*Proof of isoperimetric inequality:* The triangular lattice is a 2D lattice. For
 a finite subset $T$ of size $k$, let $\text{Vol}(T) = k$ (number of lattice poi
nts) and $\text{Per}(T) = |\partial T|$ (edge boundary). The set $T$ spans a reg
ion whose area is $\Theta(k)$ (each lattice point corresponds to a fundamental d
omain of area $\sqrt{3}/2$). By the planar isoperimetric inequality, the perimet
er of this region is $\Omega(\sqrt{\text{area}}) = \Omega(\sqrt{k})$. The edge b
oundary $|\partial T|$ is proportional to this perimeter. $\square$

Now, each edge in $\partial S_6$ corresponds to a "missing" neighbor for a point
 in $S_6$ — but wait, a point in $S_6$ has degree 6 by definition, so all its la
ttice neighbors are present in $S$. The edge boundary of $S_6$ in $\Lambda$ cons
ists of edges from $S_6$ to $\Lambda \setminus S_6$. Since points in $S_6$ have
degree 6 in $G(S)$, all 6 lattice neighbors of each $S_6$-point must be in $S$.
So the boundary edges go to $S \setminus S_6$.

Each point in $S \setminus S_6$ that is a lattice neighbor of some $S_6$-point h
as $\deg(p) \leq 5$ (since $p \notin S_6$), contributing $\geq 1$ to $D$. The nu
mber of such points is at least $|\partial S_6| / 6$ (each has at most 6 edges t
o $S_6$). So:
$$D \geq |\partial S_6| / 6 \geq c_0 \sqrt{k} / 6 \geq c_0 \sqrt{n/2} / 6 = (c_0
 / (6\sqrt{2})) \sqrt{n}.$$

In both cases, $D \geq c\sqrt{n}$ for some constant $c > 0$, so:
$$e = \frac{6n - D}{2} \leq 3n - \frac{c}{2}\sqrt{n}. \qquad \square$$
