Looking at this problem, I need to find the greatest $p$ for which the series co
nverges conditionally (converges but not absolutely).

**Setting up the asymptotics.** Let $a_n = \tan\!\left(n^{-p/2}\right)\cdot\ln\!
\left(1+n^{-2p}\right) > 0$, so the series is $\sum (-1)^n a_n$.

For large $n$ (with $p>0$ so the arguments tend to 0):
- $\tan(x) = x + \tfrac{x^3}{3}+\cdots$, so $\tan(n^{-p/2}) \sim n^{-p/2}$.
- $\ln(1+x) = x - \tfrac{x^2}{2}+\cdots$, so $\ln(1+n^{-2p}) \sim n^{-2p}$.

Therefore
$$a_n \sim n^{-p/2}\cdot n^{-2p} = n^{-5p/2}.$$

**Absolute convergence.** The series $\sum a_n$ behaves like $\sum n^{-5p/2}$, w
hich converges iff $\tfrac{5p}{2}>1$, i.e. $p>\tfrac{2}{5}$, and diverges iff $p
\le \tfrac{2}{5}$.

**Conditional convergence via Leibniz.** For the alternating series $\sum(-1)^n
a_n$ to converge, we need $a_n\to 0$ and $a_n$ eventually decreasing.

- For $p\le 0$: the arguments $n^{-p/2}$ and $n^{-2p}$ do **not** tend to $0$, s
o $a_n\not\to 0$; the series diverges.
- For $p>0$: $a_n\sim n^{-5p/2}\to 0$, and since $a_n$ is asymptotic to a decrea
sing power function, $a_n$ is eventually monotonically decreasing. By the Leibni
z test, $\sum(-1)^n a_n$ converges for every $p>0$.

**Combining.** Conditional convergence = (alternating series converges) $\wedge$
 (absolute series diverges):
- $p\le 0$: divergent (no convergence at all).
- $0<p\le \tfrac{2}{5}$: alternating converges (Leibniz), absolute diverges $\Ri
ghtarrow$ **conditional convergence**.
- $p>\tfrac{2}{5}$: absolute convergence $\Rightarrow$ not conditional.

**Boundary check at $p=\tfrac{2}{5}$.** Here $a_n\sim n^{-1}$, so $\sum a_n$ div
erges (harmonic), while $\sum(-1)^n a_n$ converges by Leibniz since $a_n\to 0$ a
nd is eventually decreasing. This is genuine conditional convergence.

For any $p>\tfrac{2}{5}$, we get $a_n\sim n^{-5p/2}$ with exponent $>1$, giving
absolute convergence — so conditional convergence is lost.

**Conclusion.** The greatest value of $p$ for conditional convergence is

$$\boxed{p=\dfrac{2}{5}}.$$
