# Problem

A remote island chain consists of $n$ research stations ($n > 2$) arranged in a perfect circle. For communication purposes, each station $k$ is linked to its two immediate neighbors: station $k-1$ and station $k+1$ (with station $n$ linked back to station 1). Initially, the security systems at all $n$ stations are deactivated.

Each station has a master toggle. However, due to the interconnected power grid, activating the toggle at any specific station simultaneously flips the security status of that station and its two direct neighbors (changing them from deactivated to activated, or vice versa).

A security engineer wants to map out the total variety of possible global security states. How many different configurations of activated and deactivated stations can be achieved across the entire island chain through any combination of toggle presses?
