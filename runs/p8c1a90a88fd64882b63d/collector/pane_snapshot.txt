## Solution

**Step 1: Evaluate the inner integral via Beta function.**

Let $I_a(n) = \int_0^1 (1-x^a)^n\,dx$. Substituting $u = x^a$ (so $x = u^{1/a}$,
 $dx = \frac{1}{a}u^{1/a-1}\,du$):

$$I_a(n) = \frac{1}{a}\int_0^1 (1-u)^n\, u^{1/a - 1}\,du = \frac{1}{a}\,B\!\left
(\tfrac{1}{a},\, n+1\right) = \frac{\Gamma(1+1/a)\,\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+1+1/a)}
.$$

**Step 2: Asymptotics of $(I_a(n))^{1/n}$ for fixed $a$.**

Using the standard ratio asymptotic $\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+1+1/a)} \sim n^
{-1/a}$ as $n\to\infty$, we get

$$I_a(n) \sim \Gamma(1+1/a)\, n^{-1/a}.$$

Taking the $n$-th root:

$$(I_a(n))^{1/n} \sim \bigl(\Gamma(1+1/a)\bigr)^{1/n}\cdot n^{-1/(an)} = \exp\!\
left(\frac{\ln\Gamma(1+1/a)}{n}\right)\cdot \exp\!\left(-\frac{\ln n}{an}\right)
 \longrightarrow 1,$$

since both $\frac{\ln\Gamma(1+1/a)}{n}\to 0$ and $\frac{\ln n}{an}\to 0$.

**Step 3: Justify exchanging limit and sum (Dominated Convergence).**

For all $x\in[0,1]$ and $a\ge 2$, we have $0\le 1-x^a\le 1$, hence $0\le (1-x^a)
^n\le 1$ and

$$0 \le I_a(n) = \int_0^1(1-x^a)^n\,dx \le 1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 0 \le (I_
a(n))^{1/n} \le 1.$$

Therefore each summand satisfies $\frac{(I_a(n))^{1/n}}{a!}\le \frac{1}{a!}$, an
d $\sum_{a=2}^\infty \frac{1}{a!}=e-2<\infty$ is a convergent dominating series.
 By the dominated convergence theorem for series:

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{a=2}^\infty \frac{(I_a(n))^{1/n}}{a!} = \sum_{a=2}^\inf
ty \frac{\lim_{n\to\infty}(I_a(n))^{1/n}}{a!} = \sum_{a=2}^\infty \frac{1}{a!}.$
$

**Step 4: Compute the sum.**

$$\sum_{a=2}^\infty \frac{1}{a!} = \sum_{a=0}^\infty \frac{1}{a!} - \frac{1}{0!}
 - \frac{1}{1!} = e - 1 - 1 = e - 2.$$

$$\boxed{e-2}$$
