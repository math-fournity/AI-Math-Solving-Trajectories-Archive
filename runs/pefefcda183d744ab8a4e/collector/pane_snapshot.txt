No. Here is the proof.

**Theorem.** Let $M \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ be a compact, orientable, regular surf
ace without boundary that is not a sphere. Then $M$ cannot have non-negative Gau
ssian curvature at every point.

**Proof.**

Since $M$ is compact and orientable without boundary, it is diffeomorphic to a c
losed orientable surface of some genus $g \geq 0$. Its Euler characteristic is
$$\chi(M) = 2 - 2g.$$
If $M$ is not a sphere, then $g \geq 1$, so $\chi(M) \leq 0$.

By the **Gauss–Bonnet theorem** (no boundary case),
$$\int_M K\, dA = 2\pi\,\chi(M) = 2\pi(2-2g).$$

Suppose, for contradiction, that $K \geq 0$ everywhere on $M$. Then $\int_M K\,
dA \geq 0$. But for $g \geq 1$,
$$\int_M K\, dA = 2\pi(2-2g) \leq 0.$$
Combining these two inequalities forces
$$\int_M K\, dA = 0.$$
Since $K \geq 0$ everywhere and the integral of a non-negative continuous functi
on over a compact set is zero, we conclude
$$K \equiv 0 \quad \text{on } M.$$

It remains to show that no compact, regular, boundaryless surface in $\mathbb{R}
^3$ can have $K \equiv 0$.

If $K \equiv 0$, every point of $M$ is parabolic (the Gauss map has rank $\leq 1
$ everywhere), so $M$ is a **developable surface**. A classical classification t
heorem (due to Hartman–Nirenberg, and earlier work on developable surfaces) stat
es:

> *Every complete developable regular surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is (a subset of)
 a plane, a cylinder over a plane curve, a cone, or the tangent developable of a
 space curve.*

Each of these is either:
- a **plane** — not compact,
- a **cylinder** over a plane curve — non-compact (the generating direction is u
nbounded), or has boundary if truncated,
- a **cone** — has a singular apex (not a regular surface there) and is non-comp
act or has boundary,
- a **tangent developable** of a space curve — has a singular edge (the curve it
self, where the surface meets its cuspidal edge) and is non-compact or has bound
ary.

In every case, the surface fails to be a compact *regular* surface *without boun
dary*. Hence no compact, regular, boundaryless surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$ can sat
isfy $K \equiv 0$.

This contradicts our deduction that $K \equiv 0$ on $M$. Therefore the assumptio
n $K \geq 0$ everywhere is false. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The sphere ($g = 0$) is the unique exception: $\chi = 2 > 0$, so $\i
nt K\, dA = 4\pi > 0$, and indeed the unit sphere (and its images under affine s
caling) has $K > 0$ everywhere. For every higher genus, the topological obstruct
ion from Gauss–Bonnet forces the existence of a point with $K < 0$.
