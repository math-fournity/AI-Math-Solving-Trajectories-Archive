## All Maximal Ideals of \(\mathbb{Z}[x]\)

**Theorem.** The maximal ideals of \(\mathbb{Z}[x]\) are exactly the ideals of t
he form
\[
\mathfrak{m} = (p,\, f(x)),
\]
where \(p\) is a prime number and \(f(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]\) is a polynomial whose
 reduction \(\bar f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_p[x]\) is irreducible. Two such pairs \((p,f
)\) and \((q,g)\) give the same ideal iff \(p=q\) and \(\bar f,\bar g\) are asso
ciates in \(\mathbb{F}_p[x]\).

---

### Proof

**Step 1. Reduction to a residue field question.**

Let \(\mathfrak m\subseteq\mathbb{Z}[x]\) be maximal and set \(K:=\mathbb{Z}[x]/
\mathfrak m\), a field. The quotient map \(\mathbb{Z}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Z}[x
]\to K\) has kernel \(\mathfrak m\cap\mathbb{Z}\), a prime ideal of \(\mathbb{Z}
\); hence \(\mathfrak m\cap\mathbb{Z}=(0)\) or \((p)\) for a prime \(p\).

**Step 2. Ruling out \(\mathfrak m\cap\mathbb{Z}=(0)\) via the general Nullstell
ensatz.**

The ring \(\mathbb{Z}\) is Jacobson (every prime is an intersection of maximal i
deals: \((0)=\bigcap_p(p)\)). The field \(K=\mathbb{Z}[x]/\mathfrak m\) is a fin
itely generated \(\mathbb{Z}\)-algebra (generated by the class of \(x\)). By the
 general Nullstellensatz (Zariski's lemma over a Jacobson base ring):

> *If \(R\) is Jacobson and \(S\) is a finitely generated \(R\)-algebra that is
a field, then \(S\) is a finite module over \(R/(\mathfrak m_S\cap R)\), where \
(\mathfrak m_S:=S\cap R\) is a **maximal** ideal of \(R\).*

Applying this with \(R=\mathbb{Z}\), \(S=K\): the contraction \(\mathfrak m\cap\
mathbb{Z}\) must be a **maximal** ideal of \(\mathbb{Z}\). The maximal ideals of
 \(\mathbb{Z}\) are exactly \((p)\) for primes \(p\). Therefore \(\mathfrak m\ca
p\mathbb{Z}\neq(0)\), and in fact \(\mathfrak m\cap\mathbb{Z}=(p)\) for some pri
me \(p\).

(Equivalently, one may argue directly: if \(\mathfrak m\cap\mathbb{Z}=(0)\), the
n \(\mathbb{Z}\hookrightarrow K\), so \(K\) has characteristic \(0\) and contain
s \(\mathbb{Q}\). But \(K\) is a finitely generated \(\mathbb{Z}\)-module by the
 Nullstellensatz, hence a finitely generated abelian group — impossible for a ch
aracteristic-\(0\) field, since \(\mathbb{Q}\) is not finitely generated as a \(
\mathbb{Z}\)-module. Contradiction.)

**Step 3. Classification via \(\mathbb{F}_p[x]\).**

Fix the prime \(p\) with \(\mathfrak m\cap\mathbb{Z}=(p)\), so \((p)\subseteq\ma
thfrak m\). Then \(\mathfrak m/(p)\) is a maximal ideal of
\[
\mathbb{Z}[x]/(p)\cong\mathbb{F}_p[x].
\]
Since \(\mathbb{F}_p[x]\) is a PID (Euclidean domain), its maximal ideals are pr
ecisely \((\bar f(x))\) where \(\bar f\in\mathbb{F}_p[x]\) is irreducible. Lifti
ng back along the quotient \(\mathbb{Z}[x]\to\mathbb{F}_p[x]\), there exists \(f
(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]\) reducing to \(\bar f\) such that
\[
\mathfrak m = (p,\, f(x)).
\]

**Step 4. Converse — every such ideal is maximal.**

Given a prime \(p\) and \(f(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]\) with \(\bar f\in\mathbb{F}_p[x]
\) irreducible, the ideal \((p,f)\) satisfies
\[
\mathbb{Z}[x]/(p,f)\;\cong\;\mathbb{F}_p[x]/(\bar f),
\]
which is a field (a finite extension of \(\mathbb{F}_p\), of degree \(\deg\bar f
\)). Hence \((p,f)\) is maximal.

**Step 5. Uniqueness statement.**

If \((p,f)=(q,g)\) as ideals, then intersecting with \(\mathbb{Z}\) gives \((p)=
(q)\), so \(p=q\). Modding out by \((p)\), the equality \((p,f)=(p,g)\) becomes
\((\bar f)=(\bar g)\) in the PID \(\mathbb{F}_p[x]\), i.e. \(\bar f,\bar g\) are
 associates (differ by a nonzero scalar in \(\mathbb{F}_p^\times\)). Choosing \(
\bar f\) monic makes the representative unique.

---

**Conclusion.** Every maximal ideal of \(\mathbb{Z}[x]\) is uniquely of the form
\[
\boxed{\;\mathfrak m=(p,\,f(x))\quad\text{with }p\text{ prime and }\bar f\in\mat
hbb{F}_p[x]\text{ monic irreducible}\;}
\]
and every ideal of this form is maximal. The residue field is the finite field \
(\mathbb{F}_{p^{\deg\bar f}}\).
