## Proof

**Setup.** The Lens space $L(p,q)$ is the quotient $S^3/\mathbb{Z}_p$, where the
 generator $\zeta$ of $\mathbb{Z}_p$ acts on $S^3 \subset \mathbb{C}^2$ by
$$\zeta \cdot (z_1, z_2) = \left(e^{2\pi i/p}\, z_1,\; e^{2\pi i q/p}\, z_2\righ
t),$$
with $\gcd(p,q)=1$. Its fundamental group is $\pi_1(L(p,q)) \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$,
 and the universal cover is $S^3$ with deck group $\mathbb{Z}_p$.

**The homomorphism and its kernel.** Let $p = ab$. The quotient homomorphism
$$\phi: \mathbb{Z}_p \longrightarrow \mathbb{Z}_a, \qquad \bar{1} \longmapsto \b
ar{1} \pmod{a}$$
has kernel
$$H = \ker\phi = \{0,\, a,\, 2a,\, \dots,\, (b{-}1)a\} \subset \mathbb{Z}_p.$$
This is the cyclic subgroup $\langle \zeta^a \rangle$ of order $b$ (since $\zeta
^a$ has order $p/\gcd(p,a) = ab/a = b$) and index $a$ in $\mathbb{Z}_p$.

**Applying the Galois correspondence.** By the classification of covering spaces
, the covering of $L(p,q)$ corresponding to the subgroup $H \subset \pi_1(L(p,q)
)$ is
$$\widetilde{L} = S^3 / H,$$
with deck transformation group $\mathbb{Z}_p / H \cong \mathbb{Z}_a$. This is an
 $a$-fold cyclic covering.

**Identifying the covering space.** The subgroup $H = \langle \zeta^a \rangle$ a
cts on $S^3$ with generator $\zeta^a$:
$$\zeta^a \cdot (z_1, z_2) = \left(e^{2\pi i a/p}\, z_1,\; e^{2\pi i qa/p}\, z_2
\right) = \left(e^{2\pi i/b}\, z_1,\; e^{2\pi i q/b}\, z_2\right),$$
since $a/p = 1/b$ and $qa/p = q/b$. This is precisely the standard $\mathbb{Z}_b
$-action defining the Lens space $L(b,\, q \bmod b)$.

It remains to verify that $L(b, q)$ is well-defined, i.e., $\gcd(b, q) = 1$. Sin
ce $\gcd(p, q) = 1$ and $b \mid p$ (as $p = ab$), every prime factor of $b$ divi
des $p$ and hence does not divide $q$. Thus $\gcd(b, q) = 1$, confirming that $L
(b, q)$ is a valid Lens space.

**Conclusion.** The cyclic covering space of $L(p,q)$ induced by the quotient ho
momorphism $\mathbb{Z}_p \to \mathbb{Z}_a$ (with $p = ab$) is

$$\boxed{L(b,\, q)}.$$

This is a $b$-fold quotient of $S^3$ and an $a$-fold cyclic covering of $L(p,q)$
, with deck transformation group $\mathbb{Z}_a$.
