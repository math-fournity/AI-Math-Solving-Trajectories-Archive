## Gelfand–Naimark Theorem for Commutative Unital \(C^{*}\)-Algebras

### Statement

Let \(A\) be a commutative unital \(C^{*}\)-algebra, and let

\[
\Delta(A) \;=\; \bigl\{\,\varphi : A \to \mathbb{C} \;\big|\; \varphi \text{ is
a nonzero } ^{*}\text{-homomorphism}\,\bigr\}
\]

be its **spectrum** (the set of all characters, i.e. nonzero multiplicative line
ar functionals on \(A\)). Equip \(\Delta(A)\) with the weak-\(^{*}\) topology in
herited from \(A^{*}\), so that \(\Delta(A)\) becomes a compact Hausdorff space.

Then the **Gelfand transform**

\[
\Gamma : A \longrightarrow C\bigl(\Delta(A)\bigr), \qquad
\Gamma(a)(\varphi) \;=\; \widehat{a}(\varphi) \;=\; \varphi(a),
\]

is an **isometric \(^{*}\)-isomorphism** of \(A\) onto \(C\bigl(\Delta(A)\bigr)\
).

In words: **every commutative unital \(C^{*}\)-algebra is canonically and isomet
rically \(^{*}\)-isomorphic to the algebra of continuous complex-valued function
s on a compact Hausdorff space** — namely, its spectrum.

---

### Explanation

#### 1. The ingredients

- A **\(C^{*}\)-algebra** is a Banach algebra over \(\mathbb{C}\) with an involu
tion \(^{*}\) satisfying \(\|a^{*}a\| = \|a\|^{2}\).
- **Unital** means it has a multiplicative identity \(1\).
- **Commutative** means \(ab = ba\) for all \(a, b \in A\).
- A **character** \(\varphi \in \Delta(A)\) is a nonzero algebra homomorphism \(
A \to \mathbb{C}\). Because \(A\) is unital, \(\varphi(1) = 1\). Every character
 is automatically continuous and satisfies \(\|\varphi\| = 1\); moreover, since
\(A\) is a \(C^{*}\)-algebra, every character is a \(^{*}\)-homomorphism: \(\var
phi(a^{*}) = \overline{\varphi(a)}\).

#### 2. \(\Delta(A)\) is a compact Hausdorff space

Each character has \(\|\varphi\| = 1\), so \(\Delta(A)\) sits inside the closed
unit ball of \(A^{*}\). By the Banach–Alaoglu theorem this ball is weak-\(^{*}\)
 compact. One checks that \(\Delta(A)\) is weak-\(^{*}\) **closed** (it is cut o
ut by the equations \(\varphi(ab)=\varphi(a)\varphi(b)\) and \(\varphi(1)=1\), w
hich are weak-\(^{*}\) continuous conditions). Being a closed subset of a compac
t set, \(\Delta(A)\) is compact. The weak-\(^{*}\) topology is Hausdorff. Hence
\(\Delta(A)\) is a compact Hausdorff space.

#### 3. The Gelfand transform is a well-defined \(^{*}\)-homomorphism

For each \(a \in A\), the evaluation map \(\widehat{a} : \varphi \mapsto \varphi
(a)\) is weak-\(^{*}\) continuous by definition of that topology, so \(\widehat{
a} \in C(\Delta(A))\). The map \(\Gamma : a \mapsto \widehat{a}\) is linear, mul
tiplicative (\(\widehat{ab}(\varphi) = \varphi(ab) = \varphi(a)\varphi(b) = \wid
ehat{a}(\varphi)\widehat{b}(\varphi)\)), unital (\(\widehat{1} \equiv 1\)), and
respects the involution (\(\widehat{a^{*}}(\varphi) = \varphi(a^{*}) = \overline
{\varphi(a)} = \overline{\widehat{a}(\varphi)}\)). Thus \(\Gamma\) is a unital \
(^{*}\)-homomorphism \(A \to C(\Delta(A))\).

#### 4. \(\Gamma\) is isometric

The key \(C^{*}\)-identity gives, for every \(a \in A\),

\[
\|a\|^{2} = \|a^{*}a\|.
\]

For a commutative unital \(C^{*}\)-algebra one has the **Gelfand–Beurling formul
a**

\[
\|a^{*}a\| \;=\; r(a^{*}a) \;=\; \sup_{\varphi \in \Delta(A)} |\varphi(a^{*}a)|
\;=\; \sup_{\varphi \in \Delta(A)} |\varphi(a)|^{2} \;=\; \|\widehat{a}\|_{\inft
y}^{2},
\]

where \(r(\cdot)\) is the spectral radius. The first equality uses that \(a^{*}a
\) is normal (in fact self-adjoint) so its norm equals its spectral radius; the
second is the standard characterization of the spectral radius via the spectrum,
 which in the commutative case is exactly \(\{\varphi(a^{*}a) : \varphi \in \Del
ta(A)\}\); the third uses that characters are \(^{*}\)-homomorphisms. Hence

\[
\|a\| = \|\widehat{a}\|_{\infty},
\]

so \(\Gamma\) is an **isometry**.

#### 5. \(\Gamma\) is surjective

Being an isometry, \(\Gamma(A)\) is a closed \(^{*}\)-subalgebra of \(C(\Delta(A
))\) that contains the constants (since \(\widehat{1} \equiv 1\)) and separates
the points of \(\Delta(A)\) (if \(\varphi_1 \neq \varphi_2\), pick \(a\) with \(
\varphi_1(a) \neq \varphi_2(a)\), i.e. \(\widehat{a}(\varphi_1) \neq \widehat{a}
(\varphi_2)\)). By the **Stone–Weierstrass theorem**, the only such subalgebra o
f \(C(\Delta(A))\) is \(C(\Delta(A))\) itself. Therefore \(\Gamma(A) = C(\Delta(
A))\), and \(\Gamma\) is surjective.

#### 6. Conclusion

\(\Gamma : A \to C(\Delta(A))\) is a bijective, isometric \(^{*}\)-homomorphism
— an **isometric \(^{*}\)-isomorphism**. This establishes the canonical identifi
cation

\[
A \;\cong\; C\bigl(\Delta(A)\bigr).
\]

#### 7. Significance

The theorem is the foundational **duality** of commutative \(C^{*}\)-algebra the
ory: it shows that commutative unital \(C^{*}\)-algebras are *exactly* the algeb
ras \(C(X)\) of continuous functions on compact Hausdorff spaces \(X\), with \(X
 = \Delta(A)\) recovered intrinsically from \(A\). This is the bridge that lets
one translate algebraic notions (ideals, quotients, spectra) into topological on
es (closed subsets, subspaces, compact spaces), and it underlies the **Gelfand d
uality** (an equivalence of categories between commutative unital \(C^{*}\)-alge
bras with unital \(^{*}\)-homomorphisms and compact Hausdorff spaces with contin
uous maps). Together with the noncommutative Gelfand–Naimark theorem (every \(C^
{*}\)-algebra embeds isometrically into \(B(H)\)), it justifies the slogan that
**noncommutative \(C^{*}\)-algebras are "noncommutative topological spaces."**
