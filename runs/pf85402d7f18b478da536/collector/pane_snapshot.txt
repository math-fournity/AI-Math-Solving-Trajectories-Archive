Looking at this problem, I need to find all $n \in \{1, \dots, 100\}$ for which
there exist integer-coefficient polynomials $f$ (degree $n$, positive leading co
efficient) and $g$ satisfying $x f(x)^2 + f(x) = (x^3 - x) g(x)^2$.

## Setting Up the Pell Equation

**Completing the square.** Multiply both sides by $4x$:

$$4x^2 f^2 + 4xf = 4x^2(x^2-1)g^2$$

$$(2xf + 1)^2 - 1 = (2x)^2(x^2-1)g^2$$

Setting $u = 2xf + 1$ and $w = 2xg$, this becomes:

$$\boxed{u^2 - (x^2 - 1)\,w^2 = 1}$$

This is the **classical polynomial Pell equation**. The constraints translate to
:
- $u(0) = 1$ and $u - 1$ is divisible by $2x$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ (all non-consta
nt coefficients of $u$ are even).
- $w$ is divisible by $2x$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ ($w(0)=0$ and all coefficients of
$w$ are even).
- $\deg f = n$ with positive leading coefficient, so $\deg u = n+1$.

## Classifying All Polynomial Solutions

The ring $\mathbb{C}[x, \sqrt{x^2-1}]$ is the coordinate ring of a genus-0 curve
 with two points at infinity, so its unit group is $\{\pm(x + \sqrt{x^2-1})^k :
k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$. Therefore **all** polynomial solutions are:

$$u = \pm\, T_k(x), \qquad w = \pm\, U_{k-1}(x)$$

where $T_k$ (Chebyshev first kind) and $U_{k-1}$ (Chebyshev second kind) have in
teger coefficients, and $\deg T_k = \deg U_{k-1} = k$.

Since $\deg u = n+1$, we need $k = n+1$.

## Checking the Constraints

**Parity of $T_k$ coefficients.** Using $T_k(x) = \frac{k}{2}\sum_{j} (-1)^j \fr
ac{(k-j-1)!}{j!(k-2j)!}(2x)^{k-2j}$:

- For $k-2j \geq 2$: the factor $2^{k-2j-1} \geq 2$ ensures the coefficient is e
ven.
- For $k-2j = 1$ (only when $k$ is odd): the coefficient equals $\pm k$, which i
s **odd**.

So **all non-constant coefficients of $T_k$ are even iff $k$ is even**.

**Parity of $U_k$ coefficients.** Using $U_k(x) = \sum_j (-1)^j \binom{k-j}{j}(2
x)^{k-2j}$:

- For $k-2j \geq 1$: factor $2^{k-2j} \geq 2$, coefficient is even.
- For $k-2j = 0$ (constant term, only when $k$ even): coefficient is $\pm 1$, **
odd**.

So **all coefficients of $U_k$ are even iff $k$ is odd** (no constant term exist
s).

**Values at 0.** $T_k(0) = \cos(k\pi/2)$ and $U_k(0) = \sin((k+1)\pi/2)$.

## Case Analysis

**Case 1: $u = T_k$, $w = \pm U_{k-1}$.** Need $T_k(0) = 1$, so $k \equiv 0 \pmo
d{4}$, giving $n \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$.
- $k$ even → non-constant coefficients of $T_k$ all even ✓
- $n = k-1$ is odd → $U_n(0) = 0$ and all coefficients of $U_n$ even ✓
- Leading coefficient of $f = (T_k - 1)/(2x)$: leading term $\frac{2^{k-1}x^k}{2
x} = 2^{k-2}x^{k-1} > 0$ ✓

**Case 2: $u = -T_k$, $w = \pm U_{k-1}$.** Need $-T_k(0) = 1$, so $k \equiv 2 \p
mod{4}$, giving $n \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$.
- All divisibility conditions are satisfied ✓
- **But** leading coefficient of $f = (-T_k - 1)/(2x)$ is $-2^{k-2} < 0$ ✗ — **f
ails the positive leading coefficient requirement.**

## Conclusion

Index $n$ is stable **if and only if** $n \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$.

**Verification ($n=3$, $k=4$):** $T_4 = 8x^4 - 8x^2 + 1$, so $f = 4x^3 - 4x$; $U
_3 = 8x^3 - 4x$, so $g = 4x^2 - 2$. One checks $xf^2 + f = 16x^7 - 32x^5 + 20x^3
 - 4x = (x^3-x)g^2$. ✓

The stable indices in $\{1, \dots, 100\}$ are $3, 7, 11, \dots, 99$, forming an
arithmetic progression with first term 3, common difference 4, last term 99:

$$\text{Count} = \frac{99 - 3}{4} + 1 = 25$$

## Answer
