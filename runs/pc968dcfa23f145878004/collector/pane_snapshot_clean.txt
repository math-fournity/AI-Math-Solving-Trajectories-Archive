## First integral (energy)

Multiply $\theta'' + \lambda\sin\theta = 0$ by $\theta'$ and integrate:

$$\tfrac12(\theta')^2 - \lambda\cos\theta = E,\qquad E\text{ constant}.$$

Let $\alpha\in(0,\pi)$ be the maximal deflection, attained where $\theta'=0$. Th
en $E=-\lambda\cos\alpha$, giving

$$(\theta')^2 = 2\lambda(\cos\theta - \cos\alpha). \tag{1}$$

## Reduction to standard elliptic form

Use the half-angle identity $\cos\theta-\cos\alpha = 2\bigl(\sin^2\tfrac\alpha2
- \sin^2\tfrac\theta2\bigr)$ and set

$$k := \sin\tfrac\alpha2 \in (0,1),\qquad \sin\tfrac{\theta(s)}2 = k\sin\phi(s).
 \tag{2}$$

Differentiating (2): $\tfrac12\cos\tfrac\theta2\,\theta' = k\cos\phi\,\phi'$, i.
e.

$$\theta' = \frac{2k\cos\phi}{\cos(\theta/2)}\,\phi' = \frac{2k\cos\phi}{\sqrt{1
-k^2\sin^2\phi}}\,\phi'. \tag{3}$$

Substituting (2) into (1): $\cos\theta-\cos\alpha = 2k^2\cos^2\phi$, so $\theta'
 = 2\sqrt\lambda\,k\cos\phi$ (positive branch on the rising quarter). Equating w
ith (3) and cancelling $2k\cos\phi$:

$$\frac{d\phi}{ds} = \sqrt\lambda\,\sqrt{1-k^2\sin^2\phi}. \tag{4}$$

Hence, with $F(\phi,k)=\int_0^\phi(1-k^2\sin^2 t)^{-1/2}dt$ the incomplete ellip
tic integral of the first kind,

$$\sqrt\lambda\,s = F(\phi(s),k). \tag{5}$$

## Imposing the boundary conditions

From $\theta(0)=0$ and (2): $\sin\phi(0)=0$, so $\phi(0)=0$ (consistent with (5)
 at $s=0$).

From $\theta(1)=0$: $k\sin\phi(1)=0$, so $\phi(1)=n\pi$ for some $n\in\mathbb N$
. Since $F(n\pi,k)=2nK(k)$ where $K(k)=F(\tfrac\pi2,k)$ is the complete elliptic
 integral, (5) at $s=1$ yields the **eigenvalue relation**

$$\boxed{\;\sqrt\lambda = 2nK(k),\qquad \lambda_n(k)=4n^2K(k)^2,\qquad n=1,2,3,\
dots\;} \tag{6}$$

## Closed-form solution via the Jacobi sine

Inverting (5) gives $\sin\phi = \operatorname{sn}(\sqrt\lambda\,s,k)$, the Jacob
i elliptic sine. Substituting into (2):

$$\boxed{\;\theta(s)=2\arcsin\!\bigl(k\,\operatorname{sn}(\sqrt\lambda\,s,\,k)\b
igr),\qquad \lambda=4n^2K(k)^2,\quad n=1,2,\dots,\; 0<k<1.\;} \tag{7}$$

## Verification

Let $u=\sqrt\lambda\,s$. From (7), $\sin(\theta/2)=k\operatorname{sn}u$, so $\co
s(\theta/2)=\operatorname{dn}u$ and

$$\theta' = \frac{2k\,\operatorname{cn}u\,\operatorname{dn}u}{\operatorname{dn}u
}\,\sqrt\lambda = 2\sqrt\lambda\,k\,\operatorname{cn}u,$$
$$\theta'' = 2\sqrt\lambda\,k\,(-\operatorname{sn}u\,\operatorname{dn}u)\sqrt\la
mbda = -2\lambda\,k\,\operatorname{sn}u\,\operatorname{dn}u.$$

Meanwhile $\sin\theta = 2\sin(\theta/2)\cos(\theta/2) = 2k\,\operatorname{sn}u\,
\operatorname{dn}u$, so $\lambda\sin\theta = 2\lambda k\,\operatorname{sn}u\,\op
eratorname{dn}u = -\theta''$. ✓

Boundary values: $\operatorname{sn}(0,k)=0\Rightarrow\theta(0)=0$; $\operatornam
e{sn}(2nK,k)=0\Rightarrow\theta(1)=0$. ✓

## Structure of the solution family

- **Trivial branch**: $k=0$ gives $\theta\equiv0$ for arbitrary $\lambda$ (the u
nbuckled state).
- **Mode $n$**: $\theta$ performs $n$ arches on $[0,1]$; $n=1$ is the first (pri
ncipal) buckling mode, $n=2$ the second, etc.
- **Bifurcation points**: as $k\to0^+$, $K(k)\to\pi/2$, so $\lambda_n\to 4n^2(\p
i/2)^2 = n^2\pi^2$. These are exactly the eigenvalues of the linearized problem
$\theta''+\lambda\theta=0$, $\theta(0)=\theta(1)=0$ — the classical Euler loads
$\lambda=n^2\pi^2$.
- **Post-buckling**: as $k\to1^-$, $K(k)\to\infty$, so $\lambda_n(k)\to\infty$;
each nontrivial branch emanates from $\lambda=n^2\pi^2$ and bends toward larger
$\lambda$, the hard-spring post-buckling behaviour of the elastica.

Equations (6)–(7) constitute the complete solution of the boundary value problem
 in terms of elliptic functions.
