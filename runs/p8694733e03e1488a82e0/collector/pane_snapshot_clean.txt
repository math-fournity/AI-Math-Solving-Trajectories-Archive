## Frobenius Solution of Bessel's Equation of Order `p` (p ∉ ℤ)

**Equation:** $x^2 y'' + x y' + (x^2 - p^2)y = 0$.

The point $x=0$ is a regular singular point, so we apply the Frobenius method. A
ssume

$$y = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n x^{n+r}, \qquad a_0 \neq 0.$$

Then $y' = \sum a_n(n+r)x^{n+r-1}$ and $y'' = \sum a_n(n+r)(n+r-1)x^{n+r-2}$. Su
bstituting:

$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n\big[(n+r)(n+r-1)+(n+r)-p^2\big]x^{n+r} + \sum_{n=0}^{\
infty} a_n x^{n+r+2} = 0,$$

i.e.

$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n\big[(n+r)^2 - p^2\big]x^{n+r} + \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_
n x^{n+r+2} = 0.$$

Shifting the index of the second sum ($n \to n-2$, valid for $n \ge 2$) and coll
ecting the coefficient of $x^{n+r}$:

- **$n=0$:** $a_0\,(r^2 - p^2) = 0$. Since $a_0 \neq 0$, the **indicial equation
** is $r^2 = p^2$, giving the two roots $r_1 = p$ and $r_2 = -p$.
- **$n=1$:** $a_1\big[(1+r)^2 - p^2\big] = 0$.
- **$n \ge 2$:** $a_n\big[(n+r)^2 - p^2\big] = -a_{n-2}$, i.e. the **recurrence*
*
$$a_n = -\frac{a_{n-2}}{(n+r)^2 - p^2} = -\frac{a_{n-2}}{(n+r-p)(n+r+p)}.$$

Since $p \notin \mathbb{Z}$, the difference $r_1 - r_2 = 2p$ is **not an integer
**, so both roots yield linearly independent Frobenius solutions (no logarithmic
 term appears).

### Solution 1: $r_1 = p$ (gives $y_1 = J_p(x)$)

With $r = p$ the recurrence becomes

$$a_n = -\frac{a_{n-2}}{(n+p)^2 - p^2} = -\frac{a_{n-2}}{n(n+2p)}, \qquad n \ge
2.$$

For $n=1$: $a_1\big[(1+p)^2-p^2\big] = a_1(1+2p) = 0$. Since $p\notin\mathbb{Z}$
, $1+2p\neq 0$, so $a_1 = 0$. By the recurrence, **all odd coefficients vanish**
: $a_{2k+1}=0$.

For even indices $n=2k$ ($k\ge 1$):

$$a_{2k} = -\frac{a_{2k-2}}{2k(2k+2p)} = -\frac{a_{2k-2}}{4\,k(k+p)}.$$

Iterating,

$$a_{2k} = \frac{(-1)^k\, a_0}{4^k\, k!\,(p+1)(p+2)\cdots(p+k)} = \frac{(-1)^k\,
 a_0\,\Gamma(p+1)}{4^k\, k!\,\Gamma(p+k+1)}.$$

Choosing the conventional normalization $a_0 = \dfrac{1}{2^p\,\Gamma(p+1)}$ give
s

$$a_{2k} = \frac{(-1)^k}{2^{\,2k+p}\, k!\, \Gamma(p+k+1)}.$$

Hence the first solution is the **Bessel function of the first kind of order $p$
**:

$$\boxed{\,y_1(x) = J_p(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k}{k!\,\Gamma(p+k+1)
}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2k+p}.\,}$$

### Solution 2: $r_2 = -p$ (gives $y_2 = J_{-p}(x)$)

With $r = -p$ the recurrence becomes

$$a_n = -\frac{a_{n-2}}{(n-p)^2 - p^2} = -\frac{a_{n-2}}{n(n-2p)}, \qquad n \ge
2.$$

For $n=1$: $a_1\big[(1-p)^2-p^2\big] = a_1(1-2p) = 0$. Since $p\notin\mathbb{Z}$
, $1-2p\neq 0$, so $a_1=0$, and again all odd coefficients vanish.

For $n=2k$:

$$a_{2k} = -\frac{a_{2k-2}}{2k(2k-2p)} = -\frac{a_{2k-2}}{4\,k(k-p)},$$

so

$$a_{2k} = \frac{(-1)^k\, a_0}{4^k\, k!\,(1-p)(2-p)\cdots(k-p)} = \frac{(-1)^k\,
 a_0\,\Gamma(1-p)}{4^k\, k!\,\Gamma(k-p+1)}.$$

Choosing $a_0 = \dfrac{1}{2^{-p}\,\Gamma(1-p)} = \dfrac{2^p}{\Gamma(1-p)}$ gives

$$a_{2k} = \frac{(-1)^k}{2^{\,2k-p}\, k!\, \Gamma(k-p+1)}.$$

Hence the second solution is the **Bessel function of the first kind of order $-
p$**:

$$\boxed{\,y_2(x) = J_{-p}(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k}{k!\,\Gamma(k-p
+1)}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2k-p}.\,}$$

### Linear Independence

Near $x=0$ the leading terms are

$$J_p(x) \sim \frac{1}{\Gamma(p+1)}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{p}, \qquad J_{-p}(x
) \sim \frac{1}{\Gamma(1-p)}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{-p}.$$

Since $p\notin\mathbb{Z}$, the exponents $p$ and $-p$ are **distinct**, so $J_p$
 and $J_{-p}$ cannot be constant multiples of one another. Equivalently, their W
ronskian is nonvanishing:

$$W[J_p, J_{-p}](x) = -\frac{2\sin(p\pi)}{\pi x} \neq 0 \quad (p\notin\mathbb{Z}
).$$

Therefore $\{J_p(x),\, J_{-p}(x)\}$ forms a fundamental system, and the **genera
l solution** of Bessel's equation (for non-integer $p$) is

$$\boxed{\,y(x) = C_1\, J_p(x) + C_2\, J_{-p}(x),\qquad p \notin \mathbb{Z}.\,}$
$
