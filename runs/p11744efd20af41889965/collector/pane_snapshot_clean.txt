## Proof

**Step 1: Rewrite the general term.**

For each $n$, write
$$\pi\sqrt{n^2+a^2} = n\pi + \pi\!\left(\sqrt{n^2+a^2}-n\right).$$

Rationalizing:
$$\sqrt{n^2+a^2}-n = \frac{a^2}{\sqrt{n^2+a^2}+n}.$$

Set
$$\theta_n := \frac{\pi a^2}{\sqrt{n^2+a^2}+n}.$$

Since $\sin(n\pi + x) = (-1)^n\sin x$, the general term becomes
$$\sin\!\left(\pi\sqrt{n^2+a^2}\right) = (-1)^n \sin(\theta_n).$$

**Step 2: Properties of $\theta_n$.**

- $\theta_n > 0$ for $a \neq 0$ (and $\theta_n = 0$ for $a=0$).
- $\theta_n \to 0$ as $n\to\infty$, since the denominator $\sqrt{n^2+a^2}+n \to
\infty$.
- $\theta_n$ is **strictly decreasing** in $n$: the denominator $\sqrt{n^2+a^2}+
n$ is increasing in $n$ (both $\sqrt{n^2+a^2}$ and $n$ increase), so $\theta_n$
decreases.

**Step 3: Case $a = 0$.**

Then $\theta_n = 0$ for all $n$, so every term is $(-1)^n\sin(0)=0$. The series
converges (to $0$).

**Step 4: Case $a \neq 0$ — apply the Leibniz alternating series test.**

Since $\theta_n \to 0$, there exists $N$ such that for all $n \geq N$, $0 < \the
ta_n < \pi/2$.

For $n \geq N$:
- $\theta_n \in (0, \pi/2)$ and $\theta_n$ is decreasing, so $b_n := \sin(\theta
_n)$ is **positive and decreasing** (sine is increasing on $(0,\pi/2)$, and $\th
eta_n$ decreases).
- $b_n \to 0$ (since $\theta_n \to 0$).

Therefore $\sum_{n=N}^{\infty} (-1)^n b_n$ is an alternating series with terms d
ecreasing to $0$. By the **Leibniz alternating series test**, it converges.

Adding back the finitely many terms $n = 1, \ldots, N-1$ does not affect converg
ence, so $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^n \sin(\theta_n)$ converges.

**Step 5: Convergence is conditional for $a\neq 0$ (not required, but noted for
completeness).**

For $n \geq N$, $\sin(\theta_n) \sim \theta_n \sim \frac{\pi a^2}{2n}$, so $\sum
 |(-1)^n\sin(\theta_n)| \sim \frac{\pi a^2}{2}\sum \frac{1}{n} = \infty$. The co
nvergence is conditional, not absolute.

**Conclusion.**

The series converges for every $a \in \mathbb{R}$: absolutely when $a=0$, and co
nditionally when $a\neq 0$.
