Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether, given a Borel probability
measure $\mu$ on $\mathbb{R}$ and a Borel set $S$ with $\mu(S)>0$, there always
exist the required sets satisfying all stated conditions.

## Answer: Yes, such sets always exist.

**Proof.**

Let $\mu$ be a Borel probability measure on $\mathbb{R}$ and let $S\subseteq\mat
hbb{R}$ be a Borel set with $\mu(S)>0$.

We construct the required sets explicitly. Set:
$$A_1 = S, \qquad A_2 = \emptyset, \qquad \widetilde{A_1} = S, \qquad \widetilde
{A_2} = \emptyset.$$

We verify each condition in the problem statement:

1. **Measurability:** $S$ is Borel by hypothesis, and $\emptyset$ is Borel, so a
ll four sets are Borel measurable.

2. **Subset conditions:** $\widetilde{A_1} = S \subseteq S = A_1$ and $\widetild
e{A_2} = \emptyset \subseteq \emptyset = A_2$. ✓

3. **Disjointness of $\widetilde{A_1}$ and $\widetilde{A_2}$:** $\widetilde{A_1}
 \cap \widetilde{A_2} = S \cap \emptyset = \emptyset$. ✓

4. **Subsets of $S$:** $\widetilde{A_1} = S \subseteq S$ and $\widetilde{A_2} =
\emptyset \subseteq S$. ✓

5. **Covering condition:** $A_1 \cup A_2 = S \cup \emptyset = S$. ✓

6. **Positive measure:** $\mu(\widetilde{A_1}) = \mu(S) > 0$ by hypothesis, so t
he condition "$\mu(\widetilde{A_1})>0$ or $\mu(\widetilde{A_2})>0$" is satisfied
. ✓

All conditions are met. Therefore, for any Borel probability measure $\mu$ on $\
mathbb{R}$ and any Borel set $S$ with $\mu(S)>0$, such sets always exist. $\squa
re$

**Remark (on the strengthened variant).** The question becomes non-trivial if on
e strengthens "either $\mu(\widetilde{A_1})>0$ or $\mu(\widetilde{A_2})>0$" to r
equire **both** $\mu(\widetilde{A_1})>0$ **and** $\mu(\widetilde{A_2})>0$ — that
 is, whether $S$ can always be partitioned into two disjoint parts each carrying
 positive measure. Under this strengthening the answer is **no** in general: tak
ing $\mu = \delta_0$ (the Dirac measure at the origin) and $S = \{0\}$, we have
$\mu(S)=1>0$, yet any partition of $S$ into two disjoint sets assigns $\{0\}$ to
 one part (measure $1$) and $\emptyset$ to the other (measure $0$), so both part
s cannot simultaneously have positive measure. The strengthened statement holds
precisely when $S$ is not an atom of $\mu$; in particular, it holds for all non-
atomic measures by Sierpiński's intermediate value theorem (which guarantees tha
t a non-atomic measure attains every value in $[0, \mu(S)]$ on some measurable s
ubset of $S$).
