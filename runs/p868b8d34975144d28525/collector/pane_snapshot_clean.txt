## Proof

**Yes**, $f^{-1}(q)$ is finite for every $q \in N$.

**Key observation.** Since $\dim M = \dim N$ and $f: M \to N$ is a smooth immers
ion, the differential $df_p : T_p M \to T_{f(p)} N$ is injective for every $p \i
n M$. Because the source and target have equal dimension, injectivity implies $d
f_p$ is an isomorphism for every $p$.

By the **inverse function theorem**, $f$ is a **local diffeomorphism**: for ever
y $p \in M$ there exists an open neighborhood $U_p \ni p$ such that $f|_{U_p} :
U_p \to f(U_p)$ is a diffeomorphism onto an open subset of $N$.

**Finiteness of fibers.** Fix any $q \in N$.

1. **$f^{-1}(q)$ is discrete.** For any $p \in f^{-1}(q)$, the local diffeomorph
ism property gives a neighborhood $U_p$ on which $f$ is injective, so $U_p \cap
f^{-1}(q) = \{p\}$. Every point of $f^{-1}(q)$ is isolated.

2. **$f^{-1}(q)$ is closed in $M$.** It is the preimage of the closed set $\{q\}
$ under the continuous map $f$.

3. **$f^{-1}(q)$ is compact.** Since $M$ is compact and $f^{-1}(q)$ is closed in
 $M$.

4. **A compact discrete space is finite.** The open cover $\{\{p\} : p \in f^{-1
}(q)\}$ by singletons (each open in the subspace topology since points are isola
ted) must admit a finite subcover, so $f^{-1}(q)$ is finite.

This holds for every $q \in N$, so the answer is **yes**.

**Remark.** The uniform convergence hypothesis is not needed for this conclusion
. (In fact, one can additionally show—using that each $f_n$ is a diffeomorphism
onto $N$ by compactness + connectedness + invariance of domain, and that suffici
ently $C^0$-close maps between compact manifolds are homotopic—that $\deg f = 1$
, so $f$ is in fact a diffeomorphism. But the finiteness of fibers follows alrea
dy from $f$ being an immersion with $M$ compact.)
