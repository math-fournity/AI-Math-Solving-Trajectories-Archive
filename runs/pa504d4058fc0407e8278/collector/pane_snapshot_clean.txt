**Answer: Yes, there exists an orthonormal basis for $L^2(\mathbb{R}^3)$.**

## Proof

We establish the existence of an orthonormal basis (in the Hilbert-space sense:
a maximal orthonormal set, equivalently a complete orthonormal system) for $L^2(
\mathbb{R}^3)$ in two ways: a general structural argument, and an explicit const
ruction.

### Part 1. $L^2(\mathbb{R}^3)$ is a separable Hilbert space

$L^2(\mathbb{R}^3)$ with the inner product
$$\langle f, g\rangle = \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} f(x)\overline{g(x)}\,dx$$
is complete (Riesz–Fischer theorem), hence a Hilbert space.

**Separability.** Let $\mathcal{B}$ be the collection of indicator functions $\m
athbf{1}_{Q}$ where $Q = [a_1,b_1]\times[a_2,b_2]\times[a_3,b_3]$ ranges over al
l axis-aligned boxes with rational endpoints. This is a countable family. The al
gebra generated by these indicators (finite linear combinations with rational co
efficients) is countable and dense in the simple functions, which are dense in $
L^2(\mathbb{R}^3)$. Hence $L^2(\mathbb{R}^3)$ is separable.

### Part 2. Every separable Hilbert space has an orthonormal basis

Let $\{u_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ be a countable dense subset of a separable Hilbert sp
ace $H$ with $u_n \neq 0$. Apply the Gram–Schmidt process: define inductively
$$e_1 = \frac{u_1}{\|u_1\|}, \qquad v_n = u_n - \sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\langle u_n, e_k
\rangle e_k, \qquad e_n = \frac{v_n}{\|v_n\|}\ \ (\text{when } v_n \neq 0).$$
Discarding any zero $v_n$, we obtain a countable orthonormal set $\{e_n\}$.

**Completeness.** Suppose $f \in H$ satisfies $\langle f, e_n\rangle = 0$ for al
l $n$. Since $\{u_n\}$ is dense, there is a subsequence $u_{n_j} \to f$. Each $u
_{n_j}$ lies in $\operatorname{span}\{e_1,\dots,e_{n_j}\}$ (by construction of G
ram–Schmidt), so $\langle f, u_{n_j}\rangle = 0$ for all $j$. By continuity of t
he inner product, $\langle f, f\rangle = \lim_j \langle f, u_{n_j}\rangle = 0$,
so $f = 0$. Thus $\{e_n\}$ is a maximal orthonormal set, i.e., an orthonormal ba
sis.

Applying this to $H = L^2(\mathbb{R}^3)$ yields an orthonormal basis.

### Part 3. Explicit construction via Hermite functions

For an explicit basis, recall the **Hermite functions**
$$h_n(x) = \frac{1}{(2^n n!\sqrt{\pi})^{1/2}}\, e^{-x^2/2}\, H_n(x), \qquad n =
0,1,2,\dots,$$
where $H_n$ are the physicists' Hermite polynomials. The family $\{h_n\}_{n \ge
0}$ is a complete orthonormal system in $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ (this is classical: th
ey are eigenfunctions of the self-adjoint harmonic oscillator, whose eigenfuncti
ons form a complete set).

Now form the tensor products on $\mathbb{R}^3$:
$$\Phi_{\mathbf{n}}(x_1,x_2,x_3) = h_{n_1}(x_1)\,h_{n_2}(x_2)\,h_{n_3}(x_3), \qq
uad \mathbf{n} = (n_1,n_2,n_3) \in \mathbb{N}_0^3.$$

- **Orthonormality:** By Fubini's theorem,
$$\langle \Phi_{\mathbf{m}}, \Phi_{\mathbf{n}}\rangle = \prod_{i=1}^{3}\langle h
_{m_i}, h_{n_i}\rangle_{L^2(\mathbb{R})} = \prod_{i=1}^{3}\delta_{m_i,n_i} = \de
lta_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}}.$$

- **Completeness:** Suppose $f \in L^2(\mathbb{R}^3)$ with $\langle f, \Phi_{\ma
thbf{n}}\rangle = 0$ for all $\mathbf{n}$. For a.e. fixed $(x_2,x_3)$, the funct
ion $x_1 \mapsto f(x_1,x_2,x_3)$ is in $L^2(\mathbb{R})$, and $\int f(x_1,x_2,x_
3)\overline{h_{n_1}(x_1)}\,dx_1 = 0$ for all $n_1$ (by Fubini and the vanishing
of the full inner product). Since $\{h_n\}$ is complete in $L^2(\mathbb{R})$, $f
(\cdot, x_2, x_3) = 0$ for a.e. $x_1$, for a.e. $(x_2,x_3)$. Hence $f = 0$ a.e.
on $\mathbb{R}^3$.

Therefore $\{\Phi_{\mathbf{n}} : \mathbf{n} \in \mathbb{N}_0^3\}$ is a complete
orthonormal system — an orthonormal basis — of $L^2(\mathbb{R}^3)$.

### Conclusion

$L^2(\mathbb{R}^3)$ is a separable Hilbert space, and every separable Hilbert sp
ace admits a countable orthonormal basis. Concretely, the tensor products of Her
mite functions provide such a basis. Hence **yes**, an orthonormal basis for $L^
2(\mathbb{R}^3)$ exists.
