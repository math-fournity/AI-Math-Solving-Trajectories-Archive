# Solution

**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

Let $\mu$ be the counting measure on $X$. We use the standard identity for the i
ntegral against counting measure:

$$\int_X f \, d\mu = \sup\left\{\sum_{x \in F} f(x) : F \subseteq X,\ F \text{ f
inite}\right\}.$$

**Justification of the identity.** The integral is $\sup\{\int s\,d\mu : 0 \le s
 \le f,\ s \text{ simple}\}$. Any simple $s = \sum_{i=1}^n a_i \mathbf{1}_{A_i}$
 with $a_i > 0$ and $A_i$ finite satisfies $\int s\,d\mu = \sum_i a_i |A_i| = \s
um_{x \in \bigcup A_i} s(x) \le \sum_{x \in \bigcup A_i} f(x)$, a finite partial
 sum of $f$. Conversely, for any finite $F$, the simple function $s = \sum_{x \i
n F} f(x)\,\mathbf{1}_{\{x\}} \le f$ has $\int s\,d\mu = \sum_{x\in F} f(x)$. If
 some $A_i$ with $a_i > 0$ is infinite, then $\int s\,d\mu = \infty$, forcing $\
int f\,d\mu = \infty$; in this case $f(x) \ge a_i > 0$ for all $x \in A_i$, so a
ny countably infinite subset of $A_i$ already yields an infinite sum. This estab
lishes the identity (with both sides possibly $+\infty$).

Now suppose $\int_X f\,d\mu = \infty$. We consider two cases.

**Case 1: There exists $x_0 \in X$ with $f(x_0) = \infty$.**
Take $S = \{x_0\}$, which is countable. Then $\sum_{x \in S} f(x) = f(x_0) = \in
fty$. ∎

**Case 2: $f(x) < \infty$ for all $x \in X$.**
By the identity above, $\sup\{\sum_{x \in F} f(x) : F \text{ finite}\} = \infty$
. For each $n \in \mathbb{N}$, there exists a finite set $F_n \subseteq X$ with
$\sum_{x \in F_n} f(x) > n$. Define
$$S = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} F_n.$$
Then $S$ is a countable union of finite sets, hence countable. Moreover, for eve
ry $n$,
$$\sum_{x \in S} f(x) \ge \sum_{x \in F_n} f(x) > n.$$
Since this holds for all $n$, we conclude $\sum_{x \in S} f(x) = \infty$. ∎

In both cases, a countable subset $S \subset X$ with $\sum_{x \in S} f(x) = \inf
ty$ exists. $\blacksquare$

### PROOF COMPLETE
