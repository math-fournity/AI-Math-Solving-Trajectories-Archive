## Solution

We evaluate $I = \displaystyle\int_0^\pi \dfrac{d\theta}{5+4\cos\theta}$ by cont
our integration.

### Step 1. Extend to a full period

Since $\cos(2\pi-\theta)=\cos\theta$, the integrand is symmetric about $\theta=\
pi$, so

$$\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{5+4\cos\theta}=2\int_0^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{5+4\cos
\theta}=2I.$$

Hence $I=\dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{5+4\cos\theta}$.

### Step 2. Convert to a contour integral

Set $z=e^{i\theta}$, so $d\theta=\dfrac{dz}{iz}$ and $\cos\theta=\dfrac{z+z^{-1}
}{2}=\dfrac{z^2+1}{2z}$. The unit circle $|z|=1$ is traversed once as $\theta$ r
uns from $0$ to $2\pi$:

$$\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{5+4\cos\theta}=\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{1}{5+\dfrac{2(z^
2+1)}{z}}\cdot\frac{dz}{iz}=\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{z}{5z+2z^2+2}\cdot\frac{dz}{iz}=\
frac{1}{i}\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{dz}{2z^2+5z+2}.$$

### Step 3. Locate the poles

Factor the denominator:

$$2z^2+5z+2=(2z+1)(z+2)=2\!\left(z+\tfrac12\right)(z+2).$$

The poles are at $z=-\tfrac12$ and $z=-2$. Only $z=-\tfrac12$ lies inside $|z|=1
$ (since $|-\tfrac12|=\tfrac12<1$), while $z=-2$ lies outside.

### Step 4. Compute the residue

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=-1/2}\frac{1}{2(z+\frac12)(z+2)}=\lim_{z\to -1/2}\frac{1
}{2(z+2)}=\frac{1}{2\cdot\frac32}=\frac{1}{3}.$$

### Step 5. Apply the residue theorem

$$\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{dz}{2z^2+5z+2}=2\pi i\cdot\frac{1}{3}=\frac{2\pi i}{3}.$$

Therefore

$$\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{5+4\cos\theta}=\frac{1}{i}\cdot\frac{2\pi i}{3}=\f
rac{2\pi}{3},$$

and finally

$$I=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2\pi}{3}=\boxed{\dfrac{\pi}{3}}.$$

### Verification (sanity check)

The standard formula $\displaystyle\int_0^\pi\frac{d\theta}{a+b\cos\theta}=\frac
{\pi}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}$ for $a>|b|$ gives, with $a=5,\,b=4$:

$$\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{25-16}}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{9}}=\frac{\pi}{3},\qquad\checkmark$$

confirming the contour-integration result.
