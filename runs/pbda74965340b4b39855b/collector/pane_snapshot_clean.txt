## Proof

**Step 1: Reduce to a two-dimensional recurrence.**

Separate the $x$-dependent and $t$-dependent parts:

$$f_{n+1}(x)=\cos x\sin x\!\int_0^{\pi/2}\!f_n(t)\,dt\;-\;\cos x\!\int_0^{\pi/2}
\!\sin t\,f_n(t)\,dt.$$

Define the two scalars
$$A_n=\int_0^{\pi/2}f_n(t)\,dt,\qquad B_n=\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin t\,f_n(t)\,dt.$$

Then
$$f_{n+1}(x)=\cos x\bigl(A_n\sin x-B_n\bigr),\qquad n\ge 1. \tag{1}$$

So for every $n\ge 2$, $f_n(x)=\cos x\,(a_n\sin x-b_n)$ with $a_n=A_{n-1},\; b_n
=B_{n-1}$.

**Step 2: Compute $A_1,B_1$ and the recurrence for $A_n,B_n$.**

$$A_1=\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos t\,dt=1,\qquad B_1=\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin t\cos t\,dt=\tfra
c12.$$

For $n\ge 2$, using $f_n(t)=\cos t\,(a_n\sin t-b_n)$ and the elementary integral
s
$$\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin t\cos t\,dt=\tfrac12,\quad \int_0^{\pi/2}\cos t\,dt=1,\quad
 \int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^2 t\cos t\,dt=\tfrac13,$$

we get
$$A_n=\tfrac12\,a_n-b_n,\qquad B_n=\tfrac13\,a_n-\tfrac12\,b_n.$$

Since $a_{n+1}=A_n,\;b_{n+1}=B_n$, the recurrence (for $n\ge 2$) is

$$\begin{pmatrix}a_{n+1}\\[2pt] b_{n+1}\end{pmatrix}=\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}
\tfrac12&-1\\[2pt]\tfrac13&-\tfrac12\end{pmatrix}}_{M}\begin{pmatrix}a_n\\[2pt]
b_n\end{pmatrix},\qquad \begin{pmatrix}a_2\\b_2\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\[
2pt]\tfrac12\end{pmatrix}. \tag{2}$$

**Step 3: Diagonalize via Cayley–Hamilton.**

The characteristic polynomial of $M$ is
$$\det(M-\lambda I)=\bigl(\tfrac12-\lambda\bigr)\bigl(-\tfrac12-\lambda\bigr)+\t
frac13=\lambda^2+\tfrac{1}{12}.$$

Setting $\omega^2=\tfrac{1}{12}$ (i.e.\ $\omega=\frac{1}{2\sqrt3}$), Cayley–Hami
lton gives

$$\boxed{M^2=-\omega^2\,I=-\tfrac{1}{12}\,I.} \tag{3}$$

Therefore
$$M^{2j}=\bigl(-\tfrac{1}{12}\bigr)^{j}I,\qquad M^{2j+1}=\bigl(-\tfrac{1}{12}\bi
gr)^{j}M. \tag{4}$$

**Step 4: Extract $a_n,b_n$ by parity.**

With $k=n-2$ and $\binom{a_2}{b_2}=\binom{1}{1/2}$:

- **$n$ even**, $n=2m$ ($m\ge1$): $k=2(m-1)$, so
$$\binom{a_{2m}}{b_{2m}}=\bigl(-\tfrac{1}{12}\bigr)^{m-1}\binom{1}{1/2}.$$
Hence $a_{2m}=\bigl(-\frac{1}{12}\bigr)^{m-1},\; b_{2m}=\frac12\bigl(-\frac{1}{1
2}\bigr)^{m-1}$, giving
$$f_{2m}(x)=\bigl(-\tfrac{1}{12}\bigr)^{m-1}\cos x\Bigl(\sin x-\tfrac12\Bigr). \
tag{5}$$

- **$n$ odd**, $n=2m+1$ ($m\ge1$): $k=2(m-1)+1$, so
$$\binom{a_{2m+1}}{b_{2m+1}}=\bigl(-\tfrac{1}{12}\bigr)^{m-1}M\binom{1}{1/2}=\bi
gl(-\tfrac{1}{12}\bigr)^{m-1}\binom{0}{1/12}=\binom{0}{\,(-1/12)^{m}\,}.$$

(The last step uses $M\binom{1}{1/2}=\binom{1/2-1/2}{1/3-1/4}=\binom{0}{1/12}$ a
nd $\frac{1}{12}(-\frac{1}{12})^{m-1}=(-\frac{1}{12})^{m}\cdot(-1)\cdot\frac{1}{
12}\cdot\frac{1}{(-1/12)^{m-1}}$... more directly: $\frac{1}{12}\bigl(-\frac{1}{
12}\bigr)^{m-1}=-\bigl(-\frac{1}{12}\bigr)^{m}$.)

Hence $a_{2m+1}=0,\; b_{2m+1}=-\bigl(-\frac{1}{12}\bigr)^{m}$, giving
$$f_{2m+1}(x)=\bigl(-\tfrac{1}{12}\bigr)^{m}\cos x. \tag{6}$$

For $m=0$ this gives $f_1(x)=\cos x$, matching the initial condition. ✓

**Step 5: Verification.**

- $f_1(x)=\cos x$. ✓ (formula (6), $m=0$)
- $f_2(x)=\cos x(\sin x-\frac12)$. Directly: $\cos x[\sin x\cdot A_1-B_1]=\cos x
[\sin x-\frac12]$. ✓ (formula (5), $m=1$)
- $f_3(x)=-\frac{1}{12}\cos x$. Directly: $A_2=\frac12\cdot1-\frac12=0$, $B_2=\f
rac13-\frac14=\frac1{12}$, so $f_3=\cos x[0-\frac1{12}]$. ✓ (formula (6), $m=1$)
- $f_4(x)=-\frac{1}{12}\cos x(\sin x-\frac12)$. From (5) with $m=2$. Check: $A_3
=\frac12\cdot0-\frac1{12}=-\frac1{12}$, $B_3=\frac13\cdot0-\frac12\cdot\frac1{12
}=-\frac1{24}$, so $f_4=\cos x[-\frac1{12}\sin x+\frac1{24}]=-\frac1{12}\cos x(\
sin x-\frac12)$. ✓

## Result

$$\boxed{f_n(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\left(-\frac{1}{12}\right)^{\!\frac{n-
1}{2}}\cos x, & n\ \text{odd},\\[10pt] \displaystyle\left(-\frac{1}{12}\right)^{
\!\frac{n}{2}-1}\cos x\!\left(\sin x-\frac{1}{2}\right), & n\ \text{even}.\end{c
ases}}$$

Equivalently, with $\omega=\frac{1}{2\sqrt3}$ (so that $M^2=-\omega^2 I$): for o
dd $n=2m+1\ge1$, $f_n=(-\omega^2)^m\cos x$; for even $n=2m\ge2$, $f_n=(-\omega^2
)^{m-1}\cos x(\sin x-\frac12)$.
