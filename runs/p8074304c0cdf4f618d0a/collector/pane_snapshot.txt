## Proof

We bound $I_n$ in absolute value and show it tends to $0$.

**Step 1: Absolute value bound.** Since $|\cos(\theta)| \leq 1$ for all real $\t
heta$,

$$|I_n| = \left|\int_{\mathbb{R}} \frac{\cos(\sqrt{x^2+n^2})}{x^2+n^2}\,d\lambda
\right| \leq \int_{\mathbb{R}} \frac{|\cos(\sqrt{x^2+n^2})|}{x^2+n^2}\,d\lambda
\leq \int_{\mathbb{R}} \frac{1}{x^2+n^2}\,d\lambda.$$

**Step 2: Evaluate the dominating integral.** For $n > 0$, substitute $u = x/n$,
 $d\lambda = n\,du$:

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} \frac{1}{x^2+n^2}\,dx = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2
(u^2+1)}\cdot n\,du = \frac{1}{n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{du}{u^2+1} = \frac
{\pi}{n}.$$

**Step 3: Take the limit.** Combining Steps 1 and 2:

$$0 \leq |I_n| \leq \frac{\pi}{n}.$$

Since $\frac{\pi}{n} \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$, by the squeeze theorem:

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} |I_n| = 0,$$

and therefore

$$\boxed{\lim_{n \to \infty} I_n = 0.}$$
