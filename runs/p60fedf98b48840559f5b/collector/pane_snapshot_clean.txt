## Solution

We analyze the convergence of
$$I(\beta) = \int_0^\infty \frac{\arctan(\ln^2 x)}{|\beta - x|^e}\,dx, \qquad \b
eta > 0,$$
where $e = 2.718\ldots$ is Euler's number. We examine the three potential source
s of divergence: $x \to 0^+$, $x \to \beta$, and $x \to \infty$.

---

### Region 1: $x \to 0^+$

As $x \to 0^+$, $\ln x \to -\infty$, so $\ln^2 x \to +\infty$ and $\arctan(\ln^2
 x) \to \pi/2$. The denominator $|\beta - x|^e \to \beta^e > 0$. Hence the integ
rand tends to the finite constant $\frac{\pi/2}{\beta^e}$, so the integral over
any interval $(0,\varepsilon)$ converges. **No restriction on $\beta$.**

---

### Region 2: $x \to \infty$

As $x \to \infty$, $\ln^2 x \to \infty$, so $\arctan(\ln^2 x) \to \pi/2$, and $|
\beta - x|^e \sim x^e$. Thus the integrand satisfies
$$\frac{\arctan(\ln^2 x)}{|\beta - x|^e} \sim \frac{\pi/2}{x^e}.$$
Since $e > 1$, the integral $\int_M^\infty x^{-e}\,dx$ converges. **No restricti
on on $\beta$.**

---

### Region 3: $x \to \beta$ (the critical singularity)

This is the decisive region. Set $c := \arctan(\ln^2\beta) \geq 0$.

**Case $\beta \neq 1$:** Then $\ln\beta \neq 0$, so $\ln^2\beta > 0$ and $c = \a
rctan(\ln^2\beta) > 0$. Near $x = \beta$ the integrand behaves as
$$\frac{\arctan(\ln^2 x)}{|\beta - x|^e} \sim \frac{c}{|x - \beta|^e}, \qquad c
> 0.$$
The integral $\int_{\beta-\delta}^{\beta+\delta} |x-\beta|^{-e}\,dx$ converges i
f and only if $e < 1$. Since $e \approx 2.718 > 1$, **the integral diverges for
every $\beta \neq 1$.**

**Case $\beta = 1$:** Here $\ln 1 = 0$, so the numerator also vanishes at $x = 1
$, and we must expand to higher order. Set $x = 1 + t$ with $t \to 0$. Then
$$\ln x = \ln(1+t) = t - \tfrac{t^2}{2} + O(t^3), \qquad \ln^2 x = t^2 + O(t^3),
$$
$$\arctan(\ln^2 x) = \ln^2 x + O((\ln^2 x)^3) = t^2 + O(t^3).$$
The denominator is $|1 - x|^e = |t|^e$. Therefore
$$\frac{\arctan(\ln^2 x)}{|1 - x|^e} \sim \frac{t^2}{|t|^e} = |t|^{2-e}.$$
The integral $\int_{-\delta}^{\delta} |t|^{2-e}\,dt$ converges if and only if $2
 - e > -1$, i.e., $e < 3$. Since $e \approx 2.718 < 3$, **the integral converges
 at $x = 1$.**

---

### Conclusion

Combining all three regions:
- For $\beta = 1$: all three regions yield convergent integrals.
- For $\beta \neq 1$: the singularity at $x = \beta$ produces a non-integrable d
ivergence (since $e > 1$ and the numerator is nonzero there).

The integral converges if and only if $\boxed{\beta = 1}$.
