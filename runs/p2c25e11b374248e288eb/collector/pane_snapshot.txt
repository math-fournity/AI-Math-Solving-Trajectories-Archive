## Proof

**Yes, it can be deduced that $F \circ \mathbf{x}$ is Euclidean differentiable a
s a function from $\mathbb{R}^2$ to $\mathbb{R}^3$.**

Fix any coordinate patch $\mathbf{x}$ on $M$. We wish to show that $F \circ \mat
hbf{x}: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ is Euclidean differentiable.

Choose any coordinate patch $\mathbf{y}$ on $N$. By hypothesis, the map
$$g := \mathbf{y}^{-1} \circ F \circ \mathbf{x}: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$$
is Euclidean differentiable.

Now observe the key factorization:
$$F \circ \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{y} \circ \bigl(\mathbf{y}^{-1} \circ F \circ \mat
hbf{x}\bigr) = \mathbf{y} \circ g.$$

This identity holds because $\mathbf{y} \circ \mathbf{y}^{-1} = \mathrm{id}$ on
the image of $\mathbf{y}$ (which is where $F \circ \mathbf{x}$ takes values, sin
ce $F: M \to N$ and $\mathbf{y}$ parametrizes a patch of $N$).

We now analyze the two factors:

1. **$g = \mathbf{y}^{-1} \circ F \circ \mathbf{x}$ is Euclidean differentiable*
* — this is given by the hypothesis.

2. **$\mathbf{y}: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ is Euclidean differentiable** —
 this is part of the *definition* of a coordinate patch (parametrization) of a s
urface in $\mathbb{R}^3$. A coordinate patch on a regular surface is, by definit
ion, a smooth (in particular, differentiable) map from an open subset of $\mathb
b{R}^2$ into $\mathbb{R}^3$ whose differential has rank 2 everywhere.

Since $g$ maps into the domain of $\mathbf{y}$ (namely the open subset of $\math
bb{R}^2$ on which $\mathbf{y}$ is defined), the composition $\mathbf{y} \circ g$
 is well-defined. The composition of Euclidean differentiable maps (between Eucl
idean spaces) is Euclidean differentiable — this is the **chain rule**.

Therefore:
$$F \circ \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{y} \circ g$$
is Euclidean differentiable as a map from $\mathbb{R}^2$ to $\mathbb{R}^3$. $\bl
acksquare$
