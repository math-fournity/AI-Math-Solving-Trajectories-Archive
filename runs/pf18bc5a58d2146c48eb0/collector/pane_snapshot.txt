Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every bounded geodesic on t
he modular surface $M = \mathbb{H}/SL(2,\mathbb{Z})$ is closed.

**Answer: No.** Not every bounded geodesic on $M$ is closed. I construct an expl
icit counterexample.

---

## Proof

**Preliminaries.** Since $-I \in SL(2,\mathbb{Z})$ acts trivially on $\mathbb{H}
$, the quotient $M = \mathbb{H}/SL(2,\mathbb{Z})$ equals $\mathbb{H}/PSL(2,\math
bb{Z})$. The modular surface is a finite-area hyperbolic orbifold with two cone
points (orders 2 and 3) and **one cusp** (at the image of $\infty$). The cusp is
 the unique non-compact end of $M$.

**Geodesics via endpoints.** Every geodesic on $M$ lifts to a geodesic $\tilde{\
gamma}$ in $\mathbb{H}$, determined by its two endpoints $\alpha, \beta \in \par
tial\mathbb{H} = \mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty\}$.

**Claim 1 (Boundedness criterion).** *The projected geodesic $\gamma = \pi \circ
 \tilde{\gamma}$ is bounded on $M$ if and only if both $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are
irrational.*

*Proof of Claim 1.* The orbit of $\infty$ under $PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})$ is $\mathbb{
Q} \cup \{\infty\}$. If $\beta \in \mathbb{Q} \cup \{\infty\}$, there exists $g
\in PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})$ with $g(\beta) = \infty$. Then $g(\tilde{\gamma}(t)) \to
\infty$ as $t \to +\infty$, so $\gamma(t)$ escapes into the cusp and is unbounde
d. Conversely, if $\beta$ is irrational, no element of $PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})$ maps
$\beta$ to $\infty$, so $\gamma$ does not escape to the cusp in the forward dire
ction; similarly for $\alpha$ in the backward direction. Since the cusp is the o
nly end of $M$, not escaping to the cusp is equivalent to being bounded. $\squar
e$

**Claim 2 (Closedness criterion).** *The geodesic $\gamma$ is closed if and only
 if $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are conjugate quadratic irrationals (i.e., roots of the
 same irreducible quadratic polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}$).*

*Proof of Claim 2.* The geodesic $\gamma$ is closed iff some hyperbolic $g \in P
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})$ preserves $\tilde{\gamma}$, i.e., $g(\{\alpha,\beta\}) = \{\al
pha,\beta\}$. If $g$ fixes both endpoints, they are the two fixed points of $g =
 \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$, which are the roots of $cz^2 + (
d-a)z - b = 0$ — a quadratic with integer coefficients, hence conjugate quadrati
c irrationals. If $g$ swaps the endpoints, then $g^2$ fixes both, and $g^2$ is h
yperbolic with fixed points $\alpha, \beta$, again yielding conjugate quadratic
irrationals. Conversely, if $\alpha, \beta$ are conjugate quadratic irrationals
(roots of $Ax^2+Bx+C=0$, $A,B,C\in\mathbb{Z}$, discriminant a non-square positiv
e integer), the stabilizer of $\alpha$ in $PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})$ is infinite cyclic
, generated by a hyperbolic element that also fixes $\beta$ (its Galois conjugat
e). Hence the geodesic is closed. $\square$

**Counterexample.** Take $\alpha = \sqrt{2}$ and $\beta = \sqrt{3}$.

- **Bounded:** Both $\sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{3}$ are irrational, so by Claim 1, the
 geodesic $\gamma$ with these endpoints is bounded.

- **Not closed:** $\sqrt{2}$ is a root of $x^2 - 2$ and $\sqrt{3}$ is a root of
$x^2 - 3$; they are **not** Galois conjugates. By Claim 2, $\gamma$ is not close
d.

*Direct verification that no $g \in PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})$ preserves $\{\sqrt{2}, \s
qrt{3}\}$:* If $g$ fixed $\sqrt{2}$, its fixed points would be $\pm\sqrt{2}$ (ro
ots of $cz^2+(d-a)z-b=0$ with $\sqrt{2}$ as a root forces the other root to be $
-\sqrt{2}$), so $g$ cannot also fix $\sqrt{3}$. If $g$ swapped them, then $g^2$
would fix both, but a hyperbolic Möbius transformation has exactly two fixed poi
nts, giving $\{\sqrt{2}, -\sqrt{2}\} = \{\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}\}$, a contradiction.

**Conclusion.** The geodesic on $M$ whose lift has endpoints $\sqrt{2}$ and $\sq
rt{3}$ is bounded but not closed. Therefore, **not every bounded geodesic on the
 modular surface $M$ is closed**.
