## Solution

**The statement is false.**

### Counterexample

Let $X = \mathbb{N}$ with the discrete topology.

**Step 1: $X$ is open in $\beta X$.** A Tychonoff space $X$ is open in its Stone
-Čech compactification $\beta X$ if and only if $X$ is locally compact. Since $\
mathbb{N}$ is discrete, every point has a compact neighborhood (namely, the sing
leton $\{n\}$), so $\mathbb{N}$ is locally compact. Hence $\mathbb{N}$ is open i
n $\beta\mathbb{N}$.

**Step 2: Each singleton $\{n\}$ is $\beta\mathbb{N}$-open.** Since $\mathbb{N}$
 is discrete, each $\{n\}$ is open in $\mathbb{N}$. Since $\mathbb{N}$ is open i
n $\beta\mathbb{N}$ and "open in open is open," each $\{n\}$ is open in $\beta\m
athbb{N}$.

**Step 3: Construct a cover of $X$ that does not cover $\beta X$.** Consider the
 family $\mathcal{U} = \bigl\{\{n\} : n \in \mathbb{N}\bigr\}$. Each member is a
 $\beta\mathbb{N}$-open set, and
$$\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}} \{n\} = \mathbb{N} = X,$$
so $\mathcal{U}$ covers $X$.

**Step 4: $\mathcal{U}$ does not cover $\beta\mathbb{N}$.** Since $\mathbb{N}$ i
s infinite and non-compact, the remainder $\beta\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{N}$ i
s nonempty (it consists of the free ultrafilters on $\mathbb{N}$, and is in fact
 uncountable). Therefore
$$\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\{n\} = \mathbb{N} \subsetneq \beta\mathbb{N},$$
so $\mathcal{U}$ does not cover $\beta\mathbb{N}$.

### Conclusion

We have exhibited a space $X = \mathbb{N}$ that is open in $\beta X$, together w
ith a cover of $X$ by $\beta X$-open sets (the singletons) that fails to cover $
\beta X$. Therefore the statement is **false**.
