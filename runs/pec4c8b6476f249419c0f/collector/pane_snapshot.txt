Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every finite group $G$ (wit
h no element of order $p^2$ for any prime $p$, and $|G| \neq p$ for any prime $p
$) must have an order-2 automorphism that moves some subgroup.

**Answer: No.** A counterexample is $G = \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$.

## Verification of the hypotheses

**Condition 1 (no element of order $p^2$):** The elements of $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathb
b{Z}$ have orders $1, 2, 3, 6$. The values $p^2$ for primes $p$ are $4, 9, 25, \
ldots$. None of $\{1, 2, 3, 6\}$ equals $p^2$ for any prime $p$. ✓

**Condition 2 ($|G| \neq p$ for each prime $p$):** $|G| = 6$, which is not prime
. ✓

## The automorphism group

Since $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ is cyclic, $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}/6\mathb
b{Z}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z})^\times = \{1, 5\}$, which has order $2$. Th
e two automorphisms are:

- $\sigma_1: g \mapsto g$ (the identity, order $1$),
- $\sigma_5: g \mapsto g^5 = g^{-1}$ (inversion, order $2$, since $5^2 = 25 \equ
iv 1 \pmod{6}$).

So the **only** automorphism of order $2$ is inversion $\sigma_5$.

## Inversion fixes every subgroup

The subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ are:
$$\{0\},\quad \langle 3 \rangle = \{0, 3\},\quad \langle 2 \rangle = \{0, 2, 4\}
,\quad \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}.$$

Applying $\sigma_5(x) = -x$:
- $\{0\} \mapsto \{0\}$,
- $\{0, 3\} \mapsto \{0, -3\} = \{0, 3\}$ (since $-3 \equiv 3 \pmod{6}$),
- $\{0, 2, 4\} \mapsto \{0, -2, -4\} = \{0, 4, 2\} = \{0, 2, 4\}$,
- $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \mapsto \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$.

Every subgroup is fixed.

## Why this is forced (general principle)

More generally, every automorphism of a cyclic group $G = \langle g \rangle$ is
of the form $\sigma_k: g \mapsto g^k$ with $\gcd(k, |G|) = 1$—a **power automorp
hism**. For any subgroup $\langle g^d \rangle$ (of order $|G|/d$), we have $\sig
ma_k(\langle g^d \rangle) = \langle g^{kd} \rangle$. Since $\gcd(k, |G|) = 1$ im
plies $\gcd(kd, |G|) = \gcd(d, |G|) = d$, the subgroups $\langle g^{kd} \rangle$
 and $\langle g^d \rangle$ have the same order. A cyclic group has a **unique**
subgroup of each order, so $\langle g^{kd} \rangle = \langle g^d \rangle$. Thus
**every** automorphism of a cyclic group fixes every subgroup.

Since $6 = 2 \cdot 3$ is squarefree, $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ satisfies conditio
n 1, and since $6$ is composite, it satisfies condition 2. Yet its only order-2
automorphism (inversion) fixes all subgroups.

## Conclusion

The answer is **no**. The group $G = \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ satisfies both hypo
theses (no element of order $p^2$ for any prime $p$, and $|G|$ is not prime), bu
t its unique automorphism of order $2$ is inversion, which fixes every subgroup.
 Therefore there does **not** always exist such an automorphism.
