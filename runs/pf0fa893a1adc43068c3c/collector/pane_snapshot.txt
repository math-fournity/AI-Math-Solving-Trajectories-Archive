## Answer

**Yes, there exists an unbounded linear operator of finite rank on a Banach spac
e.**

### Construction

Let $X$ be any infinite-dimensional Banach space (for concreteness, take $X = \e
ll^1$, the space of absolutely summable sequences). Let $Y = \mathbb{R}$ (a one-
dimensional, hence Banach, space).

We construct a linear functional $T: X \to \mathbb{R}$ (so $\operatorname{rank}(
T) \leq 1$, finite) that is unbounded.

**Step 1: A Hamel basis exists.** By the Axiom of Choice (Zorn's Lemma), every v
ector space has a Hamel basis. Let $\{e_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in I}$ be a Hamel basi
s of $X$. By normalizing, we may assume $\|e_\alpha\| = 1$ for every $\alpha \in
 I$.

**Step 2: $I$ is infinite (in fact uncountable).** Since $X$ is an infinite-dime
nsional Banach space, its Hamel basis must be uncountable (a countable-dimension
al normed space cannot be complete — this is the Baire category theorem applied
to the finite-dimensional subspaces, which are closed with empty interior). In p
articular, $I$ contains a countably infinite subset. Pick distinct indices $\{\a
lpha_n\}_{n=1}^\infty \subseteq I$.

**Step 3: Define $T$.** Define $T$ on the Hamel basis by
$$
T(e_{\alpha_n}) = n \quad (n = 1, 2, 3, \dots), \qquad T(e_\alpha) = 0 \;\text{
for } \alpha \notin \{\alpha_n\}.
$$
Extend $T$ linearly to all of $X$: every $x \in X$ has a unique representation $
x = \sum_{j=1}^{m} c_j e_{\beta_j}$ (finite sum, by definition of Hamel basis),
and we set $T(x) = \sum_{j=1}^{m} c_j T(e_{\beta_j})$. This gives a well-defined
 linear map $T: X \to \mathbb{R}$.

**Step 4: $T$ has finite rank.** The range $T(X) \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, so $\dim
 T(X) \leq 1 < \infty$.

**Step 5: $T$ is unbounded.** Consider the sequence $x_n = e_{\alpha_n} \in X$.
Then:
- $\|x_n\| = \|e_{\alpha_n}\| = 1$ for all $n$.
- $T(x_n) = T(e_{\alpha_n}) = n$, so $|T(x_n)| = n \to \infty$.

Therefore
$$
\frac{|T(x_n)|}{\|x_n\|} = n \xrightarrow[n \to \infty]{} \infty,
$$
which means $\|T\| = \sup_{\|x\|=1} |T(x)| = \infty$. Hence $T$ is **unbounded**
.

### Why this does not contradict any theorem

The relevant theorem is: *a linear functional $f$ on a normed space is bounded (
continuous) if and only if $\ker f$ is closed.* In our construction, $\ker T$ is
 a hyperplane that is **not closed** (it is dense in $X$), which is exactly why
$T$ can be unbounded. The closedness of the kernel fails because $X$ is infinite
-dimensional and we used a Hamel basis (an algebraic, non-topological object) to
 define $T$.

### Summary

| Property | Holds? |
|---|---|
| $X, Y$ Banach | Yes ($X = \ell^1$, $Y = \mathbb{R}$) |
| $T$ linear | Yes (defined via Hamel basis, extended linearly) |
| $\operatorname{rank}(T) < \infty$ | Yes ($\operatorname{rank}(T) \leq 1$) |
| $T$ bounded | **No** ($\|T\| = \infty$, witnessed by $x_n = e_{\alpha_n}$) |

Thus we have exhibited an unbounded linear operator of finite rank between Banac
h spaces. The essential ingredients are: (1) an infinite-dimensional domain (so
the Hamel basis is large enough to support a non-closed hyperplane), and (2) the
 Axiom of Choice (to obtain the Hamel basis and define the functional algebraica
lly, free of any continuity constraint).
