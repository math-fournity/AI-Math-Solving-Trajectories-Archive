## Answer

**Yes.** Every smooth automorphism of a Lie group $G$ is a diffeomorphism.

## Proof

Let $\varphi: G \to G$ be a smooth automorphism. By definition $\varphi$ is a gr
oup automorphism (hence bijective) and a smooth map. We must show that $\varphi^
{-1}$ is also smooth.

**Step 1: The differential at the identity is injective.**

Let $\mathfrak{g} = T_e G$ be the Lie algebra of $G$. Since $\varphi$ is a smoot
h homomorphism, the differential
$$d\varphi_e : \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$$
is a Lie algebra homomorphism. We claim $\ker(d\varphi_e) = \{0\}$.

Suppose $X \in \mathfrak{g}$ with $d\varphi_e(X) = 0$. Consider the one-paramete
r subgroup $\gamma(t) = \exp(tX)$. Since $\varphi$ is a smooth homomorphism, $\v
arphi(\exp(tX)) = \exp(t\, d\varphi_e(X)) = \exp(0) = e$ for all $t \in \mathbb{
R}$. Thus $\varphi$ maps the entire one-parameter subgroup $\{\exp(tX) : t \in \
mathbb{R}\}$ to the identity. But $\varphi$ is injective (being a group automorp
hism), so $\exp(tX) = e$ for all $t$. Differentiating at $t = 0$ gives $X = 0$.

Hence $d\varphi_e$ is injective.

**Step 2: The differential at the identity is an isomorphism.**

Since $G$ is a finite-dimensional Lie group, $\mathfrak{g}$ is a finite-dimensio
nal vector space. An injective linear endomorphism of a finite-dimensional vecto
r space is an isomorphism. Thus
$$d\varphi_e : \mathfrak{g} \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \mathfrak{g}$$
is a linear isomorphism.

**Step 3: $\varphi$ is a local diffeomorphism at every point.**

By the Inverse Function Theorem, since $d\varphi_e$ is an isomorphism, $\varphi$
 is a local diffeomorphism at $e$; in particular there is an open neighborhood $
U$ of $e$ on which $\varphi$ restricts to a diffeomorphism onto its image.

Now for an arbitrary $g \in G$, write $\varphi(g) = h$. Since $\varphi$ is a hom
omorphism, we have the factorization
$$\varphi = L_h \circ \varphi \circ L_{g^{-1}} \quad \text{restricted to a neigh
borhood of } g,$$
more precisely, for $x$ near $g$, writing $x = g \cdot y$ with $y$ near $e$:
$$\varphi(x) = \varphi(g)\,\varphi(y) = h \cdot \varphi(y) = L_h(\varphi(L_{g^{-
1}}(x))).$$
Since $L_h$ and $L_{g^{-1}}$ are diffeomorphisms (left translations) and $\varph
i$ is a local diffeomorphism near $e$, the composition is a local diffeomorphism
 near $g$. Therefore $d\varphi_g$ is an isomorphism for every $g \in G$.

**Step 4: $\varphi$ is a global diffeomorphism.**

$\varphi$ is a bijective local diffeomorphism. A bijective local diffeomorphism
is a global diffeomorphism: the inverse $\varphi^{-1}$ is smooth because locally
 $\varphi^{-1}$ coincides with the smooth local inverse guaranteed by the Invers
e Function Theorem at each point.

Therefore $\varphi$ is a diffeomorphism. $\blacksquare$
