**Setting up the tangency conditions.** A line $y = mx + b$ is tangent to $y = x
^2 + a$ when $x^2 - mx + (a-b) = 0$ has a double root, giving $b = a - m^2/4$.

For tangency to $x = y^2 + a$: substituting $x = (y-b)/m$ gives $my^2 - y + (b +
 ma) = 0$, requiring discriminant $1 - 4m(b+ma) = 0$, so $b = \frac{1}{4m} - ma$
.

**Finding the slopes.** Equating the two expressions for $b$:
$$a - \frac{m^2}{4} = \frac{1}{4m} - ma \implies m^3 - 4am^2 - 4am + 1 = 0$$

**Exploiting symmetry.** The parabolas are reflections across $y = x$. If a line

ing $m = -1$: $(-1)^3 - 4a(1) - 4a(-1) + 1 = -1 - 4a + 4a + 1 = 0$. So $m = -1$
is always a root.

Factoring: $(m+1)(m^2 - (4a+1)m + 1) = 0$. The quadratic has roots $m_+, m_-$ wi
th $m_+ m_- = 1$ and $m_+ + m_- = 4a+1$. Three real tangent lines exist when $(4
a+1)^2 \geq 4$.

**Equilateral triangle condition.** The three lines have slopes $-1, m_+, m_-$.
The acute angle between lines with slopes $m_1, m_2$ satisfies $\tan\theta = \le
ft|\frac{m_1 - m_2}{1 + m_1 m_2}\right|$.

For all three pairwise angles to be $60°$ ($\tan\theta = \sqrt{3}$):

_-|}{2} = \sqrt{3}$, so $(m_+ - m_-)^2 = 12$, giving $(4a+1)^2 - 4 = 12$, thus $
(4a+1)^2 = 16$ and $a = \frac{3}{4}$ or $a = -\frac{5}{4}$.

- **Between $-1$ and $m_+$:** $\left|\frac{1+m_+}{1-m_+}\right| = \sqrt{3}$, giv

For $a = \frac{3}{4}$: $m_+ + m_- = 4$, so $m_\pm = 2 \pm \sqrt{3}$. ✓ This sati
