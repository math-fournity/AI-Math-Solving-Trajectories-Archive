The problem asks for an explicit construction of a Steiner system $S(t, k, n)$ w
ith $n > k > t > 5$, $t < 10$, and $n < 200$. This is the **large Steiner system
s** open problem from the Formal Conjectures / FrontierMath benchmark.

**Why this is unsolvable:**

1. **No explicit Steiner system with $t > 5$ is known to exist.** The problem st
atement itself acknowledges this: *"No example of a Steiner system with $t > 5$
is known, despite a 2014 existence theorem by Keevash showing that such systems
must exist for sufficiently large $n$."*

2. **Keevash's theorem (2014)** is a pure existence result. It proves that for f
ixed $t, k$ and sufficiently large $n$ satisfying the necessary divisibility con
ditions, an $S(t,k,n)$ exists. However:
   - The bounds in Keevash's proof are astronomically large (tower-type bounds),
 far exceeding $n < 200$.
   - The proof is non-constructive; it does not yield an explicit block set.
   - Even for the smallest possible parameters with $t = 6$ (namely $S(6, 7, n)$
), the required $n$ from Keevash's bounds is vastly larger than 200.

3. **The only known Steiner systems with $t = 5$** are the two Witt designs: $S(
5, 6, 12)$ (small Witt design, given in the problem as solved) and $S(5, 8, 24)$
 (large Witt design). For $t \geq 6$, **zero** explicit examples are known in th

estrict to a regime where no construction method (Keevash's theorem, recursive c
onstructions, algebraic constructions) is known to produce a valid system.
