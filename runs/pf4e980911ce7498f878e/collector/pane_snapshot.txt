## Solution

**Answer: Yes.** Every open neighborhood of $X \times B_0$ inside $X \times B$ e
quals $X \times B$.

### Proof

**Key observation.** Since $I^2 = 0$, every section $f$ of $I$ satisfies $f^2 =
0$, so $f$ is nilpotent. Hence $I \subseteq \mathcal{N}_B$, the nilradical of $B
$. A closed immersion defined by a nilpotent ideal is a **homeomorphism on under
lying topological spaces** (it is a universal homeomorphism). Concretely, the ma
p on topological spaces $|B_0| \to |B|$ is bijective and the topology coincides,
 because the radical of $I$ equals the radical of $(0)$ in $B$, so the two schem
es have identical prime spectra.

**Base change preserves this.** The closed immersion $X \times B_0 \hookrightarr
ow X \times B$ is obtained by base change of $B_0 \hookrightarrow B$ along $X \t
o B$ (more precisely, along $X \times B \to B$). The ideal sheaf of $X \times B_
0$ in $X \times B$ is $I \cdot \mathcal{O}_{X \times B}$, and since $I^2 = 0$, w
e have $(I \cdot \mathcal{O}_{X \times B})^2 = 0$. Thus this ideal is again nilp
otent, and the closed immersion

$$X \times B_0 \hookrightarrow X \times B$$

is again a homeomorphism on underlying topological spaces. Equivalently, $|X \ti
mes B_0| = |X \times B|$ as sets with the same topology.

**Conclusion.** Since $X \times B_0$ and $X \times B$ have the **same underlying
 topological space**, the subset $X \times B_0 \subseteq X \times B$ is in fact
the entire space. Therefore the only open subset of $X \times B$ containing $X \
times B_0$ is $X \times B$ itself. Every open neighborhood of $X \times B_0$ in
$X \times B$ equals $X \times B$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The projectivity (or even any geometric property) of $X$ is not need
ed; the argument is purely topological and relies only on the nilpotence of $I$.
 It holds for any $\mathbb{C}$-scheme $X$.
