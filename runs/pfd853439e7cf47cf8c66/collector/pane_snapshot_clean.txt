### Preliminary observation

With $z = e^{i\theta}$ and $\theta=\pi/3$, we have $|z|=1$, so the terms $n^2 e^
{in\theta}$ do **not** tend to $0$ in magnitude. The series therefore diverges i
n the ordinary sense. The natural and standard interpretation of "evaluate $\sum
_{n\ge 1} n^2 e^{in\theta}$" for $\theta\not\equiv 0\pmod{2\pi}$ is its **Abel s
um**:

$$S \;=\; \lim_{r\to 1^-}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^2\, r^n e^{in\theta} \;=\; \lim_{
r\to 1^-} F(r e^{i\theta}),\qquad F(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^2 z^n.$$

### Closed form of $F(z)$

For $|z|<1$,

$$\sum_{n\ge 0}z^n=\frac{1}{1-z},\qquad \sum_{n\ge 1}n z^n = z\frac{d}{dz}\frac{
1}{1-z}=\frac{z}{(1-z)^2},$$

$$\sum_{n\ge 1}n^2 z^n \;=\; z\frac{d}{dz}\frac{z}{(1-z)^2}
 \;=\; z\,\frac{(1-z)^2+2z(1-z)}{(1-z)^4}
 \;=\; \frac{z(1+z)}{(1-z)^3}.$$

Since $\theta=\pi/3\not\equiv 0\pmod{2\pi}$, the point $e^{i\theta}$ is **not**
a pole of $F$ (the only pole is $z=1$). Hence $F$ extends continuously to $z=e^{
i\theta}$, and Abel's theorem gives

$$S = F(e^{i\pi/3}) = \frac{e^{i\pi/3}\bigl(1+e^{i\pi/3}\bigr)}{\bigl(1-e^{i\pi/
3}\bigr)^3}.$$

### Evaluation at $\theta=\pi/3$

Let $\omega = e^{i\pi/3} = \tfrac12 + \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i$.

**Denominator.** First,
$$1-\omega = \tfrac12 - \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i,\qquad |1-\omega|^2 = \tfrac14+\tfrac
34 = 1.$$
Then
$$(1-\omega)^2 = \Bigl(\tfrac12-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i\Bigr)^2 = \tfrac14-\tfrac{\sq
rt3}{2}i-\tfrac34 = -\tfrac12-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i,$$
and
$$(1-\omega)^3 = (1-\omega)^2(1-\omega)=\Bigl(-\tfrac12-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i\Bigr)
\Bigl(\tfrac12-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i\Bigr).$$
Expanding:
$$=-\tfrac14+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{4}i-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{4}i+\tfrac34 i^2 = -\tfrac14-\tfr
ac34 = -1.$$

**Numerator.**
$$1+\omega = \tfrac32+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i,$$
$$\omega(1+\omega)=\Bigl(\tfrac12+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i\Bigr)\Bigl(\tfrac32+\tfrac{
\sqrt3}{2}i\Bigr)=\tfrac34+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{4}i+\tfrac{3\sqrt3}{4}i+\tfrac34 i^2.$
$
$$=\Bigl(\tfrac34-\tfrac34\Bigr)+\tfrac{\sqrt3+3\sqrt3}{4}i = \sqrt3\,i.$$

### Final value

$$S = \frac{\sqrt3\,i}{-1} = -\sqrt3\,i.$$
