# Proof

**Answer: No.** The functor $X \times -$ preserves small colimits in $\mathbf{Lo
c}$ if and only if $X$ is exponentiable (equivalently, locally compact). Since n
on-locally-compact locales exist, $X \times -$ does not preserve small colimits
for all locales $X$.

---

## Step 1: Preliminaries

Recall that $\mathbf{Loc} = \mathbf{Frm}^{\mathrm{op}}$, where $\mathbf{Frm}$ is
 the category of frames (complete Heyting algebras) and frame homomorphisms (pre
serving finite meets and arbitrary joins). The product of locales $X \times Y$ c
orresponds to the coproduct of frames $O(X) \otimes O(Y)$ (the tensor product).
The category $\mathbf{Loc}$ is complete and cocomplete (since $\mathbf{Frm}$ is
complete and cocomplete), and locally small (since $\mathbf{Loc}(X,Y) = \mathbf{
Frm}(O(Y), O(X))$ is a set).

## Step 2: If $X$ is exponentiable, then $X \times -$ preserves small colimits

If $X$ is exponentiable, the exponential $(-)^X$ exists, giving an adjunction
$$X \times - \dashv (-)^X : \mathbf{Loc} \to \mathbf{Loc}.$$
As a left adjoint, $X \times -$ preserves all small colimits.

## Step 3: If $X \times -$ preserves small colimits, then $X$ is exponentiable

We apply the **General Adjoint Functor Theorem (GAFT)**: a functor $F: C \to D$
from a cocomplete, locally small category has a right adjoint provided $F$ prese
rves colimits and satisfies the solution set condition.

**$C = \mathbf{Loc}$ is cocomplete and locally small** (Step 1). ✓

**$X \times -$ preserves colimits** (by assumption). ✓

**Solution set condition.** Fix $Y \in \mathbf{Loc}$. We need a set of pairs $\{
(Z_i, f_i: Y \to X \times Z_i)\}_{i \in I}$ such that every map $f: Y \to X \tim
es Z$ factors as $f = (X \times g) \circ f_i$ for some $i$ and $g: Z_i \to Z$.

A map $f: Y \to X \times Z$ is a pair $(a, b)$ with $a: Y \to X$ and $b: Y \to Z
$. For each $a \in \mathbf{Loc}(Y, X)$ (a set, by local smallness), take the sol
ution object $(Y, (a, \mathrm{id}_Y): Y \to X \times Y)$. Then any $(a, b): Y \t
o X \times Z$ factors as:
$$(a, b) = (X \times b) \circ (a, \mathrm{id}_Y),$$
i.e., via $g = b: Y \to Z$ and $f_i = (a, \mathrm{id}_Y)$. The collection $\{(Y,
 (a, \mathrm{id}_Y)) : a \in \mathbf{Loc}(Y,X)\}$ is a set. ✓

By GAFT, $X \times -$ has a right adjoint, so $X$ is exponentiable.

## Step 4: Exponentiable = locally compact

By the **Hofmann–Lawson theorem** (and its locale-theoretic formulation due to H
yland, Johnstone, and Pitts), a locale $X$ is exponentiable in $\mathbf{Loc}$ if
 and only if $X$ is **locally compact**: every element $u \in O(X)$ satisfies $u
 = \bigvee \{v \in O(X) : v \ll u\}$, where $\ll$ denotes the way-below relation
 on the frame $O(X)$.

## Step 5: Non-locally-compact locales exist

Consider the locale $\mathrm{pt}(O(\mathbb{Q}))$ associated to the topological s
pace $\mathbb{Q}$ of rational numbers with the usual topology. The space $\mathb
b{Q}$ is sober (being $T_1$ and Hausdorff) but **not locally compact**: no point
 of $\mathbb{Q}$ has a compact neighborhood, since every neighborhood in $\mathb
b{Q}$ contains a closed bounded subset that is not compact (closed bounded subse
ts of $\mathbb{Q}$ need not be compact, as $\mathbb{Q}$ is not complete).

For sober spaces, local compactness of the space is equivalent to local compactn
ess of the associated locale. Therefore $O(\mathbb{Q})$ is a frame that is **not
** locally compact, and the corresponding locale is **not exponentiable**.

## Step 6: Conclusion

Combining Steps 2–3 and 4:

$$X \times - \text{ preserves small colimits} \iff X \text{ is exponentiable} \i
ff X \text{ is locally compact.}$$

By Step 5, there exist locales that are not locally compact (e.g., the locale of
 $\mathbb{Q}$). For such a locale $X$, the functor $X \times -$ does **not** pre
serve small colimits.

Concretely, for a non-exponentiable $X$, the functor $X \times -$ fails to prese
rve some coequalizer (quotient). This is the locale-theoretic analogue of the cl
assical topological fact (Day's theorem) that for a non-core-compact space $X$,
the product functor $X \times -$ does not preserve quotient maps.

**Therefore, the functor $X \times -$ does not preserve small colimits for all l
ocales $X$.** It does so precisely for the locally compact (exponentiable) local
es.
