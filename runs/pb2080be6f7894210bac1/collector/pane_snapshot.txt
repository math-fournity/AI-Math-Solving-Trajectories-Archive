## Problem

We are given the bivariate generating function for binary trees (z marks interna
l nodes, u marks leaves):

$$C(z,u) = \frac{1 - \sqrt{1 - 4z + 4z^2(1-u)}}{2z}$$

We determine the asymptotic distribution of the number of leaves $X_n$ in a unif
ormly random binary tree with $n$ internal nodes.

---

## Step 1: Locate the Dominant Singularity $\rho(u)$

The square-root singularity occurs where the discriminant vanishes:

$$D(z,u) := 1 - 4z + 4z^2(1-u) = (1-2z)^2 - 4z^2 u = (1 - 2z - 2z\sqrt{u})(1 - 2
z + 2z\sqrt{u}).$$

The factor $1 - 2z - 2z\sqrt{u} = 0$ gives the dominant (closest) singularity:

$$\rho(u) = \frac{1}{2(1+\sqrt{u})}.$$

At $u=1$: $\rho(1) = 1/4$, recovering the Catalan singularity. The other factor
vanishes at $z = 1/(2(1-\sqrt{u}))$, which lies strictly farther from the origin
 for $u$ near $1$, so it is subdominant.

---

## Step 2: Local Expansion Near the Singularity

Near $z = \rho(u)$, the second factor evaluates to:

$$1 - 2\rho(u) + 2\rho(u)\sqrt{u} = \frac{2\sqrt{u}}{1+\sqrt{u}},$$

while the first factor is $-2(1+\sqrt{u})(z - \rho(u))$. Therefore:

$$D(z,u) \approx -4\sqrt{u}\,(z - \rho(u)) \cdot \frac{2\sqrt{u}}{1+\sqrt{u}} \c
dot \frac{1}{2}$$

More directly: $D(z,u) \approx 4\sqrt{u}\,\rho(u)\,(1 - z/\rho(u))$, so

$$\sqrt{D(z,u)} \approx 2\,u^{1/4}\,\sqrt{\rho(u)}\,\sqrt{1 - z/\rho(u)}.$$

Since $C(z,u) = \frac{1}{2z} - \frac{\sqrt{D}}{2z}$, and $1/(2z)$ is analytic at
 $\rho(u)$, the singular part is:

$$C(z,u) \sim \underbrace{\frac{1}{2\rho(u)}}_{\text{regular}} - \underbrace{\fr
ac{u^{1/4}}{\sqrt{\rho(u)}}}_{\lambda(u)}\,\sqrt{1 - z/\rho(u)} + \text{higher o
rder}.$$

Thus the singular expansion has the standard square-root form with

$$\lambda(u) = \frac{u^{1/4}}{\sqrt{\rho(u)}} = u^{1/4}\sqrt{2(1+\sqrt{u})}.$$

---

## Step 3: Coefficient Asymptotics

Using the transfer theorem $[z^n](1-z/\rho)^{1/2} \sim -\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,\
rho^{-n}\,n^{-3/2}$:

$$[z^n]\,C(z,u) \sim \frac{\lambda(u)}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,\rho(u)^{-n}\,n^{-3/2}.$$

**Check at $u=1$:** $\lambda(1) = 2$, $\rho(1) = 1/4$, giving $[z^n]C(z,1) \sim
\frac{4^n}{\sqrt{\pi}\,n^{3/2}}$, the Catalan asymptotic. ✓

The probability generating function of $X_n$ is therefore:

$$\mathbb{E}[u^{X_n}] = \frac{[z^n]C(z,u)}{[z^n]C(z,1)} \sim \frac{\lambda(u)}{2
}\left(\frac{1}{4\,\rho(u)}\right)^n = \chi(u)\cdot f(u)^n,$$

where

$$f(u) = \frac{1+\sqrt{u}}{2}, \qquad \chi(u) = \frac{\lambda(u)}{2} = u^{1/4}\s
qrt{\frac{1+\sqrt{u}}{2}} = u^{1/4}\,f(u)^{1/2}.$$

---

## Step 4: Apply the Quasi-Powers Theorem

Set $\psi(u) = \log f(u) = \log(1+\sqrt{u}) - \log 2$. The PGF has the form $\ch
i(u)\,e^{n\psi(u)}$, so by the Quasi-Powers Theorem (Flajolet–Sedgewick, Theorem
 IX.8), $X_n$ is asymptotically Gaussian with:

**Mean:** $\mathbb{E}[X_n] \sim n\,\psi'(1)$.

$$\psi'(u) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{u}\,(1+\sqrt{u})}, \qquad \psi'(1) = \frac{1}{4}.$$

**Variance:** $\mathrm{Var}(X_n) \sim n\bigl(\psi''(1) + \psi'(1)\bigr)$.

$$\psi''(u) = -\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\frac{1}{2\sqrt{u}}+1}{(\sqrt{u}+u)^2}, \qq
uad \psi''(1) = -\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{3/2}{4} = -\frac{3}{16}.$$

$$\psi''(1) + \psi'(1) = -\frac{3}{16} + \frac{4}{16} = \frac{1}{16}.$$

The prefactor $\chi(u)$ contributes only $O(1)$ corrections to the mean and vari
ance, which vanish after normalization by $\sqrt{n}$.

---

## Step 5: Conclusion

By the Quasi-Powers Theorem, the normalized variable converges in distribution t
o a standard Gaussian:

$$\frac{X_n - \frac{n}{4}}{\sqrt{n/16}} \;\xrightarrow{d}\; \mathcal{N}(0,1).$$

Equivalently, the number of leaves in a uniformly random binary tree with $n$ in
ternal nodes is **asymptotically Gaussian (normal)** with

$$\mathbb{E}[X_n] \sim \frac{n}{4}, \qquad \mathrm{Var}(X_n) \sim \frac{n}{16}.$
$

The mechanism is the **perturbation of the square-root singularity**: as $u$ var
ies near $1$, the singularity $\rho(u) = \frac{1}{2(1+\sqrt{u})}$ moves smoothly
, and the singular exponent $1/2$ remains fixed. This "movable singularity with
fixed exponent" is precisely the condition under which the Quasi-Powers framewor
k yields a Gaussian limit law.
