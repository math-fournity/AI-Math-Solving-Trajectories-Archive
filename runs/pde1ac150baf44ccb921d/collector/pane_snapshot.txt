## Solution

**Setup.** In a completely integrable Hamiltonian system with $n$ degrees of fre
edom, the Liouville–Arnold theorem applies: assuming $M_f = \{F_i = f_i,\ i=1,\d
ots,n\}$ is compact and connected, it is diffeomorphic to an $n$-torus $\mathbb{
T}^n$, and there exist action-angle coordinates $(I, \varphi)$ in a neighborhood
 of $M_f$ such that the Hamiltonian depends only on the actions, $H = H(I)$, and
 the equations of motion on the torus $I = I^*$ are

$$\dot{\varphi} = \omega(I^*), \qquad \omega(I^*) = \frac{\partial H}{\partial I
}\bigg|_{I=I^*} \in \mathbb{R}^n.$$

The solution is $\varphi(t) = \varphi(0) + \omega(I^*)\,t \pmod{2\pi}$.

**Resonance of multiplicity $m$.** The frequency vector $\omega(I^*)$ is *resona
nt of multiplicity $m$* means that the **resonance module**

$$\Lambda = \{\, k \in \mathbb{Z}^n : \langle k,\, \omega(I^*)\rangle = 0 \,\}$$

has rank $m$ over $\mathbb{Z}$, i.e., there exist $m$ linearly independent integ
er vectors $k^{(1)}, \dots, k^{(m)} \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ with $\langle k^{(j)}, \om
ega\rangle = 0$. Equivalently, the $\mathbb{Q}$-rank of $\omega(I^*)$ — the numb
er of rationally independent components — is

$$\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Q}} \omega(I^*) = n - m.$$

**Key claim.** *Every trajectory on the torus $I = I^*$ is confined to (and dens
e on) an $(n-m)$-dimensional invariant subtorus of $\mathbb{T}^n$. The $n$-torus
 is foliated by such $(n-m)$-dimensional subtori.*

**Proof of the claim.** Consider the orbit $\mathcal{O}_{\varphi_0} = \{\varphi_
0 + \omega\, t \bmod 2\pi : t \in \mathbb{R}\}$.

*Step 1: The orbit lies in an $(n-m)$-dimensional subtorus.* Define the map

$$\pi: \mathbb{T}^n \to \mathbb{T}^m, \qquad \varphi \mapsto (\langle k^{(1)}, \
varphi\rangle, \dots, \langle k^{(m)}, \varphi\rangle) \bmod 2\pi.$$

Along any orbit, each component is constant:

$$\langle k^{(j)},\, \varphi(t)\rangle = \langle k^{(j)},\, \varphi_0\rangle + \
underbrace{\langle k^{(j)},\, \omega\rangle}_{=0}\, t = \langle k^{(j)},\, \varp
hi_0\rangle.$$

Hence the entire orbit is contained in the fiber $\pi^{-1}(c)$ where $c = \pi(\v
arphi_0)$. Each such fiber is a translate of the kernel of $\pi$, which is an $(
n-m)$-dimensional subtorus of $\mathbb{T}^n$ (a coset of the closed subgroup $\k
er\pi \cong \mathbb{T}^{n-m}$).

*Step 2: The orbit is dense in its fiber.* The remaining $n - m$ frequencies are
 rationally independent (by definition of the resonance multiplicity: the rank o
f the resonance module is exactly $m$, so no further independent integer relatio
n exists among the "effective" frequencies). By the classical Kronecker theorem,
 the linear flow with $n - m$ rationally independent frequencies is dense in $\m
athbb{T}^{n-m}$. Therefore each orbit is dense in its $(n-m)$-dimensional invari
ant subtorus.

*Step 3: Foliation.* As $\varphi_0$ ranges over $\mathbb{T}^n$, the constants $c
 = \pi(\varphi_0)$ range over $\mathbb{T}^m$, and the fibers $\pi^{-1}(c)$ give
a smooth foliation of $\mathbb{T}^n$ by $(n-m)$-dimensional subtori, each invari
ant under the flow and carrying a dense quasi-periodic motion with $n - m$ ratio
nally independent frequencies.

**Conclusion.** On a resonant torus of multiplicity $m$:

1. **No trajectory is dense in the full $n$-torus** (unlike the non-resonant cas
e $m = 0$). Each trajectory is confined to an $(n-m)$-dimensional invariant subt
orus.
2. **The $n$-torus is foliated** by a smooth $(m)$-parameter family of $(n-m)$-d
imensional invariant subtori.
3. **On each subtorus the motion is quasi-periodic** with $n - m$ rationally ind
ependent frequencies, and every orbit is dense in its subtorus.

**Special cases:**
- $m = 0$ (non-resonant): each orbit is dense in the full $\mathbb{T}^n$ — the g
eneric, ergodic-like case.
- $m = n - 1$ (maximal non-trivial resonance): only one rationally independent f
requency survives; every orbit is **periodic** (a closed curve, i.e., a 1-dimens
ional subtorus = circle).
- $m = n$: all frequencies vanish, $\omega = 0$; every point of the torus is a *
*fixed point** (equilibrium).
