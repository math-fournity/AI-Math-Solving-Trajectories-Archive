Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $|f(t)| = |g(t)|$ for all $
t$ forces the exponent sets $A$ and $B$ to be equal.

**Answer: No.** Here is a counterexample.

Let
$$f(t) = 1 + e^{2it}, \qquad g(t) = e^{it} + e^{-it}.$$

The exponent sets are $A = \{0,\, 2i\}$ and $B = \{i,\, -i\}$, which are distinc
t sets with distinct elements, and $A \neq B$.

**Verification that $|f(t)| = |g(t)|$ for all real $t$:**

Factor out $e^{it}$ from $f$:
$$f(t) = 1 + e^{2it} = e^{it}\!\left(e^{-it} + e^{it}\right) = e^{it}\cdot 2\cos
 t.$$

Therefore
$$|f(t)| = \left|e^{it}\right|\cdot|2\cos t| = 1\cdot|2\cos t| = |2\cos t|.$$

On the other hand,
$$g(t) = e^{it} + e^{-it} = 2\cos t,$$
so
$$|g(t)| = |2\cos t|.$$

Hence $|f(t)| = |g(t)|$ for every real $t$, yet $A = \{0, 2i\} \neq \{i, -i\} =
B$.

**Why this happens (structural reason):**

The condition $|f(t)| = |g(t)|$ is equivalent to $|f(t)|^2 = |g(t)|^2$, i.e.,
$$f(t)\overline{f(t)} = g(t)\overline{g(t)}.$$

Expanding:
$$|f(t)|^2 = \sum_{a,a'\in A} c_a\,\overline{c_{a'}}\, e^{(a+\overline{a'})t}, \
qquad |g(t)|^2 = \sum_{b,b'\in B} d_b\,\overline{d_{b'}}\, e^{(b+\overline{b'})t
}.$$

By the linear independence of $e^{\lambda t}$ for distinct $\lambda$ (over $\mat
hbb{R}$), equality for all $t$ forces the **multisets of exponents** $\{a+\overl
ine{a'}\}$ and $\{b+\overline{b'}\}$ (with their coefficients) to coincide. Howe
ver, the map
$$(a, a') \longmapsto a + \overline{a'}$$
from pairs of exponents to the exponents of $|f|^2$ is **not injective** on the
level of exponent sets. Different sets $A$ can yield the same set $\{a + \overli
ne{a'} : a, a' \in A\}$.

In the counterexample:
$$\{a+\overline{a'} : a,a'\in A\} = \{0,\, -2i,\, 2i,\, 0\} = \{0,\, -2i,\, 2i,\
, 0\} = \{b+\overline{b'} : b,b'\in B\},$$
and the coefficients also match (both $|f|^2$ and $|g|^2$ equal $2 + e^{2it} + e
^{-2it}$), yet $A \neq B$.

**Conclusion:** It does **not** necessarily follow that $A = B$. The equality $|
f(t)| = |g(t)|$ for all $t$ only constrains the "pairwise-sum" sets $\{a + \over
line{a'}\}$ and $\{b + \overline{b'}\}$ to coincide, and this constraint is stri
ctly weaker than $A = B$.
