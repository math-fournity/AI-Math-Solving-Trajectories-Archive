## Proof

**Goal.** Determine the decay rate function $D(n)$ such that $\frac{\Gamma(n)}{\
Gamma(n/a)^a} \sim D(n)$ as $n \to \infty$, where $a > 0$ is a constant.

**Tool.** Stirling's approximation: for $x \to \infty$,
$$\Gamma(x) = \sqrt{2\pi}\, x^{x - 1/2}\, e^{-x}\left(1 + O(1/x)\right).$$

**Step 1: Expand the numerator.**
$$\Gamma(n) = \sqrt{2\pi}\, n^{n - 1/2}\, e^{-n}\left(1 + O(1/n)\right).$$

**Step 2: Expand $\Gamma(n/a)$ and raise to the $a$-th power.**
$$\Gamma(n/a) = \sqrt{2\pi}\, (n/a)^{n/a - 1/2}\, e^{-n/a}\left(1 + O(1/n)\right
).$$

Raising to the $a$-th power:
$$\Gamma(n/a)^a = (2\pi)^{a/2}\, (n/a)^{a(n/a - 1/2)}\, e^{-n}\left(1 + O(1/n)\r
ight).$$

Simplifying the exponent of $(n/a)$:
$$a\!\left(\frac{n}{a} - \frac{1}{2}\right) = n - \frac{a}{2},$$

so
$$\Gamma(n/a)^a = (2\pi)^{a/2}\, (n/a)^{n - a/2}\, e^{-n}\left(1 + O(1/n)\right)
.$$

Expanding $(n/a)^{n-a/2} = n^{n-a/2}\, a^{-(n-a/2)} = n^{n-a/2}\, a^{a/2 - n}$:

$$\Gamma(n/a)^a = (2\pi)^{a/2}\, a^{a/2 - n}\, n^{n - a/2}\, e^{-n}\left(1 + O(1
/n)\right).$$

**Step 3: Form the ratio.**
$$\frac{\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(n/a)^a} = \frac{\sqrt{2\pi}\, n^{n-1/2}\, e^{-n}}{(2\p
i)^{a/2}\, a^{a/2-n}\, n^{n-a/2}\, e^{-n}} \cdot \left(1 + O(1/n)\right).$$

The $e^{-n}$ terms cancel. Collecting the remaining factors:

- **Power of $2\pi$:** $(2\pi)^{1/2 - a/2} = (2\pi)^{(1-a)/2}$.
- **Power of $n$:** $n^{(n-1/2)-(n-a/2)} = n^{(a-1)/2}$.
- **Power of $a$:** $a^{n - a/2} = a^n \cdot a^{-a/2}$.

Therefore:
$$\frac{\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(n/a)^a} = (2\pi)^{(1-a)/2}\, a^{-a/2}\, n^{(a-1)/2}\,
a^n \cdot \left(1 + O(1/n)\right).$$

**Step 4: Identify the decay rate function.**

$$\boxed{D(n) = \frac{(2\pi)^{(1-a)/2}}{a^{a/2}}\; n^{(a-1)/2}\; a^n}$$

with the precise asymptotic:
$$\frac{\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(n/a)^a} \sim D(n) \quad \text{as } n \to \infty.$$

**Step 5: Verification.**

- **Case $a = 1$:** $D(n) = \frac{1}{1}\cdot n^0 \cdot 1^n = 1$, and indeed $\Ga
mma(n)/\Gamma(n) = 1$. ✓

- **Case $a = 2$:** $D(n) = \frac{(2\pi)^{-1/2}}{2}\, n^{1/2}\, 2^n = \frac{\sqr
t{n}\, 2^{n-1}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}$. Direct computation gives $\Gamma(n)/\Gamma(n/2)^2
 \sim \frac{\sqrt{n}\, 2^{n-1}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}$. ✓

**Interpretation of the decay/growth behavior:**

| Regime | Behavior |
|--------|----------|
| $0 < a < 1$ | $a^n \to 0$ exponentially; $n^{(a-1)/2} \to 0$ polynomially → **
decay** |
| $a = 1$ | $D(n) = 1$ identically |
| $a > 1$ | $a^n \to \infty$ exponentially; $n^{(a-1)/2} \to \infty$ polynomiall
y → **growth** |

The dominant factor is the exponential $a^n$; the polynomial correction $n^{(a-1
)/2}$ and the constant $\frac{(2\pi)^{(1-a)/2}}{a^{a/2}}$ provide the sub-expone
ntial refinement. The exponential decay/growth rate is $\ln a$ per unit $n$.
