**Proof.**

Let $G$ be a group scheme over $k$ (the hypotheses of smoothness and affineness
are not needed for the argument). The commutativity of $G$ is equivalent to the
triviality of the commutator morphism. Concretely, define the **commutator morph
ism** of $k$-schemes

$$c : G \times_k G \longrightarrow G, \qquad (g, h) \longmapsto g\,h\,g^{-1}\,h^
{-1},$$

which is a morphism of $k$-schemes built from the multiplication, inverse, and p
rojection maps of $G$. Let $e : \operatorname{Spec} k \to G$ be the identity sec
tion, and let $\pi : G \times_k G \to \operatorname{Spec} k$ be the structure mo
rphism. Then:

$$G \text{ is commutative over } k \iff c = e \circ \pi \text{ as morphisms } G
\times_k G \to G.$$

This is an **equality of two morphisms** of $k$-schemes.

Now consider the base change along $k \hookrightarrow \bar{k}$. Since $k$ is a f
ield and $\bar{k} \neq 0$, the extension $k \to \bar{k}$ is **faithfully flat**
(every module over a field is flat, and a nonzero flat module over a field is fa
ithfully flat). The hypothesis that $G_{\bar{k}}$ is commutative over $\bar{k}$
says precisely that the base-changed commutator

$$c_{\bar{k}} : G_{\bar{k}} \times_{\bar{k}} G_{\bar{k}} \longrightarrow G_{\bar
{k}}$$

equals $(e \circ \pi)_{\bar{k}}$, i.e., the two morphisms $c$ and $e \circ \pi$
become equal after the faithfully flat base change $k \to \bar{k}$.

By **faithfully flat descent for morphisms** (fpqc descent): if two morphisms $f
, g : X \to Y$ of $k$-schemes satisfy $f_{\bar{k}} = g_{\bar{k}}$ after a faithf
ully flat extension $k \to \bar{k}$, then $f = g$ over $k$. (Indeed, the equaliz
er $\{f = g\}$ is a closed subscheme of $X$; its ideal sheaf is the kernel of $\
mathcal{O}_X \rightrightarrows \mathcal{O}_Y$ via $f^\#$ and $g^\#$, and faithfu
l flatness of $\bar{k}/k$ detects whether this kernel is all of $\mathcal{O}_X$,
 i.e., whether the equalizer is all of $X$.)

Applying this to $f = c$ and $g = e \circ \pi$, we conclude

$$c = e \circ \pi \quad \text{over } k,$$

hence $G$ is commutative over $k$.

**Remark.** The smoothness and affineness hypotheses are unnecessary: the conclu
sion holds for any group scheme over $k$ whatsoever, since the only ingredient i
s the faithful flatness of $\bar{k}/k$, which holds for any field $k$.
