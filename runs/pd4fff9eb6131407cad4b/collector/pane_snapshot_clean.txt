Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the given conditions force
the tail products $\prod_{i=m}^n (U_i+A_i)$ to converge to $I$.

**Answer: No.** The implication does not hold. Here is a counterexample.

---

**Counterexample.** Work with $2\times 2$ real matrices. Set

$$U_i = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & \tfrac{1}{2} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad A_i = 0
\qquad \text{for all } i \geq 1.$$

**Verification of the hypotheses:**

1. **$\|U_i\| = 1$:** The singular values of $U_i$ are $1$ and $\tfrac{1}{2}$, s
o the operator norm (largest singular value) is $1$. ✓

2. **$\prod_{i=r}^{\infty} U_i$ converges for all $r$:** Since all $U_i$ are equ
al, $\prod_{i=r}^{N} U_i = U^{\,N-r+1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2^{-(N-r+1
)} \end{pmatrix}$. As $N\to\infty$, this converges to $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\
0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ for every $r\geq 1$. ✓

3. **$\sum_i \|A_i\| < \infty$:** $\sum_i \|A_i\| = 0 < \infty$. ✓

**The conclusion fails:**

$$\prod_{i=m}^{n}(U_i + A_i) = U^{\,n-m+1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2^{-(n
-m+1)} \end{pmatrix}.$$

Take the sequence $m_k = n_k = k$ (so $m_k, n_k \to \infty$ with $m_k \leq n_k$)
. Then

$$\prod_{i=k}^{k}(U_i + A_i) = U = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & \tfrac{1}{2} \en
d{pmatrix} \neq I \qquad \text{for every } k.$$

Hence $\prod_{i=m}^{n}(U_i+A_i) \not\to I$ as $m,n\to\infty$. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark on why the implication fails.** The condition "$\prod_{i=r}^{\infty} U_
i$ converges" allows convergence to a **singular** matrix. In the counterexample
, the limit $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{pmatrix}$ is singular, and the t
ail products $\prod_{i=m}^{n} U_i$ do not approach $I$ — they retain the nontriv
ial factor $U$ when $n-m$ is small.

The tail-to-identity property $\prod_{i=m}^{n}(U_i+A_i)\to I$ is equivalent to c
onvergence of the infinite product to an **invertible** limit (the Cauchy criter
ion for invertible convergence). The hypotheses as stated do not guarantee inver
tibility of the limit.

**When the answer becomes Yes.** If one *additionally* requires that $\prod_{i=r
}^{\infty} U_i$ converges to an **invertible** matrix $V_r$ for every $r$, then
the answer is **Yes**. Briefly: invertibility of $V_r$ and $V_{r+1}$ gives $U_r
= V_r V_{r+1}^{-1} \to L\cdot L^{-1} = I$ (where $L = \lim_{r\to\infty} V_r$), a
nd the tail products satisfy $\prod_{i=m}^{n} U_i = V_m V_{n+1}^{-1} \to L\cdot
L^{-1} = I$. Writing $W_{m,n} = \prod_{i=m}^{n}(U_i+A_i)$ and $P_{m,n} = \prod_{
i=m}^{n} U_i$, the recursion $W_{m,n} - P_{m,n} = (W_{m,n-1}-P_{m,n-1})\,U_n + W
_{m,n-1}\,A_n$ together with $\|U_n\|=1$ and $\|W_{m,k}\|\leq \prod_{i=m}^{k}(1+
\|A_i\|)\leq e^{\sum_{i=m}^{\infty}\|A_i\|}\to 1$ yields $\|W_{m,n}-P_{m,n}\|\le
q e^{\varepsilon_m}\varepsilon_m\to 0$ where $\varepsilon_m=\sum_{i=m}^{\infty}\
|A_i\|\to 0$, so $W_{m,n}\to I$.

But under the hypotheses **as stated** (convergence to any limit, including sing
ular), the implication is **false**.
