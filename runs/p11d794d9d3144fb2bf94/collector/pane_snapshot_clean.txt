Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum pirate speed v such that the
 pirate at C can intercept both merchants (at B and D) before they reach harbor
A, where ABCD is a square of side s.

## Setup

Place the square with coordinates: A = (0,0), B = (s,0), D = (0,s), C = (s,s).

- Merchant at B travels along segment BA (the x-axis from (s,0) to (0,0)), speed
 ≤ 1.
- Merchant at D travels along segment DA (the y-axis from (0,s) to (0,0)), speed
 ≤ 1.
- Pirate starts at C = (s,s), speed ≤ v, free to move anywhere.

Both merchants must pass through A = (0,0) to reach the harbor.

## Sufficiency: v = √2 works

**Pirate strategy:** Travel in a straight line from C to A.

The pirate's distance to A is the diagonal s√2, so arrival time is s√2/v. With v
 = √2, the pirate arrives at A at time **t = s**.

Now consider any merchant strategy. A merchant traveling at full speed 1 covers
distance s (the side length) to reach A in time s. A merchant going slower reach
es A at time > s. A merchant that stops before reaching A never reaches the harb
or.

- **Both at full speed:** Both arrive at A at time s. The pirate is at A at time
 s. The pirate intercepts both simultaneously at A. ✓
- **One or both slower:** The pirate arrives at A at time s, intercepts whicheve
r merchant(s) arrive at time s, then waits at A for any later-arriving merchant
(which must pass through A). ✓
- **A merchant stops before A:** The pirate catches the other merchant at A, the
n travels from A along that merchant's segment to intercept the stopped (or slow
ly moving) merchant. Since the pirate is at A and the merchant is on the segment
 between A and its start, the pirate can always reach it (the pirate is faster,
√2 > 1, and the merchant cannot reverse direction). ✓

In all cases, the pirate intercepts both. So v = √2 is **sufficient**.

## Necessity: v < √2 is impossible

Suppose v < √2. Consider the adversarial strategy where **merchant B travels at
full speed 1**. At time t, B is at position (s − t, 0) for t ∈ [0, s].

For the pirate to intercept B at some point (s − t, 0) at time t, the pirate mus
t travel from C = (s, s) to (s − t, 0), a straight-line distance of √(t² + s²).
The earliest the pirate can arrive is:

$$\frac{\sqrt{t^2 + s^2}}{v}$$

For interception, we need:

$$\frac{\sqrt{t^2 + s^2}}{v} \leq t \implies v \geq \frac{\sqrt{t^2 + s^2}}{t} =
 \sqrt{1 + \frac{s^2}{t^2}}$$

The right-hand side is minimized when t is maximized, i.e., t = s (B at A), givi
ng:

$$v \geq \sqrt{1 + 1} = \sqrt{2}$$

For any t < s, the required v is strictly greater than √2. Therefore, if v < √2,
 **the pirate cannot intercept merchant B at any point on its path** — not even
at A — when B travels at full speed.

Since the pirate cannot even catch one merchant, it certainly cannot catch both.
 Therefore v ≥ √2 is **necessary**.

## Conclusion

The minimum value of v is:

$$\boxed{\sqrt{2}}$$

The pirate travels along the diagonal from C to A, arriving at time s (same as a
 full-speed merchant), and intercepts both merchants at or before A. No speed be
low √2 suffices because a single full-speed merchant cannot be caught at any poi
nt on its path.
