## Proof

We evaluate
$$I = \int_0^{2\pi} \log\!\bigl|1 - w e^{i\theta}\bigr|\,d\theta, \qquad |w|<1.$
$

**Step 1: The integrand is well-defined and smooth.**

Since $|w|<1$, for every $\theta\in[0,2\pi]$ we have $|we^{i\theta}|=|w|<1$, hen
ce
$$|1-we^{i\theta}|\ge 1-|w|>0.$$
Thus $1-we^{i\theta}\neq 0$ on the whole circle, so $\log|1-we^{i\theta}|$ is co
ntinuous (indeed real-analytic) on $[0,2\pi]$.

**Step 2: Reduce to a harmonic function on the closed unit disk.**

Define $f(z)=1-wz$. This is an entire function. On the closed unit disk $\overli
ne{D}=\{z:|z|\le 1\}$,
$$|wz|\le |w|<1 \implies f(z)\neq 0 \text{ on } \overline{D}.$$
Because $\overline{D}$ is simply connected and $f$ is nonvanishing there, there
exists a holomorphic branch $\log f(z)$ on a neighborhood of $\overline{D}$. Its
 real part
$$u(z)=\log|f(z)|=\log|1-wz|$$
is therefore harmonic on $\overline{D}$.

**Step 3: Apply the mean-value property.**

For a harmonic function on $\overline{D}$, the mean-value property at the origin
 gives
$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} u\!\bigl(e^{i\theta}\bigr)\,d\theta = u(0).$$
Substituting $u(z)=\log|1-wz|$:
$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log\!\bigl|1-we^{i\theta}\bigr|\,d\theta = \log|1-
w\cdot 0| = \log|1| = 0.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\int_0^{2\pi}\log\!\bigl|1-we^{i\theta}\bigr|\,d\theta = 0 \quad\text{f
or all } |w|<1.}$$

*Remark.* The hypothesis $|w|<1$ is essential: it guarantees $1-we^{i\theta}\neq
 0$ on the unit circle, so the logarithm is smooth and the mean-value property a
pplies directly. (When $|w|=1$ a zero sits on the contour and the integral must
be interpreted as an improper integral; when $|w|>1$ the answer becomes $2\pi\lo
g|w|$ by factoring out $-we^{i\theta}$.)
