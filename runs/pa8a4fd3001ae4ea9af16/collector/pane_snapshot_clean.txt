## Proof: Constructing an Ordered Field of Cardinality α for Any Infinite Cardin
al α

### Preliminary Observation

Every ordered field has characteristic 0. Indeed, in an ordered field $1 > 0$, s
o $1+1 > 1 > 0$, and by induction every finite sum $n \cdot 1 > 0$, hence $n \cd
ot 1 \neq 0$ for all $n \geq 1$. Therefore every ordered field contains a canoni
cal copy of $\mathbb{Q}$, which is the natural base for our construction.

---

### Case $\alpha = \aleph_0$

$\mathbb{Q}$ itself, with its usual ordering, is an ordered field of cardinality
 $\aleph_0$. Done.

### Case $\alpha > \aleph_0$ (uncountable): Construction by Transfinite Inductio
n

**Setup.** Let $\gamma$ be the initial ordinal of cardinality $\alpha$ (so $|\ga
mma| = \alpha$). Using the Well-Ordering Theorem (equivalent to the Axiom of Cho
ice), well-order a set of indeterminates $\{x_\beta : \beta < \gamma\}$. We cons
truct an increasing chain of ordered fields $(K_\beta, <_\beta)$ for $\beta \leq
 \gamma$ by transfinite recursion.

**Base stage.** Set $K_0 = \mathbb{Q}$ with its usual ordering.

**Successor stage.** Suppose $(K_\beta, <_\beta)$ is an ordered field and $x_\be
ta$ is transcendental over $K_\beta$. We construct an ordered field $K_{\beta+1}
 = K_\beta(x_\beta)$ extending $(K_\beta, <_\beta)$ as follows.

*Key Lemma (Ordering Extension).* **If $(K, <)$ is an ordered field and $x$ is t
ranscendental over $K$, then $K(x)$ admits an ordering extending $<$ in which $x
 > k$ for all $k \in K$.**

*Proof of Lemma.* Since $x$ is transcendental over $K$, the evaluation map $K(x)
 \hookrightarrow K((x^{-1}))$ — sending each rational function $f(x)/g(x)$ to it
s formal Laurent series expansion in $x^{-1}$ (obtained by polynomial long divis
ion) — is a well-defined field embedding. It remains to order $K((x^{-1}))$.

The field $K((t))$ of formal Laurent series in $t$ over an ordered field $(K,<)$
 is itself an ordered field: define $\sum_{n \geq n_0} a_n t^n > 0$ if and only
if the **leading coefficient** $a_{n_0}$ (the coefficient of the lowest power wi
th nonzero coefficient) satisfies $a_{n_0} > 0$ in $K$. We verify the positive c
one $P$ is valid:

- **Trichotomy:** For any nonzero series, its leading coefficient is nonzero, so
 exactly one of $s \in P$, $-s \in P$ holds. ✓
- **Multiplication closure:** The leading term of $s_1 \cdot s_2$ is the product
 of the leading terms, so $\operatorname{lc}(s_1 s_2) = \operatorname{lc}(s_1)\o
peratorname{lc}(s_2) > 0$. ✓
- **Addition closure:** If $s_1, s_2 \in P$ with leading coefficients $a, b > 0$
: if their leading degrees differ, the leading coefficient of $s_1+s_2$ is which
ever of $a, b$ corresponds to the lower degree (hence $> 0$); if their leading d
egrees coincide, the leading coefficient is $a+b > 0$ (sum of two positive eleme
nts). Either way $s_1 + s_2 \in P$. ✓

Substituting $t = x^{-1}$ orders $K((x^{-1}))$, and restricting to the subfield
$K(x)$ gives an ordering on $K(x)$ extending $<$. Since $x = (x^{-1})^{-1}$ has
leading term $x^{-1} \cdot x = 1$ with coefficient $1 > 0$, we get $x > 0$; more
 precisely, $x$ dominates every element of $K$ (its Laurent expansion $x = x$ ha
s higher "degree" than any constant), so $x > k$ for all $k \in K$. $\square$

Applying the lemma, set $K_{\beta+1} = K_\beta(x_\beta)$ with the ordering makin
g $x_\beta$ infinitely large over $K_\beta$.

**Limit stage.** For a limit ordinal $\lambda \leq \gamma$, set $K_\lambda = \bi
gcup_{\beta < \lambda} K_\beta$. Define the ordering: for $a, b \in K_\lambda$,
choose any $\beta < \lambda$ with $a, b \in K_\beta$ and declare $a <_\lambda b$
 iff $a <_\beta b$. This is well-defined because the orderings are nested (each
$<_\beta$ extends $<_\delta$ for $\delta < \beta$). Any finite collection of ele
ments of $K_\lambda$ lies in some common $K_\beta$, so field operations and orde
r-compatibility all reduce to the valid structure of $K_\beta$. Thus $(K_\lambda
, <_\lambda)$ is an ordered field. ✓

**Final field.** Set $K = K_\gamma = \mathbb{Q}(x_\beta : \beta < \gamma)$ with
the ordering $< \,= <_\gamma$.

### Cardinality Verification

**Claim:** $|K_\beta| = \max(\aleph_0, |\beta|)$ for all $\beta \leq \gamma$.

- **Base:** $|K_0| = |\mathbb{Q}| = \aleph_0 = \max(\aleph_0, 0)$. ✓
- **Successor:** $|K_{\beta+1}| = |K_\beta(x_\beta)| = |K_\beta| \cdot \aleph_0
= |K_\beta|$ (since $|K_\beta|$ is infinite; the set of rational functions in on
e variable over an infinite field $F$ has cardinality $|F| \cdot \aleph_0 = |F|$
). So $|K_{\beta+1}| = \max(\aleph_0, |\beta|) = \max(\aleph_0, |\beta+1|)$. ✓
- **Limit:** $|K_\lambda| = \left|\bigcup_{\beta<\lambda} K_\beta\right| \leq |\
lambda| \cdot \sup_{\beta<\lambda}|K_\beta| = |\lambda| \cdot \max(\aleph_0, |\l
ambda|) = \max(\aleph_0, |\lambda|)$. Also $|K_\lambda| \geq |\lambda|$ (it cont
ains the distinct indeterminates $\{x_\beta : \beta < \lambda\}$) and $|K_\lambd
a| \geq \aleph_0$ (it contains $\mathbb{Q}$). So $|K_\lambda| = \max(\aleph_0, |
\lambda|)$. ✓

Therefore $|K| = |K_\gamma| = \max(\aleph_0, |\gamma|) = \max(\aleph_0, \alpha)
= \alpha$. ✓

### Necessary Steps to Ensure Ordered Structure (Especially for Uncountable $\al
pha$)

1. **Start from $\mathbb{Q}$.** Every ordered field has characteristic 0 and con
tains $\mathbb{Q}$, so $\mathbb{Q}$ is the unavoidable base. Algebraic extension
s of $\mathbb{Q}$ are countable, so they cannot reach uncountable cardinality —
transcendental elements are essential.

2. **Adjoin transcendental elements one at a time (transfinite induction).** At
each successor stage, the new indeterminate $x_\beta$ is transcendental over the
 current field $K_\beta$. This is the crucial condition: transcendence guarantee
s that **no algebraic relation** can force a contradiction in the ordering. If $
x_\beta$ were algebraic over $K_\beta$, its minimal polynomial might have no rea
l root in the given ordering (e.g., $x^2 + 1 = 0$), making ordering extension im
possible. Transcendence sidesteps this entirely.

3. **Extend the ordering at each step via the Laurent series embedding.** The em
bedding $K_\beta(x_\beta) \hookrightarrow K_\beta((x_\beta^{-1}))$ provides a co
ncrete, well-defined ordering extension. Declaring $x_\beta$ to be "infinitely l
arge" is always consistent because the Laurent series field over an ordered fiel
d is always orderable. This step must be performed at **every** successor stage
— not just once — to maintain the ordered structure throughout the chain.

4. **Handle limit ordinals by taking unions.** When $\alpha$ is uncountable, the
 transfinite induction passes through uncountably many limit ordinals. At each l
imit stage $\lambda$, the union $K_\lambda = \bigcup_{\beta < \lambda} K_\beta$
must be equipped with the **compatible** ordering (inherited from any sufficient
ly large $K_\beta$). The nestedness of the orderings ensures consistency. This s
tep is specific to uncountable constructions — for countable $\alpha$, there are
 no uncountable limit stages to handle.

5. **Use the Axiom of Choice (Well-Ordering Theorem).** Well-ordering the index
set of size $\alpha$ requires AC. This is a set-theoretic necessity for organizi
ng the transfinite induction at uncountable cardinality.

6. **Verify cardinality at the end.** The cardinality computation $|K| = \max(\a
leph_0, \alpha) = \alpha$ confirms the construction achieves the target cardinal
ity. The key fact is that adjoining a single transcendental to an infinite field
 $F$ does not increase cardinality ($|F(x)| = |F|$), so cardinality only grows a
t limit stages, tracking $|\beta|$.

### Summary

For $\alpha = \aleph_0$, take $\mathbb{Q}$. For uncountable $\alpha$, well-order
 $\alpha$-many indeterminates and build $\mathbb{Q}(x_\beta : \beta < \gamma)$ b
y transfinite induction, extending the ordering at each successor stage via the
Laurent series embedding (making each $x_\beta$ infinitely large) and taking com
patible unions at limit stages. The resulting field is an ordered field of cardi
nality $\alpha$. The ordered structure is preserved because: (a) transcendence o
f each adjunction guarantees orderability, (b) the Laurent series construction g
ives an explicit ordering extension at each step, (c) nested orderings ensure co
nsistency at limit stages, and (d) the Axiom of Choice enables the well-ordering
 needed for the transfinite construction.
