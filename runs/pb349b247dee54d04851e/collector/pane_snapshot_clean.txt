## Proof

**Answer: Yes, weak*-convergence of the indicator functions implies $L^2$-conver
gence.**

**Key observation.** Since $\chi_{\omega_n}$ and $\chi_\omega$ take values in $\
{0,1\}$, the pointwise difference $\chi_{\omega_n}(x)-\chi_\omega(x)$ takes valu
es in $\{-1,0,1\}$. Hence
$$|\chi_{\omega_n}(x)-\chi_\omega(x)|^2 = |\chi_{\omega_n}(x)-\chi_\omega(x)|,$$
and therefore
$$\int_0^1 |\chi_{\omega_n}-\chi_\omega|^2\,dx = \int_0^1 |\chi_{\omega_n}-\chi_
\omega|\,dx = |\omega_n\,\triangle\,\omega|,$$
the Lebesgue measure of the symmetric difference. So it suffices to show $|\omeg
a_n\triangle\omega|\to 0$.

**Using the weak*-convergence.** The hypothesis is that for every $f\in L^1(0,1)
$,
$$\int_0^1 f\,(\chi_{\omega_n}-\chi_\omega)\,dx \to 0.$$

*Step 1.* Take $f\equiv 1\in L^1(0,1)$. Then
$$|\omega_n| = \int_0^1 \chi_{\omega_n}\,dx \longrightarrow \int_0^1 \chi_\omega
\,dx = |\omega|.$$

*Step 2.* Take $f = \chi_\omega\in L^1(0,1)$ (indicator functions of measurable
sets are in $L^1$ on a finite-measure space). Then
$$\int_0^1 \chi_\omega\,\chi_{\omega_n}\,dx = |\omega_n\cap\omega| \longrightarr
ow \int_0^1 \chi_\omega^2\,dx = |\omega|.$$

**Computing the symmetric difference.** By inclusion–exclusion,
$$|\omega_n\triangle\omega| = |\omega_n| + |\omega| - 2\,|\omega_n\cap\omega|.$$

Taking the limit and substituting the results of Steps 1 and 2:
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}|\omega_n\triangle\omega| = |\omega| + |\omega| - 2|\omega| =
 0.$$

**Conclusion.**
$$\int_0^1 |\chi_{\omega_n}(x)-\chi_\omega(x)|^2\,dx = |\omega_n\triangle\omega|
 \longrightarrow 0.$$

Thus weak*-convergence of the indicator functions **does** imply $L^2$-convergen
ce. $\blacksquare$
