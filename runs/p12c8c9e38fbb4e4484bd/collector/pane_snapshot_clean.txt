## Proof

Let $U : \mathbf{Vect}_k \to \mathbf{Set}$ be the forgetful functor, sending eac
h $k$-vector space to its underlying set and each linear map to its underlying f
unction. We claim $U$ has a left adjoint, namely the **free vector space** funct
or $F : \mathbf{Set} \to \mathbf{Vect}_k$.

**Construction of $F$.** For a set $S$, define
$$F(S) = k^{(S)} = \left\{ \sum_{s \in S} a_s \, s \;\middle|\; a_s \in k,\; a_s
 = 0 \text{ for all but finitely many } s \right\},$$
the vector space of finitely-supported functions $S \to k$, with pointwise addit
ion and scalar multiplication. The elements of $S$ embed as the standard basis v
ectors $\delta_s$. For a function $f : S \to T$, define
$$F(f) : F(S) \to F(T), \qquad \sum a_s \, s \;\mapsto\; \sum a_s \, f(s),$$
which is linear. One checks $F$ preserves identities and composition, so $F$ is
a functor.

**The adjunction.** We establish a natural bijection
$$\Phi_{S,V} : \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Vect}_k}(F(S), V) \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\
; \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Set}}(S, U(V))$$
for every set $S$ and vector space $V$.

- **Forward direction.** Given a linear map $\varphi : F(S) \to V$, define $\Phi
(\varphi) : S \to U(V)$ by $s \mapsto \varphi(\delta_s)$. This is a function fro
m $S$ to the underlying set of $V$.

- **Inverse direction.** Given a function $g : S \to U(V)$, define $\Phi^{-1}(g)
 : F(S) \to V$ by linear extension:
$$\Phi^{-1}(g)\!\left(\sum a_s \, s\right) = \sum a_s \, g(s).$$
This is well-defined because the sum is finite, and it is linear by construction
.

- **Mutual inverse.** For $\varphi : F(S) \to V$ linear, $\Phi^{-1}(\Phi(\varphi
))(\delta_s) = \Phi(\varphi)(s) = \varphi(\delta_s)$, so $\Phi^{-1}(\Phi(\varphi
))$ and $\varphi$ agree on the basis $\{\delta_s\}$, hence on all of $F(S)$. Con
versely, for $g : S \to U(V)$, $\Phi(\Phi^{-1}(g))(s) = \Phi^{-1}(g)(\delta_s) =
 g(s)$, so $\Phi(\Phi^{-1}(g)) = g$.

**Naturality.** We verify naturality in both variables.

- *In $V$:* For a linear map $\alpha : V \to W$, the square
$$\begin{array}{ccc}
\mathrm{Hom}(F(S), V) & \xrightarrow{\Phi_{S,V}} & \mathrm{Hom}(S, U(V)) \\
{\downarrow\, \alpha \circ -} & & {\downarrow\, U(\alpha) \circ -} \\
\mathrm{Hom}(F(S), W) & \xrightarrow{\Phi_{S,W}} & \mathrm{Hom}(S, U(W))
\end{array}$$
commutes: for $\varphi : F(S) \to V$,
$$\Phi_{S,W}(\alpha \circ \varphi)(s) = (\alpha \circ \varphi)(\delta_s) = \alph
a(\varphi(\delta_s)) = U(\alpha)(\Phi_{S,V}(\varphi)(s)).$$

- *In $S$:* For a function $h : S' \to S$, the square
$$\begin{array}{ccc}
\mathrm{Hom}(F(S), V) & \xrightarrow{\Phi_{S,V}} & \mathrm{Hom}(S, U(V)) \\
{\downarrow\, - \circ F(h)} & & {\downarrow\, - \circ h} \\
\mathrm{Hom}(F(S'), V) & \xrightarrow{\Phi_{S',V}} & \mathrm{Hom}(S', U(V))
\end{array}$$
commutes: for $\varphi : F(S) \to V$,
$$\Phi_{S',V}(\varphi \circ F(h))(s') = (\varphi \circ F(h))(\delta_{s'}) = \var
phi(\delta_{h(s')}) = \Phi_{S,V}(\varphi)(h(s')) = (\Phi_{S,V}(\varphi) \circ h)
(s').$$

**Conclusion.** The natural isomorphism $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Vect}_k}(F(S), V)
 \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Set}}(S, U(V))$ establishes that $F \dashv U$. Ther
efore, the forgetful functor $U : \mathbf{Vect}_k \to \mathbf{Set}$ **does** hav
e a left adjoint, given by the free vector space construction.
