## Proof

**Claim:** We cannot conclude $M = 0$ in general. What we *can* conclude is that
 $M$ is constant: $M_t = M_0$ for all $t$. The stronger conclusion $M = 0$ holds
 only under the additional assumption $M_0 = 0$.

### Step 1. Reduction to the case $M_0 = 0$.

Set $N_t := M_t - M_0$. Since $M_0$ is $\mathcal{F}_0$-measurable (hence a const
ant from the perspective of the filtration's evolution), $N$ is a continuous loc
al martingale with $N_0 = 0$. Subtracting a process of finite variation (here, t
he constant $M_0$) does not change the quadratic variation, so

$$\langle N \rangle = \langle M \rangle = 0.$$

Moreover, for a **continuous** local martingale the optional quadratic variation
 coincides with the predictable one: $[N] = \langle N \rangle = 0$ (the jump com
ponent, which is the only thing distinguishing $[N]$ from $\langle N\rangle$, va
nishes by continuity).

### Step 2. $N \equiv 0$.

Let $(\tau_n)_{n \geq 1}$ be a localizing sequence for $N$ so that each stopped
process $N^{\tau_n}$ is a bounded (hence true) martingale. Since $N^{\tau_n}$ is
 a square-integrable martingale starting at $0$, the Itô isometry / martingale i
dentity gives

$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[(N^{\tau_n}_t)^2\bigr] = \mathbb{E}\bigl[[N^{\tau_n}]_t\bigr]
= \mathbb{E}\bigl[[N]_{t \wedge \tau_n}\bigr] = \mathbb{E}[0] = 0.$$

Hence $N^{\tau_n}_t = 0$ almost surely for every $t \geq 0$ and every $n$. Since
 $\tau_n \to \infty$ almost surely, we obtain $N_t = 0$ a.s. for every $t$. By p
ath-continuity of $N$, the entire path is indistinguishable from $0$:

$$N \equiv 0 \quad \text{a.s.}$$

### Step 3. Conclusion.

From $N \equiv 0$ we get $M_t = M_0$ for all $t \geq 0$; that is, $M$ is **const
ant** (indistinguishable from the constant process $M_0$).

- If $M_0 = 0$, then $M \equiv 0$.
- If $M_0 \neq 0$, then $M$ is a nonzero constant local martingale with $\langle
 M \rangle = 0$, providing an explicit counterexample to the claim $M = 0$.

**Therefore, we cannot conclude $M = 0$ from $\langle M \rangle = 0$ alone.** Th
e correct conclusion is that $M$ is constant ($M_t = M_0$ for all $t$); $M = 0$
follows only when $M_0 = 0$.
