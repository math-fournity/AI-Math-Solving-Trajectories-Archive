## Proof

**Setup.** Let $s_0 = \theta + it$ be a non-trivial zero of $\zeta$, so $\zeta(s
_0) = 0$ with $0 < \theta < 1$. The Euler–Maclaurin formula gives, for large $n$
,

$$\zeta(s_0) = \zeta_n(s_0) - \frac{n^{1-s_0}}{1-s_0} + O(n^{-\theta}).$$

Since $\zeta(s_0) = 0$, we obtain

$$\zeta_n(s_0) = \frac{n^{1-s_0}}{1-s_0} + R_n, \qquad |R_n| \le C\, n^{-\theta}
 \tag{1}$$

for some constant $C > 0$ and all sufficiently large $n$.

**The logarithmic spiral.** Writing $s_0 = \theta + it$,

$$\frac{n^{1-s_0}}{1-s_0} = \frac{n^{1-\theta}\, e^{-it\ln n}}{|1-s_0|\, e^{i\ar
g(1-s_0)}} = \frac{n^{1-\theta}}{|1-s_0|}\, e^{-i(t\ln n + \arg(1-s_0))}.$$

As $n$ increases, this traces a logarithmic spiral: the modulus grows as $n^{1-\
theta}$ while the argument rotates as $-t\ln n$. This confirms the spiral struct
ure described in the problem.

**Bounding the modulus.** From (1) and the reverse triangle inequality,

$$\bigl||\zeta_n(s_0)| - \tfrac{n^{1-\theta}}{|1-s_0|}\bigr| \le |R_n| \le C\, n
^{-\theta}. \tag{2}$$

Denote $M_n := \frac{n^{1-\theta}}{|1-s_0|}$ and $E_n := |\zeta_n(s_0)| - M_n$,
so $|E_n| \le C\, n^{-\theta}$.

**Consecutive difference.** We compute

$$\bigl||\zeta_{n+1}(s_0)| - |\zeta_n(s_0)|\bigr| = |(M_{n+1} - M_n) + (E_{n+1}
- E_n)| \le |M_{n+1} - M_n| + |E_{n+1}| + |E_n|. \tag{3}$$

For the main (spiral) term, by the mean value theorem applied to $f(x) = x^{1-\t
heta}$,

$$M_{n+1} - M_n = \frac{(n+1)^{1-\theta} - n^{1-\theta}}{|1-s_0|} = \frac{(1-\th
eta)\,\xi_n^{-\theta}}{|1-s_0|}$$

for some $\xi_n \in (n, n+1)$, hence

$$|M_{n+1} - M_n| \le \frac{1-\theta}{|1-s_0|}\, n^{-\theta}. \tag{4}$$

Substituting (4) and the bound on $E_n$ into (3):

$$\bigl||\zeta_{n+1}(s_0)| - |\zeta_n(s_0)|\bigr| \le \frac{1-\theta}{|1-s_0|}\,
 n^{-\theta} + C\,(n+1)^{-\theta} + C\, n^{-\theta}. \tag{5}$$

**Conclusion.** Since $\theta > 0$ (the zero lies in the critical strip), every
term on the right-hand side of (5) is $O(n^{-\theta}) \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$.
Therefore

$$\bigl||\zeta_{n+1}(\theta+it)| - |\zeta_n(\theta+it)|\bigr| \longrightarrow 0
\quad \text{as } n \to \infty.$$

Hence, **yes**: for any $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists $N \in \mathbb{N}$ such
that for all integers $m > N$,

$$\bigl||\zeta_{m+1}(\theta+it)| - |\zeta_m(\theta+it)|\bigr| < \varepsilon.$$

**Justification summary.** At a non-trivial zero, the partial sum $\zeta_n(s_0)$
 is dominated by the Euler–Maclaurin correction term $\frac{n^{1-s_0}}{1-s_0}$,
whose modulus $M_n = \frac{n^{1-\theta}}{|1-s_0|}$ is a smooth, slowly varying f
unction of $n$. The consecutive increment $M_{n+1} - M_n$ is of order $n^{-\thet
a}$, and the remainder $R_n$ is also $O(n^{-\theta})$. Since $\theta \in (0,1)$,
 both contributions vanish, forcing the consecutive modulus differences to zero.
 The spiral tightens: each new vertex adds a step whose radial displacement shri
nks to zero, even though the spiral itself continues to expand (since $1-\theta
> 0$).
