## Solution

We seek all real solutions $(a,b,c,d)$ to
$$a+b+c=d, \qquad \frac1a+\frac1b+\frac1c=\frac1d.$$

Since reciprocals appear, $a,b,c,d\neq 0$.

**Reduction.** Substituting $d=a+b+c$ into the second equation and clearing deno
minators (all nonzero):
$$\frac{ab+bc+ca}{abc}=\frac{1}{a+b+c}\implies (a+b+c)(ab+bc+ca)=abc. \tag{$\ast
$}$$

**Key identity.** A direct expansion gives
$$(a+b)(b+c)(c+a)=a^2b+ab^2+a^2c+ac^2+b^2c+bc^2+2abc,$$
$$(a+b+c)(ab+bc+ca)=a^2b+ab^2+a^2c+ac^2+b^2c+bc^2+3abc.$$
Subtracting,
$$(a+b+c)(ab+bc+ca)-abc=(a+b)(b+c)(c+a).$$

So $(\ast)$ is equivalent to
$$(a+b)(b+c)(c+a)=0. \tag{$\ast\ast$}$$

Thus at least one of $a+b,\,b+c,\,c+a$ vanishes.

**Case 1: $a+b=0$.** Then $b=-a$ with $a\neq 0$, and $d=a+b+c=c$. We need $d\neq
 0$, so $c\neq 0$. Check:
$$\frac1a+\frac1b+\frac1c=\frac1a-\frac1a+\frac1c=\frac1c=\frac1d.\ \checkmark$$
Family: $\{(a,-a,c,c):a,c\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\}$.

**Case 2: $b+c=0$.** Then $c=-b$ with $b\neq 0$, and $d=a+b+c=a$, so $a\neq 0$.
Check:
$$\frac1a+\frac1b+\frac1c=\frac1a+\frac1b-\frac1b=\frac1a=\frac1d.\ \checkmark$$
Family: $\{(a,b,-b,a):a,b\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\}$.

**Case 3: $c+a=0$.** Then $a=-c$ with $c\neq 0$, and $d=a+b+c=b$, so $b\neq 0$.
Check:
$$\frac1a+\frac1b+\frac1c=-\frac1c+\frac1b+\frac1c=\frac1b=\frac1d.\ \checkmark$
$
Family: $\{(-c,b,c,b):b,c\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\}$.

**Completeness.** The identity shows ($\ast$)$\iff$($\ast\ast$), so every soluti
on satisfies one of the three cases above. Conversely every member of the three
families was verified to satisfy both original equations (with all entries nonze
ro). The three families overlap on their pairwise intersections (where two of th
e pair-sums vanish simultaneously), but their union is exactly the full solution
 set.

**Answer.** The set of all real solutions is
$$\boxed{\,(a,b,c,d)\in\mathbb R^4:\ a,b,c,d\neq 0,\ d=a+b+c,\ (a+b)(b+c)(c+a)=0
\,}$$
equivalently the union of
$$\{(a,-a,c,c)\},\quad \{(a,b,-b,a)\},\quad \{(-c,b,c,b)\},\qquad a,b,c\in\mathb
b R\setminus\{0\}.$$
