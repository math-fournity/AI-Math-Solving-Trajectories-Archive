## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** The unit circle can be covered by finitely many (axis-aligned)
rectangles whose total area is arbitrarily small.

---

**Setup.** The unit circle is $S^1 = \{(\cos t, \sin t) : t \in [0, 2\pi]\}$. Fi
x any $\varepsilon > 0$. We will find finitely many rectangles covering $S^1$ wi
th total area $< \varepsilon$.

**Construction.** Choose an integer $n \geq 1$ and partition $[0, 2\pi]$ into $n
$ equal subintervals $[\theta_i, \theta_{i+1}]$ where $\theta_i = 2\pi i / n$ an
d $\Delta = \theta_{i+1} - \theta_i = 2\pi/n$.

For each $i = 0, \dots, n-1$, consider the arc segment
$$A_i = \{(\cos t, \sin t) : t \in [\theta_i, \theta_{i+1}]\}.$$

Define the axis-aligned rectangle
$$R_i = [a_i, b_i] \times [c_i, d_i],$$
where
$$a_i = \min_{t \in [\theta_i, \theta_{i+1}]} \cos t, \quad b_i = \max_{t \in [\
theta_i, \theta_{i+1}]} \cos t, \quad c_i = \min_{t \in [\theta_i, \theta_{i+1}]
} \sin t, \quad d_i = \max_{t \in [\theta_i, \theta_{i+1}]} \sin t.$$

By construction, $A_i \subseteq R_i$ for every $i$, and the $n$ rectangles $R_0,
 \dots, R_{n-1}$ cover $S^1$.

**Area bound.** The area of $R_i$ is $(b_i - a_i)(d_i - c_i)$. By the Mean Value
 Theorem, for any $s, t \in [\theta_i, \theta_{i+1}]$,
$$|\cos s - \cos t| \leq |s - t| \cdot \max_{u} |\sin u| \leq \Delta,$$
$$|\sin s - \sin t| \leq |s - t| \cdot \max_{u} |\cos u| \leq \Delta,$$
since $|\sin u| \leq 1$ and $|\cos u| \leq 1$. Hence $b_i - a_i \leq \Delta$ and
 $d_i - c_i \leq \Delta$, giving
$$\operatorname{area}(R_i) \leq \Delta^2.$$

**Total area.** Summing over all $n$ rectangles:
$$\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \operatorname{area}(R_i) \leq n \cdot \Delta^2 = n \cdot \lef
t(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{n}.$$

**Conclusion.** Given $\varepsilon > 0$, choose $n > 4\pi^2 / \varepsilon$. Then
 the $n$ rectangles $R_0, \dots, R_{n-1}$ cover $S^1$ and satisfy
$$\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \operatorname{area}(R_i) \leq \frac{4\pi^2}{n} < \varepsilon.
$$

Since $\varepsilon > 0$ was arbitrary, the unit circle can be covered by finitel
y many rectangles with arbitrarily small total area. $\blacksquare$
