## Proof

We wish to determine whether
$$L = \lim_{x\to\infty}\prod_{p\leqslant x} p^{1/(2x)}$$
is finite, where the product runs over all primes $p\leqslant x$.

**Step 1: Take logarithms.**

Since every factor is positive, we may take logarithms:
$$\log L_x = \log\!\left(\prod_{p\leqslant x} p^{1/(2x)}\right) = \frac{1}{2x}\s
um_{p\leqslant x} \log p.$$

The sum $\sum_{p\leqslant x}\log p$ is **Chebyshev's theta function**, denoted $
\vartheta(x)$.

**Step 2: Apply the Prime Number Theorem.**

The Prime Number Theorem, in its equivalent form due to Chebyshev and de la Vall
ée-Poussin, states:
$$\vartheta(x) = \sum_{p\leqslant x}\log p \sim x \quad\text{as } x\to\infty.$$

That is, $\vartheta(x)/x \to 1$.

**Step 3: Compute the limit of the logarithm.**

Therefore:
$$\log L_x = \frac{\vartheta(x)}{2x} \longrightarrow \frac{1}{2}\quad\text{as }x
\to\infty.$$

**Step 4: Exponentiate.**

Since $\exp$ is continuous:
$$L = \lim_{x\to\infty}\prod_{p\leqslant x} p^{1/(2x)} = e^{1/2} = \sqrt{e}.$$

**Conclusion.** The limit exists and equals $\sqrt{e}$, which is finite. $\black
square$
