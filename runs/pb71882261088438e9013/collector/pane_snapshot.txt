## Problem Analysis

We have $w: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ with:
- $w$ analytic for $x < 0$
- $w \sim e^x$ as $x \to -\infty$ (so $w$ and all derivatives decay exponentiall
y — no issue at $-\infty$)
- $w \sim |x|^p$ as $x \to 0^-$
- $w = 0$ for $x > 0$

The only regularity issue is at $x = 0$, where $w$ transitions from $|x|^p$ (lef
t) to $0$ (right). Away from $x=0$, $w$ is smooth (analytic on $(-\infty,0)$, id
entically zero on $(0,\infty)$), and the decay at $-\infty$ ensures integrabilit
y there. So the Sobolev regularity is entirely determined by the singularity at
$x = 0$.

## Key Reduction

Near $x = 0$, $w(x) \sim (-x)^p \cdot \mathbf{1}_{x < 0}$. Define the model func
tion:
$$f(x) = (-x)_+^p := \begin{cases} (-x)^p & x < 0 \\ 0 & x \geq 0 \end{cases}$$

Since $w - f$ is smoother than $f$ near $0$ (the difference is analytic on the l
eft and zero on the right, with matching leading behavior), $w$ and $f$ have the
 same Sobolev regularity threshold. By reflection ($x \mapsto -x$), $f$ has the
same regularity as $g(x) = x_+^p$.

## Fourier Analysis of $x_+^p$

For $p > -1$ (needed for $f \in L^2_{\text{loc}}$), the Fourier transform of $x_
+^p$ is (in the distributional sense):
$$\widehat{x_+^p}(\xi) = \frac{\Gamma(p+1)}{(i\xi)^{p+1}}$$

so for large $|\xi|$:
$$|\widehat{x_+^p}(\xi)| \sim C\,|\xi|^{-(p+1)}$$

## Sobolev Membership Condition

$w \in H^s(\mathbb{R})$ requires:
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} (1+|\xi|^2)^s\,|\hat{w}(\xi)|^2\,d\xi < \infty$$

The low-frequency part is finite (since $w \in L^2$ for $p > -1/2$, which we'll
have). The high-frequency part converges iff:
$$\int_{|\xi|>1} |\xi|^{2s}\,|\xi|^{-2(p+1)}\,d\xi = \int_{|\xi|>1} |\xi|^{2s -
2p - 2}\,d\xi < \infty$$

This integral converges at infinity iff:
$$2s - 2p - 2 < -1 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad s < p + \tfrac{1}{2}$$

**Therefore:** $w \in H^s(\mathbb{R})$ if and only if $s < p + \frac{1}{2}$ (and
 $s \geq 0$, $p > -1/2$ for $L^2$).

## Applying the Condition $s > 3/2$

We need $w \in H^s$ for **some** $s > \frac{3}{2}$. This requires that the upper
 bound on admissible $s$ exceeds $\frac{3}{2}$:
$$p + \frac{1}{2} > \frac{3}{2} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad p > 1$$

- **If $p > 1$:** Choose any $s$ with $\frac{3}{2} < s < p + \frac{1}{2}$. Then
$w \in H^s$. ✓
- **If $p = 1$:** The maximal regularity is $s < \frac{3}{2}$, so no $s > \frac{
3}{2}$ works. ✗
- **If $p < 1$:** The maximal regularity is even lower. ✗

## Verification of Other Regions

- **At $x \to -\infty$:** $w \sim e^x$ decays exponentially, so $w$ and all its
derivatives are in $L^2((-\infty, -1))$. No restriction on $s$ from this region.
- **On $(0, \infty)$:** $w \equiv 0$, trivially in every $H^s$.
- **Away from $0$ on $(-\infty, 0)$:** $w$ is analytic, hence $C^\infty$, contri
buting no restriction.

Thus the singularity at $x = 0$ is the **sole** determinant of the Sobolev thres
hold.

## Conclusion

The function $w$ belongs to $H^s(\mathbb{R})$ for some $s > \frac{3}{2}$ if and
only if $p > 1$.

The **critical (threshold) value** is:

$$\boxed{p = 1}$$

For $p > 1$, $w \in H^s$ for all $s \in (\frac{3}{2},\, p + \frac{1}{2})$; for $
p \leq 1$, no $s > \frac{3}{2}$ is achievable.
