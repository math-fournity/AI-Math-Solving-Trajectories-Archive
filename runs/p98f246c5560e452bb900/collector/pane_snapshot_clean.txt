## Proof of `trivial_case`: `DivisorSumSet 0 = Set.univ`

By definition
$$\mathrm{DivisorSumSet}(0)=\{n:\mathbb N\mid \exists\, s\subseteq \operatorname
{divisors}(n),\; 0=\textstyle\sum_{i\in s} i\}.$$

(⊆) Let $n\in\mathbb N$. Take $s=\varnothing$. Then $s\subseteq\operatorname{div
isors}(n)$ trivially, and $\sum_{i\in\varnothing} i = 0$. Hence $n\in\mathrm{Div
isorSumSet}(0)$.

(⊇) $\mathrm{DivisorSumSet}(0)\subseteq\mathbb N=\mathrm{Set.univ}$ is immediate
.

Therefore $\mathrm{DivisorSumSet}(0)=\mathrm{Set.univ}$. ∎

## Proof of `positive_density`: for every $t:\mathbb N$, `DivisorSumSet t` has p
ositive density

We split on whether $t=0$.

**Case $t=0$.** By `trivial_case`, $\mathrm{DivisorSumSet}(0)=\mathrm{Set.univ}$
, which has natural density $1>0$.

**Case $t>0$.** We exhibit a positive-density subset contained in $\mathrm{Divis
orSumSet}(t)$.

*Claim.* $\{n\in\mathbb N : t\mid n\}\subseteq\mathrm{DivisorSumSet}(t)$.

*Proof of claim.* Let $n$ be a multiple of $t$, i.e. $t\mid n$. Since $t>0$ and
$t\mid n$, we have $t\in\operatorname{divisors}(n)$ (the set of positive divisor
s of $n$). Take $s=\{t\}\subseteq\operatorname{divisors}(n)$. Then
$$\sum_{i\in s} i = t.$$
So $n\in\mathrm{DivisorSumSet}(t)$. ∎

The set of multiples of $t$,
$$M_t:=\{n\in\mathbb N:t\mid n\}=\{0,t,2t,3t,\dots\},$$
has natural density
$$d(M_t)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{|\{n<N:t\mid n\}|}{N}=\frac{1}{t}>0,$$
a standard fact (the count up to $N$ is $\lfloor N/t\rfloor+O(1)$, giving densit
y exactly $1/t$).

Since $M_t\subseteq\mathrm{DivisorSumSet}(t)$ and $M_t$ has positive density $1/
t$, any natural density that $\mathrm{DivisorSumSet}(t)$ possesses is at least $
1/t>0$; in particular $\mathrm{DivisorSumSet}(t)$ has positive lower density, he
nce `HasPosDensity`. ∎

**Remark on the hint.** The "multiplicative structure" the hint points at is pre
cisely the containment $\mathrm{DivisorSumSet}(t)\supseteq t\mathbb N$ used abov
e: divisibility by $t$ is the multiplicative condition, and it forces $t$ itself
 to be an available divisor, so the singleton sum $\{t\}$ witnesses membership.
One can strengthen this — e.g. $\mathrm{DivisorSumSet}(t)$ is also upward-closed
 under multiplication by integers coprime to suitable factors — but the singleto
n-divisor argument already suffices for positive density.
