## Proof

Let $A = (X_{ij})$ be an $n\times n$ matrix with independent entries satisfying
$P(X_{ij}=1)=P(X_{ij}=-1)=\tfrac12$, so $\mathbb{E}[X_{ij}]=0$ and $\mathbb{E}[X
_{ij}^2]=1$.

**Step 1: $\mathbb{E}[\det A]=0$.**

By the Leibniz formula,
$$\det A = \sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^n X_{i,\sig
ma(i)}.$$
Each product $\prod_i X_{i,\sigma(i)}$ is a product of independent, mean-zero ra
ndom variables, hence has expectation $0$. Therefore $\mathbb{E}[\det A]=0$, and
$$\operatorname{Var}(\det A)=\mathbb{E}[(\det A)^2].$$

**Step 2: Expand $(\det A)^2$.**

$$(\det A)^2=\sum_{\sigma,\tau\in S_n}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\operatorname{sg
n}(\tau)\prod_{i=1}^n X_{i,\sigma(i)}X_{i,\tau(i)}.$$

Taking expectations and using independence across distinct entries,
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\prod_{i=1}^n X_{i,\sigma(i)}X_{i,\tau(i)}\right]=\prod_{i=1
}^n \mathbb{E}\!\left[X_{i,\sigma(i)}X_{i,\tau(i)}\right].$$

**Step 3: Evaluate each factor.**

For a fixed $i$:
- If $\sigma(i)=\tau(i)$, then $X_{i,\sigma(i)}X_{i,\tau(i)}=X_{i,\sigma(i)}^2=1
$, so the factor equals $1$.
- If $\sigma(i)\neq\tau(i)$, then $X_{i,\sigma(i)}$ and $X_{i,\tau(i)}$ are inde
pendent mean-zero variables, so $\mathbb{E}[X_{i,\sigma(i)}X_{i,\tau(i)}]=0$.

Hence the whole product is nonzero **only when $\sigma(i)=\tau(i)$ for every $i$
**, i.e. $\sigma=\tau$. In that sole case the product equals $1$.

**Step 4: Conclude.**

$$\mathbb{E}[(\det A)^2]=\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)^2\cdot 1
=\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}1=n!.$$

**Sanity checks.**
- $n=1$: $\det A=X_{11}$, $\operatorname{Var}=1=1!$. ✓
- $n=2$: $\det A=X_{11}X_{22}-X_{12}X_{21}$, and $\mathbb{E}[(\det A)^2]=1+1-2\c
dot 0=2=2!$. ✓

Therefore,

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(\det A)=n!}$$
