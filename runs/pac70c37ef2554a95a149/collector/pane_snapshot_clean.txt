## Proof

**Setup.** Let $X$ be a compact Hausdorff space, $\Gamma = C(X)$ the space of co
ntinuous real-valued functions on $X$, $\Sigma = \mathcal{B}(X)$ the Borel $\sig
ma$-algebra, and $F: C(X) \to \mathbb{R}$ a positive linear functional (i.e., $F
(f) \geq 0$ whenever $f \geq 0$) satisfying $F(\mathbf{1}) = 1$, where $\mathbf{
1}$ is the constant function $1$.

**Claim.** There exists a unique probability measure $\mu: \mathcal{B}(X) \to \m
athbb{R}$ such that
$$F(f) = \int_X f \, d\mu \quad \text{for all } f \in C(X).$$

### Part I: Uniqueness

Suppose $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$ are two regular Borel probability measures on $X$ su
ch that $\int f \, d\mu_1 = \int f \, d\mu_2 = F(f)$ for all $f \in C(X)$.

Let $E \in \mathcal{B}(X)$ and $\varepsilon > 0$. Since $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$ are
regular Borel measures on a compact Hausdorff space, there exist a compact set $
K$ and an open set $U$ with $K \subseteq E \subseteq U$ such that
$$\mu_i(U \setminus K) < \varepsilon, \quad i = 1, 2.$$

Since $X$ is compact Hausdorff (hence normal), by **Urysohn's Lemma** there exis
ts $f \in C(X)$ with $0 \leq f \leq 1$, $f|_K = 1$, and $f|_{X \setminus U} = 0$
.

Since $\int f \, d\mu_1 = \int f \, d\mu_2$, we have:
$$\mu_i(K) \leq \int f \, d\mu_i \leq \mu_i(U), \quad i = 1, 2.$$

Therefore:
$$\mu_1(E) \leq \mu_1(U) < \mu_1(K) + \varepsilon \leq \int f \, d\mu_1 + \varep
silon = \int f \, d\mu_2 + \varepsilon \leq \mu_2(U) + \varepsilon < \mu_2(K) +
2\varepsilon \leq \mu_2(E) + 2\varepsilon.$$

By symmetry, $|\mu_1(E) - \mu_2(E)| < 2\varepsilon$. Since $\varepsilon > 0$ is
arbitrary, $\mu_1(E) = \mu_2(E)$ for all $E \in \mathcal{B}(X)$. Hence $\mu_1 =
\mu_2$. $\square$
