**Problem.** Evaluate $I = \int_0^1 \int_{x_1}^1 x_2^2 \sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi x_
1}{x_2}\right) dx_2\, dx_1$ without Fubini's theorem.

---

**Proof.**

**Step 1 — Substitution in the inner integral (single-variable, not Fubini).**

For fixed $x_1 \in (0,1]$, set $t = x_1/x_2$, i.e. $x_2 = x_1/t$, $dx_2 = -x_1/t
^2\,dt$. Limits: $x_2=x_1 \Rightarrow t=1$; $x_2=1 \Rightarrow t=x_1$. Then

$$\int_{x_1}^1 x_2^2 \sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi x_1}{x_2}\right) dx_2 = \int_1^{x_1}
\frac{x_1^2}{t^2}\sin(2\pi t)\!\left(-\frac{x_1}{t^2}\right)dt = x_1^3\int_{x_1}
^1 \frac{\sin(2\pi t)}{t^4}\,dt.$$

(At $x_1=0$ the inner integral is $\int_0^1 x_2^2\sin(0)\,dx_2=0$, consistent wi
th the factor $x_1^3$.)

**Step 2 — Reduce to one outer integral.**

Define $F(x)=\int_x^1 \frac{\sin(2\pi t)}{t^4}\,dt$ for $x>0$. Then

$$I=\int_0^1 x^3 F(x)\,dx, \qquad F'(x)=-\frac{\sin(2\pi x)}{x^4}\quad\text{(FTC
)}.$$

**Step 3 — Integration by parts on the outer integral.**

For $\epsilon>0$, on $[\epsilon,1]$ take $u=F(x)$, $dv=x^3\,dx$:

$$\int_\epsilon^1 x^3 F(x)\,dx=\left[\frac{x^4}{4}F(x)\right]_\epsilon^1+\frac{1
}{4}\int_\epsilon^1\sin(2\pi x)\,dx.$$

**Step 4 — Boundary terms.**

- $x=1$: $\tfrac14 F(1)=\tfrac14\int_1^1(\cdots)\,dt=0$.
- $x=\epsilon$: Since $\sin(2\pi t)=2\pi t+O(t^3)$ near $0$, we have $\frac{\sin
(2\pi t)}{t^4}=\frac{2\pi}{t^3}+O(t^{-1})$, hence $F(\epsilon)=O(\epsilon^{-2})$
. Therefore $\frac{\epsilon^4}{4}F(\epsilon)=O(\epsilon^2)\to 0$ as $\epsilon\to
0^+$.

**Step 5 — Remaining integral.**

$$\frac14\int_0^1\sin(2\pi x)\,dx=\frac14\left[-\frac{\cos(2\pi x)}{2\pi}\right]
_0^1=\frac14\cdot\frac{-\cos(2\pi)+\cos 0}{2\pi}=\frac14\cdot\frac{-1+1}{2\pi}=0
.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$I=\lim_{\epsilon\to0^+}\!\left(0-\frac{\epsilon^4}{4}F(\epsilon)+\frac14\int_\
epsilon^1\sin(2\pi x)\,dx\right)=0-0+0=0.$$

$$\boxed{I=0}.$$
