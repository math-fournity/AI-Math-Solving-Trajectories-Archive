Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a Bernoulli measure on a sy
mbolic space is aperiodic, considering the relevant definitions from ergodic the
ory.

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $\Sigma = \mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}$ (or $\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
$) be the full shift over a finite alphabet $\mathcal{A}$ with $|\mathcal{A}| \g
eq 2$, equipped with the shift map $\sigma$. A **Bernoulli measure** $\mu$ on $\
Sigma$ is the infinite product measure
$$\mu = \prod_{i=1}^{\infty} \nu$$
where $\nu$ is a probability distribution on $\mathcal{A}$.

Recall that a measure-preserving transformation $T$ on $(X, \mathcal{B}, \mu)$ i
s **aperiodic** if the set of periodic points has $\mu$-measure zero:
$$\mu\bigl(\{x \in X : T^n x = x \text{ for some } n \geq 1\}\bigr) = 0.$$

---

**Case 1: Non-degenerate Bernoulli measure ($\nu$ assigns positive probability t
o at least two symbols).**

We show $\sigma$ is aperiodic with respect to $\mu$.

*Step 1: The set of periodic points is countable.* The set of all periodic point
s is
$$P = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} \operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^n).$$
For each $n \geq 1$, a point $x \in \operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^n)$ is completely
determined by its first $n$ symbols $(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$, since $x_{i+n} = x_i$
for all $i$. Hence $|\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^n)| \leq |\mathcal{A}|^n < \infty
$. Therefore $P$ is a countable union of finite sets, so $P$ is countable.

*Step 2: Every singleton has $\mu$-measure zero.* For any $x = (x_1, x_2, \ldots
) \in \Sigma$, we have
$$\{x\} = \bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty} [x_1 x_2 \cdots x_k],$$
where $[x_1 \cdots x_k]$ denotes the cylinder set of sequences beginning with $x
_1, \ldots, x_k$. By continuity from above:
$$\mu(\{x\}) = \lim_{k \to \infty} \mu([x_1 \cdots x_k]) = \lim_{k \to \infty} \
prod_{i=1}^{k} \nu(\{x_i\}).$$
Since $\nu$ is non-degenerate, every symbol $a \in \mathcal{A}$ satisfies $\nu(\
{a\}) < 1$ (because at least one other symbol has positive probability, and prob
abilities sum to 1). Thus each factor $\nu(\{x_i\}) \in (0, 1)$, and the infinit
e product tends to zero:
$$\mu(\{x\}) = 0.$$

*Step 3: Conclusion.* Since $P$ is countable and every singleton has $\mu$-measu
re zero, by countable additivity:
$$\mu(P) = \sum_{x \in P} \mu(\{x\}) = 0.$$
Hence $\sigma$ is aperiodic with respect to $\mu$. $\checkmark$

---

**Case 2: Degenerate Bernoulli measure ($\nu(\{a\}) = 1$ for some single $a \in
\mathcal{A}$).**

Then $\mu$ is the Dirac measure $\delta_{a^\infty}$ concentrated on the constant
 sequence $a^\infty = (a, a, a, \ldots)$. This point is a fixed point of $\sigma
$ (period 1), and $\mu(\{a^\infty\}) = 1$. Thus
$$\mu(P) \geq \mu(\{a^\infty\}) = 1,$$
so $\sigma$ is **not** aperiodic — it is periodic (of period 1) on a set of full
 measure.

---

**Conclusion.** A Bernoulli measure on a symbolic space is aperiodic **if and on
ly if it is non-degenerate**, i.e., the underlying probability distribution on t
he alphabet assigns positive probability to at least two distinct symbols. In th
e standard (non-trivial) setting where the Bernoulli measure is non-degenerate,
**yes**, it is aperiodic: the set of periodic points is countable and the non-at
omic product measure assigns zero mass to every singleton, hence zero mass to th
e entire set of periodic points.
