# Where the Partition Condition on λ Is Used in the Proof that S^λ Is Irreducibl
e

## Answer

The fact that λ is a partition (weakly decreasing sequence) is used in the **Dom
inance Lemma**, the key technical result underlying the entire proof of irreduci
bility. Specifically, it is used in the counting argument that establishes the i
dentity

$$\sum_j \min(\lambda'_j,\, k) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{k} \lambda_i,$$

where λ′ = (λ′₁, λ′₂, …) is the conjugate partition. This identity holds **only
because λ is weakly decreasing**, and without it the dominance order λ ⊵ μ canno
t be established, causing the entire proof to collapse.

---

## The Dominance Lemma

**Statement.** Let t^λ be a tableau of shape λ and t^μ a tableau of shape μ. If
every column of t^λ intersects every row of t^μ in at most one element (the *com
patibility condition*), then λ ⊵ μ, i.e.,

$$\sum_{i=1}^{k} \lambda_i \;\geq\; \sum_{i=1}^{k} \mu_i \quad \text{for all } k
 \geq 1.$$

**Proof.** Fix k ≥ 1. The first k rows of t^μ contain the set S_k of size ∑ᵢ₌₁ᵏ
μᵢ. By the compatibility condition, each column of t^λ contributes at most k ele
ments to S_k (at most one per row). Column j of t^λ has λ′ⱼ elements, so it cont
ributes at most min(λ′ⱼ, k) elements. Therefore:

$$\sum_{i=1}^{k} \mu_i \;=\; |S_k| \;\leq\; \sum_{j} \min(\lambda'_j,\, k). \tag
{$*$}$$

**Here is where the partition condition is used.** Because λ is weakly decreasin
g, its conjugate λ′ is also weakly decreasing, and we have the identity:

$$\sum_{j} \min(\lambda'_j,\, k) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{k} \lambda_i. \tag{$**$}$$

This identity holds because:

$$\sum_{j} \min(\lambda'_j, k) \;=\; \sum_{j} \sum_{i=1}^{k} \mathbf{1}[\lambda'
_j \geq i] \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{k} \#\{j : \lambda'_j \geq i\} \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{k}
 \lambda_i.$$

The crucial step is **#{j : λ′ⱼ ≥ i} = λᵢ**: the number of columns of the λ-Youn
g diagram with at least i boxes equals λᵢ. This is true **precisely because λ is
 weakly decreasing**—it ensures that the columns with ≥ i boxes are exactly the
first λᵢ columns. Combining (∗) and (∗∗):

$$\sum_{i=1}^{k} \mu_i \;\leq\; \sum_{i=1}^{k} \lambda_i \quad \text{for all } k
, \qquad \text{i.e., } \lambda \unrhd \mu. \qquad \square$$

### Why the identity fails without the partition condition

If λ is not weakly decreasing, the number of columns with ≥ i boxes need not equ
al λᵢ. For example, if λ = (2, 3) (not a partition), then λ′ = (2, 2, 1), and th
e number of columns with ≥ 1 box is 3 ≠ 2 = λ₁. The identity (∗∗) breaks down, i
nequality (∗) no longer yields λ ⊵ μ, and the Dominance Lemma fails.

## How the Dominance Lemma Implies Irreducibility

The Dominance Lemma is the foundation for three results that together prove irre
ducibility:

### 1. Standard Basis Theorem
The standard polytabloids {e_t : t standard} form a ℂ-basis of S^λ. The proof us
es the **straightening algorithm**: any polytabloid e_t is expressed as a linear
 combination of standard polytabloids. The straightening works because the Domin
ance Lemma guarantees that non-standard tabloids appearing in e_t are dominated
by standard ones, so the replacement process strictly increases "standardness" a
nd terminates. Without the Dominance Lemma, we cannot control which tabloids app
ear, and the straightening algorithm has no foundation.

### 2. Nondegeneracy of the Bilinear Form
The bilinear form on M^λ (with λ-tabloids as orthonormal basis), restricted to S
^λ, is nondegenerate. This follows from the Standard Basis Theorem and the Domin
ance Lemma: ordering standard polytabloids by dominance, the Gram matrix ⟨e_s, e
_t⟩ is **triangular with nonzero diagonal entries** (the Dominance Lemma ensures
 that ⟨e_s, e_t⟩ = 0 when s is "more standard" than t in the dominance order, an
d ⟨e_t, e_t⟩ ≠ 0). A triangular matrix with nonzero diagonal is invertible, so t
he form is nondegenerate.

### 3. Submodule Theorem
If U is a ℂS_n-submodule of M^λ, then either U ⊇ S^λ or U ⊆ (S^λ)^⊥.

*Proof sketch.* Take nonzero u ∈ U, write u = ∑ c_{ {t} } {t} over λ-tabloids. C
hoose {t} maximal in the dominance order on tabloids with c_{ {t} } ≠ 0. Apply t
he column symmetrizer κ_t = ∑_{π ∈ C_t} sgn(π)·π to u. The Dominance Lemma (appl
ied to the tabloid dominance order) ensures that the leading term survives, givi
ng κ_t · u = c · e_t for some nonzero scalar c. Hence e_t ∈ U, and since U is S_
n-stable, S^λ = ℂS_n · e_t ⊆ U.

### Irreducibility of S^λ
Let U be a nonzero submodule of S^λ. Then U is a submodule of M^λ, so by the Sub
module Theorem, either U ⊇ S^λ (hence U = S^λ) or U ⊆ (S^λ)^⊥. In the latter cas
e, U ⊆ S^λ ∩ (S^λ)^⊥ = {0} by nondegeneracy, contradicting U ≠ 0. Therefore U =
S^λ, and **S^λ is irreducible**.

## Summary

The partition condition on λ (weakly decreasing) enters the proof at **exactly o
ne critical point**: the identity

$$\#\{j : \lambda'_j \geq i\} = \lambda_i,$$

used in the proof of the **Dominance Lemma**. This identity holds because weak d
ecrease of λ ensures that the columns of the Young diagram with at least i boxes
 are exactly the first λᵢ columns. The Dominance Lemma then propagates through t
he Standard Basis Theorem, the nondegeneracy of the bilinear form, and the Submo
dule Theorem to yield irreducibility. Without λ being a partition, the identity
fails, the Dominance Lemma fails, and none of the subsequent results hold.
