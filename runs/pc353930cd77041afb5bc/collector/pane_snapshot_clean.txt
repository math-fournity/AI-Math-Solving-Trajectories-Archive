## Product of Tempered Distributions with Disjoint Conic Singular Supports

### The Condition (Hörmander's Criterion)

The **wave front set** $WF(u) \subset T^*\mathbb{R}^n \setminus 0$ is a conic su
bset of the cotangent bundle (conic = closed under positive fiber scaling: $(x,\
xi) \in WF(u) \Rightarrow (x,t\xi) \in WF(u)$ for all $t>0$). It refines the sin
gular support: the projection of $WF(u)$ onto the base $\mathbb{R}^n$ is exactly
 $\operatorname{sing\,supp}(u)$.

The product $u_1 \cdot u_2$ of two distributions $u_1, u_2 \in \mathcal{S}'(\mat
hbb{R}^n)$ can be defined if and only if:

$$WF(u_1) \cap (-WF(u_2)) = \emptyset$$

where $-WF(u_2) = \{(x,-\xi) : (x,\xi) \in WF(u_2)\}$. Equivalently, there is no
 point $x$ and nonzero covectors $\xi_1,\xi_2$ with $(x,\xi_1)\in WF(u_1)$, $(x,
\xi_2)\in WF(u_2)$, and $\xi_1+\xi_2=0$.

This is the precise meaning of "conic singular supports being disjoint": the con
ic set $WF(u_1)$ and the reflected conic set $-WF(u_2)$ do not intersect in $T^*
\mathbb{R}^n\setminus 0$.

### The Construction (Regularization + Limit)

When the condition holds, the product is defined as follows:

1. **Choose a mollifier**: Pick $\rho \in \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ with $\int
\rho\,dx = 1$, and set $\rho_\epsilon(x) = \epsilon^{-n}\rho(x/\epsilon)$.

2. **Regularize one factor**: The convolution $u_2 * \rho_\epsilon$ is a smooth
function (a Schwartz function, since $u_2$ is tempered), so $u_1 \cdot (u_2 * \r
ho_\epsilon)$ is well-defined as the product of a distribution with a smooth fun
ction.

3. **Take the limit**:
$$u_1 \cdot u_2 := \lim_{\epsilon \to 0^+} u_1 \cdot (u_2 * \rho_\epsilon)$$
in the weak-* topology of $\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^n)$, i.e., for all $\varphi \
in \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^n)$:
$$\langle u_1 \cdot u_2,\, \varphi \rangle = \lim_{\epsilon\to 0^+} \langle u_1,
\, (u_2 * \rho_\epsilon)\cdot\varphi \rangle.$$

### Why the Limit Exists

At each point $x$, the frequency cones $\Sigma_x(u_1) = \{\xi : (x,\xi)\in WF(u_
1)\}$ and $-\Sigma_x(u_2)$ are disjoint closed cones in $\mathbb{R}^n\setminus\{
0\}$. One can construct a conic partition of unity in frequency space separating
 them, which ensures the regularized product converges. Microlocally, the "bad"
singular directions of $u_1$ and $u_2$ never interact in the product because the
y point in non-colliding (non-canceling) directions.

### Independence of Regularization

The limit is independent of the choice of $\rho$. Different mollifiers agree in
the limit because the wave front set condition prevents any dependence on the sp
ecific regularization in the singular directions — only the smooth parts, which
are regularization-independent, contribute to the interaction.

### Key Properties

- **Consistency**: If both $u_1, u_2$ are smooth functions, the product agrees w
ith the ordinary pointwise product.
- **Wave front set bound**: $WF(u_1\cdot u_2) \subset WF(u_1) \cup WF(u_2) \cup
(WF(u_1)\,\dot{+}\,WF(u_2))$, where $WF(u_1)\,\dot{+}\,WF(u_2) = \{(x,\xi_1+\xi_
2) : (x,\xi_i)\in WF(u_i)\}$.
- **Commutativity**: $u_1\cdot u_2 = u_2\cdot u_1$ (the condition is symmetric i
n $u_1, u_2$).
- **Support**: $\operatorname{supp}(u_1\cdot u_2) \subset \operatorname{supp}(u_
1)\cap\operatorname{supp}(u_2)$.
- **Temperedness**: $u_1, u_2 \in \mathcal{S}' \Rightarrow u_1\cdot u_2 \in \mat
hcal{S}'$.

### Special Case: Disjoint Singular Supports

If the ordinary singular supports are disjoint, $\operatorname{sing\,supp}(u_1)\
cap\operatorname{sing\,supp}(u_2)=\emptyset$, the Hörmander condition is automat
ically satisfied (since $WF(u_i)$ projects onto $\operatorname{sing\,supp}(u_i)$
, disjoint projections force disjoint wave front sets). The construction then si
mplifies to a **patching argument**: on the open set where $u_1$ is smooth, $u_1
\cdot u_2$ is the product of a smooth function with a distribution; on the open
set where $u_2$ is smooth, it is the product of a distribution with a smooth fun
ction; these two open sets cover $\mathbb{R}^n$ and the definitions agree on the
ir overlap (where both are smooth), so by the sheaf property of distributions th
ey patch to a well-defined global distribution.
