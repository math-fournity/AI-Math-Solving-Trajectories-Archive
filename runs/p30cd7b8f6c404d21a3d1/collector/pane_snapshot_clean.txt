## Proof

**Claim.** Let $(X, \mathcal{F}, \mu)$ be a measure space, $A \in \mathcal{F}$,
and $f : A \to (0,\infty)$ a measurable function that is *strictly* positive on
$A$ (i.e. $f(x) > 0$ for every $x \in A$). If
$$\int_A f\,d\mu = 0,$$
then $\mu(A) = 0$.

**Proof.** For each $n \in \mathbb{N}$, define
$$A_n := \{x \in A : f(x) \ge 1/n\}.$$
Each $A_n$ is measurable (it is the preimage of $[1/n,\infty)$ under the measura
ble function $f$). Since $f$ is strictly positive on $A$, every $x \in A$ has $f
(x) > 0$, so there exists $n$ with $f(x) \ge 1/n$; hence
$$A = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n.$$

By the Markov (Chebyshev) inequality applied to the non-negative measurable func
tion $f$,
$$\mu(A_n) \;=\; \mu(\{x \in A : f(x) \ge 1/n\}) \;\le\; n\int_A f\,d\mu \;=\; n
 \cdot 0 \;=\; 0.$$
Thus $\mu(A_n) = 0$ for every $n$.

By countable subadditivity,
$$\mu(A) \;=\; \mu\!\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n\right) \;\le\; \sum_{n=1}^{
\infty} \mu(A_n) \;=\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} 0 \;=\; 0.$$

Since $\mu$ is non-negative, $\mu(A) \ge 0$, and we conclude
$$\mu(A) = 0. \qquad \blacksquare$$

**Remark on necessity of strict positivity.** The hypothesis "$f > 0$ on $A$" is
 essential. If $f$ is only *non-negative* (allowed to vanish), the conclusion fa
ils: e.g. $f \equiv 0$ on any set $A$ gives $\int_A f\,d\mu = 0$ regardless of $
\mu(A)$. The strict-positivity assumption is precisely what forces the level set
s $A_n$ to exhaust $A$, making the argument work.

**Answer:** Yes. If a strictly positive measurable function has Lebesgue integra
l $0$ over $A$, then $\mu(A) = 0$.
