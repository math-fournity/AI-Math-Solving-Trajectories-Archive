## Proof

**Yes**, $W$ can contain a nonzero finitely-supported sequence. We construct an
explicit example.

### Setup

Let $c_{00} \subset \ell^2$ denote the subspace of finitely-supported sequences.
 For $j \geq 0$, define $a^{(j)} \in \ell^2$ by
$$a^{(j)}_k = 2^{-k}\, k^j, \quad k \geq 1.$$
Each $a^{(j)} \in \ell^2$ since $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} 4^{-k} k^{2j} < \infty$ (ex
ponential decay dominates polynomial growth), and each $a^{(j)}$ has infinite su
pport (all entries positive).

### Claim 1: The images $[a^{(0)}], [a^{(1)}], [a^{(2)}], \ldots$ are linearly i
ndependent in $\ell^2/c_{00}$.

Suppose $\sum_{j=0}^{J} c_j\, a^{(j)} \in c_{00}$. Then for all sufficiently lar
ge $k$,
$$\sum_{j=0}^{J} c_j\, 2^{-k} k^j = 0 \implies p(k) = 0, \quad p(x) := \sum_{j=0
}^{J} c_j\, x^j.$$
The polynomial $p$ vanishes at infinitely many points, so $p \equiv 0$, giving $
c_0 = \cdots = c_J = 0$. $\square$

### Construction of $V$

Set $\epsilon_n = \frac{1}{n\,\|a^{(n-1)}\|} > 0$ and $w_n = \epsilon_n\, a^{(n-
1)}$ for $n \geq 1$. Then:
- $\|w_n\| = 1/n \to 0$,
- The images $[w_n] = \epsilon_n\, [a^{(n-1)}]$ are linearly independent in $\el
l^2/c_{00}$ (Claim 1, since $\epsilon_n \neq 0$),
- Each $w_n$ has infinite support.

Define $v_n = e_1 + w_n$ and $V = \operatorname{span}\{v_1, v_2, v_3, \ldots\}$.

### Claim 2: $V$ is infinite-dimensional.

If $\sum_{n=1}^{N} c_n\, v_n = 0$, then $(\sum c_n)\, e_1 + \sum c_n\, w_n = 0$,
 so $\sum c_n\, w_n = -(\sum c_n)\, e_1 \in c_{00}$. By linear independence of $
[w_n]$ in $\ell^2/c_{00}$, all $c_n = 0$. Thus the $v_n$ are linearly independen
t. $\square$

### Claim 3: $V \cap c_{00} = \{0\}$ (no nonzero finitely-supported sequence lie
s in $V$).

If $\sum_{n=1}^{N} c_n\, v_n \in c_{00}$, then $(\sum c_n)\, e_1 + \sum c_n\, w_
n \in c_{00}$. Since $(\sum c_n)\, e_1 \in c_{00}$, we get $\sum c_n\, w_n \in c
_{00}$, i.e., $\sum c_n\, [w_n] = 0$ in $\ell^2/c_{00}$. By linear independence,
 all $c_n = 0$. $\square$

### Claim 4: $e_1 \in \overline{V} = W$.

Since $v_n = e_1 + w_n$ and $\|v_n - e_1\| = \|w_n\| = 1/n \to 0$, we have $v_n
\to e_1$ in $\ell^2$. As $v_n \in V$, this gives $e_1 \in \overline{V} = W$. $\s
quare$

### Conclusion

The vector $e_1 = (1, 0, 0, \ldots)$ is a nonzero sequence with exactly one nonz
ero element, and it lies in $W = \overline{V}$. Meanwhile, $V$ itself is an infi
nite-dimensional subspace of $\ell^2$ containing no nonzero finitely-supported s
equence (Claim 3).

Therefore, **yes**, $W$ can contain a sequence with only finitely many nonzero e
lements.
