## Solution

We evaluate $L = \lim_{y \to +\infty} y \int_0^{+\infty} e^{-x^2} \sin(2xy)\,dx$
.

**Step 1: Integration by parts.** Set
$$I(y) = \int_0^{+\infty} e^{-x^2} \sin(2xy)\,dx.$$
Choose $u = e^{-x^2}$, $dv = \sin(2xy)\,dx$, so $du = -2x\,e^{-x^2}\,dx$ and $v
= -\dfrac{\cos(2xy)}{2y}$. Then
$$I(y) = \left[-\frac{e^{-x^2}\cos(2xy)}{2y}\right]_{0}^{+\infty} - \int_0^{+\in
fty}(-2x\,e^{-x^2})\!\left(-\frac{\cos(2xy)}{2y}\right)dx.$$

**Boundary terms.** As $x\to+\infty$, $e^{-x^2}\to 0$ (and $|\cos|\le 1$), so th
e upper limit contributes $0$. At $x=0$:
$$-\frac{e^{0}\cos 0}{2y} = -\frac{1}{2y}.$$
Hence
$$\left[-\frac{e^{-x^2}\cos(2xy)}{2y}\right]_{0}^{+\infty} = 0 - \left(-\frac{1}
{2y}\right) = \frac{1}{2y}.$$

The remaining integral is
$$-\int_0^{+\infty} \frac{2x\,e^{-x^2}\cos(2xy)}{2y}\,dx = -\frac{1}{y}\int_0^{+
\infty} x\,e^{-x^2}\cos(2xy)\,dx.$$

Therefore
$$I(y) = \frac{1}{2y} - \frac{1}{y}\int_0^{+\infty} x\,e^{-x^2}\cos(2xy)\,dx,$$
and multiplying by $y$:
$$y\,I(y) = \frac{1}{2} - \int_0^{+\infty} x\,e^{-x^2}\cos(2xy)\,dx.$$

**Step 2: Apply the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma.** The function $f(x) = x\,e^{-x^2}$
on $[0,+\infty)$ is integrable, since
$$\int_0^{+\infty} x\,e^{-x^2}\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{+\infty} e^{-u}\,du = \f
rac{1}{2} < \infty.$$
Since $f \in L^1([0,+\infty))$, the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma gives
$$\int_0^{+\infty} x\,e^{-x^2}\cos(2xy)\,dx \xrightarrow{y\to+\infty} 0.$$

**Step 3: Conclude.**
$$\lim_{y\to+\infty} y\,I(y) = \frac{1}{2} - 0 = \frac{1}{2}.$$

$$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}}$$
