## Step 1: Compute the Elite product

All of 7, 11, 13, 17, 101, 9901 are prime, so K' must be divisible by their prod

$$7 \times 11 = 77,\quad 77 \times 13 = 1001,\quad 1001 \times 17 = 17017$$
$$17017 \times 101 = 1718717,\quad 1718717 \times 9901 = 17017017017$$

This is itself an 11-digit number. The 11-digit multiples are:

## Step 2: Check which multisets allow a Standard code

A Standard code needs: last digit 0 (divisible by 2 and 5) and digit sum divisib
le by 3.

| Multiset | Digit sum | Sum mod 3 | Has 0? |
|----------|-----------|-----------|--------|
| {0:3, 1:4, 7:4} | 32 | 2 | Yes |
| {0:3, 3:4, 4:4} | 28 | 1 | Yes |
| {0:3, 1:4, 5:4} | 24 | **0** | Yes |
| {0:3, 6:4, 8:4} | 56 | 2 | Yes |
| {0:3, 5:4, 8:4} | 52 | 1 | Yes |

Only **{0:3, 1:4, 5:4}** has digit sum divisible by 3.

## Step 3: Count Standard codes with multiset {0:3, 1:4, 5:4}

Since the digit sum is always 24 (divisible by 3), the Standard condition reduce
s to:
- **Last digit = 0** (for divisibility by 2 and 5)
- **First digit ≠ 0** (code requirement)

Fix the last digit as 0. The remaining 10 positions (1–10) receive the multiset
{0:2, 1:4, 5:4}.

**Total arrangements** of these 10 digits:
$$\frac{10!}{2!\cdot 4!\cdot 4!} = \frac{3628800}{2 \times 24 \times 24} = \frac
{3628800}{1152} = 3150$$

**Arrangements with 0 in first position** (place one 0 at position 1, remaining
9 digits are {0:1, 1:4, 5:4}):
$$\frac{9!}{1!\cdot 4!\cdot 4!} = \frac{362880}{576} = 630$$

**Valid codes:**
$$3150 - 630 = \boxed{2520}$$
