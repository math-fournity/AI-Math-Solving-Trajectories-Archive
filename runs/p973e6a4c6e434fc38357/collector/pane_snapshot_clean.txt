## Proof

The orthogonal group is
$$\mathrm{O}_n = \{A \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{R}) : A^t A = I\}.$$
Its Lie algebra $\mathfrak{o}_n$ is the tangent space at the identity:
$$\mathfrak{o}_n = \{X \in M_n(\mathbb{R}) : \exists\, \gamma:(-\varepsilon,\var
epsilon)\to \mathrm{O}_n,\ \gamma(0)=I,\ \gamma'(0)=X\}.$$

**Step 1 (necessity: every element of the Lie algebra is skew-symmetric).**

Let $\gamma(t)$ be a smooth curve in $\mathrm{O}_n$ with $\gamma(0)=I$, and set
$X=\gamma'(0)$. By definition of $\mathrm{O}_n$,
$$\gamma(t)^t\,\gamma(t)=I \quad\text{for all }t.$$
Differentiating both sides at $t=0$ (using the product rule and $\frac{d}{dt}\ga
mma(t)^t = \gamma'(t)^t$):
$$\gamma'(0)^t\,\gamma(0) + \gamma(0)^t\,\gamma'(0) = 0.$$
Since $\gamma(0)=I$, this becomes
$$X^t + X = 0,$$
i.e. $X^t = -X$. Hence every $X\in\mathfrak{o}_n$ is skew-symmetric.

**Step 2 (sufficiency: every skew-symmetric matrix lies in the Lie algebra).**

Let $X\in M_n(\mathbb{R})$ be skew-symmetric, $X^t=-X$. Consider the matrix expo
nential
$$\gamma(t) = \exp(tX).$$
Using the property $(\exp M)^t = \exp(M^t)$ and the fact that $X$ and $-X$ commu
te (trivially, since $-X$ is a scalar multiple of $X$),
$$\gamma(t)^t\,\gamma(t) = \exp(tX)^t\,\exp(tX) = \exp(tX^t)\,\exp(tX) = \exp(-t
X)\,\exp(tX) = \exp(0) = I.$$
So $\gamma(t)\in\mathrm{O}_n$ for all $t$. Moreover $\gamma(0)=\exp(0)=I$ and
$$\gamma'(0) = \left.\frac{d}{dt}\exp(tX)\right|_{t=0} = X.$$
Thus $X\in\mathfrak{o}_n$.

**Conclusion.**

Combining both inclusions,
$$\boxed{\mathfrak{o}_n = \mathfrak{so}_n = \{X\in M_n(\mathbb{R}) : X^t = -X\},
}$$
the Lie algebra of $n\times n$ real skew-symmetric matrices. Its dimension is $\
binom{n}{2}=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$, since the entries are free above the diagonal and
 determined by skew-symmetry below it, with zeros on the diagonal.
