## Solution

**Setup.** Let $P(x)=a\prod_{i=1}^{5}(x-r_i)$ where each root satisfies $|r_i|=1
$. Since the probe's power output is a real-valued physical quantity, $P$ has re
al coefficients. With real coefficients and degree $5$, complex roots appear in
conjugate pairs, so there is an odd number of real roots. The only real numbers
on the unit circle are $\pm1$; and $r_i=1$ is impossible since $P(1)=3\neq0$. He
nce every real root equals $-1$.

Let $m\in\{1,3,5\}$ be the number of roots equal to $-1$, and let the remaining
roots be conjugate pairs $e^{\pm i\alpha_j}$, $j=1,\dots,p$, with $m+2p=5$.

**Determining $a$.** The product of all roots is $S=(-1)^m\prod_j e^{i\alpha_j}e
^{-i\alpha_j}=(-1)^m$. From $P(0)=-aS=2$:
$$a=-\frac{2}{S}=\frac{2}{(-1)^{m+1}}.$$
Since $m$ is odd, $(-1)^{m+1}=1$, so $a=2$.

**The constraint from $P(1)=3$.**
$$P(1)=2\cdot 2^m\prod_{j=1}^{p}(2-2\cos\alpha_j)=3 \implies 2^{m+1}\prod_j(2-2\
cos\alpha_j)=3.$$

**Evaluating $P(2)$.** Using $|2-e^{i\theta}|^2=5-4\cos\theta=1+2(2-2\cos\theta)
$ and $2-(-1)=3$:
$$P(2)=2\cdot 3^m\prod_{j=1}^{p}(5-4\cos\alpha_j)>0.$$
So $P(2)$ is always positive; we minimize it.

**Case $m=5$:** $\prod r_i=(-1)^5=-1$, all roots $-1$; then $P(1)=2\cdot 2^5=64\
neq3$. Infeasible.

**Case $m=3$:** $p=1$. The constraint gives $2^4(2-2\cos\alpha)=3$, so $2-2\cos\
alpha=3/16$. Then
$$P(2)=2\cdot 27\cdot(5-4\cos\alpha)=54\Bigl(1+2\cdot\tfrac{3}{16}\Bigr)=54\cdot
\frac{11}{8}=\frac{297}{4}=74.25.$$

**Case $m=1$:** $p=2$. Set $A=2-2\cos\alpha$, $B=2-2\cos\beta$, with $A,B\in(0,4
)$. The constraint is $2^2 AB=3$, i.e. $AB=\tfrac34$. Then
$$P(2)=2\cdot 3\cdot(1+2A)(1+2B)=6\bigl(1+2(A+B)+4AB\bigr)=6\bigl(4+2(A+B)\bigr)
=24+12(A+B).$$
To minimize $A+B$ subject to $AB=\tfrac34$ with $A,B\in(0,4)$: by AM–GM,
$$A+B\ge 2\sqrt{AB}=2\sqrt{\tfrac34}=\sqrt{3},$$
with equality at $A=B=\sqrt{3}/2\in(0,4)$ (valid, since $\cos\alpha=1-\sqrt3/4\i
n(-1,1)$). Hence
$$P(2)_{\min}=24+12\sqrt3.$$

**Comparison.** The $m=1$ case yields $24+12\sqrt3\approx44.78$, smaller than th
e $m=3$ value $\tfrac{297}{4}=74.25$.

**Achievability.** Take roots $\{-1,\,e^{\pm i\alpha},\,e^{\pm i\beta}\}$ with $
\cos\alpha=\cos\beta=1-\sqrt3/4$, and
$$P(x)=2(x+1)(x^2-2\cos\alpha\,x+1)(x^2-2\cos\beta\,x+1).$$
Then $P(0)=2$, $P(1)=3$, all roots lie on $|z|=1$, and $P(2)=24+12\sqrt3$.

**Answer.** The smallest possible value is $\boxed{24+12\sqrt3}$.
