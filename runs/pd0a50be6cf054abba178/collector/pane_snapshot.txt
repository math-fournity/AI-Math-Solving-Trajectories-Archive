## Answer

The statement is **false**.

### Analysis

Recall that an $R$-module $M$ is **simple** if $M \neq 0$ and $M$ has no proper
nonzero submodules.

**Forward direction (simple $\Rightarrow$ $M \cong R/\mathfrak{p}$ for some prim
e $\mathfrak{p}$): TRUE.**

If $M$ is simple, pick any $0 \neq m \in M$. Then $M = Rm$, so $M$ is cyclic and
 $M \cong R/\operatorname{Ann}(m)$. Since $M$ is simple, $\operatorname{Ann}(m)
= \operatorname{Ann}(M)$, and the submodules of $R/\operatorname{Ann}(M)$ corres
pond bijectively to ideals of $R$ containing $\operatorname{Ann}(M)$. Simplicity
 forces $\operatorname{Ann}(M)$ to be a **maximal** ideal. Since every maximal i
deal is prime, we get $M \cong R/\mathfrak{m}$ for a maximal (hence prime) ideal
 $\mathfrak{m}$.

**Backward direction ($M \cong R/\mathfrak{p}$ for prime $\mathfrak{p}$ $\Righta
rrow$ $M$ simple): FALSE.**

The submodule lattice of $R/\mathfrak{p}$ is isomorphic to the lattice of ideals
 of $R$ containing $\mathfrak{p}$. Thus $R/\mathfrak{p}$ is simple **if and only
 if $\mathfrak{p}$ is maximal**, not merely prime. A prime ideal that is not max
imal yields a non-simple quotient.

### Counterexample

Take $R = \mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathfrak{p} = (0)$, which is a prime ideal. Then

$$R/\mathfrak{p} \cong \mathbb{Z}.$$

But $\mathbb{Z}$ is **not** a simple $\mathbb{Z}$-module: it has proper nonzero
submodules such as $2\mathbb{Z} \subsetneq \mathbb{Z}$.

(More generally, for $R = k[x,y]$ and $\mathfrak{p} = (x)$, the quotient $R/\mat
hfrak{p} \cong k[y]$ has the proper nonzero submodule $(y)/(x) \cong k$, corresp
onding to the ideal $(x,y) \supsetneq (x)$, so it is not simple.)

### Conclusion

The correct characterization is: **$M$ is simple if and only if $M \cong R/\math
frak{m}$ for some *maximal* ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ of $R$.** Replacing "maximal" w
ith "prime" makes the converse fail, so the given statement is **false**.
