## Solution

**The statement is false in general.** The hypotheses impose no relationship bet
ween $m_A$ and $m_B$ (in particular, $m_B$ is not assumed to be the pushforward
$f_*m_A$), so $m_B$ may be chosen independently of $m_A$ and $f$, making the con
clusion fail.

### Counterexample

Let
- $A = B = [0,1]$, both compact metric spaces (hence second‑countable, so the ex
tra hypothesis is satisfied automatically),
- $f:A\to B$ be the identity map $f(x)=x$, which is a continuous surjection,
- $m_A$ be Lebesgue measure on $[0,1]$,
- $m_B = \delta_0$, the Dirac probability measure concentrated at $0$,
- $K = [0,1]\setminus\{0\}$, a Borel subset of $A$.

**Verification.**

1. **$K$ is Borel.** $\{0\}$ is closed, so $K = [0,1]\setminus\{0\}$ is open in
$[0,1]$, hence Borel.

2. **$m_A(K)=1$.** Lebesgue measure of a singleton is $0$, so
$$m_A(K) = m_A([0,1]) - m_A(\{0\}) = 1 - 0 = 1.$$

3. **$f(K)=K$.** Since $f$ is the identity, $f(K)=K=[0,1]\setminus\{0\}$.

4. **$m_B(f(K))=0\neq 1$.** The Dirac measure $\delta_0$ assigns mass $1$ to set
s containing $0$ and mass $0$ to sets not containing $0$. Since $0\notin [0,1]\s
etminus\{0\}=f(K)$,
$$m_B(f(K)) = \delta_0\big([0,1]\setminus\{0\}\big) = 0.$$

Thus $m_A(K)=1$ but $m_B(f(K))=0$, contradicting the claimed conclusion.

### Remark on what *would* make it true

The conclusion becomes true under the additional hypothesis that $m_B$ is the pu
shforward of $m_A$ along $f$, i.e. $m_B = f_*m_A$ (meaning $m_B(E)=m_A(f^{-1}(E)
)$ for every Borel $E\subseteq B$). Indeed, since $K\subseteq f^{-1}(f(K))$,
$$m_B(f(K)) = m_A\big(f^{-1}(f(K))\big) \ge m_A(K) = 1,$$
and as a probability measure $m_B(f(K))\le 1$, giving equality. (Note this also
requires $f(K)$ to be $m_B$‑measurable; for continuous $f$ on a second‑countable
 space, $f(K)$ is analytic and hence universally measurable, so the pushforward
is well defined on it.)

However, **as the problem is stated** — with $m_A$ and $m_B$ arbitrary Borel pro
bability measures and no pushforward relation — the implication does **not** hol
d, as the counterexample above shows. The second‑countability assumption on $A$
(which is in fact automatic for compact metric spaces) does not rescue the claim
, since the counterexample already lives in a second‑countable (indeed compact m
etric) setting.
