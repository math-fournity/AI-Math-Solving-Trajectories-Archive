## Proof

Define the power tower of $n$ copies of base $b$:
$$T_b(n) = \underbrace{b^{b^{\cdot^{\cdot^{b}}}}}_{n \text{ copies of } b}$$

So we compare $T_2(100)$ (tower of 100 twos) against $T_3(99)$ (tower of 99 thre
es).

**Claim.** $T_3(n) > T_2(n+1)$ for all $n \ge 2$.

Setting $n = 99$ gives $T_3(99) > T_2(100)$, i.e., the tower of 99 threes is lar
ger.

**Proof by induction on $n$.**

*Base case* ($n = 2$):
$$T_3(2) = 3^3 = 27 > 16 = 2^4 = T_2(3). \checkmark$$

*Inductive step.* Assume $T_3(n) > T_2(n+1)$ for some $n \ge 2$. We show $T_3(n+
1) > T_2(n+2)$.

Using the recursive definitions $T_3(n+1) = 3^{T_3(n)}$ and $T_2(n+2) = 2^{T_2(n
+1)}$, it suffices to prove
$$3^{T_3(n)} > 2^{T_2(n+1)}.$$

Taking $\log_2$ of both sides (a strictly increasing function), this is equivale
nt to
$$T_3(n) \cdot \log_2 3 > T_2(n+1).$$

By the induction hypothesis, $T_3(n) > T_2(n+1)$. Since $\log_2 3 \approx 1.585
> 1$,
$$T_3(n) \cdot \log_2 3 > T_3(n) \cdot 1 = T_3(n) > T_2(n+1). \checkmark$$

This completes the induction. $\blacksquare$

**Conclusion.** With $n = 99 \ge 2$, we have
$$T_3(99) > T_2(100).$$

The power tower of 99 threes is larger than the power tower of 100 twos.
