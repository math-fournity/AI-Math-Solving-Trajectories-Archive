# Proof

**Claim:** Yes. The number of roots of $p(z)$ in the open unit disk $|z|<1$ (cou
nting multiplicities) equals the topological degree of $\widehat{p}: S^1 \to S^1
$, $\widehat{p}(z) = p(z)/|p(z)|$.

---

## Setup

Let $p(z)$ be a polynomial with no zeros on the unit circle $S^1 = \{z : |z|=1\}
$. Parametrize $S^1$ by $z = e^{it}$, $t \in [0, 2\pi]$. Since $p$ has no zeros
on $S^1$, the map $\widehat{p}(e^{it}) = p(e^{it})/|p(e^{it})|$ is a smooth map
$S^1 \to S^1$.

## Step 1: The degree of $\widehat{p}$ is the winding number of $p(S^1)$ around
the origin

The topological degree of a smooth map $f: S^1 \to S^1$ is

$$\deg(f) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} \frac{d}{dt}\arg\!\big(f(e^{it})\big)\,d
t,$$

i.e., the total change in argument of $f(e^{it})$ over one period, divided by $2
\pi$. For $f = \widehat{p}$, the argument of $\widehat{p}(e^{it})$ equals the ar
gument of $p(e^{it})$ (since $|p(e^{it})| > 0$ is real and positive, it contribu
tes nothing to the argument). Therefore

$$\deg(\widehat{p}) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} \frac{d}{dt}\arg\!\big(p(e^{it
})\big)\,dt = W\big(p(S^1),\, 0\big),$$

the winding number of the closed curve $t \mapsto p(e^{it})$ around the origin.

## Step 2: The winding number equals a contour integral

The winding number of $p(S^1)$ around $0$ is

$$W\big(p(S^1),\, 0\big) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{p(S^1)} \frac{dw}{w}.$$

Substituting $w = p(z)$, $dw = p'(z)\,dz$:

$$W\big(p(S^1),\, 0\big) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=1} \frac{p'(z)}{p(z)}\,dz.
$$

## Step 3: Apply the argument principle

The **argument principle** states: if $f$ is meromorphic inside and on a simple
closed contour $\gamma$, with no zeros or poles on $\gamma$, then

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma \frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}\,dz = N(\gamma, f) - P(\gamma,
 f),$$

where $N$ and $P$ are the counts of zeros and poles inside $\gamma$, each counte
d with multiplicity.

*Justification:* $f'/f$ has a simple pole at each zero $z_0$ of $f$ with residue
 equal to the multiplicity of $z_0$, and a simple pole at each pole of $f$ with
residue equal to minus the order. The residue theorem then gives the formula.

Applying this to $f = p$ (a polynomial, hence entire, so $P = 0$) and $\gamma =
S^1$ (the unit circle, on which $p$ has no zeros by hypothesis):

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=1} \frac{p'(z)}{p(z)}\,dz = N - 0 = N,$$

where $N$ is the number of roots of $p$ in the open disk $|z| < 1$, counted with
 multiplicity.

## Step 4: Conclusion

Combining the three steps:

$$\deg(\widehat{p}) \;=\; W\big(p(S^1),\, 0\big) \;=\; \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z
|=1} \frac{p'(z)}{p(z)}\,dz \;=\; N.$$

Therefore, **the number of roots of $p$ in the unit disk $|z|<1$ (counting multi
plicities) necessarily equals the degree of $\widehat{p}: S^1 \to S^1$**, provid
ed $p$ has no roots on $S^1$.

The answer is **yes**.
