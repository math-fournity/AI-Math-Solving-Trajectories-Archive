# Problem

In a massive logistics warehouse, a floor plan is divided into a perfect grid of $2016 \times 2016$ square storage bays. The management team wants to install laser-based security barriers to outline the perimeters of specific rectangular zones.

The security system works by projecting the boundary of a rectangle (of any size, provided its edges align with the grid lines of the storage bays). For a bay to be considered "fully secured," every one of its four individual boundary segments must be covered by the perimeter of at least one of the projected rectangles.

What is the smallest number of rectangles $M$ that the management must project so that every single $1 \times 1$ storage bay in the $2016 \times 2016$ grid has all four of its sides included in the set of the rectangles' boundaries?
