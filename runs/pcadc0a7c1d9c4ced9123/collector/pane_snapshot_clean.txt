# Fourier–Bessel Series Expansions

## Preliminaries: Orthogonality and Fourier–Bessel Coefficients

The functions $\{J_0(\lambda_j x)\}_{j=1}^\infty$, where $\lambda_j$ are the pos
itive zeros of $J_0$, form an orthogonal system on $(0,1)$ with weight $x$:

$$\int_0^1 x\, J_0(\lambda_j x)\, J_0(\lambda_m x)\, dx = \frac{1}{2}\big[J_1(\l
ambda_j)\big]^2\,\delta_{jm}.$$

This follows from Sturm–Liouville theory applied to Bessel's equation of order z
ero. Consequently, any sufficiently regular $f$ admits the expansion

$$f(x) = \sum_{j=1}^\infty c_j\, J_0(\lambda_j x), \qquad c_j = \frac{2}{\big[J_
1(\lambda_j)\big]^2}\int_0^1 x\, f(x)\, J_0(\lambda_j x)\, dx.$$

We will also need the **Lommel integral identity**. For $a \neq b$ and integer $
n \ge 0$:

$$\int_0^1 x\, J_n(ax)\, J_n(bx)\, dx = \frac{x\big[a\, J_{n+1}(ax)\, J_n(bx) -
b\, J_n(ax)\, J_{n+1}(bx)\big]}{a^2 - b^2}\Bigg|_0^1.$$

**Derivation.** Let $u = J_n(ax)$, $v = J_n(bx)$. Each satisfies $y'' + \frac{1}
{x}y' + (a^2 - n^2/x^2)y = 0$ (with $a$ or $b$). Multiplying the equation for $u
$ by $v$ and for $v$ by $u$, subtracting, and using $J_n'(z) = \frac{n}{z}J_n(z)
 - J_{n+1}(z)$ gives

$$\frac{d}{dx}\big[x(uv' - vu')\big] = (b^2 - a^2)\,x\,uv.$$

Integrating and substituting the recurrence $J_n'(z) = \frac{n}{z}J_n(z) - J_{n+
1}(z)$ yields the stated formula. $\square$

## Part (a): $f(x) = x^2$

We need $c_j = \frac{2}{[J_1(\lambda_j)]^2}\int_0^1 x^3\, J_0(\lambda_j x)\, dx$
.

**Step 1.** Use $x\,J_0(\lambda x) = \frac{1}{\lambda}\frac{d}{dx}\big[x\,J_1(\l
ambda x)\big]$ (a standard identity from $\frac{d}{dz}[z\,J_1(z)] = z\,J_0(z)$).
 Integration by parts:

$$\int_0^1 x^3 J_0(\lambda x)\,dx = \frac{1}{\lambda}\int_0^1 x^2\,\frac{d}{dx}\
big[x\,J_1(\lambda x)\big]\,dx = \frac{1}{\lambda}\Big[x^3 J_1(\lambda x)\Big]_0
^1 - \frac{2}{\lambda}\int_0^1 x^2 J_1(\lambda x)\,dx.$$

**Step 2.** Evaluate $\int_0^1 x^2 J_1(\lambda x)\,dx$ using $\int x^{n+1}J_n(ax
)\,dx = \frac{x^{n+1}J_{n+1}(ax)}{a}$ with $n=1$:

$$\int_0^1 x^2 J_1(\lambda x)\,dx = \frac{J_2(\lambda)}{\lambda}.$$

**Step 3.** Combine:

$$\int_0^1 x^3 J_0(\lambda x)\,dx = \frac{J_1(\lambda)}{\lambda} - \frac{2\,J_2(
\lambda)}{\lambda^2}.$$

**Step 4.** Use the recurrence $J_2(\lambda) = \frac{2}{\lambda}J_1(\lambda) - J
_0(\lambda)$. Since $\lambda = \lambda_j$ is a zero of $J_0$, we have $J_2(\lamb
da_j) = \frac{2}{\lambda_j}J_1(\lambda_j)$, so

$$\int_0^1 x^3 J_0(\lambda_j x)\,dx = \frac{J_1(\lambda_j)}{\lambda_j} - \frac{2
}{\lambda_j^2}\cdot\frac{2\,J_1(\lambda_j)}{\lambda_j} = \frac{J_1(\lambda_j)}{\
lambda_j} - \frac{4\,J_1(\lambda_j)}{\lambda_j^3} = \frac{(\lambda_j^2 - 4)\,J_1
(\lambda_j)}{\lambda_j^3}.$$

**Step 5.** The coefficient is

$$c_j = \frac{2}{[J_1(\lambda_j)]^2}\cdot\frac{(\lambda_j^2-4)\,J_1(\lambda_j)}{
\lambda_j^3} = \frac{2(\lambda_j^2 - 4)}{\lambda_j^3\, J_1(\lambda_j)}.$$

Therefore, for $0 < x < 1$:

$$\boxed{x^2 = 2\sum_{j=1}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda_j^2 - 4}{\lambda_j^3\, J_1(\la
mbda_j)}\, J_0(\lambda_j x).}$$

## Part (b): $f(x) = J_0(kx)$, $k \neq \lambda_j$

We need $c_j = \frac{2}{[J_1(\lambda_j)]^2}\int_0^1 x\, J_0(kx)\, J_0(\lambda_j
x)\, dx$.

**Step 1.** Apply the Lommel identity with $n=0$, $a=k$, $b=\lambda_j$:

$$\int_0^1 x\, J_0(kx)\, J_0(\lambda_j x)\, dx = \frac{x\big[k\, J_1(kx)\, J_0(\
lambda_j x) - \lambda_j\, J_0(kx)\, J_1(\lambda_j x)\big]}{k^2 - \lambda_j^2}\Bi
gg|_0^1.$$

**Step 2.** Evaluate at the bounds. At $x = 0$: the factor $x$ makes the express
ion $0$. At $x = 1$:

$$\frac{k\, J_1(k)\, J_0(\lambda_j) - \lambda_j\, J_0(k)\, J_1(\lambda_j)}{k^2 -
 \lambda_j^2}.$$

Since $J_0(\lambda_j) = 0$, the first term vanishes:

$$= \frac{-\lambda_j\, J_0(k)\, J_1(\lambda_j)}{k^2 - \lambda_j^2} = \frac{\lamb
da_j\, J_0(k)\, J_1(\lambda_j)}{\lambda_j^2 - k^2}.$$

**Step 3.** The coefficient is

$$c_j = \frac{2}{[J_1(\lambda_j)]^2}\cdot\frac{\lambda_j\, J_0(k)\, J_1(\lambda_
j)}{\lambda_j^2 - k^2} = \frac{2\,\lambda_j\, J_0(k)}{(\lambda_j^2 - k^2)\, J_1(
\lambda_j)}.$$

**Step 4.** Therefore, for $0 < x < 1$ and $k \neq \lambda_j$:

$$\boxed{J_0(kx) = 2\, J_0(k)\sum_{j=1}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda_j\, J_0(\lambda_j
 x)}{(\lambda_j^2 - k^2)\, J_1(\lambda_j)}.}$$

## Summary

Both representations follow from the **Fourier–Bessel expansion** $f(x) = \sum_j
 c_j J_0(\lambda_j x)$ with coefficients $c_j = \frac{2}{[J_1(\lambda_j)]^2}\int
_0^1 x f(x) J_0(\lambda_j x)\,dx$.

- **(a)** For $f(x)=x^2$, the integral $\int_0^1 x^3 J_0(\lambda_j x)\,dx$ is re
duced via integration by parts and the recurrence $J_2 = \frac{2}{\lambda}J_1 -
J_0$ (with $J_0(\lambda_j)=0$) to $\frac{(\lambda_j^2-4)J_1(\lambda_j)}{\lambda_
j^3}$, giving the stated series.

- **(b)** For $f(x)=J_0(kx)$, the Lommel integral identity evaluates $\int_0^1 x
 J_0(kx)J_0(\lambda_j x)\,dx = \frac{\lambda_j J_0(k) J_1(\lambda_j)}{\lambda_j^
2 - k^2}$ (using $J_0(\lambda_j)=0$), giving the stated series.
