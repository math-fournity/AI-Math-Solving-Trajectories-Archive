**Answer:** All $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

**Proof.**

We show that for every $n \geq 1$, such matrices exist.

**Construction.** Let $A$ be the $n \times n$ nilpotent Jordan block with 1's on
 the superdiagonal:
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots
 & & \ddots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 &
0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B = E_{11} = e_1 e_1^T.$$

**Rank condition.** Since $A^{n-1} \neq 0$ and $A^n = 0$, we have $\operatorname
{rank} A = n - 1$. Clearly $\operatorname{rank} B = 1$. Thus
$$\operatorname{rank} A + \operatorname{rank} B = (n-1) + 1 = n \leq n. \quad \c
heckmark$$

**Centralizer condition.** Since $A$ is a single Jordan block, it is cyclic (wit
h cyclic vector $e_n$), so its centralizer is the polynomial algebra:
$$\{X : XA = AX\} = \mathbb{R}[A] = \left\{a_0 I + a_1 A + a_2 A^2 + \cdots + a_
{n-1} A^{n-1} : a_i \in \mathbb{R}\right\}.$$

Any matrix commuting with both $A$ and $B$ must lie in $\mathbb{R}[A]$, so write
 $p(A) = a_0 I + a_1 A + \cdots + a_{n-1} A^{n-1}$. The condition $p(A)B = Bp(A)
$ reads:
$$p(A)\, e_1 e_1^T = e_1 e_1^T\, p(A).$$

**Left side.** Since the first column of $A$ is zero, $Ae_1 = 0$, hence $A^k e_1
 = 0$ for all $k \geq 1$. Therefore:
$$p(A)\, e_1 = a_0\, e_1, \qquad \text{so} \quad p(A)B = a_0\, e_1 e_1^T.$$

**Right side.** Since $e_1^T A = e_2^T$, $e_2^T A = e_3^T$, $\ldots$, in general
 $e_1^T A^k = e_{k+1}^T$ for $0 \leq k \leq n-1$. Therefore:
$$e_1^T\, p(A) = a_0\, e_1^T + a_1\, e_2^T + \cdots + a_{n-1}\, e_n^T, \qquad \t
ext{so} \quad Bp(A) = a_0\, e_1 e_1^T + a_1\, e_1 e_2^T + \cdots + a_{n-1}\, e_1
 e_n^T.$$

**Equating both sides:**
$$a_0\, e_1 e_1^T = a_0\, e_1 e_1^T + a_1\, e_1 e_2^T + \cdots + a_{n-1}\, e_1 e
_n^T.$$

Since $\{e_1 e_j^T : 1 \leq j \leq n\}$ are linearly independent, this forces:
$$a_1 = a_2 = \cdots = a_{n-1} = 0.$$

Hence $p(A) = a_0 I$, and every matrix commuting with both $A$ and $B$ is a scal
ar multiple of the identity. $\checkmark$

**Small cases.** For $n = 1$: $A = (0)$, $B = (1)$, rank sum $= 0 + 1 = 1 \leq 1
$, and every $1 \times 1$ matrix is $\lambda I$. For $n = 0$ (if $0 \in \mathbb{
N}$): the condition is vacuous.

**Conclusion.** The construction works for every $n \geq 1$, so the answer is al
l $n \in \mathbb{N}$.
