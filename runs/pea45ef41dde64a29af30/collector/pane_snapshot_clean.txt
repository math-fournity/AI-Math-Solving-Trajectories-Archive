## Proof

**Yes.** The canonical image $J(X)$ is weak*-dense in $X^{**}$ for every Banach
space $X$. This is **Goldstine's theorem**. We prove the stronger statement that
 $J(B_X)$ is weak*-dense in $B_{X^{**}}$, where $B_X$, $B_{X^{**}}$ denote the c
losed unit balls.

---

**Setup.** Let $X$ be a Banach space (over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$; we trea
t the real case, the complex case follows by considering real parts). The canoni
cal map $J:X\to X^{**}$ is $J(x)(x^*)=x^*(x)$. The weak* topology on $X^{**}$ is
 $\sigma(X^{**},X^*)$, i.e. the topology of pointwise convergence on $X^*$.

A key fact: the topological dual of the locally convex space $(X^{**},\sigma(X^{
**},X^*))$ is precisely $X^*$, i.e. every weak*-continuous linear functional on
$X^{**}$ has the form $x^{**}\mapsto x^{**}(x^*)$ for some $x^*\in X^*$.

**Claim.** $\overline{J(B_X)}^{w^*}=B_{X^{**}}$.

**Proof of claim.** The inclusion $\overline{J(B_X)}^{w^*}\subseteq B_{X^{**}}$
is immediate: $J$ is an isometry so $J(B_X)\subseteq B_{X^{**}}$, and $B_{X^{**}
}$ is weak*-closed (it is the intersection $\bigcap_{x^*\in X^*}\{x^{**}:|x^{**}
(x^*)|\le\|x^*\|\}$, an intersection of closed sets).

For the reverse inclusion, suppose for contradiction that there exists $x_0^{**}
\in B_{X^{**}}\setminus \overline{J(B_X)}^{w^*}$. The set $C:=\overline{J(B_X)}^
{w^*}$ is weak*-closed and convex (closure of the convex set $J(B_X)$). By the H
ahn–Banach separation theorem applied in the locally convex space $(X^{**},\sigm
a(X^{**},X^*))$, there exists a weak*-continuous linear functional strictly sepa
rating $x_0^{**}$ from $C$. By the key fact above, this functional is evaluation
 at some $x^*\in X^*$: there exist $\alpha<\beta$ such that

$$\sup_{x\in B_X} x^*(J(x)) \le \alpha < \beta \le x^*(x_0^{**}). \tag{1}$$

(Here and below we work with real parts in the complex case.)

Since $x^*(J(x))=x^*(x)$ for $x\in X$, the left side of (1) is

$$\sup_{x\in B_X} x^*(x)=\|x^*\|,$$

using that $B_X$ is balanced, so the supremum of $x^*$ over $B_X$ equals $\|x^*\
|$. Thus $\alpha\ge\|x^*\|$.

On the other hand, $x_0^{**}\in B_{X^{**}}$ gives

$$x^*(x_0^{**})\le |x^*(x_0^{**})|\le \|x^*\|\cdot\|x_0^{**}\|\le\|x^*\|.$$

Combining: $\|x^*\|\le\alpha<\beta\le x^*(x_0^{**})\le\|x^*\|$, a contradiction.

Hence $\overline{J(B_X)}^{w^*}=B_{X^{**}}$. $\square$

**Deducing density of $J(X)$ in $X^{**}$.** Let $x^{**}\in X^{**}$, $x^{**}\neq
0$, and set $r=\|x^{**}\|>0$. For any $\varepsilon>0$, the element $x^{**}/(r+\v
arepsilon)$ lies in $B_{X^{**}}$. By the claim, there is a net $(x_\alpha)$ in $
B_X$ with $J(x_\alpha)\to x^{**}/(r+\varepsilon)$ in the weak* topology. Then $(
r+\varepsilon)x_\alpha\in X$ and

$$J\big((r+\varepsilon)x_\alpha\big)=(r+\varepsilon)J(x_\alpha)\xrightarrow{w^*}
 x^{**}.$$

So $x^{**}\in\overline{J(X)}^{w^*}$. Since $x^{**}$ was arbitrary, $\overline{J(
X)}^{w^*}=X^{**}$.

**Conclusion.** For every Banach space $X$, the canonical image $J(X)$ is weak*-
dense in $X^{**}$. $\blacksquare$
