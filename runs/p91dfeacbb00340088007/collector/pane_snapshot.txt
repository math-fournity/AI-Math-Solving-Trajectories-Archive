## Proof

We prove that for any integers $V, E \ge 0$ with $4E \le V^2$, there exists a tr
iangle-free graph on $V$ vertices with exactly $E$ edges.

**Key idea.** Every bipartite graph is triangle-free (a triangle is a cycle of l
ength 3, an odd cycle, and bipartite graphs contain no odd cycles). Moreover, an
y subgraph of a bipartite graph is again bipartite, hence triangle-free. So it s
uffices to find a bipartite graph on $V$ vertices with **at least** $E$ edges; d
eleting edges then yields exactly $E$ edges while preserving triangle-freeness.

**Maximizing edges in a bipartite graph on $V$ vertices.** A complete bipartite
graph $K_{a,b}$ has $a+b = V$ vertices and $ab$ edges. We maximize $ab$ subject
to $a + b = V$, $a, b \ge 0$. By the AM–GM inequality (or calculus), the product
 $ab = a(V-a)$ is maximized when $a$ and $b$ are as equal as possible:

- If $V = 2m$ is even: $\max ab = m \cdot m = m^2 = V^2/4$.
- If $V = 2m+1$ is odd: $\max ab = m(m+1) = (V^2 - 1)/4 = \lfloor V^2/4 \rfloor$
.

In both cases, $\max ab = \lfloor V^2/4 \rfloor$.

**Verifying $E \le \lfloor V^2/4 \rfloor$.** We are given $4E \le V^2$, i.e., $E
 \le V^2/4$.

- **$V$ even:** $V^2/4$ is an integer, so $E \le V^2/4 = \lfloor V^2/4 \rfloor$.
 ✓
- **$V$ odd:** $V^2$ is odd, so $V^2/4$ is not an integer. Since $4E$ is an even
 integer and $V^2$ is an odd integer, the inequality $4E \le V^2$ forces $4E \le
 V^2 - 1$, giving $E \le (V^2-1)/4 = \lfloor V^2/4 \rfloor$. ✓

**Construction.** In either case, set $a = \lfloor V/2 \rfloor$ and $b = \lceil
V/2 \rceil$, so $a + b = V$ and $ab = \lfloor V^2/4 \rfloor \ge E$. The complete
 bipartite graph $K_{a,b}$ has $V$ vertices, $ab \ge E$ edges, and is bipartite
(hence triangle-free). Selecting any $E$ of its edges gives a subgraph that is s
till bipartite, still on $V$ vertices, and has exactly $E$ edges.

**Conclusion.** For all $V, E$ with $4E \le V^2$, the graph constructed above is
 a triangle-free graph with $V$ vertices and $E$ edges. $\blacksquare$
