## Proof

Let
$$A(x)=\int_0^x e^{y^2}\,dy,\qquad B(x)=\int_0^x e^{2y^2}\,dy.$$
We seek $L=\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{A(x)^2}{B(x)}$.

**Step 1: Form of the limit.** Since $e^{y^2},e^{2y^2}>0$ and grow without bound
, $A(x)\to+\infty$ and $B(x)\to+\infty$ as $x\to+\infty$. Thus $A(x)^2\to+\infty
$, and the quotient is of the indeterminate form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. L'Hôp
ital's rule applies.

**Step 2: First application of L'Hôpital.** Differentiating numerator and denomi
nator (using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, $A'(x)=e^{x^2}$, $B'(x)=e^{2x^
2}$):
$$\frac{d}{dx}\bigl[A(x)^2\bigr]=2A(x)A'(x)=2A(x)e^{x^2},\qquad \frac{d}{dx}B(x)
=e^{2x^2}.$$
Hence
$$L=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{2A(x)e^{x^2}}{e^{2x^2}}=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{2A(x
)}{e^{x^2}}.$$

**Step 3: Second application of L'Hôpital.** The new quotient $\dfrac{2A(x)}{e^{
x^2}}$ is again $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$: $A(x)\to+\infty$ and $e^{x^2}\to+\inft
y$. Differentiating,
$$\frac{d}{dx}\bigl[2A(x)\bigr]=2e^{x^2},\qquad \frac{d}{dx}e^{x^2}=2x\,e^{x^2}.
$$
Therefore
$$L=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{2e^{x^2}}{2x\,e^{x^2}}=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{1}{x}
=0.$$

**Step 4: Verification of the limit existence (justifying L'Hôpital).** Each app
lication of L'Hôpital is valid because:
- The functions are differentiable on $(0,\infty)$ and the denominator's derivat
ive is nonzero there.
- Both numerator and denominator tend to $+\infty$, satisfying the $\infty/\inft
y$ hypothesis.
- The final limit $\lim_{x\to+\infty}1/x=0$ exists, which retroactively justifie
s the second (and hence the first) application of L'Hôpital.

**Conclusion.**
$$\boxed{\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\left(\int_0^x e^{y^2}\,dy\right)^2}{\int_0^x e
^{2y^2}\,dy}=0}.$$
