## Proof

**Claim:** The set $S = \{A \in M(n,\mathbb{R}) : |\lambda| \le 2 \text{ for eve
ry eigenvalue } \lambda \text{ of } A\}$ is connected (indeed, path-connected).

**Key observation:** $S$ is star-shaped with respect to the origin.

**Step 1: The origin belongs to $S$.**

The zero matrix $O \in M(n,\mathbb{R})$ has all eigenvalues equal to $0$, and $|
0| \le 2$, so $O \in S$.

**Step 2: $S$ is closed under scaling by $t \in [0,1]$.**

Let $A \in S$ and let $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$ be the eigenvalues of $A$ (l
isted with algebraic multiplicity in $\mathbb{C}$). For any $t \in [0,1]$, the e
igenvalues of $tA$ are $t\lambda_1, \dots, t\lambda_n$.

*Justification:* If $Av = \lambda v$ for some nonzero $v \in \mathbb{C}^n$, then
 $(tA)v = t\lambda v$, so $t\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $tA$. More formally, th
e characteristic polynomial satisfies
$$\det(tA - \mu I) = t^n\det\!\left(A - \tfrac{\mu}{t}I\right) = t^n\, p_A\!\lef
t(\tfrac{\mu}{t}\right),$$
so $\mu$ is a root of $p_{tA}$ if and only if $\mu/t$ is a root of $p_A$, i.e.,
the eigenvalues of $tA$ are exactly $\{t\lambda_i\}$.

Since $|t\lambda_i| = t\,|\lambda_i| \le |\lambda_i| \le 2$ for every $i$ and ev
ery $t \in [0,1]$, we conclude $tA \in S$.

**Step 3: $S$ is star-shaped, hence path-connected.**

For any $A \in S$, the map
$$\gamma : [0,1] \to S, \qquad \gamma(t) = tA$$
is a continuous path in $S$ (continuity is clear since matrix multiplication by
a scalar is continuous, and membership in $S$ was verified in Step 2) with $\gam
ma(0) = O$ and $\gamma(1) = A$.

Thus every point of $S$ is connected to the origin by a path lying entirely in $
S$. For any two points $A, B \in S$, the concatenation of the path from $A$ to $
O$ (given by $t \mapsto (1-t)A$) and the path from $O$ to $B$ (given by $t \maps
to tB$) yields a continuous path from $A$ to $B$ inside $S$.

**Conclusion:** $S$ is path-connected, and therefore connected. The answer is **
yes**, $S$ is necessarily a connected space.
