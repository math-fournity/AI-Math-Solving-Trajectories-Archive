## Proof

**Claim:** The Hartogs extension is unique.

**Step 1: Ω is connected.**

We are given that $\Omega \setminus K$ is connected (hence nonempty and open, si
nce $K$ is compact in $\mathbb{C}^n$ and therefore closed, so $\Omega \setminus
K = \Omega \cap (\mathbb{C}^n \setminus K)$ is open). Let $\{\Omega_\alpha\}_{\a
lpha}$ be the connected components of $\Omega$; each $\Omega_\alpha$ is open in
$\mathbb{C}^n$.

Since $\Omega \setminus K \subset \Omega$ is connected, it lies entirely within
a single component, say $\Omega \setminus K \subset \Omega_0$. Consequently ever
y other component $\Omega_\alpha$ ($\alpha \neq 0$) satisfies $\Omega_\alpha \su
bset K$. But $\Omega_\alpha$ is a nonempty open subset of $\mathbb{C}^n$ (if it
exists), and no nonempty open subset of $\mathbb{C}^n$ can be contained in a com
pact set (an open ball around any point is unbounded in extent / compact sets ha
ve empty interior in $\mathbb{C}^n$ unless... more precisely, a nonempty open se
t contains a ball, which is not compact). Hence no such $\Omega_\alpha$ exists,
and $\Omega = \Omega_0$ is connected.

**Step 2: Uniqueness via the identity theorem.**

Suppose $F_1, F_2 : \Omega \to \mathbb{C}$ are two holomorphic functions both ex
tending the given $f \in \mathcal{O}(\Omega \setminus K)$, i.e.,
$$F_1|_{\Omega \setminus K} = f = F_2|_{\Omega \setminus K}.$$
Then $G := F_1 - F_2$ is holomorphic on $\Omega$ and $G \equiv 0$ on $\Omega \se
tminus K$.

The set $\Omega \setminus K$ is a nonempty open subset of the connected open set
 $\Omega$. By the **identity theorem** for holomorphic functions of several vari
ables (which holds exactly as in one variable: if a holomorphic function on a co
nnected domain vanishes on a nonempty open subset, it vanishes identically), $G
\equiv 0$ on all of $\Omega$.

Therefore $F_1 = F_2$ on $\Omega$.

**Conclusion:** The Hartogs extension of any $f \in \mathcal{O}(\Omega \setminus
 K)$ to $\Omega$ is unique. The uniqueness follows not from Hartogs' theorem its
elf but from the general identity theorem, together with the fact (forced by the
 hypotheses $\Omega \setminus K$ connected and $K$ compact) that $\Omega$ is con
nected.
