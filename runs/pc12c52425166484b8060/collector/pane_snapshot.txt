## Proof

We seek sufficient conditions on a continuous, compactly supported $f:\mathbb{R}
\to\mathbb{R}$ so that, setting $f_n(x)=n^3 f(nx)$, the convolutions $f_n * g$ c
onverge uniformly to $g''$ for every smooth, compactly supported $g$.

**Claim.** The following four conditions are sufficient:

1. $f$ is continuous and compactly supported (given),
2. $\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}} f(x)\,dx = 0$,
3. $\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}} x\,f(x)\,dx = 0$,
4. $\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}} x^2\,f(x)\,dx = 2$.

### Setup

By definition of convolution and the substitution $u = ny$ ($dy = du/n$):

$$(f_n * g)(x) = \int_{\mathbb{R}} n^3 f(ny)\,g(x-y)\,dy = n^2\int_{\mathbb{R}}
f(u)\,g\!\left(x - \tfrac{u}{n}\right)du.$$

Since $f$ is compactly supported, say $\operatorname{supp} f \subseteq [-R,R]$,
the integral is effectively over $|u|\le R$.

### Taylor expansion

For each fixed $x$ and $u$, expand $g$ at $x$ to second order with integral rema
inder. With $h = -u/n$:

$$g(x+h) = g(x) + h\,g'(x) + \tfrac{h^2}{2}\,g''(x) + R_2(x,h),$$

where

$$R_2(x,h) = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^h (h-t)^2\,g'''(x+t)\,dt.$$

Substituting into the convolution:

$$(f_n*g)(x) = n^2 g(x)\!\int f - n\,g'(x)\!\int u\,f(u)\,du + \tfrac{g''(x)}{2}
\!\int u^2 f(u)\,du + n^2\!\int f(u)\,R_2\!\left(x,-\tfrac{u}{n}\right)du.$$

Under conditions (2), (3), (4), the first three terms become $0 - 0 + g''(x)$, s

$$(f_n*g)(x) - g''(x) = n^2\int_{\mathbb{R}} f(u)\,R_2\!\left(x,-\tfrac{u}{n}\ri
ght)du.$$

### Bounding the remainder (uniform convergence)

Since $g$ is smooth and compactly supported, $g'''$ is continuous and bounded: s
et $M = \|g'''\|_\infty < \infty$. For $|u|\le R$:

$$\left|R_2\!\left(x,-\tfrac{u}{n}\right)\right| \le \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{|u|/n} s
^2\,M\,ds = \frac{M\,|u|^3}{6\,n^3}.$$

Therefore, uniformly in $x$:

$$\left|(f_n*g)(x) - g''(x)\right| \le n^2\int_{-R}^{R} |f(u)|\,\frac{M\,|u|^3}{
6\,n^3}\,du = \frac{M}{6\,n}\int_{-R}^{R} |u|^3\,|f(u)|\,du.$$

The integral $C := \int_{-R}^{R}|u|^3|f(u)|\,du$ is finite (continuous integrand
 on a compact set), so

$$\|f_n*g - g''\|_\infty \le \frac{M\,C}{6\,n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.$$

This holds for every smooth, compactly supported $g$, giving uniform convergence
 $f_n*g \to g''$.

### Summary of conditions

The sufficient conditions are:

$$\boxed{\int_{\mathbb{R}} f(x)\,dx = 0,\qquad \int_{\mathbb{R}} x\,f(x)\,dx = 0
,\qquad \int_{\mathbb{R}} x^2\,f(x)\,dx = 2,}$$

together with the given hypotheses that $f$ is continuous and compactly supporte
d. Under these moment conditions, the second-order Taylor expansion of $g$ isola
tes exactly the $g''$ term, and the integral remainder is controlled uniformly b
y $\|g'''\|_\infty$ and the finite third absolute moment of $f$, yielding $\|f_n
*g - g''\|_\infty = O(1/n) \to 0$.
