## Proof

We analyze the differential equation
$$x'' + t\,x' + \frac{1}{1+t+t^2}\,x = 0, \qquad x(1)=0,\; x'(1)=1,$$
written in standard form as
$$x'' = -t\,x' - \frac{1}{1+t+t^2}\,x.$$

**Step 1: Identify the singularities of the coefficients.**

The right-hand side has two coefficient functions of $t$:
- $p(t) = -t$, which is entire (analytic on all of $\mathbb{C}$).
- $q(t) = -\dfrac{1}{1+t+t^2}$, which is meromorphic with poles exactly where $1
+t+t^2 = 0$.

The roots of $1+t+t^2=0$ are
$$t = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{1-4}}{2} = \frac{-1 \pm i\sqrt{3}}{2}.$$
Denote them $\alpha_+ = \tfrac{-1+i\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\alpha_- = \tfrac{-1-i\sqr
t{3}}{2}$.

**Step 2: Compute the distance from $t_0 = 1$ to the nearest singularity.**

$$|1 - \alpha_+| = \left|1 - \frac{-1+i\sqrt{3}}{2}\right| = \left|\frac{3 - i\s
qrt{3}}{2}\right| = \frac{\sqrt{9+3}}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{12}}{2} = \sqrt{3}.$$

$$|1 - \alpha_-| = \left|\frac{3 + i\sqrt{3}}{2}\right| = \frac{\sqrt{9+3}}{2} =
 \sqrt{3}.$$

Both singularities are at the same distance $\sqrt{3} \approx 1.732$ from $t_0 =
 1$. Hence $q(t)$ is analytic on the open disk $D(1, \sqrt{3}) = \{t : |t-1| < \
sqrt{3}\}$, and $p(t)$ is analytic everywhere.

**Step 3: Apply the analytic existence/uniqueness theorem.**

By the standard theorem for linear ODEs with analytic coefficients (Cauchy–Koval
evskaya / power-series method): if $p(t)$ and $q(t)$ are analytic in a disk $D(t
_0, \rho)$, then for any initial values $x(t_0), x'(t_0)$, the unique solution i
s analytic at $t_0$, and its Taylor series about $t_0$ converges at least in $D(
t_0, \rho)$.

Here both coefficients are analytic in $D(1, \sqrt{3})$, so the solution $x(t)$
is analytic at $t_0 = 1$ with radius of convergence
$$R \ge \sqrt{3}.$$

**Step 4: Compare with the threshold.**

Since $\sqrt{3} > 1$, we have
$$R \ge \sqrt{3} > 1,$$
and therefore $R > 1$.

**Conclusion.** The solution is analytic at $t_0 = 1$, and its radius of converg
ence satisfies $R \ge \sqrt{3} > 1$, so in particular $R > 1$.

### PROOF COMPLETE
