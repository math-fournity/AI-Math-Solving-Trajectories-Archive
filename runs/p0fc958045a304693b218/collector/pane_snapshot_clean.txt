## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle I = \int_{|z|=1} \frac{1}{2z - \sinh z}\,dz$, where t
he contour $|z|=1$ is traversed counterclockwise.

### Step 1. Locate the singularities inside $|z|=1$

The poles occur where $f(z) := 2z - \sinh z = 0$.

Clearly $z = 0$ is a root: $f(0) = 0 - 0 = 0$. Since $f'(z) = 2 - \cosh z$ and $
f'(0) = 2 - 1 = 1 \neq 0$, the zero at $z=0$ is **simple**, so $z=0$ is a simple
 pole of $1/f$.

### Step 2. Show $z=0$ is the only zero inside $|z|=1$ (Rouché's theorem)

On the circle $|z| = 1$:
- $|2z| = 2$.
- $|\sinh z| = \tfrac{1}{2}|e^z - e^{-z}| \leq \tfrac{1}{2}(e^{\operatorname{Re}
z} + e^{-\operatorname{Re}z}) = \cosh(\operatorname{Re}z) \leq \cosh(1) \approx
1.5431$.

Hence on $|z|=1$,
$$|\sinh z| \leq \cosh(1) < 2 = |2z|.$$

By **Rouché's theorem**, $f(z) = 2z - \sinh z$ and $2z$ have the same number of
zeros (counted with multiplicity) inside $|z|=1$. Since $2z$ has exactly one sim
ple zero at $z=0$, so does $f$. Thus $z=0$ is the **only** pole of $1/f$ inside
the contour, and it is simple.

### Step 3. Compute the residue

For a simple zero of $f$, the residue of $1/f$ is $1/f'(z_0)$:
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{1}{2z-\sinh z} = \frac{1}{f'(0)} = \frac{1}{2 -
\cosh 0} = \frac{1}{2-1} = 1.$$

### Step 4. Apply the residue theorem

$$I = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{1}{2z-\sinh z} = 2\pi i \cdot 1
 = 2\pi i.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{|z|=1} \frac{1}{2z - \sinh z}\,dz = 2\pi i}$$
