## Proof

**Yes, the sequence $\{f_k\}$ is bounded in $X^*$.**

**Setup.** Weak* convergence $f_k \xrightarrow{w^*} f$ means, by definition, tha
t for every $x \in X$,
$$f_k(x) \to f(x).$$

**Step 1: Pointwise boundedness.** For each fixed $x \in X$, the scalar sequence
 $\{f_k(x)\}_{k=1}^\infty$ converges to $f(x)$, hence is a bounded sequence of s
calars. Therefore
$$\sup_{k} |f_k(x)| < \infty \quad \text{for every } x \in X.$$

**Step 2: Apply the Uniform Boundedness Principle.** Each $f_k \in X^*$ is a bou
nded linear functional, i.e., a bounded linear operator $f_k : X \to \mathbb{K}$
 (where $\mathbb{K} = \mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$). We have shown that for every
 $x$ in the **Banach** space $X$,
$$\sup_k \|f_k(x)\|_{\mathbb{K}} = \sup_k |f_k(x)| < \infty.$$

By the **Uniform Boundedness Principle** (Banach–Steinhaus theorem), which appli
es because $X$ is complete, pointwise boundedness of the family $\{f_k\}$ implie
s uniform boundedness of the operator norms:
$$\sup_k \|f_k\|_{X^*} < \infty.$$

**Conclusion.** The sequence $\{f_k\}$ is bounded in $X^*$, with
$$\sup_k \|f_k\| \le M < \infty$$
for some constant $M$. In fact, one obtains the sharper estimate
$$\|f\| \le \liminf_{k\to\infty} \|f_k\| \le \sup_k \|f_k\| < \infty,$$
since $|f(x)| = \lim |f_k(x)| \le \liminf \|f_k\|\,\|x\|$, giving $\|f\| \le \li
minf \|f_k\|$.

The key hypothesis used is that $X$ is a **Banach** space (complete); the Unifor
m Boundedness Principle fails for incomplete normed spaces, so completeness is e
ssential here.
