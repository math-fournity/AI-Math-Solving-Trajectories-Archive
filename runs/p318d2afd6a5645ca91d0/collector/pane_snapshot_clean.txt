## Solution

$$I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{x\sin(3x)}{x^2+4}\,dx.$$

Since $x\sin(3x) = \operatorname{Im}\!\bigl(x\,e^{i3x}\bigr)$, define
$$f(z) = \frac{z\,e^{i3z}}{z^2+4}.$$
Then
$$I = \operatorname{Im}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx.$$

**Step 2: Identify poles.**

The denominator factors as $z^2+4 = (z-2i)(z+2i)$, giving simple poles at $z=2i$
 (upper half-plane) and $z=-2i$ (lower half-plane).

**Step 3: Apply Jordan's Lemma.**

Close the contour with the semicircular arc $C_R: z=Re^{i\theta}$, $0\le\theta\l
e\pi$, in the upper half-plane. The function on the arc is
$$g(z) = \frac{z}{z^2+4}, \qquad f(z) = g(z)\,e^{i3z}.$$

- $g(z)\to 0$ uniformly as $|z|\to\infty$ (degree of numerator < degree of denom
inator).
- The exponent coefficient $a = 3 > 0$, so $e^{i3z}$ decays in the upper half-pl
ane: $|e^{i3z}| = e^{-3\operatorname{Im}z}\le 1$ for $\operatorname{Im}z\ge 0$.

By **Jordan's Lemma**,
$$\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{C_R} g(z)\,e^{i3z}\,dz = 0.$$

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx = 2\pi i \sum_{\operatorname{Im}z_k>0}\operat
