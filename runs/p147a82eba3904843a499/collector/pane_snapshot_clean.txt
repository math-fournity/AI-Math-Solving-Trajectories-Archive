## Solution

The integrand divided by the normalizing constant $2^{n/2}\Gamma(n/2)$ is exactl
y the density of a chi-squared random variable with $n$ degrees of freedom:

$$f_n(t) = \frac{1}{2^{n/2}\Gamma(n/2)} e^{-t/2} t^{n/2-1}.$$

So the expression equals

$$P(X_n > n + \sqrt{2n}), \qquad X_n \sim \chi^2_n.$$

**CLT for chi-squared.** A $\chi^2_n$ variable is a sum of $n$ i.i.d. $\chi^2_1$
 variables, each with mean $1$ and variance $2$. Hence $X_n$ has mean $n$ and va
riance $2n$. By the Central Limit Theorem,

$$\frac{X_n - n}{\sqrt{2n}} \xrightarrow{d} Z \sim N(0,1).$$

Therefore,

$$P(X_n > n + \sqrt{2n}) = P\!\left(\frac{X_n - n}{\sqrt{2n}} > 1\right) \to P(Z
 > 1) = 1 - \Phi(1).$$

Using $\Phi(1) \approx 0.8413$,

$$1 - \Phi(1) \approx 0.1587.$$

The limit is **0.1587**, which is answer **(D)**.
