## Decomposition of $\Lambda_1 \otimes \Lambda_2$ for $A_2$

**Setup.** The Lie algebra $A_2$ is $\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{C})$, of rank 2. It
s two fundamental weights are $\omega_1, \omega_2$, with fundamental representat
ions $\Lambda_1 = V(\omega_1)$ and $\Lambda_2 = V(\omega_2)$.

- $\Lambda_1 = V(\omega_1)$ is the **defining representation** on $\mathbb{C}^3$
, of dimension 3.
- $\Lambda_2 = V(\omega_2)$ is the **contragredient representation** on $(\mathb
b{C}^3)^*$, also of dimension 3 (the dual $\bar{\mathbf{3}}$).

**Highest weight of the tensor product.** The highest weight appearing in $\Lamb
da_1 \otimes \Lambda_2$ is the sum of the two highest weights:
$$\lambda = \omega_1 + \omega_2.$$
In the Dynkin-label notation, this is the weight $(1,1)$.

**Identifying $V(\omega_1+\omega_2)$.** The highest root of $A_2$ is $\theta = \
alpha_1 + \alpha_2$. Using $\alpha_1 = 2\omega_1 - \omega_2$ and $\alpha_2 = -\o
mega_1 + 2\omega_2$, we get
$$\theta = \alpha_1 + \alpha_2 = \omega_1 + \omega_2.$$
So $\omega_1 + \omega_2$ is exactly the highest root, which means $V(\omega_1+\o
mega_2)$ is the **adjoint representation**. Its dimension is $\dim A_2 = 8$.

**Dimension check.** The full tensor product has dimension
$$\dim(\Lambda_1 \otimes \Lambda_2) = 3 \times 3 = 9.$$
The adjoint summand accounts for 8 dimensions, leaving
$$9 - 8 = 1$$
dimension unaccounted for. The only 1-dimensional representation of a semisimple
 Lie algebra is the **trivial representation** $V(0) = \mathbb{C}$.

**Confirming the trivial summand exists.** The contraction map
$$\Lambda_1 \otimes \Lambda_2 \cong \mathbb{C}^3 \otimes (\mathbb{C}^3)^* \to \m
athbb{C}, \qquad v \otimes \varphi \mapsto \varphi(v)$$
is a nonzero $A_2$-equivariant map onto the trivial representation, so the trivi
al summand is indeed present. Its kernel is the 8-dimensional invariant subspace
 $\{T \in \mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^3) : \mathrm{tr}\, T = 0\}$, which is the adjo
int representation $\mathfrak{sl}(3)$.

**Conclusion.** The Clebsch–Gordan decomposition is:
$$\boxed{\Lambda_1 \otimes \Lambda_2 \;=\; V(\omega_1+\omega_2) \;\oplus\; V(0)
\;=\; \Lambda_1\Lambda_2 \;\oplus\; \Lambda_0,}$$
i.e., the **adjoint representation (dimension 8)** plus the **trivial representa
tion (dimension 1)**:
$$\mathbf{3} \otimes \bar{\mathbf{3}} \;=\; \mathbf{8} \oplus \mathbf{1}.$$
