# Proof: Uniqueness of the Hermitian Logarithm

**Question.** If $A$ is a Hermitian positive definite matrix, is $P^* D' P$ the
only Hermitian logarithm of $A$? (Here $A = P^* D P$ is a unitary diagonalizatio
n with $D$ diagonal positive, and $D' = \log D$.)

**Answer: Yes.** The Hermitian logarithm of a Hermitian positive definite matrix
 is unique.

---

## Proof

Let $A$ be Hermitian positive definite. We must show: if $B$ is any Hermitian ma
trix satisfying $e^B = A$, then $B$ is uniquely determined (and in fact equals $
P^* (\log D) P$).

**Step 1: Spectral decomposition of $B$.** Since $B$ is Hermitian, it admits a u
nitary diagonalization
$$B = V M V^*, \qquad M = \operatorname{diag}(\mu_1, \dots, \mu_n), \quad \mu_i
\in \mathbb{R},$$
where $V$ is unitary and the $\mu_i$ are the (real) eigenvalues of $B$.

**Step 2: Exponentiating.** The matrix exponential respects the unitary conjugat
ion:
$$e^B = e^{V M V^*} = V\, e^M\, V^* = V\, \operatorname{diag}(e^{\mu_1}, \dots,
e^{\mu_n})\, V^*.$$
The hypothesis $e^B = A$ gives
$$A = V\, \operatorname{diag}(e^{\mu_1}, \dots, e^{\mu_n})\, V^*. \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Step 3: This is an eigendecomposition of $A$.** Equation $(\ast)$ exhibits $A$
 as unitarily diagonalizable with eigenvalues $e^{\mu_i} > 0$ (positive since $\
mu_i \in \mathbb{R}$), consistent with $A$ being Hermitian positive definite.

Let $\lambda_1 > \lambda_2 > \cdots > \lambda_k > 0$ be the **distinct** eigenva
lues of $A$, and let $E_j = \ker(A - \lambda_j I)$ be the corresponding eigenspa
ces. The orthogonal direct sum $E_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus E_k = \mathbb{C}^n$ is
the spectral decomposition of $\mathbb{C}^n$ determined **uniquely** by $A$.

**Step 4: The eigenvalues of $B$ are forced.** From $(\ast)$, the multiset $\{e^
{\mu_1}, \dots, e^{\mu_n}\}$ equals the multiset of eigenvalues of $A$ (with mul
tiplicity). Since $\exp : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}_{>0}$ is **injective**, each
 $\mu_i$ is uniquely recovered:
$$\mu_i = \log(\text{the corresponding eigenvalue of } A).$$
Concretely, for each $j$, the diagonal entries of $M$ corresponding to the eigen
space $E_j$ all equal $\log \lambda_j$.

**Step 5: $B$ acts as a scalar on each eigenspace of $A$.** From $(\ast)$, the c
olumns of $V$ associated to eigenvalue $e^{\mu_i} = \lambda_j$ span $E_j$. On th
is subspace, $M$ has the constant value $\log \lambda_j$, so
$$B\big|_{E_j} = V\big|_{E_j}\, (\log \lambda_j)\, I\, V\big|_{E_j}^* = (\log \l
ambda_j)\, I_{E_j}.$$
**Crucially, this holds for every choice of orthonormal basis $V$ within $E_j$**
: a scalar multiple of the identity is invariant under change of orthonormal bas
is. Therefore the action of $B$ on each $E_j$ is $\log \lambda_j \cdot I_{E_j}$,
 **independent of the particular unitary $V$**.

**Step 6: Uniqueness.** Let $P_j$ denote the orthogonal projection onto $E_j$ (d
etermined uniquely by $A$). Then every Hermitian logarithm $B$ satisfies
$$B = \sum_{j=1}^{k} (\log \lambda_j)\, P_j.$$
The right-hand side depends only on $A$ (its eigenvalues and eigenspaces), not o
n any arbitrary choice. Hence there is **exactly one** Hermitian matrix $B$ with
 $e^B = A$.

**Step 7: Identification with $P^* D' P$.** Given any unitary diagonalization $A
 = P^* D P$ with $D = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n)$ (eigenva
lues with multiplicity) and $D' = \log D$, we have
$$P^* D' P = \sum_{j=1}^{k} (\log \lambda_j)\, P_j = B,$$
since $P^* (\cdot) P$ simply selects, within each eigenspace $E_j$, the scalar $
\log \lambda_j$—and this scalar action is basis-independent. Thus $P^* D' P$ coi
ncides with the unique Hermitian logarithm regardless of the particular diagonal
izing unitary $P$ chosen.

---

## Conclusion

The Hermitian logarithm of a Hermitian positive definite matrix $A$ is **unique*
*: it equals $\sum_j (\log \lambda_j) P_j$, which is precisely $P^* D' P$ for an
y unitary diagonalization $A = P^* D P$. The injectivity of $\exp$ on $\mathbb{R
}$ forces the eigenvalues of $B$, and the fact that $B$ acts as a scalar (hence
basis-independently) on each eigenspace of $A$ forces the rest. Therefore $P^* D
' P$ is the **only** Hermitian logarithm of $A$. $\blacksquare$
