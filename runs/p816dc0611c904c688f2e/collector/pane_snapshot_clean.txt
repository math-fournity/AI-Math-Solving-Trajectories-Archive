## Proof

We wish to compute

$$L=\lim_{r\to 0}\int_{\sigma_{r}}\frac{e^{iz}}{z}\,dz,\qquad \sigma_{r}(t)=re^{
it},\; t\in[0,\pi].$$

**Step 1: Parametrize the integral.**

With $z=re^{it}$, we have $dz=ire^{it}\,dt$ and $z=re^{it}$, so

$$\int_{\sigma_{r}}\frac{e^{iz}}{z}\,dz=\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{e^{i\,re^{it}}}{re^{
it}}\,ire^{it}\,dt=i\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{i\,re^{it}}\,dt.$$

**Step 2: Show uniform convergence of the integrand.**

For $t\in[0,\pi]$ and $r>0$,

$$\left|e^{i\,re^{it}}-1\right|=\left|e^{i\,r(\cos t+i\sin t)}-1\right|=\left|e^
{-r\sin t}\,e^{ir\cos t}-1\right|.$$

Using $|e^{w}-1|\le |w|\,e^{|w|}$ with $w=ire^{it}$ and $|w|=r$,

$$\left|e^{i\,re^{it}}-1\right|\le r\,e^{r}.$$

This bound is independent of $t$, so $e^{i\,re^{it}}\to 1$ **uniformly** on $[0,
\pi]$ as $r\to 0$.

**Step 3: Exchange limit and integral.**

By uniform convergence,

$$L=\lim_{r\to 0}i\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{i\,re^{it}}\,dt=i\int_{0}^{\pi}\lim_{r\to 0}e
^{i\,re^{it}}\,dt=i\int_{0}^{\pi}1\,dt=i\pi.$$

**Verification via the residue interpretation (sanity check).** The function $e^
{iz}/z$ has a simple pole at $z=0$ with residue $\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{e
^{iz}}{z}=e^{i\cdot 0}=1$. The arc $\sigma_r$ is the upper semicircle traversed
counterclockwise (from angle $0$ to $\pi$). A standard Jordan-lemma-type estimat
e shows that the integral over a small semicircular arc around a simple pole con
tributes $i\pi\cdot\operatorname{Res}$, i.e. $i\pi\cdot 1=i\pi$, consistent with
 the direct computation above.

$$\boxed{\lim_{r\to 0}\int_{\sigma_{r}}\frac{e^{iz}}{z}\,dz=i\pi.}$$
