Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum number of representations $n
 = x^2 - y^2$ (with $x, y \geq 0$) over all positive integers $n$ with exactly $
k$ trailing zeros and $n > 10^k$, where $k \geq 2$.

## Proof

**Step 1: Counting representations via factorizations.**

Since $n = x^2 - y^2 = (x-y)(x+y)$, set $a = x-y$, $b = x+y$. Then $n = ab$ with
 $1 \leq a \leq b$, $a \equiv b \pmod{2}$ (so that $x = (a+b)/2$ and $y = (b-a)/
2$ are non-negative integers). Each such factorization gives a unique pair $(x,y
)$.

**Step 2: Parity analysis.**

Since $n$ has $k \geq 2$ trailing zeros, $4 \mid n$, so $n$ is even and divisibl
e by 4. For even $n$, the condition $a \equiv b \pmod{2}$ forces both $a, b$ eve
n. Setting $a = 2a'$, $b = 2b'$ gives $a'b' = M := n/4$ with $1 \leq a' \leq b'$
. The number of representations is therefore $\lceil d(M)/2 \rceil$, where $d(M)
$ is the number of divisors of $M$.

**Step 3: Expressing $d(M)$ in terms of valuations.**

Write $n = 2^{\alpha} \cdot 5^{\beta} \cdot r$ with $\gcd(r, 10) = 1$. The trail
ing-zero count is $\min(\alpha, \beta) = k$, so at least one of $\alpha = k$ or
$\beta = k$. The condition $n > 10^k$ means $n \geq 2 \cdot 10^k$.

Then $M = n/4 = 2^{\alpha - 2} \cdot 5^{\beta} \cdot r$, and:
$$d(M) = (\alpha - 1)(\beta + 1)\, d(r).$$

**Step 4: Minimizing over all valid configurations.**

We minimize $(\alpha - 1)(\beta + 1)\, d(r)$ subject to $\min(\alpha, \beta) = k
$, $\alpha \geq k$, $\beta \geq k$, $\gcd(r,10)=1$, and $n > 10^k$.

**Case A: $\alpha = k$, $\beta = k$.** Then $n = 10^k \cdot r$ with $r \geq 2$ (
since $n > 10^k$) and $\gcd(r,10)=1$, so $d(r) \geq 2$. This gives $d(M) \geq (k
-1)(k+1) \cdot 2 = 2(k^2 - 1)$.

**Case B: $\alpha = k$, $\beta = k + s$, $s \geq 1$.** Then $n = 2^k \cdot 5^{k+
s} \cdot r \geq 5 \cdot 10^k > 10^k$ automatically, so $r = 1$ is allowed ($d(r)
 = 1$). This gives $d(M) = (k-1)(k+s+1)$, minimized at $s = 1$: $d(M) = (k-1)(k+
2)$.

**Case C: $\beta = k$, $\alpha = k + t$, $t \geq 1$.** Similarly $r = 1$ is allo
wed, giving $d(M) = (k+t-1)(k+1)$, minimized at $t = 1$: $d(M) = k(k+1)$.

**Comparison (for $k \geq 2$):**
- Case B: $(k-1)(k+2) = k^2 + k - 2$
- Case C: $k(k+1) = k^2 + k$
- Case A: $\geq 2(k^2 - 1) = 2k^2 - 2$

Since $k^2 + k - 2 < k^2 + k < 2k^2 - 2$ for all $k \geq 2$, **Case B is optimal
**.

**Step 5: Computing the minimum.**

The optimal $n = 2^k \cdot 5^{k+1}$ (i.e., $\alpha = k$, $\beta = k+1$, $r = 1$)
 gives $M = 2^{k-2} \cdot 5^{k+1}$ with $d(M) = (k-1)(k+2)$.

Note that $(k-1)(k+2) = k^2 + k - 2$ is always even (checking both parities of $
k$), so:
$$\left\lceil \frac{d(M)}{2} \right\rceil = \frac{(k-1)(k+2)}{2} = \frac{k^2 + k
 - 2}{2}.$$

**Step 6: Verification.**

- $k = 2$: $n = 2^2 \cdot 5^3 = 500$. Trailing zeros: $\min(2,3) = 2$ ✓, $500 >
100$ ✓. $M = 125 = 5^3$, $d(M) = 4$, representations $= 2$. Formula: $(1)(4)/2 =
 2$ ✓. (The two representations: $500 = 126^2 - 124^2 = 30^2 - 20^2$.)

- $k = 3$: $n = 2^3 \cdot 5^4 = 5000$. $M = 1250 = 2 \cdot 5^4$, $d(M) = 10$, re
presentations $= 5$. Formula: $(2)(5)/2 = 5$ ✓.

## Answer

The minimum number of distinct pairs $(x, y)$ is:

$$\boxed{\dfrac{(k-1)(k+2)}{2}}$$
