## Proof

We are given an embedded surface $S$ with parametrization $\sigma:V\to U\subset
S$ whose first fundamental form is

$$ds^2 = du^2 + G(u,v)\,dv^2.$$

Thus $E=1$, $F=0$, and the coefficient of $dv^2$ is $G(u,v)$. We compute the Gau
ssian curvature $K$ from the Christoffel symbols and the Theorema Egregium.

**Christoffel symbols.** For an orthogonal parametrization ($F=0$), the Christof
fel symbols are

$$\Gamma^k_{ij}=\frac12\sum_l g^{kl}\bigl(\partial_i g_{jl}+\partial_j g_{il}-\p
artial_l g_{ij}\bigr),$$

with $g_{11}=E=1$, $g_{12}=g_{21}=0$, $g_{22}=G$, and $g^{11}=1$, $g^{22}=1/G$,
$g^{12}=0$.

Since $E=1$ is constant, $E_u=E_v=0$. The only non‑vanishing derivatives of the
metric are $G_u$ and $G_v$. A direct computation gives:

- $\Gamma^1_{22}=-\tfrac12 G_u$,
- $\Gamma^2_{12}=\Gamma^2_{21}=\tfrac{G_u}{2G}$,
- $\Gamma^2_{22}=\tfrac{G_v}{2G}$,

and all others are zero (in particular $\Gamma^1_{11}=\Gamma^1_{12}=\Gamma^1_{21
}=\Gamma^2_{11}=0$).

**Gauss equation.** The Gaussian curvature is given by the Gauss equation

$$K = \frac{R_{1212}}{\det g},\qquad \det g = EG-F^2 = G.$$

The only component we need is $R_{1212}$, which (with $F=0$, $E=1$) reduces to

$$R_{1212} = -\frac12\,G_{uu} + \Gamma^2_{12}\,\Gamma^1_{22}\cdot(\text{correcti
on})\;\dots$$

More cleanly, using the standard formula for the Riemann tensor component

$$R^1_{\;212} = \partial_1\Gamma^1_{22}-\partial_2\Gamma^1_{21}+\Gamma^1_{1m}\Ga
mma^m_{22}-\Gamma^1_{2m}\Gamma^m_{21}.$$

Substituting:

- $\partial_1\Gamma^1_{22} = \partial_u(-\tfrac12 G_u) = -\tfrac12 G_{uu}$,
- $\partial_2\Gamma^1_{21} = \partial_v(0) = 0$,
- $\Gamma^1_{1m}\Gamma^m_{22} = 0$ (since $\Gamma^1_{11}=\Gamma^1_{12}=0$),
- $\Gamma^1_{2m}\Gamma^m_{21} = \Gamma^1_{22}\Gamma^2_{21} = (-\tfrac12 G_u)\big
l(\tfrac{G_u}{2G}\bigr) = -\dfrac{G_u^2}{4G}$.

Hence

$$R^1_{\;212} = -\frac12 G_{uu} - \Bigl(-\frac{G_u^2}{4G}\Bigr) = -\frac12 G_{uu
} + \frac{G_u^2}{4G}.$$

Since $R_{1212}=g_{11}\,R^1_{\;212}=R^1_{\;212}$ (because $g_{11}=1$), we obtain

$$K = \frac{R_{1212}}{\det g} = \frac{1}{G}\Bigl(-\frac12 G_{uu}+\frac{G_u^2}{4G
}\Bigr) = -\frac{G_{uu}}{2G}+\frac{G_u^2}{4G^2}.$$

**Equivalent compact form.** Observe that

$$\frac{\partial^2}{\partial u^2}\sqrt{G} = \frac{\partial}{\partial u}\Bigl(\fr
ac{G_u}{2\sqrt{G}}\Bigr) = \frac{G_{uu}}{2\sqrt{G}}-\frac{G_u^2}{4G^{3/2}} = \fr
ac{2G\,G_{uu}-G_u^2}{4G^{3/2}}.$$

Dividing by $\sqrt{G}$,

$$\frac{(\sqrt{G})_{uu}}{\sqrt{G}} = \frac{2G\,G_{uu}-G_u^2}{4G^2} = \frac{G_{uu
}}{2G}-\frac{G_u^2}{4G^2}.$$

This is exactly the negative of the expression for $K$ above. Therefore

$$\boxed{\,K = -\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{G(u,v)}}\,\frac{\partial^2\!\sqrt{G(u,v)}}{\par
tial u^2}\,}$$

or equivalently

$$K = -\frac{G_{uu}}{2G}+\frac{G_u^2}{4G^2} = -\frac{2G\,G_{uu}-G_u^2}{4G^2}.$$

This is the classical formula for the Gaussian curvature of a surface whose firs
t fundamental form is $du^2+G(u,v)\,dv^2$ (geodesic coordinates), and it depends
 only on the intrinsic metric, confirming the Theorema Egregium.
