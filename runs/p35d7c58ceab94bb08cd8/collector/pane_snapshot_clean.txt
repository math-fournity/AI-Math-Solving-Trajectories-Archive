**Answer: Yes, $f$ is locally distance preserving.**

**Proof.**

**Step 1: $f$ is a local diffeomorphism.**

Since $f: M \to M$ is an immersion between manifolds of the same dimension, the
differential $df_p: T_p M \to T_{f(p)} M$ is injective, hence an isomorphism, fo
r every $p \in M$. By the inverse function theorem—which holds for manifolds wit
h boundary (at boundary points, $f$ maps a neighborhood in $M$ diffeomorphically
 onto a neighborhood in $M$, sending boundary to boundary)—for every $p \in M$ t
here exist open neighborhoods $V_p \ni p$ and $W_{f(p)} \ni f(p)$ such that $f|_
{V_p}: V_p \to W_{f(p)}$ is a diffeomorphism.

**Step 2: $f$ is a local Riemannian isometry.**

Since $f$ is an isometric immersion, $f^*g = g$, so $f|_{V_p}: V_p \to W_{f(p)}$
 is a diffeomorphism that preserves the Riemannian metric. In particular, $f$ pr
eserves lengths of curves: for any piecewise smooth curve $\gamma$ in $M$, $\ope
ratorname{length}(f \circ \gamma) = \operatorname{length}(\gamma)$.

**Step 3: Construction of the local neighborhood.**

Fix $p \in M$. Since $f(V_p)$ is an open neighborhood of $f(p)$ and the metric t
opology coincides with the manifold topology, there exists $r > 0$ such that the
 open metric ball $B_r(f(p)) \subset f(V_p)$. Define
$$U = V_p \cap f^{-1}\!\bigl(B_{r/4}(f(p))\bigr),$$
which is an open neighborhood of $p$. Shrink $U$ further if necessary so that $\
operatorname{diam}(U) < r/4$ (i.e., $d(x,y) < r/4$ for all $x, y \in U$), which
is possible by continuity of the distance function and compactness of small clos
ures.

**Step 4: The inequality $d(f(x), f(y)) \leq d(x, y)$.**

For any piecewise smooth curve $\gamma$ from $x$ to $y$ in $M$, the image $f \ci
rc \gamma$ is a curve from $f(x)$ to $f(y)$ of the same length. Taking the infim
um over all such $\gamma$:
$$d(f(x), f(y)) \leq d(x, y).$$

**Step 5: The inequality $d(x, y) \leq d(f(x), f(y))$.**

Since $M$ is compact (hence complete as a metric space), the distance $d(f(x), f
(y))$ is achieved by some minimizing curve $\tilde{\gamma}$ from $f(x)$ to $f(y)
$ of length $d(f(x), f(y))$.

For $x, y \in U$:
- $f(x) \in B_{r/4}(f(p))$, so $d(f(x), f(p)) < r/4$.
- $d(f(x), f(y)) \leq d(x, y) < r/4$.

Every point on $\tilde{\gamma}$ is within distance $d(f(x), f(y)) < r/4$ of $f(x
)$ (by the triangle inequality applied to sub-curves of $\tilde{\gamma}$). Hence
 every point of $\tilde{\gamma}$ lies in $B_{r/4}(f(x))$, which is contained in
$B_{r/2}(f(p))$ by the triangle inequality:
$$d(\tilde{\gamma}(t), f(p)) \leq d(\tilde{\gamma}(t), f(x)) + d(f(x), f(p)) < \
tfrac{r}{4} + \tfrac{r}{4} = \tfrac{r}{2} < r.$$

Therefore $\tilde{\gamma}$ lies entirely in $B_r(f(p)) \subset f(V_p)$.

Since $f|_{V_p}: V_p \to f(V_p)$ is a diffeomorphism and $\tilde{\gamma} \subset
 f(V_p)$, we can lift $\tilde{\gamma}$ to a curve $\gamma = (f|_{V_p})^{-1} \cir
c \tilde{\gamma}$ in $V_p \subset M$ from $x$ to $y$. Because $f|_{V_p}$ is a Ri
emannian isometry, $\operatorname{length}(\gamma) = \operatorname{length}(\tilde
{\gamma}) = d(f(x), f(y))$. Therefore:
$$d(x, y) \leq \operatorname{length}(\gamma) = d(f(x), f(y)).$$

**Step 6: Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 4 and 5, we have $d(f(x), f(y)) = d(x, y)$ for all $x, y \in U$.
 Since $p \in M$ was arbitrary, $f$ is locally distance preserving. $\blacksquar
