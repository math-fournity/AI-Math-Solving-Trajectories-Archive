## Proof

**Setup.** We are given
$$f(x) = 1 - a\cos x - b\sin x - A\cos 2x - B\sin 2x \geq 0$$
for all real $x$, where $a, b, A, B$ are real constants.

---

### Part 1: Proving $A^2 + B^2 \leq 1$

**Key observation:** Evaluate $f$ at $x$ and at $x + \pi$, then add.

Since $\cos(x+\pi) = -\cos x$, $\sin(x+\pi) = -\sin x$, $\cos(2(x+\pi)) = \cos(2
x+2\pi) = \cos 2x$, and $\sin(2(x+\pi)) = \sin 2x$, we get:

$$f(x+\pi) = 1 + a\cos x + b\sin x - A\cos 2x - B\sin 2x$$

Adding:
$$f(x) + f(x+\pi) = 2 - 2A\cos 2x - 2B\sin 2x$$

Since both $f(x) \geq 0$ and $f(x+\pi) \geq 0$, their sum is non-negative:

$$A\cos 2x + B\sin 2x \leq 1 \quad \text{for all } x$$

The expression $A\cos 2x + B\sin 2x$ achieves its maximum value $\sqrt{A^2 + B^2
}$ (by writing it as $R\cos(2x - \varphi)$ where $R = \sqrt{A^2+B^2}$). Therefor
e:

$$\sqrt{A^2 + B^2} \leq 1 \implies \boxed{A^2 + B^2 \leq 1}$$

---

### Part 2: Proving $a^2 + b^2 \leq 2$

**Key observation:** Evaluate $f$ at $x$ and at $x + \frac{\pi}{2}$, then add.

Using $\cos(x+\frac{\pi}{2}) = -\sin x$, $\sin(x+\frac{\pi}{2}) = \cos x$, $\cos
(2x+\pi) = -\cos 2x$, $\sin(2x+\pi) = -\sin 2x$:

$$f\!\left(x+\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right) = 1 + a\sin x - b\cos x + A\cos 2x + B\sin 2x
$$

Adding $f(x) + f(x+\frac{\pi}{2})$:

$$= \bigl[1 - a\cos x - b\sin x - A\cos 2x - B\sin 2x\bigr] + \bigl[1 + a\sin x
- b\cos x + A\cos 2x + B\sin 2x\bigr]$$

The $A$ and $B$ terms cancel completely, leaving:

$$f(x) + f\!\left(x+\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right) = 2 - (a+b)\cos x + (a-b)\sin x$$

Since both terms are non-negative:

$$(a+b)\cos x - (a-b)\sin x \leq 2 \quad \text{for all } x$$

The left side is a linear combination of $\cos x$ and $\sin x$, which achieves i
ts maximum value:

$$\sqrt{(a+b)^2 + (a-b)^2} = \sqrt{2a^2 + 2b^2} = \sqrt{2(a^2+b^2)}$$

Therefore:

$$\sqrt{2(a^2+b^2)} \leq 2 \implies a^2 + b^2 \leq 2 \implies \boxed{a^2 + b^2 \
leq 2}$$

### Conclusion

Both required inequalities follow from the non-negativity of $f$ by averaging $f
$ over appropriately shifted arguments:

- **$A^2 + B^2 \leq 1$** from $f(x) + f(x+\pi) \geq 0$, which eliminates the fir
st-harmonic terms.
- **$a^2 + b^2 \leq 2$** from $f(x) + f(x+\frac{\pi}{2}) \geq 0$, which eliminat
es the second-harmonic terms.

In each case, the remaining expression is a single sinusoid whose amplitude is b
ounded by the constant term, yielding the desired bounds. $\blacksquare$
