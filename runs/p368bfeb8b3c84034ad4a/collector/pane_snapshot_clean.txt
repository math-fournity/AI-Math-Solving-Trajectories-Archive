Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether, in a non-UFD Noetherian
local domain, every height-one prime $P$ admits an element $x \in P$ whose only
minimal prime over $(x)$ is $P$ itself.

Answer: No, such an element need not exist.

Counterexample. Let $k$ be a field and set $$R = k[[x,, y,, u,, v]],/,(xu - yv),
$$ the quotient of the formal power series ring by the single relation $xu =
yv$. Let $\mathfrak{m} = (x,y,u,v)$ be the maximal ideal, so $(R, \mathfrak{m})$
is a Noetherian local domain with $\dim R = 3$.

$R$ is not a UFD. In $R$ we have $xu = yv$. The elements $x, y, u, v$ are
irreducible (their leading forms are irreducible in the associated graded ring
$k[x,y,u,v]/(xu-yv)$). Since $xu = yv$ with ${x, u} \neq {y, v}$ as multisets of
irreducibles, factorization is not unique, so $R$ is not a UFD.

$R$ is a normal domain. $R$ is a hypersurface, hence Cohen–Macaulay, so it
satisfies Serre's condition $(S_2)$. The singular locus is $V(\partial(xu-yv)/
\partial x, \ldots) = V(u, v, -y, -x) = {\mathfrak{m}}$, which has codimension
$3 \geq 2$, so $(R_1)$ holds. By Serre's criterion, $R$ is normal (integrally
closed).

The height-one prime $P = (x, y)$. We have $R/P \cong k[[u, v]]$, which has
dimension $2$, so $\operatorname{ht}(P) = 3 - 2 = 1$.

The divisor class group. Since $R$ is a normal Noetherian domain, it is a Krull
domain. The variety $V(xu - yv)$ is the affine cone over the Segre variety $
\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1 \subset \mathbb{P}^3$. The class group of this
cone is $$\operatorname{Cl}(R) ;\cong; \operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^1 \times
\mathbb{P}^1),/,\mathbb{Z}\cdot[\mathcal{O}(1,1)] ;\cong; \mathbb{Z}^2,/,
\mathbb{Z}(1,1) ;\cong; \mathbb{Z},$$ and the class $[P]$ of the prime $P =
(x,y)$ (corresponding to one ruling $\mathcal{O}(1,0)$) is a generator of $
\operatorname{Cl}(R) \cong \mathbb{Z}$. In particular, $[P]$ has infinite order:
$n[P] \neq 0$ for every $n \geq 1$.

No suitable element exists. Suppose for contradiction that there exists $x_0 \in
P$ such that $P$ is the only minimal prime over $(x_0)$.

  • If $x_0 = 0$, then every prime contains $x_0$, so the only minimal prime
  over $(0)$ is $(0)$ itself (since $R$ is a domain). But $\operatorname{ht}(P)
  = 1 > 0 = \operatorname{ht}((0))$, so $P \neq (0)$ and $P$ is not a minimal
  prime over $(0)$. Contradiction.
  • So $x_0 \neq 0$. Since $R$ is a Krull domain, the principal divisor of $x_0$
  is $$\operatorname{div}(x_0) = \sum_{\operatorname{ht}(Q)=1} v_Q(x_0),[Q],$$
  where $v_Q$ is the valuation of the DVR $R_Q$, and the sum is finite. The
  class of any principal divisor is zero in $\operatorname{Cl}(R)$.

The minimal primes over $(x_0)$ are exactly the height-one primes $Q$ with
$v_Q(x_0) > 0$. If $P$ is the only such prime, then $$\operatorname{div}(x_0) =
n,[P] \quad\text{for some } n \geq 1,$$ and taking classes in $\operatorname{Cl}
(R)$ gives $n[P] = 0$.

But $[P]$ is a generator of $\operatorname{Cl}(R) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, so $[P]$
has infinite order and $n[P] \neq 0$ for all $n \geq 1$. Contradiction.

Conclusion. In the Noetherian local domain $R = k[[x,y,u,v]]/(xu - yv)$, which
is not a UFD, the height-one prime $P = (x,y)$ admits no element $x \in P$ for
which $P$ is the only minimal prime over $(x)$. The obstruction is that $[P]$
has infinite order in the divisor class group, so no power of $P$ is principal
(even up to radical). $\blacksquare$
