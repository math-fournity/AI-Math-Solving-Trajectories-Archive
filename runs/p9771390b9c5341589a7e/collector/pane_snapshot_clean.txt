## 解答

We evaluate $I = \int_0^\infty \frac{\sin(\pi x)}{x(1-x^2)}\,dx$.

**Step 1: Symmetry.** The integrand is even:
$$\frac{\sin(\pi(-x))}{(-x)(1-x^2)} = \frac{-\sin(\pi x)}{-x(1-x^2)} = \frac{\si
n(\pi x)}{x(1-x^2)}.$$
Hence
$$I = \frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\sin(\pi x)}{x(1-x^2)}\,dx = \fra
c{1}{2}\,\mathrm{Im}\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{i\pi x}}{x(1-x^
2)}\,dx}_{=:\,J}.$$

**Step 2: Singularities.** Set $g(z) = \dfrac{e^{i\pi z}}{z(1-z)(1+z)}$. The onl
y singularities are simple poles on the real axis at $z=-1,0,1$. (For the sine v
ersion these are removable, so $I$ is a genuine convergent integral; for $g$ the
y are genuine poles, requiring a principal value.)

**Step 3: Residues.**
- $\operatorname{Res}(g,0) = \lim_{z\to 0} z\,g(z) = \dfrac{1}{(1)(1)} = 1.$
- $\operatorname{Res}(g,1) = \lim_{z\to 1}(z-1)\,g(z) = \lim_{z\to 1}\dfrac{-e^{
i\pi z}}{z(1+z)} = \dfrac{-e^{i\pi}}{1\cdot 2} = \dfrac{1}{2}.$
- $\operatorname{Res}(g,-1) = \lim_{z\to -1}(z+1)\,g(z) = \dfrac{e^{-i\pi}}{(-1)
(1-(-1))} = \dfrac{-1}{-2} = \dfrac{1}{2}.$

**Step 4: Contour.** Take the standard keyhole-type contour: the real segment $[
-R,R]$ with small semicircular indents of radius $\varepsilon$ in the **upper**
half-plane around $-1,0,1$, closed by the large semicircle $C_R$ in the upper ha
lf-plane. Since $e^{i\pi z}$ decays for $\operatorname{Im}z>0$, the Jordan lemma
 gives $\int_{C_R}g\to 0$ as $R\to\infty$. There are no poles inside the contour
, so by Cauchy's theorem the total contour integral is $0$.

Each small upper-half-plane indent around a simple pole $z_0$, traversed from $z
_0-\varepsilon$ to $z_0+\varepsilon$ (i.e. $z=z_0+\varepsilon e^{i\theta}$, $\th
eta:\pi\to 0$), contributes $-i\pi\operatorname{Res}(g,z_0)$. Therefore
$$\mathrm{PV}\,J \;-\; i\pi\bigl[\operatorname{Res}(g,0)+\operatorname{Res}(g,1)
+\operatorname{Res}(g,-1)\bigr] = 0,$$
$$\mathrm{PV}\,J = i\pi\!\left(1+\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\right) = i\pi\cdot 2
= 2\pi i.$$

**Step 5: Extract $I$.** Since the sine integrand has only removable singulariti
es, the principal value equals the ordinary integral, and
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(\pi x)}{x(1-x^2)}\,dx = \operatorname{Im}(J)
 = \operatorname{Im}(2\pi i) = 2\pi.$$
Thus
$$I = \frac{1}{2}\cdot 2\pi = \pi.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \int_0^\infty \frac{\sin(\pi x)}{x(1-x^2)}\,dx = \pi}$$
