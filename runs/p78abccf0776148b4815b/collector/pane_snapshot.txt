Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a.e. convergence to 0, comb
ined with domination by the integrable function $e^{-x}$, implies almost uniform
 convergence on the infinite-measure space $[0,\infty)$.

**Answer: Yes, $f_n \to 0$ almost uniformly.**

## Proof

Let $g(x) = e^{-x}$. The key observation is that $g$ is integrable on $[0,\infty
)$ and $g(x) \to 0$ as $x \to \infty$. This means that for large $x$, the unifor
m bound on $|f_n|$ becomes arbitrarily small — independently of $n$ — while on b
ounded intervals (finite measure), Egorov's theorem applies directly.

**Construction.** Let $\varepsilon > 0$ be given. For each $k \in \mathbb{N}$, s
et $R_k = \ln(k+1)$, so that
$$e^{-R_k} = \frac{1}{k+1} < \frac{1}{k},$$
and note that $\{R_k\}$ is strictly increasing.

On each interval $[0, R_k]$, which has finite Lebesgue measure, $f_n \to 0$ a.e.
 (as restriction of a.e. convergence on $[0,\infty)$). By **Egorov's theorem**,
there exists a measurable set $E_k \subset [0, R_k]$ with
$$m(E_k) < \frac{\varepsilon}{2^k}$$
such that $f_n \to 0$ uniformly on $[0, R_k] \setminus E_k$.

Define
$$E = \bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty} E_k.$$

By countable subadditivity,
$$m(E) \le \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} m(E_k) < \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{\varepsilon}{2
^k} = \varepsilon.$$

**Uniform convergence on $E^c$.** We show $f_n \to 0$ uniformly on $E^c = [0,\in
fty) \setminus E$.

Let $\eta > 0$ be arbitrary. Choose $k \in \mathbb{N}$ with $\frac{1}{k} < \eta$
. Every $x \in E^c$ falls into one of two cases:

- **Case 1: $x \in [0, R_k]$.** Since $x \notin E \supseteq E_k$, we have $x \in
 [0, R_k] \setminus E_k$. By the Egorov conclusion on this set, there exists $N$
 such that for all $n \ge N$ and all $y \in [0, R_k] \setminus E_k$,
$$|f_n(y)| < \eta.$$

- **Case 2: $x \in (R_k, \infty)$.** Using the domination hypothesis (which hold
s for **all** $n$ simultaneously):
$$|f_n(x)| \le e^{-x} < e^{-R_k} = \frac{1}{k+1} < \frac{1}{k} < \eta \quad \tex
t{for every } n \in \mathbb{N}.$$

Combining both cases: for all $n \ge N$ and all $x \in E^c$,
$$|f_n(x)| < \eta.$$

Since $\eta > 0$ was arbitrary, $f_n \to 0$ uniformly on $E^c$. Since $\varepsil
on > 0$ was arbitrary and $m(E) < \varepsilon$, we conclude that $f_n \to 0$ **a
lmost uniformly** on $[0,\infty)$.

**Remark on the mechanism.** The integrable dominating function $e^{-x}$ serves
a dual role: (i) it provides a uniform-in-$n$ bound that decays to $0$ as $x \to
 \infty$, effectively "taming" the tail of the infinite-measure space so that th
e functions are already uniformly small there; and (ii) it confines the non-triv
ial behavior to sets of finite measure, where Egorov's theorem applies. The coun
table union over dyadically shrinking exceptional sets $E_k$ keeps the total exc
eptional measure below $\varepsilon$.
