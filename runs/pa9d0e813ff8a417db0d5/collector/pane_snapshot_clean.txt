## Proof

We wish to evaluate
$$S = \sum_{x \in \mathbb{Q}^+} \frac{1}{f(x)^2}, \qquad f\!\left(\tfrac{a}{b}\r
ight) = ab,\quad \tfrac{a}{b}\text{ in lowest terms}.$$

Each positive rational has a unique representation $x = a/b$ with $a, b \geq 1$
and $\gcd(a,b)=1$, and then $f(x) = ab$. Hence

$$S = \sum_{\substack{a,b \geq 1 \\ \gcd(a,b)=1}} \frac{1}{a^2 b^2}.$$

**Relating to the unrestricted double sum.** Consider the full double sum

$$T = \sum_{a,b \geq 1} \frac{1}{a^2 b^2} = \left(\sum_{a\geq 1}\frac{1}{a^2}\ri
ght)^{\!2} = \zeta(2)^2 = \frac{\pi^4}{36}.$$

Partition all pairs $(a,b)$ by their gcd $d = \gcd(a,b)$. Writing $a = d a'$, $b
 = d b'$ with $\gcd(a',b')=1$,

$$T = \sum_{d\geq 1}\;\sum_{\substack{a',b'\geq 1\\\gcd(a',b')=1}} \frac{1}{(d a
')^2 (d b')^2} = \sum_{d\geq 1}\frac{1}{d^4}\;\sum_{\substack{a',b'\geq 1\\\gcd(
a',b')=1}}\frac{1}{a'^2 b'^2} = \zeta(4)\cdot S.$$

**Solving for $S$.** Therefore

$$S = \frac{T}{\zeta(4)} = \frac{\zeta(2)^2}{\zeta(4)}.$$

Using the classical values $\zeta(2) = \pi^2/6$ and $\zeta(4) = \pi^4/90$,

$$S = \frac{(\pi^2/6)^2}{\pi^4/90} = \frac{\pi^4/36}{\pi^4/90} = \frac{90}{36} =
 \frac{5}{2}.$$
