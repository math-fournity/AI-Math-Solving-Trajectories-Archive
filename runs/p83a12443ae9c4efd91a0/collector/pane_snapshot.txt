## Proof

Consider the function

$$F(t) = \int_0^1 \frac{x^t - 1}{\log x}\,dx, \qquad t > -1.$$

We differentiate under the integral sign with respect to $t$. Justification: for
 $t$ in any compact subinterval of $(-1,\infty)$, the partial derivative $\frac{
\partial}{\partial t}\frac{x^t-1}{\log x} = x^t$ is continuous and bounded by an
 integrable function on $(0,1)$, so differentiation under the integral is valid.
 Thus

$$F'(t) = \int_0^1 \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\frac{x^t-1}{\log x}\right)
dx = \int_0^1 x^t\,dx = \frac{1}{t+1}.$$

Since $F(0) = \int_0^1 \frac{1-1}{\log x}\,dx = 0$, integrating gives

$$F(t) = \log(t+1).$$

The given integral is exactly $F(7)$:

$$\int_0^1 \frac{x^7 - 1}{\log x}\,dx = F(7) = \log 8 = 3\log 2.$$
