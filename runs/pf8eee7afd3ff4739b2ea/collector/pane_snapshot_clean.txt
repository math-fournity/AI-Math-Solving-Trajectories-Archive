## Proof

We claim the closed unit ball $B$ of $\ell^p(\mathbb{N})$ is weakly compact **if
 and only if $1 < p < \infty$**.

We use the following standard theorem (Kakutani's theorem): *A Banach space $X$
is reflexive if and only if its closed unit ball is weakly compact.* Combined wi
th the Eberlein–Šmulian theorem (weak compactness $\iff$ weak sequential compact
ness for the unit ball of a Banach space), this reduces the problem to determini
ng reflexivity of $\ell^p(\mathbb{N})$.

---

### Case 1: $1 < p < \infty$ — reflexive, hence weakly compact.

Let $q$ be the conjugate exponent, $\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1$. Since $1<p<\inft
y$, also $1<q<\infty$. The classical duality theorem gives an isometric isomorph
ism
$$(\ell^p)^* \cong \ell^q.$$
Applying it again,
$$(\ell^p)^{**} \cong (\ell^q)^* \cong \ell^p,$$
and the canonical embedding $J:\ell^p\to(\ell^p)^{**}$ is exactly this identific
ation (one checks on standard basis vectors $e_n$ that $J$ maps onto the copy of
 $\ell^p$ inside $(\ell^p)^{**}$). Hence $J$ is surjective, so $\ell^p$ is refle
xive. By Kakutani's theorem, $B$ is weakly compact.

---

### Case 2: $p=1$ — not weakly compact.

We show $B$ is not even weakly sequentially compact. Consider the standard unit
vectors $e_n\in\ell^1$, where $e_n(k)=\delta_{nk}$. Each $\|e_n\|_1=1$, so $\{e_
n\}\subset B$.

**Schur's theorem** states that in $\ell^1$, every weakly convergent sequence is
 norm-convergent. Now for $n\neq m$,
$$\|e_n-e_m\|_1 = \|e_n\|_1+\|e_m\|_1 = 2,$$
so no subsequence of $(e_n)$ is norm-Cauchy, hence no subsequence is norm-conver
gent. By Schur's theorem, no subsequence is weakly convergent. Thus $B$ is not w
eakly sequentially compact, and by Eberlein–Šmulian, $B$ is not weakly compact.

(Equivalently, $\ell^1$ is not reflexive: $(\ell^1)^*=\ell^\infty$, and $(\ell^\
infty)^*$ is the ba space, strictly larger than $\ell^1$ — e.g. the Banach limit
 functional lies in $(\ell^\infty)^*\setminus\ell^1$.)

---

### Case 3: $p=\infty$ — not weakly compact.

$\ell^\infty$ is not reflexive. Indeed $(\ell^\infty)^*$ is the space of bounded
 finitely-additive measures (the ba space), which strictly contains $\ell^1$. Fo
r instance, a **Banach limit** $L\in(\ell^\infty)^*$ is a continuous linear func
tional extending the ordinary limit from the convergent sequences to all of $\el
l^\infty$, is shift-invariant, and positive. Such an $L$ cannot be represented b
y any $\ell^1$ sequence: if $L(x)=\sum a_k x_k$ for some $a\in\ell^1$, then appl
ying shift-invariance $L(x)=L(Sx)$ to $x=e_n$ gives $a_n = a_{n+1}$ for all $n$,
 so $a$ is constant; but the only $\ell^1$ constant sequence is $0$, contradicti
ng $L(\mathbf{1})=1$. Hence $(\ell^\infty)^*\supsetneq\ell^1$, so $(\ell^\infty)
^{**}\supsetneq\ell^\infty$, and $\ell^\infty$ is not reflexive. By Kakutani, $B
$ is not weakly compact.

---

### Conclusion

The closed unit ball of $\ell^p(\mathbb{N})$ is weakly compact precisely for
$$\boxed{1 < p < \infty}.$$
