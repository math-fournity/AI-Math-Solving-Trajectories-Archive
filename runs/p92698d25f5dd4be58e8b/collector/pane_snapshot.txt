We compute $\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[x]} \mathbb{Z}$, where the first (rig
ht) $\mathbb{Z}$-factor carries the $\mathbb{Z}[x]$-module structure via $\phi_n
(f) = f(n)$, and the second (left) $\mathbb{Z}$-factor carries it via $\phi_m(f)
 = f(m)$.

### Step 1: Identify the modules as quotients of $\mathbb{Z}[x]$

Let $R = \mathbb{Z}[x]$. The evaluation map $\phi_n : R \to \mathbb{Z}$, $f \map
sto f(n)$, is a surjective ring homomorphism with kernel $(x - n)$. Hence, as an
 $R$-module,

$$\mathbb{Z} \cong R/(x - n),$$

where $x$ acts on $\mathbb{Z}$ by multiplication by $n$. Likewise,

$$\mathbb{Z} \cong R/(x - m),$$

where $x$ acts by multiplication by $m$.

### Step 2: Reduce the tensor product to a quotient

Using the standard isomorphism for quotient modules,

$$R/I \otimes_R R/J \;\cong\; R/(I + J),$$

we obtain

$$\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[x]} \mathbb{Z} \;\cong\; R/(x-n) \otimes_R R/(x
-m) \;\cong\; R\big/\big((x-n) + (x-m)\big) = R/(x-n,\, x-m).$$

### Step 3: Simplify the ideal $(x-n,\, x-m)$

Observe that

$$x - m = (x - n) + (n - m),$$

so the ideal $(x-n,\, x-m)$ contains the constant $n - m$. Conversely, $(x-n,\,
n-m)$ clearly contains $x - m = (x-n) + (n-m)$. Therefore

$$(x-n,\, x-m) = (x-n,\, n-m).$$

### Step 4: Compute the quotient

In the quotient $R/(x-n,\, n-m)$, the generator $x$ is identified with $n$ (from
 $x - n = 0$), reducing $R$ to $\mathbb{Z}$, and the remaining relation is $n -
m = 0$. Thus

$$R/(x-n,\, n-m) \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}/(n-m)\mathbb{Z}.$$

### Verification via the defining relation

Directly from the tensor product relations: for $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}$,

$$a \cdot x \otimes b = a \otimes x \cdot b \implies na \otimes b = a \otimes mb
,$$

so $(n-m)(a \otimes b) = 0$ for all $a, b$. Setting $a = b = 1$ shows the genera
tor $1 \otimes 1$ is annihilated by $n - m$, and there are no further relations
(every element is an integer multiple of $1 \otimes 1$). This confirms the resul
t.

---

### Result

$$\boxed{\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[x]} \mathbb{Z} \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}/(n-m
)\mathbb{Z}}$$

In particular, the tensor product is $\mathbb{Z}$ when $n = m$, and $\mathbb{Z}/
d\mathbb{Z}$ (with $d = |n-m|$) when $n \neq m$.
