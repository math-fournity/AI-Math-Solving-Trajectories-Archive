# Proof: Density of $\operatorname{span}(C_0^\infty(0,T)\cdot V)$ in $L^2(0,T;V)
$

**Answer: Yes.**

Let $V$ be a Banach space (the standard setting for Bochner spaces; in particula
r the result holds when $V$ is a Hilbert space). Denote by $C_0^\infty(0,T)\cdot
 V$ the set of functions $t\mapsto \phi(t)\,v$ with $\phi\in C_0^\infty(0,T)$ an
d $v\in V$, and by $\operatorname{span}(C_0^\infty(0,T)\cdot V)$ the set of all
finite linear combinations thereof (i.e., the algebraic tensor product $C_0^\inf
ty(0,T)\otimes V$).

**Claim.** $\operatorname{span}(C_0^\infty(0,T)\cdot V)$ is dense in $L^2(0,T;V)
$.

---

### Step 1 — Simple functions are dense in $L^2(0,T;V)$

Let $u\in L^2(0,T;V)$. By the definition of Bochner measurability, $u$ is the a.
e. limit of a sequence of simple (finite-valued, strongly measurable) functions.
 Moreover, by the standard construction (see e.g. Diestel–Uhl, *Vector Measures*
, or Yosida), there exist simple functions $s_n:(0,T)\to V$ such that

$$s_n(t)\to u(t)\quad\text{a.e.},\qquad \|s_n(t)\|_V\le 2\|u(t)\|_V\quad\text{a.
e.}$$

Hence $\|s_n(t)-u(t)\|_V\le 3\|u(t)\|_V$ a.e., and since $t\mapsto 9\|u(t)\|_V^2
\in L^1(0,T)$, the Dominated Convergence Theorem gives

$$\|s_n-u\|_{L^2(0,T;V)}^2=\int_0^T\|s_n(t)-u(t)\|_V^2\,dt\;\longrightarrow\;0.$
$

So for any $\varepsilon>0$ there exists a simple function

$$s(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_i\,\chi_{E_i}(t),\qquad v_i\in V,\; E_i\subset(0,T)\text{
 measurable},$$

with $\|s-u\|_{L^2(0,T;V)}<\varepsilon/2$.

---

### Step 2 — Approximate each $\chi_{E_i}$ by a $C_0^\infty(0,T)$ function

It is a classical result that $C_0^\infty(0,T)$ is dense in $L^2(0,T)$ (via moll
ification of compactly supported $L^2$ approximants, which themselves approximat
e $\chi_{E_i}$ by the density of $C_c(0,T)$ in $L^2(0,T)$ and the density of $C_
0^\infty$ in $C_c$ in the uniform norm).

For each $i$ with $v_i\neq 0$, choose $\phi_i\in C_0^\infty(0,T)$ such that

$$\|\phi_i-\chi_{E_i}\|_{L^2(0,T)}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2N\|v_i\|_V}.$$

For indices with $v_i=0$, set $\phi_i=0$.

---

### Step 3 — Combine

Define

$$w(t):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\phi_i(t)\,v_i\;\in\;\operatorname{span}\bigl(C_0^\infty(0
,T)\cdot V\bigr).$$

Then, using the triangle inequality and the pointwise identity $\|(\phi_i(t)-\ch
i_{E_i}(t))v_i\|_V=|\phi_i(t)-\chi_{E_i}(t)|\,\|v_i\|_V$:

$$\|w-s\|_{L^2(0,T;V)}\le\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\phi_i-\chi_{E_i}\|_{L^2(0,T)}\,\|v_i\|
_V<\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\varepsilon}{2N}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.$$

---

### Step 4 — Conclusion

By the triangle inequality,

$$\|w-u\|_{L^2(0,T;V)}\le\|w-s\|_{L^2(0,T;V)}+\|s-u\|_{L^2(0,T;V)}<\frac{\vareps
ilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=\varepsilon.$$

Since $u\in L^2(0,T;V)$ and $\varepsilon>0$ were arbitrary, every element of $L^
2(0,T;V)$ can be approximated to arbitrary precision by elements of $\operatorna
me{span}(C_0^\infty(0,T)\cdot V)$. $\qquad\blacksquare$
