## Solution

**Setup.** Let day 1=Mon … 5=Fri. Each day exactly 3 present / 2 absent, so tota
l absences = 5×2 = 10. For each child, the 5‑day attendance string has no two co
nsecutive absences ("AA") and no three consecutive presences ("PPP").

**Possible absence counts per person.** Enumerating binary strings of length 5 w
ith no "AA" and no "PPP":
- 1 absence: only `11011` (absent Wed).
- 2 absences: five patterns.
- 3 absences: only `01010` (absent Mon, Wed, Fri).
- 0 or ≥4 impossible.

**Elena vs Boris.** Elena missed two more days than Boris, and max absences = 3,
 so Boris = 1, Elena = 3.
- Elena: absent Mon, Wed, Fri; present Tue, Thu.
- Boris: only 1‑absence pattern `11011` → absent Wed; present Mon, Tue, Thu, Fri
.

Wednesday's two absentees are therefore {Boris, Elena}, so **Wed present = {Alex
ander, Sergey, Daria}**.

**Monday.** Sergey present (given); Elena absent Mon; Boris present Mon (only ab
sent Wed). To avoid Alexander & Sergey both present on a day other than the uniq

**Tuesday.** Alexander was absent Mon ⇒ must be present Tue (no "AA"). Sergey an

secutive present (Mon–Tue–Wed), forbidden. So both Sergey and Daria are absent T

**Thursday & Friday.** Elena: present Thu, absent Fri. Boris: present Thu, Fri.
- Alexander: present Tue & Wed; if present Thu → three consecutive, so **Alexand
er absent Thu**, hence **present Fri** (no "AA").
- Thursday needs one of {S, D} present; Friday needs one of {S, D} present (sinc

*Case B — Daria present Thu, Sergey present Fri:* Then Friday has Alexander & Se

one day." Forbidden.

So only **Case A** survives.
