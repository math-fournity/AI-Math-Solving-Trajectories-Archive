## Proof

**Setup and definitions.** Let $f:(X,A)\to(Y,B)$ and $g:(Y,B)\to(Z,C)$ be contin
uous maps of pairs, so $f:X\to Y$ with $f(A)\subseteq B$ and $g:Y\to Z$ with $g(
B)\subseteq C$. The composition $g\circ f:(X,A)\to(Z,C)$ is again a map of pairs
 since $(g\circ f)(A)=g(f(A))\subseteq g(B)\subseteq C$.

For a space $X$, the singular chain group $S_k(X)$ is the free abelian group on
all singular $k$-simplices $\sigma:\Delta^k\to X$. A continuous map $f:X\to Y$ i
nduces a homomorphism $f_\sharp:S_k(X)\to S_k(Y)$ by post-composition on generat
ors:
$$f_\sharp(\sigma)=f\circ\sigma:\Delta^k\to Y,$$
extended linearly to all chains. Since $f(A)\subseteq B$, we have $f_\sharp(S_k(
A))\subseteq S_k(B)$, so $f_\sharp$ descends to the quotient:
$$f_\sharp:S_k(X,A)=S_k(X)/S_k(A)\longrightarrow S_k(Y)/S_k(B)=S_k(Y,B),$$
defined by $f_\sharp([\alpha])=[f_\sharp(\alpha)]$, where $[\alpha]=\alpha+S_k(A
)$ denotes the coset. This is well-defined: if $[\alpha]=[\alpha']$, then $\alph
a-\alpha'\in S_k(A)$, so $f_\sharp(\alpha)-f_\sharp(\alpha')=f_\sharp(\alpha-\al
pha')\in f_\sharp(S_k(A))\subseteq S_k(B)$, hence $[f_\sharp(\alpha)]=[f_\sharp(
\alpha')]$.

**Step 1: Functoriality on absolute chains.** Let $\sigma:\Delta^k\to X$ be a si
ngular $k$-simplex, a generator of $S_k(X)$. Then:
$$g_\sharp\bigl(f_\sharp(\sigma)\bigr)=g_\sharp(f\circ\sigma)=g\circ(f\circ\sigm
a)=(g\circ f)\circ\sigma=(g\circ f)_\sharp(\sigma).$$

Since $g_\sharp\circ f_\sharp$ and $(g\circ f)_\sharp$ agree on every generator
$\sigma$ of the free abelian group $S_k(X)$, by linearity they agree on all of $
S_k(X)$:
$$(g\circ f)_\sharp = g_\sharp\circ f_\sharp \quad\text{on } S_k(X).\tag{$\ast$}
$$

**Step 2: Functoriality on relative chains.** Let $[\alpha]\in S_k(X,A)$ be an a
rbitrary element, represented by $\alpha\in S_k(X)$. Using the definition of the
 induced map on quotients and then Step 1:
$$(g_\sharp\circ f_\sharp)([\alpha])=g_\sharp\bigl(f_\sharp([\alpha])\bigr)=g_\s
harp\bigl([f_\sharp(\alpha)]\bigr)=\bigl[g_\sharp\bigl(f_\sharp(\alpha)\bigr)\bi
gr]=\bigl[(g\circ f)_\sharp(\alpha)\bigr]=(g\circ f)_\sharp([\alpha]).$$

The key equality $g_\sharp(f_\sharp(\alpha))=(g\circ f)_\sharp(\alpha)$ in $S_k(
Z)$ is exactly $(\ast)$, applied to the chain $\alpha\in S_k(X)$.

Since $[\alpha]$ was arbitrary, we conclude:
$$(g\circ f)_\sharp = g_\sharp\circ f_\sharp:S_k(X,A)\longrightarrow S_k(Z,C)$$

for every $k$.

**Step 3: Chain map compatibility (context).** Each $f_\sharp$ commutes with the
 boundary operator: $\partial\circ f_\sharp = f_\sharp\circ\partial$, since for
a generator $\sigma:\Delta^k\to X$,
$$\partial f_\sharp(\sigma)=\partial(f\circ\sigma)=\sum_{i=0}^k(-1)^i(f\circ\sig
ma)\circ\delta^i=\sum_{i=0}^k(-1)^i f\circ(\sigma\circ\delta^i)=f_\sharp\!\left(
\sum_{i=0}^k(-1)^i\sigma\circ\delta^i\right)=f_\sharp(\partial\sigma),$$
where $\delta^i:\Delta^{k-1}\to\Delta^k$ is the $i$-th face map. The same holds
for $g_\sharp$ and $(g\circ f)_\sharp$, and the boundary on relative chains is i
nduced from the absolute one. Thus each $f_\sharp$, $g_\sharp$, and $(g\circ f)_
\sharp$ is a chain map, and the equality $(g\circ f)_\sharp=g_\sharp\circ f_\sha
rp$ is an equality of chain maps $S_*(X,A)\to S_*(Z,C)$.

---

**Conclusion.** The induced chain map on singular chain complexes of pairs is fu
nctorial: for continuous maps of pairs $(X,A)\xrightarrow{f}(Y,B)\xrightarrow{g}
(Z,C)$,
$$(g\circ f)_\sharp = g_\sharp\circ f_\sharp:S_k(X,A)\longrightarrow S_k(Z,C)$$
for all $k\ge 0$. The proof reduces, via the definition of relative chains as qu
otients, to the elementary fact that post-composition of singular simplices sati
sfies $g\circ(f\circ\sigma)=(g\circ f)\circ\sigma$, which is associativity of fu
nction composition. $\blacksquare$
