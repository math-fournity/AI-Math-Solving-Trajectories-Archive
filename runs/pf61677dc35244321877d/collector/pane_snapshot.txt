**IMO 2007 N6.** Fix $n > 1$, and let $a, b$ be positive integers such that $nab
 - 1 \mid (na^2 - 1)^2$. Prove that $a = b$.

## Step 1: Reduction to $nab - 1 \mid (a-b)^2$

We compute $na^2 - 1$ modulo $d := nab - 1$. Observe:
$$b(na^2 - 1) = na^2 b - b, \qquad a(nab - 1) = na^2 b - a,$$
so $b(na^2 - 1) - a(nab - 1) = a - b$, giving
$$b(na^2 - 1) \equiv a - b \pmod{d}.$$

**Claim:** $\gcd(b, d) = 1$. Indeed, any common divisor $g$ of $b$ and $nab-1$ d
ivides $b$, hence divides $nab$, hence divides $nab - (nab-1) = 1$.

Since $b$ is invertible modulo $d$:
$$na^2 - 1 \equiv b^{-1}(a - b) \pmod{d},$$
so $(na^2 - 1)^2 \equiv b^{-2}(a-b)^2 \pmod d$. The divisibility $d \mid (na^2-1
)^2$ is therefore equivalent to
$$\boxed{nab - 1 \mid (a - b)^2}.$$

## Step 2: Setup for descent

Assume for contradiction that $a \neq b$. By symmetry of $(a-b)^2$ and $nab$, we
 may assume $a > b \geq 1$. Set $c = a - b \geq 1$. There exists a positive inte
ger $k$ with
$$(a - b)^2 = k(nab - 1). \tag{$\star$}$$

Rewriting with $a = b + c$:
$$c^2 = k\bigl(n b(b+c) - 1\bigr) = knb^2 + knbc - k.$$

## Step 3: Vieta jumping

View $(\star)$ as a quadratic in $a$:
$$a^2 - (2 + kn)\,b\,a + (b^2 + k) = 0. \tag{$\star\star$}$$

This is equivalent to $(a - b)^2 = k(nab - 1)$. Let $a'$ be the other root. By V
ieta's formulas:
$$a + a' = (2 + kn)b, \qquad a \cdot a' = b^2 + k.$$

Since $a$ is an integer root of a monic polynomial with integer coefficients, $a
' = (b^2 + k)/a$ is an integer. Since $a \cdot a' = b^2 + k > 0$ and $a > 0$, we
 get $a' \geq 1$.

**The pair $(a', b)$ satisfies the same equation.** Indeed $a'$ is a root of $(\
star\star)$, which is exactly $(x - b)^2 = k(nxb - 1)$, so $(a' - b)^2 = k(na'b
- 1)$. Note $na'b - 1 \geq n \cdot 1 \cdot 1 - 1 = n - 1 \geq 1 > 0$ (as $n \geq
 2$), so this is a valid divisibility relation with the same positive integer $k

## Step 4: $a' \neq b$

into $(\star)$: $c^2 = bc(nab - 1)$, so $c = b(nab - 1)$, i.e., $a - b = nab^2 -

 \geq 1$, we have $nb^2 - 1 \geq 1 > 0$, forcing $k = 0$, contradicting $k \geq
1$. Hence $a' \neq b$.

## Step 5: $a' < a$ (the descent)

We need $a' < a$, i.e., $b^2 + k < a^2$, i.e., $k < a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b) =

From $(\star)$: $k = c^2/(nab - 1)$, so it suffices to show
$$\frac{c}{nab - 1} < a + b \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c < (a + b)(nab - 1).$

Since $b \geq 1$, we have $a + b > a - b = c$. Since $n \geq 2, a \geq 2, b \geq
 1$, we have $nab - 1 \geq 4 - 1 = 3 \geq 1$. Therefore
$$(a + b)(nab - 1) \geq a + b > c. \quad\checkmark$$
