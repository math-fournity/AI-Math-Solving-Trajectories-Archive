## Proof that $n = 24$

### Step 1: The inequality holds for $n = 24$

We use the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality in the form: for any unit vector $(u,v)$,
$$\sqrt{a^2 + b^2} \ge ua + vb \quad \text{for all real } a, b.$$

Choose three unit vectors:
- $(u_1, v_1) = \left(\frac{5}{13}, \frac{12}{13}\right)$, since $\frac{25+144}{
169}=1$. ✓
- $(u_2, v_2) = (u_3, v_3) = \left(\frac{7}{25}, \frac{24}{25}\right)$, since $\
frac{49+576}{625}=1$. ✓

Applying Cauchy–Schwarz:
$$\sqrt{a^2+b^2} \ge \frac{5a+12b}{13}, \qquad \sqrt{a^2+c^2} \ge \frac{7a+24c}{
25}, \qquad \sqrt{a^2+d^2} \ge \frac{7a+24d}{25}.$$

Multiply by the coefficients $26, 25, 25$ respectively:
$$26\sqrt{a^2+b^2} \ge 26\cdot\frac{5a+12b}{13} = 2(5a+12b) = 10a + 24b,$$
$$25\sqrt{a^2+c^2} \ge 25\cdot\frac{7a+24c}{25} = 7a + 24c,$$
$$25\sqrt{a^2+d^2} \ge 25\cdot\frac{7a+24d}{25} = 7a + 24d.$$

Summing:
$$26\sqrt{a^2+b^2}+25\sqrt{a^2+c^2}+25\sqrt{a^2+d^2} \ge (10a+24b)+(7a+24c)+(7a+
24d) = 24(a+b+c+d).$$

This is exactly the desired inequality for $n=24$. $\checkmark$

### Step 2: The inequality fails for $n = 25$

$$\sqrt{a^2+c^2} = \sqrt{1+\frac{576}{49}} = \sqrt{\frac{625}{49}} = \frac{25}{7

**Left-hand side** (with $n=25$):
$$27\cdot\frac{13}{5} + 2\cdot 26\cdot\frac{25}{7} = \frac{351}{5}+\frac{1300}{7

Since
$$\frac{8957}{35} < \frac{8975}{35},$$
the inequality is **violated** for $n=25$.

### Step 3: How the unit vectors were found (completeness of the argument)

The choice of unit vectors is forced by the requirement that the Cauchy–Schwarz
lower bound produces exactly $n(a+b+c+d)$. Setting $(n+2)v_1 = n$ and $(n+1)v_2

$$v_1 = \frac{n}{n+2}, \quad u_1 = \frac{2\sqrt{n+1}}{n+2}; \qquad v_2 = \frac{n

The condition $(n+2)u_1 + 2(n+1)u_2 \ge n$ becomes:
$$2\sqrt{n+1} + 2\sqrt{2n+1} \ge n.$$

At $n=24$: $2\sqrt{25}+2\sqrt{49} = 10+14 = 24 = n$ (exact equality). $\checkmar

At $n=25$: $2\sqrt{26}+2\sqrt{51} \approx 24.48 < 25$. $\checkmark$
