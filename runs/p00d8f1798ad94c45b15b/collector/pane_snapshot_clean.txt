**Claim.** Every $n$-dimensional real Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ is isomorphic t
o $(\mathbb{R}^n, [\cdot,\cdot])$ for some Lie bracket $[\cdot,\cdot]$ on $\math
bb{R}^n$.

**Proof.**

Let $(\mathfrak{g}, [\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})$ be an $n$-dimensional real Li
e algebra. By definition $\mathfrak{g}$ is an $n$-dimensional vector space over
$\mathbb{R}$, so there exists a linear isomorphism
$$\varphi : \mathfrak{g} \to \mathbb{R}^n.$$
(For instance, pick any basis $e_1,\dots,e_n$ of $\mathfrak{g}$ and let $\varphi
$ send each vector to its coordinate tuple.)

Define a binary operation $[\cdot,\cdot]$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$ by *transport of str
ucture*:
$$[x, y] \;:=\; \varphi\!\Big(\big[\varphi^{-1}(x),\,\varphi^{-1}(y)\big]_{\math
frak{g}}\Big), \qquad x,y \in \mathbb{R}^n.$$

We verify that $[\cdot,\cdot]$ is a Lie bracket on $\mathbb{R}^n$:

*Bilinearity.* For $a,b\in\mathbb{R}$ and $x_1,x_2,y\in\mathbb{R}^n$,
$$[ax_1+bx_2,\,y] = \varphi\big([\varphi^{-1}(ax_1+bx_2),\,\varphi^{-1}(y)]_{\ma
thfrak{g}}\big) = \varphi\big(a[\varphi^{-1}x_1,\varphi^{-1}y]_{\mathfrak{g}} +
b[\varphi^{-1}x_2,\varphi^{-1}y]_{\mathfrak{g}}\big) = a[x_1,y]+b[x_2,y],$$
using linearity of $\varphi$, $\varphi^{-1}$, and bilinearity of $[\cdot,\cdot]_
{\mathfrak{g}}$. Linearity in the second argument is identical.

*Antisymmetry.* $[x,y] = \varphi([\varphi^{-1}x,\varphi^{-1}y]_{\mathfrak{g}}) =
 \varphi(-[\varphi^{-1}y,\varphi^{-1}x]_{\mathfrak{g}}) = -[y,x]$.

*Jacobi identity.* For $x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}^n$,
$$[x,[y,z]] = \varphi\big([\varphi^{-1}x,\,[\varphi^{-1}y,\varphi^{-1}z]_{\mathf
rak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}\big),$$
so summing cyclically and using the Jacobi identity in $\mathfrak{g}$ gives
$$[x,[y,z]] + [y,[z,x]] + [z,[x,y]] = \varphi(0) = 0.$$

Thus $(\mathbb{R}^n, [\cdot,\cdot])$ is a real Lie algebra.

Finally, $\varphi$ is a Lie algebra isomorphism: it is a linear isomorphism by c
onstruction, and
$$\varphi([u,v]_{\mathfrak{g}}) = [\varphi(u),\varphi(v)]$$
holds for all $u,v\in\mathfrak{g}$ directly by the definition of $[\cdot,\cdot]$
 (substitute $x=\varphi(u)$, $y=\varphi(v)$).

Therefore every $n$-dimensional real Lie algebra is isomorphic to $(\mathbb{R}^n
, [\cdot,\cdot])$ for a suitable Lie bracket on $\mathbb{R}^n$. $\blacksquare$
