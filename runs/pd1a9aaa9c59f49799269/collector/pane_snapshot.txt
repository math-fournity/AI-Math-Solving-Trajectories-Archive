# Solution

We determine the Lie algebra representation $d\rho: \mathfrak{X}(M) \to \mathrm{
End}(C^\infty(M))$ induced by differentiating the group representation $\rho(F)(
f) = f \circ F^{-1}$ at the identity.

## Setup

The Lie algebra of $\mathrm{Diff}(M)$ is $\mathfrak{X}(M)$, the space of smooth
vector fields on $M$. The exponential map is realized by the flow: for $X \in \m
athfrak{X}(M)$, let $\Phi_t^X$ denote its flow, satisfying
$$\frac{d}{dt}\Phi_t^X(p) = X\bigl(\Phi_t^X(p)\bigr), \qquad \Phi_0^X = \mathrm{
id}_M.$$

The induced Lie algebra representation is defined by
$$d\rho(X)(f) := \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0} \rho\bigl(\Phi_t^X\bigr)(f).$$

## Computation

By the definition of $\rho$,
$$\rho\bigl(\Phi_t^X\bigr)(f) = f \circ \bigl(\Phi_t^X\bigr)^{-1}.$$

Since the flow of $X$ satisfies $\bigl(\Phi_t^X\bigr)^{-1} = \Phi_{-t}^X$ (the f
low reversed in time is the flow of $-X$), we obtain
$$\rho\bigl(\Phi_t^X\bigr)(f) = f \circ \Phi_{-t}^X.$$

Evaluating at a point $p \in M$ and differentiating:
$$d\rho(X)(f)(p) = \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0} f\bigl(\Phi_{-t}^X(p)\bigr).$
$

By the chain rule, with $\gamma(t) = \Phi_{-t}^X(p)$ satisfying $\gamma'(t) = -X
\bigl(\Phi_{-t}^X(p)\bigr)$ and $\gamma(0) = p$,
$$\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0} f\bigl(\Phi_{-t}^X(p)\bigr) = df_p\bigl(\gamma
'(0)\bigr) = df_p\bigl(-X(p)\bigr) = -Xf(p).$$

Therefore
$$d\rho(X)(f) = -Xf = -\mathcal{L}_X f.$$

## Verification of the Lie algebra homomorphism property

We confirm this is a Lie algebra anti-homomorphism (consistent with the conventi
on for right/left actions). For $X, Y \in \mathfrak{X}(M)$ and $f \in C^\infty(M
)$:
$$[d\rho(X), d\rho(Y)](f) = [-X, -Y](f) = [X,Y](f) \cdot (-1)^2 \cdot \text{sign
}.$$

More carefully: $d\rho(X)(f) = -Xf$, so
$$[d\rho(X), d\rho(Y)](f) = d\rho(X)\bigl(d\rho(Y)(f)\bigr) - d\rho(Y)\bigl(d\rh
o(X)(f)\bigr) = -X(-Yf) - (-Y)(-Xf) = XYf - YXf = [X,Y]f.$$

Thus $[d\rho(X), d\rho(Y)] = d\rho([X,Y])$, confirming $d\rho$ is a Lie algebra
homomorphism. (The sign works out because each $d\rho$ contributes a minus sign,
 and the commutator of two minus signs is positive — consistent with the fact th
at $\rho$ is a left representation via the inverse, which is the natural pullbac
k action on functions.)

## Conclusion

The action of $\mathfrak{X}(M)$ on $C^\infty(M)$ corresponding to the representa
tion $\rho(F)(f) = f \circ F^{-1}$ is

$$\boxed{d\rho(X)(f) = -Xf = -\mathcal{L}_X f,}$$

i.e., a vector field $X$ acts on a smooth function $f$ by the **negative of the
Lie derivative** of $f$ along $X$. This is the natural infinitesimal pullback ac
tion: since $\rho$ sends a diffeomorphism $F$ to the pullback by $F^{-1}$, diffe
rentiating at the identity along the flow of $X$ yields the directional derivati
ve of $f$ along $-X$.
