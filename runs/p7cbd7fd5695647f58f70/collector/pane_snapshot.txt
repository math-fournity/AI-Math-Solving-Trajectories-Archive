## Solution

We minimize $\int_0^1 y'^2\,dx$ subject to $y(0)=y(1)=0$ and $\int_0^1 y^2\,dx=1
$. This is an isoperimetric problem in the calculus of variations.

### Step 1. Necessary condition (Euler–Lagrange with a Lagrange multiplier)

Introduce a multiplier $\lambda$ and consider the augmented functional
$$
J[y]=\int_0^1\!\bigl(y'^2-\lambda\,y^2\bigr)\,dx.
$$
A minimizer of the constrained problem must satisfy the Euler–Lagrange equation
for $L=y'^2-\lambda y^2$:
$$
\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial y'}\right)-\frac{\partial L}{\par
tial y}=0
\;\Longrightarrow\;
2y''+2\lambda y=0
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\boxed{y''+\lambda y=0.}
$$

### Step 2. Solve the boundary-value problem

With $y(0)=y(1)=0$:

- If $\lambda<0$: $y=A\sinh(\sqrt{-\lambda}\,x)+B\cosh(\sqrt{-\lambda}\,x)$; $y(
0)=0\Rightarrow B=0$, and $y(1)=0\Rightarrow A\sinh(\sqrt{-\lambda})=0$, forcing
 $A=0$, which violates $\int y^2=1$.
- If $\lambda=0$: $y=Ax+B$, boundary conditions force $y\equiv 0$, again infeasi
ble.
- If $\lambda>0$: $y=A\sin(\sqrt{\lambda}\,x)+B\cos(\sqrt{\lambda}\,x)$. From $y
(0)=0$ we get $B=0$; from $y(1)=0$ we need $\sin(\sqrt{\lambda})=0$, i.e.
$$\sqrt{\lambda}=n\pi,\qquad n=1,2,3,\dots$$
so $\lambda_n=n^2\pi^2$ and $y_n(x)=A_n\sin(n\pi x)$.

### Step 3. Apply the constraint $\int_0^1 y^2\,dx=1$

$$
\int_0^1 A_n^2\sin^2(n\pi x)\,dx = A_n^2\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}=1
\;\Longrightarrow\;
A_n^2=2.
$$

### Step 4. Evaluate the objective

For $y_n(x)=A_n\sin(n\pi x)$ with $A_n^2=2$:
$$
\int_0^1 y_n'^2\,dx
=\int_0^1 A_n^2\,n^2\pi^2\cos^2(n\pi x)\,dx
=2\,n^2\pi^2\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}
=n^2\pi^2.
$$

This is minimized at $n=1$, giving $\pi^2$.

### Step 5. Verify minimality (sufficiency)

The candidate minimizer is $y_*(x)=\sqrt{2}\sin(\pi x)$. To confirm it is a true
 minimum, expand any admissible $y$ (with $y(0)=y(1)=0$, $\int y^2=1$) in the si
ne basis $\{\sqrt{2}\sin(n\pi x)\}_{n\ge 1}$, which is a complete orthonormal sy
stem for $L^2[0,1]$:
$$
y(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} c_n\,\sqrt{2}\sin(n\pi x),\qquad \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_n
^2=1.
$$
Then (by Parseval for derivatives, valid since $y\in H_0^1$)
$$
\int_0^1 y'^2\,dx=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^2\pi^2\,c_n^2\;\ge\;\pi^2\sum_{n=1}^{\in
fty}c_n^2=\pi^2,
$$
with equality iff $c_1=1$ and $c_n=0$ for $n\ge 2$, i.e. $y=y_*$.

### Conclusion

The minimum value is
$$\boxed{\pi^2}$$
attained uniquely at $y(x)=\sqrt{2}\,\sin(\pi x)$.
