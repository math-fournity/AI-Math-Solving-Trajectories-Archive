## Solution

**Setup.** Place the circle as the unit circle in the complex plane. A regular $
k$-gon inscribed in it has vertices forming a coset of $\mu_k$ (the $k$-th roots
 of unity). Write the three vertex sets as
$$A = \alpha\cdot\mu_{15},\quad B = \beta\cdot\mu_{21},\quad C = \gamma\cdot\mu_
{35}.$$
Multiplying all by $\alpha^{-1}$, we may assume $A = \mu_{15}$, $B = b\cdot\mu_{
21}$, $C = c\cdot\mu_{35}$ for some $b, c$ on the unit circle.

**Intersection sizes.** For cosets of root-of-unity subgroups, $x\cdot\mu_a \cap
 y\cdot\mu_b$ is either empty or a coset of $\mu_a\cap\mu_b = \mu_{\gcd(a,b)}$.
Hence:

| Pair | $\gcd$ | Size if nonempty | Nonempty condition |
|------|--------|------------------|--------------------|
| $A\cap B$ | $\gcd(15,21)=3$ | $3$ | $b \in \mu_{15}\mu_{21}=\mu_{\mathrm{lcm}(
15,21)}=\mu_{105}$ |
| $A\cap C$ | $\gcd(15,35)=5$ | $5$ | $c \in \mu_{\mathrm{lcm}(15,35)}=\mu_{105}
$ |
| $B\cap C$ | $\gcd(21,35)=7$ | $7$ | $c/b \in \mu_{\mathrm{lcm}(21,35)}=\mu_{10
5}$ |

The triple intersection, when nonempty, is a coset of $\mu_{\gcd(15,21,35)}=\mu_
1$, so it has size $1$.

Define indicators $X = [b\in\mu_{105}]$, $Y = [c\in\mu_{105}]$, $Z = [c/b\in\mu_
{105}]$.

**Key constraint: no "exactly two."** Since $\mu_{105}$ is a group, any two of $
X,Y,Z$ imply the third: e.g. $X\wedge Z \Rightarrow c = b\cdot(c/b)\in\mu_{105}\
Rightarrow Y$. So the feasible patterns for $(X,Y,Z)$ are:
$$(0,0,0),\quad (1,0,0),\quad (0,1,0),\quad (0,0,1),\quad (1,1,1).$$

**Triple intersection in case $(1,1,1)$.** Here $b,c\in\mu_{105}$, so all three
sets lie in $\mu_{105}\cong \mathbb{Z}/105\mathbb{Z}$. Under the isomorphism $e^
{2\pi i t/105}\leftrightarrow t\pmod{105}$:
- $A=\mu_{15}$: $t\equiv 0\pmod 7$,
- $B=b\cdot\mu_{21}$: $t\equiv \beta\pmod 5$,
- $C=c\cdot\mu_{35}$: $t\equiv \gamma\pmod 3$.

Since $105=3\cdot5\cdot7$ with $3,5,7$ pairwise coprime, CRT gives a **unique**
solution mod $105$ for any $\beta,\gamma$. So the triple intersection is **alway
s** nonempty (size $1$) whenever $(X,Y,Z)=(1,1,1)$.

**Computing $n$ via inclusion–exclusion.** With $|A|+|B|+|C|=15+21+35=71$:

| Pattern | $-|A\cap B|$ | $-|A\cap C|$ | $-|B\cap C|$ | $+|A\cap B\cap C|$ | $n
$ |
|---------|--------|--------|--------|--------|-----|
| $(0,0,0)$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | $71$ |
| $(1,0,0)$ | $3$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | $68$ |
| $(0,1,0)$ | $0$ | $5$ | $0$ | $0$ | $66$ |
| $(0,0,1)$ | $0$ | $0$ | $7$ | $0$ | $64$ |
| $(1,1,1)$ | $3$ | $5$ | $7$ | $1$ | $57$ |

**Each pattern is achievable.**
- $(0,0,0)$: $b=e^{2\pi i/107}$, $c=e^{2\pi i\cdot 2/107}$ — none of $b,c,c/b$ l
ies in $\mu_{105}$ (since $107$ is prime, $\gcd(107,105)=1$).
- $(1,0,0)$: $b=1$, $c=e^{2\pi i/107}$.
- $(0,1,0)$: $b=e^{2\pi i/107}$, $c=1$.
- $(0,0,1)$: $b=c=e^{2\pi i/107}$, so $c/b=1\in\mu_{105}$ but $b,c\notin\mu_{105
}$.
- $(1,1,1)$: $b=c=1$.

**Sum of possible values:**
$$71+68+66+64+57 = 326.$$
