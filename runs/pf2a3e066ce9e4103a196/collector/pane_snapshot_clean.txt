## Proof

**Reformulation.** The condition "$n \mid a^{\varphi(n)/p} - 1$ for all $(a,n)=1
$" means the exponent of $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times$ divides $\varphi(n)/p
$. This exponent is the **Carmichael function** $\lambda(n)$. Together with $p \
mid \varphi(n)$ (ensuring $\varphi(n)/p \in \mathbb{Z}$), the full condition is:

$$p \cdot \lambda(n) \mid \varphi(n). \tag{$\star$}$$

(Note: $p \cdot \lambda(n) \mid \varphi(n)$ automatically implies $p \mid \varph
i(n)$, so the hypothesis $p \mid \varphi(n)$ is redundant once $(\star)$ holds.)

**Setup.** Write $n = 2^a \cdot \prod_{i=1}^s q_i^{b_i}$ where $q_1,\ldots,q_s$
are distinct odd primes, $b_i \geq 1$, $a \geq 0$. Recall:
- $\varphi(q_i^{b_i}) = q_i^{b_i-1}(q_i-1)$ and $\lambda(q_i^{b_i}) = \varphi(q_
i^{b_i})$ for odd $q_i$;
- $\lambda(2^a) = \varphi(2^a)$ for $a \leq 2$, while $\lambda(2^a) = 2^{a-2} =
\varphi(2^a)/2$ for $a \geq 3$;
- $\varphi(n) = \prod \varphi(\text{prime powers})$, $\lambda(n) = \operatorname
{lcm}(\lambda(\text{prime powers}))$.

For any prime $r$, with $\alpha_j = v_r(\varphi(\text{component } j))$ and $\bet
a_j = v_r(\lambda(\text{component } j))$:

$$v_r\!\left(\frac{\varphi(n)}{\lambda(n)}\right) = \sum_j \alpha_j - \max_j \be
ta_j.$$

---

**Case 1: $p$ odd.** Since $\varphi(2^a)$ is a power of $2$ (or $1$), $v_p(\varp
hi(2^a)) = v_p(\lambda(2^a)) = 0$; the $2^a$ component is irrelevant. For odd $q
_i$, $\lambda = \varphi$, so:

$$v_p\!\left(\frac{\varphi(n)}{\lambda(n)}\right) = \sum_{i=1}^s v_p(\varphi(q_i
^{b_i})) - \max_{1\le i\le s} v_p(\varphi(q_i^{b_i})).$$

This is $\geq 1$ **iff at least two** of the $v_p(\varphi(q_i^{b_i}))$ are posit
ive, i.e., at least two prime power factors $q_i^{b_i}$ satisfy $p \mid \varphi(
q_i^{b_i})$.

Now $p \mid \varphi(q_i^{b_i}) = q_i^{b_i-1}(q_i-1)$ iff:
- **$q_i = p$ and $b_i \geq 2$** (then $p \mid p^{b_i-1}$), or
- **$q_i \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$** (then $p \mid q_i - 1$; note $q_i \neq p$ here sin
ce $q_i \equiv 1 \not\equiv 0$).

(The case $q_i = p, b_i = 1$ gives $\varphi(p) = p-1 \not\equiv 0 \pmod{p}$, so
it does **not** count.)

---

**Case 2: $p = 2$.** Every odd prime $q_i$ has $v_2(\varphi(q_i^{b_i})) = v_2(q_
i - 1) \geq 1$.

*Subcase 2a: $a \geq 3$.* Here $v_2(\varphi(2^a)) = a-1$, $v_2(\lambda(2^a)) = a
-2$:

$$v_2\!\left(\frac{\varphi(n)}{\lambda(n)}\right) = (a{-}1) + \textstyle\sum_i v
_2(\varphi_i) - \max\!\big(a{-}2,\, \max_i v_2(\varphi_i)\big).$$

- If $a-2 \geq \max_i v_2(\varphi_i)$: equals $1 + \sum_i v_2(\varphi_i) \geq 1$
. ✓
- If $a-2 < \max_i v_2(\varphi_i)$: equals $(a{-}1) + (\sum_i v_2(\varphi_i) - \
max_i v_2(\varphi_i)) \geq (a{-}1) \geq 2$. ✓

So $(\star)$ **always holds** when $a \geq 3$ (and $2 \mid \varphi(n)$ is automa
tic since $\varphi(2^a)=2^{a-1}\geq 4$).

*Subcase 2b: $a \leq 2$.* Here $\lambda(2^a) = \varphi(2^a)$, so all components
have $\lambda = \varphi$. The $2^a$ component contributes $v_2(\varphi(2^a)) = 0
$ for $a \leq 1$ and $= 1$ for $a = 2$:

$$v_2\!\left(\frac{\varphi(n)}{\lambda(n)}\right) = v_2(\varphi(2^a)) + \textsty
le\sum_i v_2(\varphi_i) - \max\!\big(v_2(\varphi(2^a)),\, \max_i v_2(\varphi_i)\
big).$$

This is $\geq 1$ iff at least two components have $v_2(\varphi) \geq 1$:
- **$a \leq 1$:** need $s \geq 2$ (at least two distinct odd prime factors).
- **$a = 2$:** need $s \geq 1$ (at least one odd prime factor, i.e., $m > 1$).

---

## Summary

The natural numbers $n$ satisfying the condition are precisely those with $p \cd
ot \lambda(n) \mid \varphi(n)$. Explicitly, writing $n = 2^a \cdot m$ with $m =
\prod_{i=1}^s q_i^{b_i}$ odd:

- **$p$ odd:** At least two prime power factors $q_i^{b_i}$ of $m$ satisfy $p \m
id \varphi(q_i^{b_i})$, i.e., ($q_i = p$ and $b_i \geq 2$) or ($q_i \equiv 1 \pm
od{p}$).

- **$p = 2$:** Either $a \geq 3$ (i.e., $8 \mid n$); or $a = 2$ and $s \geq 1$ (
i.e., $4 \| n$ and $n$ has an odd prime factor); or $s \geq 2$ (i.e., $n$ has at
 least two distinct odd prime factors).

**Verification of examples:**
- $p=3, n=91=7\cdot 13$: both $7\equiv 1$ and $13\equiv 1 \pmod{3}$ → works. $\l
ambda(91)=12 \mid 24=\varphi(91)/3$. ✓
- $p=3, n=7$: only one factor with $3\mid\varphi(7)=6$ → fails. Indeed $7\nmid 2
^2-1=3$. ✓
- $p=2, n=8$: $a=3\geq 3$ → works. $\lambda(8)=2\mid 2=\varphi(8)/2$, and $8\mid
 a^2-1$ for all odd $a$. ✓
- $p=2, n=6=2\cdot 3$: $a=1, s=1<2$ → fails. Indeed $6\nmid 5^1-1=4$. ✓
- $p=2, n=12=4\cdot 3$: $a=2, s=1\geq 1$ → works. $\lambda(12)=2\mid 2=\varphi(1
2)/2$. ✓
