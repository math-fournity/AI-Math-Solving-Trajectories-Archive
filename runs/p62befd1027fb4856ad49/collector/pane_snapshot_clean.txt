## Proof

Let $M = f(1) = \max_{[0,1]} f$. Since $f$ is nonnegative, continuous, and stric
tly increasing, for every $p>0$ the function $f^p(x) = (f(x))^p$ is continuous a
nd strictly increasing on $[0,1]$. Moreover
$$f(0)^p < \int_0^1 f^p(x)\,dx < M^p,$$
since a strictly increasing continuous function is not constant on any subinterv
al. Hence by the intermediate value theorem there is a unique $x_p\in(0,1)$ with
$$f^p(x_p)=\int_0^1 f^p(x)\,dx.$$

**Claim.** $\displaystyle\lim_{p\to\infty} x_p = 1$.

Fix any $\delta\in(0,1)$. We will show that $x_p > 1-\delta$ for all sufficientl
y large $p$.

Suppose, for contradiction, that $x_p \le 1-\delta$. Then by monotonicity
$$f^p(x_p) \le f(1-\delta)^p. \tag{1}$$
On the other hand, using that $f$ is increasing on $[1-\delta/2,\,1]$,
$$\int_0^1 f^p(x)\,dx \;\ge\; \int_{1-\delta/2}^{1} f^p(x)\,dx \;\ge\; \frac{\de
lta}{2}\, f\!\left(1-\tfrac{\delta}{2}\right)^p. \tag{2}$$
Combining (1), (2), and the defining equation $f^p(x_p)=\int_0^1 f^p$,
$$f(1-\delta)^p \;\ge\; \frac{\delta}{2}\, f\!\left(1-\tfrac{\delta}{2}\right)^p
,$$
i.e.
$$\left(\frac{f(1-\delta)}{f(1-\delta/2)}\right)^p \;\ge\; \frac{\delta}{2}.$$

Because $f$ is strictly increasing, $f(1-\delta) < f(1-\delta/2)$, and both valu
es are positive (note $1-\delta/2>0$ for $\delta<1$, and $f$ is strictly increas
ing with $f(0)\ge 0$, so $f(1-\delta/2)>0$). Thus the ratio $r := f(1-\delta)/f(
1-\delta/2)$ lies in $(0,1)$, and $r^p \to 0$ as $p\to\infty$. This contradicts
$r^p \ge \delta/2 > 0$ for large $p$.

Therefore $x_p > 1-\delta$ for all sufficiently large $p$. Since $\delta\in(0,1)
$ was arbitrary and $x_p<1$ always, we conclude
$$\lim_{p\to\infty} x_p = 1.$$
