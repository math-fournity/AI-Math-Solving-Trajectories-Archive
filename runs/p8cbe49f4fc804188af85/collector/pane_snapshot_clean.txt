## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, the range of a real-coefficient bivariate polynomial can be exac
tly the open interval $(0,+\infty)$.

**Construction.** Consider
$$p(x,y) = (xy - 1)^2 + x^2.$$

**Step 1: $p(x,y) > 0$ for all $(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^2$.**

Since $p$ is a sum of two squares, $p(x,y)\ge 0$. Equality $p(x,y)=0$ would requ
ire simultaneously $x^2=0$ and $(xy-1)^2=0$, i.e. $x=0$ and $xy=1$. But $x=0$ gi
ves $xy=0\neq 1$, a contradiction. Hence $p(x,y)>0$ everywhere, so
$$\operatorname{range}(p)\subseteq (0,+\infty).$$

**Step 2: Every positive value is attained.**

Let $v\in(0,+\infty)$ be arbitrary. Set
$$x = \sqrt{v},\qquad y = \frac{1}{\sqrt{v}}.$$
Then $xy = \sqrt{v}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{v}} = 1$, so $(xy-1)^2 = 0$, and $x^2 = v
$. Therefore
$$p\!\left(\sqrt{v},\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{v}}\right) = 0 + v = v.$$
Thus every $v>0$ belongs to the range, giving
$$(0,+\infty)\subseteq \operatorname{range}(p).$$

**Conclusion.** Combining both inclusions,
$$\operatorname{range}(p) = (0,+\infty).$$

Therefore the range of a real-coefficient bivariate polynomial **can** be the op
en interval $(0,+\infty)$. $\blacksquare$
