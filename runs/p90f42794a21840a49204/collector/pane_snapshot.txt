## Proof

Let
$$X = B(t) - B(s), \qquad Y = B(v) - B(u).$$

By the defining properties of Brownian motion, increments over disjoint time int
ervals are independent. Since $0 < s < t < u < v$, the intervals $[s,t]$ and $[u
,v]$ are disjoint, so $X$ and $Y$ are independent.

Moreover, $X \sim N(0,\, t-s)$ and $Y \sim N(0,\, v-u)$, hence
$$\mathbb{E}[X^2] = t-s, \qquad \mathbb{E}[Y^2] = v-u.$$

By independence of $X$ and $Y$, the random variables $X^2$ and $Y^2$ are also in
dependent, so
$$\mathbb{E}[X^2 Y^2] = \mathbb{E}[X^2]\,\mathbb{E}[Y^2] = (t-s)(v-u).$$

Therefore
$$\operatorname{Cov}\!\left[(B(t)-B(s))^2,\,(B(v)-B(u))^2\right] = \mathbb{E}[X^
2 Y^2] - \mathbb{E}[X^2]\mathbb{E}[Y^2] = (t-s)(v-u) - (t-s)(v-u) = 0.$$
