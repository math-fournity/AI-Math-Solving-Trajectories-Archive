The statement is **false**. I'll provide a counterexample and clarify the concep
tual confusion underlying the question.

## Proof

**Clarifying the question.** The fundamental group $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ is, by defini
tion, the set of path-homotopy classes of loops based at $x_0$, equipped with th
e operation of concatenation. Thus each *element* of $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ is already
a path-homotopy class. To say "all elements are path homotopic" is to say that a
ll loops based at $x_0$ lie in the same homotopy class, i.e., that $\pi_1(X, x_0
)$ is the **trivial** group $\{e\}$.

So the question reduces to:

> *If $\pi_1(X)$ is abelian, must $\pi_1(X)$ be trivial?*

This is false.

**Counterexample: the circle $S^1$.** Take $X = S^1$, the unit circle in $\mathb
b{R}^2$, with basepoint $x_0 = (1,0)$. It is a classical result (via the coverin
g map $\mathbb{R} \to S^1$, $t \mapsto e^{2\pi i t}$) that

$$\pi_1(S^1, x_0) \cong \mathbb{Z}.$$

The isomorphism sends a loop to its **winding number** (the integer recording ho
w many times the loop winds around the circle, with sign).

**$\mathbb{Z}$ is abelian.** Addition of integers is commutative, so $\pi_1(S^1)
$ is abelian.

**$\mathbb{Z}$ is not trivial.** The integers $0$ and $1$ are distinct. Concrete
ly:
- The constant loop $c(t) = (1,0)$ has winding number $0$.
- The loop $\ell(t) = e^{2\pi i t}$ has winding number $1$.

These two loops are **not** path homotopic: if they were, lifting the homotopy t
o the universal cover $\mathbb{R}$ would give a homotopy between the constant pa
th at $0$ and the path $t \mapsto t$, which is impossible since they have differ
ent endpoints ($0$ vs. $1$) in the cover, and a path homotopy fixes endpoints.

Therefore $\pi_1(S^1)$ is abelian yet contains distinct (non-path-homotopic) ele
ments.

**Why the confusion arises.** There is a related *true* statement that the quest
ion may be conflating with the false one:

> $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ is abelian **if and only if** for any two loops $f, g$ based a
t $x_0$, the concatenated loops $f \cdot g$ and $g \cdot f$ are path homotopic.

This says the *group operation* is commutative ($[f][g] = [g][f]$, i.e., $[f \cd
ot g] = [g \cdot f]$), **not** that all elements are equal ($[f] = [g]$). Commut
ativity of the operation is a far weaker condition than triviality of the group.
 The circle $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ illustrates exactly this distinction:
concatenation of loops commutes up to homotopy (winding numbers add in either or
der), yet loops with different winding numbers remain in distinct homotopy class
es.

**Conclusion.** The implication "abelian $\Rightarrow$ all elements path homotop
ic (trivial)" fails. The circle $S^1$ with $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ is an e
xplicit counterexample: an abelian, non-trivial fundamental group.
