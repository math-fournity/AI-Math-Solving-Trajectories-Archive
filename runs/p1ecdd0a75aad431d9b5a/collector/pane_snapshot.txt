## Proof

Let $M$ be a positive definite matrix. Define
$$f(M) = \operatorname{Tr}(M\log M - M).$$

We compute the first variation. Let $H$ be an arbitrary symmetric matrix (the ta
ngent direction in the space of symmetric matrices, since $M$ is symmetric posit
ive definite). We evaluate
$$\delta f = \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0} f(M + tH).$$

**Step 1. Variation of $\operatorname{Tr}(M)$.** This is linear:
$$\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\operatorname{Tr}(M+tH) = \operatorname{Tr}(H).
$$

**Step 2. Variation of $\operatorname{Tr}(M\log M)$.** We use the integral repre
sentation of the matrix logarithm. For positive definite $M$,
$$\log M = \int_0^\infty \left[\frac{1}{1+s}\,I - (M+sI)^{-1}\right]ds.$$

Therefore
$$\operatorname{Tr}(M\log M) = \int_0^\infty \left[\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(M)}{1
+s} - \operatorname{Tr}\!\big(M(M+sI)^{-1}\big)\right]ds.$$

Now vary $M \mapsto M+tH$. The first term gives $\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(H)}{1+s
}$. For the second term, using $\frac{d}{dt}A(t)^{-1} = -A^{-1}A'A^{-1}$ and the
 product rule:
$$\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{Tr}\!\big((M+tH)(M+tH+sI)^{-1}\big)\bigg|_{t=0}$$
$$= \operatorname{Tr}\!\big(H(M+sI)^{-1}\big) - \operatorname{Tr}\!\big(M(M+sI)^
{-1}H(M+sI)^{-1}\big).$$

Using cyclicity of trace, $\operatorname{Tr}(M(M+sI)^{-1}H(M+sI)^{-1}) = \operat
orname{Tr}\!\big((M+sI)^{-1}M(M+sI)^{-1}H\big)$. Combining both terms under one
trace with $H$:
$$\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(\left[(M+sI)^{-1} - (M+sI)^{-1}M(M+sI)^{-1}\right]H\r
ight).$$

Since $(M+sI)^{-1}M(M+sI)^{-1} = (M+sI)^{-1}\big[(M+sI)-sI\big](M+sI)^{-1} = (M+
sI)^{-1} - s(M+sI)^{-2}$, the bracket becomes
$$(M+sI)^{-1} - \big[(M+sI)^{-1} - s(M+sI)^{-2}\big] = s(M+sI)^{-2}.$$

So the variation of the second term is
$$\int_0^\infty \operatorname{Tr}\!\big(s(M+sI)^{-2}H\big)\,ds.$$

Subtracting (recall the integrand has a minus sign for the second term in $\oper
atorname{Tr}(M\log M)$), the total variation of $\operatorname{Tr}(M\log M)$ is
$$\delta\,\operatorname{Tr}(M\log M) = \int_0^\infty \operatorname{Tr}\!\left(\l
eft[\frac{1}{1+s}I - s(M+sI)^{-2}\right]H\right)ds.$$

**Step 3. Evaluate the integral.** We claim
$$\int_0^\infty \left[\frac{1}{1+s}I - s(M+sI)^{-2}\right]ds = \log M + I.$$

The first part: $\int_0^\infty \frac{1}{1+s}\,ds\cdot I$ diverges, but the diver
gence cancels with the second term. To see the cancellation cleanly, diagonalize
 $M = U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i)U^\top$ with $\lambda_i > 0$. Each eigenval
ue contributes
$$\int_0^\infty \left[\frac{1}{1+s} - \frac{s}{(\lambda_i+s)^2}\right]ds.$$

Compute the second integral: $\int_0^R \frac{s}{(\lambda+s)^2}\,ds$. Writing $s
= (\lambda+s)-\lambda$,
$$\frac{s}{(\lambda+s)^2} = \frac{1}{\lambda+s} - \frac{\lambda}{(\lambda+s)^2},
$$
so
$$\int_0^R \frac{s}{(\lambda+s)^2}\,ds = \big[\log(\lambda+s)\big]_0^R + \frac{\
lambda}{\lambda+R} - 1 = \log(\lambda+R) - \log\lambda + \frac{\lambda}{\lambda+
R} - 1.$$

And $\int_0^R \frac{1}{1+s}\,ds = \log(1+R)$.

So the combined integral up to $R$ is
$$\log(1+R) - \log(\lambda+R) + \log\lambda - \frac{\lambda}{\lambda+R} + 1.$$

As $R\to\infty$: $\log(1+R)-\log(\lambda+R) \to 0$, $\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+R}\t
o 0$, leaving
$$\log\lambda + 1.$$

Thus each eigenvalue contributes $\log\lambda_i + 1$, i.e.
$$\delta\,\operatorname{Tr}(M\log M) = \operatorname{Tr}\!\big((\log M + I)\,H\b
ig).$$

**Step 4. Combine.**
$$\delta f = \delta\,\operatorname{Tr}(M\log M) - \delta\,\operatorname{Tr}(M) =
 \operatorname{Tr}\!\big((\log M + I)H\big) - \operatorname{Tr}(H) = \operatorna
me{Tr}\!\big((\log M)H\big).$$

**Step 5. Identify the gradient.** By definition of the (Fréchet) derivative, $\
delta f = \operatorname{Tr}\!\big((\nabla_M f)\,H\big)$ for all symmetric $H$. S
ince $\log M$ is symmetric (as $M$ is positive definite), we identify
$$\nabla_M f = \log M.$$

Therefore the derivative of $f(M) = \operatorname{Tr}(M\log M - M)$ with respect
 to $M$ is $\log M$. $\blacksquare$
