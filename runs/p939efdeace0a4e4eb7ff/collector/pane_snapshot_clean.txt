## Proof

**Claim.** Every conformal diffeomorphism of a closed hyperbolic surface $\Sigma
_g$ of genus $g \geq 2$ is an isometry.

**Setup.** Let $\Sigma_g$ be a closed oriented surface of genus $g \geq 2$ equip
ped with a hyperbolic metric $h$ (a Riemannian metric of constant Gaussian curva
ture $-1$). Let $f \colon \Sigma_g \to \Sigma_g$ be a conformal diffeomorphism,
i.e. a diffeomorphism such that $f^*h = \lambda \, h$ for some smooth positive f
unction $\lambda$ on $\Sigma_g$.

**Key uniqueness fact (Uniformization).** On a closed surface of genus $g \geq 2
$, given a conformal structure $[h]$, there is a **unique** Riemannian metric of
 constant curvature $-1$ within the conformal class $[h]$. This is the hyperboli
c metric furnished by the Uniformization Theorem: the universal cover is $\mathb
b{H}^2$, and the hyperbolic metric descends; uniqueness follows from the fact th
at any two constant-curvature $-1$ metrics in the same conformal class differ by
 a conformal self-map of the universal cover fixing the deck group, which must b
e an isometry of $\mathbb{H}^2$.

**The argument.** Since $f$ is conformal, $f$ preserves the conformal structure:
 $f^*[h] = [h]$. Consider the pullback metric $f^*h$. Because $f$ is conformal,
$f^*h$ lies in the same conformal class as $h$. Moreover, $f^*h$ has constant Ga
ussian curvature $-1$, since curvature $-1$ is preserved under pullback by any d
iffeomorphism (Gaussian curvature is a diffeomorphism invariant for surfaces: $K
_{f^*h} = K_h \circ f = -1$).

Thus $f^*h$ is a constant-curvature $(-1)$ metric in the conformal class $[h]$.
By the uniqueness statement above, $f^*h = h$. Therefore $\lambda \equiv 1$, and
 $f$ is an isometry.

**Conclusion.** Every conformal diffeomorphism of a closed hyperbolic surface of
 genus $g \geq 2$ is an isometry. $\blacksquare$
