## Proof

Consider the functional
$$J(f) = \int_0^1 \bigl(f'(x)^2 - 4\pi^2 f(x)^2\bigr)\,dx, \qquad X = \{f \in C^
1[0,1] : f(0)=0=f(1)\}.$$

We test $J$ on the specific admissible function $f_a(x) = a\sin(\pi x)$, where $
a \in \mathbb{R}$ is a free parameter. This function lies in $C^1[0,1]$ and sati
sfies $f_a(0)=0=f_a(1)$, so $f_a \in X$ for every $a$.

Compute:
- $f_a'(x) = a\pi\cos(\pi x)$,
- $\displaystyle\int_0^1 f_a'(x)^2\,dx = a^2\pi^2\int_0^1 \cos^2(\pi x)\,dx = a^
2\pi^2\cdot\tfrac12 = \tfrac{a^2\pi^2}{2}$,
- $\displaystyle\int_0^1 f_a(x)^2\,dx = a^2\int_0^1 \sin^2(\pi x)\,dx = a^2\cdot
\tfrac12 = \tfrac{a^2}{2}$.

Hence
$$J(f_a) = \frac{a^2\pi^2}{2} - 4\pi^2\cdot\frac{a^2}{2} = \frac{a^2\pi^2}{2}(1
- 4) = -\frac{3\pi^2 a^2}{2}.$$

As $a \to \infty$, we have $J(f_a) = -\tfrac{3\pi^2 a^2}{2} \to -\infty$.

Since $J$ is unbounded below on $X$, the infimum $\inf_{f\in X} J(f)$ is $-\inft
y$. In particular, **the infimum does not exist as a finite real value**.

### Remark (spectral interpretation)

The quadratic form $Q(f)=\int_0^1 f'^2 - 4\pi^2\!\int_0^1 f^2$ diagonalizes in t
he sine basis $\{\sin(n\pi x)\}_{n\ge 1}$ (eigenfunctions of $-d^2/dx^2$ with Di
richlet boundary conditions, eigenvalues $(n\pi)^2$). Writing $f = \sum_{n\ge 1}
 a_n\sin(n\pi x)$ gives
$$J(f) = \frac{\pi^2}{2}\sum_{n\ge 1} a_n^2\,(n^2 - 4).$$
The $n=1$ coefficient is $1^2-4 = -3 < 0$, which is precisely the negative direc
tion $\sin(\pi x)$ exploited above. The $n=2$ mode ($\sin(2\pi x)$, eigenvalue $
4\pi^2$) is the zero mode $J=0$, and all $n\ge 3$ modes are positive. The presen
ce of the single negative mode $n=1$ forces $J$ to be unbounded below, confirmin
g $\inf J = -\infty$.
