## Proof

Let $R$ be Noetherian and $M$ a finite $R$-module with a filtration
$$0 = M_0 \subset M_1 \subset \cdots \subset M_n = M, \qquad M_i/M_{i-1} \cong R
/\mathfrak p_i.$$

We prove the relationship in two stages.

---

### Lemma (associated primes in a short exact sequence)

For $0 \to M' \to M \to M'' \to 0$ with $M$ finite over a Noetherian ring,
$$\operatorname{Ass}(M) \;\subseteq\; \operatorname{Ass}(M') \cup \operatorname{
Ass}(M'').$$

*Proof of lemma.* Let $\mathfrak p \in \operatorname{Ass}(M)$, so $\mathfrak p =
 \operatorname{ann}(x)$ for some $0 \neq x \in M$, and $N := Rx \cong R/\mathfra
k p$ is a submodule of $M$.

- If $N \cap M' \neq 0$: pick $0 \neq y \in N \cap M'$. Viewing $y$ inside $N \c
ong R/\mathfrak p$, since $\mathfrak p$ is prime and $y \neq 0$ in $R/\mathfrak
p$, we have $\operatorname{ann}(y) = \mathfrak p : y = \mathfrak p$. As $y \in M
'$, this gives $\mathfrak p \in \operatorname{Ass}(M')$.
- If $N \cap M' = 0$: then $N \hookrightarrow M''$ injectively, so $M''$ contain
s a copy of $R/\mathfrak p$, and the image of $1 \in R/\mathfrak p$ has annihila
tor $\mathfrak p$. Hence $\mathfrak p \in \operatorname{Ass}(M'')$.

In either case $\mathfrak p \in \operatorname{Ass}(M') \cup \operatorname{Ass}(M
'')$. $\square$

---

### Step 1 — $\operatorname{Ass}(M) \subseteq \{\mathfrak p_1,\ldots,\mathfrak p
_n\}$

By induction on $n$.

- **Base $n=1$:** $M \cong R/\mathfrak p_1$. For any $\bar r \in R/\mathfrak p_1
$, either $\bar r = 0$ (so $r \in \mathfrak p_1$) or $r \notin \mathfrak p_1$, i
n which case $\operatorname{ann}(\bar r) = \mathfrak p_1 : r = \mathfrak p_1$ (s
ince $\mathfrak p_1$ is prime). Thus $\operatorname{Ass}(R/\mathfrak p_1) = \{\m
athfrak p_1\}$.

- **Inductive step:** apply the lemma to
$$0 \to M_{n-1} \to M \to R/\mathfrak p_n \to 0.$$
By induction $\operatorname{Ass}(M_{n-1}) \subseteq \{\mathfrak p_1,\ldots,\math
frak p_{n-1}\}$ and $\operatorname{Ass}(R/\mathfrak p_n) = \{\mathfrak p_n\}$, s
o
$$\operatorname{Ass}(M) \subseteq \{\mathfrak p_1,\ldots,\mathfrak p_{n-1}\} \cu
p \{\mathfrak p_n\} = \{\mathfrak p_1,\ldots,\mathfrak p_n\}. \qquad \checkmark$
$

---

### Step 2 — Every $\mathfrak p_i$ contains an associated prime; minimal element
s coincide

Recall that for a finite module over a Noetherian ring,
$$\operatorname{Supp}(M) = \bigcup_{\mathfrak q \in \operatorname{Ass}(M)} V(\ma
thfrak q),$$
and support is additive in short exact sequences: $\operatorname{Supp}(M) = \ope
ratorname{Supp}(M') \cup \operatorname{Supp}(M'')$. Iterating over the filtratio
n,
$$\operatorname{Supp}(M) = \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} \operatorname{Supp}(R/\mathfrak p_i
) = \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} V(\mathfrak p_i).$$

Hence
$$\bigcup_{i=1}^{n} V(\mathfrak p_i) \;=\; \bigcup_{\mathfrak q \in \operatornam
e{Ass}(M)} V(\mathfrak q). \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Each $\mathfrak p_i$ contains an associated prime.** Since $\mathfrak p_i \in
V(\mathfrak p_i) \subseteq \operatorname{Supp}(M) = \bigcup_{\mathfrak q \in \op
eratorname{Ass}(M)} V(\mathfrak q)$, there exists $\mathfrak q \in \operatorname
{Ass}(M)$ with $\mathfrak p_i \in V(\mathfrak q)$, i.e. $\mathfrak p_i \supseteq
 \mathfrak q$.

**Minimal elements of $\{\mathfrak p_1,\ldots,\mathfrak p_n\}$ lie in $\operator
name{Ass}(M)$.** Let $\mathfrak p_j$ be minimal (under inclusion) among the $\ma
thfrak p_i$. By the previous point, $\mathfrak p_j \supseteq \mathfrak q$ for so
me $\mathfrak q \in \operatorname{Ass}(M) \subseteq \{\mathfrak p_1,\ldots,\math
frak p_n\}$ (using Step 1). Write $\mathfrak q = \mathfrak p_k$. Then $\mathfrak
 p_j \supseteq \mathfrak p_k$; minimality of $\mathfrak p_j$ forces $\mathfrak p
_k = \mathfrak p_j$, so $\mathfrak p_j = \mathfrak q \in \operatorname{Ass}(M)$.

**Minimal elements of $\operatorname{Ass}(M)$ are minimal in $\{\mathfrak p_i\}$
.** Let $\mathfrak q \in \operatorname{Ass}(M)$ be minimal in $\operatorname{Ass
}(M)$; by Step 1, $\mathfrak q = \mathfrak p_j$ for some $j$. If some $\mathfrak
 p_k \subsetneq \mathfrak p_j$, then $\mathfrak p_k \supseteq \mathfrak q'$ for
some $\mathfrak q' \in \operatorname{Ass}(M)$, giving $\mathfrak q' \subseteq \m
athfrak p_k \subsetneq \mathfrak p_j = \mathfrak q$, contradicting minimality of
 $\mathfrak q$ in $\operatorname{Ass}(M)$.

Therefore the **minimal elements of $\{\mathfrak p_1,\ldots,\mathfrak p_n\}$ and
 of $\operatorname{Ass}(M)$ coincide**.

---

### The inclusion can be strict

Take $R = \mathbb Z$, $M = \mathbb Z$, with the filtration $0 \subset p\mathbb Z
 \subset \mathbb Z$ where $p$ is prime. Then $\mathfrak p_1 = (0)$, $\mathfrak p
_2 = (p)$, but
$$\operatorname{Ass}(\mathbb Z) = \{(0)\} \;\subsetneq\; \{(0),(p)\} = \{\mathfr
ak p_1,\mathfrak p_2\}.$$
Here $(p) \supsetneq (0)$ and $(0) \in \operatorname{Ass}(M)$, consistent with t
he theorem; the non-minimal prime $(p)$ is not associated.

---

### Conclusion

The relationship between $\operatorname{Ass}(M)$ and $\{\mathfrak p_1,\ldots,\ma
thfrak p_n\}$ is:

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Ass}(M) \;\subseteq\; \{\mathfrak p_1,\ldots,\mathfrak p_
n\}, \quad\text{and every } \mathfrak p_i \text{ contains some } \mathfrak q \in
 \operatorname{Ass}(M).}$$

Equivalently, the **minimal elements** of $\{\mathfrak p_1,\ldots,\mathfrak p_n\
}$ (with respect to inclusion) are exactly the minimal elements of $\operatornam
e{Ass}(M)$, and in particular belong to $\operatorname{Ass}(M)$. The inclusion $
\operatorname{Ass}(M) \subseteq \{\mathfrak p_1,\ldots,\mathfrak p_n\}$ may be p
roper: a non-minimal $\mathfrak p_i$ need not be associated, though it must cont
ain an associated prime.
