**Key Claim.** The idempotent generating $I$ is unique: if $eA = fA$ with $e^2 =
 e$ and $f^2 = f$, then $e = f$.

**Proof of Claim.** Suppose $eA = fA$ for idempotents $e, f$.

Since $e \in eA = fA$, there exists $a \in A$ with $e = fa$. Then:
$$fe = f(fa) = f^2 a = fa = e.$$

Since $f \in fA = eA$, there exists $b \in A$ with $f = eb$. Then:
$$ef = e(eb) = e^2 b = eb = f.$$

Now we have $fe = e$ and $ef = f$.

In a commutative ring, $ef = fe$, so:
$$e = fe = ef = f.$$

Therefore $e = f$, and the idempotent generating $I$ is unique. $\square$
