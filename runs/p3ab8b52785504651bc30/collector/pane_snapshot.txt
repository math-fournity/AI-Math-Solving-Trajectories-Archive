## Proof

**Parametrization.** The surface $P_t$ is the graph of $z = \frac{1}{x^2+y^2}$ o
ver the annulus $t^2 \le x^2+y^2 \le 1$. Setting $r = \sqrt{x^2+y^2}$, we have $
z = g(r) = r^{-2}$ with $r \in [t, 1]$, $\theta \in [0, 2\pi)$.

Parametrize by $\mathbf{X}(r,\theta) = (r\cos\theta,\, r\sin\theta,\, r^{-2})$.
Then

$$\mathbf{X}_r = (\cos\theta,\,\sin\theta,\,-2r^{-3}), \qquad \mathbf{X}_\theta
= (-r\sin\theta,\, r\cos\theta,\, 0),$$

$$|\mathbf{X}_r \times \mathbf{X}_\theta| = r\sqrt{1 + 4r^{-6}} = \frac{\sqrt{r^
6 + 4}}{r^2}.$$

Hence

$$A_t = 2\pi \int_t^1 \frac{\sqrt{r^6+4}}{r^2}\,dr.$$

**Divergence as $t \to 0^+$.** For all $r > 0$, $\sqrt{r^6+4} \ge \sqrt{4} = 2$,
 so

$$\frac{\sqrt{r^6+4}}{r^2} \ge \frac{2}{r^2}.$$

Therefore

$$A_t \ge 2\pi \int_t^1 \frac{2}{r^2}\,dr = 4\pi\left[-\frac{1}{r}\right]_t^1 =
4\pi\left(\frac{1}{t} - 1\right).$$

As $t \to 0^+$, the right-hand side $\frac{4\pi}{t} - 4\pi \to +\infty$, so $A_t
 \to +\infty$.

**Conclusion.** The surface area $A_t$ grows without bound as $t \to 0^+$. The l
imit $\lim_{t\to 0^+} A_t$ does **not** exist as a finite real number (it diverg
es to $+\infty$).
