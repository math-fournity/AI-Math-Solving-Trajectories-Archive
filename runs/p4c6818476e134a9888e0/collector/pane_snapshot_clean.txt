## Solution

The Hopf bundle $\mathbb{S}^3 \to \mathbb{S}^2$ is a principal $U(1)$-bundle ove
r $\mathbb{S}^2 \cong \mathbb{CP}^1$. The associated bundle construction takes a
 principal $G$-bundle $P \to M$ and a representation $\rho: G \to \operatorname{
Gl}(V)$, forming the vector bundle
$$P \times_{\rho} V := (P \times V)/\sim, \qquad (p \cdot g, v) \sim (p, \rho(g)
v).$$

**Key fact.** For a principal $U(1)$-bundle $P$ and the irreducible representati
on $\rho_n: U(1) \to \operatorname{Gl}(\mathbb{C})$ defined by $\rho_n(e^{i\thet
a}) = e^{in\theta}$, the associated line bundle $P \times_{\rho_n} \mathbb{C}$ i

$$P \times_{\rho_n} \mathbb{C} \cong \left(P \times_{\rho_1} \mathbb{C}\right)^{
\otimes n}, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z}$$
(where negative $n$ gives dual powers, consistent with $\rho_{-n} = \overline{\r
ho_n}$).

**Proof of the key fact.** The transition functions of $P \times_{\rho_n}\mathbb
{C}$ are obtained by composing the transition functions $g_{\alpha\beta}: U_\alp
ha \cap U_\beta \to U(1)$ of $P$ with $\rho_n$. If $g_{\alpha\beta} = e^{i\theta
_{\alpha\beta}}$, then the line-bundle transition functions become $e^{in\theta_
{\alpha\beta}} = (e^{i\theta_{\alpha\beta}})^n$, which are exactly the transitio
n functions of the $n$-fold tensor product. ∎

**Chern class computation.** By the Whitney product formula (or the tensor-produ
ct formula for line bundles), for line bundles $L, L'$:
$$c_1(L \otimes L') = c_1(L) + c_1(L').$$
Applying inductively,
$$c_1\!\left((P \times_{\rho_1}\mathbb{C})^{\otimes n}\right) = n \cdot c_1(P \t
imes_{\rho_1}\mathbb{C}).$$

Now, $P \times_{\rho_1}\mathbb{C}$ is precisely the line bundle whose first Cher
n class equals $c_1(\text{Hopf})$, since the Hopf bundle's Chern class is *defin
ed* via its standard associated line bundle. By the given hypothesis,
$$c_1(P \times_{\rho_1}\mathbb{C}) = c_1(\text{Hopf}) = -1.$$

Therefore,
$$c_1\!\left(\text{Hopf} \times_{\rho_n}\mathbb{C}\right) = n \cdot (-1) = \boxe
d{-n}.$$
