Metric

Claim. The expectation value map $p \mapsto E_p = \int_{[0,1]} x,dp(x)$ is
Lipschitz with respect to the 1-Wasserstein metric on the space of Borel
measures on $[0,1]$, with Lipschitz constant $C = 1$.

Recall the 1-Wasserstein distance between two finite Borel measures $p, q$ on
$[0,1]$:

$$W_1(p, q) = \inf_{\gamma \in \Pi(p,q)} \int_{[0,1]^2} |x - y|,d\gamma(x, y),$$

where $\Pi(p,q)$ is the set of couplings of $p$ and $q$ — measures on $[0,1]^2$
whose first marginal is $p$ and second marginal is $q$. A coupling exists if and
only if $p$ and $q$ have the same total mass; otherwise $\Pi(p,q) = \varnothing$
and $W_1(p,q) = +\infty$.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Case 1: Equal total mass

Let $m = p([0,1]) = q([0,1])$, and let $\gamma \in \Pi(p,q)$ be any coupling.
Then:

$$E_p - E_q = \int_{[0,1]} x,dp(x) - \int_{[0,1]} y,dq(y) = \int_{[0,1]^2} (x -
y),d\gamma(x, y),$$

since the marginals of $\gamma$ recover $p$ and $q$. Applying the triangle
inequality for integrals:

$$|E_p - E_q| = \left|\int_{[0,1]^2} (x-y),d\gamma\right| \le \int_{[0,1]^2} |x
- y|,d\gamma.$$

Taking the infimum over all couplings $\gamma \in \Pi(p,q)$:

$$\boxed{|E_p - E_q| \le W_1(p, q).}$$

This holds for any total mass $m > 0$ (including $m = 1$, the probability
measure case), with the same constant $C = 1$.

If $p([0,1]) \neq q([0,1])$, then no coupling exists, so $W_1(p, q) = +\infty$.
Since $E_p$ and $E_q$ are finite for finite measures on $[0,1]$, the inequality

$$|E_p - E_q| \le C \cdot W_1(p, q) = +\infty$$

$$|E_p - E_q| \le 1 \cdot W_1(p, q).$$
