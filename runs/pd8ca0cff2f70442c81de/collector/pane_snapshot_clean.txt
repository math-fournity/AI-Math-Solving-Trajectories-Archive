## Proof

**Answer leak check:** The problem statement contains only the problem to be sol
ved — no solution, sketch, or formalization notes. Proceeding.

---

We evaluate
$$L = \lim_{n\to\infty} n^{p+1}\int_0^1 e^{-nx}\ln(1+x^p)\,dx$$
for any natural number $p$.

**Substitution.** Let $u = nx$, so $x = u/n$, $dx = du/n$. The limits become $u:
 0 \to n$. Then
$$n^{p+1}\int_0^1 e^{-nx}\ln(1+x^p)\,dx = n^{p+1}\cdot\frac{1}{n}\int_0^n e^{-u}
\ln\!\left(1+\tfrac{u^p}{n^p}\right)du = \int_0^n e^{-u}\,\underbrace{n^p\ln\!\l
eft(1+\tfrac{u^p}{n^p}\right)}_{=:\,g_n(u)}\,du.$$

**Pointwise limit.** For each fixed $u \ge 0$, since $\ln(1+t) \sim t$ as $t\to
0$ and $u^p/n^p \to 0$,
$$g_n(u) = n^p\ln\!\left(1+\tfrac{u^p}{n^p}\right) \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} n^p
\cdot \tfrac{u^p}{n^p} = u^p.$$

**Dominating function.** Using $\ln(1+t)\le t$ for all $t\ge 0$,
$$0 \le g_n(u) = n^p\ln\!\left(1+\tfrac{u^p}{n^p}\right) \le n^p\cdot\tfrac{u^p}
{n^p} = u^p.$$

Extending the integrand to all of $[0,\infty)$ by defining it as $0$ for $u > n$
 (i.e., multiplying by $\mathbf{1}_{[0,n]}$), we get
$$0 \le e^{-u}\,g_n(u)\,\mathbf{1}_{[0,n]}(u) \le e^{-u}\,u^p,$$
and $e^{-u}u^p$ is integrable on $[0,\infty)$ (it equals the Gamma density up to
 a constant). The pointwise limit of $e^{-u}g_n(u)\mathbf{1}_{[0,n]}(u)$ is $e^{
-u}u^p$ for every fixed $u$.

**Dominated Convergence Theorem.** Applying DCT,
$$L = \lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^n e^{-u}\,g_n(u)\,du = \int_0^\infty e^{-u}\,u^p\,
du = \Gamma(p+1).$$

**Conclusion.** For natural number $p$, $\Gamma(p+1) = p!$. Therefore

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} n^{p+1}\int_0^1 e^{-nx}\ln(1+x^p)\,dx = p!}$$
