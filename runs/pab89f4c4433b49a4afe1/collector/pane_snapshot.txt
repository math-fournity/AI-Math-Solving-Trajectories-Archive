## Solution

We determine all natural numbers $n \geq 3$ such that **every** convex $n$-gon i
s covered by the $n$ closed disks having the sides as diameters.

### Key reformulation (Thales' theorem)

A point $P$ lies in the closed disk with diameter $\overline{A_iA_{i+1}}$ if and
 only if $\angle A_i P A_{i+1} \geq 90°$.

*Proof of the reformulation.* The locus of points $P$ with $\angle A_iPA_{i+1}=9
0°$ is exactly the circle with diameter $\overline{A_iA_{i+1}}$ (Thales). Inside
 that disk the angle is $>90°$ (obtuse), and outside it is $<90°$ (acute). Since
 the disk is closed, $\geq 90°$ characterizes membership. $\square$

Thus the $n$-gon is covered by the $n$ disks if and only if **every** point $P$
of the $n$-gon satisfies $\angle A_iPA_{i+1}\geq 90°$ for at least one index $i$
.

### The case $n=3$: every triangle is covered

Let $P$ be an interior point of a convex triangle $A_1A_2A_3$. The three rays $P
A_1, PA_2, PA_3$ emanate from $P$ and partition the full angle around $P$ into t
hree angles:
$$\angle A_1PA_2+\angle A_2PA_3+\angle A_3PA_1=360°.$$
Hence at least one of them is $\geq 360°/3=120°>90°$, so $P$ lies in the corresp
onding disk.

**Boundary points.** If $P$ lies in the relative interior of side $\overline{A_i
A_{i+1}}$, then $\angle A_iPA_{i+1}=180°\geq 90°$, so $P$ is in disk $i$. If $P$
 is a vertex $A_i$, then $P$ is an endpoint of the diameters $\overline{A_{i-1}A
_i}$ and $\overline{A_iA_{i+1}}$, hence lies on both corresponding circles (whic
h are included in the closed disks).

So **every triangle is covered**. $\checkmark$

### The case $n=4$: every convex quadrilateral is covered

Let $P$ be an interior point of a convex quadrilateral $A_1A_2A_3A_4$. Since the
 quadrilateral is convex and $P$ is interior, the four rays $PA_1,PA_2,PA_3,PA_4
$ appear in cyclic order around $P$, and the four consecutive angles sum to the
full angle:
$$\angle A_1PA_2+\angle A_2PA_3+\angle A_3PA_4+\angle A_4PA_1=360°.$$
Therefore at least one of them is $\geq 360°/4=90°$, so $P$ lies in the correspo
nding closed disk.

**Boundary points** are handled exactly as in the case $n=3$: a point on side $\
overline{A_iA_{i+1}}$ gives an angle of $180°$, and a vertex is an endpoint of t
wo diameters.

So **every convex quadrilateral is covered**. $\checkmark$

### The case $n\geq 5$: a counterexample exists

Consider a **regular $n$-gon** inscribed in a circle of radius $R$, with center
$O$. The center $O$ is an interior point of the $n$-gon. For every side $\overli
ne{A_iA_{i+1}}$,
$$\angle A_i\,O\,A_{i+1}=\frac{360°}{n}\leq\frac{360°}{5}=72°<90°.$$

By the reformulation, $O$ lies **strictly outside** every disk with diameter $\o
verline{A_iA_{i+1}}$. Equivalently, using the apothem $a=R\cos(\pi/n)$ and the h
alf-side $s=R\sin(\pi/n)$: $O$ is in disk $i$ iff $a\leq s$, i.e.\ $\tan(\pi/n)\
geq 1$, i.e.\ $\pi/n\geq\pi/4$, i.e.\ $n\leq 4$. For $n\geq 5$ we have $a>s$, so
 $O$ is outside all $n$ disks.

Hence for every $n\geq 5$, the regular $n$-gon is **not** covered, so the statem
ent fails.

### Conclusion

The statement holds for $n=3$ and $n=4$, and fails for every $n\geq 5$.

$$\boxed{n\in\{3,\,4\}}$$
