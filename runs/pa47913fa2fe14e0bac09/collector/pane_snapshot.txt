## Proof

Let $M$ be a compact connected surface with boundary, whose boundary consists of
 $b$ circles $C_1, \dots, C_b$. Let $V$ be a vector field on $M$ with isolated z
eros, and assume that on every boundary circle $C_i$, the vector field $V$ is th
e **exterior unit normal** (pointing out of $M$).

### Step 1. Capping off the boundary

Glue a closed disk $D_i$ to each boundary circle $C_i$. The result is a closed s
urface
$$\widetilde{M} = M \cup D_1 \cup \cdots \cup D_b.$$
Each gluing is along a circle, and $\chi(S^1)=0$, $\chi(D^2)=1$, so by additivit
y of Euler characteristic,
$$\chi(\widetilde{M}) = \chi(M) + \sum_{i=1}^{b}\bigl(\chi(D_i)-\chi(C_i)\bigr)
= \chi(M) + b. \tag{1}$$

### Step 2. Extending $V$ over the disks

On each $C_i = \partial D_i$, the vector field $V$ is the exterior normal of $M$
, hence it points **into** the disk $D_i$, i.e. radially inward toward the cente
r of $D_i$.

We extend $V$ to a vector field $\widetilde{V}$ on $\widetilde{M}$ by defining i
t on each $D_i$ as the radial inward field $\widetilde{V}(x) = -x$ (after identi
fying $D_i$ with the unit disk). This agrees with the boundary data and has a si
ngle zero at the center of $D_i$.

The index of the zero of $-x$ at the origin is $+1$: the map $x \mapsto -x$ on a
 small circle around the origin is $\theta \mapsto \theta + \pi$, which has degr
ee $+1$. Equivalently, the boundary field $-x$ on $\partial D_i$ has winding num
ber $+1$ (a rotation by $\pi$ does not change the degree), so by the Poincaré–Ho
pf index formula on a disk, any extension over $D_i$ has total index $+1$.

Thus each capped disk contributes exactly $+1$ to the total index:
$$\operatorname{ind}(\widetilde{V}) = \operatorname{ind}(V) + b, \tag{2}$$
where $\operatorname{ind}(V) = \sum_{p \in \operatorname{Zero}(V)} \operatorname
{ind}_p(V)$ is the sum of indices of the zeros of $V$ in $M$.

### Step 3. Apply Poincaré–Hopf on the closed surface

The surface $\widetilde{M}$ is closed, and $\widetilde{V}$ has only isolated zer
os, so the Poincaré–Hopf theorem gives
$$\operatorname{ind}(\widetilde{V}) = \chi(\widetilde{M}). \tag{3}$$

### Step 4. The formula

Combining (1), (2), (3):
$$\operatorname{ind}(V) + b = \chi(\widetilde{M}) = \chi(M) + b,$$
hence
$$\boxed{\operatorname{ind}(V) = \chi(M) = \chi(\widetilde{M}) - b.}$$

### Interpretation

The index of an exterior-normal vector field on a surface with boundary equals t
he Euler characteristic of that surface (taken as a surface with boundary). Equi
valently, it equals the Euler characteristic of the closed surface obtained by c
apping off the boundary, **minus the number of boundary circles**:

$$\operatorname{ind}(V) = \chi(\widetilde{M}) - b.$$

The subtraction of $b$ accounts precisely for the $+1$ index contribution forced
 on each glued disk by the exterior-normal boundary condition: capping a boundar
y circle where $V$ points outward always introduces one unit of index, so to rec
over the index of the original field on $M$, one removes those $b$ forced contri
butions from $\chi(\widetilde{M})$.

This is the natural generalization of the Poincaré–Hopf theorem to surfaces with
 boundary: the exterior-normal condition plays the role of the "outward-pointing
" hypothesis that makes the theorem close up cleanly, and the formula reduces to
 the classical $\operatorname{ind}(V)=\chi$ when $b=0$.
