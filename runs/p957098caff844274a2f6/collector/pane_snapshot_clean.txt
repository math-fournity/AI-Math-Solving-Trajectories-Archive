long grid lines; opposite sides are parallel, so the sides use exactly two of th

**One orientation.** Fix two grid directions $\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2$ (unit
vectors at $60°$). A parallelogram with sides $a\,\mathbf{e}_1$ and $b\,\mathbf{
e}_2$ has side lengths $a$ and $b$; it is a **rhombus** iff $a = b$. Set the com
mon side length to $s \ge 1$.

Place the triangle with lattice coordinates $i \ge 0,\; j \ge 0,\; i+j \le n$. A
 rhombus of side $s$ has vertices
$$(i,j),\quad (i+s,j),\quad (i,j+s),\quad (i+s,j+s).$$
The binding constraint is the top vertex: $i+s+j+s \le n$, i.e. $i+j \le n-2s$.
The number of valid positions $(i,j)$ with $i\ge0, j\ge0, i+j\le n-2s$ is the tr
iangular number
$$\frac{(n-2s+1)(n-2s+2)}{2}, \qquad s = 1, 2, \dots, \left\lfloor n/2\right\rfl
oor.$$

**Computation for $n=10$.** Summing over $s=1,\dots,5$:

| $s$ | $(n{-}2s{+}1)(n{-}2s{+}2)/2$ | value |
|-----|-------------------------------|-------|
| 1 | $9\cdot10/2$ | 45 |
| 2 | $7\cdot8/2$ | 28 |
| 3 | $5\cdot6/2$ | 15 |

$$\text{Per orientation} = 45+28+15+6+1 = 95.$$
