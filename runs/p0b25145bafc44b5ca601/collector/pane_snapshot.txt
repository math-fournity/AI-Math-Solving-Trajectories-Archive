## Solution

Let $\mathbb{X} = [0,X_1]\times\cdots\times[0,X_D]$ and $f\in\mathcal{C}^1(\math
bb{X})$. I treat the sine/cosine basis on the box; the multi-dimensional stateme
nt factors coordinate-wise, so the essential content is one-dimensional. I write
 the 1D case in detail and then state the $D$-dimensional form.

### 1. Setup (1D)

Take $X=[0,L]$ and the sine basis $\phi_n(x)=\sqrt{2/L}\sin(n\pi x/L)$, $n\ge 1$
, which is the orthonormal eigenbasis of $-\partial_{xx}$ on $[0,L]$ with Dirich
let boundary conditions $f(0)=f(L)=0$. The sine coefficients are
$$
\hat f_n \;=\; \sqrt{\tfrac{2}{L}}\int_0^L f(x)\sin(n\pi x/L)\,dx .
$$
Define $\omega_n := n\pi/L$. Integrating by parts once (justified because $f\in\
mathcal{C}^1$ and the sine factor vanishes at the endpoints),
$$
\hat f_n \;=\; -\frac{1}{\omega_n}\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{L}}\int_0^L f'(x)\cos(\omega_n
 x)\,dx \;+\; \frac{1}{\omega_n}\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{L}}\Big[f(x)\cos(\omega_n x)\Big
]_{0}^{L}.
$$
Because $\cos(\omega_n\cdot 0)=\cos(\omega_n L)=\cos(n\pi)=(-1)^n$, the boundary
 term is $\frac{1}{\omega_n}\sqrt{2/L}\big((-1)^n f(L)-f(0)\big)$.

**Case A — Dirichlet-compatible ($f(0)=f(L)=0$).** The boundary term vanishes an
d
$$
\hat f_n \;=\; -\frac{1}{\omega_n}\,\widehat{f'}_n^{\cos},
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
|\hat f_n| \;\le\; \frac{C}{\omega_n}\,\|f'\|_{L^2} \;\le\; \frac{C'}{n}.
$$
Thus $\hat f_n = O(n^{-1})$: **one continuous derivative forces at least $1/n$ d
ecay of the sine coefficients.**

**Case B — general $f\in\mathcal{C}^1$, no boundary matching.** The boundary ter
m is $O(1/\omega_n)$, so the $O(1/n)$ rate still holds; the only price is that t
he leading $1/n$ term is governed by the endpoint mismatch $(-1)^n f(L)-f(0)$ ra
ther than by $f'$ alone.

### 2. Iteration: $k$ derivatives $\Rightarrow$ $O(n^{-k})$ decay

If $f\in\mathcal{C}^k$ and the boundary traces match the basis (i.e. the even/od
d extensions of $f$ to $\mathbb{R}$ are $\mathcal{C}^k$), integration by parts r
epeats $k$ times with no boundary residue:
$$
\hat f_n \;=\; \frac{(-1)^k}{\omega_n^k}\,\widehat{f^{(k)}}_n^{\{\sin/\cos\}},
\qquad
|\hat f_n|\le \frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{L^2}}{\omega_n^k} = O(n^{-k}).
$$
The matching condition is exactly the statement that the periodic extension of $
f$ (in the appropriate parity) is $\mathcal{C}^k$; without it, each integration
by parts leaves an $O(1/\omega_n)$ boundary residue, so the rate saturates at th
e order at which the extension stops being smooth.

### 3. The converse direction (decay $\Rightarrow$ smoothness)

If $|\hat f_n|\le C\,n^{-(1+\alpha)}$ for some $\alpha>0$, then $\sum_n |n\,\hat
 f_n|<\infty$, so the termwise-differentiated series $\sum_n \omega_n\hat f_n\ph
i_n$ converges absolutely and uniformly; hence $f\in\mathcal{C}^1$. Iterating: $
|\hat f_n|=O(n^{-(k+\alpha)})$ with $\alpha>0$ implies $f\in\mathcal{C}^k$. The
strict gap of one derivative (the "$+\alpha$") is the same Sobolev-embedding thr
eshold that appears in the periodic theory: $\dot H^s\subset \mathcal{C}^k$ iff
$s>k+\tfrac12$ in 1D, and $\sum_n \omega_n^{2s}|\hat f_n|^2<\infty$ is the Sobol
ev norm.

So, up to the half-derivative embedding loss, **decay rate $n^{-s}$ $\Longleftri
ghtarrow$ $f$ has roughly $s$ derivatives** (in the $L^2$ Sobolev sense, and cla
ssically when $s$ exceeds the embedding threshold).

### 4. $D$-dimensional box

On $\mathbb{X}$ the eigenbasis is the tensor product
$$
\phi_{\mathbf n}(\mathbf x)=\prod_{d=1}^D \sqrt{2/X_d}\,\sin(n_d\pi x_d/X_d),\qq
uad \mathbf n\in\mathbb{N}^D,
$$
with frequencies $\omega_{\mathbf n}=\big(\sum_d (n_d\pi/X_d)^2\big)^{1/2}$. Int
egrating by parts in each coordinate gives
$$
\hat f_{\mathbf n}
\;=\; \frac{(-1)^{|\mathbf k|}}{\prod_d \omega_{n_d}^{k_d}}\,\widehat{\partial^{
\mathbf k}f}_{\mathbf n},
\qquad |\mathbf k|=k,
$$
whenever $f\in\mathcal{C}^{k}$ and the boundary traces match the parity of the b
asis in every coordinate. Therefore
$$
|\hat f_{\mathbf n}| \;\le\; C\,\omega_{\mathbf n}^{-k}\,\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^k},
$$
i.e. **$k$ continuous derivatives $\Rightarrow$ $O(\omega_{\mathbf n}^{-k})$ dec
ay of the multivariate coefficients**, and conversely $\sum_{\mathbf n}\omega_{\
mathbf n}^{2s}|\hat f_{\mathbf n}|^2<\infty$ with $s>k+D/2$ implies $f\in\mathca
l{C}^k$ (Sobolev embedding in dimension $D$, threshold $D/2$).

### 5. Implication for convergence of the Fourier series

The partial sums $S_N f=\sum_{|\mathbf n|_\infty\le N}\hat f_{\mathbf n}\phi_{\m
athbf n}$ approximate $f$ with error governed by the tail:
$$
\|f-S_N f\|_{L^2}^2 \;=\; \sum_{|\mathbf n|_\infty>N}|\hat f_{\mathbf n}|^2 .
$$
With $|\hat f_{\mathbf n}|=O(\omega_{\mathbf n}^{-k})$ and $k>D/2$, the tail is
summable, so $S_N f\to f$ in $L^2$ at rate $O(N^{-(k-D/2)})$; with $k>D/2+1$ the
 differentiated tail is summable and $S_N f\to f$ uniformly with one derivative.
 More generally:

- **$f\in L^2$ only** $\Rightarrow$ $\hat f_{\mathbf n}\to 0$ (Riemann–Lebesgue)
 and $L^2$-convergence, but no quantitative rate.
- **$f\in\mathcal{C}^1$ (boundary-matched)** $\Rightarrow$ $\hat f_{\mathbf n}=O
(\omega_{\mathbf n}^{-1})$ $\Rightarrow$ $L^2$-error $O(N^{-(2-D)})$ when $D<2$,
 and in any dimension an $L^2$ rate $O(N^{-(2k-D)})$ for $f\in\mathcal{C}^k$ wit
h $2k>D$.
- **$f\in\mathcal{C}^k$ with $k>D/2$** $\Rightarrow$ uniform (and, with one extr
a derivative, $\mathcal{C}^1$-uniform) convergence.
- **$f\in\mathcal{C}^\infty$** $\Rightarrow$ super-algebraic decay: $|\hat f_{\m
athbf n}|=O(\omega_{\mathbf n}^{-k})$ for every $k$ $\Rightarrow$ spectral (fast
er-than-any-polynomial) convergence.
- **$f$ analytic with a strip of analyticity of width $\rho$** $\Rightarrow$ exp
onential decay $|\hat f_{\mathbf n}|=O(e^{-\rho\,\omega_{\mathbf n}})$ $\Rightar
row$ exponential convergence of the series.

### 6. Summary of the relationship

$$
\boxed{\;f\in\mathcal{C}^k(\mathbb{X})\ \text{(with boundary traces matched to t
he basis)}\ \Longrightarrow\ |\hat f_{\mathbf n}|=O\!\big(\omega_{\mathbf n}^{-k
}\big),\quad\text{and conversely }|\hat f_{\mathbf n}|=O\!\big(\omega_{\mathbf n
}^{-(k+D/2+\varepsilon)}\big)\Longrightarrow f\in\mathcal{C}^k.\;}
$$

Equivalently, in Sobolev terms: $f\in H^s(\mathbb{X})$ iff $\sum_{\mathbf n}\ome
ga_{\mathbf n}^{2s}|\hat f_{\mathbf n}|^2<\infty$, and $H^s\subset\mathcal{C}^k$
 iff $s>k+D/2$. **Each additional continuous derivative buys one power of $\omeg
a_{\mathbf n}^{-1}$ in the coefficient decay, and that decay rate is precisely w
hat controls the convergence speed of the Fourier partial sums** — polynomial sm
oothness gives algebraic convergence, $\mathcal{C}^\infty$ gives super-algebraic
 convergence, and analyticity gives exponential convergence. Boundary mismatch (
the extension not being $\mathcal{C}^k$) caps the attainable rate at the order w
here the mismatch first appears, which is the practical reason for windowing / b
oundary-matched bases in spectral methods.
