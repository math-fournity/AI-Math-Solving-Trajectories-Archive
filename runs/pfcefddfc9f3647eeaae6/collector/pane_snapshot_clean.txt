## Reformulation

Since $A$ is PSD with rank $\leq 3$ and $a_{ii}=1$, there exist unit vectors $v_
1,\dots,v_n \in \mathbb{R}^3$ with $a_{ij}=\langle v_i,v_j\rangle$ (Gram represe
ntation). Thus
$$\max_{i\neq j} a_{ij} = \max_{i\neq j}\langle v_i,v_j\rangle = \cos\varphi,$$
where $\varphi = \min_{i\neq j}\arccos\langle v_i,v_j\rangle$ is the minimum ang
ular separation. Minimizing $\max\, a_{ij}$ = maximizing $\varphi$ = the **Tamme
s problem** on $S^2$.

## The Fejes Tóth bound

**Voronoi setup.** Place the $n$ points on $S^2$ and form the spherical Voronoi
tessellation. Each cell has area $4\pi/n$ and inradius $\geq \varphi/2$ (half th
e distance to the nearest neighbor).

**Lemma (regular spherical $k$-gon).** A regular spherical $k$-gon with inradius
 $r$ has area
$$A_k(r)=2k\arccos\!\bigl(\sin(\pi/k)\cos r\bigr)-(k-2)\pi.$$
*Derivation:* Decompose into $k$ isosceles triangles with two sides $r$ and cent
er angle $2\pi/k$. Dropping the perpendicular from center to a side gives a righ
t spherical triangle with inradius-leg $r$ and center angle $\pi/k$; Napier's ru
les give $\cos\gamma=\sin(\pi/k)\cos r$ for the half-interior angle $\gamma$. By
 Girard's theorem, $A_k=2k\gamma-(k-2)\pi$.

Among all spherical $k$-gons with inradius $\geq r$, the regular one has **minim
um** area. One checks $A_k(r)$ is decreasing and convex in $k$.

**Euler bound on average sides.** For a spherical tiling with $F=n$ cells, $E$ e
dges, $V$ vertices: $V-E+F=2$ and $3V\leq 2E$, giving $\sum k_i = 2E \leq 6F-12$
, so the average number of sides satisfies $\bar k \leq 6-12/n$.

**Applying convexity.** Since $A_k$ is convex in $k$ and $\sum k_i \leq 6n-12$,
Jensen's inequality gives
$$4\pi = \sum_{i=1}^n A_{k_i}(\varphi/2) \geq n\, A_{\bar k}(\varphi/2), \qquad
\bar k = 6-\frac{12}{n}=\frac{6(n-2)}{n}.$$

## Solving for the bound

Set $K = \frac{6(n-2)}{n}$, so $\frac{\pi}{K} = \frac{n\pi}{6(n-2)} = \omega_n$.
 The inequality $n\,A_K(\varphi/2)\leq 4\pi$ becomes:
$$n\Bigl[2K\arccos\!\bigl(\sin\omega_n\cos(\varphi/2)\bigr)-(K-2)\pi\Bigr]\leq 4
\pi.$$
Substituting $K=\frac{6(n-2)}{n}$:
$$12(n-2)\arccos\!\bigl(\sin\omega_n\cos(\varphi/2)\bigr)-(4n-12)\pi \leq 4\pi,$
$
$$12(n-2)\arccos\!\bigl(\sin\omega_n\cos(\varphi/2)\bigr)\leq 4\pi(n-2),$$
$$3\arccos\!\bigl(\sin\omega_n\cos(\varphi/2)\bigr)\leq \pi.$$
Hence $\arccos(\sin\omega_n\cos(\varphi/2))\leq \pi/3$, i.e.,
$$\sin\omega_n\cos(\varphi/2)\geq \tfrac12 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \cos(\varph
i/2)\geq \frac{1}{2\sin\omega_n}.$$

## The minimum value

Using $\cos\varphi = 2\cos^2(\varphi/2)-1$:
$$\max_{i\neq j}\,a_{ij}=\cos\varphi \geq 2\cdot\frac{1}{4\sin^2\omega_n}-1 = \f
rac{1}{2\sin^2\omega_n}-1.$$

**Tightness.** This bound is achieved (so it is the exact minimum) for:
- $n=3$: $\omega_3=\pi/2$, value $= \tfrac12-1=-\tfrac12$ — regular triangle on
a great circle.
- $n=4$: $\omega_4=\pi/3$, value $= \tfrac{2}{3}-1=-\tfrac13$ — regular tetrahed
ron.
- $n=6$: $\omega_6=\pi/4$, value $= 1-1=0$ — regular octahedron.
- $n=12$: $\omega_{12}=\pi/5$, value $= \tfrac{1}{2\sin^2(\pi/5)}-1=\tfrac{1}{\s
qrt5}$ — regular icosahedron.

In each case the Voronoi cells are congruent regular spherical polygons with $k=
6-12/n$ sides, making every inequality above an equality.

## Result

The minimum value of $\displaystyle\max_{i\neq j} a_{ij}$ is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2\sin^2\omega_n}-1 \;=\; \dfrac{\cos 2\omega_n}{2\sin^2\omega
_n} \;=\; \dfrac{\cot^2\omega_n-1}{2}}, \qquad \omega_n=\frac{n\pi}{6(n-2)}.$$
