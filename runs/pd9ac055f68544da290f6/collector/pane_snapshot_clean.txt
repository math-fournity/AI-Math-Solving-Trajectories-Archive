## Proof

Let the planar rectangle have side lengths $N$ and $M$, so its area is
$$S = NM.$$

Place the two-dimensional surface (the "screen") as the $xy$-plane, with unit no
rmal $\hat{\mathbf{z}} = (0,0,1)$.

### Step 1. Projected area in terms of the normal

The rectangle lies in some plane $\Pi$. Let $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ be a unit normal
to $\Pi$. Orthogonal projection of a planar region of area $S$ onto the $xy$-pla
ne multiplies the area by $|\cos\theta|$, where $\theta$ is the angle between $\
hat{\mathbf{n}}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{z}}$. Equivalently,
$$\cos\theta = \hat{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}} = n_z,$$
the $z$-component of $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$. Hence the projected area for a fixed or
ientation is
$$A_{\text{proj}} = S\,|n_z| = NM\,|n_z|.$$

### Step 2. Distribution of the normal under random rotation

A "random 3-space rotational orientation" means a rotation drawn uniformly from
$SO(3)$ (Haar measure). Applying such a rotation to the rectangle rotates its no
rmal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ uniformly over the unit sphere $S^2$.

For a point uniformly distributed on $S^2$, the $z$-component $n_z = \cos\theta$
 is **uniformly distributed on $[-1,1]$**. This is the standard fact that the sp
herical measure factors as $d\Omega = d\phi\, d(\cos\theta)$, so $\cos\theta$ ha
s constant density $\tfrac{1}{2}$ on $[-1,1]$.

### Step 3. Average of $|n_z|$

Since $n_z \sim \text{Uniform}[-1,1]$,
$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[|n_z|\bigr] = \int_{-1}^{1} |u|\cdot\frac{1}{2}\,du = \frac{1}
{2}\cdot 2\int_{0}^{1} u\,du = \int_{0}^{1} u\,du = \frac{1}{2}.$$

(Equivalently, $|n_z|$ is uniform on $[0,1]$ with mean $1/2$.)

### Step 4. Average projected area

By linearity of expectation,
$$A = \mathbb{E}\bigl[A_{\text{proj}}\bigr] = NM\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[|n_z|\bigr] =
NM \cdot \frac{1}{2}.$$

### Verification via Cauchy's surface area formula

As a consistency check: viewing the rectangle as a degenerate 3D convex body wit
h two faces, its total surface area is $2NM$. Cauchy's formula states that the m
ean projected area of a convex body over all orientations equals one quarter of
its surface area:
$$\bar{A} = \frac{1}{4}\cdot 2NM = \frac{NM}{2},$$
confirming the direct computation.

### Result

$$\boxed{A = \dfrac{NM}{2}}$$
