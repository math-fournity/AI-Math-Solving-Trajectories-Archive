\[

k\ge 0\right\}

\[
\varphi(1)(x)=2x,\qquad x\in A.
\]

\[
G=A\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{Z}.
\]
Concretely, elements of \(G\) are pairs \((x,n)\) with \(x\in A,\;n\in\mathbb{Z}

\[
(x,n)(y,m)=\bigl(x+2^{n}y,\;n+m\bigr).
\]
Since both \(A\) and \(\mathbb{Z}\) are infinite, \(G\) is an infinite group.

**The subgroup \(H\).** Set \(a=(1,0)\in G\) and let
\[
H=\langle a\rangle=\{(k,0):k\in\mathbb{Z}\}.
\]
Because \(A\) is torsion‑free, \(a\) has infinite order, so \(H\cong\mathbb{Z}\)

**The conjugating element.** Let \(g=(0,1)\in G\), whose inverse is \(g^{-1}=(0,
-1)\). Compute the conjugate of the generator:
\[
g\,a\,g^{-1}=(0,1)(1,0)(0,-1).
\]
First,
\[
(0,1)(1,0)=\bigl(0+2^{1}\cdot 1,\;1+0\bigr)=(2,1),
\]
then
\[
(2,1)(0,-1)=\bigl(2+2^{1}\cdot 0,\;1-1\bigr)=(2,0)=a^{2}.
\]
Hence
\[
g\,a\,g^{-1}=a^{2},
\]

\[
gHg^{-1}=\langle a^{2}\rangle=\{(2k,0):k\in\mathbb{Z}\}.
