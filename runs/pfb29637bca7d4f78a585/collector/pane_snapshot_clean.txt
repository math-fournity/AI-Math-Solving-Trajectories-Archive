## Solution

**Simplification.** Observe that
$$1+\left(\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}-1\right)=\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n},$$
so the expression becomes
$$a_{n+1}\left(\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right)^n.$$

We assume $b\neq 0$ (the limit is expressed in terms of $b$; the case $b=0$ is d
egenerate and cannot be determined from $L$ alone).

**Key estimate.** Set $d_n = a_{n+1}-a_n$. Since $a_n\to b<\infty$, we have $d_n
\to 0$. The hypothesis gives $n\,d_n \to L$.

Consider the logarithm of the power:
$$n\ln\!\left(\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right)=n\ln\!\left(1+\frac{d_n}{a_n}\right).$$

Since $d_n\to 0$ and $a_n\to b\neq 0$, we use $\ln(1+x)=x+O(x^2)$ for small $x$:

$$n\ln\!\left(1+\frac{d_n}{a_n}\right)=n\cdot\frac{d_n}{a_n}+n\cdot O\!\left(\fr
ac{d_n^2}{a_n^2}\right).$$

**First term:**
$$n\cdot\frac{d_n}{a_n}=\frac{n\,d_n}{a_n}\longrightarrow \frac{L}{b},$$
since $n\,d_n\to L$ and $a_n\to b$.

**Remainder term:**
$$n\cdot\frac{d_n^2}{a_n^2}=\frac{(n\,d_n)\cdot d_n}{a_n^2}\longrightarrow \frac
{L\cdot 0}{b^2}=0,$$
since $n\,d_n\to L$ (bounded) and $d_n\to 0$.

Therefore
$$n\ln\!\left(\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right)\longrightarrow \frac{L}{b},$$
and consequently
$$\left(\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right)^n \longrightarrow e^{L/b}.$$

**Combining.** Since $a_{n+1}\to b$ and $\left(\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right)^n\to e
^{L/b}$, by the product rule for limits:

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n+1}\left[1+\left(\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}-1\right)\right]^n =
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n+1}\left(\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right)^n = b\cdot e^{L/b}.$$

$$\boxed{\,b\,e^{\,L/b}\,}$$
