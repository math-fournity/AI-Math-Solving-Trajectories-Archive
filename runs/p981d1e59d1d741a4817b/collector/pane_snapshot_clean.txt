## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, such a non-trivial linear functional exists.

**Setup.** Let $X = C[0,1]$ equipped with the $L^2$ norm $\|f\|_2 = \bigl(\int_0
^1 |f(t)|^2\,dt\bigr)^{1/2}$, and inner product $\langle f,g\rangle = \int_0^1 f
(t)g(t)\,dt$. Note $X$ is an inner-product space but is **not complete**; its co
mpletion is the Hilbert space $L^2[0,1]$, and $X$ is dense in $L^2[0,1]$.

**Construction.** Define
$$
F(f) = \int_0^{1/2} f(t)\,dt, \qquad f \in X.
$$
Equivalently $F(f) = \langle f,\, \mathbf{1}_{[0,1/2]}\rangle_{L^2}$ where $\mat
hbf{1}_{[0,1/2]}$ is the indicator of $[0,1/2]$.

**$F$ is a bounded (non-trivial) linear functional on $X$.** Linearity is immedi
ate. For boundedness, by Cauchy–Schwarz in $L^2$,
$$
|F(f)| = \left|\int_0^{1/2} f(t)\,dt\right| \le \|f\|_2 \cdot \|\mathbf{1}_{[0,1
/2]}\|_2 = \|f\|_2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.
$$
So $F \in X^*$ with $\|F\| \le 1/\sqrt{2}$ (in fact equality holds, e.g. take $f
_n$ approximating $\mathbf{1}_{[0,1/2]}$). $F$ is non-trivial since $F(\mathbf{1
}) = 1/2 \ne 0$.

**$F$ is not represented by any $g \in X$.** Suppose for contradiction that some
 $g \in C[0,1]$ satisfies
$$
F(f) = \langle f, g\rangle = \int_0^1 f(t)\,g(t)\,dt \quad\text{for all } f \in
X.
$$
Then for all $f \in C[0,1]$,
$$
\int_0^1 f(t)\bigl(g(t) - \mathbf{1}_{[0,1/2]}(t)\bigr)\,dt = 0.
$$
Since $C[0,1]$ is dense in $L^2[0,1]$, this implies $g - \mathbf{1}_{[0,1/2]} =
0$ in $L^2[0,1]$, i.e.
$$
g = \mathbf{1}_{[0,1/2]} \quad\text{a.e. on } [0,1].
$$

Now we show this is impossible for a continuous $g$:

- On $[0,\,1/2]$: $g = 1$ a.e. A continuous function equal to $1$ a.e. on an int
erval must equal $1$ **everywhere** on that interval (otherwise a point where $g
 \ne 1$ has a neighborhood where $g \ne 1$ on a set of positive measure). Hence
$g(t)=1$ for all $t \in [0,1/2]$, in particular $g(1/2)=1$.
- On $[1/2,\,1]$: $g = 0$ a.e. By the same reasoning, $g(t)=0$ for all $t \in [1
/2,1]$, in particular $g(1/2)=0$.

This gives $g(1/2) = 1$ and $g(1/2) = 0$, a contradiction. Therefore no such con
tinuous $g$ exists.

**Conclusion.** $F(f) = \int_0^{1/2} f(t)\,dt$ is a non-trivial bounded linear f
unctional on $(C[0,1], \|\cdot\|_2)$ that cannot be written as $F(f)=\langle f,g
\rangle$ for any $g \in C[0,1]$. The obstruction is that $X$ is incomplete: its
dual is identified (via density in $L^2$ and the Riesz theorem) with $L^2[0,1]$,
 which is strictly larger than $X$ itself, so the natural map $X \hookrightarrow
 X^*$, $g \mapsto \langle\cdot,g\rangle$, is **not surjective**.
