## Proof

**Setup and notation.** Let $W_1, W_2, \ldots$ be i.i.d. and let $W$ be a random
 variable with $W \sim W_i$, independent of $\{W_i\}_{i \geq 1}$ (as indicated b
y the notation $P_W$ and "another random variable"). Let $V_n = V(W_1, \ldots, W
_n)$ and let $f \geq 0$ be a non-negative function.

**Hypothesis.** For every fixed $\epsilon > 0$,
$$g_n(V_n) := P_W\!\left\{f(V_n, W) > \epsilon\right\} \xrightarrow{P} 0 \quad \
text{as } n \to \infty,$$
where $g_n(V_n)$ is a random variable (random through $W_1, \ldots, W_n$), and "
$\xrightarrow{P}$" denotes convergence in probability over $W_1, \ldots, W_n$.

**Goal.** Show $f(V_n, W) \xrightarrow{P} 0$, i.e., for every $\epsilon > 0$,
$$P\!\left(f(V_n, W) > \epsilon\right) \to 0,$$
where the probability is over all randomness ($W_1, \ldots, W_n$ and $W$). Since
 $f \geq 0$, this is equivalent to $|f(V_n, W)| \xrightarrow{P} 0$.

**Key step: law of total probability.** Since $W$ is independent of $W_1, \ldots
, W_n$, conditioning on $W_1, \ldots, W_n$ (equivalently, on $V_n$) gives
$$P\!\left(f(V_n, W) > \epsilon\right) = E\!\left[\,P_W\!\left\{f(V_n, W) > \eps
ilon\right\}\right] = E\!\left[g_n(V_n)\right].$$

**Bounded convergence argument.** Note that $g_n(V_n)$ is a probability, so $0 \
leq g_n(V_n) \leq 1$ for all $n$. Fix any $\delta > 0$. Then
$$E[g_n(V_n)] = E\!\left[g_n(V_n)\,\mathbf{1}_{\{g_n(V_n) > \delta\}}\right] + E
\!\left[g_n(V_n)\,\mathbf{1}_{\{g_n(V_n) \leq \delta\}}\right].$$

Bounding each term:
- First term: $E[g_n\,\mathbf{1}_{\{g_n > \delta\}}] \leq 1 \cdot P(g_n > \delta
) \to 0$ by the hypothesis ($g_n \xrightarrow{P} 0$).
- Second term: $E[g_n\,\mathbf{1}_{\{g_n \leq \delta\}}] \leq \delta \cdot 1 = \
delta$.

Therefore,
$$\limsup_{n \to \infty} E[g_n(V_n)] \leq \delta.$$

Since $\delta > 0$ was arbitrary, $\limsup_{n \to \infty} E[g_n(V_n)] = 0$, and
since $E[g_n(V_n)] \geq 0$,
$$E[g_n(V_n)] \to 0.$$

(Equivalently, this follows directly from the **Bounded Convergence Theorem**: $
g_n \xrightarrow{P} 0$ and $0 \leq g_n \leq 1$ imply $E[g_n] \to 0$.)

**Conclusion.** Combining,
$$P\!\left(f(V_n, W) > \epsilon\right) = E[g_n(V_n)] \to 0 \quad \text{for every
 } \epsilon > 0.$$

Since $f \geq 0$, we have $|f(V_n, W)| = f(V_n, W)$, so
$$P\!\left(|f(V_n, W)| > \epsilon\right) \to 0 \quad \text{for every } \epsilon
> 0.$$

**The answer is yes:** $P_W\{f(V_n, W) > \epsilon\} \to 0$ in probability implie
s $|f(V_n, W)| \to 0$ in probability.

The essential reason is that $g_n(V_n) = P_W\{f(V_n,W)>\epsilon\}$ is bounded in
 $[0,1]$, so convergence in probability of a bounded random variable implies con
vergence of its expectation (by bounded convergence), and the law of total proba
bility bridges the conditional statement to the unconditional one.
