## Proof

We show that **both** τ₃ and τ₄ are the discrete topology on ℝ³. Hence τ₃ = τ₄ (
both equal to the discrete topology).

Recall: the topology generated by a subbase 𝓑 is the smallest topology containin
g 𝓑; its basic open sets are finite intersections of members of 𝓑. Thus, to prov
e a singleton {p} is open, it suffices to exhibit it as a finite intersection of
 subbase elements.

### Step 1 — Every singleton is open in τ₃ (subbase = spheres)

Fix p ∈ ℝ³. By translating, assume p = 0. Consider two spheres

- Σ₁ = {x ∈ ℝ³ : ‖x − (1,0,0)‖² = 1},  centered at (1,0,0), radius 1,
- Σ₂ = {x ∈ ℝ³ : ‖x − (2,0,0)‖² = 4},  centered at (2,0,0), radius 2.

Both pass through the origin (distance from each center to 0 equals the correspo
nding radius). Their centers and the origin are collinear along the x-axis, so t
he two spherical surfaces are tangent at the origin (they share the tangent plan
e {x = 0} there).

Compute the intersection explicitly. Writing x = (a,b,c):

- On Σ₁: a² + b² + c² − 2a + 1 = 1, i.e. a² + b² + c² = 2a.
- On Σ₂: a² + b² + c² − 4a + 4 = 4, i.e. a² + b² + c² = 4a.

Subtracting gives 2a = 4a, hence a = 0. Substituting back: b² + c² = 0, so b = c
 = 0. Therefore

Σ₁ ∩ Σ₂ = {(0,0,0)} = {p}.

This is a finite intersection of two subbase elements of τ₃, hence a basic open
set of τ₃. So {p} ∈ τ₃ for every p ∈ ℝ³.

Since every subset of ℝ³ is a union of singletons and a topology is closed under
 arbitrary unions, **every subset of ℝ³ is open in τ₃**. Thus τ₃ is the discrete
 topology.

### Step 2 — Every singleton is open in τ₄ (subbase = circles)

Fix p ∈ ℝ³; by translation assume p = 0. Let

- L = the x-axis,
- P₁ = the xy-plane,  P₂ = the xz-plane,  so P₁ ∩ P₂ = L.

Define two circles (each lying in its respective plane):

- γ₁ ⊂ P₁: center (0,1,0), radius 1. This circle passes through 0 (distance from
 (0,1,0) to 0 is 1). Moreover L is tangent to γ₁ at 0, because the nearest point
 of L to the center (0,1,0) is 0, at distance exactly 1 = radius. Hence γ₁ ∩ L =
 {0}.
- γ₂ ⊂ P₂: center (0,0,1), radius 1. This circle passes through 0 (distance from
 (0,0,1) to 0 is 1).

Now γ₁ ⊂ P₁ and γ₂ ⊂ P₂, so

γ₁ ∩ γ₂ ⊆ P₁ ∩ P₂ = L.

Combining with γ₁ ∩ L = {0}:

γ₁ ∩ γ₂ ⊆ γ₁ ∩ L = {0}.

Since 0 ∈ γ₁ and 0 ∈ γ₂, we have 0 ∈ γ₁ ∩ γ₂, and therefore

γ₁ ∩ γ₂ = {0} = {p}.

This is a finite intersection of two subbase elements of τ₄, hence a basic open
set of τ₄. So {p} ∈ τ₄ for every p ∈ ℝ³.

As in Step 1, arbitrary unions of singletons give every subset, so **every subse
t of ℝ³ is open in τ₄**. Thus τ₄ is the discrete topology.

### Step 3 — Conclusion

Both τ₃ and τ₄ are the discrete topology on ℝ³. Therefore

**τ₃ = τ₄** (and in fact both coincide with the discrete topology on ℝ³).

Intuitively: two tangent spheres already isolate a single point, and two suitabl
y placed circles (one tangent to the line of intersection of their planes at the
 point) also isolate a single point. Once every singleton is a basic open set, t
he generated topology is discrete, regardless of whether we started from spheres
 or from circles.
