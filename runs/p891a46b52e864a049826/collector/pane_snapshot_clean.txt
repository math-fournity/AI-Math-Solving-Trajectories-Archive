## Proof

We analyze the partial product

$$P_n = \prod_{k=2}^{n} \frac{p_k^2}{(p_k-2)\,p_{k+1}}.$$

**Step 1: Factor and telescope.** Split each factor:

$$\frac{p_k^2}{(p_k-2)\,p_{k+1}} = \frac{p_k}{p_{k+1}} \cdot \frac{p_k}{p_k-2}.$
$

The first part telescopes:

$$\prod_{k=2}^{n} \frac{p_k}{p_{k+1}} = \frac{p_2}{p_{n+1}} = \frac{3}{p_{n+1}}.
$$

Hence

$$P_n = \frac{3}{p_{n+1}} \cdot Q_n, \qquad Q_n := \prod_{k=2}^{n} \frac{p_k}{p_
k-2} = \prod_{k=2}^{n} \frac{1}{1-2/p_k}.$$

**Step 2: Relate $Q_n$ to a Mertens-type product.** For each prime $p \geq 3$,

$$1 - \frac{2}{p} = \left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^2 \cdot \frac{p(p-2)}{(p-1)^2}.$$

Therefore

$$\prod_{k=2}^{n}\left(1-\frac{2}{p_k}\right) = \underbrace{\left[\prod_{k=2}^{n
}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_k}\right)\right]^2}_{(A)} \cdot \underbrace{\prod_{k=2}^{n}\
frac{p_k(p_k-2)}{(p_k-1)^2}}_{(B)}.$$

**Step 3: Product (B) converges to a positive constant.** Since

$$\frac{p(p-2)}{(p-1)^2} = 1 - \frac{1}{(p-1)^2},$$

and $\sum_{p} \frac{1}{(p-1)^2} \leq \sum_{m\geq 2}\frac{1}{m^2} < \infty$, the
infinite product (B) converges to a positive constant $C_2$ (the twin-prime cons
tant):

$$\prod_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{p_k(p_k-2)}{(p_k-1)^2} = C_2 > 0.$$

**Step 4: Apply Mertens' theorem to product (A).** By Mertens' third theorem,

$$\prod_{p \leq x}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right) \sim \frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\log x},$$

so with $x = p_n$:

$$\prod_{k=2}^{n}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_k}\right) \sim \frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\log p_n}.
$$

Combining Steps 3 and 4:

$$\prod_{k=2}^{n}\left(1-\frac{2}{p_k}\right) \sim \frac{C_2\, e^{-2\gamma}}{(\l
og p_n)^2},$$

and inverting,

$$Q_n = \prod_{k=2}^{n}\frac{1}{1-2/p_k} \sim \frac{e^{2\gamma}\,(\log p_n)^2}{C
_2}.$$

**Step 5: Assemble and conclude.** Substituting back,

$$P_n = \frac{3}{p_{n+1}}\cdot Q_n \sim \frac{3\,e^{2\gamma}\,(\log p_n)^2}{C_2\
,p_{n+1}}.$$

By the Prime Number Theorem, $p_n \sim n\log n$, hence $\log p_n \sim \log n$ an
d $p_{n+1} \sim n\log n$. Thus

$$P_n \sim \frac{3\,e^{2\gamma}\,(\log n)^2}{C_2\,n\log n} = \frac{3\,e^{2\gamma
}}{C_2}\cdot\frac{\log n}{n} \;\longrightarrow\; 0.$$

The factor $\frac{\log n}{n} \to 0$ is elementary, while $\frac{3\,e^{2\gamma}}{
C_2}$ is a fixed positive constant. Therefore

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} P_n = 0.}$$

The infinite product **converges to 0**.
