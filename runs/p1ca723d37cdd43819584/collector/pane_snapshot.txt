**Statement:** A Brownian motion is a semimartingale.

**Answer: True.**

## Proof

Let $(B_t)_{t \geq 0}$ be a standard Brownian motion on a filtered probability s
pace $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, (\mathcal{F}_t), \mathbb{P})$, where the filtration
satisfies the usual conditions. We show that $B$ is a semimartingale.

**Definition.** A process $X$ is a *semimartingale* if it admits a decomposition
$$
X_t = X_0 + M_t + A_t,
$$
where $M$ is a local martingale with $M_0 = 0$ and $A$ is a càdlàg adapted proce
ss of finite variation (with $A_0 = 0$).

**Key facts about Brownian motion:**

1. **Brownian motion is a continuous local martingale.** By construction, $B$ ha
s independent increments with $B_t - B_s \sim N(0, t-s)$ for $s \leq t$, and is
adapted to $(\mathcal{F}_t)$. Hence $\mathbb{E}[B_t \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = B_s$,
so $B$ is a martingale (in particular, a local martingale).

2. **Brownian motion has continuous paths.** By definition, $t \mapsto B_t(\omeg
a)$ is continuous almost surely.

**The decomposition.** We write
$$
B_t = \underbrace{B_0}_{=0} + \underbrace{B_t - B_0}_{=B_t} + \underbrace{0}_{A_
t}.
$$
That is, we take:
- $M_t = B_t - B_0 = B_t$, which is a (continuous) local martingale with $M_0 =
0$,
- $A_t = 0$, which is a continuous adapted process of finite variation (the zero
 process has total variation $0$ on every compact interval) with $A_0 = 0$.

This gives the semimartingale decomposition
$$
B_t = 0 + B_t + 0.
$$

**Verification of the requirements:**
- $M = B$ is a local martingale: confirmed by the independent-increments propert
y and the fact that $\mathbb{E}[|B_t|] < \infty$ for all $t$ (so $B$ is even a t
rue martingale).
- $A \equiv 0$ is càdlàg, adapted, and of finite variation: trivially satisfied.
- $B_0 = 0$ is $\mathcal{F}_0$-measurable.

Therefore, Brownian motion admits a semimartingale decomposition, and is a semim
artingale. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** More generally, every continuous local martingale is a semimartingal
e (via the same trivial decomposition with $A \equiv 0$). The result also follow
s from the Bichteler–Dellacherie theorem, since Brownian motion is a *good integ
rator*: the stochastic integral $\int H\, dB$ is well-defined for all locally bo
unded predictable $H$, which characterizes semimartingales. Brownian motion is i
n fact the prototypical example of a continuous semimartingale and serves as the
 building block of Itô calculus.
