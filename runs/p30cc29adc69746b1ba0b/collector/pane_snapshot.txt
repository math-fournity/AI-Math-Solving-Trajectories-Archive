**Answer: Yes.**

**Theorem.** Let $f \in C^\infty_c(\mathbb{R}^2)$ with $\operatorname{supp} f \s
ubseteq [a,b]\times[c,d]$, and let $\varepsilon>0$. Then there exist $m\ge 1$ an
d functions $g_i\in C^\infty_c(\mathbb{R})$, $h_i\in C^\infty_c(\mathbb{R})$ wit
h $\operatorname{supp} g_i\subseteq[a,b]$, $\operatorname{supp} h_i\subseteq[c,d
]$, such that
$$\Big\|f-\sum_{i=1}^m g_i(x)h_i(y)\Big\|_\infty < \varepsilon.$$

---

**Proof.**

Set $K=[a,b]\times[c,d]$. Since $f$ is smooth on $\mathbb{R}^2$ and vanishes on
$\mathbb{R}^2\setminus K$, $f$ vanishes to all orders on $\partial K$; in partic
ular $f$ is continuous and $f|_{\partial K}=0$.

**Step 1 (Stone–Weierstrass on a slightly larger rectangle).** Pick $\eta>0$ sma
ll (with $a+\eta<b-\eta$, $c+\eta<d-\eta$) and set $K_\eta=[a-\eta,b+\eta]\times
[c-\eta,d+\eta]$. Then $f=0$ on the open strip $K_\eta\setminus K$, hence $f=0$
in a neighborhood of $\partial K_\eta$.

The algebra $\mathcal{A}$ of finite sums $\sum p_i(x)q_i(y)$ with $p_i\in C([a-\
eta,b+\eta])$, $q_i\in C([c-\eta,d+\eta])$ contains the constants and separates
the points of $K_\eta$ (a function of $x$ alone separates points with different
first coordinates; a function of $y$ alone separates points with different secon
d coordinates). By the Stone–Weierstrass theorem, $\mathcal{A}$ is dense in $C(K
_\eta)$. Therefore there exist $p_i\in C([a-\eta,b+\eta])$, $q_i\in C([c-\eta,d+
\eta])$, $i=1,\dots,m$, such that, writing $S(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^m p_i(x)q_i(y)$,
$$\|f-S\|_{C(K_\eta)} < \frac{\varepsilon}{4}.$$
Since $f=0$ near $\partial K_\eta$, we have $|S|<\varepsilon/4$ there.

**Step 2 (Cutoff to enforce the support constraint).** Choose smooth cutoffs
$$\varphi\in C^\infty_c((a,b)),\quad 0\le\varphi\le 1,\quad \varphi\equiv 1\text
{ on }[a+\eta,b-\eta],$$
$$\psi\in C^\infty_c((c,d)),\quad 0\le\psi\le 1,\quad \psi\equiv 1\text{ on }[c+
\eta,d-\eta].$$
Shrinking $\eta$ if necessary (using that $f\to 0$ at $\partial K$), assume $|f|
<\varepsilon/4$ on the $\eta$-strip $\big([a,a+\eta]\cup[b-\eta,b]\big)\times[c,
d]\;\cup\;[a,b]\times\big([c,c+\eta]\cup[d-\eta,d]\big)$.

Define
$$T(x,y)=\varphi(x)\psi(y)\,S(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^m \big(\varphi\,p_i\big)(x)\,\big(
\psi\,q_i\big)(y).$$
Then $T$ is supported in $\operatorname{supp}\varphi\times\operatorname{supp}\ps
i\subseteq[a,b]\times[c,d]$, and each $\varphi\,p_i$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R
}$, supported in $[a,b]$ (hence vanishes at $a,b$); likewise each $\psi\,q_i$ is
 continuous, supported in $[c,d]$.

We estimate $\|f-T\|_{\mathbb{R}^2}$ (both $f$ and $T$ vanish outside $K_\eta$):

- *On $\{\varphi=\psi=1\}=[a+\eta,b-\eta]\times[c+\eta,d-\eta]$*: $T=S$, so $|f-
T|=|f-S|<\varepsilon/4$.
- *On $K_\eta\setminus K$ (near $\partial K_\eta$)*: $f=0$ and $|S|<\varepsilon/
4$, so $|T|\le|S|<\varepsilon/4$, giving $|f-T|<\varepsilon/4$.
- *On the $\eta$-strip inside $K$*: $|f|<\varepsilon/4$ and $|S|\le|f|+\|f-S\|<\
varepsilon/4+\varepsilon/4=\varepsilon/2$, so $|T|\le|S|<\varepsilon/2$ and $|f-
T|\le|f|+|T|<\varepsilon/4+\varepsilon/2=3\varepsilon/4<\varepsilon$.

Combining, $\|f-T\|_\infty < 3\varepsilon/4$.

**Step 3 (Smooth approximation of the one-variable factors).** We use the standa
rd fact:

> *$C^\infty_c((a,b))$ is dense in $\{u\in C(\mathbb{R}): \operatorname{supp}u\s
ubseteq[a,b]\}$ in the sup norm.*

*Justification:* Given such $u$ (so $u(a)=u(b)=0$) and $\delta>0$, first approxi
mate $u$ by $v\in C^\infty_c(\mathbb{R})$ with $\|u-v\|<\delta$ (density of $C^\
infty_c(\mathbb{R})$ in $C_0(\mathbb{R})$). Pick $\rho\in C^\infty_c((a,b))$ wit
h $\rho\equiv1$ on $[a+\eta',b-\eta']$. Then $\rho v\in C^\infty_c((a,b))$; wher
e $\rho=1$, $\rho v=v\approx u$; where $\rho\ne1$ (near $a,b$), $|u|$ is small (
it vanishes at the endpoints) and $|\rho v|\le|v|\le|u|+\delta$ is small, so $\|
u-\rho v\|$ can be made $<\delta$ by choosing $\eta'$ appropriately. ∎

Applying this to each $u_i:=\varphi\,p_i$ (supported in $[a,b]$) and each $v_i:=
\psi\,q_i$ (supported in $[c,d]$), choose
$$g_i\in C^\infty_c((a,b))\subseteq C^\infty_c(\mathbb{R}),\qquad h_i\in C^\inft
y_c((c,d))\subseteq C^\infty_c(\mathbb{R})$$
with $\operatorname{supp}g_i\subseteq[a,b]$, $\operatorname{supp}h_i\subseteq[c,
d]$, and with the tolerances $\|u_i-g_i\|$, $\|v_i-h_i\|$ small enough that
$$\Big\|T-\sum_{i=1}^m g_i\,h_i\Big\|_\infty < \frac{\varepsilon}{4}.$$
This is possible because
$$T-\sum_i g_i h_i=\sum_i\big[(u_i-g_i)v_i+g_i(v_i-h_i)\big],$$
and there are only finitely many indices, so the bound is a finite sum of terms
each controllable by the individual tolerances (with $\|v_i\|,\|g_i\|\le\|u_i\|+
\delta_i$ bounded).

**Step 4 (Conclusion).** By the triangle inequality,
$$\Big\|f-\sum_{i=1}^m g_i(x)h_i(y)\Big\|_\infty \le \|f-T\|_\infty + \Big\|T-\s
um_i g_i h_i\Big\|_\infty < \frac{3\varepsilon}{4}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}=\vareps
ilon.$$

Each $g_i$ is smooth with compact support in $[a,b]$, each $h_i$ is smooth with
compact support in $[c,d]$, and the approximation holds on all of $\mathbb{R}^2$
 (both sides vanish outside $[a,b]\times[c,d]$). ∎
