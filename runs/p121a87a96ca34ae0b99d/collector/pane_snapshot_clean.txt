## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** For any infinite standard set $X$, under sufficiently strong sa
turation (specifically $(2^{|X|})^+$-saturation), there exist distinct $\alpha,
\beta \in {}^*X$ with $\alpha \sim \beta$.

---

**Setup.** Let $X$ be an infinite set. Fix a nonstandard point $\alpha \in {}^*X
 \setminus X$ (such points exist since $X$ is infinite and ${}^*X \supsetneq X$)
. We will find $\beta \neq \alpha$ with $U_\beta = U_\alpha$.

Consider the following type $p(\beta)$ over ${}^*X$, with one formula for each s
ubset $A \subseteq X$:

$$p(\beta) \;=\; \{\, \beta \neq \alpha \,\} \;\cup\; \{\, \beta \in {}^*A : A \
subseteq X,\; \alpha \in {}^*A \,\} \;\cup\; \{\, \beta \notin {}^*A : A \subset
eq X,\; \alpha \notin {}^*A \,\}.$$

A realization $\beta$ of $p$ satisfies: $\beta \neq \alpha$ and, for every $A \s
ubseteq X$, $\beta \in {}^*A \iff \alpha \in {}^*A$, which is exactly $U_\beta =
 U_\alpha$.

The type $p$ has $2^{|X|}$ formulas (one per subset of $X$).

**Finite satisfiability.** Let $A_1, \dots, A_n \subseteq X$ be any finite subco
llection. The sets $A_1, \dots, A_n$ partition $X$ into at most $2^n$ atoms $B$
(each atom is an intersection of some $A_i$'s and complements of the rest). Sinc
e the ${}^*$-map preserves finite Boolean operations, the sets ${}^*B$ partition
 ${}^*X$, so $\alpha$ lies in exactly one ${}^*B_0$.

**Claim:** $B_0$ is infinite. Indeed, $\alpha \in {}^*B_0$ but $\alpha \notin X
\supseteq B_0$, so $\alpha \in {}^*B_0 \setminus B_0$. If $B_0$ were finite, the
n ${}^*B_0 = B_0$ (the ${}^*$-map fixes finite sets), contradicting $\alpha \in
{}^*B_0 \setminus B_0$. Hence $B_0$ is infinite.

Since $B_0$ is infinite, ${}^*B_0$ is infinite as well (it contains $B_0$, which
 is infinite). Therefore there exists $\beta \in {}^*B_0$ with $\beta \neq \alph
a$. This $\beta$ lies in exactly the same ${}^*A_i$ as $\alpha$ (for $i = 1, \do
ts, n$), so $\beta$ realizes the finite sub-type. This establishes finite satisf
iability.

**Saturation applies.** The type $p$ is finitely satisfiable and consists of $2^
{|X|}$ formulas. Under $(2^{|X|})^+$-saturation — i.e., saturation at a cardinal
 strictly exceeding $2^{|X|}$ — every finitely satisfiable type of size $\leq 2^
{|X|}$ is realized. Therefore $p$ is realized by some $\beta \in {}^*X$.

**Conclusion.** This $\beta$ satisfies $\beta \neq \alpha$ and $U_\beta = U_\alp
ha$, i.e., $\alpha \sim \beta$ with $\alpha \neq \beta$. $\square$

**Remark.** The argument shows that every nonstandard point $\alpha \in {}^*X \s
etminus X$ has a "twin" under $\sim$ when saturation is strong enough. The key m
echanism is that the family of internal sets $\{{}^*A : A \subseteq X\}$ has onl
y $2^{|X|}$ members, so it cannot separate all points of ${}^*X$ once $|{}^*X|$
is large enough — and saturation forces $|{}^*X|$ to be large. Standard points $
x \in X$ are immune: ${}^*\{x\} = \{x\}$, so $U_x$ is the principal ultrafilter
at $x$, realized only by $x$ itself.
