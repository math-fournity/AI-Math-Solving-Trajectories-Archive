## Proof

**Axioms.** For all $x, y \in S$:
1. $x \cdot (x \cdot y) = y$ (left division / $L_x$ is an involution),
2. $(y \cdot x) \cdot x = y$ (right division / $R_x$ is an involution).

Both axioms state that left-multiplication $L_x: y \mapsto x\cdot y$ and right-m
ultiplication $R_x: y \mapsto y\cdot x$ are bijections (in fact involutions), so
 each is injective. We use injectivity freely below.

### Commutativity

**Step 1.** Apply (1) with $x$ replaced by $y\cdot x$ and $y$ replaced by $y$:
$$(y\cdot x)\cdot\bigl((y\cdot x)\cdot y\bigr)=y.$$

**Step 2.** Apply (2) with $y$ replaced by $y$ and $x$ replaced by $x$:
$$(y\cdot x)\cdot x = y.$$

**Step 3.** From Steps 1 and 2,
$$(y\cdot x)\cdot\bigl((y\cdot x)\cdot y\bigr) = (y\cdot x)\cdot x.$$
Since $L_{y\cdot x}$ is injective (it is an involution by (1)), we may cancel it
 on the left:
$$(y\cdot x)\cdot y = x. \qquad (\star)$$

**Step 4.** Apply (2) with the variable $y$ set to $x$ and $x$ set to $y$ (i.e.,
 use $(a\cdot b)\cdot b = a$ with $a=x, b=y$):
$$(x\cdot y)\cdot y = x. \qquad (\star\star)$$

**Step 5.** From $(\star)$ and $(\star\star)$,
$$(y\cdot x)\cdot y = (x\cdot y)\cdot y.$$
Since $R_y$ is injective (it is an involution by (2)), we may cancel it on the r
ight:
$$y\cdot x = x\cdot y.$$

Hence $\cdot$ is commutative. $\square$

### Not necessarily associative

Consider $S = \{0,1,2\}$ with
$$x \cdot y \;=\; -(x+y) \pmod{3}.$$

**Check (1):** $\;x\cdot(x\cdot y) = x\cdot(-(x+y)) = -\bigl(x + (-(x+y))\bigr)
= -(x - x - y) = -(-y) = y.$ ✓

**Check (2):** $\;(y\cdot x)\cdot x = (-(y+x))\cdot x = -\bigl(-(y+x) + x\bigr)
= -(-y - x + x) = -(-y) = y.$ ✓

So both axioms hold (and indeed the operation is commutative, as it must be).

**Associativity fails.** Compute with $x=0,\; y=0,\; z=1$:
$$(0\cdot 0)\cdot 1 = (-(0+0))\cdot 1 = 0\cdot 1 = -(0+1) = -1 \equiv 2 \pmod 3,
$$
$$0\cdot(0\cdot 1) = 0\cdot(-(0+1)) = 0\cdot(-1) = 0\cdot 2 = -(0+2) = -2 \equiv
 1 \pmod 3.$$
Since $2 \neq 1 \pmod 3$, we have $(x\cdot y)\cdot z \neq x\cdot(y\cdot z)$, so
$\cdot$ is **not** associative.

(More generally, $(x\cdot y)\cdot z = x+y-z$ while $x\cdot(y\cdot z) = -x+y+z \p
mod 3$; these agree only when $x \equiv z \pmod 3$.)

### Conclusion

The two axioms (1) and (2) force $\cdot$ to be commutative, but they do not forc
e associativity, as demonstrated by the operation $x\cdot y = -(x+y) \pmod 3$ on
 $\{0,1,2\}$.
