## Proof

**Numerator.** The standard Gaussian integral gives
$$\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-x^2}\,dx = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}.$$

**Denominator.** We evaluate $I = \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-x^2}\cos(2x)\,dx$ using
the Fourier transform of a Gaussian.

For $a>0$, complete the square:
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-ax^2}e^{i\omega x}\,dx = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e
^{-a\left(x-\frac{i\omega}{2a}\right)^2 - \frac{\omega^2}{4a}}\,dx = e^{-\omega^
2/(4a)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-au^2}\,du = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}\,e^{-\omega^
2/(4a)}.$$

Taking the real part (with $a=1$, $\omega=2$):
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-x^2}\cos(2x)\,dx = \sqrt{\pi}\,e^{-1}.$$

Since $e^{-x^2}\cos(2x)$ is even in $x$,
$$\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-x^2}\cos(2x)\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^
{-x^2}\cos(2x)\,dx = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\,e^{-1}.$$

**Ratio.** Therefore
$$\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-x^2}\,dx}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\inf
ty} e^{-x^2}\cos(2x)\,dx} = \frac{\dfrac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}}{\dfrac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\,
e^{-1}} = e.$$
