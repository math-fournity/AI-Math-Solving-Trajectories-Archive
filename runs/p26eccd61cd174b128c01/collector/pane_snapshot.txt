## Proof

fty\}$ with the unit sphere $S^2 \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ via stereographic project
ion from the north pole $N=(0,0,1)$.

**Key facts under stereographic projection:**
- A **line** on the Riemann sphere is a line in $\mathbb{C}$, i.e., a generalize
d circle passing through $\infty$. On $S^2$, this corresponds to a circle passin
g through $N$.
- A **geodesic** on $S^2$ (with the standard round metric) is a **great circle**
 — the intersection of $S^2$ with a plane through the origin.
- A **line through the origin** in $\mathbb{C}$ corresponds to a great circle on
 $S^2$: the plane of the corresponding circle passes through both $N$ and the ce
nter (origin) of $S^2$.

**Construction.** Let $L$ be any line in $\mathbb{C}$ (a generalized circle thro
ugh $\infty$). Pick any point $z_0 \in L$ and consider the Möbius transformation
:
$$f(z) = \frac{1}{z - z_0}$$

This is an automorphism of the Riemann sphere (an element of $PGL(2,\mathbb{C})$
), which maps generalized circles to generalized circles.

**Verification.** Since $z_0 \in L$ and $\infty \in L$ (as $L$ is a line), we ha
ve:
$$f(z_0) = \infty, \qquad f(\infty) = 0$$

Therefore $f(L)$ is a generalized circle containing both $0$ and $\infty$, which
 means $f(L)$ is a **line through the origin** in $\mathbb{C}$.

As established above, a line through the origin in $\mathbb{C}$ corresponds to a
 **great circle** on $S^2$, hence a geodesic.

**Conclusion.** Every line $L$ on the Riemann sphere can be transformed into a g
eodesic via the Möbius transformation $f(z) = \frac{1}{z - z_0}$ (for any $z_0 \
in L$).
