# Proof

**Claim.** Let $f$ be holomorphic on the punctured disk $\{z : 0 < |z-z_0| < R\}
$ (so that $z_0$ is an isolated singularity of $f$). Then $f$ admits a Laurent s
eries expansion about $z_0$:

$$f(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} a_n (z-z_0)^n,$$

converging absolutely on $0<|z-z_0|<R$, with the convergence uniform on every co
mpact sub-annulus $\{r \le |z-z_0| \le s\}$ for $0<r<s<R$.

---

## Construction of the expansion

Fix $z$ with $0 < |z-z_0| < R$. Choose radii $r, s$ with

$$0 < r < |z-z_0| < s < R.$$

Let $C_r$ and $C_s$ be the positively oriented circles $|w-z_0|=r$ and $|w-z_0|=
s$. The annulus $A = \{r \le |w-z_0| \le s\}$ lies in the domain of holomorphy o
f $f$, and $z$ lies in the interior of $A$ with $z$ not on either boundary. By t
he Cauchy integral formula on the annulus (a standard consequence of Cauchy's th
eorem applied to the domain obtained by cutting the annulus and noting the contr
ibutions along the cut cancel),

$$f(z) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C_s}\frac{f(w)}{w-z}\,dw \;-\; \frac{1}{2\pi i}\
oint_{C_r}\frac{f(w)}{w-z}\,dw.$$

(The minus sign reflects the orientation: the inner boundary of the annulus is t
raversed clockwise, equivalently $-C_r$ with positive orientation.)

### The outer integral

For $w \in C_s$ we have $|w-z_0| = s > |z-z_0|$, so

$$\frac{1}{w-z} = \frac{1}{(w-z_0)-(z-z_0)} = \frac{1}{w-z_0}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\df
rac{z-z_0}{w-z_0}} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(z-z_0)^n}{(w-z_0)^{n+1}},$$

the geometric series converging uniformly on $C_s$ (since $|z-z_0|/s < 1$). Unif
orm convergence permits termwise integration:

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C_s}\frac{f(w)}{w-z}\,dw = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\underbra
ce{\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C_s}\frac{f(w)}{(w-z_0)^{n+1}}\,dw\right)}_{a_n}
(z-z_0)^n.$$

### The inner integral

For $w \in C_r$ we have $|w-z_0| = r < |z-z_0|$, so we expand in the other direc
tion:

$$\frac{1}{w-z} = \frac{1}{(w-z_0)-(z-z_0)} = -\frac{1}{z-z_0}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\d
frac{w-z_0}{z-z_0}} = -\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(w-z_0)^m}{(z-z_0)^{m+1}},$$

converging uniformly on $C_r$ (since $r/|z-z_0|<1$). Writing $n = -(m+1)$, i.e.
$m = -n-1$ with $n \le -1$:

$$-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C_r}\frac{f(w)}{w-z}\,dw = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{-1}\under
brace{\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C_r}\frac{f(w)}{(w-z_0)^{n+1}}\,dw\right)}_{a
_n}(z-z_0)^n.$$

### Combining

$$f(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{-1} a_n(z-z_0)^n + \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n(z-z_0)^n =
 \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} a_n(z-z_0)^n,$$

where

$$a_n = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|\zeta-z_0|=\rho}\frac{f(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z_0)^{n+1
}}\,d\zeta, \qquad n\in\mathbb{Z},$$

for any $\rho \in (0,R)$.

---

## The coefficients are well-defined (independent of $\rho$)

We must verify $a_n$ does not depend on the choice of $\rho$. For $n\ge 0$, the
integrand $f(\zeta)/(\zeta-z_0)^{n+1}$ is holomorphic on the punctured disk, so
by Cauchy's theorem the integral over $|\zeta-z_0|=\rho_1$ equals that over $|\z
eta-z_0|=\rho_2$ for any $0<\rho_1<\rho_2<R$ (the annulus between them is contai
ned in the domain of holomorphy and contains no singularity of the integrand). H
ence $a_n$ is independent of $\rho$ for all $n\ge 0$.

For $n\le -1$, the same argument applies: the integrand $f(\zeta)/(\zeta-z_0)^{n
+1}$ is holomorphic on the punctured disk (the factor $(\zeta-z_0)^{n+1}$ with $
n+1\le 0$ does not introduce a singularity beyond $z_0$, which is already exclud
ed). Thus the integral is again independent of $\rho$.

So the coefficients $a_n$ are intrinsic to $f$ and $z_0$.

---

## Convergence on the full punctured disk

The construction above was carried out for an arbitrary $z$ with $0<|z-z_0|<R$,
using $r,s$ adapted to $z$. Since $r$ and $s$ can be chosen for every such $z$,
the series $\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}a_n(z-z_0)^n$ represents $f(z)$ at every po
int of the punctured disk.

For absolute convergence: fix $z$ with $0<|z-z_0|<R$. Pick $s$ with $|z-z_0|<s<R
$; the outer series $\sum_{n\ge 0}|a_n|\,|z-z_0|^n$ converges by comparison with
 the geometric series used in the outer expansion (root test: $\limsup |a_n|^{1/
n}\le 1/s$ for all $s<R$, so $\limsup |a_n|^{1/n}\le 1/R$). Similarly pick $r<|z
-z_0|$; the inner series $\sum_{n<0}|a_n|\,|z-z_0|^n$ converges by the root test
 on the negative part ($\limsup_{n\to-\infty}|a_n|^{1/|n|}\le R$ from the inner
expansion, giving $\limsup_{n\to-\infty}|a_n|^{1/|n|}\,|z-z_0|\le R\cdot|z-z_0|/
R<1$... more carefully: the inner geometric series gives $|a_n|^{1/|n|}\le 1/r$
for all $r>0$, so $\limsup_{n\to-\infty}|a_n|^{1/|n|}\le 0$ is not right—let me
redo).

Precisely: from the inner expansion with parameter $r$, the coefficients satisfy
 $|a_n|\,r^n \to 0$ boundedly for $n\to -\infty$, i.e. $|a_n| \le M_r / r^n = M_
r\, r^{|n|}$ for $n<0$, where $M_r = \max_{|\zeta-z_0|=r}|f(\zeta)|$. Then $|a_n
|\,|z-z_0|^n = |a_n|\,|z-z_0|^{-|n|} \le M_r(r/|z-z_0|)^{|n|}$, which is summabl
e since $r<|z-z_0|$. Taking $r$ arbitrarily close to $|z-z_0|$ shows the negativ
e part converges for every $z$ in the punctured disk. The symmetric argument for
 the positive part (using $s$ arbitrarily close to $|z-z_0|$ from above, and $s$
 up to $R$) gives convergence of the positive part. Hence the full Laurent serie
s converges absolutely on $0<|z-z_0|<R$.

Uniform convergence on compact sub-annuli $\{r\le|z-z_0|\le s\}$ with $0<r<s<R$
follows from the Weierstrass M-test using the bounds $|a_n|\,r^n \le M_{r'}$ (fo
r $n<0$, with $r'<r$) and $|a_n|\,s^n \le M_{s'}$ (for $n\ge 0$, with $s'>s$), g
iving geometric majorants.

---

## Uniqueness

Suppose $f(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} b_n(z-z_0)^n$ is another Laurent expan
sion converging on some punctured disk $0<|z-z_0|<R'$. Then on the overlap, $\su
m (a_n-b_n)(z-z_0)^n = 0$. Multiplying by $(z-z_0)^{-k-1}$ and integrating termw
ise over a circle $|z-z_0|=\rho$ (justified by uniform convergence on the circle
) gives $2\pi i\,(a_k - b_k) = 0$ for every $k\in\mathbb{Z}$, since $\oint (z-z_
0)^{n-k-1}dz = 2\pi i\,\delta_{n,k}$. Hence $a_k = b_k$ for all $k$: the Laurent
 expansion is unique.

---

## Conclusion

A function $f$ that is holomorphic on a punctured disk $\{0<|z-z_0|<R\}$—equival
ently, a function with an isolated singularity at $z_0$—**does** possess a Laure
nt series expansion about $z_0$, converging absolutely on the entire punctured d
isk and uniformly on compact sub-annuli, with uniquely determined coefficients

$$a_n = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|\zeta-z_0|=\rho}\frac{f(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z_0)^{n+1
}}\,d\zeta,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z},\; 0<\rho<R.$$

The principal part $\sum_{n<0}a_n(z-z_0)^n$ encodes the nature of the singularit
y: it is identically zero iff $z_0$ is removable, finite (with highest term $a_{
-m}(z-z_0)^{-m}$, $m\ge 1$) iff $z_0$ is a pole of order $m$, and infinite iff $
z_0$ is an essential singularity. In all three cases the Laurent expansion exist
s.
