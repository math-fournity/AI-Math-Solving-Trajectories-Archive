## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $f$ is uniformly continuous on $A$.

**Setup.** Since $|f(x) - f(u)| \geq 0$ and $|f(x) - f(u)| \leq g(x-u)$ for all
$x, u \in A$, we have $g(t) \geq 0$ for all $t \in A - A$ (every $t \in A-A$ is
of the form $t = x - u$ for some $x, u \in A$). Hence $g(t) = |g(t)|$ on $A - A$
.

The hypothesis $\lim_{x \to 0} g(x) = 0$ is a limit taken in the subspace $A - A
 \subseteq \mathbb{R}$: for every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists $\delta > 0$ s
uch that for all $t \in A - A$ with $0 < |t| < \delta$, we have $|g(t)| < \varep
silon$.

**Argument.** Let $\varepsilon > 0$. By the limit hypothesis, choose $\delta > 0
$ such that for all $t \in A - A$ with $0 < |t| < \delta$, $|g(t)| < \varepsilon
$.

Now take any $x, u \in A$ with $|x - u| < \delta$.

- **Case $x = u$:** $|f(x) - f(u)| = 0 < \varepsilon$. ✓

- **Case $x \neq u$:** Set $t = x - u$. Then $t \in A - A$ and $0 < |t| = |x - u
| < \delta$, so by the choice of $\delta$,
$$|f(x) - f(u)| \leq g(t) = |g(t)| < \varepsilon. \quad \checkmark$$

In both cases, $|f(x) - f(u)| < \varepsilon$.

**Conclusion.** For every $\varepsilon > 0$ we produced $\delta > 0$ such that f
or all $x, u \in A$ with $|x - u| < \delta$, $|f(x) - f(u)| < \varepsilon$. This
 is precisely the definition of uniform continuity of $f$ on $A$.
