## Proof

Let $H$ be an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space (over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{
C}$), $A: H \to H$ a bounded linear operator, $f \in H$ a fixed element, and def
ine the functional

$$J(u) = \|Au - f\|^2, \qquad u \in H.$$

We compute the Fréchet derivative of $J$ at $u$.

### Step 1. Expand $J(u+h)$

For an arbitrary perturbation $h \in H$, using linearity of $A$:

$$J(u+h) = \|A(u+h) - f\|^2 = \|(Au - f) + Ah\|^2.$$

Applying the Hilbert space identity $\|x + y\|^2 = \|x\|^2 + 2\operatorname{Re}\
langle x, y\rangle + \|y\|^2$ with $x = Au - f$ and $y = Ah$:

$$J(u+h) = \|Au - f\|^2 + 2\operatorname{Re}\langle Au - f,\, Ah\rangle + \|Ah\|
^2.$$

Subtracting $J(u) = \|Au - f\|^2$:

$$J(u+h) - J(u) = 2\operatorname{Re}\langle Au - f,\, Ah\rangle + \|Ah\|^2.$$

### Step 2. Identify the linear part

Since $A$ is bounded, it admits a bounded adjoint $A^*$. Using the defining prop
erty of the adjoint, $\langle Au - f,\, Ah\rangle = \langle A^*(Au - f),\, h\ran
gle$, so:

$$2\operatorname{Re}\langle Au - f,\, Ah\rangle = 2\operatorname{Re}\langle A^*(
Au - f),\, h\rangle.$$

This is a bounded linear functional of $h$ (bounded because $A^*$ is bounded). D
enote it by

$$L_u(h) := 2\operatorname{Re}\langle A^*(Au - f),\, h\rangle.$$

### Step 3. Show the remainder is $o(\|h\|)$

The remainder is $R(h) = \|Ah\|^2$. Since $A$ is bounded with operator norm $\|A
\|$:

$$|R(h)| = \|Ah\|^2 \le \|A\|^2 \|h\|^2.$$

Therefore

$$\frac{|R(h)|}{\|h\|} \le \|A\|^2 \|h\| \to 0 \quad \text{as } \|h\| \to 0.$$

Hence $R(h) = o(\|h\|)$.

### Step 4. Conclude

We have the decomposition

$$J(u+h) = J(u) + L_u(h) + o(\|h\|),$$

where $L_u$ is a bounded linear functional of $h$ and the remainder is $o(\|h\|)
$. By definition, $L_u$ is the Fréchet derivative of $J$ at $u$:

$$\boxed{J'(u)\,h \;=\; 2\operatorname{Re}\langle A^*(Au - f),\, h\rangle.}$$

Equivalently, identifying the derivative with its Riesz representer (real case,
or the real part convention in the complex case):

$$\boxed{\nabla J(u) = 2\,A^*(Au - f).}$$

Both forms are expressed solely in terms of $A$, $u$, and $f$, as required. $\bl
acksquare$
