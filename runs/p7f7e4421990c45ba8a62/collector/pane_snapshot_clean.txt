## Proof

**Goal.** Determine the asymptotic behavior of $S(N) := \sum_{n=1}^{N} \Omega(n)
$ as $N \to \infty$, where $\Omega(n)$ counts prime factors of $n$ with multipli
city.

**Claim.** $\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^{N} \Omega(n) = N \log\log N + O(N)$, hence
 $\sum_{n=1}^{N} \Omega(n) \sim N\log\log N$.

---

### Step 1. Re-express $\Omega(n)$ as a sum over prime powers

For each $n$, every prime factor counted with multiplicity corresponds to a prim
e power $p^k$ (with $p$ prime, $k \geq 1$) dividing $n$. Concretely, if $n = \pr
od p_i^{a_i}$, then $\Omega(n) = \sum_i a_i = \sum_{\substack{p^k \mid n \\ p\te
xt{ prime},\, k\ge 1}} 1$. Therefore

$$S(N) = \sum_{n=1}^{N} \sum_{\substack{p^k \mid n \\ p\text{ prime},\, k\ge 1}}
 1.$$

### Step 2. Swap the order of summation

A prime power $p^k$ contributes to those $n \leq N$ with $p^k \mid n$, i.e. to $
\lfloor N/p^k \rfloor$ values of $n$. Thus

$$S(N) = \sum_{\substack{p^k \leq N \\ p\text{ prime},\, k \ge 1}} \left\lfloor
\frac{N}{p^k} \right\rfloor.$$

### Step 3. Split into the $k=1$ (prime) contribution and the $k \geq 2$ (higher
 powers) contribution

$$S(N) = \underbrace{\sum_{p \leq N} \left\lfloor \frac{N}{p} \right\rfloor}_{=:
 A(N)} \;+\; \underbrace{\sum_{k=2}^{\infty} \sum_{p^k \leq N} \left\lfloor \fra
c{N}{p^k} \right\rfloor}_{=: B(N)}.$$

### Step 4. The higher-power part $B(N)$ is $O(N)$

Using $\lfloor x \rfloor \leq x$ and extending the inner sum to all primes (only
 increasing it),

$$B(N) \leq N \sum_{k=2}^{\infty} \sum_{p} \frac{1}{p^k} = N \sum_{p} \sum_{k=2}
^{\infty} \frac{1}{p^k} = N \sum_{p} \frac{1}{p(p-1)}.$$

The series $\sum_p \frac{1}{p(p-1)}$ converges, since $\frac{1}{p(p-1)} \leq \fr
ac{2}{p^2}$ for $p \geq 2$ and $\sum_p p^{-2} \leq \sum_{n=2}^{\infty} n^{-2} <
\infty$. Hence

$$B(N) = O(N).$$

### Step 5. The prime part $A(N)$ is $N\log\log N + O(N)$

Write $\lfloor N/p \rfloor = N/p + O(1)$, where the $O(1)$ error is at most $1$
per prime. Summing over the $\pi(N)$ primes up to $N$ gives an error of $O(\pi(N
))$. By the Prime Number Theorem, $\pi(N) = O(N/\log N) = O(N)$. Hence

$$A(N) = N \sum_{p \leq N} \frac{1}{p} + O(\pi(N)) = N \sum_{p \leq N} \frac{1}{
p} + O(N).$$

By **Mertens' theorem** (the second theorem of Mertens),

$$\sum_{p \leq N} \frac{1}{p} = \log\log N + M + O\!\left(\frac{1}{\log N}\right
),$$

where $M$ is the Meissel–Mertens constant. Multiplying by $N$,

$$A(N) = N\log\log N + MN + O\!\left(\frac{N}{\log N}\right) + O(N) = N\log\log
N + O(N).$$

### Step 6. Combine

$$S(N) = A(N) + B(N) = \bigl(N\log\log N + O(N)\bigr) + O(N) = N\log\log N + O(N
).$$

In particular, since $\log\log N \to \infty$,

$$\boxed{\sum_{n=1}^{N} \Omega(n) \sim N\,\log\log N \quad (N \to \infty).}$$

---

**Remark on the key ingredients.** The dominant term comes entirely from countin
g prime divisors ($k=1$); prime powers with $k\ge 2$ contribute only $O(N)$. The
 asymptotic $N\log\log N$ is then a direct consequence of Mertens' theorem $\sum
_{p\le x} 1/p \sim \log\log x$, which itself follows from the Prime Number Theor
em via partial summation. The same argument with $\omega(n)$ (distinct prime fac
tors, no multiplicity) in place of $\Omega(n)$ yields the identical asymptotic,
since the only difference is precisely the $O(N)$ higher-power contribution $B(N
)$.
