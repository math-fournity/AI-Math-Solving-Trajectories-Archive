## Part a)

**Claim:** The only function $\varphi \in C([0,1])$ satisfying $\varphi(x^2)=2\v
arphi(x)$ for $0<x\le 1$ is $\varphi\equiv 0$.

**Proof.** Setting $x=1$ gives $\varphi(1)=2\varphi(1)$, hence $\varphi(1)=0$. B
y continuity at $0$, taking $x\to 0^+$ in the functional equation yields $\varph
i(0)=2\varphi(0)$, so $\varphi(0)=0$.

Now fix any $x\in(0,1)$. Iterating the functional equation:
$$\varphi\!\bigl(x^{2^n}\bigr)=2^n\,\varphi(x),\qquad n=0,1,2,\dots$$
Since $0<x<1$, we have $x^{2^n}\to 0$ as $n\to\infty$. By continuity, $\varphi(x
^{2^n})\to\varphi(0)=0$. Thus $2^n\varphi(x)\to 0$, which forces $\varphi(x)=0$.

Combined with $\varphi(0)=\varphi(1)=0$, we conclude $\varphi\equiv 0$ on $[0,1]
$. $\square$

## Part b)

**Claim.** The nondecreasing functions $f\in C((0,1))$ satisfying $f(x^2)=f(x)^2
$ for $0<x<1$ are exactly:

- **$f\equiv 0$**, and
- **$f(x)=x^{\,q(\log_2(-\ln x))}$**, where $q:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ is conti
nuous, $1$-periodic, $q\ge 0$, and the map $s\mapsto 2^s\,q(s)$ is nondecreasing
 on $\mathbb{R}$.

In particular, the constant choice $q\equiv\alpha\ge 0$ yields the power-family
$f(x)=x^\alpha$ (including $f\equiv 1$ at $\alpha=0$).

---

**Proof.**

**Step 1 ($f\ge 0$).** Every $y\in(0,1)$ can be written as $y=x^2$ with $x=\sqrt
{y}\in(0,1)$, so $f(y)=f(x)^2\ge 0$.

**Step 2 (dichotomy: $f\equiv 0$ or $f>0$).** Suppose $f(x_0)=0$ for some $x_0\i
n(0,1)$. Since $f\ge 0$ and $f$ is nondecreasing, $f(x)=0$ for all $x\in(0,x_0]$
. From $f(x_0^{1/2})^2=f(x_0)=0$ we get $f(x_0^{1/2})=0$; since $x_0^{1/2}>x_0$,
 nondecreasingness gives $f=0$ on $(0,x_0^{1/2}]$. Iterating, $f=0$ on $(0,x_0^{
1/2^n}]$ for every $n$, and $x_0^{1/2^n}\to 1$, so $f\equiv 0$.

Henceforth assume $f>0$ on $(0,1)$.

**Step 3 (reduction to $u(2t)=2u(t)$).** Set $t=-\ln x\in(0,\infty)$ and define
$$u(t)=\ln f(e^{-t}),\qquad t>0.$$
Then $u$ is continuous (composition of continuous maps with $f>0$). Since $f$ is
 nondecreasing in $x$ and $x=e^{-t}$ is decreasing in $t$, the function $u$ is *
*nonincreasing** in $t$. The functional equation becomes:
$$u(2t)=\ln f(e^{-2t})=\ln f\!\bigl((e^{-t})^2\bigr)=\ln\!\bigl(f(e^{-t})^2\bigr
)=2\ln f(e^{-t})=2u(t).$$

**Step 4 (general continuous solution of $u(2t)=2u(t)$).** Define $p(s)=u(2^s)/2
^s$ for $s\in\mathbb{R}$. Then
$$p(s+1)=\frac{u(2^{s+1})}{2^{s+1}}=\frac{u(2\cdot 2^s)}{2\cdot 2^s}=\frac{2\,u(
2^s)}{2\cdot 2^s}=p(s),$$
so $p$ is $1$-periodic and continuous. Recovering $u$: for any $t>0$, set $s=\lo
g_2 t$:
$$u(t)=u(2^{\log_2 t})=2^{\log_2 t}\,p(\log_2 t)=t\,p(\log_2 t).$$

**Step 5 (monotonicity constraint).** Write $s=\log_2 t$ so $u(t)=2^s p(s)$. The
 condition that $u$ is nonincreasing in $t$ (equivalently in $s$) is:
$$s_1<s_2\;\Longrightarrow\;2^{s_1}p(s_1)\ge 2^{s_2}p(s_2).\tag{$\star$}$$
Setting $s_2=s_1+1$ gives $p(s_1)\ge 2\,p(s_1)$, i.e. $p(s_1)\le 0$ for all $s_1
$. Define $q=-p\ge 0$; then $(\star)$ becomes: $2^s q(s)$ is **nondecreasing** i
n $s$.

**Step 6 (back-substitution).** With $q=-p\ge 0$:
$$f(x)=e^{u(-\ln x)}=e^{(-\ln x)\,p(\log_2(-\ln x))}=e^{\ln x\cdot q(\log_2(-\ln
 x))}=x^{\,q(\log_2(-\ln x))}.$$

**Step 7 (verification).** Let $q$ be continuous, $1$-periodic, $q\ge 0$, with $
2^s q(s)$ nondecreasing. Define $f(x)=x^{q(\log_2(-\ln x))}$.

- *Continuity on $(0,1)$:* clear, since $-\ln x\in(0,\infty)$ and $q$ is continu
ous.
- *Nondecreasing:* Let $t=-\ln x$, $s=\log_2 t$. Then $\ln f = -t\,q(\log_2 t)=-
2^s q(s)$. Since $2^s q(s)$ is nondecreasing in $s$ and $s$ is increasing in $t$
, the quantity $-2^s q(s)$ is nonincreasing in $t$, so $f=e^{-2^s q(s)}$ is noni
ncreasing in $t$, hence nondecreasing in $x$. ✓
- *Functional equation:*
$$f(x^2)=(x^2)^{q(\log_2(-\ln x^2))}=x^{2\,q(\log_2(-2\ln x))}=x^{2\,q(1+\log_2(
-\ln x))}=x^{2\,q(\log_2(-\ln x))}=\bigl(x^{q(\log_2(-\ln x))}\bigr)^2=f(x)^2,$$
using $1$-periodicity of $q$. ✓

**Step 8 (special case).** Taking $q\equiv\alpha\ge 0$ (constant) gives $f(x)=x^
\alpha$, and $2^s\alpha$ is nondecreasing for $\alpha\ge 0$. This recovers the p
ower-family solutions, including $f\equiv 1$ ($\alpha=0$).

Non-constant periodic $q$ also yield valid solutions: for instance, $q(s)=1+\var
epsilon\cos(2\pi s)$ with $0<\varepsilon\le \ln 2/\sqrt{(\ln 2)^2+(2\pi)^2}$ sat
isfies all conditions (one checks $2^s q(s)$ is nondecreasing by differentiating
).

This completes the characterization. $\square$
