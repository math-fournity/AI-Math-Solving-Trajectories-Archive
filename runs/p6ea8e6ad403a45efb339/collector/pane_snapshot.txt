## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, the principal branch of the complex logarithm has a primitive on
 the set $H = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : \operatorname{Re} z > 0\}$.

**Step 1: $H$ lies within the domain of the principal logarithm.**

The principal branch $\log: \mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0] \to \mathbb{C}$ is de
fined and holomorphic on $\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]$, i.e., on the complex
plane with the non-positive real axis removed. For any $z \in H$, we have $\oper
atorname{Re} z > 0$, so $z$ is not a non-positive real number; in particular $z
\notin (-\infty,0]$. Hence
$$H \subseteq \mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0],$$
and $\log$ is holomorphic on (a neighborhood of every point of) $H$.

**Step 2: $H$ is simply connected.**

The right half-plane $H = \{z : \operatorname{Re} z > 0\}$ is convex (hence simp
ly connected): for any $z_1, z_2 \in H$ and $t \in [0,1]$, $\operatorname{Re}(tz
_1+(1-t)z_2) = t\operatorname{Re}z_1+(1-t)\operatorname{Re}z_2 > 0$. In particul
ar $H$ is simply connected.

**Step 3: A holomorphic function on a simply connected domain has a primitive.**

This is a standard consequence of Cauchy's theorem: on a simply connected domain
 $U$, every holomorphic function $f$ satisfies $\oint_\gamma f = 0$ for every cl
osed curve $\gamma$ in $U$, so $F(z) = \int_{z_0}^{z} f(\zeta)\,d\zeta$ (along a
ny path) is well-defined and satisfies $F'(z) = f(z)$. Applying this to $f = \lo
g$ on $U = H$, we conclude that $\log$ has a primitive on $H$.

**Step 4: Explicit primitive (verification).**

In fact, we can write down a primitive explicitly. Define
$$F(z) = z\log z - z, \qquad z \in H,$$
where $\log$ is the principal branch. Since $z \in H \subset \mathbb{C}\setminus
(-\infty,0]$, both $z$ and $\log z$ are well-defined and holomorphic on $H$. Dif
ferentiating (using the product rule and the fact that $(\log z)' = 1/z$ on the
domain of the principal branch):
$$F'(z) = \log z + z\cdot\frac{1}{z} - 1 = \log z + 1 - 1 = \log z.$$
Thus $F(z) = z\log z - z$ is a primitive of $\log$ on $H$, confirming the claim
directly.

**Conclusion.** The principal branch of the complex logarithm does have a primit
ive on $\{z \in \mathbb{C} : \operatorname{Re} z > 0\}$; an explicit primitive i
s $F(z) = z\log z - z$.
