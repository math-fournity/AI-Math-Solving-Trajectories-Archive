## Solution

**Yes, $X$ is necessarily second countable.**

**Key fact.** A normed space is a metric space (with $d(x,y)=\|x-y\|$), and for
metric spaces *second countable $\iff$ separable* (possesses a countable dense s
ubset). Thus it suffices to prove:

> If $Y\subseteq X$ is a closed subspace, $Y$ is separable, and $X/Y$ is separab
le, then $X$ is separable.

**Proof.**

Since $Y$ is separable, choose a countable dense subset $D=\{d_m\}_{m\in\mathbb
N}\subseteq Y$.

Since $X/Y$ is separable, choose a countable dense set of cosets $\{x_n+Y\}_{n\i
n\mathbb N}$ in $X/Y$, where each $x_n\in X$.

Define the countable set
$$
S \;=\; \{\,x_n + d_m : n,m\in\mathbb N\,\}\;\subseteq\; X.
$$
(If desired, also include $D$ itself by allowing a "zero" representative; this d
oes not affect countability or density. To keep notation clean we may assume $0\
in\{x_n\}$ by appending it, so that $D\subseteq S$.)

We claim $S$ is dense in $X$. Fix $x\in X$ and $\varepsilon>0$.

1. **Approximate the coset.** Since $\{x_n+Y\}$ is dense in $X/Y$, there exists
$n$ such that
$$
\|(x-x_n)+Y\|_{X/Y} < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
$$
By definition of the quotient norm, there exists $y\in Y$ with
$$
\|x - x_n - y\|_X < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
$$

2. **Approximate $y$ within $Y$.** Since $D$ is dense in $Y$, there exists $d_m\
in D$ with
$$
\|y - d_m\|_X < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
$$

3. **Combine.** By the triangle inequality,
$$
\|x - (x_n+d_m)\|_X \;\le\; \|x-x_n-y\|_X + \|y-d_m\|_X \;<\; \frac{\varepsilon}
{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2} \;=\; \varepsilon.
$$

Thus every $x\in X$ is within $\varepsilon$ of some point of $S$. Hence $\overli
ne{S}=X$, so $X$ is separable.

Since $X$ is a metric space and separable, $X$ is second countable. $\blacksquar
e$

**Remark.** The argument uses only the metric (norm) structure and the definitio
n of the quotient norm; it does not require completeness of $X$. Closedness of $
Y$ is used to ensure $X/Y$ carries the quotient *norm* (rather than merely a sem
inorm), so that "second countable $X/Y$" is genuinely the separability of a norm
ed space.
