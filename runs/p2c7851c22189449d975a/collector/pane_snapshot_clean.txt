Looking at this problem, I need to find nonzero integers $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ such
 that $\sum_{i<j} a_{ij} x_i x_j < n\sqrt[n]{\det A}$, where $A = (a_{ij})$ is p
ositive definite.

**Proof.**

Since $\sum_{i,j} a_{ij} x_i x_j > 0$ for all nonzero $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$, the
matrix $A$ is positive definite. In particular, all eigenvalues of $A$ are posit
ive, so $\det A > 0$, and therefore $n\sqrt[n]{\det A} > 0$.

Consider choosing $x_1, \ldots, x_n \in \{+1, -1\}$ independently and uniformly
at random. For each $i$, $\mathbb{E}[x_i] = 0$. For $i \neq j$, the random varia
bles $x_i$ and $x_j$ are independent, so:

$$\mathbb{E}[x_i x_j] = \mathbb{E}[x_i]\,\mathbb{E}[x_j] = 0 \cdot 0 = 0.$$

By linearity of expectation:

$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{1 \le i < j \le n} a_{ij}\, x_i x_j\right] = \sum_{1 \
le i < j \le n} a_{ij}\,\mathbb{E}[x_i x_j] = 0.$$

The expectation over all $2^n$ sign choices equals $0$. If every sign choice gav
e a strictly positive value, the expectation would be strictly positive—a contra
diction. Therefore, there exists at least one choice $(x_1, \ldots, x_n) \in \{+
1, -1\}^n$ such that:

$$\sum_{1 \le i < j \le n} a_{ij}\, x_i x_j \le 0.$$

Since $\det A > 0$, we have $n\sqrt[n]{\det A} > 0$, and thus:

$$\sum_{1 \le i < j \le n} a_{ij}\, x_i x_j \le 0 < n\sqrt[n]{\det A}.$$

The vector $(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$ has every entry equal to $\pm 1$, so it is a non
zero integer vector satisfying the required strict inequality. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The rationality of the $a_{ij}$ is not needed; the argument works fo
r any real positive definite matrix. The key insight is that the off-diagonal su
m $\sum_{i<j} a_{ij} x_i x_j$ has zero expectation under random independent sign
 choices, so some sign choice makes it non-positive, which is already strictly l
ess than the positive quantity $n\sqrt[n]{\det A}$.
