## Proof

**Convention (Outward-normal-first / Stokes convention).** Let $M$ be an oriente
d $(n{+}1)$-manifold with boundary. The *induced orientation* on $\partial M$ is
 defined as follows: a basis $(v_1,\dots,v_n)$ of $T_p(\partial M)$ is **positiv
ely oriented** if and only if
$$(\nu_p,\, v_1,\dots,v_n)$$
is a positively oriented basis of $T_p M$, where $\nu_p$ is the outward-pointing
 unit normal at $p$. This is the convention under which Stokes' theorem $\int_M
d\omega = \int_{\partial M}\omega$ holds.

---

**Setup.** $B^{n+1} = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^{n+1} : \|x\|\le 1\}$ carries the stand
ard orientation of $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$, given by the ordered basis $(e_1,\dots,e_
{n+1})$ and the volume form
$$\omega = dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n+1}.$$
The boundary is $S^n = \partial B^{n+1} = \{x : \|x\|=1\}$.

**Outward normal.** At a point $p = (p_1,\dots,p_{n+1})\in S^n$, the outward-poi
nting unit normal to $B^{n+1}$ is the radial vector
$$\nu(p) = p = \sum_{i=1}^{n+1} p_i\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i},$$
since the gradient of $\|x\|^2$ is $2x$, which points radially outward.

---

**Induced orientation — explicit description.**

A basis $(v_1,\dots,v_n)$ of $T_p S^n$ is positively oriented (in the induced or
ientation) if and only if
$$\det\!\big[\,p \;\big|\; v_1 \;\big|\; \cdots \;\big|\; v_n\,\big] > 0,$$
i.e., the $(n{+}1)\times(n{+}1)$ matrix whose first column is the position vecto
r $p$ (the outward normal) and whose remaining columns are $v_1,\dots,v_n$ has p
ositive determinant with respect to the standard basis of $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$.

**Orientation form.** The induced orientation is represented by the $n$-form obt
ained by contracting the volume form with the outward normal:
$$\Omega \;=\; \iota_\nu\,\omega\Big|_{S^n} \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i-1}\,x_
i\; dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{dx_i}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n+1}\;\bigg|_{S^n
}.$$

*Verification that this form gives the outward-normal-first orientation:* For ta
ngent vectors $v_1,\dots,v_n \in T_p S^n$,
$$\Omega_p(v_1,\dots,v_n) = \omega_p(\nu_p, v_1,\dots, v_n) = \det[\,p\,|\,v_1\,
|\,\cdots\,|\,v_n\,],$$
which is positive exactly when $(\nu_p, v_1,\dots,v_n)$ is positively oriented i
n $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$. This is precisely the induced boundary orientation. $\chec
kmark$

**Concrete checks.**

*$n=0$:* $S^0 = \{-1,+1\} = \partial[-1,1]$. At $p=+1$, the outward normal $+1$
is positively oriented in $\mathbb{R}$, so $+1$ receives $+$ orientation. At $p=
-1$, the outward normal $-1$ is negatively oriented, so $-1$ receives $-$ orient
ation. Thus $\partial B^1 = \{+1\}^+ - \{-1\}^-$, consistent with $\int_{-1}^{1}
 f'\,dx = f(1)-f(-1)$.

*$n=1$:* $S^1 = \partial B^2$. The orientation form is $\Omega = x_1\,dx_2 - x_2
\,dx_1$. At $p=(1,0)$: $\Omega = dx_2$, so the tangent vector $(0,1)$ (countercl
ockwise) is positive. The induced orientation is the **counterclockwise** orient
ation of $S^1$.

*$n=2$:* $S^2 = \partial B^3$. The orientation form is $\Omega = x_1\,dx_2\wedge
 dx_3 - x_2\,dx_1\wedge dx_3 + x_3\,dx_1\wedge dx_2$. At the north pole $p=(0,0,
1)$: $\Omega = dx_1\wedge dx_2$, and $(e_1,e_2)$ is positively oriented since $\
det[e_3\,|\,e_1\,|\,e_2]=+1$. At the south pole $p=(0,0,-1)$: $\Omega = -dx_1\we
dge dx_2$, so $(e_1,e_2)$ is *negatively* oriented — the orientation reverses, a
s expected for the antipodal map on an even-dimensional sphere.

---

**Summary.** The induced orientation on $S^n = \partial B^{n+1}$ is the **outwar
d-normal-first** orientation: at each $p\in S^n$, a tangent frame $(v_1,\dots,v_
n)$ is positively oriented if and only if $(p, v_1, \dots, v_n)$ is a positively
 oriented frame of $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$. Equivalently, it is the orientation deter
mined by the $n$-form
$$\boxed{\;\Omega = \sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i-1}\,x_i\; dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\wid
ehat{dx_i}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n+1}\;\Big|_{S^n}\;}$$
which is the interior product $\iota_{\mathbf{r}}(dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n+1
})$ of the standard volume form on $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ with the radial (outward-n
ormal) vector field $\mathbf{r} = \sum x_i\,\partial/\partial x_i$, restricted t
o $S^n$.
