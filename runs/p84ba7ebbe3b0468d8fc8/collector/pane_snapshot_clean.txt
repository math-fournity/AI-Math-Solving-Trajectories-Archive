## Solution

We need to find the digital root of a tower of 2013 twos, denoted $T(2013)$, whe
re

$$T(1)=2,\qquad T(k)=2^{T(k-1)}\ \text{for }k\ge 2.$$

The digital root satisfies $d(n)\equiv n\pmod 9$, with $d(n)=9$ when $9\mid n$,
and $d(n)=n\bmod 9$ otherwise. So it suffices to compute $T(2013)\bmod 9$.

**Step 1: Order of 2 modulo 9.**

$2^1\equiv2,\ 2^2\equiv4,\ 2^3\equiv8,\ 2^4\equiv7,\ 2^5\equiv5,\ 2^6\equiv1\pmo
d 9$.

So $\operatorname{ord}_9(2)=6$, and

$$T(2013)=2^{T(2012)}\equiv 2^{\,T(2012)\bmod 6}\pmod 9.$$

**Step 2: Compute $T(2012)\bmod 6$.**

For powers of 2 modulo 6: $2^1\equiv2,\ 2^2\equiv4,\ 2^3\equiv2,\ 2^4\equiv4\pmo
d 6$. Hence for $k\ge 1$,

$$2^k\equiv\begin{cases}2\pmod 6 & k\text{ odd},\\ 4\pmod 6 & k\text{ even}.\end
{cases}$$

Since $T(2012)=2^{T(2011)}$ with $T(2011)\ge 1$, this is a power of 2 with expon
ent $T(2011)$. Now $T(2011)=2^{T(2010)}$, and $T(2010)\ge 1$, so $T(2011)$ is **
even**.

Therefore $T(2012)\equiv 2^{\text{even}}\equiv 4\pmod 6$.

**Step 3: Conclude.**

$$T(2013)\equiv 2^{4}\equiv 16\equiv 7\pmod 9.$$

Since $7\ne 0$, the digital root equals the residue itself:

$$d\!\bigl(T(2013)\bigr)=7.$$
