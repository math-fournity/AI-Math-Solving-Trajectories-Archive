## Proof

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional real inner product space and $T: V \to V$ a Herm
itian (self-adjoint) operator, i.e. $T = T^*$. Let $n$ be an odd positive intege
r. We construct $S$ with $T = S^n$.

**Step 1: Diagonalize $T$.**

By the spectral theorem for self-adjoint operators on a finite-dimensional real
inner product space, there exists an orthonormal basis $\mathcal{B} = \{e_1, \do
ts, e_m\}$ of $V$ and real numbers $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_m \in \mathbb{R}$
such that

$$T e_i = \lambda_i e_i, \qquad i = 1, \dots, m.$$

Equivalently, $[T]_{\mathcal{B}} = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda
_m)$.

**Step 2: Take real $n$-th roots.**

Since $n$ is odd, the map $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$, $f(x) = x^n$, is stric
tly increasing (its derivative $n x^{n-1} \geq 0$ and vanishes only at $x=0$, wi
th $f$ injective) and surjective ($\lim_{x\to\pm\infty} f(x) = \pm\infty$, and $
f$ is continuous). Hence $f$ is a bijection of $\mathbb{R}$ onto itself. In part
icular, for every $\lambda_i \in \mathbb{R}$ there exists a unique $\mu_i \in \m
athbb{R}$ with

$$\mu_i^n = \lambda_i.$$

(Concretely, $\mu_i = \operatorname{sgn}(\lambda_i)\,|\lambda_i|^{1/n}$, which i
s real because $n$ is odd.)

**Step 3: Define $S$.**

Define $S: V \to V$ on the same orthonormal basis by

$$S e_i = \mu_i e_i, \qquad i = 1, \dots, m.$$

This is a well-defined linear operator (extended linearly), and $[S]_{\mathcal{B
}} = \operatorname{diag}(\mu_1, \dots, \mu_m)$.

**Step 4: Verify $S^n = T$.**

Iterating the definition, $S^k e_i = \mu_i^k e_i$ for every $k \geq 1$. Therefor
e

$$S^n e_i = \mu_i^n e_i = \lambda_i e_i = T e_i, \qquad i = 1, \dots, m.$$

Since $S^n$ and $T$ agree on a basis, $S^n = T$.

**Remark.** $S$ is in fact also self-adjoint, since its matrix in the orthonorma
l basis $\mathcal{B}$ is diagonal with real entries $\mu_i$, so $[S]_{\mathcal{B
}} = [S]_{\mathcal{B}}^\top = [S^*]_{\mathcal{B}}$. Thus $T$ even admits a self-
adjoint $n$-th root. (The hypothesis that $n$ is odd is essential: for $n$ even,
 $x^n \geq 0$, so any self-adjoint $n$-th root of $T$ would force $T \geq 0$, wh
ich need not hold.)
