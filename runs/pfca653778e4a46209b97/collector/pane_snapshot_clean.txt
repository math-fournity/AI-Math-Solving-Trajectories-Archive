## Proof

**Step 1: The limit is constant (Hurwitz's theorem).**

Each $f_n$ is one-to-one and holomorphic on $\mathbb{D}$ (i.e., univalent). By *
*Hurwitz's theorem**, if a sequence of univalent functions converges uniformly o
n compact subsets to $f$, then $f$ is either univalent or constant. Since $f$ is
 given to be **not** one-to-one, $f$ must be **constant**: $f \equiv c_0$ for so
me $c_0 \in \mathbb{C}$.

**Step 2: Parameterize the conformal maps.**

Every conformal map from $\mathbb{D}$ onto the right half-plane $\mathbb{A}$ has
 the form $f_n = C \circ \psi_n$, where $C(z) = \frac{1+z}{1-z}$ is the Cayley t
ransform ($C:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{A}$) and $\psi_n \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathb
b{D})$ is a disk automorphism:
$$\psi_n(z) = e^{i\theta_n}\frac{z - a_n}{1 - \overline{a_n}\,z}, \qquad |a_n|<1
,\;\theta_n\in\mathbb{R}.$$

**Step 3: Transfer convergence to the automorphisms.**

Since $f_n \to f \equiv c_0$ uniformly on compact subsets and $c_0$ satisfies $\
Re c_0 \geq 0$ (being a limit of functions with positive real part), we have $c_
0 \neq -1$ (as $\Re(-1)<0$). The inverse Cayley transform $C^{-1}(w)=\frac{w-1}{
w+1}$ is holomorphic in a neighborhood of $c_0$, so:
$$\psi_n = C^{-1}\circ f_n \;\longrightarrow\; C^{-1}\circ f \equiv \frac{c_0-1}
{c_0+1} =: \psi_0$$
uniformly on compact subsets. The limit $\psi_0$ is a **constant**.

**Step 4: The constant $\psi_0$ lies on the unit circle.**

Each $\psi_n$ maps $\mathbb{D}$ into $\mathbb{D}$, so $|\psi_0| \leq 1$. If $|\p
si_0|<1$, then $f = C(\psi_0) = \frac{1+\psi_0}{1-\psi_0}$ with $\Re f > 0$, and
 $f$ would be a (constant) conformal map — but more importantly, the limit of un
ivalent $\psi_n$ converging to an interior constant would, by Hurwitz, force $\p
si_0$ to be univalent or the $\psi_n$ to be eventually constant in a way that ma
kes $f$ one-to-one. The key point: $|\psi_0|<1$ would mean $f = C(\psi_0)$ is a
**bona fide conformal map** (one-to-one), contradicting the hypothesis. Therefor
e $|\psi_0| = 1$.

**Step 5: Compute $\Re f(0)$.**

Write $\psi_0 = e^{i\gamma}$ with $\gamma \neq \pi$ (so that $C(\psi_0)$ is fini
te). Then:
$$f(0) = c_0 = C(\psi_0) = \frac{1+e^{i\gamma}}{1-e^{i\gamma}} = \frac{e^{i\gamm
a/2}\cdot 2\cos(\gamma/2)}{e^{i\gamma/2}\cdot(-2i\sin(\gamma/2))} = \frac{\cos(\
gamma/2)}{-i\sin(\gamma/2)} = i\cot\!\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}\right).$$

This is **purely imaginary**, so:
$$\Re f(0) = 0.$$

**Conclusion.** By Hurwitz's theorem the non-injective limit $f$ is constant. Co
mposing with the inverse Cayley transform, the corresponding limit of disk autom
orphisms is a boundary point $e^{i\gamma}$ of $\mathbb{D}$, and the Cayley trans
form of any boundary point is purely imaginary. Hence $\Re f(0)=0$.

$$\boxed{\Re f(0) = 0}$$
