## Answer

**Yes, the statement is true:** for every Borel set $E \subseteq [0,1]$, $\mu(F_
n E \triangle E) \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$.

---

## Proof

**Key observations.** Each $F_n(x) = x + \tfrac{1}{n} \pmod{1}$ is a rotation of
 the circle, hence **measure-preserving**: for every Borel set $A$,
$$\mu(F_n A) = \mu(A),$$
and consequently, since $F_n A \triangle F_n B = F_n(A \triangle B)$,
$$\mu(F_n A \triangle F_n B) = \mu(A \triangle B). \tag{1}$$

Also, the rotation amount $\tfrac{1}{n} \to 0$, so $F_n \to \mathrm{id}$ pointwi
se.

---

**Step 1 (Approximation by finite unions of intervals).** Let $E$ be a Borel set
 and let $\varepsilon > 0$. Finite unions of intervals are dense in the measure
metric on Borel sets, so there exists a finite union of disjoint intervals $A =
\bigcup_{i=1}^{k} I_i$ with
$$\mu(E \triangle A) < \varepsilon. \tag{2}$$

---

**Step 2 (Triangle inequality for symmetric difference).** Using the inclusion
$$F_n E \triangle E \;\subseteq\; (F_n E \triangle F_n A) \cup (F_n A \triangle
A) \cup (A \triangle E),$$
we obtain
$$\mu(F_n E \triangle E) \leq \mu(F_n E \triangle F_n A) + \mu(F_n A \triangle A
) + \mu(A \triangle E).$$
By the measure-preservation identity (1), the first term equals $\mu(E \triangle
 A)$, which by (2) is $< \varepsilon$; the third term is also $< \varepsilon$. H
ence
$$\mu(F_n E \triangle E) \leq 2\varepsilon + \mu(F_n A \triangle A). \tag{3}$$

---

**Step 3 (Symmetric difference for a finite union of intervals).** Since $F_n A
\triangle A \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^{k}(F_n I_i \triangle I_i)$,
$$\mu(F_n A \triangle A) \leq \sum_{i=1}^{k} \mu(F_n I_i \triangle I_i).$$
For a single interval $I_i = [a_i, b_i]$, the translated set $F_n I_i$ differs f
rom $I_i$ only near the two endpoints (the wrap-around at $1 \equiv 0$ does not
increase the discrepancy), and one checks directly that
$$\mu(F_n I_i \triangle I_i) \leq \frac{2}{n}.$$
Therefore
$$\mu(F_n A \triangle A) \leq \frac{2k}{n} \xrightarrow[n \to \infty]{} 0. \tag{
4}$$

---

**Step 4 (Conclusion).** Taking $\limsup_{n \to \infty}$ in (3) and using (4),
$$\limsup_{n \to \infty} \mu(F_n E \triangle E) \leq 2\varepsilon.$$
Since $\varepsilon > 0$ was arbitrary,
$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \mu(F_n E \triangle E) = 0.$$

---

**Remark on why this is not a mixing/ergodicity statement.** This result does **
not** assert that a single rotation is mixing (irrational rotations are ergodic
but *not* mixing). The crucial point here is that we have a *sequence of distinc
t* rotations whose translation amount $\tfrac{1}{n}$ tends to $0$, so $F_n \to \
mathrm{id}$; combined with measure preservation and the density of finite unions
 of intervals, this forces $\mu(F_n E \triangle E) \to 0$ for every Borel set $E
$.
