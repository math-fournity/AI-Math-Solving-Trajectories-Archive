## Proof

Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots$ be independent with $X_i \sim U(0,1)$, and let $S = \sum_
{i=1}^{\infty} X_i$ (defined as the limit of partial sums $S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_
i$, possibly $+\infty$).

**Step 1: The event is a tail event.**

Consider $A = \{S < \infty\} = \{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} X_i < \infty\}$. For any fi
nite $k$, whether the infinite series converges does not depend on the first $k$
 terms: changing $X_1, \ldots, X_k$ (each finite) only shifts the sum by a finit
e amount, which does not affect whether the total is finite or infinite. Formall
y,

$$A = \left\{\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty} X_i < \infty\right\} \quad \text{up to a fini
te shift},$$

so $A \in \sigma(X_{k+1}, X_{k+2}, \ldots)$ for every $k$. Hence

$$A \in \bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty} \sigma(X_{k+1}, X_{k+2}, \ldots) = \mathcal{T},$$

the tail $\sigma$-algebra. By the **Kolmogorov 0–1 law** (applicable since the $
X_i$ are independent), every event in $\mathcal{T}$ has probability $0$ or $1$.
Therefore

$$P(A) \in \{0, 1\}.$$

**Step 2: Determine which value via the Strong Law of Large Numbers.**

Define $S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$. Since the $X_i$ are i.i.d. with $E[X_i] = \tfra
c{1}{2} \in (0,\infty)$, the **Strong Law of Large Numbers** gives

$$\frac{S_n}{n} \to \frac{1}{2} \quad \text{almost surely.}$$

In particular, $S_n \to +\infty$ almost surely (since $S_n \approx n/2 \to \inft
y$). The infinite series $S = \lim_{n\to\infty} S_n$ therefore equals $+\infty$
almost surely:

$$P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} X_i = +\infty\right) = 1.$$

Equivalently,

$$P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} X_i < \infty\right) = 0.$$

**Conclusion.**

By the Kolmogorov 0–1 law the probability is $0$ or $1$; by the Strong Law of La
rge Numbers (using $E[X_i] = 1/2 > 0$) the partial sums diverge to $+\infty$ alm
ost surely, forcing the value $0$:

$$\boxed{P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} X_i < \infty\right) = 0.}$$
