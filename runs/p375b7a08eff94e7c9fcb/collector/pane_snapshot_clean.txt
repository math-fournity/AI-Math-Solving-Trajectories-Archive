## Proof

**Definitions.** A topological space $X$ is *KC* if every compact subset of $X$
is closed in $X$.

We prove the specific direction asked: **if every compact subset of $X$ is KC, t
hen $X$ is KC.** (The converse is straightforward and included at the end for co
mpleteness.)

---

### Main direction

**Hypothesis:** Every compact subset $K\subseteq X$ is KC (as a space with the s
ubspace topology).

**Goal:** $X$ is KC, i.e. every compact subset of $X$ is closed in $X$.

Let $K\subseteq X$ be compact. We show $K$ is closed in $X$.

Suppose for contradiction that $K$ is not closed. Then there exists
$$x\in \operatorname{cl}_X(K)\setminus K.$$

**Claim.** $K\cup\{x\}$ is compact in $X$.

*Proof of claim.* Let $\{U_i\}_{i\in I}$ be a cover of $K\cup\{x\}$ by open sets
 in $X$. Choose $i_0\in I$ with $x\in U_{i_0}$. Then
$$K\setminus U_{i_0}$$
is a closed subset of $K$ (closed in the subspace $K$, since $U_{i_0}\cap K$ is
open in $K$). As a closed subset of the compact set $K$, it is compact. It is co
vered by $\{U_i\}$, so finitely many $U_{i_1},\dots,U_{i_n}$ cover $K\setminus U
_{i_0}$. Then $\{U_{i_0},U_{i_1},\dots,U_{i_n}\}$ covers all of $K\cup\{x\}$. He
nce $K\cup\{x\}$ is compact. $\square$

By the hypothesis, $K\cup\{x\}$ is KC.

Now $K$ is a compact subset of $K\cup\{x\}$: compactness is absolute (the subspa
ce topology that $K$ inherits from $K\cup\{x\}$ equals the subspace topology it
inherits from $X$, by transitivity of subspace topologies), so $K$ compact in $X
$ implies $K$ compact in $K\cup\{x\}$.

Since $K\cup\{x\}$ is KC, every compact subset of $K\cup\{x\}$ is closed in $K\c
up\{x\}$. In particular, **$K$ is closed in $K\cup\{x\}$**.

On the other hand, the closure of $K$ in the subspace $K\cup\{x\}$ is
$$\operatorname{cl}_{K\cup\{x\}}(K)=\operatorname{cl}_X(K)\cap(K\cup\{x\}).$$
Since $x\in\operatorname{cl}_X(K)$ and $x\in K\cup\{x\}$, we have $x\in\operator
name{cl}_{K\cup\{x\}}(K)$. But $K$ is closed in $K\cup\{x\}$, so $\operatorname{
cl}_{K\cup\{x\}}(K)=K$, giving $x\in K$.

This contradicts the choice $x\in\operatorname{cl}_X(K)\setminus K$. Therefore n
o such $x$ exists, and $K$ is closed in $X$.

Since $K$ was an arbitrary compact subset, $X$ is KC. $\blacksquare$

---

### Converse direction (for completeness)

Suppose $X$ is KC. Let $K\subseteq X$ be compact; we show $K$ (with the subspace
 topology) is KC. Let $C\subseteq K$ be compact in $K$. By absoluteness of compa
ctness, $C$ is compact in $X$. Since $X$ is KC, $C$ is closed in $X$. Then $C=C\
cap K$ is closed in $K$. Hence $K$ is KC. $\blacksquare$

---

### Conclusion

The statement is **true**: $X$ is KC if and only if every compact subset of $X$
is KC. In particular, if every compact subset of $X$ is KC, then $X$ is KC.

The key idea of the forward direction is that for a compact $K$ and a point $x\i
n\operatorname{cl}(K)\setminus K$, the set $K\cup\{x\}$ is again compact; applyi
ng the hypothesis to this larger compact set forces $K$ to be closed inside $K\c
up\{x\}$, which then traps $x$ back into $K$—a contradiction.
