## Proof

Consider the Gaussian function on $\mathbb{R}^n$:

$$f(x) = e^{-\pi|x|^2}, \qquad x \in \mathbb{R}^n.$$

**$f$ is radial.** Since $f(x)$ depends only on $|x| = \sqrt{x_1^2 + \cdots + x_
n^2}$, it is invariant under every rotation $R \in O(n)$: $f(Rx) = e^{-\pi|Rx|^2
} = e^{-\pi|x|^2} = f(x)$. Hence $f$ is radial.

**Compute the Fourier transform.** Using the convention $\hat{f}(\xi) = \int_{\m
athbb{R}^n} f(x)\,e^{-2\pi i\, x\cdot \xi}\,dx$, the Gaussian factors as a produ
ct of one-dimensional Gaussians:

$$f(x) = \prod_{j=1}^n e^{-\pi x_j^2},$$

so by Fubini and the one-dimensional identity $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-\pi t
^2} e^{-2\pi i t s}\,dt = e^{-\pi s^2}$ (obtained by completing the square: $-\p
i t^2 - 2\pi i t s = -\pi(t+is)^2 - \pi s^2$),

$$\hat{f}(\xi) = \prod_{j=1}^n \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-\pi x_j^2} e^{-2\pi i
 x_j \xi_j}\,dx_j = \prod_{j=1}^n e^{-\pi \xi_j^2} = e^{-\pi|\xi|^2}.$$

**Strict positivity.** For every $\xi \in \mathbb{R}^n$,

$$\hat{f}(\xi) = e^{-\pi|\xi|^2} > 0,$$

since the exponential of a real number is always positive, and $-\pi|\xi|^2$ is
real for all $\xi$.

Thus $f(x) = e^{-\pi|x|^2}$ is a radial function whose Fourier transform is stri
ctly positive everywhere. $\blacksquare$
