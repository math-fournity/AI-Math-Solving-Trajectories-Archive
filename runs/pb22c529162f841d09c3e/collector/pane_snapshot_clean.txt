Yes — a map $f:\mathbb{C}_\infty \to \mathbb{C}_\infty$ preserving all cross rat
ios is necessarily a Möbius map. Here is the proof.

**Proof.**

Recall the cross ratio of four distinct points $z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4\in\mathbb{C}_\in
fty$:
$$[z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4]=\frac{(z_1-z_3)(z_2-z_4)}{(z_1-z_4)(z_2-z_3)},$$
with the usual conventions for $\infty$. The hypothesis is
$$[f(z_1),f(z_2),f(z_3),f(z_4)]=[z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4]\quad\text{for all distinct }z_
1,z_2,z_3,z_4.$$

**Step 1: $f$ is injective.**

Suppose $f(z_1)=f(z_2)$ with $z_1\neq z_2$. Pick $z_3,z_4$ distinct from $z_1,z_
2$ and from each other. Then $f(z_1),f(z_2),f(z_3),f(z_4)$ has a repeated entry,
 so
$$[f(z_1),f(z_2),f(z_3),f(z_4)]=1$$
(since $[a,a,c,d]=\frac{(a-c)(a-d)}{(a-d)(a-c)}=1$). By the preservation hypothe
sis, $[z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4]=1$, which forces $z_1=z_2$ — contradiction. Hence $f$ is
 injective.

**Step 2: $f$ is surjective.**

Fix three distinct points $z_a,z_b,z_c$ and write $a=f(z_a),\,b=f(z_b),\,c=f(z_c
)$, which are distinct by Step 1. Let $w\in\mathbb{C}_\infty$ be arbitrary. Set
$\lambda=[a,b,c,w]$. The map $z\mapsto[z_a,z_b,z_c,z]$ is a Möbius map in $z$, h
ence a bijection of $\mathbb{C}_\infty$, so there exists a unique $z_4$ with $[z
_a,z_b,z_c,z_4]=\lambda$. Then
$$[a,b,c,f(z_4)]=[f(z_a),f(z_b),f(z_c),f(z_4)]=[z_a,z_b,z_c,z_4]=\lambda=[a,b,c,
w].$$
Since $z\mapsto[a,b,c,z]$ is injective (Möbius), $f(z_4)=w$. Thus $f$ is surject
ive.

**Step 3: $f$ is Möbius.**

Fix three distinct points $z_1,z_2,z_3\in\mathbb{C}_\infty$. Let $M$ be the uniq
ue Möbius map sending
$$M(f(z_1))=z_1,\quad M(f(z_2))=z_2,\quad M(f(z_3))=z_3.$$
Set $h:=M\circ f$. Since both $f$ (by hypothesis) and $M$ (being Möbius) preserv
e cross ratios, so does $h$. Moreover $h$ fixes $z_1,z_2,z_3$. For any $z\in\mat
hbb{C}_\infty$ distinct from $z_1,z_2,z_3$:
$$[z_1,z_2,z_3,h(z)]=[h(z_1),h(z_2),h(z_3),h(z)]=[z_1,z_2,z_3,z].$$
The map $z\mapsto[z_1,z_2,z_3,z]$ is a Möbius map, hence injective, so $h(z)=z$.
 Thus $h=\mathrm{id}$, giving $f=M^{-1}$, which is Möbius. $\blacksquare$

**Answer:** Yes. Any map $f:\mathbb{C}_\infty\to\mathbb{C}_\infty$ preserving al
l cross ratios is necessarily a Möbius transformation. The key points are that c
ross-ratio preservation forces injectivity (a collision makes the cross ratio eq
ual to $1$), then surjectivity (the cross ratio in one argument ranges over all
of $\mathbb{C}_\infty$), and finally a bijection fixing three points while prese
rving cross ratios must be the identity — so $f$ agrees with the Möbius map dete
rmined by any three points and their images.
