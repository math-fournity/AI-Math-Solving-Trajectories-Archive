## Proof

Since $\Sigma$ is positive semi-definite with $\det(\Sigma)=0$, the matrix $\Sig
ma$ is singular. Hence its null space is nontrivial: there exists a vector $a \i
n \mathbb{R}^n$, $a \neq 0$, such that

$$\Sigma a = 0.$$

Consider the location–scale transform $T(Y) = a^{\top} Y + b$ with $a$ as above
and $b = -a^{\top}\mu$. This is a **nonzero** transform because $a \neq 0$ (so t
he scale part is nontrivial).

We compute the variance of $a^{\top} Y$:

$$\operatorname{Var}(a^{\top} Y) = a^{\top} \Sigma\, a = a^{\top}(\Sigma a) = a^
{\top} 0 = 0.$$

A random variable with zero variance is almost surely constant, equal to its mea
n. The mean of $a^{\top} Y$ is

$$\mathbb{E}[a^{\top} Y] = a^{\top} \mu.$$

Therefore

$$a^{\top} Y = a^{\top} \mu \quad \text{almost surely},$$

and consequently

$$T(Y) = a^{\top} Y - a^{\top}\mu = 0 \quad \text{almost surely}.$$

Thus $T(Y) = a^{\top}(Y - \mu)$ is a nonzero location–scale transform (nonzero s
cale vector $a$, shift $b = -a^{\top}\mu$) that is identically zero. $\blacksqua
