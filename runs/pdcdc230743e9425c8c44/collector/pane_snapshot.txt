# Proof

**Claim:** Yes. In fact, *every* subset $\mathfrak{F} \subseteq \omega^\omega$ w
ith $|\mathfrak{F}| = \aleph_1$ has the desired property.

---

**Step 1: Such a family $\mathfrak{F}$ exists.**

The Baire space $\omega^\omega$ has cardinality $|\omega|^{|\omega|} = \aleph_0^
{\aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph_0}$. By Cantor's theorem, $2^{\aleph_0} > \aleph_0$, and
since $\aleph_1$ is the least cardinal exceeding $\aleph_0$, we have $\aleph_1 \
leq 2^{\aleph_0}$. Hence we may choose any subset $\mathfrak{F} \subseteq \omega
^\omega$ with $|\mathfrak{F}| = \aleph_1$.

---

**Step 2: Any generating subfamily of size $< \aleph_1$ is at most countable.**

Let $\mathfrak{G} \subseteq \mathfrak{F}$ with $|\mathfrak{G}| < \aleph_1$. Sinc
e $\aleph_1$ is the smallest uncountable cardinal, every cardinal strictly below
 $\aleph_1$ is at most $\aleph_0$. Therefore $|\mathfrak{G}| \leq \aleph_0$.

---

**Step 3: The set of finite compositions from $\mathfrak{G}$ is at most countabl
e.**

Define $\operatorname{Comp}(\mathfrak{G}) = \{g_1 \circ g_2 \circ \cdots \circ g
_n : n \geq 1,\; g_1, \dots, g_n \in \mathfrak{G}\}$, the set of all finite comp
ositions of elements of $\mathfrak{G}$ (each composition being a function $\omeg
a \to \omega$). The number of such compositions is bounded above by the number o
f finite sequences from $\mathfrak{G}$:

$$|\operatorname{Comp}(\mathfrak{G})| \leq \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} |\mathfrak{G}|^n.
$$

If $|\mathfrak{G}| \leq \aleph_0$, then $|\mathfrak{G}|^n \leq \aleph_0^n = \ale
ph_0$ for each $n \geq 1$, so

$$|\operatorname{Comp}(\mathfrak{G})| \leq \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \aleph_0 = \aleph
_0 \cdot \aleph_0 = \aleph_0.$$

---

**Step 4: $\mathfrak{G}$ cannot generate $\mathfrak{F}$.**

If $\mathfrak{G}$ generates $\mathfrak{F}$ in the sense of the problem, then eve
ry $f \in \mathfrak{F}$ is a finite composition of elements of $\mathfrak{G}$, i
.e., $\mathfrak{F} \subseteq \operatorname{Comp}(\mathfrak{G})$. This gives

$$\aleph_1 = |\mathfrak{F}| \leq |\operatorname{Comp}(\mathfrak{G})| \leq \aleph
_0,$$

which contradicts $\aleph_1 > \aleph_0$.

---

**Conclusion.**

No subfamily $\mathfrak{G} \subseteq \mathfrak{F}$ with $|\mathfrak{G}| < \aleph
_1$ can generate $\mathfrak{F}$ under finite composition. The argument applies t
o *any* $\mathfrak{F} \subseteq \omega^\omega$ of cardinality $\aleph_1$, so suc
h a family exists (and indeed every such family works). The essential reason is
that a generating set of size $< \aleph_1$ is necessarily countable, and a count
able set can produce at most countably many finite compositions—far fewer than t
he $\aleph_1$ functions in $\mathfrak{F}$.
