## Proof

**Claim.** The counit $\epsilon_{\mathcal{F}}: f^{*}f_{*}\mathcal{F} \to \mathca
l{F}$ is surjective for every quasi-coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$ (hence i
n particular for every coherent sheaf).

**Setup.** Since $f$ is affine, there exists an affine open cover $\{V_i = \oper
atorname{Spec} A_i\}_{i\in I}$ of $Y$ such that $U_i := f^{-1}(V_i) = \operatorn
ame{Spec} B_i$ is affine for every $i$, where $A_i \to B_i$ is the ring map corr
esponding to $f|_{U_i}$. Surjectivity of a morphism of sheaves is local on the t
arget (equivalently on the source), so it suffices to prove $\epsilon_{\mathcal{
F}}|_{U_i}$ is surjective for each $i$.

**Reduction to the affine case.** For each $i$, set $f_i := f|_{U_i}: U_i \to V_
i$. Because $f$ is affine, $f_{*}\mathcal{F}$ is quasi-coherent and its formatio
n commutes with restriction to affine opens of $Y$:
$$(f_{*}\mathcal{F})|_{V_i} \;=\; f_{i*}(\mathcal{F}|_{U_i}).$$
Likewise pullback commutes with restriction:
$$(f^{*}f_{*}\mathcal{F})|_{U_i} \;=\; f_i^{*}\big((f_{*}\mathcal{F})|_{V_i}\big
) \;=\; f_i^{*}f_{i*}(\mathcal{F}|_{U_i}).$$
Under these identifications, $\epsilon_{\mathcal{F}}|_{U_i}$ is exactly the adju
nction counit for $f_i$ applied to $\mathcal{F}|_{U_i}$. Thus we are reduced to
the case of a morphism of affine schemes.

**The affine case.** Write $\mathcal{F}|_{U_i} = \widetilde{M}$ for a $B_i$-modu
le $M$. Then:
- $f_{i*}(\mathcal{F}|_{U_i}) = \widetilde{M_{A_i}}$, where $M_{A_i}$ is $M$ vie
wed as an $A_i$-module via $A_i \to B_i$;
- $f_i^{*}f_{i*}(\mathcal{F}|_{U_i}) = \widetilde{B_i \otimes_{A_i} M}$;
- the counit corresponds to the $B_i$-linear map
$$\varphi: B_i \otimes_{A_i} M \longrightarrow M,\qquad b\otimes m \longmapsto b
\cdot m.$$

This map is **surjective**: for every $m \in M$,
$$\varphi(1\otimes m) = 1\cdot m = m,$$
so $m$ lies in the image. Hence $\epsilon_{\mathcal{F}}|_{U_i}$ is surjective.

**Conclusion.** Since each $\epsilon_{\mathcal{F}}|_{U_i}$ is surjective and the
 open sets $\{U_i\}_{i\in I}$ cover $X$, the counit $\epsilon_{\mathcal{F}}: f^{
*}f_{*}\mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{F}$ is surjective. This argument uses only quasi
-coherence of $\mathcal{F}$, so the conclusion holds for every quasi-coherent sh
eaf, and in particular for every coherent sheaf. $\blacksquare$
