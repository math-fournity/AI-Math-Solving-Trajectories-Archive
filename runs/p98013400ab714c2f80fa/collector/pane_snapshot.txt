## Proof that $d$ is measurable

**Step 1: Preliminaries.**

A standard Borel space $X$ is Borel isomorphic to a Borel subset of a Polish spa
ce. Since the weak convergence topology on $\mathcal{P}(X)$ requires a compatibl
e topology on $X$, we may assume (as is standard) that $X$ is a separable metriz
able space with its Borel $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{B}(X)$. Then $\mathcal{P}(X
)$ with the weak convergence topology is separable and metrizable, and its Borel
 $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{P}(X))$ is generated by the evaluation m
aps $\{e_A : A \in \mathcal{B}(X)\}$, where $e_A(p) = p(A)$.

**Step 2: Evaluation maps are Borel measurable.**

For each $A \in \mathcal{B}(X)$, the map $e_A: \mathcal{P}(X) \to [0,1]$, $p \ma
psto p(A)$, is Borel measurable. Indeed, for closed $F \subseteq X$, we have $p(
F) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \int f_n\,dp$ where $f_n(x) = \max(0,\, 1 - n\cdot d(x,
F))$ are bounded continuous functions; each $p \mapsto \int f_n\,dp$ is continuo
us (hence Borel) by definition of the weak topology, so $p \mapsto p(F)$ is Bore
l. The measurability extends to all $A \in \mathcal{B}(X)$ by the monotone class
 theorem (the class of $A$ for which $e_A$ is measurable is a $\sigma$-algebra c
ontaining all closed sets).

**Step 3: A countable generating algebra.**

Since $X$ is separable and metrizable, it has a countable base $\{U_n\}_{n \in \
mathbb{N}}$. Let $\mathcal{A}$ be the algebra generated by $\{U_n\}$ (all finite
 unions of finite intersections of the $U_n$ and their complements). Then $\math
cal{A}$ is **countable** and $\sigma(\mathcal{A}) = \mathcal{B}(X)$.

**Step 4: Reduction to a countable supremum.**

We claim:
$$d(p,q) = \sup_{A \in \mathcal{A}} |p(A) - q(A)|.$$

The direction $\geq$ is immediate since $\mathcal{A} \subseteq \mathcal{B}(X)$.

For the direction $\leq$: fix $p, q \in \mathcal{P}(X)$ and let $\mu = p - q$, a
 finite signed measure with $\mu(X) = 0$. Let $|\mu| = \mu^+ + \mu^-$ be the tot
al variation measure of $\mu$, a finite positive measure.

For any $B \in \mathcal{B}(X)$, by the **approximation theorem for finite measur
es** (which states: if $\mathcal{A}$ is an algebra generating $\sigma(\mathcal{A
})$ and $\nu$ is a finite measure on $\sigma(\mathcal{A})$, then for every $B \i
n \sigma(\mathcal{A})$ and $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $A \in \mathcal{A}$ wi
th $\nu(A \triangle B) < \varepsilon$), applied to $\nu = |\mu|$, there exists a
 sequence $\{A_n\} \subseteq \mathcal{A}$ with $|\mu|(A_n \triangle B) \to 0$.

Then:
$$|\mu(A_n) - \mu(B)| = |\mu(A_n \setminus B) - \mu(B \setminus A_n)| \leq |\mu|
(A_n \setminus B) + |\mu|(B \setminus A_n) = |\mu|(A_n \triangle B) \to 0.$$

Hence $|\mu(A_n)| \to |\mu(B)|$, so $\sup_{A \in \mathcal{A}} |\mu(A)| \geq |\mu
(B)|$. Since this holds for every $B \in \mathcal{B}(X)$:
$$\sup_{A \in \mathcal{A}} |p(A) - q(A)| \geq \sup_{B \in \mathcal{B}(X)} |p(B)
- q(B)| = d(p,q).$$

This establishes the claim.

**Step 5: $d$ is a countable supremum of measurable functions.**

For each $A \in \mathcal{A}$, the map
$$(p, q) \;\mapsto\; |p(A) - q(A)|$$
is Borel measurable on $\mathcal{P}(X) \times \mathcal{P}(X)$: it is the absolut
e value of $e_A \circ \pi_1 - e_A \circ \pi_2$, a difference of two measurable m
aps (compositions of the measurable evaluation map $e_A$ with the continuous pro
jections $\pi_1, \pi_2$).

Since $\mathcal{A}$ is countable:
$$d(p,q) = \sup_{A \in \mathcal{A}} |p(A) - q(A)|$$
is a **countable supremum** of Borel measurable functions, which is Borel measur
able (as $\{(p,q) : d(p,q) \leq t\} = \bigcap_{A \in \mathcal{A}} \{(p,q) : |p(A
) - q(A)| \leq t\}$ is a countable intersection of Borel sets for each $t$).

**Conclusion.** The total variation metric $d: \mathcal{P}(X) \times \mathcal{P}
(X) \to [0,1]$ is Borel measurable. $\blacksquare$
