## Characteristic Polynomial of $L_n(q)$

**Setup.** Let $L_n(q)$ denote the lattice of subspaces of $V = \mathbb{F}_q^n$,
 ordered by inclusion. This is a graded poset with rank function $r(x) = \dim(x)
$, minimum $\hat{0} = \{0\}$ (rank 0), and maximum $\hat{1} = V$ (rank $n$). The
 **characteristic polynomial** is

$$\chi_{L_n(q)}(t) = \sum_{x \in L_n(q)} \mu(\hat{0}, x)\, t^{n - r(x)} = \sum_{
k=0}^{n} \left(\sum_{\substack{x \in L_n(q)\\ \dim x = k}} \mu(\hat{0},x)\right)
 t^{n-k}.$$

The number of $k$-dimensional subspaces is the **Gaussian binomial coefficient**

$$\binom{n}{k}_q = \frac{(q^n-1)(q^{n-1}-1)\cdots(q^{n-k+1}-1)}{(q^k-1)(q^{k-1}-
1)\cdots(q-1)}.$$

---

**Step 1: The Möbius function.** We claim that for any $k$-dimensional subspace
$x$,

$$\mu(\hat{0}, x) = (-1)^k\, q^{\binom{k}{2}}.$$

*Proof by induction on $k$.* The base case $k=0$ gives $\mu(\hat{0},\hat{0})=1=(
-1)^0 q^0$. For the inductive step, the interval $[\hat{0}, x]$ is isomorphic to
 $L_k(q)$ (the subspace lattice of the $k$-dimensional space $x$). The Möbius re
cursion gives

$$\mu(\hat{0},x) = -\sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \binom{k}{j}_q \mu(\hat{0}, y_j) = -\sum_{j
=0}^{k-1} \binom{k}{j}_q (-1)^j q^{\binom{j}{2}},$$

where $y_j$ ranges over $j$-dimensional subspaces of $x$, using the induction hy
pothesis. We invoke the **$q$-binomial theorem**:

$$\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(1 - q^i z) = \sum_{j=0}^{k} \binom{k}{j}_q (-1)^j q^{\binom{
j}{2}} z^j.$$

Setting $z = 1$: the left side is $\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(1-q^i)$, which equals $0$ f
or $k \geq 1$ (the $i=0$ factor is $1-1=0$). Hence

$$\sum_{j=0}^{k} \binom{k}{j}_q (-1)^j q^{\binom{j}{2}} = 0 \quad (k \geq 1),$$

so $\sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \binom{k}{j}_q (-1)^j q^{\binom{j}{2}} = -(-1)^k q^{\binom{
k}{2}}$, and therefore

$$\mu(\hat{0},x) = (-1)^k q^{\binom{k}{2}}. \qquad \checkmark$$

---

**Step 2: Assembling the characteristic polynomial.** Substituting into the defi
nition:

$$\chi_{L_n(q)}(t) = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k}_q (-1)^k q^{\binom{k}{2}}\, t^{
n-k}.$$

---

**Step 3: A key symmetric-function identity.** We claim

$$e_k(1, q, q^2, \ldots, q^{n-1}) = q^{\binom{k}{2}} \binom{n}{k}_q,$$

where $e_k$ is the $k$-th elementary symmetric polynomial. *Proof by induction o
n $n$*, using the standard recurrence $e_k^{(n)} = e_k^{(n-1)} + q^{n-1} e_{k-1}
^{(n-1)}$ (where $e_k^{(n)} = e_k(1,q,\ldots,q^{n-1})$) and the **Gaussian binom
ial recurrence**

$$\binom{n}{k}_q = \binom{n-1}{k}_q + q^{n-k}\binom{n-1}{k-1}_q.$$

The latter follows from $[n-k]_q + q^{n-k}[k]_q = [n]_q$ (where $[m]_q = \frac{q
^m-1}{q-1}$). By induction,

$$e_k^{(n)} = q^{\binom{k}{2}}\binom{n-1}{k}_q + q^{n-1}\cdot q^{\binom{k-1}{2}}
\binom{n-1}{k-1}_q.$$

Since $\binom{k}{2} - \binom{k-1}{2} = k-1$, we have $q^{n-1}q^{\binom{k-1}{2}}
= q^{\binom{k}{2}} q^{n-k}$, so

$$e_k^{(n)} = q^{\binom{k}{2}}\left[\binom{n-1}{k}_q + q^{n-k}\binom{n-1}{k-1}_q
\right] = q^{\binom{k}{2}}\binom{n}{k}_q. \qquad \checkmark$$

---

**Step 4: Product form.** Expanding the product $\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(t - q^i)$, th
e coefficient of $t^{n-k}$ is $(-1)^k e_k(1, q, \ldots, q^{n-1})$. By Step 3 thi
s equals $(-1)^k q^{\binom{k}{2}} \binom{n}{k}_q$, which is exactly the coeffici
ent of $t^{n-k}$ in $\chi_{L_n(q)}(t)$ from Step 2. Therefore

$$\boxed{\chi_{L_n(q)}(t) = \prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(t - q^i\right) = (t-1)(t-q)(t
-q^2)\cdots(t-q^{n-1}).}$$

---

**Remark.** This is a special case of the general theory of **supersolvable latt
ices** (Rota): $L_n(q)$ is supersolvable, and its characteristic polynomial fact
ors completely over the integers into linear factors indexed by the exponents $\
{q^0, q^1, \ldots, q^{n-1}\}$. The result also confirms that $L_n(q)$ is a **geo
metric lattice** whose characteristic polynomial splits — consistent with the Ro
ta–Crapo factorization theorem.
