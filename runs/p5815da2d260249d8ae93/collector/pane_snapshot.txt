## Proof

Let $R = k[x,y]/(y^2 - x^3 - x^2)$. The defining equation is $y^2 = x^3 + x^2 =
x^2(x+1)$, a nodal cubic with a node at the origin.

**Claim:** The integral closure of $R$ (in its field of fractions) is isomorphic
 to $k[t]$, a polynomial ring over $k$.

**Step 1: Identify the fraction field and a candidate parameter.**

In $\operatorname{Frac}(R)$, set $t := y/x$. Squaring gives
$$t^2 = \frac{y^2}{x^2} = \frac{x^2(x+1)}{x^2} = x+1,$$
so $x = t^2 - 1$ and $y = tx = t(t^2-1) = t^3 - t$. Hence $x, y \in k(t)$, which
 gives
$$\operatorname{Frac}(R) = k(x,y) = k(t).$$

**Step 2: $k[t]$ contains $R$ and is integral over $R$.**

Since $x = t^2 - 1$ and $y = t^3 - t$ are both in $k[t]$, the inclusion $R \hook
rightarrow k[t]$ is well-defined (the relation $y^2 - x^3 - x^2 = 0$ is satisfie
d: $(t^3-t)^2 - (t^2-1)^3 - (t^2-1)^2 = 0$).

The element $t$ satisfies the monic polynomial
$$T^2 - (x+1) = 0 \quad \text{over } R,$$
since $t^2 = x + 1$ and $x + 1 \in R$. Therefore $t$ is integral over $R$. As $k
[t] = R[t]$ is generated over $R$ by the single integral element $t$, the ring $
k[t]$ is a finitely generated $R$-module, hence integral over $R$.

**Step 3: $k[t]$ is integrally closed.**

$k[t]$ is a principal ideal domain (hence a UFD), and every UFD is integrally cl
osed in its field of fractions $k(t)$.

**Step 4: $k[t]$ is exactly the integral closure.**

Let $\overline{R}$ denote the integral closure of $R$ in $k(t)$.

- Since $k[t]$ is integral over $R$ and lies in $k(t)$, we have $k[t] \subseteq
\overline{R}$.
- Conversely, any $\alpha \in \overline{R}$ is integral over $R$, hence integral
 over $k[t]$ (as $R \subseteq k[t]$). Since $\alpha \in k(t) = \operatorname{Fra
c}(k[t])$ and $k[t]$ is integrally closed, $\alpha \in k[t]$. Thus $\overline{R}
 \subseteq k[t]$.

Therefore $\overline{R} = k[t]$.

**Conclusion.** The integral closure of $k[x,y]/(y^2 - x^3 - x^2)$ is $k[t]$, wh
ich is a polynomial ring over $k$. The answer is **yes**.
