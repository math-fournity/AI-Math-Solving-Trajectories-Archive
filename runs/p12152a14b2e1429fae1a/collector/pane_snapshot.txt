## Proof

**Step 1: Reduce to the standard Bessel equation.**

Expand the given Sturm-Liouville equation $(rR')' + (k^2 r - \frac{m^2}{r})R = 0
$:

$$rR'' + R' + \left(k^2 r - \frac{m^2}{r}\right)R = 0.$$

Dividing through by $r$:

$$R'' + \frac{1}{r}R' + \left(k^2 - \frac{m^2}{r^2}\right)R = 0.$$

Now substitute $z = kr$, i.e., $R(r) = y(z)$ with $z = kr$. Then $R' = k\,y'$, $
R'' = k^2 y''$, and $r = z/k$, so $\frac{1}{r} = \frac{k}{z}$ and $\frac{m^2}{r^
2} = \frac{m^2 k^2}{z^2}$. Substituting:

$$k^2 y'' + \frac{k^2}{z}y' + \left(k^2 - \frac{m^2 k^2}{z^2}\right)y = 0.$$

Dividing by $k^2$:

$$y'' + \frac{1}{z}y' + \left(1 - \frac{m^2}{z^2}\right)y = 0.$$

This is the **standard Bessel equation of order $m$**.

**Step 2: Identify the two independent solutions and their behavior near $z = 0$
.**

The Bessel equation of order $m$ has two linearly independent solutions. Denote
them $w_+(z)$ and $w_-(z)$, characterized by their behavior near the regular-sin
gular point $z = 0$. By the Frobenius method, the indicial equation gives expone
nts $\pm m$, so:

$$w_+(z) \sim z^{m}, \qquad w_-(z) \sim z^{-m} \quad \text{as } z \to 0.$$

(Concretely, $w_+ = J_m$ and, for integer $m \geq 1$, $w_- = Y_m$.)

The general solution is:

$$R(r) = A\, w_+(kr) + B\, w_-(kr), \qquad A, B \in \mathbb{R}.$$

**Step 3: Apply the boundary condition $R(0) = 0$ for positive integer $m$.**

As $r \to 0^+$ (so $z = kr \to 0$):

- $w_+(kr) \sim (kr)^{m} \to 0$, since $m > 0$.
- $w_-(kr) \sim (kr)^{-m} \to +\infty$, since $m > 0$.

The condition $R(0) = 0$ requires $R$ to be **finite and equal to zero** at $r =
 0$. The term $B\,w_-(kr)$ diverges as $r \to 0$ unless $B = 0$. Therefore, the
boundedness requirement at the origin forces:

$$B = 0.$$

With $B = 0$, the solution reduces to:

$$R(r) = A\, w_+(kr).$$

Moreover, $R(0) = A \cdot w_+(0) = A \cdot 0 = 0$ (since $w_+(z) \sim z^m$ with
$m \geq 1$ vanishes at $z = 0$), so the boundary condition is automatically sati
sfied for any $A$.

**Conclusion.**

The only solutions that are well-defined at $r = 0$ with $R(0) = 0$ are scalar m
ultiples of $w_+(kr)$. Hence $R$ must be proportional to $w_+(kr)$, where $w_+(z
) \sim z^m$ near $z = 0$. $\blacksquare$
