## Proof

Let $|M| = \det(M)$. We show that the proposed inequality $|A|<|B|$ is **false**
; in fact, when $A-B$ is positive definite (and $A,B$ are positive definite), th
e reverse strict inequality holds:

$$|A| > |B|.$$

---

**Setup.** Since $A-B$ is positive definite, write $A = B + C$ with $C$ positive
 definite. Since $B$ is positive definite, it admits a positive definite square
root $B^{1/2}$, with inverse $B^{-1/2}$. Consider the congruence

$$M \;:=\; B^{-1/2} A\, B^{-1/2} \;=\; I + B^{-1/2} C\, B^{-1/2}.$$

Set $D := B^{-1/2} C\, B^{-1/2}$. Congruence by an invertible matrix preserves p
ositive definiteness, so $D$ is positive definite. Hence every eigenvalue $\lamb
da_1,\dots,\lambda_n$ of $D$ satisfies $\lambda_i > 0$.

**Determinant comparison.** Taking determinants and using $\det(B^{-1/2})^2 = 1/
\det(B)$,

$$\det(M) \;=\; \frac{\det(A)}{\det(B)} \;=\; \det(I + D) \;=\; \prod_{i=1}^{n}(
1+\lambda_i).$$

Since each $\lambda_i > 0$, each factor $(1+\lambda_i) > 1$, and therefore

$$\frac{\det(A)}{\det(B)} \;=\; \prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+\lambda_i) \;>\; 1.$$

Because $B$ is positive definite, $\det(B) > 0$. Multiplying through by $\det(B)
$ gives

$$\det(A) \;>\; \det(B), \qquad\text{i.e.,}\qquad |A| > |B|.$$

---

**Conclusion.** The condition "$A-B$ positive definite" forces $A$ to dominate $
B$ in the Loewner order, and this dominance is inherited by the determinant (a s
trictly increasing function on the positive definite cone along rays $B \mapsto
B+C$, $C\succ 0$). Thus the statement $|A|<|B|$ is **not** true; the correct rel
ation is

$$\boxed{|A| > |B|}.$$
