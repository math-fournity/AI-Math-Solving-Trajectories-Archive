**Claim:** Yes. If $M \cong N$ as $R$-modules and $\operatorname{pd}(M) < \infty

torname{pd}(N)$.

**Proof.**

Let $\varphi: M \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} N$ be an $R$-module isomorphism.

Since $\operatorname{pd}(M) < \infty$, there exists a finite projective resoluti
on of $M$:
$$0 \to P_n \xrightarrow{d_n} P_{n-1} \xrightarrow{d_{n-1}} \cdots \xrightarrow{
d_1} P_0 \xrightarrow{\varepsilon} M \to 0,$$
where each $P_i$ is a projective $R$-module and the sequence is exact.

Define $\varepsilon' := \varphi \circ \varepsilon : P_0 \to N$. Since $\varphi$
is an isomorphism,
$$\ker(\varepsilon') = \ker(\varphi \circ \varepsilon) = \ker(\varepsilon) = \op
eratorname{im}(d_1).$$
Therefore the sequence
$$0 \to P_n \xrightarrow{d_n} P_{n-1} \xrightarrow{d_{n-1}} \cdots \xrightarrow{
d_1} P_0 \xrightarrow{\varepsilon'} N \to 0$$
is exact at every position: exactness at $P_0, P_1, \dots, P_n$ is unchanged (th
e maps $d_i$ and their images/kernels are identical), and exactness at $N$ holds
 because $\varepsilon' = \varphi \circ \varepsilon$ is surjective (as a composit
ion of surjections).

This is a finite projective resolution of $N$ of length $n$, so
$$\operatorname{pd}(N) \le n.$$
Taking the infimum over all finite projective resolutions of $M$ gives $\operato
rname{pd}(N) \le \operatorname{pd}(M)$.

By symmetry, applying the same argument with $\varphi^{-1}: N \xrightarrow{\;\si
m\;} M$ to any finite projective resolution of $N$ yields $\operatorname{pd}(M)
\le \operatorname{pd}(N)$.

Combining both inequalities:
$$\operatorname{pd}(M) = \operatorname{pd}(N).$$

In particular, since $\operatorname{pd}(M)$ is finite, $\operatorname{pd}(N)$ is
 finite. $\blacksquare$
