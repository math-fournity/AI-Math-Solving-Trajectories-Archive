## Proof

We want $P\bigl(\{\omega: |X_n(\omega)| \to \infty\}\bigr)$, where $X_n$ is unif
orm on $[-n,n]^2$ and the $X_n$ are independent.

**Key observation.** $|X_n| \to \infty$ iff for every integer $M \ge 1$, the eve
nt $\{|X_n| < M\}$ occurs only finitely often. Hence
$$
\{|X_n| \to \infty\} = \bigcap_{M=1}^{\infty} \bigl\{ |X_n| < M \text{ only fini
tely often} \bigr\}.
$$

**Estimate of $P(|X_n| < M)$.** Since $X_n$ is uniform on the square $[-n,n]^2$
of area $4n^2$,
$$
P(|X_n| < M) = \frac{\operatorname{area}\bigl([-n,n]^2 \cap B(0,M)\bigr)}{4n^2}.
$$
For $n \ge M$, the disk $B(0,M) = \{x : |x| < M\}$ is entirely contained in $[-n
,n]^2$, so
$$
P(|X_n| < M) = \frac{\pi M^2}{4n^2}, \qquad n \ge M.
$$

**Summability.** For each fixed $M$,
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P(|X_n| < M) = \sum_{n < M} P(|X_n|<M) + \sum_{n \ge M} \fra
c{\pi M^2}{4n^2} < \infty,
$$
since the first sum is finite and $\sum_{n\ge M} n^{-2}$ converges.

**Borel–Cantelli.** By the first Borel–Cantelli lemma (which does not require in
dependence),
$$
P\bigl(|X_n| < M \text{ infinitely often}\bigr) = 0 \quad \text{for every } M.
$$
Equivalently, $P\bigl(|X_n| \ge M \text{ for all sufficiently large } n\bigr) =
1$ for every $M$.

**Conclusion.** Taking the countable intersection over $M = 1, 2, \ldots$,
$$
P\bigl(|X_n| \to \infty\bigr) = P\!\left(\bigcap_{M=1}^{\infty} \bigl\{ |X_n| \g
e M \text{ eventually} \bigr\}\right) = \prod_{M=1}^{\infty} 1 = 1.
$$

Therefore,
$$
\boxed{P\bigl(\{\omega: |X_n(\omega)| \to \infty\}\bigr) = 1.}
$$
