## Proof

### Setup: covariant derivative of a general tensor

For a tensor $T^{\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_r}{}_{\beta_1\cdots\beta_s}$ of type $(r,s
)$, the covariant derivative is

$$\nabla_\mu T^{\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_r}{}_{\beta_1\cdots\beta_s}
= \partial_\mu T^{\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_r}{}_{\beta_1\cdots\beta_s}
+ \sum_{i=1}^{r} \Gamma^{\alpha_i}_{\mu\lambda}\, T^{\alpha_1\cdots\lambda\cdots
\alpha_r}{}_{\beta_1\cdots\beta_s}
- \sum_{j=1}^{s} \Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu\beta_j}\, T^{\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_r}{}_{\
beta_1\cdots\lambda\cdots\beta_s}.$$

There are two structurally distinct parts:

1. **The partial-derivative (ordinary-derivative) term** $\partial_\mu T^{\cdots
}{}_{\cdots}$, which measures how the *components* change from point to point.
2. **The connection (Christoffel) terms** $\pm\,\Gamma^{\cdot}_{\mu\cdot}\,T$, o
ne $+\Gamma$ for each contravariant index and one $-\Gamma$ for each covariant i
ndex, which measure how the *basis vectors* (and dual basis vectors) twist as on
e moves through the manifold.

### Why a non-scalar tensor with constant components has non-zero covariant deri
vative

"Constant components" means precisely that, in the chosen coordinate chart,

$$\partial_\mu T^{\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_r}{}_{\beta_1\cdots\beta_s} = 0 \quad\tex
t{for all } \mu.$$

This kills **only the partial-derivative term**. The connection terms survive, b
ecause the Christoffel symbols $\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu\beta}$ are generally **non-
zero** in a general (non-Cartesian, curvilinear, or non-inertial) coordinate sys
tem. Hence

$$\nabla_\mu T^{\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_r}{}_{\beta_1\cdots\beta_s}
= \sum_{i=1}^{r} \Gamma^{\alpha_i}_{\mu\lambda}\, T^{\cdots\lambda\cdots}{}_{\cd
ots}
- \sum_{j=1}^{s} \Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu\beta_j}\, T^{\cdots}{}_{\cdots\lambda\cdo
ts}
\;\neq\; 0 \quad\text{in general.}$$

### Concrete illustration: a constant contravariant vector

Take a vector field $V^\nu$ with constant components, $\partial_\mu V^\nu = 0$.
Its covariant derivative is

$$\nabla_\mu V^\nu = \underbrace{\partial_\mu V^\nu}_{=\,0} + \Gamma^{\nu}_{\mu\
lambda}\, V^{\lambda} = \Gamma^{\nu}_{\mu\lambda}\, V^{\lambda}.$$

In Cartesian coordinates on flat space, $\Gamma^{\nu}_{\mu\lambda}=0$, so $\nabl
a_\mu V^\nu = 0$. But in, e.g., polar coordinates $(r,\theta)$ on the plane, the
 Christoffel symbols are non-zero (e.g. $\Gamma^{r}_{\theta\theta}=-r$, $\Gamma^
{\theta}_{r\theta}=1/r$), so a vector with *constant polar components* still has
 a non-vanishing covariant derivative. The same holds for a covariant vector $\o
mega_\nu$:

$$\nabla_\mu \omega_\nu = \underbrace{\partial_\mu \omega_\nu}_{=\,0} - \Gamma^{
\lambda}_{\mu\nu}\,\omega_\lambda = -\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}\,\omega_\lambda \
neq 0 \text{ in general.}$$

### Why scalars are the exception

For a scalar $\phi$ (a $(0,0)$ tensor) there are **no indices**, hence **no conn
ection terms** at all:

$$\nabla_\mu \phi = \partial_\mu \phi.$$

So constant components ($\partial_\mu\phi=0$) immediately give $\nabla_\mu\phi=0
$. The scalar has no basis-vector attachment to track, so nothing remains once t
he partial derivative vanishes. This is exactly why the phenomenon is specific t
o **non-scalar** tensors.

### Physical/geometric interpretation

A tensor is a geometric object $\mathbf{T} = T^{\cdots}{}_{\cdots}\,\mathbf{e}_{
\cdots}\otimes\cdots$. Even if the *components* $T^{\cdots}{}_{\cdots}$ are cons
tant, the *basis* $\mathbf{e}_{\cdots}$ generally varies from point to point in
a general coordinate system. The covariant derivative must differentiate the **w
hole** object, including the basis. The partial-derivative term handles the comp
onent variation; the Christoffel terms handle the basis variation. Constant comp
onents eliminate only the former; the latter is governed by the geometry of the
coordinate system and is generally non-zero.

### Conclusion

The covariant derivative of a non-scalar tensor with constant components is not
necessarily zero because the connection (Christoffel-symbol) terms — which accou
nt for the variation of the basis vectors — are generally non-zero in general co
ordinate systems. The term that **vanishes** is the **partial-derivative term**
$\partial_\mu T^{\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_r}{}_{\beta_1\cdots\beta_s}$; the survivin
g terms are the $\pm\Gamma$ connection terms, one for each tensor index.
