## Proof

Let $Z = X^2 - Y^2$. We wish to compute $E(Y \mid Z)$, which is a measurable fun
ction of $Z$, say $g(Z)$.

**Symmetry argument.** Since $X, Y \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ are independent, the j
oint distribution of $(X, Y)$ is identical to the joint distribution of $(X, -Y)
$. (Both $Y$ and $-Y$ are standard normal, and independence is preserved.)

The random variable $Z = X^2 - Y^2$ is invariant under the map $Y \mapsto -Y$, s
ince $(-Y)^2 = Y^2$. Therefore the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $Z$, namely $\s
igma(Z) = \sigma(X^2 - Y^2)$, is unchanged when we replace $Y$ by $-Y$.

Using the definition of conditional expectation as the unique (a.s.) $\sigma(Z)$
-measurable random variable satisfying the partial-averaging property, and the f
act that $(X,Y) \stackrel{d}{=} (X,-Y)$:

$$E(Y \mid Z) = E(Y \mid X^2 - Y^2).$$

Replacing $(X,Y)$ by the identically distributed $(X,-Y)$:

$$E(Y \mid X^2 - Y^2) \stackrel{d}{=} E(-Y \mid X^2 - (-Y)^2) = E(-Y \mid X^2 -
Y^2) = -E(Y \mid X^2 - Y^2).$$

Since $E(Y \mid Z) = g(Z)$ is a deterministic function of $Z$, and $g(Z) \stackr
el{d}{=} -g(Z)$, we conclude $g(Z) = -g(Z)$ almost surely, hence:

$$g(Z) = 0 \quad \text{a.s.}$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{E\!\left(Y \mid X^2 - Y^2\right) = 0.}$$
