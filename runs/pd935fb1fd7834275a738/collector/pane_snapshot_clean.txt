# Proof

Let $f \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ be irreducible of degree $5$, and let $K$ be its split
ting field over $\mathbb{Q}$ with Galois group $G = \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb
{Q})$.

**Step 1: $G$ is transitive and contains a $5$-cycle.**

Since $f$ is irreducible, $G$ acts transitively on the five roots of $f$. Hence
$5 \mid |G|$, and by Cauchy's theorem $G$ contains an element of order $5$. Any
element of order $5$ in $S_5$ is a $5$-cycle.

**Step 2: $G$ contains a transposition.**

By hypothesis $f$ has exactly two nonreal roots in $\mathbb{C}$. Nonreal roots o
f a real polynomial occur in conjugate pairs, so the two nonreal roots are $\alp
ha$ and $\bar\alpha$ (with $\alpha \neq \bar\alpha$), and the remaining three ro
ots $r_1, r_2, r_3$ are real. Complex conjugation $z \mapsto \bar z$ restricts t
o a $\mathbb{Q}$-automorphism of $K$ (since $K$ is generated by roots of a polyn
omial over $\mathbb{Q}$, hence is stable under conjugation). This automorphism f
ixes $r_1, r_2, r_3$ and swaps $\alpha \leftrightarrow \bar\alpha$. Thus it acts
 on the roots as the transposition $(\alpha\ \bar\alpha)$. So $G$ contains a tra
nsposition.

**Step 3: $G$ is primitive.**

A transitive group acting on a prime number $p$ of points is primitive: any bloc
k system for a transitive group has block size dividing $p$, so the block size i
s $1$ or $p$, meaning the action is primitive. Here $p = 5$ is prime and $G$ is
transitive, so $G \leq S_5$ is primitive.

**Step 4: Apply Jordan's theorem.**

*Jordan's theorem:* A primitive subgroup of $S_n$ containing a transposition is
equal to $S_n$.

By Step 3, $G$ is primitive on $\{1,\dots,5\}$. By Step 2, $G$ contains a transp
osition. Therefore $G = S_5$.

**Conclusion.** The Galois group of the splitting field of $f$ is

$$\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_5.$$
