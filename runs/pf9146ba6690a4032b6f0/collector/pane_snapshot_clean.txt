**Counterexample ($n=2$).** Consider $f(x) = x^4 + 2x^2 + 2x + 2$ and its compan

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 & -2 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & -2 \\ 0 &
0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

**$A$ has no real eigenvalues.** The global minimum of $f$ occurs at the unique
real root $x_0$ of $f'(x) = 4x^3 + 4x + 2 = 0$. Using $x_0^3 = -(2x_0+1)/2$, we
get $f(x_0) = x_0^2 + \frac{3}{2}x_0 + 2$, whose discriminant is $\frac{9}{4} -
8 = -\frac{23}{4} < 0$. So $f(x_0) > 0$, hence $f(x) > 0$ for all real $x$.

**$A$ is diagonalizable.** The polynomial $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ (
verified: no rational roots, and no factorization into quadratics with integer c

mathbb{Q}$ in characteristic 0, $f$ is separable, so all roots are distinct and
$A$ is diagonalizable over $\mathbb{C}$.

**The Galois group is $S_4$.** The discriminant of $f$ is $\Delta = 2256 = 16 \c
dot 141$, not a perfect square. The resolvent cubic $y^3 - 2y^2 - 8y + 12$ is ir
reducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ (no rational roots among $\pm 1, \pm 2, \pm 3, \pm 4

erline{\lambda_1}, r_3 = \lambda_2, r_4 = \overline{\lambda_2}$. Complex conjuga

**Explicit verification.** Since the $x^3$-coefficient of $f$ is 0, we have $s_1

$$\text{Substituting } y = w + 2 \text{ into } y^3 - 2y^2 - 8y + 12 = 0: \quad w
^3 + 4w^2 - 4w - 4 = 0.$$

This cubic is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ (no rational roots among $\pm 1, \pm
 2, \pm 4$). Therefore $s_1^2 \notin \mathbb{Q}$, hence $s_1^2 \notin \mathbb{Z}
$.

*.
