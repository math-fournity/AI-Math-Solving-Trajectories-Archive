## Solution

**Claim:** No such function exists. For every $f \in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$ (hence i
n particular for every $f$ that is both $L^{1}$ and Riemann integrable), one has

$$
\lim_{\omega\to\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f(x)\,e^{-ix\omega}\,dx=0.
$$

This is the **Riemann–Lebesgue lemma**. The hypothesis "$f$ is Riemann integrabl
e" is in fact redundant; $L^{1}$ alone suffices. Below is a complete proof.

---

### Proof

Denote $\widehat{f}(\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{-ix\omega}\,dx$. We prove $\
widehat{f}(\omega)\to 0$ as $|\omega|\to\infty$ for every $f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}
)$.

**Step 1 — Characteristic functions of intervals.**
Let $f=\mathbf{1}_{[a,b]}$. Then for $\omega\neq 0$,

$$
\widehat{f}(\omega)=\int_{a}^{b}e^{-ix\omega}\,dx=\frac{e^{-ia\omega}-e^{-ib\ome
ga}}{i\omega},
$$

so $|\widehat{f}(\omega)|\le \dfrac{2}{|\omega|}\to 0$.

**Step 2 — Simple functions.**
If $g=\sum_{k=1}^{N}c_{k}\,\mathbf{1}_{[a_{k},b_{k}]}$ is a step function, then
by linearity and Step 1,

$$
|\widehat{g}(\omega)|\le\sum_{k=1}^{N}|c_{k}|\cdot|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{[a_{k},b
_{k}]}}(\omega)|\to 0.
$$

**Step 3 — Density and approximation.**
Step functions are dense in $L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$: for every $f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R
})$ and every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists a step function $g$ with $\|f-g\|_{1}
<\varepsilon$.

Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and choose such a $g$. Using $|e^{-ix\omega}|=1$ and the tri
angle inequality,

$$
|\widehat{f}(\omega)|\le|\widehat{f}(\omega)-\widehat{g}(\omega)|+|\widehat{g}(\
omega)|\le\|f-g\|_{1}+|\widehat{g}(\omega)|<\varepsilon+|\widehat{g}(\omega)|.
$$

By Step 2 there is $M>0$ such that $|\widehat{g}(\omega)|<\varepsilon$ for all $
|\omega|>M$. Hence for $|\omega|>M$,

$$
|\widehat{f}(\omega)|<2\varepsilon.
$$

Since $\varepsilon>0$ was arbitrary, $\widehat{f}(\omega)\to 0$ as $|\omega|\to\
infty$.

### Conclusion

The Riemann–Lebesgue lemma shows that **every** $L^{1}$ function has a Fourier t
ransform vanishing at infinity. The additional requirement of Riemann integrabil
ity only restricts the class of admissible functions further and cannot circumve
nt this conclusion. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{No such function exists.}}
$$

For every $f$ that is both $L^{1}$ and Riemann integrable,

$$
\lim_{\omega\to\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f(x)\,e^{-ix\omega}\,dx=0.
$$
