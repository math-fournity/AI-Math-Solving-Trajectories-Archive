# Proof

Let $N = \overline{a_1 a_2 \cdots a_n}$ and $M = \overline{a_2 a_3 \cdots a_n}$.
 Then $N = a_1 \cdot 10^{n-1} + M$, so the divisibility condition $M \mid N$ is
equivalent to

$$M \mid a_1 \cdot 10^{n-1}.$$

Since $M$ is an $(n-1)$-digit number, $10^{n-2} \le M < 10^{n-1}$. Since $M \mid
 a_1 \cdot 2^{n-1} \cdot 5^{n-1}$ with $a_1 \in \{1,\dots,9\}$, every prime fact
or of $M$ lies in $\{2,3,5,7\}$.

**Key observation.** If $2 \mid M$ and $5 \mid M$, then $10 \mid M$, forcing the
 last digit of $M$ to be $0$—impossible since all digits are non-zero. Hence $M$
 is not divisible by both $2$ and $5$. Write $M = 2^a \cdot u$ (with $5 \nmid M$
) or $M = 5^c \cdot u$ (with $2 \nmid M$), where $u$ divides the part of $a_1$ c
oprime to $5$ (resp. to $2$).

---

## Upper bound: $n \le 7$

**Case 1 ($5 \nmid M$):** $M = 2^a \cdot u$, $u \le 9$, $a \le n{-}1$. Then $M \
le 9 \cdot 2^{n-1}$. The constraint $M \ge 10^{n-2}$ gives $9 \cdot 2^{n-1} \ge
10^{n-2}$, i.e. $9 \ge 5^{n-2}/2$, so $5^{n-2} \le 18$, giving $n \le 3$.

**Case 2 ($2 \nmid M$):** $M = 5^c \cdot u$, $u \le 9$ (max odd part of any $a_1
 \le 9$), $c \le n{-}1$. Then $M \le 9 \cdot 5^{n-1}$. The constraint $M \ge 10^
{n-2}$ gives $9 \cdot 5^{n-1} \ge 10^{n-2} = 2^{n-2} \cdot 5^{n-2}$, so $45 \ge
2^{n-2}$, giving $n \le 7$.

Thus $n \le 7$.

---

## Eliminating $n = 7$

For $n=7$ we need a 6-digit $M$ with $10^5 \le M < 10^6$, all digits non-zero, $
M \mid a_1 \cdot 10^6$.

Case 1 gives $n \le 3$, so only Case 2 applies: $M = 5^c \cdot u$, $c \le 6$, $u
 \in \{1,3,7,9\}$ (odd, $\gcd(u,5)=1$, $u \mid a_1$).

For $M \ge 10^5$:

| $u$ | Required $c$ | $M = u \cdot 5^c$ | Digits | Valid? |
|-----|-------------|-------------------|--------|--------|
| 9 | $c=6$ ($9\cdot5^5=28125<10^5$) | $140625$ | contains 0 | ✗ |
| 7 | $c=6$ ($7\cdot5^5=21875<10^5$) | $109375$ | contains 0 | ✗ |
| $\le 5$ | $c=6$ gives $\le 78125<10^5$ | — | — | ✗ |

No valid $M$ exists for $n=7$. Hence $n \le 6$.

---

## Construction for $n = 6$

Take $a_1 = 9$ and $M = 9 \cdot 5^5 = 28125$.

- **$M$ is 5-digit with all non-zero digits:** $2,8,1,2,5$. ✓
- **Divisibility:** $9 \cdot 10^5 = 900000 = 28125 \times 32$, so $M \mid a_1 \c
dot 10^5$. ✓
- **$N = 928125$**, digits $9,2,8,1,2,5$ — all non-zero. ✓
- **$N/M = 928125/28125 = 33$** (integer). ✓
- **Position constraints:** $a_1=9\ne0$, $a_2=2\ne0$, $a_6=5\ne0$. ✓

---

## Conclusion

The largest possible number of codes is $\boxed{6}$.
