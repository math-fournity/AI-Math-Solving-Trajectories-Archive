Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether topological separation of a
 point from a closed set in a compact Hausdorff uniform space can be upgraded to
 separation by a single entourage.

**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

Let $X$ be a compact Hausdorff space with a compatible uniformity $\mathcal{U}$,
 and let $x \in X$, $A \subseteq X$ closed with $x \notin A$. (The stated separa
tion $U \cap V = \emptyset$ with $x \in U$, $A \subseteq V$ implicitly requires
$x \notin A$; if $x \in A$ then $U[x] \cap U[A] \supseteq U[x] \neq \emptyset$ f
or any entourage, so the question is only meaningful when $x \notin A$.)

Since $X$ is compact Hausdorff, it carries a **unique** compatible uniformity (a
ll neighborhoods of the diagonal), and this uniformity is Hausdorff: it separate
s distinct points.

**Key observations:**
1. $A$ is a closed subset of a compact space, hence $A$ is **compact**.
2. The uniformity $\mathcal{U}$ is Hausdorff: for $x \neq a$, there exists $W_a
\in \mathcal{U}$ with $(x, a) \notin W_a$.

**Step 1: Local uniform separation.** For each $a \in A$, since $x \neq a$, choo
se $W_a \in \mathcal{U}$ with $(x,a) \notin W_a$. By the uniformity axioms, choo
se a **symmetric** entourage $V_a \in \mathcal{U}$ with $V_a \circ V_a \circ V_a
 \subseteq W_a$.

**Claim:** $V_a[x] \cap (V_a \circ V_a)[a] = \emptyset$.

*Proof of claim:* If $z$ lies in both, then $(x, z) \in V_a$ and $(a, z) \in V_a
 \circ V_a$, so there exists $y$ with $(a, y) \in V_a$ and $(y, z) \in V_a$. By
symmetry, $(z, y) \in V_a$ and $(y, a) \in V_a$, giving $(z, a) \in V_a \circ V_
a$. Combined with $(x, z) \in V_a$, we get $(x, a) \in V_a \circ V_a \circ V_a \
subseteq W_a$, contradicting $(x, a) \notin W_a$. $\square$

**Step 2: Compactness reduction.** Each $V_a[a]$ is a neighborhood of $a$ in the
 uniform topology (which agrees with the given topology by compatibility). Thus
$\{\operatorname{int}(V_a[a]) : a \in A\}$ is an open cover of $A$. Since $A$ is
 compact, extract a finite subcover indexed by $a_1, \dots, a_n$:
$$A \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} V_{a_i}[a_i].$$

**Step 3: Intersection entourage.** Set $V = \bigcap_{i=1}^{n} V_{a_i}$. This is
 a finite intersection of entourages, hence an entourage; it is symmetric (inter
section of symmetric sets).

**Step 4: Verification that $V[x] \cap V[A] = \emptyset$.**

Suppose for contradiction that $z \in V[x] \cap V[A]$. Then:
- $z \in V[x] \subseteq V_{a_i}[x]$ for every $i$ (since $V \subseteq V_{a_i}$),
 so $(x, z) \in V_{a_i}$ for all $i$.
- $z \in V[A]$, so $z \in V[b]$ for some $b \in A$, meaning $(b, z) \in V$.
- Since $b \in A$, we have $b \in V_{a_j}[a_j]$ for some $j$, so $(a_j, b) \in V
_{a_j}$.

Now $(a_j, b) \in V_{a_j}$ and $(b, z) \in V \subseteq V_{a_j}$, so $(a_j, z) \i
n V_{a_j} \circ V_{a_j}$, i.e., $z \in (V_{a_j} \circ V_{a_j})[a_j]$.

But also $z \in V_{a_j}[x]$. This gives $z \in V_{a_j}[x] \cap (V_{a_j} \circ V_
{a_j})[a_j] = \emptyset$ by the claim from Step 1 — **contradiction**.

Therefore $V[x] \cap V[A] = \emptyset$, and $V \in \mathcal{U}$ is the desired e
ntourage. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The proof uses three ingredients: (i) the uniformity is Hausdorff (f
rom $X$ being Hausdorff), (ii) $A$ is compact (closed in compact), enabling a fi
nite subcover, and (iii) finite intersections of entourages are entourages. Norm
ality is automatically satisfied by compact Hausdorff spaces and guarantees the
topological separation stated in the problem; the uniform separation is a streng
thening achieved via compactness of $A$.
