Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether, for a rational function $r
$ with a pole at $a$, there exists a polynomial $p$ such that $r(x) - p(1/(x-a))
 \to 0$ as $x \to a$.

**Answer: Yes, such a polynomial necessarily exists.**

## Proof

Let $r(x) = P(x)/Q(x)$ where $P, Q \in \mathbb{R}[x]$ with $\gcd(P,Q)=1$. Since
$a$ is a pole of $r$, we have $Q(a)=0$ and $P(a)\neq 0$.

Let $m \geq 1$ be the order of the zero of $Q$ at $a$, so $Q(x) = (x-a)^m\,\wide
tilde{Q}(x)$ where $\widetilde{Q}$ is a polynomial with $\widetilde{Q}(a)\neq 0$
.

**Step 1: Laurent expansion of $r$ at $a$.**

Since $\widetilde{Q}(a)\neq 0$, the function $f(x) = P(x)/\widetilde{Q}(x)$ is r
eal-analytic in a neighborhood of $a$, so it admits a convergent Taylor expansio
n:
$$f(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} b_k\,(x-a)^k, \qquad |x-a|<R$$
for some $R>0$. Therefore, for $x\neq a$ near $a$:
$$r(x) = \frac{f(x)}{(x-a)^m} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} b_k\,(x-a)^{k-m}.$$

**Step 2: Construct the polynomial $p$.**

Define the polynomial (in the variable $t$):
$$p(t) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{m} b_{m-i}\,t^{i} \;+\; b_m.$$

This is a polynomial of degree $m$ (the leading coefficient is $b_0 = P(a)/\wide
tilde{Q}(a) \neq 0$).

**Step 3: Compute the difference.**

Substituting $t = 1/(x-a)$:
$$p\!\left(\tfrac{1}{x-a}\right) = \sum_{i=1}^{m} b_{m-i}\,(x-a)^{-i} + b_m.$$

Re-indexing the sum with $k = m-i$ (so $i$ runs $1,\dots,m$ gives $k$ runs $m-1,
\dots,0$):
$$p\!\left(\tfrac{1}{x-a}\right) = \sum_{k=0}^{m-1} b_k\,(x-a)^{k-m} + b_m.$$

Now compute:
$$r(x) - p\!\left(\tfrac{1}{x-a}\right) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} b_k\,(x-a)^{k-m} -
 \sum_{k=0}^{m-1} b_k\,(x-a)^{k-m} - b_m$$
$$= \sum_{k=m}^{\infty} b_k\,(x-a)^{k-m} - b_m = \left(b_m + \sum_{k=m+1}^{\inft
y} b_k\,(x-a)^{k-m}\right) - b_m$$
$$= \sum_{j=1}^{\infty} b_{m+j}\,(x-a)^{j},$$
where we set $j = k - m$.

**Step 4: Take the limit.**

The expression $\sum_{j=1}^{\infty} b_{m+j}\,(x-a)^{j}$ is a power series in $(x
-a)$ with no constant term, converging for $|x-a|<R$. Every term $(x-a)^j \to 0$
 as $x\to a$ for $j\geq 1$, and by convergence of the power series:
$$\lim_{x\to a}\left|r(x) - p\!\left(\tfrac{1}{x-a}\right)\right| = \lim_{x\to a
}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty} b_{m+j}\,(x-a)^{j}\right| = 0.$$

**Conclusion.** The principal part of the Laurent expansion of a rational functi
on at any pole $a$ is always a **finite** sum of negative powers of $(x-a)$, whi
ch is precisely a polynomial in $1/(x-a)$. By including the constant term $b_m$
(the value $c_0$ of the Laurent series) in $p$, the analytic remainder vanishes
as $x\to a$. Hence such a polynomial $p$ **necessarily exists**. $\blacksquare$
