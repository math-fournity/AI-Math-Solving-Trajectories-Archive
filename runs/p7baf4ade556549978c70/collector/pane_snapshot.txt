## Solution

**Substitution.** Let $u = \sqrt{x}$, so $x = u^2$, $dx = 2u\,du$. The limits $x
\in[0,\infty)$ map to $u\in[0,\infty)$, and $\ln x = \ln(u^2) = 2\ln u$. Hence

$$\mathcal{J} = \int_0^{\infty} \frac{u\cdot 2\ln u}{e^{u}}\cdot 2u\,du = 4\int_
0^{\infty} u^{2}\,e^{-u}\,\ln u\,du.$$

**Reduction to the Gamma function.** Recall

$$\Gamma(s)=\int_0^{\infty}u^{s-1}e^{-u}\,du,\qquad \Gamma'(s)=\int_0^{\infty}u^
{s-1}e^{-u}\ln u\,du.$$

Setting $s-1=2$, i.e. $s=3$,

$$\int_0^{\infty}u^{2}e^{-u}\ln u\,du=\Gamma'(3),\qquad\text{so}\qquad \mathcal{
J}=4\,\Gamma'(3).$$

**Digamma identity.** Since $\Gamma'(s)=\Gamma(s)\,\psi(s)$, where $\psi$ is the
 digamma function:

- $\Gamma(3)=2!=2$.
- $\psi(1)=-\gamma$ (the Euler–Mascheroni constant).
- Recurrence $\psi(s+1)=\psi(s)+\tfrac{1}{s}$ gives
$$\psi(2)=\psi(1)+1=1-\gamma,\qquad \psi(3)=\psi(2)+\tfrac12=\tfrac32-\gamma.$$

Therefore

$$\Gamma'(3)=\Gamma(3)\,\psi(3)=2\!\left(\tfrac32-\gamma\right)=3-2\gamma,$$

and finally

$$\boxed{\mathcal{J}=4\,\Gamma'(3)=4(3-2\gamma)=12-8\gamma.}\qquad\blacksquare$$
