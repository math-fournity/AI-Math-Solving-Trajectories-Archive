# Proof

**Claim:** Yes, there exists a sequence of rational numbers $\frac{p(n)}{q(n)}$
with $p(n), q(n) \in \mathbb{Z}$ given by explicit (known) functions of $n$, suc
h that $\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{p(n)}{q(n)} = \pi$.

---

## Construction via the Leibniz series

The Gregory–Leibniz series for the arctangent gives, for $|x| \le 1$:

$$\arctan(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k\, x^{2k+1}}{2k+1}.$$

Setting $x = 1$ (where the series converges by the alternating series test, sinc
e $\frac{1}{2k+1} \to 0$ monotonically):

$$\frac{\pi}{4} = \arctan(1) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k}{2k+1} = 1 - \fr
ac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{5} - \frac{1}{7} + \cdots$$

Define the $n$-th partial sum:

$$S_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \frac{(-1)^k}{2k+1}.$$

**$S_n$ is rational.** Each term $\frac{(-1)^k}{2k+1}$ is rational, and a finite
 sum of rationals is rational. Explicitly, set

$$q(n) = \prod_{k=0}^{n}(2k+1) = 1 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdots (2n+1), \qquad p(n) =
\sum_{k=0}^{n} (-1)^k \cdot \frac{q(n)}{2k+1}.$$

Both $p(n)$ and $q(n)$ are integers given by explicit, known functions of $n$ (a
 finite product and a finite sum of integers respectively), and

$$S_n = \frac{p(n)}{q(n)}.$$

**Convergence.** Since the Leibniz series converges to $\pi/4$:

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} S_n = \frac{\pi}{4}.$$

**Scaling to $\pi$.** Define

$$P(n) = 4\,p(n), \qquad Q(n) = q(n).$$

Then $P(n), Q(n) \in \mathbb{Z}$ are explicit functions of $n$, and

$$\frac{P(n)}{Q(n)} = 4 \cdot \frac{p(n)}{q(n)} = 4\,S_n \;\longrightarrow\; 4 \
cdot \frac{\pi}{4} = \pi \quad \text{as } n \to \infty.$$

---

## Verification of the requirements

1. **$P(n)$ and $Q(n)$ are whole numbers:** $P(n) = 4p(n)$ and $Q(n) = q(n)$ are
 both integers by construction.

2. **They are known functions of $n$:** Both are given by finite, explicit formu
las (a product of odd integers and a sum of signed integer terms), requiring no
knowledge of $\pi$ itself to compute.

3. **The limit is $\pi$:** This follows from the convergence of the Gregory–Leib
niz series, a classical result proved via the integral representation $\arctan(1
) = \int_0^1 \frac{dt}{1+t^2} = \frac{\pi}{4}$ and term-by-term integration of t
he geometric series $\frac{1}{1+t^2} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^k t^{2k}$ on $[0,
1]$.

---

## Conclusion

The sequence $\displaystyle\frac{P(n)}{Q(n)} = 4\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^k}{2k+1
}$ is a sequence of rational numbers, with numerator and denominator given by ex
plicit integer-valued functions of $n$, converging to $\pi$. Therefore such a se
ries **exists**. $\blacksquare$
