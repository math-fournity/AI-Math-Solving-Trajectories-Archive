## Proof

We use the **Preimage Theorem** for transverse maps between manifolds (with the
boundary case handled by the transversality hypothesis on both $f$ and $f|_{\par
tial M}$).

**Setup.** $M$ is an $m$-manifold with boundary $\partial M$, $N$ is an $n$-mani
fold without boundary, $Q\subset N$ is a codimension-$k$ submanifold without bou
ndary, and $f\colon M\to N$ is a smooth map with both $f\pitchfork Q$ and $f|_{\
partial M}\pitchfork Q$.

**Local model of transversality.** The condition $f\pitchfork Q$ means: for ever
y $x\in f^{-1}(Q)$,
$$df_x(T_xM) + T_{f(x)}Q = T_{f(x)}N.$$
Equivalently, the composite
$$T_xM \xrightarrow{df_x} T_{f(x)}N \longrightarrow T_{f(x)}N/T_{f(x)}Q \cong \m
athbb{R}^k$$
is **surjective**, where the quotient $T_{f(x)}N/T_{f(x)}Q$ has dimension $\oper
atorname{codim}_N Q = k$.

**Kernel dimension.** The kernel of this surjective linear map $T_xM\to\mathbb{R
}^k$ is exactly
$$T_x(f^{-1}(Q)) = df_x^{-1}(T_{f(x)}Q),$$
which has dimension
$$\dim T_xM - k = m - k.$$
So every point of $f^{-1}(Q)$ in the interior of $M$ has a neighborhood in which
 $f^{-1}(Q)$ is a submanifold of dimension $m-k$, i.e., codimension $k$ in $M$.

**Boundary points.** For $x\in \partial M\cap f^{-1}(Q)$, the hypothesis $f|_{\p
artial M}\pitchfork Q$ gives
$$d(f|_{\partial M})_x(T_x\partial M) + T_{f(x)}Q = T_{f(x)}N,$$
so $f^{-1}(Q)\cap\partial M = (f|_{\partial M})^{-1}(Q)$ is a submanifold of $\p
artial M$ of codimension $k$, hence dimension $(m-1)-k$. Combined with the inter
ior behavior, the Preimage Theorem for manifolds with boundary (Guillemin–Pollac
k, Ch. 2, §8) yields that
$$P = f^{-1}(Q)$$
is a **properly embedded submanifold of $M$ with boundary**, where
$$\dim P = m - k,\qquad \partial P = P\cap\partial M.$$

**Codimension.** Since $\dim M = m$ and $\dim P = m-k$,
$$\operatorname{codim}_M P = \dim M - \dim P = m - (m-k) = k.$$

The codimension is preserved under transverse preimage: the codimension of $f^{-
1}(Q)$ in $M$ equals the codimension of $Q$ in $N$.

$$\boxed{\operatorname{codim}_M P = k.}$$
