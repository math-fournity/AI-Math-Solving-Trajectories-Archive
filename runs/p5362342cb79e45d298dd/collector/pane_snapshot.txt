**Setup.** Define $X_t := B_t - 5t$. Then $T = \inf\{t \ge 0 : X_t = -2\}$, the
first hitting time of level $-2$ by the process $X_t = B_t - 5t$, a Brownian mot
ion with drift $\mu = -5$ and diffusion coefficient $\sigma = 1$.

**Reduction to positive drift.** Set $Y_t := -X_t = -B_t + 5t$. Since $(-B_t)_{t
\ge 0}$ is again a standard Brownian motion (call it $\widetilde B_t$), we have
$$Y_t = 5t + \widetilde B_t,$$
a Brownian motion with drift $+5$ and diffusion $1$. Moreover
$$T = \inf\{t \ge 0 : X_t = -2\} = \inf\{t \ge 0 : Y_t = 2\}.$$
So $T$ is the first hitting time of level $a = 2 > 0$ by a Brownian motion with
drift $\mu = 5 > 0$ and diffusion $\sigma = 1$.

**Computing $E[T]$ via the Laplace transform.** Let $u(\lambda) = E[e^{-\lambda
T}]$. By the exponential martingale / optional stopping argument (or equivalentl
y solving the ODE $\tfrac12 u'' + \mu u' = \lambda u$ with $u(0)=1$ at the start
ing point and $u\to 0$ as the starting point $\to -\infty$), for a Brownian moti
on with drift $\mu>0$ and diffusion $\sigma=1$ hitting level $a>0$,
$$E[e^{-\lambda T}] = \exp\!\Big(a\big(\mu - \sqrt{\mu^2 + 2\lambda}\big)\Big),
\qquad \lambda \ge 0.$$
(The relevant root is $\mu - \sqrt{\mu^2+2\lambda}<0$, ensuring the transform is
 $\le 1$ and decays as $\lambda\to\infty$.)

Differentiating at $\lambda = 0$:
$$E[T] = -\frac{d}{d\lambda}\bigg|_{\lambda=0} E[e^{-\lambda T}] = -a\cdot\frac{
d}{d\lambda}\bigg|_{\lambda=0}\big(\mu-\sqrt{\mu^2+2\lambda}\big) = -a\cdot\!\le
ft(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu^2+2\lambda}}\right)\!\bigg|_{\lambda=0} = \frac{a}{\mu}.$
$

**Substituting.** With $a = 2$ and $\mu = 5$,
$$E[T] = \frac{a}{\mu} = \frac{2}{5}.$$

**Finiteness check.** Since the drift $\mu = 5 > 0$ points toward the level $a =
 2 > 0$, the hitting time is finite almost surely and has finite expectation (th
e inverse-Gaussian hitting-time density is proper and integrable), so the differ
entiation under the expectation is justified.

$$\boxed{E[T] = \dfrac{2}{5}}$$
