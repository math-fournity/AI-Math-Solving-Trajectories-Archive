## Solution

**Answer: $\dfrac{p^n - 1}{p - 1}$**

### Setup and key observation

Let $\mathbf{P}$ be such a family. Call a subset $A \subseteq S$ a *part* if $A$
 is a part of some partition in $\mathbf{P}$. The hypothesis says any two parts
intersect in at most one element. (Parts from the same partition are disjoint, s
o the condition is automatic there; the real constraint is between parts from di
fferent partitions.)

**Claim.** Any unordered pair $\{x,y\} \subseteq S$ is contained in at most one
part (across all partitions).

*Proof.* If $\{x,y\} \subseteq A \cap B$ for parts $A$ of partition $\pi_1$ and
$B$ of partition $\pi_2$:
- If $\pi_1 = \pi_2$, then $A = B$ (parts of one partition are disjoint or equal
), so it's the same part.
- If $\pi_1 \neq \pi_2$, then $|A \cap B| \geq 2$, contradicting the hypothesis.
 $\square$

### Upper bound

Each partition $\pi$ has parts of sizes $s_1, \dots, s_k$ with $s_i \geq p$, $p
\mid s_i$, and $\sum s_i = p^n$. The number of pairs it covers is
$$\sum_{i} \binom{s_i}{2} = \tfrac{1}{2}\Big(\sum s_i^2 - p^n\Big).$$
By convexity, $\sum s_i^2$ is minimized when the parts are as small and as equal
 as possible, i.e. all $s_i = p$ (giving $k = p^{n-1}$ parts). Hence
$$\sum s_i^2 \geq p^{n-1} \cdot p^2 = p^{n+1}, \qquad \text{so} \qquad \sum_i \b
inom{s_i}{2} \geq \tfrac{1}{2}(p^{n+1} - p^n) = \tfrac{p^n(p-1)}{2}.$$

Since every pair is covered at most once across all partitions, the total pairs
covered is at most $\binom{p^n}{2} = \frac{p^n(p^n-1)}{2}$. Therefore
$$|\mathbf{P}| \cdot \frac{p^n(p-1)}{2} \leq \frac{p^n(p^n-1)}{2}, \qquad \text{
giving} \qquad |\mathbf{P}| \leq \frac{p^n - 1}{p - 1}.$$

### Construction achieving the bound

Take $S = \mathbb{F}_p^n$ (the $n$-dimensional vector space over $\mathbb{F}_p$)
, so $|S| = p^n$. For each 1-dimensional subspace (direction) $D \leq \mathbb{F}
_p^n$, the collection of all affine cosets $a + D$ ($a \in \mathbb{F}_p^n$) form
s a partition of $S$ into $p^{n-1}$ parts, each of size $p$ (divisible by $p$).
Let $\mathbf{P}$ be the set of all such parallel classes.

- **Number of partitions** = number of 1-dimensional subspaces of $\mathbb{F}_p^
n$ $= \dfrac{p^n - 1}{p - 1}$.
- **Intersection condition**: two affine lines from different parallel classes a
re non-parallel, so they share at most one point (two distinct points determine
a unique line). Lines from the same class are disjoint. Hence any two parts inte
rsect in at most one element. ✓

This matches the upper bound, so the maximum is achieved.

### PROOF COMPLETE

Final Answer: $\boxed{\dfrac{p^n - 1}{p - 1}}$
