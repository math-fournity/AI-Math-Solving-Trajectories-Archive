# Maximal Subgroups of PSL(2,7)

## Setup and Basic Facts

$PSL(2,7) = SL(2,7)/\{\pm I\}$ has order
$$|SL(2,7)| = 7(7^2-1) = 336, \quad |PSL(2,7)| = 336/2 = 168 = 2^3 \cdot 3 \cdot
 7.$$
$PSL(2,7)$ is simple and is isomorphic to $PSL(3,2) = GL(3,2)$ (both act on $168
$ elements; the isomorphism is classical). We use both viewpoints: the $PSL(2,7)
$ view for the Borel subgroups and the $GL(3,2)$ view for the $S_4$ subgroups, v
ia the action on the **Fano plane** (the projective plane $\mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{
F}_2)$ with $7$ points and $7$ lines).

---

## 1. Borel Subgroups (Order 21) — Maximal

The **Borel subgroup** $B$ consists of upper-triangular matrices in $PSL(2,7)$:
$$B = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & a^{-1} \end{pmatrix} : a \in \mathbb{
F}_7^*,\ b \in \mathbb{F}_7 \right\} / \{\pm I\}.$$
Its order is $|B| = 7 \cdot 3 = 21$ (the unipotent part $C_7$ semidirect the dia
gonal torus $C_3 = \mathbb{F}_7^*/\{\pm 1\}$). This is the Frobenius group $F_{2
1} \cong C_7 \rtimes C_3$.

$B$ is the stabilizer of the point $\infty \in \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_7)$. Sinc
e $PSL(2,7)$ acts $2$-transitively on the $8$ points of $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}
_7)$, all point stabilizers are conjugate, giving **$8$ conjugate Borel subgroup
s** (one conjugacy class). The lower-triangular matrices are conjugate to the up
per-triangular ones via the Weyl element $w = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \e
nd{pmatrix} \in PSL(2,7)$, so they belong to the same conjugacy class.

**Maximality:** A subgroup properly containing $B$ would have order $k \cdot 21$
 dividing $168$ with $k > 1$, so order $42$ or $84$. A subgroup of index $4$ (or
der $42$) or index $2$ (order $84$) would yield a nontrivial homomorphism $PSL(2
,7) \to S_4$ or $\to C_2$. Since $PSL(2,7)$ is simple, the kernel is trivial or
everything. Trivial kernel would embed $PSL(2,7)$ into $S_4$ (impossible: $168 >
 24$) or $C_2$ (impossible: $168 > 2$). So **no subgroup of order $42$ or $84$ e
xists**, and $B$ is maximal. $\checkmark$

---

## 2. $S_4$ Subgroups (Order 24) — Maximal

### Existence via the Fano plane

Under the isomorphism $PSL(2,7) \cong GL(3,2)$, the group acts on the Fano plane
 $\mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{F}_2)$: $7$ points (1-dimensional subspaces of $\mathbb{F
}_2^3$) and $7$ lines (2-dimensional subspaces).

**Point stabilizers.** The stabilizer of a point $P = \langle e_1 \rangle$ consi
sts of matrices $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & a & b \\ 0 & * & * \\ 0 & * & * \end{pmatri
x}$ in $GL(3,2)$, of order $|GL(2,2)| \cdot 2^2 = 6 \cdot 4 = 24$. The projectio
n to the lower-right $2\times 2$ block gives a surjection onto $GL(2,2) \cong S_
3$ with kernel $V_4 \cong C_2^2$ (the transvections fixing $P$). The unique grou
p of order $24$ with a normal $V_4$ and quotient $S_3$ is $S_4$. Equivalently, t
he stabilizer acts faithfully on the $4$ lines not through $P$, embedding into $
S_4$; since both have order $24$, this is an isomorphism. So **each point stabil
izer is isomorphic to $S_4$**.

**Line stabilizers.** By the same argument (stabilizing a 2-dimensional subspace
), each line stabilizer has order $168/7 = 24$ and is isomorphic to $S_4$.

### Two conjugacy classes

A point is a $1$-dimensional subspace and a line is a $2$-dimensional subspace.
No element of $GL(3,2)$ maps a $1$-space to a $2$-space (elements preserve subsp
ace dimension), so **point stabilizers and line stabilizers are not conjugate**
in $PSL(2,7)$. They form **two distinct conjugacy classes**, each of size $7$.

These two classes are fused by the outer automorphism $\operatorname{Out}(PSL(2,
7)) \cong C_2$ (arising from $PGL(2,7)$, or equivalently the graph/duality autom
orphism of the Fano plane that swaps points and lines).

### Maximality

A subgroup properly containing an $S_4$ (order $24$) would have order $k \cdot 2
4$ dividing $168$ with $k > 1$. The only multiple of $24$ dividing $168$ beyond
$24$ itself is $168$ (since $48 \nmid 168$, $72 \nmid 168$, $120 \nmid 168$). So
 $S_4$ is **maximal**. $\checkmark$

No point stabilizer equals any line stabilizer: if $\operatorname{Stab}(P) = \op
eratorname{Stab}(\ell)$, this subgroup fixes both $P$ and $\ell$. If $P \in \ell
$, the flag stabilizer has order $168/21 = 8 \neq 24$. If $P \notin \ell$, the a
nti-flag stabilizer has order $168/28 = 6 \neq 24$. Contradiction either way. So
 the two classes are genuinely distinct, and both are maximal.

## 3. No Other Maximal Subgroups Exist

We check every divisor $d$ of $168 = 2^3 \cdot 3 \cdot 7$ with $d < 168$:

| Order | Exists? | Reason |
|-------|---------|--------|
| $84$ | No | Index $2$ → normal subgroup, contradicting simplicity |
| $56$ | No | Index $3$ → homomorphism to $S_3$; simple group can't embed in $S_
3$ ($168 > 6$) |
| $42$ | No | Index $4$ → homomorphism to $S_4$; $168 > 24$, impossible |
| $28$ | No | Would have normal $C_7$ (Sylow), so contained in $N_G(C_7) = B$ (o
rder $21 < 28$), contradiction |
| $24$ | **Yes — $S_4$** | Maximal (shown above) |
| $21$ | **Yes — Borel** | Maximal (shown above) |
| $14$ | No | Would have normal $C_7$, so contained in $N_G(C_7) = B$ (order $21
$), but $14 \nmid 21$ |
| $12$ | Yes ($A_4$) | Contained in $S_4$ (since $A_4 \trianglelefteq S_4$); not
 maximal |
| $8$ | Yes ($D_8$) | Sylow $2$-subgroup; contained in $S_4$ (as Sylow $2$ of $S
_4$); not maximal |
| $\leq 7$ | Yes | Contained in Borel or $S_4$; not maximal |

**Key eliminations:**

- **Order $28$:** A group of order $28 = 4 \cdot 7$ has a unique (hence normal)
Sylow $7$-subgroup $C_7$. Then the subgroup lies in $N_G(C_7) = B$ (order $21$),
 but $28 > 21$, contradiction.

- **Order $14$:** Similarly, a normal $C_7$ forces containment in $B$, but $14 \
nmid 21$.

- **Order $8$ ($D_8$):** The Sylow $2$-subgroups of $PSL(2,7)$ are $D_8$ (order
$8$), with $n_2 = 21$ (self-normalizing, since $q = 7 \equiv 7 \pmod{8}$). Each
$S_4$ contains $n_2(S_4) = 3$ Sylow $2$-subgroups. With $7$ point stabilizers: $
7 \times 3 = 21$, so **every $D_8$ is contained in some $S_4$**. Hence $D_8$ is
not maximal.

- **Order $12$ ($A_4$):** $A_4$ has a normal $V_4$; $V_4$ is contained in a Sylo
w $2$-subgroup $D_8 \leq S_4$, and $A_4 = V_4 \rtimes C_3 \leq S_4$. So $A_4$ is
 not maximal.

- **Orders $\leq 7$:** $C_7 \leq B$; $S_3 \leq B$ (normalizer of split torus); $
C_3, C_2, V_4, C_4$ are all contained in $B$ or $S_4$.

---

## 4. Summary

The **maximal subgroups of $PSL(2,7)$** (order $168$) are:

1. **Borel subgroups** $B \cong C_7 \rtimes C_3 \cong F_{21}$, order $21$:
   - Stabilizers of points in the action on $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_7)$ ($8$ po
ints).
   - **$8$ conjugate subgroups** (one conjugacy class).
   - These are the upper/lower triangular matrices.

2. **$S_4$ subgroups**, order $24$:
   - **Point stabilizers** in the Fano plane action (stabilizers of $1$-dimensio
nal subspaces in $GL(3,2)$): $7$ subgroups, one conjugacy class.
   - **Line stabilizers** in the Fano plane action (stabilizers of $2$-dimension
al subspaces): $7$ subgroups, a second conjugacy class.
   - **$14$ subgroups total in two conjugacy classes**, fused by the outer autom
orphism of $PSL(2,7)$.

No other maximal subgroups exist. The three conjugacy classes account for all ma
ximal subgroups, as verified by eliminating every other possible subgroup order.
