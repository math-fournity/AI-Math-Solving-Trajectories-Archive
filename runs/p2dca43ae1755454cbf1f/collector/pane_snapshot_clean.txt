## Proof

Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n$ be i.i.d. $\mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)$, and $S_n = X_1 +
\cdots + X_n$.

**Step 1: Mean and variance.** For each $X_i$,
$$\mathbb{E}[X_i] = \tfrac{1}{2}, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(X_i) = \tfrac{1}{12}
.$$
By independence,
$$\mathbb{E}[S_n] = \tfrac{n}{2}, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(S_n) = \tfrac{n}{12}
.$$

**Step 2: Standardize.** We seek
$$P\!\left(S_n \le \tfrac{n}{2} + n^{3/4}\right) = P\!\left(S_n - \mathbb{E}[S_n
] \le n^{3/4}\right).$$
Define the standardized sum
$$Z_n = \frac{S_n - n/2}{\sqrt{n/12}}.$$
Then the desired probability equals
$$P\!\left(Z_n \le \frac{n^{3/4}}{\sqrt{n/12}}\right) = P\!\left(Z_n \le \sqrt{1
2}\, n^{1/4}\right).$$

**Step 3: Apply the Central Limit Theorem.** Since the $X_i$ are i.i.d. with fin
ite mean $\mu = 1/2$ and finite variance $\sigma^2 = 1/12$, the classical CLT gi
ves
$$Z_n \xrightarrow{d} Z \sim N(0,1).$$

Set $a_n = \sqrt{12}\, n^{1/4}$. Note that $a_n \to +\infty$ as $n \to \infty$.

**Step 4: Compute the limit.** For any fixed $M > 0$, since $a_n \to \infty$, th
ere exists $N_M$ such that $a_n \ge M$ for all $n \ge N_M$. Hence for all $n \ge
 N_M$,
$$P(Z_n \le a_n) \ge P(Z_n \le M).$$
Taking the limit inferior and using the CLT (which gives convergence at all cont
inuity points of $\Phi$, the standard normal CDF):
$$\liminf_{n\to\infty} P(Z_n \le a_n) \ge \liminf_{n\to\infty} P(Z_n \le M) = \P
hi(M).$$
Since $M > 0$ was arbitrary and $\Phi(M) \to 1$ as $M \to \infty$, we obtain
$$\liminf_{n\to\infty} P(Z_n \le a_n) \ge 1.$$
Since probabilities are bounded above by $1$,
$$\limsup_{n\to\infty} P(Z_n \le a_n) \le 1.$$
Therefore,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} P\!\left(S_n \le \tfrac{n}{2} + n^{3/4}\right) = 1.$$

**Intuition.** The fluctuation scale of $S_n$ around its mean is $O(\sqrt{n})$,
whereas the threshold $n^{3/4}$ exceeds $\sqrt{n}$ by a factor of $n^{1/4} \to \
infty$. The threshold therefore drifts infinitely far (in standard-deviation uni
ts) to the right of the mean, so the probability that $S_n$ stays below it tends
 to $1$.
