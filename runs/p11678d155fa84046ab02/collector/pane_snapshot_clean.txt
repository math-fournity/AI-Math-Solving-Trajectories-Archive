## Proof

**Claim.** Let $G$ be a Lie group, $H \leq G$ a Lie subgroup, and $g \in G$. The
n the conjugate
$$gHg^{-1} = \{ghg^{-1} : h \in H\}$$
is a Lie subgroup of $G$.

**Proof.**

Consider the inner automorphism (conjugation map)
$$\Phi_g : G \to G, \qquad \Phi_g(x) = gxg^{-1}.$$

**Step 1: $\Phi_g$ is a Lie group automorphism.**

Left and right multiplication are smooth maps on $G$ (part of the Lie group stru
cture), and $\Phi_g = L_g \circ R_{g^{-1}}$ is a composition of smooth maps, hen
ce smooth. Its inverse is $\Phi_{g^{-1}}$, also smooth. Thus $\Phi_g$ is a diffe
omorphism of $G$. Moreover,
$$\Phi_g(xy) = g(xy)g^{-1} = (gxg^{-1})(gyg^{-1}) = \Phi_g(x)\Phi_g(y),$$
so $\Phi_g$ is a group automorphism. Hence $\Phi_g$ is a Lie group automorphism.

**Step 2: $gHg^{-1} = \Phi_g(H)$ is a subgroup.**

For $gh_1g^{-1}, gh_2g^{-1} \in gHg^{-1}$,
$$(gh_1g^{-1})(gh_2g^{-1})^{-1} = gh_1g^{-1} \cdot gh_2^{-1}g^{-1} = g(h_1h_2^{-
1})g^{-1} \in gHg^{-1},$$
since $h_1h_2^{-1} \in H$. Also $geg^{-1} = e$. So $gHg^{-1}$ is a subgroup of $
G$.

**Step 3: $gHg^{-1}$ is an (immersed / embedded) Lie subgroup with the same Lie
algebra type as $H$.**

The restriction $\varphi := \Phi_g\big|_H : H \to G$ is a smooth homomorphism (s
mooth since $\Phi_g$ is smooth and $H$ carries the submanifold/immersion topolog
y making the inclusion $i: H \hookrightarrow G$ an immersion, and $\varphi = \Ph
i_g \circ i$).

The image of $\varphi$ is exactly $gHg^{-1}$. Since $\Phi_g$ is a diffeomorphism
 of $G$, its differential $d(\Phi_g)_e : \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$ is a Lie
 algebra isomorphism. The Lie algebra of $gHg^{-1}$ is
$$\operatorname{Lie}(gHg^{-1}) = d(\Phi_g)_e\big(\operatorname{Lie}(H)\big) = \o
peratorname{Ad}(g)\,\mathfrak{h},$$
which is a Lie subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g}$ (being the image of the Lie subalgeb
ra $\mathfrak{h}$ under the Lie algebra automorphism $\operatorname{Ad}(g)$).

Concretely, $\varphi: H \to gHg^{-1}$ is a smooth bijective homomorphism with in
verse $\Phi_{g^{-1}}\big|_{gHg^{-1}}$, which is also smooth. Thus $\varphi$ is a
 Lie group isomorphism between $H$ and $gHg^{-1}$ (where $gHg^{-1}$ is given the
 unique smooth structure making $\varphi$ a diffeomorphism). In particular:

- If $H$ is **embedded**, then $gHg^{-1} = \Phi_g(H)$ is embedded, since $\Phi_g
$ is a diffeomorphism and diffeomorphisms preserve embedded submanifolds.
- If $H$ is **immersed** (a general Lie subgroup), then $gHg^{-1}$ is immersed,
with the immersion structure transported from $H$ via $\varphi$.

In either case, $gHg^{-1}$ is a Lie subgroup of $G$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Conjugation by any element $g \in G$ maps Lie subgroups to Lie subgroups, preser
ving the type (embedded/immersed) and transporting the Lie algebra via $\operato
rname{Ad}(g)$. Therefore the conjugation of a Lie subgroup of a Lie group is aga
in a Lie subgroup. $\blacksquare$
