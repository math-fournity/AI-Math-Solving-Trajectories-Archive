## Proof

Let $x^* \in \mathbb{R}$ be a minimum point of $u$, so that $u(x^*) = y = \min u
$, and $u(x) \geq y$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

**Step 1: Lower bound $y_i \geq y$ for all $i$.**

For any $x \in \mathbb{R}$ and any $i \in \mathbb{N}$,

$$\tilde{u}_i(x) = \int_{\mathbb{R}} \varphi_{1/i}(t)\, u(x - t)\, dt.$$

Since $u(x-t) \geq y$ for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$ (as $y$ is the global minimum o
f $u$), and $\varphi_{1/i} \geq 0$ with $\int \varphi_{1/i} = 1$, we have

$$\tilde{u}_i(x) = \int_{\mathbb{R}} \varphi_{1/i}(t)\, u(x-t)\, dt \geq \int_{\
mathbb{R}} \varphi_{1/i}(t)\, y\, dt = y.$$

(If $u(x-t) = +\infty$ on a set of positive $\varphi_{1/i}$-measure, the integra
l is $+\infty \geq y$; otherwise the pointwise inequality $u(x-t) \geq y$ integr
ates directly.)

Taking the infimum over $x$:

$$y_i = \min_{\mathbb{R}} \tilde{u}_i \geq y \quad \text{for all } i.$$

**Step 2: Upper bound via pointwise convergence at the minimizer.**

By hypothesis, $\tilde{u}_i \to u$ pointwise on $\mathbb{R}$. Evaluating at $x^*
$:

$$\tilde{u}_i(x^*) \to u(x^*) = y.$$

Since $y_i = \min_{\mathbb{R}} \tilde{u}_i \leq \tilde{u}_i(x^*)$ for every $i$,

$$\limsup_{i \to \infty} y_i \leq \limsup_{i \to \infty} \tilde{u}_i(x^*) = y.$$

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

From Step 1, $y_i \geq y$ for all $i$, so $\liminf_{i\to\infty} y_i \geq y$.

From Step 2, $\limsup_{i\to\infty} y_i \leq y$.

Therefore

$$\lim_{i \to \infty} y_i = y.$$

The sequence of minima $y_i = \min_{\mathbb{R}} \tilde{u}_i$ converges to $y = \
min u$. $\blacksquare$
