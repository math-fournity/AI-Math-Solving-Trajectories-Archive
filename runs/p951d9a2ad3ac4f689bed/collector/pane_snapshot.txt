Looking at this problem, I need to determine which integers $k$ can be realized
as the degree of a smooth map $f: M \to T^n$, where $M$ is a compact oriented sm
ooth $n$-manifold with $H^1_{dR}(M) = 0$.

**Answer: $k = 0$ is the only such integer.**

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $M$ be a closed (compact, without boundary) oriented smooth $n$-m
anifold with $H^1_{dR}(M) = 0$, equivalently $H^1(M; \mathbb{R}) = 0$. Let $f: M
 \to T^n$ be a smooth map. We show $\deg(f) = 0$.

**Step 1: The induced map on $H^1$ vanishes.**

The first cohomology of the torus is $H^1(T^n; \mathbb{R}) \cong \mathbb{R}^n$,
generated by classes $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n$ (the classes of the coordinate
1-forms $dx_i$). The pullback $f^*: H^1(T^n; \mathbb{R}) \to H^1(M; \mathbb{R})
= 0$ is necessarily the zero map, so
$$f^*(\alpha_i) = 0 \quad \text{for all } i = 1, \dots, n.$$

**Step 2: The cohomology ring of $T^n$ and the induced map on $H^n$.**

The cohomology ring of $T^n$ is the exterior algebra:
$$H^*(T^n; \mathbb{R}) \cong \Lambda(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n),$$
where each $\alpha_i$ has degree $1$. In particular, $H^n(T^n; \mathbb{R})$ is o
ne-dimensional, spanned by the top class
$$\alpha_1 \wedge \alpha_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge \alpha_n.$$

Since $f^*$ is a ring homomorphism and $f^*(\alpha_i) = 0$ for every $i$:
$$f^*(\alpha_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \alpha_n) = f^*(\alpha_1) \wedge \cdots \wed
ge f^*(\alpha_n) = 0 \wedge \cdots \wedge 0 = 0.$$

Thus $f^*: H^n(T^n; \mathbb{R}) \to H^n(M; \mathbb{R})$ is the zero map.

**Step 3: Relating $f^*$ on top cohomology to the degree.**

For a smooth map $f: M \to N$ between closed oriented $n$-manifolds, the induced
 map on top de Rham cohomology is multiplication by the degree. Concretely, if $
[\omega_N]$ is a generator of $H^n(N; \mathbb{R})$ and $[\omega_M]$ is the corre
sponding generator of $H^n(M; \mathbb{R})$ (determined by the orientations), the
n:
$$f^*[\omega_N] = \deg(f) \cdot [\omega_M].$$

Equivalently, choosing a volume form $\omega$ on $T^n$ with $\int_{T^n} \omega =
 1$:
$$\int_M f^*\omega = \deg(f).$$

Since $f^*[\omega] = 0$ in $H^n_{dR}(M)$, the form $f^*\omega$ is exact. As $M$
is closed, Stokes' theorem gives:
$$\int_M f^*\omega = 0.$$

Therefore $\deg(f) = 0$.

**Step 4: $k = 0$ is achieved.**

A constant map $f: M \to T^n$ is smooth and has degree $0$. Hence $k = 0$ is rea
lizable.

**Conclusion.** Every smooth map $f: M \to T^n$ has degree $0$, and the constant
 map shows degree $0$ is attainable. The unique integer $k$ for which such a map
 exists is:

$$\boxed{k = 0}.$$
