## Proof

The group SL(2,ℤ) has the classical decomposition as an amalgamated product:
$$SL(2,\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \;*_{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}\; \
mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}.$$
In particular, SL(2,ℤ) is virtually free. The principal congruence subgroup $\Ga
mma(2) = \ker\bigl(SL(2,\mathbb{Z}) \to SL(2,\mathbb{F}_2)\bigr)$ has index 6 in

&1\end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\2&1\end{pmatrix}$ freely generate it).

$$G_N = \{M \in GL(2,\mathbb{Z}) : |M_{ij}| \le N \text{ for all } i,j\}.$$

**Step 3: Bass–Serre tree action.**

Because GL(2,ℤ) is virtually free, it acts on its Bass–Serre tree $T$ — a locall
y finite tree on which GL(2,ℤ) acts cocompactly with finite vertex stabilizers.
Every element of GL(2,ℤ) is either:
- **Elliptic** (fixes a vertex): these are exactly the torsion elements, of whic
h there are only finitely many in GL(2,ℤ), hence density 0.
- **Hyperbolic** (has an axis — a bi-infinite geodesic on which it acts by trans

**Step 4: Generic pairs have disjoint axes (ping-pong).**

*Key geometric fact:* If $g, h$ are hyperbolic isometries of a tree $T$ with **d
isjoint axes**, then $\langle g, h \rangle \cong F_2$.

*Proof of key fact:* Let $\alpha_g, \alpha_h$ be the disjoint axes, and let $[Q,
 P]$ be the unique shortest bridge between them ($Q \in \alpha_g$, $P \in \alpha
_h$). Removing the bridge decomposes $T$ into two half-trees. The element $g$ tr

oint, and $g, g^{-1}, h, h^{-1}$ map the appropriate sets into each other. By th

**Step 5: Disjoint axes occur with density 1.**

We show that the set of pairs $(A,B)$ with both $A, B$ hyperbolic and disjoint a
xes has density 1 in $GL(2,\mathbb{Z})^2$.

- **Both hyperbolic:** The elliptic elements form a finite set, so the probabili
ty that $A$ or $B$ is elliptic goes to 0 as $N \to \infty$.

- **Disjoint axes (conditional on both hyperbolic):** The Bass–Serre tree $T$ is

$$O\!\left(\frac{\ell_A \cdot \ell_B}{\text{exponential growth of } T}\right) \t
o 0,$$
since the tree grows exponentially while translation lengths grow at most polyno

The set of "good" pairs — both hyperbolic with disjoint axes — has density 1, an

$$p = \lim_{N \to \infty} \Pr[\langle A, B \rangle \cong F_2] = 1.$$
