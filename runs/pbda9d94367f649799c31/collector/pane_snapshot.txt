## Sufficiency: $a \leq 1$ is achievable

Try $f(x) = cx^2$ with $c > 0$. Then $f(x)^2 = c^2 x^4$ and $f(f(x)) = c(cx^2)^2
 = c^3 x^4$. Substituting:

$$3c^2 x^4 = 2c^3 x^4 + ax^4 \implies a = c^2(3 - 2c).$$

Let $h(c) = c^2(3-2c) = 3c^2 - 2c^3$ for $c > 0$. Then $h'(c) = 6c(1-c)$, so $h$
 increases on $(0,1)$ and decreases on $(1,\infty)$, with $h(1) = 1$ (global max
), $h(3/2) = 0$, and $h(c) \to -\infty$ as $c \to \infty$. The range of $h$ on $
(0,\infty)$ is $(-\infty, 1]$.

Thus for every $a \leq 1$, choosing $c > 0$ with $c^2(3-2c) = a$ gives a valid $
f(x) = cx^2: \mathbb{R}^+ \to \mathbb{R}^+$.

## Necessity: $a > 1$ is impossible

Suppose $a > 1$ and such an $f$ exists. Define $g(x) = f(x)/x^2 > 0$ for $x > 0$
.

**Step 1: Lower bound on $g$.** Since $f(f(x)) > 0$:
$$3f(x)^2 > ax^4 \implies g(x)^2 > \frac{a}{3} \implies g(x) > \sqrt{\tfrac{a}{3
}} \quad \forall\, x > 0.$$

**Step 2: Functional relation for $g$.** From $f(f(x)) = \frac{3f(x)^2 - ax^4}{2
}$:
$$g(f(x)) = \frac{f(f(x))}{f(x)^2} = \frac{3}{2} - \frac{a}{2g(x)^2}.$$

Define $T(u) = \frac{3}{2} - \frac{a}{2u^2}$. Then $g(f(x)) = T(g(x))$, i.e., al
ong any orbit $x_{n+1} = f(x_n)$, the values $u_n = g(x_n)$ satisfy $u_{n+1} = T
(u_n)$.

**Step 3: $T$ has no fixed point for $a > 1$.** Fixed points satisfy $T(u) = u$,
 i.e., $2u^3 - 3u^2 + a = 0$. Let $p(u) = 2u^3 - 3u^2$. On $(0,\infty)$, $p$ has
 minimum $p(1) = -1$. For $a > 1$, $p(u) \geq -1 > -a$ for all $u > 0$, so $p(u)
 = -a$ has no positive solution.

**Step 4: $T(u) < u$ for all $u > 0$.** We have $T(u) - u = \frac{-(2u^3 - 3u^2
+ a)}{2u^2} = \frac{-(p(u)+a)}{2u^2} < 0$ since $p(u) + a > 0$ for all $u > 0$ w
hen $a > 1$.

**Step 5: Contradiction.** Start with any $x_0 > 0$ and set $u_n = g(x_n)$ where
 $x_{n+1} = f(x_n)$. By Step 1, $u_n > \sqrt{a/3} > 0$ for all $n$. By Step 4, $
u_{n+1} = T(u_n) < u_n$, so $(u_n)$ is strictly decreasing and bounded below by
$\sqrt{a/3}$. Hence $u_n \to L \geq \sqrt{a/3} > 0$. By continuity of $T$, $L =
T(L)$, a fixed point — contradicting Step 3.

Therefore no such $f$ exists for $a > 1$.

The set of all real $a$ for which such a function exists is $\boxed{a \leq 1}$,
