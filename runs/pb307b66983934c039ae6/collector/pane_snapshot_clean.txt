Let $w = z - 1$, so $z = 1+w$ and $2-z = 1-w$. Then

$$f(z) = \bigl[(1+w)e^{1-w} + (1-w)e^{1+w}\bigr]\,e^{w^2 + w^{-2}}.$$

**Key observation: $f$ is an even function of $w$.**

Under $w \mapsto -w$:
- $(1+w)e^{1-w} \mapsto (1-w)e^{1+w}$ and $(1-w)e^{1+w} \mapsto (1+w)e^{1-w}$, s
o the bracketed sum $S(w) = (1+w)e^{1-w}+(1-w)e^{1+w}$ is invariant: $S(-w)=S(w)
$.
- The exponent $w^2 + w^{-2}$ is invariant: $(-w)^2 + (-w)^{-2} = w^2 + w^{-2}$.

Hence $f(-w) = f(w)$: $f$ is an even function of $w$ in a punctured neighborhood
 of $w=0$.

**Consequence for the Laurent expansion.** An even function of $w$ admits a Laur
ent expansion about $w=0$ containing only even powers of $w$:

$$f = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} a_n\, w^n, \qquad a_n = 0 \text{ for all odd } n
.$$

In particular the coefficient of $w^{-1}$ — which is exactly the residue of $f$
at $z=1$ — vanishes.

**Verification (optional sanity check via series).** Expanding the two factors:

$$S(w)/e = (1+w)e^{-w} + (1-w)e^{w}.$$
$$(1+w)e^{-w} = 1 - \tfrac{w^2}{2} + \tfrac{w^3}{3} - \tfrac{w^4}{8} + \cdots,$$
$$(1-w)e^{w} = 1 - \tfrac{w^2}{2} - \tfrac{w^3}{3} - \tfrac{w^4}{8} + \cdots,$$

so $S(w)/e = 2 - w^2 - \tfrac{w^4}{4} - \cdots$ (only even powers, the odd terms
 cancel — confirming evenness). Likewise $e^{w^2} = 1 + w^2 + \tfrac{w^4}{2} + \
cdots$ and $e^{w^{-2}} = 1 + w^{-2} + \tfrac{w^{-4}}{2} + \tfrac{w^{-6}}{6} + \c
dots$ each contain only even powers. The product of three even Laurent series is
 again an even Laurent series, so no $w^{-1}$ term appears.

Therefore

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=1} f(z) = 0.$$
