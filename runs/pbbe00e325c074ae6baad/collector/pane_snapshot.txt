## Solution

**Factor the modulus.** $2013 = 3 \times 11 \times 61$. By CRT, the count of sol
utions mod 2013 equals the product of counts mod 3, mod 11, and mod 61.

**Factor the polynomial.** I seek a factorization $(n^2+an+b)(n^2+cn+d)$ with $a
+c=2$, $b+d+ac=-20$, $ad+bc=2$, $bd=-21$. Taking $b=-21, d=1$ gives $a+c=2$, $ac
=0$, $ad+bc=2$. Choosing $a=2, c=0$: $ad+bc = 2\cdot1 + (-21)\cdot0 = 2$ ✓. Ther
efore:

$$P(n) = (n^2+2n-21)(n^2+1) = \bigl((n+1)^2-22\bigr)(n^2+1).$$

**Mod 3.** $22\equiv 1\pmod 3$. Testing $n=0,1,2$:

| $n$ | $n^2+1$ | $(n+1)^2-22$ | $P(n)$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 | $1-1=0$ | 0 ✓ |
| 1 | 2 | $4-1\equiv 0$ | 0 ✓ |
| 2 | 2 | $0-1\equiv 2$ | $4\equiv 1$ ✗ |

**2 solutions** mod 3.

**Mod 11.** $22\equiv 0\pmod{11}$, so $P(n)\equiv (n^2+1)(n+1)^2\pmod{11}$. Sinc
e $11\equiv 3\pmod 4$, $-1$ is a non-residue mod 11 (squares mod 11 are $\{0,1,3
,4,5,9\}$), so $n^2+1\equiv 0$ has no solution. The only way is $(n+1)^2\equiv 0
$, i.e. $n\equiv 10\pmod{11}$.

**1 solution** mod 11.

**Mod 61.** Since 61 is prime, $P(n)\equiv 0$ iff $n^2\equiv -1$ **or** $(n+1)^2
\equiv 22\pmod{61}$.

- $61\equiv 1\pmod 4$, so $-1$ is a QR: **2 solutions** ($n\equiv 11, 50$, since
 $11^2=121\equiv -1$).
- Is 22 a QR mod 61? Legendre: $(22/61)=(2/61)(11/61)$. Since $61\equiv 5\pmod 8
$, $(2/61)=-1$. By QR, $(11/61)=(61/11)=(6/11)=(2/11)(3/11)=(-1)(1)=-1$. So $(22
/61)=(-1)(-1)=1$: **22 is a QR**, giving **2 solutions** ($n+1\equiv \pm 12$, i.
e. $n\equiv 11, 48$, since $12^2=144\equiv 22$).

**Overlap:** $n$ satisfying both means $(n+1)^2 = n^2+2n+1 \equiv -1+2n+1 = 2n \
equiv 22$, so $n\equiv 11\pmod{61}$. Indeed $n=11$ lies in both sets. So by incl
usion–exclusion:

$$|A\cup B| = 2 + 2 - 1 = \mathbf{3}\text{ solutions mod 61}.$$

**Combine via CRT:**

$$\text{Total} = 2 \times 1 \times 3 = \boxed{6}.$$
