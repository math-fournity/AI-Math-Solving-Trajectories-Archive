## Proof

Let $a_1=x, a_2=y, a_3=z, a_4=w$, with $a_i > 0$ and $\sum a_i = 1$. Set $Q = \s
um_{i=1}^4 a_i^2$.

**Rewriting the expression.** Observe that the denominator of term $i$ is $(1-a_
i)^3$ (since the sum of the other three variables is $1-a_i$), and the numerator
 is $\sum_{j \neq i} a_j^2 + a_i = Q - a_i^2 + a_i$. Thus the expression equals
$$S = \sum_{i=1}^{4} \frac{Q - a_i^2 + a_i}{(1-a_i)^3} = \sum_{i=1}^{4} g(a_i),
\quad \text{where } g(t) = \frac{Q + t - t^2}{(1-t)^3},$$
and $Q = \sum a_i^2$ is the same constant in every term.

**Convexity of $g$.** Compute:
$$g'(t) = \frac{1+3Q-t^2}{(1-t)^4}, \qquad g''(t) = \frac{2(2+6Q-t-t^2)}{(1-t)^5
}.$$
For $t \in (0,1)$: the denominator $(1-t)^5 > 0$, and the numerator satisfies $2
 + 6Q - t - t^2 > 2 - 1 - 1 + 6Q = 6Q > 0$ (since $t + t^2 < 2$ for $t<1$ and $Q
>0$). Hence **$g$ is strictly convex** on $(0,1)$ for every $Q > 0$.

**Applying Jensen's inequality.** Since $g$ is convex and all $a_i \in (0,1)$ wi
th $\sum a_i = 1$:
$$S = \sum_{i=1}^4 g(a_i) \geq 4\, g\!\left(\frac{\sum a_i}{4}\right) = 4\, g\!\
left(\tfrac{1}{4}\right).$$
Computing:
$$g\!\left(\tfrac{1}{4}\right) = \frac{Q + \tfrac{1}{4} - \tfrac{1}{16}}{\left(\
tfrac{3}{4}\right)^3} = \frac{Q + \tfrac{3}{16}}{\tfrac{27}{64}} = \frac{64Q+12}
{27}.$$
Therefore:
$$S \geq 4 \cdot \frac{64Q+12}{27} = \frac{256Q + 48}{27}.$$

**Bounding $Q$ from below.** By the Cauchy–Schwarz (QM–AM) inequality:
$$Q = \sum_{i=1}^4 a_i^2 \geq \frac{\left(\sum a_i\right)^2}{4} = \frac{1}{4}.$$

**Combining.** Since $\frac{256Q+48}{27}$ is increasing in $Q$:
$$S \geq \frac{256 \cdot \frac{1}{4} + 48}{27} = \frac{64 + 48}{27} = \frac{112}
{27}.$$

**Equality.** Equality in Jensen requires all $a_i$ equal, and equality in QM–AM
 also requires all $a_i$ equal. Both hold simultaneously when $a_1 = a_2 = a_3 =
 a_4 = \frac{1}{4}$, i.e., $x = y = z = w = \frac{1}{4}$, giving $Q = \frac{1}{4
}$ and $S = \frac{112}{27}$.

$$\boxed{\dfrac{112}{27}}$$
