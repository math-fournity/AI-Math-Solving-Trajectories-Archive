Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $Y$ must be unirational giv
en that $X$ is rational and $X \times Y$ is unirational.

**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

Recall the field-theoretic characterization: a variety $V$ over a field $k$ is *
*unirational** if and only if its function field $k(V)$ embeds (as a $k$-algebra
) into a purely transcendental extension $k(t_1, \dots, t_N)$ of $k$.

We are given that $X \times Y$ is unirational. Therefore there exists a $k$-alge
bra embedding
$$k(X \times Y) \hookrightarrow k(t_1, \dots, t_N)$$
for some $N$.

Now consider the projection morphism
$$\pi_Y : X \times Y \longrightarrow Y.$$
Since $X$ is a non-empty variety (being rational), $\pi_Y$ is a **dominant** mor
phism. A dominant morphism of irreducible varieties induces an **injective** $k$
-algebra homomorphism on function fields:
$$k(Y) \hookrightarrow k(X \times Y).$$

Composing these two embeddings:
$$k(Y) \hookrightarrow k(X \times Y) \hookrightarrow k(t_1, \dots, t_N).$$

Thus $k(Y)$ is a subfield of the purely transcendental extension $k(t_1, \dots,
t_N)$. By the field-theoretic characterization of unirationality, **$Y$ is unira
tional**. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The hypothesis that $X$ is rational is actually unnecessary — the ar
gument uses only that $X$ is non-empty (so that the projection $\pi_Y$ is domina
nt). The conclusion holds for any non-empty variety $X$ such that $X \times Y$ i
s unirational.
