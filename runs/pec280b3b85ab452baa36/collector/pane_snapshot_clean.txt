## Solution

**The surface $S$ is a torus.** The curve $(x-2)^2 + z^2 = 1$ is a circle of rad
ius $1$ in the $xz$-plane, centered at $(2,0)$. Rotating it about the $z$-axis p
roduces a torus with major radius $R=2$ and minor radius $r=1$. This is a closed
 surface bounding a solid region $V$ (the interior of the torus).

**Apply the Divergence Theorem.** With $\mathbf{n}$ the outward unit normal:

$$\int_S \mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dA = \int_V \nabla\cdot\mathbf{F}\,dV.$$

**Compute the divergence.**

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\bigl(x+\sin(yz)\bigr) + \f
rac{\partial}{\partial y}\bigl(y+e^{x+z}\bigr) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\big
l(z-x^2\cos y\bigr) = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3.$$

**Compute the volume of the torus** via Pappus's theorem. The cross-section is a
 disk of radius $1$ (area $\pi\cdot 1^2 = \pi$), whose centroid is at distance $
R=2$ from the $z$-axis. The centroid travels a circle of circumference $2\pi\cdo
t 2 = 4\pi$, so

$$\operatorname{Vol}(V) = \pi \cdot 4\pi = 4\pi^2.$$

(Equivalently, the standard torus volume formula $2\pi^2 R r^2 = 2\pi^2\cdot 2\c
dot 1 = 4\pi^2$.)

**Combine.**

$$\int_S \mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dA = 3\cdot\operatorname{Vol}(V) = 3\cdot 4\
pi^2 = \boxed{12\pi^2}.$$
