series-based) expression for $\Gamma'(1)$.

### Step 1. The Weierstrass product

The Weierstrass product representation of the Gamma function is

$$
\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)} = z\,e^{\gamma z}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{z}{n}\rig
ht)e^{-z/n},
$$

where $\gamma$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant. Taking logarithms (valid for $z
\notin\{0,-1,-2,\dots\}$, using the principal branch; we only need $z$ near $1$)

$$
-\ln\Gamma(z)=\ln z+\gamma z+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\ln\!\left(1+\frac{z}{n}\r
ight)-\frac{z}{n}\right].
\tag{1}
$$

The series converges absolutely and locally uniformly on compact subsets of $\ma
thbb{C}\setminus\{0,-1,-2,\dots\}$, so we may differentiate term-by-term.

### Step 2. Differentiate to get the digamma function

Differentiating (1) with respect to $z$:

$$
-\frac{\Gamma'(z)}{\Gamma(z)}=\frac{1}{z}+\gamma+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\frac{
1}{n+z}-\frac{1}{n}\right].
$$

Rearranging (noting $\psi(z):=\Gamma'(z)/\Gamma(z)$ is the digamma function):

$$
\boxed{\;\psi(z)=-\frac{1}{z}-\gamma+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{
1}{n+z}\right)\;}. \tag{2}
$$

Equation (2) is the **Weierstrass form of the digamma function** — itself the de
sired "alternative expression" for the logarithmic derivative of $\Gamma$.

Since $\Gamma(1)=1$, we have $\Gamma'(1)=\psi(1)\cdot\Gamma(1)=\psi(1)$. Setting

$$
\Gamma'(1)=\psi(1)=-1-\gamma+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{n+1}\

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{n+1}\right)=1-\frac{1}{N+1}\xrightarrow
