**Step 1: Diagonalize $A$.**

The matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix}1&-1&0\\-1&2&-1\\0&-1&1\end{pmatrix}$ is real sym
metric, hence diagonalizable over $\mathbb{R}$ with real eigenvalues.

The characteristic polynomial is:
$$\det(A - \lambda I) = (1-\lambda)\bigl[(2-\lambda)(1-\lambda)-1\bigr] + \bigl[
-(1-\lambda)\bigr] = (1-\lambda)\bigl[(2-\lambda)(1-\lambda)-2\bigr].$$

Expanding $(2-\lambda)(1-\lambda) - 2 = \lambda^2 - 3\lambda = \lambda(\lambda-3
)$, so
$$\det(A-\lambda I) = (1-\lambda)\,\lambda\,(\lambda-3).$$

The eigenvalues are $\lambda_1 = 0,\; \lambda_2 = 1,\; \lambda_3 = 3$.

Thus $A = P\,D\,P^{-1}$ with $D = \operatorname{diag}(0,1,3)$ for some invertibl
e $P$.

**Step 2: Express $a^n A^n$.**

For $n \ge 1$:
$$a^n A^n = P\,\operatorname{diag}\!\bigl(a^n\cdot 0^n,\; a^n\cdot 1^n,\; a^n\cd
ot 3^n\bigr)\,P^{-1} = P\,\operatorname{diag}\!\bigl(0,\; a^n,\; (3a)^n\bigr)\,P
^{-1}.$$

Since $P$ and $P^{-1}$ are fixed invertible matrices, the limit $\lim_{n\to\inft
y} a^n A^n$ exists **if and only if** each diagonal entry $0$, $a^n$, $(3a)^n$ h
as a finite limit; and the limit matrix is **non-zero** if and only if at least
one limiting diagonal entry is non-zero.

**Step 3: Analyze when $a^n$ and $(3a)^n$ converge.**

For a real number $b$, the sequence $b^n$ converges iff:
- $|b|<1$: limit $0$;
- $b=1$: limit $1$;
- $b=-1$ or $|b|>1$: diverges (oscillation or unbounded growth).

Applying this:

| Condition on $a$ | $\lim a^n$ | $\lim(3a)^n$ | Limit exists? | Non-zero? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\|a\|<\tfrac13$ | $0$ | $0$ | Yes | No (zero matrix) |
| $a=\tfrac13$ | $0$ | $1$ | Yes | **Yes** |
| $a=-\tfrac13$ | $0$ | oscillates | No | — |
| $\tfrac13<\|a\|<1$ | $0$ | diverges | No | — |
| $a=1$ | $1$ | diverges | No | — |
| $a=-1$ | oscillates | diverges | No | — |
| $\|a\|>1$ | diverges | diverges | No | — |

**Step 4: Verify $a = \tfrac{1}{3}$ gives a non-zero limit.**

With $a = \frac{1}{3}$:
$$a^n A^n = P\,\operatorname{diag}\!\bigl(0,\; (1/3)^n,\; 1\bigr)\,P^{-1} \;\lon
grightarrow\; P\,\operatorname{diag}(0,0,1)\,P^{-1}.$$

This is the projection onto the eigenspace of $\lambda = 3$. The eigenvector for
 $\lambda=3$ satisfies $(A-3I)v=0$:
$$\begin{pmatrix}-2&-1&0\\-1&-1&-1\\0&-1&-2\end{pmatrix}v=0 \implies v_2=-2v_1,\
; v_3=v_1,$$
giving the non-zero eigenvector $(1,-2,1)^T$. Hence $P\,\operatorname{diag}(0,0,
1)\,P^{-1} \neq 0$.

**Conclusion.**

The unique real number $a$ for which $\lim_{n\to\infty} a^n A^n$ exists and is n
on-zero is

$$\boxed{a = \dfrac{1}{3}}.$$
