## Generalized Cartan Formula for Non-Commuting Vector Fields

### Setup

Let $S$ be a 2-surface, $\varpi$ a smooth 1-form on $S$, and $d_1, d_2$ vector f
ields with Lie bracket $[d_1, d_2]$ not necessarily zero. We seek the invariant
formula for $d\varpi(d_1, d_2)$.

### The Commutative (Naïve) Formula

When $[d_1, d_2] = 0$, one is tempted to write the exterior derivative as the an
tisymmetrized directional derivative:

$$d\varpi(d_1, d_2) \stackrel{?}{=} d_1(\varpi(d_2)) - d_2(\varpi(d_1)).$$

This is **not correct in general** and fails precisely when the bracket is nonze
ro. The correct generalization restores a correction term.

### Derivation via Local Coordinates

Write $\varpi = \varpi_i\, dx^i$, $d_1 = d_1^j \partial_j$, $d_2 = d_2^k \partia
l_k$. By definition of the exterior derivative,

$$d\varpi = \partial_j \varpi_i\, dx^j \wedge dx^i,$$

so evaluating on $(d_1, d_2)$:

$$d\varpi(d_1, d_2) = (\partial_j \varpi_i)\bigl(d_1^j d_2^i - d_1^i d_2^j\bigr)
 = d_1^j(\partial_j \varpi_i)\,d_2^i - d_2^j(\partial_j \varpi_i)\,d_1^i. \tag{1
}$$

Now expand the directional derivatives of the component functions $\varpi(d_2) =
 \varpi_i d_2^i$ and $\varpi(d_1) = \varpi_i d_1^i$:

$$d_1(\varpi(d_2)) = d_1^j(\partial_j \varpi_i)\,d_2^i + \varpi_i\, d_1^j(\parti
al_j d_2^i),$$

$$d_2(\varpi(d_1)) = d_2^j(\partial_j \varpi_i)\,d_1^i + \varpi_i\, d_2^j(\parti
al_j d_1^i).$$

Solving each for the first term and substituting into (1):

$$d\varpi(d_1, d_2) = \Bigl[d_1(\varpi(d_2)) - \varpi_i\, d_1^j\partial_j d_2^i\
Bigr] - \Bigl[d_2(\varpi(d_1)) - \varpi_i\, d_2^j\partial_j d_1^i\Bigr].$$

Recognizing the coordinate expression of the Lie bracket,

$$[d_1, d_2]^i = d_1^j\partial_j d_2^i - d_2^j\partial_j d_1^i,$$

we obtain the **generalized Cartan formula**:

$$\boxed{\,d\varpi(d_1, d_2) = d_1\bigl(\varpi(d_2)\bigr) - d_2\bigl(\varpi(d_1)
\bigr) - \varpi\bigl([d_1, d_2]\bigr).\,}$$

### The Additional Term

Compared with the naïve commutative expression, the **additional term** is

$$-\,\varpi([d_1, d_2]).$$

### Significance of the Additional Term

**1. Tensoriality ( $C^\infty$-linearity ).** The terms $d_1(\varpi(d_2))$ and $
d_2(\varpi(d_1))$ are *not* tensorial: for a smooth function $f$,

$$d_1(\varpi(d_2))\big|_{d_1 \to fd_1} = f\, d_1(\varpi(d_2)) + (d_1 f)\,\varpi(
d_2),$$

producing an extra summand $(d_1 f)\,\varpi(d_2)$. The bracket transforms as $[f
d_1, d_2] = f[d_1, d_2] - (d_2 f)\,d_1$, so

$$-\varpi([fd_1, d_2]) = -f\,\varpi([d_1, d_2]) + (d_2 f)\,\varpi(d_1).$$

Combining with the parallel computation for $d_2(\varpi(fd_1)) = f\,d_2(\varpi(d
_1)) + (d_2 f)\,\varpi(d_1)$, the $(d_2 f)\,\varpi(d_1)$ terms cancel and the $(
d_1 f)\,\varpi(d_2)$ terms cancel, leaving

$$d\varpi(fd_1, d_2) = f\, d\varpi(d_1, d_2).$$

Thus the bracket term is **exactly the correction needed** to make $d\varpi$ a g
enuine tensor (2-form), i.e., $C^\infty$-linear in each slot. Without it, the fo
rmula would depend on the extensions of $d_1, d_2$ and would not define a pointw
ise object.

**2. Geometric meaning — failure of the flows to close.** The flows $\Phi^1_t, \
Phi^2_s$ of $d_1, d_2$ form a closed infinitesimal parallelogram iff $[d_1, d_2]
 = 0$. When the bracket is nonzero, traversing "$d_1$ then $d_2$ then $-d_1$ the
n $-d_2$" fails to return to the starting point; the residual displacement is go
verned by $[d_1, d_2]$. The term $-\varpi([d_1, d_2])$ measures the contribution
 of this **closure defect** to the circulation of $\varpi$ around the infinitesi
mal loop — it is the holonomy of the non-commuting frame.

**3. Connection to integrability (Frobenius).** The vanishing of the bracket is
precisely the Frobenius integrability condition for the distribution $\mathrm{sp
an}\{d_1, d_2\}$. The additional term therefore encodes the **obstruction to int
egrability**: when it vanishes for all 1-forms $\varpi$ annihilating the distrib
ution, the distribution is integrable and the commutative formula suffices.

**4. Coordinate independence.** The bracket term converts the coordinate-depende
nt expression (1) into an invariant formula valid in any frame, commuting or not
. It is the unique correction consistent with $d^2 = 0$ and Leibniz.

### Summary

The generalized Cartan formula for a 1-form evaluated on possibly non-commuting
vector fields is

$$d\varpi(d_1, d_2) = d_1(\varpi(d_2)) - d_2(\varpi(d_1)) - \varpi([d_1, d_2]).$
$

The additional term $-\varpi([d_1, d_2])$ compensates for the non-commutativity
of the frame: it restores tensoriality, measures the closure defect of the flows
 of $d_1$ and $d_2$, and encodes the obstruction to Frobenius integrability of t
he distribution they span.
