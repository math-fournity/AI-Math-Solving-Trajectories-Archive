Looking at this problem, I need to find the largest constant $C$ such that in an
y valid frequency assignment (where every pair of engineers shares at least one
frequency), some frequency is used by at least $Cn$ engineers.

**Answer: $C = \dfrac{3}{5}$.**

---

## Setup

Each engineer is assigned a non-empty subset of $[4] = \{1,2,3,4\}$ (the frequen
cies they can access). The pairwise communication rule means the collection of a
ssigned subsets is **pairwise intersecting**: any two subsets share at least one
 element.

Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the set of subset-types used, and let $p_A$ be the fraction
 of engineers assigned type $A \in \mathcal{F}$, with $\sum_{A \in \mathcal{F}}
p_A = 1$. The load on frequency $i$ is $L_i = \sum_{A \ni i} p_A$. We seek:

$$C = \min_{\mathcal{F},\, p} \;\max_{i \in [4]} L_i.$$

By the minimax theorem (LP duality), for a fixed intersecting family $\mathcal{F
}$:

$$\min_{p} \max_{i} \sum_{A \ni i} p_A \;=\; \max_{q \in \Delta_4} \min_{A \in \
mathcal{F}} \sum_{i \in A} q_i,$$

where $\Delta_4$ is the probability simplex. So $C = \min_{\mathcal{F}} V(\mathc
al{F})$ where $V(\mathcal{F}) = \max_{q \in \Delta_4} \min_{A \in \mathcal{F}} \
sum_{i \in A} q_i$.

---

## Upper Bound: Construction Achieving $3/5$

Use the family $\mathcal{F} = \big\{\{1,2\},\, \{1,3\},\, \{1,4\},\, \{2,3,4\}\b
ig\}$ with distribution:
- $p_{\{1,2\}} = p_{\{1,3\}} = p_{\{1,4\}} = \tfrac{1}{5}$, $\;p_{\{2,3,4\}} = \
tfrac{2}{5}$.

**Pairwise intersection check:** $\{1,j\} \cap \{1,k\} = \{1\}$ for $j \neq k$;
$\{1,j\} \cap \{2,3,4\} = \{j\}$. All pairs intersect. ✓

**Frequency loads:**
| Frequency | Load |
|-----------|------|
| 1 | $\frac{1}{5}+\frac{1}{5}+\frac{1}{5} = \frac{3}{5}$ |
| 2 | $\frac{1}{5}+\frac{2}{5} = \frac{3}{5}$ |
| 3 | $\frac{1}{5}+\frac{2}{5} = \frac{3}{5}$ |
| 4 | $\frac{1}{5}+\frac{2}{5} = \frac{3}{5}$ |

All loads equal $\frac{3}{5}$ — perfectly balanced. (For $n$ divisible by 5, ass
ign $n/5$ engineers to each 2-element type and $2n/5$ to $\{2,3,4\}$.) This show
s $C \leq \frac{3}{5}$.

---

## Lower Bound: $V(\mathcal{F}) \geq 3/5$ for Every Intersecting Family

We show that for **any** intersecting family $\mathcal{F}$ of non-empty subsets
of $[4]$, there exists $q \in \Delta_4$ with $\sum_{i \in A} q_i \geq \frac{3}{5
}$ for all $A \in \mathcal{F}$.

**If $\mathcal{F}$ contains a singleton $\{i\}$:** Every set in $\mathcal{F}$ mu
st contain $i$. Take $q_i = 1$; then every set has $q$-sum $= 1 \geq \frac{3}{5}
$. ✓

**If all sets have size $\geq 2$:** Let $\mathcal{F}_2$ be the 2-element sets in
 $\mathcal{F}$. Since two 2-subsets of $[4]$ are disjoint iff they are complemen
tary, and $\mathcal{F}_2$ is pairwise intersecting, $|\mathcal{F}_2| \leq 3$ (at
 most one from each complementary pair $\{12,34\}, \{13,24\}, \{14,23\}$).

**Case 1: $|\mathcal{F}_2| \leq 1$.** If $|\mathcal{F}_2|=0$, take $q=(\frac14,\
frac14,\frac14,\frac14)$: every 3-set has sum $\frac34 \geq \frac35$. If $\mathc
al{F}_2 = \{\{1,2\}\}$, every set contains 1 or 2. Take $q=(\frac13,\frac13,\fra
c16,\frac16)$: $q_1+q_2 = \frac23 \geq \frac35$; any 3-set containing 1 or 2 has
 sum $\geq \frac13+\frac16+\frac16 = \frac23 \geq \frac35$. ✓

**Case 2: $|\mathcal{F}_2| = 2$.** The two 2-sets must share an element; WLOG $\
{1,2\}, \{1,3\}$. Every set contains 1, or contains both 2 and 3. Take $q=(\frac
13,\frac13,\frac13,0)$: 2-sets have sum $\frac23$; any 3-set has sum $\geq \frac
23 \geq \frac35$. ✓

**Case 3: $|\mathcal{F}_2| = 3$, forming a star** $\{1,2\}, \{1,3\}, \{1,4\}$. A
 set disjoint from all three must avoid $1$ and contain $2$ (to meet $\{1,2\}$),
 $3$ (to meet $\{1,3\}$), $4$ (to meet $\{1,4\}$) — so it is $\supseteq \{2,3,4\
}$. Thus every set contains 1, or is $\{2,3,4\}$ or $\{1,2,3,4\}$. Take $q = (\f
rac25, \frac15, \frac15, \frac15)$:
- 2-sets $\{1,j\}$: $\frac25 + \frac15 = \frac35$ ✓
- 3-sets containing 1: $\geq \frac25 + \frac15 = \frac35$ ✓
- $\{2,3,4\}$: $\frac15+\frac15+\frac15 = \frac35$ ✓
- $\{1,2,3,4\}$: $1$ ✓

So $V(\mathcal{F}) \geq \frac35$. ✓

**Case 4: $|\mathcal{F}_2| = 3$, forming a triangle** $\{1,2\}, \{1,3\}, \{2,3\}
$. Take $q=(\frac13,\frac13,\frac13,0)$: 2-sets have sum $\frac23$; any 3-set ha
s sum $\geq \frac23 \geq \frac35$. ✓

All cases give $V(\mathcal{F}) \geq \frac35$, with equality in Case 3 (the star)
.

## Conclusion

By the minimax theorem, for any valid assignment with $n$ engineers, the empiric
al distribution $p_A = n_A/n$ satisfies:

$$\max_{i \in [4]} \frac{|S_i|}{n} = \max_i \sum_{A \ni i} p_A \geq V(\mathcal{F
}) \geq \frac{3}{5},$$

so some frequency is used by at least $\frac{3}{5}n$ engineers. The construction
 above shows this bound is tight. Therefore:

$$\boxed{C = \dfrac{3}{5}}$$
