## Proof

Let $K$ be a number field of signature $(r_1,r_2)$, so it has $r_1$ real embeddi
ngs and $r_2$ pairs of complex conjugate embeddings, with $:\mathbb{Q}]=r_1+2r
_2$. Let $T$ be a finite set of finite places (non-archimedean primes) of $K$, w
ith $|T|=t$.

### Definition of $T$-units

The $T$-unit group is
$$
U_T(K)=\{x\in K^\times : v_{\mathfrak p}(x)=0\ \text{for every finite prime }\ma
thfrak p\notin T\},
$$
i.e. the elements of $K^\times$ whose valuations vanish at all finite primes out
side $T$. Equivalently, these are the $S$-units for $S=T\cup\{\text{archimedean
places}\}$.

### The exact sequence

Let $\mathcal A=\bigoplus_{\mathfrak p}\mathbb Z\,\mathfrak p$ be the group of f
ractional ideals of $K$ (free abelian on all finite primes), and let
$$
\mathcal A_T=\bigoplus_{\mathfrak p\in T}\mathbb Z\,\mathfrak p\cong\mathbb Z^t
$$
be the subgroup of fractional ideals supported on $T$. Let $\mathrm{Cl}(K)$ be t
he ideal class group and $\pi:\mathcal A\to\mathrm{Cl}(K)$ the class map.

There is a commutative diagram with exact rows. The principal-ideal map $(\cdot)
:K^\times\to\mathcal A$ sends $x\mapsto(x)=\prod_{\mathfrak p}\mathfrak p^{v_{\m
athfrak p}(x)}$. For $x\in U_T(K)$ the product runs only over $\mathfrak p\in T$
, so $(x)\in\mathcal A_T$. This gives a homomorphism
$$
\varphi:U_T(K)\longrightarrow\mathcal A_T,\qquad x\longmapsto (x)=\prod_{\mathfr
ak p\in T}\mathfrak p^{v_{\mathfrak p}(x)}.
$$

Its kernel is $\{x\in K^\times:(x)=\mathcal O_K\}=\{x:v_{\mathfrak p}(x)=0\ \for
all\mathfrak p\}=U(K)$, the ordinary unit group. Its image is the set of $T$-sup
ported principal ideals, which is exactly $\ker(\mathcal A_T\xrightarrow{\pi|_T}
\mathrm{Cl}(K))$. Hence we obtain the **fundamental exact sequence**
$$
\boxed{1\longrightarrow U(K)\longrightarrow U_T(K)\xrightarrow{\ \varphi\ }\math
cal A_T\xrightarrow{\ \pi|_T\ }\mathrm{Cl}(K).}
$$
The map $\pi|_T$ need not be surjective; its image is the subgroup of $\mathrm{C
l}(K)$ generated by the classes of the primes in $T$.

### Rank

By Dirichlet's unit theorem, $U(K)\cong\mu(K)\times\mathbb Z^{r_1+r_2-1}$, where
 $\mu(K)$ is the (finite) group of roots of unity in $K$. The class group $\math
rm{Cl}(K)$ is finite, so $\operatorname{im}(\pi|_T)$ is finite and $\ker(\pi|_T)
$ is a free abelian group of rank $t$. From the exact sequence,
$$
\operatorname{rank}U_T(K)=\operatorname{rank}U(K)+\operatorname{rank}\ker(\pi|_T
)=(r_1+r_2-1)+t.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}U_T(K)=r_1+r_2+t-1.}
$$
This is the $S$-unit theorem of Dirichlet–Chevalley: each additional allowed pri
me raises the rank by one.

### Torsion subgroup

The torsion of $U_T(K)$ consists of elements of finite order, i.e. roots of unit
y. Any root of unity has valuation $0$ at every finite prime, hence lies in $U(K
)\subset U_T(K)$; conversely every torsion element of $U_T(K)$ is a root of unit
y in $K$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{U_T(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}=\mu(K).}
$$

Combining,
$$
\boxed{U_T(K)\cong\mu(K)\times\mathbb Z^{\,r_1+r_2+t-1}.}
$$

### Method of computation

The exact sequence gives an effective procedure:

1. **Compute the ordinary unit group** $U(K)$ via Dirichlet's unit theorem: dete
rmine $\mu(K)$ and a system of fundamental units of rank $r_1+r_2-1$ (e.g. by Mi
nkowski/LLL methods).

2. **Compute the class group** $\mathrm{Cl}(K)$ and the classes $[\mathfrak p]\i
n\mathrm{Cl}(K)$ of the primes $\mathfrak p\in T$.

3. **Determine $\ker(\pi|_T)\subset\mathcal A_T\cong\mathbb Z^t$**: this is the
lattice of relations among the classes $[\mathfrak p]$ ($\mathfrak p\in T$) in $
\mathrm{Cl}(K)$, i.e. all $(n_{\mathfrak p})_{\mathfrak p\in T}\in\mathbb Z^t$ w
ith $\prod_{\mathfrak p\in T}[\mathfrak p]^{n_{\mathfrak p}}=1$ in $\mathrm{Cl}(
K)$. It is a free abelian subgroup of rank $t$ (since $\mathrm{Cl}(K)$ is finite
).

4. **Lift generators of $\ker(\pi|_T)$ to $T$-units**: for each basis vector $(n
_{\mathfrak p})$ of the lattice, the ideal $\prod_{\mathfrak p\in T}\mathfrak p^
{n_{\mathfrak p}}$ is principal; find a generator $\alpha\in K^\times$ (by solvi
ng the principal-ideal problem / computing a generator via class-group machinery
). Then $\alpha\in U_T(K)$ and $\varphi(\alpha)$ equals that vector. These $\alp
ha$'s, together with the fundamental units of $U(K)$, form a full system of gene
rators of $U_T(K)/\mu(K)\cong\mathbb Z^{r_1+r_2+t-1}$.

In short: $U_T(K)$ is obtained by adjoining to $U(K)$ one "extra" generator for
each independent relation among the classes $[\mathfrak p]$ ($\mathfrak p\in T$)
 — equivalently one generator per prime in $T$, modulo the single global relatio
n forced by the class group being finite — yielding rank $(r_1+r_2-1)+t=r_1+r_2+
t-1$ and torsion $\mu(K)$.
