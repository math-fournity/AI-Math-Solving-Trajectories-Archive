Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum number of distinct angular o
rderings of 10 vertices of a convex decagon, as seen from exterior points (with
no two vertices collinear with the observation point).

## Proof

**Setup.** Let the convex 10-gon have vertices $v_1, \dots, v_{10}$ labeled with
 distinct letters. A drone at exterior point $P$ records the angular (left-to-ri
ght) order of the vertices. Two vertices $v_i, v_j$ are collinear with $P$ iff $
P$ lies on line $\ell_{ij}$ through them. The drone records a valid sequence iff
 $P$ avoids all such lines.

**Key observation.** The angular order changes only when $P$ crosses some line $
\ell_{ij}$, at which point exactly $v_i$ and $v_j$ swap. So each connected regio
n of $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \bigcup \ell_{ij}$ yields a distinct permutation. W
e need the number of regions in the **exterior** of the polygon.

**The line arrangement.** There are $m = \binom{10}{2} = 45$ lines. Using the re
gion formula for a line arrangement:
$$R = 1 + m + \sum_{P}(t_P - 1)$$
where the sum runs over all intersection points $P$ with $t_P$ lines through the
m.

**Choosing general position.** We select the convex 10-gon so that no three of t
he 45 lines are concurrent except at the 10 vertices (where 9 lines meet each).
This is achievable since degenerate configurations form a measure-zero set.

**Counting intersection points:**

- **At each vertex:** 9 lines meet, contributing $9-1=8$. Over 10 vertices: $10
\times 8 = 80$.
- **Other intersections:** Total pairs of lines $= \binom{45}{2} = 990$. Pairs s
haring a vertex: $10 \times \binom{9}{2} = 360$ (each counted once, since two di
stinct lines share at most one vertex). Remaining pairs: $990 - 360 = 630$, each
 giving a distinct point with $t=2$, contributing $1$ each.

**Total regions:**
$$R = 1 + 45 + 80 + 630 = 756$$

**Interior regions.** Inside the convex polygon, only diagonal lines divide the
interior (side-lines are supporting lines — the polygon lies entirely on one sid
e, so they don't cut through the interior). The number of diagonals is $\binom{1
0}{2} - 10 = 35$. With no three diagonals concurrent inside, each 4-element subs
et of vertices determines exactly one interior crossing, giving $\binom{10}{4} =
 210$ crossings. By the standard formula:
$$R_{\text{int}} = 1 + 35 + 210 = 246$$

**Exterior regions:**
$$R_{\text{ext}} = 756 - 246 = 510$$

**Verification with small cases:**
- $n=3$ (triangle): $R_{\text{ext}} = 6 = 3!$ ✓ (all permutations achievable fro
m outside)
- $n=4$ (quadrilateral): $R_{\text{ext}} = 14$ ✓ (total regions 18, interior 4)

**Why each exterior region gives a distinct valid sequence:** Points in the same
 region share the same angular order. Crossing any line $\ell_{ij}$ swaps exactl
y $v_i, v_j$ in the order, so different regions always yield different permutati
ons. Points in the interior of a region avoid all lines, so no two vertices are
collinear — the recording is valid. Every valid recording comes from some exteri
or region.

**Maximality.** Any concurrency of three or more lines at a non-vertex point red
uces the region count below the general-position value. Since general position i
s achievable, 510 is the maximum.

$$\boxed{510}$$
