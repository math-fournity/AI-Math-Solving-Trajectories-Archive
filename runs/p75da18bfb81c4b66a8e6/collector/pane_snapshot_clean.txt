## Proof

We evaluate $\displaystyle \int_0^{\infty} x\, I_0(2x)\, e^{-x^2}\, dx$, where $
I_0(x) = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi} e^{x\cos\theta}\,d\theta$ is the modified Bes
sel function of the first kind of order zero (the upper limit $\pi$ is the stand
ard definition; the "$\infty$" in the statement is a typo, since $\int_0^\infty
e^{x\cos\theta}d\theta$ diverges for $x>0$).

**Series representation.** The modified Bessel function has the expansion
$$I_0(z) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(z/2)^{2k}}{(k!)^2}.$$

Substituting $z = 2x$:
$$I_0(2x) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^{2k}}{(k!)^2}.$$

**Interchange sum and integral.** All terms are non-negative on $[0,\infty)$, so
 by Tonelli's theorem we may exchange:
$$\int_0^{\infty} x\, e^{-x^2}\, I_0(2x)\, dx = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(k!
)^2} \int_0^{\infty} x^{2k+1}\, e^{-x^2}\, dx.$$

**Evaluate the moments.** With the substitution $t = x^2$ (so $dt = 2x\,dx$, $x^
{2k} = t^k$):
$$\int_0^{\infty} x^{2k+1}\, e^{-x^2}\, dx = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\infty} t^{k}\,
e^{-t}\, dt = \frac{1}{2}\,\Gamma(k+1) = \frac{k!}{2}.$$

**Sum the series.** Therefore
$$\int_0^{\infty} x\, e^{-x^2}\, I_0(2x)\, dx = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(k!
)^2}\cdot\frac{k!}{2} = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k!} = \frac{e}{2
}.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \int_0^{\infty} x\, I_0(2x)\, e^{-x^2}\, dx = \frac{e}{2}
.}$$
