$$f(x) = \underbrace{\sum_{i_{u-1}=1}^{x} \sum_{i_{u-2}=1}^{i_{u-1}} \cdots \sum

**Claim.** For all $m \geq 1$,

$$S_m(n) = \binom{n+m}{m+1}.$$

**Proof by induction on $m$.**

*Base case* ($m=1$): $S_1(n) = \binom{n+1}{2}$, verified above. ✓

*Inductive step:* Assume $S_{m-1}(n) = \binom{n+m-1}{m}$. Then

$$S_m(n) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} S_{m-1}(i) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \binom{i+m-1}{m}.$$

By the **hockey-stick identity** (a direct consequence of Pascal's rule $\binom{
k}{m} = \binom{k-1}{m-1} + \binom{k-1}{m}$, telescoped):

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} \binom{i+m-1}{m} = \binom{n+m}{m+1}.$$

This completes the induction. $\square$

**Applying the claim.** With $m = u-1$ and $n = x$:

$$f(x) = S_{u-1}(x) = \binom{x+u-1}{u} = \frac{(x+u-1)!}{u!\,(x-1)!} = \frac{1}{
u!}\prod_{k=0}^{u-1}(x+k).$$

$$\prod_{k=0}^{u-1}(x+k) = x^u \prod_{k=0}^{u-1}\left(1 + \frac{k}{x}\right) = x

Therefore

$$f(x) = \frac{x^u}{u!}\left(1 + \frac{u(u-1)}{2x} + O(x^{-2})\right).$$

$$\boxed{f(x) \sim \frac{x^u}{u!} \quad \text{as } x \to \infty.}$$
