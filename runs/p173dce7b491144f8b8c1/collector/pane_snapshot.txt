**Goal.** Evaluate
$$L=\lim_{x\to 0}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{x}\Big(e^{-x^{2}(1+t)}(1+t)^{-1}-(1+t)^{-1
}\Big)\,dt.$$

**Step 1. Simplify the integrand.** Factor out $(1+t)^{-1}$:
$$L=\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-x^{2}(1+t)}-1}{1+t}\,dt.$$

**Step 2. Substitute $u=x^{2}(1+t)$.** Then $du=x^{2}\,dt$, $\;dt=du/x^{2}$, $\;
(1+t)=u/x^{2}$, so $(1+t)^{-1}=x^{2}/u$. When $t=0$, $u=x^{2}$; when $t=1$, $u=2
x^{2}$. Hence
$$\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-x^{2}(1+t)}-1}{1+t}\,dt
=\int_{x^{2}}^{2x^{2}}\big(e^{-u}-1\big)\cdot\frac{x^{2}}{u}\cdot\frac{du}{x^{2}
}
=\int_{x^{2}}^{2x^{2}}\frac{e^{-u}-1}{u}\,du.$$
Therefore
$$L=\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{1}{x}\int_{x^{2}}^{2x^{2}}\frac{e^{-u}-1}{u}\,du.$$

**Step 3. Define a continuous auxiliary function.** Let
$$f(u)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{e^{-u}-1}{u},&u\neq 0,\\[4pt]-1,&u=0,\end{cases}$$
where $f(0)=-1$ is the continuous extension since $\lim_{u\to 0}\frac{e^{-u}-1}{
u}=-1$. The function $f$ is continuous on $[0,\infty)$.

**Step 4. Apply the integral mean value theorem.** For each $x\neq 0$, $f$ is co
ntinuous on $[x^{2},2x^{2}]$, so there exists $c_{x}\in[x^{2},2x^{2}]$ with
$$\int_{x^{2}}^{2x^{2}}f(u)\,du=f(c_{x})\,(2x^{2}-x^{2})=f(c_{x})\,x^{2}.$$

**Step 5. Take the limit.** Since $x^{2}\le c_{x}\le 2x^{2}$, we have $c_{x}\to
0$ as $x\to 0$, and by continuity $f(c_{x})\to f(0)=-1$. Thus
$$L=\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{1}{x}\,f(c_{x})\,x^{2}=\lim_{x\to 0}x\,f(c_{x})=0\cdot(-1
)=0.$$

**Conclusion.**
$$\boxed{L=0}.$$
