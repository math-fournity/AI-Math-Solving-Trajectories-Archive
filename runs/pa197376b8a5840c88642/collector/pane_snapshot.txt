### Explicit construction

$$\mathfrak{K} = \begin{pmatrix} 1-\delta & \delta \\ \delta & 1-\delta \end{pma

**Define $\mathfrak{H}$ (non-ergodic):**

$$\mathfrak{H} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = I$$

### $\mathfrak{K}$ is ergodic

- **Irreducible:** Since $\delta > 0$, we have $\mathfrak{K}(1,2) = \delta > 0$
and $\mathfrak{K}(2,1) = \delta > 0$, so both states communicate. The chain is i
rreducible.
- **Aperiodic:** $\mathfrak{K}(1,1) = 1 - \delta > 0$, so state 1 has a self-loo
p; its period is 1. By irreducibility, all states share period 1.

Therefore $\mathfrak{K}$ is ergodic. $\square$

### $\mathfrak{H}$ is not ergodic

$\mathfrak{H} = I$ is the identity matrix. State 1 transitions only to itself, a

 $\{2\}$ — two separate classes — so the chain is **not irreducible**, hence not

**Row 1:** $\mathfrak{K}(1,\cdot) = (1-\delta,\; \delta)$, $\mathfrak{H}(1,\cdot

$$d_{\mathrm{TV}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\big(|(1-\delta)-1| + |\delta - 0|\big) = \tfrac

$$d_{\mathrm{TV}} = \tfrac{1}{2}(|\delta - 0| + |(1-\delta)-1|) = \tfrac{1}{2}(\

This holds under any reasonable convention (sup, max, or any row-weighted averag
