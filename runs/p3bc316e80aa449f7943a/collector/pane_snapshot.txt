## Proof

We seek the largest integer $m$ such that $a^{12}\equiv 1\pmod m$ for every inte
ger $a$ with $\gcd(a,m)=1$.

**Key tool (Carmichael's function).** Let $\lambda(m)$ denote the exponent of th
e multiplicative group $(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times$, i.e. the least positive
$\ell$ with $a^\ell\equiv 1\pmod m$ for all $a$ coprime to $m$. Then the conditi
on "$a^{12}\equiv 1\pmod m$ for all $a$ coprime to $m$" is equivalent to
$$\lambda(m)\mid 12.$$
So we must find the largest $m$ with $\lambda(m)\mid 12$.

**Structure of $\lambda$.** For $m=2^{\alpha}\prod p_i^{e_i}$ (odd primes $p_i$)
,
$$\lambda(m)=\operatorname{lcm}\bigl(\lambda(2^\alpha),\,\lambda(p_1^{e_1}),\dot
s\bigr),$$
where $\lambda(2)=1,\ \lambda(4)=2,\ \lambda(2^\alpha)=2^{\alpha-2}$ for $\alpha
\ge 3$, and $\lambda(p^e)=p^{e-1}(p-1)$ for odd $p$.

Since $12=2^2\cdot 3$, every prime-power factor of $m$ must have its $\lambda$ d
ividing $12$, **and** the lcm of all of them must still divide $12$.

---

**Odd prime powers $p^e$ with $p^{e-1}(p-1)\mid 12$.** We need $p-1\mid 12$ and
$p^{e-1}\mid 12$.

$p-1\mid 12$ gives $p\in\{2,3,5,7,13\}$; the odd ones are $3,5,7,13$.

- $p=3$: $3^{e-1}\cdot 2\mid 12\Rightarrow 3^{e-1}\mid 6\Rightarrow e\le 2$. Max
 $3^2=9$, $\lambda(9)=6$.
- $p=5$: $5^{e-1}\cdot 4\mid 12\Rightarrow 5^{e-1}\mid 3\Rightarrow e=1$. $\lamb
da(5)=4$.
- $p=7$: $7^{e-1}\cdot 6\mid 12\Rightarrow 7^{e-1}\mid 2\Rightarrow e=1$. $\lamb
da(7)=6$.
- $p=13$: $13^{e-1}\cdot 12\mid 12\Rightarrow e=1$. $\lambda(13)=12$.

No other odd prime works (e.g. $p=11$ gives $p-1=10\nmid 12$; $p=17$ gives $16\n
mid 12$).

**Power of $2$.** $\lambda(2^\alpha)=2^{\alpha-2}\mid 12=2^2\cdot 3$ for $\alpha
\ge 3$, so $\alpha-2\le 2$, i.e. $\alpha\le 4$. Max $2^4=16$, $\lambda(16)=4$. (
Also $\alpha=0,1,2$ are allowed but give smaller factors.)

**Combining.** Take the maximal prime powers:
$$m=2^4\cdot 3^2\cdot 5\cdot 7\cdot 13=16\cdot 9\cdot 5\cdot 7\cdot 13.$$
Compute: $16\cdot 9=144$; $144\cdot 5=720$; $720\cdot 7=5040$; $5040\cdot 13=655
20$.

Check the lcm condition:
$$\operatorname{lcm}\bigl(\lambda(16),\lambda(9),\lambda(5),\lambda(7),\lambda(1
3)\bigr)=\operatorname{lcm}(4,6,4,6,12)=12\mid 12.\ \checkmark$$

**Maximality.** Each prime-power factor is already the largest power of that pri
me whose $\lambda$ divides $12$, and no further odd prime $p$ can appear (since
$p-1\nmid 12$ for any other prime). Raising any existing factor would make its $
\lambda$ not divide $12$; adding any new prime would introduce a $\lambda$ not d
ividing $12$. Hence no multiple of $65520$ (other than itself) satisfies the con
dition, and $65520$ is the largest valid $m$.

---

$$\boxed{m=65520}.$$
