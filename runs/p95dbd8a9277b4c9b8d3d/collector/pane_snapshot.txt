## Proof

**Setup.** We have $I = \int_{\zeta}^{B} e^{f(z)}\,dz$ where $f$ is analytic, $f
'(\zeta)=f''(\zeta)=0$, $f'''(\zeta)\neq 0$, and $B$ lies in one of the three va
lleys of the double saddle-point.

**Step 1: Cubic approximation near $\zeta$.**

Since $f'(\zeta)=f''(\zeta)=0$ and $f'''(\zeta)\neq 0$, the Taylor expansion of
$f$ at $\zeta$ begins:

$$f(z) = f(\zeta) + \frac{f'''(\zeta)}{6}(z-\zeta)^3 + \frac{f^{(4)}(\zeta)}{24}
(z-\zeta)^4 + \cdots$$

By the condition that a cubic approximation holds near $\zeta$, we retain only t
he leading non-trivial term:

$$f(z) \approx f(\zeta) + \frac{f'''(\zeta)}{6}(z-\zeta)^3.$$

**Step 2: Cubic change of variables (Chester–Friedman–Ursell mapping).**

Introduce a new variable $t$ via the cubic mapping:

$$f(z) - f(\zeta) = \frac{t^3}{3}, \qquad t(\zeta) = 0.$$

Near $\zeta$, comparing leading terms:

$$\frac{f'''(\zeta)}{6}(z-\zeta)^3 = \frac{t^3}{3} \implies t = \left(\frac{f'''
(\zeta)}{2}\right)^{1/3}(z-\zeta) + \cdots$$

Define $\beta := \left(\frac{f'''(\zeta)}{2}\right)^{1/3}$. Then to leading orde
r:

$$dz = \frac{dt}{\beta} = \left(\frac{2}{f'''(\zeta)}\right)^{1/3} dt.$$

**Step 3: Transform the integral.**

$$I \approx e^{f(\zeta)}\left(\frac{2}{f'''(\zeta)}\right)^{1/3} \int_{0}^{T} e^
{t^3/3}\,dt,$$

where $T$ is the image of $B$ in the $t$-plane. Since $B$ lies in a valley of th
e double saddle-point, $T$ lies in one of the three valleys of $t^3/3$, i.e., in
 a direction where $\operatorname{Re}(t^3)\to -\infty$.

**Step 4: Evaluate the canonical integral — completing back to the Airy contour.
**

The three valleys of $t^3/3$ are at directions $\arg t = \pi/3,\;\pi,\;-\pi/3$ (
where $t^3 = -|t|^3$, so $e^{t^3/3}\to 0$). The condition "tails can be complete
d back" means we deform the path from $0$ to $T$ into a standard steepest-descen
t ray to the valley at infinity, and the tail integral (from $B$ onward) is negl
igible. The path is then completed to the **Airy contour** $C$, which runs from
$\infty\, e^{-i\pi/3}$ through $t=0$ to $\infty\, e^{i\pi/3}$ (connecting two ad
jacent valleys through the saddle).

The Airy function is defined by:

$$\operatorname{Ai}(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C} e^{t^3/3 - xt}\,dt,$$

so at $x=0$:

$$\int_{C} e^{t^3/3}\,dt = 2\pi i\,\operatorname{Ai}(0).$$

**Step 5: Compute $\operatorname{Ai}(0)$.**

Parametrize the two rays of $C$. For the ray $t = r\,e^{i\pi/3}$ ($r: 0\to\infty
$): $t^3 = r^3 e^{i\pi} = -r^3$, so $e^{t^3/3}=e^{-r^3/3}$, $dt = e^{i\pi/3}dr$:

$$\int_{0}^{\infty e^{i\pi/3}} e^{t^3/3}\,dt = e^{i\pi/3}\int_0^\infty e^{-r^3/3
}\,dr = e^{i\pi/3}\cdot 3^{-2/3}\,\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{1}{3}\right),$$

using the substitution $u=r^3/3$ so that $\int_0^\infty e^{-r^3/3}\,dr = 3^{-2/3
}\int_0^\infty u^{-2/3}e^{-u}\,du = 3^{-2/3}\,\Gamma(1/3)$.

Similarly, $\int_{0}^{\infty e^{-i\pi/3}} e^{t^3/3}\,dt = e^{-i\pi/3}\cdot 3^{-2
/3}\,\Gamma(1/3)$.

The full Airy contour (from $e^{-i\pi/3}$-valley to $e^{i\pi/3}$-valley):

$$\int_C e^{t^3/3}\,dt = \left(e^{i\pi/3} - e^{-i\pi/3}\right)3^{-2/3}\Gamma\!\l
eft(\tfrac{1}{3}\right) = 2i\sin\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{3}\right)\cdot 3^{-2/3}\,\Ga
mma\!\left(\tfrac{1}{3}\right) = i\sqrt{3}\cdot 3^{-2/3}\,\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{1
}{3}\right).$$

Therefore:

$$\operatorname{Ai}(0) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\cdot i\sqrt{3}\cdot 3^{-2/3}\,\Gamma\!
\left(\tfrac{1}{3}\right) = \frac{\sqrt{3}\cdot 3^{-2/3}\,\Gamma(1/3)}{2\pi} = \
frac{3^{-1/6}\,\Gamma(1/3)}{2\pi}.$$

Using the reflection formula $\Gamma(1/3)\,\Gamma(2/3) = \frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{3}} =
 2\pi\cdot 3^{-1/2}$, so $\Gamma(1/3) = \frac{2\pi\cdot 3^{-1/2}}{\Gamma(2/3)}$:

$$\operatorname{Ai}(0) = \frac{3^{-1/6}\cdot 2\pi\cdot 3^{-1/2}}{2\pi\,\Gamma(2/
3)} = \frac{3^{-2/3}}{\Gamma(2/3)} = \frac{1}{3^{2/3}\,\Gamma(2/3)}.$$

**Step 6: Assemble the final result.**

$$I = \int_{\zeta}^{B} e^{f(z)}\,dz \sim e^{f(\zeta)}\left(\frac{2}{f'''(\zeta)}
\right)^{1/3}\cdot 2\pi i\,\operatorname{Ai}(0) = \frac{2\pi i\,e^{f(\zeta)}}{\l
eft(\frac{f'''(\zeta)}{2}\right)^{1/3}}\,\operatorname{Ai}(0).$$

Substituting $\operatorname{Ai}(0) = \frac{1}{3^{2/3}\,\Gamma(2/3)}$:

$$\boxed{\int_{\zeta}^{B} e^{f(z)}\,dz \sim \frac{2\pi i\,e^{f(\zeta)}}{3^{2/3}\
,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)\left(\frac{f'''(\zeta)}{2}\right)^{1/3}}}$$

**Interpretation of the conditions:**
- **Cubic approximation near $\zeta$**: justifies truncating $f(z)$ at the cubic
 term and using the exact cubic mapping $f(z)-f(\zeta)=t^3/3$.
- **Tail integral negligible**: the contribution from the region far from $\zeta
$ (beyond $B$) vanishes, so the integral is dominated by the neighborhood of the
 saddle.
- **Tails can be completed back**: the integration path from $\zeta$ to $B$ can
be deformed (without changing the value, by Cauchy's theorem, since $e^{f(z)}$ i
s entire in the region) into the standard Airy contour $C$ passing through the s
addle and connecting two valleys, with the added tails contributing negligibly.

The appearance of the **Airy function** $\operatorname{Ai}(0)$ is the hallmark o
f a double (cubic) saddle-point, just as the Gaussian $\sqrt{2\pi/(-f''(\zeta))}
$ is the hallmark of a simple (quadratic) saddle-point. The factor $\left(f'''(\
zeta)/2\right)^{-1/3}$ plays the role analogous to $\left(-f''(\zeta)\right)^{-1
/2}$ in the simple saddle-point case, reflecting the cubic rather than quadratic
 scaling.
