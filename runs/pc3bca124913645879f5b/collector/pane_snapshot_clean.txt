## Solution

Let $X \subset \mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{C})$ be a smooth plane curve of degree $d =
4$, and let $D = p + q + r$ with $\deg D = 3$.

**Genus.** For a smooth plane curve of degree $d$, the genus is
$$g = \frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2} = \frac{3 \cdot 2}{2} = 3.$$

**Canonical class.** For a smooth plane curve of degree $d$, the canonical divis
or satisfies $K_X \sim (d-3)H$, where $H$ is a hyperplane (line) section. With $
d = 4$,
$$K_X \sim H,$$
so $K_X$ is linearly equivalent to the divisor cut on $X$ by any line in $\mathb
b{P}^2$, and $\deg K_X = 2g - 2 = 4$.

**Riemann–Roch.** Applying the Riemann–Roch theorem to $D = p + q + r$:
$$\ell(D) - \ell(K_X - D) = \deg D + 1 - g = 3 + 1 - 3 = 1.$$
Hence
$$\ell(D) = 1 + \ell(K_X - D), \qquad \deg(K_X - D) = 4 - 3 = 1.$$

So $\ell(D) = 1 + \ell(K_X - p - q - r)$, and the value of $\ell(K_X - D)$ depen
ds on whether $K_X - D$ is linearly equivalent to an effective divisor of degree
 $1$ (i.e. to a point).

**Case 1: $p, q, r$ are not collinear (the general case).** Since $K_X \sim H$ (
a line section), $K_X - D$ is effective only if there is a line $\ell$ with $\el
l \cdot X \geq p + q + r$, i.e. a line passing through $p, q, r$. Three non-coll
inear points determine no such line, so $K_X - D$ is not effective. A divisor of
 degree $1$ that is not effective has $\ell = 0$. Therefore
$$\ell(K_X - D) = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \ell(D) = 1.$$

**Case 2: $p, q, r$ are collinear.** Let $\ell$ be the line through them. Then $
\ell \cdot X = p + q + r + s$ for a fourth point $s \in X$ (counted with multipl
icity, by Bézout, since $\deg \ell \cdot \deg X = 4$). This divisor is a hyperpl
ane section, so
$$p + q + r + s \sim H \sim K_X.$$
Thus $K_X - D \sim s$, which is effective of degree $1$, giving $\ell(K_X - D) =
 1$ and hence
$$\ell(D) = 2.$$

**Conclusion.** For a smooth plane quartic ($g = 3$) and three points $p, q, r \
in X$:

$$\boxed{\dim L(p+q+r) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } p, q, r \text{ are not col
linear (general case)},\\ 2 & \text{if } p, q, r \text{ are collinear}. \end{cas
es}}$$

In particular, for general (generic) points $p, q, r$, $\dim L(p+q+r) = 1$.
