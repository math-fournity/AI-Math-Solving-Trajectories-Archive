**The contour.** The curve $\gamma$ is parametrized by $z = \tfrac{1}{2}e^{2\pi
i t}$, $0 \le t \le 1$, i.e., the circle $|z|=\tfrac12$ traversed once countercl
ockwise.

**Singularities of the integrand.** Let
$$f(z)=\frac{1}{z^{2}(e^{z}-1)}.$$
The denominator vanishes when $z^{2}=0$ or $e^{z}-1=0$, i.e. at $z=0$ and at $z=
2\pi i k$ for $k\in\mathbb{Z}$. Inside the disk $|z|\le \tfrac12$ the only such
point is $z=0$, since $|2\pi i k|=2\pi|k|\ge 2\pi>\tfrac12$ for $k\ne 0$.

**Nature of the singularity at $z=0$.** Using $e^{z}-1 = z+\dfrac{z^{2}}{2}+\dfr
ac{z^{3}}{6}+\cdots = z\!\left(1+\dfrac{z}{2}+\dfrac{z^{2}}{6}+\cdots\right)$, w
e get
$$f(z)=\frac{1}{z^{3}}\cdot\frac{1}{1+\frac{z}{2}+\frac{z^{2}}{6}+\cdots}.$$
Set $u=\dfrac{z}{2}+\dfrac{z^{2}}{6}+\cdots$ and expand $\dfrac{1}{1+u}=1-u+u^{2
}-\cdots$:
- coefficient of $z^{0}$: $1$,
- coefficient of $z^{1}$: $-\dfrac{1}{2}$,
- coefficient of $z^{2}$: $-\dfrac{1}{6}+\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}=-\dfrac{1
}{6}+\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{1}{12}$.

Hence
$$f(z)=z^{-3}-\frac{1}{2}z^{-2}+\frac{1}{12}z^{-1}+\cdots,$$
so $z=0$ is a pole of order $3$ with residue
$$\operatorname{Res}(f,0)=\frac{1}{12}.$$

**Applying the residue theorem.** Since $z=0$ is the unique singularity enclosed
 by $\gamma$,
$$\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}\frac{1}{z^{2}(e^{z}-1)}\,dz=\operatorname{Res}(f
,0)=\frac{1}{12}.$$
