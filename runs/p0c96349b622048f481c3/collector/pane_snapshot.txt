## Bounded Formal Solution

**Problem.** Solve
$$u_{rr}+\tfrac{1}{r}u_r+\tfrac{1}{r^2}u_{\theta\theta}=0,\quad 0<r<\rho,\;0<\th
eta<\gamma,$$
with $u(\rho,\theta)=f(\theta)$, $u_\theta(r,0)=u_\theta(r,\gamma)=0$, and $u$ b
ounded near $r=0$, where $0<\gamma<2\pi$.

---

### 1. Separation of variables

Seek $u(r,\theta)=R(r)\Theta(\theta)$. Substitution gives
$$\frac{r^2R''+rR'}{R}=-\frac{\Theta''}{\Theta}=\lambda.$$

The angular equation with the Neumann conditions becomes the Sturm–Liouville pro
blem
$$\Theta''+\lambda\Theta=0,\qquad \Theta'(0)=\Theta'(\gamma)=0.$$

Its eigenvalues and (normalized) eigenfunctions are
$$\lambda_n=\Bigl(\frac{n\pi}{\gamma}\Bigr)^2,\qquad \Theta_n(\theta)=\cos\Bigl(
\frac{n\pi\theta}{\gamma}\Bigr),\qquad n=0,1,2,\dots$$

### 2. Radial equation

For each $n$, the radial equation is $r^2R''+rR'-\lambda_n R=0$ (Euler equation)
.

- **$n=0$ ($\lambda_0=0$):** $R_0(r)=A_0+B_0\ln r$. Boundedness at $r=0$ forces
$B_0=0$, so $R_0$ is constant.
- **$n\ge 1$:** $R_n(r)=C_n r^{n\pi/\gamma}+D_n r^{-n\pi/\gamma}$. Boundedness a
t $r=0$ forces $D_n=0$, so $R_n(r)=C_n r^{n\pi/\gamma}$.

### 3. Series form

By superposition, the bounded solution is
$$u(r,\theta)=a_0+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n\Bigl(\frac{r}{\rho}\Bigr)^{n\pi/\gamma}
\cos\Bigl(\frac{n\pi\theta}{\gamma}\Bigr).$$

(The factor $\rho^{-n\pi/\gamma}$ is absorbed into $a_n$ for convenience.)

### 4. Determining the coefficients from $u(\rho,\theta)=f(\theta)$

Setting $r=\rho$ gives the cosine (half-range Neumann) Fourier expansion of $f$
on $[0,\gamma]$:
$$f(\theta)=a_0+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n\cos\Bigl(\frac{n\pi\theta}{\gamma}\Bigr).
$$

Using orthogonality $\int_0^\gamma \cos(n\pi\theta/\gamma)\cos(m\pi\theta/\gamma
)\,d\theta = \frac{\gamma}{2}\delta_{nm}$ for $n,m\ge1$ (and $=\gamma$ for $n=m=
0$):

$$\boxed{\;a_0=\frac{1}{\gamma}\int_0^{\gamma}f(\varphi)\,d\varphi,\qquad a_n=\f
rac{2}{\gamma}\int_0^{\gamma}f(\varphi)\cos\Bigl(\frac{n\pi\varphi}{\gamma}\Bigr
)\,d\varphi,\quad n\ge1.\;}$$

### 5. Verification

- **PDE:** Each term $(r/\rho)^{n\pi/\gamma}\cos(n\pi\theta/\gamma)$ is harmonic
 in the sector (product of a power of $r$ and a cosine in $\theta$), so the seri
es is harmonic termwise; convergence for $r<\rho$ justifies termwise differentia
tion.
- **Neumann edges:** $\partial_\theta\cos(n\pi\theta/\gamma)\big|_{\theta=0}=0$
and $\big|_{\theta=\gamma}=-(n\pi/\gamma)\sin(n\pi)=0$, so $u_\theta(r,0)=u_\the
ta(r,\gamma)=0$.
- **Arc data:** At $r=\rho$ the series reduces to the cosine Fourier series of $
f$, hence equals $f$ (in the $L^2$ sense, and pointwise wherever $f$ is suitably
 regular).
- **Boundedness:** Every radial factor is either constant ($n=0$) or a positive
power of $r$, so $u$ remains bounded as $r\to0^+$; in fact $u(r,\theta)\to a_0$.

### 6. Bounded formal solution

$$\boxed{\;u(r,\theta)=\frac{1}{\gamma}\int_0^{\gamma}f(\varphi)\,d\varphi+\sum_
{n=1}^{\infty}\Biggl[\frac{2}{\gamma}\int_0^{\gamma}f(\varphi)\cos\Bigl(\frac{n\
pi\varphi}{\gamma}\Bigr)\,d\varphi\Biggr]\Bigl(\frac{r}{\rho}\Bigr)^{n\pi/\gamma
}\cos\Bigl(\frac{n\pi\theta}{\gamma}\Bigr).\;}$$

Equivalently, using the Poisson-kernel form for the sector with Neumann edges, t
his can be written as the convolution
$$u(r,\theta)=\int_0^{\gamma}P_\gamma\!\Bigl(\frac{r}{\rho},\theta,\varphi\Bigr)
f(\varphi)\,d\varphi,$$
with sector Neumann Poisson kernel
$$P_\gamma(s,\theta,\varphi)=\frac{1}{\gamma}\Biggl[1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s^{n\p
i/\gamma}\cos\Bigl(\frac{n\pi\theta}{\gamma}\Bigr)\cos\Bigl(\frac{n\pi\varphi}{\
gamma}\Bigr)\Biggr],\qquad 0\le s<1.$$

This is the unique bounded formal solution of the stated boundary-value problem.
