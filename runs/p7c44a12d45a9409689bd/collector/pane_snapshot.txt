**Problem:** Determine the number of positive integer solutions to $x^7 + y^8 =
z^9$.

## Proof

We exhibit an infinite family of distinct positive-integer solutions, which prov
es the number of solutions is infinite.

**Construction.** Seek solutions of the form $x = 2^u,\ y = 2^v,\ z = 2^w$ with
$u, v, w$ positive integers. The equation becomes
$$2^{7u} + 2^{8v} = 2^{9w}.$$

**Analysis of the exponents.** For the sum of two powers of $2$ to itself be a p
ower of $2$, the two exponents must be equal (otherwise the larger term factors
out and leaves an odd factor $\geq 3$, which cannot be a power of $2$). Formally
:

- If $7u < 8v$: factoring $2^{7u}$ gives $2^{7u}(1 + 2^{8v - 7u})$. Since $8v -
7u \geq 1$, the factor $1 + 2^{8v-7u}$ is odd and $\geq 3$, hence not a power of
 $2$. No solution.
- If $7u > 8v$: symmetrically, $2^{8v}(2^{7u-8v}+1)$ with the odd factor $\geq 3
$. No solution.
- If $7u = 8v$: then $2^{7u} + 2^{8v} = 2 \cdot 2^{7u} = 2^{7u+1}$, and we need
$$7u + 1 = 9w. \tag{$\star$}$$

**Solving the constraints.** From $7u = 8v$, since $\gcd(7,8)=1$, we set $u = 8t
,\ v = 7t$ for a positive integer $t$. Substituting into $(\star)$:
$$56t + 1 = 9w \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 56t + 1 \equiv 0 \pmod{9}.$$
Since $56 \equiv 2 \pmod{9}$, this becomes $2t + 1 \equiv 0 \pmod{9}$, i.e., $2t
 \equiv 8 \pmod{9}$. Multiplying by the inverse of $2$ modulo $9$ (which is $5$,
 since $2\cdot 5 = 10 \equiv 1$):
$$t \equiv 8 \cdot 5 \equiv 40 \equiv 4 \pmod{9}.$$

Thus $t = 4 + 9s$ for any integer $s \geq 0$, giving
$$u = 8(4+9s) = 32 + 72s, \qquad v = 7(4+9s) = 28 + 63s,$$
$$w = \frac{56(4+9s)+1}{9} = \frac{225 + 504s}{9} = 25 + 56s.$$
