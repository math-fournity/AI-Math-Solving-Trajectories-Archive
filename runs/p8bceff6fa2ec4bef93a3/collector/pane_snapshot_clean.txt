## Solution

We use the telescoping identity. For every positive integer $k$,

$$k \cdot k! \;=\; (k+1)\,k! - k! \;=\; (k+1)! - k!.$$

**Verification of the identity.** By definition $(k+1)! = (k+1)\,k!$, so
$$(k+1)! - k! = (k+1)\,k! - k! = k\,k!.$$

Applying this to each term of the sum,

$$\sum_{k=1}^{1000} k\cdot k! \;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{1000}\bigl((k+1)! - k!\bigr).$$

Writing out the summands explicitly makes the telescoping visible:

$$\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k=1}^{1000}\bigl((k+1)! - k!\bigr)
&= (2! - 1!) + (3! - 2!) + (4! - 3!) + \cdots + (1001! - 1000!) \\
&= -1! + 1001! \\
&= 1001! - 1.
\end{aligned}$$

Every intermediate factorial $2!, 3!, \dots, 1000!$ appears once with a $+$ sign
 (as $(k+1)!$ for some $k$) and once with a $-$ sign (as $k!$ for some $k$), so
they all cancel. Only $-1!$ (from $k=1$) and $+1001!$ (from $k=1000$) survive. S
ince $1! = 1$,

$$\boxed{\sum_{k=1}^{1000} k\cdot k! \;=\; 1001! - 1.}$$
