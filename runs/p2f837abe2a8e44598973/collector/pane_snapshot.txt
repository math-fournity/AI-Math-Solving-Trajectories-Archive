## Setup

The deck has 52 non-joker cards (4 suits × 13 ranks) plus 2 jokers. After shuffl
ing, the two jokers occupy two positions; if they are at positions $i < j$, ther
e are $k = j - i - 1$ cards between them. The number of pairs giving $k$ cards b
etween is $53 - k$, out of $\binom{54}{2} = 1431$ total pairs. So:

$$P(k \text{ cards between}) = \frac{53 - k}{1431}, \quad k = 0, 1, \ldots, 52.$
$

Given $k$ cards between, they form a uniformly random $k$-subset of the 52 non-j
oker cards.

## Counting subsets with sum ≡ 0 (mod 13)

Each rank $r \in \{1, \ldots, 13\}$ appears 4 times. Modulo 13, rank 13 ≡ 0, so
we have 4 cards of each residue class $0, 1, \ldots, 12 \pmod{13}$.

Using the character/DFT method with $\omega = e^{2\pi i/13}$, the number of $k$-
subsets with sum $\equiv s \pmod{13}$ is:

$$N(k, s) = \frac{1}{13} \sum_{j=0}^{12} \omega^{-js} [y^k] \prod_{r=0}^{12} (1
+ y\,\omega^{jr})^4.$$

**For $j = 0$:** $\prod_{r=0}^{12}(1+y)^4 = (1+y)^{52}$, giving $[y^k] = \binom{
52}{k}$.

**For $j \neq 0$:** Since 13 is prime, $jr \bmod 13$ permutes $\{0, \ldots, 12\}
$, so:

$$\prod_{r=0}^{12}(1 + y\,\omega^{jr})^4 = \left[\prod_{r=0}^{12}(1 + y\,\omega^
r)\right]^4.$$

Using $\prod_{r=0}^{12}(t - \omega^r) = t^{13} - 1$ and substituting $t = -1/y$,
 one shows:

$$\prod_{r=0}^{12}(1 + y\,\omega^r) = 1 + y^{13}.$$

So for $j \neq 0$: $[y^k](1 + y^{13})^4 = \binom{4}{k/13}$ if $13 \mid k$, else
$0$.

Setting $s = 0$:

$$N(k, 0) = \frac{1}{13}\left[\binom{52}{k} + 12 \cdot \mathbf{1}_{13 \mid k} \c
dot \binom{4}{k/13}\right].$$

The probability is $P(\text{sum} \equiv 0 \mid k) = N(k,0)/\binom{52}{k} = \frac
{1}{13}\left[1 + \frac{12\,\binom{4}{k/13}}{\binom{52}{k}} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{13\
mid k}\right]$.

$$p = \sum_{k=0}^{52} \frac{53-k}{1431} \cdot \frac{1}{13}\left[1 + \frac{12\,\b

## Minimality of denominator

We have $p = \frac{77}{689} + \epsilon$ where $|\epsilon| \approx 2.197 \times 1
0^{-13} < 10^{-10}$, and $\gcd(77, 689) = \gcd(7 \cdot 11, \, 13 \cdot 53) = 1$.

The continued fraction of $77/689$ is $[0; 8, 1, 18, 4]$, with convergents $0/1,
\; 1/8,\; 1/9,\; 19/170,\; 77/689$.

By the theory of continued fractions, the closest fraction to $77/689$ with deno
minator $< 689$ is the penultimate convergent $19/170$, at distance:

$$\left|\frac{77}{689} - \frac{19}{170}\right| = \frac{1}{689 \cdot 170} = \frac
{1}{117130} \approx 8.54 \times 10^{-6}.$$

$$|p - c/d| \geq \left|\frac{77}{689} - c/d\right| - |\epsilon| \geq \frac{1}{11
7130} - 10^{-10} \approx 8.54 \times 10^{-6} \gg 10^{-10}.$$

Therefore no fraction with denominator $< 689$ approximates $p$ to within $10^{-
