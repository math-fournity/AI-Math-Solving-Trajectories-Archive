Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum size of a set of points in P
G(d,q) that meets every line (a blocking set with respect to lines).

**Answer:** The smallest such set has size $\frac{q^d - 1}{q - 1}$, achieved by
any hyperplane.

---

## Proof

**Upper bound: A hyperplane works.**

A hyperplane $H$ in $PG(d,q)$ is a $(d-1)$-dimensional projective subspace, cont
aining $\frac{q^d - 1}{q-1}$ points. We show $H$ intersects every line.

Let $\ell$ be any line in $PG(d,q)$. In projective geometry, two subspaces of di
mensions $a$ and $b$ in $PG(d,q)$ with $a + b \geq d$ must intersect in a subspa
ce of dimension at least $a + b - d$. Taking $a = d-1$ (the hyperplane) and $b =
 1$ (the line), we have $a + b = d \geq d$, so $H \cap \ell$ has dimension at le
ast $0$—that is, $H$ and $\ell$ share at least one point.

Therefore $H$ intersects every line, giving an upper bound of $\frac{q^d - 1}{q-
1}$.

**Lower bound: No smaller set suffices.**

Let $S$ be any set of points in $PG(d,q)$ that intersects every line. We show $|
S| \geq \frac{q^d - 1}{q-1}$.

If $S$ contains all points of $PG(d,q)$, then $|S| = \frac{q^{d+1}-1}{q-1} \geq
\frac{q^d-1}{q-1}$, so the bound holds trivially.

Otherwise, there exists a point $P \notin S$. Consider all lines through $P$. Th
e number of such lines equals the number of points in the quotient space $PG(d-1
,q)$, which is:

$$\frac{q^d - 1}{q - 1}.$$

Every line through $P$ must contain a point of $S$ (since $S$ intersects all lin
es). Since $P \notin S$, the point of $S$ on each such line is distinct from $P$
.

**Key observation:** Each point $Q \in S$ with $Q \neq P$ lies on exactly **one*
* line through $P$—namely, the unique line $\overline{PQ}$. Therefore, the point
s of $S$ are distributed among the lines through $P$, with each point lying on e
xactly one such line.

For every line through $P$ to contain at least one point of $S$, we need at leas
t one point of $S$ per line. Since each point of $S$ accounts for at most one li
ne through $P$:

$$|S| \geq \text{(number of lines through } P\text{)} = \frac{q^d - 1}{q - 1}.$$

**Conclusion.**

The lower bound $\frac{q^d - 1}{q-1}$ matches the upper bound achieved by a hype
rplane. Therefore, the smallest set of points in $PG(d,q)$ that intersects all l
ines has exactly

$$\boxed{\dfrac{q^d - 1}{q - 1}}$$

points, and this minimum is attained by any hyperplane.

**Remark.** For small cases this recovers classical results:
- $d = 1$: the answer is $1$ (a single point meets the unique line of $PG(1,q)$)
.
- $d = 2$: the answer is $q + 1$ (a line in $PG(2,q)$ meets every other line, si
nce any two lines in a projective plane intersect).
