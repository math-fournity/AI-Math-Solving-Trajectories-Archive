## Proof

We track a quantity through the process and derive a contradiction if all final
numbers are too large.

**Invariant.** For any collection of positive reals currently on the board, defi
ne
$$\Phi = \sum_{x \text{ on board}} \frac{1}{x^{2}}.$$

We claim $\Phi$ is non-decreasing under the operation. Indeed, suppose we erase
a number $x$ and write $a,b>0$ with $ab = 2x^{2}$. The change in $\Phi$ is
$$\Delta\Phi = \frac{1}{a^{2}}+\frac{1}{b^{2}}-\frac{1}{x^{2}}.$$
By the AM–GM inequality applied to $\frac{1}{a^{2}}$ and $\frac{1}{b^{2}}$,
$$\frac{1}{a^{2}}+\frac{1}{b^{2}} \;\ge\; \frac{2}{ab} \;=\; \frac{2}{2x^{2}} \;
=\; \frac{1}{x^{2}}.$$
Hence $\Delta\Phi \ge 0$, so $\Phi$ never decreases.

**Initial and final values.** Initially the board holds the single number $r$, s
o
$$\Phi_{\text{initial}} = \frac{1}{r^{2}}.$$
After $k^{2}-1$ operations the board holds $k^{2}$ positive real numbers $x_{1},
\dots,x_{k^{2}}$, and by the invariant
$$\sum_{i=1}^{k^{2}} \frac{1}{x_{i}^{2}} \;=\; \Phi_{\text{final}} \;\ge\; \frac
{1}{r^{2}}.$$

**Contradiction argument.** Suppose, for contradiction, that every final number
exceeds $kr$, i.e. $x_{i} > kr$ for all $i$. Then
$$\frac{1}{x_{i}^{2}} < \frac{1}{k^{2}r^{2}} \quad\text{for each } i,$$
and summing over all $k^{2}$ numbers,
$$\sum_{i=1}^{k^{2}} \frac{1}{x_{i}^{2}} \;<\; k^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{k^{2}r^{2}} \;
=\; \frac{1}{r^{2}}.$$
This contradicts the established inequality $\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k^{2}} \fr
ac{1}{x_{i}^{2}} \ge \frac{1}{r^{2}}$.

Therefore our assumption is false: at least one of the final numbers satisfies $
x_{i} \le kr$. $\blacksquare$
