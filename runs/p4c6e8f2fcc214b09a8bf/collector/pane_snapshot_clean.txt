# Solution

## Problem

Solve the heat equation
$$\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} = \frac{1}{k}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t},
\quad 0<x<a,\ t>0,$$
with Neumann (constant-flux) boundary conditions
$$u_x(0,t)=\frac{\Delta T}{a},\qquad u_x(a,t)=\frac{\Delta T}{a},\quad t>0,$$
and initial condition
$$u(x,0)=T_0,\quad 0<x<a.$$

## Step 1. Conservation of the spatial mean

Integrate the PDE over $[0,a]$:
$$\int_0^a u_{xx}\,dx = \frac{1}{k}\frac{d}{dt}\int_0^a u\,dx \;\Longrightarrow\
; u_x(a,t)-u_x(0,t)=\frac{a}{k}\frac{d}{dt}\bar u(t).$$
Using the boundary data, the left side is $\Delta T/a-\Delta T/a=0$, hence
$$\frac{d}{dt}\bar u(t)=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \bar u(t)=\bar u(0)=\frac{1}{a}\in
t_0^a T_0\,dx=T_0.$$
So the mean temperature is conserved: $\displaystyle\int_0^a u(x,t)\,dx=aT_0$ fo
r all $t$.

## Step 2. Steady-state solution

The steady state $u_s$ satisfies $u_s''=0$, so $u_s(x)=Ax+B$. The boundary condi
tions give $A=u_s'(0)=u_s'(a)=\Delta T/a$. The constant $B$ is fixed by conserva
tion of the mean:
$$\int_0^a u_s\,dx = \frac{\Delta T}{a}\cdot\frac{a^2}{2}+Ba=\frac{\Delta T\,a}{
2}+Ba=aT_0 \;\Longrightarrow\; B=T_0-\frac{\Delta T}{2}.$$
Thus
$$u_s(x)=T_0+\Delta T\!\left(\frac{x}{a}-\frac12\right).$$

## Step 3. Reduction to homogeneous Neumann problem

Set $v(x,t)=u(x,t)-u_s(x)$. Then $v$ satisfies
$$v_{xx}=\frac1k v_t,\qquad v_x(0,t)=v_x(a,t)=0,$$
with initial data
$$v(x,0)=T_0-u_s(x)=-\Delta T\!\left(\frac{x}{a}-\frac12\right)=\Delta T\!\left(
\frac12-\frac{x}{a}\right).$$

## Step 4. Cosine eigenfunction expansion

For homogeneous Neumann conditions on $(0,a)$, the eigenfunctions are $\cos(n\pi
 x/a)$ with eigenvalues $(n\pi/a)^2$, $n=0,1,2,\dots$ Since $v_t=kv_{xx}$, the t
ime factor is $\exp\!\bigl(-k(n\pi/a)^2 t\bigr)$:
$$v(x,t)=A_0+\sum_{n=1}^\infty A_n\cos\!\frac{n\pi x}{a}\,e^{-k(n\pi/a)^2 t}.$$

The coefficients come from the Fourier cosine series of $v(x,0)=\Delta T(1/2-x/a
)$.

**Mean (n=0):**
$$A_0=\frac1a\int_0^a \Delta T\!\left(\frac12-\frac{x}{a}\right)dx=\frac{\Delta
T}{a}\!\left[\frac a2-\frac a2\right]=0.$$

**n≥1:** With $\xi=x/a$,
$$A_n=\frac{2}{a}\int_0^a \Delta T\!\left(\frac12-\frac{x}{a}\right)\cos\frac{n\
pi x}{a}\,dx=2\Delta T\int_0^1\!\left(\frac12-\xi\right)\cos(n\pi\xi)\,d\xi.$$
Compute:
$$\int_0^1\cos(n\pi\xi)\,d\xi=0,\qquad \int_0^1 \xi\cos(n\pi\xi)\,d\xi=\frac{(-1
)^n-1}{(n\pi)^2}.$$
Hence
$$A_n=-2\Delta T\cdot\frac{(-1)^n-1}{(n\pi)^2}=\frac{2\Delta T\bigl(1-(-1)^n\big
r)}{(n\pi)^2}.$$
So $A_n=0$ for even $n$, and for $n=2m+1$ ($m=0,1,2,\dots$):
$$A_{2m+1}=\frac{4\Delta T}{(2m+1)^2\pi^2}.$$

## Step 5. Full solution

Combining $u=u_s+v$:
$$\boxed{\,u(x,t)=T_0+\Delta T\!\left(\frac{x}{a}-\frac12\right)+\frac{4\Delta T
}{\pi^2}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2m+1)^2}\cos\!\frac{(2m+1)\pi x}{a}\,\exp\!
\!\left(-\frac{k(2m+1)^2\pi^2}{a^2}\,t\right).\,}$$

## Verification

**PDE:** Each term $\cos\!\frac{(2m+1)\pi x}{a}e^{-k(2m+1)^2\pi^2 t/a^2}$ satisf
ies $v_t=kv_{xx}$, and the linear part $u_s$ has $u_{s,xx}=0=(1/k)u_{s,t}$. ✓

**Boundary conditions:**
$$u_x=\frac{\Delta T}{a}-\frac{4\Delta T}{\pi^2}\sum_{m=0}^\infty\frac{(2m+1)\pi
}{a(2m+1)^2}\sin\!\frac{(2m+1)\pi x}{a}e^{-\cdots}.$$
At $x=0$ and $x=a$, every $\sin\!\frac{(2m+1)\pi x}{a}=0$, leaving $u_x(0,t)=u_x
(a,t)=\Delta T/a$. ✓

**Initial condition:** At $t=0$,
$$u(x,0)=T_0+\Delta T\!\left(\frac{x}{a}-\frac12\right)+\frac{4\Delta T}{\pi^2}\
sum_{m=0}^\infty\frac{1}{(2m+1)^2}\cos\!\frac{(2m+1)\pi x}{a}.$$
The cosine series computed above gives
$$\Delta T\!\left(\frac12-\frac{x}{a}\right)=\frac{4\Delta T}{\pi^2}\sum_{m=0}^\
infty\frac{1}{(2m+1)^2}\cos\!\frac{(2m+1)\pi x}{a},$$
so the two non-constant pieces cancel and $u(x,0)=T_0$. ✓

**Long-time behavior:** As $t\to\infty$ every exponential vanishes, leaving $u\t
o u_s(x)=T_0+\Delta T(x/a-1/2)$, whose mean over $[0,a]$ is $T_0$, consistent wi
th the conserved mean. ✓
