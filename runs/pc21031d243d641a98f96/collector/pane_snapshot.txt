## Proof

Consider the standard one-sheeted hyperboloid
$$\Sigma:\ x^{2}+y^{2}-z^{2}=1.$$
Topologically $\Sigma\cong S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}$, so $\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\cong\math
bb{Z}$, generated by the **waist circle** $C_{0}=\{z=0,\ x^{2}+y^{2}=1\}$, which
 has length $2\pi$.

**Claim.** Every closed curve $\gamma\subset\Sigma$ that is not null-homotopic h
as length $\ge 2\pi$, with equality attained by $C_{0}$.

---

### Setup: cylindrical coordinates

Write points of $\Sigma$ as $(r\cos\theta,\, r\sin\theta,\, z)$ with $r\ge 0$. T
he defining equation gives
$$r^{2}=1+z^{2},\qquad\text{hence}\quad r=\sqrt{1+z^{2}}\ge 1,$$
with equality $r=1$ **only** at $z=0$ (the waist).

The Euclidean line element in cylindrical coordinates is
$$ds^{2}=dr^{2}+r^{2}\,d\theta^{2}+dz^{2}.$$

### Key inequality

Since every term is non-negative,
$$ds^{2}\ge r^{2}\,d\theta^{2}\ge d\theta^{2}\qquad(\text{because }r\ge 1).$$
Therefore, for any curve $\gamma(t)=(r(t),\theta(t),z(t))$,
$$\operatorname{len}(\gamma)=\int ds\ge\int|d\theta|.$$

### Winding number gives the lower bound

The angular projection $\pi:\Sigma\to S^{1},\ (x,y,z)\mapsto(x,y)/\sqrt{x^{2}+y^
{2}}$ induces an isomorphism $\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\pi_{1}(S^{1}
)\cong\mathbb{Z}$. Thus a closed curve $\gamma$ is **not** null-homotopic iff it
s winding number $n=\deg(\pi\circ\gamma)$ satisfies $|n|\ge 1$.

For such a curve, the total variation of $\theta$ around one period is at least
$2\pi|n|$:
$$\int_{\gamma}|d\theta|\ge 2\pi|n|\ge 2\pi.$$

Combining with the key inequality,
$$\boxed{\operatorname{len}(\gamma)\ge 2\pi.}$$

### Equality is attained

Take the waist circle $C_{0}(t)=(\cos t,\sin t,0)$, $t\in[0,2\pi]$. Here $r\equi
v 1$, $z\equiv 0$, $dr\equiv 0$, so
$$ds^{2}=d\theta^{2},\qquad \operatorname{len}(C_{0})=\int_{0}^{2\pi}dt=2\pi.$$
This curve represents the generator of $\pi_{1}(\Sigma)$, hence is not null-homo
topic, and its length is exactly $2\pi$.

Moreover, equality in $ds^{2}\ge d\theta^{2}$ forces $r\equiv 1$ (so $z\equiv 0$
) and $dr\equiv 0$, and equality in $\int|d\theta|\ge 2\pi$ forces $\theta$ to b
e monotone with $|n|=1$. Hence $C_{0}$ (traversed once, in either direction) is
the **unique** minimizer up to reparametrization.

---

### Conclusion

Yes. On the one-sheeted hyperboloid, every closed curve through any point $p$ th
at is not homotopic to a point has length at least $2\pi$. The bound is sharp, a
ttained by the waist circle (which passes through every point of the form $(\cos
\theta_{0},\sin\theta_{0},0)$; for a general point $p$, translate the waist circ
le along the ruling family — the waist circle itself passes through exactly the
points with $z=0$, and any non-null-homotopic geodesic representative through $p
$ has length $\ge 2\pi$ by the argument above, which is independent of the base
point).
