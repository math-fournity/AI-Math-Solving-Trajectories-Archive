## Proof

Let
$$W_n := a_n(X_n-\theta) - a_n(Y_n-\theta).$$
By hypothesis $W_n \xrightarrow{d} Z$ for some random variable $Z$. The quantity
 to be analyzed is
$$a_n(X_n-\theta)\,\phi_n - a_n(Y_n-\theta)\,\phi_n = \phi_n\, W_n.$$

**Step 1 — Tightness of $W_n$.**
A standard consequence of convergence in distribution is *tightness* (stochastic
 boundedness): if $W_n \xrightarrow{d} Z$, then $\{W_n\}$ is tight, i.e. $W_n =
O_p(1)$. Concretely, for every $\eta>0$ there exists $M_\eta<\infty$ such that
$$\sup_n P(|W_n| > M_\eta) \le \eta.$$
(This follows because the limit $Z$ is tight: pick $M$ with $P(|Z|>M)<\eta/2$, a
nd by the portmanteau/probability-containment characterization of weak convergen
ce, for all large $n$, $P(|W_n|>M')<\eta$ for some $M'>M$; finitely many initial
 terms are handled individually.)

**Step 2 — Multiplication by a deterministic $o(1)$ sequence.**
Let $\phi_n\to 0$ be an arbitrary deterministic sequence (no rate assumption). F
ix $\varepsilon>0$. Choose $M$ so that $\sup_n P(|W_n|>M) \le \varepsilon/2$. Si
nce $\phi_n\to 0$, there exists $N$ with $|\phi_n|\,M < \varepsilon$ for all $n\
ge N$. Then for $n\ge N$,
$$P(|\phi_n W_n| > \varepsilon) \le P(|W_n| > \varepsilon/|\phi_n|) \le P(|W_n|
> M) \le \varepsilon/2 < \varepsilon.$$
Hence $P(|\phi_n W_n|>\varepsilon)\to 0$; i.e. $\phi_n W_n \xrightarrow{p} 0$.

**Step 3 — Convergence in probability implies convergence in distribution.**
Since $\phi_n W_n \xrightarrow{p} 0$, in particular $\phi_n W_n \xrightarrow{d}
0$.

**Remark on the rate.** The argument uses only the *uniform* bound $\sup_n P(|W_
n|>M)\le\varepsilon/2$ supplied by tightness — it never requires any relationshi
p between the rate of $\phi_n\to 0$ and the rate at which $W_n$ approaches its l
imit. Therefore the conclusion holds for $\phi_n\to 0$ **arbitrarily slowly**: n
o matter how slow the decay of $\phi_n$, once $|\phi_n|<\varepsilon/M$ (which ev
entually happens for *any* sequence tending to $0$), the bound kicks in.

**Remark on the hypothesis $o(X_n-\theta)=(X_n-\theta)\cdot o(1)$.** This clarif
ies that little-$o$ terms attached to $X_n-\theta$ are understood in the multipl
icative probabilistic sense, i.e. any remainder of the form $o(X_n-\theta)$ is $
(X_n-\theta)\cdot o_p(1)$. Such remainders, when scaled by $a_n$, give $a_n(X_n-
\theta)\,o_p(1) = W_n^{(X)}\cdot o_p(1) = o_p(1)$ by the same tightness argument
 (where $W_n^{(X)}:=a_n(X_n-\theta)$ is $O_p(1)$ whenever $a_n(X_n-\theta)$ has
a weak limit, and in any case the difference $W_n$ is $O_p(1)$). Thus the little
-$o$ structure is consistent with — and absorbed by — the tightness argument abo
ve, and does not alter the conclusion.

### Conclusion

$$a_n(X_n-\theta)\,\phi_n - a_n(Y_n-\theta)\,\phi_n = \phi_n\,W_n \xrightarrow{p
} 0 \xrightarrow{d} 0,$$

for **any** deterministic $\phi_n\to 0$, however slowly. The determining fact is
 the tightness ($O_p(1)$) of $W_n = a_n(X_n-\theta)-a_n(Y_n-\theta)$, which is g
uaranteed by its convergence in distribution to $Z$, and which is rate-free with
 respect to $\phi_n$.
