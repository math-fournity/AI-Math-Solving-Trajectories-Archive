Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a "stable" partition (where
 no vertex can improve its score by switching groups or forming a singleton) alw
ays exists on a random signed complete graph.

**Answer: Yes, $p_n \to 1$. In fact, $p_n = 1$ for every $n$.**

## Proof

**Setup.** We have a complete graph $K_n$ where each edge $\{u,v\}$ has weight $
w(u,v) \in \{+1, -1\}$. A partition $\mathcal{P}$ divides vertices into groups.
The score of vertex $v$ in group $G$ is $S(v, G) = \sum_{u \in G \setminus \{v\}
} w(v, u)$. A partition is *stable* if no vertex can strictly improve its score
by either (a) switching to another existing group, or (b) forming a singleton gr
oup (which gives score $0$).

**Key tool: a potential function.** Define
$$\Phi(\mathcal{P}) = \sum_{G \in \mathcal{P}} \sum_{\{u,v\} \subseteq G} w(u,v)
,$$
the total weight of all intra-group edges.

**Claim:** When a single vertex $v$ moves from its current group $G$ to another
group $G'$ (or forms a new singleton group), the change in $\Phi$ equals the cha
nge in $v$'s score.

*Proof of claim.* When $v$ leaves $G$ and joins $G'$:
- The edges from $v$ to $G \setminus \{v\}$ are removed from the intra-group sum
: contribution changes by $-\sum_{u \in G \setminus \{v\}} w(v,u) = -S(v, G)$.
- The edges from $v$ to $G'$ become intra-group: contribution changes by $+\sum_
{u \in G'} w(v,u) = +S(v, G')$.
- All other edges are unaffected (they remain either intra-group or inter-group)
.

So $\Delta \Phi = S(v, G') - S(v, G)$, which is exactly the change in $v$'s scor
e. If $v$ forms a singleton, then $G' = \emptyset$ and $\Delta \Phi = 0 - S(v, G
) = -S(v, G)$, again matching the score change. $\square$

**Existence of a stable partition.** The set of all partitions of $n$ vertices i
s finite (it has $B_n$ elements, the $n$-th Bell number). Therefore $\Phi$ attai
ns a global maximum at some partition $\mathcal{P}^*$.

At $\mathcal{P}^*$, no single-vertex move can increase $\Phi$ (since it's a glob
al maximum). By the claim, this means no vertex can strictly improve its score b
y any single-vertex move—neither switching to another group nor forming a single
ton. Therefore $\mathcal{P}^*$ is stable.

**Conclusion.** A stable partition exists for *every* realization of edge weight
s, regardless of the random assignment. The probability is:
$$p_n = 1 \quad \text{for all } n \geq 1.$$

Therefore $p_n \to 1$ as $n \to \infty$. $\blacksquare$
