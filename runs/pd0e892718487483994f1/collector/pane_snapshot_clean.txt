## Solution

Place the triangle in coordinates with the right angle at the origin:
- $A = (0,0)$, $B = (b,0)$, $C = (0,c)$, so $\angle A = 90°$.

The semicircle has its diameter along $AB$ with center $O = (r,0)$ and radius $r
$ (diameter from $A=(0,0)$ to $(2r,0)$). The circle is tangent to $AC$ (the $y$-
axis) at $A$, since the distance from $O$ to the $y$-axis equals $r$. It is also
 tangent to $BC$ at $D$.

**Tangency to $BC$.** Line $BC$: $cx + by = bc$. Distance from $O=(r,0)$:
$$\frac{c(b-r)}{\sqrt{b^2+c^2}} = r. \tag{1}$$

**Point $D$** is the foot of the perpendicular from $O$ to $BC$. Projecting $O$
onto line $BC$:
$$D = B + \frac{(O-B)\cdot(C-B)}{|C-B|^2}(C-B) = (b,0) + \frac{b(b-r)}{b^2+c^2}(
-b,\,c),$$
$$D_x = \frac{b(c^2+br)}{b^2+c^2}, \qquad D_y = \frac{bc(b-r)}{b^2+c^2}.$$

**Computing $AD^2$:**
$$AD^2 = D_x^2 + D_y^2 = \frac{b^2}{(b^2+c^2)^2}\Big[(c^2+br)^2 + c^2(b-r)^2\Big
].$$
Expanding the bracket:
$$(c^2+br)^2 + c^2(b-r)^2 = c^4 + 2bc^2r + b^2r^2 + b^2c^2 - 2bc^2r + c^2r^2 = (
c^2+r^2)(b^2+c^2).$$
Therefore
$$AD^2 = \frac{b^2(c^2+r^2)}{b^2+c^2}. \tag{2}$$

**Using the given data.** Since $O=(r,0)$ and $C=(0,c)$:
$$CO^2 = r^2 + c^2 = 25. \tag{3}$$

Substituting (3) into (2) with $AD = 4$:
$$16 = \frac{b^2 \cdot 25}{b^2+c^2} \implies 16(b^2+c^2) = 25b^2 \implies 9b^2 =
 16c^2 \implies \frac{b}{c} = \frac{4}{3}.$$

**Finding $\cos\angle ABC$.** At vertex $B$, with $\vec{BA}=(-b,0)$ and $\vec{BC
}=(-b,c)$:
$$\cos\angle ABC = \frac{\vec{BA}\cdot\vec{BC}}{|\vec{BA}||\vec{BC}|} = \frac{b^
2}{b\sqrt{b^2+c^2}} = \frac{b}{\sqrt{b^2+c^2}}.$$

With $b = 4k$, $c = 3k$: $\sqrt{b^2+c^2} = 5k$, so
$$\cos\angle ABC = \frac{4k}{5k} = \frac{4}{5}.$$

**Consistency check.** From (1): $3k(4k-r)/(5k) = r \Rightarrow r = \tfrac{3k}{2
}$. Then $CO^2 = \tfrac{9k^2}{4} + 9k^2 = \tfrac{45k^2}{4} = 25$, giving $k = \t
frac{2\sqrt5}{3}$. Back-substituting into (2) yields $AD^2 = 16$. ✓

$$\boxed{\cos\angle ABC = \dfrac{4}{5}}$$

The answer is $\textbf{(D)}$.
