## Proof

Let $f \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ be irreducible of degree $5$, and let $K$ be its split
ting field over $\mathbb{Q}$. Let $G = \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$.

**Step 1: $G$ is a transitive subgroup of $S_5$.**

Since $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, $G$ acts transitively on the five ro
ots of $f$. Thus $G$ is (up to conjugation) one of the transitive subgroups of $
S_5$:
$$C_5,\quad D_5,\quad F_{20},\quad A_5,\quad S_5,$$
of orders $5, 10, 20, 60, 120$ respectively.

**Step 2: $G$ contains a transposition.**

Since $f$ has rational (hence real) coefficients, nonreal roots occur in conjuga
te pairs. The hypothesis "exactly two nonreal roots" means $f$ has $3$ real root
s $r_1, r_2, r_3$ and one conjugate pair $\alpha, \bar\alpha$ of nonreal roots.

Complex conjugation $\sigma: z \mapsto \bar z$ permutes the roots of $f$ (since
$f$ has real coefficients), hence maps the splitting field $K$ to itself. Theref
ore $\sigma|_K \in G$. As a permutation of the five roots, $\sigma$ fixes $r_1,
r_2, r_3$ and swaps $\alpha \leftrightarrow \bar\alpha$. This is a single transp
osition (a $2$-cycle).

**Step 3: Eliminate all transitive subgroups except $S_5$.**

We check which transitive subgroups of $S_5$ contain a transposition:

- **$C_5$**: contains only the identity and $5$-cycles. No transposition.
- **$D_5$**: its non-identity elements are $5$-cycles and products of two disjoi
nt transpositions (e.g. $(25)(34)$). No single transposition.
- **$F_{20} \cong C_5 \rtimes C_4$**: the involutions arise from the order-$2$ a
utomorphism of $C_5$ ($x \mapsto x^{-1}$), which acts as a product of two disjoi
nt transpositions (e.g. $(25)(34)$). No single transposition.
- **$A_5$**: contains only even permutations; a transposition is odd, so $A_5$ c
ontains no transposition.
- **$S_5$**: contains transpositions.

The only transitive subgroup of $S_5$ containing a transposition is $S_5$ itself
.

**Conclusion.**

Since $G$ is a transitive subgroup of $S_5$ containing a transposition, we must
have
$$\boxed{G = \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_5}.$$
