**Answer: Yes, $V$ is semisimple.**

---

**Proof.**

First, no answer is leaked in the problem statement (it only poses the question)
, so we proceed.

**Step 1 — Reduction to a finite-dimensional algebra.** Since $V$ is finite-dime
nsional over $k$, the representation $\rho\colon kG\to\operatorname{End}_k(V)$ h
as finite-dimensional image. Set
$$A:=\rho(kG)\subseteq\operatorname{End}_k(V).$$
Then $A$ is a finite-dimensional $k$-algebra, $V$ is a faithful $A$-module, and
the $kG$-submodules of $V$ are exactly the $A$-submodules of $V$. Hence
$$V\text{ is semisimple as a }kG\text{-module}\iff V\text{ is semisimple as an }
A\text{-module}.$$

**Step 2 — Base change of the image algebra.** Under base change to $k^a$, the i
mage of $k^aG$ in $\operatorname{End}_{k^a}(V_{k^a})=\operatorname{End}_{k^a}(k^
a\otimes_k V)\cong k^a\otimes_k\operatorname{End}_k(V)$ is
$$k^a\otimes_k \rho(kG)=k^a\otimes_k A=:A_{k^a}.$$
So the $k^aG$-submodules of $V_{k^a}$ are exactly the $A_{k^a}$-submodules, and
$$V_{k^a}\text{ is semisimple as a }k^aG\text{-module}\iff V_{k^a}\text{ is semi
simple as an }A_{k^a}\text{-module}.$$

**Step 3 — Semisimplicity via the Jacobson radical.** For a finite-dimensional a
lgebra $B$ over a field and a finite-dimensional $B$-module $M$, we have the sta
ndard criterion:
$$M\text{ is semisimple}\iff J(B)\cdot M=0,$$
where $J(B)$ is the Jacobson radical.

*Proof of criterion:* If $M$ is semisimple, it is a direct sum of simple modules
, each annihilated by $J(B)$ (since $J(B)$ is the intersection of annihilators o
f all simple $B$-modules), so $J(B)M=0$. Conversely, if $J(B)M=0$, then $M$ is a
 module over the semisimple algebra $B/J(B)$ (semisimple by Wedderburn–Artin), h
ence $M$ is semisimple as a $B$-module.

Applying this:
$$V_{k^a}\text{ semisimple over }A_{k^a}\iff J(A_{k^a})\cdot V_{k^a}=0.$$

**Step 4 — Behavior of the radical under separable base change.** Since $\operat
orname{char}k=0$, the field $k$ is perfect, and the algebraic extension $k^a/k$
is separable. A classical theorem on the Jacobson radical states:

> If $A$ is a finite-dimensional algebra over $k$ and $K/k$ is a separable field
 extension, then
> $$J(A\otimes_k K)=J(A)\otimes_k K.$$

(In general one only has $J(A)\otimes_k K\subseteq J(A\otimes_k K)$; equality ho
lds for separable extensions, and in particular for any extension of a perfect f
ield.)

Applying this with $K=k^a$:
$$J(A_{k^a})=J(A)\otimes_k k^a.$$

**Step 5 — Conclusion.** Combining Steps 3 and 4, the hypothesis that $V_{k^a}$
is semisimple gives
$$0=J(A_{k^a})\cdot V_{k^a}=\bigl(J(A)\otimes_k k^a\bigr)\cdot\bigl(V\otimes_k k
^a\bigr)=\bigl(J(A)\cdot V\bigr)\otimes_k k^a.$$
Since $-\otimes_k k^a$ is a faithful functor on $k$-vector spaces (a map is zero
 iff its base change is zero), we deduce
$$J(A)\cdot V=0.$$
By the criterion of Step 3, $V$ is semisimple as an $A$-module, hence semisimple
 as a $kG$-module by Step 1.

Therefore $V$ is semisimple. $\blacksquare$
