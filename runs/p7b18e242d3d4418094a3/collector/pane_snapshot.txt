## Solution

This is a two-dimensional electrostatics problem solved by the **method of image
s** for two perpendicular grounded conducting planes.

### Setup

A line charge $+\lambda$ sits at $\mathbf{r}_0 = (x_0, y_0)$ with $x_0, y_0 > 0$
. The planes $x=0$ and $y=0$ are grounded ($V=0$). We work in 2D (per unit lengt
h), so the potential of a line charge $\lambda'$ at distance $d$ is $V = -\frac{
\lambda'}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\ln d$.

### Image charges

To enforce $V=0$ on both planes simultaneously, we place **three** image line ch
arges:

| Image | Charge | Position |
|-------|--------|----------|
| 1 | $-\lambda$ | $(-x_0,\, y_0)$ |
| 2 | $-\lambda$ | $(x_0,\, -y_0)$ |
| 3 | $+\lambda$ | $(-x_0,\, -y_0)$ |

**Verification on $x=0$:** For any point $(0,y)$ on this plane, the real charge
$(x_0,y_0)$ and image $(-x_0,y_0)$ are equidistant with opposite charges $\Right
arrow$ cancel; images $(x_0,-y_0)$ and $(-x_0,-y_0)$ are equidistant with charge
s $-\lambda,+\lambda$ $\Rightarrow$ cancel. So $V=0$. ✓

**Verification on $y=0$:** By the symmetric argument (real with image 2; image 1
 with image 3), $V=0$. ✓

By uniqueness, the field in the quarter-space equals the field of the real charg
e plus all three images.

### Force computation

The force per unit length on the real charge equals the force exerted by the thr
ee image charges (the induced-charge field is reproduced by the image field at t
he real charge's location). For a line charge $\lambda'$ at $\mathbf{r}'$, the f
ield at $\mathbf{r}$ is

$$\mathbf{E} = \frac{\lambda'}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\,\frac{\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'}
{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|^2},\qquad \mathbf{F}/L = \lambda\,\mathbf{E}.$$

**Image 1** ($-\lambda$ at $(-x_0,y_0)$): $\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'=(2x_0,0)$, $|\
cdot|^2=4x_0^2$:
$$\frac{\mathbf{F}_1}{L} = \frac{\lambda(-\lambda)}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{(2x_
0,0)}{4x_0^2} = \left(-\frac{\lambda^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 x_0},\;0\right).$$

**Image 2** ($-\lambda$ at $(x_0,-y_0)$): $\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'=(0,2y_0)$, $|\
cdot|^2=4y_0^2$:
$$\frac{\mathbf{F}_2}{L} = \left(0,\;-\frac{\lambda^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 y_0}\ri
ght).$$

**Image 3** ($+\lambda$ at $(-x_0,-y_0)$): $\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'=(2x_0,2y_0)$,
 $|\cdot|^2=4(x_0^2+y_0^2)$:
$$\frac{\mathbf{F}_3}{L} = \frac{\lambda^2}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{(2x_0,2y_0)}
{4(x_0^2+y_0^2)} = \left(\frac{\lambda^2 x_0}{4\pi\varepsilon_0(x_0^2+y_0^2)},\;
\frac{\lambda^2 y_0}{4\pi\varepsilon_0(x_0^2+y_0^2)}\right).$$

### Summing components

$$\frac{F_x}{L} = \frac{\lambda^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left[-\frac{1}{x_0}+\frac{
x_0}{x_0^2+y_0^2}\right] = \frac{\lambda^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\cdot\frac{-(x_0^2
+y_0^2)+x_0^2}{x_0(x_0^2+y_0^2)} = -\frac{\lambda^2\,y_0^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\,x
_0\,(x_0^2+y_0^2)}.$$

$$\frac{F_y}{L} = \frac{\lambda^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left[-\frac{1}{y_0}+\frac{
y_0}{x_0^2+y_0^2}\right] = \frac{\lambda^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\cdot\frac{-(x_0^2
+y_0^2)+y_0^2}{y_0(x_0^2+y_0^2)} = -\frac{\lambda^2\,x_0^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\,y
_0\,(x_0^2+y_0^2)}.$$

### Result

The force per unit length on the line charge is

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \frac{\mathbf{F}}{L} = -\frac{\lambda^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_
0\,(x_0^2+y_0^2)}\left(\frac{y_0^2}{x_0}\,\hat{\mathbf{x}} \;+\; \frac{x_0^2}{y_
0}\,\hat{\mathbf{y}}\right)}$$

i.e.

$$\frac{F_x}{L} = -\frac{\lambda^2\,y_0^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\,x_0\,(x_0^2+y_0^2)
},\qquad \frac{F_y}{L} = -\frac{\lambda^2\,x_0^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\,y_0\,(x_0^2
+y_0^2)}.$$

**Physical check:** Both components are negative, so the force points toward the
 corner (the origin). This is correct: the grounded plates acquire induced charg
e of opposite sign to $\lambda$, producing an attractive force pulling the line
charge toward the nearest plate and ultimately toward the corner. Consistency li
mits: as $y_0\to\infty$ the $x$-component reduces to the single-plane result $-\
lambda^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 x_0)$ (only plane $x=0$ matters), and similarly for
$x_0\to\infty$. ✓
