## Solution

**Claim:** $\bigcap_{v=0}^{\infty} G^v = \{1\}$, i.e., the intersection is trivi
al.

### Setup and key structural facts

Let $K$ be a $p$-adic local field with absolute Galois group $G = G_K = \operato
rname{Gal}(\bar{K}/K)$. The ramification groups in upper numbering $G^v$ ($v \ge
q -1$) are defined for $G$ as inverse limits of the corresponding groups for fin
ite quotients: if $G = \varprojlim_{L/K \text{ finite Galois}} \operatorname{Gal
}(L/K)$, then
$$G^v = \varprojlim \operatorname{Gal}(L/K)^v.$$

Recall the standard structural facts:

1. **$G^{-1} = G$** and **$G^0 = I_K$**, the inertia group.
2. **$G^{0+} := \bigcup_{v > 0} G^v = P_K$**, the wild inertia group, which is a
 pro-$p$ group (the unique pro-$p$ Sylow subgroup of $I_K$).
3. For every $v > 0$, $G^v$ is a **closed normal subgroup** of $G$, and $G^v \su
bseteq P_K \subseteq I_K = G^0$.

### The crucial property: $\{G^v\}_{v>0}$ is a fundamental system of neighborhoo
ds of the identity in $P_K$

This is a foundational result in local class field theory / ramification theory
(see Serre, *Local Fields*, or the standard treatment of the Hasse–Arf theory).
Concretely:

- Each $G^v$ for $v > 0$ is **open** in $P_K$ (equivalently, of finite index in
$P_K$). This follows because for any $v > 0$, one can find a finite Galois exten
sion $L/K$ such that the image of $G^v$ in $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ is the corr
esponding upper-numbering ramification group, which is a finite-index (hence ope
n) subgroup; the inverse-limit construction preserves openness in the profinite
topology.

- The family $\{G^v\}_{v > 0}$ is **cofinal** among open normal subgroups of $P_
K$: every open normal subgroup of $P_K$ contains some $G^v$. This is because $P_
K$ is topologically generated (as a pro-$p$ group) by elements whose "ramificati
on number" is finite, and the $G^v$ filter precisely by this number.

Hence $\{G^v\}_{v > 0}$ forms a **fundamental system of neighborhoods of the ide
ntity** in the profinite group $P_K$.

### Conclusion via the Hausdorff property

$P_K$ is a profinite group, hence a **compact Hausdorff topological group**. In
any Hausdorff topological group, the intersection of all neighborhoods of the id
entity is $\{1\}$:
$$\bigcap_{\substack{U \ni 1 \\ U \text{ open}}} U = \{1\}.$$

Since $\{G^v\}_{v>0}$ is a fundamental system of neighborhoods of the identity i
n $P_K$,
$$\bigcap_{v > 0} G^v = \{1\}.$$

Now observe that $G^v \subseteq G^0 = I_K$ for all $v > 0$, so
$$\bigcap_{v=0}^{\infty} G^v = G^0 \cap \bigcap_{v>0} G^v = I_K \cap \{1\} = \{1
\}.$$

### Justification summary

The intersection $\bigcap_{v=0}^{\infty} G^v$ equals $\bigcap_{v>0} G^v$ (since
$G^v \subseteq G^0$ for $v > 0$). The groups $\{G^v\}_{v>0}$ form a fundamental
system of open neighborhoods of the identity in the wild inertia group $P_K$, wh
ich is profinite and therefore Hausdorff. In a Hausdorff topological group, the
intersection of all neighborhoods of the identity is the trivial group. Hence $\
bigcap_{v=0}^{\infty} G^v = \{1\}$.
