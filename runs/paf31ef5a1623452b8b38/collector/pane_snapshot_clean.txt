## Solution

The statement is **false**.

### Counterexample

Define $g_n(x) = \sqrt{x^2 + \tfrac{1}{n}}$ for $n \geq 1$, and let $b = 0$.

**Each $g_n$ is differentiable everywhere (in particular at $b=0$).** Indeed $g_
n$ is smooth on all of $\mathbb{R}$, since $x^2 + \tfrac{1}{n} > 0$ for all $x$,
 and
$$g_n'(x) = \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + \tfrac{1}{n}}}.$$
At $x = 0$: $g_n'(0) = 0$, so each $g_n$ is differentiable at $b = 0$.

**The limit function is $g(x) = |x|$.** For every $x \in \mathbb{R}$,
$$\sqrt{x^2 + \tfrac{1}{n}} \;\longrightarrow\; \sqrt{x^2} = |x|.$$

**The convergence is uniform on every $[-N, N]$ (in fact on all of $\mathbb{R}$)
.** We have
$$\bigl|g_n(x) - |x|\bigr| = \sqrt{x^2 + \tfrac{1}{n}} - |x| = \frac{\tfrac{1}{n
}}{\sqrt{x^2 + \tfrac{1}{n}} + |x|} \leq \frac{\tfrac{1}{n}}{\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{n}}
} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}},$$
where the inequality uses $\sqrt{x^2+\tfrac{1}{n}} + |x| \geq \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{n}
}$. The bound $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$ is independent of $x$, so $g_n \to g$ unifor
mly on $\mathbb{R}$, hence certainly uniformly on every $[-N, N]$.

**But $g(x) = |x|$ is not differentiable at $b = 0$.** The left derivative at $0
$ is $-1$ and the right derivative is $+1$.

### Conclusion

All hypotheses are satisfied — uniform convergence on every compact interval $[-
N,N]$, and differentiability of every $g_n$ at $b = 0$ — yet the limit $g = |\,\
cdot\,|$ fails to be differentiable at $b = 0$. Therefore the statement is **fal
se**.
