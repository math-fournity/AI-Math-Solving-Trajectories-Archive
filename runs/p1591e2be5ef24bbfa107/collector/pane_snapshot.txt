### I CANNOT SOLVE THIS

This is Erdős Problem 463, explicitly marked as `category research open` in the
formalization. It is a genuine open problem in analytic/combinatorial number the
ory.

**The problem asks:** Does there exist $f(n)\to\infty$ such that for all suffici
ently large $n$, there is a composite $m$ with $n+f(n) < m < n+p(m)$, where $p(m
)$ is the least prime factor of $m$?

**Key structural observations I made:**

1. **Size constraint:** Since $m$ is composite, $p(m) \le \sqrt{m} \approx \sqrt
{n}$, so we need $k := m-n < \sqrt{n}$. Conversely, any composite $m \le n+K$ wi
th all prime factors $> K$ must satisfy $m \ge (K+1)^2$, so we need $K \gtrsim \
sqrt{n}$. Thus the viable range for $k$ is roughly $(f(n),\, \sqrt{n})$.

2. **Reformulation:** We need, for each large $n$, a "rough composite" $m=n+k$ (
all prime factors $> k$) with $f(n) < k \lesssim \sqrt{n}$. The most efficient s
uch $m$ are semiprimes $m=pq$ with $\min(p,q) > k$.

3. **Semiprime reformulation:** The condition becomes finding primes $p \le q$ w
ith $n+f(n) < pq < n+p$, i.e., $pq \in (n+f(n),\, n+p)$, an interval of length $
p - f(n)$.

**Heuristic analysis strongly suggests the answer is "yes":**

- For primes $p \in (f(n), \sqrt{n})$, each gives one candidate $q = \lceil n/p
\rceil$ that must be prime (probability $\sim 1/\log(n/p)$).
- The expected number of suitable semiprimes is $\sim \sum_{p \in (f(n),\sqrt{n}
)} \frac{1}{\log(n/p)} \approx \frac{4\sqrt{n}}{(\log n)^2} \to \infty$.
- This suggests $f(n)$ could even grow nearly as fast as $\sqrt{n}$, far exceedi
ng the mere $f(n)\to\infty$ required.

**Why a rigorous proof is beyond reach (and why this is open):**

- Proving the existence of rough composites (or semiprimes with a large least fa
ctor) in **short intervals for every $n$** runs directly into the **parity barri
er** of sieve theory — sieve methods alone cannot distinguish primes
