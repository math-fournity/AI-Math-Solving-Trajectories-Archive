## Proof

**Claim.** For every $q \in [1,\infty]$, the graph $G(B)=\{(v,v'):v\in W^{1,q}(0
,1),\, v(1)=0\}$ is closed in $L^{q}(0,1)\times L^{q}(0,1)$.

**Key facts used.**

1. **Completeness.** $W^{1,q}(0,1)$ is a Banach space for every $q\in[1,\infty]$
, with norm $\|v\|_{W^{1,q}}=\|v\|_{L^{q}}+\|v'\|_{L^{q}}$.

2. **Continuous trace in one dimension.** For every $q\in[1,\infty]$, the pointw
ise evaluation (trace) map
$$T:W^{1,q}(0,1)\to\mathbb{R},\qquad T(v)=v(1)$$
is a bounded linear operator. Indeed, every $v\in W^{1,q}(0,1)$ has an absolutel
y continuous representative satisfying $v(1)=v(x)+\int_{x}^{1}v'(t)\,dt$ for a.e
. $x$; integrating in $x$ over $(0,1)$ (or using the essential-supremum bound wh
en $q=\infty$) gives
$$|v(1)|\le \|v\|_{L^{q}}+\|v'\|_{L^{q}}=\|v\|_{W^{1,q}},$$
so $T$ is bounded with $\|T\|\le 1$.

**Closedness argument.** Let $(v_{n},Bv_{n})=(v_{n},v_{n}')$ be a sequence in $G
(B)$ converging in $L^{q}\times L^{q}$ to some $(f,g)$, i.e.
$$v_{n}\to f\ \text{in }L^{q},\qquad v_{n}'\to g\ \text{in }L^{q}.$$

Then $(v_{n})$ is Cauchy in $W^{1,q}$, since
$$\|v_{n}-v_{m}\|_{W^{1,q}}=\|v_{n}-v_{m}\|_{L^{q}}+\|v_{n}'-v_{m}'\|_{L^{q}}\to
 0.$$
By completeness of $W^{1,q}$, there exists $u\in W^{1,q}(0,1)$ with $v_{n}\to u$
 in $W^{1,q}$. Convergence in $W^{1,q}$ implies convergence in $L^{q}$ of both t
he function and its derivative, so by uniqueness of limits
$$u=f\ \text{in }L^{q},\qquad u'=g\ \text{in }L^{q}.$$

It remains to check the boundary condition. Since $T$ is bounded (hence continuo
us),
$$u(1)=T(u)=\lim_{n\to\infty}T(v_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}v_{n}(1)=0.$$
Therefore $u\in D(B)$ and $Bu=u'=g$, which gives
$$(f,g)=(u,u')\in G(B).$$

Since the limit of every convergent sequence in $G(B)$ again lies in $G(B)$, the
 graph $G(B)$ is closed in $L^{q}(0,1)\times L^{q}(0,1)$.

**Remark on the role of the boundary condition.** The condition $v(1)=0$ is esse
ntial: it is preserved under $W^{1,q}$-convergence precisely because the one-dim
ensional trace map is continuous for every $q\ge 1$. Without such a condition fi
xing the additive constant, the derivative operator on $W^{1,q}$ would have a no
n-closed graph in $L^{q}\times L^{q}$ (constants can be added in the limit witho
ut affecting the derivative). The anchor $v(1)=0$ removes this kernel and yields
 a closed graph.
