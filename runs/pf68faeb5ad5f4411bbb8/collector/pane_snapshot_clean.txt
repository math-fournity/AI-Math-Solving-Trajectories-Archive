## Proof

Consider the integral
$$I = \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} \langle x, b\rangle^3 \, e^{-\frac{1}{4}|x|^2}\, dx, \
qquad x, b \in \mathbb{R}^3.$$

**Symmetry argument.** The domain $\mathbb{R}^3$ is invariant under the involuti
on $x \mapsto -x$. Under this change of variables:

- The Gaussian weight is even: $e^{-\frac{1}{4}|-x|^2} = e^{-\frac{1}{4}|x|^2}$.
- The cubic factor is odd: $\langle -x, b\rangle^3 = (-\langle x, b\rangle)^3 =
-\langle x, b\rangle^3$.
- The volume element is preserved: $d(-x) = dx$.

Hence the full integrand $f(x) = \langle x, b\rangle^3 e^{-\frac{1}{4}|x|^2}$ sa
tisfies
$$f(-x) = -f(x),$$
i.e. $f$ is an **odd** function on the symmetric domain $\mathbb{R}^3$.

**Convergence.** The Gaussian factor $e^{-\frac{1}{4}|x|^2}$ decays super-polyno
mially, so $|f|$ is integrable and $I$ is absolutely convergent. Therefore the s
ymmetry argument applies rigorously (no cancellation-of-divergences subtlety):

$$I = \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} f(x)\,dx = \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} f(-x)\,dx = \int_{\math
bb{R}^3} (-f(x))\,dx = -I.$$

Thus $2I = 0$, giving

$$\boxed{I = 0}.$$

**Verification via moments (sanity check).** Decompose $x = (x_1, x_2, x_3)$ and
 $b = (b_1, b_2, b_3)$. Then
$$\langle x, b\rangle^3 = \Big(\sum_{i=1}^3 b_i x_i\Big)^3 = \sum_{i,j,k} b_i b_
j b_k\, x_i x_j x_k.$$
Every monomial $x_i x_j x_k$ with the Gaussian weight $e^{-\frac{1}{4}|x|^2}$ in
tegrates to $0$ over $\mathbb{R}^3$ unless each index appears an even number of
times (standard Gaussian moment fact). With three factors (odd total degree), at
 least one index must appear an odd number of times, so every term vanishes. Thi
s independently confirms $I = 0$.
