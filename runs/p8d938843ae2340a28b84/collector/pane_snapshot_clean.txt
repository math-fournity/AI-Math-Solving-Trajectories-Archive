Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the identity connected comp
onent $G'$ of the isometry group preserving a compact set $K \subseteq \mathbb{R
}^3$ must be isomorphic to $SO(k)$ for some $k \in \{1,2,3\}$.

**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

### Step 1: $G$ is a compact Lie group.

The Euclidean group $E(3) = \mathbb{R}^3 \rtimes O(3)$ consists of isometries $f
_{A,b}(x) = Ax + b$ with $A \in O(3)$, $b \in \mathbb{R}^3$.

**$G$ is closed:** If $f_{A_n, b_n} \to f_{A,b}$ in $E(3)$ and $f_{A_n,b_n}(K) =
 K$ for all $n$, then since convergence in $E(3)$ is uniform on compact sets and
 $K$ is compact, $f_{A,b}(K) = K$.

**$G$ is bounded as a subset of $E(3)$:** Since $K$ is compact, $K \subseteq B(0
,R)$ for some $R$. For any $f_{A,b} \in G$, we have $AK + b = K$, so $b \in K -
AK \subseteq B(0, 2R)$ (using that $A$ orthogonal preserves norms). Thus the tra
nslation parts are uniformly bounded, and $O(3)$ is already compact. So $G \subs
eteq O(3) \times B(0,2R)$, a compact set. Being a closed subset of a compact set
, $G$ is compact.

### Step 2: $G$ injects into $O(3)$.

Consider the projection $\pi: E(3) \to O(3)$, $\pi(f_{A,b}) = A$, a Lie group ho
momorphism with kernel $\mathbb{R}^3$ (the translation subgroup). The restrictio
n $\pi|_G: G \to O(3)$ has kernel $G \cap \mathbb{R}^3$, which is a compact subg
roup of $\mathbb{R}^3$. The only compact subgroup of $(\mathbb{R}^3, +)$ is $\{0
\}$, so $\pi|_G$ is injective.

Since $G$ is compact, $\pi(G)$ is a compact (hence closed) subgroup of $O(3)$, a
nd $\pi|_G: G \xrightarrow{\sim} \pi(G)$ is a Lie group isomorphism.

### Step 3: $G'$ is a connected closed subgroup of $SO(3)$.

The identity component $G'$ maps isomorphically to the identity component of $\p
i(G) \subseteq O(3)$. Since $SO(3)$ is the identity component of $O(3)$, any con
nected subgroup of $O(3)$ lies in $SO(3)$. Thus $G' \cong (\pi(G))_0$, a connect
ed closed subgroup of $SO(3)$.

### Step 4: Classification of connected closed subgroups of $SO(3)$.

Let $H$ be a connected closed subgroup of $SO(3)$. Since $\dim SO(3) = 3$, we ha
ve $\dim H \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$.

- **$\dim H = 0$:** $H$ is connected and 0-dimensional, so $H = \{e\} \cong SO(1
)$.

- **$\dim H = 1$:** A 1-dimensional connected compact Lie group is $S^1$. Its Li
e algebra $\mathfrak{h}$ is a 1-dimensional subalgebra of $\mathfrak{so}(3)$; ev
ery 1-dimensional subspace of a Lie algebra is a subalgebra. In $\mathfrak{so}(3
)$, each such subalgebra is spanned by an infinitesimal rotation about some axis
, and the corresponding connected subgroup is $SO(2)$ (rotations about that axis
). So $H \cong SO(2)$.

- **$\dim H = 2$:** A 2-dimensional connected compact Lie group must be a torus
$T^2$ (the only 2-dimensional compact Lie algebra is abelian, since the non-abel
ian 2-dimensional Lie algebra is solvable-non-nilpotent and cannot be the Lie al
gebra of a compact group). But $T^2$ has rank 2, while the maximal torus of $SO(
3)$ is $SO(2) \cong S^1$ (rank 1). A rank-2 torus cannot embed in a rank-1 compa
ct group. Contradiction — so no such $H$ exists.

- **$\dim H = 3$:** $H$ is an open subgroup of the connected group $SO(3)$. Sinc
e $H$ is connected, $H = SO(3)$.

### Step 5: Conclusion.

The connected closed subgroups of $SO(3)$ are exactly $\{e\} \cong SO(1)$, $SO(2
)$, and $SO(3)$. Since $G'$ is isomorphic to one of these, $G' \cong SO(k)$ for
some $k \in \{1, 2, 3\}$.

**All three cases are realized:** $K = \{0\}$ gives $G' \cong SO(3)$; a line seg
ment gives $G' \cong SO(2)$; a generic asymmetric compact set gives $G' \cong SO
(1)$.
