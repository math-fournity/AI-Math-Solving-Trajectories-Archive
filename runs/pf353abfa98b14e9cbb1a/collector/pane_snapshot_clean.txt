## Proof

**Claim:** The condition implies that $L$ is the trivial line bundle, i.e., $L \
cong \mathcal{O}_X$.

**Key observation.** For a line bundle $L$ on $X$, a section $s \in \Gamma(U, L)
$ over an open set $U$ is nowhere vanishing on $U$ (i.e., $V(s) = \varnothing$)
if and only if $s$ trivializes $L|_U$. Concretely, a nowhere-vanishing section $
s$ defines an $\mathcal{O}_U$-module isomorphism
$$
\varphi_s : \mathcal{O}_U \longrightarrow L|_U, \qquad f \longmapsto f \cdot s,
$$
whose inverse sends a local section $t$ of $L|_U$ to the unique function $f$ wit
h $t = f \cdot s$ (uniqueness and existence following from the fact that $s$ gen
erates $L|_U$ as an $\mathcal{O}_U$-module at every point, since $s(x) \neq 0$ m
eans $s$ is a basis of the one-dimensional fiber $L_x$).

**Applying the hypothesis.** The hypothesis states that for **every** open subse
t $U \subseteq X$, there exists a section $s \in \Gamma(U,L)$ with $V(s) = \varn
othing$. By the observation above, this means $L|_U \cong \mathcal{O}_U$ for eve
ry open $U$.

**Specializing to $U = X$.** Taking $U = X$ in particular, there exists a global
 section $s \in \Gamma(X, L)$ that is nowhere vanishing on all of $X$. This glob
al trivialization gives
$$
L \cong \mathcal{O}_X.
$$

**Conclusion.** The hypothesis forces $L$ to be globally trivial. Equivalently,
$L$ is isomorphic to the structure sheaf $\mathcal{O}_X$; its class in $\operato
rname{Pic}(X)$ is the identity. $\blacksquare$
