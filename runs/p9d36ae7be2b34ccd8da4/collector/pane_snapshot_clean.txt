**Problem.** Calculate $\mathbb{E}[W(s)\,e^{W(s)}]$, where $W(s)$ is a standard
Brownian motion at time $s$.

**Proof.**

Since $W$ is a standard Brownian motion, at any fixed time $s \geq 0$ the random
 variable $W(s)$ is normally distributed with mean $0$ and variance $s$:

$$W(s) \sim N(0, s).$$

Write $X = W(s)$, so $X \sim N(0,s)$. We seek $\mathbb{E}[X\,e^{X}]$.

Consider the moment generating function of $X$:

$$M(t) := \mathbb{E}[e^{tX}].$$

For $X \sim N(0,s)$, a standard computation gives

$$M(t) = \exp\!\left(\tfrac{s\,t^{2}}{2}\right).$$

Indeed, completing the square in the Gaussian integral,
$$M(t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi s}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{tx}\,e^{-x^{2}/(2s)}\
,dx = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi s}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-(x-ts)^{2}/(2s)}\,e^{st
^{2}/2}\,dx = e^{st^{2}/2}.$$

Differentiating under the expectation (justified since $e^{tX}$ is dominated by
an integrable function on any bounded interval of $t$, as $X$ is Gaussian):

$$M'(t) = \mathbb{E}[X\,e^{tX}].$$

On the other hand, differentiating the closed form,

$$M'(t) = \frac{d}{dt}\,e^{st^{2}/2} = s\,t\,e^{st^{2}/2}.$$

Equating and evaluating at $t = 1$:

$$\mathbb{E}[X\,e^{X}] = M'(1) = s\cdot 1 \cdot e^{s/2} = s\,e^{s/2}.$$

Substituting back $X = W(s)$:

$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}[W(s)\,e^{W(s)}] = s\,e^{s/2}.}$$
