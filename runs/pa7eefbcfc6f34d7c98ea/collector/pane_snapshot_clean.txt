### Step 1. Separate variables

Seek solutions of the form $u(x,t)=X(x)\,T(t)$. Substituting into

$$\alpha(t)\,w(x)\,u_t-\bigl(r(x)\,u_x\bigr)_x+p(x)\,u=0$$

gives

$$\alpha(t)\,w(x)\,X\,T'-T\,\bigl(r\,X'\bigr)'+p\,X\,T=0.$$

Dividing by $X\,T$ and separating the $t$- and $x$-dependent parts:

$$\alpha(t)\,\frac{T'}{T}=\frac{(r\,X')'-p\,X}{w\,X}=-\lambda,\qquad \lambda\in\
mathbb{R}.$$

This yields two ordinary differential equations.

**(Temporal equation):**

$$\alpha(t)\,T'(t)+\lambda\,T(t)=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T(t)=T(0)\,\exp\!\Bigl(-\lambda\int_0^t\frac{ds}{\alpha(s)}\Bigr). \tag{1}$$

**(Spatial equation — Sturm–Liouville eigenvalue problem):**

$$-\bigl(r(x)\,X'(x)\bigr)'+p(x)\,X(x)=\lambda\,w(x)\,X(x),\qquad a\le x\le b, \
tag{2}$$

with the **homogeneous boundary conditions** inherited from those on $u$:

$$\alpha_0\,X(a)+\alpha_1\,X'(a)=0,\qquad \beta_0\,X(b)+\beta_1\,X'(b)=0. \tag{3
}$$

### Step 2. Properties of the Sturm–Liouville problem

Assume the standard regularity hypotheses for a regular Sturm–Liouville system:

$$r\in C^1[a,b],\quad p,w\in C[a,b],\quad r(x)>0,\quad w(x)>0\quad\text{on }[a,b
],$$

and that the boundary conditions (3) are **separated** (each involves only one e
ndpoint), which is the case here since $\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\beta_0,\beta_1\ge 0$
and not both coefficients at an endpoint vanish simultaneously.

Under these hypotheses the problem (2)–(3) is a **regular self-adjoint Sturm–Lio
uville system**. The classical theory gives:

1. **Real, discrete spectrum.** There exists an infinite sequence of eigenvalues
$$\lambda_1<\lambda_2<\lambda_3<\cdots,\qquad \lambda_n\to+\infty,$$
each of geometric multiplicity one.

2. **Orthogonal eigenfunctions.** The corresponding eigenfunctions $\{X_n\}_{n=1
}^\infty$ are orthogonal with respect to the weight $w$:
$$\int_a^b w(x)\,X_m(x)\,X_n(x)\,dx=0,\qquad m\neq n. \tag{4}$$

3. **Completeness.** The eigenfunctions form a complete orthogonal system in $L^
2_w(a,b)$. Hence any $f\in L^2_w(a,b)$ admits the expansion
$$f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty c_n\,X_n(x),\qquad
c_n=\frac{\displaystyle\int_a^b w(x)\,f(x)\,X_n(x)\,dx}
{\displaystyle\int_a^b w(x)\,X_n^2(x)\,dx}. \tag{5}$$

*Self-adjointness check.* For two eigenfunctions $X_m,X_n$,
$$\int_a^b\!\bigl[X_m\,LX_n-X_n\,LX_m\bigr]dx
=\Bigl[r\bigl(X_mX_n'-X_nX_m'\bigr)\Bigr]_a^b.$$
At $x=a$: $\alpha_0X_n(a)+\alpha_1X_n'(a)=0$ and the same for $X_m$; since $(\al
pha_0,\alpha_1)\neq(0,0)$, the vectors $(X_n(a),X_n'(a))$ and $(X_m(a),X_m'(a))$
 are both proportional to $(\alpha_1,-\alpha_0)$, so $X_mX_n'-X_nX_m'=0$ at $a$.
 Similarly at $b$. Hence the boundary term vanishes, confirming self-adjointness
 and the orthogonality (4).

### Step 3. Build the general solution

For each eigenpair $(\lambda_n,X_n)$, the temporal factor from (1) is

$$T_n(t)=\exp\!\Bigl(-\lambda_n\int_0^t\frac{ds}{\alpha(s)}\Bigr),$$

where we have normalized $T_n(0)=1$. By linearity, the general solution of the P
DE satisfying the boundary conditions is

$$u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty c_n\,X_n(x)\,
\exp\!\Bigl(-\lambda_n\int_0^t\frac{ds}{\alpha(s)}\Bigr). \tag{6}$$

Each term individually satisfies the PDE (by construction) and the homogeneous b
oundary conditions (since each $X_n$ does); linearity preserves both properties.

### Step 4. Impose the initial condition

Setting $t=0$ in (6) and using $T_n(0)=1$:

$$u(x,0)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty c_n\,X_n(x)=f(x).$$

By the completeness relation (5), this is satisfied precisely when

$$\boxed{\;c_n=\frac{\displaystyle\int_a^b w(x)\,f(x)\,X_n(x)\,dx}
{\displaystyle\int_a^b w(x)\,X_n^2(x)\,dx}\;}. \tag{7}$$
