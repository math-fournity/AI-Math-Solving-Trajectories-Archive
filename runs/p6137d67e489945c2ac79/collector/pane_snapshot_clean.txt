## Solution

**Setup.** The rationals in $[0,1]$ have Lebesgue measure zero, so they contribu
te nothing to the integral. We work only with irrationals, whose decimal expansi
ons are unique (non-terminating, non-repeating).

**Decomposition by leading-zero count.** For an irrational $x = 0.d_1 d_2 d_3 \c
dots \in (0,1]$, let $n(x)$ be the number of leading zeros before the first non-
zero digit. Define
$$A_n = \{x \in (0,1] \setminus \mathbb{Q} : n(x) = n\}.$$

The condition $n(x) = n$ means $d_1 = \cdots = d_n = 0$ and $d_{n+1} \neq 0$, i.
e.,
$$x \in [10^{-(n+1)},\, 10^{-n}) \quad (n \geq 1), \qquad x \in [0.1,\, 1] \quad
 (n=0).$$

Since removing the rationals (measure zero) does not change Lebesgue measure,
$$m(A_n) = 10^{-n} - 10^{-(n+1)} = 9 \cdot 10^{-(n+1)}, \qquad n \geq 0.$$

**Computing the integral.** The $A_n$ are disjoint and cover all irrationals in
$(0,1]$, so by countable additivity and the fact that $f = 18n$ on $A_n$:
$$\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} 18n \cdot m(A_n) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
 18n \cdot 9 \cdot 10^{-(n+1)} = \frac{162}{10}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n \cdot 10^{-
n}.$$

Using the standard identity $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n r^n = \frac{r}{(1-r)^2}$ with
 $r = \tfrac{1}{10}$:
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n \cdot 10^{-n} = \frac{1/10}{(9/10)^2} = \frac{1/10}{81/1
00} = \frac{10}{81}.$$

Therefore
$$\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx = \frac{162}{10} \cdot \frac{10}{81} = \frac{162}{81} = \box
ed{2}.$$
