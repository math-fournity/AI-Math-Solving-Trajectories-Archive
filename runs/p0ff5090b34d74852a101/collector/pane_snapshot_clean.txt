## Proof

**Answer: Yes.**

Consider the exponential function $f(z) = e^z$, defined on the entire complex pl
ane $\mathbb{C}$.

**$f$ is holomorphic.** The exponential function is entire (holomorphic on all o
f $\mathbb{C}$); this is a standard fact.

**$f'$ is non-zero everywhere.** We have $f'(z) = e^z$. Since $e^z \neq 0$ for e
very $z \in \mathbb{C}$, the derivative is non-zero everywhere.

**$f$ is non-injective.** The exponential function is periodic with period $2\pi
 i$:
$$e^{z + 2\pi i} = e^z \quad \text{for all } z \in \mathbb{C}.$$
In particular, $f(0) = e^0 = 1$ and $f(2\pi i) = e^{2\pi i} = 1$, yet $0 \neq 2\
pi i$. Hence $f$ is not injective.

We have exhibited a holomorphic function (in fact, an entire function) whose der
ivative is non-zero everywhere but which is not injective. Therefore, such a fun
ction exists. $\blacksquare$
