## Proof

We determine $H^*(SU(n);\mathbb{Z})$ as a graded ring by induction on $n$, using
 the fibration
$$SU(n-1) \hookrightarrow SU(n) \xrightarrow{\;p\;} S^{2n-1}$$
where $p$ projects a matrix to its last column (a unit vector in $\mathbb{C}^n$,
 hence a point of $S^{2n-1}$), and the fiber over the north pole $e_n$ consists
of matrices fixing $e_n$, i.e. $SU(n-1)$ embedded in the upper-left block.

### Base case

$SU(1)=\{1\}$, so $H^*(SU(1))=\mathbb{Z}$ in degree $0$. $SU(2)\cong S^3$, so $H
^*(SU(2))=\mathbb{Z}[x_2]/(x_2^2)$ with $|x_2|=3$, an exterior algebra on one ge
nerator of degree $3$. This matches the claimed form for $n=2$.

### Inductive step: the Serre spectral sequence

Consider the Serre cohomology spectral sequence (with $\mathbb{Z}$ coefficients)
 for the fibration $SU(n-1)\to SU(n)\to S^{2n-1}$. The $E_2$ page is
$$E_2^{p,q}=H^p(S^{2n-1})\otimes H^q(SU(n-1)).$$

By the inductive hypothesis,
$$H^*(SU(n-1))\cong \Lambda_{\mathbb{Z}}(x_2,x_4,\dots,x_{2n-4}),\qquad |x_{2i}|
=2i+1,\; i=1,\dots,n-2,$$
an exterior algebra on generators of degrees $3,5,\dots,2n-3$.

Since the base $S^{2n-1}$ has cohomology only in degrees $0$ and $2n-1$, the $E_
2$ page is nonzero only in the two columns $p=0$ and $p=2n-1$:
$$E_2^{0,q}=H^q(SU(n-1)),\qquad E_2^{2n-1,q}=H^{q+2n-1}\text{-shifted copy of }H
^q(SU(n-1)).$$

### No differentials

The first possible nonzero differential originating from the $p=0$ column is $d_
{2n-1}:E_{2n-1}^{0,q}\to E_{2n-1}^{2n-1,q-2n+2}$. We claim all such differential
s vanish.

The key point: the fibration $SU(n-1)\to SU(n)\to S^{2n-1}$ has a section over t
he complement of the south pole (it is a principal $SU(n-1)$-bundle, locally tri
vial), and more importantly the total space $SU(n)$ is a compact connected Lie g
roup, so $H^*(SU(n))$ is an exterior algebra on odd-degree generators (Hopf's th
eorem). But we can also argue directly within the spectral sequence:

The Euler class of this bundle lives in $H^{2n}(S^{2n-1})=0$, so the $d_{2n-1}$
differential — which is multiplication by the Euler class — is zero. Concretely,
 $d_{2n-1}(1)=e=0$ since $H^{2n}(S^{2n-1})=0$, and by the Leibniz rule (the spec
tral sequence is multiplicative), $d_{2n-1}$ vanishes on all of $E_{2n-1}^{0,*}$
, which is generated as a ring by $1$ and the $x_{2i}$.

Since the only nonzero differential possible is $d_{2n-1}$ (higher differentials
 would land beyond $p=2n-1$, which is zero), the spectral sequence collapses at
$E_2$.

### Reading off the cohomology

Because the spectral sequence collapses and the two nonzero columns are concentr
ated in $p\in\{0,2n-1\}$, there are no extension problems (the filtration on $H^
k(SU(n))$ has at most two graded pieces, and since everything is free abelian, t
he additive structure is determined). We obtain additively:
$$H^*(SU(n))\cong H^*(SU(n-1))\otimes H^*(S^{2n-1})\cong \Lambda(x_2,\dots,x_{2n
-4})\otimes \Lambda(y),$$
where $|y|=2n-1$ and $y^2=0$ (since $2(2n-1)$ exceeds the topological dimension
$\dim SU(n)=n^2-1$ for $n\ge 2$, or simply because $y$ has odd degree and the ri
ng is graded-commutative).

### Multiplicative structure

The collapse at $E_2=E_\infty$ means the ring structure is the tensor product ri
ng structure. Setting $x_{2n-2}:=y$, we get
$$H^*(SU(n))\cong \Lambda_{\mathbb{Z}}(x_2,x_4,\dots,x_{2n-2}),\qquad |x_{2i}|=2
i+1.$$

This is an exterior algebra: each generator squares to zero ($x_{2i}^2=0$ by gra
ded-commutativity since $|x_{2i}|$ is odd), and distinct generators anticommute:
 $x_{2i}x_{2j}=-x_{2j}x_{2i}$.

### Conclusion

By induction, for every $n\ge 1$:

$$\boxed{H^*(SU(n);\mathbb{Z})\;\cong\;\Lambda_{\mathbb{Z}}(x_3,\,x_5,\,\dots,\,
x_{2n-1}),\qquad |x_{2i-1}|=2i-1,\;\; i=1,\dots,n-1,}$$

an exterior algebra over $\mathbb{Z}$ on $n-1$ generators of odd degrees $3,5,7,
\dots,2n-1$, with relations $x_{2i-1}^2=0$ and $x_{2i-1}\,x_{2j-1}=-x_{2j-1}\,x_
{2i-1}$.

A basis of $H^k(SU(n))$ is given by all products $x_{i_1}x_{i_2}\cdots x_{i_r}$
with $3\le i_1<i_2<\dots<i_r\le 2n-1$, $i_j$ odd, and $i_1+\dots+i_r=k$. In part
icular $H^*(SU(n))$ is zero in every odd... (in every even) degree, and the tota
l rank (Betti number sum) is $2^{n-1}$, consistent with the Euler characteristic
 $\chi(SU(n))=0$ for $n\ge 2$.
