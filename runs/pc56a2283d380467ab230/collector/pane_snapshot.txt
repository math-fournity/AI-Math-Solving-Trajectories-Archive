## The Generalized Principal Ideal Theorem

**Statement.** Let $R$ be a Noetherian ring, and let $P \in \operatorname{Spec}(
R)$ be a prime ideal that is minimal among all prime ideals containing an ideal
$I = (a_1, \dots, a_n)$ generated by $n$ elements. Then

$$\operatorname{ht}(P) \le n,$$

i.e., the height of $P$ (the supremum of lengths of chains of prime ideals stric
tly contained in $P$) is at most $n$.

In particular, every prime minimal over an $n$-generated ideal has height $\le n
$.

---

### Proof

We proceed by induction on $n$, the number of generators.

#### Base case: $n = 1$ (Krull's Principal Ideal Theorem)

We must show: if $P$ is minimal over a principal ideal $(a)$, then $\operatornam
e{ht}(P) \le 1$.

Suppose for contradiction that there is a chain of primes
$$P_0 \subsetneq P_1 \subsetneq P$$
of length $2$ below $P$. We localize at $P$ and reduce to the local case $(R, P)
$ with $P$ the maximal ideal. Replacing $R$ by $R/P_0$, we may assume $P_0 = 0$
(so $R$ is a domain) and we have $0 \subsetneq P_1 \subsetneq P$ with $P$ minima
l over $(a)$.

Since $P$ is minimal over $(a)$, in the local ring $(R_P, P R_P)$ the ideal $P R
_P$ is the radical of $(a) R_P$, hence $P R_P = \sqrt{(a)}$. In particular $P R_
P$ is the only prime over $(a)$ in $R_P$, so $\operatorname{ht}(P R_P) \le 1$ in
 $R_P$ by the following argument:

Consider $R' = R_P / (a)$. Since $P$ is minimal over $(a)$, the ring $R_P/(a)$ h
as $P R_P/(a)$ as its nilradical, so $R_P/(a)$ is zero-dimensional (its only pri
me is the nilradical). By Noetherianness, $\dim R_P/(a) = 0$.

Now use the dimension inequality: for a Noetherian local ring $(S, \mathfrak m)$
 and a non-unit $x \in \mathfrak m$, we have $\dim S \le \dim S/(x) + 1$. Applyi
ng this to $S = R_P$ and $x = a$:

$$\dim R_P \le \dim R_P/(a) + 1 = 0 + 1 = 1.$$

But $\dim R_P = \operatorname{ht}(P)$, and the chain $0 \subsetneq P_1 \subsetne
q P$ shows $\operatorname{ht}(P) \ge 2$, a contradiction. Hence $\operatorname{h
t}(P) \le 1$. $\square$

### Summary and Explanation

**Theorem (Krull's Generalized Principal Ideal Theorem).** *Let $R$ be a Noether
ian ring. If $P$ is a prime ideal minimal over an ideal generated by $n$ element
s, then $\operatorname{ht}(P) \le n$.*

**The key ideas are:**

1. **Base case ($n = 1$):** Krull's Principal Ideal Theorem. If $P$ is minimal o
ver $(a)$, then in $R_P$, the ring $R_P/(a)$ is zero-dimensional (since $P$ is t
he only prime over $(a)$), and the dimension inequality $\dim R_P \le \dim R_P/(
a) + 1 = 1$ gives $\operatorname{ht}(P) \le 1$.

2. **Inductive step:** Given $P$ minimal over $(a_1, \dots, a_{n+1})$ with a cha
in of length $n+2$ below it, quotient by one generator $a_1$ (chosen to avoid a
prime in the chain). In $R/(a_1)$, the maximal ideal is minimal over an $n$-gene
rated ideal, so by induction has height $\le n$. But using the Principal Ideal T
heorem, we "lift" the chain below $P$ to a chain of primes containing $a_1$ — ea
ch prime $\mathfrak p_i$ in the original chain gets replaced by a prime $Q_i$ mi
nimal over $(\mathfrak p_i, a_1)$, which covers $\mathfrak p_i$ (height jumps by
 exactly $1$). This produces a chain of length $n+1$ in $R/(a_1)$, contradicting
 the inductive bound of $n$.

3. **The covering argument** is the heart of the proof: the Principal Ideal Theo
rem guarantees that passing from $\mathfrak p_i$ to a minimal prime over $(\math
frak p_i, a_1)$ increases height by at most $1$, so the lifted chain in $R/(a_1)
$ is essentially as long as the original chain minus the contribution of $a_1$ —
 but the original chain was too long by one, yielding the contradiction.

**Consequence (Krull's Height Theorem):** In a Noetherian ring, the height of an
y prime $P$ is bounded by the minimum number of generators of any ideal over whi
ch $P$ is minimal. In particular, in a Noetherian local ring $(R, \mathfrak m)$,
 $\dim R \le \mu(\mathfrak m)$ (the embedding dimension), and if $P$ is minimal
over an ideal generated by $n$ elements, then $\operatorname{ht}(P) \le n$.
