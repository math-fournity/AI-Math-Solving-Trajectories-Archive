## Solution by the Fourier Method

**Problem (with notation corrected: $\nu \to u$).** Solve
$$u_{tt} = u_{xx} + 1, \quad (x,t)\in(0,\pi)\times\mathbb{R}_+,$$
$$u(0,t)=u(\pi,t)=0,\quad u(x,0)=0,\quad u_t(x,0)=0.$$

---

### Step 1. Eigenfunction expansion

The homogeneous spatial operator $-\partial_{xx}$ on $(0,\pi)$ with Dirichlet bo
undary conditions has eigenfunctions $\sin(nx)$ and eigenvalues $n^2$, $n=1,2,\d
ots$. We seek
$$u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} T_n(t)\sin(nx).$$

Each term satisfies the boundary conditions automatically since $\sin(n\cdot 0)=
\sin(n\pi)=0$.

### Step 2. Expand the forcing term

Expand the constant $1$ in a sine series on $(0,\pi)$:
$$1=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} f_n\sin(nx),\qquad f_n=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi \sin(nx)\
,dx=\frac{2}{n\pi}\bigl(1-(-1)^n\bigr).$$
Hence
$$f_n=\begin{cases}\dfrac{4}{n\pi}, & n\ \text{odd},\\[4pt] 0, & n\ \text{even}.
\end{cases}$$

### Step 3. ODEs for the time coefficients

Substituting the expansion into the PDE and matching Fourier coefficients:
$$T_n''(t)+n^2 T_n(t)=f_n,\qquad T_n(0)=0,\quad T_n'(0)=0.$$

**Case $n$ even:** $f_n=0$, so $T_n''+n^2 T_n=0$ with zero data gives $T_n\equiv
 0$.

**Case $n$ odd:** $f_n=\dfrac{4}{n\pi}$. A constant particular solution is $T_n^
{(p)}=\dfrac{f_n}{n^2}=\dfrac{4}{n^3\pi}$. The general solution is
$$T_n(t)=A\cos(nt)+B\sin(nt)+\frac{4}{n^3\pi}.$$
From $T_n(0)=0$: $A=-\dfrac{4}{n^3\pi}$. From $T_n'(0)=nB=0$: $B=0$. Thus
$$T_n(t)=\frac{4}{n^3\pi}\bigl(1-\cos(nt)\bigr),\qquad n\ \text{odd}.$$

### Step 4. The solution

$$\boxed{\,u(x,t)=\frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{\substack{n=1\\ n\ \mathrm{odd}}}^{\infty}\
frac{1-\cos(nt)}{n^3}\,\sin(nx)\,}$$

or equivalently, writing $n=2k+1$,
$$u(x,t)=\frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos\bigl((2k+1)t\bigr)}{(2k+1)
^3}\,\sin\bigl((2k+1)x\bigr).$$

### Step 5. Verification

- **Boundary conditions:** Each $\sin(nx)$ vanishes at $x=0,\pi$. ✓
- **Initial displacement:** $u(x,0)=\sum \frac{4}{n^3\pi}(1-1)\sin(nx)=0$. ✓
- **Initial velocity:** $u_t(x,0)=\sum \frac{4}{n^3\pi}\cdot n\sin(0)\sin(nx)=0$
. ✓
- **PDE:** Compute
$$u_{tt}=\sum_{n\ \text{odd}}\frac{4}{n\pi}\cos(nt)\sin(nx),\qquad u_{xx}=-\sum_
{n\ \text{odd}}\frac{4}{n\pi}\bigl(1-\cos(nt)\bigr)\sin(nx).$$
Therefore
$$u_{tt}-u_{xx}=\sum_{n\ \text{odd}}\frac{4}{n\pi}\bigl[\cos(nt)+1-\cos(nt)\bigr
]\sin(nx)=\sum_{n\ \text{odd}}\frac{4}{n\pi}\sin(nx)=1.$$
The last equality is exactly the sine expansion of the constant $1$ from Step 2.
 Hence $u_{tt}=u_{xx}+1$. ✓

The series converges absolutely and uniformly together with its first and second
 derivatives (the coefficients decay as $1/n^3$, and each $x$- or $t$-differenti
ation costs a factor of $n$, still leaving $1/n$ decay for the second derivative
s, which is summable), so term-by-term differentiation is justified and the veri
fication above is rigorous.
