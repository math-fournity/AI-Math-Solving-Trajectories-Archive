Proof

Answer: Yes. A Vitali set provides such an example.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Construction

Let $V \subseteq [0,1]$ be a Vitali set: choose exactly one representative from
each equivalence class of $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}$ that intersects $[0,1]$.
Equivalently, $V$ contains exactly one element from each coset $x + \mathbb{Q}$
that meets $[0,1]$.

Step 1: $V$ is nonmeasurable

Suppose $V$ were Lebesgue measurable.

  • If $\mu(V) > 0$, then by Steinhaus's theorem (see Step 2 below), $V - V$
  contains an open interval around $0$, hence contains some nonzero rational
  $q$. But $x - y = q \in \mathbb{Q} \setminus {0}$ with $x, y \in V$ means $x,
  y$ lie in the same coset of $\mathbb{Q}$, contradicting the construction of
  $V$ (one representative per coset). So $\mu(V) > 0$ is impossible.
  • If $\mu(V) = 0$, then the countable union $\bigcup_{q \in \mathbb{Q} \cap
  [0,1]} (V + q)$ would be a null set. But this union contains $[0,1]$ (every
  point of $[0,1]$ differs from some element of $V$ by a rational in $[0,1]$),
  giving $1 = \mu([0,1]) \leq 0$, a contradiction.

Both cases lead to contradictions, so $V$ is nonmeasurable. $\checkmark$

Step 2: Every measurable subset $E \subseteq V$ is null

Let $E \subseteq V$ be Lebesgue measurable. We show $\mu(E) = 0$.

Suppose for contradiction that $\mu(E) > 0$. By Steinhaus's theorem:

│ If $E$ is measurable with $\mu(E) > 0$, then $E - E$ contains an open
│ neighborhood of $0$.

Proof of Steinhaus's theorem (for completeness): By inner regularity, there
exists compact $K \subseteq E$ with $\mu(K) > 0$. The convolution $\mathbf{1}K *
\mathbf{1}{-K}$ is continuous (since $K$ is bounded) and equals $\mu(K) > 0$ at
$0$. By continuity, it remains positive in some neighborhood $(-\delta, \delta)$
of $0$, meaning $K \cap (K + h) \neq \emptyset$ for all $|h| < \delta$, i.e., $h
\in K - K \subseteq E - E$. $\square$

Applying Steinhaus: $E - E$ contains an open interval $(-\delta, \delta)$, which
contains a nonzero rational $q$.

But $E \subseteq V$ implies $E - E \subseteq V - V$, and as noted above:

$$V - V \cap \mathbb{Q} = {0},$$

since any $x - y \in \mathbb{Q}$ with $x, y \in V$ forces $x, y$ into the same
coset, hence $x = y$.

This contradicts $q \in E - E \subseteq V - V$ with $q \in \mathbb{Q} \setminus
{0}$.

Therefore $\mu(E) = 0$. $\checkmark$

Conclusion

The Vitali set $V$ is:

  • Nonmeasurable (Step 1), and
  • Every measurable subset $E \subseteq V$ satisfies $\mu(E) = 0$ (Step 2),
  i.e., $\mu_*(V) = 0$.

Such a set exists. $\blacksquare$
