**Answer: Yes.** A bialgebra map between Hopf algebras is automatically a Hopf a
lgebra map.

**Proof.**

Let $H, H'$ be Hopf algebras over a field $k$, with antipodes $S_H, S_{H'}$ resp
ectively. Let $\phi: H \to H'$ be a bialgebra map, i.e., $\phi$ is simultaneousl
y an algebra homomorphism and a coalgebra homomorphism. We must show
$$\phi \circ S_H = S_{H'} \circ \phi.$$

**Step 1: Convolution setup.** Since $\phi$ is a coalgebra map, the map
$$\Phi: \text{Hom}(H, H') \to \text{Hom}(H, H'), \qquad f \mapsto \phi \circ f$$
is not what we use; instead we work directly in the convolution monoid $\text{Ho
m}(H, H')$. Recall the convolution product: for $f, g \in \text{Hom}(H, H')$,
$$(f * g)(h) = \sum_{(h)} f\bigl(h_{(1)}\bigr)\, g\bigl(h_{(2)}\bigr),$$
with unit $u := \eta_{H'} \circ \epsilon_H$ (where $\eta_{H'}$ is the unit map o
f $H'$ and $\epsilon_H$ the counit of $H$). This is well-defined because $H'$ is
 an algebra, and it forms a monoid since $H$ is coassociative.

**Step 2: $\phi \circ S_H$ is a convolution inverse of $\phi$.** Using that $\ph
i$ is an algebra map (so $\phi(ab) = \phi(a)\phi(b)$) and the antipode axiom in
$H$:
$$\bigl((\phi \circ S_H) * \phi\bigr)(h) = \sum_{(h)} \phi\bigl(S_H(h_{(1)})\big
r)\,\phi(h_{(2)}) = \phi\!\left(\sum_{(h)} S_H(h_{(1)})\,h_{(2)}\right) = \phi\b
igl(\epsilon_H(h)\cdot 1_H\bigr) = \epsilon_H(h)\cdot 1_{H'} = u(h).$$
The symmetric computation gives $\bigl(\phi * (\phi \circ S_H)\bigr) = u$. Hence
 $\phi \circ S_H$ is a (two-sided) convolution inverse of $\phi$.

**Step 3: $S_{H'} \circ \phi$ is also a convolution inverse of $\phi$.** Using t
hat $\phi$ is a coalgebra map, so $\Delta_{H'}(\phi(h)) = \sum_{(h)} \phi(h_{(1)
}) \otimes \phi(h_{(2)})$, and the antipode axiom in $H'$:
$$\bigl(\phi * (S_{H'} \circ \phi)\bigr)(h) = \sum_{(h)} \phi(h_{(1)})\, S_{H'}\
!\bigl(\phi(h_{(2)})\bigr) = \sum_{(\phi(h))} (\phi(h))_{(1)}\, S_{H'}\!\bigl((\
phi(h))_{(2)}\bigr) = \epsilon_{H'}(\phi(h))\cdot 1_{H'} = \epsilon_H(h)\cdot 1_
{H'} = u(h),$$
where we used $\epsilon_{H'} \circ \phi = \epsilon_H$ (counit preservation). The
 symmetric computation gives $\bigl((S_{H'} \circ \phi) * \phi\bigr) = u$. Hence
 $S_{H'} \circ \phi$ is also a two-sided convolution inverse of $\phi$.

**Step 4: Uniqueness.** In any monoid, a two-sided inverse is unique: if $a * b
= b * a = e$ and $a * c = c * a = e$, then $b = b * e = b * (a * c) = (b * a) *
c = e * c = c$. Applying this in the convolution monoid $\text{Hom}(H, H')$ to t
he element $\phi$ with its two inverses $\phi \circ S_H$ and $S_{H'} \circ \phi$
, we conclude
$$\phi \circ S_H = S_{H'} \circ \phi.$$

Therefore $\phi$ respects the antipodes, and so $\phi$ is a Hopf algebra map. $\
blacksquare$

**Remark.** The argument uses both halves of the bialgebra structure: the algebr
a-map property of $\phi$ in Step 2, and the coalgebra-map property of $\phi$ in
Step 3. The only structural input beyond bialgebra-ness is the *existence* of an
tipodes (giving convolution inverses of the identity in each Hopf algebra) and t
he *uniqueness* of convolution inverses in a monoid — both of which hold in ever
y Hopf algebra.
