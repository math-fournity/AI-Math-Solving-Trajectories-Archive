## Proof

Let $E$ be a simple $R$-module, and suppose $E^{(n)} \cong E^{(m)}$ as $R$-modul
es for positive integers $n, m$. We prove $n = m$.

**Step 1. The endomorphism ring of $E$ is a division ring.**

Since $E$ is simple, Schur's lemma gives that $D := \operatorname{End}_{R}(E)$ i
s a division ring: any nonzero endomorphism of $E$ has kernel a proper submodule
, hence $0$, and image a nonzero submodule, hence $E$, so it is an isomorphism a
nd thus invertible.

**Step 2. Identify $\operatorname{End}_{R}(E^{(k)})$ with $M_{k}(D)$.**

For any positive integer $k$, write $E^{(k)} = E_{1} \oplus \cdots \oplus E_{k}$
 with each $E_{i} = E$. An endomorphism $f \in \operatorname{End}_{R}(E^{(k)})$
is determined by its components $f_{ij} := \pi_{i} \circ f \circ \iota_{j} \in \
operatorname{End}_{R}(E) = D$, where $\iota_{j}: E_{j} \hookrightarrow E^{(k)}$
and $\pi_{i}: E^{(k)} \twoheadrightarrow E_{i}$ are the canonical inclusion and
projection. The map $f \mapsto (f_{ij})$ is a ring isomorphism
$$
\operatorname{End}_{R}(E^{(k)}) \;\cong\; M_{k}(D).
$$

**Step 3. Translate the module isomorphism into a ring isomorphism.**

The isomorphism $E^{(n)} \cong E^{(m)}$ induces, by conjugation, a ring isomorph
ism
$$
\operatorname{End}_{R}(E^{(n)}) \;\cong\; \operatorname{End}_{R}(E^{(m)}),
$$
and hence by Step 2,
$$
M_{n}(D) \;\cong\; M_{m}(D) \quad \text{as rings.}
$$

**Step 4. Show $M_{n}(D) \cong M_{m}(D)$ forces $n = m$.**

Fix a ring isomorphism $\varphi: M_{n}(D) \xrightarrow{\sim} M_{m}(D)$. Such a $
\varphi$ is, in particular, an isomorphism of the ring $M_{n}(D)$ acting on its
**left regular module** $M_{n}(D)$ onto the ring $M_{m}(D)$ acting on its left r
egular module $M_{m}(D)$: for $a, x \in M_{n}(D)$,
$$
\varphi(a \cdot x) = \varphi(a)\varphi(x) = \varphi(a) \cdot \varphi(x),
$$
where the left dot on the right-hand side is the left $M_{m}(D)$-action on itsel
f. Hence $\varphi$ is an isomorphism of left modules
$$
{}_{M_{n}(D)}M_{n}(D) \;\cong\; {}_{M_{m}(D)}M_{m}(D),
$$
and in particular the two regular modules have the same **composition length**.

Now compute these lengths. In $M_{k}(D)$, let $L_{j}$ be the set of matrices who
se entries are zero outside column $j$. Each $L_{j}$ is a minimal left ideal: it
 is a left ideal, and as a left $M_{k}(D)$-module it is simple (any nonzero elem
ent generates all of $L_{j}$, since left-multiplying by a suitable matrix sends
a nonzero column vector to any prescribed nonzero column vector, using that $D$
is a division ring). Moreover
$$
M_{k}(D) = L_{1} \oplus L_{2} \oplus \cdots \oplus L_{k},
$$
a direct sum of $k$ simple left modules. Therefore
$$
\operatorname{length}\bigl({}_{M_{k}(D)}M_{k}(D)\bigr) = k.
$$

Applying this to $k = n$ and $k = m$, the equality of composition lengths from t
he ring isomorphism gives
$$
n = m.
$$

This completes the proof. $\blacksquare$
