# How the Prime 2 Splits in the Splitting Field of $X^3 - 3X + 1$

## Step 1: Irreducibility and the Galois group

Let $f(X) = X^3 - 3X + 1$. By the rational root theorem, the only candidates are
 $\pm 1$:
$$f(1) = -1 \neq 0, \qquad f(-1) = 3 \neq 0.$$
So $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$.

The discriminant of $X^3 + pX + q$ is $-4p^3 - 27q^2$. Here $p=-3,\, q=1$:
$$\Delta = -4(-27) - 27(1) = 108 - 27 = 81 = 9^2.$$
Since $\Delta$ is a perfect square, the Galois group is $A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\
mathbb{Z}$, and the splitting field $K$ equals $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ for any root
 $\alpha$ of $f$. In particular $K/\mathbb{Q}$ is a **cyclic cubic** (Galois) ex
tension.

## Step 2: Identifying the field and the ring of integers

Set $\beta = \zeta_9 + \zeta_9^{-1} = 2\cos(2\pi/9)$. Then:
$$\beta^3 = (\zeta_9^3 + \zeta_9^{-3}) + 3(\zeta_9 + \zeta_9^{-1}) = 2\cos(2\pi/
3) + 3\beta = -1 + 3\beta,$$
so $\beta^3 - 3\beta + 1 = 0$. Thus $\alpha = \beta$ and
$$K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_9 + \zeta_9^{-1}) = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_9)^+,$$
the maximal real subfield of the 9th cyclotomic field. This is the unique cyclic
 cubic field of conductor $9$, with discriminant $\operatorname{disc}(K) = 9^2 =
 81$.

Since $\operatorname{disc}(f) = 81 = \operatorname{disc}(K)$, the index $[\mathc
al{O}_K : \mathbb{Z}[\alpha]]$ satisfies
$$[\mathcal{O}_K : \mathbb{Z}[\alpha]]^2 = \frac{\operatorname{disc}(f)}{\operat
orname{disc}(K)} = 1,$$
so $\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}[\alpha]$. The Dedekind–Kummer theorem therefore a
pplies directly at **every** prime $p$.

## Step 3: Factoring $f$ modulo 2

Reduce $f$ modulo 2:
$$\bar{f}(X) = X^3 + X + 1 \in \mathbb{F}_2[X].$$
Check for roots in $\mathbb{F}_2$:
$$\bar{f}(0) = 1 \neq 0, \qquad \bar{f}(1) = 1 + 1 + 1 = 1 \neq 0.$$
A cubic with no roots is irreducible, so $\bar{f}$ is **irreducible** over $\mat
hbb{F}_2$.

## Step 4: Conclusion via Dedekind–Kummer

Since $2 \nmid \operatorname{disc}(K) = 81$, the prime $2$ is unramified. Since
$\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}[\alpha]$ and $\bar{f}$ is irreducible mod $2$, the i
deal $(2)$ remains prime in $\mathcal{O}_K$:

$$\boxed{(2) = \mathfrak{p}, \quad e(\mathfrak{p}/2) = 1, \quad f(\mathfrak{p}/2
) = 3, \quad \mathcal{O}_K/\mathfrak{p} \cong \mathbb{F}_8.}$$

The prime **2 is inert** in the splitting field $K$.

## Verification via conductor criterion

For the cyclic cubic field of conductor $9$, a prime $p \neq 3$ splits completel
y iff $p \equiv \pm 1 \pmod{9}$ (equivalently, $p$ is a cubic residue mod $9$),
and is inert otherwise. Since $2 \equiv 2 \pmod{9}$, which is neither $1$ nor $8
$, the prime $2$ is inert — confirming the result.
