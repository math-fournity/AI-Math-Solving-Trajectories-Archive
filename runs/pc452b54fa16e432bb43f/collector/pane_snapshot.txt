## Solution

We seek the smallest width $w$ of an infinite strip (region between two parallel
 lines distance $w$ apart) such that **every** triangle of area $1$ can be place
d inside it.

### Key reduction

A triangle fits inside a strip of width $w$ iff there exists a direction in whic
h the triangle's **width** (distance between the two supporting lines perpendicu
lar to that direction) is at most $w$. The minimum such width over all direction
s is the triangle's *minimum width*.

For a triangle, the width in the direction perpendicular to a side equals the al
titude to that side. Hence

$$\text{min width of triangle} = \min(h_a, h_b, h_c) = \frac{2}{\max(a,b,c)},$$

since $h_a = 2\,\text{Area}/a = 2/a$ (with Area $=1$), and the smallest altitude
 corresponds to the longest side.

So the required strip width is

$$w = \max_{\text{Area}=1}\;\frac{2}{\max(a,b,c)} = \frac{2}{\displaystyle\min_{
\text{Area}=1}\max(a,b,c)}.$$

### Minimizing the longest side for fixed area

Among all triangles of area $1$, we minimize the longest side $L = \max(a,b,c)$.
 For a fixed upper bound $L$ on every side, the maximum possible area is attaine
d by the equilateral triangle of side $L$ (by Heron's formula / symmetry, spread
ing sides equally maximizes area under the constraint $a,b,c \le L$). Its area i
s $\frac{\sqrt3}{4}L^2$.

Requiring area $\ge 1$ forces $\frac{\sqrt3}{4}L^2 \ge 1$, i.e.

$$L \ge \frac{2}{\sqrt[4]{3}},$$

with equality at the equilateral triangle of side $L = 2/\sqrt[4]{3}$.

### Computing the answer

For this equilateral triangle, every altitude equals

$$h = \frac{\sqrt3}{2}\,L = \frac{\sqrt3}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{\sqrt[4]{3}} = \frac{\
sqrt3}{\sqrt[4]{3}} = 3^{1/2 - 1/4} = \sqrt[4]{3}.$$

Thus the minimum width of the equilateral triangle of area $1$ is $\sqrt[4]{3}$,
 and every other triangle of area $1$ has a smaller minimum width (its longest s
ide is larger, so $2/\max(a,b,c)$ is smaller). Therefore $\sqrt[4]{3}$ is both a
ttained and is the maximum, giving the required strip width.

$$\boxed{\sqrt[4]{3}}$$
