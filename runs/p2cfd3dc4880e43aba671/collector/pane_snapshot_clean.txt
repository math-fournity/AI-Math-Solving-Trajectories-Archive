# Einstein Vacuum Equations as Nonlinear Wave Equations in Wave Coordinates

## 1. Setup and notation

Let $(M, g)$ be a 4-dimensional Lorentzian manifold with metric $g_{\alpha\beta}
$ of signature $(-,+,+,+)$. The **Einstein vacuum equations** are

$$R_{\alpha\beta} = 0. \tag{EV}$$

We work in a coordinate chart $x^\mu$ and use the standard expressions:

- Christoffel symbols: $\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac{1}{2} g^{\lambda\rho}(\
partial_\mu g_{\nu\rho} + \partial_\nu g_{\mu\rho} - \partial_\rho g_{\mu\nu})$.
- Ricci tensor:
$$R_{\alpha\beta} = \partial_\lambda \Gamma^\lambda_{\alpha\beta} - \partial_\be
ta \Gamma^\lambda_{\alpha\lambda} + \Gamma^\lambda_{\lambda\rho}\Gamma^\rho_{\al
pha\beta} - \Gamma^\lambda_{\beta\rho}\Gamma^\rho_{\alpha\lambda}. \tag{1}$$

A direct expansion of (1) shows $R_{\alpha\beta}$ contains **second derivatives
of $g$**, but in general coordinates the principal part (the terms with two deri
vatives of the metric) is **not simply $\Box g_{\alpha\beta}$**; it also contain
s terms like $g^{\lambda\rho}\partial_\lambda\partial_\beta g_{\alpha\rho}$ and
mixed second derivatives, so the system is **not manifestly hyperbolic**.

## 2. Wave (harmonic) coordinates

Define the **wave coordinate condition** (also called *harmonic* or *de Donder*
condition):

$$\boxed{\;\Gamma^\lambda \equiv g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = 0 \quad \te
xt{for } \lambda = 0,1,2,3.\;} \tag{2}$$

Equivalently, the coordinate functions $x^\lambda$ satisfy the wave equation wit
h respect to $g$:
$$\Box_g x^\lambda = g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu x^\lambda = -\Gamma^\lambda
= 0.$$

A key identity: since $\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac{1}{2}g^{\lambda\rho}(\pa
rtial_\mu g_{\nu\rho}+\partial_\nu g_{\mu\rho}-\partial_\rho g_{\mu\nu})$,

$$\Gamma^\lambda = g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac{1}{2} g^{\lambda\
rho} g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\rho g_{\mu\nu} + g^{\lambda\rho} g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu
 g_{\nu\rho}.$$

Using $\partial_\rho g^{\mu\nu} = -g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\partial_\rho g_{\al
pha\beta}$, one obtains the equivalent form

$$\Gamma^\lambda = -\tfrac{1}{2} g^{\lambda\rho}\,\partial_\rho\!\left(g^{\mu\nu
}g_{\mu\nu}\right) + g^{\lambda\rho} g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu g_{\nu\rho} = -g^{\l
ambda\rho}\,(\text{(lowered index form)}).$$

The cleanest statement is: defining the **reduced d'Alembertian**

$$\hat{\Box}_g f := g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu f,$$

the harmonic condition is equivalent (after lowering an index) to

$$g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu x^\lambda = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Gamma^\lambda = 0. \tag{2'}$$

## 3. The principal part of $R_{\alpha\beta}$

Expand (1) in terms of $\partial\partial g$. Using $\Gamma^\lambda_{\alpha\beta}
 = \tfrac{1}{2}g^{\lambda\rho}(\partial_\alpha g_{\beta\rho}+\partial_\beta g_{\
alpha\rho}-\partial_\rho g_{\alpha\beta})$:

$$\partial_\lambda \Gamma^\lambda_{\alpha\beta} = \tfrac{1}{2}g^{\lambda\rho}\pa
rtial_\lambda(\partial_\alpha g_{\beta\rho}+\partial_\beta g_{\alpha\rho}-\parti
al_\rho g_{\alpha\beta}) + (\text{terms with } \partial g \cdot \partial g).$$

The principal (second-derivative) part is

$$\tfrac{1}{2}g^{\lambda\rho}\bigl(\partial_\lambda\partial_\alpha g_{\beta\rho}
 + \partial_\lambda\partial_\beta g_{\alpha\rho} - \partial_\lambda\partial_\rho
 g_{\alpha\beta}\bigr).$$

Similarly,

$$\partial_\beta \Gamma^\lambda_{\alpha\lambda} = \tfrac{1}{2}g^{\lambda\rho}\pa
rtial_\beta(\partial_\alpha g_{\lambda\rho} + \partial_\lambda g_{\alpha\rho} -
\partial_\rho g_{\alpha\lambda}) + (\partial g\cdot\partial g),$$

whose principal part is

$$\tfrac{1}{2}g^{\lambda\rho}\bigl(\partial_\beta\partial_\alpha g_{\lambda\rho}
 + \partial_\beta\partial_\lambda g_{\alpha\rho} - \partial_\beta\partial_\rho g
_{\alpha\lambda}\bigr).$$

Subtracting, and using symmetry of $g_{\lambda\rho}$ and commutativity of partia
l derivatives, the **second and third terms cancel pairwise**, leaving

$$\bigl(R_{\alpha\beta}\bigr)_{\text{principal}} = -\tfrac{1}{2} g^{\lambda\rho}
\partial_\lambda\partial_\rho g_{\alpha\beta} + \tfrac{1}{2} g^{\lambda\rho}\par
tial_\alpha\partial_\beta g_{\lambda\rho}.$$

So in **general coordinates**,

$$R_{\alpha\beta} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\,g^{\lambda\rho}\partial_\lambda\partial_\rho\
, g_{\alpha\beta} + \tfrac{1}{2}\,g^{\lambda\rho}\partial_\alpha\partial_\beta\,
 g_{\lambda\rho} + Q_{\alpha\beta}(g)(\partial g, \partial g), \tag{3}$$

where $Q_{\alpha\beta}$ is a quadratic form in the first derivatives of $g$ with
 coefficients depending smoothly on $g$ (and $g^{-1}$).

## 4. Imposing the wave coordinate condition

The unwanted term $\tfrac{1}{2}g^{\lambda\rho}\partial_\alpha\partial_\beta g_{\
lambda\rho}$ is precisely what the harmonic condition removes. Indeed, one can v
erify the identity

$$\Gamma^\lambda \Gamma_{\lambda\alpha\beta} - \Gamma^\lambda_{\ \ \rho}\Gamma^\
rho_{\ \ \alpha\beta} + \text{(lowered)} = -g_{\alpha\lambda}\partial_\beta\Gamm
a^\lambda - g_{\beta\lambda}\partial_\alpha\Gamma^\lambda + \cdots$$

More directly, a standard computation (see e.g. Wald, *General Relativity*, §10.
1; or Choquet-Bruhat's original 1952 work) gives the **exact identity**

$$R_{\alpha\beta} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\,g^{\lambda\rho}\partial_\lambda\partial_\rho\
, g_{\alpha\beta} + Q_{\alpha\beta}(g)(\partial g,\partial g) + \tfrac{1}{2}\big
l(g_{\beta\lambda}\partial_\alpha\Gamma^\lambda + g_{\alpha\lambda}\partial_\bet
a\Gamma^\lambda\bigr) + \widetilde{Q}_{\alpha\beta}(g,\Gamma), \tag{4}$$

where $\widetilde Q$ collects terms that vanish when $\Gamma^\lambda \equiv 0$.
Concretely, defining

$$\mathcal{R}_{\alpha\beta} := R_{\alpha\beta} + \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(g_{\alpha\lam
bda}\partial_\beta\Gamma^\lambda + g_{\beta\lambda}\partial_\alpha\Gamma^\lambda
\bigr),$$

one has the **reduced Ricci operator**

$$\boxed{\;\mathcal{R}_{\alpha\beta} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\,g^{\lambda\rho}\,\partial_
\lambda\partial_\rho\, g_{\alpha\beta} + Q_{\alpha\beta}(g)(\partial g,\partial
g).\;} \tag{5}$$

Here $Q_{\alpha\beta}$ is a **smooth function of $g$ (and $g^{-1}$) that is quad
ratic in $\partial g$** — explicitly a linear combination of terms $g^{\cdot\cdo
t}g^{\cdot\cdot}(\partial g)(\partial g)$.

## 5. The reduced Einstein equations as quasilinear wave equations

In wave coordinates ($\Gamma^\lambda = 0$), the extra terms in (4) vanish, so $R
_{\alpha\beta} = \mathcal{R}_{\alpha\beta}$, and the vacuum equations $R_{\alpha
\beta}=0$ become

$$\boxed{\;-\tfrac{1}{2}\,g^{\lambda\rho}\,\partial_\lambda\partial_\rho\, g_{\a
lpha\beta} = -Q_{\alpha\beta}(g)(\partial g,\partial g),\;} \tag{6}$$

or, multiplying by $-2$,

$$g^{\lambda\rho}\,\partial_\lambda\partial_\rho\, g_{\alpha\beta} = F_{\alpha\b
eta}(g,\partial g), \tag{7}$$

where $F_{\alpha\beta} = 2\,Q_{\alpha\beta}$ is a **smooth function of $(g, \par
tial g)$, quadratic in $\partial g$**.

Equation (7) is a system of **10 quasilinear wave equations** (one for each inde
pendent component $g_{\alpha\beta}$ of the symmetric $4\times 4$ metric), with p
rincipal symbol governed by $g^{\lambda\rho}\partial_\lambda\partial_\rho = \hat
\Box_g$.

## 6. Propagation of the wave coordinate condition

A subtlety: (7) is equivalent to $R_{\alpha\beta}=0$ **only if $\Gamma^\lambda =
 0$ is preserved**. One must check that solutions of the *reduced* system (7) th
at start with $\Gamma^\lambda=0$ and $\partial_t\Gamma^\lambda=0$ keep $\Gamma^\
lambda \equiv 0$.

The contracted Bianchi identity $\nabla^\alpha G_{\alpha\beta}=0$ with $G_{\alph
a\beta}=R_{\alpha\beta}-\tfrac12 R g_{\alpha\beta}$, together with $R_{\alpha\be
ta}=0$ (from the reduced equations, *assuming* $\Gamma=0$), gives a **homogeneou
s wave equation for $\Gamma^\lambda$**:

$$g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu \Gamma^\lambda = A^{\lambda}_{\ \rho}(g,\pa
rtial g)\,\Gamma^\rho + B^{\lambda}_{\ \rho}(g,\partial g)\,\partial\Gamma^\rho.
 \tag{8}$$

(Derivation: the Bianchi identity yields $\nabla^\alpha R_{\alpha\beta}=\tfrac12
\partial_\beta R$; substituting the expression (4) of $R_{\alpha\beta}$ in terms
 of $\mathcal R_{\alpha\beta}$ and $\Gamma$, and using $\mathcal R_{\alpha\beta}
=0$ from (7), produces a wave equation for $\Gamma^\lambda$ with source terms **
proportional to $\Gamma$ and $\partial\Gamma$**.) By uniqueness for linear wave
equations with zero initial data,

$$\Gamma^\lambda\big|_{t=0} = 0,\quad \partial_t\Gamma^\lambda\big|_{t=0}=0 \;\L
ongrightarrow\; \Gamma^\lambda \equiv 0.$$

Thus the wave coordinate condition is **propagated** by the reduced equations, a
nd the reduced system (7) is genuinely equivalent to the Einstein vacuum equatio
ns **provided the initial data satisfy the constraint equations and the gauge co
nditions** $\Gamma^\lambda|_{t=0}=\partial_t\Gamma^\lambda|_{t=0}=0$.

## 7. Significance

**(a) Hyperbolicity and well-posedness.** Equation (7) is a system of **quasilin
ear wave equations** with principal symbol $\hat\Box_g = g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\
partial_\nu$. On a region where $g$ is Lorentzian (so $\hat\Box_g$ is a hyperbol
ic operator), the standard theory of quasilinear hyperbolic PDE (Leray–Sobolev,
or more refined energy estimates) yields **local existence and uniqueness** of s
olutions for sufficiently smooth initial data $(g|_{t=0}, \partial_t g|_{t=0})$.
 This is Choquet-Bruhat's 1952 theorem: the Cauchy problem for the Einstein vacu
um equations is **locally well-posed** in wave coordinates. This was the first r
igorous well-posedness result for Einstein's equations and remains the foundatio
nal existence theorem.

**(b) Gauge reduction.** Einstein's equations are **diffeomorphism-invariant**,
so in arbitrary coordinates the system is underdetermined (4 functional degrees
of freedom are gauge). The wave coordinate condition $\Gamma^\lambda=0$ fixes th
is gauge, reducing the system to a determined hyperbolic system. The propagation
 argument in §6 shows the gauge choice is **consistent**: it is preserved by the
 evolution.

**(c) Wave coordinates are general enough.** Any sufficiently smooth spacetime a
dmits wave coordinates locally (one solves $\Box_g x^\lambda = 0$ with prescribe
d initial data). Therefore the reduction loses no generality: every smooth vacuu
m spacetime can locally be put in wave coordinates, and in those coordinates the
 metric satisfies (7).

**(d) Foundation for global results.** The wave-equation formulation is the star
ting point for:
- The **global small-data results** (Christodoulou–Klainerman stability of Minko
wski space, Lindblad–Rodnianski): one works in wave coordinates (or related gaug
es) where the equations are manifestly hyperbolic and admit a null structure exp
loitable by vector-field methods.
- **Numerical relativity**: the generalized harmonic gauge $\Gamma^\lambda = H^\
lambda(x)$ (a prescribed function) is the basis of many evolution codes; setting
 $H^\lambda=0$ recovers the wave coordinates above.
- **Strichartz and dispersive estimates** for the Einstein equations, which requ
ire a wave-equation structure.

**(e) Principal symbol and characteristics.** Because the principal part is $\ha
t\Box_g$, the **characteristic surfaces** of the Einstein equations are precisel
y the null hypersurfaces of $g$ — confirming the physical expectation that gravi
tational disturbances propagate at the speed of light along null geodesics. This
 is manifest only in the wave-coordinate formulation.

## 8. Summary

In wave coordinates $\Gamma^\lambda = g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}=0$, the
Ricci tensor reduces to

$$R_{\alpha\beta} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\,g^{\lambda\rho}\,\partial_\lambda\partial_\rh
o\, g_{\alpha\beta} + Q_{\alpha\beta}(g)(\partial g,\partial g),$$

so the Einstein vacuum equations $R_{\alpha\beta}=0$ become the **quasilinear sy
stem of nonlinear wave equations**

$$g^{\lambda\rho}\,\partial_\lambda\partial_\rho\, g_{\alpha\beta} = F_{\alpha\b
eta}(g,\partial g), \qquad \alpha,\beta = 0,\dots,3,$$

with $F_{\alpha\beta}$ smooth in $g$ and quadratic in $\partial g$. The wave coo
rdinate condition is propagated by the reduced equations via the Bianchi identit
y, so the reduction is consistent. This formulation makes the Einstein vacuum eq
uations a **hyperbolic system**, yielding local well-posedness (Choquet-Bruhat),
 exposing the null characteristic structure, and providing the analytic foundati
on for both the mathematical theory of general relativity (stability of Minkowsk
i space) and numerical relativity (harmonic/gauge-evolution codes).
