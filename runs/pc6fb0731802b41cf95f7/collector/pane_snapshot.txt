- The circumcenter $O$
- The nine-point center $N$
- The first Fermat point $F_1$
- The second Fermat point $F_2$

**Proof.**

**Step 1: The Lester circle degenerates for an equilateral triangle.**

3}}{4}a^2$. For any prescribed area $S > 0$, such an equilateral triangle exists

In an equilateral triangle, by the full $D_3$ symmetry, the following classical
centers all coincide at the centroid $G$:
- **Circumcenter** $O = G$: The perpendicular bisectors meet at the centroid.
- **Orthocenter** $H = G$: The altitudes meet at the centroid.
- **Nine-point center** $N = \tfrac{1}{2}(O + H) = G$.
- **First Fermat point** $F_1 = G$: The Fermat point is the point from which eac
h side subtends $120°$. At the centroid, $\angle AGB = \angle BGC = \angle CGA =
 120°$ (verified: $GA = GB = GC = a/\sqrt{3}$, so $\cos\angle AGB = \frac{a^2/3
+ a^2/3 - a^2}{2a^2/3} = -\frac{1}{2}$, giving $120°$). ✓
- **Second Fermat point** $F_2 = G$: The second Fermat point is the isogonal con
jugate of $F_1$. The isogonal conjugate of the incenter is itself; in an equilat
eral triangle the centroid coincides with the incenter, so $F_2 = G$. ✓

Thus all four points $O, N, F_1, F_2$ coincide at $G$, and the Lester circle deg
enerates to a point circle of radius $0$.

**Step 2: The infimum is $0$.**

The radius of the Lester circle is always non-negative, so $0$ is a lower bound.
