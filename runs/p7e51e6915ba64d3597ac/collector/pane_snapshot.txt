## Theorem

A closed curve Γ on the unit sphere S² is the tangent indicatrix of some closed
space curve if and only if **the origin lies in the relative interior of the con
vex hull of Γ**.

Equivalently: for every unit vector **n**, either Γ meets both open hemispheres
determined by the great circle {**x**·**n** = 0}, or Γ lies entirely in that gre
at circle.

---

## Proof

### Necessity

Let α: [0, L] → ℝ³ be a closed space curve parametrized by arc length, with tang
ent T(s) = α'(s) tracing Γ. Since α is closed:

$$\int_0^L T(s)\,ds = \alpha(L) - \alpha(0) = \mathbf{0}.$$

This integral is a weighted average of points of Γ with **strictly positive** we
ight density ds. Suppose 0 is **not** in the relative interior of conv(Γ). Then
there exists a supporting hyperplane of conv(Γ) (within aff(Γ)) passing through
the origin: a nonzero vector **n** (with nonzero projection onto aff(Γ)) such th
at **x**·**n** ≥ 0 for all **x** ∈ Γ, with strict inequality for some **x** ∈ Γ.
 Then:

$$0 = \mathbf{n} \cdot \int_0^L T(s)\,ds = \int_0^L T(s)\cdot\mathbf{n}\,ds > 0,
$$

since the integrand is nonnegative everywhere and positive on a set of positive
measure — a contradiction. Hence 0 ∈ relint(conv(Γ)). ∎

### Sufficiency

Let γ: [0, P] → S² be a smooth closed parametrization of Γ. We seek a smooth fun
ction σ > 0 such that:

$$\int_0^P \gamma(t)\,\sigma(t)\,dt = \mathbf{0}. \tag{*}$$

Define the set of all such integrals:

$$\mathcal{I} = \left\{\int_0^P \gamma(t)\,\sigma(t)\,dt \;\middle|\; \sigma \in
 C^\infty,\; \sigma > 0\right\}.$$

**Claim:** $\mathcal{I} = \operatorname{relint}(\operatorname{conv}(\Gamma))$.

**(i) $\mathcal{I} \subseteq \operatorname{relint}(\operatorname{conv}(\Gamma))$
:** Each integral is a positively-weighted average of points of Γ, hence lies in
 conv(Γ). Since σ > 0 everywhere, the integral cannot lie on the relative bounda
ry of conv(Γ) — a boundary point would require zero weight on some exposed face,
 contradicting σ > 0.

**(ii) $\mathcal{I}$ is open in aff(Γ):** Given $\int \gamma\,\sigma_0 \in \math
cal{I}$ and any direction **v** ∈ aff(Γ), since the image of γ spans aff(Γ), the

psilon\tau > 0$ for small ε, and $\int \gamma(\sigma_0 + \varepsilon\tau) = \int
 \gamma\,\sigma_0 + \varepsilon\mathbf{v}$. So we can move freely in any directi

**(iii) $\operatorname{relint}(\operatorname{conv}(\Gamma)) \subseteq \mathcal{I
}$:** Since $\mathcal{I}$ is a nonempty open convex subset of conv(Γ) in the rel

t}(\operatorname{conv}(\Gamma))$, the ray from $q$ through $p$ extends beyond $p

operatorname{conv}(\Gamma)$. Write $p = (1-s)\,q + s\,p'$ with $s = 1/(1+t) \in
(0,1)$.

Now approximate $p'$ by $\int \gamma\,\sigma_1$ with $\sigma_1 > 0$ smooth: writ

Then $(1-s)\sigma_0 + s\sigma_1 > 0$ and $\int \gamma[(1-s)\sigma_0 + s\sigma_1]

By the claim, $0 \in \operatorname{relint}(\operatorname{conv}(\Gamma))$ implies

**Construction of α:** Define

$$\alpha(t) = \int_0^t \gamma(u)\,\sigma(u)\,du.$$

Then:
- $\alpha'(t) = \gamma(t)\,\sigma(t)$, so $|\alpha'(t)| = \sigma(t) > 0$: **α is
 regular**.
- The unit tangent is $T = \alpha'/|\alpha'| = \gamma(t)$, which **traces Γ** as
 $t$ ranges over $[0, P]$.
- $\alpha(P) = \int_0^P \gamma\,\sigma = \mathbf{0} = \alpha(0)$: **α is closed*
*.

Hence α is a closed space curve whose tangent indicatrix is Γ. ∎

### Equivalent hemisphere formulation

The condition $0 \in \operatorname{relint}(\operatorname{conv}(\Gamma))$ is equi
valent to:

> **For every $\mathbf{n} \in S^2$, either Γ intersects both $\{\mathbf{x} \cdot
 \mathbf{n} > 0\}$ and $\{\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{n} < 0\}$, or $\Gamma \subset
 \{\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{n} = 0\}$.**

This holds because $0 \notin \operatorname{relint}(\operatorname{conv}(\Gamma))$
 if and only if there exists $\mathbf{n}$ with $\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{n} \geq
 0$ for all $\mathbf{x} \in \Gamma$ and $\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{n} > 0$ for so
me $\mathbf{x} \in \Gamma$ — that is, Γ is contained in a closed hemisphere but
not entirely in its equator.

**Remark.** This properly handles the planar case: if Γ is a great circle (the t
angent indicatrix of a closed planar curve), it is contained in a closed hemisph
ere but lies entirely in the equator, so the condition is satisfied. A closed se
micircle, by contrast, is contained in a closed hemisphere with only part on the
 equator, so it fails the condition and indeed cannot be a tangent indicatrix (t
he integral $\int \sin t\, \sigma(t)\,dt > 0$ for any $\sigma > 0$).
