## Proof

We prove that for all positive reals $x, y, z$,
$$\sum_{\text{cyc}} \frac{x^{3}}{y^{2}+z^{2}} \geq \frac{x+y+z}{2}.$$

**Step 1: Cauchy–Schwarz (Titu/Engel form).**

Write each term as $\dfrac{x^{3}}{y^{2}+z^{2}}=\dfrac{x^{4}}{x(y^{2}+z^{2})}$. T
hen

$$
\sum_{\text{cyc}} \frac{x^{3}}{y^{2}+z^{2}}
=\sum_{\text{cyc}} \frac{x^{4}}{x(y^{2}+z^{2})}
\geq \frac{(x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2})^{2}}{\displaystyle\sum_{\text{cyc}} x(y^{2}+z^{2}
)}.
$$

It therefore suffices to show

$$
\frac{(x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2})^{2}}{\sum_{\text{cyc}} x(y^{2}+z^{2})} \geq \frac{x+y+
z}{2},
$$

i.e.,

$$
2(x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2})^{2} \geq (x+y+z)\sum_{\text{cyc}} x(y^{2}+z^{2}). \tag{$\st
ar$}
$$

**Step 2: Reformulate in symmetric sums.**

Let $p=x+y+z$, $\,q=xy+yz+zx$, $\,r=xyz$. Then

$$
x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}=p^{2}-2q,
$$

and

$$
\sum_{\text{cyc}} x(y^{2}+z^{2})=x y^{2}+x z^{2}+y z^{2}+y x^{2}+z x^{2}+z y^{2}
=(x+y+z)(xy+yz+zx)-3xyz=pq-3r.
$$

Thus ($\star$) becomes

$$
2(p^{2}-2q)^{2} \geq p(pq-3r),
$$

which expands to

$$
2p^{4}-9p^{2}q+8q^{2}+3pr \geq 0. \tag{$\star\star$}
$$

**Step 3: Apply Schur's inequality.**

Schur's inequality (with parameter $t=1$) gives

$$
p^{3}+9r \geq 4pq \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 9r \geq 4pq-p^{3}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 3pr \geq \frac{4p^{2}q-p^{4}}{3}.
$$

Substituting this lower bound for $3pr$ into ($\star\star$):

$$
2p^{4}-9p^{2}q+8q^{2}+3pr
\;\geq\;
2p^{4}-9p^{2}q+8q^{2}+\frac{4p^{2}q-p^{4}}{3}
=\frac{5p^{4}-23p^{2}q+24q^{2}}{3}.
$$

**Step 4: A quadratic in $t=q/p^{2}$.**

Set $t=\dfrac{q}{p^{2}}$. Since $q=xy+yz+zx\leq \dfrac{(x+y+z)^{2}}{3}=\dfrac{p^
{2}}{3}$, we have $0<t\leq \dfrac{1}{3}$. Now

$$
5p^{4}-23p^{2}q+24q^{2}=p^{4}\bigl(5-23t+24t^{2}\bigr)=p^{4}\cdot 24\!\left(t-\t
frac{5}{8}\right)\!\left(t-\tfrac{1}{3}\right).
$$

The roots of $24t^{2}-23t+5$ are $t=\dfrac{23\pm 7}{48}$, namely $\dfrac{5}{8}$
and $\dfrac{1}{3}$.

For $t\in\bigl(0,\,\tfrac{1}{3}\bigr]$, both factors $\bigl(t-\tfrac{5}{8}\bigr)
$ and $\bigl(t-\tfrac{1}{3}\bigr)$ are $\leq 0$, so their product is $\geq 0$. H
ence

$$
5p^{4}-23p^{2}q+24q^{2}\geq 0,
$$

which gives ($\star\star$), hence ($\star$), hence the desired inequality.

**Equality.** Equality in Cauchy–Schwarz requires $\dfrac{x^{2}}{x(y^{2}+z^{2})}
$ to be constant, i.e. $\dfrac{x}{y^{2}+z^{2}}$ equal for all three; equality in
 Schur and in $q=p^{2}/3$ both require $x=y=z$. Indeed at $x=y=z$ every term equ
als $\dfrac{x}{2}$ and both sides equal $\dfrac{3x}{2}$. $\blacksquare$
