# Galois Group of $x^3 - x - 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$

## Step 1: Irreducibility

Let $f(x) = x^3 - x - 1$. By the Rational Root Theorem, the only possible ration
al roots are $\pm 1$. We check:

$$f(1) = 1 - 1 - 1 = -1 \neq 0, \qquad f(-1) = -1 + 1 - 1 = -1 \neq 0.$$

Since $f$ is a cubic with no rational root, it is **irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}
$**.

## Step 2: The Discriminant

For a depressed cubic $x^3 + px + q$, the discriminant is

$$\Delta = -4p^3 - 27q^2.$$

Here $p = -1$ and $q = -1$, so

$$\Delta = -4(-1)^3 - 27(-1)^2 = 4 - 27 = -23.$$

## Step 3: Determining the Galois Group

Let $K$ be the splitting field of $f$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, and let $G = \mathrm{Ga
l}(K/\mathbb{Q})$.

Since $f$ is irreducible of degree $3$, the group $G$ acts transitively on the t
hree roots $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3$, so $G$ is a transitive subgroup of $S
_3$. The transitive subgroups of $S_3$ are $S_3$ itself and $A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z
}/3\mathbb{Z}$.

A standard fact: for an irreducible cubic over a field of characteristic $\neq 2
$, the Galois group is contained in $A_3$ (i.e., is $A_3$) **if and only if** th
e discriminant is a square in the base field.

**Proof of the fact.** The discriminant satisfies

$$\Delta = \prod_{i < j}(\alpha_i - \alpha_j)^2 = \left[\prod_{i<j}(\alpha_i - \
alpha_j)\right]^2.$$

Let $\delta = \prod_{i<j}(\alpha_i - \alpha_j) \in K$. Then $\Delta = \delta^2$,
 and for any $\sigma \in G$ viewed as a permutation of the roots,

$$\sigma(\delta) = \mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\cdot \delta.$$

Thus $\sigma(\delta) = \delta$ for all $\sigma \in G$ iff every $\sigma$ is even
, i.e., $G \subseteq A_3$. By Galois theory, $\delta \in \mathbb{Q}$ iff $\sigma
(\delta) = \delta$ for all $\sigma \in G$. Therefore $G \subseteq A_3$ iff $\del
ta \in \mathbb{Q}$, which (since $\Delta = \delta^2$ and $\delta \in K$) is equi
valent to $\Delta$ being a square in $\mathbb{Q}$.

## Step 4: Conclusion

We have $\Delta = -23$. Since $-23 < 0$, it is not a square in $\mathbb{Q}$ (a s
quare of a rational number is non-negative). Therefore $G \not\subseteq A_3$, wh
ich forces

$$G = S_3.$$

Equivalently, $|G| = 6$, and the splitting field $K$ has degree $6$ over $\mathb
b{Q}$: it is $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha, \sqrt{\Delta}) = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha, \sqrt{-23})
$, where $\alpha$ is any root of $f$.

$$\boxed{\mathrm{Gal}(x^3-x-1,\,\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_3}$$
