**Answer: $k = 2$.**

## Reformulation

A placement where no pawn attacks another by a rook's move means no two pawns sh
are a row or column — so the pawns form a **permutation**: pawn in row $i$ sits
in column $\pi(i)$, where $\pi$ is a permutation of $\{1,\dots,8\}$.

Two pawns at $(i,\pi(i))$ and $(j,\pi(j))$ form a **knight's move** iff $\{|i-j|
,\,|\pi(i)-\pi(j)|\}=\{1,2\}$. Since $\pi$ is a permutation, the only relevant c
ases are:
- $|i-j|=1$ (adjacent rows): need $|\pi(i)-\pi(j)|\neq 2$;
- $|i-j|=2$: need $|\pi(i)-\pi(j)|\neq 1$.

Two pawns lie on a common diagonal iff $|i-j|=|\pi(i)-\pi(j)|$, i.e. they share
the value $i-\pi(i)$ (NW–SE diagonal) or $i+\pi(i)$ (NE–SW diagonal). We seek
$$k=\min_{\text{valid }\pi}\;\max\Bigl(\max_v\#\{i:i-\pi(i)=v\},\;\max_v\#\{i:i+
\pi(i)=v\}\Bigr).$$

## Upper bound: $k\le 2$ (a valid placement with no diagonal holding $\ge 3$ paw
ns)

Take
$$\pi=(4,7,2,5,8,1,6,3),\quad\text{i.e. positions }(1,4),(2,7),(3,2),(4,5),(5,8)
,(6,1),(7,6),(8,3).$$

**Rook condition:** columns $4,7,2,5,8,1,6,3$ are all distinct. ✓

**Knight condition.** Adjacent rows ($|i-j|=1$): column differences are $3,5,3,3
,7,5,3$ — none equals $2$. Rows at distance $2$: differences are $2,2,6,4,2,2$ —
 none equals $1$. For $|i-j|\ge 3$ no knight move is possible. ✓

**Diagonals.**
- $i-\pi(i)$: $-3,-5,1,-1,-3,5,1,5$. Multiplicities: $-3\mapsto 2$, $1\mapsto 2$
, $5\mapsto 2$, others $\mapsto 1$. Max $=2$.
- $i+\pi(i)$: $5,9,5,9,13,7,13,11$. Multiplicities: $5\mapsto 2$, $9\mapsto 2$,
$13\mapsto 2$, others $\mapsto 1$. Max $=2$.

So every diagonal carries at most $2$ pawns, giving $k\le 2$.

## Lower bound: $k\ge 2$ (no valid placement is a full non-attacking-queens plac
ement)

Suppose, for contradiction, that some valid placement has **at most $1$ pawn on
every diagonal** (both directions). Together with the rook condition this is exa
ctly a **non-attacking $8$-queens placement**: all $i-\pi(i)$ are distinct and a
ll $i+\pi(i)$ are distinct.

Combine the queens condition with the knight condition:
- **Adjacent rows** $i,i+1$: queens give $|\pi(i)-\pi(i+1)|\neq 1$ (else $|i-j|=
|\Delta c|=1$, same diagonal); knight gives $\neq 2$; permutation gives $\neq 0$
. Hence $|\pi(i)-\pi(i+1)|\ge 3$.
- **Rows at distance $2$**, $i,i+2$: queens give $|\pi(i)-\pi(i+2)|\neq 2$; knig
ht gives $\neq 1$; permutation gives $\neq 0$. Hence $|\pi(i)-\pi(i+2)|\ge 3$.

Therefore **every three consecutive rows have their three columns pairwise at di
stance $\ge 3$**.

Now observe: **no $3$-element subset of $\{1,\dots,8\}$ that contains $3$ (or $6
$) has all pairwise differences $\ge 3$.** Indeed, numbers at distance $\ge 3$ f
rom $3$ lie in $\{6,7,8\}$, but among $6,7,8$ the largest pairwise difference is
 $2<3$; so at most one of them can be chosen, yielding fewer than $3$ elements.
The same argument (by symmetry) applies to $6$.

Yet the column value $3$ must occur (it is some $\pi(r)$), and row $r$ belongs t
o some block of three consecutive rows (e.g. $(1,2,3)$ if $r\le 3$, …, $(6,7,8)$
 if $r\ge 6$). So $3$ lies in a $3$-element column-set that should be pairwise $
\ge 3$ apart — a contradiction. The same applies to $6$.

Hence no valid placement can have all diagonals carrying $\le 1$ pawn; every val
id placement has some diagonal with $\ge 2$ pawns. So $k\ge 2$.

## Conclusion

Combining $k\le 2$ and $k\ge 2$,

$$\boxed{k=2}.$$
