## Answer

**No**, $T$ cannot in general be continuously extended to $W_0^{1,p}(\Omega)$. S
uch an extension exists **if and only if** $T$ is continuous on $C_0^\infty(\Ome
ga)$ with respect to the $W^{1,p}$-norm (not merely with respect to the standard
 test-function topology).

### Proof

**Setup and the key distinction.** The space $C_0^\infty(\Omega)$ carries (at le
ast) two natural topologies:

1. **The test-function (LF) topology** — the usual inductive-limit topology unde
r which the continuous dual is the space $\mathcal{D}'(\Omega)$ of distributions
.
2. **The $W^{1,p}$-norm topology** — the restriction of the Sobolev norm $\|\cdo
t\|_{W^{1,p}}$ to $C_0^\infty(\Omega)$.

The hypothesis "$T$ is a continuous linear functional on $C_0^\infty(\Omega)$" r
efers to topology (1): $T\in\mathcal{D}'(\Omega)$ is a distribution. This is **s
trictly weaker** than being continuous in topology (2), because the LF-topology
is finer (has more open sets) than the norm topology restricted to $C_0^\infty$.
 Concretely, a distribution $T$ is norm-continuous on $C_0^\infty$ iff there exi
sts $C>0$ with

$$|T(\varphi)|\le C\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,p}},\qquad\forall\,\varphi\in C_0^\infty(\O
mega). \tag{$\star$}$$

**Necessity and sufficiency of ($\star$).** Since $C_0^\infty(\Omega)$ is dense
in $W_0^{1,p}(\Omega)$ (given), a standard result on extension of bounded linear
 operators from dense subspaces says:

> A linear functional $T$ on $C_0^\infty(\Omega)$ extends to a *continuous* line
ar functional on $W_0^{1,p}(\Omega)$ **if and only if** $T$ is bounded on $C_0^\
infty(\Omega)$ with respect to the $W^{1,p}$-norm, i.e. ($\star$) holds.

- *Sufficiency:* If ($\star$) holds, define $\tilde T(u)=\lim_{k}T(\varphi_k)$ f
or any $\varphi_k\in C_0^\infty$ with $\varphi_k\to u$ in $W^{1,p}$. The bound (
$\star$) makes this well-defined, linear, and continuous with $\|\tilde T\|\le C
$.
- *Necessity:* If a continuous extension $\tilde T\in(W_0^{1,p})^*$ exists, then
 $|T(\varphi)|=|\tilde T(\varphi)|\le\|\tilde T\|\,\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,p}}$, givin
g ($\star$).

So the question reduces to: **does distributional continuity imply ($\star$)?**
It does not.

**Counterexample.** Let $\Omega=(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}$, any $1\le p<\infty$, an
d fix $x_0\in\Omega$. Define

$$T(\varphi)=\varphi'(x_0),\qquad\varphi\in C_0^\infty(0,1).$$

This is the distributional derivative of the Dirac mass: $T=-\delta'_{x_0}$ (up
to sign), hence $T\in\mathcal{D}'(\Omega)$, i.e. $T$ is continuous on $C_0^\inft
y$ in the test-function topology.

We show ($\star$) **fails**. Pick $\psi\in C_0^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ with $\psi'(0
)=1$ and $\operatorname{supp}\psi\subset(-1,1)$, and set

$$\varphi_n(x)=\tfrac{1}{n}\,\psi\!\bigl(n(x-x_0)\bigr),\qquad n\ge1.$$

For $n$ large enough, $\operatorname{supp}\varphi_n\subset(x_0-\tfrac1n,x_0+\tfr
ac1n)\subset(0,1)$, so $\varphi_n\in C_0^\infty(0,1)$. Now:

- $T(\varphi_n)=\varphi_n'(x_0)=\psi'(0)=1$ for every $n$.
- $\|\varphi_n\|_{L^p}^p=\displaystyle\int_0^1\Bigl|\tfrac1n\psi(n(x-x_0))\Bigr|
^p dx=\frac{1}{n^{p+1}}\int|\psi|^p\to0.$
- $\|\varphi_n'\|_{L^p}^p=\displaystyle\int_0^1|\psi'(n(x-x_0))|^p dx=\frac1n\in
t|\psi'|^p\to0.$

Hence $\|\varphi_n\|_{W^{1,p}}\to0$ while $T(\varphi_n)=1\not\to0$. So no consta
nt $C$ can satisfy ($\star$); $T$ is **not** $W^{1,p}$-norm-continuous on $C_0^\
infty$, and therefore **cannot** be continuously extended to $W_0^{1,p}(\Omega)$
.

**Conclusion.** The density of $C_0^\infty(\Omega)$ in $W_0^{1,p}(\Omega)$ guara
ntees a *unique* extension once a continuous extension exists, but it does **not
** by itself guarantee *existence*. Existence requires the additional norm-bound
edness condition ($\star$), which is strictly stronger than being a distribution
. The functional $T(\varphi)=\varphi'(x_0)$ is a continuous linear functional on
 $C_0^\infty(\Omega)$ (a distribution) that admits no continuous extension to $W
_0^{1,p}(\Omega)$. $\blacksquare$
