## Proof

We seek the constant $B$ minimizing the error between $\pi(x)$ and $\frac{x}{\ln
 x - B}$, with the error bounded by $O\!\left(\frac{x}{\ln^3 x}\right)$.

**Step 1: Asymptotic expansion of $\pi(x)$.**

By the Prime Number Theorem (with the strongest known error term), $\pi(x) = \op
eratorname{li}(x) + O\!\left(x\,e^{-c\sqrt{\ln x}}\right)$, where the remainder
is smaller than $O(x/\ln^k x)$ for every fixed $k$. Hence to determine $B$ up to
 the $O(x/\ln^3 x)$ level, it suffices to match $\operatorname{li}(x)$.

The logarithmic integral has the well-known asymptotic expansion (obtained by re
peated integration by parts):

$$\operatorname{li}(x) \sim \frac{x}{\ln x} + \frac{x}{\ln^2 x} + \frac{2x}{\ln^
3 x} + \frac{6x}{\ln^4 x} + \cdots + \frac{(n-1)!\,x}{\ln^n x} + \cdots$$

**Step 2: Expansion of $\frac{x}{\ln x - B}$.**

Write $\frac{x}{\ln x - B} = \frac{x}{\ln x}\cdot\frac{1}{1 - B/\ln x}$ and expa
nd the geometric series (valid asymptotically for large $x$):

$$\frac{x}{\ln x - B} = \frac{x}{\ln x}\left(1 + \frac{B}{\ln x} + \frac{B^2}{\l
n^2 x} + \frac{B^3}{\ln^3 x} + \cdots\right) = \frac{x}{\ln x} + \frac{Bx}{\ln^2
 x} + \frac{B^2 x}{\ln^3 x} + \frac{B^3 x}{\ln^4 x} + \cdots$$

**Step 3: Match coefficients term by term.**

Comparing with $\operatorname{li}(x)$:

| Order | $\operatorname{li}(x)$ coefficient | $\frac{x}{\ln x - B}$ coefficient
 | Match requires |
|-------|-------------------------------------|---------------------------------
----|-----------------|
| $x/\ln x$ | $1$ | $1$ | always |
| $x/\ln^2 x$ | $1$ | $B$ | $B = 1$ |
| $x/\ln^3 x$ | $2$ | $B^2$ | $B^2 = 2$ (impossible simultaneously with $B=1$) |

The $x/\ln^2 x$ term forces $B = 1$. With this choice, the $x/\ln^3 x$ coefficie
nt of the approximation is $B^2 = 1$, while $\operatorname{li}(x)$ has $2$. The
residual at this order is

$$\operatorname{li}(x) - \frac{x}{\ln x - 1} = (2-1)\frac{x}{\ln^3 x} + (6-1)\fr
ac{x}{\ln^4 x} + \cdots = \frac{x}{\ln^3 x} + O\!\left(\frac{x}{\ln^4 x}\right)
= O\!\left(\frac{x}{\ln^3 x}\right).$$

**Step 4: Any other $B$ gives a larger error.**

If $B \neq 1$, the leading mismatch occurs at order $x/\ln^2 x$:

$$\operatorname{li}(x) - \frac{x}{\ln x - B} = (1-B)\frac{x}{\ln^2 x} + O\!\left
(\frac{x}{\ln^3 x}\right),$$

which is $O(x/\ln^2 x)$ — strictly larger than $O(x/\ln^3 x)$. Therefore $B = 1$
 is the **unique** value achieving the $O(x/\ln^3 x)$ bound.

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

Since $\pi(x) - \operatorname{li}(x) = o(x/\ln^3 x)$, the error $\pi(x) - \frac{
x}{\ln x - 1}$ is dominated by the $\operatorname{li}(x) - \frac{x}{\ln x - 1}$
term, which is $O(x/\ln^3 x)$. No other constant achieves this order.

$$\boxed{B = 1}$$
