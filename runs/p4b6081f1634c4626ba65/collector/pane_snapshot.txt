## Proof

**Claim:** There exists a complemented lattice with a non-prime maximal ideal.

**Example:** The diamond lattice $M_3$.

**Construction.** Let $M_3$ be the five-element lattice $\{0, a, b, c, 1\}$ with
 $0 < a, b, c < 1$, where $a, b, c$ are pairwise incomparable, and the meet and
join are given by:
- $a \wedge b = a \wedge c = b \wedge c = 0$,
- $a \vee b = a \vee c = b \vee c = 1$,
- $0 \wedge x = 0$, $0 \vee x = x$, $1 \wedge x = x$, $1 \vee x = 1$ for all $x$
.

**$M_3$ is complemented.** The element $0$ has complement $1$ (and vice versa).
Each of $a, b, c$ is a complement of the other two: e.g., $a \wedge b = 0$ and $
a \vee b = 1$. Hence every element has a complement, so $M_3$ is complemented.

**The ideals of $M_3$.** An ideal is a nonempty, downward-closed, join-closed su
bset. The candidates are:
- $\{0\}$,
- $\{0, a\}$, $\{0, b\}$, $\{0, c\}$,
- $\{0, a, b\}$, $\{0, a, c\}$, $\{0, b, c\}$ — but these are **not** ideals, si
nce e.g. $a, b \in \{0,a,b\}$ yet $a \vee b = 1 \notin \{0,a,b\}$.
- $\{0, a, b, c\}$ — not an ideal, since $a \vee b = 1 \notin \{0,a,b,c\}$.

So the only proper ideals are $\{0\}$, $\{0,a\}$, $\{0,b\}$, $\{0,c\}$.

**The maximal ideals are $\{0,a\}$, $\{0,b\}$, $\{0,c\}$.** Each is maximal sinc
e the only ideal strictly containing it is $M_3$ itself (any two-element ideal $
\{0,x\}$ cannot be enlarged to a three-element ideal, as shown above, and the on
ly larger ideal is the whole lattice).

**$\{0, a\}$ is not prime.** An ideal $I$ is prime if $I \neq M_3$ and $x \wedge
 y \in I \Rightarrow x \in I \text{ or } y \in I$. Take $x = b$ and $y = c$:
$$b \wedge c = 0 \in \{0, a\},$$
yet $b \notin \{0, a\}$ and $c \notin \{0, a\}$.

Therefore $\{0, a\}$ is a maximal ideal that is not prime.

**Conclusion.** The complemented lattice $M_3$ has a maximal ideal $\{0, a\}$ wh
ich is not prime. Hence such a lattice exists. $\blacksquare$
