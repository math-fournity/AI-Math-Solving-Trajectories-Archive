Proof

Setup and definitions.

Let $A \subseteq B$ where $A$ and $B$ are integral domains. The inclusion $A
\hookrightarrow B$ is an injective ring homomorphism. Denote the multiplicative
identities by $1_A$ and $1_B$; under the inclusion we have $1_A = 1_B =: 1$ (the
inclusion being a unital ring homomorphism, or equivalently $1_A$ maps to
$1_B$).

Recall the characteristic of a ring $R$ with $1\neq 0$ is $$\operatorname{char}
R = \min{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0} : n\cdot 1_R = 0_R},$$ and $\operatorname{char} R =
0$ if this set is empty. Equivalently, $\operatorname{char} R$ is the non-
negative generator of the kernel of the canonical ring homomorphism $$
\varphi_R : \mathbb{Z} \to R,\qquad n \mapsto n\cdot 1_R.$$

Key observation.

For every integer $n$, the element $n\cdot 1_A \in A$ is sent by the inclusion $
\iota : A \hookrightarrow B$ to $n\cdot 1_B \in B$. Since $\iota$ is injective,
$$n\cdot 1_A = 0_A \iff \iota(n\cdot 1_A) = \iota(0_A) \iff n\cdot 1_B = 0_B.$$
Therefore $$\ker \varphi_A = {n \in \mathbb{Z} : n\cdot 1_A = 0_A} = {n \in
\mathbb{Z} : n\cdot 1_B = 0_B} = \ker \varphi_B.$$

Conclusion.

The characteristic is the non-negative generator of $\ker \varphi_R$. Since $
\ker\varphi_A = \ker\varphi_B$, their generators coincide: $$\operatorname{char}
A = \operatorname{char} B.$$

In particular, $\operatorname{char} A = p$ if and only if $\operatorname{char} B
= p$, for any prime $p$ (and likewise $\operatorname{char} A = 0$ iff $
\operatorname{char} B = 0$). $\blacksquare$
