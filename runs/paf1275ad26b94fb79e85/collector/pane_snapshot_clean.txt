## WKB Approximation to the Eigenvalues

We consider
$$
\frac{d^2 y}{dx^2} = (E - V(x))\,y(x), \qquad y(\pm\infty)=0, \qquad V(\pm\infty
)=\infty .
$$

Since $V(\pm\infty)=\infty$, every energy level $E$ is bounded, and for each $E$
 there are exactly two (simple) **turning points** $x_1(E)<x_2(E)$ defined by
$$
V(x_1)=V(x_2)=E, \qquad V(x)<E \;\text{ for }\; x_1<x<x_2 .
$$
The interval $(x_1,x_2)$ is the **classically allowed** region ($E-V>0$); the ex
terior is **forbidden** ($V-E>0$).

### 1. WKB ansatz

Write $y(x)=\exp\!\big(\int^x S(x')\,dx'\big)$. Substitution gives the Riccati e
quation
$$
S' + S^2 = E - V(x).
$$
Expand $S=S_0+S_1+\cdots$ with $S_0^2 = E-V$. At next order, $S_0' + 2S_0 S_1 =
0$, so $S_1 = -S_0'/(2S_0)$ and the amplitude factor is $\exp(\int S_1 dx)=(E-V)
^{-1/4}$.

- **Allowed region** $x_1<x<x_2$: $S_0=\pm i\sqrt{E-V}$, giving the oscillatory
WKB form
$$
y \sim (E-V)^{-1/4}\Big[C_+\,e^{\,i\!\int\sqrt{E-V}\,dx}+C_-\,e^{-i\!\int\sqrt{E
-V}\,dx}\Big].
$$

- **Forbidden region**: $S_0=\pm\sqrt{V-E}$; the boundary condition $y(\pm\infty
)=0$ selects the **decaying** branch
$$
y \sim (V-E)^{-1/4}\exp\!\Big(-\!\int\sqrt{V-E}\,dx\Big).
$$

### 2. Connection at the turning points

Near each turning point $V$ is locally linear (generic case), so the equation re
duces to the Airy equation. The standard Airy-matching connection formulas give:

- **Left turning point $x_1$** — the decaying solution for $x<x_1$ connects to
$$
y \sim 2\,(E-V)^{-1/4}\cos\!\Big(\int_{x_1}^{x}\sqrt{E-V}\,dx - \tfrac{\pi}{4}\B
ig), \qquad x_1<x<x_2 .
$$

- **Right turning point $x_2$** — the decaying solution for $x>x_2$ connects to
$$
y \sim 2\,(E-V)^{-1/4}\cos\!\Big(\int_{x}^{x_2}\sqrt{E-V}\,dx - \tfrac{\pi}{4}\B
ig), \qquad x_1<x<x_2 .
$$

### 3. Quantization condition

Both expressions must represent the same solution throughout the allowed region.
 Set
$$
\theta(x)=\int_{x_1}^{x}\sqrt{E-V(x')}\,dx', \qquad I(E)=\int_{x_1}^{x_2}\sqrt{E
-V(x')}\,dx' .
$$
Then $\int_x^{x_2}\sqrt{E-V}\,dx = I-\theta$, so the right-hand form is
$$
\cos\!\Big(I-\theta-\tfrac{\pi}{4}\Big)=\cos\!\Big((I-\tfrac{\pi}{2})-(\theta-\t
frac{\pi}{4})\Big).
$$
Writing $\phi=\theta-\pi/4$, consistency of $\cos\phi$ with
$$
\cos\!\big((I-\tfrac{\pi}{2})-\phi\big)=\cos(I-\tfrac{\pi}{2})\cos\phi+\sin(I-\t
frac{\pi}{2})\sin\phi
$$
for **all** $x$ (hence all $\phi$) requires the $\sin\phi$ coefficient to vanish
:
$$
\sin\!\Big(I-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\Big)=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad I(E)=\Big(n+\tfrac1
2\Big)\pi,\qquad n=0,1,2,\dots
$$

### 4. Result

The WKB (Bohr–Sommerfeld) approximation to the eigenvalues $E_n$ is obtained by
inverting

$$
\boxed{\;\int_{x_1(E)}^{x_2(E)}\sqrt{E-V(x)}\,dx \;=\; \Big(n+\tfrac12\Big)\pi,\
qquad n=0,1,2,\dots\;}
$$

where $x_1(E)<x_2(E)$ are the two turning points satisfying $V(x_1)=V(x_2)=E$.

**Consistency check.** For the harmonic oscillator $V=\tfrac12 x^2$ (in units $\
hbar=m=\omega=1$, so the equation is $y''=(E-\tfrac12 x^2)y$), the turning point
s are $x_{1,2}=\mp\sqrt{2E}$ and
$$
\int_{-\sqrt{2E}}^{\sqrt{2E}}\sqrt{E-\tfrac{x^2}{2}}\,dx = \tfrac{\pi E}{2\cdot
\tfrac12}\;(\text{area of semi-ellipse}) = \pi E .
$$
The condition $\pi E=(n+\tfrac12)\pi$ gives $E_n=n+\tfrac12$, the exact spectrum
 — confirming the formula (WKB is exact for the quadratic potential, as expected
).
