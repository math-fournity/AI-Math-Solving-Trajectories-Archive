**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

Let $(X, d)$ be a metric space whose metric is bounded, i.e. there exists $M < \
infty$ such that $d(x,y) \le M$ for all $x, y \in X$. Assume $(X,d)$ has the Hei
ne–Borel property: a subset of $X$ is compact if and only if it is closed and bo
unded. (The forward implication—compact $\Rightarrow$ closed and bounded—holds a
utomatically in any metric space; the substantive content of the Heine–Borel pro
perty is the converse: closed and bounded $\Rightarrow$ compact.)

Let $F \subseteq X$ be an arbitrary closed set. We show $F$ is compact.

1. **$F$ is closed.** This is given.

2. **$F$ is bounded.** Since $F \subseteq X$ and $d$ is bounded on all of $X$ by
 $M$, we have for every $x, y \in F$:
$$d(x,y) \le M.$$
Hence $\operatorname{diam}(F) \le M < \infty$, so $F$ is bounded. (Boundedness i
s hereditary: every subset of a bounded set is bounded.)

3. **$F$ is compact.** $F$ is closed and bounded, so by the Heine–Borel property
 of $(X,d)$, $F$ is compact.

Since $F$ was an arbitrary closed set, **every closed subset of $X$ is compact**
. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The key observation is that a bounded metric makes *every* subset bo
unded, so the "bounded" hypothesis in the Heine–Borel characterization becomes v
acuous. Thus "closed and bounded" collapses to "closed", and the Heine–Borel pro
perty reduces to "closed $\Leftrightarrow$ compact" (for the non-trivial directi
on). In particular the whole space $X$ itself, being closed and bounded, is comp
act.
