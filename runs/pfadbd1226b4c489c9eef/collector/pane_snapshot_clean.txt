## Proof

**Step 1: Simplify the sectional curvature under the given conditions.**

The denominator of $K(x^i; u, v)$ is the squared area of the parallelogram spann
ed by $u$ and $v$:

$$(g_{km}g_{\ell n} - g_{k\ell}g_{mn})\,u^k v^\ell u^m v^n = (u\cdot u)(v\cdot v
) - (u\cdot v)^2.$$

Since $u \perp v$, we have $u \cdot v = g_{k\ell}\,u^k v^\ell = 0$. Since $v$ is
 a unit vector, $v \cdot v = 1$. Therefore the denominator reduces to:

$$|u|^2 = g_{ij}\,u^i u^j.$$

So the sectional curvature becomes:

$$K(x^i; u, v) = \frac{R_{k\ell mn}\,u^k v^\ell u^m v^n}{|u|^2}.$$

**Step 2: Define the mean curvature as the average over all unit $v \perp u$.**

The (normalized) mean curvature in the direction $u$ is:

$$K_u = \frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(S^{n-2})}\int_{\substack{v \perp u \\ |v|=1}} K(x^
i; u, v)\,d\Omega = \frac{1}{|u|^2\,\mathrm{Vol}(S^{n-2})}\,R_{k\ell mn}\,u^k u^
m \int_{\substack{v\perp u\\|v|=1}} v^\ell v^n\,d\Omega.$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the angular integral using isotropy of the sphere.**

By the rotational symmetry of the unit sphere in the $(n{-}1)$-dimensional subsp
ace orthogonal to $u$, the second moment of $v$ is:

$$\int v^\ell v^n\,d\Omega = \frac{\mathrm{Vol}(S^{n-2})}{n-1}\left(g^{\ell n} -
 \frac{u^\ell u^n}{|u|^2}\right),$$

where $P^{\ell n} = g^{\ell n} - u^\ell u^n/|u|^2$ is the orthogonal projection
onto the subspace $\perp u$.

**Step 4: Substitute and split into two terms.**

$$K_u = \frac{1}{(n-1)|u|^2}\,R_{k\ell mn}\,u^k u^m\left(g^{\ell n} - \frac{u^\e
ll u^n}{|u|^2}\right).$$

This gives two terms:

- **Term 1:** $\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n-1)|u|^2}\,R_{k\ell mn}\,u^k u^m\,g^{\ell
 n}$

- **Term 2:** $\displaystyle -\frac{1}{(n-1)|u|^4}\,R_{k\ell mn}\,u^k u^\ell u^m
 u^n$

**Step 5: Show Term 2 vanishes.**

By the antisymmetry of the Riemann tensor in its first index pair, $R_{k\ell mn}
 = -R_{\ell k mn}$. Therefore:

$$R_{k\ell mn}\,u^k u^\ell = -R_{\ell k mn}\,u^k u^\ell = -R_{k\ell mn}\,u^\ell
u^k = -R_{k\ell mn}\,u^k u^\ell,$$

where the last equality renames dummy indices $k \leftrightarrow \ell$. This for
ces $R_{k\ell mn}\,u^k u^\ell = 0$, so **Term 2 = 0**.

**Step 6: Evaluate Term 1 using the Ricci tensor.**

We need $g^{\ell n} R_{k\ell mn}$. Using the pair symmetries:

$$R_{k\ell mn} = -R_{\ell k mn} \quad\text{and}\quad R_{\ell k mn} = -R_{\ell k
nm},$$

so $R_{k\ell mn} = R_{\ell k nm}$. Therefore:

$$g^{\ell n} R_{k\ell mn} = g^{\ell n} R_{\ell k\, nm}.$$

In $R_{\ell k\, nm}$, the indices $\ell$ (1st of the first pair) and $n$ (1st of
 the second pair) are contracted. This is precisely the Ricci tensor contraction
 $R_{ij} = g^{ab}R_{a i\, b j}$, giving:

$$g^{\ell n} R_{\ell k\, nm} = R_{km}.$$

**Step 7: Assemble the final result.**

$$K_u = \frac{R_{km}\,u^k u^m}{(n-1)\,|u|^2} = \frac{R_{ij}\,u^i u^j}{(n-1)\,g_{
ij}\,u^i u^j}.$$

---

### Result

The normalized mean curvature in the direction $u$ is:

$$\boxed{K_u = \frac{R_{ij}\,u^i u^j}{(n-1)\,g_{ij}\,u^i u^j}}$$

where $n$ is the dimension of the manifold, $R_{ij}$ is the Ricci tensor, and $|
u|^2 = g_{ij}\,u^i u^j$. This is the average of the sectional curvature $K(u,v)$
 over all unit vectors $v$ orthogonal to $u$: the numerator $R_{ij}\,u^i u^j = \
mathrm{Ric}(u,u)$ is the Ricci curvature in direction $u$, and the factor $(n-1)
$ accounts for the dimension of the space of directions $v$ orthogonal to $u$.
