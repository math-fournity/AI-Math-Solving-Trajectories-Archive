## Answer

Both integrals are finite if and only if $\displaystyle p < \frac{2\log 2}{\log
3}$.

---

## Proof

Let $\mu$ denote the Cantor–Lebesgue measure (the Stieltjes measure $d\sigma$ as
sociated with the Cantor function $\sigma$). It is a probability measure support
ed on the Cantor set $K\subset[0,1]$, obtained as the self‑similar measure with
contractions $S_0(x)=x/3$, $S_2(x)=(x+2)/3$ and equal weights $1/2,1/2$.

Set $\nu:=\mu\times\mu$, a probability measure supported on $K\times K\subset\ma
thbb R^2$.

**Key facts.**

1. *Self‑similarity dimension.* $K$ is the self‑similar set of an IFS with two m
aps of ratio $1/3$ satisfying the open set condition, so
$$\dim_H K = \frac{\log 2}{\log 3}=:d_0.$$
Likewise $K\times K$ is self‑similar with four maps of ratio $1/3$, hence
$$\dim_H(K\times K)=2d_0 =: d = \frac{2\log 2}{\log 3}.$$

2. *Ahlfors regularity.* The Cantor measure $\mu$ is Ahlfors $d_0$–regular: ther
e exist constants $0<c\le C<\infty$ such that for every $x\in K$ and every $0<r\
le 1$,
$$c\,r^{d_0}\le \mu(B(x,r))\le C\,r^{d_0}.$$
Consequently the product $\nu=\mu\times\mu$ is Ahlfors $d$–regular on $K\times K
$: for every $a\in K\times K$ and $0<r\le 1$,
$$c'\,r^{d}\le \nu(B(a,r))\le C'\,r^{d}. \tag{$\star$}$$

3. *Energy lemma.* Let $\eta$ be a finite Borel measure on $\mathbb R^n$ that is
 Ahlfors $d$–regular at a point $a$ (i.e. $(\star)$ holds at $a$ for small $r$).
 Then
$$\int |z-a|^{-p}\,d\eta(z)<\infty \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p<d.$$
*Proof of lemma.* Split the support into dyadic annuli $A_n=\{z:2^{-(n+1)}<|z-a|
\le 2^{-n}\}$. On $A_n$ we have $|z-a|^{-p}\asymp 2^{np}$, and $\eta(A_n)\le \et
a(B(a,2^{-n}))\le C\,2^{-nd}$ while $\eta(A_n)\ge \eta(B(a,2^{-n}))-\eta(B(a,2^{
-(n+1)}))\ge (c-C2^{-d})\,2^{-nd}>0$. Hence
$$\int |z-a|^{-p}\,d\eta \asymp \sum_{n\ge 0} 2^{np}\,\eta(A_n)\asymp \sum_{n\ge
 0}2^{n(p-d)},$$
which converges exactly when $p<d$. The contribution from $\{|z-a|\ge 1\}$ is bo
unded since $\eta$ is finite and the integrand is bounded there. ∎

---

### Part (a)

The integral is
$$I_a(p)=\iint_{[0,1]^2}\frac{d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)}{(x^2+y^2)^{p/2}}=\int_{K\times K
}|z|^{-p}\,d\nu(z),\qquad z=(x,y).$$
The denominator vanishes only at $z=(0,0)$, and $(0,0)\in K\times K$. Away from
a neighborhood of the origin the integrand is continuous and bounded on the comp
act support, so finiteness is governed entirely by the behavior near $(0,0)$.

By ($\star$) applied at $a=(0,0)$, $\nu(B((0,0),r))\asymp r^d$ for $0<r\le 1$. (
Indeed $K\times K\cap[0,3^{-n}]^2=3^{-n}(K\times K)$ and $\nu(3^{-n}(K\times K))
=4^{-n}=(3^{-n})^{d}$, confirming exact scaling.) The energy lemma with $a=(0,0)
$ gives
$$I_a(p)<\infty \iff p<d=\frac{2\log 2}{\log 3}.$$

---

### Part (b)

Fix $(u,v)\in K\times K$. The integral is
$$I_b(p;u,v)=\int_{K\times K}\frac{d\nu(z)}{|z-(u,v)|^{p}},\qquad z=(x,y).$$
The only singularity is at $z=(u,v)\in K\times K$. By the Ahlfors regularity ($\
star$), which holds *uniformly* at every point of $K\times K$, we have $\nu(B((u
,v),r))\asymp r^{d}$ for $0<r\le 1$. The energy lemma (applied at $a=(u,v)$) yie
lds, independently of $(u,v)\in K\times K$,
$$I_b(p;u,v)<\infty \iff p<d=\frac{2\log 2}{\log 3}.$$

---

### Conclusion

For **both** parts (a) and (b), the integral is finite exactly for
$$\boxed{\,p<\dfrac{2\log 2}{\log 3}\,}.$$

The threshold is the Hausdorff dimension of $K\times K$ (equivalently twice the
Hausdorff dimension $\log 2/\log 3$ of the Cantor set), reflecting the Ahlfors r
egularity of the Cantor measure at every point of its support.
