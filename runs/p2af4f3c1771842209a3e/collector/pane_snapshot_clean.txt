## Proof

We interpret $\xi(s)$ as the Riemann zeta function $\zeta(s)$, since the identit
y $\zeta(s)^2 = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} d(n)\,n^{-s}$ is the classical Dirichlet ser
ies for the divisor function (obtained from $\zeta(s)=\sum n^{-s}$ and the convo
lution $d = \mathbf{1}*\mathbf{1}$). The completed $\xi$-function is entire, so
it cannot be meant here.

---

**Step 1 — Perron's formula.** Set $D(s) = \sum_{n\ge 1} d(n)n^{-s} = \zeta(s)^2
$, absolutely convergent for $\Re(s)>1$. By (truncated) Perron's formula, with $
c = 1+1/\log N$ and $T\ge 2$,

$$\sum_{n\le N} d(n) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-iT}^{c+iT} \zeta(s)^2\,\frac{N^s}
{s}\,ds \;+\; E_1,$$

where the truncation error satisfies $E_1 = O\!\bigl(N^{1+\epsilon}/T\bigr)$ (us
ing $d(n)\ll n^\epsilon$ and the standard Perron remainder estimate).

---

**Step 2 — Contour shift.** Let $\alpha = \tfrac12+\epsilon$ (small $\epsilon>0$
) and consider the rectangle with vertices $c\pm iT,\;\alpha\pm iT$. The integra
nd $f(s)=\zeta(s)^2\,N^s/s$ has poles inside at:

- $s=1$: **double pole** (from $\zeta(s)^2$, since $\zeta$ has a simple pole at
$1$),
- $s=0$: **simple pole** (from $1/s$; note $\zeta(0)^2 = \tfrac14\neq 0$).

By the residue theorem,

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-iT}^{c+iT} f(s)\,ds = \operatorname{Res}_{s=1}f(s) + \
operatorname{Res}_{s=0}f(s) - \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\alpha-iT}^{\alpha+iT} f(s)\
,ds + E_H,$$

where $E_H$ collects the two horizontal integrals at height $\pm T$.

---

**Step 3 — Residue at $s=1$ (the main term).** Near $s=1$,

$$\zeta(s) = \frac{1}{s-1} + \gamma + O(s-1), \qquad\text{so}\qquad \zeta(s)^2 =
 \frac{1}{(s-1)^2} + \frac{2\gamma}{s-1} + O(1).$$

Also

$$\frac{N^s}{s} = \frac{N\cdot N^{s-1}}{1+(s-1)} = N\Bigl(1 + (s-1)\log N + \cdo
ts\Bigr)\Bigl(1-(s-1)+\cdots\Bigr) = N\Bigl(1+(s-1)(\log N - 1)+\cdots\Bigr).$$

Multiplying,

$$f(s) = \Bigl(\frac{1}{(s-1)^2}+\frac{2\gamma}{s-1}+\cdots\Bigr)\;N\Bigl(1+(s-1
)(\log N-1)+\cdots\Bigr).$$

The coefficient of $\frac{1}{s-1}$ is

$$\operatorname{Res}_{s=1} f(s) = N\cdot 2\gamma + N(\log N - 1) = N\log N + (2\
gamma-1)N.$$

---

**Step 4 — Residue at $s=0$.** Since $\zeta(0)=-\tfrac12$,

$$\operatorname{Res}_{s=0} f(s) = \zeta(0)^2\cdot N^0 = \tfrac14.$$

---

**Step 5 — The shifted vertical integral.** On $\Re(s)=\alpha=\tfrac12+\epsilon$
 with $|t|\ge 1$, the convexity bound gives $|\zeta(\alpha+it)|\ll |t|^{(1-\alph
a)/2+\epsilon'} = |t|^{1/4-\epsilon/2+\epsilon'}$. Hence

$$|f(s)| \ll N^{1/2+\epsilon}\,|t|^{-1/2-\epsilon+2\epsilon'},$$

which is integrable in $t$ (exponent $<-\tfrac12$). Therefore

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\alpha-i\infty}^{\alpha+i\infty} f(s)\,ds = O\!\bigl(N^{
1/2+\epsilon}\bigr),$$

and the truncation to height $T$ introduces only $O(N^{1/2+\epsilon}T^{-\delta})
$, negligible.

---

**Step 6 — Horizontal integrals.** The horizontal contribution at height $T$ is

$$\int_{\alpha}^{c}|\zeta(\sigma+iT)|^2\,\frac{N^\sigma}{T}\,d\sigma.$$

- For $\sigma\in[1,c]$: $|\zeta(\sigma+iT)|\ll 1$, giving $O(N^c/T)=O(N/T)$.
- For $\sigma\in[\alpha,1]$: $|\zeta(\sigma+iT)|^2\ll T^{1-\sigma+\epsilon''}$,
so the integral is $\frac{1}{T}\int_\alpha^1 T^{1-\sigma+\epsilon''}N^\sigma\,d\
sigma = T^{\epsilon''}\int_\alpha^1 (N/T)^\sigma\,d\sigma$.

**Choosing $T=N$**, this becomes $O(N^{\epsilon''})$, and the Perron error becom
es $E_1 = O(N^{1+\epsilon}/N) = O(N^\epsilon)$. All are negligible.

---

**Step 7 — Assembly.** Combining every term,

$$\sum_{n\le N} d(n) = N\log N + (2\gamma-1)N + \tfrac14 + O\!\bigl(N^{1/2+\epsi
lon}\bigr).$$

Since $(2\gamma-1)N = O(N) = o(N\log N)$ and $O(N^{1/2+\epsilon})=o(N\log N)$, t
he dominant term is $N\log N$. Therefore

$$\boxed{\sum_{n\le N} d(n) \;\sim\; N\log N \qquad (N\to\infty).}$$
