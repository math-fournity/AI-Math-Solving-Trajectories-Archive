## Proof

**Answer: Yes, $f^*$ is injective.**

The key tool is the Universal Coefficient Theorem for cohomology with field coef
ficients, together with naturality and a basic fact from linear algebra.

---

**Step 1: UCT with field coefficients.**

Since $\mathbb{k}$ is a field, $\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{k}}(-, \mathbb{k})
 = 0$. The Universal Coefficient Theorem for cohomology gives natural isomorphis
ms:

$$H^i(X; \mathbb{k}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(H_i(X; \mathbb{k}),\, \mathbb{k}),
 \qquad H^i(Y; \mathbb{k}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(H_i(Y; \mathbb{k}),\, \mathb
b{k}).$$

**Step 2: Naturality.**

The UCT isomorphism is natural with respect to maps induced by continuous maps.
Since $f: X \to Y$ is continuous, the following diagram commutes:

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
H^i(Y; \mathbb{k}) & \xrightarrow{\;f^*\;} & H^i(X; \mathbb{k}) \\
\downarrow{\cong} & & \downarrow{\cong} \\
\operatorname{Hom}(H_i(Y; \mathbb{k}), \mathbb{k}) & \xrightarrow{\;(f_*)^\vee\;
} & \operatorname{Hom}(H_i(X; \mathbb{k}), \mathbb{k})
\end{array}
$$

where $(f_*)^\vee$ is the dual (transpose) of $f_*: H_i(X; \mathbb{k}) \to H_i(Y
; \mathbb{k})$, defined by $(f_*)^\vee(\varphi) = \varphi \circ f_*$.

By commutativity of the diagram, $f^*$ is injective if and only if $(f_*)^\vee$
is injective.

**Step 3: Linear algebra lemma.**

*Lemma.* Let $T: V \to W$ be a linear map of vector spaces over $\mathbb{k}$. Th
e dual map $T^\vee: W^* \to V^*$, $T^\vee(\varphi) = \varphi \circ T$, is inject
ive if and only if $T$ is surjective.

*Proof of lemma.*
- ($\Leftarrow$) Suppose $T$ is surjective and $T^\vee(\varphi) = 0$, i.e., $\va
rphi \circ T = 0$. For any $w \in W$, surjectivity gives $v \in V$ with $T(v) =
w$, so $\varphi(w) = \varphi(T(v)) = 0$. Hence $\varphi = 0$, proving $T^\vee$ i
s injective.
- ($\Rightarrow$) Suppose $T$ is not surjective, so $\operatorname{im}(T) \subse
tneq W$. There exists a nonzero linear functional $\varphi \in W^*$ vanishing on
 $\operatorname{im}(T)$ (extend a basis of $\operatorname{im}(T)$ to a basis of
$W$ and define $\varphi$ to be zero on $\operatorname{im}(T)$ and nonzero on the
 remaining basis vectors). Then $T^\vee(\varphi) = \varphi \circ T = 0$ but $\va
rphi \neq 0$, so $T^\vee$ is not injective. $\square$

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Apply the lemma with $T = f_*: H_i(X; \mathbb{k}) \to H_i(Y; \mathbb{k})$. By hy
pothesis, $f_*$ is surjective. Therefore $(f_*)^\vee$ is injective. By the natur
ality of the UCT (Step 2), $f^*$ is injective.

---

**Remark.** The hypotheses that $X, Y$ are finite simplicial complexes, that $X$
 is a subcomplex of $Y$, and that $f$ is the inclusion map are not needed for th
e conclusion. The result holds for any continuous map $f: X \to Y$ between any t
opological spaces, provided the coefficient ring is a field (so that $\operatorn
ame{Ext}$ vanishes and the UCT provides a natural isomorphism). The linear algeb
ra fact (dual of a surjection is an injection) holds even for infinite-dimension
al vector spaces.
