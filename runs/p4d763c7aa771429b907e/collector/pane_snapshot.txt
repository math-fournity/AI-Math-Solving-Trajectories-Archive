Looking at this problem, I need to determine when the product of two repunits R_
m · R_n (where R_k = 111...1 with k ones) is a palindrome.

## Setting Up the Structure

Let R_k = (10^k − 1)/9, the repunit with k ones. The product R_m · R_n is a conv
olution: the raw digit at position j (0-indexed from least significant) equals t
he number of pairs (i, j−i) with 0 ≤ i ≤ m−1, 0 ≤ j−i ≤ n−1.

Assuming m ≤ n, the raw digit sequence (least to most significant) is:

$$1, 2, 3, \ldots, m-1, \underbrace{m, m, \ldots, m}_{n-m+1 \text{ times}}, m-1,
 \ldots, 2, 1$$

This raw pattern is **always symmetric** (palindromic). The question is whether
carrying preserves this.

## Case 1: min(m,n) ≤ 9 — Palindrome ✓

If m ≤ 9, every raw digit is at most m ≤ 9, so **no carrying occurs**. The raw p
alindromic pattern is the final number, which is a palindrome.

*Example:* R_3 · R_5 = 111 · 11111 = 1233321. ✓

## Case 2: min(m,n) ≥ 10 — Not a Palindrome ✗

Assume 10 ≤ m ≤ n ≤ 20. I show the product is never a palindrome by identifying
two symmetric positions with different digits.

**Position 9 (from the right):** Raw digit = 10 (since 9 < m). Carry-in from pos
ition 8 is 0 (raw digit 9 < 10). So: total = 10, **final digit = 0**, carry-out
= 1.

**Position m+n−11 (the symmetric counterpart of position 9):** By symmetry of ra
w digits, this position also has raw digit 10. I need to track the carry reachin
g it.

The carry of 1 from position 9 propagates upward through positions 10, 11, ....
I verify the carry reaching position m+n−11 is always **1**:

- **m = 10–18:** Carry stays 1 through the ascending part (totals 12–19, all car
ry 1), through the plateau (m+1 ≤ 19, carry 1), and into the descending part unt
il it dies. Position m+n−11 has raw digit 10, carry-in 1 → total 11 → **final di
git = 1**.

- **m = 19:** Carry becomes 2 at position 18 (total 20), stays 2 through the pla
teau, drops back to 1 at position n+1 (raw 17 + carry 2 = 19, carry 1). Position
 m+n−11 = n+8 is after n+1, so carry-in = 1 → total 11 → **final digit = 1**.

- **m = 20:** Carry becomes 2 at position 18, stays 2 through the plateau, drops
 to 1 at position n+2 (raw 17 + carry 2 = 19, carry 1). Position m+n−11 = n+9 is
 after n+2, so carry-in = 1 → total 11 → **final digit = 1**.

In every case: **position 9 has digit 0, position m+n−11 has digit 1**, and 0 ≠
1, so the product is **not a palindrome**.

*Verification:* R_10² = 1234567900987654321 — reading from both ends: ...7,**9**

## Counting

R_m · R_n is a palindrome **if and only if** min(m,n) ≤ 9.

Counting ordered pairs (m,n) with 1 ≤ m,n ≤ 20 and min(m,n) ≤ 9:

$$\text{Total pairs} - \text{pairs with both} \geq 10 = 20 \times 20 - 11 \times
 11 = 400 - 121 = \boxed{279}$$

h n ≤ 9: 20×9 = 180. Pairs with both ≤ 9: 9×9 = 81. Result: 180 + 180 − 81 = 279
