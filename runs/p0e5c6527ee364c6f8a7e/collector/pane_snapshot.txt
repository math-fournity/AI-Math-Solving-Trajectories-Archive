## Proof

We analyze the given first coordinate of the solution:
$$x_1(t) = 5t^2\cos(3t) + 2\sin(3t) - e^{2t}\sin(3t) + 4t.$$

**Key principle.** For a linear system $x' = Ax$ with constant $n \times n$ matr
ix $A$, every component of every solution is a linear combination of scalar basi
s functions of the form
$$t^k e^{\lambda t}\cos(\mu t) \quad\text{and}\quad t^k e^{\lambda t}\sin(\mu t)
,$$
where $\lambda + i\mu$ is an eigenvalue of $A$ and $k$ ranges from $0$ up to (Jo
rdan block size $- 1$). Specifically:

- A **real** eigenvalue $\lambda$ with a Jordan block of size $m$ contributes th
e functions $t^k e^{\lambda t}$ for $k = 0, 1, \ldots, m-1$, using $m$ real dime
nsions.
- A **complex conjugate pair** $\lambda \pm i\mu$ with a complex Jordan block of
 size $m$ contributes $t^k e^{\lambda t}\cos(\mu t)$ and $t^k e^{\lambda t}\sin(
\mu t)$ for $k = 0, 1, \ldots, m-1$, using $2m$ real dimensions.

The dimension $n$ equals the sum of the real dimensions of all Jordan blocks.

**Term-by-term decomposition of $x_1(t)$.**

| Term | $\lambda$ | $\mu$ | $k$ | Eigenvalue $\lambda+i\mu$ | Min. Jordan block
 size |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $5t^2\cos(3t)$ | $0$ | $3$ | $2$ | $0+3i$ | $3$ (since $k_{\max}=2$) |
| $2\sin(3t)$ | $0$ | $3$ | $0$ | $0+3i$ | already covered |
| $-e^{2t}\sin(3t)$ | $2$ | $3$ | $0$ | $2+3i$ | $1$ |
| $4t$ | $0$ | $0$ | $1$ | $0$ (real) | $2$ (since $k_{\max}=1$) |

**Three distinct eigenvalues are required:**

1. **Eigenvalue $0 + 3i$** (complex pair $\pm 3i$): The term $t^2\cos(3t)$ force
s a complex Jordan block of size $\geq 3$. A block of size exactly $3$ yields th
e six basis functions $\cos(3t), \sin(3t), t\cos(3t), t\sin(3t), t^2\cos(3t), t^
2\sin(3t)$, which span both $t^2\cos(3t)$ and $\sin(3t)$. Real dimension: $2 \ti
mes 3 = 6$.

2. **Eigenvalue $2 + 3i$** (complex pair $2 \pm 3i$): The term $e^{2t}\sin(3t)$
forces this eigenvalue with a complex Jordan block of size $\geq 1$. A block of
size $1$ yields $e^{2t}\cos(3t)$ and $e^{2t}\sin(3t)$. Real dimension: $2 \times
 1 = 2$.

3. **Eigenvalue $0$** (real): The term $4t$ forces a real Jordan block of size $
\geq 2$. A block of size $2$ yields $1$ and $t$. Real dimension: $2$.

**These eigenvalues are distinct** ($0+3i \neq 2+3i \neq 0$), so no Jordan block
 can serve double duty; each requires its own separate block.

**Lower bound.** Each of the three groups is forced by a term that cannot be pro
duced by the other eigenvalues (the functions $t^k e^{\lambda t}\cos(\mu t)$ for
 different $(\lambda, \mu)$ are linearly independent). Hence $n \geq 6 + 2 + 2 =
 10$.

**Upper bound (achievability).** Construct $A$ in real Jordan canonical form wit
h:
- one $6 \times 6$ block for the complex pair $\pm 3i$ with complex Jordan block
 size $3$,
- one $2 \times 2$ block for the complex pair $2 \pm 3i$ with complex Jordan blo
ck size $1$,
- one $2 \times 2$ real Jordan block for eigenvalue $0$.

This gives $A$ of size $10 \times 10$. The span of the resulting scalar basis fu
nctions includes all four terms of $x_1(t)$ (with the remaining basis functions
assigned coefficient $0$). Since $x_1(t)$ is the first component of $e^{At}c$ fo
r a suitable constant vector $c$, and the first row of $e^{At}$ is a linear comb
ination of all basis functions, we can choose $c$ so that $x_1(t)$ matches the g
iven expression exactly.

**Conclusion.** The minimum dimension is:

$$\boxed{n = 10}$$
