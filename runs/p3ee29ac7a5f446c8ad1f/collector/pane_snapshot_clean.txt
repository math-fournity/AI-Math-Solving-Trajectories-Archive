No. **No subset of the metric space $\mathbb{Q}\times\mathbb{Q}$ has a condensat
ion point.**

## Proof

Recall that a point $p$ in a metric space $(X,d)$ is a *condensation point* of a
 set $A\subseteq X$ if every open neighborhood of $p$ contains **uncountably** m
any points of $A$.

Here the ambient metric space is $X = \mathbb{Q}\times\mathbb{Q}$, equipped with
 the Euclidean metric $d\bigl((x_1,y_1),(x_2,y_2)\bigr)=\sqrt{(x_1-x_2)^2+(y_1-y
_2)^2}$, i.e. the subspace metric inherited from $\mathbb{R}^2$.

**Step 1: $\mathbb{Q}\times\mathbb{Q}$ is countable.**

$\mathbb{Q}$ is countable, and the Cartesian product of two countable sets is co
untable. Hence $X=\mathbb{Q}\times\mathbb{Q}$ is countable.

**Step 2: Every subset of $X$ is countable.**

Let $A\subseteq X$. Since $A\subseteq \mathbb{Q}\times\mathbb{Q}$ and a subset o
f a countable set is countable, $A$ is countable (at most).

**Step 3: No neighborhood in $X$ can contain uncountably many points of $A$.**

Let $p\in X$ and let $U$ be any open neighborhood of $p$ in $X$. Then $U\subsete
q X$, so $U\cap A\subseteq A$. Since $A$ is countable, $U\cap A$ is countable. I
n particular, $U\cap A$ is **not** uncountable.

**Step 4: No point of $X$ is a condensation point of any subset.**

A condensation point of $A$ requires *every* neighborhood of $p$ to contain unco
untably many points of $A$. By Step 3, *no* neighborhood of any point $p\in X$ c
ontains uncountably many points of $A$ (for any $A\subseteq X$). Therefore no po
int $p\in X$ is a condensation point of any subset $A\subseteq X$.

**Conclusion.** Since the ambient space is $\mathbb{Q}\times\mathbb{Q}$ itself (
condensation points are defined relative to the metric space in which the set li
ves), and every subset of $\mathbb{Q}\times\mathbb{Q}$ is countable, no subset o
f $\mathbb{Q}\times\mathbb{Q}$ possesses a condensation point. $\blacksquare$
