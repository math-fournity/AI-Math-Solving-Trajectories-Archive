## Proof

Suppose, for contradiction, that such polynomials exist. Write

$$a(x)=\sum_i a_i x^i,\quad b(x)=\sum_i b_i x^i,\quad c(y)=\sum_j c_j y^j,\quad
d(y)=\sum_j d_j y^j.$$

Consider the coefficient matrix $M$ of the bivariate polynomial $P(x,y)=1+xy+x^2
y^2$, indexed by $(i,j)$, so that $M_{ij}=[x^i y^j]\,P(x,y)$. The only nonzero e
ntries are

$$M_{00}=M_{11}=M_{22}=1,$$

and all other entries are $0$.

On the other hand, the coefficient matrix of $a(x)c(y)$ is the outer product $(a
_i c_j)_{i,j}$, which is a rank-$\le 1$ matrix. Likewise the coefficient matrix
of $b(x)d(y)$ is $(b_i d_j)_{i,j}$, also rank $\le 1$. Hence $M$ is a sum of two
 matrices each of rank $\le 1$, so

$$\operatorname{rank}(M)\le 2.$$

Now restrict attention to the submatrix indexed by rows and columns $\{0,1,2\}$.
 This submatrix is

$$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix},$$

the $3\times 3$ identity matrix, which has rank $3$. Since a submatrix cannot ha
ve rank exceeding that of the full matrix, we obtain

$$\operatorname{rank}(M)\ge 3.$$

This contradicts $\operatorname{rank}(M)\le 2$.

Therefore no such polynomials $a(x),b(x),c(y),d(y)$ exist. $\blacksquare$
