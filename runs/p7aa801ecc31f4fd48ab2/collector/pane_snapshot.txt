## Proof

We interpret "the KL divergence between the standard Gaussian $f$ and the standa
rd Cauchy $g$" as $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(f\|g)$, the forward KL with $f$ as the refere
nce (base) measure:

$$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(f\|g)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\log\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}\,dx.$$

(We also address the reverse direction $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(g\|f)$ at the end.)

### Absolute continuity

Both $f$ and $g$ are strictly positive on all of $\mathbb{R}$, so $f\ll g$ and t
he log-ratio is defined everywhere. The only possible source of infinitude is th
e growth of the integrand at infinity.

### Decomposing the integrand

With
$$f(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/2},\qquad g(x)=\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})},$$

we compute
$$\log f(x)=-\tfrac12\log(2\pi)-\tfrac{x^{2}}{2},\qquad \log g(x)=-\log\pi-\log(
1+x^{2}).$$

Hence
$$\log\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=\log\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2\pi}}-\frac{x^{2}}{2}+\log(1+x^{2}
)=\log\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}-\frac{x^{2}}{2}+\log(1+x^{2}).$$

Therefore
$$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(f\|g)=\underbrace{\log\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int f\,dx}_{(A)}\;-
\;\underbrace{\frac12\int x^{2}f(x)\,dx}_{(B)}\;+\;\underbrace{\int \log(1+x^{2}
)\,f(x)\,dx}_{(C)}.$$

### Term (A)

$\int f\,dx=1$, so $(A)=\log\sqrt{\pi/2}$, a finite constant.

### Term (B)

$\int x^{2}f(x)\,dx=\mathbb{E}_{f}[X^{2}]=\operatorname{Var}_{f}(X)=1$ since $f$
 is the standard Gaussian. Thus $(B)=\tfrac12$, finite.

### Term (C)

This is $\mathbb{E}_{f}[\log(1+X^{2})]$ with $X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$. We bound i
t.

- **Non-negativity:** $\log(1+x^{2})\ge 0$ for all $x$, so the integrand in (C)
is non-negative; the integral is a well-defined element of $[0,\infty]$.
- **Upper bound:** For $t\ge 0$, $\log(1+t)\le t$. Taking $t=x^{2}$ gives $\log(
1+x^{2})\le x^{2}$. Hence
$$0\le \mathbb{E}_{f}[\log(1+X^{2})]\le \mathbb{E}_{f}[X^{2}]=1<\infty.$$

So (C) is finite.

### Combining

All three terms (A), (B), (C) are finite, and (C) is bounded between $0$ and $1$
. Consequently

$$\boxed{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(f\|g)=\log\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}-\frac12+\mathbb{E}_{f}[\
log(1+X^{2})]<\infty.}$$

In fact $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(f\|g)\in\bigl[\log\sqrt{\pi/2}-\tfrac12,\;\log\sqrt{\pi
/2}+\tfrac12\bigr]\approx[-0.12,\;0.88]$, a concrete finite range.

### Remark on the reverse direction

For completeness, $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(g\|f)=\int g(x)\log\frac{g(x)}{f(x)}\,dx$ con
tains the term $\tfrac12\int x^{2}g(x)\,dx=\tfrac12\mathbb{E}_{g}[X^{2}]$, the h
alf-variance of the standard Cauchy — which is $+\infty$ (the Cauchy has no fini
te second moment; $x^{2}g(x)\sim 1/(\pi x)\not\to 0$ fast enough, and $\int_{1}^
{\infty}x^{2}/(1+x^{2})\,dx$ diverges). The remaining terms are finite, so $D_{\
mathrm{KL}}(g\|f)=+\infty$.

### Conclusion

The Kullback–Leibler divergence $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(f\|g)$ of the standard Gaussian
 $f$ from the standard Cauchy $g$ is **finite**. (The reverse divergence $D_{\ma
thrm{KL}}(g\|f)$ is infinite, owing to the Cauchy's infinite second moment.)
