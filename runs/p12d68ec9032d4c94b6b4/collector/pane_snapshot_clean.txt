over an algebraically closed field contains a non-zero ad-nilpotent element (the

ad-nilpotent).

## Theorem

**Every non-zero Lie algebra over an algebraically closed field of characteristi
c 0 contains a non-zero ad-nilpotent element.**

## Proof

Let $L$ be a non-zero Lie algebra over an algebraically closed field $k$ of char
acteristic 0. We split into three cases.

### Case 1: $Z(L) \neq 0$

Take any $0 \neq z \in Z(L)$. Then $\operatorname{ad} z = 0$, which is nilpotent
. So $z$ is a non-zero ad-nilpotent element. $\checkmark$

### Case 2: $Z(L) = 0$ and the radical $R \neq 0$

Since $R$ is a non-zero solvable ideal of $L$, its derived series
$$R \supset R^{(1)} = [R,R] \supset R^{(2)} = [R^{(1)},R^{(1)}] \supset \cdots$$
terminates. Let $A = R^{(m)}$ be the last non-zero term. Then:

- **$A$ is abelian:** $[A, A] = R^{(m+1)} = 0$.
- **$A$ is an ideal of $L$:** Each derived term $R^{(j)}$ is a characteristic id
eal of $R$ (stable under all derivations of $R$, since for any derivation $D$ of
 $R$, $D([R, R^{(j-1)}]) \subseteq [R, R^{(j-1)}] + [R, R^{(j-1)}] = R^{(j)}$).
Since $R$ is an ideal of $L$, each $\operatorname{ad}_x$ ($x \in L$) restricts t
o a derivation of $R$, so $[x, A] \subseteq A$. Thus $A \trianglelefteq L$.

Now take any $0 \neq a \in A$. Since $A$ is an ideal, $\operatorname{ad} a$ maps
 $L$ into $[A, L] \subseteq A$. Applying $\operatorname{ad} a$ again maps into $
[A, [A, L]] \subseteq [A, A] = 0$. Therefore
$$(\operatorname{ad} a)^2 = 0,$$
so $a$ is a non-zero ad-nilpotent element. $\checkmark$

### Case 3: $Z(L) = 0$ and $R = 0$ (i.e., $L$ is semisimple)

Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0, a semisimple Lie algebra
 $L$ admits a **root space decomposition** with respect to a Cartan subalgebra $
H$:
$$L = H \oplus \bigoplus_{\alpha \in \Phi} L_\alpha,$$
where $\Phi$ is a finite set of non-zero roots (linear forms on $H$) and each ro
ot space $L_\alpha = \{x \in L : [h, x] = \alpha(h)\,x \text{ for all } h \in H\
}$ is one-dimensional.

**$\Phi$ is non-empty.** Since $L$ is semisimple, $L = [L, L]$. If $\Phi = \empt
yset$, then $L = H$, which is abelian (Cartan subalgebras of semisimple Lie alge
bras are abelian). But then $[L, L] = 0$, contradicting $L = [L, L] \neq 0$. So
$\Phi \neq \emptyset$.

Pick any root $\alpha \in \Phi$ and any non-zero $e_\alpha \in L_\alpha$. We cla
im $e_\alpha$ is ad-nilpotent. Indeed, $\operatorname{ad}(e_\alpha)$ acts by:
- $\operatorname{ad}(e_\alpha): H \to L_\alpha$ (since $[e_\alpha, h] = -\alpha(
h)\,e_\alpha$),
- $\operatorname{ad}(e_\alpha): L_\beta \to L_{\beta + \alpha}$ for each root $\
beta$.

Iterating, $(\operatorname{ad} e_\alpha)^n$ maps $L_\beta$ into $L_{\beta + n\al
pha}$ and $H$ into $L_{n\alpha}$. Since $\Phi$ is finite, for all sufficiently l
arge $n$, neither $\beta + n\alpha$ nor $n\alpha$ is a root or zero, so the corr
esponding root spaces vanish. Therefore $(\operatorname{ad} e_\alpha)^N = 0$ for
 $N$ large enough, proving $e_\alpha$ is a non-zero ad-nilpotent element. $\chec
kmark$

### Conclusion

In every case, $L$ contains a non-zero ad-nilpotent element. Therefore **yes**,
every non-zero Lie algebra over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0 has a non-zero ad-nilpotent element. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** Cases 1 and 2 are characteristic-free (they work over any field). On
ly Case 3 uses characteristic 0 (for the root space decomposition of semisimple
Lie algebras). The result also holds in positive characteristic for algebraicall
