**Proof.**

Recall that $d_S(e, w) = \min\{|v| : v \in F(S),\; v =_G w\}$, where $v =_G w$ d

**Algorithm:**

1. For $k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots, n$:
   - (a) Enumerate all words $v \in F(S)$ of length $k$. Since $S$ is finite, th

   - (b) For each such $v$, use the word problem algorithm to decide whether $v
=_G w$, i.e., whether $vw^{-1} =_G e$.
   - (c) If any $v$ of length $k$ satisfies $v =_G w$, output $k$ and halt.
2. Output $n$.

**Termination:** The outer loop runs at most $n + 1$ iterations. At $k = n$, the
 word $w$ itself has length $n$ and trivially satisfies $w =_G w$, so a match is
 found no later than $k = n$. Each inner check invokes the word problem algorith

**Correctness:** The algorithm outputs the smallest $k$ for which there exists a
 word of length $k$ representing the same group element as $w$. This is precisel

**Effectiveness:** Every step is effective: enumerating words of bounded length
over a finite alphabet is computable, and each equality check reduces to the wor

Therefore, $d_S(e, w)$ is computable for every word $w \in F(S)$. $\blacksquare$
