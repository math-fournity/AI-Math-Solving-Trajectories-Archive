## Answer

**Yes.** If $\pi(a) \geq 0$ in every cyclic representation $\pi: A \to B(H)$, th
en $a \geq 0$ in $A$.

## Proof

Let $A$ be a C\*-algebra and $a \in A$ such that $\pi(a) \geq 0$ in $B(H)$ for e
very cyclic representation $\pi$ of $A$.

**Claim 1: $a$ is self-adjoint.**

For any cyclic representation $\pi$, the hypothesis $\pi(a) \geq 0$ implies $\pi
(a)$ is self-adjoint, i.e.
$$\pi(a) = \pi(a)^* = \pi(a^*),$$
so $\pi(a - a^*) = 0$.

Consider the **universal representation**
$$\pi_u = \bigoplus_{\varphi \in S(A)} \pi_\varphi : A \to B(H_u),$$
where $S(A)$ is the state space of $A$ and $(\pi_\varphi, H_\varphi, \xi_\varphi
)$ is the GNS triple of the state $\varphi$. Each $\pi_\varphi$ is cyclic (with
cyclic vector $\xi_\varphi$), so by hypothesis $\pi_\varphi(a - a^*) = 0$ for ev
ery $\varphi \in S(A)$. Hence
$$\pi_u(a - a^*) = \bigoplus_\varphi \pi_\varphi(a - a^*) = 0.$$
The universal representation $\pi_u$ is faithful (indeed isometric) for every C\
*-algebra — this is the standard Gelfand–Naimark theorem. Therefore $a - a^* = 0
$, i.e. $a = a^*$.

**Claim 2: $\varphi(a) \geq 0$ for every state $\varphi \in S(A)$.**

For a state $\varphi$, let $(\pi_\varphi, H_\varphi, \xi_\varphi)$ be its GNS tr
iple. The representation $\pi_\varphi$ is cyclic, so by hypothesis $\pi_\varphi(
a) \geq 0$. The GNS construction gives
$$\varphi(a) = \langle \pi_\varphi(a)\, \xi_\varphi,\, \xi_\varphi \rangle.$$
Since $\pi_\varphi(a) \geq 0$ and $\xi_\varphi$ is a unit vector (in the non-uni
tal case, pass to the unitization; the cyclic vector still satisfies $\|\xi_\var
phi\|^2 = \varphi(1) = 1$), the quadratic form of a positive operator is non-neg
ative:
$$\varphi(a) = \langle \pi_\varphi(a)\, \xi_\varphi,\, \xi_\varphi \rangle \geq
0.$$

**Claim 3: $a \geq 0$ in $A$.**

We use the standard characterization of positivity in C\*-algebras:

> *A self-adjoint element $a \in A$ is positive if and only if $\varphi(a) \geq
0$ for every state $\varphi \in S(A)$.*

*Justification of the characterization.* If $a \geq 0$, then $a = b^*b$ for some
 $b$, and every state is positive, so $\varphi(a) = \varphi(b^*b) \geq 0$. Conve
rsely, suppose $a = a^*$ and $a \not\geq 0$. Then the spectrum $\sigma(a)$ conta
ins a negative value $\lambda < 0$. By the functional calculus in the abelian C\
*-subalgebra $C^*(a) \cong C(\sigma(a))$, the evaluation functional $\mathrm{ev}
_\lambda : C^*(a) \to \mathbb{C}$, $f \mapsto f(\lambda)$, is a character (hence
 a positive linear functional of norm $1$ on $C^*(a)$) with $\mathrm{ev}_\lambda
(a) = \lambda < 0$. By the Hahn–Banach / Krein extension theorem, this extends t
o a state $\varphi$ on $A$ with $\varphi(a) = \lambda < 0$, contradicting $\varp
hi(a) \geq 0$ for all states. Hence $a \geq 0$.

By Claim 1, $a$ is self-adjoint; by Claim 2, $\varphi(a) \geq 0$ for every state
 $\varphi$. Applying the characterization, we conclude
$$a \geq 0 \quad \text{in } A.$$

**Remark on the role of cyclicity.** The hypothesis is stated only for *cyclic*
representations, which is the weakest natural hypothesis one can use: every stat
e produces a cyclic (GNS) representation, and the family of all GNS representati
ons assembles into the faithful universal representation. Thus testing positivit
y on cyclic representations alone is already sufficient to detect positivity in
$A$. In fact, the argument shows the sharper statement: $\pi(a) \geq 0$ for ever
y cyclic $\pi$ $\iff$ $\varphi(a) \geq 0$ for every state $\varphi$ $\iff$ $a \g
eq 0$ in $A$.
