## Analysis

Young's convolution inequality states that for $1 \le p, q, r \le \infty$ satisf
ying $\frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{q} = 1 + \frac{1}{r}$, we have $\|f * g\|_r \le \|f
\|_p \|g\|_q$.

Setting $p = 1$: the relation becomes $1 + \frac{1}{q} = 1 + \frac{1}{r}$, i.e.
$q = r$. So the claimed inequality reduces to
$$\|f * g\|_q \le \|f\|_1 \|g\|_q, \qquad 1 \le q \le \infty.$$

This is **valid**, and is in fact a direct consequence of Minkowski's integral i
nequality.

## Proof

Let $f \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$ and $g \in L^q(\mathbb{R}^n)$ with $1 \le q < \inf
ty$ (the case $q = \infty$ is handled separately below). Write the convolution a
s
$$(f * g)(x) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} f(y)\, g(x - y)\, dy.$$

Then
$$\|f * g\|_q = \left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} f(y)\, g(x-y
)\, dy\right|^q dx\right)^{1/q}.$$

Applying **Minkowski's integral inequality** (the integral form of the triangle
inequality, valid for $q \ge 1$),
$$\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} f(y)\, g(x-y)\, dy\right|^
q dx\right)^{1/q} \le \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} |f(y)|^q |g(
x-y)|^q dx\right)^{1/q} dy.$$

Since $|f(y)|$ is independent of $x$, we factor it out:
$$= \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} |f(y)| \left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} |g(x-y)|^q dx\right)^{1
/q} dy.$$

By translation invariance of Lebesgue measure, $\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} |g(x-y)|^q d
x = \|g\|_q^q$, so the inner factor is $\|g\|_q$. Therefore
$$\|f * g\|_q \le \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} |f(y)| \cdot \|g\|_q\, dy = \|g\|_q \int_{
\mathbb{R}^n} |f(y)|\, dy = \|f\|_1 \|g\|_q.$$

**Case $q = \infty$:** Here $(f*g)(x) = \int f(y) g(x-y) dy$, so
$$|(f*g)(x)| \le \int |f(y)| |g(x-y)| dy \le \int |f(y)| \|g\|_\infty dy = \|f\|
_1 \|g\|_\infty.$$
Taking the supremum over $x$ gives $\|f*g\|_\infty \le \|f\|_1 \|g\|_\infty$.

## Conclusion

Young's convolution inequality **is valid** when $p = 1$. With $p = 1$ the scali
ng relation forces $q = r$, and the inequality $\|f * g\|_q \le \|f\|_1 \|g\|_q$
 follows from Minkowski's integral inequality (for $1 \le q < \infty$) and from
the elementary pointwise bound (for $q = \infty$). This is the endpoint case of
Young's inequality, sometimes called the $L^1$–$L^q$ contraction property of con
volution.
