## Answer

**Yes, $g$ is necessarily real-analytic on $(-1,\infty)$.**

### Proof

The key observation is that $g$ extends from the real interval $(-1,\infty)$ to
a **holomorphic** function on the complex half-plane
$$\Omega := \{z \in \mathbb{C} : \operatorname{Re}(z) > -1\},$$
and a holomorphic function restricted to the real line is real-analytic.

**Definition of the extension.** For $z \in \Omega$, set
$$G(z) := \int_0^1 f(t)\, t^z\, dt = \int_0^1 f(t)\, e^{z\ln t}\, dt.$$
For $t \in (0,1]$ we have $\ln t \le 0$, and $t^z = e^{z\ln t}$ is well-defined.
 Near $t = 0$, $|f(t)\,e^{z\ln t}| = |f(t)|\,t^{\operatorname{Re}(z)}$, which is
 integrable on $(0,1]$ whenever $\operatorname{Re}(z) > -1$ (since $f$ is bounde
d on $[0,1]$, being $C^\infty$ on a compact interval, and $\int_0^1 t^\sigma\,dt
 < \infty$ for $\sigma > -1$). Hence $G(z)$ is well-defined on $\Omega$ and agre
es with $g$ on $(-1,\infty)$.

**Holomorphy of $G$.** We verify the standard hypotheses for holomorphy of a par
ameter-dependent integral:

1. For each fixed $t \in (0,1]$, the map $z \mapsto f(t)\,e^{z\ln t}$ is entire
(hence holomorphic on $\Omega$).

2. **Local domination.** Let $K \subset \Omega$ be compact. Choose $\sigma > -1$
 with $\operatorname{Re}(z) \ge \sigma$ for all $z \in K$. Since $t \in (0,1]$ g
ives $\ln t \le 0$, we have $t^{\operatorname{Re}(z)} \le t^{\sigma}$. With $M :
= \sup_{[0,1]}|f| < \infty$,
$$|f(t)\,e^{z\ln t}| \le M\,t^{\sigma}, \qquad t \in (0,1],\; z \in K.$$
The dominating function $M\,t^{\sigma}$ is integrable on $(0,1]$ because $\sigma
 > -1$.

By the standard theorem (e.g., differentiation under the integral sign applied v
ia Morera's theorem, or the dominated convergence theorem applied to difference
quotients), $G$ is holomorphic on $\Omega$, with
$$G'(z) = \int_0^1 f(t)\,(\ln t)\,t^z\,dt,$$
and similarly for all higher derivatives.

**Conclusion.** Since $G$ is holomorphic on the open set $\Omega \supset (-1,\in
fty)$, its restriction to the real line $(-1,\infty)$—which is exactly $g$—is re
al-analytic: around every $x_0 \in (-1,\infty)$, $g$ admits a convergent power s
eries expansion with positive radius of convergence (given by the Taylor series
of $G$ at $x_0$).

**Remark.** The smoothness of $f$ is far more than needed—only boundedness and m
easurability of $f$ are used. The real-analyticity of $g$ comes entirely from th
e analytic dependence of the kernel $t^x = e^{x\ln t}$ on $x$, not from any anal
yticity of $f$. In particular, the hypothesis that $f$ need not be real-analytic
 is irrelevant to the conclusion.
