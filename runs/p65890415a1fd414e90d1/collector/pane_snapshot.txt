# Proof

## Setting up the limit system

The given system is
$$\dot x = y, \qquad \dot y = x - x^3 - \delta y + \gamma e^{-t}, \qquad \delta,
\gamma > 0.$$

Since $\gamma e^{-t}\to 0$ as $t\to\infty$, the system is **asymptotically auton
omous** with limit (autonomous) system
$$\dot x = y, \qquad \dot y = x - x^3 - \delta y. \tag{$\ast$}$$

This is the damped Duffing oscillator with double-well potential $V(x)=-\tfrac12
 x^2 + \tfrac14 x^4$, i.e. $\ddot x + \delta\dot x - x + x^3 = 0$.

## Equilibria and their stability in ($\ast$)

Equilibria satisfy $y=0$ and $x-x^3 = x(1-x^2)=0$, giving
$$p_0=(0,0), \qquad p_+ = (1,0), \qquad p_- = (-1,0).$$

The Jacobian of ($\ast$) at $(x_0,0)$ is
$$J(x_0) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1-3x_0^2 & -\delta \end{pmatrix}.$$

- **At $p_0=(0,0)$:** $\det J = -1 < 0$, so $p_0$ is a **saddle** (one positive,
 one negative eigenvalue).
- **At $p_\pm=(\pm 1,0)$:** $\det J = 2 > 0$, $\operatorname{tr} J = -\delta < 0
$. Both eigenvalues have negative real part, so $p_\pm$ are **asymptotically sta
ble** (sinks — nodes or foci depending on $\delta^2 \gtrless 8$).

The energy $E(x,y)=\tfrac12 y^2 - \tfrac12 x^2 + \tfrac14 x^4$ satisfies $\dot E
 = -\delta y^2 \le 0$ along ($\ast$), so every trajectory of ($\ast$) is bounded
 and converges to the set of equilibria; in particular there are no periodic orb
its, and the only invariant sets are the three equilibria together with the stab
le/unstable manifolds of the saddle $p_0$.

## Boundedness of trajectories of the nonautonomous system

For the full system, define the same energy $E$. Then
$$\dot E = -\delta y^2 + \gamma e^{-t} y \le -\delta y^2 + \gamma e^{-t}|y| \le
\frac{\gamma^2 e^{-2t}}{4\delta}.$$

Integrating,
$$E(t) \le E(0) + \int_0^\infty \frac{\gamma^2 e^{-2s}}{4\delta}\,ds = E(0) + \f
rac{\gamma^2}{8\delta}.$$

Since $E(x,y)\to +\infty$ as $|(x,y)|\to\infty$, every trajectory is **bounded**
 and exists for all $t\ge 0$.

## Applying the asymptotically autonomous theorem

By the classical theorem on asymptotically autonomous systems (Thieme / Markov–B
hatia–Szegő; see also Mischaikoff–Hirsch–Theim), if a solution of $\dot z = f(z,
t)$ with $f(\cdot,t)\to f_\infty(\cdot)$ uniformly on compact sets is bounded, t
hen its $\omega$-limit set $\omega(z_0)$ is:

1. **nonempty, compact, connected;**
2. **invariant under the limit flow** $\dot z = f_\infty(z)$;
3. **a chain-transitive / internally chain-transitive** subset of the limit flow
;
4. **contained in the union of $\omega$-limit sets of the limit system.**

For the limit system ($\ast$), the only $\omega$-limit sets of bounded orbits ar
e the equilibria $\{p_+\}$, $\{p_-\}$, and $\{p_0\}$ (the saddle, which is the $
\omega$-limit of itself along its stable manifold), together with the heteroclin
ic connections from $p_0$ to $p_\pm$ lying on the stable manifolds of $p_\pm$ (t
hese are the unstable manifolds of $p_0$). There are no periodic orbits (energy
is strictly decreasing off the $x$-axis) and no chaotic invariant sets (planar g
radient-like structure).

By internal chain transitivity, the $\omega$-limit set of the nonautonomous syst
em must be a connected invariant set of ($\ast$) made up of equilibria and conne
cting orbits. Hence the possibilities are:

- the stable equilibrium $\{p_+\} = \{(1,0)\}$,
- the stable equilibrium $\{p_-\} = \{(-1,0)\}$,
- the saddle $\{p_0\} = \{(0,0)\}$ (only for the special initial condition lying
 on the stable manifold of $p_0$),
- a heteroclinic orbit connecting $p_0$ to $p_+$ or to $p_-$ (i.e. an unstable-m
anifold branch of the saddle, which is itself a complete orbit of ($\ast$)).

Because the forcing $\gamma e^{-t}$ is exponentially small and the stable equili
bria $p_\pm$ are hyperbolic sinks, generic trajectories converge to one of $p_\p
m$. Convergence to $p_0$ or to a heteroclinic connection is non-generic (codimen
sion-one, requiring the trajectory to lie asymptotically on the stable manifold
of the saddle).

## Conclusion

The possible $\omega$-limit sets of trajectories of the given asymptotically aut
onomous system are precisely the compact connected invariant sets of the limit a
utonomous system ($\ast$), namely:

$$\boxed{\;\{(1,0)\},\quad \{(-1,0)\},\quad \{(0,0)\},\quad \text{or a heterocli
nic orbit of }(\ast)\text{ connecting }(0,0)\text{ to }(\pm 1,0).\;}$$

Equivalently: every bounded trajectory converges to one of the two stable equili
bria $(\pm 1,0)$, except for the exceptional trajectory that converges to the sa
ddle $(0,0)$, or to a heteroclinic connection from the saddle to a sink. There a
re no periodic or more complicated $\omega$-limit sets, since the limit system i
s gradient-like with strictly decreasing energy.
