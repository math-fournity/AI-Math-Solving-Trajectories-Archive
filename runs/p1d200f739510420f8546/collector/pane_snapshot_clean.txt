## Proof

We show that such a measure exists by constructing an explicit invariant probabi
lity measure distinct from Lebesgue measure.

**The map.** Let $T:[0,1)\to[0,1)$ be $T(x)=2x \bmod 1$. A probability measure $
\mu$ is *invariant* under $T$ if
$$\mu(T^{-1}(A))=\mu(A)\quad\text{for every Borel set }A\subseteq[0,1).$$

**Construction: the Dirac mass at the fixed point $0$.**

Observe that $T(0)=2\cdot 0 \bmod 1 = 0$, so $0$ is a fixed point of $T$. Define
 $\mu=\delta_0$, the Dirac probability measure concentrated at $0$:
$$\mu(A)=\delta_0(A)=\begin{cases}1 & 0\in A,\\ 0 & 0\notin A.\end{cases}$$

**Invariance.** For any Borel set $A$,
$$0\in T^{-1}(A) \iff T(0)\in A \iff 0\in A.$$
Therefore
$$\mu(T^{-1}(A))=\delta_0(T^{-1}(A))=\mathbf{1}_{0\in T^{-1}(A)}=\mathbf{1}_{0\i
n A}=\delta_0(A)=\mu(A).$$
Hence $\delta_0$ is $T$-invariant.

**Distinctness from Lebesgue measure.** Take $A=[0,\tfrac12)$. Then
$$\lambda(A)=\tfrac12,\qquad \mu(A)=\delta_0([0,\tfrac12))=1,$$
since $0\in[0,\tfrac12)$. Thus $\mu\neq\lambda$.

**Conclusion.** $\mu=\delta_0$ is a probability measure on $[0,1)$, it is invari
ant under the doubling map $T(x)=2x\bmod 1$, and it is not the Lebesgue measure.
 Hence yes, there exists an invariant non-Lebesgue probability measure for $T$.

(Remark: more generally, every periodic orbit $\{x,Tx,\dots,T^{n-1}x\}$ of $T$ s
upports the invariant measure $\frac1n\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta_{T^k x}$; e.g. the
orbit $\{1/3,2/3\}$ gives $\frac12(\delta_{1/3}+\delta_{2/3})$, another non-Lebe
sgue invariant measure. The Dirac mass at $0$ above is the simplest instance.)
