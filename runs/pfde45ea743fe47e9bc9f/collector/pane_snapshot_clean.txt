## Proof

**Definition.** A function $f$ is *locally bounded* at $x$ if there exist $\delt
a > 0$ and $M > 0$ such that $|f(y)| \le M$ for all $y \in (x-\delta, x+\delta)$
. Let $B(f) = \{x \in \mathbb{R} : f \text{ is not locally bounded at } x\}$.

### Step 1: $B(f)$ is always closed.

If $x \notin B(f)$, then $f$ is locally bounded at $x$: there exist $\delta, M >
 0$ with $|f(y)| \le M$ on $(x-\delta, x+\delta)$. For any $z \in (x-\delta, x+\
delta)$, this same interval is a neighborhood of $z$ on which $f$ is bounded by
$M$, so $z \notin B(f)$. Thus $(x-\delta,x+\delta) \subseteq \mathbb{R}\setminus
 B(f)$, proving $\mathbb{R}\setminus B(f)$ is open, hence $B(f)$ is closed.

### Step 2: Every closed set $E$ arises as $B(f)$.

**Case $E = \emptyset$:** Take $f \equiv 0$. Then $B(f) = \emptyset = E$.

**Case $E \neq \emptyset$:** Since $E$ is a closed subset of the separable metri
c space $\mathbb{R}$, it possesses a countable dense subset $\{x_n\}_{n=1}^\inft
y$. Define $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ by:

$$f(x) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{1}{d(x,\,E)} & \text{if } x \notin E, \\[6pt] n &
\text{if } x = x_n \text{ for some } n, \\[4pt] 0 & \text{if } x \in E \setminus
 \{x_n : n \in \mathbb{N}\}, \end{cases}$$

where $d(x, E) = \inf\{|x - y| : y \in E\}$.

**Claim: $B(f) = E$.**

**(a) $f$ is finite everywhere.** For $x \notin E$: since $E$ is closed, $d(x, E
) > 0$, so $1/d(x,E)$ is finite. For $x \in E$: $f(x)$ equals either $n$ (a posi
tive integer) or $0$, both finite.

**(b) $f$ is locally bounded at every $x \notin E$ (so $\mathbb{R}\setminus E \s
ubseteq \mathbb{R}\setminus B(f)$).** Let $\delta = d(x,E)/2 > 0$. For any $y \i
n (x-\delta, x+\delta)$, the triangle inequality gives $d(y, E) \ge d(x, E) - |x
 - y| > d(x,E)/2$, so $f(y) = 1/d(y,E) < 2/d(x,E)$. Thus $f$ is bounded on $(x-\
delta, x+\delta)$.

**(c) $f$ is locally unbounded at every $x \in E$ (so $E \subseteq B(f)$).** We
consider two sub-cases based on the position of $x$ relative to $E$.

*Case (i): $x \in \partial E$ (boundary of $E$).* Every neighborhood $U$ of $x$
intersects $\mathbb{R}\setminus E$ (by definition of boundary). Take any sequenc
e $y_k \in U \setminus E$ with $y_k \to x$. Then $d(y_k, E) \to 0$, so $f(y_k) =
 1/d(y_k, E) \to +\infty$. Hence $f$ is unbounded on $U$.

*Case (ii): $x \in E^\circ$ (interior of $E$).* Let $U$ be any neighborhood of $
x$. Since $x \in E^\circ$, the set $U \cap E^\circ$ is a nonempty open set (in t
he subspace topology of $E$). Since $\{x_n\}$ is dense in $E$, the set $\{x_n\}
\cap U$ is infinite. Moreover, for any $N \in \mathbb{N}$, only finitely many of
 the $x_n$ have index $n \le N$, so there exist $x_n \in U$ with $n$ arbitrarily
 large. At such points, $f(x_n) = n \to \infty$. Hence $f$ is unbounded on $U$.

Since every $x \in E$ is either in $\partial E$ or $E^\circ$ (because $E = \part
ial E \cup E^\circ$), this covers all points of $E$.

**(d) Conclusion.** From (b) and (c): $\mathbb{R}\setminus E \subseteq \mathbb{R
