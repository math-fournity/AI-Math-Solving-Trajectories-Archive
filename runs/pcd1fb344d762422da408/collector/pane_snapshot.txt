# Noether's Theorem for Fields — Derivation of the Continuity Equation

## Setup

Consider a field theory with Lagrangian density

$$\mathcal{L}(x^\mu, A^\mu, \partial_\nu A^\mu),$$

where $A^\mu(x^\nu)$ are the fields ($\mu = 0,1,\dots$ indexing the field compon
ents) and $x^\rho$ are the spacetime coordinates. The action is

$$S[A] = \int_\Omega \mathcal{L}\big(x^\rho, A^\mu(x), \partial_\nu A^\mu(x)\big
)\, d^4x.$$

## 1. Infinitesimal transformation

Consider a continuous one-parameter family of transformations combining a **coor
dinate transformation** and a **field transformation**:

$$x^\rho \;\longrightarrow\; x'^\rho = x^\rho + \delta x^\rho = x^\rho + \epsilo
n\,\xi^\rho(x),$$

$$A^\mu(x) \;\longrightarrow\; A'^\mu(x') = A^\mu(x) + \delta A^\mu(x) = A^\mu(x
) + \epsilon\,\Psi^\mu(x),$$

where $\epsilon$ is an infinitesimal parameter, $\xi^\rho$ is the generator of t
he coordinate transformation, and $\Psi^\mu$ is the total field variation at the
 **same spacetime point** (we distinguish below the total and intrinsic variatio
ns). Define the **intrinsic (form) variation** of the field:

$$\bar\delta A^\mu(x) \;=\; A'^\mu(x) - A^\mu(x) \;=\; \delta A^\mu(x) - (\parti
al_\rho A^\mu)\,\delta x^\rho \;=\; \epsilon\big(\Psi^\mu - (\partial_\rho A^\mu
)\xi^\rho\big).$$

We write $\bar\delta A^\mu = \epsilon\,\phi^\mu$ with $\phi^\mu = \Psi^\mu - (\p
artial_\rho A^\mu)\xi^\rho$.

## 2. Variation of the action

The variation of the action under this transformation is

$$\delta S = \int_{\Omega'} \mathcal{L}\big(x', A'(x'), \partial A'(x')\big)\,d^
4x' - \int_\Omega \mathcal{L}\big(x, A(x), \partial A(x)\big)\,d^4x.$$

Using $d^4x' = (1 + \epsilon\,\partial_\rho\xi^\rho)\,d^4x$ and expanding to fir
st order in $\epsilon$:

$$\delta S = \int_\Omega \left[\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial A^\mu}\,\bar\d
elta A^\mu + \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\rho A^\mu)}\,\bar\delt
a(\partial_\rho A^\mu) + \partial_\rho\!\big(\mathcal L\,\delta x^\rho\big)\righ
t] d^4x.$$

Since $\bar\delta$ commutes with $\partial_\rho$ (both are at fixed $x$), $\bar\
delta(\partial_\rho A^\mu) = \partial_\rho(\bar\delta A^\mu)$. Substituting and
integrating the second term by parts:

$$\delta S = \int_\Omega \left[\underbrace{\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\part
ial A^\mu} - \partial_\rho\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\rho A^\mu
)}\right)}_{\displaystyle E_\mu\;(\text{Euler–Lagrange})}\bar\delta A^\mu + \par
tial_\rho\!\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\rho A^\mu)}\bar\de
lta A^\mu + \mathcal L\,\delta x^\rho\right)\right] d^4x.$$

## 3. On-shell simplification

When the fields satisfy the **Euler–Lagrange equations**

$$E_\mu \;=\; \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial A^\mu} - \partial_\rho\frac{\pa
rtial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\rho A^\mu)} \;=\; 0,$$

the first term vanishes, leaving

$$\delta S\big|_{\text{on-shell}} = \int_\Omega \partial_\rho\!\left[\frac{\part
ial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\rho A^\mu)}\,\bar\delta A^\mu + \mathcal L\,\d
elta x^\rho\right] d^4x.$$

## 4. Symmetry assumption

The transformation is a **symmetry** if the action is invariant (up to a boundar
y term). We allow the Lagrangian density to change by a total divergence:

$$\delta\mathcal L + \partial_\rho(\mathcal L\,\delta x^\rho) = \epsilon\,\parti
al_\rho\,\Lambda^\rho,$$

i.e., $\mathcal L\,d^4x \to \mathcal L\,d^4x + \epsilon\,\partial_\rho\Lambda^\r
ho\,d^4x$. Equivalently, on-shell:

$$\partial_\rho\!\left[\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\rho A^\mu)}\
,\bar\delta A^\mu + \mathcal L\,\delta x^\rho - \Lambda^\rho\right] = 0.$$

## 5. Identification of the conserved current

Restoring $\bar\delta A^\mu = \epsilon(\Psi^\mu - (\partial_\sigma A^\mu)\xi^\si
gma)$ and $\delta x^\rho = \epsilon\,\xi^\rho$, and dividing by $\epsilon$, we i
dentify the **Noether current**:

$$\boxed{\;j^\rho \;=\; \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\rho A^\mu)}
\,\big(\Psi^\mu - (\partial_\sigma A^\mu)\,\xi^\sigma\big) \;+\; \mathcal L\,\xi
^\rho \;-\; \Lambda^\rho\;}$$

or, equivalently, in terms of the canonical momentum $\pi_\mu^{\;\rho} := \dfrac
{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\rho A^\mu)}$:

$$j^\rho \;=\; \pi_\mu^{\;\rho}\,\Psi^\mu \;-\; \big(\pi_\mu^{\;\rho}\,\partial_
\sigma A^\mu - \mathcal L\,\delta^\rho_{\;\sigma}\big)\,\xi^\sigma \;-\; \Lambda
^\rho.$$

The term in parentheses is the **canonical energy–momentum tensor**:

$$T^{\rho}_{\;\;\sigma} \;=\; \pi_\mu^{\;\rho}\,\partial_\sigma A^\mu \;-\; \mat
hcal L\,\delta^\rho_{\;\sigma},$$

so the current decomposes as

$$j^\rho \;=\; \pi_\mu^{\;\rho}\,\Psi^\mu \;-\; T^{\rho}_{\;\;\sigma}\,\xi^\sigm
a \;-\; \Lambda^\rho.$$

## 6. The continuity equation

By the symmetry condition derived on-shell, this current is divergence-free:

$$\boxed{\;\partial_\rho\, j^\rho \;=\; 0\;}$$

This is the **equation of continuity**. It expresses the local conservation law
associated with the continuous symmetry. The conserved charge is obtained by int
egrating the time component over a spatial hypersurface:

$$Q \;=\; \int_{\Sigma}\, j^0\, d^3x, \qquad \frac{dQ}{dt} = \int_{\Sigma}\, \pa
rtial_0 j^0\, d^3x = -\int_{\Sigma}\, \partial_i j^i\, d^3x = -\oint_{\partial\S
igma} j^i\, dS_i = 0,$$

where the last equality uses the divergence theorem and the assumption that fiel
ds vanish sufficiently fast at spatial infinity.

## 7. Summary of the generalization from mechanics to fields

| Mechanics (finite DOF) | Field theory (infinite DOF) |
|---|---|
| $L(q_i, \dot q_i, t)$ | $\mathcal{L}(A^\mu, \partial_\rho A^\mu, x^\rho)$ |
| $q_i(t)$ | $A^\mu(x^\rho)$ |
| $\dot q_i$ | $\partial_\rho A^\mu$ |
| $p_i = \partial L/\partial\dot q_i$ | $\pi_\mu^{\;\rho} = \partial\mathcal L/\
partial(\partial_\rho A^\mu)$ |
| $Q = \sum_i p_i\,\delta q_i - H\,\delta t$ | $j^\rho = \pi_\mu^{\;\rho}\,\bar\
delta A^\mu + \mathcal L\,\delta x^\rho - \Lambda^\rho$ |
| $\dot Q = 0$ | $\partial_\rho j^\rho = 0$ |

The key structural change is that the single conserved quantity $Q$ becomes a **
conserved current** $j^\rho$ — a local, spacetime-distributed object whose conse
rvation is expressed by the vanishing four-divergence $\partial_\rho j^\rho = 0$
, rather than by a single time derivative $\dot Q = 0$. This locality is the ess
ential content of Noether's theorem generalized to field theory: **every continu
ous symmetry of the action yields a locally conserved current, whose associated
charge is conserved globally.**
