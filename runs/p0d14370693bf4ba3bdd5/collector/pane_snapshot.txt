## Characterizing Unstable Pairs

**Lemma.** For distinct positive integers $a < b$, $(a+b) \mid ab$ if and only i
f $a = kx(x+y)$ and $b = ky(x+y)$ for some coprime positive integers $x < y$ and
 positive integer $k$.

*Proof.* Let $d = \gcd(a,b)$, $a = dx$, $b = dy$ with $\gcd(x,y)=1$. Then $a+b =
 d(x+y)$ and $ab = d^2xy$, so $(a+b)\mid ab \iff d(x+y) \mid d^2xy \iff (x+y) \m
id dxy$. Since $\gcd(x, x+y) = \gcd(x,y) = 1$ and $\gcd(y, x+y) = 1$, we get $\g
cd(xy, x+y) = 1$, so $(x+y) \mid d$, i.e., $d = k(x+y)$. Then $a = kx(x+y)$, $b
= ky(x+y)$. $\square$

## $G_{40}$ Is Not Bipartite

When $n = 40$, two new edges appear: $(10, 40)$ and $(24, 40)$ (from $(x,y,k) =
(1,4,2)$ and $(3,5,1)$ respectively).

**Verification:**
- $(10,40)$: $10+40=50$, $10\cdot40=400$, $400/50=8$ ✓
- $(24,40)$: $24+40=64$, $24\cdot40=960$, $960/64=15$ ✓

In the unique (up to swap) 2-coloring of the component, $10$ is Red and $24$ is
Blue (they are connected by the path $10\text{-}15\text{-}30\text{-}6\text{-}12\
text{-}24$ of length 5, which is odd, forcing opposite colors).

Vertex $40$ is adjacent to both $10$ (Red) and $24$ (Blue), so it cannot be assi
gned either color without creating a monochromatic edge. Equivalently, we have t

$$10 \to 15 \to 30 \to 6 \to 12 \to 24 \to 40 \to 10$$
