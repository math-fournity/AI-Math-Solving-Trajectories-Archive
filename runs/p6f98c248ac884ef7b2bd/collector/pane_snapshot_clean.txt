## Vitali–Carathéodory Theorem

**Answer-leak check.** The problem statement contains only the question; no solu
tion, answer, or proof sketch is embedded. Proceeding.

---

### Statement

Let \(f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}\) be Lebesgue-measurable and integrable. For every \(
\varepsilon>0\) there exist functions \(u,v:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}\) such that

1. \(u\le f\le v\) on \([a,b]\),
2. \(u\) is **upper semicontinuous** (u.s.c.) and \(v\) is **lower semicontinuou
s** (l.s.c.),
3. \(\displaystyle\int_a^b (v-u)\,dx<\varepsilon\).

Equivalently: \(f\) can be approximated **from above by a lower semicontinuous f
unction** and **from below by an upper semicontinuous function**, with arbitrari
ly small \(L^{1}\)-error. In particular one obtains sequences \((u_n)\) u.s.c. a
nd \((v_n)\) l.s.c. with
\[
u_n\le f\le v_n,\qquad \int_a^b(v_n-u_n)\,dx\to 0,
\]
hence \(u_n\to f\) and \(v_n\to f\) in \(L^{1}\), and (after passing to a subseq
uence) almost everywhere.

If \(f\) is bounded, \(u,v\) may be chosen bounded as well.

---

### Proof

We use two standard facts about Lebesgue measure on \([a,b]\):

- **(Regularity)** For every measurable set \(E\subseteq[a,b]\) and every \(\del
ta>0\) there exist an open set \(O\supseteq E\) and a compact set \(K\subseteq E
\) with \(m(O\setminus E)<\delta\) and \(m(E\setminus K)<\delta\).
- **(Simple approximation)** Every integrable \(f\) is the \(L^{1}\)-limit of si
mple functions; in particular, for every \(\eta>0\) there are simple functions \
(\varphi\le f\le\psi\) with \(\int(\psi-\varphi)\,dx<\eta\).

Recall the semicontinuity of characteristic functions:
- If \(O\) is open, \(\chi_O\) is **l.s.c.** (since \(\{\chi_O>\alpha\}\) is ope
n for every \(\alpha\)).
- If \(K\) is closed, \(\chi_K\) is **u.s.c.** (since \(\{\chi_K<\alpha\}\) is o
pen for every \(\alpha\)).

A non-negative linear combination of l.s.c. functions is l.s.c., and of u.s.c. f
unctions is u.s.c.

---

**Step 1 — Reduction to non-negative \(f\).** Write \(f=f^{+}-f^{-}\) with \(f^{
+},f^{-}\ge 0\) integrable. If the theorem holds for non-negative integrable fun
ctions with tolerance \(\varepsilon/2\), we obtain \(v^{\pm}\ge f^{\pm}\) l.s.c.
 and \(u^{\pm}\le f^{\pm}\) u.s.c. with \(\int(v^{\pm}-f^{\pm})<\varepsilon/2\)
and \(\int(f^{\pm}-u^{\pm})<\varepsilon/2\). Setting
\[
v=v^{+}-u^{-},\qquad u=u^{+}-v^{-},
\]
we have \(u\le f\le v\), \(v\) is l.s.c. (l.s.c. minus u.s.c. is l.s.c.), \(u\)
is u.s.c., and \(\int(v-u)=\int(v^{+}-u^{-})-\int(u^{+}-v^{-})\le \int(v^{+}-f^{
+})+\int(f^{-}-u^{-})+\int(f^{+}-u^{+})+\int(v^{-}-f^{-})<\varepsilon\). So it s
uffices to treat \(f\ge 0\).

---

**Step 2 — Approximation from above by an l.s.c. function (non-negative case).**
 Let \(f\ge 0\) be integrable and let \(\varepsilon>0\). Choose a simple functio
n \(\psi=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_i\,\chi_{E_i}\ge f\) (with \(c_i>0\), \(E_i\) measurabl
e, pairwise disjoint) such that
\[
\int_a^b(\psi-f)\,dx<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]
By regularity, for each \(i\) pick an open set \(O_i\supseteq E_i\) with
\[
m(O_i\setminus E_i)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2\,N\,c_i}.
\]
Define
\[
v=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_i\,\chi_{O_i}.
\]
Each \(\chi_{O_i}\) is l.s.c. and \(c_i\ge 0\), so \(v\) is l.s.c. Since \(O_i\s
upseteq E_i\), we have \(v\ge\psi\ge f\). Moreover
\[
\int_a^b(v-\psi)\,dx=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_i\,m(O_i\setminus E_i)<\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{
\varepsilon}{2N}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\int_a^b(v-f)\,dx=\int(v-\psi)+\int(\psi-f)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsi
lon}{2}=\varepsilon.
\]

---

**Step 3 — Approximation from below by a u.s.c. function (non-negative case).**
Choose a simple function \(\varphi=\sum_{i=1}^{N}d_i\,\chi_{F_i}\le f\) (with \(
d_i>0\), \(F_i\) measurable, pairwise disjoint) such that
\[
\int_a^b(f-\varphi)\,dx<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]
By regularity, for each \(i\) pick a compact set \(K_i\subseteq F_i\) with
\[
m(F_i\setminus K_i)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2\,N\,d_i}.
\]
Define
\[
u=\sum_{i=1}^{N}d_i\,\chi_{K_i}.
\]
Each \(\chi_{K_i}\) is u.s.c. (compact \(\Rightarrow\) closed \(\Rightarrow\) \(
\chi_{K_i}\) u.s.c.) and \(d_i\ge 0\), so \(u\) is u.s.c. Since \(K_i\subseteq F
_i\), we have \(u\le\varphi\le f\). Moreover
\[
\int_a^b(\varphi-u)\,dx=\sum_{i=1}^{N}d_i\,m(F_i\setminus K_i)<\frac{\varepsilon
}{2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\int_a^b(f-u)\,dx=\int(f-\varphi)+\int(\varphi-u)<\varepsilon.
\]

---

**Step 4 — Combining.** From Steps 2 and 3 (applied with tolerance \(\varepsilon
/2\) each, or directly with \(\varepsilon\) and summing) we obtain \(u\le f\le v
\) with \(u\) u.s.c., \(v\) l.s.c., and
\[
\int_a^b(v-u)\,dx=\int(v-f)+\int(f-u)<\varepsilon.
\]

---

**Step 5 — Producing convergent sequences.** Apply the construction with \(\vare
psilon=1/n\) to obtain u.s.c. \(u_n\le f\) and l.s.c. \(v_n\ge f\) with \(\int(v
_n-u_n)<1/n\). Then
\[
0\le\int|v_n-f|\,dx\le\int(v_n-u_n)\,dx\to 0,\qquad 0\le\int|f-u_n|\,dx\le\int(v
_n-u_n)\,dx\to 0,
\]
so \(u_n\to f\) and \(v_n\to f\) in \(L^{1}\). Passing to a subsequence (standar
d consequence of \(L^{1}\)-convergence) gives \(u_{n_k}\to f\) and \(v_{n_k}\to
f\) almost everywhere.

---

**Step 6 — Bounded case.** If \(|f|\le M\), truncate the constructions: replace
each \(c_i\) by \(\min(c_i,M)\) and each \(d_i\) by \(\min(d_i,M)\), and interse
ct the open sets \(O_i\) with \(\{|x|<M\}\) type level sets. Concretely, one may
 first replace \(f\) by \(\tilde f=\max(-M,\min(f,M))=f\) (no change) and observ
e that the simple-function approximants can be chosen with values in \([-M,M]\);
 the open-set/compact-set thickening does not increase the range beyond \([-M,M]
\) if one clips. Hence \(u,v\) can be chosen with \(|u|,|v|\le M\). ∎

---

### Summary of the approximating sequences' properties

| Property | Lower approximant \(u\) | Upper approximant \(v\) |
|---|---|---|
| Semicontinuity | upper semicontinuous | lower semicontinuous |
| Position relative to \(f\) | \(u\le f\) | \(v\ge f\) |
| Integral error | \(\int(f-u)\,dx<\varepsilon\) | \(\int(v-f)\,dx<\varepsilon\)
 |
| Sandwich | \(u\le f\le v\), \(\int(v-u)<\varepsilon\) | |
| Convergence | \(u_n\to f\) in \(L^{1}\) (and a.e. subsequence) | \(v_n\to f\)
in \(L^{1}\) (and a.e. subsequence) |
| Boundedness | if \(f\) bounded, \(u\) bounded | if \(f\) bounded, \(v\) bounde
d |

The heart of the theorem is that **open sets give l.s.c. majorants** and **close
d/compact sets give u.s.c. minorants**, combined with the **regularity of Lebesg
ue measure** (approximation of measurable sets from outside by open sets and fro
m inside by compact sets). This is what bridges measurability — an \(L^{1}\)/set
-theoret notion — to semicontinuity, a topological notion.
