## Solution

**Step 1: Simplify the left-hand side.**

The double sum is symmetric in $i$ and $j$. Expanding:
$$\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n} x_i x_j(x_i^3+x_j^3) = \sum_{i,j} x_i^4 x_j + \su
m_{i,j} x_i x_j^4.$$

For the first term, the sums separate:
$$\sum_{i,j} x_i^4 x_j = \left(\sum_i x_i^4\right)\!\left(\sum_j x_j\right).$$

For the second term, swapping the roles of $i$ and $j$ gives
$$\sum_{i,j} x_i x_j^4 = \left(\sum_j x_j^4\right)\!\left(\sum_i x_i\right) = \l
eft(\sum_i x_i^4\right)\!\left(\sum_i x_i\right).$$

Hence
$$\text{LHS} = 2\left(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^4\right)\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i\right).$
$

**Step 2: Reduce the inequality.**

If $\sum x_i = 0$ then all $x_i=0$ and the inequality is trivial. Otherwise, div
iding both sides by $\sum x_i > 0$, the inequality becomes
$$2\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^4 \le C\left(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i\right)^4.$$

By homogeneity (both sides are degree 4), set $S = \sum x_i = 1$ with $x_i \ge 0
$. We need the smallest $C$ such that
$$2\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^4 \le C \quad\text{whenever } x_i\ge 0,\ \sum x_i = 1.$$

**Step 3: Maximize $\sum x_i^4$ on the simplex.**

The function $f(x) = \sum x_i^4$ is strictly convex. Its maximum over the compac
t convex set $\{x\ge 0,\ \sum x_i = 1\}$ is attained at an extreme point, i.e.,
at a vertex where one coordinate equals $1$ and all others equal $0$. At such a
vertex, $f = 1$.

(Equivalently, since $(\sum x_i)^4 = \sum x_i^4 + \text{(non-negative cross term
s)} \ge \sum x_i^4$, we get $\sum x_i^4 \le (\sum x_i)^4 = 1$ directly.)

Therefore $\max \sum x_i^4 = 1$, and the smallest admissible $C$ is
$$C = 2\cdot 1 = 2.$$

**Step 4: Verify equality.**

Take $x_1 = 1$, $x_2 = \cdots = x_n = 0$. Then
$$\text{LHS} = \sum_{i,j}x_i x_j(x_i^3+x_j^3) = x_1\cdot x_1\cdot(x_1^3+x_1^3) =
 2,$$
$$\text{RHS} = C\cdot 1^5 = C.$$
Equality forces $C \ge 2$, confirming sharpness.

**Conclusion.** The smallest constant is $\boxed{C = 2}$, independent of $n\ge 2
$.
