## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $\omega = 0$.

**Proof by contradiction.** Suppose $\omega \neq 0$. Then there exists a point $
p \in A$ with $\omega_p \neq 0$. Writing $\omega = \sum_I a_I\, dx^I$ (sum over
increasing $k$-tuples $I$), the condition $\omega_p \neq 0$ means some coefficie
nt satisfies $a_I(p) \neq 0$ for some increasing multi-index $I = (i_1, \dots, i
_k)$.

Since $a_I$ is continuous (the form is smooth) and $a_I(p) \neq 0$, there is an
open neighborhood $U \subseteq A$ of $p$ on which $a_I$ keeps a constant sign —
say $a_I > 0$ on $U$.

**Constructing a test $k$-chain.** Choose $\delta > 0$ small enough that the fol
lowing $k$-cube lies in $U$. Let $c: [0,1]^k \to A$ be the affine $k$-cube that
varies only the coordinates $i_1, \dots, i_k$ in a small box of side $\delta$ ar
ound $p$, keeping every other coordinate fixed at $p$'s value:
$$c(t_1, \dots, t_k) = p + \delta\sum_{\ell=1}^{k} t_\ell\, e_{i_\ell},$$
where $e_{i_\ell}$ is the standard basis vector. For $\delta$ sufficiently small
, the image of $c$ lies in $U \subseteq A$.

**Computing the pullback.** Under $c$, the coordinate functions satisfy:
- $x^{i_\ell} \circ c = p^{i_\ell} + \delta\, t_\ell$, so $c^*(dx^{i_\ell}) = \d
elta\, dt_\ell$;
- $x^j \circ c = p^j$ (constant) for $j \notin \{i_1,\dots,i_k\}$, so $c^*(dx^j)
 = 0$.

Therefore, for any increasing $k$-tuple $J$:
$$c^*(dx^J) = \bigwedge_{j \in J} c^*(dx^j).$$
This is nonzero only if every index of $J$ lies in $\{i_1,\dots,i_k\}$. Since $|
J| = k = |\{i_1,\dots,i_k\}|$, this forces $J = I$ as sets, and since both are i
ncreasing, $J = I$. For $J \neq I$, the pullback $c^*(dx^J) = 0$.

For $J = I$:
$$c^*(dx^I) = \bigwedge_{\ell=1}^{k} \delta\, dt_\ell = \delta^k\, dt_1 \wedge \
cdots \wedge dt_k.$$

**Evaluating the integral.** Thus
$$\int_c \omega = \int_{[0,1]^k} c^*\omega = \int_{[0,1]^k} a_I(c(t))\, \delta^k
\, dt_1 \cdots dt_k = \delta^k \int_{[0,1]^k} a_I(c(t))\, dt.$$

Since $a_I > 0$ on $U$ and the image of $c$ lies in $U$, the integrand $a_I(c(t)
)$ is strictly positive everywhere on $[0,1]^k$. Hence
$$\int_c \omega = \delta^k \int_{[0,1]^k} a_I(c(t))\, dt > 0.$$

This contradicts the hypothesis that $\int_c \omega = 0$ for every $k$-chain $c$
 in $A$.

**Conclusion.** The contradiction forces $\omega_p = 0$ at every $p \in A$, i.e.
, $\omega = 0$. $\blacksquare$
