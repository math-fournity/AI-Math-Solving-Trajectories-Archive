**Claim:** Yes. The group $\mathrm{Aut}(X)$ of Lie automorphisms of a Lie group
$X$ is always a subgroup of the diffeomorphism group $\mathrm{Diff}(X)$ of the u
nderlying smooth manifold of $X$.

**Definitions.**

- $\mathrm{Diff}(X)$ denotes the group of all diffeomorphisms of the smooth mani
fold underlying $X$, with group operation given by composition.
- $\mathrm{Aut}(X)$ denotes the group of **Lie automorphisms** of $X$: maps $\va
rphi\colon X\to X$ that are simultaneously group automorphisms (group homomorphi

**Proof.**

We verify the subgroup criterion. Let $H = \mathrm{Aut}(X)$ and $G = \mathrm{Dif
f}(X)$.

**Step 1: $H \subseteq G$ as a set.**

Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{Aut}(X)$. By definition of a Lie automorphism, $\varphi
$ is a smooth bijection $X \to X$ whose inverse $\varphi^{-1}$ is also smooth. H
ence $\varphi$ is a diffeomorphism of the underlying smooth manifold of $X$, so
$\varphi \in \mathrm{Diff}(X) = G$.

**Step 2: $H$ is a group under the operation inherited from $G$.**

- **Identity:** The identity map $\mathrm{id}_X$ is a smooth group automorphism
(it is a Lie group isomorphism), so $\mathrm{id}_X \in H$.
- **Closure under composition:** If $\varphi, \psi \in H$, then $\varphi\circ\ps
i$ is a group automorphism (composition of group automorphisms) and is smooth wi
th smooth inverse $\psi^{-1}\circ\varphi^{-1}$, so $\varphi\circ\psi \in H$.
- **Closure under inverses:** If $\varphi \in H$, then $\varphi^{-1}$ is a group
 automorphism (the inverse of a group automorphism) and is smooth with smooth in
verse $\varphi$, so $\varphi^{-1} \in H$.

The group operation on $H$ is composition, which is exactly the operation on $G$
. Therefore $H$ is a subgroup of $G$.

**Conclusion.**

Every Lie automorphism of $X$ is, in particular, a diffeomorphism of the underly
ing manifold, and $\mathrm{Aut}(X)$ is closed under composition and inversion wi
thin $\mathrm{Diff}(X)$. Hence

$$\mathrm{Aut}(X) \leq \mathrm{Diff}(X).$$
