## Multidimensional Fourier Series on a Rectangular Domain

### 1. Setup and periodic extension

Let $\mathbb{X} = [0,X_1]\times\cdots\times[0,X_D]$ with $X_d>0$, and let $f:\ma
thbb{X}\to\mathbb{C}$ (taken as $f\in L^2(\mathbb{X})$ for the core theory). Ext
end $f$ to a function on $\mathbb{R}^D$ that is **separately periodic** with per
iod $X_d$ in the $d$-th coordinate:

$$
f(x_1+X_1,x_2,\dots,x_D)=f(x_1,\dots),\quad\ldots,\quad f(\dots,x_D+X_D)=f(\dots
).
$$

This identifies $\mathbb{X}$ with the $D$-torus $\mathbb{T}^D = \prod_{d=1}^D \m
athbb{R}/X_d\mathbb{Z}$.

### 2. The orthonormal basis

For each multi-index $\mathbf{n}=(n_1,\dots,n_D)\in\mathbb{Z}^D$ define the **te
nsor-product plane wave**

$$
\phi_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x}) \;=\; \prod_{d=1}^{D} e^{\,2\pi i\, n_d x_d/X_d}
\;=\; e^{\,2\pi i\,\sum_{d=1}^D n_d x_d/X_d}.
$$

These form a **complete orthonormal system** in $L^2(\mathbb{X})$ with respect t
o the inner product

$$
\langle f,g\rangle \;=\; \frac{1}{\prod_{d=1}^D X_d}\int_{\mathbb{X}} f(\mathbf{
x})\,\overline{g(\mathbf{x})}\,d\mathbf{x}.
$$

Orthogonality follows from the 1D identity $\frac1{X_d}\int_0^{X_d}e^{2\pi i(n_d
-m_d)x_d/X_d}dx_d=\delta_{n_d,m_d}$ multiplied over $d$; completeness follows be
cause finite linear combinations of the $\phi_{\mathbf{n}}$ are dense in $L^2(\m
athbb{X})$ (tensor products of the dense 1D trigonometric system).

### 3. Fourier coefficients and the series

The **multidimensional Fourier coefficients** are

$$
\boxed{\;
\widehat{f}(\mathbf{n}) \;=\; \langle f,\phi_{\mathbf{n}}\rangle
\;=\; \frac{1}{\prod_{d=1}^D X_d}\int_{\mathbb{X}} f(\mathbf{x})\,
\exp\!\Big(-2\pi i\sum_{d=1}^D \frac{n_d x_d}{X_d}\Big)\,d\mathbf{x},
\qquad \mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^D.
\;}
$$

The **multidimensional Fourier series** of $f$ is

$$
\boxed{\;
f(\mathbf{x}) \;\sim\; \sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^D} \widehat{f}(\mathbf{n})\
,
\exp\!\Big(2\pi i\sum_{d=1}^D \frac{n_d x_d}{X_d}\Big).
\;}
$$

Equivalently, expanding the product, it is the **iterated series**

$$
\sum_{n_1\in\mathbb{Z}}\cdots\sum_{n_D\in\mathbb{Z}}
\widehat{f}(n_1,\dots,n_D)\prod_{d=1}^D e^{2\pi i n_d x_d/X_d}.
$$

The **rectangular partial sums** are

$$
S_{\mathbf{N}}f(\mathbf{x}) \;=\; \sum_{|n_1|\le N_1}\cdots\sum_{|n_D|\le N_D}
\widehat{f}(\mathbf{n})\,\phi_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x}),
\qquad \mathbf{N}=(N_1,\dots,N_D).
$$

### 4. Convergence theorems

**(a) $L^2$ convergence (Riesz–Fischer / Parseval).**
If $f\in L^2(\mathbb{X})$, then

$$
\big\|S_{\mathbf{N}}f - f\big\|_{L^2(\mathbb{X})}\xrightarrow[\min_d N_d\to\inft
y]{}0,
$$

and **Parseval's identity** holds:

$$
\frac{1}{\prod_d X_d}\int_{\mathbb{X}}|f(\mathbf{x})|^2\,d\mathbf{x}
\;=\; \sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^D}\big|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{n})\big|^2.
$$

This is immediate because $\{\phi_{\mathbf{n}}\}$ is a complete orthonormal syst
em; dimension plays no extra role. The same holds for $L^p$, $1<p<\infty$, in no
rm (the $D$-fold tensor product of the 1D Riesz projection is bounded on $L^p$ b
y the M. Riesz theorem iterated).

**(b) Pointwise a.e. convergence (multidimensional Carleson–Hunt).**
If $f\in L^p(\mathbb{X})$ with $1<p<\infty$, then the rectangular partial sums c
onverge to $f$ **almost everywhere**:

$$
S_{\mathbf{N}}f(\mathbf{x})\to f(\mathbf{x})\quad\text{for a.e. }\mathbf{x}\in\m
athbb{X}.
$$

*Reason.* The rectangular partial sum factorizes as an iterated 1D partial-sum o
perator:
$$
S_{\mathbf{N}}f = T_{N_1}^{(1)}\,T_{N_2}^{(2)}\cdots T_{N_D}^{(D)} f,
$$
where $T_N^{(d)}$ is the 1D Fourier partial-sum operator acting in the $d$-th va
riable. The 1D Carleson–Hunt theorem gives, for $g\in L^p(\mathbb{R})$, $1<p<\in
fty$, the a.e. convergence $T_N g\to g$ together with the boundedness of the Car
leson maximal operator $\mathcal{C}g=\sup_N|T_N g|$ on $L^p$. Applying this succ
essively in each variable (Fubini + the maximal bound controls the exceptional s
ets) yields a.e. convergence of the rectangular sums. (The foundational higher-d
imensional case is due to C. Fefferman, *Pointwise convergence of double Fourier
 series*, 1973.)

**(c) Pointwise convergence at regular points (Dirichlet–Jordan / Dini, multidim
ensional).**
Suppose at a point $\mathbf{x}$ the $2^D$ one-sided limits

$$
f_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})=\lim_{\substack{t_d\downarrow 0\\ t_d>0\iff \varepsi
lon_d=1}}
f(x_1+(-1)^{1-\varepsilon_1}t_1,\dots,x_D+(-1)^{1-\varepsilon_D}t_D),\qquad \var
epsilon\in\{0,1\}^D,
$$

all exist, and $f$ satisfies a **multidimensional Dini condition** (e.g. the mix
ed modulus of continuity $\omega_{\mathbf{x}}(\boldsymbol\delta)=\sum_{\varepsil
on}|f(\mathbf{x}\pm\boldsymbol\delta)-f_\varepsilon(\mathbf{x})|$ satisfies $\in
t_0^\delta\omega_{\mathbf{x}}(\boldsymbol\delta)\prod_d d\delta_d/\delta_d<\inft
y$), or $f$ is of **bounded variation in each variable separately** in a neighbo
rhood of $\mathbf{x}$. Then

$$
S_{\mathbf{N}}f(\mathbf{x})\xrightarrow[\min_d N_d\to\infty]{}
\frac{1}{2^D}\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^D} f_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x}),
$$

the average of the $2^D$ one-sided limits. At a point of continuity this average
 is just $f(\mathbf{x})$; for $D=1$ this reduces to the classical $(f(x+)+f(x-))
/2$.

**(d) Uniform and absolute convergence (smooth case).**
If $f\in C^{k_1\cdots k_D}(\mathbb{X})$ with periodic boundary compatibility and
 the mixed derivatives are integrable with $\sum_{\mathbf{n}}|\widehat{f}(\mathb
f{n})|<\infty$, the series converges **absolutely and uniformly** to $f$. A suff
icient concrete condition: if $f$ has continuous mixed partial derivatives up to
 total order $D$ (e.g. $f\in C^D$ with all first-order partials of bounded varia
tion), then $|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{n})|=O\!\big(\prod_d(1+|n_d|)^{-1}\big)$, givi
ng absolute convergence. More generally, if for some $\alpha>D$ one has $|\wideh
at{f}(\mathbf{n})|=O((1+\|\mathbf{n}\|)^{-\alpha})$, the series converges absolu
tely and uniformly to a continuous $f$.

### 5. Summary

The multidimensional Fourier series of $f$ on $\mathbb{X}=\prod_d[0,X_d]$ is

$$
f(\mathbf{x})\sim\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^D}\widehat{f}(\mathbf{n})\,e^{2\p
i i\sum_d n_d x_d/X_d},
\qquad
\widehat{f}(\mathbf{n})=\frac{1}{\prod_d X_d}\int_{\mathbb{X}}f(\mathbf{x})\,e^{
-2\pi i\sum_d n_d x_d/X_d}\,d\mathbf{x},
$$

built from the tensor product of the 1D Fourier bases. It converges:

- **in $L^2$ (indeed $L^p$, $1<p<\infty$) norm** for every $f\in L^p(\mathbb{X})
$ — Parseval holds;
- **almost everywhere** (rectangular partial sums) for every $f\in L^p(\mathbb{X
})$, $1<p<\infty$, by the multidimensional Carleson–Hunt theorem;
- **pointwise** at every point satisfying a multidimensional Dini/bounded-variat
ion condition, to the average of the $2^D$ one-sided limits;
- **absolutely and uniformly** when the coefficients are summable (e.g. $f$ suff
iciently smooth with periodic compatibility).
