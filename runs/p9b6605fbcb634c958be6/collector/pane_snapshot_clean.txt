## Proof

Let $S = \sum_{i=1}^n a_i$, $Q = \sum_{i=1}^n a_i^2$, and $P = \sum_{i<j} a_i a_
j$, so $S^2 = Q + 2P$.

The divisibility condition $Q \mid S^2 - 1$ becomes $Q \mid 2P - 1$ (since $S^2
- 1 = Q + 2P - 1$).

**Bounding $2P - 1$:** By Cauchy–Schwarz, $S^2 \le nQ$, so $2P = S^2 - Q \le (n-
1)Q$, giving $2P - 1 < (n-1)Q$. Since all $a_i \ge 1$ and $n \ge 2$, we have $P
\ge \binom{n}{2} \ge 1$, so $2P - 1 \ge 1 > 0$.

Thus $2P - 1 = kQ$ for some integer $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n-2\}$, which gives:
$$S^2 = (k+1)Q + 1.$$

Setting $m = k+1$, we need $S^2 = mQ + 1$ with $m \in \{2, 3, \ldots, n-1\}$.

### Key Parity Lemma

**Since $a_i^2 \equiv a_i \pmod{2}$, we always have $Q \equiv S \pmod{2}$.**

**Case $S$ even:** Then $Q$ is even. From $S^2 = mQ + 1$: since $S$ even, $S^2 \
equiv 0 \pmod{4}$, so $mQ \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$. But $Q$ even forces $mQ$ even, so
$mQ \pmod{4} \in \{0, 2\}$ — **contradiction**. No solution exists with $S$ even
.

**Case $S$ odd:** Then $Q$ is odd. From $S^2 = mQ + 1$: since $S$ odd, $S^2 \equ
iv 1 \pmod{8}$, so $mQ \equiv 0 \pmod{8}$. Since $Q$ is odd, this forces $\mathb
f{8 \mid m}$.

### Ruling out $n \le 8$

For $S$ even: impossible (shown above).
For $S$ odd: we need $8 \mid m$ with $2 \le m \le n-1$. The smallest multiple of
 8 in this range is $m = 8$, requiring $n - 1 \ge 8$, i.e., $n \ge 9$.

**Therefore $n \le 8$ is impossible.**

### Constructing a solution for $n = 9$

Take $m = 8$ (so $k = 7$). We need $S^2 = 8Q + 1$ with $S$ odd and $v_2(S^2 - 1)
 = 3$ (so that $Q = (S^2-1)/8$ is an odd integer).

This requires $S \equiv 3$ or $5 \pmod{8}$. Try $S = 11$ ($11 \equiv 3 \pmod{8}$
):
$$Q = \frac{121 - 1}{8} = 15.$$

We need 9 positive integers summing to 11 with sum of squares 15. Since $11 = 9
+ 2$, we distribute 2 extra units among nine 1's: take **seven 1's and two 2's**
:
$$a_1 = a_2 = \cdots = a_7 = 1, \quad a_8 = a_9 = 2.$$
$$S = 7 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot 2 = 11, \qquad Q = 7 \cdot 1^2 + 2 \cdot 2^2 = 7 + 8 =
 15.$$

**Verification:**
$$S^2 - 1 = 121 - 1 = 120 = 8 \times 15 = 8Q.$$

So $Q \mid S^2 - 1$. ✓
