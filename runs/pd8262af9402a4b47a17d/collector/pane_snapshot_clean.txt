# Solution

## Setup

We seek the number of non-isomorphic semidirect products $N \rtimes_\varphi H$ w
here $N = \mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z}$, $H = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$, and $p$ is an
 odd prime. Such semidirect products are classified by homomorphisms $\varphi: H
 \to \operatorname{Aut}(N)$, up to the equivalence that yields isomorphic groups
.

## Step 1: The automorphism group

$$\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z})^
\times,$$

which is cyclic of order $\varphi(p^2) = p(p-1)$. Since $\gcd(p,\, p-1)=1$, this
 is cyclic.

## Step 2: Counting the homomorphisms

A homomorphism $\varphi: \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z} \to \operatorname{Aut}(N)$ is de
termined by the image of a generator $g$ of $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$, which must
 be an element of $\operatorname{Aut}(N)$ of order dividing $p$.

Since $\operatorname{Aut}(N)$ is cyclic of order $p(p-1)$, it has a **unique** s
ubgroup of order $p$, namely
$$\{1,\; 1+p,\; 1+2p,\; \dots,\; 1+(p-1)p\} \pmod{p^2}.$$
(These are exactly the units congruent to $1 \bmod p$; each non-identity element
 has order exactly $p$ since $(1+kp)^p \equiv 1 \pmod{p^2}$ for all $k$.)

Thus there are exactly **$p$** homomorphisms $\varphi_0, \varphi_1, \dots, \varp
hi_{p-1}$, where $\varphi_k(g)$ = multiplication by $1+kp$. Among these, $\varph
i_0$ is trivial and $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_{p-1}$ are non-trivial.

## Step 3: Isomorphism criterion

A standard sufficient condition for $N \rtimes_{\varphi_k} H \cong N \rtimes_{\v
arphi_l} H$ is: there exist $\alpha \in \operatorname{Aut}(N)$ and $\beta \in \o
peratorname{Aut}(H)$ such that
$$\varphi_l \circ \beta = \alpha \circ \varphi_k \circ \alpha^{-1} \quad \text{(
as maps } H \to \operatorname{Aut}(N)\text{)}.$$

Since $\operatorname{Aut}(N) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z})^\times$ is **abeli
an**, conjugation by $\alpha$ is trivial, so the condition reduces to:
$$\varphi_l \circ \beta = \varphi_k.$$

## Step 4: The trivial action is isolated

- $\varphi_0$ (trivial) yields the **abelian** group $\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z} \
times \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$.
- Each $\varphi_k$ with $k \neq 0$ yields a **non-abelian** group (the action of
 $H$ on $N$ is non-trivial, so some $h \in H$ acts non-trivially on some $n \in
N$).

An abelian group cannot be isomorphic to a non-abelian group, so the trivial sem
idirect product is in its own isomorphism class.

## Step 5: All non-trivial actions give isomorphic groups

Take any two non-trivial $\varphi_k, \varphi_l$ with $k, l \in \{1, \dots, p-1\}
$. We seek $\beta \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})$, say $\beta(g)
 = g^m$ with $m \in \{1, \dots, p-1\}$, such that $\varphi_l \circ \beta = \varp
hi_k$.

Evaluating at $g$:
$$\varphi_l(g^m) = (1+lp)^m \equiv 1 + mlp \pmod{p^2},$$
since higher powers of $lp$ are divisible by $p^2$. We need $1 + mlp \equiv 1 +
kp \pmod{p^2}$, i.e.,
$$ml \equiv k \pmod{p}.$$

Since $l \not\equiv 0 \pmod{p}$, it is invertible mod $p$, and we set $m \equiv
k\, l^{-1} \pmod{p}$, giving a valid $m \in \{1, \dots, p-1\}$. Hence $\beta$ ex
ists, and **all $p-1$ non-trivial semidirect products are mutually isomorphic**.

## Step 6: Necessity (no further collapsing)

The trivial action gives an abelian group; the non-trivial actions give non-abel
ian groups. These cannot coincide. Within the non-trivial class, we have shown a
ll are isomorphic. No further identification is possible across the abelian/non-
abelian divide.

## Conclusion

The $p$ homomorphisms fall into exactly **2** isomorphism classes of semidirect
products:

1. **$\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$** (abelian, from t
he trivial action $\varphi_0$).
2. **The non-abelian group of order $p^3$ with exponent $p^2$**, $\mathbb{Z}/p^2
\mathbb{Z} \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ (from any of the $p-1$ non-trivial ac
tions, all isomorphic).
