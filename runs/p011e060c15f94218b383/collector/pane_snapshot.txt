## Solution

**Setup.** Let $X_1, \dots, X_n$ be i.i.d. $LN(\mu, \sigma^2)$, so each $X_i = e
^{Y_i}$ with $Y_i \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2)$. The density of a single observation is

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{x\,\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}} \exp\!\left(-\frac{(\ln x - \mu)^2}{2\si
gma^2}\right), \quad x > 0.$$

The sample mean is $\bar{X}_n = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i$.

**General saddlepoint approximation.** For i.i.d. random variables with common c
umulant generating function (CGF) $K(t) = \log M(t) = \log E[e^{tX}]$, the saddl
epoint approximation to the density of $\bar{X}_n$ at $\bar{x}$ is (Daniels, 195
4):

$$\boxed{f_{\bar{X}_n}(\bar{x}) \approx \sqrt{\frac{n}{2\pi\, K''(\hat{t})}}\; \
exp\!\Big(n\big[K(\hat{t}) - \hat{t}\,\bar{x}\big]\Big)}$$

where the **saddlepoint** $\hat{t} = \hat{t}(\bar{x})$ is the unique solution to
 the saddlepoint equation

$$K'(\hat{t}) = \bar{x}.$$

**CGF of the log-normal.** The moment generating function of $X \sim LN(\mu,\sig
ma^2)$ is

$$M(t) = E[e^{tX}] = \int_0^\infty e^{tx}\,\frac{1}{x\,\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\exp\
!\left(-\frac{(\ln x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right) dx.$$

A critical property of the log-normal distribution is that **this integral diver
ges for all $t > 0$**, so $M(t) = \infty$ for $t > 0$. The MGF is finite only on
 $(-\infty, 0]$. Consequently, the CGF

$$K(t) = \log M(t)$$

is defined only for $t \in (-\infty, 0]$, and must be evaluated via the Laplace-
transform integral (no closed form exists):

$$K(t) = \log\!\left(\int_0^\infty e^{tx}\,\frac{1}{x\,\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\exp\
!\left(-\frac{(\ln x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right) dx\right), \quad t \le 0.$$

**Derivatives of $K$.** The first and second derivatives are:

$$K'(t) = \frac{M'(t)}{M(t)} = E_t[X], \qquad K''(t) = \frac{M''(t)\,M(t) - [M'(
t)]^2}{M(t)^2} = \mathrm{Var}_t(X),$$

where $E_t$ and $\mathrm{Var}_t$ denote expectation and variance under the expon
entially tilted density $f_t(x) = e^{tx}f(x)/M(t)$. Explicitly:

$$K'(t) = \frac{\displaystyle\int_0^\infty x\,e^{tx}\,f(x)\,dx}{\displaystyle\in
t_0^\infty e^{tx}\,f(x)\,dx}, \qquad K''(t) = \frac{\displaystyle\int_0^\infty x
^2\,e^{tx}\,f(x)\,dx}{\displaystyle\int_0^\infty e^{tx}\,f(x)\,dx} - \left(K'(t)
\right)^2.$$

**The saddlepoint equation.** The saddlepoint $\hat{t}$ solves

$$K'(\hat{t}) = \frac{\displaystyle\int_0^\infty x\,e^{\hat{t} x}\,\frac{1}{x\,\
sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{(\ln x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right) dx}{\di
splaystyle\int_0^\infty e^{\hat{t} x}\,\frac{1}{x\,\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\exp\!\le
ft(-\frac{(\ln x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right) dx} = \bar{x}.$$

**Domain of validity.** Since $K(t)$ is defined only for $t \le 0$:
- At $t = 0$: $K'(0) = E[X] = e^{\mu + \sigma^2/2}$.
- As $t \to -\infty$: $K'(t) \to 0^+$.

Since $K'(t)$ is strictly increasing (as $K''(t) > 0$), the saddlepoint $\hat{t}
 \in (-\infty, 0]$ exists **if and only if**

$$0 < \bar{x} \le e^{\mu + \sigma^2/2} = E[X].$$

For $\bar{x} > E[X]$, the saddlepoint would require $\hat{t} > 0$, where $M(t) =
 \infty$, so **the standard saddlepoint approximation does not exist** in the ri
ght tail. This is a well-known fundamental limitation: the log-normal distributi
on has no finite MGF for $t > 0$, so the saddlepoint method breaks down in the u
pper tail.

**Final result.** The saddlepoint approximation to the p.d.f. of $\bar{X}_n$ is:

$$\boxed{f_{\bar{X}_n}(\bar{x}) \approx \sqrt{\frac{n}{2\pi\, K''(\hat{t})}}\; \
exp\!\Big(n\big[K(\hat{t}) - \hat{t}\,\bar{x}\big]\Big), \qquad 0 < \bar{x} \le
e^{\mu+\sigma^2/2},}$$

where:
- $K(t) = \log\!\displaystyle\left(\int_0^\infty e^{tx}\,\frac{1}{x\,\sigma\sqrt
{2\pi}}\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{(\ln x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right) dx\right)$ for $t
 \le 0$,
- $\hat{t} \le 0$ is the unique solution to $K'(\hat{t}) = \bar{x}$,
- $K''(\hat{t}) = \mathrm{Var}_{\hat{t}}(X)$ is the variance under the tilted di
stribution.

The integrals defining $K$, $K'$, and $K''$ have no closed-form expressions and
must be evaluated numerically. For $\bar{x} > e^{\mu + \sigma^2/2}$, the standar
d saddlepoint approximation is unavailable because the log-normal MGF diverges f
or all $t > 0$.
