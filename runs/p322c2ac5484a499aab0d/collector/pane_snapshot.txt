Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a weighted, strongly connec
ted digraph with ≥3 vertices, non-empty edges, and non-zero weights can have all
 distinct simple-path weight sums.

**Answer: Yes, it is possible.**

**Construction.** Take the directed 3-cycle on vertices $\{A, B, C\}$ with edges
 and weights:
$$A \xrightarrow{1} B, \quad B \xrightarrow{2} C, \quad C \xrightarrow{4} A$$

**Verification of graph properties:**
- *Strongly connected:* The cycle $A \to B \to C \to A$ lets us reach any vertex
 from any other. ✓
- *At least 3 vertices:* Exactly 3. ✓
- *Non-empty edge set:* 3 edges. ✓
- *Non-zero weights:* $1, 2, 4 \neq 0$. ✓

**Enumerating all non-trivial simple paths.** In a 3-vertex graph, a simple path
 has at most 2 edges (3 distinct vertices). The complete list:

| Path | Weight sum |
|------|-----------|
| $A \to B$ | $1$ |
| $B \to C$ | $2$ |
| $C \to A$ | $4$ |
| $A \to B \to C$ | $1+2=3$ |
| $B \to C \to A$ | $2+4=6$ |
| $C \to A \to B$ | $4+1=5$ |

The weight sums are $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$ — all six values are distinct. ✓

**Why these are all the paths.** Length-1 paths are exactly the three edges. Len
gth-2 paths require two consecutive edges; the only possibilities following the
cycle are $A \to B \to C$, $B \to C \to A$, and $C \to A \to B$. No length-3 sim
ple path exists (it would require 4 distinct vertices). Hence the six paths abov
e are exhaustive.

**Why the weights work.** The key insight is choosing weights as powers of 2 ($1
, 2, 4$). The three single-edge sums are $\{1,2,4\}$ and the three two-edge sums
 are $\{3, 5, 6\}$. These two sets are disjoint (since $1+2=3 < 4$, the smallest
 two-edge sum is less than the largest single-edge sum, yet they don't collide),
 and within each set all values are distinct. More precisely, the single-edge we
ights $\{1,2,4\}$ are distinct by choice, and the two-edge sums $\{1{+}2, 2{+}4,
 4{+}1\} = \{3,6,5\}$ are distinct because no two pairs of $\{1,2,4\}$ share the
 same sum (which follows from the weights being distinct). Cross-type collisions
 are avoided because $\{1,2,4\} \cap \{3,5,6\} = \emptyset$.

Therefore, such a digraph exists.
