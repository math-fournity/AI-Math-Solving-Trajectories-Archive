## Solution

**Claim**: The statement "No parametrized surface is closed in ℝ³" is **False**.

### Fitzpatrick's Definition

In Fitzpatrick's *Advanced Calculus* (2009), a **parametrized surface** is the i
mage of a continuously differentiable function **r** : ℛ → ℝ³, where ℛ is a **re
gion** in ℝ². A *region* in Fitzpatrick's terminology is the union of an open co
nnected set with some, none, or all of its boundary points.

### Counterexample: The *xy*-Plane

Take ℛ = ℝ², which is a region (it is open and connected, with no boundary point
s).

Define **r** : ℝ² → ℝ³ by:
$$\mathbf{r}(u, v) = (u,\, v,\, 0).$$

**Continuously differentiable**: The partial derivatives are constant — **r**_u
= (1, 0, 0) and **r**_v = (0, 1, 0) — so **r** is C¹ (indeed C^∞).

**Image**:
$$\mathbf{r}(\mathbb{R}^2) = \{(x, y, 0) \in \mathbb{R}^3 : x, y \in \mathbb{R}\
},$$
the *xy*-plane in ℝ³.

**Closedness**: The *xy*-plane is the preimage of the closed set {0} under the c
ontinuous projection π₃ : ℝ³ → ℝ defined by π₃(x, y, z) = z:
$$\mathbf{r}(\mathbb{R}^2) = \pi_3^{-1}(\{0\}).$$
Since the preimage of a closed set under a continuous function is closed, the *x
y*-plane is closed in ℝ³.

### Conclusion

The *xy*-plane is a parametrized surface (per Fitzpatrick's definition: C¹ map f
rom a region ℛ = ℝ² to ℝ³) whose image is a closed subset of ℝ³. This directly c
ontradicts the universal claim that *no* parametrized surface is closed in ℝ³.

Therefore, the statement is **False**. ∎
