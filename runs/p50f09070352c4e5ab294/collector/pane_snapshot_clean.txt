## Solution

We evaluate the contour integral
$$I = \int_{|z-\pi|=\pi} \frac{\sin(\pi z)}{z^2 - \pi^2}\,dz.$$

**Step 1: Identify the singularities.**

The denominator factors as
$$z^2 - \pi^2 = (z-\pi)(z+\pi),$$
so the integrand has potential singularities at $z = \pi$ and $z = -\pi$.

**Step 2: Determine which singularities lie inside the contour.**

The contour is the circle $|z - \pi| = \pi$, centered at $z = \pi$ with radius $
\pi$.

- $z = \pi$: distance from center is $|\pi - \pi| = 0 < \pi$. **Inside.**
- $z = -\pi$: distance from center is $|-\pi - \pi| = 2\pi > \pi$. **Outside.**

So only $z = \pi$ is enclosed.

**Step 3: Classify the singularity at $z = \pi$.**

The denominator has a simple zero at $z = \pi$ (since $z + \pi \neq 0$ there). T
he numerator at $z = \pi$ is
$$\sin(\pi \cdot \pi) = \sin(\pi^2) \neq 0,$$
since $\pi^2$ is not an integer multiple of $\pi$. Hence $z = \pi$ is a **simple
 pole**.

**Step 4: Compute the residue at $z = \pi$.**

For a simple pole at $z = \pi$:
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=\pi} \frac{\sin(\pi z)}{(z-\pi)(z+\pi)} = \lim_{z \to \p
i} (z-\pi)\cdot\frac{\sin(\pi z)}{(z-\pi)(z+\pi)} = \lim_{z\to\pi}\frac{\sin(\pi
 z)}{z+\pi} = \frac{\sin(\pi^2)}{2\pi}.$$

**Step 5: Apply the residue theorem.**

Taking the contour in the positive (counterclockwise) orientation:
$$I = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}_{z=\pi} = 2\pi i \cdot \frac{\sin(\pi^2)}{
2\pi} = i\sin(\pi^2).$$

**Result:**

$$\boxed{\int_{|z-\pi|=\pi} \frac{\sin(\pi z)}{z^{2}-\pi^{2}}\,dz = i\,\sin(\pi^
2)}$$
