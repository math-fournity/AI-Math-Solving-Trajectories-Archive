## Proof

Let $(R, \mathfrak{m})$ be a Noetherian local ring and let $E = E_R(R/\mathfrak{
m})$ denote the injective hull of the residue field.

**Step 1: $E$ is $\mathfrak{m}$-torsion.**

Every element $x \in E$ is annihilated by some power of $\mathfrak{m}$. This is
a standard property of $E_R(R/\mathfrak{m})$: the module $E$ is the union of the
 submodules $(0 :_E \mathfrak{m}^n)$ for $n \geq 1$. Indeed, $E$ is an essential
 extension of $R/\mathfrak{m}$, and one shows by induction that every element of
 $E$ is killed by a power of $\mathfrak{m}$ (using that $E$ is injective and the
 structure of submodules containing $R/\mathfrak{m}$).

**Step 2: $E$ is an Artinian $R$-module.**

This is a classical theorem of Matlis (1958). The key argument proceeds via the
$\mathfrak{m}$-adic completion $\hat{R}$. The module $E$ carries a natural $\hat
{R}$-module structure, and as an $\hat{R}$-module:
$$E \cong E_{\hat{R}}(\hat{R}/\hat{\mathfrak{m}}).$$

Since $\hat{R}$ is a complete Noetherian local ring, Matlis duality establishes
an anti-equivalence between the category of Noetherian $\hat{R}$-modules and the
 category of Artinian $\hat{R}$-modules, with the duality functor being $\text{H
om}_{\hat{R}}(-, E)$. Under this duality, $\hat{R}$ (which is Noetherian) corres
ponds to $E$, so $E$ is Artinian as an $\hat{R}$-module, hence also as an $R$-mo
dule.

**Step 3: The Krull dimension of $E$ is $0$.**

The Krull dimension of a module (in the sense of Gabriel–Rentschler, which is th
e standard notion for arbitrary — not necessarily finitely generated — modules)
is defined recursively:

- $\text{Kdim}(M) = -1$ if $M = 0$.
- $\text{Kdim}(M) \geq \alpha + 1$ if there exists a strictly descending chain $
M = M_0 \supsetneq M_1 \supsetneq M_2 \supsetneq \cdots$ with $\text{Kdim}(M_i /
 M_{i+1}) \geq \alpha$ for all $i$.
- $\text{Kdim}(M)$ is the smallest ordinal satisfying the above.

A module $M$ has $\text{Kdim}(M) = 0$ if and only if $M \neq 0$ and $M$ is Artin
ian (i.e., satisfies the descending chain condition on submodules). This is beca
use:
- If $M$ is Artinian and nonzero, there is no infinite strictly descending chain
 of submodules with nonzero successive quotients (the DCC forbids it), so $\text
{Kdim}(M) \not\geq 1$, while $\text{Kdim}(M) \geq 0$ since $M \neq 0$. Hence $\t
ext{Kdim}(M) = 0$.
- Conversely, $\text{Kdim}(M) = 0$ means no infinite descending chain with quoti
ents of dimension $\geq 0$ (i.e., nonzero quotients), which is exactly the Artin
ian condition.

Since $E = E_R(R/\mathfrak{m})$ is Artinian (Step 2) and nonzero, we conclude:
$$\text{Kdim}\big(E_R(R/\mathfrak{m})\big) = 0.$$

**Remark.** This answer is consistent with the support-based perspective: since
$E$ is $\mathfrak{m}$-torsion (Step 1), for any prime $\mathfrak{p} \subsetneq \
mathfrak{m}$ we can pick $s \in \mathfrak{m} \setminus \mathfrak{p}$, which is a
 unit in $R_\mathfrak{p}$, and $s^n$ kills every element of $E$ for suitable $n$
, forcing $E_\mathfrak{p} = 0$. Thus $\text{Supp}(E) = \{\mathfrak{m}\}$, which
has dimension $0$.
