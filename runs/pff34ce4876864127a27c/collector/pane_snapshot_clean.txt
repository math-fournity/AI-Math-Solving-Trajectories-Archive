## Proof

**Step 1: Area formula via the Jacobian.**

Since $g$ is holomorphic and univalent, it is a biholomorphism from $\mathbb{D}$
 onto its image $\Omega = g(\mathbb{D})$. Writing $g = u + iv$, the Jacobian det
erminant of the map $(x,y) \mapsto (u,v)$ is
$$J_g(z) = u_x v_y - u_y v_x = |g'(z)|^2.$$
Therefore the area of the image is
$$\operatorname{Area}(\Omega) = \iint_{\mathbb{D}} |g'(z)|^2\, dA(z).$$

**Step 2: Expand in a power series and apply Parseval.**

Write $g(z) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n z^n$ (the series starts at $n=1$ since $g(
0)=0$), so $g'(z) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n\, a_n\, z^{n-1}$. The condition $|g'(0
)| = k$ gives $|a_1| = k$.

Using polar coordinates $z = re^{i\theta}$ and Parseval's identity on the circle
:
$$\int_0^{2\pi} |g'(re^{i\theta})|^2\, d\theta = 2\pi \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^2 |a
_n|^2 r^{2(n-1)}.$$

Hence
$$\operatorname{Area}(\Omega) = \int_0^1 \int_0^{2\pi} |g'(re^{i\theta})|^2 r\,
d\theta\, dr = 2\pi \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^2 |a_n|^2 \int_0^1 r^{2n-1}\, dr.$$

Since $\int_0^1 r^{2n-1}\, dr = \frac{1}{2n}$, we obtain
$$\operatorname{Area}(\Omega) = \pi \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n\, |a_n|^2.$$

**Step 3: Lower bound.**

Every term in the sum is non-negative, so
$$\operatorname{Area}(\Omega) = \pi \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n\, |a_n|^2 \;\geq\; \pi
\, |a_1|^2 = \pi k^2.$$

**Step 4: The bound is attained.**

The function $g(z) = kz$ is holomorphic and injective on $\mathbb{D}$, satisfies
 $g(0)=0$ and $|g'(0)|=k$, and its image is the disk $\{w : |w| < k\}$, which ha
s area $\pi k^2$. Equivalently, in the series expansion all $a_n = 0$ for $n \ge
q 2$, so the area formula gives exactly $\pi k^2$.

**Conclusion.**

The minimum area of the image is

$$\boxed{\pi k^2}.$$
