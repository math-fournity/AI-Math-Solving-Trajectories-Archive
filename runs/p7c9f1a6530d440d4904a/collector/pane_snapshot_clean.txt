## Weak Formulation of the Biharmonic Problem (Clamped Plate)

### Setup

The strong problem is

$$
\begin{cases}
\Delta^2 u = f & \text{in } \Omega,\\
u = 0 & \text{on } \partial\Omega,\\
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial n} = 0 & \text{on } \partial\Omega,
\end{cases}
$$

with $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^2$ bounded, open, $\partial\Omega$ regular, $f\in
L^2(\Omega)$, and $\Delta^2=\Delta\Delta$.

The boundary conditions $u=0$ and $\partial u/\partial n=0$ are **essential (Dir
ichlet-type)** boundary conditions for a fourth-order operator. They must be bui
lt into the function space, not into the variational equation. The appropriate s
pace is

$$
H_0^2(\Omega)=\Bigl\{v\in H^2(\Omega):\; v=0,\;\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}=0\t
ext{ on }\partial\Omega\Bigr\}.
$$

### Derivation

Take a test function $v\in H_0^2(\Omega)$, multiply the PDE by $v$, and integrat
e over $\Omega$:

$$
\int_\Omega (\Delta^2 u)\, v\, dx = \int_\Omega f\, v\, dx. \tag{1}
$$

Apply Green's identity for the biharmonic operator (integrate by parts twice). T
he standard identity is

$$
\int_\Omega (\Delta^2 u)\, v\, dx
= \int_\Omega \Delta u\, \Delta v\, dx
- \int_{\partial\Omega} \Delta u\, \frac{\partial v}{\partial n}\, ds
+ \int_{\partial\Omega} \frac{\partial(\Delta u)}{\partial n}\, v\, ds. \tag{2}
$$

Since $v\in H_0^2(\Omega)$, we have $v=0$ and $\partial v/\partial n=0$ on $\par
tial\Omega$, so **both boundary integrals vanish**. Substituting (2) into (1) gi
ves

$$
\int_\Omega \Delta u\, \Delta v\, dx = \int_\Omega f\, v\, dx. \tag{3}
$$

### Weak formulation

Define the bilinear form and linear functional

$$
a(u,v)=\int_\Omega \Delta u\, \Delta v\, dx,\qquad
L(v)=\int_\Omega f\, v\, dx.
$$

> **Weak formulation.** Find $u\in H_0^2(\Omega)$ such that
> $$a(u,v)=L(v)\qquad\forall\, v\in H_0^2(\Omega),$$
> i.e.
> $$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_\Omega \Delta u\, \Delta v\, dx = \int_\Omega f\, v
\, dx\qquad\forall\, v\in H_0^2(\Omega).}$$

### Consistency check (well-posedness sketch)

- **Continuity.** By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, $|a(u,v)|\le\|\Delta u\|_{L^
2}\|\Delta v\|_{L^2}\le C\|u\|_{H^2}\|v\|_{H^2}$, and $|L(v)|\le\|f\|_{L^2}\|v\|
_{L^2}\le\|f\|_{L^2}\|v\|_{H^2}$. So $a$ and $L$ are continuous on $H_0^2(\Omega
)$.
- **Coercivity.** On $H_0^2(\Omega)$, the norm $\|v\|_{H^2}$ is equivalent to $\
|\Delta v\|_{L^2}$ (this is a standard elliptic-regularity / Poincaré-type resul
t for $H_0^2$; equivalently, $\int_\Omega(\Delta v)^2 dx = \int_\Omega\sum_{ij}(
v_{x_ix_j})^2 dx$ on $H_0^2(\Omega)$, giving $\|\Delta v\|_{L^2}^2\ge c\|v\|_{H^
2}^2$). Hence $a(v,v)=\|\Delta v\|_{L^2}^2\ge c\|v\|_{H^2}^2$, i.e. $a$ is coerc
ive.
- **Lax–Milgram.** With $a$ continuous and coercive and $L$ continuous on the Hi
lbert space $H_0^2(\Omega)$, the Lax–Milgram theorem yields existence and unique
ness of $u\in H_0^2(\Omega)$ solving the weak problem.

### Note on the boundary conditions

The two conditions $u=0$ and $\partial u/\partial n=0$ on $\partial\Omega$ are *
*essential** boundary conditions for the fourth-order operator $\Delta^2$; they
are enforced by the choice of the space $H_0^2(\Omega)$. There are no **natural*
* (Neumann-type) boundary conditions in this problem, which is why no boundary t
erms remain in (3). This is the classical **clamped plate** model.
