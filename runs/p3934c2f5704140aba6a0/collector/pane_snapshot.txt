**Theorem.** For every open, connected subset $U \subseteq \mathbb{C}$, there ex
ists a continuous surjection $f: \mathbb{D} \to U$.

**Proof.**

The answer is **yes**. We construct such a surjection in three stages.

---

**Stage 1: Reduce $\mathbb{D}$ to $\mathbb{R}^2$ and then to $\mathbb{R}$.**

The open unit disk $\mathbb{D} = \{z : |z|<1\}$ is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^2
$ via
$$\phi(z) = \frac{z}{1-|z|}, \qquad \phi^{-1}(w) = \frac{w}{1+|w|}.$$
The projection $\pi_1 : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$, $\pi_1(x,y)=x$, is a conti
nuous surjection. Composing, we obtain a continuous surjection $\pi_1 \circ \phi
 : \mathbb{D} \to \mathbb{R}$.

It therefore suffices to show: **every open, connected $U \subseteq \mathbb{C}$
is a continuous image of $\mathbb{R}$.**

---

**Stage 2: Construct a compact, connected, locally connected exhaustion of $U$.*
*

Since $U$ is open in $\mathbb{C} \cong \mathbb{R}^2$, the collection of all clos
ed disks with rational center and rational radius contained in $U$ is countable
and covers $U$. Enumerate them as $\{D_k\}_{k=1}^{\infty}$, and fix a basepoint
$p \in D_1$.

Since $U$ is open and connected in $\mathbb{R}^2$, it is path-connected (indeed
polygonally path-connected). Define an increasing sequence of sets inductively:
- $K_1 = D_1$.
- $K_{n+1} = K_n \cup D_{n+1} \cup \gamma_n$, where $\gamma_n$ is a polygonal pa
th in $U$ from $p$ to the center of $D_{n+1}$.

Each $K_n$ is a **finite** union of closed disks and polygonal paths, hence:
- **Compact** (finite union of compact sets in $\mathbb{R}^2$),
- **Connected** (each piece intersects $K_{n-1}$ or contains $p$, so the union i
s connected),
- **Locally connected** (finite union of compact, locally connected sets with co
nnected intersections is locally connected),
- **Second-countable** (subspace of $\mathbb{R}^2$).

Moreover, $K_n \subseteq K_{n+1}$ and $\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} K_n \supseteq \big
cup_{k=1}^{\infty} D_k = U$, so $\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} K_n = U$.

---

**Stage 3: Build a continuous surjection $\mathbb{R} \to U$ by stitching Peano c
urves.**

By the **Hahn–Mazurkiewicz theorem**, each $K_n$ (being compact, connected, loca
lly connected, and metrizable) is a continuous image of $[0,1]$. Furthermore, si
nce each $K_n$ is path-connected, we can choose the surjection $h_n : [0,1] \to
K_n$ so that $h_n(0) = h_n(1) = p$:

> *Construction of $h_n$:* Take any surjection $f:[0,1]\to K_n$ (guaranteed by H
ahn–Mazurkiewicz). Let $\alpha:[0,1]\to K_n$ be a path from $p$ to $f(0)$ and $\
beta:[0,1]\to K_n$ a path from $f(1)$ to $p$. Define
> $$h_n(t) = \begin{cases} \alpha(3t) & t \in [0,\tfrac{1}{3}] \\ f(3t-1) & t \i
n [\tfrac{1}{3}, \tfrac{2}{3}] \\ \beta(3t-2) & t \in [\tfrac{2}{3}, 1] \end{cas
es}$$
> Then $h_n$ is continuous, surjective (since $f([0,1])=K_n$), and $h_n(0)=h_n(1
)=p$.

Now define $g : [0,\infty) \to U$ by
$$g(t) = h_{n+1}(t - n), \qquad t \in [n, n+1], \quad n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$$

**Continuity:** On each open interval $(n, n+1)$, $g$ is continuous. At each int
eger $t = n \geq 1$:
$$\lim_{t \to n^-} g(t) = h_n(1) = p = h_{n+1}(0) = \lim_{t \to n^+} g(t).$$
So $g$ is continuous on $[0, \infty)$.

**Surjectivity:** $g([0,\infty)) = \bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty} h_{n+1}([0,1]) = \bigc
up_{n=1}^{\infty} K_n = U$.

Extend to all of $\mathbb{R}$ by setting $g(t) = p$ for $t \leq 0$. This is cont
inuous at $t = 0$ (since $g(0) = p = h_1(0) = \lim_{t \to 0^+} g(t)$), and yield
s a continuous surjection $g : \mathbb{R} \to U$.

---

**Stage 4: Compose.**

The map
$$F = g \circ \pi_1 \circ \phi : \mathbb{D} \xrightarrow{\;\phi\;} \mathbb{R}^2
\xrightarrow{\;\pi_1\;} \mathbb{R} \xrightarrow{\;g\;} U$$
is a composition of continuous surjections, hence is itself a **continuous surje
ction** from $\mathbb{D}$ onto $U$.

---

**Conclusion.** For every open, connected subset $U$ of the complex plane, there
 exists a continuous surjection from the open unit disk $\mathbb{D}$ onto $U$. T
he function need not be injective — and indeed, by allowing non-injectivity (via
 space-filling curve techniques), we can always cover the entire target. $\black
