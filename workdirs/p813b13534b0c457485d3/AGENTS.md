# Problem

A high-tech security firm is designing a bi-hexagonal sensor array for a satellite. The array consists of 13 specific sensor nodes arranged in a rigid geometric pattern:
1. One central hub node.
2. Six nodes forming the vertices of a regular hexagon immediately surrounding the hub.
3. Six nodes forming the outermost tips of a symmetric six-pointed star (the points of a larger equilateral triangle overlapping another).

Each of the 13 nodes must be activated in one of two distinct energy states: "Ion-Positive" (represented by a red light) or "Gamma-Neutral" (represented by a green light).

To prevent constructive interference that would fry the circuitry, the system has a critical safety constraint: no three nodes assigned to the same energy state can be positioned such that they form the vertices of an equilateral triangle of any size or orientation within the 13-node grid.

Let $N$ be the total number of valid ways to assign energy states to all 13 nodes without violating the interference constraint. Find $N$.
