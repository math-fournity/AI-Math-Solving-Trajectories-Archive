We evaluate the integral

$$I = \int_0^{2\pi} e^{e^{it}}\, dt.$$

**Parameterization.** Set $z(t) = e^{it}$ for $t \in [0, 2\pi]$. As $t$ runs fro
m $0$ to $2\pi$, the point $z(t)$ traverses the unit circle $|z|=1$ once counter
clockwise. Differentiating,

$$dz = i e^{it}\, dt = i z\, dt \quad\Longrightarrow\quad dt = \frac{dz}{i z}.$$

**Rewrite the integral in terms of $z$.** Since $e^{it} = z$, the integrand beco
mes $e^z$, and

$$I = \oint_{|z|=1} e^z \cdot \frac{dz}{i z} = \frac{1}{i}\oint_{|z|=1} \frac{e^
z}{z}\, dz.$$

**Apply Cauchy's integral formula.** The function $f(z) = e^z$ is entire, so in
particular it is holomorphic on and inside the unit circle. The integrand $\frac
{e^z}{z}$ has a single simple pole at $z = 0$, which lies inside $|z|=1$. By Cau
chy's integral formula,

$$\oint_{|z|=1} \frac{e^z}{z}\, dz = 2\pi i \cdot f(0) = 2\pi i \cdot e^0 = 2\pi
 i.$$

**Compute the value.** Therefore

$$I = \frac{1}{i} \cdot 2\pi i = 2\pi.$$

**Verification via series (independent check).** Expanding $e^{e^{it}}$ as a pow
er series,

$$e^{e^{it}} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{e^{int}}{n!}.$$

Integrating term by term over $[0, 2\pi]$,

$$\int_0^{2\pi} e^{int}\, dt = \begin{cases} 2\pi & n = 0, \\ 0 & n \neq 0. \end
{cases}$$

Hence only the $n=0$ term survives, giving $I = \frac{1}{0!}\cdot 2\pi = 2\pi$,
confirming the result.

$$\boxed{\int_0^{2\pi} e^{e^{it}}\, dt = 2\pi}$$
