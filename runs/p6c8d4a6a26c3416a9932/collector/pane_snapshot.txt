## Proof

**Setup.** We have $2n$ lines divided into $n$ pairs, where the two lines within
 each pair are parallel. To maximize regions, we choose the $n$ pairs at $n$ dis
tinct angles (so lines from *different* pairs always intersect) and place them i
n general position (no three lines concurrent).

**Key fact.** When $m$ lines are placed one at a time, the $i$-th line added cre
ates (number of intersection points it has with previously added lines) $+\,1$ n
ew regions.

**General position baseline.** For $2n$ lines in general position (no two parall
el, no three concurrent), the $i$-th line added intersects all $i-1$ previous li
nes, creating $i$ new regions. The total is:
$$R_{\text{general}} = 1 + \sum_{i=1}^{2n} i = 1 + \frac{2n(2n+1)}{2} = 2n^2 + n
 + 1.$$

**Accounting for parallelism.** In our configuration, each parallel pair causes
exactly one "missing" intersection: the two lines within a pair do not cross, wh
ereas in general position they would. Consider adding the lines in any order. Fo
r each pair $(a_j, b_j)$, whichever line is added second is parallel to one alre
ady-present line, so it has **one fewer** intersection than it would in general
position — producing **one fewer** region.

Since there are $n$ pairs, the total number of lost intersections is exactly $n$
, and each lost intersection reduces the region count by exactly $1$. Therefore:
$$R_{\max} = R_{\text{general}} - n = (2n^2 + n + 1) - n = 2n^2 + 1.$$

**Achievability.** This bound is attained by choosing $n$ distinct directions an
d placing two parallel lines in each direction, ensuring no three lines are conc
urrent. Then:
- Every pair of lines from *different* pairs intersects (contributing the full g
eneral-position count), and
- The only missing intersections are the $n$ within-pair non-crossings.

**Verification with small cases:**
- $n = 0$: $1$ region. Formula: $2(0)^2 + 1 = 1$. ✓
- $n = 1$: Two parallel lines divide the plane into $3$ regions. Formula: $2(1)^
2 + 1 = 3$. ✓
- $n = 2$: Two pairs of parallel lines (e.g., two horizontal + two vertical) for
m a $3 \times 3$ grid of $9$ regions. Formula: $2(2)^2 + 1 = 9$. ✓
- $n = 3$: Six lines in three parallel pairs. General position for 6 lines gives
 $22$ regions; we lose $3$ intersections (one per pair), giving $19$. Formula: $
2(3)^2 + 1 = 19$. ✓

**Conclusion.** The largest number of regions into which a plane can be divided
by $n$ pairs of parallel lines is:

$$\boxed{2n^2 + 1}$$
