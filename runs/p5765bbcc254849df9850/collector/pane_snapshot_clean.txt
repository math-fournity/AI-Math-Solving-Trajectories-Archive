## Solution

**Setup.** Let $I$ be the incenter of $\triangle ABC$, so $AI$, $BI$, $CI$ are t
he angle bisectors. The incircle is centered at $I$ with radius $r$. Points $P$,
 $Q$, $R$ lie on the incircle along rays $IA$, $IB$, $IC$ respectively (each at
distance $r$ from $I$).

**Key fact.** Since $I$ is the incenter, $AI$ bisects $\angle BAC$:
$$\angle BAI = \angle CAI = \frac{40°}{2} = 20°.$$

**Central angle at $I$.** The well-known incenter relation gives:
$$\angle BIC = 90° + \frac{\angle A}{2} = 90° + 20° = 110°.$$

Since $Q$ lies on ray $IB$ and $R$ lies on ray $IC$, the central angle subtendin
g arc $QR$ is:
$$\angle QIR = \angle BIC = 110°.$$

**Locating $P$ on the circle.** The three rays $IA$, $IB$, $IC$ partition the fu
ll angle around $I$:
$$\angle AIB + \angle BIC + \angle CIA = \left(90°+\tfrac{C}{2}\right) + \left(9
0°+\tfrac{A}{2}\right) + \left(90°+\tfrac{B}{2}\right) = 270° + 90° = 360°.$$

Both $\angle AIB = 90° + C/2 > 90°$ and $\angle AIC = 90° + B/2 > 90°$, so ray $
IA$ (hence point $P$) lies **outside** the $110°$ sector between rays $IB$ and $
IC$. Therefore $P$ lies on the **major** arc $QR$, and the arc $QR$ **not contai
ning** $P$ is the minor arc of measure $110°$.

**Inscribed angle theorem.** The inscribed angle $\angle QPR$ equals half the me
asure of the arc $QR$ that does not contain $P$:
$$\angle QPR = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 110° = 55°.$$

$$\boxed{\angle QPR = 55°}$$
