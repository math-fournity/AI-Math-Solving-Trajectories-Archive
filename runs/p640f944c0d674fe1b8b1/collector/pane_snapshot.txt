## Proof

**Answer: Yes, $K$ must be extremely disconnected.**

We prove this by chaining three standard equivalences.

---

**Step 1. A unital $C^*$-subalgebra of $\mathcal{B}(H)$ that is $\sigma$-strongl
y closed is a von Neumann algebra.**

Let $\pi: C(K)\to\mathcal{B}(H)$ be the given representation. Since $C(K)$ is un
ital, restricting to the essential subspace $\pi(1)H$ we may assume the represen
tation is non-degenerate, so $\pi(1)=I$. Set $A=\pi(C(K))\subseteq\mathcal{B}(H)
$, a unital $C^*$-subalgebra.

By the von Neumann bicommutant theorem together with the equivalence of the stan
dard locally convex topologies on $\mathcal{B}(H)$ (Takesaki, Vol. I, Ch. II): f
or a unital $*$-subalgebra $A\subseteq\mathcal{B}(H)$, the following are equival
ent:
$$A=A'';\quad A\text{ is weakly closed};\quad A\text{ is strongly closed};\quad
A\text{ is }\sigma\text{-weakly closed};\quad A\text{ is }\sigma\text{-strongly
closed}.$$
(These topologies share the same closed unital $*$-subalgebras; the unit ball is
 strongly/$\sigma$-strongly dense in the weak/$\sigma$-weak closure, and Kaplans
ky density transfers closedness.)

Hence $A=\pi(C(K))$ is a von Neumann algebra, i.e. $A=A''$.

---

**Step 2. A von Neumann algebra is monotone complete; in particular $C(K)$ (abst
ractly, via $\pi$) is monotone complete.**

Every von Neumann algebra is monotone complete: any bounded increasing net $\{a_
\lambda\}$ of self-adjoint elements admits a supremum in the algebra (realized a
s the strong limit of the net, or equivalently via the Borel functional calculus
 / the lattice-theoretic completeness of the projection lattice). Since $\pi$ is
 an isometric $*$-isomorphism onto its range, $C(K)$ itself is monotone complete
: every bounded increasing net $\{f_\lambda\}\subset C(K)_{\mathrm{sa}}$ has a l
east upper bound in $C(K)_{\mathrm{sa}}$.

---

**Step 3. $C(K)$ is monotone complete if and only if $K$ is extremely disconnect
ed.**

We prove the forward direction (the one we need). Assume $C(K)$ is monotone comp
lete. Let $U\subseteq K$ be open. Consider the family
$$\mathcal{F}=\{f\in C(K,\mathbb R): 0\le f\le 1,\; f|_{K\setminus U}=0\},$$
directed upward by the pointwise order (for $f,g\in\mathcal{F}$, $\max(f,g)\in\m
athcal{F}$). It is bounded, so by monotone completeness $g=\sup\mathcal{F}$ exis
ts in $C(K)$.

- For $x\in U$: since $K$ is compact Hausdorff (hence normal) and $\{x\}$ is clo
sed with $x\in U$, Urysohn gives $f\in\mathcal{F}$ with $f(x)=1$. Thus $g(x)=1$
on $U$.
- For $x\in K\setminus\overline{U}$: $K\setminus\overline{U}$ is an open neighbo
rhood of $x$ disjoint from $U$, and every $f\in\mathcal{F}$ vanishes on $K\setmi
nus U\supseteq K\setminus\overline{U}$, so $f(x)=0$; hence $g(x)=0$ on $K\setmin
us\overline{U}$.

By continuity of $g$ and density of $U$ in $\overline{U}$, $g=1$ on $\overline{U
}$. Therefore
$$g=\mathbf{1}_{\overline U}\quad\text{(pointwise)},$$
and since $g$ is continuous, $\overline U=g^{-1}(\{1\})$ is closed **and** $g^{-
1}((1/2,1])=\overline U$ is open. Thus $\overline U$ is clopen; in particular $\
overline U$ is open.

Since the closure of every open set is open, $K$ is extremely disconnected.

(The converse — extreme disconnectedness implies monotone completeness — is the
classical Stonean theorem: for an increasing bounded net $\{f_\lambda\}$, the po
intwise supremum is lower semicontinuous, and extreme disconnectedness forces th
e upper/lower semicontinuous envelopes to coincide, yielding a continuous suprem
um. This completes the equivalence but is not needed for our implication.)

---

**Conclusion.**

Combining the three steps:
$$\pi(C(K))\text{ is }\sigma\text{-strongly closed in }\mathcal{B}(H)\;\Longrigh
tarrow\;\pi(C(K))\text{ is a von Neumann algebra}\;\Longrightarrow\;C(K)\text{ i
s monotone complete}\;\Longrightarrow\;K\text{ is extremely disconnected}.$$

Therefore $K$ must be extremely disconnected. $\blacksquare$
