## Proof

The statement is **true**.

**Setup.** Let $A_k > 0$ be positive definite $n\times n$ matrices ($n>1$) with
$\sum_{k=1}^\infty A_k$ convergent. The norm in question is
$$\|A\| = \sup_{\|x\|\le 1}\langle Ax,x\rangle.$$
For a positive definite matrix, by the spectral theorem $A = Q\Lambda Q^\top$ wi
th $\Lambda = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n)$, $\lambda_i > 0$,
so
$$\langle Ax,x\rangle = \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i\, (Q^\top x)_i^2,$$
and the supremum over $\|x\|\le 1$ is attained at the eigenvector for $\lambda_{
\max}$. Hence
$$\|A_k\| = \lambda_{\max}(A_k).$$

**Key observations.**

1. Since each $A_k$ is positive definite, every diagonal entry $(A_k)_{ii}$ is s
trictly positive (as $e_i^\top A_k e_i > 0$).

2. The convergence of $\sum_{k=1}^\infty A_k$ means each entry $(A_k)_{ij}$ form
s a convergent scalar series. In particular, for each fixed $i$, the series $\su
m_{k=1}^\infty (A_k)_{ii}$ converges. Since each term is positive, this is a con
vergent series of non-negative reals, hence
$$\sum_{k=1}^\infty (A_k)_{ii} < +\infty \quad \text{for every } i=1,\dots,n.$$

3. Summing over the diagonal,
$$\sum_{k=1}^\infty \operatorname{tr}(A_k) = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \sum_{i=1}^n (A_k
)_{ii} = \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{k=1}^\infty (A_k)_{ii} < +\infty,$$
where exchanging the order of summation is justified since all terms are non-neg
ative (Tonelli's theorem).

4. For a positive definite matrix, all eigenvalues are positive, so
$$\lambda_{\max}(A_k) \le \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i(A_k) = \operatorname{tr}(A_k).$
$

**Conclusion.** Combining the above,
$$\sum_{k=1}^\infty \|A_k\| = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \lambda_{\max}(A_k) \le \sum_{k=
1}^\infty \operatorname{tr}(A_k) < +\infty.$$

Therefore $\sum_{k=1}^\infty \|A_k\|$ converges. $\blacksquare$
