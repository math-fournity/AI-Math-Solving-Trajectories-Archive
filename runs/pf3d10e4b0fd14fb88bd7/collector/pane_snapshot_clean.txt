**Answer: Yes.** Under the standard hypothesis that $S$ and $T$ are separable me
trizable spaces (in particular Polish), convergence in distribution of $(X_n,Y_n
)$ to $(X,Y)$ on the product space $S\times T$ is characterized by

$$\mathbf{E}\big[f(X_n)\,g(Y_n)\big]\;\longrightarrow\;\mathbf{E}\big[f(X)\,g(Y)
\big]\qquad\forall\,f\in C_b(S),\;\forall\,g\in C_b(T).$$

That is, the "rectangular" test functions $f\otimes g$ are convergence-determini
ng on $S\times T$.

---

### Proof

Let $\mu_n:=\mathrm{Law}(X_n,Y_n)$ and $\mu:=\mathrm{Law}(X,Y)$ on $S\times T$ (
equipped with the product topology, hence metrizable and separable). Define the
algebra

$$\mathcal A \;:=\; \operatorname{span}\{\,f\otimes g : f\in C_b(S),\; g\in C_b(
T)\,\}\;\subset\; C_b(S\times T),$$

where $(f\otimes g)(x,y)=f(x)g(y)$. Note $\mathcal A$ is indeed an algebra: $(f_
1\otimes g_1)(f_2\otimes g_2)=(f_1f_2)\otimes(g_1g_2)\in\mathcal A$.

#### ($\Rightarrow$) Weak convergence implies the rectangular condition.

If $\mu_n\Rightarrow\mu$, then by the defining (Portmanteau) property $\int h\,d
\mu_n\to\int h\,d\mu$ for every $h\in C_b(S\times T)$. Each $f\otimes g$ is boun
ded and continuous on $S\times T$, so the condition holds.

#### ($\Leftarrow$) The rectangular condition implies weak convergence.

Assume $\int f\otimes g\,d\mu_n\to\int f\otimes g\,d\mu$ for all $f\in C_b(S)$,
$g\in C_b(T)$. By linearity, $\int h\,d\mu_n\to\int h\,d\mu$ for every $h\in\mat
hcal A$.

**Step 1 — Marginal convergence.** Taking $g\equiv 1$ gives $\int f\,d\mathrm{La
w}(X_n)\to\int f\,d\mathrm{Law}(X)$ for all $f\in C_b(S)$, i.e. $X_n\Rightarrow
X$. Taking $f\equiv 1$ gives $Y_n\Rightarrow Y$.

**Step 2 — Tightness of $\{\mu_n\}$.** Weak convergence on a separable metrizabl
e space implies tightness (Prohorov), so $\{\mathrm{Law}(X_n)\}$ and $\{\mathrm{
Law}(Y_n)\}$ are tight. For $\varepsilon>0$ choose compact $K\subset S$ with $\m
athrm{Law}(X_n)(K)\ge 1-\varepsilon/2$ for all $n$, and compact $L\subset T$ wit
h $\mathrm{Law}(Y_n)(L)\ge 1-\varepsilon/2$ for all $n$. Then $K\times L$ is com
pact in $S\times T$ and

$$\mu_n(K\times L)\;\ge\;1-\varepsilon\qquad\forall n,$$

so $\{\mu_n\}$ is tight.

**Step 3 — Uniqueness of subsequential limits.** Let $\mu_{n_k}\Rightarrow\nu$ b
e any weakly convergent subsequence (existing by tightness). For all $f\in C_b(S
)$, $g\in C_b(T)$,

$$\int f\otimes g\,d\nu \;=\; \lim_{k\to\infty}\int f\otimes g\,d\mu_{n_k} \;=\;
 \int f\otimes g\,d\mu,$$

hence $\int h\,d\nu=\int h\,d\mu$ for every $h\in\mathcal A$.

*Claim:* $\nu=\mu$.

The algebra $\mathcal A$ contains the constants ($1\otimes 1$) and separates poi
nts of $S\times T$: if $(x,y)\neq(x',y')$, then either $x\neq x'$ or $y\neq y'$;
 in the first case pick $f\in C_b(S)$ with $f(x)\neq f(x')$ (Urysohn on a metriz
able space) so $(f\otimes 1)$ separates the two points, and similarly for the se
cond case. For every compact $C\subset S\times T$, the restriction $\mathcal A|_
C$ is therefore uniformly dense in $C(C)$ by the Stone–Weierstrass theorem. Cons
equently

$$\int h\,d\nu\big|_C \;=\; \int h\,d\mu\big|_C\qquad\forall\,h\in C(C),$$

and the Riesz representation theorem gives $\nu|_C=\mu|_C$ (equality as measures
 on $C$).

Since $S\times T$ is separable metrizable (hence Polish if $S,T$ are), every Bor
el probability on it is tight (Ulam). For any Borel set $B\subset S\times T$ and
 any $\varepsilon>0$, choose compact $C$ with $\nu(C^c)<\varepsilon$ and $\mu(C^
c)<\varepsilon$; then

$$\big|\nu(B)-\mu(B)\big|\;\le\;\big|\nu(B\cap C)-\mu(B\cap C)\big|+\nu(C^c)+\mu
(C^c)\;<\;2\varepsilon.$$

Letting $\varepsilon\to0$ yields $\nu(B)=\mu(B)$ for all Borel $B$, so $\nu=\mu$
.

**Step 4 — Conclusion.** Every subsequential weak limit of the tight sequence $\
{\mu_n\}$ equals $\mu$. A tight sequence with a unique subsequential weak limit
converges weakly to that limit: otherwise there would exist $h\in C_b(S\times T)
$ and a subsequence along which $\int h\,d\mu_{n_k}$ stays bounded away from $\i
nt h\,d\mu$; by tightness a further sub-subsequence converges weakly, necessaril
y to $\mu$, a contradiction. Hence $\mu_n\Rightarrow\mu$, i.e. $(X_n,Y_n)\Righta
rrow(X,Y)$.

---

Combining both directions, the condition

$$\mathbf{E}[f(X_n)g(Y_n)]\to\mathbf{E}[f(X)g(Y)]\quad\forall\,f\in C_b(S),\,g\i
n C_b(T)$$

is equivalent to convergence in distribution of $(X_n,Y_n)$ to $(X,Y)$ on $S\tim
es T$ (for $S,T$ separable metrizable, in particular Polish). $\blacksquare$
