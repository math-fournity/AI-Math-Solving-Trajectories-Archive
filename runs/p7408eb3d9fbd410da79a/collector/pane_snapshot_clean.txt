Let $f = (f_Y, f_S)$ where $f_Y: X \to Y$ and $f_S: X \to S^1$ are the coordinat
e maps (compositions with the projections). It suffices to show that $f_S$ is nu
ll-homotopic, for then a homotopy $H: f_S \simeq \mathrm{const}_{s_0}$ (where $s
_0 \in S^1$ is the basepoint) yields a homotopy
$$F(x,t) = (f_Y(x), H(x,t))$$
from $f = (f_Y, f_S)$ to $(f_Y, \mathrm{const}_{s_0}) = (g, 1)$ with $g = f_Y$.

So we must prove: every map $X \to S^1$ is null-homotopic, i.e. $[X, S^1]$ is a
singleton.

**Key fact.** $S^1$ is an Eilenberg–Mac Lane space $K(\mathbb{Z}, 1)$. For any C
W complex $X$, there is a natural bijection
$$[X, S^1] \;\cong\; H^1(X; \mathbb{Z}).$$
(This is the standard classification of homotopy classes of maps into $K(\mathbb
{Z},1)$; it follows, for instance, from obstruction theory, or from the fact tha
t $S^1$ represents $H^1(-;\mathbb{Z})$ on the homotopy category of CW complexes.
)

**Computing $H^1(X;\mathbb{Z})$.** By the Universal Coefficient Theorem,
$$0 \to \mathrm{Ext}(H_0(X), \mathbb{Z}) \to H^1(X;\mathbb{Z}) \to \mathrm{Hom}(
H_1(X), \mathbb{Z}) \to 0.$$
Since $X$ is connected, $H_0(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, and $\mathrm{Ext}(\mathbb{Z},
 \mathbb{Z}) = 0$. Hence
$$H^1(X;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(H_1(X), \mathbb{Z}).$$
By the Hurewicz theorem (applicable since $X$ is connected), $H_1(X) \cong \pi_1
(X)^{\mathrm{ab}}$, the abelianization of the fundamental group.

**Using finiteness of $\pi_1(X)$.** The abelianization of a finite group is a fi
nite abelian group. For any finite abelian group $A$,
$$\mathrm{Hom}(A, \mathbb{Z}) = 0,$$
since the image of a finite group in the torsion-free group $\mathbb{Z}$ must be
 trivial. Therefore
$$H^1(X;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(\pi_1(X)^{\mathrm{ab}}, \mathbb{Z}) = 0.$
$

**Conclusion.** Since $[X, S^1] \cong H^1(X;\mathbb{Z}) = 0$, every map $X \to S
^1$ is null-homotopic. In particular $f_S \simeq \mathrm{const}_{s_0}$. Let $H:
X \times I \to S^1$ be such a homotopy with $H(x,0) = f_S(x)$ and $H(x,1) = s_0$
. Setting $g = f_Y$ and defining
$$F(x,t) = (g(x), H(x,t)),$$
we obtain a homotopy from $f = (f_Y, f_S)$ to $(g, 1): X \to Y \times S^1$. $\bl
acksquare$
