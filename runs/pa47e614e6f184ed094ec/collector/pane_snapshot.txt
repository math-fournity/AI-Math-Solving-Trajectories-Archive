### Construction of $N$

Let $x \in G$ be nontrivial. Since $\bigcap_{i \geq 1} \gamma_i(G) = \{1\}$, the
re exists an integer $n \geq 1$ such that $x \notin \gamma_n(G)$.

Set $N = \gamma_n(G)$. Then:

- **$N$ is normal in $G$**: each term of the lower central series is a character
istic (hence normal) subgroup.
- **$x \notin N$**: by the choice of $n$.
- **$G/N$ is nilpotent**: the lower central series of $G/\gamma_n(G)$ satisfies
$\gamma_n(G/\gamma_n(G)) = \gamma_n(G)/\gamma_n(G) = \{1\}$, so $G/N$ is nilpote
nt of class at most $n-1$.

It remains to show that $G/N$ is **torsion-free**.

### Key Lemma

**Lemma.** *Let $H$ be a nilpotent group such that every quotient $\gamma_i(H)/\
gamma_{i+1}(H)$ is torsion-free (as an abelian group). Then $H$ is torsion-free.
*

**Proof of Lemma.** We argue by induction on the nilpotency class $c$ of $H$.

- **Base case ($c = 0$):** $H = \{1\}$, which is torsion-free.

- **Inductive step ($c \geq 1$):** Let $h \in H$ with $h^m = 1$ for some $m \geq
 1$. Consider the image $\bar{h}$ of $h$ in the abelianization $H^{\mathrm{ab}}
= H/\gamma_2(H) = \gamma_1(H)/\gamma_2(H)$. Since $\bar{h}^m = \overline{h^m} =
\bar{1}$ and $H^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is torsion-free by hypothesis, we conclude $\bar{
h} = \bar{1}$, i.e., $h \in \gamma_2(H)$.

  Now $h \in \gamma_2(H)$ and $h^m = 1$. The subgroup $\gamma_2(H)$ is nilpotent
 of class at most $c - 1$, and its lower central series quotients are $\gamma_i(
H)/\gamma_{i+1}(H)$ for $i \geq 2$, which are torsion-free by hypothesis. By the
 inductive hypothesis, $\gamma_2(H)$ is torsion-free, so $h = 1$. $\square$

### Completing the proof

The lower central series of $H = G/\gamma_n(G)$ is:
$$G/\gamma_n(G) \supseteq \gamma_2(G)/\gamma_n(G) \supseteq \cdots \supseteq \ga
mma_{n-1}(G)/\gamma_n(G) \supseteq \{1\}.$$

The successive quotients are:
$$\frac{\gamma_i(G)/\gamma_n(G)}{\gamma_{i+1}(G)/\gamma_n(G)} \cong \gamma_i(G)/
\gamma_{i+1}(G), \quad i = 1, \ldots, n-1,$$

which are torsion-free by hypothesis (condition 2 of the definition). By the Lem
ma, $G/\gamma_n(G)$ is torsion-free.

The subgroup $N = \gamma_n(G)$ satisfies all required properties:
- $N \trianglelefteq G$,
- $G/N$ is nilpotent (class $\leq n-1$),
- $G/N$ is torsion-free (by the Lemma),
- $x \notin N$ (by construction).

Therefore, **yes**, for every nontrivial element $x$ of a (finitely generated) r
