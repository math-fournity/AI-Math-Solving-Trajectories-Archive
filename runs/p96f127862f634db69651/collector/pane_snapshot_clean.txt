## Proof

**Yes**, we can conclude that $(z - T)$ is a bounded operator.

**Key observation.** The hypothesis states that the inequality
$$\|(z-T)x\| \ge |\operatorname{Im}(z)|\,\|x\|$$
holds for *any* $x \in \mathcal{H}$ (and all $z \in \mathbb{C}$). For the expres
sion $(z-T)x$ to be meaningful for **every** $x \in \mathcal{H}$, the operator $
T$ must be defined on all of $\mathcal{H}$; that is, $\mathcal{D}(T) = \mathcal{
H}$.

**Step 1: $T$ is symmetric.** Since $T$ is self-adjoint, it is in particular sym
metric:
$$\langle Tx, y\rangle = \langle x, Ty\rangle \quad \text{for all } x, y \in \ma
thcal{H}.$$

**Step 2: Apply the Hellinger–Toeplitz theorem.** The Hellinger–Toeplitz theorem
 states that a symmetric operator defined on the entire Hilbert space $\mathcal{
H}$ is necessarily bounded. The proof proceeds via the Closed Graph Theorem:

Suppose $x_n \to x$ in $\mathcal{H}$ and $Tx_n \to y$ in $\mathcal{H}$. For ever
y $z \in \mathcal{H}$, using symmetry of $T$ (valid since $\mathcal{D}(T) = \mat
hcal{H}$):
$$\langle y, z\rangle = \lim_{n\to\infty} \langle Tx_n, z\rangle = \lim_{n\to\in
fty} \langle x_n, Tz\rangle = \langle x, Tz\rangle = \langle Tx, z\rangle.$$
Since this holds for all $z \in \mathcal{H}$, we conclude $y = Tx$. Hence the gr
aph of $T$ is closed. By the Closed Graph Theorem, $T$ is bounded.

**Step 3: Conclude $(z-T)$ is bounded.** Since $T$ is bounded and the identity o
perator $I$ is bounded, the operator
$$z - T = zI - T$$
is a linear combination of bounded operators, hence bounded.

**Remark.** The given inequality is in fact the standard resolvent estimate sati
sfied by *every* self-adjoint operator (bounded or unbounded). However, the cruc
ial point is that the hypothesis quantifies over all $x \in \mathcal{H}$, which
forces $\mathcal{D}(T) = \mathcal{H}$. It is this domain condition — not the ine
quality itself — that, together with self-adjointness, yields boundedness via He
llinger–Toeplitz. An unbounded self-adjoint operator satisfies the same inequali
ty but only on its proper dense domain $\mathcal{D}(T) \subsetneq \mathcal{H}$.
