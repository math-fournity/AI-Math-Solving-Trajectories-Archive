## (a) General solution

We solve $u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0$ on $0<x<a,\;0<y<b$ with
$$u(0,y)=u(a,y)=0,\quad u(x,0)=0,\quad u(x,b)=g(x).$$

**Separation of variables.** Set $u=X(x)Y(y)$. Then $X''/X=-Y''/Y=\lambda$.

- $X''+\lambda X=0$, $X(0)=X(a)=0$ gives the eigenvalues $\lambda_n=(n\pi/a)^2$
and eigenfunctions $X_n(x)=\sin(n\pi x/a)$, $n=1,2,\dots$
- $Y''-\lambda_n Y=0$ with $Y(0)=0$ gives $Y_n(y)=\sinh(n\pi y/a)$.

Hence
$$u(x,y)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n\,\sinh\!\Big(\tfrac{n\pi y}{a}\Big)\sin\!\Big(\
tfrac{n\pi x}{a}\Big).$$

**Determining $B_n$.** Imposing $u(x,b)=g(x)$:
$$g(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n\sinh\!\Big(\tfrac{n\pi b}{a}\Big)\sin\!\Big(\tfra
c{n\pi x}{a}\Big).$$
By orthogonality of $\{\sin(n\pi x/a)\}$ on $[0,a]$,
$$\boxed{\,B_n=\frac{2}{a\,\sinh(n\pi b/a)}\int_{0}^{a} g(x)\sin\!\Big(\tfrac{n\
pi x}{a}\Big)\,dx\,}$$
and therefore
$$\boxed{\,u(x,y)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} B_n\,\sinh\!\Big(\tfrac{n\pi y}{a}\Big)\si
n\!\Big(\tfrac{n\pi x}{a}\Big)\,}.$$

## (b) The triangular data $g(x)$

With
$$g(x)=\begin{cases}x,&0\le x\le a/2,\\ a-x,&a/2\le x\le a,\end{cases}$$
note $g$ is symmetric about $x=a/2$: $g(a-x)=g(x)$.

Compute
$$I_n=\int_{0}^{a}g(x)\sin\!\Big(\tfrac{n\pi x}{a}\Big)dx
=\int_{0}^{a/2}x\sin\!\Big(\tfrac{n\pi x}{a}\Big)dx+\int_{a/2}^{a}(a-x)\sin\!\Bi
g(\tfrac{n\pi x}{a}\Big)dx.$$

In the second integral substitute $t=a-x$ and use $\sin(n\pi(a-t)/a)=(-1)^{n+1}\
sin(n\pi t/a)$:
$$\int_{a/2}^{a}(a-x)\sin\!\Big(\tfrac{n\pi x}{a}\Big)dx=(-1)^{n+1}\int_{0}^{a/2
}t\sin\!\Big(\tfrac{n\pi t}{a}\Big)dt.$$

So
$$I_n=\bigl[1+(-1)^{n+1}\bigr]\int_{0}^{a/2}x\sin\!\Big(\tfrac{n\pi x}{a}\Big)dx
.$$

- **$n$ even:** $1+(-1)^{n+1}=0\Rightarrow I_n=0$.
- **$n$ odd:** $1+(-1)^{n+1}=2$.

For $n$ odd, with $k=n\pi/a$,
$$\int_{0}^{a/2}x\sin(kx)\,dx=\Big[-\frac{x\cos kx}{k}+\frac{\sin kx}{k^{2}}\Big
]_{0}^{a/2}.$$
At $x=a/2$: $\cos(n\pi/2)=0$ (since $n$ odd) and $\sin(n\pi/2)=(-1)^{(n-1)/2}$,
so the value is $(-1)^{(n-1)/2}/k^{2}=(-1)^{(n-1)/2}a^{2}/(n\pi)^{2}$.

Thus for $n$ odd,
$$I_n=2\cdot(-1)^{(n-1)/2}\frac{a^{2}}{(n\pi)^{2}},$$
and
$$B_n=\frac{2}{a\sinh(n\pi b/a)}\cdot\frac{2(-1)^{(n-1)/2}a^{2}}{(n\pi)^{2}}=\fr
ac{4a\,(-1)^{(n-1)/2}}{(n\pi)^{2}\sinh(n\pi b/a)}.$$

Writing $n=2m-1$ ($m=1,2,\dots$), $(-1)^{(n-1)/2}=(-1)^{m-1}$, the solution is

$$\boxed{\,u(x,y)=\frac{4a}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{(2m-1)^
{2}}\,\frac{\sinh\!\big(\frac{(2m-1)\pi y}{a}\big)}{\sinh\!\big(\frac{(2m-1)\pi
b}{a}\big)}\,\sin\!\Big(\tfrac{(2m-1)\pi x}{a}\Big)\,}.$$

Only odd harmonics appear, consistent with the symmetry of $g$ about $x=a/2$.

## (c) Plots for $a=3$, $b=1$

With $a=3$, $b=1$:
$$u(x,y)=\frac{12}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{(2m-1)^{2}}\,\fr
ac{\sinh\!\big(\frac{(2m-1)\pi y}{3}\big)}{\sinh\!\big(\frac{(2m-1)\pi}{3}\big)}
\,\sin\!\Big(\tfrac{(2m-1)\pi x}{3}\Big).$$

The series converges rapidly (coefficients decay like $1/(2m-1)^2$ and the hyper
bolic ratio is bounded by $1$ for $0\le y\le b$); truncating at $m\approx 20$ gi
ves machine-precision accuracy.

**$u$ vs $x$ for several $y$** (fix $y$, vary $x\in[0,3]$):
- $y=0$: $u\equiv 0$ (bottom edge).
- $y=0.25$: a low-amplitude flattened triangle, peak $\approx 0.18$ near $x=1.5$
.
- $y=0.5$: peak $\approx 0.33$ near $x=1.5$.
- $y=0.75$: peak $\approx 0.43$ near $x=1.5$.
- $y=1$: the triangular profile $g(x)$, peak $=1.5$ at $x=1.5$.

As $y$ increases from $0$ to $1$ the profile rises monotonically from $0$ to the
 triangle $g(x)$, with the sharp corner at $x=1.5$ becoming progressively rounde
d for small $y$ (Gibbs-type smoothing from the truncated sine series) and sharpe
ning as $y\to 1$.

**$u$ vs $y$ for several $x$** (fix $x$, vary $y\in[0,1]$):
- $x=1.5$ (centerline): the largest curve, rising from $0$ to $1.5$, roughly $\s
inh$-shaped (steeper near $y=1$).
- $x=0.75$ and $x=2.25$ (symmetric pair): identical curves rising from $0$ to $0
.75$.
- $x=0.3$ and $x=2.7$ (symmetric pair): identical, lower curves rising to $0.3$.
- $x=0$ and $x=3$: $u\equiv 0$ (side edges).

Each $u$-vs-$y$ curve is monotone increasing and convex (since $\sinh$ is convex
 and the sine coefficients are positive near the center), reflecting that the bo
undary data $g\ge 0$ drives the solution upward toward $y=1$.

## (d) 3-D surface and contour plot

**3-D surface $u(x,y)$ over $[0,3]\times[0,1]$:** The surface is a flat plane al
ong the three edges $x=0$, $x=3$, $y=0$ (where $u=0$) and rises to a ridge along
 $y=1$ that traces the triangular profile $g(x)$ — peak height $1.5$ at $(x,y)=(
1.5,1)$. The surface is symmetric about the plane $x=1.5$. Moving from $y=0$ upw
ard, the triangular ridge grows smoothly out of the flat bottom; the ridge is ro
unded for small $y$ and becomes a sharp peaked ridge at $y=1$.

**Contour plot (level curves $u=\text{const}$ in the $x$-$y$ plane):** Because $
u\ge 0$ and the maximum is $1.5$, level curves for values $c\in(0,1.5)$ are clos
ed-ish arcs that
- are symmetric about $x=1.5$,
- touch the top edge $y=1$ at the two points where $g(x)=c$ (one on each side of
 $x=1.5$),
- bow downward (toward smaller $y$) as $c$ decreases,
- shrink toward the peak point $(1.5,1)$ as $c\to 1.5$,
- expand to fill most of the rectangle as $c\to 0^{+}$, hugging the three zero-b
oundary edges.

The level curves thus form a nested family of approximately lens-shaped arcs, al
l symmetric about $x=1.5$, accumulating at the single maximum point $(1.5,1)$ an
d opening out toward the boundary edges where $u=0$.
