# Relationship Between the Covariant Derivative in $E$ and on $S$

## Setup

Let $S$ be an $m$-dimensional submanifold of Euclidean space $E$, with $\nabla =
 \nabla^S$ the induced (Levi-Civita) connection on $S$. Let $X$ be a vector fiel
d on $S$ (extended, for the purpose of differentiation in $E$, to a neighborhood
 of $S$ in $E$) and $p = p(t)$ a curve in $S$.

At each point $p(t) \in S$, the ambient tangent space $T_{p(t)}E$ splits orthogo
nally as

$$T_{p(t)}E = T_{p(t)}S \oplus \big(T_{p(t)}S\big)^{\perp},$$

where $T_{p(t)}S$ is the tangent space to the submanifold and $\big(T_{p(t)}S\bi
g)^{\perp}$ is the normal space. Consequently every vector in $T_{p(t)}E$ — and
in particular the ambient covariant derivative $\dfrac{DX}{dt}$ — decomposes uni
quely into a **tangential** component and a **normal** component.

## The Gauss Formula

The fundamental relationship is the **Gauss formula**:

$$\boxed{\;\frac{DX}{dt} = \frac{\nabla X}{dt} + \alpha\!\left(X, \frac{dp}{dt}\
right)\;}$$

where:

- $\dfrac{DX}{dt}$ is the covariant derivative of $X$ computed in the ambient Eu
clidean space $E$ (i.e., componentwise differentiation, since $E$ is flat);
- $\dfrac{\nabla X}{dt} = \nabla_{\dot p} X$ is the **tangential component** — t
he covariant derivative of $X$ *on the submanifold* $S$ with respect to the indu
ced connection;
- $\alpha\!\left(X, \dot p\right)$ is the **normal component**, called the **sec
ond fundamental form** (or shape form) of $S$ in $E$, evaluated on the pair $(X,
 \dot p)$.

## Derivation

Since $E$ is Euclidean, we may identify vector fields with their component funct
ions in a fixed orthonormal frame, and $\dfrac{DX}{dt}$ is simply the componentw
ise derivative of $X(p(t))$ along the curve. The result is a vector in $T_{p(t)}
E$. Projecting orthogonally onto $T_{p(t)}S$ gives a vector that depends only on
 the values of $X$ along $S$ and on the intrinsic geometry of $S$; this projecti
on is precisely $\dfrac{\nabla X}{dt}$. The orthogonal complement — the part of
$\dfrac{DX}{dt}$ perpendicular to $S$ — measures how $S$ curves inside $E$ and i
s exactly the second fundamental form term.

Concretely, if $\Pi^{\top}$ and $\Pi^{\perp}$ denote the orthogonal projections
onto $T_{p(t)}S$ and $\big(T_{p(t)}S\big)^{\perp}$ respectively, then

$$\frac{\nabla X}{dt} = \Pi^{\top}\!\left(\frac{DX}{dt}\right), \qquad \alpha\!\
left(X, \dot p\right) = \Pi^{\perp}\!\left(\frac{DX}{dt}\right).$$

## Nature of the Additional Term

The additional term $\alpha\!\left(X, \dot p\right)$ has the following propertie
s:

1. **It is normal to $S$.** By construction $\alpha\!\left(X, \dot p\right) \in
\big(T_{p(t)}S\big)^{\perp}$, so it is perpendicular to every tangent vector of
$S$ at $p(t)$.

2. **It is bilinear and symmetric.** The second fundamental form $\alpha : \math
frak{X}(S) \times \mathfrak{X}(S) \to \mathfrak{X}(S)^{\perp}$ is $\mathcal{C}^{
\infty}(S)$-bilinear, and — because the ambient connection is torsion-free (Eucl
idean) and $\nabla^S$ is the induced Levi-Civita connection — it is **symmetric*
*:
   $$\alpha(X, Y) = \alpha(Y, X) \quad \text{for all vector fields } X, Y \text{
 on } S.$$
   This symmetry is the content of the **Gauss equation** relating the torsion o
f the ambient connection to that of the induced connection.

3. **It measures extrinsic curvature.** The term $\alpha\!\left(X, \dot p\right)
$ captures how $S$ is "bent" inside $E$. It vanishes identically if and only if
$S$ is (locally) totally geodesic — in particular, if $S$ is an open subset of a
n affine subspace of $E$. For a general submanifold it is nonzero and encodes th
e deviation of $S$ from being flat in the ambient space.

4. **It depends on the embedding, not just the intrinsic metric.** Unlike $\nabl
a^S$, which is determined entirely by the Riemannian metric induced on $S$, the
second fundamental form depends on *how* $S$ sits inside $E$. Two isometric embe
ddings of the same abstract Riemannian manifold can have different second fundam
ental forms.

## Summary

The covariant derivative of $X$ along $p(t)$ in the ambient Euclidean space $E$
equals the covariant derivative of $X$ along $p(t)$ *on the submanifold* $S$ (th
e tangential part) **plus** the second fundamental form $\alpha\!\left(X, \dot p
\right)$ (the normal part). The intrinsic derivative $\dfrac{\nabla X}{dt}$ is t
he orthogonal projection of the ambient derivative onto the tangent space of $S$
, while the additional term $\alpha\!\left(X, \dot p\right)$ is the orthogonal p
rojection onto the normal bundle — a symmetric, bilinear, normal-vector-valued f
orm that measures the extrinsic curvature of $S$ within $E$.
