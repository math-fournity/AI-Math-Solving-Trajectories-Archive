# Derivation of the Quadratic Casimir Operator $C_2^{(D)}$

## Setup

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a semisimple Lie algebra over $\mathbb{C}$ with Cartan sub
algebra $\mathfrak{h}$, rank $r$. Fix a choice of simple roots $\{\alpha_1,\dots
,\alpha_r\}$, which determines the set of positive roots $\Phi^+$. Let $(\cdot,\
cdot)$ be the invariant symmetric bilinear form on $\mathfrak{h}^*$ (induced by
the Killing form, normalized so that $(\alpha,\alpha)\neq 0$ for each root).

The **Cartan–Weyl basis** consists of:
- Cartan elements $\{H_i\}_{i=1}^r$ for $\mathfrak{h}$, with dual $\{H^i\}$ defi
ned by $(H_i, H^j)=\delta_i^{\,j}$,
- Root vectors $\{E_\alpha\}_{\alpha\in\Phi}$, normalized so that
$$[E_\alpha, E_{-\alpha}] = H_\alpha, \qquad \alpha(H_\alpha)=(\alpha,\alpha),$$
where $H_\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}$ is the element corresponding to $\alpha$ under t
he form: $(H_\alpha, H)=\alpha(H)$ for all $H\in\mathfrak{h}$.

## Step 1 — Definition of the quadratic Casimir

The quadratic Casimir element (in the universal enveloping algebra $U(\mathfrak{
g})$) is
$$C \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{r} H_i H^i \;+\; \sum_{\alpha\in\Phi} E_\alpha E_{-\alpha}
.$$
It is central in $U(\mathfrak{g})$: $[C, X]=0$ for all $X\in\mathfrak{g}$. Hence
 by Schur's lemma, on any irreducible representation $D$ with highest weight $\l
ambda$, $C$ acts as a scalar — this scalar is $C_2^{(D)}$.

## Step 2 — Reorganize using the commutator

Pair the root terms by $\pm\alpha$ and use $[E_\alpha,E_{-\alpha}]=H_\alpha$:
$$E_\alpha E_{-\alpha} + E_{-\alpha}E_\alpha \;=\; 2\,E_{-\alpha}E_\alpha + H_\a
lpha.$$
Thus
$$C \;=\; \sum_i H_i H^i \;+\; \sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^+}\bigl(2\,E_{-\alpha}E_\alph
a + H_\alpha\bigr).$$

## Step 3 — Act on the highest weight vector

Let $|\lambda\rangle$ be a highest weight vector of $D$, so $H|\lambda\rangle=\l
ambda(H)|\lambda\rangle$ for $H\in\mathfrak{h}$ and, crucially,
$$E_\alpha|\lambda\rangle = 0 \quad\text{for all } \alpha\in\Phi^+.$$
Therefore every $E_{-\alpha}E_\alpha|\lambda\rangle = 0$, and the raising/loweri
ng cross terms drop out:
$$C\,|\lambda\rangle \;=\; \Bigl(\sum_i H_i H^i + \sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^+} H_\alph
a\Bigr)|\lambda\rangle.$$

## Step 4 — Evaluate the Cartan part

For the first sum,
$$\sum_i H_i H^i\,|\lambda\rangle \;=\; \sum_i \lambda(H_i)\,\lambda(H^i)\,|\lam
bda\rangle \;=\; (\lambda,\lambda)\,|\lambda\rangle,$$
since $\lambda = \sum_i \lambda(H^i)\,H_i$ dually and $(\lambda,\lambda)=\sum_i\
lambda(H_i)\lambda(H^i)$.

## Step 5 — Evaluate the root-sum part; introduce the Weyl vector

For each positive root,
$$H_\alpha\,|\lambda\rangle \;=\; \lambda(H_\alpha)\,|\lambda\rangle \;=\; (\lam
bda,\alpha)\,|\lambda\rangle,$$
using $(H_\alpha,H)=\alpha(H)$ and the dual pairing. Summing,
$$\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^+} H_\alpha\,|\lambda\rangle \;=\; \Bigl(\lambda,\;\sum_{\
alpha\in\Phi^+}\alpha\Bigr)|\lambda\rangle.$$

Define the **Weyl vector** (half-sum of positive roots)
$$\delta \;:=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^+}\alpha,$$
so that $\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^+}\alpha = 2\delta$. Then
$$\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^+} H_\alpha\,|\lambda\rangle \;=\; (\lambda,\,2\delta)\,|\
lambda\rangle.$$

## Step 6 — Combine

$$C\,|\lambda\rangle \;=\; \bigl[(\lambda,\lambda) + (\lambda,2\delta)\bigr]\,|\
lambda\rangle \;=\; \bigl(\lambda,\;\lambda+2\delta\bigr)\,|\lambda\rangle.$$

Since $C$ is central, this eigenvalue is constant on all of $D$. Therefore the q
uadratic Casimir eigenvalue is

$$\boxed{\,C_2^{(D)} \;=\; \bigl(\lambda,\;\lambda+2\delta\bigr)\,}.$$

Equivalently, completing the square,

$$C_2^{(D)} \;=\; \bigl(\lambda+\delta,\;\lambda+\delta\bigr) - (\delta,\delta),
$$

which is the familiar "shift by the Weyl vector" form: the Casimir is the square
d length of the shifted weight $\lambda+\delta$ minus the constant $(\delta,\del
ta)$.
