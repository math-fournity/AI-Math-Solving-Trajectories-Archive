## Proof

We compute

$$E\!\left[e^{-\alpha t} S(t)\right] = e^{-\alpha t}\, s\, e^{(\alpha-\lambda\si
gma)t}\, E\!\left[(\sigma+1)^{N(t)}\right] = s\, e^{-\lambda\sigma t}\, E\!\left
[(\sigma+1)^{N(t)}\right].$$

Since $N(t)\sim\text{Poisson}(\lambda t)$, its probability generating function i
s

$$E\!\left[z^{N(t)}\right] = \exp\!\big(\lambda t(z-1)\big),\qquad z\ge 0.$$

Setting $z=\sigma+1$ (valid since $\sigma>-1$ so $\sigma+1>0$):

$$E\!\left[(\sigma+1)^{N(t)}\right] = \exp\!\big(\lambda t\cdot\sigma\big) = e^{
\lambda\sigma t}.$$

Therefore

$$E\!\left[e^{-\alpha t} S(t)\right] = s\, e^{-\lambda\sigma t}\, e^{\lambda\sig
ma t} = s.$$

The discounted asset price is a martingale, and the expectation equals the initi
al price $s$, independent of $t$.
