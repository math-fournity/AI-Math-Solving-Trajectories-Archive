# Second Homotopy Group of $\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus (L \cup \{p\})$

## Problem

Determine $\pi_2(\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus (L \cup \{p\}))$, where $L$ is a line in $\mathbb{R}^3$ and $p$ is a point not on $L$.

## Setup

Without loss of generality, let $L$ be the $z$-axis and $p = (1, 0, 0)$.

## Step 1: One-Point Compactification

Work in $S^3 = \mathbb{R}^3 \cup \{\infty\}$. The line $L$ together with the point at infinity becomes an unknotted circle:
$$C = L \cup \{\infty\} \subset S^3.$$
The point $p$ does not lie on $C$ (since $p \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and $p \notin L$). Therefore:
$$\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus (L \cup \{p\}) = S^3 \setminus (C \cup \{p\}) = (S^3 \setminus C) \setminus \{p\}.$$

Since $C$ is an unknotted circle in $S^3$, its complement is an open solid torus:
$$S^3 \setminus C \cong S^1 \times \operatorname{int}(D^2) \cong S^1 \times \mathbb{R}^2.$$

Under this homeomorphism, $p$ maps to some point $(s_0, v_0) \in S^1 \times \mathbb{R}^2$. Setting $Y = (S^1 \times \mathbb{R}^2) \setminus \{(s_0, v_0)\}$, we have:
$$\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus (L \cup \{p\}) \cong Y.$$

## Step 2: Fundamental Group via Van Kampen

Cover $Y$ by two open sets:
- $U = S^1 \times (\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{v_0\})$, which deformation retracts to $S^1 \times S^1 = T^2$, so $\pi_1(U) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$.
- $V = (S^1 \setminus \{s_0\}) \times \mathbb{R}^2 \cong \mathbb{R}^3$, so $\pi_1(V) = 0$.

Their union is $Y$: any point $(s, v) \neq (s_0, v_0)$ either has $s \neq s_0$ (so it lies in $V$) or $v \neq v_0$ (so it lies in $U$).

The intersection is:
$$U \cap V = (S^1 \setminus \{s_0\}) \times (\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{v_0\}) \cong \mathbb{R} \times (\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{v_0\}) \simeq S^1,$$
so $\pi_1(U \cap V) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by a loop around $v_0$ in the $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{v_0\}$ factor.

The inclusion $U \cap V \hookrightarrow U$ sends this generator to $(0, 1) \in \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z} = \pi_1(U)$ (the second factor, corresponding to the loop around $v_0$). The inclusion $U \cap V \hookrightarrow V$ sends it to $0$.

By the Seifert–Van Kampen theorem:
$$\pi_1(Y) \cong \pi_1(U) *_{\pi_1(U \cap V)} \pi_1(V) = \mathbb{Z}^2 / \langle\!\langle (0,1) \rangle\!\rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}.$$

The surviving generator is the $S^1$ factor, confirming that the map $\pi_1(Y) \to \pi_1(S^1 \times \mathbb{R}^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ induced by inclusion is an isomorphism.

## Step 3: Universal Cover

The universal cover of $S^1 \times \mathbb{R}^2$ is $\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}^2 = \mathbb{R}^3$, with covering map:
$$\pi: \mathbb{R}^3 \to S^1 \times \mathbb{R}^2, \quad (t, u, v) \mapsto (e^{2\pi i t}, u, v).$$

Since $\pi_1(Y) \to \pi_1(S^1 \times \mathbb{R}^2)$ is an isomorphism, the universal cover of $Y$ is the universal cover of $S^1 \times \mathbb{R}^2$ with the preimage of the removed point deleted:
$$\widetilde{Y} = \mathbb{R}^3 \setminus \pi^{-1}(s_0, v_0) = \mathbb{R}^3 \setminus \{(n + t_0,\, u_0,\, v_0) : n \in \mathbb{Z}\},$$
where $e^{2\pi i t_0} = s_0$. After a translation, this is:
$$\widetilde{Y} = \mathbb{R}^3 \setminus \{(n, 0, 0) : n \in \mathbb{Z}\}.$$

This is $\mathbb{R}^3$ with a countably infinite discrete set of points removed from a line.

## Step 4: Simple Connectivity of $\widetilde{Y}$

$\widetilde{Y}$ is simply connected. Any loop $\gamma: S^1 \to \widetilde{Y}$ can be perturbed (by a small homotopy) to avoid the removed points, since the points are $0$-dimensional and the loop is $1$-dimensional in a $3$-manifold — there is enough ambient dimension to push the loop off the points. Once the loop avoids all removed points, it lies in $\mathbb{R}^3$ minus a closed discrete set, and can be contracted to a point (again perturbing to avoid the points during the contraction).

## Step 5: $\pi_2$ via Hurewicz and Alexander Duality

Since $\widetilde{Y}$ is simply connected, the Hurewicz theorem gives:
$$\pi_2(\widetilde{Y}) \cong H_2(\widetilde{Y}).$$

Since covering maps induce isomorphisms on $\pi_n$ for $n \geq 2$:
$$\pi_2(Y) \cong \pi_2(\widetilde{Y}) \cong H_2(\widetilde{Y}).$$

To compute $H_2(\widetilde{Y})$, embed in $S^3$. We have $\widetilde{Y} = S^3 \setminus A$ where:
$$A = \{(n, 0, 0) : n \in \mathbb{Z}\} \cup \{\infty\} \subset S^3.$$

**Description of $A$:** As $n \to \pm\infty$, the points $(n, 0, 0)$ converge to $\infty$ in $S^3$. Thus $A$ is the one-point compactification of the countable discrete space $\mathbb{Z}$, i.e., a convergent sequence (homeomorphic to $\{0\} \cup \{1/k : k \geq 1\}$). In particular, $A$ is compact.

**Connected components of $A$:** Each point $(n, 0, 0)$ is isolated in $A$ (hence its own connected component), and $\{\infty\}$ is a connected component (adding any isolated point to it yields a disconnected set). So $A$ has countably many connected components.

**Čech cohomology of $A$:** Since $A$ is compact, we apply Alexander duality with Čech cohomology. A locally constant function $f: A \to \mathbb{Z}$ must satisfy $f(\infty) = f(n, 0, 0)$ for all $|n|$ sufficiently large (by local constancy at $\infty$). Thus:
$$\check{H}^0(A) \cong \mathbb{Z} \oplus \bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z},$$
where the first $\mathbb{Z}$ records the eventual value $f(\infty)$, and the direct sum records the finitely many deviations $f(n,0,0) - f(\infty)$. The reduced cohomology is:
$$\check{\widetilde{H}}^0(A) \cong \bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}.$$
For $i > 0$, $\check{H}^i(A) = 0$ since $A$ is $0$-dimensional.

**Alexander duality** (for compact $A \subset S^3$):
$$\widetilde{H}_i(S^3 \setminus A) \cong \check{\widetilde{H}}^{2-i}(A).$$

For $i = 2$:
$$H_2(\widetilde{Y}) = \widetilde{H}_2(S^3 \setminus A) \cong \check{\widetilde{H}}^0(A) \cong \bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}.$$

## Step 6: Module Structure (Remark)

As a $\mathbb{Z}[\pi_1]$-module with $\pi_1(Y) \cong \mathbb{Z} = \langle t \rangle$, the group $\pi_2(Y)$ is a free $\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}]$-module of rank $1$:
$$\pi_2(Y) \cong \mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}] \cong \mathbb{Z}[t, t^{-1}].$$
The generator is the class of a small $2$-sphere encircling the removed point $p$, and the $\mathbb{Z}$-action (deck transformation of the universal cover) shifts this sphere to encircle the next copy of $p$ in the universal cover. As an abelian group (forgetting the module structure), this is $\bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}$.

## Answer

$$\boxed{\pi_2\!\left(\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus (L \cup \{p\})\right) \cong \bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}}$$

the free abelian group of countably infinite rank. Equivalently, as a $\mathbb{Z}[\pi_1]$-module with $\pi_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}$, it is $\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}] \cong \mathbb{Z}[t, t^{-1}]$ (free of rank $1$ over the group ring).
