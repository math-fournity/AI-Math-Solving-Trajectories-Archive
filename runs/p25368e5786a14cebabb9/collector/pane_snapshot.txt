## Proof

**Answer leak check:** The problem statement contains only the problem, no solut
ion or hints. Proceeding.

**Claim:** The limit equals $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

**Step 1: Substitution.** Set $u = nt$, so $t = u/n$, $dt = du/n$, and $t \in [0
,1]$ becomes $u \in [0,n]$. Then

$$
\int_0^1 \frac{\sin nt}{t}\,f(t)\,dt
= \int_0^n \frac{\sin u}{u/n}\,f\!\left(\frac{u}{n}\right)\frac{du}{n}
= \int_0^n \frac{\sin u}{u}\,f\!\left(\frac{u}{n}\right)du.
$$

**Step 2: Split the integral.** Fix $A > 0$ (to be chosen large). Write

$$
\int_0^n \frac{\sin u}{u}\,f\!\left(\frac{u}{n}\right)du
= \int_0^A \frac{\sin u}{u}\,f\!\left(\frac{u}{n}\right)du
+ \int_A^n \frac{\sin u}{u}\,f\!\left(\frac{u}{n}\right)du.
$$

**Step 3: The first piece.** On $[0,A]$, $f$ is smooth hence bounded, and $f(u/n
) \to f(0) = 1$ uniformly on $[0,A]$ since $u/n \le A/n \to 0$. Therefore, by un
iform convergence (the integrand is dominated by $\|f\|_\infty \cdot \frac{|\sin
 u|}{u}$, integrable on $[0,A]$),

$$
\int_0^A \frac{\sin u}{u}\,f\!\left(\frac{u}{n}\right)du \;\longrightarrow\; f(0
)\int_0^A \frac{\sin u}{u}\,du = \int_0^A \frac{\sin u}{u}\,du.
$$

**Step 4: The tail is uniformly small.** Since $f$ is smooth on $[0,1]$, it is b
ounded: $|f(t)| \le M$. Hence for all $n > A$,

$$
\left|\int_A^n \frac{\sin u}{u}\,f\!\left(\frac{u}{n}\right)du\right|
\le M \left|\int_A^n \frac{\sin u}{u}\,du\right|.
$$

The improper integral $\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin u}{u}\,du$ converges (by the Dir
ichlet test, since $\frac{1}{u}\downarrow 0$ and $\sin u$ has bounded primitives
). Therefore the tail $\int_A^\infty \frac{\sin u}{u}\,du \to 0$ as $A\to\infty$
, uniformly in $n$:

$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|\int_A^n \frac{\sin u}{u}\,f\!\left(\frac{u}{n}\right)
du\right|
\le M\left|\int_A^\infty \frac{\sin u}{u}\,du\right| \xrightarrow{A\to\infty} 0.
$$

**Step 5: Combine.** For any $A > 0$,

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^n \frac{\sin u}{u}\,f\!\left(\frac{u}{n}\right)du
= \int_0^A \frac{\sin u}{u}\,du + R(A),
$$

where $|R(A)| \le M\,|\int_A^\infty \frac{\sin u}{u}du| \to 0$ as $A\to\infty$.
Letting $A\to\infty$,

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1 \frac{\sin nt}{t}\,f(t)\,dt
= \int_0^\infty \frac{\sin u}{u}\,du.
$$

**Step 6: The Dirichlet integral.** It is a classical result that

$$
\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin u}{u}\,du = \frac{\pi}{2}.
$$

(For completeness: $\int_0^\infty e^{-su}\sin u\,du = \frac{1}{1+s^2}$, so $\int
_0^\infty \frac{\sin u}{u}\,du = \int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty e^{-su}\sin u\,ds\,d
u = \int_0^\infty \frac{ds}{1+s^2} = \arctan(s)\big|_0^\infty = \frac{\pi}{2}$,
justified by Fubini/Tonelli on the regularized integral followed by the monotone
 limit $s\to 0^+$.)

**Conclusion.** Since $f(0)=1$,

$$
\boxed{\lim_{n \to \infty} \int_0^1 \frac{\sin nt}{t}\,f(t)\,dt = \frac{\pi}{2}}
.
$$
