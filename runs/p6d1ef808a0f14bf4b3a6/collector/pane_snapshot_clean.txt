Looking at this problem, I need to find a lower bound on the number of "fair" re
vision programs (pairs of permutations where $x = y$).

## Proof

**Setup.** For each problem $p$, let $\alpha(p)$ denote the day Jean-Baptiste so
lves $p$ and $\beta(p)$ the day Marie-Odile solves $p$. Then:
- $x = \#\{p : \alpha(p) < \beta(p)\}$
- $y = \#\{p : \beta(p) < \alpha(p)\}$

A program is fair iff $x = y$.

**Construction.** Fix any permutation $a = (a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_{100})$. We cons
truct $2^{50}$ permutations $b$ such that $(a, b)$ is fair.

Partition the 100 days into 50 consecutive pairs: $\{1,2\}, \{3,4\}, \ldots, \{9
9,100\}$.

For each pair $\{2k{-}1,\, 2k\}$, independently choose one of two options:
- **No swap:** $b_{2k-1} = a_{2k-1}$, $b_{2k} = a_{2k}$.
- **Swap:** $b_{2k-1} = a_{2k}$, $b_{2k} = a_{2k-1}$.

Since $a$ is a permutation and we only reorder values within pairs, $b$ is also
a permutation of $\{1, \ldots, 100\}$.

**Verification of fairness.** Consider any pair $\{2k{-}1,\, 2k\}$:

- **No swap:** Both $a_{2k-1}$ and $a_{2k}$ are solved on the same day by both p
eople, so $\alpha = \beta$ for each. Contribution to $x - y$: **0**.

- **Swap:** Problem $a_{2k-1}$ is solved by JB on day $2k{-}1$ and by MO on day
$2k$, so $\alpha(a_{2k-1}) = 2k{-}1 < 2k = \beta(a_{2k-1})$: contributes **+1 to
 $x$**. Problem $a_{2k}$ is solved by JB on day $2k$ and by MO on day $2k{-}1$,
so $\beta(a_{2k}) = 2k{-}1 < 2k = \alpha(a_{2k})$: contributes **+1 to $y$**. Ne
t contribution to $x - y$: **0**.

In both cases, each pair contributes **equally** to $x$ and $y$. Since this hold
s for all 50 pairs independently, we conclude $x = y$ for every constructed prog
ram. Hence every such $(a, b)$ is fair.

**Counting.** There are $2^{50}$ independent binary choices (swap/no-swap per pa
ir), yielding $2^{50}$ distinct permutations $b$ for each fixed $a$. Since there
 are $100!$ choices for $a$, and different choices of $a$ or swap-patterns produ
ce distinct programs, we obtain:

$$N \;\ge\; 100! \times 2^{50}.$$

**Conclusion.** The lower bound is $C = 2^{50}$.
