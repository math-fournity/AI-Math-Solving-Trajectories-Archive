## Proof

Let $r^2 = x^2 + y^2$, so $r \to 0^+$ as $(x,y) \to (0,0)$. The expression is

$$f(x,y) = \frac{1}{r^2}\, e^{-\frac{r^2}{x^4+y^4}}.$$

**Key bound.** Since $x^4 + y^4 \leq (x^2 + y^2)^2 = r^4$ (every term in the exp
ansion of $(x^2+y^2)^2 = x^4 + 2x^2y^2 + y^4$ is non-negative), we have

$$\frac{r^2}{x^4 + y^4} \;\geq\; \frac{r^2}{r^4} \;=\; \frac{1}{r^2}.$$

Therefore, since the exponential is increasing,

$$0 \;\leq\; e^{-\frac{r^2}{x^4+y^4}} \;\leq\; e^{-1/r^2},$$

and consequently

$$0 \;\leq\; f(x,y) \;=\; \frac{1}{r^2}\,e^{-\frac{r^2}{x^4+y^4}} \;\leq\; \frac
{1}{r^2}\,e^{-1/r^2}.$$

**Limit of the upper bound.** Set $t = 1/r^2 \to +\infty$ as $r \to 0^+$. Then

$$\frac{1}{r^2}\,e^{-1/r^2} = t\,e^{-t} \;\xrightarrow{t\to+\infty}\; 0,$$

since exponentials dominate polynomials (e.g., $t\,e^{-t} \to 0$ by a single app
lication of L'Hôpital, or by $t \leq e^{t/2}$ for $t$ large, giving $t\,e^{-t} \
leq e^{-t/2} \to 0$).

**Conclusion.** The function $f(x,y)$ is non-negative and squeezed between $0$ a
nd a quantity tending to $0$, independently of the direction of approach. By the
 squeeze theorem,

$$\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)} \frac{1}{x^2+y^2}\, e^{-\frac{x^2+y^2}{x^4+y^4}} \;=\; 0.
$$
