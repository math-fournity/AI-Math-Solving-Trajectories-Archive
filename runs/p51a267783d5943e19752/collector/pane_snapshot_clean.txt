## Proof

**Setup.** The Poincaré disk model is the open unit disk $D = \{(x,y): x^2+y^2<1
\}$ equipped with the metric
$$ds^2 = \frac{4\,dx^2 + 4\,dy^2}{(1-x^2-y^2)^2}.$$
This is the standard Poincaré disk metric, and a direct computation of the Gauss
ian curvature gives
$$K \equiv -1$$
everywhere on $D$ (constant negative curvature).

**Suppose, for contradiction**, that there exists a simple closed geodesic $\gam
ma$ in $D$. Since $\gamma$ is simple and closed, by the Jordan curve theorem it
bounds an interior region $R \subset D$ that is homeomorphic to a closed disk, s
o $\chi(R) = 1$.

**Apply the Gauss–Bonnet theorem** to the region $R$ with boundary $\partial R =
 \gamma$:
$$\iint_R K\,dA + \int_\gamma k_g\,ds + \sum_i \alpha_i = 2\pi\,\chi(R),$$
where $k_g$ is the geodesic curvature and $\alpha_i$ are the exterior angles at
any corner points.

Because $\gamma$ is a geodesic, its geodesic curvature vanishes identically:
$$k_g \equiv 0.$$
Because $\gamma$ is a smooth simple closed curve (geodesics are smooth), there a
re no corner points, so $\sum_i \alpha_i = 0$.

Substituting $K = -1$, $\chi(R)=1$, $k_g = 0$, and $\sum \alpha_i = 0$:
$$\iint_R (-1)\,dA = 2\pi,$$
$$-\operatorname{Area}(R) = 2\pi,$$
$$\operatorname{Area}(R) = -2\pi.$$

**Contradiction.** The area of any region must be non-negative (in fact strictly
 positive for a region with interior), yet we obtain $\operatorname{Area}(R) = -
2\pi < 0$. This is impossible.

**Conclusion.** The contradiction arose from assuming the existence of a simple
closed geodesic. Therefore, **no simple closed geodesic can exist on the Poincar
é disk model of the hyperbolic plane**.

Intuitively, the constant negative curvature $K=-1$ forces every closed curve bo
unding a disk to have total turning that exceeds what a geodesic (with $k_g\equi
v 0$) can provide; the "deficit" $-\iint K\,dA = \operatorname{Area}(R) > 0$ mus
t be made up by positive geodesic curvature along the boundary, so the boundary
cannot be a geodesic. This is the hallmark of negative curvature: geodesics dive
rge, and no geodesic can close up simply.
