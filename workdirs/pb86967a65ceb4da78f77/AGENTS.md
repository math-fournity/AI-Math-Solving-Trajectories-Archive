# Problem

In a bustling coastal town, a lighthouse keeper named Elias logs a sequence of daily tide levels over a period of several days. Each level he records is a whole number of meters, ranging from a minimum of 1 meter to a maximum of 10 meters. He writes these values in a vertical logbook, starting from the first day at the top down to the final day at the bottom.

An inspector named Marcus visits the lighthouse and decides to create a corresponding "frequency log." Next to every single entry in Elias's log, Marcus writes down how many times that specific tide level appears throughout Elias’s entire record. This results in a second column of numbers of the exact same length.

When the inspector finished, he noticed a remarkable symmetry: if he reads his frequency log from the bottom entry up to the top entry, the sequence of numbers is identical to Elias’s original tide log read from the top down to the bottom.

Based on these recording rules and the observed symmetry, what is the maximum possible number of days (the greatest possible length) that could be in Elias’s log?
