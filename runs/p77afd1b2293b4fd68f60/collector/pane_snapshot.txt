## Proof

**Claim:** The set of irrational numbers $\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}$ cannot
be expressed as a countable union of closed sets (i.e., it is not an $F_\sigma$
set).

**Proof by contradiction.** Suppose, for contradiction, that
$$
\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q} = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} F_n,
$$
where each $F_n$ is closed.

Taking complements, we obtain
$$
\mathbb{Q} = \mathbb{R}\setminus\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} F_n = \bigcap_{n=1}^{\inf
ty} (\mathbb{R}\setminus F_n) = \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} U_n,
$$
where each $U_n = \mathbb{R}\setminus F_n$ is open. Thus $\mathbb{Q}$ would be a
 $G_\delta$ set.

Since $\mathbb{Q}$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}$, and $\mathbb{Q}\subseteq U_n$ for e
very $n$, each $U_n$ is a dense open subset of $\mathbb{R}$.

Now enumerate the rationals: $\mathbb{Q} = \{q_1, q_2, q_3, \dots\}$. Each singl
eton $\{q_k\}$ is closed and has empty interior (it is nowhere dense). Also, eac
h $F_n = \mathbb{R}\setminus U_n$ is closed; since $U_n$ is dense, $F_n$ has emp
ty interior, so each $F_n$ is nowhere dense.

Combining the two decompositions:
$$
\mathbb{R} = \mathbb{Q} \cup (\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}) = \left(\bigcup_{k=
1}^{\infty} \{q_k\}\right) \cup \left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} F_n\right).
$$

This expresses $\mathbb{R}$ as a countable union of closed, nowhere dense sets (
the singletons $\{q_k\}$ and the sets $F_n$). In other words, $\mathbb{R}$ would
 be **meager** (of first category) in itself.

**This contradicts the Baire Category Theorem**, which states that a complete me
tric space (in particular, $\mathbb{R}$ with the usual metric) is **not** meager
 in itself — it is of second category. Equivalently, a countable union of closed
 nowhere dense subsets of a complete metric space cannot equal the whole space.

The contradiction shows that our assumption was false. Therefore $\mathbb{Q}$ is
 not a $G_\delta$ set, and consequently $\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}$ is not a
n $F_\sigma$ set.

**Conclusion:** The set of irrational numbers **cannot** be expressed as the uni
on of a countable collection of closed sets. $\blacksquare$
