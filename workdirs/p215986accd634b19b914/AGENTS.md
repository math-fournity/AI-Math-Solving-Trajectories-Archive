# Problem

In a futuristic energy grid, three central power hubs—Zion ($z$), Yield ($y$), and Xebec ($x$)—operate using complex-valued voltage phases. The local stabilization protocol dictates that the square of a hub's voltage phase, when added to five times the voltage phase of its predecessor in the grid’s triangular feedback loop, must equal ten times its own voltage phase.

Specifically, the grid engineers have established the following three operational constraints:
1. The square of Zion's voltage plus five times Xebec's voltage equals ten times Zion's voltage.
2. The square of Yield's voltage plus five times Zion's voltage equals ten times Yield's voltage.
3. The square of Xebec's voltage plus five times Yield's voltage equals ten times Xebec's voltage.

Each hub's voltage is represented by a complex number. Given that these feedback equations must all be satisfied simultaneously, determine the sum of all possible values for Zion’s voltage phase ($z$).
