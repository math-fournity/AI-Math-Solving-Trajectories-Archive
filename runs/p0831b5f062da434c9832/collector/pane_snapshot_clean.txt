**Proof.**

Let $F$ be a free group with basis $X$, and let $N=\langle g^{2}:g\in F\rangle$
be the subgroup generated by all squares.

**Step 1: $F/N$ has exponent $2$, hence is abelian.**

For any $g\in F$, $g^{2}\in N$, so $(gN)^{2}=N$ in $F/N$; every element is its o
wn inverse. A group in which every element has order dividing $2$ is abelian: fo
r $a,b$ we have $(ab)^{2}=e$ and $a^{2}=b^{2}=e$, so $abab=e$ gives $ab=b^{-1}a^
{-1}=ba$. Thus $F/N$ is an elementary abelian $2$-group, i.e. a $\mathbb{Z}_{2}$
-vector space with scalar multiplication $\bar{0}\cdot(gN)=N$, $\bar{1}\cdot(gN)
=gN$.

**Step 2: The commutator subgroup $[F,F]$ is contained in $N$.**

For $a,b\in F$, since $a^{2},b^{2}\in N$ we have $a^{-1}\equiv a$ and $b^{-1}\eq
uiv b\pmod{N}$. Hence
$$[a,b]=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab\equiv a\,b\,a\,b=(ab)^{2}\equiv 1\pmod{N}.$$
Since $(ab)^{2}\in N$, this shows $[a,b]\in N$. Therefore $[F,F]\subseteq N$.

**Step 3: Identify $F/N$ via the abelianization.**

Since $[F,F]\subseteq N$, the quotient $F/N$ is a quotient of the abelianization
 $F/[F,F]\cong\mathbb{Z}^{(X)}$. Let $\pi:F\to\mathbb{Z}^{(X)}$ be the abelianiz
ation map (sending each $x\in X$ to the standard basis vector $e_{x}$). The imag
e $\pi(N)$ is generated by $\{\pi(g^{2}):g\in F\}=\{2\pi(g):g\in F\}=2\,\mathbb{
Z}^{(X)}$ (taking $g=x\in X$ gives $2e_{x}$, generating $2\,\mathbb{Z}^{(X)}$).
By the third isomorphism theorem,
$$F/N\;\cong\;\big(\mathbb{Z}^{(X)}\big)\big/\big(2\,\mathbb{Z}^{(X)}\big)\;\con
g\;(\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{(X)}.$$

**Step 4: $X/N$ is a basis.**

Under the isomorphism above, the coset $xN$ corresponds to $e_{x}\bmod 2\,\mathb
b{Z}^{(X)}$, i.e. to the standard basis vector of $(\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{(X)}$. Ther
efore $X/N=\{xN:x\in X\}$ maps bijectively onto the standard basis of $(\mathbb{
Z}_{2})^{(X)}$.

- **Spanning:** Any $gN\in F/N$ is a product of elements $xN$ and their inverses
; but $(xN)^{-1}=xN$ (exponent $2$), so $gN$ is a $\mathbb{Z}_{2}$-linear combin
ation of the $xN$. Hence $X/N$ spans.
- **Linear independence:** If $\prod_{x\in S}xN=N$ (a finite product, equal to a
 $\mathbb{Z}_{2}$-sum being zero), then $\sum_{x\in S}e_{x}\in 2\,\mathbb{Z}^{(X
)}$, which forces each coefficient $1$ to be even — impossible unless $S=\varnot
hing$. So $X/N$ is linearly independent.

**Conclusion.** $X/N$ is a basis of the $\mathbb{Z}_{2}$-vector space $F/N$. Equ
ivalently, $F/N\cong(\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{(X)}$ with $X/N$ as the standard basis.
