## Solution

**Claim.** For $n \ge 1$, the only integer $k$ for which there exists a smooth m
ap $f:M\to T^n$ of degree $k$ is $\boxed{k=0}$.

---

### Setup

Both $M$ and $T^n=(S^1)^n$ are compact, oriented, smooth $n$-manifolds, so the d
egree of a smooth map $f:M\to T^n$ is the unique integer $\deg(f)=k$ satisfying
$$\int_M f^*\omega = k\int_{T^n}\omega \qquad\text{for every } \omega\in\Omega^n
(T^n).$$
Equivalently, $f^*:H^n_{dR}(T^n)\to H^n_{dR}(M)$ is multiplication by $k$ once b
oth groups are identified with $\mathbb{R}$ via the orientations.

### Cohomology ring of the torus

The de Rham cohomology of the torus is the exterior algebra on its first cohomol
ogy:
$$H^*_{dR}(T^n)\cong \Lambda(x_1,\dots,x_n),\qquad \deg x_i=1,\quad H^1_{dR}(T^n
)\cong\mathbb{R}^n,$$
and a generator of $H^n_{dR}(T^n)\cong\mathbb{R}$ is the top product $x_1\wedge\
cdots\wedge x_n$.

### The induced map on $H^1$ vanishes

The hypothesis $H^1_{dR}(M)=0$ forces
$$f^*:H^1_{dR}(T^n)\longrightarrow H^1_{dR}(M)=0$$
to be the zero map: $f^*(x_i)=0$ for every $i$.

### The induced map on $H^n$ vanishes

The pullback $f^*$ is a homomorphism of graded rings (it respects the wedge/cup
product). Since the entire cohomology ring of $T^n$ is generated as an exterior
algebra by the degree-one classes $x_i$, and $f^*$ annihilates every generator,
$f^*$ annihilates every positive-degree class. In particular
$$f^*(x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge x_n)=f^*(x_1)\wedge\cdots\wedge f^*(x_n)=0\wedge\cdo
ts\wedge 0=0.$$
Thus $f^*=0$ on $H^n_{dR}(T^n)$, which means $\deg(f)=0$.

### Existence of a degree-$0$ map

The constant map $c:M\to T^n$ is smooth. It factors through a point, so $c^*$ ki
lls every positive-degree cohomology class; in particular $c^*=0$ on $H^n$, givi
ng $\deg(c)=0$. Hence $k=0$ is attained.

### Conclusion

Every smooth map $f:M\to T^n$ has degree $0$, and a degree-$0$ map exists. There
fore the set of realizable degrees is
$$\{\,k\in\mathbb{Z} : \exists\, f:M\to T^n \text{ smooth with } \deg(f)=k\,\}=\
{0\}.$$

The key point is that $H^1_{dR}(M)=0$ kills the pullback on $H^1$, and because t
he cohomology ring of $T^n$ is generated in degree $1$, this forces the pullback
 on $H^n$ — hence the degree — to vanish.
