## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $\overline{E} = \mathbb{R}^2$ necessarily.

**Setup.** Let $F \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ be a finite set such that $E \cup F$ is
closed. Since $F$ is finite, $F$ is closed, so $\overline{F} = F$. Using that th
e closure of a finite union equals the union of closures:

$$\overline{E} \cup F = \overline{E} \cup \overline{F} = \overline{E \cup F} = E
 \cup F,$$

where the last equality uses that $E \cup F$ is closed. Since $E \subseteq \over
line{E}$, subtracting $E$ from both sides gives

$$\overline{E} \setminus E \;\subseteq\; F.$$

Set $G := \overline{E} \setminus E$. Then $G$ is finite and $\overline{E} = E \c
up G$.

**Decomposition of the plane.** Let $U := \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \overline{E}$,
which is open. Then

$$\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus G \;=\; E \,\cup\, U,$$

and $E$, $U$ are **disjoint** open sets (disjoint because $E \subseteq \overline
{E}$ while $U = \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \overline{E}$).

**Connectedness argument.** The key topological fact: *$\mathbb{R}^2$ minus fini
tely many points is path-connected.* Indeed, given any two points $a, b \in \mat
hbb{R}^2 \setminus G$, choose an intermediate point $c$ on a circle centered at
$a$ (or any large circle). There are uncountably many choices for $c$, but for e
ach point $p \in G$, the set of positions of $c$ making segment $\overline{ac}$
or $\overline{cb}$ pass through $p$ is at most two (determined by the line throu
gh $a, p$ or $b, p$). Since $G$ is finite, only finitely many choices of $c$ are
 excluded. Hence a valid $c$ exists, giving a polygonal path $a \to c \to b$ in
$\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus G$.

Since $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus G$ is path-connected, it is connected. But we have
 written it as

$$\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus G = E \cup U,$$

a union of two **disjoint open** subsets. A connected space cannot be decomposed
 into two disjoint non-empty open subsets. Since $E$ is non-empty (by hypothesis
), we must have $U = \emptyset$.

**Conclusion.** $U = \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \overline{E} = \emptyset$, so

$$\overline{E} = \mathbb{R}^2. \qquad \blacksquare$$
