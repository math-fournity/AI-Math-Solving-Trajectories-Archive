## Proof

We consider the Burgers equation $u_t + u u_x = \epsilon u_{xx}$ on $\mathbb{R}\
times(0,\infty)$ with smooth, bounded initial data $u(x,0)=u_0(x)$ satisfying $u
_0(x)\to u_\pm$ as $x\to\pm\infty$ for constants $u_\pm\in\mathbb{R}$. We assume
 $u_0 - u_\pm$ decays sufficiently fast (e.g., exponentially) at $\pm\infty$.

### Case $\epsilon > 0$ (viscous Burgers): the limit exists and equals 0

**Step 1: Cole–Hopf transformation.** Define $\Phi(x)=\int_0^x u_0(s)\,ds$ and $
\phi_0(x)=e^{-\Phi(x)/(2\epsilon)}>0$. As $x\to+\infty$, $\Phi(x)=u_+ x + c_+ +
o(1)$ (the $o(1)$ from the integrable tail of $u_0-u_+$), so
$$\phi_0(x)\sim C_+\,e^{-\alpha_+ x},\qquad \alpha_+=\frac{u_+}{2\epsilon},\quad
 C_+=e^{-c_+/(2\epsilon)}>0.$$
Similarly $\phi_0(x)\sim C_-\,e^{-\alpha_- x}$ as $x\to-\infty$ with $\alpha_-=u
_-/(2\epsilon)$.

Let $\phi$ solve the heat equation $\phi_t=\epsilon\phi_{xx}$ with initial data
$\phi_0$:
$$\phi(x,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G(x-y,t)\,\phi_0(y)\,dy,\qquad G(z,t)=\frac{e
^{-z^2/(4\epsilon t)}}{\sqrt{4\pi\epsilon t}}.$$
The integral converges for all $t>0$ since the Gaussian's super-exponential deca
y dominates any exponential growth of $\phi_0$. Since $\phi_0>0$ and the heat eq
uation preserves positivity, $\phi(x,t)>0$ everywhere. The Cole–Hopf formula
$$u(x,t)=-2\epsilon\,\frac{\phi_x(x,t)}{\phi(x,t)}$$
then gives a smooth solution of the Burgers equation for $t>0$.

**Step 2: Far-field asymptotics of $\phi$.** We use the standard identity (by co
mpleting the square):
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G(x-y,t)\,e^{-\alpha y}\,dy = e^{-\alpha x+\alpha^2\eps
ilon t}.$$
Write $\phi_0(y)=C_+\,e^{-\alpha_+ y}+r_+(y)$ where $r_+(y)\to 0$ as $y\to+\inft
y$ and $r_+$ is bounded. Then
$$\phi(x,t)=C_+\,e^{-\alpha_+ x+\alpha_+^2\epsilon t}+\int G(x-y,t)\,r_+(y)\,dy.
$$
For the remainder: given $\delta>0$, choose $R$ with $|r_+(y)|<\delta$ for $y>R$
. The contribution from $y>R$ is $O(\delta)$; the contribution from $y\le R$ is
$O(e^{-(x-R)^2/(4\epsilon t)})=o(e^{-\alpha_+ x})$. Hence
$$\phi(x,t)=C_+\,e^{-\alpha_+ x+\alpha_+^2\epsilon t}\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)\quad\tex
t{as }x\to+\infty.$$
Differentiating (the same argument applies to derivatives of the heat kernel):
$$\phi_x(x,t)=-\alpha_+\,\phi(x,t)\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr),\qquad \phi_{xx}(x,t)=\alph
a_+^2\,\phi(x,t)\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr).$$

**Step 3: Cancellation in $u_x$.** Since
$$u_x=-2\epsilon\,\frac{\phi_{xx}\,\phi-\phi_x^2}{\phi^2},$$
substituting the asymptotics:
$$\frac{\phi_{xx}\,\phi-\phi_x^2}{\phi^2}=\frac{\alpha_+^2\,\phi^2(1+o(1))-\alph
a_+^2\,\phi^2(1+o(1))}{\phi^2}=o(1).$$
The leading terms **cancel exactly** — this reflects the fact that a pure expone
ntial $\phi=Ae^{-\alpha x+\alpha^2\epsilon t}$ yields $u\equiv 2\epsilon\alpha=u
_+$ (constant), so $u_x\equiv 0$. The far-field approaches this constant-coeffic
ient solution, and corrections are $o(1)$. Therefore
$$\boxed{\lim_{x\to+\infty}u_x(x,t)=0.}$$

**Step 4: Limit as $x\to-\infty$.** The identical argument with $\alpha_-$ in pl
ace of $\alpha_+$ gives
$$\phi(x,t)\sim C_-\,e^{-\alpha_- x+\alpha_-^2\epsilon t},\quad \phi_x\sim -\alp
ha_-\,\phi,\quad \phi_{xx}\sim\alpha_-^2\,\phi\quad\text{as }x\to-\infty,$$
and the same cancellation yields
$$\boxed{\lim_{x\to-\infty}u_x(x,t)=0.}$$

**Remark on the case $u_\pm=0$.** Then $\alpha_\pm=0$ and $\phi_0\to C_\pm>0$ (c
onstants). The heat equation preserves constants in the far field, so $\phi_x,\p
hi_{xx}\to 0$ while $\phi\to C_\pm>0$, giving $u_x\to -2\epsilon(0-0)/C_\pm^2=0$
 directly.

### Case $\epsilon=0$ (inviscid Burgers): the limit exists and equals 0 (before
shocks)

The method of characteristics gives $u(x,t)=u_0(\xi)$ with $x=\xi+u_0(\xi)t$, an
d
$$u_x(x,t)=\frac{u_0'(\xi)}{1+u_0'(\xi)\,t}.$$
As $x\to+\infty$, we have $\xi\to+\infty$ (since $x\sim\xi+u_+ t$), so $u_0'(\xi
)\to 0$, giving $u_x\to 0$. Similarly $u_x\to 0$ as $x\to-\infty$. After shock f
ormation, $u_x$ ceases to exist classically at shocks, but shocks are localized;
 in the far field the entropy solution still satisfies $u_x\to 0$ (in the distri
butional sense, $u_x$ is a measure supported on shocks, which vanishes at infini
ty).

### Case $\epsilon<0$: ill-posed

The Cole–Hopf transform yields the **backward** heat equation $\phi_t=-|\epsilon
|\phi_{xx}$, which is ill-posed (Hadamard unstable). The initial value problem i
s not well-posed for generic smooth data, so the question of limits of $u_x$ is
not meaningful.

### Conclusion

For the Burgers equation $u_t+uu_x=\epsilon u_{xx}$:

| Regime | $\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}u_x(x,t)$ | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| $\epsilon>0$ | **Exists, equals $0$** | Cole–Hopf → heat equation; exponential
 far-field of $\phi$ preserved, leading to exact cancellation in $u_x=-2\epsilon
(\phi_{xx}\phi-\phi_x^2)/\phi^2$ |
| $\epsilon=0$ | **Exists, equals $0$** (pre-shock; far-field post-shock) | Char
acteristics: $u_x=u_0'(\xi)/(1+u_0'(\xi)t)\to 0$ since $\xi\to\pm\infty$ |
| $\epsilon<0$ | **Not meaningful** | Backward heat equation is ill-posed |

The key mechanism for the viscous case: the Cole–Hopf transformation converts th
e nonlinear Burgers equation into the linear heat equation. The far-field of $\p
hi$ is governed by a pure exponential $e^{-\alpha x+\alpha^2\epsilon t}$, which
corresponds under Cole–Hopf to a **constant** $u\equiv u_\pm$ (hence $u_x\equiv
0$). Sub-exponential corrections from the initial data produce only $o(1)$ pertu
rbations, so $u_x\to 0$ at $\pm\infty$.
