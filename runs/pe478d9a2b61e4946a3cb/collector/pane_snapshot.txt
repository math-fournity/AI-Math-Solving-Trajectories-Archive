## Solution

We solve the homogeneous convolution Volterra equation

$$f(x) = \int_0^x t\,e^t\, f(x-t)\,dt, \qquad x \ge 0.$$

### Step 1. Identify the structure

Set $g(t) = t\,e^t$. The equation is the **convolution equation**

$$f = g * f, \qquad (g*f)(x) = \int_0^x g(t)\,f(x-t)\,dt.$$

### Step 2. Take the Laplace transform

Let $F(s) = \mathcal{L}\{f\}(s)$ and $G(s) = \mathcal{L}\{g\}(s)$. The convoluti
on theorem gives

$$F(s) = G(s)\,F(s).$$

The Laplace transform of $g(t)=t\,e^t$ is

$$G(s) = \frac{1}{(s-1)^2}, \qquad \Re s > 1.$$

Hence

$$F(s)\!\left(1 - \frac{1}{(s-1)^2}\right) = 0 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; F(s)\,\fr
ac{s(s-2)}{(s-1)^2} = 0.$$

### Step 3. Uniqueness

For any $f$ of exponential order (the natural class for which the Laplace transf
orm exists), $F(s)$ is **analytic** in a right half-plane $\Re s > \sigma_0$. Th
e factor

$$\varphi(s) := 1 - \frac{1}{(s-1)^2} = \frac{s(s-2)}{(s-1)^2}$$

is analytic on $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{1\}$ and is **not identically zero** (its z
eros are isolated, at $s=0$ and $s=2$). The product of two analytic functions on
 a connected domain that vanishes identically forces at least one factor to vani
sh identically. Since $\varphi\not\equiv 0$, we must have

$$F(s) \equiv 0.$$

By injectivity of the Laplace transform on functions of exponential order,

$$\boxed{\,f(x) \equiv 0\,}.$$

### Step 4. Verification

Substituting $f\equiv 0$:

$$\int_0^x t\,e^t\cdot 0\,dt = 0 = f(x). \;\checkmark$$

### Step 5. Elementary confirmation (no transform machinery)

- $f(0) = \int_0^0(\cdots)\,dt = 0$.
- Differentiating via the Leibniz rule,
  $$f'(x) = \bigl[t\,e^t f(x-t)\bigr]_{t=x} + \int_0^x t\,e^t\,f'(x-t)\,dt = x\,
e^x f(0) + \int_0^x t\,e^t\,f'(x-t)\,dt.$$
  Since $f(0)=0$, the boundary term vanishes, so $f'$ satisfies the **same** equ
ation, and $f'(0)=0$.
- By induction, $f^{(n)}(0)=0$ for every $n$. For analytic (or even $C^\infty$ o
f exponential order) $f$, this forces $f\equiv 0$, consistent with the Laplace-t
ransform conclusion.

### Conclusion

The unique solution (in the class of functions of exponential order, which inclu
des all continuous solutions of Volterra convolution equations with exponentiall
y bounded kernels) is the **trivial solution**

$$f(x) = 0, \qquad \text{for all } x \ge 0.$$

The kernel $g(t)=te^t$ has Laplace transform $G(s)=1/(s-1)^2$, which never equal
s $1$ on the domain of analyticity of $F$ in a way that would support a nontrivi
al exponential eigenfunction (the candidate poles $s=0,2$ fail the boundary cond
ition $f(0)=0$), so no nonzero solution exists.
