Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether finiteness of the stopping

**Answer: No, the proof does not require τ to be finite.**

**Proof.**

Recall that $X_\tau$ is $\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurable if and only if for every $
B \in \mathcal{E}$ and every $t \in I$:
$$\{X_\tau \in B\} \cap \{\tau \le t\} \in \mathcal{F}_t.$$

The key observation is that this condition only ever involves the behavior of $X
_\tau$ on the set $\{\tau \le t\}$, where $t \in I$ is a finite index. On $\{\ta
u \le t\}$, the stopping time $\tau$ takes values in $\{s \in I : s \le t\}$, wh
ich consists entirely of **finite** values. Therefore $X_\tau(\omega) = X_{\tau(
\omega)}(\omega)$ is well-defined on $\{\tau \le t\}$ regardless of whether $\ta
u$ can take the value $+\infty$ elsewhere.

**The standard argument.** Fix $B \in \mathcal{E}$ and $t \in I$. Since $I$ is a
t most countable, the set $\{s \in I : s \le t\}$ is at most countable, and we d
ecompose:

$$\{X_\tau \in B\} \cap \{\tau \le t\} = \bigcup_{\substack{s \in I \\ s \le t}}
 \Big(\{X_s \in B\} \cap \{\tau = s\}\Big).$$

This equality holds because on $\{\tau \le t\}$, $\tau$ must equal some $s \in I
$ with $s \le t$, and there $X_\tau = X_s$.

Now we verify each piece is in $\mathcal{F}_t$:

1. **$\{X_s \in B\} \in \mathcal{F}_s$:** Since $X$ is $\mathbb{F}$-adapted, $X_
s$ is $\mathcal{F}_s$-measurable.

2. **$\{\tau = s\} \in \mathcal{F}_s$:** Since $\tau$ is a stopping time, $\{\ta
u \le u\} \in \mathcal{F}_u$ for all $u \in I$. Then
$$\{\tau = s\} = \{\tau \le s\} \setminus \bigcup_{\substack{u \in I \\ u < s}}
\{\tau \le u\}.$$
Each $\{\tau \le u\} \in \mathcal{F}_u \subseteq \mathcal{F}_s$ (for $u \le s$),
 and the union is countable (since $I$ is at most countable), so $\{\tau = s\} \
in \mathcal{F}_s$.

3. Therefore $\{X_s \in B\} \cap \{\tau = s\} \in \mathcal{F}_s \subseteq \mathc
al{F}_t$ (since $s \le t$).

4. The countable union over $\{s \in I : s \le t\}$ remains in $\mathcal{F}_t$.

**Why finiteness of $\tau$ is not needed.** The entire argument operates exclusi
vely on $\{\tau \le t\}$ for finite $t \in I$. The set $\{\tau = +\infty\}$ (if
it is nonempty) is never intersected with any $\{\tau \le t\}$ for $t \in I$, so
 the value of $X_\tau$ on $\{\tau = +\infty\}$—or even whether $X_\tau$ is defin
ed there—is completely irrelevant to the $\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurability condit
ion. The definition of $\mathcal{F}_\tau$ itself only tests intersections $A \ca
p \{\tau \le t\}$ for $t \in I$, which automatically excludes $\{\tau = +\infty\
}$.

In other words, the $\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurability of $X_\tau$ is a statement
about the restriction of $X_\tau$ to $\bigcup_{t \in I}\{\tau \le t\} = \{\tau <
 +\infty\}$, and on this set $\tau$ is always finite, so $X_\tau$ is always well
-defined. The proof never invokes, and never needs, the assumption that $\tau$ i
s finite almost surely or everywhere. $\blacksquare$
