## Proof

We consider the nonlinear oscillator
$$u'' + u = |u|^{p-1}u, \qquad p>1,$$
with a solution $u(t)$ blowing up at $T>0$, i.e. $u(t)\to +\infty$ as $t\nearrow
 T$. The conserved energy is
$$E=\tfrac12(u')^2+\tfrac12 u^2-\tfrac{1}{p+1}|u|^{p+1}=\text{const}.$$

We prove
$$\lim_{t\nearrow T}\frac{u(t)}{C\,(T-t)^{-\alpha}}=1,\qquad C=\Bigl(\frac{2(p+1
)}{(p-1)^2}\Bigr)^{\!1/(p-1)},\quad \alpha=\frac{2}{p-1}.$$

---

### Step 1. Eventual monotonicity and sign of $u'$

Since $u(t)\to+\infty$, for all $t$ sufficiently close to $T$ we have $u(t)>0$ a
nd arbitrarily large. From energy conservation,
$$(u')^2 = 2E - u^2 + \frac{2}{p+1}u^{p+1}. \tag{1}$$
Because $p+1>2$, the term $\frac{2}{p+1}u^{p+1}$ dominates $2E-u^2$ as $u\to\inf
ty$, so $(u')^2\to\infty$. Since $u\to+\infty$, $u$ is eventually strictly incre
asing; hence there exists $t_0<T$ such that
$$u'(t)>0 \quad\text{and}\quad u(t)>0 \qquad\text{for all }t\in[t_0,T). \tag{2}$
$

---

### Step 2. Leading-order relation between $u'$ and $u$

Rewrite (1) as
$$(u')^2 = \frac{2}{p+1}\,u^{p+1}\Bigl(1+\underbrace{\frac{(p+1)(2E-u^2)}{2\,u^{
p+1}}}_{\displaystyle =:\,\eta(t)}\Bigr). \tag{3}$$
Since $p+1>2$, we have $\eta(t)\to 0$ as $t\nearrow T$ (i.e. as $u\to\infty$). T
aking the positive square root (using (2)):
$$u' = \sqrt{\frac{2}{p+1}}\;u^{(p+1)/2}\,\bigl(1+\varepsilon(t)\bigr), \qquad \
varepsilon(t):=\sqrt{1+\eta(t)}-1. \tag{4}$$
By continuity of $x\mapsto\sqrt{1+x}-1$ at $0$, we have
$$\varepsilon(t)\to 0 \quad\text{as }t\nearrow T. \tag{5}$$

---

### Step 3. Integration and the Cesàro-type estimate

Divide (4) by $u^{(p+1)/2}$ and use $\frac{d}{dt}\bigl[u^{-(p-1)/2}\bigr]=-\frac
{p-1}{2}\,u^{-(p+1)/2}u'$:
$$\frac{d}{dt}\Bigl[u^{-(p-1)/2}\Bigr] = -\frac{p-1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2}{p+1}}\,\bi
gl(1+\varepsilon(t)\bigr). \tag{6}$$
Integrate (6) over $[t,T)$ (noting $u(T^-)^{-(p-1)/2}=0$ since $u\to+\infty$):
$$u(t)^{-(p-1)/2} = \frac{p-1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2}{p+1}}\;\Bigl((T-t)+\int_t^T\vare
psilon(s)\,ds\Bigr). \tag{7}$$

**Claim.** $\displaystyle\frac{1}{T-t}\int_t^T\varepsilon(s)\,ds \xrightarrow[t\
nearrow T]{} 0$.

*Proof of claim.* Let $\delta>0$. By (5) choose $t_1<T$ with $|\varepsilon(s)|<\
delta$ for $s\in[t_1,T)$. Then for every $t\in[t_1,T)$,
$$\Bigl|\frac{1}{T-t}\int_t^T\varepsilon(s)\,ds\Bigr|\le \frac{1}{T-t}\int_t^T|\
varepsilon(s)|\,ds \le \delta.$$
Since $\delta>0$ is arbitrary, the claim holds. $\square$

Applying the claim to (7):
$$u(t)^{-(p-1)/2} = \frac{p-1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2}{p+1}}\;(T-t)\,\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)
. \tag{8}$$

---

### Step 4. Inversion and conclusion

Raise (8) to the power $-2/(p-1)$ (the map $x\mapsto x^{-2/(p-1)}$ is continuous
 and sends $1+o(1)$ to $1+o(1)$):
$$u(t) = \Bigl[\frac{p-1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2}{p+1}}\Bigr]^{-2/(p-1)}(T-t)^{-2/(p-1)
}\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr). \tag{9}$$
Simplify the prefactor:
$$\Bigl[\frac{p-1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2}{p+1}}\Bigr]^{2/(p-1)} = \Bigl[\frac{(p-1)^2}
{4}\cdot\frac{2}{p+1}\Bigr]^{1/(p-1)} = \Bigl[\frac{(p-1)^2}{2(p+1)}\Bigr]^{1/(p
-1)},$$
hence
$$\Bigl[\frac{p-1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2}{p+1}}\Bigr]^{-2/(p-1)} = \Bigl[\frac{2(p+1)}
{(p-1)^2}\Bigr]^{1/(p-1)} = C.$$
Substituting into (9) with $\alpha=2/(p-1)$:
$$u(t) = C\,(T-t)^{-\alpha}\,\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr), \qquad t\nearrow T.$$
Therefore
$$\boxed{\;\lim_{t\nearrow T}\frac{u(t)}{C\,(T-t)^{-\alpha}}=1\;}$$
with $C=\bigl(2(p+1)/(p-1)^2\bigr)^{1/(p-1)}$ and $\alpha=2/(p-1)$. $\blacksquar
e$
