## Proof

Let $K = \ker(\pi \circ f : G \to H/A)$. Since $\pi \circ f$ is a homomorphism,
$K$ is a normal subgroup of $G$.

Because $A \trianglelefteq H$ and $[H:A] = 2$, the quotient $H/A$ has order $2$,
 hence $H/A \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.

By the first isomorphism theorem, $G/K \cong \operatorname{im}(\pi \circ f) \leq
slant H/A \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. Thus $[G:K] = |G/K| \in \{1, 2\}$, so $
K$ is a normal subgroup of $G$ of index $1$ or $2$.

Since $G$ is simple, its only normal subgroups are $\{e\}$ and $G$. Hence $K \in
 \{\{e\}, G\}$.

- If $K = G$, we are done.
- Suppose for contradiction $K = \{e\}$. Then $G/K \cong G$ embeds into $\mathbb
{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, so $|G|$ divides $2$, giving $|G| \in \{1, 2\}$.
  - $|G| = 2$ is excluded by the hypothesis $\#G \neq 2$.
  - $|G| = 1$ means $G = \{e\}$, in which case $K = \{e\} = G$, contradicting $K
 = \{e\} \neq G$.

Both possibilities for $|G|$ are impossible, so $K = \{e\}$ cannot occur. Theref
ore $K = G$.

Equivalently, $\pi \circ f$ is the trivial homomorphism, which means $f(G) \subs
eteq A$.

$$\boxed{\ker(\pi \circ f) = G}$$
