### Setup

Let $d(n)$ denote the units digit of $n$. The protocol defines $a_0=a$ and $a_{i
+1}=a_i/d(a_i)$. For the sequence to be a valid sequence of integers reaching 1,
 every step needs $d(a_i)\neq 0$ and $d(a_i)\mid a_i$, and some $a_k=1$.

**Key reduction.** The sequence starting at $n$ is just $n$ followed by the sequ
ence starting at $n/d(n)$. Hence:
$$n\text{ is Optimized}\iff n=1\ \text{or}\ \bigl(d(n)\in\{2,\dots,9\},\ d(n)\mi
d n,\ \text{and }n/d(n)\text{ is Optimized}\bigr).$$

**Excluding $d=1$.** If $d(n)=1$ and $n>1$, then $n/1=n$, so the sequence is con
stant and never reaches 1. Thus no Optimized number $>1$ ends in 1 (or 0).

### Generation rule

Write $n=md$ where $d=d(n)\in\{2,\dots,9\}$ and $m=n/d(n)$ is Optimized. The req
uirement "units digit of $n$ is $d$" becomes
$$md\equiv d\pmod{10}\iff d(m-1)\equiv 0\pmod{10}.$$
So every Optimized $n>1$ with $n<1000$ is obtained by taking an already-known Op
timized $m$ and a digit $d\in\{2,\dots,9\}$ with $d(m-1)\equiv0\pmod{10}$ and $m
d<1000$. Since $d\ge2$ and $m\ge2$ give $md>m$, this is a well-founded BFS.
