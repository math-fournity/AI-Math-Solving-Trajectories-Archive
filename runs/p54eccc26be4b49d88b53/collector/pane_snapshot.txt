**Case 1: All four digits are 1.** $N = 1111$: LHS $= 1\cdot1\cdot1\cdot1 = 1$,
RHS $= 2\cdot1\cdot1\cdot1\cdot1 - 1 = 1$. ✓ **(1 solution)**

**Case 2: Exactly three digits are 1.** WLOG $a=b=c=1$, $d = k \in \{2,\dots,9\}
$:
$$\text{LHS} = 1\cdot1\cdot1\cdot(2k-1) = 2k-1, \quad \text{RHS} = 2\cdot1\cdot1
\cdot1\cdot k - 1 = 2k-1. \quad \checkmark$$

This works for **every** $k \in \{2,\dots,9\}$. The non-1 digit can be in any of

**Case 3: Exactly two digits are 1.** WLOG $a=b=1$, $c,d \geq 2$:
$$(2c-1)(2d-1) = 4cd - 2c - 2d + 1 = 2cd - 1 \implies (c-1)(d-1) = 0.$$
Since $c,d \geq 2$, we have $(c-1)(d-1) \geq 1 > 0$. **No solutions.**

**Case 4: Exactly one digit is 1.** WLOG $a=1$, $b,c,d \geq 2$:
$$(2b-1)(2c-1)(2d-1) = 2bcd - 1.$$
Expanding the LHS: $8bcd - 4(bc+bd+cd) + 2(b+c+d) - 1 = 2bcd - 1$, giving:
$$g(b,c,d) := 3bcd - 2(bc+bd+cd) + (b+c+d) = 0.$$

I show $g > 0$ for $b,c,d \geq 2$. Note $\frac{\partial g}{\partial b} = 3cd - 2
c - 2d + 1$. Treating this as linear in $c$: $c(3d-2) - (2d-1)$. For $d \geq 2$,

$2(3d-2)-(2d-1) = 4d - 3 \geq 5 > 0$. Thus $\frac{\partial g}{\partial b} > 0$,
and by symmetry $g$ is increasing in each variable. The minimum is $g(2,2,2) = 2
