## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, such a line always exists.

**Step 1: The plane condition is automatic.**

In $\mathbf{CP}^3$, let $\ell$ be any line and $P$ any plane. By the projective
dimension theorem,
$$\dim(\ell \cap P) \geq \dim \ell + \dim P - \dim \mathbf{CP}^3 = 1 + 2 - 3 = 0
.$$
Hence every line in $\mathbf{CP}^3$ meets every plane. The condition "intersects
 the given plane $P$" is therefore satisfied by **every** line, and imposes no c
onstraint.

**Step 2: Reduce to finding a line meeting two given lines.**

It remains to show that for any two lines $L_1, L_2 \subset \mathbf{CP}^3$ (with
 $L_1, L_2 \not\subset P$), there exists a line $\ell$ meeting both $L_1$ and $L
_2$.

**Step 3: Schubert calculus on the Grassmannian.**

Let $G = G(1,3)$ be the Grassmannian of lines in $\mathbf{CP}^3$, a smooth proje
ctive variety of dimension $4$. For a fixed line $L$, the set
$$\Sigma(L) = \{\ell \in G : \ell \cap L \neq \emptyset\}$$
is a Schubert variety of codimension $1$ in $G$. Under the Plücker embedding $G
\hookrightarrow \mathbf{P}^5$ (where $G$ is the Klein quadric), $\Sigma(L)$ is a
 hyperplane section, hence an ample divisor.

**Step 4: The intersection is non-empty.**

We need $\Sigma(L_1) \cap \Sigma(L_2) \neq \emptyset$. In the Plücker embedding,
 $\Sigma(L_i)$ corresponds to a hyperplane $H_i \subset \mathbf{P}^5$. The inter
section $H_1 \cap H_2$ is a linear subspace $\mathbf{P}^3 \subset \mathbf{P}^5$.
 By the projective dimension theorem applied to $G$ (dimension $4$) and $\mathbf
{P}^3$ inside $\mathbf{P}^5$:
$$\dim(G \cap \mathbf{P}^3) \geq \dim G + \dim \mathbf{P}^3 - \dim \mathbf{P}^5
= 4 + 3 - 5 = 2 \geq 0.$$
Therefore $G \cap H_1 \cap H_2 = \Sigma(L_1) \cap \Sigma(L_2)$ is non-empty (in
fact, of dimension at least $2$).

**Conclusion.** There exists a line $\ell$ meeting both $L_1$ and $L_2$. By Step
 1, this line automatically meets the plane $P$ as well. Hence a line intersecti
ng the given plane and both given lines always exists. $\blacksquare$
