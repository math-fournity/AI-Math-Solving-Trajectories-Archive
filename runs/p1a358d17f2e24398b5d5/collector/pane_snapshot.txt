# Proof

We are given two field equations:

- **(E1)** $R_{\mu\nu} - \dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathcal{L}_{X}g_{\mu\nu} - c\,g_{\mu\nu}
 = 0$
- **(E2)** $R_{\mu\nu} - \dfrac{1}{2}\,R\,g_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda\,g_{\mu\nu} = 0$
 (Einstein's equation with cosmological constant)

and asked whether the two conditions

$$\mathcal{L}_{X}g_{\mu\nu} = R\,g_{\mu\nu}, \qquad c = -\Lambda$$

can hold simultaneously.

## Substitution

Assume both conditions hold. Substituting $\mathcal{L}_{X}g_{\mu\nu} = R\,g_{\mu
\nu}$ into (E1):

$$R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2}\,R\,g_{\mu\nu} - c\,g_{\mu\nu} = 0.$$

Now substituting $c = -\Lambda$:

$$R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2}\,R\,g_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda\,g_{\mu\nu} = 0.$$

This is precisely **(E2)**.

## Conclusion

Under the two conditions $\mathcal{L}_{X}g_{\mu\nu} = R\,g_{\mu\nu}$ and $c = -\
Lambda$, equation (E1) reduces **identically** to equation (E2). There is no alg
ebraic contradiction between the two conditions: they are mutually compatible, a
nd their joint imposition makes the two field equations coincide.

Therefore, **yes**, the conditions $\mathcal{L}_{X}g_{\mu\nu} = R\,g_{\mu\nu}$ a
nd $c = -\Lambda$ can hold simultaneously. The justification is that simultaneou
s substitution of both conditions into (E1) reproduces (E2) exactly, so the two
equations become identical rather than inconsistent.
