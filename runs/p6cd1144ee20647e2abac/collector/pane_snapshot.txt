## Verification of the Hessian Identity

**Claim.** For a smooth function $u$ on a Riemannian manifold $(M, g)$ with Levi
-Civita connection $\nabla$, and for smooth vector fields $X, Y$ on $M$,

$$\nabla^2_{(X,Y)} u \;=\; X(Yu) - (\nabla_X Y)\,u.$$

---

### 1. Definition of the Hessian

The Hessian of $u$ is the covariant derivative of the 1-form $du$:

$$\nabla^2 u \;:=\; \nabla(du), \qquad \nabla^2 u \in \Gamma(T^*M \otimes T^*M).
$$

Recall that for any 1-form $\alpha$ and vector field $X$, the covariant derivati
ve is defined by the Leibniz rule against the natural pairing:

$$(\nabla_X \alpha)(Y) \;=\; X\!\big(\alpha(Y)\big) - \alpha(\nabla_X Y), \qquad
 \forall\, Y \in \Gamma(TM).$$

Setting $\alpha = du$ and using $\alpha(Y) = du(Y) = Yu$, we obtain

$$\nabla^2_{(X,Y)} u \;=\; (\nabla_X du)(Y) \;=\; X\!\big(du(Y)\big) - du(\nabla
_X Y) \;=\; X(Yu) - (\nabla_X Y)\,u.$$

This is precisely the stated identity. What remains is to **verify** that the ri
ght-hand side actually defines a tensor (i.e., is $C^\infty(M)$-linear in both a
rguments), confirming that the Hessian is a genuine $(0,2)$-tensor and that the
formula is consistent.

---

### 2. Tensoriality in $Y$ (the second argument)

Let $f \in C^\infty(M)$. We check $\nabla^2_{(X,\,fY)} u = f\,\nabla^2_{(X,Y)} u
$.

$$\nabla^2_{(X,\,fY)} u \;=\; X\!\big((fY)u\big) - \big(\nabla_X (fY)\big)\,u.$$

Expand each term using the Leibniz rule:

- $X\!\big((fY)u\big) = X(fYu) = X(f)\,Yu + f\,X(Yu)$,
- $\nabla_X(fY) = X(f)\,Y + f\,\nabla_X Y$ (Leibniz rule for $\nabla$).

Substituting:

$$\nabla^2_{(X,\,fY)} u \;=\; \big[X(f)\,Yu + f\,X(Yu)\big] - \big[X(f)\,Y + f\,
\nabla_X Y\big]\,u.$$

Since $Yu$ and $Yu$ are functions, $(X(f)\,Y)u = X(f)\,Yu$. Collecting:

$$\nabla^2_{(X,\,fY)} u \;=\; X(f)\,Yu + f\,X(Yu) - X(f)\,Yu - f\,(\nabla_X Y)\,
u \;=\; f\,\big[X(Yu) - (\nabla_X Y)\,u\big] \;=\; f\,\nabla^2_{(X,Y)} u. \quad\
checkmark$$

The $X(f)\,Yu$ terms cancel exactly — this cancellation is the mechanism by whic
h the connection term "absorbs" the non-tensorial part of $X(Yu)$.

---

### 3. Tensoriality in $X$ (the first argument)

Let $f \in C^\infty(M)$. We check $\nabla^2_{(fX,\,Y)} u = f\,\nabla^2_{(X,Y)} u
$.

$$\nabla^2_{(fX,\,Y)} u \;=\; (fX)(Yu) - \big(\nabla_{fX} Y\big)\,u.$$

Expand:

- $(fX)(Yu) = f\,X(Yu)$ (a derivation applied to a function),
- $\nabla_{fX} Y = f\,\nabla_X Y$ ($\nabla$ is $C^\infty$-linear in the first ar
gument).

Substituting:

$$\nabla^2_{(fX,\,Y)} u \;=\; f\,X(Yu) - f\,(\nabla_X Y)\,u \;=\; f\,\big[X(Yu)
- (\nabla_X Y)\,u\big] \;=\; f\,\nabla^2_{(X,Y)} u. \quad\checkmark$$

---

### 4. Symmetry (consistency check via torsion-freeness)

Since $\nabla$ is the Levi-Civita connection, it is **torsion-free**: $\nabla_X
Y - \nabla_Y X = [X, Y]$. Using the identity:

$$\nabla^2_{(X,Y)} u - \nabla^2_{(Y,X)} u \;=\; \big[X(Yu) - (\nabla_X Y)u\big]
- \big[Y(Xu) - (\nabla_Y X)u\big]$$
$$= \;X(Yu) - Y(Xu) - (\nabla_X Y - \nabla_Y X)\,u \;=\; [X,Y]\,u - [X,Y]\,u \;=
\; 0.$$

Hence $\nabla^2_{(X,Y)} u = \nabla^2_{(Y,X)} u$: the Hessian is **symmetric**, a
s expected for the Levi-Civita connection. This confirms the formula is consiste
nt with the well-known symmetry of the Hessian.

---

### 5. Conclusion

Starting from the intrinsic definition $\nabla^2 u = \nabla(du)$ and applying th
e defining Leibniz rule for the covariant derivative of a 1-form, we derived

$$\boxed{\;\nabla^2_{(X,Y)} u \;=\; X(Yu) - (\nabla_X Y)\,u\;}$$

and verified that the right-hand side is $C^\infty(M)$-linear in both $X$ and $Y
$, hence a genuine $(0,2)$-tensor. The cancellation of the $X(f)$-terms in the $
Y$-slot check is precisely the role played by the connection term $-(\nabla_X Y)
u$: it converts the non-tensorial second derivative $X(Yu)$ into a tensorial obj
ect. The symmetry of the Hessian follows as a corollary of torsion-freeness, pro
viding an independent consistency check.
