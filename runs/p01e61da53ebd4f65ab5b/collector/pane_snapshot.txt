## Proof

**Setup.** Let $\mathbb{X} = [0, X_1] \times \cdots \times [0, X_D]$ be a box in
 $\mathbb{R}^D$. We expand $f \in \mathcal{C}^k(\mathbb{X})$ in the multidimensi
onal Fourier series (assuming periodic extension to $\mathbb{R}^D$):

$$f(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{\mathbf{n} \in \mathbb{Z}^D} \hat{f}_{\mathbf{n}}\, e^{2
\pi i \,\mathbf{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}(\mathbf{x})}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\xi}(\m
athbf{x}) = (x_1/X_1, \dots, x_D/X_D),$$

with Fourier coefficients

$$\hat{f}_{\mathbf{n}} = \frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{X})}\int_{\mathbb{X}} f(\
mathbf{x})\, e^{-2\pi i\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}(\mathbf{x})}\,d\mathbf{
x}, \qquad \mathbf{n} = (n_1,\dots,n_D)\in\mathbb{Z}^D.$$

Denote the multi-index $\boldsymbol{\alpha} = (\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_D)$, $|\bol
dsymbol{\alpha}| = \sum_j \alpha_j$, and the frequency magnitude $|\mathbf{n}| =
 \sqrt{n_1^2+\cdots+n_D^2}$.

---

**Key Lemma (integration by parts in $D$ dimensions).** For any coordinate $j \i
n \{1,\dots,D\}$ and any $\mathbf{n}$ with $n_j \neq 0$, integrating by parts al
ong $x_j$ (the boundary terms vanish because $f$ is periodic on $\mathbb{X}$):

$$\hat{f}_{\mathbf{n}} = \frac{1}{2\pi i\, n_j / X_j}\cdot\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(
\mathbb{X})}\int_{\mathbb{X}} \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_j}(\mathbf{x})\, e^{-
2\pi i\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}(\mathbf{x})}\,d\mathbf{x} = \frac{X_j}{2
\pi i\, n_j}\,\widehat{\partial_{x_j} f}_{\mathbf{n}}.$$

Iterating this for a multi-index $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$ with $|\boldsymbol{\alpha
}| = m \le k$ and all $n_j$ with $\alpha_j > 0$ nonzero:

$$\hat{f}_{\mathbf{n}} = \prod_{j:\alpha_j>0}\left(\frac{X_j}{2\pi i\, n_j}\righ
t)^{\alpha_j}\widehat{\partial^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} f}_{\mathbf{n}}.$$

Taking absolute values and using $|\widehat{\partial^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} f}_{\
mathbf{n}}| \le \|\partial^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} f\|_{L^1(\mathbb{X})}/\mathrm{V
ol}(\mathbb{X}) \le C_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}$ (since $\partial^{\boldsymbol{\alph
a}} f$ is continuous on the compact box, hence bounded):

$$|\hat{f}_{\mathbf{n}}| \le C\prod_{j=1}^{D}\frac{1}{|n_j|^{\alpha_j}} \le \fra
c{C'}{|\mathbf{n}|^{m}}.$$

---

**Main Theorem.** *If $f \in \mathcal{C}^k(\mathbb{X})$ (with periodic boundary
behavior), then its Fourier coefficients decay as*

$$\boxed{|\hat{f}_{\mathbf{n}}| = O\!\left(|\mathbf{n}|^{-k}\right) \quad \text{
as } |\mathbf{n}|\to\infty.}$$

*That is, each order of continuous differentiability contributes one additional
power of decay to the Fourier coefficients.*

**Proof.** Choose $m = k$ and any multi-index $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$ with $|\bold
symbol{\alpha}|=k$ (e.g., $\boldsymbol{\alpha} = (k,0,\dots,0)$). Since $f \in \
mathcal{C}^k$, $\partial^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}f$ is continuous on the compact bo
x $\mathbb{X}$, so $\|\partial^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}f\|_{L^\infty} < \infty$. Ap
plying the lemma along the coordinate $j$ with $\alpha_j = k$:

$$|\hat{f}_{\mathbf{n}}| \le \frac{C}{|n_j|^k} \le \frac{C'}{|\mathbf{n}|^k},$$

where the last step uses $|n_j| \ge |\mathbf{n}|/\sqrt{D}$ when $|n_j| = \max_\e
ll |n_\ell| \ge |\mathbf{n}|/\sqrt{D}$. Choosing the coordinate $j$ that achieve
s the maximum $|n_j|$ at each $\mathbf{n}$ gives the uniform bound $|\hat{f}_{\m
athbf{n}}| = O(|\mathbf{n}|^{-k})$. $\square$

---

**Refinement (bounded variation).** If additionally $\partial^{\boldsymbol{\alph
a}}f$ is of bounded variation for some $|\boldsymbol{\alpha}|=k$, one further in
tegration by parts (using the total-variation bound on the derivative of $\parti
al^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}f$) yields the sharper rate $|\hat{f}_{\mathbf{n}}| = O(
|\mathbf{n}|^{-(k+1)})$.

**Converse direction (qualitative).** The decay $|\hat{f}_{\mathbf{n}}| = O(|\ma
thbf{n}|^{-(k+\varepsilon)})$ for some $\varepsilon > 1$ (in the sense that $\su
m_{\mathbf{n}} |\mathbf{n}|^{k}\,|\hat{f}_{\mathbf{n}}| < \infty$, i.e., absolut
e summability with weight $|\mathbf{n}|^k$) implies, by termwise differentiation
 of the Fourier series, that $f \in \mathcal{C}^k(\mathbb{X})$. This establishes
 the **duality**:

$$f \in \mathcal{C}^k \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \text{sufficiently fast decay of }
 \hat{f}_{\mathbf{n}} \text{ (roughly } |\mathbf{n}|^{-(k+1+\varepsilon)}\text{
for the converse)}.$$

---

**Summary of the relationship.** Smoothness and Fourier-coefficient decay are du
al: **each order of continuous differentiability of $f$ buys one power of $|\mat
hbf{n}|^{-1}$ in the asymptotic decay of $\hat{f}_{\mathbf{n}}$**. Precisely, $f
 \in \mathcal{C}^k(\mathbb{X})$ (periodic) implies $|\hat{f}_{\mathbf{n}}| = O(|
\mathbf{n}|^{-k})$, and conversely, decay faster than $|\mathbf{n}|^{-(k+1)}$ (w
ith summability margin) guarantees $f \in \mathcal{C}^k$. This is the multidimen
sional generalization of the classical one-dimensional smoothness–decay principl
e, and the proof mechanism — integration by parts transferring derivatives from
$f$ onto powers of $1/n_j$ — works identically in every dimension.
