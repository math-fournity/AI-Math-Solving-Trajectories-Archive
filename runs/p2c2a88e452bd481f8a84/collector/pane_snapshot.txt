## Proof

**Answer: True**

Let $G = \langle S \rangle$ be a finitely generated group with solvable word pro
blem, and let $w \in F(S)$ be a word. We show that $d_S(e, w)$ is computable.

**Key observation.** The word metric distance $d_S(e, w)$ equals the minimal len
gth of a word over $S \cup S^{-1}$ that represents the same group element as $w$
. Since $w$ itself is such a word, we have the upper bound
$$d_S(e, w) \leq |w|,$$
where $|w|$ is the free-group length of $w$.

**Algorithm.** Enumerate all words $u$ over $S \cup S^{-1}$ of length $k = 0, 1,
 2, \dots, |w|$. For each candidate $u$ of length $k$, use the solvable word pro
blem to decide whether $u$ and $w$ represent the same element of $G$, i.e., whet
her $w^{-1}u =_G e$. The first (smallest) $k$ for which such a $u$ is found is e
xactly $d_S(e, w)$.

**Termination.** For each fixed $k$, there are finitely many words of length $k$
 (since $S$ is finite), and each equality check $w^{-1}u =_G e$ is decidable by
the solvable word problem. The search range $0 \leq k \leq |w|$ is finite, and $
k = |w|$ is guaranteed to succeed (take $u = w$). Hence the algorithm always ter
minates and returns the correct value.

**Conclusion.** The solvable word problem provides a decision procedure for test
ing whether two words represent the same group element, and the bounded search u
p to $|w|$ makes the computation of $d_S(e, w)$ effective. Therefore $d_S(e, w)$
 is computable for any word $w$.
