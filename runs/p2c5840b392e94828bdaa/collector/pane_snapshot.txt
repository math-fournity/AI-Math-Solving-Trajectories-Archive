**Answer: No.** The maximal unramified subextension of $LM/K$ need not be $TU$.

**Key observation.** While $TU/K$ is always unramified (compositum of unramified
 extensions is unramified) and $TU \subseteq LM$, the compositum of two totally
ramified extensions can contain a nontrivial unramified extension. This means th
e maximal unramified subextension of $LM/K$ can be strictly larger than $TU$.

**Counterexample.** Let $K = \mathbb{Q}_3$ (which is henselian with respect to t
he $3$-adic valuation, residue field $\mathbb{F}_3$).

Define:
- $L = \mathbb{Q}_3(\sqrt{3})$, and
- $M = \mathbb{Q}_3(\sqrt{6})$.

**Step 1: $L/K$ is totally ramified, so $T = K$.**

The polynomial $x^2 - 3$ is Eisenstein over $\mathbb{Q}_3$ (since $v_3(3) = 1$ a
nd $v_3(3^2) = 2 > 1$). Therefore $L/K$ is totally ramified of degree 2, and its
 maximal unramified subextension is $T = K$.

**Step 2: $M/K$ is totally ramified, so $U = K$.**

The polynomial $x^2 - 6$ is Eisenstein over $\mathbb{Q}_3$ (since $v_3(6) = 1$ a
nd $v_3(6^2) = 2 > 1$). Therefore $M/K$ is totally ramified of degree 2, and its
 maximal unramified subextension is $U = K$.

**Step 3: $TU = K$.**

Since $T = U = K$, we have $TU = K$.

**Step 4: $LM/K$ contains a nontrivial unramified extension.**

We have $LM = \mathbb{Q}_3(\sqrt{3}, \sqrt{6})$. Since $\sqrt{6}/\sqrt{3} = \sqr
t{2}$, the field $LM$ contains $\mathbb{Q}_3(\sqrt{2})$.

Now consider $\mathbb{Q}_3(\sqrt{2})/\mathbb{Q}_3$. The element $2$ is a $3$-adi
c unit, and its residue $\bar{2} = 2 \in \mathbb{F}_3^*$. Since $\mathbb{F}_3^*
= \{1, 2\}$ and $1^2 \equiv 2^2 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$, the element $2$ is not a squ
are in $\mathbb{F}_3$. Therefore $x^2 - 2$ is irreducible and separable over $\m
athbb{F}_3$, and by Hensel's lemma, $x^2 - 2$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}_3$
. The extension $\mathbb{Q}_3(\sqrt{2})/\mathbb{Q}_3$ is unramified of degree 2
(it corresponds to the separable quadratic extension $\mathbb{F}_9/\mathbb{F}_3$
 of residue fields).

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

The maximal unramified subextension $V$ of $LM/K$ satisfies $V \supseteq \mathbb
{Q}_3(\sqrt{2}) \supsetneq K = TU$. Therefore $V \neq TU$.

**Remark.** The phenomenon at work is that the compositum of totally ramified ex
tensions need not be totally ramified. In the example, $L$ and $M$ are both tota

 of ramification ($\sqrt{3}$ vs.\ $\sqrt{6} = \sqrt{2}\cdot\sqrt{3}$), and their

s always *an* unramified subextension of $LM/K$, but it need not be the *maximal

ramified parts of $L/K$ and $M/K$.
