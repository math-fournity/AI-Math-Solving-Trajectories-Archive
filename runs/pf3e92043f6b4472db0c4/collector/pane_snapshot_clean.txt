## Solution

We find the residue of $f(z)=\dfrac{\cos(\pi z)}{z^{2}\sin(\pi z)}$ at $z=1$.

**Step 1: Identify the nature of the singularity at $z=1$.**

Near $z=1$, set $w=z-1$. Then:
- $\cos(\pi z)=\cos(\pi+\pi w)=-\cos(\pi w)=-1+\dfrac{\pi^{2}w^{2}}{2}-\cdots$,
so $\cos(\pi\cdot 1)=-1\neq 0$.
- $\sin(\pi z)=\sin(\pi+\pi w)=-\sin(\pi w)=-\pi w+\dfrac{\pi^{3}w^{3}}{6}-\cdot
s$, a **simple zero** at $w=0$.
- $z^{2}=(1+w)^{2}$, with $z^{2}\big|_{z=1}=1\neq 0$.

Since the numerator is nonzero at $z=1$ and the denominator $z^{2}\sin(\pi z)$ h
as a simple zero there (the factor $z^{2}$ is nonzero and $\sin(\pi z)$ has a si
mple zero), $f$ has a **simple pole** at $z=1$.

**Step 2: Compute the residue.**

For a simple pole of $g(z)/h(z)$ with $h(a)=0$, $h'(a)\neq 0$, $g(a)\neq 0$:

$$\operatorname{Res}(f,a)=\frac{g(a)}{h'(a)}.$$

Here $g(z)=\cos(\pi z)$ and $h(z)=z^{2}\sin(\pi z)$.

$$g(1)=\cos\pi=-1.$$

$$h'(z)=2z\sin(\pi z)+z^{2}\pi\cos(\pi z),$$

$$h'(1)=2(1)\cdot\sin\pi+(1)^{2}\cdot\pi\cos\pi=0+\pi(-1)=-\pi.$$

Therefore:

$$\operatorname{Res}(f,1)=\frac{g(1)}{h'(1)}=\frac{-1}{-\pi}=\frac{1}{\pi}.$$

**Step 3: Verification via Laurent expansion.**

With $w=z-1$:

$$f(z)=\frac{-1+\frac{\pi^{2}w^{2}}{2}-\cdots}{(1+2w+w^{2})(-\pi w+\frac{\pi^{3}
w^{3}}{6}-\cdots)}=\frac{1}{\pi w}\cdot\frac{1-\frac{\pi^{2}w^{2}}{2}+\cdots}{(1
+w)^{2}\left(1-\frac{\pi^{2}w^{2}}{6}+\cdots\right)}.$$

The factor $\dfrac{1-\frac{\pi^{2}w^{2}}{2}+\cdots}{(1+w)^{2}(1-\frac{\pi^{2}w^{
2}}{6}+\cdots)}$ is analytic at $w=0$ with value $1$ at $w=0$. Hence the coeffic
ient of $w^{-1}$ (the residue) is $\dfrac{1}{\pi}\cdot 1=\dfrac{1}{\pi}$, confir
ming the result.

**Note on the stated order.** The problem states the pole is of order 2, but the
 analysis shows it is in fact a simple pole (order 1): the numerator $\cos(\pi z
)$ does not vanish at $z=1$, and only one factor of the denominator vanishes (si
mply). The residue is nonetheless well-defined and equals $1/\pi$. One may also
verify that applying the order-2 residue formula $\displaystyle\lim_{z\to 1}\fra
c{d}{dz}\bigl[(z-1)^{2}f(z)\bigr]$ yields the same value, since $(z-1)^{2}f(z)=(
z-1)\cdot\underbrace{(z-1)f(z)}_{\to\,1/\pi}$, whose derivative at $z=1$ is $1/\
pi$.

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Res}(f,1)=\dfrac{1}{\pi}}$$
