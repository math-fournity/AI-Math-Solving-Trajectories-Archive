## Answer

**No.** A finitely generated group need not have a proper subgroup of finite ind
ex.

## Proof

We exhibit a counterexample: **Higman's group** (Higman, 1951), which is finitel
y generated, infinite, and has no nontrivial finite quotient. We show that such
a group cannot possess any proper finite-index subgroup.

### The group

Let

$$G = \langle\, a, b, c, d \;\mid\; a^{-1}ba = b^{2},\; b^{-1}cb = c^{2},\; c^{-
1}dc = d^{2},\; d^{-1}ad = a^{2}\,\rangle.$$

This group is finitely generated (by four elements). Higman proved that $G$ is *
*infinite**. We prove below that $G$ has **no nontrivial finite quotient**, and
then deduce that $G$ has no proper finite-index subgroup.

### Step 1: $G$ has no nontrivial finite quotient

Suppose $\varphi: G \to F$ is a homomorphism onto a finite group $F$. Write $\ba
r a, \bar b, \bar c, \bar d$ for the images, and let $n_a, n_b, n_c, n_d$ be the
ir orders in $F$.

From the relation $a^{-1}ba = b^{2}$, the element $\bar b$ is conjugate to $\bar
 b^{2}$, so $\bar b$ and $\bar b^{2}$ have the same order. Since $\operatorname{
ord}(\bar b^{2}) = n_b/\!\gcd(n_b,2)$, equality of orders forces $\gcd(n_b,2)=1$
, i.e. $n_b$ is **odd**. The same argument applied to each of the four relations
 shows that $n_a, n_b, n_c, n_d$ are all odd.

Conjugation by $\bar a$ sends $\bar b \mapsto \bar b^{2}$, hence sends $\bar b \
mapsto \bar b^{2^{k}}$ upon $k$-fold iteration. Since $\bar a$ has order $n_a$,
conjugation by $\bar a^{n_a} = e$ is the identity, giving $\bar b = \bar b^{2^{n
_a}}$, i.e.

$$2^{n_a} \equiv 1 \pmod{n_b}.$$

Cyclically, we obtain:

$$2^{n_a}\equiv 1\!\pmod{n_b},\quad 2^{n_b}\equiv 1\!\pmod{n_c},\quad 2^{n_c}\eq
uiv 1\!\pmod{n_d},\quad 2^{n_d}\equiv 1\!\pmod{n_a}.$$

Now suppose for contradiction that some $n_i > 1$. Let $p$ be the **smallest pri
me** dividing any of $n_a, n_b, n_c, n_d$; without loss say $p \mid n_a$. From $
2^{n_d}\equiv 1\pmod{n_a}$ we get $p \mid 2^{n_d}-1$, so the multiplicative orde
r $\operatorname{ord}_p(2)$ divides $n_d$. Since $p$ is odd, $\operatorname{ord}
_p(2) \mid p-1 < p$. Let $q$ be any prime divisor of $\operatorname{ord}_p(2)$.
Then $q \mid n_d$ and $q \le \operatorname{ord}_p(2) \le p-1 < p$. But $q$ is th
en a prime divisor of one of the $n_i$ that is strictly smaller than $p$, contra
dicting the minimality of $p$.

Therefore every $n_i = 1$, so $\bar a = \bar b = \bar c = \bar d = e$ and $F$ is
 trivial. ∎

### Step 2: $G$ has no proper finite-index subgroup

Suppose for contradiction that $H \le G$ is a proper subgroup with $[G:H] = m <
\infty$. The left-multiplication action of $G$ on the coset space $G/H$ yields a
 homomorphism

$$\rho: G \longrightarrow S_m.$$

Its kernel $\ker\rho = \bigcap_{g\in G} gHg^{-1}$ (the *core* of $H$) is a norma
l subgroup of $G$ contained in $H$. Since $H$ is proper, $\ker\rho \le H \subset
neq G$, so $\ker\rho \ne G$, meaning $\rho$ is **nontrivial**. Consequently $G/\
ker\rho \cong \rho(G)$ is a nontrivial finite group — a nontrivial finite quotie
nt of $G$, contradicting Step 1.

Hence $G$ admits no proper subgroup of finite index.

### Conclusion

Higman's group $G$ is finitely generated (and infinite) yet has no proper finite
-index subgroup. Therefore the answer to the question is **no**: a finitely gene
rated group does **not** always have a proper subgroup of finite index.
