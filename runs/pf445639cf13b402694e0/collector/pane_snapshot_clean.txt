## Proof

**Yes, it is possible for $g$ to have infinite order while $\tau(g) = 0$.**

### The Group

Consider the Baumslag–Solitar group $BS(1,2) = \langle a, t \mid tat^{-1} = a^2
\rangle$ with the finite symmetric generating set $S = \{a^{\pm 1}, t^{\pm 1}\}$

### Step 1: $a$ has infinite order

The group $BS(1,2)$ admits a faithful representation as the semidirect product $
\mathbb{Z}[1/2] \rtimes \mathbb{Z}$, where the generator $t$ (corresponding to $
1 \in \mathbb{Z}$) acts on $\mathbb{Z}[1/2]$ by multiplication by $2$, and $a$ c
orresponds to the element $1 \in \mathbb{Z}[1/2]$. Under this identification, $a
^n$ corresponds to $(n, 0) \in \mathbb{Z}[1/2] \rtimes \mathbb{Z}$. Since $\math
bb{Z}[1/2]$ is torsion-free, $(n,0) = (0,0)$ if and only if $n = 0$. Therefore $
a$ has infinite order.

### Step 2: The key relation

From the defining relation $tat^{-1} = a^2$, I prove by induction that:
$$t^k a t^{-k} = a^{2^k} \quad \text{for all } k \geq 0.$$

**Base case ($k=0$):** trivial. **Inductive step:** assuming $t^k a t^{-k} = a^{
2^k}$, we have
$$t^{k+1} a t^{-(k+1)} = t(t^k a t^{-k})t^{-1} = t\, a^{2^k}\, t^{-1} = (tat^{-1
})^{2^k} = (a^2)^{2^k} = a^{2^{k+1}}.$$

### Step 3: Bounding $\|a^{2^k}\|$

The relation $a^{2^k} = t^k a t^{-k}$ gives an explicit word for $a^{2^k}$ in th
e generators $S$:
$$a^{2^k} = \underbrace{t \cdots t}_{k} \cdot a \cdot \underbrace{t^{-1} \cdots
t^{-1}}_{k},$$
which has length $2k + 1$. Therefore:
$$\|a^{2^k}\|_S \leq 2k + 1.$$

### Step 4: Applying Fekete's Lemma

The word length is subadditive: $\|gh\| \leq \|g\| + \|h\|$, so the sequence $f(
n) = \|a^n\|$ satisfies $f(n+m) \leq f(n) + f(m)$. By **Fekete's Lemma**, the li
mit exists and equals the infimum:
$$\tau(a) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\|a^n\|}{n} = \inf_{n \geq 1} \frac{\|a^n\

### Step 5: Concluding $\tau(a) = 0$

From Step 3:
$$\frac{\|a^{2^k}\|}{2^k} \leq \frac{2k+1}{2^k} \xrightarrow{k \to \infty} 0.$$

Since $\tau(a) = \inf_n \frac{\|a^n\|}{n} \leq \inf_k \frac{\|a^{2^k}\|}{2^k} =
0$, and word lengths are non-negative so $\tau(a) \geq 0$, we conclude:
$$\tau(a) = 0.$$

### Conclusion

The element $a \in BS(1,2)$ has infinite order (Step 1) yet $\tau(a) = 0$ (Step
5). This provides an explicit counterexample showing that infinite order does **
not** imply positive asymptotic translation length in general.
