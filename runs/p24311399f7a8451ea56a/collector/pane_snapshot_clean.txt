## Proof

Let the triangle be $ABC$ with baseline $AC = 8$ km and apex $B$. Two lines para
llel to $AC$ are drawn, meeting sides $AB$ and $BC$, dividing the triangle into
three regions of equal area (each $= \frac{1}{3}$ of the total area).

**Key fact.** A line parallel to the base of a triangle cuts off a smaller trian
gle similar to the original. If the linear scale factor (ratio of corresponding
lengths) is $k$, then the area ratio is $k^2$.

Let the two parallel lines be $\ell_1$ (closer to $B$) and $\ell_2$ (closer to $
AC$), with lengths $L_1$ and $L_2$ respectively.

**Region structure.** The three equal-area regions, from apex $B$ downward, are:
1. The small triangle at the top (above $\ell_1$), area $= \frac{1}{3}$ of total
.
2. The trapezoid between $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$, area $= \frac{1}{3}$ of total.
3. The trapezoid between $\ell_2$ and $AC$, area $= \frac{1}{3}$ of total.

**Finding $\ell_1$.** The triangle above $\ell_1$ is similar to $\triangle ABC$
with area ratio $\frac{1}{3}$. Hence the linear scale factor is
$$k_1 = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}, \qquad L_1 = k_1 \cdot AC = \
tfrac{8}{\sqrt{3}}.$$

**Finding $\ell_2$.** The triangle above $\ell_2$ (from $B$ down to $\ell_2$) co
ntains the top triangle plus the middle trapezoid, so its area is $\frac{1}{3} +
 \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{3}$ of the total. This triangle is similar to $\triangle
 ABC$ with area ratio $\frac{2}{3}$, giving linear scale factor
$$k_2 = \sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}}, \qquad L_2 = k_2 \cdot AC = 8\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}} =
\frac{8\sqrt{6}}{3}.$$

**Verification.**
- Top triangle area ratio: $k_1^2 = \frac{1}{3}$. ✓
- Triangle down to $\ell_2$ area ratio: $k_2^2 = \frac{2}{3}$, so middle trapezo
id $= \frac{2}{3} - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{3}$. ✓
- Bottom trapezoid $= 1 - \frac{2}{3} = \frac{1}{3}$. ✓

The line closest to the baseline $AC$ is $\ell_2$, and its length is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{8\sqrt{6}}{3} \text{ km}}.$$
