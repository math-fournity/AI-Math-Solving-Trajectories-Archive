## Proof

We use the standard CW decomposition of $\mathbb{R}P^n$, which has exactly one c
ell $e^k$ in each dimension $k = 0, 1, \dots, n$. The $k$-skeleton is $\mathbb{R
}P^k$, and the $k$-cell $e^k$ is attached via the canonical double covering map
$S^{k-1} \to \mathbb{R}P^{k-1}$.

### Cellular chain complex

The cellular chain group $C_k(\mathbb{R}P^n) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ is generated by t
he single $k$-cell $e^k$. The cellular boundary map $d_k : C_k \to C_{k-1}$ is d
etermined by the degree of the attaching map composed with the quotient onto the
 $(k{-}1)$-cell. A standard computation (see Hatcher, *Algebraic Topology*, §2.2
 or the computation via the antipodal map on $S^{k-1}$) gives:

$$d_k = 1 + (-1)^k,$$

i.e., $d_k$ is multiplication by $2$ when $k$ is even, and multiplication by $0$
 when $k$ is odd.

### The top-dimensional homology for odd $n$

Assume $n$ is odd. Then:

- $d_n = 1 + (-1)^n = 1 - 1 = 0$, so $\ker(d_n) = C_n = \mathbb{Z}\langle e^n \r
angle$.
- There is no $(n{+}1)$-cell, so $\operatorname{im}(d_{n+1}) = 0$.

Therefore:

$$H_n(\mathbb{R}P^n) = \ker(d_n) / \operatorname{im}(d_{n+1}) = \mathbb{Z}\langl
e e^n \rangle / 0 \cong \mathbb{Z}.$$

### Identifying the generator

The generator of $H_n(\mathbb{R}P^n) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ is the class of the top c
ell:

$$\boxed{[e^n] \in H_n(\mathbb{R}P^n),}$$

i.e., the **fundamental class** $[\mathbb{R}P^n]$ of the orientable manifold $\m
athbb{R}P^n$ (orientability for odd $n$ follows from $w_1(\mathbb{R}P^n) = (n{+}
1)\,a \in H^1(\mathbb{R}P^n;\mathbb{Z}/2)$, which vanishes when $n$ is odd).

### Relation to the covering space

To make the generator concrete, consider the 2-fold covering map $p : S^n \to \m
athbb{R}P^n$. Since $n$ is odd, the antipodal map on $S^n$ is orientation-preser
ving, so $\mathbb{R}P^n$ inherits an orientation. With respect to this orientati
on, the fundamental class $[\mathbb{R}P^n]$ satisfies:

$$p_*([S^n]) = 2\,[\mathbb{R}P^n],$$

because $p$ is a 2-sheeted covering (each point of $\mathbb{R}P^n$ has two preim
ages, each contributing $+1$ to the local degree). This confirms that $[\mathbb{
R}P^n]$ generates $H_n(\mathbb{R}P^n) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and that $p_*$ maps the
generator of $H_n(S^n)$ to twice the generator of $H_n(\mathbb{R}P^n)$.

### Summary

The generator of $H_n(\mathbb{R}P^n) = \mathbb{Z}$ (for odd $n$) is the **fundam
ental class** $[\mathbb{R}P^n]$, represented in the cellular chain complex by th
e top cell $e^n$ (since $d_n = 0$ makes $e^n$ a cycle, and there are no $(n{+}1)
$-cells to produce boundaries). Equivalently, it is the unique class $\alpha \in
 H_n(\mathbb{R}P^n)$ such that $p_*(\,[S^n]\,) = 2\alpha$ under the double cover
 $p : S^n \to \mathbb{R}P^n$.
