## Proof

**Claim.** Yes — if $G$ is finite and quasi-isometric to $H$, then $H$ is also f
inite.

Equip $G$ with a word metric $d_G$ coming from any finite generating set (since
$G$ is finite, we may take $S = G\setminus\{e\}$). The Cayley graph of $G$ is fi
nite, so $(G,d_G)$ is a **bounded** metric space; let

$$D := \operatorname{diam}(G,d_G) \le |G|-1 < \infty.$$

Equip $H$ with a proper left-invariant metric $d_H$ (for finitely generated $H$
this is the word metric; such a metric exists for any countable group, and "quas
i-isometric to $G$" presupposes $H$ carries such a metric). By hypothesis there
is a quasi-isometry $f:G\to H$ with constants $L\ge 1$, $C\ge 0$, i.e.

1. for all $g_1,g_2\in G$: $\;\frac{1}{L}d_G(g_1,g_2)-C \;\le\; d_H(f(g_1),f(g_2
)) \;\le\; L\,d_G(g_1,g_2)+C$,
2. $f(G)$ is $C$-dense in $H$: for every $h\in H$ there exists $g\in G$ with $d_
H(h,f(g))\le C$.

**Step 1: $H$ is bounded.** For any $h_1,h_2\in H$, by $C$-density choose $g_1,g
_2\in G$ with $d_H(h_i,f(g_i))\le C$. Then

$$
d_H(h_1,h_2)\;\le\; d_H(h_1,f(g_1))+d_H(f(g_1),f(g_2))+d_H(f(g_2),h_2)
\;\le\; C + \bigl(L\,d_G(g_1,g_2)+C\bigr) + C
\;=\; L\,d_G(g_1,g_2)+3C.
$$

Since $d_G(g_1,g_2)\le D$, we obtain

$$
\operatorname{diam}(H,d_H)\;\le\; L\,D+3C\;<\;\infty.
$$

So $H$ is a bounded metric space.

**Step 2: A bounded group with a proper left-invariant metric is finite.** The m
etric $d_H$ is **proper**: every closed ball $\overline{B}(h_0,R)=\{h\in H : d_H
(h_0,h)\le R\}$ is finite (for a word metric this is immediate — there are only
finitely many words of bounded length; for a general proper left-invariant metri
c on a discrete group it holds by definition of properness). Since $H$ is bounde
d, say $\operatorname{diam}(H)\le M$, we have

$$
H \;=\; \overline{B}(e,M),
$$

which is a ball of finite radius and hence finite. Therefore $|H|<\infty$.

**Conclusion.** Quasi-isometry preserves boundedness, and a group carrying a bou
nded proper left-invariant metric must be finite. Hence $H$ is finite. $\blacksq
uare$
