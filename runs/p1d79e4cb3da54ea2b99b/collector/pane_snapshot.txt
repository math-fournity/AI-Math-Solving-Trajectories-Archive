## Proof

We wish to show
$$\sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k}\binom{(p-1)n}{k}\binom{pn+k}{k} = \binom{pn}{n}^2, \
qquad p \ge 2.$$

**Step 1: Expand $\binom{pn+k}{k}$ via Vandermonde.**

Using the Chu–Vandermonde convolution with $a = pn$, $b = k$, $r = k$:
$$\binom{pn+k}{k} = \sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{pn}{j}\binom{k}{k-j} = \sum_{j=0}^{k}\b
inom{pn}{j}\binom{k}{j}.$$

Substituting into the LHS:
$$\text{LHS} = \sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{(p-1)n}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{p
n}{j}\binom{k}{j}.$$

**Step 2: Swap the order of summation.**

Since $0 \le j \le k \le n$:
$$\text{LHS} = \sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{pn}{j}\sum_{k=j}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{(p-1)
n}{k}\binom{k}{j}.$$

**Step 3: Simplify using $\binom{n}{k}\binom{k}{j} = \binom{n}{j}\binom{n-j}{k-j
}$.**

$$\text{LHS} = \sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{pn}{j}\binom{n}{j}\sum_{k=j}^{n}\binom{n-j}{
k-j}\binom{(p-1)n}{k}.$$

Setting $m = k - j$ (so $m$ ranges from $0$ to $n-j$):
$$\text{LHS} = \sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{pn}{j}\binom{n}{j}\sum_{m=0}^{n-j}\binom{n-j
}{m}\binom{(p-1)n}{m+j}.$$

**Step 4: Apply Vandermonde to the inner sum.**

Rewrite $\binom{(p-1)n}{m+j} = \binom{(p-1)n}{(p-1)n - m - j}$. The inner sum be
comes
$$\sum_{m=0}^{n-j}\binom{n-j}{m}\binom{(p-1)n}{(p-1)n - j - m}.$$

By Chu–Vandermonde with $a = n-j$, $b = (p-1)n$, $r = (p-1)n - j$:
$$= \binom{(n-j)+(p-1)n}{(p-1)n - j} = \binom{pn - j}{(p-1)n - j} = \binom{pn-j}
{n}.$$

So:
$$\text{LHS} = \sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{pn}{j}\binom{n}{j}\binom{pn-j}{n}.$$

**Step 5: Factor the product $\binom{pn}{j}\binom{pn-j}{n}$.**

$$\binom{pn}{j}\binom{pn-j}{n} = \frac{(pn)!}{j!\,(pn-j)!}\cdot\frac{(pn-j)!}{n!
\,(pn-j-n)!} = \frac{(pn)!}{n!\,(pn-n)!}\cdot\frac{(pn-n)!}{j!\,(pn-n-j)!} = \bi
nom{pn}{n}\binom{(p-1)n}{j}.$$

Therefore:
$$\text{LHS} = \binom{pn}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{(p-1)n}{j}\binom{n}{j}.$$

**Step 6: Apply Vandermonde once more.**

Writing $\binom{n}{j} = \binom{n}{n-j}$:
$$\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{(p-1)n}{j}\binom{n}{n-j} = \binom{(p-1)n + n}{n} = \binom
{pn}{n},$$

where the sum naturally terminates at $j = n$ (since $\binom{n}{n-j}=0$ for $j >
 n$), and all terms with $j > (p-1)n$ vanish as well (and $(p-1)n \ge n$ since $
p \ge 2$).

**Conclusion.**

$$\text{LHS} = \binom{pn}{n}\cdot\binom{pn}{n} = \binom{pn}{n}^2 = \text{RHS}. \
qquad \blacksquare$$
