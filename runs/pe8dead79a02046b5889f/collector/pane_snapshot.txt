## Proof

The given polynomial is
$$f(x) = x^{12} + x^{11} + \cdots + x + 1 = \frac{x^{13}-1}{x-1}.$$

Since $13$ is prime, this is precisely the $13$th cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_{1
3}(x)$, whose roots are the primitive $13$th roots of unity $\zeta, \zeta^2, \ld
ots, \zeta^{12}$, where $\zeta = e^{2\pi i/13}$.

**The splitting field.** The splitting field of $\Phi_{13}(x)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$
 is the cyclotomic field $K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta)$, obtained by adjoining a single
 primitive $13$th root of unity.

**The Galois group.** Every $\mathbb{Q}$-automorphism of $K$ is determined by it
s action on $\zeta$, and it must send $\zeta$ to another root of $\Phi_{13}$, i.
e., to $\zeta^a$ for some $a$ with $\gcd(a,13)=1$. This gives an injection
$$\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \hookrightarrow (\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z})^{\t
imes}.$$
Conversely, for each $a \in (\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z})^{\times}$, the map $\sigma
_a : \zeta \mapsto \zeta^a$ extends to a $\mathbb{Q}$-automorphism of $K = \math
bb{Q}(\zeta)$. Therefore
$$\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z})^{\times}.$$

**Structure of $(\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z})^{\times}$.** The multiplicative group
of a finite field is cyclic. Hence
$$(\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z})^{\times} \cong C_{12},$$
the cyclic group of order $12 = 13 - 1$. (Concretely, $2$ is a primitive root mo
d $13$, since the powers $2^1,2^2,\ldots,2^{12}$ give all nonzero residues mod $
13$, confirming the group is cyclic of order $12$.)

**Solvability.** The Galois group is cyclic, hence abelian. Every abelian group
is solvable (its derived series terminates at the identity after one step). Ther
efore the Galois group is solvable.

### Conclusion

The Galois group of $x^{12} + x^{11} + \cdots + x + 1 = \Phi_{13}(x)$ over $\mat
hbb{Q}$ is
$$\operatorname{Gal}(\Phi_{13}/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z})^{\tim
es} \cong C_{12},$$
the cyclic group of order $12$. Since it is cyclic (in particular abelian), **it
 is solvable**.
