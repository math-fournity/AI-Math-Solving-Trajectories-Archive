### Setup: Spherical Coordinates

Let $x = \rho\sin\phi\cos\theta$, $y = \rho\sin\phi\sin\theta$, $z = \rho\cos\ph
i$, with $\rho \geq 0$, $\phi \in [0,\pi]$, $\theta \in [0,2\pi)$, and $dV = \rh
o^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta$.

**Constraint 1:** $z \geq \sqrt{\dfrac{x^2+y^2}{3}}$.

With $x^2+y^2 = \rho^2\sin^2\phi$ and $z = \rho\cos\phi$ (and $z\geq 0$ forced b
y the RHS $\geq 0$), this gives
$$\rho\cos\phi \geq \frac{\rho\sin\phi}{\sqrt{3}} \;\Longrightarrow\; \sqrt{3}\c
os\phi \geq \sin\phi \;\Longrightarrow\; \tan\phi \leq \sqrt{3} \;\Longrightarro
w\; \phi \leq \frac{\pi}{3}.$$

**Constraint 2:** $2z \leq x^2+y^2+z^2 \leq 9$.

Since $x^2+y^2+z^2 = \rho^2$:
- Upper bound: $\rho^2 \leq 9 \;\Longrightarrow\; \rho \leq 3$.
- Lower bound: $2\rho\cos\phi \leq \rho^2$. For $\rho > 0$, this gives $\rho \ge
q 2\cos\phi$.

For $\phi \in [0,\pi/3]$, we have $\cos\phi \in [1/2, 1]$, so $2\cos\phi \in [1,
2] < 3$; the interval $[2\cos\phi,\,3]$ is always nonempty.

### Region in spherical coordinates

$$E:\quad 0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi,\quad 0 \leq \phi \leq \frac{\pi}{3},\quad 2\c
os\phi \leq \rho \leq 3.$$

### Evaluating the integral

The integrand is $\dfrac{1}{x^2+y^2+z^2} = \dfrac{1}{\rho^2}$, so

$$\iiint_E \frac{1}{\rho^2}\,\rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta
= \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{\pi/3}\!\!\int_{2\cos\phi}^{3} \sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi
\,d\theta.$$

**Inner integral** ($\rho$):
$$\int_{2\cos\phi}^{3} d\rho = 3 - 2\cos\phi.$$

**Middle integral** ($\phi$):
$$\int_0^{\pi/3} \sin\phi\,(3 - 2\cos\phi)\,d\phi = \int_0^{\pi/3}\!\bigl(3\sin\
phi - \sin 2\phi\bigr)\,d\phi.$$

An antiderivative is $-3\cos\phi + \tfrac{1}{2}\cos 2\phi$:
- At $\phi = \pi/3$: $-3\cdot\tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot(-\tfrac{1}{2}) = -
\tfrac{3}{2} - \tfrac{1}{4} = -\tfrac{7}{4}$.
- At $\phi = 0$: $-3\cdot 1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 1 = -\tfrac{5}{2}$.

Difference: $-\tfrac{7}{4} - (-\tfrac{5}{2}) = -\tfrac{7}{4} + \tfrac{10}{4} = \
tfrac{3}{4}$.

**Outer integral** ($\theta$):
$$\int_0^{2\pi} d\theta = 2\pi.$$

### Result

$$\iiint_E \frac{1}{x^2+y^2+z^2}\,dV = 2\pi \cdot \frac{3}{4} = \boxed{\dfrac{3\
pi}{2}}.$$
