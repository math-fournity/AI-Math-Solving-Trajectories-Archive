## Proof

**Answer: Yes, such structures exist.**

**Interpretation.** A structure that is simultaneously an infinite-dimensional v
ector space over $\mathbb{R}$ and a field is, in the natural and standard sense,
 a field extension $F \supseteq \mathbb{R}$ such that $[F:\mathbb{R}] = \infty$
(the scalar multiplication is the restriction of the field multiplication to $\m
athbb{R} \times F$). We exhibit an explicit example.

---

**Construction.** Let $F = \mathbb{R}(t)$, the field of rational functions in on
e indeterminate $t$ with real coefficients.

**Step 1: $F$ is a field.**
$\mathbb{R}[t]$ is an integral domain (a commutative ring with no zero divisors)
. The field of fractions of any integral domain is a field. Since $\mathbb{R}(t)
 = \operatorname{Frac}(\mathbb{R}[t])$, it is a field.

**Step 2: $F$ is a vector space over $\mathbb{R}$.**
$\mathbb{R}$ embeds into $\mathbb{R}(t)$ via $a \mapsto a/1$ (constant rational
functions). Define scalar multiplication by $a \cdot \frac{f(t)}{g(t)} = \frac{a
\,f(t)}{g(t)}$ for $a \in \mathbb{R}$, $f/g \in \mathbb{R}(t)$. This is just the
 restriction of the field multiplication, so all vector space axioms (distributi
vity, associativity of scalar multiplication, $1$ acts as identity) follow immed
iately from the field axioms. Hence $\mathbb{R}(t)$ is an $\mathbb{R}$-vector sp
ace.

**Step 3: $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} F = \infty$.**
Consider the set $\{1, t, t^{2}, t^{3}, \dots\} \subseteq \mathbb{R}(t)$, where
$t^{n} = t^{n}/1$. Suppose
$$a_{0} + a_{1}t + a_{2}t^{2} + \cdots + a_{n}t^{n} = 0 \quad (a_{i} \in \mathbb
{R}).$$
The left-hand side is the rational function $\frac{a_{0}+a_{1}t+\cdots+a_{n}t^{n
}}{1}$. A rational function equals zero if and only if its numerator is the zero
 polynomial. Therefore $a_{0}+a_{1}t+\cdots+a_{n}t^{n}$ is the zero polynomial,
which forces $a_{0}=a_{1}=\cdots=a_{n}=0$.

Thus $\{1, t, t^{2}, \dots\}$ is linearly independent over $\mathbb{R}$. Being a
n infinite linearly independent set, the dimension of $\mathbb{R}(t)$ over $\mat
hbb{R}$ is infinite.

---

**Remark (why this is essentially the only mechanism).** $\mathbb{R}$ is real cl
osed, so its algebraic closure is $\mathbb{C} = \mathbb{R}(i)$ with $[\mathbb{C}
:\mathbb{R}]=2$. Consequently every *algebraic* extension of $\mathbb{R}$ has de
gree at most $2$. Any field extension $F/\mathbb{R}$ with $[F:\mathbb{R}]>2$—in
particular any infinite-dimensional one—must be *transcendental*. The example $\
mathbb{R}(t)$ is the simplest such extension: $t$ is transcendental over $\mathb
b{R}$, and adjoining it produces an infinite-degree extension. More generally, $
\mathbb{R}(t_{1},t_{2},\dots)$ or any transcendental extension of $\mathbb{R}$ g
ives further examples.

---

**Conclusion.** The field $\mathbb{R}(t)$ of rational functions over $\mathbb{R}
$ is simultaneously a field and an infinite-dimensional vector space over $\math
bb{R}$. Therefore, infinite-dimensional vector spaces over $\mathbb{R}$ that are
 also fields **do exist**.

### PROOF COMPLETE
