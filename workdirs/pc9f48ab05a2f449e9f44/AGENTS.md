# Problem

A network security firm is testing a new encryption protocol for a cluster of $n$ distinct servers ($n \ge 2$). The protocol is deemed "triple-key stable" if there exists a specific set of three distinct prime numbers $\{p, q, r\}$ that governs data packet interference.

The stability condition is defined as follows: Suppose a technician assigns $n$ distinct positive integer identification codes, $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$, to the servers. If the identification codes are chosen such that for every possible pair of servers $(j, k)$, the difference between their codes $|a_j - a_k|$ is divisible by at least one of the three primes $p, q,$ or $r$, then for the protocol to be stable, one of those three primes must necessarily divide every one of the $\binom{n}{2}$ total differences.

Determine how many integers $n$ in the range $2 \le n \le 2025$ qualify as triple-key stable.
