## Proof

**Answer: Yes.**

**Step 1: $S$ is a smooth 1-dimensional manifold without boundary.**

Since $\operatorname{rank} Df(a) = n$ for every $a \in S = f^{-1}(\{0\})$, the v
alue $0$ is a regular value of $f$ (on $S$). By the Regular Level Set Theorem (a
 consequence of the Implicit Function Theorem), $S$ is a smooth embedded submani
fold of $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ of codimension $n$, hence of dimension $(n+1) - n = 1
$. Being a level set of a smooth submersion (on a neighborhood of $S$), $S$ has
no boundary. As a submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$, $S$ is Hausdorff and second
-countable. By hypothesis $S$ is path-connected, hence connected.

**Step 2: Classification of connected 1-manifolds.**

A standard theorem in differential topology states:

> *Every connected, Hausdorff, second-countable, 1-dimensional smooth manifold w
ithout boundary is diffeomorphic to either $\mathbb{R}$ or $S^1$.*

(The proof: equip the manifold with a Riemannian metric; the unit-speed geodesic
 flow from any point either closes up — giving $S^1$ — or defines a global diffe
omorphism from an interval — giving $\mathbb{R}$; second-countability rules out
the "long line" type pathologies.)

Applying this to $S$, we have two cases.

**Step 3: Case $S \cong \mathbb{R}$.**

Let $\varphi: \mathbb{R} \to S$ be a diffeomorphism. Set $I = \mathbb{R}$ (an op
en connected subset of $\mathbb{R}$) and $g = \varphi$. Then:
- $g(I) = \varphi(\mathbb{R}) = S$. ✓
- Since $\varphi$ is a diffeomorphism, $D\varphi(t): \mathbb{R} \to T_{\varphi(t
)}S$ is a linear isomorphism for every $t$, so $\operatorname{rank} Dg(t) = 1$ f
or all $t \in I$. ✓

**Step 4: Case $S \cong S^1$.**

Let $\varphi: S^1 \to S$ be a diffeomorphism. Choose any $\varepsilon > 0$ and s
et $I = (0,\, 2\pi + \varepsilon)$, an open connected subset of $\mathbb{R}$. Pa
rametrize $S^1$ by
$$h: I \to S^1, \qquad h(t) = (\cos t,\, \sin t) \in S^1 \subset \mathbb{R}^2.$$
Then:
- $h$ is smooth and $h'(t) = (-\sin t,\, \cos t) \neq (0,0)$ for all $t$, so $h$
 is an immersion ($\operatorname{rank} Dh = 1$ everywhere).
- Since $I$ has length $2\pi + \varepsilon > 2\pi$, the image $h(I)$ covers all
of $S^1$ (every angle is attained), so $h(I) = S^1$.

Now set $g = \varphi \circ h: I \to S \subset \mathbb{R}^{n+1}$. Then:
- $g$ is smooth (composition of smooth maps).
- $g(I) = \varphi(h(I)) = \varphi(S^1) = S$. ✓
- $Dg(t) = D\varphi(h(t)) \circ Dh(t)$. Since $h$ is an immersion and $\varphi$
is a diffeomorphism (hence $D\varphi$ is an isomorphism on tangent spaces), the
composition has rank 1. ✓

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

In both possible cases ($S \cong \mathbb{R}$ or $S \cong S^1$), we have construc
ted an open connected subset $I \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ and a smooth map $g: I \to
 \mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ with $g(I) = S$ and $\operatorname{rank} Dg(t) = 1$ for all $
t \in I$.

Therefore, **yes**, such a smooth function $g$ always exists. $\blacksquare$
