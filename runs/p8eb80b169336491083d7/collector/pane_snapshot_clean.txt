## Solution

We seek the resolvent $R(x,t;\lambda)$ of the kernel $K(x,t)=xe^{t}$ on $[0,1]$,
 defined by

$$R(x,t;\lambda)=\frac{D(x,t;\lambda)}{D(\lambda)},$$

where $D(\lambda)$ is the **Fredholm determinant** and $D(x,t;\lambda)$ is the *
*first Fredholm minor**.

### Step 1. Recognize the kernel is separable (degenerate)

$$K(x,t)=xe^{t}=\varphi_{1}(x)\,\psi_{1}(t),\qquad \varphi_{1}(x)=x,\quad \psi_{
1}(t)=e^{t}.$$

The kernel has rank $1$. A key consequence: every Fredholm determinant/minor of
order $\ge 2$ vanishes, because the corresponding matrices have rank $\le 1$ and
 hence zero determinants.

### Step 2. Compute the Fredholm determinant $D(\lambda)$

The Fredholm series is

$$D(\lambda)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-\lambda)^{n}}{n!}\int_{0}^{1}\!\cdots\
!\int_{0}^{1}\det\bigl(s_{i},s_{j})\bigr]_{i,j=1}^{n}\,ds_{1}\cdots ds_{n}.$$

- **$n=1$ term:** $\displaystyle -\lambda\int_{0}^{1}K(s,s)\,ds=-\lambda\int_{0}
^{1}se^{s}\,ds.$

  Integration by parts: $\int_{0}^{1}se^{s}\,ds=\bigl[se^{s}-e^{s}\bigr]_{0}^{1}
=(e-e)-(0-1)=1$.

  So the $n=1$ term equals $-\lambda\cdot 1=-\lambda$.

- **$n\ge 2$ terms:** The matrix $(s_i,s_j)]_{i,j=1}^{n}=[s_i e^{s_j}]$ is the
 outer product of the column vector $(s_1,\dots,s_n)^{\top}$ and the row vector
$(e^{s_1},\dots,e^{s_n})$, hence has rank $\le 1$. For $n\ge 2$ its determinant
is $0$.

Therefore

$$\boxed{D(\lambda)=1-\lambda.}$$

### Step 3. Compute the first Fredholm minor $D(x,t;\lambda)$

The series for the first minor is

$$D(x,t;\lambda)=K(x,t)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-\lambda)^{n}}{n!}\int\cdots\i
nt\det\begin{pmatrix}K(x,t)&K(x,s_{1})&\cdots&K(x,s_{n})\\K(s_{1},t)&K(s_{1},s_{
1})&\cdots&K(s_{1},s_{n})\\\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\K(s_{n},t)&K(s_{n},s_{1}
)&\cdots&K(s_{n},s_{n})\end{pmatrix}ds_{1}\cdots ds_{n}.$$

- **$n=0$ term:** $K(x,t)=xe^{t}$.

- **$n=1$ term:**
  $$-\lambda\int_{0}^{1}\det\begin{pmatrix}K(x,t)&K(x,s)\\K(s,t)&K(s,s)\end{pmat
rix}ds=-\lambda\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(x,t)K(s,s)-K(x,s)K(s,t)\bigr]ds.$$

  We compute each piece:
  - $K(x,t)K(s,s)=xe^{t}\cdot se^{s}$, so $\displaystyle K(x,t)\int_{0}^{1}K(s,s
)\,ds=xe^{t}\cdot 1=xe^{t}$.
  - $K(x,s)K(s,t)=(xe^{s})(se^{t})=xe^{t}\,se^{s}$, so $\displaystyle \int_{0}^{
1}K(x,s)K(s,t)\,ds=xe^{t}\int_{0}^{1}se^{s}\,ds=xe^{t}\cdot 1=xe^{t}$.

  Hence the $n=1$ term $=-\lambda\bigl[xe^{t}-xe^{t}\bigr]=0$.

- **$n\ge 2$ terms:** The $(n+1)\times(n+1)$ determinant matrix is again an oute
r product $[\,u_i v_j\,]$ with $u_0=x,\;u_i=s_i$ and $v_0=e^{t},\;v_j=e^{s_j}$,
so it has rank $\le 1$. For $n\ge 2$ (matrix size $\ge 3$), the determinant is $
0$.

Therefore

$$\boxed{D(x,t;\lambda)=K(x,t)=xe^{t}.}$$

### Step 4. Form the resolvent

$$\boxed{R(x,t;\lambda)=\frac{D(x,t;\lambda)}{D(\lambda)}=\frac{xe^{t}}{1-\lambd
a}.}$$

### Verification

The resolvent must satisfy the resolvent equation
$$R(x,t;\lambda)=K(x,t)+\lambda\int_{0}^{1}K(x,s)\,R(s,t;\lambda)\,ds.$$

Substituting $R(s,t;\lambda)=\dfrac{se^{t}}{1-\lambda}$:

$$\lambda\int_{0}^{1}(xe^{s})\frac{se^{t}}{1-\lambda}\,ds=\frac{\lambda xe^{t}}{
1-\lambda}\int_{0}^{1}se^{s}\,ds=\frac{\lambda xe^{t}}{1-\lambda}\cdot 1=\frac{\
lambda xe^{t}}{1-\lambda}.$$

Then
$$K(x,t)+\lambda\int_{0}^{1}K(x,s)R(s,t;\lambda)\,ds=xe^{t}+\frac{\lambda xe^{t}
}{1-\lambda}=xe^{t}\!\left(1+\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}\right)=xe^{t}\cdot\frac{1
}{1-\lambda}=\frac{xe^{t}}{1-\lambda}=R(x,t;\lambda).\;\checkmark$$

The resolvent has a single pole at $\lambda=1$, which is the unique characterist
ic value of the kernel (consistent with the one-dimensional nature of the separa
ble kernel).
