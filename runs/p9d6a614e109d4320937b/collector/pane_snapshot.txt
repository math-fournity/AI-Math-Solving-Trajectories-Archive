## Solution

**Answer: Yes.** Under the given hypotheses, $M$ has the same homology groups as
 $S^3$.

### Proof

We compute $H_k(M;\mathbb{Z})$ for $k = 0,1,2,3$ using the hypotheses: $M$ is co
mpact, connected, orientable, and without boundary (i.e., a closed orientable 3-
manifold), with $H_1(M;\mathbb{Z}) = 0$.

**$H_0(M;\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$.** Since $M$ is connected, its zeroth homolog
y is $\mathbb{Z}$.

**$H_1(M;\mathbb{Z}) = 0$.** This is the hypothesis.

**$H_3(M;\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$.** A closed (compact, no boundary) connected
orientable $n$-manifold has $H_n(M;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by t
he fundamental class $[M]$. This is a standard consequence of orientability (the
 orientation sheaf is constant $\mathbb{Z}$, and compactness with empty boundary
 gives top homology $\mathbb{Z}$ rather than $0$).

**$H_2(M;\mathbb{Z}) = 0$.** This is the only nontrivial step. We use Poincaré d
uality and the universal coefficient theorem.

By **Poincaré duality** (valid since $M$ is a closed orientable 3-manifold):
$$H_2(M;\mathbb{Z}) \cong H^{3-2}(M;\mathbb{Z}) = H^1(M;\mathbb{Z}).$$

By the **universal coefficient theorem for cohomology**:
$$H^1(M;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(H_1(M;\mathbb{Z}),\, \mathbb{Z}) \;
\oplus\; \operatorname{Ext}(H_0(M;\mathbb{Z}),\, \mathbb{Z}).$$

Now evaluate each term:
- $\operatorname{Hom}(H_1(M;\mathbb{Z}),\, \mathbb{Z}) = \operatorname{Hom}(0,\,
 \mathbb{Z}) = 0$, since $H_1 = 0$ by hypothesis.
- $\operatorname{Ext}(H_0(M;\mathbb{Z}),\, \mathbb{Z}) = \operatorname{Ext}(\mat
hbb{Z},\, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$, since $\mathbb{Z}$ is free abelian (hence projective
), and $\operatorname{Ext}$ vanishes in the first argument for free abelian grou
ps.

Therefore $H^1(M;\mathbb{Z}) = 0$, and by Poincaré duality, $H_2(M;\mathbb{Z}) =
 0$.

### Conclusion

The homology groups of $M$ are:
$$H_k(M;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \begin{cases} \mathbb{Z} & k = 0, \\ 0 & k = 1, \\ 0 &
 k = 2, \\ \mathbb{Z} & k = 3, \\ 0 & k \geq 4. \end{cases}$$

These are exactly the homology groups of $S^3$. Hence, yes, $M$ has the same hom
ology groups as a 3-sphere.

**Remark.** This is a statement about *homology* only. The condition $H_1(M;\mat
hbb{Z})=0$ does **not** force $\pi_1(M)=0$, so $M$ need not be homeomorphic to $
S^3$ (e.g., the Poincaré homology sphere is a counterexample to that stronger cl
aim). The question asks only about homology groups, and for those the answer is
affirmative.
