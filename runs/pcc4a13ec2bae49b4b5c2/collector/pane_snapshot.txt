# Proof

**Claim:** In general, no. If $X$ is an infinite-dimensional separable reflexive
 subspace of $\ell_\infty$, then $X^*$ cannot be identified with (i.e., is not i
somorphic to) a subspace of $\ell_1$. The identification is possible only when $
X$ is finite-dimensional.

---

## Step 1. Infinite-dimensional separable reflexive subspaces of $\ell_\infty$ e
xist.

Every separable Banach space $Y$ embeds isometrically into $\ell_\infty$. Indeed
, since $Y$ is separable, the closed unit ball $B_{Y^*}$ is weak$^*$-compact and
 weak$^*$-metrizable, hence weak$^*$-separable; let $(y_n^*)_{n\geq 1}$ be weak$
^*$-dense in $B_{Y^*}$. Then
$$T: Y \to \ell_\infty, \qquad T(y) = (y_n^*(y))_{n\geq 1}$$
is a linear isometry, since $\|y\| = \sup_{y^* \in B_{Y^*}} |y^*(y)| = \sup_n |y
_n^*(y)|$ by density. In particular, $Y = \ell_2$ (which is separable and reflex
ive) embeds isometrically into $\ell_\infty$, giving an infinite-dimensional sep
arable reflexive subspace $X \cong \ell_2$ of $\ell_\infty$.

## Step 2. If $X^*$ embeds into $\ell_1$, then $X^*$ has the Schur property.

The space $\ell_1$ has the **Schur property**: every weakly convergent sequence
in $\ell_1$ is norm-convergent (Schur's theorem). The Schur property is inherite
d by closed subspaces: if $Z \subseteq \ell_1$ is a closed subspace and $(z_n)$
is a sequence in $Z$ converging weakly to $z \in Z$ (weak convergence in $Z$), t
hen $z_n \to z$ weakly in $\ell_1$, hence $z_n \to z$ in norm. Therefore any sub
space of $\ell_1$ has the Schur property. So if $X^*$ is isomorphic to a subspac
e of $\ell_1$, then $X^*$ has the Schur property.

## Step 3. $X$ reflexive implies $X^*$ reflexive.

This is standard: $X$ reflexive means the canonical embedding $J: X \to X^{**}$
is surjective. Then $J^*: X^{***} \to X^*$ is a surjective isomorphism with inve
rse the canonical embedding of $X^*$ into $X^{***}$, so $X^*$ is reflexive.

## Step 4. A reflexive Banach space with the Schur property is finite-dimensiona
l.

Let $Z$ be a reflexive Banach space with the Schur property. By the Banach–Alaog
lu theorem (applied to $Z = Z^{**}$), the closed unit ball $B_Z$ is weakly compa
ct. By the Eberlein–Šmulian theorem, $B_Z$ is weakly sequentially compact: every
 sequence in $B_Z$ has a weakly convergent subsequence. By the Schur property, t
his subsequence converges in norm. Hence $B_Z$ is (norm-)sequentially compact, a
nd therefore norm-compact (in metric spaces, sequential compactness equals compa
ctness). But by Riesz's lemma, the closed unit ball of a normed space is norm-co
mpact if and only if the space is finite-dimensional. Therefore $\dim Z < \infty
$.

## Step 5. Conclusion.

Suppose $X^*$ can be identified with a subspace of $\ell_1$. By Step 2, $X^*$ ha
s the Schur property. By Step 3, $X^*$ is reflexive. By Step 4, $X^*$ is finite-
dimensional, hence $X$ is finite-dimensional.

Conversely, if $X$ is finite-dimensional, then $X^*$ is finite-dimensional and t
rivially embeds into $\ell_1$ (any $n$-dimensional Banach space embeds into $\el
l_1^n \subseteq \ell_1$).

Since by Step 1 there exist infinite-dimensional separable reflexive subspaces o
f $\ell_\infty$ (e.g., an isometric copy of $\ell_2$), for such $X$ the dual $X^
*$ cannot be identified with a subspace of $\ell_1$.

**Answer:** No, not in general. $X^*$ can be identified with a subspace of $\ell
_1$ **if and only if** $X$ is finite-dimensional.
