Looking at the problem, I need to determine whether a Cauchy sequence of step fu
nctions (in the $L^1$ seminorm) that converges a.e. to $f$ must also converge to
 $f$ in $L^1$.

The answer is **yes**, and the key tool is Fatou's lemma applied to the scalar n
on-negative functions $\|f_n - f_m\|_E$.

---

**Proof.**

Recall that the $L^1$ seminorm of a measurable function $g: X \to E$ is
$$\|g\|_1 = \int_X \|g(x)\|_E \, d\mu(x).$$
Since each $f_n$ is a step function (finite image, support of finite measure), e
ach $f_n$ belongs to $L^1(X; E)$. The sequence $(f_n)$ is Cauchy in this seminor
m: for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $N$ such that for all $m, n \geq N$,
$$\int_X \|f_n - f_m\|_E \, d\mu < \varepsilon. \tag{$\ast$}$$

We are given that $f_n \to f$ almost everywhere. Since each $f_n$ is measurable
and the a.e. limit of measurable functions is measurable (after redefining on a
null set), $f$ is measurable. For each fixed $n$, the map $x \mapsto \|f_n(x) -
f_m(x)\|_E$ is a non-negative measurable scalar function, and as $m \to \infty$,
$$\|f_n(x) - f_m(x)\|_E \longrightarrow \|f_n(x) - f(x)\|_E \quad \text{a.e.}$$

**Applying Fatou's lemma.** Fix $n \geq N$. Apply Fatou's lemma to the sequence
of non-negative measurable functions $\bigl(\|f_n - f_m\|_E\bigr)_{m \geq N}$ (i
ndexed by $m$):

$$\int_X \|f_n - f\|_E \, d\mu \;\leq\; \liminf_{m \to \infty} \int_X \|f_n - f_
m\|_E \, d\mu.$$

By $(\ast)$, for all $m \geq N$ (and our fixed $n \geq N$), $\int_X \|f_n - f_m\
|_E \, d\mu < \varepsilon$. Therefore the $\liminf$ over $m \to \infty$ is also
$\leq \varepsilon$:

$$\int_X \|f_n - f\|_E \, d\mu \;\leq\; \varepsilon.$$

**Conclusion.** We have shown: for every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists $N$ suc
h that for all $n \geq N$,
$$\|f_n - f\|_1 = \int_X \|f_n - f\|_E \, d\mu \leq \varepsilon.$$

Hence $f_n \to f$ in the $L^1$ seminorm. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The proof also shows $f \in L^1(X; E)$: for large $n$, $\|f_n - f\|_
1 \leq \varepsilon < \infty$, so $f_n - f \in L^1$, and since $f_n \in L^1$, we
get $f \in L^1$ by the triangle inequality. The argument uses only Fatou's lemma
 for scalar non-negative functions — no vector-valued integration theory beyond
the definition of the Bochner integral of step functions is required.
