## Proof

**Setup.** Let $T$ be a tournament on $n=12$ vertices. For each vertex $v$, let
$d^+(v)$ denote its out-degree. We have $\sum_v d^+(v) = \binom{12}{2} = 66$.

**Key identity.** Every triple of vertices is either:
- **Transitive** (one vertex beats both others), or
- **A directed 3-cycle** (Steady Circuit).

A triple $\{a,b,c\}$ is transitive iff exactly one vertex in the triple has out-
degree 2 within the triple. The number of transitive triples equals $\sum_v \bin
om{d^+(v)}{2}$, because $\binom{d^+(v)}{2}$ counts the pairs of vertices that $v
$ beats, and each such pair $\{a,b\}$ together with $v$ forms a transitive tripl
e where $v$ is the source. (In a 3-cycle, no single vertex beats both others, so
 3-cycles are not counted.)

Therefore:
$$\text{(number of Steady Circuits)} = \binom{12}{3} - \sum_{v} \binom{d^+(v)}{2
} = 220 - \sum_{v} \binom{d^+(v)}{2}.$$

**Minimizing the sum.** To maximize Steady Circuits, we minimize $S = \sum_v \bi
nom{d^+(v)}{2} = \sum_v \frac{d^+(v)(d^+(v)-1)}{2}$.

The function $f(x) = \frac{x(x-1)}{2}$ is **convex** for $x \geq 0$ (since $f''(
x) = 1 > 0$). By Jensen's inequality, $S$ is minimized when the out-degrees are
as equal as possible.

Since $\sum d^+(v) = 66$ and there are 12 vertices, the average out-degree is $6
6/12 = 5.5$. The most balanced integer distribution is:
$$d^+(v) \in \{5, 6\}, \quad \text{with six vertices of out-degree 5 and six of
out-degree 6.}$$

Check: $6 \times 5 + 6 \times 6 = 30 + 36 = 66$. ✓

**Computing the minimum:**
$$S_{\min} = 6\binom{5}{2} + 6\binom{6}{2} = 6 \times 10 + 6 \times 15 = 60 + 90
 = 150.$$

$\binom{a}{2}+\binom{b}{2} > \binom{a+1}{2}+\binom{b-1}{2}$, so balancing always
