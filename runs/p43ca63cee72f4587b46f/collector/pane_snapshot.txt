## Proof

Define $g(z) = f(z) - z$. Since $f$ is entire, so is $g$, and for every integer
$n$,
$$g(n) = f(n) - n = 0.$$
Thus $g$ vanishes at every integer.

**Claim:** $f$ (and hence $g$) is a polynomial.

Consider $h(w) = f(1/w)$ for $w \neq 0$. The hypothesis $\lim_{|z|\to\infty}|f(z
)|=\infty$ means that as $w \to 0$, $|h(w)| \to \infty$. Hence $w=0$ is a pole o
f $h$, i.e., $\infty$ is a pole of the entire function $f$. An entire function w
hose only singularity in $\widehat{\mathbb{C}}$ is a pole at $\infty$ is a polyn
omial. Therefore $f$ is a polynomial, and so is $g = f - z$.

Now $g$ is a polynomial that vanishes at every integer $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, givin
g infinitely many distinct zeros. A nonzero polynomial has only finitely many ro
ots, so $g \equiv 0$.

Therefore $f(z) = z$ for all $z \in \mathbb{C}$.
