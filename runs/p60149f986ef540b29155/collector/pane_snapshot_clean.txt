## Proof

**Claim.** The minimum positive integer $n$ with $2000$ distinct prime factors i
s
$$n = \prod_{i=1}^{2000} p_i = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 \cdot 11 \cdots p_{2000
},$$
the product of the first $2000$ primes (the $2000$th primorial, $p_{2000}\#$).

**Proof.**

Every positive integer with exactly $2000$ distinct prime factors has the form
$$n = q_1^{a_1} q_2^{a_2} \cdots q_{2000}^{a_{2000}},$$
where $q_1 < q_2 < \cdots < q_{2000}$ are distinct primes and each $a_i \geq 1$.

We minimize $n$ over two independent choices: the exponents and the primes.

**Step 1 (Exponents).** Since each $a_i \geq 1$ and every prime $q_i \geq 2$, ra
ising any exponent above $1$ strictly increases $n$. Hence the minimum is achiev
ed when $a_i = 1$ for all $i$, giving $n = q_1 q_2 \cdots q_{2000}$.

**Step 2 (Primes).** Among all choices of $2000$ distinct primes, the product $q
_1 q_2 \cdots q_{2000}$ is minimized by selecting the $2000$ smallest primes. In
deed, suppose some choice uses a prime $q_j = r$ but omits a smaller prime $s <
r$ (where $s$ is not among the chosen primes). Replacing $r$ by $s$ gives a prod
uct smaller by the factor $s/r < 1$, a strict decrease. Repeatedly applying such
 replacements terminates at the set $\{p_1, p_2, \ldots, p_{2000}\} = \{2, 3, 5,
 7, 11, \ldots\}$, the first $2000$ primes. This set is the unique minimizer sin
ce all $p_i$ are distinct and any other set of $2000$ primes has some element la
rger than the corresponding $p_i$.

**Conclusion.** Combining both steps, the minimum is
$$\boxed{n = \prod_{i=1}^{2000} p_i = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 \cdot 11 \cdots
p_{2000} = p_{2000}\#.}$$

This integer is squarefree, has exactly $2000$ distinct prime factors, and is st
rictly smaller than any other positive integer with $2000$ distinct prime factor
s (since any other such integer either uses a larger prime in place of some $p_i
$, or has some exponent $a_i \geq 2$, or both — each of which increases the valu
e).
