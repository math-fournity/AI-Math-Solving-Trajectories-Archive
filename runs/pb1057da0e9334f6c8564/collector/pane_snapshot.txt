## Proof

Let $X_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_i, \sigma_i^2)$ independently for $i = 1,2,3,4$. D
efine the successive differences
$$Y_1 = X_1 - X_2,\qquad Y_2 = X_2 - X_3,\qquad Y_3 = X_3 - X_4.$$
Then
$$P(X_1 > X_2 > X_3 > X_4) = P(Y_1 > 0,\; Y_2 > 0,\; Y_3 > 0).$$

Since each $Y_j$ is a linear combination of independent normals, the vector $\ma
thbf{Y} = (Y_1, Y_2, Y_3)$ is **trivariate normal** with

**Mean vector:**
$$\boldsymbol{\mu}_Y = \begin{pmatrix} \mu_1 - \mu_2 \\ \mu_2 - \mu_3 \\ \mu_3 -
 \mu_4 \end{pmatrix}.$$

**Covariance matrix.** Using independence of the $X_i$:
- $\operatorname{Var}(Y_1) = \sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2$,
- $\operatorname{Var}(Y_2) = \sigma_2^2 + \sigma_3^2$,
- $\operatorname{Var}(Y_3) = \sigma_3^2 + \sigma_4^2$,
- $\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1, Y_2) = \operatorname{Cov}(X_1 - X_2,\, X_2 - X_3) = -
\operatorname{Var}(X_2) = -\sigma_2^2$,
- $\operatorname{Cov}(Y_2, Y_3) = \operatorname{Cov}(X_2 - X_3,\, X_3 - X_4) = -
\operatorname{Var}(X_3) = -\sigma_3^2$,
- $\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1, Y_3) = \operatorname{Cov}(X_1 - X_2,\, X_3 - X_4) = 0
$ (disjoint indices, independence).

Hence
$$\Sigma_Y = \begin{pmatrix} \sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2 & -\sigma_2^2 & 0 \\ -\sigm
a_2^2 & \sigma_2^2 + \sigma_3^2 & -\sigma_3^2 \\ 0 & -\sigma_3^2 & \sigma_3^2 +
\sigma_4^2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

**Final expression.** Let $\Phi_3(\,\cdot\,; \boldsymbol{m}, \Sigma)$ denote the
 trivariate normal CDF with mean $\boldsymbol{m}$ and covariance $\Sigma$. Then
$$\boxed{P(X_1 > X_2 > X_3 > X_4) \;=\; \Phi_3\!\bigl(\mathbf{0};\; \boldsymbol{
\mu}_Y,\; \Sigma_Y\bigr),}$$
i.e. the probability that a $\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_Y, \Sigma_Y)$ vector l

$$P(X_1 > X_2 > X_3 > X_4) = \iiint_{(0,\infty)^3} \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3/2}|\Sigma_
Y|^{1/2}} \exp\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{y} - \boldsymbol{\mu}_Y)^\top \Sigma
_Y^{-1}(\mathbf{y} - \boldsymbol{\mu}_Y)\right) d\mathbf{y}.$$

**Sanity check (i.i.d. case).** If all $X_i$ are i.i.d. (same $\mu$, same $\sigm

$$P = \frac{1}{4!} = \frac{1}{24}.$$
