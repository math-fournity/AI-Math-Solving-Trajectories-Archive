Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $\sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}^d}
|g(x - An)|^2 \leq C\|g\|_{L^2}$ for all $x$, given $g \in L^2(\mathbb{R}^d)$ wi
th compact support and $A$ invertible.

**Answer: No, such a constant $C$ does not exist in general.**

## Proof by Counterexample

Take $d = 1$ and $A = [1]$ (the $1 \times 1$ identity matrix), so $\det A = 1 \n
eq 0$.

Define
$$g(x) = x^{-1/3}\,\mathbf{1}_{(0,\,1/4]}(x).$$

**$g$ has compact support.** Clearly $\operatorname{supp}(g) \subseteq [0, 1/4]$
. ✓

**$g \in L^2(\mathbb{R})$.** We compute
$$\|g\|_{L^2}^2 = \int_0^{1/4} x^{-2/3}\,dx = \left[3\,x^{1/3}\right]_0^{1/4} =
3\cdot(1/4)^{1/3} < \infty.$$
So $\|g\|_{L^2} = \sqrt{3\cdot(1/4)^{1/3}} > 0$, a fixed finite constant. ✓

**Evaluating the sum.** For $x \in (0, 1/4]$, consider $\sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}}
|g(x-n)|^2$. The term $g(x-n)$ is nonzero only when $x - n \in (0, 1/4]$, i.e.,
$n \in [x - 1/4,\, x)$. Since $x \in (0, 1/4]$, this interval lies in $(-1/4,\,
1/4]$, and the only integer in this range is $n = 0$. Therefore, for all $n \neq
 0$, $g(x-n) = 0$, and
$$\sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} |g(x-n)|^2 = |g(x)|^2 = x^{-2/3}, \qquad x \in (0, 1/4
].$$

**The sum is unbounded.** As $x \to 0^+$, we have $x^{-2/3} \to +\infty$. Since
$\|g\|_{L^2}$ is a fixed positive constant, the ratio
$$\frac{\sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} |g(x-n)|^2}{\|g\|_{L^2}} = \frac{x^{-2/3}}{\sqrt
{3\cdot(1/4)^{1/3}}} \to +\infty \quad \text{as } x \to 0^+.$$

Hence no finite constant $C$ can satisfy $\sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} |g(x-n)|^2 \le
q C\,\|g\|_{L^2}$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

## Remark

The key issue is that $g \in L^2$ with compact support does **not** imply $g \in
 L^\infty$. The function $g$ can have integrable (square-integrable) singulariti
es, and when the lattice $A\mathbb{Z}^d$ is such that only a single translate co
ntributes near the singularity (as when $\operatorname{supp}(g)$ fits inside a f
undamental domain of $A\mathbb{Z}^d$), the sum $\sum_n |g(x-An)|^2$ inherits the
 unboundedness of $|g|^2$. A bound of the form $C\|g\|_{L^2}$ would require addi
tional regularity (e.g., $g \in L^\infty$), which is not assumed.
