## Proof

Let $t = \dfrac{x^5}{1+x}\in K(x)$, so $t$ is transcendental over $K$ (since $x$
 is transcendental over $K$ and $K(x)/K(t)$ is algebraic, as shown below). We co
mpute $[K(x):K(t)]$.

**Step 1: $x$ satisfies a degree-5 polynomial over $K(t)$.**

From $t = \dfrac{x^5}{1+x}$ we get $x^5 = t(1+x) = t + tx$, i.e.
$$x^5 - t\,x - t = 0.$$
Thus $x$ is a root of
$$F(T) = T^5 - t\,T - t \in K(t)[T],$$
a polynomial of degree $5$ in $T$. Hence $[K(x):K(t)] \le 5$, and it remains to
show $F$ is irreducible over $K(t)$.

**Step 2: Rewrite $F$ as a polynomial linear in $t$.**

$$F(T) = T^5 - t(T+1) \in K[T,t].$$

**Step 3: Irreducibility in $K[T,t]$.**

View $F$ as a polynomial in the variable $t$ over $K[T]$:
$$F = -(T+1)\,t + T^5 \in K[T][t].$$
This is degree $1$ in $t$. A degree-one polynomial $a(T)\,t + b(T)\in K[T][t]$ i
s irreducible in $K[T][t]$ iff its coefficients $a(T), b(T)$ are coprime in $K[T
]$ (otherwise it factors as a common divisor times a degree-one polynomial). Her
e
$$a(T) = -(T+1), \qquad b(T) = T^5,$$
and $\gcd(T+1,\, T^5) = 1$ since $T+1$ and $T$ are coprime. Therefore $F$ is irr
educible in $K[T][t] = K[T,t]$.

**Step 4: Gauss's lemma gives irreducibility over $K(t)$.**

Since $K[T,t] = K[t][T]$, the polynomial $F(T) = T^5 - tT - t$ is irreducible in
 $K[t][T]$. As a polynomial in $T$ over the UFD $K[t]$, its coefficients are $1,
 -t, -t$, whose gcd in $K[t]$ is $1$; so $F$ is *primitive* in $K[t][T]$. By Gau
ss's lemma, a primitive polynomial that is irreducible over $K[t]$ is irreducibl
e over $K(t)$. Hence $F$ is irreducible in $K(t)[T]$.

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

Since $F(T) = T^5 - tT - t$ is irreducible over $K(t)$ and has $x$ as a root, it
 is the minimal polynomial of $x$ over $K(t)$. Therefore
$$[K(x):K(t)] = \deg F = 5.$$

*(Separability note: $F'(T) = 5T^4 - t$. If $\operatorname{char} K \ne 5$, clear
ly $F'\not\equiv 0$. If $\operatorname{char} K = 5$, then $F'(T) = -t \ne 0$ in
$K(t)$. So $F$ is separable in every characteristic, and $K(x)/K(t)$ is a separa
ble extension of degree $5$.)*
