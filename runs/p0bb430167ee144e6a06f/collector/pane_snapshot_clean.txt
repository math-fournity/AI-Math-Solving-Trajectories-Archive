**Step 1: Characterizing selective semigroups.**

A selective semigroup $(S, \times)$ satisfies $x \times x = x$ (idempotency foll
ows from selectivity) and $x \times y \in \{x, y\}$.

Define $x \geq y$ iff $x \times y = x$ **and** $y \times x = x$ (x dominates y).
 Define $x \sim y$ iff neither $x \geq y$ nor $y \geq x$, which means either $x
\times y = x, y \times x = y$ (left-zero between them) or $x \times y = y, y \ti
mes x = x$ (right-zero between them).

**Claim:** $\geq$ is a total preorder on $S$.

- *Reflexivity:* $x \times x = x$, so $x \geq x$. ✓
- *Totality:* For any $x \neq y$, since $x \times y \in \{x,y\}$ and $y \times x
 \in \{x,y\}$, either one dominates or $x \sim y$. ✓
- *Transitivity:* If $x \geq y$ and $y \geq z$, then $x \times y = x, y \times x
 = x, y \times z = y, z \times y = y$. By associativity: $(x \times y) \times z
= x \times z$ and $x \times (y \times z) = x \times y = x$, so $x \times z = x$.
 Similarly $(z \times y) \times x = y \times x = x$ and $z \times (y \times x) =
 z \times x$, so $z \times x = x$. Thus $x \geq z$. ✓
