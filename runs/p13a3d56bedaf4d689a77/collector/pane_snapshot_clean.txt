## Setup and Reformulation

Each sequence $(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$ uses $n$ of the $n+1$ elements of $X$, so it om
its exactly one element. Define $a_{n+1}$ as the omitted element. This extends e
ach sequence to a **permutation** $\sigma = (a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_n, a_{n+1}) \in
 S_{n+1}$, establishing a bijection between sequences and permutations of $X$.

## Characterizing the Non-Separated Condition

Two sequences are *non-separated* if every shared element appears at the same po
sition in both.

**Case 1 (same omitted element):** Two distinct sequences omitting the same elem
ent share all $n$ elements. If non-separated, every element is at the same posit
ion, so the sequences are identical—contradiction. Thus distinct sequences with
the same omitted element are **always separated**.

**Case 2 (different omitted elements $x \neq y$):** The sequences share $n-1$ el
ements. At each position $i$, either $a_i = b_i$ (shared element at matching pos
ition) or the entries are the respective omitted elements: $a_i = y$ and $b_i =
x$. Since $y$ appears exactly once in the first sequence (at some position $p$)
and $x$ appears exactly once in the second (at some position $q$), the non-separ
ated condition forces $p = q$, and at all other positions $a_i = b_i$.

**Conclusion:** Two sequences omitting different elements are non-separated **if
 and only if** they differ at exactly one position. In permutation terms, $\sigm
a'$ is obtained from $\sigma$ by swapping the values at positions $p$ and $n+1$
for some $p \in [n]$—that is, $\sigma' = \sigma \circ (p,\; n{+}1)$.

## The Conflict Graph

Define a graph $G$ on $S_{n+1}$ where $\sigma, \sigma'$ are adjacent iff they ar
e non-separated, i.e., $\sigma^{-1}\sigma' \in T$ where $T = \{(k, n{+}1) : k \i
n [n]\}$. This is the **Cayley graph** $\operatorname{Cay}(S_{n+1}, T)$.

We seek $\alpha(G)$, the maximum independent set.

## Key Observation: Bipartiteness

Every generator in $T$ is a **transposition**, hence an **odd** permutation. The
refore $\sigma^{-1}\sigma' \in T$ implies $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$ have **opposite
 parity**. The graph $G$ is **bipartite** with parts:
- $A = \{\text{even permutations}\}$, $|A| = \frac{(n+1)!}{2}$
- $B = \{\text{odd permutations}\}$, $|B| = \frac{(n+1)!}{2}$

## Lower Bound: $(n+1)!/2$

The set $A$ of all even permutations is an **independent set**: if $\sigma, \sig
ma' \in A$ are adjacent, then $\sigma^{-1}\sigma' \in T$ is a transposition (odd
), but $\sigma^{-1}\sigma'$ is even (product of two even permutations)—**contrad
iction**. So:
$$\alpha(G) \geq \frac{(n+1)!}{2}$$

## Upper Bound: $(n+1)!/2$

The transposition $(1, n{+}1) \in T$, so for every $\sigma \in S_{n+1}$, the pai
r $\{\sigma,\; \sigma \circ (1, n{+}1)\}$ is an **edge** of $G$. The map $\sigma
 \mapsto \sigma \circ (1, n{+}1)$ is a fixed-point-free involution (since $(1,n{
+}1)^2 = e$ and $\sigma \circ (1,n{+}1) \neq \sigma$), partitioning all $(n+1)!$
 vertices into $\frac{(n+1)!}{2}$ pairs, each forming an edge.

Any independent set contains **at most one vertex per pair**, so:
$$\alpha(G) \leq \frac{(n+1)!}{2}$$

## Conclusion

Combining both bounds:
$$\alpha(G) = \frac{(n+1)!}{2}$$

**Construction achieving the bound:** Take all sequences $(a_1, \ldots, a_n)$ su
ch that the extended permutation $(a_1, \ldots, a_n, a_{n+1})$ is **even**. This
