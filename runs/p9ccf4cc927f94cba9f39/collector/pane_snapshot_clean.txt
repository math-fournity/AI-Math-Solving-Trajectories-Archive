## Solution

Let the junction of the two rods be at $x = 0$. Let rod 1 occupy $x < 0$ (with p
arameters $\rho_1, E_1$) and rod 2 occupy $x > 0$ (with parameters $\rho_2, E_2$
). Denote the longitudinal displacement of a cross-section from its equilibrium
position by $u_1(x,t)$ for $x \le 0$ and $u_2(x,t)$ for $x \ge 0$. Let $A$ be th
e (common) cross-sectional area.

### Derivation of the equation of motion

Consider a small element $[x, x+dx]$ of a homogeneous rod. The longitudinal forc
e (tension) at a cross-section is $N = EA\,\partial u/\partial x$. Newton's seco
nd law for the element gives

$$\rho A\,dx\,\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = \left. EA\frac{\partial u}{\pa
rtial x}\right|_{x+dx} - \left. EA\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right|_{x} = EA\
,\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}\,dx,$$

so within each homogeneous segment

$$\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = c^2\,\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}, \q
quad c = \sqrt{\frac{E}{\rho}}.$$

### The boundary-value problem

**Governing equations (for $t > 0$):**

$$\frac{\partial^2 u_1}{\partial t^2} = c_1^2\,\frac{\partial^2 u_1}{\partial x^
2}, \quad x < 0, \quad c_1 = \sqrt{\frac{E_1}{\rho_1}},$$

$$\frac{\partial^2 u_2}{\partial t^2} = c_2^2\,\frac{\partial^2 u_2}{\partial x^
2}, \quad x > 0, \quad c_2 = \sqrt{\frac{E_2}{\rho_2}}.$$

**Matching (conjugation) conditions at the junction $x = 0$ (for all $t \ge 0$):
**

1. *Continuity of displacement* (the joined ends move together):
$$u_1(0, t) = u_2(0, t).$$

2. *Continuity of force* (Newton's third law at the joint — the internal forces
exerted by each rod on the other are equal and opposite):
$$E_1\,A\,\frac{\partial u_1}{\partial x}\Big|_{x=0} = E_2\,A\,\frac{\partial u_
2}{\partial x}\Big|_{x=0},$$
or, since $A$ is the same for both rods,
$$E_1\,\frac{\partial u_1}{\partial x}(0, t) = E_2\,\frac{\partial u_2}{\partial
 x}(0, t).$$

**Initial conditions (at $t = 0$):** longitudinal displacements and velocities a
re prescribed for every cross-section:

$$u_1(x, 0) = \varphi_1(x), \quad x \le 0,$$
$$u_2(x, 0) = \varphi_2(x), \quad x \ge 0,$$
$$\frac{\partial u_1}{\partial t}(x, 0) = \psi_1(x), \quad x \le 0,$$
$$\frac{\partial u_2}{\partial t}(x, 0) = \psi_2(x), \quad x \ge 0,$$

where $\varphi_1, \varphi_2$ are the given initial displacement functions and $\
psi_1, \psi_2$ the given initial velocity functions. Compatibility at the joint
requires $\varphi_1(0) = \varphi_2(0)$.

**Conditions at infinity (boundedness / radiation):** the solution must remain b
ounded (or, equivalently, no waves arrive from infinity — only waves generated b
y the initial data and reflections/transmissions at the joint propagate outward)
:

$$u_1(x,t) \to 0 \text{ (bounded)} \quad \text{as } x \to -\infty,$$
$$u_2(x,t) \to 0 \text{ (bounded)} \quad \text{as } x \to +\infty.$$

### Summary

The complete boundary-value problem consists of:

- **Two wave equations**, one on each half-line $x<0$ and $x>0$, with wave speed
s $c_1=\sqrt{E_1/\rho_1}$ and $c_2=\sqrt{E_2/\rho_2}$ respectively;
- **Two conjugation conditions at $x=0$**: continuity of displacement $u_1(0,t)=
u_2(0,t)$ and continuity of force $E_1\,u_{1x}(0,t)=E_2\,u_{2x}(0,t)$;
- **Four initial conditions** specifying the prescribed longitudinal displacemen
ts $\varphi_{1,2}(x)$ and velocities $\psi_{1,2}(x)$ at $t=0$ on each half-line;
- **Boundedness conditions** as $x\to\pm\infty$.

This system uniquely determines the deflections $u_1(x,t)$ and $u_2(x,t)$ of the
 composite rod for all $t>0$.
