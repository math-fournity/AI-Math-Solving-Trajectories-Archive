## Solution

We analyze
$$L = \lim_{z\to\infty}\frac{\Gamma(k,iz)}{\Gamma(k)},\qquad z\in\mathbb{R},$$
where $\Gamma(k,w)=\int_w^\infty t^{k-1}e^{-t}\,dt$ is the upper incomplete gamm
a function (defined for complex $w$ by analytic continuation, with the path to $
+\infty$ taken in the sector where $e^{-t}$ decays).

### Asymptotic expansion

The standard asymptotic expansion of the upper incomplete gamma function (see e.
g. DLMF §8.11) is, as $|w|\to\infty$ in the sector $|\arg w|<\tfrac{3\pi}{2}$,
$$
\Gamma(k,w)\sim w^{k-1}e^{-w}\left[1+\frac{k-1}{w}+\frac{(k-1)(k-2)}{w^2}+\cdots
\right].
$$

Set $w=iz$ with $z\to+\infty$ real. Then $\arg(iz)=\tfrac{\pi}{2}$, well inside
the sector of validity, so
$$
\Gamma(k,iz)\sim (iz)^{k-1}e^{-iz}\left[1+\frac{k-1}{iz}+O(z^{-2})\right].
$$
Hence
$$
\frac{\Gamma(k,iz)}{\Gamma(k)}\sim \frac{(iz)^{k-1}e^{-iz}}{\Gamma(k)}\left[1+O(
z^{-1})\right],\qquad z\to+\infty.
$$

### Magnitude of the leading term

For real $z>0$ and real $k$,
$$
\left|(iz)^{k-1}e^{-iz}\right|=|z|^{k-1}\cdot|e^{-iz}|=z^{\,k-1},
$$
since $|e^{-iz}|=|\cos z-i\sin z|=1$. Therefore
$$
\left|\frac{\Gamma(k,iz)}{\Gamma(k)}\right|\sim \frac{z^{k-1}}{|\Gamma(k)|}.
$$

### Case analysis

- **$k>1$:** $z^{k-1}\to\infty$. The modulus grows without bound while the phase
 $e^{-iz}$ rotates indefinitely, so the expression oscillates with diverging amp
litude. **No limit.**

- **$k=1$:** Here $\Gamma(1,w)=e^{-w}$ and $\Gamma(1)=1$, so
  $$\frac{\Gamma(1,iz)}{\Gamma(1)}=e^{-iz}=\cos z-i\sin z,$$
  which oscillates forever on the unit circle. **No limit.**

- **$0<k<1$:** $z^{k-1}=z^{-(1-k)}\to 0$, so the leading term — and with it the
whole asymptotic series — tends to $0$. One may make this rigorous: the full exp
ansion gives $\Gamma(k,iz)=O(z^{k-1})=o(1)$, hence
  $$\lim_{z\to+\infty}\frac{\Gamma(k,iz)}{\Gamma(k)}=0.$$

### Verification for integer $k$ (the typical case)

For $k=n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$, the incomplete gamma has the closed form
$$
\Gamma(n,w)=(n-1)!\,e^{-w}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{w^{j}}{j!},
$$
so
$$
\frac{\Gamma(n,iz)}{\Gamma(n)}=e^{-iz}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{(iz)^{j}}{j!}.
$$
The dominant summand is $e^{-iz}(iz)^{n-1}/(n-1)!$, of modulus $z^{n-1}/(n-1)!$,
 which grows for $n\ge 2$ and stays constant (equal to $1$) for $n=1$, while $e^
{-iz}$ never settles. Thus for every positive integer $n$ the limit **does not e
xist**.

### Conclusion

For the usual range $k\ge 1$ (in particular for every positive integer $k$), the
 factor $e^{-iz}$ oscillates persistently and the prefactor $z^{k-1}$ does not d
ecay, so **the limit does not exist**. The only regime in which a finite limit e
xists is $0<k<1$, where
$$
\boxed{\lim_{z\to+\infty}\frac{\Gamma(k,iz)}{\Gamma(k)}=0\quad(0<k<1).}
$$
For $k\ge 1$ the limit does not exist (the ratio oscillates; it diverges in magn
itude when $k>1$).
