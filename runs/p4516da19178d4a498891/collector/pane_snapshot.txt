## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. Every finite ring with identity $R$ satisfying $\forall a \in R,
\ a+a=0$ can be embedded as a subring of $\mathrm{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{F}_2)
$ for some $n$.

**Step 1: $R$ is an $\mathbb{F}_2$-algebra.**

The condition $a+a=0$ for all $a\in R$ means $2a=0$ for all $a$, so $R$ has char
acteristic $2$. Since $R$ has identity $1$, the prime subring generated by $1$ i
s isomorphic to $\mathbb{F}_2$. The additive group $(R,+)$ is thus an elementary
 abelian $2$-group, i.e., a vector space over $\mathbb{F}_2$. Scalar multiplicat
ion by $\mathbb{F}_2$ is well-defined ($0\cdot a = 0$, $1\cdot a = a$), and the
ring multiplication is $\mathbb{F}_2$-bilinear. Hence $R$ is a finite-dimensiona
l $\mathbb{F}_2$-algebra. Let $n = \dim_{\mathbb{F}_2} R$ (finite since $R$ is f
inite).

**Step 2: The left regular representation.**

Define $\varphi: R \to \mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}_2}(R)$ by $\varphi(r) = L_r$, wh
ere $L_r(x) = rx$ (left multiplication). Since multiplication in $R$ is $\mathbb
{F}_2$-bilinear, each $L_r$ is an $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear endomorphism of $R$. Ide
ntifying $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}_2}(R)$ with $\mathrm{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb
{F}_2)$ (after choosing an $\mathbb{F}_2$-basis of $R$), we view $\varphi$ as a
map $R \to \mathrm{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{F}_2)$.

**Step 3: $\varphi$ is a ring homomorphism.**

For $r,s,x\in R$:
- $\varphi(r+s)(x) = (r+s)x = rx+sx = L_r(x)+L_s(x) = (\varphi(r)+\varphi(s))(x)
$, so $\varphi(r+s)=\varphi(r)+\varphi(s)$.
- $\varphi(rs)(x) = (rs)x = r(sx) = L_r(L_s(x)) = (\varphi(r)\circ\varphi(s))(x)
$, so $\varphi(rs)=\varphi(r)\varphi(s)$.
- $\varphi(1)(x) = 1\cdot x = x = \mathrm{id}_R(x)$, so $\varphi(1) = I_n$.

Thus $\varphi$ is a unital ring homomorphism.

**Step 4: $\varphi$ is injective.**

Suppose $\varphi(r) = 0$, i.e., $L_r$ is the zero map. Then $L_r(1) = r\cdot 1 =
 r = 0$. Hence $\ker\varphi = \{0\}$.

**Conclusion.**

$\varphi$ is an injective unital ring homomorphism $R \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Ma
t}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{F}_2)$, so $R$ is isomorphic to a subring of $\mathrm{Mat
}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{F}_2)$ with $n = \dim_{\mathbb{F}_2} R$. Therefore every f
inite ring with identity satisfying $\forall a\,(a+a=0)$ embeds as a subring of
some matrix ring over $\mathbb{F}_2$.
