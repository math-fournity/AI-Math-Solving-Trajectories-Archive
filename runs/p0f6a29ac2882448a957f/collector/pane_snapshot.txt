## Solution

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains only the problem and proce
dural constraints — no solutions, hints, or formalization notes. Proceeding.

---

**Compute** $\displaystyle I_n=\int_0^{\infty}\!\left(1+\frac{x}{n}\right)^{\!-n
}\sin\!\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)dx$, then $\lim_{n\to\infty} I_n$.

**Substitution.** Set $u=x/n$, so $x=nu$, $dx=n\,du$:

$$I_n \;=\; n\int_0^{\infty}(1+u)^{-n}\sin(u)\,du.$$

We prove $I_n\to 0$ by bounding $|I_n|$ on two regions.

---

**Estimate on $[0,1]$.** For $u\in[0,1]$, since $u^2/2\le u/2$, we have $\ln(1+u
)\ge u-u^2/2\ge u/2$, hence $(1+u)^{-n}\le e^{-nu/2}$. Using $|\sin u|\le u$:

$$n\int_0^1 (1+u)^{-n}|\sin u|\,du \;\le\; n\int_0^1 e^{-nu/2}\,u\,du \;=\; \fra
c{4}{n}\Bigl[1-e^{-n/2}\bigl(1+\tfrac{n}{2}\bigr)\Bigr] \;\le\; \frac{4}{n}.$$

(The last equality is the standard integral $\int_0^a t e^{-t}\,dt = 1-e^{-a}(1+
a)$ after the change $t=nu/2$.)

---

**Estimate on $[1,\infty)$.** For $u\ge 1$ and $n\ge 2$, using $|\sin u|\le 1$:

$$n\int_1^{\infty}(1+u)^{-n}|\sin u|\,du \;\le\; n\int_1^{\infty}(1+u)^{-n}\,du
\;=\; \frac{n}{n-1}\,2^{\,1-n}.$$

This tends to $0$ exponentially.

---

**Combining.** For all $n\ge 2$,

$$|I_n| \;\le\; \frac{4}{n} \;+\; \frac{n\cdot 2^{1-n}}{n-1} \;\xrightarrow{\,n\
to\infty\,}\; 0.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^{\infty}\!\left(1+\frac{x}{n}\right)^{\!-n}\sin
\!\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)dx \;=\; 0.}$$
