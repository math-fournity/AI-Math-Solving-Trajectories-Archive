## Proof

**Definition.** For a non-negative function $f$ on a set $X$, the sum over $X$ i
s defined as
$$\sum_{x \in X} f(x) \;=\; \sup\left\{\sum_{x \in F} f(x) \;:\; F \subseteq X,\
; F \text{ finite}\right\} \;\in\; [0, \infty].$$

**Claim.** Yes. If $\sum_{x \in X} f(x) = \infty$, then there exists a countable
 subset $Y \subseteq X$ with $\sum_{x \in Y} f(x) = \infty$.

**Construction.** Since the total sum is infinite, the supremum over finite-subs
et sums is $+\infty$. Hence for every positive integer $n$, there exists a finit
e set $F_n \subseteq X$ such that
$$\sum_{x \in F_n} f(x) > n.$$
(If no such $F_n$ existed for some $n$, the supremum would be $\leq n$, contradi
cting infinitude of the sum.)

Define
$$Y \;=\; \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} F_n.$$

**$Y$ is countable.** Each $F_n$ is finite, and $Y$ is a countable union of fini
te sets, hence countable.

**The sum over $Y$ is infinite.** Since $F_n \subseteq Y$ for every $n$, and $F_
n$ is a finite subset of $Y$, we have
$$\sum_{x \in Y} f(x) \;=\; \sup\left\{\sum_{x \in G} f(x) \;:\; G \subseteq Y,\
; G \text{ finite}\right\} \;\geq\; \sum_{x \in F_n} f(x) \;>\; n$$
for every $n \geq 1$. A quantity that exceeds every positive integer $n$ must be
 $+\infty$. Therefore
$$\sum_{x \in Y} f(x) = \infty.$$

**Conclusion.** The countable subset $Y = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} F_n$ satisfies
the requirement. The answer to the question is **yes**.
