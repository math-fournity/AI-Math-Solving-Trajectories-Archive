# IMO 1976, Problem 4 — Solution

**Claim.** The largest number expressible as a product of positive integers whos
e sum is $1976$ is
$$\boxed{2 \cdot 3^{658}}.$$

---

## Proof

We must maximize $\prod_{i=1}^{k} a_i$ over all $k \ge 1$ and all positive integ
ers $a_1, \dots, a_k$ with $\sum a_i = 1976$.

### Step 1. No summand equals $1$.

Suppose some $a_i = 1$. Since the sum is $1976 \ge 2$, there is some other summa
nd $a_j \ge 2$. Replacing the pair $(1, a_j)$ by the single summand $a_j + 1$ ke
eps the total sum fixed but changes the product factor from $1 \cdot a_j = a_j$
to $a_j + 1 > a_j$. This strictly increases the product, contradicting maximalit
y. Hence **no summand is $1$** in an optimal decomposition.

### Step 2. No summand is $\ge 5$.

Suppose some $a_i = n \ge 5$. Replace it by the two summands $3$ and $n - 3$ (bo
th $\ge 2$, so still no $1$'s). The sum is unchanged, and the product factor cha
nges from $n$ to $3(n-3) = 3n - 9$. Since $n \ge 5$ we have $3n - 9 - n = 2n - 9
 \ge 1 > 0$, so $3(n-3) > n$, strictly increasing the product. Contradiction. He
nce **every summand is $2$, $3$, or $4$** in an optimal decomposition.

### Step 3. Replace every $4$ by $2 + 2$.

A summand $4$ may be replaced by $2 + 2$: the sum is unchanged and the product f
actor $4 = 2 \cdot 2$ is unchanged. Thus we may assume (without loss of optimali
ty) that **every summand is $2$ or $3$**.

### Step 4. At most two $2$'s.

If there are three or more $2$'s, replace three of them ($2+2+2 = 6$) by two $3$
's ($3+3 = 6$). The sum is unchanged, and the product factor changes from $2^3 =
 8$ to $3^2 = 9 > 8$, strictly increasing the product. Contradiction. Hence **th
ere are at most two $2$'s**.

### Step 5. Determine the optimal decomposition.

Write the sum as $1976 = 3m + 2r$ where $r \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ is the number of $2$
's and $m$ is the number of $3$'s.

- $1976 = 3 \cdot 658 + 2$, so $1976 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$.
- Thus $r = 1$, $m = 658$: we use **$658$ threes and one two**.

Indeed $r$ is forced: $r = 0$ would require $3 \mid 1976$ (false), and $r = 2$ w
ould require $1976 - 4 = 1972$ divisible by $3$, but $1972 = 3 \cdot 657 + 1$ (f
alse). Only $r = 1$ works, giving $m = (1976 - 2)/3 = 658$.

The product is therefore
$$2 \cdot 3^{658}.$$

### Step 6. Verification of optimality.

Steps 1–4 showed that any optimal decomposition uses only $2$'s and $3$'s with a
t most two $2$'s. Step 5 showed the congruence $1976 \equiv 2 \pmod 3$ forces ex
actly one $2$ and $658$ threes. Each transformation in Steps 1, 2, 4 was a *stri
ct* improvement, and Step 3 was *neutral*, so any decomposition not of this form
 is strictly suboptimal. Hence $2 \cdot 3^{658}$ is the unique maximum.

---

The largest number which is the product of positive integers whose sum is $1976$
 is $\,2 \cdot 3^{658}$.
