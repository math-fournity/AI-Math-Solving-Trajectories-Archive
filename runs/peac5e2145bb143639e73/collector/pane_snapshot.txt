## Proof

**Simplify the general term.** Let

$$a_k = \frac{k(k+1)^k}{k^{k-1}}.$$

Combining the powers of $k$ in numerator and denominator:

$$a_k = k^{1-(k-1)} \cdot (k+1)^k = k^{2-k}\cdot(k+1)^k = k^2 \cdot \frac{(k+1)^
k}{k^k} = k^2\left(1+\frac{1}{k}\right)^k.$$

**Apply the divergence test.** The sequence $\left(1+\frac{1}{k}\right)^k$ is cl
assical: it is monotonically increasing and converges to $e$. Since it is increa
sing and $\left(1+\frac{1}{1}\right)^1 = 2$, we have for every $k \geq 1$:

$$\left(1+\frac{1}{k}\right)^k \geq 2.$$

Therefore:

$$a_k = k^2\left(1+\frac{1}{k}\right)^k \geq 2k^2 \xrightarrow{k\to\infty} +\inf
ty.$$

In particular, $a_k \not\to 0$.

**Conclusion.** Since the general term $a_k$ does not converge to zero (in fact
$a_k \to +\infty$), by the **divergence test** (nth-term test) the series

$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{k(k+1)^k}{k^{k-1}}$$

**diverges**. Moreover, since every term is positive and the terms grow without
bound, the series diverges to $+\infty$.
