**Theorem.** Let $L$ be a finite-dimensional unital $\mathbb{C}$-algebra (i.e.,
a ring containing $\mathbb{C}$ with the same identity, finite-dimensional as a $
\mathbb{C}$-vector space). Then $L$ admits a submultiplicative norm.

**Proof.**

**Construction.** Choose any $\mathbb{C}$-vector space norm $\|\cdot\|_0$ on $L$

i\|_0 = \sum |c_i|$. This is well-defined since $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} L = n < \inft

Since $L$ is finite-dimensional, the unit sphere $\{v : \|v\|_0 = 1\}$ is compac
t, so the supremum is finite and attained.

**Verification.**

*Nonnegativity and definiteness.* Clearly $\|a\| \geq 0$. If $\|a\| = 0$, then $
\ell_a = 0$, i.e., $av = 0$ for all $v \in L$. Taking $v = 1_L$ (which exists si

*Triangle inequality.* Since $\ell_{a+b} = \ell_a + \ell_b$,
$$\|a+b\| = \|\ell_a + \ell_b\|_{\mathrm{op}} \leq \|\ell_a\|_{\mathrm{op}} + \|
\ell_b\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \|a\| + \|b\|.$$

*Submultiplicativity.* For $a, b \in L$, associativity gives $\ell_{ab} = \ell_a

$$\|ab\| = \|\ell_a \circ \ell_b\|_{\mathrm{op}} \leq \|\ell_a\|_{\mathrm{op}} \
cdot \|\ell_b\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \|a\| \cdot \|b\|.$$
