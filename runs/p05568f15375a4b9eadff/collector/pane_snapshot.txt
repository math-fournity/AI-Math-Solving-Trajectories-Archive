## Proof

Let the 100 students be $s_1, \ldots, s_{100}$ and the 450 clubs be $C_1, \ldots
, C_{450}$. For each student $s$, let $C(s)$ denote the set of clubs containing
$s$, and let $d_s = |C(s)|$.

**Reformulating the conditions.**

**Condition 1** (any two clubs share ≥ 3 students): Every pair of clubs is conta
ined in at least 3 students' sets, so:
$$\sum_{s} \binom{d_s}{2} \geq 3\binom{450}{2} = 3 \cdot 101025 = 303075. \tag{1
}$$

**Condition 2** (any five clubs share ≤ 1 student): If two students $s \neq s'$
were both in 5 common clubs, those 5 clubs would share ≥ 2 students, violating C
ondition 2. Therefore:
$$|C(s) \cap C(s')| \leq 4 \quad \text{for all } s \neq s'. \tag{2}$$

**Assume for contradiction** that no four clubs have exactly 1 student in common
. Then for every 4-element subset $T \subseteq \{C_1, \ldots, C_{450}\}$, the nu
mber of students containing all clubs in $T$ is either 0 or at least 2.

**Key counting argument.** Fix any student $s$ with $d_s$ clubs. The number of 4
-subsets of $C(s)$ is $\binom{d_s}{4}$. Each such 4-subset $T$ is contained in s
tudent $s$, so it is contained in at least 1 student. By our assumption, it is c
ontained in **at least 2** students, meaning there exists some student $s' \neq
s$ with $T \subseteq C(s')$.

Now, by condition (2), $|C(s) \cap C(s')| \leq 4$. The 4-subsets of $C(s)$ that
are also contained in $C(s')$ are exactly the 4-subsets of $C(s) \cap C(s')$, wh
ich number at most $\binom{4}{4} = 1$.

Therefore, **each other student $s'$ can "cover" at most 1** of the $\binom{d_s}
{4}$ 4-subsets of $C(s)$. Since there are only 99 other students:
$$\binom{d_s}{4} \leq 99 \quad \text{for every student } s. \tag{3}$$

Since $\binom{9}{4} = 126 > 99$ and $\binom{8}{4} = 70 \leq 99$, condition (3) f
orces:
$$d_s \leq 8 \quad \text{for every student } s. \tag{4}$$

**Deriving the contradiction.** With $d_s \leq 8$ for all 100 students:
$$\sum_{s} \binom{d_s}{2} \leq 100 \cdot \binom{8}{2} = 100 \cdot 28 = 2800.$$

But condition (1) requires $\sum_s \binom{d_s}{2} \geq 303075$, and:
$$2800 < 303075.$$

This is a contradiction. Therefore, our assumption that no four clubs have exact
ly 1 student in common is false.

**Conclusion.** It is necessary that some four clubs have exactly 1 student in c
ommon. $\blacksquare$
