### Step 1. Normal form of every element

The group is $\Bbb H_n = \langle S, R\rangle$ with relations
$$R^{2^n}=1,\qquad S^2=-1,\qquad SR=R^{-1}S,\qquad S^{-1}R=R^{-1}S^{-1}.$$

The relation $SR = R^{-1}S$ (and its inverse form $S^{-1}R = R^{-1}S^{-1}$) lets
 us move any occurrence of $S$ (or $S^{-1}$) to the *right* past any power of $R
$, at the cost of inverting the power of $R$. Concretely, by induction
$$S\,R^k = R^{-k}S,\qquad S^{-1}R^k = R^{-k}S^{-1}\qquad\text{for all }k\in\math
bb Z.$$

Hence every word in $S,R$ can be rewritten so that all powers of $R$ appear on t
he left and at most one factor of $S$ (or $S^{-1}$) appears on the right. Since
$S^{-1} = -S$ (because $S^2=-1$, so $S^{-1}=-S$) and $-1 = R^{2^{n-1}}$ (verifie
d below), every $S^{-1}$ can be absorbed into a power of $R$ times $S$. Therefor
e every element of $\Bbb H_n$ has the form
$$R^k \quad\text{or}\quad R^k S\qquad\text{for some }k\in\mathbb Z.$$

### Step 2. Consistency check: $-1 = R^{2^{n-1}}$

Since $\omega$ is a primitive $2^n$-th root of unity, $\omega^{2^{n-1}} = -1$. T
hus
$$R^{2^{n-1}} = \begin{pmatrix}\omega^{2^{n-1}}&0\\0&\omega^{-2^{n-1}}\end{pmatr
ix} = \begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix} = -I.$$

This equals $S^2$, confirming the relations are consistent and that $S^2 = R^{2^
{n-1}}$, so $-1\in\langle R\rangle$ as claimed. In particular $S^4 = (S^2)^2 = (
-I)^2 = I$, so $S$ has order $4$.

### Step 3. The $2^n$ elements $R^k$ are distinct

$R$ has order exactly $2^n$ (since $\omega$ is primitive), so
$$1,\; R,\; R^2,\;\dots,\; R^{2^n-1}$$
are $2^n$ distinct elements.

### Step 4. The $2^n$ elements $R^k S$ are distinct

If $R^k S = R^m S$, right-multiplying by $S^{-1}$ gives $R^k = R^m$, hence $k\eq
uiv m\pmod{2^n}$. So
$$S,\; RS,\; R^2S,\;\dots,\; R^{2^n-1}S$$
are $2^n$ distinct elements.

### Step 5. The two families are disjoint

Every $R^k$ is a *diagonal* matrix $\mathrm{diag}(\omega^k,\omega^{-k})$, while
every $R^k S$ is *off-diagonal*:
$$R^k S = \begin{pmatrix}\omega^k&0\\0&\omega^{-k}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&
-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}0&-\omega^k\\\omega^{-k}&0\end{pmatrix}.$$

A diagonal matrix cannot equal an off-diagonal matrix, so $\{R^k\}\cap\{R^k S\}=
\varnothing$.

### Step 6. Conclusion

Combining Steps 3–5, every element of $\Bbb H_n$ is uniquely of the form $R^k$ (
$0\le k<2^n$) or $R^k S$ ($0\le k<2^n$), and these two families are disjoint. Th
erefore
$$|\Bbb H_n| = 2^n + 2^n = 2^{n+1}.$$

The complete list of elements is
$$\boxed{\Bbb H_n \;=\;\bigl\{\,R^k : 0\le k\le 2^n-1\,\bigr\}\;\cup\;\bigl\{\,R
^k S : 0\le k\le 2^n-1\,\bigr\},\qquad |\Bbb H_n| = 2^{n+1}.}$$

Explicitly, writing each element as a matrix:

- **Diagonal elements** ($2^n$ of them):
$$R^k = \begin{pmatrix}\omega^k&0\\0&\omega^{-k}\end{pmatrix},\qquad k=0,1,\dots
,2^n-1.$$

- **Off-diagonal elements** ($2^n$ of them):
$$R^k S = \begin{pmatrix}0&-\omega^k\\\omega^{-k}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad k=0,1,\d
ots,2^n-1.$$

This is the generalized quaternion group of order $2^{n+1}$, the binary analogue
