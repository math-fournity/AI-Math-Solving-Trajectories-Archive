Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the pointwise condition $\l
im_{h\to 0} \frac{\Delta_h^n f(x)}{h^n} = 0$ for all $x$, with $f$ continuous, f
orces $f$ to be a polynomial of degree $\leq n-1$.

**Answer: No** (for $n \geq 2$). I'll provide an explicit counterexample and ver
ify it, then note the special case $n=1$.

---

## Key Identity

For any polynomial $p$ of degree $< n$, the $n$-th finite difference vanishes id
entically:
$$\Delta_h^n p(x) = \sum_{k=0}^n (-1)^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}\,p(x+kh) = 0 \quad \text
{for all } x,h.$$
In particular, evaluating at $x=0$ with $p(t)=t^j$ ($j < n$):
$$\sum_{k=0}^n (-1)^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}\,k^j = 0 \quad \text{for } j = 0, 1, \dots
, n-1. \tag{$\star$}$$

## Counterexample for $n \geq 2$

Define:
$$f(x) = \begin{cases} x^{n-1} & \text{if } x \geq 0, \\ 0 & \text{if } x < 0. \
end{cases}$$

**Continuity:** Since $n \geq 2$, we have $\lim_{x\to 0^+} x^{n-1} = 0 = f(0)$ a
nd $\lim_{x\to 0^-} 0 = 0 = f(0)$. So $f$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$.

**Verification of the limit condition.** Fix any $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

**Case 1: $x > 0$.** Choose $\delta = x/2$. For $|h| < \delta$, all points $x, x
+h, x+2h, \dots, x+nh$ lie in $(0, \infty)$, where $f(t) = t^{n-1}$, a polynomia
l of degree $n-1 < n$. By the key identity, $\Delta_h^n f(x) = 0$ for all such $
h$. Hence $\frac{\Delta_h^n f(x)}{h^n} \to 0$.

**Case 2: $x < 0$.** Choose $\delta = |x|/2$. For $|h| < \delta$, all points $x+
kh$ ($k=0,\dots,n$) lie in $(-\infty, 0)$, where $f \equiv 0$. Thus $\Delta_h^n
f(x) = 0$ and $\frac{\Delta_h^n f(x)}{h^n} \to 0$.

**Case 3: $x = 0$.** Compute directly:
$$\Delta_h^n f(0) = \sum_{k=0}^n (-1)^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}\,f(kh).$$

- **If $h > 0$:** Then $kh \geq 0$ for all $k \geq 0$, so $f(kh) = (kh)^{n-1}$ f
or $k \geq 1$ and $f(0) = 0$. Thus:
$$\Delta_h^n f(0) = h^{n-1}\sum_{k=1}^n (-1)^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}\,k^{n-1} = h^{n-1
}\sum_{k=0}^n (-1)^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}\,k^{n-1} = h^{n-1}\cdot 0 = 0,$$
where the last equality uses $(\star)$ with $j = n-1 < n$ (and the $k=0$ term co
ntributes $(-1)^n\binom{n}{0}\cdot 0^{n-1} = 0$ since $n \geq 2$).

- **If $h < 0$:** Then $kh \leq 0$ for all $k \geq 0$, so $f(kh) = 0$ for all $k
$. Thus $\Delta_h^n f(0) = 0$.

Therefore $\frac{\Delta_h^n f(0)}{h^n} = 0$ for all $h \neq 0$, and the limit is
 $0$.

**Conclusion of verification:** $\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\Delta_h^n f(x)}{h^n} = 0$ f
or every $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

**$f$ is not a polynomial of degree $\leq n-1$:** On $(-\infty, 0)$, $f \equiv 0
$. Any polynomial that is identically zero on an open interval must be the zero
polynomial. But $f(x) = x^{n-1} \neq 0$ for $x > 0$, so $f$ is not the zero poly
nomial. Hence $f$ is not a polynomial at all, let alone of degree $\leq n-1$.

## The Special Case $n = 1$

For $n = 1$, the answer is **yes**: the condition becomes $\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(
x+h)-f(x)}{h} = 0$ for all $x$, which means $f$ is differentiable everywhere wit
h $f'(x) = 0$. Since $f$ is continuous (hence satisfies the hypotheses of the Me
an Value Theorem on every closed interval), $f$ is constant on $\mathbb{R}$—a po
lynomial of degree $\leq 0$.

## Summary

For $n \geq 2$, $f$ is **not** necessarily a polynomial of degree $\leq n-1$. Th
e function $f(x) = \begin{cases} x^{n-1} & x \geq 0 \\ 0 & x < 0 \end{cases}$ pr
ovides an explicit counterexample: it is continuous, satisfies $\lim_{h\to 0}\fr
ac{\Delta_h^n f(x)}{h^n}=0$ for every $x$ (being locally a degree-$(n{-}1)$ poly
nomial away from the origin, and having $\Delta_h^n f(0)=0$ identically by the f
inite-difference identity $(\star)$), yet is not a polynomial.

The essential reason the counterexample works is that the condition is **pointwi
se**: it controls the behavior at each fixed $x$ separately, but does not enforc
e any **uniformity** across $x$. A piecewise polynomial of degree $n-1$ can sati
sfy the pointwise condition at every point—including the junction—because the $n
$-th finite difference of any degree-$(n{-}1)$ polynomial vanishes, and at the j
unction point the algebraic identity $(\star)$ ensures exact cancellation.
