## Proof

**Notation.** Let $D(n)=f(n)$ denote the last nonzero digit of $n!$. Since $v_2(
n!)>v_5(n!)$ for all $n\ge 2$, we have $n!=2^{v_2(n!)}\,5^{v_5(n!)}\,P(n)$ where
 $P(n)=\prod_{i=1}^n i'$ with $i'=i$ stripped of all factors of $2$ and $5$ (so
$P(n)$ is coprime to $10$). Thus
$$D(n)\equiv 2^{\,v_2(n!)-v_5(n!)}\,P(n)\pmod{10}.$$
For $n\ge 2$ this is even, and an even residue mod $10$ is uniquely determined b
y its residue mod $5$. Hence it suffices to compute $D(n)\pmod 5$.

### A recursion for $D(n)\pmod 5$

Write $n=5q+r$ with $0\le r\le 4$. Observe that $(5j)'=j'$ (removing the single
factor of $5$ from $5j$ leaves $j$ with its 2's and further 5's removed). Hence
$$P(n)=P(q)\cdot Q(n),\qquad Q(n)=\prod_{\substack{1\le i\le n\\5\nmid i}} i'.$$
Also $v_5(n!)=q+v_5(q!)$ and $v_2(n!)=v_2(q!)+W(n)$ where $W(n)=\sum_{5\nmid i\l
e n}v_2(i)$. Setting $E(n)=v_2(n!)-v_5(n!)$,
$$E(n)=E(q)+W(n)-q.$$
Therefore
$$D(n)\equiv D(q)\cdot 2^{W(n)-q}\cdot Q(n)\pmod 5.$$
Now $2^{W(n)}Q(n)=\prod_{5\nmid i\le n}2^{v_2(i)}\cdot i/2^{v_2(i)}=\prod_{5\nmi
d i\le n}i=:S(n)\pmod 5$, so
$$D(n)\equiv D(q)\cdot 2^{-q}\cdot S(n)\pmod 5.$$
The product $S(n)$ over each complete block $\{5j+1,\dots,5j+4\}$ is $\equiv 1\c
dot2\cdot3\cdot4\equiv -1\pmod 5$, giving
$$S(n)\equiv (-1)^q\cdot r!\pmod 5.$$
Since $(-2)^{-1}\equiv 2\pmod 5$, we get $2^{-q}(-1)^q=(-2)^{-q}\equiv 2^q\pmod
5$, and thus

$$\boxed{D(n)\equiv D(q)\cdot 2^q\cdot r!\pmod 5},\qquad q=\lfloor n/5\rfloor,\;
 r=n\bmod 5.\tag{$\star$}$$

**Verification.** $D(5)\equiv D(1)\cdot 2\cdot 0!=2\pmod5\Rightarrow D(5)=2$ ✓.
$D(10)\equiv D(2)\cdot 4\cdot 0!\equiv 2\cdot4=8\equiv3\pmod5\Rightarrow D(10)=8
$ ✓. $D(7)\equiv D(1)\cdot2\cdot2!=4\pmod5\Rightarrow D(7)=4$ ✓.

### Closed form

Write $n$ in base $5$: $n=r_0+r_1\cdot5+\cdots+r_m\cdot5^m$. Iterating $(\star)$
 with $q_0=n,\;q_{i+1}=\lfloor q_i/5\rfloor,\;r_i=q_i\bmod 5$:
$$D(n)\equiv\prod_{i\ge0}2^{q_{i+1}}\,r_i!\;=\;2^{\,\sum_{j\ge1}\lfloor n/5^j\rf

### Part (a)

Let $n=5^{a_1}+5^{a_2}+\cdots+5^{a_k}$ with the $a_i$ **distinct** nonnegative i
ntegers, and set $s=a_1+\cdots+a_k$.

Since the $a_i$ are distinct, the base-$5$ expansion of $n$ has digits $r_j\in\{
0,1\}$ (namely $r_j=1$ iff $j\in\{a_1,\dots,a_k\}$). Because $0!=1!=1$,
$$\prod_i r_i! = 1.$$
By $(\star\star)$, $D(n)\equiv 2^{v_5(n!)}\pmod 5$. Now
$$v_5(n!)=\sum_{j\ge1}\Big\lfloor\frac{n}{5^j}\Big\rfloor=\sum_{i=1}^k\sum_{j=1}
^{a_i}5^{a_i-j}=\sum_{i=1}^k\frac{5^{a_i}-1}{4}.$$
To reduce this modulo $4$ (the order of $2$ mod $5$), expand $5^{a_i}=(4+1)^{a_i
}=1+4a_i+16\binom{a_i}{2}+\cdots$, so
$$\frac{5^{a_i}-1}{4}=a_i+4\binom{a_i}{2}+\cdots\equiv a_i\pmod 4.$$
Therefore
$$v_5(n!)\equiv\sum_{i=1}^k a_i=s\pmod 4,$$
and consequently
$$D(n)\equiv 2^{\,v_5(n!)}\equiv 2^s\pmod 5.\tag{$\dagger$}$$

For $s\ge 1$, at least one $a_i\ge1$, so $n\ge 5\ge 2$, making $D(n)$ even. An e
ven digit is uniquely determined by its residue mod $5$, so $f(n)=D(n)$ is compl
etely determined by $s$. For $s=0$ the only possibility is $k=1,a_1=0,n=1$, givi
ng $f(1)=1$, also consistent with $2^0\equiv1\pmod5$.

Hence $f(5^{a_1}+\cdots+5^{a_k})$ depends only on $s=a_1+\cdots+a_k$. $\quad\bla
cksquare$

### Part (b)

By $(\dagger)$, for $s\ge 1$ the function $g(s)$ is the unique **even** digit co
ngruent to $2^s\pmod 5$:
$$\begin{array}{c|cccc}
s\bmod 4 & 1&2&3&0\\ \hline
2^s\bmod 5&2&4&3&1\\
g(s)&2&4&8&6
\end{array}$$
Explicitly: $g(1)=2,\;g(2)=4,\;g(3)=8,\;g(4)=6,\;g(5)=2,\;\dots$

Since $2^s\pmod 5$ has period $4$, we have $g(s+4)=g(s)$ for every $s\ge 1$. For
 $p=1,2,3$ we have $g(1)\ne g(1+p)$ (namely $2\ne4,2\ne8,2\ne6$), so no smaller
period works.

The least positive integer $p$ with $g(s)=g(s+p)$ for all $s\ge1$ is

$$\boxed{p=4}.$$

$\blacksquare$
