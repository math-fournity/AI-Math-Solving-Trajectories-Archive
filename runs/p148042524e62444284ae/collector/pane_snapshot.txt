## Proof

**Part 1: Nilpotent case — YES, A and B must be similar.**

Let A be nilpotent over a field (say ℂ, or any field). Its Jordan canonical form
 is a direct sum of Jordan blocks J_{s₁}, J_{s₂}, …, J_{s_m} with eigenvalue 0,
where s₁ ≥ s₂ ≥ … ≥ s_m is a partition of n = dim. Two nilpotent matrices are si
milar iff they have the same Jordan form, i.e., the same partition.

Define, for each k ≥ 0:
- r_k = rank(A^k), with r_0 = n (since A^0 = I).
- The number of Jordan blocks of size **at least** k is
  $$n_k = r_{k-1} - r_k.$$

*Justification:* A^k kills every Jordan block J_s with s ≤ k (contributes 0 to r
ank) and reduces every block J_s with s > k to a matrix of rank s − k (the super
diagonal chain shifted). Hence
$$r_k = \sum_{s_i > k} (s_i - k).$$
Therefore
$$r_{k-1} - r_k = \sum_{s_i > k-1}(s_i - (k-1)) - \sum_{s_i>k}(s_i-k) = \sum_{s_
i \ge k} 1 = \#\{i : s_i \ge k\} = n_k.$$

So the sequence (r_0, r_1, r_2, …) determines every n_k, and the n_k in turn det
ermine the partition: the number of blocks of size **exactly** k is n_k − n_{k+1
}. Thus the entire Jordan form (the partition) is uniquely determined by the ran
ks of the powers.

Since rank(A^k) = rank(B^k) for all k ≥ 1, and r_0 = n is common (same size), th
e partitions for A and B coincide. Hence A and B have identical Jordan forms and
 are similar. ∎

**Part 2: Non-nilpotent case — NO, the conclusion fails.**

The ranks of powers detect only the *nilpotent part* of the Jordan structure; th
ey are blind to the actual eigenvalues (as long as an eigenvalue is nonzero, its
 Jordan blocks contribute full rank to every power).

**Counterexample.** Take
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = I_2, \qquad B = \begin{pmat
rix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

For every k ≥ 1:
- A^k = I_2, so rank(A^k) = 2.
- B^k = diag(1, 2^k), which is invertible, so rank(B^k) = 2.

Thus rank(A^k) = rank(B^k) = 2 for all k ≥ 1. Yet A and B are not similar: A has
 eigenvalues {1, 1} while B has eigenvalues {1, 2}, and similar matrices have th
e same eigenvalues with the same multiplicities.

(Even more starkly: A = I_2 and B = diag(1, 2) both have all powers of full rank
 2, but they are plainly not conjugate.)

Therefore, when the nilpotency hypothesis is dropped, equality of the ranks of a
ll powers does **not** force similarity. The rank sequence captures the sizes of
 the nilpotent Jordan blocks but loses all information about the nonzero eigenva
lues.

**Summary.**
- Nilpotent A, B with rank(A^k) = rank(B^k) for all k ≥ 1 ⟹ A ~ B. (Yes.)
- Without nilpotency: the same conclusion **fails**; ranks of powers do not dete
rmine the spectrum, so similarity is not forced.
