# Proof

**Claim:** Yes. The theorem that every dense ideal in a $C^*$-algebra admits an
approximate unit implies that every $C^*$-algebra has an approximate unit.

**Proof.**

Let $A$ be an arbitrary $C^*$-algebra. We show that the hypotheses of the given
theorem are satisfied with the choice $I = A$.

1. **$A$ is a two-sided ideal of $A$.** For any $a, b \in A$, we have $ab \in A$
 and $ba \in A$ (since $A$ is closed under its own multiplication). Thus $A$ is
a two-sided ideal (in fact, the unit ideal) of itself. This holds whether "ideal
" is interpreted as a closed two-sided ideal or merely an algebraic two-sided id
eal, since $A$ is closed in itself.

2. **$A$ is dense in $A$.** The closure of $A$ in $A$ is $A$ itself: $\overline{
A} = A$. Hence $A$ is dense in $A$.

Since $I = A$ is an ideal of $A$ that is dense in $A$, the theorem applies direc
tly. We conclude that $A$ admits an approximate unit.

Since $A$ was an arbitrary $C^*$-algebra, it follows that **every $C^*$-algebra
admits an approximate unit**. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The implication is immediate because the statement of the theorem pl
aces no restriction that the dense ideal be *proper* (i.e., $I \neq A$). The alg
ebra $A$ is trivially both an ideal of itself and dense in itself, so the theore
m specializes to the desired conclusion by setting $I = A$. Had the theorem been
 restricted to *proper* dense ideals, the implication would not follow so direct
ly (for instance, $A = \mathbb{C}$ admits no proper dense ideals at all), and a
separate argument would be required.
