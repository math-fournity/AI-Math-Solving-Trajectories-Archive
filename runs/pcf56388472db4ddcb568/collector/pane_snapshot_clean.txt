# Derivation of the Inverse Radon Transform (Parallel Beam)

## 1. Setup and Definitions

Let \(f(x,y)\) be a sufficiently nice (e.g. compactly supported, continuous) ima
ge on \(\mathbb{R}^2\). The **parallel-beam Radon transform** is the line integr
al of \(f\) along the line making angle \(\theta\) with the \(x\)-axis, at signe
d distance \(l\) from the origin:

\[
R(l,\theta) \;=\; \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f\!\bigl(l\cos\theta - s\sin\theta,\;
l\sin\theta + s\cos\theta\bigr)\,ds .
\]

A point on this line is parameterized as
\((x,y) = (l\cos\theta - s\sin\theta,\; l\sin\theta + s\cos\theta)\),
i.e. \((l,s)\) are coordinates rotated by \(\theta\). Equivalently, fixing \(\th
eta\), the projection \(R(\cdot,\theta)\) is the integral of \(f\) along the dir
ection \((-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)\).

Define the **1D Fourier transform of the projection** (in the \(l\) variable):

\[
\widehat{R}_\theta(k) \;=\; \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} R(l,\theta)\,e^{-i2\pi k l}\
,dl .
\]

Define the **2D Fourier transform of \(f\)**:

\[
F(u,v) \;=\; \int_{\mathbb{R}^2} f(x,y)\,e^{-i2\pi(ux+vy)}\,dx\,dy .
\]

Our goal: recover \(f(x,y)\) from \(R(l,\theta)\).

---

## 2. The Projection–Slice Theorem

**Claim.** \(\widehat{R}_\theta(k) = F(k\cos\theta,\; k\sin\theta)\).

*Proof.* Substitute the definition of \(R\) into \(\widehat{R}_\theta\):

\[
\widehat{R}_\theta(k)
= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(l\cos\theta - s\sin\t
heta,\, l\sin\theta + s\cos\theta)\,ds\right] e^{-i2\pi k l}\,dl .
\]

Change variables from \((l,s)\) to \((x,y)\) via the rotation
\[
x = l\cos\theta - s\sin\theta,\qquad y = l\sin\theta + s\cos\theta .
\]
This is an orthogonal rotation, so \(dx\,dy = dl\,ds\) (Jacobian \(=1\)). Also
\[
l = x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta .
\]
Hence

\[
\widehat{R}_\theta(k)
= \int_{\mathbb{R}^2} f(x,y)\,e^{-i2\pi k (x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta)}\,dx\,dy
= F(k\cos\theta,\, k\sin\theta). \qquad\blacksquare
\]

**Interpretation.** The 1D Fourier transform of the projection at angle \(\theta
\) equals the 2D Fourier transform of \(f\) evaluated along the radial slice thr
ough the origin at angle \(\theta\) in frequency space. Sweeping \(\theta\in[0,\
pi)\) covers all of frequency space (each direction appears once, since \(R(l,\t
heta+\pi)=R(-l,\theta)\)).

---

## 3. Inverting via the 2D Inverse Fourier Transform

The inverse 2D Fourier transform is

\[
f(x,y) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^2} F(u,v)\,e^{i2\pi(ux+vy)}\,du\,dv .
\]

Convert to polar frequency coordinates \((u,v)=(k\cos\theta,\,k\sin\theta)\), wi
th Jacobian \(du\,dv = |k|\,dk\,d\theta\):

\[
f(x,y) = \int_{0}^{\pi}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} F(k\cos\theta,\,k\sin\theta)\,e
^{i2\pi k(x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta)}\,|k|\,dk\,d\theta .
\]

(We integrate \(\theta\) over \([0,\pi)\) and \(k\) over \((-\infty,\infty)\); t
his covers the plane once, with the factor \(|k|\) from the polar Jacobian.)

Apply the projection–slice theorem \(F(k\cos\theta,k\sin\theta)=\widehat{R}_\the
ta(k)\):

\[
\boxed{\;f(x,y) = \int_{0}^{\pi}\!\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \widehat{R}_\the
ta(k)\,|k|\,e^{i2\pi k(x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta)}\,dk\right]d\theta .\;}
\]

---

## 4. The Filtered Back-Projection Form

Define the **filtered projection** (a function of the spatial variable \(l\)):

\[
G(l,\theta) \;=\; \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \widehat{R}_\theta(k)\,|k|\,e^{i2\pi k
 l}\,dk
\;=\; \mathcal{F}^{-1}_{1D}\!\bigl\{\,|k|\,\widehat{R}_\theta(k)\,\bigr\}(l).
\]

That is, \(G(\cdot,\theta)\) is the projection \(R(\cdot,\theta)\) convolved wit
h the inverse Fourier transform of the **ramp filter** \(|k|\). Then the reconst
ruction becomes the **back-projection** of the filtered projections:

\[
\boxed{\;f(x,y) \;=\; \int_{0}^{\pi} G\!\bigl(x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta,\;\theta
\bigr)\,d\theta .\;}
\]

This is the **filtered back-projection (FBP) formula**: filter each projection w
ith the ramp filter \(|k|\) in frequency space, then back-project (integrate ove
r all angles) the filtered value at the point \((x,y)\)'s signed distance \(l=x\
cos\theta+y\sin\theta\).

---

## 5. Convolution Form of the Ramp Filter

The ramp filter \(|k|\) in the spatial domain is a distribution. To express \(G\
) as an explicit convolution with \(R\), use the identity (in the distributional
 sense, with a suitable regularization / windowing for practical implementation)

\[
\mathcal{F}^{-1}\{|k|\}(l) \;=\; -\frac{1}{2\pi^{2} l^{2}}\quad\text{(principal
value)},
\]

equivalently obtained from \(|k| = 2\pi k\cdot \tfrac{\operatorname{sgn}(k)}{2\p
i}\) and \(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\{\operatorname{sgn}(k)\}(l)=\tfrac{1}{i\pi l}\), tog
ether with the derivative property. Concretely, since \(\mathcal{F}\{h'(l)\}(k)=
i2\pi k\,\widehat{h}(k)\),

\[
|k|\,\widehat{R}_\theta(k) \;=\; \frac{1}{i2\pi}\,\mathcal{F}\!\left\{\frac{\par
tial}{\partial l}H(l,\theta)\right\}(k),
\qquad
H(l,\theta)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\{\operatorname{sgn}(k)\widehat{R}_\theta(k)\}(l).
\]

A cleaner, standard regularization: define the ramp-filtered projection as a **H
ilbert-transform-based** convolution. Using \(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\{\operatorname{sg
n}(k)\}(l)=\frac{1}{i\pi l}\) and \(|k| = k\cdot\operatorname{sgn}(k)\), one obt
ains the **convolution-based reconstruction**:

\[
\boxed{\;
f(x,y) \;=\; \int_{0}^{\pi}\!\left[\,R(\cdot,\theta) * h_{\mathrm{ramp}}\,\right
]\!\bigl(x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta\bigr)\,d\theta,
\;}
\]

where the ramp-filter kernel (in the principal-value / regularized sense) is

\[
h_{\mathrm{ramp}}(l) \;=\; \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|k|\,e^{i2\pi k l}\,dk,
\]

and, equivalently, writing the filtering as a derivative of the Hilbert transfor
m,

\[
\bigl[R(\cdot,\theta)*h_{\mathrm{ramp}}\bigr](l)
= -\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial l}\!\left(\mathrm{p.v.}\!\int_{-
\infty}^{\infty}\frac{R(t,\theta)}{l-t}\,dt\right)
= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} R(t,\theta)\,h_{\mathrm{ramp}}(l-t)\,dt .
\]

In practice one band-limits \(|k|\) to \(|k|\le K\) (Ram–Lak filter \(h_{\mathrm
{RL}}(l)=K^{2}\,\mathrm{sinc}(Kl)-\tfrac{K^{2}}{2}\mathrm{sinc}^{2}(Kl)\), or Sh
epp–Logan, cosine, Hann windows) to make the kernel an ordinary function and to
handle the singularity at \(l=0\).

---

## 6. Summary of the Logical Chain

1. **Radon transform** \(R(l,\theta)\): line integrals of \(f\) along parallel b
eams at angle \(\theta\).
2. **Projection–slice theorem**: \(\widehat{R}_\theta(k)=F(k\cos\theta,k\sin\the
ta)\) — proved by the rotation change of variables \((l,s)\to(x,y)\).
3. **Inverse 2D FT in polar coordinates**: introduces the Jacobian factor \(|k|\
), giving
   \(f(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\pi}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{R}_\theta(k)|k|e^{i2
\pi k(x\cos\theta+y\sin\theta)}\,dk\,d\theta\).
4. **Filtered back-projection**: the inner integral is the inverse 1D FT of \(|k
|\widehat{R}_\theta(k)\), i.e. the ramp-filtered projection \(G(l,\theta)\); the
 outer integral back-projects it:
   \(f(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\pi}G(x\cos\theta+y\sin\theta,\theta)\,d\theta\).
5. **Convolution form**: the ramp filter \(|k|\) corresponds (in the principal-v
alue sense) to the kernel \(h_{\mathrm{ramp}}(l)=-\tfrac{1}{2\pi^{2}l^{2}}\), or
 equivalently to a derivative of the Hilbert transform of the projection, yieldi
ng the final convolution-based reconstruction formula. Band-limiting regularizes
 the kernel for numerical use.

This completes the derivation of the inverse Radon transform for parallel-beam g
eometry, from the projection–slice theorem through Fourier inversion to the filt
ered back-projection / convolution reconstruction formula.
