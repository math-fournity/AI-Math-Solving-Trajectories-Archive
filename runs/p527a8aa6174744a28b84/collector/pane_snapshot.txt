## The Condition

**Necessary and sufficient condition:** For every $j \in \{1,\ldots,m\}$ and eve
ry $\lambda = (\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m) \in \mathbb{R}^m_+$ with $\sum_i \lam
bda_i = 1$ and $\sum_i \lambda_i u_i = u_j$, we have
$$y_j \;\leq\; \sum_{i=1}^m \lambda_i\, y_i. \tag{$\star$}$$

Equivalently, defining the **lower convex envelope**
$$h(x) \;=\; \min\Bigl\{\,\textstyle\sum_i \lambda_i\, y_i \;:\; \lambda \geq 0,
\;\sum_i \lambda_i = 1,\;\sum_i \lambda_i u_i = x\Bigr\}, \qquad x \in P:=\opera
torname{conv}\{u_1,\ldots,u_m\},$$

bove the lower convex envelope of the data).

## Proof of Necessity

Suppose a convex $f:\mathbb{R}^k\to\mathbb{R}$ with $f(u_i)=y_i$ exists. Take an
y $j$ and any $\lambda\geq 0$, $\sum\lambda_i=1$, $\sum\lambda_i u_i = u_j$. By
convexity of $f$:
$$y_j = f(u_j) = f\!\Bigl(\sum_i \lambda_i u_i\Bigr) \;\leq\; \sum_i \lambda_i\,
 f(u_i) = \sum_i \lambda_i\, y_i.$$
This is exactly $(\star)$. $\square$

## Proof of Sufficiency

Assume $(\star)$ holds. We construct a finite convex $f:\mathbb{R}^k\to\mathbb{R

**Step 1: The lower convex envelope $h$ passes through every data point.**

The function $h$ is well-defined on the polytope $P = \operatorname{conv}\{u_i\}

0$ for $i\neq j$ gives $h(u_j) \leq y_j$. Condition $(\star)$ says that every fe
asible $\lambda$ with $\sum\lambda_i u_i = u_j$ satisfies $\sum\lambda_i y_i \ge

$$h(u_j) = y_j \quad\text{for all } j=1,\ldots,m. \tag{1}$$

**Step 2: $h$ is polyhedral convex on $P$.**

The epigraph of $h$ is:
$$\operatorname{epi}(h) = \bigl\{(x,t)\in P\times\mathbb{R} : \exists\,\lambda\g
eq 0,\;\sum\lambda_i=1,\;U\lambda=x,\;y^\top\lambda \leq t\bigr\},$$
where $U=[u_1|\cdots|u_m]\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times m}$. This is the projection of t
he polyhedron $\{(\lambda,x,t): \lambda\geq 0,\;\mathbf{1}^\top\lambda=1,\;U\lam
bda=x,\;y^\top\lambda\leq t\}$ onto the $(x,t)$-coordinates, hence is itself a p
olyhedron. Therefore $h$ is a **polyhedral convex function**: on $P$ it can be w
ritten as
$$h(x) = \max\{\ell_1(x),\;\ell_2(x),\;\ldots,\;\ell_N(x)\}, \qquad x\in P, \tag
{2}$$
where each $\ell_j:\mathbb{R}^k\to\mathbb{R}$ is an affine function. (These corr
espond to the facets of $\operatorname{epi}(h)$; if $P$ is lower-dimensional, th

**Step 3: Extension to all of $\mathbb{R}^k$.**

Define
$$f(x) \;=\; \max\{\ell_1(x),\;\ell_2(x),\;\ldots,\;\ell_N(x)\}, \qquad x\in\mat
hbb{R}^k.$$

- **Convexity:** $f$ is the pointwise maximum of finitely many affine (hence con
vex) functions, so $f$ is convex.
- **Finite everywhere ($\operatorname{dom}f = \mathbb{R}^k$):** Each $\ell_j$ is

- **Interpolation:** For each $u_i \in P$, by (2) we have $f(u_i) = h(u_i) = y_i

Therefore $f$ is a convex function with $\operatorname{dom}f = \mathbb{R}^k$ sat
isfying $f(u_i)=y_i$ for all $i=1,\ldots,m$. $\square$
