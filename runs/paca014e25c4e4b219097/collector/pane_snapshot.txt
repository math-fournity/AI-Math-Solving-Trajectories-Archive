### Key Theoretical Fact

For a covering space $p: \tilde{X} \to X$ with $p(\tilde{x}_1) = p(\tilde{x}_2)
= x$, the subgroups $p_*(\pi_1(\tilde{X}, \tilde{x}_1))$ and $p_*(\pi_1(\tilde{X
}, \tilde{x}_2))$ are **conjugate** in $\pi_1(X, x)$, but need not be **equal**.

Specifically, if $\tilde{\gamma}$ is any path in $\tilde{X}$ from $\tilde{x}_1$
to $\tilde{x}_2$, then $\gamma = p \circ \tilde{\gamma}$ is a loop in $X$ based
at $x$, and:

$$p_*(\pi_1(\tilde{X}, \tilde{x}_2)) = [\gamma]^{-1} \cdot p_*(\pi_1(\tilde{X},
\tilde{x}_1)) \cdot [\gamma].$$

These subgroups are equal for all choices of $\tilde{x}_2$ in the fiber if and o
nly if $p_*(\pi_1(\tilde{X}, \tilde{x}_1))$ is a **normal** subgroup of $\pi_1(X
, x)$.

### Counterexample

Let $X = S^1 \vee S^1$ (the wedge of two circles), so $\pi_1(X, x) \cong F_2 = \
langle a, b \rangle$, the free group on two generators. This group is non-abelia
n.

Take the subgroup $H = \langle a \rangle = \{a^n : n \in \mathbb{Z}\} \leq F_2$.
 By the Galois correspondence for covering spaces, there exists a covering space
 $p: \tilde{X} \to X$ (with $\tilde{X}$ path-connected) corresponding to $H$, su
ch that for a chosen basepoint $\tilde{x}_1 \in p^{-1}(x)$:

$$p_*(\pi_1(\tilde{X}, \tilde{x}_1)) = H = \langle a \rangle.$$

The fiber $p^{-1}(x)$ is in bijection with the right cosets $\{gH : g \in F_2\}$
. Let $\tilde{x}_2 \in p^{-1}(x)$ be the point corresponding to the coset $bH$.
By the standard theory of covering spaces:

$$p_*(\pi_1(\tilde{X}, \tilde{x}_2)) = bHb^{-1} = \langle bab^{-1} \rangle.$$

### Verifying the Subgroups Differ

We claim $\langle a \rangle \neq \langle bab^{-1} \rangle$ in $F_2$.

In the free group $F_2 = \langle a, b \rangle$, the element $bab^{-1}$ is a **re
duced word** of length 3. It is not equal to any power $a^k$ (which has length $
|k|$ and involves only the letter $a$). By the uniqueness of reduced words in a
free group, $bab^{-1} \notin \langle a \rangle$.

Since $bab^{-1} \notin \langle a \rangle = H$, we have $\langle bab^{-1} \rangle
 \neq \langle a \rangle$.

### Conclusion

$$p_*(\pi_1(\tilde{X}, \tilde{x}_1)) = \langle a \rangle \neq \langle bab^{-1} \
rangle = p_*(\pi_1(\tilde{X}, \tilde{x}_2)),$$

even though $p(\tilde{x}_1) = p(\tilde{x}_2) = x$ and both $\tilde{X}$ and $X$ a
re path-connected. Therefore, the equality $p_*(\pi_1(\tilde{X}, x_1)) = p_*(\pi
_1(\tilde{X}, x_2))$ is **not always true**. It holds precisely when the subgrou
p $p_*(\pi_1(\tilde{X}, x_1))$ is normal in $\pi_1(X, x)$ (i.e., when the coveri
ng is regular/normal).
