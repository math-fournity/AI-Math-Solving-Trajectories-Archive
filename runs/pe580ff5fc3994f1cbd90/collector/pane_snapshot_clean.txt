## Proof

Write $T = I + K$ where $(Kf)(t) = \int_0^t (1-2s)f(s)\,ds$. For each fixed $t \
in [0,1]$, the map $f \mapsto (Tf)(t)$ is the bounded linear functional
$$L_t(f) = f(t) + \int_0^t (1-2s)f(s)\,ds.$$

By the Riesz representation theorem for $C[0,1]$, $L_t(f) = \int f\,d\mu_t$ wher
e $\mu_t$ is the signed Borel measure
$$\mu_t = (1-2s)\,\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}(s)\,ds \;+\; \delta_t,$$
i.e. an absolutely continuous part with density $(1-2s)$ on $[0,t]$ plus a point
 mass of weight $1$ at $t$. The norm of $L_t$ equals the total variation of $\mu
_t$:
$$\|L_t\| = \|\mu_t\|_{\mathrm{TV}} = \int_0^t |1-2s|\,ds \;+\; 1.$$

Set $g(t) = \int_0^t |1-2s|\,ds$. Since $1-2s$ vanishes at $s = \tfrac12$:
- For $0 \le t \le \tfrac12$: $g(t) = \int_0^t (1-2s)\,ds = t - t^2$.
- For $\tfrac12 \le t \le 1$: $g(t) = \int_0^{1/2}(1-2s)\,ds + \int_{1/2}^t (2s-
1)\,ds = \tfrac14 + \bigl[s^2-s\bigr]_{1/2}^t = t^2 - t + \tfrac12$.

Since $g'(t) = |1-2t| \ge 0$, $g$ is nondecreasing on $[0,1]$, attaining its max
imum at $t=1$:
$$g(1) = 1 - 1 + \tfrac12 = \tfrac12.$$

Hence $\|L_t\| = 1 + g(t)$ is maximized at $t = 1$, giving $\sup_t \|L_t\| = 1 +
 \tfrac12 = \tfrac32$.

Now compute the operator norm. Since $\|Tf\|_\infty = \sup_{t \in [0,1]} |(Tf)(t
)|$,
$$\|T\| = \sup_{\|f\|\le 1}\sup_{t \in [0,1]} |L_t(f)| = \sup_{t \in [0,1]}\sup_
{\|f\|\le 1} |L_t(f)| = \sup_{t \in [0,1]} \|L_t\| = \tfrac32,$$
where the interchange of suprema is valid because they range over an independent
 product set.

It remains to confirm the value $\tfrac32$ is actually attained (in the limit).
Consider continuous functions $f_\varepsilon$ with $\|f_\varepsilon\|_\infty = 1
$ defined by $f_\varepsilon(s) = \operatorname{sgn}(1-2s)$ on $[0, 1-\varepsilon
]$ (so $+1$ on $[0,\tfrac12)$, $-1$ on $(\tfrac12, 1-\varepsilon]$) and $f_\vare
psilon(1) = 1$, extended linearly on $[1-\varepsilon, 1]$. Then
$$L_1(f_\varepsilon) = f_\varepsilon(1) + \int_0^1 (1-2s)f_\varepsilon(s)\,ds \;
\xrightarrow{\varepsilon \to 0}\; 1 + \int_0^1 |1-2s|\,ds = 1 + \tfrac12 = \tfra
c32,$$
since the only loss occurs on the interval $[1-\varepsilon,1]$ of length $\varep
silon$, where $|1-2s| \le 1$, contributing a vanishing error. Thus $\|T\| \ge \t
frac32$, and together with the upper bound $\|T\| \le \tfrac32$ we conclude
$$\boxed{\|T\| = \tfrac32}.$$
