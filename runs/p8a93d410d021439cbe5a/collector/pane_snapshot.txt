## Proof

**Setup.** Let $H$ be an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space, and let $x, y \in H
$ be two distinct unit vectors ($\|x\| = \|y\| = 1$, $x \neq y$).

### Part 1: Existence of a unitary $U$ with $Ux = y$

We construct such a $U$ explicitly.

**Case A: $x$ and $y$ are linearly dependent.** Then $y = e^{i\theta} x$ for som
e $\theta \in \mathbb{R}$ (since both are unit vectors). The operator $U = e^{i\
theta} I$ is unitary and satisfies $Ux = y$.

**Case B: $x$ and $y$ are linearly independent.** Set $\alpha = \langle x, y \ra
ngle$ and $v = y - \alpha x$. Then $v \neq 0$ (by linear independence) and $\lan
gle x, v \rangle = \langle x, y \rangle - \alpha \langle x, x \rangle = \alpha -
 \alpha = 0$, so $v \perp x$. Let $e_2 = v / \|v\|$, and $e_1 = x$. Then $\{e_1,
 e_2\}$ is an orthonormal pair. Write
$$y = \alpha\, e_1 + \beta\, e_2, \qquad \text{where } \beta = \|v\|, \quad |\al
pha|^2 + |\beta|^2 = 1.$$

Define $U$ on $\operatorname{span}\{e_1, e_2\}$ by:
$$Ue_1 = y = \alpha\, e_1 + \beta\, e_2, \qquad Ue_2 = -\bar{\beta}\, e_1 + \bar
{\alpha}\, e_2.$$

The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} \alpha & -\bar{\beta} \\ \beta & \bar{\alpha} \end{p
matrix}$ is unitary (its columns are orthonormal since $|\alpha|^2 + |\beta|^2 =
 1$). Extend $U$ to all of $H$ by setting $U = I$ on $\{e_1, e_2\}^\perp$. Then
$U$ is unitary on $H$ and $Ux = Ue_1 = y$. $\checkmark$

### Part 2: Counting the unitary operators $U$ with $Ux = y$

Fix any unitary $U_0$ with $U_0 x = y$ (constructed above). For any unitary $U$
with $Ux = y$, the operator $W = U_0^{-1} U$ is unitary and satisfies $Wx = x$.
Conversely, for any unitary $W$ with $Wx = x$, the operator $U = U_0 W$ is unita
ry with $Ux = y$. This gives a bijection:

$$\{U \text{ unitary} : Ux = y\} \;\longleftrightarrow\; \{W \text{ unitary} : W
x = x\}.$$

**The stabilizer $\{W : Wx = x\}$ is isomorphic to the unitary group of $x^\perp
$.** Indeed, $H = \mathbb{C}x \oplus x^\perp$, and any unitary $W$ fixing $x$ mu
st also fix $x^\perp$ (since $W$ preserves inner products: $\langle Wz, x\rangle
 = \langle Wz, Wx\rangle = \langle z, x\rangle = 0$ for $z \in x^\perp$). Thus $
W = 1 \oplus W'$ where $W'$ is a unitary on $x^\perp$.

Since $H$ is infinite-dimensional, $x^\perp$ is also infinite-dimensional. The u
nitary group of an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space is **uncountably infinite*
*. (For a separable $H$, its cardinality is $2^{\aleph_0}$, the cardinality of t
he continuum; for non-separable $H$, it is even larger.)

Therefore, the set of unitary operators $U$ with $Ux = y$ is **infinite** (uncou
ntably so).

### Answer

A unitary operator $U$ with $y = Ux$ **always exists** for any pair of distinct
unit vectors in an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space, and the number of such un
itary operators is **infinite** (uncountably infinite, with cardinality $2^{\ale
ph_0}$ in the separable case).

$$\boxed{\infty}$$
