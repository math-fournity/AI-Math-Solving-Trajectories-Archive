# Solution

## Setup

On the triangular grid, the drone can face three directions separated by 120°. L
et the unit vectors in these directions be **a**, **b**, **c**, with **a** + **b
** + **c** = **0** (since three unit vectors 120° apart sum to zero). The drone
starts at the origin **P₀ = 0**, facing direction **a**.

Since a 120° turn always changes the heading to one of the *other two* direction
s (never the same), the direction process is a random walk on {**a**, **b**, **c
**} where from any direction, the next is one of the other two, each with probab
ility 1/2.

## Termination Condition

The segments are unit lattice edges. On the triangular lattice (a planar graph),
 two edges intersect **iff they share a vertex** — non-parallel edges meet only
at vertices, and parallel collinear unit edges either share an endpoint, coincid
e, or are disjoint.

When the drone lays segment *n* (from **P**_{n-1} to **P**_n), it shares endpoin
t **P**_{n-1} with segment *n*−1 (expected connection). If **P**_{n-1} were a pr
eviously visited vertex, the mission would have already terminated. So terminati
on at step *n* occurs **iff P_n is a previously visited vertex** (P_n = P_j for
some j ≤ n−2), or the new segment coincides with an old one (which requires P_{n
-1} to be old — contradiction). Thus:

> **The mission terminates at the first *n* where P_n ∈ {P_0, …, P_{n−2}}.**

## Step-by-Step Analysis

**Steps 1–2:** P₁ = **a**, P₂ = **a**+**b** or **a**+**c**. Neither equals any p
rior vertex. No termination.

**Step 3:** Four equally likely direction sequences:

| Sequence | P₃ | Previously visited? |
|---|---|---|
| (**a**,**b**,**c**) | **a**+**b**+**c** = **0** = P₀ | **Yes — terminate** |
| (**a**,**c**,**b**) | **a**+**c**+**b** = **0** = P₀ | **Yes — terminate** |
| (**a**,**b**,**a**) | 2**a**+**b** | No — continue |
| (**a**,**c**,**a**) | 2**a**+**c** | No — continue |

So **P(N = 3) = 1/2**.

## The Alternating Pattern

In the non-terminating cases, the drone alternates between two directions (e.g.,
 **a**,**b**,**a**,**b**,…). By symmetry (swapping **b** ↔ **c**), both cases ar
e identical. Consider the **a**/**b** alternation.

Positions: P_{2k} = k(**a**+**b**), P_{2k+1} = (k+1)**a** + k**b**.

Since **a** and **b** are linearly independent (120° apart), **all these positio
ns are distinct** — the pattern never self-intersects. The path forms a zigzag m
arching monotonically in the **a**+**b** direction.

## Breaking the Pattern

At each step *n* ≥ 4, the drone faces **a** or **b** and must turn to:
- the **other pattern direction** (continue, probability 1/2), or
- direction **c** (break, probability 1/2).

When breaking at step *n*, the last two steps used one **a** and one **b**, so:

$$P_{n-1} = P_{n-3} + \mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} = P_{n-3} - \mathbf{c}$$

Breaking gives: $P_n = P_{n-1} + \mathbf{c} = P_{n-3}$.

Since *n*−3 ≥ 0 and *n*−3 < *n*, **P_{n-3} is a previously visited vertex** → mi
ssion terminates.

**Key identity:** $\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} + \mathbf{c} = \mathbf{0}$ guarantees
 that switching to the third direction after two steps in the other two always r
eturns to the position 3 steps prior.

## Probability Distribution

The drone must survive the initial step-3 check (prob 1/2), then continue the pa
ttern for each subsequent step (prob 1/2 each) until breaking (prob 1/2):

$$P(N = n) = \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{n-2}, \quad n \geq 3$$

**Verification:** $\sum_{n=3}^{\infty} (1/2)^{n-2} = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} (1/2)^k
 = 1$ ✓ (mission terminates with probability 1).

## Expected Value

$$E[N] = \sum_{n=3}^{\infty} n \cdot \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{n-2} = \sum_{k=1
}^{\infty} (k+2)\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^k = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} k\left(\tfrac{
1}{2}\right)^k + 2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^k$$

Using $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} k r^k = \frac{r}{(1-r)^2}$ with $r = 1/2$:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} k\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^k = \frac{1/2}{(1/2)^2} = 2, \q
quad \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^k = 1$$

$$\boxed{E[N] = 2 + 2 \cdot 1 = 4}$$

The expected number of cable segments laid when the mission terminates is **4**.
