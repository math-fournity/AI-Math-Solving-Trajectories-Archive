**Problem.** Find all differentiable $f:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$ for which there
 exists $a>0$ with $f'(a/x)=x/f(x)$ for all $x>0$.

---

### Step 1. Reformulate the equation

Substitute $x\mapsto a/x$ (a bijection of $(0,\infty)$) into the given equation
$f'(a/x)=x/f(x)$. Writing $t=a/x$, i.e. $x=a/t$, we obtain
$$
f'(t)=\frac{a/t}{f(a/t)}=\frac{a}{t\,f(a/t)},\qquad t>0. \tag{1}
$$
Conversely (1) immediately gives back the original equation, so (1) is equivalen
t to the problem.

### Step 2. A conserved quantity

Define $P(x)=f(x)\,f(a/x)$. Note $P(a/x)=f(a/x)\,f(x)=P(x)$, so $P$ is invariant
 under $x\mapsto a/x$. Differentiate:
$$
P'(x)=f'(x)\,f(a/x)+f(x)\,f'(a/x)\cdot\Bigl(-\frac{a}{x^{2}}\Bigr). \tag{2}
$$
Using (1): $f'(x)\,f(a/x)=\dfrac{a}{x}$.
Using the original equation $f'(a/x)=x/f(x)$: $f(x)\,f'(a/x)=x$, hence the secon
d term in (2) is $x\cdot(-a/x^{2})=-a/x$.
Therefore
$$
P'(x)=\frac{a}{x}-\frac{a}{x}=0.
$$
So $P(x)=f(x)\,f(a/x)=C$ for some **constant** $C>0$ (positive since $f>0$).

### Step 3. Reduce to a separable ODE

From $f(a/x)=C/f(x)$, substitute into (1):
$$
f'(x)=\frac{a}{x}\cdot\frac{f(x)}{C}=\frac{a}{C}\cdot\frac{f(x)}{x}. \tag{3}
$$
This is separable: $\dfrac{f'(x)}{f(x)}=\dfrac{a}{C}\cdot\dfrac{1}{x}$. Integrat
ing,
$$
\ln f(x)=\frac{a}{C}\ln x+K\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(x)=A\,x^{\,p},\qquad p:=\
frac{a}{C},\; A>0. \tag{4}
$$
So every solution is a **power function**.

### Step 4. Determine the parameters

Impose $f(x)f(a/x)=C$ on (4):
$$
A\,x^{p}\cdot A\Bigl(\frac{a}{x}\Bigr)^{p}=A^{2}a^{p}=C. \tag{5}
$$
Together with $p=a/C$ this gives $p=a/(A^{2}a^{p})$, i.e.
$$
A^{2}\,p\,a^{\,p-1}=1. \tag{6}
$$
Conversely, (6) is exactly the condition that $f(x)=Ax^{p}$ satisfies the origin
al equation (direct substitution: $f'(a/x)=Ap(a/x)^{p-1}=Ap\,a^{p-1}x^{1-p}$ and
 $x/f(x)=x^{1-p}/A$; equality $\iff Ap\,a^{p-1}=1/A\iff A^{2}pa^{p-1}=1$).

Now analyze when a **positive** $a$ satisfying (6) exists.

- **$p>0$:** $A^{2}p>0$, so $a^{p-1}=1/(A^{2}p)>0$.
  - If $p\neq 1$: $a=\bigl(A^{2}p\bigr)^{1/(1-p)}>0$. ✓
  - If $p=1$: (6) becomes $A^{2}=1$, so $A=1$; then $a$ is **arbitrary** positiv
e. This gives $f(x)=x$.
- **$p=0$:** (6) gives $0=1$, impossible. (Consistent: a constant $f$ gives $f'\
equiv0\neq x/f$.)
- **$p<0$:** $A^{2}p<0$ while $a^{p-1}>0$, so LHS of (6) is negative $\neq 1$. I
mpossible.

### Step 5. Verification

Take any $A>0$, $p>0$.
- If $p\neq1$, set $a=(A^{2}p)^{1/(1-p)}>0$. Then $A^{2}p\,a^{p-1}=A^{2}p\cdot(A
^{2}p)^{(p-1)/(1-p)}=A^{2}p\cdot(A^{2}p)^{-1}=1$. ✓
- If $p=1$, take $A=1$ and any $a>0$: $f(x)=x$, $f'(a/x)=1=x/x$. ✓

All such functions map $(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$ and are differentiable.

### Conclusion

The differentiable functions $f:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$ satisfying the conditio

$$
\boxed{\,f(x)=c\,x^{p},\qquad c>0,\; p>0\,}
$$

with the parameter $a>0$ given by
$$
a=(c^{2}p)^{\,1/(1-p)}\quad(p\neq1),\qquad\text{and}\quad f(x)=x\;(p=1,\,c=1)\te

$$

(Equivalently: every pair $(c,p)$ with $c>0$, $p>0$ yields a solution, except th
