## Proof

Let $\Phi$ be a homogeneous PPP of intensity $\lambda$ on $\mathbb{R}$. Restrict
ing to $\mathbb{R}^+$, the points $\{x_0, x_1, \dots\}$ with $0 < x_0 < x_1 < \d
ots$ form a PPP of intensity $\lambda$ on $\mathbb{R}^+$.

**Step 1: Distribution of the gaps.**

By the independent increments property of a PPP, the inter-point spacings are i.
i.d. $\operatorname{Exp}(\lambda)$. Writing
$$x_0 = E_0, \qquad x_k = E_0 + E_1 + \cdots + E_k,$$
where $E_0, E_1, \dots, E_k$ are i.i.d. $\operatorname{Exp}(\lambda)$. Indeed $x
_0 \sim \operatorname{Exp}(\lambda)$ and $x_k$ is the sum of $k+1$ i.i.d. expone
ntials, giving the stated Erlang density
$$f_{x_k}(x) = \frac{\lambda^{k+1} x^k}{k!} e^{-\lambda x}, \qquad x \ge 0.$$

**Step 2: Set up the CCDF.**

Let $R = x_0/x_k$ and fix $r \in [0,1]$. We want $\Pr(R > r)$. Write $T = E_1 +
\cdots + E_k \sim \operatorname{Gamma}(k,\lambda)$, independent of $E_0$. Then
$$\Pr(R > r) = \Pr\!\left(\frac{E_0}{E_0 + T} > r\right) = \Pr\!\big((1-r)E_0 >
r\,T\big) = \Pr\!\left(E_0 > \tfrac{r}{1-r}\,T\right).$$

Condition on $T = t$. Since $E_0 \sim \operatorname{Exp}(\lambda)$,
$$\Pr(E_0 > \tfrac{r}{1-r}\,t \mid T = t) = \exp\!\left(-\lambda \tfrac{r}{1-r}\
,t\right).$$

**Step 3: Average over $T$.**

$$\Pr(R > r) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\exp\!\left(-\tfrac{\lambda r}{1-r}\,T\right)\r
ight] = M_T\!\left(-\tfrac{\lambda r}{1-r}\right),$$
where $M_T$ is the moment-generating function of $T \sim \operatorname{Gamma}(k,
\lambda)$:
$$M_T(s) = \left(1 - \frac{s}{\lambda}\right)^{-k}.$$

Substituting $s = -\lambda r/(1-r)$:
$$\Pr(R > r) = \left(1 - \frac{-\lambda r/(1-r)}{\lambda}\right)^{-k} = \left(1
+ \frac{r}{1-r}\right)^{-k} = \left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)^{-k} = (1-r)^k.$$

**Step 4: Boundary behaviour.**

- At $r = 0$: $(1-0)^k = 1$, consistent with $\Pr(R > 0) = 1$.
- At $r = 1$: $(1-1)^k = 0$, consistent with $\Pr(R > 1) = 0$ (since $x_0 \le x_
k$ a.s.).

**Result.** The CCDF of the ratio $R = x_0/x_k$ is, for $0 \le r \le 1$,
$$\boxed{\Pr\!\left(\frac{x_0}{x_k} > r\right) = (1-r)^k.}$$

Equivalently, $R \sim \operatorname{Beta}(1,k)$ with density $f_R(r) = k(1-r)^{k
-1}$. The result is independent of $\lambda$ — a consequence of scale invariance
: the common rate $\lambda$ cancels when taking the ratio of two quantities that
 both scale as $1/\lambda$.
