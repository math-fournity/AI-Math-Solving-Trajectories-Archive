## Proof

**Goal.** Derive the differential form of the Itô integral

$$I(t)=\int_{0}^{t}B(s)\,dB(s)$$

where $B(t)$ is a standard Brownian motion, using Itô's rules.

---

### Itô's formula (the rule we use)

For a twice-continuously-differentiable function $f$ applied to Brownian motion,

$$df\bigl(B(t)\bigr)=f'\bigl(B(t)\bigr)\,dB(t)+\tfrac{1}{2}\,f''\bigl(B(t)\bigr)
\,dt.$$

The extra $\tfrac{1}{2}f''\,dt$ term — absent in ordinary calculus — is the hall
mark of Itô calculus and reflects the non-zero quadratic variation of Brownian m
otion, $dB^{2}=dt$.

---

### Choosing the integrand

We want to recover the integrand $B(t)$ in front of $dB(t)$. So we pick $f$ such
 that

$$f'(x)=x \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}x^{2},\qquad f''(x)=1.$$

---

### Applying Itô's formula

Substituting $f(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}x^{2}$ into Itô's formula:

$$d\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}B(t)^{2}\right)=B(t)\,dB(t)+\tfrac{1}{2}\,dt.$$

Rearranging to isolate $B(t)\,dB(t)$:

$$\boxed{\,B(t)\,dB(t)=d\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}B(t)^{2}\right)-\tfrac{1}{2}\,dt\,}.
$$

This is the **differential form** of the integrand of the Itô integral.

---

### Integrating to obtain the closed form (verification)

Integrating both sides from $0$ to $t$:

$$\int_{0}^{t}B(s)\,dB(s)=\int_{0}^{t}d\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}B(s)^{2}\right)-\int_
{0}^{t}\tfrac{1}{2}\,ds.$$

The first integral is a total differential, so it telescopes:

$$\int_{0}^{t}d\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}B(s)^{2}\right)=\tfrac{1}{2}B(t)^{2}-\tfrac{1
}{2}B(0)^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}B(t)^{2},$$

since $B(0)=0$ for standard Brownian motion. The second integral is deterministi
c:

$$\int_{0}^{t}\tfrac{1}{2}\,ds=\tfrac{1}{2}t.$$

Therefore

$$\int_{0}^{t}B(s)\,dB(s)=\tfrac{1}{2}B(t)^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}t.$$

---

### Interpretation: the Itô correction

In ordinary calculus, $\int x\,dx=\tfrac{1}{2}x^{2}$. The Itô result differs by
the **correction term** $-\tfrac{1}{2}t$. This term arises precisely from the se
cond-order contribution $\tfrac{1}{2}f''\,dt$ in Itô's formula, which encodes th
e fact that Brownian motion has non-vanishing quadratic variation:

$$\langle B\rangle_{t}=t.$$

The differential form

$$d\!\left(\int_{0}^{t}B(s)\,dB(s)\right)=B(t)\,dB(t)=d\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}B(t)^
{2}\right)-\tfrac{1}{2}\,dt$$

is the desired result, derived entirely from Itô's rules.
