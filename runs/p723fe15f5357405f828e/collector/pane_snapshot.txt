**The closure of $\mathcal{H}^{k+p}_0$ in $H^k(0,2\pi)$ is $H^k(0,2\pi)$.**

### Proof

Recall the definition: a subset $D$ of a topological space $X$ is **dense** in $
X$ if and only if the closure of $D$ equals $X$:
$$\overline{D} = X.$$

Equivalently, $D$ is dense in $X$ if every point $x \in X$ is either an element
of $D$ or a limit point of $D$ (i.e., every neighborhood of $x$ intersects $D$).
 This is exactly the statement that the closure of $D$ in $X$ is all of $X$.

We are given that $\mathcal{H}^{k+p}_0$ is dense in $H^k(0,2\pi)$. Applying the
definition of density directly with $D = \mathcal{H}^{k+p}_0$ and $X = H^k(0,2\p
i)$ (equipped with the $H^k$-norm topology), we obtain:
$$\overline{\mathcal{H}^{k+p}_0}^{\,H^k(0,2\pi)} = H^k(0,2\pi).$$

This is immediate from the definition: density of a set in a space **means** its
 closure in that space is the entire space. No additional argument is required.
