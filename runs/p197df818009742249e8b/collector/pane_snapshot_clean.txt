## Answer

**No.** Global exponential stability is **not** in general preserved under a glo
bal $\mathcal{C}^1$ diffeomorphism. It is preserved under *linear* (or more gene
rally *bi-Lipschitz*) coordinate changes, but a general nonlinear $\mathcal{C}^1
$ diffeomorphism can distort the metric so badly that the exponential decay rate
 is destroyed. Below is an explicit counterexample.

---

### Counterexample ($n=1$)

**Original system.** Take
$$
\dot x = f(x) = -x, \qquad x\in\mathbb R.
$$
$f$ is smooth, $f(0)=0$, and the solution is $x(t)=x(0)e^{-t}$, so
$$
|x(t)| = |x(0)|\,e^{-t},
$$
i.e. $\Sigma$ is globally exponentially stable (GES) at $0$ with constants $M=1,
\lambda=1$.

**Coordinate change.** Define
$$
T(x) = \operatorname{sgn}(x)\,\ln(1+|x|).
$$
* $T(0)=0$.
* $T$ is $\mathcal C^\infty$ on $\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$, and at $0$ we have $T
(x)/x\to 1$, so $T'(0)=1$; moreover $T'(x)=1/(1+|x|)>0$ for $x\neq 0$ and $T'(x)
\to 1$ as $x\to 0$, so $T\in\mathcal C^1(\mathbb R)$.
* $T'>0$ everywhere $\Rightarrow$ $T$ strictly increasing; $T(x)\to\pm\infty$ as
 $x\to\pm\infty$ $\Rightarrow$ $T$ is onto. Hence $T$ is a **global $\mathcal C^
1$ diffeomorphism** of $\mathbb R$ with $T(0)=0$.

**Transformed system.** With $z=T(x)$,
$$
\dot z = g(z) = T'(T^{-1}(z))\,f(T^{-1}(z)).
$$
For $z>0$, $T^{-1}(z)=e^{z}-1$ and $T'(T^{-1}(z))=1/e^{z}$, so
$$
g(z) = \tfrac{1}{e^{z}}\bigl(-(e^{z}-1)\bigr) = -(1-e^{-z}).
$$
By symmetry, $g(z)=-\operatorname{sgn}(z)(1-e^{-|z|})$. Thus
$$
\Sigma':\ \dot z = -(1-e^{-|z|})\operatorname{sgn}(z).
$$

**$\Sigma'$ is not GES.** The trajectory in $z$-coordinates is
$$
z(t)=T(x(t))=T\!\bigl(x(0)e^{-t}\bigr)=\operatorname{sgn}(x(0))\,\ln\!\bigl(1+|x
(0)|e^{-t}\bigr).
$$
Set $x(0)=u>0$, so $z(0)=\ln(1+u)$ and $z(t)=\ln(1+u\,e^{-t})$.

Suppose, for contradiction, that $\Sigma'$ were GES: there would exist $M,\lambd
a>0$ with
$$
z(t)\le M\,z(0)\,e^{-\lambda t}\qquad\forall\,u>0,\ t\ge 0.
$$
Choose $t=\ln u$ (so $u\,e^{-t}=1$). Then
$$
z(t)=\ln 2,\qquad M\,z(0)\,e^{-\lambda t}=M\,\ln(1+u)\,u^{-\lambda}\xrightarrow[
u\to\infty]{}0.
$$
So the GES bound demands $\ln 2\le M\ln(1+u)\,u^{-\lambda}$ for all $u>0$, but t
he right-hand side tends to $0$ as $u\to\infty$ while the left-hand side is the
positive constant $\ln 2$. **Contradiction.**

Equivalently, one can see the failure directly from the vector field: for large
$|z|$, $g(z)\approx -\operatorname{sgn}(z)$, so trajectories decay *linearly* ($
z(t)\approx z(0)-t$), not exponentially — the time to reach the origin grows lik
e $|z(0)|$, which is incompatible with any uniform exponential bound.

---

### Conclusion

We exhibited a smooth system $\dot x=-x$ that is GES at the origin, and a global
 $\mathcal C^1$ diffeomorphism $T(x)=\operatorname{sgn}(x)\ln(1+|x|)$ with $T(0)
=0$, such that the transformed system $\dot z=-(1-e^{-|z|})\operatorname{sgn}(z)
$ is **not** globally exponentially stable. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{$\Sigma'$ need not be globally exponentially stable.}}
$$

(GES is preserved only under coordinate changes that are bi-Lipschitz with a *un
iform* Lipschitz constant and inverse — i.e. under changes that do not stretch o
r compress distances by an unbounded factor. A general $\mathcal C^1$ diffeomorp
hism such as $\ln(1+|x|)$ compresses large distances without bound, and this is
exactly what destroys the exponential rate.)
