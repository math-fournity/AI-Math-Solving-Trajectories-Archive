## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $\mu(\mathbb{R}\setminus(A+\mathbb{Q}))=0$.

Let $B=\mathbb{R}\setminus(A+\mathbb{Q})$. We show $\mu(B)=0$.

**Step 1: Reduction to the circle.** Let $\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ and
$f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{T}$ the quotient map. Set $C=f(A+\mathbb{Q})\subset\math
bb{T}$. Since $A+\mathbb{Q}$ is invariant under translation by every rational (h
ence by every integer),
$$f^{-1}(C)=\{x:\exists\,y\in A+\mathbb{Q},\ x-y\in\mathbb{Z}\}=(A+\mathbb{Q})+\
mathbb{Z}=A+\mathbb{Q},$$
using $\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{Q}$. Therefore $B=f^{-1}(\mathbb{T}\setminus C)$
, and since $B$ is $\mathbb{Z}$-periodic,
$$\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(B)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mu_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}\setminus
C),$$
which equals $0$ iff $\mu_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}\setminus C)=0$. So it suffices
 to prove $\mu_{\mathbb{T}}(C)=1$.

**Step 2: $C$ is invariant under the dense subgroup $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$.** F
or $g\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ represented by $q\in\mathbb{Q}$,
$$C+g=f(A+\mathbb{Q})+f(q)=f(A+\mathbb{Q}+q)=f(A+\mathbb{Q})=C,$$
since $A+\mathbb{Q}+q=A+\mathbb{Q}$. So $C$ is $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$-invariant
.

**Step 3: Ergodicity lemma.** *If $E\subset\mathbb{T}$ is measurable and $E+g=E$
 for all $g\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$, then $\mu_{\mathbb{T}}(E)\in\{0,1\}$.*

*Proof of lemma.* For $g\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$, $E\triangle(E+g)=\varnothing$
, so $\mu(E\triangle(E+g))=0$. The map $\varphi(t)=\mu_{\mathbb{T}}(E\cap(E+t))$
 is continuous in $t$ (a standard consequence of density of continuous functions
 in $L^{1}$, or of the fact that translation acts continuously on $L^{1}$). Sinc
e $\mu(E\triangle(E+t))=2\mu(E)-2\varphi(t)$ and this vanishes on the dense set
$\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$, continuity gives $\mu(E\triangle(E+t))=0$ for **all** $
t\in\mathbb{T}$. Hence $\varphi(t)\equiv\mu(E)$. Integrating over $t$ and applyi
ng Fubini,
$$\mu(E)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi(t)\,dt=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\math
bf{1}_{E}(x)\mathbf{1}_{E}(x+t)\,dx\,dt=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbf{1}_{E}(x)\,\mu(
E)\,dx=\mu(E)^{2}.$$
Thus $\mu(E)\in\{0,1\}$. $\square$

Applying the lemma to $C$: $\mu_{\mathbb{T}}(C)\in\{0,1\}$.

**Step 4: $\mu_{\mathbb{T}}(C)>0$.** Since $\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(A)=\sum_{n\in\mathb
b{Z}}\mu(A\cap[n,n+1))>0$, some interval piece $A_{n_{0}}=A\cap[n_{0},n_{0}+1)$
has positive measure. The restriction $f|_{[n_{0},n_{0}+1)}\to\mathbb{T}$ is mea
sure-preserving, so
$$\mu_{\mathbb{T}}(f(A_{n_{0}}))=\mu(A_{n_{0}})>0.$$
But $f(A_{n_{0}})\subset f(A)\subset f(A+\mathbb{Q})=C$, so $\mu_{\mathbb{T}}(C)
\geqslant\mu_{\mathbb{T}}(f(A_{n_{0}}))>0$.

**Step 5: Conclusion.** By Step 3, $\mu_{\mathbb{T}}(C)\in\{0,1\}$; by Step 4, $
\mu_{\mathbb{T}}(C)>0$; hence $\mu_{\mathbb{T}}(C)=1$, i.e. $\mu_{\mathbb{T}}(\m
athbb{T}\setminus C)=0$. By Step 1,
$$\mu\bigl(\mathbb{R}\setminus(A+\mathbb{Q})\bigr)=\mu(B)=0.$$

Therefore the Lebesgue measure of $\mathbb{R}\setminus(A+\mathbb{Q})$ **is zero*
*. $\blacksquare$
