## Proof

Define $g_n(x) = n\log\!\bigl(1 + \tfrac{f(x)}{n}\bigr)$ for $n \geq 1$.

**Step 1: Pointwise limit.**

For any $x$ with $f(x) < \infty$, set $t = f(x)/n \to 0^+$ as $n \to \infty$. Us
ing the standard limit $\frac{\log(1+t)}{t} \to 1$ as $t \to 0$,

$$g_n(x) = n\log\!\Bigl(1+\tfrac{f(x)}{n}\Bigr) = f(x)\cdot\frac{\log\bigl(1+\tf
rac{f(x)}{n}\bigr)}{f(x)/n} \;\longrightarrow\; f(x)\cdot 1 = f(x).$$

Since $f$ is integrable ($\int_X f\,d\mu = 1 < \infty$), we have $f(x) < \infty$
 $\mu$-a.e. Hence $g_n \to f$ $\mu$-a.e.

**Step 2: Dominating function.**

For all $t \geq 0$, the concavity of $\log$ gives $\log(1+t) \leq t$. Applying t
his with $t = f(x)/n \geq 0$:

$$0 \leq g_n(x) = n\log\!\Bigl(1+\tfrac{f(x)}{n}\Bigr) \leq n\cdot\frac{f(x)}{n}
 = f(x), \qquad \mu\text{-a.e.}$$

Since $f$ is integrable, $f$ serves as an $L^1$-dominating function independent
of $n$.

**Step 3: Apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem.**

We have $g_n \to f$ a.e., $|g_n| \leq f$ a.e. with $f \in L^1(\mu)$. By the Domi
nated Convergence Theorem,

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_X g_n\,d\mu = \int_X f\,d\mu = 1.$$

**Conclusion:**

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_X n\log\!\biggl(1+\frac{f(x)}{n}\biggr)\,d\mu = 1
.}$$
