## Solution

We are given $\mathbb{v} = \operatorname{curl}\,\mathbb{u}$ with $\mathbb{u} = (
xy,\, xz^2,\, x^2y)$, and asked for the flux of $\mathbb{v}$ through the surface
 of the solid bounded by the paraboloid $z = 1 - x^2 - y^2$ and the planes $x =
0$ and $z = 0$.

**Step 1: Identify the solid and its boundary.**

The paraboloid $z = 1 - x^2 - y^2$ has vertex $(0,0,1)$ and opens downward, meet
ing the plane $z = 0$ along the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$. The plane $x = 0$ slices
 this region in half. The solid is

$$\Omega = \{(x,y,z) : x \ge 0,\; z \ge 0,\; z \le 1 - x^2 - y^2\}.$$

Its boundary $\partial\Omega$ consists of three pieces — the paraboloid, the dis
k in $z=0$, and the flat face in $x=0$ — which together form a **closed** surfac
e.

**Step 2: Apply the divergence theorem.**

Since $\mathbb{v} = \operatorname{curl}\,\mathbb{u}$, we use the standard identi
ty

$$\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{curl}\,\mathbb{u}) = 0$$

identically for any smooth $\mathbb{u}$. By the divergence theorem,

$$\iint_{\partial\Omega} \mathbb{v}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iiint_{\Omega} \operator
name{div}\,\mathbb{v}\,dV = \iiint_{\Omega} 0\,dV = 0.$$

**Conclusion.**

The flux of $\mathbb{v} = \operatorname{curl}\,\mathbb{u}$ through the closed su
rface of the solid is

$$\boxed{0}.$$
