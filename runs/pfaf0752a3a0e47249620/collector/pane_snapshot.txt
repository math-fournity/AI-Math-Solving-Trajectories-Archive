Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a non-decreasing sequence o
f continuous functions in $L_p(0,\infty)$ can converge pointwise to $f \in L_p(0
,\infty)$ **without** converging in $L_p$ norm.

**Answer: No, such a sequence does not exist.** Every non-decreasing sequence in
 $L_p(0,\infty)$ that converges pointwise to an $L_p$ function must also converg
e in $L_p$ norm.

---

**Proof.**

Let $\{f_n\}$ be a non-decreasing sequence of continuous functions in $L_p(0,\in
fty)$ (for $p > 0$) with $f_n \to f$ pointwise and $f \in L_p(0,\infty)$. We sho
w $\|f_n - f\|_p \to 0$.

**Step 1: Reduction to a non-negative, non-increasing sequence.**

Since $\{f_n(x)\}$ is non-decreasing in $n$ and converges to $f(x)$, we have $f_
n(x) \leq f(x)$ for every $x$ and every $n$. Set
$$g_n := f - f_n \geq 0.$$
Then $g_n \to 0$ pointwise, and $\{g_n\}$ is **non-increasing** (since $\{f_n\}$
 is non-decreasing). Moreover,
$$|f_n - f|^p = g_n^p,$$
since $g_n \geq 0$. The sequence $\{g_n^p\}$ is non-negative, non-increasing, an
d converges to $0$ pointwise.

**Step 2: Integrable domination.**

We need $g_1^p = (f - f_1)^p \in L_1(0,\infty)$ to apply the monotone convergenc
e theorem to the non-increasing sequence $\{g_n^p\}$.

- **For $p \geq 1$:** By the convexity inequality $(a+b)^p \leq 2^{p-1}(a^p + b^
p)$ for $a, b \geq 0$,
$$(f - f_1)^p \leq (|f| + |f_1|)^p \leq 2^{p-1}\bigl(|f|^p + |f_1|^p\bigr).$$
Since $f, f_1 \in L_p$, the right-hand side is in $L_1$, so $g_1^p \in L_1$.

- **For $0 < p \leq 1$:** By subadditivity $(a+b)^p \leq a^p + b^p$ for $a, b \g
eq 0$,
$$(f - f_1)^p \leq |f|^p + |f_1|^p \in L_1.$$

In both cases, $g_1^p \in L_1(0,\infty)$.

**Step 3: Apply the Monotone Convergence Theorem (non-increasing version).**

The sequence $\{g_n^p\}$ is non-negative, non-increasing, converges pointwise to
 $0$, and $g_1^p \in L_1$. Applying the monotone convergence theorem to the non-
decreasing sequence $\{g_1^p - g_n^p\}$ (which is non-negative, non-decreasing,
and converges to $g_1^p$ pointwise):
$$\int_0^\infty (g_1^p - g_n^p)\,d\mu \;\longrightarrow\; \int_0^\infty g_1^p\,d
\mu,$$
which gives
$$\int_0^\infty g_n^p\,d\mu \;\longrightarrow\; 0.$$

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Therefore
$$\|f_n - f\|_p^p = \int_0^\infty |f_n - f|^p\,d\mu = \int_0^\infty g_n^p\,d\mu
\;\longrightarrow\; 0,$$
so $f_n \to f$ in $L_p$ norm.

**Remark.** The continuity of $f_n$ plays no role in the argument; the result ho
lds for any non-decreasing sequence in $L_p$ converging pointwise to an $L_p$ li
mit. The key mechanism is that monotonicity forces $f_n \leq f$, providing the d
omination $(f - f_1)^p \in L_1$ needed for the (dominated/monotone) convergence
theorem. $\blacksquare$
