## Proof that $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{Z}}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ f
or $n \geq 1$

**Setup.** Let $X = \mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{Z}} = \operatorname{Proj} \mathbb{Z}[x
_0, \dots, x_n]$ with $n \geq 1$. The function field is $K = \mathbb{Q}(x_1/x_0,
 \dots, x_n/x_0)$.

**Step 1: $\operatorname{Pic}(X) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(X)$.**

The scheme $X$ is regular: it is smooth over $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}$, be
ing covered by affine charts $D_+(x_i) \cong \mathbb{A}^n_{\mathbb{Z}} = \operat
orname{Spec}\,\mathbb{Z}[t_1,\dots,t_n]$, and $\mathbb{Z}[t_1,\dots,t_n]$ is a r
egular ring (polynomial ring over the regular ring $\mathbb{Z}$). By the Ausland
er–Buchsbaum theorem, every regular local ring is a UFD, so $X$ is locally facto
rial. For a locally factorial Noetherian integral separated scheme, the natural
map $\operatorname{Cl}(X) \to \operatorname{Pic}(X)$ (sending a Weil divisor to
its associated line bundle) is an isomorphism.

**Step 2: Identify the codimension-1 points.**

The height-1 homogeneous prime ideals of $\mathbb{Z}[x_0,\dots,x_n]$ (excluding
the irrelevant ideal) are of two types:

- **Vertical:** $(p)$ for a rational prime $p$, giving the divisor $V_+(p) \cong
 \mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{F}_p}$.
- **Horizontal:** $(f)$ for an irreducible homogeneous polynomial $f$ of positiv
e degree, giving the divisor $V_+(f)$.

**Step 3: Vertical divisors are trivial in $\operatorname{Cl}(X)$.**

For a rational prime $p$, the element $p \in K^*$ is a rational function. We com
pute $\operatorname{div}(p) = \sum_Y v_Y(p)\,[Y]$ over all codim-1 points $Y$:

- At $Y = V_+(p)$: $p$ is a uniformizer in the DVR $\mathcal{O}_{X,Y}$, so $v_Y(
p) = 1$.
- At $Y = V_+(q)$ for $q \neq p$: $p \notin (q)$, so $p$ is a unit, giving $v_Y(
p) = 0$.
- At $Y = V_+(f)$ for $f$ irreducible homogeneous of positive degree: since $f$
has positive degree and $p$ has degree $0$, $p \notin (f)$ (otherwise $f \mid p$
, forcing $f$ to be a constant, contradiction). So $p$ is a unit and $v_Y(p) = 0
$.

Hence $\operatorname{div}(p) = [V_+(p)]$, which gives $[V_+(p)] = 0$ in $\operat
orname{Cl}(X)$.

**Step 4: Horizontal divisors are multiples of the hyperplane class.**

Let $H = V_+(x_0)$, a hyperplane. Let $f$ be irreducible homogeneous of degree $
d > 0$. The ratio $f/x_0^d \in K^*$ is a rational function. We compute $\operato
rname{div}(f/x_0^d)$:

- At $Y = V_+(f)$: $v_Y(f) = 1$ (uniformizer), and $v_Y(x_0) = 0$ (since $x_0 \n
otin (f)$ when $f \neq x_0$ up to units, by irreducibility). So $v_Y(f/x_0^d) =
1$.
- At $Y = H = V_+(x_0)$: $v_H(f) = 0$ (since $f \neq x_0$ up to units implies $x
_0 \nmid f$ by irreducibility), and $v_H(x_0) = 1$. So $v_H(f/x_0^d) = -d$.
- At $Y = V_+(g)$ for any other irreducible homogeneous $g$ of positive degree:
$v_Y(f) = 0$ and $v_Y(x_0) = 0$ (both by irreducibility), so $v_Y(f/x_0^d) = 0$.
- At $Y = V_+(p)$: $v_Y(f) = 0$ (since $f$ irreducible of positive degree means
$p \nmid f$) and $v_Y(x_0) = 0$, so $v_Y(f/x_0^d) = 0$.

Therefore $\operatorname{div}(f/x_0^d) = [V_+(f)] - d[H]$, yielding $[V_+(f)] =
d[H]$ in $\operatorname{Cl}(X)$.

(For $f = x_0$ itself: $\operatorname{div}(x_0/x_0) = \operatorname{div}(1) = 0
= [H] - 1\cdot[H]$, consistent.)

Combining Steps 3 and 4: every prime divisor is rationally equivalent to $d[H]$
for some $d \geq 0$, so $\operatorname{Cl}(X)$ is generated by $[H]$.

**Step 5: $[H]$ has infinite order.**

Consider the generic fiber: the flat map $X \to \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}$ h
as generic fiber $X_{\mathbb{Q}} = \mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{Q}}$. The restriction (
pullback) map $\operatorname{Pic}(X) \to \operatorname{Pic}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})$ sen
ds $\mathcal{O}(1) \mapsto \mathcal{O}(1)$, i.e., $[H] \mapsto [H_{\mathbb{Q}}]$
. Since $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{Q}}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ generat
ed by $\mathcal{O}(1)$ (standard result over a field), the map $\mathbb{Z}\cdot[
H] \to \mathbb{Z}$ is surjective.

If $m[H] = 0$ in $\operatorname{Cl}(X)$ for some $m > 0$, then restricting to th
e generic fiber gives $m[H_{\mathbb{Q}}] = 0$ in $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{P}^n
_{\mathbb{Q}}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, forcing $m = 0$, a contradiction. Hence $[H]$
has infinite order.

**Conclusion.** $\operatorname{Cl}(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}\cdot[H] \cong \mathbb{Z}$
, and via the isomorphism $\operatorname{Cl}(X) \xrightarrow{\sim} \operatorname
{Pic}(X)$, the class $[H]$ corresponds to the line bundle $\mathcal{O}(1)$.

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{Z}}) \cong \mathbb{Z}, \quad \
text{generated by } \mathcal{O}(1).}$$

(For $n = 0$, $\mathbb{P}^0_{\mathbb{Z}} = \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}$ and $\
operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}) = 0$ since $\mathbb{Z}$ is a PI
D.)
