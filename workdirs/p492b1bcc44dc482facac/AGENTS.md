# Problem

In the mountainous kingdom of Terraphall, a master architect is designing a decorative mosaic for a grand hall. The mosaic is shaped like a perfect equilateral triangle with a side length of 10 units. To create the pattern, the architect has partitioned this large triangular space into 100 identical, small equilateral triangles, each with a side length of 1 unit, using a grid of lines parallel to the three outer edges.

The design calls for highlighting "gemstone clusters." A gemstone cluster is defined as any region formed by joining exactly two of these small unit triangles that share a common edge (creating a rhombus shape). These clusters can vary in orientation and position across the entire 10-unit grid.

The architect needs to calculate the total number of unique gemstone clusters that exist within the bounds of the large 10-unit triangle. How many such distinct rhombuses can be found in the grid?
