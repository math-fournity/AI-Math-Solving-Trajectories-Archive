## Proof

We are given a vector field $v = (U, V, W)$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$ satisfying
$$\nabla \cdot v = 0, \qquad \nabla \times v = \alpha\, v,$$
where $\alpha: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}$ is a scalar field.

**Key identity.** The divergence of a curl is identically zero:
$$\nabla \cdot (\nabla \times v) = 0 \quad \text{for any smooth } v.$$

Applying this to the given relation $\nabla \times v = \alpha\, v$:

$$0 = \nabla \cdot (\nabla \times v) = \nabla \cdot (\alpha\, v).$$

**Product rule for divergence.** For a scalar field $\alpha$ and vector field $v
$:
$$\nabla \cdot (\alpha\, v) = (\nabla \alpha) \cdot v + \alpha\,(\nabla \cdot v)
.$$

Substituting the two given conditions $\nabla \cdot v = 0$ and $\nabla \times v
= \alpha v$:

$$0 = (\nabla \alpha) \cdot v + \alpha \cdot 0 = v \cdot \nabla \alpha.$$

Therefore,

$$\boxed{v \cdot \nabla \alpha = 0}.$$

**Interpretation.** The scalar field $\alpha$ is constant along the flow lines (
integral curves) of $v$. This is a natural compatibility condition: the Beltrami
-type equation $\nabla \times v = \alpha v$ combined with the solenoidal conditi
on $\nabla \cdot v = 0$ forces $\alpha$ to be advected unchanged along $v$'s own
 streamlines.
