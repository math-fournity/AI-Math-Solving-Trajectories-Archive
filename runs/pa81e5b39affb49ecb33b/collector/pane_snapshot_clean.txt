## Proof

A representation $R: G \to GL(V)$ is, by definition, a **group homomorphism**. T
his means that for all $g, h \in G$,

$$R(gh) = R(g)\,R(h),$$

where the right-hand side is matrix multiplication in $GL(V)$.

Apply this with $h = g^{-1}$, the group inverse of $g$ (which exists since $G$ i
s a group):

$$R(g\,g^{-1}) = R(g)\,R(g^{-1}).$$

Since $g\,g^{-1} = e$ (the identity of $G$), and any representation maps the ide
ntity to the identity matrix (because $R(e) = R(e\,e) = R(e)^2$, and $R(e)$ is i
nvertible, so multiplying by $R(e)^{-1}$ gives $R(e) = I$), we obtain

$$I = R(g)\,R(g^{-1}).$$

By the uniqueness of the matrix inverse in $GL(V)$, this gives

$$R(g^{-1}) = R(g)^{-1}.$$

The left-hand side $R^{-1}(g)$ in the problem statement denotes the matrix inver
se of the irrep matrix $R(g)$, i.e., $[R(g)]^{-1}$. The right-hand side $R(g^{-1
})$ is the irrep of the group inverse $g^{-1}$. We have shown these are equal.

**Note on irreducibility:** The argument above uses only the homomorphism proper
ty of $R$, which holds for **every** representation — reducible or irreducible.
The irreducibility hypothesis is not needed; the statement is true for all repre
sentations.

Therefore the statement is **true**:

$$R^{-1}(g) = R(g^{-1}).$$
