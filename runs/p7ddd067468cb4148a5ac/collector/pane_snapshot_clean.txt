## Proof

**Notation.** For $q = a/b$ in lowest terms ($b > 0$, $\gcd(a,b)=1$), denote $\o
peratorname{den}(q) = b$. The condition is $\operatorname{den}(f(q)) = \operator
name{den}(q)$ for all $q \in \mathbb{Q}$, which splits into:

- **(C1)** $b \cdot f(a/b) \in \mathbb{Z}$ for all $a \in \mathbb{Z},\; b \geq 1
$ (denominator of $f(q)$ divides $b$).
- **(C2)** $\gcd(b\cdot f(a/b),\; b) = 1$ whenever $\gcd(a,b)=1$ (denominator eq
uals $b$ exactly).

---

**Step 1: $f(0) \in \mathbb{Z}$ and reduction to $f(0)=0$.**

Since $\operatorname{den}(0)=1$, condition (C1) gives $f(0) \in \mathbb{Z}$. Set
 $m = f(0)$ and replace $f$ by $\tilde{f}(x) = f(x) - m$. Then $\tilde{f}(0)=0$,
 $\tilde{f}$ is $C^1$, and $\tilde{f}$ still satisfies (C1) and (C2) (subtractin
g an integer preserves both the integrality of $b\tilde{f}(a/b)$ and the coprima
lity $\gcd(b\tilde{f}(a/b), b)=1$). **Henceforth assume $f(0)=0$.**

---

**Step 2: $f'$ is integer-valued on a dense set, hence everywhere.**

For each $b \geq 1$, define $F_b : \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}$ by $F_b(a) = b\cdo
t f(a/b)$. This is well-defined into $\mathbb{Z}$ by (C1).

For any $a \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $b \geq 1$, the Mean Value Theorem applied to $f$
 on $[a/b,\,(a+1)/b]$ gives a point $\xi_{a,b} \in (a/b,\,(a+1)/b)$ with

$$f'\!\left(\xi_{a,b}\right) = \frac{f((a{+}1)/b) - f(a/b)}{1/b} = b\Big(f\!\lef
t(\tfrac{a+1}{b}\right) - f\!\left(\tfrac{a}{b}\right)\Big) = F_b(a{+}1) - F_b(a
) \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

Now fix any $x_0 \in \mathbb{R}$. For each large $b$, set $a_b = \lfloor x_0\, b
 \rfloor$, so $x_0 \in [a_b/b,\;(a_b+1)/b]$. The corresponding $\xi_b := \xi_{a_
b, b}$ satisfies $|\xi_b - x_0| \leq 1/b \to 0$ and $f'(\xi_b) \in \mathbb{Z}$.
Since $f'$ is continuous,

$$f'(x_0) = \lim_{b\to\infty} f'(\xi_b) \in \mathbb{Z},$$

because $\mathbb{Z}$ is closed. **Thus $f'(\mathbb{R}) \subseteq \mathbb{Z}$.**

---

**Step 3: $f'$ is constant.**

The function $f': \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{Z}$ is continuous, and $\mathbb{R}$ is
connected while $\mathbb{Z}$ is totally disconnected. Therefore $f'$ is constant
: $f'(x) = c$ for some $c \in \mathbb{Z}$ and all $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

Integrating with $f(0)=0$: $f(x) = cx$.

---

**Step 4: Determine $c$ using (C2).**

For $q = a/b$ with $\gcd(a,b)=1$: $f(a/b) = ca/b$, so $b\cdot f(a/b) = ca$. Cond
ition (C2) requires $\gcd(ca, b) = 1$ whenever $\gcd(a,b)=1$.

Since $\gcd(a,b)=1$ implies $\gcd(ca,b) = \gcd(c,b)$, we need $\gcd(c,b)=1$ for
**every** $b \geq 1$. This forces $c = \pm 1$ (any other integer shares a prime
factor with some $b$).

---

**Step 5: Restore the constant and verify.**

Recall we reduced to $f(0)=0$ by subtracting $m = f(0) \in \mathbb{Z}$. Thus the
 general solution is

$$f(x) = x + n \quad \text{or} \quad f(x) = -x + n, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

**Verification.** For $f(x) = \varepsilon x + n$ with $\varepsilon \in \{+1,-1\}
$ and $n \in \mathbb{Z}$: given $q = a/b$ in lowest terms,

$$f(q) = \frac{\varepsilon a + nb}{b}, \qquad \gcd(\varepsilon a + nb,\; b) = \g
cd(\varepsilon a,\; b) = \gcd(a,b) = 1.$$

So $f(q)$ has denominator $b$. Both $f$ and $f'$ are continuous, so $f \in C^1$.

**Conclusion.** The continuously differentiable functions satisfying the conditi
on are exactly

$$\boxed{f(x) = x + n \quad \text{and} \quad f(x) = -x + n, \qquad n \in \mathbb
{Z}.}$$
