## Part (1)

Let $f(t)=xt^2+yt$ with $x>0$ and $t\in[0,1]$. Since $x>0$, the parabola opens u
pward with vertex at $t^*=-\frac{y}{2x}$, where $f(t^*)=-\frac{y^2}{4x}$. Also $
f(0)=0$, $f(1)=x+y$.

**Case 1: $t^*<0$ (i.e. $y>0$).** $f$ is increasing on $[0,1]$. Max $=x+y$, Min
$=0$, so $D=x+y$.

**Case 2: $t^*>1$ (i.e. $y<-2x$).** $f$ is decreasing on $[0,1]$. Max $=0$, Min
$=x+y$, so $D=-x-y$.

**Case 3: $t^*\in[0,1]$ (i.e. $-2x\le y\le 0$).** Min $=-\frac{y^2}{4x}$. Max $=
\max(0,\,x+y)$.

- **3a:** $x+y\ge 0$ (i.e. $-x\le y\le 0$): Max $=x+y$, so $D=x+y+\frac{y^2}{4x}
=\frac{(2x+y)^2}{4x}$.
- **3b:** $x+y<0$ (i.e. $-2x\le y<-x$): Max $=0$, so $D=\frac{y^2}{4x}$.

One checks continuity at $y=0,\,-x,\,-2x$. In summary:

$$D(x,y)=\begin{cases} x+y & y>0\\[4pt] \dfrac{(2x+y)^2}{4x} & -x\le y\le 0\\[6p
t] \dfrac{y^2}{4x} & -2x\le y<-x\\[6pt] -x-y & y<-2x \end{cases}$$

## Part (2)

The condition is: $\exists\, z$ such that $\forall\, t\in[0,1]$, $\;0\le xt^2+yt
+z\le 1$. This requires

$$\max_{t\in[0,1]}(-f(t))\;\le\;\min_{t\in[0,1]}(1-f(t))\quad\Longleftrightarrow
\quad -\min f\le 1-\max f\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad D(x,y)\le 1.$$

So $S=\{(x,y):x>0,\; D(x,y)\le 1\}$. Applying $D\le 1$ region by region:

- **$y>0$:** $x+y\le 1\Rightarrow y\le 1-x$ (needs $x<1$).
- **$-x\le y\le 0$:** $(2x+y)^2\le 4x\Rightarrow 2x+y\le 2\sqrt{x}$ (since $2x+y
>0$), i.e. $y\le 2\sqrt{x}-2x$.
- **$-2x\le y<-x$:** $y^2\le 4x\Rightarrow y\ge -2\sqrt{x}$ (since $y<0$).
- **$y<-2x$:** $-x-y\le 1\Rightarrow y\ge -x-1$ (needs $x<1$).

**Assembling $S$:**

For $0<x\le 1$: the four regions concatenate seamlessly to give $\boxed{-x-1\le
y\le 1-x}$ (a strip of width $2$ between the parallel lines $y=1-x$ and $y=-x-1$
).

For $1\le x\le 4$: regions 1 and 4 are empty; the binding constraints from regio
ns 2 and 3 give $\boxed{-2\sqrt{x}\le y\le 2\sqrt{x}-2x}$.

At $x=4$: both bounds equal $-4$, so $S$ narrows to the single point $(4,-4)$. F
or $x>4$: $S$ is empty.

**Outline of $S$:** The upper boundary is $y=1-x$ for $0<x\le 1$, then $y=2\sqrt
{x}-2x$ for $1\le x\le 4$. The lower boundary is $y=-x-1$ for $0<x\le 1$, then $
y=-2\sqrt{x}$ for $1\le x\le 4$. At $x=1$ both upper and lower curves join smoot
hly (matching values and slopes), and the region closes at the cusp point $(4,-4
)$. As $x\to 0^+$, $S$ approaches the open segment $\{(0,y):-1\le y\le 1\}$.

## Part (3)

Here $0\le x\le 1$ and $z$ is a coordinate. For fixed $(x,y)$, valid $z$ satisfi
es $-\min f\le z\le 1-\max f$, giving a $z$-interval of length $1-D(x,y)$. Thus

$$\text{Vol}(V)=\int_0^1\!\int_{-x-1}^{1-x}\!\bigl(1-D(x,y)\bigr)\,dy\,dx,$$

splitting the $y$-range into the four sub-intervals from Part (1):

$$I_1=\int_0^{1-x}(1-x-y)\,dy=\tfrac{(1-x)^2}{2},\qquad I_4=\int_{-x-1}^{-2x}(1+
x+y)\,dy=\tfrac{(1-x)^2}{2}.$$

$$I_2=\int_{-x}^{0}\!\Bigl(1-\tfrac{(2x+y)^2}{4x}\Bigr)dy=\Bigl[u-\tfrac{u^3}{12
x}\Bigr]_{x}^{2x}=x-\tfrac{7x^2}{12},\quad (u=2x+y).$$

$$I_3=\int_{-2x}^{-x}\!\Bigl(1-\tfrac{y^2}{4x}\Bigr)dy=\Bigl[y-\tfrac{y^3}{12x}\
Bigr]_{-2x}^{-x}=x-\tfrac{7x^2}{12}.$$

(The equalities $I_1=I_4$ and $I_2=I_3$ reflect the symmetry $D(x,y)=D(x,-2x-y)$
, i.e. reflection about $y=-x$.)

Summing:

$$I_1+I_2+I_3+I_4=(1-x)^2+2x-\tfrac{7x^2}{6}=1-2x+x^2+2x-\tfrac{7x^2}{6}=1-\tfra
c{x^2}{6}.$$

Therefore

$$\text{Vol}(V)=\int_0^1\!\Bigl(1-\frac{x^2}{6}\Bigr)dx=\Bigl[x-\frac{x^3}{18}\B
