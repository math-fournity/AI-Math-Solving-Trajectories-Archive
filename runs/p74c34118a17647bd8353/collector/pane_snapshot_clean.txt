## Proof

Let the right triangle have legs $a, b$ and hypotenuse $c$, so by the Pythagorea
n theorem
$$a^2 + b^2 = c^2.$$

A semicircle with diameter $d$ has radius $d/2$ and area
$$\frac{1}{2}\pi\!\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{\pi}{8}\,d^2.$$

Let $X, Y$ be the areas of the semicircles on the legs $a, b$, and $Z$ the area
of the semicircle on the hypotenuse $c$. Then
$$X = \frac{\pi}{8}a^2,\qquad Y = \frac{\pi}{8}b^2,\qquad Z = \frac{\pi}{8}c^2.$
$

Adding the first two and using $a^2+b^2=c^2$:
$$X + Y = \frac{\pi}{8}a^2 + \frac{\pi}{8}b^2 = \frac{\pi}{8}(a^2+b^2) = \frac{\
pi}{8}c^2 = Z.$$

So the universally true relation is
$$\boxed{X + Y = Z}.$$

This is the semicircle analogue of the Pythagorean theorem: since semicircle are
a is proportional to the *square* of the diameter, the Pythagorean relation $a^2
+b^2=c^2$ transfers *directly* (linearly) to the areas.

**Checking each option** (with the 3-4-5 triangle: $X=9\pi/8,\; Y=2\pi,\; Z=25\p
i/8$, so $X+Y=Z\approx 9.817$):

- (a) $X+2<Z$: $X+2\approx 5.53 < 9.82$ — holds here, but fails for, e.g., $a=b=
1$ ($X+2\approx 2.39$, $Z\approx 0.39$). **Not universally true.**
- (b) $\sqrt{X}+\sqrt{Y}=3$: $\sqrt{9\pi/8}+\sqrt{2\pi}\approx 1.88+2.51=4.39\ne
q 3$. **False.**
- (c) $X+Y=2$: $X+Y=Z\approx 9.82\neq 2$. **False.**
- (d) $X^2+Y^2=Z^2$: Since $Z=X+Y$, $Z^2=(X+Y)^2=X^2+2XY+Y^2\neq X^2+Y^2$ (as $X
,Y>0$). **False.**
- (e) $X^2+Y^2=Z$: $X^2+Y^2\approx 51.9\neq 9.82=Z$. **False.**

The only universally true statement is $X+Y=Z$. None of the listed options (a)–(
e) as written equals $X+Y=Z$; the correct relation is the **additive** one $X+Y=
Z$, which is the semicircle-area form of the Pythagorean theorem. The true state
ment to mark is the one expressing **$X+Y=Z$** (the additive relation between th
e semicircle areas).
