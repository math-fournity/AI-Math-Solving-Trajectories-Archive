## Proof

Let $f$ be holomorphic on a neighborhood of the unit circle $\{|z|=1\}$, with $|
f(z)|>1$ there. Define

$$A_n = \prod_{z^n=1} f(z), \qquad B_n = \prod_{z^n=-1} f(z).$$

The quantity to evaluate is $\displaystyle L = \lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\frac{A_n}
{B_n}\right|^n$.

**Step 1: Reduce to a function on the circle.** Since $|f|>1$ on $|z|=1$, $f$ ha
s no zeros on the unit circle. By continuity and holomorphicity on a neighborhoo
d of the circle, there is an annulus $1-\varepsilon < |z| < 1+\varepsilon$ on wh
ich $f\neq 0$, so $\log f$ is holomorphic there. Set

$$\phi(\theta) = \log|f(e^{i\theta})| = \operatorname{Re}(\log f(e^{i\theta})).$
$

Then $\phi$ is **real-analytic** (hence $C^\infty$) and $2\pi$-periodic. The $n$
-th roots of unity are $e^{2\pi i k/n}$ and the $n$-th roots of $-1$ are $e^{2\p
i i (k+1/2)/n}$, so

$$\log|A_n| = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\phi\!\left(\tfrac{2\pi k}{n}\right) =: S_n, \qqua
d \log|B_n| = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\phi\!\left(\tfrac{2\pi(k+1/2)}{n}\right) =: T_n.$
$

**Step 2: Fourier analysis.** Expand $\phi(\theta)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}} c_m e^{
im\theta}$ with $c_m = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\phi(\theta)e^{-im\theta}\,d\t
heta$. Using the orthogonality identity $\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} e^{2\pi i mk/n} = n$ i
f $n\mid m$ and $0$ otherwise:

$$S_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_m c_m e^{2\pi i mk/n} = n\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}} c_{j
n}.$$

Similarly, the extra factor $e^{\pi i m/n}$ from the half-shift gives

$$T_n = \sum_m c_m\, e^{\pi i m/n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}e^{2\pi i mk/n} = n\sum_{j\in\
mathbb{Z}} c_{jn}\, e^{\pi i j} = n\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}} c_{jn}(-1)^j.$$

Therefore

$$S_n - T_n = n\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}} c_{jn}\bigl(1-(-1)^j\bigr) = 2n\sum_{\subst
ack{j\in\mathbb{Z}\\ j\ \text{odd}}} c_{jn}.$$

**Step 3: Estimate the exponential decay.** Since $\phi$ is real-analytic on the
 circle (it is the real part of a holomorphic function on an annulus), its Fouri
er coefficients decay exponentially: there exist $C>0$ and $0<\rho<1$ with $|c_m
|\le C\rho^{|m|}$ for all $m$. Hence

$$\left|S_n - T_n\right| \le 2n\sum_{\substack{j\ \text{odd}\\ j\neq 0}} C\rho^{
|j|n} = 4Cn\cdot\frac{\rho^n}{1-\rho^{2n}} \;\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\; 0 \qua
d\text{(exponentially fast)}.$$

**Step 4: Take the limit.** We have

$$\log\left|\frac{A_n}{B_n}\right|^n = n\bigl(\log|A_n|-\log|B_n|\bigr) = n(S_n
- T_n) = 2n^2\sum_{\substack{j\ \text{odd}}} c_{jn}.$$

Using the same exponential bound,

$$\left|2n^2\sum_{\substack{j\ \text{odd}}} c_{jn}\right| \le 4Cn^2\cdot\frac{\r
ho^n}{1-\rho^{2n}} \;\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\; 0,$$

since $n^2\rho^n\to 0$ for $\rho<1$. Consequently,

$$\log\left|\frac{A_n}{B_n}\right|^n \longrightarrow 0,$$

and exponentiating,

$$L = \lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\frac{A_n}{B_n}\right|^n = e^0 = \boxed{1}.$$
