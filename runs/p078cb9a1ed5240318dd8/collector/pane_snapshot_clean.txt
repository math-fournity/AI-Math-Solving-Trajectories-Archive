We evaluate $I(k) = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} e^{ik\tan\phi}\,d\phi$ for $k\in\mathb
b{R}$.

### Step 1: Substitution

Let $t = \tan\phi$. Then $dt = \sec^2\phi\,d\phi = (1+t^2)\,d\phi$, so $d\phi =
\dfrac{dt}{1+t^2}$. As $\phi: -\pi/2 \to \pi/2$, we have $t: -\infty \to \infty$
. Thus

$$I(k) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{ikt}}{1+t^2}\,dt.$$

### Step 2: Contour integration for $k > 0$

Consider $f(z) = \dfrac{e^{ikz}}{1+z^2} = \dfrac{e^{ikz}}{(z-i)(z+i)}$ and the c
losed contour $\Gamma_R$ consisting of the real segment $[-R, R]$ and the semici
rcle $C_R = \{Re^{i\theta} : \theta\in[0,\pi]\}$ in the **upper** half-plane.

**Vanishing of the arc integral.** On $C_R$, $z = Re^{i\theta}$ and
$$|e^{ikz}| = e^{-kR\sin\theta} \leq 1 \quad (\text{since } \sin\theta\geq 0,\ k
>0).$$
Hence
$$\left|\int_{C_R} f(z)\,dz\right| \leq \frac{1}{R^2-1}\cdot \pi R \xrightarrow{
R\to\infty} 0.$$

**Residue.** The only pole inside $\Gamma_R$ is $z = i$ (simple pole). Its resid
ue is
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=i} f(z) = \frac{e^{ik\cdot i}}{i+i} = \frac{e^{-k}}{2i}.
$$

By the residue theorem,
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{ikt}}{1+t^2}\,dt = 2\pi i \cdot \frac{e^{-k}}
{2i} = \pi e^{-k}, \qquad k > 0.$$

### Step 3: Contour integration for $k < 0$

Now close in the **lower** half-plane (semicircle $C_R^-$ traversed clockwise).
For $k < 0$, $|e^{ikz}| = e^{-kR\sin\theta}$ with $\sin\theta \leq 0$ in the low
er half-plane gives $|e^{ikz}|\leq 1$, and the arc integral again vanishes.

The only pole enclosed is $z = -i$, with residue
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=-i} f(z) = \frac{e^{ik(-i)}}{-i-i} = \frac{e^{k}}{-2i}.$
$

The clockwise orientation contributes a factor of $-2\pi i$:
$$I(k) = -2\pi i \cdot \frac{e^{k}}{-2i} = \pi e^{k} = \pi e^{-|k|}, \qquad k <
0.$$

### Step 4: The case $k = 0$

$$I(0) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{1+t^2} = \arctan(t)\Big|_{-\infty}^{\i
nfty} = \pi = \pi e^{0}.$$

### Conclusion

Combining all three cases:

$$\boxed{I(k) = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} e^{ik\tan\phi}\,d\phi = \pi\, e^{-|k|}, \q
quad k\in\mathbb{R}.}$$
