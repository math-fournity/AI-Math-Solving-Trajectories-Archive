## Proof

Let $Z_i = X_i - Y_i$. Then $Z_1, Z_2, \ldots$ are i.i.d. with
$$\mathbb{E}[Z_i] = \mathbb{E}[X_i] - \mathbb{E}[Y_i] = 0 - 1 = -1,$$
$$\operatorname{Var}(Z_i) = \operatorname{Var}(X_i) + \operatorname{Var}(Y_i) =
1 + 1 = 2,$$
where independence of $X_i$ and $Y_i$ is used.

The quantity of interest is
$$P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^n X_i \ge \sum_{i=1}^n Y_i\right) = P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^n
Z_i \ge 0\right).$$

Set $W_n = \sum_{i=1}^n Z_i$. Then $\mathbb{E}[W_n] = -n$ and $\operatorname{Var
}(W_n) = 2n$. Standardizing,
$$P(W_n \ge 0) = P\!\left(\frac{W_n - (-n)}{\sqrt{2n}} \ge \frac{0 - (-n)}{\sqrt
{2n}}\right) = P\!\left(\frac{W_n + n}{\sqrt{2n}} \ge \sqrt{\frac{n}{2}}\right).
$$

By the Central Limit Theorem, since the $Z_i$ are i.i.d. with finite mean $-1$ a
nd finite variance $2$,
$$\frac{W_n + n}{\sqrt{2n}} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n (Z_i - \mathbb{E}[Z_i])}{\sqrt{
n\operatorname{Var}(Z_i)}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0,1).$$

Let $\Phi$ denote the standard normal CDF. Then
$$P\!\left(\frac{W_n + n}{\sqrt{2n}} \ge \sqrt{\tfrac{n}{2}}\right) = 1 - \Phi\!
\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{n}{2}}\right) + \text{(negligible CLT error)}.$$

Since $\sqrt{n/2} \to \infty$ as $n \to \infty$ and $\Phi(t) \to 1$ as $t \to \i
nfty$, we get
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^n X_i \ge \sum_{i=1}^n Y_i\right) = \lim
_{n\to\infty}\left[1 - \Phi\!\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{n}{2}}\right)\right] = 0.$$

**Intuition:** $\sum X_i$ has mean $0$ while $\sum Y_i$ has mean $n$; the gap of
 $n$ in means grows linearly, while fluctuations grow only as $\sqrt{n}$, so the
 probability that the normal sum catches up to the exponential sum vanishes.
