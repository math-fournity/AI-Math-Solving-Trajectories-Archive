## Proof

We prove the claim by contradiction.

**Setup.** Suppose, toward a contradiction, that no such $c$ exists. Then for ev
ery $c \in F$ there is some $p_c \in Q$ with
$$p_c \Vdash \dot{a} \neq c.$$

**Using $n$-linkedness.** Since $|F| \leq n$, the family $\{p_c : c \in F\} \sub
seteq Q$ has cardinality $\leq n$. By hypothesis $Q$ is $n$-linked, meaning ever
y subfamily of $Q$ of size $\leq n$ has a common extension (lower bound). Hence
there exists $q \in \mathbb{P}$ with $q \leq p_c$ for every $c \in F$.

**Deriving the contradiction.** Forcing statements are preserved downward: if $p
_c \Vdash \dot{a} \neq c$ and $q \leq p_c$, then $q \Vdash \dot{a} \neq c$. Appl
ying this for every $c \in F$, we obtain
$$q \Vdash \dot{a} \neq c \quad\text{for all } c \in F,$$
i.e.,
$$q \Vdash \dot{a} \notin F.$$

But we are given $\Vdash \dot{a} \in F$, which means *every* condition forces $\
dot{a} \in F$; in particular $q \Vdash \dot{a} \in F$. This contradicts $q \Vdas
h \dot{a} \notin F$ (a condition cannot force both $\dot{a} \in F$ and $\dot{a}
\notin F$, since $q$ has some extension into the generic filter and the generic
value of $\dot{a}$ is a single element).

**Conclusion.** The contradiction shows that our supposition was false. Therefor
e there exists $c \in F$ such that for all $p \in Q$,
$$p \nVdash \dot{a} \neq c.$$

The element $c$ is: **any member of $F$ for which no $p \in Q$ forces $\dot{a} \
neq c$** — whose existence is guaranteed by the argument above (if every $c \in
F$ were "ruled out" by some $p_c \in Q$, the $n$-linkedness of $Q$ would yield a
 single $q$ ruling out all of $F$, contradicting $\Vdash \dot{a} \in F$).
