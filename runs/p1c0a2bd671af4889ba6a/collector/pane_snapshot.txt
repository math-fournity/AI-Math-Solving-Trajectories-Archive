Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum time to get $n$ adult worms
from 1 adult worm, where cutting an adult worm (length 1m) into pieces of length
s $a$ and $1-a$ creates two worms that grow at 1 m/hr, becoming adults after $1-
a$ and $a$ hours respectively.

**Answer:** $T(n) = 1 - \dfrac{1}{2^{n-1}}$ hours.

---

## Proof

Let $T(n)$ denote the minimum time to obtain $n$ adult worms from one adult worm
.

### Upper Bound (Construction)

We prove $T(n) \leq 1 - 2^{1-n}$ by induction.

**Base case:** $T(1) = 0 = 1 - 2^0$. ✓

**Inductive step:** Assume $T(k) \leq 1 - 2^{1-k}$ for all $k < n$. Cut the init
ial worm into a piece of length $a = 2^{1-n}$ and a piece of length $1-a = 1 - 2
^{1-n}$.

- **Long piece** (length $1-a$): becomes adult after time $a = 2^{1-n}$ hours. F
rom it, produce $n-1$ adults in time $T(n-1) \leq 1 - 2^{2-n}$. Total: $2^{1-n}
+ 1 - 2^{2-n} = 1 - 2^{1-n}$.
- **Short piece** (length $a$): becomes adult after time $1-a = 1 - 2^{1-n}$ hou
rs, yielding 1 adult. Total: $1 - 2^{1-n}$.

Both branches finish by time $1 - 2^{1-n}$. ✓

### Lower Bound

We prove $T(n) \geq 1 - 2^{1-n}$ by induction.

**Base case:** $T(1) = 0 = 1 - 1$. ✓

**Inductive step:** In any strategy, the first cut divides the worm into pieces
of lengths $a$ and $1-a$ ($0 < a < 1$). The piece of length $a$ becomes adult at
 time $1-a$ and its lineage produces some $j$ adults ($1 \leq j \leq n-1$). The
piece of length $1-a$ becomes adult at time $a$ and its lineage produces $n-j$ a
dults.

By the induction hypothesis, the total time is at least:
$$\max\Big((1-a) + \big(1 - 2^{1-j}\big),\;\; a + \big(1 - 2^{1-(n-j)}\big)\Big)
$$

**Optimizing over $a$:** The expression $\max(f(a), g(a))$ where $f(a) = (1-a) +
 (1-2^{1-j})$ is decreasing and $g(a) = a + (1 - 2^{1-(n-j)})$ is increasing is
minimized when $f(a) = g(a)$, giving:
$$a = \frac{1 + (1 - 2^{1-j}) - (1 - 2^{1-(n-j)})}{2} = \frac{1 - 2^{1-j} + 2^{1
-(n-j)}}{2}$$

Since all $T$-values lie in $[0,1)$, one verifies $0 < a < 1$, so this is feasib
le. The balanced time equals:
$$\frac{1 + (1-2^{1-j}) + (1-2^{1-(n-j)})}{2} = \frac{3 - 2^{1-j} - 2^{1-(n-j)}}
{2}$$

**Optimizing over $j$:** We minimize the above, equivalently maximizing $h(j) =
2^{1-j} + 2^{1-(n-j)}$ over $j \in \{1, \ldots, n-1\}$.

The function $f(k) = 2^{1-k}$ is convex. For $h(j) = f(j) + f(n-j)$: the derivat
ive $h'(j) = f'(j) - f'(n-j)$ is negative for $j < n/2$ and positive for $j > n/
2$ (since $f'$ is increasing). Thus $h$ is **minimized** at $j = n/2$ and **maxi
mized** at the endpoints $j=1$ or $j = n-1$.

At $j = 1$ (or $j = n-1$):
$$h(1) = 2^{0} + 2^{1-(n-1)} = 1 + 2^{2-n}$$

Therefore the minimum possible time is:
$$\frac{3 - 1 - 2^{2-n}}{2} = \frac{2 - 2^{2-n}}{2} = 1 - 2^{1-n}$$

So $T(n) \geq 1 - 2^{1-n}$. ✓

### Conclusion

Combining both bounds:

$$\boxed{T(n) = 1 - \frac{1}{2^{n-1}} \text{ hours}}$$
