Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether, for every non-constant con
tinuous $f:[0,1]\to[0,1]$, there exists an interval $(a,b)$ where $f$ is non-con
stant and a concave or convex $g:(a,b)\to[0,1]$ with $|f_p(x)-g_p(x)|<1/100$ for
 all $x\in[0,1]$.

Since $g$ is defined on $(a,b)$ but the condition is on $[0,1]$, I interpret $f_
p=f$ (already on $[0,1]$) and $g_p$ as the extension of $g$ to $[0,1]$ by consta
nt padding (using one-sided limits at endpoints, which exist since $g$ is concav
e/convex and bounded).

**The answer is NO.** I provide a counterexample.

---

**Counterexample.** Let $f(x)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sin(4\pi x)$. This is cont
inuous, non-constant, and maps $[0,1]\to[0,1]$. Note:
$$f(0)=\tfrac12,\quad f(\tfrac18)=1,\quad f(\tfrac38)=0,\quad f(\tfrac58)=1,\qua
d f(\tfrac78)=0,\quad f(1)=\tfrac12.$$

Suppose for contradiction that there exist $(a,b)$ with $0\le a<b\le 1$, $f$ non
-constant on $(a,b)$, and a continuous concave or convex $g:(a,b)\to[0,1]$ with
$|f_p(x)-g_p(x)|<\frac{1}{100}$ for all $x\in[0,1]$.

**Step 1: $a<\frac18$ and $b>\frac78$.**

If $a>0$: On $[0,a]$, $g_p$ is constant, say $g_p\equiv c$. Then $|f(x)-c|<\frac
{1}{100}$ for all $x\in[0,a]$, so the oscillation of $f$ on $[0,a]$ is $<\frac{2
}{100}=\frac{1}{50}$. But if $a\ge\frac18$, then $0,\frac18\in[0,a]$ and $|f(0)-
f(\frac18)|=\frac12>\frac{1}{50}$, contradiction. So $a<\frac18$.

If $a=0$: $a<\frac18$ trivially.

If $b<1$: On $[b,1]$, $g_p$ is constant, say $g_p\equiv d$. Then the oscillation
 of $f$ on $[b,1]$ is $<\frac{1}{50}$. But if $b\le\frac78$, then $\frac78,1\in[
b,1]$ and $|f(\frac78)-f(1)|=\frac12>\frac{1}{50}$, contradiction. So $b>\frac78
$.

If $b=1$: $b>\frac78$ trivially.

**Step 2: Contradiction on $(a,b)$.**

Since $a<\frac18$ and $b>\frac78$, all four points $\frac18,\frac38,\frac58,\fra
c78$ lie in $(a,b)$. On $(a,b)$, $f_p=f$ and $g_p=g$, so $|f(x)-g(x)|<\frac{1}{1
00}$ for all $x\in(a,b)$. Therefore:
$$g(\tfrac18)>\tfrac{99}{100},\quad g(\tfrac38)<\tfrac{1}{100},\quad g(\tfrac58)
>\tfrac{99}{100},\quad g(\tfrac78)<\tfrac{1}{100}.$$

**Case 1: $g$ is concave.** The points $\frac18<\frac38<\frac58$ are equally spa
ced ($\frac38=\frac{1/8+5/8}{2}$). By concavity:
$$g(\tfrac38)\ge\frac{g(\frac18)+g(\frac58)}{2}>\frac{99/100+99/100}{2}=\frac{99
}{100}.$$
But $g(\frac38)<\frac{1}{100}<\frac{99}{100}$. **Contradiction.**

**Case 2: $g$ is convex.** The points $\frac38<\frac58<\frac78$ are equally spac
ed ($\frac58=\frac{3/8+7/8}{2}$). By convexity:
$$g(\tfrac58)\le\frac{g(\frac38)+g(\frac78)}{2}<\frac{1/100+1/100}{2}=\frac{1}{1
00}.$$
But $g(\frac58)>\frac{99}{100}>\frac{1}{100}$. **Contradiction.**

(If $g$ is affine—both concave and convex—both cases apply, yielding contradicti
on from each.)

In all cases we reach a contradiction. Therefore, for this $f$, no such interval
 $(a,b)$ and function $g$ exist.

**Conclusion.** The answer is **no**: there exists a non-constant continuous fun
ction $f:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ (namely $f(x)=\frac12+\frac12\sin(4\pi x)$) for which no
 interval $(a,b)$ on which $f$ is non-constant and no continuous concave or conv
ex $g:(a,b)\to[0,1]$ can satisfy $|f_p(x)-g_p(x)|<\frac{1}{100}$ for all $x\in[0
,1]$.

The essential obstacle is that a concave (resp. convex) function is unimodal—it
can change monotonicity direction at most once—so it cannot approximate a functi
on that oscillates with amplitude exceeding $\frac{2}{100}$ more than once, whil
e the padding condition forces the interval $(a,b)$ to be large enough to contai
n multiple oscillations.
