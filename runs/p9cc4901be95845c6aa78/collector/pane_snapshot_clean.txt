**Answer:** No. A nonlinear positive operator $N: E \to E$ mapping a cone $K \su
bseteq E$ into itself does **not** necessarily satisfy $N(0)=0$.

---

### Proof

We exhibit an explicit counterexample.

**Setup.** Let $E = \mathbb{R}$ with the usual Banach space structure (absolute
value norm). Let $K = [0,\infty) \subseteq \mathbb{R}$; this is a closed convex
cone with $K \cap (-K) = \{0\}$ and $\lambda K \subseteq K$ for all $\lambda \ge
q 0$, so $K$ is a proper cone in $E$.

**The operator.** Define $N: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ by
$$N(x) = x^2 + 1.$$

**$N$ is nonlinear.** Indeed, $N(2x) = 4x^2 + 1 \neq 2(x^2+1) = 2N(x)$ in genera
l (e.g., at $x=1$: $N(2)=5 \neq 4 = 2N(1)$), and $N(x+y) = x^2+2xy+y^2+1 \neq N(
x)+N(y)$. So $N$ is not linear; it is a nonlinear operator.

**$N$ maps $K$ into itself (positivity).** For any $x \in K = [0,\infty)$, we ha
ve $x^2 \geq 0$, hence
$$N(x) = x^2 + 1 \geq 1 > 0,$$
so $N(x) \in K$. Thus $N(K) \subseteq K$, i.e., $N$ is a positive operator on th
e cone $K$.

**$N(0) \neq 0$.** Computing at the origin:
$$N(0) = 0^2 + 1 = 1 \neq 0.$$

**Conclusion.** The operator $N(x)=x^2+1$ is a nonlinear positive operator on th
e Banach space $E=\mathbb{R}$ mapping the cone $K=[0,\infty)$ into itself, yet $
N(0)=1\neq 0$. Therefore a nonlinear positive operator does **not** necessarily
satisfy $N(0)=0$.

---

**Remark.** The contrast is with the *linear* case: if $L:E\to E$ is linear and
$L(K)\subseteq K$, then $L(0)=0$ holds automatically by linearity ($L(0)=L(0\cdo
t 0)=0\cdot L(0)=0$). The question's hypothesis of *nonlinearity* is precisely w
hat removes this automatic guarantee, and the counterexample above confirms that
 $N(0)=0$ becomes an *additional* independent condition—one that must be imposed
 separately when needed (as is common, for instance, in Krasnoselskii's fixed-po
int theory on cones, where $N(0)=0$ is often listed as an explicit hypothesis ra
ther than derived).
