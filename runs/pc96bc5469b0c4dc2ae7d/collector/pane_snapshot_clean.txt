Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether an elliptic curve over $\ma
thbb{Q}$ with good reduction at 2 and 3 always admits a minimal integral Weierst
rass equation in short form $y^2 = x^3 + Ax + B$.

## Proof

**Answer: No.** In fact, for *any* elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ with good re
duction at 2, no short Weierstrass equation $y^2 = x^3 + Ax + B$ with $A, B \in
\mathbb{Z}$ can be a minimal Weierstrass equation. The good reduction hypothesis
 at 2 makes minimality *impossible* in short form, not easier.

**Key facts.**

1. **Good reduction and the minimal discriminant.** An elliptic curve $E/\mathbb
{Q}$ has good reduction at a prime $p$ if and only if the minimal discriminant $
\Delta_{\min}$ satisfies $v_p(\Delta_{\min}) = 0$ (Silverman, *The Arithmetic of
 Elliptic Curves*, Theorem VII.5.1). Thus, if $E$ has good reduction at 2, then
$v_2(\Delta_{\min}) = 0$.

2. **Discriminant of the short Weierstrass form.** For any equation $y^2 = x^3 +
 Ax + B$ with $A, B \in \mathbb{Z}$, the associated quantities are $a_1 = a_2 =
a_3 = 0$, $a_4 = A$, $a_6 = B$, giving:
$$b_2 = 0,\quad b_4 = 2A,\quad b_6 = 4B,\quad b_8 = -A^2.$$
The discriminant is:
$$\Delta = -b_2^2 b_8 - 8b_4^3 - 27b_6^2 + 9b_2 b_4 b_6 = -8(2A)^3 - 27(4B)^2 =
-64A^3 - 432B^2 = -16(4A^3 + 27B^2).$$

3. **The factor of 16 is unavoidable.** Since $A, B \in \mathbb{Z}$, the quantit
y $4A^3 + 27B^2$ is an integer, so:
$$v_2(\Delta) = v_2(16) + v_2(4A^3 + 27B^2) \geq 4 + 0 = 4.$$
This holds for **every** short Weierstrass equation with integer coefficients, r
egardless of the choice of $A$ and $B$. The factor $16 = 2^4$ is built into the
short form's discriminant formula and cannot be removed by any admissible change
 of variables that preserves the short form (the only such changes are $x = u^2
x'$, $y = u^3 y'$, which scale $\Delta$ by $u^{12}$, preserving or increasing $v
_2$).

**The contradiction.** Suppose $E/\mathbb{Q}$ has good reduction at 2, so $v_2(\
Delta_{\min}) = 0$. If $y^2 = x^3 + Ax + B$ were a minimal integral Weierstrass
equation for $E$, its discriminant would equal $\Delta_{\min}$, requiring:
$$v_2(\Delta) = v_2(\Delta_{\min}) = 0.$$
But by the computation above, $v_2(\Delta) \geq 4 > 0$ for every short Weierstra
ss equation. This is a contradiction.

**Conclusion.** No short Weierstrass equation $y^2 = x^3 + Ax + B$ with $A, B \i
n \mathbb{Z}$ can be minimal at the prime 2 when the curve has good reduction at
 2. The condition of good reduction at 2 (which is part of the hypothesis) is pr
ecisely what makes it impossible: good reduction demands $v_2(\Delta_{\min}) = 0
$, while the short form forces $v_2(\Delta) \geq 4$. The hypothesis of good redu
ction at 3 is not even needed for the argument.

Therefore, it is **not** always possible to find a minimal integral Weierstrass
equation of the form $y^2 = x^3 + Ax + B$ for an elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}
$ with good reduction at 2 and 3. $\blacksquare$
