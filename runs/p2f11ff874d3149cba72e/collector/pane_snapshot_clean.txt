## Proof

A strongly regular graph $G$ with parameters $(n, k, \lambda, \mu)$ has $n$ vert
ices, is $k$-regular, every pair of adjacent vertices has $\lambda$ common neigh
bors, and every pair of non-adjacent vertices has $\mu$ common neighbors.

**Complement parameters.** The complement $\bar{G}$ of a strongly regular graph
with parameters $(n, k, \lambda, \mu)$ is itself strongly regular with parameter
s

$$
(n,\; n-1-k,\; n-2k+\mu-2,\; n-2k+\lambda).
$$

*Derivation.* Each vertex has degree $n-1-k$ in $\bar{G}$. For two adjacent vert
ices $u,v$ in $\bar{G}$ (i.e., non-adjacent in $G$): they have $\mu$ common neig
hbors in $G$, and the remaining $n - 2 - \mu$ vertices (excluding $u,v$ and thei
r $\mu$ common neighbors in $G$) are common neighbors in $\bar{G}$, giving $\lam
bda' = n - 2 - \mu$...

Let me be more careful. Two vertices $u, v$ adjacent in $\bar{G}$ means non-adja
cent in $G$. Common neighbors in $\bar{G}$: a vertex $w \neq u,v$ is a common ne
ighbor of $u,v$ in $\bar{G}$ iff $w$ is adjacent to both $u$ and $v$ in $\bar{G}
$, i.e., non-adjacent to both in $G$. Among the $n-2$ other vertices: $\mu$ are
common neighbors in $G$ (adjacent to both $u,v$ in $G$). Vertices adjacent to $u
$ in $G$ but not $v$: $k - \mu$. Adjacent to $v$ in $G$ but not $u$: $k - \mu$.
Adjacent to neither: $n - 2 - \mu - 2(k-\mu) = n - 2 - 2k + \mu$. So $\lambda' =
 n - 2 - 2k + \mu$.

Similarly for two non-adjacent vertices in $\bar{G}$ (adjacent in $G$): they hav
e $\lambda$ common neighbors in $G$. Vertices adjacent to $u$ not $v$: $k - 1 -
\lambda$. Adjacent to $v$ not $u$: $k - 1 - \lambda$. Adjacent to neither: $n -
2 - \lambda - 2(k-1-\lambda) = n - 2k + \lambda$. So $\mu' = n - 2k + \lambda$.

Thus $\bar{G}$ has parameters $(n,\; n-1-k,\; n-2k+\mu-2,\; n-2k+\lambda)$.

**Isomorphism constraint.** If $G \cong \bar{G}$, their parameters coincide:

$$k = n-1-k, \quad \lambda = n-2k+\mu-2, \quad \mu = n-2k+\lambda.$$

From the first equation: $n = 2k+1$.

Substituting into the second: $\lambda = (2k+1) - 2k + \mu - 2 = \mu - 1$, so $\
lambda = \mu - 1$.

Substituting into the third: $\mu = (2k+1) - 2k + \lambda = \lambda + 1$, so $\m
u = \lambda + 1$.

These are consistent: $\mu = \lambda + 1$.

**Conclusion.** The complement $\bar{G}$ has parameters

$$k' = k, \qquad \lambda' = \lambda, \qquad \mu' = \mu,$$

with the constraint $\mu = \lambda + 1$ (equivalently $\lambda = \mu - 1$). That
 is, the complement of a self-complementary strongly regular graph has the **sam
e** parameters $(k, \lambda, \mu)$ as the original, and these parameters satisfy
 $\boxed{\mu = \lambda + 1}$.
