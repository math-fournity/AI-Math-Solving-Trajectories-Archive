## Solution

**The answer is No.** Not every continuous homomorphism $G(\mathbb{Q}_p) \to S^1
$ is trivial. I provide an explicit counterexample.

### Counterexample: $G = \mathrm{SL}_1(D)$, the quaternion division algebra

Let $D$ be the (unique) quaternion division algebra over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ (which e
xists since $\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{Q}_p) \cong \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ contains a u
nique element of order $2$). Define
$$G = \mathrm{SL}_1(D) = \{x \in D^* : \mathrm{Nrd}(x) = 1\},$$
the algebraic group of reduced-norm-one elements.

**$G$ is simply connected and semisimple.** Over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$, we have $
D \otimes \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p \cong M_2(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p)$, so $G_{\bar{\mathbb
{Q}}_p} \cong \mathrm{SL}_2$, which is simply connected (type $A_1$) and semisim
ple. These are geometric properties, so $G$ is simply connected and semisimple o
ver $\mathbb{Q}_p$.

**$G$ is anisotropic over $\mathbb{Q}_p$.** Since $D$ is a division algebra, $G(
\mathbb{Q}_p) = \mathrm{SL}_1(D)$ is compact (a closed subgroup of $\mathcal{O}_
D^*$, the units of the maximal order). A compact $p$-adic group admits no nontri
vial unipotent one-parameter subgroups, so $G$ has no proper parabolic subgroups
 over $\mathbb{Q}_p$.

### Construction of a nontrivial continuous homomorphism

Let $\mathcal{O}_D$ be the maximal order of $D$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{p}
_D$. The residue field $\mathcal{O}_D/\mathfrak{p}_D \cong \mathbb{F}_{p^2}$ (a
quadratic extension of $\mathbb{F}_p$, since $D$ has residue degree $2$).

The reduction map $\rho: \mathcal{O}_D^* \to (\mathcal{O}_D/\mathfrak{p}_D)^* =
\mathbb{F}_{p^2}^*$ is a surjective group homomorphism. The reduced norm $\mathr
m{Nrd}: D^* \to \mathbb{Q}_p^*$ reduces modulo $p$ to the field norm $N_{\mathbb
{F}_{p^2}/\mathbb{F}_p}: \mathbb{F}_{p^2}^* \to \mathbb{F}_p^*$. Therefore $\rho
$ restricts to a surjective homomorphism:
$$\rho: \mathrm{SL}_1(D) = \ker(\mathrm{Nrd}) \;\longrightarrow\; \ker(N_{\mathb
b{F}_{p^2}/\mathbb{F}_p}) = \mu_{p+1} = \{x \in \mathbb{F}_{p^2}^* : N(x)=1\}.$$
The group $\mu_{p+1}$ is cyclic of order $p+1$.

**Continuity.** The kernel $\mathrm{SL}_1(D)^1 = \mathrm{SL}_1(D) \cap (1+\mathf
rak{p}_D)$ is open in $\mathrm{SL}_1(D)$ (in the $p$-adic topology), so $\rho$ i
s continuous. The inclusion $\iota: \mu_{p+1} \hookrightarrow S^1$ (roots of uni
ty in $\mathbb{C}^*$) is continuous (discrete finite group).

**The composition**
$$\chi = \iota \circ \rho: G(\mathbb{Q}_p) = \mathrm{SL}_1(D) \;\longrightarrow\
; S^1$$
**is a nontrivial continuous homomorphism**, since $\rho$ is surjective and $|\m
u_{p+1}| = p+1 \geq 3$.

athbb{Q}_p)^+$). Hence every homomorphism $G(\mathbb{Q}_p) \to S^1$ is trivial i

The counterexample arises specifically in the **anisotropic** case, where $G(\ma
