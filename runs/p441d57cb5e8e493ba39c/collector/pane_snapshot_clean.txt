## Proof

We determine the cardinality of $S = \{A \in M_3(\mathbb{Z}) : AA^{\top} = I\}$.

**Step 1: The rows are integer unit vectors.**

Let $A$ have rows $r_1, r_2, r_3 \in \mathbb{Z}^3$. The condition $AA^{\top} = I
$ means:
- $\|r_i\|^2 = (AA^{\top})_{ii} = 1$ for each $i$,
- $r_i \cdot r_j = (AA^{\top})_{ij} = 0$ for $i \neq j$.

Since $r_i = (a, b, c) \in \mathbb{Z}^3$ and $a^2 + b^2 + c^2 = 1$, exactly one
of $a, b, c$ is $\pm 1$ and the other two are $0$. Thus each $r_i$ is a signed s
tandard basis vector: $r_i \in \{\pm e_1, \pm e_2, \pm e_3\}$.

**Step 2: The rows form a signed permutation.**

The orthogonality condition $r_i \cdot r_j = 0$ for $i \neq j$ forces the three
rows to use three *distinct* basis directions (two signed basis vectors in the s
ame direction are parallel, hence not orthogonal unless one is zero, which is im
possible). Therefore the rows determine:
- a permutation $\sigma \in S_3$ selecting which basis direction each row uses,
and
- a sign $\varepsilon_i \in \{+1, -1\}$ for each row.

**Step 3: Count.**

- Number of permutations of $\{1,2,3\}$: $3! = 6$.
- Number of sign choices for 3 rows: $2^3 = 8$.

Every such choice yields a valid orthogonal matrix, and conversely every element
 of $S$ arises this way. Hence:

$$|S| = 6 \times 8 = 48.$$
