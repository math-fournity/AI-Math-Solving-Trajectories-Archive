## Proof

We prove that $E_P[X] = E_Q[X]$.

**Setup.** We have $(\Omega, \mathcal{G}, P)$ and $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, Q)$ wit
h $\mathcal{F} \subseteq \mathcal{G}$ and $P|_{\mathcal{F}} = Q$ (i.e., $P$ and
$Q$ agree on every event in $\mathcal{F}$). $X$ is $\mathcal{F}$-measurable and
$Q$-integrable.

**Key observation.** Since $X$ is $\mathcal{F}$-measurable, the integral of $X$
depends only on the measure restricted to $\mathcal{F}$. Formally, we apply the
standard machine.

**Step 1: Indicator functions.** Let $X = \mathbf{1}_A$ for $A \in \mathcal{F}$.
 Then
$$E_P[\mathbf{1}_A] = P(A) = Q(A) = E_Q[\mathbf{1}_A],$$
where the middle equality uses $P|_{\mathcal{F}} = Q$ and $A \in \mathcal{F}$.

**Step 2: Simple functions.** Let $X = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i \mathbf{1}_{A_i}$ with
 $A_i \in \mathcal{F}$. By linearity and Step 1,
$$E_P[X] = \sum_i a_i P(A_i) = \sum_i a_i Q(A_i) = E_Q[X].$$

**Step 3: Nonnegative $\mathcal{F}$-measurable functions.** Let $X \geq 0$ be $\
mathcal{F}$-measurable. There exists a sequence of $\mathcal{F}$-measurable simp
le functions $0 \leq X_n \uparrow X$. By the Monotone Convergence Theorem applie
d to both $P$ and $Q$:
$$E_P[X] = \lim_n E_P[X_n] = \lim_n E_Q[X_n] = E_Q[X].$$
In particular, $\int X\,dP = \int X\,dQ$, so $X$ is $P$-integrable iff $Q$-integ
rable (for $X \geq 0$).

**Step 4: General $\mathcal{F}$-measurable $X$.** Write $X = X^+ - X^-$ with $X^
\pm \geq 0$ and $\mathcal{F}$-measurable. Since $X$ is $Q$-integrable, $E_Q[X^+]
, E_Q[X^-] < \infty$. By Step 3, $E_P[X^\pm] = E_Q[X^\pm] < \infty$, so $X$ is $
P$-integrable, and by linearity:
$$E_P[X] = E_P[X^+] - E_P[X^-] = E_Q[X^+] - E_Q[X^-] = E_Q[X].$$

**Conclusion.** The equality $E_P[X] = E_Q[X]$ holds. The essential reason is th
at the integral of an $\mathcal{F}$-measurable function is determined entirely b
y the measure on $\mathcal{F}$, and $P$ and $Q$ coincide there. (Note: the large
r $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{G}$ on which $P$ is defined plays no role, since $X
$ is not $\mathcal{G}$-measurable-only — it is already $\mathcal{F}$-measurable,
 so only the restriction $P|_{\mathcal{F}} = Q$ matters.)
