## Solution

We seek the most general entire function $f$ satisfying
$$\lim_{z\to\infty}\frac{f(z)^2}{z^3}=0.$$

**Step 1. The growth condition forces $f$ to be a polynomial.**

The limit condition means $|f(z)|^2/|z|^3 \to 0$ as $|z|\to\infty$, i.e.
$$|f(z)| = o\!\left(|z|^{3/2}\right) \quad\text{as } |z|\to\infty.$$
In particular, for $|z|$ sufficiently large, $|f(z)| \le |z|^{3/2} \le |z|^2$. B
y the generalized Liouville theorem (an entire function bounded by $C|z|^n$ for
large $|z|$ is a polynomial of degree $\le n$), $f$ is a polynomial of degree at
 most $2$.

**Step 2. Rule out degree $2$.**

Write $f(z)=az^2+bz+c$ with $a\ne 0$. Then
$$\frac{f(z)^2}{z^3}=\frac{a^2 z^4+2abz^3+\cdots}{z^3}=a^2 z+2ab+O(1/z).$$
As $z\to\infty$ along the positive real axis, $a^2 z\to\infty\ne 0$, contradicti
ng the limit being $0$. Hence $a=0$.

**Step 3. Degree $\le 1$ works.**

With $f(z)=bz+c$ (arbitrary $b,c\in\mathbb{C}$),
$$\frac{f(z)^2}{z^3}=\frac{b^2 z^2+2bcz+c^2}{z^3}=\frac{b^2}{z}+\frac{2bc}{z^2}+
\frac{c^2}{z^3}\xrightarrow[z\to\infty]{}0.$$

**Conclusion.** The most general entire function satisfying the condition is
$$\boxed{f(z)=az+b,\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{C}\text{ arbitrary}.}$$
