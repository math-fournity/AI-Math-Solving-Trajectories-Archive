Looking at this problem, I need to find all natural numbers $n$ such that $\{1,
2, \ldots, 2n\}$ can be partitioned into $n$ pairs where the product of the pair
-sums is a perfect square.

**Answer: all natural numbers $n \geq 2$.**

---

## Proof

### $n = 1$ is impossible

The only partition of $\{1, 2\}$ is the pair $(1,2)$ with sum $3$. The product i
s $3$, which is not a perfect square.

### Even $n \geq 2$: construction via symmetric pairing

Pair each $k$ with $2n+1-k$ for $k = 1, 2, \ldots, n$:
$$(1,\, 2n),\ (2,\, 2n{-}1),\ \ldots,\ (n,\, n{+}1).$$
Every pair has sum $2n+1$, so the product of all sums is $(2n+1)^n$. Since $n$ i
s even, $(2n+1)^n = \bigl((2n+1)^{n/2}\bigr)^2$ is a perfect square. $\checkmark
$

### Odd $n \geq 3$: construction by splitting into a base block and symmetric re
mainder

**The base block.** Take the six smallest numbers $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$ and pair the
m as:
$$(1,5),\ (2,4),\ (3,6),$$
with sums $6, 6, 9$. Their product is $6 \cdot 6 \cdot 9 = 324 = 18^2$, a perfec
t square.

**The symmetric remainder.** The remaining $2n - 6$ numbers are $\{7, 8, \ldots,
 2n\}$. Pair them symmetrically:
$$(7,\, 2n),\ (8,\, 2n{-}1),\ \ldots,\ (n{+}3,\, n{+}4).$$
Each pair has sum $2n + 7$. There are $n - 3$ such pairs. Since $n$ is odd, $n -
 3$ is **even**, so the product of these sums is $(2n+7)^{n-3} = \bigl((2n+7)^{(
n-3)/2}\bigr)^2$, a perfect square.

**Combining.** The total product is
$$(2n+7)^{n-3} \times 324,$$
a product of two perfect squares, hence a perfect square. $\checkmark$

**Verification that all elements are used.** The base block uses $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\
}$; the symmetric remainder uses $\{7, 8, \ldots, 2n\}$. Together these are exac
tly $\{1, 2, \ldots, 2n\}$, with no overlap. The number of pairs is $3 + (n-3) =
 n$. $\checkmark$

**Edge case $n = 3$:** Here $n - 3 = 0$, so there are no symmetric pairs — only
the base block $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$ with product $324 = 18^2$. $\checkmark$

### Conclusion

- $n = 1$: impossible (only sum is $3$, not a square).
- $n \geq 2$ even: symmetric pairing gives product $(2n+1)^n$, a square.
- $n \geq 3$ odd: the split construction gives a product of two squares, hence a
 square.

Therefore, the natural numbers $n$ for which such a partition exists are exactly
 **all $n \geq 2$**.
