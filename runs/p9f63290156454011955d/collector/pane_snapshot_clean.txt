**Answer: The statement is TRUE.**

**Proof:**

Since $\{a_i\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}$ is a basis for the normed linear space, the vec
tors are linearly independent. This holds regardless of whether "basis" is inter
preted as a Hamel basis (algebraic basis, where every element is a *finite* line
ar combination) or a Schauder basis (where every element has a *unique* represen
tation as a convergent series $\sum_{i=1}^\infty c_i a_i$). In both cases, linea
r independence of the basis vectors is either part of the definition or an immed
iate consequence: for a Schauder basis, if some nontrivial finite combination $\
sum_{k=1}^n c_{i_k} a_{i_k} = 0$ held, then $0$ would have two distinct represen
tations (the trivial one with all coefficients zero, and this finite one), contr
adicting uniqueness.

Now fix any $i > 2$ and suppose $v \in \mathbb{C}(a_1 + a_2) \cap \mathbb{C}a_i$
. Then there exist scalars $\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{C}$ such that:
$$v = \alpha(a_1 + a_2) = \beta\, a_i.$$

Rearranging, we obtain:
$$\alpha\, a_1 + \alpha\, a_2 - \beta\, a_i = 0.$$

Since $i > 2$, the vectors $a_1, a_2, a_i$ are three **distinct** elements of th

ion to this linear combination equaling zero is:
$$\alpha = 0, \quad \alpha = 0, \quad -\beta = 0,$$

which forces $\alpha = 0$ and $\beta = 0$, and consequently $v = 0$.

Since $v$ was an arbitrary element of the intersection, we conclude:
$$\mathbb{C}(a_1 + a_2) \cap \mathbb{C}\, a_i = \{0\} \quad \text{for all } i >
2.$$

The key point is that $a_1 + a_2$ involves only basis vectors with indices $\leq
