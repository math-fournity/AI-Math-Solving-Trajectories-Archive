## Proof

Let $M_1$ and $M_2$ be the compact connected 2-manifolds presented by the polygo
nal words $w_1$ and $w_2$, respectively, and suppose the alphabets of $w_1$ and
$w_2$ are disjoint. Let $P$ be the polygon whose boundary word is the concatenat
ion $w_1 w_2$, and let $M$ be the surface obtained from $P$ by identifying edges
 according to the labels.

**Claim.** $M \cong M_1 \# M_2$.

**Construction.** Draw a diagonal $d$ in $P$ connecting the vertex separating th
e $w_1$-portion from the $w_2$-portion to the opposite vertex (the start/end ver
tex of the combined word). This diagonal cuts $P$ into two polygons:

- $P_1$, whose boundary word is $w_1$ followed by the edge $d$ (traversed in one
 direction), and
- $P_2$, whose boundary word is $w_2$ followed by the edge $d$ (traversed in the
 opposite direction, i.e., $d^{-1}$).

Since the alphabets of $w_1$ and $w_2$ are disjoint, every label appearing in $w
_1$ is identified only with another occurrence in $w_1$, and likewise for $w_2$.
 Hence the edge identifications within $P_1$ are exactly those prescribed by $w_
1$, and within $P_2$ exactly those prescribed by $w_2$. The only edge shared bet
ween $P_1$ and $P_2$ is the diagonal $d$, which appears once on each polygon and
 is identified to itself when we reglue.

**Interpreting each piece.** The polygon $P_1$ with boundary word $w_1 \cdot d$
presents a surface obtained from $M_1$ by removing an open disk: the edge $d$ is
 a boundary component (it is not identified with any other edge in $P_1$), so it
 bounds a "hole." Formally, capping this hole with a disk (i.e., attaching a 2-c
ell along $d$) recovers $M_1$, since $w_1$ alone is a presentation of $M_1$. Thu
s $P_1$ presents $M_1 \setminus \mathring{D^2}$.

By the identical argument, $P_2$ presents $M_2 \setminus \mathring{D^2}$.

**Regluing.** The original surface $M$ is recovered by gluing $P_1$ and $P_2$ ba
ck along $d$. This is precisely the definition of the connected sum: remove an o
pen disk from each of $M_1$ and $M_2$ and identify the resulting boundary circle
s via a homeomorphism. Therefore

$$M \cong (M_1 \setminus \mathring{D^2}) \cup_{\partial} (M_2 \setminus \mathrin
g{D^2}) = M_1 \# M_2.$$

**Conclusion.** The 2-manifold presented by the concatenated word $w_1 w_2$ is t
he connected sum $M_1 \# M_2$ of the two original manifolds. $\blacksquare$
