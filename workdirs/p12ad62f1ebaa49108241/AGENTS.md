# Problem

A city planning department has designed a rectangular transit network consisting of an $m \times n$ grid of blocks. Each segment of the grid (the edges of the $m \times n$ unit squares) is designated as a one-way street. The flow of traffic is governed by two strict urban ordinances:

1. **Perimeter Flow:** All streets located on the very outer boundary of the $m \times n$ rectangular grid are directed such that they form a single, continuous clockwise loop around the entire city.
2. **Intersection Balance:** Every interior intersection (where four street segments meet) must be configured as a balanced node, meaning exactly two streets must lead into the intersection and exactly two streets must lead away from it.

A "Clockwise Block" is defined as a unit square within the grid where the one-way traffic on all four of its surrounding streets flows in a clockwise direction.

Determine $N$, the minimum possible number of Clockwise Blocks that can exist in any such city grid.
