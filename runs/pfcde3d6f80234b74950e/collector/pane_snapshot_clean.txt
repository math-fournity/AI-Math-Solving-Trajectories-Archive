## Solution

Let me set up notation. Objects of the slice category $C_{/S}$ are pairs $(A, a)
$ where $a: A \to S$ is a morphism in $C$. A morphism $\varphi: (A,a) \to (B,b)$
 in $C_{/S}$ is a morphism $\varphi: A \to B$ in $C$ satisfying $b \circ \varphi
 = a$. The forgetful functor $F$ sends $(A,a) \mapsto A$ and $\varphi \mapsto \v
arphi$.

Write the three objects as
$$X = (X_0,\, x: X_0 \to S),\quad Y = (Y_0,\, y: Y_0 \to S),\quad Z = (Z_0,\, z:
 Z_0 \to S).$$

The given morphisms $f: X \to Z$ and $g: Y \to Z$ in $C_{/S}$ are morphisms $f:
X_0 \to Z_0$ and $g: Y_0 \to Z_0$ in $C$ satisfying the slice compatibility cond
itions:
$$z \circ f = x, \qquad z \circ g = y. \tag{$\star$}$$

The hypothesis $F(f) = F(g) \circ h'$ says that in $C$
$$f = g \circ h', \qquad h': X_0 \to Y_0.$$

For $h'$ to define a morphism $h: X \to Y$ **in** $C_{/S}$ (with $F(h)=h'$), the
 single additional requirement is the slice compatibility
$$y \circ h' = x.$$

But this holds **automatically** by combining the two identities in $(\star)$ wi
th the factorization $f = g \circ h'$:
$$y \circ h' \;=\; (z \circ g) \circ h' \;=\; z \circ (g \circ h') \;=\; z \circ
 f \;=\; x.$$

Therefore $h := h'$ is a well-defined morphism $X \to Y$ in $C_{/S}$ with $F(h)
= h'$.

### Conclusion

**Yes.** Moreover this is not special to schemes: the argument uses only the def
inition of the slice category and the compatibility conditions $(\star)$, so it
is valid in **every** category $C$. In particular it holds when $C$ is the categ
ory of schemes. The forgetful functor $F: C_{/S} \to C$ is **faithful**, and the
 lifting of $h'$ is forced uniquely by the slice structure — no geometric proper
ty of schemes is required.
