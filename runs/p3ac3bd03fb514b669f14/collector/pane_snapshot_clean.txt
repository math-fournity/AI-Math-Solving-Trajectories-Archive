## Solution

The inequality does **not** hold. We disprove it by a scaling argument.

**Setup.** Choose a nonzero, non-negative bump $\phi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$
 with $\phi \geq 0$ and $\phi \not\equiv 0$ (e.g. a standard smooth mollifier).
Then
$$A := \int_{\mathbb{R}} \phi^3\,dx > 0, \qquad B := \int_{\mathbb{R}} (\phi')^2

with both quantities finite (since $\phi$ has compact support).

fty(\mathbb{R})$. Then
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} u_\lambda^3\,dx = \lambda^3 \int_{\mathbb{R}} \phi^3\,dx = \
lambda^3 A,$$
and
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} (u_\lambda')^2\,dx + \int_{\mathbb{R}} u_\lambda^2\,dx = \la

**Contradiction.** Suppose the inequality held, i.e. there existed a constant $C
 > 0$ (independent of $u$) such that for every $u \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R})$,
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} u^3\,dx \leq C\!\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}} (u')^2\,dx + \int_{\
mathbb{R}} u^2\,dx\right).$$
Applying this to $u_\lambda$ gives
$$\lambda^3 A \leq C\,\lambda^2 B \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \lambda \leq C\,\fra
c{B}{A} \qquad \text{for all } \lambda > 0.$$
Since $A, B, C$ are fixed positive constants, this bounds $\lambda$ from above —
 a contradiction, as $\lambda$ may be taken arbitrarily large.

**Root cause.** The right-hand side $\|(u', u)\|_{L^2}^2$ is **quadratic** in $u
$, while the left-hand side $\|u\|_{L^3}^3$ is **cubic**. Under the dilation $u
\mapsto \lambda u$ (amplitude scaling, no spatial rescaling), the cubic term gro
ws as $\lambda^3$ whereas the quadratic right-hand side grows only as $\lambda^2
$. No homogeneous quadratic quantity can control a homogeneous cubic quantity un
der unbounded amplitude scaling.

**Conclusion.** The proposed interpolation inequality fails for all $u \in C^\in
fty(\mathbb{R})$; it does **not** hold.
