## Proof

Let $G \sim \mathrm{ER}(N, p)$, $q = 1-p$, and let $\Delta$ denote the maximum d
egree. Set the threshold
$$T = Np + 2\sqrt{Npq}.$$
We show that $P(\Delta \le T) \to 0$ as $N \to \infty$.

**Setup.** For vertex $i$, let $D_i = \deg(i) \sim \mathrm{Bin}(N{-}1,\, p)$ wit
h mean $\mu = (N{-}1)p$ and variance $\sigma^2 = (N{-}1)pq$. Define indicators $
X_i = \mathbf{1}\{D_i > T\}$ and $X = \sum_{i=1}^N X_i$, the count of vertices e
xceeding $T$. Then $\{\Delta \le T\} = \{X = 0\}$, so $P(\Delta \le T) = P(X=0)$
.

---

**Step 1: $\mathbb{E}[X] \to \infty$.**

Note $T - \mu = p + 2\sqrt{Npq}$. In the regime $Npq \to \infty$ (the meaningful
 regime for the normal approximation), we have $\sqrt{Npq} \to \infty$ and $p =
o(\sqrt{Npq})$, so
$$\frac{T - \mu}{\sigma} = \frac{p + 2\sqrt{Npq}}{\sqrt{(N{-}1)pq}} \;\longright
arrow\; 2.$$
By the CLT (valid since $\sigma^2 = (N{-}1)pq \to \infty$),
$$P(D_i > T) = P\!\left(\frac{D_i - \mu}{\sigma} > \frac{T-\mu}{\sigma}\right) \
;\longrightarrow\; 1 - \Phi(2) \;=:\; \alpha \;>\; 0.$$
Therefore $\mathbb{E}[X] = N \cdot P(D_1 > T) \sim \alpha N \to \infty$.

---

**Step 2: $\mathrm{Var}(X) = O(N)$, hence $\mathrm{Var}(X)/\mathbb{E}[X]^2 \to 0
$.**

The degrees $D_i, D_j$ ($i \ne j$) are *not* independent: they share the edge $(
i,j)$. Let $W = \mathbf{1}\{\text{edge }(i,j)\text{ present}\} \sim \mathrm{Bern
}(p)$. Conditional on $W$, write
$$D_i = W + D_i', \qquad D_j = W + D_j',$$
where $D_i', D_j' \sim \mathrm{Bin}(N{-}2,\, p)$ are **independent** (they invol
ve disjoint edge sets). Define $f(w) = P(D' > T - w)$ for $D' \sim \mathrm{Bin}(
N{-}2, p)$. By conditional independence:
$$\mathbb{E}[X_i X_j] = \mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{E}[X_i X_j \mid W]\big] = \mathbb
{E}\big[f(W)^2\big], \qquad \mathbb{E}[X_i] = \mathbb{E}[f(W)].$$
Hence
$$\mathrm{Cov}(X_i, X_j) = \mathbb{E}[f(W)^2] - \mathbb{E}[f(W)]^2 = \mathrm{Var
}(f(W)).$$

Now $f$ takes two values: $f(0) = P(D' > T)$ and $f(1) = P(D' > T{-}1) = f(0) +
P(D' = \lceil T \rceil)$. The difference $|f(1) - f(0)|$ equals a point probabil
ity of $\mathrm{Bin}(N{-}2, p)$ evaluated near $\mu + 2\sigma$. By the **local C
LT**, this point probability is
$$\Theta\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{(N{-}2)pq}}\right) = \Theta\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt
{Npq}}\right).$$
Therefore
$$\mathrm{Cov}(X_i, X_j) = \mathrm{Var}(f(W)) = pq \cdot (f(1) - f(0))^2 = pq \c
dot O\!\left(\frac{1}{Npq}\right) = O\!\left(\frac{1}{N}\right).$$

Since $\mathrm{Var}(X_i) = \mathbb{E}[X_i](1 - \mathbb{E}[X_i]) = O(1)$, we get
$$\mathrm{Var}(X) = \sum_i \mathrm{Var}(X_i) + \sum_{i \ne j} \mathrm{Cov}(X_i,
X_j) = N \cdot O(1) + N(N{-}1) \cdot O(1/N) = O(N).$$

---

**Step 3: Conclusion via the second moment method.**

By Chebyshev's inequality,
$$P(X = 0) \;\le\; P\big(|X - \mathbb{E}[X]| \ge \mathbb{E}[X]\big) \;\le\; \fra
c{\mathrm{Var}(X)}{\mathbb{E}[X]^2} = \frac{O(N)}{\alpha^2 N^2} \;\longrightarro
w\; 0.$$

Therefore
$$P(\Delta \le T) = P(X = 0) \;\longrightarrow\; 0.$$

---

**Remark on other regimes.** If $Npq \not\to \infty$ (e.g., $p = c/N$ with $c$ c
onstant), then $T = c + 2\sqrt{c}$ is a fixed constant while the degrees are app
roximately $\mathrm{Poisson}(c)$ and the maximum of $N$ such variables grows as
$\Theta(\log N / \log\log N) \to \infty$. Thus $P(\Delta \le T) \to 0$ in this r
egime as well.

---

**Result.** In all asymptotic regimes, the probability that the maximum degree o
f $\mathrm{ER}(N, p)$ is at most $Np + 2\sqrt{Npq}$ tends to **0**.

The threshold $Np + 2\sqrt{Npq}$ lies only $2$ standard deviations above the mea
n degree, while the maximum over $N$ vertices typically reaches $Np + \sqrt{2 Np
q \log N}$ — far above this level. The expected number of vertices exceeding the
 threshold is $\sim \alpha N \to \infty$, and the weak pairwise correlations (ea
ch pair sharing a single edge) are too small to change the conclusion.

$$\boxed{P\!\left(\Delta \le Np + 2\sqrt{Npq}\right) \;\longrightarrow\; 0}$$
