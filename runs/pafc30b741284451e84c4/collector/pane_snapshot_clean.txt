### Partial fraction decomposition

Factor the denominator:
$$x^2+3x+2 = (x+1)(x+2).$$

Decompose:
$$\frac{1}{(x+1)(x+2)} = \frac{A}{x+1}+\frac{B}{x+2},\quad A(x+2)+B(x+1)=1.$$
Setting $x=-1$: $A=1$. Setting $x=-2$: $-B=1\Rightarrow B=-1$. Hence
$$f(x)=\frac{1}{x+1}-\frac{1}{x+2}.$$

### nth derivative formula

For $g(x)=\frac{1}{x+a}$,
$$g^{(n)}(x)=\frac{(-1)^n\, n!}{(x+a)^{n+1}}.$$

Applying with $n=100$ at $x=0$ (note $(-1)^{100}=1$):
$$\left(\frac{1}{x+1}\right)^{(100)}\bigg|_{0}=\frac{100!}{1^{101}}=100!,\qquad
\left(\frac{1}{x+2}\right)^{(100)}\bigg|_{0}=\frac{100!}{2^{101}}.$$

Therefore
$$f^{(100)}(0)=100!-\frac{100!}{2^{101}}=100!\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{101}}\right).$$

### Numerical evaluation (10% relative error)

Since $2^{101}\approx 2.535\times 10^{30}$, the factor $\frac{1}{2^{101}}\approx
 3.9\times 10^{-31}$ is utterly negligible compared to $1$ — far below any 10% t
olerance. Thus
$$f^{(100)}(0)\approx 100!.$$

Using Stirling's approximation (well within 10%):
$$100!\approx 9.33\times 10^{157}.$$

(More precisely $100!=9.332621544\ldots\times 10^{157}$, but the requested toler
ance makes the leading digits $9.3\times 10^{157}$ sufficient.)

### Result

$$\boxed{f^{(100)}(0)=100!\left(1-2^{-101}\right)\approx 9.33\times 10^{157}}$$
