**Proof.**

We build the desired sequence in two phases: a *growth phase* that produces a la

cover everything.

**Notation.** For a sequence $g_1, \dots, g_k$, its *achievable set* $S$ is the
set of all subsequence products (including the empty product $= e$). Appending a
n element $g$ updates $S$ to $S \cup Sg$, since any subsequence either excludes
$g$ (giving a product in $S$) or includes $g$ as its last term (giving a product
 in $Sg$).

**Lemma 1 (Growth Lemma).** *Let $S \subseteq G$ with $e \in S$ and $1 \le |S| \
le |G|/2$. Then there exists $g \in G$ with $|S \cup Sg| \ge \tfrac{3}{2}|S|$.*

*Proof.* Count $\sum_{g \in G} |S \cap Sg|$. Each ordered pair $(a,b) \in S \tim
es S$ contributes to exactly one $g$ (namely $g = b^{-1}a$), so the sum equals $
|S|^2$. The average of $|S \cap Sg|$ over all $g$ is $|S|^2/|G| \le |S|/2$ (usin
g $|S| \le |G|/2$). Since $|S \cap Se| = |S| > |S|^2/|G|$ (as $|S| < |G|$), the
identity has *above-average* overlap, so some $g \ne e$ has $|S \cap Sg| \le |S|
^2/|G| \le |S|/2$. Then
$$|S \cup Sg| = 2|S| - |S \cap Sg| \ge 2|S| - \tfrac{1}{2}|S| = \tfrac{3}{2}|S|.
 \qquad \square$$

**Lemma 2 (Growth Phase).** *Starting from $S_0 = \{e\}$, we can choose $g_1, \d

**Lemma 3 (Large Set Covers Differences).** *If $S \subseteq G$ with $|S| > |G|/

*Proof.* For any $x \in G$, the sets $S$ and $xS$ satisfy $|S| + |xS| = 2|S| > |

**Lemma 4 (Inverse-Extension).** *Let $g_1, \dots, g_k$ be a sequence with achie

*Proof.* Take $a, b \in S$. Write $a = g_{i_1} \cdots g_{i_r}$ (subsequence of t

j_s}$ (subsequence, $j_1 < \cdots < j_s \le k$).

$$b^{-1} = g_{j_s}^{-1} \cdots g_{j_1}^{-1}$$

Thus $ab^{-1}$ is achievable for all $a, b \in S$, i.e., $SS^{-1}$ is contained
in the achievable set. $\square$

**Combining the lemmas.** Given a nontrivial finite group $G$ ($|G| \ge 2$):

1. By Lemma 2, choose $g_1, \dots, g_k$ with $k \le \frac{\ln|G|}{\ln(3/2)} + 1$

3. By Lemma 4, the extended sequence $g_1, \dots, g_k, g_k^{-1}, \dots, g_1^{-1}
