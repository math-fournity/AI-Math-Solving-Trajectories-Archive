## Answer

**No**, $G/\Gamma$ does not necessarily have a $G$-invariant measure. The existe
nce of such a measure is governed by a compatibility condition between the modul
ar function of $G$ and that of $\Gamma$, and this condition can fail.

---

### The Criterion

Let $\Delta_G$ denote the modular function of $G$ (defined by $\mu(Eg)=\Delta_G(
g)\,\mu(E)$ for a left Haar measure $\mu$ on $G$). Since $\Gamma$ is discrete, i
t is unimodular, so $\Delta_\Gamma\equiv 1$.

**Theorem.** *Let $G$ be a locally compact Hausdorff group and $\Gamma$ a discre
te subgroup. Then $G/\Gamma$ admits a nonzero $G$-invariant regular Borel measur
e if and only if*
$$\Delta_G\big|_\Gamma \equiv 1.$$

### Proof of Necessity

Suppose $\nu$ is a nonzero $G$-invariant regular Borel measure on $G/\Gamma$. De
fine a positive linear functional on $C_c(G)$ by the **Weil formula**:

$$\mu(f) \;=\; \int_{G/\Gamma}\!\Bigl(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma} f(g\gamma)\Bigr)\,d
\nu(g\Gamma), \qquad f\in C_c(G).$$

The sum is finite for each $g$ (since $f$ has compact support and $\Gamma$ is di
screte), and the resulting function $\tilde f(g\Gamma)=\sum_\gamma f(g\gamma)$ i
s continuous with compact support on $G/\Gamma$, so the integral is well-defined
.

**Left-invariance of $\mu$:** For $g_0\in G$, set $L_{g_0}f(g)=f(g_0^{-1}g)$. Th
en
$$\widetilde{L_{g_0}f}(g\Gamma)=\sum_\gamma f(g_0^{-1}g\gamma)=\tilde f(g_0^{-1}
g\Gamma),$$
so $\mu(L_{g_0}f)=\int \tilde f(g_0^{-1}g\Gamma)\,d\nu=\int\tilde f\,d\nu=\mu(f)
$ by $G$-invariance of $\nu$. Thus $\mu$ is a **left Haar measure** on $G$.

**Right-invariance under $\Gamma$:** For $\gamma_0\in\Gamma$, set $R_{\gamma_0}f
(g)=f(g\gamma_0)$. Then
$$\widetilde{R_{\gamma_0}f}(g\Gamma)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f(g\gamma\gamma_0)=\s
um_{\gamma'\in\Gamma}f(g\gamma')=\tilde f(g\Gamma),$$
since right-multiplication by $\gamma_0$ permutes $\Gamma$. Hence $\mu(R_{\gamma
_0}f)=\mu(f)$, i.e., $\mu$ is **right-invariant under every element of $\Gamma$*
*.

But for a left Haar measure, $\mu(E\gamma_0)=\Delta_G(\gamma_0)\,\mu(E)$. Combin
ing with right-invariance under $\gamma_0$:
$$\Delta_G(\gamma_0)\,\mu(E)=\mu(E)\quad\text{for all }E.$$
Since $\mu$ is nonzero, $\Delta_G(\gamma_0)=1$ for every $\gamma_0\in\Gamma$. $\
quad\square_{\text{necessity}}$

### Proof of Sufficiency

Conversely, suppose $\Delta_G|_\Gamma\equiv 1$. Let $\mu$ be a left Haar measure
 on $G$. Define $\nu$ on $G/\Gamma$ by
$$\int_{G/\Gamma}\tilde f\,d\nu \;=\; \int_G f\,d\mu, \qquad \tilde f(g\Gamma)=\
sum_{\gamma}f(g\gamma),$$
for $\tilde f\in C_c(G/\Gamma)$ that arise from some $f\in C_c(G)$. One must che
ck this is well-defined: if $\tilde f = 0$, then $\int_G f\,d\mu=0$. This follow
s from the **Weil consistency condition**: the map $f\mapsto\tilde f$ combined w
ith $\mu$ gives a well-defined functional precisely when $\Delta_G|_\Gamma=\Delt
a_\Gamma$, which is our hypothesis. The resulting $\nu$ is $G$-invariant by the
same left-invariance computation as above (run in reverse), and it is a nonzero
regular Borel measure. $\quad\square_{\text{sufficiency}}$

---

### Counterexample

It remains to show that the condition $\Delta_G|_\Gamma\equiv 1$ can **fail**.

**The group.** Let $G$ be the **affine group of the line** (the "$ax+b$ group"):
$$G=\{(a,b):a>0,\;b\in\mathbb R\},\qquad (a,b)(a',b')=(aa',\,ab'+b).$$
This is a locally compact Hausdorff topological group (a Lie group diffeomorphic
 to $(0,\infty)\times\mathbb R$).

**Modular function.** The left Haar measure is $\frac{da\,db}{a^2}$ and the righ
t Haar measure is $\frac{da\,db}{a}$, giving
$$\Delta_G(a,b)=\frac{1}{a}.$$

**The discrete subgroup.** Set
$$\Gamma=\{(2^n,0):n\in\mathbb Z\}.$$
This is a subgroup since $(2^m,0)(2^n,0)=(2^{m+n},0)$, isomorphic to $\mathbb Z$
. It is **discrete**: the points $(2^n,0)$ are isolated in $G$ (they accumulate
only at the "ends" $a\to 0$ and $a\to\infty$, not at any point of $G$).

**The modular function on $\Gamma$:**
$$\Delta_G(2^n,0)=\frac{1}{2^n}=2^{-n}.$$
In particular $\Delta_G(2,0)=\tfrac12\neq 1$, so $\Delta_G\big|_\Gamma\not\equiv
 1$.

**Conclusion.** By the necessity direction proved above, $G/\Gamma$ admits **no*
* nonzero $G$-invariant regular Borel measure.

---

### Final Answer

$G/\Gamma$ does **not** necessarily have a $G$-invariant measure. Such a measure
 exists if and only if the modular function of $G$ is trivial on $\Gamma$ (i.e.,
 $\Delta_G|_\Gamma\equiv 1$). The affine group $G=\{(a,b):a>0,b\in\mathbb R\}$ w
ith the discrete subgroup $\Gamma=\{(2^n,0):n\in\mathbb Z\}$ provides an explici
t counterexample, since $\Delta_G(2,0)=\frac12\neq 1$.
