## Problem

Does there exist $z_0 \notin \{a_n\}_{n\geq 1}$ with $f(z_0)=0$, where $f(z)=\pr
od_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{p_n}(z/a_n)$ is the entire function from the Weierstrass Fac
torization Theorem?

## Proof

**Answer: No.** The Weierstrass product has zeros exactly at the prescribed sequ
ence $\{a_n\}$ and nowhere else.

**Setup.** Let $\{a_n\}_{n\geq 1}$ be a sequence of nonzero complex numbers with
 $|a_n|\to\infty$ (repetitions allowed, encoding multiplicity). The elementary f
actors are
$$E_p(w)=(1-w)\exp\!\Big(w+\frac{w^2}{2}+\cdots+\frac{w^p}{p}\Big),\quad p\geq 1
;\qquad E_0(w)=1-w.$$
The integers $p_n\geq 0$ are chosen so that
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big|\frac{1}{a_n}\Big|^{p_n+1}<\infty, \tag{$\star$}$$
which is the standard convergence condition in the Weierstrass Factorization The
orem (such $p_n$ always exist since $|a_n|\to\infty$; one may take $p_n=n$, for
instance). The theorem then guarantees that
$$f(z)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{p_n}(z/a_n)$$
converges uniformly on compact subsets of $\mathbb{C}$ to an entire function. We
 show its zero set is exactly $\{a_n\}$.

**Key identity.** For $|w|<1$, using $\log(1-w)=-\sum_{k\geq 1}w^k/k$,
$$\log E_p(w)=\log(1-w)+\sum_{k=1}^{p}\frac{w^k}{k}=-\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}\frac{
w^k}{k}. \tag{1}$$
In particular $E_p(w)=0\iff w=1$, and for $|w|<1$, $E_p(w)\neq 0$.

**Step 1 — The tail product is nonvanishing on a large disk.** Fix $R>0$. Since
$|a_n|\to\infty$, there exists $N$ such that $|a_n|>2R$ for all $n>N$. For $|z|\
leq R$ and $n>N$, we have $|z/a_n|\leq \tfrac12<1$, so by (1),
$$\bigl|\log E_{p_n}(z/a_n)\bigr|\leq\sum_{k=p_n+1}^{\infty}\frac{|z/a_n|^k}{k}\
leq\frac{|z/a_n|^{p_n+1}}{(p_n+1)(1-|z/a_n|)}\leq\frac{2\,|z/a_n|^{p_n+1}}{p_n+1
}\leq 2\Big|\frac{R}{a_n}\Big|^{p_n+1}.$$
By $(\star)$, the series $\sum_{n>N}\bigl|\log E_{p_n}(z/a_n)\bigr|$ converges u
niformly on $|z|\leq R$. Hence
$$g_R(z):=\prod_{n>N}E_{p_n}(z/a_n)=\exp\!\Big(\sum_{n>N}\log E_{p_n}(z/a_n)\Big
)$$
converges uniformly on $|z|\leq R$ to a holomorphic function which is **nowhere
zero** on $|z|\leq R$ (it is an exponential).

**Step 2 — The full product.** Write
$$f(z)=\underbrace{\prod_{n=1}^{N}E_{p_n}(z/a_n)}_{\displaystyle F_N(z)}\cdot g_
R(z).$$
The finite product $F_N$ is entire, and its zeros on $|z|\leq R$ are exactly tho
se $a_n$ with $n\leq N$ and $|a_n|\leq R$ (each $E_{p_n}(z/a_n)$ vanishes only a
t $z=a_n$). The factor $g_R$ is nonzero on $|z|\leq R$ by Step 1. Therefore
$$\{z\in\overline{D(0,R)}:f(z)=0\}=\{a_n:1\leq n\leq N,\ |a_n|\leq R\}=\{a_n:|a_
n|\leq R\}.$$

**Step 3 — Conclusion.** Let $z_0\notin\{a_n\}_{n\geq 1}$. Choose $R>|z_0|$. By
Step 2, the zeros of $f$ in $\overline{D(0,R)}$ are precisely the $a_n$ lying in
 that disk. Since $z_0$ is not among the $a_n$, we have $f(z_0)\neq 0$.

Since $R$ was arbitrary (any $R>|z_0|$ works), this holds for every $z_0\notin\{
a_n\}$. Hence the zero set of $f$ is exactly $\{a_n\}_{n\geq 1}$ (with the presc
ribed multiplicities), and **no** complex number outside $\{a_n\}$ is a zero of
$f$.

$\blacksquare$
