No. The $k^{\text{th}}$ singular cohomology of $X$ need not vanish for $k > n$.

**Proof.**

The three facts stated in the problem are all correct, but they apply to *differ
ent classes of sheaves*. The apparent contradiction is resolved by observing tha
t the vanishing theorem for sheaf cohomology in degrees $> n$ applies to **coher
ent analytic sheaves**, not to constant sheaves.

**Fact 1.** For a complex manifold $X$ of complex dimension $n$ (real dimension
$2n$), singular cohomology $H^k_{\mathrm{sing}}(X; \mathbb{Z}) = 0$ for $k > 2n$
. This is purely topological: $X$ is a topological manifold of real dimension $2
n$, so it has no cohomology above degree $2n$.

**Fact 2.** The vanishing $H^k(X, \mathcal{F}) = 0$ for $k > n$ is the **Cartan–
Serre theorem** (a consequence of Theorem B for Stein manifolds, extended via Ca
rtan's theorem on coherent sheaves on complex manifolds): for every **coherent a
nalytic sheaf** $\mathcal{F}$ on a complex manifold of dimension $n$, one has $H
^k(X, \mathcal{F}) = 0$ for $k > n$.

**Fact 3.** For a locally contractible space (which a complex manifold is, being
 locally homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$), sheaf cohomology of the **constant
sheaf** $\underline{\mathbb{Z}}$ agrees with singular cohomology: $H^k(X, \under
line{\mathbb{Z}}) \cong H^k_{\mathrm{sing}}(X; \mathbb{Z})$.

**The resolution.** The constant sheaf $\underline{\mathbb{Z}}$ is **not** a coh
erent analytic sheaf. Coherent analytic sheaves are $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules loca
lly finitely presented; the constant sheaf $\underline{\mathbb{Z}}$ is not even
an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module (it is only a sheaf of abelian groups / $\mathbb{Z}$-m
odules). Therefore the vanishing theorem of Fact 2 does **not** apply to $\under
line{\mathbb{Z}}$, and there is no contradiction between Facts 2 and 3.

**Counterexample.** Let $X = \mathbb{CP}^n$, a compact complex manifold of compl
ex dimension $n$. Its singular cohomology is
$$H^k_{\mathrm{sing}}(\mathbb{CP}^n; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \begin{cases} \mathbb{Z}
& k = 0, 2, 4, \dots, 2n, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$
In particular $H^{2n}_{\mathrm{sing}}(\mathbb{CP}^n; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z
} \neq 0$, and $2n > n$ for $n \geq 1$. So the singular cohomology is nonzero in
 degree $2n > n$.

By Fact 3, this means $H^{2n}(\mathbb{CP}^n, \underline{\mathbb{Z}}) \cong \math
bb{Z} \neq 0$ as well — confirming that the constant sheaf does **not** satisfy
the vanishing $H^k = 0$ for $k > n$. This is consistent because $\underline{\mat
hbb{Z}}$ is not coherent.

**Conclusion.** The answer is **no**. The vanishing of sheaf cohomology in degre
es $> n$ holds for coherent analytic sheaves, while the agreement with singular
cohomology holds for the constant sheaf, which is not coherent. These two facts
concern disjoint classes of sheaves, so they cannot be combined to deduce vanish
ing of singular cohomology in degrees $> n$. The example of $\mathbb{CP}^n$ show
s explicitly that such vanishing fails.
