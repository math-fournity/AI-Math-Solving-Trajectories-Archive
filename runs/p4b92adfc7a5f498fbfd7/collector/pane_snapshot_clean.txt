## Solution

**Total thermal energy** (with unit density and specific heat) is

$$E(t) = \iiint_V u(x,y,z,t)\, dV,$$

where $V$ is the ball of radius $R=2$.

### Step 1: Differentiate under the integral

$$\frac{dE}{dt} = \iiint_V \frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\, dV = \iiint_V \bigl(\
nabla^2 u + 5\bigr)\, dV = \iiint_V \nabla^2 u\, dV + 5\,\text{Vol}(V).$$

### Step 2: Apply the divergence theorem

Since $\nabla^2 u = \nabla \cdot (\nabla u)$, the divergence theorem gives

$$\iiint_V \nabla^2 u\, dV = \oiint_{\partial V} \nabla u \cdot \hat{\mathbf n}\
, dS.$$

On the surface $\partial V$ we are given $\nabla u \cdot \hat{\mathbf n} = 6$, s
o

$$\oiint_{\partial V} \nabla u \cdot \hat{\mathbf n}\, dS = 6 \cdot \text{Area}(
\partial V) = 6 \cdot 4\pi R^2 = 6 \cdot 4\pi \cdot 4 = 96\pi.$$

### Step 3: Volume term

$$\text{Vol}(V) = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3 = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi \cdot 8 = \tfrac{32}{3}\
pi,$$

so

$$5\,\text{Vol}(V) = 5 \cdot \tfrac{32}{3}\pi = \tfrac{160}{3}\pi.$$

### Step 4: Combine

$$\frac{dE}{dt} = 96\pi + \tfrac{160}{3}\pi = \tfrac{288}{3}\pi + \tfrac{160}{3}
\pi = \tfrac{448}{3}\pi.$$

This is a **constant**, independent of $t$. Integrating from $0$ to $t$:

$$E(t) = E(0) + \tfrac{448}{3}\pi\, t.$$

### Step 5: Initial energy

$$E(0) = \iiint_V u(x,y,z,0)\, dV = \iiint_V f(x,y,z)\, dV.$$

### Result

$$\boxed{\,E(t) = \iiint_V f(x,y,z)\, dV + \tfrac{448}{3}\,\pi\, t\,}$$

where $V$ is the ball of radius $2$. The total thermal energy grows linearly in
time at the constant rate $\dfrac{448}{3}\pi$, which is the sum of the inward bo
undary flux contribution $96\pi$ (from the prescribed normal derivative $6$ over
 the surface area $16\pi$) and the uniform source contribution $\tfrac{160}{3}\p
i$ (from the constant source term $5$ over the volume $\tfrac{32}{3}\pi$).
