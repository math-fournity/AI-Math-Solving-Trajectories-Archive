Looking at this problem, I need to evaluate a 6-dimensional integral with two Di

## Analysis

The delta functions impose:
- $\delta(x_1 - x_2 + x_3 - x_4)$: forces $x_4 = x_1 - x_2 + x_3$, with the cons
traint $x_4 \in [0,1]$
- $\delta(x_2 - x_3 + x_5 - x_6)$: forces $x_6 = x_2 - x_3 + x_5$, with the cons
traint $x_6 \in [0,1]$

Integrating out $x_4$ and $x_6$ via the delta functions, the integral becomes th
e 4-dimensional volume:

$$I = \text{Vol}\{(x_1, x_2, x_3, x_5) \in [0,1]^4 : 0 \le x_1 - x_2 + x_3 \le 1
, \; 0 \le x_2 - x_3 + x_5 \le 1\}$$

## Key factorization

Let $t = x_2 - x_3$. For **fixed** $x_2, x_3$ (hence fixed $t$), the two constra
ints become independent:

- **Constraint on $x_1$:** $t \le x_1 \le 1 + t$, combined with $x_1 \in [0,1]$
gives $x_1 \in [\max(0,t),\, \min(1,1+t)]$.
- **Constraint on $x_5$:** $-t \le x_5 \le 1 - t$, combined with $x_5 \in [0,1]$
 gives $x_5 \in [\max(0,-t),\, \min(1,1-t)]$.

Computing the lengths:

**Case $t \ge 0$:** $x_1$-length $= 1 - t$; $x_5$-length $= 1 - t$. Product $= (
1-t)^2$.

**Case $t \le 0$:** $x_1$-length $= 1 + t$; $x_5$-length $= 1 + t$. Product $= (
1+t)^2$.

In both cases the product is $(1 - |t|)^2$. Therefore:

$$I = \int_0^1\!\!\int_0^1 (1 - |x_2 - x_3|)^2 \, dx_2\, dx_3$$

## Reducing to a single integral

The difference $s = x_2 - x_3$ of two independent $\text{Uniform}[0,1]$ variable
s has the **triangular density** $f(s) = 1 - |s|$ for $s \in [-1,1]$. Thus:

$$I = \int_{-1}^{1} (1 - |s|)^2 \cdot (1 - |s|)\, ds = \int_{-1}^{1} (1 - |s|)^3
\, ds$$

## Final evaluation

By symmetry:

$$I = 2\int_0^1 (1 - s)^3\, ds = 2\left[-\frac{(1-s)^4}{4}\right]_0^1 = 2 \cdot
\frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$$

## Result

$$\boxed{I = \dfrac{1}{2}}$$
