Looking at this problem, I need to find the coordinate ring of the intersection
of two affine curves living in different charts of $\mathbb{P}^2(k)$, restricted
 to the overlap where both $x_0 \neq 0$ and $x_2 \neq 0$.

## Setting Up the Coordinates

Let $[x_0 : x_1 : x_2]$ be homogeneous coordinates on $\mathbb{P}^2(k)$.

**Chart $x_0 \neq 0$:** Set $x_0 = 1$. Affine coordinates: $x = x_1/x_0$, $y = x
_2/x_0$. The first curve is $Z(y^n - x^{n-1} + 1)$ with coordinate ring $\mathbb
{C}[x,y]/(y^n - x^{n-1} + 1)$.

**Chart $x_2 \neq 0$:** Set $x_2 = 1$. Affine coordinates: $u = x_0/x_2$, $v = x
_1/x_2$. The second curve is $Z(v^n - u + u^n)$ with coordinate ring $\mathbb{C}
[u,v]/(v^n - u + u^n)$.

## Coordinate Transformation on the Overlap

On the open set $U = \{x_0 \neq 0\} \cap \{x_2 \neq 0\}$, a point $[1 : x : y]$
(in the first chart) equals $[u : v : 1]$ (in the second chart) when:

$$[u : v : 1] = [u/u : v/u : 1/u] = [1 : v/u : 1/u]$$

So $x = v/u$ and $y = 1/u$, giving:
$$u = \frac{1}{y}, \qquad v = \frac{x}{y}$$

The coordinate ring of $U$ is $\mathcal{O}(U) = \mathbb{C}[x, y, y^{-1}]$ (local
izing at $y$ since $x_2 \neq 0 \Leftrightarrow y \neq 0$).

## Transforming the Second Curve into the $(x,y)$ Chart

The second curve $v^n - u + u^n = 0$ becomes, with $u = 1/y$ and $v = x/y$:

$$\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)^n - \frac{1}{y} + \frac{1}{y^n} = 0$$

Multiplying through by $y^n$:

$$x^n - y^{n-1} + 1 = 0$$

## Verification: Transforming the First Curve into the $(u,v)$ Chart

For symmetry, the first curve $y^n - x^{n-1} + 1 = 0$ with $y = 1/u$, $x = v/u$
gives:

$$\frac{1}{u^n} - \frac{v^{n-1}}{u^{n-1}} + 1 = 0$$

Multiplying by $u^n$:

$$u^n - uv^{n-1} + 1 = 0$$

## The Coordinate Ring of the Intersection

The intersection of the two curves on $U$ is the closed subscheme $V(I_1 + I_2)
\subset U$, where:
- $I_1 = (y^n - x^{n-1} + 1)$ (the first curve in the $(x,y)$ chart),
- $I_2 = (x^n - y^{n-1} + 1)$ (the second curve transformed to the $(x,y)$ chart
).

The coordinate ring of the intersection is:

$$\boxed{\mathcal{O}(U)/(I_1 + I_2) = \mathbb{C}[x,\, y,\, y^{-1}]\,\big/\,\big(
y^n - x^{n-1} + 1,\;\; x^n - y^{n-1} + 1\big)}$$

**Equivalently**, in the $(u,v)$ chart (localizing at $u$ since $x_0 \neq 0 \Lef
trightarrow u \neq 0$):

$$\mathbb{C}[u,\, v,\, u^{-1}]\,\big/\,\big(v^n - u + u^n,\;\; u^n - uv^{n-1} +
1\big)$$

These two presentations are isomorphic via the change of variables $u = 1/y$, $v
 = x/y$.

## Consistency Check

From the two equations $y^n = x^{n-1} - 1$ and $x^n = y^{n-1} - 1$, we can deriv
e:
- From (1): $y^{n-1} = (x^{n-1}-1)/y$. Substituting into (2): $x^n = (x^{n-1}-1)
/y - 1$, giving $y(x^n + 1) = x^{n-1} - 1$.
- From (2): $x^{n-1} = (y^{n-1}-1)/x$. Substituting into (1): $y^n = (y^{n-1}-1)
/x - 1$, giving $x(y^n + 1) = y^{n-1} - 1$.

The potential degenerate case $y = 0$ (i.e., $x_2 = 0$) is excluded by our local
ization at $y$, confirming that the localization is necessary and consistent wit
h the condition $x_2 \neq 0$. Specifically, $y = 0$ forces $x^{n-1} = 1$ and $x^
n = -1$, giving $x = -1$ (requiring $n$ odd)—but this point lies outside $U$ sin
ce $y = 0 \Leftrightarrow x_2 = 0$.
