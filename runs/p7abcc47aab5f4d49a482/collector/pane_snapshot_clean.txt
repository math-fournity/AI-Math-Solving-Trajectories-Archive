## Setup and Sign Convention

The integral equation is:

$$\frac{1}{\pi}\,\text{P.V.}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{u(x)}{x-t}\,dx = \fr
ac{1}{1+t^2} \equiv -v(t),$$

so $v(t) = -\dfrac{1}{1+t^2}$.

Using the standard Hilbert transform $\mathcal{H}[g](t) = \frac{1}{\pi}\,\text{P
.V.}\!\int \frac{g(x)}{t-x}\,dx$, the left side equals $-\mathcal{H}[u](t)$, hen
ce:

$$\mathcal{H}[u](t) = v(t) = -\frac{1}{1+t^2}.$$

## Constructing the Complex Function

We seek $f(z)=u+iv$ analytic in the upper half-plane with $v(x,0)=-\frac{1}{1+x^
2}$. Consider:

$$\boxed{f(z) = \frac{1}{z+i}}.$$

The only pole is at $z=-i$ (lower half-plane), so $f$ is analytic in $\operatorn
ame{Im} z > 0$, and $|f(z)|\to 0$ as $|z|\to\infty$.

Writing $z=x+iy$, so $z+i = x+i(y+1)$:

$$f(z)=\frac{x-i(y+1)}{x^2+(y+1)^2},$$

giving

$$u(x,y)=\frac{x}{x^2+(y+1)^2},\qquad v(x,y)=\frac{-(y+1)}{x^2+(y+1)^2}.$$

On the real axis ($y=0$):

$$u(x)=\frac{x}{1+x^2},\qquad v(x)=-\frac{1}{1+x^2}.\quad\checkmark$$

## Verification of the Integral Equation

We compute $\text{P.V.}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{u(x)}{x-t}\,dx = \text{P.V
.}\!\int\frac{x}{(1+x^2)(x-t)}\,dx$.

Partial fractions:

$$\frac{x}{(1+x^2)(x-t)}=\frac{t/(1+t^2)}{x-t}+\frac{1-tx}{(1+t^2)(1+x^2)}.$$

Using $\text{P.V.}\!\int\frac{dx}{x-t}=0$, $\int\frac{dx}{1+x^2}=\pi$, and $\tex
t{P.V.}\!\int\frac{x\,dx}{1+x^2}=0$ (odd integrand):

$$\text{P.V.}\!\int\frac{x}{(1+x^2)(x-t)}\,dx = 0 + \frac{1}{1+t^2}\cdot\pi - \f
rac{t}{1+t^2}\cdot 0 = \frac{\pi}{1+t^2}.$$

Therefore:

$$\frac{1}{\pi}\,\text{P.V.}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{u(x)}{x-t}\,dx = \fra
c{1}{1+t^2}.\quad\checkmark$$

## The Two Properties (Hilbert Transform Pair Conditions)

The two properties ensuring $u(x)$ and $v(x)$ form a Hilbert transform pair are:

**Property 1 — Analyticity.** $f(z)=u(x,y)+iv(x,y)$ is analytic (holomorphic) in
 the upper half-plane $\operatorname{Im}z>0$, i.e., the Cauchy–Riemann equations
 $u_x=v_y$, $u_y=-v_x$ hold there. This allows Cauchy's integral formula to be a
pplied.

*Verification for $f(z)=1/(z+i)$:* Direct computation gives

$$u_x = v_y = \frac{(y+1)^2-x^2}{[x^2+(y+1)^2]^2},\qquad u_y = -v_x = \frac{-2x(
y+1)}{[x^2+(y+1)^2]^2}.\quad\checkmark$$

**Property 2 — Vanishing at infinity.** $f(z)\to 0$ as $|z|\to\infty$ in the upp
er half-plane. This ensures the large semicircular arc contributes nothing when
applying Cauchy's formula, so only the real-axis integral remains.

*Verification:* $|f(z)|=\frac{1}{|z+i|}\to 0$ as $|z|\to\infty$.$\quad\checkmark
$

## Why These Two Properties Give the Hilbert Transform Pair

For $f$ analytic in the upper half-plane and vanishing at infinity, integrate $f
(z)/(z-z_0)$ over a semicircular contour. Letting $z_0\to x_0\in\mathbb{R}$ from
 above and applying the Plemelj–Sokhotski formula:

$$f(x_0)=\frac{1}{\pi i}\,\text{P.V.}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{f(x)}{x-x_0}
\,dx.$$

Separating real and imaginary parts:

$$u(x_0)=\frac{1}{\pi}\,\text{P.V.}\!\int\frac{v(x)}{x-x_0}\,dx=-\mathcal{H}[v](
x_0),$$

$$v(x_0)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\,\text{P.V.}\!\int\frac{u(x)}{x-x_0}\,dx=\mathcal{H}[u](
x_0).$$

Thus $v=\mathcal{H}[u]$ and $u=-\mathcal{H}[v]$, the Hilbert transform pair rela
tion. Analyticity is needed for Cauchy's formula; vanishing at infinity is neede
d for the arc to drop out. Both are satisfied by $f(z)=\frac{1}{z+i}$.

## Summary

$$f(z)=\frac{1}{z+i},\qquad u(x)=\frac{x}{1+x^2},\qquad v(x)=-\frac{1}{1+x^2}.$$

The two properties are: **(1) analyticity of $f$ in the upper half-plane**, and
**(2) $f(z)\to 0$ as $|z|\to\infty$**.
