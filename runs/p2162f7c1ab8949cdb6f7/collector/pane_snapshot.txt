**Proof.**

We show that any linear operator $T:E\to E^*$ which maps every Cauchy sequence i

Recall that $E^*$, the dual of any normed space, is complete (a Banach space), s
o Cauchy sequences in $E^*$ are precisely the convergent ones, and in particular
 every Cauchy sequence in $E^*$ is bounded.

**Claim.** If $T:E\to E^*$ is linear and sends every Cauchy sequence of $E$ to a
 Cauchy sequence of $E^*$, then $T$ is bounded.

*Proof of claim.* Suppose, for contradiction, that $T$ is unbounded. Then there
exists a sequence $(u_n)\subset E$ with $\|u_n\|=1$ for all $n$ and $\|Tu_n\|\to
\infty$.

Define
$$
v_n \;=\; \frac{u_n}{\sqrt{\|Tu_n\|}}.
$$
Then
$$
\|v_n\| \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{\|Tu_n\|}} \;\longrightarrow\; 0,
$$
so $(v_n)$ converges to $0$ in $E$; in particular $(v_n)$ is Cauchy. By hypothes
is, $(Tv_n)$ is Cauchy in $E^*$, hence bounded.

But by linearity,
$$
\|Tv_n\| \;=\; \frac{\|Tu_n\|}{\sqrt{\|Tu_n\|}} \;=\; \sqrt{\|Tu_n\|} \;\longrig
htarrow\; \infty,
$$
so $(Tv_n)$ is unbounded — contradicting the fact that Cauchy sequences are boun
ded.

Hence $T$ must be bounded, i.e. continuous. $\square$

**Existence of a counterexample.** For any infinite-dimensional Banach space $E$
 (e.g. $E=\ell^2$), discontinuous linear maps $T:E\to E^*$ exist: pick a discont
inuous linear functional $f:E\to\mathbb{R}$ (constructed via a Hamel basis) and
a fixed nonzero $\varphi\in E^*$, and set $T(x)=f(x)\,\varphi$. This $T$ is line
ar and unbounded, hence discontinuous. By the claim, it cannot send every Cauchy
 sequence to a Cauchy sequence.

**Conclusion.** The property "maps Cauchy sequences to Cauchy sequences" forces
a linear operator $T:E\to E^*$ to be continuous. Therefore it is **not** true th
at every linear operator — in particular not every discontinuous one — maps Cauc
