Looking at this problem, I need to find the 2-adic valuation (exponent of 2) in
S(7,7).

## Setting up the valuation recurrence

Let $f(m,n) = v_2(S(m,n))$, the 2-adic valuation. Since $S(m,n) = S(m-1,n)\cdot
S(m,n-1)$, multiplicativity of valuations gives:

$$f(m,n) = f(m-1,n) + f(m,n-1) \quad (m\ge 2,\; n\ge 2)$$

with boundary conditions $f(1,n) = v_2(n)$ and $f(m,1) = v_2(1) = 0$.

## Closed form

This is a Pascal-type recurrence. The boundary values live on the first row $\{(
1,j): j\ge 2\}$ and first column $\{(i,1): i\ge 1\}$ (which contribute 0). A pat
h from boundary point $(1,j)$ to $(m,n)$ must step **down** first (to $(2,j)$),
otherwise it stays on the boundary and hits another boundary point. From $(2,j)$
 to $(m,n)$ we take $(m-2)$ down-steps and $(n-j)$ right-steps freely. So:

$$f(m,n) = \sum_{j=2}^{n} \binom{(m-2)+(n-j)}{m-2}\, v_2(j).$$

**Verification on small cases:** $f(2,3) = \binom{1}{0}\cdot 1 + \binom{0}{0}\cd
ot 0 = 1$, and indeed $S(2,3)=S(1,3)\cdot S(2,2)=3\cdot 2=6$, so $v_2=1$. ✓
$f(3,4)=\binom{2}{1}\cdot1+\binom{1}{1}\cdot0+\binom{0}{0}\cdot2=3+0+2=5$, and d
irect recurrence gives $f(3,4)=f(2,4)+f(3,3)=3+2=5$. ✓

## Computing f(7,7)

With $m=7$, so $m-2=5$:

$$f(7,7) = \sum_{j=2}^{7} \binom{12-j}{5}\, v_2(j).$$

The valuations $v_2(j)$ for $j=2,\dots,7$: $v_2(2)=1,\; v_2(3)=0,\; v_2(4)=2,\;
v_2(5)=0,\; v_2(6)=1,\; v_2(7)=0$.

| $j$ | $\binom{12-j}{5}$ | $v_2(j)$ | product |
|-----|-------------------|----------|---------|
| 2   | $\binom{10}{5}=252$ | 1 | 252 |
| 3   | $\binom{9}{5}=126$  | 0 | 0 |
| 4   | $\binom{8}{5}=56$   | 2 | 112 |
| 5   | $\binom{7}{5}=21$   | 0 | 0 |
| 6   | $\binom{6}{5}=6$    | 1 | 6 |
| 7   | $\binom{5}{5}=1$    | 0 | 0 |

$$f(7,7) = 252 + 112 + 6 = 370.$$

Both the closed form and the direct recurrence computation agree: $f(7,7)=370$.

## Conclusion

The largest integer $k$ such that $2^k$ divides $S(7,7)$ is $\boxed{370}$.
