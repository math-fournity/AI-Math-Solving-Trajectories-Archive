## Solution

**Setup.** We have a bivariate normal sample with $n=50$ and the MLE $\hat{\rho}
=0.85$. We seek asymptotic confidence limits for $\phi = \sin^{-1}(\rho)$, $-\pi
/2 < \phi < \pi/2$.

**Step 1 — Asymptotic variance of $\hat{\rho}$.** For the MLE of the correlation
 coefficient in a bivariate normal (all parameters unknown), the asymptotic vari
ance is

$$\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\rho}) \approx \frac{1-\rho^{2}}{n}.$$

**Step 2 — Variance-stabilizing transformation.** We seek $g(\rho)$ such that $g
'(\rho)^{2}\cdot\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\rho})$ is constant (free of $\rho$). Se
tting

$$g'(\rho)^{2}\cdot\frac{1-\rho^{2}}{n} = \frac{1}{n},$$

we require

$$g'(\rho) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}.$$

Integrating:

$$g(\rho) = \int \frac{d\rho}{\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}} = \sin^{-1}(\rho) = \phi.$$

Thus $\phi = \sin^{-1}(\rho)$ is precisely the variance-stabilizing transformati
on, and

$$\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\phi}) = \operatorname{Var}\!\bigl(\sin^{-1}(\hat{\rho
})\bigr) \approx \frac{1}{n} = \frac{1}{50},$$

a constant independent of $\rho$.

**Step 3 — Asymptotic distribution and confidence limits.** By the delta method,

$$\hat{\phi} = \sin^{-1}(\hat{\rho}) \;\;\dot{\sim}\;\; N\!\left(\phi,\;\frac{1}
{n}\right).$$

Therefore the asymptotic $(1-\alpha)$ confidence limits for $\phi$ are

$$\sin^{-1}(\hat{\rho}) \;\pm\; \frac{z_{\alpha/2}}{\sqrt{n}}.$$

**Step 4 — Numerical computation.** With $\hat{\rho}=0.85$ and $n=50$:

$$\hat{\phi} = \sin^{-1}(0.85) \approx 1.0161 \text{ rad}, \qquad \frac{1}{\sqrt
{50}} \approx 0.1414.$$

General form:

$$\boxed{1.0161 \;\pm\; z_{\alpha/2}\,(0.1414)}.$$

For a **95%** confidence interval ($z_{0.025}=1.96$):

$$\text{Margin} = 1.96 \times 0.1414 \approx 0.2772,$$

$$\text{Lower} = 1.0161 - 0.2772 = 0.7389, \qquad \text{Upper} = 1.0161 + 0.2772
 = 1.2933.$$

Both limits lie in $(-\pi/2,\;\pi/2) = (-1.5708,\;1.5708)$. ✓

$$\boxed{0.739 \;<\; \phi \;<\; 1.293 \quad (95\%\text{ asymptotic CI}).}$$

**Step 5 — Conversion back to $\rho$ (optional).** Using $\rho = \sin(\phi)$:

$$\rho_{L} = \sin(0.7389) \approx 0.674, \qquad \rho_{U} = \sin(1.2933) \approx
0.962.$$

So the corresponding 95% asymptotic CI for $\rho$ is approximately $(0.674,\;0.9
62)$.

$$\sin^{-1}(0.85) \pm \frac{z_{\alpha/2}}{\sqrt{50}} \;=\; 1.0161 \pm 0.1414\,z_
