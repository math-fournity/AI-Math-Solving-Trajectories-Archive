## Solution

**Key observation:** The unit sphere is $x^2+y^2+z^2=1$, so every point on it sa
tisfies $|x|\le 1$ and $|y|\le 1$, hence $x+y \le 2$. The plane $x+y=14$ lies en
tirely outside the unit sphere: the distance from the origin to this plane is $\
tfrac{14}{\sqrt{2}}\approx 9.9 > 1$. Therefore the intersection of the unit sphe
re and the plane $x+y=14$ is **empty** — there is no closed curve to integrate o
ver.

**Verification via the requested surface integral (Stokes' theorem).**

Let $F=\langle P,Q,R\rangle=\langle yz+y,\; xz+5x,\; xy+2y\rangle$. Compute the
curl:

$$\nabla\times F=\left\langle \frac{\partial R}{\partial y}-\frac{\partial Q}{\p
artial z},\; \frac{\partial P}{\partial z}-\frac{\partial R}{\partial x},\; \fra
c{\partial Q}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right\rangle$$

- $\dfrac{\partial R}{\partial y}=x+2$, $\dfrac{\partial Q}{\partial z}=x$ → fir
st component $=2$
- $\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial z}=y$, $\dfrac{\partial R}{\partial x}=y$ → secon
d component $=0$
- $\dfrac{\partial Q}{\partial x}=z+5$, $\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial y}=z+1$ → t
hird component $=4$

So $\nabla\times F=\langle 2,0,4\rangle$, a constant vector.

The unit normal to the plane $x+y=14$ is $\mathbf{n}=\dfrac{\langle 1,1,0\rangle
}{\sqrt{2}}$, giving

$$\nabla\times F\cdot\mathbf{n}=\frac{2\cdot1+0\cdot1+4\cdot0}{\sqrt{2}}=\frac{2
}{\sqrt{2}}=\sqrt{2}.$$

By Stokes' theorem, the line integral equals the flux of $\nabla\times F$ throug
h the disk $D$ in the plane $x+y=14$ bounded by the intersection curve:

$$\oint_C F\cdot d\mathbf{r}=\iint_D (\nabla\times F)\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dA=\sqrt{2
}\cdot\operatorname{Area}(D).$$

The disk $D$ consists of points satisfying $x+y=14$ and $x^2+y^2+z^2\le 1$. Sinc
e every such point would need $x+y=14$ while $x^2+y^2+z^2\le 1$ forces $x+y\le\s
qrt{2}\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\le\sqrt{2}<14$, **no point satisfies both conditions**. Thu
s $D=\varnothing$ and $\operatorname{Area}(D)=0$.

$$\oint_C F\cdot d\mathbf{r}=\sqrt{2}\cdot 0=0.$$

The intersection curve is empty, so the line integral is trivially zero.
