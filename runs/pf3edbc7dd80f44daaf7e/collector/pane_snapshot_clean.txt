## Solution

We evaluate the contour integral

$$\oint_C \frac{\cos\pi z}{z^2-1}\,dz$$

where $C$ is the rectangle with vertices $2+i,\;2-i,\;-2+i,\;-2-i$, i.e. the rec
tangle $\{x+iy : -2\le x\le 2,\;-1\le y\le 1\}$, traversed once (positively, by
convention).

### Singularities inside the contour

$$\frac{\cos\pi z}{z^2-1}=\frac{\cos\pi z}{(z-1)(z+1)}$$

has simple poles at $z=1$ and $z=-1$. Both lie on the real axis with $-2<-1<1<2$
 and imaginary part $0\in[-1,1]$, so **both poles are inside** the rectangle.

### Residues

**At $z=1$ (simple pole):**

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=1}=\lim_{z\to 1}(z-1)\frac{\cos\pi z}{(z-1)(z+1)}=\frac{
\cos\pi}{2}=\frac{-1}{2}=-\frac12.$$

**At $z=-1$ (simple pole):**

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=-1}=\lim_{z\to -1}(z+1)\frac{\cos\pi z}{(z-1)(z+1)}=\fra
c{\cos(-\pi)}{-2}=\frac{-1}{-2}=\frac12.$$

(Using $\cos(-\pi)=\cos\pi=-1$.)

### Apply the Residue Theorem

$$\oint_C \frac{\cos\pi z}{z^2-1}\,dz = 2\pi i\left(\operatorname{Res}_{z=1}+\op
eratorname{Res}_{z=-1}\right)=2\pi i\left(-\frac12+\frac12\right)=2\pi i\cdot 0.
$$

### Result

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\oint_C \frac{\cos\pi z}{z^2-1}\,dz = 0.}$$

The two residues cancel because $\cos\pi z$ takes the value $-1$ at both $z=\pm
1$, and the factors $(z\pm 1)$ contribute opposite signs, making the residues ne
gatives of each other.
