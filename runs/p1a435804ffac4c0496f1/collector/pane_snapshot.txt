 Note $f$ is **even**: $f(-t) = f(t)$.

**Zero at $t=0$?** $f(0) = 1 - 0 = 1 \neq 0$. So $t=0$ is not a zero, and all ze

**Boundary.** At $t = 2n$: $|\cos(2\pi n)| = 1$ and $\frac{4n^2}{4n^2} = 1$, so
$f(2n) = 0$. This is always a solution.

k, k+1]$, $k = 0, \ldots, 2n-1$. On each, $|\cos(\pi t)|$ equals 1 at both endpo

**Key values:**
- At integers $t = k$ ($0 \leq k < 2n$): $f(k) = 1 - \frac{k^2}{4n^2} > 0$.
- At half-integers $t = k+\tfrac{1}{2}$: $f(k+\tfrac{1}{2}) = 0 - \frac{(k+1/2)^
2}{4n^2} < 0$.

**First half $[k, k+\tfrac{1}{2}]$: exactly 1 zero.** Here $|\cos(\pi t)|$ is st
rictly decreasing (from 1 to 0) while $\frac{t^2}{4n^2}$ is strictly increasing,

$$g(s) = \sin(\pi s) - \frac{(k + \tfrac{1}{2} + s)^2}{4n^2} = 0.$$

$$g'(\tfrac{1}{2}) = \pi\cos(\tfrac{\pi}{2}) - \frac{2n}{2n^2} = -\frac{1}{n} <

concave (at most 2 zeros) and we have found the interior zero plus $s = \tfrac{1
