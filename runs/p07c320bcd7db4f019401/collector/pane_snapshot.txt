## Solution

Let $x = \frac{1}{t}$. As $t \to 0$, we have $x \to \pm\infty$, and:

- $\dfrac{1}{t^2(1+t^2)} = \dfrac{x^2}{1+1/x^2} = \dfrac{x^4}{x^2+1}$
- $\sin\!\left(\dfrac{1}{t}\sin\dfrac{1}{t}\right) = \sin(x\sin x)$
- $e^{-1/t^2} = e^{-x^2}$

So the limit becomes:

$$\lim_{x\to\pm\infty} \frac{x^4}{x^2+1}\,\sin(x\sin x)\,e^{-x^2}.$$

**Bounding.** Since $|\sin(x\sin x)|\le 1$ for all $x$,

$$\left|\frac{x^4}{x^2+1}\,\sin(x\sin x)\,e^{-x^2}\right| \le \frac{x^4}{x^2+1}\
,e^{-x^2} \le x^2\,e^{-x^2},$$

where the last inequality uses $\dfrac{x^4}{x^2+1}\le x^2$ (since $x^4 \le x^2(x
^2+1)=x^4+x^2$).

**The bound tends to 0.** For $|x|\ge 1$,

$$x^2 e^{-x^2} = \frac{x^2}{e^{x^2}} \le \frac{x^2}{1+x^2+\frac{x^4}{2}} \le \fr
ac{2}{x^2}\to 0 \quad\text{as } |x|\to\infty.$$

(Equivalently, $x^2 e^{-x^2}\to 0$ since the exponential $e^{x^2}$ grows faster
than any polynomial.)

**Conclusion by the Squeeze Theorem.** We have

$$-x^2 e^{-x^2} \;\le\; \frac{x^4}{x^2+1}\,\sin(x\sin x)\,e^{-x^2} \;\le\; x^2 e
^{-x^2},$$

and both bounds $\to 0$ as $|x|\to\infty$. Therefore

$$\boxed{\lim_{t \to 0}\frac{1}{t^{2}\left(1+t^{2}\right)}\sin\left(\frac{1}{t}\
sin\frac{1}{t}\right)e^{-\frac{1}{t^{2}}}=0.}$$

The essential reason is that the Gaussian factor $e^{-1/t^2}$ decays faster than
 any polynomial in $1/|t|$ grows, while the sine factor remains bounded — so the
 entire expression is squeezed to $0$.
