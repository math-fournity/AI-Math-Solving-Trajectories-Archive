## Setup and Simplification

Let $b_i = 1 - a_i \in [0,1]$, so the Global Retention Factor is $R = \prod_{j=1
}^n b_j$. The efficiency of tank $i$ is

$$c_i \;=\; 1 - \frac{a_i}{1-R} \;=\; \frac{(1-a_i) - R}{1-R} \;=\; \frac{b_i -
R}{1-R},$$

and the total performance is $P = \prod_{i=1}^n c_i$. We require $R < 1$ (i.e.,
not all $a_i = 0$) to avoid division by zero.

**Key observation: $c_i \ge 0$.** Since $R = b_i \cdot \prod_{j \ne i} b_j \le b
_i$ (as each $b_j \le 1$), we have $b_i - R \ge 0$, so $c_i \ge 0$.

**Key observation: $\sum c_i \le n-1$.** Let $A = \sum_{j=1}^n a_j$. Then

$$\sum_{i=1}^n c_i = n - \frac{A}{1-R}.$$

By the union bound (Bernoulli's inequality / inclusion–exclusion), $1 - R = 1 -
\prod(1-a_j) \le \sum a_j = A$, so $\frac{A}{1-R} \ge 1$, giving $\sum c_i \le n
 - 1$.

## Upper Bound for Fixed $n$

Since $c_i \ge 0$ and $\sum c_i \le n-1$, by the AM–GM inequality:

$$P = \prod_{i=1}^n c_i \;\le\; \left(\frac{\sum c_i}{n}\right)^{\!n} \;\le\; \l
eft(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)^{\!n}.$$

## The Bound is Tight (Approached)

Take $a_1 = a_2 = \cdots = a_n = a$ with $a \to 0^+$. Then:

- $R = (1-a)^n = 1 - na + \frac{n(n-1)}{2}a^2 - \cdots$
- $1 - R = na - \frac{n(n-1)}{2}a^2 + O(a^3)$
- $c_i = 1 - \frac{a}{1-R} = 1 - \frac{1}{n - \frac{n(n-1)}{2}a + O(a^2)} \;\lon
grightarrow\; 1 - \frac{1}{n} = \frac{n-1}{n}$ as $a \to 0^+$.

All $c_i$ are equal, and $\sum c_i \to n - 1$, so both AM–GM inequalities become
 tight in the limit. Therefore:

$$\sup P \;=\; \left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)^{\!n} = \left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right)^{
\!n} \quad \text{(for fixed } n\text{)}.$$

This supremum is approached but never achieved (it would require $a=0$, which gi
ves $R=1$).

## Supremum Over All $n$

The sequence $\left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right)^n$ is well-known to be strictly incre
asing in $n$ (for $n \ge 1$) with limit $e^{-1}$. Since the problem allows $n$ t
o be any natural number, we take the supremum over all $n$:

$$\sup_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right)^n = \lim_{n \to \infty}\l
eft(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right)^n = \frac{1}{e}.$$

This value is not attained for any finite $n$, but is approached arbitrarily clo
sely by choosing large $n$ and small equal $a_i$.

## Conclusion

The supremum of the total system performance is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{e}}.$$
