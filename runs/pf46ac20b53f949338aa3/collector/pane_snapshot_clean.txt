## Theorem

Let G be any group (in particular any finite group of Lie type such as GL_n(F_q)
, its analogues, or a unipotent group over F_q). A subset S ⊆ G has the property
 that the distinct left translates {gS : g ∈ G} form a partition of G (i.e., G i
s the disjoint union of the left translates of S) **if and only if S is a left c
oset of a subgroup of G**.

Equivalently (since a left coset of H is a right coset of a conjugate of H), S i
s a coset of some subgroup of G.

## Proof

### (⇐) Cosets of subgroups work.

Let H ≤ G be a subgroup and let S = H s_0 for some s_0 ∈ G (a left coset). For a
ny g ∈ G,
$$gS = g(Hs_0) = (gH)s_0.$$
The left cosets {gH : g ∈ G} partition G. Right-multiplication by s_0 is a bijec
tion G → G, so it carries the partition {gH} to the partition {(gH)s_0} = {gS}.
Hence the distinct left translates of S are pairwise disjoint and their union is
 G:
$$G = \bigsqcup_{gH \,\in\, G/H} gS.$$
So every left coset of a subgroup satisfies the condition. ∎ (sufficiency)

### (⇒) Only cosets of subgroups work.

Suppose the distinct left translates of S partition G. Define the **stabilizer o
f S under left translation**:
$$H := \{g \in G : gS = S\}.$$
It is immediate that H is a subgroup of G (if gS = S and hS = S then (gh)S = g(h
S) = gS = S, and g^{-1}S = S).

**Step 1 — distinct translates are indexed by left cosets of H.** For g_1, g_2 ∈
 G,
$$g_1 S = g_2 S \iff S = g_2^{-1}g_1 S \iff g_2^{-1}g_1 \in H \iff g_1 H = g_2 H
.$$
Hence the distinct left translates of S are in bijection with the left cosets G/
H, and there are exactly [G : H] of them.

**Step 2 — size constraint.** Each translate gS has size |S|. Since the [G : H]
distinct translates are pairwise disjoint and cover G,
$$|G| = [G : H]\cdot|S| \quad\Longrightarrow\quad |S| = |H|.$$
(For infinite groups, replace cardinalities by the bijection gS ↔ gH throughout;
 the argument is cardinality-free once we use the bijection in Step 1.)

**Step 3 — disjointness forces S into a single H-coset.** Because the translates
 are pairwise disjoint, for any g ∉ H the cosets eH and gH are distinct, so
$$S = eS \quad\text{and}\quad gS \quad\text{are disjoint:}\quad S \cap gS = \emp
tyset \quad (g \notin H).$$
Equivalently, if there exist s_1, s_2 ∈ S with s_1 = g s_2, then g ∈ H. Taking g
 = s_1 s_2^{-1} (which is the unique element with s_1 = g s_2), we obtain
$$s_1 s_2^{-1} \in H \quad\text{for all } s_1, s_2 \in S, \qquad\text{i.e.,}\qua
d SS^{-1} \subseteq H.$$

Now fix any s_0 ∈ S. For every s ∈ S we have s s_0^{-1} ∈ H, hence s ∈ H s_0. Th
erefore
$$S \subseteq H s_0.$$
But |S| = |H| = |H s_0| (Step 2), so the inclusion is equality:
$$S = H s_0,$$
a left coset of the subgroup H. ∎ (necessity)

## Conclusion

Combining both directions, the subsets S ⊆ G for which G is the disjoint union o
f the left translates of S are **precisely the (left) cosets of subgroups of G**
:
$$\boxed{\,S \text{ works} \iff S = Hs \text{ for some subgroup } H \le G \text{
 and some } s \in G\,}.$$

This characterization is uniform across all groups. Specializing to the families
 mentioned in the problem:

- **G = GL_n(F_q):** the admissible S are exactly the cosets Hs of subgroups H ≤
 GL_n(F_q) — e.g., cosets of parabolic subgroups, Borel subgroups, the unipotent
 radical, tori, SL_n(F_q) (when it is normal, cosets are its cosets), or any oth
er subgroup.
- **Unipotent groups U_n(F_q) (upper unitriangular matrices):** the admissible S
 are the cosets of subgroups of U_n(F_q) — for instance cosets of the various na
tural subgroups determined by prescribed zero patterns below the superdiagonal.
- **Other finite groups of Lie type (SL_n, Sp_{2n}, SO_n, E_8(q), …):** identica
lly, the admissible S are the cosets of their subgroups.

No hypothesis on G beyond being a group is used; the Lie-type structure provides
 a rich supply of subgroups (and hence of admissible cosets) but does not alter
the characterization itself.
