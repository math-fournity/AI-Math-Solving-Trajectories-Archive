## Solution

The statement is **true**.

**Proof.**

Since $f_n \to f$ in measure, there exists a subsequence $f_{n_k} \to f$ a.e. Al
ong this subsequence $|f_{n_k}| \leq 1$, so $|f| \leq 1$ a.e. Consequently $|f_n
 - f| \leq |f_n| + |f| \leq 2$ a.e.

We decompose:
$$f_n g_n - fg = f_n(g_n - g) + (f_n - f)g.$$

**Term 1.** Since $|f_n| \leq 1$,
$$\|f_n(g_n - g)\|_p^p = \int |f_n|^p |g_n - g|^p \leq \int |g_n - g|^p = \|g_n
- g\|_p^p \to 0.$$

**Term 2.** We show $\|(f_n - f)g\|_p \to 0$.

*Domination:* Since $|f_n - f| \leq 2$ a.e.,
$$|(f_n - f)g|^p \leq 2^p |g|^p \in L_1$$
because $g \in L_p$.

*Convergence in measure:* We claim $(f_n - f)g \to 0$ in measure. For any $\vare
psilon, M > 0$,
$$\{|(f_n-f)g| > \varepsilon\} \subseteq \{|f_n - f| > \varepsilon/M\} \cup \{|g
| > M\},$$
so
$$\mu(|(f_n-f)g| > \varepsilon) \leq \mu(|f_n - f| > \varepsilon/M) + \mu(|g| >
M).$$

Given $\delta > 0$, since $|g|^p \in L_1$, by Chebyshev $\mu(|g| > M) \leq \|g\|
_p^p / M^p \to 0$ as $M \to \infty$; choose $M$ so this is $< \delta/2$. Since $
f_n \to f$ in measure, $\mu(|f_n - f| > \varepsilon/M) \to 0$; for large $n$ thi
s is $< \delta/2$. Hence $\mu(|(f_n-f)g| > \varepsilon) < \delta$ for large $n$,
 proving $(f_n-f)g \to 0$ in measure.

By continuity of $x \mapsto |x|^p$, $|(f_n-f)g|^p \to 0$ in measure as well.

*Applying generalized dominated convergence:* We have $|(f_n-f)g|^p \to 0$ in me
asure and $|(f_n-f)g|^p \leq 2^p|g|^p \in L_1$. By the dominated convergence the
orem (measure version), $\int |(f_n-f)g|^p \to 0$, i.e., $\|(f_n-f)g\|_p \to 0$.

**Conclusion.** Both $\|f_n(g_n-g)\|_p \to 0$ and $\|(f_n-f)g\|_p \to 0$, so by
the triangle inequality
$$\|f_n g_n - fg\|_p \leq \|f_n(g_n - g)\|_p + \|(f_n-f)g\|_p \to 0.$$

Thus $f_n g_n \to fg$ in $L_p$. $\blacksquare$
