## Derivation of the Kirchhoff Diffraction Formula

### Step 1. Identify the governing equation and the Green's function

Let the diffracted field $\psi$ satisfy the **homogeneous Helmholtz equation** i
n the observation region $V$ (the half-space behind the screen, containing the o
bservation point $\rho$):

$$(\nabla^2 + k^2)\,\psi(r) = 0, \qquad r\in V.$$

Let $G(r,\rho)=\dfrac{e^{ik\,|r-\rho|}}{4\pi\,|r-\rho|}$ be the **outgoing free-
space Green's function**, satisfying

$$(\nabla^2 + k^2)\,G(r,\rho) = -\delta^{(3)}(r-\rho).$$

### Step 2. Obtain the surface-integral representation via Gauss's theorem

Apply **Green's second identity** (which is a direct corollary of Gauss's diverg
ence theorem $\int_V\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf{F}\,d^3r=\int_S\mathbf{F}\!\cdot\!\ma
thbf{n}\,dS$ with $\mathbf{F}=\psi\nabla G - G\nabla\psi$) to $\psi$ and $G$ ove
r $V$:

$$\int_V\bigl(\psi\,\nabla^2 G - G\,\nabla^2\psi\bigr)\,d^3r \;=\; \int_S\bigl(\
psi\,\nabla G - G\,\nabla\psi\bigr)\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}\;dS.$$

Substituting the equations for $\psi$ and $G$:

$$\int_V\bigl[\psi\,(-k^2 G-\delta) - G\,(-k^2\psi)\bigr]\,d^3r = -\int_V\psi\,\
delta^{(3)}(r-\rho)\,d^3r = -\psi(\rho).$$

Hence

$$\boxed{\;\psi(\rho) = \int_S\bigl[\psi(r)\,\nabla G(r,\rho) - G(r,\rho)\,\nabl
a\psi(r)\bigr]\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}\;dS.\;}$$

This is the starting equation with the **volume term $\int_V\phi\,G\,d^3r$ absen
t**, because the diffraction region $V$ is **source-free** — all sources (the in
cident wave) are encoded in the boundary data on $S$. The factor $f(r)$ in the o
riginal statement is absorbed into the boundary values of $\psi$ on each part of
 $S$.

### Step 3. Decompose the closed surface $S$

The boundary of the observation region consists of three parts:

$$S = S_{\text{ap}} \;\cup\; S_{\text{screen}} \;\cup\; S_{R},$$

where $S_{\text{ap}}$ is the **aperture**, $S_{\text{screen}}$ is the **opaque p
art of the screen**, and $S_R$ is a **large hemisphere of radius $R$** closing t
he volume at infinity (taken at finite $R$ and then $R\to\infty$).

### Step 4. Apply the Kirchhoff boundary conditions

The **Kirchhoff approximation** prescribes:

| Surface | $\psi$ | $\nabla\psi$ |
|---|---|---|
| $S_{\text{ap}}$ (aperture) | incident plane wave $e^{ik_i\cdot r}$ | $\nabla e
^{ik_i\cdot r}$ |
| $S_{\text{screen}}$ (opaque) | $0$ | $0$ |
| $S_R$ (hemisphere) | outgoing spherical wave $\dfrac{e^{ik_i r}}{r}\,f(\theta,
\phi)$ | radial derivative |

The screen contribution vanishes identically, so only the aperture and the hemis
phere survive.

### Step 5. Aperture integral

On $S_{\text{ap}}$, substituting $\psi = e^{ik_i\cdot r}$ and $\nabla\psi = \nab
la e^{ik_i\cdot r}$:

$$\int_{\text{ap}}\bigl[e^{ik_i\cdot r}\,\nabla G(r,\rho) - G(r,\rho)\,\nabla e^
{ik_i\cdot r}\bigr]\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}\;dS.$$

This is the **first term** of the target expression.

### Step 6. Hemisphere integral — using the del operator on a sphere

On $S_R$ the outward normal is $\mathbf{n}=\hat{r}$, so the normal component of
the gradient reduces to the **radial derivative** (key property of $\nabla$ on a
 spherical surface):

$$\nabla(\cdot)\cdot\mathbf{n}\;\Big|_{S_R} = \frac{\partial}{\partial r}(\cdot)
\Big|_{r=R}, \qquad dS = R^2\,d\Omega.$$

The boundary field is $\psi\big|_{S_R} = \dfrac{e^{ik_i r}}{r}\,f(\theta,\phi)$.
 Since $f(\theta,\phi)$ depends only on angles (not on $r$), the radial derivati
ve factorizes:

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left[\frac{e^{ik_i r}}{r}\,f(\theta,\phi)\right]
 = f(\theta,\phi)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left[\frac{e^{ik_i r}}{r}\right
].$$

Therefore the hemisphere integral becomes

$$\int f(\theta,\phi)\left[\frac{e^{ik_i r}}{r}\,\frac{\partial G(r,\rho)}{\part
ial r} - G(r,\rho)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left(\frac{e^{ik_i r}}{r}\righ
t)\right]_{r=R}\!R^2\,d\Omega.$$

This is the **second term** of the target expression.

### Step 7. Combine

Adding the aperture and hemisphere contributions (the screen contributing nothin
g):

$$\boxed{\;\psi(\rho) = \int_{\text{ap}} \bigl[e^{ik_i\cdot r}\,\nabla G(r,\rho)
 - G(r,\rho)\,\nabla e^{ik_i\cdot r}\bigr]\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}\;dS \;+\; \int f(\
theta,\phi)\left[\frac{e^{ik_i r}}{r}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial r}G(r,\rho) - G(
r,\rho)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\frac{e^{ik_i r}}{r}\right]_{r=R}\!R^2\,d\Om
ega.\;}$$

### Summary of key ingredients used

1. **Gauss's theorem** (divergence theorem) → Green's second identity → the surf
ace-integral representation, with the volume term vanishing because $V$ is sourc
e-free.
2. **Del-operator property on a sphere**: $\nabla(\cdot)\cdot\hat{r} = \partial(
\cdot)/\partial r$ and $dS=R^2\,d\Omega$, converting the vector surface integral
 on the hemisphere into a radial-derivative form.
3. **Kirchhoff boundary conditions**: undisturbed incident plane wave on the ape
rture, zero field on the opaque screen, outgoing spherical wave $\frac{e^{ik_i r
}}{r}f(\theta,\phi)$ on the closing hemisphere — the **aperture approximation**
and the **outgoing-wave condition**.
