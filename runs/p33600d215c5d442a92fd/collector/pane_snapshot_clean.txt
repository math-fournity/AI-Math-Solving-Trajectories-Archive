## Proof

**Claim:** $K(x,y) = 2^{xy}$ is a positive definite kernel on $\mathbb{N}$.

**Key idea.** Write $2^{xy} = e^{(\ln 2)\,xy}$ and expand the exponential as a p
ower series. Each monomial term $x^k y^k$ is a rank-one positive semidefinite ke
rnel, and the series has strictly positive coefficients, so the sum is positive
semidefinite.

---

**Proof.** Set $c = \ln 2 > 0$. For any $x, y \in \mathbb{N}$,

$$2^{xy} = e^{c\,xy} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{c^k}{k!}\, x^k y^k.$$

Let $x_1, \dots, x_n \in \mathbb{N}$ and $a_1, \dots, a_n \in \mathbb{R}$. Defin
e the partial sums

$$S_N \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{N} \frac{c^k}{k!}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i\, x_i^k\right)
^{\!2}.$$

**Step 1: Each $S_N \geq 0$.** Every summand is a product of the positive scalar
 $c^k/k!$ and a square, hence non-negative. So $S_N \geq 0$ for every $N$, and t
he sequence $(S_N)$ is non-decreasing.

**Step 2: Relate $S_N$ to the kernel sum.** Expanding the square and exchanging
the finite sums,

$$S_N = \sum_{k=0}^{N} \frac{c^k}{k!} \sum_{i,j=1}^{n} a_i a_j\, x_i^k x_j^k = \
sum_{i,j=1}^{n} a_i a_j \sum_{k=0}^{N} \frac{(c\, x_i x_j)^k}{k!}.$$

**Step 3: Take $N \to \infty$.** For each fixed pair $(i,j)$, the inner partial
sum converges to $e^{c\,x_i x_j} = 2^{x_i x_j}$, which is finite. Since this is
a finite linear combination (over $i,j$) of convergent sequences,

$$\lim_{N\to\infty} S_N = \sum_{i,j=1}^{n} a_i a_j\, e^{c\,x_i x_j} = \sum_{i,j=
1}^{n} a_i a_j\, 2^{x_i x_j}.$$

**Step 4: Conclude.** The sequence $(S_N)$ is non-decreasing with $S_0 = \bigl(\
sum_i a_i\bigr)^2 \geq 0$, so every $S_N \geq 0$. A non-increasing-from-below se
quence of non-negative reals has a non-negative limit:

$$\sum_{i,j=1}^{n} a_i a_j\, 2^{x_i x_j} = \lim_{N\to\infty} S_N \;\geq\; 0.$$

Since $a_1, \dots, a_n \in \mathbb{R}$ and $x_1, \dots, x_n \in \mathbb{N}$ were
 arbitrary, $K(x,y)=2^{xy}$ is positive semidefinite on $\mathbb{N}$.

Moreover, $K$ is strictly positive definite: if $\sum_{i,j} a_i a_j\, 2^{x_i x_j
} = 0$, then $S_N = 0$ for all $N$ (since $S_N \geq 0$ and $S_N \nearrow 0$). In
 particular $S_0 = (\sum_i a_i)^2 = 0$, and more generally $\sum_i a_i x_i^k = 0
$ for all $k \geq 0$. Taking $k = 0, 1, \dots, n-1$ gives a Vandermonde system (
the $x_i$ are distinct natural numbers), forcing $a_i = 0$ for all $i$.

**Therefore $2^{xy}$ is a (strictly) positive definite kernel on $\mathbb{N}$.**
 $\blacksquare$

### PROOF COMPLETE
