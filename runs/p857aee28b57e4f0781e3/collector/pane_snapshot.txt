## Proof

Let $X$ be an infinite set, and let $G = \operatorname{FSym}(X)$ denote the subg
roup of all permutations of $X$ that move only finitely many elements (the *fini
tary symmetric group*). Recall that every element of $G$ has a well-defined sign
 (parity): writing a finitary permutation as a product of transpositions, the pa
rity of the number of transpositions is independent of the factorization, since
this reduces to the sign of a permutation of some finite subset of $X$.

The statement claims that every infinite subset of $G$ must contain an even perm
utation. We exhibit an infinite subset of $G$ consisting entirely of odd permuta
tions.

Fix an element $a \in X$, and consider the set of transpositions
$$
S = \{\, (a\; b) : b \in X \setminus \{a\}\,\}.
$$
Each element of $S$ is a transposition, hence an odd permutation, and each moves
 only two elements, so $S \subseteq G$. Since $X$ is infinite, $X \setminus \{a\
}$ is infinite, so $S$ is infinite.

Thus $S$ is an infinite subset of $G$ containing no even permutation. This direc
tly contradicts the claimed statement.

Therefore the statement is **false**. $\blacksquare$
