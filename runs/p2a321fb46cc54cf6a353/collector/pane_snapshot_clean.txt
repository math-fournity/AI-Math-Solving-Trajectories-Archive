## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. Every unitary irreducible representation of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ of
even dimension has determinant $+1$. In fact, the determinant is $+1$ for *every
* irreducible representation of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, regardless of the parity of it
s dimension.

---

**Step 1 — Parametrizing the representations.** The unitary irreducible represen
tations of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ are indexed by the spin $j \in \{0,\tfrac12,1,\tfrac
32,2,\dots\}$. The representation $\rho_j$ has dimension $2j+1$. Thus *even* dim
ensions correspond to *half-integer* $j$:
$$2j+1 \text{ even} \iff j \in \{\tfrac12,\tfrac32,\tfrac52,\dots\}.$$

**Step 2 — Reducing to the maximal torus.** $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is compact and conn
ected, so every $g\in\mathrm{SU}(2)$ lies in some maximal torus, and all maximal
 tori are conjugate. The standard maximal torus is
$$T = \left\{ h(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix} e^{i\theta}&0\\0&e^{-i\theta}\end{pmatri
x} : \theta\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.$$
Hence every $g\in\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is conjugate to some $h(\theta)\in T$. Since th
e determinant is invariant under conjugation,
$$\det\rho_j(g)=\det\rho_j(h(\theta)),$$
so it suffices to compute $\det\rho_j(h(\theta))$.

**Step 3 — Eigenvalues of $h(\theta)$ in $\rho_j$.** In the spin-$j$ representat
ion, $h(\theta)$ acts diagonally on the standard weight basis $\{|j,m\rangle : m
=-j,-j+1,\dots,j\}$ with eigenvalues
$$\rho_j(h(\theta))\,|j,m\rangle = e^{im\theta}\,|j,m\rangle.$$
(This is the standard weight decomposition: the highest weight is $j$, and the w
eights are $m=-j,-j+1,\dots,j$, each with multiplicity one.)

**Step 4 — Computing the determinant.** Therefore
$$\det\rho_j(h(\theta))=\prod_{m=-j}^{j} e^{im\theta}=e^{i\theta\sum_{m=-j}^{j}m
}.$$
The weights are symmetric about $0$, so
$$\sum_{m=-j}^{j} m = 0,$$
whether $j$ is an integer or a half-integer (the summands pair as $m$ and $-m$,
with $m=0$ contributing $0$ when $j$ is an integer). Consequently
$$\det\rho_j(h(\theta))=e^{i\theta\cdot 0}=1.$$

**Step 5 — Conclusion.** Since $\det\rho_j(g)=\det\rho_j(h(\theta))=1$ for every
 $g\in\mathrm{SU}(2)$, we have
$$\det\rho_j(g)=+1\qquad\text{for all }g\in\mathrm{SU}(2),\text{ for every }j.$$

In particular, taking $j\in\{\tfrac12,\tfrac32,\tfrac52,\dots\}$ (the even-dimen
sional representations), **every unitary irreducible representation of $\mathrm{
SU}(2)$ of even dimension has determinant $+1$.**

---

**Remark (structural viewpoint).** The same conclusion follows abstractly: $\mat
hrm{SU}(2)$ is perfect, i.e. $[\mathrm{SU}(2),\mathrm{SU}(2)]=\mathrm{SU}(2)$, b
ecause its Lie algebra $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ is simple and hence equals its own der
ived algebra. The map $\det\circ\,\rho_j:\mathrm{SU}(2)\to U(1)$ is a continuous
 homomorphism into an abelian group, so it factors through the abelianization $\
mathrm{SU}(2)/[\mathrm{SU}(2),\mathrm{SU}(2)]=\{e\}$, and is therefore trivial.
This confirms $\det\rho_j\equiv 1$ for all $j$ without any case analysis on the
parity of the dimension.
