## Proof

**Setup.** We have 200 cards labeled 5, 200 labeled 2, and 200 labeled 1. Each g
roup must sum to 9. Since $2\cdot 5 = 10 > 9$, every group contains **at most on
e** 5‑card.

Let $g$ = number of groups that contain a 5‑card, and $h$ = number of groups tha
t contain no 5‑card. The total number of groups is $N = g + h$.

**Upper bound.** A group containing a 5 contributes $5$ from its 5‑card, so the
remaining cards in it (all 2s and 1s) sum to $9 - 5 = 4$. A group with no 5 cont
ributes $9$ from 2s and 1s. Let $t$ and $u$ be the total numbers of 2‑cards and
1‑cards used. Then the total contribution of 2‑cards and 1‑cards to the overall
sum is

$$2t + u = 4g + 9h.$$

Since at most 200 cards of each type are available, $t \le 200$ and $u \le 200$,
 hence

$$4g + 9h = 2t + u \le 2\cdot 200 + 200 = 600.$$

Because $h \ge 0$, we have $4h \le 9h$, so

$$4(g+h) = 4g + 4h \le 4g + 9h \le 600,$$

which gives

$$N = g + h \le 150.$$

**Construction achieving 150.** Form:
- $50$ groups of type $\{5, 2, 2\}$ (sum $= 9$),
- $100$ groups of type $\{5, 2, 1, 1\}$ (sum $= 9$).

Card usage:
- 5‑cards: $50 + 100 = 150 \le 200$,
- 2‑cards: $50\cdot 2 + 100\cdot 1 = 200 \le 200$,
- 1‑cards: $50\cdot 0 + 100\cdot 2 = 200 \le 200$.

All constraints are satisfied, and the total number of groups is $50 + 100 = 150
$.

**Conclusion.** The upper bound is $150$ and it is attainable, so the maximum nu
mber of groups is $\boxed{150}$.

### PROOF COMPLETE
