The phrase "functors of opposite variance" refers to a pair of **contravariant**
 functors (each reversing the direction of morphisms, as opposed to the usual co
variant case). The notion of adjunction must be adapted because the standard hom
-set isomorphism for covariant adjunctions does not type-check when both functor
s reverse arrows.

### Definition

Let $\mathcal{L}$ and $\mathcal{K}$ be locally small categories. Let
$$F : \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathcal{K}, \qquad G : \mathcal{K}^{\mathrm
{op}} \to \mathcal{L}$$
be two **contravariant** functors (equivalently written $F:\mathcal{L}\to\mathca
l{K}$ and $G:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{L}$, each of opposite/variance-reversing typ
e). We say that $F$ and $G$ form an **adjunction of opposite variance** (or are
**mutually right adjoint**) if there exists a family of bijections of sets

$$\varphi_{X,Y} : \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{K}}\!\bigl(F(X),\, Y\bigr) \;\xrightarr
ow{\;\sim\;}\; \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{L}}\!\bigl(G(Y),\, X\bigr),$$

indexed by objects $X \in \mathcal{L}$ and $Y \in \mathcal{K}$, that is **natura
l in both variables**.

### Verification of typing (naturality is well-defined)

Both sides are functors $\mathcal{L} \times \mathcal{K} \to \mathbf{Set}$:

- **Left side** $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{K}}(F(X), Y)$: In $X$, $F$ is contravari
ant so $F(X)$ reverses with $X$; $F(X)$ sits in the contravariant (first) slot o
f $\mathrm{Hom}$, so the composite is **covariant** in $X$. In $Y$, $Y$ is in th
e covariant (second) slot, so **covariant** in $Y$.
- **Right side** $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{L}}(G(Y), X)$: In $Y$, $G$ is contravar
iant so $G(Y)$ reverses with $Y$; $G(Y)$ sits in the contravariant (first) slot,
 so the composite is **covariant** in $Y$. In $X$, $X$ is in the covariant (seco
nd) slot, so **covariant** in $X$.

Both sides are covariant in $X$ and covariant in $Y$, hence both are genuine bif
unctors $\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{K}\to\mathbf{Set}$, and naturality of $\varph
i$ is the standard naturality of a natural isomorphism between such bifunctors.
$\checkmark$

### Equivalent formulations

**(1) Mutually left adjoint (dual version).** Dually, $F$ and $G$ are **mutually
 left adjoint** if there is a natural isomorphism
$$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{K}}(Y,\, F(X)) \;\cong\; \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{L}}(X,\
, G(Y)),$$
where both sides are now functors $\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{K}^{\
mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}$ (contravariant in each variable). The "right" and "l
eft" versions are dual to each other; either may be taken as the definition, wit
h the other obtained by passing to opposite categories.

**(2) Unit–counit formulation.** Since $F$ and $G$ are both contravariant, the c
omposites
$$GF : \mathcal{L} \to \mathcal{L} \quad\text{and}\quad FG : \mathcal{K} \to \ma
thcal{K}$$
are **covariant** endofunctors. The adjunction is equivalently specified by natu
ral transformations
$$\eta : \mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{L}} \longrightarrow GF, \qquad \varepsilon : \mat
hrm{Id}_{\mathcal{K}} \longrightarrow FG$$
(the **unit** and **counit**) satisfying the **triangle identities**:
$$G(\varepsilon_{F(X)}) \circ \eta_{G(F(X))} = \mathrm{id}_{G(F(X))} \;\;\text{?
 }$$

More precisely (tracking contravariance carefully): the unit $\eta_X : X \to GF(
X)$ is obtained as $\varphi_{X,F(X)}(\mathrm{id}_{F(X)})$, and the counit $\vare
psilon_Y : Y \to FG(Y)$ is obtained as $\varphi^{-1}_{G(Y),Y}(\mathrm{id}_{G(Y)}
)$. The triangle identities read

$$\xymatrix@C=3em{
G \ar[r]^{\eta\, G} \ar@{=}[dr] & GFG \ar[d]^{G\,\varepsilon} & &
F \ar[r]^{\varepsilon\, F} \ar@{=}[dr] & FGF \ar[d]^{F\,\eta} \\
& G & & & F
}$$

i.e., $G\varepsilon \circ \eta G = \mathrm{id}_G$ and $F\eta \circ \varepsilon F
 = \mathrm{id}_F$, where the juxtapositions respect the contravariance of $F$ an
d $G$ (so, e.g., "$G\varepsilon$" means $G$ applied to the morphism $\varepsilon
$, which reverses its direction).

**(3) Universal property / representability formulation.** For each $X \in \math
cal{L}$, the functor $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{K}}(F(X), -) : \mathcal{K} \to \mat
hbf{Set}$ is representable with representing object $G^{-1}$-side given by $G(-)
 \to X$; concretely, $G$ is defined (up to natural isomorphism) by the requireme
nt that $G(Y)$ represents the functor $Y \mapsto \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{K}}(F(X)
, Y)$, and dually $F(X)$ represents $X \mapsto \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{L}}(G(Y),
X)$.

### Summary

An **adjunction between functors of opposite variance** is a pair of contravaria
nt functors $F:\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{K}$ and $G:\mathcal{K}^{\mat
hrm{op}}\to\mathcal{L}$ together with a natural isomorphism

$$\boxed{\;\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{K}}\!\bigl(F(X),\,Y\bigr) \;\cong\; \mathrm{Ho
m}_{\mathcal{L}}\!\bigl(G(Y),\,X\bigr)\;}$$

natural in $X\in\mathcal{L}$ and $Y\in\mathcal{K}$ (the "mutually right adjoint"
 form), equivalently characterizable by unit and counit natural transformations
$\eta:\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{L}}\to GF$, $\varepsilon:\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{K}}\t
o FG$ satisfying the triangle identities, where $GF$ and $FG$ are the induced co
variant endofunctors.
