## Proof

**Answer: Yes, $E$ must intersect every curve $D \subset X$ with $g(D) \geq 3$.*
*

**Setup.** Let $X$ be a K3 surface and $E \subset X$ a smooth elliptic curve. Si
nce $E$ has genus $1$, the adjunction formula on the K3 surface ($K_X = 0$) give
s
$$E^2 = 2g(E) - 2 = 0.$$
Thus $[E]$ is an **isotropic** vector in the Néron–Severi lattice $\operatorname
{NS}(X)$.

**Hodge index theorem.** For a K3 surface, $h^{2,0}(X) = 1$, so the intersection
 form on $\operatorname{NS}(X)$ has signature $(1, \rho - 1)$, where $\rho = \rh
o(X)$: exactly one positive direction.

**Key lemma.** *Let $\Lambda$ be a real lattice of signature $(1, n)$ with $n \g
eq 1$, and let $L \in \Lambda$ be a nonzero isotropic vector ($L^2 = 0$). Then t
he form on $L^\perp / \mathbb{R}L$ is negative definite. In particular, for ever
y $M \in L^\perp$ with $M \notin \mathbb{R}L$, one has $M^2 < 0$; and for every
$M \in L^\perp$, $M^2 \leq 0$.*

*Proof of lemma.* $L^\perp$ is a hyperplane in $\Lambda$ containing $L$ (since $
L^2 = 0$). Geometrically, $L^\perp$ is the tangent hyperplane to the light cone
at the null line $\mathbb{R}L$. The restriction of the form to $L^\perp$ is nega
tive semi-definite with radical exactly $\mathbb{R}L$: if there were $M \in L^\p
erp$ with $M^2 > 0$, then $\operatorname{span}(L, M)$ would carry a form with ma
trix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & M^2\end{pmatrix}$ having **one positive eigen
value**, and since $\Lambda$ has only one positive eigenvalue in total, the orth
ogonal complement $(\operatorname{span}(L,M))^\perp$ would have to be negative s
emi-definite. But the radical of $\operatorname{span}(L,M)$ is $\mathbb{R}L \not
\subset (\operatorname{span}(L,M))^\perp$ in a way compatible with non-degenerac
y of $\Lambda$—concretely, $L$ pairs trivially with all of $L^\perp \supset \ope
ratorname{span}(L,M)^\perp$, forcing $L$ into the radical of $\Lambda$, contradi
cting non-degeneracy. Hence no such $M$ exists, and $L^\perp/\mathbb{R}L$ is neg
ative definite. $\square$

**Applying the lemma.** Take $\Lambda = \operatorname{NS}(X)_\mathbb{R}$ (signat
ure $(1, \rho-1)$) and $L = [E]$ (isotropic). For any divisor class $[D] \in \op
eratorname{NS}(X)$ with $E \cdot D = 0$, the lemma gives
$$D^2 \leq 0, \quad \text{with equality iff } [D] \in \mathbb{R}[E].$$

**Conclusion.** Let $D \subset X$ be a curve with $g(D) \geq 3$. By adjunction o
n the K3,
$$D^2 = 2g(D) - 2 \geq 4 > 0.$$
If $E \cdot D = 0$, the lemma would force $D^2 \leq 0$, contradicting $D^2 \geq
4$. Therefore $E \cdot D \neq 0$.

Since $E$ and $D$ are distinct irreducible curves (they have different genera: $
g(E) = 1 < 3 \leq g(D)$), their intersection number on the smooth surface $X$ is
 non-negative: $E \cdot D \geq 0$. Combined with $E \cdot D \neq 0$, we obtain
$$E \cdot D > 0.$$

A positive intersection number means $E$ and $D$ have nonempty (in fact positive
-dimensional, since both are curves on a surface and $E \cdot D > 0$ counts inte
rsection points with multiplicity) geometric intersection. Hence **$E$ must inte
rsect $D$**. $\blacksquare$
