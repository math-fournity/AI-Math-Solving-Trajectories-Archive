## Proof

**Analysis of F.** The function $F$ is non-decreasing and right-continuous. We e
xamine its structure:

- For $x < 0$: $F(x) = 0$ (constant).
- At $x = 0$: $F(0) = 3 - e^{0} = 2$, while $F(0^{-}) = \lim_{x \nearrow 0} F(x)
 = 0$. Hence $F$ has a **jump discontinuity** at $x = 0$ of size $F(0) - F(0^{-}
) = 2$.
- For $x > 0$: $F(x) = 3 - e^{-3x}$, which is $C^1$ with $F'(x) = 3e^{-3x}$.
- As $x \to +\infty$: $F(x) \to 3$; as $x \to -\infty$: $F(x) \to 0$. Total mass
 $\mu(\mathbb{R}) = 3$.

**Decomposition of the Lebesgue–Stieltjes measure.** The measure $\mu$ determine
d by $\mu(a,b] = F(b)-F(a)$ splits into two parts corresponding to the continuou
s and jump portions of $F$:

1. **Absolutely continuous part** (from the continuous, differentiable portion o
f $F$ on $(0,\infty)$): On $(0,\infty)$, $F$ is absolutely continuous with deriv
ative $F'(x) = 3e^{-3x}$, so the measure restricted to $(0,\infty)$ has density
$3e^{-3x}$ with respect to $\lambda$. On $(-\infty, 0)$, $F$ is constant, contri
buting no measure. Thus:
$$\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}(A) = \int_A 3e^{-3x}\,\mathbf{1}_{(0,\infty)}(x)\,d\lambda(x
).$$

2. **Singular part** (from the jump at $x=0$): The jump of size $2$ at $x=0$ pro
duces a point mass (Dirac atom) of weight $2$ at $\{0\}$:
$$\mu_{\mathrm{s}} = 2\,\delta_{0}, \qquad \text{i.e., } \mu_{\mathrm{s}}(A) = 2
\,\mathbf{1}_{A}(0).$$

**Verification of the decomposition.**

- $\mu_{\mathrm{ac}} \ll \lambda$: clear, since it is given by a density.
- $\mu_{\mathrm{s}} \perp \lambda$: the measure $\mu_{\mathrm{s}}$ is supported
on $\{0\}$, which has $\lambda$-measure zero.
- Total mass: $\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathbb{R}) = \int_0^\infty 3e^{-3x}\,dx = 1$,
and $\mu_{\mathrm{s}}(\mathbb{R}) = 2$, giving $\mu(\mathbb{R}) = 3 = \lim_{x\to
\infty}F(x) - \lim_{x\to-\infty}F(x)$. ✓
- No singular continuous part exists, since $F$ is absolutely continuous on each
 of $(-\infty,0)$ and $(0,\infty)$, with the only non-ac behavior being the sing
le jump at $0$.

**Conclusion.** The Lebesgue decomposition of $\mu$ relative to $\lambda$ is:

$$\boxed{\mu = \mu_{\mathrm{ac}} + \mu_{\mathrm{s}}, \quad \text{where } \frac{d
\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}}{d\lambda}(x) = 3e^{-3x}\,\mathbf{1}_{(0,\infty)}(x), \quad \m
u_{\mathrm{s}} = 2\,\delta_{0}.}$$
