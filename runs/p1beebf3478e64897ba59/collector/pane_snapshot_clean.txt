Looking at this problem, I need to determine what the existence of bijection $f:
 X \to \{\emptyset\} \cup X \cup X^2$ implies about $X$, and then whether $g: X
\cup X^3 \to X^2 \cup X^4$ must exist.

**Answer: Yes, such a bijection $g$ exists.**

## Proof

**Step 1: $X$ must be infinite.**

Suppose $|X| = n < \infty$. Since $X^2 \subseteq \{\emptyset\} \cup X \cup X^2$,
 we have $n^2 = |X^2| \leq |\{\emptyset\} \cup X \cup X^2| = |X| = n$, so $n^2 \
leq n$, giving $n \leq 1$.

- If $n = 0$: $|\{\emptyset\} \cup \emptyset \cup \emptyset| = 1 \neq 0$. Contra
diction.
- If $n = 1$: Write $X = \{a\}$, so $X^2 = \{(a,a)\}$. Under the Kuratowski defi
nition, $(a,a) = \{\{a\}\} \neq a$ and $(a,a) \neq \emptyset$ (since $\{\{a\}\}$
 is nonempty), and also $a$ could equal $\emptyset$ but then $\{\emptyset\}$ and
 $\{(a,a)\} = \{\{\{\emptyset\}\}\}$ are still distinct singletons. In all cases
 at least two of $\emptyset, a, (a,a)$ are distinct, so $|\{\emptyset\} \cup X \
cup X^2| \geq 2 > 1$. Contradiction.

Therefore $X$ is infinite. In particular, $|X| \geq 2$ and $X \neq \emptyset$.

**Step 2: From $f$, derive $|X^2| = |X|$.**

Since $X^2 \subseteq \{\emptyset\} \cup X \cup X^2$ and $f$ is a bijection:
$$|X^2| \leq |\{\emptyset\} \cup X \cup X^2| = |X|.$$
Conversely, since $X$ is nonempty, pick $x_0 \in X$; the map $x \mapsto (x, x_0)
$ injects $X$ into $X^2$, so $|X| \leq |X^2|$. Hence $|X^2| = |X|$.

**Step 3: Derive $|X^3| = |X|$ and $|X^4| = |X|$.**

Let $\varphi: X^2 \to X$ be a bijection (exists by Step 2). The map $(x, y) \map
sto (x, \varphi(y))$ is a bijection $X \times X^2 \to X \times X$, so $|X^3| = |
X \times X^2| = |X \times X| = |X^2| = |X|$.

Similarly, $|X^4| = |X^2 \times X^2| = |X^2| \cdot |X^2| = |X| \cdot |X| = |X^2|
 = |X|$.

**Step 4: Show $|X| + |X| = |X|$ (without Choice).**

Since $|X| \geq 2$, pick distinct $x_0, x_1 \in X$. The map $X \times \{0,1\} \t
o X \times X$ defined by $(x, 0) \mapsto (x, x_0)$ and $(x, 1) \mapsto (x, x_1)$
 is injective (since $x_0 \neq x_1$). Thus $|X \sqcup X| = |X \times \{0,1\}| \l
eq |X \times X| = |X|$. The reverse inequality is trivial, so $|X| + |X| = |X|$.

**Step 5: Conclude $|X \cup X^3| = |X^2 \cup X^4| = |X|$.**

- $X \subseteq X \cup X^3$ gives $|X \cup X^3| \geq |X|$.
- $|X \cup X^3| \leq |X| + |X^3| = |X| + |X| = |X|$ (Steps 3 and 4).

So $|X \cup X^3| = |X|$.

- $X^2 \subseteq X^2 \cup X^4$ gives $|X^2 \cup X^4| \geq |X^2| = |X|$.
- $|X^2 \cup X^4| \leq |X^2| + |X^4| = |X| + |X| = |X|$ (Steps 2, 3, and 4).

So $|X^2 \cup X^4| = |X|$.

**Conclusion:** Both $X \cup X^3$ and $X^2 \cup X^4$ have cardinality $|X|$, so
there exists a bijection $g: X \cup X^3 \to X^2 \cup X^4$. $\blacksquare$
