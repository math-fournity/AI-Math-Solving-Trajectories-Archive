**Proof.**

Recall that the Poincaré dual of a closed oriented $k$-dimensional submanifold $
Y \subseteq M$ is the cohomology class $\operatorname{PD}(Y) \in H_c^{\,n-k}(M)$
 (where $n = \dim M$) uniquely characterized by

$$
\int_Y \alpha \;=\; \int_M \operatorname{PD}(Y)\wedge \alpha
\qquad\text{for every closed } k\text{-form } \alpha \text{ on } M,
$$

where the right-hand side is well-defined because $\operatorname{PD}(Y)$ has com
pact support. Equivalently, $\operatorname{PD}(Y)$ is the unique class in $H_c^{
\,n-k}(M)$ pairing with every $[\alpha]\in H^k(M)$ to give $\int_Y\alpha$.

We must show $\operatorname{PD}(Y_0)=\operatorname{PD}(Y_1)$, i.e. that for ever
y closed $k$-form $\alpha$ on $M$,

$$
\int_{Y_0}\alpha \;=\; \int_{Y_1}\alpha. \tag{$\ast$}
$$

**Step 1: Pull back to the homotopy and apply Stokes.**

Let $\alpha$ be any closed $k$-form on $M$, and consider the pullback $\omega :=
 F^*\alpha$, a $k$-form on $Y\times[0,1]$. Since $d\alpha=0$,

$$
d\omega \;=\; d(F^*\alpha) \;=\; F^*(d\alpha) \;=\; 0.
$$

Give $Y\times[0,1]$ the product orientation (orientation of $Y$ followed by the
standard orientation $dt$ of $[0,1]$). Its boundary is

$$
\partial\bigl(Y\times[0,1]\bigr) \;=\; \bigl(Y\times\{1\}\bigr)\;-\;\bigl(Y\time
s\{0\}\bigr),
$$

where $Y\times\{1\}$ carries the orientation of $Y$ (outward normal $+\partial_t
$) and $Y\times\{0\}$ carries the opposite orientation (outward normal $-\partia
l_t$). By Stokes' theorem,

$$
0 \;=\; \int_{Y\times[0,1]} d\omega \;=\; \int_{\partial(Y\times[0,1])}\omega
\;=\; \int_{Y\times\{1\}} F^*\alpha \;-\; \int_{Y\times\{0\}} F^*\alpha. \tag{1}
$$

**Step 2: Relate the boundary integrals to integrals over $Y_0$ and $Y_1$.**

The restriction of $F$ to $Y\times\{0\}$ is precisely $F_0:Y\to Y_0$, an orienta
tion-preserving diffeomorphism onto $Y_0\subseteq M$ (where $Y_0$ carries its gi
ven orientation as a submanifold of $M$). Hence, using the change-of-variables f
ormula for orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms,

$$
\int_{Y\times\{0\}} F^*\alpha \;=\; \int_Y F_0^*\,\alpha \;=\; \int_{Y_0}\alpha.
 \tag{2}
$$

(The sign is $+$ because $F_0$ is orientation-preserving and $Y\times\{0\}$, *wi
th the boundary orientation*, is $-Y$; but the minus sign from the boundary orie
ntation is already absorbed into the $-$ in equation (1). Equivalently: the term
 $-\int_{Y\times\{0\}}F^*\alpha$ in (1) equals $-\int_Y F_0^*\alpha = -\int_{Y_0
}\alpha$, since $F_0$ orientation-preserving gives $\int_Y F_0^*\alpha = \int_{Y
_0}\alpha$.)

Likewise, $F_1:Y\to Y_1$ is an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism, and $Y\tim
es\{1\}$ carries the orientation of $Y$, so

$$
\int_{Y\times\{1\}} F^*\alpha \;=\; \int_Y F_1^*\,\alpha \;=\; \int_{Y_1}\alpha.
 \tag{3}
$$

Substituting (2) and (3) into (1):

$$
0 \;=\; \int_{Y_1}\alpha \;-\; \int_{Y_0}\alpha,
$$

which gives $(\ast)$: $\displaystyle\int_{Y_0}\alpha = \int_{Y_1}\alpha$ for eve
ry closed $k$-form $\alpha$.

**Step 3: Conclude equality of Poincaré duals.**

By the defining property of the Poincaré dual, $(\ast)$ says

$$
\int_M \operatorname{PD}(Y_0)\wedge\alpha \;=\; \int_M \operatorname{PD}(Y_1)\we
dge\alpha
\qquad\text{for every }[\alpha]\in H^k(M).
$$

Equivalently, the class $\operatorname{PD}(Y_0)-\operatorname{PD}(Y_1)\in H_c^{\
,n-k}(M)$ pairs to zero with every class in $H^k(M)$ under the cup-product pairi
ng

$$
H_c^{\,n-k}(M)\times H^k(M)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\qquad ([\beta],[\alpha])\m
apsto\int_M\beta\wedge\alpha.
$$

By **Poincaré duality**, this pairing is nondegenerate: $H_c^{\,n-k}(M)\cong \bi
gl(H^k(M)\bigr)^*$. Therefore a class in $H_c^{\,n-k}(M)$ that pairs trivially w
ith every $[\alpha]\in H^k(M)$ must be zero. Hence

$$
\operatorname{PD}(Y_0)-\operatorname{PD}(Y_1)=0\in H_c^{\,n-k}(M),
$$

i.e.

$$
\boxed{\,\operatorname{PD}(Y_0)=\operatorname{PD}(Y_1)\,}.
$$

$\blacksquare$
