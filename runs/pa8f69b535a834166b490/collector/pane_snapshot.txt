Looking at this problem, I need to find all $n > 1$ such that for **any** initia

## Setup

With $p=3$, a move selects $k \in \{0,1,2\}$ (mod 3) and applies $a_i \mapsto a_
i - a_{i+k}$.

**Case $k=0$:** All entries become $a_i - a_i = 0$. Any sequence containing a $k
=0$ move yields all zeros — divisible by any $n$. So only sequences using $k \in
 \{1,2\}$ matter.

The linear transformations are:
$$T_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \q
uad T_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

Both are **circulant matrices**, so they commute and are simultaneously diagonal
izable by the Fourier basis.

## Eigenvalue Analysis

$T_1 = \text{circ}(1,-1,0)$ has eigenvalues $\lambda_0 = 0$, $\lambda_1 = 1-\ome
ga$, $\lambda_2 = 1-\omega^2$ where $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$.

Setting $\alpha = 1-\omega$, $\beta = 1-\omega^2$, key identities:
- $\alpha + \beta = 3$, $\alpha\beta = 3$
- $|\alpha| = |\beta| = \sqrt{3}$, $\arg(\alpha) = -\pi/6$, $\arg(\beta) = \pi/6
$

Since $T_1, T_2$ commute, any product of 10 matrices equals $T_1^a T_2^b$ with $
a+b=10$. Its eigenvalues are:
$$0, \quad \alpha^a\beta^b, \quad \beta^a\alpha^b$$

## Computing Entries

For $T_1^a T_2^b$ (a circulant $\text{circ}(c_0,c_1,c_2)$), with $d = a-b$ and $
s = a+b = 10$:

$$\lambda_1 = \alpha^a\beta^b = 3^{5} e^{-id\pi/6} = 243\, e^{-id\pi/6}$$

The entries are recovered via:
$$c_0 = \tfrac{2}{3}\text{Re}(\lambda_1) = 162\cos(d\pi/6)$$
$$c_1 = 162\cos((d{+}4)\pi/6), \quad c_2 = 162\cos((d{-}4)\pi/6)$$

For $a+b=10$ with $a,b \geq 0$, we have $d \in \{0, \pm 2, \pm 4, \pm 6, \pm 8,
\pm 10\}$. Computing all cases:

| $d$ | $(c_0, c_1, c_2)$ |
|-----|-------------------|
| $0$ | $(162, -81, -81)$ |
| $\pm 2$ | $(\pm 81, \mp 162, \pm 81)$ |
| $\pm 4$ | $(\mp 81, \mp 81, \pm 162)$ |
| $\pm 6$ | $(\mp 162, \pm 81, \pm 81)$ |
| $\pm 8$ | $(\mp 81, \pm 162, \mp 81)$ |
| $\pm 10$ | $(\pm 81, \pm 81, \mp 162)$ |

**All entries across all products are $\pm 81$ or $\pm 162$.** The GCD is $\gcd(
81, 162) = 81$.

## Conclusion

The condition $(10, n) \in S$ holds iff **every** entry of **every** product mat

- If $n \nmid 81$: some product has entry 81 (not divisible by $n$); choosing th

- If $n \mid 81$: all entries ($\pm 81, \pm 162$) are divisible by $n$. ✓
