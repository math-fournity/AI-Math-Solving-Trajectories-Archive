## Proof

The surface $S$ is the unit disk $\mathbb{D}$ equipped with the Riemannian metri
c
$$ds^2 = E\,du^2 + 2F\,du\,dv + G\,dv^2 = \frac{4(du^2+dv^2)}{(1-u^2-v^2)^2}.$$
This is precisely the **Poincaré disk model** of the hyperbolic plane. We show i
t is **complete**.

---

**Key estimate: the boundary is at infinite distance.**

Consider a radial path from the origin to a point at Euclidean radius $r_0 < 1$,
 parametrized by $\gamma(t) = (t, 0)$, $0 \le t \le r_0$. Its length in the metr
ic $ds^2$ is
$$L(\gamma) = \int_0^{r_0} \sqrt{E(\gamma(t))}\,dt = \int_0^{r_0} \frac{2}{1-t^2
}\,dt = \ln\frac{1+r_0}{1-r_0}.$$
As $r_0 \to 1^-$, this tends to $+\infty$. Hence every point on the boundary $\p
artial\mathbb{D}$ is at infinite Riemannian distance from the origin (and by the
 triangle inequality, from any interior point).

---

**Closed metric balls are compact.**

Let $p \in \mathbb{D}$ and $R > 0$. We claim the closed ball $\overline{B}(p, R)
 = \{q \in \mathbb{D} : d(p,q) \le R\}$ is a compact subset of $\mathbb{D}$.

Since $E = G = 4/(1-u^2-v^2)^2 \ge 4$ everywhere on $\mathbb{D}$ (because $1-u^2
-v^2 < 1$), the Riemannian distance satisfies $d(p,q) \ge 2\,|p-q|_{\text{Eucl}}
$, where $|p-q|_{\text{Eucl}}$ is the Euclidean distance. Therefore
$$\overline{B}(p,R) \subseteq \{q \in \mathbb{D} : |p-q|_{\text{Eucl}} \le R/2\}
.$$
The right-hand side is a closed Euclidean ball of finite radius centered at $p \
in \mathbb{D}$. Since $p$ is in the interior of $\mathbb{D}$, for $R$ not too la
rge this is contained in $\mathbb{D}$; for large $R$ we use the radial estimate
above: any point at Euclidean distance approaching $1-|p|$ from $p$ toward the b
oundary is already at Riemannian distance $\to\infty$, so points with $d(p,q)\le
 R$ must satisfy a strictly positive Euclidean distance from $\partial\mathbb{D}
$. Concretely, combining the radial divergence: the set $\overline{B}(p,R)$ is b
ounded away from $\partial\mathbb{D}$, hence is a closed and bounded subset of $
\mathbb{D}$ that is contained in a compact subset $K \Subset \mathbb{D}$ (a smal
ler closed disk $\overline{B}_{\text{Eucl}}(0,\rho)$ with $\rho < 1$).

Being a closed subset of a compact set, $\overline{B}(p,R)$ is compact.

---

**Conclusion via Hopf–Rinow.**

A Riemannian manifold is (geodesically) complete if and only if every closed met
ric ball is compact (equivalently, every Cauchy sequence converges, equivalently
 every geodesic extends for all time). We have shown that every closed metric ba
ll in $(\mathbb{D}, ds^2)$ is compact. Therefore, by the Hopf–Rinow theorem, $S$
 is **complete**.

---

**Remark.** The completeness is not automatic for arbitrary abstract surfaces on
 the disk; it depends crucially on the specific metric. Here the factor $4/(1-u^
2-v^2)^2$ blows up near the boundary at exactly the rate needed to push $\partia
l\mathbb{D}$ to infinite distance. Had we used, e.g., $E = G = 1/(1-u^2-v^2)$ (a
 milder blow-up), the radial integral $\int_0^{r_0} dt/(1-t^2) = \tfrac{1}{2}\ln
\frac{1+r_0}{1-r_0}$ would still diverge, but replacing the metric by a bounded
one (e.g. $E=G=1$) would make the disk incomplete. The given metric is the stand
ard complete hyperbolic metric, isometric to $\mathbb{H}^2$.
