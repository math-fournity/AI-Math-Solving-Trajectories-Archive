Looking at this problem, I need to find $k$ where $k \equiv \sum_{(n,x_n)\in T}
n\lfloor nx_n/p\rfloor \pmod{p}$.

**Setup.** For each $n \in M = \{1,\ldots,p-1\}$, there is a unique $x_n \in M$
with $nx_n \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$ (the modular inverse). Writing $nx_n = pq_n + 1$ w
here $q_n = \lfloor nx_n/p \rfloor$, the sum becomes $S = \sum_{n=1}^{p-1} n\,q_
n$.

**Key symmetry: pairing $n$ with $p-n$.** Since $(p-n)x_{p-n} \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$
, we get $-n\,x_{p-n} \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$, so $x_{p-n} = p - x_n$. Then:
$$q_{p-n} = \frac{(p-n)(p-x_n)-1}{p} = \frac{p^2 - p(n+x_n) + nx_n - 1}{p} = p -
 (n+x_n) + q_n.$$

**Computing the paired contribution modulo $p$:**
$$n\,q_n + (p-n)\,q_{p-n} = n\,q_n + (p-n)\bigl(p - n - x_n + q_n\bigr) = p\,q_n
 + (p-n)(p-n-x_n).$$

Reducing mod $p$ (using $p-n \equiv -n$ and $p-n-x_n \equiv -n-x_n$):
$$n\,q_n + (p-n)\,q_{p-n} \equiv (-n)(-n-x_n) = n(n+x_n) = n^2 + nx_n \equiv n^2
 + 1 \pmod{p}.$$

**Summing over all pairs.** Since $p$ is odd, the pairs $(n,\, p-n)$ for $n = 1,
 \ldots, \tfrac{p-1}{2}$ partition $M$:
$$S \equiv \sum_{n=1}^{(p-1)/2} \bigl(n^2 + 1\bigr) = \sum_{n=1}^{(p-1)/2} n^2 +
 \frac{p-1}{2} \pmod{p}.$$

**Evaluating the square sum.** With $m = \tfrac{p-1}{2}$:
$$\sum_{n=1}^{(p-1)/2} n^2 = \frac{m(m+1)(2m+1)}{6} = \frac{\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot\f
rac{p+1}{2}\cdot p}{6} = \frac{p(p^2-1)}{24}.$$

Since $p \geq 5$ is prime, $\gcd(p,24)=1$. Also $p^2-1 = (p-1)(p+1)$ is divisibl
e by $8$ (product of two consecutive even numbers) and by $3$ (since $p \not\equ
iv 0\pmod{3}$), so $24 \mid (p^2-1)$. Thus $\frac{p(p^2-1)}{24}$ is an integer d
ivisible by $p$, giving:
$$\sum_{n=1}^{(p-1)/2} n^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{p}.$$

**Conclusion:**
$$S \equiv 0 + \frac{p-1}{2} = \frac{p-1}{2} \pmod{p}.$$

**Verification.** For $p=5$: $S = 0+2+3+12 = 17 \equiv 2 = \frac{4}{2}$ ✓. For $
p=7$: $S = 52 \equiv 3 = \frac{6}{2}$ ✓. For $p=11$: $S = 236 \equiv 5 = \frac{1
0}{2}$ ✓. For $p=13$: $S = 331 \equiv 6 = \frac{12}{2}$ ✓.

$$\boxed{k = \dfrac{p-1}{2}}$$
