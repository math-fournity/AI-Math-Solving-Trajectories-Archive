## Proof

**Setup.** Place the 30 stations at the vertices of a regular 30-gon on a circle
. Any 4 distinct stations form a convex cyclic quadrilateral (points on a circle
 are in convex position). Label the 4 chosen stations $P_1, P_2, P_3, P_4$ in cl
ockwise order. Let the clockwise arc-gaps between consecutive chosen stations be
 $a, b, c, d$ (measured in units of one station-spacing), so
$$a+b+c+d = 30, \qquad a,b,c,d \ge 1.$$
Each arc unit subtends $360^\circ/30 = 12^\circ$ at the center.

**Interior angles.** By the inscribed angle theorem, the interior angle at $P_1$
 subtends the arc $P_4 \to P_3 \to P_2$ (the arc opposite $P_1$, of length $b+c$
), so
$$\angle P_1 = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 12^\circ\cdot(b+c) = 6(b+c)^\circ.$$
Cyclically,
$$\angle P_1 = 6(b+c),\quad \angle P_2 = 6(c+d),\quad \angle P_3 = 6(d+a),\quad
\angle P_4 = 6(a+b).$$
(Check: the four sum to $6\cdot 2(a+b+c+d)=360^\circ$, as required.)

The Smooth Turn Requirement (each interior angle $<120^\circ$) becomes
$$a+b \le 19,\quad b+c \le 19,\quad c+d \le 19,\quad d+a \le 19.$$
Since $(a+b)+(c+d)=30$ and each $\le 19$, each is $\ge 11$; likewise $b+c, d+a \
in [11,19]$.

**Counting ordered gap-quadruples.** Let $N$ be the number of ordered quadruples
 $(a,b,c,d)$ of positive integers summing to 30 with all four adjacent pair-sums
 $\le 19$. Fix $a,c$ and count valid $(b,d)$. With $m=\min(a,c)$, $M=\max(a,c)$,
 we have $b+d = 30-a-c$, and $b,d \le 19-M$. The number of valid $(b,d)$ equals
$$\min(29{-}a{-}c,\,19{-}M) - \max(1,\,11{-}m) + 1.$$
A case analysis on $m$ (using $M\ge m$, $M\le 18$, $a+c\le 28$) gives:

l $\times 2$ | row total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1\le m\le 9$ | $m\le M\le 8{+}m$ | $9{+}m{-}M$ | $9$ | $(8{+}7{+}\cdots{+}1)\
times2=72$ | $81$ each, $\times 9$ |

Summing:
$$N = 9\cdot 81 + 81 + 49 + 25 + 9 + 1 = 729+81+49+25+9+1 = 894.$$

**From gap-quadruples to station sets.** Each ordered quadruple $(a,b,c,d)$ toge
ther with a choice of starting station $s\in\{0,\dots,29\}$ determines the set $
\{s,\;s{+}a,\;s{+}a{+}b,\;s{+}a{+}b{+}c\}\pmod{30}$. This gives $N\cdot 30 = 894
\cdot 30 = 26820$ (quadruple, starting-station) pairs.

Each valid set of 4 stations is counted **exactly 4 times** in this enumeration:
 the clockwise gaps from a fixed set are uniquely determined once the starting v
ertex is chosen, and there are exactly 4 choices of starting vertex (the 4 eleme
nts of the set). (The two traversal directions do not double the count, since th
e counterclockwise traversal from a vertex corresponds to the reversed gap-quadr
uple placed clockwise from a *different* vertex — already accounted for among th
e 4 clockwise placements, as the reversed quadruple is itself one of the $N$ cou
nted quadruples.)

Therefore the number of distinct station sets is
$$\frac{N\cdot 30}{4} = \frac{894 \cdot 30}{4} = \frac{26820}{4} = 6705.$$

**Sanity check.** For $n=6$ stations with the same $<120^\circ$ rule, the method
 gives $N=2$ (quadruples $(1,2,1,2)$ and $(2,1,2,1)$) and $\frac{2\cdot 6}{4}=3$
 sets — precisely the three "rectangles" $\{0,1,3,4\},\{1,2,4,5\},\{0,2,3,5\}$,
each with all angles $90^\circ$. This confirms the divisor $4$.

**Answer.** The number of valid sets of 4 stations is $\boxed{6705}$.
