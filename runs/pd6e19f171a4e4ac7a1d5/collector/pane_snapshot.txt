## Solution

We evaluate the power series

$$S(z) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{n^3}.$$

### Step 1. Identify the special function

The **polylogarithm** of order $s$ is defined by the power series

$$\operatorname{Li}_s(z) := \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{n^s}, \qquad |z| < 1,
$$

and extended by analytic continuation to a larger domain. Setting $s = 3$ gives
exactly our series:

$$\operatorname{Li}_3(z) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{n^3}.$$

This order-3 polylogarithm is called the **trilogarithm**. Hence

$$\boxed{S(z) = \operatorname{Li}_3(z).}$$

### Step 2. Verify convergence

For $|z| < 1$, absolute convergence follows from the ratio test:

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} \left|\frac{z^{n+1}/(n+1)^3}{z^n/n^3}\right| = |z|\cdot\lim_
{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^3 = |z| < 1.$$

On the boundary $|z| = 1$, we have $\left|\frac{z^n}{n^3}\right| = \frac{1}{n^3}
$, and $\sum n^{-3}$ converges (it is the convergent $p$-series with $p = 3 > 1$
). By the Weierstrass $M$-test the series converges absolutely and uniformly on
the closed unit disk $|z| \le 1$, so $S$ is continuous there and analytic on $|z
| < 1$.

### Step 3. Relate to the logarithm via differentiation (sanity check)

Differentiating term-by-term (valid inside the disk of convergence):

$$\frac{d}{dz}\operatorname{Li}_3(z) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n-1}}{n^2} =
\frac{1}{z}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^n}{n^2} = \frac{1}{z}\operatorname{Li}_2(z
),$$

where $\operatorname{Li}_2(z) = -\int_0^z \frac{\log(1-t)}{t}\,dt$ is the diloga
rithm. Iterating,

$$\frac{d}{dz}\operatorname{Li}_2(z) = \frac{1}{z}\operatorname{Li}_1(z) = -\fra
c{\log(1-z)}{z},$$

since $\operatorname{Li}_1(z) = \sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{z^n}{n} = -\log(1-z)$. Thus t
he trilogarithm sits in the standard polylogarithm ladder

$$\operatorname{Li}_3(z) = \int_0^z \frac{\operatorname{Li}_2(t)}{t}\,dt = \int_
0^z \frac{1}{t}\int_0^t \frac{-\log(1-u)}{u}\,du\,dt,$$

with the normalization $\operatorname{Li}_3(0) = 0$, matching $S(0) = 0$. This c
onfirms the identification.

### Step 4. Special values (illustrative)

- $S(1) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^3} = \zeta(3)$ (Apéry's constant), so $\
operatorname{Li}_3(1) = \zeta(3)$.
- $S(-1) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n}{n^3} = -\frac{3}{4}\zeta(3)$, i.e. $
\operatorname{Li}_3(-1) = -\tfrac{3}{4}\zeta(3)$.

These agree with the classical values of the trilogarithm, providing further con
firmation.

### Conclusion

The given power series is precisely the order-3 polylogarithm (trilogarithm):

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{z^n}{n^3} = \operatorname{Li}_3(z), \qquad |z| \le 1
,$$

with analytic continuation to $\mathbb{C}\setminus[1,\infty)$ via the integral r
epresentation $\operatorname{Li}_3(z) = \int_0^z \frac{\operatorname{Li}_2(t)}{t
}\,dt$.
