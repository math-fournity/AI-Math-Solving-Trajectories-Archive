Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether there exist natural numbers
 $a, b, c > 10^{10}$ such that $abc$ is divisible by each of $a+2012$, $b+2012$,
 $c+2012$.

**Answer: Yes, such natural numbers exist.**

## Proof

**Reformulation.** The condition $(a+2012) \mid abc$ is equivalent to $(a+2012)
\mid 2012\,bc$, since
$$abc = (a+2012)\,bc - 2012\,bc.$$
Likewise the other two conditions become $(b+2012)\mid 2012\,ac$ and $(c+2012)\m
id 2012\,ab$.

**Construction.** We build $a, b, c$ in a chain. Set:
$$a + 2012 = 2012\,b, \qquad c + 2012 = 2012^2\, b(b-1),$$
and choose $b$ so that $b + 2012$ divides the fixed constant
$$N := 2012^3 \cdot 2013 \cdot (2012^2 \cdot 2013 - 1).$$
Specifically, take $b + 2012 = N$, i.e., $b = N - 2012$.

**Verification of the three divisibility conditions.**

*Condition 1:* $(a+2012)\mid 2012\,bc$. We have $a+2012 = 2012\,b$, and $2012\,b
c = 2012\,b\cdot c$, so $2012\,b \mid 2012\,bc$ holds trivially. ✓

*Condition 3:* $(c+2012)\mid 2012\,ab$. We have $c+2012 = 2012^2\,b(b-1)$, and $
2012\,ab = 2012\cdot 2012(b-1)\cdot b = 2012^2\,b(b-1)$. Thus $c+2012 = 2012\,ab
$ exactly, so divisibility holds. ✓

*Condition 2:* $(b+2012)\mid 2012\,ac$. We compute:
$$2012\,ac = 2012\cdot 2012(b-1)\cdot\bigl(2012^2\,b(b-1)-2012\bigr) = 2012^3\,(
b-1)\bigl(2012\,b(b-1)-1\bigr).$$
Reducing modulo $b+2012$: since $b\equiv -2012$, we get $b-1\equiv -2013$ and $b
(b-1)\equiv (-2012)(-2013)=2012\cdot 2013$, hence
$$2012\,b(b-1)-1 \equiv 2012^2\cdot 2013 - 1 \pmod{b+2012}.$$
Therefore:
$$2012\,ac \equiv 2012^3\cdot(-2013)\cdot(2012^2\cdot 2013 - 1) \pmod{b+2012}.$$
Since $b+2012 = N = 2012^3\cdot 2013\cdot(2012^2\cdot 2013-1)$, we have $N \mid
2012\,ac$, i.e., $(b+2012)\mid 2012\,ac$. ✓

**Size verification.** We compute:
- $2012^2\cdot 2013 - 1 = 4{,}048{,}144 \cdot 2013 - 1 = 8{,}148{,}913{,}871$,
- $2012^3 = 8{,}144{,}896{,}128$,
- $N = 8{,}144{,}896{,}128 \cdot 2013 \cdot 8{,}148{,}913{,}871 \approx 1.34\tim
es 10^{23}$.

Thus $b = N - 2012 > 10^{10}$. Then:
- $a = 2012(b-1) > 2012\cdot 10^{10} > 10^{10}$,
- $c = 2012^2\,b(b-1) - 2012 > 2012^2\cdot 10^{10}\cdot(10^{10}-1) > 10^{10}$.

All three exceed $10^{10}$, and all three divisibility conditions hold. $\blacks
quare$
