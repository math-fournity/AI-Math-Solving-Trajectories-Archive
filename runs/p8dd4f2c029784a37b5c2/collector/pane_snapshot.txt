## Proof

**The upper triangular subgroup of $SL(2, F_q)$.**

The upper triangular subgroup (the standard Borel subgroup $B$) consists of all
matrices in $SL(2, F_q)$ that are upper triangular:

$$B = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & a^{-1} \end{pmatrix} \;\middle|\; a \
in F_q^*,\; b \in F_q \right\}.$$

This is indeed a subgroup: the identity ($a=1, b=0$) belongs, products and inver
ses remain of this form, and the determinant condition $\det = a \cdot a^{-1} =
1$ is automatically satisfied.

**Claim:** All matrices in $B$ share a common eigenvector, namely $e_1 = \begin{
pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$.

**Verification.** For any $M = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & a^{-1} \end{pmatrix}
 \in B$:

$$M \, e_1 = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & a^{-1} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1
 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} a \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = a \begin{pmatrix} 1
 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = a \, e_1.$$

Thus $e_1$ is an eigenvector of $M$ with eigenvalue $a$. Since this holds for **
every** $M \in B$ (for every choice of $a \in F_q^*$ and $b \in F_q$), the vecto
r $e_1$ is a **common eigenvector** for the entire subgroup.

**Uniqueness (up to scaling).** We show $e_1$ is the only common eigenvector up
to scalar multiples. Let $v = (x, y)^T$ be a common eigenvector for all $M \in B
$. For each $M$, there exists $\lambda_M$ with $Mv = \lambda_M v$:

$$\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & a^{-1} \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{p
matrix} = \begin{pmatrix} ax + by \\ a^{-1} y \end{pmatrix} = \lambda_M \begin{p
matrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}.$$

- **If $y \neq 0$:** The second component gives $\lambda_M = a^{-1}$. The first
component then requires $ax + by = a^{-1}x$, i.e., $by = (a^{-1} - a)x$, for **a
ll** $b \in F_q$. Since $y \neq 0$, this forces $b = \frac{(a^{-1}-a)x}{y}$ to b
e a single fixed value—contradicting the requirement that it hold for every $b \
in F_q$ (as $|F_q| \geq 2$). Hence $y \neq 0$ is impossible.

- **If $y = 0$:** Then $v = (x, 0)^T$ is a scalar multiple of $e_1$, and the eig
envalue is $\lambda_M = a$, which is consistent for all $M$.

Therefore the common eigenvectors are exactly the scalar multiples of $e_1$, and
 $e_1$ is the unique common eigenvector up to scaling.

**Conclusion.** Yes, all matrices in the upper triangular subgroup of $SL(2, F_q
)$ share a common eigenvector. The vector $e_1 = (1, 0)^T$ is a common eigenvect
or (with eigenvalue equal to the upper-left entry $a$ for each matrix $\begin{pm
atrix} a & b \\ 0 & a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}$), and it is the unique such vector up t
o scalar multiples. This holds for every finite field $F_q$ regardless of the va
lue of $q$.
