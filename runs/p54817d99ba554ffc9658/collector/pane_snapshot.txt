## Proof

Let $H = \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$, the Hilbert space of square-summable sequences, wi
th inner product $\langle a, b\rangle = \sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n \overline{b_n}$ an
d norm $\|a\| = \left(\sum_{n=1}^\infty |a_n|^2\right)^{1/2}$.

Let $(e_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ be the standard orthonormal basis, where $e_n$ has
a $1$ in position $n$ and $0$ elsewhere. Define
$$x_n := \frac{1}{n}\, e_n, \qquad n \ge 1.$$

**Not absolutely summable.** We have $\|x_n\| = \frac{1}{n}\|e_n\| = \frac{1}{n}
$. Hence
$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \|x_n\| = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n} = +\infty,$$
the divergent harmonic series. So $(x_n)$ is not absolutely summable.

**Summable (convergent in $H$).** Consider the partial sums $S_N = \sum_{n=1}^N
x_n = \sum_{n=1}^N \frac{1}{n} e_n = \left(1, \tfrac{1}{2}, \dots, \tfrac{1}{N},
 0, 0, \dots\right)$. Let $s = \left(\tfrac{1}{n}\right)_{n\ge 1} \in \ell^2$, w
hich is valid since
$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6} < \infty.$$
Then
$$\|S_N - s\|^2 = \sum_{n=N+1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} \xrightarrow{N\to\infty} 0,$
$
since the tail of a convergent series vanishes. Therefore $S_N \to s$ in $\ell^2
$, i.e.,
$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty x_n = s \quad \text{in } H.$$

Thus $(x_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ is summable in $H = \ell^2(\mathbb{N})$ but not ab
solutely summable, as required.
