## Solution

We compute the Cauchy Principal Value

$$P.V.\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{dx}{x(x^2+1)}.$$

**Key observation — oddness.** The integrand

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{x(x^2+1)}$$

satisfies

$$f(-x) = \frac{1}{(-x)\big((-x)^2+1\big)} = \frac{1}{-x(x^2+1)} = -f(x),$$

so $f$ is an **odd function**.

**Definition of the PV.** With a singularity at $x=0$ and the domain $(-\infty,\
infty)$,

$$P.V.\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx = \lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0
^{+}}\left[\int_{-R}^{-\varepsilon} f(x)\,dx + \int_{\varepsilon}^{R} f(x)\,dx\r
ight].$$

**Symmetric cancellation.** For every $R > \varepsilon > 0$, the domain of integ
ration $[-R,-\varepsilon]\cup[\varepsilon,R]$ is symmetric about the origin. Sin
ce $f$ is odd,

$$\int_{-R}^{-\varepsilon} f(x)\,dx = -\int_{\varepsilon}^{R} f(x)\,dx,$$

hence

$$\int_{-R}^{-\varepsilon} f(x)\,dx + \int_{\varepsilon}^{R} f(x)\,dx = 0$$

for **all** $R,\varepsilon$. The two limits are therefore limits of the identica
lly-zero quantity, giving $0$.

**Verification via partial fractions.** Decompose

$$\frac{1}{x(x^2+1)} = \frac{1}{x} - \frac{x}{x^2+1}.$$

Both $\frac{1}{x}$ and $\frac{x}{x^2+1}$ are odd, so each has Cauchy principal v
alue $0$ over $(-\infty,\infty)$ (symmetric cancellation at both the origin and
at infinity). Thus

$$P.V.\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x(x^2+1)} = P.V.\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\f
rac{dx}{x} - P.V.\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x\,dx}{x^2+1} = 0 - 0 = 0.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{P.V.\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x(x^2+1)} = 0}$$
