## Proof

We wish to compute $\operatorname{Var}(X_t)$ for
$$X_t = \int_0^t W_s^+\, dW_s, \qquad W_s^+ = \max(W_s, 0).$$

**Step 1 — Itô isometry.** The integrand $W_s^+ = \max(W_s,0)$ is an adapted, co
ntinuous process, hence square-integrable on $[0,t]$. By the Itô isometry,
$$\operatorname{Var}(X_t) = \mathbb{E}\big[X_t^2\big] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0
^t (W_s^+)^2\, ds\right] = \int_0^t \mathbb{E}\!\left[(W_s^+)^2\right] ds,$$
where the exchange of expectation and integral is justified by Fubini–Tonelli (t
he integrand is non-negative).

**Step 2 — Compute $\mathbb{E}[(W_s^+)^2]$.** Since $W_s \sim \mathcal{N}(0, s)$
, its density is symmetric about $0$. We have
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[(W_s^+)^2\right] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[W_s^2\,\mathbf{1}_{\{W_
s \ge 0\}}\right].$$

By the symmetry of the Gaussian distribution about $0$, the random variables $W_
s^2\,\mathbf{1}_{\{W_s\ge 0\}}$ and $W_s^2\,\mathbf{1}_{\{W_s \le 0\}}$ are iden
tically distributed (the map $x\mapsto -x$ preserves $x^2$ and swaps the two hal
f-lines). Their sum is $W_s^2$, so each equals half:
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[W_s^2\,\mathbf{1}_{\{W_s \ge 0\}}\right] = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\ma
thbb{E}[W_s^2] = \tfrac{1}{2}\,s.$$

**Step 3 — Integrate.** Substituting back,
$$\operatorname{Var}(X_t) = \int_0^t \frac{s}{2}\, ds = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{t^
2}{2} = \frac{t^2}{4}.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(X_t) = \dfrac{t^{2}}{4}}$$
