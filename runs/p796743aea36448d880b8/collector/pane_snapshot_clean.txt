## Proof

Let $g(z) = z\,\dfrac{f'(z)}{f(z)}$. We will show that the average of $\operator
name{Re} g$ over the circle $|z|=R$ equals $N$; the desired inequality then foll
ows immediately, since the maximum of a continuous function is at least its aver
age.

**Step 1: A useful integral identity.**

By the argument principle, since $f$ is holomorphic in a neighborhood of $\overl
ine{D(0,R)}$ and has no zeros on $\partial D(0,R)$,

$$
N = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=R} \frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}\,dz.
$$

Parameterize the circle by $z = Re^{i\theta}$, $\theta\in[0,2\pi)$, so that $dz
= i\,Re^{i\theta}\,d\theta = i\,z\,d\theta$. Substituting,

$$
N = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_0^{2\pi} \frac{f'(Re^{i\theta})}{f(Re^{i\theta})}\cdot
i\,Re^{i\theta}\,d\theta
   = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} Re^{i\theta}\,\frac{f'(Re^{i\theta})}{f(Re^{i\t
heta})}\,d\theta
   = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} g\!\left(Re^{i\theta}\right)\,d\theta.
$$

**Step 2: Take real parts.**

Since $N$ is a non-negative integer (hence real), taking real parts of both side
s gives

$$
N = \operatorname{Re}(N) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} \operatorname{Re}\,g\!\le
ft(Re^{i\theta}\right)\,d\theta.
$$

**Step 3: Average versus maximum.**

The function $\theta \mapsto \operatorname{Re}\,g(Re^{i\theta})$ is continuous o
n $[0,2\pi]$ (since $f$ has no zeros on $|z|=R$, the logarithmic derivative $f'/
f$ is continuous there, and so is $g$). The right-hand side is precisely its ave
rage over the circle. For any continuous real-valued function, the maximum is at
 least the average:

$$
\max_{|z|=R} \operatorname{Re}\,g(z) = \max_{0\le\theta<2\pi} \operatorname{Re}\
,g\!\left(Re^{i\theta}\right) \;\ge\; \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} \operatorname{
Re}\,g\!\left(Re^{i\theta}\right)\,d\theta = N.
$$

Recalling $g(z) = z\,f'(z)/f(z)$, this is exactly

$$
\max_{|z|=R} \operatorname{Re}\!\left(z\,\frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}\right) \geq N. \qqua
d\blacksquare
$$
