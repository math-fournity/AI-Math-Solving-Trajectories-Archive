## Solution

**Setup.** The curve is $\gamma(t) = (x(t), r(t), 0)$ with
$$x(t) = \log\!\bigl(\cot\tfrac{t}{2}\bigr) - \cos t, \qquad r(t) = \sin t, \qqu
ad t\in\bigl(0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}\bigr).$$

**Endpoints.** As $t\to 0^{+}$: $\cot(t/2)\to\infty$, so $x\to+\infty$ and $r\to
 0$. At $t=\pi/2$: $\cot(\pi/4)=1$, so $x = \log 1 - \cos(\pi/2) = 0$ and $r = \
sin(\pi/2)=1$. Thus the curve runs from $(\infty,0)$ to $(0,1)$ in the $(x,r)$-p
lane, meeting the $yz$-plane ($x=0$) at the circle of radius $1$.

Rotating about the $x$-axis, the bounded region lies in $x\in[0,\infty)$, and it
s volume is
$$V = \pi\int_{0}^{\infty} r^{2}\,dx.$$

**Compute $x'(t)$.** Using $\log\cot(t/2) = \log\cos(t/2)-\log\sin(t/2)$:
$$\frac{d}{dt}\log\cot\tfrac{t}{2} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\tan\tfrac{t}{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\
cot\tfrac{t}{2} = -\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{\sin(t/2)\cos(t/2)} = -\frac{1}{\sin
 t} = -\csc t.$$
Hence
$$x'(t) = -\csc t + \sin t = \frac{\sin^{2}t - 1}{\sin t} = -\frac{\cos^{2}t}{\s
in t}.$$

**Convert the volume integral to $t$.** As $t$ increases from $0$ to $\pi/2$, $x
$ decreases from $\infty$ to $0$, so $dx = x'(t)\,dt < 0$. Therefore
$$V = \pi\int_{0}^{\infty} r^{2}\,dx = \pi\int_{\pi/2}^{0} \sin^{2}t\;\frac{dx}{
dt}\,dt = -\pi\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sin^{2}t\cdot x'(t)\,dt.$$
Substituting $x'(t) = -\cos^{2}t/\sin t$:
$$V = -\pi\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sin^{2}t\left(-\frac{\cos^{2}t}{\sin t}\right)dt = \p
i\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sin t\,\cos^{2}t\;dt.$$

**Evaluate.** Let $u=\cos t$, $du=-\sin t\,dt$; $t=0\Rightarrow u=1$, $t=\pi/2\R
ightarrow u=0$:
$$V = \pi\int_{1}^{0}u^{2}(-du) = \pi\int_{0}^{1}u^{2}\,du = \pi\cdot\frac{1}{3}
.$$

$$\boxed{V = \dfrac{\pi}{3}}.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
