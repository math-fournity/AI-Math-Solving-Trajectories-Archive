## Solution

A rank-1 matrix $X = ab^T$ is determined by the pair $(a, b)$ with $a \in \mathb
b{R}^m$ and $b \in \mathbb{R}^n$, giving $m + n$ raw parameters.

**Counting the redundancy.** For any nonzero scalar $c \in \mathbb{R}\setminus\{
0\}$,
$$(ca)\left(\tfrac{1}{c}b\right)^T = ca \cdot \tfrac{1}{c}b^T = ab^T = X.$$
So the representation $(a,b) \mapsto ab^T$ is **not injective**: each nonzero $c
$ gives the same $X$. This is a one-parameter family of equivalent representatio
ns, removing exactly $1$ degree of freedom.

**Verifying the count is exact.** The map $\varphi: \mathbb{R}^m \times \mathbb{
R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$, $\varphi(a,b)=ab^T$, has image equal to the se
t of rank-$\leq 1$ matrices. On the open set where $a\neq 0$ and $b\neq 0$, the
fiber over any rank-1 $X$ is exactly
$$\varphi^{-1}(X) = \{(ca,\, b/c) : c \in \mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\},$$
a 1-dimensional orbit under the $\mathbb{R}^*$-action $c\cdot(a,b) = (ca, b/c)$.
 The action is free (no fixed points when $a,b\neq 0$) and proper on this domain
, so the quotient is a smooth manifold of dimension
$$(m+n) - 1 = m+n-1.$$

**Conclusion.** The number of degrees of freedom of a rank-1 matrix $X = ab^T$ w
ith $a\in\mathbb{R}^m$, $b\in\mathbb{R}^n$ is

$$\boxed{m+n-1}.$$
