## Proof

### Step 1. A universal relation

Set $z=1/(xy)$ (so $xyz=1$). The three arguments become $x+1/y$, $y+xy$, $\frac{
1+y}{xy}$.

**Claim.** For any $a,b>0$ with $ab>1$:
$$f(a)+f(b)+f\!\Big(\tfrac{a+b+2}{ab-1}\Big)=1. \tag{$\star$}$$

*Proof of claim.* Set $x=\frac{ab-1}{b+1}$, $y=\frac{b+1}{a+1}$, $z=\frac{a+1}{a
b-1}$. One checks $x,y,z>0$ (since $ab>1$) and $xyz=1$. Direct computation gives
 $x+1/y=a$, $y+1/z=b$, $z+1/x=\frac{a+b+2}{ab-1}$. $\square$

Denote $h(a,b)=\frac{a+b+2}{ab-1}$.

### Step 2. First consequences

**$f(2)=1/3$:** Set $a=b=2$ in $(\star)$: $3f(2)=1$.

**Relation (C):** Set $a=b$ (with $a>1$) in $(\star)$: $2f(a)+f\!\big(\frac{2}{a
-1}\big)=1$. Substituting $t=\frac{2}{a-1}$ (so $a=1+\frac{2}{t}$):
$$f(t)=1-2f\!\Big(1+\tfrac{2}{t}\Big), \qquad \forall\, t>0. \tag{C}$$

Since $f>0$, this gives $f(t)<1$ for all $t$, hence $0<f(t)<1$.

### Step 3. Reduction to Jensen's equation

Define $g(t)=f(t)-\frac{1}{t+1}$ and $\tilde g(p)=g\!\big(\tfrac{1}{p}-1\big)$ f
or $p\in(0,1)$.

**Key computation.** Note $h(a,b)+1=\frac{(a+1)(b+1)}{ab-1}$, so
$$\frac{1}{a+1}+\frac{1}{b+1}+\frac{1}{h(a,b)+1}=1.$$
Since $f(a)+f(b)+f(h)=1$ as well, subtracting gives $g(a)+g(b)+g(h(a,b))=0$.

Now apply (C) in the form $g(t)=-2g\!\big(1+\frac{2}{t}\big)$ to $g(h)$:
$$g(a)+g(b)=2g\!\Big(1+\tfrac{2}{h(a,b)}\Big). \tag{G}$$

Set $p=\frac{1}{a+1}$, $q=\frac{1}{b+1}$. Then $a=\frac{1}{p}-1$, $b=\frac{1}{q}
-1$, and the condition $ab>1$ becomes $p+q<1$. A direct calculation gives
$$\frac{1}{\big(1+\frac{2}{h(a,b)}\big)+1}=\frac{p+q}{2}.$$

So (G) becomes **Jensen's midpoint equation**:
$$\tilde g(p)+\tilde g(q)=2\,\tilde g\!\Big(\tfrac{p+q}{2}\Big), \qquad p,q\in(0
,1),\; p+q<1. \tag{J}$$

Relation (C) in $\tilde g$-form becomes:
$$\tilde g(p)=-2\,\tilde g\!\Big(\tfrac{1-p}{2}\Big), \qquad p\in(0,1). \tag{D}$
$

### Step 4. Solving via boundedness

From $0<f<1$ we get $|g|<1$, hence $\tilde g$ is **bounded** on $(0,1)$.

For any $p,q\in(0,\tfrac12)$ we have $p+q<1$, so (J) holds with midpoint in $(0,
\tfrac12)$. Thus $\tilde g$ satisfies Jensen's equation on the open interval $(0
,\tfrac12)$ and is bounded there. By the classical theorem (bounded + Jensen $\R
ightarrow$ affine), there exist constants $\alpha,\beta$ with
$$\tilde g(p)=\alpha p+\beta, \qquad p\in(0,\tfrac12).$$

**Extending to $(\tfrac12,1)$ via (D):** For $p\in(\tfrac12,1)$, $\frac{1-p}{2}\
in(0,\tfrac14)\subset(0,\tfrac12)$, so
$$\tilde g(p)=-2\tilde g\!\Big(\tfrac{1-p}{2}\Big)=-2\Big(\alpha\tfrac{1-p}{2}+\
beta\Big)=-\alpha(1-p)-2\beta.$$

**Cross-constraint:** Take $p\in(0,\tfrac12)$, $q\in(\tfrac12,1)$ with $p+q<1$.
Then $\frac{p+q}{2}\in(0,\tfrac12)$, and (J) gives:
$$\underbrace{(\alpha p+\beta)}_{\tilde g(p)}+\underbrace{(-\alpha(1-q)-2\beta)}
_{\tilde g(q)}=2\underbrace{(\alpha\tfrac{p+q}{2}+\beta)}_{\tilde g((p+q)/2)}.$$
Simplifying: $\alpha(p+q)-\alpha-\beta=\alpha(p+q)+2\beta$, hence $\alpha=-3\bet
a$.

Substituting back: $\tilde g(p)=\beta(1-3p)$ on $(0,\tfrac12)$, and on $(\tfrac1
2,1)$: $\tilde g(p)=3\beta(1-p)-2\beta=\beta(1-3p)$. So
$$\tilde g(p)=\beta(1-3p), \qquad p\in(0,1).$$

### Step 5. Form of $f$ and positivity

Since $g(t)=\tilde g\!\big(\frac{1}{t+1}\big)=\beta\!\left(1-\frac{3}{t+1}\right
)=\beta\cdot\frac{t-2}{t+1}$:
$$f(t)=\frac{1}{t+1}+\beta\cdot\frac{t-2}{t+1}=\frac{\beta\, t+(1-2\beta)}{t+1}.
$$

**Positivity $f(t)>0$ for all $t>0$** requires $\beta\, t+(1-2\beta)>0$ for all
$t>0$:
- $\beta<0$: fails as $t\to\infty$.
- $\beta>\tfrac12$: fails as $t\to 0^+$ (numerator $\to 1-2\beta<0$).
- $\beta=0$: $f(t)=\frac{1}{t+1}>0$. ✓
- $\beta=\tfrac12$: $f(t)=\frac{t}{2(t+1)}>0$ for $t>0$. ✓
- $0<\beta<\tfrac12$: numerator $\geq\min(1-2\beta,\,\beta t)>0$. ✓

So $\beta\in\big[0,\tfrac12\big]$.

### Step 6. Verification

For any $\beta\in[0,\tfrac12]$, set $f(t)=\frac{\beta t+(1-2\beta)}{t+1}$. Param
etrize $x=\frac{a}{b},\,y=\frac{b}{c},\,z=\frac{c}{a}$ (so $xyz=1$); the three a
rguments are $\frac{a+c}{b},\,\frac{a+b}{c},\,\frac{b+c}{a}$. Then
$$f\!\Big(\tfrac{a+c}{b}\Big)=\frac{\beta(a+c)+(1-2\beta)b}{a+b+c},$$
and cyclically. Summing all three:
$$\frac{\beta\cdot 2(a+b+c)+(1-2\beta)(a+b+c)}{a+b+c}=\frac{(2\beta+1-2\beta)(a+
b+c)}{a+b+c}=1. \;\checkmark$$

---

## Conclusion

All solutions are
$$\boxed{f(t)=\frac{\beta\, t+(1-2\beta)}{t+1}, \qquad \beta\in\Big[0,\tfrac12\B
ig].}$$

Equivalently, $f(t)=\frac{1}{t+1}+\beta\cdot\frac{t-2}{t+1}$ for $0\le\beta\le\f
rac{1}{2}$. The two extreme cases are $f(t)=\frac{1}{t+1}$ (at $\beta=0$) and $f
(t)=\frac{t}{2(t+1)}$ (at $\beta=\frac12$).
