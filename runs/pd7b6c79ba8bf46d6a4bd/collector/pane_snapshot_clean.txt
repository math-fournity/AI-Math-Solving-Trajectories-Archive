## Proof

Let $L(z)=\log\frac{1}{1-z}=-\log(1-z)$, so that
$$\frac{d}{dz}L(z)=\frac{1}{1-z}=(1-z)^{-1}.$$

We work in a $\Delta$-domain at the dominant singularity $z=1$. The hypothesis i
s that $g$ is $\Delta$-analytic and
$$g(z)=O\!\left((1-z)^{A}\,L(z)^{k}\right),\qquad z\to 1,\ z\in\Delta.$$

### 1. A differentiation lemma for the scale $(1-z)^{\alpha}L(z)^{j}$

**Lemma.** For $\alpha\in\mathbb{C}$, $j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$, and $r\in\mathbb
{Z}_{\ge 0}$,
$$\frac{d^{r}}{dz^{r}}\Big[(1-z)^{\alpha}L(z)^{j}\Big]=(1-z)^{\alpha-r}\,P_{r,j}
(L(z)),$$
where $P_{r,j}$ is a polynomial in $L$ of degree $\le j$ whose leading coefficie
nt (the coefficient of $L^{j}$) is
$$(-1)^{r}\,(\alpha)_{\downarrow r}:=(-1)^{r}\,\alpha(\alpha-1)\cdots(\alpha-r+1
),\qquad (\alpha)_{\downarrow 0}:=1.$$

*Proof by induction on $r$.* The case $r=0$ is trivial with $P_{0,j}(u)=u^{j}$.
Assume the claim at order $r$. Using $\frac{d}{dz}(1-z)^{\beta}=-(\beta)(1-z)^{\
beta-1}$ and $\frac{d}{dz}L(z)=(1-z)^{-1}$,
$$\frac{d}{dz}\Big[(1-z)^{\alpha-r}P_{r,j}(L)\Big]=(1-z)^{\alpha-r-1}\Big[-(\alp
ha-r)P_{r,j}(L)+P'_{r,j}(L)\Big].$$
The bracketed expression is a polynomial in $L$ of degree $\le j$. Its leading c
oefficient is $-(\alpha-r)$ times the leading coefficient of $P_{r,j}$, i.e.
$$-(\alpha-r)\,(-1)^{r}(\alpha)_{\downarrow r}=(-1)^{r+1}(\alpha)_{\downarrow(r+
1)},$$
since $(\alpha)_{\downarrow(r+1)}=(\alpha)_{\downarrow r}\,(\alpha-r)$. (The der
ivative term $P'_{r,j}$ only contributes to degrees $\le j-1$.) This proves the
induction. $\square$

In particular, the degree in $L$ never increases under differentiation, and the
dominant (highest-degree-in-$L$) part transforms by the simple multiplicative fa
ctor $(-1)^{r}(\alpha)_{\downarrow r}$ together with the shift $(1-z)^{\alpha}\m
apsto(1-z)^{\alpha-r}$.

### 2. Singular expansion of $g$ and termwise differentiation

Because $g$ is $\Delta$-analytic and $g(z)=O((1-z)^{A}L(z)^{k})$, $g$ admits in
the $\Delta$-domain a (finite) singular expansion of the form
$$g(z)\sim\sum_{(\alpha,j)\in S}c_{\alpha,j}\,(1-z)^{\alpha}L(z)^{j},\qquad z\to
 1,\ z\in\Delta,$$
where $S$ is a finite set of pairs with $\operatorname{Re}\alpha\ge A$ and $0\le
 j\le k$, and the remainder is of higher order than every displayed term. (Terms
 with $\operatorname{Re}\alpha>A$, or with $\operatorname{Re}\alpha=A$ and $j<k$
, are lower-order; the displayed sum captures all terms up to the prescribed acc
uracy $(1-z)^{A}L(z)^{k}$.)

By the Lemma, differentiating each summand $r$ times is legitimate inside the $\
Delta$-domain (the function is analytic there, $z=1$ being on the boundary), and
 yields
$$\frac{d^{r}}{dz^{r}}\Big[c_{\alpha,j}(1-z)^{\alpha}L(z)^{j}\Big]=c_{\alpha,j}\
,(1-z)^{\alpha-r}\,P_{r,j}(L(z)),$$
with $P_{r,j}$ of degree $\le j$ and leading coefficient $(-1)^{r}(\alpha)_{\dow
narrow r}$. Summing over $(\alpha,j)\in S$ gives the singular expansion of $g^{(
r)}$:

$$\boxed{\;\frac{d^{r}}{dz^{r}}g(z)\sim\sum_{(\alpha,j)\in S}c_{\alpha,j}\,(1-z)
^{\alpha-r}\left[(-1)^{r}(\alpha)_{\downarrow r}\,L(z)^{j}+\sum_{m=0}^{j-1}b_{\a
lpha,j,r}^{(m)}\,L(z)^{m}\right]\;}$$

for suitable coefficients $b_{\alpha,j,r}^{(m)}$ (determined recursively by the
Lemma). The leading-in-$L$ part of each term is therefore
$$c_{\alpha,j}\,(-1)^{r}(\alpha)_{\downarrow r}\,(1-z)^{\alpha-r}L(z)^{j}.$$

### 3. The resulting scale / big-$O$ bound

Every term in the expansion of $g^{(r)}$ is of the form $(1-z)^{\alpha-r}L(z)^{m
}$ with $\operatorname{Re}\alpha\ge A$ and $m\le j\le k$. Hence the slowest-deca
ying term is bounded by $(1-z)^{A-r}L(z)^{k}$. The remainder, being the $r$-fold
 derivative of a remainder that was $o\!\left((1-z)^{A}L(z)^{k}\right)$, is like
wise absorbed. Therefore

$$\boxed{\;\frac{d^{r}}{dz^{r}}g(z)=O\!\left((1-z)^{A-r}\,L(z)^{k}\right),\qquad
 z\to 1,\ z\in\Delta.\;}$$

Moreover, $g^{(r)}$ remains $\Delta$-analytic (differentiation preserves analyti
city in the $\Delta$-domain), so the singular expansion above is valid in the sa
me $\Delta$-domain.

### 4. Summary

The singular expansion of the $r$-th derivative is obtained from that of $g$ by
the **singular differentiation rule**:

* shift every exponent: $(1-z)^{\alpha}\longrightarrow(1-z)^{\alpha-r}$;
* multiply the coefficient of the top power $L(z)^{j}$ in each summand by $(-1)^
{r}(\alpha)_{\downarrow r}$;
* the lower powers of $L$ in each summand are adjusted by the recursion $P_{r+1,
j}=-(\alpha-r)P_{r,j}+P'_{r,j}$.

In particular, the scale degrades by exactly $r$ units in the algebraic factor a
nd **not at all** in the logarithmic factor:
$$g(z)=O\!\left((1-z)^{A}L(z)^{k}\right)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad g^{(r)}(z)=O\!
\left((1-z)^{A-r}L(z)^{k}\right).$$
