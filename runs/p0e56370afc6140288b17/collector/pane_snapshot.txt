The statement as written has "$n \to 0$", but the meaningful (and true) result i
s the standard tail estimate with $n \to \infty$. With $n \to 0^+$ the claim is
generally false (e.g. $f(x)=1/(1+|x|)^{2/p}$ on $\mathbb{R}$ with Lebesgue measu
re lies in $L^p$ yet $\mu(\{|f|>n\})\to\infty$ as $n\to 0^+$ while $n^p\to 0$, g
iving an indeterminate form that need not vanish). I therefore prove the correct
 version:

**Claim.** Let $f\in L^p(X,\mu)$ with $1\le p<\infty$. Then
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} n^{p}\,\mu\bigl(\{x\in X:|f(x)|>n\}\bigr)=0.$$

**Proof.**

Since $f\in L^{p}(\mu)$, we have $|f|^{p}\in L^{1}(\mu)$, i.e.
$$\int_{X}|f|^{p}\,d\mu<\infty.$$

For each $n>0$ set $E_{n}:=\{x\in X:|f(x)|>n\}$. On $E_{n}$ we have $|f(x)|^{p}>
n^{p}$, hence
$$n^{p}\,\mu(E_{n})=\int_{E_{n}}n^{p}\,d\mu\le\int_{E_{n}}|f|^{p}\,d\mu\le\int_{
X}|f|^{p}\,d\mu<\infty,\tag{1}$$
so the quantity $n^{p}\mu(E_{n})$ is finite for every $n$.

It remains to show it tends to $0$. Consider the family of functions
$$g_{n}:=|f|^{p}\,\mathbf{1}_{E_{n}}=|f|^{p}\,\mathbf{1}_{\{|f|>n\}}.$$

For every $x\in X$:
- If $f(x)=0$, then $g_{n}(x)=0$ for all $n$.
- If $f(x)\neq 0$, then $|f(x)|$ is a fixed positive number, so for all $n>|f(x)
|$ we have $x\notin E_{n}$, i.e. $g_{n}(x)=0$.

Thus $g_{n}(x)\to 0$ pointwise on $X$ as $n\to\infty$. Moreover,
$$0\le g_{n}(x)\le |f(x)|^{p}\in L^{1}(\mu)\quad\text{for all }n.$$

By the Dominated Convergence Theorem,
$$\int_{X}g_{n}\,d\mu=\int_{E_{n}}|f|^{p}\,d\mu\;\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\;0.\ta
g{2}$$

Combining the lower bound from (1) with (2),
$$0\le n^{p}\,\mu(E_{n})\le\int_{E_{n}}|f|^{p}\,d\mu\;\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\;
0.$$

By the squeeze theorem,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{p}\,\mu\bigl(\{x\in X:|f(x)|>n\}\bigr)=0.$$

$\blacksquare$
