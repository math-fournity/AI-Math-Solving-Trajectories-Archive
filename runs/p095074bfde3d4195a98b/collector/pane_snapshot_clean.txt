## Proof

**Setup and interpretation.** For a topology $\tau$ on $\mathbb{R}$, saying "$f$
 is continuous in $\tau$" means $f:(\mathbb{R},\tau)\to(\mathbb{R},\tau)$ is con
tinuous, i.e. $f^{-1}(U)\in\tau$ for every $U\in\tau$. Let
- $\tau_{\mathrm{up}}$ = upper topology, generated by basic opens $(a,b]$;
- $\tau_{\mathrm{low}}$ = lower topology, generated by basic opens $[a,b)$;
- $\tau_{\mathrm{us}}$ = usual topology, generated by basic opens $(a,b)$.

We assume $f$ is continuous in both $\tau_{\mathrm{up}}$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{low}
}$, and prove $f$ is continuous in $\tau_{\mathrm{us}}$.

---

**Lemma.** A set $U\subseteq\mathbb{R}$ is usual-open iff it is both upper-open
and lower-open. Equivalently, $\tau_{\mathrm{us}}=\tau_{\mathrm{up}}\cap\tau_{\m
athrm{low}}$ (as collections of sets).

*Proof of Lemma.* ($\Rightarrow$) Every basic usual-open $(a,b)$ is upper-open,
since $(a,b)=\bigcup_{n}(a,\,b-\tfrac1n]$ (for $n$ large enough that $b-\tfrac1n
>a$), a union of upper-basic opens. Likewise $(a,b)=\bigcup_{n}[a+\tfrac1n,\,b)$
 is lower-open. Hence every usual-open set is both upper- and lower-open.

($\Leftarrow$) Let $U$ be both upper-open and lower-open, and let $x\in U$.
- Upper-openness gives a basic $(c_0,d_0]$ with $x\in(c_0,d_0]\subseteq U$; set
$a=c_0$. Then $a<x$ and $(a,x]\subseteq(c_0,d_0]\subseteq U$.
- Lower-openness gives a basic $[e_0,f_0)$ with $x\in[e_0,f_0)\subseteq U$; set
$d=f_0$. Then $x<d$ and $[x,d)\subseteq[e_0,f_0)\subseteq U$.

Combining, $(a,d)=(a,x]\cup[x,d)\subseteq U$, and $x\in(a,d)$. Thus every $x\in
U$ has an open interval around it contained in $U$, so $U\in\tau_{\mathrm{us}}$.
 $\square$

---

**Two rays belong to both topologies.** We record:
- $(a,\infty)\in\tau_{\mathrm{low}}$, since $(a,\infty)=\bigcup_{n\ge1}[a+\tfrac
1n,\infty)$ and each ray $[e,\infty)=\bigcup_{f\to\infty}[e,f)$ is lower-open.
- $(-\infty,b)\in\tau_{\mathrm{up}}$, since $(-\infty,b)=\bigcup_{n}(-n,\,b-\tfr
ac1n]$ (for $n$ large) and each ray $(-\infty,d]=\bigcup_{c\to-\infty}(c,d]$ is
upper-open.

Of course $(a,\infty)\in\tau_{\mathrm{up}}$ and $(-\infty,b)\in\tau_{\mathrm{low
}}$ are immediate (they are unions of basic opens of the respective topologies).
 So **both rays $(a,\infty)$ and $(-\infty,b)$ lie in $\tau_{\mathrm{up}}\cap\ta
u_{\mathrm{low}}$**, hence in $\tau_{\mathrm{us}}$ by the Lemma — as expected, t
hey are the usual subbasic opens.

---

**Main argument.** The rays $\{(a,\infty):a\in\mathbb{R}\}\cup\{(-\infty,b):b\in
\mathbb{R}\}$ form a subbasis for $\tau_{\mathrm{us}}$, so it suffices to show $
f^{-1}((a,\infty))$ and $f^{-1}((-\infty,b))$ are usual-open for all $a,b$.

Fix $a\in\mathbb{R}$. Since $(a,\infty)\in\tau_{\mathrm{up}}$ and $f$ is upper-c
ontinuous, $f^{-1}((a,\infty))\in\tau_{\mathrm{up}}$. Since $(a,\infty)\in\tau_{
\mathrm{low}}$ (shown above) and $f$ is lower-continuous, $f^{-1}((a,\infty))\in
\tau_{\mathrm{low}}$. By the Lemma, $f^{-1}((a,\infty))\in\tau_{\mathrm{us}}$.

Fix $b\in\mathbb{R}$. Since $(-\infty,b)\in\tau_{\mathrm{low}}$ and $f$ is lower
-continuous, $f^{-1}((-\infty,b))\in\tau_{\mathrm{low}}$. Since $(-\infty,b)\in\
tau_{\mathrm{up}}$ (shown above) and $f$ is upper-continuous, $f^{-1}((-\infty,b
))\in\tau_{\mathrm{up}}$. By the Lemma, $f^{-1}((-\infty,b))\in\tau_{\mathrm{us}
}$.

Thus the preimage of every usual subbasic open is usual-open, hence the preimage
 of every usual-open set (a union of finite intersections of subbasic opens) is
usual-open. Therefore $f:(\mathbb{R},\tau_{\mathrm{us}})\to(\mathbb{R},\tau_{\ma
thrm{us}})$ is continuous. $\square$
