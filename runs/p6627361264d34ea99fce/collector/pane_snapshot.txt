## Solution

### Setup

The unperturbed problem (ε = 0) is the standard Sturm–Liouville problem

$$\phi'' + \lambda\,\phi = 0, \qquad \phi(0)=\phi(L)=0,$$

with eigenvalues and (L²-normalized) eigenfunctions

$$\lambda_n^{(0)} = \left(\frac{n\pi}{L}\right)^2, \qquad \varphi_n(x) = \sin\!\
left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right).$$

We perturb the **first** eigenvalue $\lambda_1^{(0)} = (\pi/L)^2$.

Because the perturbation is **nonlinear**, the amplitude of the eigenfunction is
 physically meaningful and cannot be gauged away. The problem fixes it via the n
ormalization

$$\int_0^L \phi^2\,dx = a^2. \tag{N}$$

### Perturbation expansion

Write

$$\phi = \phi_0 + \varepsilon\,\phi_1 + \varepsilon^2\phi_2 + \cdots, \qquad \la
mbda = \lambda_0 + \varepsilon\,\lambda_1^{(c)} + \cdots,$$

where $\lambda_0 = (\pi/L)^2$ and the superscript $(c)$ distinguishes the **corr
ection** from the eigenvalue index. Each $\phi_k$ satisfies the homogeneous boun
dary conditions $\phi_k(0)=\phi_k(L)=0$.

**Leading order** ($\varepsilon^0$):

$$\phi_0'' + \lambda_0\,\phi_0 = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \phi_0(x) = A\sin\!\left(
\frac{\pi x}{L}\right).$$

The normalization (N) at leading order gives

$$\int_0^L A^2\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi x}{L}\right)dx = A^2\frac{L}{2} = a^2 \;\L
ongrightarrow\; A = a\sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}. \tag{A}$$

**First order** ($\varepsilon^1$):

$$\phi_1'' + \lambda_0\,\phi_1 = -\lambda_1^{(c)}\,\phi_0 + \phi_0^3. \tag{O(ε)}
$$

### Solvability (Fredholm) condition

The operator $\mathcal{L} = d^2/dx^2 + \lambda_0$ with Dirichlet conditions has
a one-dimensional kernel spanned by $\sin(\pi x/L)$. For (O(ε)) to have a soluti
on, the RHS must be orthogonal to this kernel:

$$\int_0^L \bigl(-\lambda_1^{(c)}\,\phi_0 + \phi_0^3\bigr)\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi
x}{L}\right)dx = 0.$$

Substituting $\phi_0 = A\sin(\pi x/L)$:

$$-\lambda_1^{(c)}\,A\int_0^L \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi x}{L}\right)dx + A^3\int_0
^L \sin^4\!\left(\frac{\pi x}{L}\right)dx = 0.$$

The needed integrals (set $u=\pi x/L$):

$$\int_0^L \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi x}{L}\right)dx = \frac{L}{2}, \qquad \int_0^L
 \sin^4\!\left(\frac{\pi x}{L}\right)dx = \frac{3L}{8}.$$

Therefore

$$-\lambda_1^{(c)}\,A\cdot\frac{L}{2} + A^3\cdot\frac{3L}{8} = 0 \;\Longrightarr
ow\; \lambda_1^{(c)} = \frac{3}{4}\,A^2.$$

Using (A), $A^2 = 2a^2/L$, so

$$\boxed{\;\lambda_1^{(c)} = \frac{3\,a^2}{2L}\;}$$

and the perturbed first eigenvalue is

$$\boxed{\;\lambda_1 = \left(\frac{\pi}{L}\right)^2 + \varepsilon\,\frac{3\,a^2}
{2L} + O(\varepsilon^2).\;}$$

### Sketch of $a$ as a function of $\lambda$

Inverting the leading-order relation $\lambda - (\pi/L)^2 \approx \varepsilon\,\
dfrac{3a^2}{2L}$ gives

$$a^2 \approx \frac{2L}{3\,\varepsilon}\left(\lambda - \left(\frac{\pi}{L}\right
)^2\right), \qquad a \approx \sqrt{\frac{2L}{3\varepsilon}}\;\sqrt{\lambda - \le
ft(\frac{\pi}{L}\right)^2}.$$

This is a **sideways parabola** in the $(\lambda, a)$-plane with vertex at $\big
l((\pi/L)^2,\,0\bigr)$:

```
        a
        ^
        |        .  (ε > 0: parabola opens to the right)
        |      .
        |    .
        |  .
        |.___________> λ
       (π/L)²
```

- **For $\varepsilon > 0$ (hardening nonlinearity):** the branch exists for $\la
mbda \ge (\pi/L)^2$; $a$ grows like $\sqrt{\lambda - (\pi/L)^2}$ from the vertex
. The eigenvalue **increases** with amplitude.
- **For $\varepsilon < 0$ (softening nonlinearity):** the branch exists for $\la
mbda \le (\pi/L)^2$; the parabola opens to the **left**, and $a$ grows as $\lamb
da$ decreases below $(\pi/L)^2$.

In both cases $a=0$ recovers the linear eigenvalue $\lambda = (\pi/L)^2$, and th
e departure from this point is the purely nonlinear, amplitude-dependent shift —
 the hallmark of a nonlinear eigenvalue problem. The curve is symmetric in $a$ (
only $a^2$ appears), so both $+a$ and $-a$ branches are present, forming a compl
ete parabola.
