Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum number of stations that can
remain on a 10×10 grid with no axis-aligned rectangle, then subtract from 100.

## Proof

**Setup.** Place the grid as a 10×10 binary matrix where 1 = station remains. A
rectangle with sides parallel to the grid corresponds to a 2×2 all-ones submatri
x: two rows and two columns with all four entries equal to 1. We seek the maximu
m number of 1s with no 2×2 all-ones submatrix (the Zarankiewicz number $z(10,10;
2,2)$), then subtract from 100.

---

### Upper Bound: at most 34 stations can remain

Let $r_i$ be the number of 1s in row $i$, $i=1,\dots,10$. A rectangle occurs whe
n two rows share two common columns. So any pair of columns can co-appear in **a
t most one** row. The total number of column-pairs appearing within rows is:

$$\sum_{i=1}^{10} \binom{r_i}{2} \leq \binom{10}{2} = 45.$$

We maximize $\sum r_i$ subject to this constraint. The marginal cost of increasi
ng a row from size $r$ to $r+1$ is $\binom{r+1}{2}-\binom{r}{2}=r$. To maximize
the total with budget 45, fill the cheapest marginal costs first:

- Bring all 10 rows from 0→1: cost 0, total 10.
- Bring all 10 rows from 1→2: cost 10, total 20, budget used 10.
- Bring all 10 rows from 2→3: cost 20, total 30, budget used 30.
- Bring 5 rows from 3→4: cost 15, total 35, budget used 45.

So the **maximum possible** $\sum r_i = 35$, achieved **uniquely** by 5 rows of
size 4 and 5 rows of size 3, using all 45 column-pairs exactly once. Any other d
istribution with $\sum r_i = 35$ exceeds budget 45 (by convexity of $\binom{r}{2
}$).

**This unique distribution is impossible.** It requires a pairwise balanced desi
gn $\text{PBD}(10,\{3,4\})$ with exactly 5 blocks of size 4 and 5 blocks of size
 3, where every pair of the 10 points appears in exactly one block.

For each point $p$, let $a$ = number of size-4 blocks containing $p$, $b$ = numb
er of size-3 blocks containing $p$. Since $p$ pairs with 9 others, each pair cov
ered once: $3a + 2b = 9$. The only non-negative integer solutions are $(a,b)=(3,
0)$ and $(a,b)=(1,3)$.

Let $x$ = number of points with $(a,b)=(3,0)$, $y$ = number with $(a,b)=(1,3)$.
Then $x+y=10$ and $3x+y=20$ (total size-4 block incidences $=5\cdot4$), giving $
x=5,\, y=5$.

The 5 points of type $(3,0)$ appear in **zero** size-3 blocks. So all 5 size-3 b
locks are drawn entirely from the 5 type-$(1,3)$ points. These 5 blocks cover $5
\cdot\binom{3}{2}=15$ pair-appearances, but only $\binom{5}{2}=10$ distinct pair
s exist among 5 points. Since each pair appears at most once, $15\leq 10$—a **co
ntradiction**.

Therefore 35 is unattainable, so the maximum is at most **34**.
