## Setup

Triangle ABC is isosceles with AB = BC. Point P satisfies ∠ABP = 80°, ∠CBP = 20°
, and AC = BP.

**Key observation:** The position of ray BP relative to the angle at B determine
s ∠ABC. There are three possible regions for P:

1. **P between rays BA and BC:** ∠ABP + ∠CBP = ∠ABC → ∠ABC = 100°
2. **P beyond ray BC (opposite side from A):** ∠ABP = ∠ABC + ∠CBP → ∠ABC = 60°
3. **P beyond ray BA (opposite side from C):** ∠CBP = ∠ABC + ∠ABP → ∠ABC = −60°
(impossible)

So two valid cases exist.

## Case 1: ∠ABC = 100°

With AB = BC = 1, by the Law of Cosines:
$$AC = \sqrt{2 - 2\cos 100°} = 2\sin 50°$$

So BP = 2sin50°. In triangle BCP: BC = 1, BP = 2sin50°, ∠CBP = 20°.

$$CP^2 = 1 + 4\sin^2 50° - 4\sin 50°\cos 20°$$
