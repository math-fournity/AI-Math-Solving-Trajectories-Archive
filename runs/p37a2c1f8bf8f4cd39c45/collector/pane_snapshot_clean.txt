## Solution

Let $p(x) = x^{2n} + a_{2n-1}x^{2n-1} + \cdots + a_1 x + a_0$ be monic of degree
 $2n$, with $n$ even. We are given
$$p(1/k) = k^2 \quad\text{for } k \in \{-n,\ldots,-1,1,\ldots,n\},$$
i.e. for the $2n$ nonzero integers with $1 \le |k| \le n$.

**Define the auxiliary polynomial.** Set
$$f(x) \;=\; x^{2n}\,p(1/x) \;-\; x^{2n+2}.$$
The equation $p(1/x)=x^2$ (for $x\ne 0$) is equivalent to $f(x)=0$.

Expanding,
$$x^{2n}p(1/x) \;=\; \sum_{i=0}^{2n} a_i\, x^{2n-i} \;=\; 1 + a_{2n-1}\,x + a_{2
n-2}\,x^2 + \cdots + a_0\, x^{2n},$$
so
$$f(x) \;=\; 1 + a_{2n-1}x + \cdots + a_0 x^{2n} \;-\; x^{2n+2}.$$

**Key structural facts about $f$:**
- $\deg f = 2n+2$, with leading coefficient $-1$ (from the $-x^{2n+2}$ term).
- The coefficient of $x^{2n+1}$ in $f$ is $0$.
- The constant term is $f(0)=1$.

**Known roots.** For each $k$ with $1\le|k|\le n$, substituting $x=k$:
$$f(k) = k^{2n}\,p(1/k) - k^{2n+2} = k^{2n}\cdot k^2 - k^{2n+2} = 0.$$
So $f$ vanishes at $x = \pm 1, \pm 2, \ldots, \pm n$ — that is $2n$ distinct rea
l roots.

**Factoring.** Since these $2n$ roots are exactly $\pm k$ for $k=1,\ldots,n$,
$$f(x) \;=\; -\Bigl(\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x^2-k^2)\Bigr)\,g(x),$$
where $g$ is a degree-$2$ polynomial and the leading coefficient $-1$ forces $g$
 to be **monic**: $g(x)=x^2+bx+c$.

**Determining $g$ from the two free coefficients.**

*Using the constant term.* The product $E(x):=\prod_{k=1}^n(x^2-k^2)$ is an even
 polynomial with $E(0)=\prod_{k=1}^n(-k^2)=(-1)^n (n!)^2$. Since $n$ is even, $(
-1)^n=1$, so $E(0)=(n!)^2$. Then
$$f(0) = -E(0)\,g(0) = -(n!)^2\, c = 1 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; c = -\frac{1}{(n!
)^2}.$$

*Using the vanishing $x^{2n+1}$ coefficient.* Since $E(x)$ is even, $E(x)\cdot g
(x) = E(x)(x^2+c) + b\,x\,E(x)$. The term $b\,x\,E(x)$ is the only odd-degree to
p contribution; its leading term is $b\,x^{2n+1}$. Hence the $x^{2n+1}$ coeffici
ent of $f=-E\cdot g$ equals $-b$. Setting it to $0$ gives $b=0$.

Therefore
$$g(x) \;=\; x^2 - \frac{1}{(n!)^2}, \qquad f(x) \;=\; -\Bigl(\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x^
2-k^2)\Bigr)\Bigl(x^2 - \tfrac{1}{(n!)^2}\Bigr).$$

**Reading off the solutions.** The roots of $f$ are
$$x \;=\; \pm 1,\ \pm 2,\ \ldots,\ \pm n \quad\text{(the given values)},$$
together with the roots of $g$:
$$x \;=\; \pm\,\frac{1}{n!}.$$

These two values are nonzero (so $1/x$ is defined) and, since $n\ge 2$ is even,
$\frac{1}{n!}\le \frac{1}{2} < 1$, so they are distinct from every $\pm k$ with
$1\le k\le n$. Hence they are genuinely *other* real numbers satisfying $p(1/x)=
x^2$.

**Conclusion.** The only additional real numbers $x$ for which $p(1/x)=x^2$ are
$$\boxed{\,x = \dfrac{1}{n!}\ \text{ and }\ x = -\dfrac{1}{n!}\,}.$$
