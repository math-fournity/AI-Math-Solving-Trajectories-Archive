## Computation of $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{C}^*)$

**Claim.** $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{C}^*) \cong \mathbb{Z
}$, generated by $\pi_1^*\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}(1)$, where $\pi_1 : \mathbb{
P}^n \times \mathbb{C}^* \to \mathbb{P}^n$ is the first projection.

---

### Step 1. Recognize the product as projective space over a ring

The variety $\mathbb{C}^*$ is the affine scheme $\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{C}[
t, t^{-1}]$. Therefore

$$\mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{C}^* \;\cong\; \mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{C}[t,\,t^{-1}
]} \;=\; \operatorname{Proj}\, \mathbb{C}[t, t^{-1}][x_0, \dots, x_n],$$

i.e., it is projective $n$-space over the ring $A := \mathbb{C}[t, t^{-1}]$.

### Step 2. The Picard group of projective space over a ring

We use the following standard theorem (see EGA II, §4.2, or Stacks Project, Tag
0BCH):

> **Theorem.** *For any commutative ring $A$ and $n \geq 1$, the map*
> $$\mathbb{Z} \oplus \operatorname{Pic}(A) \;\longrightarrow\; \operatorname{Pi
c}(\mathbb{P}^n_A), \qquad (k,\,[M]) \;\mapsto\; \bigl[\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n
_A}(k) \otimes \pi^* \widetilde{M}\bigr]$$
> *is an isomorphism of abelian groups, where $\pi: \mathbb{P}^n_A \to \operator
name{Spec}\,A$ is the structure morphism.*

**Sketch of proof.** Cover $\mathbb{P}^n_A$ by the standard opens $U_i = D_+(x_i
) \cong \mathbb{A}^n_A = \operatorname{Spec}\,A[x_0/x_i, \dots, \widehat{x_i/x_i
}, \dots, x_n/x_i]$. Every line bundle is represented by a Čech 1-cocycle $(g_{i
j})$ with $g_{ij} \in \mathcal{O}^*(U_i \cap U_j)$. On each intersection $U_i \c
ap U_j = \operatorname{Spec}\,A[\dots][x_i/x_j, x_j/x_i]$, every unit factors un
iquely as $u \cdot (x_i/x_j)^k$ where $u \in A^*$ and $k \in \mathbb{Z}$. The co
cycle condition forces the $u$-parts to glue to a line bundle on $\operatorname{
Spec}\,A$ (giving the $\operatorname{Pic}(A)$ factor) and the $(x_i/x_j)^k$-part
s give $\mathcal{O}(k)$ (giving the $\mathbb{Z}$ factor). The splitting is uniqu
e, yielding the direct sum. $\square$

### Step 3. The Picard group of $A = \mathbb{C}[t, t^{-1}]$ is trivial

The ring $\mathbb{C}[t, t^{-1}]$ is the localization of the polynomial ring $\ma
thbb{C}[t]$ at the multiplicative set $\{t^m : m \geq 0\}$.

- $\mathbb{C}[t]$ is a PID, hence a UFD, hence integrally closed.
- Localization preserves both the UFD property and integral closedness, so $\mat
hbb{C}[t, t^{-1}]$ is a UFD and is integrally closed (i.e., a normal domain).

For a Noetherian normal domain $A$, there is a canonical isomorphism $\operatorn
ame{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\,A) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(A)$ (the divisor class
group), since every Weil divisor on a normal affine scheme is Cartier, and line
bundles correspond to Cartier divisor classes. A UFD has trivial class group: ev
ery height-one prime ideal is principal, so every Weil divisor is principal. The
refore

$$\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{C}[t, t^{-1}]) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{
C}[t, t^{-1}]) \;=\; 0.$$

### Step 4. Combine

Applying the theorem from Step 2 with $A = \mathbb{C}[t, t^{-1}]$ and using Step
 3:

$$\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{C}^*) \;=\; \operatorname{Pic}(
\mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]}) \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z} \oplus \operatorname{P
ic}(\mathbb{C}[t, t^{-1}]) \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z} \oplus 0 \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}.$$

The generator is $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]}}(1) = \pi_1^*
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}(1)$, the pullback of the hyperplane bundle from the f
irst factor. The second factor $\mathbb{C}^*$ contributes nothing because it car
ries no nontrivial line bundles.

---

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{C}^*) \;\cong\; \mathbb{
Z},}$$

freely generated by $\pi_1^*\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}(1)$.
