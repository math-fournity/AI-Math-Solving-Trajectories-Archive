No. Simplicity is a Morita invariant, so this is impossible. Here is the proof.

## Proof

Recall that two rings $R$ and $T$ are **Morita equivalent** if there is an equiv
alence of categories $F\colon R\text{-Mod} \to T\text{-Mod}$. By Morita theory,
every such equivalence is implemented by a progenerator: there exist an $(R,T)$-
bimodule $P$ and a $(T,R)$-bimodule $Q$ together with bimodule isomorphisms

$$\mu\colon P\otimes_T Q \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} R \quad\text{(as $(R,R)$-bimodul
es)}, \qquad \nu\colon Q\otimes_R P \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} T \quad\text{(as $(T,
T)$-bimodules)},$$

satisfying the Morita context compatibility: $\mu(p\otimes q)\cdot p' = p\cdot \
nu(q\otimes p')$ and $q\cdot\mu(p\otimes q') = \nu(q\otimes p)\cdot q'$ for all
$p,p'\in P$, $q,q'\in Q$.

**Claim.** There is an order-preserving bijection between the two-sided ideals o
f $R$ and the two-sided ideals of $T$.

*Construction.* Given a two-sided ideal $I\trianglelefteq R$, set

$$\Phi(I) \;=\; \nu\!\bigl(Q\otimes_R I\otimes_R P\bigr) \;\subseteq\; T.$$

Since $I$ is a two-sided ideal of $R$, the subspace $Q\otimes_R I\otimes_R P$ is
 a $(T,T)$-sub-bimodule of $Q\otimes_R R\otimes_R P\cong Q\otimes_R P\cong T$, s
o $\Phi(I)$ is a two-sided ideal of $T$.

Conversely, given a two-sided ideal $J\trianglelefteq T$, set

$$\Psi(J) \;=\; \mu\!\bigl(P\otimes_T J\otimes_T Q\bigr) \;\subseteq\; R,$$

which is a two-sided ideal of $R$ by the same reasoning.

*Verification that $\Psi\circ\Phi = \mathrm{id}$.* An element of $\Psi(\Phi(I))$
 is a finite sum of terms $\mu\bigl(p''\otimes \nu(q\otimes i'\otimes p')\otimes
 q'\bigr)$ with $i'\in I$. By the Morita context compatibility,

$$\mu\bigl(p''\otimes \nu(q\otimes i'\otimes p')\otimes q'\bigr) \;=\; \mu(p''\o
times q)\cdot i'\cdot \mu(p'\otimes q').$$

Since $\mu$ is surjective (it is an isomorphism $P\otimes_T Q\xrightarrow{\sim}
R$), the elements $\mu(p''\otimes q)$ and $\mu(p'\otimes q')$ range over all of
$R$. Hence

$$\Psi(\Phi(I)) \;=\; \left\{\sum_k r_k\cdot i_k\cdot r_k' \;:\; r_k,r_k'\in R,\
; i_k\in I\right\} \;=\; R\cdot I\cdot R \;=\; I,$$

where the last equality uses that $I$ is a two-sided ideal. The reverse inclusio
n $I\subseteq\Psi(\Phi(I))$ follows by writing $1_R=\sum_j \mu(p_j\otimes q_j)$
(possible by surjectivity of $\mu$) so that $i = \sum_j i\cdot\mu(p_j\otimes q_j
)=\sum_j \mu(i\cdot p_j\otimes q_j)$, and each $i\cdot p_j\in I\cdot P$ shows $i
\in\Psi(\Phi(I))$.

Thus $\Psi\circ\Phi=\mathrm{id}$, and by symmetry $\Phi\circ\Psi=\mathrm{id}$.

**Conclusion.** The maps $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ are mutually inverse bijections betwe
en the lattices of two-sided ideals of $R$ and $T$. In particular, $T$ has exact
ly two two-sided ideals ($0$ and $T$) if and only if $R$ has exactly two two-sid
ed ideals ($0$ and $R$). That is, $T$ is simple if and only if $R$ is simple.

Therefore, if $T$ is simple and $R$ is Morita equivalent to $T$, then $R$ must a
lso be simple. It is **not possible** for two Morita equivalent rings $R$ and $T
$ to have $T$ simple while $R$ is not. $\blacksquare$
