# Infinitesimal Generator of $T(t) = e^{At}$

## Setup

Let $X$ be a Hilbert space and $A \in \mathcal{L}(X)$ a bounded linear operator.
 Since $A$ is bounded, the exponential
$$e^{At} := \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{t^n A^n}{n!}$$
converges in operator norm for every $t \geq 0$, and $\{T(t)\}_{t \geq 0} = \{e^
{At}\}_{t \geq 0}$ is a $C_0$-semigroup on $X$.

## Definition of the Generator

The infinitesimal generator $G$ of $\{T(t)\}_{t\ge 0}$ is defined by
$$Gx = \lim_{t \to 0^+} \frac{T(t)x - x}{t},$$
with domain $D(G)$ consisting of all $x \in X$ for which this limit exists (in t
he norm of $X$).

## Claim

$G = A$ and $D(G) = X$.

## Proof

**Step 1: A formal identity.** For every $x \in X$ and $t \geq 0$,
$$\frac{e^{At}x - x}{t} - Ax = \frac{1}{t}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^n A^n}{n!}x
 - Ax = \sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n-1}A^n}{n!}x.$$

**Step 2: Norm estimate.** Using $\|A^n\| \le \|A\|^n$,
$$\left\|\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n-1}A^n}{n!}x\right\| \le \|x\|\sum_{n=2}^{
\infty}\frac{t^{n-1}\|A\|^n}{n!} = \|x\|\,\|A\|\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n-1}\
|A\|^{n-1}}{n!}.$$
Setting $m = n-1$,
$$= \|x\|\,\|A\|\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(t\|A\|)^{m}}{(m+1)!} \le \|x\|\,\|A\|\
cdot t\|A\|\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(t\|A\|)^{m-1}}{m!} \cdot \frac{1}{m+1}\cdot
(m+1)\cdot\frac{1}{1}.$$
A cleaner bound: for $n \ge 2$, $n! \ge 2\cdot(n-2)!\cdot 1$ is not the tightest
; instead use directly
$$\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n-1}\|A\|^n}{n!} = \|A\|\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(
t\|A\|)^{n-1}}{n!} = \|A\|\cdot\frac{1}{t\|A\|}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(t\|A\|)
^{n}}{n!} = \frac{1}{t}\left(e^{t\|A\|} - 1 - t\|A\|\right).$$
Hence
$$\left\|\frac{e^{At}x - x}{t} - Ax\right\| \le \|x\|\cdot\frac{e^{t\|A\|} - 1 -
 t\|A\|}{t}.$$

**Step 3: The bound vanishes as $t \to 0^+$.** By the Taylor expansion $e^{s} =
1 + s + \frac{s^2}{2}e^{\xi}$ for some $\xi$ between $0$ and $s$,
$$\frac{e^{t\|A\|} - 1 - t\|A\|}{t} = \frac{(t\|A\|)^2}{2t}e^{\xi} = \frac{t\|A\
|^2}{2}e^{\xi} \xrightarrow{t\to 0^+} 0.$$

Therefore, for **every** $x \in X$,
$$\lim_{t\to 0^+}\frac{e^{At}x - x}{t} = Ax,$$
which shows $D(G) = X$ and $Gx = Ax$ for all $x \in X$, i.e. $G = A$.

## Verification of generator properties (consistency check)

- **Domain is all of $X$**: established above.
- **Semigroup property**: $e^{A(t+s)} = e^{At}e^{As}$ follows from the power-ser
ies definition and absolute convergence.
- **Strong continuity at $0$**: $\|e^{At}x - x\| \le (e^{t\|A\|}-1)\|x\| \to 0$
as $t\to 0^+$, so $T(t)x \to x$ for all $x\in X$; this is the $C_0$ property.
- **Generator–semigroup link**: the computation above is exactly the defining li
mit of the generator, confirming $G=A$.

## Conclusion

The infinitesimal generator of the $C_0$-semigroup $T(t)=e^{At}$ is the operator
 $A$ itself, with full domain:

$$\boxed{G = A, \qquad D(G) = X.}$$
