## Proof

raically closed field $k$ of arbitrary characteristic. Let $f: Y \to X$ be a res
olution of singularities (so $Y$ is smooth, hence Gorenstein) satisfying:
- $R^if_*\mathcal{O}_Y = 0$ for all $i > 0$,
- $R^if_*\omega_Y = 0$ for all $i > 0$.

Since $X$ is projective over a field, it admits a dualizing complex $\omega_X^\b
ullet$. Since $Y$ is smooth of dimension $n$, its dualizing complex is $\omega_Y
^\bullet = \omega_Y[n]$, where $\omega_Y$ is the invertible canonical bundle.

**Step 1: $Rf_*\mathcal{O}_Y = \mathcal{O}_X$.**

Since $f$ is proper birational and $X$ is normal, we have $f_*\mathcal{O}_Y = \m
athcal{O}_X$ (a standard result: the natural map $\mathcal{O}_X \to f_*\mathcal{
O}_Y$ is an isomorphism because $f$ is an isomorphism over the dense open smooth
 locus and $X$ is normal, hence $S2$; equivalently, this follows from Zariski's
Main Theorem / connectedness of fibers). Combined with the hypothesis $R^if_*\ma
thcal{O}_Y = 0$ for $i > 0$, we obtain:
$$Rf_*\mathcal{O}_Y = \mathcal{O}_X \quad \text{(concentrated in degree 0).}$$

**Step 2: Apply Grothendieck duality.**

By Grothendieck–Verdier duality for the proper morphism $f$, for any $F \in D^b_
c(Y)$:
$$Rf_*R\mathcal{H}om_Y(F,\, f^!\omega_X^\bullet) \;\cong\; R\mathcal{H}om_X(Rf_*
F,\, \omega_X^\bullet).$$

Since $f$ is a morphism of finite type between schemes possessing dualizing comp
lexes, $f^!\omega_X^\bullet = \omega_Y^\bullet = \omega_Y[n]$. Taking $F = \math
cal{O}_Y$:
$$Rf_*\omega_Y[n] \;\cong\; R\mathcal{H}om_X(Rf_*\mathcal{O}_Y,\, \omega_X^\bull
et).$$

Applying Step 1 ($Rf_*\mathcal{O}_Y = \mathcal{O}_X$):
$$Rf_*\omega_Y[n] \;\cong\; R\mathcal{H}om_X(\mathcal{O}_X,\, \omega_X^\bullet)
\;=\; \omega_X^\bullet. \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Step 3: The derived trace map is an isomorphism.**

The trace map $\mathrm{Tr}_f: Rf_*\omega_Y^\bullet \to \omega_X^\bullet$ is the
counit of the adjunction $Rf_* \dashv f^!$. Under the duality isomorphism of Ste
p 2, this trace map corresponds to the morphism
$$R\mathcal{H}om_X(Rf_*\mathcal{O}_Y,\, \omega_X^\bullet) \;\longrightarrow\; R\
mathcal{H}om_X(\mathcal{O}_X,\, \omega_X^\bullet) = \omega_X^\bullet$$
induced by the unit of adjunction $\eta: \mathcal{O}_X \to Rf_*\mathcal{O}_Y$.

Since $Rf_*\mathcal{O}_Y = \mathcal{O}_X$ and $\eta: \mathcal{O}_X \to \mathcal{
O}_X$ is the identity (it is the map on functions induced by the birational morp
hism $f$, which is an isomorphism over the dense open smooth locus of $X$), the
induced map on $R\mathcal{H}om$ is the identity. Therefore:
$$\mathrm{Tr}_f: Rf_*\omega_Y[n] \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; \omega_X^\bullet$$
is an isomorphism in the derived category.

**Step 4: Take cohomology in degree $-n$.**

Taking $H^{-n}$ of the isomorphism $(\ast)$:
$$H^{-n}\!\bigl(Rf_*\omega_Y[n]\bigr) \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; H^{-n}(\omega_X
^\bullet).$$

The left-hand side is $H^0(Rf_*\omega_Y) = f_*\omega_Y$. The right-hand side is
$H^{-n}(\omega_X^\bullet)$, which is by definition the **canonical (dualizing) s
heaf** $\omega_X$ of the $n$-dimensional variety $X$. (For a normal variety, $H^
{-n}(\omega_X^\bullet)$ coincides with the reflexive canonical sheaf $j_*\omega_
{X_{\mathrm{reg}}}$, where $j: X_{\mathrm{reg}} \hookrightarrow X$; this is stan
dard—normality gives the $S2$ condition, and $H^{-n}(\omega_X^\bullet)$ is the u
nique $S2$ sheaf extending $\omega_{X_{\mathrm{reg}}}$.)

Therefore:
$$f_*\omega_Y \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; \omega_X.$$

This isomorphism is precisely the trace map on $H^{-n}$, so **the trace is an is
omorphism**.

**Step 5 (Remark on the role of $R^if_*\omega_Y = 0$).** The condition $R^if_*\o
mega_Y = 0$ for $i > 0$ was not needed for the conclusion $f_*\omega_Y \cong \om
ega_X$. From the isomorphism $(\ast)$, we have $H^{j}(\omega_X^\bullet) = R^{j+n
}f_*\omega_Y$ for all $j$. The vanishing $R^if_*\omega_Y = 0$ for $i > 0$ (i.e.,
 for $j > -n$) is equivalent to $\omega_X^\bullet$ being concentrated in degree
$-n$, i.e., to $X$ being **Cohen–Macaulay**. Thus the hypothesis $R^if_*\omega_Y
 = 0$ ensures $X$ is CM (a condition that does not follow from $R^if_*\mathcal{O
}_Y = 0$ alone in positive characteristic), but the trace isomorphism $f_*\omega
_Y \cong \omega_X$ holds using only $R^if_*\mathcal{O}_Y = 0$ together with norm
ality.

**Conclusion.** Under the given hypotheses, $f_*\omega_Y = \omega_X$; the trace
map is an isomorphism. $\blacksquare$
