## Solution

We evaluate
$$I = \oint_{|z|=2018} \frac{\pi^{1/z}}{(1+3z^2)^{2018}}\,dz.$$

**Singularities of the integrand.** Let $f(z)=\dfrac{\pi^{1/z}}{(1+3z^2)^{2018}}
$.

- $z=0$: $\pi^{1/z}=e^{(\ln\pi)/z}$ has an essential singularity.
- $1+3z^2=0 \Rightarrow z=\pm\dfrac{i}{\sqrt3}$: poles of order $2018$.

All three satisfy $|z|<2018$ (since $1/\sqrt3\approx 0.577$), so every finite si
ngularity lies inside the contour.

**Residue at infinity.** Use the change of variable $w=1/z$:
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=\infty} f(z) = -\operatorname{Res}_{w=0}\frac{f(1/w)}{w^
2}.$$
Now
$$f(1/w)=\frac{\pi^{w}}{(1+3/w^2)^{2018}}=\frac{\pi^{w}\,w^{4036}}{(w^2+3)^{2018
}},$$
hence
$$\frac{f(1/w)}{w^2}=\frac{\pi^{w}\,w^{4034}}{(w^2+3)^{2018}}.$$
At $w=0$: $\pi^w$ is analytic with $\pi^0=1$; $(w^2+3)^{2018}=3^{2018}(1+w^2/3)^
{2018}$ is analytic and nonzero at $w=0$. Therefore the expression has a **zero
of order $4034$** at $w=0$, so its Laurent expansion contains no $w^{-1}$ term.
Thus
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=\infty} f(z)=0.$$

**Applying the residue theorem.** The sum of all residues (including the one at
infinity) is zero:
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}f+\operatorname{Res}_{z=i/\sqrt3}f+\operatorname{Res}_
{z=-i/\sqrt3}f+\operatorname{Res}_{z=\infty}f=0.$$
Since $\operatorname{Res}_{z=\infty}f=0$ and every finite singularity is inside
$|z|=2018$,
$$I = 2\pi i\Big(\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}f+\operatorname{Res}_{z=i/\sqrt3}f+\ope
