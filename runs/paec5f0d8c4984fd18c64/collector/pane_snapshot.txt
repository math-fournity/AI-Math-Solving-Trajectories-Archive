## Proof

The function $f(z) = \dfrac{1}{\sin z}$ is meromorphic on $\mathbb{C}$, with pol
es exactly at the zeros of $\sin z$, namely $z = n\pi$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

**Identifying the poles inside $\gamma$.** By hypothesis, $0$ lies in the interi
or of $\gamma$, and every point of $\{\pi n : n \in \mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\}$
lies in $\mathbb{C}\setminus|\gamma|$ (i.e., in the exterior region, off the tra
ce of $\gamma$). Since $\gamma$ is a simple closed path, its interior and exteri
or are well separated, so the only pole of $f$ inside $\gamma$ is $z = 0$.

**Computing the residue at $z = 0$.** Near $z = 0$,
$$\sin z = z - \frac{z^{3}}{6} + \cdots = z\!\left(1 - \frac{z^{2}}{6} + \cdots\
right),$$
so
$$\frac{1}{\sin z} = \frac{1}{z}\cdot\frac{1}{1 - z^{2}/6 + \cdots} = \frac{1}{z
}\left(1 + \frac{z^{2}}{6} + \cdots\right) = \frac{1}{z} + \frac{z}{6} + \cdots.
$$
The coefficient of $z^{-1}$ in this Laurent expansion is $1$, hence
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\frac{1}{\sin z} = 1.$$

(Equivalently, since $z=0$ is a simple zero of $\sin z$ with $(\sin z)' \big|_{z
=0} = \cos 0 = 1$, the residue is $\dfrac{1}{\cos 0} = 1$.)

**Applying the residue theorem.** Since $f$ is holomorphic on and inside $\gamma
$ except for the single pole at $z = 0$ in the interior, the residue theorem giv
es
$$\int_{\gamma} \frac{1}{\sin z}\,dz = 2\pi i \sum_{\text{poles inside } \gamma}
 \operatorname{Res} = 2\pi i \cdot 1 = 2\pi i.$$

Therefore,
$$\boxed{\int_{\gamma} \frac{1}{\sin z}\,dz = 2\pi i.}$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
