## Proof

We are given
\[
\Phi(s)=2a_0+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n\bigl(e^{\sqrt n\,s}+e^{-\sqrt n\,s}\bigr),
\]
absolutely and uniformly convergent on every closed interior strip \(-h+\varepsi
lon\le \Re s\le h-\varepsilon\), and we must compute
\[
F(u)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{a-i\infty}^{a+i\infty}\frac{\Phi(s)\,e^{-us}}{s^2}\,d
s,\qquad 0<a<h,\;u>0.
\]

### 1. Justifying termwise integration

Fix \(0<a<h\) and choose \(\varepsilon>0\) with \(a<h-\varepsilon\). By the hypo
thesis \(\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log|a_n|}{\sqrt n}=-h\), for all sufficientl
y large \(n\),
\[
|a_n|\le e^{(-h+\varepsilon)\sqrt n}.
\]
On the line \(\Re s=a\),
\[
\bigl|e^{\sqrt n\,s}+e^{-\sqrt n\,s}\bigr|\le 2e^{a\sqrt n},
\]
hence
\[
\bigl|a_n\bigl(e^{\sqrt n\,s}+e^{-\sqrt n\,s}\bigr)\bigr|\le 2\,e^{(-h+\varepsil
on+a)\sqrt n}.
\]
Since \(-h+\varepsilon+a<0\), the series converges absolutely and uniformly on \
(\Re s=a\). Moreover \(|e^{-us}/s^2|=e^{-ua}/|s|^2\) is integrable in \(\Im s\).
 Therefore we may interchange sum and integral:
\[
F(u)=2a_0\,I(-u)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n\bigl[I(\sqrt n-u)+I(-\sqrt n-u)\bigr],
\]
where
\[
I(t):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{a-i\infty}^{a+i\infty}\frac{e^{ts}}{s^2}\,ds.
\]

### 2. Evaluating \(I(t)\)

The integrand \(\frac{e^{ts}}{s^2}\) has a double pole at \(s=0\), lying to the
left of the Bromwich line \(\Re s=a>0\).

- **If \(t>0\):** close the contour to the left (a large left half-plane, on whi
ch \(e^{ts}\to 0\) since \(t>0\) and \(\Re s\to-\infty\)). The only singularity
enclosed is \(s=0\). The residue of \(\frac{e^{ts}}{s^2}\) at the double pole \(
s=0\) is the coefficient of \(1/s\), namely
  \[
  \operatorname*{Res}_{s=0}\frac{e^{ts}}{s^2}=t.
  \]
  Hence \(I(t)=t\).

- **If \(t<0\):** close the contour to the right. No singularities are enclosed
(the pole \(s=0\) is to the left of \(\Re s=a\)), and \(e^{ts}\to 0\) as \(\Re s
\to+\infty\). Hence \(I(t)=0\).

(At \(t=0\) the value is irrelevant since it occurs only for isolated \(u\).) Th
us
\[
I(t)=\max(t,0).
\]

### 3. Substituting back

Since \(u>0\):
- \(I(-u)=0\) (because \(-u<0\));
- \(I(-\sqrt n-u)=0\) for every \(n\ge 1\) (because \(-\sqrt n-u<0\));
- \(I(\sqrt n-u)=\sqrt n-u\) when \(\sqrt n>u\), i.e. \(n>u^2\), and \(=0\) when
 \(n<u^2\).

Therefore all terms involving \(a_0\) and all the \(e^{-\sqrt n\,s}\)-contributi
ons vanish, and only the \(e^{+\sqrt n\,s}\)-terms with \(n>u^2\) survive:

\[
\boxed{\,F(u)=\sum_{\substack{n\ge 1\\ n>u^2}} a_n\bigl(\sqrt n-u\bigr)\,}.
\]

Equivalently, writing \(N(u)=\lfloor u^2\rfloor\),
\[
F(u)=\sum_{n=N(u)+1}^{\infty}a_n(\sqrt n-u).
\]

### 4. Structure of \(F\) and the uniqueness statement

\(F(u)\) is a piecewise-linear, continuous, decreasing-where-defined function of
 \(u>0\): each summand \(a_n(\sqrt n-u)\) is active for \(0<u<\sqrt n\) and cont
ributes the constant slope \(-a_n\). Hence, on any open interval \((\sqrt m,\sqr
t{m+1})\) between consecutive "switch-off" points,
\[
F'(u)=-\sum_{n\ge m+1}a_n,
\]
so that the **jump of \(F'\) at \(u=\sqrt m\)** equals
\[
F'(\sqrt m\,^-)-F'(\sqrt m\,^+)=a_m.
\]
Thus the coefficients \((a_n)_{n\ge 1}\) are completely determined by \(F\): \(a
_m\) is the jump of the derivative of \(F\) at \(u=\sqrt m\). Once all \(a_n\) (
\(n\ge 1\)) are known, \(a_0\) is recovered from \(\Phi(0)=2a_0+2\sum_{n\ge 1}a_
n\).

Consequently \(\Phi\equiv 0\implies F\equiv 0\implies\) every jump of \(F'\) is
zero \(\implies a_n=0\) for all \(n\ge 1\), and then \(a_0=0\). This proves the
stated uniqueness: \(\Phi\) vanishes identically only when all coefficients vani
sh.
