Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the unitary operator $U$ in
 the spectral representation of a self-adjoint operator with a cyclic vector is
unique up to an isomorphism.

## Proof

Let $A \in L(H)$ be self-adjoint with cyclic vector $x$, and let $E_x$ be the as
sociated spectral measure on $\sigma(A)$. Suppose $U_1, U_2: L^2(\sigma(A), E_x)
 \to H$ are two unitary operators both implementing the spectral representation,
 i.e.,

$$U_i^*\, A\, U_i = M_t, \qquad i = 1, 2,$$

where $M_t$ denotes multiplication by the identity function $t \mapsto t$ on $L^
2(\sigma(A), E_x)$.

**Step 1: The intertwining operator.** Define $V = U_2^*\, U_1 : L^2(\sigma(A),
E_x) \to L^2(\sigma(A), E_x)$. Then $V$ is unitary, and:

$$V\, M_t = U_2^*\, U_1\, M_t = U_2^*\, U_1\, (U_1^*\, A\, U_1) = U_2^*\, A\, U_
1 = (U_2^*\, A\, U_2)(U_2^*\, U_1) = M_t\, V.$$

So $V$ commutes with $M_t$.

**Step 2: The commutant of $M_t$.** Since $M_t$ is multiplication by the identit
y function on $\sigma(A) \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, the von Neumann algebra generate
d by $M_t$ is the full multiplication algebra $\{M_\phi : \phi \in L^\infty(\sig
ma(A), E_x)\}$. (Every bounded Borel function of $t$ is a bounded multiplication
 operator, and by the bicommutant theorem these generate the full $L^\infty$ mul
tiplication algebra.)

Now, if $V$ commutes with $M_t$, it commutes with the entire von Neumann algebra
 generated by $M_t$, i.e., with all $M_\phi$, $\phi \in L^\infty$. Set $\phi_0 =
 V(\mathbf{1})$, where $\mathbf{1}$ is the constant function. Then for any bound
ed measurable $\psi$:

$$V(\psi) = V(M_\psi\, \mathbf{1}) = M_\psi\, V(\mathbf{1}) = M_\psi\, \phi_0 =
\phi_0 \cdot \psi.$$

By density of bounded functions in $L^2(\sigma(A), E_x)$, we get $V(f) = \phi_0
\cdot f$ for all $f \in L^2(\sigma(A), E_x)$, i.e., $V = M_{\phi_0}$. Since $V$
is bounded, $\phi_0 \in L^\infty(\sigma(A), E_x)$.

**Step 3: Unitarity forces $|\phi_0| = 1$ a.e.** Since $V = M_{\phi_0}$ is unita
ry:

$$\|V f\|^2 = \int |\phi_0|^2 |f|^2\, dE_x = \|f\|^2 = \int |f|^2\, dE_x \quad \
forall\, f,$$

which gives $|\phi_0(t)| = 1$ for $E_x$-a.e. $t$.

**Step 4: Converse — every unit-modulus function gives a valid $U$.** Conversely
, for any measurable $\phi: \sigma(A) \to \mathbb{C}$ with $|\phi| = 1$ a.e., th
e operator $M_\phi$ is unitary on $L^2(\sigma(A), E_x)$ and commutes with $M_t$.
 If $U_0$ is any valid spectral unitary, then $U_0\, M_\phi$ is also valid:

$$(U_0\, M_\phi)^*\, A\, (U_0\, M_\phi) = M_\phi^*\, U_0^*\, A\, U_0\, M_\phi =
M_\phi^*\, M_t\, M_\phi = M_t.$$

**Step 5: Classification.** The set of all unitary operators $U: L^2(\sigma(A),
E_x) \to H$ satisfying $U^* A U = M_t$ is exactly

$$\{\, U_0\, M_\phi : \phi \in L^\infty(\sigma(A), E_x),\; |\phi| = 1 \text{ a.e
.}\,\}$$

for any fixed valid $U_0$. The operators $M_\phi$ with $|\phi| = 1$ a.e. are pre
cisely the **unitary automorphisms (isomorphisms)** of $L^2(\sigma(A), E_x)$ tha
t preserve the spectral representation structure — they are the unitary operator
s commuting with $M_t$, i.e., the unitary elements of the commutant $\{M_t\}'$.

**Conclusion.** Yes, the unitary operator $U$ is unique up to an isomorphism. An
y two unitary operators implementing the spectral representation $U^* A U = M_t$
 differ by composition (on the right) with a unitary automorphism of $L^2(\sigma
(A), E_x)$ that commutes with $M_t$, and these automorphisms are exactly the mul
tiplication operators $M_\phi$ by measurable functions of unit modulus. $\blacks
quare$
