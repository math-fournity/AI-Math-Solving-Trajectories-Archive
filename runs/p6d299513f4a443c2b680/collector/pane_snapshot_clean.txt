Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $n$ PSD matrices with trace
 1 and pairwise zero product must each be rank-1 projections onto an orthonormal
 basis.

**Answer: Yes.**

## Proof

**Step 1: Orthogonality of ranges.**

For positive semidefinite $A, B$, the condition $AB = 0$ implies $\operatorname{
range}(B) \subseteq \ker(A)$. Since $A \succeq 0$, we have $\ker(A) = \operatorn
ame{range}(A)^\perp$ (a standard fact: for PSD $A$, $\mathbb{C}^n = \operatornam
e{range}(A) \oplus \ker(A)$ is an orthogonal decomposition). Therefore:
$$\operatorname{range}(P_j) \subseteq \operatorname{range}(P_i)^\perp \quad \tex
t{for all } i \neq j.$$
So the subspaces $V_i := \operatorname{range}(P_i)$ are **pairwise orthogonal**.

**Step 2: Dimension constraint.**

Since $V_1, \dots, V_n$ are pairwise orthogonal subspaces of $\mathbb{C}^n$:
$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} \dim V_i = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \operatorname{rank}(P_i) \leq n.$$

**Step 3: Each $P_i$ is nonzero, hence rank $\geq 1$.**

Since $\operatorname{Tr}(P_i) = 1 > 0$ and $P_i \succeq 0$, the matrix $P_i$ has
 at least one positive eigenvalue, so $P_i \neq 0$ and $\operatorname{rank}(P_i)
 \geq 1$.

**Step 4: Combining the bounds.**

From Step 3: $\sum_{i=1}^n \operatorname{rank}(P_i) \geq n$.
From Step 2: $\sum_{i=1}^n \operatorname{rank}(P_i) \leq n$.

Therefore $\sum_{i=1}^n \operatorname{rank}(P_i) = n$, which forces
$$\operatorname{rank}(P_i) = 1 \quad \text{for every } i \in \{1, \dots, n\}.$$

**Step 5: Constructing the orthonormal basis.**

Each $P_i$ is PSD, rank 1, with $\operatorname{Tr}(P_i) = 1$. A rank-1 PSD matri
x has the form $P_i = \lambda_i v_i v_i^*$ for some unit vector $v_i$ and $\lamb
da_i > 0$. The trace is $\lambda_i$, so $\lambda_i = 1$. Thus:
$$P_i = u_i u_i^*$$
for some unit vector $u_i \in \mathbb{C}^n$.

Since $\operatorname{range}(P_i) = \operatorname{span}(u_i)$ and the ranges are
pairwise orthogonal (Step 1), the vectors $u_1, \dots, u_n$ are pairwise orthogo
nal. They are $n$ pairwise orthogonal unit vectors in $\mathbb{C}^n$, hence they
 form an **orthonormal basis**.

Yes, it is necessarily the case. The constraints $\operatorname{Tr}(P_i) = 1$ (f
