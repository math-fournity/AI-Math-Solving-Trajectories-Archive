## Proof

We claim the only analytic function satisfying the conditions is the identically
 zero function $g \equiv 0$.

**Step 1: The zeros of $g$ accumulate inside $D$.**

For every integer $n \geq 2$, the point $\tfrac{1}{n}$ lies in the open unit dis
k $D$ (since $0 < \tfrac{1}{n} < 1$), and by hypothesis $g\!\left(\tfrac{1}{n}\r
ight) = 0$. The sequence $\left\{\tfrac{1}{n}\right\}_{n \geq 2}$ is a sequence
of *distinct* points of $D$ converging to $0$, and $0 \in D$.

**Step 2: Apply the identity theorem.**

Since $g$ is analytic on the connected open set $D$, and the zero set of $g$ con
tains a sequence of distinct points with a limit point $0$ lying inside $D$, the
 identity theorem for analytic functions forces
$$g(z) = 0 \quad \text{for all } z \in D.$$

**Step 3: Verify the growth bound.**

For $g \equiv 0$ we have $|g(z)| = 0 \leq 4e^{|z|} + |z+1|^{2}$ for every $z \in
 D$, since the right-hand side is strictly positive. Thus the bound is automatic
ally satisfied.

**Conclusion.** The identity theorem already determines $g$ completely from the
zero condition alone; the growth bound is consistent with (and does not further
restrict) the unique solution. Hence the unique analytic function on $D$ satisfy
ing both conditions is

$$\boxed{g \equiv 0.}$$
