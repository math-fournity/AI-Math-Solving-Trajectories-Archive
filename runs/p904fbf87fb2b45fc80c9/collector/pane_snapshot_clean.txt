## Proof

Place the regular $n$-gon on the complex plane with center $O$ at the origin and
 circumradius $OA_1=1$. The vertices are
$$A_k = \omega^{k-1}, \quad k=1,2,\dots,n, \qquad \omega = e^{2\pi i/n},$$
so that $A_iA_j = |\omega^{i-1}-\omega^{j-1}|$.

**Key polynomial identity.** The $n$-th roots of unity are the roots of $z^n-1$:
$$z^n - 1 = \prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(z-\omega^k).$$
Differentiating both sides,
$$nz^{n-1} = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\;\prod_{\substack{m=0\\m\ne k}}^{n-1}(z-\omega^m).
$$
Evaluating at $z=\omega^k$, every term in the sum vanishes except the $k$-th:
$$n\,\omega^{k(n-1)} = \prod_{\substack{m=0\\m\ne k}}^{n-1}(\omega^k-\omega^m).
\tag{$\ast$}$$

**Take absolute values and multiply over all $k$.** Since $|\omega^{k(n-1)}|=1$,
 equation $(\ast)$ gives
$$\prod_{\substack{m=0\\m\ne k}}^{n-1}|\omega^k-\omega^m| = n.$$
Multiplying over $k=0,1,\dots,n-1$:
$$\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\;\prod_{\substack{m=0\\m\ne k}}^{n-1}|\omega^k-\omega^m| = n
^n. \tag{$\ast\ast$}$$

**Count multiplicities.** In the double product ($\ast\ast$), every ordered pair
 $(k,m)$ with $k\ne m$ contributes the factor $|\omega^k-\omega^m|$ exactly once
. Grouping ordered pairs into unordered pairs $\{k,m\}$, each unordered pair con
tributes $|\omega^k-\omega^m|\cdot|\omega^m-\omega^k| = |\omega^k-\omega^m|^2$.
Hence
$$\prod_{0\le k<m\le n-1}|\omega^k-\omega^m|^2 = n^n.$$
Taking the square root (all factors are positive real numbers),
$$\prod_{0\le k<m\le n-1}|\omega^k-\omega^m| = n^{n/2}.$$

**Relabel to the original vertices.** The map $(k,m)\mapsto(i,j)=(k+1,m+1)$ is a
 bijection from pairs $0\le k<m\le n-1$ to pairs $1\le i<j\le n$, and $|\omega^k
-\omega^m| = A_{k+1}A_{m+1}$. Therefore
$$\boxed{\prod_{1\le i<j\le n} A_iA_j \;=\; n^{\,n/2}.}$$

**Sanity checks.**
- $n=2$: two antipodal points at distance $2$; product $=2 = 2^{2/2}$. ✓
- $n=3$: equilateral triangle with circumradius $1$, side $\sqrt3$; product $=(\
sqrt3)^3=3\sqrt3 = 3^{3/2}$. ✓
- $n=4$: unit square, four sides of length $\sqrt2$ and two diagonals of length
$2$; product $=(\sqrt2)^4\cdot 2^2 = 16 = 4^{4/2}$. ✓
