## Proof

**Setup.** Consider the integral operator $T$ acting on $C[0,1]$ (equivalently $
L^\infty[0,1]$) with the sup norm $\|f\|_\infty = \sup_{x\in[0,1]}|f(x)|$, defin
ed by
$$Tf(x) = \int_0^1 \frac{f(y)}{1+x^2+y^2}\,dy.$$

**Step 1: Upper bound.** The kernel $K(x,y)=\frac{1}{1+x^2+y^2}$ is strictly pos
itive on $[0,1]^2$. For any $f$ with $\|f\|_\infty\le 1$,
$$|Tf(x)| \le \int_0^1 \frac{|f(y)|}{1+x^2+y^2}\,dy \le \int_0^1 \frac{dy}{1+x^2
+y^2} =: g(x).$$
Hence $\|Tf\|_\infty \le \sup_{x\in[0,1]} g(x)$, so $\|T\| \le \sup_{x\in[0,1]}
g(x)$.

**Step 2: Lower bound (attainment).** Take $f\equiv 1$, which has $\|f\|_\infty=
1$. Then $Tf(x)=g(x)\ge 0$, so $\|Tf\|_\infty = \sup_{x\in[0,1]} g(x)$. Therefor
e $\|T\| \ge \sup_{x\in[0,1]} g(x)$.

Combining Steps 1 and 2:
$$\|T\| = \sup_{x\in[0,1]} g(x), \qquad g(x)=\int_0^1 \frac{dy}{1+x^2+y^2}.$$

**Step 3: Compute $g(x)$.**
$$g(x) = \int_0^1 \frac{dy}{(1+x^2)+y^2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}\arctan\!\left(
\frac{y}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}\right)\Bigg|_{0}^{1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}\arctan\!\le
ft(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}\right).$$

**Step 4: Maximize $g(x)$ over $x\in[0,1]$.** Substitute $t=\sqrt{1+x^2}\in[1,\s
qrt{2}]$ and define
$$h(t)=\frac{\arctan(1/t)}{t}.$$
Differentiate:
$$h'(t)=\frac{t\cdot\dfrac{-1/t^2}{1+1/t^2}-\arctan(1/t)}{t^2}=\frac{-\dfrac{t}{
t^2+1}-\arctan(1/t)}{t^2}.$$
For $t>0$, both $\frac{t}{t^2+1}>0$ and $\arctan(1/t)>0$, so the numerator is st
rictly negative. Thus $h'(t)<0$ for all $t>0$; $h$ is strictly decreasing on $[1
,\sqrt{2}]$.

Since $g(x)=h(\sqrt{1+x^2})$ and $\sqrt{1+x^2}$ is increasing in $x$, $g(x)$ is
**strictly decreasing** in $x$ on $[0,1]$. The maximum is attained at $x=0$:
$$\|T\| = g(0) = \int_0^1 \frac{dy}{1+y^2} = \arctan(y)\Big|_0^1 = \arctan(1) =
\frac{\pi}{4}.$$

**Conclusion.** The operator norm of $T$ is
$$\boxed{\|T\| = \dfrac{\pi}{4}}.$$
