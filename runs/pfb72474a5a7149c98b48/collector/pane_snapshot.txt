**Proof.** Define two auxiliary functions
$$p(x)=f(x)\sin x+f'(x)\cos x,\qquad q(x)=f(x)\cos x-f'(x)\sin x.$$
A direct computation gives
$$p'(x)=\bigl(f(x)+f''(x)\bigr)\cos x,\qquad q'(x)=-\bigl(f(x)+f''(x)\bigr)\sin
x.$$
Also, by the Pythagorean identity,
$$p(x)^2+q(x)^2=f(x)^2+f'(x)^2.$$
In particular $p(0)=f'(0)$, $q(0)=f(0)$, so $p(0)^2+q(0)^2=(f(0))^2+(f'(0))^2=4$
.

Since $|f(x)|\le 1$, we have $(f(0))^2\le 1$, hence
$$(f'(0))^2=4-(f(0))^2\ge 3,\qquad\text{so }\ |f'(0)|\ge\sqrt 3>1. \tag{$\ast$}$
$

Suppose, for contradiction, that $f(x)+f''(x)\ne 0$ for every real $x$. Since $f
+f''$ is continuous, it has constant sign on $\mathbb R$.

**Case 1: $f(x)+f''(x)>0$ for all $x$.** Then $p'(x)$ has the same sign as $\cos
 x$. On the interval $(-\tfrac{\pi}{2},\tfrac{\pi}{2})$ we have $\cos x>0$, so $
p$ is strictly increasing there. Hence
$$p(-\tfrac{\pi}{2})<p(0)<p(\tfrac{\pi}{2}).$$
Now $p(\tfrac{\pi}{2})=f(\tfrac{\pi}{2})\sin\tfrac{\pi}{2}+f'(\tfrac{\pi}{2})\co
s\tfrac{\pi}{2}=f(\tfrac{\pi}{2})\le 1$, and $p(-\tfrac{\pi}{2})=f(-\tfrac{\pi}{
2})\sin(-\tfrac{\pi}{2})+f'(-\tfrac{\pi}{2})\cos(-\tfrac{\pi}{2})=-f(-\tfrac{\pi
}{2})\ge -1$. Since $p(0)=f'(0)$, we obtain
$$-1<f'(0)<1,$$
i.e. $|f'(0)|<1$, contradicting $(\ast)$.

**Case 2: $f(x)+f''(x)<0$ for all $x$.** Apply Case 1 to the function $g(x)=-f(x
)$. Then $|g(x)|\le 1$, $g$ is twice continuously differentiable, $(g(0))^2+(g'(
0))^2=(f(0))^2+(f'(0))^2=4$, and $g(x)+g''(x)=-(f(x)+f''(x))>0$ for all $x$. Cas
e 1 then yields $|g'(0)|<1$, i.e. $|f'(0)|<1$, again contradicting $(\ast)$.

Both cases lead to a contradiction, so our assumption was false. Therefore $f(x)
+f''(x)=0$ for some real $x$; call it $y$.
