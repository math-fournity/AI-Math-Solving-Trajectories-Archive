$$X^2 + Y^2 = \frac{b^2(b+c)^2 + 2ab\cos\gamma\,(b+c)(a+c) + a^2(a+c)^2}{S^2}.$$

The linear terms give:
$$-pX + \frac{p\cos\gamma - q}{\sin\gamma}\,Y = \frac{1}{2S}\Big[-(a-b+c)\big(b\

The $\cos\gamma$-terms in the linear part **cancel exactly**, leaving:

using the law of cosines $c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos\gamma$.

**Computing $C$.** Expanding the bracket:
$$2(b+c)(a+c) + (a+b)S = (a+b+c)(a+b+2c) + 2ab = S(a+b+2c) + 2ab.$$

$$\cos\gamma = 0 \implies \boxed{\angle BCA = 90°}.$$
