## Proof

We seek the Taylor expansion of the map $f(A) = A^{-1}$ about the identity $I$,
through second order.

### Setup

Write a perturbation of the identity as $A = I + tX$, where $X$ is an arbitrary
direction matrix (in the space of $n \times n$ matrices) and $t \in \mathbb{R}$
is a small parameter. The Taylor expansion of $f$ at $I$ along direction $X$ is

$$f(I + tX) = f(I) + t\, Df_I(X) + \tfrac{t^2}{2}\, D^2f_I(X, X) + O(t^3),$$

where $Df_I$ and $D^2f_I$ are the first and second Fréchet derivatives.

### Key tool: the Neumann series

For $\|tX\| < 1$, the inverse admits the geometric (Neumann) series

$$(I + tX)^{-1} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} (-tX)^k = I - tX + t^2 X^2 - t^3 X^3 + \cd
ots.$$

This series is absolutely convergent for $\|tX\|<1$ and gives an exact represent
ation of $(I+tX)^{-1}$. Expanding it in powers of $t$ and matching against the T
aylor form yields the derivatives directly.

### First-order term

The coefficient of $t^1$ in the Neumann series is $-X$. Comparing with the Taylo
r form $t\, Df_I(X)$:

$$Df_I(X) = -X.$$

Equivalently, from the defining limit:
$$Df_I(X) = \lim_{t\to 0}\frac{(I+tX)^{-1} - I}{t} = \lim_{t\to 0}\frac{-tX + t^
2X^2 - \cdots}{t} = -X.$$

### Second-order term

The coefficient of $t^2$ in the Neumann series is $+X^2$. Comparing with the Tay
lor form $\tfrac{t^2}{2}\,D^2f_I(X,X)$:

$$\tfrac{1}{2}\,D^2f_I(X, X) = X^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad D^2f_I(X, X) = 2\,X
^2.$$

One may verify the bilinearity directly: the mixed second derivative is obtained
 from the coefficient of $st$ in $(I + sX + tY)^{-1}$. Using the Neumann series
with $B = sX + tY$:

$$(I + sX + tY)^{-1} = I - (sX+tY) + (sX+tY)^2 - \cdots.$$

The quadratic term is $(sX+tY)^2 = s^2X^2 + st(XY+YX) + t^2Y^2$, so the $st$-coe
fficient is $XY + YX$. Hence

$$D^2f_I(X, Y) = XY + YX,$$

which is symmetric in $X,Y$ (as required of a second derivative) and specializes
 to $D^2f_I(X,X) = 2X^2$, consistent with above.

### Assembling the expansion

Setting $H = A - I$ (so $X = H$, $t = 1$), the expansion through second order is

$$\boxed{\,A^{-1} \;=\; I \;-\; (A - I) \;+\; (A - I)^2 \;+\; O\!\big((A-I)^3\bi
g).\,}$$

In derivative form:
- **Zeroth order:** $f(I) = I$.
- **First order:** $Df_I(H) = -H$.
- **Second order:** $\tfrac{1}{2}D^2f_I(H, H) = H^2$.

These are precisely the first three terms of the Neumann series $A^{-1} = \sum_{
k\ge 0}(I-A)^k = I + (I-A) + (I-A)^2 + \cdots$, confirming consistency: $I - (A-
I) + (A-I)^2 = I + (I-A) + (I-A)^2$.
