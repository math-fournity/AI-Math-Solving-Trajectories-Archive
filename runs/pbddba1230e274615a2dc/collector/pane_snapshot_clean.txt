## Proof

Let $a = \tfrac12$ and let $\phi_a(z) = \dfrac{z-a}{1-\bar a\,z} = \dfrac{z-\tfr
ac12}{1-\tfrac12 z}$ be the disc automorphism sending $a$ to $0$.

**Step 1: Factor out the zero at $a$.** Since $f(a)=0$ and $f(\mathbb D)\subset\
mathbb D$, by the Schwarz lemma (composed with $\phi_a$) there exists a holomorp
hic $h:\mathbb D\to\overline{\mathbb D}$ with $|h|\le 1$ such that
$$f(z) = \phi_a(z)\,h(z).$$

**Step 2: Use the vanishing derivative.** Differentiating,
$$f'(z)=\phi_a'(z)\,h(z)+\phi_a(z)\,h'(z).$$
At $z=a$ we have $\phi_a(a)=0$, so $f'(a)=\phi_a'(a)\,h(a)$. A direct computatio
n gives
$$\phi_a'(z)=\frac{1-|a|^2}{(1-\bar a z)^2},\qquad \phi_a'(a)=\frac{1}{1-|a|^2}=
\frac{1}{1-\tfrac14}=\frac43.$$
The condition $f'(a)=0$ forces $h(a)=0$.

**Step 3: Factor out the second zero.** Applying the Schwarz lemma to $h$ (which
 vanishes at $a$ and maps into $\overline{\mathbb D}$), there exists holomorphic
 $k:\mathbb D\to\overline{\mathbb D}$ with $|k|\le 1$ such that
$$h(z)=\phi_a(z)\,k(z),\qquad\text{hence}\qquad f(z)=\phi_a(z)^2\,k(z).$$

**Step 4: Evaluate at $0$.** Since $\phi_a(0)=\dfrac{0-\tfrac12}{1}=-\tfrac12$,
$$f(0)=\phi_a(0)^2\,k(0)=\tfrac14\,k(0),\qquad |f(0)|=\tfrac14\,|k(0)|\le\tfrac1
4.$$

**Step 5: The bound is attained.** Take $k(z)\equiv e^{i\theta}$ (constant of mo
dulus $1$) and $f(z)=e^{i\theta}\phi_a(z)^2$. Then:
- $f(a)=0$ and $f'(a)=e^{i\theta}\cdot 2\phi_a(a)\phi_a'(a)=0$ ✓
- $|f(z)|=|\phi_a(z)|^2<1$ for $z\in\mathbb D$ ✓
- $|f(0)|=\tfrac14$ ✓

Therefore
$$\sup_{f\in\mathcal F}|f(0)|=\boxed{\dfrac14}.$$
