## Proof

We show that such a $T$ always exists. The argument is purely order-theoretic an
d does not require distributivity, complementedness, density, or even the regula

### Step 1: Reduction via coinitial sequences

Since $U$ and $V$ are chains in $L\setminus\{0\}$, we consider two cases.

**Case A: $U$ has a minimum element $u_0$.** Then $u_0 \leq u$ for all $u \in U$
. Set $T = \{u_0 \wedge v : v \in V\}$. Since $V$ is a chain, $T$ is a chain. By
 condition (3), each $u_0 \wedge v \neq 0$, so $T \subset L\setminus\{0\}$. We c
laim $\inf T = 0$: if $c > 0$ is a lower bound of $T$, then $c \leq u_0 \wedge v
$ for all $v \in V$, so $c \leq u_0$ and $c \leq v$ for all $v \in V$. Since $u_
0 = \min U$, $c \leq u$ for all $u \in U$. Thus $c$ is a lower bound of $U \cup
V$, giving $c \leq \inf(U\cup V) = 0$, a contradiction. So $\inf T = 0$ and $|T|
 \leq |V| \leq |U\cup V|$. The case where $V$ has a minimum is symmetric.

**Case B: Neither $U$ nor $V$ has a minimum.** Then both are infinite chains wit
h no least element.

Since $U$ is a chain with no minimum, it admits a **coinitial well-ordered decre
asing sequence**: there exists an ordinal $\lambda$ and a strictly decreasing se
quence $(u_\alpha)_{\alpha < \lambda}$ in $U$ such that for every $u \in U$, som

 $\lambda \leq |U|$. (This uses the Axiom of Choice; $\operatorname{ci}(U)$ is i
n fact a regular cardinal, though we only need $\lambda \leq |U|$.)

Similarly, $V$ admits a coinitial decreasing sequence $(v_\beta)_{\beta < \sigma
}$ with $\sigma = \operatorname{ci}(V) \leq |V|$.

### Step 2: Diagonal construction

Set $\theta = \max(\lambda, \sigma)$. Since $|U|$ and $|V|$ are infinite cardina
ls, $|U \cup V| = \max(|U|,|V|)$, so:
$$\theta = \max(\lambda, \sigma) \leq \max(|U|, |V|) = |U \cup V|.$$

Since $\lambda \leq \theta$ and $\sigma \leq \theta$, and $\operatorname{cf}(\la
mbda) \leq \lambda \leq \theta$ (similarly for $\sigma$), there exist **non-decr
easing cofinal maps** $f: \theta \to \lambda$ (meaning $\sup f[\theta] = \lambda
$) and $g: \theta \to \sigma$.

**Construction.** For each $\xi < \theta$, define:
$$w_\xi = u_{f(\xi)} \wedge v_{g(\xi)}, \qquad T = \{w_\xi : \xi < \theta\}.$$

### Step 3: Verification

**$T \subset L \setminus \{0\}$:** Since $u_{f(\xi)} \in U$ and $v_{g(\xi)} \in
V$, condition (3) gives $w_\xi = u_{f(\xi)} \wedge v_{g(\xi)} \neq 0$.

**$T$ is totally ordered:** For $\xi < \xi' < \theta$, since $f$ is non-decreasi

decreasing). Similarly $v_{g(\xi)} \geq v_{g(\xi')}$. Hence:
$$w_\xi = u_{f(\xi)} \wedge v_{g(\xi)} \geq u_{f(\xi')} \wedge v_{g(\xi')} = w_{
\xi'}.$$
So $T$ is a chain.

**$\inf T = 0$:** Suppose for contradiction that $c > 0$ is a lower bound of $T$

$\xi$. Since $f$ is cofinal in $\lambda$, for any $\alpha < \lambda$ there exist

$c \leq u_\alpha$ for all $\alpha < \lambda$.

Since $(u_\alpha)_{\alpha < \lambda}$ is coinitial in $U$, for any $u \in U$ the
re exists $\alpha$ with $u_\alpha \leq u$, so $c \leq u$. Thus **$c$ is a lower
bound of $U$**.

By the identical argument using $g$ and $(v_\beta)$, **$c$ is a lower bound of $
V$**.

Therefore $c$ is a lower bound of $U \cup V$, so $c \leq \inf(U \cup V) = 0$, co

Hence the only lower bound of $T$ that is $\geq 0$ is $0$ itself. Since $0$ is t

**$|T| \leq |U \cup V|$:** $|T| \leq \theta \leq |U \cup V|$.
