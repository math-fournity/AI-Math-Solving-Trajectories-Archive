## Proof

Let $F$ be a field (the phrase "field of prime elements" denotes a field, in par
ticular one whose prime subfield has prime characteristic; the argument below is
 characteristic-independent). Let $M = M_{n \times n}(F)$ be the full matrix alg
ebra, and let

$$S = \{A \in M : AX = XA \text{ for all } X \in M\}$$

be the **center** of $M$. We determine $\dim_F S$.

---

### Step 1. Scalar matrices lie in $S$.

For any $\lambda \in F$, the scalar matrix $\lambda I$ satisfies

$$(\lambda I)X = \lambda X = X(\lambda I)$$

for every $X \in M$. Hence $\{\lambda I : \lambda \in F\} \subseteq S$. This is
a 1-dimensional subspace (spanned by $I$), so $\dim_F S \geq 1$.

---

### Step 2. Every element of $S$ is scalar.

Let $A = (a_{ij}) \in S$. We use the standard matrix units $E_{kl}$ (the matrix
with $1$ in position $(k,l)$ and $0$ elsewhere), which form a basis of $M$. Sinc
e $A$ commutes with every $E_{kl}$, in particular $AE_{kl} = E_{kl}A$ for all $k
, l$.

Compute both sides. The product $AE_{kl}$ has every column zero except column $l
$, which equals column $k$ of $A$; explicitly $(AE_{kl})_{il} = a_{ik}$ and all
other entries $0$. The product $E_{kl}A$ has every row zero except row $k$, whic
h equals row $l$ of $A$; explicitly $(E_{kl}A)_{kj} = a_{lj}$ and all other entr
ies $0$.

Equality $AE_{kl} = E_{kl}A$ forces, comparing the $(k,l)$-entry:

- From $AE_{kl}$: entry $(k,l)$ is $a_{kk}$.
- From $E_{kl}A$: entry $(k,l)$ is $a_{ll}$.

So $a_{kk} = a_{ll}$ for **all** $k, l$. Thus all diagonal entries of $A$ are eq
ual to some common value $\lambda \in F$.

Now compare an off-diagonal entry. Take $k \neq l$ and look at entry $(k,l)$:
- From $AE_{kl}$: entry $(k,l)$ is $a_{kk} = \lambda$.
- From $E_{kl}A$: entry $(k,l)$ is $a_{ll} = \lambda$.

These agree, giving no new information. Instead, to force off-diagonal entries t
o vanish, use $E_{kl}$ with $k \neq l$ and examine entry $(i,j)$ with $(i,j) \ne
q (k,l)$:

Take $k \neq l$ and consider entry $(k, j)$ for $j \neq l$. From $AE_{kl}$ this
entry is $0$ (only column $l$ is nonzero). From $E_{kl}A$ this entry is $a_{lj}$
. Hence $a_{lj} = 0$ for all $j \neq l$. Since $l$ was arbitrary (with $k \neq l
$), for every row index $l$ and every $j \neq l$, $a_{lj} = 0$.

Therefore every off-diagonal entry of $A$ is zero, and $A = \lambda I$.

---

### Step 3. Conclusion.

We have shown $S = \{\lambda I : \lambda \in F\} = F \cdot I$, a 1-dimensional s
ubspace of $M_{n \times n}(F)$ (for every $n \geq 1$). Therefore

$$\boxed{\dim_F S = 1}.$$
