## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, such a self-adjoint extension $S: L^2 \to L^2$ exists.

**Setup.** Since $D \subset L^2$ is a finite-dimensional subspace, it is closed,
 and we have the orthogonal decomposition
$$L^2 = D \oplus D^\perp.$$
Let $P_D: L^2 \to D$ denote the orthogonal projection onto $D$. The operator $T:
 D \to D$ is self-adjoint on $D$, meaning $\langle Tf, g\rangle = \langle f, Tg\
rangle$ for all $f, g \in D$ (equivalently, $T$ is represented by a Hermitian ma
trix in any orthonormal basis of $D$).

**Construction.** Define $S: L^2 \to L^2$ by
$$Sf := T(P_D f), \qquad f \in L^2.$$
Since $P_D f \in D$ and $T: D \to D \subset L^2$, the map $S$ is well-defined on
 all of $L^2$. It is bounded (composition of the bounded projection $P_D$ with t
he bounded operator $T$ on the finite-dimensional space $D$), so $S$ is a bounde
d operator on $L^2$.

**$S$ extends $T$.** For $f \in D$, we have $P_D f = f$, hence
$$Sf = T(P_D f) = Tf.$$
So $S|_D = T$.

**$S$ is self-adjoint.** For any $f, g \in L^2$, write $f = f_D + f_\perp$ and $
g = g_D + g_\perp$ with $f_D, g_D \in D$ and $f_\perp, g_\perp \in D^\perp$. The
n $P_D f = f_D$ and $P_D g = g_D$, so
$$\langle Sf, g\rangle = \langle T f_D,\, g_D + g_\perp\rangle = \langle T f_D,
g_D\rangle + \langle T f_D, g_\perp\rangle.$$
Since $T f_D \in D$ and $g_\perp \in D^\perp$, the second term vanishes, giving
$$\langle Sf, g\rangle = \langle T f_D, g_D\rangle.$$
Using self-adjointness of $T$ on $D$,
$$\langle T f_D, g_D\rangle = \langle f_D, T g_D\rangle.$$
Now $\langle f_D, T g_D\rangle = \langle f_D + f_\perp, T g_D\rangle$ since $T g
_D \in D$ and $f_\perp \in D^\perp$. Therefore
$$\langle Sf, g\rangle = \langle f, T g_D\rangle = \langle f, T(P_D g)\rangle =
\langle f, Sg\rangle.$$
This holds for all $f, g \in L^2$, so $S$ is self-adjoint.

**Conclusion.** The operator $S = T \circ P_D$ is a bounded self-adjoint operato
r on $L^2$ that agrees with $T$ on $D$. Hence such an extension always exists. $
\blacksquare$
