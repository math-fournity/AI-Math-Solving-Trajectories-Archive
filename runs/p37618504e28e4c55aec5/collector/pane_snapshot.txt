## Proof

$$a^{2}+e^{2}+2ea\cos\theta=\bigl|a+e\,e^{i\theta}\bigr|^{2},$$
since $|a+e e^{i\theta}|^{2}=(a+e\cos\theta)^{2}+(e\sin\theta)^{2}=a^{2}+e^{2}+2
ae\cos\theta$. Hence
$$I(a)=2\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\ln\bigl|a+e\,e^{i\theta}\bigr|\,d\theta=2\int_{0}^{2\p
i}\ln\bigl|a+e\,e^{i\theta}\bigr|\,d\theta,$$
using $2\pi$-periodicity and evenness. Set
$$J(a)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\ln\bigl|a+e\,e^{i\theta}\bigr|\,d\theta,\qq
uad I(a)=4\pi\,J(a).$$

**Step 2 — Apply Jensen's formula.** Consider the entire function $f(z)=a+ez$, a

s $|z_{0}|=|a|/e$. Jensen's formula on the unit circle gives
$$J(a)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\ln\bigl|f(e^{i\theta})\bigr|\,d\theta=\ln|f
(0)|+\sum_{|z_{k}|<1}\ln\frac{1}{|z_{k}|},$$
where the sum runs over zeros strictly inside the unit disk.

**Case 1: $|a|<e$ (i.e. $-e<a<e$).** Then $|z_{0}|=|a|/e<1$, so the zero lies in
side:
$$J(a)=\ln|a|+\ln\frac{1}{|a|/e}=\ln|a|+\ln\frac{e}{|a|}=\ln|a|+\bigl(1-\ln|a|\b

Thus $I(a)=4\pi\ln e=4\pi$.

**Case 2: $|a|>e$.** Then $|z_{0}|=|a|/e>1$, the zero lies outside the unit disk

$$J(a)=\ln|a|.$$
Hence $I(a)=4\pi\ln|a|$.
