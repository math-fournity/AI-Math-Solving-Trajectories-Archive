## Proof

Let $X$ be a convex subset of $\mathbb{R}^2$ with two holes. Since $X$ is convex
 except for the two removed holes, it deformation retracts onto a wedge of two c
ircles (a figure-eight graph): pick a basepoint and draw two loops, one encircli
ng each hole. Concretely, retract $X$ onto a graph consisting of two simple clos
ed curves meeting at a single basepoint. Hence

$$X \simeq S^1 \vee S^1,$$

so

$$\pi_1(X) \cong \pi_1(S^1 \vee S^1) \cong F_2 = \langle a, b \rangle,$$

the free group on two generators, where $a$ is the class of a loop going once ar
ound the first hole and $b$ the class of a loop going once around the second hol
e.

The homotopy classes of based loops in $X$ are in bijection with the elements of
 $\pi_1(X) = F_2$. The free group $F_2$ is **infinite** (in fact countably infin
ite): its elements are all reduced words in $a, b, a^{-1}, b^{-1}$, and there ar
e words of every length, e.g. $a, a^2, a^3, \dots$ alone already give infinitely
 many distinct elements.

Now compare with the classes listed in the problem:

- **Trivial class**: the identity element $1$.
- **Loops around one hole**: the classes $a$ and $b$ (and their inverses $a^{-1}
, b^{-1}$, which wind in the opposite direction — already four distinct classes,
 not two).
- **Loops around both holes**: this corresponds to words like $ab$ or $ba$ (depe
nding on order), i.e. a single composite loop winding once around each.
- **Figure-eight loops**: this is essentially the same type as "loops around bot
h holes" — a loop tracing through both circles of the wedge, corresponding to a
word involving both $a$ and $b$.

These listed categories account for only finitely many elements of $F_2$ (essent
ially $1, a^{\pm1}, b^{\pm1}, ab, ba$, and a handful of composites). They do **n
ot** account for, for example:

- $a^2, a^3, a^4, \dots$ — loops winding twice, three times, four times, … aroun
d the first hole,
- $b^2, b^3, \dots$ — likewise for the second hole,
- $aba,\; bab,\; abab,\; a^2b,\; ab^2,\; aba^{-1},\; \dots$ — arbitrary reduced
words of length $\geq 3$.

Each reduced word gives a distinct element of $F_2$ (by the normal form theorem
for free groups), hence a distinct homotopy class. Since there are reduced words
 of every positive length, there are infinitely many such classes beyond those l
isted.

**Conclusion.** The number of distinct homotopy classes of loops is **infinite**
 (countably infinite, in bijection with $F_2$). The classes listed in the proble
m — trivial, around one hole, around both holes, figure-eight — are only finitel
y many of them. There are many additional classes, namely all the elements of th
e free group $F_2$ not among those listed, such as $a^2, b^2, abab, aba, bab, a^
3, \dots$, one for each reduced word in $\{a, b, a^{-1}, b^{-1}\}$ not equivalen
t to one already enumerated.
