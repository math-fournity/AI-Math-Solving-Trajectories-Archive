## Proof

**Answer: Yes.**

Consider the metric $d(x,y) = |x^3 - y^3|$ on $\mathbb{R}$, and take $a = 0$.

**$d$ is a metric.** The map $\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$, $\varphi(x)=x^3$
, is injective, so $d(x,y)=|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|$ is the pullback of the stand
ard metric through an injection. Hence:
- $d(x,y)\ge 0$, with $d(x,y)=0 \iff \varphi(x)=\varphi(y) \iff x=y$;
- symmetry $d(x,y)=d(y,x)$ is immediate;
- the triangle inequality follows from the ordinary triangle inequality:
$$d(x,z)=|\varphi(x)-\varphi(z)|\le |\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|+|\varphi(y)-\varphi(
z)|=d(x,y)+d(y,z).$$

**The function $f(x)=d(x,0)=|x^3|=|x|^3$ is differentiable everywhere.**

For $x>0$: $f(x)=x^3$, so $f'(x)=3x^2$.

For $x<0$: $x^3<0$, so $f(x)=|x^3|=-x^3$, giving $f'(x)=-3x^2$.

At $x=0$:
$$f'(0)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(h)-f(0)}{h}=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{|h|^3}{h}=\lim_{h\to
 0}|h|^2\cdot\frac{|h|}{h}=0,$$
since $|h|^2\to 0$ and $|h|/h$ is bounded.

Thus $f$ is differentiable at every point of $\mathbb{R}$ (with $f'(x)=3x^2\oper
atorname{sgn}(x)$ for $x\ne 0$ and $f'(0)=0$).

Therefore such a metric and point exist. $\blacksquare$
