### The N/C theorem (recall)

For any subgroup $H \le G$, conjugation by an element $n \in N_G(H)$ gives an au
tomorphism $\varphi_n \colon H \to H$, $\varphi_n(h) = n h n^{-1}$. The map

$$\varphi \colon N_G(H) \to \operatorname{Aut}(H), \qquad n \mapsto \varphi_n$$

is a group homomorphism with kernel $\ker\varphi = C_G(H)$. Hence by the First I
somorphism Theorem,

$$N_G(H)/C_G(H) \cong \varphi\bigl(N_G(H)\bigr) \le \operatorname{Aut}(H).$$

The image lands in $\operatorname{Aut}(H)$, **not** necessarily in $\operatornam
e{Inn}(H) \le \operatorname{Aut}(H)$.

### Counterexample

Let $G = S_4$ and let $H = V_4 = \{e,\,(12)(34),\,(13)(24),\,(14)(23)\}$ be the
Klein four-group.

**Step 1: $H$ is abelian, so $\operatorname{Inn}(H) = \{1\}$.**

Inner automorphisms are conjugations $h \mapsto x h x^{-1}$; since $H$ is abelia
n, every such conjugation is the identity. Thus $\operatorname{Inn}(H)$ is the t
rivial group.

**Step 2: $H \trianglelefteq S_4$, so $N_G(H) = S_4$.**

The Klein four-group $V_4$ is a well-known normal subgroup of $S_4$ (it is the u
nion of the identity and all products of two disjoint transpositions, and this s
et is closed under conjugation). Hence $N_{S_4}(V_4) = S_4$.

**Step 3: $C_G(H) = H$, so $N_G(H)/C_G(H) \cong S_3$.**

Conjugation gives a homomorphism $S_4 \to \operatorname{Aut}(V_4) \cong \operato
rname{GL}(2,\mathbb{F}_2) \cong S_3$ with kernel $C_{S_4}(V_4)$. Since $|S_4| =
24$ and $|\operatorname{Aut}(V_4)| = 6$, and the quotient $S_4/V_4 \cong S_3$ al
ready has order $6$, the kernel has order $|S_4|/6 = 4$. The only subgroup of $S
_4$ of order $4$ contained in $V_4$ (which is abelian, hence $V_4 \le C_{S_4}(V_
4)$) is $V_4$ itself. Therefore $C_{S_4}(V_4) = V_4 = H$, and

$$\frac{N_G(H)}{C_G(H)} = \frac{S_4}{V_4} \cong S_3,$$

which has order $6$.

**Step 4: Contradiction.**

If $N_G(H)/C_G(H)$ were isomorphic to a subgroup of $\operatorname{Inn}(H)$, the
n $S_3$ (order $6$) would embed into the trivial group $\operatorname{Inn}(H) =
\{1\}$ (order $1$), which is impossible.

### Conclusion

The factor group $N_G(H)/C_G(H)$ is **not** always isomorphic to a subgroup of $
\operatorname{Inn}(H)$. The N/C theorem guarantees only an embedding into $\oper

l) while $N_G(H)/C_G(H)$ is nontrivial—as in the example $G = S_4$, $H = V_4$ ab
ove—the embedding into $\operatorname{Inn}(H)$ fails.
