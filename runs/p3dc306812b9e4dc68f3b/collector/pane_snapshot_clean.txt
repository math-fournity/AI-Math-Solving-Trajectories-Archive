## Proof

**Claim:** Not every graph arises as the commutativity graph of a group.

**Key observation.** Let $H$ be any group with identity $e$. Since $ey = ye$ for
 every $y \in H$, the identity $e$ commutes with every element of $H$. In the co
mmutativity graph of $H$, this means $e$ is a **universal vertex**: it is relate
d (via $\bot$) to every vertex, including itself.

Therefore:

> **Necessary condition.** If a graph $(S, \bot)$ is the commutativity graph of
some group, then $S$ must contain a universal vertex — an element $u \in S$ such
 that $u \bot s$ for all $s \in S$.

**Counterexample.** Consider the set $S = \{a, b\}$ equipped with the relation
$$
\bot \;=\; \{(a,a),\; (b,b)\}.
$$
This relation is:
- **Reflexive:** $(a,a) \in \bot$ and $(b,b) \in \bot$.
- **Symmetric:** the only off-diagonal pairs would be $(a,b)$ and $(b,a)$, neith
er of which is in $\bot$, so symmetry holds vacuously.

Thus $(S, \bot)$ is a valid graph under the given definition. However, it has **
no universal vertex**: $a$ is not related to $b$, and $b$ is not related to $a$.

**Suppose for contradiction** that $(S, \bot)$ is the commutativity graph of som
e group $G$ with underlying set $S = \{a, b\}$. Then $G$ is a group of order 2,
so it has a unique identity element $e \in \{a, b\}$. By the observation above,
$e$ must commute with every element of $G$, meaning $e \bot x$ for both $x \in S
$. In particular, $e$ is related to the other (non-identity) element. But in our
 graph, $a \not\bot b$, so neither $a$ nor $b$ is universal — contradicting the
requirement that the identity be universal.

(Equivalently: the only group of order 2 is $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, which is a
belian, so its commutativity graph is the complete graph $\{(a,a),(a,b),(b,a),(b
,b)\}$, not our graph.)

**Conclusion.** The graph $(\{a,b\}, \{(a,a),(b,b)\})$ is reflexive and symmetri
c but cannot be realized as the commutativity graph of any group, because every
commutativity graph must contain a universal vertex (the identity), and this gra
ph does not.

Therefore, **not every graph arises as the commutativity graph of some group.**
$\blacksquare$
