**Answer: Yes.** If all zeros of a monic polynomial $f$ are real, then all zeros
 of $f'$ are also real.

## Proof

Let $f(z) = \prod_{i=1}^{n}(z - r_i)$ be a monic polynomial of degree $n \geq 1$
, where each $r_i \in \mathbb{R}$ (zeros listed with multiplicity). We prove tha
t every zero of $f'$ is real.

We use the **Gauss–Lucas theorem**: the zeros of $f'$ lie in the convex hull of
the zeros of $f$.

### Proof of Gauss–Lucas (specialized)

Write $f(z) = \prod_{i=1}^{n}(z-r_i)$. Then
$$\frac{f'(z)}{f(z)} = \sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{z-r_i}.$$

Suppose $z_0$ is a zero of $f'$ with $f(z_0)\neq 0$ (i.e., $z_0$ is not also a z
ero of $f$, hence not one of the $r_i$). Then
$$0 = f'(z_0) = f(z_0)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{z_0-r_i},$$
so
$$\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{z_0-r_i} = 0.$$

Taking complex conjugates and using $\overline{r_i}=r_i$ (since $r_i\in\mathbb{R
}$):
$$\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\overline{z_0}-r_i} = 0.$$

Now multiply the original equation by $\overline{z_0}$... more directly, rewrite
 each term:
$$\frac{1}{z_0-r_i} = \frac{\overline{z_0}-r_i}{|z_0-r_i|^2}.$$

So
$$0 = \sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\overline{z_0}-r_i}{|z_0-r_i|^2} = \overline{z_0}\sum_
{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{|z_0-r_i|^2} - \sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{r_i}{|z_0-r_i|^2}.$$

Let $w_i = \frac{1}{|z_0-r_i|^2} > 0$ (positive real weights). Then
$$\overline{z_0}\sum w_i = \sum w_i r_i.$$

Since $\sum w_i > 0$ and each $r_i \in \mathbb{R}$, the right side $\sum w_i r_i
$ is **real**. Therefore $\overline{z_0}\sum w_i$ is real, which forces $\overli
ne{z_0}$ (hence $z_0$) to be real.

### Handling the case $f(z_0)=0$

If $z_0$ is a zero of both $f$ and $f'$, then $z_0 = r_j$ for some $j$, which is
 real by assumption. So this case is already covered.

### Conclusion

Every zero of $f'$ is either a (real) zero of $f$, or a point $z_0$ with $f(z_0)
\neq 0$ which the argument above shows must be real. Hence **all zeros of $f'$ a
re real**. $\blacksquare$
