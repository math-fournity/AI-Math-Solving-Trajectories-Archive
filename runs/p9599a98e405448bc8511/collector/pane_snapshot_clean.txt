## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $I = (0)$.

**Setup.** Let $\pi: R \to R/I$ be the canonical quotient map (a surjective ring
 homomorphism with $\ker \pi = I$), and let $\varphi: R/I \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}
 R$ be the given ring isomorphism. Define
$$f := \varphi \circ \pi : R \longrightarrow R.$$
Then $f$ is a **surjective** ring endomorphism of $R$ with $\ker f = I$. It suff
ices to prove:

> **Lemma.** Every surjective ring endomorphism of a commutative Noetherian ring
 (with unity) is injective.

**Proof of Lemma.** Let $f: R \to R$ be a surjective ring homomorphism with $f(1
)=1$. For each $n \geq 0$ set
$$K_n := \ker(f^n), \qquad n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$$
Each $K_n$ is an ideal of $R$ (since $f^n$ is a ring homomorphism). Moreover $K_
n \subseteq K_{n+1}$: if $f^n(x)=0$ then $f^{n+1}(x)=f(f^n(x))=f(0)=0$.

Since $R$ is **Noetherian**, the ascending chain of ideals
$$K_0 \subseteq K_1 \subseteq K_2 \subseteq \cdots$$
stabilizes: there exists $N \geq 0$ such that $K_N = K_{N+1}$.

Because $f$ is surjective, every iterate $f^n$ is surjective; in particular $f^N
$ is surjective. Now take any $x \in K_1 = \ker(f)$. By surjectivity of $f^N$, t
here exists $y \in R$ with $f^N(y) = x$. Then
$$f^{N+1}(y) = f\!\big(f^N(y)\big) = f(x) = 0,$$
so $y \in K_{N+1} = K_N$. Therefore
$$x = f^N(y) = 0.$$
Since $x \in K_1$ was arbitrary, $K_1 = \ker(f) = (0)$, i.e., $f$ is injective.
$\blacksquare$

**Conclusion.** Applying the lemma to $f = \varphi \circ \pi$, we obtain $\ker(f
) = (0)$. But $\ker(f) = \ker(\pi) = I$. Hence $I = (0)$.
