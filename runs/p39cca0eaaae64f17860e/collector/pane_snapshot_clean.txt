## Proof

**Claim.** There exists a simple group that is non co-hopfian.

**Construction.** Let $\Omega = \{1, 2, 3, \dots\}$ be a countably infinite set,
 and let $G = A_\infty$ be the **finitary alternating group** on $\Omega$: the g
roup of all even permutations of $\Omega$ with finite support. We show that $G$
is simple and admits an injective, non-surjective endomorphism.

---

### Step 1. $A_\infty$ is simple.

This is a classical result. We recall the argument. $A_\infty$ is generated by a
ll 3-cycles $(a\ b\ c)$ with $a,b,c \in \Omega$ distinct. Let $N \trianglelefteq
 A_\infty$ be a nontrivial normal subgroup, and pick $\sigma \in N$, $\sigma \ne
q \mathrm{id}$. Then $\sigma$ moves some point $a \in \Omega$; say $\sigma(a) =
b \neq a$. Since $\sigma$ has finite support, there exists $c \in \Omega$ with $
c \notin \{a, b\}$ and $c$ not in the support of $\sigma$ (so $\sigma(c)=c$). Co
nsider the 3-cycle $\tau = (a\ b\ c) \in A_\infty$ and the commutator
$$[\sigma, \tau] = \sigma^{-1}\tau^{-1}\sigma\tau \in N.$$
A direct computation shows $[\sigma,\tau]$ is a nontrivial element of $N$ whose
support is strictly smaller than that of $\sigma$. Repeating this reduction, we
eventually obtain a 3-cycle in $N$. Once $N$ contains a single 3-cycle $(a\ b\ c
)$, normality and the fact that $A_\infty$ acts transitively on ordered triples
of distinct elements (via 3-cycles, which are even) imply $N$ contains every 3-c
ycle, hence $N = A_\infty$. Therefore $A_\infty$ is simple. $\square$

---

### Step 2. $A_\infty$ is non co-hopfian.

Define $\varphi: G \to G$ as follows. For $\pi \in A_\infty$, let $\varphi(\pi)$
 be the permutation of $\Omega$ given by
$$\varphi(\pi)(1) = 1, \qquad \varphi(\pi)(n) = \pi(n-1)+1 \;\text{ for } n \geq
 2.$$

**$\varphi(\pi) \in A_\infty$:** The support of $\varphi(\pi)$ is $\{m+1 : m \in
 \operatorname{supp}(\pi)\}$, which is finite. On $\{2,3,\dots\}$, the map $\var
phi(\pi)$ is conjugate to $\pi$ via the shift $s: \Omega \to \Omega\setminus\{1\
}$, $s(k)=k+1$ (i.e., $\varphi(\pi)|_{\{2,3,\dots\}} = s \circ \pi \circ s^{-1}$
), so it is an even permutation there; combined with fixing $1$, $\varphi(\pi)$
is even. Hence $\varphi(\pi) \in A_\infty$.

**$\varphi$ is a homomorphism:** For $\pi, \rho \in G$ and $n \geq 2$,
$$\varphi(\pi\rho)(n) = (\pi\rho)(n-1)+1 = \pi(\rho(n-1))+1,$$
$$\varphi(\pi)\bigl(\varphi(\rho)(n)\bigr) = \varphi(\pi)\bigl(\rho(n-1)+1\bigr)
 = \pi\bigl(\rho(n-1)+1-1\bigr)+1 = \pi(\rho(n-1))+1.$$
For $n=1$ both sides give $1$. So $\varphi(\pi\rho)=\varphi(\pi)\varphi(\rho)$.

**$\varphi$ is injective:** If $\varphi(\pi)=\mathrm{id}$, then for all $n\geq 2
$, $\pi(n-1)+1 = n$, so $\pi(k)=k$ for all $k\geq 1$; thus $\pi = \mathrm{id}$.

**$\varphi$ is not surjective:** Every element in the image of $\varphi$ fixes t
he point $1$. But the 3-cycle $(1\ 2\ 3) \in A_\infty$ does not fix $1$, so $(1\
 2\ 3) \notin \varphi(G)$. Hence $\varphi(G) \subsetneq G$.

---

### Conclusion.

$G = A_\infty$ is a simple group (Step 1) and $\varphi: G \to G$ is an injective
 endomorphism that is not surjective (Step 2). Therefore $G$ is a simple group t
hat is non co-hopfian. $\blacksquare$
