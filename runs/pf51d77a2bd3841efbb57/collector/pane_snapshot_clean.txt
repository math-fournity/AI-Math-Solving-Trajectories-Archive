**Claim.** The series
$$\sum_{n=N}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n\,(\log n)\,(\log\log n)\cdots(\log_{j} n)\,(\lo
g_{j+1} n)^{p}}$$
converges if and only if $p > 1$.

---

**Proof.**

Choose $N$ large enough that all the iterated logarithms $\log n, \log\log n, \d
ots, \log_{j+1} n$ are defined and positive for $n \ge N$. Define
$$f(x) = \frac{1}{x\,(\log x)\,(\log\log x)\cdots(\log_{j} x)\,(\log_{j+1} x)^{p
}}.$$

For $x \ge N$, $f$ is positive, continuous, and (eventually) decreasing, since e
ach factor in the denominator is positive and increasing. By the **integral test
**, the series converges if and only if the improper integral
$$\int_{N}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx$$
converges.

**Substitution.** Introduce the iterated logarithms as a chain of variables:
$$t_{0} = x,\quad t_{1} = \log x,\quad t_{2} = \log\log x,\quad \dots,\quad t_{k
+1} = \log t_{k},\quad \dots,\quad t_{j+1} = \log_{j+1} x.$$

The differentials satisfy the telescoping relation
$$dt_{k+1} = \frac{dt_{k}}{t_{k}} \qquad (k = 0, 1, \dots, j).$$

Unrolling this recursion,
$$dt_{j+1} = \frac{dt_{j}}{t_{j}} = \frac{dt_{j-1}}{t_{j-1}\,t_{j}} = \cdots = \
frac{dt_{0}}{t_{0}\,t_{1}\,\cdots\,t_{j}} = \frac{dx}{x\,(\log x)\,(\log\log x)\
cdots(\log_{j} x)}.$$

Therefore the integrand transforms as
$$f(x)\,dx = \frac{dx}{x\,(\log x)\cdots(\log_{j} x)\,(\log_{j+1} x)^{p}} = \fra
c{dt_{j+1}}{t_{j+1}^{\,p}}.$$

Setting $u = t_{j+1} = \log_{j+1} x$, the integral becomes
$$\int_{N}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx = \int_{\log_{j+1} N}^{\infty} \frac{du}{u^{p}}.$$

As $x \to \infty$, each iterated logarithm $t_{k}(x) \to \infty$, so in particul
ar $u = \log_{j+1} x \to \infty$. Thus we are reduced to the $p$-integral
$$\int_{a}^{\infty} \frac{du}{u^{p}}, \qquad a = \log_{j+1} N > 0,$$
which converges if and only if $p > 1$ (diverging logarithmically when $p = 1$ a
nd as a power when $p < 1$).

By the integral test, the original series shares the same convergence behavior.
Hence the series converges **if and only if $p > 1$**. $\blacksquare$
