## Galois Group of $X^3 - X - t$ over $\mathbb{C}(t)$

We view $f(X) = X^3 - X - t$ as a polynomial in $X$ over the field $K = \mathbb{
C}(t)$ (with $\mathbb{C}[t] \subset \mathbb{C}(t)$). Since $\operatorname{char}
K = 0$, the polynomial is separable. We determine $\operatorname{Gal}(f/K)$ in t
wo steps: irreducibility, then the discriminant test.

### Step 1. Irreducibility over $\mathbb{C}(t)$

Suppose for contradiction that $f$ has a root $r(t) \in \mathbb{C}(t)$. Write $r
 = p/q$ with $p, q \in \mathbb{C}[t]$ coprime. The equation $r^3 - r = t$ gives

$$p^3 - p q^2 = t\, q^3, \qquad\text{i.e.,}\qquad p\,(p^2 - q^2) = t\, q^3.$$

Since $\gcd(p,q)=1$, we have $\gcd(p, q^3)=1$, so $p \mid t$ in $\mathbb{C}[t]$.
 Hence $p$ is either a constant $c$ or $p = ct$ for some $c \in \mathbb{C}^\time
s$.

**Case $p = c$ (constant).** Then $c(c^2 - q^2) = t\,q^3$. If $q$ is constant, t
he RHS is linear in $t$ while the LHS is constant — contradiction. If $q$ is non
constant of degree $d \geq 1$, then $\deg(\text{LHS}) \leq 2d$ while $\deg(\text
{RHS}) = 1 + 3d > 2d$ — contradiction.

**Case $p = ct$.** Then $ct(c^2 t^2 - q^2) = t q^3$, so $c(c^2 t^2 - q^2) = q^3$
, i.e. $c^3 t^2 = q^2(c + q)$. Coprimality $\gcd(p,q)=1$ with $p = ct$ gives $\g
cd(q, t) = 1$, so $q^2 \mid c^3$, forcing $q$ to be a constant $d$. Then $c^3 t^
2 = d^2(c+d)$, where the LHS has degree $2$ in $t$ and the RHS is constant — con
tradiction.

Both cases are impossible, so $f$ has no root in $\mathbb{C}(t)$. A cubic with n

### Step 2. Discriminant

$$\Delta = -4(-1)^3 - 27(-t)^2 = 4 - 27t^2.$$

We check whether $\Delta$ is a square in $\mathbb{C}(t)$. A rational function is
