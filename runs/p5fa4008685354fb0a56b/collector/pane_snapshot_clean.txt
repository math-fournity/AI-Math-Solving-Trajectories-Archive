## Problem

Determine the domain of the self-adjoint extension of the Laplace-Beltrami opera
tor $-\Delta$ on $L^2(M)$, where $M$ is a complete Riemannian manifold.

---

## Proof

**Answer.** On a complete Riemannian manifold $M$, the operator $-\Delta$ is **e
ssentially self-adjoint** on $C_c^\infty(M)$. Its unique self-adjoint extension
has domain

$$D\!\left(-\overline{\Delta}\right) \;=\; \bigl\{\, f \in L^2(M) \;:\; \Delta f
 \in L^2(M) \text{ in the sense of distributions}\,\bigr\}.$$

We prove this in several steps.

---

### Step 1 — Setup and symmetry

Let $A_0 := -\Delta\big|_{C_c^\infty(M)}$ with domain $D(A_0) = C_c^\infty(M)$,
which is dense in $L^2(M)$. For $f, g \in C_c^\infty(M)$, Green's identity (no b
oundary terms, since $f,g$ are compactly supported) gives

$$\langle A_0 f,\, g\rangle_{L^2} = \int_M (-\Delta f)\,\bar g\, dV = \int_M \la
ngle \nabla f, \nabla \bar g\rangle\, dV = \int_M f\,(-\Delta \bar g)\, dV = \la
ngle f,\, A_0 g\rangle_{L^2}.$$

Hence $A_0$ is **symmetric** and **non-negative** ($\langle A_0 f, f\rangle = \|
\nabla f\|^2 \geq 0$).

---

### Step 2 — The adjoint is the maximal operator

The adjoint $A_0^*$ has domain

$$D(A_0^*) = \bigl\{u \in L^2(M) : \exists\, v \in L^2(M),\; \langle -\Delta\var
phi,\, u\rangle = \langle \varphi,\, v\rangle \;\;\forall\,\varphi \in C_c^\inft
y(M)\bigr\}.$$

The condition $\langle -\Delta\varphi, u\rangle = \langle \varphi, v\rangle$ for
 all $\varphi \in C_c^\infty$ is exactly the statement $-\Delta u = v$ **distrib
utionally**. Therefore

$$D(A_0^*) = \bigl\{u \in L^2(M) : \Delta u \in L^2(M) \text{ distributionally}\
bigr\}, \qquad A_0^* u = -\Delta u.$$

We call this the **maximal operator** $A_1$. Clearly $A_0 \subseteq A_1$.

---

### Step 3 — Cutoff functions (using completeness)

Fix $p \in M$ and let $r(x) = d(x,p)$. By the Hopf–Rinow theorem, completeness i
mplies that closed metric balls $\overline{B(p,R)}$ are compact. Choose $\psi \i
n C^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ with $\psi \equiv 1$ on $(-\infty, 1]$, $\psi \equiv 0$
on $[2,\infty)$, $0 \leq \psi \leq 1$, $|\psi'| \leq 2$. Define

$$\chi_n(x) := \psi\!\left(\frac{r(x)}{n}\right).$$

The function $r$ is Lipschitz with $|\nabla r| = 1$ a.e. (away from the cut locu
s and $p$, which has measure zero), so $\chi_n$ is Lipschitz with compact suppor
t in $\overline{B(p,2n)}$, and

$$\chi_n \equiv 1 \text{ on } B(p,n), \qquad |\nabla \chi_n| \leq \frac{2}{n} \q
uad \text{a.e.}$$

These are the only properties we need. (Lipschitz cutoffs suffice for weak integ
ration by parts; equivalently, one smooth-approximates $\chi_n$.)

---

### Step 4 — Key Lemma: the energy identity on the maximal domain

**Lemma.** *If $u \in D(A_1)$, i.e., $u \in L^2(M)$ and $\Delta u \in L^2(M)$ di
stributionally, then $\nabla u \in L^2(M)$ and*

$$\int_M |\nabla u|^2\, dV = \langle u,\, -\Delta u\rangle_{L^2}.$$

*Proof.* (We take $u$ real; the complex case follows by applying to $\operatorna
me{Re}u$ and $\operatorname{Im}u$.) Since $\chi_n$ has compact support and $u \i
n D(A_1)$, weak integration by parts gives

$$\int_M \chi_n^2\, |\nabla u|^2\, dV \;=\; -\int_M \chi_n^2\, u\, \Delta u\, dV
 \;-\; 2\int_M \chi_n\, u\, \langle \nabla \chi_n,\, \nabla u\rangle\, dV. \tag{
$\ast$}$$

Set $a_n := \int_M \chi_n^2 |\nabla u|^2\, dV \geq 0$ and $b_n := \bigl(\int_M |
\nabla\chi_n|^2\, |u|^2\, dV\bigr)^{1/2}$. Since $|\nabla\chi_n| \leq 2/n$ and $
\operatorname{supp}(\nabla\chi_n) \subset B(p,2n)$:

$$b_n \;\leq\; \frac{2}{n}\,\|u\|_{L^2} \;\longrightarrow\; 0.$$

By Cauchy–Schwarz, the second term in $(\ast)$ is bounded by $2\sqrt{a_n}\, b_n$
. Thus

$$a_n \;\leq\; \Bigl|-\!\int_M \chi_n^2\, u\,\Delta u\, dV\Bigr| + 2\sqrt{a_n}\,
 b_n.$$

By dominated convergence ($|\chi_n^2 u\,\Delta u| \leq |u\,\Delta u| \in L^1$ vi
a Cauchy–Schwarz), the first term converges to $|\langle u, -\Delta u\rangle|$.
Since $b_n \to 0$, this shows $\{a_n\}$ is bounded, with $\limsup a_n \leq \lang
le u, -\Delta u\rangle$. By Fatou's lemma ($\chi_n^2 \to 1$ pointwise):

$$\int_M |\nabla u|^2\, dV \;\leq\; \liminf_{n\to\infty} a_n \;\leq\; \langle u,
 -\Delta u\rangle \;<\; \infty.$$

So $\nabla u \in L^2(M)$. Now that $|\nabla u|^2 \in L^1$, dominated convergence
 gives $a_n \to \int_M |\nabla u|^2\, dV$, and the second term in $(\ast)$ satis
fies $|2\int \chi_n u\langle\nabla\chi_n,\nabla u\rangle| \leq 2\sqrt{a_n}\, b_n
 \to 0$. Taking $n\to\infty$ in $(\ast)$:

$$\int_M |\nabla u|^2\, dV = \langle u,\, -\Delta u\rangle_{L^2}. \qquad \blacks
quare$$

---

### Step 5 — Deficiency indices are zero

To prove essential self-adjointness, we show $\ker(A_0^* - iI) = \{0\}$ and $\ke
r(A_0^* + iI) = \{0\}$.

**Case $A_0^* u = iu$:** Then $u \in D(A_1)$ and $-\Delta u = iu$. By the Lemma:

$$\int_M |\nabla u|^2\, dV = \langle u,\, -\Delta u\rangle = \langle u,\, iu\ran
gle = i\,\|u\|_{L^2}^2.$$

The left-hand side is **real and non-negative**; the right-hand side is **purely
 imaginary**. This forces $\|u\|_{L^2}^2 = 0$, hence $u = 0$.

**Case $A_0^* u = -iu$:** Then $-\Delta u = -iu$, and

$$\int_M |\nabla u|^2\, dV = \langle u,\, -\Delta u\rangle = \langle u,\, -iu\ra
ngle = -i\,\|u\|_{L^2}^2,$$

again real $=$ purely imaginary, forcing $u = 0$.

Both deficiency spaces are trivial, so **$A_0$ is essentially self-adjoint** on
$C_c^\infty(M)$.

---

### Step 6 — The domain of the self-adjoint extension

Since $A_0$ is essentially self-adjoint, its closure $\overline{A_0}$ is the uni
que self-adjoint extension, and $\overline{A_0} = A_0^* = A_1$. Therefore the do
main of the self-adjoint extension of $-\Delta$ is:

$$\boxed{D\!\left(-\overline{\Delta}\right) = \bigl\{\, f \in L^2(M) : \Delta f
\in L^2(M) \text{ in the sense of distributions}\,\bigr\}.}$$

**Additional properties of this domain:**

- By **elliptic regularity**, every $f$ in this domain belongs to $H^2_{\mathrm{
loc}}(M)$ (locally Sobolev $H^2$).
- By the **Key Lemma**, every $f$ in this domain also satisfies $\nabla f \in L^
2(M)$ with the energy identity $\|\nabla f\|_{L^2}^2 = \langle f, -\Delta f\rang
le_{L^2}$.
- This domain coincides with the closure of $C_c^\infty(M)$ under the **graph no
rm** $\|f\|_{L^2} + \|\Delta f\|_{L^2}$.
- On $M = \mathbb{R}^n$ with the Euclidean metric, this recovers the classical S
obolev space $H^2(\mathbb{R}^n)$.

The essential point is that **completeness of $M$** is what forces the minimal o
perator (closure of $-\Delta$ on $C_c^\infty$) to equal the maximal operator (di
stributional Laplacian on $L^2$), via the existence of cutoff functions with $|\
nabla \chi_n| \to 0$—a property that fails on incomplete manifolds, where bounda
ry conditions at the "missing" boundary must be specified, leading to multiple s
elf-adjoint extensions.
