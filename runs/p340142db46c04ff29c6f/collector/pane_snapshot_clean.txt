## Solution

We determine all tempered distributions $T \in \mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R})$ satisfy
ing $x\,T = 1$, where the right-hand side is the regular distribution associated
 to the constant function $1$.

### Step 1. A particular solution: the principal value

Recall the principal value distribution
$$
\operatorname{p.v.}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{x}\right) \in \mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}), \qq
uad
\left\langle \operatorname{p.v.}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{x}\right), \varphi \right\rang
le
= \lim_{\varepsilon \to 0^+} \int_{|x|>\varepsilon} \frac{\varphi(x)}{x}\,dx .
$$
For every test function $\varphi$,
$$
\left\langle x\cdot \operatorname{p.v.}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{x}\right), \varphi \rig
ht\rangle
= \left\langle \operatorname{p.v.}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{x}\right), x\,\varphi \right
\rangle
= \lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^+} \int_{|x|>\varepsilon} \frac{x\,\varphi(x)}{x}\,dx
= \int_{\mathbb{R}} \varphi(x)\,dx
= \langle 1, \varphi \rangle .
$$
Hence $x\cdot \operatorname{p.v.}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{x}\right) = 1$, so $\operator
name{p.v.}(1/x)$ is a particular solution (and it is tempered since $1/x$ is loc
ally integrable in the p.v. sense and decays appropriately).

### Step 2. The homogeneous equation $xS = 0$

Suppose $T_1, T_2$ both solve $xT = 1$. Then $S := T_1 - T_2$ satisfies $xS = 0$
. We claim $S = c\,\delta_0$ for some constant $c$.

**Support of $S$.** For any $\varphi$ with $\operatorname{supp}\varphi \subset \
mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}$, the function $\psi(x) := \varphi(x)/x$ is a valid test
 function with the same support, and
$$
\langle S, \varphi \rangle = \langle S, x\,\psi \rangle = \langle xS, \psi \rang
le = 0 .
$$
Thus $\operatorname{supp} S \subseteq \{0\}$.

**Structure of point-supported distributions.** Any distribution supported at $\
{0\}$ is a finite linear combination of derivatives of the Dirac delta:
$$
S = \sum_{k=0}^{N} c_k\, \delta_0^{(k)} .
$$

**Applying $xS = 0$.** Using the identity $x\,\delta_0^{(k)} = -k\,\delta_0^{(k-
1)}$ (with $\delta_0^{(-1)} := 0$), which follows from $\langle x\,\delta_0^{(k)
}, \varphi\rangle = \langle \delta_0^{(k)}, x\varphi\rangle = -(x\varphi)^{(k)}\
big|_{0} = -k\,\varphi^{(k-1)}(0) = \langle -k\,\delta_0^{(k-1)}, \varphi\rangle
$:
$$
0 = xS = \sum_{k=0}^{N} c_k\, x\,\delta_0^{(k)} = \sum_{k=1}^{N} (-k\,c_k)\,\del
ta_0^{(k-1)} = \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} \bigl(-(j+1)\,c_{j+1}\bigr)\,\delta_0^{(j)} .
$$
Linear independence of $\{\delta_0^{(j)}\}_{j\geq 0}$ forces $(j+1)\,c_{j+1} = 0
$ for every $j = 0,\dots,N-1$, hence $c_1 = c_2 = \cdots = c_N = 0$. Therefore $
S = c_0\,\delta_0$.

### Step 3. Verification and conclusion

Every distribution of the form $T = \operatorname{p.v.}(1/x) + c\,\delta_0$ sati
sfies
$$
xT = x\cdot \operatorname{p.v.}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{x}\right) + c\, x\,\delta_0 = 1
 + 0 = 1 ,
$$
and is tempered (both $\operatorname{p.v.}(1/x)$ and $\delta_0$ are tempered). C
onversely, by Steps 1–2, any two solutions differ by $c\,\delta_0$.

### Final Answer

The set of all tempered distribution solutions of $x\,T = 1$ is
$$
\boxed{\,T = \operatorname{p.v.}\!\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) + c\,\delta_0, \qquad
 c \in \mathbb{C}\, .}
$$
