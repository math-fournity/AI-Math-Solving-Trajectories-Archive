**Answer: Yes.** Under the stated hypotheses, $C^\infty(\bar U)$ is dense in $W^
{k,p}(U)$.

---

**Proof.**

The key geometric input is the *segment property*, which we verify for piecewise
 $C^1$ boundaries, and the key analytic input is continuity of translations in $
L^p$ for $p<\infty$. The argument never requires flattening the boundary (which
would need $C^k$ regularity for $W^{k,p}$); instead we *shift* the function into
 the interior and then mollify. This works for all $k$ simultaneously.

---

**Step 1. The segment property.**

We say $\partial U$ has the *segment property* if there exist finitely many open
 sets $V_1,\dots,V_N$ covering $\partial U$ and nonzero vectors $y^1,\dots,y^N$
such that

$$x + t\,y^j \in U \quad\text{for all } x\in \bar U\cap V_j,\; t\in(0,1),\; j=1,
\dots,N.$$

*Claim.* A bounded open set with piecewise $C^1$ boundary satisfies the segment
property.

*Justification.* At any point of a smooth piece of $\partial U$, the boundary is
 locally a $C^1$ graph and the inward unit normal $y$ satisfies $x+ty\in U$ for
all sufficiently small $t>0$, uniformly in a neighborhood (by continuity of the
normal and compactness of the local piece). At a junction of two smooth pieces (
a corner/edge), the two one-sided inward normals span a cone of directions point
ing into $U$; choosing any vector in the interior of this cone gives $x+ty\in U$
 for small $t>0$ uniformly near the junction. (The standard meaning of "piecewis
e $C^1$" excludes cusp-type tangential meetings, so such a cone is nonempty.) Si
nce $\bar U$ is compact, finitely many such neighborhoods and directions suffice
. $\square$

---

**Step 2. Partition of unity.**

Choose $V_0\subset\subset U$ so that $\{V_0,V_1,\dots,V_N\}$ covers $\bar U$. Le
t $\{\zeta_j\}_{j=0}^N$ be a smooth partition of unity subordinate to this cover
: $\zeta_j\in C_c^\infty(V_j)$, $0\le\zeta_j\le 1$, $\sum_{j=0}^N \zeta_j = 1$ o
n $\bar U$.

Set $u_j := u\,\zeta_j$, so $u = \sum_{j=0}^N u_j$ and $u_j\in W^{k,p}(U)$ with
$\operatorname{supp}(u_j)\subset V_j\cap\bar U$.

---

**Step 3. Interior patch ($j=0$).**

Since $\operatorname{supp}(u_0)\subset V_0\subset\subset U$, extending $u_0$ by
zero to $\mathbb R^n$ gives $\tilde u_0\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb R^n)$. Mollify:

$$v_0^\epsilon := \eta_\epsilon * \tilde u_0 \;\in\; C^\infty(\mathbb R^n).$$

By standard mollifier estimates, $v_0^\epsilon\to \tilde u_0$ in $W^{k,p}(\mathb
b R^n)$ as $\epsilon\to 0$. For $\epsilon$ small, $\operatorname{supp}(v_0^\epsi
lon)\subset V_0\subset\bar U$, so $v_0^\epsilon\big|_{\bar U}\in C^\infty(\bar U
)$ and $\|v_0^\epsilon - u_0\|_{W^{k,p}(U)}\to 0$.

---

**Step 4. Boundary patches ($j=1,\dots,N$): shifting into the interior.**

Fix $j\ge 1$ and let $y^j$ be the vector from the segment property. For $\delta>
0$ define the *shifted* function

$$u_j^\delta(x) := u_j(x+\delta\, y^j), \qquad x\in\bar U.$$

By the segment property, for $\delta>0$ small enough, $x+\delta y^j\in U$ whenev
er $x\in\operatorname{supp}(\zeta_j)\cap\bar U$, so $u_j^\delta$ is well-defined
 on $\bar U$. Moreover $\operatorname{supp}(u_j^\delta)$ is compactly contained
in $U$ (the support has moved a positive distance away from $\partial U$).

*Convergence of the shift.* Extend $u_j$ by zero to $\mathbb R^n$. For any multi
-index $|\alpha|\le k$,

$$D^\alpha u_j^\delta(x) = (D^\alpha u_j)(x+\delta y^j),$$

so

$$\|u_j^\delta - u_j\|_{W^{k,p}(U)}^p = \sum_{|\alpha|\le k}\bigl\|(D^\alpha u_j
)(\cdot+\delta y^j) - D^\alpha u_j(\cdot)\bigr\|_{L^p(U)}^p.$$

Each $D^\alpha u_j\in L^p(\mathbb R^n)$ (extended by zero, supported in a bounde
d set). By **continuity of translations in $L^p(\mathbb R^n)$** for $1\le p<\inf
ty$, each term tends to $0$ as $\delta\to 0$. Hence

$$u_j^\delta \to u_j \quad\text{in } W^{k,p}(U) \text{ as } \delta\to 0.$$

---

**Step 5. Mollification of the shifted function.**

Since $\operatorname{supp}(u_j^\delta)\subset\subset U$, extending $u_j^\delta$
by zero gives a function in $W^{k,p}(\mathbb R^n)$. Mollify:

$$v_j^{\epsilon,\delta} := \eta_\epsilon * u_j^\delta \;\in\; C^\infty(\mathbb R
^n).$$

For $\epsilon$ small (relative to the distance of $\operatorname{supp}(u_j^\delt
a)$ to $\partial U$), $\operatorname{supp}(v_j^{\epsilon,\delta})\subset U$, hen
ce $v_j^{\epsilon,\delta}\big|_{\bar U}\in C^\infty(\bar U)$. Moreover $v_j^{\ep
silon,\delta}\to u_j^\delta$ in $W^{k,p}(\mathbb R^n)$ as $\epsilon\to 0$.

---

**Step 6. Diagonal selection and conclusion.**

Given $\varepsilon>0$, for each $j=0,\dots,N$:

- Choose $\delta_j>0$ small enough that $\|u_j^{\delta_j}-u_j\|_{W^{k,p}(U)}<\va
repsilon/\bigl(2(N+1)\bigr)$ (for $j=0$, skip the shift; set $\delta_0=0$).
- Choose $\epsilon_j>0$ small enough that $\|v_j^{\epsilon_j,\delta_j}-u_j^{\del
ta_j}\|_{W^{k,p}(U)}<\varepsilon/\bigl(2(N+1)\bigr)$.

Define

$$u_\varepsilon := \sum_{j=0}^N v_j^{\epsilon_j,\delta_j}\Big|_{\bar U}.$$

Then $u_\varepsilon\in C^\infty(\bar U)$, and by the triangle inequality:

$$\|u_\varepsilon - u\|_{W^{k,p}(U)} \le \sum_{j=0}^N \Bigl(\|v_j^{\epsilon_j,\d
elta_j}-u_j^{\delta_j}\|_{W^{k,p}(U)} + \|u_j^{\delta_j}-u_j\|_{W^{k,p}(U)}\Bigr
) < \varepsilon.$$

Setting $\varepsilon = 1/m$ yields a sequence $\{u_m\}\subset C^\infty(\bar U)$
with $u_m\to u$ in $W^{k,p}(U)$.

---

**Remark on the hypotheses.**

- **$p<\infty$ is essential:** the proof uses continuity of translations in $L^p
$, which fails for $p=\infty$.
- **Piecewise $C^1$ suffices for all $k$:** the argument uses only *translation*
 (which preserves all derivatives) and *mollification* (which is interior), neve
r differentiation of a boundary-flattening map. This is why $C^1$ (even piecewis
e $C^1$) regularity of $\partial U$ suffices for every $k$, in contrast to the e
xtension-operator approach which would require $C^k$ boundary.
