**Step 1: Identify the nature of the integral.**

The integrand $f(t) = \frac{\cos(\pi t/2)}{1-t^2}$ has a potential singularity a
t $t=1$ (where $1-t^2=0$). However, near $t=1$:
$$\cos(\pi t/2) = \cos\!\bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{2}+\tfrac{\pi(t-1)}{2}\bigr) = -\sin\!
\bigl(\tfrac{\pi(t-1)}{2}\bigr) \approx -\tfrac{\pi}{2}(t-1),$$
$$1-t^2 = -(t-1)(1+t) \approx -2(t-1).$$
So $f(t) \to \frac{-\pi(t-1)/2}{-2(t-1)} = \frac{\pi}{4}$ as $t\to 1$. The singu
larity is **removable**, so the integral converges as an ordinary (improper) int
egral. At infinity, $|f(t)|\sim 1/t^2$, so it converges absolutely there as well
.

**Step 2: Reduce to a full-line integral.**

Since $f(t)$ is even ($\cos$ is even, $1-t^2$ is even):
$$I = \int_0^\infty \frac{\cos(\pi t/2)}{1-t^2}\,dt = \frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^
{\infty}\frac{\cos(\pi t/2)}{1-t^2}\,dt.$$

**Step 3: Contour integration for the general integral.**

Consider, for $a>0$,
$$J(a) = \mathrm{PV}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{iax}}{1-x^2}\,dx.$$

Take $g(z)=\frac{e^{iaz}}{1-z^2}$ and close the contour in the **upper half-plan
e** (valid since $a>0$ gives $|e^{iaz}|=e^{-a\,\mathrm{Im}\,z}\to 0$ as $\mathrm
{Im}\,z\to+\infty$). The poles at $z=\pm1$ lie on the real axis; we indent **abo
ve** both via small semicircles $z=z_0+\epsilon e^{i\theta}$, $\theta:\pi\to 0$.
 No poles are enclosed, so by Cauchy's theorem the full contour integral is $0$:

$$\mathrm{PV}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} g(x)\,dx \;+\; (\text{indents}) \;+\; (\tex
t{large arc}\to 0) \;=\; 0.$$

Each indent above a simple pole $z_0$ with residue $r$ contributes $-i\pi r$.

**Residues:**
- At $z=1$: $\;\mathrm{Res}=\displaystyle\lim_{z\to1}(z-1)\frac{e^{iaz}}{(1-z)(1
+z)}=\frac{e^{ia}}{-2}$.
- At $z=-1$: $\;\mathrm{Res}=\displaystyle\lim_{z\to-1}(z+1)\frac{e^{iaz}}{(1-z)
(1+z)}=\frac{e^{-ia}}{2}$.

**Indent contributions:**
$$-i\pi\cdot\frac{e^{ia}}{-2} + (-i\pi)\cdot\frac{e^{-ia}}{2} = \frac{i\pi}{2}\b
igl(e^{ia}-e^{-ia}\bigr) = \frac{i\pi}{2}\cdot 2i\sin a = -\pi\sin a.$$

Therefore:
$$J(a) - \pi\sin a = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathrm{PV}\int_{-\infty}^{\inf
ty}\frac{e^{iax}}{1-x^2}\,dx = \pi\sin a, \qquad a>0.$$

**Step 4: Take the real part and specialize.**

Taking real parts (and noting the singularity is removable, so PV = ordinary int
egral):
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos(ax)}{1-x^2}\,dx = \pi\sin a, \qquad a>0.$$

(Consistency check: the imaginary part gives $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(
ax)}{1-x^2}\,dx=0$, which holds since the integrand is odd. ✓)

Setting $a = \pi/2$:
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos(\pi x/2)}{1-x^2}\,dx = \pi\sin\!\bigl(\tfrac
{\pi}{2}\bigr) = \pi.$$

**Step 5: Conclude.**

$$I = \frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos(\pi t/2)}{1-t^2}\,dt = \frac{
1}{2}\cdot\pi = \frac{\pi}{2}.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_0^{\infty}\frac{\cos(\pi t/2)}{1-t^2}\,dt = \frac{\pi
}{2}.}$$
