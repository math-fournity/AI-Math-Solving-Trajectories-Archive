## Proof

Let $E_m = \{x \in [a,b] : g(x) > m\}$. We must show $n^m = \mu(E_m) \to 0$ as $
m\to\infty$.

**Step 1: The sets are nested decreasing.** For $m_1 < m_2$, if $g(x) > m_2$ the
n $g(x) > m_1$, so $E_{m_2} \subseteq E_{m_1}$. Hence
$$E_1 \supseteq E_2 \supseteq E_3 \supseteq \cdots.$$

**Step 2: The intersection is empty.** Since $g$ maps into $\mathbb{R}_+$ (the f
inite nonnegative reals), every value $g(x)$ is a finite real number. Therefore
for every $x \in [a,b]$ there exists an integer $m > g(x)$, giving $x \notin E_m
$. Consequently
$$\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty} E_m = \{x \in [a,b] : g(x) > m \text{ for all } m \in \
mathbb{N}\} = \varnothing.$$

**Step 3: Apply continuity from above.** The Lebesgue measure $\mu$ on $[a,b]$ s
atisfies $\mu([a,b]) = b - a < \infty$. For a decreasing sequence of measurable
sets $E_1 \supseteq E_2 \supseteq \cdots$ with $\mu(E_1) < \infty$, the continui
ty-from-above property of measures gives
$$\lim_{m\to\infty} \mu(E_m) = \mu\!\left(\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty} E_m\right).$$

Since $\mu(E_1) \le \mu([a,b]) = b-a < \infty$, the hypothesis is satisfied. By
Step 2 the intersection is empty, so
$$\lim_{m\to\infty} n^m = \lim_{m\to\infty} \mu(E_m) = \mu(\varnothing) = 0.$$

**Conclusion.** Yes, $n^m \to 0$ as $m \to \infty$.

**Remark on the hypotheses.** Both assumptions are essential:
- *Finiteness of the ambient measure.* On an infinite-measure domain, continuity
 from above requires $\mu(E_{m_0})<\infty$ for some $m_0$; without it the conclu
sion can fail (e.g. $g\equiv +\infty$ is excluded here, but even finite-valued f
unctions on $\mathbb{R}$ with $g\equiv 1$ give $n^m=\mu(\mathbb{R})=\infty$ for
$m<1$).
- *Finite values of $g$.* If $g$ were allowed to take $+\infty$ on a set of posi
tive measure, then $\bigcap_m E_m = \{g=+\infty\}$ would have positive measure a
nd $n^m$ would converge to that positive value rather than to $0$. The codomain
$\mathbb{R}_+$ rules this out.
