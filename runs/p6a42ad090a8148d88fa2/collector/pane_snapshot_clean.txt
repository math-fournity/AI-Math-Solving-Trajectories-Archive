## Proof

**Identifying End(ℝ).** The continuous endomorphisms of (ℝ, +) as a topological
group are exactly the maps f_c(x) = cx for c ∈ ℝ. Under pointwise addition and c
omposition, this set forms a ring isomorphic to ℝ (with its usual ring structure
). Thus the question reduces to: **does ℝ, as a ring, have a countable minimal c
over by proper subrings?** That is, is the covering number σ(ℝ) = ℵ₀?

**Step 1: ℝ cannot be covered by finitely many proper subrings.**

Every subring of ℝ is in particular an additive subgroup of (ℝ, +). The group (ℝ
, +) is **divisible**: for every n ≥ 1, nℝ = ℝ. A divisible group has no proper
subgroups of finite index (since G/nG = 0 for all n).

By the theorem of B.H. Neumann (1954): if a group G = H_1 ∪ ⋯ ∪ H_n is a finite
union of subgroups, then some H_i has finite index in G. Since (ℝ, +) is divisib
le, it has no proper finite-index subgroups. Therefore (ℝ, +) cannot be expresse
d as a finite union of proper subgroups, and consequently **ℝ cannot be covered
by finitely many proper subrings**. Hence σ(ℝ) ≥ ℵ₀.

**Step 2: ℝ can be covered by countably many proper subrings.**

Let T be a transcendence basis of ℝ over ℚ. Then |T| = 2^{ℵ₀}, and ℝ is algebrai
c over ℚ(T).

**Partition the basis.** Since |T| = 2^{ℵ₀} and 2^{ℵ₀} · ℵ₀ = 2^{ℵ₀}, we can par
tition T into countably many nonempty parts:
$$T = \bigsqcup_{n=1}^{\infty} T_n, \quad \text{each } T_n \neq \emptyset.$$

**Construct proper subfields.** For each n ≥ 1, define:
$$F_n = \mathbb{Q}(T \setminus T_n),$$
the subfield of ℝ generated by ℚ and T \ T_n. Since T_n is nonempty and T is alg
ebraically independent, every element of T_n is transcendental over F_n, so F_n
is a **proper subfield** of ℝ.

Now let F_n^{\text{alg}} denote the **relative algebraic closure** of F_n in ℝ:
$$F_n^{\text{alg}} = \{x \in \mathbb{R} : x \text{ is algebraic over } F_n\}.$$

This is a subfield of ℝ containing F_n. It is still **proper**: any t ∈ T_n is t
ranscendental over F_n (by algebraic independence of T), hence t ∉ F_n^{\text{al
g}}.

**The countable family covers ℝ.** We claim:
$$\mathbb{R} = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} F_n^{\text{alg}}.$$

*Proof of claim.* Let x ∈ ℝ. Since T is a transcendence basis, x is algebraic ov
er ℚ(T). Let m(X) ∈ ℚ(T)[X] be the minimal polynomial of x over ℚ(T). The coeffi
cients of m are rational functions involving only **finitely many** elements of
T, say t_1, …, t_k. These elements lie in finitely many parts T_{n_1}, …, T_{n_j
} (where j ≤ k).

Since there are countably many parts and only finitely many are involved, there
exists an index n* ∉ {n_1, …, n_j}. Then {t_1, …, t_k} ⊆ T \ T_{n*}, so all coef
ficients of m lie in ℚ(T \ T_{n*}) = F_{n*}. Therefore x is algebraic over F_{n*
}, which means x ∈ F_{n*}^{\text{alg}}. ∎

Each F_n^{\text{alg}} is a proper subfield, hence a proper subring of ℝ. We have
 exhibited a countable cover of ℝ by proper subrings.

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 1 and 2:
- ℝ **cannot** be covered by finitely many proper subrings (σ(ℝ) ≥ ℵ₀), by the d
ivisibility of (ℝ, +) and Neumann's theorem.
- ℝ **can** be covered by countably many proper subrings (σ(ℝ) ≤ ℵ₀), by the tra
nscendence basis partition construction.

Therefore **σ(ℝ) = ℵ₀**: the minimal cover of ℝ by proper subrings is countable.

Since End(ℝ) ≅ ℝ as a ring, we conclude:

**Yes, End(ℝ) has a countable minimal cover.** The covering number is exactly ℵ₀
 — countably many proper subrings suffice, and finitely many do not.
