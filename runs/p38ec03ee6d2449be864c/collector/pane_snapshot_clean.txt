## Solution

We determine the number of zeros of $f(z) = z - 2 - e^{-z}$ in the right half-pl
ane $P = \{z : \Re(z) > 0\}$.

**Step 1: No zeros on the boundary (imaginary axis).**

Set $z = iy$ ($y \in \mathbb{R}$). Then
$$f(iy) = iy - 2 - e^{-iy} = \bigl(-2 - \cos y\bigr) + i\bigl(y + \sin y\bigr).$
$
The real part vanishing requires $\cos y = -2$, which is impossible. Hence $f$ h
as no zeros on the imaginary axis.

**Step 2: Rouché's theorem on a large semicircle.**

For $R > 3$, let $\Gamma_R$ be the closed contour consisting of the segment $\{i
y : |y| \le R\}$ and the right semicircular arc $\{Re^{i\theta} : -\pi/2 \le \th
eta \le \pi/2\}$. Write $f(z) = (z-2) + (-e^{-z})$ and compare $|z-2|$ with $|e^
{-z}|$ on $\Gamma_R$:

- **On the imaginary-axis segment** ($z = iy$, $|y| \le R$): $|z - 2| = \sqrt{4
+ y^2} \ge 2$ and $|e^{-z}| = |e^{-iy}| = 1$. So $|z-2| \ge 2 > 1 = |e^{-z}|$.

- **On the semicircular arc** ($|z| = R$, $\Re(z) \ge 0$): $|z - 2| \ge R - 2$ a
nd $|e^{-z}| = e^{-\Re(z)} \le 1$. For $R \ge 4$, $|z - 2| \ge R - 2 \ge 2 > 1 \
ge |e^{-z}|$.

Thus on all of $\Gamma_R$, $|z - 2| > |e^{-z}|$. By **Rouché's theorem**, $f(z)
= (z-2) - e^{-z}$ has the same number of zeros inside $\Gamma_R$ as $z - 2$, nam
ely **exactly 1** (the point $z = 2$).

**Step 3: Verify the unique zero is real and lies in $P$.**

For $y = 0$ (real $z = x$), the equation becomes $g(x) = x - 2 - e^{-x} = 0$. Si
nce $g'(x) = 1 + e^{-x} > 0$, $g$ is strictly increasing. We have $g(2) = -e^{-2
} < 0$ and $g(3) = 1 - e^{-3} > 0$, so there is exactly one real root $x_0 \in (
2, 3) \subset P$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Since Rouché gives exactly one zero inside $\Gamma_R$ for every $R \ge 4$, and t
here are no zeros on the imaginary axis, the total number of zeros in the open r
ight half-plane is exactly **1**.
