Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a finitely presented infini
te group can have only finitely many finite retracts.

## Proof

**Answer: Yes.** The free group $F_2 = \langle a, b \mid \;\rangle$ is an exampl
e.

**Definitions.** A *retract* of a group $G$ is a subgroup $H \leq G$ for which t
here exists a homomorphism $r: G \to H$ with $r|_H = \mathrm{id}_H$ (a *retracti
on*). Equivalently, $H$ is the image of an idempotent endomorphism of $G$.

**The example.** Let $G = F_2 = \langle a, b \mid \;\rangle$, the free group of
rank 2. This group is:
- **Finitely presented**: it has the finite presentation $\langle a, b \mid \;\r
angle$ (two generators, no relations).
- **Infinite**: it contains elements of arbitrary order (e.g., $a, a^2, a^3, \ld
ots$ are all distinct).

**Claim.** The only finite retract of $F_2$ is the trivial subgroup $\{1\}$.

**Proof of claim.** Let $H$ be a retract of $F_2$. Since a retract is in particu
lar a subgroup, $H \leq F_2$. By the **Nielsen–Schreier theorem**, every subgrou
p of a free group is free. Therefore $H$ is a free group (of some rank $\kappa$,
 where $\kappa$ is a cardinal).

Now, a free group $F_\kappa$ is finite if and only if $\kappa = 0$, in which cas
e $F_0 = \{1\}$ is the trivial group. (For $\kappa \geq 1$, the group is infinit
e: it surjects onto $\mathbb{Z}$, or simply contains the infinite cyclic subgrou
p generated by any free generator.)

Therefore, the only finite retract of $F_2$ is the trivial subgroup $\{1\}$.

**The trivial subgroup is indeed a retract.** The trivial homomorphism $r: F_2 \
to \{1\}$ satisfies $r|_{\{1\}} = \mathrm{id}_{\{1\}}$, so $\{1\}$ is a retract.

**Conclusion.** The group $F_2 = \langle a, b \mid \;\rangle$ is finitely presen
ted, infinite, and has exactly **one** finite retract (the trivial subgroup). He
nce it has only finitely many finite retracts. $\blacksquare$
