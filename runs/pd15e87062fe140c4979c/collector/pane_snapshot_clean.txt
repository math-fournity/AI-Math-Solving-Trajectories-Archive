**Claim:** The order of $f_k(z) = \prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1 - \frac{z}{n^k}\ri
ght)$ is $\lambda = \dfrac{1}{k}$ (for $k > 1$, so that the genus-0 product conv
erges).

### Step 1. Convergence and the zero set

The product is a genus-0 Weierstrass product. It converges absolutely and unifor
mly on compact sets iff $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^k} < \infty$, i.e., $k >
 1$. Under this assumption $f_k$ is entire with simple zeros at $z = n^k$ ($n =
1, 2, 3, \ldots$).

The **exponent of convergence** of the zeros $\{n^k\}$ is
$$
\rho_1 = \inf\left\{\alpha > 0:\ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{k\alpha}} < \inf
ty\right\} = \frac{1}{k},
$$
since $\sum n^{-k\alpha}$ converges iff $k\alpha > 1$.

### Step 2. Upper bound: $\lambda \leq 1/k$

For $|z| = r$,
$$
\log|f_k(z)| \leq \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\log\!\left(1 + \frac{r}{n^k}\right).
$$
Set $N = \lceil r^{1/k}\rceil$ and split the sum.

- **Terms $n \leq N$:** Here $r/n^k \geq 1$, so $\log(1 + r/n^k) \leq \log(2r) \
leq C\log r$ (for large $r$). There are $N \sim r^{1/k}$ such terms, contributin
g $O(r^{1/k}\log r)$.

- **Terms $n > N$:** Here $r/n^k < 1$, so $\log(1 + r/n^k) \leq r/n^k$. Using an
 integral comparison,
$$
\sum_{n > N}\frac{r}{n^k} \leq r\int_{N}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x^k} = \frac{r}{(k-1)
N^{k-1}} \leq C\, r\cdot r^{-(k-1)/k} = C\, r^{1/k}.
$$

Combining,
$$
\log M(r) \leq C_1\, r^{1/k}\log r \qquad (r \to \infty).
$$
Therefore
$$
\frac{\log\log M(r)}{\log r} \leq \frac{\log(C_1 r^{1/k}\log r)}{\log r} = \frac
{1}{k} + \frac{\log\log r}{\log r} + o(1) \;\longrightarrow\; \frac{1}{k}.
$$
Hence $\lambda \leq 1/k$.

### Step 3. Lower bound: $\lambda \geq 1/k$

Take $z = -r < 0$. Every factor is positive and
$$
|f_k(-r)| = \prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1 + \frac{r}{n^k}\right) \geq \prod_{n=1}^
{N}\left(1 + \frac{r}{n^k}\right),
$$
where $N = \lfloor r^{1/k}\rfloor$. For $n \leq N$ we have $r/n^k \geq 1$, so ea
ch factor $\geq 2$. Thus
$$
|f_k(-r)| \geq 2^{N} \geq 2^{r^{1/k}/2} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \log|f_k(-r)|
\geq c\, r^{1/k}
$$
for a constant $c > 0$ and all large $r$. Since $M(r) \geq |f_k(-r)|$,
$$
\log M(r) \geq c\, r^{1/k} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{\log\log M(r)}{\log r
} \geq \frac{1}{k} + o(1).
$$
Hence $\lambda \geq 1/k$.

### Step 4. Conclusion

From Steps 2 and 3,
$$
\boxed{\lambda = \frac{1}{k}}.
$$

**Consistency check ($k=2$):** $\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1 - z/n^2) = \frac{\sin(\pi
\sqrt{z})}{\pi\sqrt{z}}$, and $|\sin(\pi\sqrt{z})| \leq e^{\pi|\operatorname{Im}
\sqrt{z}|} \leq e^{\pi\sqrt{|z|}}$, giving order $1/2 = 1/k$. ✓
