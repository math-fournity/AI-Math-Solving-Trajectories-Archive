Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether there exists a symmetric ma
trix that is **not** positive semidefinite (PSD), yet has **two distinct** princ
ipal submatrices (obtained by deleting a row and corresponding column) that **ar
e** PSD.

## Analysis

For a symmetric matrix $A$, the notation $A_{-i}$ means the principal submatrix
obtained by deleting the $i$-th row and $i$-th column.

**Key observation:** A matrix can fail to be PSD due to a single "bad" principal
 substructure, while many of its proper principal submatrices remain PSD. In par
ticular, the obstruction to PSD-ness need not appear in every $(n-1)\times(n-1)$
 principal submatrix.

## Construction (2×2 case)

Take
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

**$A$ is not PSD.** The eigenvalues of $A$ are $1+2=3$ and $1-2=-1$. Since $-1<0
$, $A$ is not positive semidefinite.

**$A_{-1}$ is PSD.** Deleting row 1 and column 1 gives the $1\times 1$ matrix $(
1)$, which is PSD since $1\ge 0$.

**$A_{-2}$ is PSD.** Deleting row 2 and column 2 gives the $1\times 1$ matrix $(
1)$, which is PSD since $1\ge 0$.

The two indices $i_1=1$ and $i_2=2$ are distinct, and both submatrices are PSD w
hile $A$ itself is not. $\square$

## Construction (3×3 case, for robustness)

In case the problem implicitly requires $n\ge 3$, here is a 3×3 example:
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

**$A$ is not PSD.** This is block-diagonal: $\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&1\end{pmatrix
}\oplus(1)$, with eigenvalues $3,-1,1$. The eigenvalue $-1<0$ means $A$ is not P
SD.

**$A_{-1}$ is PSD.** Deleting row/column 1:
$$A_{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}=I_2,$$
which is PSD (eigenvalues $1,1$).

**$A_{-2}$ is PSD.** Deleting row/column 2:
$$A_{-2}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}=I_2,$$
which is PSD.

The indices $i_1=1\neq 2=i_2$ are distinct, both submatrices are PSD, and $A$ is
 not PSD. $\square$

## Why this works (structural reason)

A symmetric matrix is PSD iff **all** its eigenvalues are nonnegative, equivalen
tly iff **all** its principal minors are nonnegative (Sylvester's criterion exte
nded). Deleting a row/column can remove the very principal substructure responsi
ble for the negative eigenvalue. In the 3×3 example, the negative eigenvalue $-1
$ comes from the $2\times 2$ block $\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&1\end{pmatrix}$ (rows/
columns 1,2). Deleting **either** row/column 1 or row/column 2 destroys this blo
ck, leaving the identity matrix. Only deleting row/column 3 would preserve the b
ad block (and indeed $A_{-3}=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&1\end{pmatrix}$ is **not** PS
D). This shows it is entirely possible for the obstruction to be "dodged" by two
 different deletions.

## Conclusion

**Yes**, it is possible. The matrix
$$A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&1\end{pmatrix}$$
is symmetric, not positive semidefinite (eigenvalue $-1<0$), and both principal
submatrices $A_{-1}=(1)$ and $A_{-2}=(1)$ are positive semidefinite, with $i_1=1
\neq 2=i_2$.
