## Proof

### Setup

Consider a perfect fluid in flat spacetime with metric $\eta^{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{di
ag}(+1,-1,-1,-1)$ (we set $c=1$ during the relativistic derivation and restore i
t when taking the nonrelativistic limit). The energy–momentum tensor of a perfec
t fluid is

$$
T^{\mu\nu}=(p+\rho)\,u^\mu u^\nu - p\,\eta^{\mu\nu},
$$

where $\rho$ is the proper energy density, $p$ the pressure, and $u^\mu$ the 4-v
elocity normalized to $u_\mu u^\mu=1$. The dynamics is governed by the conservat
ion law

$$
\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0. \tag{1}
$$

### Step 1. Rewrite the conservation law

Substituting the perfect-fluid form of $T^{\mu\nu}$ into (1):

$$
\partial_\mu\!\big[(p+\rho)\,u^\mu u^\nu\big]-\partial^\nu p = 0,
$$

i.e.

$$
\partial_\mu\!\big[(p+\rho)\,u^\mu u^\nu\big]=\partial^\nu p. \tag{2}
$$

Expanding the derivative of the product,

$$
u^\nu\,\partial_\mu\!\big[(p+\rho)u^\mu\big]+(p+\rho)\,u^\mu\partial_\mu u^\nu=\
partial^\nu p. \tag{3}
$$

### Step 2. Longitudinal projection — energy/continuity equation

Contract (3) with $u_\nu$. Using $u_\nu u^\nu=1$ and the identity

$$
u_\nu\,\partial_\mu u^\nu=\tfrac12\,\partial_\mu(u_\nu u^\nu)=0,
$$

the second term vanishes, giving

$$
\boxed{\;\partial_\mu\!\big[(p+\rho)u^\mu\big]=u^\mu\partial_\mu p.\;} \tag{4}
$$

This is the relativistic energy (continuity) equation.

### Step 3. Transverse projection — relativistic Euler equation

Project (3) perpendicular to $u^\nu$ using the projector $h^{\nu}{}_{\sigma}=\de
lta^\nu{}_\sigma-u^\nu u_\sigma$. Since $h^\nu{}_\sigma u^\sigma=0$, the first t
erm of (3) drops out, and using $h^\nu{}_\sigma u^\mu\partial_\mu u^\sigma=u^\mu
\partial_\mu u^\nu$ (the $u^\nu u_\sigma u^\mu\partial_\mu u^\sigma$ piece vanis
hes by the same identity), we obtain

$$
\boxed{\;(p+\rho)\,u^\mu\partial_\mu u^\nu = h^{\nu}{}_{\sigma}\,\partial^\sigma
 p = \big(\eta^{\nu\sigma}-u^\nu u^\sigma\big)\partial_\sigma p.\;} \tag{5}
$$

This is the **relativistic Euler equation**. It is exact; we now take its nonrel
ativistic limit.

### Step 4. Nonrelativistic limit

Restore factors of $c$. The 4-velocity becomes $u^\mu=\gamma(c,\mathbf v)$ with
$\gamma=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$, the coordinate $x^0=ct$ so that $\partial_0=(1/c)\,
\partial_t$, and the normalization is $u_\mu u^\mu=c^2$. The energy–momentum ten
sor with this convention reads

$$
T^{\mu\nu}=(p+\rho)\,\frac{u^\mu u^\nu}{c^2}-p\,\eta^{\mu\nu}.
$$

The nonrelativistic regime is defined by

$$
v\ll c,\qquad \gamma\simeq 1,\qquad \rho=\rho_0 c^2+\varepsilon\;\text{ with }\;
\varepsilon\ll \rho_0 c^2,\qquad p\ll \rho,
$$

where $\rho_0$ is the rest-mass density. Hence $(p+\rho)/c^2\simeq \rho_0$, $u^0
\simeq c$, $u^i\simeq v^i$, and

$$
u^\mu\partial_\mu = u^0\partial_0+u^j\partial_j \simeq c\cdot\frac{1}{c}\partial
_t+v^j\partial_j = \partial_t+\mathbf v\!\cdot\!\nabla. \tag{6}
$$

**Continuity equation.** In the NR limit, equation (4) becomes

$$
\partial_0\!\big[(p+\rho)u^0\big]+\partial_j\!\big[(p+\rho)u^j\big]=u^\mu\partia
l_\mu p.
$$

The left-hand side is

$$
\frac{1}{c}\partial_t(\rho_0 c^2\cdot c)+\partial_j(\rho_0 c^2 v^j)=c^2\big[\par
tial_t\rho_0+\partial_j(\rho_0 v^j)\big],
$$

while the right-hand side is $(\partial_t+\mathbf v\!\cdot\!\nabla)p=O(\rho_0 v^
2)$. Dividing by $c^2$,

$$
\partial_t\rho_0+\nabla\!\cdot\!(\rho_0\mathbf v)=\frac{1}{c^2}(\partial_t+\math
bf v\!\cdot\!\nabla)p=O\!\left(\frac{v^2}{c^2}\right)\to 0.
$$

Thus, to leading order,

$$
\boxed{\;\partial_t\rho_0+\nabla\!\cdot\!(\rho_0\mathbf v)=0.\;} \tag{7}
$$

**Euler equation.** Take the spatial components $\nu=i$ of (5). The right-hand s
ide is

$$
\big(\eta^{i\sigma}-u^i u^\sigma/c^2\big)\partial_\sigma p.
$$

In the NR limit $u^i u^\sigma/c^2=O(v^2/c^2)\to 0$, and $\eta^{i\sigma}\partial_
\sigma p=\eta^{ii}\partial_i p=-\partial_i p$. So the RHS tends to $-\partial_i
p$.

The left-hand side is, using $(p+\rho)/c^2\simeq\rho_0$, (6), and $u^i\simeq v^i
$,

$$
\frac{p+\rho}{c^2}\,u^\mu\partial_\mu u^i \;\longrightarrow\; \rho_0\,(\partial_
t+v^j\partial_j)\,v^i.
$$

Equating both sides,

$$
\rho_0\big(\partial_t v^i+v^j\partial_j v^i\big)=-\partial_i p,\qquad i=1,2,3. \
tag{8}
$$

(One may verify that the dropped term $u^i u^\nu/c^2\cdot u^\mu\partial_\mu p$ a
nd the contribution from the longitudinal equation entering (3) are both $O(v^2/
c^2)$ and vanish in the limit.)

### Step 5. Result

Writing (8) in vector form, the **Euler equation for a perfect (inviscid) fluid*
* in the nonrelativistic limit is

$$
\boxed{\;\rho_0\!\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf v}{\partial t}+(\mathbf v\!\cdot\!\
nabla)\mathbf v\right)=-\nabla p.\;} \tag{9}
$$

Together with the continuity equation (7) and an equation of state $p=p(\rho_0)$
, this closes the nonrelativistic perfect-fluid system. The convective derivativ
e $\partial_t\mathbf v+(\mathbf v\!\cdot\!\nabla)\mathbf v$ is the material deri
vative $D\mathbf v/Dt$, so (9) is equivalently $\rho_0\,D\mathbf v/Dt=-\nabla p$
: the acceleration of a fluid element equals the force per unit volume from the
pressure gradient, with no viscous or heat-conduction terms — precisely the defi
ning property of a perfect fluid.
