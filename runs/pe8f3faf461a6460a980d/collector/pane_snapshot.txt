**Problem.** Fifteen players stand in a line, one meter apart. Place them at pos
itions $1,2,\dots,15$. A route giving awards is a permutation $(a_1,a_2,\dots,a_
{15})$ of these positions, and the total distance is
$$D=\sum_{i=1}^{14}|a_{i+1}-a_i|.$$
We want the maximum possible $D$.

---

**Upper bound.** Expand each absolute value as "larger minus smaller." Then
$$D=\sum_{j=1}^{15} c_j\, a_j,$$
where each $c_j$ records how many times position $j$ is the larger endpoint of a
n edge minus how many times it is the smaller endpoint. In the path graph on 15
vertices, the two endpoints of the path have degree 1, so their coefficients are
 $\pm1$; the other 13 vertices have degree 2, so their coefficients lie in $\{-2
,0,+2\}$. Also every edge contributes one $+1$ and one $-1$, hence
$$\sum_{j=1}^{15} c_j = 0.$$

To bound $D$ from above we maximize $\sum c_j j$ over all coefficient vectors sa
tisfying these constraints (a relaxation, so still a valid upper bound). To maxi
mize, put the largest positive coefficients on the largest positions and the mos
t negative coefficients on the smallest positions. The best feasible configurati
on is:

- seven $+2$'s on positions $9,10,\dots,15$,
- six $-2$'s on positions $1,2,\dots,6$,
- two $-1$'s on positions $7,8$.

Check the sum: $7\cdot2+6\cdot(-2)+2\cdot(-1)=14-12-2=0$. ✓ Thus
$$D \le 2(9+10+\cdots+15)-2(1+2+\cdots+6)-(7+8).$$
Compute:
$$9+\cdots+15 = 84,\qquad 1+\cdots+6=21,$$
$$D \le 2\cdot 84 - 2\cdot 21 - 15 = 168-42-15 = 111.$$
(The symmetric option — six $+2$'s, seven $-2$'s, two $+1$'s — also gives $2\cdo
t75-2\cdot28+17=111$, and every other feasible split gives strictly less, so $11
1$ is the tight relaxed maximum.) Hence **$D\le 111$** for every route.

---

**Construction attaining 111.** Take the route
$$(8,\,15,\,1,\,14,\,2,\,13,\,3,\,12,\,4,\,11,\,5,\,10,\,6,\,9,\,7).$$
This is a valid permutation of $\{1,\dots,15\}$. Its consecutive differences are
$$7,\,14,\,13,\,12,\,11,\,10,\,9,\,8,\,7,\,6,\,5,\,4,\,3,\,2,$$
which sum to
$$7+14+13+12+11+10+9+8+7+6+5+4+3+2 = 111.$$

So the upper bound is achieved.

---

**Answer.** The total distance covered by the longest possible route is $\boxed{
111}$ meters.
