# Normal Form of the Double-Hopf Bifurcation

## 1. Setup and Reduction

Consider the autonomous system
$$\dot{x} = f(x,\mu), \qquad x \in \mathbb{R}^n,\; \mu \in \mathbb{R}^2,$$
with equilibrium $x=0$ at $\mu=0$. The Jacobian $J = D_x f(0,0)$ is diagonalizab
le with eigenvalues $\pm i\omega_0$, $\pm i\omega_1$ ($\omega_0, \omega_1 > 0$)
and remaining eigenvalues having nonzero real parts. By the Center Manifold Theo
rem, there exists a 4-dimensional center manifold $W^c$ tangent to the eigenspac
e of $\pm i\omega_0, \pm i\omega_1$. We restrict to $W^c$ and work in $\mathbb{R
}^4 \cong \mathbb{C}^2$.

Introduce complex coordinates $(z_1, z_2) \in \mathbb{C}^2$ so that $z_1$ is ass
ociated with $+i\omega_0$ and $z_2$ with $+i\omega_1$. Since $J$ is diagonalizab
le, the linear part becomes
$$\dot{z}_1 = i\omega_0\, z_1, \qquad \dot{z}_2 = i\omega_1\, z_2.$$

As $\mu$ varies, the eigenvalue pairs move off the imaginary axis. Under the tra
nsversality (nondegeneracy) assumption, we may use the real parts as unfolding p
arameters:
$$\lambda_{1,2}(\mu) = \alpha_1 \pm i\omega_1(\mu), \qquad \lambda_{3,4}(\mu) =
\alpha_2 \pm i\omega_2(\mu),$$
with $\alpha_1(0)=\alpha_2(0)=0$, $\omega_1(0)=\omega_0$, $\omega_2(0)=\omega_1$
, and $(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)$ serving as independent bifurcation parameters. We ex
tend the system by adjoining $\dot{\alpha}_1=0$, $\dot{\alpha}_2=0$ and apply no
rmal form theory to the extended system.

## 2. Poincaré–Dulac Normal Form Procedure

We seek a near-identity change of variables $z = w + h(w,\bar{w})$ (with $h$ a f
ormal power series starting at degree $\geq 2$) that eliminates all **non-resona
nt** monomials. A monomial $z_1^{j_1}\bar{z}_1^{k_1}z_2^{j_2}\bar{z}_2^{k_2}$ in
 the $\dot{z}_1$ equation is **resonant** if its linear eigenvalue equals that o
f $z_1$:
$$(j_1 - k_1)\,i\omega_0 + (j_2 - k_2)\,i\omega_1 = i\omega_0,$$
i.e.,
$$\boxed{(j_1 - k_1 - 1)\,\omega_0 + (j_2 - k_2)\,\omega_1 = 0.} \tag{R1}$$

Similarly, a monomial in the $\dot{z}_2$ equation is resonant if
$$\boxed{(j_1 - k_1)\,\omega_0 + (j_2 - k_2 - 1)\,\omega_1 = 0.} \tag{R2}$$

All non-resonant monomials can be removed order-by-order. The normal form retain
s only resonant monomials (plus the linear part and parameter terms, which are a
lways resonant since $\alpha_i$ have eigenvalue $0$).

## 3. Nonresonant Case: $\omega_0/\omega_1$ Irrational

**Solving (R1):** Since $\omega_0/\omega_1$ is irrational, $(j_2-k_2)\,\omega_1/
\omega_0$ is irrational unless $j_2 = k_2$. Setting $j_2=k_2$ gives $j_1 - k_1 =
 1$. The resonant monomials in $\dot{z}_1$ are:
$$z_1\,|z_1|^{2k_1}\,|z_2|^{2k_2}, \qquad k_1, k_2 \geq 0.$$

**Solving (R2):** By the same argument, $j_1 = k_1$ and $j_2 - k_2 = 1$. Resonan
t monomials in $\dot{z}_2$:
$$z_2\,|z_1|^{2k_1}\,|z_2|^{2k_2}, \qquad k_1, k_2 \geq 0.$$

**Normal form (nonresonant case):**
$$\boxed{\dot{z}_1 = \bigl(\alpha_1 + i\omega_1(\mu)\bigr)z_1 + z_1\sum_{\substa
ck{j,k\geq 0\\j+k\geq 1}} c_{jk}\,|z_1|^{2j}|z_2|^{2k},}$$
$$\boxed{\dot{z}_2 = \bigl(\alpha_2 + i\omega_2(\mu)\bigr)z_2 + z_2\sum_{\substa
ck{j,k\geq 0\\j+k\geq 1}} d_{jk}\,|z_1|^{2j}|z_2|^{2k},}$$
where $c_{jk}, d_{jk} \in \mathbb{C}$ are coefficients determined by the origina
l vector field $f$.

**Cubic truncation** (lowest-order nonlinear terms, $j+k=1$):
$$\dot{z}_1 = (\alpha_1 + i\omega_1)\,z_1 + c_{10}\,z_1|z_1|^2 + c_{01}\,z_1|z_2
|^2,$$
$$\dot{z}_2 = (\alpha_2 + i\omega_2)\,z_2 + d_{10}\,z_2|z_1|^2 + d_{01}\,z_2|z_2
|^2.$$

**Polar form.** Setting $z_k = r_k e^{i\theta_k}$ and writing $c_{jk} = a_{jk} +
 ib_{jk}$, $d_{jk} = p_{jk}+iq_{jk}$:
$$\dot{r}_1 = \alpha_1 r_1 + a_{10}\,r_1^3 + a_{01}\,r_1 r_2^2,$$
$$\dot{r}_2 = \alpha_2 r_2 + p_{10}\,r_2 r_1^2 + p_{01}\,r_2^3,$$
$$\dot{\theta}_1 = \omega_1 + b_{10}\,r_1^2 + b_{01}\,r_2^2,$$
$$\dot{\theta}_2 = \omega_2 + q_{10}\,r_1^2 + q_{01}\,r_2^2.$$

The amplitude equations $(\dot{r}_1, \dot{r}_2)$ decouple from the phase equatio
ns $(\dot{\theta}_1, \dot{\theta}_2)$, and the phases enter only through their s
um/difference. This reflects the **$S^1 \times S^1$ symmetry**: the normal form
is invariant under independent phase rotations $z_1 \mapsto e^{i\varphi_1}z_1$,
$z_2 \mapsto e^{i\varphi_2}z_2$.

## 4. Resonant Case: $\omega_0/\omega_1 = m/n$ (coprime, $m,n > 0$)

Substituting $\omega_0 = (m/n)\omega_1$ into (R1) and multiplying by $n/\omega_1
$:
$$m(j_1 - k_1 - 1) + n(j_2 - k_2) = 0.$$

This is a linear Diophantine equation. Since $\gcd(m,n)=1$, the general solution
 is:
$$j_1 - k_1 = 1 + n\ell, \qquad j_2 - k_2 = -m\ell, \qquad \ell \in \mathbb{Z}.$
$

Similarly, (R2) gives:
$$j_1 - k_1 = n\ell', \qquad j_2 - k_2 = 1 - m\ell', \qquad \ell' \in \mathbb{Z}
.$$

**Classification of resonant terms in $\dot{z}_1$:**

- **$\ell = 0$:** $j_1-k_1=1$, $j_2=k_2$. Terms: $z_1\,|z_1|^{2j}|z_2|^{2k}$ — *
same as nonresonant case*.

- **$\ell = -s$ ($s \geq 1$):** $j_1-k_1 = 1-ns$, $j_2-k_2 = ms$.
  - If $ns \geq 2$: $k_1 = j_1 + ns - 1$, $j_2 = k_2 + ms$. Base monomial ($j_1=
0, k_2=0$): $\bar{z}_1^{ns-1}\,z_2^{ms}$, of degree $s(m+n)-1$.
  - (If $n=1, s=1$: $j_1-k_1=0$, base monomial $z_2^m$, degree $m$ — still cover
ed by $\bar{z}_1^0 z_2^m$.)

- **$\ell = s$ ($s \geq 1$):** $j_1-k_1 = 1+ns$, $j_2-k_2 = -ms$.
  - $j_1 = k_1 + 1 + ns$, $k_2 = j_2 + ms$. Base monomial ($k_1=0, j_2=0$): $z_1
^{ns+1}\,\bar{z}_2^{ms}$, of degree $s(m+n)+1$.

**Classification of resonant terms in $\dot{z}_2$:**

- **$\ell' = 0$:** $z_2\,|z_1|^{2j}|z_2|^{2k}$ — *same as nonresonant case*.

- **$\ell' = s$ ($s \geq 1$):** $j_1-k_1 = ns$, $j_2-k_2 = 1-ms$.
  - Base monomial: $z_1^{ns}\,\bar{z}_2^{ms-1}$, degree $s(m+n)-1$.

- **$\ell' = -s$ ($s \geq 1$):** $j_1-k_1 = -ns$, $j_2-k_2 = 1+ms$.
  - Base monomial: $\bar{z}_1^{ns}\,z_2^{ms+1}$, degree $s(m+n)+1$.

**Normal form (resonant case, $m+n \geq 3$):**

$$\dot{z}_1 = (\alpha_1 + i\omega_1)\,z_1 + z_1\sum_{\substack{j,k\geq 0\\j+k\ge
q 1}} c_{jk}\,|z_1|^{2j}|z_2|^{2k} + \sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\left[\bar{z}_1^{ns-1}\,
z_2^{ms}\sum_{j,k\geq 0} c_{jk}^{(s)}|z_1|^{2j}|z_2|^{2k} + z_1^{ns+1}\,\bar{z}_
2^{ms}\sum_{j,k\geq 0}\,\hat{c}_{jk}^{(s)}|z_1|^{2j}|z_2|^{2k}\right]$$

$$\dot{z}_2 = (\alpha_2 + i\omega_2)\,z_2 + z_2\sum_{\substack{j,k\geq 0\\j+k\ge
q 1}} d_{jk}\,|z_1|^{2j}|z_2|^{2k} + \sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\left[z_1^{ns}\,\bar{z}_
2^{ms-1}\sum_{j,k\geq 0} d_{jk}^{(s)}|z_1|^{2j}|z_2|^{2k} + \bar{z}_1^{ns}\,z_2^
{ms+1}\sum_{j,k\geq 0}\,\hat{d}_{jk}^{(s)}|z_1|^{2j}|z_2|^{2k}\right]$$

where all coefficients are complex constants (at $\mu=0$; parameter dependence e
nters at higher order).

**Lowest-order additional resonant terms** (at degree $m+n-1$, from $s=1$):

| Equation | Term | Degree |
|----------|------|--------|
| $\dot{z}_1$ | $\bar{z}_1^{n-1}\,z_2^{m}$ | $m+n-1$ |
| $\dot{z}_2$ | $z_1^{n}\,\bar{z}_2^{m-1}$ | $m+n-1$ |

**Truncated normal form (up to degree $m+n-1$, assuming $m+n-1 \geq 3$, i.e., $m
+n \geq 4$):**

If $m+n \geq 4$, the cubic terms ($j+k=1$) are the only nonlinear terms up to de
gree $m+n-1$, and the normal form coincides with the nonresonant case:
$$\dot{z}_1 = (\alpha_1 + i\omega_1)\,z_1 + c_{10}\,z_1|z_1|^2 + c_{01}\,z_1|z_2
|^2 + \cdots$$
$$\dot{z}_2 = (\alpha_2 + i\omega_2)\,z_2 + d_{10}\,z_2|z_1|^2 + d_{01}\,z_2|z_2
|^2 + \cdots$$
where $\cdots$ includes the additional resonant terms at degree $m+n-1$:
$$+ \;a\,\bar{z}_1^{n-1}z_2^m + \text{(h.o.t.)} \quad\text{in } \dot{z}_1, \qqua
d +\; b\,z_1^n\bar{z}_2^{m-1} + \text{(h.o.t.)} \quad\text{in } \dot{z}_2.$$

**Symmetry reduction.** In the resonant case, the $S^1 \times S^1$ symmetry of t
he nonresonant normal form breaks to a single $S^1$: the normal form is invarian
t under the **linked phase rotation**
$$z_1 \mapsto e^{in\varphi}\,z_1, \qquad z_2 \mapsto e^{im\varphi}\,z_2,$$
since every resonant monomial $z_1^{j_1}\bar{z}_1^{k_1}z_2^{j_2}\bar{z}_2^{k_2}$
 satisfies $n(j_1-k_1)+m(j_2-k_2) = n$ (in $\dot{z}_1$) or $= m$ (in $\dot{z}_2$
), making the phase factor $e^{i\varphi[n(j_1-k_1)+m(j_2-k_2)]}$ equal to $e^{in
\varphi}$ or $e^{im\varphi}$ respectively. This reduced symmetry means the two p
hases are no longer independent: the amplitude equations alone no longer form a
closed subsystem, and the dynamics is richer (phase-locking becomes possible).

## 5. Explicit Examples

### 1:2 Resonance ($m=1, n=2$, $\omega_1 = 2\omega_0$)

Additional resonant terms at degree $m+n-1 = 2$:
- $\dot{z}_1$: $\bar{z}_1\,z_2$ (eigenvalue $-i\omega_0 + i\omega_1 = i\omega_0$
 ✓)
- $\dot{z}_2$: $z_1^2$ (eigenvalue $2i\omega_0 = i\omega_1$ ✓)

**Normal form (up to cubic order):**
$$\dot{z}_1 = (\alpha_1 + i\omega_1)\,z_1 + a\,\bar{z}_1 z_2 + c_{10}\,z_1|z_1|^
2 + c_{01}\,z_1|z_2|^2,$$
$$\dot{z}_2 = (\alpha_2 + i\omega_2)\,z_2 + b\,z_1^2 + d_{10}\,z_2|z_1|^2 + d_{0
1}\,z_2|z_2|^2.$$

Symmetry: $z_1 \mapsto e^{i2\varphi}z_1$, $z_2 \mapsto e^{i\varphi}z_2$.

### 1:3 Resonance ($m=1, n=3$, $\omega_1 = 3\omega_0$)

Additional resonant terms at degree $3$:
- $\dot{z}_1$: $\bar{z}_1^2\,z_2$ (eigenvalue $-2i\omega_0 + i\omega_1 = i\omega
_0$ ✓)
- $\dot{z}_2$: $z_1^3$ (eigenvalue $3i\omega_0 = i\omega_1$ ✓)

**Normal form (up to cubic order):**
$$\dot{z}_1 = (\alpha_1 + i\omega_1)\,z_1 + a\,\bar{z}_1^2 z_2 + c_{10}\,z_1|z_1
|^2 + c_{01}\,z_1|z_2|^2,$$
$$\dot{z}_2 = (\alpha_2 + i\omega_2)\,z_2 + b\,z_1^3 + d_{10}\,z_2|z_1|^2 + d_{0
1}\,z_2|z_2|^2.$$

Symmetry: $z_1 \mapsto e^{i3\varphi}z_1$, $z_2 \mapsto e^{i\varphi}z_2$.

### 2:3 Resonance ($m=2, n=3$)

Additional resonant terms at degree $4$:
- $\dot{z}_1$: $\bar{z}_1^2\,z_2^2$ (eigenvalue $-2i\omega_0+2i\omega_1 = 2i(\om
ega_1-\omega_0) = 2i\cdot\frac{\omega_1}{3} = i\omega_0$ ✓)
- $\dot{z}_2$: $z_1^3\,\bar{z}_2$ (eigenvalue $3i\omega_0 - i\omega_1 = 3\cdot\f
rac{2}{3}i\omega_1 - i\omega_1 = i\omega_1$ ✓)

**Normal form (up to degree 4):**
$$\dot{z}_1 = (\alpha_1 + i\omega_1)\,z_1 + c_{10}\,z_1|z_1|^2 + c_{01}\,z_1|z_2
|^2 + a\,\bar{z}_1^2 z_2^2 + \cdots$$
$$\dot{z}_2 = (\alpha_2 + i\omega_2)\,z_2 + d_{10}\,z_2|z_1|^2 + d_{01}\,z_2|z_2
|^2 + b\,z_1^3\bar{z}_2 + \cdots$$

## 6. Summary

| | Nonresonant ($\omega_0/\omega_1 \notin \mathbb{Q}$) | Resonant ($\omega_0/\o
mega_1 = m/n$) |
|---|---|---|
| **Resonant monomials in $\dot{z}_1$** | $z_1\|z_1\|^{2j}\|z_2\|^{2k}$ only | $
z_1\|z_1\|^{2j}\|z_2\|^{2k}$ **plus** $\bar{z}_1^{ns-1}z_2^{ms}\|z_1\|^{2j}\|z_2
\|^{2k}$ and $z_1^{ns+1}\bar{z}_2^{ms}\|z_1\|^{2j}\|z_2\|^{2k}$ for $s\geq 1$ |
| **Resonant monomials in $\dot{z}_2$** | $z_2\|z_1\|^{2j}\|z_2\|^{2k}$ only | $
z_2\|z_1\|^{2j}\|z_2\|^{2k}$ **plus** $z_1^{ns}\bar{z}_2^{ms-1}\|z_1\|^{2j}\|z_2
\|^{2k}$ and $\bar{z}_1^{ns}z_2^{ms+1}\|z_1\|^{2j}\|z_2\|^{2k}$ for $s\geq 1$ |
| **Lowest extra term degree** | — | $m+n-1$ |
| **Symmetry** | $S^1 \times S^1$ (independent phases) | $S^1$ (linked: $z_1\to
e^{in\varphi}z_1,\; z_2\to e^{im\varphi}z_2$) |
| **Amplitude/phase decoupling** | Yes (at cubic order) | No (phases couple thro
ugh resonant terms) |

The key structural difference is that in the nonresonant case, the normal form h
as $S^1\times S^1$ symmetry, amplitude equations decouple, and the dynamics redu
ces to a 2D amplitude system (plus drifting phases). In the resonant case, the s
ymmetry reduces to a single $S^1$, additional monomials $\bar{z}_1^{n-1}z_2^m$ (
in $\dot{z}_1$) and $z_1^n\bar{z}_2^{m-1}$ (in $\dot{z}_2$) appear at degree $m+
n-1$, and phase-locking between the two oscillators becomes possible, leading to
 qualitatively richer bifurcation diagrams including torus breakdown and period-
doubling cascades.
