## Solution

**Setting up the system.** Let $e_1 = x_1+x_2+x_3+x_4$ and $e_2 = \sum_{i<j} x_i
 x_j$ be the elementary symmetric polynomials. For the chamber missing $x_k$, th
e sum of pairwise products of the other three equals $e_2 - x_k(e_1 - x_k)$. So
each chamber equation becomes:

$$e_2 - e_1 x_k + x_k^2 + x_k = 2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x_k^2 + (1-e_1)\,x_k
 + (e_2-2) = 0.$$

**Key observation.** Every $x_k$ ($k=1,2,3,4$) satisfies the *same* quadratic eq
uation:

$$t^2 + (1-e_1)\,t + (e_2 - 2) = 0.$$

A quadratic has at most two distinct roots, so the four $x_i$ take at most two d
istinct values, say $a$ and $b$ (with $a+b = e_1-1$ and $ab = e_2-2$ by Vieta's)
.

---

**Case 1: All four equal** ($x_1=x_2=x_3=x_4=a$). Each equation gives $3a^2+a=2$
, i.e., $3a^2+a-2=0=(3a-2)(a+1)$, so $a=\tfrac{2}{3}$ or $a=-1$.

- $(\tfrac{2}{3},\tfrac{2}{3},\tfrac{2}{3},\tfrac{2}{3})$: product $= \left(\tfr
ac{2}{3}\right)^4 = \tfrac{16}{81}$.
- $(-1,-1,-1,-1)$: product $= 1$.

**Case 2: Three equal $a$, one equal $b$** (WLOG $x_1=x_2=x_3=a,\; x_4=b$). Then
 $e_1=3a+b$, $e_2=3a^2+3ab$.

- From Vieta's: $a+b = e_1-1 = 3a+b-1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2a=1 \;\Rightarrow\; a=\tf
rac{1}{2}$.
- From $ab = e_2-2 = 3a^2+3ab-2$: $\;-2ab = 3a^2-2 \;\Rightarrow\; b = \frac{2-3
a^2}{2a} = \frac{2-3/4}{1} = \tfrac{5}{4}$.

**Verification** with $(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac54)$:
- Chamber A: $3\cdot\tfrac14 + \tfrac54 = \tfrac34+\tfrac54 = 2$ ✓
- Chamber B: $\tfrac14 + 2\cdot\tfrac{5}{8} + \tfrac12 = \tfrac14+\tfrac54+\tfra
c12 = 2$ ✓
- Chambers C, D: identical by symmetry ✓

This gives **4 ordered configurations** (choosing which coordinate is $\tfrac54$
), each with product $\left(\tfrac12\right)^3\cdot\tfrac54 = \tfrac{5}{32}$. Tot
al: $4\times\tfrac{5}{32} = \tfrac{5}{8}$.

**Case 3: Two equal $a$, two equal $b$** ($a\neq b$). Then $e_1=2a+2b$, $e_2=a^2
+b^2+4ab$.

- Vieta's: $a+b = 2(a+b)-1 \;\Rightarrow\; a+b=1$.
- $ab = e_2-2 = (1-2ab)+4ab-2 = 2ab-1 \;\Rightarrow\; ab=1$.

But $a+b=1,\; ab=1$ gives discriminant $1-4=-3<0$: **no real solutions**.

(The $k=1$ split is the same as Case 2 with labels swapped — no new configuratio
ns.)

 | $\tfrac{16}{81}$ |

| Three $\tfrac12$, one $\tfrac54$ (4 orderings) | $\tfrac{5}{32}$ | 4 | $\tfrac
{5}{8}$ |

**Sum of all products:**

$$\frac{16}{81} + 1 + \frac{5}{8} = \frac{128}{648} + \frac{648}{648} + \frac{40
5}{648} = \frac{1181}{648}.$$

Since $\gcd(1181, 648) = 1$, this is already in lowest terms.

$$\boxed{\dfrac{1181}{648}}$$
