## Proof

Let $f(z) = \frac{e^{-z/2 - z^2/4}}{\sqrt{1-z}} = \sum_{n \geq 0} a_n z^n$, wher
e we take the principal branch of $(1-z)^{-1/2}$ with branch cut on $[1,\infty)$
.

### Identifying the dominant singularity

The factor $e^{-z/2 - z^2/4}$ is entire, so the only singularity of $f$ is the b
ranch point at $z = 1$ coming from $(1-z)^{-1/2}$. This is the unique singularit
y on the circle of convergence $|z| = 1$, so it dominates the asymptotic behavio
r of the coefficients.

### Local expansion near $z = 1$

Set $w = 1 - z$, so $w \to 0$ as $z \to 1$. Then:

$$-\frac{z}{2} - \frac{z^2}{4} = -\frac{1-w}{2} - \frac{(1-w)^2}{4} = -\frac{1}{
2} + \frac{w}{2} - \frac{1 - 2w + w^2}{4} = -\frac{3}{4} + w - \frac{w^2}{4}.$$

Hence:

$$f(z) = e^{-3/4} \cdot e^{w - w^2/4} \cdot w^{-1/2}.$$

Expanding the analytic factor:

$$e^{w - w^2/4} = 1 + \left(w - \frac{w^2}{4}\right) + \frac{1}{2}\left(w - \fra
c{w^2}{4}\right)^2 + \cdots = 1 + w + \frac{w^2}{4} + \cdots$$

So:

$$f(z) = e^{-3/4}\left(w^{-1/2} + w^{1/2} + \frac{1}{4}w^{3/2} + \cdots\right),
\qquad w = 1-z.$$

### Applying the transfer theorem

By the singularity analysis transfer theorem (Flajolet–Sedgewick): for $\alpha \
notin \{0, 1, 2, \ldots\}$,

$$[z^n]\,(1-z)^{\alpha} \sim \frac{n^{-\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(-\alpha)}, \qquad n \to
 \infty.$$

Applying this term by term:

- $[z^n]\,w^{-1/2} = [z^n]\,(1-z)^{-1/2} \sim \dfrac{n^{-1/2}}{\Gamma(1/2)} = \d
frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi n}}.$

- $[z^n]\,w^{1/2} = [z^n]\,(1-z)^{1/2} \sim \dfrac{n^{-3/2}}{\Gamma(-1/2)} = -\d
frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}\, n^{3/2}}.$

- $[z^n]\,w^{3/2} \sim \dfrac{n^{-5/2}}{\Gamma(-3/2)} = \dfrac{3}{4\sqrt{\pi}\,
n^{5/2}}.$

Combining:

$$a_n \sim e^{-3/4}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi n}} - \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}\,n^{3/2}}
 + \frac{3}{16\sqrt{\pi}\,n^{5/2}} + \cdots\right) = \frac{e^{-3/4}}{\sqrt{\pi n
}}\left(1 - \frac{1}{2n} + \frac{3}{16\,n^2} + O(n^{-3})\right).$$

### Result

The asymptotic estimate of the coefficients is:

$$\boxed{a_n = [z^n]\,\frac{e^{-z/2-z^2/4}}{\sqrt{1-z}} \sim \frac{e^{-3/4}}{\sq
rt{\pi\, n}} \quad \text{as } n \to \infty.}$$

The key steps are: (1) the exponential factor is entire so $z=1$ is the unique d
ominant singularity; (2) evaluating the exponential at $z=1$ gives the constant
$e^{-3/4}$; (3) the leading singular type is $(1-z)^{-1/2}$, whose coefficients
behave as $n^{-1/2}/\Gamma(1/2) = 1/\sqrt{\pi n}$ by the transfer theorem.
