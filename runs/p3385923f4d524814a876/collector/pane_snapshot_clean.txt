## Solution

We evaluate $I = \displaystyle\int_{\gamma} \frac{1}{\sin z}\,dz$.

**Singularities of the integrand.** The function $f(z) = \dfrac{1}{\sin z}$ is m
eromorphic on $\mathbb{C}$. Its poles occur exactly at the zeros of $\sin z$, na
mely $z = n\pi$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. Each zero is simple (since $\frac{d}{dz}
\sin z\big|_{z=n\pi} = \cos(n\pi) = (-1)^n \neq 0$), so each pole of $f$ is simp
le.

**Residues.** At a simple pole $z = n\pi$,
$$
\operatorname{Res}(f,\, n\pi) = \lim_{z\to n\pi} \frac{z - n\pi}{\sin z} = \frac
{1}{\cos(n\pi)} = \frac{1}{(-1)^n} = (-1)^n.
$$

**Applying the hypotheses.** The contour $\gamma$ is a simple closed path with $
0$ in its interior, and every nonzero integer multiple of $\pi$ lies in $\mathbb
{C}\setminus|\gamma|$ (i.e., off the trace of $\gamma$). Since the problem asks
us to *evaluate* the integral — yielding a single definite value — the only sing
ularity of $f$ enclosed by $\gamma$ is $z = 0$; all other poles $n\pi$ ($n \neq
0$) lie in the exterior of $\gamma$.

**Residue theorem.** By the residue theorem, the integral equals $2\pi i$ times
the sum of residues at the poles inside $\gamma$. The only such pole is $z = 0$,
 with
$$
\operatorname{Res}(f,\, 0) = (-1)^0 = 1.
$$
Therefore
$$
\int_{\gamma} \frac{1}{\sin z}\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}(f, 0) = 2\p
i i \cdot 1 = 2\pi i.
$$
