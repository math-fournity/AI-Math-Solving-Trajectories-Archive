# Solution

**Answer: Yes.** If $G+G$ has positive (two-dimensional Lebesgue) measure, then
it necessarily has nonempty interior.

---

## Proof

**Step 1: Parametrize $G+G$ and identify its fibers.**

Write $G = \{(x, f(x)) : x \in [0,1]\}$. Then
$$G + G = \{(x+y,\; f(x)+f(y)) : x,y \in [0,1]\}.$$
Set $s = x+y \in [0,2]$. For a fixed $s$, the admissible $x$-values form
$$I_s := [\max(0,s-1),\;\min(1,s)],$$
and the fiber of $G+G$ over $s$ (i.e., the set of second coordinates paired with
 first coordinate $s$) is
$$F_s := \{f(x) + f(s-x) : x \in I_s\}.$$
Since $x \mapsto f(x) + f(s-x)$ is continuous and $I_s$ is compact and connected
, $F_s$ is a compact connected subset of $\mathbb{R}$, hence a closed interval:
$$F_s = [m(s),\, M(s)], \qquad m(s) := \min_{x\in I_s}\bigl(f(x)+f(s-x)\bigr),\q
uad M(s) := \max_{x\in I_s}\bigl(f(x)+f(s-x)\bigr).$$
Therefore
$$G + G = \bigl\{(s,t) : s\in[0,2],\; m(s)\le t \le M(s)\bigr\}.$$

**Step 2: $m$ and $M$ are continuous on $[0,2]$.**

Consider the compact set
$$K := \{(s,x) : s\in[0,2],\; x\in I_s\} \subset \mathbb{R}^2,$$
which is closed (defined by continuous inequalities $\max(0,s-1)\le x\le \min(1,
s)$) and bounded, hence compact. The function
$$h(s,x) := f(x) + f(s-x)$$
is continuous on $K$. The correspondence $s \mapsto I_s$ is continuous (compact-
valued, continuous in the Hausdorff metric). By Berge's maximum theorem, the val
ue functions
$$M(s) = \max_{x\in I_s} h(s,x), \qquad m(s) = \min_{x\in I_s} h(s,x)$$
are continuous on $[0,2]$. Clearly $m(s)\le M(s)$ for all $s$.

**Step 3: Positive measure forces $M-m$ to be positive on an open interval.**

Since $G+G$ is compact (continuous image of $[0,1]^2$), it is measurable; its me
asure is given by Fubini/Cavalieri as
$$|G+G| = \int_0^2 \bigl(M(s) - m(s)\bigr)\,ds.$$
(The fiber over $s$ is the interval $[m(s),M(s)]$ of length $M(s)-m(s)$.) The in
tegrand $M - m \ge 0$ is continuous. If the integral is strictly positive, then
$M - m$ cannot vanish identically, so there exists $s_0$ with $M(s_0) > m(s_0)$.
 By continuity, there is an open interval $J \ni s_0$ on which $M(s) > m(s)$ for
 all $s\in J$.

**Step 4: $G+G$ has nonempty interior.**

Consider the set
$$U := \{(s,t) : s\in J,\; m(s) < t < M(s)\}.$$
Since $m$ and $M$ are continuous, $U$ is open (it is the intersection of the ope
n strips $\{t > m(s)\}$ and $\{t < M(s)\}$ with the open set $J\times\mathbb{R}$
). It is nonempty: pick any $s\in J$ and any $t$ strictly between $m(s)$ and $M(
s)$. Moreover $U \subseteq G+G$ since $m(s)\le t\le M(s)$ there. Hence $U$ is a
nonempty open subset of $G+G$, i.e., $G+G$ has nonempty interior. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** The hypothesis of positive measure is essential: for $f(x)=x$ one ge
ts $G+G = \{(s,s):s\in[0,2]\}$, a line segment of measure zero (and empty interi
or); for constant $f$, $G+G$ is a horizontal segment, also of measure zero. The
argument shows that the only way $G+G$ can have positive measure is for the vert
ical fibers $[m(s),M(s)]$ to have positive length on a set of positive $s$-measu
re, and continuity of $m,M$ then automatically promotes this to an open region.
