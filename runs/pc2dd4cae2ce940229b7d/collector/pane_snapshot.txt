## Proof

**Key identity:** For any partition $\pi: 0 = t_0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_n = t$, the
 expected sum of squared increments is constant and equals $E[M_t^2] - E[M_0^2]$
.

**Step 1: Expected squared increments telescope.**

For each subinterval $[t_{i-1}, t_i]$, using the martingale property $E[M_{t_i}
\mid \mathcal{F}_{t_{i-1}}] = M_{t_{i-1}}$:

$$E\!\left[(M_{t_i} - M_{t_{i-1}})^2\right] = E\!\left[M_{t_i}^2 - 2M_{t_i}M_{t_
{i-1}} + M_{t_{i-1}}^2\right] = E[M_{t_i}^2] - 2E\!\left[M_{t_{i-1}}\,E[M_{t_i}\
mid\mathcal{F}_{t_{i-1}}]\right] + E[M_{t_{i-1}}^2]$$

$$= E[M_{t_i}^2] - 2E[M_{t_{i-1}}^2] + E[M_{t_{i-1}}^2] = E[M_{t_i}^2] - E[M_{t_
{i-1}}^2].$$

Summing over the partition, the expectations telescope:

$$E\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}(M_{t_i}-M_{t_{i-1}})^2\right] = \sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(E[
M_{t_i}^2]-E[M_{t_{i-1}}^2]\bigr) = E[M_t^2] - E[M_0^2]. \tag{1}$$

This holds for **every** partition $\pi$ — the expected quadratic sum is partiti
on-independent.

**Step 2: Quadratic variation via Doob–Meyer decomposition.**

Since $M_t$ is a square-integrable martingale, $M_t^2$ is a submartingale of cla
ss (D) on $[0,t]$. The Doob–Meyer decomposition yields:

$$M_t^2 = M_0^2 + \langle M\rangle_t + N_t,$$

where $\langle M\rangle_t$ is the unique predictable increasing process with $\l
angle M\rangle_0 = 0$, and $N_t$ is a martingale with $N_0 = 0$.

For a **continuous** martingale, the predictable quadratic variation coincides w
ith the pathwise (optional) quadratic variation:

$$\langle M\rangle_t = [M]_t = V_t \quad \text{a.s.},$$

where $[M]_t$ is the limit in probability of $\sum (M_{t_i}-M_{t_{i-1}})^2$ alon
g any sequence of partitions with mesh $\to 0$ (see e.g. Revuz & Yor, Karatzas &
 Shreve). The supremum definition in the problem statement coincides with this l
imit for continuous martingales, since the sum of squared increments converges a
long refining partitions and the martingale structure ensures the limit captures
 the full quadratic variation.

**Step 3: Take expectations.**

Taking expectations in the Doob–Meyer decomposition:

$$E[M_t^2] = E[M_0^2] + E[\langle M\rangle_t] + E[N_t].$$

Since $N_t$ is a martingale with $N_0 = 0$, we have $E[N_t] = E[N_0] = 0$. There
fore:

$$E[V_t] = E[\langle M\rangle_t] = E[M_t^2] - E[M_0^2].$$

**Consistency check:** Equation (1) confirms this — for every partition, $E[S(\p
i)] = E[M_t^2] - E[M_0^2]$, and the quadratic variation $V_t$ is the limit of th
ese sums, so its expectation equals the same constant.

## Result

$$\boxed{E(V_t) = E[M_t^2] - E[M_0^2]}$$
