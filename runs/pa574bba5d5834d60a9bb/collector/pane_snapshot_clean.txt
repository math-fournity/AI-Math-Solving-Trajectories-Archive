## Proof

**Claim.** The constant function $u \equiv 1$ satisfies $u \in W^{1,p}(\Omega)$
but $u \notin W_0^{1,p}(\Omega)$. Consequently $W_0^{1,p}(\Omega) \neq W^{1,p}(\
Omega)$.

Here $\Omega = B^n(0,1) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ and $p \in [1,\infty)$.

---

### Step 1. $u \equiv 1$ belongs to $W^{1,p}(\Omega)$.

The function $u(x) = 1$ is smooth on $\overline{\Omega}$. Its weak gradient is $
\nabla u \equiv 0$. Hence

$$
\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}^p = \int_\Omega |u|^p\,dx + \int_\Omega |\nabla u|^p\,dx
 = \int_\Omega 1\,dx + 0 = |\Omega| < \infty.
$$

Since $|\Omega| = |B^n(0,1)| = \omega_n < \infty$, we have $u \in W^{1,p}(\Omega
)$.

---

### Step 2. Poincaré inequality for $W_0^{1,p}(\Omega)$.

We use the standard fact: there exists a constant $C = C(\Omega, p) > 0$ such th
at for every $v \in W_0^{1,p}(\Omega)$,

$$
\int_\Omega |v|^p\,dx \;\le\; C \int_\Omega |\nabla v|^p\,dx. \tag{$\star$}
$$

*Proof sketch of $(\star)$.* For $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$, extend by zer
o to $\mathbb{R}^n$ and apply the standard Poincaré inequality on a ball (or use
 the representation $\varphi(x) = \int_0^\infty \partial_r \varphi(x + r\omega)\
,dr$ integrated over directions, then Jensen/Hölder). This gives $(\star)$ for t
est functions; the general case follows by density of $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ in $W
_0^{1,p}(\Omega)$ and continuity of both sides of $(\star)$ in the $W^{1,p}$-nor
m.

---

### Step 3. $u \equiv 1$ does **not** belong to $W_0^{1,p}(\Omega)$.

Suppose for contradiction that $u \in W_0^{1,p}(\Omega)$. Applying $(\star)$ to
$v = u$:

$$
\int_\Omega |u|^p\,dx \;\le\; C \int_\Omega |\nabla u|^p\,dx.
$$

Since $u \equiv 1$ and $\nabla u \equiv 0$, this gives

$$
|\Omega| \;=\; \int_\Omega 1\,dx \;\le\; C \cdot 0 \;=\; 0.
$$

But $|\Omega| = |B^n(0,1)| = \omega_n > 0$, a contradiction. Therefore $u \notin
 W_0^{1,p}(\Omega)$.

---

### Step 4. Conclusion.

We have exhibited $u \equiv 1$ with

$$
u \in W^{1,p}(\Omega) \quad\text{but}\quad u \notin W_0^{1,p}(\Omega).
$$

Since $W_0^{1,p}(\Omega) \subseteq W^{1,p}(\Omega)$ always holds (as the closure
 of $C_c^\infty(\Omega) \subseteq W^{1,p}(\Omega)$), the existence of such a $u$
 proves the inclusion is strict:

$$
W_0^{1,p}(\Omega) \;\subsetneq\; W^{1,p}(\Omega).
$$

Equivalently, $W_0^{1,p}(\Omega) \neq W^{1,p}(\Omega)$. $\blacksquare$
