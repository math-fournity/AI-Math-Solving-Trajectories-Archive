## Proof

We seek the asymptotic expansion of
$$\Gamma(p, x) = \int_{x}^{\infty} t^{p-1} e^{-t}\,dt, \qquad x \gg 1,$$
via repeated integration by parts.

### Setup of one integration-by-parts step

Set
$$u = t^{p-1}, \quad dv = e^{-t}\,dt \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; du = (p-1)t^{p-2}\,
dt, \quad v = -e^{-t}.$$

Then
$$\Gamma(p,x) = \Big[-t^{p-1}e^{-t}\Big]_{x}^{\infty} + (p-1)\int_{x}^{\infty} t
^{p-2}e^{-t}\,dt.$$

The boundary term at $t=\infty$ vanishes (exponential decay dominates any power)
, and at $t=x$ it contributes $+x^{p-1}e^{-x}$. Hence the recurrence
$$\Gamma(p,x) = x^{p-1}e^{-x} + (p-1)\,\Gamma(p-1,x). \tag{1}$$

### Iteration

Apply (1) to $\Gamma(p-1,x)$:
$$\Gamma(p-1,x) = x^{p-2}e^{-x} + (p-2)\,\Gamma(p-2,x).$$

Substituting back:
$$\Gamma(p,x) = e^{-x}\big[x^{p-1} + (p-1)x^{p-2}\big] + (p-1)(p-2)\,\Gamma(p-2,
x). \tag{2}$$

Apply (1) once more to $\Gamma(p-2,x)$:
$$\Gamma(p-2,x) = x^{p-3}e^{-x} + (p-3)\,\Gamma(p-3,x).$$

Substituting into (2):
$$\Gamma(p,x) = e^{-x}\big[x^{p-1} + (p-1)x^{p-2} + (p-1)(p-2)x^{p-3}\big] + (p-
1)(p-2)(p-3)\,\Gamma(p-3,x). \tag{3}$$

### Asymptotic ordering ($x \gg 1$)

Each successive term in the bracket is smaller than the previous one by a factor
 of $x$:
$$x^{p-1} \;\gg\; x^{p-2} \;\gg\; x^{p-3}.$$

The remainder $(p-1)(p-2)(p-3)\,\Gamma(p-3,x)$ is of order $x^{p-4}e^{-x}$ (sinc
e $\Gamma(p-3,x) \sim x^{p-4}e^{-x}$ by the leading term of (1)), which is small
er than the last retained term $x^{p-3}e^{-x}$ by a factor of $1/x$. Thus trunca
ting after three terms gives a valid asymptotic expansion to the displayed order
.

### Result

Factoring out $x^{p-1}e^{-x}$, the first three terms of the asymptotic series ar
e

$$\boxed{\;\Gamma(p,x) \sim x^{p-1}e^{-x}\left[1 + \frac{p-1}{x} + \frac{(p-1)(p
-2)}{x^{2}} + \cdots\right], \qquad x \to \infty.\;}$$

Equivalently, in un-factored form:
$$\Gamma(p,x) \sim e^{-x}\Big[x^{p-1} + (p-1)x^{p-2} + (p-1)(p-2)x^{p-3} + \cdot
s\Big].$$

The general $n$-th term (for $n \ge 0$) is
$$e^{-x}\,\frac{(p-1)(p-2)\cdots(p-n)}{x^{\,n-p+1}} \;=\; x^{p-1}e^{-x}\,\frac{(
p-1)(p-2)\cdots(p-n)}{x^{n}},$$
confirming the pattern exhibited by the first three terms.
