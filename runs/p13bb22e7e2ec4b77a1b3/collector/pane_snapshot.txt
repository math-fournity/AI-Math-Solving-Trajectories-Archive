## Spectrum of the One-Dimensional Quantum Harmonic Oscillator

We determine the spectrum of the self-adjoint operator on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ defi
ned by

$$Pu = -\frac{d^2 u}{dx^2} + x^2 u.$$

### Step 1. Ladder operators

Introduce the (formal) operators on Schwartz functions $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})$
:

$$a = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(x + \frac{d}{dx}\right), \qquad a^\dagger = \frac{
1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(x - \frac{d}{dx}\right).$$

A direct computation gives the canonical commutation relation

$$[a, a^\dagger] = aa^\dagger - a^\dagger a = 1,$$

and the factorization

$$P = -\frac{d^2}{dx^2} + x^2 = 2\,a^\dagger a + 1.$$

Define the **number operator** $N = a^\dagger a$, so $P = 2N + 1$.

### Step 2. A ground state

Solve $a\,\psi_0 = 0$:

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(x + \frac{d}{dx}\right)\psi_0 = 0 \implies \psi_0'(x)
= -x\,\psi_0(x) \implies \psi_0(x) = C\,e^{-x^2/2}.$$

With $C = \pi^{-1/4}$, $\|\psi_0\|_{L^2} = 1$. Then

$$P\psi_0 = (2N+1)\psi_0 = 1\cdot\psi_0,$$

so $\psi_0$ is a normalized eigenfunction with eigenvalue $\lambda_0 = 1$.

### Step 3. The ladder generates eigenvalues

If $P\psi = \lambda\psi$, then using $[P,a^\dagger]=2a^\dagger$ and $[P,a]=-2a$:

$$P(a^\dagger\psi) = a^\dagger P\psi + 2a^\dagger\psi = (\lambda+2)\,a^\dagger\p
si,$$
$$P(a\psi) = aP\psi - 2a\psi = (\lambda-2)\,a\psi.$$

Thus $a^\dagger$ raises the eigenvalue by $2$ and $a$ lowers it by $2$.

### Step 4. Eigenvalues are bounded below

For any eigenfunction $\psi$ with $P\psi=\lambda\psi$,

$$\lambda = \langle \psi, P\psi\rangle = 2\|a\psi\|^2 + \|\psi\|^2 \ge 1,$$

with equality iff $a\psi=0$, i.e. $\psi\propto\psi_0$. So eigenvalues cannot des
cend below $1$.

Applying the lowering operator repeatedly to any eigenfunction must terminate at
 $\psi_0$ after finitely many steps (otherwise we would produce an eigenfunction
 with eigenvalue $<1$, contradicting the bound). Hence every eigenvalue is of th
e form $1 + 2n$ for some $n\in\mathbb{N}_0$.

### Step 5. All values $2n+1$ occur, with multiplicity one

Define

$$\psi_n = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}}\,(a^\dagger)^n \psi_0.$$

By the raising relation, $P\psi_n = (2n+1)\psi_n$. Using $[a,(a^\dagger)^n]=n(a^
\dagger)^{n-1}$ one shows $\|\psi_n\|=1$ and $\langle\psi_m,\psi_n\rangle=\delta
_{mn}$, so the $\{\psi_n\}_{n\ge 0}$ form an orthonormal family. Explicitly,

$$\psi_n(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2^n n!}}\,\frac{1}{\pi^{1/4}}\,H_n(x)\,e^{-x^2/2},$
$

where $H_n$ is the $n$-th Hermite polynomial.

### Step 6. Completeness of the eigenbasis

The functions $\{H_n(x)e^{-x^2/2}\}$ are well known to be complete in $L^2(\math
bb{R})$ (e.g. the Hermite functions form a complete orthonormal system; this fol
lows from the fact that their Fourier transform permutes them, so any $L^2$ func
tion orthogonal to all of them must vanish). Therefore there are no other eigenv
alues, and $P$ has no continuous spectrum.

### Step 7. Self-adjointness and discreteness

$P$ is essentially self-adjoint on $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})$ (its coefficients a
re real and the potential $x^2$ is confining; equivalently the deficiency indice
s are $(0,0)$). Since the potential $x^2\to\infty$ as $|x|\to\infty$, the resolv
ent $(P-zI)^{-1}$ is compact for $z$ in the resolvent set, so the spectrum is pu
rely discrete, consisting entirely of the eigenvalues found above.

### Conclusion

The spectrum of $P = -\dfrac{d^2}{dx^2} + x^2$ is purely discrete:

$$\boxed{\sigma(P) = \{\,2n+1 : n = 0,1,2,\dots\,\} = \{1,\,3,\,5,\,7,\,\dots\},
}$$

with each eigenvalue $2n+1$ simple (multiplicity one), and a corresponding norma
lized eigenfunction

$$\psi_n(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2^n n!\,\sqrt{\pi}}}\,H_n(x)\,e^{-x^2/2}.$$
