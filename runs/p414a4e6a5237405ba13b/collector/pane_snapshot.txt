### 1. Existence of a Dualizing Complex

**Setup.** Let $S$ be a finite-dimensional Gorenstein ring (Noetherian, finite K
rull dimension, and $\operatorname{id}_S S < \infty$), and let $R \cong S/I$ for
 some ideal $I \subseteq S$.

**Claim.** $R$ admits a dualizing complex. Explicitly, $D_R^{\bullet} := R\opera
torname{Hom}_S(R, S)$ is a dualizing complex for $R$.

**Proof.** We verify the three defining properties of a dualizing complex.

**(a) $D_R^{\bullet} \in D^b_c(R)$ (bounded with finitely generated cohomology).
**

Since $R = S/I$ is finitely generated as an $S$-module, $\operatorname{Hom}_S(R,
 S)$ is a finitely generated $S$-module, hence a finitely generated $R$-module.
Because $S$ is Gorenstein (in particular, $S$ has finite injective dimension, sa
y $\operatorname{id}_S S = n < \infty$), an injective resolution $S \to E^{\bull
et}$ of $S$ as an $S$-module has length $n$. Applying $\operatorname{Hom}_S(R, -
)$ to this bounded injective resolution yields a bounded complex of $R$-modules:

$$D_R^{\bullet} = \operatorname{Hom}_S(R, E^{\bullet}), \quad \text{with } E^i =
 0 \text{ for } i > n.$$

Each term $\operatorname{Hom}_S(R, E^i)$ is an $R$-module, and the cohomology $H
^i(D_R^{\bullet}) = \operatorname{Ext}^i_S(R, S)$ is finitely generated over $S$
 (hence over $R$) since $R$ is finitely generated over the Noetherian ring $S$.
Thus $D_R^{\bullet} \in D^b_c(R)$.

**(b) $D_R^{\bullet}$ has finite injective dimension over $R$.**

The complex $D_R^{\bullet}$ is built from the bounded injective resolution $E^{\
bullet}$ of $S$ over itself. Each $E^i$ is an injective $S$-module, and $\operat
orname{Hom}_S(R, E^i)$ is an injective $R$-module (since $\operatorname{Hom}_R(-
, \operatorname{Hom}_S(R, E^i)) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_S(R \otimes_R -, E^i) \
cong \operatorname{Hom}_S(-, E^i)$ is exact). Therefore $D_R^{\bullet}$ is repre
sented by a bounded complex of injective $R$-modules of length $\leq n$, giving
$\operatorname{id}_R D_R^{\bullet} \leq n < \infty$.

**(c) The double-duality map $R \to R\operatorname{Hom}_R(D_R^{\bullet}, D_R^{\b
ullet})$ is an isomorphism.**

This is the standard **Grothendieck duality** construction. Since $S$ is Gorenst
ein, $S$ (concentrated in degree 0) is a dualizing complex for $S$. The functor
$R\operatorname{Hom}_S(R, -)$ transports the dualizing complex from $S$ to $R$.
By the general theory of residual complexes and dualizing complexes (Grothendiec
k–Hartshorne *Residues and Duality*), if $f: \operatorname{Spec} R \to \operator
name{Spec} S$ is the closed immersion induced by $S \twoheadrightarrow R$, then

$$D_R^{\bullet} = f^! D_S^{\bullet} = R\operatorname{Hom}_S(R, S),$$

and the adjunction/counit formalism gives the isomorphism $R \xrightarrow{\sim}
R\operatorname{Hom}_R(D_R^{\bullet}, D_R^{\bullet})$.

Concretely, one verifies: $R\operatorname{Hom}_R(D_R^{\bullet}, D_R^{\bullet}) =

,S)\big)$. Using the tensor-evaluation isomorphism and the fact that $R \otimes^
L_R R\operatorname{Hom}_S(R,S) \cong R\operatorname{Hom}_S(R,S)$, together with
the standard adjunction $R\operatorname{Hom}_R(-, R\operatorname{Hom}_S(R,S)) \c

tains the desired isomorphism $R \xrightarrow{\sim} R\operatorname{Hom}_R(D_R^{\
bullet}, D_R^{\bullet})$.

**Conclusion of Part 1.** $D_R^{\bullet} = R\operatorname{Hom}_S(R, S)$ is a dua
lizing complex for $R$. Therefore **$R$ admits a dualizing complex.** $\square$

### 2. Relation to the Gorenstein Property of $R$

**Claim.** Let $R$ be a Noetherian ring admitting a dualizing complex $D_R^{\bul

$$R \text{ is Gorenstein} \iff D_R^{\bullet} \cong R[\sigma] \text{ in } D^b(R)
\text{ for some } \sigma \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

In the local case $(R, \mathfrak{m}, k)$ with the **normalized** dualizing compl
ex (normalized so that $H^{-\dim R}(D_R^{\bullet}) \neq 0$ and $H^i(D_R^{\bullet

$$R \text{ is Gorenstein} \iff D_R^{\bullet} \cong R[-\dim R] \text{ in } D^b(R)
,$$

i.e., the dualizing complex is quasi-isomorphic to $R$ concentrated in a single
degree.

**Proof.**

**($\Leftarrow$) Suppose $D_R^{\bullet} \cong R[\sigma]$ for some $\sigma$.** Th
en $R[\sigma]$ has finite injective dimension (since $D_R^{\bullet}$ does, by pr
operty (b) above), so $\operatorname{id}_R R = \operatorname{id}_R R[\sigma] < \
infty$. A Noetherian ring of finite injective dimension is Gorenstein by definit
ion. Hence $R$ is Gorenstein.

**($\Rightarrow$) Suppose $R$ is Gorenstein, i.e., $\operatorname{id}_R R < \inf
ty$.** Then $R$ (concentrated in degree 0) itself satisfies all three defining p
roperties of a dualizing complex:
- $R \in D^b_c(R)$: trivially bounded with finitely generated cohomology.
- $\operatorname{id}_R R < \infty$: by the Gorenstein hypothesis.
- $R\operatorname{Hom}_R(R, R) \cong R$: the double-duality map $R \to R\operato
rname{Hom}_R(R,R) \cong R$ is the identity.

So $R$ is itself a dualizing complex for $R$. By the **uniqueness of dualizing c
omplexes** (up to shift and tensoring with a rank-1 projective module): if $D_1^
{\bullet}$ and $D_2^{\bullet}$ are two dualizing complexes for $R$, then $D_2^{\
bullet} \cong D_1^{\bullet}[\sigma] \otimes_R L$ for some $\sigma \in \mathbb{Z}
$ and some invertible $R$-module $L$ (in the derived category).

Applying this to $D_1^{\bullet} = D_R^{\bullet}$ and $D_2^{\bullet} = R$:

$$R \cong D_R^{\bullet}[\sigma] \otimes^L_R L \quad \text{for some } \sigma, L.$
$

In the **local** case, every invertible module is free of rank 1, so $L \cong R$
 and:

$$D_R^{\bullet} \cong R[-\sigma] \quad \text{in } D^b(R).$$

With the normalization convention (the normalized dualizing complex has $H^{-\di
m R}(D_R^{\bullet}) \neq 0$ and vanishes above degree $-\dim R$), and since for
a Gorenstein local ring of dimension $d$, the injective resolution of $R$ has it
s top nonzero injective in degree $d$, we get $\sigma = -\dim R$, i.e.:

$$D_R^{\bullet} \cong R[-\dim R].$$

For the **non-local** case, the invertible module $L$ need not be trivial, but $
R$ is Gorenstein if and only if $D_R^{\bullet} \cong R[\sigma] \otimes_R L$ for
invertible $L$, which localizes to the shift condition at every prime. Equivalen
tly, $R$ is Gorenstein if and only if the dualizing complex is, up to shift and
line bundle twist, isomorphic to $R$ itself. $\square$
