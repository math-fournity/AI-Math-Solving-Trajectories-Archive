## Solution

We evaluate
$$S=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{2}n}{3^{m}\bigl(n3^{m}+m3^{n}
\bigr)}.$$

**Step 1. Rewrite the summand.** Expanding the denominator,
$$a_{m,n}:=\frac{m^{2}n}{n3^{2m}+m3^{m+n}}=\frac{m^{2}n}{3^{m+n}\bigl(m+n\,3^{m-
n}\bigr)}.$$

**Step 2. Symmetrize.** Swapping $m\leftrightarrow n$ gives
$$a_{n,m}=\frac{n^{2}m}{3^{m+n}\bigl(n+m\,3^{n-m}\bigr)}.$$

Setting $r=3^{m-n}$ (so $3^{n-m}=1/r$),
$$a_{m,n}+a_{n,m}=\frac{1}{3^{m+n}}\left[\frac{m^{2}n}{m+nr}+\frac{n^{2}m}{n+m/r
}\right]=\frac{1}{3^{m+n}}\cdot\frac{m^{2}n+n^{2}mr}{m+nr}.$$

Since $m^{2}n+n^{2}mr=mn(m+nr)$, the factor $(m+nr)$ cancels:
$$\boxed{a_{m,n}+a_{n,m}=\frac{mn}{3^{m+n}}.}$$

**Step 3. Use symmetry of the summation region.** Since both indices range over
$\{1,2,\dots\}$, relabeling $m\leftrightarrow n$ shows $\sum_{m,n\ge1}a_{m,n}=\s
um_{m,n\ge1}a_{n,m}=S$. Hence
$$2S=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(a_{m,n}+a_{n,m})=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{mn}{3^{m+n}}.$$

**Step 4. Factor and evaluate.** The double sum separates:
$$2S=\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m}{3^{m}}\right)\!\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\
frac{n}{3^{n}}\right).$$

Using $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}kx^{k}=\frac{x}{(1-x)^{2}}$ at $x=\tfrac13$:
$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{k}{3^{k}}=\frac{1/3}{(2/3)^{2}}=\frac{1/3}{4/9}=\frac
{3}{4}.$$

Therefore
$$2S=\frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{3}{4}=\frac{9}{16}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad S=\frac{9
}{32}.$$

**Numerical check.** For $m=1,n=2$: $a_{1,2}=\frac{2}{45}$, $a_{2,1}=\frac{4}{13
5}$, sum $=\frac{10}{135}=\frac{2}{27}=\frac{mn}{3^{m+n}}$. ✓
