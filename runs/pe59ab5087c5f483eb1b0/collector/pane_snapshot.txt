## Proof

**Claim.** Yes. If $R$ is a commutative ring with unity $1\neq 0$ and $R^m \cong
 R^n$ as $R$-modules, then $m=n$.

---

**Step 1. $R$ admits a maximal ideal.**

Since $R$ is a commutative ring with $1\neq 0$, the set of proper ideals of $R$
is nonempty (it contains $\{0\}$, which is proper because $1\notin\{0\}$) and pa
rtially ordered by inclusion. Every chain of proper ideals has an upper bound (i
ts union, which is again proper since $1$ is not in any member of the chain). By
 Zorn's Lemma, $R$ has a maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$.

**Step 2. $R/\mathfrak{m}$ is a field.**

This is the standard characterization of maximal ideals in commutative rings wit
h unity: $\mathfrak{m}$ is maximal iff $R/\mathfrak{m}$ is a field. Let $k := R/
\mathfrak{m}$.

**Step 3. Reduce the isomorphism modulo $\mathfrak{m}$.**

Suppose $\varphi : R^m \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} R^n$ is an $R$-module isomorphism.
 Apply the functor $-\otimes_R k$, equivalently, quotient out by $\mathfrak{m}$:

$$\bar{\varphi} : R^m \otimes_R k \longrightarrow R^n \otimes_R k.$$

Since $R^m \otimes_R k \cong k^m$ and $R^n \otimes_R k \cong k^n$ (as $k$-vector
 spaces, via $e_i \otimes 1 \mapsto e_i$), and since tensoring with $k$ preserve
s isomorphisms, $\bar\varphi$ is a $k$-linear isomorphism

$$k^m \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} k^n.$$

**Step 4. Conclude by dimension.**

A $k$-linear isomorphism between finite-dimensional vector spaces over the field
 $k$ preserves dimension, so

$$\dim_k k^m = \dim_k k^n,$$

i.e. $m = n$. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** The commutativity hypothesis is essential. The argument hinges on th
e existence of a two-sided maximal ideal whose quotient is a division ring (or f
ield), which is guaranteed for nonzero commutative unital rings. For general non
commutative rings the analogous statement — the *invariant basis number* (IBN) p
roperty — can fail (e.g. for the endomorphism ring $\operatorname{End}_k(V)$ of
an infinite-dimensional vector space, where $R \cong R^2$ as left $R$-modules).
