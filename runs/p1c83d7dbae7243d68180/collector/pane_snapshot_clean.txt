## Proof

**Claim.** Every locally finite graph $\Gamma$ with maximum degree $d$ (in parti
cular every $d$-regular vertex-transitive graph) admits a proper edge-coloring w
ith $d+1$ colors.

We combine two classical ingredients: **Vizing's theorem** (finite case) and a *
*compactness principle** for countable graphs.

---

### 1. Vizing's theorem (finite graphs)

For every finite (multi)graph $H$ with maximum degree $\Delta(H)\le d$, there ex
ists a proper edge-coloring $c:E(H)\to\{1,\dots,d+1\}$, i.e. a coloring in which
 incident edges receive distinct colors. This is Vizing's theorem.

---

### 2. Compactness setup

Let $C=\{1,\dots,d+1\}$ be the color set and let $E=E\Gamma$ be the (countable)
edge set. A *candidate coloring* is any function $c:E\to C$; the space of all ca
ndidates is the product
\[
\mathcal X \;=\; C^{E} \;=\; \prod_{e\in E} C.
\]
Since each factor $C$ is finite (hence compact Hausdorff) and $E$ is countable,
$\mathcal X$ is compact and metrizable — equivalently, this is the compactness g
iven by **Kőnig's infinity lemma** for countable products of finite sets, so no
choice beyond countable choice is needed.

For each vertex $v\in V\Gamma$ define the *local constraint set*
\[
F_v \;=\; \bigl\{\,c\in\mathcal X \;:\; \text{the edges incident to }v\text{ rec
eive pairwise distinct colors under }c\,\bigr\}.
\]
Because $v$ has finite degree (local finiteness), the condition "$c$ satisfies t
he constraint at $v$" depends on only finitely many coordinates of $c$, so $F_v$
 is a **closed** (indeed clopen) subset of $\mathcal X$.

A proper $(d+1)$-edge-coloring of $\Gamma$ is exactly an element of
\[
\bigcap_{v\in V\Gamma} F_v.
\]

---

### 3. Finite intersection property

We verify the finite intersection property: for every finite set $S\subseteq V\G
amma$,
\[
\bigcap_{v\in S} F_v \;\neq\; \varnothing.
\]

The constraint imposed by $\bigcup_{v\in S}F_v$ involves only the edges incident
 to some vertex of $S$. Because $\Gamma$ is locally finite and $S$ is finite, th
e set
\[
E_S \;=\; \{\,e\in E : e\text{ is incident to some }v\in S\,\}
\]
is **finite**. Let $H_S$ be the finite subgraph of $\Gamma$ with edge set $E_S$
(and the vertices touched by $E_S$). Every vertex of $H_S$ has degree $\le d$, s
o $\Delta(H_S)\le d$. By Vizing's theorem (§1), $H_S$ admits a proper edge-color
ing with $d+1$ colors. Extending this coloring arbitrarily to all of $E$ (e.g. c
oloring every edge outside $E_S$ with color $1$) yields an element of $\bigcap_{
v\in S}F_v$. Hence the intersection is non-empty.

---

### 4. Compactness yields a global coloring

The family $\{F_v\}_{v\in V\Gamma}$ is a family of closed subsets of the compact
 space $\mathcal X$ with the finite intersection property. By compactness,
\[
\bigcap_{v\in V\Gamma} F_v \;\neq\; \varnothing.
\]
Any element $c^{\star}$ of this intersection is, by definition of the $F_v$, a f
unction $c^{\star}:E\to\{1,\dots,d+1\}$ such that at every vertex the incident e
dges receive pairwise distinct colors. That is, $c^{\star}$ is a proper edge-col
oring of $\Gamma$ using $d+1$ colors.

---

### 5. Applying the hypotheses

The graph $\Gamma$ is $d$-regular (each vertex has degree exactly $d$, hence max
imum degree $d$), locally finite, and $V\Gamma$ is countably infinite. The argum
ent above uses only:
- **local finiteness** — so that each $F_v$ is closed and each $E_S$ is finite;
- **maximum degree $\le d$** — so Vizing's theorem applies to every $H_S$;
- **countability of $E$** — so that $\mathcal X=C^{E}$ is compact via Kőnig's le
mma (a fully elementary compactness principle).

Vertex-transitivity guarantees regularity (hence the uniform degree $d$) but is
not otherwise needed for the coloring argument; the result holds for arbitrary l
ocally finite graphs of maximum degree $d$.

---

### Conclusion

**Yes.** A locally finite, $d$-regular, vertex-transitive graph $\Gamma$ with co
untably infinite vertex set admits a proper edge-coloring with $d+1$ colors. The
 proof combines Vizing's theorem for finite subgraphs with a compactness (Kőnig'
s-infinity-lemma) argument over the countable product of color sets.
