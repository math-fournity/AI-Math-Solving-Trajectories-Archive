## Absolute Derivative of a Contravariant Vector

**Setup.** Let $v^i$ be a contravariant vector field defined along a curve $x^k
= x^k(t)$ in a manifold with affine connection $\Gamma^i_{jk}$. We seek a deriva
tive of $v^i$ along the curve that itself transforms as a contravariant vector.

**Why the ordinary derivative fails.** Under a coordinate change $x^i \to x'^i$,
 the vector transforms as
$$v'^i = \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^j}\,v^j.$$
Differentiating along the curve (using the chain rule on both factors):
$$\frac{dv'^i}{dt} = \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^j}\frac{dv^j}{dt} + \frac{\
partial^2 x'^i}{\partial x^j\,\partial x^k}\,v^j\,\frac{dx^k}{dt}.$$
The second (inhomogeneous) term shows that $\dfrac{dv^i}{dt}$ does **not** trans
form as a tensor. We must cancel it.

**The Christoffel transformation law.** The connection coefficients transform as
$$\Gamma'^{i}_{mn}\frac{\partial x'^m}{\partial x^j}\frac{\partial x'^n}{\partia
l x^k} = \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^l}\,\Gamma^{l}_{jk} - \frac{\partial^2
x'^i}{\partial x^j\,\partial x^k},$$
so that
$$\frac{\partial^2 x'^i}{\partial x^j\,\partial x^k} = \frac{\partial x'^i}{\par
tial x^l}\,\Gamma^{l}_{jk} - \Gamma'^{i}_{mn}\frac{\partial x'^m}{\partial x^j}\
frac{\partial x'^n}{\partial x^k}.$$

**Substitution.** Insert this into the inhomogeneous term of $\frac{dv'^i}{dt}$:
$$\frac{dv'^i}{dt} = \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^j}\frac{dv^j}{dt} + \frac{\
partial x'^i}{\partial x^l}\,\Gamma^{l}_{jk}\,v^j\,\frac{dx^k}{dt} - \Gamma'^{i}
_{mn}\,\underbrace{\frac{\partial x'^m}{\partial x^j}v^j}_{v'^m}\,\underbrace{\f
rac{\partial x'^n}{\partial x^k}\frac{dx^k}{dt}}_{dx'^n/dt}.$$

Hence
$$\frac{dv'^i}{dt} + \Gamma'^{i}_{mn}\,v'^m\,\frac{dx'^n}{dt} = \frac{\partial x
'^i}{\partial x^j}\frac{dv^j}{dt} + \frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^l}\,\Gamma^{
l}_{jk}\,v^j\,\frac{dx^k}{dt}.$$

Relabeling dummy indices ($l \leftrightarrow j$) in the second term on the right
:
$$\frac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^l}\,\Gamma^{l}_{jk}\,v^j\,\frac{dx^k}{dt} = \f
rac{\partial x'^i}{\partial x^j}\,\Gamma^{j}_{lk}\,v^l\,\frac{dx^k}{dt},$$
so that
$$\boxed{\;\frac{dv'^i}{dt} + \Gamma'^{i}_{mn}\,v'^m\,\frac{dx'^n}{dt} = \frac{\
partial x'^i}{\partial x^j}\left(\frac{dv^j}{dt} + \Gamma^{j}_{lk}\,v^l\,\frac{d
x^k}{dt}\right).\;}$$

**Conclusion.** The combination
$$\frac{dv^i}{dt} + \Gamma^{i}_{jk}\,v^j\,\frac{dx^k}{dt}$$
transforms homogeneously as a contravariant vector. It is therefore a proper ten
sorial derivative along the curve, and we **define** the **absolute (intrinsic)
derivative** of the contravariant vector $v^i$ along $x^k(t)$ to be

$$\boxed{\;\frac{Dv^i}{Dt} \;=\; \frac{dv^i}{dt} \;+\; \Gamma^{i}_{jk}\,v^{j}\,\
frac{dx^{k}}{dt}.\;}$$

**Interpretation.** The first term is the ordinary rate of change of the compone
nts of $v^i$ along the curve; the second term $\Gamma^{i}_{jk}v^j\,dx^k/dt$ is t
he connection correction accounting for the "turning of the basis" along the cur
ve. Geometrically, $\frac{Dv^i}{Dt}=0$ is the equation of **parallel transport**
 of $v^i$ along the curve, and in general $\frac{Dv^i}{Dt}$ measures the covaria
nt rate of change of $v^i$ along the curve — the projection of the field's chang
e onto the transported frame.
