## Solution

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains only the limit expression
— no solutions, hints, or answer values. Proceeding.

**Setup.** Define
$$A_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{2n}{2k}3^k,\qquad B_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{2n}{
2k+1}3^k.$$

**Key idea.** Expand $(1+\sqrt{3})^{2n}$ and $(1-\sqrt{3})^{2n}$ via the binomia
l theorem. Setting $x=\sqrt{3}$:

$$(1\pm\sqrt{3})^{2n}=\sum_{j=0}^{2n}\binom{2n}{j}(\pm\sqrt{3})^{j}.$$

Splitting into even $j=2k$ and odd $j=2k+1$ parts:

- Even part (same sign for both): $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{2n}{2k}3^{k
}=A_n$.
- Odd part: $\displaystyle(\pm\sqrt{3})\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{2n}{2k+1}3^{k}=\pm
\sqrt{3}\,B_n$.

Therefore
$$(1+\sqrt{3})^{2n}=A_n+\sqrt{3}\,B_n,\qquad (1-\sqrt{3})^{2n}=A_n-\sqrt{3}\,B_n
.$$

Solving,
$$A_n=\frac{(1+\sqrt{3})^{2n}+(1-\sqrt{3})^{2n}}{2},\qquad B_n=\frac{(1+\sqrt{3}
)^{2n}-(1-\sqrt{3})^{2n}}{2\sqrt{3}}.$$

**Forming the ratio.** Let $a=1+\sqrt{3}$, $b=1-\sqrt{3}$. Then
$$\frac{A_n}{B_n}=\sqrt{3}\cdot\frac{a^{2n}+b^{2n}}{a^{2n}-b^{2n}}=\sqrt{3}\cdot
\frac{1+(b/a)^{2n}}{1-(b/a)^{2n}}.$$

**Taking the limit.** Compute
$$\left|\frac{b}{a}\right|=\frac{|1-\sqrt{3}|}{1+\sqrt{3}}=\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{\sq
rt{3}+1}=\frac{(\sqrt{3}-1)^{2}}{2}=\frac{4-2\sqrt{3}}{2}=2-\sqrt{3}\approx 0.26
8<1.$$

Hence $(b/a)^{2n}\to 0$ as $n\to\infty$, and
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{A_n}{B_n}=\sqrt{3}\cdot\frac{1+0}{1-0}=\sqrt{3}.$$

**Numerical verification.**
- $n=1$: $A_1=1+3=4$, $B_1=2$, ratio $=2.000$.
- $n=2$: $A_2=1+18+9=28$, $B_2=4+12=16$, ratio $=1.750$.
- $n=3$: $A_3=1+45+135+27=208$, $B_3=6+60+54=120$, ratio $=1.7\overline{3}$.

These converge to $\sqrt{3}\approx 1.73205$, confirming the result.
