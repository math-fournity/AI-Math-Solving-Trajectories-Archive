## Proof

**Claim.** There exists a continuous surjection from $S^1$ onto $[0,1]$.

**Construction.** Identify $S^1$ with the unit circle in $\mathbb{R}^2$:
$$S^1 = \{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^2 : x^2+y^2=1\}.$$
Define $f:S^1\to\mathbb{R}$ by
$$f(x,y)=\frac{1+y}{2}.$$

**Continuity.** The map $f$ is the composition of the projection $\pi_2:S^1\to\m
athbb{R}$, $\pi_2(x,y)=y$ (the restriction of a continuous linear map to $S^1$,
hence continuous), with the affine map $t\mapsto\frac{1+t}{2}$ (continuous on $\
mathbb{R}$). Therefore $f$ is continuous.

**Image is contained in $[0,1]$.** For every $(x,y)\in S^1$ we have $y\in[-1,1]$
, so $\frac{1+y}{2}\in\left[0,1\right]$. Thus $f$ is a well-defined map $S^1\to[
0,1]$.

**Surjectivity.** Let $t\in[0,1]$ be arbitrary. Set $y_0=2t-1$, so $y_0\in[-1,1]
$. Choose
$$x_0=\sqrt{1-y_0^{2}}\ge 0.$$
Then $x_0^2+y_0^2=1-y_0^2+y_0^2=1$, so $(x_0,y_0)\in S^1$, and
$$f(x_0,y_0)=\frac{1+y_0}{2}=\frac{1+(2t-1)}{2}=t.$$
Since $t\in[0,1]$ was arbitrary, $f$ is surjective.

**Conclusion.** The map $f(x,y)=\frac{1+y}{2}$ is a continuous surjection from $
S^1$ onto $[0,1]$. Hence such a map exists. $\blacksquare$
