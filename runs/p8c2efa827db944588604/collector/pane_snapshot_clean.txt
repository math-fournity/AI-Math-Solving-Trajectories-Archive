## Proof

Let the two-digit number be $N = 10a + b$, where $a \in \{1,2,\dots,9\}$ is the
tens digit and $b \in \{0,1,\dots,9\}$ is the units digit. The number obtained b
y swapping the digits is $M = 10b + a$.

**Condition (1):** $|a - b| = 5$.

**Condition (2):** The swapped number differs from twice the original by $4$. Se
tting up the equation
$$10b + a = 2(10a + b) - 4,$$
(the swapped number is $4$ less than double the original — the only reading cons
istent with both conditions having a solution):

$$10b + a = 20a + 2b - 4$$
$$8b = 19a - 4$$
$$b = \frac{19a - 4}{8}.$$

For $b$ to be an integer, we need $19a - 4 \equiv 0 \pmod{8}$. Since $19 \equiv
3 \pmod{8}$:
$$3a - 4 \equiv 0 \pmod{8} \implies 3a \equiv 4 \pmod{8} \implies a \equiv 4 \cd
ot 3 \equiv 12 \equiv 4 \pmod{8}.$$

With $a \in \{1,\dots,9\}$, the only value satisfying $a \equiv 4 \pmod{8}$ is $
a = 4$ (the next, $a = 12$, is out of range).

Substituting $a = 4$:
$$b = \frac{19 \cdot 4 - 4}{8} = \frac{76 - 4}{8} = \frac{72}{8} = 9.$$

**Verification:**
- $N = 49$, so $a = 4$, $b = 9$, and $|a - b| = |4 - 9| = 5$. ✓ (Condition 1)
- Swapped number $M = 94$. Twice the original $= 2 \cdot 49 = 98$, and $98 - 94
= 4$. ✓ (Condition 2)

Since $a = 4$ is the unique digit in range satisfying the congruence, there is e
xactly one such number.

**All two-digit natural numbers satisfying the conditions:** $\boxed{49}$.
