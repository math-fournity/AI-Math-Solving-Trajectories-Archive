**Divergence Theorem.** Since $E$ is a bounded solid with outward-oriented bound
ary $\partial E$, and $\vec F$ is smooth everywhere,

$$\iint_{\partial E} \vec F \cdot d\vec S = \iiint_E \nabla \cdot \vec F \, dV.$
$

**Compute the divergence.** With $\vec F = (xy^2+\cos(yz))\hat i - (x^2+\sin(zx)
)\hat j + (z+\cos(xy))\hat k$:

$$\nabla\cdot\vec F = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(xy^2+\cos(yz)) + \frac{\partia
l}{\partial y}\!\bigl(-(x^2+\sin(zx))\bigr) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(z+\cos
(xy)).$$

- $\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(xy^2+\cos(yz)) = y^2 + 0 = y^2$ (since $yz$ is in
dependent of $x$),
- $\frac{\partial}{\partial y}(-(x^2+\sin(zx))) = 0$ (since $x^2$ and $\sin(zx)$
 are independent of $y$),
- $\frac{\partial}{\partial z}(z+\cos(xy)) = 1 + 0 = 1$ (since $xy$ is independe
nt of $z$).

Hence $\nabla\cdot\vec F = y^2 + 1$.

**Describe $E$ (Steinmetz solid).** $E$ is the intersection of the two cylinders
 $x^2+y^2\le 1$ and $y^2+z^2\le 1$. Fixing $y\in[-1,1]$, the constraints give
$$x\in\bigl[-\sqrt{1-y^2},\,\sqrt{1-y^2}\bigr],\qquad z\in\bigl[-\sqrt{1-y^2},\,
\sqrt{1-y^2}\bigr].$$
So the cross-section at height $y$ is a square of side $2\sqrt{1-y^2}$, with are
a $4(1-y^2)$.

**Integrate.**

$$\iiint_E (y^2+1)\,dV = \int_{-1}^{1} (y^2+1)\cdot 4(1-y^2)\,dy = 4\int_{-1}^{1
}(y^2+1)(1-y^2)\,dy.$$

Simplify the integrand:
$$(y^2+1)(1-y^2) = 1 - y^2 + y^2 - y^4 = 1 - y^4.$$

Therefore
$$\iiint_E (y^2+1)\,dV = 4\int_{-1}^{1}(1-y^4)\,dy = 4\left[y - \frac{y^5}{5}\ri
ght]_{-1}^{1} = 4\left[\left(1-\tfrac15\right)-\left(-1+\tfrac15\right)\right] =
 4\cdot\frac{8}{5} = \frac{32}{5}.$$

**Result.**

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \iint_{\partial E}\vec F\cdot d\vec S = \frac{32}{5}}$$
