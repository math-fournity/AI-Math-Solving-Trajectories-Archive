# IMO 2019 Problem 1 — Solution

**Problem.** Find all functions $f:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}$ such that for all $a
,b\in\mathbb{Z}$:
$$f(2a)+2f(b)=f(f(a+b)). \tag{P}$$

**Answer.** The solutions are $f(x)=0$ and $f(x)=2x+c$ for any constant $c\in\ma
thbb{Z}$.

---

## Verification

**$f\equiv 0$:** Both sides equal $0$. ✓

**$f(x)=2x+c$:**
$$f(2a)+2f(b)=(4a+c)+2(2b+c)=4(a+b)+3c,$$
$$f(f(a+b))=f(2(a+b)+c)=2(2(a+b)+c)+c=4(a+b)+3c. \;\checkmark$$

---

## Proof of uniqueness

Denote the assertion $(P)$ at $(a,b)$ by $P(a,b)$. The key observation is that t
he right-hand side $f(f(a+b))$ depends only on $s:=a+b$. Hence for fixed $s$, th
e quantity $f(2a)+2f(b)$ is constant as $a$ ranges over $\mathbb{Z}$ (with $b=s-
a$).

**Comparing $P(a,b)$ and $P(a+1,b-1)$** (both have $a+b=(a+1)+(b-1)=s$):
$$f(2a)+2f(b)=f(2a+2)+2f(b-1),$$
which rearranges to
$$f(2a+2)-f(2a)=2\bigl(f(b)-f(b-1)\bigr). \tag{1}$$

The left-hand side of (1) depends only on $a$; the right-hand side depends only
on $b$. Since they are equal for **all** $a,b\in\mathbb{Z}$, both sides must equ
al a single constant. Denote it $2k$ (it is even, being twice an integer). Then:

- $f(2a+2)-f(2a)=2k$ for all $a$, so $f(2a)=2ka+D$ where $D=f(0)$;
- $f(b)-f(b-1)=k$ for all $b\in\mathbb{Z}$.

The second relation telescopes: for every $n\in\mathbb{Z}$,
$$f(n)=kn+D, \qquad D:=f(0).$$
(This is consistent with the first relation: $f(2a)=2ka+D$.) Thus **every** solu
tion is linear: $f(x)=kx+D$.

**Substituting $f(x)=kx+D$ into $(P)$:**
$$\underbrace{(2ka+D)+2(kb+D)}_{2k(a+b)+3D}=\underbrace{k\bigl(k(a+b)+D\bigr)+D}
_{k^{2}(a+b)+kD+D}.$$

Equating coefficients of $(a+b)$ and the constant term:
$$2k=k^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad 3D=kD+D.$$

- $k(2-k)=0\Rightarrow k\in\{0,2\}$.
- $D(2-k)=0$.

**Case $k=0$:** $D\cdot 2=0\Rightarrow D=0$, giving $f\equiv 0$.

**Case $k=2$:** $D\cdot 0=0$, so $D$ is arbitrary, giving $f(x)=2x+c$ for any $c
\in\mathbb{Z}$.

Both families were verified above, so they constitute all solutions. $\blacksqua
re$
