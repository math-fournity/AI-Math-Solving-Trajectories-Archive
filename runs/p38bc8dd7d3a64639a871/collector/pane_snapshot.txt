### Equilibrium points

Setting $\dot{x}=0$ and $\dot{y}=0$:

- $\dot{x}=x(\mu-x)=0 \;\Rightarrow\; x=0$ or $x=\mu$.
- $\dot{y}=y(\mu-2x)=0 \;\Rightarrow\; y=0$ or $x=\mu/2$.

Combining both conditions:

| Condition | Equilibrium |
|---|---|
| $x=0,\;y=0$ | $(0,0)$ |
| $x=\mu,\;y=0$ | $(\mu,0)$ |
| $x=0$ and $x=\mu/2$ simultaneously | only possible when $\mu=0$, giving the wh
ole $y$-axis $x=0$ |

Hence for $\mu\neq 0$ there are **two** equilibria: $(0,0)$ and $(\mu,0)$.
At $\mu=0$ they coalesce into a **line of equilibria** along the $y$-axis ($x=0$
, all $y$).

---

### Linearization (Jacobian)

$$J(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}\mu-2x & 0\\ -2y & \mu-2x\end{pmatrix}$$

The eigenvalues are **repeated**: $\lambda_1=\lambda_2=\mu-2x$.

**At $(0,0)$:** $J=\mu I$, so $\lambda=\mu$ (double, star node).
- $\mu<0$: stable star node.
- $\mu>0$: unstable star node.

**At $(\mu,0)$:** $J=-\mu I$, so $\lambda=-\mu$ (double, star node).
- $\mu>0$: stable star node.
- $\mu<0$: unstable star node.

**Stability exchange at $\mu=0$:** the two equilibrium branches $x=0$ and $x=\mu
$ swap stability.

---

### Stability diagram in the $(\mu,x)$ plane ($y=0$)

The equilibrium curves in the $(\mu,x)$-plane are the two straight lines

$$x=0 \quad\text{and}\quad x=\mu.$$

- **Branch $x=0$** (the $\mu$-axis): stable for $\mu<0$, unstable for $\mu>0$.
- **Branch $x=\mu$** (line of slope $1$ through the origin): unstable for $\mu<0
$, stable for $\mu>0$.

The two branches cross at the origin $(\mu,x)=(0,0)$ and **exchange stability**
there. This is the hallmark of a **transcritical bifurcation**.

> **Bifurcation type at $\mu=0$: transcritical bifurcation.** No new equilibria
are created or destroyed; the two existing branches merely interchange their sta
bility as $\mu$ passes through $0$.

### Equations of the phase paths

$$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{y(\mu-2x)}{x(\mu-x)}$$

Separating variables:

$$\frac{dy}{y}=\frac{\mu-2x}{x(\mu-x)}\,dx.$$

Partial fractions:

$$\frac{\mu-2x}{x(\mu-x)}=\frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{\mu-x}.$$

Integrating:

$$\ln|y|=\ln|x|+\ln|\mu-x|+C=\ln|x(\mu-x)|+C,$$

so

$$\boxed{\,y = K\,x(\mu-x)\,},\qquad K=\text{const.}$$

Each phase path is a **parabola** (for $\mu\neq 0$) with vertex at $x=\mu/2$, pa
ssing through both equilibria $(0,0)$ and $(\mu,0)$ on the $x$-axis. The $x$-axi
s itself ($y=0$, i.e. $K=0$) and the line $x=0$ (formally $K\to\infty$) are inva
riant sets.

---

### Phase diagrams

**Direction of flow.** On any path, $\dot{x}=x(\mu-x)$ determines the horizontal
 motion. The sign of $\dot{x}$ is independent of $y$, so the flow direction alon
g each parabola is set purely by the $x$-interval:

- Between the two equilibria ($0$ and $\mu$): $\dot{x}>0$ (flow toward $x=\mu$).
- Outside that interval: $\dot{x}<0$ (flow away, toward $-\infty$ or $+\infty$).

This is consistent with the stability found above: the stable equilibrium attrac
ts from the interval between the equilibria; the unstable one repels.

---

**Case $\mu=-1$.** Equilibria: $(0,0)$ **stable**, $(-1,0)$ **unstable**.

Phase paths: $y=K\,x(-1-x)=-K\,x(1+x)$, parabolas with vertex at $x=-\tfrac12$,
opening downward for $K>0$ and upward for $K<0$, all threading through $(0,0)$ a
nd $(-1,0)$.

- For $-1<x<0$: $\dot{x}=x(-1-x)>0$, flow moves right toward $(0,0)$.
- For $x>0$: $\dot{x}<0$, flow moves left toward $(0,0)$.
- For $x<-1$: $\dot{x}<0$, flow moves left, away from $(-1,0)$, to $-\infty$.

So $(0,0)$ is an attractor (stable star node) and $(-1,0)$ a repeller (unstable
star node). All paths in the strip $-1<x<0$ and those with $x>0$ converge to $(0
,0)$; paths with $x<-1$ escape to $-\infty$.

---

**Case $\mu=0$.** System: $\dot{x}=-x^2,\;\dot{y}=-2xy$. The **entire $y$-axis i
s a line of equilibria**.

Phase paths: $y=K\,x(0-x)=-Kx^2$, i.e. $y=-Kx^2$, parabolas with vertex at the o
rigin, opening downward for $K>0$ and upward for $K<0$.

Flow: $\dot{x}=-x^2<0$ for all $x\neq 0$.
- $x>0$: $x$ decreases monotonically toward $0$ (solution $x(t)=x_0/(1+x_0 t)\to
 0^+$); paths approach the $y$-axis asymptotically.
- $x<0$: $x$ decreases to $-\infty$ in finite time ($x(t)=x_0/(1+x_0 t)$ blows u
p at $t=-1/x_0>0$); paths escape to $x\to-\infty$.

So every point with $x>0$ is attracted to the line of equilibria $x=0$; every po
int with $x<0$ is repelled to $-\infty$. The $y$-axis is semi-stable: attracting
 from the right, repelling from the left. This is the bifurcation configuration
in which the two equilibria have merged into a non-isolated set.

---

**Case $\mu=1$.** Equilibria: $(0,0)$ **unstable**, $(1,0)$ **stable**.

Phase paths: $y=K\,x(1-x)$, parabolas with vertex at $x=\tfrac12$, opening downw
ard for $K>0$ and upward for $K<0$, threading through $(0,0)$ and $(1,0)$.

- For $0<x<1$: $\dot{x}=x(1-x)>0$, flow moves right toward $(1,0)$.
- For $x>1$: $\dot{x}<0$, flow moves left toward $(1,0)$.
- For $x<0$: $\dot{x}<0$, flow moves left, away from $(0,0)$, to $-\infty$.

So $(1,0)$ is the attractor (stable star node) and $(0,0)$ a repeller (unstable
star node). All paths with $x>0$ converge to $(1,0)$; paths with $x<0$ escape to
 $-\infty$.

### Summary

| $\mu$ | $(0,0)$ | $(\mu,0)$ | Phase-path family |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\mu<0$ | stable | unstable | $y=Kx(\mu-x)$, parabolas through both equilibria
 |
| $\mu=0$ | line of equilibria $x=0$ (semi-stable: attracting from $x>0$, repell
ing from $x<0$) | — | $y=-Kx^2$ |
| $\mu>0$ | unstable | stable | $y=Kx(\mu-x)$, parabolas through both equilibria
 |

The two equilibrium branches $x=0$ and $x=\mu$ in the $(\mu,x)$-plane cross at t
he origin and **exchange stability** there: a **transcritical bifurcation** at $
\mu=0$.
