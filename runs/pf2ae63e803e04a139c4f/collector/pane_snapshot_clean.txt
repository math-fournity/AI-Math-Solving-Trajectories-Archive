## Solution

**The growth order of $f$ is $\rho = \dfrac{1}{a}$.**

### Proof

The function $f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{z^{2n}}{((2n)!)^a}$ is entire for e
very $a > 0$, since the coefficients decay super-exponentially (ratio test: cons
ecutive non-zero terms satisfy $\left|\frac{c_{2n+2}}{c_{2n}}\right| = \frac{1}{
((2n+2)!)^a / ((2n)!)^a} = \frac{1}{((2n+1)(2n+2))^a} \to 0$).

**Standard formula.** For an entire function $f(z) = \sum_{m=0}^\infty c_m z^m$,
 the order of growth is given by ([Hadamard's formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/
wiki/Entire_function#Order)):

$$\rho = \limsup_{\substack{m \to \infty \\ c_m \neq 0}} \frac{m \log m}{\log(1/
|c_m|)}.$$

**Identify the coefficients.** Writing $f(z) = \sum_{m=0}^\infty c_m z^m$:

$$c_m = \begin{cases} \dfrac{1}{(m!)^a}, & m \text{ even},\\[6pt] 0, & m \text{
odd}. \end{cases}$$

The non-zero coefficients occur at $m = 2n$. Since the odd coefficients vanish,
the limsup is determined entirely by the even subsequence $m = 2n \to \infty$.

**Compute the limit along even $m$.** For even $m$:

$$\log\!\left(\frac{1}{|c_m|}\right) = a\,\log(m!).$$

By **Stirling's approximation**, $\log(m!) = m\log m - m + O(\log m)$ as $m \to
\infty$. Substituting:

$$\frac{m \log m}{\log(1/|c_m|)} = \frac{m \log m}{a\,(m \log m - m + O(\log m))
} = \frac{m\log m}{a\, m\log m\left(1 - \dfrac{1}{\log m} + O\!\left(\dfrac{1}{(
\log m)^2}\right)\right)}.$$

As $m \to \infty$, the factor $\frac{1}{\log m} \to 0$, so:

$$\frac{m \log m}{\log(1/|c_m|)} \;\longrightarrow\; \frac{1}{a}.$$

Since this limit exists (and equals $1/a$) along the even subsequence, and the o
dd coefficients contribute nothing to the limsup, we conclude:

$$\rho = \limsup_{\substack{m\to\infty\\c_m\neq 0}} \frac{m\log m}{\log(1/|c_m|)
} = \frac{1}{a}.$$

**Sanity check.** For $a = 1$: $f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{z^{2n}}{(2n)!} =
\cosh z$, which is a function of order $1$. Indeed $\frac{1}{a} = 1$. ✓

For $a = 2$: the non-zero coefficients satisfy $\log(1/|c_{2n}|) = 2\log((2n)!)
\sim 4n\log(2n)$, while $2n\log(2n) / (4n\log(2n)) \to 1/2 = 1/a$. ✓

### Conclusion

The growth order of $f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{z^{2n}}{((2n)!)^a}$ is

$$\boxed{\rho = \frac{1}{a}}.$$
