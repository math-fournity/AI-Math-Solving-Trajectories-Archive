## Solution

**Claim.** Two endomorphisms $S, T \in \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(V)$ satis
fy $S \sim T$ (i.e. $S = BTA$ for invertible $A, B$) if and only if $\operatorna
me{rank}(S) = \operatorname{rank}(T)$. Consequently the number of equivalence cl
asses is the number of possible ranks, namely $\boxed{n+1}$.

---

### Step 1 — Rank is an invariant of the equivalence relation

Suppose $S = BTA$ with $A, B : V \to V$ invertible. Then
$$\operatorname{im}(S) = \operatorname{im}(BTA) = B\bigl(\operatorname{im}(TA)\b
igr) = B\bigl(\operatorname{im}(T)\bigr),$$
where the last equality uses that $A$ is surjective, so $\operatorname{im}(TA) =
 \operatorname{im}(T)$. Since $B$ is an isomorphism, $\dim B(\operatorname{im}(T
)) = \dim \operatorname{im}(T)$. Hence
$$\operatorname{rank}(S) = \dim\operatorname{im}(S) = \dim\operatorname{im}(T) =
 \operatorname{rank}(T).$$
So equivalent maps have equal rank.

### Step 2 — Same rank implies equivalent

Fix a basis of $V$ and identify endomorphisms with $n \times n$ real matrices. W
e show every matrix $M$ of rank $r$ is equivalent (in the sense $M = BJA$ with $
A, B$ invertible) to the canonical block matrix
$$J_r = \begin{pmatrix} I_r & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Let $M : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$ have rank $r$, so $\dim\operatorname{im}
(M) = r$ and $\dim\ker(M) = n - r$.

* Choose a basis $f_1, \dots, f_r, f_{r+1}, \dots, f_n$ of $\mathbb{R}^n$ with $
f_1, \dots, f_r$ a basis of $\operatorname{im}(M)$.
* For each $i = 1, \dots, r$, pick $g_i$ with $Mg_i = f_i$. The vectors $g_1, \d
ots, g_r$ are linearly independent: if $\sum_{i=1}^r c_i g_i \in \ker M$, then $
0 = M(\sum c_i g_i) = \sum c_i f_i$, forcing all $c_i = 0$.
* Choose $g_{r+1}, \dots, g_n$ to be a basis of $\ker M$. Then $g_1, \dots, g_n$
 is a basis of $\mathbb{R}^n$ (independent by construction: the first $r$ are in
dependent and lie outside $\ker M$ modulo it, the last $n-r$ span $\ker M$).

Let $Q$ be the matrix whose columns are $g_1, \dots, g_n$ and $P$ the matrix who
se columns are $f_1, \dots, f_n$. Both are invertible. Now
$$Mg_i = \begin{cases} f_i & i \le r, \\ 0 & i > r, \end{cases}$$
so the $i$-th column of $P^{-1}MQ$ is $P^{-1}Mg_i$, which equals $e_i$ for $i \l
e r$ and $0$ for $i > r$. That is,
$$P^{-1} M Q = J_r, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad M = P\, J_r\, Q^{-1}.$$
Setting $B = P$ and $A = Q^{-1}$ (both invertible) gives $M = B J_r A$.

### Step 3 — Counting the classes

If $\operatorname{rank}(S) = \operatorname{rank}(T) = r$, then by Step 2 both $S
$ and $T$ are equivalent to $J_r$, hence (by symmetry and transitivity) $S \sim
T$. Combined with Step 1, the equivalence classes are in bijection with the set
of possible ranks.

The rank of an endomorphism of an $n$-dimensional space ranges over $\{0, 1, 2,
\dots, n\}$, and every value is realized (e.g. by $J_r$). Therefore the number o
f equivalence classes is

$$\boxed{n+1}.$$
