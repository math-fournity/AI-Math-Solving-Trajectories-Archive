## Proof

We compute
$$L = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\ln(2^{p_{n+1}} - 1)}{\ln(2^{p_n} - 1)},$$
where $p_n$ denotes the $n$-th prime.

**Step 1: Asymptotic expansion of $\ln(2^p - 1)$.**

For any positive real $p$,
$$2^p - 1 = 2^p\!\left(1 - 2^{-p}\right),$$
so
$$\ln(2^p - 1) = p\ln 2 + \ln\!\left(1 - 2^{-p}\right).$$

Using $\ln(1-x) = -x + O(x^2)$ as $x \to 0$, and noting $2^{-p} \to 0$:
$$\ln(2^p - 1) = p\ln 2 - 2^{-p} + O\!\left(4^{-p}\right).$$

**Step 2: Ratio of consecutive primes.**

By the Prime Number Theorem, $p_n \sim n\ln n$. Hence
$$\frac{p_{n+1}}{p_n} \sim \frac{(n+1)\ln(n+1)}{n\ln n} = \frac{n+1}{n}\cdot\fra
c{\ln(n+1)}{\ln n} \longrightarrow 1\cdot 1 = 1.$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the limit.**

Applying Step 1 to both numerator and denominator:
$$L = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{p_{n+1}\ln 2 - 2^{-p_{n+1}} + O(4^{-p_{n+1}})}{p_n
\ln 2 - 2^{-p_n} + O(4^{-p_n})}.$$

Factor out $p_{n+1}\ln 2$ and $p_n\ln 2$ respectively:
$$L = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{p_{n+1}}{p_n}\cdot\frac{1 - \dfrac{2^{-p_{n+1}}}{p
_{n+1}\ln 2} + O\!\left(\dfrac{4^{-p_{n+1}}}{p_{n+1}\ln 2}\right)}{1 - \dfrac{2^
{-p_n}}{p_n\ln 2} + O\!\left(\dfrac{4^{-p_n}}{p_n\ln 2}\right)}.$$

Since $p_n \to \infty$, the terms $\dfrac{2^{-p_n}}{p_n\ln 2}$ and $\dfrac{4^{-p
_n}}{p_n\ln 2}$ tend to $0$ (exponential decay dominates polynomial growth). The
 same holds for $p_{n+1}$. Therefore both the numerator and the denominator insi
de the fraction tend to $1$, giving the second factor the limit $1$.

By Step 2, the first factor $\dfrac{p_{n+1}}{p_n} \to 1$.

Combining:
$$L = 1 \cdot 1 = 1.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\ln(2^{p_{n+1}} - 1)}{\ln(2^{p_n} - 1)} = 1}.
$$
