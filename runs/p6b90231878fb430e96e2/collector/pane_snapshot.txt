## Proof

We count the number of complementary three-card sets in the 27-card deck.

**Key observation.** Take any two distinct cards. For each attribute (shape, col
or, shade), the third card that completes a complementary set is *uniquely deter
mined*:

- If the two cards agree on that attribute, the third card must have the **same*
* value (to satisfy "all three the same").
- If the two cards differ on that attribute, the third card must have the **rema
ining** value (to satisfy "all three different").

Since each attribute has exactly 3 values, in both cases the required value is w
ell-defined and is one of the three valid values. Therefore the third card is un
iquely determined and belongs to the deck.

**The third card is distinct from both chosen cards.** The two chosen cards diff
er in at least one attribute (they are distinct cards). In that attribute, the t
hird card takes the remaining value, which differs from both chosen values. Henc
e the third card is different from both.

**Counting.** Every unordered pair of distinct cards determines exactly one comp
lementary triple. The number of pairs is

$$\binom{27}{2} = \frac{27 \cdot 26}{2} = 351.$$

Each complementary triple contains $\binom{3}{2} = 3$ pairs, so each triple is c
ounted exactly 3 times in the 351 pairs. Therefore the number of complementary t
riples is

$$\frac{351}{3} = 117.$$
