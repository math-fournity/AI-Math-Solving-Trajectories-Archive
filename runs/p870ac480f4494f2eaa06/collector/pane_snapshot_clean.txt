## Proof

**Definition.** For a positive integer $n$, let $A_n = \{x \in \mathbb{Z}^+ : 1
< x < n,\ \gcd(x,n)=1\}$. We call $n$ *Stable* if every element of $A_n$ is prim
e.

**Key Lemma.** Let $q$ be the smallest prime not dividing $n$. Then $n$ is Stabl
e if and only if $q^2 \ge n$.

*Proof of Lemma.* Every composite number $c$ with $\gcd(c,n)=1$ has all its prim
e factors not dividing $n$, hence each prime factor is $\ge q$. The smallest suc
h composite is therefore $q^2$ (using $q$ twice), and every other composite copr
ime to $n$ is $\ge q^2$. Since $q \nmid n$, we have $\gcd(q^2, n)=1$, so $q^2$ i
s indeed coprime to $n$.

- If $q^2 < n$: then $q^2 \in A_n$ (since $1 < q^2 < n$ and $\gcd(q^2,n)=1$) and
 $q^2$ is composite, so $n$ is **not** Stable.
- If $q^2 \ge n$: then every composite coprime to $n$ is $\ge q^2 \ge n$, so no
composite lies in $A_n$. Hence every element of $A_n$ is prime, and $n$ is Stabl
e. $\square$

**Enumeration.** Let $p_1=2, p_2=3, p_3=5, \ldots$ be the primes. Suppose $n$ is
 divisible by $p_1, p_2, \ldots, p_k$ but not by $p_{k+1}$, so $q = p_{k+1}$. Th
en $n$ must be a multiple of $P_k = p_1 p_2 \cdots p_k$, not divisible by $p_{k+
1}$, and satisfy $n \le p_{k+1}^2$.

- **$k=0$** ($2 \nmid n$, $q=2$): $n \le 4$, $n$ odd $\Rightarrow n \in \{1, 3\}
$.
- **$k=1$** ($2 \mid n,\ 3 \nmid n$, $q=3$): $n \le 9$, $n$ even, $3 \nmid n$ $\
Rightarrow n \in \{2, 4, 8\}$.
- **$k=2$** ($6 \mid n,\ 5 \nmid n$, $q=5$): $n \le 25$, $6 \mid n$, $5 \nmid n$
 $\Rightarrow n \in \{6, 12, 18, 24\}$.
- **$k=3$** ($30 \mid n,\ 7 \nmid n$, $q=7$): $n \le 49$, $30 \mid n$, $7 \nmid
n$ $\Rightarrow n \in \{30\}$ (since $60 > 49$).
- **$k=4$** ($210 \mid n,\ 11 \nmid n$, $q=11$): $n \le 121$, but $P_4 = 210 > 1
21$ — no solutions.
- **$k \ge 4$**: $P_k \ge 210 > p_{k+1}^2$ is violated for all larger $k$ (the p
rimorial grows faster), so no further solutions.

**Verification** of each candidate:

| $n$ | $A_n$ | All prime? |
|-----|-------|-----------|
| 1 | $\emptyset$ | ✓ (vacuous) |
| 2 | $\emptyset$ | ✓ (vacuous) |

| 4 | $\{3\}$ | ✓ |

| 18 | $\{5,7,11,13,17\}$ | ✓ |
| 24 | $\{5,7,11,13,17,19,23\}$ | ✓ |
| 30 | $\{7,11,13,17,19,23,29\}$ | ✓ |

**Sum:**
$$1+2+3+4+6+8+12+18+24+30 = 108.$$

The sum of all positive integers $n$ that yield a Stable Network is $\boxed{108}
$.
