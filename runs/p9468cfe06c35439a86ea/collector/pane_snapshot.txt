Proof

Claim. $\operatorname{Pic}(Z)=\operatorname{Pic}(G/P)\cong\mathbb{Z}$.

We work over an algebraically closed field $k$ of characteristic $0$. The
argument has three steps.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 1. $\operatorname{Pic}(G/P)\cong X(P)$, the character group of $P$.

Since $\operatorname{char}k=0$, the connected semisimple group $G$ is linearly
reductive. A standard consequence (Mumford, GIT) is that for a linearly
reductive connected group acting on a variety, every line bundle admits a $G$-
linearization, and the set of linearizations of a fixed line bundle is a torsor
under $X(G)=\operatorname{Hom}(G,\mathbb{G}_m)$. The restriction map

$$\operatorname{Pic}^{G}(G/P)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(G/P)$$

is therefore surjective, with kernel $X(G)$.

Now $G$ is semisimple, hence $G=[G,G]$; every character kills the derived
subgroup, so $X(G)=0$. Thus

$$\operatorname{Pic}(G/P)\cong \operatorname{Pic}^{G}(G/P).$$

A $G$-linearized line bundle $\mathcal{L}$ on $G/P$ is determined by its fiber
at the base point $x_{0}=eP$, which is a one-dimensional representation of the
stabilizer $\operatorname{Stab}{G}(x{0})=P$. One-dimensional representations of
$P$ are exactly its characters. Conversely, every character $\chi:P\to\mathbb{G}
_m$ yields the associated line bundle

$$\mathcal{L}{\chi}=G\times^{P}\mathbb{A}^{1}{\chi},$$

which is $G$-linearized. These two constructions are mutually inverse, giving

$$\operatorname{Pic}^{G}(G/P)\cong X(P).$$

Combining,

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Pic}(G/P)\cong X(P).}$$

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 2. $X(P)\cong X(L)\cong X(Z(L)^{\circ})$, where $L$ is a Levi factor of
$P$.

Let $R_{u}(P)$ be the unipotent radical of $P$ and $L=P/R_{u}(P)$ a Levi
quotient (reductive). Any character $\chi:P\to\mathbb{G}m$ is trivial on $R{u}
(P)$, since a unipotent group admits no nontrivial characters. Hence restriction
gives

$$X(P)\cong X(L).$$

Write $L=Z(L)^{\circ}\cdot [L,L]$ with $[L,L]$ semisimple (hence $X([L,L])=0$,
as $[L,L]$ equals its own derived group). Characters vanish on $[L,L]$, so

$$X(L)\cong X(Z(L)^{\circ}).$$

Thus $\operatorname{Pic}(G/P)\cong X(Z(L)^{\circ})$.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 3. For a maximal parabolic, $Z(L)^{\circ}\cong\mathbb{G}_m$, so
$X(Z(L)^{\circ})\cong\mathbb{Z}$.

Fix a maximal torus $T\subset G$ and a Borel $B\supset T$; let $S$ be the set of
simple roots. A standard parabolic containing $B$ is determined by a subset
$I\subseteq S$:

$$P=P_{I},\qquad L\text{ has simple roots }I,\qquad [L,L]\text{ has root system
with simple roots }I.$$

The rank of the connected center of $L$ is

$$\operatorname{rk}Z(L)^{\circ}=\operatorname{rk}G-\operatorname{rk}[L,L]=|S|-|
I|=|S\setminus I|.$$

A maximal parabolic corresponds to $I=S\setminus{\alpha}$ for a single simple
root $\alpha$, i.e. $|S\setminus I|=1$. Therefore

$$Z(L)^{\circ}\cong\mathbb{G}_m,\qquad X(Z(L)^{\circ})=X(\mathbb{G}
_m)\cong\mathbb{Z}.$$

$$\operatorname{Pic}(Z)=\operatorname{Pic}(G/P)\cong X(P)\cong
