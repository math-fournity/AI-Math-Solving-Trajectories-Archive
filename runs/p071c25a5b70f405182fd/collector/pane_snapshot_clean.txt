## Proof

We claim that $f(r) = e^{-r}$ is an explicit example satisfying all three condit
ions.

**Smoothness.** The function $f(r) = e^{-r}$ is the restriction to $[0,\infty)$
of an entire function, hence $f \in C^\infty([0,\infty))$.

**Verification of the three $L^2$ conditions.** We use the standard identity
$$\int_0^\infty r^n\, e^{-ar}\, dr = \frac{n!}{a^{n+1}}, \qquad a > 0,\ n \in \m
athbb{N}.$$

Since $f(r) = e^{-r}$ and $f'(r) = -e^{-r}$, we have $|f(r)| = |f'(r)| = e^{-r}$
.

**Condition 1: $f(r)\cdot r \in L^2([0,\infty))$.**
$$\int_0^\infty \bigl(f(r)\, r\bigr)^2\, dr = \int_0^\infty r^2\, e^{-2r}\, dr =
 \frac{2!}{2^3} = \frac{2}{8} = \frac{1}{4} < \infty. \checkmark$$

**Condition 2: $f'(r)\cdot r \in L^2([0,\infty))$.**
$$\int_0^\infty \bigl(f'(r)\, r\bigr)^2\, dr = \int_0^\infty r^2\, e^{-2r}\, dr
= \frac{1}{4} < \infty. \checkmark$$

**Condition 3: $f'(r)\cdot r^2 \in L^2([0,\infty))$.**
$$\int_0^\infty \bigl(f'(r)\, r^2\bigr)^2\, dr = \int_0^\infty r^4\, e^{-2r}\, d
r = \frac{4!}{2^5} = \frac{24}{32} = \frac{3}{4} < \infty. \checkmark$$

All three integrals are finite, so $f(r)\cdot r,\ f'(r)\cdot r,\ f'(r)\cdot r^2
\in L^2([0,\infty))$.

Therefore $f(r) = e^{-r}$ is an explicit smooth function on $[0,\infty)$ satisfy
ing all the required conditions. $\blacksquare$
