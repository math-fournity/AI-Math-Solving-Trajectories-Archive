The statement is **true** (for sufficiently regular, decaying $u$, e.g. $u\in\ma
thcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^N)$). Below is the proof.

## Proof

Recall the integral (principal-value) definition of the fractional Laplacian for
 $s\in(0,1)$:

$$
(-\Delta)^s u(x)=C_{N,s}\,\text{P.V.}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^N}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|
^{N+2s}}\,dy,
$$

where $C_{N,s}>0$ is the standard normalization constant. We assume $u$ is, say,
 a Schwartz function, so that all integrals below are absolutely convergent (aft
er the principal-value cancellation) and Fubini-type exchanges are justified.

**Goal.** Show
$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}^N}(-\Delta)^s u(x)\,dx=0.
$$

**Step 1. Integrate and exchange order.** Using the definition,

$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}^N}(-\Delta)^s u(x)\,dx
= C_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^N}\left(\text{P.V.}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^N}\frac{u(x)-u(y
)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\right)dx.
$$

For $u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^N)$ the singularity at $x=y$ is cancelled by the
 numerator $u(x)-u(y)=O(|x-y|)$, and the tails are controlled by the rapid decay
 of $u$. Hence the double integral is absolutely convergent and we may drop the
principal value and apply Fubini:

$$
= C_{N,s}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^N\times\mathbb{R}^N}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,d
y\,dx.
$$

**Step 2. Symmetry argument.** The kernel $K(x,y)=|x-y|^{-N-2s}$ is symmetric: $
K(x,y)=K(y,x)$. Perform the change of variables $(x,y)\mapsto(y,x)$ (which has J
acobian $1$ and preserves $\mathbb{R}^N\times\mathbb{R}^N$):

$$
I:=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^N\times\mathbb{R}^N}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\,dx
= \iint_{\mathbb{R}^N\times\mathbb{R}^N}\frac{u(y)-u(x)}{|y-x|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy =
 -I.
$$

Thus $I=-I$, so $2I=0$, hence $I=0$.

**Step 3. Conclusion.** Therefore

$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}^N}(-\Delta)^s u(x)\,dx = C_{N,s}\cdot I = C_{N,s}\cdot 0 = 0.
$$

**Fourier-transform verification (alternative).** For $u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R
}^N)$, the fractional Laplacian acts as multiplication by $|\xi|^{2s}$ in Fourie
r space: $\widehat{(-\Delta)^s u}(\xi)=|\xi|^{2s}\widehat{u}(\xi)$. Hence

$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}^N}(-\Delta)^s u(x)\,dx = \widehat{(-\Delta)^s u}(0)=|0|^{2s}\,\
widehat{u}(0)=0\cdot\widehat{u}(0)=0,
$$

since $s>0$ and $\widehat{u}(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^N}u(x)\,dx<\infty$ for Schwartz
 $u$. This confirms the result independently.

**Remark on hypotheses.** The conclusion holds for any $u$ in the natural domain
 of $(-\Delta)^s$ for which the integral $\int_{\mathbb{R}^N}(-\Delta)^s u\,dx$
is well defined (e.g. $u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^N)$, or more generally $u\in H
^s(\mathbb{R}^N)$ with sufficient decay so that the integral converges). The ess
ential ingredient is the symmetry of the kernel, which forces the total integral
 to vanish.
