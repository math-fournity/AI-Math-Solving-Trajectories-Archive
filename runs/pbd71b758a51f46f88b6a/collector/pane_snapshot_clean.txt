## Proof

Let $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ be i.i.d. uniform on $[0,1]$ and set $S_n = x_1 + \cd
ots + x_n$. The integral in question is
$$I_n = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{n}{S_n}\right] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{1}{S_n/
n}\right].$$

By the Strong Law of Large Numbers, $\frac{S_n}{n} \to \frac{1}{2}$ almost surel
y, hence $\frac{n}{S_n} \to 2$ a.s. The challenge is justifying passage of the l
imit through the expectation, since $\frac{n}{S_n}$ is unbounded (it blows up wh
en $S_n$ is near $0$).

---

**Step 1: The integral is finite for $n \geq 2$.**

The density of $S_n$ (Irwin–Hall) satisfies, for $s \in [0,1]$,
$$f_{S_n}(s) = \frac{s^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}.$$
Thus
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{n}{S_n}\,;\, S_n \le 1\right] = \int_0^1 \frac{n}{s}\c
dot\frac{s^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\,ds = \frac{n}{(n-1)!}\cdot\frac{1}{n-1} < \infty \qua
d (n\ge 2),$$
and on $\{S_n > 1\}$ we have $n/S_n \le n$, so the full expectation is finite.

---

**Step 2: Splitting the expectation.**

Fix $\varepsilon \in (0,\tfrac12)$ and write
$$I_n = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{n}{S_n}\,;\, S_n > \varepsilon n\rig
ht]}_{A_n} + \underbrace{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{n}{S_n}\,;\, S_n \le \varepsilo
n n\right]}_{B_n}.$$

**The main term $A_n$.** On $\{S_n > \varepsilon n\}$ we have $\frac{n}{S_n} \le
 \frac{1}{\varepsilon}$, a fixed constant. Moreover $\frac{n}{S_n}\,\mathbf{1}_{
\{S_n > \varepsilon n\}} \to 2\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{1/2 > \varepsilon\}} = 2$ a.s.
By the Bounded Convergence Theorem,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} A_n = 2.$$

**The tail term $B_n$.** Split further: $B_n = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{n}{S_n};\
, S_n \le 1\right] + \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{n}{S_n};\, 1 < S_n \le \varepsilon
n\right]$.

- From Step 1, $\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{n}{S_n};\, S_n \le 1\right] = \dfrac{n}{
(n-1)(n-1)!} \to 0$ super-exponentially.

- For the second piece, since $S_n > 1$ gives $n/S_n \le n$,
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{n}{S_n};\, 1 < S_n \le \varepsilon n\right] \le n\,\ma
thbb{P}(S_n \le \varepsilon n).$$
By Hoeffding's inequality (each $x_i \in [0,1]$, mean $1/2$), for $\varepsilon <
 1/2$,
$$\mathbb{P}(S_n \le \varepsilon n) \le \exp\!\bigl(-n\,D(\varepsilon \,\|\, \tf
rac12)\bigr),$$
where $D(\varepsilon\|\tfrac12) > 0$ is the KL divergence. Hence
$$n\,\mathbb{P}(S_n \le \varepsilon n) \le n\,e^{-n D(\varepsilon\|1/2)} \xright
arrow{n\to\infty} 0.$$

Therefore $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty} B_n = 0$.

---

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

Combining both parts,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} I_n = \lim_{n\to\infty} A_n + \lim_{n\to\infty} B_n = 2 + 0
= 2.$$

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1\!\cdots\!\int_0^1 \frac{n}{x_1+\cdots+x_n}\,d
x_1\cdots dx_n = 2}$$
