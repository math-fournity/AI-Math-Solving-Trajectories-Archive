## Proof

**Interpretation.** Since $c_n(Y) \in CH^n(Y)$ and $c_n(X) \in CH^n(X)$ live in
different groups, the equation $c_n(Y) = d\cdot c_n(X)$ is understood as the pus
hforward relation $\pi_* c_n(Y) = d\cdot c_n(X)$ in $CH^n(X)$, which upon taking
 degrees yields $\int_Y c_n(Y) = d\int_X c_n(X)$ (Euler-characteristic multiplic
ativity).

### Step 1: $\pi$ is finite étale

The morphism $\pi$ is finite, dominant, and $\dim Y = \dim X = n$. Both $Y$ and
$X$ are nonsingular, hence regular and Cohen–Macaulay. By the **miracle flatness
 theorem** (a theorem of commutative algebra, valid in any characteristic): a lo
cal homomorphism $A \to B$ with $A$ regular, $B$ Cohen–Macaulay, and $\dim B = \
dim A + \dim B/\mathfrak{m}_A B$ is flat. Here $\dim B = n$, $\dim A = n$, and $
\dim B/\mathfrak{m}_A B = 0$ (finiteness), so the condition is satisfied. Thus $
\pi$ is **flat**.

Since $\pi$ is flat (Step 1) and unramified (hypothesis), and flat + unramified
= **étale**, $\pi$ is a finite étale cover of degree $d$.

### Step 2: $T_Y \cong \pi^* T_X$

Since $\pi$ is étale, $\Omega_{Y/X} = 0$. The standard exact sequence of differe
ntials
$$\pi^*\Omega_X \longrightarrow \Omega_Y \longrightarrow \Omega_{Y/X} \longright
arrow 0$$
gives a surjection $\pi^*\Omega_X \twoheadrightarrow \Omega_Y$. Both sheaves are
 locally free of rank $n$ (as $X$, $Y$ are nonsingular of dimension $n$), and a
surjective endomorphism of locally free sheaves of the same rank is an isomorphi
sm. Hence $\pi^*\Omega_X \cong \Omega_Y$. Dualizing:
$$T_Y \cong \pi^* T_X.$$

### Step 3: $c_n(Y) = \pi^* c_n(X)$

By functoriality of Chern classes under pullback:
$$c_n(Y) = c_n(T_Y) = c_n(\pi^* T_X) = \pi^* c_n(T_X) = \pi^* c_n(X).$$

### Step 4: Projection formula gives $\pi_* c_n(Y) = d \cdot c_n(X)$

The **projection formula** in intersection theory (Fulton, *Intersection Theory*
, Prop. 2.5(c)) states that for a proper morphism $\pi: Y \to X$, $\alpha \in CH
^*(X)$, $\beta \in CH_*(Y)$:
$$\pi_*(\pi^*\alpha \cdot \beta) = \alpha \cdot \pi_*\beta.$$

Set $\alpha = c_n(X)$ and $\beta = [Y]$:
$$\pi_*(\pi^* c_n(X) \cdot [Y]) = c_n(X) \cdot \pi_*[Y].$$

The left side is $\pi_*(c_n(Y))$ by Step 3. The right side is $c_n(X) \cdot d[X]
 = d \cdot c_n(X)$, since $\pi_*[Y] = d\cdot [X]$ (the pushforward of the fundam
ental class under a degree-$d$ finite morphism). Therefore:
$$\boxed{\pi_* c_n(Y) = d \cdot c_n(X) \quad \text{in } CH^n(X).}$$

### Step 5: Degree form (Euler-characteristic multiplicativity)

Since $\pi$ is proper (finite), pushforward preserves degree: $\int_Y \alpha = \
int_X \pi_*\alpha$ for $\alpha \in CH^n(Y)$. Applying this to $c_n(Y)$:
$$\int_Y c_n(Y) = \int_X \pi_* c_n(Y) = \int_X d\cdot c_n(X) = d\int_X c_n(X).$$

By the Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch / Gauss–Bonnet–Chern theorem (valid in any char
acteristic), $\int_X c_n(T_X) = \chi_{\ell}(X)$ (the $\ell$-adic Euler character
istic), so this reads $\chi_\ell(Y) = d\cdot \chi_\ell(X)$.

| 1 | Miracle flatness (commutative algebra) | **None** — valid in any character
istic |

| 4 | Projection formula for Chow groups | **None** — Fulton, over any field |

**No step uses**: resolution of singularities, the Lefschetz principle, reductio
n to characteristic $0$, the complex topology, covering-space theory, or any ana
lytic input. The proof is entirely algebraic.

### Why the question is non-trivial

One might expect the multiplicativity $\chi(Y) = d\,\chi(X)$ to rely on the topo
logical argument that a finite étale cover is a degree-$d$ covering space in the
 complex topology — an argument available only in characteristic $0$. The algebr
aic proof above replaces this with: (i) the étale property giving $T_Y \cong \pi
^*T_X$ (so the tangent bundle pulls back, making $c_n$ pull back), and (ii) the
projection formula (which converts pullback-then-pushforward into multiplication
 by the degree). Both ingredients are characteristic-independent.

The hypothesis **unramified** is essential: it ensures there is no Riemann–Hurwi
tz correction term. In positive characteristic, a finite purely inseparable morp
hism (which is ramified everywhere) would *not* satisfy the conclusion — but suc
h a morphism is excluded by the unramified hypothesis. The unramified condition
also forces separability at the generic point, ruling out the purely inseparable
 case entirely.

### Conclusion

**Yes**, the equation $c_n(Y) = d \cdot c_n(X)$ (understood as $\pi_* c_n(Y) = d
 \cdot c_n(X)$ in $CH^n(X)$, or equivalently $\int_Y c_n(Y) = d\int_X c_n(X)$) h
olds for any algebraically closed field $\Bbbk$, including those of positive cha
racteristic. The proof is purely algebraic and characteristic-independent. $\bla
cksquare$
