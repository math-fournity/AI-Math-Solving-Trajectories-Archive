## Proof

Let $G = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} = \langle g_1, g_2 \mid
 g_1^2 = e,\ g_2^2 = e \rangle$, where $g_1$ generates the first copy and $g_2$
the second.

### Part 1: $G$ is infinite

By the normal form theorem for free products, every element of $G$ has a unique
expression as a **reduced word**: an alternating product of non-identity element
s from the two factors. Since each factor is $\mathbb{Z}_2$ (whose only non-iden
tity element is its generator), a reduced word is exactly an alternating word
$$g_{i_1} g_{i_2} \cdots g_{i_n}, \qquad i_k \in \{1,2\},\quad i_k \neq i_{k+1},
\quad n \geq 0.$$
Consecutive letters from the same factor would collapse ($g_1 g_1 = e$, $g_2 g_2
 = e$), so reduction forces alternation. The words
$$g_1,\ g_2,\ g_1 g_2,\ g_2 g_1,\ g_1 g_2 g_1,\ g_2 g_1 g_2,\ g_1 g_2 g_1 g_2,\
\dots$$
are all reduced and pairwise distinct (different lengths or different starting l
etters give different normal forms). There is one such word for every length $n
\geq 1$, yielding infinitely many distinct elements. Hence **$G$ is infinite** (
indeed $G \cong D_\infty$, the infinite dihedral group).

### Part 2: The normal subgroup $N$ and $|G/N|$

The commutator is
$$[g_1, g_2] = g_1\, g_2\, g_1^{-1}\, g_2^{-1}.$$
Since $g_1^2 = g_2^2 = e$, we have $g_1^{-1} = g_1$ and $g_2^{-1} = g_2$, so
$$[g_1, g_2] = g_1 g_2 g_1 g_2 = (g_1 g_2)^2.$$

Let $N = \langle\!\langle (g_1 g_2)^2 \rangle\!\rangle$ be the normal closure of
 this element. Then
$$G/N = \langle g_1, g_2 \mid g_1^2 = e,\ g_2^2 = e,\ (g_1 g_2)^2 = e \rangle.$$

The relation $(g_1 g_2)^2 = e$ gives $g_1 g_2 g_1 g_2 = e$, hence
$$g_1 g_2 = (g_1 g_2)^{-1} = g_2^{-1} g_1^{-1} = g_2 g_1.$$
So **$g_1$ and $g_2$ commute** in $G/N$. With commutativity and $g_1^2 = g_2^2 =
 e$, every element of $G/N$ reduces to one of
$$e,\quad g_1,\quad g_2,\quad g_1 g_2.$$
These four are distinct: $g_1 \neq e$ and $g_2 \neq e$ are forced by the relatio
ns (the presentation $\langle g_1,g_2 \mid g_1^2=g_2^2=e,\ g_1 g_2 = g_2 g_1\ran
gle$ is exactly the Klein four-group $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$, which h
as order $4$), and $g_1 g_2 \neq e$ (else $g_1 = g_2^{-1} = g_2$, collapsing the
 two free factors, which the relation $(g_1g_2)^2=e$ does not force).

Concretely, $G/N \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$, the Klein four-group.

### Answer

$$\boxed{|G/N| = 4}.$$

$G$ has infinitely many elements (the alternating words are all distinct), and $
G/N \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ has **4 elements**.
