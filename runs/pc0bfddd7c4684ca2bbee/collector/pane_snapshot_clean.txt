Looking at this problem, I need to prove two things: the limsup inequality, and
that 1 is the best constant.

## Proof

**Part 1: $\limsup_{n\to\infty} n\!\left(\frac{1+a_{n+1}}{a_n}-1\right) \geq 1$.
**

Suppose for contradiction that $\limsup_{n\to\infty} n\!\left(\frac{1+a_{n+1}}{a
_n}-1\right) < 1$. Then there exist $\alpha \in (0,1)$ and an integer $N_0$ such
 that for all $n \geq N_0$:
$$
\frac{1+a_{n+1}}{a_n} \leq 1 + \frac{\alpha}{n}, \qquad\text{i.e.,}\qquad a_{n+1
} \leq a_n\cdot\frac{n+\alpha}{n} - 1. \tag{1}
$$

Since $a_{n+1} > 0$, equation (1) forces $a_n > \frac{n}{n+\alpha}$ for all $n \
geq N_0$. Define
$$
r_n := a_n - \frac{n}{n+\alpha} > 0, \qquad n \geq N_0. \tag{2}
$$

**Step A (polynomial upper bound on $a_n$).** From (1), $\frac{a_n}{1+a_{n+1}} \
geq \frac{n}{n+\alpha}$ for $n \geq N_0$. Taking the product from $N_0$ to $M$:
$$
\prod_{n=N_0}^{M}\frac{a_n}{1+a_{n+1}} \geq \prod_{n=N_0}^{M}\frac{n}{n+\alpha}.
$$
The left side telescopes as $\frac{a_{N_0}}{1+a_{M+1}}\prod_{n=N_0+1}^{M}\frac{a
_n}{1+a_n} \leq \frac{a_{N_0}}{1+a_{M+1}}$ (since each $\frac{a_n}{1+a_n}<1$). T
he right side equals $\frac{\Gamma(M+1)\,\Gamma(N_0+\alpha)}{\Gamma(N_0)\,\Gamma
(M+1+\alpha)} \sim C_0\, M^{-\alpha}$ with $C_0 = \frac{\Gamma(N_0+\alpha)}{\Gam
ma(N_0)} > 0$, using $\frac{\Gamma(M+1)}{\Gamma(M+1+\alpha)} \sim M^{-\alpha}$.
Therefore
$$
\frac{a_{N_0}}{1+a_{M+1}} \geq C_0\, M^{-\alpha} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a_{M+
1} \leq \frac{a_{N_0}}{C_0}\,M^{\alpha} - 1,
$$
so $r_n \leq C_1\, n^{\alpha}$ for some constant $C_1 > 0$ and all sufficiently
large $n$.

**Step B ( $r_n$ decreases to $-\infty$, contradiction).** Substituting $a_n = \
frac{n}{n+\alpha} + r_n$ into (1):
$$
a_{n+1} \leq \left(\frac{n}{n+\alpha}+r_n\right)\frac{n+\alpha}{n} - 1 = r_n\cdo
t\frac{n+\alpha}{n}.
$$
Hence
$$
r_{n+1} = a_{n+1} - \frac{n+1}{n+1+\alpha} \leq r_n\cdot\frac{n+\alpha}{n} - \fr
ac{n+1}{n+1+\alpha},
$$
which gives
$$
r_{n+1} - r_n \leq \frac{\alpha\, r_n}{n} - \frac{n+1}{n+1+\alpha}. \tag{3}
$$
Using the bound $r_n \leq C_1 n^{\alpha}$ from Step A:
$$
r_{n+1} - r_n \leq \alpha C_1\, n^{\alpha - 1} - \frac{n+1}{n+1+\alpha}.
$$
Since $\alpha < 1$, we have $\alpha C_1\, n^{\alpha-1} \to 0$, while $\frac{n+1}
{n+1+\alpha} \to 1$. Thus for all $n \geq N_1$ (some sufficiently large $N_1$),
$$
r_{n+1} - r_n \leq -\tfrac{1}{2}.
$$
Summing from $N_1$ to $n-1$: $r_n \leq r_{N_1} - \frac{1}{2}(n - N_1)$, which is
 negative for $n > N_1 + 2\,r_{N_1}$. This contradicts $r_n > 0$ from (2).

Therefore $\limsup_{n\to\infty} n\!\left(\frac{1+a_{n+1}}{a_n}-1\right) \geq 1$.
 $\blacksquare$

**Part 2: The constant $1$ cannot be replaced by any larger number.**

Let $c > 1$ be arbitrary. Choose $a_n = n^p$ with $1 < p < c$. Then
$$
\frac{1+a_{n+1}}{a_n} = \frac{1+(n+1)^p}{n^p} = \frac{1}{n^p} + \left(1+\frac{1}
{n}\right)^p.
$$
Using the binomial expansion $(1+1/n)^p = 1 + \frac{p}{n} + \frac{p(p-1)}{2n^2}
+ O(n^{-3})$:
$$
n\!\left(\frac{1+a_{n+1}}{a_n}-1\right) = n^{1-p} + p + \frac{p(p-1)}{2n} + O(n^
{-2}).
$$
Since $p > 1$, $n^{1-p} \to 0$, and the remaining terms vanish, so
$$
\lim_{n\to\infty} n\!\left(\frac{1+a_{n+1}}{a_n}-1\right) = p < c.
$$
Thus $\limsup = p < c$, and the inequality $\limsup \geq c$ **fails** for this s
equence. Since $c > 1$ was arbitrary, no number larger than $1$ can serve as the
 lower bound. Moreover, taking $p \to 1^+$ makes $\limsup \to 1^+$, confirming $
1$ is sharp. $\blacksquare$
