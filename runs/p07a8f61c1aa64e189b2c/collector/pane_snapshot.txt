## Answer

The condition $A\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0$ does **not** imply that $A$ is finite. It i
s equivalent to $A$ being a torsion group, and infinite torsion groups exist.

**Proof.**

**Step 1: $A\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0$ if and only if $A$ is a torsion group.**

($\Leftarrow$) Suppose $A$ is torsion. For any $a\in A$ and any $q\in\mathbb{Q}$
, choose $n\geq 1$ with $na=0$. Write $q=n\cdot\frac{q}{n}$. Then
$$a\otimes q = a\otimes\left(n\cdot\frac{q}{n}\right) = (na)\otimes\frac{q}{n} =
 0\otimes\frac{q}{n} = 0.$$
Since every simple tensor vanishes, $A\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0$.

($\Rightarrow$) Suppose $A$ is not torsion. Then there exists $a\in A$ of infini
te order, so the subgroup $\mathbb{Z}a\subseteq A$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}$
. Since $\mathbb{Q}$ is flat as a $\mathbb{Z}$-module, the inclusion $\mathbb{Z}
a\hookrightarrow A$ induces an injection
$$\mathbb{Z}a\otimes\mathbb{Q}\hookrightarrow A\otimes\mathbb{Q}.$$
But $\mathbb{Z}a\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong\mathbb{Z}\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong\mathbb{Q}
\neq 0$, so $A\otimes\mathbb{Q}\neq 0$. Contrapositively, $A\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0\
Rightarrow A$ is torsion.

**Step 2: A torsion group need not be finite.**

Consider
$$A=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$$
for any prime $p$. This is a direct sum of infinitely many copies of $\mathbb{Z}
/p\mathbb{Z}$, hence is an infinite abelian group. Every element has order divid
ing $p$, so $A$ is torsion. By Step 1, $A\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0$.

Yet $A$ is infinite (it has cardinality $\aleph_0$, being a countable direct sum
 of nontrivial finite groups).

**Conclusion.** The condition $A\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0$ is equivalent to $A$ being
a torsion group, which is strictly weaker than being finite. The group $\bigoplu
s_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ is a concrete counterexample: it satisfi
es $A\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0$ but is infinite. Therefore $A\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0$ does
 **not** imply that $A$ is finite. $\blacksquare$
