**Key idea.** Write $A(x,y)$ as a sum of indicator functions and apply Tonelli's
 theorem to swap the sum and integral.

**Step 1: Rewrite $A$ as a sum.** By definition,
$$A(x,y) = \#\{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^2 : m^2+n^2 \le x^2+y^2\} = \sum_{(m,n)\in\mat
hbb{Z}^2} \mathbf{1}\!\left[m^2+n^2 \le x^2+y^2\right].$$

**Step 2: Swap sum and integral (Tonelli).** Every summand is non-negative, so T
onelli's theorem gives
$$I := \iint A(x,y)\,e^{-x^2-y^2}\,dx\,dy = \sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^2} \iint_{x
^2+y^2\,\ge\, m^2+n^2} e^{-x^2-y^2}\,dx\,dy.$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the inner integral.** Let $r^2 = m^2+n^2$. Switching to polar
 coordinates,
$$\iint e^{-x^2-y^2}\,dx\,dy = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{\infty} e^{-\rho^2}\rho\
,d\rho\,d\theta = \pi.$$
The integral over the disk $\rho \le r$ is
$$\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{r} e^{-\rho^2}\rho\,d\rho\,d\theta = 2\pi\cdot\tfrac{
1}{2}(1-e^{-r^2}) = \pi(1-e^{-r^2}).$$
Hence the integral over the exterior region $\rho \ge r$ is
$$\pi - \pi(1-e^{-r^2}) = \pi\,e^{-r^2} = \pi\,e^{-(m^2+n^2)}.$$

**Step 4: Sum over the lattice.** Therefore
$$I = \pi\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^2} e^{-m^2-n^2} = \pi\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z
}} e^{-m^2}\right)^{\!2},$$
since the double sum factors (the summand $e^{-m^2-n^2}=e^{-m^2}e^{-n^2}$ is a p
roduct of independent terms, and the sum is absolutely convergent).

**Step 5: Express in terms of $g$.** We have
$$\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}} e^{-m^2} = e^{-0} + 2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty} e^{-m^2} = 1 +
2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty} e^{-m^2}.$$
Given $g = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} e^{-k^2} = 1 + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} e^{-k^2}$, we
get $\sum_{m=1}^{\infty} e^{-m^2} = g-1$, so
$$\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}} e^{-m^2} = 1 + 2(g-1) = 2g - 1.$$

**Conclusion.**
$$\boxed{I = \pi\,(2g-1)^2}.$$
