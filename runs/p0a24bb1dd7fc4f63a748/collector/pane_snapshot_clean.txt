**Goal.** Evaluate
$$L = \lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{n}\left(1+\frac{x}{n}\right)^{n+1}e^{-2x}\,dx.$
$

**Step 1 — Extend to a fixed domain.** Define, for each $n\geq 1$,
$$f_n(x) = \left(1+\frac{x}{n}\right)^{n+1}e^{-2x}\,\mathbf{1}_{[0,n]}(x),\qquad
 x\geq 0.$$
Then $\displaystyle\int_0^n\left(1+\frac{x}{n}\right)^{n+1}e^{-2x}\,dx = \int_0^
{\infty} f_n(x)\,dx$, so $L = \lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^\infty f_n(x)\,dx$.

**Step 2 — Pointwise limit.** Fix $x\geq 0$. For all $n>x$ the indicator equals
$1$, and
$$\left(1+\frac{x}{n}\right)^{n+1}=\left(1+\frac{x}{n}\right)^{n}\!\left(1+\frac
{x}{n}\right)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}e^{x}\cdot 1=e^{x}.$$
Hence
$$f_n(x)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}e^{x}\,e^{-2x}=e^{-x}\qquad\text{for every }x\
geq 0.$$

**Step 3 — Dominating function.** For $x\in[0,n]$ we have $x/n\in[0,1]$, so $1+x
/n\leq 2$. Using $\ln(1+u)\leq u$ (valid for $u>-1$) with $u=x/n$,
$$\left(1+\frac{x}{n}\right)^{n}=e^{\,n\ln(1+x/n)}\leq e^{\,n\cdot(x/n)}=e^{x}.$
$
Therefore, for $x\in[0,n]$,
$$f_n(x)=\left(1+\frac{x}{n}\right)^{n}\!\left(1+\frac{x}{n}\right)e^{-2x}\leq e
^{x}\cdot 2\cdot e^{-2x}=2e^{-x}.$$
For $x>n$, $f_n(x)=0\leq 2e^{-x}$. Thus
$$0\leq f_n(x)\leq 2e^{-x}\quad\text{for all }x\geq 0,\;n\geq 1.$$
The function $g(x)=2e^{-x}$ is integrable on $[0,\infty)$ since $\int_0^\infty 2
e^{-x}\,dx=2<\infty$.

**Step 4 — Apply Lebesgue's Dominated Convergence Theorem.** Since $f_n\to e^{-x
}$ pointwise on $[0,\infty)$ and $|f_n|\leq g\in L^1([0,\infty))$, the DCT permi
ts interchange of limit and integral:
$$L=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^\infty f_n(x)\,dx=\int_0^\infty\lim_{n\to\infty}f_n(
x)\,dx=\int_0^\infty e^{-x}\,dx=\Big[-e^{-x}\Big]_0^\infty=1.$$

**Conclusion.**
$$\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{n}\left(1+\frac{x}{n}\right)^{n
+1}e^{-2x}\,dx=1.}$$
