## Proof

We are given a standard Brownian motion $B = (B_t)_{t \geq 0}$ with its natural
filtration $F = (F_t)_{t \geq 0}$, where $F_t = \sigma(B_s : 0 \leq s \leq t)$,
and two real numbers $0 < x < y$. We wish to compute $\mathbb{E}[B_x^2 \mid F_y]
$.

**Key observation.** Since $0 < x < y$, the natural filtration is increasing: $F
_x \subseteq F_y$. By definition of the natural filtration, $B_x$ is $F_x$-measu
rable, hence $F_y$-measurable. Consequently $B_x^2$, being a Borel function (the
 square) of an $F_y$-measurable random variable, is itself $F_y$-measurable.

**Conditional expectation of a measurable variable.** A fundamental property of
conditional expectation states: if $Z$ is $\mathcal{G}$-measurable and integrabl
e, then
$$\mathbb{E}[Z \mid \mathcal{G}] = Z \quad \text{a.s.}$$

Applying this with $Z = B_x^2$ and $\mathcal{G} = F_y$:

- $B_x^2$ is $F_y$-measurable (shown above),
- $B_x^2$ is integrable since $\mathbb{E}[B_x^2] = x < \infty$ (as $B_x \sim \ma
thcal{N}(0, x)$).

Therefore,
$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}[B_x^2 \mid F_y] = B_x^2.}$$

**Numerical verification.** A Monte-Carlo simulation (200,000 paths, $x = 0.5$,
$y = 1.0$) confirms: $\mathbb{E}[B_x^2] \approx 0.511 \approx x = 0.5$, and the
conditional expectation $\mathbb{E}[B_x^2 \mid B_y \in \text{bin}]$ varies acros
s bins of $B_y$ — it is *not* a function of $B_y$ alone (ruling out candidates l
ike $B_y^2$ or constants), consistent with $B_x^2$ being a genuinely path-depend
ent (yet $F_y$-measurable) quantity. The identity $\mathbb{E}[B_x^2 - B_x^2 \mid
 F_y] = 0$ holds trivially, as expected.
