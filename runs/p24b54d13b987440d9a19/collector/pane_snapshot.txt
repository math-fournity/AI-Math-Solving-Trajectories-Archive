## Answer: Yes, $T$ is a stopping time.

**Setup.** Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, (\mathcal{F}_t)_{t\ge 0}, P)$ be a filtere
d probability space with a **right-continuous** filtration ($\mathcal{F}_{t+}=\m
athcal{F}_t$). Let $X=(X_t)_{t\ge 0}$ be an **adapted** process with **left-cont
inuous** sample paths. Let $G\subseteq\mathbb{R}$ be **open**. Define the first
hitting time (debut)
$$T \;=\; \inf\{t\ge 0:\, X_t\in G\},\qquad \inf\emptyset:=+\infty.$$
We prove $\{T\le t\}\in\mathcal{F}_t$ for every $t\ge 0$.

---

**Step 1 (Countable reduction of $\{T<u\}$).** We claim that for every $u>0$,
$$\{T<u\}\;=\;\bigcup_{\substack{s\in\mathbb{Q}\\ 0\le s<u}}\{X_s\in G\}.\tag{$\
ast$}$$

*Proof of $(\ast)$.* The inclusion "$\supseteq$" is immediate: if $X_s\in G$ for
 some rational $s<u$, then $T\le s<u$.

For "$\subseteq$": suppose $T(\omega)<u$. Then there exists $s_0\in[0,u)$ with $
X_{s_0}(\omega)\in G$. Since $G$ is open, pick $\delta>0$ with $B(X_{s_0}(\omega
),\delta)\subseteq G$. By **left-continuity** of $s\mapsto X_s(\omega)$ at $s_0$
, there exists $\eta>0$ such that $|X_s(\omega)-X_{s_0}(\omega)|<\delta$ for all
 $s\in(s_0-\eta,\,s_0]$, hence $X_s(\omega)\in G$ on this interval.

- If $s_0>0$: shrink $\eta$ so that $s_0-\eta>0$, then choose a rational $s\in(s
_0-\eta,\,s_0)$. Then $0<s<u$ and $X_s(\omega)\in G$.
- If $s_0=0$: then $X_0(\omega)\in G$; $0$ is rational and $0<u$ (since $T(\omeg
a)<u$ and $T(\omega)\le s_0=0$), so $s=0$ works.

In either case $X_s(\omega)\in G$ for some rational $s\in[0,u)$. $\square$

**Step 2 (Measurability of $\{T<u\}$).** Since $X$ is adapted, $\{X_s\in G\}\in\
mathcal{F}_s\subseteq\mathcal{F}_u$ for every $s<u$. The union in $(\ast)$ is co
untable, so
$$\{T<u\}\in\mathcal{F}_u,\qquad\forall\, u>0.\tag{$\ast\ast$}$$

**Step 3 (From strict to non-strict inequality via right-continuity).** We use t
he identity
$$\{T\le t\}\;=\;\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\{T<t+\tfrac{1}{n}\}.\tag{$\ast\ast\ast$}
$$
(If $T\le t$ then $T<t+1/n$ for all $n$; conversely, $T<t+1/n$ for all $n$ gives
 $T\le\inf_n(t+1/n)=t$.)

Applying $(\ast\ast)$ with $u=t+1/n$, each $\{T<t+1/n\}\in\mathcal{F}_{t+1/n}$.
Hence
$$\{T\le t\}\;=\;\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\{T<t+\tfrac{1}{n}\}\;\in\;\bigcap_{n=1}^
{\infty}\mathcal{F}_{t+1/n}\;=\;\mathcal{F}_{t+}.$$

By **right-continuity of the filtration**, $\mathcal{F}_{t+}=\mathcal{F}_t$. The
refore
$$\{T\le t\}\in\mathcal{F}_t,\qquad\forall\, t\ge 0,$$
which is precisely the definition of $T$ being a stopping time. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark on the role of each hypothesis.**

- **Left-continuity of $X$ + openness of $G$** (Step 1): together they let us re
place the uncountable union $\bigcup_{s<u}\{X_s\in G\}$ by a countable one over
$\mathbb{Q}$, because hitting $G$ at $s_0$ forces a whole left-neighborhood of $
s_0$ to remain in $G$, guaranteeing a nearby rational witness.
- **Right-continuity of $(\mathcal{F}_t)$** (Step 3): it promotes the a priori m
embership $\{T\le t\}\in\mathcal{F}_{t+}$ (obtained by approximating $t$ from ab
ove) down to $\mathcal{F}_t$. Without it, one only gets $\{T\le t\}\in\mathcal{F
}_{t+}\supsetneq\mathcal{F}_t$ in general, and $T$ need not be a stopping time.

Both ingredients are essential, and together they yield the conclusion.
