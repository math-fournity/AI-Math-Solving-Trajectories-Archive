## Proof

**Setup: Gaussian map.** Let $P$ be the polyhedron with $V$ vertices, $E$ edges,
 $F$ faces. On the unit sphere $S^2$, construct the **normal fan** (Gaussian map
) $G$: each face $f$ maps to its outward unit normal $\mathbf{n}_f$ (a vertex of
 $G$); each edge $e$ maps to the great-circle arc connecting the normals of its
two adjacent faces (an edge of $G$); each vertex $v$ maps to its normal cone $N(
v)$ (a spherical polygon, i.e., a face of $G$). Then $G$ has $F$ vertices, $E$ e
dges, $V$ faces, and satisfies $F - E + V = 2$.

Let $-G$ denote the antipodal image of $G$ (reflect every point through the orig
in). The face of $-G$ corresponding to vertex $v$ is $-N(v)$.

**Key characterization.** Two points $p, q \in P$ are antipodal iff there exists
 a direction $\mathbf{d}$ such that $p$ maximizes $\langle \mathbf{d}, \cdot \ra
ngle$ over $P$ and $q$ minimizes it. Equivalently, $\mathbf{d} \in N(p)$ and $-\
mathbf{d} \in N(q)$, i.e., $\mathbf{d} \in N(p) \cap (-N(q))$.

- **Vertices $u, v$ are antipodal** iff $N(u) \cap (-N(v)) \neq \emptyset$, i.e.
, the face $N(u)$ of $G$ intersects the face $-N(v)$ of $-G$.
- **Edge-midpoints $m_{e_1}, m_{e_2}$ are antipodal** iff there exists $\mathbf{
d}$ in the (closed) arc of $e_1$ in $G$ and in the (closed) arc of $e_2$ in $-G$
, i.e., these two arcs intersect. (The midpoint of edge $e$ achieves the extremu
m of $\langle \mathbf{d}, \cdot \rangle$ iff $\mathbf{d}$ lies in the normal con
e of $e$, which is the closed arc.)

**General position.** The conditions on $P$ ensure $G$ and $-G$ are in general p
osition on $S^2$:

1. **No vertex of $G$ coincides with a vertex of $-G$:** This would mean $\mathb
f{n}_f = -\mathbf{n}_g$ for faces $f, g$, i.e., $f \parallel g$. But if $f \para
llel g$, any edge of $f$ not shared with $g$ is parallel to $g$ (its direction i
s perpendicular to $\mathbf{n}_f = -\mathbf{n}_g$), violating the edge-face cond
ition. So no parallel faces exist.

2. **No vertex of $G$ lies on an edge of $-G$ (and vice versa):** $\mathbf{n}_f$
 lies on the arc of $e$ in $-G$ iff $-\mathbf{n}_f$ lies on the arc of $e$ in $G
$ iff $e$ is parallel to $f$. By condition, this happens only when $e$ is adjace
nt to $f$, but then $-\mathbf{n}_f$ is an *endpoint* of $e$'s arc in $G$, and on
e checks it does not lie on the interior of $e$'s arc in $-G$ (arcs have length
$< \pi$).

3. **No edge of $G$ overlaps an edge of $-G$:** Overlap requires the same great
circle, i.e., parallel edges $e_1 \parallel e_2$. For $e_1 = e_2$, the arcs in $
G$ and $-G$ are antipodal and disjoint (length $< \pi$). For $e_1 \neq e_2$, par
allel edges are excluded by hypothesis.

Thus all intersections between $G$ and $-G$ are **transversal edge-edge crossing
s**.

**Counting intersection points $I$.** Let $I$ be the number of crossing points b
etween edges of $G$ and edges of $-G$. Each crossing involves one $G$-edge (arc
of $e_1$) and one $-G$-edge (arc of $e_2$), giving an ordered pair $(e_1, e_2)$
with $e_1 \neq e_2$.

If the arc of $e_1$ in $G$ crosses the arc of $e_2$ in $-G$ at point $\mathbf{p}
$, then by antipodal symmetry, the arc of $e_2$ in $G$ crosses the arc of $e_1$
in $-G$ at $-\mathbf{p}$. So each unordered pair $\{e_1, e_2\}$ with crossing ar
cs contributes **exactly 2** points to $I$.

Since $B$ counts unordered pairs $\{e_1, e_2\}$ whose midpoint-arcs cross (and b
y general position, crossings are the only possible intersections), we get:
$$I = 2B.$$

**Euler's formula on the overlay.** Overlay $G$ and $-G$ on $S^2$. The combined
graph has:
- **Vertices:** $V' = 2F + I$ ( $F$ from $G$, $F$ from $-G$, plus $I$ crossings)
.
- **Edges:** Each of the $E$ edges of $G$ is split by the crossings on it; total
 $G$-segments $= E + I$. Similarly for $-G$: $E + I$. So $E' = 2E + 2I$.
- **Faces:** Each face of the overlay is $N(u) \cap (-N(v))$ for some ordered pa
ir $(u, v)$ of vertices. Since normal cones are geodesically convex, each non-em
pty intersection is connected. The number of non-empty intersections equals the
number of ordered antipodal pairs $(u, v)$. Antipodality is symmetric ($\mathbf{
d} \in N(u) \cap (-N(v)) \Rightarrow -\mathbf{d} \in N(v) \cap (-N(u))$) and no
vertex is antipodal to itself, so this count is $2A$. Thus $F' = 2A$.

Euler's formula on $S^2$:
$$V' - E' + F' = 2$$
$$(2F + I) - (2E + 2I) + 2A = 2$$
$$2A = 2 - 2F + 2E + I.$$

**Combining.** Substituting $I = 2B$:
$$2A = 2 - 2F + 2E + 2B$$
$$A - B = 1 - F + E = E - F + 1.$$

By Euler's formula for $P$: $V - E + F = 2$, so $E - F = V - 2$. Therefore:

$$\boxed{A - B = V - 1.}$$

Equivalently, $A - B = E - F + 1$.
