## Proof

 functional and no solution, answer, or proof sketch. Proceeding.

---

We wish to find the norm of the linear functional
$$f(x) = \sum_{j=1}^{\infty} \xi_j \,\frac{1}{\sqrt{j(j+1)}}, \qquad x = (\xi_1,
 \xi_2, \ldots) \in l^2.$$

### Step 1. Identify the representing vector

Define $y = (y_1, y_2, \ldots)$ with $y_j = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{j(j+1)}}$. Then form
ally
$$f(x) = \sum_{j=1}^{\infty} \xi_j\, y_j = \langle x, y \rangle,$$
the standard $l^2$ inner product. For this representation to be valid we need $y
 \in l^2$.

### Step 2. Verify $y \in l^2$ and compute $\|y\|$

$$\|y\|^2 = \sum_{j=1}^{\infty} y_j^2 = \sum_{j=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{j(j+1)}.$$

Using the partial-fraction (telescoping) identity
$$\frac{1}{j(j+1)} = \frac{1}{j} - \frac{1}{j+1},$$
the series telescopes:
$$\sum_{j=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{j(j+1)} = \sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{j}-\f
rac{1}{j+1}\right) = \lim_{N\to\infty}\left(1 - \frac{1}{N+1}\right) = 1.$$

Hence $\|y\|^2 = 1$, so $\|y\| = 1$ and $y \in l^2$.

### Step 3. $f$ is well-defined and bounded on $l^2$

For any $x \in l^2$, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,
$$|f(x)| = |\langle x, y\rangle| \le \|x\|\,\|y\| = \|x\|\cdot 1 = \|x\|.$$
Thus $f$ is a bounded linear functional with
$$\|f\| = \sup_{\|x\|\le 1} |f(x)| \le 1.$$

### Step 4. The bound is attained

Take $x = y$ (which has $\|y\| = 1$). Then
$$f(y) = \langle y, y\rangle = \|y\|^2 = 1.$$
Since $\|y\| = 1$ and $|f(y)| = 1$, we have $\|f\| \ge 1$.

### Step 5. Conclusion

Combining the upper and lower bounds,
$$\|f\| = 1.$$

By the Riesz representation theorem, this is consistent: every bounded linear fu
nctional on $l^2$ has the form $f(x) = \langle x, y\rangle$ with $\|f\| = \|y\|$
, and we have shown $\|y\| = 1$.

$$\boxed{\|f\| = 1}$$
