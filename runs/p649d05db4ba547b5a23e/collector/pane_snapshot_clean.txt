## Answer

**Yes — it is always possible.** On a 2‑dimensional Lorentzian manifold topologi
cally an (open) disc, there exist global coordinates $(t,x)$ covering the whole
manifold in which
\[
ds^{2}=\Omega^{2}(t,x)\,(-dt^{2}+dx^{2}).
\]

(The target line element has signature $(1,1)$, so the manifold is necessarily L
orentzian; a Riemannian metric could not be written in this form with a real $\O
mega$.)

## Proof

Let $(M,g)$ be a 2‑dimensional Lorentzian manifold diffeomorphic to the open dis
c $D\cong\mathbb R^{2}$.

### 1. Global null frame

At every point of $M$ there are exactly two null directions. They form two smoot
h line subbundles of $TM$. Since $D$ is **contractible**, every line bundle over
 it is trivial, and since $D$ is **simply connected**, the twofold cover that or
ders the two null directions is trivial. Hence we may choose **global, smooth, n
onvanishing** vector fields $L$ and $M$ with

\[
g(L,L)=0,\qquad g(M,M)=0,\qquad g(L,M)\neq 0\ \text{everywhere},
\]

i.e. $L$ and $M$ are the two null directions, globally defined and transverse.

### 2. The null foliations have no closed leaves

Identify $M$ with $\mathbb R^{2}$ via a diffeomorphism and regard $L$ as a nonva
nishing vector field on the plane. Suppose $L$ had a closed orbit $\gamma$. By t
he Jordan curve theorem $\gamma$ bounds a compact disk $B$. Along $\partial B=\g
amma$ the field $L$ is tangent to $\gamma$, so the unit vector $L/|L|$ winds $\p
m 1$ times around $S^{1}$ as one traverses $\gamma$. By the **Poincaré–Hopf inde
x theorem** the index of $L$ in $B$ equals $\pm1$, forcing a zero of $L$ inside
$B$ — contradicting $L\neq0$ everywhere. So **no closed leaf exists**.

### 3. Every leaf is a properly embedded line

A non‑proper orbit of a planar flow possesses a nonempty $\omega$‑ or $\alpha$‑l
imit set. By the **Poincaré–Bendixson theorem** (and its standard extension), a
compact minimal set of a planar flow is either a fixed point or a periodic orbit
. Since $L$ has no fixed points (it is nonvanishing) and no periodic orbits (Ste
p 2), there are **no recurrent orbits**; consequently every orbit of $L$ is **pr
oper**. Each leaf is therefore a properly embedded copy of $\mathbb R$.

The same holds for $M$.

### 4. Topological triviality of the foliation (Kaplan)

A classical theorem of Kaplan states that every foliation of the plane by proper
ly embedded lines is topologically equivalent to the foliation by parallel lines
. In particular, for each of $\mathcal F_{L}$ and $\mathcal F_{M}$ there exists
a smooth embedded curve ($\sigma_{L}$, $\sigma_{M}$ respectively) that is **tran
sverse** to the foliation and **meets every leaf exactly once**.

### 5. Smooth global first integrals $u$ and $v$

Define $u:M\to\mathbb R$ as follows: for $p\in M$, let $u(p)$ be the parameter o
f the unique intersection of the $L$‑leaf through $p$ with $\sigma_{L}$. Concret
ely, if $\Phi_{L}$ is the flow of $L$ and $h_{L}$ a defining function for $\sigm
a_{L}$ (so $dh_{L}(L)\neq0$ along $\sigma_{L}$ by transversality), the hitting t
ime $\tau(p)$ defined implicitly by
\[
h_{L}\bigl(\Phi_{L}(p,\tau(p))\bigr)=0
\]
is smooth by the implicit function theorem, and $u(p)=s_{L}\bigl(\Phi_{L}(p,\tau
(p))\bigr)$ (with $s_{L}$ the parameter along $\sigma_{L}$) is smooth. By constr
uction $L(u)=0$ and $du\neq0$ (transversality gives $du$ nonzero on directions t
ransverse to $L$). Thus $u$ is a smooth submersion constant on $L$‑leaves.

Repeating the construction for $M$ yields a smooth submersion $v$ with $M(v)=0$,
 $dv\neq0$.

### 6. $(u,v)$ is a global diffeomorphism

Since $L$ and $M$ are transverse, $du$ and $dv$ are linearly independent, so $(u
,v):M\to\mathbb R^{2}$ is a **local diffeomorphism**. It remains to show injecti
vity. Suppose two points $p,q$ satisfy $(u(p),v(p))=(u(q),v(q))$. Then $p,q$ lie
 on the same $L$‑leaf and the same $M$‑leaf. Along an $L$‑leaf the function $v$
satisfies $L(v)\neq0$ (because $M$ is transverse to $L$, so $dv$ does not annihi
late $L$); hence $v$ is **strictly monotone** along each $L$‑leaf and an $L$‑lea
f and an $M$‑leaf can intersect **at most once**. Therefore $p=q$.

A local diffeomorphism that is injective is a global diffeomorphism onto its (op
en, by invariance of domain) image $U\subseteq\mathbb R^{2}$.

### 7. The metric is conformally flat in $(u,v)$

In $(u,v)$ coordinates $L=\partial_{u}$ and $M=\partial_{v}$ are null, so
\[
g_{uu}=g(L,L)=0,\qquad g_{vv}=g(M,M)=0,\qquad g_{uv}=g(L,M)\neq0.
\]
Thus
\[
g = 2g_{uv}\,du\,dv .
\]
Set
\[
t=\tfrac12(u+v),\qquad x=\tfrac12(u-v).
\]
Then $du\,dv = dt^{2}-dx^{2}$, so
\[
g = 2g_{uv}\,(dt^{2}-dx^{2}) = \Omega^{2}\,(-dt^{2}+dx^{2}),
\qquad \Omega^{2}:=-2g_{uv}.
\]
Choosing the labelling of $u,v$ (or their signs) so that $g_{uv}<0$ gives $\Omeg
a^{2}>0$, a smooth positive conformal factor. The functions $(t,x)$ are global c
oordinates on $M$ because $(u,v)$ is a global diffeomorphism.

### Conclusion

The simple connectivity (indeed contractibility) of the disc guarantees a global

theorem makes each foliation a smooth product; and the transversality of the two
 null families makes $(u,v)$, hence $(t,x)$, a global diffeomorphism. Therefore
**every** 2‑dimensional Lorentzian manifold topologically a disc admits global c
oordinates $(t,x)$ with
\[
ds^{2}=\Omega^{2}(t,x)\,(-dt^{2}+dx^{2}).
