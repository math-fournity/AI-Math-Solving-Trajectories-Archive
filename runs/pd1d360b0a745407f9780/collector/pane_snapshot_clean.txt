## Proof

f this power series (in $1/\lambda$) upgrades to *locally uniform* convergence,
which is the actual hypothesis needed for analyticity. Absolute convergence at e
ach point alone would not suffice; the power-series structure is what bridges th

---

**Step 1 — Reformulate as a power series in $z = 1/\lambda$.**

Define $g(\lambda) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} A^n/\lambda^n = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} A^n
 z^n$ with $z = 1/\lambda$. This is a power series in $z$ with coefficients in t
he Banach space $\mathfrak L(X)$ (equipped with the operator norm).

**Step 2 — Radius of convergence and absolute convergence.**

By Gelfand's formula, $\lim_{n\to\infty} \|A^n\|^{1/n} = r(A)$. The (vector-valu
ed) root test gives radius of convergence $1/r(A)$ in the $z$-variable, i.e. the
 series converges absolutely in operator norm for $|z| < 1/r(A)$, equivalently f
or $|\lambda| > r(A)$. This is exactly the hypothesis.

**Step 3 — Absolute convergence implies locally uniform convergence on $|\lambda
| > r(A)$.**

Let $K \subset \{|\lambda| > r(A)\}$ be compact. Then there exists $R$ with $r(A
) < R$ such that $|\lambda| \geq R$ for all $\lambda \in K$. For every $\lambda
\in K$,
$$\left\|\frac{A^n}{\lambda^n}\right\| \leq \frac{\|A^n\|}{R^n}.$$
Moreover $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \|A^n\|/R^n < \infty$, since
$$\limsup_{n\to\infty} \left(\frac{\|A^n\|}{R^n}\right)^{1/n} = \frac{r(A)}{R} <
 1.$$
By the Weierstrass $M$-test (valid for Banach-space-valued series), $\sum A^n/\l
ambda^n$ converges **absolutely and uniformly on $K$**. Since $K$ was arbitrary,
 the convergence is locally uniform on $\{|\lambda| > r(A)\}$.

**Step 4 — Each summand is analytic.**

For each $n \geq 0$, the map $h_n(\lambda) = A^n \lambda^{-n}$ is an $\mathfrak
L(X)$-valued function. It is the product of the constant operator $A^n$ and the
scalar analytic function $\lambda \mapsto \lambda^{-n}$ on $\mathbb C \setminus
\{0\}$. Scalar multiplication is continuous and bilinear, so $h_n$ is analytic (
strongly holomorphic, hence by Dunford's theorem also weakly holomorphic) on $\m
athbb C \setminus \{0\}$, in particular on $\{|\lambda| > r(A)\}$.

**Step 5 — Apply the vector-valued Weierstrass theorem.**

The classical Weierstrass theorem extends to Banach-space-valued functions: *a s
eries of analytic Banach-space-valued functions that converges locally uniformly
 has an analytic sum, and differentiation may be performed term by term.* (Equiv
alently, one verifies weak analyticity: for every $x \in X$ and $\varphi \in X^*
$, the scalar series $\sum \varphi(A^n x)/\lambda^n$ converges locally uniformly
, hence is analytic by the scalar Weierstrass theorem; then Dunford's theorem pr
omotes weak analyticity + norm-continuity to strong analyticity.)

Therefore $g(\lambda) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} A^n/\lambda^n$ is analytic on $\{|\l
ambda| > r(A)\}$.

**Step 6 — Recover analyticity of the resolvent.**

From the given identity $\lambda R_\lambda(A) = g(\lambda)$, we have
$$R_\lambda(A) = \frac{1}{\lambda}\, g(\lambda).$$
On $\{|\lambda| > r(A)\} \subset \mathbb C \setminus \{0\}$, the scalar function
 $\lambda \mapsto 1/\lambda$ is analytic, and the product of a scalar analytic f
unction with a Banach-space-valued analytic function is analytic. Hence $f(\lamb
da) = R_\lambda(A)$ is analytic on $\{|\lambda| > r(A)\}$.

---

**Conclusion.** The deduction is valid, but the logical chain is:

$$\text{absolute convergence} \;\xRightarrow{\text{power-series structure + root
 test}}\; \text{locally uniform convergence} \;\xRightarrow{\text{Weierstrass th
eorem (vector-valued)}}\; \text{analyticity}.$$

The intermediate arrow is the essential step: *pointwise* absolute convergence a
lone does **not** imply analyticity in general — one needs locally uniform conve
rgence. For this specific series the power-series structure (in $1/\lambda$, wit
h radius governed by $r(A)$) guarantees that absolute convergence on the open ex
terior $\{|\lambda| > r(A)\}$ automatically yields locally uniform convergence t
here. So yes, the resolvent is analytic on $\{|\lambda| > r(A)\}$, and this foll
ows directly from the displayed Neumann-series identity together with the absolu
te convergence — *provided one notes that absolute convergence of a power series
 on its open domain of convergence entails local uniformity*, which is the stand
ard bridge to analyticity.
