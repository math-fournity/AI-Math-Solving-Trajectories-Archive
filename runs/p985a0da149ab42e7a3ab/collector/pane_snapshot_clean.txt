## Solution

We compute $\int_{[0,1]} x^3 \, d\mu_F$, where $\mu_F$ is the Lebesgue–Stieltjes
 measure induced by the Cantor function $F$.

**Step 1: Identify the measure.** The Cantor function $F$ is constant on every c
omplementary (middle-third) interval of the Cantor set $C$. Hence $\mu_F$ assign
s zero mass to each such interval, and $\mu_F$ is supported on $C$. Since $F$ is
 continuous, $\mu_F$ has no atoms, and $\mu_F(C)=1$ (as $F(1)-F(0)=1$).

**Step 2: Represent $\mu_F$ as a pushforward of the fair-coin measure.** Every $
x\in C$ has a ternary expansion using only digits $0$ and $2$. Write
$$x = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} 2a_i \, 3^{-i}, \qquad a_i \in \{0,1\}.$$
The map $\varphi:\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}\to C$, $\varphi((a_i)) = \sum 2a_i\,3^{-i}$
, is a bijection (mod endpoints, which form a $\mu_F$-null set). Under $\varphi$
, the measure $\mu_F$ is the pushforward of the product measure $\bigotimes_{i=1
}^\infty \tfrac12(\delta_0+\delta_1)$ (fair coins). This is verified by checking
 that the $\mu_F$-measure of a basic cylinder $\{x: \text{first } n\text{ ternar
y digits fixed}\}$ equals $2^{-n}$, matching $F$'s increment of $2^{-n}$ on each
 $n$-th level Cantor interval.

**Step 3: Reduce to a moment computation.** Let $X_i = 2a_i\,3^{-i}$ be independ
ent, with $X_i\in\{0,\,2\cdot 3^{-i}\}$ each with probability $\tfrac12$. Then $
x=\sum_i X_i$ and
$$\int_{[0,1]} x^3\,d\mu_F = \mathbb E\!\left[\Big(\sum_{i=1}^\infty X_i\Big)^3\
right].$$

The moments of $X_i$ are
$$\mathbb E[X_i] = 3^{-i},\quad \mathbb E[X_i^2]=2\cdot 3^{-2i},\quad \mathbb E[
X_i^3]=4\cdot 3^{-3i}.$$

Define the geometric sums
$$S_1=\sum_{i\ge1}3^{-i}=\frac{1/3}{1-1/3}=\frac12,\quad S_2=\sum_{i\ge1}3^{-2i}
=\frac{1/9}{1-1/9}=\frac18,\quad S_3=\sum_{i\ge1}3^{-3i}=\frac{1/27}{1-1/27}=\fr
ac1{26}.$$

**Step 4: Expand the cube.** By independence,
$$\mathbb E\!\left[\Big(\sum_i X_i\Big)^3\right] = \underbrace{\sum_i \mathbb E[
X_i^3]}_{(A)} + \underbrace{3\sum_{i\ne j}\mathbb E[X_i^2]\mathbb E[X_j]}_{(B)}
+ \underbrace{6\sum_{i<j<k}\mathbb E[X_i]\mathbb E[X_j]\mathbb E[X_k]}_{(C)}.$$

- **(A):** $\displaystyle \sum_i 4\cdot 3^{-3i}=4S_3=\frac{4}{26}=\frac{2}{13}.$

- **(B):** $\displaystyle 3\Big[\Big(\sum_i \mathbb E[X_i^2]\Big)\Big(\sum_j \ma
thbb E[X_j]\Big)-\sum_i \mathbb E[X_i^2]\mathbb E[X_i]\Big]=3\big[2S_2\cdot S_1
- 2S_3\big]=3\Big[2\cdot\tfrac18\cdot\tfrac12-2\cdot\tfrac1{26}\Big]=3\Big[\tfra
c18-\tfrac1{13}\Big]=3\cdot\tfrac5{104}=\tfrac{15}{104}.$

- **(C):** Using $\sum_{i<j<k}a_ia_ja_k=\tfrac16\big[(\sum a_i)^3-3(\sum a_i)(\s
um a_i^2)+2\sum a_i^3\big]$ with $a_i=3^{-i}$:
$$\sum_{i<j<k}3^{-i-j-k}=\tfrac16\big[S_1^3-3S_1S_2+2S_3\big]=\tfrac16\Big[\tfra
c18-3\cdot\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac18+\tfrac2{26}\Big]=\tfrac16\Big[\tfrac{26-39+16}{2
08}\Big]=\tfrac16\cdot\tfrac3{208}=\tfrac1{416}.$$
Hence $(C)=6\cdot\tfrac1{416}=\tfrac3{208}.$

**Step 5: Sum.** With common denominator $208=16\cdot 13$:
$$\frac{2}{13}+\frac{15}{104}+\frac{3}{208}=\frac{32}{208}+\frac{30}{208}+\frac{
3}{208}=\frac{65}{208}=\frac{5}{16}.$$

**Verification by symmetry.** The Cantor distribution is symmetric about $\tfrac
12$, so $\mathbb E[(x-\tfrac12)^3]=0$, giving $\mathbb E[x^3]=\tfrac32\,\mathbb
E[x^2]-\tfrac34\,\mathbb E[x]+\tfrac18$. A parallel computation gives $\mathbb E
[x^2]=\tfrac38$ (and $\mathbb E[x]=\tfrac12$), so $\mathbb E[x^3]=\tfrac32\cdot\
tfrac38-\tfrac34\cdot\tfrac12+\tfrac18=\tfrac9{16}-\tfrac38+\tfrac18=\tfrac9{16}
-\tfrac14=\tfrac5{16}$. ✓ (The variance $\tfrac38-\tfrac14=\tfrac18$ also matche
s the known value for the Cantor distribution.)

$$\boxed{\int_{[0,1]} x^3\,d\mu_F = \frac{5}{16}}.$$
