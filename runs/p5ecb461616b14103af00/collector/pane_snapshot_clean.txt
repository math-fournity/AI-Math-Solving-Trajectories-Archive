## Solution

We work in $G = SL_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$ where $p$ is an odd prime. Let $A \in G$ wit
h characteristic polynomial $P_A(x) = x^2 - \text{tr}(A)\,x + 1$ irreducible ove
r $\mathbb{F}_p$.

### Step 1: Eigenvalues lie in $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}\setminus\mathbb{F}_p$

Since $P_A$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_p$, its roots $\lambda, \lambda'$ li
e in $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}\setminus\mathbb{F}_p$. By Vieta, $\lambda\lambda' = \det(
A) = 1$, so $\lambda' = \lambda^{-1}$. The Frobenius automorphism sends $\lambda
 \mapsto \lambda^p$, so $\lambda^p = \lambda^{-1}$, giving:

$$\lambda^{p+1} = 1.$$

### Step 2: The eigenvalue belongs to the norm-one subgroup

The norm map $N: \mathbb{F}_{p^2}^* \to \mathbb{F}_p^*$, $N(\alpha) = \alpha^{p+
1}$, has kernel $\ker N = \{\alpha \in \mathbb{F}_{p^2}^* : \alpha^{p+1}=1\}$ of
 order $p+1$. Since $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}^*$ is cyclic (order $p^2-1$), its subgroup
 $\ker N$ is **cyclic of order $p+1$**.

Our eigenvalue $\lambda$ satisfies $\lambda^{p+1}=1$, so $\lambda \in \ker N$.

### Step 3: Order of $A$ equals order of $\lambda$

Over $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$, the matrix $A$ is conjugate to $\operatorname{diag}(\la
mbda, \lambda^{-1})$, so $\operatorname{ord}(A) = \operatorname{ord}(\lambda)$.

### Step 4: The maximum $p+1$ is achieved

Let $\lambda$ be a **generator** of $\ker N$ (order $p+1$). Since $p+1 > 2$ for
$p \geq 3$, we have $\lambda \neq \pm 1$, so $\lambda \notin \mathbb{F}_p$. Its
minimal polynomial over $\mathbb{F}_p$ is:

$$(x-\lambda)(x-\lambda^p) = x^2 - \underbrace{(\lambda+\lambda^p)}_{t\,\in\,\ma
thbb{F}_p}\,x + \underbrace{\lambda^{p+1}}_{=\,1},$$

which is $x^2 - tx + 1$, irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_p$. The companion matrix

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & t \end{pmatrix} \in SL_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$$

has this characteristic polynomial (irreducible, as required) and $\operatorname
{ord}(A) = \operatorname{ord}(\lambda) = p+1$.

### Conclusion

The maximum order of an element in $SL_2(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})$ whose characte
ristic polynomial $x^2 - \text{tr}(A)x + 1$ has no roots in $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb
{Z}$ is:

$$\boxed{p+1}$$
