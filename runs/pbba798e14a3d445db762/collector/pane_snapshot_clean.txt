Looking at this problem, I need to find the number of points on an elliptic curv
e over ℤ/221ℤ given that it has points of order 11 and 7.

## Solution

**Step 1: Chinese Remainder Theorem decomposition.**

Since $221 = 13 \times 17$ with $\gcd(13, 17) = 1$, by the Chinese Remainder The
orem:
$$E(\mathbb{Z}/221\mathbb{Z}) \cong E(\mathbb{F}_{13}) \times E(\mathbb{F}_{17})
$$
so $|E| = |E(\mathbb{F}_{13})| \cdot |E(\mathbb{F}_{17})|$.

**Step 2: Hasse's bound.**

By Hasse's theorem, for an elliptic curve over $\mathbb{F}_p$:
$$||E(\mathbb{F}_p)| - (p+1)| \leq 2\sqrt{p}$$

Applying the Hasse bound to the curve over $\mathbb{Z}/221\mathbb{Z}$ (treating
$n = 221$):
$$||E| - (221+1)| \leq 2\sqrt{221}$$

Computing: $2\sqrt{221} \approx 29.73$, so:
$$|E| \in [222 - 29.73,\; 222 + 29.73] = [192.27,\; 251.73]$$

Since $|E|$ is an integer: $|E| \in \{193, 194, \ldots, 251\}$.

**Step 3: Divisibility constraints from the torsion points.**

- There exists $P \neq \mathcal{O}$ with $11P = \mathcal{O}$, so $P$ has order $
11$ (since $11$ is prime). By Lagrange's theorem, $11 \mid |E|$.
- There exists $Q \neq \mathcal{O}$ with $7Q = \mathcal{O}$, so $Q$ has order $7
$ (since $7$ is prime). By Lagrange's theorem, $7 \mid |E|$.

Since $\gcd(7, 11) = 1$, we need $77 \mid |E|$.

**Step 4: Finding the unique value.**

The multiples of $77$ in the range $[193, 251]$ are:

| Multiple | Value | In range? |
|----------|-------|-----------|
| $1 \times 77$ | $77$ | No ($< 193$) |
| $2 \times 77$ | $154$ | No ($< 193$) |
| $3 \times 77$ | $231$ | **Yes** ($193 \leq 231 \leq 251$) |
| $4 \times 77$ | $308$ | No ($> 251$) |

The only multiple of $77$ in the Hasse interval is $|E| = 231$.

**Step 5: Verification that $231$ is achievable.**

We can verify $231 = 21 \times 11$ or $231 = 11 \times 21$, where:
- $|E(\mathbb{F}_{13})| = 21$ gives $a_{13} = 14 - 21 = -7$, with $|-7| = 7 \leq
 2\sqrt{13} \approx 7.21$ ✓
- $|E(\mathbb{F}_{17})| = 11$ gives $a_{17} = 18 - 11 = 7$, with $|7| = 7 \leq 2
\sqrt{17} \approx 8.25$ ✓

Both traces lie within the Hasse bounds, and by Waterhouse's theorem, these grou
p orders are realized by elliptic curves over the respective prime fields. By CR
T, choosing $b, c \pmod{221}$ that reduce to appropriate values mod $13$ and mod
 $17$ yields a curve with $|E(\mathbb{Z}/221\mathbb{Z})| = 231$.

The group $E(\mathbb{Z}/221\mathbb{Z}) \cong E(\mathbb{F}_{13}) \times E(\mathbb
{F}_{17})$ has order $231 = 3 \times 7 \times 11$, which is divisible by both $7
$ and $11$, confirming the existence of points $P$ (order $11$) and $Q$ (order $
7$).

## Answer

The number of points on $E$ is $\boxed{231}$.
