## Solution

The fat Cantor set $C$ is constructed by iteratively removing the middle $1/4$ o
f each remaining interval. At each stage every interval of length $L$ is replace
d by two intervals of length $\frac{3}{8}L$ (since $\frac{1-1/4}{2}=\frac{3}{8}$
): the left piece $(3/8)C$ and the right piece $5/8+(3/8)C$.

The formula
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{2^n} P(t_{n,i})\Bigl(\frac{1}{2^n}\Bigr),\qquad t
_{n,i}\in C_{n,i},$$
assigns **equal weight $1/2^n$ to each of the $2^n$ intervals**, regardless of l
ength. This is precisely integration against the self-similar Cantor measure $\m
u$ that gives weight $1/2$ to the left piece and $1/2$ to the right piece. Since
 the intervals shrink to points as $n\to\infty$, the limit is independent of the
 choice of $t_{n,i}$ and equals
$$\int_C x^2\,d\mu(x).$$

### Computing the second moment

Let $M_k=\int_C x^k\,d\mu(x)$. The self-similarity $C=(3/8)C\;\sqcup\;\bigl(5/8+
(3/8)C\bigr)$ with weights $1/2,1/2$ gives
$$M_k=\tfrac12\Bigl(\tfrac38\Bigr)^k M_k+\tfrac12\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\Bigl
(\tfrac58\Bigr)^{k-j}\Bigl(\tfrac38\Bigr)^j M_j.$$

**$k=0$:** $M_0=1$ (normalization). ✓

**$k=1$:**
$$M_1=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac38 M_1+\tfrac12\Bigl(\tfrac58 M_0+\tfrac38 M_1\Bigr)=\t
frac{3}{8}M_1+\tfrac{5}{16}.$$
Hence $\frac{5}{8}M_1=\frac{5}{16}$, so $M_1=\frac12$ (consistent with symmetry
about $1/2$). ✓

**$k=2$:**
$$M_2=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac{9}{64}M_2+\tfrac12\Bigl[\Bigl(\tfrac58\Bigr)^2 M_0+2\B
igl(\tfrac58\Bigr)\Bigl(\tfrac38\Bigr)M_1+\Bigl(\tfrac38\Bigr)^2 M_2\Bigr].$$
Substituting $M_0=1$, $M_1=1/2$:
$$M_2=\tfrac{9}{128}M_2+\tfrac12\Bigl[\tfrac{25}{64}+\tfrac{15}{64}+\tfrac{9}{64
}M_2\Bigr]=\tfrac{9}{128}M_2+\tfrac{20}{64}+\tfrac{9}{128}M_2=\tfrac{18}{128}M_2
+\tfrac{20}{64}.$$
Therefore
$$\Bigl(1-\tfrac{18}{128}\Bigr)M_2=\tfrac{20}{64}\;\Longrightarrow\;\tfrac{110}{
128}M_2=\tfrac{20}{64},$$
$$M_2=\frac{20}{64}\cdot\frac{128}{110}=\frac{40}{110}=\boxed{\frac{4}{11}}.$$

### Conclusion

The average value of $P(x)=x^2$ on the fat Cantor set $C$ (under the equal-weigh
t/Cantor-measure averaging specified by the given formula) is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{4}{11}}.$$

Between the two candidates $\frac{4}{11}$ and $\frac{38}{105}$, the correct valu
e is **$\frac{4}{11}$**.
