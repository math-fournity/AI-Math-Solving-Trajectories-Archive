## Classification of Isometries of $\mathbb{H}^n$ by Fixed Points

An isometry $g \in \mathrm{Isom}(\mathbb{H}^n)$, $g \neq \mathrm{id}$, acts on t
he compactified space $\overline{\mathbb{H}^n} = \mathbb{H}^n \cup \partial\math
bb{H}^n$ (which is homeomorphic to a closed ball). Since $\overline{\mathbb{H}^n
}$ is compact, the fixed-point set $\mathrm{Fix}(g) \subset \overline{\mathbb{H}
^n}$ is a closed, $g$-invariant subset. The classification is built on *where* t
his fixed set meets the interior $\mathbb{H}^n$ versus the boundary at infinity
$\partial\mathbb{H}^n$.

### The three types

1. **Elliptic.** $g$ fixes at least one point *inside* $\mathbb{H}^n$:
   $$\mathrm{Fix}(g) \cap \mathbb{H}^n \neq \varnothing.$$
   In the ball model, after conjugating the fixed point to the origin, $g$ becom
es an element of the stabilizer $O(n)$, i.e. a Euclidean orthogonal transformati
on. The fixed set in $\overline{\mathbb{H}^n}$ is a totally geodesic subspace th
rough the fixed point (a point, a geodesic, …, up to all of $\mathbb{H}^n$ for t
he identity). Elliptic isometries have no fixed points on $\partial\mathbb{H}^n$
 that are "attracting/repelling"; every boundary fixed point is neutral.

2. **Parabolic.** $g$ has no fixed point in $\mathbb{H}^n$ and exactly one fixed
 point on the boundary:
   $$\mathrm{Fix}(g) \cap \mathbb{H}^n = \varnothing, \qquad \mathrm{Fix}(g) \ca
p \partial\mathbb{H}^n = \{\xi\}.$$
   In the upper half-space model with $\xi = \infty$, a parabolic isometry is a
non-trivial Euclidean translation $x \mapsto x + v$ (possibly composed with an o
rthogonal transformation fixing the $v$-direction). It preserves every horospher
e centered at $\xi$ and acts on each as a Euclidean isometry. There is no geodes
ic in $\mathbb{H}^n$ invariant as a *set* with $g$ acting as a translation along
 it; instead $g$ moves every point a uniformly bounded distance (the translation
 length $\inf_{p} d(p, gp) = 0$ is not attained).

3. **Hyperbolic (loxodromic).** $g$ has no fixed point in $\mathbb{H}^n$ and exa
ctly two fixed points on the boundary:
   $$\mathrm{Fix}(g) \cap \mathbb{H}^n = \varnothing, \qquad \mathrm{Fix}(g) \ca
p \partial\mathbb{H}^n = \{\xi_+, \xi_-\}.$$
   The two boundary points are joined by a unique geodesic $\gamma \subset \math
bb{H}^n$, the *axis* of $g$, which is invariant as a set. On $\gamma$, $g$ acts
as a translation by a positive length $\ell > 0$ (the *translation length*, atta
ined exactly on $\gamma$). In the upper half-space model with $\xi_\pm = 0, \inf
ty$, $g$ is a dilation $x \mapsto \lambda x$ ($\lambda \neq 1$) possibly compose
d with an orthogonal transformation fixing the vertical axis. Points of $\mathbb
{H}^n$ flow toward the attracting fixed point $\xi_+$ and away from the repellin
g fixed point $\xi_-$ under iteration.

### Why the list is exhaustive

Let $g \neq \mathrm{id}$. Consider the displacement function $\delta(p) = d(p, g
p)$ on $\overline{\mathbb{H}^n}$ (extended to the boundary by limiting Busemann
behavior).

- If $\delta$ attains the value $0$ somewhere in $\mathbb{H}^n$, then $g$ fixes
an interior point: **elliptic**.
- Otherwise $g$ has no interior fixed point. The boundary action of $g$ on $\par
tial\mathbb{H}^n \cong S^{n-1}$ (a Möbius/conformal transformation) has at least
 one fixed point by compactness of $S^{n-1}$ (it extends continuously to the com
pact boundary). A non-identity Möbius transformation of $S^{n-1}$ has either exa
ctly one or exactly two fixed points:
  - exactly one boundary fixed point $\Rightarrow$ **parabolic**;
  - exactly two boundary fixed points $\Rightarrow$ **hyperbolic**.

These cases are mutually exclusive and cover every non-trivial isometry, giving
the three-type classification.
