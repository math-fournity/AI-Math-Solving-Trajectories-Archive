## Proof

We compute the Lebesgue integral $\int_{\bar{C}} x^2\,d\mu$, where $\bar{C} = [0
,1]\setminus C$ and $C$ is the Cantor set.

**Step 1: The Cantor set has Lebesgue measure zero.**

The Cantor set $C$ is constructed by iteratively removing open middle thirds fro
m $[0,1]$. At stage $n\geq 0$, we remove $2^n$ open intervals, each of length $3
^{-(n+1)}$. The total length removed is

$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} 2^n \cdot 3^{-(n+1)} = \frac{1}{3}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left
(\frac{2}{3}\right)^n = \frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\frac{2}{3}} = \frac{1}{3}\cd
ot 3 = 1.$$

Since the total measure of all removed intervals equals $1 = m([0,1])$, the rema
ining set $C$ has Lebesgue measure

$$m(C) = m([0,1]) - 1 = 0.$$

**Step 2: Reduce the integral over $\bar{C}$ to the integral over $[0,1]$.**

Since $[0,1] = C \cup \bar{C}$ is a disjoint union and $f(x)=x^2$ is nonnegative
 and measurable, by additivity of the Lebesgue integral over disjoint measurable
 sets,

$$\int_{[0,1]} x^2\,d\mu = \int_{C} x^2\,d\mu + \int_{\bar{C}} x^2\,d\mu.$$

Because $m(C)=0$, any integral over $C$ of an integrable function vanishes. Inde
ed, $0 \leq x^2 \leq 1$ on $C\subseteq[0,1]$, so $x^2$ is bounded and integrable
 over $C$, and

$$0 \leq \int_{C} x^2\,d\mu \leq \int_{C} 1\,d\mu = m(C) = 0,$$

hence $\int_{C} x^2\,d\mu = 0$.

**Step 3: Evaluate the integral over $[0,1]$.**

The function $x^2$ is continuous, hence Riemann integrable on $[0,1]$, and its L
ebesgue integral coincides with its Riemann integral:

$$\int_{[0,1]} x^2\,d\mu = \int_{0}^{1} x^2\,dx = \left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{0}
^{1} = \frac{1}{3}.$$

**Step 4: Conclude.**

Combining Steps 2 and 3:

$$\int_{\bar{C}} x^2\,d\mu = \int_{[0,1]} x^2\,d\mu - \int_{C} x^2\,d\mu = \frac
{1}{3} - 0 = \frac{1}{3}.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{\bar{C}} x^2\,d\mu = \frac{1}{3}}$$
