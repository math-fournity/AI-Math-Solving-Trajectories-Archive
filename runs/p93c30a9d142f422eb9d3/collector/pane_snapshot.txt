## Solution

Let $\angle A = \angle D = \alpha$. By the Law of Cosines applied to both triang
les (using the sides of length 13 opposite the angle $\alpha$):

**Triangle $ABC$:** $BC^2 = AB^2 + AC^2 - 2\cdot AB\cdot AC\cos\alpha$

$$13^2 = 20^2 + AC^2 - 2\cdot 20\cdot AC\cos\alpha$$
$$AC^2 - 40\,AC\cos\alpha + 231 = 0$$

**Triangle $DEF$:** $EF^2 = DE^2 + DF^2 - 2\cdot DE\cdot DF\cos\alpha$

$$DF^2 - 40\,DF\cos\alpha + 231 = 0$$

Thus $AC$ and $DF$ are both roots of the same quadratic $x^2 - 40x\cos\alpha + 2
31 = 0$. By Vieta's formulas:

$$AC + DF = 40\cos\alpha, \qquad AC\cdot DF = 231$$

Given $AC - DF = 10$, we get $AC = 20\cos\alpha + 5$ and $DF = 20\cos\alpha - 5$
. Substituting into the product:

$$(20\cos\alpha)^2 - 25 = 231$$
$$400\cos^2\alpha = 256$$
$$\cos^2\alpha = \frac{16}{25}, \qquad \cos\alpha = \frac{4}{5}$$

(taking the positive root since $\alpha$ is a triangle angle). Hence $\sin\alpha
 = \frac{3}{5}$, and

$$AC = 20\cdot\tfrac{4}{5} + 5 = 21, \qquad DF = 11 \quad (\text{check: } 21\cdo
t 11 = 231\checkmark,\; 21-11=10\checkmark)$$

The area of $\triangle ABC$ is:

$$\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot AB\cdot AC\cdot\sin\alpha = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot
20\cdot 21\cdot\tfrac{3}{5} = 10\cdot 21\cdot\tfrac{3}{5} = 126$$
