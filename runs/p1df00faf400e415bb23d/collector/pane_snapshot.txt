Looking at this problem, I need to find the distance KY where K is the circumcen
ter of triangle ABG, and Y is the intersection of line KJ with the line from I (
circumcenter of BCG) through the midpoint of BC.

## Proof

**Setup.** Place the centroid $G$ at the origin, and $A = (6, 0)$ (since $AG = 6
$). Since $G$ is the centroid, $A + B + C = 0$, so $C = -A - B = (-6 - b_1, -b_2
)$ where $B = (b_1, b_2)$.

**Finding K and J.** Both circumcenters $K$ (of $\triangle ABG$) and $J$ (of $\t
riangle ACG$) are equidistant from $G = (0,0)$ and $A = (6,0)$, so both lie on t
he perpendicular bisector of $GA$, which is the line $x = 3$.

- **Circle K** (through $A, B, G$), radius $5$: $K = (3, k_2)$ with $|K|^2 = 9 +
 k_2^2 = 25$, so $k_2 = \pm 4$. Choose $k_2 = 4$ (by reflection symmetry).

- **Circle J** (through $A, C, G$), radius $8$: $J = (3, j_2)$ with $|J|^2 = 9 +
 j_2^2 = 64$, so $j_2 = \pm\sqrt{55}$.

**Key observation:** Line $KJ$ is the vertical line $x = 3$.

**Determining the sign of $j_2$.** From $B$ lying on circle $K$: $(b_1 - 3)^2 +
(b_2 - 4)^2 = 25$, which expands to:
$$b_1^2 + b_2^2 = 6b_1 + 8b_2 \quad \text{...(1)}$$

From $C$ lying on circle $J$: $(9 + b_1)^2 + (b_2 + j_2)^2 = 64$, which (using (
1)) simplifies to:
$$24b_1 + (8 + 2j_2)\, b_2 = -72 \quad \text{...(3)}$$

If $j_2 = +\sqrt{55}$ (same side as $K$), substituting into (1) and (3) yields a
 quadratic in $b_2$ with **negative discriminant** — no real solution. So we mus
t have $j_2 = -\sqrt{55}$ (opposite side from $K$).

**Finding I (circumcenter of $\triangle BCG$).** The midpoint of $BC$ is $M = \f
rac{B+C}{2} = \frac{-A}{2} = (-3, 0)$. Since $I$ is equidistant from $B$ and $C$
, it lies on the perpendicular bisector of $BC$ through $M$. Since $I$ is also e
quidistant from $G$ and $B$, solving the perpendicular bisector equations gives:

$$I = \left(\frac{-9 - 6b_1 - 4b_2}{3},\; \frac{(6b_1 + 4b_2)(b_1 + 3)}{3\,b_2}\
right)$$

**Finding Y.** The line from $I$ through $M = (-3, 0)$ has direction $M - I = \l
eft(\frac{6b_1 + 4b_2}{3},\; -I_y\right)$. Setting $D = 6b_1 + 4b_2$, the parame
tric form $I + \lambda(M - I)$ reaches $x = 3$ when:

$$\lambda = \frac{3 - I_x}{D/3} = \frac{18 + D}{D}$$

The $y$-coordinate of $Y$ is:
$$Y_y = I_y(1 - \lambda) = I_y \cdot \frac{-18}{D} = \frac{-18 \cdot D(b_1+3)/(3
b_2)}{D} = \frac{-6(b_1 + 3)}{b_2}$$

**Computing KY.** With $K = (3, 4)$:
$$KY = |Y_y - 4| = \left|\frac{-6(b_1+3)}{b_2} - 4\right| = \left|\frac{-(6b_1 +
 4b_2 + 18)}{b_2}\right| = \left|\frac{D + 18}{b_2}\right|$$

From equation (3) with $j_2 = -\sqrt{55}$: $24b_1 + (8 - 2\sqrt{55})b_2 = -72$,
so:
$$6b_1 = \frac{-72 - (8 - 2\sqrt{55})b_2}{4} = -18 - 2b_2 + \frac{\sqrt{55}}{2}\
,b_2$$
$$D = 6b_1 + 4b_2 = -18 + 2b_2 + \frac{\sqrt{55}}{2}\,b_2 = -18 + \frac{4 + \sqr
t{55}}{2}\,b_2$$
$$D + 18 = \frac{4 + \sqrt{55}}{2}\,b_2$$

Therefore:
$$KY = \left|\frac{(4 + \sqrt{55})\,b_2 / 2}{b_2}\right| = \frac{4 + \sqrt{55}}{
2}$$

**Verification.** Both valid triangle configurations (there are two, from the qu
adratic) yield $b_2 > 0$ and give $KY \approx 5.708 = (4 + \sqrt{55})/2$, confir
ming the result is independent of which triangle is chosen.

$$\boxed{KY = \dfrac{4 + \sqrt{55}}{2}}$$
