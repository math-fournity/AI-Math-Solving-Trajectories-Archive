## Proof

**Yes, such a function exists.** We construct one explicitly.

**Construction.** For each integer $n \geq 1$, define a triangular "bump" $T_n$
centered at $x = n$ with height $\tfrac{1}{2}$ and base width $\tfrac{1}{n^2}$:

$$
T_n(x) = \begin{cases}
\tfrac{1}{2}\Big(1 - n^2|x - n|\Big), & |x - n| \leq \tfrac{1}{2n^2},\\[4pt]
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

Define $f(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} T_n(x)$.

**The bumps are disjoint.** The support of $T_n$ is $\big[n - \tfrac{1}{2n^2},\;
 n + \tfrac{1}{2n^2}\big]$. For $n \geq 2$, the half-width $\tfrac{1}{2n^2} \leq
 \tfrac{1}{8} < \tfrac{1}{2}$, so consecutive supports (centered at integers dis
tance $1$ apart) do not overlap. Hence at every $x$, at most one term in the sum
 is nonzero.

**Continuity and range.** Each $T_n$ is continuous, the supports are disjoint an
d locally finite (any bounded interval meets only finitely many), so $f$ is cont
inuous. Since every $T_n(x) \leq \tfrac{1}{2}$ and the supports are disjoint, $f
(x) \leq \tfrac{1}{2} < 1$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}_+$. Clearly $f \geq 0$.

**Integrability.** The area of a triangle with base $\tfrac{1}{n^2}$ and height
$\tfrac{1}{2}$ is

$$
\int_0^\infty T_n(x)\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\tfrac{1}{n^2}\cdot\tfrac{1}{2} = \t
frac{1}{4n^2}.
$$

By disjointness of supports and Tonelli's theorem (all terms non-negative),

$$
\int_0^\infty f(x)\,dx = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_0^\infty T_n(x)\,dx = \sum_{n=1
}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4n^2} = \frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{\pi^2}{6} = \frac{\pi^2}{24} <
\infty.
$$

**Divergent subseries.** Take the strictly increasing sequence $q_n = n+1$ for $
n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$ (so $q_0 = 1, q_1 = 2, \dots$). Each $q_n$ is the center of
bump $T_{n+1}$, where $T_{n+1}(q_n) = \tfrac{1}{2}$. Therefore

$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} f(q_n) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \tfrac{1}{2} = \infty.
$$

**Conclusion.** The function $f$ is non-negative, continuous, satisfies $f(x) \l
eq \tfrac{1}{2} < 1$ everywhere, has finite integral $\int_0^\infty f\,dx = \tfr
ac{\pi^2}{24}$, yet $\sum_{n=0}^\infty f(q_n) = \infty$ along the strictly incre
asing sequence $q_n = n+1$. $\blacksquare$
