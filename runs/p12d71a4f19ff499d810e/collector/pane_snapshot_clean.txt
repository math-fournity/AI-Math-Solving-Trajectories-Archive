**Answer: Yes, such a set exists.**

**Construction.** Enumumerate the rationals in $[0,1]$ as $\{q_1, q_2, q_3, \dot
s\}$. Fix $\varepsilon = \tfrac{1}{2}$. For each $i \geq 1$, define the open int
erval
$$I_i = \left(q_i - \frac{\varepsilon}{2^{i+1}},\; q_i + \frac{\varepsilon}{2^{i
+1}}\right),$$
and set
$$U = \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} I_i, \qquad S = U \cap (0,1).$$

**$S$ is open.** Each $I_i$ is open in $\mathbb{R}$, so $U$ is open, and $S = U
\cap (0,1)$ is the intersection of two open sets, hence open in $\mathbb{R}$. Cl
early $S \subseteq [0,1]$.

**$S$ is dense in $[0,1]$.** Every rational $q_i \in (0,1)$ belongs to $I_i \sub
seteq U$, and since $q_i \in (0,1)$, we have $q_i \in S$. Thus $S$ contains $\ma
thbb{Q} \cap (0,1)$. The set $\mathbb{Q} \cap (0,1)$ is dense in $[0,1]$ (its cl
osure in $\mathbb{R}$ is $[0,1]$, since $0$ and $1$ are limit points of rational
s in $(0,1)$). Therefore
$$[0,1] = \overline{\mathbb{Q} \cap (0,1)} \subseteq \overline{S},$$
so $\overline{S} \supseteq [0,1]$, i.e., $S$ is dense in $[0,1]$.

**$m(S) < 1$.** By countable subadditivity of Lebesgue measure,
$$m(U) \leq \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} m(I_i) = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} 2 \cdot \frac{\vare
psilon}{2^{i+1}} = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \frac{\varepsilon}{2^{i}} = \varepsilon =
 \frac{1}{2}.$$
Since $S \subseteq U$, we have $m(S) \leq m(U) \leq \tfrac{1}{2} < 1$.

**Conclusion.** $S$ is an open subset of $[0,1]$, dense in $[0,1]$, with $m(S) \
leq \tfrac{1}{2} < 1$. Hence such a set exists. $\blacksquare$
