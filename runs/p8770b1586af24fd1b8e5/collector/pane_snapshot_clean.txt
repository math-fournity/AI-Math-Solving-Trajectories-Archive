## Proof

Let $S_n = \sum_{t=1}^{n} a_t$ and $f(a_1, a_2, \dots) = \liminf_{n} S_n$.

**Step 1: Reduce liminf to a limit.** Since $a_t \geq 0$ for all $t$, the sequen
ce $(S_n)$ is non-decreasing. For any non-decreasing sequence, the liminf coinci
des with the (extended) limit:

$$f(a_1, a_2, \dots) = \liminf_{n} S_n = \lim_{n} S_n = \sum_{t=1}^{\infty} a_t
\;\in\; [0, +\infty].$$

**Step 2: Compute the partial derivative at a point where $f$ is finite.** Fix a
 sequence $a = (a_1, a_2, \dots)$ with $a_t \geq 0$ and $\sum_{t=1}^{\infty} a_t
 < +\infty$. Fix an index $t \in \mathbb{N}$ and consider perturbing only $a_t$
by a small $\varepsilon$ (with $\varepsilon > 0$, or $\varepsilon < 0$ when $a_t
 > 0$, so that $a_t + \varepsilon \geq 0$). For every $n \geq t$,

$$S_n(a + \varepsilon\, e_t) = \sum_{s=1}^{n} a_s + \varepsilon = S_n(a) + \vare
psilon,$$

while for $n < t$, $S_n$ is unchanged. Taking the limit in $n$ (which exists by
monotonicity):

$$f(a + \varepsilon\, e_t) = \lim_{n} S_n(a + \varepsilon\, e_t) = \lim_{n} S_n(
a) + \varepsilon = f(a) + \varepsilon.$$

Therefore the difference quotient is identically $1$:

$$\frac{f(a + \varepsilon\, e_t) - f(a)}{\varepsilon} = 1 \quad \text{for all ad
missible } \varepsilon \neq 0,$$

and hence

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial a_t}(a) = \lim_{\varepsilon \to 0} \frac{f(a + \var
epsilon\, e_t) - f(a)}{\varepsilon} = 1.$$

This holds for **every** $t \in \mathbb{N}$: each $a_t$ enters every partial sum
 $S_n$ with $n \geq t$ linearly with coefficient $1$, and the limit preserves th
is coefficient.

**Step 3: Address the divergent case.** If $\sum_{t=1}^{\infty} a_t = +\infty$,
then $f(a) = +\infty$. Any finite perturbation of a single coordinate still leav
es $f = +\infty$, so the difference quotient becomes $+\infty - (+\infty)$, whic
h is undefined. Thus the partial derivative is not defined at such points in the
 classical sense.

**Conclusion.** At every point $a \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathbb{N}}$ where $f
$ is finite (i.e., where $\sum a_t < \infty$), the partial derivative exists and

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial a_t}\!\left(\liminf_{n}\sum_{s=1}^{n} a_s\right) = 1
\quad \text{for every } t \in \mathbb{N}.$$

The key reason is that non-negativity makes $(S_n)$ monotone, so $\liminf = \lim
 = \sum_{t=1}^{\infty} a_t$, and each variable $a_t$ contributes linearly with c
oefficient $1$ to this sum. The statement is **true** (with the natural caveat t
hat the derivative is defined only where $f$ is finite).
