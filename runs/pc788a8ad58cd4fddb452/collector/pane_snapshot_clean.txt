## Proof

Let $\alpha=\angle A$, $\beta=\angle B$, $\gamma=\angle C$. Place the circumcirc
le $\omega$ as the unit circle centered at the origin. We use the standard facts
:

- The internal bisector of $\angle B$ meets $\omega$ again at $X$, the midpoint
of arc $AC$ not containing $B$.
- The internal bisector of $\angle C$ meets $\omega$ again at $Y$, the midpoint
of arc $AB$ not containing $C$.
- $S$ is the midpoint of arc $CAB$, i.e. the midpoint of the major arc $BC$ cont
aining $A$; equivalently $S$ is the antipode of the midpoint $M_A$ of the minor
arc $BC$.

**Parametrization.** Place $M_A$ at angle $0$, so its antipode $S$ is at angle $
\pi$, i.e. $S=(-1,0)$. Since the minor arc $BC$ has measure $2\alpha$, set
$$B=(\cos\alpha,-\sin\alpha)\;(\text{angle }-\alpha),\qquad C=(\cos\alpha,\sin\a
lpha)\;(\text{angle }+\alpha).$$
From $\operatorname{arc}CA\text{ (not containing }B)=2\beta$ and $\operatorname{
arc}AB\text{ (not containing }C)=2\gamma$, point $A$ lies at angle $\alpha+2\bet
a$:
$$A=(\cos(\alpha+2\beta),\,\sin(\alpha+2\beta)).$$
Consequently
$$X=(\cos(\alpha+\beta),\,\sin(\alpha+\beta)),\qquad Y=(\cos(\pi+\beta),\,\sin(\
pi+\beta))=(-\cos\beta,\,-\sin\beta).$$

**Finding $K$.** Write $K=C+t(X-C)$ on line $CX$. The condition $\angle KAC=90^\
circ$ gives $(K-A)\cdot(C-A)=0$, i.e.
$$|C-A|^2+t\,(X-C)\cdot(C-A)=0.$$
Using the unit-circle identity $(P(\theta_1)-P(\theta_2))\cdot(P(\theta_3)-P(\th
eta_4))=\cos(\theta_1-\theta_3)-\cos(\theta_1-\theta_4)-\cos(\theta_2-\theta_3)+
\cos(\theta_2-\theta_4)$:
$$|C-A|^2=2-2\cos 2\beta=4\sin^2\beta,$$
$$(X-C)\cdot(C-A)=\cos\beta-\cos(-\beta)-\cos 0+\cos(-2\beta)=\cos 2\beta-1=-2\s
in^2\beta.$$
Hence $t=-4\sin^2\beta/(-2\sin^2\beta)=2$, so
$$K=2X-C=\bigl(2\cos(\alpha+\beta)-\cos\alpha,\;2\sin(\alpha+\beta)-\sin\alpha\b
igr).$$

**Finding $L$.** Similarly $L=B+s(Y-B)$ with $(L-A)\cdot(B-A)=0$:
$$|B-A|^2=2-2\cos 2(\alpha+\beta)=4\sin^2\gamma,$$
$$(Y-B)\cdot(B-A)=\cos\gamma-\cos\gamma-1+\cos 2\gamma=-2\sin^2\gamma,$$
so $s=2$ and
$$L=2Y-B=\bigl(-2\cos\beta-\cos\alpha,\;-2\sin\beta+\sin\alpha\bigr).$$

**Comparing $SK$ and $SL$.** With $S=(-1,0)$, put $u=\alpha+\beta$:
\begin{align*}
|K-S|^2&=(2\cos u-\cos\alpha+1)^2+(2\sin u-\sin\alpha)^2\\
&=(2\cos u-\cos\alpha)^2+(2\sin u-\sin\alpha)^2+2(2\cos u-\cos\alpha)+1\\
&=\bigl(4-4\cos(u-\alpha)+1\bigr)+4\cos u-2\cos\alpha+1\\
&=6+4\cos(\alpha+\beta)-4\cos\beta-2\cos\alpha.
\end{align*}
\begin{align*}
|L-S|^2&=(1-2\cos\beta-\cos\alpha)^2+(\sin\alpha-2\sin\beta)^2\\
&=(2\cos\beta+\cos\alpha)^2+(2\sin\beta-\sin\alpha)^2-2(2\cos\beta+\cos\alpha)+1
\\
&=\bigl(4+4\cos(\alpha+\beta)+1\bigr)-4\cos\beta-2\cos\alpha+1\\
&=6+4\cos(\alpha+\beta)-4\cos\beta-2\cos\alpha.
\end{align*}
Thus $|K-S|^2=|L-S|^2$, and therefore $SK=SL$. $\blacksquare$
