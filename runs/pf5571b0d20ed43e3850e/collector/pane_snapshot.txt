# Residue Theorem Computation of $\int_{0}^{2\pi} \frac{\cos(3\theta)}{5-3\cos\t
heta}\,d\theta$

**Answer-leak check:** The problem statement contains only the integral to evalu
ate — no solution, hint, or answer is embedded. Proceeding.

## Setup: substitution $z = e^{i\theta}$

With $z = e^{i\theta}$, we have $d\theta = \dfrac{dz}{iz}$, $\cos\theta = \dfrac
{z+z^{-1}}{2}$, and $\cos 3\theta = \dfrac{z^{3}+z^{-3}}{2}$. The unit circle $|
z|=1$ is traversed once counterclockwise. Then

$$
5 - 3\cos\theta = 5 - \tfrac{3}{2}(z+z^{-1}) = \frac{10z - 3z^{2}-3}{2z},
\qquad
\cos 3\theta = \frac{z^{6}+1}{2z^{3}}.
$$

The integral becomes

$$
I = \oint_{|z|=1} \frac{z^{6}+1}{2z^{3}}\cdot\frac{2z}{10z-3z^{2}-3}\cdot\frac{d
z}{iz}
= \frac{1}{i}\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{z^{6}+1}{z^{3}(10z-3z^{2}-3)}\,dz.
$$

Factor the quadratic: $3z^{2}-10z+3 = 3(z-3)(z-\tfrac13)$, so $10z-3z^{2}-3 = -3
(z-3)(z-\tfrac13)$. Hence

$$
I = \frac{-1}{3i}\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{z^{6}+1}{z^{3}(z-3)(z-\tfrac13)}\,dz.
$$

## Locating the poles

Set $f(z) = \dfrac{z^{6}+1}{z^{3}(z-3)(z-\tfrac13)}$. The poles are:
- $z = 0$: order $3$, **inside** $|z|=1$.
- $z = \tfrac13$: simple, **inside** $|z|=1$.
- $z = 3$: simple, **outside** $|z|=1$ (excluded).

By the residue theorem,

$$
I = \frac{-1}{3i}\cdot 2\pi i\Big[\operatorname{Res}(f,0)+\operatorname{Res}\!\b
ig(f,\tfrac13\big)\Big]
= -\frac{2\pi}{3}\Big[\operatorname{Res}(f,0)+\operatorname{Res}\!\big(f,\tfrac1
3\big)\Big].
$$

## Residue at $z = \tfrac13$ (simple pole)

$$
\operatorname{Res}\!\big(f,\tfrac13\big)
= \lim_{z\to 1/3}\left(z-\tfrac13\right)f(z)
= \frac{(1/3)^{6}+1}{(1/3)^{3}\,(1/3-3)}.
$$

Numerator: $\frac{1}{729}+1 = \frac{730}{729}$. Denominator: $\frac{1}{27}\cdot\
!\left(-\frac{8}{3}\right) = -\frac{8}{81}$. Thus

$$
\operatorname{Res}\!\big(f,\tfrac13\big) = \frac{730/729}{-8/81} = \frac{730}{72
9}\cdot\!\left(-\frac{81}{8}\right) = -\frac{730}{72} = -\frac{365}{36}.
$$

## Residue at $z = 0$ (order $3$)

Write $f(z) = g(z)/z^{3}$ with $g(z) = \dfrac{z^{6}+1}{(z-3)(z-\tfrac13)}$. Then

$$
\operatorname{Res}(f,0) = \frac{g''(0)}{2!}.
$$

Since the $z^{6}$ term in $z^{6}+1$ first contributes at order $z^{6}$ when expa
nded against the analytic part of $1/((z-3)(z-\tfrac13))$, the Taylor coefficien
ts of $g$ up to $z^{2}$ coincide with those of

$$
h(z) = \frac{1}{(z-3)(z-\tfrac13)} = \frac{1}{z^{2}-\tfrac{10}{3}z+1} = \frac{1}
{1-\big(\tfrac{10}{3}z - z^{2}\big)}.
$$

Let $u = \tfrac{10}{3}z - z^{2}$. Then $h = 1 + u + u^{2} + \cdots$, with $u^{2}
 = \tfrac{100}{9}z^{2} + O(z^{3})$, so

$$
h(z) = 1 + \tfrac{10}{3}z + \Big(-1+\tfrac{100}{9}\Big)z^{2} + O(z^{3}) = 1 + \t
frac{10}{3}z + \tfrac{91}{9}z^{2} + O(z^{3}).
$$

Therefore $g''(0) = h''(0) = 2\cdot\tfrac{91}{9} = \tfrac{182}{9}$, and

$$
\operatorname{Res}(f,0) = \frac{182/9}{2} = \frac{91}{9} = \frac{364}{36}.
$$

## Summing the residues

$$
\operatorname{Res}(f,0) + \operatorname{Res}\!\big(f,\tfrac13\big) = \frac{364}{
36} - \frac{365}{36} = -\frac{1}{36}.
$$

## Final value

$$
I = -\frac{2\pi}{3}\cdot\!\left(-\frac{1}{36}\right) = \frac{2\pi}{108} = \boxed
{\dfrac{\pi}{54}}.
$$

## Verification (independent check)

The standard identity $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\cos(n\theta)}{a-b\cos\
theta}\,d\theta = \frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{a^{2}-b^{2}}}\left(\frac{a-\sqrt{a^{2}-b^{2}
}}{b}\right)^{\!n}$ for $a>|b|>0$, with $a=5$, $b=3$, $n=3$, gives $\sqrt{25-9}=
4$ and $\frac{5-4}{3}=\frac13$, so

$$
I = \frac{2\pi}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{27} = \frac{\pi}{54},
$$

confirming the residue-theorem result.
