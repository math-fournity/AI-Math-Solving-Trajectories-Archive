## Proof

example.

\infty}\rho(u)\,du=1$. Define:

$$f(t,T)=-T^2\,\rho'\!\bigl(T(e^{-t}-1)\bigr).$$

Since $\rho$ is $C^\infty$ and $(t,T)\mapsto T(e^{-t}-1)$ is $C^\infty$, the fun
ction $f$ is $C^\infty$ in both variables.

**Computation of $A_n$.** Substituting $s=e^{-t}$ (so $dt=-ds/s$, and $t\in[0,1]
$ maps to $s\in[e^{-1},1]$):

$$\int_0^1 e^{-nt}f(t,T)\,dt=-T^2\int_{e^{-1}}^{1}s^{n-1}\,\rho'\!\bigl(T(s-1)\b
igr)\,ds.$$

Now substitute $u=T(s-1)$, so $s=1+u/T$, $ds=du/T$:

$$=-T\int_{T(e^{-1}-1)}^{0}\!\left(1+\frac{u}{T}\right)^{n-1}\!\rho'(u)\,du.$$

For $T$ sufficiently large, $T(e^{-1}-1)<-1$, so the support of $\rho'$ (contain
ed in $[-1,1]$) lies within the integration range. Thus:

$$=-T\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{u}{T}\right)^{n-1}\!\rho'(u)\,du.$$

Expanding via the binomial theorem (valid for $T$ large enough that $|u/T|<1$ on

$$\left(1+\frac{u}{T}\right)^{n-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}\frac{u^j}{T^j}

Therefore:

$$\int_0^1 e^{-nt}f(t,T)\,dt=-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}\frac{1}{T^{j-1}}\in

- $I_0=\int\rho'(u)\,du=0$ (since $\rho$ is compactly supported).

- For $j\geq 2$: $I_j$ is some finite constant depending on $\rho$.

**Taking the limit $T\to\infty$:**

- The $j=0$ term vanishes identically ($I_0=0$).
- The $j=1$ term gives $-\binom{n-1}{1}\cdot T^{0}\cdot(-1)=(n-1)$.
- For $j\geq 2$, each term is $O(T^{-(j-1)})\to 0$.

Hence:

$$A_n=\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_0^1 e^{-nt}f(t,T)\,dt=n-1.$$

cessarily bounded.
