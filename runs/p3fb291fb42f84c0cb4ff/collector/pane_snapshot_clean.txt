## Solution

We evaluate
$$L = \lim_{|z|\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{C}}|w|\,e^{-|z-w|^2}\,dA(w).$$

**Step 1: Center the Gaussian.** Substitute $u = w - z$ (a translation of $\math
bb{C}$, so $dA$ is preserved):
$$I(z) := \int_{\mathbb{C}}|w|\,e^{-|z-w|^2}\,dA(w) = \int_{\mathbb{C}}|z+u|\,e^
{-|u|^2}\,dA(u).$$

**Step 2: Sandwich via the triangle inequality.** For all $u\in\mathbb{C}$,
$$|z|-|u| \;\le\; |z+u| \;\le\; |z|+|u|.$$
Multiplying by $e^{-|u|^2}\ge 0$ and integrating:
$$\int_{\mathbb{C}}(|z|-|u|)\,e^{-|u|^2}\,dA \;\le\; I(z) \;\le\; \int_{\mathbb{
C}}(|z|+|u|)\,e^{-|u|^2}\,dA.$$

**Step 3: Compute the two Gaussian integrals.** In polar coordinates $u = re^{i\
theta}$, $dA = r\,dr\,d\theta$:

$$\int_{\mathbb{C}}e^{-|u|^2}\,dA = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^\infty e^{-r^2}\,r\,
dr\,d\theta = 2\pi\cdot\tfrac12 = \pi.$$

$$\int_{\mathbb{C}}|u|\,e^{-|u|^2}\,dA = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^\infty r\,e^{-r
^2}\,r\,dr\,d\theta = 2\pi\int_0^\infty r^2 e^{-r^2}\,dr = 2\pi\cdot\frac{\sqrt{
\pi}}{4} = \frac{\pi^{3/2}}{2}.$$

**Step 4: Conclude.** The bounds become
$$|z|\,\pi - \frac{\pi^{3/2}}{2} \;\le\; I(z) \;\le\; |z|\,\pi + \frac{\pi^{3/2}
}{2}.$$

As $|z|\to\infty$, the lower bound $|z|\pi - \tfrac{\pi^{3/2}}{2}\to +\infty$, f
orcing
$$I(z)\to +\infty.$$

**Interpretation.** The kernel $e^{-|z-w|^2}$ concentrates $w$ near $z$ (a Gauss
ian "bump" of fixed width centered at $z$). When $|z|$ is large, every $w$ in th
is bump has $|w|\approx |z|$, so the integral behaves like $|z|\cdot\int e^{-|u|
^2}dA = \pi|z|$, which diverges.

$$\boxed{\lim_{z\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{C}}|w|\,e^{-|z-w|^2}\,dA(w) = +\infty.}$
$
