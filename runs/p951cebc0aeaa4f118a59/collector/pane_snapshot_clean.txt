## Proof

**Setup.** The park $R(\alpha)$ is the convex pentagon with vertices $(0,1-\alph
a),\;(\alpha,0),\;(1,0),\;(1,1),\;(0,1)$. This is the unit square $[0,1]^2$ with
 the bottom-left corner truncated by the line segment from $(0,1-\alpha)$ to $(\
alpha,0)$.

**Equation of the cut line.** The line through $(0,1-\alpha)$ and $(\alpha,0)$ i
s
$$\frac{x}{\alpha} + \frac{y}{1-\alpha} = 1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (1-\al
pha)\,x + \alpha\,y = \alpha(1-\alpha).$$
The pentagon $R(\alpha)$ keeps the side containing $(1,1)$, so
$$R(\alpha) = \bigl\{(x,y)\in[0,1]^2 : (1-\alpha)\,x + \alpha\,y \;\ge\; \alpha(
1-\alpha)\bigr\}.$$

**Universal Zone.** $R = \bigcap_{\alpha\in(0,1)} R(\alpha)$, so $(x,y)\in R$ if
f
$$f(\alpha) \;:=\; (1-\alpha)x + \alpha y - \alpha(1-\alpha) \;\ge\; 0 \quad\tex
t{for all } \alpha\in(0,1).$$

Expanding:
$$f(\alpha) = \alpha^2 + \alpha\,(y - x - 1) + x.$$

This is a convex quadratic in $\alpha$ (leading coefficient $1>0$). Its unconstr
ained minimum is at
$$\alpha^* = \frac{1 + x - y}{2}.$$

**Endpoint check.** $f(0) = x \ge 0$ and $f(1) = y \ge 0$ for all $(x,y)\in[0,1]
^2$. By convexity, if $\alpha^*\notin(0,1)$ then $f\ge 0$ on $[0,1]$ automatical
ly. The cases $\alpha^*\le 0$ (i.e. $y\ge 1+x$) and $\alpha^*\ge 1$ (i.e. $y\le
x-1$) each reduce to a single corner of the square, so for all interior points $
\alpha^*\in(0,1)$.

**Binding constraint.** For $\alpha^*\in(0,1)$, the minimum value is
$$f(\alpha^*) = x - \frac{(y-x-1)^2}{4} = x - \frac{(1+x-y)^2}{4}.$$
Requiring $f(\alpha^*)\ge 0$ gives
$$(1+x-y)^2 \;\le\; 4x.$$

**Simplifying the region.** Taking square roots (with $x\ge 0$):
$$-2\sqrt{x} \;\le\; 1+x-y \;\le\; 2\sqrt{x}.$$

- **Upper bound on $1+x-y$:** $y \ge 1+x-2\sqrt{x} = (1-\sqrt{x})^2$.
- **Lower bound on $1+x-y$:** $y \le 1+x+2\sqrt{x} = (1+\sqrt{x})^2$. Since $(1+
\sqrt{x})^2\ge 1\ge y$ in the unit square, this is automatically satisfied.

Therefore
$$R = \bigl\{(x,y) : 0\le x\le 1,\;(1-\sqrt{x})^2 \le y \le 1\bigr\}.$$

**Verification via envelope.** The boundary of $R$ is the envelope of the family
 of cut lines $(1-\alpha)x+\alpha y = \alpha(1-\alpha)$. Differentiating w.r.t.
$\alpha$ gives $-x+y-(1-2\alpha)=0$, i.e. $\alpha=\alpha^*$, and substituting ba
ck yields $(1+x-y)^2=4x$, confirming the curve $y=(1-\sqrt{x})^2$. It passes thr
ough $(0,1)$ and $(1,0)$, the two fixed corners, as expected.

**Computing the area.**
$$\text{Area}(R) = \int_0^1\Bigl[1-(1-\sqrt{x})^2\Bigr]\,dx = \int_0^1\bigl(2\sq
rt{x}-x\bigr)\,dx.$$
$$= \left[\frac{4}{3}\,x^{3/2}-\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^1 = \frac{4}{3}-\frac{1}{2
}=\frac{8}{6}-\frac{3}{6}=\frac{5}{6}.$$

**Spot check.** At $(x,y)=(\tfrac14,\tfrac14)$: $(1-\sqrt{\tfrac14})^2=(1-\tfrac
12)^2=\tfrac14=y$, on the boundary. Indeed $f(\alpha)=\alpha^2-\tfrac34\alpha+\t
frac14$ has discriminant $\tfrac{9}{16}-1<0$, so $f>0$ for all $\alpha$. ✓ At $(
\tfrac14,\tfrac1{10})$: below the curve, and $f(\alpha)=\alpha^2-1.15\alpha+0.25
$ has positive discriminant with roots in $(0,1)$, so it is excluded. ✓

**Answer.** The area of the Universal Zone $R$ is $\boxed{\dfrac{5}{6}}$.
