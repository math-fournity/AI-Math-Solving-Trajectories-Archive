## Proof

**Notation.** Let $G=(V,E)$ be the graph, $|V|=2009$. The condition states: for
any distinct $u,v\in V$, $|N(u)\cap N(v)|=1$, where $N(x)$ denotes the open neig
hborhood of $x$.

### Step 1: Adjacency Matrix Equation

Let $A$ be the adjacency matrix, $D=\operatorname{diag}(\deg(v_1),\dots,\deg(v_n
))$, $J$ the all-ones matrix. Then:
$$(A^2)_{ij}=\begin{cases}\deg(v_i) & i=j\\ 1 & i\neq j\end{cases}$$
so $A^2 = J - I + D$.

### Step 2: The Regular Case is Impossible for $n=2009$

Suppose $G$ is $k$-regular. Then $D=kI$ and $A^2 = J+(k-1)I$. The eigenvalues of
 $A^2$ are $n+k-1$ (once, eigenvector $\mathbf{1}$) and $k-1$ (with multiplicity
 $n-1$). Since $A\mathbf{1}=k\mathbf{1}$, we get $k^2=n+k-1$, i.e., $n=k^2-k+1$.

The remaining $n-1$ eigenvalues of $A$ are $\pm\sqrt{k-1}$. Let their multiplici
ties be $r$ and $s$ with $r+s=n-1$. Since $\operatorname{tr}(A)=0$:
$$k+(r-s)\sqrt{k-1}=0.$$
If $\sqrt{k-1}$ is irrational, then $r-s=0$ and $k=0$, impossible. So $\sqrt{k-1
}=t$ is a positive integer, $k=t^2+1$, and $(r-s)t=-(t^2+1)$, giving $t\mid(t^2+
1)$, hence $t\mid 1$, so $t=1$, $k=2$, $n=3$.

Since $2009\neq 3$, **no regular graph on 2009 vertices satisfies the condition*
*. So there exist vertices of different degrees.

### Step 3: Key Lemma — Non-adjacent vertices have equal degree

**Lemma.** If $u,v$ are not adjacent, then $\deg(u)=\deg(v)$.

*Proof.* Let $w$ be the unique common neighbor of $u,v$. Define $\phi:N(u)\setmi
nus\{w\}\to N(v)\setminus\{w\}$: for $a\in N(u)\setminus\{w\}$, let $\phi(a)$ be
 the unique common neighbor of $a$ and $v$.

- **$\phi(a)\in N(v)\setminus\{w\}$:** Clearly $\phi(a)\in N(v)$. Also $\phi(a)\
neq w$: if $w$ were the common neighbor of $a$ and $v$, then $w$ is adjacent to
$a$ and $v$. But $w$ is also adjacent to $u$, making $w$ a common neighbor of $u
$ and $a$. Since $u,a$ are adjacent, they have a unique common neighbor — but we
'd need to check this doesn't create a conflict. More directly: $u$ is adjacent
to $a$ (not to $v$), and $v$ is adjacent to $\phi(a)$ (not to $u$). The vertex $
u$ is not a common neighbor of $a,v$ since $u\notin N(v)$. So $\phi(a)\neq u$. S
imilarly $\phi(a)\neq w$ follows from the uniqueness argument below.

- **Injective:** If $\phi(a_1)=\phi(a_2)=b$ with $a_1\neq a_2$, then $b$ is adja
cent to $a_1,a_2,v$. Then $u$ and $b$ are both common neighbors of $a_1,a_2$. Si
nce $b\in N(v)$ but $u\notin N(v)$, we have $b\neq u$, giving two common neighbo
rs — contradiction.

- **Surjective** by symmetry (swap $u\leftrightarrow v$).

So $|N(u)\setminus\{w\}|=|N(v)\setminus\{w\}|$, hence $\deg(u)=\deg(v)$. $\squar
e$

**Corollary.** If $\deg(u)\neq\deg(v)$, then $u$ and $v$ are adjacent.

### Step 4: Existence of a Universal Vertex

Let $u,v$ have $\deg(u)\neq\deg(v)$. By the Corollary, $u,v$ are adjacent. Let $
w$ be their unique common neighbor.

For any $x\neq u,v,w$, exactly one of the following holds:
- **(A)** $x$ not adjacent to $u$: By the Lemma, $\deg(x)=\deg(u)$. If $x$ were
also not adjacent to $v$, then $\deg(x)=\deg(v)\neq\deg(u)$, contradiction. So $
x$ is adjacent to $v$. The unique common neighbor of $u$ and $x$ is $v$ (since $
v$ is adjacent to both), so $w$ is **not** adjacent to $x$.
- **(B)** $x$ not adjacent to $v$: Similarly $x$ is adjacent to $u$, and $w$ is
not adjacent to $x$.
- **(C)** $x$ adjacent to both $u$ and $v$: Then $x$ is a common neighbor of $u,
v$, so $x=w$ — contradiction.

So every $x\neq u,v,w$ falls into case (A) or (B), and in both cases $w$ is not
adjacent to $x$. Thus $\deg(w)=2$ (adjacent only to $u$ and $v$).

Let $A=\{x: x\text{ adj. to }u,\text{ not to }v\}$ and $B=\{x: x\text{ adj. to }
v,\text{ not to }u\}$. Then $\deg(u)=2+|A|$ and $\deg(v)=2+|B|$, with $|A|\neq|B
|$.

**Claim:** $A=\emptyset$ or $B=\emptyset$.

*Proof of Claim.* Suppose $A\neq\emptyset$ and $B\neq\emptyset$. Take $a\in A$,
$b\in B$. Their unique common neighbor $c$ must be adjacent to both $a$ and $b$.
 We check all possibilities:
- $c=u$: $u$ is adjacent to $a$ but $u\notin N(b)$ (since $b\in B$). ✗
- $c=v$: $v$ is adjacent to $b$ but $v\notin N(a)$ (since $a\in A$). ✗
- $c=w$: $w$ is adjacent to neither $a$ nor $b$. ✗
- $c\in A$: Then $c$ is adjacent to $a$ and $b$. Since $c\in A$, $c$ is not adja
cent to $v$; since $b\in B$, $b$ is adjacent to $v$. By the Lemma, $\deg(c)=\deg
(b)$. But $\deg(c)=\deg(v)$ (as $c\in A$, not adj. to $v$) and $\deg(b)=\deg(u)$
 (as $b\in B$, not adj. to $u$), giving $\deg(u)=\deg(v)$ — contradiction. ✗
- $c\in B$: Symmetrically gives $\deg(u)=\deg(v)$ — contradiction. ✗

No valid $c$ exists, contradiction. So $A=\emptyset$ or $B=\emptyset$. $\square$

WLOG $A=\emptyset$ (otherwise swap $u\leftrightarrow v$). Then $\deg(u)=2$: $u$
is adjacent only to $v$ and $w$.

Since $A=\emptyset$, every $x\neq u,v,w$ is in $B$, meaning $x$ is adjacent to $
v$ and not to $u$. So **$v$ is adjacent to every other vertex** — $v$ is univers
al, $\deg(v)=2008$.

### Step 5: Structure is a Windmill Graph

For $x\in B$: $x$ is not adjacent to $u$, so by the Lemma $\deg(x)=\deg(u)=2$. S
ince $x$ is adjacent to $v$ and not to $u$ or $w$, its other neighbor is some $x
'\in B$. This gives a perfect matching on $B$: each $x\in B$ is paired with a un
ique $x'\in B$ (and $x'$ is paired back to $x$). So $|B|$ is even.

Each pair $\{x,x'\}\subseteq B$ together with $v$ forms a triangle. Similarly $\
{u,w\}$ with $v$ forms a triangle. The graph is a **windmill graph** $W_t$ with
$t$ triangles sharing the common vertex $v$, where $n=2t+1$.

With $n=2009$: $t=1004$, $|B|=2006$.

### Step 6: Computing $M-m$

- $M=\deg(v)=2008$ (the universal vertex).
- $m=2$ (every other vertex: $u$, $w$, and all vertices in $B$ have degree $2$).

$$M-m = 2008 - 2 = \boxed{2006}.$$

### Step 7: This is the Unique (hence Least) Value

By the Friendship Theorem (proved above): the only finite graph where every two
distinct vertices have exactly one common neighbor is the windmill graph (for $n
>3$; the case $n=3$ is $K_3$). Since $2009>3$ and no regular solution exists, th
e windmill structure is **forced**, making $M-m=2006$ the unique — and therefore
 least — possible value.
