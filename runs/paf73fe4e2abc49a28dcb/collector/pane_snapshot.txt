Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether separate continuity (bounde
d in each variable by specific Schwartz seminorms) implies a joint product bound
 with those same seminorms.

**Answer: False.**

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $N = M = 1$ and $D_1 = D_2 = D$ with $D \geq 2$. Let $\{h_n\}_{n
\geq 0}$ be the Hermite functions, which form an orthonormal basis of $L^2(\math
bb{R})$ and lie in $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})$. Write $\hat{f}(n) = \langle f, h_n
\rangle_{L^2}$.

**Key growth estimate.** Using the raising/lowering operator relations $x = (a^+
 + a^-)/\sqrt{2}$, $\frac{d}{dx} = (a^- - a^+)/\sqrt{2}$ with $a^\pm h_n = \sqrt
{n + (\text{const})}\, h_{n\pm 1}$, one shows:
$$\sup_{x}\bigl|x^\alpha D^\beta h_n(x)\bigr| \asymp n^{(|\alpha|+|\beta|)/2}.$$
Hence $\|h_n\|_D = \sum_{|\alpha|,|\beta|<D} \sup|x^\alpha D^\beta h_n| \asymp n
^{D-1}$, with the dominant term at $|\alpha|=|\beta|=D-1$.

**Construction of the counterexample.** Define the bilinear form:
$$T(f,g) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} n^{2D-1}\,\hat{f}(n)\,\hat{g}(n).$$

*Well-definedness:* For $f,g \in \mathcal{S}$, the coefficients $\hat{f}(n), \ha
t{g}(n)$ decay faster than any power of $n$ (standard property of Schwartz funct
ions in the Hermite basis). Since $n^{2D-1}$ is polynomial, the series converges
 absolutely.

**Condition 1 holds.** Fix $g \in \mathcal{S}$. Set $K_g = \sum_{n} n^{2D-1}\hat
{g}(n)\,h_n$. Since $|\hat{g}(n)| \leq C_k n^{-k}$ for every $k$, choosing $k$ l
arge enough gives $\sum n^{4D-2}|\hat{g}(n)|^2 < \infty$, so $K_g \in L^2(\mathb
b{R})$ (indeed $K_g \in \mathcal{S}$). Then:
$$T(f,g) = \langle f, K_g\rangle_{L^2}, \quad |T(f,g)| \leq \|f\|_{L^2}\,\|K_g\|
_{L^2}.$$
For $D \geq 2$, the norm $\|f\|_D$ controls $\|f\|_{L^2}$: indeed $\|f\|_D \geq
\sup|f| + \sup|xf|$, and $\|f\|_{L^2}^2 \leq \pi\sup(|f|^2(1+x^2)) \leq C\,\sup|
f|\cdot\sup|xf| \leq C\|f\|_D^2$. Therefore:
$$|T(f,g)| \leq C\,\|f\|_D\,\|K_g\|_{L^2} = c_1(g)\,\|f\|_D. \quad\checkmark$$

**Condition 2 holds** by the identical argument with $f$ and $g$ interchanged. $
\checkmark$

**$T$ is a distribution (kernel theorem applies).** For fixed $g \in \mathcal{S}
$, $f \mapsto \langle f, K_g\rangle$ is continuous on $\mathcal{S}$ (since $K_g
\in \mathcal{S}$). Likewise for fixed $f$. So $T$ is separately continuous in th
e full Schwartz topology, and the Schwartz kernel theorem guarantees $T$ is a di
stribution on $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^2)$.

**The product bound fails.** Set $e_n = h_n / \|h_n\|_D$, so $\|e_n\|_D = 1$. Th
en:
$$T(e_n, e_n) = n^{2D-1}\cdot\frac{|\langle h_n, h_n\rangle|^2}{\|h_n\|_D^2} = \
frac{n^{2D-1}}{\|h_n\|_D^2} \asymp \frac{n^{2D-1}}{n^{2(D-1)}} = n \;\longrighta
rrow\; \infty.$$
Yet $\|e_n\|_D = 1$ for all $n$. Therefore no constant $C$ satisfies $|T(f,g)| \
leq C\,\|f\|_D\,\|g\|_D$ for all $f,g \in \mathcal{S}$.

**Why the Uniform Boundedness Principle does not save the conclusion.** One migh
t try to apply Banach–Steinhaus on the Banach completion $X = \overline{\mathcal
{S}}^{\|\cdot\|_D}$. The family $\{L_g : \|g\|_D \leq 1\}$ of functionals on $X$
 is pointwise bounded on $\mathcal{S}$ (by condition 2), but $\mathcal{S}$ is a
*proper dense* subspace of $X$ and is meager (first category) in $X$. The UBP re
quires pointwise boundedness on a non-meager set (or the entire Banach space), s
o it cannot be applied. This is precisely the gap that the counterexample exploi
ts.

**Remark.** The distribution $T$ *does* satisfy a product bound $|T(f,g)| \leq C
'\|f\|_{D'}\|g\|_{D'}$ for some larger $D' > D$ — this is guaranteed by the kern
el theorem itself. The point is that the specific orders $D_1, D_2$ from the sep
arate continuity conditions are insufficient.
