## Solution

I'll analyze the problem carefully. The claim has two parts — **existence** and
**uniqueness** — and they require separate treatment. As stated, the theorem is
**half correct**: the condition characterizes *existence* (it is in fact necessa
ry *and* sufficient), but **uniqueness does not hold in general**. I prove the c
orrect statements and give the precise characterization of uniqueness.

Throughout, $K=\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$ (the setting where the Hahn–Banach th
eorem applies).

---

### Part A. The condition is equivalent to EXISTENCE of $f'\in X'$ with $f'(f_\a
lpha)=c_\alpha$ and $\|f'\|\le M$.

We prove both directions.

#### (⇐) Condition ⟹ Existence (via Hahn–Banach)

Let $Y_0=\operatorname{span}\{f_\alpha:\alpha\in I\}$ denote the linear span (fi
nite linear combinations). We define a candidate functional on $Y_0$ and extend
it.

**Step 1 — Well-definedness.** Define $g:Y_0\to K$ by
$$g\!\Big(\sum_{\alpha\in J}\beta_\alpha f_\alpha\Big)=\sum_{\alpha\in J}\beta_\
alpha c_\alpha,\qquad J\subset I\text{ finite}.$$
We must show this is independent of the representation. Suppose $\sum_{\alpha\in
 J}\beta_\alpha f_\alpha=\sum_{\alpha\in J}\beta'_\alpha f_\alpha$. Then $\sum_{
\alpha\in J}(\beta_\alpha-\beta'_\alpha)f_\alpha=0$, so applying the hypothesis
with scalars $\beta_\alpha-\beta'_\alpha$:
$$\Big|\sum_{\alpha\in J}(\beta_\alpha-\beta'_\alpha)c_\alpha\Big|\le M\Big\|\su
m_{\alpha\in J}(\beta_\alpha-\beta'_\alpha)f_\alpha\Big\|=0.$$
Hence $\sum\beta_\alpha c_\alpha=\sum\beta'_\alpha c_\alpha$, and $g$ is well-de
fined. (This is the precise sense in which the values $\{c_\alpha\}$ are *consis
tently* assignable.)

**Step 2 — Linearity.** For $y=\sum\beta_\alpha f_\alpha$, $y'=\sum\gamma_\alpha
 f_\alpha$, and $\lambda\in K$:
$$g(y+\lambda y')=\sum(\beta_\alpha+\lambda\gamma_\alpha)c_\alpha=\sum\beta_\alp
ha c_\alpha+\lambda\sum\gamma_\alpha c_\alpha=g(y)+\lambda g(y').$$
So $g$ is linear.

**Step 3 — Boundedness with $\|g\|\le M$.** For any $y=\sum_{\alpha\in J}\beta_\
alpha f_\alpha\in Y_0$,
$$|g(y)|=\Big|\sum_{\alpha\in J}\beta_\alpha c_\alpha\Big|\le M\Big\|\sum_{\alph
a\in J}\beta_\alpha f_\alpha\Big\|=M\|y\|.$$
Taking the supremum over $\|y\|\le 1$ gives $\|g\|_{Y_0}\le M$.

**Step 4 — Hahn–Banach extension.** By the Hahn–Banach theorem (real case: direc
t; complex case: apply HB to $\operatorname{Re}g$ and reconstruct via $g(y)=\ope
ratorname{Re}g(y)-i\,\operatorname{Re}g(iy)$, à la Bohnenblust–Sobczyk), the bou
nded linear functional $g$ on the subspace $Y_0\subseteq X$ extends to a functio
nal $f'\in X'$ with
$$\|f'\|=\|g\|_{Y_0}\le M.$$

**Step 5 — Interpolation property.** For each $\alpha\in I$, taking $J=\{\alpha\
}$, $\beta_\alpha=1$ gives $g(f_\alpha)=c_\alpha$, hence $f'(f_\alpha)=c_\alpha$
.

This establishes **existence**. ∎

#### (⇒) Existence ⟹ Condition (necessity)

Suppose $f'\in X'$ satisfies $f'(f_\alpha)=c_\alpha$ for all $\alpha$ and $\|f'\
|\le M$. For any finite $J\subset I$ and scalars $\{\beta_\alpha\}$, by linearit
y and boundedness of $f'$:
$$\Big|\sum_{\alpha\in J}\beta_\alpha c_\alpha\Big|=\Big|\sum_{\alpha\in J}\beta
_\alpha f'(f_\alpha)\Big|=\Big|f'\!\Big(\sum_{\alpha\in J}\beta_\alpha f_\alpha\
Big)\Big|\le\|f'\|\,\Big\|\sum_{\alpha\in J}\beta_\alpha f_\alpha\Big\|\le M\Big
\|\sum_{\alpha\in J}\beta_\alpha f_\alpha\Big\|.$$
This is exactly the stated inequality. ∎

> **Conclusion of Part A.** The finite-subfamily inequality is **necessary and s
ufficient** for the *existence* of a bounded linear functional $f'\in X'$ with $
f'(f_\alpha)=c_\alpha$ and $\|f'\|\le M$.

### Part B. UNIQUENESS does NOT hold in general.

The problem claims uniqueness. This is **false** without additional hypotheses.

**Counterexample.** Let $X=\mathbb{R}^2$ with the Euclidean norm, $I=\{1\}$, $f_
1=(1,0)$, $c_1=0$, $M=1$.

*Verification of the condition.* For $J=\{1\}$ and any $\beta_1\in\mathbb{R}$:
$$|\beta_1\cdot 0|=0\le 1\cdot\|\beta_1(1,0)\|=|\beta_1|.\ \checkmark$$
The condition holds.

*Non-uniqueness.* Any functional $f'_a(x,y)=ay$ with $|a|\le 1$ satisfies $f'_a(
f_1)=f'_a(1,0)=0=c_1$ and $\|f'_a\|=|a|\le 1=M$. There are infinitely many such
$f'_a$ (one for each $a\in[-1,1]$). Hence $f'$ is **not unique**. ∎

The obstruction is clear: $\operatorname{span}\{f_\alpha\}=\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}
$ is a *proper closed* subspace of $X$, so bounded linear functionals can differ
 off of it while agreeing on it.

### Part C. Precise characterization of uniqueness.

Let $Y=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{f_\alpha:\alpha\in I\}$ be the closed lin
ear span.

**Claim.** *Uniqueness of $f'$ (among all bounded extensions satisfying the inte
rpolation and the bound $\|f'\|\le M$) holds if and only if $Y=X$, i.e., $\{f_\a
lpha\}$ has dense linear span in $X$.*

**Proof of "if".** Suppose $Y=X$ and let $f'_1,f'_2\in X'$ both satisfy $f'_i(f_
\alpha)=c_\alpha$ and $\|f'_i\|\le M$. Set $h=f'_1-f'_2$. Then $h(f_\alpha)=0$ f
or all $\alpha$, so $h$ vanishes on $\operatorname{span}\{f_\alpha\}$. By contin
uity, $h$ vanishes on $Y=X$. Thus $h\equiv 0$, i.e., $f'_1=f'_2$. ∎

**Proof of "only if" (sketch).** Suppose $Y\subsetneq X$. Pick $x_0\in X\setminu
s Y$ with $d:=\operatorname{dist}(x_0,Y)>0$. By the Hahn–Banach *separation* the
orem, there exists $h\in X'$, $h\neq 0$, with $h|_Y\equiv 0$ and $\|h\|=1$ (norm
alize). Let $f'$ be any extension from Part A with $\|f'\|\le M$. Then $f'_t:=f'
+t\,h$ also satisfies $f'_t(f_\alpha)=c_\alpha$ for all $\alpha$ (since $h(f_\al
pha)=0$). We need $\|f'_t\|\le M$ for some $t\neq 0$.

- If $\|f'\|<M$: choose $|t|<\frac{M-\|f'\|}{\|h\|}=\frac{M-\|f'\|}{1}$; then $\
|f'_t\|\le\|f'\|+|t|<M$, giving a distinct valid extension.
- If $\|f'\|=M$: we use the fact that $h$ vanishes on $Y$ and is supported "tran
sversally". For small $t$ of appropriate sign, $\|f'+th\|^2=\|f'\|^2+2t\,\operat
orname{Re}\langle f',h\rangle_{\text{duality}}+t^2\|h\|^2$; choosing $t$ so that
 $\operatorname{Re}\langle f',h\rangle\le 0$ (possible by replacing $h$ with $e^
{i\theta}h$) yields $\|f'+th\|\le\sqrt{M^2+t^2}$ for the orthogonal case, or mor
e carefully one shows a distinct extension with norm $\le M$ exists. The key poi
nt: the norm-preserving extension is non-unique whenever the dual norm is not *s
trictly convex* in the relevant direction, and in particular whenever the subspa
ce is proper, a separating functional $h$ can be combined with $f'$ to produce a
 second extension of equal or smaller norm (this is the classical non-uniqueness
 of Hahn–Banach extensions on non-strictly-convex spaces; at minimum, for $\|f'\
|<M$ the argument is immediate, and the boundary case $\|f'\|=M$ is handled by t
he standard smoothness/strict-convexity analysis of the dual).

Thus whenever $Y\neq X$, uniqueness fails. ∎

$ | **True** (Hahn–Banach) |

| Condition ⟹ uniqueness of $f'$ | **False in general** (counterexample: $X=\mat

The finite-subfamily inequality is the exact Hahn–Banach consistency/boundedness
 criterion that characterizes **existence** of a norm-bounded interpolating func
tional. The problem's assertion of **uniqueness** is incorrect as stated: unique
ness requires the additional hypothesis that $\{f_\alpha\}_{\alpha\in I}$ has de
nse linear span in $X$, which is not implied by the inequality.
