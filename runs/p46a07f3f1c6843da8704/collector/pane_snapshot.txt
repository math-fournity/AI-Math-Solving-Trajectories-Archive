## Proof

Define $F(x) = \int_0^x \frac{dt}{1+t^\alpha}$. The recurrence $\int_{u_{n-1}}^{
u_n} \frac{du}{1+u^\alpha} = 1$ gives $F(u_n) - F(u_{n-1}) = 1$, hence

$$F(u_n) = F(u_0) + n. \tag{$\star$}$$

Since the integrand is positive, $F$ is strictly increasing and $u_n = F^{-1}(F(
u_0)+n)$ whenever $F(u_0)+n$ lies in the range of $F$.

---

### Case 1: $0 < \alpha < 1$

The integral $\int_0^\infty \frac{dt}{1+t^\alpha}$ diverges since $\frac{1}{1+t^
\alpha} \sim t^{-\alpha}$ with $\alpha < 1$. Thus $F(x) \to \infty$ as $x\to\inf
ty$, the sequence $\{u_n\}$ is defined for all $n\geq 0$, and $u_n \to \infty$.

**Asymptotics of $F$.** Using the identity $\frac{1}{1+t^\alpha} = \frac{t^{-\al
pha}}{1+t^{-\alpha}} = t^{-\alpha} - \frac{t^{-2\alpha}}{1+t^{-\alpha}}$, we obt
ain

$$F(x) = \int_0^x t^{-\alpha}\,dt - \int_0^x \frac{t^{-2\alpha}}{1+t^{-\alpha}}\
,dt = \frac{x^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha} - R(x),$$

where $R(x) = \int_0^x \frac{t^{-2\alpha}}{1+t^{-\alpha}}\,dt$. The integrand of
 $R$ satisfies $\frac{t^{-2\alpha}}{1+t^{-\alpha}} \sim t^{-2\alpha}$ as $t\to\i
nfty$. If $2\alpha > 1$, then $R(x)$ converges to a finite limit, so $R(x) = O(1
) = o(x^{1-\alpha})$. If $2\alpha \leq 1$, iterating the same expansion yields t
he same conclusion. In all cases,

$$F(x) \sim \frac{x^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha} \quad\text{as } x\to\infty. \tag{1}$$

**Asymptotics of $u_n$.** From $(\star)$ and (1): $\frac{u_n^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alph
a} \sim n$, so

$$u_n \sim \bigl((1-\alpha)\,n\bigr)^{1/(1-\alpha)}. \tag{2}$$

**Convergence of $\sum u_n^\beta$.** From (2), $u_n^\beta \sim C\, n^{\beta/(1-\
alpha)}$ for a constant $C > 0$. By the $p$-test, $\sum u_n^\beta$ converges if
and only if

$$\frac{\beta}{1-\alpha} > 1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \beta > 1-\alpha \qua
d\Longleftrightarrow\quad \alpha + \beta > 1.$$

At the boundary $\alpha + \beta = 1$, we have $u_n^\beta \sim C\,n$ and the seri
es diverges.

---

### Case 2: $\alpha > 1$

The integral $L = \int_0^\infty \frac{dt}{1+t^\alpha}$ converges (since $\frac{1
}{1+t^\alpha}\sim t^{-\alpha}$ with $\alpha > 1$). Hence $F$ is bounded: $F(x) <
 L$ for all $x \geq 0$.

The relation $F(u_n) = F(u_0) + n$ requires $F(u_0) + n < L$, i.e., $n < L - F(u
_0)$. Therefore the sequence $\{u_n\}$ has only **finitely many** terms (at most
 $\lfloor L - F(u_0)\rfloor$ terms beyond $u_0$).

Consequently $\sum u_n^\beta$ is a **finite sum**, which converges for every $\b
eta \in \mathbb{R}$.

---

### Summary

| Range of $\alpha$ | Convergence of $\sum u_n^\beta$ |
|---|---|
| $0 < \alpha < 1$ | Converges $\iff \alpha + \beta > 1$; diverges if $\alpha +
\beta \leq 1$ |
| $\alpha > 1$ | Converges for all $\beta$ (the series is finite) |
