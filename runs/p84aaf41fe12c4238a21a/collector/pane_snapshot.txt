## Proof

Let $f(z) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} c_k z^k$ be analytic in $|z| < R$, and fix $0 <
r < R$. Define $M(r) = \max_{|z|=r} |f(z)|$. By the maximum modulus principle, t
he maximum is attained on the circle $|z| = r$.

**Cauchy's estimate via the integral formula.** For every $n \ge 0$,
$$
c_n = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=r} \frac{f(z)}{z^{n+1}}\,dz.
$$
Parametrizing $z = r e^{i\theta}$, $dz = i r e^{i\theta}\,d\theta$, this becomes
$$
c_n = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(r e^{i\theta})\, r^{-n} e^{-in\theta}\,d\the
ta. \tag{1}
$$
Set $h(\theta) := f(r e^{i\theta})\, r^{-n} e^{-in\theta}$, so $c_n = \frac{1}{2
\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} h(\theta)\,d\theta$. Applying the triangle inequality for inte
grals and then the bound $|f(r e^{i\theta})| \le M(r)$,
$$
|c_n| \le \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} |h(\theta)|\,d\theta = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_
0^{2\pi} |f(r e^{i\theta})|\, r^{-n}\,d\theta \le \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} M(
r)\, r^{-n}\,d\theta = \frac{M(r)}{r^n}. \tag{2}
$$

**Using the hypothesis of equality.** We are given that for some particular $n$
and some $0 < r < R$,
$$
|c_n| = \frac{M(r)}{r^n}. \tag{3}
$$
Combining (2) and (3), both inequalities in (2) must be equalities.

*Equality in the second inequality.* Since $|f(r e^{i\theta})| \le M(r)$ is cont
inuous and integrates to $2\pi M(r)$, we must have
$$
|f(r e^{i\theta})| = M(r) \quad \text{for all } \theta. \tag{4}
$$

*Equality in the first inequality.* Equality $|\int h| = \int |h|$ holds only wh
en $h(\theta)$ has a constant argument almost everywhere: there exists $\varphi
\in \mathbb{R}$ such that
$$
h(\theta) = |h(\theta)|\, e^{i\varphi} \quad \text{a.e.}
$$
By continuity this holds for all $\theta$. Using (4), $|h(\theta)| = M(r)\,r^{-n
}$, so
$$
f(r e^{i\theta})\, r^{-n} e^{-in\theta} = M(r)\, r^{-n}\, e^{i\varphi},
$$
and hence
$$
f(r e^{i\theta}) = M(r)\, e^{i\varphi}\, e^{in\theta} = \frac{M(r)\,e^{i\varphi}
}{r^n}\,(r e^{i\theta})^n. \tag{5}
$$

Writing $z = r e^{i\theta}$, equation (5) says
$$
f(z) = C\, z^n \quad \text{for all } |z| = r, \qquad C := \frac{M(r)\,e^{i\varph
i}}{r^n}. \tag{6}
$$

**Identifying the constant.** The coefficient of $z^n$ in the power series $C z^
n$ is $C$, while in $f(z) = \sum_k c_k z^k$ it is $c_n$. Since $f(z) = C z^n$ on
 $|z| = r$, comparing the $n$-th Taylor coefficient (computed at $0$, which lies
 inside $|z|<r$) gives $C = c_n$. Thus
$$
f(z) = c_n\, z^n \quad \text{for all } |z| = r. \tag{7}
$$

**Applying the identity theorem.** The function $g(z) := f(z) - c_n z^n$ is anal
ytic in $|z| < R$ (being a difference of analytic functions). By (7), $g$ vanish
es on the entire circle $|z| = r$, which is an infinite set with accumulation po
ints inside the disk $|z| < R$. By the identity theorem for analytic functions,
$$
g(z) \equiv 0 \quad \text{in } |z| < R.
$$

Therefore
$$
\boxed{\,f(z) = c_n\, z^n \quad \text{for all } |z| < R.\,}
$$

Equivalently, every coefficient $c_k$ with $k \neq n$ vanishes. $\blacksquare$
