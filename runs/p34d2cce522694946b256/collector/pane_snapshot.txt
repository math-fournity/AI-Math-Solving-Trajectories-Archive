## Solution

Since every term in the series for $y$ and the factor $xe^{-x^2/2}$ are non-nega
tive on $[0,\infty)$, the monotone convergence theorem justifies interchanging t

$$I = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4^{n}(n!)^{2}}\int_0^{\infty}x^{2n+1}e^{-x^2/2
}\,dx.$$

**Step 2: Evaluate the inner integral.**

Substitute $t = x^2/2$, so $x\,dx = dt$ and $x^{2n} = (2t)^n$:

$$\int_0^{\infty}x^{2n+1}e^{-x^2/2}\,dx = \int_0^{\infty}(2t)^{n}e^{-t}\,dt = 2^
{n}\Gamma(n+1) = 2^{n}n!.$$

**Step 3: Sum the resulting series.**

$$I = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{n}n!}{4^{n}(n!)^{2}} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\fra
c{1}{2^{n}\,n!} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1/2)^{n}}{n!} = e^{1/2} = \sqrt{e}.$
$

Therefore,

$$\int_0^{\infty}xe^{-x^2/2}y\,dx = \boxed{\sqrt{e}}.$$
