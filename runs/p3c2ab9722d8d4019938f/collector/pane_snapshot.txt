## Solution

The group is $G = \langle a, b, c \mid aba^{-1}b^{-1}c^2 = 1 \rangle$, with the
relation $[a,b] = c^{-2}$.

**Index 3 normal subgroups ↔ surjective homomorphisms $G \to \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb
{Z}$.**

Since $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ is the unique group of order 3, every normal subg
roup $N \trianglelefteq G$ with $[G:N]=3$ yields $G/N \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{
Z}$. Two surjective homomorphisms have the same kernel iff they differ by an aut
omorphism of $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$. Since $|\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}/3\m
athbb{Z})| = 2$:

$$\#\{\text{index 3 normal subgroups}\} = \frac{\#\{\text{surjective homomorphis
ms } G \to \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}\}}{2}.$$

**Counting homomorphisms.** A homomorphism $\phi: G \to \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$
is determined by $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma) = (\phi(a), \phi(b), \phi(c)) \in (\ma
thbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})^3$, subject to the relation being satisfied. Since $\mathbb
{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ is abelian, the commutator $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$ maps to $0$, so the
 relation $aba^{-1}b^{-1}c^2 = 1$ imposes:

$$0 + 2\gamma = 0 \quad \text{in } \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}.$$

Since $\gcd(2,3)=1$, multiplication by $2$ is invertible mod $3$, forcing $\gamm
a = 0$.

Thus $\phi$ is determined freely by $(\alpha, \beta) \in (\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
)^2$ with $\gamma = 0$, giving $3^2 = 9$ homomorphisms total.

**Surjectivity.** $\phi$ is surjective iff at least one of $\alpha, \beta$ is no
nzero. The only non-surjective case is $(\alpha,\beta) = (0,0)$. So the number o
f surjective homomorphisms is $9 - 1 = 8$.

**Quotient by automorphisms.** The nontrivial automorphism $x \mapsto 2x$ of $\m
athbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ sends $(\alpha, \beta) \mapsto (2\alpha, 2\beta)$. For any
 nonzero $(\alpha,\beta)$, the pair $\{(\alpha,\beta), (2\alpha, 2\beta)\}$ cons
ists of two distinct elements (equality would force $\alpha = \beta = 0$). Hence
 the 8 surjective homomorphisms split into $8/2 = 4$ orbits.

**Conclusion.** The number of index 3 normal subgroups of $G$ is $\boxed{4}$.
