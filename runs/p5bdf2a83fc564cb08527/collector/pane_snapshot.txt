## Analysis

This is **Erdős Problem 141**, formally stated as a Lean conjecture. The main st
atement `erdos_141` asks:

> Let $k \geq 3$. Are there $k$ consecutive primes in arithmetic progression?

That is, for every $k \geq 3$, does there exist a set $s$ of $k$ consecutive pri
mes that simultaneously form an arithmetic progression?

## Status of the Problem

This is a **genuinely open research problem** in number theory, as indicated by
the `category research open` annotation in the formalization itself.

- **$k = 11$ is open**: The variant `eleven` (marked `open`) asks whether 11 con

- **Infinitude is open even for $k = 3$**: The variant `infinite_three` (marked
`open`) states that it is unknown whether there are infinitely many 3-term progr

- **The general case is open**: The variant `infinite_general_case` (marked `ope
n`) asks whether for every $k \geq 3$ there are infinitely many such progression
s.

The main conjecture `erdos_141` — that for **every** $k \geq 3$ there exists **a
t least one** arithmetic progression of $k$ consecutive primes — is unresolved.
No proof or disproof is known. The problem is listed as open on erdosproblems.co
m and is a well-known open question in additive/computational number theory.
