## Proof

Let $S$ and $T$ be sets with $|S|=\mu$ and $|T|=\lambda$, so that $2^{\mu}=\math
cal P(S)$ and $2^{\lambda}=\mathcal P(T)$ as Boolean algebras (with supremum $=\
cup$, infimum $=\cap$, complement, and order $\subseteq$). Let

$$\varphi:\mathcal P(S)\to\mathcal P(T)$$

be a Boolean algebra isomorphism, i.e. a bijection preserving suprema and infima
 whose inverse also preserves suprema and infima.

**Step 1 — $\varphi$ is an order isomorphism.** In any lattice $a\le b\iff a\vee
 b=b$. Since $\varphi$ preserves suprema,
$$a\le b\implies a\vee b=b\implies \varphi(a)\vee\varphi(b)=\varphi(b)\implies \
varphi(a)\le\varphi(b).$$
Thus $\varphi$ is order-preserving; being bijective with order-preserving invers
e, it is an order isomorphism. In particular $\varphi$ maps the least element $0
=\varnothing$ of $\mathcal P(S)$ to the least element $0'=\varnothing$ of $\math
cal P(T)$: indeed $0\le A$ for all $A$, so $\varphi(0)\le\varphi(A)$ for all $A$
, and by surjectivity $\varphi(0)\le B$ for every $B\in\mathcal P(T)$, hence $\v
arphi(0)=0'$.

**Step 2 — $\varphi$ maps atoms bijectively to atoms.** Recall an element $a$ is
 an *atom* iff $a>0$ and there is no $x$ with $0<x<a$. This is purely order-theo
retic. If $a$ is an atom, then $\varphi(a)>\varphi(0)=0'$ (since $a>0$ and an or
der isomorphism preserves strict order). If $0'<y<\varphi(a)$, write $y=\varphi(
x)$; then $0<x<a$, contradicting that $a$ is an atom. Hence $\varphi(a)$ is an a
tom. The same argument applied to $\varphi^{-1}$ gives the converse, so $\varphi
$ restricts to a bijection between the atoms of $\mathcal P(S)$ and the atoms of
 $\mathcal P(T)$.

**Step 3 — Count the atoms.** The atoms of $\mathcal P(S)$ are exactly the singl
etons $\{s\}$, $s\in S$: these are precisely the nonempty subsets having no none
mpty proper subset. There are $|S|=\mu$ of them. Likewise $\mathcal P(T)$ has ex
actly $|T|=\lambda$ atoms.

**Step 4 — Conclude.** A bijection between the atom sets gives
$$\mu=\#\{\text{atoms of }\mathcal P(S)\}=\#\{\text{atoms of }\mathcal P(T)\}=\l
ambda.$$

Hence $\mu=\lambda$. $\blacksquare$
