Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether testing local minimality on
 every algebraic curve through the origin suffices to guarantee that $0$ is a lo
cal minimum of $f$.

**Answer: No.** I'll construct a counterexample for $n = 2$ (which extends to an
y $n \geq 2$).

---

## Counterexample

**Setup.** Let $g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ be the standard flat function:
$$g(x) = \begin{cases} e^{-1/x^2} & x \neq 0, \\ 0 & x = 0. \end{cases}$$
This is $C^\infty$ and *flat* at $0$: $g^{(k)}(0)=0$ for all $k$, equivalently $
g(x)=o(|x|^N)$ for every $N$.

Define $f:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$ by
$$f(x,y) = y^2 - 2y\,g(x).$$
Since $g\in C^\infty$, we have $f\in C^\infty(\mathbb{R}^2)$. Note $f(0,0)=0$.

---

## Step 1: $0$ is NOT a local minimum of $f$

For $x\neq 0$, evaluate $f$ at the point $(x,\, g(x))$:
$$f(x,\,g(x)) = g(x)^2 - 2g(x)\cdot g(x) = -g(x)^2 < 0.$$
Since $g(x)\neq 0$ for $x\neq 0$ and $(x,g(x))\to (0,0)$ as $x\to 0$, there are
points arbitrarily close to $0$ where $f<0=f(0,0)$. Hence $0$ is **not** a local
 minimum.

The "valley" $\{(x, g(x)) : x\neq 0\}$ lies along the curve $y = e^{-1/x^2}$, wh
ich is **not algebraic** — it cannot be parametrized by polynomials (or even alg
ebraic/Puiseux functions), since $e^{-1/x^2}$ is transcendental and flat.

---

## Step 2: $0$ IS a local minimum of $f$ on every algebraic curve

Let $\gamma(t)=(p(t),\,q(t))$ be a polynomial curve with $\gamma(0)=(0,0)$, i.e.
, $p(0)=q(0)=0$. We show $f\circ\gamma$ has a local minimum at $t=0$.

**Case 1: $q\equiv 0$.** Then $f(\gamma(t))=0$ for all $t$, so $t=0$ is a (non-s
trict) local minimum. ✓

**Case 2: $q\not\equiv 0$.** Write $q(t)=a_k t^k + O(t^{k+1})$ with $a_k\neq 0$,
 $k\geq 1$. Then
$$q(t)^2 = a_k^2\, t^{2k} + O(t^{2k+1}).$$

**Sub-case 2a: $p\equiv 0$.** Then $g(p(t))=g(0)=0$, so $f(\gamma(t))=q(t)^2\geq
 0 = f(0,0)$, with strict inequality for small $t\neq 0$. ✓

**Sub-case 2b: $p\not\equiv 0$.** Write $p(t)=b_m t^m + O(t^{m+1})$ with $b_m\ne
q 0$, $m\geq 1$. For small $|t|$, $|p(t)|\geq \tfrac{|b_m|}{2}|t|^m > 0$ (when $
t\neq 0$), so
$$|g(p(t))| \leq e^{-c/|t|^{2m}}$$
for some constant $c>0$. Since $e^{-c/|t|^{2m}} = o(|t|^N)$ for **every** $N$, t
he flatness of $g$ gives:
$$\left|2\,q(t)\,g(p(t))\right| \leq C\,|t|^k \cdot o(|t|^k) = o(|t|^{2k}).$$

Therefore:
$$f(\gamma(t)) = q(t)^2 - 2q(t)\,g(p(t)) = a_k^2\, t^{2k}\bigl(1 + o(1)\bigr).$$

For sufficiently small $|t|>0$, the factor $(1+o(1))>0$, so $f(\gamma(t)) > 0 =
f(0,0)$. Thus $t=0$ is a **strict** local minimum of $f\circ\gamma$. ✓

---

## Step 3: Extension to $n\geq 2$ and to broader notions of "algebraic curve"

**Higher dimensions.** For $n\geq 2$, define
$$f(x_1,\ldots,x_n) = x_2^2 - 2x_2\,g(x_1) + x_3^2 + \cdots + x_n^2.$$
The same argument applies: the non-negative terms $x_3^2+\cdots+x_n^2$ only help
, and the key estimate (flatness of $g$ vs. polynomial growth) is unchanged. The
 valley $(x_1, g(x_1), 0,\ldots,0)$ still shows $0$ is not a local minimum.

**Semi-algebraic / Puiseux curves.** If "algebraic curve" is interpreted more br
oadly (semi-algebraic curves, or branches of real algebraic varieties parametriz
ed by Puiseux series $\gamma(t)=(c_1 t^{\alpha_1}+\cdots,\, c_2 t^{\alpha_2}+\cd
ots)$ with $\alpha_i\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}$), the argument still holds: $g(\gamma_1(
t))$ remains flat (since $\gamma_1(t)\to 0$ and $g$ is flat at $0$), so the lead
ing term $c_2^2 t^{2\alpha_2}$ dominates, giving $f(\gamma(t))>0$ for small $t>0
$.

---

## Conclusion

The function $f(x,y)=y^2 - 2y\,e^{-1/x^2}$ (with $g(0)=0$) is $C^\infty$, does *
*not** have a local minimum at $0$ (the valley along the non-algebraic curve $y=
e^{-1/x^2}$ dips below $0$), yet $0$ **is** a local minimum of $f$ restricted to
 every algebraic curve through the origin (because the flat function $g$ is negl
igible compared to any polynomial term).

The answer is **No**: $0$ need not be a local minimum of $f$.
