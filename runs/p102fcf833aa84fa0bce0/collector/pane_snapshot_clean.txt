We start from the Lagrangian for a particle of mass $m$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ under
a conservative force $F = -\nabla V$:

$$L(q, \dot{q}) = \tfrac{1}{2} m \dot{q}^2 - V(q),$$

where $q = (q^1, \dots, q^n)$, $\dot{q}^2 = \dot{q}\cdot\dot{q} = \sum_{i=1}^n (
\dot{q}^i)^2$, and $V = V(q)$ depends only on position.

**Step 1 — Conjugate momenta (Legendre transform setup).**

For each coordinate $q^i$ define the conjugate momentum

$$p_i := \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}^i}.$$

Computing:

$$p_i = \frac{\partial}{\partial \dot{q}^i}\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2} m \sum_{j=1}^n (\d
ot{q}^j)^2 - V(q)\Bigr) = m \dot{q}^i.$$

Hence, in vector form,

$$p = m\dot{q}, \qquad \dot{q} = \frac{p}{m}.$$

The map $\dot{q} \mapsto p$ is invertible (its Jacobian is $m\,\mathrm{Id}$, wit
h determinant $m^n \neq 0$), so the Legendre transform is well defined.

**Step 2 — The Hamiltonian via the Legendre transform.**

The Hamiltonian is defined as the Legendre transform of $L$ with respect to $\do
t{q}$:

$$H(q, p) = \sum_{i=1}^n p_i \dot{q}^i - L(q, \dot{q}),$$

with $\dot{q}$ expressed as a function of $p$, i.e. $\dot{q} = p/m$. Substitutin
g:

$$H(q, p) = p \cdot \frac{p}{m} - \Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2} m \Bigl(\frac{p}{m}\Bigr)^2
 - V(q)\Bigr) = \frac{p^2}{m} - \frac{p^2}{2m} + V(q) = \frac{p^2}{2m} + V(q).$$

Thus

$$\boxed{H(q, p) = \frac{p^2}{2m} + V(q)},$$

the sum of kinetic energy $T = p^2/(2m)$ and potential energy $V(q)$. (The Hamil
tonian equals the total energy because $L$ is natural — quadratic in velocities
and time-independent.)

**Step 3 — Hamilton's equations.**

Hamilton's canonical equations are

$$\dot{q}^i = \frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i}, \qquad \dot{p}_i = -\frac{\partia
l H}{\partial q^i}, \qquad i = 1, \dots, n.$$

Computing the partial derivatives of $H = \frac{1}{2m}\sum_j (p_j)^2 + V(q)$:

$$\frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i} = \frac{p_i}{m}, \qquad \frac{\partial H}{\par
tial q^i} = \frac{\partial V}{\partial q^i}.$$

Therefore

$$\dot{q}^i = \frac{p_i}{m}, \qquad \dot{p}_i = -\frac{\partial V}{\partial q^i}
.$$

**Step 4 — Consistency with Newton's second law.**

Differentiating the first equation, $\dot{q}^i = p_i/m$, gives $\ddot{q}^i = \do
t{p}_i/m$. Substituting the second equation:

$$m\ddot{q}^i = \dot{p}_i = -\frac{\partial V}{\partial q^i} = (\nabla V)_i \cdo
t (-1) \;\Longrightarrow\; m\ddot{q} = -\nabla V = F.$$

This is precisely Newton's second law for a conservative force, confirming the H
amiltonian formulation is equivalent to the original Lagrangian/Newtonian dynami
cs.

**Step 5 — Equivalence with the Euler–Lagrange equation (verification of the Leg
endre construction).**

The Euler–Lagrange equation from $L$ is

$$\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}^i} - \frac{\partial L}{\partial
 q^i} = 0.$$

We have $\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}^i} = p_i$ and $\frac{\partial L}{\pa
rtial q^i} = -\frac{\partial V}{\partial q^i}$, so

$$\dot{p}_i + \frac{\partial V}{\partial q^i} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \dot{p}_i
= -\frac{\partial V}{\partial q^i} = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial q^i},$$

which is exactly the second Hamilton equation. The first Hamilton equation $\dot
{q}^i = \partial H/\partial p_i = p_i/m$ is the inverse of the momentum definiti
on $p_i = m\dot{q}^i$. Thus the two Hamilton equations are precisely the Euler–L
agrange equation rewritten via the Legendre transform, completing the derivation
.

**Summary.** Starting from $L(q,\dot{q}) = \tfrac12 m\dot{q}^2 - V(q)$, the Lege
ndre transform yields the conjugate momentum $p = m\dot{q}$ and the Hamiltonian

$$H(q,p) = \frac{p^2}{2m} + V(q),$$

giving Hamilton's equations

$$\dot{q} = \frac{p}{m}, \qquad \dot{p} = -\nabla V,$$

equivalent to $m\ddot{q} = -\nabla V$.
