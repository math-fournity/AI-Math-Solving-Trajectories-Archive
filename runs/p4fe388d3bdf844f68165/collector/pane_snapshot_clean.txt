# Transverse Oscillations of a Simply Supported Beam Under a Partially Distribut
ed Load

## Setup

Consider a uniform Euler–Bernoulli beam of length $l$, simply supported at $x=0$
 and $x=l$. Let $u(x,t)$ denote the transverse displacement, $\rho$ the mass den
sity, $S$ the cross-sectional area, $E$ Young's modulus, and $I$ the area moment
 of inertia. The beam is initially at rest in the straight equilibrium position.

At $t=0$ a uniformly distributed load of intensity $q_0$ is applied over the seg
ment $x_1<x<x_2$ and maintained for all $t>0$.

## Governing Equation and Conditions

The equation of motion is

$$\rho S\,\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}+EI\,\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial
 x^{4}}=q(x),\qquad q(x)=q_{0}\,\mathbf{1}_{[x_{1},x_{2}]}(x).$$

**Boundary conditions** (simple supports — zero deflection and zero bending mome
nt):

$$u(0,t)=u(l,t)=0,\qquad u''(0,t)=u''(l,t)=0.$$

**Initial conditions** (beam starts from rest in the straight position):

$$u(x,0)=0,\qquad \dot u(x,0)=0.$$

## Eigenfunction Expansion

The eigenfunctions of the simply supported beam satisfying all four boundary con
ditions are

$$\varphi_n(x)=\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l},\qquad n=1,2,3,\dots$$

with corresponding eigenfrequencies

$$\omega_n=\sqrt{\frac{EI}{\rho S}}\left(\frac{n\pi}{l}\right)^{2}.$$

Expand the solution and the load:

$$u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}T_n(t)\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l},\qquad q(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\
infty}a_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}.$$

The Fourier coefficients of the load are

$$a_n=\frac{2}{l}\int_{0}^{l}q(x)\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}\,dx=\frac{2q_0}{l}\int_{x_
1}^{x_2}\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}\,dx=\frac{2q_0}{n\pi}\left[\cos\frac{n\pi x_1}{l}-\
cos\frac{n\pi x_2}{l}\right].$$

## Modal Equations

Substituting into the PDE and using the orthogonality of $\{\sin(n\pi x/l)\}$ gi
ves, for each $n$,

$$\ddot T_n+\omega_n^{2}\,T_n=\frac{a_n}{\rho S},\qquad T_n(0)=0,\quad \dot T_n(
0)=0.$$

This is a forced harmonic oscillator with a constant (step) forcing. The solutio
n is

$$T_n(t)=\frac{a_n}{\rho S\,\omega_n^{2}}\bigl(1-\cos\omega_n t\bigr).$$

Since $\rho S\,\omega_n^{2}=EI\,(n\pi/l)^{4}$, the modal amplitude becomes

$$\frac{a_n}{\rho S\,\omega_n^{2}}=\frac{2q_0}{n\pi}\cdot\frac{l^{4}}{EI\,n^{4}\
pi^{4}}\left[\cos\frac{n\pi x_1}{l}-\cos\frac{n\pi x_2}{l}\right]=\frac{2q_0\,l^
{4}}{EI\,\pi^{5}}\cdot\frac{1}{n^{5}}\left[\cos\frac{n\pi x_1}{l}-\cos\frac{n\pi
 x_2}{l}\right].$$

## Solution

$$\boxed{\displaystyle u(x,t)=\frac{2q_0\,l^{4}}{EI\,\pi^{5}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\frac{\sin\!\frac{n\pi x}{l}}{n^{5}}\left(\cos\!\frac{n\pi x_1}{l}-\cos\!\frac{n
\pi x_2}{l}\right)\bigl(1-\cos\omega_n t\bigr),\qquad \omega_n=\sqrt{\frac{EI}{\
rho S}}\left(\frac{n\pi}{l}\right)^{2}.}$$

## Interpretation

The displacement decomposes naturally into a **static part** and an **oscillator
y part**:

$$u(x,t)=u_{\mathrm{st}}(x)-u_{\mathrm{osc}}(x,t),$$

where

$$u_{\mathrm{st}}(x)=\frac{2q_0\,l^{4}}{EI\,\pi^{5}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\si
n\frac{n\pi x}{l}}{n^{5}}\left(\cos\frac{n\pi x_1}{l}-\cos\frac{n\pi x_2}{l}\rig
ht)$$

is the static deflection under the distributed load (the new equilibrium about w
hich the beam oscillates), and

$$u_{\mathrm{osc}}(x,t)=\frac{2q_0\,l^{4}}{EI\,\pi^{5}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{
\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l}}{n^{5}}\left(\cos\frac{n\pi x_1}{l}-\cos\frac{n\pi x_2}{l}\
right)\cos\omega_n t$$

is the free vibration superimposed on it. Each mode oscillates at its own natura
l frequency $\omega_n$, so the motion is in general **not periodic** (the freque
ncies $\omega_n\propto n^2$ are not commensurate).

## Convergence

The series converges absolutely and uniformly in both $x$ and $t$ because the ge
neral term is bounded by $C/n^{5}$ (the trigonometric factors are bounded by 2).
 This regularity is more than sufficient: $u$ is twice continuously differentiab
le in $t$ and four times in $x$ away from the load discontinuities $x=x_1,x_2$,
where the fourth derivative has the expected jump discontinuities inherited from
 the piecewise-constant load.

## Verification of Conditions

- **PDE:** Each term satisfies $\rho S\,\ddot T_n\sin(\cdot)+EI\,T_n(n\pi/l)^4\s
in(\cdot)=a_n\sin(\cdot)$; summing recovers the PDE with the Fourier series of $
q(x)$. ✓
- **Boundary conditions:** $\sin(n\pi\cdot 0/l)=\sin(n\pi\cdot l/l)=0$ and $(n\p
i/l)^2\sin(n\pi\cdot 0/l)=(n\pi/l)^2\sin(n\pi\cdot l/l)=0$ for all $n$. ✓
- **Initial conditions:** At $t=0$, $\cos\omega_n\cdot 0=1$, so every factor $(1
-\cos\omega_n t)=0$, giving $u(x,0)=0$; differentiating, $\dot u(x,0)\propto\sin
\omega_n\cdot 0=0$. ✓
