## Shape Derivative of $J(\Omega) = \int_\Omega \nabla\phi \cdot \nabla\psi \, d
x$

### Setup

Let $f := \nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi$. Since $\phi,\psi\in H^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\ma
thbb{R}^N)$, we have $\nabla\phi,\nabla\psi\in H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$, hence $f\in
H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^N)\subset C^0(\mathbb{R}^N)$ (after modification o
n a null set, and in dimension $N$ this is at least continuous locally; in any c
ase $f$ and $\nabla f$ are well-defined locally). The key point: **$\phi$ and $\
psi$ are fixed functions on $\mathbb{R}^N$ and do not move with the domain**. On
ly the integration region $\Omega$ is deformed.

Let $V\in C^0([0,\tau];C^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^N,\mathbb{R}^N))$ and let $
T_t:\mathbb{R}^N\to\mathbb{R}^N$ be the flow generated by $V$ (with $T_0=\mathrm
{Id}$, $\partial_t T_t = V\circ T_t$). Set $\Omega_t := T_t(\Omega)$. The Euleri
an shape derivative is

$$
dJ(\Omega;V) := \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0} J(\Omega_t) = \left.\frac{d}{dt}
\right|_{t=0}\int_{\Omega_t} f(x)\,dx .
$$

### Change of variables

Pulling back to the reference domain $\Omega$ via $x = T_t(y)$,

$$
J(\Omega_t) = \int_{\Omega} f(T_t(y))\,\det DT_t(y)\,dy .
$$

Both factors are differentiable at $t=0$:

- $\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0} f(T_t(y)) = \nabla f(y)\cdot V(y)$, since $\p
artial_t T_t|_{t=0}=V$ and $f\in H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$.
- $\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\det DT_t(y) = \operatorname{div} V(y)$ (Jacob
i's formula, using $\partial_t DT_t|_{t=0}=DV$).

Therefore, differentiating under the integral sign (justified by the regularity
of $f$ and $V$ on the bounded Lipschitz domain $\Omega$),

$$
dJ(\Omega;V) = \int_{\Omega}\bigl[\nabla f\cdot V + f\,\operatorname{div} V\bigr
]\,dx = \int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}(fV)\,dx .
$$

### Boundary form

Since $\Omega$ is bounded with Lipschitz boundary, the divergence theorem (Gauss
–Green) applies to the vector field $fV\in C^0(\overline\Omega;\mathbb{R}^N)$ wi
th $\operatorname{div}(fV)\in L^1(\Omega)$:

$$
\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}(fV)\,dx = \int_{\partial\Omega} f\,(V\cdot n)\,d
S,
$$

where $n$ is the outward unit normal to $\partial\Omega$ (defined $dS$-a.e. on $
\partial\Omega$ by the Lipschitz assumption).

### Result

$$
\boxed{\; dJ(\Omega;V) = \int_{\partial\Omega} \bigl(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi\b
igr)\,(V\cdot n)\,dS \;}
$$

**Remarks.**
1. The derivative is purely **boundary-supported**: only the normal component $V
\cdot n$ of the velocity on $\partial\Omega$ contributes. This is consistent wit
h the structure theorem for shape derivatives (Hadamard/Zolésio): for a function
al of the form $\int_\Omega f\,dx$ with $f$ independent of the domain, the shape
 derivative is a distribution supported on $\partial\Omega$.
2. The tangential component of $V$ on $\partial\Omega$ does not appear — a repar
ametrization of the boundary leaving the set $\Omega$ unchanged does not change
$J$.
3. The $H^2_{\mathrm{loc}}$ regularity of $\phi,\psi$ ensures $f=\nabla\phi\cdot
\nabla\psi\in H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$, which is exactly what is needed to make $\nab
la f\cdot V$ integrable over $\Omega$ and to justify the differentiation under t
he integral. The Lipschitz regularity of $\partial\Omega$ is exactly what is nee
ded to invoke the divergence theorem and obtain the boundary form.
4. Equivalently, in volume form: $dJ(\Omega;V)=\int_\Omega\operatorname{div}\!\b
igl((\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi)\,V\bigr)\,dx$, which reduces to the boundary int
egral above.
