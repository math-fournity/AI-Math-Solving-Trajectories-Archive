# Problem

In a remote desert, a team of archaeologists has discovered a prehistoric ritual site enclosed by four stone walls that form a perfectly circular perimeter. The site is a four-sided enclosure where the corners—Points Alpha, Beta, Gamma, and Delta—all touch the circular boundary.

The survey team records the following wall lengths:
- The northern wall, connecting Alpha to Beta, measures exactly 3 kilometers.
- The eastern wall (Beta to Gamma) and the western wall (Delta to Alpha) are identical, each measuring 5 kilometers.
- The southern wall, connecting Gamma to Delta, measures 8 kilometers.

The chief engineer needs to determine the radius of the circle that encompasses all four corners of this site. This radius can be expressed in the simplest radical form as $\frac{a \sqrt{b}}{c}$, where $a$, $b$, and $c$ are positive integers, $a$ and $c$ share no common factors, and $b$ is square-free.

Calculate the value of $a + b + c$.
