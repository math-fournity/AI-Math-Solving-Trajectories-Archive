**Problem.** Describe all curves in $\mathbb{R}^3$ with constant curvature $\kap
pa>0$ and constant torsion $\tau$.

**Answer.** Up to rigid motion, such a curve is uniquely determined by $(\kappa,
\tau)$. It is a **circular helix** when $\tau\neq 0$, and a **circle** of radius
 $1/\kappa$ when $\tau=0$. Explicitly, with $\omega=\sqrt{\kappa^{2}+\tau^{2}}$
and $\{D,e_{1},e_{2}\}$ any orthonormal basis of $\mathbb{R}^{3}$,

$$
\alpha(s)=\frac{\kappa}{\omega^{2}}\bigl(\sin(\omega s)\,e_{1}-\cos(\omega s)\,e
_{2}\bigr)+\frac{\tau}{\omega^{2}}\,s\,D \quad+\text{const}.
$$

---

### Proof

Let $\alpha(s)$ be parametrized by arclength, with Frenet frame $\{T,N,B\}$ and
constant $\kappa>0$, $\tau$. The Frenet–Serret equations are

$$
T'=\kappa N,\qquad N'=-\kappa T+\tau B,\qquad B'=-\tau N. \tag{1}
$$

**Step 1 — A conserved vector (the axis).** Define

$$
D:=\tau T+\kappa B.
$$

Using (1),

$$
D'=\tau T'+\kappa B'=\tau\kappa N+\kappa(-\tau N)=0.
$$

So $D$ is a **constant** vector, with $|D|^{2}=\tau^{2}+\kappa^{2}=\omega^{2}$.
This is the axis direction of the helix.

**Step 2 — The tangential component along $D$ is constant.** Since $D$ is consta
nt,

$$
(T\cdot D)'=T'\cdot D=\kappa N\cdot(\tau T+\kappa B)=0,
$$

so $T\cdot D$ is constant. At any instant $T\cdot D=\tau\,|T|^{2}+\kappa\,T\cdot
 B=\tau$ (since $T\perp B$, $|T|=1$). Hence

$$
T\cdot D=\tau\quad\text{for all }s. \tag{2}
$$

Decompose $T$ into parts parallel and perpendicular to $D$:

$$
T(s)=\underbrace{\frac{\tau}{\omega^{2}}D}_{\text{constant}}+\;T_{\perp}(s),\qqu
ad T_{\perp}:=T-\frac{\tau}{\omega^{2}}D. \tag{3}
$$

By (2), $T_{\perp}\perp D$, and $|T_{\perp}|^{2}=1-\tau^{2}/\omega^{2}=\kappa^{2
}/\omega^{2}$, so $T_{\perp}$ has **constant** magnitude $\kappa/\omega$ and lie
s in the fixed plane $D^{\perp}$.

**Step 3 — $T_{\perp}$ rotates uniformly.** Since $D$ is constant, $T_{\perp}'=T
'=\kappa N$. Differentiating again and using (1) together with $B=(D-\tau T)/\ka
ppa$:

$$
T_{\perp}''=\kappa N'=\kappa(-\kappa T+\tau B)=-\kappa^{2}T+\tau(D-\tau T)=-\ome
ga^{2}T+\tau D.
$$

Substituting $T=T_{\perp}+\frac{\tau}{\omega^{2}}D$ gives $\tau D=\omega^{2}\cdo
t\frac{\tau}{\omega^{2}}D$, so the $D$-terms cancel:

$$
T_{\perp}''=-\omega^{2}T_{\perp}. \tag{4}
$$

Equation (4) is the harmonic oscillator in the plane $D^{\perp}$. With $|T_{\per
p}|=\kappa/\omega$ constant, the solution is uniform circular motion of radius $
\kappa/\omega$ and angular speed $\omega$: there exist orthonormal $e_{1},e_{2}\
in D^{\perp}$ such that

$$
T_{\perp}(s)=\frac{\kappa}{\omega}\bigl(\cos(\omega s)\,e_{1}+\sin(\omega s)\,e_
{2}\bigr) \tag{5}
$$

(after absorbing a phase into the choice of $e_{1},e_{2}$).

**Step 4 — Integrate to obtain $\alpha$.** From (3) and (5),

$$
T(s)=\frac{\tau}{\omega^{2}}D+\frac{\kappa}{\omega}\bigl(\cos(\omega s)\,e_{1}+\
sin(\omega s)\,e_{2}\bigr).
$$

Integrating,

$$
\alpha(s)=\alpha(0)+\frac{\tau}{\omega^{2}}\,s\,D+\frac{\kappa}{\omega^{2}}\bigl
(\sin(\omega s)\,e_{1}-\cos(\omega s)\,e_{2}\bigr)+\frac{\kappa}{\omega^{2}}\,e_
{2}.
$$

Absorbing the constant $\frac{\kappa}{\omega^{2}}e_{2}+\alpha(0)$ into a transla
tion, and replacing $e_{1},e_{2}$ by a rotated pair to flip the cosine sign, we
obtain the canonical form

$$
\boxed{\;\alpha(s)=\frac{\kappa}{\omega^{2}}\bigl(\sin(\omega s)\,e_{1}-\cos(\om
ega s)\,e_{2}\bigr)+\frac{\tau}{\omega^{2}}\,s\,D\;} \tag{6}
$$

with $\{D,e_{1},e_{2}\}$ orthonormal and $\omega=\sqrt{\kappa^{2}+\tau^{2}}$.

**Step 5 — Verify.** From (6): $T=\frac{\kappa}{\omega^{2}}\omega(\cos\,\,e_{1}+
\sin\,\,e_{2})+\frac{\tau}{\omega^{2}}D=\frac{\kappa}{\omega}(\cos\,\,e_{1}+\sin
\,\,e_{2})+\frac{\tau}{\omega}D$, so $|T|=1$. Then $T'=\kappa(-\sin\,\,e_{1}+\co
s\,\,e_{2})$, giving $|T'|=\kappa$ and $N=(-\sin\,\,e_{1}+\cos\,\,e_{2})$. Compu
ting $B=T\times N=\frac{\tau}{\omega}D\times N+\dots$ one finds $B=\frac{\kappa}
{\omega}D-\frac{\tau}{\omega}(\cos\,\,e_{1}+\sin\,\,e_{2})$, and $B'=-\frac{\tau
}{\omega}\omega(-\sin\,\,e_{1}+\cos\,\,e_{2})=-\tau N$. So curvature $=\kappa$ a
nd torsion $=\tau$, both constant. ✓

**Step 6 — Geometric description and uniqueness.**

- The first term of (6) is a circle of radius $r=\kappa/\omega^{2}$ in the plane
 spanned by $e_{1},e_{2}$ (perpendicular to $D$). The second term is uniform tra
nslation along $D$ at speed $\tau/\omega^{2}$. Thus (6) is a **circular helix**
of radius $\kappa/\omega^{2}$, axis direction $D$, and pitch $2\pi\tau/\omega^{2
}$.

- **$\tau=0$:** then $\omega=\kappa$, $D=\kappa B$ is the (constant) binormal, a
nd (6) reduces to a **circle** of radius $1/\kappa$ in the plane orthogonal to $
D$.

- **Uniqueness:** The Frenet–Serret system (1) is a linear ODE with *constant* c
oefficients for the frame $(T,N,B)$. Its solution is determined by the initial f
rame $(T_{0},N_{0},B_{0})\in\mathrm{SO}(3)$ and the constants $\kappa,\tau$. Two
 different initial frames differ by a rotation $R\in\mathrm{SO}(3)$, which sends
 the entire curve to $R\alpha$; adding $\alpha(0)$ accounts for translation. Hen
ce **up to rigid motion, the curve is uniquely determined by $(\kappa,\tau)$**,
and is given by (6).

This completes the classification. $\blacksquare$
