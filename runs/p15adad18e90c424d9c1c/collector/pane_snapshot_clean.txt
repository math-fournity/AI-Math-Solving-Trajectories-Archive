## Solution

We evaluate the integral
$$I = \int_{0}^{2\pi} e^{\cos\theta}\,\cos(n\theta - \sin\theta)\,d\theta$$
for a non-negative integer $n$.

**Step 1: Embed in a complex exponential.**

Since $e^{\cos\theta}$ is real, $I$ is the real part of
$$\widetilde{I} = \int_{0}^{2\pi} e^{\cos\theta}\,e^{i(n\theta - \sin\theta)}\,d
\theta.$$

Combine the exponents:
$$\cos\theta - i\sin\theta + in\theta = e^{-i\theta} + in\theta,$$
so
$$\widetilde{I} = \int_{0}^{2\pi} e^{\,e^{-i\theta} + in\theta}\,d\theta = \int_
{0}^{2\pi} e^{\,e^{-i\theta}}\,e^{in\theta}\,d\theta.$$

**Step 2: Expand $e^{e^{-i\theta}}$ as a power series.**

$$e^{e^{-i\theta}} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(e^{-i\theta}\right)^k}{k!} =
 \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-ik\theta}}{k!}.$$

This series converges absolutely and uniformly in $\theta$ (since $\sum 1/k!$ co
nverges), so we may interchange sum and integral:
$$\widetilde{I} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{i(n-k)\theta}
\,d\theta.$$

**Step 3: Orthogonality of complex exponentials.**

For integers $n,k$:
$$\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{i(n-k)\theta}\,d\theta = \begin{cases} 2\pi & n = k,\\[4pt]
0 & n \neq k.\end{cases}$$

Hence the entire series collapses to the single term $k=n$:
$$\widetilde{I} = \frac{1}{n!}\cdot 2\pi = \frac{2\pi}{n!}.$$

**Step 4: Take the real part.**

Since $\frac{2\pi}{n!}$ is purely real,
$$I = \operatorname{Re}\!\left(\widetilde{I}\right) = \frac{2\pi}{n!},$$
and the imaginary part $\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{\cos\theta}\sin(n\theta-\sin\theta)\,d
\theta = 0$ as a consistency check.

**Verification ($n=0$):** $I = \int_0^{2\pi}e^{\cos\theta}\cos(\sin\theta)\,d\th
eta = \operatorname{Re}\int_0^{2\pi}e^{e^{-i\theta}}d\theta = 2\pi\cdot\frac{1}{
0!}=2\pi$. ✓

**Result.** For every non-negative integer $n$,

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi} e^{\cos\theta}\cos(n\theta-\sin\theta)\,d\
theta = \frac{2\pi}{n!}.}$$
