# Proof

**Interpretation.** Here $D(u)(p) = \nabla u(p)$ denotes the gradient of $u$ at
$p$, and $\frac{\partial u}{\partial \nu}(p) = \nabla u(p)\cdot \nu(p)$ is the o
utward normal derivative. We assume $U\subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is a bounded open se
t with $C^1$ boundary $\partial U$, and $u\in C^1(\overline{U})$ with $u=0$ on $
\partial U$.

**Claim.** The inequality holds, and in fact **equality** holds:
$$|D(u)(p)| = \left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial \nu}(p)\right|, \qquad p\in\parti
al U.$$

**Proof.** Fix $p\in\partial U$. Let $\tau\in T_p(\partial U)$ be any tangent ve
ctor to $\partial U$ at $p$. Choose a $C^1$ curve $\gamma:(-\varepsilon,\varepsi
lon)\to\partial U$ with $\gamma(0)=p$ and $\gamma'(0)=\tau$. Since $u\equiv 0$ o
n $\partial U$, we have
$$u(\gamma(t)) = 0 \quad\text{for all } t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon).$$
Differentiating at $t=0$ (chain rule, valid since $u\in C^1$):
$$0 = \frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0} u(\gamma(t)) = \nabla u(p)\cdot \gamma'(0) = \nabl
a u(p)\cdot \tau.$$

Since this holds for **every** tangent vector $\tau\in T_p(\partial U)$, the gra
dient $\nabla u(p)$ is orthogonal to the entire tangent space $T_p(\partial U)$.
 The orthogonal complement of $T_p(\partial U)$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ is the one-dim
ensional space spanned by the unit normal $\nu(p)$. Therefore $\nabla u(p)$ is p
arallel to $\nu(p)$:
$$\nabla u(p) = \bigl(\nabla u(p)\cdot \nu(p)\bigr)\,\nu(p) = \frac{\partial u}{
\partial \nu}(p)\,\nu(p).$$

Taking Euclidean norms and using $|\nu(p)|=1$:
$$|D(u)(p)| = |\nabla u(p)| = \left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial \nu}(p)\right|\cd
ot|\nu(p)| = \left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial \nu}(p)\right|.$$

Hence
$$|D(u)(p)| \leq \left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial \nu}(p)\right|,$$
with **equality**. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The essential ingredient is that $u=0$ on $\partial U$ forces the ta
ngential component of $\nabla u$ to vanish on the boundary, leaving only the nor
mal component. Thus on $\partial U$ the full gradient magnitude equals the magni
tude of the normal derivative.
