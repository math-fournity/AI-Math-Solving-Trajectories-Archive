## Spectrum of the Dirichlet and Neumann Laplacians on $[0,\infty)$

**Setup.** Both $T$ (Dirichlet) and $S$ (Neumann) act as $-d^2/dx^2$ on $L^2[0,\
infty)$. The minimal symmetric operator $A_0 = -d^2/dx^2$ with $D(A_0) = C_c^\in
fty(0,\infty)$ has deficiency indices $(1,1)$, so its self-adjoint extensions ar
e parametrized by a boundary condition at $x=0$. The Dirichlet condition $f(0)=0
$ and Neumann condition $f'(0)=0$ each yield a self-adjoint extension. Thus $T$
and $S$ are self-adjoint, and their spectra are real.

---

**Step 1: Non-negativity — $\sigma(T), \sigma(S) \subseteq [0,\infty)$.**

For $f \in D(T)$ (Dirichlet), integration by parts gives
$$\langle Tf, f\rangle = \int_0^\infty (-f'')\overline{f}\,dx = \int_0^\infty |f
'|^2\,dx \geq 0,$$
where the boundary term $[-f'\bar f]_0^\infty$ vanishes: $f(0)=0$ by the Dirichl
et condition, and $f(x)\to 0$ as $x\to\infty$ since $f\in H^1[0,\infty)\subset L
^2$. The same computation holds for $f\in D(S)$ (Neumann) since $f'(0)=0$. Hence
 both operators are non-negative, so $\sigma \subseteq [0,\infty)$.

---

**Step 2: No eigenvalues.**

*Case $\lambda < 0$.* Write $\lambda = -\mu^2$, $\mu > 0$. The general solution
of $-f'' = -\mu^2 f$ is $f(x) = Ae^{-\mu x} + Be^{\mu x}$. The $L^2[0,\infty)$ c
ondition forces $B=0$, so $f(x) = Ae^{-\mu x}$. For **Dirichlet**: $f(0) = A = 0
$, so $f\equiv 0$. For **Neumann**: $f'(0) = -\mu A = 0$, so $A=0$ and $f\equiv
0$. No negative eigenvalues for either operator.

*Case $\lambda = 0$.* Solutions of $-f''=0$ are $f(x) = ax + b$. The $L^2$ condi
tion forces $a=0$, leaving $f = b$ (constant). But a nonzero constant is not in
$L^2[0,\infty)$. So $\lambda=0$ is not an eigenvalue for either operator.

*Case $\lambda > 0$.* Write $\lambda = k^2$, $k>0$. The general solution is $f(x
) = A\sin(kx) + B\cos(kx)$, which is not in $L^2[0,\infty)$ unless $A=B=0$. So n
o positive eigenvalues.

**Conclusion:** Neither $T$ nor $S$ has any eigenvalues; the point spectrum is e
mpty.

**Step 3: $[0,\infty) \subseteq \sigma$ — the spectrum fills the entire half-lin
e.**

We use **Weyl's criterion**: $\lambda \in \sigma(A)$ iff there exists a sequence
 $(f_n)$ with $\|f_n\|=1$ and $\|(A-\lambda)f_n\|\to 0$.

*Subcase $\lambda = k_0^2 > 0$.* Let $\varphi \in C_c^\infty([0,\infty))$ with $
\varphi \equiv 1$ on $[0,1]$, $\operatorname{supp}\varphi \subseteq [0,2]$. For
**Dirichlet**, also require $\varphi(0)=0$; for **Neumann**, require $\varphi'(0
)=0$. (Such $\varphi$ is easily constructed.) Define
$$f_n(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\,g(x)\,\varphi(x/n),$$
where $g(x) = \sin(k_0 x)$ (Dirichlet) or $g(x) = \cos(k_0 x)$ (Neumann). Note $
f_n(0)=0$ in the Dirichlet case (since $g(0)=0$ and $\varphi(0)=0$) and $f_n'(0)
=0$ in the Neumann case (since $g'(0)=0$ and $\varphi'(0)=0$), so $f_n$ lies in
the appropriate domain. Also $f_n \in H^2 \cap H_0^1$ (resp. $H^2$ with $f_n'(0)
=0$).

**Norm:** By the substitution $u = x/n$ and the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma (the aver
age of $|g|^2$ over a period is $1/2$),
$$\|f_n\|^2 = \frac{1}{n}\int_0^{2n} |g(x)|^2 |\varphi(x/n)|^2\,dx = \int_0^2 |g
(nu)|^2|\varphi(u)|^2\,du \;\longrightarrow\; \tfrac{1}{2}\int_0^2|\varphi(u)|^2
\,du > 0.$$

**Residual:** Since $g'' = -k_0^2 g$,
$$(A - k_0^2)f_n = -f_n'' - k_0^2 f_n = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Big[-2g'(x)\frac{\var
phi'(x/n)}{n} - g(x)\frac{\varphi''(x/n)}{n^2}\Big].$$
Hence
$$\|(A-k_0^2)f_n\|^2 \leq \frac{C_1}{n^2}\int_0^2|g'(nu)|^2|\varphi'(u)|^2\,du +
 \frac{C_2}{n^4}\to 0.$$
By Weyl's criterion, $k_0^2 \in \sigma(A)$ for every $k_0 > 0$.

*Subcase $\lambda = 0$.* Take $\varphi \in C_c^\infty([0,\infty))$ with $\varphi
(0)=0$ (Dirichlet) or $\varphi'(0)=0$ (Neumann), $\varphi \not\equiv 0$, and set
 $f_n(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\varphi(x/n)$. Then $f_n$ is in the appropriate dom
ain, $\|f_n\|^2 = \int_0^\infty|\varphi(u)|^2\,du > 0$, and
$$\|Af_n\|^2 = \frac{1}{n^5}\int_0^\infty|\varphi''(x/n)|^2\,dx = \frac{1}{n^4}\
int_0^\infty|\varphi''(u)|^2\,du \to 0.$$
By Weyl's criterion, $0 \in \sigma(A)$.

**Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 1–3:

$$\boxed{\sigma(T) = \sigma(S) = [0,\infty),}$$

and in both cases the spectrum is **purely continuous** (purely absolutely conti
nuous): there are no eigenvalues, and the spectral measure is absolutely continu
ous with multiplicity one on $(0,\infty)$.
