## Proof

**Setup and interpretation.** We have:
- $B \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ homeomorphic to the closed unit disk $D^2$.
- $h: \partial B \to \partial B$ a homeomorphism of the boundary.
- $F: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ a homeomorphism mapping $D^2$ onto $B$, and
 equal to the identity outside some disk.
- $g: D^2 \to D^2$ a homeomorphism of the unit disk whose restriction to $\parti
al D^2 = S^1$ agrees with $F^{-1} \circ h \circ F$ (such a $g$ exists by the Ale
xander trick, since every homeomorphism of $S^1$ extends to a homeomorphism of $
D^2$).

The map $F \circ g \circ F^{-1}: B \to B$ is a homeomorphism of $B$ whose restri
ction to $\partial B$ equals $h$. We claim it extends to a homeomorphism of $\ma
thbb{R}^2$.

**Step 1: Extend $g$ to a homeomorphism of $\mathbb{R}^2$.**

Since $F$ is a homeomorphism of all of $\mathbb{R}^2$ (it is a homeomorphism on
$D^2$ and the identity outside a disk, gluing continuously), it suffices to exte
nd the disk homeomorphism $g: D^2 \to D^2$ to a homeomorphism $\widetilde{g}: \m
athbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$, for then $F \circ \widetilde{g} \circ F^{-1}$ will
 be the desired extension.

Define $\widetilde{g}: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ by **radial (Alexander) ex
tension**:

$$\widetilde{g}(x) = \begin{cases} g(x) & \text{if } |x| \leq 1, \\ |x| \cdot g\
!\left(\dfrac{x}{|x|}\right) & \text{if } |x| \geq 1. \end{cases}$$

Here $g(x/|x|)$ denotes the value of $g$ on the unit circle in the direction of
$x$, which is well-defined since $g$ is defined on all of $D^2 \supset S^1$.

**Step 2: $\widetilde{g}$ is well-defined and continuous.**

On the interface $|x| = 1$, both formulas give $g(x)$, so $\widetilde{g}$ is wel
l-defined. It is continuous on the closed disk $\{|x| \leq 1\}$ (where it equals
 $g$) and on the exterior $\{|x| \geq 1\}$ (where it is a composition of continu
ous maps: the radial projection $x \mapsto x/|x|$, the map $g|_{S^1}$, and scali
ng by $|x|$). By the pasting lemma, $\widetilde{g}$ is continuous on all of $\ma
thbb{R}^2$.

**Step 3: $\widetilde{g}$ is a bijection with continuous inverse.**

Construct the inverse $\widetilde{g}^{-1}$ by the same radial extension applied
to $g^{-1}: D^2 \to D^2$:

$$\widetilde{g}^{-1}(y) = \begin{cases} g^{-1}(y) & \text{if } |y| \leq 1, \\ |y
| \cdot g^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{y}{|y|}\right) & \text{if } |y| \geq 1. \end{cases}
$$

For $|x| \geq 1$: $\widetilde{g}(x) = |x| \cdot g(x/|x|)$, so $|\widetilde{g}(x)
| = |x|$ (since $g(x/|x|) \in S^1$) and $\widetilde{g}(x)/|\widetilde{g}(x)| = g
(x/|x|)$. Then

$$\widetilde{g}^{-1}(\widetilde{g}(x)) = |\widetilde{g}(x)| \cdot g^{-1}\!\left(
\frac{\widetilde{g}(x)}{|\widetilde{g}(x)|}\right) = |x| \cdot g^{-1}(g(x/|x|))
= |x| \cdot \frac{x}{|x|} = x.$$

Similarly $\widetilde{g}(\widetilde{g}^{-1}(y)) = y$ for $|y| \geq 1$, and on $D
^2$ both compositions are the identity since $g$ and $g^{-1}$ are inverses there
. Thus $\widetilde{g}$ is a bijection with continuous inverse, hence a homeomorp
hism of $\mathbb{R}^2$.

**Step 4: Conjugate to obtain the extension of $F \circ g \circ F^{-1}$.**

Since $F: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ and $\widetilde{g}: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \m
athbb{R}^2$ are both homeomorphisms, the composition

$$\Phi := F \circ \widetilde{g} \circ F^{-1}: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$$

is a homeomorphism of $\mathbb{R}^2$.

On $B = F(D^2)$, every point $y \in B$ can be written as $y = F(x)$ with $x \in
D^2$, and $\widetilde{g}(x) = g(x)$ (since $|x| \leq 1$). Therefore:

$$\Phi(y) = F(\widetilde{g}(F^{-1}(y))) = F(g(F^{-1}(y))) = (F \circ g \circ F^{
-1})(y).$$

So $\Phi|_B = F \circ g \circ F^{-1}$, meaning $\Phi$ is an extension of $F \cir
c g \circ F^{-1}$ to a homeomorphism of all of $\mathbb{R}^2$. $\square$

**Conclusion:** Yes, $F \circ g \circ F^{-1}$ can be extended to a homeomorphism
 of the entire $\mathbb{R}^2$. The key ingredients are (1) the Alexander/radial
extension of any disk homeomorphism $g: D^2 \to D^2$ to a plane homeomorphism $\
widetilde{g}: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$, and (2) the fact that $F$ is itsel
f a homeomorphism of $\mathbb{R}^2$, so conjugation $F \circ \widetilde{g} \circ
 F^{-1}$ preserves the property of being a plane homeomorphism while agreeing wi
th $F \circ g \circ F^{-1}$ on $B$.
