## Problem

Find the envelope of the family of circles whose centers lie on the parabola $y
= x^2$ and which pass through the vertex $(0,0)$.

## Solution

**Setting up the family.** A point on the parabola $y=x^2$ has coordinates $(a,
a^2)$, where $a$ is a real parameter. The circle centered at $(a, a^2)$ passing
through the origin $(0,0)$ has radius

$$r = \sqrt{a^2 + a^4} = |a|\sqrt{1+a^2}, \qquad r^2 = a^2(1+a^2).$$

Hence the family of circles is

$$(x-a)^2 + (y-a^2)^2 = a^2 + a^4.$$

**Expanding.** Expanding the left side:

$$x^2 - 2ax + a^2 + y^2 - 2a^2 y + a^4 = a^2 + a^4.$$

The $a^2$ and $a^4$ terms cancel, giving the clean one-parameter family

$$\boxed{F(x,y,a) \;=\; x^2 + y^2 - 2ax - 2a^2 y \;=\; 0.} \tag{1}$$

Note the simplification: the parameter $a$ enters only through the linear term $
-2ax$ and the quadratic term $-2a^2 y$.

**Envelope conditions.** The envelope of a one-parameter family $F(x,y,a)=0$ is
obtained by eliminating $a$ from

$$F = 0, \qquad \frac{\partial F}{\partial a} = 0. \tag{2}$$

Compute the partial derivative:

$$\frac{\partial F}{\partial a} = -2x - 4ay = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = -2
ay \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a = -\frac{x}{2y}, \qquad (y \neq 0). \tag{3}$$

**Substituting back.** Insert $a = -x/(2y)$ into (1). We compute each term:

- $-2ax = -2\!\left(-\dfrac{x}{2y}\right)\!x = \dfrac{x^2}{y}$,
- $-2a^2 y = -2\!\left(\dfrac{x^2}{4y^2}\right)\!y = -\dfrac{x^2}{2y}$.

So (1) becomes

$$x^2 + y^2 + \frac{x^2}{y} - \frac{x^2}{2y} = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x^2 +
 y^2 + \frac{x^2}{2y} = 0.$$

Multiplying through by $2y$ (valid since $y \neq 0$ in this branch):

$$2x^2 y + 2y^3 + x^2 = 0,$$

which rearranges to

$$\boxed{\,x^2(2y+1) + 2y^3 = 0\,} \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \boxed{\,x^2
= -\frac{2y^3}{\,2y+1\,}\,}. \tag{4}$$

**The degenerate case $y = 0$.** When $y = 0$, condition (3) gives $x = 0$, and
then (1) gives $F(0,0,a) = 0$ for **every** value of $a$. Thus the origin $(0,0)
$ is a *singular* point of the family: every circle of the family passes through
 it. It is the degenerate component of the envelope — the locus where all member
s of the family meet.

**Verification that (4) is genuinely tangent to each member.** Fix a parameter v
alue $a$. The envelope point corresponding to $a$ is obtained from (3): $x = -2a
y$, and substituting into (4):

$$4a^2 y^2(2y+1) + 2y^3 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2y\bigl[2a^2(2y+1) + y^2\b
igr] = 0.$$

Discarding $y=0$ (the degenerate origin), we solve

$$y^2 + 4a^2 y + 2a^2 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y = -2a^2 \pm a\sqrt{4a^2 -
2} \cdot\frac{1}{\,1\,}\dots$$

More cleanly, the quadratic $y^2 + 4a^2 y + 2a^2 = 0$ has discriminant $16a^4 -
8a^2 = 8a^2(2a^2-1)$, which is non-negative for $|a| \geq 1/\sqrt{2}$. For each
such $a$, the envelope point $(x,y)=(-2ay,\, y)$ lies on both the circle (1) and
 the curve (4), and since it is obtained from the simultaneous system $F = \part
ial_a F = 0$, the circle and the envelope share the same tangent line there. Thi
s confirms (4) is the envelope (the locus of points of tangency), with the origi
n as the degenerate member.

**Geometric description.** Equation (4), $x^2(2y+1) = -2y^3$, is a **semicubical
-cusp-type curve** (a rational algebraic curve). It is defined for $-1/2 < y \le
q 0$ (the right side $-2y^3 \geq 0$ requires $y \leq 0$, and the denominator $2y
+1 > 0$ requires $y > -1/2$), symmetric about the $y$-axis, with a cusp at the o
rigin and asymptotic behavior $y \to -1/2$ as $|x| \to \infty$.

## Result

The envelope of the family of circles centered on $y = x^2$ and passing through
the vertex $(0,0)$ is

$$\boxed{\,x^2(2y+1) + 2y^3 = 0\,}$$

together with the degenerate point $(0,0)$ (through which every member of the fa
mily passes).
