## Solution

The recurrence is $5x_{i+1} - x_i - 3x_i x_{i+1} = 1$. Solving for $x_{i+1}$:

$$x_{i+1}(5 - 3x_i) = 1 + x_i \implies x_{i+1} = \frac{1 + x_i}{5 - 3x_i}.$$

This is the Möbius transformation $f(x) = \frac{1+x}{5-3x}$, with associated mat
rix

$$M = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -3 & 5 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \operatorname{tr}(M)=
6,\ \det(M)=8.$$

The condition $x_1 = x_{43}$ means $f^{42}(x_1) = x_1$, i.e., $x_1$ is a fixed p
oint of $f^{42}$.

**Diagonalizing $M$.** The eigenvalues satisfy $\lambda^2 - 6\lambda + 8 = 0$, g
iving $\lambda = 4$ and $\lambda = 2$ (distinct). Hence

$$M^n = P\begin{pmatrix}4^n & 0 \\ 0 & 2^n\end{pmatrix}P^{-1}.$$

Since $4^{42} \ne 2^{42}$, the matrix $M^{42}$ is **not** a scalar multiple of t
he identity, so $f^{42}$ is not the identity map. Therefore $f^{42}(x)=x$ is a g
enuine quadratic equation with exactly two roots.

**Fixed points of $f$.** Setting $f(x) = x$:

$$\frac{1+x}{5-3x} = x \implies 1+x = 5x - 3x^2 \implies 3x^2 - 4x + 1 = 0 \impl
ies (3x-1)(x-1)=0,$$

so the fixed points are $x = 1$ and $x = \tfrac{1}{3}$.

**Fixed points of $f^{42}$ equal fixed points of $f$.** Since $M$ has two distin
ct eigenvalues, $f$ is conjugate (over $\mathbb{R}$) to $g(y) = \mu y$ with $\mu
 = 4/2 = 2 \ne 1$. Then $f^{42}$ is conjugate to $g^{42}(y) = 2^{42}y$, whose fi
xed points are exactly $y = 0$ and $y = \infty$ — the same two fixed points as $
g$, hence the same two fixed points as $f$. Thus the only solutions to $f^{42}(x
_1) = x_1$ are $x_1 = 1$ and $x_1 = \tfrac{1}{3}$.

**Case 1: $x_1 = 1$.** Then $x_2 = \frac{2}{2} = 1$, and inductively all $x_i =
1$. The sum is

$$S_1 = 42 \cdot 1 = 42.$$

**Case 2: $x_1 = \tfrac{1}{3}$.** Then $x_2 = \frac{1+\frac13}{5 - 3\cdot\frac13
} = \frac{4/3}{4} = \frac{1}{3}$, and inductively all $x_i = \tfrac{1}{3}$. The
sum is

$$S_2 = 42 \cdot \tfrac{1}{3} = 14.$$

**Product of all possible values:**

$$S_1 \cdot S_2 = 42 \times 14 = \boxed{588}.$$
