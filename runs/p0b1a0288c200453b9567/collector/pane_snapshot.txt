## Proof

**Step 1: Simplify the constraint system.**

The manifold $M$ is defined by:
$$f_1: 2x^2 + 2 = z^2 + w^2, \qquad f_2: 3x^2 + y^2 = z^2 + w^2.$$

Subtracting $f_1$ from $f_2$:
$$(3x^2 + y^2) - (2x^2 + 2) = 0 \implies x^2 + y^2 = 2.$$

Substituting back into $f_1$:
$$z^2 + w^2 = 2x^2 + 2 = 2(x^2 + 1).$$

So $M$ is equivalently described by:
$$x^2 + y^2 = 2, \qquad z^2 + w^2 = 2(x^2+1).$$

**Step 2: Verify $M$ is a smooth 2-manifold (Jacobian has full rank on $M$).**

The gradients of the two defining functions are:
$$\nabla f_1 = (4x,\; 0,\; -2z,\; -2w), \qquad \nabla f_2 = (6x,\; 2y,\; -2z,\;
-2w).$$

Suppose these are linearly dependent at some point of $M$: $\nabla f_2 = \lambda
\, \nabla f_1$ for some $\lambda$.

- **If $\lambda = 1$:** then $6x = 4x \Rightarrow x = 0$ and $2y = 0 \Rightarrow
 y = 0$, contradicting $x^2 + y^2 = 2$.
- **If $\lambda \neq 1$:** then from the $z$- and $w$-components, $-2z = -2\lamb
da z$ and $-2w = -2\lambda w$ force $z = 0$ and $w = 0$, contradicting $z^2 + w^
2 = 2(x^2+1) \geq 2 > 0$.

Both cases yield contradictions, so the Jacobian has rank 2 everywhere on $M$. B
y the regular level set theorem, $M$ is a smooth closed 2-dimensional submanifol
d of $\mathbb{R}^4$.

**Step 3: Identify the topology of $M$.**

The first constraint $x^2 + y^2 = 2$ forces $(x, y)$ to lie on a circle $S^1$ of
 radius $\sqrt{2}$.

For each such $(x, y)$, the second constraint forces $(z, w)$ to lie on a circle
 $S^1$ of radius $\sqrt{2(x^2+1)}$. Since $x^2 + 1 \geq 1 > 0$ for all $x$, this
 radius is **always strictly positive** and varies smoothly with $x$.

Define the map
$$\Phi: S^1 \times S^1 \to M, \qquad \Phi(\theta, \phi) = \bigl(\sqrt{2}\cos\the
ta,\; \sqrt{2}\sin\theta,\; \sqrt{2(\cos^2\theta \cdot 2 + 1)}\cos\phi,\; \sqrt{
2(2\cos^2\theta+1)}\sin\phi\bigr).$$

Wait — let me reparametrize cleanly. Let $x = \sqrt{2}\cos\theta$, $y = \sqrt{2}
\sin\theta$. Then $x^2 + y^2 = 2$ is satisfied, and
$$z^2 + w^2 = 2(2\cos^2\theta + 1).$$

Set $R(\theta) = \sqrt{2(2\cos^2\theta + 1)} > 0$, and $z = R(\theta)\cos\phi$,
$w = R(\theta)\sin\phi$. This gives a smooth bijection
$$\Phi: S^1 \times S^1 \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} M$$
with smooth inverse (recover $\theta$ from $(x,y)$, then $\phi$ from $(z,w)$). H
ence $M$ is **diffeomorphic to the 2-torus** $T^2 = S^1 \times S^1$.

**Step 4: Compute the Euler characteristic.**

The Euler characteristic is a topological invariant, so
$$\chi(M) = \chi(S^1 \times S^1) = \chi(S^1)\cdot\chi(S^1) = 0 \cdot 0 = 0.$$

Equivalently, the torus admits a cell decomposition with $b_0 = 1$, $b_1 = 2$, $
b_2 = 1$, giving $\chi = 1 - 2 + 1 = 0$.

$$\boxed{\chi(M) = 0}$$
