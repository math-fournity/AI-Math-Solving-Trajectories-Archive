## Proof

**Answer: Yes, the two constructions are isomorphic (in fact, canonically).**

Let me set up notation. Write $[M]$ for the isomorphism class $\langle M\rangle$
 in $\mathrm{Proj}(A)$. The monoid operation is $[M]+[N]=[M\times N]=[M\oplus N]
$ (finite products and coproducts coincide for modules). The unit is $[0]$.

---

**Construction 1.** Let $F$ be the free abelian group on the set $\mathrm{Proj}(
A)$, and let $R\subseteq F$ be the subgroup generated by
$$[M_2]-[M_1]-[M_3]$$
for every short exact sequence $0\to M_1\to M_2\to M_3\to 0$ of finitely generat
ed projective $A$-modules. Then $K_0^{(1)}(A)=F/R$.

**Construction 2.** This is the Grothendieck group (group completion) of the com
mutative monoid $\mathrm{Proj}(A)$. Equivalently (standard fact for any commutat
ive monoid), it is the quotient $F/S$ where $S\subseteq F$ is the subgroup gener
ated by
$$[M\oplus N]-[M]-[N]\qquad\text{for all }[M],[N]\in\mathrm{Proj}(A).$$
The equivalence-relation description in the problem (formal differences modulo s
tabilization by some $[P]$) yields exactly this same group; this is the classica
l equivalence between the "formal differences with cancellation" and "free abeli
an group modulo additivity" presentations of the Grothendieck group of a commuta
tive monoid.

So it suffices to prove $R=S$.

---

**$S\subseteq R$.** For any $[M],[N]\in\mathrm{Proj}(A)$, the sequence
$$0\to M\xrightarrow{i}M\oplus N\xrightarrow{\pi}N\to 0$$
(with $i$ the inclusion and $\pi$ the projection) is a short exact sequence. Sin
ce $M$ and $N$ are finitely generated projective, so is $M\oplus N$; all three t
erms lie in $\mathrm{Proj}(A)$. Therefore the generator $[M\oplus N]-[M]-[N]$ of
 $S$ belongs to $R$.

**$R\subseteq S$.** Consider any short exact sequence $0\to M_1\to M_2\to M_3\to
 0$ with $M_1,M_2,M_3$ finitely generated projective. Since $M_3$ is projective,
 this sequence **splits**: there is a section $M_3\to M_2$, giving $M_2\cong M_1
\oplus M_3$. Hence $[M_2]=[M_1\oplus M_3]$ in $\mathrm{Proj}(A)$, and
$$[M_2]-[M_1]-[M_3]=[M_1\oplus M_3]-[M_1]-[M_3]\in S.$$
Thus every generator of $R$ lies in $S$.

---

Since $R=S$, we have $K_0^{(1)}(A)=F/R=F/S=K_0^{(2)}(A)$ as quotients of the *sa
me* free abelian group by the *same* subgroup. The two constructions are therefo
re not merely isomorphic but **identical** (hence canonically isomorphic).

**Remark on hypotheses.** The argument uses only that (i) every short exact sequ
ence appearing in Construction 1 has projective middle and quotient terms (so it
 splits), and (ii) direct sums of f.g. projectives are f.g. projective (so the s
plit sequence $0\to M\to M\oplus N\to N\to 0$ is admissible in Construction 1).
Both hold for any ring with $1$, commutative or not. The "possibly commutative"
qualifier is immaterial.
