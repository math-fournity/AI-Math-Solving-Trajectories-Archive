**Step 1: Characterize the product $\sigma$-algebra.**

The product $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{L}(\Omega) \otimes \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N}
)$ is generated by all rectangles $A \times B$ with $A \in \mathcal{L}(\Omega)$
and $B \subseteq \mathbb{N}$. Since every $B \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ satisfies $B
= \bigcup_{i \in B} \{i\}$, every generating rectangle decomposes as
$$A \times B = \bigcup_{i \in B} (A \times \{i\}).$$
Hence the product $\sigma$-algebra is generated by the "slice-rectangles" $\{A \
times \{i\} : A \in \mathcal{L}(\Omega),\; i \in \mathbb{N}\}$.

Now consider the collection
$$\mathcal{C} := \left\{ \bigcup_{i \in \mathbb{N}} (A_i \times \{i\}) : A_i \in
 \mathcal{L}(\Omega) \text{ for each } i \right\}.$$
Every element of $\mathcal{C}$ is a countable union of slice-rectangles, so $\ma
thcal{C} \subseteq \mathcal{L}(\Omega) \otimes \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})$. Convers
ely, $\mathcal{C}$ is itself a $\sigma$-algebra:
- **Closed under complement:** $\left(\bigcup_i A_i \times \{i\}\right)^c = \big
cup_i (A_i^c \times \{i\}) \in \mathcal{C}$.
- **Closed under countable unions:** $\bigcup_n \left(\bigcup_i A_i^{(n)} \times
 \{i\}\right) = \bigcup_i \left(\bigcup_n A_i^{(n)} \times \{i\}\right) \in \mat
hcal{C}$.

Since $\mathcal{C}$ contains all generators $A \times \{i\}$, we get $\mathcal{L
}(\Omega) \otimes \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N}) \subseteq \mathcal{C}$. Therefore
$$\mathcal{L}(\Omega) \otimes \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N}) = \mathcal{C} = \left\{ \b
igcup_{i \in \mathbb{N}} (A_i \times \{i\}) : A_i \in \mathcal{L}(\Omega) \right

$$f^{-1}(E) = \{(x, i) \in \Omega \times \mathbb{N} : f(x, i) \in E\} = \bigcup_

Since $f^{-1}(E) \in \mathcal{L}(\Omega) \otimes \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})$ for ev
