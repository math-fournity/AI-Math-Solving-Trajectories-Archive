**Proof.**

rak{g}=T_{e}G$ its Lie algebra. For $x\in G$, denote by $\mu_{x}:G\to G$ the lef
t translation $y\mapsto xy$, a diffeomorphism with inverse $\mu_{x^{-1}}$.

We must show that the map
\[
\{\text{left-invariant } \omega\in\Omega^{n}(G)\}\;\longrightarrow\;\bigwedge^{n
}\mathfrak{g}^{*},\qquad \omega\mapsto \omega_{e},
\]
is a bijection. We prove injectivity (uniqueness) and surjectivity (existence) s

**Uniqueness (injectivity).** Suppose $\omega$ is left-invariant, i.e. $\mu_{x}^
{*}\omega=\omega$ for all $x\in G$. Evaluating this equality at $y=e$ on tangent

\[
(\mu_{x}^{*}\omega)_{e}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})=\omega_{e}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n}).
\]
By definition of pullback, the left side equals
\[
\omega_{\mu_{x}(e)}\big((d\mu_{x})_{e}v_{1},\dots,(d\mu_{x})_{e}v_{n}\big)=\omeg
a_{x}\big((d\mu_{x})_{e}v_{1},\dots,(d\mu_{x})_{e}v_{n}\big).
\]
Hence
\[
\omega_{x}\big((d\mu_{x})_{e}v_{1},\dots,(d\mu_{x})_{e}v_{n}\big)=\omega_{e}(v_{
1},\dots,v_{n}).
\]
Since $(d\mu_{x})_{e}:\mathfrak{g}\to T_{x}G$ is a linear isomorphism (because $
\mu_{x}$ is a diffeomorphism), every $n$-tuple of tangent vectors at $x$ is of t
he form $\big((d\mu_{x})_{e}v_{1},\dots,(d\mu_{x})_{e}v_{n}\big)$ for a unique $
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}$. Therefore the above formula completely determ
ines $\omega_{x}$ for every $x\in G$ in terms of $\omega_{e}$. Explicitly, for $
w_{1},\dots,w_{n}\in T_{x}G$:
\[
\omega_{x}(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})=\omega_{e}\big((d\mu_{x^{-1}})_{x}w_{1},\dots,(d\m
u_{x^{-1}})_{x}w_{n}\big).
\]
Thus two left-invariant $n$-forms with the same value at $e$ coincide at every p
oint, hence are equal. $\square$

**Existence (surjectivity).** Conversely, given any $\alpha\in\bigwedge^{n}\math
frak{g}^{*}$, define an $n$-form $\omega$ on $G$ by
\[
\omega_{x}(w_{1},\dots,w_{n}):=\alpha\big((d\mu_{x^{-1}})_{x}w_{1},\dots,(d\mu_{
x^{-1}})_{x}w_{n}\big),\qquad w_{i}\in T_{x}G.
\]
This is well defined and smooth because the map $(x,w)\mapsto (d\mu_{x^{-1}})_{x
}w$ is smooth (it is the differential of the smooth map $(x,y)\mapsto x^{-1}y$).
 By construction $\omega_{e}=\alpha$. We verify left-invariance: for $a\in G$,
\[
(\mu_{a}^{*}\omega)_{y}(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})=\omega_{ay}\big((d\mu_{a})_{y}u_{1},\
dots,(d\mu_{a})_{y}u_{n}\big).
\]
Applying the definition with $x=ay$ and $w_{i}=(d\mu_{a})_{y}u_{i}$:
\[
=\alpha\big((d\mu_{(ay)^{-1}})_{ay}(d\mu_{a})_{y}u_{1},\dots\big).
\]
Since $(ay)^{-1}=y^{-1}a^{-1}$, we have $\mu_{(ay)^{-1}}\circ\mu_{a}=\mu_{y^{-1}
}$, so by the chain rule
\[
(d\mu_{(ay)^{-1}})_{ay}\circ(d\mu_{a})_{y}=(d\mu_{y^{-1}})_{y}.
\]
Therefore
\[
(\mu_{a}^{*}\omega)_{y}(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})=\alpha\big((d\mu_{y^{-1}})_{y}u_{1},\
dots,(d\mu_{y^{-1}})_{y}u_{n}\big)=\omega_{y}(u_{1},\dots,u_{n}).
\]
Hence $\mu_{a}^{*}\omega=\omega$, so $\omega$ is left-invariant. $\square$

**Conclusion.** The evaluation map $\omega\mapsto\omega_{e}$ is a linear isomorp
hism between the space of left-invariant $n$-forms on $G$ and $\bigwedge^{n}\mat
hfrak{g}^{*}$. In particular, a left-invariant form $\omega$ is uniquely determi

\[
\omega_{x}=(d\mu_{x})_{e}\cdot\omega_{e}=(\mu_{x}^{-1})^{*}\omega_{e},
\]
i.e. $\omega_{x}$ is the pullback of $\omega_{e}$ along the inverse of left tran
slation by $x$. The geometric reason is that left translations act transitively
on $G$ (every point is the image of $e$ under some $\mu_{x}$), so a form equivar
iant under this transitive action is fixed everywhere once it is fixed at one po
int.
