**Key idea.** Write each cosine in exponential form:

$$\prod_{k=1}^{m}\cos(kx)=\frac{1}{2^{m}}\prod_{k=1}^{m}\bigl(e^{ikx}+e^{-ikx}\b
igr)=\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,+1\}^{m}}e^{i\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\v
arepsilon_k k\right)x}.$$

Integrating over $[0,2\pi]$ kills every term with nonzero frequency, and $\int_0
^{2\pi}1\,dx=2\pi$. Hence

$$I_m=\frac{2\pi}{2^{m}}\,N(m),$$

where $N(m)$ is the number of sign vectors $\varepsilon\in\{-1,+1\}^{m}$ with $\
sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_k k=0$, i.e. the number of ways to split $\{1,2,\dots,m
\}$ into two parts of equal sum.

**Necessary condition.** The total sum is $S_m=\frac{m(m+1)}{2}$. An equal split
 requires $S_m$ even, i.e. $m(m+1)\equiv 0\pmod 4$. Since exactly one of $m,m+1$
 is even, this means the even factor is divisible by $4$:

$$m\equiv 0\pmod 4 \quad\text{or}\quad m\equiv 3\pmod 4.$$

For $1\le m\le 10$ the candidates are $m\in\{3,4,7,8\}$; all other $m$ give $S_m
$ odd, so $N(m)=0$ and $I_m=0$.

**Sufficiency — explicit partitions.** It remains to exhibit, for each candidate
, a sign choice with zero sum (so $N(m)\ge 1$ and $I_m\ne 0$):

| $m$ | $S_m$ | target $S_m/2$ | subset summing to $S_m/2$ | sign vector $\varep
silon$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $3$ | $6$ | $3$ | $\{1,2\}$ | $(+,+,-):\ 1+2-3=0$ |
| $4$ | $10$ | $5$ | $\{1,4\}$ | $(+,-,-,+):\ 1-2-3+4=0$ |
| $7$ | $28$ | $14$ | $\{1,2,4,7\}$ | $(+,+,-,+,-,-,+):\ 1+2-3+4-5-6+7=0$ |
| $8$ | $36$ | $18$ | $\{1,2,3,4,8\}$ | $(+,+,+,+,-,-,-,+):\ 1+2+3+4-5-6-7+8=0$
|

Each row gives a valid zero-sum sign vector, so $N(m)\ge 1$ and $I_m\ne 0$ for $
m\in\{3,4,7,8\}$.

**Conclusion.** The integers $m$ with $1\le m\le 10$ and $I_m\ne 0$ are

$$\boxed{m\in\{3,\,4,\,7,\,8\}}.$$
