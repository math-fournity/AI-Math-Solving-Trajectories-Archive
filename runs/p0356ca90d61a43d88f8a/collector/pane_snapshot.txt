**Claim.** Let $K$ be the smallest subfield of $\mathbb{C}$ containing all roots

**Proof.**

Every root of unity is a root of some $x^n - 1 \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$, hence is alge

all roots of unity is contained in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, the field of all alg
ebraic numbers (the algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}$ inside $\mathbb{C}$):

$$K \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{Q}}.$$

Now $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ is countable: there are only countably many polynomi
als in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$, each with finitely many roots. Hence $K$, being a subfie
ld of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, is also countable.

On the other hand, $\mathbb{C}$ is uncountable. Thus

$$\mathbb{C} \supsetneq \overline{\mathbb{Q}} \supseteq K,$$

so there exist elements of $\mathbb{C}$ that do not lie in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}
}$.
