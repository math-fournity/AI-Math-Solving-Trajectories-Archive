## Reformulation

For a direction $u$ (unit vector), let $M(u) = \max_{x\in S}\langle x,u\rangle$
and $m(u) = \min_{x\in S}\langle x,u\rangle$, with width $w(u)=M(u)-m(u)$. The m
idline $L$ satisfies $\langle x,u\rangle = \frac{M(u)+m(u)}{2}$. The band $B_S(K
,t)$ is the set of $x$ with $|\langle x,u\rangle - \frac{M(u)+m(u)}{2}| \le \fra
c{t}{2}w(u)$, i.e.,

$$\langle x,u\rangle \in \left[\frac{2m(u)+M(u)}{3}\cdot\frac{3}{1}\bigg|_{t=1/3
},\;\ldots\right]$$

More generally, the condition $\langle x,u\rangle \in [m(u)+\frac{t}{3}\cdot\fra
c{3}{1}w(u),\; M(u)-\frac{t}{3}\cdot\frac{3}{1}w(u)]$ simplifies at $t=1/3$ to:

$$\frac{2m(u)+M(u)}{3} \;\le\; \langle x,u\rangle \;\le\; \frac{m(u)+2M(u)}{3}.$
$

That is, $\langle x,u\rangle$ lies in the **middle third** of the projection int
erval $[m(u),M(u)]$.

**The problem reduces to:** Find the smallest $t$ such that for every convex bod
y $S\subset\mathbb{R}^2$, there exists $p\in S$ with $\langle p,u\rangle$ in the
 middle $t$-fraction of $[m(u),M(u)]$ for every direction $u$.

I claim the answer is $t = \frac{1}{3}$.

## Upper bound: $t = \frac{1}{3}$ always works

**Theorem.** For every convex body $S\subset\mathbb{R}^2$, there exists $p\in S$
 such that for all unit vectors $u$,
$$\frac{2m(u)+M(u)}{3} \le \langle p,u\rangle \le \frac{m(u)+2M(u)}{3}.$$

**Proof.** Define the family of closed half-planes:
$$\mathcal{F} = \{H_u^- : u\in S^1\} \cup \{H_u^+ : u\in S^1\},$$
where $H_u^- = \{x : \langle x,u\rangle \ge \frac{2m(u)+M(u)}{3}\}$ and $H_u^+ =
 \{x : \langle x,u\rangle \le \frac{m(u)+2M(u)}{3}\}$.

Note that $H_u^- \subseteq \{x:\langle x,u\rangle \ge m(u)\}$ and $H_u^+ \subset
eq \{x:\langle x,u\rangle \le M(u)\}$, so $\bigcap\mathcal{F} \subseteq S$. Sinc
e $S$ is compact, each $H_u^\pm \cap S$ is compact convex. By Helly's theorem in
 $\mathbb{R}^2$ (and the finite intersection property via compactness of $S$), i
t suffices to show that **any 3 members of $\mathcal{F}$ have nonempty intersect
ion**.

For each direction $u$, there is exactly one lower half-plane $H_u^-$ and one up
per half-plane $H_u^+$. Three chosen half-planes involve at most 3 directions. W
e enumerate the cases:

**Case A: Three lower bounds** for distinct directions $u_1,u_2,u_3$. Let $p_i\i
n S$ with $\langle p_i,u_i\rangle = M(u_i)$. Set $p^* = \frac{1}{3}(p_1+p_2+p_3)
\in S$. Then:
$$\langle p^*,u_j\rangle = \tfrac{1}{3}\bigl(M(u_j)+\langle p_{i_1},u_j\rangle+\
langle p_{i_2},u_j\rangle\bigr) \ge \tfrac{1}{3}\bigl(M(u_j)+2m(u_j)\bigr). \che
ckmark$$

**Case B: Two lower bounds ($u_1,u_2$) + one upper bound ($u_3$).** Let $p_i$ ac
hieve $M(u_i)$ for $i=1,2$ and $p_3'$ achieve $m(u_3)$. Set $p^*=\frac{1}{3}(p_1
+p_2+p_3')\in S$. Then:
- $\langle p^*,u_j\rangle \ge \frac{1}{3}(M(u_j)+2m(u_j))$ for $j=1,2$. ✓
- $\langle p^*,u_3\rangle = \frac{1}{3}(\langle p_1,u_3\rangle+\langle p_2,u_3\r
angle+m(u_3)) \le \frac{1}{3}(2M(u_3)+m(u_3))$. ✓

**Case C: One lower + two upper bounds.** Symmetric to Case B (replace $M$ with
$m$ and negate). ✓

**Case D: Three upper bounds.** Symmetric to Case A. ✓

**Case E: Two half-planes from one direction $u_1$ (one lower, one upper) + one
from $u_2$.** Let $p_1^+$ achieve $M(u_1)$, $p_1^-$ achieve $m(u_1)$, $p_2$ achi
eve $M(u_2)$ (if the third is a lower bound) or $m(u_2)$ (if upper). Set $p^*=\f
rac{1}{3}(p_1^++p_1^-+p_2)\in S$. Then:
- $\langle p^*,u_1\rangle = \frac{1}{3}(M(u_1)+m(u_1)+\langle p_2,u_1\rangle)$.
Since $m(u_1)\le\langle p_2,u_1\rangle\le M(u_1)$, this lies in $[\frac{2m(u_1)+
M(u_1)}{3},\,\frac{m(u_1)+2M(u_1)}{3}]$. ✓
- $\langle p^*,u_2\rangle = \frac{1}{3}(\langle p_1^+,u_2\rangle+\langle p_1^-,u
_2\rangle+M(u_2)) \ge \frac{1}{3}(2m(u_2)+M(u_2))$ (if lower bound needed). ✓ (U
pper bound case symmetric.)

**Case F: All three from one direction.** At most two distinct half-planes (one
lower, one upper), forming a strip of width $w(u)/3 > 0$ (or a single line if $w
(u)=0$). Nonempty. ✓

Every case yields a feasible point in $S$. By Helly's theorem, $\bigcap\mathcal{
F}\neq\emptyset$. $\blacksquare$

## Lower bound: $t < \frac{1}{3}$ fails

**Example.** Let $S$ be the triangle with vertices $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, $(0,1)$.

- Direction $u=(1,0)$: $m=0$, $M=1$, $w=1$. Band requires $x \ge \frac{1}{2}-\fr
ac{t}{2}$.
- Direction $u=(0,1)$: similarly $y \ge \frac{1}{2}-\frac{t}{2}$.
- Direction $u=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1,1)$: $m=0$, $M=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, $w=\frac
{1}{\sqrt{2}}$. Band requires $x+y \le \frac{1}{2}+\frac{t}{2}$.

From the first two: $x+y \ge 1-t$. From the third: $x+y \le \frac{1}{2}+\frac{t}
{2}$. Combining:
$$1-t \le \tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{t}{2} \implies \tfrac{1}{2} \le \tfrac{3t}{2} \imp
lies t \ge \tfrac{1}{3}.$$

For $t<\frac{1}{3}$, these three constraints are inconsistent, so $S\cap\bigcap_
K B_S(K,t)=\emptyset$.

At $t=\frac{1}{3}$, the unique feasible point is the centroid $(\frac{1}{3},\fra
c{1}{3})$, which one verifies satisfies the middle-third condition for **all** d
irections (the ratio $|\langle g,u\rangle - c(u)|/(w(u)/2)$ achieves its maximum
 of $\frac{1}{3}$ at $u=(1,0)$, $u=(0,1)$, $u=(1,1)/\sqrt{2}$, and $u=(1,-1)/\sq
