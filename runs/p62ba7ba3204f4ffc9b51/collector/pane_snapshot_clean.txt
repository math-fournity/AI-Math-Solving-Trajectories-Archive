**Proof.**

**Yes**, such a function exists.

**Construction.** Let $f(z) = z$ and let $\gamma = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| = 1\
}$ be the unit circle.

**Verification of all required properties:**

1. **$f$ is entire and non-constant:** $f(z) = z$ is holomorphic on all of $\mat
hbb{C}$ (entire) and is clearly non-constant.

2. **$\gamma$ is an analytic simple closed curve surrounding the origin:** The u
nit circle is parametrized by $z(t) = e^{it}$, $t \in [0, 2\pi]$, which is a rea
l-analytic parametrization. It is simple (no self-intersections), closed, and th
e origin $0$ lies in its interior.

3. **$|f(z)|$ is constant on $\gamma$:** For every $z \in \gamma$, we have $|z|
= 1$, so $|f(z)| = |z| = 1$, which is constant on $\gamma$.

Therefore, there exists a non-constant entire holomorphic function whose modulus
 is constant on an analytic simple closed curve surrounding the origin. $\blacks
quare$

**Remark.** More generally, for any circle $\gamma = \{|z - z_0| = R\}$ with $|z
_0| < R$ (so that $\gamma$ surrounds the origin), the function $f(z) = (z - z_0)
^k$ for any $k \geq 1$ is non-constant, entire, and satisfies $|f(z)| = R^k$ (co
nstant) on $\gamma$. Non-circular examples also exist: for instance, $f(z) = z^2
 - Rz$ with $R$ large has $|f| = 1$ on a non-circular analytic simple closed lev
el curve near the origin (a smooth perturbation of $\{|z| = 1/R\}$), on which $f
$ is conformal.
