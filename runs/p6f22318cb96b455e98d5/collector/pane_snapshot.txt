Let $G^D = \operatorname{Hom}(G, \mathbb{G}_m)$ denote the Cartier dual. We
claim $G^D$ is étale.

Step 1: Reduction to the separably closed case.

Étaleness is fpqc-local on the base: a $k$-scheme $X$ is étale over $k$ if and
only if $X \times_k k_s$ is étale over $k_s$, where $k_s$ is a separable closure
of $k$. (The extension $k \to k_s$ is a filtered colimit of finite separable
extensions, hence faithfully flat, and étaleness descends under faithfully flat
morphisms.) Thus it suffices to show that $G^D \times_k k_s$ is étale over
$k_s$.

Step 2: Description of $G$ and $G^D$ over $k_s$.

Since $G$ is finite étale over $k$, after base change to $k_s$ it becomes a
constant group scheme: $$G \times_k k_s ;\cong; \underline{\Gamma}_{k_s}$$ for a
finite abelian group $\Gamma$ with $|\Gamma| = n$ (the order equals $\dim_k
k[G]$ since $G$ is étale, so $k[G]$ is a product of separable field extensions
whose total dimension is $n$).

The Cartier dual of a constant group scheme $\underline{\Gamma}$ is the
diagonalizable group scheme $D(\Gamma)$: $$G^D \times_k k_s ;\cong; D(\Gamma)
{k_s} ;=; \operatorname{Spec}\bigl(k_s[\Gamma]\bigr),$$ where $k_s[\Gamma] =
\bigoplus{\gamma \in \Gamma} k_s, e_\gamma$ is the group algebra with
multiplication $e_\gamma \cdot e_{\gamma'} = e_{\gamma+\gamma'}$.

Since $k_s$ is separably closed and $p \nmid n = |\Gamma|$, the group $k_s^$

\widehat{\Gamma} := \operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma, k_s^)$ has order $|
\widehat{\Gamma}| = |\Gamma| = n$ (the pairing $\Gamma \times \widehat{\Gamma}

The Fourier transform gives an isomorphism of $k_s$-algebras: $$\Phi :
k_s[\Gamma] ;\xrightarrow{;\sim;}; \prod_{\chi \in \widehat{\Gamma}} k_s, \qquad
e_\gamma ;\longmapsto; \bigl(\chi(\gamma)\bigr)_{\chi \in \widehat{\Gamma}}.$$

This is a $k_s$-algebra homomorphism (since $\chi(\gamma+\gamma') =
\chi(\gamma)\chi(\gamma')$), and it is an isomorphism because:

  • Both sides have $k_s$-dimension $|\Gamma| = |\widehat{\Gamma}|$.

of $k_s$, it is a separable (étale) $k_s$-algebra. Hence $k_s[\Gamma]$ is
separable over $k_s$.
