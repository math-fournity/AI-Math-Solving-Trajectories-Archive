## Proof

**Setup.** Place the conic in standard position with foci $F_1=(-c,0)$, $F_2=(c,
0)$ on the $x$-axis. Let $P=(x_0,y_0)$ be a point on the curve, and set
$$r_1 = |PF_1|,\qquad r_2 = |PF_2|.$$
The unit vectors from $P$ toward the foci are
$$\hat{u}_1 = \frac{(-c-x_0,\,-y_0)}{r_1},\qquad \hat{u}_2 = \frac{(c-x_0,\,-y_0
)}{r_2}.$$
The **internal bisector** of $\angle F_1PF_2$ points along $\hat{u}_1+\hat{u}_2$
. Computing its components:

$$
(\hat{u}_1+\hat{u}_2)_x = \frac{c(r_1-r_2)-x_0(r_1+r_2)}{r_1 r_2},\qquad
(\hat{u}_1+\hat{u}_2)_y = \frac{-y_0(r_1+r_2)}{r_1 r_2}.
$$

We also use the identity
$$
r_1^2 - r_2^2 = (x_0+c)^2+y_0^2 - \bigl((x_0-c)^2+y_0^2\bigr) = 4cx_0. \tag{$\st
ar$}
$$

---

### (a) Ellipse: the normal bisects $\angle F_1PF_2$

The ellipse is $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1$ with $c^2=a^2-b^2$, and the
 defining relation is
$$r_1 + r_2 = 2a.$$
From ($\star$): $\;r_1 - r_2 = \dfrac{r_1^2-r_2^2}{r_1+r_2} = \dfrac{4cx_0}{2a}
= \dfrac{2cx_0}{a}.$

Substituting into the bisector components:

$$
(\hat{u}_1+\hat{u}_2)_x = \frac{c\cdot\frac{2cx_0}{a} - x_0\cdot 2a}{r_1 r_2}
= \frac{2x_0\bigl(\frac{c^2}{a}-a\bigr)}{r_1 r_2}
= \frac{2x_0\,\frac{c^2-a^2}{a}}{r_1 r_2}
= \frac{-2b^2 x_0}{a\,r_1 r_2},
$$

$$
(\hat{u}_1+\hat{u}_2)_y = \frac{-y_0\cdot 2a}{r_1 r_2} = \frac{-2a\,y_0}{r_1 r_2
}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\hat{u}_1+\hat{u}_2 = \frac{-2ab^2}{r_1 r_2}\left(\frac{x_0}{a^2},\;\frac{y_0}{b
^2}\right).
$$

The gradient of $f(x,y)=\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}$ at $P$ is $\nabla f = 2
\bigl(\frac{x_0}{a^2},\frac{y_0}{b^2}\bigr)$, so the **normal** to the ellipse a
t $P$ is along $\bigl(\frac{x_0}{a^2},\frac{y_0}{b^2}\bigr)$.

Since $\hat{u}_1+\hat{u}_2$ is a nonzero scalar multiple of the normal direction
, the internal bisector of $\angle F_1PF_2$ **is the normal**. $\quad\blacksquar
e$

### (b) Hyperbola: the tangent bisects $\angle F_1PF_2$

The hyperbola is $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}-\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1$ with $c^2=a^2+b^2$. By sy
mmetry it suffices to treat the right branch ($x_0>0$), where $F_2=(c,0)$ is the
 nearer focus and
$$r_1 - r_2 = 2a.$$
From ($\star$): $\;r_1 + r_2 = \dfrac{4cx_0}{r_1-r_2} = \dfrac{4cx_0}{2a} = \dfr
ac{2cx_0}{a}.$

Substituting into the bisector components:

$$
(\hat{u}_1+\hat{u}_2)_x = \frac{c\cdot 2a - x_0\cdot\frac{2cx_0}{a}}{r_1 r_2}
= \frac{2c\bigl(a-\frac{x_0^2}{a}\bigr)}{r_1 r_2}
= \frac{2c\,\frac{a^2-x_0^2}{a}}{r_1 r_2}.
$$

From the hyperbola equation, $x_0^2 = a^2 + \dfrac{a^2 y_0^2}{b^2}$, so $a^2 - x
_0^2 = -\dfrac{a^2 y_0^2}{b^2}$. Thus

$$
(\hat{u}_1+\hat{u}_2)_x = \frac{-2ac\,y_0^2/b^2}{r_1 r_2},
\qquad
(\hat{u}_1+\hat{u}_2)_y = \frac{-y_0\cdot\frac{2cx_0}{a}}{r_1 r_2} = \frac{-2c\,
x_0 y_0/a}{r_1 r_2}.
$$

Factoring:

$$
\hat{u}_1+\hat{u}_2 = \frac{-2c\,y_0}{r_1 r_2}\left(\frac{a\,y_0}{b^2},\;\frac{x
_0}{a}\right).
$$

The gradient of $g(x,y)=\frac{x^2}{a^2}-\frac{y^2}{b^2}$ at $P$ is $\nabla g = 2
\bigl(\frac{x_0}{a^2},-\frac{y_0}{b^2}\bigr)$, so the **normal** to the hyperbol
a is along $\bigl(\frac{x_0}{a^2},-\frac{y_0}{b^2}\bigr)$. A direction perpendic
ular to this (hence along the **tangent**) is

$$
\left(\frac{y_0}{b^2},\;\frac{x_0}{a^2}\right) = \frac{1}{a}\left(\frac{a\,y_0}{
b^2},\;\frac{x_0}{a}\right).
$$

Since $\hat{u}_1+\hat{u}_2$ is a nonzero scalar multiple of $\bigl(\frac{ay_0}{b
^2},\frac{x_0}{a}\bigr)$, which is exactly the tangent direction, the internal b
isector of $\angle F_1PF_2$ **is the tangent**.

The left branch follows by the reflection symmetry $x\mapsto -x$ (which swaps th
e foci and preserves the tangent-bisects-angle conclusion). $\quad\blacksquare$

**Summary.** In both cases the key is the constant-sum (ellipse) or constant-dif
ference (hyperbola) property, which via $r_1^2-r_2^2=4cx_0$ fixes $r_1\pm r_2$ i
n terms of $x_0$. Substituting into the angle-bisector vector $\hat{u}_1+\hat{u}
_2$ yields a vector parallel to the gradient $\bigl(\frac{x_0}{a^2},\pm\frac{y_0
}{b^2}\bigr)$: parallel to the **normal** for the ellipse (sign $+$), and parall
el to the **tangent** for the hyperbola (sign $-$, which rotates the bisector by
 $90°$ from the normal to the tangent).
