# Problem

In a remote sector of the galaxy, a specialized automated factory produces crystalline fuel rods. The quality level of the rods is represented by a sequence of positive integers, where $f(n)$ denotes the purity grade of the $n$-th rod produced in a continuous batch.

The factory's central computer follows two strict operational protocols:
(i) The very first rod produced in any batch must have a purity grade of exactly 1.
(ii) For every rod produced after the first, the purity grades of three consecutive rods—the $n$-th, $(n+1)$-th, and $(n+2)$-th—must satisfy a specific equilibrium: the product of the purity of the $n$-th and $(n+2)$-th rods must be exactly 1997 units greater than the square of the purity of the $(n+1)$-th rod.

Based on these industrial constraints, how many unique sequences of purity grades are theoretically possible for the factory to produce?
