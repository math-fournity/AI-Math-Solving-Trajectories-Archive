**Claim:** Yes, the preimage of a critical value of a smooth function $F : \math
bb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ can be a smooth manifold of dimension greater than zero.

**Proof.**

We construct an explicit example for $n \geq 2$.

Define $F : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ by
$$F(x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n) = x_1^2.$$

This is a smooth function.

**Step 1: Identify the critical points.** The gradient is
$$\nabla F = (2x_1,\, 0,\, \ldots,\, 0).$$
This vanishes if and only if $x_1 = 0$. Hence the set of critical points of $F$
is the hyperplane
$$\mathrm{Crit}(F) = \{(0, x_2, \ldots, x_n) \in \mathbb{R}^n\}.$$

**Step 2: Identify the critical values.** A critical value is the image of a cri
tical point under $F$. For any critical point $(0, x_2, \ldots, x_n)$,
$$F(0, x_2, \ldots, x_n) = 0^2 = 0.$$
Thus the unique critical value is $c = 0$.

**Step 3: Compute the preimage of the critical value.**
$$F^{-1}(0) = \{(x_1, \ldots, x_n) \in \mathbb{R}^n : x_1^2 = 0\} = \{(0, x_2, \
ldots, x_n) : (x_2, \ldots, x_n) \in \mathbb{R}^{n-1}\}.$$
This is the hyperplane $\{x_1 = 0\}$.

**Step 4: Verify it is a smooth manifold of positive dimension.** The hyperplane
 $\{x_1 = 0\}$ is a smooth embedded submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^n$ of dimension $
n - 1$. It is globally the graph of the smooth function $x_1 = 0$ over the coord
inates $(x_2, \ldots, x_n)$, hence diffeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^{n-1}$.

Since $n \geq 2$, we have $n - 1 \geq 1 > 0$.

**Conclusion.** The preimage $F^{-1}(0)$ of the critical value $0$ is a smooth m
anifold of dimension $n - 1 > 0$. Therefore, the preimage of a critical value of
 a smooth function $F : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ **can** be a smooth manifol
d of dimension greater than zero. $\blacksquare$
