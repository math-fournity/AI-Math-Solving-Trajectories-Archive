**Answer: Yes, *f* is locally injective on *M*.**

**Proof.**

Let $g = f|_M : M \to \mathbb{R}^n$. The hypothesis is that for every $x \in M$
and every *nonzero* $v \in T_x M$, $Df(x)v \neq 0$. Since $Dg(x) = Df(x)\big|_{T
_x M}$, this says $\ker(Dg(x)) = \{0\}$, i.e. $Dg(x) : T_x M \to \mathbb{R}^n$ i
s injective for every $x \in M$. Thus $g$ is an **immersion**.

**Claim.** Every immersion is locally injective.

*Proof of claim.* Fix $x_0 \in M$ and let $k = \dim M$. Choose a coordinate char
t $\varphi : U_0 \to V$ with $U_0 \subset \mathbb{R}^k$ open, $V \subset M$ a ne
ighborhood of $x_0$, and $\varphi(0) = x_0$. Set $F = g \circ \varphi : U_0 \to
\mathbb{R}^n$. Then
$$DF(0) = Dg(x_0) \circ D\varphi(0)$$
is injective, being a composition of injective linear maps. Its image $W = DF(0)
(\mathbb{R}^k)$ is a $k$-dimensional subspace of $\mathbb{R}^n$.

Choose a linear map $\pi : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^k$ such that $\pi|_W : W
\to \mathbb{R}^k$ is an isomorphism (project onto $W$ along a complementary subs
pace, then identify $W \cong \mathbb{R}^k$). Then
$$D(\pi \circ F)(0) = \pi \circ DF(0) : \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}^k$$
is an isomorphism. By the **Inverse Function Theorem**, $\pi \circ F$ is a diffe
omorphism on some neighborhood $U \subset U_0$ of $0$, hence injective on $U$.

Now if $a, b \in U$ satisfy $F(a) = F(b)$, then $\pi(F(a)) = \pi(F(b))$, so $(\p
i \circ F)(a) = (\pi \circ F)(b)$, and injectivity of $\pi \circ F$ on $U$ gives
 $a = b$. Thus $F$ is injective on $U$.

Since $\varphi$ is a diffeomorphism, $g = F \circ \varphi^{-1}$ is injective on
$\varphi(U)$, a neighborhood of $x_0$ in $M$.

Since $x_0$ was arbitrary, $f$ is locally injective on $M$. $\square$

**Remark.** The condition is exactly that $f|_M$ is an immersion, and the key to
ol is the Inverse Function Theorem applied to a suitable projection of $f|_M$ on
to $\mathbb{R}^k$. Note that global injectivity on $M$ need not hold (e.g. a fig
ure-eight immersion), but *local* injectivity always does.
