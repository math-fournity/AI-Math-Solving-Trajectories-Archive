## Proof

Let $q = e^{-\pi\sqrt{2}}$ and consider
$$P = \prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+q^n).$$

**Step 1: Reduce to Dedekind eta function.**

Using $1+q^n = \frac{1-q^{2n}}{1-q^n}$,
$$P = \frac{\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{2n})}{\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^n)} = \frac
{\phi(q^2)}{\phi(q)}, \qquad \phi(q):=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^n).$$

The Dedekind eta function is $\eta(\tau)=q_0^{1/24}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q_0^n)
$ with $q_0=e^{2\pi i\tau}$, so $\phi(q_0)=q_0^{-1/24}\eta(\tau)$.

**Step 2: Identify the $\tau$ values.**

For $q=e^{-\pi\sqrt{2}}=e^{2\pi i\tau_1}$: $\tau_1 = \dfrac{-\pi\sqrt{2}}{2\pi i
}=\dfrac{i}{\sqrt{2}}$, giving
$$\phi(q)=e^{\pi\sqrt{2}/24}\,\eta\!\left(\tfrac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\right).$$

For $q^2=e^{-2\pi\sqrt{2}}=e^{2\pi i\tau_2}$: $\tau_2=i\sqrt{2}$, giving
$$\phi(q^2)=e^{\pi\sqrt{2}/12}\,\eta(i\sqrt{2}).$$

Hence
$$P = e^{\pi\sqrt{2}/24}\cdot\frac{\eta(i\sqrt{2})}{\eta(i/\sqrt{2})}.$$

**Step 3: Apply the modular transformation.**

The eta function satisfies $\eta(-1/\tau)=\sqrt{-i\tau}\,\eta(\tau)$. With $\tau
=i/\sqrt{2}$,
$$-\frac{1}{\tau}=-\frac{1}{i/\sqrt{2}}=i\sqrt{2},$$
so
$$\eta(i\sqrt{2})=\eta(-1/\tau)=\sqrt{-i\cdot\tfrac{i}{\sqrt{2}}}\;\eta\!\left(\
tfrac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\right)=\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}}\;\eta\!\left(\tfrac{i}{\sqr
t{2}}\right)=2^{-1/4}\,\eta\!\left(\tfrac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\right).$$

Therefore
$$\frac{\eta(i\sqrt{2})}{\eta(i/\sqrt{2})}=2^{-1/4}.$$

**Step 4: Conclude.**

$$P = e^{\pi\sqrt{2}/24}\cdot 2^{-1/4} = \frac{e^{\pi\sqrt{2}/24}}{\sqrt[4]{2}}.
$$
