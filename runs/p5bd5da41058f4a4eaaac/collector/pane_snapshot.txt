## Proof

Let $f$ be an endomorphism of $\mathbb{K}^n$ with $n$ pairwise distinct eigenval
ues $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$. For each $i$, let $E_i = \ker(f - \lambda_i \
mathrm{Id})$ be the eigenspace associated to $\lambda_i$.

**Step 1: $f$ is diagonalizable and each $E_i$ is one-dimensional.**

Since the eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$ are pairwise distinct, the c
haracteristic polynomial of $f$ splits as $\chi_f(X) = \prod_{i=1}^n (X - \lambd
a_i)$, which has $n$ distinct roots in $\mathbb{K}$. Eigenvectors associated to
pairwise distinct eigenvalues are linearly independent; since there are $n$ of t
hem in an $n$-dimensional space, they form a basis. Hence $f$ is diagonalizable
and $\mathbb{K}^n = \bigoplus_{i=1}^n E_i$. Moreover $\dim E_i = 1$ for every $i
$, since the $n$ one-dimensional contributions already sum to $n = \dim \mathbb{
K}^n$.

**Step 2: Every invariant subspace is a direct sum of eigenspaces.**

Let $W \subseteq \mathbb{K}^n$ be an $f$-invariant subspace. For each $i$, consi
der the projection $\pi_i : \mathbb{K}^n \to E_i$ associated to the direct sum d
ecomposition $\mathbb{K}^n = \bigoplus_i E_i$. Concretely, $\pi_i$ is the polyno
mial projector
$$\pi_i = \prod_{j \neq i} \frac{f - \lambda_j \mathrm{Id}}{\lambda_i - \lambda_
j},$$
which is a polynomial in $f$. Since $W$ is stable under $f$, it is stable under
every polynomial in $f$, hence under every $\pi_i$. Therefore $\pi_i(W) \subsete
q W$, and
$$W = \sum_{i=1}^n \pi_i(W) = \bigoplus_{i=1}^n (W \cap E_i),$$
because $\pi_i(W) \subseteq W \cap E_i$ and the sum of the $E_i$ is direct.

Since $\dim E_i = 1$, the subspace $W \cap E_i$ is either $\{0\}$ or $E_i$. Sett
ing $S = \{i : W \cap E_i = E_i\}$, we obtain
$$W = \bigoplus_{i \in S} E_i.$$

**Step 3: Conversely, every such direct sum is invariant.**

For any subset $S \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\}$, the subspace $W_S = \bigoplus_{i \
in S} E_i$ is $f$-invariant, since each $E_i$ is stable under $f$ (it is an eige
nspace) and a sum of invariant subspaces is invariant.

**Conclusion.**

The $f$-invariant subspaces of $\mathbb{K}^n$ are exactly the subspaces of the f
orm
$$\bigoplus_{i \in S} E_i, \qquad S \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\},$$
where $E_i = \ker(f - \lambda_i \mathrm{Id})$ is the eigenspace of the eigenvalu
e $\lambda_i$. In particular, there are exactly $2^n$ invariant subspaces, index
ed by the subsets of the set of eigenvalues.
