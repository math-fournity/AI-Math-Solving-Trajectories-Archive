## Proof

We determine the radius of convergence of
$$S(z)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty \left[\frac{z(z+n)}{n}\right]^{n}.$$

**Step 1: Rewrite the general term.**

$$\frac{z(z+n)}{n}=\frac{z^{2}+zn}{n}=z+\frac{z^{2}}{n},$$

so the $n$-th term is
$$a_n(z)=\left(z+\frac{z^{2}}{n}\right)^{\!n}.$$

**Step 2: Apply the root test.**

The $n$-th root of the absolute value of the general term is
$$|a_n(z)|^{1/n}=\left|z+\frac{z^{2}}{n}\right|.$$

As $n\to\infty$,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}|a_n(z)|^{1/n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|z+\frac{z^{2}}{n}\right
|=|z|.$$

**Step 3: Convergence inside the disk $|z|<1$.**

For $|z|<1$, the limit $\lim |a_n(z)|^{1/n}=|z|<1$. By the root test, the series
 converges absolutely.

**Step 4: Divergence outside the disk $|z|>1$.**

For $|z|>1$, the limit $\lim |a_n(z)|^{1/n}=|z|>1$. Hence for all sufficiently l
arge $n$, $|a_n(z)|^{1/n}>1$, i.e. $|a_n(z)|>1$. In particular $a_n(z)\not\to 0$
, so the series diverges.

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

The series converges (absolutely) for every $z$ with $|z|<1$ and diverges for ev
ery $z$ with $|z|>1$. Therefore the radius of convergence is

$$\boxed{R=1}.$$
