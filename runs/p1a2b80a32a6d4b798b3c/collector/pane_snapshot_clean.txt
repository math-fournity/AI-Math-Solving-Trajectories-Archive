## Solution

We work on $L_2^{(c)}(-\pi,\pi)$, the closed subspace of even, square‑integrable
 functions. The operator is

$$Ku(x)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}k(x+\xi)\,u(\xi)\,d\xi.$$

Since $k$ is even and $2\pi$‑periodic, $k(x+\xi)=k(-x-\xi)=k(x+\xi)$ is symmetri
c in a sense that makes $K$ well‑defined on even functions (and in fact $K$ maps
 every function to an even function, as shown below).

### Diagonalization on the cosine basis

The family $\{\cos nx\}_{n=0}^{\infty}$ is a complete orthogonal basis of $L_2^{
(c)}(-\pi,\pi)$. Test $u(\xi)=\cos n\xi$:

$$K\cos(n\,\cdot)(x)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}k(x+\xi)\cos(n\xi)\,d\xi.$$

Substitute $t=x+\xi$ ($d\xi=dt$, interval length $2\pi$). By $2\pi$‑periodicity
of $k$ the integral over $[x-\pi,x+\pi]$ equals that over $[-\pi,\pi]$:

$$=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}k(t)\cos\bigl(n(t-x)\bigr)\,dt
=\cos(nx)\!\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}k(t)\cos(nt)\,dt\;+\;\sin(nx)\!\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}k(t)
\sin(nt)\,dt.$$

Because $k$ is even, $k(t)\sin(nt)$ is odd, so the second integral vanishes. For
 the first integral, the given Fourier cosine series $k(x)=\tfrac12 k_0+\sum_{m\
ge1}k_m\cos mx$ uses the convention $k_n=\tfrac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}k(t)\cos(nt)
\,dt$, hence

$$\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}k(t)\cos(nt)\,dt=\pi k_n.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{K\cos(n\,\cdot)=\pi k_n\,\cos(n\,\cdot)},\qquad n=0,1,2,\dots$$

The constant function $\cos 0\,x\equiv 1$ is an eigenfunction with eigenvalue $\
pi k_0$; for $n\ge1$, $\cos nx$ is an eigenfunction with eigenvalue $\pi k_n$.

### Completeness — these are all eigenvalues/eigenfunctions

The system $\{\cos nx\}_{n\ge0}$ is complete in $L_2^{(c)}(-\pi,\pi)$. Any eigen
function $u\in L_2^{(c)}$ expands as $u=\sum_{n\ge0} a_n\cos nx$, and $Ku=\sum_{
n\ge0}a_n\,\pi k_n\cos nx$. The equation $Ku=\lambda u$ becomes, coefficient‑wis
e, $a_n(\pi k_n-\lambda)=0$ for every $n$. Thus a nonzero solution exists only w
hen $\lambda=\pi k_n$ for some $n$, and then $u$ lies in the span of those $\cos
 mx$ with $\pi k_m=\lambda$. Hence:

- **Eigenvalues:** $\lambda_n=\pi k_n$, $n=0,1,2,\dots$ (listed with multiplicit
y equal to the number of indices $m$ with $k_m=k_n$).
- **Eigenfunctions:** $\cos nx$ (and linear combinations of $\cos mx$ over indic
es sharing the same $k_m$).

If some $k_n=0$, the corresponding $\cos nx$ lies in the kernel of $K$ (eigenval
ue $0$); if $k_n\ne0$ it is a genuine nonzero eigenvalue. Since $k\in L_2$, $k_n
\to0$, so $\lambda_n\to0$ and $K$ is compact (Hilbert–Schmidt), consistent with
the discrete spectrum found.

### Comparison with Example 3 (the convolution operator $Ku=\int k(x-\xi)u(\xi)\
,d\xi$)

For the convolution kernel $k(x-\xi)$ one obtains, by the same substitution,

$$K_{\text{conv}}\cos(n\,\cdot)=\pi k_n\cos(n\,\cdot),\qquad K_{\text{conv}}\sin
(n\,\cdot)=\pi k_n\sin(n\,\cdot),$$

i.e. **both** $\cos nx$ and $\sin nx$ (equivalently $e^{inx}$) share the eigenva
lue $\pi k_n$; the operator is translation‑invariant and diagonalized by the ful
l trigonometric system on all of $L_2(-\pi,\pi)$.

For the present operator with kernel $k(x+\xi)$, repeating the computation with
$u(\xi)=\sin n\xi$ gives

$$K\sin(n\,\cdot)(x)=-\pi k_n\,\sin(nx),$$

so on the **odd** subspace the eigenvalue picks up a minus sign. The "$x+\xi$" k
ernel is not translation‑invariant; it is the composition of the convolution wit
h the reflection $\xi\mapsto-\xi$, which flips the sign on odd functions. Restri
cted to $L_2^{(c)}$ (even functions), reflection acts as the identity, so the tw
o operators coincide there: both have eigenvalues $\{\pi k_n\}$ with eigenfuncti
ons $\{\cos nx\}$. The two operators differ precisely on the odd subspace, where
 the sign of every eigenvalue is reversed.
