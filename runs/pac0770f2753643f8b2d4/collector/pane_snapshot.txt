Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the unit variety (norm-one
torus) of a number field can always be parametrized by rational functions over Q
.

**Answer: No.** In general, unit varieties of number fields cannot be parametriz
ed by rational functions with rational coefficients. While this is possible for
number fields of degree ≤ 4, there exist number fields of higher degree whose un
it varieties are not Q-rational.

## Proof

### Step 1: The Unit Variety

Let K/Q be a number field of degree n with Q-basis {α₁,…,αₙ}. The norm map N_{K/
Q}: K → Q is a homogeneous polynomial of degree n:

$$N_{K/Q}(x_1\alpha_1 + \cdots + x_n\alpha_n) = F(x_1,\ldots,x_n), \quad F \in \
mathbb{Z}[x_1,\ldots,x_n].$$

The **unit variety** is the norm-one torus:

$$T^1 = V(F - 1) \subset \mathbb{A}^n_\mathbb{Q}$$

This is an (n−1)-dimensional algebraic torus over Q, specifically $T^1 = R_{K/\m
athbb{Q}}^{(1)}(\mathbb{G}_m)$, the kernel of the norm map in the Weil restricti
on $R_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{G}_m)$. A parametrization of T¹ by rational functio
ns with rational coefficients is precisely a Q-birational map $\mathbb{A}^{n-1}_
\mathbb{Q} \dashrightarrow T^1$, i.e., Q-rationality of T¹.

### Step 2: Positive Results for Small Degrees

- **n = 2 (quadratic):** T¹ is the conic $x^2 - dy^2 = 1$ with Q-rational point
(1,0), parametrized by $x = \frac{1+dt^2}{1-dt^2},\; y = \frac{2t}{1-dt^2}$.

- **n = 3 (cubic):** By Voskresenskii (1972), the norm-one torus of any cubic ex
tension is Q-rational.

- **n = 4 (quartic):** By Voskresenskii and Kunyavskii (1981), the norm-one toru
s of any quartic extension is Q-rational.

### Step 3: Connection to the Noether Problem

For K/Q Galois with G = Gal(K/Q), the character group of T¹ is the lattice $M =
\mathbb{Z}[G]/\mathbb{Z}$ (where $\mathbb{Z}$ embeds via the norm element $N = \
sum_{g \in G} g$). The **permutation torus** $T_{\text{perm}} = R_{K/\mathbb{Q}}
(\mathbb{G}_m)$ has character group $\mathbb{Z}[G]$, and its Q-rationality is eq
uivalent to the **Noether problem** for G having a positive answer.

The exact sequence of G-lattices $0 \to \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}[G] \to \mathbb
{Z}[G]/\mathbb{Z} \to 0$ yields additivity of flabby classes:

$$[\mathbb{Z}[G]]_{\text{fl}} = [\mathbb{Z}]_{\text{fl}} + [\mathbb{Z}[G]/\mathb
b{Z}]_{\text{fl}}$$

Since $\mathbb{Z}$ (with trivial G-action) is a permutation lattice, $[\mathbb{Z
}]_{\text{fl}} = 0$. Therefore:

$$[\mathbb{Z}[G]]_{\text{fl}} = [\mathbb{Z}[G]/\mathbb{Z}]_{\text{fl}}$$

By **Voskresenskii's theorem**, two tori are stably Q-birational if and only if
their character lattices have the same flabby class. Hence $T_{\text{perm}}$ and
 $T^1$ are **stably Q-birational**: $T_{\text{perm}}$ is stably Q-rational if an
d only if $T^1$ is stably Q-rational.

### Step 4: Counterexample via Saltman's Theorem

Let $p \geq 5$ be prime. Galois extensions K/Q with $\text{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \c
ong (\mathbb{Z}/p)^2$ exist (e.g., compositum of two linearly disjoint cyclic de
gree-p extensions, constructible via class field theory).

**Theorem (Saltman, 1984):** For $p \geq 5$, the Noether problem for $G = (\math
bb{Z}/p)^2$ has a **negative** answer: the fixed field $\mathbb{Q}(x_g : g \in G
)^G$ is not purely transcendental over Q. Equivalently, the permutation torus fo
r $(\mathbb{Z}/p)^2$ is not stably Q-rational.

*Saltman's method:* For any stably Q-rational variety X, the unramified Brauer g
roup satisfies $\text{Br}_{\text{nr}}(X/\mathbb{Q}) = \text{Br}(\mathbb{Q})$. Sa
ltman computed that for the quotient $\mathbb{A}^{p^2}/(\mathbb{Z}/p)^2$, the un
ramified Brauer group contains a nontrivial element from

$$H^2\big((\mathbb{Z}/p)^2,\, \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\big) \cong \mathbb{Z}/p.$$

For $p \geq 5$, this element survives the ramification conditions (it remains un
ramified at all divisors), yielding $\text{Br}_{\text{nr}} \neq \text{Br}(\mathb
b{Q})$, which obstructs stable rationality. (For $p = 2, 3$, the corresponding e
lement is killed by ramification, which is why the Noether problem has a positiv
e answer in those cases.)

### Step 5: Conclusion

By Step 3, since the permutation torus for $(\mathbb{Z}/p)^2$ ($p \geq 5$) is no
t stably Q-rational (Saltman), the norm-one torus $T^1 = R_{K/\mathbb{Q}}^{(1)}(
\mathbb{G}_m)$ is also not stably Q-rational. Since Q-rationality implies stable
 Q-rationality, $T^1$ is **not Q-rational**.

Therefore, for the number field K with $\text{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{
Z}/p)^2$ ($p \geq 5$), there exist **no** rational functions $f_1, \ldots, f_{p^
2-1} \in \mathbb{Q}(t_1, \ldots, t_{p^2-1})$ giving a birational parametrization
 of the unit variety.

**Unit varieties of number fields cannot, in general, be parametrized by rationa
l functions with rational coefficients.** $\blacksquare$
