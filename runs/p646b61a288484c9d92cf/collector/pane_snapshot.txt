## Proof

**The answer is no in general.** Mutual subobject status does not force isomorph
ism. We give a counterexample in the category **Grp** of groups.

Let $A = F_2$, the free group on two generators $\{a,b\}$, and let $B = F_3$, th
e free group on three generators $\{x,y,z\}$.

**$A$ is a subobject of $B$.** The assignment $a \mapsto x,\; b \mapsto y$ exten
ds, by the universal property of free groups, to a homomorphism $F_2 \to F_3$. T
his map is injective because $\{x,y\}$ freely generates a free subgroup of $F_3$
 (any subset of a free basis freely generates a free subgroup). Hence there is a
 monomorphism $A \hookrightarrow B$.

**$B$ is a subobject of $A$.** By the Nielsen–Schreier theorem, the commutator s
ubgroup $[F_2, F_2]$ is a free group of countably infinite rank. In particular i
t contains a free subgroup of rank $3$, which is isomorphic to $F_3$. Composing
an isomorphism $F_3 \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \langle\text{three generators of }[F_
2,F_2]\rangle$ with the inclusion $[F_2,F_2] \hookrightarrow F_2$ yields a monom
orphism $F_3 \to F_2$. Hence there is a monomorphism $B \hookrightarrow A$.

**$A \not\cong B$.** The abelianization of $F_n$ is $\mathbb{Z}^n$. Since $\math
bb{Z}^2 \not\cong \mathbb{Z}^3$ (free abelian groups of different finite rank ar
e non-isomorphic), we have $F_2 \not\cong F_3$.

**Conclusion.** We have exhibited monomorphisms $A \to B$ and $B \to A$ with $A
\not\cong B$. Therefore, in a general category, "$A$ is a subobject of $B$" and
"$B$ is a subobject of $A$" does **not** imply $A \cong B$.

**Remark.** In the category **Set** the analogous statement *is* true — this is
the Schröder–Bernstein (Cantor–Bernstein) theorem. So the property is category-d
ependent, not a general categorical law. The failure above shows that the "Canto
r–Bernstein property" is a special feature of certain categories, not a conseque
nce of the subobject relation alone.
