**Answer: Yes, it is always true.**

## Proof

We prove by induction on the number of variables $n$ that for every non-zero pol
ynomial $F \in \mathbb{Z}[x_1, \dots, x_n]$, there are infinitely many tuples $(
a_1, \dots, a_n) \in \mathbb{N}^n$ with $F(a_1, \dots, a_n) \neq 0$.

**Base case ($n = 1$).** Let $F \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ be a non-zero polynomial of d
egree $d \geq 0$. A non-zero univariate polynomial of degree $d$ has at most $d$
 roots in $\mathbb{Z}$ (hence in $\mathbb{N}$). Since $\mathbb{N}$ is infinite,
there are infinitely many $a \in \mathbb{N}$ with $F(a) \neq 0$.

**Inductive step.** Assume the statement holds for $n-1$ variables. Let $F \in \
mathbb{Z}[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ be non-zero. Write $F$ as a polynomial in $x_n$ with
 coefficients in $\mathbb{Z}[x_1, \dots, x_{n-1}]$:

$$F = \sum_{k=0}^{d} g_k(x_1, \dots, x_{n-1})\, x_n^{k},$$

where $d \geq 0$ and at least one $g_k$ is non-zero (since $F$ is non-trivial).
Let $j$ be the largest index with $g_j \not\equiv 0$; then $g_j \in \mathbb{Z}[x
_1, \dots, x_{n-1}]$ is a non-zero polynomial in $n-1$ variables.

By the induction hypothesis, there are infinitely many tuples $(a_1, \dots, a_{n
-1}) \in \mathbb{N}^{n-1}$ such that

$$g_j(a_1, \dots, a_{n-1}) \neq 0.$$

For each such $(a_1, \dots, a_{n-1})$, consider the univariate specialization

$$h(x_n) := F(a_1, \dots, a_{n-1}, x_n) = \sum_{k=0}^{d} g_k(a_1, \dots, a_{n-1}
)\, x_n^{k}.$$

Since the leading coefficient $g_j(a_1, \dots, a_{n-1}) \neq 0$ and all $g_k$ wi
th $k > j$ are identically zero, $h$ is a non-zero univariate polynomial of degr
ee exactly $j$. Therefore $h$ has at most $j$ roots, so there are infinitely man
y $a_n \in \mathbb{N}$ with $h(a_n) \neq 0$, i.e.,

$$F(a_1, \dots, a_{n-1}, a_n) \neq 0.$$

Since there are infinitely many valid $(n-1)$-tuples and for each one infinitely
 many valid $a_n$, the set of $n$-tuples $(a_1, \dots, a_n) \in \mathbb{N}^n$ wi
th $F(a_1, \dots, a_n) \neq 0$ is infinite. $\blacksquare$
