**Answer: Yes.** For every divergent series $\sum a_n$ with positive terms, ther

$$b_n \;=\; \min\!\left\{\frac{1}{n},\;\frac{2^{-n}}{a_n}\right\}, \qquad n \ge
1.$$

**Verification of the three required properties.**

*Positivity.* Since $a_n > 0$ and $n \ge 1$, both $\frac{1}{n}$ and $\frac{2^{-n

*Convergence to zero.* By definition $b_n \le \frac{1}{n}$, hence $b_n \to 0$ as
 $n \to \infty$.

*Convergence of $\sum a_n b_n$.* Since $b_n \le \frac{2^{-n}}{a_n}$, we have
$$a_n\, b_n \;\le\; a_n \cdot \frac{2^{-n}}{a_n} \;=\; 2^{-n}.$$
Therefore, by comparison with the convergent geometric series,
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n\, b_n \;\le\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} 2^{-n} \;=\; 1 \;<\;
 \infty.$$
