## Proof

**Claim.** There exists a continuous surjective function $f : S^3 \to S^2$.

**Construction (the Hopf fibration).**

Identify $S^3$ with the unit sphere in $\mathbb{C}^2$:
$$S^3 = \{(z_1, z_2) \in \mathbb{C}^2 : |z_1|^2 + |z_2|^2 = 1\}.$$

Identify $S^2$ with the complex projective line $\mathbb{CP}^1$ via the standard
 homeomorphism $\mathbb{CP}^1 \cong \mathbb{C} \cup \{\infty\} \cong S^2$ (the R
iemann sphere). Concretely, a point $[w_1 : w_2] \in \mathbb{CP}^1$ (with $(w_1,
 w_2) \neq (0,0)$, defined up to scaling by $\mathbb{C}^\times$) corresponds to
$w_1/w_2 \in \mathbb{C} \cup \{\infty\}$, and stereographic projection identifie
s $\mathbb{C} \cup \{\infty\}$ with $S^2$.

Define
$$f : S^3 \longrightarrow \mathbb{CP}^1 \cong S^2, \qquad f(z_1, z_2) = [z_1 : z
_2].$$

**Continuity.** The map $\pi : \mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \{0\} \to \mathbb{CP}^1$,
$\pi(w_1, w_2) = [w_1 : w_2]$, is the standard quotient map defining $\mathbb{CP
}^1$, hence continuous. Since $S^3 \subset \mathbb{C}^2 \setminus \{0\}$ (every
point of $S^3$ has norm $1 \neq 0$), the map $f$ is the restriction of $\pi$ to
$S^3$, and is therefore continuous. Composing with the homeomorphism $\mathbb{CP
}^1 \cong S^2$ preserves continuity.

**Surjectivity.** Let $[w_1 : w_2] \in \mathbb{CP}^1$ be arbitrary, with $(w_1,
w_2) \neq (0,0)$. Set
$$(z_1, z_2) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{|w_1|^2 + |w_2|^2}}(w_1, w_2).$$
Then $|z_1|^2 + |z_2|^2 = 1$, so $(z_1, z_2) \in S^3$, and
$$f(z_1, z_2) = [z_1 : z_2] = [w_1 : w_2],$$
since $(z_1, z_2)$ is a nonzero complex scalar multiple of $(w_1, w_2)$. Hence e
very point of $\mathbb{CP}^1 \cong S^2$ is in the image of $f$, so $f$ is surjec
tive.

**Conclusion.** The map $f(z_1, z_2) = [z_1 : z_2]$ is a well-defined continuous
 surjection from $S^3$ onto $\mathbb{CP}^1 \cong S^2$. Therefore, **yes**, there
 exists a continuous surjective function from $S^3$ to $S^2$. $\blacksquare$
