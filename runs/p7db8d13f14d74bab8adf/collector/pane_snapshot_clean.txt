## Solution

The heat equation $\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial
x^2}$ is posed on the interval $[0, \pi]$ with **homogeneous Dirichlet** boundar
y conditions $u(0,t) = u(\pi,t) = 0$.

**Step 1: Identify the eigenfunction expansion.**

With Dirichlet boundary conditions on $[0,\pi]$, the eigenfunctions of $-\partia
l^2/\partial x^2$ are $\sin(nx)$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_n = n^2$ for $n = 1,
2, 3, \ldots$. The solution takes the form

$$u(x,t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n \, e^{-n^2 t} \sin(nx),$$

where $b_n = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi \tanh(x)\sin(nx)\,dx$ are the Fourier sine
coefficients of the initial data $u(x,0) = \tanh(x)$.

**Step 2: Analyze the long-time behavior.**

Every eigenvalue $n^2 \geq 1 > 0$, so every mode $e^{-n^2 t}$ decays to zero as
$t \to \infty$. The slowest-decaying mode is $n=1$ (rate $e^{-t}$), but it still
 vanishes.

Equivalently, the steady-state solution $u_\infty(x)$ satisfies $u_\infty'' = 0$
 with $u_\infty(0) = u_\infty(\pi) = 0$. The general solution $u_\infty(x) = Ax
+ B$ together with both boundary conditions forces $A = 0$ and $B = 0$, so $u_\i
nfty \equiv 0$.

**Step 3: Conclude.**

Since all modes decay exponentially and the unique steady state is identically z
ero,

$$\lim_{t \to \infty} u(x,t) = 0 \quad \text{for all } x \in [0,\pi].$$

In particular,

$$\boxed{\lim_{t \to \infty} u(1,t) = 0.}$$
