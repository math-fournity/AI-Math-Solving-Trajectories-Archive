## Setup

Let $R$ be a UFD and let $F$ be its field of fractions. Recall:

- An element $f \in R[x]$ is **irreducible** if $f$ is nonzero, not a unit, and
whenever $f = gh$, then $g$ or $h$ is a unit in $R[x]$.
- The units of $R[x]$ are exactly the units of $R$ (since $\deg(gh) = \deg g + \
deg h$ and $R$ is an integral domain).
- A polynomial $f \in R[x]$ is **primitive** if $\gcd$ of its coefficients is a
unit, i.e., no irreducible (prime) element of $R$ divides all its coefficients.
- **Gauss's Lemma**: The product of two primitive polynomials is primitive.
- **Content**: For any $f \in R[x]$, we can write $f = c(f)\cdot f_1$ where $c(f
) \in R$ (the content, well-defined up to units) and $f_1$ is primitive.

## The Two Types of Irreducible Elements in R[x]

**Claim.** The irreducible elements of $R[x]$ are exactly of the following two t
ypes:

1. **(Constant type)** Irreducible elements of $R$, viewed as constant polynomia
ls.
2. **(Primitive positive-degree type)** Primitive polynomials of positive degree
 that are irreducible in $F[x]$.

### Proof of the Claim

**Step 1. Both types are indeed irreducible in $R[x]$.**

*Type 1.* If $p \in R$ is irreducible in $R$ and $p = gh$ in $R[x]$, then compar
ing degrees: $\deg p = 0 = \deg g + \deg h$, so both $g, h$ are constants, i.e.,
 elements of $R$. Since $p$ is irreducible in $R$, one of $g, h$ is a unit in $R
$, hence a unit in $R[x]$.

*Type 2.* Let $f \in R[x]$ be primitive with $\deg f > 0$ and irreducible in $F[
x]$. Suppose $f = gh$ in $R[x]$. Then this is also a factorization in $F[x]$. Si
nce $f$ is irreducible in $F[x]$, one of $g, h$ is a unit in $F[x]$, i.e., a non
zero constant in $F$. Say $g = a/b \in F$ with $a, b \in R$, $b \neq 0$. Then $b
f = ah$ in $R[x]$. Taking contents: $b\cdot c(f) = a\cdot c(h)$ up to units. Sin
ce $f$ is primitive, $c(f)$ is a unit, so $b$ and $a\cdot c(h)$ are associates.
This means $a$ divides $b$ (up to $c(h)$). More directly: since $g = a/b$ is a c
onstant and $g \mid f$ in $R[x]$, we have $f = (a/b)h$, so $bf = ah$. Since $f$
is primitive and $h \in R[x]$, by Gauss's Lemma $c(h) \sim c(f)\cdot (\text{cont
ent of } a/b\text{-part})$... Let me be cleaner:

Since $f$ is primitive and $f = gh$ with $g \in F$ constant, write $g = c \in F^
\times$. Then $h = f/c$. For $h$ to be in $R[x]$, every coefficient of $f$ must
be divisible by $c$ in $R$ (after clearing denominators). Write $c = a/b$ with $
\gcd(a,b)=1$ in $R$. Then $bf = ah$, so $a \mid b\cdot(\text{each coeff of }f)$.
 Since $\gcd(a,b)=1$, $a \mid$ each coeff of $f$. But $f$ is primitive, so $a$ i
s a unit. Similarly $b$ must divide each coeff of $h$; but also $h = (b/a)f$ and
 $a$ is a unit so $h = (b\cdot u) f$ for unit $u$, meaning $h \in R[x]$ requires
 $b \mid$ each coeff of $f$, and primitivity of $f$ forces $b$ to be a unit. So
$c = a/b$ is a unit in $R$, hence $g$ is a unit in $R[x]$. ∎

**Step 2. Every irreducible element of $R[x]$ is of one of these two types.**

Let $f \in R[x]$ be irreducible. Write $f = c(f)\cdot f_1$ where $c(f) \in R$ an
d $f_1$ is primitive.

- If $\deg f = 0$: then $f \in R$ is irreducible in $R$ (any factorization in $R
$ is a factorization in $R[x]$, and units coincide). This is **Type 1**.

- If $\deg f > 0$: Then $c(f)$ must be a unit in $R$; otherwise $f = c(f)\cdot f
_1$ is a nontrivial factorization (both factors non-unit: $c(f)$ non-unit in $R$
 hence non-unit in $R[x]$, and $f_1$ has positive degree hence non-unit). So $f$
 is primitive.

  Now suppose $f$ is **reducible in $F[x]$**: $f = g\cdot h$ with $g, h \in F[x]
$, both of positive degree. Clear denominators: write $g = (1/d_1)\bar{g}$, $h =
 (1/d_2)\bar{h}$ with $\bar{g}, \bar{h} \in R[x]$ primitive (factor out contents
) and $d_1, d_2 \in R\setminus\{0\}$. Then
  $$d_1 d_2 f = \bar{g}\cdot \bar{h}.$$
  By Gauss's Lemma, $\bar{g}\bar{h}$ is primitive. Taking contents of both sides
: $d_1 d_2 \cdot c(f) \sim c(\bar{g}\bar{h}) \sim 1$ (unit). Since $f$ is primit
ive, $c(f) \sim 1$, so $d_1 d_2$ is a unit. Thus (up to a unit) $f = \bar{g}\bar
{h}$ in $R[x]$, with both factors of positive degree — contradicting irreducibil
ity of $f$ in $R[x]$.

  Therefore $f$ is **irreducible in $F[x]$**. This is **Type 2**. ∎

## Condition for a Primitive Polynomial to be Irreducible

**Theorem (Gauss).** Let $R$ be a UFD with field of fractions $F$. A primitive p
olynomial $f \in R[x]$ of positive degree is **irreducible in $R[x]$** if and on
ly if it is **irreducible in $F[x]$**.

### Proof

**(⇒)** Shown in Step 2 above: if $f$ is primitive and irreducible in $R[x]$ wit
h positive degree, then $f$ is irreducible in $F[x]$.

**(⇐)** Shown in Step 1 (Type 2) above: if $f$ is primitive and irreducible in $
F[x]$, then $f$ is irreducible in $R[x]$. The key mechanism is Gauss's Lemma: an
y factorization in $R[x]$ is a factorization in $F[x]$, and any factorization in
 $F[x]$ can be "pulled back" to $R[x]$ (up to units) precisely because the produ
ct of primitive polynomials is primitive, so no content is lost.

Concretely, if $f = gh$ in $R[x]$, then $f = gh$ in $F[x]$, so one of $g, h$ (sa
y $g$) is a unit in $F[x]$, i.e., a nonzero constant $c \in F^\times$. As proved
 in Step 1, primitivity of $f$ forces $c$ to be a unit of $R$, so $g$ is a unit
in $R[x]$. ∎

## Summary

In $R[x]$ where $R$ is a UFD with fraction field $F$, the irreducible elements a
re precisely:

1. **Irreducible constants**: irreducible elements of $R$ (constant polynomials)
.
2. **Irreducible primitive polynomials**: primitive polynomials of positive degr
ee that remain irreducible in $F[x]$.

A primitive polynomial $f \in R[x]$ (of positive degree) is irreducible in $R[x]
$ **if and only if** it is irreducible in $F[x]$ — this is Gauss's Lemma, and it
 is the fundamental bridge between factorization in $R[x]$ and factorization in
$F[x]$.
