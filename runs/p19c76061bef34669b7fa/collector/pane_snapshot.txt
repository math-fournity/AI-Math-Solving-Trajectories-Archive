## Solution

**Setup.** We have 14 i.i.d. uniform[0,1] random variables, paired into 7 fixed
pairs. For pair $k$, define:
- $L_k = \min(a_{2k-1}, a_{2k})$ (left endpoint)
- $R_k = \max(a_{2k-1}, a_{2k})$ (right endpoint)
- Stability zone $I_k = [L_k, R_k]$

The system is synchronized iff $\bigcap_{k=1}^{7} I_k \neq \varnothing$, i.e., $
\max_k L_k \le \min_k R_k$.

**Key observation.** Since all 14 points are i.i.d. continuous, all $14!$ relati
ve orderings are equally likely. The condition $\max L_k \le \min R_k$ means the
 rightmost left endpoint precedes (or coincides with) the leftmost right endpoin
t. With probability 1 all points are distinct, so this means:

> In the sorted order of all 14 points, **all 7 left endpoints occupy the first
7 positions** and **all 7 right endpoints occupy the last 7 positions**.

*Proof of equivalence:* If the rightmost $L$ is at sorted position $p$ and the l
eftmost $R$ is at position $q$ with $p < q$, then all 7 left endpoints lie in po
sitions $1,\ldots,p$ and all 7 right endpoints lie in positions $q,\ldots,14$. S
ince there are exactly 7 of each, we must have $p=7$ and $q=8$. Conversely, if t
he first 7 positions are all left endpoints, then $\max L$ is at position 7 and
$\min R$ is at position 8, so $\max L < \min R$. ✓

**Counting favorable orderings.** Among the $14!$ equally likely orderings:

- For each of the 7 pairs, choose which of its 2 points is the left endpoint: $2
^7$ ways.
- Arrange the 7 chosen left endpoints in positions $1,\ldots,7$: $7!$ ways.
- Arrange the 7 right endpoints in positions $8,\ldots,14$: $7!$ ways.

$$P = \frac{2^7 \cdot (7!)^2}{14!}$$

**Computation via prime factorization.**

$$2^7 \cdot (7!)^2 = 2^7 \cdot (2^4 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5 \cdot 7)^2 = 2^{15} \cdot
3^4 \cdot 5^2 \cdot 7^2$$

$$14! = 2^{11} \cdot 3^5 \cdot 5^2 \cdot 7^2 \cdot 11 \cdot 13$$

$$P = \frac{2^{15} \cdot 3^4 \cdot 5^2 \cdot 7^2}{2^{11} \cdot 3^5 \cdot 5^2 \cd
ot 7^2 \cdot 11 \cdot 13} = \frac{2^4}{3 \cdot 11 \cdot 13} = \frac{16}{429}$$

**Verification of the formula on small cases:**
- $n=1$: $P = 2\cdot 1/2! = 1$ ✓ (single interval always nonempty)
