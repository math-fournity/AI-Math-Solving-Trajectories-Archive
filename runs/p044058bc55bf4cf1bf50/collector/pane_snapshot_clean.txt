## Evaluation of $\int_0^\infty \frac{1}{\cosh x}\,dx$ via Contour Integration

Since $\cosh(x)$ is an even function of $x$, $\operatorname{sech}(x) = 1/\cosh(x

$$\int_0^\infty \operatorname{sech}(x)\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\operatorname{sech}(x)\,dx.$$

It suffices to compute $I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{sech}(x)\,dx$.

where $e^{iz}+e^{-iz}=0$, i.e. $e^{2iz}=-1$, giving

$$z = \frac{\pi}{2} + n\pi,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},$$

i.e. the poles of $f$ are at $z_k = i\pi\!\left(k+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)$, $k\in\ma
thbb{Z}$, all simple.

Consider the rectangular contour $\mathcal{C}_R$ with vertices $-R,\ R,\ R+i\pi,
\ -R+i\pi$, traversed counterclockwise. The key identity is

$$\cosh(z+i\pi) = -\cosh(z),\qquad\text{hence}\qquad \operatorname{sech}(z+i\pi)
=-\operatorname{sech}(z).$$

Parametrize the four sides:

**Step 4: The vertical sides vanish as $R\to\infty$.**

$$|\cosh(R+iy)|^2 = \cosh^2 R - \sin^2 y \geq \cosh^2 R - 1 = \sinh^2 R,$$

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=i\pi/2}\operatorname{sech}(z) = \frac{1}{\sinh(i\pi/2)}
