## Proof

**Setup.** Let $c_{00}$ denote the space of finitely supported sequences, equipp
ed with the $\ell^1$ norm $\|\cdot\|_1$. Let $f: (c_{00}, \|\cdot\|_1) \to \math
bb{C}$ be linear, non-zero, and continuous (hence bounded). We seek the number o
f Hahn–Banach extensions of $f$ to $(\ell^1, \|\cdot\|_1)$, i.e., linear maps $F
: \ell^1 \to \mathbb{C}$ with $F|_{c_{00}} = f$ and $\|F\| = \|f\|$.

**Key fact: $c_{00}$ is dense in $\ell^1$.** For any $y = (y_n) \in \ell^1$, the
 truncations $y^{(N)} = (y_1, \dots, y_N, 0, 0, \dots)$ lie in $c_{00}$ and $\|y
 - y^{(N)}\|_1 = \sum_{n > N} |y_n| \to 0$.

**Unique continuous extension.** Since $f$ is bounded with $\|f\| < \infty$, for
 any $y \in \ell^1$ and any sequence $(x^{(k)}) \subset c_{00}$ with $x^{(k)} \t
o y$ in $\ell^1$-norm, the values $f(x^{(k)})$ form a Cauchy sequence:
$$|f(x^{(k)}) - f(x^{(j)})| \le \|f\| \cdot \|x^{(k)} - x^{(j)}\|_1 \to 0.$$
Hence $\lim_k f(x^{(k)})$ exists. If $(\tilde{x}^{(k)})$ is another approximatin
g sequence, then $\|x^{(k)} - \tilde{x}^{(k)}\|_1 \to 0$, so $|f(x^{(k)}) - f(\t
ilde{x}^{(k)})| \le \|f\|\|x^{(k)}-\tilde{x}^{(k)}\|_1 \to 0$, and the limit is
the same. Thus
$$F(y) := \lim_{k\to\infty} f(x^{(k)})$$
is well-defined. One checks $F$ is linear, $F|_{c_{00}} = f$, and $\|F\| = \|f\|
$ (the inequality $\|F\|\le\|f\|$ follows by continuity of the limit; the revers
e inequality is immediate since $F$ extends $f$). So $F$ is a Hahn–Banach extens
ion.

**Uniqueness.** Let $G: \ell^1 \to \mathbb{C}$ be any Hahn–Banach extension: $G|
_{c_{00}} = f$ and $\|G\| = \|f\| < \infty$, so $G$ is bounded and therefore con
tinuous. For any $y \in \ell^1$, pick $(x^{(k)}) \subset c_{00}$ with $x^{(k)} \
to y$. By continuity of $G$:
$$G(y) = \lim_k G(x^{(k)}) = \lim_k f(x^{(k)}) = F(y).$$
Hence $G = F$ on all of $\ell^1$.

**Conclusion.** Every Hahn–Banach extension coincides with the unique continuous
 extension $F$. Therefore there is exactly **one** Hahn–Banach extension.

The answer is: **One**.
