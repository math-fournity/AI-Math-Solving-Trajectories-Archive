## Proof

**Claim:** For the Lebesgue measure $\mu$ on $\mathbb{R}$, $\left[\mu(\mathbb{R}
)\right]^{1/p} = \infty$ for every $0 < p < \infty$.

**Step 1: Compute $\mu(\mathbb{R})$.**

The Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{R}$ is defined on the Borel (and Lebesgue) $\si
gma$-algebra, and it assigns to each interval $[a,b]$ its length $b-a$. Since

$$\mathbb{R} = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} [-n, n],$$

and $[-n,n] \subseteq [-(n+1), n+1]$, by continuity from below,

$$\mu(\mathbb{R}) = \mu\!\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} [-n,n]\right) = \lim_{n\to
\infty} \mu([-n,n]) = \lim_{n\to\infty} 2n = \infty.$$

Hence $\mu(\mathbb{R}) = \infty$.

**Step 2: Evaluate $\left[\mu(\mathbb{R})\right]^{1/p}$.**

We must show $\infty^{1/p} = \infty$ for $0 < p < \infty$.

By definition, $\infty^{1/p}$ is the supremum in $[0, \infty]$ of $\{a^{1/p} : a
 \in [0,\infty)\}$, i.e., the limit of $a^{1/p}$ as $a \to \infty$. Since $0 < p
 < \infty$, we have $\frac{1}{p} > 0$. For any $M > 0$, choose $a = M^{p}$ (whic
h is a finite positive real). Then

$$a^{1/p} = \left(M^{p}\right)^{1/p} = M.$$

Since $M$ was arbitrary and $a \to \infty$ as $M \to \infty$, we have

$$\lim_{a\to\infty} a^{1/p} = \infty.$$

Therefore $\infty^{1/p} = \infty$.

**Step 3: Conclude.**

Combining Steps 1 and 2,

$$\left[\mu(\mathbb{R})\right]^{1/p} = \infty^{1/p} = \infty, \qquad \forall\, 0
 < p < \infty.$$

Thus the equality $\left[\mu(\mathbb{R})\right]^{1/p} = \infty$ **holds** for ev
ery $0 < p < \infty$.
