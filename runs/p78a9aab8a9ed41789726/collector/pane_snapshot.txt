## Proof

We proceed by induction on $|S|$.

**Base case** ($|S| = 0$): Then $S = \varnothing$, and the only subset is $\varn
othing$ itself. The single-element list $(\varnothing)$ satisfies all three cond
itions vacuously.

**Inductive step**: Suppose the claim holds for every finite set of size $n \geq
 0$. Let $S$ be a finite set with $|S| = n+1$. Choose any element $x \in S$ and
set $T = S \setminus \{x\}$, so $|T| = n$.

By the induction hypothesis applied to $T$, there exists a list
$$L = (A_0, A_1, \dots, A_{2^n - 1})$$
of all subsets of $T$ such that:
- $A_0 = \varnothing$,
- every subset of $T$ appears exactly once, and
- $A_{i+1}$ is obtained from $A_i$ by adding or removing a single element, for e
ach $0 \leq i < 2^n - 1$.

Now construct the following list of subsets of $S$:
$$\mathcal{L} = (A_0,\; A_1,\; \dots,\; A_{2^n-1},\; A_{2^n-1} \cup \{x\},\; A_{
2^n-2} \cup \{x\},\; \dots,\; A_0 \cup \{x\}).$$

We verify the three required properties:

**(1) First element is the empty set.** The first element is $A_0 = \varnothing$
. ✓

**(2) Every subset of $S$ appears exactly once.** Every subset $B \subseteq S$ e
ither does not contain $x$ (so $B \subseteq T$, hence $B = A_i$ for a unique $i$
, appearing in the first half) or contains $x$ (so $B = A_i \cup \{x\}$ for a un
ique $i$, appearing in the second half). The two halves are disjoint since subse
ts in the first half exclude $x$ and those in the second half contain $x$. Thus
every subset of $S$ appears exactly once. ✓

**(3) Successive elements differ by exactly one element.** Within the first half
, consecutive pairs $(A_i, A_{i+1})$ differ by one element by the induction hypo
thesis. Within the second half, consecutive pairs are $(A_{i+1} \cup \{x\},\, A_
i \cup \{x\})$, which differ by the same single element that distinguishes $A_{i
+1}$ from $A_i$ (the $\{x\}$ cancels). At the junction between the two halves, t
he pair is $(A_{2^n-1},\, A_{2^n-1} \cup \{x\})$, which differs by exactly the s
ingle element $x$. ✓

All three conditions are satisfied, so the claim holds for $|S| = n+1$.

By induction, the claim holds for every finite set $S$. $\blacksquare$
