## Euler–Lagrange Equation for the Third-Order Energy Functional

**Setup.** Let $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ be a smooth domain and $u:\Omega\
to\mathbb{R}$ a smooth function. The third gradient $\nabla^3 u$ is the tensor o
f all third partial derivatives $u_{ijk}=\partial_i\partial_j\partial_k u$, and
its squared (Frobenius) norm is
$$\|\nabla^3 u\|^2=\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n} u_{ijk}^2.$$
The energy functional is
$$E[u]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla^3 u\|^2\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\sum
_{i,j,k}u_{ijk}^2\,dx.$$

**First variation.** Let $u_\varepsilon = u+\varepsilon\varphi$ with $\varphi\in
 C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ a compactly supported variation. The stationarity condition
 is
$$0=\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{\varepsilon=0}E[u_\varepsilon]=\int_{\O
mega}\sum_{i,j,k}u_{ijk}\,\varphi_{ijk}\,dx.$$

j\partial_k\,u_{ijk}\;\varphi\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}\partial_i\partial_j\partial_k\,
u_{ijk}\;\varphi\,dx.$$

$$\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n}\partial_i\partial_j\partial_k\,u_{ijk}=0.$$

**Simplification.** Since $u_{ijk}=\partial_i\partial_j\partial_k\,u$, we have
$$\partial_i\partial_j\partial_k\,u_{ijk}=\partial_i\partial_j\partial_k\,\parti

Therefore
$$\sum_{i,j,k}\partial_i^2\,\partial_j^2\,\partial_k^2\,u=\left(\sum_i\partial_i

**Result.** The Euler–Lagrange equation is the **triharmonic equation**:
$$\boxed{\Delta^3 u = 0\quad\text{in }\Omega,}$$
i.e. $\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n}\partial_i^2\partial_j^2\partial_k^2\,u=0$, accompanied
by natural boundary conditions on $\partial\Omega$ (since the functional depends
 on third derivatives, the natural boundary conditions involve traces of $u$, it
s normal derivative, and its second normal derivative, depending on the boundary
