## Solution

**Setup.** Let $p = 2017$ and $n = p - 1 = 2016$. We count ordered pairs $(a,b)$
 with $1 \le a,b \le p(p-1)$ and $p \mid (a^b - b^a)$, since $c$ is then uniquel
y determined.

**CRT decomposition.** Since $\gcd(p, p-1) = 1$, the map $a \mapsto (a \bmod p,\
; a \bmod (p-1))$ is a bijection on residues mod $p(p-1)$. The interval $\{1, \l
dots, p(p-1)\}$ is a complete residue system, so each pair $(a \bmod p,\; a \bmo
d (p{-}1))$ with $a \bmod p \in \{0,\ldots,p{-}1\}$ and $a \bmod (p{-}1) \in \{0
,\ldots,p{-}2\}$ occurs **exactly once**.

**Case analysis mod $p$.**

- **Both $a \equiv 0$, $b \equiv 0 \pmod{p}$:** Then $a^b \equiv b^a \equiv 0$.
Count: $(p-1)^2 = n^2$.
- **Exactly one $\equiv 0$:** If $a \equiv 0, b \not\equiv 0$, then $a^b \equiv
0$ but $b^a \not\equiv 0$. Never works. Likewise the other way.
- **Both $\not\equiv 0 \pmod{p}$:** By Fermat's little theorem, $a^b \equiv \alp
ha^{\beta} \pmod{p}$ where $\alpha = a \bmod p \in \{1,\ldots,p{-}1\}$ and $\bet
a = b \bmod (p{-}1) \in \{0,\ldots,n{-}1\}$. Similarly $b^a \equiv \gamma^{\delt
a}$ with $\gamma = b \bmod p$, $\delta = a \bmod (p{-}1)$. We need $\alpha^{\bet
a} \equiv \gamma^{\delta} \pmod{p}$.

**Counting Case 2.** Fix a primitive root $g \bmod p$ and write $\alpha = g^u$,
$\gamma = g^v$ with $u,v \in \{0,\ldots,n{-}1\}$. The condition becomes $u\beta
\equiv v\delta \pmod{n}$. Since each $(\alpha, \delta)$ and $(\gamma, \beta)$ pa
ir occurs once, we get:

$$N_2 = \sum_{\beta=0}^{n-1}\sum_{\delta=0}^{n-1} \#\{(u,v) \in \{0,\ldots,n{-}1
\}^2 : u\beta \equiv v\delta \pmod{n}\}.$$

For fixed $\beta, \delta$, the number of solutions is $n \cdot \gcd(\gcd(\beta,n
),\, \gcd(\delta,n))$ (standard counting: the congruence $u\beta \equiv t$ has $
\gcd(\beta,n)$ solutions when $\gcd(\beta,n) \mid t$, and summing over compatibl
e $t$ gives $n \cdot \gcd(d_\beta, d_\delta)$).

So $N_2 = n \cdot S$ where $S = \sum_{\beta,\delta} \gcd(\gcd(\beta,n), \gcd(\de
lta,n))$.

**Simplifying $S$.** Group by $d = \gcd(\beta,n)$, $e = \gcd(\delta,n)$. The cou
nt of $\beta$ with $\gcd(\beta,n)=d$ is $\varphi(n/d)$. Using $\gcd(d,e) = \sum_
{k \mid d,\, k \mid e} \varphi(k)$:

$$S = \sum_{k \mid n} \varphi(k) \left(\sum_{\substack{d \mid n \\ k \mid d}} \v
arphi(n/d)\right)^2 = \sum_{k \mid n} \varphi(k) \cdot (n/k)^2 = n^2 \sum_{k \mi
d n} \frac{\varphi(k)}{k^2},$$

since $\sum_{d \mid n,\, k \mid d} \varphi(n/d) = \sum_{j \mid n/k} \varphi(j) =
 n/k$.

Therefore:

$$\boxed{N_2 = n^3 \sum_{k \mid n} \frac{\varphi(k)}{k^2}}.$$

**Verification with $p=3$:** $n=2$, $\sum = 1 + \tfrac{1}{4} = \tfrac{5}{4}$, $N
_2 = 8 \cdot \tfrac{5}{4} = 10$, $N_1 = 4$, $N = 14$. Direct enumeration confirm
s $N_2 = 10$. ✓

**Computation for $n = 2016 = 2^5 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 7$.** The sum $\sum_{k \mid n}
 \varphi(k)/k^2$ is multiplicative:

$$\prod_{q \mid n} \sum_{j=0}^{v_q(n)} \frac{\varphi(q^j)}{q^{2j}} = \underbrace
{\left(1 + \tfrac{1}{4} + \tfrac{2}{16} + \tfrac{4}{64} + \tfrac{8}{256} + \tfra
c{16}{1024}\right)}_{q=2} \cdot \underbrace{\left(1 + \tfrac{2}{9} + \tfrac{6}{8
1}\right)}_{q=3} \cdot \underbrace{\left(1 + \tfrac{6}{49}\right)}_{q=7}$$

$$= \frac{95}{64} \cdot \frac{35}{27} \cdot \frac{55}{49} = \frac{182875}{84672}
 = \frac{26125}{12096}.$$

(Here $182875 = 5^3 \cdot 7 \cdot 11 \cdot 19$ and $84672 = 2^6 \cdot 3^3 \cdot
7^2$; canceling the common factor $7$.)

Now:

$$\frac{n^3}{12096} = \frac{2016^3}{12096} = \frac{2^{15} \cdot 3^6 \cdot 7^3}{2
^6 \cdot 3^3 \cdot 7} = 2^9 \cdot 3^3 \cdot 7^2 = 512 \cdot 27 \cdot 49 = 677376
.$$

$$N_2 = 677376 \times 26125 = 17{,}696{,}448{,}000.$$

$$N_1 = n^2 = 2016^2 = 4{,}064{,}256.$$

$$N = N_1 + N_2 = 17{,}700{,}512{,}256.$$

$$N \bmod 1{,}000{,}000 = 512256.$$
