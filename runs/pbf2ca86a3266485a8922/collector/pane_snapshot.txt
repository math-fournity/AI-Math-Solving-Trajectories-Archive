**Answer: Yes, $D$ is biholomorphically equivalent to the unit disk.**

**Proof.**

Let $\phi: D \setminus \{P_0\} \to \mathbb{D}^* = \{z : 0 < |z| < 1\}$ be the gi
ven biholomorphism. We extend $\phi$ across $P_0$ and show the extension is a bi
holomorphism $D \to \mathbb{D}$.

**Step 1: $\phi$ extends holomorphically to $P_0$.**

Choose a local coordinate $\zeta: U \xrightarrow{\sim} \{|\zeta| < \epsilon\}$ o
n $R$ with $U \subset D$ and $\zeta(P_0) = 0$. Define
$$g = \phi \circ \zeta^{-1}: \{0 < |\zeta| < \epsilon\} \to \mathbb{D}^*.$$
Then $g$ is holomorphic and bounded ($|g| < 1$). By **Riemann's removable singul
arity theorem**, $g$ extends to a holomorphic function $\hat{g}: \{|\zeta| < \ep
silon\} \to \mathbb{C}$ with $|\hat{g}(0)| \leq 1$.

If $|\hat{g}(0)| = 1$, then $|\hat{g}|$ attains its maximum at the interior poin
t $0$, so by the **maximum modulus principle**, $\hat{g}$ is constant—contradict
ing the injectivity of $g$ on the punctured disk. Hence $|\hat{g}(0)| < 1$.

Define $\Phi: D \to \mathbb{D}$ by $\Phi(P) = \phi(P)$ for $P \in D \setminus \{
P_0\}$ and $\Phi(P_0) = \hat{g}(0)$. Then $\Phi$ is holomorphic on $D$.

**Step 2: $\hat{g}$ is injective on the full disk $\{|\zeta| < \epsilon\}$.**

We know $g$ is injective on $\{0 < |\zeta| < \epsilon\}$. Suppose $\hat{g}(0) =
\hat{g}(\zeta_0)$ for some $0 < |\zeta_0| < \epsilon$. Let $k \geq 1$ and $m \ge
q 1$ be the orders of vanishing of $\hat{g} - \hat{g}(0)$ at $0$ and $\zeta_0$ r

hat{g}(\zeta) = w$ has $k$ solutions near $0$ and $m$ solutions near $\zeta_0$,
all lying in $\{0 < |\zeta| < \epsilon\}$. Thus $g$ takes the value $w$ at least
 $k + m \geq 2$ times, contradicting injectivity. Therefore $\hat{g}$ is injecti
ve on $\{|\zeta| < \epsilon\}$, so $\Phi$ is a local biholomorphism at $P_0$.

**Step 3: $\Phi(P_0) = 0$.**

Suppose $\Phi(P_0) \neq 0$. Then $\Phi(P_0) \in \mathbb{D}^*$, so there exists $
P' \in D \setminus \{P_0\}$ with $\phi(P') = \Phi(P_0)$, i.e., $\Phi(P_0) = \Phi
(P')$ with $P' \neq P_0$.

Now $\Phi$ is holomorphic on $D$, injective on $D \setminus \{P_0\}$ (since $\ph
i$ is), and a local biholomorphism at $P_0$ (Step 2). Thus $\Phi: D \to \Phi(D)$
 is a **surjective local biholomorphism**, hence a **covering map**. Since $\Phi
(P_0) \in \mathbb{D}^*$, we have $\Phi(D) = \mathbb{D}^* \cup \{\Phi(P_0)\} = \m
athbb{D}^*$.

The covering degree equals the number of preimages of any point. For any $w \in
\mathbb{D}^* \setminus \{\Phi(P_0)\}$, the unique preimage lies in $D \setminus
\{P_0\}$, so the degree is $1$. But $w = \Phi(P_0)$ has two preimages ($P_0$ and
 $P'$), contradicting the constant-degree property of covering maps.

Therefore $\Phi(P_0) = 0$.

**Step 4: $\Phi: D \to \mathbb{D}$ is a biholomorphism.**

$\Phi$ is holomorphic and:
- **Injective**: $\Phi(P_0) = 0 \notin \mathbb{D}^* = \phi(D \setminus \{P_0\})$

- **Surjective**: $\Phi(D) = \Phi(D \setminus \{P_0\}) \cup \{\Phi(P_0)\} = \mat

A holomorphic bijection between Riemann surfaces is a biholomorphism. $\square$
