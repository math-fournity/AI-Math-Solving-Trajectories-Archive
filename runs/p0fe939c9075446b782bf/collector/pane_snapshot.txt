Solution

Answer: Yes. For every ultraweakly closed two-sided ideal $I$ in a von Neumann
algebra $M$, there exists an ultraweakly closed two-sided ideal $J \subset M$
with $M = I \oplus J$.

Proof

The key fact is that every ultraweakly closed two-sided ideal in a von Neumann
algebra is generated by a central projection.

Step 1: $I$ has a unit. Since $I$ is a norm-closed $$-subalgebra of $M$ (closed
ideals in a $C^$-algebra are self-adjoint), $I$ is itself a $C^*$-algebra.
Moreover, $I$ is ultraweakly closed, hence (by the bicommutant theorem, applied
to $I$ acting on the underlying Hilbert space) $I$ is a von Neumann algebra.
Therefore $I$ possesses a unit $p$, which is a projection in $M$.

Step 2: $p$ is central. For any $b \in M$:

  • Since $I$ is a right ideal, $bp \in I$; as $p$ is the unit of $I$, we get
  $p(bp) = bp$, i.e. $pbp = bp$.
  • Since $I$ is a left ideal, $pb \in I$; as $p$ is the unit of $I$, we get
  $(pb)p = pb$, i.e. $pbp = pb$.

Combining: $bp = pbp = pb$ for all $b \in M$. Hence $p \in Z(M)$, and $I = Mp =
pM = pMp$.

Step 3: Construct $J$ and verify the direct sum. Set $J := M(1-p)$. Since $1-p$
is also a central projection, $J$ is an ultraweakly closed two-sided ideal. We
verify:

  • Sum: Every $x \in M$ decomposes as $x = \underbrace{xp}{\in, I} +
  \underbrace{x(1-p)}{\in, J}$.
  • Trivial intersection: If $x \in I \cap J$, then $x = xp$ (since $x \in I =
  Mp$) and $x = x(1-p)$ (since $x \in J = M(1-p)$). But $xp = x(1-p)$ implies $x
  = xp = x(1-p) = x - xp$, so $xp = 0$, hence $x = 0$.

Therefore $M = I \oplus J$. $\blacksquare$
