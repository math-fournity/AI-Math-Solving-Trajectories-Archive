## Solution

We evaluate $\displaystyle S = \sum_{k=1}^n \binom{n}{k} k^2$.

**Generating function approach.** Start from the binomial theorem:

$$\sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k} x^k = (1+x)^n.$$

Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$:

$$\sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k} k\, x^{k-1} = n(1+x)^{n-1}.$$

Multiply both sides by $x$:

$$\sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k} k\, x^k = n\,x\,(1+x)^{n-1}. \tag{1}$$

Differentiate (1) with respect to $x$:

$$\sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k} k^2\, x^{k-1} = n(1+x)^{n-1} + n(n-1)\,x\,(1+x)^{n-2
}.$$

Now set $x = 1$:

$$\sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k} k^2 = n\cdot 2^{n-1} + n(n-1)\cdot 2^{n-2}.$$

Factor out $2^{n-2}$:

$$\sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k} k^2 = 2^{n-2}\bigl(2n + n(n-1)\bigr) = 2^{n-2}\,n\,(
n+1).$$

Since the $k=0$ term contributes $\binom{n}{0}\cdot 0^2 = 0$, the sum from $k=1$
 equals the sum from $k=0$:

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^n \binom{n}{k} k^2 = n(n+1)\,2^{n-2}}.$$

**Verification.**
- $n=1$: $\binom{1}{1}\cdot 1 = 1$; formula gives $1\cdot 2\cdot 2^{-1} = 1$. ✓
- $n=2$: $2\cdot 1 + 1\cdot 4 = 6$; formula gives $2\cdot 3\cdot 2^{0} = 6$. ✓
- $n=3$: $3\cdot 1 + 3\cdot 4 + 1\cdot 9 = 24$; formula gives $3\cdot 4\cdot 2 =
 24$. ✓
