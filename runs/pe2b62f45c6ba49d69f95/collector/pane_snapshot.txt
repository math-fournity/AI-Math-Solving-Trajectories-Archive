Yes, it is true that $\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(f^{-1}(0)) \le n-1$.

## Proof

Let $Z = f^{-1}(0)$. We prove $\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(Z) \le n-1$.

**Step 1: $Z$ has empty interior.**

Suppose for contradiction that $Z$ contains a nonempty open ball $B \subseteq \m
athbb{R}^n$. Then $f|_B \equiv 0$. Since $f$ is real-analytic on the connected o
pen set $\mathbb{R}^n$, vanishing on a nonempty open set implies (by the identit
y theorem for real-analytic functions: the Taylor series at any point of $B$ is
identically zero, and analytic continuation along paths propagates this to all o
f $\mathbb{R}^n$) that $f \equiv 0$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$, contradicting the hypothe
sis. Hence $Z$ has empty interior.

**Step 2: $Z$ is a real-analytic set, hence admits a locally finite (in fact cou
ntable, globally) stratification into real-analytic submanifolds.**

$Z$ is the zero locus of a real-analytic function, so it is a (closed) real-anal
ytic subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$. By the Łojasiewicz structure theorem for real-ana
lytic sets, every real-analytic subset $A$ of an open set $U \subseteq \mathbb{R
}^n$ admits a **semianalytic stratification**: there is a locally finite partiti
on of $A$ into real-analytic submanifolds (strata) $S_\alpha$ of $\mathbb{R}^n$,
 each connected, satisfying the frontier condition. Concretely, this is obtained
 by iteratively taking the singular locus (which is itself a real-analytic set o
f strictly smaller dimension) until reaching smooth points.

Globally, cover $\mathbb{R}^n$ by the countable family of compact balls $\overli
ne{B}(0, m)$, $m = 1, 2, \dots$. On each ball the stratification is locally fini
te (hence, by compactness, finite). Taking the union over $m$, we obtain $Z$ as
a **countable** union of real-analytic submanifolds:
$$Z = \bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty} M_j, \qquad M_j \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n \text{ a con
nected real-analytic submanifold.}$$

**Step 3: Each stratum has dimension $\le n-1$.**

Each $M_j$ is an open subset of $Z$ (in the stratification, strata are locally c
losed and cover $Z$; the top-dimensional strata are open in $Z$). If some $M_j$
had dimension $n$, then $M_j$ would be an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$ containe
d in $Z$, contradicting Step 1. Therefore $\dim M_j \le n-1$ for every $j$.

**Step 4: Hausdorff dimension of each stratum.**

A $k$-dimensional $C^1$ submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^n$ has Hausdorff dimension ex
actly $k$ (it is locally a graph of a $C^1$ function over $\mathbb{R}^k$, and su
ch graphs have Hausdorff dimension $k$). Hence
$$\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(M_j) = \dim M_j \le n-1 \quad \text{for every } j.$$

**Step 5: Countable stability of Hausdorff dimension.**

Hausdorff dimension is countably stable:
$$\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\!\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty} M_j\right) = \sup_{j} \dim_{\
mathcal{H}}(M_j).$$
(Indeed, $\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\bigcup E_j) \ge \sup \dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E_j)$ is i
mmediate, and $\le$ follows because $\mathcal{H}^s(\bigcup E_j) \le \sum \mathca
l{H}^s(E_j)$, so if every $\mathcal{H}^s(E_j) = 0$ then $\mathcal{H}^s(\bigcup E
_j) = 0$.)

Applying this with $s = n-1 + \varepsilon$ for any $\varepsilon > 0$: each $\dim
_{\mathcal{H}}(M_j) \le n-1 < s$, so $\mathcal{H}^s(M_j) = 0$, hence $\mathcal{H
}^s(Z) = 0$, giving $\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(Z) \le n-1$.

## Conclusion

For every $n > 1$ and every real-analytic $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ not i
dentically zero,
$$\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\bigl(f^{-1}(0)\bigr) \le n-1.$$

The answer is **yes**.
