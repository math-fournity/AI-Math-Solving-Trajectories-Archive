Looking at this problem, I need to find P(Status Alpha | second container is M).

## Setup

- 52 containers: 13 Red (R), 13 Black-S (B), 26 Miscellaneous (M).
- **Status Alpha**: 2 B's processed before any R.
- **Status Beta**: an R processed before 2 B's.
- M's are neutral — they don't affect the race between B's and R's.
- Container 2 is known to be M. Container 1 is unknown.

## Key Observation

The race between Status Alpha and Status Beta depends **only on the relative ord
er of R's and B's** among all containers. Miscellaneous containers are simply sk
ipped — they contribute nothing to either trigger.

Conditioning on "container 2 is M" removes one M from a known position but does
**not** alter the relative ordering of the 13 R's and 13 B's among the remaining
 51 positions. Their relative order remains a uniformly random permutation of 13
 R's and 13 B's.

## Direct Computation (verification via conditioning on container 1)

**Distribution of container 1 given container 2 is M:**

$$P(\text{c1}=R \mid \text{c2}=M) = \frac{(13/52)(26/51)}{26/52} = \frac{13}{51}
$$

$$P(\text{c1}=B \mid \text{c2}=M) = \frac{13}{51}, \qquad P(\text{c1}=M \mid \te
xt{c2}=M) = \frac{25}{51}$$

(Check: 13+13+25 = 51. ✓)

**Case 1: c1 = R (prob 13/51).** A Red appeared before 2 Black-S → Status Beta a
lready triggered. P(Alpha) = **0**.

**Case 2: c1 = B (prob 13/51).** One B found, zero R found. Need one more B befo
re any R. Remaining non-M: 13 R + 12 B = 25 containers in random order. Status A
lpha iff the first of these is B:

$$p_1 = \frac{12}{25}$$

**Case 3: c1 = M (prob 25/51).** Zero B, zero R found. Remaining non-M: 13 R + 1
3 B = 26 containers in random order. Status Alpha iff the first two are both B:

$$p_0 = \frac{\binom{13}{2}}{\binom{26}{2}} = \frac{13 \cdot 12}{26 \cdot 25} =
\frac{6}{25}$$

## Combining

$$P(\text{Alpha} \mid \text{c2}=M) = \frac{13}{51}\cdot 0 + \frac{13}{51}\cdot\f
rac{12}{25} + \frac{25}{51}\cdot\frac{6}{25}$$

$$= \frac{156 + 150}{1275} = \frac{306}{1275} = \frac{6}{25}$$

## Why the conditioning is irrelevant

Since M's are neutral, the outcome of the race is determined entirely by the rel
ative order of the 26 non-M containers (13 R + 13 B). Fixing position 2 as M sim
ply removes one M from a known slot; the 13 R's and 13 B's remain in a uniformly
 random relative order across the other 51 positions. Therefore:

$$P(\text{Alpha} \mid \text{c2}=M) = P(\text{Alpha}) = \frac{\binom{13}{2}}{\bin
om{26}{2}} = \frac{6}{25}$$

The detailed case analysis confirms this: the weighted average over the unknown
first container collapses exactly to 6/25.

## Answer
