**Proof.** Let $S \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ be uncountable. We must show $S$ contain
s a sequence of distinct elements that does not converge to $0$.

For each positive integer $n$, define
$$A_n = S \cap \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| \ge 1/n\}.$$

Since $S \setminus \{0\} = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n$, and $S$ is uncountable (
so $S \setminus \{0\}$ is uncountable), not all $A_n$ can be countable — a count
able union of countable sets is countable. Hence there exists some $n$ such that
 $A_n$ is uncountable, and in particular infinite.

Choose any sequence $(z_k)_{k \ge 1}$ of distinct elements of $A_n$. Then $|z_k|
 \ge 1/n$ for every $k$. A sequence converging to $0$ must eventually satisfy $|
z_k| < 1/n$, which is impossible here. Therefore $(z_k)$ does not converge to $0
$.

Thus every uncountable subset of $\mathbb{C}$ contains such a sequence.
