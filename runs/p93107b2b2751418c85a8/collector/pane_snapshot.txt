## Proof

Let $\operatorname{Syl}_p(G)$ denote the set of Sylow $p$-subgroups of $G$, so $
|\operatorname{Syl}_p(G)| = n_p$. Fix a Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$ and let it act on
 $\operatorname{Syl}_p(G)$ by conjugation.

**Step 1: Orbit sizes are powers of $p$.**

By the orbit-stabilizer theorem, each orbit size divides $|P|$, which is a power
 of $p$. Hence every orbit has size $p^a$ for some $a \ge 0$.

**Step 2: The unique fixed point is $P$ itself.**

A Sylow $p$-subgroup $Q$ is a fixed point iff $P \le N_G(Q)$. Since $Q \triangle
lefteq N_G(Q)$, the subgroup $Q$ is the *unique* Sylow $p$-subgroup of $N_G(Q)$.
 But $P \le N_G(Q)$ and $|P| = |Q|$ (both are Sylow in $G$), so $P$ is also a Sy
low $p$-subgroup of $N_G(Q)$. By uniqueness, $P = Q$.

Thus the only orbit of size $1$ is $\{P\}$, and every other orbit has size $p^{a
_i}$ with $a_i \ge 1$. Partitioning gives:

$$n_p = 1 + \sum_i p^{a_i}, \qquad a_i \ge 1. \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Step 3: Deriving the conclusion.**

The hypothesis is $n_p \not\equiv 1 \pmod{p^2}$, i.e., $n_p - 1 \not\equiv 0 \pm
od{p^2}$. By $(\ast)$:

$$n_p - 1 = \sum_i p^{a_i}.$$

Suppose, for contradiction, that *no* orbit has size $p$. Then every $a_i \ge 2$
, so each $p^{a_i} \equiv 0 \pmod{p^2}$, whence

$$n_p - 1 = \sum_i p^{a_i} \equiv 0 \pmod{p^2},$$

i.e., $n_p \equiv 1 \pmod{p^2}$, contradicting the hypothesis.

Therefore at least one index $i$ has $a_i = 1$, meaning the corresponding orbit
has size exactly $p$. Denoting a Sylow $p$-subgroup in that orbit by $Q$, the or
bit of $Q$ under conjugation by $P$ has size $p$. $\blacksquare$
