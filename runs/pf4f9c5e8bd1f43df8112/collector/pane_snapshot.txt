For each $n \in \mathbb{N}$, define
$$E_n = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : f^{(n)}(z) = 0\}.$$
Since $f^{(n)}$ is entire (hence continuous), each $E_n$ is a closed subset of $
\mathbb{C}$.

The hypothesis states that for every $z \in \mathbb{C}$, there exists $n_z \in \
mathbb{N}$ with $f^{(n_z)}(z) = 0$, which means
$$\mathbb{C} = \bigcup_{n \in \mathbb{N}} E_n.$$

Now $\mathbb{C}$ is a complete metric space. By the **Baire Category Theorem**,
a complete metric space cannot be written as a countable union of closed sets wi
th empty interior. Therefore, at least one $E_N$ must have nonempty interior; th
at is, there exists some $N \in \mathbb{N}$ and a nonempty open disk $D \subsete

n entire function that vanishes on an open set. By the **identity theorem** for
holomorphic functions, an entire function that vanishes on a set with a limit po
int must vanish identically. Since $D$ has limit points,
$$f^{(N)} \equiv 0 \quad \text{on all of } \mathbb{C}.$$

This means $f^{(N)}$ is identically zero, so $f$ is a polynomial of degree at mo
st $N - 1$.
