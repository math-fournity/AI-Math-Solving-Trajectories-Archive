# Weak Formulation and Galerkin-FEM Formulation for the Rope Deflection Problem

## The Problem

We consider the Dirichlet boundary value problem

$$
\begin{cases}
-u'' + \dfrac{k}{T}\,u = \dfrac{w}{T} & \text{in } (0,1),\\[4pt]
u(0)=0,\\
u(1)=0.
\end{cases}
$$

---

## 1. Functional Setting

Let $V := H_0^1(0,1)$, the standard Sobolev space

$$
H_0^1(0,1) = \Big\{ v \in L^2(0,1)\;\Big|\; v' \in L^2(0,1),\; v(0)=v(1)=0 \Big\
},
$$

equipped with the norm $\|v\|_{H_0^1}^2 = \int_0^1 (v')^2\,dx$.

---

## 2. Weak (Variational) Formulation

**Derivation.** Let $u$ be a smooth solution of the strong form and let $v \in V
$ be a test function. Multiply the equation by $v$ and integrate over $(0,1)$:

$$
-\int_0^1 u''\,v\,dx + \frac{k}{T}\int_0^1 u\,v\,dx = \frac{w}{T}\int_0^1 v\,dx.
$$

Integrate the second-derivative term by parts:

$$
-\int_0^1 u''\,v\,dx = \int_0^1 u'\,v'\,dx - \big[u'\,v\big]_0^1.
$$

Since $v \in H_0^1(0,1)$, we have $v(0)=v(1)=0$, so the boundary term vanishes.
We obtain

$$
\int_0^1 u'\,v'\,dx + \frac{k}{T}\int_0^1 u\,v\,dx = \frac{w}{T}\int_0^1 v\,dx.
$$

**Weak formulation.** Define the bilinear form $a:V\times V\to\mathbb{R}$ and th
e linear functional $\ell:V\to\mathbb{R}$ by

$$
a(u,v) := \int_0^1 u'\,v'\,dx + \frac{k}{T}\int_0^1 u\,v\,dx, \qquad
\ell(v) := \frac{w}{T}\int_0^1 v\,dx.
$$

> **Find $u \in V = H_0^1(0,1)$ such that**
> $$a(u,v) = \ell(v) \quad \text{for all } v \in V.$$

**Well-posedness.** The bilinear form $a$ is:
- **Continuous**: by Cauchy–Schwarz, $|a(u,v)| \le \big(1 + \tfrac{k}{T}\big)\|u
\|_{H_0^1}\|v\|_{H_0^1}$.
- **Coercive**: $a(v,v) = \|v'\|_{L^2}^2 + \tfrac{k}{T}\|v\|_{L^2}^2 \ge \min\!\
big(1,\tfrac{k}{T}\big)\,\|v\|_{H_0^1}^2$ (using the Poincaré inequality $\|v'\|
_{L^2}^2 \ge \|v\|_{L^2}^2$ on $(0,1)$, so in fact $a(v,v)\ge \|v'\|_{L^2}^2 \ge
 C\|v\|_{H_0^1}^2$).

The functional $\ell$ is continuous since $|\ell(v)| \le \tfrac{w}{T}\|v\|_{L^2}
 \le \tfrac{w}{T}\|v\|_{H_0^1}$.

By the **Lax–Milgram theorem**, there exists a unique $u \in V$ solving the weak
 problem.

---

## 3. Galerkin–Finite Element Formulation

### 3.1 Discretization

Introduce a partition of $[0,1]$ into $N$ elements. Let $0 = x_0 < x_1 < \cdots
< x_N = 1$ with mesh size $h = \max_i (x_i - x_{i-1})$. Define the finite-dimens
ional subspace

$$
V_h := \Big\{ v_h \in C^0([0,1])\;\Big|\; v_h\big|_{[x_{i-1},x_i]} \in \mathbb{P
}_1,\; v_h(0)=v_h(1)=0 \Big\} \subset V,
$$

the space of continuous, piecewise-linear functions vanishing at the endpoints.
A basis $\{\varphi_j\}_{j=1}^{N-1}$ of $V_h$ is given by the **hat functions** a
ssociated with the interior nodes $x_j$, $j=1,\dots,N-1$:

$$
\varphi_j(x_i) = \delta_{ij}, \qquad \operatorname{supp}(\varphi_j) = [x_{j-1},
x_{j+1}].
$$

### 3.2 Galerkin formulation

> **Find $u_h \in V_h$ such that**
> $$a(u_h, v_h) = \ell(v_h) \quad \text{for all } v_h \in V_h,$$
> i.e.,
> $$\int_0^1 u_h'\,v_h'\,dx + \frac{k}{T}\int_0^1 u_h\,v_h\,dx = \frac{w}{T}\int
_0^1 v_h\,dx \quad \forall\, v_h \in V_h.$$

### 3.3 Algebraic system

Writing $u_h = \sum_{j=1}^{N-1} u_j\,\varphi_j$ and choosing $v_h = \varphi_i$ f
or $i=1,\dots,N-1$, we obtain the linear system

$$
\sum_{j=1}^{N-1} a(\varphi_j, \varphi_i)\,u_j = \ell(\varphi_i), \qquad i=1,\dot
s,N-1,
$$

which in matrix form reads

$$
\big(A\big)\,\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{f},
$$

where the **stiffness matrix** $A$ and the **load vector** $\mathbf{f}$ are

$$
A_{ij} = \underbrace{\int_0^1 \varphi_j'\,\varphi_i'\,dx}_{K_{ij}\;(\text{stiffn
ess})} + \frac{k}{T}\underbrace{\int_0^1 \varphi_j\,\varphi_i\,dx}_{M_{ij}\;(\te
xt{mass})}, \qquad f_i = \frac{w}{T}\int_0^1 \varphi_i\,dx.
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\boxed{\;\Big(K + \tfrac{k}{T}\,M\Big)\,\mathbf{u} = \tfrac{w}{T}\,\mathbf{f}\;}
$$

where:
- $K$ is the **stiffness matrix**: $K_{ij} = \int_0^1 \varphi_j'\varphi_i'\,dx$,
- $M$ is the **mass matrix**: $M_{ij} = \int_0^1 \varphi_j\varphi_i\,dx$,
- $\mathbf{f}$ is the **load vector** with $f_i = \int_0^1 \varphi_i\,dx$.

### 3.4 Element-level contributions (uniform mesh)

For a uniform mesh with element length $h = 1/N$, the local stiffness and mass m
atrices on each element $[x_{i-1}, x_i]$ (in local coordinates) are

$$
K^{(e)} = \frac{1}{h}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
M^{(e)} = \frac{h}{6}\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
\mathbf{f}^{(e)} = \frac{w}{T}\,\frac{h}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix},
$$

which are assembled (with the standard assembly procedure and elimination of the
 boundary degrees of freedom corresponding to $u(0)=u(1)=0$) into the global sys
tem.

### 3.5 Convergence

Since $V_h \subset V$ and $a$ is continuous and coercive (uniformly in $h$), **C
éa's lemma** guarantees

$$
\|u - u_h\|_{H_0^1} \le \frac{1+\tfrac{k}{T}}{\min(1,\tfrac{k}{T})}\,\inf_{v_h \
in V_h}\|u - v_h\|_{H_0^1}.
$$

Using standard interpolation estimates for $\mathbb{P}_1$ elements, $\inf_{v_h\i
n V_h}\|u-v_h\|_{H_0^1} \le C\,h\,|u|_{H^2}$, so

$$
\|u - u_h\|_{H_0^1} \le C\,h\,|u|_{H^2},
$$

i.e., **first-order convergence** in the $H^1$ norm (and second-order in $L^2$ v
ia the Aubin–Nitsche duality argument), provided $u \in H^2(0,1)$, which holds h
ere since the data $w/T$ is constant.
