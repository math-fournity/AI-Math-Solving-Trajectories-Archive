## Solution

**Claim:** The set contains exactly one element, namely $f \equiv 0$. The answer
 is **(b)**.

**Proof.**

Let $f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}$ be continuous with
$$\int_0^1 t^n f(t)\,dt = 0, \qquad n=1,2,3,\dots$$

**Step 1: Reduce to all moments vanishing.**

Define $g(t) = t\,f(t)$. Since $f$ is continuous on $[0,1]$, so is $g$. For ever
y $n \geq 0$,
$$\int_0^1 t^n g(t)\,dt = \int_0^1 t^{n+1} f(t)\,dt = 0,$$
because $n+1 \geq 1$ and the hypothesis applies. Hence
$$\int_0^1 t^n g(t)\,dt = 0 \quad\text{for all } n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$$

**Step 2: Use Weierstrass approximation.**

By linearity of the integral, $\int_0^1 p(t)\,g(t)\,dt = 0$ for every polynomial
 $p$.

By the Weierstrass approximation theorem, polynomials are uniformly dense in $C[
0,1]$. Choose a sequence of polynomials $p_k \to g$ uniformly on $[0,1]$. Then
$$\int_0^1 g(t)^2\,dt = \lim_{k\to\infty}\int_0^1 p_k(t)\,g(t)\,dt = \lim_{k\to\
infty} 0 = 0.$$

Since $g$ is continuous and $\int_0^1 g(t)^2\,dt = 0$, we conclude $g \equiv 0$
on $[0,1]$.

**Step 3: Conclude $f \equiv 0$.**

Since $g(t) = t\,f(t) = 0$ for all $t \in [0,1]$, we have $f(t) = 0$ for all $t
\in (0,1]$. By continuity of $f$ at $0$,
$$f(0) = \lim_{t\to 0^+} f(t) = 0.$$

Therefore $f \equiv 0$ on $[0,1]$.

**Step 4: Verify and count.**

The zero function clearly satisfies $\int_0^1 t^n \cdot 0\,dt = 0$ for all $n \g
eq 1$, so it belongs to the set. By Steps 1–3 it is the only member. The set is
a singleton.

The cardinality is **1**, corresponding to option **(b) contains a single elemen
t**.
