## Proof

We classify short exact sequences of groups
$$1 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\iota} E \xrightarrow{\pi} \mathbb{Z} \to 1$$
up to isomorphism of the middle group $E$ (equivalently, up to equivalence of ex
tensions).

**Step 1: Every such extension splits.**

The quotient $\mathbb{Z}$ is a free group (free on one generator). A standard re
sult in homological algebra / group theory states that a group $Q$ is projective
 in the category of groups if and only if $Q$ is free. Since $\mathbb{Z}$ is fre
e, it is projective, and therefore every short exact sequence
$$1 \to N \to E \to \mathbb{Z} \to 1$$
splits: there exists a homomorphic section $s\colon \mathbb{Z}\to E$ with $\pi\c
irc s = \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{Z}}$.

Consequently every extension is a semidirect product:
$$E \cong \mathbb{Z} \rtimes_\varphi \mathbb{Z}$$
for some homomorphism $\varphi\colon \mathbb{Z}\to \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z})$.

**Step 2: Classify the actions.**

We have $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z})\cong \{\pm 1\}\cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ (t
he only automorphisms of $\mathbb{Z}$ are $n\mapsto n$ and $n\mapsto -n$). A hom
omorphism
$$\varphi\colon \mathbb{Z}\to \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z})\cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{
Z}$$
is determined by $\varphi(1)\in\{\pm 1\}$. Hence there are exactly two actions:

- **Trivial action** $\varphi(1)=+1$: the generator of the quotient $\mathbb{Z}$
 acts on the kernel $\mathbb{Z}$ as the identity.
- **Inversion action** $\varphi(1)=-1$: the generator of the quotient acts on th
e kernel by $n\mapsto -n$.

**Step 3: Identify the resulting groups and check non-isomorphism.**

- **Trivial action:** $E\cong \mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}$, the direct product. T
his group is **abelian**.

- **Inversion action:** $E\cong \mathbb{Z}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}$ with presentation
$$\langle\, a,b \;\mid\; bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\,\rangle,$$
which is the fundamental group of the Klein bottle. This group is **non-abelian*
* (e.g. $ba = a^{-1}b \neq ab$).

Since one group is abelian and the other is not, they are not isomorphic. No two
 distinct actions yield isomorphic groups.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Every extension of $\mathbb{Z}$ by $\mathbb{Z}$ splits (because $\mathbb{Z}$ is
free, hence projective). The split extensions are classified by $\mathrm{Hom}(\m
athbb{Z},\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}))\cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, giving exactly
two semidirect products: the direct product $\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}$ (abelia
n) and the Klein bottle group $\langle a,b\mid bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\rangle$ (non-abel
ian). These two are non-isomorphic.
