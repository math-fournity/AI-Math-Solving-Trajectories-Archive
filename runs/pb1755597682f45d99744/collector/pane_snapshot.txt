## Proof

Let $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ satisfy both equations.

**Step 1 — Transform equation (2).** Setting $u=x+y,\;v=x-y$ (a bijection $\math
bb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2$), equation (2) becomes
$$f(uv)=u\,f(v)+v\,f(u)\qquad\forall\,u,v\in\mathbb{R}.\tag{2'}$$
Taking $u=v=0$ gives $f(0)=0$; taking $u=v=1$ gives $f(1)=2f(1)$, so $f(1)=0$.

**Step 2 — Transform equation (1).** With $s=x+y,\;t=x-y$ (so $x=\tfrac{s+t}{2}$
), equation (1) becomes
$$f\!\Big(\tfrac{3(s+t)}{2}\Big)=f\!\Big(\tfrac{s}{s^{2}+1}\Big)+f\!\Big(\tfrac{
t}{t^{2}+1}\Big)\qquad\forall\,s,t.\tag{1'}$$
Setting $t=0$ and using $f(0)=0$:
$$f\!\Big(\tfrac{3s}{2}\Big)=f\!\Big(\tfrac{s}{s^{2}+1}\Big)\qquad\forall\,s.\ta
g{3}$$
Define $h(s):=f\!\big(\tfrac{s}{s^{2}+1}\big)=f\!\big(\tfrac{3s}{2}\big)$. Then
(1') reads $h(s+t)=h(s)+h(t)$, so **$h$ is additive** (hence $\mathbb{Q}$-linear
). Since $f(r)=f\!\big(\tfrac{3(2r/3)}{2}\big)=h\!\big(\tfrac{2r}{3}\big)=\tfrac
{2}{3}h(r)$, we have
$$f=\tfrac{2}{3}\,h.\tag{$\ast$}$$

**Step 3 — Leibniz rule for $h$.** Substituting $(\ast)$ into (2'):
$$\tfrac{2}{3}h(uv)=u\cdot\tfrac{2}{3}h(v)+v\cdot\tfrac{2}{3}h(u)\;\Longrightarr
ow\;h(uv)=u\,h(v)+v\,h(u)\quad\forall\,u,v.\tag{5}$$

**Step 4 — Key identity from (1).** From (3) and $(\ast)$, using $\mathbb{Q}$-li
nearity ($h\!\big(\tfrac{3s}{2}\big)=\tfrac32 h(s)$):
$$h\!\Big(\tfrac{s}{s^{2}+1}\Big)=\tfrac{3}{2}\,h(s)\qquad\forall\,s.\tag{6}$$

**Step 5 — Compute $h\!\big(\tfrac{s}{s^{2}+1}\big)$ via the Leibniz rule.** Let
 $c=s^{2}+1$. Using $h(1)=h\!\big(\tfrac{1}{c}\cdot c\big)=\tfrac{1}{c}h(c)+c\,h
\!\big(\tfrac{1}{c}\big)$ and $h(1)=0$:
$$h\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{c}\Big)=-\frac{h(c)}{c^{2}}.$$
Now $h(c)=h(s^{2}+1)=h(s^{2})=2s\,h(s)$ (additivity + (5) with $u=v=s$). Hence
$$h\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{s^{2}+1}\Big)=-\frac{2s\,h(s)}{(s^{2}+1)^{2}}.$$
Applying (5) with $a=s,\;b=\tfrac{1}{s^{2}+1}$:
$$h\!\Big(\tfrac{s}{s^{2}+1}\Big)=s\!\left(-\frac{2s\,h(s)}{(s^{2}+1)^{2}}\right
)+\frac{h(s)}{s^{2}+1}=h(s)\cdot\frac{1-s^{2}}{(s^{2}+1)^{2}}.\tag{7}$$

**Step 6 — Conclude $h\equiv 0$.** Combining (6) and (7):
$$h(s)\cdot\frac{1-s^{2}}{(s^{2}+1)^{2}}=\frac{3}{2}\,h(s)\qquad\forall\,s.$$
If $h(s_{0})\neq 0$ for some $s_{0}$, then $\dfrac{1-s_{0}^{2}}{(s_{0}^{2}+1)^{2
}}=\dfrac{3}{2}$, i.e.
$$3s_{0}^{4}+8s_{0}^{2}+1=0.$$
Setting $u=s_{0}^{2}\ge 0$: $3u^{2}+8u+1=0$, whose roots are $u=\dfrac{-4\pm\sqr
t{13}}{3}$. Since $\sqrt{13}<4$, **both roots are negative** — no non-negative r
eal solution exists. Contradiction.

Therefore $h(s)=0$ for every $s$, and by $(\ast)$, $f\equiv 0$.

**Verification.** $f\equiv 0$ satisfies both equations trivially.

The unique solution is $\boxed{f\equiv 0}$.
