## Answer

**Yes, $U = E \setminus A$ is affine if and only if $A$ is non-empty.**

---

## Proof

Let $E$ be an elliptic curve over an algebraically closed field $k$, and let $A
\subseteq E$ be a finite set of closed points. Since $k$ is algebraically closed
, every closed point is $k$-rational, so write $A = \{P_1, \dots, P_n\}$ (with $
n \geq 0$).

We treat two cases.

### Case 1: $A = \emptyset$ (i.e., $n = 0$)

Then $U = E$. The elliptic curve $E$ is a **proper** (complete) $k$-variety of d
imension $1$. If $E$ were affine, then the structure morphism $E \to \operatorna
me{Spec} k$ would be both **proper** and **affine**, hence **finite** (a morphis
m that is both proper and affine is finite). A finite $k$-scheme has dimension $
0$, contradicting $\dim E = 1$. Therefore $E$ is not affine, and $U = E$ is not
affine.

### Case 2: $A \neq \emptyset$ (i.e., $n \geq 1$)

Consider the effective (Cartier) divisor
$$D = P_1 + P_2 + \cdots + P_n$$
on the smooth projective curve $E$. Its support is $\operatorname{Supp}(D) = A$,
 and its degree is
$$\deg D = n \geq 1 > 0.$$

We use two standard facts from the theory of algebraic curves:

**Fact A (Ampleness criterion on curves).** On a smooth projective curve over an
 algebraically closed field, a Cartier divisor $D$ is ample if and only if $\deg
 D > 0$.

*Justification.* The ampleness of $D$ is equivalent to $D$ having positive degre
e: if $\deg D > 0$, then for any coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$, $\mathcal{F}(mD)$
 is globally generated for $m \gg 0$ (by Riemann–Roch, since $\ell(\mathcal{F}(m
D))$ grows like $m \deg D$), so $D$ is ample. Conversely, if $\deg D \leq 0$, th
en no positive multiple of $D$ can be very ample (a very ample divisor must have
 positive degree to give an embedding into projective space), so $D$ is not ampl
e. $\square$

By Fact A, since $\deg D = n > 0$, the divisor $D$ is **ample**.

**Fact B (Affineness of the complement of an ample divisor).** Let $X$ be a proj
ective scheme over a field and $D$ an effective ample Cartier divisor. Then $X \
setminus \operatorname{Supp}(D)$ is an affine open subset.

*Justification.* Since $D$ is ample, some positive multiple $mD$ is very ample,
giving a closed immersion $\varphi: X \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}^N$ such that $m
D = \varphi^* H$ for a hyperplane $H$. Then $\operatorname{Supp}(mD) = \operator
name{Supp}(D) = A$, and $X \setminus \operatorname{Supp}(D) = \varphi^{-1}(\math
bb{P}^N \setminus H)$. Since $\mathbb{P}^N \setminus H \cong \mathbb{A}^N$ is af
fine and $\varphi$ is a closed immersion, $X \setminus \operatorname{Supp}(D)$ i
s a closed subscheme of an affine scheme, hence affine. $\square$

Applying Fact B to our ample divisor $D$ on $E$:
$$U = E \setminus A = E \setminus \operatorname{Supp}(D) \quad \text{is affine.}
$$

### Conclusion

Combining both cases:
$$U = E \setminus A \text{ is affine} \iff A \neq \emptyset.$$

In particular, for any **non-empty** finite set $A$ of closed points (the case o
f primary interest), the open subset $U = E \setminus A$ **is affine**. $\blacks
quare$
