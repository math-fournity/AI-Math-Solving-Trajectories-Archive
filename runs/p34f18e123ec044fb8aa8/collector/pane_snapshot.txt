## Proof

We seek all distributions $f \in \mathcal{D}'(\mathbb{R})$ satisfying $e^x f = 0
$.

**Definition of the product.** For a smooth function $g \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R})
$ and a distribution $f \in \mathcal{D}'(\mathbb{R})$, the product $gf \in \math
cal{D}'(\mathbb{R})$ is defined by
$$\langle gf,\, \varphi \rangle := \langle f,\, g\,\varphi \rangle, \qquad \fora
ll\, \varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}).$$

Thus $e^x f = 0$ means
$$\langle f,\, e^x \varphi \rangle = 0 \qquad \forall\, \varphi \in C_c^\infty(\
mathbb{R}). \tag{1}$$

**Key observation.** The function $e^x$ is smooth and **strictly positive** on a
ll of $\mathbb{R}$; in particular it never vanishes. Consider the multiplication
 map
$$M: C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}) \to C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}), \qquad M(\varphi) = e^x
\varphi.$$

This map is a **linear isomorphism** of $C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ onto itself:

- *Well-defined:* $e^x \varphi$ is smooth and $\operatorname{supp}(e^x\varphi) =
 \operatorname{supp}(\varphi)$, so $e^x\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$.
- *Injective:* $e^x \varphi = 0 \Rightarrow \varphi = 0$ since $e^x \neq 0$.
- *Surjective:* Given $\psi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$, set $\varphi = e^{-x}\p
si$. Then $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ (same reasoning, since $e^{-x}$ i
s smooth and nonzero) and $M(\varphi) = e^x \cdot e^{-x}\psi = \psi$.

**Conclusion.** Since $M$ is surjective, as $\varphi$ ranges over all of $C_c^\i
nfty(\mathbb{R})$, the test function $\psi := e^x\varphi$ also ranges over all o
f $C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$. Therefore (1) is equivalent to
$$\langle f,\, \psi \rangle = 0 \qquad \forall\, \psi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})
.$$

By definition, this means $f = 0$ as a distribution.

**Answer.** The only distribution $f \in \mathcal{D}'(\mathbb{R})$ satisfying $e
^x f = 0$ is the zero distribution:
$$\boxed{f = 0.}$$
