## Solution

We compute \(E(X_1 \mid S_n)\) where \(S_n = X_1 + \cdots + X_n\) and \(\mathcal
{G} = \sigma(S_n)\).

**Key observation (symmetry).** Since \(X_1, \dots, X_n\) are identically distri
buted, the joint distribution of \((X_1, \dots, X_n)\) is invariant under permut
ation of indices — i.e. the sequence is exchangeable. Consequently, for every \(
i \in \{1, \dots, n\}\),

\[
E(X_i \mid S_n) = E(X_1 \mid S_n).
\]

*Justification.* The random variable \(S_n = X_1 + \cdots + X_n\) is a symmetric
 function of \((X_1, \dots, X_n)\): it is unchanged by any permutation of the in
dices. Since the joint law of \((X_1, \dots, X_n)\) is invariant under permutati
ons (exchangeability, which follows from identical distribution together with th
e symmetry of the joint structure here), the conditional law of \(X_i\) given \(
S_n\) is the same for every \(i\). Hence the conditional expectations coincide.

**Summing the conditional expectations.** Taking conditional expectation of both
 sides of \(S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i\) given \(\sigma(S_n)\), and using linearity
together with the fact that \(S_n\) is \(\sigma(S_n)\)-measurable:

\[
S_n = E(S_n \mid S_n) = E\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^n X_i \;\Big|\; S_n\right) = \sum_{i
=1}^n E(X_i \mid S_n).
\]

Applying the symmetry \(E(X_i \mid S_n) = E(X_1 \mid S_n)\) for all \(i\):

\[
S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n E(X_1 \mid S_n) = n \, E(X_1 \mid S_n).
\]

**Conclusion.** Solving for \(E(X_1 \mid S_n)\):

\[
\boxed{E(X_1 \mid S_n) = \frac{S_n}{n}.}
\]

Equivalently, since \(\mathcal{G} = \sigma(S_n)\), every \(\mathcal{G}\)-measura
ble random variable is a (Borel) function of \(S_n\), so the result may be writt
en as

\[
E(X_1 \mid \mathcal{G}) = \frac{S_n}{n}.
\]
