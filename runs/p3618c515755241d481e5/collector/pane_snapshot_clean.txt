## Solution

**Setup.** The outpost starts with 1 unit of fuel on the morning of Day 1. A pro
tocol initiated on Day $d$ with parameter $x$ (a positive integer) immediately c
onsumes $x$ units and delivers $3x$ units on the morning of Day $d+x$. We wish t
o maximize fuel in possession at the end of Day 10.

**Key observation — daily growth rate.** A protocol with parameter $x$ multiplie
s its invested fuel by $3$ over $x$ days, giving a per-day growth factor of $3^{
1/x}$. Since $x\ge 1$, this factor is maximized at $x=1$, yielding a factor of $
3$ per day. No protocol can more than triple fuel in a single day, and larger $x
$ strictly underperforms $x=1$ on a per-day basis.

**Upper bound via a potential function.** Assign to each unit of fuel present at
 the *beginning* of Day $d$ (after that morning's deliveries) the weight
$$w_d = 3^{\,10-d}.$$
This is exactly the number of remaining investment opportunities (Days $d, d+1,
\dots, 9$) raised to the tripling factor, i.e. the maximum a unit could grow to
by the start of Day 10.

Let $P_d$ be the total weighted potential at the beginning of Day $d$. Consider
what happens during Day $d$:

- **Idle carryover.** Any unit not invested on Day $d$ simply carries to Day $d+
1$, its weight dropping from $3^{10-d}$ to $3^{9-d}=3^{10-d}/3$. This strictly *
decreases* potential.

- **A protocol with parameter $x$ initiated on Day $d$.** It consumes $x$ units
of weight $x\cdot 3^{10-d}$ and produces $3x$ units on the morning of Day $d+x$,
 of total weight
$$3x\cdot 3^{10-(d+x)} = x\cdot 3^{10-d+1-x}.$$
  - For $x=1$: produced weight $= x\cdot 3^{10-d}$, equal to consumed weight — *
*conserved**.
  - For $x\ge 2$: produced weight $= x\cdot 3^{10-d+1-x}\le x\cdot 3^{10-d-1}<x\
cdot 3^{10-d}$ — **strictly decreases**.

Combining both effects, $P_{d+1}\le P_d$, with equality if and only if *every* u
nit of fuel on Day $d$ is invested in $x=1$ protocols.

**Applying the bound.** Initially $P_1 = 1\cdot 3^{10-1}=3^9$. At the beginning
of Day 10, each unit has weight $3^0=1$, so $P_{10}=F_{10}$ (the actual fuel cou
nt). Since potential is non-increasing,
$$F_{10}\le P_{10}\le P_1 = 3^9 = 19683.$$
On Day 10 itself, no protocol can help (any $x\ge 1$ delivers on Day $\ge 11$),
so fuel at the *end* of Day 10 is at most $F_{10}\le 3^9$.

**Achieving the bound.** The following strategy attains equality. On each of Day
s $1$ through $9$, invest *all* available fuel into $x=1$ protocols (initiating
as many separate $x=1$ protocols as there are fuel units). Each such protocol tr
iples the invested fuel by the next morning:

| Day (morning) | Fuel | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $1$ | invest $1$ in one $x{=}1$ protocol |
| 2 | $3$ | invest $3$ in three $x{=}1$ protocols |
| 3 | $9$ | invest all in $x{=}1$ protocols |
| 4 | $27$ | invest all |
| 5 | $81$ | invest all |
| 6 | $243$ | invest all |
| 7 | $729$ | invest all |
| 8 | $2187$ | invest all |
| 9 | $6561$ | invest all |
| 10 | $19683$ | invest nothing (useless) |

On the morning of Day 10 the final batch of $3\cdot 6561=19683$ units arrives, a
nd no investment is made, so the outpost holds exactly $3^9=19683$ units at the
end of Day 10. Every day used $x=1$ and invested all fuel, so the potential was
conserved at every step, matching the upper bound.

**Conclusion.** The maximum amount of fuel the outpost can have at the end of Da

$$\boxed{3^9 = 19683}.$$
