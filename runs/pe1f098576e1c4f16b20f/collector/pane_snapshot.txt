**Claim:** Yes, we can conclude that $x = y$.

**Proof.**

Recall that a sequence $\{u_n\}$ in a Banach space $X$ converges weakly to $u$ (
written $u_n \rightharpoonup u$) if and only if $\varphi(u_n) \to \varphi(u)$ fo
r every continuous linear functional $\varphi \in X^*$.

We use three standard facts:

**(F1) Weak limits are unique.** The weak topology on $X$ is Hausdorff, since $X
^*$ separates points of $X$ (a consequence of the Hahn–Banach theorem). Hence a
sequence can have at most one weak limit.

**(F2) Strong convergence implies weak convergence.** If $u_n \to u$ in norm, th
en for every $\varphi \in X^*$,
$$|\varphi(u_n) - \varphi(u)| \le \|\varphi\| \cdot \|u_n - u\| \to 0,$$
so $u_n \rightharpoonup u$. This holds in any normed space, finite- or infinite-
dimensional.

**(F3) Subsequences of weakly convergent sequences converge weakly to the same l
imit.** If $u_n \rightharpoonup u$ and $\{u_{n_k}\}$ is any subsequence, then $\
varphi(u_{n_k}) \to \varphi(u)$ for every $\varphi \in X^*$, since $\varphi(u_n)
 \to \varphi(u)$ implies every subsequence of this scalar sequence converges to
the same limit. Thus $u_{n_k} \rightharpoonup u$.

**Applying these to the problem:**

By hypothesis, $x_n \rightharpoonup x$. Since $\{y_n\}$ is a subsequence of $\{x
_n\}$, **(F3)** gives
$$y_n \rightharpoonup x.$$

By hypothesis, $y_n \to y$ strongly. By **(F2)**,
$$y_n \rightharpoonup y.$$

Now $\{y_n\}$ has two weak limits, $x$ and $y$. By **(F1)**, weak limits are uni
que, so
$$x = y. \qquad \blacksquare$$

**Remark on the hypotheses.** The conclusion $x = y$ is valid in *any* normed sp
ace — the assumptions of infinite-dimensionality and boundedness of $\{x_n\}$ ar
e not needed for this particular conclusion. (Weak convergence already implies b
oundedness by the Uniform Boundedness Principle, so the boundedness hypothesis i
s automatically satisfied; and the argument above is dimension-independent.) The
se extra hypotheses do not invalidate the conclusion; they simply play no essent
ial role in the proof.
