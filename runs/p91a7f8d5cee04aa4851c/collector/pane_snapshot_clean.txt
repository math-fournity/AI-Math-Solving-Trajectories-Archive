## Proof

The equation is linear in $P$, so solutions form a vector space.

### Step 1: $P$ must be even

The substitution $(x,y,z)\mapsto(-x,-y,-z)$ preserves the constraint ($2(-x)(-y)
(-z)=-2xyz=-(x+y+z)$). Under it, the equation becomes the same equation with $P(
t)$ replaced by $P(-t)$. By linearity, $P_{\text{even}}(t)=\frac{P(t)+P(-t)}{2}$
 and $P_{\text{odd}}(t)=\frac{P(t)-P(-t)}{2}$ each independently satisfy the equ
ation.

**Show $P_{\text{odd}}=0$:** Set $x=y=t$, so $z=\frac{2t}{2t^2-1}$. The equation
 for $P_{\text{odd}}$ gives:
$$\frac{2P_{\text{odd}}(t)}{tz}+\frac{P_{\text{odd}}(z)}{t^2}=P_{\text{odd}}(0)+
P_{\text{odd}}(t-z)+P_{\text{odd}}(z-t)=0,$$
since $P_{\text{odd}}$ is odd. Multiplying by $t^2 z$:
$$2t\,P_{\text{odd}}(t)+z\,P_{\text{odd}}(z)=0.$$
If $\deg P_{\text{odd}}=2m+1$ with leading coefficient $a$, then as $t\to\infty$
: $z\sim 1/t$, so $2t\,P_{\text{odd}}(t)\sim 2a\,t^{2m+2}$ while $z\,P_{\text{od
d}}(z)\sim \frac{a}{t^{2m+2}}\to 0$. This forces $a=0$, contradiction. Hence $P_
{\text{odd}}=0$ and $P$ is even.

### Step 2: Derive a functional equation for $Q$ where $P(t)=Q(t^2)$

Set $z=\frac{x+y}{2xy-1}$ and take $y\to 0$ with $x\neq 0$ fixed. Then $z\to -x$
.

**Expansion:** $z=-x-(1+2x^2)y+O(y^2)$, so (using $P$ even):
$$P(z)=P(x)+2x(1+2x^2)Q'(x^2)\,y+O(y^2).$$

**LHS** $=\frac{xP(x)+zP(z)}{xyz}+\frac{P(y)}{zx}$. Computing:
- $xP(x)+zP(z)=-(1+2x^2)\bigl[P(x)+2x^2Q'(x^2)\bigr]\,y+O(y^2)$,
- $xyz=-x^2y+O(y^2)$,

so $\frac{xP(x)+zP(z)}{xyz}\to\frac{(1+2x^2)[P(x)+2x^2Q'(x^2)]}{x^2}$, and $\fra
c{P(y)}{zx}\to-\frac{P(0)}{x^2}$.

**RHS** $=P(x-y)+P(y-z)+P(z-x)\to P(x)+P(x)+P(2x)=2P(x)+P(2x)$.

Equating and setting $s=x^2$:
$$(1+2s)\bigl[Q(s)+2s\,Q'(s)\bigr]-Q(0)=s\bigl[2Q(s)+Q(4s)\bigr],$$
which simplifies to:
$$Q(s)+2s(1+2s)\,Q'(s)-Q(0)=s\,Q(4s).\tag{$\star$}$$

### Step 3: Solve $(\star)$

Write $Q(s)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}b_j s^j$. Comparing coefficients of $s^k$ for $k\geq 1
$:
$$(2k+1)\,b_k=\bigl(4^{k-1}-4k+4\bigr)\,b_{k-1}.$$

- $k=1$: $b_1=\frac{4^0-4+4}{3}\,b_0=\frac{1}{3}\,b_0$.
- $k=2$: $b_2=\frac{4^1-8+4}{5}\,b_1=\frac{0}{5}\,b_1=0$.

Since $b_2=0$, the recurrence gives $b_k=0$ for all $k\geq 2$. Thus:
$$Q(s)=b_0+\frac{b_0}{3}\,s=\frac{b_0}{3}(s+3),\qquad P(t)=\frac{b_0}{3}(t^2+3)=
\lambda(t^2+3).$$

### Step 4: Verification

For $P(t)=t^2+3$:

$$\text{LHS}=\frac{x^3+y^3+z^3+3(x+y+z)}{xyz}.$$

Using $x+y+z=2xyz$ and $x^3+y^3+z^3=(x+y+z)^3-3(x+y+z)(xy+yz+zx)+3xyz$:
$$\text{LHS}=8x^2y^2z^2-6(xy+yz+zx)+3+6=8x^2y^2z^2-6(xy+yz+zx)+9.$$

$$\text{RHS}=(x-y)^2+(y-z)^2+(z-x)^2+9=2(x^2+y^2+z^2)-2(xy+yz+zx)+9$$
$$=2(x+y+z)^2-6(xy+yz+zx)+9=8x^2y^2z^2-6(xy+yz+zx)+9.$$

LHS $=$ RHS. ✓ By linearity, $P(t)=\lambda(t^2+3)$ works for all $\lambda\in\mat
hbb{R}$.

### Conclusion

The real-coefficient polynomials satisfying the equation are exactly:
$$\boxed{P(t)=\lambda(t^2+3),\quad \lambda\in\mathbb{R}.}$$
