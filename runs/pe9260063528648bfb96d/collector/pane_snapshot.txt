## Proof

**Setup.** Six islands with every pair connected gives the complete graph $K_6$,
 which has $\binom{6}{2}=15$ edges. Choosing a set of inactive cables is the sam
e as choosing a set of active cables (its complement). The bureau requires the a
ctive cables to keep all six islands connected, so we must count the number of *
*connected spanning subgraphs** of $K_6$, i.e., the number of connected labeled
graphs on 6 vertices.

**Notation.** Let $g(n)=2^{\binom{n}{2}}$ be the total number of labeled graphs
on $n$ vertices, and $c(n)$ the number of **connected** labeled graphs on $n$ ve
rtices.

**Recurrence.** Fix a distinguished vertex (say vertex 1). In any labeled graph
on $n$ vertices, let the connected component containing vertex 1 have exactly $k
$ vertices ($1\le k\le n$). We choose the other $k-1$ members of this component
from the remaining $n-1$ vertices in $\binom{n-1}{k-1}$ ways; this component mus
t be connected ($c(k)$ ways); the remaining $n-k$ vertices form an arbitrary gra
ph ($g(n-k)$ ways). Hence

$$g(n)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n-1}{k-1}\,c(k)\,g(n-k),\qquad c(n)=g(n)-\sum_{k=1}^
{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k-1}\,c(k)\,g(n-k).$$

**Base case.** $g(1)=1,\;c(1)=1$.

**Compute up to $n=6$.**

| $n$ | $g(n)=2^{\binom n2}$ | $c(n)$ |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $1$ | $1$ |
| 2 | $2$ | $2-1=1$ |
| 3 | $8$ | $8-2-2=4$ |
| 4 | $64$ | $64-8-6-12=38$ |
| 5 | $1024$ | $1024-64-32-48-152=728$ |
| 6 | $32768$ | see below |

**Detailed computation for $n=6$.**

$$c(6)=32768-\sum_{k=1}^{5}\binom{5}{k-1}c(k)\,g(6-k).$$

| $k$ | $\binom{5}{k-1}$ | $c(k)$ | $g(6-k)$ | product |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 1 | $g(5)=1024$ | $1024$ |
| 2 | 5 | 1 | $g(4)=64$ | $320$ |
| 3 | 10 | 4 | $g(3)=8$ | $320$ |
| 4 | 10 | 38 | $g(2)=2$ | $760$ |
| 5 | 5 | 728 | $g(1)=1$ | $3640$ |

Sum of subtracted terms: $1024+320+320+760+3640=6064$.

$$c(6)=32768-6064=26704.$$

**Interpretation.** Each connected labeled graph on 6 vertices corresponds to ex
actly one valid choice of active cables, hence exactly one valid set of inactive
 cables (its complement). Therefore the number of different sets of inactive cab
les the bureau can choose is

$$\boxed{26704}.$$
