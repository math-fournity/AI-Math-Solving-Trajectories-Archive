# Problem

In a bustling telecommunications hub, there are $n$ distinct circular satellite coverage zones mapped on a 2D monitoring screen, where $n \geq 2$. The zones are arranged such that every pair of satellite boundaries overlaps at exactly two distinct signal-interference points, and no three boundaries ever intersect at the same location. 

The maintenance team has identified all $n(n-1)$ unique interference points across the entire map. Each point is assigned a specific frequency channel (represented by a color) from a set of $n$ available distinct channels. The assignment must follow two rules: 
1. Every one of the $n$ available channels must be used at least once across the map.
2. For any single satellite boundary, the interference points located on that specific boundary must involve exactly $k$ distinct frequency channels.

Determine the total number of possible integer pairs $(n, k)$ that satisfy these conditions, given the physical constraint that $2 \le k \le n \le 10$.
