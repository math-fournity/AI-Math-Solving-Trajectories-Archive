## Proof

**Claim.** Let $X, Y$ be Banach spaces and $T : X \to Y$ a linear operator with
the property

$$x_n \rightharpoonup 0 \text{ weakly in } X \;\Longrightarrow\; Tx_n \to 0 \tex
t{ in norm in } Y.$$

Then $T$ is bounded (hence continuous).

**Proof.** Suppose, for contradiction, that $T$ is unbounded. Then

$$\sup_{\|y\|=1} \|Ty\| = +\infty,$$

so for every $n \geq 1$ there exists $y_n \in X$ with $\|y_n\| = 1$ and $\|Ty_n\
| \geq n$.

Define $x_n := \dfrac{y_n}{n}$. Then

$$\|x_n\| = \frac{\|y_n\|}{n} = \frac{1}{n} \to 0.$$

In particular $x_n \to 0$ in norm, and norm convergence implies weak convergence
, so $x_n \rightharpoonup 0$ weakly in $X$.

By the hypothesis on $T$, we must have $Tx_n \to 0$ in norm. However,

$$\|Tx_n\| = \left\|T\!\left(\frac{y_n}{n}\right)\right\| = \frac{\|Ty_n\|}{n} \
geq \frac{n}{n} = 1 \quad\text{for all } n.$$

Thus $\|Tx_n\| \not\to 0$, contradicting $Tx_n \to 0$ in norm.

The contradiction shows that $T$ must be bounded, i.e. continuous. $\blacksquare
$

**Remark.** The argument uses only that norm convergence implies weak convergenc
e (so any norm-null sequence is a valid test sequence for the hypothesis) and th
at an unbounded linear operator admits unit vectors on which $\|T\|$ grows witho
ut bound. The conclusion is therefore **yes**: such an operator is necessarily c
ontinuous.
