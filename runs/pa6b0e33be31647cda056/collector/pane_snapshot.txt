## Solution

**Setup.** Each array is a unit square (side 1 km, area 1 km²). Since the northe
rn edges are parallel, the two squares share the same orientation, so their edge
s are mutually parallel. Let the center of Alpha be the origin and let the cente
r of Omega be displaced by $(\Delta x, \Delta y)$ along the two edge directions.
 With $|{\Delta x}|, |{\Delta y}| \le 1$, the overlap is a rectangle of area

$$A = (1-|\Delta x|)(1-|\Delta y|) = \tfrac{1}{16}.$$

We must minimize the center-to-center distance $d = \sqrt{\Delta x^2 + \Delta y^
2}$.

**Reduction.** Set $a = 1-|\Delta x|$, $b = 1-|\Delta y|$, with $a,b \in (0,1]$
and $ab = 1/16$. Then

$$d^2 = (1-a)^2 + (1-b)^2, \qquad b = \frac{1}{16a}.$$

Define $f(a) = (1-a)^2 + \left(1 - \tfrac{1}{16a}\right)^2$ on $a \in [1/16, 1]$
.

**Critical points.** Setting $f'(a)=0$:

$$-2(1-a) + 2\!\left(1-\tfrac{1}{16a}\right)\!\frac{1}{16a^2} = 0 \;\;\Longright
arrow\;\; 256a^4 - 256a^3 + 16a - 1 = 0.$$

Factoring out the symmetric solution $a=b=1/4$ (i.e. root $a=1/4$):

$$256a^4-256a^3+16a-1 = (4a-1)(64a^3-48a^2-12a+1).$$

The cubic, after the shift $a = u + \tfrac14$, becomes $16u^3 - 6u - 1 = 0$, i.e
. $u^3 - \tfrac38 u - \tfrac1{16}=0$. With $u = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\cos\theta$ and
 $\cos 3\theta = \tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$, so $3\theta = \tfrac{\pi}{4}$, the relevant
 root (in $[1/16,1]$) is

$$u_0 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\cos\tfrac{\pi}{12} = \tfrac{\sqrt3+1}{4}, \qquad a_0 =
 \tfrac{2+\sqrt3}{4} \approx 0.933.$$

Then $b_0 = \tfrac{1}{16a_0} = \tfrac{2-\sqrt3}{4}$, giving

$$|\Delta x| = 1-a_0 = \tfrac{2-\sqrt3}{4}, \qquad |\Delta y| = 1-b_0 = \tfrac{2
+\sqrt3}{4}.$$

**Value at the minimum.**

$$d^2 = \left(\tfrac{2-\sqrt3}{4}\right)^2 + \left(\tfrac{2+\sqrt3}{4}\right)^2
= \frac{(2-\sqrt3)^2+(2+\sqrt3)^2}{16} = \frac{14}{16} = \frac78.$$

**Comparison with other candidates.**
- Boundary $a=1$ (or $a=1/16$): $d = 15/16 \approx 0.9375$.
- Symmetric critical point $a=b=1/4$: $d = \tfrac{3}{4}\sqrt2 \approx 1.061$ (lo
cal maximum).
- Interior critical point $a_0=\tfrac{2+\sqrt3}{4}$: $d = \sqrt{7/8} = \tfrac{\s
qrt{14}}{4} \approx 0.9354$.

Since $\tfrac{\sqrt{14}}{4} < \tfrac{15}{16}$ (as $14/16 \cdot 16 = 14 < 225/16$
... checking: $7/8 = 0.875 < 225/256 \approx 0.879$), the interior critical poin
t gives the global minimum.

**Answer.** The minimum possible distance between the two central monitoring sta
tions is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{\sqrt{14}}{4}\ \text{km}}.$$
