## Solution

**Problem.** Find the residue of $f(z) = \dfrac{z}{1-\cos(2z)}$ at each pole $z
= \pi k$, $k \in \mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$ (each pole is of order 2).

---

### Step 1. Locate and confirm the poles

The denominator vanishes when $1 - \cos(2z) = 0$, i.e. $\cos(2z)=1$, i.e. $2z =
2\pi k$, so $z = \pi k$ for $k \in \mathbb{Z}$.

Near $z = \pi k$, set $w = z - \pi k$. Then $\cos(2z) = \cos(2w + 2\pi k) = \cos
(2w)$, and

$$1 - \cos(2w) = 2\sin^{2}w = 2w^{2} - \tfrac{2}{3}w^{4} + \cdots$$

so $1-\cos(2z)$ has a **double zero** at $z = \pi k$. For $k \neq 0$ the numerat
or $z = w + \pi k$ is nonzero at $w=0$, hence $z = \pi k$ is a **pole of order 2
**. (At $k=0$ the numerator also vanishes, giving a different order; this is exc
luded by hypothesis.)

---

### Step 2. Laurent expansion around $z = \pi k$

With $w = z - \pi k$ and $a := \pi k$:

$$f(z) = \frac{w + a}{2\sin^{2}w} = \frac{w+a}{2w^{2}}\cdot\frac{w^{2}}{\sin^{2}
w}.$$

Using $\dfrac{w}{\sin w} = 1 + \dfrac{w^{2}}{6} + O(w^{4})$, so $\dfrac{w^{2}}{\
sin^{2}w} = 1 + \dfrac{w^{2}}{3} + O(w^{4})$:

$$f(z) = \frac{w+a}{2w^{2}}\left(1 + \frac{w^{2}}{3} + O(w^{4})\right) = \frac{a
}{2w^{2}} + \frac{1}{2w} + \frac{a}{6} + \frac{w}{6} + \cdots$$

The coefficient of $\dfrac{1}{w} = \dfrac{1}{z - \pi k}$ is $\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}
}$.

---

### Step 3. Verification via the order-2 residue formula

For a pole of order 2 at $z = a$:

$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=a} f = \lim_{z\to a}\frac{d}{dz}\bigl[(z-a)^{2}f(z)\bigr
].$$

Here $(z-a)^{2}f(z) = \dfrac{(w+a)\,w^{2}}{1-\cos 2w} = \dfrac{w+a}{2}\left(\dfr
ac{w}{\sin w}\right)^{2} := g(w)$.

Differentiating:

$$g'(w) = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{w}{\sin w}\right)^{2} + \frac{w+a}{2}\cdot 2\fr
ac{w}{\sin w}\cdot\frac{d}{dw}\!\left(\frac{w}{\sin w}\right).$$

At $w = 0$: $\dfrac{w}{\sin w}\to 1$, and $\dfrac{d}{dw}\!\left(\dfrac{w}{\sin w
}\right) = \dfrac{\sin w - w\cos w}{\sin^{2}w} \sim \dfrac{w^{3}/3}{w^{2}} = \df
rac{w}{3}\to 0$. Therefore

$$g'(0) = \frac{1}{2}\cdot 1 + \frac{a}{2}\cdot 2\cdot 1\cdot 0 = \frac{1}{2}.$$

This confirms the residue is **independent of $k$**.

---

### Conclusion

The residue of $f(z) = \dfrac{z}{1-\cos(2z)}$ at each pole $z = \pi k$ ($k \in \
mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$) is

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Res}_{z=\pi k} f = \dfrac{1}{2}}.$$
