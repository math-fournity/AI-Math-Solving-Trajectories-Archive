## Proof

**Parametrization.** The torus $\mathbb{T}^2 = \{(w,x,y,z) : w^2+x^2=1,\; y^2+z^
2=1\}$ is the product $S^1\times S^1 \subset \mathbb{R}^4$. Parametrize it by
$$\Phi(\alpha,\beta) = (\cos\alpha,\;\sin\alpha,\;\cos\beta,\;\sin\beta),\qquad
\alpha,\beta\in[0,2\pi],$$
with the product orientation $d\alpha\wedge d\beta$.

**Pullback of the form.** From $w=\cos\alpha$ and $y=\cos\beta$:
$$dw = -\sin\alpha\,d\alpha,\qquad dy = -\sin\beta\,d\beta,$$
so
$$dw\wedge dy = \sin\alpha\,\sin\beta\;d\alpha\wedge d\beta.$$

**Pullback of the integrand.** Since $x=\sin\alpha$, $y=\cos\beta$, $z=\sin\beta
$:
$$xyz = \sin\alpha\cos\beta\sin\beta.$$
Hence
$$\Phi^*(xyz\,dw\wedge dy) = \sin\alpha\cos\beta\sin\beta\cdot\sin\alpha\sin\bet
a\;d\alpha\wedge d\beta = \sin^2\alpha\,\cos\beta\,\sin^2\beta\;d\alpha\wedge d\
beta.$$

**Evaluate.** The integral separates:
$$\int_{\mathbb{T}^2} xyz\,dw\wedge dy = \left(\int_0^{2\pi}\sin^2\alpha\,d\alph
a\right)\!\left(\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\beta\,\sin^2\beta\,d\beta\right).$$

The first factor:
$$\int_0^{2\pi}\sin^2\alpha\,d\alpha = \pi.$$

The second factor, with $u=\sin\beta$, $du=\cos\beta\,d\beta$:
$$\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\beta\,\sin^2\beta\,d\beta = \left[\frac{\sin^3\beta}{3}\righ
t]_0^{2\pi} = 0.$$

Therefore
$$\int_{\mathbb{T}^2} xyz\,dw\wedge dy = \pi\cdot 0 = 0.$$

(Note: the result is $0$ regardless of the choice of orientation, so the sign co
nvention on $\mathbb{T}^2$ is immaterial.)
