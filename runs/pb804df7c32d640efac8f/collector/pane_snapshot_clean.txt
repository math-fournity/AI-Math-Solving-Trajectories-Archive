## Solution

**Answer-leak check:** The problem text contains no solutions, solution sketches
, formalization notes, or embedded answer values. Proceeding.

**Key observation.** Compute the divergence of
$$F(x,y,z)=\bigl(xz-x\cos z,\;-yz+y\cos z,\;-4-(x^2+y^2)\bigr):$$

$$\frac{\partial F_1}{\partial x}=z-\cos z,\qquad \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial y
}=-z+\cos z,\qquad \frac{\partial F_3}{\partial z}=0.$$

Hence
$$\nabla\cdot F=(z-\cos z)+(-z+\cos z)+0=0.$$

**Closing the surface.** The upper hemisphere $S_R=\{x^2+y^2+z^2=R^2,\;z\ge 0\}$
 is not closed. Cap it with the disk $D_R=\{x^2+y^2\le R^2,\;z=0\}$ to form the
closed boundary $\partial V$ of the upper half-ball $V$. With the **outward** un
it normal $\mathbf n$ on $S_R$ (radially outward, positive $z$-component) and th
e outward normal on $D_R$ pointing downward, $\mathbf n=(0,0,-1)$, the divergenc
e theorem gives

$$\iint_{S_R}F\cdot\mathbf n\,dS+\iint_{D_R}F\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=\iiint_V(\nabla\
cdot F)\,dV=0.$$

Therefore
$$\iint_{S_R}F\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=-\iint_{D_R}F\cdot(0,0,-1)\,dS.$$

**Evaluating the disk integral.** On $D_R$ we have $z=0$, so
$$F(x,y,0)=\bigl(-x,\;y,\;-4-x^2-y^2\bigr),$$
and
$$F\cdot(0,0,-1)=-(-4-x^2-y^2)=4+x^2+y^2.$$

Thus, using polar coordinates $(r,\theta)$ on $D_R$,
$$\iint_{D_R}(4+x^2+y^2)\,dA=\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^R(4+r^2)\,r\,dr\,d\theta=2\
pi\Bigl[2r^2+\tfrac{r^4}{4}\Bigr]_0^R=4\pi R^2+\frac{\pi}{2}R^4.$$

**Flux through the hemisphere.**
$$\Phi(R):=\iint_{S_R}F\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=-\Bigl(4\pi R^2+\frac{\pi}{2}R^4\Bigr)
.$$

**Verification by direct computation.** Parametrize $S_R$ by $x=R\sin\varphi\cos
\theta$, $y=R\sin\varphi\sin\theta$, $z=R\cos\varphi$, with $\varphi\in[0,\pi/2]
$, $\theta\in[0,2\pi]$. Then $\mathbf n\,dS=R\sin\varphi\,(x,y,z)\,d\varphi\,d\t
heta$, and
$$F\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=R\sin\varphi\bigl[-2y^2z+(y^2-x^2)\cos z-4z\bigr]d\varphi\
,d\theta.$$
Integrating over $\theta$ kills the $(y^2-x^2)\cos z$ term (since $\int_0^{2\pi}
\cos 2\theta\,d\theta=0$) and uses $\int_0^{2\pi}\sin^2\theta\,d\theta=\pi$, $\i
nt_0^{2\pi}d\theta=2\pi$:
$$\Phi(R)=-2\pi R^4\!\int_0^{\pi/2}\!\sin^3\varphi\cos\varphi\,d\varphi-8\pi R^2
\!\int_0^{\pi/2}\!\sin\varphi\cos\varphi\,d\varphi=-\frac{\pi}{2}R^4-4\pi R^2,$$
confirming the result.

**Maximizing.** For $R\ge 0$ (the surface depends only on $R^2$, so negative $R$
 gives the same flux),
$$\Phi(R)=-4\pi R^2-\frac{\pi}{2}R^4,\qquad \Phi'(R)=-8\pi R-2\pi R^3=-2\pi R(4+
R^2).$$
For $R>0$, $\Phi'(R)<0$, so $\Phi$ is strictly decreasing on $(0,\infty)$. The m
aximum is therefore attained at the endpoint $R=0$, where
$$\Phi(0)=0.$$

For every $R>0$, $\Phi(R)<0=\Phi(0)$, so $R=0$ is the unique global maximizer.

$$\boxed{R=0}$$
