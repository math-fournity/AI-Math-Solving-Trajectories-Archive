## Solution

**Answer: A (0)**

**Identifying the distribution.** Recall the MGF of a $N(0,\sigma^2)$ distributi
on is $e^{\sigma^2 t^2/2}$. The term $e^{t^2/(2n)}$ is therefore the MGF of $N(0
, 1/n)$.

Since $\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{6}$, the prefactor $\frac{6}{\pi
^2}$ normalizes the weights, so $M_X(t)$ is a valid MGF representing a **mixture
 of Gaussians**:

$$X \sim N\!\left(0,\tfrac{1}{N}\right), \qquad P(N=n)=\frac{6}{\pi^2 n^2},\quad
 n=1,2,\dots$$

Equivalently, $X = Z/\sqrt{N}$ where $Z\sim N(0,1)$ is independent of $N$.

**Computing $P(X\in\mathbb{Q})$.** Conditioning on $N$ and using the law of tota
l probability:

$$P(X\in\mathbb{Q})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P(X\in\mathbb{Q}\mid N=n)\,P(N=n).$$

For each fixed $n\geq 1$, the conditional distribution $X\mid N=n \sim N(0,1/n)$
 is a **non-degenerate Gaussian**, hence absolutely continuous with respect to L
ebesgue measure. Every countable set — in particular $\mathbb{Q}$ — has Lebesgue
 measure zero, so

$$P(X\in\mathbb{Q}\mid N=n)=0 \quad\text{for every } n\geq 1.$$

Therefore

$$P(X\in\mathbb{Q})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}0\cdot\frac{6}{\pi^2 n^2}=0.$$

The probability that $X$ is rational is $\boxed{0}$, i.e. answer **A**.
