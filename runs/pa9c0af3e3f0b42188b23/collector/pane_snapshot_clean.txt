## Proof

**Setup.** A Hopf algebra structure on $A = \mathbb{k}[x_1, x_2]$ with the stand
ard algebra structure is determined by comultiplication $\Delta: A \to A \otimes
 A$ and counit $\varepsilon: A \to \mathbb{k}$ (algebra homomorphisms), plus an
antipode $S$. Since $A$ is commutative, $S$ is an algebra homomorphism and is **
unique** if it exists. Thus we count bialgebra structures $(\Delta, \varepsilon)
$ admitting an antipode, up to isomorphism (algebra automorphisms of $A$ preserv
ing the coalgebra structure).

**Step 1: Normalization of the counit.** Let $a_i = \varepsilon(x_i) \in \mathbb
{k}$. The affine change of variables $\varphi(x_i) = x_i - a_i$ is an algebra au
tomorphism of $A$. Conjugating the bialgebra structure by $\varphi$ yields an is
omorphic bialgebra with counit $\varepsilon'(x_i) = 0$. So **WLOG $\varepsilon(x
_i) = 0$**.

**Step 2: Form of the comultiplication.** Write $A \otimes A \cong \mathbb{k}[x_
1, x_2, y_1, y_2]$ where $x_i = x_i \otimes 1$ and $y_i = 1 \otimes x_i$. The co
unit axiom $(\varepsilon \otimes \mathrm{id})\Delta(x_i) = x_i$ forces $\Delta(x
_i)\big|_{x=0} = y_i$, and $(\mathrm{id} \otimes \varepsilon)\Delta(x_i) = x_i$
forces $\Delta(x_i)\big|_{y=0} = x_i$. Hence:
$$\Delta(x_i) = x_i + y_i + \text{(terms of total degree} \geq 2 \text{ in } x_1
, x_2, y_1, y_2).$$

**Step 3: Formal group law.** Define $F_i(x, y) = \Delta(x_i) \in \mathbb{k}[x_1
, x_2, y_1, y_2]$. Coassociativity $(\Delta \otimes \mathrm{id})\Delta = (\mathr
m{id} \otimes \Delta)\Delta$ translates to the **formal group law axiom**:
$$F(x, F(y, z)) = F(F(x, y), z).$$
Since each $F_i$ is a polynomial, $F = (F_1, F_2)$ is a **polynomial formal grou
p law** of dimension 2 over $\mathbb{k}$, with $F(0,0) = 0$ and linear part $F_i
 \approx x_i + y_i$.

**Step 4: Lie algebra via BCH (characteristic zero).** Over a field of character
istic zero, the category of formal group laws is equivalent to the category of L
ie algebras, via the **Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff (BCH) formula**:
$$F(x, y) = x + y + \tfrac{1}{2}[x,y] + \tfrac{1}{12}\big([x,[x,y]] + [y,[y,x]]\
big) + \cdots$$
The Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ associated to $F$ is 2-dimensional, with bracket
determined by the degree-2 part of $F$.

**Step 5: Polynomial forces nilpotent.** The BCH series for $\mathfrak{g}$ equal
s the Taylor expansion of $F$ at the origin. Since $F$ is a **polynomial**, its
Taylor expansion is $F$ itself — a **finite** power series. Therefore the BCH se
ries **terminates**. A classical result states:

> *The BCH series for a Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ terminates (is a polynomial)
if and only if $\mathfrak{g}$ is nilpotent.*

Hence $\mathfrak{g}$ is **nilpotent**.

**Step 6: 2-dimensional nilpotent $\Rightarrow$ abelian.** Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be
 a 2-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra. Then $[\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}] \subs
eteq Z(\mathfrak{g})$ and $\dim[\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}] \leq 1$. Suppose for
 contradiction that $\dim[\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}] = 1$, say $[\mathfrak{g},
\mathfrak{g}] = \mathbb{k} e_1$ with $[e_1, e_2] = e_1$ for some basis $\{e_1, e
_2\}$. Then $\mathrm{ad}(e_2): e_1 \mapsto [e_2, e_1] = -e_1$, so $\mathrm{ad}(e
_2)$ has eigenvalue $-1 \neq 0$, contradicting the nilpotence of $\mathrm{ad}(e_
2)$ (required since $\mathfrak{g}$ is nilpotent). Therefore $[\mathfrak{g}, \mat
hfrak{g}] = 0$, i.e., **$\mathfrak{g}$ is abelian**.

**Step 7: Uniqueness of the polynomial group law.** An abelian Lie algebra yield
s the **additive** formal group law:
$$F(x, y) = x + y, \quad \text{i.e.,} \quad \Delta(x_i) = x_i \otimes 1 + 1 \oti
mes x_i.$$
Since a polynomial is uniquely determined by its Taylor series, $F(x,y) = x + y$
 is the **unique** polynomial group law with abelian Lie algebra. There are no o
ther polynomial comultiplications.

**Step 8: The antipode exists.** The additive bialgebra is a Hopf algebra with a
ntipode $S(x_i) = -x_i$, satisfying $m \circ (S \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delt
a = \varepsilon$. Since this is the **only** bialgebra structure (up to isomorph
ism), it is the only Hopf algebra structure.

**Conclusion.** Up to isomorphism, there is exactly **one** Hopf algebra structu
re on $\mathbb{k}[x_1, x_2]$ with the standard algebra structure — the standard
additive (connected, unipotent) Hopf algebra corresponding to the algebraic grou
p $\mathbb{G}_a^2$.
