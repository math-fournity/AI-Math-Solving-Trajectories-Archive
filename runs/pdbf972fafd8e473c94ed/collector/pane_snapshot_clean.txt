# Singular Cohomology and the Cellular Cochain Complex

## Statement

Let $X$ be a CW complex and $G$ an abelian group. Then for every $n \ge 0$ there
 is a natural isomorphism

$$H^n(X;G)\;\cong\;H^n\bigl(\mathcal C^\bullet(X;G)\bigr),$$

where the right-hand side is the cohomology of the cellular cochain complex

$$\cdots \longrightarrow \mathcal C^{n-1}(X;G) \xrightarrow{\;\delta_{n-1}\;} \m
athcal C^{n}(X;G) \xrightarrow{\;\delta_n\;} \mathcal C^{n+1}(X;G) \longrightarr
ow \cdots$$

with $\mathcal C^n(X;G) := \operatorname{Hom}\bigl(\mathcal C_n(X),G\bigr) = \op
eratorname{Hom}\bigl(H_n(X^n,X^{n-1}),G\bigr)$, the group of cellular $n$-cochai
ns, and coboundary $\delta_n = \partial_{n+1}^{*}$ dual to the cellular boundary
.

## Proof

The argument proceeds by showing that the singular cochain complex $C^\bullet_{\
mathrm{sing}}(X;G)=\operatorname{Hom}(C_\bullet^{\mathrm{sing}}(X),G)$ is chain-
homotopy equivalent to the cellular cochain complex $\mathcal C^\bullet(X;G)$, s
o that they have isomorphic cohomology. We use the standard cellular approximati
on theorem and the long exact sequences of the triples $(X^n,X^{n-1},X^{n-2})$.

---

### Step 1. The cellular chain complex computes singular homology

For a CW complex $X$, let $X^n$ denote the $n$-skeleton. Define the cellular cha
in group

$$\mathcal C_n(X) := H_n(X^n,X^{n-1}).$$

Since $X^n/X^{n-1}$ is a wedge of $n$-spheres (one per $n$-cell), $\mathcal C_n(
X)$ is the free abelian group generated by the $n$-cells of $X$. The cellular bo
undary map

$$\partial_n : \mathcal C_n(X) \longrightarrow \mathcal C_{n-1}(X)$$

is defined as the composition

$$H_n(X^n,X^{n-1}) \xrightarrow{\;\partial\;} H_{n-1}(X^{n-1}) \xrightarrow{\;j_
{n-1}\;} H_{n-1}(X^{n-1},X^{n-2}),$$

where $\partial$ is the connecting homomorphism of the pair $(X^n,X^{n-1})$ and
$j_{n-1}$ is the map induced by inclusion. One verifies $\partial_{n-1}\circ\par
tial_n = 0$ from the triple $(X^n,X^{n-1},X^{n-2})$.

**Cellular homology theorem.** $H_n(\mathcal C_\bullet(X)) \cong H_n^{\mathrm{si
ng}}(X)$ for all $n$.

*Sketch.* The long exact sequence of the pair $(X^n,X^{n-1})$ gives

$$H_{n+1}(X^{n+1},X^n)\xrightarrow{\partial_{n+1}}H_n(X^n,X^{n-1})\xrightarrow{\
partial_n}H_{n-1}(X^{n-1},X^{n-2}),$$

and the inclusion $X^n\hookrightarrow X$ induces $H_n(X^n)\to H_n(X)$. A standar
d diagram chase using the long exact sequences of the pairs $(X^n,X^{n-1})$ and
the fact that $H_k(X^n,X^{n-1})=0$ for $k\ne n$ (cellular approximation: any map
 $S^k\to X^n$ with $k>n$ factors up to homotopy through $X^{n-1}$, and any map $
D^{k}\to X^n$ with $k<n+1$ bounding a $k$-cycle in $X^n$ can be pushed into $X^{
n-1}$) shows:

- $H_n(X^n) \to H_n(X)$ is an isomorphism for $k=n$ (and $H_k(X^n)=0$ for $k>n$)
,
- the sequence $0\to H_n(X^n)\to H_n(X^n,X^{n-1})\xrightarrow{\partial_n} H_{n-1
}(X^{n-1})\to 0$ is exact,

so that $\ker\partial_n / \operatorname{im}\partial_{n+1} \cong H_n(X)$. $\squar
e$

---

### Step 2. The cellular chain complex is chain-homotopy equivalent to the singu
lar chain complex

More precisely, there is a natural chain map

$$\varepsilon : \mathcal C_\bullet(X) \longrightarrow C_\bullet^{\mathrm{sing}}(
X)$$

and a chain map in the other direction, together with chain homotopies, exhibiti
ng a chain homotopy equivalence. Concretely, $\mathcal C_n(X)=H_n(X^n,X^{n-1})$
maps to $C_n^{\mathrm{sing}}(X)$ by choosing for each $n$-cell $e^n_\alpha$ a ch
aracteristic map $\Phi_\alpha:(D^n,S^{n-1})\to (X^n,X^{n-1})$ and sending the ge
nerator $[\Phi_\alpha]$ to the singular chain $\Phi_\alpha\cdot [D^n]$ (the push
forward of a fixed triangulation of $D^n$). The cellular boundary formula $\part
ial_n[\Phi_\alpha]=\sum_\beta d_{\alpha\beta}[\Phi_\beta]$ with $d_{\alpha\beta}
=\deg(p_\beta\circ\Phi_\alpha|_{S^{n-1}})$ ensures this is a chain map, and the
comparison theorem (Hatcher, Theorem 2.35) gives a chain homotopy equivalence

$$C_\bullet^{\mathrm{sing}}(X)\;\simeq\;\mathcal C_\bullet(X).$$

---

### Step 3. Apply $\operatorname{Hom}(-,G)$

The functor $\operatorname{Hom}(-,G)$ is additive and hence preserves chain homo
topy equivalences: a chain homotopy $h$ between $f$ and $g$ dualizes to a cochai
n homotopy $h^*$ between $f^*$ and $g^*$. Therefore applying $\operatorname{Hom}
(-,G)$ to the equivalence of Step 2 yields a chain (cochain) homotopy equivalenc
e

$$\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(C_\bullet^{\mathrm{sing}}(X),G\bigr)\;\simeq\;\operato
rname{Hom}\bigl(\mathcal C_\bullet(X),G\bigr).$$

The left-hand side is precisely the singular cochain complex $C^\bullet_{\mathrm
{sing}}(X;G)$, whose cohomology is by definition $H^n(X;G)$. The right-hand side
 is the cellular cochain complex

$$\mathcal C^\bullet(X;G),\qquad \mathcal C^n(X;G)=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal C
_n(X),G),\qquad \delta_n=\partial_{n+1}^{*}.$$

---

### Step 4. Cohomology is invariant under chain homotopy equivalence

Chain homotopy equivalent complexes have isomorphic homology (and cohomology). T
his is the standard fact: if $f:C\to D$ is a chain map with a chain homotopy inv
erse $g:D\to C$, then $f_*$ and $g_*$ are inverse isomorphisms on homology. The
same holds after applying any additive functor such as $\operatorname{Hom}(-,G)$
.

---

### Conclusion

Combining Steps 2–4:

$$\boxed{\,H^n(X;G)\;\cong\;H^n\bigl(\mathcal C^\bullet(X;G)\bigr)\,}$$

naturally in $X$ and $G$. Concretely, the cellular cochain group is

$$\mathcal C^n(X;G)=\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(H_n(X^n,X^{n-1}),\,G\bigr),$$

the free product of one copy of $G$ per $n$-cell, and the coboundary $\delta_n:\
mathcal C^n\to\mathcal C^{n+1}$ is the transpose of the cellular boundary $\part
ial_{n+1}$, i.e. on generators $\delta_n$ is given by the same integer matrix $(
d_{\alpha\beta})$ as the cellular boundary but acting on the right (contravarian
tly):

$$(\delta_n\varphi)(e^{n+1}_\beta)=\sum_\alpha d_{\beta\alpha}\,\varphi(e^n_\alp
ha).$$

Thus singular cohomology of a CW complex is computed by a cochain complex with o
ne copy of $G$ per cell, indexed by dimension, with coboundary the transpose of
the cellular boundary matrix. This is the cellular cohomology theorem, dual to t
he cellular homology theorem of Step 1.
