## Wave Operators for $H_0 = -i\,d/dx$ and $H_1 = -i\,d/dx + V(x)$

### Step 1. Conjugation identity

Set $W(x) := \int_0^x V(s)\,ds$ and let $U$ be the multiplication operator
$$
(Uf)(x) = e^{iW(x)}f(x), \qquad U^{-1}g(x)=e^{-iW(x)}g(x).
$$
A direct computation verifies the hint:
$$
(U^{-1}H_0 U f)(x)=e^{-iW(x)}\bigl[-i\,\tfrac{d}{dx}\bigl(e^{iW(x)}f(x)\bigr)\bi
gr]
= e^{-iW(x)}\bigl[W'(x)e^{iW(x)}f(x)-i\,e^{iW(x)}f'(x)\bigr]
= V(x)f(x)-if'(x)=(H_1 f)(x).
$$
Hence
$$
H_1 = U^{-1} H_0 U.
$$

### Step 2. Propagators

From the conjugation,
$$
e^{itH_1}=U^{-1}\,e^{itH_0}\,U.
$$
Since $itH_0 = it(-i\,d/dx)=t\,d/dx$, Taylor's formula gives
$$
(e^{itH_0}g)(x)=e^{t\,d/dx}g(x)=g(x+t).
$$
Therefore, for any $g$,
$$
(e^{itH_1}g)(x)=e^{-iW(x)}\,(e^{itH_0}(Ug))(x)=e^{-iW(x)}\,e^{iW(x+t)}\,g(x+t).
\tag{1}
$$

### Step 3. The wave operator $\Omega_+$

By definition
$$
\Omega_+ f = \lim_{t\to\infty} e^{itH_1}\,e^{-itH_0}f \quad (\text{limit in }L^2
(\mathbb R)).
$$
Since $e^{-itH_0}f(x)=e^{-t\,d/dx}f(x)=f(x-t)$, applying (1) with $g(x)=f(x-t)$:
$$
(e^{itH_1}e^{-itH_0}f)(x)=e^{-iW(x)}\,e^{iW(x+t)}\,f\bigl((x+t)-t\bigr)
= e^{\,i\,[W(x+t)-W(x)]}\,f(x).
$$
Writing the phase explicitly,
$$
\boxed{\;\bigl(e^{itH_1}e^{-itH_0}f\bigr)(x)=\exp\!\Bigl(i\int_x^{x+t}V(s)\,ds\B
igr)\,f(x).\;}
\tag{2}
$$

### Step 4. Taking $t\to\infty$

Define the multiplication operators
$$
(M_t f)(x):=\exp\!\Bigl(i\int_x^{x+t}V(s)\,ds\Bigr)\,f(x),\qquad |M_t f|=|f|,
$$
so each $M_t$ is unitary on $L^2(\mathbb R)$. The limit $\Omega_+f=\lim_{t\to\in
fty}M_t f$ exists in $L^2$ precisely when the phases $m_t(x):=\exp\!\bigl(i\int_
x^{x+t}V\bigr)$ converge (in the $L^2$-multiplication sense) on the support of $
f$.

**Case A — $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty}V(s)\,ds$ converges (improper integral)
.**
Then for every $x$,
$$
\int_x^{+\infty}V(s)\,ds=\int_0^{+\infty}V(s)\,ds-\int_0^x V(s)\,ds
$$
is a finite number, and
$$
m_t(x)\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{}m(x):=\exp\!\Bigl(i\int_x^{+\infty}V(s)\,ds\Bigr
)\quad\text{pointwise.}
$$
Since $|m_t f|=|f|\in L^2$, dominated convergence yields $M_t f\to Mf$ in $L^2$
for **every** $f\in L^2(\mathbb R)$, where
$$
\boxed{\;(\Omega_+ f)(x)=\exp\!\Bigl(i\int_x^{+\infty}V(s)\,ds\Bigr)\,f(x).\;}
\tag{3}
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{\;D(\Omega_+)=L^2(\mathbb R),\;}
$$
and $\Omega_+$ is the unitary multiplication operator by $\exp\!\bigl(i\int_x^{+
\infty}V(s)\,ds\bigr)$.

**Case B — $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty}V(s)\,ds$ does not converge.**
Then for every $x$ (at which $V$ is locally integrable) the phase $\int_x^{x+t}V
$ diverges as $t\to\infty$, so $m_t(x)$ fails to converge pointwise. For $M_t f$
 to converge in $L^2$ one needs, by the Cauchy criterion,
$$
\int_\mathbb R\bigl|m_{t+s}(x)-m_t(x)\bigr|^2|f(x)|^2\,dx\;\xrightarrow[t\to\inf
ty]{}\;0\quad\text{for every fixed }s.
$$
Since $|m_{t+s}-m_t|^2=|e^{i\int_{x+t}^{x+t+s}V}-1|^2$ oscillates without decayi
ng on sets of positive measure wherever $f\neq 0$, this forces $f=0$ a.e. Hence
$$
D(\Omega_+)=\{0\}.
$$

### Step 5. Verification (intertwining)

In Case A, $\Omega_+ = e^{iW(+\infty)}\,U^{-1}$ up to an $x$-independent phase o
f modulus $1$, hence is unitary. The intertwining relation follows directly from
 $H_1=U^{-1}H_0U$ and the fact that the constant $e^{iW(+\infty)}$ commutes with
 $H_0$:
$$
H_1\,\Omega_+=U^{-1}H_0U\,U^{-1}e^{iW(+\infty)}=U^{-1}e^{iW(+\infty)}H_0=\Omega_
+\,H_0.
$$
So $\Omega_+$ is a genuine wave operator mapping the free dynamics to the intera
cting dynamics.

### Summary

The forward wave operator is the multiplication operator
$$
\boxed{\;(\Omega_+ f)(x)=\exp\!\Bigl(i\int_x^{+\infty}V(s)\,ds\Bigr)\,f(x),\;}
$$
and its domain is
$$
\boxed{\;D(\Omega_+)=\bigl\{f\in L^2(\mathbb R):\lim_{t\to\infty}\exp\!\bigl(i\!
\int_x^{x+t}V(s)\,ds\bigr)\,f(x)\ \text{exists in }L^2(\mathbb R)\bigr\}.\;}
$$
In particular, **if the improper integral $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty}V(s)\,ds
$ converges**, then $D(\Omega_+)=L^2(\mathbb R)$ and $\Omega_+$ is the unitary o
perator given by (3); **if the integral diverges**, then $D(\Omega_+)=\{0\}$ and
 the (nontrivial) wave operator does not exist.
