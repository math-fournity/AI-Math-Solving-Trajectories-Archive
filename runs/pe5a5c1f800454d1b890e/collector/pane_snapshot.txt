## Proof

**Setup and notation.** Let $W$ be a standard Brownian motion. For $n \in \mathb
b{N}_0$ and $k = 1, \dots, 2^n$, the dyadic interval is $J_{k,n} = ((k-1)2^{-n},
\, k2^{-n}]$, and $\Delta W(J_{k,n}) = W_{k2^{-n}} - W_{(k-1)2^{-n}}$.

**Identifying the left half.** The interval $J_{l,m} = ((l-1)2^{-m},\, l2^{-m}]$
 has left half $((l-1)2^{-m},\, (l-\tfrac12)2^{-m}]$. Observing that
$$J_{2l-1,\,m+1} = ((2l-2)\,2^{-(m+1)},\, (2l-1)\,2^{-(m+1)}] = ((l-1)2^{-m},\,
(2l-1)\,2^{-(m+1)}],$$
we see that $J_{2l-1,\,m+1}$ is exactly the left half of $J_{l,m}$.

The hypothesis "$J_{k,n} \subset J_{l,m}$ lying in the left half" therefore mean
s
$$J_{k,n} \subset J_{2l-1,\,m+1}.$$

Since $J_{k,n}$ has length $2^{-n}$ and $J_{2l-1,\,m+1}$ has length $2^{-(m+1)}$
, this forces $n \ge m+1$.

**Decomposing the increment over $J_{2l-1,\,m+1}$.** Since $n \ge m+1$, the inte
rval $J_{2l-1,\,m+1}$ (a level-$(m+1)$ dyadic interval) is partitioned by level-
$(n+1)$ dyadic subintervals. Among these are the two children of $J_{k,n}$, name
ly $J_{2k-1,\,n+1}$ and $J_{2k,\,n+1}$ (both contained in $J_{k,n} \subset J_{2l
-1,\,m+1}$). Let $R$ denote the sum of Brownian increments over all the *other*
level-$(n+1)$ subintervals of $J_{2l-1,\,m+1}$. Then
$$\Delta W(J_{2l-1,\,m+1}) = \Delta W(J_{2k-1,\,n+1}) + \Delta W(J_{2k,\,n+1}) +
 R.$$

**Computing the expectation.** Setting $A = \Delta W(J_{2k-1,\,n+1})$, $B = \Del
ta W(J_{2k,\,n+1})$, we have
$$E[(A - B)\,\Delta W(J_{2l-1,\,m+1})] = E[(A-B)(A + B + R)] = E[A^2 - B^2] + E[
(A-B)\,R].$$

*First term.* Both $J_{2k-1,\,n+1}$ and $J_{2k,\,n+1}$ have length $2^{-(n+1)}$,
 so
$$E[A^2] = \operatorname{Var}(A) = 2^{-(n+1)}, \qquad E[B^2] = \operatorname{Var
}(B) = 2^{-(n+1)},$$
giving $E[A^2 - B^2] = 0$.

*Second term.* The intervals contributing to $R$ are level-$(n+1)$ dyadic interv
als strictly disjoint from $J_{2k-1,\,n+1}$ and $J_{2k,\,n+1}$ (they are the *ot
her* subintervals of $J_{2l-1,\,m+1}$). By the independent increments property o
f Brownian motion, $A$, $B$, and $R$ are mutually independent. Hence
$$E[(A - B)\,R] = E[A]\,E[R] - E[B]\,E[R] = 0 - 0 = 0.$$

**Conclusion.** Both terms vanish, so
$$E[(\Delta W(J_{2k-1,\,n+1}) - \Delta W(J_{2k,\,n+1}))\,\Delta W(J_{2l-1,\,m+1}
)] = 0. \qquad \blacksquare$$
