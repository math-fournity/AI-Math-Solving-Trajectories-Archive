## Proof

The answer is **yes**.

Let $\Sigma$ be a compact oriented surface of genus $g$ with $b \geq 1$ boundary
 components.

### Step 1: Structure of $H_1(\Sigma, \partial\Sigma)$

From the long exact sequence of the pair $(\Sigma, \partial\Sigma)$:
$$H_1(\partial\Sigma) \xrightarrow{i_*} H_1(\Sigma) \xrightarrow{j_*} H_1(\Sigma
, \partial\Sigma) \xrightarrow{\partial} \widetilde{H}_0(\partial\Sigma) \to 0$$

We have $\widetilde{H}_0(\partial\Sigma) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{b-1}$, $\ker \partial
 = \operatorname{im} j_*$, and $\operatorname{im} i_* \cong \mathbb{Z}^{b-1}$ (g
enerated by boundary loops subject to $\sum_k [\partial_k] = 0$). This yields a
short exact sequence:
$$0 \to \mathbb{Z}^{2g} \to H_1(\Sigma, \partial\Sigma) \to \mathbb{Z}^{b-1} \to
 0$$
so $H_1(\Sigma, \partial\Sigma) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{2g+b-1}$.

### Step 2: Geometric decomposition of $\Sigma$

Decompose $\Sigma = H \cup P$ where:
- $H$ is a genus-$g$ surface with one boundary component (the **handle part**),
- $P$ is a planar surface (genus $0$) with $b+1$ boundary components (the **plan
ar part**),
- $H \cap P = C$ is a simple closed curve (one boundary component of $H$ identif
ied with one of $P$).

The $b$ boundary components of $\Sigma$ are the $b$ free boundary components $\p
artial_1, \dots, \partial_b$ of $P$. Crucially, $\operatorname{int}(H) \cap \ope
ratorname{int}(P) = \emptyset$.

### Step 3: The $\mathbb{Z}^{2g}$ part (handle classes) via simple closed curves

The image of $j_*$ is generated by the standard handle curves $a_1, b_1, \dots,
a_g, b_g$, representable by simple closed curves in $\operatorname{int}(H)$. Sin
ce $\ker j_* = \operatorname{im} i_*$ is generated by boundary loops, any class
$v \in \operatorname{im} j_*$ equals $j_*(\bar{v}_{\mathrm{handle}})$ for some h
andle component $\bar{v}_{\mathrm{handle}} \in \mathbb{Z}^{2g}$.

Let $d = \gcd(\bar{v}_{\mathrm{handle}})$. If $d = 0$, the class is zero (empty
union). If $d \geq 1$, write $\bar{v}_{\mathrm{handle}} = d \cdot w$ with $w$ pr
imitive in $\mathbb{Z}^{2g}$.

**Key fact:** The mapping class group of a genus-$g$ surface with one boundary c
omponent surjects onto $\operatorname{Sp}(2g, \mathbb{Z})$ (for $g \geq 1$), and
 $\operatorname{Sp}(2g, \mathbb{Z})$ acts transitively on primitive vectors. The
refore $w$ is representable by a single simple closed curve $c$ in $\operatornam
e{int}(H)$. Then $v$ is represented by $d$ parallel copies of $c$, which are pai
rwise disjoint. (For $g = 0$, this part is trivial.)

### Step 4: The $\mathbb{Z}^{b-1}$ part (boundary connections) via properly embe
dded arcs

Choose arcs $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_{b-1}$ in $P$, where $\alpha_j$ is a proper
ly embedded arc from $\partial_1$ to $\partial_{j+1}$. These can be chosen pairw
ise disjoint: on a planar surface with $b+1$ boundary components, we select dist
inct endpoints on $\partial_1$ for each arc and route them through distinct regi
ons. (When $b=1$, this part is trivial.)

Their connecting homomorphism images are $\partial[\alpha_j] = [\partial_{j+1}]
- [\partial_1] \in \widetilde{H}_0(\partial\Sigma)$, which generate $\mathbb{Z}^
{b-1}$.

A class $w = \sum_{j=1}^{b-1} k_j [\alpha_j]$ is represented by $|k_j|$ parallel
 copies of $\alpha_j$ (oriented by $\operatorname{sgn}(k_j)$) for each $j$. Sinc
e the $\alpha_j$ are disjoint, we take tubular neighborhoods of each that are mu
tually disjoint, and place the parallel copies within each neighborhood. This yi
elds a disjoint union of properly embedded arcs in $P$.

### Step 5: Combining both parts

Given $\gamma \in H_1(\Sigma, \partial\Sigma)$, the short exact sequence splits
(since $\mathbb{Z}^{b-1}$ is free), and the geometric decomposition provides a n
atural splitting: write $\gamma = v + w$ with $v \in \operatorname{im} j_*$ (han
dle part) and $w$ in the $\mathbb{Z}^{b-1}$ part (boundary connections).

- Represent $v$ by disjoint simple closed curves in $\operatorname{int}(H)$ (Ste
p 3).
- Represent $w$ by disjoint properly embedded arcs in $P$ (Step 4).

Since $\operatorname{int}(H) \cap P = \emptyset$, the simple closed curves and p
roperly embedded arcs are disjoint from each other. Their union is a disjoint un
ion of simple closed curves and properly embedded arcs representing $\gamma$.

The zero class is represented by the empty union. This completes the proof. $\bl
acksquare$
