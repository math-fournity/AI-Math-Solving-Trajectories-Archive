Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $M^*(A \times B) = M^*(A)M^
*(B)$ holds for all $A, B \subset \mathbb{R}$, where $M^*$ is the Lebesgue outer
 measure (defined via coverings by open intervals on $\mathbb{R}$ and open recta
ngles on $\mathbb{R}^2$).

**Answer: The statement is TRUE.**

## Proof

We adopt the convention $0 \cdot \infty = 0$. We establish both inequalities.

### Upper Bound: $M^*(A \times B) \leq M^*(A)\,M^*(B)$

**Case 1: $M^*(A) = 0$ or $M^*(B) = 0$.** WLOG $M^*(A)=0$. For each $n \geq 1$,
cover $A$ by open intervals $\{I_i\}$ with $\sum |I_i| < \varepsilon$. Then $A \
times [-n,n] \subset \bigcup_i (I_i \times [-n,n])$, so $M^*(A \times [-n,n]) \l
eq 2n\varepsilon$. Since $\varepsilon$ is arbitrary, $M^*(A \times [-n,n]) = 0$.
 Now $A \times B \subset A \times \mathbb{R} = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A \times [-n
,n]$, so by countable subadditivity:
$$M^*(A \times B) \leq \sum_{n=1}^\infty M^*(A \times [-n,n]) = 0 = 0 \cdot M^*(
B). \quad\checkmark$$

**Case 2: $M^*(A) > 0$ and $M^*(B) > 0$.** For $\varepsilon > 0$, cover $A$ by o
pen intervals $\{I_i\}$ with $\sum |I_i| < M^*(A) + \varepsilon$ (if $M^*(A) < \
infty$; if $M^*(A)=\infty$, replace by any large $N$). Cover $B$ by open interva
ls $\{J_j\}$ with $\sum |J_j| < M^*(B) + \varepsilon$. Then $A \times B \subset
\bigcup_{i,j} I_i \times J_j$ and:
$$\sum_{i,j} |I_i|\,|J_j| = \left(\sum_i |I_i|\right)\left(\sum_j |J_j|\right) <
 (M^*(A)+\varepsilon)(M^*(B)+\varepsilon).$$
Taking $\varepsilon \to 0$ (or $N \to \infty$): $M^*(A \times B) \leq M^*(A)\,M^
*(B)$. $\checkmark$

### Lower Bound: $M^*(A \times B) \geq M^*(A)\,M^*(B)$

Let $A \times B \subset \bigcup_{i=1}^\infty R_i$ where each $R_i = I_i \times J
_i$ is an open rectangle. We show $\sum_i |I_i|\,|J_i| \geq M^*(A)\,M^*(B)$.

Define the function $S: \mathbb{R} \to [0, \infty]$ by:
$$S(x) = \sum_{i=1}^\infty |J_i|\,\mathbf{1}_{I_i}(x).$$

Since each $I_i$ is an open interval (hence measurable), each $\mathbf{1}_{I_i}$
 is measurable, and $S$ is a sum of non-negative measurable functions, hence mea
surable. By the Monotone Convergence Theorem (or Tonelli's theorem):
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} S(x)\,dx = \sum_{i=1}^\infty |J_i| \int_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbf
{1}_{I_i}(x)\,dx = \sum_{i=1}^\infty |J_i|\,|I_i|. \quad (\star)$$

**Key observation:** For each $x \in A$, the vertical slice $\{x\} \times B \sub
set \bigcup_i R_i$. Thus $B \subset \bigcup_{\{i \,:\, x \in I_i\}} J_i$, meanin
g the collection $\{J_i : x \in I_i\}$ is an open-interval covering of $B$. By d
efinition of outer measure:
$$S(x) = \sum_{\{i\,:\,x \in I_i\}} |J_i| \geq M^*(B), \qquad \forall\, x \in A.
$$

Therefore $S(x) \geq M^*(B)\,\mathbf{1}_A(x)$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

**Applying the upper Lebesgue integral.** Recall that for any set $E \subset \ma
thbb{R}$, the upper Lebesgue integral of its indicator satisfies $\overline{\int
}\,\mathbf{1}_E = M^*(E)$. (Proof: if $g \geq \mathbf{1}_E$ is measurable, then
$\{g \geq 1\} \supset E$ is measurable with $M(\{g\geq 1\}) \geq M^*(E)$, so $\i
nt g \geq M^*(E)$; conversely, any open $U \supset E$ gives $\mathbf{1}_U \geq \
mathbf{1}_E$ with $\int \mathbf{1}_U = M(U) \to M^*(E)$.) Also, $\overline{\int}
(c\,f) = c\,\overline{\int}\,f$ for $c \geq 0$.

Since $S$ is measurable and $S \geq M^*(B)\,\mathbf{1}_A$:
$$\sum_i |I_i|\,|J_i| \stackrel{(\star)}{=} \int S \geq \overline{\int}\!\left(M
^*(B)\,\mathbf{1}_A\right) = M^*(B)\,\overline{\int}\,\mathbf{1}_A = M^*(B)\,M^*
(A).$$

**Verification of edge cases in the lower bound:**
- If $M^*(B) = \infty$ and $M^*(A) > 0$: Then $S(x) = \infty$ on $A$. Since $M^*
(A) > 0$, no null set contains $A$, so $\int S = \infty \geq M^*(A) \cdot \infty
 = \infty$. $\checkmark$
- If $M^*(A) = 0$: The inequality $\sum |I_i||J_i| \geq M^*(B) \cdot 0 = 0$ is t
rivial. $\checkmark$

Since $\sum_i |I_i|\,|J_i| \geq M^*(A)\,M^*(B)$ holds for **every** covering by
open rectangles, taking the infimum yields:
$$M^*(A \times B) \geq M^*(A)\,M^*(B). \quad\checkmark$$

### Conclusion

Combining both inequalities:
$$\boxed{M^*(A \times B) = M^*(A)\,M^*(B)}$$
for all $A, B \subset \mathbb{R}$ (with the convention $0 \cdot \infty = 0$). Th
e statement is **true**.

The crucial ingredient in the lower bound is that the covering rectangles have *
*interval** sides, making $S(x) = \sum |J_i|\,\mathbf{1}_{I_i}(x)$ a **measurabl
e** majorant of $M^*(B)\,\mathbf{1}_A$. This allows us to pass from the pointwis
e inequality $S \geq M^*(B)\,\mathbf{1}_A$ to the integral inequality $\int S \g
eq M^*(B)\,M^*(A)$ via the upper Lebesgue integral — even when $A$ is non-measur
able.
