## Proof

Let the three distinct points be $A, B, C$, with integer coordinates, lying on a
 circle of radius $r > 0$. Denote the side lengths of $\triangle ABC$ by
$$a = |BC|,\quad b = |CA|,\quad c = |AB|.$$
We must show $\max(a,b,c) \ge r^{1/3}$.

**Step 1: The squared side lengths are positive integers.** Since the coordinate
s are integers, each squared distance is a sum of squares of integer differences
, hence a positive integer. In particular $a^2, b^2, c^2 \ge 1$, so $a, b, c \ge
 1$.

**Step 2: The area is at least $1/2$.** The (doubled) area
$$2K = \big|x_A(y_B - y_C) + x_B(y_C - y_A) + x_C(y_A - y_B)\big|$$
is an integer by the shoelace formula. Three distinct points on a circle cannot
be collinear (a line meets a circle in at most two points), so $K > 0$, hence $2
K \ge 1$, i.e.
$$K \ge \tfrac12.$$

**Step 3: Relate the sides to $r$.** The circumradius formula gives
$$r = \frac{abc}{4K}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad abc = 4rK \ge 4r\cdot\tfrac12 = 2r.$
$

**Step 4: Contradiction argument.** Suppose, for contradiction, that all three p
airwise distances are strictly less than $r^{1/3}$:
$$a < r^{1/3},\quad b < r^{1/3},\quad c < r^{1/3}.$$
Multiplying,
$$abc < r^{1/3}\cdot r^{1/3}\cdot r^{1/3} = r.$$
But Step 3 gives $abc \ge 2r$. Combining,
$$2r \le abc < r,$$
which forces $2r < r$, i.e. $r < 0$, contradicting $r > 0$.

Therefore our assumption is false, and at least one of $a, b, c$ satisfies
$$\max(a,b,c) \ge r^{1/3}.$$

That is, two of the three points are separated by a distance of at least $r^{1/3
}$. $\blacksquare$
