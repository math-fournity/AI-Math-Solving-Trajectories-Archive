## Proof

Let $J$ denote the $n \times n$ all-ones matrix, so $J = \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^T$
 has rank $1$. Consider the one-parameter family of matrices
$$M(x) \;=\; A - xJ,$$
i.e. the matrix whose $(i,j)$-entry is $a_{ij} - x$. Concretely, $M(x)$ has:

- diagonal entries $c_i - x$,
- strict-upper entries $a - x$,
- strict-lower entries $b - x$.

**Step 1. $\det M(x)$ is affine (degree $\le 1$) in $x$.**

Since $J = \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^T$ has rank $1$, the matrix determinant lemma gi
ves, whenever $A$ is invertible,
$$\det M(x) = \det(A - x\,\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^T) = \det(A)\bigl(1 - x\,\mathbf{
1}^T A^{-1}\mathbf{1}\bigr),$$
which is a polynomial in $x$ of degree at most $1$. Both sides of this identity
are polynomials in the entries of $A$ and in $x$; since they agree on the Zarisk
i-open set where $A$ is invertible, they agree identically. Hence $\det M(x) = \
alpha x + \beta$ for some constants $\alpha,\beta$ (depending on $A$), with
$$\beta = \det M(0) = \det A.$$

**Step 2. Evaluate at $x = a$ and $x = b$.**

At $x = a$: the strict-upper entries become $a - a = 0$, so $M(a)$ is **lower tr
iangular** with diagonal entries $c_i - a$. Thus
$$\det M(a) = \prod_{i=1}^n (c_i - a) = p(a).$$

At $x = b$: the strict-lower entries become $b - b = 0$, so $M(b)$ is **upper tr
iangular** with diagonal entries $c_i - b$. Thus
$$\det M(b) = \prod_{i=1}^n (c_i - b) = p(b).$$

**Step 3. Solve for $\beta = \det A$.**

From $\det M(x) = \alpha x + \beta$:
$$\alpha\, a + \beta = p(a), \qquad \alpha\, b + \beta = p(b).$$

Subtracting: $\alpha(a - b) = p(a) - p(b)$, so $\alpha = \dfrac{p(a) - p(b)}{a -
 b}$ (valid since $a \neq b$).

Therefore
$$\det A = \beta = p(a) - \alpha\, a = p(a) - a\,\frac{p(a) - p(b)}{a - b} = \fr
ac{p(a)(a - b) - a\bigl(p(a) - p(b)\bigr)}{a - b}.$$
Expanding the numerator:
$$p(a)(a-b) - a\,p(a) + a\,p(b) = a\,p(a) - b\,p(a) - a\,p(a) + a\,p(b) = a\,p(b
) - b\,p(a).$$
Hence
$$\det A = \frac{a\,p(b) - b\,p(a)}{a - b} = \frac{b\,p(a) - a\,p(b)}{b - a}. \q
quad \blacksquare$$
