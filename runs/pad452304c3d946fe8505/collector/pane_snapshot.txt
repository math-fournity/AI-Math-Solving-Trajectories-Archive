Looking at this problem, I need to find the dimension of the secant variety $\si

\mathbb{P}^5$, under the Plücker embedding.

## Setup

**The Grassmannian and its embedding.** The Grassmannian $G(2,6)$ parametrizes 2
-dimensional subspaces of $\mathbb{C}^6$ (equivalently, lines in $\mathbb{P}^5$)
. Under the Plücker embedding, it sits inside:
$$\mathbb{P}\!\left(\bigwedge\nolimits^2 \mathbb{C}^6\right) = \mathbb{P}^{14}.$
$
Its dimension is $\dim G(2,6) = 2(6-2) = 8$.

**Expected dimension of the secant variety.** For a variety $X \subset \mathbb{P
}^N$ of dimension $d$, the secant variety $\sigma_2(X)$ has expected dimension $
\min(2d+1,\, N)$. Here:
$$\min(2\cdot 8 + 1,\, 14) = \min(17, 14) = 14.$$
So the expected dimension is $14$, which would fill the entire ambient $\mathbb{
P}^{14}$.

## Identifying the secant variety via 2-form rank

A point of $\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^2 \mathbb{C}^6)$ is a 2-form $\omega$ on $\math
bb{C}^6$, represented by a $6\times 6$ skew-symmetric matrix $M_\omega$. The **r
ank** of a skew-symmetric matrix is always even, so the possible ranks are $2, 4
, 6$.

- **Rank 2:** $\omega$ is decomposable ($\omega = v \wedge w$), i.e., $\omega$ i
s a point of $G(2,6)$.
- **Rank 4:** $\omega$ can be written as a sum of two decomposable 2-forms, $\om
ega = v_1\wedge w_1 + v_2\wedge w_2$, i.e., $\omega$ lies on a secant line of $G
(2,6)$.
- **Rank 6:** $\omega$ is a general 2-form, not on any secant line.

Therefore:
$$\sigma_2\!\left(G(2,6)\right) = \{\omega \in \mathbb{P}(\wedge^2 \mathbb{C}^6)
 : \operatorname{rank}(M_\omega) \leq 4\}.$$

## The Pfaffian equation

The rank of a $6\times 6$ skew-symmetric matrix is $\leq 4$ if and only if its *
*full Pfaffian** vanishes:
$$\operatorname{Pf}(M_\omega) = 0.$$
For a $6\times 6$ skew-symmetric matrix, the Pfaffian is the single polynomial o
f degree $3$ in the Plücker coordinates $p_{ij}$ ($1\le i<j\le 6$) given by:
$$\operatorname{Pf} = p_{12}p_{34}p_{56} - p_{12}p_{35}p_{46} + \cdots \quad (\t
ext{sum over the 15 perfect matchings of } \{1,\dots,6\}).$$
This is the unique degree-3 equation cutting out the rank $\leq 4$ locus, since
the only minor controlling rank $< 6$ for a $6\times 6$ skew-symmetric matrix is
 the Pfaffian itself.

## Computing the dimension

The secant variety is thus:
$$\sigma_2\!\left(G(2,6)\right) = V\!\left(\operatorname{Pf}\right) \subset \mat
hbb{P}^{14}.$$
This is a **cubic hypersurface** in $\mathbb{P}^{14}$, defined by a single non-z
ero polynomial equation. A hypersurface in $\mathbb{P}^{14}$ has dimension:
$$14 - 1 = 13.$$

## Defectivity check

The expected dimension was $14$ (filling the ambient space), but the actual dime
nsion is $13$. The **defect** is:
$$\delta = 14 - 13 = 1.$$
This confirms that $G(2,6)$ is a **defective** variety with respect to its secon
d secant variety — a classical result. The defect arises because the single Pfaf
fian equation imposes one independent condition, preventing the secant variety f
rom filling $\mathbb{P}^{14}$.

## Conclusion

The secant variety $\sigma_2(G(2,6))$ is the cubic Pfaffian hypersurface in $\ma
thbb{P}^{14}$, which has dimension

$$\boxed{13}.$$
