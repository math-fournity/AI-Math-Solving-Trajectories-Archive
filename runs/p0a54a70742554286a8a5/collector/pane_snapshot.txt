## Proof

We evaluate $\displaystyle L = \lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^\pi e^{\,in\cos x}\,e^{-n
\sin x}\,dx$.

**Step 1: Simplify the integrand.** Combining the exponentials,
$$f_n(x) := e^{in\cos x - n\sin x}, \qquad |f_n(x)| = e^{-n\sin x}.$$

Since $\sin x \ge 0$ on $[0,\pi]$, we have $|f_n(x)|\le 1$, and the decay is con
trolled by $\sin x$.

**Step 2: Uniform convergence on the interior.** Fix any $\delta\in(0,\pi/2)$. O
n $[\delta,\,\pi-\delta]$ we have $\sin x \ge \sin\delta > 0$, hence
$$|f_n(x)| \le e^{-n\sin\delta}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0 \quad\text{uniformly
on }[\delta,\pi-\delta].$$
Therefore $f_n\to 0$ uniformly on $[\delta,\pi-\delta]$, and
$$\left|\int_\delta^{\pi-\delta} f_n(x)\,dx\right| \le (\pi-2\delta)\,e^{-n\sin\
delta}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.$$

**Step 3: Bounding the two boundary pieces.** Using the Jordan-type inequality $
\sin x \ge \frac{2x}{\pi}$ for $x\in[0,\pi/2]$ (applied to $x$ near $0$ and to $
\pi-x$ near $\pi$):

*Near $0$:* for $x\in[0,\delta]\subset[0,\pi/2]$,
$$\left|\int_0^\delta f_n(x)\,dx\right| \le \int_0^\delta e^{-n\sin x}\,dx \le \
int_0^\delta e^{-2nx/\pi}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{2n}\bigl(1-e^{-2n\delta/\pi}\bigr) \l
e \frac{\pi}{2n}.$$

*Near $\pi$:* setting $t=\pi-x$ and using $\sin x=\sin t\ge 2t/\pi$ for $t\in[0,
\delta]$,
$$\left|\int_{\pi-\delta}^{\pi} f_n(x)\,dx\right| \le \int_0^\delta e^{-n\sin t}
\,dt \le \frac{\pi}{2n}.$$

**Step 4: Combine.** Splitting the integral and applying the triangle inequality
,
$$\left|\int_0^\pi f_n(x)\,dx\right| \le \underbrace{(\pi-2\delta)\,e^{-n\sin\de
lta}}_{\to\,0} + \underbrace{\frac{\pi}{2n}}_{\to\,0} + \underbrace{\frac{\pi}{2
n}}_{\to\,0} \xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{} 0.$$

Since the right-hand side tends to $0$ for every fixed $\delta>0$, we conclude
$$L = \lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^\pi e^{in\cos x}\,e^{-n\sin x}\,dx = 0.$$

**Verification (optional, via contour integration).** Writing $in\cos x - n\sin
x = ni(\cos x+i\sin x)=ni\,e^{ix}$ and substituting $z=e^{ix}$ converts the inte
gral into $\frac{1}{i}\int_C \frac{e^{niz}}{z}\,dz$ over the upper semicircle. E
xpanding $e^{niz}$ in power series and integrating termwise yields $\int_0^\pi e
^{ni e^{ix}}dx = \pi - 2\,\mathrm{Si}(n)$, where $\mathrm{Si}(n)=\int_0^n\frac{\
sin t}{t}\,dt\to\frac{\pi}{2}$. Hence the limit is $\pi-2\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}=0$,
confirming the result.
