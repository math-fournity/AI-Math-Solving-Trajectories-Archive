**Step 1: Reduce modulo 9.**

$$E(n)=n^{3}-9n+27 \equiv n^{3} \pmod{9}.$$

By Fermat's little theorem (or direct check), $n^{3}\bmod 9 \in \{0,1,8\}$:
- $n\equiv 0,3,6\pmod 9 \Rightarrow n^{3}\equiv 0$,
- $n\equiv 1,4,7\pmod 9 \Rightarrow n^{3}\equiv 1$,
- $n\equiv 2,5,8\pmod 9 \Rightarrow n^{3}\equiv 8\equiv -1$.

So $E(n)\bmod 9 \in \{0,1,8\}$.

**Step 2: Rule out Drift Error $0$.**

For $E(n)\equiv 0\pmod{81}$ we'd need $E(n)\equiv 0\pmod 9$, forcing $3\mid n$.
Write $n=3k$:

$$E(3k)=27k^{3}-27k+27=27(k^{3}-k+1).$$

Now $k^{3}-k=k(k-1)(k+1)$ is a product of three consecutive integers, divisible
by $3$, so $k^{3}-k\equiv 0\pmod 3$, giving $k^{3}-k+1\equiv 1\pmod 3$. Hence

$$E(3k)=27\cdot(\text{something }\equiv 1\!\pmod 3)\equiv 27\pmod{81}.$$
