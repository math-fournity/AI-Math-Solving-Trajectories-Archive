## Proof

in(\pi/7)$, and $|DH|=|DE|=s$.

**Step 1: Direction of ray $GH$.**

At $G = V_6$ (angle $12\pi/7$), the adjacent vertices are $F=V_5$ (angle $10\pi/
7$) and $A=V_0$ (angle $0$). Computing the directions from $G$:

- Direction $G \to F$: $\frac{1350}{7}°$
- Direction $G \to A$: $\frac{450}{7}°$

The interior angle at $G$ is $\frac{900}{7}°$, spanning from $\frac{450}{7}°$ to
 $\frac{1350}{7}°$.

Since $H$ is inside and $\angle HGF = \frac{270}{7}°$, the ray $GH$ is obtained
by rotating $GF$ toward the interior by $\frac{270}{7}°$:

$$\text{dir}(GH) = \frac{1350}{7}° - \frac{270}{7}° = \frac{1080}{7}° = \frac{6\
pi}{7}$$

**Step 2: Locate $H$ using $|DH| = s$.**

Since $D = V_3$ is at angle $6\pi/7$, we have $D = (\cos\frac{6\pi}{7}, \sin\fra
c{6\pi}{7})$. Parametrize:

$$H = G + t\!\left(\cos\tfrac{6\pi}{7},\, \sin\tfrac{6\pi}{7}\right), \quad t >
0$$

Setting $u = t - 1$ and expanding $|DH|^2$:

$$|DH|^2 = 1 + 2u\cos\tfrac{6\pi}{7} + u^2$$

Setting this equal to $s^2 = 4\sin^2(\pi/7) = 2 - 2\cos(2\pi/7)$, and using $a =
 \cos(\pi/7)$, $b = \sin(\pi/7)$ with $\cos(6\pi/7) = -a$ and $\cos(2\pi/7) = 2a
^2 - 1$:

$$u^2 - 2au + 1 = 4 - 4a^2 \implies u^2 - 2au + 4a^2 - 3 = 0$$

$$u = a \pm \sqrt{3}\,b \implies t = 1 + a \pm \sqrt{3}\,b$$

The $+$ solution gives $t \approx 2.65$ (outside the heptagon), so:

$$t = 1 + a - \sqrt{3}\,b, \quad |HG| = t$$

**Step 3: Compute $x = \angle DHG$ via the law of cosines.**

In $\triangle DHG$: $|DH| = 2b$, $|HG| = 1 + a - \sqrt{3}\,b$, and $|DG|^2 = 2 +
 2a$ (since $D,V_3$ and $G,V_6$ are 3 apart).

**Numerator:**
$$|DH|^2 + |HG|^2 - |DG|^2 = 4b^2 + (1+a-\sqrt{3}\,b)^2 - (2+2a)$$

Expanding and using $a^2 + b^2 = 1$:

$$= 7b^2 + a^2 - 1 - 2\sqrt{3}\,b(1+a) = 6b^2 - 2\sqrt{3}\,b(1+a) = 2b\bigl(3b -
 \sqrt{3}(1+a)\bigr)$$

**Denominator:**
$$2|DH||HG| = 4b(1+a-\sqrt{3}\,b)$$

**Ratio:**
$$\cos x = \frac{2b\bigl(3b - \sqrt{3}(1+a)\bigr)}{4b(1+a-\sqrt{3}\,b)} = \frac{
3b - \sqrt{3}(1+a)}{2(1+a-\sqrt{3}\,b)}$$

Factor: $3b - \sqrt{3}(1+a) = \sqrt{3}\bigl(\sqrt{3}\,b - (1+a)\bigr)$ and $2(1+
a-\sqrt{3}\,b) = -2\bigl(\sqrt{3}\,b-(1+a)\bigr)$.

Since $\sqrt{3}\,b \neq 1+a$, these factors cancel:

$$\cos x = \frac{\sqrt{3}\bigl(\sqrt{3}\,b-(1+a)\bigr)}{-2\bigl(\sqrt{3}\,b-(1+a
)\bigr)} = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$$

Since $0° < x < 180°$:

$$x = 150°$$
