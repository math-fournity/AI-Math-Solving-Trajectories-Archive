## Proof

Let $G = \langle x, y \mid x^3 = y^2 = (xy)^3 = e \rangle$. We derive a normal f
orm and show $|G| = 12$.

### Step 1: Useful rewriting rules

From the relations $x^3 = e$ and $y^2 = e$ we reduce all powers: $x^i$ with $i \
in \{0,1,2\}$ and $y^j$ with $j \in \{0,1\}$.

From $(xy)^3 = e$, i.e. $xyxyxy = e$:
- Multiply on the right by $y$: $xyxyx \cdot y^2 = y$, so $xyxyx = y$, hence $xy
xy = yx^{-1} = yx^2$. **(R3)**

From $(yx)^3 = e$ (which is equivalent to $(xy)^3 = e$, since $(yx)^3 = y(xy)^2x
$ and $(xy)^3=e \Rightarrow (xy)^2 = (xy)^{-1}=y x^2$, giving $(yx)^3 = y\cdot y
x^2 \cdot x = y^2 x^3 = e$):
- $yxyxyx = e$, multiply on the right by $x^{-1}$: $yxyxy = x^2$, so $yxyx = x^2
 y$. **(R4)**

From **(R4)**: $yxy \cdot x = x^2 y$, so $yxy = x^2 y x^{-1} = x^2 y x^2$. **(R5
)**

From **(R3)**: $x \cdot yxy = yx^2$, so $yxy = x^{-1} y x^2 = x^2 y x^2$. Consis
tent with **(R5)**.

Now compute $yx^2 y$. Using $yxy = x^2 y x^2$ **(R5)**:
$$yx^2 y = yx \cdot (xy) = \ldots$$
Directly: $yx^2 y = yx \cdot xy$, and from $yxy = x^2 y x^2$ we get $yx = x^2 y
x^2 y^{-1} = x^2 y x^2 y$. Then
$$yx^2 y = (x^2 y x^2 y)\cdot x y = x^2 y x^2 (yx) y = x^2 y x^2 (x^2 y x^2 y) y
 = x^2 y x^4 y x^2 y^2 = x^2 y\, x\, y\, x^2.$$
Now apply **(R5)** to the middle $yxy$: $x^2(yxy)x^2 = x^2(x^2 y x^2)x^2 = x^4 y
 x^4 = x\, y\, x$. Thus
$$yx^2 y = xyx. \quad \textbf{(R6)}$$

**Verification of (R6):** $yx^2 y \cdot (xyx)^{-1} = yx^2 y \cdot x^2 y x^2 = yx
^2(yx^2 y)x^2 = yx^2(xyx)x^2 = yx^3 y x^3 = y^2 = e$. ✓

### Step 2: Reduction to normal form

After applying $x^3 \to e$ and $y^2 \to e$, every word has the form
$$x^{a_0}\, y\, x^{a_1}\, y\, x^{a_2} \cdots y\, x^{a_k}$$
with $a_0, a_k \in \{0,1,2\}$ and $a_1,\dots,a_{k-1} \in \{1,2\}$ (interior $x$-
blocks nonempty so that $y$'s are separated).

Whenever two $y$'s are separated by a single $x$-block $x^{a_i}$ ($a_i \in \{1,2
\}$), the subword $y\, x^{a_i}\, y$ is replaced using:
- **(R5):** $yxy \to x^2 y x^2$ (one $y$ instead of two),
- **(R6):** $yx^2 y \to xyx$ (one $y$ instead of two).

Each replacement reduces the $y$-count by exactly one. The replacement $x^2 y x^
2$ or $xyx$ merges its bordering $x$-powers with the neighboring $x$-blocks (red
uced mod 3 via $x^3 \to e$), preserving the alternating form. By induction on th
e number of $y$'s, **every word reduces to a form with at most one $y$**:

$$\boxed{\text{Normal form: } x^a \text{ or } x^a\, y\, x^b, \quad a,b \in \{0,1
,2\}.}$$

This gives at most $3 + 9 = 12$ elements, so $|G| \le 12$.

### Step 3: A concrete quotient of order 12

Consider $A_4$ (the alternating group on $\{1,2,3,4\}$, of order $12$). Set
$$x = (1\,2\,3), \qquad y = (1\,2)(3\,4).$$
Then $x^3 = e$, $y^2 = e$, and $xy = (1\,3\,4)$ is a $3$-cycle, so $(xy)^3 = e$.
 Thus the assignment $x \mapsto (1\,2\,3)$, $y \mapsto (1\,2)(3\,4)$ extends to
a homomorphism $\varphi: G \to A_4$.

Evaluating the $12$ normal forms under $\varphi$:

| Normal form | Image in $A_4$ | Cycle type |
|---|---|---|
| $e$ | $()$ | identity |
| $x$ | $(1\,2\,3)$ | 3-cycle |
| $x^2$ | $(1\,3\,2)$ | 3-cycle |
| $y$ | $(1\,2)(3\,4)$ | double transposition |
| $xy$ | $(1\,3\,4)$ | 3-cycle |
| $x^2 y$ | $(2\,3\,4)$ | 3-cycle |
| $yx$ | $(2\,4\,3)$ | 3-cycle |
| $xyx$ | $(1\,2\,4)$ | 3-cycle |
| $x^2 yx$ | $(1\,3)(2\,4)$ | double transposition |
| $yx^2$ | $(1\,4\,3)$ | 3-cycle |
| $xyx^2$ | $(1\,4)(2\,3)$ | double transposition |
| $x^2 yx^2$ | $(1\,4\,2)$ | 3-cycle |

These are $1$ identity $+\ 3$ double transpositions $+\ 8$ distinct $3$-cycles $
= 12$ distinct elements, i.e. **all of $A_4$**. Hence $\varphi$ is surjective an
d $|G| \ge 12$.

### Step 4: Conclusion

Combining $|G| \le 12$ (from the normal form) and $|G| \ge 12$ (from the surject
ion onto $A_4$):

$$|G| = 12.$$

The normal form is $x^a$ ($a \in \{0,1,2\}$) or $x^a y x^b$ ($a,b \in \{0,1,2\}$
), and the group $G$ is isomorphic to $A_4$.
