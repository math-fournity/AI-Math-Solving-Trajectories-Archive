**Proof.**

Define $e: \mathcal{F}(X) \to (0, \infty)$ by:
$$e(F) = \sum_{\substack{i \geq 1 \\ F \cap U_i = \emptyset}} 2^{-i}.$$

**Step 1: $e$ is well-defined and takes values in $(0,\infty)$.**

Since $X$ is metrizable (hence $T_1$), every finite set $F$ is closed. As $X$ is
 infinite and $F$ is finite, $X \setminus F$ is non-empty and open. Since $\{U_i

_{i_0} = \emptyset$, giving $e(F) \geq 2^{-i_0} > 0$. Also $e(F) \leq \sum_{i=1}
^{\infty} 2^{-i} = 1$, so $e(F) \in (0, 1] \subset (0, \infty)$.

**Step 2 (Dense $\Rightarrow$ $e \to 0$):** Let $F_n = \{x_1, \ldots, x_n\}$ and
 suppose $\{x_n : n \geq 1\}$ is dense in $X$. Given $\varepsilon > 0$, choose $
N$ with $\sum_{i > N} 2^{-i} < \varepsilon$. For each $i \in \{1, \ldots, N\}$,
since $U_i$ is non-empty open and $\{x_n\}$ is dense, there exists $k_i$ with $x
_{k_i} \in U_i$. Set $n_0 = \max(k_1, \ldots, k_N)$. For all $n \geq n_0$, we ha
ve $F_n \supseteq \{x_{k_1}, \ldots, x_{k_N}\}$, so $F_n \cap U_i \neq \emptyset
$ for every $i \leq N$. Therefore:
$$e(F_n) = \sum_{\substack{i > N \\ F_n \cap U_i = \emptyset}} 2^{-i} \leq \sum_
{i > N} 2^{-i} < \varepsilon.$$
Thus $e(F_n) \to 0$.

}$). Hence $e(F_n) \geq 2^{-i_0} > 0$ for all $n$, so $e(F_n) \not\to 0$.
