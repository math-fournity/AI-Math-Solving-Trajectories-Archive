Looking at this problem, I need to find all functions $f: \mathbb{Z}^+ \to \math
bb{Z}^+$ satisfying:
1. $f(f(f(x))) = x$ for all $x$
2. $f(x-1) + f(x) + f(x+1) = 3x$ for all $x > 1$

## Setting Up the Recurrence

Let $g(x) = f(x) - x$. Constraint 2 becomes:

$$(x-1+g(x-1)) + (x+g(x)) + (x+1+g(x+1)) = 3x$$

$$g(x-1) + g(x) + g(x+1) = 0 \quad \text{for all } x > 1$$

This gives the recurrence $g(n+1) = -g(n) - g(n-1)$ for $n \geq 2$, with charact
eristic equation $r^2 + r + 1 = 0$ (roots are primitive cube roots of unity). So
 $g$ is **periodic with period 3**:

Setting $a = g(1)$, $b = g(2)$, we get $g(3) = -a-b$, $g(4) = a$, $g(5) = b$, ..
. repeating.

Thus:
- $f(3k+1) = 3k+1+a$
- $f(3k+2) = 3k+2+b$
- $f(3k+3) = 3k+3-a-b$

## Positivity Constraints

For $f$ to map into $\mathbb{Z}^+$ (checking $k=0$ gives the tightest bounds):
- $1+a \geq 1 \Rightarrow a \geq 0$
- $2+b \geq 1 \Rightarrow b \geq -1$
- $3-a-b \geq 1 \Rightarrow a+b \leq 2$

Since $f(x) = x + g(x)$ with $x$ integer, $a, b$ must be integers.

## Enumerating Candidates

The integer pairs $(a,b)$ with $a \geq 0$, $b \geq -1$, $a+b \leq 2$:

| $(a,b)$ | $f(1),f(2),f(3)$ | $f^3=\text{id}$? |
|---------|-------------------|-----------------|

## Verifying the Three Solutions

**Solution 1** $(a,b)=(0,0)$: $f = \text{id}$. Trivially $f^3 = \text{id}$ and $
f(x-1)+f(x)+f(x+1) = (x-1)+x+(x+1) = 3x$. ✓

**Solution 2** $(a,b)=(1,1)$: $f(3k+1)=3k+2$, $f(3k+2)=3k+3$, $f(3k+3)=3k+1$. Ea
ch block $\{3k+1,3k+2,3k+3\}$ is a forward 3-cycle, so $f^3 = \text{id}$. Check:
 $f(1)+f(2)+f(3) = 2+3+1 = 6 = 3\cdot2$. ✓

**Solution 3** $(a,b)=(2,-1)$: $f(3k+1)=3k+3$, $f(3k+2)=3k+1$, $f(3k+3)=3k+2$. E
ach block is a backward 3-cycle, so $f^3 = \text{id}$. Check: $f(1)+f(2)+f(3) =
3+1+2 = 6 = 3\cdot2$. ✓

## Why No Other Solutions Exist

Any valid $f$ must have the periodic-shift form above (forced by the linear recu
rrence from constraint 2). The positivity constraints restrict $(a,b)$ to exactl
y 9 integer pairs, and checking $f^3 = \text{id}$ eliminates all but 3. The key
insight: $f^3 = \text{id}$ requires all orbits to have length 1 or 3 (not 2), wh
ich rules out the involution cases.
