## Answer: Yes — $T_pM$ is a faithful $\mathbb{R}G$-module (under the standard a
ssumptions that the action is effective and $M$ is connected).

## Proof

Let $\rho: G \to \mathrm{GL}(T_pM)$ be the **slice representation** $g \mapsto d
g_p$, and let
$$K := \ker \rho = \{g \in G : dg_p = \mathrm{id}_{T_pM}\}.$$
We prove the key identity
$$K = \ker\bigl(G \to \mathrm{Diff}(M)\bigr), \tag{$\ast$}$$
from which faithfulness of $\rho$ follows immediately whenever the action is eff
ective.

### Inclusion $\ker(\text{action}) \subseteq K$
Trivial: an element acting as the identity on all of $M$ acts as the identity on
 $T_pM$.

### Inclusion $K \subseteq \ker(\text{action})$
Let $g \in K$, so $dg_p = \mathrm{id}$. By the **Slice theorem**, there is a $G$
-equivariant diffeomorphism $\varphi: U_p \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} V \subseteq T_p
M$, where $V$ is a $G$-invariant open neighborhood of $0$ and $G$ acts on $T_pM$
 via the linear action $h \cdot v = dh_p(v)$. Since $g$ acts on $T_pM$ as $dg_p
= \mathrm{id}$, equivariance gives
$$g\big|_{U_p} = \varphi^{-1} \circ \mathrm{id} \circ \varphi = \mathrm{id}_{U_p
}.$$
So $g$ is the identity on the **open** set $U_p$.

Now use **finiteness of $G$**: average any Riemannian metric on $M$ over $G$ to
obtain a $G$-invariant metric. Then every $g \in G$ acts as an **isometry**. A b
asic fact of Riemannian geometry is that an isometry of a *connected* Riemannian
 manifold is uniquely determined by its $1$-jet at a single point (it sends geod
esics to geodesics and is determined through the exponential map). Pick any $q \
in U_p$: we have $g(q) = q$ and $dg_q = \mathrm{id}$ (because $g = \mathrm{id}$
on $U_p$). Therefore $g = \mathrm{id}_M$.

Hence $g$ lies in the kernel of the action, establishing $(\ast)$. $\square$

### Conclusion
From $(\ast)$: the slice representation $\rho$ is faithful **if and only if** th
e action $G \curvearrowright M$ is effective. Under the standard convention that
 "a group acts on a manifold" means an effective action (and $M$ is connected),
$T_pM$ is a faithful $\mathbb{R}G$-module.

### Remark on the hypotheses
The condition $M^G = \{p\}$ (a single fixed point) is **not** the ingredient tha
t forces faithfulness — it merely guarantees the action is nontrivial and single
s out $p$. The real work is done by three things:

1. **Finiteness of $G$** — which provides a $G$-invariant Riemannian metric, tur
ning every group element into an isometry.
2. **Effectiveness of the action** — which makes $\ker(\text{action})$ trivial.
3. **Connectedness of $M$** — which lets the "isometry determined by one $1$-jet
" principle propagate the local identity on $U_p$ to a global identity.

The same argument shows more generally: *for any effective action of a finite gr
oup on a connected manifold, the slice representation at every point is faithful
* — the fixed-point structure is irrelevant. The point of the "one fixed point"
hypothesis here is that it makes the question non-vacuous and tempts one toward
the (incorrect) shortcut "if $g$ fixes $U_p$ then $g$ fixes only $p$, contradict
ion" — which conflates $M^g$ with $M^G$. The correct route is the finite-order/i
sometry identity principle above.
