## Proof

Let $f$ be analytic on the open unit disk $D$, and suppose $|f(1/n)| \le e^{-n}$
 for every positive integer $n$.

**Step 1: $f(0)=0$.**

The sequence $z_n = 1/n$ lies in $D$ and converges to $0 \in D$. Since $f$ is an
alytic, it is continuous at $0$. The bound gives
$$|f(1/n)| \le e^{-n} \to 0 \quad (n\to\infty).$$
Hence $f(1/n)\to 0$, and by continuity $f(0)=\lim_{n\to\infty}f(1/n)=0$.

**Step 2: The zero at $0$ has infinite order, forcing $f\equiv 0$.**

Suppose, for contradiction, that $f$ is not identically zero. Then $f$ has a zer
o at $0$ of some finite order $m\ge 1$. Thus we may write
$$f(z) = z^m h(z),$$
where $h$ is analytic on $D$ and $h(0)\neq 0$.

Evaluating at $z=1/n$:
$$|f(1/n)| = n^{-m}\,|h(1/n)| \le e^{-n},$$
so
$$|h(1/n)| \le n^{m} e^{-n}.$$

Since $h$ is continuous at $0$, $h(1/n)\to h(0)$ as $n\to\infty$. Taking the lim
it of the right-hand side:
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} n^{m} e^{-n} = 0$$
(exponential decay dominates polynomial growth). Therefore
$$|h(0)| = \lim_{n\to\infty}|h(1/n)| \le \lim_{n\to\infty} n^{m}e^{-n} = 0,$$
which gives $h(0)=0$, contradicting the definition of $m$ (which requires $h(0)\
neq 0$).

**Conclusion.**

The contradiction shows that $f$ cannot have a zero of finite order at $0$; the
only analytic function on $D$ vanishing to infinite order at an interior point i
s the zero function. Hence

$$\boxed{f \equiv 0.}$$
