**Proof.**

We prove that for any partition of $\{1, 2, \ldots, 1978\}$ into 6 parts (countr
ies), there exist $x, y, z$ in the same part with $x + y = z$.

**Step 1: Multicolor Ramsey numbers for triangles.**

Let $R_r$ denote the $r$-color Ramsey number for triangles: the smallest $N$ suc
h that any $r$-coloring of the edges of $K_N$ contains a monochromatic triangle.
 We establish the recurrence:

$$R_r \leq r(R_{r-1} - 1) + 2.$$

*Proof of recurrence.* Consider a complete graph on $r(R_{r-1}-1)+2$ vertices wh
ose edges are colored with $r$ colors. Pick any vertex $v$; it has $r(R_{r-1}-1)
+1$ neighbors. By the pigeonhole principle, at least $R_{r-1}$ of these neighbor
s are connected to $v$ by edges of the same color, say color $c$. If any edge am
ong these $R_{r-1}$ vertices also has color $c$, we immediately have a monochrom
atic triangle in color $c$. Otherwise, the edges among these $R_{r-1}$ vertices
use only $r-1$ colors, so by the definition of $R_{r-1}$, there exists a monochr
omatic triangle in one of the remaining colors. $\square$

**Step 2: Compute the bound for $R_6$.**

Starting from $R_1 = 3$ (three vertices with one color always form a monochromat
ic triangle):

| $r$ | Bound on $R_r$ |
|-----|-----------------|
| 1 | $3$ |
| 2 | $2(3-1)+2 = 6$ |
| 3 | $3(6-1)+2 = 17$ |
| 4 | $4(17-1)+2 = 66$ |
| 5 | $5(66-1)+2 = 327$ |
| 6 | $6(327-1)+2 = 1958$ |

Thus $R_6 \leq 1958$.

**Step 3: From Ramsey to Schur (the key reduction).**

Suppose for contradiction that $\{1, 2, \ldots, 1978\}$ is partitioned into 6 co

numbers from the same country — that is, there is no monochromatic solution to $

Construct an edge-coloring of the complete graph $K_{1979}$ on vertices $\{0, 1,
 2, \ldots, 1978\}$ as follows: for vertices $i < j$, color the edge $\{i, j\}$

- $b - a$ is in country $C$,
- $c - b$ is in country $C$,
- $c - a$ is in country $C$,
