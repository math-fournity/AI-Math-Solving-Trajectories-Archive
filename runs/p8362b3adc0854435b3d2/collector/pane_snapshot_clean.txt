## Proof

Consider the surface $N = \{(x,y,z) \in \mathbb{R}^3 \mid x^4 + y^4 + z^2 = 1\}$
.

**Step 1: $N$ is a smooth surface.**

Let $f(x,y,z) = x^4 + y^4 + z^2$. The gradient is $\nabla f = (4x^3, 4y^3, 2z)$,
 which vanishes only at the origin. Since $f(0,0,0) = 0 \neq 1$, the origin is n
ot on $N$, so $1$ is a regular value of $f$. By the regular value theorem, $N =
f^{-1}(1)$ is a smooth surface.

**Step 2: $N$ is compact.**

$N$ is closed (preimage of the closed set $\{1\}$ under a continuous map) and bo
unded (since $x^4 \leq 1$, $y^4 \leq 1$, $z^2 \leq 1$ on $N$, we have $|x|,|y|,|
z| \leq 1$). So $N$ is a compact smooth surface.

**Step 3: $N$ is star-shaped with respect to the origin, hence homeomorphic to $
S^2$.**

For any nonzero vector $(x,y,z)$ and $t > 0$, consider
$$f(tx, ty, tz) = t^4(x^4 + y^4) + t^2 z^2.$$
This is a strictly increasing function of $t$ for $t > 0$ (its derivative $4t^3(
x^4+y^4) + 2tz^2 > 0$ for $t > 0$ and $(x,y,z) \neq 0$). At $t = 0$, $f = 0 < 1$
, and as $t \to \infty$, $f \to \infty$. By the intermediate value theorem, ther
e exists a unique $t_0 > 0$ such that $f(t_0 x, t_0 y, t_0 z) = 1$.

Thus every ray from the origin intersects $N$ in exactly one point. The radial p
rojection map
$$\rho: S^2 \to N, \quad \rho(p) = \frac{p}{\sqrt[4]{f(p)}\text{ appropriately s
caled}}$$
gives a homeomorphism: explicitly, $\rho(p) = t_0 p$ where $t_0$ is the unique p
ositive solution of $f(t_0 p) = 1$. This is continuous with continuous inverse (
radial projection from $N$ back to $S^2$).

Therefore $N \cong S^2$.

**Step 4: Compute the Euler characteristic.**

The Euler characteristic is a topological invariant, so
$$\chi(N) = \chi(S^2) = 2.$$

This can be verified by the standard cell decomposition of $S^2$ (one 0-cell, on
e 2-cell, giving $\chi = 1 - 0 + 1 = 2$), or by the Gauss–Bonnet theorem, or by
noting $S^2$ has genus $g = 0$ so $\chi = 2 - 2g = 2$.

$$\boxed{\chi(N) = 2}$$
