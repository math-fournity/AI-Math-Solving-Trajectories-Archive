## Solution

Let $S(n) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} i^{101}$. It is a classical fact (Faulhaber) that $S(
n)$ is a polynomial in $n$ of degree $102$. We determine its leading coefficient
.

**Key identity.** For all positive integers $n$,
$$S(n) - S(n-1) = n^{101},$$
with $S(0) = 0$.

**Extracting the leading coefficient.** Suppose
$$S(n) = c\, n^{102} + \text{(lower degree terms)}.$$
Then
$$S(n) - S(n-1) = c\bigl[n^{102} - (n-1)^{102}\bigr] + \text{(lower degree terms
)}.$$

Expanding $(n-1)^{102} = n^{102} - 102\, n^{101} + \cdots$, the leading term of
the difference is
$$n^{102} - (n-1)^{102} = 102\, n^{101} + \text{(lower powers of } n\text{)}.$$

The lower-degree terms of $S$ contribute only powers $n^{100}$ and below to the
difference $S(n)-S(n-1)$. Therefore the coefficient of $n^{101}$ in $S(n)-S(n-1)
$ is exactly $102c$.

Since $S(n) - S(n-1) = n^{101}$, the coefficient of $n^{101}$ on the right-hand
side is $1$. Hence
$$102c = 1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad c = \frac{1}{102}.$$

**Conclusion.** The leading coefficient of $f$ is $\dfrac{1}{102}$. With $a = 1$
, $b = 102$, which are coprime, we get
$$a + b = 1 + 102 = \boxed{103}.$$
