## Proof

We wish to maximize $\int_0^1 x\,f(x)\,dx$ subject to
$$\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx = 1, \qquad \int_0^1 x^2 f(x)\,dx = 1, \qquad f(x)\ge 0 \tex
t{ on }[0,1].$$

### Upper bound

The key observation is the pointwise inequality valid for every $x\in[0,1]$ (ind
eed for all real $x$):
$$x \;\le\; \frac{1+x^2}{2},$$
since $\frac{1+x^2}{2}-x = \frac{(1-x)^2}{2}\ge 0$.

Multiplying by $f(x)\ge 0$ and integrating over $[0,1]$ preserves the inequality
:
$$\int_0^1 x\,f(x)\,dx \;\le\; \int_0^1 \frac{1+x^2}{2}\,f(x)\,dx \;=\; \frac12\
int_0^1 f(x)\,dx + \frac12\int_0^1 x^2 f(x)\,dx.$$

Applying the two constraints $\int_0^1 f = 1$ and $\int_0^1 x^2 f = 1$:
$$\int_0^1 x\,f(x)\,dx \;\le\; \frac12\cdot 1 + \frac12\cdot 1 \;=\; 1.$$

Hence the functional is bounded above by $1$.

### The bound is sharp (attainment in the admissible class)

Equality in the pointwise bound $x\le(1+x^2)/2$ holds exactly when $(1-x)^2=0$,
i.e. at $x=1$. Equality in the integrated bound therefore requires $(1-x)^2 f(x)
=0$ a.e., meaning all the mass of $f$ is concentrated at $x=1$.

Interpreting the admissible class as nonnegative measures on $[0,1]$ (the natura
l setting for a moment problem, and the closure of the class of nonnegative dens
ities), the Dirac measure $f=\delta_1$ is admissible and satisfies
$$\int_0^1 d\delta_1 = 1, \qquad \int_0^1 x^2\,d\delta_1 = 1^2 = 1, \qquad \int_
0^1 x\,d\delta_1 = 1.$$

Thus the upper bound $1$ is achieved.

(Equivalently, within the class of genuine $L^1$ functions the feasible set is a
pproached by a sequence of densities concentrating near $x=1$ — e.g. $f_\varepsi
lon$ uniform on $[1-\varepsilon,1]$ — for which $\int f_\varepsilon=1$, $\int x^
2 f_\varepsilon\to 1$, and $\int x f_\varepsilon\to 1$; so $1$ is the supremum,
and it is attained in the closed/measure sense described above.)

### Conclusion

The maximum value of $\displaystyle\int_0^1 x\,f(x)\,dx$ is

$$\boxed{1}.$$
