# Problem

In a remote sector of the ocean, three research buoys—$A$, $B$, and $C$—form a triangular perimeter. The distance between $A$ and $B$ is $3\sqrt{30} - \sqrt{10}$ kilometers, the distance between $B$ and $C$ is exactly $12$ kilometers, and the distance between $C$ and $A$ is $3\sqrt{30} + \sqrt{10}$ kilometers. Two smaller data-collection nodes, $M$ and $N$, are anchored at the midpoints of the cables connecting $A$ to $B$ and $A$ to $C$, respectively.

A signal transmission path $l$ is established passing through the triangle's center of circumvection $O$ (the circumcenter) and its primary signal hub $H$ (the orthocenter). Engineers project two perpendicular lines from buoys $B$ and $C$ to intersect the path $l$ at calibration points $E$ and $F$. A secondary signal path $l'$ is then mapped by reflecting path $l$ across the line segment $BC$. This reflected path $l'$ intersects the lines $AE$ and $AF$ at junction points $P$ and $Q$. The intersection of the lines $BP$ and $CQ$ defines a critical coordinate $K$.

To stabilize the network, three virtual nodes $X$, $Y$, and $Z$ are generated by reflecting coordinate $K$ across the perpendicular bisectors of sides $BC$, $CA$, and $AB$, respectively. Two monitoring stations, $R$ and $S$, are positioned at the midpoints of the segments $XY$ and $XZ$. Finally, a central synchronization tower $T$ is placed at the intersection of the lines $MR$ and $NS$.

The distance from the circumvention center $O$ to the tower $T$ is found to be a rational number of kilometers expressed as $\frac{p}{q}$ in simplest form, where $p$ and $q$ are relatively prime positive integers. Calculate the value of $100p + q$.
