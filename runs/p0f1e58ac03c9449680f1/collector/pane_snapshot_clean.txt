We evaluate
$$L=\lim_{n\to\infty} S_n,\qquad S_n=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2\pi)^{2k}(-1)^k\
,(n+1)!}{(n+2k+1)!},\quad n\ge 4.$$

### Step 1. Integral representation via the Beta function

For non-negative integers $a,b$,
$$B(a+1,b+1)=\int_0^1 t^a(1-t)^b\,dt=\frac{a!\,b!}{(a+b+1)!}.$$

Taking $a=n+1$, $b=2k$:
$$\frac{(n+1)!\,(2k)!}{(n+2k+2)!}=\int_0^1 t^{n+1}(1-t)^{2k}\,dt.$$

Since $(n+2k+2)!=(n+2k+2)\,(n+2k+1)!$,
$$\frac{(n+1)!}{(n+2k+1)!}=\frac{n+2k+2}{(2k)!}\int_0^1 t^{n+1}(1-t)^{2k}\,dt. \
tag{1}$$

### Step 2. Summation under the integral

Substituting (1) into $S_n$:
$$S_n=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(2\pi)^{2k}(-1)^k\,\frac{n+2k+2}{(2k)!}\int_0^1 t^{n+1}
(1-t)^{2k}\,dt.$$

**Justification of interchange.** For $t\in[0,1]$ and fixed $n$,
$$\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2\pi(1-t))^{2k}}{(2k)!}(n+2k+2)\le (n+2)\cosh(2\pi)+
2\pi\sinh(2\pi),$$
a finite constant independent of $t$, hence integrable against $t^{n+1}$ on $[0,
1]$. By dominated convergence (Fubini–Tonelli, since absolute convergence holds)
,
$$S_n=\int_0^1 t^{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^k\bigl(2\pi(1-t)\bigr)^{2k}}
{(2k)!}\,(n+2k+2)\,dt. \tag{2}$$

### Step 3. Evaluating the inner sum

Write $n+2k+2=(n+2)+2k$ and set $x=2\pi(1-t)$.

- $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^k x^{2k}}{(2k)!}=\cos x$.
- Differentiating $\cos x=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^k x^{2k}}{(2k)!}$ gives
$-\sin x=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^k\,2k\,x^{2k-1}}{(2k)!}$, hence $\display
style\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^k\,k\,x^{2k}}{(2k)!}=-\frac{x\sin x}{2}$.

Therefore the inner sum in (2) equals
$$(n+2)\cos x-x\sin x=(n+2)\cos\!\bigl(2\pi(1-t)\bigr)-2\pi(1-t)\sin\!\bigl(2\pi
(1-t)\bigr).$$

Using $\cos(2\pi(1-t))=\cos(2\pi t)$ and $\sin(2\pi(1-t))=-\sin(2\pi t)$:
$$S_n=\int_0^1 t^{n+1}\Bigl[(n+2)\cos(2\pi t)+2\pi(1-t)\sin(2\pi t)\Bigr]\,dt. \
tag{3}$$

### Step 4. Taking $n\to\infty$

Split (3):
$$S_n=(n+2)\underbrace{\int_0^1 t^{n+1}\cos(2\pi t)\,dt}_{I_n}+2\pi\underbrace{\
int_0^1 t^{n+1}(1-t)\sin(2\pi t)\,dt}_{J_n}.$$

**The tail $J_n$.** Since $|\sin|\le 1$,
$$|J_n|\le\int_0^1 t^{n+1}(1-t)\,dt=\frac{1}{(n+2)(n+3)}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]
{}0.$$

**The leading term $(n+2)I_n$.** Integrate by parts with $u=\cos(2\pi t)$, $dv=t
^{n+1}dt$:
$$I_n=\Bigl[\frac{t^{n+2}}{n+2}\cos(2\pi t)\Bigr]_0^1+\frac{2\pi}{n+2}\int_0^1 t
^{n+2}\sin(2\pi t)\,dt=\frac{1}{n+2}+\frac{2\pi}{n+2}\int_0^1 t^{n+2}\sin(2\pi t
)\,dt,$$
since $\cos(2\pi)=\cos 0=1$ and $0^{n+2}=0$. Multiplying by $n+2$:
$$(n+2)I_n=1+2\pi\int_0^1 t^{n+2}\sin(2\pi t)\,dt.$$

The remainder is bounded by
$$\Bigl|2\pi\int_0^1 t^{n+2}\sin(2\pi t)\,dt\Bigr|\le\frac{2\pi}{n+3}\xrightarro
w[n\to\infty]{}0.$$

Hence $(n+2)I_n\to 1$.

### Step 5. Conclusion

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} S_n=\lim_{n\to\infty}\bigl[(n+2)I_n+2\pi J_n\bigr]=1+0=\boxe
d{1}.$$
