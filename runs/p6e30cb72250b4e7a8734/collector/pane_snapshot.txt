## Proof

**Claim:** The statement is **true**. For every $1 \leq j \leq k$, there exists
a subgroup $H \leq G$ with $|H| = p^j$.

The proof combines Sylow's First Theorem with the subgroup-structure theorem for
 finite $p$-groups.

---

**Step 1: Existence of a Sylow $p$-subgroup.**

By Sylow's First Theorem, since $p^k \mid |G|$ and $p^{k+1} \nmid |G|$, the grou
p $G$ possesses a subgroup $P$ of order $p^k$. Such a $P$ is called a Sylow $p$-
subgroup of $G$, and it is in particular a finite $p$-group.

---

**Step 2: Every finite $p$-group of order $p^k$ has a subgroup of order $p^j$ fo
r each $0 \leq j \leq k$.**

We prove this by induction on $k$.

*Base case* ($k = 1$): A group of order $p$ has the trivial subgroup (order $p^0
 = 1$) and itself (order $p^1$). Done.

*Inductive step*: Assume the result for all $p$-groups of order $p^{k-1}$, and l
et $P$ have order $p^k$ with $k \geq 2$.

A standard theorem on finite $p$-groups states that the center $Z(P)$ is nontriv
ial (this follows from the class equation: $|P| = |Z(P)| + \sum [P : C_P(x_i)]$,
 where each index $[P:C_P(x_i)]$ is a positive power of $p$, forcing $p \mid |Z(
P)|$).

Since $Z(P)$ is a nontrivial $p$-group, it contains an element $z$ of order $p$.
 Let $N = \langle z \rangle$. Because $z \in Z(P)$, the subgroup $N$ is central,
 hence **normal** in $P$, and $|N| = p$.

Now consider the quotient $\overline{P} = P/N$, which has order $p^{k-1}$. By th
e inductive hypothesis, $\overline{P}$ has subgroups $\overline{H}_j$ of order $
p^j$ for every $0 \leq j \leq k-1$.

Let $\pi : P \to P/N$ be the canonical projection. For each $j$ with $0 \leq j \
leq k-1$, the preimage $H_{j+1} = \pi^{-1}(\overline{H}_j)$ is a subgroup of $P$
, and by the correspondence theorem (Lagrange applied to the kernel $N$ of order
 $p$):

$$|H_{j+1}| = |N| \cdot |\overline{H}_j| = p \cdot p^j = p^{j+1}.$$

As $j$ ranges over $0, 1, \dots, k-1$, the orders $p^{j+1}$ range over $p^1, p^2
, \dots, p^k$. Together with the trivial subgroup (order $p^0$), this gives subg
roups of every order $p^0, p^1, \dots, p^k$.

This completes the induction.

---

**Step 3: Conclusion.**

From Step 1, $G$ has a Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$ of order $p^k$. From Step 2, $P$ (
hence $G$) contains subgroups of orders $p^1, p^2, \dots, p^k$. Therefore, for e
very $1 \leq j \leq k$, there exists a subgroup $H$ of $G$ with $|H| = p^j$.

The statement is **true**. $\blacksquare$
