## Proof

**Setup.** Model the situation as a directed graph $D$ on 16 vertices (spies), w
here $A \to B$ means "$A$ watches $B$." The condition is antisymmetric: for any
pair, at most one directed edge exists. The hypothesis says every 10-vertex indu
ced subgraph contains a directed Hamiltonian cycle. We must show the same for ev
ery 11-vertex induced subgraph.

**Step 1: Degree bounds.** For any vertex $v$, let $d^+(v)$ and $d^-(v)$ denote
its out- and in-degree in $D$. In any 10-subset $S$ containing $v$, the Hamilton
ian cycle requires $v$ to have out-degree $\geq 1$ and in-degree $\geq 1$ within
 $S$. To minimize $v$'s out-neighbors in $S$, we pick 9 vertices from the $15 -
d^+(v)$ non-out-neighbors of $v$. If $d^+(v) \leq 6$, then $15 - d^+(v) \geq 9$,
 so we could choose 9 non-out-neighbors, giving $v$ out-degree $0$ in $S$—contra
diction. Hence $d^+(v) \geq 7$. Similarly $d^-(v) \geq 7$. By antisymmetry, $d^+
(v) + d^-(v) \leq 15$, so:
$$d^+(v) + d^-(v) \in \{14, 15\} \quad \text{for every vertex } v.$$

**Step 2: Assume for contradiction** that some 11-subset $T$ has no Hamiltonian
cycle.

**Step 3: Every vertex in $T$ has a non-neighbor in $T$.** For each $w \in T$, t
he 10-subset $T \setminus \{w\}$ has a Hamiltonian cycle $C_w = (u_1, u_2, \ldot
s, u_{10}, u_1)$. If $w$ could be inserted into $C_w$—meaning some $u_i \to w$ a
nd $w \to u_{i+1}$—then $T$ would have a Hamiltonian cycle, contradicting our as
sumption. So insertion fails.

Define:
- $A = \{i : u_i \to w\}$, $\quad B = \{i : w \to u_i\}$, $\quad N = \{i : \text
{no edge between } u_i \text{ and } w\}$.

These partition $\{1, \ldots, 10\} = \mathbb{Z}_{10}$. From Step 1, $w$ has at l
east $7 - 5 = 2$ in-neighbors in $T$ (at most 5 of its $\geq 7$ in-neighbors lie
 outside $T$), so $|A| \geq 2$. Similarly $|B| \geq 2$.

Insertion failure means: for all $i$, not both $i \in A$ and $i+1 \in B$ (mod 10
), i.e., $(A+1) \cap B = \emptyset$.

**Claim:** $N \neq \emptyset$. Suppose $N = \emptyset$, so $A \cup B = \mathbb{Z
}_{10}$ and $A \cap B = \emptyset$ with both nonempty. The condition $(A+1) \cap
 B = \emptyset$ means $A + 1 \subseteq A$ (since $A+1 \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_{10}
= A \cup B$ and $A+1$ avoids $B$). But $|A+1| = |A|$ and $A+1 \subseteq A$ force
s $A+1 = A$, meaning $A$ is invariant under the cyclic shift $i \mapsto i+1$. Th
e only subsets of $\mathbb{Z}_{10}$ invariant under this shift are $\emptyset$ a
nd $\mathbb{Z}_{10}$ itself, contradicting $|A| \geq 2$ and $|B| \geq 2$.

So $N \neq \emptyset$: $w$ has at least one non-neighbor in $T \setminus \{w\} \
subseteq T$.

**Step 4: Counting contradiction.** Since every $w \in T$ has a non-neighbor in
$T$, and $d^+(w) + d^-(w) \in \{14, 15\}$:
- If $d^+(w) + d^-(w) = 15$, then $w$ is adjacent to all 15 other vertices—no no
n-neighbor exists, contradicting Step 3.
- So $d^+(w) + d^-(w) = 14$ for every $w \in T$, meaning each $w$ has **exactly
one** non-neighbor among all 15 other vertices.

By Step 3, this unique non-neighbor must lie in $T$. Non-adjacency is symmetric
(no edge $w \to u$ iff no edge $u \to w$), so the map $w \mapsto n(w)$ assigning
 each vertex its non-neighbor is an involution without fixed points—a **perfect
matching** on $T$.

But $|T| = 11$ is odd, and no perfect matching exists on an odd number of vertic
es. **Contradiction.**

**Conclusion.** Our assumption was false: every 11-subset $T$ has a Hamiltonian
cycle. That is, any 11 spies can be numbered so that the first watches the secon
d, the second watches the third, ..., and the eleventh watches the first. $\blac
