## Proof

We use the addition formula for sine:

$$\sin(x+y) = \sin x \cos y + \cos x \sin y.$$

Substituting into the definition of $T$:

$$T(f)(x) = \int_0^1 \sin(x+y)\,f(y)\,dy = \sin x \int_0^1 \cos y\, f(y)\,dy + \
cos x \int_0^1 \sin y\, f(y)\,dy.$$

Define the two linear functionals on $C[0,1]$:

$$A(f) = \int_0^1 \cos y\, f(y)\,dy, \qquad B(f) = \int_0^1 \sin y\, f(y)\,dy.$$

Then for every $f \in C[0,1]$:

$$T(f) = A(f)\,\sin x + B(f)\,\cos x.$$

**The range is contained in $\operatorname{span}\{\sin x,\, \cos x\}$.** Hence $
\dim(\operatorname{Range}(T)) \le 2$.

**The functions $\sin x$ and $\cos x$ are linearly independent** on $[0,1]$ (the
y are not scalar multiples of one another), so $\dim(\operatorname{span}\{\sin x
,\cos x\}) = 2$.

**The image of $f \mapsto (A(f), B(f))$ is all of $\mathbb{R}^2$.** It suffices
to show that $A$ and $B$ are linearly independent as functionals. If they were d
ependent, there would exist $(\alpha,\beta)\neq(0,0)$ with $\alpha\, A(f) + \bet
a\, B(f) = 0$ for all $f$, i.e.,

$$\int_0^1 (\alpha \cos y + \beta \sin y)\, f(y)\,dy = 0 \quad \forall f \in C[0
,1].$$

By the fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations, this forces $\alpha \cos
 y + \beta \sin y = 0$ for all $y \in [0,1]$. But $\cos y$ and $\sin y$ are line
arly independent functions (e.g., evaluating at $y=0$ gives $\alpha = 0$, and at
 $y=\pi/2$ gives $\beta = 0$), a contradiction. Hence $A$ and $B$ are independen
t, and the map $f \mapsto (A(f),B(f))$ is surjective onto $\mathbb{R}^2$.

Therefore every linear combination $a\sin x + b\cos x$ is attained, and

$$\operatorname{Range}(T) = \operatorname{span}\{\sin x,\, \cos x\}.$$

**The dimension of the range space of $T$ is $\boxed{2}$.**
