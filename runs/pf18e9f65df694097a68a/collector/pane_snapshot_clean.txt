**Proof.**

We show that such a homeomorphism exists.

**Step 1: An order isomorphism between the rationals.**

The ordered sets $\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1)$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ are both countable, dens
e, linear orders without endpoints. By Cantor's back-and-forth theorem (any two
countable dense linear orders without endpoints are order-isomorphic), there exi
sts an order isomorphism
$$g:\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1)\;\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\;\mathbb{Q}.$$

**Step 2: Unique extension to the Dedekind completions.**

Any order isomorphism between dense linear orders extends uniquely to an order i
somorphism of their Dedekind completions. The Dedekind completion of $\mathbb{Q}
\cap(0,1)$ is $[0,1]$ (the endpoints $0$ and $1$ are added since $\mathbb{Q}\cap
(0,1)$ has no least or greatest element), and the Dedekind completion of $\mathb
b{Q}$ is $\overline{\mathbb{R}}=[-\infty,+\infty]$ (the points $\pm\infty$ are a
dded).

Thus $g$ extends uniquely to an order isomorphism
$$\widehat{g}:[0,1]\;\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\;\overline{\mathbb{R}}.$$
Since $g$ is order-preserving, $\widehat{g}$ sends the least element $0$ to $-\i
nfty$ and the greatest element $1$ to $+\infty$.

**Step 3: Restrict to the interiors.**

Removing the two endpoints, $\widehat{g}$ restricts to an order isomorphism
$$f := \widehat{g}\big|_{(0,1)}:(0,1)\;\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\;\overline{\mathb
b{R}}\setminus\{-\infty,+\infty\}=\mathbb{R}.$$
An order isomorphism between intervals in $\mathbb{R}$ is strictly monotone and
continuous with continuous inverse, hence a homeomorphism. So $f:(0,1)\to\mathbb
{R}$ is a homeomorphism.

**Step 4: $f$ maps rationals onto rationals.**

By construction $f$ extends $g$, so for every $q\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1)$ we have
$f(q)=g(q)\in\mathbb{Q}$. Conversely, for any $r\in\mathbb{Q}$, the point $g^{-1
}(r)\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1)$ satisfies $f(g^{-1}(r))=r$. Hence
$$f\bigl(\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1)\bigr)=\mathbb{Q}.$$

**Step 5: The restriction is a homeomorphism.**

The restriction of a homeomorphism to any subspace is a homeomorphism onto its i
mage (with the subspace topologies). Therefore
$$f\big|_{\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1)}:\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1)\;\longrightarrow\;\mathbb{Q}$
$
is a homeomorphism, where $\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1)$ carries the subspace topology fr
om $(0,1)$ (equivalently from $\mathbb{R}$) and $\mathbb{Q}$ carries the subspac
e topology from $\mathbb{R}$.

**Conclusion.** There exists a homeomorphism $f:(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}$ whose restri
ction $f:(0,1)\cap\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{Q}$ is also a homeomorphism. $\blacksquar
