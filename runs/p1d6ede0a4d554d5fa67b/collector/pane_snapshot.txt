## Proof

Let $C_k$ be the upper semicircle of radius $k$ centered at the origin, traverse
d counter-clockwise. We parameterize it by
$$z = k e^{i\theta}, \qquad \theta \in [0, \pi],$$
so that $dz = i k e^{i\theta}\, d\theta$ and the traversal from $\theta = 0$ (th
e point $k$) to $\theta = \pi$ (the point $-k$) through the upper half-plane is
counter-clockwise.

The integral becomes
$$
\int_{C_k} \frac{\cos z}{z}\, dz
= \int_0^\pi \frac{\cos(k e^{i\theta})}{k e^{i\theta}}\, i k e^{i\theta}\, d\the
ta
= i \int_0^\pi \cos(k e^{i\theta})\, d\theta.
$$

Now examine the limit $k \to 0$. For every $\theta \in [0,\pi]$,
$$
\cos(k e^{i\theta}) \to \cos(0) = 1 \quad \text{as } k \to 0.
$$
Moreover, since $|k e^{i\theta}| = k$,
$$
|\cos(k e^{i\theta}) - 1|
= \left| \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^n (k e^{i\theta})^{2n}}{(2n)!} \right|
\le \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{k^{2n}}{(2n)!}
= \cosh k - 1,
$$
which is independent of $\theta$ and tends to $0$ as $k \to 0$. Hence $\cos(k e^
{i\theta}) \to 1$ **uniformly** on $[0,\pi]$, and we may pass the limit inside t
he integral:
$$
\lim_{k \to 0} \int_{C_k} \frac{\cos z}{z}\, dz
= i \int_0^\pi \lim_{k \to 0} \cos(k e^{i\theta})\, d\theta
= i \int_0^\pi 1\, d\theta
= i\pi.
$$

**Consistency check.** The function $\cos z / z$ has a simple pole at $z = 0$ wi
th residue $\operatorname{Res}_{z=0} \frac{\cos z}{z} = \cos(0) = 1$. A full cou
nter-clockwise circle around the origin would give $2\pi i \cdot 1 = 2\pi i$; th
e upper semicircle contributes exactly half of this, namely $\pi i$, which agree
s with our computation.

Therefore,
$$
\boxed{\lim_{k \to 0} \int_{C_k} \frac{\cos z}{z}\, dz = i\pi.}
$$
