## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $\mathfrak{p}$ is necessarily principal.

**Setup.** Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field and let $\mathfrak{p}\in\ope
ratorname{Spec}k[x,y]$ be an irreducible prime ideal defining a curve $C\subset\
mathbb{A}_k^2$. By "curve" we mean an algebraic set of pure dimension $1$. Since
 $\dim k[x,y]=2$ (a polynomial ring in two variables over a field), the coordina
te ring $k[C]=k[x,y]/\mathfrak{p}$ has dimension $1$, so by the dimension formul
a
$$\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})=\dim k[x,y]-\dim k[C]=2-1=1.$$
Thus $\mathfrak{p}$ is a **height-one** prime ideal of $k[x,y]$.

**Key fact: $k[x,y]$ is a UFD.** Since $k$ is a field (hence a UFD), and a polyn
omial ring over a UFD is again a UFD, $k[x]$ is a UFD, and then $k[x,y]=k[x][y]$
 is a UFD.

**Height-one primes in a UFD are principal.** Let $R$ be a UFD and $\mathfrak{q}
\subset R$ a prime ideal of height $1$. Pick any nonzero element $a\in\mathfrak{
q}$. Since $R$ is a UFD, $a$ admits a factorization $a=u\pi_1\cdots\pi_r$ into a
 unit $u$ and irreducible elements $\pi_i$. Because $\mathfrak{q}$ is prime and
$a\in\mathfrak{q}$, at least one factor $\pi_i$ lies in $\mathfrak{q}$. Each irr
educible element $\pi_i$ generates a prime ideal $(\pi_i)$ (in a UFD, irreducibl
e elements are prime), and $(\pi_i)$ is a nonzero prime ideal, hence has height
$\geq 1$. Since $\mathfrak{q}$ has height exactly $1$ and $(\pi_i)\subseteq\math
frak{q}$ with $(\pi_i)$ prime of height $\geq 1$, we must have $(\pi_i)=\mathfra
k{q}$. Therefore $\mathfrak{q}=(\pi_i)$ is principal.

**Conclusion.** Applying this to $R=k[x,y]$ and $\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{p}$, the
 height-one prime $\mathfrak{p}$ is generated by a single irreducible polynomial
