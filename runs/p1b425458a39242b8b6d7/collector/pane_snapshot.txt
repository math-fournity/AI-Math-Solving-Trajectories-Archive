Looking at this problem, I need to determine which grid dimensions $(n,m)$ admit
 a permutation $f$ of $\mathbb{Z}_n \times \mathbb{Z}_m$ such that the map $v \m
apsto v + f(v) \pmod{(n,m)}$ is also a permutation. This is exactly the **comple
te mapping** problem from group theory.

## Proof

**Reformulation.** Let $G = \mathbb{Z}_n \times \mathbb{Z}_m$. Condition 1 (No C
ongestion) requires $f: G \to G$ to be injective, hence bijective (a permutation
). Condition 2 (Interference-Free Routing) requires the map $g: v \mapsto v + f(
v) \pmod{(n,m)}$ to also be injective, hence bijective. A permutation $f$ of a g
roup $G$ such that $x \mapsto x \cdot f(x)$ is also a permutation is called a **
complete mapping** of $G$.

**Applying the Hall–Paige theorem.** The Hall–Paige theorem (proved for abelian
groups by Hall and Paige, and in full generality by Wilcox et al.) states:

> A finite group $G$ admits a complete mapping if and only if its Sylow 2-subgro
up is trivial or non-cyclic.

**Analyzing the Sylow 2-subgroup of $G = \mathbb{Z}_n \times \mathbb{Z}_m$.** Wr
ite $n = 2^a \cdot n'$ and $m = 2^b \cdot m'$ with $n', m'$ odd. The Sylow 2-sub
group of $G$ is $P = \mathbb{Z}_{2^a} \times \mathbb{Z}_{2^b}$.

- **Both $n, m$ odd** ($a = b = 0$): $P$ is trivial → complete mapping exists. ✓
- **Exactly one of $n, m$ even** (say $a \geq 1, b = 0$): $P \cong \mathbb{Z}_{2
^a}$ is a non-trivial cyclic group → no complete mapping. ✗
- **Both $n, m$ even** ($a, b \geq 1$): $P = \mathbb{Z}_{2^a} \times \mathbb{Z}_
{2^b}$. Since $\gcd(2^a, 2^b) \geq 2$, this group is **non-cyclic** → complete m
apping exists. ✓

**Verification of small cases:**
- $(n,m) = (1,2)$: $G = \mathbb{Z}_2$. The two permutations are $\text{id}$ (giv
ing $g \equiv 0$) and the swap (giving $g \equiv 1$). Neither is a permutation.
✗ (exactly one even)
- $(n,m) = (2,2)$: $G = \mathbb{Z}_2^2$. The permutation $f(0,0)=(0,0), f(0,1)=(
1,0), f(1,0)=(1,1), f(1,1)=(0,1)$ gives $g$ values $(0,0), (1,1), (0,1), (1,0)$
— all distinct. ✓ (both even)
- $(n,m) = (1,3)$: $f = \text{id}$ gives $g(x) = 2x \pmod{3}$, a permutation sin
ce $\gcd(2,3)=1$. ✓ (both odd)

**Necessity for the cyclic case (parity obstruction).** When exactly one of $n,
m$ is even, $|G| = nm$ is even and $G$ has a non-trivial cyclic Sylow 2-subgroup
. For any permutation $f$, $\sum_{v \in G} g(v) = \sum v + \sum f(v) = 2\sum v$.
 If $g$ were a permutation, $\sum g(v) = \sum v$, requiring $\sum v \equiv 0$ in
 $G$. But the unique element of order 2 in the cyclic Sylow 2-subgroup creates a
 parity obstruction (the sum over $G$ equals this element of order 2, which is n
onzero), contradicting the requirement. Hence no complete mapping exists.

**Conclusion.** $(n, m) \in S$ if and only if $n$ and $m$ have the **same parity
** (both odd or both even).

**Counting.** In $\{1, 2, \ldots, 100\}$, there are 50 odd and 50 even numbers.

$$|S| = \underbrace{50 \times 50}_{\text{both odd}} + \underbrace{50 \times 50}_
{\text{both even}} = 2500 + 2500 = \boxed{5000}$$
