## Solution

**Setup.** The force on the mass is attractive, directed towards the center, wit
h magnitude $\frac{mk^2}{r^3}$. Taking the radial coordinate $r$ measured outwar
d from the center, the equation of motion is

$$m\ddot{r} = -\frac{mk^2}{r^3}, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \ddot{r} = -\frac{k^2}{
r^3}.$$

The point starts at $r=a$ from rest, so the initial conditions are $r(0)=a$, $\d
ot r(0)=0$.

**Energy.** The potential is
$$V(r) = -\int F\,dr = -\int\!\left(-\frac{mk^2}{r^3}\right)dr = -\frac{mk^2}{2r
^2}.$$
Conservation of energy gives
$$\tfrac12 m\dot r^{\,2} - \frac{mk^2}{2r^2} = E.$$
Evaluating at $t=0$ (where $\dot r=0$, $r=a$):
$$E = -\frac{mk^2}{2a^2}.$$

**Velocity as a function of $r$.** Substituting $E$:
$$\tfrac12 m\dot r^{\,2} = \frac{mk^2}{2}\!\left(\frac{1}{r^2}-\frac{1}{a^2}\rig
ht),$$
$$\dot r^{\,2} = k^2\!\left(\frac{1}{r^2}-\frac{1}{a^2}\right).$$
Since the particle moves inward, $\dot r < 0$, hence
$$\dot r = -k\sqrt{\frac{1}{r^2}-\frac{1}{a^2}} = -\frac{k}{ra}\sqrt{a^2-r^2}.$$

**Time to reach the center.** Separating variables,
$$dt = -\frac{dr}{\dot r} = \frac{ra\,dr}{k\sqrt{a^2-r^2}}.$$
Integrating from $r=a$ down to $r=0$:
$$T = \int_{0}^{a}\frac{ra\,dr}{k\sqrt{a^2-r^2}} = \frac{a}{k}\int_{0}^{a}\frac{
r\,dr}{\sqrt{a^2-r^2}}.$$
With the substitution $u = a^2-r^2$, $du=-2r\,dr$:
$$\int_{0}^{a}\frac{r\,dr}{\sqrt{a^2-r^2}} = \int_{0}^{a^2}\frac{du}{2\sqrt{u}}
= \Big[\sqrt{u}\Big]_{0}^{a^2} = a.$$

Therefore
$$\boxed{T = \frac{a^2}{k}}.$$
