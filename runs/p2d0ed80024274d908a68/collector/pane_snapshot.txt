## Proof

Let $b = a^p$. Since $a$ is a $p$th root of an element of $L$, we have $b = a^p
\in L \subseteq E$. Hence $p(x) = x^p - b \in E[x]$.

**Claim:** $p(x) = x^p - b$ is irreducible in $E[x]$.

Since $\operatorname{char} E = p$, we have $p'(x) = px^{p-1} = 0$, so $p(x)$ is
inseparable. Over the extension $E(a)$ (where $a^p = b$), we have $x^p - b = (x-
a)^p$.

Suppose for contradiction that $p(x)$ is reducible in $E[x]$: write $p(x) = f(x)
\,h(x)$ with $f \in E[x]$ monic of degree $d$, $1 \le d \le p-1$. Over $E(a)$, t
he only root of $p(x)$ is $a$ (with multiplicity $p$), so $f(x) = (x-a)^d$ in $E
(a)[x]$.

The coefficient of $x^{d-1}$ in $(x-a)^d$ is $-da$. Since $f \in E[x]$, this coe
fficient lies in $E$, so $da \in E$. But $1 \le d \le p-1$ and $\operatorname{ch
ar} E = p$, so $d \not\equiv 0 \pmod{p}$, meaning $d$ is a unit in $E$. Therefor
e $a \in E$, contradicting the hypothesis $a \notin E$.

Hence $p(x)$ is irreducible in $E[x]$. $\square$

**Conclusion.** Since $E$ is a field, $E[x]$ is a principal ideal domain, so eve
ry irreducible element is prime. The hypothesis $p(x) \mid g(x)^n$ (with $n \ge
1$) together with $p(x)$ prime yields

$$p(x) \mid g(x).$$

**Answer: Yes**, $p(x)$ divides $g(x)$.
