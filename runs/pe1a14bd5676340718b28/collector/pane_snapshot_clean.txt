## Proof

Let $M$ and $M'$ be compact connected oriented $n$-manifolds with nonempty bound
ary, and let $f:(M,\partial M)\to(M',\partial M')$ be a continuous map of pairs
such that $f|_{\partial M}:\partial M\to\partial M'$ is a homeomorphism. We prov
e $\deg(f)=\pm 1$.

### Step 1: Homological setup

Since $M$ and $M'$ are compact connected oriented $n$-manifolds with nonempty bo
undary:

- $H_n(M,\partial M)\cong\mathbb{Z}$, generated by the relative fundamental clas
s $[M,\partial M]$.
- $H_n(M',\partial M')\cong\mathbb{Z}$, generated by $[M',\partial M']$.
- $H_n(M)=0$ and $H_n(M')=0$.

The vanishing $H_n(M)=0$ follows from Poincaré–Lefschetz duality: $H_n(M)\cong H
^0(M,\partial M)$. The long exact sequence in cohomology gives $0\to H^0(M,\part
ial M)\to H^0(M)\xrightarrow{i^*} H^0(\partial M)$. Since $M$ is connected, $H^0
(M)\cong\mathbb{Z}$, and since $\partial M\neq\emptyset$, the restriction $i^*$
is injective. Hence $H^0(M,\partial M)=0$, so $H_n(M)=0$. Likewise $H_n(M')=0$.

The **degree** of $f$ is the integer $d=\deg(f)$ defined by
$$f_*([M,\partial M])=d\cdot[M',\partial M'].$$

### Step 2: Injectivity of the boundary maps

The long exact sequences of the pairs give
$$H_n(M)\to H_n(M,\partial M)\xrightarrow{\partial_M}H_{n-1}(\partial M)\to H_{n
-1}(M),$$
$$H_n(M')\to H_n(M',\partial M')\xrightarrow{\partial_{M'}}H_{n-1}(\partial M')\
to H_{n-1}(M').$$

Since $H_n(M)=0$ and $H_n(M')=0$, the boundary maps $\partial_M$ and $\partial_{
M'}$ are **injective**.

A standard property of the fundamental class states that the boundary of the rel
ative fundamental class is the fundamental class of the boundary (with induced o
rientation):
$$\partial_M([M,\partial M])=[\partial M],\qquad \partial_{M'}([M',\partial M'])
=[\partial M'].$$

### Step 3: Naturality

Since $f$ is a map of pairs, naturality of the long exact sequence yields the co
mmutative diagram:
$$\begin{array}{ccc}
H_n(M,\partial M) & \xrightarrow{\partial_M} & H_{n-1}(\partial M)\\[4pt]
\downarrow{\scriptstyle f_*} & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle (f|_{\partial M})_*}\\[
4pt]
H_n(M',\partial M') & \xrightarrow{\partial_{M'}} & H_{n-1}(\partial M')
\end{array}$$

Applying this to $[M,\partial M]$:
$$\partial_{M'}\bigl(f_*([M,\partial M])\bigr)=(f|_{\partial M})_*\bigl(\partial
_M([M,\partial M])\bigr)=(f|_{\partial M})_*([\partial M]).$$

The left side equals $\partial_{M'}(d\cdot[M',\partial M'])=d\cdot[\partial M']$
. Therefore:
$$\boxed{d\cdot[\partial M']=(f|_{\partial M})_*([\partial M]).}\tag{$\star$}$$

### Step 4: Computing the right-hand side

Write the decompositions into connected components:
$$\partial M=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{k}\partial M_i,\qquad \partial M'=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^
{k}\partial M'_j,$$
where the counts agree since $f|_{\partial M}$ is a homeomorphism. The homeomorp
hism $f|_{\partial M}$ sends each $\partial M_i$ homeomorphically onto $\partial
 M'_{\sigma(i)}$ for some permutation $\sigma\in S_k$.

Now $H_{n-1}(\partial M')\cong\mathbb{Z}^k$ with basis $\{[\partial M'_j]\}_{j=1
}^k$ (the fundamental classes of the components, since each $\partial M'_j$ is a
 closed oriented $(n-1)$-manifold). Each restriction $f|_{\partial M_i}:\partial
 M_i\to\partial M'_{\sigma(i)}$ is a homeomorphism of oriented closed $(n-1)$-ma
nifolds, hence has degree $\epsilon_i\in\{+1,-1\}$. Therefore:
$$(f|_{\partial M})_*([\partial M])=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\epsilon_i\cdot[\partial M'_{\
sigma(i)}].\tag{$\star\star$}$$

### Step 5: Comparing coefficients

Combining $(\star)$ and $(\star\star)$:
$$d\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{k}[\partial M'_j]=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\epsilon_i\cdot[\partial M'_
{\sigma(i)}].$$

Since $\{[\partial M'_j]\}$ is a basis of the free abelian group $H_{n-1}(\parti
al M')$, we compare the coefficient of each basis element $[\partial M'_j]$. On
the left, the coefficient of $[\partial M'_j]$ is $d$. On the right, the coeffic
ient of $[\partial M'_j]$ is $\epsilon_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}$. Therefore:
$$d=\epsilon_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}\quad\text{for every }j=1,\dots,k.$$

Since each $\epsilon_i\in\{+1,-1\}$, we conclude:
$$\deg(f)=d=\pm 1.$$

**Remark.** The argument also shows that all the signs $\epsilon_i$ coincide (ea
ch equals $d$), so $f|_{\partial M}$ either preserves orientation on every bound
ary component or reverses it on every boundary component. $\blacksquare$
