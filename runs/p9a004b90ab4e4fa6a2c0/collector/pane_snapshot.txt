## Proof

We wish to evaluate
$$L=\lim_{x\to\infty}\int_0^x \cos\!\left(\frac{\pi t^2}{2}\right)dt=\int_0^\inf
ty \cos\!\left(\frac{\pi t^2}{2}\right)dt.$$

**Step 1: Reduction to a Gaussian-type integral.** Define
$$I=\int_0^\infty e^{i\pi t^2/2}\,dt,$$
so that $L=\Re(I)$. Substitute $u=t\sqrt{\pi/2}$, i.e. $t=u\sqrt{2/\pi}$, $dt=\s
qrt{2/\pi}\,du$, and $\pi t^2/2=u^2$:
$$I=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\int_0^\infty e^{iu^2}\,du.$$

**Step 2: Contour evaluation of $\int_0^\infty e^{iu^2}du$.** Consider the entir
e function $f(z)=e^{iz^2}$ and the closed contour $\Gamma_R$ consisting of:
- $\gamma_1$: the segment $[0,R]$ on the real axis,
- $\gamma_2$: the circular arc $z=Re^{i\theta}$, $0\le\theta\le\pi/4$,
- $\gamma_3$: the segment from $Re^{i\pi/4}$ back to $0$, parametrized as $z=re^
{i\pi/4}$, $r:R\to 0$.

By Cauchy's theorem, $\oint_{\Gamma_R}e^{iz^2}dz=0$.

**The arc $\gamma_2$ vanishes as $R\to\infty$.** On the arc, $z=Re^{i\theta}$, s
o
$$\Re(iz^2)=\Re(iR^2 e^{2i\theta})=R^2\Re(i\cos 2\theta-\sin 2\theta)=-R^2\sin 2
\theta.$$
For $\theta\in[0,\pi/4]$, $\sin 2\theta\ge \frac{4\theta}{\pi}$ (since $\sin u\g
e 2u/\pi$ for $u\in[0,\pi/2]$), hence
$$|e^{iz^2}|=e^{-R^2\sin 2\theta}\le e^{-4R^2\theta/\pi}.$$
Therefore
$$\left|\int_{\gamma_2}e^{iz^2}dz\right|\le R\int_0^{\pi/4}e^{-4R^2\theta/\pi}d\
theta=R\cdot\frac{\pi}{4R^2}(1-e^{-R^2})\to 0.$$

**Step 3: Taking the limit.** As $R\to\infty$,
$$\int_0^\infty e^{ix^2}dx+\int_{\gamma_3}e^{iz^2}dz=0.$$
On $\gamma_3$ (traversed from $Re^{i\pi/4}$ to $0$), $z=re^{i\pi/4}$, $dz=e^{i\p
i/4}dr$ (with $r$ decreasing; reversing orientation):
$$\int_{\gamma_3}e^{iz^2}dz=-e^{i\pi/4}\int_0^\infty e^{i(re^{i\pi/4})^2}dr=-e^{
i\pi/4}\int_0^\infty e^{ir^2 e^{i\pi/2}}dr=-e^{i\pi/4}\int_0^\infty e^{-r^2}dr.$
$
Using the classical Gaussian value $\int_0^\infty e^{-r^2}dr=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}$
:
$$\int_0^\infty e^{ix^2}dx=e^{i\pi/4}\cdot\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}.$$

**Step 4: Conclude.** Substituting back,
$$I=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\cdot e^{i\pi/4}\cdot\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}=\frac{\sqrt 2}{2
}\,e^{i\pi/4}=\frac{\sqrt 2}{2}\cdot\frac{1+i}{\sqrt 2}=\frac{1+i}{2}.$$
Hence
$$L=\Re(I)=\boxed{\tfrac12}.$$

*Remark on existence of the limit.* The convergence of $\int_0^\infty e^{i\pi t^
2/2}dt$ (as an improper Riemann integral) follows from the vanishing-arc estimat
e above together with the Dirichlet-type oscillation: integrating by parts on $[
A,B]$ gives $\left|\int_A^B e^{i\pi t^2/2}dt\right|\le \frac{2}{\pi A}+O(A^{-3})
$, which tends to $0$ as $A\to\infty$, so the Cauchy criterion is satisfied and
the limit exists.
