**Claim:** The mapping $r \mapsto M_r$ is **strictly increasing**; that is, for
$0 \le r < R$, we have $M_r < M_R$.

**Proof.**

Let $f$ be a non-constant entire function, and let $0 \le r < R$. Since $f$ is e
ntire, it is holomorphic on the closed disk $\overline{D}_R = \{z : |z| \le R\}$
.

**Step 1: The maximum on $\overline{D}_R$ is $M_R$.**

By the **maximum modulus principle**, a non-constant holomorphic function on a b
ounded domain does not attain its maximum modulus in the interior. Since $f$ is
holomorphic on the open disk $D_R = \{|z| < R\}$ and continuous on the closure $
\overline{D}_R$, the maximum of $|f|$ over $\overline{D}_R$ is attained on the b
oundary $|z| = R$. Hence

$$\max_{|z| \le R} |f(z)| = M_R.$$

**Step 2: $M_r \le M_R$.**

The circle $|z| = r$ is contained in $\overline{D}_R$, so $M_r \le \max_{|z|\le
R}|f(z)| = M_R$.

**Step 3: Equality $M_r = M_R$ is impossible (strictness).**

Suppose for contradiction that $M_r = M_R$. Then there exists a point $z_0$ with
 $|z_0| = r$ such that

$$|f(z_0)| = M_r = M_R = \max_{|z| \le R} |f(z)|.$$

Since $r < R$, the point $z_0$ lies in the **interior** of the disk $\overline{D
}_R$ (i.e., $|z_0| < R$). Thus $|f|$ attains its maximum over $\overline{D}_R$ a
t an interior point. By the maximum modulus principle, $f$ must be **constant**
on $D_R$.

Since $f$ is constant on the open disk $D_R$ and $f$ is entire (holomorphic on a
ll of $\mathbb{C}$), the **identity theorem** (uniqueness of analytic continuati
on) implies $f$ is constant on all of $\mathbb{C}$.

This contradicts the hypothesis that $f$ is non-constant. Therefore $M_r \ne M_R
$, and combined with Step 2 we conclude

$$M_r < M_R \quad \text{whenever } 0 \le r < R.$$

**Conclusion.** The mapping $r \mapsto M_r$ is **strictly increasing** on $[0, \
infty)$ for every non-constant entire function $f$.
