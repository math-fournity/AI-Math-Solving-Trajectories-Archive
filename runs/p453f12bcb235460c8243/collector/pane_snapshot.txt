Proof

Answer: Yes. The ring of regular functions $k[X]$ of an affine variety $X$ over
an algebraically closed field $k$ always has a countable basis as a $k$-vector
space.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Setup. Since $X$ is an affine variety over $k$, by definition $X$ is an affine
variety of finite type over $k$: there exists a closed embedding $X
\hookrightarrow \mathbb{A}^n_k$ for some finite $n$, and the ring of regular
functions is $$k[X] \cong k[x_1, \dots, x_n]/I(X),$$ where $I(X) \subset k[x_1,
\dots,x_n]$ is the ideal of $X$.

Step 1: $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ has a countable $k$-basis. The set of monomials $$
\mathcal{M} = \left{ x_1^{a_1} x_2^{a_2} \cdots x_n^{a_n} : (a_1,\dots,a_n) \in
\mathbb{N}^n \right}$$ forms a $k$-vector space basis of $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$. The
index set $\mathbb{N}^n$ is a finite Cartesian product of countable sets, hence
countable. Therefore $\mathcal{M}$ is a countable basis.

Step 2: A quotient of a countably-dimensional vector space is at most countably-
dimensional. Let $V$ be a $k$-vector space with a countable basis ${e_i}{i \in
\mathbb{N}}$, and let $W \subseteq V$ be a subspace. The quotient $V/W$ is
generated (as a $k$-vector space) by the images ${\bar e_i}{i\in\mathbb{N}}$ of
the basis elements under the quotient map. Hence $V/W$ admits a generating set
of cardinality at most countable. Extracting a maximal linearly independent
subset from this generating set (Zorn's lemma / standard basis extraction)
yields a basis of $V/W$ of cardinality at most countable.

Step 3: Apply to $k[X]$. Taking $V = k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ (countably-dimensional
over $k$ by Step 1) and $W = I(X)$, we obtain $$k[X] = V/W,$$ which by Step 2
has a $k$-basis of cardinality at most countable. $\square$

Remark. The argument uses only that $X$ is of finite type over $k$ (so that
$k[X]$ is a quotient of a polynomial ring in finitely many variables). The
cardinality of the ground field $k$ itself is irrelevant: whether $k$ is
countable (e.g., $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$) or uncountable (e.g., $\mathbb{C}$),
the monomial basis of $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ is indexed by $\mathbb{N}^n$, which is
countable, and the quotient cannot increase the dimension beyond countable.
