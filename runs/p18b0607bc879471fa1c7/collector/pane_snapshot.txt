## Solution

**Setup.** Recall the general construction of the free category $\mathbf{Free}(G
)$ generated by a directed graph $G$:

- **Objects** are the vertices of $G$.
- **Morphisms** from $x$ to $y$ are finite *paths* from $x$ to $y$, i.e. sequenc
es $e_1 e_2 \cdots e_n$ of edges with $\mathrm{src}(e_1)=x$, $\mathrm{tgt}(e_n)=
y$, and $\mathrm{tgt}(e_i)=\mathrm{src}(e_{i+1})$ for $i=1,\dots,n-1$. For each
vertex $x$ there is also the *empty path* $\mathrm{id}_x$ (a path of length $0$)
.
- **Composition** of paths is concatenation: $(e_1\cdots e_n)\circ(f_1\cdots f_m
)=f_1\cdots f_m\, e_1\cdots e_n$ when the target of the first matches the source
 of the second.
- **Identities** are the empty paths, which act as units under concatenation.

The universal property: any graph homomorphism from $G$ into the underlying grap
h of a category $\mathcal C$ extends uniquely to a functor $\mathbf{Free}(G)\to\
mathcal C$.

**The specific graph.** Let $G$ be the digraph with
- one vertex, call it $\star$;
- two distinct loop edges $a:\star\to\star$ and $b:\star\to\star$.

**Objects.** Since $G$ has a single vertex, $\mathbf{Free}(G)$ has a single obje
ct, namely $\star$.

**Morphisms.** A path in $G$ is a finite sequence of edges $e_1 e_2\cdots e_n$ w
here each $e_i\in\{a,b\}$ and the composability condition $\mathrm{tgt}(e_i)=\ma
thrm{src}(e_{i+1})$ holds. Because every edge is a loop on $\star$, we have $\ma
thrm{tgt}(e_i)=\star=\mathrm{src}(e_{i+1})$ automatically for all $i$. Hence **e
very** finite word over the alphabet $\{a,b\}$ is a valid path, and conversely e
very path is such a word. Including the empty path $\varepsilon$ (the identity a
t $\star$), the hom-set is

$$\mathrm{Hom}(\star,\star)=\{a,b\}^{*}=\bigl\{\varepsilon,\; a,\; b,\; aa,\; ab
,\; ba,\; bb,\; aaa,\; aab,\;\dots\bigr\},$$

the set of all finite words (including the empty word) on two letters.

**Composition.** With only one object, every pair of morphisms is composable. Co
mposition is concatenation of words:

$$w\circ v = v\,w \qquad\text{(or }w\,v\text{, depending on diagrammatic order c
onvention),}$$

i.e. the word obtained by writing the two words one after another. This is assoc
iative because concatenation of strings is associative, and $\varepsilon$ is a t
wo-sided unit because concatenating with the empty word changes nothing.

**Structure.** Thus $\mathbf{Free}(G)$ is a **one-object category** whose single
 hom-set $\{a,b\}^{*}$ forms a monoid under concatenation — specifically the **f
ree monoid on two generators** $a$ and $b$. Equivalently, it is the free categor
y on one object and two generating endomorphisms.

**Universal property (concretized).** For any category $\mathcal C$ and any choi
ce of an object $X\in\mathcal C$ together with two endomorphisms $f,g\in\mathrm{
End}_{\mathcal C}(X)$, there is a unique functor $F:\mathbf{Free}(G)\to\mathcal
C$ with $F(\star)=X$, $F(a)=f$, $F(b)=g$. Concretely, $F$ sends a word $w=e_1\cd
ots e_n$ (each $e_i\in\{a,b\}$) to the composite $F(e_1)\circ\cdots\circ F(e_n)$
, i.e. to the corresponding product of $f$'s and $g$'s in $\mathrm{End}_{\mathca
l C}(X)$, and sends $\varepsilon$ to $\mathrm{id}_X$. Uniqueness follows because
 every morphism in $\mathbf{Free}(G)$ is a word in $a,b$, so a functor is determ
ined by its values on the generators $a$ and $b$.

**Summary.** The free category generated by the digraph with one vertex and two
distinct loops is the **free monoid on two generators, regarded as a one-object
category**: it has a single object $\star$; its morphisms are all finite words o
ver the two-letter alphabet $\{a,b\}$ (including the empty word $\varepsilon$ as
 the identity); composition is word concatenation; and it satisfies the universa
l property that choosing any object with two endomorphisms in any category deter
mines a unique functor out of it.
