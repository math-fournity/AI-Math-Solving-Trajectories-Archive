## Solution

The formula is **true**.

**Justification.**

The key observation is a pointwise identity on the inner integral. For any two r
eal numbers $t, s$, the set $A(t,s)$ is by definition the closed interval with e
ndpoints $t$ and $s$:

$$A(t,s) = \begin{cases} [t,s] & t\le s \\ [s,t] & s\le t \end{cases} \;=\; [\mi
n(t,s),\,\max(t,s)].$$

Its Lebesgue measure is the length of this interval, namely $|t-s|$. Hence, for
every $t,s\in\mathbb{R}$,

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{1}_{A(t,s)}(x)\,dx \;=\; \lambda\bigl(A(t,s)\bigr) \
;=\; |t-s|. \tag{1}$$

Now set $t = g(u)$ and $s = g(v)$. Since $g$ is continuous (hence Borel measurab
le), the map $(u,x)\mapsto \mathbb{1}_{A(g(u),g(v))}(x)$ is jointly measurable w
ith respect to the product of the measure induced by $F$ and Lebesgue measure. M
oreover the integrand is **nonnegative**, so Tonelli's theorem applies and allow
s us to evaluate the iterated integral by computing the inner integral first:

$$\int\!\int_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{1}_{A(g(u),g(v))}(x)\,dx\,dF(u) \;=\; \int \Bi
gl(\int_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{1}_{A(g(u),g(v))}(x)\,dx\Bigr) dF(u).$$

Applying identity (1) with $t=g(u),\, s=g(v)$ to the inner integral gives

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{1}_{A(g(u),g(v))}(x)\,dx \;=\; |g(u)-g(v)|,$$

and therefore

$$\int\!\int_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{1}_{A(g(u),g(v))}(x)\,dx\,dF(u) \;=\; \int |g(
u)-g(v)|\,dF(u),$$

which is exactly the left-hand side. Both sides are equal as functions of the fr
ee parameter $v$ (and may take the value $+\infty$ simultaneously, in which case
 equality still holds in $[0,+\infty]$).

**Remark on the role of continuity.** The pointwise identity (1) holds for *any*
 real $t,s$ regardless of $g$. The continuity of $g$ is used to guarantee joint
measurability of the integrand, so that Tonelli's theorem justifies exchanging/c
omputing the iterated integral. Without such a measurability hypothesis the oute
r integral might not even be well-defined.
