## Proof

Mona has 12 unit matchsticks. Each regular polygon has $n$ sides ($n \ge 3$) and
 each side is built from $s$ matchsticks laid end-to-end ($s \ge 1$ integer), so
 a polygon uses $m = ns$ matchsticks. A matchstick is either a whole side ($s=1$
) or a fraction of a side ($s \ge 2$). Different polygons cannot share matchstic
ks (overlapping is forbidden), so the matchsticks are partitioned among the poly
gons with $\sum m_i = 12$.

**Area formula.** A regular $n$-gon with side length $s$ has area
$$A = \frac{n}{4}\,s^2 \cot\frac{\pi}{n} = \frac{m^2}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{n}\cot\fra
c{\pi}{n}.$$

**Efficiency factor is minimized at the triangle.** Define $f(n) = \frac{1}{n}\c
ot\frac{\pi}{n}$ for $n \ge 3$. Setting $x = \pi/n \in (0, \pi/3]$, we have $f =
 \frac{x}{\pi}\cot x$. Since $\frac{d}{dx}(x\cot x) = \cot x - x\csc^2 x < 0$ on
 $(0,\pi/3]$ (e.g. $\cot(\pi/3)-(\pi/3)\csc^2(\pi/3) = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}-\tfrac{
\pi}{3}\cdot\tfrac{4}{3} < 0$), $f$ is **increasing** in $n$. Hence $f(n) \ge f(
3) = \frac{1}{3\sqrt3}$ for every $n \ge 3$, with equality only at $n=3$.

**Lower bound on each polygon's area.** For any polygon using $m$ matchsticks,
$$A \ge \frac{m^2}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{3\sqrt3} = \frac{m^2}{12\sqrt3},$$
with equality iff the polygon is an equilateral triangle ($n=3$).

**Minimizing the sum of squares.** For a partition $m_1+\dots+m_k = 12$ with eac
h $m_i \ge 3$,
$$\text{Total area} \ge \frac{1}{12\sqrt3}\sum_{i=1}^k m_i^2.$$
To minimize $\sum m_i^2$ subject to $\sum m_i = 12$ and $m_i \ge 3$: the sum of
squares is minimized when the parts are as small and as equal as possible. Since
 each $m_i \ge 3$ and $12 = 4\cdot 3$, the unique minimizer is $m_1=m_2=m_3=m_4=
3$, giving $\sum m_i^2 = 4\cdot 9 = 36$. Any other partition (e.g. $3{+}3{+}6$:
$54$; $4{+}4{+}4$: $48$; $3{+}4{+}5$: $50$; $6{+}6$: $72$; a single $12$-gon: $1
44$) yields a strictly larger sum of squares.

Therefore
$$\text{Total area} \ge \frac{36}{12\sqrt3} = \frac{3}{\sqrt3} = \sqrt3.$$

**Achievability.** Four equilateral triangles of side $1$, each using $3$ matchs
ticks (total $12$), placed disjointly, have total area
$$4 \cdot \frac{\sqrt3}{4}\cdot 1^2 = \sqrt3.$$
This matches the lower bound, so it is optimal.
