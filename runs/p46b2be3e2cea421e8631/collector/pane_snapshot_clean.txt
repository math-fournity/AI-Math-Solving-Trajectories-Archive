## Solution

Let $g(z) = f(z) - az - b$. Then $g$ is entire and $g''(z) = f''(z)$, so $\opera
torname{Re}(g''(z)) > 0$ for all $z \in \mathbb{C}$. The number of solutions to
$f(z) = az + b$ equals the number of zeros of $g$. Thus the problem reduces to:

> What is the maximum number of zeros of an entire function $g$ with $\operatorn
ame{Re}(g''(z)) > 0$ everywhere?

**Step 1: $g''$ is constant.**

Set $h = g''$. Then $h$ is entire and $\operatorname{Re}(h(z)) > 0$ for all $z$.
 The Möbius map
$$\phi(w) = \frac{w - 1}{w + 1}$$
sends the right half-plane $\{w : \operatorname{Re}(w) > 0\}$ biholomorphically
onto the open unit disk. Since $\operatorname{Re}(h(z)) > 0$, we have $h(z) + 1
\neq 0$ (as $\operatorname{Re}(h(z)+1) > 1$), so $\phi \circ h$ is a well-define
d entire function with $|\phi(h(z))| < 1$ for all $z$. By Liouville's theorem, $
\phi \circ h$ is constant, hence $h = g''$ is constant, say $g''(z) = c$ with $\
operatorname{Re}(c) > 0$.

**Step 2: $g$ is a quadratic.**

Integrating twice, $g(z) = \tfrac{c}{2}z^2 + \alpha z + \beta$ for some $\alpha,
 \beta \in \mathbb{C}$. Since $\operatorname{Re}(c) > 0$, in particular $c \neq
0$, so $g$ is a genuine quadratic polynomial. A quadratic has at most **2** zero
s (counting multiplicity), and at most 2 distinct zeros.

**Step 3: The bound 2 is attained.**

Take $c = 1$ (so $\operatorname{Re}(c) = 1 > 0$) and $g(z) = \tfrac{1}{2}z^2 - \
tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{1}{2}(z-1)(z+1)$, with two distinct zeros $z = \pm 1$. Sett
ing $f(z) = g(z) + az + b$ for any $a, b \in \mathbb{C}$ gives a holomorphic fun
ction with $\operatorname{Re}(f''(z)) = \operatorname{Re}(g''(z)) = 1 > 0$ and $
f(z) = az + b$ having exactly two solutions.

**Conclusion.** The maximum number of solutions is $\boxed{2}$.
