Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum number of minutes for Benoît
 to identify all bulb-switch pair correspondences.

**Setup.** Let $n = 2023$. There are $\binom{n}{2} = n \cdot 1011$ bulbs, one pe
r pair of switches. Each bulb $\{i,j\}$ lights up iff both switches $i,j$ are on
. Benoît controls the switches (he knows which is which) but doesn't know the bu
lb labels. He needs to match each bulb to its pair.

**Key reformulation.** Benoît knows the full state sequence (he controls it). Fo
r each bulb, he observes its on/off pattern over time — a binary vector. The bul
b for pair $\{i,j\}$ has signature $\mathbf{a}_i \wedge \mathbf{a}_j$ (component
wise AND of the two switch sequences). Benoît succeeds iff all $\binom{n}{2}$ si
gnatures are distinct, since he can then match each observed signature to the un
ique pair producing it.

---

**Upper bound: $T = 2n - 2$ suffices.**

*Strategy.* Turn on switches $1, 2, \ldots, n$ (steps $1$ to $n$), then turn off
 switches $1, 2, \ldots, n{-}2$ (steps $n{+}1$ to $2n{-}2$). Total: $2n - 2$ ste
ps.

Switch $i$ (for $1 \le i \le n{-}2$) is on during $[i,\; n{+}i{-}1]$. Switches $
n{-}1$ and $n$ are on during $[n{-}1,\; 2n{-}2]$ and $[n,\; 2n{-}2]$ respectivel
y (never turned off).

The signature of each pair is the interval where both are on:

| Pair type | Signature interval |
|---|---|
| $\{i,j\},\; 1 \le i < j \le n{-}2$ | $[j,\; n{+}i{-}1]$ |
| $\{i, n{-}1\},\; 1 \le i \le n{-}2$ | $[n{-}1,\; n{+}i{-}1]$ |
| $\{i, n\},\; 1 \le i \le n{-}2$ | $[n,\; n{+}i{-}1]$ |
| $\{n{-}1, n\}$ | $[n,\; 2n{-}2]$ |

All intervals are valid (start $\le$ end) and **pairwise distinct**: the four ty
pes have distinct starting points ($\le n{-}2$, $n{-}1$, $n$, $n$) with the last
 two distinguished by their endpoints ($\le 2n{-}3$ vs. $2n{-}2$), and within ea
ch type the pair $(i,j)$ is uniquely recovered. So all $\binom{n}{2}$ signatures
 are distinct. ✓

---

**Lower bound: $T \ge 2n - 2$ is necessary.**

Call a switch *persistent* if, once first turned on, it is never turned off (its
 sequence is $0\cdots0\,1\cdots1$).

**Claim:** At most $2$ switches can be persistent.

*Proof.* Suppose $3$ switches $a, b, c$ are persistent, with first-on times $f_a
 \le f_b \le f_c$. For any time $t < f_c$: switch $c$ is off, so both pairs $\{a
,c\}$ and $\{b,c\}$ are off. For any time $t \ge f_c$: all three are on (each wa
s turned on by time $f_c$ and never turned off), so both $\{a,c\}$ and $\{b,c\}$
 are on. Therefore $\{a,c\}$ and $\{b,c\}$ have **identical** signatures — contr
adiction. $\square$

**Every switch must be turned on** at least once: if switch $i$ is never on, all
 $n{-}1 \ge 2$ pairs involving $i$ have the empty signature, making them indisti
nguishable.

**Counting toggles.** At most $2$ switches are persistent (each needs $\ge 1$ to
ggle). At least $n{-}2$ switches are non-persistent — turned off at some point —
 so each needs $\ge 2$ toggles (on then off). Therefore:

$$T \ge 2(n-2) + 2 = 2n - 2.$$

**Conclusion.** The upper and lower bounds match:

$$\boxed{T = 2n - 2 = 2 \times 2023 - 2 = 4044}.$$
