## Proof

**Step 1: Power series analysis (motivation).**

Suppose $D(t) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n t^n$ is analytic at $0$. Then $D'(t) = \su
m (n+1)a_{n+1}t^n$ and $D(2t) = \sum a_n 2^n t^n$. Substituting:

$$(n+1)a_{n+1} = (2^{n+1}-1)a_n, \quad a_0 = D(0) = 0.$$

This forces $a_n = 0$ for all $n$. So the only **analytic** solution is $D \equi
v 0$. Nontrivial solutions (if they exist) must be non-analytic at $0$.

**Step 2: Ansatz as a series of exponentials.**

Motivated by the Laplace transform (which yields $F(s) = F(s/2)/(s+1)$, with pol
es at $s = -2^k$), we seek solutions of the form:

$$D(t) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} c_k \, e^{-2^k t}.$$

Substituting into the equation $D'(t) = 2D(2t) - D(t)$:

- $D'(t) = -\sum_{k=0}^\infty c_k \, 2^k \, e^{-2^k t}$
- $2D(2t) = 2\sum_{k=0}^\infty c_k \, e^{-2^{k+1} t} = 2\sum_{k=1}^\infty c_{k-1
} \, e^{-2^k t}$
- $D(t) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty c_k \, e^{-2^k t}$

Matching coefficients of $e^{-2^k t}$:

- **$k=0$:** $-c_0 = -c_0$ (automatically satisfied).
- **$k \geq 1$:** $-c_k \, 2^k = 2c_{k-1} - c_k$, giving $c_k(1 - 2^k) = 2c_{k-1
}$, hence:

$$c_k = \frac{-2}{2^k - 1}\, c_{k-1}.$$

**Step 3: Explicit coefficients.**

Iterating the recurrence with $c_0 = C$ (free parameter):

$$c_k = C \cdot \frac{(-2)^k}{\prod_{m=1}^{k}(2^m - 1)},$$

where the empty product ($k=0$) equals $1$. So:

$$\boxed{D(t) = C \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-2)^k}{\prod_{m=1}^{k}(2^m - 1)} \,
 e^{-2^k t}.}$$

**Step 4: Convergence.**

Since $2^m - 1 \geq 2^{m-1}$ for $m \geq 1$, we have $\prod_{m=1}^k(2^m-1) \geq
2^{k(k-1)/2}$. Thus:

$$|c_k| \leq |C| \cdot \frac{2^k}{2^{k(k-1)/2}} = |C| \cdot 2^{-k(k-3)/2}.$$

For $k \geq 4$, the exponent $-k(k-3)/2 \to -\infty$ super-exponentially, so $|c
_k|$ decays super-exponentially. Combined with $e^{-2^k t} \leq 1$ for $t \geq 0
$, the series converges **absolutely and uniformly** on $[0, \infty)$. The same
super-exponential decay ensures that all termwise derivatives also converge unif
ormly on compact subsets of $(0,\infty)$, so $D \in C^\infty(0,\infty)$ and is c
ontinuous at $t=0$.

**Step 5: Verification of $D(0) = 0$.**

We compute the partial sums $S_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} c_k / C$ and prove by inductio
n that:

$$S_n = \frac{(-1)^n}{\prod_{m=1}^{n}(2^m - 1)}.$$

*Base case:* $S_0 = 1 = (-1)^0 / 1$. ✓

*Inductive step:*
$$S_n = S_{n-1} + \frac{(-2)^n}{\prod_{m=1}^n(2^m-1)} = \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{\prod_
{m=1}^{n-1}(2^m-1)} + \frac{(-1)^n \cdot 2^n}{\prod_{m=1}^{n-1}(2^m-1)\cdot(2^n-
1)}$$

$$= \frac{(-1)^{n-1}\bigl[(2^n-1) - 2^n\bigr]}{\prod_{m=1}^{n-1}(2^m-1)\cdot(2^n
-1)} = \frac{(-1)^{n-1}(-1)}{\prod_{m=1}^{n}(2^m-1)} = \frac{(-1)^n}{\prod_{m=1}
^{n}(2^m-1)}. \quad \checkmark$$

Since $\prod_{m=1}^n(2^m-1) \to \infty$ super-exponentially, $S_n \to 0$. Theref
ore:

$$D(0) = C \cdot \lim_{n\to\infty} S_n = 0.$$

The initial condition $D(0) = 0$ is satisfied **automatically** for every value
of $C$.

**Step 6: Verification that $D$ satisfies the ODE.**

By the uniform convergence established in Step 4, we may differentiate term-by-t
erm for $t > 0$. The coefficient matching in Step 2 then confirms $D'(t) = 2D(2t
) - D(t)$ for all $t > 0$. By continuity at $t = 0$ and the fact that $D(0) = 0$
, the equation extends to $t = 0$ as well: $D'(0^+) = 2D(0) - D(0) = 0$.

**Conclusion.**

The functional differential equation $D'(t) = 2D(2t) - D(t)$ with $D(0) = 0$ **c
an be solved in closed form**. The general solution is the one-parameter family:

$$D(t) = C \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-2)^k}{\prod_{m=1}^{k}(2^m - 1)} \, e^{-2^
k t}, \qquad C \in \mathbb{R},$$

where $C = 0$ gives the trivial solution $D \equiv 0$, and $C \neq 0$ gives nont
rivial solutions that are $C^\infty$-smooth on $(0,\infty)$, continuous at $t=0$
, flat at $t=0$ (all derivatives vanish there), and non-analytic at $t=0$. The i
nitial condition $D(0)=0$ is satisfied for all $C$ due to the telescoping identi
ty $S_n = (-1)^n/\prod_{m=1}^n(2^m-1) \to 0$.
