Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the vanishing of $\langle x
 \cup f^*y, [M]\rangle$ for all groups $G$, all $y \in H^*(BG;\mathbb{Q})$, and
all maps $f: M \to BG$ forces $x = 0$.

**Answer: Yes, $x = 0$ follows.**

**Proof.**

The key idea is to show that the collection of all pullbacks $\{f^*y\}$, ranging
 over all topological groups $G$, all $y \in H^*(BG;\mathbb{Q})$, and all contin
uous maps $f: M \to BG$, spans the entire cohomology $H^*(M;\mathbb{Q})$. Then t
he non-degeneracy of the Poincaré duality pairing forces $x = 0$.

**Step 1: Realizing any rational cohomology class as a pullback from some $BG$.*
*

Let $n = \dim M$. Fix a degree $k$ with $0 \leq k \leq n$, and let $\alpha \in H
^k(M;\mathbb{Q})$ be arbitrary. We claim there exists a topological group $G$, a
 class $y \in H^k(BG;\mathbb{Q})$, and a continuous map $f: M \to BG$ such that
$f^*y = \alpha$.

For $k = 0$: Take $G$ to be the trivial group, $BG = \mathrm{pt}$, $y = 1$, and
any $f$. Then $f^*y = 1$, which spans $H^0(M;\mathbb{Q})$ (for connected $M$; th
e general case is similar).

For $k \geq 1$: Consider the Eilenberg–Mac Lane space $K(\mathbb{Z}, k-1)$, whic
h is a topological abelian group (this is standard: $K(\mathbb{Z}, 0) = \mathbb{
Z}$ is discrete, $K(\mathbb{Z}, 1) = S^1$, and inductively $K(\mathbb{Z}, m)$ ca
rries a topological abelian group structure with $B\,K(\mathbb{Z}, m) \cong K(\m
athbb{Z}, m+1)$). Set $G = K(\mathbb{Z}, k-1)$, so $BG \simeq K(\mathbb{Z}, k)$.

The space $K(\mathbb{Z}, k)$ represents the functor $H^k(-;\mathbb{Z})$: there i
s a fundamental class $\iota_k \in H^k(K(\mathbb{Z}, k);\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathb
b{Z}$ such that the map $[M, K(\mathbb{Z}, k)] \to H^k(M;\mathbb{Z})$ given by $
f \mapsto f^*\iota_k$ is a bijection.

By the universal coefficient theorem, $H^k(M;\mathbb{Q}) \cong H^k(M;\mathbb{Z})
 \otimes \mathbb{Q}$ (since $\operatorname{Tor}(H_{k-1}(M), \mathbb{Q}) = 0$). T
hus any $\alpha \in H^k(M;\mathbb{Q})$ can be written as $\alpha = \frac{p}{q}\,
\beta$ for some $\beta \in H^k(M;\mathbb{Z})$ and integers $p, q$ with $q \neq 0
$.

Let $f: M \to K(\mathbb{Z}, k) = BG$ be the map (guaranteed by representability)
 satisfying $f^*\iota_k = \beta$. Set $y = \frac{p}{q}\,\iota_k \in H^k(BG;\math
bb{Q})$. Then:
$$f^*y = \frac{p}{q}\,f^*\iota_k = \frac{p}{q}\,\beta = \alpha.$$

**Step 2: The vanishing condition forces $x = 0$.**

Write $x = \sum_{k=0}^{n} x_k$ with $x_k \in H^k(M;\mathbb{Q})$. By Step 1, for
any degree $j$ and any $\alpha \in H^j(M;\mathbb{Q})$, there exist $G, y, f$ wit
h $f^*y = \alpha$. The hypothesis gives:
$$0 = \langle x \cup f^*y,\, [M]\rangle = \langle x \cup \alpha,\, [M]\rangle.$$

Choose $j = n - k$ and let $\alpha \in H^{n-k}(M;\mathbb{Q})$ be arbitrary. The
degree-$n$ component of $x \cup \alpha$ is $x_k \cup \alpha$ (since $x_i \cup \a
lpha$ has degree $i + (n-k) = n$ only when $i = k$). Therefore:
$$0 = \langle x \cup \alpha,\, [M]\rangle = \langle x_k \cup \alpha,\, [M]\rangl
e \quad \text{for all } \alpha \in H^{n-k}(M;\mathbb{Q}).$$

By Poincaré duality, the pairing $H^k(M;\mathbb{Q}) \times H^{n-k}(M;\mathbb{Q})
 \to \mathbb{Q}$ given by $(a, b) \mapsto \langle a \cup b, [M]\rangle$ is non-d
egenerate (since $M$ is a closed oriented manifold). Therefore $x_k = 0$.

Since this holds for every $k$, we conclude $x = \sum_k x_k = 0$. $\blacksquare$
