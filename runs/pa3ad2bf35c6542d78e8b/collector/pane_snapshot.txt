{Q}$, so $|T| = 2^{\aleph_0}$ and every finite subset of $T$ is algebraically in

$$X = \{(t, t^2) \in \mathbb{R}^2 : t \in T\}.$$

**Key fact:** Algebraic independence over $\mathbb{Q}$ implies algebraic indepen
dence over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$. *(Proof: if $f \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}[x_1
,\dots,x_n]$ is nonzero with $f(a_1,\dots,a_n)=0$, its coefficients lie in a fin
ite extension $K/\mathbb{Q}$. Writing $f = \sum e_j f_j$ with $e_j$ a $\mathbb{Q
}$-basis of $K$ and $f_j \in \mathbb{Q}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$, since $K \cap \mathbb{Q
}(a_1,\dots,a_n) = \mathbb{Q}$ (algebraic $\cap$ purely transcendental), each $f
_j(a_1,\dots,a_n)=0$, forcing $f=0$.)*

**Cardinality.** The map $t \mapsto (t,t^2)$ is injective, so $|X| = |T| = 2^{\a
leph_0}$. ✓

**No three collinear.** A line $y = mx+b$ meets the parabola $y=x^2$ where $x^2
- mx - b = 0$, a quadratic with at most 2 roots. So three distinct points on the
 parabola are never collinear, ruling out angles $0$ and $\pi$. ✓

**Angle condition.** Take distinct $A=(a,a^2), B=(b,b^2), C=(c,c^2) \in X$ with
$a,b,c \in T$ distinct. Represent as complex numbers: $\vec{BA} = (a-b)(1+i(a+b)
)$, $\vec{BC} = (c-b)(1+i(c+b))$.

The angle $\phi = \angle ABC$ satisfies: $\phi/\pi \in \mathbb{Q}$ iff $e^{2i\ph
i}$ is a root of unity. We compute (real factors cancel):
$$e^{2i\phi} = \frac{(1+i(c+b))(1-i(a+b))}{(1+i(a+b))(1-i(c+b))}.$$

Suppose $\phi/\pi \in \mathbb{Q}$, so $e^{2i\phi} = \zeta$ for some root of unit
y $\zeta \neq 1$. Setting $u=a+b, v=c+b$:
$$(1+iv)(1-iu) = \zeta(1+iu)(1-iv).$$
Expanding: $(1-\zeta)(1+uv) + i(v-u)(1+\zeta) = 0$, giving:
$$1 + (a+b)(c+b) - \cot\phi \cdot (c-a) = 0,$$
using $\frac{1+\zeta}{1-\zeta} = i\cot\phi$. Since $\phi$ is a rational multiple
 of $\pi$, $\cot\phi \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ (as $e^{i\phi}$ is a root of uni
ty, hence algebraic, so $\cos\phi, \sin\phi, \cot\phi$ are algebraic; $\cot(\pi/
2)=0$ is handled too).

This is a **nonzero** polynomial in $a,b,c$ with coefficients in $\overline{\mat
hbb{Q}}$:
$$p(x,y,z) = xz + xy + yz + y^2 - (\cot\phi)\,z + (\cot\phi)\,x + 1,$$
which has constant term $1$. Since $a,b,c$ are distinct elements of $T$, algebra

ntradiction**.

By symmetry, the same holds for angles at $A$ and $C$. ✓

**Avoiding the unit circle.** A point $(t,t^2) \in X$ lies on $S^1$ iff $t^4 + t
^2 - 1 = 0$. The roots are algebraic, but every $t \in T$ is transcendental. So
$X \cap S^1 = \emptyset$. ✓

**Conclusion.** We have exhibited $X$ with $|X| = 2^{\aleph_0}$, all angles irra

$$\boxed{n = 0}$$
