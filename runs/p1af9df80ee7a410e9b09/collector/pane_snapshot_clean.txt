**Theorem.** Every unit vector in $\mathbb{C}^d$ can be approximated arbitrarily
 well by vectors in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\,d}$ lying in the closed unit ball.
 In fact, the approximants can be chosen to have norm *exactly* $1$.

**Proof.**

Let $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ denote the field of algebraic numbers (the algebraic
 closure of $\mathbb{Q}$ in $\mathbb{C}$). We use the standard Hermitian norm $\
|z\|^2 = \sum_{i=1}^d |z_i|^2$ on $\mathbb{C}^d$.

**Step 1. $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ is dense in $\mathbb{C}$.**

$\mathbb{Q}$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}$, so $\mathbb{Q}+i\mathbb{Q}$ is dense in $
\mathbb{C}$. Every element $q+ir$ with $q,r\in\mathbb{Q}$ is algebraic, since it
 is a root of
$$x^2 - 2qx + (q^2+r^2)=0 \in \mathbb{Q}[x].$$
Hence $\mathbb{Q}+i\mathbb{Q}\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, and so $\overline{
\mathbb{Q}}$ is dense in $\mathbb{C}$. Consequently $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\,d}
$ is dense in $\mathbb{C}^d$.

**Step 2. The norm of an algebraic vector is algebraic.**

Let $w=(w_1,\dots,w_d)\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\,d}$, $w\neq 0$. For each $i$,
the complex conjugate $\overline{w_i}$ is algebraic: if $w_i$ has minimal polyno
mial $p\in\mathbb{Q}[x]$, then $p(\overline{w_i})=\overline{p(w_i)}=0$ (since $p
$ has real coefficients). Therefore $|w_i|^2 = w_i\overline{w_i}$ is algebraic (
product of algebraic numbers), and
$$\|w\|^2 = \sum_{i=1}^d |w_i|^2$$
is algebraic (sum of algebraic numbers). Since $\|w\|^2 \geq 0$ and $\|w\|^2$ is
 algebraic, $\|w\|=\sqrt{\|w\|^2}$ is a root of $x^2 - \|w\|^2 = 0$ with algebra
ic coefficients, hence $\|w\|\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$.

**Step 3. Normalization preserves algebraicity and stays in the unit ball.**

Since $\|w\|\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ and $\|w\|\neq 0$, each coordinate of
$$\widehat{w} := \frac{w}{\|w\|}$$
is a quotient of two algebraic numbers, hence algebraic. Moreover $\|\widehat{w}
\| = 1$, so $\widehat{w}$ lies in the closed unit ball.

**Step 4. Approximation.**

Let $v\in\mathbb{C}^d$ with $\|v\|=1$, and let $\varepsilon>0$. By density (Step
 1), choose $w\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\,d}$ with $\|w-v\|<\varepsilon$ and $w\
neq 0$ (possible since $v\neq 0$). Then
$$\bigl|\|w\|-1\bigr| = \bigl|\|w\|-\|v\|\bigr| \leq \|w-v\| < \varepsilon,$$
so $\|w\|\geq 1-\varepsilon > 0$ for $\varepsilon<1$.

Set $\widehat{w}=w/\|w\|$. Then $\widehat{w}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\,d}$, $\|
\widehat{w}\|=1\leq 1$, and
$$\widehat{w}-v = \frac{w-\|w\|v}{\|w\|} = \frac{(w-v)+(1-\|w\|)v}{\|w\|},$$
so
$$\|\widehat{w}-v\| \leq \frac{\|w-v\|+|1-\|w\||\cdot\|v\|}{\|w\|} < \frac{\vare
psilon+\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon} = \frac{2\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}.$$

As $\varepsilon\to 0$, this tends to $0$. Hence $\widehat{w}$ is an algebraic ve
ctor of norm $1$ (therefore in the unit ball) approximating $v$ to any desired a
ccuracy. $\blacksquare$
