## Proof

**Setup.** Normalize $L=1$ (the problem is scale-invariant). Choose $n$ points i
ndependently and uniformly on $[0,1]$. Sorting them as $x_1 \le \cdots \le x_n$
and setting $x_0=0,\; x_{n+1}=1$, the $n+1$ parts have lengths (spacings)
$$S_i = x_i - x_{i-1}, \qquad i=1,\dots,n+1, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^{n+1} S_i = 1.$$
We seek $P\!\left(\max_i S_i \le \tfrac{1}{n}\right)$.

**Step 1 — Distribution of the spacings.** The joint density of the order statis
tics $(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ is $n!$ on the simplex $0\le x_1\le\cdots\le x_n\le 1$. T
he change of variables $S_i = x_i-x_{i-1}$ is linear with Jacobian $1$, so the j
oint density of $(S_1,\dots,S_{n+1})$ on $\{S_i\ge0,\;\sum S_i=1\}$ is constant,
 i.e. the spacings are **uniform on the $n$-simplex** (Dirichlet$(1,\dots,1)$ wi
th $n+1$ components).

**Step 2 — Inclusion–exclusion (Whitworth's formula).** For spacings uniform on
the simplex summing to $1$, the probability that every spacing is $\le a$ is
$$P(\max_i S_i \le a) = \sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor 1/a\rfloor} (-1)^j \binom{n+1}{j}(1-
ja)^n,$$
where terms with $1-ja<0$ are taken as $0$.

*Derivation.* By inclusion–exclusion,
$$P(\max_i S_i \le a)=1-P\!\left(\bigcup_i\{S_i>a\}\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{n+1}(-1)^
j\binom{n+1}{j}P(S_1>a,\dots,S_j>a).$$
The event $S_1>a,\dots,S_j>a$ forces $\sum_{i=1}^j S_i > ja$. Substituting $S_i'
 = S_i - a$ for $i\le j$ (so $S_i'\ge0$) and keeping $S_i$ for $i>j$, the remain
ing variables sum to $1-ja$ and are uniform on a simplex of dimension $n-j$ (whe
n $1-ja\ge0$); the volume ratio gives probability $(1-ja)^n$. When $1-ja<0$ the
probability is $0$. This yields the formula.

**Step 3 — Substitute $a=1/n$.** Here $1/a = n$, so $\lfloor 1/a\rfloor = n$ and
 every term has $1-j/n\ge0$:
$$P = \sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^j\binom{n+1}{j}\left(1-\frac{j}{n}\right)^n = \frac{1}{
n^n}\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^j\binom{n+1}{j}(n-j)^n.$$

Set $m=n-j$ (so $j=n-m$, $m=0,\dots,n$):
$$P = \frac{1}{n^n}\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-m}\binom{n+1}{n-m}\,m^n = \frac{(-1)^n}
{n^n}\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^{m}\binom{n+1}{m+1}\,m^n,$$
using $\binom{n+1}{n-m}=\binom{n+1}{m+1}$.

**Step 4 — Evaluate the sum by a finite-difference identity.** Let $N=n+1$ and $
f(\ell)=(\ell-1)^n$, a polynomial of degree $n<N$ in $\ell$. The $N$-th alternat
ing binomial sum vanishes:
$$\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}(-1)^\ell\binom{N}{\ell}f(\ell)=0 \qquad(\deg f < N).$$
Now rewrite our sum with $\ell=m+1$:
$$\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^m\binom{n+1}{m+1}m^n = \sum_{\ell=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{\ell-1}\bin
om{n+1}{\ell}(\ell-1)^n = -\sum_{\ell=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{n+1}{\ell}(\ell-
1)^n.$$
Adding and subtracting the $\ell=0$ term:
$$-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{n+1}{\ell}(\ell-1)^n = -\Bigl[\underbrac
e{\sum_{\ell=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{n+1}{\ell}(\ell-1)^n}_{=\,0\text{ (finite
 difference)}} - (-1)^0\binom{n+1}{0}(-1)^n\Bigr] = -[\,0 - (-1)^n\,] = (-1)^n.$
$

**Step 5 — Conclude.**
$$P = \frac{(-1)^n}{n^n}\cdot(-1)^n = \frac{(-1)^{2n}}{n^n} = \frac{1}{n^n}.$$

**Verification on small cases.**
- $n=1$: one point, two parts summing to $1$; both $\le 1$ always, so $P=1=1/1^1
$. ✓
- $n=2$: the inclusion–exclusion sum gives $1-3\cdot\tfrac14=\tfrac14=1/2^2$; co
nfirmed by direct integration over the order-statistic triangle. ✓
- $n=3$: $1-4(2/3)^3+6(1/3)^3 = 1-\tfrac{32}{27}+\tfrac{6}{27}=\tfrac{1}{27}=1/3
^3$. ✓

**Answer.** The probability that every part has length $\le L/n$ is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{n^n}}.$$
