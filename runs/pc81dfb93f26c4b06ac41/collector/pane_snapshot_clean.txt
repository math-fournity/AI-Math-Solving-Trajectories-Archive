## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $\|T_n x_n\| \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$.

**Justification.** For each $n \in \mathbb{N}$, since $T_n: X_n \to Y_n$ is a bo
unded linear operator, the operator norm satisfies the defining inequality

$$\|T_n x\| \le \|T_n\|\,\|x\| \quad \text{for all } x \in X_n.$$

Applying this to $x = x_n$ and using the hypothesis $\|T_n\| \le 1$,

$$\|T_n x_n\| \le \|T_n\|\,\|x_n\| \le 1 \cdot \|x_n\| = \|x_n\|.$$

By hypothesis $\|x_n\| \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$. Since $\|T_n x_n\| \ge 0$ for a
ll $n$ and $\|T_n x_n\| \le \|x_n\|$, the squeeze theorem gives

$$\|T_n x_n\| \to 0 \quad \text{as } n \to \infty.$$

**Remark on the hypotheses.** The argument uses only:
1. The operator-norm inequality $\|T_n x_n\| \le \|T_n\|\,\|x_n\|$ (which holds
for any bounded linear operator, regardless of whether the domain is complete).
2. The uniform bound $\|T_n\| \le 1$.
3. $\|x_n\| \to 0$.

The completeness of $Y_n$ (Banach) is not needed for this conclusion; it is a st
andard standing assumption in the uniform boundedness principle context but play
s no role here. Likewise, completeness of $X_n$ is irrelevant. The key point is
that the bound on $\|T_n\|$ is *uniform* in $n$; without a uniform bound the con
clusion could fail (e.g., take $X_n = Y_n = \mathbb{R}$, $T_n = n \cdot \mathrm{
id}$, $x_n = 1/n$, giving $\|T_n x_n\| = 1 \not\to 0$). With the uniform bound $
\|T_n\| \le 1$, the conclusion is forced.
