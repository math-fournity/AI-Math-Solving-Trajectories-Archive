## Proof

The answer is **no**: we cannot conclude that $c_i \in \mathbb{Z}$.

**Counterexample.** Let $n = 2$ and
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B = \begin{pmatrix} 0
 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Both $A$ and $B$ have integer entries. Since $A$ has distinct eigenvalues $1$ an
d $3$, its characteristic polynomial equals its minimal polynomial:
$$\chi_A(x) = m_A(x) = (x-1)(x-3) = x^2 - 4x + 3.$$

Both $A$ and $B$ are diagonal, so $AB = BA$; i.e., $B$ commutes with $A$.

Because $\deg m_A = 2 = n$, the matrices $\{I, A\}$ are linearly independent ove
r $\mathbb{C}$, and every matrix commuting with $A$ has a **unique** representat
ion $B = c_0 I + c_1 A$ with $c_0, c_1 \in \mathbb{C}$. Solving entry-wise:
$$c_0 + c_1 = 0, \qquad c_0 + 3c_1 = 1,$$
gives
$$c_1 = \tfrac{1}{2}, \qquad c_0 = -\tfrac{1}{2}.$$

Thus $B = -\tfrac{1}{2} I + \tfrac{1}{2} A$, and the coefficients $c_0, c_1 \not
in \mathbb{Z}$.

**Uniqueness rules out any integer-coefficient expression.** Any expression $B =
 p(A)$ with $p \in \mathbb{C}[x]$ differs from $-\tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{x}{2}$ by
 a multiple of $m_A$, i.e.
$$p(x) \equiv -\tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{x}{2} \pmod{m_A}.$$
The class of $-\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{x}{2}$ in $\mathbb{Q}[x]/(m_A)$ is fixed, so
the degree-$<2$ part of $p$ is always $-\tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{x}{2}$, which is n
ot in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$. Concretely, the image of $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x
]/(m_A) \cong \{(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^2 : a\equiv b \pmod 2\}$ (via evaluation at $
1$ and $3$) consists of pairs with $a\equiv b\pmod 2$, but $B$ corresponds to $(
0,1)$ with $0\not\equiv 1\pmod 2$. Hence **no** integer-coefficient polynomial i
n $A$ equals $B$.

Therefore the hypothesis that $B = c_0 + c_1 A + \cdots + c_k A^k$ does **not**
force $c_i \in \mathbb{Z}$.

### PROOF COMPLETE
