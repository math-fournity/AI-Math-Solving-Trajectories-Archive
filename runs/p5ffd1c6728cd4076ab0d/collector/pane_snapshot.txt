## Solution

We evaluate $I = \int_0^1 \frac{(\log(1-x))^4}{x}\,dx$.

### Step 1: Substitution

Let $u = 1-x$, so $x = 1-u$ and $dx = -du$. The limits $x:0\to1$ become $u:1\to0
$, hence

$$I = \int_1^0 \frac{(\log u)^4}{1-u}(-du) = \int_0^1 \frac{(\log u)^4}{1-u}\,du
.$$

### Step 2: Series expansion

For $u \in [0,1)$ we use the geometric series $\frac{1}{1-u} = \sum_{k=0}^{\inft
y} u^k$. By monotone convergence (the integrand is non-negative), we may interch
ange sum and integral:

$$I = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \int_0^1 u^k (\log u)^4\,du.$$

### Step 3: Evaluate the moments

For $k \ge 0$ and integer $n \ge 0$, the standard identity is

$$\int_0^1 u^k (\log u)^n\,du = \frac{(-1)^n\, n!}{(k+1)^{n+1}},$$

obtained by substituting $u = e^{-t}$:

$$\int_0^1 u^k(\log u)^n\,du = (-1)^n\int_0^\infty t^n e^{-(k+1)t}\,dt = (-1)^n\
frac{n!}{(k+1)^{n+1}}.$$

With $n=4$:

$$\int_0^1 u^k(\log u)^4\,du = \frac{(-1)^4\cdot 4!}{(k+1)^5} = \frac{24}{(k+1)^
5}.$$

### Step 4: Sum in terms of the polylogarithm

Therefore

$$I = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{24}{(k+1)^5} = 24\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m^5
}.$$

The **polylogarithm** is defined by $\operatorname{Li}_s(z) = \sum_{m=1}^{\infty
}\frac{z^m}{m^s}$, so at $z=1$,

$$\operatorname{Li}_5(1) = \sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m^5} = \zeta(5).$$

Hence

$$\boxed{I = 24\,\operatorname{Li}_5(1) = 24\,\zeta(5).}$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
