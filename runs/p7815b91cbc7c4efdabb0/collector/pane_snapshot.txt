# Mean of the Process

## Problem

Compute $\mathbb{E}[x(t)]$ for
$$x(t) = x_0\, e^{(c - k^2/2)t}\, e^{-k W(t)},$$
where $W(t)$ is a Wiener process.

## Proof

By linearity of expectation and the fact that $x_0$, $c$, $k$, $t$ are determini
stic constants,

$$\mathbb{E}[x(t)] = x_0\, e^{(c - k^2/2)t}\, \mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{-k W(t)}\righ
t].$$

Since $W(t)$ is a Wiener process, $W(t) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, t)$, i.e. a Gaussian
 random variable with mean $0$ and variance $t$. Consequently $-k W(t) \sim \mat
hcal{N}(0, k^2 t)$.

The expectation $\mathbb{E}[e^{-k W(t)}]$ is the moment-generating function of t
he Gaussian $-k W(t) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, k^2 t)$ evaluated at $1$:

$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{-k W(t)}\right] = \exp\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\, k^2 t\right)
.$$

Substituting back:

$$\mathbb{E}[x(t)] = x_0\, e^{(c - k^2/2)t}\, e^{k^2 t/2} = x_0\, e^{ct}.$$

The $-k^2/2$ term in the exponent of $x(t)$ is precisely the Itô correction that
 cancels the $k^2/2$ coming from the Gaussian MGF, leaving a clean exponential g
rowth at rate $c$.

## Result

$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}[x(t)] = x_0\, e^{ct}}$$
