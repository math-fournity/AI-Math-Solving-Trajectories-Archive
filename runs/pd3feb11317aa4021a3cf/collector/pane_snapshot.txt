## Proof

We count the number of subsets $S \subseteq \{1,2,\dots,10\}$ with $|S|\ge 2$ th
at contain a divisibility pair (some $m,n\in S$ with $m\mid n$, $m\ne n$).

**Total subsets of size $\ge 2$:** $2^{10} - \binom{10}{0} - \binom{10}{1} = 102
4 - 1 - 10 = 1013$.

**Subtract the "bad" subsets** — those of size $\ge 2$ with *no* divisibility pa
ir, i.e., antichains in the divisibility poset of size $\ge 2$.

Since $1$ divides every other element, any antichain containing $1$ has size $1$
. So antichains of size $\ge 2$ live entirely in $\{2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9,10\}$.

**Divisibility edges** among $\{2,\dots,10\}$ (where $a\sim b$ means one divides
 the other):
$$2\text{-}4,\; 2\text{-}6,\; 2\text{-}8,\; 2\text{-}10,\; 3\text{-}6,\; 3\text{
-}9,\; 4\text{-}8,\; 5\text{-}10.$$

An antichain is an independent set in this graph. Vertex $7$ is isolated, contri
buting a factor of $2$.

**Count independent sets on $\{2,3,4,5,6,8,9,10\}$** (edges as above, minus $7$)
:

*Case A: $2$ included.* Then $4,6,8,10$ excluded. Remaining free vertices: $\{3,
5,9\}$ with edge $3\text{-}9$ ($5$ isolated).
- Independent sets of $\{3,9\}$: $\emptyset,\{3\},\{9\}$ → $3$; times $2$ for $5
$ → $6$.

*Case B: $2$ excluded.* Work on $\{3,4,5,6,8,9,10\}$ with edges $3\text{-}6, 3\t
ext{-}9, 4\text{-}8, 5\text{-}10$. Three disconnected components:
- $\{3,6,9\}$ (star, center $3$): $\emptyset,\{6\},\{9\},\{6,9\},\{3\}$ → $5$.
- $\{4,8\}$: $\emptyset,\{4\},\{8\}$ → $3$.
- $\{5,10\}$: $\emptyset,\{5\},\{10\}$ → $3$.
- Total: $5\times 3\times 3 = 45$.

Total independent sets on the $8$-vertex graph: $6 + 45 = 51$.

Including isolated vertex $7$: $51 \times 2 = 102$ independent sets total on $\{
2,\dots,10\}$.

**Antichains of size $\ge 2$:** subtract the empty set ($1$) and the $9$ singlet
ons:
$$102 - 1 - 9 = 92.$$

**Final count:**
$$1013 - 92 = \boxed{921}.$$
