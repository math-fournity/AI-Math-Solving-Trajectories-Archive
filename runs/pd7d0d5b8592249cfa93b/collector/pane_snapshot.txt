## Proof

**Setup (coordinates).** Place $t_1$ as the $x$-axis ($y=0$). Since $t_1$ is tan
gent to both circles on the same side, take
$$O_1=(0,r_1),\quad A=(0,0),\quad O_2=(2\sqrt{r_1r_2},\,r_2),\quad D=(2\sqrt{r_1
r_2},\,0),$$
where $|O_1O_2|=r_1+r_2$ (external tangency) gives the horizontal offset $2\sqrt
{r_1r_2}$. The parallel tangent $t_2$ to $\omega_1$ is $y=2r_1$.

Let $t_3$ be the line through $D$ with slope $m$: $\;y=m\!\left(x-2\sqrt{r_1r_2}
\right)$.

**Finding $B$ and $C$.** Intersecting $t_3$ with $t_2$:
$$B=\left(2\sqrt{r_1r_2}+\frac{2r_1}{m},\;2r_1\right).$$

For $C$: substitute $u=x-2\sqrt{r_1r_2}$, $y=mu$ into $(x-2\sqrt{r_1r_2})^2+(y-r
_2)^2=r_2^2$:
$$u^2+(mu-r_2)^2=r_2^2\;\Longrightarrow\;u\big((1+m^2)u-2mr_2\big)=0.$$
The root $u=0$ is $D$; the other gives
$$C=\left(2\sqrt{r_1r_2}+\frac{2mr_2}{1+m^2},\;\frac{2m^2r_2}{1+m^2}\right).$$

**Key claim.** The circumcenter of $\triangle ABC$ lies on the $y$-axis, i.e. th
e circumcircle is tangent to $t_1$ at $A$.

Let $O=(0,k)$. Since $A=(0,0)$, we have $|OA|^2=k^2$. We determine $k$ from $|OB
|^2=k^2$ and from $|OC|^2=k^2$ and show the two values coincide.

*From $B$:* $|OB|^2=k^2$ gives
$$\left(2\sqrt{r_1r_2}+\frac{2r_1}{m}\right)^2+(2r_1-k)^2=k^2,$$
$$k=\frac{\left(2\sqrt{r_1r_2}+\frac{2r_1}{m}\right)^2+4r_1^2}{4r_1}.$$
Expanding,
$$\left(2\sqrt{r_1r_2}+\frac{2r_1}{m}\right)^2=4r_1\!\left(r_2+\frac{2\sqrt{r_1r
_2}}{m}+\frac{r_1}{m^2}\right),$$
so
$$k_B=(r_1+r_2)+\frac{2\sqrt{r_1r_2}}{m}+\frac{r_1}{m^2}.\tag{1}$$

*From $C$:* $|OC|^2=k^2$ gives
$$\left(2\sqrt{r_1r_2}+\frac{2mr_2}{1+m^2}\right)^2+\left(\frac{2m^2r_2}{1+m^2}-
k\right)^2=k^2,$$
$$k=\frac{\left(2\sqrt{r_1r_2}+\frac{2mr_2}{1+m^2}\right)^2+\frac{4m^4r_2^2}{(1+
m^2)^2}}{\;\frac{4m^2r_2}{1+m^2}\;}.$$
The numerator equals $\frac{4}{(1+m^2)^2}\Big[\big(\sqrt{r_1r_2}(1+m^2)+mr_2\big
)^2+m^4r_2^2\Big]$. Expanding and collecting:
$$\big(\sqrt{r_1r_2}(1+m^2)+mr_2\big)^2+m^4r_2^2=(1+m^2)\,r_2\big[r_1+m^2(r_1+r_
2)+2m\sqrt{r_1r_2}\big].$$
Hence the numerator is $\frac{4r_2\big[r_1+m^2(r_1+r_2)+2m\sqrt{r_1r_2}\big]}{1+
m^2}$, and
$$k_C=\frac{r_1+m^2(r_1+r_2)+2m\sqrt{r_1r_2}}{m^2}=(r_1+r_2)+\frac{2\sqrt{r_1r_2
}}{m}+\frac{r_1}{m^2}.\tag{2}$$

**Conclusion.** From (1) and (2), $k_B=k_C=:k$. Therefore $O=(0,k)$ is equidista
nt from $A$, $B$, and $C$. Since $A,B,C$ are non-collinear ($A\in t_1$, $B\in t_
2$, $C\in\omega_2$, with $t_3\not\ni A$ for generic $m$), $O$ is the unique circ
umcenter, and it lies on the $y$-axis — the line through $A$ perpendicular to $t
_1$. The circumcircle passes through $A\in t_1$ with its center on the perpendic
ular to $t_1$ at $A$, so it is tangent to $t_1$ at $A$. $\quad\blacksquare$
