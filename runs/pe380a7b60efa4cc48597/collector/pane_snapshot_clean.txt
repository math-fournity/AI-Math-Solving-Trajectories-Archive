## Solution

We are given the Laplace equation $\theta_{xx} + \theta_{yy} = 0$ and the change
 of variables $x = \ln u$, $y = \ln v$, i.e. $u = e^x$, $v = e^y$.

### Chain rule for first derivatives

Since $u$ depends only on $x$ and $v$ depends only on $y$ (with $u,v$ treated as
 the new independent variables):

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = e^x = u, \qquad \frac{\partial v}{\partial x}
= 0$$
$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = 0, \qquad \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = e^y
= v$$

By the chain rule:

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\frac{\partial}{\pa
rtial u} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial x}\frac{\partial}{\partial v} = u\,\frac{\
partial}{\partial u}$$

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\frac{\partial}{\pa
rtial u} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\frac{\partial}{\partial v} = v\,\frac{\
partial}{\partial v}$$

### Second derivatives

**$\partial^2\theta/\partial x^2$:**

$$\frac{\partial^2\theta}{\partial x^2} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\!\left(\fr
ac{\partial\theta}{\partial x}\right) = u\,\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\!\left(u\
,\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial u}\right) = u\!\left(\frac{\partial\theta}{\part
ial u} + u\,\frac{\partial^2\theta}{\partial u^2}\right) = u\,\frac{\partial\the
ta}{\partial u} + u^2\,\frac{\partial^2\theta}{\partial u^2}$$

**$\partial^2\theta/\partial y^2$:** By the identical computation (swapping $u\l
eftrightarrow v$, $x\leftrightarrow y$):

$$\frac{\partial^2\theta}{\partial y^2} = v\,\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial v} +
 v^2\,\frac{\partial^2\theta}{\partial v^2}$$

### Transformed PDE

Substituting into $\theta_{xx} + \theta_{yy} = 0$:

$$\boxed{u^2\,\frac{\partial^2\theta}{\partial u^2} + u\,\frac{\partial\theta}{\
partial u} + v^2\,\frac{\partial^2\theta}{\partial v^2} + v\,\frac{\partial\thet
a}{\partial v} = 0}$$

### Mixed partial derivative $\partial^2\theta/\partial x\,\partial y$

$$\frac{\partial^2\theta}{\partial x\,\partial y} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\
!\left(\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial y}\right) = u\,\frac{\partial}{\partial u}
\!\left(v\,\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial v}\right)$$

Since $u$ and $v$ are independent variables, $v$ is constant with respect to $u$
, so:

$$\frac{\partial^2\theta}{\partial x\,\partial y} = u\,v\,\frac{\partial^2\theta
}{\partial u\,\partial v}$$

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial^2\theta}{\partial x\,\partial y} = uv\,\frac{\partial^2\
theta}{\partial u\,\partial v}}$$
