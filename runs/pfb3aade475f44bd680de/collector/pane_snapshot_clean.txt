## Verification of Stokes' Theorem

$$\iint_S (\nabla \times \mathbf{A})\cdot \mathbf{n}\, dS = \oint_C \mathbf{A}\c
dot d\mathbf{r}$$

with $\mathbf{A} = 3y\,\hat{\imath} - xz\,\hat{\jmath} + y^2\,\mathbf{k}$, $S$ t

at $z = 2$ (i.e. $x^2 + y^2 = 4$).

### Step 1 — Compute the curl

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{A} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{\imath} & \hat{\jmath} & \math
bf{k} \\ \partial_x & \partial_y & \partial_z \\ 3y & -xz & y^2 \end{vmatrix}$$

- $\hat{\imath}$: $\dfrac{\partial(y^2)}{\partial y} - \dfrac{\partial(-xz)}{\pa
rtial z} = 2y - (-x) = 2y + x$
- $\hat{\jmath}$: $\dfrac{\partial(3y)}{\partial z} - \dfrac{\partial(y^2)}{\par
tial x} = 0 - 0 = 0$
- $\mathbf{k}$: $\dfrac{\partial(-xz)}{\partial x} - \dfrac{\partial(3y)}{\parti
al y} = -z - 3 = -(z+3)$

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{A} = (2y + x)\,\hat{\imath} + 0\,\hat{\jmath} - (z+3)\,\

### Step 2 — Surface integral (left-hand side)

Parametrize $S$ by $\mathbf{r}(x,y) = \bigl(x,\; y,\; \tfrac{x^2+y^2}{2}\bigr)$

$$\mathbf{r}_x = (1,0,x),\qquad \mathbf{r}_y = (0,1,y),\qquad \mathbf{r}_x \time
s \mathbf{r}_y = (-x,\,-y,\,1)$$

The $z$-component is $+1 > 0$, so this is the upward (outward) normal, consisten

$$(\nabla \times \mathbf{A})\cdot(\mathbf{r}_x \times \mathbf{r}_y) = (2y+x)(-x)

$$= -2xy - x^2 - z - 3$$

Substitute $z = \tfrac{x^2+y^2}{2}$:

$$= -2xy - x^2 - \tfrac{x^2+y^2}{2} - 3 = -2xy - \tfrac{3}{2}x^2 - \tfrac{1}{2}y

- $-2xy = -r^2\sin 2\theta \Rightarrow$ integrates to $0$.

$$-2\pi r^2 - 6\pi$$

### Step 3 — Line integral (right-hand side)

$$= -\bigl(12\cdot\pi + 8\cdot\pi\bigr) = -20\pi$$
