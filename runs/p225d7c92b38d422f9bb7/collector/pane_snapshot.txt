## Proof

Let $A(N)=\#\{n\in\{1,\dots,N\}: n\text{ contains digit }1\}$ and $B(N)=N-A(N)=\
#\{n\in\{1,\dots,N\}: n\text{ contains no digit }1\}$. The condition $A(N)=N/2$
is equivalent to

$$B(N)=N/2,$$

so $N$ must be even. We seek three-digit $N$ ($100\le N\le 999$).

**Baseline.** $B(99)=8+8\cdot 9=80$: the one-digit numbers without $1$ are $\{2,
\dots,9\}$ (8), and the two-digit numbers without $1$ have tens digit in $\{2,\d
ots,9\}$ (8 choices) and units digit in $\{0,2,\dots,9\}$ (9 choices), giving $7
2$.

---

**Block $[100,199]$.** Every number has hundreds digit $1$, hence contains $1$.
So $B(N)=B(99)=80$ for all $N\in[100,199]$. The equation $80=N/2$ gives

$$N=160.$$

---

**Block $[200,299]$.** The hundreds digit $2$ contains no $1$, so for $N=200+t$
($0\le t\le 99$),
$$B(N)=B(199)+H(t)=80+H(t),$$
where $H(t)=\#\{m\in\{0,\dots,t\}: m\text{ (written as two digits) contains no d
igit }1\}$ (and $B(199)=80$ since $100$–$199$ all contain $1$). The equation bec
omes
$$H(t)=20+t/2,\qquad t\text{ even},\; 0\le t\le 98. \tag{$\ast$}$$

Write $t=10a+b$ ($0\le a,b\le 9$). A direct count gives
$$H(t)=\begin{cases}
b, & a=0,\; b\ge 1,\\
1, & a=0,\; b=0,\\
9, & a=1,\\
9(a-1)+b, & a\ge 2,\; b\ge 1,\\
9(a-1)+1, & a\ge 2,\; b=0.
\end{cases}$$

For even $t$, solving ($\ast$) by cases:

- $a=0$: $H-t/2=b/2\le 4<20$.
- $a=1$: $H-t/2=4-b/2\le 4<20$.
- $a\ge 2,\ b=0$: $H-t/2=4a-8=20\Rightarrow a=7$, giving $t=70$.
- $a\ge 2,\ b\ge 2$ even: $H-t/2=4a-9+b/2=20\Rightarrow 4a+b/2=29$. With $b\in\{
2,4,6,8\}$: $a=7,b=2$ gives $28+1=29$, i.e. $t=72$; all other $(a,b)$ fail.

Thus $t\in\{70,72\}$, yielding

$$N\in\{270,\,272\}.$$

Verification: $B(270)=80+H(70)=80+55=135=270/2$; $B(272)=80+H(72)=80+56=136=272/
2$. ✓

---

**Blocks $[300,999]$.** For the block $[100k,\,100k+99]$ with $k\in\{3,\dots,9\}
$ (hundreds digit $k$ contains no $1$), write $N=100k+t$. Each full hundred-bloc
k $[100j,100j+99]$ with $j\in\{3,\dots,k-1\}$ contributes $9\cdot 9=81$ numbers
without $1$ (tens and units each in $\{0,2,\dots,9\}$), and $B(299)=80+H(99)=80+
81=161$. Hence
$$B(N)=\underbrace{161+81(k-3)}_{=\,81k-82}+H(t).$$
The equation $B(N)=N/2=50k+t/2$ becomes
$$H(t)-t/2=82-31k.$$

For $k\ge 3$, the right side is $\le 82-93=-11<0$. But for every even $t\in[0,98
]$ one has $H(t)\ge t/2$ (from the formula above, $H(t)-t/2\ge 0$, with equality
 only at $t\in\{18,20,22\}$). So the left side is $\ge 0$, a contradiction. No s
olutions exist for $N\ge 300$.

---

**Conclusion.** The three-digit values of $N$ for which exactly half of $1,2,\do
ts,N$ contain the digit $1$ are

$$\boxed{N\in\{160,\;270,\;272\}}.$$
