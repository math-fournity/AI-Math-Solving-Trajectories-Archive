## Proof

**Setup.** We have 6 points in the plane (no three collinear), with a straight s
egment drawn between every pair (15 edges total). A crossing occurs when two non
-adjacent edges (sharing no endpoint) intersect at an interior point. Each cross
ing corresponds to a 4-element subset of points in convex position: given 4 poin
ts in general position, they form either a convex quadrilateral (exactly one cro
ssing, between the two diagonals) or one point lies inside the triangle of the o
ther three (zero crossings). Thus **the number of crossings equals the number of
 convex 4-subsets** among the 6 points.

---

### Lower Bound: At least 3 crossings

Suppose a drawing of K₆ has *c* crossings. Insert a new vertex at each crossing
point, splitting the two crossing edges. This produces a planar graph with:
- **v = 6 + c** vertices (6 original + 1 per crossing),
- **e = 15 + 2c** edges (each crossing splits 2 edges, adding 2 edges).

For any planar graph with v ≥ 3: e ≤ 3v − 6. Therefore:

$$15 + 2c \le 3(6+c) - 6 = 12 + 3c$$

$$c \ge 3$$

Every straight-line drawing is a drawing, so the rectilinear crossing number is
at least 3.

### Upper Bound: A configuration with exactly 3 crossings

Place the 6 towers at:

$$A = (0,\, 10),\quad B = (-10,\, {-10}),\quad C = (10,\, {-10})$$
$$D = (0,\, 0),\quad E = (-4,\, {-5}),\quad F = (4,\, {-5})$$

Here A, B, C form a large outer triangle, and D, E, F lie inside it, with D near
 vertex A, E near vertex B, F near vertex C, and the inner triangle DEF containi
ng the centroid of ABC. One verifies no three points are collinear (general posi
tion).

We count convex 4-subsets among all $\binom{6}{4} = 15$ four-element subsets, or
ganized by type:

**Type 1: 3 outer + 1 inner** (3 subsets: {A,B,C,D}, {A,B,C,E}, {A,B,C,F}).
Each inner point lies inside triangle ABC, so the 4-point set is not convex. **→
 0 convex subsets.**

**Type 2: 1 outer + 3 inner** (3 subsets: {A,D,E,F}, {B,D,E,F}, {C,D,E,F}).
- {A,D,E,F}: D=(0,0) lies inside triangle AEF (verified: D is on the same side a
s A relative to line EF, and on the same side as the opposite vertex for lines A
E and AF). Not convex.
- {B,D,E,F}: E=(-4,−5) lies inside triangle BDF (verified by the half-plane test
 against all three edges). Not convex.
- {C,D,E,F}: By left-right symmetry, F lies inside triangle CDE. Not convex.

**→ 0 convex subsets.**

**Type 3: 2 outer + 2 inner** (9 subsets). For each, we test whether any of the
4 points lies inside the triangle of the other 3:

| Subset | Result | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| {A,B,D,E} | **Convex** | All 4 points outside the triangle of the other 3 |
| {A,B,D,F} | Not convex | D inside triangle ABF |
| {A,B,E,F} | Not convex | E inside triangle ABF |
| {A,C,D,E} | Not convex | D inside triangle ACE |
| {A,C,D,F} | **Convex** | Symmetric to {A,B,D,E} |
| {A,C,E,F} | Not convex | F inside triangle ACE |
| {B,C,D,E} | Not convex | E inside triangle BCD |
| {B,C,D,F} | Not convex | F inside triangle BCD (symmetric) |
| {B,C,E,F} | **Convex** | All 4 points outside the triangle of the other 3 |

**→ 3 convex subsets.**

**Total convex 4-subsets = 0 + 0 + 3 = 3**, giving exactly **3 crossings**.

The three crossings are between the diagonal pairs of the three convex quadrilat
erals:
- Edges AE and BD (crossing at $(−40/11,\, −40/11)$),
- Edges AF and CD (crossing at $(40/11,\, −40/11)$),
- Edges BF and CE (crossing at $(0,\, −45/7)$).

These are three distinct interior points, none coinciding with a tower.
