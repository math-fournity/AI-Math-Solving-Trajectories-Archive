## Proof

**Setup.** Let $p$ and $q$ be positive integers with $p$ prime, $p \mid q-1$, an
d $(p+q) \mid (p^2 + 2020q^2)$.

**Key reduction.** Since $p \equiv -q \pmod{p+q}$, we have $p^2 \equiv q^2 \pmod
{p+q}$. Therefore:
$$p^2 + 2020q^2 \equiv q^2 + 2020q^2 = 2021\,q^2 \pmod{p+q}.$$
So the divisibility condition becomes $(p+q) \mid 2021\,q^2$.

**Coprimality.** Since $p \mid (q-1)$, we get $q \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$, hence $\gcd
(p,q) = \gcd(p,1) = 1$. Therefore:
$$\gcd(p+q,\, q) = \gcd(p,\, q) = 1.$$
Since $p+q$ is coprime to $q$ (and hence to $q^2$), the condition $(p+q) \mid 20
21\,q^2$ forces:
$$\boxed{(p+q) \mid 2021}.$$

Note this is also sufficient: if $(p+q)\mid 2021$, then $(p+q)\mid 2021q^2$, and
 the congruence above gives $(p+q)\mid(p^2+2020q^2)$.

**Factoring.** $2021 = 43 \times 47$, so the positive divisors are $1, 43, 47, 2
021$. Since $p\geq 2$ and $q\geq 1$, we have $p+q\geq 3$, leaving $p+q \in \{43,
 47, 2021\}$.

For each case, $q = (p+q) - p$, so $q - 1 = (p+q) - p - 1$, and the condition $p
 \mid (q-1)$ becomes $p \mid \bigl((p+q) - 1\bigr)$.

**Case 1: $p+q = 43$.** Need $p \mid 42$. Prime divisors of $42 = 2\cdot 3\cdot
7$: $p \in \{2, 3, 7\}$, giving $q = 41, 40, 36$ (all $\geq 1$). ✓

**Case 2: $p+q = 47$.** Need $p \mid 46$. Prime divisors of $46 = 2\cdot 23$: $p
 \in \{2, 23\}$, giving $q = 45, 24$. ✓

**Case 3: $p+q = 2021$.** Need $p \mid 2020$. Prime divisors of $2020 = 2^2\cdot
 5\cdot 101$: $p \in \{2, 5, 101\}$, giving $q = 2019, 2016, 1920$. ✓

**Collecting all valid primes:**
$$p \in \{2,\, 3,\, 5,\, 7,\, 23,\, 101\}.$$

**Verification (spot checks).**
- $p=2, q=41$: $p+q=43$, $p^2+2020q^2 = 4 + 2020\cdot1681 = 3395624 = 43\times 7
8968$. ✓
- $p=23, q=24$: $p+q=47$, $529 + 2020\cdot576 = 1164049 = 47\times 24767$. ✓
- $p=101, q=1920$: $p+q=2021$, and by the congruence argument $(2021)\mid 2021\c
dot q^2$, hence $(2021)\mid(p^2+2020q^2)$. ✓

**Sum:**
$$2 + 3 + 5 + 7 + 23 + 101 = \boxed{141}.$$
