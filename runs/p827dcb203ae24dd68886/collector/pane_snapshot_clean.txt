## Proof

Let $d \geq 1$ be a square-free natural number, and let $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}
)$ with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$. The fundamental unit $\varepsilon_d$ i
s, by definition, a unit of $\mathcal{O}_K$. Hence if $\varepsilon_d = 1 + \sqrt
{d}$, then $1 + \sqrt{d}$ must itself be a unit of $\mathcal{O}_K$.

**Step 1: $1+\sqrt{d}$ is a unit only when $d = 2$.**

The element $1 + \sqrt{d}$ lies in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{d}] \subseteq \mathcal{O}_K
$ for every $d$. Its norm (in both the $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{d}]$ and $\mathcal{O}_K
$ senses, since norm is intrinsic to the field) is
$$N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(1+\sqrt{d}) = (1+\sqrt{d})(1-\sqrt{d}) = 1 - d.$$
An element of $\mathcal{O}_K$ is a unit if and only if its norm is $\pm 1$. Ther
efore we require
$$|1 - d| = 1 \implies d \in \{0, 2\}.$$
Since $d$ is a natural number and $d=0$ is excluded (not a valid radicand for a
quadratic field), the **only** candidate is $d = 2$.

**Step 2: For $d = 2$, $1+\sqrt{2}$ is indeed the fundamental unit.**

Here $d = 2 \equiv 2 \pmod{4}$, so $\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]$. The u
nit group is generated by the smallest unit $> 1$. We have $1 + \sqrt{2} \approx
 2.414$ with norm $-1$, so it is a unit. Suppose there were a unit $\eta$ with $
1 < \eta < 1 + \sqrt{2}$. Any unit of $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]$ has the form $a + b
\sqrt{2}$ with $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $a^2 - 2b^2 = \pm 1$. The condition $1
< a + b\sqrt{2} < 1 + \sqrt{2}$ with $b \geq 1$ (since $b = 0$ gives $a = \pm 1$
, yielding $\eta = 1$) forces $b = 1$ and then $a^2 = 2 \cdot 1 \pm 1 \in \{1, 3
\}$, so $a = 1$ (giving exactly $1 + \sqrt{2}$) — the case $a^2 = 3$ is impossib
le over $\mathbb{Z}$. With $b = 1, a = 1$ we recover $\eta = 1 + \sqrt{2}$ itsel
f, and $a = -1$ gives $-1+\sqrt{2} \approx 0.414 < 1$. No smaller unit $>1$ exis
ts. Hence $1 + \sqrt{2}$ is the fundamental unit.

**Conclusion.**

The requirement that $1 + \sqrt{d}$ be a unit forces $d = 2$ uniquely, and for $
d = 2$ the element $1 + \sqrt{2}$ is confirmed to be the fundamental unit of $\m
athbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$. Therefore $d = 2$ is the **only** square-free natural numbe
r with the stated property.
