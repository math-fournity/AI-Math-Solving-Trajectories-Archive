## Fundamental Group of the Torus

**Claim:** $\pi_1(T, x_0) \cong \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$, where $T = S^1 \t
imes S^1$.

### Proof

We give two independent arguments.

**Argument 1: Product of spaces.**

A general theorem in homotopy theory states that for based spaces $(X, x_0)$ and
 $(Y, y_0)$, the fundamental group of the product is the product of the fundamen
tal groups:

$$\pi_1(X \times Y, (x_0, y_0)) \cong \pi_1(X, x_0) \times \pi_1(Y, y_0).$$

*Proof of the theorem.* Let $p_X : X \times Y \to X$ and $p_Y : X \times Y \to Y
$ be the projections. These induce homomorphisms
$$(p_X)_* : \pi_1(X \times Y) \to \pi_1(X), \qquad (p_Y)_* : \pi_1(X \times Y) \
to \pi_1(Y).$$
Together they give a homomorphism
$$\Phi : \pi_1(X \times Y) \to \pi_1(X) \times \pi_1(Y), \qquad \Phi([\gamma]) =
 ((p_X)_*[\gamma], (p_Y)_*[\gamma]).$$

- **Injectivity:** If $\Phi([\gamma]) = ([c_{x_0}], [c_{y_0}])$, then $p_X \circ
 \gamma$ and $p_Y \circ \gamma$ are null-homotopic in $X$ and $Y$ respectively.
Let $H_X : [0,1]^2 \to X$ and $H_Y : [0,1]^2 \to Y$ be the homotopies to the con
stant loops. Then $H(s,t) = (H_X(s,t), H_Y(s,t))$ is a homotopy from $\gamma$ to
 the constant loop at $(x_0, y_0)$ in $X \times Y$. So $[\gamma]$ is trivial.

- **Surjectivity:** Given $[\alpha] \in \pi_1(X)$ and $[\beta] \in \pi_1(Y)$, de
fine $\gamma(s) = (\alpha(s), \beta(s))$. This is a loop in $X \times Y$ based a
t $(x_0, y_0)$, and $\Phi([\gamma]) = ([\alpha], [\beta])$.

Hence $\Phi$ is an isomorphism. $\square$

Applying this with $X = Y = S^1$ and the classical result $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mat
hbb{Z}$ (via the covering map $\mathbb{R} \to S^1$, $t \mapsto e^{2\pi i t}$, an
d the path/loop lifting property), we obtain

$$\pi_1(T) = \pi_1(S^1 \times S^1) \cong \pi_1(S^1) \times \pi_1(S^1) \cong \mat
hbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}.$$

**Argument 2: Via the standard cell structure (van Kampen / CW-complex).**

The torus $T$ has a CW-structure with one 0-cell $e^0$, two 1-cells $a, b$, and
one 2-cell $e^2$ attached along the word $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$. The 1-skeleton $T^{(1
)}$ is a wedge $S^1 \vee S^1$ with $\pi_1 \cong F(a,b)$, the free group on two g
enerators. Attaching the 2-cell along $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$ imposes the single relati
on $aba^{-1}b^{-1} = 1$, i.e. $ab = ba$. By the Seifert–van Kampen theorem,

$$\pi_1(T) \cong \langle a, b \mid aba^{-1}b^{-1} = 1 \rangle \cong \langle a, b
 \mid ab = ba \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}.$$

Both arguments agree:

$$\boxed{\pi_1(T) \cong \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}.}$$
