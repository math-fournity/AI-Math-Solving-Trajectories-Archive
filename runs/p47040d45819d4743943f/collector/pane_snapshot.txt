$$V_i \;=\; \bigl(0,\tfrac12\bigr)\;\cup\;\bigl(\tfrac12,\;\tfrac12+\tfrac{1}{i+

viewed as a subset of $[0,1]$. (Note $\tfrac12+\tfrac{1}{i+1}\le \tfrac12+\tfrac

**Nested.** For $i\ge 2$ we have $\tfrac{1}{i+1}<\tfrac{1}{i}$, hence
$$\bigl(\tfrac12,\tfrac12+\tfrac{1}{i+1}\bigr)\;\subsetneq\;\bigl(\tfrac12,\tfra
c12+\tfrac{1}{i}\bigr),$$
and therefore $V_i\subsetneq V_{i-1}$. In particular $V_i\subseteq V_{i-1}$ for
all $i$.

**No stabilization.** Since $V_i\subsetneq V_{i-1}$ for every $i\ge 2$, there is
 no index $N$ with $V_N=V_n$ for all $n\ge N$.

**The intersection.** We compute
$$\bigcap_{i=1}^{\infty} V_i \;=\; \bigl(0,\tfrac12\bigr)\;\cup\;\bigcap_{i=1}^{
\infty}\bigl(\tfrac12,\tfrac12+\tfrac{1}{i+1}\bigr).$$
The second intersection is empty: if a point $x$ belonged to every $\bigl(\tfrac

ac12$. Also $\tfrac12\notin V_i$ for any $i$. Hence
$$\bigcap_{i=1}^{\infty} V_i \;=\; \bigl(0,\tfrac12\bigr).$$

**Open and non-empty.** In the subspace topology of $[0,1]$,
$$\bigl(0,\tfrac12\bigr) \;=\; [0,1]\cap\bigl(0,\tfrac12\bigr),$$
which is open; and it is clearly non-empty.

has an infinite intersection $\bigcap_{i=1}^{\infty}V_i=(0,\tfrac12)$ that is op

for all $n\ge N$). Thus the answer is **yes**, it is possible.
