Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether, for any sequence of finite
-measure non-atomic measurable sets, one can always find a finite-measure set $B
$ intersecting every $E_n$ in positive measure.

**Answer: No, it is not always possible.**

## Proof (by counterexample)

**Measure space.** Let $X = \mathbb{N}$, $M = 2^{\mathbb{N}}$, and $m$ be the co
unting measure (so $m(A) = |A|$, with $m(A) = \infty$ for infinite sets).

**The sequence.** For each $n \geq 1$, define:
$$E_n = \{2n-1,\; 2n\}.$$

**Verification of hypotheses:**

1. *Finite measure:* $m(E_n) = 2 < \infty$ for every $n$. ✓

2. *Not an atom:* The singleton $\{2n-1\} \subseteq E_n$ satisfies $0 < m(\{2n-1
\}) = 1 < 2 = m(E_n)$, so $E_n$ is not an atom. ✓

**No finite-measure $B$ can intersect all $E_n$ positively:**

Suppose, for contradiction, that $B \in M$ satisfies $m(B) < \infty$ and $m(B \c
ap E_n) > 0$ for all $n \geq 1$.

Since $m$ is the counting measure, $m(B) < \infty$ means $B$ is a **finite** set
, say $|B| = k$ for some $k \in \mathbb{N}$.

The sets $\{E_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ are **pairwise disjoint** (since $E_n \subsete
q \{2n-1, 2n\}$ and these pairs are disjoint for different $n$). Therefore, $B$
can intersect at most $k$ of the sets $E_n$ nontrivially—each nonempty intersect
ion $B \cap E_n$ consumes at least one element of $B$, and $B$ has only $k$ elem
ents.

Consequently, there exists some $N$ such that $B \cap E_N = \emptyset$, giving:
$$m(B \cap E_N) = 0,$$
which contradicts the requirement that $m(B \cap E_n) > 0$ for all $n$.

**Conclusion.** The pairwise disjointness of the $E_n$, combined with the fact t
hat any finite-measure set $B$ (under counting measure) is finite, makes it impo
ssible for $B$ to meet every $E_n$ in a set of positive measure. The answer to t
he question is **no**. $\blacksquare$
