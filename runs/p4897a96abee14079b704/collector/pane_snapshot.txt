**Claim.** Let $(M,\omega)$ be a symplectic manifold and $X$ a smooth vector fie
ld on $M$ with complete flow $\{g_s\}_{s\in\mathbb{R}}$. If $\mathcal{L}_X\omega
 = 0$, then $g_s^*\omega = \omega$ for all $s\in\mathbb{R}$; in particular the f
low is symplectic.

**Proof.**

Fix a point $p\in M$ and consider the time-dependent $2$-form along the integral
 curve of $X$ through $p$:
$$\Omega(s) := (g_s^*\omega)_p \in \Lambda^2 T_p^* M.$$
(We evaluate at the fixed base point $p$, so $\Omega(s)$ is a curve in the fixed
 vector space $\Lambda^2 T_p^*M$; this makes the differentiation below legitimat
e without dragging base points along.)

We compute $\frac{d}{ds}\Omega(s)$. By the standard identity for the derivative
of a pullback by a flow (a consequence of Cartan's magic formula $\mathcal{L}_X
= i_X d + d\,i_X$),
$$\frac{d}{ds}g_s^*\omega = g_s^*(\mathcal{L}_X\omega).$$
Evaluating at $p$,
$$\Omega'(s) = \bigl(g_s^*(\mathcal{L}_X\omega)\bigr)_p.$$

By hypothesis $\mathcal{L}_X\omega = 0$ everywhere on $M$, so the right-hand sid
e vanishes identically:
$$\Omega'(s) = 0 \quad\text{for all } s.$$

Hence $\Omega(s)$ is constant in $s$. At $s=0$ we have $g_0 = \mathrm{id}$, so
$$\Omega(0) = (g_0^*\omega)_p = \omega_p.$$
Therefore $\Omega(s) = \omega_p$ for all $s$, i.e.
$$(g_s^*\omega)_p = \omega_p \quad\text{for all } s\in\mathbb{R},\ p\in M.$$

Since $p$ was arbitrary,
$$g_s^*\omega = \omega \quad\text{for all } s\in\mathbb{R}.$$

Thus every $g_s$ is a symplectomorphism; the flow $\{g_s\}$ is symplectic. $\bla
cksquare$

**Remark (necessity).** The converse is immediate: differentiating $g_s^*\omega=
\omega$ at $s=0$ gives $\mathcal{L}_X\omega=0$. Hence $\mathcal{L}_X\omega=0$ is
 in fact *equivalent* to the flow being symplectic. A vector field satisfying th
is condition is called a **symplectic** (or **locally Hamiltonian**) vector fiel
d.
