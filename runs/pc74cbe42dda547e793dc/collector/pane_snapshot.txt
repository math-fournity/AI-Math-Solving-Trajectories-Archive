# Finite Element Formulation with Upwind Artificial Viscosity: Stability, Conver
gence, and Comparison with Galerkin Linear FEM

## 1. The continuous problem

Find $u \in C^2(0,1)\cap C[0,1]$ such that

$$-\varepsilon u''(x) + b\,u'(x) = 1, \quad 0<x<1, \qquad u(0)=\alpha,\; u(1)=\b
eta,$$

with $\varepsilon>0$ and $\alpha,\beta,b\in\mathbb{R}$. This is a **singularly p
erturbed convection–diffusion** equation. The ratio $|b|/\varepsilon$ measures t
he dominance of convection; when $|b|\gg \varepsilon$ a boundary layer of width
$O(\varepsilon/|b|)$ forms at the **outflow** boundary.

**Exact solution** (for $b\neq 0$, useful for reference). The homogeneous equati
on has characteristic roots $r=0$ and $r=b/\varepsilon$, and a particular soluti
on is $u_p = x/b$. Hence

$$u(x) = \frac{x}{b} + C_1 + C_2\,e^{bx/\varepsilon}, \qquad C_2 = \frac{\beta-\
alpha-1/b}{e^{b/\varepsilon}-1},\quad C_1 = \alpha - C_2.$$

For $b>0$ the exponential grows, so the layer sits at $x=1$ (outflow); for $b<0$
 it sits at $x=0$.

## 2. Weak (variational) formulation

Set $V_0 := H_0^1(0,1)$ and $V_{\alpha\beta}:=\{v\in H^1(0,1): v(0)=\alpha,\, v(
1)=\beta\}$. Multiplying by a test function $v\in V_0$ and integrating by parts
(the boundary term $[\varepsilon u' v]_0^1$ vanishes because $v|_{\partial}=0$)
gives:

$$\boxed{\;\text{Find } u\in V_{\alpha\beta}:\quad a(u,v) = (f,v)\quad\forall\,
v\in V_0,\;}$$

with the bilinear and linear forms

$$a(u,v) := \varepsilon\,(u',v') + b\,(u',v), \qquad (f,v):=(1,v)=\int_0^1 v\,dx
.$$

**Coercivity.** For $v\in V_0$,
$$a(v,v) = \varepsilon\|v'\|_{L^2}^2 + b\,(v',v) = \varepsilon\|v'\|^2 + \tfrac{
b}{2}[v^2]_0^1 = \varepsilon\|v'\|^2,$$
so $a$ is $V_0$-coercive with constant $\varepsilon$. Existence/uniqueness follo
w from Lax–Milgram. Note the coercivity constant is the **small** parameter $\va
repsilon$ — the seed of all difficulties.

## 3. Galerkin linear finite element formulation

### 3.1 Discrete spaces

Uniform mesh $0=x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_N=1$, $h=1/N$, $x_i=ih$. Let $\{\varphi_i\}_{i=
0}^N$ be the standard piecewise-linear "hat" basis, $\varphi_i(x_j)=\delta_{ij}$
. Define

$$V_h := \mathrm{span}\{\varphi_0,\dots,\varphi_N\}\subset H^1(0,1), \qquad V_{h
,0}:=\mathrm{span}\{\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_{N-1}\}\subset V_0.$$

### 3.2 Discrete problem

$$\boxed{\;\text{Find } u_h\in V_h,\; u_h(0)=\alpha,\; u_h(1)=\beta:\quad a(u_h,
v_h)=(1,v_h)\quad\forall\, v_h\in V_{h,0}.\;}$$

Writing $u_h = \alpha\varphi_0 + \beta\varphi_N + \sum_{j=1}^{N-1} u_j\varphi_j$
, the unknowns $(u_1,\dots,u_{N-1})$ satisfy a linear system $A\mathbf{u}=\mathb
f{f}$.

### 3.3 Stiffness matrix entries (uniform mesh)

Using the standard integrals

$$(\varphi_i',\varphi_j') = \frac{1}{h}\begin{cases}2 & i=j\\-1 & |i-j|=1\\0 & \
text{otherwise}\end{cases}, \qquad (\varphi_i',\varphi_j)=\frac{1}{2}\begin{case
s}-1 & j=i-1\\+1 & j=i+1\\0 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases},$$

the $i$-th interior equation (for $1\le i\le N-1$) reads

$$\frac{\varepsilon}{h}\bigl(-u_{i-1}+2u_i-u_{i+1}\bigr) + \frac{b}{2}\bigl(-u_{
i-1}+u_{i+1}\bigr) = h,$$

or, dividing by $h$,

$$\frac{\varepsilon}{h^2}\Bigl[(-1-\mathrm{Pe})\,u_{i-1} + 2\,u_i + (-1+\mathrm{
Pe})\,u_{i+1}\Bigr] = 1, \qquad \mathrm{Pe}:=\frac{|b|\,h}{2\varepsilon}\;(\text
{mesh Péclet number}).$$

## 4. Stability and convergence of the Galerkin linear FEM

### 4.1 Coercivity-based stability

By Céa's lemma, $\|u-u_h\|_{H^1}\le \frac{M}{\varepsilon}\inf_{v_h\in V_h}\|u-v_
h\|_{H^1}$, with $M$ the continuity constant of $a$. The factor $1/\varepsilon$
is the **stability constant**: it blows up as $\varepsilon\to 0$, so the energy
estimate becomes meaningless in the convection-dominated regime.

### 4.2 Loss of the discrete maximum principle (DMP)

The off-diagonal coefficient multiplying $u_{i+1}$ (for $b>0$) is $\frac{\vareps
ilon}{h^2}(\mathrm{Pe}-1)$. The stiffness matrix is an **M-matrix** (non-positiv
e off-diagonals, positive diagonal, row-diagonal dominance) **if and only if**

$$\mathrm{Pe} = \frac{bh}{2\varepsilon} \le 1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad h \l
e \frac{2\varepsilon}{|b|}.$$

- **If $\mathrm{Pe}\le 1$:** the matrix is an M-matrix, the DMP holds, and the d
iscrete solution is monotone (no spurious oscillations).
- **If $\mathrm{Pe}>1$:** the M-matrix property is lost, the DMP fails, and the
Galerkin solution develops **unphysical oscillations** of amplitude $O(1)$ near
the outflow boundary layer. The method does **not converge** in the maximum norm
 for fixed $\varepsilon$ until $h$ is refined below the threshold $2\varepsilon/
|b|$.

### 4.3 Convergence estimates

- **Energy ($H^1$) norm:** $\|u-u_h\|_{H^1}\le \dfrac{C}{\varepsilon}\,h\,|u|_{H
^2}$. The constant $C/\varepsilon$ degenerates as $\varepsilon\to 0$.
- **$L^2$ norm** (duality/Aubin–Nitsche): $\|u-u_h\|_{L^2}\le \dfrac{C}{\varepsi
lon}\,h^2\,|u|_{H^2}$ — second order, but again with the $1/\varepsilon$ degrada
tion.
- **No uniform convergence:** in the convection-dominated regime ($\mathrm{Pe}\g
g 1$) the error does **not** tend to zero as $h\to 0$ with $\varepsilon$ fixed,
because of the oscillations; $\|u-u_h\|_\infty$ stays $O(1)$ until $h<2\varepsil
on/|b|$.

**Summary for Galerkin:** formally second-order in $L^2$ and first-order in $H^1
$, but **conditionally stable** (requires $h\le 2\varepsilon/|b|$) and **non-rob
ust** with respect to $\varepsilon$.

## 5. Finite element formulation with upwind artificial viscosity

### 5.1 Idea

Add an **artificial diffusion** term $\delta\ge 0$ to the bilinear form, chosen
so that the discrete Péclet number is brought back to $\le 1$, restoring the M-m
atrix property. Define the **modified bilinear form**

$$\boxed{\;a_\delta(u,v) := (\varepsilon+\delta)\,(u',v') + b\,(u',v).\;}$$

The discrete problem becomes:

$$\boxed{\;\text{Find } u_h^\delta\in V_h,\; u_h^\delta(0)=\alpha,\; u_h^\delta(
1)=\beta:\quad a_\delta(u_h^\delta,v_h)=(1,v_h)\quad\forall\, v_h\in V_{h,0}.\;}
$$

### 5.2 Choice of $\delta$ — full upwind

The natural (first-order) upwind choice is

$$\boxed{\;\delta = \frac{|b|\,h}{2}.\;}$$

With this, the **effective Péclet number** becomes

$$\widetilde{\mathrm{Pe}} := \frac{|b|\,h}{2(\varepsilon+\delta)} = \frac{|b|\,h
}{2\varepsilon+|b|h} = \frac{\mathrm{Pe}}{1+\mathrm{Pe}} < 1 \quad\text{for all
} h,\varepsilon>0.$$

Hence the M-matrix property holds **unconditionally**.

### 5.3 Resulting discrete system

For $b>0$, $\delta=bh/2$, the $i$-th interior equation is

$$\frac{\varepsilon+bh/2}{h}(-u_{i-1}+2u_i-u_{i+1}) + \frac{b}{2}(-u_{i-1}+u_{i+
1}) = h,$$

which simplifies to

$$\Bigl(-\frac{\varepsilon}{h}-b\Bigr)u_{i-1} + \Bigl(\frac{2\varepsilon}{h}+b\B
igr)u_i + \Bigl(-\frac{\varepsilon}{h}\Bigr)u_{i+1} = h.$$

All off-diagonal entries are **strictly negative** and the diagonal is positive
with row-sum zero on the homogeneous part: this is precisely the **first-order u
pwind finite-difference scheme** embedded in the FEM framework. The case $b<0$ i
s symmetric (layer at $x=0$), using $\delta=|b|h/2$.

### 5.4 Equivalent Petrov–Galerkin / SUPG viewpoint (remark)

The artificial-viscosity method can be reinterpreted as a Petrov–Galerkin method
 with test functions $v_h + \frac{h}{2(\varepsilon+\delta)}b\,v_h'$, i.e. a memb
er of the **streamline-upwind** family. The choice $\delta=|b|h/2$ corresponds t
o **full upwind**; more refined choices (e.g. $\delta = \frac{|b|h}{2}\coth\math
rm{Pe}-\varepsilon$, the **Scharfetter–Gummel / exponential fitting** scheme) yi
eld the exact nodal values on uniform meshes for this 1D problem.

## 6. Stability and convergence of the upwind artificial-viscosity FEM

### 6.1 Stability

- **Coercivity:** $a_\delta(v,v)=(\varepsilon+\delta)\|v'\|^2\ge (\varepsilon+\t
frac{|b|h}{2})\|v'\|^2$. The coercivity constant is now $\varepsilon+\delta = O(
h)$ (independent of how small $\varepsilon$ is), a dramatic improvement over the
 Galerkin constant $\varepsilon$.
- **Discrete maximum principle:** since $\widetilde{\mathrm{Pe}}<1$ always, the
stiffness matrix is an M-matrix for **every** $h$ and $\varepsilon$. Consequentl
y the discrete solution satisfies the DMP, is **monotone**, and exhibits **no sp
urious oscillations** — robustly, for all parameter regimes.

### 6.2 Convergence

The error splits into an **interpolation** part and a **consistency (artificial-
diffusion) perturbation**:

$$\|u - u_h^\delta\|_{H^1} \le C\Bigl(\inf_{v_h\in V_h}\|u-v_h\|_{H^1} + \delta\
,|u|_{H^2}\Bigr) \le C\bigl(h + \delta\bigr)|u|_{H^2} = O(h),$$

since $\delta=|b|h/2=O(h)$. Key points:

- **First-order** in $H^1$ (and in $L^2$ via duality, $\|u-u_h^\delta\|_{L^2}=O(
h)$), with constants **independent of $\varepsilon$** — the method is **uniforml
y (robustly) convergent**.
- The price of upwinding is **numerical diffusion**: the artificial term $\delta
 u''$ smears the boundary layer over a width $O(\delta/\varepsilon)=O(|b|h/\vare
psilon)$, so the layer is **resolved but broadened** — the solution is qualitati
vely correct (monotone, no wiggles) but the layer location is smeared by one $O(
h)$ cell.
- **Uniform convergence in $L^\infty$** holds (away from the layer, $O(h)$; in t
he layer, the smearing is $O(h)$), in stark contrast to Galerkin.

## 7. Comparison: Galerkin linear FEM vs. upwind artificial viscosity

| Property | Galerkin linear FEM | Upwind artificial viscosity ($\delta=|b|h/2$)
 |
|---|---|---|
| Bilinear form | $a(u,v)=\varepsilon(u',v')+b(u',v)$ | $a_\delta(u,v)=(\varepsi
lon+\delta)(u',v')+b(u',v)$ |
| Coercivity constant | $\varepsilon$ (degenerates as $\varepsilon\to0$) | $\var
epsilon+\delta=O(h)$ (robust) |
| Mesh Péclet threshold | requires $\mathrm{Pe}\le 1$, i.e. $h\le 2\varepsilon/|
b|$ | $\widetilde{\mathrm{Pe}}<1$ **for all** $h$ |
| M-matrix / DMP | only when $h\le 2\varepsilon/|b|$ | **always** |
| Oscillations | severe when $\mathrm{Pe}>1$ (amplitude $O(1)$) | **none** (mono
tone) |
| Convergence order ($L^2$) | $O(h^2)$ when stable; fails when $\mathrm{Pe}>1$ |
 $O(h)$, **uniform in $\varepsilon$** |
| Convergence order ($H^1$) | $O(h)$ with constant $1/\varepsilon$ | $O(h)$ with
 $\varepsilon$-independent constant |
| Boundary layer | sharp but oscillatory if unresolved | **smeared** over $O(h)$
, but non-oscillatory |
| Robustness vs. $\varepsilon$ | non-robust | **robust** |
| Relation to FDM | central differences | first-order upwind differences |

### Interpretation

- **Galerkin** is formally higher order (second in $L^2$) but **non-robust**: it
 is accurate only when the mesh resolves the layer ($h\lesssim \varepsilon/|b|$)
, which is prohibitively expensive when $\varepsilon\ll |b|$. Outside this regim
e it produces oscillations that destroy convergence.
- **Upwind artificial viscosity** trades **one order of accuracy** for **uncondi
tional stability and robustness**: it is only first-order and smears the layer,
but it converges uniformly for all $\varepsilon$ and $h$, satisfies the DMP, and
 never oscillates. It is the method of choice in convection-dominated regimes wh
ere robustness matters more than local accuracy.
- A compromise between the two is the **streamline-upwind Petrov–Galerkin (SUPG)
** method with a $\varepsilon$-dependent $\delta$ (e.g. Scharfetter–Gummel), whi
ch retains second-order nodal exactness in 1D while keeping stability — but that
 lies beyond the "full upwind" scheme requested here.

## 8. Conclusion

The upwind artificial-viscosity FEM is obtained from the Galerkin formulation by
 replacing the diffusion coefficient $\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon+\delta$, $\
delta=|b|h/2$, in the bilinear form:

$$a_\delta(u_h,v_h)=(\varepsilon+\tfrac{|b|h}{2})(u_h',v_h')+b(u_h',v_h)=(1,v_h)
.$$

This reduces the effective Péclet number below unity for every mesh, restores th
e M-matrix property and the discrete maximum principle, eliminates oscillations,
 and yields first-order, **$\varepsilon$-uniformly convergent** approximations.
Compared with the Galerkin linear FEM — which is formally second-order but only
conditionally stable ($h\le 2\varepsilon/|b|$), loses the DMP, and oscillates vi
olently when convection dominates — the upwind method sacrifices one order of ac
curacy and smears the boundary layer in exchange for unconditional stability, mo
notonicity, and robustness in the singularly perturbed regime.
