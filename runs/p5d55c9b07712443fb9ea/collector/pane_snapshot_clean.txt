We solve the Volterra integral equation of the second kind with convolution kern
el
$$F(t) = a\sin t - 2\int_0^t F(u)\cos(t-u)\,du.$$

**Step 1: Recognize the convolution structure.** The integral $\int_0^t F(u)\cos
(t-u)\,du = (F * \cos)(t)$ is a convolution. Apply the Laplace transform $\mathc
al{L}$, with $f(s)=\mathcal{L}\{F\}$, using $\mathcal{L}\{\sin t\}=\frac{1}{s^2+
1}$ and $\mathcal{L}\{\cos t\}=\frac{s}{s^2+1}$, and the convolution theorem $\m
athcal{L}\{F*\cos\}=f(s)\cdot\frac{s}{s^2+1}$:

$$f(s) = \frac{a}{s^2+1} - 2\,f(s)\,\frac{s}{s^2+1}.$$

**Step 2: Solve algebraically.** Collecting $f(s)$:

$$f(s)\!\left(1+\frac{2s}{s^2+1}\right)=\frac{a}{s^2+1},\qquad f(s)\,\frac{s^2+2
s+1}{s^2+1}=\frac{a}{s^2+1},$$

$$f(s)\,(s+1)^2 = a \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(s)=\frac{a}{(s+1)^2}.$$

**Step 3: Invert.** Since $\mathcal{L}^{-1}\!\left\{\frac{1}{(s+1)^2}\right\}=t\
,e^{-t}$ (a standard shift/transform pair), we obtain

$$F(t) = a\,t\,e^{-t}.$$

**Step 4: Direct verification.** Compute $I(t)=\int_0^t a\,u\,e^{-u}\cos(t-u)\,d
u$. Substituting $v=t-u$:

$$I(t)=a\,e^{-t}\!\left[t\!\int_0^t e^v\cos v\,dv - \int_0^t v\,e^v\cos v\,dv\ri
ght].$$

Using $\int e^v\cos v\,dv=\tfrac12 e^v(\sin v+\cos v)$ and integration by parts
for the second integral,

$$\int_0^t e^v\cos v\,dv=\tfrac12\bigl(e^t(\sin t+\cos t)-1\bigr),$$
$$\int_0^t v\,e^v\cos v\,dv=\tfrac{t}{2}e^t(\sin t+\cos t)-\tfrac12 e^t\sin t.$$

Substituting and simplifying, the $e^t(\sin t+\cos t)$ terms cancel:

$$I(t)=a\,e^{-t}\!\left[-\frac{t}{2}+\frac12 e^t\sin t\right]=\frac{a}{2}\sin t-
\frac{a}{2}\,t\,e^{-t}.$$

Hence
$$2I(t)=a\sin t - a\,t\,e^{-t}=a\sin t - F(t),$$

so $F(t)=a\sin t-2I(t)=a\,t\,e^{-t}$, confirming the solution satisfies the orig
inal equation. Also $F(0)=0=a\sin 0-0$, consistent with the equation at $t=0$.

**Conclusion.** The unique continuous solution is

$$\boxed{F(t)=a\,t\,e^{-t}}.$$
