# Disjoint Union as Coproduct in **Set**

## Setup

Work in the category **Set**, where objects are sets and morphisms are functions
 $f : X \to Y$ between sets.

Let $\{X_i\}_{i \in I}$ be a family of sets indexed by a set $I$. Define their *
*disjoint union** (coproduct candidate):

$$\coprod_{i \in I} X_i \;=\; \bigcup_{i \in I} \bigl(X_i \times \{i\}\bigr) \;=
\; \{(x, i) : i \in I,\; x \in X_i\}.$$

For each $j \in I$, define the **injection** (inclusion morphism)

$$\iota_j : X_j \longrightarrow \coprod_{i \in I} X_i, \qquad \iota_j(x) = (x, j
).$$

This is a well-defined function since $(x,j) \in X_j \times \{j\} \subseteq \cop
rod_{i \in I} X_i$.

## The Universal Property to Verify

For every set $Y$ and every family of functions $\{f_i : X_i \to Y\}_{i \in I}$,
 there exists a **unique** function $f : \coprod_{i \in I} X_i \to Y$ such that
the following diagram commutes for every $j \in I$:

$$f \circ \iota_j = f_j \quad \text{for all } j \in I.$$

## Existence of the Mediating Morphism

Given a family $\{f_i : X_i \to Y\}_{i \in I}$, **define** $f : \coprod_{i \in I
} X_i \to Y$ by

$$f(x, j) := f_j(x).$$

**Well-definedness.** Every element of $\coprod_{i \in I} X_i$ has the form $(x,
 j)$ for a *unique* index $j \in I$ and a *unique* element $x \in X_j$. The uniq
ueness of $j$ comes from the fact that the sets $X_i \times \{i\}$ are pairwise
disjoint (since the tags $\{i\}$ are distinct singletons): if $(x, j) = (x', j')
$, then $j = j'$ and $x = x'$. Hence the value $f_j(x)$ is unambiguously determi
ned — there is no conflict between different indices. So $f$ is a well-defined f
unction.

**Commutativity.** For any $j \in I$ and any $x \in X_j$:

$$(f \circ \iota_j)(x) = f(\iota_j(x)) = f(x, j) = f_j(x).$$

Thus $f \circ \iota_j = f_j$ for every $j \in I$, as required.

## Uniqueness of the Mediating Morphism

Suppose $g : \coprod_{i \in I} X_i \to Y$ is any function satisfying $g \circ \i
ota_j = f_j$ for every $j \in I$. We show $g = f$.

Let $(x, j) \in \coprod_{i \in I} X_i$ be arbitrary. Then $x \in X_j$, and

$$g(x, j) = g(\iota_j(x)) = (g \circ \iota_j)(x) = f_j(x) = f(x, j).$$

Since $(x,j)$ was arbitrary, $g = f$ on all of $\coprod_{i \in I} X_i$. Hence th
e mediating morphism is **unique**.

## Conclusion

We have exhibited:

1. An object $\coprod_{i \in I} X_i$ in **Set**,
2. A family of injection morphisms $\iota_j : X_j \to \coprod_{i \in I} X_i$,
3. For every cocone $(Y, \{f_i\}_{i \in I})$, a **unique** mediating morphism $f
 : \coprod_{i \in I} X_i \to Y$ with $f \circ \iota_j = f_j$ for all $j \in I$.

This is precisely the universal property of the coproduct. Therefore the disjoin
t union $\coprod_{i \in I} X_i$, equipped with the canonical injections $\{\iota
_j\}_{j \in I}$, **is the coproduct** of the family $\{X_i\}_{i \in I}$ in the c
ategory **Set**. $\blacksquare$
