**Answer: Yes.** Every analytic (holomorphic) map $f: \hat{\mathbb{C}} \to \hat{
\mathbb{C}}$ is a rational function.

**Proof.**

Let $f: \hat{\mathbb{C}} \to \hat{\mathbb{C}}$ be holomorphic. We consider two c
ases.

**Case 1: $f$ is constant.** A constant map is rational (a constant polynomial $
c \in \mathbb{C}$, or the constant $\infty$, which is the degenerate rational fu
nction $P/Q$ with $Q \equiv 0$).

**Case 2: $f$ is non-constant.** We view $f$ as a meromorphic function on $\hat{
\mathbb{C}}$: at every point $p$ with $f(p) \neq \infty$, $f$ is holomorphic int
o $\mathbb{C}$; at every point $p$ with $f(p) = \infty$, $f$ has a pole.

Since $f$ is non-constant and holomorphic, the fiber $f^{-1}(\infty)$ is a discr
ete subset of $\hat{\mathbb{C}}$ (the preimage of a point under a non-constant h
olomorphic map between Riemann surfaces is discrete). By compactness of $\hat{\m
athbb{C}}$, a discrete subset is finite. Write

$$f^{-1}(\infty) = \{a_1, \dots, a_{n'}\} \cup (\{\infty\} \text{ if } \infty \t
ext{ is a pole}),$$

where $a_1, \dots, a_{n'} \in \mathbb{C}$ are the finite poles with respective o
rders $m_1, \dots, m_{n'}$.

For each finite pole $a_i$, the principal part of $f$ at $a_i$ is

$$P_i(z) = \sum_{j=1}^{m_i} \frac{c_{i,j}}{(z - a_i)^j}.$$

If $\infty$ is a pole of order $m_0$, the principal part at $\infty$ is a polyno
mial in $z$ of degree $m_0$:

$$P_\infty(z) = \sum_{j=1}^{m_0} d_j\, z^j.$$

(If $\infty$ is not a pole, set $P_\infty \equiv 0$.)

Define

$$h(z) := f(z) - \sum_{i=1}^{n'} P_i(z) - P_\infty(z).$$

At each finite pole $a_i$, subtracting $P_i$ cancels the singular part, so $h$ i
s holomorphic at $a_i$. At $\infty$, subtracting $P_\infty$ cancels the pole (if
 any), so $h$ extends holomorphically to $\infty$ with a finite value. At all ot
her points, both $f$ and the principal parts are holomorphic. Thus

$$h: \hat{\mathbb{C}} \to \mathbb{C}$$

is a holomorphic function (taking only finite values) on the compact Riemann sph
ere. By compactness, $|h|$ attains its maximum; the maximum modulus principle th
en forces $h$ to be constant, say $h \equiv c \in \mathbb{C}$. (Equivalently: $h
$ is entire on $\mathbb{C}$ and extends holomorphically to $\infty$, so it is bo
unded, hence constant by Liouville's theorem.)

Therefore

$$f(z) = c + \sum_{i=1}^{n'} P_i(z) + P_\infty(z),$$

which is a sum of a constant, finitely many rational functions of the form $\fra
c{c_{i,j}}{(z - a_i)^j}$, and a polynomial. Combining these over a common denomi
nator yields $f(z) = \frac{A(z)}{B(z)}$ for polynomials $A, B$ with $B \not\equi
v 0$. Hence $f$ is a rational function. $\blacksquare$
