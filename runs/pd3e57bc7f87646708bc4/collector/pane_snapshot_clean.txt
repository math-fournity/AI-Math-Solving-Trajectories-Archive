## Proof

**Setup.** We count non-negative integers $n \le 10^k$ whose decimal digits all
lie in $\{0,1,2,7\}$ and with $3 \mid n$.

**Reducing to digit strings.** The number $10^k = 1\underbrace{00\cdots0}_{k}$ h
as all digits in $\{0,1,2,7\}$, but $10^k \equiv 1 \pmod 3$, so it is **not** di
visible by 3 and contributes nothing. Every number in $[0,\,10^k-1]$ whose digit
s lie in $\{0,1,2,7\}$ corresponds bijectively to a length-$k$ string over $\{0,
1,2,7\}$ (pad with leading zeros), and conversely every such string gives a numb
er $<10^k$ (the largest is $\underbrace{77\cdots7}_{k} < 10^k$). The number $0$
itself corresponds to the all-zero string. Hence the desired count equals the nu
mber of length-$k$ strings over $\{0,1,2,7\}$ whose digit sum is $\equiv 0 \pmod
 3$.

**Residues of the digits.** Modulo 3:
$$0\mapsto 0,\quad 1\mapsto 1,\quad 2\mapsto 2,\quad 7\mapsto 1.$$
So at each position the residue added is $0$ (one choice), $1$ (two choices: dig
its $1,7$), or $2$ (one choice: digit $2$).

**Recurrence.** Let $a_n,b_n,c_n$ be the counts of length-$n$ strings with digit
 sum $\equiv 0,1,2\pmod 3$. Appending one digit shifts the residue class by $0$
(1 way), by $1$ (2 ways), or by $2$ (1 way), giving
$$
\begin{pmatrix}a_{n+1}\\ b_{n+1}\\ c_{n+1}\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}1&1&2\\ 2&1&1\\ 1&2&1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}a_n\\ b_n\\ c_n\end{pmatrix},
\qquad (a_0,b_0,c_0)=(1,0,0).
$$
(Empty string has sum $0$.) One checks $a_n+b_n+c_n=4^n$, as expected.

**Diagonalization.** With $t=1-\lambda$, the characteristic polynomial of $M$ is
$$\det(M-\lambda I)=t^3-6t+9=(t+3)(t^2-3t+3).$$
Thus $t\in\{-3,\,\tfrac{3+i\sqrt3}{2},\,\tfrac{3-i\sqrt3}{2}\}$, and the eigenva
lues of $M$ are
$$\lambda_1=4,\qquad \lambda_2=\tfrac{-1-i\sqrt3}{2}=\omega^2,\qquad \lambda_3=\
tfrac{-1+i\sqrt3}{2}=\omega,$$
where $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$ is a primitive cube root of unity. Therefore
$$a_k=\alpha\cdot 4^k+\beta\,\omega^k+\gamma\,\omega^{2k}.$$

**Determining constants.** Directly: $a_0=1$, $a_1=1$ (only digit $0$), $a_2=5$.
 By the cyclic symmetry of the three residue classes under the digit set, $\beta
=\gamma$. Then
$$a_0=\alpha+2\beta=1,\qquad a_1=4\alpha+\beta(\omega+\omega^2)=4\alpha-\beta=1.
$$
Solving: $\alpha=\tfrac13$, $\beta=\tfrac13$, and $a_0=\tfrac13+\tfrac23=1$ ✓. H
ence
$$a_k=\frac{4^k+\omega^k+\omega^{2k}}{3}.$$

**Evaluating the root-of-unity sum.** Using $1+\omega+\omega^2=0$:
$$\omega^k+\omega^{2k}=\begin{cases}2 & k\equiv 0\pmod 3,\\ -1 & k\not\equiv 0\p
mod 3.\end{cases}$$

**Integrality check.** Since $4\equiv 1\pmod 3$, we have $4^k\equiv 1\pmod 3$, s
o both $4^k+2$ and $4^k-1$ are divisible by $3$; the formula always yields an in
teger.

**Verification.**
- $k=1$: digits $\{0,1,2,7\}$, only $0$ is divisible by $3$ → $a_1=1=(4-1)/3$ ✓.
- $k=2$: $a_2=(16-1)/3=5$ ✓ (matches the recurrence $a_2=1+2+2\cdot1=5$).
- $k=3$: $a_3=(64+2)/3=22$ ✓ (recurrence: $a_3=5+5+2\cdot6=22$).

**Answer.** The number of non-negative integers $n\le 10^k$ satisfying (i) and (
ii) is

$$\boxed{\begin{cases}\dfrac{4^k+2}{3}, & 3\mid k,\\[6pt]\dfrac{4^k-1}{3}, & 3\n
mid k.\end{cases}}$$
