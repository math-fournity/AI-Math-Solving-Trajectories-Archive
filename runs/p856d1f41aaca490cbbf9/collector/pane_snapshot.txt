Let $Y_n = \sum_{k=1}^{n} X_{2k-1}$ (sum of $n$ Rademachers) and $Z_n = \sum_{k=
1}^{n} X_{2k}$ (sum of $n$ Cauchys). $Y_n$ and $Z_n$ are independent, and
$$\frac{X_1+\cdots+X_{2n}}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{Y_n}{\sqrt{n}} + \frac{Z_n}{\sqrt{n
}}.$$

**Key fact (Cauchy stability).** The Cauchy distribution is strictly stable: the
 sum of $n$ i.i.d. standard Cauchy random variables has the same law as $n\cdot
C$, where $C$ is a single standard Cauchy. Hence $Z_n \stackrel{d}{=} nC$, and
$$\frac{Z_n}{\sqrt{n}} \stackrel{d}{=} \sqrt{n}\,C.$$

**Reduction.** Writing $W_n := Y_n/\sqrt{n}$, condition on $Y_n$:
$$P\!\left(\frac{S_{2n}}{\sqrt{n}} \ge 2\right) = P\!\left(\sqrt{n}\,C + W_n \ge
 2\right) = P\!\left(C \ge \frac{2 - W_n}{\sqrt{n}}\right).$$

Define $A_n := \dfrac{2 - W_n}{\sqrt{n}} = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{n}} - \dfrac{Y_n}{n}.
$ Using the Cauchy tail $P(C \ge t) = \dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{1}{\pi}\arctan(t)$,
$$P\!\left(\frac{S_{2n}}{\sqrt{n}} \ge 2\right) = E\!\left[\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1
}{\pi}\arctan(A_n)\right] = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{\pi}\,E[\arctan(A_n)].$$

**Bounding $E[\arctan(A_n)] \to 0$.** Using $|\arctan(t)| \le |t|$,
$$|E[\arctan(A_n)]| \le E|A_n| \le \frac{2}{\sqrt{n}} + \frac{E|Y_n|}{n}.$$

Since $Y_n$ is a sum of $n$ i.i.d. Rademacher variables with $\operatorname{Var}
(Y_n)=n$, Jensen gives
$$E|Y_n| \le \sqrt{E[Y_n^2]} = \sqrt{n}.$$

Therefore
$$E|A_n| \le \frac{2}{\sqrt{n}} + \frac{\sqrt{n}}{n} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{n}} \xrigh
tarrow{n\to\infty} 0.$$

Hence $E[\arctan(A_n)] \to 0$, and

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} P\!\left(\frac{X_1+\cdots+X_{2n}}{\sqrt{n}} \ge 2\right) = \
frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{\pi}\cdot 0 = \boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}}.$$

**Intuition.** The Cauchy component $Z_n/\sqrt{n} \stackrel{d}{=} \sqrt{n}\,C$ h
as scale $\sqrt{n}\to\infty$; it spreads out and dominates the bounded-in-distri
bution Rademacher component $Y_n/\sqrt{n}=O_p(1)$. A Cauchy of diverging scale p
laces essentially all its mass symmetrically, so the threshold $2$ becomes negli
gible and the probability tends to $\tfrac12$.
