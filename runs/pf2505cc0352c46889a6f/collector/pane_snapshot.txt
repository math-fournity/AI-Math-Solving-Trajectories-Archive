## Problem

Let $V$ be an infinite-dimensional vector space over $\mathbb{F} \in \{\mathbb{R
}, \mathbb{C}\}$, with $\dim V = \alpha$ (an infinite cardinal). Determine $\dim
 V^*$, the algebraic dual.

## Claim

$$\dim V^* = |\mathbb{F}|^{\alpha} = 2^{\alpha}.$$

## Proof

**Step 1: Identify $V^*$ with a function space.**

Let $B$ be a basis of $V$, so $|B| = \alpha$ and $V \cong \bigoplus_{B} \mathbb{
F}$ (the direct sum, i.e., finitely-supported functions $B \to \mathbb{F}$). By
the universal property of the direct sum (coproduct),

$$V^* = \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}\!\Bigl(\bigoplus_{B} \mathbb{F},\, \math
bb{F}\Bigr) \;\cong\; \prod_{B} \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{F}, \mat
hbb{F}) \;\cong\; \prod_{B} \mathbb{F} \;=\; \mathbb{F}^{B},$$

the set of **all** functions $B \to \mathbb{F}$ with pointwise operations. Concr
etely: a linear functional is uniquely determined by its (completely arbitrary)
values on the basis $B$, and every choice of values extends uniquely to a linear
 functional. Hence

$$|V^*| = |\mathbb{F}|^{|B|} = |\mathbb{F}|^{\alpha}.$$

**Step 2: Compute $|V^*|$ for $\mathbb{F} = \mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$.**

Both $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$ have cardinality $|\mathbb{F}| = 2^{\aleph_{0
}}$ (the continuum). Since $\alpha$ is an infinite cardinal,

$$|V^*| = (2^{\aleph_{0}})^{\alpha} = 2^{\aleph_{0} \cdot \alpha} = 2^{\alpha},$
$

using $\aleph_{0} \cdot \alpha = \alpha$ for infinite $\alpha$.

**Step 3: A lemma on cardinality vs. dimension.**

*Lemma.* Let $W$ be a vector space over $\mathbb{F}$ with $\dim W = \beta$, wher
e $\beta$ is an infinite cardinal. Then $|W| = \max(\beta, |\mathbb{F}|)$.

*Proof of lemma.* Fix a basis $S$ of $W$ with $|S| = \beta$. Every element of $W
$ is a **finite** $\mathbb{F}$-linear combination of basis vectors. The number o
f finite subsets of $S$ is $\beta$ (since $\beta$ is infinite), and each finite
subset of size $n$ supports $|\mathbb{F}|^{n}$ combinations. Thus

$$|W| = \sum_{n < \omega} \binom{\beta}{n} \cdot |\mathbb{F}|^{n} = \beta \cdot
|\mathbb{F}| = \max(\beta, |\mathbb{F}|). \qquad \square$$

**Step 4: Recover $\dim V^*$ from $|V^*|$.**

Set $\beta = \dim V^*$ (which is infinite, since $V^* \supseteq$ the span of the
 coordinate functionals, already of dimension $\alpha$, and in fact larger). App
lying the lemma to $W = V^*$:

$$|V^*| = \max(\beta,\, |\mathbb{F}|) = 2^{\alpha}.$$

Because $\alpha \geq \aleph_{0}$, we have $2^{\alpha} \geq 2^{\aleph_{0}} = |\ma
thbb{F}|$. Therefore $|\mathbb{F}| \leq 2^{\alpha}$, and the equation $\max(\bet
a, |\mathbb{F}|) = 2^{\alpha}$ forces

$$\beta = 2^{\alpha}.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\dim V^* = |\mathbb{F}|^{\alpha} = 2^{\alpha}.}$$

The first equality holds for any field $\mathbb{F}$ with $|\mathbb{F}|^{\alpha}
\geq |\mathbb{F}|$ (automatic for infinite $\alpha$); the second specializes to
$\mathbb{F} = \mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$ using $|\mathbb{F}| = 2^{\aleph_{0}}$.
