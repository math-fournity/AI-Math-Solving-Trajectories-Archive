Proof

Suppose $G/Z(G)$ is cyclic. Then there exists $g \in G$ such that

$$G/Z(G) = \langle, gZ(G), \rangle.$$

Hence every coset of $Z(G)$ in $G$ is of the form $(gZ(G))^k = g^k Z(G)$ for
some integer $k$. Therefore every element $x \in G$ lies in some coset $g^k
Z(G)$, so we may write

$$x = g^k z$$

for some integer $k$ and some $z \in Z(G)$.

Now take any two elements $x, y \in G$. Write $x = g^k z$ and $y = g^\ell w$
with $z, w \in Z(G)$. Since $z, w$ are central, they commute with every element
of $G$, in particular with $g^k$ and $g^\ell$. Thus

$$xy = (g^k z)(g^\ell w) = g^k g^\ell z w = g^{k+\ell} z w,$$

and

$$yx = (g^\ell w)(g^k z) = g^\ell g^k w z = g^{k+\ell} z w.$$

So $xy = yx$. Since $x, y$ were arbitrary, $G$ is abelian.

An abelian group equals its own center: every element commutes with every other
element, so every element is central, i.e., $G \subseteq Z(G)$. Since $Z(G)
\subseteq G$ always holds, we conclude

$$G = Z(G). \qquad \blacksquare$$
