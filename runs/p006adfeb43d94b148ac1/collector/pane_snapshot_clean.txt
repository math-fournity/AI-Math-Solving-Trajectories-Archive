## Proof

We show that no tiling of the $10\times 10$ board by T-tetrominoes exists, so th

**Checkerboard coloring.** Color the $10\times 10$ board in the usual checkerboa
rd pattern, so each square is black or white according to the parity of $i+j$. T
he board has $50$ white and $50$ black squares.

**Key fact.** Every T-tetromino, regardless of orientation or position, covers e
xactly $3$ squares of one color and $1$ square of the other.

*Proof of key fact.* A T-tetromino consists of three collinear squares plus one
square attached to the middle of that line. In the line of three, adjacent squar
es have opposite colors, so the line contributes colors $(A, B, A)$ for some col
ors $A, B$ — that is, $2$ of color $A$ and $1$ of color $B$. The attached square
 is edge-adjacent to the middle square (color $B$), hence has color $A$. Thus th
e tetromino covers $3$ squares of color $A$ and $1$ of color $B$. $\square$

**Counting.** Suppose a tiling by $25$ T-tetrominoes exists. Let $k$ be the numb
er of tetrominoes covering $3$ white squares (and $1$ black), and $25-k$ the num
ber covering $3$ black (and $1$ white). The total number of white squares covere
d is
$$3k + 1\cdot(25-k) = 2k + 25,$$
and the total number of black squares covered is
$$1\cdot k + 3(25-k) = 75 - 2k.$$

For a complete covering of the board we need both totals to equal $50$:
$$2k + 25 = 50 \implies k = 12.5.$$

But $k$ must be an integer, since it counts tetrominoes. This is a contradiction
.

Equivalently, each T-tetromino contributes a color imbalance of $\pm 2$, so the
total imbalance of $25$ tetrominoes is an even integer. But the board has imbala
nce $0$, requiring $k = 25-k$, i.e. $k=12.5$, impossible since $25$ is odd.

Therefore the $10\times 10$ checkerboard cannot be tiled by T-tetrominoes at all
. The number of distinct complete coverings is

$$\boxed{0}.$$
