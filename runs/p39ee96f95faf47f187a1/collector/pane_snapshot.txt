$$M^n = \begin{pmatrix} a_{n+1} & a_n \\ a_n & a_{n-1} \end{pmatrix} \quad (n \g

Since $M^{m+n} = M^m M^n$, comparing the $(1,2)$-entries gives the **addition fo

$$a_{m+n} = a_{m+1}\,a_n + a_m\,a_{n-1}. \tag{1}$$

**Step 2: Consecutive terms are coprime.**

Taking determinants: $\det(M^n) = (\det M)^n = (-1)^n$, so
$$a_{n+1}\,a_{n-1} - a_n^2 = (-1)^n. \tag{2}$$
Any common divisor of $a_n$ and $a_{n+1}$ divides the left side $a_{n+1}a_{n-1}
- a_n^2 = \pm 1$, hence
$$\gcd(a_n,\, a_{n+1}) = 1 \quad \text{for all } n \ge 0. \tag{3}$$

**Step 3: Euclidean reduction — $\gcd(a_m, a_n) = a_{\gcd(m,n)}$.**

From (1): $a_{m+n} = a_{m+1}\,a_n + a_m\,a_{n-1}$, so modulo $a_m$:
$$a_{m+n} \equiv a_{m+1}\,a_n \pmod{a_m}.$$
By (3), $\gcd(a_{m+1}, a_m)=1$, so $a_{m+1}$ is invertible mod $a_m$, giving
$$\gcd(a_{m+n},\, a_m) = \gcd(a_n,\, a_m). \tag{4}$$

For $n > m$, write $n = m + (n-m)$ and apply (4):
$$\gcd(a_n,\, a_m) = \gcd(a_{n-m},\, a_m).$$
