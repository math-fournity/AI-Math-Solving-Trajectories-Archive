**Answer.** The solutions are exactly $f(n) = cn$ for a fixed positive integer $
c$.

**Verification.** If $f(n)=cn$, then $a+f(b)=a+cb$ and $a^{2}+bf(a)=a^{2}+bca=a(
a+cb)$, so $a+f(b)\mid a^{2}+bf(a)$ for all $a,b$. ✓

**Proof of uniqueness.** Let $f:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N$ satisfy
$$a+f(b)\mid a^{2}+bf(a)\qquad\text{whenever }a+b>C. \tag{$*$}$$

**Step 1 — Reformulation.** For $a+b>C$, since $a\equiv -f(b)\pmod{a+f(b)}$, we
have $a^{2}\equiv f(b)^{2}$, so $(*)$ is equivalent to
$$a+f(b)\mid bf(a)+f(b)^{2}. \tag{$\star$}$$

**Step 2 — Linear bounds on $f$.**
*Lower bound.* Taking $b=1$ in $(\star)$ (valid for $a>C-1$):
$$a+f(1)\mid f(a)+f(1)^{2}.$$
The left side is positive and divides the positive integer $f(a)+f(1)^{2}$, so $
f(a)+f(1)^{2}\ge a+f(1)$, i.e.
$$f(a)\ge a-K,\qquad K:=f(1)(f(1)-1)\ge 0.$$
In particular $f(a)\to\infty$.

*Upper bound.* Taking $a=1$ in $(*)$ (valid for $b>C-1$):
$$1+f(b)\mid 1+bf(1),$$
hence $f(b)\le bf(1)$ for all $b>C-1$.

**Step 3 — $f$ is eventually affine.** From the lower-bound relation, for every
$a>C-1$ there is an integer $u(a)\ge 1$ with
$$f(a)+f(1)^{2}=u(a)\bigl(a+f(1)\bigr). \tag{1}$$
The upper bound gives $u(a)(a+f(1))\le f(1)a+f(1)^{2}=f(1)(a+f(1))$, so
$$1\le u(a)\le f(1)\qquad(a>C-1).$$
Thus $u$ is bounded.

Since $f(a)\to\infty$, $f$ is not constant; choose $b_{0}$ with $f(b_{0})\ne f(1
)$. Apply $(\star)$ with this fixed $b_{0}$ (valid for $a>C-b_{0}$) and substitu
te (1):
$$b_{0}u(a)(a+f(1))-b_{0}f(1)^{2}+f(b_{0})^{2}\equiv 0\pmod{a+f(b_{0})}.$$
Because $a+f(1)\equiv f(1)-f(b_{0})\pmod{a+f(b_{0})}$, this yields
$$a+f(b_{0})\mid b_{0}\bigl(f(1)-f(b_{0})\bigr)\,u(a)-\bigl(b_{0}f(1)^{2}-f(b_{0
})^{2}\bigr). \tag{$\dagger$}$$
The right-hand side is bounded (as $u(a)\le f(1)$), while $a+f(b_{0})\to\infty$.
 Hence for all sufficiently large $a$ the right-hand side equals $0$, giving
$$u(a)=\frac{b_{0}f(1)^{2}-f(b_{0})^{2}}{b_{0}\bigl(f(1)-f(b_{0})\bigr)}=:c\quad
\text{(a constant, with }1\le c\le f(1)\text{)}.$$
So for all $a\ge A_{0}$ (some threshold, enlarged so that $2A_{0}>C$),
$$f(a)=c\bigl(a+f(1)\bigr)-f(1)^{2}=ca+r,\qquad r:=cf(1)-f(1)^{2}. \tag{2}$$

**Step 4 — The constant term vanishes.** Take $a,b\ge A_{0}$; then (2) applies t
o both and $a+b>C$. From $(*)$,
$$a+cb+r\mid a^{2}+b(ca+r)=a^{2}+cab+br.$$
Modulo $a+cb+r$: $a\equiv -(cb+r)$, so $a+cb\equiv -r$ and $a(a+cb)\equiv r(cb+r
)=cbr+r^{2}$. Hence
$$a+cb+r\mid cbr+r^{2}+br=r\bigl((c+1)b+r\bigr).$$
Fix $b\ge A_{0}$ and let $a\to\infty$: the divisor tends to $\infty$ while $r((c
+1)b+r)$ is fixed, so $r((c+1)b+r)=0$. Since $c\ge 1$ makes $(c+1)b+r>0$ for lar
ge $b$, we conclude $r=0$. Therefore
$$f(a)=ca\qquad\text{for all }a\ge A_{0}. \tag{3}$$

**Step 5 — Extension to all positive integers.** Let $a\in\mathbb N$ be arbitrar
y. For any $b\ge A_{0}$ with $a+b>C$, (3) gives $f(b)=cb$, and $(*)$ yields $a+c
b\mid a^{2}+bf(a)$. Multiplying by $c$ and reducing modulo $a+cb$ (using $cb\equ
iv -a$):
$$c\bigl(a^{2}+bf(a)\bigr)\equiv ca^{2}-af(a)=a\bigl(ca-f(a)\bigr)\pmod{a+cb}.$$
Thus $a+cb\mid a(ca-f(a))$. Let $g_{b}=\gcd(a,a+cb)=\gcd(a,cb)\le a$. Then $\fra
c{a+cb}{g_{b}}\mid ca-f(a)$. As $b\to\infty$ (over $b\ge A_{0}$),
$$\frac{a+cb}{g_{b}}\ge\frac{a+cb}{a}\longrightarrow\infty.$$
The fixed integer $ca-f(a)$ is divisible by arbitrarily large positive integers,
 so $ca-f(a)=0$; i.e. $f(a)=ca$.

**Conclusion.** Every solution is of the form $f(n)=cn$ for a fixed $c\in\mathbb
 N$, $c\ge 1$, and every such function works. $\blacksquare$
