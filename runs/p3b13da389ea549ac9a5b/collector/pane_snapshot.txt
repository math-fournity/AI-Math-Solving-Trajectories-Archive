(the geometric mean of eigenvalues).

The Minkowski determinant theorem states that for positive definite Hermitian $X

$$d(X+Y) \geq d(X) + d(Y),$$
with equality if and only if $X$ and $Y$ are positive scalar multiples of each o
ther.

Since $\det(ABC) = \det(A)\det(B)\det(C)$, we have $d(ABC) = d(A)\,d(B)\,d(C)$.
Applying Minkowski to each factor in the denominator:

$$d(I_n + 3A) \geq d(I_n) + d(3A) = 1 + 3\,d(A),$$
$$d(A + 8B) \geq d(A) + 8\,d(B),$$
$$d(B + 9C) \geq d(B) + 9\,d(C),$$
$$d(C + 6I_n) \geq d(C) + 6.$$

Setting $a = d(A),\; b = d(B),\; c = d(C) > 0$, the original expression is bound

$$g(a,b,c) = \frac{abc}{(1+3a)(a+8b)(b+9c)(c+6)}.$$

**Step 2: Maximize the scalar function.**

\infty$, the maximum is attained at an interior critical point. Setting partial

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial a}:\quad \frac{1}{a} - \frac{3}{1+3a} - \frac{1}{a+8b
