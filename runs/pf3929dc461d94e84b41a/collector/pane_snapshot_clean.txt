## Proof

We seek the leading-order asymptotic approximation of

$$I = \int_{-1}^{1} e^{x\,t^3}\,dt,\qquad x\to\infty.$$

### Step 1. Locate the dominant contribution

The phase function is $\phi(t)=t^3$. On $[-1,1]$:

$$\phi(1)=1,\qquad \phi(-1)=-1,\qquad \phi'(t)=3t^2,\qquad \phi'(1)=3>0.$$

The maximum of $\phi$ is attained at the **boundary point** $t=1$, and $\phi'(1)
=3\neq 0$. The contribution from $t=-1$ is $O(e^{-x})$, exponentially smaller th
an the $e^{x}$ contribution from $t=1$. Hence the entire leading-order behavior
comes from a neighborhood of $t=1$.

### Step 2. Boundary Laplace (Watson's lemma)

Set $t=1-u$ with $u\ge 0$. Then

$$t^3=(1-u)^3=1-3u+3u^2-u^3,$$

so

$$e^{x\,t^3}=e^{x}\,e^{-3xu+3xu^2-xu^3}.$$

The natural scale is $u\sim 1/x$ (set by the linear term $-3xu$). Substitute $u=
v/x$:

$$\int_{0}^{\delta} e^{x\,t^3}\,du = e^{x}\int_{0}^{\delta} e^{-3xu+3xu^2-xu^3}\
,du = \frac{e^{x}}{x}\int_{0}^{x\delta} e^{-3v+3v^2/x-v^3/x^2}\,dv.$$

As $x\to\infty$, the exponent $-3v+3v^2/x-v^3/x^2\to -3v$ pointwise, and the upp
er limit $x\delta\to\infty$. By dominated convergence (the integrand is dominate
d by $e^{-3v+O(v^2/x)}$ which for large $x$ is bounded by $e^{-2v}$ on the relev
ant range),

$$\int_{0}^{x\delta} e^{-3v+3v^2/x-v^3/x^2}\,dv \;\longrightarrow\; \int_{0}^{\i
nfty} e^{-3v}\,dv = \frac{1}{3}.$$

### Step 3. Remainder is negligible

The integral over $[-1,\,1-\delta]$ is bounded by $e^{x(1-\delta)^3}\cdot 2 = O\
!\big(e^{x(1-\delta)^3}\big)$. Since $(1-\delta)^3<1$, this is $o(e^x/x)$. The c
ontribution from $t=-1$ is $O(e^{-x})$, also negligible.

### Result

Combining Steps 2 and 3,

$$\boxed{I \sim \frac{e^{x}}{3x}\quad\text{as }x\to\infty.}$$
