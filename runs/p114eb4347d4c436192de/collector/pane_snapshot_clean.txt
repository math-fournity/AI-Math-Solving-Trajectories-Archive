**Answer: Yes, $a_n = 0$ for all $n \ge 1$.**

**Proof.**

Let $f(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n x^n$, and let $R \in [0,\infty]$ be its radi
us of convergence, given by $\frac{1}{R} = \limsup_{n\to\infty} |a_n|^{1/n}$.

**Step 1: The radius of convergence is infinite.**

A basic fact about power series: $\sum a_n x^n$ converges absolutely for $|x| <
R$ and diverges for $|x| > R$. By hypothesis, for every $x \in (0,\infty)$ the s
eries converges (its sum is $0$). If $R$ were finite, then choosing any $x > R$
(which is positive) would give a point in $(0,\infty)$ where the series diverges
 — contradicting the hypothesis. Hence $R \ge x$ for every $x > 0$, so $R = \inf
ty$.

**Step 2: $f$ is real-analytic on all of $\mathbb{R}$ and vanishes on an open in
terval.**

Since $R = \infty$, the series converges for every real $x$, and $f$ is real-ana
lytic on $\mathbb{R}$ (in fact, it extends to an entire function on $\mathbb{C}$
). By hypothesis $f(x) = 0$ for every $x \in (0,\infty)$.

**Step 3: All coefficients vanish.**

The function $f$ is real-analytic on $\mathbb{R}$ and identically zero on the op
en interval $(0,\infty)$. A real-analytic function that vanishes on an open set
vanishes identically on its entire connected domain (this follows from the ident
ity theorem: the zeros of a nonzero analytic function are isolated, but $(0,\inf
ty)$ contains non-isolated zeros). Therefore $f \equiv 0$ on $\mathbb{R}$.

Since $f(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n x^n$ is the Taylor expansion of $f$ about
$0$, the coefficients satisfy
$$a_n = \frac{f^{(n)}(0)}{n!} \quad (n \ge 1).$$
Because $f \equiv 0$, every derivative $f^{(n)}(0) = 0$, and thus
$$a_n = 0 \quad \text{for all } n \ge 1. \qquad \blacksquare$$
