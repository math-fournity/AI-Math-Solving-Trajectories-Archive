## Proof

Let $f(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{n} a_k x^k$ be a polynomial of degree $n$.

**Step 1: The growth condition forces $f$ to be constant.**

A non-constant polynomial of degree $n \geq 1$ satisfies $|f(x)| \to \infty$ as
$x \to \infty$. The condition that $f(x)$ approaches a constant value as $x \to
\infty$ therefore forces $n = 0$, i.e., $f(x) = a_0$ is a constant polynomial.

**Step 2: Apply the functional equation.**

With $f(x) = a_0$:
- $f(1/x) = a_0$ for all $x \neq 0$.
- $f''(x) = 0$ for all $x$.

The equation $f(1/x) = f''(x)$ gives $a_0 = 0$.
