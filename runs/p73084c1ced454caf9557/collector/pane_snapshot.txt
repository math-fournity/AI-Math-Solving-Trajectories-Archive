**Problem:** Let $L = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{5}, \omega)$, where $\omega = \frac{-1
+\sqrt{3}}{2}$, and determine $|G(L/\mathbb{Q})|$.

### Step 1: Degree of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{5})/\mathbb{Q}$

The polynomial $f(x) = x^3 - 5$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ by Eisenstein's
 criterion at $p = 5$. Hence $\sqrt[3]{5}$ has minimal polynomial $x^3 - 5$ over
 $\mathbb{Q}$, giving
$$[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{5}) : \mathbb{Q}] = 3.$$

### Step 2: Degree of $\mathbb{Q}(\omega)/\mathbb{Q}$

$\omega = \frac{-1+\sqrt{3}}{2}$ is a primitive cube root of unity, with minimal
 polynomial $x^2 + x + 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Thus
$$[\mathbb{Q}(\omega) : \mathbb{Q}] = 2.$$

### Step 3: The intersection $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{5}) \cap \mathbb{Q}(\omega)$

$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{5}) \subset \mathbb{R}$ since $\sqrt[3]{5}$ is real and all
 its elements are real. On the other hand, $\mathbb{Q}(\omega) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqr
t{-3})$, which contains non-real elements (e.g., $\omega$ itself). Therefore
$$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{5}) \cap \mathbb{Q}(\omega) \subset \mathbb{R} \cap \mathb
b{Q}(\sqrt{-3}) = \mathbb{Q}.$$

(Any element of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$ that is real must lie in $\mathbb{Q}$, s
ince $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}) = \{a + b\sqrt{-3} : a,b \in \mathbb{Q}\}$ and this
is real iff $b = 0$.)

So $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{5}) \cap \mathbb{Q}(\omega) = \mathbb{Q}$.

### Step 4: Degree of $L/\mathbb{Q}$

Since $\gcd(3, 2) = 1$ and the intersection is $\mathbb{Q}$,
$$[L : \mathbb{Q}] = [\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{5}, \omega) : \mathbb{Q}] = [\mathbb{Q
}(\sqrt[3]{5}) : \mathbb{Q}] \cdot [\mathbb{Q}(\omega) : \mathbb{Q}] = 3 \cdot 2
 = 6.$$

### Step 5: $L$ is the splitting field of $x^3 - 5$

The three roots of $x^3 - 5$ are $\sqrt[3]{5},\; \omega\sqrt[3]{5},\; \omega^2\s
qrt[3]{5}$, all of which lie in $L = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{5}, \omega)$. Conversel
y, $L$ is generated by these roots. Hence $L$ is the splitting field of the sepa
rable polynomial $x^3 - 5$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ (separable since $\mathbb{Q}$ has c
haracteristic $0$).

Therefore $L/\mathbb{Q}$ is a Galois extension, and
$$|G(L/\mathbb{Q})| = [L : \mathbb{Q}] = 6.$$
