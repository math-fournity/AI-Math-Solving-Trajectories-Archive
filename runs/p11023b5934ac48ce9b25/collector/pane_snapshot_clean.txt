## Setup

The sequence $\varepsilon_n$ is constant on dyadic blocks. For $j = 0, 1, 2, \ld
ots$, define **block $j$** as the index set $\{2^j, 2^j+1, \ldots, 2^{j+1}-1\}$
(of length $2^j$), on which $\varepsilon_n = (-1)^j$. Explicitly:

| $j$ | block | sign | length |
|-----|-------|------|--------|
| 0 | $\{1\}$ | $+1$ | 1 |
| 1 | $\{2,3\}$ | $-1$ | 2 |
| 2 | $\{4,\dots,7\}$ | $+1$ | 4 |
| 3 | $\{8,\dots,15\}$ | $-1$ | 8 |

For a positive decreasing $f$, set the **block sum**
$$B_j = (-1)^j a_j, \qquad a_j := \sum_{n=2^j}^{2^{j+1}-1} f(n) > 0.$$

The partial sum at the end of block $m$ is $T_m = \sum_{j=0}^{m} B_j$, and withi
n block $m+1$ the partial sums deviate from $T_m$ by at most $a_{m+1}$ (all term
s in a block share the same sign). Thus:

- If $a_j \not\to 0$, the series **diverges** (block sums $B_j \not\to 0$ forces
 $T_m$ to diverge, hence $S_N$ diverges).
- If $a_j \to 0$ and $a_j$ is eventually decreasing, then $\sum B_j = \sum (-1)^
j a_j$ converges by the **Leibniz alternating series test**, and the within-bloc
k excursions $a_{m+1}\to 0$ force the full sequence of partial sums $S_N$ to con

## Part (a): $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\varepsilon_n}{n}$ — **Dive
rges**

$$\int_{2^j}^{2^{j+1}} \frac{dx}{x} \;\le\; a_j \;\le\; \frac{1}{2^j} + \int_{2^
j}^{2^{j+1}} \frac{dx}{x},$$
$$\ln 2 \;\le\; a_j \;\le\; \frac{1}{2^j} + \ln 2.$$

Therefore
$$a_j \;\longrightarrow\; \ln 2 \neq 0.$$

The block sums satisfy $B_j = (-1)^j a_j \to \pm\ln 2 \neq 0$. In particular $B_

$$T_{2k+1} - T_{2k} = B_{2k+1} = -a_{2k+1} \;\longrightarrow\; -\ln 2,$$
so the subsequences $\{T_{2k}\}$ and $\{T_{2k+1}\}$ cannot share a common limit.
 Since a subsequence of $\{S_N\}$ diverges, the series **diverges**.

## Part (b): $\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{\varepsilon_n}{n\ln n}$ — *
*Converges**

Here $f(n) = 1/(n\ln n)$, decreasing for $n \ge 2$. We estimate $a_j$ for $j \ge
 1$ (so that $2^j \ge 2$):
$$\int_{2^j}^{2^{j+1}} \frac{dx}{x\ln x} \;\le\; a_j \;\le\; \frac{1}{2^j \ln(2^
j)} + \int_{2^j}^{2^{j+1}} \frac{dx}{x\ln x}.$$

The integral evaluates to
$$\int_{2^j}^{2^{j+1}} \frac{dx}{x\ln x} = \Big[\ln\ln x\Big]_{2^j}^{2^{j+1}} =
\ln\!\big((j+1)\ln 2\big) - \ln\!\big(j\ln 2\big) = \ln\!\frac{j+1}{j} = \ln\!\B
ig(1+\frac{1}{j}\Big).$$

Using $\ln(1+1/j) = 1/j - 1/(2j^2) + O(1/j^3)$ and the bound $\frac{1}{2^j \ln(2
^j)} = \frac{1}{2^j \, j\ln 2} = O(2^{-j}/j)$, we obtain the asymptotic
$$a_j = \frac{1}{j} - \frac{1}{2j^2} + O\!\left(\frac{1}{j^3}\right).$$

**Two consequences:**

1. **$a_j \to 0$**: immediate from $a_j \sim 1/j$.

2. **$a_j$ is eventually decreasing**: from the asymptotic,
$$a_j - a_{j+1} = \left(\frac{1}{j}-\frac{1}{j+1}\right) - \left(\frac{1}{2j^2}-
\frac{1}{2(j+1)^2}\right) + O\!\left(\frac{1}{j^3}\right) = \frac{1}{j(j+1)} - \
frac{2j+1}{2j^2(j+1)^2} + O\!\left(\frac{1}{j^3}\right) \sim \frac{1}{j^2} > 0$$
for all sufficiently large $j$.

Since $\{a_j\}$ is positive, eventually decreasing, and tends to $0$, the **Leib
niz alternating series test** gives that
$$\sum_{j=1}^{\infty} B_j = \sum_{j=1}^{\infty} (-1)^j a_j \quad \text{converges
.}$$

**Lifting to the original series.** Let $L = \sum_{j=1}^{\infty} B_j$ and let $S
_N = \sum_{n=2}^{N} \varepsilon_n/(n\ln n)$. For $N$ lying in block $m+1$ (i.e.,
 $2^{m+1} \le N \le 2^{m+2}-1$),
$$S_N = T_m + \sum_{n=2^{m+1}}^{N} \frac{\varepsilon_n}{n\ln n}, \qquad T_m := \
sum_{j=1}^{m} B_j \to L.$$
All terms in the tail have sign $(-1)^{m+1}$, so
$$\left|S_N - T_m\right| \le a_{m+1} \longrightarrow 0.$$
Therefore $S_N \to L$, and the series **converges** (conditionally, since $\sum
1/(n\ln n)$ diverges by the integral test).
