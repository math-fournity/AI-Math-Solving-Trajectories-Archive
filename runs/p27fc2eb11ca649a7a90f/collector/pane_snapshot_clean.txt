## Proof

Let $T: L^2(\mathbb R) \to L^2(\mathbb R)$ be defined by $T(f) = f * g$, where $
g \ge 0$, $g \in L^1(\mathbb R)$.

### Linearity

For $a,b \in \mathbb C$ and $f,h \in L^2$,
$$T(af+bh) = (af+bh)*g = a(f*g) + b(h*g) = a\,T(f) + b\,T(h),$$
so $T$ is linear.

### Upper bound: $\|T\| \le \|g\|_1$

We use the Fourier transform $\widehat{\cdot}$ with the convention $\widehat{f}(
\xi) = \int_{\mathbb R} f(x) e^{-2\pi i x\xi}\,dx$. By the convolution theorem,
$$\widehat{f*g} = \widehat{f}\,\widehat{g}.$$
Since $g \in L^1$, $\widehat{g}$ is bounded and
$$|\widehat{g}(\xi)| \le \int_{\mathbb R} |g(x)|\,dx = \|g\|_1 \quad \text{for a
ll } \xi.$$
By Plancherel's theorem, $\|\widehat{f}\|_2 = \|f\|_2$ and $\|\widehat{f*g}\|_2
= \|f*g\|_2$. Hence
$$\|T(f)\|_2 = \|f*g\|_2 = \|\widehat{f}\,\widehat{g}\|_2 \le \|\widehat{g}\|_\i
nfty \|\widehat{f}\|_2 \le \|g\|_1\,\|f\|_2.$$
Therefore $\|T\| \le \|g\|_1$.

### Lower bound: $\|T\| \ge \|g\|_1$

Since $g \ge 0$, $\widehat{g}$ is continuous (as $g \in L^1$) and
$$\widehat{g}(0) = \int_{\mathbb R} g(x)\,dx = \|g\|_1.$$

For each $n \ge 1$, define $\widehat{f_n}(\xi) = \sqrt{\tfrac{n}{2}}\,\mathbf 1_
{[-1/n,\,1/n]}(\xi)$. Then
$$\|\widehat{f_n}\|_2^2 = \tfrac{n}{2}\cdot \tfrac{2}{n} = 1,$$
so by Plancherel, $\|f_n\|_2 = 1$.

Now
$$\|T(f_n)\|_2 = \|\widehat{f_n}\,\widehat{g}\|_2 = \left(\int_{-1/n}^{1/n} \tfr
ac{n}{2}\,|\widehat{g}(\xi)|^2\,d\xi\right)^{1/2}.$$

Since $\widehat{g}$ is continuous at $0$ with $\widehat{g}(0) = \|g\|_1$, for ev
ery $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $N$ such that for all $n \ge N$ and all $\xi
\in [-1/n, 1/n]$,
$$|\widehat{g}(\xi)| \ge \|g\|_1 - \varepsilon.$$
Therefore
$$\|T(f_n)\|_2 \ge (\|g\|_1 - \varepsilon)\left(\int_{-1/n}^{1/n} \tfrac{n}{2}\,
d\xi\right)^{1/2} = (\|g\|_1 - \varepsilon)\cdot 1 = \|g\|_1 - \varepsilon.$$

Since $\|f_n\|_2 = 1$, this gives $\|T\| \ge \|g\|_1 - \varepsilon$ for every $\
varepsilon > 0$, hence $\|T\| \ge \|g\|_1$.

### Conclusion

Combining both inequalities,
$$\boxed{\|T\| = \|g\|_1}.$$
