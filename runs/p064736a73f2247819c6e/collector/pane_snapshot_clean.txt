## Proof

**Setup.** Let $A(x) \in \mathbb{R}_{+}^{n \times n}$ be a nonnegative irreducib
le matrix whose every entry $A_{ij}(x)$ is a posynomial in the variable $x = (x_
1, \dots, x_m) \in \mathbb{R}_{++}^m$. We are given posynomial inequality constr
aints $g_k(x) \le 1$, $k = 1, \dots, K$, where each $g_k$ is a posynomial. The g
oal is to

$$
\min_{x \succ 0} \; \rho\!\big(A(x)\big) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad g_k(x) \le 1,\;
 k=1,\dots,K,
$$

where $\rho(\cdot)$ denotes the spectral radius (Perron–Frobenius eigenvalue).

---

**Key tool — Collatz–Wielandt characterization.** For a nonnegative irreducible
matrix $A$, the Perron–Frobenius eigenvalue satisfies

$$
\rho(A) = \min_{u \in \mathbb{R}_{++}^n}\; \max_{i=1,\dots,n}\; \frac{(Au)_i}{u_
i}.
$$

Equivalently, for any $t > 0$,

$$
\rho(A) \le t \;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\; \exists\, u \in \mathbb{R}_{++}^n \tex
t{ such that } Au \le t\,u,
$$

i.e. $\sum_{j} A_{ij} u_j \le t\,u_i$ for every $i = 1,\dots,n$.

---

**Reformulation.** Introducing an auxiliary scalar $t > 0$ and an auxiliary posi
tive vector $u \in \mathbb{R}_{++}^n$, the original problem is equivalent to

$$
\begin{aligned}
\min_{x \succ 0,\; u \succ 0,\; t > 0}\quad & t \\
\text{s.t.}\quad & \sum_{j=1}^n A_{ij}(x)\,u_j \;\le\; t\,u_i, && i = 1,\dots,n,
 \\
& g_k(x) \;\le\; 1, && k = 1,\dots,K.
\end{aligned}
$$

*Equivalence argument.*
$(\Rightarrow)$ If $\bar x$ is feasible for the original problem with value $\rh
o(A(\bar x))$, set $t = \rho(A(\bar x))$ and let $u > 0$ be the Perron eigenvect
or of $A(\bar x)$; then $A(\bar x)u = t\,u$, so all constraints hold.
$(\Leftarrow)$ Any feasible $(x, u, t)$ of the reformulation satisfies $A(x)u \l
e t\,u$, hence by Collatz–Wielandt $\rho(A(x)) \le t$. Since $g_k(x) \le 1$, $x$
 is feasible for the original problem with objective value $\le t$. Minimizing $
t$ therefore yields exactly $\min \rho(A(x))$.

---

**Why this is a geometric program.** Recall a GP in standard form is

$$
\min \; f_0(z) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad f_\ell(z) \le 1,
$$

where $f_0$ is a monomial and each $f_\ell$ is a posynomial, over $z \succ 0$. W
e verify each piece with variables $z = (x, u, t) \in \mathbb{R}_{++}^{m+n+1}$:

1. **Objective.** $t$ is a monomial in $z$. ✓

2. **Spectral-radius constraints.** Each $A_{ij}(x)$ is a posynomial in $x$. The
 product $A_{ij}(x)\,u_j$ is a posynomial in $(x, u_j)$ (a posynomial times a mo
nomial is a posynomial). The sum $\sum_j A_{ij}(x)\,u_j$ is a posynomial in $(x,
 u)$. The right-hand side $t\,u_i$ is a monomial in $(t, u_i)$. Dividing both si
des by the monomial $t\,u_i$ gives

$$
\frac{1}{t\,u_i}\sum_{j=1}^n A_{ij}(x)\,u_j \;\le\; 1,
$$

   and a posynomial divided by a monomial is a posynomial. So each of the $n$ co
nstraints is a posynomial inequality. ✓

3. **Original constraints.** Each $g_k(x) \le 1$ is already a posynomial inequal
ity in $x$, hence in $z$. ✓

All constraints are posynomial inequalities and the objective is a monomial, so
the reformulation is a geometric program in standard form.

---

**Resulting GP.** The geometric program is:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\min_{x \succ 0,\; u \succ 0,\; t > 0}\quad & t \\
\text{s.t.}\quad & \sum_{j=1}^n A_{ij}(x)\,u_j \;\le\; t\,u_i, \qquad i = 1,\dot
s,n, \\
& g_k(x) \;\le\; 1, \qquad k = 1,\dots,K.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

After a log-change of variables ($x_i = e^{y_i}$, $u_i = e^{v_i}$, $t = e^{\tau}
$) it becomes a convex optimization problem, solvable globally by standard GP so
lvers. The optimal $x^\star$ recovers the minimizer of the original spectral-rad
ius problem, and $t^\star = \rho(A(x^\star))$.
