## Proof

The operator $T$ acts on the representation space $V_\rho$ of the irreducible re
presentation $\rho$. We compute its trace directly using linearity of the trace.

**Step 1: Linearity of trace.**

$$\operatorname{tr}(T) = \operatorname{tr}\!\left(\frac{1}{\#G}\sum_{g\in G}\phi
(g^{-1})\,\rho_g\right) = \frac{1}{\#G}\sum_{g\in G}\phi(g^{-1})\,\operatorname{
tr}(\rho_g).$$

**Step 2: Identify the trace of $\rho_g$.**

By definition, the character of the representation $\rho$ is $\chi_\rho(g) = \op
eratorname{tr}(\rho_g)$. Substituting:

$$\operatorname{tr}(T) = \frac{1}{\#G}\sum_{g\in G}\phi(g^{-1})\,\chi_\rho(g).$$

**Step 3: Recognize the inner product of class functions.**

The standard inner product on class functions of a finite group $G$ (over a fiel
d $k$ of characteristic zero) is

$$(\phi, \psi) = \frac{1}{\#G}\sum_{g\in G}\phi(g^{-1})\,\psi(g).$$

Since $\phi$ is a class function and $\chi_\rho$ is a character (hence also a cl
ass function), the sum above is exactly this inner product:

$$\operatorname{tr}(T) = (\phi,\, \chi_\rho).$$

**Step 4: Apply the hypothesis.**

The problem states $(\phi, \chi_\rho) = 0$. Therefore

$$\operatorname{tr}(T) = 0.$$

$\blacksquare$
