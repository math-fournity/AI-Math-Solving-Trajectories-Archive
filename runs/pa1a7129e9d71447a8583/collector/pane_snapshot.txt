**Answer: Yes, $A$ and $B$ commute.**

**Proof.** Assume $e^{xA}e^{yB} = e^{yB}e^{xA}$ for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$.

The matrix exponential is an entire (hence smooth) function of its scalar parame
ter, so both sides of the identity are smooth functions of $x$ and $y$. We may t
herefore differentiate.

**Step 1.** Differentiate the identity with respect to $x$:
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\bigl(e^{xA}e^{yB}\bigr) = \frac{\partial}{\partial
 x}\bigl(e^{yB}e^{xA}\bigr).$$
Using $\frac{d}{dx}e^{xA} = Ae^{xA}$, this gives
$$Ae^{xA}e^{yB} = e^{yB}Ae^{xA}.$$
Setting $x = 0$ (so $e^{0\cdot A} = I$):
$$Ae^{yB} = e^{yB}A \qquad \text{for all } y \in \mathbb{R}. \tag{1}$$

**Step 2.** Differentiate (1) with respect to $y$:
$$\frac{d}{dy}\bigl(Ae^{yB}\bigr) = \frac{d}{dy}\bigl(e^{yB}A\bigr),$$
$$ABe^{yB} = Be^{yB}A.$$
Setting $y = 0$ (so $e^{0\cdot B} = I$):
$$AB = BA.$$

Thus $A$ and $B$ commute. $\blacksquare$
