Looking at this problem, I need to find all integer pairs $(a,b)$ such that $x^2
+ax+b$ divides some monic polynomial with all non-leading coefficients in $\{+1,
-1\}$.

## Proof

**Step 1: $b \in \{1, -1\}$.**

If $(x^2+ax+b) \cdot P(x) = Q(x) = x^n + c_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+c_0$ with $P \in
\mathbb{Z}[x]$, then evaluating at $x=0$: $b \cdot P(0) = c_0 = \pm 1$. Since $P
(0) \in \mathbb{Z}$, we get $b \mid 1$, so $b \in \{1,-1\}$.

**Step 2: Bounding $|a|$.**

Let $\alpha, \beta$ be roots of $x^2+ax+b$, so $|\alpha\beta|=|b|=1$. If $|\alph
a|>1$ (hence $|\beta|<1$), then from $Q(\alpha)=0$:
$$|\alpha|^n = |c_{n-1}\alpha^{n-1}+\cdots+c_0| \leq |\alpha|^{n-1}+\cdots+1 = \
frac{|\alpha|^n-1}{|\alpha|-1}$$
This gives $|\alpha|^{n+1} \leq 2|\alpha|^n - 1 < 2|\alpha|^n$, so $|\alpha|<2$.

The larger root has modulus $\frac{|a|+\sqrt{a^2-4b}}{2}$.
- **$b=1$:** Need $\frac{|a|+\sqrt{a^2-4}}{2}<2$ (when $|a|\geq 2$), giving $|a|
\leq 2$. For $|a|\leq 1$ the roots lie on the unit circle ($|\alpha|=1$), so no
bound from above is needed — all $|a|\leq 2$ survive. For $|a|\geq 3$: $\frac{3+
\sqrt{5}}{2}\approx 2.618 > 2$. **Ruled out.**
- **$b=-1$:** Need $\frac{|a|+\sqrt{a^2+4}}{2}<2$, giving $|a|+\sqrt{a^2+4}<4$.
For $|a|=2$: $2+\sqrt{8}\approx 4.83>4$. **Ruled out.** For $|a|\leq 1$: $1+\sqr
t{5}\approx 3.24<4$. **Survives.**

So candidates: $b=1$, $a\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}$ and $b=-1$, $a\in\{-1,0,1\}$.

**Step 3: Verify each candidate.**

**Reducible cases** ($x^2+ax+b$ factors over $\mathbb{Z}$, roots are integers wi
th product $\pm 1$):

- $(a,b)=(-2,1)$: $(x-1)^2$. Take $Q(x)=x^3-x^2-x+1=(x-1)^2(x+1)$. Coefficients
$\{-1,-1,1\}$. ✓
- $(a,b)=(2,1)$: $(x+1)^2$. Take $Q(x)=x^3+x^2-x-1=(x+1)^2(x-1)$. Coefficients $
\{1,-1,-1\}$. ✓
- $(a,b)=(0,-1)$: $(x-1)(x+1)$. Take $Q(x)=x^3+x^2-x-1=(x^2-1)(x+1)$. Coefficien
ts $\{1,-1,-1\}$. ✓

**Irreducible cases with $b=1$ (roots on unit circle):**

- $(a,b)=(0,1)$: $x^2+1$, roots $\pm i$. Take $Q(x)=x^3+x^2+x+1=(x^2+1)(x+1)$. ✓
- $(a,b)=(1,1)$: $x^2+x+1$ (cyclotomic $\Phi_3$). Take $Q(x)=x^2+x+1$, $P(x)=1$.
 ✓
- $(a,b)=(-1,1)$: $x^2-x+1$ (cyclotomic $\Phi_6$). Take $Q(x)=x^2-x+1$, $P(x)=1$
. ✓

**Irreducible cases with $b=-1$ (real roots, golden ratio related):**

- $(a,b)=(1,-1)$: $x^2+x-1$. Take $Q(x)=x^2+x-1$, $P(x)=1$. ✓
- $(a,b)=(-1,-1)$: $x^2-x-1$. Take $Q(x)=x^2-x-1$, $P(x)=1$. ✓

In each case, $Q$ is monic with all $c_i\in\{+1,-1\}$, and $P(x)=Q(x)/(x^2+ax+b)
\in\mathbb{Z}[x]$ (by Gauss's lemma, since both $Q$ and $x^2+ax+b$ are monic wit
h integer coefficients).

**Step 4: Compute the sum.**

$$S=\{(-2,1),(-1,1),(0,1),(1,1),(2,1),(-1,-1),(0,-1),(1,-1)\}$$

$$\sum_{(a,b)\in S}(a^2+b^2) = (4+1)+(1+1)+(0+1)+(1+1)+(4+1)+(1+1)+(0+1)+(1+1)$$
$$= 5+2+1+2+5+2+1+2 = \boxed{20}$$
