Looking at this problem, I need to verify that the quotient of the torus $T = S^
1 \times S^1$ by the involution $h(z,w) = (-z, \overline{w})$ is homeomorphic to
 the Klein bottle $K$.

**Note on interpretation:** The complex conjugation $\overline{w}$ is non-trivia
l only when $w \in S^1 \subset \mathbb{C}$ (for $w \in [-1,1] \subset \mathbb{R}
$, we have $\overline{w} = w$, which would yield a cylinder, not a Klein bottle)
. Thus $K$ is interpreted as the standard mapping-torus model $(D^1 \times S^1)/
((-1,w) \sim (1,\overline{w}))$, which is the Klein bottle.

---

**Proof.**

**Step 0: $h$ is a free involution.**

Let $h(z,w) = (-z, \overline{w})$ on $T = S^1 \times S^1 \subset \mathbb{C}^2$.

- **Involution:** $h^2(z,w) = h(-z, \overline{w}) = (-(-z), \overline{\overline{
w}}) = (z, w)$. ✓
- **Free:** If $h(z,w) = (z,w)$, then $-z = z$ forces $z = 0 \notin S^1$, a cont
radiction. So $h$ has no fixed points. ✓
- **Homeomorphism:** $h$ is continuous (componentwise multiplication/conjugation
 on $S^1$) with $h^{-1} = h$. ✓

Thus $\langle h \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ acts freely and properly o
n $T$, and the quotient $T/\langle h \rangle$ is a compact Hausdorff space (in f
act a closed 2-manifold, since the action is free and properly discontinuous).

**Step 1: Angular coordinates and the fundamental domain.**

Parametrize $S^1 = \{e^{i\alpha} : \alpha \in \mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\}$. In a
ngular coordinates $(\alpha, \beta)$ with $z = e^{i\alpha}$, $w = e^{i\beta}$, t
he involution becomes:
$$h(\alpha, \beta) = (\alpha + \pi,\; -\beta).$$

Define the **fundamental domain** $D = [0, \pi] \times S^1$ and the embedding:
$$F: D \hookrightarrow T, \qquad F(\alpha, w) = (e^{i\alpha},\, w).$$

**Claim:** Every $\langle h \rangle$-orbit in $T$ meets $F(D)$.

*Proof.* Take $(e^{i\alpha}, w) \in T$ with $\alpha \in [0, 2\pi)$.
- If $\alpha \in [0, \pi]$: the point lies in $F(D)$.
- If $\alpha \in (\pi, 2\pi)$: then $h(e^{i\alpha}, w) = (e^{i(\alpha+\pi)}, \ov
erline{w}) = (e^{i(\alpha - \pi)}, \overline{w})$ (since $\alpha + \pi \equiv \a
lpha - \pi \pmod{2\pi}$), and $\alpha - \pi \in (0, \pi) \subset [0,\pi]$, so $h
(e^{i\alpha}, w) \in F(D)$. $\square$

**Step 2: The boundary identification.**

For an **interior point** $\alpha \in (0, \pi)$: the orbit is $\{(e^{i\alpha}, w
),\; (e^{i(\alpha+\pi)}, \overline{w})\}$. Since $\alpha + \pi \in (\pi, 2\pi) \
notin [0,\pi]$, the orbit meets $F(D)$ exactly once — no identification in the i
nterior.

For a **boundary point**:
- $\alpha = 0$: $F(0, w) = (1, w)$, and $h(1, w) = (-1, \overline{w}) = F(\pi, \
overline{w})$. So $(0, w) \sim (\pi, \overline{w})$.
- $\alpha = \pi$: $F(\pi, w) = (-1, w)$, and $h(-1, w) = (1, \overline{w}) = F(0
, \overline{w})$. Same identification.

Thus the quotient $T/\langle h \rangle$ is obtained from $D = [0, \pi] \times S^
1$ by identifying $(0, w) \sim (\pi, \overline{w})$ on the boundary.

**Step 3: The induced map is a homeomorphism.**

Let $\sim_D$ be the equivalence relation on $D$ generated by $(0, w) \sim_D (\pi
, \overline{w})$. The embedding $F$ induces $\bar{F}: D/{\sim_D} \to T/\langle h
 \rangle$.

- **Well-defined:** If $(\alpha_1, w_1) \sim_D (\alpha_2, w_2)$, either they are
 equal (trivial) or $\{(\alpha_1, w_1), (\alpha_2, w_2)\} = \{(0, w), (\pi, \ove
rline{w})\}$. In the latter case, $F(0, w) = (1, w)$ and $F(\pi, \overline{w}) =
 (-1, \overline{w}) = h(1, w) = h(F(0,w))$, so the images are in the same $\lang
le h \rangle$-orbit. ✓

- **Surjective:** Every orbit meets $F(D)$ (Step 1). ✓

- **Injective:** Suppose $F(\alpha_1, w_1) \sim_{\langle h \rangle} F(\alpha_2,
w_2)$ with both in $D$. If $F(\alpha_1, w_1) = F(\alpha_2, w_2)$, injectivity of
 $F$ gives $(\alpha_1, w_1) = (\alpha_2, w_2)$. Otherwise $F(\alpha_2, w_2) = h(
F(\alpha_1, w_1))$, i.e., $(e^{i\alpha_2}, w_2) = (e^{i(\alpha_1+\pi)}, \overlin
e{w_1})$ with $\alpha_1, \alpha_2 \in [0, \pi]$ and $\alpha_1 + \pi \in [\pi, 2\
pi]$.
  - $\alpha_1 = 0$: $\alpha_1 + \pi = \pi \Rightarrow \alpha_2 = \pi$, $w_2 = \o
verline{w_1}$, so $(0, w_1) \sim_D (\pi, \overline{w_1})$. ✓
  - $\alpha_1 = \pi$: $\alpha_1 + \pi \equiv 0 \Rightarrow \alpha_2 = 0$, $w_2 =
 \overline{w_1}$, so $(\pi, w_1) \sim_D (0, \overline{w_1})$. ✓
  - $\alpha_1 \in (0, \pi)$: $\alpha_1 + \pi \in (\pi, 2\pi)$, so $e^{i\alpha_2}
 = e^{i(\alpha_1+\pi)}$ requires $\alpha_2 = \alpha_1 - \pi \in (-\pi, 0) \notin
 [0,\pi]$, contradiction. No identification in the interior. ✓

Since $D/{\sim_D}$ is compact (quotient of the compact space $D$) and $T/\langle
 h \rangle$ is Hausdorff, the continuous bijection $\bar{F}$ is a **homeomorphis
m**.

**Step 4: Rescaling to match $K$.**

Define the homeomorphism $\phi: [0, \pi] \to D^1 = [-1, 1]$ by $\phi(\alpha) = \
frac{2\alpha}{\pi} - 1$, with $\phi(0) = -1$ and $\phi(\pi) = 1$.

The product map $\Phi = \phi \times \mathrm{id}_{S^1}: [0, \pi] \times S^1 \to D
^1 \times S^1$ is a homeomorphism that sends the boundary identification $(0, w)
 \sim (\pi, \overline{w})$ to:
$$(-1, w) \sim (1, \overline{w}).$$

This is precisely the defining identification of $K$. Therefore $\Phi$ descends
to a homeomorphism of quotients:
$$([0, \pi] \times S^1)/((0, w) \sim (\pi, \overline{w})) \;\cong\; (D^1 \times
S^1)/((-1, w) \sim (1, \overline{w})) = K.$$

**Conclusion.**

Composing the homeomorphisms from Steps 3 and 4:
$$T/\langle h \rangle \;\cong\; ([0, \pi] \times S^1)/((0, w) \sim (\pi, \overli
ne{w})) \;\cong\; (D^1 \times S^1)/((-1, w) \sim (1, \overline{w})) = K.$$

Hence $T/(z,w) \sim h(z,w)$ is homeomorphic to the Klein bottle $K$. $\blacksqua
