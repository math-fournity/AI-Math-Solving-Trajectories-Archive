## Sufficient conditions for absolute convergence

Write the integral as $\int_0^\infty x^a f(x)\,dx = \int_0^1 + \int_1^\infty$ an
d treat the two ends separately. The following are **sufficient** conditions (wi
th $a\notin\mathbb Z$ fixed):

**(C1) Local integrability.** $f$ is (Lebesgue/Riemann) integrable on every comp
act subinterval of $(0,\infty)$.

**(C2) Behavior at the origin.** There exist $\delta_1>0$ and $M_1>0$ such that,
 for all sufficiently small $x>0$,
$$|f(x)|\le M_1\,x^{-a-1+\delta_1}.$$
Then $x^a|f(x)|\le M_1 x^{-1+\delta_1}$, and $\int_0^1 x^{-1+\delta_1}\,dx = 1/\
delta_1<\infty$, so $\int_0^1 x^a f(x)\,dx$ converges absolutely.

**(C3) Behavior at infinity.** There exist $\delta_2>0$ and $M_2>0$ such that, f
or all sufficiently large $x$,
$$|f(x)|\le M_2\,x^{-a-1-\delta_2}.$$
Then $x^a|f(x)|\le M_2 x^{-1-\delta_2}$, and $\int_1^\infty x^{-1-\delta_2}\,dx
= 1/\delta_2<\infty$, so $\int_1^\infty x^a f(x)\,dx$ converges absolutely.

Together (C1)–(C3) imply absolute convergence of $\int_0^\infty x^a f(x)\,dx$. E
quivalently, in asymptotic notation: it suffices that
$$|f(x)|=O\!\bigl(x^{-a-1+\delta_1}\bigr)\ \text{as }x\to0^+,\qquad |f(x)|=O\!\b
igl(x^{-a-1-\delta_2}\bigr)\ \text{as }x\to\infty,$$
for some $\delta_1,\delta_2>0$. (When $a>-1$ the origin condition is compatible
with bounded $f$; the conditions are sharp in the sense that the borderline expo
nents $-a-1$ at both ends are exactly the non-integrable ones.)

## Evaluation by contour integration

Assume now that $f(z)$ extends to a single-valued analytic function on $\mathbb
C\setminus\{z_k\}$ whose only singularities are isolated (poles or essential sin
gularities) $z_1,z_2,\dots$, **none of which lies on $[0,\infty)$**, and that th
e growth estimates above hold with enough uniformity that the two circular arcs
below vanish.

Because $a\notin\mathbb Z$, the power $z^a$ is multivalued. Choose the branch cu
t along the positive real axis and define
$$z^a := |z|^a e^{ia\arg z},\qquad \arg z\in(0,2\pi).$$
Set $F(z):=z^a f(z)$, which is analytic on $\mathbb C\setminus\bigl([0,\infty)\c
up\{z_k\}\bigr)$.

**Keyhole contour.** For $0<\varepsilon<R$ let $\mathcal C_{\varepsilon,R}$ cons
ist of, traversed counter-clockwise:
- the upper edge $L^+:\ z=x+i0^+$, $x$ from $\varepsilon$ to $R$;
- the large circle $C_R:\ |z|=R$, $\arg z$ from $0^+$ to $2\pi^-$;
- the lower edge $L^-:\ z=x-i0^+$, $x$ from $R$ to $\varepsilon$;
- the small circle $C_\varepsilon:\ |z|=\varepsilon$, $\arg z$ from $2\pi^-$ to
$0^+$.

**Contributions of the straight edges.** On $L^+$ we have $\arg z=0$, so $z^a=x^
a$ and
$$\int_{L^+} F(z)\,dz=\int_\varepsilon^R x^a f(x)\,dx.$$
On $L^-$ we have $\arg z=2\pi$, so $z^a=x^a e^{2\pi i a}$, and the orientation i
s reversed:
$$\int_{L^-} F(z)\,dz=\int_R^\varepsilon x^a e^{2\pi i a}f(x)\,dx=-e^{2\pi i a}\
int_\varepsilon^R x^a f(x)\,dx.$$
Hence the two edges together contribute
$$\bigl(1-e^{2\pi i a}\bigr)\int_\varepsilon^R x^a f(x)\,dx.$$

**Vanishing of the arcs.** Using (C2) uniformly near $0$,
$$\biggl|\int_{C_\varepsilon} z^a f(z)\,dz\biggr|\le 2\pi\varepsilon\cdot \varep
silon^{a}\cdot O(\varepsilon^{-a-1+\delta_1})=O(\varepsilon^{\delta_1})\xrightar
row[\varepsilon\to0]{}0.$$
Using (C3) uniformly near $\infty$,
$$\biggl|\int_{C_R} z^a f(z)\,dz\biggr|\le 2\pi R\cdot R^{a}\cdot O(R^{-a-1-\del
ta_2})=O(R^{-\delta_2})\xrightarrow[R\to\infty]{}0.$$

**Residue theorem.** Let $\Sigma_{\varepsilon,R}$ denote the sum of residues of
$F$ at those $z_k$ lying in the annulus $\varepsilon<|z|<R$ (equivalently, enclo
sed by $\mathcal C_{\varepsilon,R}$). By the residue theorem,
$$\oint_{\mathcal C_{\varepsilon,R}} z^a f(z)\,dz=2\pi i\,\Sigma_{\varepsilon,R}
.$$
Letting $\varepsilon\to0$ and $R\to\infty$, the arc contributions vanish and $\S
igma_{\varepsilon,R}\to\sum_k\operatorname{Res}\bigl(z^a f(z),z_k\bigr)$, while
the straight-edge contribution tends to $\bigl(1-e^{2\pi i a}\bigr)\int_0^\infty
 x^a f(x)\,dx$. Therefore
$$\bigl(1-e^{2\pi i a}\bigr)\int_0^\infty x^a f(x)\,dx=2\pi i\sum_k\operatorname
{Res}\bigl(z^a f(z),z_k\bigr).$$

Since $1-e^{2\pi i a}=-2i\,e^{i\pi a}\sin(\pi a)\neq0$ (because $a\notin\mathbb
Z$), we may divide:

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \int_0^\infty x^a f(x)\,dx \;=\; \frac{2\pi i}{\,1-e^{2\p
i i a}\,}\sum_k\operatorname{Res}\bigl(z^a f(z),\,z_k\bigr)\;=\;-\frac{\pi\,e^{-
i\pi a}}{\sin(\pi a)}\sum_k\operatorname{Res}\bigl(z^a f(z),\,z_k\bigr).}$$

**Remarks on the hypotheses used.**
1. The branch cut along $[0,\infty)$ is legitimate precisely because $f$ has no
singularity there; the multivaluedness of $z^a$ is the only source of the jump $
1-e^{2\pi i a}$ between the two edges, and this jump is non-zero exactly when $a
\notin\mathbb Z$.
2. The arc estimates require the bounds (C2),(C3) to hold *uniformly in arg* on
the circles, which is guaranteed when $f$ is analytic off $[0,\infty)\cup\{z_k\}
$ and the asymptotic estimates are radial (e.g. $f(z)=O(|z|^{-a-1+\delta_1})$ as
 $|z|\to0$ and $f(z)=O(|z|^{-a-1-\delta_2})$ as $|z|\to\infty$, uniformly in $\a
rg z\in(0,2\pi)$).
3. If $f$ decays only as $O(|z|^{-a-1})$ the large arc need not vanish; one then
 either strengthens the decay or deforms the contour / uses indentation, but the
 clean formula above requires the strict $\delta_2>0$ margin. Likewise at the or
igin a strict $\delta_1>0$ margin is needed for the small arc to vanish.

This completes the derivation of both the sufficient conditions and the contour-
integral evaluation.
