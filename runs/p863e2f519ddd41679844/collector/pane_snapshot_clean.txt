The answer is **no**. A Borel set of positive Lebesgue measure need not contain
any interval. The standard counterexample is the **Smith–Volterra–Cantor (SVC) s
et** (a "fat Cantor set").

---

## Construction of the SVC set

Start with $[0,1]$. We iteratively remove open middle intervals, but of shrinkin
g length so that the total removed measure is strictly less than $1$.

- **Step 1:** Remove the open middle interval of length $\tfrac{1}{4}$ from $[0,
1]$, leaving $[0,\tfrac{3}{8}]\cup[\tfrac{5}{8},1]$.
- **Step 2:** From each of the two remaining intervals, remove the open middle i
nterval of length $\tfrac{1}{16}$.
- **Step $n$:** From each of the $2^{n-1}$ remaining closed intervals, remove th
e open middle interval of length $\tfrac{1}{4^n}$.

Define
$$C = [0,1]\setminus\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{k} I_{n,k},$$
where $I_{n,k}$ are the open intervals removed at step $n$.

---

## $C$ is Borel

Each $I_{n,k}$ is open, so $\bigcup_{n,k} I_{n,k}$ is open, and $C$ is **closed*
*, hence Borel.

---

## $C$ has positive measure

The total measure removed is
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} 2^{n-1}\cdot\frac{1}{4^n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{n-
1}}{2^{2n}} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n+1}} = \frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{8}+\fr
ac{1}{16}+\cdots = \frac{1}{2}.$$

Therefore
$$m(C) = 1 - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2} > 0.$$

---

## $C$ contains no interval

After step $n$, the construction leaves $2^n$ closed intervals. By the symmetry
of the construction, all of them have the same length. The total remaining lengt
h is
$$1 - \sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{k+1}} = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2^{n+1}},$$
so each remaining interval has length
$$L_n = \frac{1}{2^n}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\right) \xrightarrow{n\t
o\infty} 0.$$

Now suppose for contradiction that $C$ contains a nonempty open interval $(a,b)$
 with $b-a>0$. Choose $n$ large enough that $L_n < b-a$. At stage $n$, every rem
aining closed interval has length $L_n < b-a$, so $(a,b)$ cannot be contained in
 any single remaining interval. Hence $(a,b)$ must intersect one of the open gap
s removed at or before stage $n$, i.e. $(a,b)\cap\bigl([0,1]\setminus C\bigr)\ne
q\varnothing$. This contradicts $(a,b)\subseteq C$.

Therefore $C$ has **empty interior** and contains no interval.

---

## Conclusion

The Smith–Volterra–Cantor set $C$ is a **closed** (hence Borel) subset of $\math
bb{R}$ with **positive Lebesgue measure** ($m(C)=\tfrac{1}{2}$) that **contains
no interval**. Thus a Borel set of nonzero measure need not contain an interval
as a subset.
