**Claim.** Let $p$ be prime, $F = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$, and $f \in F[x_1,\dot
s,x_n]$ a nonzero polynomial with $\deg_i f < p$ for every $i$. Then $f$ is **no
t** identically zero as a function $F^n \to F$.

**Proof.** We proceed by induction on $n$.

**Base case ($n = 1$).** Let $f \in F[x]$ be nonzero with $\deg f < p$. A nonzer
o polynomial of degree $d$ over a field has at most $d$ roots. Since $\deg f \le
 p - 1 < p = |F|$, the polynomial $f$ cannot vanish at all $p$ elements of $F$.
Hence $f$ is not the zero function.

**Inductive step.** Assume the claim holds for $n - 1$ variables. Write
$$f(x_1,\dots,x_n) = \sum_{j=0}^{p-1} f_j(x_1,\dots,x_{n-1})\, x_n^{j},$$
where each $f_j \in F[x_1,\dots,x_{n-1}]$. Since $\deg_n f < p$, the exponent of
 $x_n$ in every monomial of $f$ is at most $p-1$, so this representation is vali
d, and each $f_j$ satisfies $\deg_i f_j \le \deg_i f < p$ for $i = 1,\dots,n-1$.

Suppose for contradiction that $f$ vanishes on all of $F^n$. Fix any point $(a_1
,\dots,a_{n-1}) \in F^{n-1}$ and consider the univariate polynomial
$$g(x_n) := f(a_1,\dots,a_{n-1}, x_n) = \sum_{j=0}^{p-1} f_j(a_1,\dots,a_{n-1})\
, x_n^{j} \in F[x_n].$$
This has degree $< p$ and, by assumption, vanishes at every $x_n \in F$. By the
base case, $g$ must be the zero polynomial, so
$$f_j(a_1,\dots,a_{n-1}) = 0 \quad \text{for every } j = 0,\dots,p-1.$$
Since $(a_1,\dots,a_{n-1})$ was arbitrary, each $f_j$ vanishes on all of $F^{n-1
}$. By the inductive hypothesis (each $f_j$ has $\deg_i f_j < p$), this forces e
very $f_j$ to be the zero polynomial. Therefore $f = \sum_j f_j x_n^j = 0$ as a
polynomial, contradicting the assumption that $f$ is nonzero.

Hence $f$ cannot be identically zero as a function. $\blacksquare$
