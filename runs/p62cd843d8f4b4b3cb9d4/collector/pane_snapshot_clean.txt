**Proof.**

Place a coordinate system with $W$ at the origin and the line $AW$ as the $x$-ax
is. Since $A,I,W$ are collinear (with $I$ between $A$ and $W$, as $W$ is the mid
point of arc $BC$ not containing $A$), write
$$A=(d,0),\qquad I=(t,0),\qquad W=(0,0),\qquad 0<t<d.$$

**Circumcircle $\omega$.** Its center $O$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of
$AW$, so $O=(d/2,k)$ for some $k\ne 0$ (the case $k=0$ corresponds to the isosce
les case $AB=AC$, excluded). The radius satisfies $r^2=(d/2)^2+k^2$, hence $\ome
ga$ has equation
$$x^2+y^2-dx-2ky=0. \tag{1}$$

**The line $BC$.** The arc-midpoint $W$ is the circumcenter of $\triangle BIC$,
so $WB=WC=WI=t$. Thus $B,C$ lie on the circle $x^2+y^2=t^2$ and on $\omega$. The

$$dx+2ky=t^2. \tag{2}$$

**The line $AP$.** Let $P=(p_x,p_y)\in\omega$ satisfy $\angle API=90^\circ$, i.e
. $(A-P)\cdot(I-P)=0$:
$$(d-p_x)(t-p_x)+p_y^2=0. \tag{3}$$
Using $(1)$ to replace $p_y^2 = dp_x+2kp_y-p_x^2$ in $(3)$:
$$dt - tp_x + 2kp_y = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad tp_x-2kp_y=dt. \tag{4}$$
Since $A=(d,0)$ satisfies $(4)$, the locus $(4)$ is precisely the line $AP$:
$$tx-2ky=dt. \tag{4}$$

**Point $S=AP\cap BC$.** Adding $(2)$ and $(4)$: $(d+t)x=t^2+dt=t(t+d)$, so $x=t
$. Substituting into $(2)$: $2ky=t^2-dt=t(t-d)$, hence
$$S=\Bigl(t,\;\frac{t(t-d)}{2k}\Bigr). \tag{5}$$

**Point $D=AP\cap\ell$, where $\ell$ is the line through $W$ perpendicular to $A
W$.** Since $AW$ is the $x$-axis, $\ell$ is the line $x=0$. From $(4)$ with $x=0
$: $-2ky=dt$, so
$$D=\Bigl(0,\;-\frac{dt}{2k}\Bigr). \tag{6}$$

**Point $E=BC\cap\ell$.** From $(2)$ with $x=0$: $2ky=t^2$, so
$$E=\Bigl(0,\;\frac{t^2}{2k}\Bigr). \tag{7}$$

**Computing $IE^2$.** With $I=(t,0)$ and $E=(0,t^2/(2k))$:
$$IE^2 = t^2+\left(\frac{t^2}{2k}\right)^2 = t^2+\frac{t^4}{4k^2}. \tag{8}$$

$$SD^2=\bigl(t-0\bigr)^2+\left(\frac{t(t-d)}{2k}-\Bigl(-\frac{dt}{2k}\Bigr)\righ
t)^2 = t^2+\left(\frac{t(t-d)+dt}{2k}\right)^2.$$
Since $t(t-d)+dt = t^2-td+dt = t^2$,
$$SD^2 = t^2+\left(\frac{t^2}{2k}\right)^2 = t^2+\frac{t^4}{4k^2}. \tag{9}$$

**Conclusion.** From $(8)$ and $(9)$, $SD^2=IE^2$, and both lengths are non-nega
tive, hence
$$\boxed{SD=IE.}$$
