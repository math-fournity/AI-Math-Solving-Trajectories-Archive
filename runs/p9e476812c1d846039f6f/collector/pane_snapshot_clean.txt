## Proof

**Claim:** The analytic functions $f$ on the unit disk $\mathbb{D} = \{z : |z|<1
\}$ satisfying $|f(z^2)| \geq |f(z)|$ for all $z\in\mathbb{D}$ are exactly the c
onstant functions.

---

**Step 1: Iterating the inequality.**

For any $z\in\mathbb{D}$, we have $|z|<1$, so $z^2\in\mathbb{D}$, and inductivel
y $z^{2^n}\in\mathbb{D}$ for all $n\geq 0$. Applying the given inequality repeat
edly:

$$|f(z)| \leq |f(z^2)| \leq |f(z^4)| \leq \cdots \leq |f(z^{2^n})| \quad \text{f
or all } n\geq 1.$$

**Step 2: Taking the limit.**

Since $|z|<1$, we have $z^{2^n}\to 0$ as $n\to\infty$. By continuity of $f$ (ana
lytic implies continuous),

$$|f(z^{2^n})| \to |f(0)| \quad \text{as } n\to\infty.$$

Combining with Step 1,

$$|f(z)| \leq |f(0)| \quad \text{for all } z\in\mathbb{D}.$$

**Step 3: Applying the Maximum Modulus Principle.**

The bound $|f(z)|\leq |f(0)|$ holds on the entire disk $\mathbb{D}$, so $|f|$ at
tains its maximum at the interior point $z=0$. By the Maximum Modulus Principle,
 an analytic function on a connected open set that attains its maximum modulus i
n the interior must be constant. Since $\mathbb{D}$ is connected, $f$ is constan
t.

**Step 4: Verification of the converse.**

If $f$ is constant, say $f\equiv c$, then $|f(z^2)|=|c|=|f(z)|$ for all $z$, so
$|f(z^2)|\geq |f(z)|$ holds (with equality).

---

**Conclusion:** The functions satisfying the condition are precisely the constan
t functions.
