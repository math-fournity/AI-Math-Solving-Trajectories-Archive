# Problem

In a specialized laboratory, a chemist is working with a library of chemical compounds represented by the set $S$. A compound belongs to $S$ if and only if the indices of its molecular components are composed exclusively of prime numbers less than or equal to 10.

The chemist needs to select a collection of $n$ distinct compounds from this library. Her goal is to ensure that, regardless of which $n$ compounds are chosen, there will always be a subset of exactly 4 distinct compounds whose combined molecular structure forms a "balanced complex." A complex is considered balanced if the total count of every prime-indexed component in the product of the four compounds is an even integer.

What is the minimum value of $n$ required to guarantee that such a group of 4 compounds can always be found?
