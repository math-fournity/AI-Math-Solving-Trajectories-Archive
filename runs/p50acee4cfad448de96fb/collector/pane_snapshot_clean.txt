## Part 1: Casting the Volterra System as a Linear Model via Kronecker Tensor Pr
oduct

### 1.1 Input Vector and Kronecker Products

Define the **input snapshot vector** at time $t$ with memory order $M$:

$$\mathbf{x}(t, M) = \big[x(t),\; x(t-1),\; \ldots,\; x(t-M)\big]^T \in \mathbb{
R}^{M+1}.$$

Define the **$k$-fold Kronecker product**:

$$\mathbf{x}^{[k]}(t, M) = \underbrace{\mathbf{x}(t,M) \otimes \mathbf{x}(t,M) \
otimes \cdots \otimes \mathbf{x}(t,M)}_{k \text{ times}} \in \mathbb{R}^{(M+1)^k
}.$$

By the definition of the Kronecker product, the entry of $\mathbf{x}^{[k]}(t,M)$
 at the position indexed by the multi-index $(t_1, t_2, \ldots, t_k)$ with $t_i
\in \{0, 1, \ldots, M\}$ is exactly

$$\big[\mathbf{x}^{[k]}(t,M)\big]_{(t_1,\ldots,t_k)} = \prod_{i=1}^{k} x(t - t_i
).$$

### 1.2 Vectorizing the Volterra Kernel

Define $\mathbf{h}_{M,k} \in \mathbb{R}^{(M+1)^k}$ as the **vectorized $k$-th or
der Volterra kernel**, whose entries are $h_k(t_1, \ldots, t_k)$ arranged in the
 **same lexicographic order** as the entries of $\mathbf{x}^{[k]}(t, M)$. Then t
he $k$-th order Volterra contribution becomes an inner product:

$$\sum_{t_1, \ldots, t_k = 0}^{M} h_k(t_1, \ldots, t_k) \prod_{i=1}^{k} x(t - t_
i) = \big[\mathbf{x}^{[k]}(t, M)\big]^T \mathbf{h}_{M,k}.$$

### 1.3 Assembling the Linear Model

Define the **data row vector** for the $k$-th order term:

$$\mathbf{D}_x(t, k, M) = \big[\mathbf{x}^{[k]}(t, M)\big]^T \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \
times (M+1)^k}.$$

Stacking all $p$ orders, define the **combined parameter vector** and **combined
 data row vector**:

$$\mathbf{g}_M = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{h}_{M,1} \\ \mathbf{h}_{M,2} \\ \vdots
\\ \mathbf{h}_{M,p} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{N(M)}, \qquad \mathbf{D}_x(t,
M) = \big[\mathbf{D}_x(t,1,M),\; \ldots,\; \mathbf{D}_x(t,p,M)\big] \in \mathbb{
R}^{1 \times N(M)},$$

where $N(M) = \sum_{k=1}^{p} (M+1)^k$ is the total number of parameters.

Collecting data over $s = 1, \ldots, t$, the **batch data matrix** is:

$$\mathbf{D}_x(t, M) = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{D}_x(1, M) \\ \mathbf{D}_x(2, M)
\\ \vdots \\ \mathbf{D}_x(t, M) \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{t \times N(M)}, \q
quad \mathbf{y}_t = \begin{bmatrix} y(1) \\ \vdots \\ y(t) \end{bmatrix}, \quad
\mathbf{e}_t = \begin{bmatrix} e(1) \\ \vdots \\ e(t) \end{bmatrix}.$$

The Volterra system is now cast as the **linear regression model**:

$$\boxed{\mathbf{y}_t = \mathbf{D}_x(t, M)\, \mathbf{g}_M + \mathbf{e}_t.}$$

Equivalently, at each time instant:

$$y(t) = \boldsymbol{\phi}^T(t, M)\, \mathbf{g}_M + e(t),$$

where $\boldsymbol{\phi}(t, M) = \mathbf{D}_x^T(t, M) = \big[\mathbf{x}^{[1]}(t,
M)^T, \ldots, \mathbf{x}^{[p]}(t,M)^T\big]^T \in \mathbb{R}^{N(M)}$ is the **reg
ressor vector**.

## Part 2: RLS Lattice Algorithm for Estimating $\mathbf{g}_M$

### 2.1 RLS Cost Function

The RLS estimate at time $t$, memory order $M$, minimizes the exponentially weig
hted least-squares cost:

$$J(t, M) = \sum_{s=1}^{t} \lambda^{t-s} \big[y(s) - \boldsymbol{\phi}^T(s, M)\,
 \hat{\mathbf{g}}_M(t)\big]^2, \qquad 0 < \lambda \leq 1,$$

where $\lambda$ is the **forgetting factor**. The solution is:

$$\hat{\mathbf{g}}_M(t) = \mathbf{R}_M^{-1}(t)\, \mathbf{p}_M(t),$$

with $\mathbf{R}_M(t) = \sum_{s=1}^{t} \lambda^{t-s} \boldsymbol{\phi}(s,M)\bold
symbol{\phi}^T(s,M)$ and $\mathbf{p}_M(t) = \sum_{s=1}^{t} \lambda^{t-s} \boldsy
mbol{\phi}(s,M)\, y(s)$.

### 2.2 Nested (Order-Recursive) Structure of the Regressor

The key property enabling the lattice is the **nested structure**: when $M \to M
+1$, the input vector gains one component $x(t-M-1)$, and the Kronecker products
 grow accordingly. Specifically:

$$\mathbf{x}(t, M+1) = \big[\mathbf{x}(t,M)^T,\; x(t-M-1)\big]^T,$$

and the regressor decomposes as:

$$\boldsymbol{\phi}(t, M+1) = \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{\phi}(t, M) \\ \boldsy
mbol{\zeta}(t, M+1) \end{bmatrix},$$

where $\boldsymbol{\zeta}(t, M+1) \in \mathbb{R}^{\Delta N(M)}$ collects **all n
ew Kronecker product terms** involving $x(t-M-1)$. For each $k = 1, \ldots, p$,
the new terms in $\mathbf{x}^{[k]}(t, M+1)$ are those products $\prod_{i=1}^k x(
t - t_i)$ where at least one $t_i = M+1$. The dimension of the new block is:

$$\Delta N(M) = N(M+1) - N(M) = \sum_{k=1}^{p} \big[(M+2)^k - (M+1)^k\big].$$

### 2.3 Backward Prediction Errors — Orthogonal Basis

Define the **backward prediction error** at order $m$ ($m = 1, 2, \ldots, M$) as
 the residual of the new block $\boldsymbol{\zeta}(t, m)$ after projecting onto
the existing regressor $\boldsymbol{\phi}(t, m-1)$:

$$\mathbf{b}_m(t) = \boldsymbol{\zeta}(t, m) - \mathbf{K}_m^T(t-1)\, \boldsymbol
{\phi}(t, m-1) \in \mathbb{R}^{\Delta N(m-1)},$$

where $\mathbf{K}_m(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{N(m-1) \times \Delta N(m-1)}$ is the **ba
ckward prediction filter**:

$$\mathbf{K}_m(t) = \mathbf{R}_{m-1}^{-1}(t) \sum_{s=1}^{t} \lambda^{t-s} \bolds
ymbol{\phi}(s, m-1)\, \boldsymbol{\zeta}^T(s, m).$$

For the base case ($m = 0$): $\mathbf{b}_0(t) = \boldsymbol{\phi}(t, 0) = \big[x
(t), x(t)^2, \ldots, x(t)^p\big]^T \in \mathbb{R}^p$.

**Orthogonality Property.** By construction (Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization in t
he exponentially weighted inner product $\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle_
t = \sum_{s=1}^t \lambda^{t-s} \mathbf{u}^T(s) \mathbf{v}(s)$), the backward pre
diction errors satisfy:

$$\sum_{s=1}^{t} \lambda^{t-s}\, \mathbf{b}_i(s)\, \mathbf{b}_j^T(s) = \mathbf{0
}, \qquad i \neq j.$$

Thus $\{\mathbf{b}_0(t), \mathbf{b}_1(t), \ldots, \mathbf{b}_M(t)\}$ forms an **
orthogonal basis** for the column space of $\mathbf{D}_x(t, M)$.

### 2.4 Forward Prediction Errors

Define the **forward prediction error** at order $m$ as the residual of $\boldsy
mbol{\zeta}(t, m)$ after projecting onto the time-shifted regressor $\boldsymbol
{\phi}(t-1, m-1)$:

$$\mathbf{f}_m(t) = \boldsymbol{\zeta}(t, m) - \mathbf{A}_m^T(t-1)\, \boldsymbol
{\phi}(t-1, m-1) \in \mathbb{R}^{\Delta N(m-1)},$$

where $\mathbf{A}_m(t)$ is the **forward prediction filter**:

$$\mathbf{A}_m(t) = \mathbf{R}_{m-1}^{-1}(t-1) \sum_{s=1}^{t} \lambda^{t-s} \bol
dsymbol{\phi}(s-1, m-1)\, \boldsymbol{\zeta}^T(s, m).$$

### 2.5 Error Energy Matrices and Cross-Correlation

Define the **backward error energy matrix**:

$$\mathbf{E}_m^b(t) = \sum_{s=1}^{t} \lambda^{t-s}\, \mathbf{b}_m(s)\, \mathbf{b
}_m^T(s) \in \mathbb{R}^{\Delta N(m-1) \times \Delta N(m-1)}.$$

Define the **forward error energy matrix**:

$$\mathbf{E}_m^f(t) = \sum_{s=1}^{t} \lambda^{t-s}\, \mathbf{f}_m(s)\, \mathbf{f
}_m^T(s).$$

Define the **cross-correlation matrix** (capturing the temporal shift relationsh
ip):

$$\boldsymbol{\Delta}_m(t) = \sum_{s=1}^{t} \lambda^{t-s}\, \mathbf{f}_{m-1}(s)\
, \mathbf{b}_{m-1}^T(s-1).$$

### 2.6 Reflection Coefficient Matrices

The **forward and backward reflection coefficient matrices** are:

$$\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_m^f(t) = \boldsymbol{\Delta}_m(t)\, \big[\mathbf{E}_{m-1}^
b(t-1)\big]^{-1}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_m^b(t) = \boldsymbol{\Delta}_m^T(t)
\, \big[\mathbf{E}_{m-1}^f(t)\big]^{-1}.$$

### 2.7 Order-Recursive (Lattice) Updates

The forward and backward prediction errors at order $m$ are related to those at
order $m-1$ through the **lattice recursion**. Using the decomposition $\boldsym
bol{\phi}(t-1, m-1) = [\boldsymbol{\phi}(t-1, m-2)^T, \boldsymbol{\zeta}(t-1, m-
1)^T]^T$ and the orthogonality of $\mathbf{b}_{m-1}(t-1)$ to $\boldsymbol{\phi}(
t-1, m-2)$, the projection of $\boldsymbol{\zeta}(t, m)$ onto $\boldsymbol{\phi}
(t-1, m-1)$ decomposes into a projection onto $\boldsymbol{\phi}(t-1, m-2)$ plus
 a projection onto $\mathbf{b}_{m-1}(t-1)$. This yields:

$$\boxed{\mathbf{f}_m(t) = \mathbf{f}_{m-1}^{(m)}(t) - \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_m^f(t
)\, \mathbf{b}_{m-1}(t-1),}$$

$$\boxed{\mathbf{b}_m(t) = \mathbf{b}_{m-1}^{(m)}(t) - \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_m^b(t
)\, \mathbf{f}_{m-1}(t),}$$

where $\mathbf{f}_{m-1}^{(m)}(t)$ denotes the forward prediction error of $\bold
symbol{\zeta}(t,m)$ from $\boldsymbol{\phi}(t-1, m-2)$ alone, and $\mathbf{b}_{m
-1}^{(m)}(t)$ denotes the backward prediction error of $\boldsymbol{\zeta}(t,m)$

### 2.8 Conversion Factor (Likelihood Variable)

Define the **conversion factor** $\gamma_m(t) \in (0, 1]$:

$$\gamma_0(t) = 1, \qquad \gamma_m(t) = \gamma_{m-1}(t) - \mathbf{b}_m^T(t)\, \b

$$\mathbf{b}_m(t) = \gamma_{m-1}(t)\, \boldsymbol{\beta}_m(t), \qquad \mathbf{f}

$$\mathbf{E}_m^b(t+1) = \lambda\, \mathbf{E}_m^b(t) + \mathbf{b}_m(t+1)\, \bolds

$$\varepsilon_0(t) = y(t),$$
