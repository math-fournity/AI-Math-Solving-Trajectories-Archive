Let $P(z) = z^{113} - 180z^{23} + 115z^{7} - 32z^2 - 3z - 10$. We use Rouché's t
heorem to count zeros inside $|z|<2$ and inside $|z|<1$, then subtract.

### Zeros inside $|z| < 2$

On $|z| = 2$, compare $z^{113}$ against the remaining terms:

$$|z^{113}| = 2^{113}, \qquad |{-180z^{23}}| + |115z^7| + |32z^2| + |3z| + |10|
= 180\cdot 2^{23} + 115\cdot 2^7 + 128 + 6 + 10.$$

Since $2^{113} \gg 180\cdot 2^{23}$ (as $2^{90} \gg 180$), we have the strict in
equality

$$|z^{113}| > |{-180z^{23}} + 115z^7 - 32z^2 - 3z - 10| \quad \text{on } |z|=2.$
$

By Rouché's theorem, $P(z)$ and $z^{113}$ have the same number of zeros inside $
|z|<2$, namely **113**. The strict inequality also guarantees $P(z)\neq 0$ on $|
z|=2$.

### Zeros inside $|z| < 1$

On $|z| = 1$, the magnitudes of the terms are:

$$|z^{113}|=1,\quad |{-180z^{23}}|=180,\quad |115z^7|=115,\quad |32z^2|=32,\quad
 |3z|=3,\quad |10|=10.$$

The term $-180z^{23}$ has the largest modulus. Check domination:

$$|{-180z^{23}}| = 180 > 1 + 115 + 32 + 3 + 10 = 161 = |z^{113} + 115z^7 - 32z^2
 - 3z - 10|.$$

By Rouché's theorem, $P(z)$ and $-180z^{23}$ have the same number of zeros insid
e $|z|<1$, namely **23**. The strict inequality also guarantees $P(z)\neq 0$ on
$|z|=1$.

### Zeros in the annulus $1 \le |z| \le 2$

Since $P$ has no zeros on the boundary circles $|z|=1$ and $|z|=2$,

$$N(1 \le |z| \le 2) = N(|z|\le 2) - N(|z|<1) = 113 - 23 = \boxed{90}.$$
