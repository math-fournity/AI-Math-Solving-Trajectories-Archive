# Solution

## Part (a): Singular locus of $V(F)$

Let $F$ be an irreducible quadric in $\mathbf{CP}^n$, written as
$$F = \sum_{0 \le i \le j \le n} a_{ij}\, x_i x_j.$$
Associate to $F$ the symmetric $(n+1)\times(n+1)$ matrix $A = (a_{ij})$ (with th
e off-diagonal entries halved appropriately, or using the convention $F = \mathb
f{x}^T A\, \mathbf{x}$). Then
$$\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_k} = 2(A\mathbf{x})_k,$$
so the singular locus of $V(F)$ is
$$\operatorname{Sing}(V(F)) = \{[\mathbf{x}] \in \mathbf{CP}^n : A\mathbf{x} = \
mathbf{0}\} = \mathbf{P}(\ker A) \subseteq \mathbf{CP}^n.$$

This is a **linear subspace** of $\mathbf{CP}^n$ of dimension $n - \operatorname
{rank}(A)$ (or empty if $\operatorname{rank}(A) = n+1$, i.e., $A$ is invertible)
.

**Constraint from irreducibility.** If $\operatorname{rank}(A) \le 1$, then $F$
is a scalar multiple of a square of a linear form $\ell^2$ (rank 1) or $F = 0$ (
rank 0), both of which are reducible. Since $F$ is irreducible, $\operatorname{r
ank}(A) \ge 2$, so:
$$\dim \operatorname{Sing}(V(F)) = n - \operatorname{rank}(A) \le n - 2.$$

> **Answer (a):** The singular locus of $V(F)$ is the linear subspace $\mathbf{P
}(\ker A) \subseteq \mathbf{CP}^n$ of dimension $n - \operatorname{rank}(A)$, wh
ere $A$ is the symmetric matrix of $F$. For irreducible $F$, this has dimension
$\le n-2$ (in particular, $V(F)$ is smooth iff $\operatorname{rank}(A)=n+1$).

## Part (b): The complement $\mathbf{CP}^{\binom{n+2}{2}-1} \setminus U$

Set $N = \binom{n+2}{2} - 1 = \frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2} - 1$. The projective space $\
mathbf{CP}^N$ parametrizes all quadric hypersurfaces in $\mathbf{CP}^n$ (i.e., a
ll symmetric $(n+1)\times(n+1)$ matrices up to scalar). The open set $U$ consist
s of quadrics that are **both irreducible and non-singular**. Its complement is:
$$\mathbf{CP}^N \setminus U = \{\text{reducible quadrics}\} \cup \{\text{singula
r quadrics}\}.$$

**Key claim (for $n \ge 2$): every reducible quadric is singular.**

A reducible quadric factors as $F = \ell_1 \cdot \ell_2$ (linear forms, possibly
 equal).

- If $\ell_1 = \ell_2$: $F = \ell^2$, and $\nabla F = 2\ell\,\nabla\ell$, which
vanishes on $V(\ell)$ — a hyperplane, nonempty for $n \ge 1$.
- If $\ell_1 \neq \ell_2$: $\nabla F = \ell_2\,\nabla\ell_1 + \ell_1\,\nabla\ell
_2$, which vanishes on $V(\ell_1) \cap V(\ell_2)$. For $n \ge 2$, two distinct h
yperplanes in $\mathbf{CP}^n$ always meet in a nonempty linear subspace of dimen
sion $\ge n-2 \ge 0$.

Hence for $n \ge 2$, $\{\text{reducible}\} \subseteq \{\text{singular}\}$, and t
herefore:
$$\mathbf{CP}^N \setminus U = \{\text{singular quadrics}\} = \{F : \det(A) = 0\}
.$$

This is the **discriminant hypersurface** of quadrics: the zero locus of $\det(A
)$, which is a polynomial of degree $n+1$ in the matrix entries (equivalently, i
n the coefficients $a_{ij}$). It is an irreducible hypersurface of degree $n+1$
in $\mathbf{CP}^N$.

> **Answer (b):** For $n \ge 2$, the complement $\mathbf{CP}^{\binom{n+2}{2}-1}
\setminus U$ is the **discriminant hypersurface**
> $$\{F \in \mathbf{CP}^N : \det(A_F) = 0\},$$
> i.e., the degree-$(n+1)$ hypersurface consisting of all singular quadrics. (Ev
ery reducible quadric is singular when $n \ge 2$, so the reducible ones contribu
te no additional component beyond the singular locus of the discriminant.)
