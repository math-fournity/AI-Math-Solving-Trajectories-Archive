# G-equivariant functions $f: G/H \to G/K$

## Setup

Let $G$ act on $G/H$ and $G/K$ by left multiplication: $g \cdot (xH) = (gx)H$ an
d $g \cdot (yK) = (gy)K$. A function $f: G/H \to G/K$ is **$G$-equivariant** if
$$f(g \cdot xH) = g \cdot f(xH) \quad \text{for all } g, x \in G.$$

## The bijection

**Claim.** There is a natural bijection
$$\operatorname{Hom}_G(G/H,\; G/K) \;\longleftrightarrow\; (G/K)^H,$$
where $(G/K)^H = \{aK \in G/K : h \cdot aK = aK \text{ for all } h \in H\}$ is t
he set of $H$-fixed points in $G/K$.

## Proof

**Step 1: From an equivariant map to a fixed point.**

Let $f: G/H \to G/K$ be $G$-equivariant. Consider the base point $H = eH \in G/H
$. Its stabilizer under the $G$-action is exactly $H$ (since $gH = H \iff g \in
H$). Set $p := f(H) \in G/K$. For any $h \in H$,
$$h \cdot p = h \cdot f(H) = f(h \cdot H) = f(H) = p,$$
so $p \in (G/K)^H$. This defines a map $\Phi: \operatorname{Hom}_G(G/H, G/K) \to
 (G/K)^H$ by $\Phi(f) = f(H)$.

**Step 2: From a fixed point to an equivariant map.**

Conversely, let $p = aK \in (G/K)^H$. Define
$$f_p(xH) := x \cdot p = xaK.$$
This is **well-defined**: if $xH = x'H$, then $x' = xh$ for some $h \in H$, and
$$f_p(x'H) = xhaK = x \cdot (h \cdot aK) = x \cdot aK = f_p(xH),$$
using that $aK$ is $H$-fixed. It is **$G$-equivariant**:
$$f_p(g \cdot xH) = f_p(gxH) = gx \cdot aK = g \cdot (x \cdot aK) = g \cdot f_p(
xH).$$
This defines a map $\Psi: (G/K)^H \to \operatorname{Hom}_G(G/H, G/K)$.

**Step 3: The two maps are inverse.**

- $(\Phi \circ \Psi)(p) = \Phi(f_p) = f_p(H) = e \cdot aK = aK = p$.
- $(\Psi \circ \Phi)(f) = \Psi(f(H)) = f_{f(H)}$. For any $xH \in G/H$, since $x
H = x \cdot H$ and $f$ is equivariant, $f(xH) = f(x \cdot H) = x \cdot f(H) = f_
{f(H)}(xH)$. So $f_{f(H)} = f$.

Hence $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ are inverse bijections. $\square$

## Explicit description

Writing $p = aK$, the fixed-point condition $aK \in (G/K)^H$ is:
$$h \cdot aK = aK \;\forall h \in H \iff haK = aK \;\forall h \in H \iff a^{-1}h
a \in K \;\forall h \in H \iff a^{-1}Ha \subseteq K.$$

Therefore:

> **The $G$-equivariant functions $f: G/H \to G/K$ are precisely the maps of the
 form**
> $$f(xH) = xaK,$$
> **where $a \in G$ satisfies $a^{-1}Ha \subseteq K$. Two elements $a, a' \in G$
 determine the same function if and only if $aK = a'K$ (i.e., $a^{-1}a' \in K$).
**

Equivalently, $\operatorname{Hom}_G(G/H, G/K) \cong \{aK \in G/K : a^{-1}Ha \sub
seteq K\}$, the set of $H$-fixed cosets in $G/K$.

## Special cases and consistency checks

- **$H = \{e\}$:** Every $aK$ is $H$-fixed, so $\operatorname{Hom}_G(G, G/K) \co
ng G/K$. Indeed a $G$-equivariant map from $G$ (the free transitive $G$-set) to
$G/K$ is determined by the image of $e$, which is arbitrary in $G/K$.

- **$H = K$:** The condition becomes $a^{-1}Ha \subseteq H$, i.e., $a \in N_G(H)
$ (the normalizer). So $\operatorname{Hom}_G(G/H, G/H) \cong N_G(H)/H$. These ar
e the $G$-equivariant self-maps of $G/H$, which form the Weyl group $N_G(H)/H$ a
cting on the right — consistent with the well-known fact that $\operatorname{Aut
}_G(G/H) \cong N_G(H)/H$.

- **$H \subseteq K$ (taking $a = e$):** The identity-type map $xH \mapsto xK$ is
 the natural projection $G/H \to G/K$, which is $G$-equivariant — as expected.

- **Existence condition:** A $G$-equivariant map $G/H \to G/K$ exists iff there
is some $a$ with $a^{-1}Ha \subseteq K$, i.e., iff some conjugate of $H$ is cont
ained in $K$.
