## Proof

We work in $\ell^1$ with sequences indexed by $n \ge 1$ and norm $\|(x_n)\|_1 =
\sum_{n=1}^\infty |x_n|$. The operator is
$$
(Ax)_n = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{a^k}{k!}\, x_{n+k}, \qquad a > 0.
$$

**Upper bound: $\|A\| \le e^a$.**

For $x \in \ell^1$,
$$
\|Ax\|_1 = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \left|\sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{a^k}{k!} x_{n+k}\righ
t|
\le \sum_{n=1}^\infty \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{a^k}{k!} |x_{n+k}|
= \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{a^k}{k!} \sum_{n=1}^\infty |x_{n+k}|.
$$
(The interchange is justified since all terms are non-negative.) For each fixed
$k \ge 0$,
$$
\sum_{n=1}^\infty |x_{n+k}| = \sum_{m=k+1}^\infty |x_m| \le \sum_{m=1}^\infty |x
_m| = \|x\|_1.
$$
Hence
$$
\|Ax\|_1 \le \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{a^k}{k!} \|x\|_1 = e^a \|x\|_1,
$$
so $\|A\| \le e^a$.

**Lower bound: $\|A\| \ge e^a$.**

For $N \ge 1$, let $x^{(N)} = e_N$, the unit vector with $x_N = 1$ and $x_m = 0$
 for $m \ne N$. Then $\|x^{(N)}\|_1 = 1$, and
$$
(Ax^{(N)})_n = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{a^k}{k!} x^{(N)}_{n+k} = \begin{cases} \d
frac{a^{N-n}}{(N-n)!}, & 1 \le n \le N, \\[6pt] 0, & n > N. \end{cases}
$$
Therefore
$$
\|Ax^{(N)}\|_1 = \sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{a^{N-n}}{(N-n)!} = \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} \frac{
a^j}{j!}.
$$
Since $\|x^{(N)}\|_1 = 1$,
$$
\|A\| \ge \frac{\|Ax^{(N)}\|_1}{\|x^{(N)}\|_1} = \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} \frac{a^j}{j!}
.
$$
Letting $N \to \infty$,
$$
\|A\| \ge \lim_{N\to\infty} \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} \frac{a^j}{j!} = e^a.
$$

**Conclusion.** Combining both bounds,
$$
\boxed{\|A\| = e^a}.
$$
