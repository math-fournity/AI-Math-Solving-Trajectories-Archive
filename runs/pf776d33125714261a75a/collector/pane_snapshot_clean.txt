## Solution

We seek the general solution of the first-order linear PDE

$$A(x,y)\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + B(x,y)\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}
+ C(x,y)\,u = R(x,y). \tag{1}$$

### Step 1. The characteristic system

Equation (1) is a first-order linear PDE. We apply the **method of characteristi
cs**. The associated characteristic (Lagrange–Charpit) system is

$$\frac{dx}{A(x,y)} = \frac{dy}{B(x,y)} = \frac{du}{R(x,y) - C(x,y)\,u}. \tag{2}
$$

### Step 2. Characteristic curves in the $(x,y)$-plane

From the first two ratios in (2) we obtain the ODE for the characteristic curves
 projected onto the $(x,y)$-plane:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{B(x,y)}{A(x,y)}. \tag{3}$$

Its general integral defines a one-parameter family of curves. Let

$$\phi(x, y) = \text{const} \tag{4}$$

be the general solution of (3); that is, $\phi$ is a first integral of the plana
r system $\dot x = A,\; \dot y = B$. Each value of the constant selects one char
acteristic curve. We call $\phi(x,y)$ the **characteristic invariant**.

### Step 3. The ODE for $u$ along a characteristic

Assume $A(x,y) \neq 0$ on the region of interest (the case $B \neq 0$ is handled
 symmetrically by swapping the roles of $x$ and $y$). Along a characteristic cur
ve $\phi(x,y) = c$, we regard $y$ as a function of $x$ determined by (3), and co
mpute

$$\frac{du}{dx} = u_x + u_y\,\frac{dy}{dx} = u_x + \frac{B}{A}\,u_y.$$

From the PDE (1), $A\,u_x + B\,u_y = R - C\,u$, so dividing by $A$:

$$u_x + \frac{B}{A}\,u_y = \frac{R - C\,u}{A}.$$

Hence along each characteristic, $u$ satisfies the **first-order linear ODE**

$$\frac{du}{dx} + \frac{C(x,y)}{A(x,y)}\,u = \frac{R(x,y)}{A(x,y)}, \tag{5}$$

where $y = y(x; c)$ is the characteristic curve $\phi(x,y) = c$.

### Step 4. Integrating factor and general solution of the ODE

Equation (5) has the integrating factor

$$\mu(x) = \exp\!\left(\int^{x} \frac{C\!\bigl(\xi,\, y(\xi;c)\bigr)}{A\!\bigl(\
xi,\, y(\xi;c)\bigr)}\,d\xi\right).$$

Multiplying (5) by $\mu$ and integrating gives

$$u\,\mu = \int^{x} \frac{R\!\bigl(\xi,\, y(\xi;c)\bigr)}{A\!\bigl(\xi,\, y(\xi;
c)\bigr)}\,\mu(\xi)\,d\xi + F(c),$$

where $F$ is an arbitrary constant of integration — one per characteristic, henc
e an **arbitrary function** of the characteristic invariant $c = \phi(x,y)$.

### Step 5. General form of the solution

Substituting back $c = \phi(x,y)$ and writing the integrals along the characteri
stic through $(x,y)$, the **general solution** of (1) is

$$\boxed{\;u(x,y) = e^{-\displaystyle\int^{x}\frac{C(\xi,\,y(\xi))}{A(\xi,\,y(\x
i))}\,d\xi}\left[\;\int^{x}\frac{R(\xi,\,y(\xi))}{A(\xi,\,y(\xi))}\,e^{\displays
tyle\int^{\xi}\frac{C(\tau,\,y(\tau))}{A(\tau,\,y(\tau))}\,d\tau}\,d\xi \;+\; F\
!\bigl(\phi(x,y)\bigr)\right]\;} \tag{6}$$

where:

- $\phi(x,y) = \text{const}$ is the general integral of the characteristic equat
ion $\dfrac{dx}{A} = \dfrac{dy}{B}$, i.e. the family of characteristic curves;
- along each characteristic, $y = y(\xi)$ is determined by $\phi(\xi, y) = \phi(
x,y)$;
- $F$ is an **arbitrary (sufficiently smooth) function** of one variable, encodi
ng the freedom to prescribe data on a non-characteristic curve;
- all integrals are evaluated along the characteristic curve passing through the
 point $(x,y)$.

### Verification

Set $P(x,y) = \int^{x}\frac{C}{A}\,d\xi$ (the exponent, taken along the characte
ristic). Then (6) reads

$$u = e^{-P}\!\left[\int^{x}\frac{R}{A}\,e^{P}\,d\xi + F(\phi)\right].$$

- **Along a characteristic** ($\phi$ fixed), differentiating in $x$ recovers ODE
 (5) by the standard linear-ODE formula, so (5) holds.
- Since (5) is exactly (1) divided by $A$ with $u_y$ eliminated via $dy/dx = B/A
$, equation (1) holds on every characteristic, hence everywhere.
- The term $e^{-P}F(\phi)$ is the **general solution of the homogeneous equation
** $A\,u_x + B\,u_y + C\,u = 0$ (it satisfies (5) with $R=0$), and the integral
term is a **particular solution** of the inhomogeneous equation. Their sum is th
e general solution.

This confirms that (6) gives the complete general solution of the PDE (1), with
the single arbitrary function $F$ reflecting the fact that a first-order PDE in
two independent variables has a general solution depending on one arbitrary func
tion.
