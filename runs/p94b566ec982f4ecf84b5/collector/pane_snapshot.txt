**Answer: No.**

**Proof.**

First, a general observation. Since $Y$ is closed in $X$ and $A \subseteq Y$, we
 have
$$\overline{A}^{\,X} \subseteq \overline{Y}^{\,X} = Y.$$
On the other hand, $A$ being dense in $Y$ means $\overline{A}^{\,Y} = Y$. But $\
overline{A}^{\,Y} = \overline{A}^{\,X} \cap Y$, so
$$Y = \overline{A}^{\,X} \cap Y \subseteq \overline{A}^{\,X}.$$
Combining the two inclusions,
$$\overline{A}^{\,X} = Y.$$
Therefore $A$ is dense in $X$ **if and only if** $Y = X$. In particular, wheneve
r $Y \subsetneq X$, no dense subset of $Y$ can be dense in $X$.

It remains to exhibit a case with $Y \subsetneq X$.

**Counterexample.** Let $X = [0,1]$ with the usual topology (compact), and let $
f \colon X \to X$ be $f(x) = x/2$, which is continuous. Then
$$f^{n}(X) = \bigl[0,\, 2^{-n}\bigr],$$
so
$$Y = \bigcap_{n \in \mathbb{N}} f^{n}(X) = \bigcap_{n \ge 0} \bigl[0,\, 2^{-n}\
bigr] = \{0\}.$$
Take $A = \{0\} \subseteq Y$. Then $A$ is dense in $Y$ (trivially, $\overline{A}
^{\,Y} = \{0\} = Y$). However,
$$\overline{A}^{\,X} = \{0\} \neq [0,1] = X.$$
Thus $A$ is dense in $Y$ but **not** dense in $X$.

Hence the answer to the question is **no**: it does not follow in general that $
A$ is dense in $X$.
