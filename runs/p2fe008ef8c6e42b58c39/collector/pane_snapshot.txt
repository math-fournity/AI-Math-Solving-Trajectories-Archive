## Solution

We seek the smallest positive integer $N$ such that $(x^2-1)(y^2-1)=N$ has at le
ast two solutions with $1 < x \le y$.

**Setup.** Let $S = \{x^2 - 1 : x \ge 2\} = \{3, 8, 15, 24, 35, 48, 63, 80, 99,
120, 143, \ldots\}$. We need $N = a \cdot b$ with $a, b \in S$, $a \le b$, in at
 least two distinct ways.

**Exhaustive check below 360.** For $a \cdot b < 360$ with $a \le b$ and $a, b \
in S$:

| $a$ | valid $b$ (with $a \le b$, $ab<360$) | products |
|-----|---------------------------------------|----------|
| 3 | 3, 8, 15, 24, 35, 48, 63, 80, 99 | 9, 24, 45, 72, 105, 144, 189, 240, 297
|
| 8 | 8, 15, 24, 35 | 64, 120, 192, 280 |
| 15 | 15 | 225 |
| 24 | (none, since $24^2=576$) | — |

The complete list of products below 360 is:
$$9, 24, 45, 64, 72, 105, 120, 144, 189, 192, 225, 240, 280, 297.$$

Each appears exactly once — no collisions.

**At $N = 360$:** Two representations exist:
$$3 \times 120 = 360 \quad\text{and}\quad 15 \times 24 = 360.$$

Verification:
- $3 = 2^2-1$, $120 = 11^2-1$: $(x,y)=(2,11)$, $\;1<2\le 11$ ✓, $\;(4-1)(121-1)=
3\cdot120=360$ ✓
- $15 = 4^2-1$, $24 = 5^2-1$: $(x,y)=(4,5)$, $\;1<4\le 5$ ✓, $\;(16-1)(25-1)=15\
cdot24=360$ ✓

These are two distinct pairs with $1 < x \le y$.

**Conclusion.** Since no $N < 360$ admits two representations and $N = 360$ does
, the smallest such $N$ is:

$$\boxed{360}$$
