zeta(s)$ the Riemann zeta function, and $G(n)$ the Goldbach partition function $
G(n) = \#\{(p,q): p+q=n,\, p\le q,\, p,q\text{ prime}\}$. The notation $s\nearro
w 1$ is interpreted as $s\to 1^+$, since $P(s)$ and $\zeta(s)$ converge only for

**Step 1: The LHS tends to 0.**

The prime zeta function satisfies the Möbius inversion identity:
$$P(s) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{\mu(k)}{k}\log\zeta(ks).$$

As $s\to 1^+$, the $k=1$ term gives $\log\zeta(s) \sim \log\frac{1}{s-1}$ (since
 $\zeta(s)\sim\frac{1}{s-1}$), while for $k\ge 2$, $\zeta(ks)$ remains bounded (
as $ks\to k > 1$). Therefore:
$$P(s) = \log\frac{1}{s-1} + O(1).$$

Squaring:
$$P(s)^2 = \left(\log\tfrac{1}{s-1}\right)^2 + O\!\left(\log\tfrac{1}{s-1}\right
).$$

Dividing by $\zeta(s) = \frac{1}{s-1}(1+O(s-1))$:
$$\frac{P(s)^2}{\zeta(s)} = (s-1)\left(\log\tfrac{1}{s-1}\right)^2\bigl(1+o(1)\b
igr).$$

Since $x\log^2(1/x)\to 0$ as $x\to 0^+$, we conclude:
$$\frac{P(s)^2}{\zeta(s)} \to 0 \quad\text{as } s\to 1^+.$$

**Step 2: The RHS tends to a positive finite constant.**

The RHS is $2\sum_{r=3}^{\infty}\frac{G(2r)}{(2r)^{2s}}$. We show this sum conve
rges for $s\ge 1$ and has a positive limit.

*Convergence.* By the Brun–Selberg sieve, the Goldbach representation count sati
sfies:
$$G(2r) \ll \frac{2r}{\log^2(2r)}\prod_{\substack{p\mid 2r\\p>2}}\frac{p-1}{p-2}
 \ll \frac{r\,\log\log r}{\log^2 r}.$$

Therefore:
$$\frac{G(2r)}{(2r)^2} \ll \frac{\log\log r}{r\,\log^2 r}.$$

The series $\sum_{r\ge 3}\frac{\log\log r}{r\log^2 r}$ converges (by the integra
l test: substituting $u=\log x$ gives $\int \frac{\log u}{u^2}\,du$, which conve
rges). Hence $\sum_{r=3}^{\infty}\frac{G(2r)}{(2r)^2}$ converges absolutely.

*Positivity.* Since $G(n)\ge 0$ for all $n$ (it is a count) and $G(6)=1$ (as $6=
3+3$), we have:
$$\sum_{r=3}^{\infty}\frac{G(2r)}{(2r)^2} \ge \frac{G(6)}{36} = \frac{1}{36} > 0
.$$

*Limit.* Since $G(2r)\ge 0$ and the sum converges at $s=1$, by monotone/dominate
d convergence:
$$\lim_{s\to 1^+}\sum_{r=3}^{\infty}\frac{G(2r)}{(2r)^{2s}} = \sum_{r=3}^{\infty
}\frac{G(2r)}{(2r)^2} = C > 0.$$

Thus the RHS $\to 2C > 0$.

**Step 3: The limit is 0, not 2.**

Combining Steps 1 and 2:
$$\lim_{s\to 1^+}\frac{P(s)^2/\zeta(s)}{2\sum_{r=3}^{\infty}G(2r)/(2r)^{2s}} = \
frac{0}{2C} = 0 \neq 2.$$
