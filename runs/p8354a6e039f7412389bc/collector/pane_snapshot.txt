**Answer: Yes, two semisimple square matrices over a finite field with identical
 characteristic polynomials are always similar.**

---

**Proof.**

Let $F$ be a finite field (the argument in fact works over any field). Let $A, B
 \in M_n(F)$ be semisimple matrices with $\chi_A(x) = \chi_B(x)$.

**Step 1: Semisimplicity via the minimal polynomial.**

A matrix is semisimple (i.e., the associated $F[x]$-module is semisimple) if and
 only if its minimal polynomial is squarefree — a product of distinct monic irre
ducible polynomials. Since $A$ and $B$ are semisimple, write

$$\chi_A(x) = \prod_{i=1}^{k} p_i(x)^{m_i}, \qquad p_1, \ldots, p_k \text{ disti
nct monic irreducibles},\; m_i \ge 1.$$

Then $\mu_A(x) = \prod_{i=1}^{k} p_i(x)$ (squarefree), and likewise for $B$.

**Step 2: Module decomposition of a semisimple operator.**

View $V = F^n$ as an $F[x]$-module via $x \cdot v = Av$. Since $A$ is semisimple
, $V$ is a semisimple $F[x]$-module. By the structure theorem for semisimple mod
ules over the PID $F[x]$, every semisimple module is a direct sum of simple modu
les, each isomorphic to $F[x]/(p)$ for some monic irreducible $p$. Grouping equa
l summands:

$$V \cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^{k} \left(F[x]/(p_i)\right)^{\oplus\, m_i}.$$

Here the irreducible $p_i$ appearing are exactly the irreducible factors of $\mu
_A$ (equivalently, of $\chi_A$).

**Step 3: The characteristic polynomial records the multiplicities.**

The action of $x$ on the simple module $F[x]/(p_i)$ — a $\deg(p_i)$-dimensional
$F$-vector space — is represented by the companion matrix of $p_i$, whose charac
teristic polynomial is $p_i$ itself. Therefore each copy of $F[x]/(p_i)$ contrib
utes a factor of $p_i$ to the characteristic polynomial, and $m_i$ copies contri
bute $p_i^{m_i}$. Hence

$$\chi_A(x) = \prod_{i=1}^{k} p_i(x)^{m_i},$$

where the exponent $m_i$ is **exactly** the number of copies of the simple summa
nd $F[x]/(p_i)$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Applying the same decomposition to $B$:

$$V_B \cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^{k'} \left(F[x]/(p_i')\right)^{\oplus\, m_i'}, \qqua
d \chi_B(x) = \prod_{i=1}^{k'} (p_i')^{m_i'}.$$

Since $\chi_A = \chi_B$, unique factorization in $F[x]$ gives $k = k'$ and, afte
r reindexing, $p_i = p_i'$ and $m_i = m_i'$ for every $i$. Thus $V$ and $V_B$ ar
e isomorphic as $F[x]$-modules, which is precisely the statement that $A$ and $B
$ are similar. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on why semisimplicity is essential.** For a general (non-semisimple) ma
trix, the characteristic polynomial $\prod p_i^{m_i}$ only records the *total* m
ultiplicity $m_i$ of each irreducible $p_i$, not the sizes of the individual Jor
dan/primary blocks. Different partitions of $m_i$ yield non-similar matrices wit
h the same characteristic polynomial. Semisimplicity forces every primary compon
ent to be a direct sum of copies of the *single* irreducible $F[x]/(p_i)$ — ther
e is only one partition ($1+1+\cdots+1$, $m_i$ times) — so the characteristic po
lynomial determines the module uniquely.
