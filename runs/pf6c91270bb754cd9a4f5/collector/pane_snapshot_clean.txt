## Proof

**Claim:** $c_0 = \tfrac{1}{2}$.

---

### Upper bound: $c_0 \le \tfrac{1}{2}$

Let $\sum a_n$ be any convergent series with $a_n \ge 0$, and let $c > \tfrac{1}
{2}$. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality applied to the partial sums,

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{\sqrt{a_n}}{n^c}
= \sum_{n=1}^{N} \sqrt{a_n} \cdot \frac{1}{n^c}
\le \left(\sum_{n=1}^{N} a_n\right)^{\!1/2}\!\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{1}{n^{2c
}}\right)^{\!1/2}.
$$

Since $\sum a_n$ converges, $\sum_{n=1}^{N} a_n$ is bounded. Since $2c > 1$, the
 series $\sum \frac{1}{n^{2c}}$ converges, so its partial sums are bounded. Henc
e the partial sums of $\sum \frac{\sqrt{a_n}}{n^c}$ are bounded above; being a s
eries of non-negative terms, it converges.

Therefore for **every** convergent $\sum a_n \ge 0$, the series $\sum \frac{\sqr
t{a_n}}{n^c}$ converges for all $c > \tfrac{1}{2}$, giving $c_0 \le \tfrac{1}{2}
$.

---

### Lower bound: $c_0 \ge \tfrac{1}{2}$ (sharpness)

We exhibit a convergent series $\sum a_n$ with $a_n \ge 0$ for which $\sum \frac
{\sqrt{a_n}}{n^{1/2}}$ diverges.

Set, for $n \ge 2$,
$$
a_n = \frac{1}{n\,(\log n)^2}.
$$

**Convergence of $\sum a_n$:** By the integral test, $\int_2^{\infty} \frac{dx}{
x(\log x)^2} = \frac{1}{\log 2} < \infty$, so $\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n(\l
og n)^2}$ converges.

**Divergence of $\sum \frac{\sqrt{a_n}}{n^{1/2}}$:** We have $\sqrt{a_n} = \frac
{1}{\sqrt{n}\,\log n}$, so
$$
\frac{\sqrt{a_n}}{n^{1/2}} = \frac{1}{n\,\log n}.
$$
The series $\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n\log n}$ diverges (again by the integr
al test: $\int_2^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x\log x} = \infty$).

Hence at $c = \tfrac{1}{2}$ there exists a convergent $\sum a_n \ge 0$ for which
 $\sum \frac{\sqrt{a_n}}{n^{c}}$ diverges, so $c_0 \ge \tfrac{1}{2}$.

---

### Conclusion

Combining both parts, $c_0 = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
