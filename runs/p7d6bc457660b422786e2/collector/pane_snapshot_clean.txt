## Solution

We seek the natural number $n$ such that $\mathbf{Q}(z_5, z_7) = \mathbf{Q}(z_n)
$, where $z_k = e^{2\pi i/k}$.

**Claim:** $n = 35$.

**Proof.**

**Step 1: $\mathbf{Q}(z_5, z_7) \subseteq \mathbf{Q}(z_{35})$.**

Since $z_5 = e^{2\pi i/5} = e^{2\pi i \cdot 7/35} = z_{35}^7$ and $z_7 = e^{2\pi
 i/7} = e^{2\pi i \cdot 5/35} = z_{35}^5$, both $z_5$ and $z_7$ lie in $\mathbf{
Q}(z_{35})$. Hence $\mathbf{Q}(z_5, z_7) \subseteq \mathbf{Q}(z_{35})$.

**Step 2: $\mathbf{Q}(z_{35}) \subseteq \mathbf{Q}(z_5, z_7)$.**

Since $\gcd(5, 7) = 1$, by Bézout's identity there exist integers $a, b$ with $5
a + 7b = 1$. Taking $a = 3, b = -2$: $5 \cdot 3 + 7 \cdot (-2) = 1$. Dividing by
 $35$:

$$\frac{1}{35} = \frac{3}{7} - \frac{2}{5}.$$

Therefore

$$z_{35} = e^{2\pi i/35} = e^{2\pi i(3/7 - 2/5)} = e^{2\pi i \cdot 3/7} \cdot e^
{-2\pi i \cdot 2/5} = z_7^3 \cdot z_5^{-2} \in \mathbf{Q}(z_5, z_7).$$

Hence $\mathbf{Q}(z_{35}) \subseteq \mathbf{Q}(z_5, z_7)$.

**Step 3: Degree verification.**

Combining Steps 1 and 2, $\mathbf{Q}(z_5, z_7) = \mathbf{Q}(z_{35})$.

As a consistency check: since $\gcd(5,7)=1$, the cyclotomic fields $\mathbf{Q}(z
_5)$ and $\mathbf{Q}(z_7)$ intersect trivially ($\mathbf{Q}(z_5) \cap \mathbf{Q}
(z_7) = \mathbf{Q}(z_{\gcd(5,7)}) = \mathbf{Q}$), so

$$[\mathbf{Q}(z_5, z_7) : \mathbf{Q}] = \phi(5) \cdot \phi(7) = 4 \cdot 6 = 24.$
$

On the other hand, $\phi(35) = \phi(5)\phi(7) = 4 \cdot 6 = 24$, so $[\mathbf{Q}
(z_{35}) : \mathbf{Q}] = 24$. The degrees match, confirming the equality. $\squa
re$

The natural number is $\boxed{n = 35}$.
