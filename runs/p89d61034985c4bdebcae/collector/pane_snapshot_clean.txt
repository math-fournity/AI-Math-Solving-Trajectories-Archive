**Problem.** Determine the values of $p$ for which the unit ball in $\ell^p(\mat
hbb{N})$ is weakly compact.

**Answer.** The unit ball in $\ell^p(\mathbb{N})$ is weakly compact if and only
if $1 < p < \infty$.

---

**Proof.**

The key tool is the following classical theorem:

> **Kakutani's Theorem.** A Banach space $X$ is reflexive (i.e., the canonical e
mbedding $J: X \to X^{**}$ is surjective) if and only if its closed unit ball $B
_X$ is weakly compact.

(Weak compactness of the unit ball is equivalent to reflexivity; this is sometim
es stated as part of the Kakutan–Eberlein–Šmulian circle of results. The Eberlei
n–Šmulian theorem further tells us that weak compactness is equivalent to weak s
equential compactness, but we only need the Kakutani characterization here.)

Thus the problem reduces to determining for which $p$ the space $\ell^p(\mathbb{
N})$ is reflexive. We treat the cases $1 < p < \infty$, $p = 1$, and $p = \infty
$ separately.

---

**Case 1: $1 < p < \infty$.** Let $q$ be the conjugate exponent defined by $\fra
c{1}{p} + \frac{1}{q} = 1$. Since $1 < p < \infty$, we also have $1 < q < \infty
$. A standard result in Banach space theory gives an isometric isomorphism

$$(\ell^p)^* \cong \ell^q.$$

Applying this result again to $\ell^q$ (which is legitimate since $1 < q < \inft
y$), with conjugate exponent $p$:

$$(\ell^q)^* \cong \ell^p.$$

Composing, $(\ell^p)^{**} \cong \ell^p$. Moreover, tracing through the standard
identification, the composition $(\ell^p)^{**} \to \ell^p$ agrees with the canon
ical embedding $J: \ell^p \to (\ell^p)^{**}$ (one verifies that the standard dua
l-pairing identification sends a functional on $\ell^q$ given by pairing against
 $x \in \ell^p$ back to $x$ itself). Hence $J$ is surjective, $\ell^p$ is reflex
ive, and by Kakutani's theorem its closed unit ball is weakly compact.

---

**Case 2: $p = 1$.** Here $(\ell^1)^* \cong \ell^\infty$. The space $\ell^1$ is
separable (the finitely supported sequences with rational coefficients are count
able and dense). However, $\ell^\infty$ is **not** separable: for each binary se
quence $a \in \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$ (uncountably many), the characteristic funct
ion $\mathbf{1}_a \in \ell^\infty$, and any two distinct such functions are at d
istance $1$, so $\ell^\infty$ cannot have a countable dense subset.

If $\ell^1$ were reflexive, then $\ell^1 \cong (\ell^1)^{**} \cong (\ell^\infty)
^*$. A standard fact is that if a Banach space $Y$ is separable, then $Y^*$ need
 not be separable, but if $Y^*$ is separable then $Y$ is separable. Contrapositi
vely: if $Y$ is non-separable, then... — this direction is not directly useful.
Instead, we use the cleaner argument:

If $\ell^1$ were reflexive, then $(\ell^1)^* = \ell^\infty$ would be reflexive (
the dual of a reflexive space is reflexive). A reflexive space is isomorphic to
its double dual. But $\ell^\infty$ is non-separable while containing the non-ref
lexive closed subspace $c_0$ (the space of sequences converging to $0$). A close
d subspace of a reflexive space is reflexive (since the unit ball of the subspac
e is a weakly closed subset of the weakly compact unit ball of the ambient space
, hence weakly compact). Since $c_0$ is not reflexive — for instance, $(c_0)^* \
cong \ell^1$ and $(c_0)^{**} \cong \ell^\infty \not\cong c_0$ because $\ell^\inf
ty$ is non-separable and $c_0$ is separable — this is a contradiction. Hence $\e
ll^1$ is not reflexive, and its unit ball is not weakly compact.

*(A more direct argument: $\ell^1$ separable but $(\ell^1)^* = \ell^\infty$ non-
separable already rules out reflexivity, since a reflexive separable space has s
eparable dual: if $X$ is separable and reflexive, $X^* \cong $ a quotient of $X^
{**} \cong X$, and... — more cleanly, if $X$ is reflexive then $X$ separable $\i
ff$ $X^*$ separable. Since $\ell^1$ is separable but $\ell^\infty$ is not, $\ell
^1$ is not reflexive.)*

---

**Case 3: $p = \infty$.** The space $\ell^\infty$ contains $c_0$ as a closed sub
space. As noted above, $c_0$ is not reflexive. Since a closed subspace of a refl
exive Banach space is reflexive, $\ell^\infty$ cannot be reflexive. Therefore it
s closed unit ball is not weakly compact.

---

**Conclusion.** Combining the three cases:

$$\text{The closed unit ball of } \ell^p(\mathbb{N}) \text{ is weakly compact }
\iff 1 < p < \infty.$$
