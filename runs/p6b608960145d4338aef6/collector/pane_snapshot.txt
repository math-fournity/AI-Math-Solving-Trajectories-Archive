Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $\min \sigma_M(x) \leq \min
 \sigma_{eMe}(exe)$ for a positive element $x$ in a von Neumann algebra $M$ with
 projection $e \in M$.

**Answer: The statement is TRUE.**

## Proof

Let $M$ be a von Neumann algebra faithfully represented on a Hilbert space $H$,
so $M \subseteq B(H)$ with $I \in M$. Let $x \in M$ be positive ($x \geq 0$, i.e
., $x = x^*$ and $\sigma(x) \subseteq [0,\infty)$) and let $e \in M$ be a projec
tion ($e = e^* = e^2$).

**Step 1: $exe$ is a positive element of $eMe$.**

Since $x = x^*$, we have $(exe)^* = e^*x^*e^* = exe$, so $exe$ is self-adjoint.
For any $y \in eMe$ (so $y = eye$), the element $exe$ satisfies $e(exe)e = exe$,
 confirming $exe \in eMe$. Positivity: for any $\xi \in H$,
$$\langle (exe)\xi, \xi\rangle = \langle ex(e\xi), \xi\rangle = \langle x(e\xi),
 e\xi\rangle \geq 0,$$
since $x \geq 0$. Hence $exe \geq 0$ in $eMe$.

**Step 2: Spectrum in a von Neumann algebra equals spectrum as an operator.**

For a normal element $a$ in a von Neumann algebra $M \subseteq B(H)$, $\sigma_M(
a) = \sigma_{B(H)}(a)$. Indeed, if $a - \lambda$ is invertible in $B(H)$, then $
(a-\lambda)^{-1} = f(a)$ via Borel functional calculus (with $f(t) = 1/(t-\lambd
a)$ on $\sigma(a)$), and since $M$ is a von Neumann algebra containing $a$, Bore
l functional calculus keeps $f(a) \in M$. Thus invertibility in $B(H)$ implies i
nvertibility in $M$.

Applying this:
- $\sigma_M(x) = \sigma_{B(H)}(x)$, so $\min \sigma_M(x) = \min \sigma_{B(H)}(x)
$.
- $eMe$ is a von Neumann algebra on $eH$, and $exe \in eMe$ is self-adjoint, so
$\sigma_{eMe}(exe) = \sigma_{B(eH)}(exe|_{eH})$.

**Step 3: Relating spectra to quadratic forms.**

For any bounded positive operator $a$ on a Hilbert space $K$:
$$\min \sigma(a) = \inf_{\substack{\eta \in K \\ \|\eta\|=1}} \langle a\eta, \et
a\rangle.$$

*Justification:* Since $a \geq 0$, we have $a - \min\sigma(a)\cdot I \geq 0$, so
 $\langle a\eta,\eta\rangle \geq \min\sigma(a)$ for all unit $\eta$, giving $\in
f \geq \min\sigma(a)$. Conversely, for $\lambda_0 = \min\sigma(a) \in \sigma(a)$
, there exists a sequence of approximate unit eigenvectors $\{\eta_n\}$ with $\|
(a-\lambda_0)\eta_n\| \to 0$, $\|\eta_n\|=1$, which gives $\langle a\eta_n,\eta_
n\rangle \to \lambda_0$, so $\inf \leq \lambda_0$.

Applying this:
$$\min \sigma_M(x) = \inf_{\substack{\eta \in H \\ \|\eta\|=1}} \langle x\eta, \
eta\rangle, \qquad \min \sigma_{eMe}(exe) = \inf_{\substack{\xi \in eH \\ \|\xi\
|=1}} \langle (exe)\xi, \xi\rangle.$$

**Step 4: The quadratic form of $exe$ on $eH$ coincides with that of $x$.**

For $\xi \in eH$, we have $e\xi = \xi$, so:
$$\langle (exe)\xi, \xi\rangle = \langle e\,x\,(e\xi), \xi\rangle = \langle ex\x
i, \xi\rangle = \langle x\xi, e\xi\rangle = \langle x\xi, \xi\rangle,$$
using $e = e^*$ and $e\xi = \xi$.

Therefore:
$$\min \sigma_{eMe}(exe) = \inf_{\substack{\xi \in eH \\ \|\xi\|=1}} \langle x\x
i, \xi\rangle.$$

**Step 5: Comparison via subset inclusion.**

Since $eH \subseteq H$, the set of unit vectors in $eH$ is a subset of the set o
f unit vectors in $H$:
$$\{\xi \in eH : \|\xi\| = 1\} \subseteq \{\eta \in H : \|\eta\| = 1\}.$$

The infimum over a subset is at least the infimum over the whole set:
$$\inf_{\substack{\xi \in eH \\ \|\xi\|=1}} \langle x\xi, \xi\rangle \;\geq\; \i
nf_{\substack{\eta \in H \\ \|\eta\|=1}} \langle x\eta, \eta\rangle.$$

Substituting the identifications from Steps 3 and 4:
$$\min \sigma_{eMe}(exe) \;\geq\; \min \sigma_M(x).$$

Equivalently:
$$\boxed{\min \sigma_M(x) \leq \min \sigma_{eMe}(exe).}$$

**The statement is true.** The inequality holds because compressing a positive o
perator to a subspace (via a projection) can only raise the minimum of the spect
rum, never lower it — the infimum of the quadratic form over a subspace is alway
s at least the infimum over the entire space.
