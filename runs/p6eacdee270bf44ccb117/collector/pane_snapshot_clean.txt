## Solution

We minimize $P = x + y + z$ subject to $2xyz = 2x + 4y + 7z$ with $x, y, z > 0$.

### Step 1: Lagrange multiplier equations

Set $g(x,y,z) = 2xyz - 2x - 4y - 7z = 0$. At an interior extremum, $\nabla P = \
lambda \nabla g$:

$$1 = \lambda(2yz - 2),\quad 1 = \lambda(2xz - 4),\quad 1 = \lambda(2xy - 7).$$

Eliminating $\lambda$:

$$2yz - 2 = 2xz - 4 = 2xy - 7. \tag{*}$$

### Step 2: Derive relations among $x, y, z$

From the first two: $2z(y - x) = -2$, i.e. $z(x - y) = 1$. $\quad(1)$

From the last two: $2x(z - y) = -3$, i.e. $x(y - z) = \tfrac{3}{2}$. $\quad(2)$

From the first and third: $2y(z - x) = -5$, i.e. $y(x - z) = \tfrac{5}{2}$. $\qu
ad(3)$

Note $(3)$ is consistent with $(1)+(2)$ since $x - z = (x-y)+(y-z)$.

### Step 3: Solve the system

From $(1)$: $x = y + \tfrac{1}{z}$. Substituting into $(2)$: $\left(y + \tfrac{1
}{z}\right)(y - z) = \tfrac{3}{2}$, which (using $(3)$) reduces to

$$2yz\,(y - z) = 5z - 2y. \tag{A}$$

The original constraint, with $x = y + \tfrac{1}{z}$, becomes

$$2y^2 z = 4y + \tfrac{2}{z} + 7z. \tag{B}$$

**Try $z = 2$.** Equation (A): $4y(y - 2) = 10 - 2y$, i.e. $2y^2 - 3y - 5 = 0$,
giving $y = \tfrac{5}{2}$ (positive root). Check (B): LHS $= 2 \cdot \tfrac{25}{
4} \cdot 2 = 25$; RHS $= 4 \cdot \tfrac{5}{2} + 1 + 14 = 10 + 1 + 14 = 25$. ✓

Then $x = \tfrac{5}{2} + \tfrac{1}{2} = 3$.

### Step 4: Verify

$$2xyz = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot \tfrac{5}{2} \cdot 2 = 30, \qquad 2x + 4y + 7z = 6 + 10
 + 14 = 30.\ \checkmark$$

$$P = 3 + \tfrac{5}{2} + 2 = \tfrac{15}{2}.$$

### Step 5: Confirm this is the global minimum

The feasible surface $\{x,y,z > 0 : 2xyz = 2x + 4y + 7z\}$ requires $yz > 1$ (so
 that $x = \frac{4y+7z}{2(yz-1)} > 0$). As $yz \to 1^+$, $x \to \infty$ and $P \
to \infty$; as any variable $\to \infty$, $P \to \infty$. Hence $P$ attains its
minimum at the unique interior critical point found above.

### Answer

$$\boxed{P_{\min} = \dfrac{15}{2}}$$
