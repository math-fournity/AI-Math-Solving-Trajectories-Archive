## Proof

Let $B = A - A^t$. Then $B$ is skew-symmetric ($B^t = -B$) of size $2n \times 2n
$, with $B_{ii}=0$ and, for $i<j$,
$$B_{ij} = A_{ij} - A_{ji} \in \{-1,0,1\}, \quad P(B_{ij}=1)=P(B_{ij}=-1)=\tfrac
14,\; P(B_{ij}=0)=\tfrac12.$$

**Independence.** For distinct unordered pairs $\{i,j\}$, the variables $B_{ij}$
 depend on disjoint pairs of independent entries of $A$, so $\{B_{ij}: i<j\}$ ar
e independent. Each satisfies $\mathbb{E}[B_{ij}]=0$ and
$$\mathbb{E}[B_{ij}^2] = \tfrac14+\tfrac14 = \tfrac12, \qquad \mathbb{E}[B_{ij}^
{2k+1}]=0.$$

**Pfaffian.** Since $B$ is skew-symmetric of even size, $\det B = \operatorname{
pf}(B)^2$, hence $\mathbb{E}[\det B]=\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{pf}(B)^2]$. The Pf
affian is
$$\operatorname{pf}(B)=\sum_{M}\operatorname{sgn}(M)\prod_{\{i,j\}\in M}B_{ij},$
$
where $M$ ranges over perfect matchings of $\{1,\dots,2n\}$ (each pair written w
ith $i<j$).

**Squaring and taking expectation.**
$$\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{pf}(B)^2]=\sum_{M,M'}\operatorname{sgn}(M)\operatorna
me{sgn}(M')\;\mathbb{E}\!\left[\prod_{\{i,j\}\in M}B_{ij}\prod_{\{i',j'\}\in M'}
B_{i'j'}\right].$$

By independence, the expectation factors over unordered pairs. A pair $\{i,j\}$
appears in $M$ at most once and in $M'$ at most once, so its total multiplicity
is $0,1,$ or $2$. For the product's expectation to be nonzero, every pair must o
ccur with **even** multiplicity (odd moments vanish). Thus each pair occurs $0$
or $2$ times, meaning the set of pairs in $M$ equals the set of pairs in $M'$. S
ince both are perfect matchings, $M=M'$.

Therefore only diagonal terms survive:
$$\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{pf}(B)^2]=\sum_{M}\operatorname{sgn}(M)^2\prod_{\{i,j
\}\in M}\mathbb{E}[B_{ij}^2]=\sum_{M}\prod_{\{i,j\}\in M}\frac12.$$

Each matching has exactly $n$ pairs, so each term equals $(1/2)^n$. The number o
f perfect matchings of $2n$ elements is $(2n-1)!!=\frac{(2n)!}{2^n n!}$. Hence
$$\mathbb{E}[\det(A-A^t)]=(2n-1)!!\cdot\left(\frac12\right)^n=\frac{(2n)!}{2^n n
!}\cdot\frac{1}{2^n}=\boxed{\dfrac{(2n)!}{4^n\,n!}}.$$

**Check.** For $n=1$: $\frac{2!}{4\cdot1}=\frac12$, and $\det\begin{pmatrix}0&b\
\-b&0\end{pmatrix}=b^2$ with $\mathbb{E}[b^2]=\frac12$. ✓ For $n=2$: $\frac{24}{
32}=\frac34=3\cdot\frac14$, matching $3!!$ matchings each contributing $(1/2)^2$
. ✓
