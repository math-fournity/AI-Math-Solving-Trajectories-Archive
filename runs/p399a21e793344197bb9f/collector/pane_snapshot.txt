## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. For any polynomial $p(z)$ of degree $n \ge 2$, there exists $R >
 0$ such that $\displaystyle\int_{|z|=R} \frac{1}{p(z)}\,dz = 0$.

**Proof via the residue at infinity.**

Let $p(z) = a_n z^n + a_{n-1} z^{n-1} + \cdots + a_0$ with $a_n \ne 0$ and $n \g
e 2$. The function $f(z) = 1/p(z)$ is meromorphic on the Riemann sphere, with po
les exactly at the (finitely many) zeros of $p$.

The sum of all residues of a meromorphic function on the Riemann sphere — includ
ing the residue at infinity — equals zero. We compute the residue at infinity:

$$\operatorname{Res}_{\infty} f = -\operatorname{Res}_{w=0}\left[\frac{1}{w^2} f
\!\left(\tfrac{1}{w}\right)\right].$$

Since $p(1/w) = w^{-n}\bigl(a_n + a_{n-1}w + \cdots + a_0 w^n\bigr)$, we get

$$\frac{1}{w^2}f\!\left(\tfrac{1}{w}\right) = \frac{1}{w^2}\cdot\frac{w^n}{a_n +
 a_{n-1}w + \cdots + a_0 w^n} = \frac{w^{n-2}}{a_n + a_{n-1}w + \cdots + a_0 w^n
}.$$

The denominator is analytic and nonzero at $w = 0$ (its value there is $a_n \ne
0$). Since $n \ge 2$, the numerator $w^{n-2}$ has a **non-negative** power, so t
he Laurent expansion of $\frac{1}{w^2}f(1/w)$ about $w=0$ contains no $1/w$ term
. Hence

$$\operatorname{Res}_{w=0}\left[\frac{1}{w^2}f\!\left(\tfrac{1}{w}\right)\right]
 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \operatorname{Res}_{\infty} f = 0.$$

Therefore the sum of all **finite** residues of $1/p(z)$ is $0$.

Now let $z_1, \dots, z_m$ be the distinct zeros of $p$, and set

$$R_0 = \max_{1 \le j \le m} |z_j|, \qquad R = R_0 + 1.$$

Then $|z|=R$ encloses every zero of $p$ and $p$ has no zeros on $|z|=R$ (so the
integrand is holomorphic on a neighborhood of the contour). By the residue theor
em,

$$\int_{|z|=R} \frac{1}{p(z)}\,dz = 2\pi i \sum_{j=1}^{m} \operatorname{Res}\!\l
eft(\frac{1}{p},\, z_j\right) = 2\pi i \cdot 0 = 0.$$

**Direct verification (alternative).** For $|z|$ large, $1/p(z) = \frac{1}{a_n z
^n}\bigl(1 + O(1/z)\bigr) = \sum_{k=n}^{\infty} c_k\, z^{-k}$, a Laurent series
in $1/z$ whose lowest power is $z^{-n}$ with $n \ge 2$. Termwise integration ove
r $|z|=R$ gives $\oint z^{-k}\,dz = 0$ for every $k \ge 2$, so the integral vani
shes for all sufficiently large $R$.

Both arguments confirm: **such an $R$ exists** (indeed, any $R$ strictly larger
than the largest modulus of the zeros of $p$ works).
