## Solution

Let \(f:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2\) be defined by \(f(x,y)=(u,v)=(e^{2x+y},\,e
^{x+y})\).

**Step 1. Image and injectivity.**

Since exponentials are positive, \(u>0\) and \(v>0\), so the image is contained
in \((0,\infty)^2\). Conversely, given any \((u,v)\in(0,\infty)^2\), set
\[
x=\ln u-\ln v=\ln\!\tfrac{u}{v},\qquad y=2\ln v-\ln u=\ln\!\tfrac{v^2}{u}.
\]
Then \(e^{2x+y}=e^{2\ln(u/v)+\ln(v^2/u)}=e^{\ln u}=u\) and \(e^{x+y}=e^{\ln(u/v)
+\ln(v^2/u)}=e^{\ln v}=v\). Hence \(f\) is a bijection from \(\mathbb{R}^2\) ont
o \((0,\infty)^2\), with smooth inverse \(g(u,v)=(\ln(u/v),\,\ln(v^2/u))\).

**Step 2. Jacobian of \(f\).**

\[
Df(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}2e^{2x+y}&e^{2x+y}\\ e^{x+y}&e^{x+y}\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
\det Df(x,y)=2e^{2x+y}\,e^{x+y}-e^{2x+y}\,e^{x+y}=e^{2x+y}\,e^{x+y}=e^{3x+2y}.
\]
Since \(u=e^{2x+y}\) and \(v=e^{x+y}\), we have \(uv=e^{3x+2y}\), hence
\[
\det Df(x,y)=uv>0.
\]

**Step 3. Density of the pushforward measure.**

For any Borel set \(B\subseteq(0,\infty)^2\), by the change-of-variables formula
,
\[
f[\lambda_2](B)=\lambda_2(f^{-1}(B))=\int_{B}\bigl|\det Dg(u,v)\bigr|\,d\lambda_
2(u,v),
\]
where \(g=f^{-1}\). Since \(\det Dg(u,v)=1/\det Df(g(u,v))=1/(uv)\), we obtain
\[
f[\lambda_2](B)=\int_{B}\frac{1}{uv}\,d\lambda_2(u,v).
\]
For \(B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^2\setminus(0,\infty)^2\), we have \(f^{-1}(B)=\varnot
hing\), so \(f[\lambda_2](B)=0\).

Therefore the pushforward measure \(f[\lambda_2]\) is absolutely continuous with
 respect to \(\lambda_2\), and its Radon–Nikodym density is

\[
\boxed{\dfrac{df[\lambda_2]}{d\lambda_2}(u,v)=\frac{1}{uv}\,\mathbf{1}_{(0,\inft
y)^2}(u,v).}
\]
