## Proof

Take $B = \mathbb{C}^2$ with coordinatewise operations:
$$(a_1,a_2)+(b_1,b_2)=(a_1+b_1,a_2+b_2),\quad \lambda(a_1,a_2)=(\lambda a_1,\lam
bda a_2),$$
$$(a_1,a_2)(b_1,b_2)=(a_1 b_1,a_2 b_2),$$
and the norm $\|(a_1,a_2)\|=\max(|a_1|,|a_2|)$.

**$B$ is a Banach algebra with unit.** Completeness of $(\mathbb{C}^2,\|\cdot\|_
\infty)$ is standard (a sequence is Cauchy/ convergent iff each coordinate is, i
n $\mathbb{C}$). The norm is submultiplicative:
$$\|(a_1,a_2)(b_1,b_2)\|=\max(|a_1 b_1|,|a_2 b_2|)\le \max(|a_1|,|a_2|)\max(|b_1
|,|b_2|)=\|(a_1,a_2)\|\,\|(b_1,b_2)\|.$$
The unit of $B$ is $\mathbf{1}_B=(1,1)$, since $(1,1)(a_1,a_2)=(a_1,a_2)$.

**Define $C=\{(a,0):a\in\mathbb{C}\}\subset B$.** This is a linear subspace clos
ed under coordinatewise multiplication:
$$(a,0)+(b,0)=(a+b,0),\quad \lambda(a,0)=(\lambda a,0),\quad (a,0)(b,0)=(ab,0).$
$
It is complete: a sequence $(a_n,0)$ is Cauchy/convergent in $C$ iff $a_n$ is Ca
uchy/convergent in $\mathbb{C}$, and the limit $(\lim a_n,0)\in C$. Equivalently
, $C$ is the kernel of the continuous projection $(a_1,a_2)\mapsto a_2$, hence c
losed in the complete space $B$, hence complete. The norm is the restriction of
$\|\cdot\|_\infty$, i.e. $\|(a,0)\|=|a|$.

**$C$ has a unit, and it is not $\mathbf{1}_B$.** Set $e=(1,0)\in C$. For every
$(a,0)\in C$,
$$e(a,0)=(1,0)(a,0)=(a,0),\qquad (a,0)e=(a,0)(1,0)=(a,0).$$
So $e=(1,0)$ is the unit of $C$. But
$$e=(1,0)\neq(1,1)=\mathbf{1}_B.$$

Thus $C\subset B$ is a Banach subalgebra (with inherited operations and norm) po
ssessing a unit element $(1,0)$ distinct from the unit $(1,1)$ of $B$.
