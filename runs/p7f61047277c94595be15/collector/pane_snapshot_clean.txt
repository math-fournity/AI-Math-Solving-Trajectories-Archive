# Proof

Let $L = K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]/M$, and let $\pi: K[X_1,\ldots,X_n] \to L$ be the quo
tient map. Consider the natural map
$$\phi: K \longrightarrow L, \qquad a \longmapsto \pi(a) = \overline{a}.$$

**Step 1: $\phi$ is an injective field homomorphism.**

Since $K$ is a field, $\ker(\phi)$ is an ideal of $K$, hence either $\{0\}$ or $
K$. But $\phi(1) = \overline{1} \neq \overline{0}$ (since $M$ is a proper ideal)
, so $\ker(\phi) = \{0\}$. Thus $\phi$ embeds $K$ into $L$, making $L$ a field e
xtension of $K$.

**Step 2: $L$ is a finitely generated $K$-algebra.**

As a $K$-algebra, $L$ is generated by $\overline{X_1}, \ldots, \overline{X_n}$ (
every element of $L$ is the class of a polynomial in $X_1,\ldots,X_n$, which is
a $K$-linear combination of monomials in these generators). So $L$ is a finitely
 generated $K$-algebra that is also a field.

**Step 3: Apply Zariski's Lemma.**

Zariski's Lemma states: *if a field $L$ is a finitely generated algebra over a f
ield $K$ (via a homomorphism $K \hookrightarrow L$), then $L$ is a finite algebr
aic extension of $K$.*

Applying this to our situation, $L$ is a finite algebraic extension of $K$ (via
the embedding $\phi$).

**Step 4: $K$ algebraically closed forces $L = K$.**

Since $K$ is algebraically closed, it admits no nontrivial algebraic extensions.
 Every element $\alpha \in L$ is algebraic over $K$, so its minimal polynomial o
ver $K$ is linear (because $K$ is algebraically closed, every non-constant polyn
omial in $K[T]$ splits into linear factors). Hence $\alpha \in \phi(K)$.

Therefore $\phi: K \to L$ is **surjective**, and consequently $\phi$ is an isomo
rphism of fields.

**Step 5: Construct $\sigma$.**

Set $\sigma = \phi^{-1}: L \to K$. This is an isomorphism of fields. For any $a
\in K$:
$$\sigma(\overline{a}) = \phi^{-1}(\phi(a)) = \phi^{-1}(\overline{a}) = a.$$

Thus $\sigma$ fixes every element of $K$ (viewed inside $L$ via the natural embe
dding $a \mapsto \overline{a}$).

**Conclusion.** Yes, such an isomorphism exists. The inverse of the natural embe
dding $K \hookrightarrow K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]/M$ is an isomorphism $\sigma: K[X_1,\
ldots,X_n]/M \to K$ satisfying $\sigma(\overline{a}) = a$ for all $a \in K$. The
 essential ingredients are: (1) Zariski's Lemma, which forces the quotient to be
 an algebraic extension of $K$, and (2) the algebraic closedness of $K$, which f
orces that algebraic extension to be trivial, making the natural embedding $K \h
ookrightarrow L$ itself an isomorphism.
