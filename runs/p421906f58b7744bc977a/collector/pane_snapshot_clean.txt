Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the inversion map $f \mapst
o f^{-1}$ is continuous on $H$, the space of uniformly continuous self-homeomorp
hisms of $G$ (with uniformly continuous inverses) under the uniform metric.

**Interpretation.** Since the inversion map is stated to go "from $H$ to $H$," e
ach $f \in H$ must be a self-homeomorphism of $G$ (i.e., $f: G \to G$ is a bijec
tion), so that $f^{-1}: G \to G$ also lies in $H$. The metric $d(f,g) = \sup_{z
\in G}|f(z)-g(z)|$ is well-defined on such functions.

---

**Theorem.** The inversion map $\iota: H \to H$, $\iota(f) = f^{-1}$, is continu
ous. That is, if $f_n \to f$ uniformly on $G$ with $f, f_n \in H$, then $f_n^{-1
} \to f^{-1}$ uniformly on $G$.

**Proof.**

Suppose $f_n \to f$ uniformly on $G$, i.e., $\|f_n - f\|_\infty := \sup_{z \in G
}|f_n(z) - f(z)| \to 0$.

**Step 1: Reduce to the case $f = \mathrm{id}$.**

Define $h_n = f_n \circ f^{-1}: G \to G$. Since $f_n$ and $f^{-1}$ are both bije
ctions of $G$ onto $G$, so is $h_n$; thus $h_n$ is a self-homeomorphism of $G$ w
ith $h_n^{-1} = f \circ f_n^{-1}: G \to G$.

Compute the uniform distance from $h_n$ to the identity. For any $y \in G$, let
$x = f^{-1}(y) \in G$, so $y = f(x)$. Then:
$$h_n(y) - y = f_n(f^{-1}(y)) - f(f^{-1}(y)) = f_n(x) - f(x).$$
Since $f^{-1}: G \to G$ is a bijection, as $y$ ranges over $G$, $x = f^{-1}(y)$
ranges over all of $G$. Therefore:
$$\|h_n - \mathrm{id}\|_\infty = \sup_{y \in G}|h_n(y) - y| = \sup_{x \in G}|f_n
(x) - f(x)| = \|f_n - f\|_\infty \to 0.$$

**Step 2: Show $\|h_n^{-1} - \mathrm{id}\|_\infty \leq \|h_n - \mathrm{id}\|_\in
fty \to 0$.**

Write $h_n = \mathrm{id} + g_n$ where $g_n := h_n - \mathrm{id}$, so $\|g_n\|_\i
nfty = \|h_n - \mathrm{id}\|_\infty \to 0$.

For any $y \in G$, set $x_n = h_n^{-1}(y) \in G$ (well-defined since $h_n^{-1}:
G \to G$). Then $h_n(x_n) = y$, i.e., $x_n + g_n(x_n) = y$, giving:
$$h_n^{-1}(y) - y = x_n - y = -g_n(x_n).$$
Since $x_n \in G$:
$$|h_n^{-1}(y) - y| = |g_n(x_n)| \leq \sup_{z \in G}|g_n(z)| = \|g_n\|_\infty.$$
Taking the supremum over $y \in G$:
$$\|h_n^{-1} - \mathrm{id}\|_\infty \leq \|h_n - \mathrm{id}\|_\infty \to 0.$$

**Step 3: Conclude using uniform continuity of $f^{-1}$.**

Since $f_n^{-1} = (h_n \circ f)^{-1} = f^{-1} \circ h_n^{-1}$, we have for all $
y \in G$:
$$|f_n^{-1}(y) - f^{-1}(y)| = |f^{-1}(h_n^{-1}(y)) - f^{-1}(y)|.$$

By hypothesis, $f^{-1}$ is **uniformly continuous** on $G$. Given $\varepsilon >
 0$, there exists $\delta > 0$ such that for all $z, w \in G$, $|z - w| < \delta
 \implies |f^{-1}(z) - f^{-1}(w)| < \varepsilon$.

By Step 2, for all sufficiently large $n$, $\|h_n^{-1} - \mathrm{id}\|_\infty <
\delta$, so for every $y \in G$:
$$|h_n^{-1}(y) - y| < \delta \implies |f^{-1}(h_n^{-1}(y)) - f^{-1}(y)| < \varep
silon.$$
Therefore $\|f_n^{-1} - f^{-1}\|_\infty \leq \varepsilon$ for all large $n$, pro
ving $f_n^{-1} \to f^{-1}$ uniformly on $G$. $\blacksquare$

---

**Remarks on the hypotheses.**

- **Uniform continuity of $f^{-1}$ is essential.** It converts the uniform conve
rgence $h_n^{-1} \to \mathrm{id}$ (Step 2) into uniform convergence of the compo
sition $f^{-1} \circ h_n^{-1} \to f^{-1}$. Mere continuity of $f^{-1}$ would onl
y yield pointwise convergence, since $G$ is open (hence non-compact) and continu
ous functions on non-compact sets need not preserve uniform convergence under co
mposition.

- **The self-homeomorphism property ($f(G) = G$) is essential.** It ensures $h_n
^{-1}(y) \in G$ so that $g_n(h_n^{-1}(y))$ is controlled by $\|g_n\|_\infty$, an
d it ensures $f_n^{-1}$ and $f^{-1}$ share the common domain $G$.

- **Boundedness and openness of $G$** are not directly used in the estimate of S
tep 2 (which holds for any domain), but they are relevant to the meaningfulness
of the hypotheses: on a non-compact domain like an open bounded set, uniform con
tinuity of $f$ and $f^{-1}$ is a genuine restriction (not automatic), making $H$
 a proper subspace of all self-homeomorphisms.

The inversion map $f \mapsto f^{-1}$ is **continuous** from $H$ to $H$.
