# Proof: An Infinite-Dimensional Separable Hilbert Space over ℂ is a Banach Lie
Group

## Setup and Definitions

Let \(H\) be an infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space over \(\mathbb{C}\)
, with inner product \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\) and induced norm \(\|x\|=\sqr
t{\langle x,x\rangle}\). Since \(H\) is complete with respect to this norm, it i
s a **Banach space**. By separability and infinite dimensionality, \(H\) is isom
etrically isomorphic to \(\ell^2(\mathbb{C})\).

A **Banach Lie group** is a group \(G\) equipped with the structure of a Banach
manifold (modeled on some Banach space) such that:
- the multiplication map \(m: G \times G \to G\), \(m(g,h)=gh\), is smooth, and
- the inversion map \(\iota: G \to G\), \(\iota(g)=g^{-1}\), is smooth.

We show that \(H\) admits the structure of an **abelian Banach Lie group** under
 vector addition.

## Step 1: \(H\) is a Banach manifold

A Banach manifold is a topological space locally modeled on a Banach space, with
 smooth transition maps. The space \(H\) itself is a Banach space, so it is triv
ially a Banach manifold: it is covered by the single global chart \(\varphi: H \
to H\), \(\varphi(x)=x\), with model Banach space \(H\). There are no transition
 maps to check (the single overlap is the identity). The topology is the norm to
pology, which is Hausdorff. Since \(H\) is separable, it is second-countable, en
suring the manifold is paracompact (a standard requirement for manifolds to supp
ort smooth partitions of unity).

## Step 2: Group structure under addition

Define the group operation on \(H\) to be **vector addition**:
$$m: H \times H \to H, \quad m(x,y) = x + y.$$
- **Identity element**: \(0 \in H\), since \(x + 0 = x\) for all \(x\).
- **Inverse**: \(\iota(x) = -x\), since \(x + (-x) = 0\).
- **Associativity**: \((x+y)+z = x+(y+z)\) holds by vector space axioms.
- **Commutativity**: \(x+y = y+x\), so the group is **abelian**.

Thus \((H, +)\) is an abelian group.

## Step 3: Smoothness of the group operations

**Addition is smooth.** The map \(m: H \times H \to H\) is bilinear and continuo
us (since \(\|x+y\| \le \|x\| + \|y\|\)). A continuous linear (or more generally
, continuous multilinear) map between Banach spaces is smooth (\(C^\infty\)). Co
ncretely:
- The Fréchet derivative of \(m\) at \((x_0, y_0)\) is the bounded linear map \(
Dm_{(x_0,y_0)}(u,v) = u + v\), which is independent of the base point.
- The second and higher derivatives are zero (since the first derivative is cons
tant), so \(m\) is \(C^\infty\).

**Inversion is smooth.** The map \(\iota: H \to H\), \(\iota(x) = -x\), is a bou
nded linear map (with operator norm 1). Every bounded linear map between Banach
spaces is smooth: its derivative is itself (constant), and all higher derivative
s vanish. Hence \(\iota\) is \(C^\infty\).

## Step 4: Verification of all Banach Lie group axioms

We verify:
1. **Underlying space is a Banach manifold**: ✓ (Step 1 — single global chart on
 the Banach space \(H\)).
2. **Group structure**: ✓ (Step 2 — \((H,+)\) is an abelian group).
3. **Multiplication is smooth**: ✓ (Step 3 — addition is continuous bilinear, he
nce \(C^\infty\)).
4. **Inversion is smooth**: ✓ (Step 3 — negation is bounded linear, hence \(C^\i
nfty\)).

All axioms of a Banach Lie group are satisfied.

## Step 5: Addressing the classical (finite-dimensional) caveat

In the **classical** sense, a "Lie group" is sometimes defined as a group that i
s a **finite-dimensional** smooth manifold. Under that strict definition, no inf
inite-dimensional space — including \(H\) — can be a Lie group, since \(H\) is n
ot locally homeomorphic to any \(\mathbb{R}^n\) or \(\mathbb{C}^n\) (it is infin
ite-dimensional).

However, the problem explicitly states "given that it is a Banach space," direct
ing us to the **generalized (Banach) Lie group** framework, in which the model s
pace is an arbitrary Banach space rather than \(\mathbb{R}^n\). In this framewor
k — which is the standard and well-established generalization of Lie theory to i
nfinite dimensions (cf. the theory of Banach–Lie groups, Hilbert–Lie groups, and
 more generally Fréchet–Lie groups) — the answer is affirmative.

## Conclusion

An infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space \(H\) over \(\mathbb{C}\), being
 a Banach space, **can be considered a Lie group** in the sense of **Banach Lie
groups**: under vector addition, \(H\) is an **abelian Banach Lie group**. The g
roup operations (addition and negation) are smooth (in fact, real-analytic) maps
 in the Banach-space sense, and \(H\) is trivially a Banach manifold via its glo
bal identity chart. The separability of \(H\) ensures the underlying manifold is
 second-countable and paracompact.
