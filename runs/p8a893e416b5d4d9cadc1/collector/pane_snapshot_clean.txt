# Lebesgue Decomposition Theorem

**Theorem (Lebesgue Decomposition).** Let $(X, \mathfrak{A}, \mu)$ be a $\sigma$
-finite positive measure space, and let $\nu$ be a complex measure (or a real me
asure, or a $\sigma$-finite positive signed measure) on $\mathfrak{A}$. Then $\n
u$ admits a unique decomposition

$$\nu = \nu_a + \nu_s,$$

where:

1. **$\nu_a$ is absolutely continuous with respect to $\mu$** (denoted $\nu_a \l
l \mu$): every $\mu$-null set is $\nu_a$-null, i.e.,
   $$\mu(E) = 0 \implies \nu_a(E) = 0 \quad \text{for all } E \in \mathfrak{A}.$
$

2. **$\nu_s$ is singular with respect to $\mu$** (denoted $\nu_s \perp \mu$): th
ere exists a measurable set $A \in \mathfrak{A}$ such that
   $$\mu(A) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \nu_s\big|_{X \setminus A} = 0,$$
   i.e., $\nu_s$ is concentrated on a $\mu$-null set.

Moreover, both $\nu_a$ and $\nu_s$ are measures of the same type as $\nu$ (compl
ex, real, or $\sigma$-finite positive, respectively), and the decomposition is *
*unique**: if $\nu = \nu_a' + \nu_s'$ is another such decomposition with $\nu_a'
 \ll \mu$ and $\nu_s' \perp \mu$, then $\nu_a = \nu_a'$ and $\nu_s = \nu_s'$.

## Proof of Existence and Uniqueness

**Existence.** We treat the case where $\nu$ is a complex measure; the real and
$\sigma$-finite positive cases follow by the Jordan decomposition (or directly b
y the same argument applied to the total variation).

*Step 1 — Reduction to the positive $\sigma$-finite case.* By the Jordan decompo
sition, a complex measure $\nu$ has finite total variation $|\nu|$, which is a f
inite (hence $\sigma$-finite) positive measure. It suffices to decompose the fin
ite positive measure $|\nu|$ with respect to $\mu$; the decomposition of $\nu$ t
hen follows by the Radon–Nikodym theorem applied to the absolutely continuous pa
rt.

*Step 2 — The finite positive case.* Assume first that $\nu$ is a **finite** pos
itive measure. Consider the family of measurable sets

$$\mathcal{N} = \{E \in \mathfrak{A} : \mu(E) = 0\}.$$

Since $\nu$ is finite, $\sup\{\nu(E) : E \in \mathcal{N}\} \leq \nu(X) < \infty$
. Choose a sequence $(E_n) \subseteq \mathcal{N}$ with $\nu(E_n) \to \sup_{\math
cal{N}} \nu$. Set

$$A = \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} E_n.$$

Then $\mu(A) = 0$ (countable subadditivity, since each $\mu(E_n)=0$), and by mon
otonicity $\nu(A) = \sup_{\mathcal{N}} \nu$ (using that $\nu$ is finite and the
$E_n$ can be chosen so that $A$ attains the supremum via a standard exhaustion a
rgument: replace $E_n$ by $\bigcup_{k \le n} E_k$ and use continuity from below)
.

Define

$$\nu_s(E) = \nu(E \cap A), \qquad \nu_a(E) = \nu(E \setminus A) = \nu(E) - \nu_
s(E).$$

Then $\nu = \nu_a + \nu_s$ trivially. Since $\mu(A) = 0$, $\nu_s$ is concentrate
d on $A$, so $\nu_s \perp \mu$.

It remains to show $\nu_a \ll \mu$. Suppose $\mu(E) = 0$. Then $E \cup A \in \ma
thcal{N}$ (since $\mu(E \cup A) \le \mu(E) + \mu(A) = 0$), so

$$\nu(E \cup A) \le \nu(A).$$

But $E \setminus A \subseteq E$ and $\mu(E \setminus A) = 0$, and

$$\nu(E \cup A) = \nu(A) + \nu(E \setminus A) \ge \nu(A),$$

so $\nu(E \setminus A) = 0$, i.e., $\nu_a(E) = 0$. Hence $\nu_a \ll \mu$.

*Step 3 — The $\sigma$-finite case.* If $\nu$ is $\sigma$-finite positive, write
 $X = \bigcup_n X_n$ with $\nu(X_n) < \infty$. Apply Step 2 to each restriction
$\nu|_{X_n}$ (which is finite) to obtain $\nu|_{X_n} = \nu_{a,n} + \nu_{s,n}$ wi
th $\nu_{a,n} \ll \mu$ and $\nu_{s,n} \perp \mu$. Set

$$\nu_a = \sum_n \nu_{a,n}, \qquad \nu_s = \sum_n \nu_{s,n}.$$

Then $\nu = \nu_a + \nu_s$, $\nu_a \ll \mu$ (a countable union of $\mu$-null set
s is $\mu$-null), and $\nu_s \perp \mu$ (each $\nu_{s,n}$ is supported on a $\mu
$-null set $A_n$; then $\nu_s$ is supported on $\bigcup_n A_n$, which is $\mu$-n
ull).

**Uniqueness.** Suppose $\nu = \nu_a + \nu_s = \nu_a' + \nu_s'$ with $\nu_a, \nu
_a' \ll \mu$ and $\nu_s, \nu_s' \perp \mu$. Then

$$\nu_a - \nu_a' = \nu_s' - \nu_s.$$

The left side is absolutely continuous w.r.t. $\mu$; the right side is singular
w.r.t. $\mu$ (a difference of two singular measures is singular). A measure that
 is both absolutely continuous and singular w.r.t. $\mu$ must be zero: if $\eta
\ll \mu$ and $\eta \perp \mu$, then $\eta$ is supported on a $\mu$-null set $A$,
 but $\eta \ll \mu$ forces $\eta(A) = 0$, and since $\eta$ is concentrated on $A
$, $\eta \equiv 0$.

Hence $\nu_a = \nu_a'$ and $\nu_s = \nu_s'$. ∎

## Connection to the Radon–Nikodym Theorem

Since $\nu_a \ll \mu$ and (in the complex/real/finite case) $\nu_a$ is a finite
signed/complex measure while $\mu$ is $\sigma$-finite, the **Radon–Nikodym theor
em** applies: there exists a unique $f \in L^1(\mu)$ (integrable, complex- or re
al-valued) such that

$$\nu_a(E) = \int_E f \, d\mu \quad \text{for all } E \in \mathfrak{A}.$$

Thus the full Lebesgue decomposition can be written as

$$\boxed{\nu(E) = \int_E f \, d\mu + \nu_s(E), \qquad \nu_s \perp \mu,\; f \in L
^1(\mu),}$$

with $f$ (the Radon–Nikodym derivative, $f = d\nu_a/d\mu$) and $\nu_s$ uniquely
determined.

## Summary

The Lebesgue Decomposition Theorem states that any complex (or real, or $\sigma$
-finite positive) measure $\nu$ on a $\sigma$-finite positive measure space $(X,
 \mathfrak{A}, \mu)$ decomposes **uniquely** as $\nu = \nu_a + \nu_s$, where $\n
u_a \ll \mu$ (absolutely continuous) and $\nu_s \perp \mu$ (singular). The absol
utely continuous part is representable as integration against a Radon–Nikodym de
rivative $d\nu_a/d\mu$.
