Looking at this problem, I need to determine the smallest possible residue field
 of a perfectoid field as defined in the problem.

## Analysis

**Definition given:** A field of characteristic $p > 0$ is a *perfectoid field*
if it is complete and every nonzero element has a unique $p$-th root (i.e., the
Frobenius map $\Phi: x \mapsto x^p$ is bijective — this is the perfection condit
ion).

**Key claim:** The residue field of a perfectoid field must itself be a perfect
field of characteristic $p$.

**Proof of the claim:** Let $K$ be a perfectoid field with valuation $v$, valuat
ion ring $\mathcal{O}_K$, and maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}_K$. The residue field
is $k = \mathcal{O}_K / \mathfrak{m}_K$.

Since Frobenius $\Phi(x) = x^p$ is bijective on $K$:
- **$\Phi$ maps $\mathcal{O}_K$ onto $\mathcal{O}_K$:** If $y \in \mathcal{O}_K$
, then $y = x^p$ for some $x \in K$, and $v(x) = v(y)/p \geq 0$, so $x \in \math
cal{O}_K$.
- **$\Phi$ maps $\mathfrak{m}_K$ onto $\mathfrak{m}_K$:** If $y \in \mathfrak{m}
_K$, then $v(y) > 0$, so $v(x) = v(y)/p > 0$, giving $x \in \mathfrak{m}_K$.

Therefore $\Phi$ descends to a bijection on the quotient $k = \mathcal{O}_K/\mat
hfrak{m}_K$, proving $k$ is **perfect**.

**Smallest perfect field:** A perfect field of characteristic $p$ that is algebr
aic over the prime field must contain $\mathbb{F}_p$, which is itself perfect an
d has exactly $p$ elements. No field of characteristic $p$ can have fewer than $
p$ elements (it must contain the prime field $\mathbb{F}_p$).

**Realizability:** The residue field $\mathbb{F}_p$ is achieved concretely. Take
 $K = \widehat{\mathbb{F}_p((t))^{perf}}$, the completion of the perfection of $
\mathbb{F}_p((t))$. This is a complete perfect field of characteristic $p$ with
the $t$-adic valuation (value group $\mathbb{Z}[1/p]$, which is non-discrete), a
nd its residue field is $\mathbb{F}_p$.

**Minimizing over all primes:** The problem allows any prime $p > 0$. The residu
e field has at least $p$ elements, and the smallest prime is $p = 2$. The field
$\mathbb{F}_2$ (2 elements) is realized as the residue field of the perfectoid f
ield $\widehat{\mathbb{F}_2((t))^{perf}}$.

## Answer

The smallest possible number of elements in the residue field of a perfectoid fi
eld is $\boxed{2}$.
