## Problem

Given two polynomials $f, g \in k[x,y,z]$, where $k$ is an algebraically closed
field, and they have no common zero, determine whether there exist $m, n \in k[x
,y,z]$ such that $mf + ng = 1$.

## Answer

**Yes.** Such polynomials $m, n$ always exist.

## Proof

Let $I = (f, g) \subseteq k[x,y,z]$ be the ideal generated by $f$ and $g$. The h
ypothesis that $f$ and $g$ have no common zero means precisely that the affine v
ariety
$$V(I) = \{P \in \mathbb{A}^3_k : h(P) = 0 \text{ for all } h \in I\} = \{P \in
\mathbb{A}^3_k : f(P) = 0 \text{ and } g(P) = 0\}$$
is empty: $V(I) = \varnothing$.

We invoke the **Weak Hilbert Nullstellensatz**:

> **Theorem (Weak Nullstellensatz).** Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field a
nd let $J \subseteq k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ be an ideal. Then $V(J) = \varnothing$ i
f and only if $J = k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$, i.e., $1 \in J$.

*Proof of the theorem (for completeness).* The direction "$J = (1) \Rightarrow V
(J) = \varnothing$" is immediate: $1(P) = 1 \neq 0$ for every point $P$, so no p
oint can vanish on all of $J$.

For the converse, suppose $V(J) = \varnothing$ but $J \neq (1)$. Then $J$ is con
tained in some maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m} \subseteq k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$. By Zar
iski's Lemma (a consequence of the Noether normalization / field-theoretic argum
ent: $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]/\mathfrak{m}$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra that is
 a field, hence is a finite, and therefore algebraic, extension of $k$; since $k
$ is algebraically closed, $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]/\mathfrak{m} \cong k$), every maxim
al ideal of $k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ is of the form
$$\mathfrak{m} = (x_1 - a_1, \dots, x_n - a_n)$$
for some point $(a_1, \dots, a_n) \in k^n$. This point $(a_1, \dots, a_n)$ satis
fies $h(a_1, \dots, a_n) = 0$ for every $h \in \mathfrak{m} \supseteq J$, hence
$(a_1, \dots, a_n) \in V(J)$. This contradicts $V(J) = \varnothing$. Therefore $
J = (1)$. $\square$

Applying the Weak Nullstellensatz to our ideal $I = (f, g)$: since $k$ is algebr
aically closed and $V(I) = \varnothing$, we conclude
$$I = (f, g) = k[x, y, z],$$
i.e., $1 \in (f, g)$. By definition of the ideal generated by $f$ and $g$, the s
tatement $1 \in (f, g)$ means there exist polynomials $m, n \in k[x, y, z]$ such
 that
$$m \cdot f + n \cdot g = 1.$$

This is exactly the desired conclusion. $\blacksquare$
