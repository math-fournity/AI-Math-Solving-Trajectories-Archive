## Proof

Let $f \in F[x]$ be the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $F$, say $f(x) = a_n
 x^n + \cdots + a_0$ with $\deg f = n$. Since $\sigma: F \to L$ is a monomorphis
m, applying $\sigma$ to the coefficients of $f$ yields a polynomial
$$\sigma(f)(x) = \sigma(a_n) x^n + \cdots + \sigma(a_0) \in L[x].$$
Because $\sigma$ is injective, $\sigma(a_n) \neq 0$, so $\deg \sigma(f) = n = \d
eg f$.

**Claim.** There is a bijection between the set of extensions of $\sigma$ to a m
onomorphism $\tau: F(\alpha) \to L$ and the set of distinct roots of $\sigma(f)$
 in $L$.

*Proof of Claim.*

($\Rightarrow$) Suppose $\tau: F(\alpha) \to L$ extends $\sigma$, i.e., $\tau|_F
 = \sigma$. Since $f(\alpha) = 0$, applying $\tau$ gives
$$0 = \tau(f(\alpha)) = \sigma(a_n)\tau(\alpha)^n + \cdots + \sigma(a_0) = \sigm
a(f)(\tau(\alpha)).$$
Hence $\tau(\alpha)$ is a root of $\sigma(f)$ in $L$.

($\Leftarrow$) Conversely, let $\beta \in L$ be any root of $\sigma(f)$. Every e
lement of $F(\alpha)$ can be written uniquely as $g(\alpha)$ for some $g \in F[x
]$ with $\deg g < n$ (since $f$ is the minimal polynomial of degree $n$). Define
$$\tau_\beta: F(\alpha) \to L, \qquad \tau_\beta(g(\alpha)) = \sigma(g)(\beta),$
$
where $\sigma(g)$ is obtained by applying $\sigma$ to the coefficients of $g$.

We must verify this is well-defined. If $g(\alpha) = h(\alpha)$ with $\deg g, \d
eg h < n$, then $g - h$ is divisible by $f$ in $F[x]$, say $g - h = qf$. Then $\
sigma(g) - \sigma(h) = \sigma(q)\sigma(f)$, so $\sigma(g)(\beta) - \sigma(h)(\be
ta) = \sigma(q)(\beta)\sigma(f)(\beta) = 0$ since $\beta$ is a root of $\sigma(f
)$. Thus $\tau_\beta$ is well-defined.

It is straightforward to check that $\tau_\beta$ is a field homomorphism: it pre
serves addition and multiplication (since evaluation at $\beta$ does, and $\sigm
a$ preserves coefficient arithmetic), and $\tau_\beta(1) = 1$. Moreover $\tau_\b
eta|_F = \sigma$, so it extends $\sigma$. Being a nonzero field homomorphism, it
 is a monomorphism.

Finally, these two constructions are inverse to each other: the extension $\tau$
 maps to the root $\tau(\alpha)$, and the root $\beta$ yields the extension $\ta
u_\beta$ with $\tau_\beta(\alpha) = \beta$. This establishes the bijection. $\sq
uare$

Since $L$ is algebraically closed, $\sigma(f)$ splits completely in $L$, so ever
y root of $\sigma(f)$ lies in $L$. The number of distinct roots of $\sigma(f)$ e
quals the number of distinct roots of $f$ (as $\sigma$ is injective on coefficie
nts, $\sigma(f)$ and $f$ have the same formal structure; in particular, if $f$ i
s separable then $\sigma(f)$ is separable, and if $f$ has repeated roots in char
acteristic $p$, so does $\sigma(f)$).

**Conclusion.** The number of ways $\sigma$ can be extended to a monomorphism fr
om $F(\alpha)$ to $L$ equals the number of distinct roots of the minimal polynom
ial $f$ of $\alpha$ over $F$ in $L$. Equivalently, it equals the number of disti
nct roots of $f$ (since $L$ is algebraically closed, all roots of $f$ lie in $L$
). In particular, if $f$ is separable (e.g., if $F$ has characteristic $0$ or $F
$ is a perfect field), this number is $\deg f$.
