## Proof

Let $s(m)$ denote the digit sum of $m$. We seek the smallest $n$ such that any $
n$ consecutive positive integers contain at least one $m$ with $7 \mid s(m)$.

### Step 1: Every decade contains a good number

For any non-negative integer $a$, the numbers $10a, 10a+1, \ldots, 10a+9$ have d
igit sums
$$s(10a+d) = s(a) + d, \quad d = 0, 1, \ldots, 9.$$
These are 10 consecutive integers, so at least one is divisible by 7. Thus **eve
ry decade $\{10a, \ldots, 10a+9\}$ contains at least one number with digit sum d
ivisible by 7**.

### Step 2: Upper bound — at most 12 consecutive bad numbers

Since every decade contains a good number, the longest run of consecutive "bad"
numbers can span at most from the last good number in one decade to the first go
od number in the next.

Let $r = s(a) \bmod 7$. In decade $a$, good positions $d \in \{0,\ldots,9\}$ sat
isfy $r + d \equiv 0 \pmod{7}$, i.e., $d \equiv -r \pmod{7}$. The good positions
 are $d_1 = (7-r) \bmod 7$ and, if $d_1 + 7 \le 9$, also $d_1 + 7$.

**Last good position in decade $a$:**
- If $r \in \{0, 5, 6\}$: two good numbers, last at $d = 7, 9, 8$ respectively.
- If $r \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$: one good number, last at $d = 6, 5, 4, 3$ respectiv
ely.

The **minimum** last good position is $3$, achieved when $r = 4$.

**First good position in decade $a+1$:** $d'_1 = (7 - r') \bmod 7$ where $r' = s
(a+1) \bmod 7$. The **maximum** first good position is $6$, achieved when $r' =
1$.

The number of consecutive bad numbers across the boundary is:
$$9 + (\text{first good in decade } a+1) - (\text{last good in decade } a) \le 9
 + 6 - 3 = 12.$$

### Step 3: The bound is tight — 12 consecutive bad numbers exist

We need $s(a) \equiv 4 \pmod{7}$ and $s(a+1) \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$. Take $a = 99$:
- $s(99) = 18 \equiv 4 \pmod{7}$ ✓
- $s(100) = 1 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$ ✓

In decade 99 (numbers 990–999): $r = 4$, so the only good number is at $d=3$, na
mely $993$ ($s = 21 = 3 \times 7$).

In decade 100 (numbers 1000–1009): $r = 1$, so the only good number is at $d=6$,
 namely $1006$ ($s = 7$).

The 12 consecutive integers $994, 995, \ldots, 1005$ all have digit sums **not**
 divisible by 7:

| Number | Digit sum | mod 7 |
|--------|----------|-------|
| 994 | 22 | 1 |
| 995 | 23 | 2 |
| 996 | 24 | 3 |
| 997 | 25 | 4 |
| 998 | 26 | 5 |
| 999 | 27 | 6 |
| 1000 | 1 | 1 |
| 1001 | 2 | 2 |
| 1002 | 3 | 3 |
| 1003 | 4 | 4 |
| 1004 | 5 | 5 |
| 1005 | 6 | 6 |

None is divisible by 7, so $n > 12$.

### Step 4: Conclusion

The maximum run of consecutive integers with no digit sum divisible by 7 is exac
tly 12 (Step 2 gives the upper bound, Step 3 achieves it). Therefore any 13 cons
ecutive positive integers must contain at least one with digit sum divisible by
7, while 12 need not.

$$\boxed{n = 13}$$
