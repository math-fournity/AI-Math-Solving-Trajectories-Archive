## Proof

### Setup

Consider the first-order model:

$$y_u = \beta_0 + \sum_{i=1}^{k} \beta_i x_{iu} + \varepsilon_u, \quad u = 1, \l
dots, N$$

where $\varepsilon_u \sim \text{iid}(0, \sigma^2)$. Assume the design is **cente
red**, i.e., $\bar{x}_i = \frac{1}{N}\sum_u x_{iu} = 0$ for all $i$ (standard fo
r first-order designs). Under centering, the intercept $\beta_0$ is orthogonal t
o the slope coefficients, and the information (moment) matrix for the slopes $\b
oldsymbol{\beta} = (\beta_1, \ldots, \beta_k)^T$ is:

$$D = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{u=1}^{N} \mathbf{x}_u \mathbf{x}_u^T$$

where $\mathbf{x}_u = (x_{1u}, \ldots, x_{ku})^T$. $D$ is a $k \times k$ positiv
e definite matrix with entries:

$$d_{ij} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{u=1}^{N} x_{iu}\, x_{ju}$$

The diagonal elements are $d_{ii} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_u x_{iu}^2 = c_i^2 > 0$.

The variance of the least-squares estimator $\hat{\beta}_i$ is:

$$\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\beta}_i) = \frac{\sigma^2}{N}\,[D^{-1}]_{ii}$$

### The Key Inequality

**Claim**: For each $i = 1, \ldots, k$,

$$[D^{-1}]_{ii} \;\geq\; \frac{1}{d_{ii}} = \frac{1}{c_i^2}$$

with equality if and only if $d_{ij} = 0$ for all $j \neq i$.

**Proof of the claim.** Partition $D$ by separating the $i$-th row/column from t
he rest:

$$D = \begin{pmatrix} d_{ii} & \mathbf{d}_i^T \\ \mathbf{d}_i & D_{-i} \end{pmat
rix}$$

where $\mathbf{d}_i = (d_{ij})_{j \neq i} \in \mathbb{R}^{k-1}$ is the vector of
 off-diagonal elements in the $i$-th row, and $D_{-i}$ is the $(k{-}1)\times(k{-
}1)$ principal submatrix obtained by deleting the $i$-th row and column.

Since $D$ is positive definite, $D_{-i}$ is also positive definite (principal su
bmatrices of positive definite matrices are positive definite), so $D_{-i}^{-1}$
 exists and is positive definite.

By the **Schur complement / block inversion formula**, the $(i,i)$-entry of $D^{
-1}$ is:

$$[D^{-1}]_{ii} = \frac{1}{d_{ii} - \mathbf{d}_i^T\, D_{-i}^{-1}\, \mathbf{d}_i}
$$

The **Schur complement** $s_i = d_{ii} - \mathbf{d}_i^T D_{-i}^{-1} \mathbf{d}_i
$ satisfies $s_i \leq d_{ii}$ because:

$$\mathbf{d}_i^T\, D_{-i}^{-1}\, \mathbf{d}_i \;\geq\; 0$$

This quadratic form is non-negative because $D_{-i}^{-1}$ is positive definite.
Moreover, **equality holds if and only if $\mathbf{d}_i = \mathbf{0}$**, since a
 positive definite matrix $D_{-i}^{-1}$ gives $\mathbf{v}^T D_{-i}^{-1} \mathbf{
v} = 0 \iff \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$.

Therefore:

$$[D^{-1}]_{ii} = \frac{1}{d_{ii} - \mathbf{d}_i^T D_{-i}^{-1} \mathbf{d}_i} \;\
geq\; \frac{1}{d_{ii}} = \frac{1}{c_i^2}$$

with **equality if and only if** $\mathbf{d}_i = \mathbf{0}$, i.e., $d_{ij} = 0$
 for all $j \neq i$. $\square$

### Variance Minimization and Equality Conditions

Translating back to the variance:

$$\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\beta}_i) = \frac{\sigma^2}{N}\,[D^{-1}]_{ii} \;\geq\;
 \frac{\sigma^2}{N\, c_i^2}$$

**Equality holds** — i.e., $\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\beta}_i)$ achieves its mini
mum $\sigma^2/(Nc_i^2)$ — **if and only if** $d_{ij} = 0$ for all $j \neq i$.

In design terms, $d_{ij} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_u x_{iu}\,x_{ju} = 0$ means factor $i
$ is **orthogonal** to factor $j$ across the design points. So equality for a si
ngle $\hat{\beta}_i$ requires factor $i$ to be orthogonal to **all** other facto
rs.

### Full Orthogonality: Equality for All $i$

When equality holds for **every** $i = 1, 2, \ldots, k$, we need $d_{ij} = 0$ fo
r all $i \neq j$. This means $D$ is a **diagonal matrix**:

$$D = \operatorname{diag}(c_1^2,\; c_2^2,\; \ldots,\; c_k^2)$$

and its inverse is simply:

$$D^{-1} = \operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{1}{c_1^2},\; \frac{1}{c_2^2},\; \ldo
ts,\; \frac{1}{c_k^2}\right)$$

giving:

$$\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\beta}_i) = \frac{\sigma^2}{N\, c_i^2}, \quad i = 1, \
ldots, k$$

This is the **minimum variance** achievable for each $\hat{\beta}_i$ given the r
espective diagonal element $d_{ii} = c_i^2$.

### Summary of the Relationship

The connection between variance minimization and orthogonality is:

1. **The lower bound** $\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\beta}_i) \geq \sigma^2/(Nc_i^2)
$ arises from the Schur complement: the off-diagonal entries $d_{ij}$ ($j \neq i
$) contribute a non-negative term $\mathbf{d}_i^T D_{-i}^{-1} \mathbf{d}_i$ that
 **inflates** $[D^{-1}]_{ii}$ beyond $1/d_{ii}$. This term represents the "conta
mination" of $\hat{\beta}_i$'s variance by correlations with other factors.

2. **Equality for a single $i$** holds iff the $i$-th row/column of $D$ has all
off-diagonal elements zero — i.e., factor $i$ is orthogonal to all other factors
.

3. **Equality for all $i = 1, \ldots, k$** holds iff $D$ is fully diagonal — i.e
., the design is **completely orthogonal**. The columns of the design matrix (th
e factor columns) are mutually orthogonal, $D = \operatorname{diag}(c_1^2, \ldot
s, c_k^2)$, and each $\hat{\beta}_i$ achieves its minimum variance $\sigma^2/(Nc
_i^2)$ independently.

4. **Physical interpretation**: When the design is orthogonal, the estimation of
 each $\beta_i$ is "decoupled" from all other coefficients — there is no varianc
e inflation due to collinearity. The diagonal element $d_{ii} = c_i^2$ measures
the "information" (spread) of factor $i$ in the design, and the orthogonality en
sures this information is used purely for estimating $\beta_i$ without any loss
to disentangling correlations with other factors.
