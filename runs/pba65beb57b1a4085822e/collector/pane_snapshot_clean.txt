Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every measurable set $E \su
bset \mathbb{R}$ of positive Lebesgue measure must contain a point $x$ that is t
he midpoint of two distinct points of $E$. This is equivalent to asking whether
$E$ must contain a nontrivial 3-term arithmetic progression.

**Answer: Yes, such an $x$ necessarily exists.**

**Proof.**

The condition $x = \tfrac{1}{2}(y+z)$ with $y,z \in E$, $y \neq z$, and $x \in E
$ is equivalent to finding $x, x+t, x+2t \in E$ with $t \neq 0$ (a 3-term arithm
etic progression in $E$), where $x+t$ is the desired midpoint.

**Step 1: An integral identity.**

For $t \in \mathbb{R}$, define
$$f(t) = m\bigl(E \cap (E - t) \cap (E - 2t)\bigr),$$
where $E - t = \{e - t : e \in E\}$. Note $f(t) > 0$ means there exists $x \in E
$ with $x+t \in E$ and $x+2t \in E$, giving a 3-term AP with common difference $
t$.

Since $f \geq 0$, by Tonelli's theorem:
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} f(t)\,dt = \int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbf{1}_E(x
)\,\mathbf{1}_E(x+t)\,\mathbf{1}_E(x+2t)\,dx\,dt.$$

Apply the change of variables $y = x + t$ (so $t = y - x$ and $x + 2t = 2y - x$)
, which has unit Jacobian:
$$= \int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbf{1}_E(x)\,\mathbf{1}_E(y)\,\mathbf
{1}_E(2y - x)\,dx\,dy = \int_E m\bigl(E \cap (2y - E)\bigr)\,dy.$$

**Step 2: The integrand is positive at a density point.**

Since $m(E) > 0$, by the Lebesgue Density Theorem, $E$ has a density point $y_0
\in E$:
$$\lim_{\varepsilon \to 0} \frac{m\bigl(E \cap [y_0 - \varepsilon,\, y_0 + \vare
psilon]\bigr)}{2\varepsilon} = 1.$$

The reflected set $2y_0 - E = \{2y_0 - e : e \in E\}$ also has density $1$ at $y
_0$, since
$$\frac{m\bigl((2y_0 - E) \cap [y_0 - \varepsilon,\, y_0 + \varepsilon]\bigr)}{2
\varepsilon} = \frac{m\bigl(E \cap [y_0 - \varepsilon,\, y_0 + \varepsilon]\bigr
)}{2\varepsilon} \to 1.$$

If $A$ and $B$ both have density $1$ at $y_0$, then $A \cap B$ has density $1$ a
t $y_0$, because
$$m(A \cap B \cap [y_0-\varepsilon, y_0+\varepsilon]) \geq m(A \cap [\cdots]) +
m(B \cap [\cdots]) - 2\varepsilon,$$
so dividing by $2\varepsilon$ gives a limit $\geq 1 + 1 - 1 = 1$.

Therefore $E \cap (2y_0 - E)$ has density $1$ at $y_0$, and in particular
$$m\bigl(E \cap (2y_0 - E)\bigr) > 0.$$

**Step 3: Positivity of the integral.**

The map $y \mapsto m(E \cap (2y - E))$ is continuous in measure (translation con
tinuity of Lebesgue measure gives $m((2y-E)\,\triangle\,(2y_0-E)) \to 0$ as $y \
to y_0$), so $m(E \cap (2y - E)) > 0$ for all $y$ in some neighborhood $U$ of $y
_0$. Since $y_0$ is a density point of $E$, $m(E \cap U) > 0$. Thus
$$\int_E m\bigl(E \cap (2y - E)\bigr)\,dy \geq \int_{E \cap U} m\bigl(E \cap (2y
 - E)\bigr)\,dy > 0.$$

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Since $\int_{\mathbb{R}} f(t)\,dt > 0$ and $f \geq 0$, the set $\{t : f(t) > 0\}
$ has positive measure. Since $\{0\}$ has measure zero, there exists $t \neq 0$
with $f(t) > 0$, i.e.,
$$m\bigl(E \cap (E - t) \cap (E - 2t)\bigr) > 0.$$

Choose any $z$ in this set. Then $z \in E$, $z + t \in E$, $z + 2t \in E$, and $
t \neq 0$ ensures these are three distinct points. Setting $x = z + t$, $y = z +
 2t$, we have $x \in E$ and
$$x = z + t = \tfrac{1}{2}(z + z + 2t) = \tfrac{1}{2}(z + y),$$
with $y, z \in E$ and $y \neq z$ (since $t \neq 0$). $\blacksquare$
