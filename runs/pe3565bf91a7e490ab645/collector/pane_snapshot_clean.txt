## Derivation of Green's Identities from the Divergence Theorem

**Divergence theorem (Gauss's theorem).** For a smooth vector field $\mathbf{F}$
 defined on a region $V$ bounded by a closed surface $\sigma$ with outward unit
normal $\mathbf{n}$,

$$
\int_V \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}\, d\tau = \oint_\sigma \mathbf{F}\cdot \mathbf{n}
\, d\sigma.
$$

---

### Green's First Identity

Let $\phi$ and $\psi$ be twice continuously differentiable scalar fields on $V$.
 Choose the vector field

$$
\mathbf{F} = \phi\,\nabla\psi.
$$

Compute its divergence using the product rule $\nabla\cdot(u\mathbf{G}) = \nabla
 u \cdot \mathbf{G} + u\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf{G}$:

$$
\nabla\cdot(\phi\,\nabla\psi) = \nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi + \phi\,\nabla\cdot(\n
abla\psi) = \nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi + \phi\,\nabla^2\psi.
$$

Substituting into the divergence theorem:

$$
\int_V \bigl(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi + \phi\,\nabla^2\psi\bigr)\, d\tau = \oin
t_\sigma (\phi\,\nabla\psi)\cdot\mathbf{n}\, d\sigma.
$$

This is **Green's first identity**:

$$
\boxed{\int_V \bigl(\phi\,\nabla^2\psi + \nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi\bigr)\, d\tau
 = \oint_\sigma (\phi\,\nabla\psi)\cdot\mathbf{n}\, d\sigma.}
$$

---

### Green's Second Identity

Green's first identity holds for any pair of smooth scalar fields, so we may int
erchange the roles of $\phi$ and $\psi$:

$$
\int_V \bigl(\psi\,\nabla^2\phi + \nabla\psi\cdot\nabla\phi\bigr)\, d\tau = \oin
t_\sigma (\psi\,\nabla\phi)\cdot\mathbf{n}\, d\sigma.
$$

Now subtract this swapped version from the original first identity. The common g
radient term $\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi = \nabla\psi\cdot\nabla\phi$ cancels in
the volume integral, leaving

$$
\int_V \bigl(\phi\,\nabla^2\psi - \psi\,\nabla^2\phi\bigr)\, d\tau = \oint_\sigm
a \bigl(\phi\,\nabla\psi - \psi\,\nabla\phi\bigr)\cdot\mathbf{n}\, d\sigma.
$$

This is **Green's second identity**:

$$
\boxed{\int_V \bigl(\phi\,\nabla^2\psi - \psi\,\nabla^2\phi\bigr)\, d\tau = \oin
t_\sigma \bigl(\phi\,\nabla\psi - \psi\,\nabla\phi\bigr)\cdot\mathbf{n}\, d\sigm
a.}
$$

---

### Summary

- **First identity**: Apply the divergence theorem to $\mathbf{F}=\phi\nabla\psi
$ and expand $\nabla\cdot(\phi\nabla\psi)$ via the product rule.
- **Second identity**: Write the first identity with $\phi\leftrightarrow\psi$ s
wapped and subtract; the symmetric $\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi$ terms cancel, yie
lding the antisymmetric form.
