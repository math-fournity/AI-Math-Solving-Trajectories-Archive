## Proof

---

We use the **layer-cake (Cavalieri) representation** of the integral of a non-ne
gative measurable function. For any non-negative measurable $f$ on $(X,\mathcal
F,\mu)$,

$$
\int_X f\,d\mu \;=\; \int_0^\infty \mu\big(\{x\in X : f(x)>t\}\big)\,dt.
\]

*Justification.* Writing $f(x)=\int_0^{f(x)}dt$ (valid since $f\ge 0$) and apply
ing Tonelli's theorem to the non-negative integrand $\mathbf 1_{\{f>t\}}(x)$ on
the product space $X\times[0,\infty)$ allows us to swap the order of integration
:

$$
\int_X f(x)\,d\mu(x)
= \int_X\!\int_0^\infty \mathbf 1_{\{f(x)>t\}}\,dt\,d\mu(x)
= \int_0^\infty\!\int_X \mathbf 1_{\{f(x)>t\}}\,d\mu(x)\,dt
= \int_0^\infty \mu(\{x:f(x)>t\})\,dt.
$$

(No finiteness hypothesis is needed; both sides may be $+\infty$, and Tonelli ap
plies to non-negative functions regardless.)

---

Applying this to the given tail measure $\mu(\{x:f(x)>t\})=\dfrac{1}{t^2+1}$ for
 all $t\ge 0$:

$$
\int_X f\,d\mu \;=\; \int_0^\infty \frac{1}{t^2+1}\,dt.
$$

The integrand is continuous on $[0,\infty)$ and decays like $t^{-2}$, so the imp
roper integral converges. Evaluating,

$$
\int_0^\infty \frac{1}{t^2+1}\,dt
= \lim_{R\to\infty}\Big[\arctan t\Big]_{0}^{R}
= \lim_{R\to\infty}\arctan R - \arctan 0
= \frac{\pi}{2}-0
= \frac{\pi}{2}.
$$

---

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_X f\,d\mu \;=\; \frac{\pi}{2}.}$$
