## Proof

We consider the contour $C_N$, the square with vertices $\pm(N+\tfrac{1}{2}) \pm
 i(N+\tfrac{1}{2})$, traversed counterclockwise. We wish to show:

$$\oint_{C_N} \pi \csc(\pi z)\,\frac{P(z)}{Q(z)}\,dz \;\longrightarrow\; 0 \quad
\text{as } N\to\infty.$$

### Setup and key identity

Write $z = x + iy$. The fundamental identity is:

$$|\sin(\pi z)|^2 = \sin^2(\pi x) + \sinh^2(\pi y),$$

so

$$|\csc(\pi z)| = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\sin^2(\pi x) + \sinh^2(\pi y)}}.$$

Since $\deg Q = \deg P + 1$, there exists a constant $C > 0$ such that for all $
|z|$ sufficiently large:

$$\left|\frac{P(z)}{Q(z)}\right| \le \frac{C}{|z|}.$$

On $C_N$, every point satisfies $|z| \ge N + \tfrac{1}{2}$ (the closest point on
 the square to the origin is at distance $N+\tfrac{1}{2}$). Hence on $C_N$:

$$\left|\frac{P(z)}{Q(z)}\right| \le \frac{C}{N+\tfrac{1}{2}}.$$

### Bounding the integral

$$\left|\oint_{C_N} \pi\csc(\pi z)\,\frac{P(z)}{Q(z)}\,dz\right| \le \frac{\pi C
}{N+\tfrac{1}{2}} \oint_{C_N} |\csc(\pi z)|\,|dz|.$$

It suffices to show that $\oint_{C_N}|\csc(\pi z)|\,|dz|$ remains **bounded** as
 $N\to\infty$. We estimate the four sides separately.

**Horizontal sides** ($y = \pm(N+\tfrac{1}{2})$, $x \in [-(N+\tfrac{1}{2}),\, N+
\tfrac{1}{2}]$):

Here $\sinh^2(\pi y) = \sinh^2(\pi(N+\tfrac{1}{2}))$, so

$$|\csc(\pi z)| \le \frac{1}{\sinh(\pi(N+\tfrac{1}{2}))} = \operatorname{csch}\!
\bigl(\pi(N+\tfrac{1}{2})\bigr).$$

The length of each horizontal side is $2N+1$, so the contribution of both horizo
ntal sides is at most

$$2(2N+1)\,\operatorname{csch}\!\bigl(\pi(N+\tfrac{1}{2})\bigr) \;\longrightarro
w\; 0 \quad\text{(exponentially fast)}.$$

**Vertical sides** ($x = \pm(N+\tfrac{1}{2})$, $y \in [-(N+\tfrac{1}{2}),\, N+\t
frac{1}{2}]$):

Since $N+\tfrac{1}{2}$ is a half-integer,

$$\sin\!\bigl(\pi(N+\tfrac{1}{2})\bigr) = \sin(\pi N + \tfrac{\pi}{2}) = (-1)^N,
$$

so $\sin^2(\pi x) = 1$. Therefore

$$|\csc(\pi z)| = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \sinh^2(\pi y)}} = \frac{1}{\cosh(\pi y)} =
 \operatorname{sech}(\pi y).$$

The contribution of one vertical side is

$$\int_{-(N+1/2)}^{N+1/2} \operatorname{sech}(\pi y)\,dy \;\le\; \int_{-\infty}^
{\infty} \operatorname{sech}(\pi y)\,dy \;=\; 1,$$

using the standard integral $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{sech}(u)\,du =
 \pi$ with the substitution $u = \pi y$. Both vertical sides together contribute
 at most $2$.

### Combining the bounds

$$\oint_{C_N} |\csc(\pi z)|\,|dz| \;\le\; 2 \;+\; 2(2N+1)\,\operatorname{csch}\!
\bigl(\pi(N+\tfrac{1}{2})\bigr),$$

which is **bounded** uniformly in $N$ (in fact it approaches $2$). Therefore:

$$\left|\oint_{C_N} \pi\csc(\pi z)\,\frac{P(z)}{Q(z)}\,dz\right| \;\le\; \frac{\
pi C}{N+\tfrac{1}{2}}\left(2 + 2(2N+1)\operatorname{csch}\!\bigl(\pi(N+\tfrac{1}
{2})\bigr)\right) \;\longrightarrow\; 0$$

as $N \to \infty$, since the factor in parentheses is bounded and $\frac{1}{N+1/
2} \to 0$.

### Conclusion

The integral of $\pi\csc(\pi z)\,\frac{P(z)}{Q(z)}$ around the square contour $C
_N$ **vanishes** as $N \to \infty$ through the positive integers.

The essential reasons are:
1. **On horizontal sides**, $|\csc(\pi z)|$ decays exponentially (as $\operatorn
ame{csch}(\pi N)$), overwhelming the linear contour length.
2. **On vertical sides**, $|\csc(\pi z)| = \operatorname{sech}(\pi y)$ is integr
able over $\mathbb{R}$ (exponential decay in $|y|$), so the integral over the gr
owing vertical segment remains bounded.
3. **The rational factor** $|P(z)/Q(z)| = O(1/|z|) = O(1/N)$ on $C_N$ (using $\d
eg Q = \deg P + 1$) supplies the final factor of $1/N$ that drives the whole exp
