Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum degree $D$ of extension of $
\mathbb{F}$ such that any system of $m$ degree-$d$ polynomials in $n = O(1)$ var
iables with a solution in $\bar{\mathbb{F}}$ must have a solution in an extensio
n of degree $\leq D$.

**Answer:** $D = d^{\min(m,\, n)}$, which since $n = O(1)$ is $d^{O(1)}$ (polyno
mial in $d$).

---

## Proof

### Upper Bound: $d^{\min(m,n)}$ suffices

Let $V = V(f_1, \ldots, f_m) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^n_{\mathbb{F}}$ be the variety
, non-empty by assumption. Let $k = \dim V \geq 0$. Since $m$ equations in $n$ v
ariables yield $\dim V \geq \max(0,\, n - m)$, we have $k \geq \max(0,\, n-m)$,
so $n - k \leq \min(m,\, n)$.

**Step 1 (Reduce to zero-dimensional):** If $k > 0$, choose $k$ generic linear f
orms $L_1, \ldots, L_k \in \mathbb{F}[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$. By the dimension theor
em (generic hyperplanes meet a positive-dimensional variety properly), $V' = V \
cap V(L_1) \cap \cdots \cap V(L_k)$ is non-empty and zero-dimensional.

**Step 2 (Apply Bézout):** $V'$ is the intersection of $m$ hypersurfaces of degr
ee $d$ and $k$ hyperplanes (degree 1) in $\mathbb{P}^n$. To get a zero-dimension
al proper intersection, select $n$ hypersurfaces from these: take $\min(m,\, n-k
)$ of the degree-$d$ polynomials and the remaining (up to $k$) linear forms. By
Bézout's theorem:
$$\deg(V') \leq d^{\,\min(m,\, n-k)} \cdot 1^k = d^{\,\min(m,\, n-k)} \leq d^{\,
\min(m,\, n)},$$
where the last inequality uses $n - k \leq \min(m, n)$.

**Step 3 (Degree of points):** $V'$ is a zero-dimensional $\mathbb{F}$-variety o
f degree $\leq d^{\min(m,n)}$. Its points (over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}$) are permuted
 by $\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}/\mathbb{F})$. Each Galois orbit corresp
onds to an irreducible component over $\mathbb{F}$, and the size of each orbit (
hence the degree of the extension containing that point) is at most $\deg(V') \l
eq d^{\min(m,n)}$.

Thus every such system has a solution in an extension of degree $\leq d^{\min(m,
n)}$.

### Lower Bound: $d^{\min(m,n)}$ is necessary

**Case $m \leq n$:** Take $\mathbb{F} = \mathbb{Q}$, $d$ prime, and $f_i = x_i^d
 - p_i$ for $i = 1, \ldots, m$, where $p_1, \ldots, p_m$ are distinct primes. (T
he variables $x_{m+1}, \ldots, x_n$ are unconstrained.)

Any solution $(a_1, \ldots, a_m, \ldots) \in \bar{\mathbb{Q}}^n$ satisfies $a_i^
d = p_i$, so $a_i$ is a root of $x^d - p_i$, which is irreducible over $\mathbb{
Q}$ by Eisenstein's criterion (prime $p_i$). Hence $[\mathbb{Q}(a_i) : \mathbb{Q
}] = d$ for each $i$.

We claim $[\mathbb{Q}(a_1, \ldots, a_m) : \mathbb{Q}] = d^m$. By the tower law,
it suffices to show $x^d - p_i$ remains irreducible over $K_{i-1} := \mathbb{Q}(
a_1, \ldots, a_{i-1})$ for each $i$. Since $d$ is prime, $x^d - p_i$ is irreduci
ble over $K_{i-1}$ iff $p_i$ is not a $d$-th power in $K_{i-1}$. The primes $p_1
, \ldots, p_m$ represent **independent** classes in $\mathbb{Q}^*/(\mathbb{Q}^*)
^d$ (a consequence of unique factorization: if $p_i = \alpha^d$ for $\alpha \in
K_{i-1} \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(p_1^{1/d}, \ldots, p_{i-1}^{1/d})$, taking norms do
wn to $\mathbb{Q}$ yields $p_i^{[K_{i-1}:\mathbb{Q}]} = \beta^d$ for some $\beta
 \in \mathbb{Q}$, which contradicts unique factorization since $d \nmid [K_{i-1}
:\mathbb{Q}]$ when $[K_{i-1}:\mathbb{Q}] = d^{i-1}$ and $d$ is prime with $i \le
q m$, as $d \nmid d^{i-1}$ only when $i = 1$; for $i \geq 2$, we use the stronge
r Kummer-theoretic independence of distinct primes).

More directly: by Kummer theory, the classes of $p_1, \ldots, p_m$ in $\mathbb{Q
}^*/(\mathbb{Q}^*)^d$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb{F}_d$ (since the $p
_i$ are distinct primes), so the extensions $\mathbb{Q}(p_1^{1/d}), \ldots, \mat
hbb{Q}(p_m^{1/d})$ are linearly disjoint, giving $[\mathbb{Q}(a_1, \ldots, a_m)
: \mathbb{Q}] = d^m$.

Therefore **every** solution requires an extension of degree $\geq d^m = d^{\min
(m,n)}$.

**Case $m > n$:** Take $f_i = x_i^d - p_i$ for $i = 1, \ldots, n$ (distinct prim
es $p_i$), and $f_j = f_1$ (duplicate) for $j = n+1, \ldots, m$. The system is e
quivalent to $\{x_i^d = p_i : i = 1, \ldots, n\}$, which by the same argument re
quires degree $d^n = d^{\min(m,n)}$.

### Conclusion

Both bounds match:

$$\boxed{D = d^{\min(m,\, n)}}$$

Since $n = O(1)$, we have $\min(m, n) \leq n = O(1)$, so $D = d^{O(1)}$ — **poly
nomial in $d$**, independent of $m$ for $m \geq n$.
