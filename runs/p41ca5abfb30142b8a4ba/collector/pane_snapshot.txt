## Proof

The positive roots of $x = \tan(x)$ are the intersections of $y = x$ with $y = \
tan(x)$. Since $\tan$ has vertical asymptotes at $x = \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$ ($k
\in \mathbb{Z}$), and on each interval $\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+k\pi,\;\frac{\pi}{2}
+(k+1)\pi\right)$ the function $\tan$ increases continuously from $-\infty$ to $
+\infty$, there is exactly one root in each such interval (for $k \geq 0$, givin
g positive roots).

**Locating $x_n$.** Consider the interval $\left((n-\tfrac12)\pi,\;(n+\tfrac12)\
pi\right)$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ At the left endpoint $\tan \to -\infty < x$;
 at $x = n\pi$, $\tan(n\pi) = 0 < n\pi$; at the right endpoint $\tan \to +\infty
 > x$. Hence the unique root $x_n$ lies in $\left(n\pi,\;(n+\tfrac12)\pi\right)$
.

**Asymptotic expansion.** Write $x_n = n\pi + \delta_n$ with $0 < \delta_n < \fr
ac{\pi}{2}$. The equation becomes
$$\tan(\delta_n) = n\pi + \delta_n.$$
As $n \to \infty$, the right side grows without bound, forcing $\delta_n \to \fr
ac{\pi}{2}^-$. Set $\delta_n = \frac{\pi}{2} - \varepsilon_n$ with $\varepsilon_
n \to 0^+$. Then
$$\tan(\delta_n) = \cot(\varepsilon_n) = \frac{\cos\varepsilon_n}{\sin\varepsilo
n_n}.$$
The equation $\cot(\varepsilon_n) = n\pi + \frac{\pi}{2} - \varepsilon_n$ gives,
 using $\sin\varepsilon_n \sim \varepsilon_n$ and $\cos\varepsilon_n \sim 1$:
$$\frac{1}{\varepsilon_n} \sim n\pi + \frac{\pi}{2}, \qquad \text{i.e., } \varep
silon_n \sim \frac{1}{(n+\tfrac12)\pi}.$$
Therefore
$$x_n = n\pi + \frac{\pi}{2} - \varepsilon_n = \left(n+\tfrac12\right)\pi - \var
epsilon_n, \qquad \varepsilon_n \to 0.$$

**Computing the limit.**
$$x_n - x_{n-1} = \left[\left(n+\tfrac12\right)\pi - \varepsilon_n\right] - \lef
t[\left(n-\tfrac12\right)\pi - \varepsilon_{n-1}\right] = \pi - \varepsilon_n +
\varepsilon_{n-1}.$$
Since $\varepsilon_n \to 0$ and $\varepsilon_{n-1} \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}(x_n - x_{n-1}) = \pi.$$
