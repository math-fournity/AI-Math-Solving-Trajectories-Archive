## Proof

**Answer: Yes, $\ell(G) = \tfrac{1}{2}$.**

**Setup and key properties.** Since $a_s \in \{-1,1\}$, the map $X:[0,T]\to\math
bb{R}$ is Lipschitz with constant $1$:
$$|X_t - X_u| = \left|\int_u^t a_s\,ds\right| \le |t-u|.$$
Moreover $X$ is absolutely continuous with $X'_t = a_t$ a.e., so $|X'_t|=1$ a.e.
 Since $X_0=\tfrac12$ and $|X_t-X_0|\le t\le \tfrac12$, we have $X_t\in[0,1]$ fo
r all $t\in[0,T]$, i.e., $X([0,T])\subset[0,1]$.

**Applying the area formula.** The area formula for Lipschitz maps $f:\mathbb{R}
\to\mathbb{R}$ (Federer; Evans–Gariepy, §3.4) states: for any measurable $A\subs
et\mathbb{R}$ and any non-negative measurable $g$,
$$\int_A g(x)\,|f'(x)|\,dx = \int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{x\in f^{-1}(y)\cap A} g(x)\,
dy.$$

Apply this with $f=X$, $A=[0,T]$, and $g(t)=\mathbf{1}_S(X_t)$.

**Left-hand side.** Since $|X'_t|=1$ a.e.,
$$\text{LHS} = \int_0^T \mathbf{1}_S(X_t)\cdot 1\,dt = \int_0^T \mathbf{1}_S(X_t
)\,dt = \ell(G).$$

**Right-hand side.** Setting $N(y):=\#\{t\in[0,T]:X_t=y\}$ (the multiplicity fun
ction, taking values in $\{0,1,2,\dots,\infty\}$),
$$\text{RHS} = \int_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbf{1}_S(y)\,N(y)\,dy.$$

Thus:
$$\ell(G) = \int_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbf{1}_S(y)\,N(y)\,dy. \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Integrability of $N$.** Taking $g\equiv 1$ in the area formula gives
$$\int_0^T |X'_t|\,dt = \int_{\mathbb{R}} N(y)\,dy.$$
The left side equals $T=\tfrac12<\infty$, so $N\in L^1(\mathbb{R})$; in particul
ar $N$ is measurable and $N(y)<\infty$ for a.e. $y$.

**Conclusion.** Since $X([0,T])\subset[0,1]$, we have $N(y)=0$ for $y\notin[0,1]
$, so $(\ast)$ becomes
$$\ell(G) = \int_{[0,1]} \mathbf{1}_S(y)\,N(y)\,dy.$$
Let $S^c:=[0,1]\setminus S$; by hypothesis $\ell(S^c)=0$. Then
$$\ell(G) = \int_{[0,1]} N(y)\,dy - \int_{S^c} N(y)\,dy = \tfrac12 - \int_{S^c}
N(y)\,dy.$$
Since $N\ge 0$ is measurable and $\ell(S^c)=0$, the integral of $N$ over the nul
l set $S^c$ is $0$:
$$\int_{S^c} N(y)\,dy = 0.$$
Therefore
$$\ell(G) = \tfrac12 = \ell([0,T]). \qquad\blacksquare$$
