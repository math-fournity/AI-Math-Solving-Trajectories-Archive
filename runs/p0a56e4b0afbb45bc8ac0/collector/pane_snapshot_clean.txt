## Proof

$$A = \left(-\tfrac{b}{2}, 0\right),\quad C = \left(\tfrac{b}{2}, 0\right),\quad
 B = (0, h), \quad h = \sqrt{a^2 - \tfrac{b^2}{4}}.$$

**Incircle.** By symmetry the incenter is $I = (0, r)$, where $r = \frac{bh}{2a+
b}$ (area $= \frac{bh}{2}$, semiperimeter $= a + \frac{b}{2}$).

**Line $BC$:** $2hx + by - bh = 0$, with $\sqrt{4h^2+b^2} = 2a$.

**Locating $P$ and $Q$.** Since $PQ \parallel BC$ with $P \in AB$, $Q \in AC$, t

$$Q = \left(1 - \tfrac{b}{2},\, 0\right), \qquad P = \left(\tfrac{1-b}{2},\, \tf
rac{h}{b}\right).$$

The line $PQ$ (parallel to $BC$) is: $2hx + by + h(b-2) = 0$.

**Tangency condition.** The two lines parallel to $BC$ and tangent to $\omega$ a
re $BC$ itself (signed distance $-r$ from $I$) and the line on the opposite side

$$h(b-2) = r(2a - b) = \frac{bh}{2a+b}(2a-b).$$
Dividing by $h$ and cross-multiplying:
$$(b-2)(2a+b) = b(2a-b) \implies 2b^2 - 4a - 2b = 0 \implies \boxed{a = \tfrac{b
(b-1)}{2}}. \quad (1)$$

**Angle bisector condition.** We have $M = \text{midpoint of } BC = \left(\frac{
b}{4}, \frac{h}{2}\right)$. The condition $\angle CQM = \angle PQM$ means $QM$ b

$$\vec{QP} = \left(-\tfrac{1}{2},\, \tfrac{h}{b}\right), \quad |\vec{QP}| = \fra
c{\sqrt{b^2+4h^2}}{2b} = \frac{a}{b}.$$
$$\vec{QM} = \left(\tfrac{3b}{4}-1,\, \tfrac{h}{2}\right).$$

The angle-bisector direction is $\frac{\vec{QC}}{|\vec{QC}|} + \frac{\vec{QP}}{|
\vec{QP}|} = \left(1 - \frac{b}{2a},\; \frac{h}{a}\right)$.

For $QM$ to be parallel to this:
$$\frac{\frac{3b}{4}-1}{1 - \frac{b}{2a}} = \frac{h/2}{h/a} = \frac{a}{2}.$$
Solving: $\frac{3b}{4} - 1 = \frac{a}{2} - \frac{b}{4}$, giving $b - 1 = \frac{a

$$\boxed{a = 2(b-1)}. \quad (2)$$
