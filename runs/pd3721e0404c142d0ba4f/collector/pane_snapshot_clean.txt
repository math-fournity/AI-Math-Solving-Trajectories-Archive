**Answer: Yes.** A holomorphic homeomorphism of the unit disc onto itself extend
s continuously to a map $\overline{U}\to\overline{U}$.

---

### Proof

Let $U=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}$ and let $f:U\to U$ be a homeomorphism that is h
olomorphic.

**Step 1. $f$ is biholomorphic (an automorphism of $U$).**

Since $f$ is a homeomorphism it is bijective. We claim $f'(z)\neq 0$ for every $
z\in U$. Suppose for contradiction that $f'(z_{0})=0$ at some $z_{0}\in U$. Then
 $z_{0}$ is a zero of $f(z)-f(z_{0})$ of order $m\geq 2$. By the local mapping t
heorem, there exist neighborhoods $V$ of $z_{0}$ and $W$ of $f(z_{0})$ such that
 every $w\in W\setminus\{f(z_{0})\}$ has exactly $m$ distinct preimages in $V$.
This contradicts the injectivity of $f$. Hence $f'$ never vanishes.

By the holomorphic inverse function theorem, $f^{-1}:U\to U$ is holomorphic. Thu
s $f$ is a biholomorphic self-map of $U$, i.e. an automorphism of the disc.

**Step 2. Classification of disc automorphisms.**

Pick $a\in U$ with $f(a)=0$ (such $a$ exists since $f$ is onto). Define the Möbi
us disc automorphism
$$\phi_{a}(z)=\frac{z-a}{1-\overline{a}\,z},\qquad |a|<1,$$
which maps $U$ biholomorphically onto $U$ and sends $a\mapsto 0$. Set
$$g:=f\circ\phi_{a}^{-1}:U\to U.$$
Then $g$ is an automorphism of $U$ fixing $0$. By the Schwarz lemma applied to b
oth $g$ and $g^{-1}$ (both fix $0$ and map $U$ into $U$), we get $|g(z)|\le|z|$
and $|g^{-1}(z)|\le|z|$, which forces $|g(z)|=|z|$. Equality in Schwarz's lemma
gives
$$g(z)=e^{i\theta}z\quad\text{for some }\theta\in\mathbb{R}.$$
Therefore
$$f(z)=e^{i\theta}\,\phi_{a}(z)=e^{i\theta}\,\frac{z-a}{1-\overline{a}\,z}.$$

**Step 3. Continuous extension to $\overline{U}$.**

The map $f(z)=e^{i\theta}\frac{z-a}{1-\overline{a}\,z}$ is a Möbius transformati
on defined on $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{1/\overline{a}\}$. Since $|a|<1$, the pole $
1/\overline{a}$ lies outside $\overline{U}$, so $f$ is continuous (indeed holomo
rphic) on an open set containing $\overline{U}$. In particular $f$ restricts to
a continuous map $\overline{U}\to\mathbb{C}$.

It remains to check that $f(\overline{U})\subseteq\overline{U}$. For $|z|<1$ we
already know $f(z)\in U$. For $|z|=1$ we compute:
$$|z-a|^{2}=(z-a)(\overline{z}-\overline{a})=|z|^{2}-z\overline{a}-a\overline{z}
+|a|^{2}=1-z\overline{a}-a\overline{z}+|a|^{2},$$
$$|1-\overline{a}\,z|^{2}=(1-\overline{a}z)(1-a\overline{z})=1-\overline{a}z-a\o
verline{z}+|a|^{2}.$$
These are equal when $|z|=1$, so $|f(z)|=1$ on $\partial U$. Hence $f$ maps $\pa
rtial U$ into $\partial U\subseteq\overline{U}$, and combined with $f(U)\subsete
q U$, we obtain
$$f(\overline{U})\subseteq\overline{U}.$$

Thus $f$ extends to a continuous map $f:\overline{U}\to\overline{U}$. $\blacksqu
are$
