Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether reducing the GCD computatio
n modulo a prime $q$ can only increase (or maintain) the degree of the GCD of $f
$ and $x^p - 1$.

**Answer: The statement is TRUE.**

**Proof.**

Let $g = \gcd_{\mathbb{Q}}(f,\, x^p - 1)$, taken to be the monic GCD in $\mathbb
{Q}[x]$.

**Step 1: $g \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ and $g$ is monic.**

Since $x^p - 1$ is monic in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$, it factors over $\mathbb{Z}$ as a p
roduct of monic irreducible factors (cyclotomic polynomials). The monic GCD $g$
divides $x^p - 1$ in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$, so by unique factorization in $\mathbb{Q}[
x]$, $g$ is a product of a subset of these monic irreducible factors. Each facto
r lies in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$, so $g \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$, and $g$ is monic. $\square$

**Step 2: $g$ divides $f$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$.**

We know $g \mid f$ in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$. Since $g$ is monic and $g \in \mathbb{Z}[
x]$, polynomial long division of $f \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ by $g$ over $\mathbb{Z}$
produces a quotient $h \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ and remainder $r \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ wi
th $\deg r < \deg g$. This same division in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ must yield the same
quotient and remainder (the algorithm is identical since $g$ is monic). Since $g
 \mid f$ in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$, the remainder is $0$. Hence $f = g \cdot h$ with $h
 \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$, so $g \mid f$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$. $\square$

**Step 3: Reduction modulo $q$ preserves the divisibility.**

Since $g$ is monic, its leading coefficient is $1 \not\equiv 0 \pmod{q}$, so $\b
ar{g} \in \mathbb{Z}_q[x]$ satisfies $\deg(\bar{g}) = \deg(g)$.

From $g \mid f$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$: writing $f = g \cdot h$ with $h \in \mathbb{
Z}[x]$, reducing mod $q$ gives $\bar{f} = \bar{g} \cdot \bar{h}$, so $\bar{g} \m
id \bar{f}$ in $\mathbb{Z}_q[x]$.

From $g \mid (x^p - 1)$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$: similarly, $\bar{g} \mid \overline{x
^p - 1}$ in $\mathbb{Z}_q[x]$.

**Step 4: Conclusion.**

Since $\bar{g}$ is a common divisor of $\bar{f}$ and $x^p - 1$ in $\mathbb{Z}_q[
x]$, and $\mathbb{Z}_q$ is a field (so $\mathbb{Z}_q[x]$ is a PID), we have:

$$\bar{g} \;\Big|\; \gcd_{\mathbb{Z}_q}(\bar{f},\; x^p - 1).$$

Therefore:

$$\deg\!\big(\gcd_{\mathbb{Z}_q}(f,\, x^p - 1)\big) \;\geq\; \deg(\bar{g}) \;=\;
 \deg(g) \;=\; \deg\!\big(\gcd_{\mathbb{Q}}(f,\, x^p - 1)\big).$$

**Remark (strict inequality is possible).** The inequality can be strict. For ex
ample, take $f = x - 2$, $p = 3$, $q = 7$. Over $\mathbb{Q}$: $f(2^3 - 1) = f(7)
 \neq 0$ in the sense that $2$ is not a root of $x^3 - 1$ (since $2^3 - 1 = 7 \n
eq 0$), so $\gcd_{\mathbb{Q}}(x-2,\, x^3-1) = 1$, degree $0$. Over $\mathbb{Z}_7
$: $2^3 - 1 = 7 \equiv 0 \pmod{7}$, so $x = 2$ is a root of $x^3 - 1$, giving $\
gcd_{\mathbb{Z}_7}(x-2,\, x^3-1) = x - 2$, degree $1 \geq 0$. $\square$
