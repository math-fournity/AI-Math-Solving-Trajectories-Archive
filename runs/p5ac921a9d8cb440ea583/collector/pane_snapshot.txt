## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. For every infinite set $X$, there exists a group structure on $X
$ in which every element has order at most 2.

---

**Step 1 (Reduction to elementary abelian 2-groups).** A group in which every el
ement has order at most 2 is necessarily abelian: for any $a, b \in G$ we have $
(ab)^2 = e$, i.e. $abab = e$; multiplying on the left by $a$ and on the right by
 $b$ gives $ba = ab$ (using $a^2 = b^2 = e$). Such a group is thus a vector spac
e over $\mathbb{F}_2$ (with scalar multiplication $0 \cdot g = e$, $1 \cdot g =
g$), hence isomorphic to a direct sum $\bigoplus_{\lambda} \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z
} = (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{(\lambda)}$ for some index set $\lambda$.

**Step 2 (Cardinality of the direct sum).** The group $(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^
{(\lambda)}$ consists of all finitely-supported functions $\lambda \to \{0,1\}$.

- If $\lambda = n < \omega$ is finite, then $|(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{(n)}| =
2^n$.
- If $\lambda$ is infinite, the elements are indexed by finite subsets of $\lamb
da$. The collection $[\lambda]^{<\omega}$ of finite subsets of an infinite set $
\lambda$ has cardinality $\lambda$ (each $[\lambda]^k$ for fixed finite $k$ has
size $\lambda$, and $\lambda \cdot \aleph_0 = \lambda$). For each finite subset
of size $k$ there are $2^k$ choices, so the total cardinality is $\sum_{k<\omega
} \lambda \cdot 2^k = \lambda$.

Hence $|(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{(\lambda)}| = \lambda$ for every infinite card
inal $\lambda$.

**Step 3 (Construction on $X$).** Let $X$ be an infinite set with $|X| = \kappa$
. Take $\lambda = \kappa$ and form the group $G = (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{(\ka
ppa)}$. By Step 2, $|G| = \kappa = |X|$. Choose any bijection $\varphi: X \to G$
 and transport the group structure to $X$ by
$$x \star y \;=\; \varphi^{-1}\!\bigl(\varphi(x) + \varphi(y)\bigr).$$
Then $(X, \star)$ is a group isomorphic to $G$, so every element of $X$ has orde
r at most 2.

**Conclusion.** Every infinite set admits a group structure in which every eleme
nt has order at most 2. $\blacksquare$
