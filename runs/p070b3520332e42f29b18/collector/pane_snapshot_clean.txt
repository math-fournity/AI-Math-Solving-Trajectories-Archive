## Proof

We determine the number of conjugacy classes of order‑3 elements in $PGL(3,\math
bb Z)=GL(3,\mathbb Z)/\{\pm I\}$.

**Step 1. Lifting order‑3 elements of $PGL(3,\mathbb Z)$ to $GL(3,\mathbb Z)$.**

An element $[A]\in PGL(3,\mathbb Z)$ has order $3$ iff $[A]^3=[I]$ and $[A]\ne[I
]$, i.e. $A^3=\pm I$ and $A\ne\pm I$.

- If $A^3=I$, then $\operatorname{ord}(A)\in\{1,3\}$; since $A\ne\pm I$, we have
 $\operatorname{ord}(A)=3$.
- If $A^3=-I$, then $\operatorname{ord}(A)\mid 6$ and $\operatorname{ord}(A)\nmi
d 3$. The possibility $\operatorname{ord}(A)=2$ forces $A=-I$ (since $A^3=-I,\ A
^2=I\Rightarrow A=-I$), excluded. Hence $\operatorname{ord}(A)=6$.

The map $A\mapsto -A$ bijects $\{A:A^3=I,\ A\ne I\}$ with $\{A:A^3=-I,\ A\ne -I\
}$, since $(-A)^3=-A^3$.

**Step 2. $GL(3,\mathbb Z)$-conjugacy classes of elements with $A^3=I$, $A\ne I$
.**

Such an $A$ makes $M=\mathbb Z^3$ a $\mathbb Z[C_3]$-lattice ($C_3=\langle t:t^3
=1\rangle$, $t$ acts as $A$). The minimal polynomial divides $x^3-1=(x-1)(x^2+x+
1)$. Over $\mathbb Q$ the only nontrivial rational canonical form of degree $3$
with $A^3=I$ has characteristic polynomial $x^3-1$, i.e. $\mathbb Q\otimes M\con
g\mathbb Q[C_3]\cong\mathbb Q\oplus\mathbb Q(\omega)$ ($\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$).

The classical Diederichsen–Reiner classification of indecomposable $\mathbb Z[C_
3]$-lattices (finite representation type for $C_3$) gives exactly three indecomp
osables:
- $\mathbb Z$ (trivial action, rank $1$),
- $\mathbb Z[\omega]$ (faithful, rank $2$),
- $\mathbb Z[C_3]$ (regular, rank $3$, indecomposable).

A rank‑$3$ $\mathbb Z[C_3]$-lattice with rationalization $\mathbb Q\oplus\mathbb
 Q(\omega)$ decomposes (Krull–Schmidt for lattices over orders) into indecomposa
bles whose ranks sum to $3$ and whose rationalizations sum to $\mathbb Q\oplus\m
athbb Q(\omega)$. The only possibilities are:
1. $\mathbb Z[C_3]$ (rank $3$), and
2. $\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z[\omega]$ (rank $1+2=3$).

These are non‑isomorphic (one indecomposable, one decomposable), giving exactly
**two** $GL(3,\mathbb Z)$-conjugacy classes of elements of order $3$:
- $C_1$: the regular representation, companion matrix of $x^3-1$ (the $3$-cycle
permutation matrix),
- $C_2$: $\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z[\omega]$, block‑diagonal $\operatorname{diag}
(1,\bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr])$.

**Step 3. $GL(3,\mathbb Z)$-classes with $A^3=-I$, $A\ne -I$.**

Via $A\leftrightarrow -A$, these correspond bijectively (and conjugacy‑preservin
g) to the two classes above, giving two classes $D_1=-C_1$, $D_2=-C_2$ (elements
 of order $6$).

**Step 4. Passing to $PGL(3,\mathbb Z)$.**

Conjugacy in $PGL(3,\mathbb Z)$ is $[A]\sim[B]\iff B=\varepsilon\,PAP^{-1}$ for
some $P\in GL(3,\mathbb Z)$, $\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}$. Thus the $PGL$-class of $
[A]$ is the union of the $GL$-class of $A$ and the $GL$-class of $-A$.

The four $GL$-classes $\{C_1,C_2,D_1,D_2\}$ are paired by negation $A\mapsto -A$
 as $C_i\leftrightarrow D_i$. We verify no further merging occurs: if $A\in C_i$
 ($A^3=I$) and $B\in C_j$ ($B^3=I$) with $[A]\sim[B]$, then $B=\varepsilon PAP^{
-1}$ gives $B^3=\varepsilon^3 I=I$, so $\varepsilon=1$ and $C_i=C_j$. If $A\in C
_i$ and $B\in D_j$ ($B^3=-I$), then $\varepsilon=-1$ and $-B=PAP^{-1}\in C_i$, f
orcing $D_j=D_i$. Hence the orbits under negation are exactly
$$\{C_1,D_1\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{C_2,D_2\}.$$

**Conclusion.** There are exactly $\boxed{2}$ conjugacy classes of order‑$3$ mat
rices in $PGL(3,\mathbb Z)$.
