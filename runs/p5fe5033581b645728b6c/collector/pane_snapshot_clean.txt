# Universal Cover of ℝP² ∨ S¹

## Setup and Fundamental Group

Let $X = \mathbb{R}P^2 \vee S^1$ with basepoint $x_0$ at the wedge point. By the
 Seifert–van Kampen theorem:

$$\pi_1(X) = \pi_1(\mathbb{R}P^2) * \pi_1(S^1) = \langle\, a, b \mid a^2 = 1 \,\
rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z},$$

where $a$ is the generator of $\pi_1(\mathbb{R}P^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
$ and $b$ is the generator of $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.

## Building Blocks

The universal covers of the pieces are:
- **$\widetilde{\mathbb{R}P^2} = S^2$**, a 2-sheeted cover with deck group $\mat
hbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ acting by the antipodal map. The basepoint $x_0$ has **2 pre
images** (an antipodal pair) on each $S^2$.
- **$\widetilde{S^1} = \mathbb{R}$**, an infinite-sheeted cover with deck group
$\mathbb{Z}$ acting by integer translations. The basepoint $x_0$ has preimages a
t **every integer** $n \in \mathbb{Z} \subset \mathbb{R}$.

## Bass–Serre Tree

The group $G = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z}$ is a free product $A * B$ wi
th $A = \langle a \mid a^2=1\rangle$ and $B = \langle b \rangle$. Its **Bass–Ser
re tree** $T$ is the bipartite tree with:
- **$A$-vertices** (cosets $G/A$): each has degree $|A| = 2$.
- **$B$-vertices** (cosets $G/B$): each has degree $|B| = \aleph_0$ (countably i
nfinite).
- **Edges** (elements of $G$): edge $g$ connects $gA$ to $gB$.

This is a tree (no cycles, simply connected as a graph) in which every $A$-verte
x has exactly 2 neighbors and every $B$-vertex has countably infinitely many nei
ghbors.

## The Universal Cover as a Tree of Spaces

The universal cover $\widetilde{X}$ is built by placing a copy of the universal
cover of each piece at each vertex of the Bass–Serre tree:

- **At each $A$-vertex**: place a copy of $S^2$ (the universal cover of $\mathbb
{R}P^2$).
- **At each $B$-vertex**: place a copy of $\mathbb{R}$ (the universal cover of $
S^1$).
- **Gluing**: when an $A$-vertex and a $B$-vertex are adjacent in $T$, identify
one preimage-of-basepoint point on the $S^2$ with one integer point on the $\mat
hbb{R}$-line.

Concretely:
- Each $S^2$ has **2 attachment points** (an antipodal pair — the two preimages
of $x_0$ under $S^2 \to \mathbb{R}P^2$). Each attachment point is glued to a **d
ifferent** $\mathbb{R}$-line. This matches the degree-2 property of $A$-vertices
.
- Each $\mathbb{R}$-line has an $S^2$ attached at **every integer point** (each
integer is a preimage of $x_0$ under $\mathbb{R} \to S^1$). Each integer point c
onnects to a **different** $S^2$. This matches the countably-infinite degree of
$B$-vertices.

**Simply connectedness**: Each piece ($S^2$ and $\mathbb{R}$) is simply connecte
d, the gluing graph (Bass–Serre tree) is a tree (simply connected), and the glui
ng points are well-behaved. By van Kampen applied inductively over the tree, $\w
idetilde{X}$ is simply connected. Since $G$ acts freely on $\widetilde{X}$ with
quotient $X$, this is indeed the universal cover.

## Drawing as a Subset of $\mathbb{R}^3$

**Main spine.** Take the $x$-axis $L_0 = \{(t, 0, 0) : t \in \mathbb{R}\}$ as th
e initial copy of $\mathbb{R}$.

**First layer of spheres.** At each integer point $(n, 0, 0)$, place a small sph
ere $S^2_n$ of radius $r$ (say $r = 1/4$) centered at $(n, 0, r)$, so that:
- **South pole** $(n, 0, 0)$ lies on $L_0$ — this is the attachment point to the
 main line.
- **North pole** $(n, 0, 2r)$ is the second attachment point, where a new $\math
bb{R}$-line begins.

**Branching lines.** From each north pole $(n, 0, 2r)$, extend a new copy of $\m
athbb{R}$ in the $y$-direction:
$$L_n^{(1)} = \{(n,\, s,\, 2r) : s \in \mathbb{R}\}.$$
These lines are pairwise disjoint (distinct $x$-coordinates) and disjoint from $
L_0$ (distinct $z$-coordinates).

**Second layer of spheres.** At each integer point $(n, m, 2r)$ on each line $L_
n^{(1)}$, place another small $S^2$ with south pole at $(n, m, 2r)$ and north po
le at $(n, m, 2r + 2r')$. From each such north pole, extend a new $\mathbb{R}$-l
ine in the $z$-direction.

**Recursive continuation.** At each subsequent level, spheres are placed at inte
ger points along each new line, and from the free pole of each sphere, a new $\m
athbb{R}$-line extends in a fresh direction. Since the Bass–Serre tree is counta
ble and $\mathbb{R}^3$ admits uncountably many distinct directions, we assign a
unique direction to each branch (e.g., by indexing branches with rationals and m
apping them to distinct unit vectors). Choosing all radii sufficiently small (de
creasing with depth) prevents any self-intersections.

## Key Features

1. **Simply connected.** $\widetilde{X}$ is the universal cover; it is simply co
nnected by construction (tree of simply connected pieces over a tree).

2. **Tree of spaces (Bass–Serre structure).** Contracting each $S^2$ to a point
and each $\mathbb{R}$-line to an edge yields the Bass–Serre tree of $G = \mathbb
{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z}$: a bipartite tree with degree-2 vertices (from $S^
2$'s) and countably-infinite-degree vertices (from $\mathbb{R}$-lines).

3. **Each $S^2$ bridges exactly two $\mathbb{R}$-lines.** The two attachment poi
nts are antipodal on $S^2$, reflecting the 2-sheeted cover $S^2 \to \mathbb{R}P^
2$.

4. **Each $\mathbb{R}$-line carries $S^2$'s at every integer.** This reflects th
e infinite cyclic cover $\mathbb{R} \to S^1$ with fiber $\mathbb{Z}$.

5. **No cycles.** The underlying graph is a tree, so there are no loops in the g
luing pattern. The only "loops" in the space are within individual $S^2$'s, whic
h are simply connected.

6. **Deck transformation group $G = \langle a, b \mid a^2 = 1\rangle$.**
   - The generator $a$ (order 2) acts on each $S^2$ as the **antipodal map**, sw
apping the two attachment points and thereby swapping the two $\mathbb{R}$-lines
 adjacent to that $S^2$. This is the lift of the nontrivial deck transformation
of $S^2 \to \mathbb{R}P^2$.
   - The generator $b$ (infinite order) acts on each $\mathbb{R}$-line as **tran
slation by 1**, shifting the integer-labeled $S^2$'s along the line. This is the
 lift of the generator of the deck group of $\mathbb{R} \to S^1$.
   - Together, $a$ and $b$ move through the tree, and $G$ acts freely and transi
tively on each fiber of $\widetilde{X} \to X$.

7. **Local structure.** Away from attachment points, $\widetilde{X}$ is locally
a 2-manifold (on $S^2$'s) or a 1-manifold (on $\mathbb{R}$-lines). At each attac
hment point, the local structure is a wedge $S^2 \vee \mathbb{R}$ (a disk meetin
g a line at a point), which maps down to the local structure $\mathbb{R}P^2 \vee
 S^1$ at the basepoint of $X$.

8. **Embeddability in $\mathbb{R}^3$.** The space is 2-dimensional. The $S^2$ pi
eces embed naturally in $\mathbb{R}^3$, the $\mathbb{R}$-lines embed as straight
 segments, and the countable tree of spaces can be embedded without self-interse
ction by assigning distinct directions to branches and using decreasing radii at
 deeper levels.
