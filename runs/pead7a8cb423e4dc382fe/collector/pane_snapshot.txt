## Proof

We analyze the exponential generating function
$$f(z) = \sum_{n\ge 0}\frac{\tilde b(n)}{n!}z^n = \frac{1}{2-e^z}$$
via singularity analysis.

### Singularities

The poles of $f$ occur where $e^z = 2$, i.e.,
$$z_k = \log 2 + 2\pi i k,\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.$$

Their moduli are
$$|z_k| = \sqrt{(\log 2)^2 + 4\pi^2 k^2}.$$

The unique dominant singularity (closest to the origin) is the real positive pol
e
$$\rho := z_0 = \log 2.$$
All other poles have modulus $\ge \sqrt{(\log 2)^2+4\pi^2} \approx 6.32$, wherea
s $\rho = \log 2 \approx 0.693$, so the non-dominant poles are exponentially far
ther away and contribute only $O(|z_{\pm 1}|^{-n})$ corrections.

### Local expansion at the dominant pole

Set $w = z - \rho$. Since $e^\rho = 2$,
$$2 - e^z = 2 - 2e^w = 2(1-e^w) = -2w - w^2 - \tfrac{1}{3}w^3 - \cdots = -2w\big
l(1 + \tfrac{w}{2} + \cdots\bigr).$$
Hence
$$f(z) = \frac{1}{-2w\bigl(1+\frac{w}{2}+\cdots\bigr)} = -\frac{1}{2w}\bigl(1-\t
frac{w}{2}+\cdots\bigr) = \frac{1}{2(\rho-z)} + \text{analytic at }\rho.$$
Equivalently,
$$f(z) \sim \frac{1}{2\rho}\cdot\frac{1}{1-z/\rho}\qquad (z\to\rho).$$

### Transfer

By the standard transfer theorem for simple poles (a special case of Flajolet–Od
lyzko singularity analysis), the coefficient of $z^n$ in $f(z)$ satisfies
$$[z^n]\,f(z) \sim \frac{1}{2\rho}\,\rho^{-n}.$$
Since $[z^n]f(z) = \tilde b(n)/n!$, we obtain
$$\frac{\tilde b(n)}{n!} \sim \frac{1}{2\log 2}\,(\log 2)^{-n} = \frac{1}{2(\log
 2)^{n+1}}.$$

### Result

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \tilde b(n) \sim \frac{n!}{2\,(\log 2)^{\,n+1}}\qquad (n\
to\infty).}$$

The error term is exponentially smaller, governed by the next-nearest poles $z_{
\pm1}=\log 2\pm 2\pi i$, contributing corrections of relative size $\bigl(\rho/|
z_{\pm1}|\bigr)^n = \bigl(\log 2/\sqrt{(\log 2)^2+4\pi^2}\bigr)^n$, which decay
geometrically.
