## Proof

**Claim:** The limit exists and equals $0$.

Let $h(x) = e^{g(x)}$. Then $h'(x) = g'(x)\,e^{g(x)}$, which is exactly the quan
tity whose limit we seek.

---

**Step 1: $g'(x) \to +\infty$ as $x \to -\infty$.**

Fix any $a$. For $x < a$,
$$g'(x) = g'(a) - \int_x^a g''(t)\,dt.$$
Since $g''(t) \le D < 0$ for all $t$,
$$\int_x^a g''(t)\,dt \;\le\; D(a - x) \;\xrightarrow{x\to -\infty}\; -\infty,$$
because $D<0$ and $a-x\to+\infty$. Hence $g'(x) \to +\infty$.

---

**Step 2: $g(x) \to -\infty$ as $x \to -\infty$.**

Since $g'(x)\to+\infty$, there exists $M$ with $g'(x)\ge 1$ for all $x\le M$. Th
en
$$g(x) = g(M) - \int_x^M g'(t)\,dt \;\le\; g(M) - (M-x) \;\xrightarrow{x\to-\inf
ty}\; -\infty.$$
Thus $h(x)=e^{g(x)}\to 0^+$.

---

**Step 3: $h$ is convex for sufficiently negative $x$.**

$$h''(x) = \bigl(g''(x) + (g'(x))^2\bigr)\,e^{g(x)}.$$
Since $g'(x)\to+\infty$, we have $(g'(x))^2\to+\infty$, while $g''(x)\le D$ is b
ounded above. Hence for all sufficiently negative $x$, $(g'(x))^2 + g''(x) > 0$,
 so $h''(x)>0$.

---

**Step 4: $h$ is increasing for sufficiently negative $x$.**

$h'(x) = g'(x)\,e^{g(x)}$. Since $g'(x)\to+\infty$ and $e^{g(x)}>0$, we have $h'
(x)>0$ for all sufficiently negative $x$.

---

**Step 5: The limit exists and equals $0$.**

For all $x$ sufficiently negative, $h$ is convex (Step 3) and increasing (Step 4
), with $h(x)\to 0^+$ (Step 2) and $h'(x)>0$.

Since $h$ is convex, $h'$ is an increasing function of $x$. Therefore, as $x\to-
\infty$, $h'(x)$ is **decreasing** (in the leftward direction) and bounded below
 by $0$, so it converges:
$$h'(x) \to L \;\ge\; 0.$$

**Suppose $L>0$.** Then $h'(x)\ge L$ for all sufficiently negative $x$, so for a
ny fixed $a$:
$$h(a) - h(x) = \int_x^a h'(t)\,dt \;\ge\; L(a-x)\;\xrightarrow{x\to-\infty}\;+\
infty,$$
giving $h(x)\to -\infty$, which contradicts $h(x)>0$.

Therefore $L=0$, and
$$\lim_{x\to -\infty} g'(x)\,e^{g(x)} = \lim_{x\to-\infty} h'(x) = 0. \qquad\bla
cksquare$$
