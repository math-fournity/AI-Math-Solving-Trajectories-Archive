**Goal.** Compute the matrix derivative (gradient) of

$$f(\Sigma) = \operatorname{trace}\!\bigl(\Sigma^{1/2}\, S^{-1}\, \Sigma^{1/2}\b
igr)$$

with respect to $\Sigma$, where $\Sigma, S \succ 0$ are positive definite (hence
 symmetric).

---

### Step 1. Simplify the expression using the cyclic property of trace.

Since $\Sigma^{1/2}$ is symmetric and $\Sigma^{1/2}\Sigma^{1/2} = \Sigma$,

$$f(\Sigma) = \operatorname{trace}\!\bigl(\Sigma^{1/2}\, S^{-1}\, \Sigma^{1/2}\b
igr) = \operatorname{trace}\!\bigl(S^{-1}\, \Sigma^{1/2}\Sigma^{1/2}\bigr) = \op
eratorname{trace}\!\bigl(S^{-1}\,\Sigma\bigr).$$

So $f(\Sigma) = \operatorname{trace}(S^{-1}\Sigma)$.

---

### Step 2. Compute the differential.

Let $A = S^{-1}$ (constant, symmetric). Then

$$df = \operatorname{trace}(A\, d\Sigma).$$

Using the Frobenius inner product $\langle X, Y\rangle = \operatorname{trace}(X^
{\top} Y)$,

$$df = \operatorname{trace}(A\, d\Sigma) = \operatorname{trace}(A^{\top}\, d\Sig
ma) = \langle A^{\top},\, d\Sigma\rangle.$$

Since $S$ is positive definite, $S^{-1}$ is symmetric, so $A^{\top} = A = S^{-1}
$. Hence

$$df = \langle S^{-1},\, d\Sigma\rangle.$$

By definition of the matrix gradient, $\nabla_{\Sigma} f$ is the unique matrix s
atisfying $df = \langle \nabla_{\Sigma} f,\, d\Sigma\rangle$ for all $d\Sigma$.
Therefore

$$\nabla_{\Sigma} f = S^{-1}.$$

---

### Step 3. Verification via direct differentiation (without the cyclic simplifi
cation).

Let $A = \Sigma^{1/2}$. Differentiating $A^2 = \Sigma$ gives the Sylvester relat
ion

$$A\, dA + dA\, A = d\Sigma.$$

Now differentiate $f = \operatorname{trace}(A\, S^{-1}\, A)$:

$$df = \operatorname{trace}(dA\, S^{-1}\, A) + \operatorname{trace}(A\, S^{-1}\,
 dA).$$

By cyclicity of trace,

$$df = \operatorname{trace}(S^{-1}\, A\, dA) + \operatorname{trace}(S^{-1}\, dA\
, A) = \operatorname{trace}\!\bigl(S^{-1}\,(A\, dA + dA\, A)\bigr) = \operatorna
me{trace}(S^{-1}\, d\Sigma).$$

This confirms $df = \langle S^{-1}, d\Sigma\rangle$, giving the same gradient $\
nabla_{\Sigma} f = S^{-1}$.

### Result

$$\boxed{\dfrac{\partial}{\partial \Sigma}\, \operatorname{trace}\!\bigl(\Sigma^
{1/2}\, S^{-1}\, \Sigma^{1/2}\bigr) \;=\; S^{-1}.}$$

The key insight is that the cyclic property of the trace collapses $\Sigma^{1/2}
 S^{-1} \Sigma^{1/2}$ into $S^{-1}\Sigma$, reducing the problem to the elementar

ls $S^{-1}$ here since $S^{-1}$ is symmetric.
