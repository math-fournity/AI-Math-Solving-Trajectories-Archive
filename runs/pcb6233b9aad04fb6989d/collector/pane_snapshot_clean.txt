## Proof

Let $\|\cdot\|$ denote the operator norm on $\mathcal{B}(V)$. We prove
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(I+\frac{A_n}{n}\right)^n = \exp(A) := \sum_{k=0}^{\inft
y}\frac{A^k}{k!},$$
where the series converges in operator norm since $A$ is bounded.

We split the argument into two steps.

**Step 1 (the classical limit).** *If $B$ is a fixed bounded operator, then $\le
ft(I+\frac{B}{n}\right)^n \to \exp(B)$ in operator norm.*

Set $S_n = \left(I+\frac{B}{n}\right)^n$ and $T_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{B^k}{k!}
$. Expanding the binomial (operators $I$ and $B/n$ commute),
$$S_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{B^k}{n^k} = \sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{n(n-1)\c
dots(n-k+1)}{n^k}\,\frac{B^k}{k!}.$$
Writing $c_{n,k} = \prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\bigl(1-\tfrac{j}{n}\bigr)$ (with $c_{n,0}=1
$), we have $S_n - T_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n}(c_{n,k}-1)\frac{B^k}{k!}$. Using $1-\pro
d_j(1-x_j)\le \sum_j x_j$ for $x_j\in[0,1]$ (valid once $n\ge 2$ so every factor
 lies in $[0,1]$),
$$0\le 1-c_{n,k}\le \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{j}{n}=\frac{k(k-1)}{2n}.$$
Hence, with $M=\|B\|$,
$$\|S_n-T_n\|\le \sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{k(k-1)}{2n}\frac{M^k}{k!}\le \frac{1}{2n}\s
um_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{k(k-1)\,M^k}{k!}=\frac{M^2\,e^{M}}{2n}\xrightarrow[n\to\i
nfty]{}0.$$
Since $T_n\to\exp(B)$ by definition of the exponential series, we conclude $S_n\
to\exp(B)$.

**Step 2 (perturbation by $A_n-A$).** *We show $\left(I+\frac{A_n}{n}\right)^n -
 \left(I+\frac{A}{n}\right)^n \to 0$ in operator norm.*

Because $A_n\to A$, the sequence $(\|A_n\|)$ is bounded; set
$$C := \sup_{n\ge 1}\max\bigl(\|A_n\|,\|A\|\bigr)<\infty,\qquad \delta_n:=\|A_n-
A\|\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.$$
Define $X_n := I+\frac{A_n}{n}$ and $Y_n := I+\frac{A}{n}$. Then
$$\|X_n\|\le 1+\frac{C}{n},\qquad \|Y_n\|\le 1+\frac{C}{n},\qquad \|X_n-Y_n\|=\f
rac{\delta_n}{n}.$$
Using the telescoping identity $X_n^n - Y_n^n = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}X_n^{\,n-1-k}(X_
n-Y_n)\,Y_n^{\,k}$,
$$\left\|X_n^n-Y_n^n\right\|\le \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\|X_n\|^{n-1-k}\,\|X_n-Y_n\|\,\|
Y_n\|^{k}\le \frac{\delta_n}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(1+\frac{C}{n}\right)^{n-1}.
$$
The sum has $n$ identical terms, each bounded by $(1+C/n)^{n-1}\le (1+C/n)^n\le
e^{C}$ (using $1+t\le e^t$). Therefore
$$\left\|X_n^n-Y_n^n\right\|\le \frac{\delta_n}{n}\cdot n\cdot e^{C}=e^{C}\,\del
ta_n\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.$$

**Conclusion.** By the triangle inequality in $\mathcal{B}(V)$,
$$\left\|\left(I+\frac{A_n}{n}\right)^n-\exp(A)\right\|\le \left\|\left(I+\frac{
A_n}{n}\right)^n-\left(I+\frac{A}{n}\right)^n\right\|+\left\|\left(I+\frac{A}{n}
\right)^n-\exp(A)\right\|.$$
The first term tends to $0$ by Step 2; the second tends to $0$ by Step 1 (applie
d with $B=A$). Hence
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(I+\frac{A_n}{n}\right)^n=\exp(A),$$
with convergence in the operator norm. $\blacksquare$
