## Proof

We determine the necessary and sufficient condition on the symmetric matrix $A$.

### Necessity: $A$ must be the zero matrix

**Step 1: $A$ is positive semidefinite.**

Set $y = 0$ (which satisfies $\|y\|_2 = 0 \leq 1$). The inequality becomes:
$$\frac{1}{2}\, x^T A\, x \;\geq\; 0$$
for all $x$ with $\|x\|_2 \leq 1$. By homogeneity of the quadratic form (replaci
ng $x$ by $t x$ and letting $t \to 0$), this holds for all unit-norm $x$ if and
only if it holds for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$. Hence $x^T A x \geq 0$ for all $x
$, i.e., $A \succeq 0$.

**Step 2: $A$ is negative semidefinite.**

Set $x = y$ (with $\|x\|_2 \leq 1$, so both constraints are satisfied). The ineq
uality becomes:
$$\frac{1}{2}\bigl(x^T A x - x^T A x\bigr) \;\geq\; x^T A\, x,$$
that is,
$$0 \;\geq\; x^T A\, x$$
for all $x$ with $\|x\|_2 \leq 1$. Again by homogeneity, this extends to all $x
\in \mathbb{R}^n$. Hence $x^T A x \leq 0$ for all $x$, i.e., $A \preceq 0$.

**Step 3: Combine.**

From Step 1, every eigenvalue $\lambda_i$ of $A$ satisfies $\lambda_i \geq 0$. F
rom Step 2, every eigenvalue satisfies $\lambda_i \leq 0$. Therefore $\lambda_i
= 0$ for all $i$. Since $A$ is symmetric (hence orthogonally diagonalizable), $A
 = 0$.

### Sufficiency: $A = 0$ satisfies the inequality

If $A = 0$, then $x^T A x = 0$, $y^T A y = 0$, and $x^T A y = 0$ for all $x, y$.
 The inequality becomes $0 \geq 0$, which holds trivially for all $x, y$ with $\
|x\|_2, \|y\|_2 \leq 1$. $\checkmark$

### Conclusion

The inequality $\frac{1}{2}(x^T A x - y^T A y) \geq x^T A y$ holds for all $x, y
$ with $\|x\|_2, \|y\|_2 \leq 1$ if and only if $A$ is the zero matrix, i.e., $\
boxed{A = 0}$.

The key observation is that the feasible region $\{(x,y) : \|x\|_2 \leq 1,\; \|y
\|_2 \leq 1\}$ contains both the point $y = 0$ (forcing $A \succeq 0$) and the d
iagonal $x = y$ (forcing $A \preceq 0$), which together pin $A$ to zero.
