## Theorem

Every finite-dimensional C*-algebra is *-isomorphic to a finite direct sum of fu
ll matrix algebras $\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k} M_{n_i}(\mathbb{C})$.

## Proof

Let $A$ be a finite-dimensional C\*-algebra.

### Step 1: Faithful representation on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space

For every nonzero $a \in A$, there exists a state $\varphi$ on $A$ with $\varphi
(a^*a) > 0$ (since $\|a\|^2 = \sup_{\varphi} \varphi(a^*a)$). Because $A$ is fin
ite-dimensional, finitely many states $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_m$ suffice to s
eparate all points. The direct sum of the corresponding GNS representations
$$\pi = \pi_{\varphi_1} \oplus \cdots \oplus \pi_{\varphi_m}$$
is a faithful \*-representation of $A$ on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space $H
\cong \mathbb{C}^N$ (each GNS space is finite-dimensional since $A$ is). Thus $A
 \cong \pi(A)$ as C\*-algebras, where $\pi(A) \subseteq M_N(\mathbb{C})$ is a \*
-subalgebra.

### Step 2: $\pi(A)$ is semisimple (Jacobson radical is zero)

Let $J$ be the Jacobson radical of $\pi(A)$; it is nilpotent since $\pi(A)$ is f
inite-dimensional (Artinian).

**$J$ is \*-stable:** If $a \in J$, then $1 - xa$ is invertible in the unitizati
on for all $x \in \pi(A)$. Taking adjoints, $(1-xa)^* = 1 - a^*x^*$ is invertibl
e for all $x$, which means $a^* \in J$.

For $a \in J$, since $J$ is a \*-stable two-sided ideal, $a^*a \in J$, hence $a^
*a$ is nilpotent. Being self-adjoint, $a^*a$ is normal, so $\|a^*a\|$ equals its
 spectral radius, which is $0$. By the C\*-identity $\|a\|^2 = \|a^*a\| = 0$, so
 $a = 0$. Therefore $J = \{0\}$.

### Step 3: Wedderburn–Artin decomposition

Since $\pi(A)$ is a finite-dimensional semisimple algebra over the algebraically
 closed field $\mathbb{C}$, the Wedderburn–Artin theorem yields an algebra isomo
rphism
$$\pi(A) \cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^{k} M_{n_i}(\mathbb{C}),$$
where we used that the only finite-dimensional division algebra over $\mathbb{C}
$ is $\mathbb{C}$ itself (any element generates a finite algebraic field extensi
on of $\mathbb{C}$, which must be $\mathbb{C}$). The irreducible $\pi(A)$-module
s are $V_i \cong \mathbb{C}^{n_i}$.

### Step 4: Orthogonal isotypic decomposition of $H$

As a $\pi(A)$-module, $H$ decomposes into isotypic components:
$$H = \bigoplus_{i=1}^{k} W_i, \qquad W_i \cong V_i \otimes \mathbb{C}^{m_i}$$
for some multiplicities $m_i \geq 0$ (with $m_i > 0$ for each $i$ since $\pi$ is
 faithful).

**The $W_i$ are mutually orthogonal.** The projection $P_i$ onto $W_i$ along $\b
igoplus_{j \neq i} W_j$ is a $\pi(A)$-module map, so $P_i \in \pi(A)'$. Since $\
pi(A)$ is \*-stable, the commutant $\pi(A)'$ is \*-stable, hence $P_i^* \in \pi(
A)'$.

Now $\ker P_i = \bigoplus_{j \neq i} W_j$ is $\pi(A)$-invariant, so $(\ker P_i)^
\perp$ is also $\pi(A)$-invariant (if $W$ is $\pi(A)$-invariant, then for $w \in
 W^\perp$, $v \in W$: $\langle \pi(a)w, v\rangle = \langle w, \pi(a^*)v\rangle =
 0$). Since $(\ker P_i)^\perp$ is a submodule containing $W_i$ and contained in
the complement of $\bigoplus_{j\neq i} W_j$, the isotypic decomposition forces $
(\ker P_i)^\perp = W_i$. Thus $\operatorname{ran} P_i^* = W_i = \operatorname{ra
n} P_i$ and $\ker P_i^* = (\operatorname{ran} P_i)^\perp = W_i^\perp = \ker P_i$
, giving $P_i^* = P_i$. So each $P_i$ is an orthogonal projection and the $W_i$
are mutually orthogonal.

### Step 5: Matrix block form

Choose an orthonormal basis for each $W_i$ adapted to $W_i = V_i \otimes \mathbb
{C}^{m_i}$. Combining these (the $W_i$ are orthogonal) gives an orthonormal basi
s of $H$. With respect to this basis, the action of $\pi(A)$ on $W_i$ is $M_{n_i
}(\mathbb{C}) \otimes I_{m_i}$: the $i$-th Wedderburn–Artin block acts on $V_i \
cong \mathbb{C}^{n_i}$, with identity on the multiplicity space $\mathbb{C}^{m_i
}$. Therefore
$$\pi(A) = \bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\bigl(M_{n_i}(\mathbb{C}) \otimes I_{m_i}\bigr) \s
ubseteq M_N(\mathbb{C})$$
as a \*-subalgebra (the involution is conjugate transpose, since the basis is or
thonormal).

### Step 6: Conclusion

The map
$$\psi: \pi(A) \longrightarrow \bigoplus_{i=1}^{k} M_{n_i}(\mathbb{C}), \qquad \
bigoplus_i (B_i \otimes I_{m_i}) \longmapsto (B_1, \dots, B_k)$$
is a \*-isomorphism: it preserves addition, multiplication, scalar multiplicatio
n, and the involution (conjugate transpose in each block), and is bijective. Com
posing with the faithful \*-representation $A \xrightarrow{\sim} \pi(A)$, we obt
ain
$$A \;\cong\; \bigoplus_{i=1}^{k} M_{n_i}(\mathbb{C})$$
as C\*-algebras. $\blacksquare$
