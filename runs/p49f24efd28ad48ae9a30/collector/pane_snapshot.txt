## Proof

**Setup.** Using Knuth's up-arrow notation, the sequences are:
- $G_1 = 3 \uparrow^4 3$, and $G_n = 3 \uparrow^{G_{n-1}} 3$ for $n \geq 2$ (i.e
., $G_{n-1}$ up-arrows between two 3's).
- $H_1 = 4 \uparrow^4 4$, and $H_n = 4 \uparrow^{H_{n-1}} 4$ for $n \geq 2$ (i.e
., $H_{n-1}$ up-arrows between two 4's).

**Key monotonicity fact.** For integers $a \geq 2$, $b \geq 1$, and $k \geq 1$,
the hyperoperation $a \uparrow^k b$ is strictly increasing in each of $a$, $b$,
and $k$ (for $a \geq 2$). This follows from the recursive definition $a \uparrow
^k b = a \uparrow^{k-1}(a \uparrow^k (b-1))$ and induction.

**Base case: $H_1 > G_1$.**

$$G_1 = 3 \uparrow^4 3, \qquad H_1 = 4 \uparrow^4 4.$$

Both use the same number of up-arrows (4). Since $4 > 3$ in both the base and th
e right operand, by monotonicity:

$$4 \uparrow^4 4 > 3 \uparrow^4 3 \implies H_1 > G_1.$$

(One can also see this concretely: $G_1 = 3\uparrow^3(3\uparrow^3 3)$ involves t
wo nested $\uparrow^3$ applications, while $H_1 = 4\uparrow^3(4\uparrow^3(4\upar
row^3 4))$ involves three, with a larger base throughout—so $H_1$ is vastly larg
er.)

**Inductive step: $H_{n-1} > G_{n-1} \implies H_n > G_n$.**

Assume $H_{n-1} > G_{n-1}$ for some $n \geq 2$. Then:
$$G_n = 3 \uparrow^{G_{n-1}} 3, \qquad H_n = 4 \uparrow^{H_{n-1}} 4.$$

Comparing the three arguments of the hyperoperation:
- **Base:** $4 > 3$,
- **Number of arrows:** $H_{n-1} > G_{n-1}$ (by the induction hypothesis),
- **Right operand:** $4 > 3$.

By monotonicity of $a \uparrow^k b$ in all three parameters, $H_n > G_n$.

**Conclusion.** By induction, $H_n > G_n$ for **all** $n \geq 1$. The inequality
 holds already at $n = 1$, so the smallest $n$ such that $H_n > G_n$ is:

$$\boxed{n = 1}$$
