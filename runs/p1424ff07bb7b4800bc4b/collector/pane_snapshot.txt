## Proof

**Step 1: The defining inequality is satisfied by every real-valued function.**

Consider the inequality
$$|f(x)| \le 2 + f(x)^2.$$
Setting $t = f(x) \in \mathbb{R}$ and $u = |t| \ge 0$, the inequality becomes
$$u \le 2 + u^2 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u^2 - u + 2 \ge 0.$$
The quadratic $q(u) = u^2 - u + 2$ has discriminant $\Delta = (-1)^2 - 4\cdot 1\
cdot 2 = 1 - 8 = -7 < 0$ with positive leading coefficient, so $q(u) > 0$ for ev
ery $u \in \mathbb{R}$. Equivalently, its minimum on $[0,\infty)$ is attained at
 $u = \tfrac12$ and equals $q(\tfrac12) = \tfrac14 - \tfrac12 + 2 = \tfrac74 > 0
$.

Therefore $|t| \le 2 + t^2$ holds for **every** real number $t$, and consequentl
y the inequality $|f(x)| \le 2 + f(x)^2$ is satisfied by *every* function $f:[a,
b]\to\mathbb{R}$, at every point $x$.

**Step 2: The set in question is all of $C[a,b]$.**

Let
$$S = \bigl\{f \in C[a,b] : |f(x)| \le 2 + f(x)^2 \text{ for all } x \in [a,b]\b
igr\}.$$
By Step 1, the pointwise condition imposes no restriction whatsoever on the valu
es of $f$. Hence every continuous function on $[a,b]$ belongs to $S$:
$$S = C[a,b].$$

**Step 3: $C[a,b]$ with the sup norm is complete.**

It is a classical result that the space $C[a,b]$ of continuous real-valued (or c
omplex-valued) functions on a compact interval $[a,b]$, equipped with the sup no
rm
$$\|f\|_\infty = \sup_{x\in[a,b]} |f(x)|, \qquad \rho_\infty(f,g) = \|f-g\|_\inf
ty,$$
is a complete metric space. The standard argument: given a Cauchy sequence $(f_n
)$ in $C[a,b]$, the pointwise limit $f(x) = \lim_{n\to\infty} f_n(x)$ exists (si
nce $(f_n(x))$ is Cauchy in $\mathbb{R}$ for each fixed $x$); uniform Cauchy-nes
s implies the convergence is uniform; a uniform limit of continuous functions is
 continuous, so $f \in C[a,b]$; and uniform convergence is exactly convergence i
n $\rho_\infty$.

**Conclusion.**

Since $S = C[a,b]$ and $(C[a,b], \rho_\infty)$ is complete, the set $S$ is a com
plete metric space with respect to the sup norm metric $\rho_\infty$.
