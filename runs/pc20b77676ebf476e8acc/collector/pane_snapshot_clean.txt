## Solution

**Setup.** The efficiency rating is

$$n = \frac{3xy - 1}{x+y},$$

where $x, y$ are positive integers (container counts) and $x+y \neq 0$. We seek
all $n \in \{1,\dots,100\}$ for which such $x,y$ exist.

**Key transformation.** Rearranging $3xy - 1 = n(x+y)$:

$$3xy - nx - ny = 1 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; 9xy - 3nx - 3ny = 3 \;\;\Longrightar
row\;\; (3x-n)(3y-n) = n^2 + 3.$$

Let $A = 3x-n$, $B = 3y-n$. Then $AB = n^2+3$, and integrality of $x,y$ requires

$$A \equiv -n \pmod{3}, \qquad B \equiv -n \pmod{3}.$$

Since $x,y \geq 1$, we need $A > -n$ and $B > -n$; as $AB = n^2+3 > 0$, both $A,
B$ have the same sign, and the negative-sign option forces $|A|,|B| \leq n-1$, g
iving $|A||B| \leq (n-1)^2 < n^2+3$ (for $n\geq 2$; checked separately for $n=1$
). So **$A,B$ must be positive divisors** of $n^2+3$, both congruent to $-n \pmo
d 3$.

**A quadratic-residue lemma.** For any odd prime $p \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$, $-3$ is
*not* a quadratic residue mod $p$:
$$\left(\frac{-3}{p}\right) = \left(\frac{-1}{p}\right)\left(\frac{3}{p}\right)
= 1 \cdot \left(\frac{p}{3}\right)(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot 1} = \left(\frac{2}{3
}\right)\cdot 1 = -1,$$
since $p \equiv 2\pmod 3 \Rightarrow p \equiv 5 \pmod 6 \Rightarrow (p-1)/2$ is
even. Hence if $p \equiv 2\pmod 3$ (odd) divides $a^2+3$, then $a^2 \equiv -3 \p
mod p$ would make $-3$ a residue — contradiction. So **every odd prime factor of
 $a^2+3$ is $\equiv 1 \pmod 3$** (or equals $3$).

Now split by $n \bmod 6$.

**Case $n \equiv 0 \pmod{3}$ (i.e. $n \equiv 0,3 \pmod 6$).** Need $A \equiv B \
equiv 0 \pmod 3$, so $9 \mid AB = n^2+3$. But $n \equiv 0 \pmod 3 \Rightarrow n^
2+3 \equiv 3 \pmod 9$. Impossible. **No solutions.**

**Case $n \equiv 4 \pmod 6$ (even, $n \equiv 1 \pmod 3$).** Here $n^2+3$ is odd
(so no factor $2$) and $3 \nmid n^2+3$ (since $n^2+3 \equiv 1 \pmod 3$). By the
lemma, *every* prime factor of $n^2+3$ is $\equiv 1 \pmod 3$, hence *every* divi
sor is $\equiv 1 \pmod 3$. But we need divisors $\equiv -n \equiv 2 \pmod 3$. No
ne exist. **No solutions.**

**Case $n \equiv 1 \pmod 6$ (odd, $n \equiv 1 \pmod 3$).** Need $A \equiv B \equ
iv 2 \pmod 3$. Now $n^2+3$ is even; in fact $n^2 \equiv 1 \pmod 8 \Rightarrow n^
2+3 \equiv 4 \pmod 8$, so $n^2+3 = 4q$ with $q$ odd and $q \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ (al
l prime factors of $q$ are $\equiv 1\pmod 3$ by the lemma). Take
$$A = 2,\quad B = 2q = \frac{n^2+3}{2}.$$
Then $A \equiv 2$, $B \equiv 2\pmod 3$ (since $q\equiv 1$), and
$$x = \frac{A+n}{3} = \frac{n+1}{3} \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0},\qquad y = \frac{B+n}{3}
 = \frac{n^2+n+3}{2\cdot 3} \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}.$$
**All such $n$ work.**

**Case $n \equiv 2 \pmod 3$ (i.e. $n \equiv 2,5 \pmod 6$).** Need $A \equiv B \e
quiv -n \pmod 3$. Take $A = 1$, $B = n^2+3$. Check: $1 \equiv -n \pmod 3 \iff n
\equiv 2 \pmod 3$ ✓; and $n^2+3 \equiv n^2 \equiv 1 \equiv -n \pmod 3$ (since $n
\equiv 2 \Rightarrow -n\equiv 1$) ✓. Then
$$x = \frac{1+n}{3} \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0},\qquad y = \frac{n^2+3+n}{3} = \frac{n(n
+1)+3}{3} \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}.$$
**All such $n$ work.**

**Conclusion.** $n \in [1,100]$ is achievable iff $n \equiv 1, 2,$ or $5 \pmod 6
$.

**Summing.** Count and sum each residue class in $\{1,\dots,100\}$:

| $n \bmod 6$ | values | count | sum |
|---|---|---|---|
| $1$ | $1,7,\dots,97$ | $17$ | $17\cdot 49 = 833$ |
| $2$ | $2,8,\dots,98$ | $17$ | $17\cdot 50 = 850$ |
| $5$ | $5,11,\dots,95$ | $16$ | $16\cdot 50 = 800$ |

Total sum $= 833 + 850 + 800 = \boxed{2483}$.

*Verification via complement:* the failing classes are $n\equiv 0,3,4\pmod 6$, w
ith sums $816+867+884 = 2567$; and $5050 - 2567 = 2483$. ✓
