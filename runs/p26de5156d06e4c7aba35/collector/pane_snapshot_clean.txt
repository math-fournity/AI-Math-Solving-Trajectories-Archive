## Proof

In general coordinates $x^i$, the contravariant components of the acceleration v
ector of a particle are given by the geodesic expression

$$a^i \;=\; \frac{d^2 x^i}{dt^2} \;+\; \Gamma^i_{jk}\,\frac{dx^j}{dt}\,\frac{dx^
k}{dt},$$

where $\Gamma^i_{jk}$ are the Christoffel symbols of the second kind,

$$\Gamma^i_{jk} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,g^{il}\bigl(\partial_j g_{lk} + \partial_k g
_{lj} - \partial_l g_{jk}\bigr).$$

For cylindrical coordinates $(x^1,x^2,x^3)=(r,\theta,z)$ the metric is diagonal
with

$$g_{11}=1,\qquad g_{22}=(x^1)^2=r^2,\qquad g_{33}=1,$$

so the inverse metric is

$$g^{11}=1,\qquad g^{22}=\frac{1}{r^2},\qquad g^{33}=1.$$

The only non-constant metric component is $g_{22}=r^2$, whose only non-vanishing
 derivative is $\partial_1 g_{22}=2r$. We compute the non-zero Christoffel symbo
ls.

**Symbol $\Gamma^1_{22}$.** Using $l=1$ (since $g^{11}=1$ is the only non-zero i
nverse entry for $i=1$):

$$\Gamma^1_{22} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}g^{11}\bigl(\partial_2 g_{12}+\partial_2 g_{12
}-\partial_1 g_{22}\bigr) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}(1)(-2r) \;=\; -r \;=\; -x^1.$$

**Symbol $\Gamma^2_{12}=\Gamma^2_{21}$.** Using $l=2$ (since $g^{22}=1/r^2$ is t
he relevant inverse entry for $i=2$):

$$\Gamma^2_{12} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}g^{22}\bigl(\partial_1 g_{22}+\partial_2 g_{21
}-\partial_2 g_{12}\bigr) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{r^2}\cdot(2r) \;=\; \f
rac{1}{r} \;=\; \frac{1}{x^1}.$$

By symmetry in the lower indices $\Gamma^2_{21}=\Gamma^2_{12}=1/r$.

All other Christoffel symbols vanish, since every other combination either invol
ves only constant metric components or derivatives that are zero.

Now substitute into the acceleration formula. Writing $\dot{x}^i = dx^i/dt$ and
$\ddot{x}^i = d^2x^i/dt^2$:

**Component $a^1$ ($r$-component):**

$$a^1 \;=\; \ddot{x}^1 \;+\; \Gamma^1_{22}\,\dot{x}^2\dot{x}^2 \;=\; \ddot{r} \;
-\; r\,\dot{\theta}^{\,2}.$$

(The only non-zero $\Gamma^1_{jk}\dot{x}^j\dot{x}^k$ term is $\Gamma^1_{22}\dot{
x}^2\dot{x}^2$.)

**Component $a^2$ ($\theta$-component):**

$$a^2 \;=\; \ddot{x}^2 \;+\; 2\,\Gamma^2_{12}\,\dot{x}^1\dot{x}^2 \;=\; \ddot{\t
heta} \;+\; \frac{2}{r}\,\dot{r}\,\dot{\theta}.$$

(The factor $2$ comes from the two equal terms $\Gamma^2_{12}\dot{x}^1\dot{x}^2$
 and $\Gamma^2_{21}\dot{x}^2\dot{x}^1$.)

**Component $a^3$ ($z$-component):**

$$a^3 \;=\; \ddot{x}^3 \;=\; \ddot{z},$$

since all Christoffel symbols with upper index $3$ vanish.

### Result

The contravariant components of the acceleration vector in cylindrical coordinat
es $(r,\theta,z)$ are

$$\boxed{\,a^1 \;=\; \ddot{r} \;-\; r\,\dot{\theta}^{\,2},\qquad a^2 \;=\; \ddot
{\theta} \;+\; \frac{2}{r}\,\dot{r}\,\dot{\theta},\qquad a^3 \;=\; \ddot{z}\,}.$
$

These are the familiar expressions: the radial component contains the centripeta
l term $-r\dot{\theta}^2$, the angular component contains the Coriolis-type term
 $\frac{2}{r}\dot{r}\dot{\theta}$, and the axial component is just the plain sec
ond derivative $\ddot{z}$.
