**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

Let $A, B \succ 0$ be symmetric positive definite, and suppose $\|Ax\|\le \|Bx\|
$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^n$.

**Step 1: Reformulate the hypothesis as a Loewner order.**

Since $A,B$ are symmetric,
$$
\|Ax\|^2 = x^\top A^\top A\,x = x^\top A^2 x,\qquad \|Bx\|^2 = x^\top B^2 x.
$$
Thus $\|Ax\|\le\|Bx\|$ for all $x$ is equivalent to
$$
x^\top A^2 x \le x^\top B^2 x \quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^n,
$$
i.e. $B^2 - A^2 \succeq 0$, or $A^2 \preceq B^2$ in Loewner order.

**Step 2: Apply the Löwner–Heinz theorem (operator monotonicity of $t\mapsto t^{
1/2}$).**

The Löwner–Heinz theorem states that $f(t)=t^\alpha$ is operator monotone on $[0
,\infty)$ for every $0\le\alpha\le 1$: if $X\succeq Y\succeq 0$, then $X^\alpha\
succeq Y^\alpha$.

Take $X=B^2$, $Y=A^2$ (both $\succeq 0$ since $A,B\succ 0$), and $\alpha=\tfrac1
2$. From $B^2\succeq A^2$ we obtain
$$
(B^2)^{1/2}\succeq (A^2)^{1/2}.
$$
Because $A$ and $B$ are positive definite, $(A^2)^{1/2}=A$ and $(B^2)^{1/2}=B$ (
the unique positive definite square root of $A^2$ is $A$). Hence
$$
B \succeq A,\qquad\text{i.e.}\quad A\preceq B.
$$

**Step 3: Loewner order implies eigenvalue-wise order.**

Let eigenvalues be ordered increasingly: $\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\cdots\le\lamb
da_n$. By the Courant–Fischer min–max principle,
$$
\lambda_i(M)=\min_{\substack{V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n\\ \dim V=i}}\;\max_{0\ne x\i
n V}\frac{x^\top M x}{x^\top x}.
$$
Since $A\preceq B$, we have $x^\top A x\le x^\top B x$ for every $x$, so for eve
ry subspace $V$,
$$
\max_{0\ne x\in V}\frac{x^\top A x}{x^\top x}\le \max_{0\ne x\in V}\frac{x^\top
B x}{x^\top x}.
$$
Taking the minimum over all $i$-dimensional subspaces $V$ gives
$$
\lambda_i(A)\le \lambda_i(B)\qquad\text{for every }i=1,\dots,n.
$$

**Conclusion.** The hypothesis $\|Ax\|\le\|Bx\|$ for all $x$ implies $A^2\preceq
 B^2$, which by Löwner–Heinz (with exponent $1/2$) implies $A\preceq B$, which b
y Courant–Fischer implies $\lambda_i(A)\le\lambda_i(B)$ for all $i$. Therefore t
he answer is **yes**.
