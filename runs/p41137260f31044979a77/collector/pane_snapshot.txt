I'll solve this problem using pole-polar duality with respect to the incircle.

## Proof

**Setup.** A rhombus is a tangential quadrilateral, so it has an incircle. Place
 the incircle as the unit circle centered at the origin $O$. A tangent line to t
he unit circle at angle $\theta$ is
$$L(\theta):\quad x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta = 1.$$

Since $ABCD$ is a parallelogram, opposite sides are parallel, so their tangent p
oints on the incircle are antipodal. Thus we may write:
$$AB = L(\alpha),\quad BC = L(\beta),\quad CD = L(\alpha+\pi),\quad DA = L(\beta
+\pi)$$
for some angles $\alpha, \beta$. Since $EF$ and $GH$ are tangent to the incircle
, write $EF = L(\phi)$ and $GH = L(\psi)$. The four points are:
$$E = L(\alpha)\cap L(\phi),\ F = L(\beta)\cap L(\phi),\ G = L(\alpha+\pi)\cap L
(\psi),\ H = L(\beta+\pi)\cap L(\psi).$$

**Pole–polar duality.** For the unit circle, the polar (dual) of the tangent lin
e $L(\theta)$ is the point $T(\theta) = (\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ on the circle,
and conversely. A standard theorem of conics states: *if a point $P$ is the inte
rsection of the two tangents to a conic at $T_1$ and $T_2$, then the polar of $P
$ is the line $T_1T_2$* (the chord of contact). Therefore:
- the dual of $E = L(\alpha)\cap L(\phi)$ is the line $T(\alpha)T(\phi)$;
- the dual of $F = L(\beta)\cap L(\phi)$ is the line $T(\beta)T(\phi)$;
- the dual of $G = L(\alpha+\pi)\cap L(\psi)$ is the line $T(\alpha+\pi)T(\psi)$
;
- the dual of $H = L(\beta+\pi)\cap L(\psi)$ is the line $T(\beta+\pi)T(\psi)$.

The pole of a line $\ell$ is the intersection of the polars of any two of its po
ints. Hence:
$$\text{pole}(EH) = T(\alpha)T(\phi)\ \cap\ T(\beta+\pi)T(\psi),$$
$$\text{pole}(FG) = T(\beta)T(\phi)\ \cap\ T(\alpha+\pi)T(\psi).$$

**Key fact.** For the unit circle, a line at distance $d>0$ from $O$ has its pol
e at distance $1/d$ from $O$ along the perpendicular from $O$ to the line. Conse
quently, two lines are parallel iff their poles are collinear with $O$. Thus it
suffices to show that $\text{pole}(EH)$ and $\text{pole}(FG)$ are collinear with
 the origin.

**Complex coordinate computation.** Identify the plane with $\mathbb{C}$; points
 on the unit circle are $e^{i\theta}$. Set
$$a = e^{i\alpha},\ b = e^{i\beta},\ p = e^{i\phi},\ q = e^{i\psi},$$
so that $T(\alpha)=a,\ T(\beta)=b,\ T(\alpha+\pi)=-a,\ T(\beta+\pi)=-b,\ T(\phi)
=p,\ T(\psi)=q$.

*Chord intersection formula.* The line through two unit-circle points $z_1,z_2$
satisfies $z + z_1 z_2\,\bar z = z_1+z_2$ (obtained from the reality of $(z-z_1)
/(z_2-z_1)$ using $\bar z_i = 1/z_i$). Solving the two linear equations for the
intersection of chord$(z_1,z_2)$ and chord$(z_3,z_4)$ gives:
$$z \;=\; \frac{z_3 z_4\,(z_1+z_2)\;-\;z_1 z_2\,(z_3+z_4)}{z_3 z_4 - z_1 z_2}.$$

Applying this:

- $\text{pole}(EH) = \text{chord}(a,p)\cap\text{chord}(-b,q)$, with $(z_1,z_2,z_
3,z_4)=(a,p,-b,q)$:
$$z_{EH} = \frac{(-b)q(a+p) - ap(-b+q)}{(-b)q - ap} = \frac{pq(a+b)-ab(p-q)}{ap+
bq}.$$

- $\text{pole}(FG) = \text{chord}(b,p)\cap\text{chord}(-a,q)$, with $(z_1,z_2,z_
3,z_4)=(b,p,-a,q)$:
$$z_{FG} = \frac{(-a)q(b+p) - bp(-a+q)}{(-a)q - bp} = \frac{pq(a+b)-ab(p-q)}{aq+
bp}.$$

Both share the **same numerator** $N = pq(a+b)-ab(p-q)$. Therefore:
$$\frac{z_{EH}}{z_{FG}} = \frac{aq+bp}{ap+bq} = \frac{e^{i(\alpha+\psi)}+e^{i(\b
eta+\phi)}}{e^{i(\alpha+\phi)}+e^{i(\beta+\psi)}}.$$

**The ratio is real.** Factor $e^{iM}$ with $M=\tfrac{\alpha+\beta+\phi+\psi}{2}
$ from both numerator and denominator:
$$\frac{z_{EH}}{z_{FG}} = \frac{2e^{iM}\cos\!\bigl(\tfrac{\alpha+\psi-\beta-\phi
}{2}\bigr)}{2e^{iM}\cos\!\bigl(\tfrac{\alpha+\phi-\beta-\psi}{2}\bigr)} = \frac{
\cos\!\bigl(\tfrac{\alpha-\beta+\psi-\phi}{2}\bigr)}{\cos\!\bigl(\tfrac{\alpha-\
beta+\phi-\psi}{2}\bigr)}.$$

This is a ratio of two real numbers, hence **real** (in the non-degenerate case
where the denominator is nonzero; degenerate cases follow by continuity).

**Conclusion.** Since $z_{EH}/z_{FG}\in\mathbb{R}$, the two poles lie on the sam
e line through the origin. By the key fact above, the lines $EH$ and $FG$ are pa
rallel. $\blacksquare$
