## Proof

Let
$$I = \int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln x}{1+x^{2}}\,dx.$$

We show $I=0$ by the substitution $x = 1/t$.

With $x = 1/t$, we have $dx = -\dfrac{1}{t^{2}}\,dt$, and the limits transform a
s $x\to 0^{+}\Rightarrow t\to\infty$, $x\to\infty\Rightarrow t\to 0^{+}$. Also $
\ln x = \ln(1/t) = -\ln t$, and
$$1+x^{2}=1+\frac{1}{t^{2}}=\frac{1+t^{2}}{t^{2}}.$$

Therefore
$$I=\int_{\infty}^{0}\frac{-\ln t}{\,(1+t^{2})/t^{2}\,}\left(-\frac{1}{t^{2}}\ri
ght)dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{-\ln t}{1+t^{2}}\,dt=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln t
}{1+t^{2}}\,dt=-I.$$

(The change of sign from the two minus signs — one from $-\ln t$, one from $dx=-
dt/t^{2}$ — combined with the reversal of limits yields the displayed result.)

Thus $I = -I$, so $2I = 0$, and hence

$$\boxed{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln x}{1+x^{2}}\,dx = 0.}$$
