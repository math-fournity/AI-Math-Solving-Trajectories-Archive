# Solution

**Answer:** $k(n) = n^2 - n$.

## Proof

We interpret "orthogonally equivalent" in the standard sense: $A$ and $B$ are or
thogonally equivalent if $B = U A V$ for some orthogonal matrices $U, V \in O(n)
$. This transformation preserves rank and singular values.

### Lower bound: $k(n) \geq n^2 - n$

Let $A$ be any real $n \times n$ matrix with $\operatorname{rank}(A) = r$. By th
e Singular Value Decomposition, there exist orthogonal matrices $U, V$ such that
$$U^T A V = \Sigma = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_r, 0, \dots, 0)
,$$
where $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_r > 0$. The matrix $\Sigma$ is diagonal, so it ha
s nonzero entries only on the diagonal (at most $r$ of them) and zeros everywher
e else. Thus $\Sigma$ has at least $n^2 - r$ zero entries. Since $r \leq n$, we
have $n^2 - r \geq n^2 - n$. Hence every matrix is orthogonally equivalent to on
e with at least $n^2 - n$ zeros.

### Upper bound: $k(n) \leq n^2 - n$

We exhibit a matrix for which no orthogonal equivalence can produce more than $n
^2 - n$ zeros. Take any invertible matrix $A$ (e.g., the identity), so $\operato
rname{rank}(A) = n$.

Suppose for contradiction that $B = U A V$ has at least $n^2 - n + 1$ zero entri
es, i.e., at most $n - 1$ nonzero entries. A matrix with at most $m$ nonzero ent
ries has at most $m$ nonzero rows, hence rank at most $m$. (Equivalently, the ra
nk is bounded by the number of nonzero entries, since each nonzero row must cont
ain at least one nonzero entry.) Therefore
$$\operatorname{rank}(B) \leq n - 1.$$
But orthogonal equivalence preserves rank, so $\operatorname{rank}(B) = \operato
rname{rank}(A) = n$, a contradiction.

Thus no invertible matrix can be orthogonally equivalent to a matrix with $n^2 -
 n + 1$ or more zeros. It follows that $k(n) \leq n^2 - n$.

### Conclusion

Combining both bounds,
$$\boxed{k(n) = n^2 - n}.$$
