**Proof.**

Consider the parity (even/odd) of each coordinate of a lattice point in $\mathbb
{Z}^3$. Each of the three coordinates is either even or odd, so every lattice po
int belongs to one of $2^3 = 8$ parity classes, indexed by triples in $\{0,1\}^3
$.

We have $|S| = 9$ lattice points and only $8$ parity classes. By the Pigeonhole
Principle, there exist two distinct points $P, Q \in S$ belonging to the same pa
rity class.

Since $P$ and $Q$ share the same parity class, for each coordinate $i \in \{1,2,
3\}$, the coordinates $P_i$ and $Q_i$ have the same parity. Therefore $P_i - Q_i
$ is even for every $i$, which means each $\frac{P_i + Q_i}{2}$ is an integer. H
ence

$$
M = \frac{P+Q}{2}
$$

is a lattice point.

Since $P \neq Q$, the point $M$ is the midpoint of the segment $\overline{PQ}$ a
nd lies strictly in the interior of $\overline{PQ}$. Thus $M$ is a lattice point
 in the interior of a line segment joining two distinct points of $S$.

$\blacksquare$
