## Solution

**Encode the three colors as elements of $\mathbb{Z}_3 = \{0,1,2\}$.** Let $\chi
:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{Z}_3$ be a coloring (natural numbers $= 1,2,3,\dots$).

### Key observation

For a triple $(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{Z}_3^3$, the condition "all the same color or al
l three different colors" is equivalent to
$$x+y+z \equiv 0 \pmod 3.$$
*Proof of the observation.* If all are equal, $x+y+z=3x\equiv 0$. If all are dif
ferent, $\{x,y,z\}=\{0,1,2\}$, so $x+y+z=3\equiv 0$. Conversely, if $x+y+z\equiv
 0$ and two are equal, say $x=y$, then $2x+z\equiv 0$, i.e. $z\equiv x$, so all
three are equal. Hence "sum $\equiv 0$" forces "all equal or all distinct." $\sq
uare$

So the problem's condition becomes: for all $a,b\geq 1$,
$$\chi(a)+\chi(b)+\chi\bigl(2000(a+b)\bigr)\equiv 0 \pmod 3. \tag{$\ast$}$$

### Necessity: $\chi$ must be an arithmetic progression mod 3

Fix $n\geq 2$ and take any $a\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}$ with $b=n-a$. From $(\ast)$,
$$\chi(a)+\chi(n-a)\equiv -\chi(2000n)\pmod 3,$$
where the right-hand side depends only on $n$. Hence $\chi(a)+\chi(n-a)$ is **co
nstant** as $a$ ranges over $1,\dots,n-1$.

For $n\geq 3$ we may use $a=1$ and $a=2$:
$$\chi(1)+\chi(n-1)=\chi(2)+\chi(n-2),$$
so
$$\chi(n-1)-\chi(n-2)=\chi(2)-\chi(1)\qquad\text{for all } n\geq 3.$$
Setting $m=n-1\geq 2$, the forward difference is constant:
$$\chi(m)-\chi(m-1)=d,\qquad d:=\chi(2)-\chi(1),\quad\text{for all }m\geq 2.$$
Therefore $\chi$ is an arithmetic progression modulo $3$:
$$\boxed{\;\chi(n)=\alpha+(n-1)d\pmod 3,\qquad \alpha:=\chi(1)\in\mathbb{Z}_3,\;
 d\in\mathbb{Z}_3.\;}$$

### Sufficiency: every such progression works

Take $\chi(n)=\alpha+(n-1)d$. Then
$$\chi(a)+\chi(b)+\chi\bigl(2000(a+b)\bigr)=3\alpha+\bigl[(a-1)+(b-1)+(2000(a+b)
-1)\bigr]d.$$
The bracket equals $2001(a+b)-3$. Since $2001=3\cdot 667\equiv 0\pmod 3$ and $3\
equiv 0$, the whole sum is
$$3\alpha + 2001(a+b)d - 3d \equiv 0\pmod 3$$
for **every** choice of $a,b$ and every $\alpha,d\in\mathbb{Z}_3$. By the observ
ation, the triple is always "all same or all different." $\checkmark$

### Description of all colorings

There are exactly **9** colorings, parametrized by $(\alpha,d)\in\mathbb{Z}_3^2$
, given by
$$\chi(n)\equiv \alpha+(n-1)d\pmod 3,\qquad n=1,2,3,\dots$$

Explicitly:

- **$d=0$ (3 colorings):** monochromatic — every number gets the single color $\
alpha\in\{0,1,2\}$.
- **$d=1$ (3 colorings):** the colors cycle $1,2,3,1,2,3,\dots$ (mod 3) starting
 from color $\alpha$; i.e. $\chi(n)\equiv\alpha+n-1\pmod 3$.
- **$d=2$ (3 colorings):** the colors cycle in the reverse order; i.e. $\chi(n)\
equiv\alpha+2(n-1)\pmod 3$.

These 9 are pairwise distinct (different $(\alpha,d)$ give different functions o
n $\mathbb{N}$), and we have shown every valid coloring is of this form and ever
y coloring of this form is valid.

### Conclusion

The colorings satisfying the condition are precisely the **arithmetic progressio
ns modulo $3$**: $\chi(n)\equiv \alpha+(n-1)d\pmod 3$ for arbitrary $\alpha,d\in
\{0,1,2\}$, giving **9** colorings in total — three monochromatic and six in whi
ch the colors cycle periodically with period $3$ (three forward, three backward)
.
