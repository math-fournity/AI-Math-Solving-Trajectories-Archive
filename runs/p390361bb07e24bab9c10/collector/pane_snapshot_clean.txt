## Solution

**Setup using barycentric coordinates.** Let $AC = b$. I use barycentric coordin
ates with respect to $\triangle ABC$ with side lengths $BC = a$, $CA = b$, $AB =
 c$.

**Step 1: Find L in barycentric coordinates.**

- $M$ is the midpoint of $BC$: $M = (0:1:1)$, so the median $AM$ (through $A=(1:
0:0)$ and $M=(0:1:1)$) has equation $y = z$.
- By the angle bisector theorem, $K$ on $AC$ satisfies $AK:KC = AB:BC = c:a$, so
 $K = (a:0:c)$. The line $BK$ (through $B=(0:1:0)$ and $K=(a:0:c)$) has equation
 $cx = az$.
- Intersection $L$: from $y = z$ and $cx = az$, set $z = c$ to get $x = a$, $y =
 c$. Thus $L = (a : c : c)$.

**Step 2: Equation of line $CL$.**

The line through $C = (0:0:1)$ and $L = (a:c:c)$ is:
$$\det\begin{pmatrix} x & y & z \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ a & c & c \end{pmatrix} = -cx +
ay = 0 \implies ay = cx.$$

**Step 3: Circumcenter in barycentric coordinates.**

The circumcenter is $O = \bigl(a^2(b^2+c^2-a^2) : b^2(c^2+a^2-b^2) : c^2(a^2+b^2
-c^2)\bigr)$.

**Step 4: Apply the collinearity condition $O \in CL$.**

Substituting $O$ into $ay = cx$:
$$a \cdot b^2(c^2 + a^2 - b^2) = c \cdot a^2(b^2 + c^2 - a^2).$$

Dividing by $a \neq 0$:
$$b^2(c^2 + a^2 - b^2) = ac(b^2 + c^2 - a^2).$$

**Step 5: Solve for $b^2$.**

Let $p = b^2$. Expanding and rearranging:
$$p(a^2 + c^2 - p) = ac(p + c^2 - a^2)$$
$$p(a^2 + c^2 - ac) - p^2 - ac(c^2 - a^2) = 0$$
$$p^2 - p(a^2 + c^2 - ac) - ac(a^2 - c^2) = 0.$$

This is a quadratic in $p$:
$$p = \frac{(a^2 + c^2 - ac) \pm \sqrt{(a^2 + c^2 - ac)^2 + 4ac(a^2 - c^2)}}{2}.
$$

The discriminant is:
$$\Delta = (a^2 + c^2 - ac)^2 + 4ac(a^2 - c^2).$$

**Step 6: Select the valid root.**

The product of roots equals $-ac(a^2 - c^2)$. When $a > c$ (as in the numerical
case), this product is negative, so exactly one root is positive. We take the $+
$ sign:

$$\boxed{AC^2 = \frac{a^2 + c^2 - ac + \sqrt{(a^2 + c^2 - ac)^2 + 4ac(a^2 - c^2)
}}{2}}.$$

**Step 7: Numerical verification for $a = 10$, $c = 6$.**

Compute:
- $a^2 + c^2 - ac = 100 + 36 - 60 = 76$
- $4ac(a^2 - c^2) = 4 \cdot 10 \cdot 6 \cdot (100 - 36) = 240 \cdot 64 = 15360$
- $\Delta = 76^2 + 15360 = 5776 + 15360 = 21136 = 16 \cdot 1321$
- $\sqrt{\Delta} = 4\sqrt{1321}$

Therefore:
$$AC^2 = \frac{76 + 4\sqrt{1321}}{2} = 38 + 2\sqrt{1321}.$$

**Verification:** With $b^2 = 38 + 2\sqrt{1321}$, I confirm $p^2 - 76p - 3840 =
0$:
$$p^2 = (38)^2 + 2 \cdot 38 \cdot 2\sqrt{1321} + 4 \cdot 1321 = 1444 + 152\sqrt{
1321} + 5284 = 6728 + 152\sqrt{1321}$$
$$76p = 2888 + 152\sqrt{1321}$$
$$p^2 - 76p - 3840 = 6728 - 2888 - 3840 = 0. \checkmark$$

Numerically, $b^2 \approx 110.69$, $b \approx 10.52$, and the triangle with side
s $10, 6, 10.52$ satisfies the triangle inequality. A coordinate check confirms
the circumcenter lies on line $CL$ (slopes of $CL$ and $CO$ agree to 5 decimal p
laces).

**Answer:** For $a = 10$ and $c = 6$,

$$AC^2 = 38 + 2\sqrt{1321}.$$
