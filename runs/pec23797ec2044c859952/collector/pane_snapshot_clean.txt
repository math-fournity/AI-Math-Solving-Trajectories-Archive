## Solution

**Notation.** The problem uses the same symbol $a$ for both the radius and the o
pening angle of the sector. I keep this notation: the sector is
$$\Omega = \{(\rho,\phi): 0<\rho<a,\;0<\phi<a\},$$
with boundary data
$$u\big|_{\rho=a}=\tfrac{u_0}{a}\phi,\qquad u\big|_{\phi=0}=0,\qquad u\big|_{\ph
i=a}=u_0,$$
and we require $u$ to be harmonic in $\Omega$ and regular (bounded) at the verte
x $\rho=0$.

---

**Ansatz.** Recall that the angular coordinate $\phi$ is harmonic in any sector
that does not encircle the origin:
$$\Delta\phi=\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\!\left(\rho\frac{\parti
al\phi}{\partial\rho}\right)+\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial\
phi^{2}}=0+0=0,$$
since $\phi$ depends on neither $\rho$ nor itself quadratically. Hence the funct
ion
$$\boxed{\;u(\rho,\phi)=\frac{u_0}{a}\,\phi\;}$$
is harmonic in $\Omega$.

---

**Verification of the boundary conditions.**

1. *Radial side $\phi=0$:*
$$u(\rho,0)=\frac{u_0}{a}\cdot 0=0.\;\checkmark$$

2. *Radial side $\phi=a$:*
$$u(\rho,a)=\frac{u_0}{a}\cdot a=u_0.\;\checkmark$$

3. *Circular arc $\rho=a$:*
$$u(a,\phi)=\frac{u_0}{a}\,\phi.\;\checkmark$$

4. *Regularity at the vertex $\rho=0$:* $u=\frac{u_0}{a}\phi$ is independent of
$\rho$ and bounded ($|\phi|\le a$), so it is regular at $\rho=0$.

All three prescribed boundary values are matched, and the corner compatibility c
onditions are satisfied as well:
$$(\rho=a,\phi=0):\;\tfrac{u_0}{a}\cdot 0=0=u_0\big|_{\phi=0},\qquad (\rho=a,\ph
i=a):\;\tfrac{u_0}{a}\cdot a=u_0.$$

---

**Uniqueness.** The difference $w=u-\frac{u_0}{a}\phi$ of any two solutions is h
armonic in $\Omega$, vanishes on the entire boundary $\partial\Omega$ (both radi
al sides and the arc), and is regular at the vertex. By the maximum principle ap
plied to the bounded domain $\Omega_\varepsilon=\{\varepsilon<\rho<a,\,0<\phi<a\
}$ and letting $\varepsilon\to0$ (using regularity at the vertex to control the
inner arc), $w\equiv 0$. Hence the solution is unique.

---

**Answer.**

$$\boxed{\,u(\rho,\phi)=\dfrac{u_0}{a}\,\phi\,}$$

The key observation is that, because the opening angle of the sector equals the
radius $a$, the linear-in-$\phi$ harmonic function $\frac{u_0}{a}\phi$ simultane
ously satisfies the inhomogeneous data on the arc ($\frac{u_0}{a}\phi$), the zer
o data on $\phi=0$, and the constant data $u_0$ on $\phi=a$. No Fourier series c
orrection is needed.
