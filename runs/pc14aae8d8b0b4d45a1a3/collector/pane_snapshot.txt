**Claim.** Every monomorphism in the category of finite-dimensional Lie algebras
 over a field is injective (and conversely, so monomorphisms are exactly the inj
ective homomorphisms).

**Proof.**

Let $\mathsf{Lie}_{\mathrm{fd}}(k)$ denote the category of finite-dimensional Li
e algebras over a field $k$, with morphisms the Lie algebra homomorphisms (i.e.
$k$-linear maps preserving the bracket).

Let $f \colon \mathfrak g \to \mathfrak h$ be a morphism in this category.

**(1) Injective $\Rightarrow$ monomorphism.**
This holds in any concrete category whose morphisms are set-functions with funct
ion-composition: if $f$ is injective as a function and $f \circ u = f \circ v$,
then for every $x$, $f(u(x)) = f(v(x))$, so $u(x)=v(x)$ by injectivity, hence $u
=v$. Thus every injective homomorphism is a monomorphism.

**(2) Monomorphism $\Rightarrow$ injective (the nontrivial direction).**
Suppose $f$ is **not** injective, i.e. $\ker f \neq 0$. Pick a nonzero element $
x \in \ker f$.

Consider the one-dimensional abelian Lie algebra $\mathfrak a = k\cdot e$ with b
racket $[e,e]=0$; it is finite-dimensional. We use it as a test object.

*Key observation.* Every $k$-linear map $\varphi \colon \mathfrak a \to \mathfra
k g$ is automatically a Lie algebra homomorphism. Indeed, for $a = \alpha e$, $b
 = \beta e \in \mathfrak a$,
$$\varphi([a,b]) = \varphi(\alpha\beta[e,e]) = \varphi(0) = 0,$$
while
$$[\varphi(a),\varphi(b)] = [\alpha\varphi(e),\,\beta\varphi(e)] = \alpha\beta[\
varphi(e),\varphi(e)] = 0,$$
by antisymmetry of the bracket. Hence $\varphi([a,b]) = [\varphi(a),\varphi(b)]$
 for all $a,b$.

Define two linear (hence Lie algebra) homomorphisms $u, v \colon \mathfrak a \to
 \mathfrak g$ by
$$u(e) = x, \qquad v(e) = 0.$$
Since $x \neq 0$, we have $u \neq v$. But
$$(f \circ u)(e) = f(x) = 0 \quad (\text{since } x \in \ker f),$$
$$(f \circ v)(e) = f(0) = 0.$$
As $\mathfrak a$ is generated by $e$, this gives $f \circ u = f \circ v$.

We have produced two distinct morphisms $u \neq v$ with $f \circ u = f \circ v$.
 Therefore $f$ is **not** a monomorphism.

By contraposition, every monomorphism is injective.

**Conclusion.** Combining (1) and (2), the monomorphisms in $\mathsf{Lie}_{\math
rm{fd}}(k)$ are precisely the injective Lie algebra homomorphisms. In particular
, **yes**: every monomorphism in the category of finite-dimensional Lie algebras
 over a field is injective.
