## Proof

alue, or proof sketch. Proceeding.

**Goal.** Evaluate
$$I=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(5x)}{1+\bigl(x-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\bigr)^{2}}\,
dx.$$

**Step 1. Shift the variable.** Let $u=x-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so $x=u+\dfrac{\pi}{2}
$ and $dx=du$. The limits are unchanged:
$$I=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin\!\bigl(5u+\tfrac{5\pi}{2}\bigr)}{1+u^{2}}\
,du.$$

Since $\dfrac{5\pi}{2}=2\pi+\dfrac{\pi}{2}$,
$$\sin\!\Bigl(5u+\frac{5\pi}{2}\Bigr)=\sin\!\Bigl(5u+\frac{\pi}{2}\Bigr)=\cos(5u
).$$

Hence
$$I=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos(5u)}{1+u^{2}}\,du.$$

**Step 2. Reduce to a complex integral.** Consider
$$J=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{i5u}}{1+u^{2}}\,du.$$
Taking real parts gives $I=\operatorname{Re}J$ (the imaginary part, $\int \frac{
\sin(5u)}{1+u^2}du$, vanishes by odd symmetry, consistent with $J$ being real).

**Step 3. Apply the residue theorem.** The integrand
$$f(z)=\frac{e^{i5z}}{1+z^{2}}=\frac{e^{i5z}}{(z-i)(z+i)}$$
has simple poles at $z=\pm i$. Since $5>0$, we close the contour in the upper ha
lf-plane (where $e^{i5z}$ decays because $\operatorname{Im}z>0$ gives $|e^{i5z}|
=e^{-5\operatorname{Im}z}\to 0$). Only the pole $z=i$ lies inside.

The residue at $z=i$:
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=i}f(z)=\lim_{z\to i}(z-i)\frac{e^{i5z}}{(z-i)(z+i)}=\fra
c{e^{i5i}}{2i}=\frac{e^{-5}}{2i}.$$

By Jordan's lemma the arc contribution vanishes, so
$$J=2\pi i\cdot\frac{e^{-5}}{2i}=\pi e^{-5}.$$

**Step 4. Conclude.** Since $J=\pi e^{-5}$ is real,
$$I=\operatorname{Re}J=\pi e^{-5}.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(5x)}{1+\bigl(x-\tfrac{\p
i}{2}\bigr)^{2}}\,dx=\pi e^{-5}}.$$
