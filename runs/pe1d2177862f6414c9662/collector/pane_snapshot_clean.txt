**Answer: Yes, the regular points of X̃ are dense in X̃.**

**Step 1: X̃ is constructible.**

X = V(I) is an algebraic (hence constructible) subset of ℂⁿ. The projection π: ℂ
ⁿ → ℂⁿ⁻¹ is a regular (polynomial) map. By Chevalley's theorem, the image of a c
onstructible set under a regular map is constructible. So X̃ = π(X) is a construc
tible subset of ℂⁿ⁻¹.

**Step 2: Setup with the Zariski closure.**

Let W = X̃̄ be the Zariski closure of X̃ in ℂⁿ⁻¹; W is an algebraic set. Decompose
W into irreducible components: W = W₁ ∪ ⋯ ∪ Wₖ.

**Step 3: X̃ is dense in each irreducible component of W.**

For each i, X̃ ∩ Wᵢ is constructible in Wᵢ. Its closure in Wᵢ equals X̃̄ ∩ Wᵢ = W ∩
 Wᵢ = Wᵢ (since Wᵢ ⊂ W). Hence X̃ ∩ Wᵢ is dense in Wᵢ.

**Step 4: X̃ contains a Zariski-open dense subset of each Wᵢ.**

A constructible dense subset of an irreducible variety contains a non-empty Zari
ski-open subset. (Write it as a finite union of locally closed sets; at least on

le space is open.) So there exists Oᵢ, Zariski-open and dense in Wᵢ, with Oᵢ ⊂ X̃
 ∩ Wᵢ.

**Step 5: The smooth locus.**

For each irreducible Wᵢ, the smooth locus Wᵢˢᵐ is a non-empty Zariski-open dense
 subset of Wᵢ (classical fact over ℂ).

**Step 6: Identifying manifold points of X̃.**

Define:
$$R = \bigcup_{i}\left(O_i \cap W_i^{\mathrm{sm}} \setminus \bigcup_{j \neq i} W
_j\right)$$

**Claim:** Every point of R is a manifold point of X̃.

*Proof.* Let p ∈ R. Then p ∈ Wᵢ for some i, p ∉ Wⱼ for j ≠ i, p is a smooth poin
t of Wᵢ, and p ∈ Oᵢ ⊂ X̃.

Since p ∉ Wⱼ for j ≠ i and each Wⱼ is Euclidean-closed, there is a Euclidean nei
ghborhood V of p with V ∩ Wⱼ = ∅ for all j ≠ i. Thus V ∩ W = V ∩ Wᵢ.

Since Oᵢ is Zariski-open (hence Euclidean-open) in Wᵢ and p ∈ Oᵢ ⊂ X̃, there is a
 Euclidean neighborhood V' ⊂ V of p with V' ∩ Wᵢ ⊂ Oᵢ ⊂ X̃. Then:

- V' ∩ X̃ ⊃ V' ∩ Wᵢ = V' ∩ W (since V' ∩ Wᵢ ⊂ X̃).
- V' ∩ X̃ ⊂ V' ∩ W = V' ∩ Wᵢ (since X̃ ⊂ W).

So V' ∩ X̃ = V' ∩ Wᵢ, which is a complex-analytic submanifold of V' (since Wᵢ is
smooth at p). ∎

**Step 7: R is dense in X̃.**

For each i, Oᵢ ∩ Wᵢˢᵐ is the intersection of two non-empty Zariski-open subsets
of irreducible Wᵢ, hence Zariski-open and dense in Wᵢ. The set ⋃_{j≠i}(Wⱼ ∩ Wᵢ)
is a proper Zariski-closed subset of Wᵢ (since Wᵢ is irreducible and not contain
ed in any Wⱼ). So each summand Oᵢ ∩ Wᵢˢᵐ \ ⋃_{j≠i} Wⱼ is Zariski-open and dense
in Wᵢ.

Therefore R is Zariski-dense in W = ⋃ Wᵢ. Over ℂ, Zariski-density implies Euclid
ean-density (proper Zariski-closed subsets have empty Euclidean interior). Since
 X̃ ⊂ W and R ⊂ X̃, R is Euclidean-dense in X̃.

**Conclusion:** The manifold points of X̃ contain the dense set R, so the regular
 points of X̃ — where X̃ behaves like a complex-analytic manifold — are dense in X̃
