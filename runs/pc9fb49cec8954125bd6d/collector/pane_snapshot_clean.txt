## Proof

**Claim:** Every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ of $\mathbb{Z}[X,Y]$ contains a pr
ime number.

**Setup.** Let $\mathfrak{m}$ be a maximal ideal of $\mathbb{Z}[X,Y]$ and set $K
 = \mathbb{Z}[X,Y]/\mathfrak{m}$, which is a field. Let $\bar{X}, \bar{Y}$ denot
e the images of $X, Y$ in $K$, so $K = \mathbb{Z}[\bar{X}, \bar{Y}]$.

The contraction $\mathfrak{m} \cap \mathbb{Z}$ is a prime ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$
(being the contraction of a prime ideal), so it is either $(0)$ or $(p)$ for som
e prime $p$. If $\mathfrak{m} \cap \mathbb{Z} = (p)$, then $p \in \mathfrak{m}$
and we are done. We show that $\mathfrak{m} \cap \mathbb{Z} = (0)$ is impossible
.

**Assume for contradiction that $\mathfrak{m} \cap \mathbb{Z} = (0)$.** Then the
 natural map $\mathbb{Z} \hookrightarrow K$ is injective, so $K$ has characteris
tic $0$ and contains $\mathbb{Z}$, hence (being a field) contains $\mathbb{Q} =
\operatorname{Frac}(\mathbb{Z})$.

**Step 1: $K$ is a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ (Zariski's Lemma).** Since $
\mathbb{Q} \subseteq K$ and $K = \mathbb{Z}[\bar{X}, \bar{Y}]$, we have $K = \ma
thbb{Q}[\bar{X}, \bar{Y}]$. Thus $K$ is a field that is finitely generated as a
$\mathbb{Q}$-algebra. By Zariski's Lemma, $K$ is a finite algebraic extension of
 $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Step 2: Each generator is integral over $\mathbb{Z}[1/N]$ for some $N$.** Sinc
e $\bar{X}$ and $\bar{Y}$ are algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$, let $f(T) = T^n + c_{
n-1}T^{n-1} + \cdots + c_0 \in \mathbb{Q}[T]$ and $g(T) = T^m + d_{m-1}T^{m-1} +
 \cdots + d_0 \in \mathbb{Q}[T]$ be their minimal polynomials over $\mathbb{Q}$.
 Let $N_X$ and $N_Y$ be the least common multiples of the denominators of $\{c_0
, \ldots, c_{n-1}\}$ and $\{d_0, \ldots, d_{m-1}\}$ respectively. Set $N = N_X \
cdot N_Y \geq 1$.

Then $f(T) \in \mathbb{Z}[1/N_X][T]$ is monic, so $\bar{X}$ is integral over $\m
athbb{Z}[1/N_X]$, hence over $\mathbb{Z}[1/N]$. Similarly $\bar{Y}$ is integral
over $\mathbb{Z}[1/N]$.

**Step 3: $K$ is a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}[1/N]$-module (hence integral o
ver $\mathbb{Z}[1/N]$).** Since $K$ is a field containing $\mathbb{Q}$, we have
$1/N \in K$, so:
$$K = \mathbb{Z}[\bar{X}, \bar{Y}] = \mathbb{Z}[\bar{X}, \bar{Y}][1/N] = \mathbb
{Z}[1/N][\bar{X}, \bar{Y}].$$
Since $\bar{X}$ and $\bar{Y}$ are integral over $\mathbb{Z}[1/N]$, the ring $\ma
thbb{Z}[1/N][\bar{X}, \bar{Y}]$ is finitely generated as a $\mathbb{Z}[1/N]$-mod
ule (a standard result: an algebra generated by finitely many integral elements
is a finite module). Therefore $K$ is integral over $\mathbb{Z}[1/N]$.

**Step 4: $\mathbb{Z}[1/N]$ must be a field — contradiction.** Since $K$ is a fi
eld and $K$ is integral over $R = \mathbb{Z}[1/N]$, the ring $R$ must itself be
a field. Indeed, for any nonzero $r \in R$, its inverse $1/r$ lies in $K$ (since
 $K$ is a field) and is integral over $R$, so $(1/r)^k + a_{k-1}(1/r)^{k-1} + \c
dots + a_0 = 0$ for some $a_i \in R$. Multiplying through by $r^k$ gives $1 = -r
(a_{k-1} + a_{k-2}r + \cdots + a_0 r^{k-1})$, exhibiting an inverse of $r$ in $R
$.

But $\mathbb{Z}[1/N]$ is **not** a field for any $N \geq 1$:
- If $N = 1$, then $\mathbb{Z}[1/N] = \mathbb{Z}$, which is not a field.
- If $N > 1$, choose any prime $p \nmid N$ (such a prime exists since there are
infinitely many primes). Then $1/p \notin \mathbb{Z}[1/N]$, since every element
of $\mathbb{Z}[1/N]$ is of the form $a/N^k$ ($a \in \mathbb{Z}$, $k \geq 0$), wh
ose reduced denominator has only prime factors dividing $N$. So $\mathbb{Z}[1/N]
$ is not a field.

This is a contradiction. Therefore $\mathfrak{m} \cap \mathbb{Z} \neq (0)$.

**Conclusion.** Since $\mathfrak{m} \cap \mathbb{Z}$ is a nonzero prime ideal of
 $\mathbb{Z}$, it equals $(p)$ for some prime number $p$. Hence $p \in \mathfrak
{m}$: **every maximal ideal of $\mathbb{Z}[X,Y]$ contains a prime number.** $\bl
acksquare$
