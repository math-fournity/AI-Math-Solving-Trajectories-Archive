## Proof

**The answer is Yes.** Such an algorithm exists. I describe it constructively.

### Key Idea

Every finite subgroup $G \leq GL_n(\mathbb{Q})$ preserves some full-rank lattice
 $\Lambda \subset \mathbb{Q}^n$. If $\Lambda = T(\mathbb{Z}^n)$ for some $T \in
GL_n(\mathbb{Q})$, then $G$-invariance of $\Lambda$ forces $T^{-1}gT \in GL_n(\m
athbb{Z})$ for every $g \in G$. The lattice is constructed by "averaging" $\math
bb{Z}^n$ over the group, and every step is algorithmic.

### Step 1: Enumerate the group

Given $A_1, \ldots, A_k \in GL_n(\mathbb{Q})$ generating a finite group $G$, enu
merate all elements:

- Initialize $S = \{I_n\}$.
- Repeatedly: for each $s \in S$ and each generator $A_i$, compute $sA_i$. If th
e result is not already in $S$ (compared entry-wise as exact rationals), add it.
- Continue until no new elements appear.

Since $G$ is finite, this terminates with $S = G$. All arithmetic is over $\math
bb{Q}$, so every step is exact and algorithmic.

### Step 2: Construct a $G$-invariant lattice

Define
$$\Lambda = \sum_{g \in G} g(\mathbb{Z}^n) = \left\{ \sum_{g \in G} g\,\mathbf{z
}_g : \mathbf{z}_g \in \mathbb{Z}^n \right\}.$$

**Claim 1:** $\Lambda$ is a full-rank lattice in $\mathbb{Q}^n$.

Each $g(\mathbb{Z}^n)$ is a rank-$n$ lattice (the $\mathbb{Z}$-span of the colum
ns of $g$, which are $n$ linearly independent rational vectors). In particular $
\mathbb{Z}^n = I_n(\mathbb{Z}^n) \subseteq \Lambda$ (since $I_n \in G$), so $\La
mbda$ contains $\mathbb{Z}^n$ and has full rank. Being a finitely generated $\ma
thbb{Z}$-submodule of $\mathbb{Q}^n$ of rank $n$, it is a full-rank lattice.

**Claim 2:** $\Lambda$ is $G$-invariant, i.e., $h(\Lambda) = \Lambda$ for every
$h \in G$.

$$h(\Lambda) = \sum_{g \in G} hg(\mathbb{Z}^n).$$

As $g$ ranges over $G$, the product $hg$ ranges over all of $G$ (left multiplica
tion by $h$ is a bijection on $G$). Therefore
$$h(\Lambda) = \sum_{g' \in G} g'(\mathbb{Z}^n) = \Lambda. \quad \checkmark$$

### Step 3: Compute $\Lambda$ algorithmically via Hermite Normal Form

The lattice $\Lambda$ is the $\mathbb{Z}$-column-span of the $n \times (|G|\cdot
 n)$ matrix
$$M = [\,g_1 \mid g_2 \mid \cdots \mid g_{|G|}\,],$$
formed by concatenating all group elements as column blocks.

**Clearing denominators:** Let $d$ be a common denominator of all entries of all
 $g \in G$ (e.g., the LCM of all denominators, computable since there are finite
ly many rational entries). Then $dM$ is an integer matrix, and $d\Lambda$ is the
 $\mathbb{Z}$-column-span of $dM$, which is a sublattice of $\mathbb{Z}^n$.

**HNF computation:** Compute the Hermite Normal Form of the integer matrix $dM$.
 This is a standard, terminating algorithm (using integer row/column operations)
. The result yields an $n \times n$ integer matrix $W$ whose columns form a $\ma
thbb{Z}$-basis of $d\Lambda$, so that
$$d\Lambda = W(\mathbb{Z}^n).$$

Set $T = \frac{1}{d}\,W$. Then $T \in GL_n(\mathbb{Q})$ (since $W$ is nonsingula
r, being a basis matrix of a full-rank lattice), and
$$\Lambda = T(\mathbb{Z}^n).$$

### Step 4: Verify integrality

For each generator $A_i \in G$, using $G$-invariance (Claim 2):
$$A_i(\Lambda) = \Lambda \implies A_i\, T(\mathbb{Z}^n) = T(\mathbb{Z}^n) \impli
es T^{-1}A_i\, T(\mathbb{Z}^n) = \mathbb{Z}^n.$$

This means $T^{-1}A_i T$ maps $\mathbb{Z}^n$ into $\mathbb{Z}^n$, so $T^{-1}A_i
T \in M_n(\mathbb{Z})$. Since $A_i$ is invertible, $T^{-1}A_i T$ is invertible,
hence $T^{-1}A_i T \in GL_n(\mathbb{Z})$.

### Summary of the algorithm

1. **Enumerate** $G = \langle A_1, \ldots, A_k \rangle$ by closure under multipl
ication (terminates since $G$ is finite).
2. **Clear denominators:** compute $d = \operatorname{lcm}$ of all denominators
of entries of all $g \in G$.
3. **Form** the $n \times (|G|n)$ integer matrix $dM$ by concatenating all $d\cd
ot g$.
4. **Compute HNF** of $dM$ to obtain basis matrix $W \in M_n(\mathbb{Z})$.
5. **Output** $T = \frac{1}{d} W$.

Every step uses only exact rational/integer arithmetic and standard algorithms (
group enumeration, LCM, Hermite Normal Form), all of which terminate. The output
 $T \in GL_n(\mathbb{Q})$ satisfies $T^{-1}A_iT \in GL_n(\mathbb{Z})$ for all $i
 = 1, \ldots, k$.
