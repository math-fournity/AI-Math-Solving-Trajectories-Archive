## Solution

We evaluate
$$I(a) = \int_{0}^{1} \frac{x^{a-1}}{(1-x)^{a+1}} \exp\!\left(\frac{x}{x-1}\righ
t) dx.$$

**Substitution.** Set
$$t = \frac{x}{1-x}, \qquad\text{so that}\qquad x = \frac{t}{1+t},\quad 1-x = \f
rac{1}{1+t},\quad dx = \frac{dt}{(1+t)^2}.$$
As $x: 0 \to 1^{-}$, we have $t: 0 \to +\infty$.

**Transform each factor.**

- Exponent:
$$\frac{x}{x-1} = \frac{x}{-(1-x)} = -\frac{x}{1-x} = -t, \qquad\text{so}\quad \
exp\!\left(\frac{x}{x-1}\right) = e^{-t}.$$

- Power of $x$:
$$x^{a-1} = \left(\frac{t}{1+t}\right)^{a-1} = \frac{t^{a-1}}{(1+t)^{a-1}}.$$

- Power of $(1-x)$:
$$(1-x)^{a+1} = \frac{1}{(1+t)^{a+1}}.$$

- Ratio:
$$\frac{x^{a-1}}{(1-x)^{a+1}} = \frac{t^{a-1}}{(1+t)^{a-1}} \cdot (1+t)^{a+1} =
t^{a-1}(1+t)^{2}.$$

**Assemble the integral.** Multiplying by $dx = \frac{dt}{(1+t)^2}$ and $e^{-t}$
:
$$I(a) = \int_{0}^{\infty} t^{a-1}(1+t)^{2}\, e^{-t}\, \frac{dt}{(1+t)^{2}} = \i
nt_{0}^{\infty} t^{a-1}\, e^{-t}\, dt.$$

The factors $(1+t)^2$ and $(1+t)^{-2}$ cancel exactly, leaving the defining inte
gral of the Gamma function.

**Convergence.** The substitution shows convergence is equivalent to that of $\i
nt_0^\infty t^{a-1}e^{-t}\,dt$, which holds for $a > 0$. (Directly: near $x=0$ w
e need $a>0$ for $x^{a-1}$ integrability; near $x=1^{-}$ the factor $e^{-x/(1-x)
}$ vanishes faster than any power of $(1-x)$, so the singularity from $(1-x)^{-(
a+1)}$ is always suppressed.)

**Result.**

$$\boxed{I(a) = \int_{0}^{1} \frac{x^{a-1}}{(1-x)^{a+1}} \exp\!\left(\frac{x}{x-
1}\right) dx = \Gamma(a), \qquad a > 0.}$$
