## Proof

**Step 1: Identify the intersection points.**

The circle $C_1 = \{|z-1|=1\}$ has center $1$ and radius $1$, so its topmost and
 bottommost points are $1+i$ and $1-i$. The line $L_+ = \{\operatorname{Im}(z)=1
\}$ is tangent to $C_1$ at $1+i$, and $L_- = \{\operatorname{Im}(z)=-1\}$ is tan
gent to $C_1$ at $1-i$.

Similarly, $C_2 = \{|z+1|=1\}$ has center $-1$ and radius $1$; $L_+$ is tangent
to $C_2$ at $-1+i$, and $L_-$ is tangent to $C_2$ at $-1-i$.

Hence the tangency conditions require
$$f(1+i) = -1+i, \qquad f(1-i) = -1-i,$$
together with the fixed-point condition
$$f(0) = 0.$$

**Step 2: Determine the Möbius transformation.**

Write $f(z) = \frac{az+b}{cz+d}$. The condition $f(0)=0$ gives $b/d = 0$, so $b=
0$. Thus $f(z) = \frac{az}{cz+d}$. By the conjugate symmetry of the two remainin
g conditions, we may take $a,c,d \in \mathbb{R}$; then $f(\bar z) = \overline{f(
z)}$ automatically, so it suffices to enforce $f(1+i) = -1+i$. Normalize $d=1$:
$$f(z) = \frac{az}{cz+1}, \qquad a,c \in \mathbb{R}.$$

The equation $f(1+i) = -1+i$ reads
$$\frac{a(1+i)}{c(1+i)+1} = -1+i.$$
The denominator is $(c+1)+ci$. Multiplying both sides by the denominator:
$$a(1+i) = (-1+i)\bigl((c+1)+ci\bigr).$$
Expanding the right side:
$$(-1+i)(c+1) + (-1+i)ci = -(c+1)+i(c+1) - ci + i^2 c = -(2c+1) + i.$$
Equating real and imaginary parts of $a(1+i) = a + ai$ with $-(2c+1)+i$:
$$a = -(2c+1), \qquad a = 1.$$
So $a=1$ and $1 = -(2c+1)$, giving $c = -1$. Therefore
$$\boxed{f(z) = \frac{z}{1-z}.}$$

**Verification.**
- $f(0) = 0$. ✓
- $f(1+i) = \frac{1+i}{1-(1+i)} = \frac{1+i}{-i} = (1+i)\cdot i = i+i^2 = -1+i$.
 ✓
- $f(1-i) = \frac{1-i}{i} = (1-i)(-i) = -i+i^2 = -1-i$. ✓

**Step 3: Verify the circle-interior to circle-exterior mapping.**

Parametrize $C_1$ by $z = 1+e^{i\theta}$. Then
$$f(1+e^{i\theta}) = \frac{1+e^{i\theta}}{1-(1+e^{i\theta})} = \frac{1+e^{i\thet
a}}{-e^{i\theta}} = -1 - e^{-i\theta}.$$
Since $\bigl|(-1-e^{-i\theta})-(-1)\bigr| = |e^{-i\theta}| = 1$, the image of $C
_1$ is exactly $C_2 = \{|z+1|=1\}$.

To determine which side maps to which, test the center of $C_1$, namely $z=1$ (a
n interior point): $f(1) = \frac{1}{0} = \infty$, which lies in the exterior of
$C_2$. Testing another interior point $z=\tfrac12$: $f(\tfrac12) = \frac{1/2}{1/
2} = 1$, and $|1-(-1)| = 2 > 1$, confirming it is exterior to $C_2$. Hence the i
nterior of $C_1$ maps to the exterior of $C_2$. ✓

**Step 4: Image of the line $\operatorname{Re}(z) = \tfrac12$.**

Write $z = \tfrac12 + iy$ with $y \in \mathbb{R}$. Then
$$f(z) = \frac{\tfrac12 + iy}{1 - (\tfrac12 + iy)} = \frac{\tfrac12 + iy}{\tfrac
12 - iy}.$$
Since $\bigl|\tfrac12+iy\bigr| = \bigl|\tfrac12-iy\bigr|$, we get $|f(z)| = 1$ f
or every $y$. Thus the image lies on the unit circle $|w|=1$.

Conversely, as $y$ ranges over $\mathbb{R}$, the argument of $f(z)$ is
$$\arg f(z) = 2\arg\!\bigl(\tfrac12+iy\bigr) = 2\arctan(2y) \in (-\pi,\pi),$$
which sweeps the entire unit circle. The line $\operatorname{Re}(z)=\tfrac12$ pa
sses through the point at infinity in the extended plane, and $f(\infty) = \lim_
{z\to\infty}\frac{z}{1-z} = -1$, which is precisely the point on $|w|=1$ corresp
onding to $y\to\pm\infty$. Hence the point $-1$ is included, and the image is th
e **complete** unit circle.

**Conclusion.** The required Möbius transformation is
$$f(z) = \frac{z}{1-z},$$
and the image of the line $\{z : \operatorname{Re}(z) = \tfrac12\}$ under this t
ransformation is the unit circle
$$\boxed{\{w : |w| = 1\}}.$$
