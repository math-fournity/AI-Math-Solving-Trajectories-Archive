## Proof

Let $n = 7^7$ (odd). We evaluate $S_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sec^2\!\left(\frac{k\pi
}{n}\right)$.

**Step 1: Convert $\sec^2$ to $\csc^2$.**

Since $\sec\theta = \csc\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta\right)$,
$$\sec^2\!\left(\frac{k\pi}{n}\right) = \csc^2\!\left(\frac{(n-2k)\pi}{2n}\right
).$$

As $k$ ranges over $0,1,\ldots,n-1$, the quantity $n-2k$ ranges over $\{n,\, n{-
}2,\, \ldots,\, 1,\, {-}1,\, \ldots,\, {-(n{-}2)}\}$ — all odd integers from $-(
n{-}2)$ to $n$ (since $n$ is odd, every $n-2k$ is odd, and $n-2k=0$ never occurs
). Using $\csc^2(x)=\csc^2(-x)$, the multiset of absolute values $|n-2k|$ is:
- $\{1,3,5,\ldots,n-2\}$, each appearing **twice** (once from $k<(n/2)$, once fr
om $k>(n/2)$),
- $\{n\}$, appearing **once** (from $k=0$).

Therefore:
$$S_n = 2\!\!\sum_{\substack{j=1,3,5,\ldots\\ j\le n-2}}\!\csc^2\!\left(\frac{j\
pi}{2n}\right) + \csc^2\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{2n}\right) = 2A + \csc^2\!\left(\tfra
c{\pi}{2}\right) = 2A + 1, \tag{1}$$
where $A = \sum_{j\text{ odd},\,1\le j\le n-2}\csc^2\!\left(\frac{j\pi}{2n}\righ
t)$.

**Step 2: Use the classical identity.**

We use the well-known result (derivable from the partial-fraction expansion of $
\csc^2$, or from the product formula for $\sin(n\theta)/\sin\theta$):
$$\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\csc^2\!\left(\frac{k\pi}{N}\right) = \frac{N^2-1}{3}. \tag{2}
$$

*(Proof sketch of (2): The function $f(\theta)=\sin(N\theta)/\sin\theta$ vanishe
s at $\theta=k\pi/N$, $k=1,\ldots,N{-}1$, and equals $N$ at $\theta=0$. Writing
$f(\theta)=N\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}\bigl(1-\sin^2\theta/\sin^2(k\pi/N)\bigr)$ and comp
aring the $\theta^2$ coefficient with the Taylor expansion $f(\theta)=N\bigl(1-(
N^2-1)\theta^2/6+\cdots\bigr)$ yields (2).)*

**Step 3: Split by parity with $N=2n$.**

Setting $N=2n$ in (2):
$$\sum_{k=1}^{2n-1}\csc^2\!\left(\frac{k\pi}{2n}\right) = \frac{4n^2-1}{3}. \tag
{3}$$

**Even-indexed terms** ($k=2m$, $m=1,\ldots,n{-}1$):
$$\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\csc^2\!\left(\frac{2m\pi}{2n}\right)=\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\csc^2\!
\left(\frac{m\pi}{n}\right)=\frac{n^2-1}{3}. \tag{4}$$

**Odd-indexed terms** (by subtraction from (3)):
$$\sum_{\substack{k=1,3,5,\ldots\\k\le 2n-1}}\csc^2\!\left(\frac{k\pi}{2n}\right
)=\frac{4n^2-1}{3}-\frac{n^2-1}{3}=n^2. \tag{5}$$

**Step 4: Extract $A$ from the odd-indexed sum.**

The odd values $k=1,3,\ldots,2n{-}1$ (total $n$ values) split by the symmetry $\
csc^2\!\bigl(\frac{k\pi}{2n}\bigr)=\csc^2\!\bigl(\frac{(2n-k)\pi}{2n}\bigr)$ int
o three groups:
- $k=1,3,\ldots,n-2$: $(n-1)/2$ values (sum $=A$),
- $k=n$: $1$ value, contributing $\csc^2(\pi/2)=1$,
- $k=n+2,n+4,\ldots,2n-1$: $(n-1)/2$ values (sum $=A$, by symmetry).

So (5) gives $2A + 1 = n^2$, hence:
$$A = \frac{n^2-1}{2}. \tag{6}$$

**Step 5: Combine.**

From (1) and (6):
$$S_n = 2A + 1 = (n^2-1)+1 = n^2.$$

$$\sum_{k=0}^{7^7-1}\sec^2\!\left(\frac{k\pi}{7^7}\right) = (7^7)^2 = 7^{14} = \
