The statement is **TRUE**.

## Proof

Since $A$ and $B$ are positive definite real matrices, they are invertible, and
their Moore-Penrose inverses coincide with their ordinary inverses:
$$A^\dagger = A^{-1}, \qquad B^\dagger = B^{-1}.$$

We are given $0 \leq A \leq B$, meaning $A \succ 0$, $B \succ 0$, and $B - A \su
cceq 0$ (Loewner order).

**Step 1: Congruence by $B^{-1/2}$.**

Since $B \succ 0$, the matrix $B^{-1/2}$ exists and is symmetric. Congruence pre
serves the Loewner order, so from $A \leq B$ we get
$$B^{-1/2} A\, B^{-1/2} \;\leq\; B^{-1/2} B\, B^{-1/2} = I.$$

Set $C := B^{-1/2} A\, B^{-1/2}$. Since $A \succ 0$, we have $C \succ 0$, and fr
om the above,
$$0 \prec C \leq I.$$

**Step 2: Spectral argument for $C^{-1}$.**

Since $C \succ 0$, it is diagonalizable by an orthogonal matrix with all eigenva
lues $\lambda_i > 0$. The inequality $C \leq I$ gives $\lambda_i \leq 1$ for all
 $i$, and $C \succ 0$ gives $\lambda_i > 0$. Hence
$$0 < \lambda_i \leq 1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \lambda_i^{-1} \geq 1.$$

The eigenvalues of $C^{-1}$ are $\lambda_i^{-1} \geq 1$, so every eigenvalue of
$C^{-1} - I$ is $\geq 0$. Since $C^{-1} - I$ is symmetric, this means
$$C^{-1} \succeq I.$$

**Step 3: Translate back.**

We compute
$$C^{-1} = \bigl(B^{-1/2} A\, B^{-1/2}\bigr)^{-1} = B^{1/2} A^{-1} B^{1/2}.$$

Thus
$$B^{1/2} A^{-1} B^{1/2} \;\succeq\; I.$$

Congruence by $B^{-1/2}$ (which preserves order) yields
$$A^{-1} \;\succeq\; B^{-1/2}\, I\, B^{-1/2} = B^{-1}.$$

**Conclusion.**

We have shown
$$0 \;\prec\; B^{-1} \;\leq\; A^{-1},$$
that is,
$$0 \;\leq\; B^\dagger \;\leq\; A^\dagger.$$

Therefore the statement is **true**: for positive definite real matrices, $0 \le
q A \leq B$ implies $0 \leq B^\dagger \leq A^\dagger$. $\blacksquare$
