**Step 1: From ball means to spherical means.**

Define the ball average and spherical average:
$$A_r f(x) = \frac{1}{|B(0,r)|}\int_{B(0,r)} f(x+y)\,dy, \qquad S_r f(x) = \frac
{1}{|\partial B(0,r)|}\int_{\partial B(0,r)} f(x+y)\,dS(y).$$

Using polar coordinates, $|B(0,r)| = \frac{|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|}{n}r^n$ and
$$A_r f(x) = \frac{n}{r^n}\int_0^r s^{n-1} S_s f(x)\,ds.$$

The hypothesis gives $A_r f(x) = f(x)$ for all $x\in\mathbb{R}^n$ and all $0 < r
 < 1$. Since $f$ is bounded, $s \mapsto S_s f(x)$ is continuous (by dominated co
nvergence), so $r \mapsto A_r f(x)$ is differentiable for $r>0$. Differentiating
 $A_r f(x) = f(x)$:
$$0 = \frac{d}{dr}\left[\frac{n}{r^n}\int_0^r s^{n-1}S_s f(x)\,ds\right] = -\fra
c{n}{r}A_r f(x) + \frac{n}{r}S_r f(x) = \frac{n}{r}\bigl(S_r f(x) - f(x)\bigr).$
$

Hence **$S_r f(x) = f(x)$ for all $x$ and all $0 < r < 1$**.

**Step 2: $f$ is smooth (mollification argument).**

Let $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ be a **radial** mollifier: $\varphi(y
) = \psi(|y|)$, $\operatorname{supp}\varphi \subset B(0,\varepsilon)$ with $\var
epsilon < 1$, and $\int \varphi = 1$. Since $f$ is bounded, the convolution
$$(f * \varphi)(x) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} f(x-y)\,\varphi(y)\,dy$$
is well-defined and $C^\infty$ (differentiation falls on $\varphi$).

Using polar coordinates and the radiality of $\varphi$:
$$(f*\varphi)(x) = \int_0^\varepsilon \psi(r)\,r^{n-1}\,|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|\,S_r
f(x)\,dr = f(x)\int_0^\varepsilon \psi(r)\,r^{n-1}\,|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|\,dr = f(x
)\int \varphi(y)\,dy = f(x).$$

So $f * \varphi = f$. Since $f*\varphi$ is $C^\infty$, **$f$ is $C^\infty$**.

**Step 3: $f$ is harmonic.**

Since $f$ is smooth, expand in a Taylor series around $x$:
$$f(x+y) = f(x) + \nabla f(x)\cdot y + \frac{1}{2}y^T D^2f(x)\,y + o(|y|^2).$$

Averaging over $B(0,r)$: the linear term vanishes by symmetry, and $\frac{1}{|B(
0,r)|}\int_{B(0,r)} y_i y_j\,dy = \frac{r^2}{n+2}\delta_{ij}$. Thus
$$A_r f(x) = f(x) + \frac{r^2}{2(n+2)}\Delta f(x) + o(r^2).$$

The hypothesis $A_r f(x) = f(x)$ for all $r < 1$ forces $\Delta f(x) = 0$ for ev
ery $x$. Therefore **$f$ is harmonic on $\mathbb{R}^n$**.

**Step 4: Liouville's theorem — bounded harmonic implies constant.**

$f$ is harmonic on all of $\mathbb{R}^n$ and bounded: $|f| \le M$. By the gradie
nt estimate for harmonic functions (a consequence of the mean value property app
lied to the partial derivatives, which are themselves harmonic):
$$|\nabla f(x)| \le \frac{nM}{R} \quad \text{for every } R > 0,$$
obtained from $|\partial_i f(x)| \le \frac{n}{R}\sup_{B(x,R)}|f| \le \frac{nM}{R
}$. Letting $R \to \infty$ gives $\nabla f(x) = 0$ for every $x$.

Hence $f$ is constant. $\blacksquare$
