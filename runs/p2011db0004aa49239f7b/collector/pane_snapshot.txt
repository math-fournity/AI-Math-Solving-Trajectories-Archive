## Proof

**The answer is No.** For any function $f$ with $\lim_{\epsilon \to 0^+} f(\epsi
lon) = 0$, it is **not** always possible to find such a complete bipartite subgr
aph. We prove this by exhibiting, for every fixed $\epsilon > 0$ and every $\del
ta > 0$, a bipartite graph with $\geq (1-\epsilon) \cdot 2^{2n}$ edges that cont
ains no $K_{s,s}$ with $s = 2^{(1-\delta)n}$, for all sufficiently large $n$.

---

### Setup

Let $N = 2^n$. Consider a random bipartite graph $G$ on parts $A, B$ with $|A| =
 |B| = N$, where each of the $N^2$ possible edges is included independently with
 probability $1 - \epsilon/2$. Equivalently, the complement $\bar{G}$ is a rando
m bipartite graph with edge probability $\epsilon/2$.

A $K_{s,s}$ in $G$ corresponds to an **empty** $s \times s$ rectangle in $\bar{G
}$ (a pair $S \subseteq A, T \subseteq B$ with $|S| = |T| = s$ and no $\bar{G}$-
edges between them).

### Claim 1: $G$ has at least $(1-\epsilon)N^2$ edges with high probability

The edge count is $\operatorname{Bin}(N^2,\, 1-\epsilon/2)$ with mean $(1-\epsil
on/2)N^2 \geq (1-\epsilon)N^2$. By a Chernoff bound:

$$\Pr\!\bigl[\text{edges} < (1-\epsilon)N^2\bigr] \leq e^{-\Omega(N^2)} \to 0.$$

### Claim 2: $G$ contains no $K_{s,s}$ with $s = N^{1-\delta}$ with high probabi
lity

For any **fixed** pair $(S, T)$ with $|S| = |T| = s$, the probability that all $
s^2$ edges between $S$ and $T$ are present in $G$ is:

$$(1 - \epsilon/2)^{s^2} \leq e^{-(\epsilon/2)\, s^2}.$$

By the union bound over all $\binom{N}{s}^2$ choices of $(S, T)$:

$$\Pr[\exists\, K_{s,s}] \leq \binom{N}{s}^2 e^{-(\epsilon/2)\,s^2} \leq \left(\
frac{eN}{s}\right)^{2s} e^{-(\epsilon/2)\,s^2} = \exp\!\left(2s\!\left(1 + \ln\f
rac{N}{s}\right) - \frac{\epsilon}{2}\,s^2\right).$$

Substituting $s = N^{1-\delta}$, so $\ln(N/s) = \delta \ln N$:

- **Positive term:** $2s(1 + \delta \ln N) = 2N^{1-\delta}(1 + \delta \ln N) = O
\!\left(N^{1-\delta}\,\ln N\right).$
- **Negative term:** $\frac{\epsilon}{2}\,s^2 = \frac{\epsilon}{2}\,N^{2(1-\delt
a)}.$

Since $2(1-\delta) > 1 - \delta$ for any $\delta \in (0,1)$, we have $N^{2(1-\de
lta)} \gg N^{1-\delta}\ln N$ as $N \to \infty$. Therefore the exponent tends to
$-\infty$, giving:

$$\Pr[\exists\, K_{s,s}] \to 0 \quad \text{as } N \to \infty.$$

### Combining the claims

By the union bound:

$$\Pr\!\bigl[\text{edges} < (1-\epsilon)N^2 \;\text{OR}\; \exists\, K_{s,s}\bigr
] \to 0.$$

Therefore, for all sufficiently large $n$, there **exists** a bipartite graph $G
$ on $N \times N$ parts with $\geq (1-\epsilon)N^2$ edges that contains **no** $
K_{s,s}$ with $s = N^{1-\delta} = 2^{(1-\delta)n}$.

### Applying to the problem

Let $f$ be any function with $\lim_{\epsilon \to 0^+} f(\epsilon) = 0$.

- **If $f(\epsilon) = 0$ for some $\epsilon > 0$:** then $s = 2^n = N$, requirin
g a $K_{N,N}$ (complete graph). But a graph with $\geq (1-\epsilon)N^2 < N^2$ ed
ges is not complete, so the statement fails trivially.

- **If $f(\epsilon) > 0$ for all $\epsilon > 0$:** Fix any $\epsilon > 0$ and se
t $\delta = f(\epsilon) > 0$. By the construction above, for all sufficiently la
rge $n$, there exists a graph with $\geq (1-\epsilon) \cdot 2^{2n}$ edges and no
 $K_{s,s}$ with $s = 2^{(1-f(\epsilon))n}$.

In both cases, the statement fails. $\blacksquare$

### Remark on the true order of magnitude

In a random graph with density $1-\epsilon$, the threshold for $K_{s,s}$ is dete
rmined by $\binom{N}{s}^2 (1-\epsilon)^{s^2} \approx 1$, which gives $\epsilon s
 \approx 2\ln(N/s)$. Setting $s = c\ln N$ yields $c \approx 2/\epsilon$, so the
largest $K_{s,s}$ has $s = \Theta(\log N / \epsilon) = \Theta(n/\epsilon)$ — **p
olynomial** in $n$, which is exponentially smaller than $2^{(1-f(\epsilon))n}$ f
or any $f(\epsilon) < 1$. This confirms that the exponential-size complete bipar
tite subgraph cannot be guaranteed.
