**Proof.**

We work in the real projective plane $\mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{R}) = \mathrm{PG}(2,\
mathbb{R})$, the projective space of dimension $2$ over the field $\mathbb{R}$.
A *collineation* is a bijection of the point set that sends every projective lin
e onto a projective line. A *linear transformation* (in the projective sense) is
 a map induced by an invertible $\mathbb{R}$-linear automorphism of $\mathbb{R}^
3$, i.e., an element of $\mathrm{PGL}(3,\mathbb{R})$.

The key tool is the **Fundamental Theorem of Projective Geometry**: for a projec
tive space $\mathrm{PG}(n,F)$ with $n \geq 2$ over a field (or division ring) $F
$, every collineation is a *semilinear* transformation — that is, it is induced
by a map $T: F^{n+1} \to F^{n+1}$ of the form
$$T(v) = A\,\sigma(v),$$
where $A \in \mathrm{GL}(n+1,F)$ and $\sigma$ is a field automorphism of $F$ act
ing coordinate-wise. (See, e.g., Artin, *Geometric Algebra*, or any standard ref
erence on projective geometry.)

Since the real projective plane has dimension $n = 2 \geq 2$, the theorem applie
s: every collineation of $\mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{R})$ is semilinear.

It remains to determine the field automorphisms of $\mathbb{R}$.

**Claim.** The only field automorphism of $\mathbb{R}$ is the identity.

*Proof of claim.* Let $\sigma: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be a field automorphis
m. Since $\sigma(1) = 1$, it fixes every rational number. A real number $x$ is n
on-negative if and only if $x = y^2$ for some $y \in \mathbb{R}$. Applying $\sig
ma$,
$$\sigma(x) = \sigma(y^2) = \sigma(y)^2 \geq 0.$$
Thus $\sigma$ sends non-negative reals to non-negative reals, and consequently s
ends positive reals to positive reals. Hence $\sigma$ preserves the order: $a <
b \implies b - a > 0 \implies \sigma(b-a) > 0 \implies \sigma(a) < \sigma(b)$. B
eing an order-preserving field automorphism that fixes $\mathbb{Q}$, and since $
\mathbb{Q}$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}$, $\sigma$ is forced to be the identity: for
 any $x \in \mathbb{R}$, take rationals $p_n \nearrow x$ and $q_n \searrow x$; t
hen $p_n = \sigma(p_n) \leq \sigma(x) \leq \sigma(q_n) = q_n$, so $\sigma(x) = x
$ by the squeeze theorem. $\square$

Since $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{R}) = \{\mathrm{id}\}$, every semilinear transformat
ion of $\mathbb{R}^3$ is in fact a *linear* transformation. Combining with the F
undamental Theorem, every collineation of $\mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{R})$ is a projec
tive linear transformation, i.e., lies in $\mathrm{PGL}(3,\mathbb{R})$.

Therefore, no collineation of the real projective plane can exist that is not a
linear transformation.
