## Proof

We are given that $f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}$ is entire, $|f(z)|\le 100\ln|z|$ f
or $|z|\ge 2$, and $f(i)=2i$.

**Step 1: $f$ has sub-polynomial growth.**

For any $\varepsilon>0$, we have $\ln|z| = o(|z|^\varepsilon)$ as $|z|\to\infty$
. Hence for all sufficiently large $|z|$,
$$|f(z)|\le 100\ln|z|\le C_\varepsilon\,|z|^\varepsilon$$
for some constant $C_\varepsilon>0$. Thus $f(z)=o(|z|^\varepsilon)$ for every $\
varepsilon>0$.

**Step 2: Apply the generalized Liouville theorem.**

By the Cauchy estimate / generalized Liouville theorem: if an entire function sa
tisfies $|f(z)|\le C|z|^\alpha$ for large $|z|$ with $\alpha\ge 0$, then $f$ is
a polynomial of degree at most $\lfloor \alpha\rfloor$.

Taking $\varepsilon < 1$ (e.g. $\varepsilon = 1/2$), we get $|f(z)|\le C|z|^{1/2
}$ for large $|z|$, so $f$ is a polynomial of degree at most $0$, i.e. **$f$ is
constant**.

**Step 3: Determine the constant.**

Since $f$ is constant, $f(z)\equiv c$ for some $c\in\mathbb{C}$. The condition $
f(i)=2i$ gives $c=2i$.

**Step 4: Verify the bound.**

For $|z|\ge 2$, $|f(z)|=|2i|=2\le 100\ln 2\approx 69.3\le 100\ln|z|$. ✓
