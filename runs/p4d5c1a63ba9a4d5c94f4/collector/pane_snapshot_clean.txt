**Answer: Yes, $T$ is necessarily countable.**

**Proof.**

If $T = \emptyset$ or $T$ is a singleton, the result is trivial. Suppose $|T| \g
eq 2$ and fix a point $t_0 \in T$. For each $t \in T$, set $v_t = t - t_0 \in \m
athbb{R}^n$, so $v_{t_0} = 0$.

**Step 1: All pairwise inner products are algebraic.**

For $s, t \in T$, the distance $\|s - t\|$ is algebraic by hypothesis. Since the
 algebraic numbers $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ form a field (closed under addition,
subtraction, multiplication), $\|s-t\|^2 \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$. By the pola
rization identity,
$$\langle v_s, v_t\rangle = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\|v_s\|^2 + \|v_t\|^2 - \|v_s - v_
t\|^2\bigr) = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\|s-t_0\|^2 + \|t-t_0\|^2 - \|s-t\|^2\bigr) \in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}.$$

**Step 2: Reduce to a finite-dimensional frame with algebraic coefficients.**

The set $\{v_t : t \in T\} \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ spans a subspace of dimension $
m \leq n$. Choose a maximal linearly independent (over $\mathbb{R}$) subset $v_{
t_1}, \ldots, v_{t_m}$, where $m \geq 1$ since $|T|\geq 2$. Form the $m \times m
$ Gram matrix
$$G_{jk} = \langle v_{t_j}, v_{t_k}\rangle, \qquad j,k \in \{1,\ldots,m\}.$$
By Step 1, every entry of $G$ is algebraic. Since $v_{t_1},\ldots,v_{t_m}$ are l
inearly independent, $G$ is positive definite, hence invertible. Its inverse $G^
{-1}$ also has algebraic entries: $\det G$ is a polynomial in algebraic entries
(hence algebraic and nonzero), and each entry of $G^{-1}$ is an algebraic cofact
or divided by $\det G$, which lies in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$.

**Step 3: Every $v_t$ has algebraic coordinates in this frame.**

For any $t \in T$, write $v_t = \sum_{k=1}^m c_k\, v_{t_k}$ (unique representati
on by linear independence). Taking inner products with $v_{t_j}$ for $j=1,\ldots
,m$:
$$\langle v_t, v_{t_j}\rangle = \sum_{k=1}^m c_k\, G_{jk}, \qquad j = 1,\ldots,m
.$$
This is the linear system $G\, c = d$ where $d_j = \langle v_t, v_{t_j}\rangle \
in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ by Step 1. Thus
$$c = G^{-1} d \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}^m,$$
since both $G^{-1}$ and $d$ have algebraic entries and $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ i
s a field.

**Step 4: Count.**

The map $t \mapsto c \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}^m$ is injective (the representati
on of $v_t$ in the basis $v_{t_1},\ldots,v_{t_m}$ is unique, and $v_t = v_{t'}$
implies $t = t'$). Therefore
$$|T| \leq |\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^m| = \aleph_0 \cdot \aleph_0 \cdots \aleph_0 =
 \aleph_0,$$
since the set $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ of algebraic numbers is countable, and a f
inite product of countable sets is countable.

Hence $T$ is countable. $\blacksquare$
