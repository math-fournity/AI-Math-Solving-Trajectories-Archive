## Proof

**Goal.** Evaluate $I = \displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-x} x^{2}\log x\, dx$.

**Step 1 — Relate the integral to $\Gamma'$.**

Recall the Gamma function, valid for $s>0$:
$$\Gamma(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-x}x^{s-1}\,dx.$$
Differentiating under the integral sign with respect to $s$ (justified by domina
ted convergence on compact $s$-subintervals) gives
$$\Gamma'(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-x}x^{s-1}\log x\,dx.$$
Setting $s=3$,
$$I=\Gamma'(3).$$

**Step 2 — Compute $\Gamma'(1)$ via the Euler product limit (no digamma).**

We use the Euler limit form of $\Gamma$:
$$\Gamma(s)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n!\,n^{s}}{s(s+1)\cdots(s+n)},\qquad s>0.$$
Taking logarithms,
$$\log\Gamma(s)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Bigl[\log n!+s\log n-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\log(s+k)\B
igr].$$
Differentiating with respect to $s$ (the limit is uniform near any $s>0$, so the
 limit and derivative commute):
$$\frac{\Gamma'(s)}{\Gamma(s)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Bigl[\log n-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac
{1}{s+k}\Bigr].$$
At $s=1$, using $\Gamma(1)=1$:
$$\Gamma'(1)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Bigl[\log n-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{1+k}\Bigr]=\li
m_{n\to\infty}\Bigl[\log n-\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\frac{1}{k}\Bigr]=-\gamma,$$
where $\gamma=\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}-\log
 n\Bigr)$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant.

**Step 3 — Climb the recurrence.**

From $\Gamma(s+1)=s\,\Gamma(s)$, differentiating gives
$$\Gamma'(s+1)=\Gamma(s)+s\,\Gamma'(s).$$
Using $\Gamma(1)=\Gamma(2)=1$ and $\Gamma(3)=2$:

- $s=1$: $\;\Gamma'(2)=\Gamma(1)+1\cdot\Gamma'(1)=1+(-\gamma)=1-\gamma.$
- $s=2$: $\;\Gamma'(3)=\Gamma(2)+2\,\Gamma'(2)=1+2(1-\gamma)=3-2\gamma.$

**Step 4 — Conclude.**

$$\boxed{\,I=\Gamma'(3)=3-2\gamma\,}.$$
