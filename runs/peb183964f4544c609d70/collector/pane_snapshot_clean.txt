## Solution

**Step 1: Verify the inverse exists on the relevant range.**

$f(x) = x^3 - 2x^2 + 5$, so $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 4x = x(3x-4)$.

Critical points at $x = 0$ (local max, $f(0)=5$) and $x = 4/3$ (local min, $f(4/
3) = 103/27 \approx 3.81$). For $x > 4/3$, $f$ is strictly increasing, hence inj
ective there.

**Step 2: Find the preimages of the bounds 37 and 149.**

$f(4) = 64 - 32 + 5 = 37$. ✓
$f(6) = 216 - 72 + 5 = 149$. ✓

Since $4, 6 > 4/3$, $f$ is a bijection $[4,6] \to [37,149]$, so $f^{-1}(37) = 4$
 and $f^{-1}(149) = 6$.

**Step 3: Apply the inverse-function integral formula.**

$$\int_a^b f^{-1}(y)\,dy = b\,f^{-1}(b) - a\,f^{-1}(a) - \int_{f^{-1}(a)}^{f^{-1
}(b)} f(x)\,dx$$

(with $a=37$, $b=149$):

$$\int_{37}^{149} f^{-1}(y)\,dy = 149\cdot 6 - 37\cdot 4 - \int_4^6 (x^3 - 2x^2
+ 5)\,dx$$

$$\int_4^6 (x^3 - 2x^2 + 5)\,dx = \left[\frac{x^4}{4} - \frac{2x^3}{3} + 5x\righ

So $\displaystyle\int_4^6 f(x)\,dx = 210 - \frac{124}{3} = \frac{630 - 124}{3} =

$$\int_{37}^{149} f^{-1}(y)\,dy = 894 - 148 - \frac{506}{3} = 746 - \frac{506}{3
