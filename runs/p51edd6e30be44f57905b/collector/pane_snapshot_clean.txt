## Proof

Let $n = 2^{1000000}$. Note $n \geq 1$. The inequality in question becomes

$$\binom{2n}{n} > 2^{2n}.$$

Recall the binomial theorem identity:

$$\sum_{k=0}^{2n} \binom{2n}{k} = (1+1)^{2n} = 2^{2n}.$$

Every term $\binom{2n}{k}$ in this sum is a strictly positive integer (for $0 \l
eq k \leq 2n$). The sum contains $2n+1$ terms, and for $n \geq 1$ we have $2n+1
\geq 3$, so there are at least two other positive terms besides $\binom{2n}{n}$
(for instance, $\binom{2n}{0} = 1$ and $\binom{2n}{2n} = 1$).

Therefore $\binom{2n}{n}$ is a **proper** summand of the total:

$$\binom{2n}{n} < \sum_{k=0}^{2n} \binom{2n}{k} = 2^{2n}.$$

Hence

$$\binom{2n}{n} < 2^{2n},$$

which directly contradicts the proposed inequality $\binom{2n}{n} > 2^{2n}$.

Since $n = 2^{1000000} \geq 1$, the argument applies. The inequality

$$\binom{2\cdot 2^{1000000}}{2^{1000000}} > 2^{2\cdot 2^{1000000}}$$

**does not hold**; in fact the reverse strict inequality holds:

$$\binom{2\cdot 2^{1000000}}{2^{1000000}} < 2^{2\cdot 2^{1000000}}.$$
