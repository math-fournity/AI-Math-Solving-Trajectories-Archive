**Answer: Yes.** A finitely-sheeted covering of the interior of a compact manifo
ld with boundary is again the interior of a compact manifold with boundary.

---

**Proof.**

Let $M \cong \operatorname{int}(N)$, where $N$ is a compact topological manifold
 with boundary $\partial N$. Let $p \colon M' \to M$ be a finitely-sheeted cover
ing.

**Step 1: Collar decomposition of $M$.**

By the Brown collar theorem, $\partial N$ has a collar neighborhood: there exist
s an embedding
$$c \colon \partial N \times [0,1] \hookrightarrow N, \qquad c(x,0)=x \text{ for
 all } x\in\partial N.$$
Set $W = c\bigl(\partial N \times (0,1]\bigr) \subset M$ and $C = N \setminus c\
bigl(\partial N \times [0,1)\bigr)$. Then:

- $C$ is a compact manifold with boundary $\partial C = c(\partial N \times \{1\
}) \cong \partial N$,
- $W \cong \partial N \times (0,1]$,
- $M = C \cup_{\partial C} W$, with $C \cap W = \partial C \cong \partial N$.

Decompose $\partial N = B_1 \sqcup \cdots \sqcup B_r$ into connected components
(each $B_j$ a closed manifold). Then $W = \bigsqcup_j B_j \times (0,1]$.

**Step 2: The covering over the collar region.**

Since $p$ is finite-sheeted, $p^{-1}(C)$ is compact. Moreover $p^{-1}(C) \to C$
is a finite covering of a compact manifold with boundary, so $p^{-1}(C)$ is itse
lf a compact manifold with boundary
$$\partial\bigl(p^{-1}(C)\bigr) = p^{-1}(\partial C) = p^{-1}\!\Bigl(\bigsqcup_j
 B_j \times \{1\}\Bigr).$$

Now consider the restriction over each collar piece. For each $j$, the map
$$p^{-1}\bigl(B_j \times (0,1]\bigr) \longrightarrow B_j \times (0,1]$$
is a finite covering. Since $(0,1]$ is contractible, $\pi_1\bigl(B_j \times (0,1
]\bigr) \cong \pi_1(B_j)$, and the universal cover of $B_j \times (0,1]$ is $\wi
detilde{B}_j \times (0,1]$. Therefore every connected finite covering of $B_j \t
imes (0,1]$ is of the form $E \times (0,1] \to B_j \times (0,1]$, where $E \to B
_j$ is a connected finite-sheeted covering. Consequently
$$p^{-1}\bigl(B_j \times (0,1]\bigr) \;\cong\; \bigsqcup_{k=1}^{m_j} E_{j,k} \ti
mes (0,1],$$
where each $E_{j,k} \to B_j$ is a connected finite covering. Since $B_j$ is a cl
osed manifold, each $E_{j,k}$ is also a closed manifold (compact, without bounda
ry).

The boundary of this piece (at the $t=1$ end) is
$$\bigsqcup_{k} E_{j,k} \times \{1\} \;\cong\; \bigsqcup_k E_{j,k},$$
which is exactly the corresponding part of $\partial\bigl(p^{-1}(C)\bigr)$.

**Step 3: Compactification.**

Define
$$N' \;=\; p^{-1}(C) \;\cup\; \bigcup_{j,k} \bigl(E_{j,k} \times [0,1]\bigr),$$
where each $E_{j,k} \times \{1\}$ is identified with the corresponding boundary
component of $p^{-1}(C)$ via the covering map.

- **$N'$ is compact:** it is a finite union of compact pieces.
- **$N'$ is a topological manifold with boundary:** $p^{-1}(C)$ is a manifold wi
th boundary, each $E_{j,k}\times[0,1]$ is a manifold with boundary, and they are
 glued along a common boundary component $E_{j,k}\times\{1\}$ via a homeomorphis
m. The boundary of $N'$ is $\partial N' = \bigsqcup_{j,k} E_{j,k}\times\{0\} \co
ng \bigsqcup_{j,k} E_{j,k}$, with collar neighborhoods $E_{j,k}\times[0,\varepsi
lon)$.
- **$\operatorname{int}(N') = M'$:** Removing $\partial N'$ from $N'$ gives
$$N' \setminus \partial N' = p^{-1}(C) \;\cup\; \bigcup_{j,k}\bigl(E_{j,k}\times
(0,1]\bigr) = p^{-1}(C) \cup p^{-1}(W) = p^{-1}(M) = M'.$$

Therefore $M' \cong \operatorname{int}(N')$ where $N'$ is a compact manifold wit
h boundary. $\blacksquare$
