Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum radius of 6 non-intersecting
 equal circles drawn on a unit sphere.

## Proof

**Setup.** A circle on the unit sphere $S^2$ with angular radius $\theta$ center
ed at $C$ is the set $\{P \in S^2 : d(P,C) = \theta\}$, where $d$ is spherical d
istance. Its Euclidean radius is $\sin\theta$.

**Non-intersection condition.** Two circles of equal angular radius $\theta \leq
 \pi/2$, centered at $A, B$ with $d(A,B)=\delta$, intersect iff $\delta \leq 2\t
heta$.

*Proof.* For a point $P$ with $d(P,A)=d(P,B)=\theta$, the spherical law of cosin
es in $\triangle APB$ gives:
$$\cos\theta = \cos\theta\cos\delta + \sin\theta\sin\delta\cos\alpha$$
so $\cos\alpha = \cot\theta\cdot\tan(\delta/2)$. A solution exists iff $|\cos\al
pha|\leq 1$, which (for $\theta\le\pi/2$, $\delta>0$) reduces to $\delta \leq 2\
theta$. $\square$

**Reduction to point packing.** For 6 non-intersecting circles of angular radius
 $\theta$, all pairwise center distances satisfy $\delta_{ij} \geq 2\theta$. To
maximize $\theta$, we maximize the minimum pairwise distance $\delta$ among 6 po
ints on $S^2$, then set $\theta = \delta/2$.

**Key bound.** *For any 6 points on $S^2$, some pair has angular distance $\leq
\pi/2$.*

*Proof.* Let $v_1,\dots,v_6$ be the corresponding unit vectors in $\mathbb{R}^3$
. Suppose for contradiction all pairwise dot products are $< 0$ (i.e., all dista
nces $> \pi/2$).

**Lemma.** *At most $n+1$ vectors in $\mathbb{R}^n$ can have all pairwise dot pr
oducts strictly negative.*

*Proof of Lemma.* Let $v_1,\dots,v_k$ satisfy $\langle v_i,v_j\rangle < 0$ for $
i\neq j$. We show they are **affinely independent**. Suppose $\sum \lambda_i v_i
 = 0$ with $\sum \lambda_i = 0$, not all $\lambda_i = 0$. Set $P=\{i:\lambda_i>0
\}$, $N=\{i:\lambda_i<0\}$; both nonempty since $\sum\lambda_i=0$. Then $w := \s
um_{i\in P}\lambda_i v_i = -\sum_{j\in N}\lambda_j v_j$, so:
$$\|w\|^2 = -\sum_{i\in P,\, j\in N} \lambda_i\lambda_j\langle v_i,v_j\rangle.$$
For $i\in P, j\in N$: $\lambda_i\lambda_j < 0$ and $\langle v_i,v_j\rangle < 0$,
 so each summand $-\lambda_i\lambda_j\langle v_i,v_j\rangle < 0$. Thus $\|w\|^2
< 0$, contradiction. So the vectors are affinely independent, giving $k \leq n+1
$. $\square$

Applying the lemma with $n=3$: at most $4$ vectors in $\mathbb{R}^3$ can have al
l pairwise dot products negative. Since $6 > 4$, some pair has $\langle v_i,v_j\
rangle \geq 0$, i.e., angular distance $\leq \pi/2$. $\square$

**Achievability.** Place the 6 centers at the vertices of a regular octahedron:
$\pm e_1, \pm e_2, \pm e_3 \in S^2$. Pairwise angular distances are $\pi/2$ (non
-antipodal) and $\pi$ (antipodal), so the minimum is $\pi/2$. With angular radiu
s $\theta = \pi/4$, every pair satisfies $\delta_{ij} \geq \pi/2 = 2\theta$: non
-antipodal circles are tangent, antipodal circles are far apart. Thus 6 non-inte
rsecting circles of angular radius $\pi/4$ exist.

**Conclusion.** The maximum angular radius is $\theta = \pi/4$, achieved by the
octahedral configuration. The Euclidean radius of each circle is:

$$r = \sin\theta = \sin\frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}.$$
