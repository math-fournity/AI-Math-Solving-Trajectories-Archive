## Proof

**Claim.** The range of possible values of $K_{\lambda,\mu}$ is exactly the set
of all non-negative integers $\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} = \{0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots\}$.

**Background.** The Kostka number $K_{\lambda,\mu}$ counts the number of semista
ndard Young tableaux (SSYT) of shape $\lambda$ and weight $\mu$. In particular,
$K_{\lambda,\mu} \geq 0$ always, since it counts a set. Moreover, $K_{\lambda,\m
u} = 0$ unless $\lambda \geq \mu$ in dominance order, and $K_{\lambda,\mu} \geq
1$ whenever $\lambda \geq \mu$.

When $\mu = (1^n) = (1,1,\ldots,1)$, an SSYT of weight $(1^n)$ is the same as a
**standard** Young tableau of shape $\lambda$ (each entry $1, 2, \ldots, n$ appe
ars exactly once, and semistandardness with distinct entries forces strict incre
ase along rows and columns). Therefore:
$$K_{\lambda,(1^n)} = f^\lambda,$$
where $f^\lambda$ is the number of standard Young tableaux of shape $\lambda$, g
iven by the **hook-length formula**:
$$f^\lambda = \frac{n!}{\prod_{c \in \lambda} h(c)}.$$

### Step 2: Every positive integer $m \geq 1$ is achievable.

Fix any $m \geq 1$. Set $n = m + 1$, $\lambda = (m, 1)$, and $\mu = (1^{m+1}) =

**Compute $f^{(m,1)}$ via the hook-length formula.** The shape $(m,1)$ has $m+1$

$$K_{(m,1),\,(1^{m+1})} = m.$$
