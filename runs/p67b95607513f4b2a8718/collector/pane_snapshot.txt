Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether every normal domain is equi
valent to an integrally closed domain.

**Answer: Yes.**

**Proof.**

Recall the standard definitions from commutative algebra:

- A domain $R$ is called **integrally closed** if every element of its field of
fractions $K = \operatorname{Frac}(R)$ that is integral over $R$ already belongs
 to $R$.
- A domain $R$ is called **normal** if it is integrally closed in its field of f
ractions.

These are the **same definition**. Explicitly:

$$R \text{ is normal} \iff R \text{ is integrally closed in } \operatorname{Frac
}(R) \iff R \text{ is an integrally closed domain.}$$

Now let $R$ be any normal domain. By the definition above, $R$ is integrally clo
sed in $\operatorname{Frac}(R)$. Therefore $R$ is itself an integrally closed do
main.

Since $R$ is equal to (hence isomorphic to, hence birational to, hence equivalen
t under any standard equivalence relation to) an integrally closed domain—namely
 itself—the answer is **yes**.

More explicitly: under any notion of "equivalent" commonly used in commutative a
lgebra or algebraic geometry (isomorphism, birational equivalence, Morita equiva
lence—which for commutative rings coincides with isomorphism), $R$ is equivalent
 to the integrally closed domain $R$.

$\blacksquare$
