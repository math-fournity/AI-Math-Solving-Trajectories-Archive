## Proof

**Step 1: The sequence is positive, decreasing, and tends to 0.**

Since $0 < x_1 < 1$, we have $0 < 1 - x_1 < 1$, hence $x_2 = x_1(1-x_1)$ satisfi
es $0 < x_2 < x_1 < 1$. By induction, $0 < x_{n+1} < x_n < 1$ for all $n \ge 1$.
 Thus $\{x_n\}$ is strictly decreasing and bounded below by $0$, so it converges
 to some limit $L \ge 0$. Passing to the limit in $x_{n+1} = x_n(1 - x_n)$ gives
 $L = L(1-L)$, i.e., $L^2 = 0$, so $L = 0$.

**Step 2: Transform to the reciprocal.**

Set $y_n = \dfrac{1}{x_n}$. Since $x_n \to 0^+$, we have $y_n \to +\infty$, and
in particular $y_n > 1$ for all sufficiently large $n$. Then

$$
y_{n+1} = \frac{1}{x_{n+1}} = \frac{1}{x_n(1-x_n)} = \frac{y_n}{1 - x_n} = \frac
{y_n}{1 - \frac{1}{y_n}} = \frac{y_n^2}{y_n - 1}.
$$

Rewriting,

$$
y_{n+1} = \frac{y_n^2}{y_n - 1} = y_n \cdot \frac{y_n}{y_n - 1} = y_n\!\left(1 +
 \frac{1}{y_n - 1}\right) = y_n + \frac{y_n}{y_n - 1}.
$$

Therefore

$$
y_{n+1} - y_n = \frac{y_n}{y_n - 1} = 1 + \frac{1}{y_n - 1}.
$$

Since $y_n \to +\infty$, the term $\dfrac{1}{y_n - 1} \to 0$, and consequently

$$
\lim_{n \to \infty}\,(y_{n+1} - y_n) = 1.
$$

**Step 3: Apply the Stolz–Cesàro theorem.**

The Stolz–Cesàro theorem states: if $\{b_n\}$ is strictly increasing with $b_n \
to +\infty$, and $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n+1}-a_n}{b_{n+1}-b_n}$
 exists (finite or infinite), then $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_n}{b_n
}$ exists and equals the same value.

Take $a_n = y_n$ and $b_n = n$. Then $b_n$ is strictly increasing with $b_n \to
+\infty$, and

$$
\frac{a_{n+1} - a_n}{b_{n+1} - b_n} = \frac{y_{n+1} - y_n}{1} = y_{n+1} - y_n \;
\longrightarrow\; 1.
$$

Hence, by Stolz–Cesàro,

$$
\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{y_n}{n} = 1.
$$

**Step 4: Conclude.**

Since $y_n = \dfrac{1}{x_n}$, the above gives

$$
\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n\,x_n} = 1,
$$

and therefore

$$
\boxed{\lim_{n \to \infty} n\,x_n = 1}.
$$
