## Proof

**Step 1: Compute initial terms.**

$a_1=20,\ a_2=30,\ a_3=70,\ a_4=180,\ a_5=470,\ a_6=1230,\ \ldots$

All terms are positive (the recurrence preserves positivity since $a_{n+2} = 3a_
{n+1}-a_n > a_{n+1} > 0$ by induction).

**Step 2: Establish the invariant.**

For the recurrence $u_{n+2}=3u_{n+1}-u_n$ (characteristic polynomial $x^2-3x+1$)
, the quantity
$$I_n = a_{n+1}^2 - 3a_na_{n+1} + a_n^2$$
is constant. Indeed, substituting $a_{n+2}=3a_{n+1}-a_n$:
$$a_{n+2}^2 - 3a_{n+1}a_{n+2}+a_{n+1}^2 = (3a_{n+1}-a_n)^2 - 3a_{n+1}(3a_{n+1}-a
_n)+a_{n+1}^2 = a_{n+1}^2-3a_na_{n+1}+a_n^2.$$

Evaluating at $n=1$: $I = 30^2 - 3\cdot20\cdot30 + 20^2 = 900-1800+400 = -500$.

So for all $n\geq 1$:
$$a_{n+1}^2 - 3a_na_{n+1} + a_n^2 = -500. \tag{$\star$}$$

**Step 3: Reduce to a factorization.**

Suppose $1+5a_na_{n+1}=k^2$ for some positive integer $k$. Then $5a_na_{n+1}=k^2
-1$, i.e., $a_na_{n+1}=\frac{k^2-1}{5}$ (so $k\equiv\pm1\pmod{5}$).

From $(\star)$: $a_{n+1}^2+a_n^2 = -500+3a_na_{n+1} = -500+\frac{3(k^2-1)}{5}=\f
rac{3k^2-2503}{5}$.

Now compute:
$$(a_{n+1}+a_n)^2 = a_{n+1}^2+2a_na_{n+1}+a_n^2 = \frac{3k^2-2503}{5}+\frac{2(k^
2-1)}{5}=\frac{5k^2-2505}{5}=k^2-501.$$

Therefore:
$$k^2-(a_{n+1}+a_n)^2 = 501,$$
$$(k-(a_{n+1}+a_n))\big(k+(a_{n+1}+a_n)\big)=501. \tag{$\star\star$}$$

**Step 4: Factor 501 and enumerate cases.**

$501 = 3\times 167$, and $167$ is prime (not divisible by $2,3,5,7,11,13$; $13^2
=169>167$).

Since all $a_n>0$, we have $a_{n+1}+a_n>0$, and $k>0$ with $k^2>(a_{n+1}+a_n)^2$
, so both factors in $(\star\star)$ are positive. Setting $d=k-(a_{n+1}+a_n)$ an
d $e=k+(a_{n+1}+a_n)$ with $d\cdot e=501$, $0<d<e$:

| Case | $d$ | $e$ | $k=\frac{d+e}{2}$ | $a_{n+1}+a_n=\frac{e-d}{2}$ |
|------|-----|-----|-----|-----|
| 1 | 1 | 501 | 251 | 250 |
| 2 | 3 | 167 | 85 | 82 |

**Case 2:** $k=85$, $a_n+a_{n+1}=82$. Then $a_na_{n+1}=\frac{85^2-1}{5}=\frac{72
24}{5}=1444.8$, which is **not an integer**. Impossible.

**Case 1:** $k=251$, $a_n+a_{n+1}=250$. Then $a_na_{n+1}=\frac{251^2-1}{5}=\frac
{63000}{5}=12600$. So $a_n,a_{n+1}$ are roots of $t^2-250t+12600=0$, with discri
minant $62500-50400=12100=110^2$, giving $t=\frac{250\pm110}{2}\in\{70,180\}$.

Thus $\{a_n,a_{n+1}\}=\{70,180\}$, which matches $a_3=70,\ a_4=180$. **Verificat
ion:** $1+5\cdot70\cdot180=1+63000=63001=251^2$. ✓

**Step 5: Confirm uniqueness.**

The invariant $(\star)$ is constant for all $n$, so equation $(\star\star)$ hold
s for **every** $n$ satisfying the perfect-square condition. Since $501=3\times1

on-integer, the **only** solution is $n=3$.
