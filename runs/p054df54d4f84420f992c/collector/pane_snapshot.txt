## Equilibrium

Setting both derivatives to zero:
- $6x - 2y = 0 \implies y = 3x$.
- $y - 3x - x^3 = 3x - 3x - x^3 = -x^3 = 0 \implies x = 0$.

The **unique equilibrium** is the origin $(0,0)$.

## Linearization is degenerate

The Jacobian is
$$J(x,y) = \begin{pmatrix} -3-3x^2 & 1 \\ 6 & -2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad J(0,0) =
\begin{pmatrix} -3 & 1 \\ 6 & -2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

$\operatorname{tr} J = -5$, $\det J = (-3)(-2) - 6 = 0$. The eigenvalues are $\l
ambda = 0$ and $\lambda = -5$. The linearization has a **zero eigenvalue**, so l
inear analysis is inconclusive — we must use a Lyapunov (or center-manifold) app
roach.

## Lyapunov function

Consider
$$V(x,y) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,x^2 + \tfrac{1}{12}\,y^2,$$
which is positive definite and radially unbounded. Computing $\dot V$ along traj
ectories:

$$\dot V = x\,\dot x + \frac{y}{6}\,\dot y = x\,(y - 3x - x^3) + \frac{y}{6}\,(6
x - 2y).$$

Expanding:

$$\dot V = xy - 3x^2 - x^4 + xy - \frac{y^2}{3} = 2xy - 3x^2 - \frac{y^2}{3} - x
^4.$$

The quadratic part factors:

$$3x^2 - 2xy + \frac{y^2}{3} = \frac{1}{3}\bigl(9x^2 - 6xy + y^2\bigr) = \frac{1
}{3}(3x - y)^2.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{\dot V = -\frac{1}{3}(3x - y)^2 - x^4 \;\leq\; 0.}$$

## Negative definiteness

$\dot V = 0$ requires both $x = 0$ (from the $-x^4$ term) and $3x - y = 0$ (from
 the squared term), giving $x = 0,\; y = 0$. Hence $\dot V$ is **negative defini
te**:

$$\dot V(x,y) < 0 \quad \text{for all } (x,y) \neq (0,0).$$

## Conclusion

Since $V$ is positive definite and radially unbounded, and $\dot V$ is negative
definite on $\mathbb{R}^2$, by **Lyapunov's direct method** (global version):

> **The origin is the unique equilibrium and is globally asymptotically stable.*
*

Every trajectory $(x(t), y(t))$ satisfies $V(x(t),y(t)) \to 0$ as $t \to +\infty
$, hence $(x(t),y(t)) \to (0,0)$.

**Remark on convergence rate.** Because the linearization has a zero eigenvalue,
 convergence is not exponential. The center-manifold reduction (center direction
 $y = 3x$, stable direction $y = -2x$) gives reduced dynamics $\dot u = -\tfrac{
2}{5}\,u^3 + O(u^5)$ on the center manifold, confirming algebraic (sub-exponenti
al) decay $\sim t^{-1/2}$, consistent with $\dot V \leq -x^4$.
