## Proof

For $n \geq 3$, the squaring map on $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is never surjective

or $4\mid n$, squaring mod $4$ is not injective). Hence QNRs always exist. If $n

t exist — contradiction. So $n$ must admit primitive roots, meaning $n \in \{4,\

**Step 2: Rule out $n=4$.**

Squares mod $4$ are $\{0,1\}$, so $2$ and $3$ are QNRs. But $\gcd(2,4)=2>1$, so
$2$ is not a primitive root. Fails.

powers $g^{j}$ ($j$ odd, $1\le j\le m-1$). Primitive roots are $g^j$ with $\gcd(
j,m)=1$.

*Unit condition:* Every odd $j\in\{1,\dots,m-1\}$ must satisfy $\gcd(j,m)=1$. If

2^t+1$ is a **Fermat prime**.
