Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether separate boundedness of a b
ilinear map on infinite-dimensional normed spaces implies joint boundedness.

## Analysis

The two given conditions say that $T$ is **separately continuous** (bounded in e
ach variable when the other is fixed). The question is whether separate continui
ty implies **joint continuity** (existence of $K$ with $\|T(x,y)\| \le K\|x\|\|y
\|$).

**Key distinction:** If $V$ and $W$ were **Banach spaces** (complete), the Unifo
rm Boundedness Principle would give a positive answer. But the problem only assu
mes **normed** vector spaces (not necessarily complete), and this distinction is
 decisive.

## Proof that no such $K$ need exist

**Counterexample.** Let $V = W = c_{00}$ (the space of finitely-supported real s
equences) equipped with the $\ell^2$ norm $\|x\|_2 = \left(\sum_{n} x_n^2\right)
^{1/2}$. This is an infinite-dimensional normed vector space that is **not compl
ete** (its completion is $\ell^2$).

Define the bilinear map:
$$T(x, y) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n\, x_n\, y_n.$$

Since $x$ and $y$ are finitely supported, the sum is finite, so $T$ is well-defi
ned and clearly bilinear.

**Verification of the first condition.** For fixed $x \in c_{00}$, by the Cauchy
–Schwarz inequality (applied to a finite sum):
$$|T(x,y)| = \left|\sum_n n\, x_n\, y_n\right| \le \left(\sum_n n^2 x_n^2\right)
^{1/2}\!\left(\sum_n y_n^2\right)^{1/2} = C(x)\,\|y\|_2,$$
where $C(x) = \left(\sum_n n^2 x_n^2\right)^{1/2} < \infty$ (finite sum since $x
$ is finitely supported). ✓

**Verification of the second condition.** By symmetry, for fixed $y \in c_{00}$:
$$|T(x,y)| \le D(y)\,\|x\|_2, \quad D(y) = \left(\sum_n n^2 y_n^2\right)^{1/2} <
 \infty. \quad\checkmark$$

**No uniform $K$ exists.** Let $e_n$ denote the $n$-th standard basis vector. Th
en $\|e_n\|_2 = 1$ and:
$$T(e_n, e_n) = n \cdot 1 \cdot 1 = n.$$

If a constant $K$ with $|T(x,y)| \le K\|x\|_2\|y\|_2$ existed, we would need:
$$n = |T(e_n, e_n)| \le K \cdot 1 \cdot 1 = K \quad \text{for all } n \ge 1,$$
which is impossible since $n \to \infty$.

## Conclusion

There does **not** necessarily exist such a constant $K$. The two separate bound
edness conditions do not imply joint boundedness for bilinear maps on infinite-d
imensional normed vector spaces.

The failure stems from the lack of **completeness**: the Uniform Boundedness Pri
nciple (which would promote separate boundedness to uniform boundedness) require
s Banach (complete) spaces. The space $c_{00}$ is incomplete, allowing the "oper
ator norms" $C(x)$ to be unbounded on the unit ball $\{x : \|x\| \le 1\}$, which
 is exactly what the counterexample exploits.
