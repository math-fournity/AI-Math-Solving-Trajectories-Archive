 of the convex $n$-gon uses $n - 3$ non-crossing diagonals. A coloring is *Stabl

**Theorem.** For $n \ge 4$, a 2-coloring is Stable if and only if it is non-mono
chromatic and all same-colored vertices form a single contiguous block on the ri

### Proof of Sufficiency (Contiguous non-monochromatic $\Rightarrow$ Stable)

Let the black block be vertices $1, 2, \ldots, k$ and the white block be $k+1, \
ldots, n$ (with $1 \le k \le n-1$). Use the diagonal $(k, n)$ (bichromatic: $k$
is black, $n$ is white). This splits the polygon into:
- **Sub-polygon A** $= (k, k+1, \ldots, n)$: vertex $k$ is black, all others whi
te. Fan from $k$: diagonals $(k, k+2), (k, k+3), \ldots, (k, n-1)$, all bichroma
tic. ✓
- **Sub-polygon B** $= (n, 1, 2, \ldots, k)$: vertex $n$ is white, all others bl
ack. Fan from $n$: diagonals $(n, 2), (n, 3), \ldots, (n, k-1)$, all bichromatic
. ✓

All diagonals are bichromatic and non-crossing. $\square$

### Proof of Necessity (Stable $\Rightarrow$ Contiguous non-monochromatic)

**Monochromatic is not Stable:** If all vertices share one color, every diagonal
 is monochromatic, but $n \ge 4$ requires at least one diagonal. ✗

**Non-contiguous is not Stable:** We prove by strong induction on $n$ that every
 Stable coloring is contiguous.

**Base case $n = 4$:** The two diagonals are $(1,3)$ and $(2,4)$. A coloring is
Stable iff at least one is bichromatic, i.e., it is NOT the case that both $\{1,
