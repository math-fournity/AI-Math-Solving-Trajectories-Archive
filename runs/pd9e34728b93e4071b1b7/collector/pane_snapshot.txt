## Solution

The curve is $C: y = \frac{a}{2}(e^{x/a}+e^{-x/a}) = a\cosh(x/a)$, a catenary wi
th minimum $y=a$ at $x=0$.

**Setup.** For $y=t$ we have $\cosh(x/a)=t/a$, giving $x = \pm\, a\,\operatornam
e{arccosh}(t/a)$. The relevant segment of $C$ with $a\le y\le t$ spans $x\in[-a\
,\operatorname{arccosh}(t/a),\; a\,\operatorname{arccosh}(t/a)]$.

**Length $l(t)$.** Since $y'=\sinh(x/a)$,
$$\sqrt{1+(y')^2}=\sqrt{1+\sinh^2(x/a)}=\cosh(x/a)=y/a.$$
By symmetry,
$$l(t)=2\int_0^{a\,\operatorname{arccosh}(t/a)}\cosh(x/a)\,dx=2a\sinh(x/a)\Big|_
0^{a\,\operatorname{arccosh}(t/a)}=2a\sinh(\operatorname{arccosh}(t/a)).$$
Using $\sinh(\operatorname{arccosh} u)=\sqrt{u^2-1}$,
$$l(t)=2a\sqrt{(t/a)^2-1}=2\sqrt{t^2-a^2}.$$

**Area $S(t)$.** The region bounded by $y=t$ (above) and $C$ (below) is
$$S(t)=2\int_0^{a\,\operatorname{arccosh}(t/a)}\bigl(t-a\cosh(x/a)\bigr)\,dx=2\B
igl[tx-a^2\sinh(x/a)\Bigr]_0^{a\,\operatorname{arccosh}(t/a)}.$$
$$S(t)=2\Bigl[ta\,\operatorname{arccosh}(t/a)-a^2\sinh(\operatorname{arccosh}(t/
a))\Bigr]=2ta\,\operatorname{arccosh}(t/a)-2a\sqrt{t^2-a^2}.$$

**The ratio.** Using $\operatorname{arccosh}(t/a)=\ln\!\Bigl(\dfrac{t+\sqrt{t^2-
a^2}}{a}\Bigr)$,
$$\frac{S(t)}{l(t)\ln t}=\frac{2a\bigl[t\,\operatorname{arccosh}(t/a)-\sqrt{t^2-
a^2}\bigr]}{2\sqrt{t^2-a^2}\,\ln t}=\frac{a}{\ln t}\left[\frac{t\,\operatorname{
arccosh}(t/a)}{\sqrt{t^2-a^2}}-1\right].$$

**Asymptotics as $t\to\infty$.** Since $\sqrt{t^2-a^2}=t\sqrt{1-a^2/t^2}=t-\dfra
c{a^2}{2t}+O(t^{-3})$,
$$t+\sqrt{t^2-a^2}=2t-\frac{a^2}{2t}+O(t^{-3}),$$
$$\operatorname{arccosh}(t/a)=\ln\!\Bigl(\frac{2t}{a}\Bigr)+O(t^{-2})=\ln t+\ln(
2/a)+O(t^{-2}).$$
Therefore
$$\frac{t\,\operatorname{arccosh}(t/a)}{\sqrt{t^2-a^2}}=\frac{t\bigl[\ln t+\ln(2
/a)+O(t^{-2})\bigr]}{t\bigl[1+O(t^{-2})\bigr]}=\ln t+\ln(2/a)+O(t^{-2}),$$
and
$$\frac{S(t)}{l(t)\ln t}=\frac{a}{\ln t}\Bigl[\ln t+\ln(2/a)-1+O(t^{-2})\Bigr]=a
\left(1+\frac{\ln(2/a)-1}{\ln t}+O(t^{-2})\right).$$

As $t\to\infty$, the correction term $\dfrac{\ln(2/a)-1}{\ln t}\to 0$, hence

$$\boxed{\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{S(t)}{l(t)\ln t}=a.}$$
