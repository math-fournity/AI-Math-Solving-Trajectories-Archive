## Proof

We wish to find the largest natural number $m$ with $\displaystyle I(m)=\int_0^\
infty \frac{(\ln x)^m}{e^x}\,dx < 2$.

**Step 1: Identify $I(m)$ as a derivative of the Gamma function.**

Since $\Gamma(s)=\int_0^\infty x^{s-1}e^{-x}\,dx$, differentiating under the int
egral $m$ times gives
$$\Gamma^{(m)}(s)=\int_0^\infty x^{s-1}(\ln x)^m e^{-x}\,dx.$$
Setting $s=1$:
$$I(m)=\Gamma^{(m)}(1).$$

**Step 2: Express via the complete Bell polynomial.**

Using $\Gamma'=\Gamma\,\psi$ (digamma) and iterating, $\Gamma^{(m)}(z)=\Gamma(z)
\,B_m\!\bigl(\psi(z),\psi'(z),\dots,\psi^{(m-1)}(z)\bigr)$, where $B_m$ is the c
omplete exponential Bell polynomial (all coefficients positive). At $z=1$:
- $\Gamma(1)=1$,
- $\psi(1)=-\gamma$ (Euler–Mascheroni constant),
- $\psi^{(k)}(1)=(-1)^{k+1}k!\,\zeta(k+1)$ for $k\ge 1$.

**Step 3: Sign analysis.**

A monomial $\prod x_{j_i}$ in $B_m$ with $\sum j_i=m$ has sign $\prod(-1)^{j_i}=
(-1)^m$. Hence **every** term of $B_m$ has sign $(-1)^m$, so
$$\operatorname{sgn} I(m)=(-1)^m.$$
Thus $I(m)<0$ for odd $m$ and $I(m)>0$ for even $m$. For the integral to be a *p
ositive* real number below $2$, only even $m$ are relevant (odd $m$ give negativ
e values). We seek the largest even $m$ with $0<I(m)<2$.

**Step 4: Compute $I(0),I(2),I(4)$.**

- $I(0)=\Gamma(1)=1<2$. ✓
- $I(2)=B_2(-\gamma,\zeta(2))=\gamma^2+\zeta(2)=\gamma^2+\dfrac{\pi^2}{6}$.

  Using $\gamma<0.578$ so $\gamma^2<0.3341$, and $\pi^2/6<1.645$:
  $$I(2)<0.3341+1.645=1.9791<2.\ \checkmark$$

- $I(4)=B_4(-\gamma,\zeta(2),-2\zeta(3),6\zeta(4))=\gamma^4+6\gamma^2\zeta(2)+3\
zeta(2)^2+8\gamma\zeta(3)+6\zeta(4)$.

  All five terms are positive. In particular, using just the last term:
  $$I(4)\ge 6\zeta(4)=6\cdot\frac{\pi^4}{90}=\frac{\pi^4}{15}>6>2.\ \boldsymbol{
\times}$$

**Step 5: All even $m\ge 4$ exceed $2$.**

The Bell polynomial $B_m$ contains the single-block term $x_m$ (coefficient $1$)
. For even $m$,
$$I(m)\ge x_m=\psi^{(m-1)}(1)=(m-1)!\,\zeta(m).$$
For every even $m\ge 4$: $(m-1)!\ge 3!=6$ and $\zeta(m)\ge 1$, so
$$I(m)\ge 6>2.$$
Hence no even $m\ge 4$ satisfies $I(m)<2$.

**Step 6: Odd $m\ge 3$ have $|I(m)|>2$ (so they fail the natural $|I(m)|<2$ read
ing).**

For odd $m\ge 3$, $|I(m)|\ge |x_m|=(m-1)!\,\zeta(m)\ge 2!\cdot\zeta(3)=2\zeta(3)
\approx 2.404>2$. So $|I(m)|>2$ for all odd $m\ge 3$, while $|I(1)|=\gamma<2$.

**Conclusion.**

| $m$ | $I(m)$ | $<2$? (positive value / $|I(m)|<2$) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | $1$ | ✓ |
| 1 | $-\gamma\approx -0.577$ | $|I|<2$ ✓ |
| 2 | $\gamma^2+\pi^2/6\approx 1.978$ | ✓ |
| 3 | $\approx -5.44$ | $|I|>2$ ✗ |
| 4 | $\approx 23.6$ | ✗ |
| $\ge 4$ even | $\ge 6$ | ✗ |
| $\ge 3$ odd | $|I|\ge 2\zeta(3)>2$ | ✗ |

The largest natural number $m$ for which the integral is (in absolute value, equ
ivalently as a positive real for even $m$) less than $2$ is

$$\boxed{m=2}.$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
