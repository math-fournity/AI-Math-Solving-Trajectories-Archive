## Proof

Let $g(t) = \arcsin(\sin t)$. This is a continuous, $2\pi$-periodic function. We
 first establish its key properties.

**Symmetry.** Since $\arcsin$ is odd and $\sin$ is odd,
$$g(-t) = \arcsin(\sin(-t)) = \arcsin(-\sin t) = -\arcsin(\sin t) = -g(t),$$
so $g$ is **odd**.

**Periodicity.** Since $\sin(t+2\pi)=\sin t$, we have $g(t+2\pi)=g(t)$, so $g$ i
s $2\pi$-periodic.

**Mean value over one period.** Because $g$ is odd and $2\pi$-periodic, integrat
ing over the symmetric interval $[-\pi,\pi]$ (one full period):
$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} g(t)\,dt = 0,$$
since the integral of an odd function over a symmetric interval vanishes. Hence
the mean value of $g$ over any period is $0$.

**Averaging lemma.** Let $g$ be continuous and $T$-periodic with mean $\mu = \fr
ac{1}{T}\int_0^T g(t)\,dt$. Then for any $a>0$,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^a g(nx)\,dx = a\,\mu.$$

*Proof of the lemma.* Substitute $u = nx$, so $\int_0^a g(nx)\,dx = \frac{1}{n}\
int_0^{na} g(u)\,du$. Write $na = q_n T + r_n$ with $q_n = \lfloor na/T\rfloor$
and $0\le r_n < T$. By periodicity,
$$\int_0^{na} g(u)\,du = q_n\int_0^T g(u)\,du + \int_0^{r_n} g(u)\,du = q_n T\mu
 + O(1).$$
Dividing by $n$:
$$\frac{1}{n}\int_0^{na} g(u)\,du = \frac{q_n T}{n}\mu + O(1/n).$$
Since $q_n T/n \to a$ as $n\to\infty$, the limit is $a\mu$. $\square$

**Applying the lemma.** Here $g(t)=\arcsin(\sin t)$ is continuous and $2\pi$-per
iodic with mean $\mu = 0$, and $a=1$. Therefore
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1 \arcsin(\sin(nx))\,dx = 1\cdot 0 = 0.$$
