## Proof

**Claim:** $Y(k) = X(k)$ does **not** hold in general. The natural map $Y(k) \to
 X(k)$ is a bijection if and only if $X$ has no $k$-rational nodes.

### Setup

Since $X$ is a stable curve over $k$, it is a proper, geometrically connected, g
eometrically reduced curve whose singularities are at worst ordinary double poin
ts (nodes). The normalization $\nu: Y \to X$ is a finite birational morphism tha
t is an isomorphism over the smooth locus $X^{\mathrm{sm}}$. The only points whe
re $Y$ and $X$ differ are the nodes.

### Analysis at $k$-rational points

Let $P \in X(k)$ be a $k$-rational point. There are two cases:

**Case 1: $P$ is a smooth point.** Then $\nu$ is an isomorphism in a neighborhoo
d of $P$, so $P$ lifts uniquely to a $k$-rational point $\tilde{P} \in Y(k)$. Th
is gives a bijection between $k$-rational points of $Y$ and $X$ lying over the s
mooth locus.

**Case 2: $P$ is a node.** Since $P$ is $k$-rational, the completed local ring o
f $X$ at $P$ is $\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,P} \cong k[[u,v]]/(uv)$ (split node) o
r $k[[u,v]]/(v^2 - c\,u^2)$ for some $c \in k^{\times} \setminus (k^{\times})^2$
 (non-split node).

- **Split node** ($\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,P} \cong k[[u,v]]/(uv)$): The normal
ization is $k[[u]] \times k[[v]]$, so the fiber $\nu^{-1}(P)$ consists of **two*
* points, each with residue field $k$. Thus $P$ lifts to two distinct $k$-ration
al points of $Y$. The map $Y(k) \to X(k)$ is **not injective**.

- **Non-split node** ($\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,P} \cong k[[u,v]]/(v^2 - c\,u^2)
$, $c \notin (k^\times)^2$): Setting $w = v/u$, the normalization is $k(\sqrt{c}
)[[u]]$, which has residue field $k(\sqrt{c}) \neq k$. The fiber $\nu^{-1}(P)$ i
s a **single** point with residue field $k(\sqrt{c})$, which is **not** $k$-rati
onal. Thus $P \in X(k)$ has **no** $k$-rational preimage in $Y$. The map $Y(k) \
to X(k)$ is **not surjective**.

### Surjectivity of $Y(k) \to X(k)$ in the forward direction

Any $Q \in Y(k)$ maps to $P = \nu(Q) \in X$ with $k \hookrightarrow \kappa(P) \h
ookrightarrow \kappa(Q) = k$, forcing $\kappa(P) = k$, so $P \in X(k)$. Thus the
 image of $Y(k)$ always lands in $X(k)$.

### Conclusion

The natural map $Y(k) \to X(k)$ is:
- A bijection over the smooth locus,
- **Two-to-one** at each $k$-rational **split** node (failure of injectivity),
- **Zero-to-one** at each $k$-rational **non-split** node (failure of surjectivi
ty).

Therefore $Y(k) = X(k)$ **if and only if** $X$ has no $k$-rational nodes. Since
a stable curve may certainly possess $k$-rational nodes (split or non-split), th
e equality $Y(k) = X(k)$ **does not hold in general**.

### Concrete counterexamples

**Non-injectivity (split node):** Over any field $k$, let $X$ be an irreducible
stable curve of genus $2$ with a single $k$-rational split node (e.g., a degener
ation of a genus-2 hyperelliptic curve $y^2 = f(x)$ where $f$ has a double root
at a $k$-rational point, with the two tangent directions defined over $k$). Then
 the node $P \in X(k)$ has two $k$-rational preimages in $Y$, so $|Y(k)| > |X(k)
|$ and $Y(k) \neq X(k)$.

**Non-surjectivity (non-split node):** Over $k = \mathbb{R}$, let $X$ be a stabl
e curve with a non-split $\mathbb{R}$-rational node (the two tangent directions
at the node are complex conjugate, i.e., defined over $\mathbb{C} \setminus \mat
hbb{R}$). Then the node $P \in X(\mathbb{R})$ has a single preimage in $Y$ with
residue field $\mathbb{C}$, which is not an $\mathbb{R}$-rational point. Thus $P
 \in X(\mathbb{R})$ has no preimage in $Y(\mathbb{R})$, so $Y(\mathbb{R}) \neq X
(\mathbb{R})$.
