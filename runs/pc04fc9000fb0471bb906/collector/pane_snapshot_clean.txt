## Proof

Let $f \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ be a separable cubic, and let $G$ be the Galois group
of its splitting field $K$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Since $f$ is separable, $G$ acts f
aithfully on the three roots $\{\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3\}$, giving an embeddi
ng $G \hookrightarrow S_3$. Hence $G$ is (isomorphic to) a subgroup of $S_3$.

The subgroups of $S_3$ are, up to isomorphism:
- $S_3$ itself (order $6$),
- $A_3 \cong C_3$ (order $3$),
- $C_2$ (order $2$, generated by a transposition),
- $\{e\}$ (the trivial group, order $1$).

The question excludes $S_3$ and $A_3$, so we must determine which of $C_2$ and $
\{e\}$ actually occur for separable cubics over $\mathbb{Q}$, and confirm they a
re the only remaining possibilities.

**Case 1: $f$ splits completely over $\mathbb{Q}$.** Then all three roots lie in
 $\mathbb{Q}$, so $K = \mathbb{Q}$ and $G = \{e\}$, the trivial group. This is r
ealized, e.g., by $f(x) = x(x-1)(x-2)$, which is separable.

**Case 2: $f$ has exactly one root in $\mathbb{Q}$.** Then $f$ factors as a line
ar factor times an irreducible separable quadratic over $\mathbb{Q}$:
$$f(x) = (x - r)\,g(x), \qquad r \in \mathbb{Q},\quad g \in \mathbb{Q}[x]\text{
irreducible quadratic}.$$
The splitting field is $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})$ where $D$ is the discriminant
of $g$ (a non-square rational number, since $g$ is irreducible). Thus $:\mathb
b{Q}] = 2$ and $G \cong C_2$. This is realized, e.g., by $f(x) = x(x^2 - 2)$, wh
ose splitting field is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ with Galois group $C_2$.

**No other subgroups remain.** Since $G \leq S_3$ always, and the only subgroups
 of $S_3$ are $S_3, A_3, C_2, \{e\}$, excluding $S_3$ and $A_3$ leaves exactly $
C_2$ and $\{e\}$, both of which we have shown occur.

Therefore, besides $S_3$ and $A_3$, the possible Galois groups of a separable cu
bic over $\mathbb{Q}$ are:

$$\boxed{C_2 \text{ (cyclic of order 2)} \quad \text{and} \quad \{e\} \text{ (th
e trivial group)}.}$$
