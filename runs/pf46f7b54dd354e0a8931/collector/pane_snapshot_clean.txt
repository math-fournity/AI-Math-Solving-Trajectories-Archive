## Proof

**Answer: Yes, $E$ is necessarily countable.**

**Key observation.** For any $\varepsilon > 0$, the set $E \cap \{z \in \mathbb{
C} : |z| \geq \varepsilon\}$ is finite.

*Proof of the observation.* Suppose for contradiction that $E \cap \{|z| \geq \v
arepsilon\}$ is infinite. Then we may choose a sequence $(e_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}
}$ of distinct elements from this set. Since $|e_n| \geq \varepsilon > 0$ for ev
ery $n$, the sequence $(e_n)$ cannot converge to $0$. This contradicts the hypot
hesis that every sequence of distinct elements of $E$ converges to $0$. $\square
$

**Decomposition.** Since every nonzero complex number has positive modulus, for
each $z \in E \setminus \{0\}$ there exists an integer $n \geq 1$ with $|z| \geq
 1/n$. Hence

$$E \setminus \{0\} \;=\; \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\,\bigl(E \cap \{|z| \geq 1/n\}\
bigr).$$

The forward inclusion holds because any $z \in E\setminus\{0\}$ satisfies $|z|>0
$, so $|z|\geq 1/n$ for some $n$; the reverse inclusion is immediate since $\{|z
|\geq 1/n\}\subseteq \mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}$.

**Counting.** By the observation, each set $E \cap \{|z| \geq 1/n\}$ is finite.
Therefore $E \setminus \{0\}$ is a countable union of finite sets, hence countab
le. Adding back the single point $\{0\}$ (whether or not $0 \in E$), we conclude
 that $E$ is countable. $\blacksquare$
