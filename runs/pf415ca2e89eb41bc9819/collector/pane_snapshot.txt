**Claim:** The infimum is $\boxed{1}$.

**Proof.**

Let $I = \int_0^1 (u'(t))^2\,dt$. Since $\max_{t\in[0,1]}|u(t)|=1$, there exists
 $t_0\in[0,1]$ with $|u(t_0)|=1$. Since $u(0)=0$, we have $t_0>0$.

By the fundamental theorem and Cauchy–Schwarz,
$$
|u(t_0)| = \left|\int_0^{t_0} u'(t)\,dt\right| \le \left(\int_0^{t_0} 1^2\,dt\ri
ght)^{1/2}\!\left(\int_0^{t_0} (u'(t))^2\,dt\right)^{1/2} \le \sqrt{t_0}\,\sqrt{
I}.
$$
Hence $1 \le \sqrt{t_0}\sqrt{I}$, so
$$
I \ge \frac{1}{t_0} \ge \frac{1}{1} = 1,
$$
since $t_0 \le 1$. Thus $I \ge 1$ for every admissible $u$.

**Attainment.** Take $u(t) = t$. Then $u\in C^1[0,1]$, $u(0)=0$, $\max_{[0,1]}|u
(t)| = u(1) = 1$, and
$$
\int_0^1 (u'(t))^2\,dt = \int_0^1 1\,dt = 1.
$$
(Equality in Cauchy–Schwarz requires $u'$ constant on $[0,t_0]$ and $t_0=1$, whi
ch is exactly $u(t)=t$ or $u(t)=-t$.)

Therefore the infimum is achieved and equals $1$.
