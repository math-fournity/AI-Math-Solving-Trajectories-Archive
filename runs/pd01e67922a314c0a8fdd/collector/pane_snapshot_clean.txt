# Solution

## Setup and Given Information

**Production ratios:**
- P(Anomaly) = 1/100
- P(Grade-A) = 99/100

**Scanner** (95% accurate):
- P(scanner → "Deviation" | Anomaly) = 0.95
- P(scanner → "Deviation" | Grade-A) = 0.05

**Lab audit** (90% accurate, applied only to diverted chips):
- P(lab → "Defective" | Anomaly) = 0.90
- P(lab → "Defective" | Grade-A) = 0.10

**Goal:** Find P(Anomaly | lab says "Defective").

## Key Observation: The Diversion Rate Cancels

The problem states historically 5% of chips are diverted. Let me verify consiste
ncy: the computed diversion rate is
$$P(\text{diverted}) = 0.05 \times 0.99 + 0.95 \times 0.01 = 0.0495 + 0.0095 = 0
.059 = 5.9\%$$

This differs from the stated 5%. However, this discrepancy is **irrelevant**: wh
en we compute the posterior, the overall diversion rate $P(\text{diverted})$ app
ears in both numerator and denominator and cancels. What matters is the **ratio*
* of anomaly to grade-A chips *among diverted chips*, which depends only on the
likelihood ratio and priors, not on the marginal diversion rate.

## Computing the Posterior Among Diverted Chips

$$\frac{P(\text{Anomaly} \mid \text{diverted})}{P(\text{Grade-A} \mid \text{dive
rted})} = \frac{P(\text{diverted} \mid \text{Anomaly}) \cdot P(\text{Anomaly})}{
P(\text{diverted} \mid \text{Grade-A}) \cdot P(\text{Grade-A})} = \frac{0.95 \ti
mes 0.01}{0.05 \times 0.99} = \frac{0.0095}{0.0495} = \frac{95}{495} = \frac{19}
{99}$$

So among diverted chips, the odds of Anomaly : Grade-A = 19 : 99, giving:
$$P(\text{Anomaly} \mid \text{diverted}) = \frac{19}{118}, \qquad P(\text{Grade-
A} \mid \text{diverted}) = \frac{99}{118}$$

## Applying the Lab Audit (Bayes' Theorem)

$$P(\text{Defective} \mid \text{diverted}) = P(\text{Defective} \mid \text{Anoma
ly}) \cdot \frac{19}{118} + P(\text{Defective} \mid \text{Grade-A}) \cdot \frac{
99}{118}$$
$$= 0.90 \times \frac{19}{118} + 0.10 \times \frac{99}{118} = \frac{17.1 + 9.9}{
118} = \frac{27}{118}$$

$$P(\text{Anomaly} \mid \text{Defective}) = \frac{P(\text{Defective} \mid \text{
Anomaly}) \cdot P(\text{Anomaly} \mid \text{diverted})}{P(\text{Defective} \mid
\text{diverted})} = \frac{0.90 \times \frac{19}{118}}{\frac{27}{118}} = \frac{0.
90 \times 19}{27} = \frac{17.1}{27} = \frac{171}{270} = \frac{19}{30}$$

## Verification

- $\gcd(19, 30) = 1$ since 19 is prime and $30 = 2 \times 3 \times 5$, so $\frac
{19}{30}$ is irreducible.
- Sanity check: $\frac{19}{30} \approx 0.633$, which is reasonable — the lab is
fairly accurate (90%), and while anomalies are rare (1%), a chip reaching the la
b is already suspicious (diverted by scanner), so the posterior is meaningfully
above 50%.

## Answer

$$P(\text{Anomaly} \mid \text{Defective}) = \frac{19}{30}, \qquad a + b = 19 + 3
0 = \boxed{49}$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
