Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum number of distinct integers
$k$ (from 1 to 99) such that some $k$-subset of 100 canisters has total mass exa
ctly $S$, where the total mass of all 100 canisters is $2S$.

**Answer: 97**

## Proof

**Setup.** Let the canister masses be $a_1, \ldots, a_{100} > 0$ with $\sum a_i
= 2S$. Integer $k$ is a *Balance Point* if some $k$-element subset has sum $S$.

**Step 1: Complementarity.** If a $k$-subset $A$ has sum $S$, its complement has
 size $100-k$ and sum $2S - S = S$. So $k$ is a balance point if and only if $10
0-k$ is. Balance points thus come in pairs $\{k, 100-k\}$, plus possibly $k=50$.

**Step 2: If $k=1$ is a balance point, the only balance points are $\{1, 99\}$.*
*

If $k=1$ is a balance point, some canister has mass $S$. The remaining 99 canist
ers have total mass $S$. For any $k$ with $2 \le k \le 98$:
- A $k$-subset *including* the $S$-mass canister requires the other $k-1 \ge 1$
canisters to sum to $0$, impossible.
- A $k$-subset *excluding* it is a $k$-subset of 99 canisters (total $S$) summin
g to $S$, so its complement (within those 99) has size $99-k \ge 1$ and sum $0$,
 impossible.

So only $k=1$ and $k=99$ work — just **2** balance points.

**Step 3: Upper bound without $k=1$.** If $k=1$ (equivalently $k=99$) is not a b
alance point, the remaining candidates are $\{2, 3, \ldots, 98\}$, giving at mos
t **97** balance points. Since $97 > 2$, the optimal strategy excludes $k=1$.

**Step 4: Construction achieving 97 balance points.**

We construct 100 canisters with all $k \in \{2, 3, \ldots, 98\}$ as balance poin
ts.

*Recursive construction.* We build the set for $n$ canisters (total $2T$, target
 $T$) with balance points $\{2, \ldots, n-2\}$:

- **Base case** ($n=4$): Four canisters of mass $T/2$. Total $= 2T$. Any 2-subse
t sums to $T$. ✓
- **Inductive step** ($n \ge 6$): Take two canisters of mass $T/2$. The remainin
g $n-2$ canisters must sum to $T$, and by induction (applied with target $T/2$)
have subsets of every size in $\{2, \ldots, n-4\}$ summing to $T/2$.

  - $k=2$: the pair $\{T/2, T/2\}$ sums to $T$. ✓
  - $k=j$ for $3 \le j \le n-3$: take one $T/2$-canister plus a $(j-1)$-subset o
f the remaining $n-2$ canisters summing to $T/2$. Since $j-1 \in \{2, \ldots, n-
4\}$, this exists by induction. ✓
  - $k=n-2$: complement of $k=2$. ✓
  - $k=n-3$: complement of $k=3$. ✓

  So all $k \in \{2, \ldots, n-2\}$ are balance points.

*Applying to $n=100$:* The recursion $100 \to 98 \to \cdots \to 4$ (48 steps) pr
oduces masses:
$$\underbrace{S/2, S/2}_{2},\; \underbrace{S/4, S/4}_{2},\; \ldots,\; \underbrac
e{S/2^{48}, S/2^{48}}_{2},\; \underbrace{S/2^{49}, S/2^{49}, S/2^{49}, S/2^{49}}
_{4}.$$

**Verification of total mass:**
$$2\sum_{i=1}^{48} \frac{S}{2^i} + 4\cdot\frac{S}{2^{49}} = 2S\!\left(1 - \frac{
1}{2^{48}}\right) + \frac{4S}{2^{49}} = 2S - \frac{2S}{2^{48}} + \frac{2S}{2^{48
}} = 2S. \checkmark$$

All masses are strictly positive. By the inductive argument, every $k \in \{2, 3
, \ldots, 98\}$ is a balance point, giving **97** distinct balance points.

**Step 5: Optimality.** By Step 2, including $k=1$ yields at most 2 balance poin
ts. Excluding $k=1$ and $k=99$ leaves at most 97 candidates ($\{2,\ldots,98\}$),
 all achieved by the construction. Therefore 97 is the maximum.
