## Proof

We evaluate the contour integral
$$I = \int_{|z|=2} \frac{1}{z^2+z+1}\,dz.$$

**Step 1: Locate the singularities.** The integrand is rational with poles at th
e roots of $z^2+z+1=0$. By the quadratic formula,
$$z = \frac{-1\pm\sqrt{1-4}}{2} = \frac{-1\pm i\sqrt{3}}{2}.$$
Denote $z_1 = \frac{-1+i\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $z_2 = \frac{-1-i\sqrt{3}}{2}$. Both s
atisfy $|z_k|=1<2$, so both poles lie strictly inside the contour $|z|=2$. They
are simple poles (distinct roots of an irreducible quadratic).

**Step 2: Compute the residues.** For a simple pole $z_k$ of $1/Q(z)$ with $Q(z)
=z^2+z+1$, the residue is
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_k}\frac{1}{Q(z)} = \frac{1}{Q'(z_k)} = \frac{1}{2z_k+1
}.$$

**Step 3: Sum the residues.** Using Vieta's relations for $z^2+z+1=0$:
$$z_1+z_2 = -1, \qquad z_1 z_2 = 1.$$
Observe that
$$(2z_1+1)+(2z_2+1) = 2(z_1+z_2)+2 = 2(-1)+2 = 0,$$
so $2z_2+1 = -(2z_1+1)$. Therefore
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_1} + \operatorname{Res}_{z=z_2} = \frac{1}{2z_1+1} + \
frac{1}{2z_2+1} = \frac{1}{2z_1+1} + \frac{1}{-(2z_1+1)} = 0.$$

**Step 4: Apply the residue theorem.** Since both poles lie inside $|z|=2$ and t
he contour is positively oriented,
$$I = 2\pi i \sum_{k=1}^{2}\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_k}\frac{1}{z^2+z+1} = 2\pi i
\cdot 0 = 0.$$

**Conclusion.**
$$\boxed{\int_{|z|=2} \frac{1}{z^2+z+1}\,dz = 0}.$$
