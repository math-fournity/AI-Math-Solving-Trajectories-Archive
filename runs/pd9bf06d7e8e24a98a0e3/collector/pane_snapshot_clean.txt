## Proof

We show that $\{e_n\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}$ with $e_n(x)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2N}}\exp\!\
bigl(\tfrac{i\pi n}{N}x\bigr)$ is an orthonormal basis of $L^2(-N,N)$, by verify
ing (i) orthonormality and (ii) completeness.

### (i) Orthonormality

The inner product on $L^2(-N,N)$ is $\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{-N}^{N}f(x)\overli
ne{g(x)}\,dx$. Since $\overline{e_n(x)}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2N}}e^{-i\pi n x/N}$,

$$\langle e_m,e_n\rangle=\frac{1}{2N}\int_{-N}^{N}\exp\!\Bigl(\tfrac{i\pi(m-n)}{
N}x\Bigr)\,dx.$$

**Case $m=n$.** The integrand is $1$, so
$$\langle e_n,e_n\rangle=\frac{1}{2N}\int_{-N}^{N}1\,dx=\frac{1}{2N}\cdot 2N=1.$
$

**Case $m\neq n$.** Put $k=m-n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$. Then
$$\int_{-N}^{N}e^{i\pi k x/N}\,dx=\frac{N}{i\pi k}\Bigl[e^{i\pi k x/N}\Bigr]_{-N
}^{N}=\frac{N}{i\pi k}\bigl(e^{i\pi k}-e^{-i\pi k}\bigr)=\frac{2N\sin(\pi k)}{\p
i k}.$$

Since $k$ is a nonzero integer, $\sin(\pi k)=0$, hence $\langle e_m,e_n\rangle=0
$.

Thus $\{e_n\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}$ is an orthonormal system.

### (ii) Completeness

We prove that the orthonormal system is complete, i.e. its closed linear span is
 all of $L^2(-N,N)$. We use the **Fejér kernel / Cesàro summability** argument,
which gives $L^2$-convergence without any a priori completeness assumption.

**Reduction to continuous functions.** $C_c(-N,N)$ (equivalently $C[-N,N]$ restr
icted to the interior, with endpoints handled by periodic extension) is dense in
 $L^2(-N,N)$. It therefore suffices to show that every $2N$-periodic continuous
function $f$ on $\mathbb{R}$ is the $L^2(-N,N)$-limit of finite linear combinati
ons of the $e_n$.

**Fourier coefficients.** For $f\in C(\mathbb{T}_{2N})$ (continuous and $2N$-per
iodic), define
$$\widehat f_n=\langle f,e_n\rangle=\frac{1}{2N}\int_{-N}^{N}f(x)e^{-i\pi n x/N}
\,dx,\qquad S_N^{\text{part}}f(x)=\sum_{n=-M}^{M}\widehat f_n\,e^{i\pi n x/N}.$$

**Fejér means.** The Cesàro mean of the partial sums is
$$\sigma_M f(x)=\frac{1}{M+1}\sum_{j=0}^{M}\Bigl(\sum_{n=-j}^{j}\widehat f_n\,e^
{i\pi n x/N}\Bigr)=\sum_{n=-M}^{M}\Bigl(1-\frac{|n|}{M+1}\Bigr)\widehat f_n\,e^{
i\pi n x/N}.$$

A direct computation (convolving $f$ with the normalized Dirichlet sums) gives t
he closed form
$$\sigma_M f(x)=\frac{1}{2N}\int_{-N}^{N}f(x-t)\,F_M(t)\,dt,\qquad F_M(t)=\frac{
1}{M+1}\Bigl(\frac{\sin\!\bigl(\tfrac{(M+1)\pi t}{N}\bigr)}{\sin\!\bigl(\tfrac{\
pi t}{N}\bigr)}\Bigr)^{2},$$
the **Fejér kernel**, where the ratio is interpreted as $(M+1)^2$ when $\sin(\pi
 t/N)=0$.

**Three properties of $F_M$.**
1. $F_M(t)\ge 0$ for all $t$.
2. $\frac{1}{2N}\int_{-N}^{N}F_M(t)\,dt=1$ (each Dirichlet sum has integral $2N$
, and $\sigma_M 1 = 1$).
3. For any $\delta\in(0,N)$: $\sup_{\delta\le|t|\le N}F_M(t)\le\frac{1}{(M+1)\si
n^2(\pi\delta/N)}\to 0$ as $M\to\infty$.

**Uniform convergence.** Using properties 1–3,
\begin{align*}
|\sigma_M f(x)-f(x)|&=\Bigl|\frac{1}{2N}\int_{-N}^{N}\bigl(f(x-t)-f(x)\bigr)F_M(
t)\,dt\Bigr|\\
&\le\frac{1}{2N}\int_{|t|<\delta}|f(x-t)-f(x)|F_M(t)\,dt+\frac{1}{2N}\int_{\delt
a\le|t|\le N}|f(x-t)-f(x)|F_M(t)\,dt\\
&\le\sup_{|t|<\delta}\|f(\cdot-t)-f(\cdot)\|_\infty\cdot 1+2\|f\|_\infty\cdot\su
p_{\delta\le|t|\le N}F_M(t).
\end{align*}
By uniform continuity of $f$ on the compact torus, the first term is $<\varepsil
on/2$ for $\delta$ small (independent of $M$); then for $M$ large the second ter
m is $<\varepsilon/2$ by property 3. Hence
$$\|\sigma_M f-f\|_\infty\to 0\quad(M\to\infty).$$

**$L^2$ convergence.** Uniform convergence on $[-N,N]$ implies
$$\|\sigma_M f-f\|_{L^2(-N,N)}\le\sqrt{2N}\,\|\sigma_M f-f\|_\infty\to 0.$$
Since each $\sigma_M f$ is a finite linear combination of the $e_n$, every conti
nuous $2N$-periodic $f$ lies in $\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{e_n\}$.

**Conclusion of completeness.** Continuous $2N$-periodic functions are dense in
$L^2(-N,N)$: indeed, $C_c(-N,N)\subseteq L^2(-N,N)$ is dense, and every element
of $C_c(-N,N)$ can be $L^2$-approximated by $2N$-periodic continuous functions (
e.g. by convolving with a smooth compactly supported mollifier and periodically
extending). Therefore
$$\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{e_n:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}=L^2(-N,N).$$

### Conclusion

The family $\{e_n\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}$ is an orthonormal system (part i) whose cl
osed linear span equals $L^2(-N,N)$ (part ii). Hence it is an **orthonormal basi
s** of $L^2(-N,N)$. $\blacksquare$
