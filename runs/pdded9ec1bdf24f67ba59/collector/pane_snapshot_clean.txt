## Proof

Write $M = A + iB$ where $A, B \in \mathbb{R}^{2n \times n}$ are the real and im
aginary parts of $M$. For $w \in \mathbb{C}^n$, write $w = u + iv$ with $u, v \i
n \mathbb{R}^n$. Then

$$Mw = (A+iB)(u+iv) = (Au - Bv) + i(Av + Bu),$$

so $\operatorname{re}[Mw] = Au - Bv$. Thus we must show: for every $r \in \mathb
b{R}^{2n}$, there exist $u, v \in \mathbb{R}^n$ with

$$Au - Bv = r.$$

Equivalently, the real $2n \times 2n$ matrix $C := [\,A \mid -B\,]$ is surjectiv
e (hence invertible, being square).

**Interpreting the hypothesis.** The hypothesis states that every nonzero vector
 $z \in \mathbb{C}^{1 \times 2n}$ in the (complex) left null space of $M$ has at
 least one non-real entry. Equivalently, **no nonzero real vector** $z$ satisfie
s $zM = 0$.

For real $z$, the condition $zM = 0$ is $z(A+iB)=0$, i.e., $zA = 0$ and $zB = 0$
. Hence the hypothesis is equivalent to:

$$\{z \in \mathbb{R}^{2n} : zA = 0 \text{ and } zB = 0\} = \{0\}. \tag{$\ast$}$$

**The left null space of $C$.** A real row vector $z$ satisfies $z\,C = 0$ iff $
zA = 0$ and $z(-B) = 0$, i.e., iff $zA = 0$ and $zB = 0$. By $(\ast)$, the only
such $z$ is $z = 0$.

Therefore $C = [\,A \mid -B\,]$ has trivial left null space over $\mathbb{R}$. S
ince $C$ is a $2n \times 2n$ real matrix, trivial left null space means $C$ has
full rank $2n$, so $C$ is invertible and hence surjective.

**Conclusion.** Given any $r = (r_1, \dots, r_{2n})^T \in \mathbb{R}^{2n}$, surj
ectivity of $C$ yields $(u, v) \in \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}^n$ with $C\bin
om{u}{v} = r$, i.e., $Au - Bv = r$. Setting $w_j = u_j + i v_j$ for $j = 1, \dot
s, n$ gives

$$\operatorname{re}\!\left[M\binom{w_1}{\vdots}{w_n}\right] = Au - Bv = \binom{r
_1}{\vdots}{r_{2n}}.$$

This completes the proof. $\blacksquare$
