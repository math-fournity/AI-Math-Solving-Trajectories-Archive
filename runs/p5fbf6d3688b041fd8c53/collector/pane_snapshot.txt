## Proof

**Setup.** The cube is $C = [0,1]^3$. A circular disk of radius $r$ lies in some
 plane. Let the plane have unit normal $\mathbf{n}=(n_1,n_2,n_3)$, and let $\mat
hbf{u},\mathbf{v}$ be orthonormal vectors spanning the plane. The disk is center
ed at $\mathbf{p}=(p_1,p_2,p_3)$, so every point on its boundary is
$$\mathbf{p} + r(\cos\theta\,\mathbf{u} + \sin\theta\,\mathbf{v}), \quad \theta\
in[0,2\pi).$$

**Constraint from each face.** For the disk to lie inside the cube, for each coo
rdinate axis $i\in\{1,2,3\}$ and every $\theta$:
$$0 \;\le\; p_i + r(\cos\theta\,u_i + \sin\theta\,v_i) \;\le\; 1.$$

The maximum of $\cos\theta\,u_i+\sin\theta\,v_i$ over $\theta$ is $\sqrt{u_i^2+v
_i^2}$. Since $\{\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n}\}$ is an orthonormal basis, $u
_i^2+v_i^2 = 1-n_i^2$. Define $a_i := \sqrt{1-n_i^2}$. The two inequalities per
axis become
$$p_i + r\,a_i \le 1 \quad\text{and}\quad p_i - r\,a_i \ge 0,$$
i.e., $r\,a_i \le p_i \le 1 - r\,a_i$, which is feasible only if $r\,a_i \le \tf
rac12$.

**Optimal centering.** Choosing $\mathbf{p}=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ (the c
ube's center) makes all per-axis constraints tightest simultaneously, giving
$$r \;\le\; \frac{1}{2\,a_i} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{1-n_i^2}} \quad\text{for each } i
.$$

**Optimizing the orientation.** To maximize $r$ we minimize $\max_i a_i = \max_i
\sqrt{1-n_i^2}$, equivalently maximize $\min_i n_i^2$. Since $n_1^2+n_2^2+n_3^2=
1$, the minimum of the $n_i^2$ is maximized when they are equal:
$$n_1^2=n_2^2=n_3^2=\tfrac13, \qquad \mathbf{n}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1,1,1).$$

Then $a_i = \sqrt{1-\tfrac13}=\sqrt{\tfrac23}$ for all $i$, and
$$r = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2/3}} = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}} = \frac{\sqrt{6}}{4}
.$$

**Verification via the hexagonal cross-section.** The plane $x+y+z=\tfrac32$ (no
rmal $(1,1,1)/\sqrt{3}$, through the center) cuts the cube in a regular hexagon
with vertices $(1,\tfrac12,0),(1,0,\tfrac12),(\tfrac12,0,1),(0,\tfrac12,1),(0,1,
\tfrac12),(\tfrac12,1,0)$. The circumradius (center $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12
)$ to any vertex) is $\sqrt{(\tfrac12)^2+0+(\tfrac12)^2}=\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$. The
 inradius of a regular hexagon equals circumradius $\times \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, g
iving $\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}=\tfrac{\sqrt6}{4}$. This confirms
 the bound is achieved.

**Conclusion.** The largest circular disk contained in the unit cube has radius

$$\boxed{\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{4}}.$$
