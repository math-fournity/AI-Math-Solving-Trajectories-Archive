**Answer: Yes.** Every conformal bijection $f: D(0,1) \to D(0,1)$ is necessarily
 a Möbius transformation (homography). Below is the proof.

---

## Proof

Let $D = D(0,1)$ be the open unit disk. We interpret "conformal map $f: D \to D$
" as a biholomorphic bijection of $D$ onto itself (the standard meaning of the n
otation). The hypothesis that $f'$ is non-vanishing is in fact automatic for suc
h a map, since a bijective holomorphic map has nowhere-vanishing derivative (oth
erwise local non-injectivity would contradict global injectivity).

Let $f: D \to D$ be a biholomorphic self-map of the disk. We prove $f$ is a Möbi
us transformation.

**Step 1 — Reduce to a map fixing the origin.**

Set $a := f^{-1}(0) \in D$, so that $f(a) = 0$. Consider the disk automorphism
$$
\phi_a(z) = \frac{z - a}{1 - \overline{a}\,z},
$$
which is a Möbius transformation mapping $D$ biholomorphically onto $D$ and send
ing $a \mapsto 0$. Its inverse is $\phi_a^{-1} = \phi_{-a}$.

Define
$$
h := f \circ \phi_a^{-1} : D \longrightarrow D.
$$
Then $h$ is a biholomorphic self-map of $D$ (composition of two biholomorphic se
lf-maps), and
$$
h(0) = f\bigl(\phi_a^{-1}(0)\bigr) = f(a) = 0.
$$
So $h$ is a biholomorphic self-map of $D$ fixing the origin.

**Step 2 — Apply the Schwarz lemma in both directions.**

Since $h: D \to D$ is holomorphic and $h(0)=0$, the **Schwarz lemma** gives
$$
|h(z)| \le |z| \quad \text{for all } z \in D, \qquad |h'(0)| \le 1. \tag{1}
$$

The inverse $h^{-1}: D \to D$ is also holomorphic and satisfies $h^{-1}(0)=0$, s
o the Schwarz lemma applied to $h^{-1}$ gives
$$
|h^{-1}(w)| \le |w| \quad \text{for all } w \in D. \tag{2}
$$
Writing $w = h(z)$ in (2) yields $|z| \le |h(z)|$ for all $z \in D$.

Combining with (1):
$$
|h(z)| = |z| \quad \text{for all } z \in D. \tag{3}
$$

**Step 3 — Use the equality case of the Schwarz lemma.**

Equality in the Schwarz lemma (either $|h(z)|=|z|$ for some $z\ne 0$, or $|h'(0)
|=1$) forces $h$ to be a rotation:
$$
h(z) = e^{i\theta}\, z \qquad (\theta \in \mathbb{R}). \tag{4}
$$

**Step 4 — Conclude.**

From $h = f \circ \phi_a^{-1}$ we obtain $f = h \circ \phi_a$, hence by (4):
$$
\boxed{\,f(z) = e^{i\theta}\,\frac{z - a}{1 - \overline{a}\,z}\,}, \qquad |a|<1,
\ \theta\in\mathbb{R}.
$$

This is a Möbius transformation (homography): it is a ratio of two affine-linear
 functions of $z$ (with the conjugate $\overline{a}$ appearing only as a constan
t coefficient, which is fixed for a given map).

**Conclusion.** Every conformal bijection of the unit disk onto itself is of the
 form $e^{i\theta}\frac{z-a}{1-\overline{a}z}$, hence is a homography. The hypot
hesis of non-vanishing derivative is automatically satisfied and does not need t
o be assumed separately. $\blacksquare$
