## Proof

The packet starts at $(0,0,0)$ and must reach $(2,2,2)$, moving only in the thre
e positive coordinate directions (right = $+x$, up = $+y$, deep = $+z$). Each mo
ve advances exactly one unit along one axis.

**Step count.** To go from coordinate $0$ to coordinate $2$ along each of the th
ree axes, the packet must make exactly $2$ right-moves, $2$ up-moves, and $2$ de
ep-moves. The total number of moves is therefore

$$2 + 2 + 2 = 6.$$

Every shortest path consists of exactly these $6$ moves; the only freedom is the
 **order** in which the three types of moves are arranged.

**Counting arrangements.** A path is completely determined by choosing which $2$
 of the $6$ positions are right-moves, which $2$ of the remaining $4$ are up-mov
es (the last $2$ being deep-moves). The number of distinct orderings of a multis
et with $2$ copies each of three distinct symbols is the multinomial coefficient

$$\frac{6!}{2!\;2!\;2!} = \frac{720}{2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2} = \frac{720}{8} = 90.$$

**Equivalently**, choosing positions directly:

$$\binom{6}{2}\binom{4}{2}\binom{2}{2} = 15 \cdot 6 \cdot 1 = 90.$$

**Validity of every arrangement.** Since every move is in a positive axis direct
ion, the packet never leaves the grid $[0,2]^3$ (coordinates stay between $0$ an
d $2$), and every ordering of the $6$ moves produces a valid grid path from $(0,
0,0)$ to $(2,2,2)$. No arrangement is excluded, so the count is exact.

$$\boxed{90}$$
