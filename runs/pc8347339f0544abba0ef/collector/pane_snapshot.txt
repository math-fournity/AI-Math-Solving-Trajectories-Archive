 \to S^n(1)$ with $f(q) = p$ for some $q$ and $df_q(T_q S^m) = V$.

**Answer: Yes, always.**

**Proof.**

Let $p \in S^n \subset \mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ and let $V \subset T_p S^n$ be an $m$-d
imensional subspace. Recall that $T_p S^n = p^\perp = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^{n+1} :
 \langle x, p \rangle = 0\}$, so $V \subset p^\perp$.

**Key observation:** Since $V \subset p^\perp$, every $v \in V$ is orthogonal to
 $p$. Since $p \neq 0$, the vector $p$ is linearly independent of $V$. Therefore
$$W := \operatorname{span}(p, V) \subset \mathbb{R}^{n+1}$$
is an $(m+1)$-dimensional linear subspace.

**Construction.** Define
$$\Sigma := S^n \cap W = \{x \in S^n : x \in W\}.$$
Since $W$ is an $(m+1)$-dimensional linear subspace of $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ passin
g through the origin, $\Sigma = W \cap S^n$ is a **great $m$-sphere**: it is the
 unit sphere in $W \cong \mathbb{R}^{m+1}$.

**$\Sigma$ is isometric to $S^m(1)$.** The subspace $W \cong \mathbb{R}^{m+1}$ i
nherits the Euclidean inner product from $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$, and $\Sigma = \{x \
in W : \|x\| = 1\}$ is the unit sphere in $W$. The Riemannian metric on $\Sigma$
 induced from $S^n(1)$ is the same as the metric induced from $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$
 restricted to $W$, which is the standard round metric of constant sectional cur
vature $1$ on $S^m(1)$. Thus $\Sigma$ is isometric to the unit sphere $S^m(1)$.

**The tangent space at $p$ is exactly $V$.** The point $p$ lies in $\Sigma$ (sin
ce $p \in S^n$ and $p \in W$). The tangent space to $\Sigma$ at $p$ is:
$$T_p \Sigma = W \cap p^\perp = \operatorname{span}(p, V) \cap p^\perp.$$
Any vector in $\operatorname{span}(p, V) \cap p^\perp$ has the form $ap + v$ wit
h $a \in \mathbb{R}$, $v \in V$, and $\langle ap + v, p \rangle = a\|p\|^2 = a =
 0$. So $a = 0$ and the vector is just $v \in V$. Conversely, every $v \in V$ li
es in $W \cap p^\perp$. Therefore:
$$T_p \Sigma = V.$$

**The embedding.** The inclusion map $\iota: \Sigma \hookrightarrow S^n$ is a sm
ooth embedding. Since $\Sigma$ with the induced metric is isometric to $S^m(1)$,
 composing with any isometry $\phi: S^m(1) \to \Sigma$ (sending some point $q \i
n S^m$ to $p$) gives an isometric embedding:
$$f = \iota \circ \phi: S^m(1) \to S^n(1)$$
with $f(q) = p$ and $df_q(T_q S^m) = T_p \Sigma = V$.

**Conclusion.** For **any** point $p \in S^n$ and **any** $m$-dimensional subspa
ce $V \subset T_p S^n$ (with $m < n$), the great $m$-sphere $\Sigma = S^n \cap \
operatorname{span}(p, V)$ provides an isometric embedding of $S^m(1)$ into $S^n(
1)$ whose tangent space at $p$ is exactly $V$. The construction works for every
choice of $V$, so the answer is **yes, it is always possible**.
