# Solution

## Setting Up the Folding

Let the triangle have vertices $A$, $B$, $C$ with sides $a = BC = 14$, $b = CA =
 12$, $c = AB = 10$. Let $M_a, M_b, M_c$ be the midpoints of $BC, CA, AB$ respec
tively, and $G$ the centroid.

The three medians $AM_a$, $BM_b$, $CM_c$ (all passing through $G$) divide the tr
iangle into 6 small triangles. Folding along these medians creates a tetrahedron
.

## Identifying the Tetrahedron's Vertices and Edges

**Vertices $A, B, C$ collapse to one point.** Each side of the triangle is split
 by a midpoint: $AB = AM_c + M_cB$, $BC = BM_a + M_aC$, $CA = CM_b + M_bA$. When
 we fold along median $CM_c$, the segments $AM_c$ and $BM_c$ (both equal to $c/2
 = 5$) coincide, bringing $A$ and $B$ together. Similarly, folding along $AM_a$
brings $B$ and $C$ together (via $BM_a = CM_a = 7$), and folding along $BM_b$ br
ings $C$ and $A$ together (via $CM_b = AM_b = 6$). So $A, B, C$ all map to a sin
gle vertex $V$.

The four vertices of the tetrahedron are $V, M_a, M_b, M_c$.

**Edge lengths.** The six edges are:

| Edge | Source | Length |
|------|--------|--------|
| $VM_a$ | $BM_a = CM_a = a/2$ | $7$ |
| $VM_b$ | $AM_b = CM_b = b/2$ | $6$ |
| $VM_c$ | $AM_c = BM_c = c/2$ | $5$ |
| $M_aM_b$ | midsegment $\parallel AB$ | $c/2 = 5$ |
| $M_bM_c$ | midsegment $\parallel BC$ | $a/2 = 7$ |
| $M_cM_a$ | midsegment $\parallel CA$ | $b/2 = 6$ |

**Opposite edges are equal:** $VM_a = M_bM_c = 7$, $\;VM_b = M_cM_a = 6$, $\;VM_
c = M_aM_b = 5$. This is a **disphenoid** (equifacial tetrahedron) with all four
 faces congruent $5$-$6$-$7$ triangles.

## Computing the Circumradius

A disphenoid with edge lengths $p, q, r$ (each appearing as a pair of opposite e
dges) can be realized with vertices at
$$(x,y,z),\quad (x,-y,-z),\quad (-x,y,-z),\quad (-x,-y,z),$$
where the three distinct edge lengths satisfy:
$$p = 2\sqrt{y^2+z^2},\quad q = 2\sqrt{x^2+z^2},\quad r = 2\sqrt{x^2+y^2}.$$

All four vertices lie at distance $R = \sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}$ from the origin (the
circumcenter), so:

$$p^2 + q^2 + r^2 = 4(y^2+z^2) + 4(x^2+z^2) + 4(x^2+y^2) = 8(x^2+y^2+z^2) = 8R^2
.$$

With $p=7, q=6, r=5$:

$$R^2 = \frac{49+36+25}{8} = \frac{110}{8} = \frac{55}{4}, \qquad R = \frac{\sqr
t{55}}{2}.$$

## Verification

Solving for $x^2, y^2, z^2$:
- $x^2 = \frac{(x^2+y^2)+(x^2+z^2)-(y^2+z^2)}{2} = \frac{25/4+9-49/4}{2} = \frac
{3}{2} > 0$ ✓
- $y^2 = \frac{19}{4} > 0$ ✓, $\quad z^2 = \frac{15}{2} > 0$ ✓

All positive, confirming the disphenoid exists. The face triangle inequality $5+
6>7$ is also satisfied. ✓

## Result

The **diameter** of the circumscribed sphere is:

$$\boxed{\sqrt{55}}$$
