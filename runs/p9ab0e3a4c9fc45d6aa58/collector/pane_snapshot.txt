## Proof

**Yes**, the size of jumps in a Gauss-Markov semimartingale can be random.

### Construction

Let $B=(B_t)_{0\le t\le 1}$ be a standard Brownian motion, let $Z\sim N(0,1)$ be
 independent of $B$, and fix $t_0\in(0,1)$. Define

$$X_t = B_t + Z\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{t\ge t_0\}},\qquad 0\le t\le 1.$$

The process $X$ has a single jump at time $t_0$ of size $\Delta X_{t_0}=Z$, whic
h is a non-degenerate (random) Gaussian random variable. I claim $X$ is a Gauss-
Markov semimartingale with respect to its natural filtration $\mathcal{F}_t^X=\s
igma(X_s:s\le t)$.

### 1. $X$ is Gaussian

For any $t_1,\dots,t_n$, the vector $(X_{t_1},\dots,X_{t_n})$ is a linear transf
ormation of the jointly Gaussian vector $(B_{t_1},\dots,B_{t_n},Z)$, since $X_{t
_i}=B_{t_i}+Z\cdot\mathbf{1}_{t_i\ge t_0}$. Hence $X$ is a Gaussian process.

### 2. $X$ is Markov

For a Gaussian process, the Markov property is equivalent to matching conditiona
l means and variances for all $s<t$:

$$E[X_t\mid\mathcal{F}_s^X]=E[X_t\mid X_s],\qquad \operatorname{Var}(X_t\mid\mat
hcal{F}_s^X)=\operatorname{Var}(X_t\mid X_s).$$

**Case 1: $s<t<t_0$.** Here $X=B$ on $[0,t_0)$, so this is the Markov property o
f Brownian motion. ✓

**Case 2: $s<t_0\le t$.** Here $\mathcal{F}_s^X=\mathcal{F}_s^B$ (since $X_u=B_u
$ for $u\le s$), and $X_t=B_t+Z$ with $Z\perp\mathcal{F}_s^B$.

- $E[X_t\mid\mathcal{F}_s^X]=E[B_t+Z\mid\mathcal{F}_s^B]=B_s=X_s$.
- $E[X_t\mid X_s]=E[B_t+Z\mid B_s]=B_s=X_s$ (since $Z\perp B_s$, $E[Z]=0$).
- $\operatorname{Var}(X_t\mid\mathcal{F}_s^X)=\operatorname{Var}(B_t+Z\mid\mathc
al{F}_s^B)=(t-s)+1$.
- $\operatorname{Var}(X_t\mid X_s)=(t+1)-\frac{s^2}{s}=(t-s)+1$. ✓

**Case 3: $t_0\le s<t$.** Here $B_{t_0}=\lim_{u\uparrow t_0}X_u$ is $\mathcal{F}
_s^X$-measurable, and $Z=X_{t_0}-B_{t_0}$, so $\mathcal{F}_s^X=\mathcal{F}_s^B\v
ee\sigma(Z)$.

- $E[X_t\mid\mathcal{F}_s^X]=E[B_t+Z\mid\mathcal{F}_s^B\vee\sigma(Z)]=B_s+Z=X_s$
.
- $E[X_t\mid X_s]$: Since $\operatorname{Cov}(B_t+Z,\,B_s+Z)=s+1=\operatorname{V
ar}(B_s+Z)$, we get $E[B_t+Z\mid B_s+Z]=X_s$.
- $\operatorname{Var}(X_t\mid\mathcal{F}_s^X)=\operatorname{Var}(B_t\mid\mathcal
{F}_s^B\vee\sigma(Z))=t-s$ (Brownian motion remains a martingale under independe
nt initial enlargement).
- $\operatorname{Var}(X_t\mid X_s)=(t+1)-\frac{(s+1)^2}{s+1}=t-s$. ✓

All cases match, so $X$ is Markov.

### 3. $X$ is a semimartingale

Decompose $X_t = B_t + A_t$ where $A_t = Z\,\mathbf{1}_{t\ge t_0}$.

- **$B$ is a local martingale w.r.t. $\mathcal{F}_t^X$**: For $t<t_0$, $\mathcal
{F}_t^X=\mathcal{F}_t^B$. For $t\ge t_0$, $\mathcal{F}_t^X=\mathcal{F}_t^B\vee\s
igma(Z)$ where $Z$ is independent of $B$. By the initial enlargement theorem (Ja
cod), enlarging by an independent $\sigma$-algebra preserves the semimartingale
property; in fact $B$ remains a Brownian motion (hence martingale) under $\mathc
al{F}_t^B\vee\sigma(Z)$.
- **$A$ is a finite-variation process adapted to $\mathcal{F}_t^X$**: $A$ has a
single jump of size $Z$ at $t_0$, and $Z$ is $\mathcal{F}_{t_0}^X$-measurable, s
o $A$ is adapted with total variation $|Z|<\infty$ a.s.

Thus $X=B+A$ is a semimartingale decomposition with respect to $\mathcal{F}_t^X$
.

### Conclusion

$X$ is a Gauss-Markov semimartingale whose jump $\Delta X_{t_0}=Z\sim N(0,1)$ is
 random. Therefore, **the size of jumps in a Gauss-Markov semimartingale can be
random**.
