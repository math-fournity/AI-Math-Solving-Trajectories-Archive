# Problem

A specialized logistics drone follows a strict recharging protocol as it moves through a series of stations indexed $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$. The drone’s battery capacity at station $n$ is denoted by an integer $a_n$.

To determine the battery capacity at the next station ($a_{n+1}$), the drone follows this calculation: it takes its current capacity $a_n$ and determines how many full units of size $n$ it contains (the floor of the quotient $a_n/n$). It then multiplies this number of units by $n$ and adds exactly $1$ to the result to reach the capacity $a_{n+1}$.

The drone’s capacity at every station must always be a positive integer. If the drone reaches station 30 and its battery capacity is measured to be exactly $a_{30} = 30$, how many different possible values could the initial capacity $a_1$ have been?
