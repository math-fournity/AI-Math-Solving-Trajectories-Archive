## Solution

**Setup.** Let triangle $ABC$ be scalene and acute, with angles $A, B, C$ (in de
grees), $A+B+C=180°$.

- $T$ is the **Fermat point** (first isogonic center $X_{13}$): the unique inter
ior point with $\angle ATB = \angle BTC = \angle CTA = 120°$ (the third equality
 follows automatically from the first two).
- $E$ is the center of the circle through the midpoints of $AB, BC, CA$ — i.e.,
the center of the **nine-point circle**, the nine-point center $X_5$.

**Barycentric coordinates.** Using trilinear-to-barycentric conversion (multiply
 by side lengths $a,b,c$):

$$T = \left(\frac{a}{\sin(A+60°)} : \frac{b}{\sin(B+60°)} : \frac{c}{\sin(C+60°)
}\right), \qquad E = \bigl(a\cos(B{-}C) : b\cos(C{-}A) : c\cos(A{-}B)\bigr).$$

**Collinearity condition.** $B = (0:1:0)$. The points $B, T, E$ are collinear if
f

$$\det\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ t_A & t_B & t_C \\ n_A & n_B & n_C \end{pmatr
ix} = 0 \;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\; t_A\, n_C = t_C\, n_A.$$

Substituting:

$$\frac{a\cos(A-B)}{\sin(A+60°)} = \frac{c\cos(B-C)}{\sin(C+60°)},$$

and since $ac\neq 0$:

$$\cos(A-B)\,\sin(C+60°) = \cos(B-C)\,\sin(A+60°). \tag{$\star$}$$

**Expanding via the product-to-sum identity** $\cos X\sin Y = \tfrac12[\sin(Y+X)
+\sin(Y-X)]$, and using $A+B+C=180°$:

| Term | Argument | Simplified |
|------|----------|------------|
| $\sin(C+60°+A-B)$ | $C+A+60°-B = (180°-B)+60°-B$ | $240°-2B$ |
| $\sin(C+60°-A+B)$ | $C-A+B+60° = (180°-2A)+60°$ | $240°-2A$ |
| $\sin(A+60°+B-C)$ | $A+B+60°-C = (180°-C)+60°-C$ | $240°-2C$ |
| $\sin(A+60°-B+C)$ | $A-B+C+60° = (180°-2B)+60°$ | $240°-2B$ |

So ($\star$) becomes:

$$\sin(240°-2B) + \sin(240°-2A) = \sin(240°-2C) + \sin(240°-2B),$$

which simplifies to:

$$\sin(240°-2A) = \sin(240°-2C).$$

**Solving.** Either $240°-2A = 240°-2C \Rightarrow A=C$ (rejected — triangle is
scalene), or

$$240°-2A = 180°-(240°-2C) \;\Longrightarrow\; 2(A+C) = 300° \;\Longrightarrow\;
 A+C = 150°.$$

Since $A+C = 180°-B$:

$$180°-B = 150° \;\Longrightarrow\; \boxed{B = 30°}.$$

**Verification.** Take $B=30°, A=80°, C=70°$ (scalene, acute). Then
$$\text{LHS} = \cos 50°\sin 130° = \cos 50°\sin 50° = \tfrac12\sin 100°,$$
$$\text{RHS} = \cos(-40°)\sin 140° = \cos 40°\sin 40° = \tfrac12\sin 80°.$$
Since $\sin 100°=\sin 80°$, LHS $=$ RHS. ✓

The measure of the interior angle at outpost $B$ is $\angle ABC = 30°$.
