## Proof

**Notation.** All indices are cyclic modulo $n$. Let $S = \sum_{i=1}^n x_i x_{i+
1}$ and $T = \sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - x_{i+1})^2$.

### Step 1: Reformulation

Since
$$\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - x_{i+1})^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2 + \sum_{i=1}^n x_{i+1}^2 -
 2\sum_{i=1}^n x_i x_{i+1} = 2\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2 - 2S,$$
we obtain
$$S = \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2 - \frac{T}{2}.$$

The sum $\sum x_i^2 = \sum_{k=1}^n k^2 = \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}$ is fixed regard
less of the arrangement. Therefore **maximizing $S$ is equivalent to minimizing
$T$**.

### Step 2: A lower bound on total variation

**Lemma 1.** For any cyclic permutation of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$, $\displaystyle\sum_
{i=1}^n |x_i - x_{i+1}| \geq 2(n-1).$

*Proof.* Let $U = \sum_{x_{i+1}>x_i}(x_{i+1}-x_i)$ and $D = \sum_{x_{i+1}<x_i}(x
_i-x_{i+1})$ be the total upward and downward movement. Since the arrangement is
 cyclic (a closed walk), $U = D$, so $\sum|x_i-x_{i+1}| = 2U$.

The value $n$ appears somewhere and $1$ appears somewhere. Traversing the cycle
from the position of $1$ to the position of $n$, the net change is $n-1$, which
is a lower bound on the upward movement along that arc. Hence $U \geq n-1$, givi
ng $\sum|x_i-x_{i+1}| \geq 2(n-1)$. $\square$

### Step 3: A lower bound on $T$

Set $d_i = |x_i - x_{i+1}|$. Each $d_i \geq 1$ (distinct adjacent values) and, b
y Lemma 1, $\sum d_i \geq 2(n-1)$.

Write $d_i = 1 + e_i$ with $e_i \geq 0$ integers. Then $\sum e_i = \sum d_i - n
\geq 2(n-1)-n = n-2$, and
$$T = \sum d_i^2 = \sum(1+e_i)^2 = n + 2\sum e_i + \sum e_i^2.$$

**Lemma 2.** For non-negative integers $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ with $\sum e_i \geq n-2$
, we have $2\sum e_i + \sum e_i^2 \geq 3(n-2)$, with equality iff exactly $n-2$
of the $e_i$ equal $1$ and the rest equal $0$.

*Proof.* Since $e_i^2 \geq e_i$ for non-negative integers (with equality iff $e_
i \in \{0,1\}$), we get $\sum e_i^2 \geq \sum e_i \geq n-2$. Hence $2\sum e_i +
\sum e_i^2 \geq 2(n-2)+(n-2) = 3(n-2)$. Equality requires $\sum e_i = n-2$ and e
very $e_i \in\{0,1\}$, i.e., exactly $n-2$ of them are $1$. $\square$

Combining: $T \geq n + 3(n-2) = 4n - 6$.

### Step 4: The bound is achieved

Consider the arrangement (indices cyclic):
$$\boxed{1,\; 3,\; 5,\; \ldots,\; 2\!\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rceil\!-1,\;
2\!\left\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rfloor,\; 2\!\left\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rf
loor\!-2,\; \ldots,\; 4,\; 2}$$
That is, the odd numbers in increasing order followed by the even numbers in dec
reasing order.

- **Even $n=2m$:** the cycle is $1,3,5,\ldots,2m\!-\!1,\,2m,\,2m\!-\!2,\ldots,4,
2$. The successive differences are $2,\ldots,2$ ($m-1$ times), $1$, $2,\ldots,2$
 ($m-1$ times), $1$. Total: $n-2$ differences of $2$ and $2$ differences of $1$.

- **Odd $n=2m+1$:** the cycle is $1,3,5,\ldots,2m\!+\!1,\,2m,\,2m\!-\!2,\ldots,4
,2$. The successive differences are $2,\ldots,2$ ($m$ times), $1$, $2,\ldots,2$
($m-1$ times), $1$. Total: $n-2$ differences of $2$ and $2$ differences of $1$.

In both cases $T = 4(n-2)+2\cdot 1 = 4n-6$, matching the lower bound.

### Step 5: The maximum value

$$S_{\max} = \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} - \frac{4n-6}{2} = \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} -
2n + 3 = \frac{2n^3+3n^2-11n+18}{6}.$$

**Verification for small $n$:**
- $n=2$: $\frac{16+12-22+18}{6}=\frac{24}{6}=4$. Arrangement $(1,2)$: $1\cdot2+2
\cdot1=4$. ✓
- $n=3$: $\frac{54+27-33+18}{6}=\frac{66}{6}=11$. Arrangement $(1,2,3)$: $2+6+3=
11$. ✓
- $n=4$: $\frac{128+48-44+18}{6}=\frac{150}{6}=25$. Arrangement $(1,3,4,2)$: $3+
12+8+2=25$. ✓

**Integrality:** $2n^3+3n^2-11n+18 \equiv n(2n+1)(n+1) \pmod{6}$, and among $n,\
,n+1,\,2n+1$ there is always a multiple of $2$ and a multiple of $3$, so the exp
ression is always divisible by $6$.

### Conclusion

The largest possible value is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{2n^3+3n^2-11n+18}{6}},$$

achieved by the cyclic arrangement $1,3,5,\ldots,2\lceil n/2\rceil-1,\;2\lfloor
n/2\rfloor,\;2\lfloor n/2\rfloor-2,\;\ldots,\;4,\;2$.
