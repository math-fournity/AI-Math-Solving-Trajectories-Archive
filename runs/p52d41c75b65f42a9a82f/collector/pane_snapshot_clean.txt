## Solution

Let the $n$ points be represented by unit vectors $v_1, v_2, \ldots, v_n \in \ma
thbb{R}^3$ with $|v_i| = 1$ (since they lie on a sphere of radius $1$).

**The quantity to maximize is**
$$S = \sum_{1 \le i < j \le n} |v_i - v_j|^2.$$

**Key identity.** Expanding each term,
$$|v_i - v_j|^2 = |v_i|^2 + |v_j|^2 - 2\, v_i \cdot v_j.$$

Summing over all pairs $i < j$:
- Each $|v_i|^2 = 1$ appears in exactly $(n-1)$ pairs (paired with every other i
ndex), so
  $$\sum_{i<j} (|v_i|^2 + |v_j|^2) = (n-1)\sum_{i=1}^n |v_i|^2 = (n-1)\cdot n.$$
- Also $\sum_{i<j} 2\,v_i \cdot v_j = \left|\sum_{i=1}^n v_i\right|^2 - \sum_{i=
1}^n |v_i|^2 = \left|\sum_i v_i\right|^2 - n.$

Therefore
$$S = n(n-1) - \left(\left|\sum_i v_i\right|^2 - n\right) = n(n-1) + n - \left|\
sum_i v_i\right|^2 = n^2 - \left|\sum_{i=1}^n v_i\right|^2.$$

**Upper bound.** Since $\left|\sum_i v_i\right|^2 \ge 0$,
$$S \le n^2,$$
with equality if and only if $\sum_{i=1}^n v_i = 0$.

**Achievability.** It remains to show that $n$ unit vectors on the sphere can su
m to zero.

- For $n = 1$: there are no pairs, so $S = 0 = 1^2 - |v_1|^2 = 0$; the bound $n^
2 = 1$ is *not* attained, but the formula $S = n^2 - |\sum v_i|^2$ gives the cor
rect value $0$ since $|\sum v_i|^2 = 1$ is forced. So the maximum for $n=1$ is $
0$.
- For $n \ge 2$: place the $n$ points as the vertices of a **regular $n$-gon ins
cribed in the equator** (a great circle of the sphere). Each vertex is a unit ve
ctor, and by symmetry their vector sum is $\sum_i v_i = 0$. Hence $S = n^2$ is a
ttained.

(For $n = 2$ this is a pair of antipodal points; for $n = 3$ an equilateral tria
ngle on a great circle; etc.)

**Conclusion.** For $n \ge 2$, the largest possible value of the sum of the squa
res of the pairwise distances is

$$\boxed{n^2}.$$

(For $n = 1$ the sum is vacuously $0$.)
