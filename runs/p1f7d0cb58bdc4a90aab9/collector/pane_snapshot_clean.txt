## Proof

A cube has **8 vertices** and **12 edges**. At each vertex, exactly 3 edges meet
, and these 3 edges are mutually perpendicular (they lie along the three orthogo
nal axes of the cube).

**Counting at each vertex:**

At each vertex, the 3 edges form pairwise right angles. The number of such pairs
 is:

$$\binom{3}{2} = 3 \text{ right angles per vertex}$$

**No double-counting:** Any two distinct edges of a cube share **at most one** v
ertex. Therefore each pair of perpendicular edges is counted at exactly one vert
ex, and no right angle is counted twice.

**Total from vertices:**

$$8 \text{ vertices} \times 3 \text{ right angles/vertex} = 24$$

**Verification via faces:**

Each of the 6 faces is a square with 4 right angles at its 4 corners:

$$6 \text{ faces} \times 4 \text{ right angles/face} = 24$$

Each face-corner right angle corresponds to exactly one vertex right angle — spe
cifically, the angle between the two edges of that face meeting at that vertex.
The two counting methods (by vertices and by faces) identify the **same** 24 rig
ht angles, confirming the count.

**On the "additional 90-degree relationships" from face orientation:**

The square structure of each face is precisely *what makes* adjacent edges perpe
ndicular at each corner. The face-based perspective does not introduce new right
 angles beyond the 24 at the vertices; it provides an equivalent characterizatio
n of the same 24 right angles. On any square face, the only right angles are at
the 4 corners (where edges meet at vertices) — opposite edges are parallel, not
perpendicular, so no additional right angles arise in the interior of a face. Li
kewise, edges on different faces that do not share a vertex are skew lines (they
 do not intersect), so they do not form right angles by intersection.

**Conclusion:**

The total number of right angles formed by the intersection of the struts throug
hout the entire cubic structure is:

$$\boxed{24}$$
