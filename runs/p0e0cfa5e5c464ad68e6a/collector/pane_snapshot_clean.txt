Yes, the statement is **true** (with the standard normalization of Hausdorff mea
sure). Here is the proof.

---

## Theorem

Let $(M^n, g)$ be an $n$-dimensional Riemannian manifold with Riemannian measure
 $\mu$ (the volume measure induced by the volume form $dV_g = \sqrt{\det g}\, dx
^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^n$). Let $H^n$ denote the $n$-dimensional Hausdorff meas
ure on $M$ defined using the Riemannian distance $d_g$, with the normalization c
onstant
$$c_n = \frac{\omega_n}{2^n}, \qquad \omega_n = \operatorname{vol}(B_{\mathbb{R}
^n}(0,1)),$$
so that $H^n$ agrees with Lebesgue measure on $(\mathbb{R}^n, g_{\text{eucl}})$.
 Then
$$\mu = H^n.$$

---

## Proof

Both $\mu$ and $H^n$ are locally finite Borel (in fact Radon) measures on $M$. W
e prove their equality via the **Besicovitch differentiation theorem**, computin
g the local density ratio at every point.

### Step 1. Normal coordinates and the volume asymptotics

Fix $p \in M$ and let $\exp_p : T_p M \to M$ be the exponential map, which is a
diffeomorphism from a neighborhood of $0 \in T_p M \cong \mathbb{R}^n$ onto a ne
ighborhood $U$ of $p$. In these **normal coordinates** the metric satisfies
$$g_{ij}(0) = \delta_{ij}, \qquad \partial_k g_{ij}(0) = 0,$$
so the volume density $\theta(x) := \sqrt{\det g(x)}$ has the expansion
$$\theta(x) = 1 + O(|x|^2) \quad \text{as } |x| \to 0.$$

(Equivalently, in polar coordinates $x = r u$ with $u \in S^{n-1}$, the Jacobian
 of $\exp_p$ is $\theta(r,u)\, r^{n-1}$ with $\theta(r,u) = 1 - \tfrac{1}{6}\ope
ratorname{Ric}_p(u,u)\, r^2 + O(r^3)$.)

A key fact: in normal coordinates the **metric ball is exactly the Euclidean bal
l**,
$$B_g(p, r) = \exp_p\bigl(\{v \in T_p M : |v|_g < r\}\bigr),$$
because radial geodesics are minimizing and $d_g(p, \exp_p(v)) = |v|_g$ for $|v|
$ small.

### Step 2. Asymptotics of $\mu(B_g(p,r))$

$$\mu\bigl(B_g(p,r)\bigr) = \int_{|x|<r} \theta(x)\, dx = \int_0^r \int_{S^{n-1}
} \theta(t,u)\, t^{n-1}\, du\, dt = \omega_n\, r^n\bigl(1 + O(r^2)\bigr).$$

### Step 3. Asymptotics of $H^n(B_g(p,r))$

Near $p$, the Riemannian distance $d_g$ and the Euclidean distance $|\cdot|$ in
normal coordinates are uniformly comparable with distortion $1 + O(r^2)$:
$$d_g(x, y) = |x - y|\bigl(1 + O(r^2)\bigr), \qquad x, y \in B_{\text{eucl}}(0,
r),$$
since $g_{ij} = \delta_{ij} + O(r^2)$. Consequently, for any set $A \subset B_{\
text{eucl}}(0,r)$, its $d_g$-diameter and its Euclidean diameter satisfy
$$\operatorname{diam}_g(A) = \operatorname{diam}_{\text{eucl}}(A)\bigl(1 + O(r^2
)\bigr).$$

Applying this to the computation of $H^n$ (which is a limit of covers by sets of
 small $d_g$-diameter), and using that $H^n$ on $(\mathbb{R}^n, g_{\text{eucl}})
$ equals Lebesgue measure by the choice of $c_n$:
$$H^n_g\bigl(B_g(p,r)\bigr) = \omega_n\, r^n \bigl(1 + O(r^2)\bigr).$$

### Step 4. The density ratio

Combining Steps 2 and 3, for **every** $p \in M$:
$$\frac{\mu\bigl(B_g(p,r)\bigr)}{H^n\bigl(B_g(p,r)\bigr)} = \frac{\omega_n r^n (
1 + O(r^2))}{\omega_n r^n (1 + O(r^2))} \longrightarrow 1 \quad \text{as } r \to
 0.$$
By symmetry the reciprocal ratio $H^n(B_g(p,r))/\mu(B_g(p,r)) \to 1$ as well.

### Step 5. Application of the Besicovitch differentiation theorem

The **Besicovitch covering theorem** holds on Riemannian manifolds (they are loc
ally bi-Lipschitz equivalent to $\mathbb{R}^n$ via coordinate charts, and the di
rectionally-limited covering property is preserved). Hence the **Besicovitch dif
ferentiation theorem** applies: for any two locally finite Borel measures $\alph
a, \beta$ on $M$, the limit
$$D(\alpha, \beta)(p) := \lim_{r \to 0} \frac{\alpha(B(p,r))}{\beta(B(p,r))}$$
exists for $\beta$-a.e. $p$, and $d\alpha_a / d\beta = D(\alpha,\beta)$, where $
\alpha_a$ is the part of $\alpha$ absolutely continuous with respect to $\beta$
(so $\alpha = \alpha_a + \alpha_s$ with $\alpha_s \perp \beta$).

Apply this with $(\alpha, \beta) = (\mu, H^n)$. By Step 4, $D(\mu, H^n)(p) = 1$
for every $p$, hence $H^n$-a.e. Therefore
$$\mu_a = H^n, \qquad \mu = H^n + \mu_s, \quad \mu_s \perp H^n. \tag{$\ast$}$$

Apply the theorem again with $(\alpha, \beta) = (H^n, \mu)$. By the symmetric pa
rt of Step 4, $D(H^n, \mu)(p) = 1$ for every $p$, hence $\mu$-a.e. Therefore
$$H^n_a = \mu, \qquad H^n = \mu + \sigma_s, \quad \sigma_s \perp \mu. \tag{$\ast
\ast$}$$

Substituting $(\ast\ast)$ into $(\ast)$:
$$\mu = (\mu + \sigma_s) + \mu_s = \mu + \sigma_s + \mu_s \implies \sigma_s + \m
u_s = 0.$$
Since $\sigma_s$ and $\mu_s$ are **positive** measures, $\sigma_s = \mu_s = 0$.
Hence
$$\boxed{\mu = H^n.}$$

---

## Remark on normalization

The normalization constant $c_n = \omega_n / 2^n$ in the definition of $H^n$ is
essential for the equality $\mu = H^n$. It is chosen precisely so that $H^n$ coi
ncides with Lebesgue measure on Euclidean $\mathbb{R}^n$. If one uses the **unno
rmalized** Hausdorff measure $\widetilde{H}^n$ (with $c_n = 1$, i.e. $\widetilde
{H}^n_\delta(E) = \inf \sum (\operatorname{diam} U_i)^n$), then on $\mathbb{R}^n
$ one has $\widetilde{H}^n = \frac{2^n}{\omega_n}\,\text{Lebesgue}$, and consequ
ently on every Riemannian manifold
$$\mu = \frac{\omega_n}{2^n}\,\widetilde{H}^n.$$
So the two measures are always **equal up to the universal constant** $\omega_n/
2^n$; with the standard (Lebesgue-normalizing) convention they are exactly equal
.
