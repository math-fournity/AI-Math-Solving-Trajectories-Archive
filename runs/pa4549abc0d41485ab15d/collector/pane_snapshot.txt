Looking at this problem, I need to compare two topologies: the natural Fréchet t
opology on $C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})$ (which controls all derivatives)
versus the subspace topology induced from the compact-open topology on $C(\mathb

## Proof

**Setup.** Let $\tau_{\mathrm{co}}$ denote the compact-open topology on $C(\math
bb{R};\mathbb{R})$, generated by the seminorms
$$p_K(f) = \sup_{x \in K} |f(x)|, \quad K \subset \mathbb{R} \text{ compact}.$$
Let $\tau_{\infty}$ denote the natural Fréchet topology on $C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R
};\mathbb{R})$, generated by the seminorms
$$p_{K,m}(f) = \max_{0 \le j \le m}\; \sup_{x \in K} |f^{(j)}(x)|, \quad K \subs
et \mathbb{R} \text{ compact},\; m \in \mathbb{N}_0.$$

The question is whether $\tau_{\infty}$ is **strictly** finer than the subspace
topology $\tau_{\mathrm{co}}\big|_{C^{\infty}}$.

---

**Step 1: $\tau_{\infty}$ is at least as fine as $\tau_{\mathrm{co}}\big|_{C^{\i
nfty}}$.**

For every compact $K$, the seminorm $p_{K,0}(f) = \sup_{x\in K}|f(x)| = p_K(f)$.
 Thus every $\tau_{\mathrm{co}}$-subbasic open set, when restricted to $C^{\inft
y}$, is $\tau_{\infty}$-open. Hence
$$\tau_{\mathrm{co}}\big|_{C^{\infty}} \subseteq \tau_{\infty}.$$

---

**Step 2: $\tau_{\infty}$ is strictly finer than $\tau_{\mathrm{co}}\big|_{C^{\i
nfty}}$.**

Consider the sequence $f_n(x) = \frac{1}{n}\sin(nx) \in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\m
athbb{R})$.

**Convergence in $\tau_{\mathrm{co}}$:** For any compact $K \subset \mathbb{R}$,
$$p_K(f_n) = \sup_{x \in K} \frac{|\sin(nx)|}{n} \le \frac{1}{n} \longrightarrow
 0.$$
So $f_n \to 0$ in the compact-open topology (in fact, uniformly on all of $\math
bb{R}$).

**Non-convergence in $\tau_{\infty}$:** Take $K = [0, 2\pi]$ and $m=1$. Since $f
_n'(x) = \cos(nx)$,
$$p_{[0,2\pi],\,1}(f_n) \;\ge\; \sup_{x \in [0,\,2\pi]} |f_n'(x)| \;=\; \sup_{x
\in [0,\,2\pi]} |\cos(nx)| \;=\; 1$$
for every $n \ge 1$ (the supremum is attained at $x=0$, where $\cos(0)=1$). Ther
efore $p_{[0,2\pi],1}(f_n) \not\to 0$, which means $f_n \not\to 0$ in $\tau_{\in
fty}$.

---

**Conclusion.** The sequence $(f_n)$ converges to $0$ in the topology of uniform
 convergence on compact sets but **fails** to converge in the $C^{\infty}$ topol
ogy. Therefore the topology induced by $C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})$ is **
strictly more refined** than the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets
. $\blacksquare$
