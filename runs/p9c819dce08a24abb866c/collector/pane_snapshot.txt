## Proof

**Claim.** Yes. If $(X_n)$ and $(Y_n)$ are independent for each $n$, with $X_n \
xrightarrow{d} X$ and $Y_n \xrightarrow{d} Y$, and if $X$ and $Y$ are independen
t, then $(X_n, Y_n) \xrightarrow{d} (X, Y)$.

**Proof via characteristic functions.**

For each $n$, since $X_n$ and $Y_n$ are independent, the joint characteristic fu
nction of $(X_n, Y_n)$ factors:

$$\varphi_{(X_n,Y_n)}(s,t) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{i(sX_n + tY_n)}\right] = \math
bb{E}\!\left[e^{isX_n}\right]\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{itY_n}\right] = \varphi_{X_n
}(s)\,\varphi_{Y_n}(t).$$

By the hypothesis $X_n \xrightarrow{d} X$ and $Y_n \xrightarrow{d} Y$, Lévy's co
ntinuity theorem (univariate form) gives pointwise convergence of the marginal c
haracteristic functions:

$$\varphi_{X_n}(s) \to \varphi_X(s), \qquad \varphi_{Y_n}(t) \to \varphi_Y(t) \q
uad \text{for all } s, t \in \mathbb{R}.$$

Therefore, for every $(s,t) \in \mathbb{R}^2$,

$$\varphi_{(X_n,Y_n)}(s,t) = \varphi_{X_n}(s)\,\varphi_{Y_n}(t) \;\longrightarro
w\; \varphi_X(s)\,\varphi_Y(t).$$

Since $X$ and $Y$ are independent, the product $\varphi_X(s)\,\varphi_Y(t)$ is p
recisely the joint characteristic function of the pair $(X,Y)$:

$$\varphi_{(X,Y)}(s,t) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{i(sX+tY)}\right] = \varphi_X(s)\,\
varphi_Y(t).$$

The function $(s,t) \mapsto \varphi_X(s)\,\varphi_Y(t)$ is continuous at the ori
gin (being a product of two characteristic functions, each continuous at $0$ wit
h value $1$). By the multivariate Lévy continuity theorem, pointwise convergence
 of joint characteristic functions to a function continuous at the origin implie
s convergence in distribution. Hence

$$(X_n, Y_n) \xrightarrow{d} (X, Y). \qquad \blacksquare$$

**Remark on the hypotheses.** Both independence assumptions are essential:
- The independence of $X_n$ and $Y_n$ (for each $n$) is what permits the factori
zation $\varphi_{(X_n,Y_n)} = \varphi_{X_n}\varphi_{Y_n}$.
- The independence of $X$ and $Y$ is what identifies the limit $\varphi_X\varphi
_Y$ as the characteristic function of the joint law of $(X,Y)$. Without it, the
limit of the products need not correspond to any joint distribution with margina
ls $X$ and $Y$.
