## Solution

We evaluate

$$\oint_{|z-1|=3} e^{-\frac{1}{(z-1)^2}}\,dz.$$

**Step 1: Locate the singularity.** Let $w = z-1$. The integrand becomes $e^{-1/
w^2}$, which has an isolated singularity at $w=0$, i.e. $z=1$. The contour $|z-1
|=3$ becomes $|w|=3$, a positively oriented circle enclosing $w=0$.

**Step 2: Classify the singularity.** Since $-1/w^2$ has a pole of order $2$ at
$w=0$, the composition $e^{-1/w^2}$ has an **essential singularity** at $w=0$.

**Step 3: Compute the Laurent expansion.** Using the Taylor series of the expone
ntial,

$$e^{-1/w^2} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n!}\left(-\frac{1}{w^2}\right)^n = \
sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n!}\, w^{-2n}.$$

Writing out the terms:

$$e^{-1/w^2} = 1 - \frac{1}{w^2} + \frac{1}{2!\,w^4} - \frac{1}{3!\,w^6} + \cdot
s$$

**Step 4: Extract the residue.** The residue at $w=0$ is the coefficient of $w^{
-1}$ in the Laurent expansion. Every term in the series above has an **even** po
wer of $w$: $w^0, w^{-2}, w^{-4}, w^{-6}, \dots$. There is **no** $w^{-1}$ term.
 Hence

$$\operatorname{Res}_{w=0}\, e^{-1/w^2} = 0.$$

**Step 5: Apply the residue theorem.** The contour $|w|=3$ encloses the unique s
ingularity at $w=0$, and the function is analytic everywhere else inside and on
the contour. Therefore

$$\oint_{|z-1|=3} e^{-\frac{1}{(z-1)^2}}\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot \operatorname{Res}_{
w=0}\, e^{-1/w^2} = 2\pi i \cdot 0 = 0.$$

## Result

$$\boxed{\oint_{|z-1|=3} e^{-\frac{1}{(z-1)^2}}\,dz = 0}$$

The integral vanishes because the Laurent expansion of $e^{-1/w^2}$ contains onl
y even powers of $w$, so the $w^{-1}$ coefficient — and hence the residue — is z
ero, despite the presence of an essential singularity inside the contour.
