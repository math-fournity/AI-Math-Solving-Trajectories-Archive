## Proof

**Setup.** Let $f(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$ be a symmetric function (invariant under ev
ery permutation of its arguments) for which the integrals below converge. Define
 the full integral
$$I := \int_0^\infty \!\!\cdots\!\!\int_0^\infty f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\,dx_1\cdots d
x_n,$$
and the "ordered" integral
$$J := \int_0^\infty \int_0^{x_n}\!\cdots\!\int_0^{x_n} f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\,dx_1\
cdots dx_{n-1}\,dx_n.$$
The region of integration for $J$ is
$$R_n := \{(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^n : 0\le x_i \le x_n \text{ for
 all } i=1,\ldots,n-1\},$$
i.e. the set where $x_n = \max(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$.

**Partition of the positive orthant.** For each $k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$, define
$$R_k := \{(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^n : 0\le x_i \le x_k \text{ for
 all } i\neq k\},$$
the region where $x_k$ is the (weak) maximum. The positive orthant $\mathbb{R}_{
\ge 0}^n$ is the union $\bigcup_{k=1}^n R_k$. The overlaps $R_k\cap R_l$ (where
$x_k=x_l$ is the common maximum) lie in hyperplanes of lower dimension, hence ha
ve $n$-dimensional measure zero. Therefore
$$I = \sum_{k=1}^n \int_{R_k} f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\,dx_1\cdots dx_n.$$

**Symmetry makes all summands equal.** Let $\sigma_k$ be the transposition swapp
ing indices $1$ and $k$ (the identity when $k=1$). The change of variables $y_i
= x_{\sigma_k(i)}$ is a measure-preserving involution mapping $R_k$ bijectively
onto $R_1 = R_n$ (after relabeling, the region where the distinguished variable
is the maximum). Since $f$ is symmetric, $f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=f(y_1,\ldots,y_n)$,
so
$$\int_{R_k} f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\,dx_1\cdots dx_n = \int_{R_n} f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\,
dx_1\cdots dx_n = J.$$
Hence every summand equals $J$, and
$$I = \sum_{k=1}^n J = n\,J.$$

**Verification for $n=2$.** Here $R_2 = \{0\le x_1\le x_2\}$ and $R_1=\{0\le x_2
\le x_1\}$; these two triangles partition the first quadrant (up to the diagonal
 of measure zero), giving $I=2J$, which is exactly the given identity. This conf
irms $C=2=n$.

**Conclusion.** The constant exists and equals
$$\boxed{C = n}.$$
