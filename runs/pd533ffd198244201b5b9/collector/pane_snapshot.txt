Looking at this integral, I need to identify the region of integration and find
a suitable coordinate transformation.

**Step 1: Identify the region.**

The bounds are:
- $z \in [0, 2]$
- $y \in [0, \sqrt{2z - z^2}]$
- $x \in [0, \sqrt{2z - z^2 - y^2}]$

The constraint $x^2 + y^2 \leq 2z - z^2$ rewrites as $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 \leq 2z$,
i.e., $x^2 + y^2 + (z-1)^2 \leq 1$. This is the **ball of radius 1 centered at $
(0,0,1)$**, restricted to $x \geq 0, y \geq 0$ (with $z \geq 0$ automatic since
$z \in [0,2]$).

**Step 2: Convert to spherical coordinates centered at the origin.**

Let $x = r\sin\phi\cos\theta$, $y = r\sin\phi\sin\theta$, $z = r\cos\phi$.

- The integrand $(x^2+y^2+z^2)^{-1/2} = 1/r$.
- The volume element is $r^2 \sin\phi\, dr\, d\phi\, d\theta$.
- So the integrand × Jacobian = $r\sin\phi\, dr\, d\phi\, d\theta$.

**Step 3: Determine the new bounds.**

The ball $x^2 + y^2 + (z-1)^2 \leq 1$ becomes $r^2 - 2r\cos\phi + 1 \leq 1$, giv
ing $r \leq 2\cos\phi$ (requiring $\cos\phi \geq 0$, so $\phi \in [0, \pi/2]$).

The constraints $x \geq 0, y \geq 0$ give $\theta \in [0, \pi/2]$.

**Step 4: Evaluate.**

$$I = \int_0^{\pi/2} d\theta \int_0^{\pi/2} \sin\phi\, d\phi \int_0^{2\cos\phi}
r\, dr$$

The $\theta$-integral: $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

The $r$-integral: $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\cos\phi} r\, dr = \frac{(2\cos\phi)^2}
{2} = 2\cos^2\phi$.

So:
$$I = \frac{\pi}{2} \int_0^{\pi/2} 2\cos^2\phi\,\sin\phi\, d\phi = \pi \int_0^{\
pi/2} \cos^2\phi\,\sin\phi\, d\phi$$

Substituting $u = \cos\phi$, $du = -\sin\phi\, d\phi$, with $\phi=0 \Rightarrow
u=1$ and $\phi=\pi/2 \Rightarrow u=0$:

$$I = \pi \int_0^1 u^2\, du = \pi \cdot \frac{1}{3} = \frac{\pi}{3}$$

**Result:**

$$\boxed{\dfrac{\pi}{3}}$$
