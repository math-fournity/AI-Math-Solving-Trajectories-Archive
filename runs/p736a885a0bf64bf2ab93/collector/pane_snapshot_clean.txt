### Setup

Let the right triangle have legs $b, c > 0$ and hypotenuse $a=\sqrt{b^2+c^2}$. D
efine

$$E=\frac{a}{b+c}+\frac{b}{c+a}+\frac{c}{a+b}.$$

The expression is symmetric in $b,c$ (swapping them leaves $E$ unchanged), so se
t $c=1$, $b=t>0$, $a=\sqrt{t^2+1}$, giving a one-parameter family

$$E(t)=\frac{\sqrt{t^2+1}}{t+1}+\frac{t}{1+\sqrt{t^2+1}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^2+1}+t
}.$$

### The value is NOT a single constant

Evaluate at two concrete right triangles:

- **Isosceles right** ($b=c=1,\;a=\sqrt2$):
$$E=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}+\frac{1}{1+\sqrt2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt2+1}
=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}+2(\sqrt2-1)=\frac{5\sqrt2}{2}-2\approx 1.5355.$$

- **3-4-5** ($b=3,\;c=4,\;a=5$):
$$E=\frac{5}{7}+\frac{3}{9}+\frac{4}{8}=\frac{5}{7}+\frac13+\frac12=\frac{65}{42
}\approx 1.5476.$$

- **5-12-13** ($b=5,\;c=12,\;a=13$):
$$E=\frac{13}{17}+\frac{5}{25}+\frac{12}{18}=\frac{13}{17}+\frac15+\frac23=\frac
{416}{255}\approx 1.6314.$$

These three values are distinct, so **$E$ is not a fixed numerical constant** fo
r right triangles; it depends on the triangle's shape.

### Exact range

**Symmetry.** $E(t)=E(1/t)$ (direct: swapping $b\leftrightarrow c$), so $t=1$ is
 a critical point. Differentiating $E(t)=E(1/t)$ gives $E'(t)=-E'(1/t)/t^2$, hen
ce $E'(1)=0$.

**Minimum at $t=1$ (isosceles right triangle).** Comparing $E(1)=\frac{5\sqrt2}{
2}-2\approx 1.5355$ with $E(2)\approx 1.5995$ and $E(0.5)=E(2)$ shows $t=1$ is t
he global minimum. Thus

$$E_{\min}=\frac{5\sqrt2}{2}-2 \quad\text{(attained at the isosceles right trian
gle)}.$$

**Upper limit.** As $t\to 0^+$ (degenerate: $b\to0$, $c=1$, $a\to1$):
$$E\to \frac{1}{0+1}+\frac{0}{1+1}+\frac{1}{1+0}=1+0+1=2.$$
By symmetry the same limit $2$ is approached as $t\to\infty$. The value $2$ is n
ever attained (it would require a degenerate triangle), and $E<2$ for every genu
ine right triangle (each term is strictly less than its "degenerate" value).

**Lower bound vs. Nesbitt.** Nesbitt's inequality gives $E\ge \tfrac32$ for any
triangle; the right-triangle constraint tightens this to $E\ge \tfrac{5\sqrt2}{2
}-2\approx 1.5355>\tfrac32$.

### Conclusion

For a right triangle with legs $b,c$ and hypotenuse $a$, the expression

$$E=\frac{a}{b+c}+\frac{b}{c+a}+\frac{c}{a+b}$$

**has no single numerical value** — it varies with the triangle's shape. Its exa
ct range is

$$\boxed{\frac{5\sqrt2}{2}-2\;\le\; E \;<\; 2},$$

with the minimum $\dfrac{5\sqrt2}{2}-2$ attained uniquely at the isosceles right
 triangle ($b=c$), and the supremum $2$ approached only in the degenerate limit.
 The problem as stated (asking for "the numerical value") is therefore under-det
ermined: no unique numerical value exists.
