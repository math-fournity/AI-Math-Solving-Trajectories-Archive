# Proof

**Claim:** The inequality $|1+q-\#E(\mathbb{F}_q)|\leq 2\sqrt{q}$ holds for ever
y singular genus 1 curve $E$ over a finite field $\mathbb{F}_q$.

Here "singular genus 1 curve" means a projective plane cubic (arithmetic genus 1
) possessing a singularity. Its normalization has geometric genus 0, hence is $\
mathbb{P}^1$.

---

## Setup

Let $E/\mathbb{F}_q$ be a singular cubic. It has exactly one singular point $P_0
\in E(\mathbb{F}_q)$ (over the algebraic closure; since $E$ is defined over $\ma
thbb{F}_q$ and the singular locus is intrinsic, $P_0$ is $\mathbb{F}_q$-rational
). The singular point is either a **node** or a **cusp**.

Denote by $E_{\text{ns}}=E\setminus\{P_0\}$ the nonsingular locus. Then
$$\#E(\mathbb{F}_q)=\#E_{\text{ns}}(\mathbb{F}_q)+1.$$

The group structure on $E_{\text{ns}}$ identifies it with a one-dimensional alge
braic group over $\mathbb{F}_q$:

- **Cusp:** $E_{\text{ns}}\cong \mathbb{G}_a$.
- **Node:** $E_{\text{ns}}\cong \mathbb{G}_m$ if the node is *split* (the two ta
ngent directions at $P_0$ are $\mathbb{F}_q$-rational), and $E_{\text{ns}}$ is a
 *nonsplit torus* (the kernel of the norm $N:\mathbb{F}_{q^2}^*\to\mathbb{F}_q^*
$) if the node is *nonsplit*.

---

## Case analysis

**Case 1 — Cuspidal.** $E_{\text{ns}}\cong\mathbb{G}_a$, so $\#E_{\text{ns}}(\ma
thbb{F}_q)=\#\mathbb{F}_q=q$. Hence
$$\#E(\mathbb{F}_q)=q+1,\qquad |1+q-\#E(\mathbb{F}_q)|=|1+q-(q+1)|=0\leq 2\sqrt{
q}.\ \checkmark$$

**Case 2 — Split nodal.** $E_{\text{ns}}\cong\mathbb{G}_m$, so $\#E_{\text{ns}}(
\mathbb{F}_q)=\#\mathbb{F}_q^*=q-1$. Hence
$$\#E(\mathbb{F}_q)=(q-1)+1=q,\qquad |1+q-\#E(\mathbb{F}_q)|=|1+q-q|=1\leq 2\sqr
t{q}.\ \checkmark$$

**Case 3 — Nonsplit nodal.** $E_{\text{ns}}\cong\ker(N:\mathbb{F}_{q^2}^*\to\mat
hbb{F}_q^*)$, which has order $\frac{q^2-1}{q-1}=q+1$. Hence
$$\#E(\mathbb{F}_q)=(q+1)+1=q+2,\qquad |1+q-\#E(\mathbb{F}_q)|=|1+q-(q+2)|=1\leq
 2\sqrt{q}.\ \checkmark$$

---

## Verification of the bound

In every case,
$$|1+q-\#E(\mathbb{F}_q)|\in\{0,1\}.$$

Since $\mathbb{F}_q$ is a finite field, $q\geq 2$, and therefore
$$2\sqrt{q}\geq 2\sqrt{2}\approx 2.83>1\geq |1+q-\#E(\mathbb{F}_q)|.$$

Thus $|1+q-\#E(\mathbb{F}_q)|\leq 2\sqrt{q}$ holds in all three cases.

---

## Conclusion

For any singular genus 1 curve over $\mathbb{F}_q$, the quantity $|1+q-\#E(\math
bb{F}_q)|$ is either $0$ (cuspidal) or $1$ (nodal, split or nonsplit), both of w
hich are bounded by $2\sqrt{q}$ for all $q\geq 2$. The inequality **holds** for
singular genus 1 curves—in fact it holds with a much stronger, constant bound.
