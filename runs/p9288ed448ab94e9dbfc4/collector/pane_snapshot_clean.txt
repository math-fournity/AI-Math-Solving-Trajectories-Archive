## Solution

Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion $g(x+g(x)+2g(y))=g(2x)+g(2y)$.

**Step 1: Locate the zero of $g$.** Since $g$ is bijective, there is a unique $a
\in\mathbb{R}$ with $g(a)=0$.

**Step 2: Use $P(x,a)$ (so $g(y)=g(a)=0$):**
$$g(x+g(x))=g(2x)+g(2a). \tag{1}$$

**Step 3: Use $P(a,y)$ (so $g(x)=g(a)=0$):**
$$g(a+2g(y))=g(2a)+g(2y). \tag{2}$$

**Step 4: Show $g(2a)=0$.** Set $y=a$ in (2):
$$g(a+2g(a))=g(2a)+g(2a)\implies g(a+0)=2g(2a)\implies g(a)=2g(2a).$$
Since $g(a)=0$, we get $2g(2a)=0$, hence $g(2a)=0$.

**Step 5: Conclude $a=0$.** We have $g(a)=0$ and $g(2a)=0$. By injectivity of $g
$, $a=2a$, so $a=0$. Therefore $g(0)=0$.

**Step 6: Determine $g$.** With $a=0$, equation (1) becomes
$$g(x+g(x))=g(2x)+g(0)=g(2x).$$
By injectivity of $g$,
$$x+g(x)=2x\implies g(x)=x\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}.$$

**Step 7: Verification.** For $g(x)=x$:
$$g(x+g(x)+2g(y))=g(x+x+2y)=g(2x+2y)=2x+2y,$$
$$g(2x)+g(2y)=2x+2y.$$
Both sides agree, and $g(x)=x$ is bijective.

**Conclusion.** The unique bijective function satisfying the equation is
$$\boxed{g(x)=x}.$$
