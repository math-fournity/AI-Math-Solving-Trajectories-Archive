## Proof

We interpret the problem as concerning the set $S_n$ of $n \times n$ real symmet
ric matrices (for a fixed $n$) whose eigenvalues all satisfy $|\lambda| \le 2$.
We show $S_n$ is compact in the space $\mathrm{Sym}_n(\mathbb{R})$ of real symme
tric matrices (a finite-dimensional vector space of dimension $\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$
, equipped with any norm — all norms being equivalent).

**Key equivalence.** A real symmetric matrix $A$ has real eigenvalues, and its s
pectral radius equals its operator (spectral) norm:
$$\rho(A) = \max_i |\lambda_i(A)| = \|A\|_{\mathrm{op}} := \sup_{\|x\|=1} \|Ax\|
.$$
Hence the condition "$|\lambda| \le 2$ for every eigenvalue" is equivalent to $\
|A\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le 2$. Thus
$$S_n = \{\, A \in \mathrm{Sym}_n(\mathbb{R}) : \|A\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le 2 \,\}.$
$

**Closedness.** The map $A \mapsto \|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ is continuous on $M_n(\m
athbb{R})$ (it is a norm on a finite-dimensional space, hence continuous; equiva
lently, it is the largest singular value, a continuous function of the entries).
 The subspace $\mathrm{Sym}_n(\mathbb{R})$ is closed in $M_n(\mathbb{R}) \cong \
mathbb{R}^{n^2}$ (it is the zero set of the continuous map $A \mapsto A - A^\top
$). Therefore $S_n = \mathrm{Sym}_n(\mathbb{R}) \cap \|{\cdot}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{
-1}([0,2])$ is an intersection of two closed sets, hence closed.

**Boundedness.** The Frobenius norm $\|A\|_F = \bigl(\sum_{i,j} a_{ij}^2\bigr)^{
1/2}$ satisfies
$$\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \|A\|_F \le \sqrt{n}\,\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}},$$
since $\|A\|_F^2 = \sum_i \sigma_i^2 \le n \cdot (\max_i \sigma_i)^2 = n\,\|A\|_
{\mathrm{op}}^2$. For $A \in S_n$, $\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le 2$, so
$$\|A\|_F \le \sqrt{n}\cdot 2.$$
Thus every entry of $A$ is bounded (each $|a_{ij}| \le \|A\|_F \le 2\sqrt{n}$),
so $S_n$ is a bounded subset of $\mathbb{R}^{n^2}$.

**Conclusion.** $S_n$ is a closed and bounded subset of the finite-dimensional E
uclidean space $\mathrm{Sym}_n(\mathbb{R}) \cong \mathbb{R}^{n(n+1)/2}$. By the
Heine–Borel theorem, $S_n$ is compact. $\blacksquare$
