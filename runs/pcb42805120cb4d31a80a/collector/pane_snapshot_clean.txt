## Proof

**Step 1: Asymptotics of $f(x)$ as $x\to\infty$.**

The function $f$ satisfies $f(x)\ln f(x) = e^x$. Setting $y = f(x)$, we have $y\
ln y = e^x$. Taking logarithms:
$$\ln y + \ln\ln y = x.$$
As $x\to\infty$, $e^x\to\infty$, so $y\to\infty$ and $\ln y\to\infty$. From the
equation, $\ln y = x - \ln\ln y$. Since $\ln y \to \infty$, we get $\ln\ln y = o
(\ln y) = o(x)$, hence
$$\ln y = x - \ln\ln y = x - O(\ln x),$$
so $y = e^{x - O(\ln x)} = \frac{e^x}{x^{O(1)}}$. In particular $f(x) \to \infty
$ and more precisely $f(x) \sim \frac{e^x}{x}$ (since $\ln\ln y \sim \ln x$ give
s $\ln y = x - \ln x + o(\ln x)$, thus $y = \frac{e^x}{x}\cdot e^{o(\ln x)}$).

**Step 2: Identify the form of the limit.**

Consider
$$L = \lim_{x\to\infty}\left(1+\frac{\ln x}{f(x)}\right)^{\frac{f(x)}{x}}.$$

Since $f(x)\sim \frac{e^x}{x}\to\infty$ and $\ln x$ grows much slower, $\frac{\l
n x}{f(x)} \to 0$. Meanwhile $\frac{f(x)}{x}\sim \frac{e^x}{x^2}\to\infty$. This
 is a $1^\infty$ indeterminate form, so we take logarithms:
$$\ln L = \lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(x)}{x}\ln\!\left(1+\frac{\ln x}{f(x)}\right).
$$

**Step 3: Use the Taylor expansion of $\ln(1+u)$ for small $u$.**

Set $u = \frac{\ln x}{f(x)}\to 0$. Then $\ln(1+u) = u - \frac{u^2}{2} + O(u^3)$,
 so
$$\frac{f(x)}{x}\ln\!\left(1+\frac{\ln x}{f(x)}\right) = \frac{f(x)}{x}\left(\fr
ac{\ln x}{f(x)} - \frac{(\ln x)^2}{2\,f(x)^2} + O\!\left(\frac{(\ln x)^3}{f(x)^3
}\right)\right).$$
$$= \frac{\ln x}{x} - \frac{(\ln x)^2}{2\,x\,f(x)} + O\!\left(\frac{(\ln x)^3}{x
\,f(x)^2}\right).$$

**Step 4: Show each term vanishes.**

- First term: $\dfrac{\ln x}{x}\to 0$ as $x\to\infty$.
- Second term: using $f(x)\sim \frac{e^x}{x}$, $\dfrac{(\ln x)^2}{2\,x\,f(x)}\si
m \dfrac{(\ln x)^2}{2\,e^x}\to 0$.
- Remainder: $\dfrac{(\ln x)^3}{x\,f(x)^2}\sim \dfrac{(\ln x)^3\,x}{e^{2x}}\to 0
$.

Therefore $\ln L = 0$, and consequently
$$L = e^0 = 1.$$
