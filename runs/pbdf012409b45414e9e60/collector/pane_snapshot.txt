## Solution

**Answer: Yes, such $x$ and $B$ exist.**

**Proof.**

Let $A \in \mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$ with $(P \otimes \lambda
)(A) > 0$. For each $x \in \mathbb{R}$, define the **$x$-section** of $A$:

$$A_x = \{\omega \in \Omega : (\omega, x) \in A\}.$$

**Step 1: Each section $A_x$ is $\mathcal{F}$-measurable.**

For a fixed $x \in \mathbb{R}$, consider the map $\varphi_x : \Omega \to \Omega
\times \mathbb{R}$ defined by $\varphi_x(\omega) = (\omega, x)$. This map is mea
surable from $(\Omega, \mathcal{F})$ to $(\Omega \times \mathbb{R}, \mathcal{F}
\otimes \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))$, since for any measurable rectangle $F \times
C$ (with $F \in \mathcal{F}$, $C \in \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$):

$$\varphi_x^{-1}(F \times C) = \begin{cases} F & \text{if } x \in C, \\ \emptyse
t & \text{if } x \notin C, \end{cases}$$

which is in $\mathcal{F}$. Since the measurable rectangles generate $\mathcal{F}
 \otimes \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$, $\varphi_x$ is measurable. Therefore $A_x = \
varphi_x^{-1}(A) \in \mathcal{F}$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

**Step 2: Apply Fubini–Tonelli.**

Since $A \in \mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$ and $\mathbf{1}_A \geq
 0$ is measurable, Tonelli's theorem gives:

$$(P \otimes \lambda)(A) = \int_{\Omega \times \mathbb{R}} \mathbf{1}_A \, d(P \
otimes \lambda) = \int_{\mathbb{R}} \left( \int_{\Omega} \mathbf{1}_A(\omega, x)
 \, dP(\omega) \right) d\lambda(x) = \int_{\mathbb{R}} P(A_x) \, d\lambda(x).$$

By hypothesis, $(P \otimes \lambda)(A) > 0$, so:

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} P(A_x) \, d\lambda(x) > 0.$$

**Step 3: Conclude there exists $x$ with $P(A_x) > 0$.**

The integrand $x \mapsto P(A_x)$ is non-negative and $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$-m
easurable (this is part of the Fubini–Tonelli conclusion). If $P(A_x) = 0$ for $
\lambda$-almost every $x$, then the integral would be $0$, contradicting the hyp
othesis. Therefore the set

$$S = \{x \in \mathbb{R} : P(A_x) > 0\}$$

has positive $\lambda$-measure, and in particular is nonempty. Choose any $x_0 \
in S$.

**Step 4: Construct $B$.**

Set $B = A_{x_0}$. By Step 1, $B \in \mathcal{F}$. By the choice of $x_0$, $P(B)
 = P(A_{x_0}) > 0$. By definition of the section, for every $\omega \in B = A_{x
_0}$, we have $(\omega, x_0) \in A$, which means $B \times \{x_0\} \subset A$.

Thus we have produced $x_0 \in \mathbb{R}$ and $B \in \mathcal{F}$ with $P(B) >
0$ and $B \times \{x_0\} \subset A$. $\blacksquare$
