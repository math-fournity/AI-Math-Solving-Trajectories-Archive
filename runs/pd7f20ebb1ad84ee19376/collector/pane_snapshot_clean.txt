# Proof

**Claim:** We cannot reach $baaa\ldots a$ ($b$ followed by $2003$ copies of $a$)
 from $aaa\ldots ab$ ($2003$ copies of $a$ followed by $b$).

## Direct argument (the starting word is irreducible)

The two replacement rules are local: they replace a length-3 block by a single l
etter (or vice-versa):
$$aba \leftrightarrow b, \qquad bba \leftrightarrow a.$$

A rule can fire only when the current word contains the substring $aba$ or $bba$
.

Consider the initial word $W_0 = a^{2003}b = \underbrace{aaa\ldots aaa}_{2003}b$
. Its length-3 substrings are:
- $\underbrace{aaa\ldots aaa}_{2001\text{ substrings}}$ (entirely within the blo
ck of $a$'s), and
- the final substring $aab$ (the last two $a$'s and the trailing $b$).

Every length-3 substring of $W_0$ is either $aaa$ or $aab$. **Neither $aba$ nor
$bba$ ever appears.** Therefore no replacement rule can be applied to $W_0$ at a
ll — the word is *irreducible* (a normal form under the rewriting system). Conse
quently no sequence of moves can ever leave $W_0$.

Since the target word $W^* = ba^{2003}$ is a different word from $W_0 = a^{2003}
b$ (they differ already in the first letter), and we cannot make even a single m
ove from $W_0$, the target is unreachable.

## Invariant confirmation (group-theoretic)

For robustness, we exhibit an invariant that also rules out the transformation.
Interpreting the relations $aba = b$ and $bba = a$ in the **group** $G = \langle
 a, b \mid aba = b,\; bba = a\rangle$:

- From $bba = a$, right-multiplying by $a^{-1}$ gives $b^2 = e$.
- From $aba = b$ and $b^2 = e$: $b^{-1}(aba) = e \Rightarrow (ba)^2 = e$, i.e. $
bab = a^{-1}$.

Thus $G \cong D_\infty = \langle a, b \mid b^2 = e,\; bab = a^{-1}\rangle$, the
infinite dihedral group, in which $a$ has **infinite order**. Using $ba = a^{-1}
b$ (from $bab = a^{-1}$), we compute in $G$:
$$ba^{2003} = (ba)\,a^{2002} = a^{-1}b\,a^{2002} = a^{-2}b\,a^{2001} = \cdots =
a^{-2003}b.$$

So $\varphi(ba^{2003}) = a^{-2003}b$ while $\varphi(a^{2003}b) = a^{2003}b$ unde
r the natural homomorphism $\varphi$ from the monoid to $G$. These are equal onl
y if $a^{2003} = a^{-2003}$, i.e. $a^{4006} = e$, which is false since $a$ has i
nfinite order. Hence $a^{2003}b \neq ba^{2003}$ already in the group, so certain
ly not in the monoid generated by the two rewriting rules.

## Conclusion

The initial word $a^{2003}b$ admits no applicable rule (it is irreducible), so n
o move is possible from it; and independently, a group invariant distinguishes t
he two words. Therefore the word $ba^{2003}$ **cannot** be reached from $a^{2003
}b$.
