# Proof

**Statement:** For any topological spaces $X$, $Y$, and $Z$, if $X$ and $Y$ are
homeomorphic, then $X \times Z$ and $Y \times Z$ are homeomorphic.

**Claim:** The statement is **true**.

---

## Proof

Suppose $X$ and $Y$ are homeomorphic. Then there exists a homeomorphism $f \colo
n X \to Y$, i.e., a bijection such that both $f$ and $f^{-1} \colon Y \to X$ are
 continuous.

Define the map
$$F \colon X \times Z \to Y \times Z, \qquad F(x, z) = (f(x), z).$$

This is the product map $f \times \mathrm{id}_Z$, where $\mathrm{id}_Z \colon Z
\to Z$ is the identity map.

**Step 1: $F$ is a bijection.**

Since $f$ is a bijection and $\mathrm{id}_Z$ is a bijection, the product $f \tim
es \mathrm{id}_Z$ is a bijection. Explicitly, the inverse is
$$F^{-1} \colon Y \times Z \to X \times Z, \qquad F^{-1}(y, z) = (f^{-1}(y), z)
= f^{-1} \times \mathrm{id}_Z.$$

**Step 2: $F$ is continuous.**

Recall that $X \times Z$ and $Y \times Z$ are endowed with the product topologie
s. A map into a product space is continuous if and only if each component functi
on is continuous. The components of $F$ are:
- $\pi_Y \circ F = f \circ \pi_X$, which is continuous as the composition of the
 continuous projection $\pi_X \colon X \times Z \to X$ with the continuous map $
f$.
- $\pi_Z \circ F = \mathrm{id}_Z \circ \pi_Z = \pi_Z$, which is continuous as th
e projection map.

Hence $F$ is continuous.

**Step 3: $F^{-1}$ is continuous.**

By the same argument, $F^{-1} = f^{-1} \times \mathrm{id}_Z$ has components:
- $\pi_X \circ F^{-1} = f^{-1} \circ \pi_Y$, continuous since $f^{-1}$ is contin
uous and $\pi_Y$ is continuous.
- $\pi_Z \circ F^{-1} = \pi_Z$, continuous.

Hence $F^{-1}$ is continuous.

**Conclusion.**

$F \colon X \times Z \to Y \times Z$ is a bijection with both $F$ and $F^{-1}$ c
ontinuous. Therefore $F$ is a homeomorphism, so $X \times Z \cong Y \times Z$.

Since the argument applies to arbitrary topological spaces $X, Y, Z$ (whenever $
X \cong Y$), the statement is **true**. $\blacksquare$
