## Proof

**Yes, it follows — but trivially, and the Killing condition is irrelevant.**

### Setup

On a Riemannian manifold $(M, g)$, the canonical affine connection is the **Levi
-Civita connection** $\nabla$, which is uniquely characterized by the two condit
ions:

1. **Metric compatibility:** $\nabla g = 0$.
2. **Torsion-free:** $T = 0$.

The torsion tensor of a connection $\nabla$ is defined by
$$T(X, Y) = \nabla_X Y - \nabla_Y X - [X, Y].$$

### Key fact: Torsion vanishes identically

For the Levi-Civita connection on a Riemannian manifold, the torsion-free condit
ion is part of the **definition**. Therefore
$$T \equiv 0$$
as a $(1,2)$-tensor field on $M$, at every point, for all inputs.

### Lie derivative of the zero tensor

The Lie derivative is a derivation on tensor fields that is $\mathbb{R}$-linear
and respects the tensor type. For the identically-vanishing tensor field $T = 0$
, we compute directly from the definition:

$$\mathcal{L}_X T = \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{(\phi_t)^* T - T}{t},$$

where $\phi_t$ is the flow of $X$. Since $T = 0$ everywhere, $(\phi_t)^* T = 0$
for all $t$, so

$$\mathcal{L}_X T = \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{0 - 0}{t} = 0.$$

Equivalently, in any local coordinate frame, every component of $T$ is the zero
function, and the Lie derivative acts componentwise via $X^k \partial_k T^i_{\ j
k} + \text{connection terms from the Lie derivative on indices}$; since all comp
onents and their derivatives vanish, every component of $\mathcal{L}_X T$ vanish
es.

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{\mathcal{L}_X T = 0 \text{ holds — but not because } X \text{ is Killin
g.}}$$

The conclusion $\mathcal{L}_X T = 0$ is true for **every** vector field $X$ on a
 Riemannian manifold, Killing or not, because the torsion tensor of the Levi-Civ
ita connection is **identically zero** by definition. The hypothesis $\mathcal{L
}_X g = 0$ (the Killing equation) plays no role whatsoever.

The question is therefore vacuously satisfied: the implication "$\mathcal{L}_X g
 = 0 \Rightarrow \mathcal{L}_X T = 0$" is true, but only because the consequent
is always true, making the implication trivially valid rather than a genuine con
sequence of the Killing property.
