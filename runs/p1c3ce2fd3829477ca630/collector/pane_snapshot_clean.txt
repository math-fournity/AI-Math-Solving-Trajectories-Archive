## Proof

We evaluate the improper integral

$$I = \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{x^2}\,dx = \lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{0}^{R} e^{x^2}\,dx.
$$

**Claim:** The integral diverges; that is, $I = +\infty$.

**Proof.** For all $x \geq 0$, we have $x^2 \geq 0$, hence $e^{x^2} \geq e^0 = 1
$. Therefore, for every $R \geq 0$,

$$\int_{0}^{R} e^{x^2}\,dx \;\geq\; \int_{0}^{R} 1\,dx \;=\; R.$$

Taking the limit as $R \to \infty$,

$$I = \lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{0}^{R} e^{x^2}\,dx \;\geq\; \lim_{R\to\infty} R \;=
\; +\infty.$$

Since the integrand $e^{x^2}$ is strictly positive and continuous on $[0,\infty)
$, the partial integrals $\int_0^R e^{x^2}\,dx$ are non-negative and monotonical
ly increasing in $R$. The lower bound $R$ shows these partial integrals grow wit
hout bound. Hence the improper integral diverges to $+\infty$:

$$\boxed{\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{x^2}\,dx = +\infty.}$$
