## Analysis of $\alpha$

The function $\alpha$ is constant on each interval $\left(\frac{1}{3^n}, \frac{1
}{3^{n-1}}\right]$ with value $\frac{1}{2^n}$, and $\alpha(0)=0$. The discontinu
ities (jumps) occur at the points $c_n = \frac{1}{3^n}$ for $n=1,2,3,\ldots$

**Computing the jump at $c_n = \frac{1}{3^n}$:**

- Left limit: for $x$ slightly less than $\frac{1}{3^n}$, we are in the interval
 $\left(\frac{1}{3^{n+1}}, \frac{1}{3^n}\right]$, so $\alpha(c_n^-) = \frac{1}{2
^{n+1}}$.
- Right limit: for $x$ slightly greater than $\frac{1}{3^n}$, we are in the inte
rval $\left(\frac{1}{3^n}, \frac{1}{3^{n-1}}\right]$, so $\alpha(c_n^+) = \frac{
1}{2^n}$.

Thus the jump is:
$$\Delta\alpha(c_n) = \alpha(c_n^+) - \alpha(c_n^-) = \frac{1}{2^n} - \frac{1}{2
^{n+1}} = \frac{1}{2^{n+1}}.$$

**At $x=0$:** $\alpha(0^+) = \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2^n} = 0 = \alpha(0)$, so
 there is no jump at $0$.

## Evaluating the integral

Since $f(x)=x$ is continuous on $[0,1]$ and $\alpha$ is a step function with jum
ps at $c_n = \frac{1}{3^n}$, the Riemann-Stieltjes integral exists and equals th
e sum of $f$ evaluated at each jump point times the jump:

$$\int_0^1 x\,d\alpha(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} f(c_n)\cdot\Delta\alpha(c_n) = \s
um_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{3^n}\cdot\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}.$$
