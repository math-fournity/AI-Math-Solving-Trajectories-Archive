## Proof

**Setup and reformulation.** For a direction $u$ (unit normal to line $K$), let
$h_S(u) = \max_{x\in S}\langle x,u\rangle$ be the support function. The two supp
ort lines parallel to $K$ are at heights $h_S(u)$ and $-h_S(-u)$, so the width i
s $w = h_S(u)+h_S(-u)$, and the midline $\overline{L}$ is at height $\frac{h_S(u
)-h_S(-u)}{2}$.

A point $p\in S$ lies in $B_S(K,t)$ iff its distance to $\overline{L}$ is at mos
t $\frac{t}{2}w$, i.e.,
$$\left|\langle p,u\rangle - \frac{h_S(u)-h_S(-u)}{2}\right| \leq \frac{t}{2}\bi
gl(h_S(u)+h_S(-u)\bigr).$$

Define $a(u) = h_S(u)-\langle p,u\rangle \geq 0$ and $b(u) = \langle p,u\rangle
+ h_S(-u) \geq 0$ (the distances from $p$ to the two support lines). Then the mi
dline is at $\langle p,u\rangle + \frac{a-b}{2}$, so the condition becomes:
$$\frac{|a(u)-b(u)|}{a(u)+b(u)} \leq t \quad \text{for all } u. \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Reduction to the Minkowski measure of symmetry.** Setting $r(u)=\max\!\bigl(\f
rac{a}{b},\frac{b}{a}\bigr)\geq 1$, we have $\frac{|a-b|}{a+b}=\frac{r-1}{r+1}$.
 The **Minkowski measure of symmetry** is
$$s(S)=\inf_{p\in S}\sup_u r(u),$$
so condition $(\ast)$ holds for some $p$ iff $t\geq \frac{s(S)-1}{s(S)+1}$. The
smallest $t$ working for **all** $S$ is therefore
$$t^\ast = \sup_S \frac{s(S)-1}{s(S)+1} = \frac{s_{\max}-1}{s_{\max}+1},$$
and it remains to find $s_{\max}=\sup_S s(S)$ over all planar convex bodies.

**Key bound: $s(S)\leq 2$ in the plane.** Let $g$ be the centroid of $S$ (so $g$
 is an interior point). Fix a direction $u$ and let $a=h_S(u)-\langle g,u\rangle
$, $b=\langle g,u\rangle+h_S(-u)$. We show $a\leq 2b$; by symmetry ($u\mapsto -u
$) this gives $b\leq 2a$, hence $r\leq 2$.

Translate so $g=0$. Let $f(t)=\operatorname{length}\bigl(S\cap\{x:\langle x,u\ra
ngle=t\}\bigr)$ for $t\in[-b,a]$. By the Brunn–Minkowski theorem in $\mathbb{R}^
2$, $f$ is **concave** on $[-b,a]$ with $f(-b)=f(a)=0$. The centroid condition g
ives:
$$\int_{-b}^{a} t\, f(t)\,dt = 0, \quad\text{i.e.,}\quad \int_0^a t\,f(t)\,dt =
\int_{-b}^0 |t|\,f(t)\,dt. \tag{$\dagger$}$$

**Lower bound on the left side of $(\dagger)$:** By concavity, $f(t)\geq f(0)\fr
ac{a-t}{a}$ for $t\in[0,a]$ (chord from $(0,f(0))$ to $(a,0)$), so
$$\int_0^a t\,f(t)\,dt \geq \frac{f(0)}{a}\int_0^a t(a-t)\,dt = \frac{f(0)\,a^2}
{6}.$$

**Upper bound on the right side of $(\dagger)$:** By concavity on $[-b,a]$, expr
essing $0$ as a convex combination of $t\in[-b,0]$ and $a$: $0=\frac{a}{a-t}\,t+
\frac{-t}{a-t}\,a$, so $f(0)\geq\frac{a}{a-t}f(t)$, giving $f(t)\leq f(0)\frac{a
-t}{a}$ for $t\in[-b,0]$. Thus
$$\int_{-b}^0 |t|\,f(t)\,dt \leq \frac{f(0)}{a}\int_{-b}^0 |t|(a-t)\,dt = \frac{
f(0)}{a}\int_0^b s(a+s)\,ds = \frac{f(0)\,b^2(3a+2b)}{6a}.$$

Combining via $(\dagger)$: $\frac{f(0)\,a^2}{6}\leq\frac{f(0)\,b^2(3a+2b)}{6a}$,
 i.e., $a^3\leq 3ab^2+2b^3$. Setting $r=a/b>0$: $r^3-3r-2\leq 0$, which factors
as $(r-2)(r+1)^2\leq 0$. Since $(r+1)^2>0$, we get $r\leq 2$, i.e., $a\leq 2b$.
$\checkmark$

**Equality for triangles.** For a triangle $T$ with centroid $g$, in the directi
on toward a vertex, $a=R$ (circumradius) and $b=r$ (inradius) with $R=2r$, givin
g $a/b=2$. The centroid is the unique minimizer of $s$ for a simplex, so $s(T)=2
$. Hence $s_{\max}=2$.

**Conclusion.** The smallest $t$ is
$$t^\ast = \frac{s_{\max}-1}{s_{\max}+1} = \frac{2-1}{2+1} = \boxed{\dfrac{1}{3}
}.$$

This is achieved (necessarily) by triangles: for a triangle, no point $p$ makes
$\sup_u\frac{|a-b|}{a+b}<\frac{1}{3}$, while $t=\frac{1}{3}$ suffices for the ce
ntroid. For all other convex bodies, $s(S)<2$ so $t=\frac{1}{3}$ is more than en
