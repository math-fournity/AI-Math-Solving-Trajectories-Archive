**Setup.** Let $X = \ell^1$ with $\|x\| = \sum_{n=1}^\infty |x_n|$. The operator
 $B$ acts by

$$(Bx)_n = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{b^n}{n!}\, x_{n+k} = \frac{b^n}{n!}\sum_{k=1}
^\infty x_{n+k}, \qquad n \ge 1.$$

Define the tail sum $S_n := \sum_{k=1}^\infty x_{n+k} = \sum_{j=n+1}^\infty x_j$
, so $(Bx)_n = \frac{b^n}{n!}\,S_n$.

---

**Upper bound.** For $x \in \ell^1$,

$$\|Bx\| = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{b^n}{n!}\,|S_n| \le \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{b
^n}{n!}\sum_{j=n+1}^\infty |x_j|.$$

Swapping the order of summation (all terms non-negative): for fixed $j$, the ind
ex $n$ satisfies $n+1 \le j$, i.e. $1 \le n \le j-1$. Hence

$$\|Bx\| \le \sum_{j=2}^\infty |x_j|\sum_{n=1}^{j-1}\frac{b^n}{n!} \le \sum_{j=2
}^\infty |x_j|\underbrace{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b^n}{n!}}_{= e^b - 1} = (e^b
- 1)\sum_{j=2}^\infty |x_j| \le (e^b - 1)\,\|x\|.$$

Therefore $\|B\| \le e^b - 1$.

---

**Lower bound.** Take $x = e_j$, the $j$-th standard basis vector ($\|e_j\|=1$).
 Then $x_{n+k}=1$ iff $n+k=j$, i.e. $k=j-n\ge 1$, which requires $n \le j-1$. Th
us

$$(Be_j)_n = \begin{cases} \frac{b^n}{n!}, & 1 \le n \le j-1,\\ 0, & n \ge j.\en
d{cases}$$

So $\|Be_j\| = \sum_{n=1}^{j-1}\frac{b^n}{n!}$. The partial sums $a_j := \sum_{n
=1}^{j-1}\frac{b^n}{n!}$ are increasing in $j$ and converge to $e^b - 1$, hence

$$\|B\| \ge \sup_{j\ge 1}\|Be_j\| = \sup_{j\ge 1} a_j = \lim_{j\to\infty} a_j =
e^b - 1.$$

---

**Conclusion.** Combining both bounds,

$$\boxed{\|B\| = e^b - 1}.$$
