Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum length of a sequence of cons
ecutive "resistant" numbers, where $n \geq 2$ is resistant if $\gcd(n, \sigma(n)
) = 1$.

**Answer: 5**

## Proof

**Key Lemma.** $\sigma(n)$ is odd if and only if $n$ is a perfect square or twic
e a perfect square.

*Proof of Lemma.* Write $n = 2^a \prod p_i^{e_i}$ (odd primes $p_i$). Then $\sig
ma(n) = (2^{a+1}-1) \prod \sigma(p_i^{e_i})$. Since $2^{a+1}-1$ is always odd, a
nd $\sigma(p_i^{e_i}) = 1 + p_i + \cdots + p_i^{e_i}$ has $e_i + 1$ odd terms (h
ence odd iff $e_i$ is even), we get: $\sigma(n)$ is odd iff every $e_i$ is even,
 i.e., $n = 2^a m^2$ with $m$ odd. If $a$ is even, $n$ is a square; if $a$ is od
d, $n = 2 \cdot (2^{(a-1)/2} m)^2$ is twice a square. $\square$

**Upper Bound: No run of 6 consecutive resistant numbers exists.**

Any 6 consecutive integers contain 3 even numbers of the form $a, a+2, a+4$. For
 an even number $n$ to be resistant, we need $\gcd(n, \sigma(n)) = 1$, which req
uires $\sigma(n)$ to be odd (since $2 \mid n$). By the Lemma, each of $a, a+2, a
+4$ must be of the form $4s^2$ (even square) or $2s^2$ (twice a square).

**Claim:** No three numbers $a, a+2, a+4$ (with $a \geq 2$) can all be of the fo
rm $4s^2$ or $2s^2$.

*Proof of Claim.* Call $4s^2$ "type S" and $2s^2$ "type T". Two type-S numbers d
iffering by 2 would require $4(s'^2 - s^2) = 2$, i.e., $s'^2 - s^2 = 1/2$, impos
sible. Two type-T numbers differing by 2 would require $2(s'^2 - s^2) = 2$, i.e.
, $(s'-s)(s'+s) = 1$, giving $s = 0$ (so $a = 0$, excluded). So adjacent pairs m
ust be different types, forcing the pattern (S,T,S) or (T,S,T).

- **Case (S,T,S):** $a = 4s_1^2,\; a+2 = 2t^2,\; a+4 = 4s_3^2$. This gives $t^2
- 2s_1^2 = 1$ and $2s_3^2 - t^2 = 1$. Adding: $s_3^2 - s_1^2 = 1$, so $(s_3 - s_
1)(s_3 + s_1) = 1$, yielding $s_1 = 0$, hence $a = 0 < 2$. Contradiction.

- **Case (T,S,T):** $a = 2t_1^2,\; a+2 = 4s^2,\; a+4 = 2t_3^2$. This gives $2s^2
 - t_1^2 = 1$ and $t_3^2 - 2s^2 = 1$. Adding: $t_3^2 - t_1^2 = 2$, so $(t_3 - t_
1)(t_3 + t_1) = 2$, yielding $t_1 = 1/2$, not an integer. Contradiction.

Both cases are impossible for $a \geq 2$. $\square$

Therefore, among any 3 even numbers $a, a+2, a+4$ with $a \geq 2$, at least one
has $\sigma$ even, hence $2 \mid \gcd(n, \sigma(n))$, so it is not resistant. No
 6 consecutive resistant numbers exist.

**Lower Bound: A run of 5 consecutive resistant numbers exists.**

Consider $\{575, 576, 577, 578, 579\}$.

- **$575 = 5^2 \cdot 23$:** $\sigma(575) = 31 \cdot 24 = 744 = 2^3 \cdot 3 \cdot
 31$. Since $575 = 5^2 \cdot 23$ shares no prime factor with $744$, $\gcd = 1$.
✓

- **$576 = 2^6 \cdot 3^2$:** $\sigma(576) = 127 \cdot 13 = 1651 = 127 \cdot 13$.
 Since $576 = 2^6 \cdot 3^2$ shares no prime factor with $1651$, $\gcd = 1$. ✓

- **$577$ (prime):** $\sigma(577) = 578 = 2 \cdot 17^2$. $\gcd(577, 578) = 1$. ✓

- **$578 = 2 \cdot 17^2$:** $\sigma(578) = 3 \cdot 307 = 921 = 3 \cdot 307$. Sin
ce $578 = 2 \cdot 17^2$ shares no prime factor with $921$, $\gcd = 1$. ✓

- **$579 = 3 \cdot 193$:** $\sigma(579) = 4 \cdot 194 = 776 = 2^3 \cdot 97$. Sin
ce $579 = 3 \cdot 193$ shares no prime factor with $776$, $\gcd = 1$. ✓

(Here $193$ is prime, verified by checking divisibility by all primes $\leq \sqr
t{193} \approx 13.9$.)

**Boundary check:** $574 = 2 \cdot 7 \cdot 41$ has $\sigma(574) = 3 \cdot 8 \cdo
t 42 = 1008$, and $\gcd(574, 1008) = 14 \neq 1$ (not resistant). $580 = 2^2 \cdo
t 5 \cdot 29$ has $\sigma(580) = 7 \cdot 6 \cdot 30 = 1260$, and $\gcd(580, 1260
) = 20 \neq 1$ (not resistant). So the run is exactly 5.

**Conclusion.** The maximum length of a sequence of consecutive resistant number
s is $\boxed{5}$.
