## Proof

**Claim:** $P\!\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty} C_n\right) = 1$.

**Step 1: The events $C_n$ are independent.**

Since $(X_n)$ are independent random variables and $C_n = \{|X_n| \geq nK\}$ dep
ends only on $X_n$, the events $(C_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ are independent.

**Step 2: Reduce to a question about $\sum P(C_n)$.**

By the second Borel–Cantelli lemma, since the $C_n$ are independent, if $\sum_{n
=1}^{\infty} P(C_n) = \infty$ then $P(\limsup C_n) = 1$. So it suffices to show
$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P(|X_1| \geq nK) = \infty$ (using identical distribution to
 replace $X_n$ by $X_1$).

**Step 3: Relate the sum to $E[|X_1|]$ via the tail-integral formula.**

For a non-negative random variable $Y$,
$$E[Y] = \int_0^{\infty} P(Y \geq t)\, dt.$$

Applying this to $Y = |X_1|$ and partitioning $[0,\infty)$ into intervals $[nK,
(n+1)K)$:

$$E[|X_1|] = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \int_{nK}^{(n+1)K} P(|X_1| \geq t)\, dt.$$

Since $t \mapsto P(|X_1| \geq t)$ is non-increasing, on each interval $[nK, (n+1
)K)$ we have $P(|X_1| \geq t) \leq P(|X_1| \geq nK)$, so

$$E[|X_1|] \leq \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} K \cdot P(|X_1| \geq nK) = K\!\left(1 + \sum
_{n=1}^{\infty} P(|X_1| \geq nK)\right).$$

**Step 4: Conclude the sum diverges.**

By hypothesis, $X_1$ is not integrable, i.e., $E[|X_1|] = \infty$. From Step 3,

$$\infty = E[|X_1|] \leq K\!\left(1 + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P(|X_1| \geq nK)\right
).$$

Since $K > 0$ is finite, this forces

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P(|X_1| \geq nK) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P(C_n) = \infty.$$

**Step 5: Apply the second Borel–Cantelli lemma.**

The events $C_n$ are independent (Step 1) and $\sum P(C_n) = \infty$ (Step 4). B
y the second Borel–Cantelli lemma,

$$P\!\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty} C_n\right) = 1. \qquad \blacksquare$$
