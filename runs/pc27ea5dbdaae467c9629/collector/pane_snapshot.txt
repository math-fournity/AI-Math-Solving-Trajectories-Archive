Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the supremum $S = \sup\{|g(
z_1)-g(z_2)| : g: U \to \mathbb{D} \text{ analytic}\}$ is attained by some analy
tic $f: U \to \mathbb{D}$.

**Answer: Yes, such an $f$ always exists.**

**Proof.**

**Step 1: The supremum $S$ is finite and positive.**

Finiteness is immediate: for any $g: U \to \mathbb{D}$, $|g(z_1) - g(z_2)| \leq
|g(z_1)| + |g(z_2)| < 2$, so $S \leq 2$.

For positivity: since $U$ is bounded, there exists $R > 0$ with $U \subset B(0,
R)$. The function $g_0(z) = \frac{z}{2R}$ is analytic, maps $U \to \mathbb{D}$,
and is non-constant. Since $z_1 \neq z_2$, $|g_0(z_1) - g_0(z_2)| = \frac{|z_1 -
 z_2|}{2R} > 0$. Hence $S \geq \frac{|z_1-z_2|}{2R} > 0$.

**Step 2: Apply Montel's theorem to extract a convergent subsequence.**

The family $\mathcal{F} = \{g : U \to \mathbb{D} \text{ analytic}\}$ is uniforml
y bounded (by 1), hence is a **normal family** by Montel's theorem. Choose a seq
uence $(g_n) \subset \mathcal{F}$ with $|g_n(z_1) - g_n(z_2)| \to S$. By normali
ty, there exists a subsequence $(g_{n_k})$ converging uniformly on compact subse
ts of $U$ to an analytic function $g^* : U \to \overline{\mathbb{D}}$.

**Step 3: The limit $g^*$ attains the supremum.**

Since $\{z_1, z_2\}$ is compact, uniform convergence on compacts gives:
$$g^*(z_1) = \lim_{k\to\infty} g_{n_k}(z_1), \qquad g^*(z_2) = \lim_{k\to\infty}
 g_{n_k}(z_2).$$
Therefore:
$$|g^*(z_1) - g^*(z_2)| = \lim_{k\to\infty} |g_{n_k}(z_1) - g_{n_k}(z_2)| = S.$$

**Step 4: $g^*$ maps $U$ into $\mathbb{D}$ (not just $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$).**

We have $g^*: U \to \overline{\mathbb{D}}$ analytic. Suppose for contradiction t
hat $|g^*(z_0)| = 1$ for some $z_0 \in U$. By the **maximum modulus principle**,
 since $|g^*|$ attains its maximum at an interior point of the domain $U$, $g^*$
 must be constant. A constant of modulus 1 gives $|g^*(z_1) - g^*(z_2)| = 0$, co
ntradicting $S > 0$ (from Step 1).

Therefore $|g^*(z)| < 1$ for all $z \in U$, i.e., $g^*: U \to \mathbb{D}$.

**Conclusion.**

Setting $f = g^*$, we have $f: U \to \mathbb{D}$ analytic with $|f(z_1) - f(z_2)
| = S$. The supremum is attained. $\blacksquare$
