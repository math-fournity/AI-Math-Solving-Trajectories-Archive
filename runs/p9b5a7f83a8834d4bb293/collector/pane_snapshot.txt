Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether isomorphic projective cover
s of simple objects force the simple objects to be isomorphic, in a general abel
ian category.

**Answer: Yes, $X$ and $Y$ are necessarily isomorphic.**

**Proof.**

Let $\pi_X: P(X) \to X$ and $\pi_Y: P(Y) \to Y$ be the projective covers. Suppos
e $P(X) \cong P(Y)$; let $\phi: P(X) \to P(Y)$ be an isomorphism. Set $P = P(X)$
 and define $\psi := \pi_Y \circ \phi: P \to Y$.

Since $\pi_Y$ is an epimorphism and $\phi$ is an isomorphism, $\psi$ is an epimo
rphism. Since $\ker(\pi_Y)$ is superfluous in $P(Y)$ and $\phi$ is an isomorphis
m, $\ker(\psi) = \phi^{-1}(\ker(\pi_Y))$ is superfluous in $P$.

So we have two epimorphisms $\pi_X: P \to X$ and $\psi: P \to Y$, both with supe
rfluous kernels, both targeting simple objects.

**Lemma.** If $f: P \to S$ is an epimorphism with superfluous kernel, where $S$
is simple, then $\ker(f)$ is the *unique* maximal subobject of $P$.

*Proof of Lemma.* Since $S$ is simple and $f$ is epi, $P/\ker(f) \cong S$ is sim
ple, so $\ker(f)$ is a maximal subobject of $P$. Let $M$ be any maximal subobjec
t of $P$. If $M \not\subseteq \ker(f)$, then $M + \ker(f)$ is a subobject strict
ly containing $M$ (since $\ker(f)$ has elements outside $M$). By maximality of $
M$, this gives $M + \ker(f) = P$. But $\ker(f)$ is superfluous, so $M + \ker(f)
= P$ forces $M = P$, contradicting that $M$ is a proper subobject. Therefore $M
\subseteq \ker(f)$. Since $M$ is maximal and $\ker(f) \neq P$ (as $f$ is epi), w
e conclude $M = \ker(f)$. $\square$

Applying the Lemma to $\pi_X: P \to X$: $\ker(\pi_X)$ is the unique maximal subo
bject of $P$.

Applying the Lemma to $\psi: P \to Y$: $\ker(\psi)$ is the unique maximal subobj
ect of $P$.

Therefore $\ker(\pi_X) = \ker(\psi)$, and by the first isomorphism theorem:
$$X \cong P/\ker(\pi_X) = P/\ker(\psi) \cong Y.$$

Hence $X \cong Y$. $\blacksquare$

The key insight is that a superfluous maximal subobject must be the *only* maxim
al subobject — any other maximal subobject $M$ would, together with the superflu
ous kernel, generate all of $P$, forcing $M = P$, a contradiction. This pins dow
n the quotient (the simple object) uniquely from the projective cover.
