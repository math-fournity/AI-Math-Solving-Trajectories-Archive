# Proof

**Claim:** Yes. If $F:\mathcal C\to\mathcal D$ is left-cancellable, then $F$ is
injective on morphisms.

**Setup.** Recall that $F$ being *left-cancellable* means: for every category $\
mathcal B$ and every pair of functors $G,H:\mathcal B\to\mathcal C$, if $F\circ
G = F\circ H$ then $G=H$. (This is the statement that $F$ is an epimorphism in $
\mathbf{Cat}$.)

**Key observation.** Let $\mathbf{2}$ denote the "walking arrow" category: two o
bjects $0,1$ and a single non-identity morphism $\alpha:0\to 1$. A functor $K:\m
athbf{2}\to\mathcal C$ is completely determined by a morphism of $\mathcal C$: i
t picks objects $K(0), K(1)$ and a morphism $K(\alpha):K(0)\to K(1)$. Thus there
 is a natural bijection

$$\operatorname{Fun}(\mathbf{2},\,\mathcal C)\;\cong\;\operatorname{Mor}(\mathca
l C),$$

and likewise $\operatorname{Fun}(\mathbf{2},\mathcal D)\cong\operatorname{Mor}(\
mathcal D)$. Under this bijection, post-composition with $F$ sends a morphism $f
:a\to b$ in $\mathcal C$ to the morphism $F(f):F(a)\to F(b)$ in $\mathcal D$.

**The argument.** Suppose $f,g$ are morphisms in $\mathcal C$ with $F(f)=F(g)$ (
as morphisms in $\mathcal D$). We must show $f=g$.

Define functors $G,H:\mathbf{2}\to\mathcal C$ by:
- $G$ corresponds to $f$: $G(0)=\operatorname{dom}(f)$, $G(1)=\operatorname{cod}
(f)$, $G(\alpha)=f$.
- $H$ corresponds to $g$: $H(0)=\operatorname{dom}(g)$, $H(1)=\operatorname{cod}
(g)$, $H(\alpha)=g$.

Since $F(f)=F(g)$ as morphisms in $\mathcal D$, they share the same domain and c
odomain in $\mathcal D$:

$$F(\operatorname{dom}f)=\operatorname{dom}(F(f))=\operatorname{dom}(F(g))=F(\op
eratorname{dom}g),$$
$$F(\operatorname{cod}f)=\operatorname{cod}(F(f))=\operatorname{cod}(F(g))=F(\op
eratorname{cod}g).$$

Therefore $F\circ G$ and $F\circ H$ agree on objects (both send $0$ to $\operato
rname{dom}(F(f))$ and $1$ to $\operatorname{cod}(F(f))$) and agree on the morphi
sm $\alpha$ (both send it to $F(f)=F(g)$). Hence

$$F\circ G = F\circ H.$$

By left-cancellability of $F$ (applied with $\mathcal B=\mathbf{2}$), we conclud
e $G=H$. In particular $G(\alpha)=H(\alpha)$, i.e., $f=g$.

**Conclusion.** $F(f)=F(g)\Rightarrow f=g$, so $F$ is injective on morphisms. $\
blacksquare$
