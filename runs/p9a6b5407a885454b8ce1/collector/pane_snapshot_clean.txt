## Solution

**Setup.** Label the vertices of the regular decagon $0, 1, 2, \ldots, 9$ around
 the polygon. A chord joining vertices $i$ and $j$ (with $i \neq j$) is a **diag
onal** if and only if $j \not\equiv i \pm 1 \pmod{10}$.

**Parallelism criterion.** In a regular $n$-gon inscribed in a circle, the chord
 $\{i, j\}$ is perpendicular to the radius through the midpoint of the arc, whic
h lies at angle $\frac{(i+j)\pi}{n}$. Two chords are parallel if and only if the
ir perpendicular directions coincide, i.e.,

$$i + j \equiv k + l \pmod{n}.$$

For $n = 10$, distinct residues $s \in \{0, 1, \ldots, 9\}$ give distinct direct
ions (since $s \equiv s' \pmod{10}$ with $s, s' \in \{0,\ldots,9\}$ forces $s =
s'$). So we group diagonals by the value $s = i + j \pmod{10}$ and count pairs w
ithin each group.

**Counting chords per sum.** For a fixed $s$, the equation $i + j \equiv s \pmod
{10}$ assigns $j = s - i$ to each $i$, giving 10 ordered pairs. We need $i \neq
j$, i.e., $2i \not\equiv s \pmod{10}$.

- **$s$ even:** $2i \equiv s \pmod{10}$ has solutions $i \in \{s/2,\; s/2 + 5\}$
 (2 values), leaving $10 - 2 = 8$ ordered pairs $= \mathbf{4}$ unordered chords.
- **$s$ odd:** $2i$ is always even, so $2i \equiv s \pmod{10}$ has **no** soluti
on; all 10 ordered pairs are valid $= \mathbf{5}$ unordered chords.

**Subtracting edges.** An edge $\{i, i+1\}$ has sum $2i+1$, which is always **od
d**. As $i$ ranges over $0, \ldots, 9$, the value $2i+1 \pmod{10}$ cycles throug
h $1, 3, 5, 7, 9$ twice each. So each odd $s$ accounts for exactly **2 edges**,
and even $s$ accounts for **0 edges**.

**Diagonals per sum:**

| Sum $s$ | Chords | Edges | Diagonals | Pairs $\binom{d}{2}$ |
|---------|--------|-------|-----------|---------------------|
| $s \in \{0,2,4,6,8\}$ (even) | 4 | 0 | 4 | $\binom{4}{2}=6$ |
| $s \in \{1,3,5,7,9\}$ (odd) | 5 | 2 | 3 | $\binom{3}{2}=3$ |

**Total.** Summing over all 10 directions:

$$5 \times \binom{4}{2} + 5 \times \binom{3}{2} = 5 \times 6 + 5 \times 3 = 30 +
 15 = \boxed{45}.$$
