## Proof

Consider $f(z) = \dfrac{z}{e^z - 1}$ and a closed contour $\gamma$ enclosing two
 poles and one removable singularity of $f$.

**Singularities of $f$.** The denominator vanishes when $e^z = 1$, i.e. at $z_k
= 2\pi i k$ for $k \in \mathbb{Z}$.

**At $z_0 = 0$ (the case $k=0$):** Since $e^z - 1 = z + \dfrac{z^2}{2} + \cdots$
, we have
$$f(z) = \frac{z}{z\left(1 + \frac{z}{2} + \cdots\right)} = \frac{1}{1 + z/2 + \
cdots},$$
which is analytic in a neighborhood of $0$. Hence $z=0$ is a **removable singula
rity** (with value $f(0)=1$) and contributes no residue.

**At $z_k = 2\pi i k$ for $k \neq 0$:** These are simple zeros of $e^z-1$ (since
 $(e^z-1)' = e^{z_k} = 1 \neq 0$), so each is a **simple pole** of $f$. The resi
due at $z_k$ is
$$\operatorname{Res}(f, z_k) = \lim_{z \to z_k} (z - z_k)\frac{z}{e^z - 1} = \fr
ac{z_k}{e^{z_k}} = \frac{2\pi i k}{1} = 2\pi i k.$$

**Identifying the enclosed singularities.** The problem states $\gamma$ encloses
 exactly one removable singularity and two poles. The unique removable singulari
ty is $z=0$. The two poles must be a pair of the $z_k$ with $k \neq 0$. The natu
ral (and minimal) configuration enclosing $z=0$ together with two poles is the p
air of nearest poles to the origin, namely
$$z_1 = 2\pi i \quad (k=1), \qquad z_{-1} = -2\pi i \quad (k=-1).$$

**Residues at the two enclosed poles:**
$$\operatorname{Res}(f, 2\pi i) = 2\pi i \cdot 1 = 2\pi i, \qquad \operatorname{
Res}(f, -2\pi i) = 2\pi i \cdot (-1) = -2\pi i.$$

**Applying the residue theorem.** The removable singularity at $z=0$ contributes
 $0$ (the function is analytic there after removal). Thus
$$\int_\gamma \frac{z}{e^z - 1}\,dz = 2\pi i \sum \operatorname{Res} = 2\pi i \b
igl(2\pi i + (-2\pi i)\bigr) = 2\pi i \cdot 0 = 0.$$

**Remark on generality.** Even if the two enclosed poles were some other pair $z
_k, z_m$ (not necessarily $\pm 2\pi i$), the sum of residues would be $2\pi i(k+
m)$, and the integral would be $2\pi i \cdot 2\pi i(k+m) = -4\pi^2(k+m)$. For th
e canonical contour enclosing the removable singularity at the origin together w
ith the two adjacent poles $z_{\pm 1}$, the symmetric pairing forces $k+m = 0$,
giving $0$.

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \int_\gamma \frac{z}{e^z-1}\,dz = 0}$$
