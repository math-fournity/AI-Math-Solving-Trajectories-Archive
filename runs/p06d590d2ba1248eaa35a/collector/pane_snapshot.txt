## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, there exists a finite-index subgroup $H \le G$ such that $x$ is
fixed by the restricted action of $H$.

**Construction.** Since $K$ is $G$-invariant and finite, the action of $G$ on $X
$ restricts to an action of $G$ on $K$: for every $g \in G$ and every $y \in K$,
 we have $\varphi(g, y) \in K$. This action on the finite set $K$ is by permutat
ions, so it defines a group homomorphism
$$\rho : G \longrightarrow \operatorname{Sym}(K), \qquad \rho(g) = \big(y \mapst
o \varphi(g,y)\big).$$

Let $H := \ker(\rho) = \{g \in G : \varphi(g, y) = y \text{ for all } y \in K\}$
.

**$H$ has finite index in $G$.** Since $K$ is finite, $\operatorname{Sym}(K)$ is
 a finite group. The image $\rho(G)$ is a subgroup of $\operatorname{Sym}(K)$, h
ence finite. By the first isomorphism theorem,
$$[G : H] = [G : \ker(\rho)] = |\rho(G)| \le |\operatorname{Sym}(K)| < \infty.$$

**$x$ is a fixed point of the $H$-action.** Since $x \in K$ (as $K = \{x, \varph
i(g_0, x), \ldots, \varphi(g_n, x)\}$), every $h \in H = \ker(\rho)$ fixes every
 point of $K$ by definition, and in particular $\varphi(h, x) = x$ for all $h \i
n H$.

**Remark.** In fact $H = \ker(\rho)$ fixes *every* point of $K$, not just $x$. T
he stabilizer $\operatorname{Stab}(x) = \{g \in G : \varphi(g,x) = x\}$ contains
 $\ker(\rho)$, so $[G : \operatorname{Stab}(x)] \le [G : \ker(\rho)] < \infty$,
confirming that $\operatorname{Stab}(x)$ itself is a finite-index subgroup fixin
g $x$.

Therefore, taking $H = \ker(\rho)$ (or $H = \operatorname{Stab}(x)$), we obtain
a finite-index subgroup of $G$ for which $x$ is a fixed point. $\blacksquare$
