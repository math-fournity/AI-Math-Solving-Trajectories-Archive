Looking at this problem, I need to determine the minimum class size that guarant
ees either (a) a group of 6 students all pairwise acquainted (shared a prior cla
ss), or (b) a group of 6 students all pairwise unacquainted (first class togethe
r).

## Proof

**Model.** Consider a class of $n$ students. For each pair of students $\{u, v\}
$, color the edge $uv$ **red** if $u$ and $v$ have taken at least one other clas
s together before, and **blue** if this is the first class they share. This give
s a 2-coloring of the edges of the complete graph $K_n$.

**Equivalence to Ramsey's theorem.**
- Condition (a) — "six students where every pair has taken at least one other cl
ass together" — is exactly a red $K_6$ (a set of 6 vertices with all $\binom{6}{
2}=15$ edges colored red).
- Condition (b) — "six students where every pair is together in a class for the
first time" — is exactly a blue $K_6$ (a set of 6 vertices with all edges colore
d blue).

Thus the question asks: what is the smallest $n$ such that every 2-coloring of $
K_n$ contains a monochromatic $K_6$?

**This is precisely the diagonal Ramsey number $R(6,6)$.**

By Ramsey's theorem, $R(s,t)$ is the smallest integer $n$ such that every red/bl
ue coloring of the edges of $K_n$ contains either a red $K_s$ or a blue $K_t$. S
etting $s = t = 6$, the answer to the problem is:

$$\boxed{R(6,6)}$$

**Existence and finiteness.** Ramsey's theorem guarantees that $R(6,6)$ is a wel
l-defined finite integer. The classical recursive upper bound gives:
$$R(s,t) \leq R(s-1, t) + R(s,t-1),$$
so $R(6,6) \leq 2 \cdot R(5,6)$, and iterating down to known base cases (e.g., $
R(3,3)=6$, $R(4,4)=18$, $R(5,5)=43$) yields a finite upper bound. A lower bound
is provided by explicit constructions (e.g., via the probabilistic method or alg
ebraic constructions) showing $R(6,6) \geq 102$.

**Why this is the exact answer.** The problem asks for the *guaranteed* threshol
d — the smallest $n$ forcing one of the two configurations in *every* possible a
cquaintance pattern. This is exactly the definition of $R(6,6)$: for $n < R(6,6)
$, there exists a 2-coloring of $K_n$ with no monochromatic $K_6$ (corresponding
 to a class arrangement avoiding both conditions), while for $n = R(6,6)$, every
 2-coloring must contain a monochromatic $K_6$ (corresponding to a guaranteed sa
tisfaction of condition (a) or (b)).

The answer is $R(6,6)$.
