## Proof

We work in ordinal arithmetic. For a fixed ordinal $\alpha > 1$, the function
$$f(\xi) = \alpha \cdot \xi$$
is a **normal function**: it is strictly increasing (since $\alpha > 1$) and con
tinuous (it commutes with suprema of nonempty sets, i.e. $\alpha \cdot \sup_{i}
\xi_i = \sup_i (\alpha \cdot \xi_i)$).

We seek the least ordinal $\beta > 0$ with $f(\beta) = \beta$, i.e. the least no
nzero fixed point of $f$.

**Construction.** Define the iteration of $f$ starting from $1$:
$$\beta_0 = 1, \qquad \beta_{n+1} = f(\beta_n) = \alpha \cdot \beta_n.$$
Explicitly:
- $\beta_0 = 1 = \alpha^0$,
- $\beta_1 = \alpha \cdot 1 = \alpha = \alpha^1$,
- $\beta_2 = \alpha \cdot \alpha = \alpha^2$,
- $\beta_{n} = \alpha^n$ by induction.

Set
$$\beta \;=\; \sup_{n < \omega} \beta_n \;=\; \sup_{n < \omega} \alpha^n \;=\; \
alpha^{\omega}.$$

**$\beta$ is a fixed point.** By continuity of left multiplication,
$$\alpha \cdot \beta \;=\; \alpha \cdot \sup_{n<\omega} \alpha^n \;=\; \sup_{n<\
omega} (\alpha \cdot \alpha^n) \;=\; \sup_{n<\omega} \alpha^{n+1} \;=\; \sup_{n<
\omega} \alpha^n \;=\; \beta,$$
where the last equality uses that $\{\alpha^{n+1} : n < \omega\}$ is cofinal in
$\{\alpha^n : n < \omega\}$ (since $\alpha^0 = 1 \leq \alpha$). Hence $\alpha \c
dot \beta = \beta$.

**Minimality.** Suppose $\gamma$ is any ordinal with $0 < \gamma < \beta$ and $\
alpha \cdot \gamma = \gamma$. Since $\gamma > 0$, we have $\gamma \geq 1 = \alph
a^0$. By induction, assume $\gamma \geq \alpha^n$; then by monotonicity of left
multiplication,
$$\gamma \;=\; \alpha \cdot \gamma \;\geq\; \alpha \cdot \alpha^n \;=\; \alpha^{
n+1}.$$
Thus $\gamma \geq \alpha^n$ for **every** $n < \omega$, so
$$\gamma \;\geq\; \sup_{n<\omega} \alpha^n \;=\; \beta,$$
contradicting $\gamma < \beta$. Therefore no nonzero fixed point lies below $\be
ta$.

**Conclusion.** The least ordinal $\beta > 0$ satisfying $\alpha \cdot \beta = \
beta$ is
$$\boxed{\beta \;=\; \alpha^{\omega} \;=\; \sup_{n < \omega} \alpha^{n}.}$$
