## Solution

**Setup.** Place the cube with corners at all points of $\{0,1\}^3$. By the tran
sitive symmetry of the cube on its corners, we may fix $B$ at the origin without
 loss of generality. Then $A$ and $C$ are distinct corners in $\{0,1\}^3 \setmin
us \{\mathbf{0}\}$, and the vectors $\vec{BA} = A$, $\vec{BC} = C$ have componen
ts in $\{0,1\}$.

**Key parameters.** The angle at $B$ satisfies:
$$\cos \angle ABC = \frac{A \cdot C}{|A|\,|C|} = \frac{k}{\sqrt{mn}}$$
where:
- $m = |A|^2$ = Hamming weight of $A$ (number of 1s), $m \in \{1,2,3\}$,
- $n = |C|^2$ = Hamming weight of $C$, $n \in \{1,2,3\}$,
- $k = A \cdot C$ = number of coordinates where both $A$ and $C$ are 1.

Since all components are non-negative, $k \geq 0$, so $\cos\theta \geq 0$ and ev
ery angle lies in $[0°, 90°]$.

**Feasibility constraints.** For distinct $\{0,1\}^3$ vectors with weights $m, n
$ and overlap $k$:
$$\max(0,\, m+n-3) \le k \le \min(m,n), \quad \text{and} \quad \text{not } (m =
n = k).$$
The last condition excludes $A = C$ (identical vectors = same corner).

**Complete enumeration of valid $(m, n, k)$:**

| $(m,n,k)$ | $\cos\theta = k/\sqrt{mn}$ | $\theta$ |
|---|---|---|
| $(1,1,0)$ | $0$ | $90°$ |
| $(1,2,0)$ | $0$ | $90°$ |
| $(1,2,1)$ | $1/\sqrt{2}$ | $45°$ |
| $(1,3,1)$ | $1/\sqrt{3}$ | $\approx 54.74°$ |
| $(2,1,0)$ | $0$ | $90°$ |
| $(2,1,1)$ | $1/\sqrt{2}$ | $45°$ |
| $(2,2,0)$ | $0$ | $90°$ |
| $(2,2,1)$ | $1/2$ | $60°$ |
| $(2,3,2)$ | $2/\sqrt{6}$ | $\approx 35.26°$ |
| $(3,1,1)$ | $1/\sqrt{3}$ | $\approx 54.74°$ |
| $(3,2,2)$ | $2/\sqrt{6}$ | $\approx 35.26°$ |

The case $(3,3,3)$ is the only one with $m=n=3$ and is excluded (it forces $A=C$
).

**Verification with explicit corners** (all with $B = (0,0,0)$):

- $\cos = 2/\sqrt{6}$: $A=(1,1,0),\; C=(1,1,1)$. Dot $= 2$, $|A|=\sqrt{2}$, $|C|
=\sqrt{3}$. ✓
- $\cos = 1/\sqrt{2}$: $A=(1,0,0),\; C=(1,1,0)$. Dot $= 1$, $|A|=1$, $|C|=\sqrt{
2}$. ✓
- $\cos = 1/\sqrt{3}$: $A=(1,0,0),\; C=(1,1,1)$. Dot $= 1$, $|A|=1$, $|C|=\sqrt{
3}$. ✓
- $\cos = 1/2$: $A=(1,1,0),\; C=(1,0,1)$. Dot $= 1$, $|A|=\sqrt{2}$, $|C|=\sqrt{
2}$. ✓
- $\cos = 0$: $A=(1,0,0),\; C=(0,1,0)$. Dot $= 0$. ✓

**Distinct values.** The five distinct cosine values are:
$$0, \quad \frac{1}{2}, \quad \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \quad \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}, \qua
d \frac{2}{\sqrt{6}}$$
corresponding to angles $90°,\; 60°,\; 45°,\; \arccos(1/\sqrt{3}),\; \arccos(2/\
sqrt{6})$.

These are all different (numerically $0,\; 0.5,\; 0.707,\; 0.577,\; 0.816$), and
 the enumeration is exhaustive.

**The number of unique values the Transmission Angle can take is $\boxed{5}$.**
