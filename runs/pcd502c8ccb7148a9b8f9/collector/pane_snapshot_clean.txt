## Proof

**Answer: YES.**

Let $a_1, \ldots, a_t \in R_S$ be pairwise distinct. The hypothesis states that
for every prime $p$ and every prime ideal $P$ of $R_S$ lying above $p$, each $a_
i$ is congruent modulo $P$ to some rational integer $a_i' \in \mathbb{Z}$. We mu
st show that the set of primes $p$ for which $a_1', \ldots, a_t'$ fail to be pai
rwise distinct is finite.

**Key observation.** Since $a_i' \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $P \cap \mathbb{Z} = p\math
bb{Z}$, we have $a_i' \equiv a_j' \pmod{p}$ if and only if $a_i' \equiv a_j' \pm
od{P}$. Since $a_i \equiv a_i' \pmod{P}$ and $a_j \equiv a_j' \pmod{P}$, this is
 equivalent to:
$$a_i \equiv a_j \pmod{P},$$
i.e., $P \mid (a_i - a_j)$ in $R_S$.

**Finiteness argument.** For each pair $i \neq j$, the element $a_i - a_j$ is a
**nonzero** element of $R_S$ (by pairwise distinctness). The ring $R_S$ is a Ded
ekind domain (the ring of $S$-integers of a number field, with $S$ containing al
l infinite places). In a Dedekind domain, every nonzero principal ideal factors
into a finite product of prime ideals:
$$(a_i - a_j) = \mathfrak{p}_1^{e_1} \cdots \mathfrak{p}_m^{e_m}.$$
Thus only finitely many prime ideals $P$ of $R_S$ divide $(a_i - a_j)$, and cons
equently only finitely many rational primes $p$ lie below them.

**Conclusion.** The $a_i'$ are not pairwise distinct (for a given $p$ and $P$ ab
ove $p$) if and only if there exist $i \neq j$ with $P \mid (a_i - a_j)$. The se
t of primes $p$ for which this occurs is contained in the union, over all finite
ly many pairs $(i,j)$ with $i \neq j$, of the finitely many rational primes lyin
g below the finitely many prime ideal divisors of $(a_i - a_j)$. This is a **fin
ite** union of finite sets, hence finite.

Therefore, $a_1', \ldots, a_t'$ are not pairwise distinct for only finitely many
 primes $p$.
