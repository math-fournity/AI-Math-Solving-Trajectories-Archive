## Proof

Let $G$ be a finite group and $H \trianglelefteq G$ a normal subgroup. Let $\pi:
 G \to G/H$ be the canonical projection. We wish to determine
$$\bigcap_{\substack{\chi \in \operatorname{Irr}(G) \\ H \subseteq \ker \chi}} \
ker(\chi).$$

**Step 1: Identify the relevant irreducible characters.**

A standard result in character theory states that the irreducible characters of
$G$ whose kernel contains $H$ are precisely the inflations of irreducible charac
ters of $G/H$. Concretely, the map
$$\operatorname{Irr}(G/H) \longrightarrow \{\chi \in \operatorname{Irr}(G) : H \
subseteq \ker \chi\}, \qquad \bar\chi \longmapsto \bar\chi \circ \pi$$
is a bijection. For an inflated character $\chi = \bar\chi \circ \pi$, we have
$$\ker(\chi) = \pi^{-1}(\ker(\bar\chi)).$$

**Step 2: Reduce to an intersection over $G/H$.**

Using the correspondence above and the fact that preimages commute with intersec
tions:
$$\bigcap_{\substack{\chi \in \operatorname{Irr}(G) \\ H \subseteq \ker \chi}} \
ker(\chi) \;=\; \bigcap_{\bar\chi \in \operatorname{Irr}(G/H)} \pi^{-1}(\ker(\ba
r\chi)) \;=\; \pi^{-1}\!\left(\bigcap_{\bar\chi \in \operatorname{Irr}(G/H)} \ke
r(\bar\chi)\right).$$

**Step 3: The intersection of all irreducible character kernels of a finite grou
p is trivial.**

We claim that for any finite group $K$,
$$\bigcap_{\psi \in \operatorname{Irr}(K)} \ker(\psi) = \{e_K\}.$$

*Proof of claim.* Consider the (left) regular representation $\rho_{\mathrm{reg}
}$ of $K$ on $\mathbb{C}[K]$, with basis $\{e_k : k \in K\}$ and action $\rho_{\
mathrm{reg}}(g)\, e_{k} = e_{gk}$. This representation is **faithful**: if $\rho
_{\mathrm{reg}}(g) = \mathrm{Id}$, then in particular $\rho_{\mathrm{reg}}(g)\,
e_e = e_e$, but $\rho_{\mathrm{reg}}(g)\, e_e = e_g$, so $e_g = e_e$ and hence $
g = e$.

By the Artin–Wedderburn / Peter–Weyl decomposition for finite groups, the regula
r representation decomposes as
$$\rho_{\mathrm{reg}} \cong \bigoplus_{\psi \in \operatorname{Irr}(K)} \psi(1)\c
dot \psi,$$
where each irreducible $\psi$ appears with multiplicity $\psi(1)$. Therefore
$$\ker(\rho_{\mathrm{reg}}) = \bigcap_{\psi \in \operatorname{Irr}(K)} \ker(\psi
).$$
Since $\rho_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is faithful, $\ker(\rho_{\mathrm{reg}}) = \{e_K\}$,
proving the claim. $\square$

**Step 4: Conclude.**

Applying the claim with $K = G/H$:
$$\bigcap_{\bar\chi \in \operatorname{Irr}(G/H)} \ker(\bar\chi) = \{e \cdot H\}
= \{H\} \quad (\text{the identity coset in } G/H).$$

Therefore
$$\bigcap_{\substack{\chi \in \operatorname{Irr}(G) \\ H \subseteq \ker \chi}} \
ker(\chi) \;=\; \pi^{-1}(\{H\}) \;=\; H.$$

---

**Conclusion.** The intersection of the kernels of all irreducible characters of
 $G$ that contain $H$ is exactly $H$ itself:
$$\boxed{\bigcap_{\substack{\chi \in \operatorname{Irr}(G) \\ H \subseteq \ker \
chi}} \ker(\chi) = H.}$$
