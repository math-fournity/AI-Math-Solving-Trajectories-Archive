# Irreducible Complex Representations of Lie Groups

## 1. General framework

Let $G$ be a Lie group. A **complex representation** is a Lie group homomorphism
 $\rho: G \to \mathrm{GL}(V)$ for a finite-dimensional complex vector space $V$.
 It is **irreducible** if $V$ admits no nontrivial $G$-stable subspace. The clas
sification of irreducibles depends heavily on the structural type of $G$.

A key reduction comes from the **connected component**: $G/G^0$ is discrete, and
 representations of $G$ are built by inducing/projecting from representations of
 $G^0$ together with an action of the component group. So the essential problem
is the classification for **connected** Lie groups, which by Lie's theory reduce
s (in the simply-connected case) to representations of the Lie algebra $\mathfra
k{g}$.

---

## 2. Abelian Lie groups

**Theorem.** *Every irreducible finite-dimensional complex representation of a c
onnected abelian Lie group $G$ is one-dimensional.*

*Proof.* Let $\rho: G \to \mathrm{GL}(V)$ be irreducible. Since $G$ is abelian,
the operators $\{\rho(g): g \in G\}$ commute pairwise. Being a commuting family
of linear maps on a finite-dimensional complex vector space, they have a **commo
n eigenvector** $v \neq 0$ (by simultaneous triangularization, or by noting each
 $\rho(g)$ has an eigenspace and these eigenspaces are $G$-stable and nested). T
he line $\mathbb{C}v$ is then $G$-stable. By irreducibility, $V = \mathbb{C}v$,
so $\dim V = 1$. $\square$

**Consequence.** Irreducible complex representations of a connected abelian $G$
are exactly the **characters** $\chi: G \to \mathbb{C}^\times$, i.e. Lie group h
omomorphisms into $\mathbb{C}^\times$.

**Examples.**

- $G = \mathbb{R}^n$: characters are $\chi_\lambda(x) = e^{\lambda \cdot x}$ for
 $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}^n$. The unitary (bounded) ones correspond to $\lambda \
in i\mathbb{R}^n$.
- $G = U(1) = S^1$: irreducibles are $\chi_n(e^{i\theta}) = e^{in\theta}$, $n \i
n \mathbb{Z}$ (integrality from single-valuedness). This is the Fourier decompos
ition.
- $G = \mathbb{C}^\times$: characters $z \mapsto z^a \bar z^b$ are not holomorph
ic unless $b=0$; as a real Lie group, irreducibles are $z \mapsto |z|^{s} (z/|z|
)^n$, $s \in \mathbb{C}, n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

## 3. Compact Lie groups

This is the cleanest case, governed by the **Peter–Weyl theorem**.

**Theorem (Peter–Weyl).** *Let $G$ be a compact Lie group. Then:*

1. *Every finite-dimensional complex representation is unitarizable, hence compl
etely reducible (semisimple).*
2. *Every irreducible complex representation is finite-dimensional.*
3. *The set $\widehat{G}$ of (unitary) equivalence classes of irreducibles is co
untable.*
4. *$L^2(G) \cong \widehat{\bigoplus}_{\pi \in \widehat{G}} V_\pi \otimes V_\pi^
*$ as $G \times G$-representations (Hilbert direct sum).*

*Sketch.* Complete reducibility: average any Hermitian form over the Haar measur
e to obtain a $G$-invariant inner product; orthogonal complement of a subreprese
ntation is a complement. Finiteness of irreducibles: an irreducible unitary rep
embeds into $L^2(G)$ via matrix coefficients, and $L^2(G)$ decomposes discretely
 because compactness makes the regular representation decompose into finite-dime
nsional pieces. The Hilbert decomposition follows from spectral theory of compac
t self-adjoint operators (convolution operators). $\square$

**Structure of compact groups.** A connected compact Lie group $G$ has Lie algeb
ra $\mathfrak{g}$ that is **reductive**: $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{
g}) \oplus [\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]$ with $[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]$ compa
ct semisimple. Correspondingly $G \cong (Z(G)^0 \times G_{\mathrm{ss}})/\Gamma$
where $G_{\mathrm{ss}}$ is simply-connected compact semisimple and $\Gamma$ is a
 finite central subgroup. Irreducibles of $G$ are precisely those irreducibles o
f the universal cover that descend through $\Gamma$, i.e. those on which $\Gamma
$ acts trivially.

**Example: $SU(2)$.** Irreducibles are $V_n = \mathrm{Sym}^n(\mathbb{C}^2)$, $n
= 0,1,2,\dots$, with $\dim V_n = n+1$. These are indexed by the dominant integra
l weight $n$ for $\mathfrak{sl}_2$.

**Example: $SO(3)$.** $SO(3) \cong SU(2)/\{\pm I\}$, so only those $V_n$ with $-
I$ acting trivially descend, i.e. $n$ even. Irreducibles are $V_{2\ell}$, $\ell
= 0,1,2,\dots$, of dimensions $1,3,5,\dots$ — the spherical harmonics of degree
$\ell$.

## 4. Semisimple Lie groups

Let $G$ be connected semisimple (e.g. $SL_n(\mathbb{R})$, $SL_n(\mathbb{C})$, $S
O(p,q)$, $Sp_{2n}(\mathbb{R})$). The Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ is semisimple. T
he classification splits into two regimes.

### 4.1 Finite-dimensional irreducibles (Cartan–Weyl highest weight theory)

For a **complex semisimple** Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}$ (equivalent
ly, a connected simply-connected **complex** semisimple Lie group), the finite-d
imensional irreducibles are classified by the **highest weight theorem**:

**Theorem (Cartan–Weyl).** *Fix a Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$ and a choice
of positive roots. There is a bijection*
$$\{\text{dominant integral weights } \lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*\} \;\longleftri
ghtarrow\; \{\text{finite-dim irreducible } \mathfrak{g}\text{-modules}\}.$$
*A weight $\lambda$ is dominant integral if $\langle \lambda, \alpha^\vee\rangle
 \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$ for every positive root $\alpha$.*

The irreducible $L(\lambda)$ is constructed as the **Verma module quotient** $M(
\lambda)/M(\lambda)_{\text{max proper}}$, or equivalently characterized by havin
g a highest weight vector $v_\lambda$ annihilated by all positive root spaces.

**For real semisimple groups** $G$ with complexification $G_\mathbb{C}$: finite-
dimensional irreducibles of $G$ are exactly the restrictions of finite-dimension
al irreducibles of $G_\mathbb{C}$ (every finite-dim rep of $\mathfrak{g}_\mathbb
{R}$ complexifies to one of $\mathfrak{g}_\mathbb{C}$). If $G$ is not simply con
nected, one keeps only those $L(\lambda)$ trivial on the kernel of the universal
 cover $G \to \widetilde G$.

**Example: $SL_2(\mathbb{C})$.** Dominant integral weights are $n \in \mathbb{Z}
_{\geq 0}$, giving $L(n) = \mathrm{Sym}^n(\mathbb{C}^2)$, $\dim = n+1$.

**Example: $SL_n(\mathbb{C})$.** Dominant integral weights are $\lambda = (\lamb
da_1 \geq \dots \geq \lambda_{n-1} \geq 0)$ with $\lambda_i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq
 0}$; irreducibles are the Schur modules $S_\lambda(\mathbb{C}^n)$.

### 4.2 Infinite-dimensional irreducibles (for non-compact semisimple groups)

When $G$ is **non-compact semisimple**, finite-dimensional representations are t
ypically not unitary, and the physically/harmonically interesting irreducibles a
re **infinite-dimensional**. The full classification is the subject of the **Lan
glands classification**.

**Theorem (Langlands, simplified).** *Let $G$ be a real reductive group with max
imal compact $K$ and a minimal parabolic $P_0 = M_0 A_0 N_0$ (Langlands decompos
ition). Every irreducible admissible $(\mathfrak{g},K)$-module is the unique irr
educible quotient of a **standard module***
$$I(P,\sigma,\nu) = \mathrm{Ind}_P^G(\sigma \otimes e^\nu \otimes 1),$$
*where $P = MAN$ is a standard parabolic, $\sigma$ is a (tempered) irreducible r
epresentation of $M$, and $\nu \in \mathfrak{a}^*_{\mathbb{C}}$ with $\mathrm{Re
}\,\nu$ dominant. The Langlands quotient $J(P,\sigma,\nu)$ is irreducible when $
\nu$ is in the positive Weyl chamber. The tempered $\sigma$ themselves are built
 from discrete series of $M$.*

This gives a **parametrization** (not always a clean bijection without caveats a
bout reducibility points) of irreducible admissible representations.

**Discrete series (Harish-Chandra).** A connected semisimple $G$ with finite cen
ter has **discrete series representations** (irreducibles appearing discretely i
n $L^2(G)$) **if and only if $\mathrm{rank}(G) = \mathrm{rank}(K)$**, i.e. $G$ h
as a compact Cartan subgroup. They are parametrized by **regular characters** of
 that compact Cartan.

**Example: $SL_2(\mathbb{R})$.** Here $\mathrm{rank}(G) = 1 = \mathrm{rank}(K=SO
(2))$, so discrete series exists. The irreducible unitary representations consis
t of:
- **Principal series** $P^{\pm}_{it}$, $t \in \mathbb{R}$ (induced from minimal
parabolic, parameter $\nu = it$);
- **Complementary series** $C_s$, $0 < s < 1$ (non-tempered, $\nu$ real);
- **Discrete series** $D_n^{\pm}$, $n \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}$ (holomorphic/anti
-holomorphic);
- **Limits of discrete series** $D_0^{\pm}$;
- **Trivial representation**.

This is the full unitary dual, obtained by Bargmann's classification.

## 5. Summary table

| Type of $G$ (connected) | Classification of irreducible complex reps |
|---|---|
| Abelian | One-dimensional characters $\chi: G \to \mathbb{C}^\times$ |
| Compact (Peter–Weyl) | Finite-dim, unitary, countable; indexed by highest weig
hts of the semisimple part + characters of the center, modulo the finite central
 quotient |
| Complex semisimple, finite-dim | Dominant integral highest weights (Cartan–Wey
l) |
| Real non-compact semisimple, admissible | Langlands classification: irreducibl
e quotients of standard induced modules $I(P,\sigma,\nu)$ |
| Real non-compact semisimple, unitary | Unitary dual — subcollection of Langlan
ds parameters satisfying unitarity (e.g. Bargmann for $SL_2(\mathbb{R})$) |

The unifying thread: **abelian** $\Rightarrow$ simultaneous eigenvectors force 1
-dim; **compact** $\Rightarrow$ Haar averaging gives complete reducibility and P
eter–Weyl discreteness; **semisimple** $\Rightarrow$ root system + highest weigh
t controls finite-dim theory, and parabolic induction + tempered data controls t
he infinite-dim (Langlands) theory.
