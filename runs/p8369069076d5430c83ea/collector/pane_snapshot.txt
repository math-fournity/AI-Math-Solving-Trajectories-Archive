## Proof

**Claim.** Yes. For every $f\in C([0,1])$ there exists a sequence $f_j\in C^\inf
ty([0,1])$ with $f_j(x)\le f(x)$ for all $x\in[0,1]$ and $f_j\to f$ uniformly on
 $[0,1]$.

**Step 1. A continuous extension to $\mathbb{R}$.** Extend $f$ to $\widetilde f:
\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ by
$$
\widetilde f(x)=\begin{cases}f(0),&x<0,\\ f(x),&0\le x\le 1,\\ f(1),&x>1.\end{ca
ses}
$$
Then $\widetilde f$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$ and constant outside $[0,1]$,
hence uniformly continuous on $\mathbb{R}$. Define its modulus of continuity
$$
\omega(\delta):=\sup_{\substack{x,y\in\mathbb{R}\\|x-y|\le\delta}}\bigl|\widetil
de f(x)-\widetilde f(y)\bigr|,\qquad \delta\ge 0.
$$
Uniform continuity gives $\omega(\delta)\to 0$ as $\delta\to 0^+$.

**Step 2. Mollification.** Let $\rho\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ be a standard no
nnegative mollifier with $\operatorname{supp}\rho\subseteq[-1,1]$ and $\int_{\ma
thbb{R}}\rho=1$, and set $\rho_\varepsilon(t)=\varepsilon^{-1}\rho(t/\varepsilon

 Define
$$
g_\varepsilon(x):=(\widetilde f*\rho_\varepsilon)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde
 f(x-t)\,\rho_\varepsilon(t)\,dt,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}.
$$
Each $g_\varepsilon$ is $C^\infty$ on $\mathbb{R}$.

**Step 3. Approximation bound.** For every $x\in\mathbb{R}$,
$$
\bigl|g_\varepsilon(x)-\widetilde f(x)\bigr|
=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl(\widetilde f(x-t)-\widetilde f(x)\bigr)\rho_\varep
silon(t)\,dt\right|

$$

ilon\ge0$ integrates to $1$. In particular, for $x\in[0,1]$ (where $\widetilde f
=f$),
$$
g_\varepsilon(x)\le f(x)+\omega(\varepsilon),\qquad x\in[0,1]. \tag{1}
$$

**Step 4. Shifting below $f$.** Define, for $x\in[0,1]$,
$$
f_\varepsilon(x):=g_\varepsilon(x)-\omega(\varepsilon).
$$
Then $f_\varepsilon\in C^\infty([0,1])$, and by (1),
$$
f_\varepsilon(x)\le f(x),\qquad x\in[0,1].
$$
Moreover, using the lower bound $g_\varepsilon(x)\ge \widetilde f(x)-\omega(\var

\bigl|f_\varepsilon(x)-f(x)\bigr|

$$
Hence
$$
\|f_\varepsilon-f\|_{\infty,[0,1]}\le 2\,\omega(\varepsilon)\xrightarrow[\vareps

$$

**Step 5. The sequence.** Taking $\varepsilon_j=1/j$ and $f_j:=f_{\varepsilon_j}
\big|_{[0,1]}$, we obtain $f_j\in C^\infty([0,1])$ with $f_j(x)\le f(x)$ for all

Therefore such a sequence always exists. $\blacksquare$
