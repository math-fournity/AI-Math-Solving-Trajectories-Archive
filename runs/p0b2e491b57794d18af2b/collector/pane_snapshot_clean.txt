## Proof

**Step 1: Locating the roots.**

For $n \geq 1$, consider $f(x) = \tan x - x$ on $(n\pi,\,(n+\tfrac{1}{2})\pi)$.
We have $f(n\pi) = -n\pi < 0$ and $f(x)\to+\infty$ as $x\to(n+\tfrac12)\pi^-$. S
ince $f'(x)=\sec^2 x - 1 = \tan^2 x > 0$ on this interval, $f$ is strictly incre
asing, so there is exactly one zero $r_n \in (n\pi,\,(n+\tfrac12)\pi)$.

**Step 2: Reparametrization.**

Write $r_n = a_n - \beta_n$ where $a_n = (n+\tfrac12)\pi$ and $\beta_n \in (0,\t
frac\pi2)$. The equation $\tan r_n = r_n$ becomes
$$\cot\beta_n = a_n - \beta_n, \qquad\text{i.e.,}\quad \tan\beta_n = \frac{1}{a_
n-\beta_n},\quad r_n = a_n - \beta_n = \cot\beta_n.$$

**Step 3: The key difference.**

$$r_{n+1}-r_n-\pi = (a_{n+1}-\beta_{n+1})-(a_n-\beta_n)-\pi = \beta_n - \beta_{n
+1},$$
since $a_{n+1}-a_n=\pi$. So we must prove $0 < \beta_n - \beta_{n+1} < \frac{1}{
(n^2+n)\pi}$.

**Step 4: Implicit differentiation.**

Define $\beta=\beta(a)$ by $F(\beta,a)=\tan\beta\cdot(a-\beta)-1=0$. Then
$$F_a = \tan\beta,\qquad F_\beta = \sec^2\beta\cdot(a-\beta)-\tan\beta.$$
At the solution, $a-\beta = r$ and $\tan\beta=1/r$, so
$$F_\beta = \sec^2\beta\cdot r - \frac1r = r\!\left(1+\frac1{r^2}\right)-\frac1r
 = r.$$
Therefore
$$\frac{d\beta}{da} = -\frac{F_a}{F_\beta} = -\frac{\tan\beta}{r} = -\frac{1}{r^
2}.$$

**Step 5: Mean Value Theorem.**

Applying the MVT to $\beta(a)$ on $[a_n, a_{n+1}]$:
$$\beta_{n+1}-\beta_n = \left.\frac{d\beta}{da}\right|_{a=\xi}\cdot\pi = -\frac{
\pi}{\tilde r^2}$$
for some $\xi\in(a_n,a_{n+1})$, where $\tilde r = \xi - \beta(\xi)\in(r_n, r_{n+
1})$. Thus
$$\beta_n - \beta_{n+1} = \frac{\pi}{\tilde r^2} > 0,$$
which gives the **left inequality** $r_{n+1}-r_n-\pi > 0$.

**Step 6: Proving $r_n^2 > (n^2+n)\pi^2$.**

Since $\tilde r > r_n$, it suffices to show $r_n^2 > (n^2+n)\pi^2$. Using $r_n =
 \cot\beta_n$ and $a_n = \beta_n + \cot\beta_n$:
$$r_n^2 - (n^2+n)\pi^2 = \cot^2\beta_n - \bigl(a_n^2 - \tfrac{\pi^2}{4}\bigr) =
\cot^2\beta_n - (\beta_n+\cot\beta_n)^2 + \frac{\pi^2}{4} = \frac{\pi^2}{4} - \b
eta_n^2 - 2\beta_n\cot\beta_n.$$

We bound the two negative terms:

- Since $r_n > n\pi$ and $\tan\beta_n = 1/r_n < 1/(n\pi)$, and $\beta_n < \tan\b
eta_n$ (because $\tan x > x$ for $x\in(0,\pi/2)$), we get $\beta_n < \frac{1}{n\
pi} \le \frac{1}{\pi}$, hence $\beta_n^2 < \frac{1}{\pi^2}$.
- Since $\tan\beta_n > \beta_n$, we have $\cot\beta_n < 1/\beta_n$, hence $2\bet
a_n\cot\beta_n < 2$.

Therefore
$$r_n^2 - (n^2+n)\pi^2 > \frac{\pi^2}{4} - \frac{1}{\pi^2} - 2.$$

We claim $\frac{\pi^2}{4} > 2 + \frac{1}{\pi^2}$. This is equivalent to $\pi^4 -
 8\pi^2 - 4 > 0$. Setting $t = \pi^2$, we need $t^2 - 8t - 4 > 0$, i.e., $t > 4
+ 2\sqrt{5}$. Since $\pi > 3$ (the circumference $2\pi$ of the unit circle excee
ds the perimeter $6$ of the inscribed regular hexagon), we have $t = \pi^2 > 9$.
 And $4 + 2\sqrt{5} < 4 + 5 = 9$ (since $\sqrt{5} < \frac{5}{2}$, as $5 < \frac{
25}{4}$). Thus $t > 9 > 4+2\sqrt{5}$, confirming the claim.

Hence $r_n^2 > (n^2+n)\pi^2$ for all $n \geq 1$.

**Step 7: Conclusion.**

Since $\tilde r > r_n$ and $r_n^2 > (n^2+n)\pi^2$:
$$\beta_n - \beta_{n+1} = \frac{\pi}{\tilde r^2} < \frac{\pi}{(n^2+n)\pi^2} = \f
rac{1}{(n^2+n)\pi}.$$

Combining Steps 5 and 7:
$$\boxed{0 < r_{n+1} - r_n - \pi < \frac{1}{(n^2+n)\pi}} \qquad \text{for all }
n \ge 1. \qquad \blacksquare$$
