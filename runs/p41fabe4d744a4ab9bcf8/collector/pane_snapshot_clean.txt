Yes, such an extension exists. This is the **Stein extension theorem** (in its b
asic $L^2$ form for smooth domains). Below is a self-contained proof.

---

## Theorem

Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^N$ be a bounded open set with $C^\infty$ boundary
. There exists a linear operator $E : L^2(\Omega) \to L^2(\mathbb{R}^N)$ and a c
onstant $C = C(\Omega) > 0$ such that for every $f \in L^2(\Omega)$,
$$
(Ef)\big|_\Omega = f \quad\text{a.e.,}\qquad \|Ef\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^N)} \le C\,\
|f\|_{L^2(\Omega)}.
$$

---

## Proof

### Step 1. Local flattening of the boundary

Since $\partial\Omega$ is compact and $C^\infty$, there exist finitely many open
 sets $U_1,\dots,U_M \subset \mathbb{R}^N$ covering a tubular neighborhood of $\
partial\Omega$, and $C^\infty$ diffeomorphisms
$$
\Phi_j : U_j \to B(0,r_j) \subset \mathbb{R}^N
$$
such that
$$
\Phi_j(U_j \cap \Omega) = B'(0,r_j)\times(0,r_j), \qquad \Phi_j(U_j\cap\partial\
Omega) = B'(0,r_j)\times\{0\},
$$
where $B'$ denotes a ball in $\mathbb{R}^{N-1}$. Set $U_0 := \Omega \setminus \b
igcup_{j=1}^M \overline{U_j\cap\partial\Omega}$; this is an interior patch (posi
tive distance from $\partial\Omega$). The family $\{U_0,U_1,\dots,U_M\}$ covers
$\overline\Omega$.

Choose a smooth partition of unity $\{\eta_j\}_{j=0}^M$ subordinate to this cove
r, with $\sum_j \eta_j \equiv 1$ on $\overline\Omega$. Define $f_j := \eta_j f$,
 so $f = \sum_j f_j$ on $\Omega$ and
$$
\sum_j \|f_j\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2 \le K_1\,\|f\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2
\tag{1}
$$
for some $K_1$ depending only on the (finitely many, smooth) $\eta_j$.

### Step 2. Extension on the interior patch

On $U_0$, the function $f_0$ is supported in a set at positive distance from $\p
artial\Omega$. Define
$$
F_0(x) := \begin{cases} f_0(x), & x\in U_0\cap\Omega,\\ 0, & \text{otherwise.}\e
nd{cases}
$$
Then $F_0\in L^2(\mathbb{R}^N)$ and $\|F_0\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^N)} = \|f_0\|_{L^2(
\Omega)}$.

### Step 3. Extension on each boundary patch by reflection

Fix $j\ge 1$. Pull back to the half-ball: set
$$
g_j(y) := f_j(\Phi_j^{-1}(y)), \qquad y\in H_j := B'(0,r_j)\times(0,r_j).
$$
Because $\Phi_j$ is a $C^1$ diffeomorphism on the compact closure $\overline{U_j
}$, the Jacobian determinant satisfies
$$
0 < m_j \le |\det D\Phi_j(x)| \le M_j < \infty \quad\text{on } U_j,
$$
hence
$$
\sqrt{m_j}\,\|g_j\|_{L^2(H_j)} \le \|f_j\|_{L^2(U_j\cap\Omega)} \le \sqrt{M_j}\,
\|g_j\|_{L^2(H_j)}.
\tag{2}
$$

Now extend $g_j$ from the half-ball $H_j$ to the full ball $B_j := B'(0,r_j)\tim
es(-r_j,r_j)$ by **even reflection across $\{y_N=0\}$**:
$$
\widetilde g_j(y',y_N) := \begin{cases} g_j(y',y_N), & y_N \ge 0,\\[2pt] g_j(y',
-y_N), & y_N < 0. \end{cases}
$$
This is a measurable function on $B_j$ and
$$
\|\widetilde g_j\|_{L^2(B_j)}^2 = 2\,\|g_j\|_{L^2(H_j)}^2.
\tag{3}
$$

Push forward to $\mathbb{R}^N$: define
$$
F_j(x) := \begin{cases} \widetilde g_j(\Phi_j(x)), & x\in \Phi_j^{-1}(B_j),\\ 0,
 & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
$$
Using (2) and (3),
$$
\|F_j\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^N)}^2 \le M_j\,\|\widetilde g_j\|_{L^2(B_j)}^2 = 2M_j\,\
|g_j\|_{L^2(H_j)}^2 \le \frac{2M_j}{m_j}\,\|f_j\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2.
\tag{4}
$$

### Step 4. Glue the pieces together

Define
$$
(Ef)(x) := F_0(x) + \sum_{j=1}^M F_j(x), \qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^N.
$$

**Recovery on $\Omega$.** For $x\in\Omega$, the support of $F_j$ outside $\Omega
$ lies in the reflected copy $\Phi_j^{-1}(B_j)\setminus\overline\Omega$, which i
s disjoint from $\Omega$. Hence on $\Omega$ only the "original" part of each $F_
j$ contributes:
$$
(Ef)\big|_\Omega = f_0 + \sum_{j=1}^M f_j = \Big(\sum_{j=0}^M \eta_j\Big) f = f
\quad\text{a.e.}
$$

**Norm bound.** By the triangle inequality in $L^2$, (1), (4), and $\|F_0\|=\|f_
0\|$,
$$
\|Ef\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^N)} \le \|F_0\| + \sum_{j=1}^M \|F_j\|
\le \sqrt{K_1}\,\|f\| + \sum_{j=1}^M \sqrt{\tfrac{2M_j}{m_j}}\,\|f_j\|.
$$
Applying (1) again,
$$
\|Ef\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^N)}^2 \le (M+1)\Big(\|F_0\|^2 + \sum_{j=1}^M \|F_j\|^2\Bi
g)
\le (M+1)\,K_1\Big(1 + \sum_{j=1}^M \tfrac{2M_j}{m_j}\Big)\,\|f\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^
2.
$$
Setting
$$
C := \sqrt{(M+1)\,K_1\Big(1 + 2\sum_{j=1}^M \tfrac{M_j}{m_j}\Big)} < \infty,
$$
which depends only on the geometry of $\Omega$ (the charts $\Phi_j$ and the part
ition $\eta_j$), we obtain
$$
\boxed{\;\|Ef\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^N)} \le C\,\|f\|_{L^2(\Omega)}.\;}
$$

Linearity of $E$ is immediate from the construction (each step is linear in $f$)
. $\qquad\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** The smoothness of $\partial\Omega$ is used only to guarantee (i) a f
inite smooth atlas flattening the boundary and (ii) uniformly bounded, non-degen
erate Jacobians on compact sets. In fact the same argument works for Lipschitz d
omains (Stein's theorem), but the proof above is the clean version for $C^\infty
$ boundaries.
