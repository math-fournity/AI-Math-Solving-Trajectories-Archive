## Proof

me{Re}(z)=1$, define $g(z)=\overline{f(2-\bar z)}$. This is entire, and on the l
ine $\operatorname{Re}(z)=1$ (where $z=1+iy$, so $2-\bar z = z$):
$$g(1+iy)=\overline{f(1+iy)}=f(1+iy),$$
since $f$ is real there. By the identity theorem, $f(z)=\overline{f(2-\bar z)}$
for all $z$. The same argument on $\operatorname{Re}(z)=2$ gives $f(z)=\overline
{f(4-\bar z)}$ for all $z$.

**Step 2 — Periodicity.** From the two identities:
$$\overline{f(2-\bar z)}=\overline{f(4-\bar z)} \implies f(2-\bar z)=f(4-\bar z)
 \quad \forall\, z.$$
Setting $w=\bar z$: $f(2-w)=f(4-w)$ for all $w$, i.e., $f(u)=f(u+2)$ for all $u$
. So **$f$ has period $2$**.

**Step 3 — Factor through $\mathbb{C}^*$.** Since $f$ is entire with period $2$,
 there exists a holomorphic function $h$ on $\mathbb{C}^*=\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0
\}$ such that
$$f(z)=h\!\left(e^{\pi i z}\right).$$

**Step 4 — Symmetry of $h$.** From $f(z)=\overline{f(2-\bar z)}$ and the identit
y $e^{\pi i(2-\bar z)}=e^{2\pi i}\cdot e^{-\pi i\bar z}=\overline{e^{\pi i z}}$,
 we obtain
$$h(w)=\overline{h(\bar w)} \quad \forall\, w\in\mathbb{C}^*.$$
In particular, $h$ is real-valued on $\mathbb{R}^*$.

**Step 5 — $h$ extends to $0$ with $h(0)=0$.** For $z=x+iy$ with $1\le x\le 2$ a
nd $y\to+\infty$:
$$w=e^{\pi i z}=e^{-\pi y}\,e^{\pi i x}\to 0, \qquad \arg w=\pi x\in[\pi,2\pi]\t
ext{ (lower half-plane)}.$$
The hypothesis gives $h(w)\to 0$ as $w\to 0$ through the lower half-plane. By th
e symmetry $h(w)=\overline{h(\bar w)}$, the same holds through the upper half-pl
ane. Along the real axis ($x=1$: $w\to 0^-$; $x=2$: $w\to 0^+$), $h(w)\to 0$ as
well. Thus $h(w)\to 0$ as $w\to 0$ from **all** directions. By Riemann's removab
le singularity theorem, $h$ extends holomorphically to $0$ with $h(0)=0$.

**Step 6 — $h$ extends to $\infty$ with $h(\infty)=0$.** For $y\to-\infty$ with
$1\le x\le 2$:
$$|w|=e^{-\pi y}=e^{\pi|y|}\to\infty, \qquad \arg w=\pi x\in[\pi,2\pi].$$
The decay hypothesis (as $|y|\to\infty$) gives $h(w)\to 0$ as $w\to\infty$ throu
gh the lower half-plane. By the symmetry, the same holds through the upper half-
plane and along the real axis. So $h$ is bounded in a neighborhood of $\infty$,
and $\infty$ is a removable singularity with $h(\infty)=0$.

**Step 7 — Conclusion.** The function $h$ is now holomorphic on the entire Riema
nn sphere $\widehat{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}$ (it extends holomorphi
cally to both $0$ and $\infty$). A function holomorphic on all of $\widehat{\mat
hbb{C}}$ is constant. Since $h(0)=0$, we conclude
$$h\equiv 0, \qquad\text{hence}\qquad f(z)=h(e^{\pi i z})\equiv 0.$$

$\blacksquare$
