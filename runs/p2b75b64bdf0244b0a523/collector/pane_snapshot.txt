## Proof

**Interpretation.** The expression denotes the double power series

$$
S(u,v)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{m^{3}\,u^{m}\,v^{n}}{n!},
$$

obtained as the limit of its rectangular partial sums. We determine all $(u,v)\i
n\mathbb{R}^{2}$ for which this series converges.

**Separation of variables.** The general term factors as a product of a function
 of $m$ and a function of $n$:

$$
\frac{m^{3}\,u^{m}\,v^{n}}{n!}=\bigl(m^{3}u^{m}\bigr)\cdot\Bigl(\frac{v^{n}}{n!}
\Bigr).
$$

Hence the double series is the product of two single series:

$$
S(u,v)=\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}m^{3}u^{m}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\
frac{v^{n}}{n!}\right).
$$

**The $v$‑factor.** The series $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{v^{n}}{n!}=e^{v}$ conve
rges (absolutely) for every $v\in\mathbb{R}$, since $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|v
|^{n}}{n!}=e^{|v|}<\infty$.

**The $u$‑factor.** Consider $A(u)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}m^{3}u^{m}$.

- *Radius of convergence.* Apply the ratio test to the absolute values:
  $$
  \lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{(m+1)^{3}|u|^{m+1}}{m^{3}|u|^{m}}=|u|\cdot\lim_{m\to\in
fty}\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{m}\Bigr)^{3}=|u|.
  $$
  The series converges absolutely when $|u|<1$ and diverges when $|u|>1$.

- *Boundary $|u|=1$.* The general term has magnitude $|m^{3}u^{m}|=m^{3}\to\inft
y$, so the terms do not tend to zero. The series diverges (the necessary conditi
on for convergence fails).

Thus $A(u)$ converges if and only if $|u|<1$.

**Combining the factors.** The double series converges absolutely if and only if
 both factor series converge absolutely. The $v$‑series converges absolutely for
 all $v$; the $u$‑series converges absolutely exactly for $|u|<1$. Hence

$$
S(u,v)\ \text{converges (absolutely)}\iff |u|<1,\quad v\in\mathbb{R}.
$$

**No conditional convergence outside the strip.** If $|u|\ge 1$, set $n=0$ in th
e double series. The sub‑series $\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}m^{3}u^{m}$ has terms of mag
nitude $m^{3}|u|^{m}\ge m^{3}\not\to 0$, so the general term of the double serie
s does not tend to zero. Therefore the double series diverges for $|u|\ge 1$, re
gardless of $v$.

**Region of convergence.** The region of convergence in $\mathbb{R}^{2}$ is the
open vertical strip

$$
\boxed{\;\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\,-1<u<1\}\;}
$$

i.e. all points with $-1<u<1$ and $v$ arbitrary. On this region the sum equals

$$
S(u,v)=\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}m^{3}u^{m}\right)e^{v},
$$

where the $u$‑series is a rational function of $u$ (obtainable by differentiatin
g $\sum u^{m}=\frac{1}{1-u}$ three times and combining), but its closed form is
not required for the region of convergence.
