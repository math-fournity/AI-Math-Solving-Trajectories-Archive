## Proof

^{2q-1}$.

**Key identity (Frobenius).** In $\mathbb{F}_q[t]$, for any polynomial $f(t) = \
sum a_i t^i$ with $a_i \in \mathbb{F}_q$, we have $f(t)^q = \sum a_i^q t^{iq} =
\sum a_i t^{iq} = f(t^q)$. Therefore:
$$S(t)^{2q} = \big(S(t)^2\big)^q = S(t^q)^2.$$

Since every root $\alpha$ of $S(t)$ satisfies $\alpha^{q+1}=1$, $\alpha \neq 1$,
 we get $\alpha^{q(q+1)}=1$ and $\alpha^q = \alpha^{-1} \neq 1$, so $\alpha$ is
also a root of $S(t^q)$. Thus $S(t) \mid S(t^q)$, and:
$$P(t) = S(t)^{2q-1} = \frac{S(t)^{2q}}{S(t)} = \frac{S(t^q)^2}{S(t)} \in \mathb
b{F}_q[t].$$

**Expanding $S(t^q)^2$.** Since $S(t^q) = \sum_{i=0}^{q} t^{iq}$, we get $S(t^q)
^2 = \sum_{m=0}^{2q} c_m\, t^{mq}$, where $c_m = \min(m+1,\; 2q-m+1)$ (the coeff
icient of $t^m$ in $S(t)^2$).

**Expanding $1/S(t)$ as a formal power series.**
$$\frac{1}{S(t)} = \frac{1-t}{1-t^{q+1}} = (1-t)\sum_{k \ge 0} t^{k(q+1)}.$$
So the coefficient $d_n$ of $t^n$ in $1/S(t)$ is:
- $d_n = +1$ if $n \equiv 0 \pmod{q+1}$,
- $d_n = -1$ if $n \equiv 1 \pmod{q+1}$,
- $d_n = 0$ otherwise.

**Extracting the coefficient.** Let $N = q^2+q+1$. The coefficient of $t^N$ in $
P(t) = S(t^q)^2 \cdot \frac{1}{S(t)}$ is:
$$[t^N]\, P(t) = \sum_{m=0}^{2q} c_m \cdot d_{N - mq},$$
where $d_j = 0$ for $j < 0$. We need $N - mq \geq 0$, i.e., $m \leq \lfloor N/q
\rfloor = \lfloor q+1+1/q \rfloor = q+1$ (for $q \geq 2$).

**Computing $j_m = N - mq \pmod{q+1}$.** Since $q \equiv -1 \pmod{q+1}$ and $q^2
 \equiv 1 \pmod{q+1}$:
$$j_m = q^2 + q + 1 - mq \equiv 1 + (-1) + 1 + m = 1 + m \pmod{q+1}.$$

For $d_{j_m} \neq 0$, we need $j_m \equiv 0$ or $1 \pmod{q+1}$, i.e., $m \equiv
q$ or $m \equiv 0 \pmod{q+1}$.

Within $0 \leq m \leq q+1$, the contributing values are $m = 0$, $m = q$, and $m
 = q+1$:

| $m$ | $j_m = N - mq$ | $j_m \bmod (q{+}1)$ | $d_{j_m}$ | $c_m$ | Contribution
|
|-----|----------------|---------------------|-----------|-------|-------------|
| $0$ | $q^2+q+1$ | $1$ | $-1$ | $1$ | $-1$ |
| $q$ | $q+1$ | $0$ | $+1$ | $q+1$ | $q+1$ |
| $q+1$ | $1$ | $1$ | $-1$ | $q$ | $-q$ |

(Here $c_0 = \min(1, 2q+1) = 1$, $c_q = \min(q+1, q+1) = q+1$, $c_{q+1} = \min(q
+2, q) = q$.)

**Total:**
$$[t^{q^2+q+1}]\, P(t) = (-1) + (q+1) + (-q) = 0.$$
