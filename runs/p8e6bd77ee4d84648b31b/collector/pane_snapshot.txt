## Solution

We compute the length of the line $y = x$ inside the unit disk $A = \{x^2 + y^2
< 1\}$ under the Poincaré metric
$$ds^2 = \frac{4\,dx^2 + 4\,dy^2}{(1 - x^2 - y^2)^2}.$$

**Parametrization.** On the line $y = x$ we set $y = x$, so $dy = dx$. Hence
$$dx^2 + dy^2 = 2\,dx^2, \qquad x^2 + y^2 = 2x^2.$$
The line lies inside the disk when $2x^2 < 1$, i.e. $x \in \bigl(-\tfrac{1}{\sqr
t{2}},\,\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigr)$.

**Line element.** Substituting,
$$ds = \frac{2\sqrt{2}\,dx}{1 - 2x^2}.$$

**Length integral.**
$$L = \int_{-1/\sqrt{2}}^{1/\sqrt{2}} \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{1 - 2x^2}\,dx.$$

Set $u = \sqrt{2}\,x$, so $dx = du/\sqrt{2}$, and the limits become $u \in (-1,
1)$:
$$L = \int_{-1}^{1} \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{1 - u^2}\cdot\frac{du}{\sqrt{2}} = \int_{-1
}^{1} \frac{2}{1 - u^2}\,du = 2\Bigl[\operatorname{arctanh} u\Bigr]_{-1}^{1}.$$

**Divergence.** As $u \to 1^{-}$, $\operatorname{arctanh} u = \tfrac{1}{2}\ln\fr
ac{1+u}{1-u} \to +\infty$, and similarly $\operatorname{arctanh} u \to -\infty$
as $u \to -1^{+}$. Equivalently, near $u = 1$,
$$\frac{2}{1 - u^2} = \frac{2}{(1-u)(1+u)} \sim \frac{1}{1 - u},$$
which is not integrable (logarithmic divergence). Therefore
$$L = +\infty.$$

**Geometric interpretation.** The line $y = x$ is a diameter of the disk, hence
a geodesic of the Poincaré metric passing through the origin. Its two endpoints
lie on the boundary circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$, which is at infinite hyperbolic dist
ance from every interior point. Consequently the full segment has infinite lengt
h.

$$\boxed{L = \infty}$$
