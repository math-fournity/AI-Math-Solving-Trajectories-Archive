## Proof

We consider the Helmholtz equation on a bounded domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R
}^n$ with smooth boundary $\partial\Omega$:

$$\nabla^2 u + k^2 u = 0 \quad \text{in } \Omega,$$

subject to homogeneous boundary conditions. We treat the two canonical cases — D
irichlet ($u|_{\partial\Omega}=0$) and Neumann ($\partial u/\partial n|_{\partia
l\Omega}=0$) — and show that **nontrivial solutions exist only for a discrete se
t of values of $k$**.

---

### 1. Reformulation as an eigenvalue problem

Setting $\lambda = k^2$, the equation becomes the eigenvalue problem for the (ne
gative) Laplacian:

$$-\nabla^2 u = \lambda\, u \quad \text{in } \Omega, \qquad Bu = 0 \quad \text{o
n } \partial\Omega,$$

where $B$ denotes the boundary operator ($u$ or $\partial u/\partial n$). A nont
rivial solution $u \not\equiv 0$ exists precisely when $\lambda$ is an eigenvalu
e of $-\nabla^2$ with the given boundary condition.

---

### 2. Spectral theorem for the Dirichlet/Neumann Laplacian

Let $H = L^2(\Omega)$ with inner product $\langle f,g\rangle = \int_\Omega f\,\o
verline{g}\,dV$.

**Dirichlet case.** Define the operator
$$A_D : D(A_D) \subset H \to H, \quad A_D u = -\nabla^2 u, \quad D(A_D) = H_0^1(
\Omega) \cap H^2(\Omega).$$

**Neumann case.** Define
$$A_N : D(A_N) \subset H \to H, \quad A_N u = -\nabla^2 u, \quad D(A_N) = \{u \i
n H^2(\Omega) : \partial u/\partial n = 0 \text{ on } \partial\Omega\}.$$

Both $A_D$ and $A_N$ are **self-adjoint, positive-semidefinite** operators on $H
$ with **compact resolvent** (this follows from $\Omega$ being bounded with smoo
th boundary, via the Rellich–Kondrachov theorem: the embedding $H_0^1(\Omega) \h
ookrightarrow L^2(\Omega)$ is compact).

By the **spectral theorem for self-adjoint operators with compact resolvent**, t
he spectrum is a **discrete** sequence of real eigenvalues
$$0 \le \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \cdots, \qquad \lambda_n \to +\infty,$$
with a corresponding orthonormal basis $\{\phi_n\}$ of eigenfunctions in $L^2(\O
mega)$.

---

### 3. Discreteness of admissible $k$

Since nontrivial solutions require $\lambda = k^2$ to be an eigenvalue, and the
eigenvalues form a discrete set $\{\lambda_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$, the admissible val
ues of $k$ are

$$k = \sqrt{\lambda_n}, \qquad n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$$

(Dirichlet: all $\lambda_n > 0$; Neumann: $\lambda_1 = 0$ is allowed, giving the
 constant solution $k=0$.) These values form a **discrete, countable set** accum
ulating only at $+\infty$.

---

### 4. Positivity / energy argument (direct proof of discreteness)

For completeness, we give a direct variational argument showing the eigenvalues
are isolated. For the Dirichlet case, the Rayleigh quotient is

$$R[u] = \frac{\int_\Omega |\nabla u|^2\,dV}{\int_\Omega |u|^2\,dV}, \qquad u \i
n H_0^1(\Omega),\ u \ne 0.$$

By the Poincaré inequality (valid since $\Omega$ is bounded), $R[u] \ge C > 0$,
so $\lambda_1 = \inf R[u] > 0$ and is attained. Inductively, $\lambda_{n+1} = \i
nf\{R[u] : u \perp \phi_1,\dots,\phi_n\}$, and each step yields a strictly large
r eigenvalue (orthogonality to the previous eigenfunctions forces $R$ to increas
e). The compactness of the embedding ensures each infimum is attained, producing
 an **isolated** eigenvalue. Hence the admissible $k$-values are isolated points
, not an interval.

---

### 5. Conclusion

For the Helmholtz equation $\nabla^2 u + k^2 u = 0$ on a bounded domain with hom
ogeneous Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions, **nontrivial solutions exist
only for a discrete, countable set of values of $k$** — namely $k = \sqrt{\lambd
a_n}$ where $\{\lambda_n\}$ are the eigenvalues of $-\nabla^2$ with the prescrib
ed boundary condition. For all other values of $k$, the only solution is the tri
vial one $u \equiv 0$.

These special values of $k$ are the **eigenvalues (or resonant wavenumbers)** of
 the boundary-value problem, and the corresponding solutions are the **eigenfunc
tions** (normal modes).
