## Proof

We evaluate
$$L = \lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{nx^{2}}{1+n^{2}x^{4}}\,dx$$
using the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem (LDCT).

**Step 1. Pointwise limit of the integrand.**

Define $f_n(x)=\dfrac{nx^{2}}{1+n^{2}x^{4}}$ on $[-1,1]$.

- At $x=0$: $f_n(0)=0$ for every $n$, so $\lim_{n\to\infty}f_n(0)=0$.
- At $x\neq 0$: dividing numerator and denominator by $n^{2}$,
$$f_n(x)=\frac{x^{2}/n}{x^{4}+1/n^{2}}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\frac{0}{x^{4}}=
0.$$

Hence $f_n\to 0$ pointwise a.e. on $[-1,1]$ (in fact everywhere).

**Step 2. An integrable dominating function.**

We claim $f_n(x)\le \tfrac12$ for all $x\in[-1,1]$ and all $n\ge 1$.

Set $u=x^{2}\ge 0$ and consider $g(u)=\dfrac{nu}{1+n^{2}u^{2}}$. Then
$$g'(u)=\frac{n(1-n^{2}u^{2})}{(1+n^{2}u^{2})^{2}},$$
which vanishes at $u=1/n$ (the unique critical point in $u>0$). Since $g(0)=0$ a
nd $g(u)\to 0$ as $u\to\infty$, this critical point is the global maximum:
$$g(1/n)=\frac{n\cdot(1/n)}{1+n^{2}\cdot(1/n^{2})}=\frac{1}{1+1}=\frac12.$$

Therefore $f_n(x)=g(x^{2})\le \tfrac12$ for all $x$ and all $n\ge 1$.

The constant function $g(x)\equiv \tfrac12$ is Lebesgue-integrable on the finite
 interval $[-1,1]$, with $\int_{-1}^{1}\tfrac12\,dx=1<\infty$.

**Step 3. Apply LDCT.**

The sequence $\{f_n\}$ satisfies:
1. $f_n$ is measurable on $[-1,1]$ for each $n$ (it is continuous),
2. $f_n\to 0$ pointwise a.e.,
3. $|f_n(x)|\le \tfrac12\in L^{1}([-1,1])$ for all $n$ and all $x$.

By the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{-1}^{1}f_n(x)\,dx=\int_{-1}^{1}\lim_{n\to\infty}f_n(x)\
,dx=\int_{-1}^{1}0\,dx=0.$$

**Conclusion.**

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{nx^{2}}{1+n^{2}x^{4}}\
,dx=0.}$$
