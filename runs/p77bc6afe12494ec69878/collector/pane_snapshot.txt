## (a) Solution via Fourier (x, y) and Fourier sine (z) transforms

**Domain & conditions.** $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$, $z\in(0,\infty)$, $t>0$:
$$u_t=k(u_{xx}+u_{yy}+u_{zz}),\qquad u(x,y,z,0)=f(x,y,z),\qquad u(x,y,0,t)=0.$$

**Transforms.** Use the Fourier transform in the free variables $x,y$ and the Fo
urier *sine* transform in $z$ (the natural choice for a Dirichlet condition at $
z=0$):
$$\widehat{u}(\xi,\eta,\zeta,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\inft
y}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty} u(x,y,z,t)\,e^{-i(\xi x+\eta y)}\sin(\zeta z)\,dz\,dy\,d
x,$$
with inversion
$$u(x,y,z,t)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{-\i
nfty}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{u}(\xi,\eta,\zeta,t)\,e^{i(\xi x+\et
a y)}\sin(\zeta z)\,d\zeta\,d\eta\,d\xi.$$

**Transforming the PDE.** Under the Fourier transform in $x,y$:
$$u_{xx}\mapsto -\xi^{2}\widehat{u},\qquad u_{yy}\mapsto -\eta^{2}\widehat{u}.$$
For the sine transform in $z$, integrate $u_{zz}$ by parts twice. With $u(x,y,0,
t)=0$ and assuming $u,u_z\to0$ as $z\to\infty$:
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{zz}\sin(\zeta z)\,dz=\underbrace{\bigl[u_z\sin(\zeta z)\bi
gr]_{0}^{\infty}}_{0}-\zeta\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!u_z\cos(\zeta z)\,dz=-\zeta\Bigl
(\underbrace{\bigl[u\cos(\zeta z)\bigr]_{0}^{\infty}}_{0-u(0)}+\zeta\!\int_{0}^{
\infty}\!u\sin(\zeta z)\,dz\Bigr).$$
Since $u(0)=0$, the boundary term vanishes, giving $u_{zz}\mapsto -\zeta^{2}\wid
ehat{u}$.

Hence the PDE collapses to a single ODE in $t$:
$$\frac{\partial\widehat{u}}{\partial t}=-k(\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}+\zeta^{2})\,\wideha
t{u}.$$

**Solving the ODE.** With initial value $\widehat{u}(\xi,\eta,\zeta,0)=\widehat{
f}(\xi,\eta,\zeta)$:
$$\boxed{\;\widehat{u}(\xi,\eta,\zeta,t)=\widehat{f}(\xi,\eta,\zeta)\,e^{-k(\xi^
{2}+\eta^{2}+\zeta^{2})t}\;}$$

**Inverting.** Therefore
$$\boxed{\;u(x,y,z,t)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}\frac{2}{\pi}\iiint \widehat{f}(\xi,\eta
,\zeta)\,e^{-k(\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}+\zeta^{2})t}\,e^{i(\xi x+\eta y)}\sin(\zeta z)\,
d\zeta\,d\eta\,d\xi\;}$$
where
$$\widehat{f}(\xi,\eta,\zeta)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty} f(x',y',z')\,e^{-i(\xi x'+\eta y')}\sin(\zeta z')\,dz'\,dy
'\,dx'.$$
This is the solution to part (a), obtained purely from the transform definitions
 and the ODE — no convolution theorem invoked.

## (b) Simplification via convolution (method of images)

Substitute the expression for $\widehat{f}$ into the inverse and interchange int
egration:
$$u=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}\frac{2}{\pi}\iiint_{\Omega} f(x',y',z')\Bigl[\iiint e^{i\
xi(x-x')+i\eta(y-y')}\,e^{-k(\xi^{2}+\eta^{2})t}\,d\xi\,d\eta\;\cdot\;\int_{0}^{
\infty}\sin(\zeta z')\sin(\zeta z)\,e^{-k\zeta^{2}t}\,d\zeta\Bigr]dz'\,dy'\,dx',
$$
with $\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,\infty)$. The two factors separate.

**The $(x,y)$ factor — a 2D Gaussian convolution.** Completing the square (or re
cognizing the Fourier transform of a Gaussian):
$$\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}} e^{i\xi(x-x')+i\eta(y-y')}\,e^{-k(\xi
^{2}+\eta^{2})t}\,d\xi\,d\eta=\frac{1}{4\pi kt}\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{(x-x')^{2}+(y-
y')^{2}}{4kt}\Bigr).$$

**The $z$ factor — sine-transform kernel.** Using $\sin A\sin B=\tfrac12[\cos(A-
B)-\cos(A+B)]$ and the standard integral $\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos(\zeta a)\,e^{-k\
zeta^{2}t}\,d\zeta=\tfrac12\sqrt{\pi/(kt)}\,e^{-a^{2}/(4kt)}$:
$$\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sin(\zeta z')\sin(\zeta z)\,e^{-k\zeta^{2}t}\,d
\zeta=\frac{1}{\pi}\cdot\frac12\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{kt}}\Bigl[e^{-(z-z')^{2}/(4kt)}-
e^{-(z+z')^{2}/(4kt)}\Bigr]=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi kt}}\Bigl[e^{-(z-z')^{2}/(4kt)}-
e^{-(z+z')^{2}/(4kt)}\Bigr].$$

**Combine.** The product of the prefactors is
$$\frac{1}{4\pi kt}\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi kt}}=\frac{1}{8(\pi kt)^{3/2}}=\frac
{1}{(4\pi kt)^{3/2}}.$$
Thus the solution collapses to a single convolution over the half-space:

$$\boxed{\;u(x,y,z,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\int
_{0}^{\infty} G_{D}(x-x',\,y-y',\,z,z',t)\,f(x',y',z')\,dz'\,dy'\,dx'\;}$$

with the **Dirichlet heat kernel** (obtained by the method of images — an "image
 source" of strength $-1$ at the reflected point $z'=-z'$):

$$\boxed{\;G_{D}(x,y,z,z',t)=\frac{1}{(4\pi kt)^{3/2}}\Biggl[\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{
x^{2}+y^{2}+(z-z')^{2}}{4kt}\Bigr)-\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}+(z+z')^{2}}{4k
t}\Bigr)\Biggr].\;}$$

Equivalently, recognizing the first exponential as the free-space 3D heat kernel
 $\Phi_{3}(x,y,z-z',t)$ and the second as $\Phi_{3}(x,y,z+z',t)$ (the image):
$$G_{D}=\Phi_{3}(x,y,z-z',t)-\Phi_{3}(x,y,z+z',t),\qquad \Phi_{3}(X,t)=\frac{1}{
(4\pi kt)^{3/2}}e^{-|X|^{2}/(4kt)}.$$

**Verification.**
- *Boundary condition:* at $z=0$ both exponentials coincide (since $(0-z')^{2}=(
0+z')^{2}=z'^{2}$), so $G_{D}=0$ and $u(x,y,0,t)=0$. ✓
- *Initial condition:* as $t\to0^{+}$, $\Phi_{3}(\cdot,t)\to\delta$, so the firs
t term picks $f(x',y',z')$ at $(x',y',z')=(x,y,z)$ (with $z>0$), while the image
 term concentrates at $z'=-z<0$, outside the integration domain $\{z'>0\}$ and c
ontributes nothing. Hence $u\to f$. ✓
- *PDE:* $G_{D}$ is a difference of two free-space heat kernels (both solving th
e heat equation in $(x,y,z,t)$ for $z\neq z'$), so it satisfies $u_t=k\Delta u$
away from the source; the convolution with $f$ inherits this. ✓

This is the simplified convolution form: the solution is the initial data convol
ved against the Dirichlet heat kernel on the half-space, which is precisely the
free-space kernel minus its reflection across the boundary $z=0$.
