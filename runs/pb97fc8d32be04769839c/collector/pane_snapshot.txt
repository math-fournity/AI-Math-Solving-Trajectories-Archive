## Solution

We solve the heat equation in a disk of radius $a$ with homogeneous Neumann boun
dary condition via separation of variables in polar coordinates.

### Step 1: Separation of variables

Seek solutions of the form $u(r,\theta,t) = R(r)\Theta(\theta)T(t)$. Substitutin
g into

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = k\left[\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\
!\left(r\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\right) + \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial^2 u}{
\partial \theta^2}\right],$$

and dividing by $kRT\Theta$:

$$\frac{T'}{kT} = \frac{1}{R}\left(R'' + \frac{1}{r}R'\right) + \frac{1}{r^2}\fr
ac{\Theta''}{\Theta} = -\lambda^2,$$

where we set the separation constant to $-\lambda^2 \le 0$ (negative values woul
d make $T$ grow exponentially, which is unphysical for the heat equation on a bo
unded domain; the case $\lambda=0$ is treated separately).

This gives three ODEs:

- **Time:** $T' + k\lambda^2 T = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; T(t) = e^{-k\lambda^2 t}$
.
- **Angular:** $\Theta'' + n^2\Theta = 0$, with periodicity $\Theta(\theta+2\pi)
=\Theta(\theta)$ forcing $n = 0,1,2,\dots$. Solutions: $\Theta(\theta) = A\cos(n
\theta) + B\sin(n\theta)$ (for $n=0$: constant).
- **Radial:** $r^2 R'' + rR' + (\lambda^2 r^2 - n^2)R = 0$, i.e. Bessel's equati
on of order $n$.

### Step 2: Radial eigenfunctions and the Neumann condition

The radial equation has general solution $R(r) = C\,J_n(\lambda r) + D\,Y_n(\lam
bda r)$. Since $u$ must be bounded at $r=0$ and $Y_n$ is singular there, we set
$D=0$, giving $R(r) = J_n(\lambda r)$.

The Neumann boundary condition $\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}(a,\theta,t)=0$ req
uires

$$R'(a) = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; J_n'(\lambda a) = 0.$$

Denote the positive zeros of $J_n'$ by $\alpha_{n,m}$ ($m=1,2,3,\dots$), so $\la
mbda_{n,m} = \alpha_{n,m}/a$.

**Special case $n=0$, $\lambda=0$:** $J_0'(0)=0$ is trivially satisfied, and the
 radial equation with $\lambda=0$ gives $R(r)=\text{const}$, $\Theta=\text{const
}$, $T=\text{const}$. This is the steady (time-independent) mode, which we label
 $m=0$.

### Step 3: Eigenfunction expansion

The complete set of eigenfunctions is:

$$\bigl\{\,J_0(\lambda_{0,m}\,r)\;:\; m=1,2,\dots\,\bigr\} \;\cup\; \bigl\{\,J_n
(\lambda_{n,m}\,r)\cos(n\theta),\; J_n(\lambda_{n,m}\,r)\sin(n\theta)\;:\; n\ge1
,\; m=1,2,\dots\,\bigr\} \;\cup\;\{1\}.$$

The general solution is:

$$\boxed{u(r,\theta,t) = A_{0,0} + \sum_{m=1}^{\infty}A_{0,m}\,J_0\!\left(\frac{
\alpha_{0,m}\,r}{a}\right)e^{-k\alpha_{0,m}^2 t/a^2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{
m=1}^{\infty}\left[A_{n,m}\cos(n\theta)+B_{n,m}\sin(n\theta)\right]J_n\!\left(\f
rac{\alpha_{n,m}\,r}{a}\right)e^{-k\alpha_{n,m}^2 t/a^2}}$$

where $\alpha_{n,m}$ is the $m$-th positive zero of $J_n'(x)$.

### Step 4: Determining the coefficients from the initial condition

Setting $t=0$:

$$f(r,\theta) = A_{0,0} + \sum_{m=1}^{\infty}A_{0,m}\,J_0\!\left(\frac{\alpha_{0
,m}r}{a}\right) + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left[A_{n,m}\cos(n\thet
a)+B_{n,m}\sin(n\theta)\right]J_n\!\left(\frac{\alpha_{n,m}r}{a}\right).$$

**Orthogonality relations.** The Bessel functions $J_n(\alpha_{n,m}r/a)$ are ort
hogonal on $[0,a]$ with weight $r$:

$$\int_0^a J_n\!\left(\frac{\alpha_{n,m}r}{a}\right)J_n\!\left(\frac{\alpha_{n,m
'}r}{a}\right) r\,dr = \frac{a^2}{2}\bigl[1 - (\alpha_{n,m}/\alpha_{n,m})^2\bigr
]\bigl[J_n(\alpha_{n,m})\bigr]^2 \cdot \delta_{mm'}.$$

More precisely, using the standard identity and $J_n'(\alpha_{n,m})=0$:

$$\int_0^a \left[J_n\!\left(\frac{\alpha_{n,m}r}{a}\right)\right]^2 r\,dr = \fra
c{a^2}{2}\left[1 - \frac{n^2}{\alpha_{n,m}^2}\right]\left[J_n(\alpha_{n,m})\righ
t]^2.$$

(The factor $1-n^2/\alpha_{n,m}^2$ is positive since $\alpha_{n,m} > n$ for all
$m\ge1$; this is the Neumann normalization, differing from the Dirichlet case wh
ere one gets $[J_{n+1}(\alpha_{n,m})]^2/2$.)

The trigonometric functions are orthogonal on $[0,2\pi]$ in the usual way. Combi
ning both orthogonalities:

**Steady mode ($n=0$, $m=0$):**

$$A_{0,0} = \frac{1}{\pi a^2}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^a f(r,\theta)\,r\,dr\,d\the
ta.$$

(This is the average of $f$ over the disk — the only mode that survives as $t\to
\infty$, consistent with the Neumann condition conserving total heat.)

**Axisymmetric modes ($n=0$, $m\ge1$):**

$$A_{0,m} = \frac{2}{\pi a^2\left[1 - \frac{0}{\alpha_{0,m}^2}\right]J_0(\alpha_
{0,m})^2}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^a f(r,\theta)\,J_0\!\left(\frac{\alpha_{0,m}r}{
a}\right) r\,dr\,d\theta = \frac{2}{\pi a^2 J_0(\alpha_{0,m})^2}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\
!\int_0^a f(r,\theta)\,J_0\!\left(\frac{\alpha_{0,m}r}{a}\right) r\,dr\,d\theta.
$$

**General modes ($n\ge1$, $m\ge1$):**

$$A_{n,m} = \frac{2}{\pi a^2\left[1 - \frac{n^2}{\alpha_{n,m}^2}\right]J_n(\alph
a_{n,m})^2}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^a f(r,\theta)\,J_n\!\left(\frac{\alpha_{n,m}r
}{a}\right)\cos(n\theta)\, r\,dr\,d\theta,$$

$$B_{n,m} = \frac{2}{\pi a^2\left[1 - \frac{n^2}{\alpha_{n,m}^2}\right]J_n(\alph
a_{n,m})^2}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^a f(r,\theta)\,J_n\!\left(\frac{\alpha_{n,m}r
}{a}\right)\sin(n\theta)\, r\,dr\,d\theta.$$

### Step 5: Verification

- **PDE:** Each term $J_n(\lambda_{n,m}r)\cos(n\theta)\,e^{-k\lambda_{n,m}^2 t}$
 satisfies the heat equation by construction (Bessel's equation + angular equati
on + time equation all derived from the PDE). ✓
- **Boundary condition:** $J_n'(\alpha_{n,m})=0 \Rightarrow \partial_r[J_n(\alph
a_{n,m}r/a)]|_{r=a} = (\alpha_{n,m}/a)J_n'(\alpha_{n,m})=0$. ✓
- **Regularity at origin:** $J_n$ is bounded at $r=0$; $Y_n$ excluded. ✓
- **Periodicity:** $\cos(n\theta),\sin(n\theta)$ are $2\pi$-periodic. ✓
- **Initial condition:** The eigenfunctions $\{J_n(\alpha_{n,m}r/a)\cos(n\theta)
,\,J_n(\alpha_{n,m}r/a)\sin(n\theta)\}$ together with the constant form a comple
te orthogonal system on the disk (with weight $r$), so the expansion matches $f$
 in the $L^2$ sense (and pointwise under standard regularity assumptions on $f$)
. ✓
- **Long-time behavior:** All terms with $m\ge1$ decay as $t\to\infty$; only $A_
{0,0}$ (the spatial average of $f$) remains — physically, the Neumann (insulated
) boundary traps all heat, so the temperature equilibrates to the mean of the in
itial distribution. ✓

### Summary

The solution is the eigenfunction expansion above, with eigenvalues $\lambda_{n,
m}=\alpha_{n,m}/a$ determined by the Neumann condition $J_n'(\alpha_{n,m})=0$, a
nd coefficients given by the weighted $L^2$-projections of $f$ onto the orthogon
al eigenbasis $\{J_n(\alpha_{n,m}r/a)\cos(n\theta),\,J_n(\alpha_{n,m}r/a)\sin(n\
theta)\}$ on the disk with weight $r$.
