## Proof

We prove the **Krasnoselskii local bifurcation theorem** in this setting: under
the stated hypotheses, **at least one** non-trivial branch (continuum of non-tri
vial solutions) emanates from $(\mu_0,0)$.

### 1. Setup and Fredholm structure

Since $L\in\mathcal L(X,Y)$ is Fredholm of index zero and $\mu_0\neq 0$ is an is
olated real eigenvalue of $L$ of finite (odd) multiplicity $m$, the operator
$$T_0 := L - \mu_0 I \in \mathcal L(X,Y)$$
is Fredholm of index zero with
$$\dim\ker T_0 = \dim\operatorname{coker} T_0 = m < \infty, \qquad m \text{ odd}
.$$
(That $T_0$ is Fredholm of index $0$ follows from $\mu_0$ being an isolated eige
nvalue of finite algebraic multiplicity of the Fredholm operator $L$; the index
is preserved.) The linearization of $F$ at $(\mu_0,0)$ in the $x$-variable is ex
actly $T_0$.

Decompose
$$X = V \oplus W, \qquad Y = V' \oplus W',$$
where $V := \ker T_0$ ($\dim V = m$), $W$ is a closed complement in $X$, $V' :=
\operatorname{coker} T_0 \cong V$ (using index $0$), and $W' := \operatorname{ra
n} T_0$. Then
$$T_0|_W : W \to W'$$
is a Banach-space isomorphism. Let $P:X\to V$ and $Q:Y\to V'$ be the correspondi
ng projections, and identify $V'\cong V$.

### 2. Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction

Write $x = v + w$ with $v\in V$, $w\in W$. The equation $F(\mu,v+w)=0$ is equiva
lent to the pair
$$
\begin{cases}
(I-Q)\,F(\mu, v+w) = 0 &\quad(\text{range equation, in } W'),\\[2pt]
Q\,F(\mu, v+w) = 0 &\quad(\text{bifurcation equation, in } V').
\end{cases}
$$

Because $(I-Q)T_0|_W = T_0|_W:W\to W'$ is invertible and $f(\mu,x)=o(\|x\|)$, th
e implicit function theorem gives a unique $C^1$ map $w = w(\mu,v)$, defined nea
r $(\mu_0,0)$, with $w(\mu_0,0)=0$, $D_v w(\mu_0,0)=0$, solving the range equati
on. Substituting yields the **finite-dimensional bifurcation equation**
$$\Phi(\mu, v) := Q\,F(\mu,\, v + w(\mu,v)) = 0, \qquad \Phi:\mathbb R\times V\t
o V.$$

Key properties of $\Phi$:
- $\Phi(\mu,0)=0$ for all $\mu$ near $\mu_0$ (the trivial branch).
- $\Phi(\mu_0, v) = o(\|v\|)$, since $Q T_0 v = 0$ for $v\in V$ and $f=\,o(\|x\|
)$.
- $D_v\Phi(\mu,0) = Q(L-\mu I)|_V + (\text{terms vanishing at }v=0) = (\mu_0-\mu
)\,I_V$, because $Q(L-\mu_0 I)|_V = 0$ and $Q(-\mu I)|_V = -\mu\,I_V$ while $Q(L
)|_V = \mu_0\,I_V$ (as $Lv=\mu_0 v$ for $v\in V$). Hence
$$\det D_v\Phi(\mu,0) = (\mu_0 - \mu)^m.$$

### 3. The odd-multiplicity degree argument

Fix a small ball $B\subset V$ around $0$. For $\mu\neq\mu_0$ sufficiently close
to $\mu_0$, the eigenvalue $\mu_0$ is isolated, so $L-\mu I$ is invertible on th
e full space near $0$; consequently the **only** solution of $\Phi(\mu,v)=0$ in
$\overline B$ that is small is $v=0$ — *provided no non-trivial branch exists*.
Under that supposition, the Brouwer degree
$$d(\mu) := \deg(\Phi(\mu,\cdot),\, B,\, 0) = \operatorname{sign}\det D_v\Phi(\m
u,0) = \operatorname{sign}\big((\mu_0-\mu)^m\big).$$

Because $m$ is **odd**,
$$d(\mu) = \operatorname{sign}(\mu_0-\mu) = \begin{cases} +1, & \mu < \mu_0,\\ -
1, & \mu > \mu_0. \end{cases}$$

Thus $d(\mu)$ **changes sign** as $\mu$ crosses $\mu_0$. But the Brouwer degree
is homotopy-invariant on the set $\overline B\times\{\mu : \Phi(\mu,v)\neq 0 \te
xt{ on } \partial B\}$; if the only zero in $\overline B$ for all $\mu$ near $\m
u_0$ were the trivial one $v=0$, the degree would be **constant** for $\mu\neq\m
u_0$ near $\mu_0$. The sign change is a contradiction.

Therefore, for $\mu$ arbitrarily close to $\mu_0$ (on at least one side), there
exist solutions $v\neq 0$ of $\Phi(\mu,v)=0$ with $\|v\|$ arbitrarily small. Lif
ting back via $x = v + w(\mu,v)$ gives non-trivial solutions $(\mu,x)$, $x\neq 0
$, of $F(\mu,x)=0$ accumulating at $(\mu_0,0)$.

### 4. Existence of a branch

The set $\mathcal S$ of non-trivial solutions of $F(\mu,x)=0$ in a small neighbo
rhood of $(\mu_0,0)$ is closed (relative to the neighborhood) and, by the implic
it function theorem applied away from the kernel direction, locally a one-dimens
ional $C^1$ curve wherever $D_{(\mu,x)}F$ has rank $\dim X - 1$. The accumulatio
n at $(\mu_0,0)$ established in Step 3, together with the local structure theore
m for the zero set of a Fredholm map, yields a **continuum** (connected componen
t) of non-trivial solutions whose closure contains $(\mu_0,0)$. This is a non-tr
ivial branch emanating from $(\mu_0,0)$.

### 5. Conclusion

The oddness of the multiplicity $m$ is precisely the condition that forces the d
egree to change sign across $\mu_0$, which rules out the possibility that $(\mu_
0,0)$ is an isolated trivial solution. Hence bifurcation is guaranteed:

> **At least one non-trivial branch emanates from $(\mu_0,0)$.**

(The theorem guarantees existence, not uniqueness: in the presence of additional
 nonlinear structure there may be more than one branch, but the hypotheses given
 — Fredholm index zero, $\mu_0\neq 0$ a real eigenvalue of **odd** multiplicity,
 $f(\mu,x)=o(\|x\|)$ — ensure that the number of non-trivial branches is **at le
ast one**.)
