Looking at this problem, I need to find the number of reachable configurations o
f coins on an m×n grid under the given diagonal-slide move.

## Proof

**Setup.** Label the grid squares $(i,j)$ with $1 \le i \le m$, $1 \le j \le n$.
 Initially, $(m-1)(n-1)$ coins occupy all squares $(i,j)$ with $1 \le i \le m-1$
, $1 \le j \le n-1$. The empty squares are exactly the last row $\{(m,j): 1 \le
j \le n\}$ and last column $\{(i,n): 1 \le i \le m-1\}$, giving $m+n-1$ empty sq
uares forming an L-shape.

**Key observation: the empty squares form a monotone lattice path.** Consider a
path from $(1,n)$ to $(m,1)$ using only **down** steps (increase $i$ by 1) and *
*left** steps (decrease $j$ by 1). Such a path visits exactly $m+n-1$ squares. T
he initial empty set is the path that first goes down $m-1$ times from $(1,n)$ t
o $(m,n)$, then left $n-1$ times from $(m,n)$ to $(m,1)$ — i.e., the word $D^{m-
1}L^{n-1}$.

**Claim (Invariant).** *The empty squares always form a monotone lattice path fr
om $(1,n)$ to $(m,1)$.*

**Claim (Move = corner flip).** *A legal move corresponds exactly to flipping a
$DL$-corner of the path into an $LD$-corner.*

*Proof.* A legal move slides a coin from $(i,j)$ to $(i+1,j+1)$, requiring $(i+1
,j)$, $(i,j+1)$, $(i+1,j+1)$ all empty. In the path, the three squares $(i,j+1)
\to (i+1,j+1) \to (i+1,j)$ form a **down-then-left** elbow (a $DL$ corner) at $(
i+1,j+1)$. The move removes $(i+1,j+1)$ from the empty set and adds $(i,j)$, rep
lacing the elbow $(i,j+1)\to(i+1,j+1)\to(i+1,j)$ with $(i,j+1)\to(i,j)\to(i+1,j)
$ — a **left-then-down** ($LD$) corner. This preserves the monotone-path propert
y. Conversely, every $DL$ corner of the path corresponds to a legal move. $\squa
re$

**Claim (All paths are reachable).** *Starting from $D^{m-1}L^{n-1}$, repeated $
DL \to LD$ flips generate all monotone paths from $(1,n)$ to $(m,1)$.*

*Proof.* The flip $DL \to LD$ moves an $L$ one position to the left (past an adj
acent $D$). In the initial word $D^{m-1}L^{n-1}$, the $k$-th $L$ (from the left)
 sits at position $m-1+k$. In any target word $W$ with $m-1$ $D$'s and $n-1$ $L$
's, the $k$-th $L$ sits at some position $p_k \le m-1+k$ (since at most $m-1$ $D
$'s can precede it). We move the first $L$ left to $p_1$, then the second $L$ le
ft to $p_2$, and so on — each step is a valid $DL \to LD$ flip. This reaches $W$
. $\square$

**Bijection.** Different monotone paths give different empty sets, hence differe
nt (complementary) occupied configurations. The number of coins is always $mn -
(m+n-1) = (m-1)(n-1)$. $\checkmark$

**Counting.** The number of monotone lattice paths from $(1,n)$ to $(m,1)$ using
 $m-1$ down steps and $n-1$ left steps is

$$\binom{(m-1)+(n-1)}{m-1} = \binom{m+n-2}{m-1}.$$

**Verification on small cases:**
- $m=n=2$: $\binom{2}{1}=2$. Configurations $\{(1,1)\}$ and $\{(2,2)\}$. $\check
mark$
- $m=2, n=3$: $\binom{3}{1}=3$. Configurations $\{(1,1),(1,2)\}$, $\{(1,1),(2,3)
\}$, $\{(2,2),(2,3)\}$. $\checkmark$
- $m=2, n=4$: $\binom{4}{1}=4$. Traced through 4 configurations. $\checkmark$

## Answer

The number of distinct reachable configurations is

$$\boxed{\dbinom{m+n-2}{m-1}}.$$
