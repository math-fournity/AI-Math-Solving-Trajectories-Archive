$$\prod_{i=1}^k x_i \cdot \sum_{i=1}^k x_i^{n-1} \le \sum_{i=1}^k x_i^{n+k-1}$$
is equivalent (dividing the LHS product across the sum) to
$$\sum_{i=1}^k x_i^{n-1}\!\prod_{j=1}^k x_j \;\le\; \sum_{i=1}^k x_i^{n+k-1},$$
which under $x_i = e^{a_i}$ becomes
$$\sum_{i=1}^k e^{b_i} \;\le\; \sum_{i=1}^k e^{c_i}, \qquad b_i := (n-1)a_i + S,
\quad c_i := (n+k-1)a_i.$$

**Step 1 — Equal total mass.**
$$\sum_{i=1}^k b_i = (n-1)S + kS = (n+k-1)S = \sum_{i=1}^k c_i.$$

**Step 2 — Majorization $\mathbf{c} \succ \mathbf{b}$.** Relabel so $a_1 \ge a_2

both are already sorted decreasingly.

For $m \in \{1,\dots,k-1\}$,
$$\sum_{i=1}^m c_i - \sum_{i=1}^m b_i = (n+k-1)\!\sum_{i=1}^m a_i - (n-1)\!\sum_

j.$$
Hence
$$(k-m)\sum_{i=1}^m a_i \;\ge\; m(k-m)\,a_m \;\ge\; m\sum_{j=m+1}^k a_j,$$
