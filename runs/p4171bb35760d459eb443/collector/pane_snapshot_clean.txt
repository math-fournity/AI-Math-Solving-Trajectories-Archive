## Proof

Set coordinates with $A=(0,0)$ and $B=(1,0)$, so $AB=1$. Since $\frac{AP}{AB}=\f
rac{1}{42}$, we have $AP=\frac{1}{42}$ and $PB=\frac{41}{42}$, giving $P=\left(\
frac{1}{42},0\right)$.

**Circle with diameter $AB$:** center $\left(\frac12,0\right)$, radius $\frac12$
, equation $\left(x-\frac12\right)^2+y^2=\frac14$.

**Finding $S$ and $T$:** The line perpendicular to $AB$ through $P$ is $x=\frac{
1}{42}$. Substituting:
$$\left(\frac{1}{42}-\frac12\right)^2+y^2=\frac14 \;\Longrightarrow\; \left(-\fr
ac{20}{42}\right)^2+y^2=\frac14 \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{100}{441}+y^2=\frac14$
$
$$y^2=\frac14-\frac{100}{441}=\frac{441-400}{1764}=\frac{41}{1764} \;\Longrighta
rrow\; y=\pm\frac{\sqrt{41}}{42}$$

So $S=\left(\frac{1}{42},\frac{\sqrt{41}}{42}\right)$ and $T=\left(\frac{1}{42},
-\frac{\sqrt{41}}{42}\right)$.

**Circle $s$ (diameter $SP$):** $SP=\frac{\sqrt{41}}{42}$, so radius $=\frac{\sq
rt{41}}{84}$.
$$A(s)=\pi\cdot\frac{41}{84^2}=\pi\cdot\frac{41}{7056}$$

**Circle $t$ (diameter $ST$):** $ST=\frac{2\sqrt{41}}{42}=\frac{\sqrt{41}}{21}$,
 so radius $=\frac{\sqrt{41}}{42}$.
$$A(t)=\pi\cdot\frac{41}{42^2}=\pi\cdot\frac{41}{1764}=\pi\cdot\frac{164}{7056}$
$

**Circle $a$ (diameter $AP$):** $AP=\frac{1}{42}$, so radius $=\frac{1}{84}$.
$$A(a)=\pi\cdot\frac{1}{7056}$$

**Circle $b$ (diameter $PB$):** $PB=\frac{41}{42}$, so radius $=\frac{41}{84}$.
$$A(b)=\pi\cdot\frac{41^2}{84^2}=\pi\cdot\frac{1681}{7056}$$

**Computing the ratio:**
$$A(s)+A(t)=\pi\cdot\frac{41+164}{7056}=\pi\cdot\frac{205}{7056}$$
$$A(a)+A(b)=\pi\cdot\frac{1+1681}{7056}=\pi\cdot\frac{1682}{7056}$$
$$\frac{A(s)+A(t)}{A(a)+A(b)}=\frac{205}{1682}$$

Since $205=5\cdot 41$ and $1682=41^2+1=2\cdot 29^2$, we have $\gcd(205,1682)=1$
(as $1682\bmod 41 = 1$).

$$\boxed{\dfrac{205}{1682}}$$
