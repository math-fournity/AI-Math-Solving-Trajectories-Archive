## Proof

Let $F(x,y) = \begin{bmatrix} P \\ Q \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} x^2+y^2+4y
\\ x^3+xy^2+2xy \end{bmatrix}$, and let $C$ be a positively oriented (counterclo
ckwise) simple closed curve enclosing region $D$.

**Step 1: Compute the scalar curl.**

$$\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} = 3x^2 + y^2 + 2y, \qquad \frac{\partial P}{\par
tial y} = 2y + 4.$$

$$\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = 3x^2 + y^2 + 2
y - 2y - 4 = 3x^2 + y^2 - 4.$$

**Step 2: Apply Green's Theorem.**

$$\int_C F \cdot d\mathbf{x} = \iint_D \left(3x^2 + y^2 - 4\right)\, dA.$$

**Step 3: Minimize the double integral over all regions $D$ bounded by simple cl
osed curves.**

The integrand $g(x,y) = 3x^2 + y^2 - 4$ is:
- **negative** inside the ellipse $\mathcal{E} = \{(x,y) : 3x^2 + y^2 < 4\}$,
- **zero** on the boundary $3x^2 + y^2 = 4$,
- **positive** outside $\mathcal{E}$.

For any region $D$, decompose:
$$\iint_D g\, dA = \iint_{D \cap \mathcal{E}} g\, dA + \iint_{D \setminus \mathc
al{E}} g\, dA.$$

The first term is $\geq \iint_{\mathcal{E}} g\, dA$ (since $g < 0$ on $\mathcal{
E}$, including less of $\mathcal{E}$ makes the term *less negative*, i.e., large
r). The second term is $\geq 0$ (since $g \geq 0$ on $D \setminus \mathcal{E}$,
and equals $0$ only when $D \setminus \mathcal{E} = \emptyset$).

Therefore:
$$\iint_D g\, dA \geq \iint_{\mathcal{E}} g\, dA,$$
with equality if and only if $D = \mathcal{E}$, i.e., $D \setminus \mathcal{E} =
 \emptyset$ and $D \cap \mathcal{E} = \mathcal{E}$.

Hence the minimum is achieved precisely when $D = \mathcal{E}$, so the minimizin
g curve is the **ellipse $3x^2 + y^2 = 4$, traversed counterclockwise**.

**Step 4: Compute the minimum value.**

Substitute $u = \sqrt{3}\,x,\ v = y$, so $3x^2 + y^2 = u^2 + v^2$ and $dA = \fra
c{1}{\sqrt{3}}\,du\,dv$. The region becomes the disk $u^2 + v^2 \leq 4$ (radius
$2$).

$$\iint_{\mathcal{E}} (3x^2+y^2-4)\, dA = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\i
nt_0^2 (r^2 - 4)\,r\,dr\,d\theta = \frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{3}} \left[\frac{r^4}{4} - 2
r^2\right]_0^2 = \frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{3}}(4 - 8) = -\frac{8\pi}{\sqrt{3}}.$$

**Conclusion.** The simple closed curve that minimizes $\int_C F \cdot d\mathbf{
x}$ is the **ellipse $3x^2 + y^2 = 4$ oriented counterclockwise**, and the minim
um value of the integral is $\boxed{-\dfrac{8\pi}{\sqrt{3}}}$.
