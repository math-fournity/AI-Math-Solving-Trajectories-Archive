## Solution

**Claim:** Yes. The probability is $0$.

Let $A$ be $m \times n$ and $B$ be $n \times p$, with all entries i.i.d. Gaussia
n. We prove $\Pr\!\bigl(\operatorname{rank}(AB) < \min(\operatorname{rank}(A),\o
peratorname{rank}(B))\bigr) = 0$.

---

**Step 1 — Both matrices are full rank almost surely.**

A matrix with i.i.d. Gaussian entries has full rank with probability $1$: the de
terminant of every maximal square submatrix is a nonzero polynomial in the entri
es, hence vanishes only on a measure-zero algebraic set. Therefore, almost surel
y,
$$
\operatorname{rank}(A) = \min(m,n), \qquad \operatorname{rank}(B) = \min(n,p).
$$
Consequently
$$
\min\bigl(\operatorname{rank}(A),\operatorname{rank}(B)\bigr) = \min\bigl(\min(m
,n),\,\min(n,p)\bigr) = \min(m,n,p) \quad \text{a.s.}
$$

---

**Step 2 — A dimension formula for the product.**

View $B:\mathbb{R}^p \to \mathbb{R}^n$ and $A:\mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$. Th
en $\operatorname{im}(B) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ and $\ker(A) \subseteq \mathbb{
R}^n$, and the standard identity gives
$$
\operatorname{rank}(AB) = \operatorname{rank}(B) - \dim\bigl(\ker(A) \cap \opera
torname{im}(B)\bigr).
$$

---

**Step 3 — The two subspaces are in general position almost surely.**

Since $A$ and $B$ have **independent** entries, the random subspace $\ker(A) \su
bseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ (depending only on $A$) and the random subspace $\operatorn
ame{im}(B) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ (depending only on $B$) are independent. Two
independent random subspaces of $\mathbb{R}^n$ (drawn from distributions invaria
nt under the action of $O(n)$, as Gaussian-induced distributions are) meet in ge
neral position almost surely, so
$$
\dim\bigl(\ker(A) \cap \operatorname{im}(B)\bigr) = \max\!\bigl(0,\;\dim\ker(A)
+ \dim\operatorname{im}(B) - n\bigr) \quad \text{a.s.}
$$
Using $\dim\ker(A) = n - \min(m,n) = \max(0,\,n-m)$ and $\dim\operatorname{im}(B
) = \min(n,p)$ a.s.:
$$
\dim\bigl(\ker(A) \cap \operatorname{im}(B)\bigr) = \max\!\bigl(0,\;\max(0,n-m)
+ \min(n,p) - n\bigr) \quad \text{a.s.}
$$

---

**Step 4 — Compute $\operatorname{rank}(AB)$ almost surely.**

$$
\operatorname{rank}(AB) = \min(n,p) - \max\!\bigl(0,\;\max(0,n-m) + \min(n,p) -
n\bigr) \quad \text{a.s.}
$$

We verify this equals $\min(m,n,p)$ in every case:

- **$n \le m$:** $\max(0,n-m)=0$, so $\operatorname{rank}(AB)=\min(n,p)-\max(0,\
min(n,p)-n)=\min(n,p)=\min(m,n,p)$. ✓
- **$n>m$, $p\le m$:** $\min(n,p)=p\le m$, so $\max(0,(n-m)+p-n)=\max(0,p-m)=0$,
 giving $\operatorname{rank}(AB)=p=\min(m,n,p)$. ✓
- **$n>m$, $p>m$:** $\min(n,p)>m$, so $\max(0,(n-m)+\min(n,p)-n)=\min(n,p)-m>0$,
 giving $\operatorname{rank}(AB)=\min(n,p)-(\min(n,p)-m)=m=\min(m,n,p)$. ✓

Hence
$$
\operatorname{rank}(AB) = \min(m,n,p) \quad \text{a.s.}
$$

---

**Step 5 — Conclusion.**

Combining Steps 1 and 4,
$$
\operatorname{rank}(AB) = \min(m,n,p) = \min\bigl(\operatorname{rank}(A),\operat
orname{rank}(B)\bigr) \quad \text{a.s.}
$$
Therefore the strict inequality $\operatorname{rank}(AB) < \min(\operatorname{ra
nk}(A),\operatorname{rank}(B))$ holds only on a null set:
$$
\boxed{\;\Pr\!\bigl(\operatorname{rank}(AB) < \min(\operatorname{rank}(A),\opera
torname{rank}(B))\bigr) = 0.\;}
$$
