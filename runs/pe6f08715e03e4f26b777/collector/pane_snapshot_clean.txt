## Solution

Let $M = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$ be a matrix over $\mathbb
{F}_{37}$.

**Reformulation.** Computing $M^2$:
$$M^2 = \begin{pmatrix} a^2+bc & b(a+d) \\ c(a+d) & bc+d^2 \end{pmatrix}.$$
The first four congruences state exactly that $M^2 \equiv M \pmod{37}$, i.e. $M$
 is **idempotent**. The fifth congruence states $\det(M) = ad - bc \equiv 1 \pmo
d{37}$, so $M$ is **invertible** over $\mathbb{F}_{37}$.

**Key step.** If $M$ is idempotent ($M^2 = M$) and invertible, then multiplying
both sides of $M^2 = M$ on the left (or right) by $M^{-1}$ gives
$$M = I.$$
Indeed, $M^2 = M \implies M^{-1}M^2 = M^{-1}M \implies M = I$.

**Verification.** For $M = I$, i.e. $(a,b,c,d) = (1,0,0,1)$:
- $a^2+bc = 1+0 = 1 \equiv a$ ✓
- $b(a+d) = 0 \equiv b$ ✓
- $c(a+d) = 0 \equiv c$ ✓
- $bc+d^2 = 0+1 = 1 \equiv d$ ✓
- $ad-bc = 1-0 = 1 \equiv 1$ ✓

All five congruences are satisfied.

**Uniqueness.** Since any solution must be an invertible idempotent, and the onl
y invertible idempotent is the identity, the solution $(a,b,c,d) = (1,0,0,1)$ is
 unique.

The total number of solutions is $\boxed{1}$.
