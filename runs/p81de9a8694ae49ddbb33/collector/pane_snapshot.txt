## Solution

**No**, it does not follow that $X_1 = X_2$. The map

$$\Phi(X) = PXP + (1-P)X(1-P)$$

is the *pinching* (conditional expectation) onto the block-diagonal subalgebra a
ssociated with the decomposition $H = PH \oplus (1-P)H$. It is not injective: it
 discards the off-diagonal blocks $PX(1-P)$ and $(1-P)XP$. A concrete counterexa
mple suffices.

---

### Counterexample

Let $H = \mathbb{C}^2$ and let $P$ be the projection onto the first coordinate:

$$P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad 1-P = \begin{pmatrix}
 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

For an arbitrary operator $X = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \in
B(\mathbb{C}^2)$, a direct computation gives

$$PXP = \begin{pmatrix} a & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad (1-P)X(1-P) = \begi
n{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & d \end{pmatrix},$$

so

$$\Phi(X) = PXP + (1-P)X(1-P) = \begin{pmatrix} a & 0 \\ 0 & d \end{pmatrix}.$$

Thus $\Phi$ retains only the diagonal entries $(a,d)$ and discards the off-diago
nal entries $(b,c)$.

Now set

$$X_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad X_2 = \begin{pmatri
x} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = I.$$

Then

$$\Phi(X_1) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = I, \qquad \Phi(X_2)
 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = I.$$

Hence

$$PX_1P + (1-P)X_1(1-P) = PX_2P + (1-P)X_2(1-P),$$

yet $X_1 \neq X_2$ (they differ in the $(1,2)$-entry).

---

### Explanation

Writing any $X \in B(H)$ in block form with respect to $H = PH \oplus (1-P)H$,

$$X = \begin{pmatrix} PXP & PX(1-P) \\ (1-P)XP & (1-P)X(1-P) \end{pmatrix},$$

the given equality constrains only the two diagonal blocks $PXP$ and $(1-P)X(1-P
)$. The off-diagonal blocks $PX(1-P)$ and $(1-P)XP$ are completely unconstrained
, so two operators can agree under $\Phi$ while differing in those blocks. There
fore the implication $X_1 = X_2$ **fails** in general.
