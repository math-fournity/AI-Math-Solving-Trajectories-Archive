## Proof

Let $d = \deg f$ and $e = \deg g$. We claim

$$i(C, D) = d \cdot e.$$

This is **Bézout's Theorem**. We prove it via the Hilbert polynomial of the sche
me-theoretic intersection.

### Setup

Work in the homogeneous coordinate ring $S = k[x_0, x_1, x_2]$ of $\mathbb{P}^2_
k$. The curves $C = V_+(f)$ and $D = V_+(g)$ are defined by the homogeneous idea
l $I = (f, g) \subset S$.

Since $S$ is a UFD and $f, g$ share no common factor, $g$ is a non-zero-divisor
modulo $(f)$. Hence $(f, g)$ is a **regular sequence** in $S$, and $S/(f,g)$ is
a **complete intersection** of codimension $2$ in $\operatorname{Spec} S$. Proje
ctively, the saturated ideal $\widetilde{I} = (f,g)$ (complete intersections of
the expected codimension in $\mathbb{P}^2$ are saturated) defines the scheme-the
oretic intersection $C \cap D$, a $0$-dimensional subscheme of $\mathbb{P}^2$ (p
roper intersection, since the common zero locus has dimension $2 - 2 = 0$).

### Intersection number as length

The intersection number $i(C, D)$ is the sum of local intersection multiplicitie
s over all points $P \in C \cap D$:

$$i(C, D) = \sum_{P \in C \cap D} \ell\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^2, P}/(f, g)
\bigr) = \operatorname{length}\bigl(C \cap D\bigr),$$

i.e. the length of the $0$-dimensional scheme $C \cap D$. This length equals the
 value of the Hilbert polynomial of $S/(f,g)$ for all sufficiently large degrees
.

### Hilbert series computation

The Hilbert series of $S = k[x_0, x_1, x_2]$ is

$$H_S(t) = \frac{1}{(1-t)^3}.$$

Since $(f, g)$ is a regular sequence of degrees $d, e$, the Hilbert series of th
e complete intersection $S/(f,g)$ is

$$H_{S/(f,g)}(t) = \frac{(1-t^d)(1-t^e)}{(1-t)^3} = \frac{(1 + t + \cdots + t^{d
-1})(1 + t + \cdots + t^{e-1})}{1 - t}.$$

### Extracting the Hilbert polynomial

Write the numerator as $Q(t) = (1 + t + \cdots + t^{d-1})(1 + t + \cdots + t^{e-
1})$, a polynomial of degree $d + e - 2$. Then

$$H_{S/(f,g)}(t) = \frac{Q(t)}{1 - t}.$$

For $n > d + e - 2$, the coefficient of $t^n$ in $Q(t)/(1-t)$ equals $Q(1)$, bec
ause $Q(t)/(1-t) = Q(t)(1 + t + t^2 + \cdots)$ and for $n$ exceeding $\deg Q$, t
he coefficient is the sum of all coefficients of $Q$, namely $Q(1)$.

Evaluating:

$$Q(1) = (1 + 1 + \cdots + 1)(1 + 1 + \cdots + 1) = d \cdot e.$$

Therefore the Hilbert polynomial of $S/(f,g)$ is the **constant** $d \cdot e$.

### Conclusion

The length of the $0$-dimensional scheme $C \cap D$ — which is exactly the inter
section number $i(C, D)$ — equals the value of this Hilbert polynomial:

$$\boxed{i(C, D) = \deg(f)\,\deg(g).}$$

### PROOF COMPLETE
