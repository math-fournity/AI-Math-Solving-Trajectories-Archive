## Proof

**Claim:** $C_0(\mathbb{R})$ is a Banach space (i.e., it is complete under the s
upremum norm).

**Setup.** Recall
$$C_0(\mathbb{R}) = \bigl\{f\in C(\mathbb{R}) : \lim_{|x|\to\infty} f(x)=0\bigr\
}, \qquad \|f\|_\infty = \sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}} |f(x)|.$$

Every $f\in C_0(\mathbb{R})$ is bounded: given $\varepsilon=1$, there is $R$ wit
h $|f(x)|<1$ for $|x|>R$, and $f$ is bounded on the compact set $[-R,R]$, so $\|
f\|_\infty<\infty$. Thus $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ is a genuine norm on $C_0(\mathbb{R}
)$. It suffices to show that $C_0(\mathbb{R})$ is **closed** in the Banach space
 $C_b(\mathbb{R})$ of bounded continuous functions (which is complete under $\|\
cdot\|_\infty$, being a closed subspace of $\ell^\infty(\mathbb{R})$).

**Closedness.** Let $(f_n)\subset C_0(\mathbb{R})$ converge in supremum norm to
some $f\in C_b(\mathbb{R})$; we show $f\in C_0(\mathbb{R})$.

*Continuity:* $f$ is the uniform limit of continuous functions, hence continuous
.

*Vanishing at infinity:* Let $\varepsilon>0$. Choose $N$ with $\|f_N-f\|_\infty<
\varepsilon/2$. Since $f_N\in C_0(\mathbb{R})$, there exists $R>0$ such that $|f
_N(x)|<\varepsilon/2$ whenever $|x|>R$. Then for all $|x|>R$,
$$|f(x)|\le |f(x)-f_N(x)|+|f_N(x)|< \frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=
\varepsilon.$$
Hence $\lim_{|x|\to\infty}f(x)=0$, so $f\in C_0(\mathbb{R})$.

**Conclusion.** $C_0(\mathbb{R})$ is a closed subspace of the Banach space $C_b(
\mathbb{R})$, and a closed subspace of a complete normed space is complete. Ther
efore $C_0(\mathbb{R})$ is itself a Banach space. $\blacksquare$
