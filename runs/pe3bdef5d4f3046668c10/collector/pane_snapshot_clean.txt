### Step 1 — Consequences of "$y=f(x)-1$ is odd"

Let $g(x)=f(x)-1$. Oddness gives $g(-x)=-g(x)$, hence
$$f(-x)=2-f(x),\qquad f(0)=1.$$
Differentiating $f(x)+f(-x)=2$:
- $f'(x)-f'(-x)=0\Rightarrow f'$ is even,
- $f''(x)+f''(-x)=0\Rightarrow f''$ is odd, so $f''(0)=0$,
- $f'''(x)-f'''(-x)=0\Rightarrow f'''$ is even.

In particular $f(0)=1=\cos 0$, so $x=0$ is the boundary point of $f(x)>\cos x$.
