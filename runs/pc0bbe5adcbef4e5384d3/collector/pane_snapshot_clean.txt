## Answer

**No, it is not always true.** The two conditions given do not determine the Jor
dan block structure completely, so two matrices can satisfy both conditions yet
fail to be similar.

### Counterexample

Work over an algebraically closed field (e.g., $\mathbb{C}$). Consider the follo
wing two $6 \times 6$ matrices in Jordan normal form:

$$A = J_5(0) \oplus J_1(0), \qquad B = J_4(0) \oplus J_2(0),$$

where $J_k(0)$ denotes the $k \times k$ nilpotent Jordan block with eigenvalue $
0$.

**Same characteristic polynomial.** Both $A$ and $B$ are nilpotent $6 \times 6$
matrices, so both have characteristic polynomial
$$\chi_A(t) = \chi_B(t) = t^6.$$

**Same number of linearly independent eigenvectors for the (unique) eigenvalue $
0$.** For a single Jordan block $J_k(0)$, the eigenspace $\ker(J_k(0))$ is $1$-d
imensional (spanned by $e_1$). Therefore the geometric multiplicity of the eigen
value $0$ equals the *number of Jordan blocks*:
- $A$ has $2$ blocks $\Rightarrow$ geometric multiplicity $= 2$.
- $B$ has $2$ blocks $\Rightarrow$ geometric multiplicity $= 2$.

So both matrices have exactly $2$ linearly independent eigenvectors for the eige
nvalue $0$.

**Yet $A$ and $B$ are not similar.** The Jordan canonical form is a complete inv
ariant for similarity: two matrices are similar if and only if they have the sam
e Jordan form (up to permutation of blocks). The Jordan forms of $A$ and $B$ dif
fer — one has block sizes $(5,1)$, the other has block sizes $(4,2)$ — so $A \no
t\sim B$.

### Why the conditions are insufficient

The characteristic polynomial fixes, for each eigenvalue $\lambda$, its **algebr
aic multiplicity** $a_\lambda$ (the total size of all Jordan blocks for $\lambda
$). The number of linearly independent eigenvectors for $\lambda$ is the **geome
tric multiplicity** $g_\lambda$, which equals the **number** of Jordan blocks fo
r $\lambda$.

However, similarity is determined by the full **multiset of Jordan block sizes**
 for each eigenvalue. Knowing only $a_\lambda$ (sum of block sizes) and $g_\lamb
da$ (number of blocks) does **not** determine the individual block sizes. Concre
tely, for $a_\lambda = 6$ and $g_\lambda = 2$, the possible partitions are
$$(5,1),\quad (4,2),\quad (3,3),$$
each giving a distinct similarity class. The given conditions cannot distinguish
 these.

### Conclusion

The statement is **false**. The characteristic polynomial together with the geom
etric multiplicity of each eigenvalue does not determine the Jordan block sizes,
 hence does not determine the similarity class.
