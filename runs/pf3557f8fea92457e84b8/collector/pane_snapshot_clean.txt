## Proof

$$I = \int_{0}^{2\pi} e^{-i\theta}\,e^{e^{i\theta}}\,d\theta.$$

**Step 1: Expand the inner exponential.** Since $e^{z}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{
z^{n}}{n!}$ for all $z\in\mathbb{C}$, set $z=e^{i\theta}$ (with $|e^{i\theta}|=1
$):

$$e^{e^{i\theta}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(e^{i\theta})^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\i
nfty}\frac{e^{in\theta}}{n!}.$$

The series converges absolutely and uniformly in $\theta$ (Weierstrass $M$-test
with $M_n=1/n!$), so we may interchange the sum and integral.

**Step 2: Multiply by $e^{-i\theta}$ and integrate termwise.**

$$I=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{i(n-1)\theta}\,d\theta.$$

**Step 3: Evaluate each inner integral.** For any integer $k$,

$$\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{ik\theta}\,d\theta=\begin{cases}2\pi, & k=0,\\[4pt]\dfrac{e^
{ik\theta}}{ik}\Big|_{0}^{2\pi}=0, & k\neq 0.\end{cases}$$

Here $k=n-1$, which equals $0$ exactly when $n=1$.

**Step 4: Extract the surviving term.** Every term with $n\neq 1$ vanishes; only
 $n=1$ contributes:

$$I=\frac{1}{1!}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{i(1-1)\theta}\,d\theta=\frac{1}{1}\cdot 2\pi=2
\pi.$$

**Verification via Cauchy's formula.** Parametrize the unit circle $\gamma$ by $
z=e^{i\theta}$, $dz=ie^{i\theta}d\theta$, so $d\theta=dz/(iz)$. Then

$$I=\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-i\theta}e^{e^{i\theta}}d\theta=\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{1}{z}\,
e^{z}\,\frac{dz}{iz}=\frac{1}{i}\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{e^{z}}{z^{2}}\,dz.$$

By Cauchy's integral formula for derivatives, $\oint \frac{e^{z}}{z^{2}}dz = 2\p
i i\cdot e'(0)=2\pi i$. Hence

$$I=\frac{1}{i}\cdot 2\pi i = 2\pi.$$

Both methods agree.

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-i\theta}e^{e^{i\theta}}\,d\theta=2\pi}$
$
