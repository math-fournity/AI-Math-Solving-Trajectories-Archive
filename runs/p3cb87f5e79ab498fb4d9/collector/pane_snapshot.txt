## Proof

**Setup and definitions.** Over ℚ, the cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_{15,\mathbb{Q
}}(x)$ is the minimal polynomial of a primitive 15th root of unity over ℚ, which
 is the irreducible polynomial

$$\Phi_{15,\mathbb{Q}}(x) = x^8 - x^7 + x^5 - x^4 + x^3 - x + 1,$$

of degree $\phi(15) = 8$.

Over $\mathbb{F}_p$ (with $p \nmid 15$), we define $\Phi_{15,\mathbb{F}_p}(x)$ a
nalogously as the minimal polynomial of a primitive 15th root of unity over $\ma
thbb{F}_p$. This is well-defined as the minimal polynomial of any primitive 15th
 root of unity in $\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$, and has degree equal to $\operatorn
ame{ord}_{15}(p)$, the multiplicative order of $p$ modulo 15.

**Key fact about the group structure.** By the Chinese Remainder Theorem:

$$(\mathbb{Z}/15\mathbb{Z})^* \cong (\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})^* \times (\mathbb{Z
}/5\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}.$$

The exponent of $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ is $\oper
atorname{lcm}(2, 4) = 4$. Therefore, every element of $(\mathbb{Z}/15\mathbb{Z})
^*$ has order dividing 4. In particular, for any prime $p$ with $\gcd(p, 15) = 1
$:

$$\operatorname{ord}_{15}(p) \mid 4, \quad \text{so} \quad \operatorname{ord}_{1
5}(p) \in \{1, 2, 4\}.$$

**Degree comparison.** The reduction $\Phi_{15,\mathbb{Q}}(x) \bmod p$ has degre
e 8 (the leading coefficient is 1, which is nonzero modulo any prime $p$). On th
e other hand:

$$\deg \Phi_{15,\mathbb{F}_p}(x) = \operatorname{ord}_{15}(p) \leq 4 < 8 = \deg\
!\left(\Phi_{15,\mathbb{Q}}(x) \bmod p\right).$$

Since the two polynomials have different degrees, they cannot be equal in $\math
bb{F}_p[x]$.

**Why the degree formula holds.** For $p \nmid n$, the cyclotomic polynomial $\P
hi_n(x) \bmod p$ factors over $\mathbb{F}_p$ into $\phi(n)/\operatorname{ord}_n(
p)$ distinct irreducible factors, each of degree $\operatorname{ord}_n(p)$. Each
 irreducible factor is the minimal polynomial of a primitive $n$-th root of unit
y (with different factors corresponding to different Frobenius orbits on the pri
mitive roots). The minimal polynomial $\Phi_{n,\mathbb{F}_p}(x)$ is one such fac
tor, hence has degree $\operatorname{ord}_n(p)$.

For $\Phi_{15,\mathbb{Q}}(x) \bmod p = \Phi_{15,\mathbb{F}_p}(x)$ to hold, we wo
uld need $\Phi_{15,\mathbb{Q}}(x) \bmod p$ to be irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_p$
 (so that it equals the minimal polynomial), which requires $\operatorname{ord}_
{15}(p) = \phi(15) = 8$. But as shown above, $\operatorname{ord}_{15}(p) \leq 4$
 for all primes $p$ coprime to 15, so this is impossible.

**Conclusion.** Since $(\mathbb{Z}/15\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
\times \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ is not cyclic, no prime $p$ can have order 8 modu
lo 15. The degree of $\Phi_{15,\mathbb{F}_p}(x)$ is always at most 4, while the
degree of $\Phi_{15,\mathbb{Q}}(x) \bmod p$ is always 8. Therefore, the equality
 $\Phi_{15,\mathbb{Q}}(x) \bmod p = \Phi_{15,\mathbb{F}_p}(x)$ holds for **no**
prime $p \neq 3, 5$.

The set of such primes is $\boxed{\emptyset}$.
