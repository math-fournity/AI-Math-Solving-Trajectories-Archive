## Answer

**Yes**, $a \cup b$ can be a torsion element in $H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})$.

### Key Observation: Graded Commutativity Forces 2-Torsion

For any space $X$ and any $a \in H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})$, the cup product is graded
-commutative:
$$a \cup a = (-1)^{1 \cdot 1}\, a \cup a = -\,a \cup a,$$
so $2(a \cup a) = 0$. Thus $a \cup a$ is **always** a 2-torsion element (possibl
y zero). The question reduces to: does there exist a space where $a \cup a \neq
0$?

### Example: The Klein Bottle

Let $X = K$ be the Klein bottle, with its standard CW structure: one 0-cell, two
 1-cells $a, b$, and one 2-cell $e^{2}$ attached via the word $aba^{-1}b$.

**Homology and cohomology.** The cellular boundary maps are $\partial_{1} = 0$ a
nd $\partial_{2}(e^{2}) = a + b - a + b = 2b$. Hence:
- $H_{1}(K) = \mathbb{Z}\langle a \rangle \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2\langle b \rangle$,
- $H_{2}(K) = 0$.

By the Universal Coefficient Theorem:
- $H^{1}(K,\mathbb{Z}) = \operatorname{Hom}(H_{1}(K),\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$,
generated by $\alpha$ with $\alpha(a)=1,\;\alpha(b)=0$,
- $H^{2}(K,\mathbb{Z}) = \operatorname{Ext}(H_{1}(K),\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}/2$
, generated by $\gamma$ (dual to $e^{2}$ modulo $2$).

**Cup product computation.** Triangulate the 2-cell (a square with vertices $v_{
0},v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}$ in counterclockwise order) as $[v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}] + [v_{0
},v_{2},v_{3}]$. The boundary edges map to 1-cells as:
$$[v_{0},v_{1}] = a,\quad [v_{1},v_{2}] = b,\quad [v_{2},v_{3}] = -a,\quad [v_{3
},v_{0}] = b,$$
and the diagonal $[v_{0},v_{2}] = a + b$ as a 1-chain.

Using the Alexander–Whitney diagonal approximation, for 1-cochains $f, g$:
$$(f \cup g)(e^{2}) = f([v_{0},v_{1}])\,g([v_{1},v_{2}]) + f([v_{0},v_{2}])\,g([
v_{2},v_{3}]) = f(a)\,g(b) - \bigl(f(a)+f(b)\bigr)\,g(a).$$

Setting $f = g = \alpha$ (so $\alpha(a)=1,\;\alpha(b)=0$):
$$(\alpha \cup \alpha)(e^{2}) = 1 \cdot 0 - (1+0)\cdot 1 = -1.$$

Thus $\alpha \cup \alpha = -\gamma$ in $C^{2}(K,\mathbb{Z})$, which represents t
he **generator** of $H^{2}(K,\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}/2$ (since $-\gamma \equiv
\gamma \pmod{2\mathbb{Z}}$).

### Conclusion

The generator $\alpha \in H^{1}(K,\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ satisfies:
- $\alpha \cup \alpha \neq 0$ in $H^{2}(K,\mathbb{Z})$,
- $2(\alpha \cup \alpha) = 0$ by graded commutativity,
- so $\alpha \cup \alpha$ is a **nonzero 2-torsion element** of $H^{2}(K,\mathbb
{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$.

Taking $a = b = \alpha$, the cup product $a \cup b$ is torsion. $\blacksquare$
