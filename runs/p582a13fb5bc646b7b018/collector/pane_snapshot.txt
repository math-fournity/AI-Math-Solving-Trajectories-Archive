**Answer: The equality does NOT hold in general.**

## Proof

I provide two counterexamples — one trivial and one with $M_0 = 0$.

### Counterexample 1: Constant martingale

Let $M_t = 1$ for all $t \geq 0$. This is a (valid) martingale.

Then $\int_0^t M_s\, ds = t$. For any fixed $a > 0$ and all $t > a$:
$$\mathbf{1}\!\left\{\int_0^t M_s\,ds > a\right\} = \mathbf{1}\{t > a\} = 1,$$
so
$$\mathbf{E}\!\left(M_t;\, \textstyle\int_0^t M_s\,ds > a\right) = 1 \quad \text
{for all } t > a.$$

Therefore:
$$\lim_{t\to\infty} \mathbf{E}\!\left(M_t;\, \textstyle\int_0^t M_s\,ds > a\righ
t) = 1, \qquad \lim_{a\to\infty} 1 = 1 \neq 0.$$

### Counterexample 2: Brownian motion ($M_0 = 0$)

The failure persists even for a nontrivial martingale with $M_0 = 0$. Let $M_t =
 B_t$ be standard Brownian motion.

**Joint distribution.** The pair $\bigl(B_t,\; \int_0^t B_s\,ds\bigr)$ is jointl
y Gaussian with zero means and:
$$\operatorname{Var}(B_t) = t, \qquad \operatorname{Var}\!\left(\textstyle\int_0
^t B_s\,ds\right) = \frac{t^3}{3}, \qquad \operatorname{Cov}\!\left(B_t,\, \text
style\int_0^t B_s\,ds\right) = \int_0^t \min(t,s)\,ds = \frac{t^2}{2}.$$

**Conditional expectation formula.** For jointly Gaussian $(X,Y)$ with zero mean
s, $\mathbf{E}[X\mid Y] = \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)}{\operatorname{Var}(Y)}\
,Y$, hence:
$$\mathbf{E}\!\left[X\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y > a\}}\right] = \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(
X,Y)}{\operatorname{Var}(Y)}\;\mathbf{E}\!\left[Y\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y > a\}}\right]
.$$

For $Y \sim N(0,\sigma^2)$, we have $\mathbf{E}[Y\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y>a\}}] = \sigm
a\,\varphi(a/\sigma)$, where $\varphi$ is the standard normal density.

**Applying with** $X = B_t$, $Y = \int_0^t B_s\,ds$, $\sigma = \sqrt{t^3/3}$:

$$\mathbf{E}\!\left(B_t\,\mathbf{1}_{\{\int_0^t B_s\,ds > a\}}\right) = \frac{t^
2/2}{t^3/3}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{t^3}{3}}\cdot\varphi\!\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{t^3/3}}\r
ight) = \frac{3}{2t}\cdot\frac{t^{3/2}}{\sqrt{3}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,e^{
-3a^2/(2t^3)}.$$

Simplifying:
$$= \frac{\sqrt{3\,t}}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}\; e^{-3a^2/(2t^3)}.$$

**Inner limit ($t \to \infty$, $a > 0$ fixed).** As $t \to \infty$:
- $\sqrt{t} \to \infty$,
- $e^{-3a^2/(2t^3)} \to e^0 = 1$,

so the expression $\to +\infty$.

**Outer limit ($a \to \infty$).** Since the inner limit is $+\infty$ for every $
a > 0$:
$$\lim_{a\to\infty}\lim_{t\to\infty} \mathbf{E}\!\left(B_t;\, \textstyle\int_0^t
 B_s\,ds > a\right) = \lim_{a\to\infty}(+\infty) = +\infty \neq 0.$$

### Conclusion

The equality fails for the constant martingale $M_t \equiv 1$ (giving limit $1$)
 and also for Brownian motion $M_t = B_t$ with $M_0 = 0$ (giving limit $+\infty$
). Therefore the equality **does not hold** in general.
