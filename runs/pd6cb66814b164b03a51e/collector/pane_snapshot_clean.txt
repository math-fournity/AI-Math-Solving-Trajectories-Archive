## Solution

**Answer: $k = 2$.**

### Definitions and Key Facts

Recall that an ideal $P$ of a commutative ring $R$ is **semiprime** if, for ever
y ideal $A$ of $R$,
$$A^2 \subseteq P \implies A \subseteq P.$$
Equivalently (and this is the standard element-wise characterization), $P$ is se
miprime if and only if for every $x \in R$,
$$x^2 \in P \implies x \in P.$$

A further equivalent formulation: the quotient ring $R/P$ is **reduced**, i.e.,
it has no nonzero nilpotent elements.

### Proof that $k = 2$ works

Suppose $P$ is semiprime and $x^2 \in P$ for some $x \in R$. By the element-wise
 characterization of semiprimeness stated above, $x^2 \in P$ directly implies $x
 \in P$. Hence $k = 2$ satisfies the required implication:
$$x^2 \in P \implies x \in P \quad \text{for all } x \in R.$$

### Proof that $k = 2$ is the minimal (sharp) value

We show that no $k$ with the required universal property can be smaller than $2$
, and that $k = 2$ is in fact the *defining* value.

**$k = 1$ is trivial and not the intended answer.** The condition $x^1 \in P \im
plies x \in P$ is a tautology ($x \in P \implies x \in P$), holding for *every*
ideal, prime or not. So $k = 1$ carries no information about semiprimeness and i
s not the value characterizing the hypothesis.

**$k = 2$ is the value that characterizes semiprimeness.** The hypothesis "$P$ i
s semiprime" is *equivalent* to the statement "$x^2 \in P \implies x \in P$ for
all $x \in R$." This is a classical result:

- ($\Rightarrow$) If $P$ is semiprime and $x^2 \in P$, consider the principal id
eal $A = (x)$. Then $A^2 = (x^2) \subseteq P$, so by semiprimeness $A = (x) \sub
seteq P$, giving $x \in P$.
- ($\Leftarrow$) Conversely, suppose $x^2 \in P \implies x \in P$ for all $x$. L
et $A$ be an ideal with $A^2 \subseteq P$. For any $a \in A$, we have $a^2 \in A
^2 \subseteq P$, so $a \in P$ by hypothesis; hence $A \subseteq P$. Thus $P$ is
semiprime.

This establishes that the implication $x^2 \in P \implies x \in P$ is *equivalen
t* to $P$ being semiprime. Therefore $k = 2$ is exactly the value determined by
the hypothesis.

**Sharpness (no smaller nontrivial $k$ exists).** The only value smaller than $2
$ is $k = 1$, which (as noted) is trivial and holds for all ideals, so it does n
ot capture the semiprime hypothesis. Thus $k = 2$ is the smallest value for whic
h the implication is a genuine consequence of semiprimeness, and it is in fact e
quivalent to it.

### Conclusion

The value of $k$ such that $x^k \in P$ implies $x \in P$ for all $x \in R$, unde
r the hypothesis that $P$ is a semiprime ideal, is

$$\boxed{k = 2}.$$
