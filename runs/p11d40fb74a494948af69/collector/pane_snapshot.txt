## Proof

We work in the product probability space $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$ where $\Omeg
a = \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{R}}$, $\mathcal{F}$ is the product $\sigma$-algebra, and $P
$ is the product measure (each coordinate independently 0 or 1 with probability
1/2). An outcome $\omega$ determines $X(\omega) = \{x \in \mathbb{R} : \omega(x)
 = 1\}$.

**Key fact:** Every event $A \in \mathcal{F}$ depends on at most countably many
coordinates — there exists countable $S \subset \mathbb{R}$ such that membership
 in $A$ is determined by $\{\omega(x) : x \in S\}$.

**Step 1: Measurability is invariant under countable changes.**

If $\omega, \omega'$ differ on a countable set $D$, then $X(\omega) \triangle X(
\omega') \subset D$, which is countable and hence Lebesgue measurable. So $X(\om
ega)$ is measurable iff $X(\omega')$ is.

**Step 2: No nonempty event in $\mathcal{F}$ is contained in $M = \{\omega : X(\
omega) \text{ is Lebesgue measurable}\}$.**

Let $B \in \mathcal{F}$ be nonempty, depending on countable $S \subset \mathbb{R
}$. Pick $\omega \in B$. Since $\mathbb{R} \setminus S$ is uncountable, it conta
ins a non-measurable set $V$ (e.g., a Vitali set). Define $\omega' \in \Omega$ b
y:
- $\omega'(x) = \omega(x)$ for $x \in S$,
- $\omega'(x) = 1$ for $x \in V$,
- $\omega'(x) = 0$ for $x \in \mathbb{R} \setminus (S \cup V)$.

Then $\omega' \in B$ (agrees with $\omega$ on $S$). But $X(\omega') = (X(\omega)
 \cap S) \cup V$. Since $X(\omega) \cap S$ is countable (hence measurable) and $
V \subset \mathbb{R} \setminus S$, if $X(\omega')$ were measurable, then $V = X(
\omega') \setminus (X(\omega) \cap S)$ would be measurable — contradiction. So $
\omega' \in B \setminus M$, hence $B \not\subset M$.

**Step 3: The inner measure of $M$ is 0.**

$$P_*(M) = \sup\{P(B) : B \in \mathcal{F},\, B \subset M\} = 0$$

since by Step 2, only $B = \emptyset$ qualifies.

**Step 4 (symmetric): No nonempty event in $\mathcal{F}$ is contained in $M^c$.*
*

For any nonempty $B \in \mathcal{F}$ depending on countable $S$, and $\omega \in
 B$, define $\omega''$ to agree with $\omega$ on $S$ and be $0$ on $\mathbb{R} \
setminus S$. Then $\omega'' \in B$ and $X(\omega'') = X(\omega) \cap S$ is count
able, hence measurable. So $B \not\subset M^c$, giving $P_*(M^c) = 0$ and $P^*(M
) = 1$.

**Step 5: Conclusion.**

$M$ has inner measure $0$ and outer measure $1$, so it is not in the completion
of $\mathcal{F}$. Every event of positive probability contains outcomes yielding
 non-measurable sets (Step 2), while every event of positive probability also co
ntains outcomes yielding measurable sets (Step 4). No positive probability can b
e assigned to $M$ from below.

The probability that $X$ is Lebesgue measurable is $\boxed{0}$.
