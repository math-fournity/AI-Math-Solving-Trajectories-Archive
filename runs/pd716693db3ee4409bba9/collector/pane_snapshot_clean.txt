## Solution: Laplace's Equation Outside a Circular Disk

We solve Laplace's equation in the exterior region $r \geq a$ (in polar coordina
tes):

$$\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial r^2} + \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r} +
 \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial \theta^2} = 0, \qquad r > a,$$

with the boundary condition $u(a,\theta)$ prescribed on the circle $r = a$, and
the requirement that $u$ remains **bounded as $r \to \infty$**.

---

### General exterior solution

By separation of variables $u(r,\theta) = R(r)\Theta(\theta)$, with $\Theta$ bei
ng $2\pi$-periodic, we obtain $\Theta_n = \cos n\theta, \sin n\theta$ ($n = 0, 1
, 2, \dots$) and radial equation

$$r^2 R'' + r R' - n^2 R = 0,$$

whose solutions are $R = r^n$ and $R = r^{-n}$ for $n \geq 1$, and $R = \text{co
nst}$ or $R = \ln r$ for $n = 0$.

**Boundedness at infinity** forces us to discard $r^n$ ($n \geq 1$) and $\ln r$
(both blow up as $r \to \infty$). The general bounded exterior solution is there
fore

$$\boxed{u(r,\theta) = A_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} r^{-n}\bigl(B_n \cos n\theta +
D_n \sin n\theta\bigr), \qquad r \geq a.}$$

Evaluating at $r = a$:

$$u(a,\theta) = A_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a^{-n}\bigl(B_n \cos n\theta + D_n \si
n n\theta\bigr).$$

The boundary data determines the coefficients by Fourier matching.

---

### Part (a): $u(a,\theta) = \ln 2 + 4\cos 3\theta$

Matching term-by-term at $r = a$:

- **Constant term:** $A_0 = \ln 2$.
- **$\cos 3\theta$ term:** $a^{-3} B_3 = 4 \implies B_3 = 4a^3$.
- **All other coefficients:** $B_n = 0$ ($n \neq 3$), $D_n = 0$ for all $n$.

Substituting back:

$$\boxed{u(r,\theta) = \ln 2 + 4\left(\frac{a}{r}\right)^{\!3} \cos 3\theta, \qq
uad r \geq a.}$$

**Verification.** Each term satisfies Laplace's equation: $\ln 2$ is a constant
(harmonic), and $r^{-3}\cos 3\theta$ is the $n=3$ separated solution (a standard
 harmonic function). At $r = a$: $u(a,\theta) = \ln 2 + 4\cos 3\theta$. ✓ As $r
\to \infty$: $u \to \ln 2$, which is bounded. ✓

---

### Part (b): $u(a,\theta) = f(\theta)$

Expand the boundary data in a Fourier series:

$$f(\theta) = \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl(a_n \cos n\theta + b_n \s
in n\theta\bigr),$$

with Fourier coefficients

$$a_0 = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(\theta)\,d\theta, \qquad a_n = \frac{1}{\pi
}\int_0^{2\pi} f(\theta)\cos n\theta\,d\theta, \qquad b_n = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^
{2\pi} f(\theta)\sin n\theta\,d\theta.$$

Matching at $r = a$:

- $A_0 = \dfrac{a_0}{2}$,
- $a^{-n} B_n = a_n \implies B_n = a_n\, a^n$,
- $a^{-n} D_n = b_n \implies D_n = b_n\, a^n$.

Substituting into the general solution:

$$\boxed{u(r,\theta) = \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{a}{r}\righ
t)^{\!n}\bigl(a_n \cos n\theta + b_n \sin n\theta\bigr), \qquad r \geq a.}$$

**Equivalently**, substituting the integral formulas for $a_n, b_n$ and summing
the series (using the Poisson kernel for the exterior), one obtains the **exteri
or Poisson integral formula**:

$$u(r,\theta) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} \frac{r^2 - a^2}{r^2 - 2ar\cos(\thet
a - \phi) + a^2}\, f(\phi)\,d\phi, \qquad r > a.$$

This is derived as follows. Writing the series solution and using the identity

$$\frac{1}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \rho^n \cos n\alpha = \frac{1 - \rho^2}{2(1
- 2\rho\cos\alpha + \rho^2)}, \qquad |\rho| < 1,$$

with $\rho = a/r \in (0,1)$ (since $r > a$), we get

$$u(r,\theta) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(\phi)\left[1 + 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty
}\left(\frac{a}{r}\right)^n \cos n(\theta-\phi)\right]d\phi = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int
_0^{2\pi} \frac{r^2 - a^2}{r^2 - 2ar\cos(\theta-\phi) + a^2}\,f(\phi)\,d\phi.$$

**Verification.** The kernel $\frac{r^2 - a^2}{r^2 - 2ar\cos(\theta-\phi)+a^2}$
is harmonic in $(r,\theta)$ for $r > a$ (it is the real part of $\frac{re^{i\the
ta}+ae^{i\phi}}{re^{i\theta}-ae^{i\phi}}$, up to sign), so $u$ is harmonic. As $
r \to a^+$, the kernel approaches the Dirac delta $2\pi\delta(\theta - \phi)$, r
ecovering $u(a,\theta) = f(\theta)$. As $r \to \infty$, $u \to \frac{a_0}{2} = \
frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f(\phi)\,d\phi$, which is bounded. ✓

---

**Summary.** The exterior solution is obtained from the interior Poisson formula
 by the substitution $r \mapsto a^2/r$ (Kelvin inversion), which maps the exteri
or domain $r > a$ to the interior $r < a$ and preserves harmonicity. This is why
 the exterior solution has the factor $(a/r)^n$ instead of $(r/a)^n$.
