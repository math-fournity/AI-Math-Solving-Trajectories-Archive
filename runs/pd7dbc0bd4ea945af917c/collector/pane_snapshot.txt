Looking at this problem, I need to find the largest constant $k$ such that $AP +
 BQ \geq k \cdot HC$ always holds, given the condition $PX = QY$.

## Setup and Coordinates

Place $C$ at the origin with the internal bisector of $\angle C$ along the $x$-a
xis. Let $\angle C = 2\gamma$, so the **external bisector of $\angle C$ is the $
y$-axis**. Set:
$$A = (b\cos\gamma,\; b\sin\gamma), \qquad B = (a\cos\gamma,\; -a\sin\gamma),$$
where $a = BC$, $b = AC$, $a \neq b$, and the triangle is acute (so $\gamma < 45
°$, $\cos 2\gamma > 0$).

## Key Points: $X$, $Y$, $H$

**Altitude from $A$** (direction $(\sin\gamma, \cos\gamma)$, perpendicular to $B
C$) meets the $y$-axis at:
$$Y = \left(0,\; -\frac{b\cos 2\gamma}{\sin\gamma}\right).$$

**Altitude from $B$** (direction $(-\sin\gamma, \cos\gamma)$, perpendicular to $
AC$) meets the $y$-axis at:
$$X = \left(0,\; \frac{a\cos 2\gamma}{\sin\gamma}\right).$$

**Orthocenter** $H$ (intersection of the two altitudes):
$$H = \left(\frac{(a+b)\cos 2\gamma}{2\cos\gamma},\; \frac{(a-b)\cos 2\gamma}{2\
sin\gamma}\right).$$

A standard check gives $HC = c\cot C$ where $c = AB$, consistent with $CH = 2R\c
os C$.

## External Bisector of $\angle AHB$

The unit vectors from $H$ toward $A$ and $B$ are:
$$\hat{HA} = (\sin\gamma,\; \cos\gamma), \qquad \hat{HB} = (\sin\gamma,\; -\cos\
gamma).$$

- **Internal bisector** direction: $\hat{HA} + \hat{HB} = (2\sin\gamma, 0)$ — **
horizontal**.
- **External bisector** direction: $\hat{HA} - \hat{HB} = (0, 2\cos\gamma)$ — **
vertical**.

So the beam from $H$ is the **vertical line** $x = H_x = \frac{(a+b)\cos 2\gamma
}{2\cos\gamma}$.

## Finding $P$ and $Q$

**Point $P$** on segment $AX$: The $x$-coordinate along $AX$ (from $A$ to $X$) g
oes from $b\cos\gamma$ to $0$. At $x = H_x$, the fraction from $A$ is:
$$f_P = 1 - \frac{H_x}{b\cos\gamma} = 1 - \frac{(a+b)\cos 2\gamma}{2b\cos^2\gamm
a}.$$

**Point $Q$** on segment $BY$: similarly,
$$f_Q = 1 - \frac{(a+b)\cos 2\gamma}{2a\cos^2\gamma}.$$

Then $AP = f_P|AX|$, $PX = (1-f_P)|AX|$, $BQ = f_Q|BY|$, $QY = (1-f_Q)|BY|$.

## The Condition $PX = QY$

Computing $|AX|^2$ and $|BY|^2$:
$$|AX|^2 = b^2 - 2ab\cos 2\gamma + \frac{a^2\cos^2 2\gamma}{\sin^2\gamma}, \qqua
d |BY|^2 = a^2 - 2ab\cos 2\gamma + \frac{b^2\cos^2 2\gamma}{\sin^2\gamma}.$$

The condition $(1-f_P)|AX| = (1-f_Q)|BY|$ simplifies (after canceling common pos
itive factors) to:
$$\frac{|AX|}{b} = \frac{|BY|}{a} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\,|AX| = b\,|BY
|.$$

Squaring and substituting, setting $a^2|AX|^2 = b^2|BY|^2$ and simplifying (fact
oring out $a^2 - b^2 \neq 0$):

$$\cos 2\gamma\left[-2ab + \frac{\cos 2\gamma\,(a^2+b^2)}{\sin^2\gamma}\right] =
 0.$$

Since $\cos 2\gamma \neq 0$ (acute triangle), and using $\sin^2\gamma = \frac{1-
\cos 2\gamma}{2}$:

$$\boxed{\cos 2\gamma = \frac{ab}{a^2 + ab + b^2}}.$$

## Computing the Ratio $(AP + BQ)/HC$

Set $D = a^2 + ab + b^2$. From the condition: $\cos 2\gamma = ab/D$, giving:
$$\sin^2\gamma = \frac{a^2+b^2}{2D}, \qquad \cos^2\gamma = \frac{(a+b)^2}{2D}, \
qquad \tan^2\gamma = \frac{a^2+b^2}{(a+b)^2}.$$

**Simplifying $f_P, f_Q$:** Substituting,
$$1 - f_P = \frac{(a+b)\cdot(ab/D)}{2b\cdot(a+b)^2/(2D)} = \frac{a}{a+b}, \quad\
text{so}\quad f_P = \frac{b}{a+b}, \qquad f_Q = \frac{a}{a+b}.$$

Since $a|AX| = b|BY|$, write $|AX| = bt$, $|BY| = at$. Then:
$$AP + BQ = f_P \cdot bt + f_Q \cdot at = t\left(\frac{b^2 + a^2}{a+b}\right) =
\frac{t(a^2+b^2)}{a+b}.$$

**Finding $t^2$:** From $b^2t^2 = |AX|^2$, substituting and simplifying with com
mon denominator $D(a^2+b^2)$:

$$t^2 = \frac{(a+b)^2(a^2 - ab + b^2)}{D(a^2+b^2)}.$$

**Therefore:**
$$(AP+BQ)^2 = t^2\cdot\frac{(a^2+b^2)^2}{(a+b)^2} = \frac{(a^2-ab+b^2)(a^2+b^2)}
{D}.$$

**Computing $HC^2$:** Using $c^2 = a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C$ and $\cot C = \frac{ab}{(a
+b)\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}$:

$$c^2 = \frac{(a+b)^2(a^2-ab+b^2)}{D}, \qquad HC = c\cot C = \frac{ab\sqrt{a^2-a
b+b^2}}{\sqrt{D(a^2+b^2)}}.$$

$$HC^2 = \frac{a^2b^2(a^2-ab+b^2)}{D(a^2+b^2)}.$$

**The ratio:**
$$\frac{(AP+BQ)^2}{HC^2} = \frac{(a^2-ab+b^2)(a^2+b^2)}{D} \cdot \frac{D(a^2+b^2
)}{a^2b^2(a^2-ab+b^2)} = \frac{(a^2+b^2)^2}{a^2b^2}.$$

$$\frac{AP+BQ}{HC} = \frac{a^2+b^2}{ab} = \frac{a}{b} + \frac{b}{a}.$$

## Conclusion

By AM-GM, $\frac{a}{b} + \frac{b}{a} \geq 2$, with equality iff $a = b$. Since $
a \neq b$ is given, the ratio is **strictly greater than 2**, but can be made ar
bitrarily close to $2$ by taking $a/b \to 1$ (the limiting triangle has $\cos C
= 1/3$, all angles acute, so nearby triangles remain valid).

Therefore the largest $k$ such that $AP + BQ \geq k \cdot HC$ holds for all vali
d configurations is:

$$\boxed{k = 2}.$$
