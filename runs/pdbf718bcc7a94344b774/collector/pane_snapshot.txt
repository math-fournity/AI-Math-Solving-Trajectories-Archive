## Proof

**Claim.** For every $n \geq 1$, the $n$-sphere $S^n$ admits a CW decomposition
consisting of exactly one $0$-cell and one $n$-cell (and no cells of any other d
imension).

**Construction.** Take a single $0$-cell $e^0 = \{*\}$. Take a single $n$-cell $
e^n$, which is an open $n$-disk. To build the CW complex we must specify how $e^
n$ is attached to the $0$-skeleton $X^{(0)} = \{*\}$.

The closed $n$-cell is the closed $n$-disk $D^n$, whose boundary is $\partial D^
n = S^{n-1}$. The attaching map is a continuous map
$$\varphi : S^{n-1} = \partial D^n \longrightarrow X^{(0)} = \{*\}.$$
Since the target is a single point, $\varphi$ is necessarily the unique (constan
t) map $\varphi(p) = *$ for all $p \in S^{n-1}$.

The resulting CW complex is the quotient space
$$X = X^{(0)} \cup_\varphi e^n = D^n \big/ \big(\partial D^n \sim \{*\}\big).$$

**This quotient is $S^n$.** The space $D^n / \partial D^n$ — the closed $n$-disk
 with its entire boundary collapsed to a single point — is homeomorphic to $S^n$
. This is a standard fact; here is the explicit argument.

Define $\pi : D^n \to S^n$ by
$$\pi(x) = \bigl(2\sqrt{1-\|x\|^2}\, x,\; 2\|x\|^2 - 1\bigr),$$
where we view $S^n \subset \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R} = \mathbb{R}^{n+1}$. T
his is the inverse of the stereographic-style projection: it sends the center $x
=0$ to the south pole $(0,\dots,0,-1)$, and sends every point of $\partial D^n$
(where $\|x\|=1$) to the north pole $(0,\dots,0,1)$, since then $2\sqrt{1-\|x\|^
2}\,x = 0$ and $2\|x\|^2-1 = 1$.

The map $\pi$ is continuous, surjective, and constant (equal to the north pole)
exactly on $\partial D^n$, and injective on $\operatorname{int}(D^n)$. By the un
iversal property of the quotient topology, $\pi$ descends to a continuous biject
ion
$$\bar{\pi} : D^n / \partial D^n \longrightarrow S^n.$$
The domain $D^n/\partial D^n$ is compact (as the quotient of the compact space $
D^n$), and $S^n$ is Hausdorff. A continuous bijection from a compact space onto
a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism. Hence
$$D^n / \partial D^n \cong S^n.$$

**Verification of the CW axioms.**

1. *(Closure-finiteness.)* The closure $\overline{e^n} = X$ meets only the cells
 $e^0$ and $e^n$ — finitely many. The $0$-cell's closure meets only itself. So t
he closure of every cell meets finitely many cells. ✓

2. *(Weak topology.)* A subset $A \subseteq X$ is closed if and only if $A \cap
\overline{e^0}$ and $A \cap \overline{e^n}$ are closed. Here $\overline{e^0} = \
{*\}$ and $\overline{e^n} = X$. So the condition reduces to: $A$ is closed iff $
A \cap X = A$ is closed in $X$ — which is tautological — and $A \cap \{*\}$ is c
losed, which is automatic. This is exactly the quotient topology on $D^n/\partia
l D^n$, which is the definition of $X$. ✓

3. *(Characteristic maps.)* The characteristic map for $e^n$ is the quotient map
 $D^n \to X$, which restricts to a homeomorphism $\operatorname{int}(D^n) \to e^
n$. The characteristic map for $e^0$ is the identity on $D^0$. ✓

**The case $n = 0$.** $S^0 = \{-1, +1\}$ is two points, which is a CW complex wi
th two $0$-cells. It cannot be built from a single $0$-cell and a single $0$-cel
l... but the question specifies "an $n$-cell and a $0$-cell," i.e. one cell of e
ach of two distinct dimensions. For $n=0$ this would be one $0$-cell only (a sin
gle point), which is not $S^0$. So the construction above applies for $n \geq 1$
.

**Conclusion.** For every $n \geq 1$, $S^n$ admits a CW decomposition with exact
ly one $0$-cell and one $n$-cell: the $0$-cell is a point, and the $n$-cell is a
ttached by the unique map $S^{n-1} \to \{*\}$, producing $D^n/\partial D^n \cong
 S^n$. $\blacksquare$
