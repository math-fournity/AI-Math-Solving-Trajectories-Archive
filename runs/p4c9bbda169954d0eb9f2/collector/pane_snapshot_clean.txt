## Solution

The statement is **not** necessarily true.

**Counterexample.** Let $p \geq 1$ be given. Set
$$a_0 = 2^{1/p}, \qquad a_k = 0 \;\text{ for all } k \geq 1.$$

**Verification of the hypothesis.** We have
$$\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} |a_k|^p = |a_0|^p + 0 = 2 < \infty,$$
so $\bigl(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|a_k|^p\bigr)^{1/p} = 2^{1/p} < +\infty$. The hypot
hesis is satisfied.

**Evaluation of the claimed inequality.** Since $a_k = 0$ for $k \geq 1$ (and $0
\cdot\log 0$ is interpreted as $0$ via the continuous extension $x\log x \to 0$
as $x\downarrow 0$),
$$\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} |a_k|^p \log|a_k| = |a_0|^p \log|a_0| = 2\cdot\log\!\bigl(
2^{1/p}\bigr) = \frac{2\log 2}{p}.$$

Since $\log 2 > 0$ and $p \geq 1$, we obtain
$$\frac{2\log 2}{p} > 0,$$
which is **strictly positive**, hence violates $\sum |a_k|^p \log|a_k| \leq 0$.

Therefore the inequality is **not** necessarily true under the stated hypothesis
. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** The obstruction is that the hypothesis only controls the *total mass
* $\sum|a_k|^p$, not the size of individual terms. A single term with $|a_0|>1$
contributes a strictly positive amount $|a_0|^p\log|a_0|>0$, and with all other
terms zero there is nothing to offset it.

If one *additionally* imposed $\sum|a_k|^p \leq 1$, then every $|a_k|^p \leq 1$,
 hence $\log|a_k| = \tfrac{1}{p}\log|a_k|^p \leq 0$, giving $|a_k|^p\log|a_k|\le
q 0$ termwise and the inequality would hold. But mere finiteness of the $\ell^p$
 norm is insufficient, as the counterexample above shows.
