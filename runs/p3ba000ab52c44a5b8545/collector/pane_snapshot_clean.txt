## Schwarz-Christoffel Transformation: Derivation

### Setup and Goal

We seek a conformal map $z = f(\tau)$ taking the upper half-plane $\operatorname
{Im}(\tau) > 0$ onto the interior of a polygon in the $z$-plane. The polygon has
 vertices $z_1, z_2, \ldots, z_n$ (in order) with interior angles $\alpha_1\pi,
\alpha_2\pi, \ldots, \alpha_n\pi$. The preimages of the vertices on the real axi
s are $\rho_1 < \rho_2 < \cdots < \rho_n$, with $\infty$ possibly serving as one
 preimage.

### Step 1: Conformality away from vertices

Inside the upper half-plane, $f$ must be conformal, so $f'(\tau) \neq 0$ there.
The only points where $f'$ can vanish or blow up are the prevertices $\rho_i$ on
 the boundary, where the map must create corners.

### Step 2: Argument of $f'$ on the real axis — the key geometric observation

As $\tau$ travels along the real axis from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$, the image $z
= f(\tau)$ traverses the polygon boundary. Between consecutive prevertices $\rho
_i$ and $\rho_{i+1}$, the image is a straight side, so $\arg f'(\tau)$ is **cons
tant** on each open interval $(\rho_i, \rho_{i+1})$.

At each prevertex $\rho_i$, the boundary turns by the **exterior angle** $\beta_
i\pi = \pi - \alpha_i\pi = (1-\alpha_i)\pi$. This means $\arg f'$ jumps by exact
ly $\beta_i\pi = (1-\alpha_i)\pi$ as $\tau$ crosses $\rho_i$.

### Step 3: Building $f'$ from its argument jumps

We need a function whose argument is constant on each interval $(\rho_i, \rho_{i
+1})$ and jumps by $(1-\alpha_i)\pi$ at $\rho_i$. Consider the factor $(\tau - \
rho_i)$. On the real axis:

- For $\tau < \rho_i$: $\arg(\tau - \rho_i) = \pi$.
- For $\tau > \rho_i$: $\arg(\tau - \rho_i) = 0$.

So crossing $\rho_i$ from left to right, $\arg(\tau - \rho_i)$ **decreases** by
$\pi$. To get a **jump of $+(1-\alpha_i)\pi$** (an increase), we need the expone
nt $-(1-\alpha_i) = \alpha_i - 1$, i.e., the factor $(\tau - \rho_i)^{\alpha_i -
 1}$:

$$\arg(\tau-\rho_i)^{\alpha_i-1} = (\alpha_i-1)\arg(\tau-\rho_i),$$

which jumps by $(\alpha_i - 1)(-\pi) = (1-\alpha_i)\pi$. ✓

**Sign convention check**: The exterior turning angle is $\beta_i\pi = (1-\alpha
_i)\pi$. The factor $(\tau-\rho_i)^{\alpha_i-1}$ produces exactly this jump. Set
ting $k_i = \alpha_i - 1$, the jump is $-k_i\pi = (1-\alpha_i)\pi = \beta_i\pi$.
 ✓

### Step 4: The derivative formula

Combining all factors, the most general function with the correct argument behav
ior on the real axis and analyticity in the upper half-plane is:

$$\boxed{\frac{dz}{d\tau} = f'(\tau) = C\,(\tau-\rho_1)^{k_1}(\tau-\rho_2)^{k_2}
\cdots(\tau-\rho_n)^{k_n}}$$

where $k_i = \alpha_i - 1$ and $C$ is a complex constant. Integrating:

$$\boxed{z = f(\tau) = C_0 + C\int^{\tau} (\zeta-\rho_1)^{k_1}(\zeta-\rho_2)^{k_
2}\cdots(\zeta-\rho_n)^{k_n}\,d\zeta}$$

with $C_0$ a translation and $C$ controlling scale and rotation.

### Step 5: Significance of the constants $k_i$

Each $k_i = \alpha_i - 1$ is determined by the **interior angle** $\alpha_i\pi$
of the polygon at vertex $z_i$:

- **$k_i$ encodes the corner geometry.** The exponent $k_i$ controls how much th
e tangent direction rotates at vertex $z_i$. A jump of $-k_i\pi = (1-\alpha_i)\p
i$ in $\arg f'$ is precisely the exterior angle at that vertex.

- **Range of $k_i$:** For a convex polygon, $0 < \alpha_i < 2$, so $-1 < k_i < 1
$. For a simple polygon (possibly non-convex), $0 < \alpha_i < 2$ still holds, g
iving $-1 < k_i < 1$. Re-entrant (reflex) corners have $\alpha_i > 1$, i.e., $k_
i > 0$; sharp convex corners have $\alpha_i < 1$, i.e., $k_i < 0$.

- **Angle-sum consistency:** For a closed $n$-gon, $\sum \alpha_i = n-2$, so $\s
um k_i = \sum(\alpha_i - 1) = (n-2) - n = -2$. This ensures the boundary closes
up (the total turning is $2\pi$).

- **Branch-point interpretation:** At each prevertex $\rho_i$, $f'$ has an algeb
raic singularity of order $k_i$. The map behaves locally as $z - z_i \propto (\t
au - \rho_i)^{\alpha_i}$, which opens the straight angle $\pi$ (upper half-plane
 boundary) into the interior angle $\alpha_i\pi$ — exactly the corner-flattening
/stretching required.

- **$C$ and $C_0$** are not determined by angles alone; they fix the overall sca
le, rotation, and translation. Three real parameters can be fixed by normalizati
on (e.g., sending three prevertices to prescribed positions via Möbius invarianc
e of the half-plane), leaving the polygon's shape fully determined by the $k_i$
and the remaining prevertex positions.

### Step 6: Verification of closure

On the real axis between $\rho_i$ and $\rho_{i+1}$, $\arg f'$ is constant (each
factor contributes a fixed argument), so the image is a straight segment. At $\r
ho_i$, $\arg f'$ jumps by $(1-\alpha_i)\pi$, producing the exterior angle. The t
otal jump is $\sum(1-\alpha_i)\pi = (n - \sum\alpha_i)\pi = (n-(n-2))\pi = 2\pi$
, confirming the boundary closes after a full $2\pi$ turn. ∎
