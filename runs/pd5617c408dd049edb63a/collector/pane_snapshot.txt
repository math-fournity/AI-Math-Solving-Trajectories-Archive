## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. If a discrete group $G$ admits a Følner sequence, then $G$ is co
untable.

**Setup.** Let $(F_n)_{n=1}^{\infty}$ be a Følner sequence for $G$. By definitio
n, each $F_n$ is a finite, nonempty subset of $G$, and for every $g \in G$,
$$\frac{|gF_n \,\triangle\, F_n|}{|F_n|} \;\longrightarrow\; 0 \quad \text{as }
n \to \infty.$$

**Key step.** Fix an arbitrary $g \in G$. Since left multiplication by $g$ is a
bijection, $|gF_n| = |F_n|$. Hence
$$|gF_n \,\triangle\, F_n| \;=\; |gF_n| + |F_n| - 2\,|gF_n \cap F_n| \;=\; 2|F_n
| - 2\,|gF_n \cap F_n|.$$

Dividing by $|F_n|$,
$$\frac{|gF_n \,\triangle\, F_n|}{|F_n|} \;=\; 2 - 2\,\frac{|gF_n \cap F_n|}{|F_
n|}.$$

The Følner condition forces the left side to $0$, so
$$\frac{|gF_n \cap F_n|}{|F_n|} \;\longrightarrow\; 1.$$

In particular, for all sufficiently large $n$, $|gF_n \cap F_n| > 0$, i.e. $gF_n
 \cap F_n \neq \emptyset$.

**Extracting $g$ from the Følner sets.** Whenever $gF_n \cap F_n \neq \emptyset$
, there exist $x, y \in F_n$ with $gx = y$, hence
$$g = y\,x^{-1} \in F_n\,F_n^{-1}.$$

So for every $g \in G$ there is an $N(g)$ such that $g \in F_n F_n^{-1}$ for all
 $n \geq N(g)$. Therefore
$$G \;\subseteq\; \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} F_n\,F_n^{-1}.$$

**Counting.** Each $F_n F_n^{-1}$ is finite (it has at most $|F_n|^2$ elements).
 The right-hand side is a countable union of finite sets, hence **countable**. S
ince $G$ is contained in a countable set, $G$ is countable. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** This is exactly why, for uncountable discrete amenable groups, one m
ust use Følner *nets* (indexed by arbitrary directed sets) rather than Følner *s
equences*. A sequence is intrinsically countable, and the argument above shows i
t cannot "reach" every element of an uncountable group. The existence of a Følne
r sequence is therefore strictly stronger than amenability for uncountable group
s; it is equivalent to amenability **plus countability**.
