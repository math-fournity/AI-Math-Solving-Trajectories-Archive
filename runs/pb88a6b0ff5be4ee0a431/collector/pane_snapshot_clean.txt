## Proof

Let $X_1, \dots, X_n$ be i.i.d. $\text{Uniform}[\theta_1, \theta_2]$ with $\thet
a_1 < \theta_2$, and let $X_{(1)} = \min_i X_i$ and $X_{(n)} = \max_i X_i$ denot
e the extreme order statistics.

**Sufficiency.** The joint density is
$$
f(x_1,\dots,x_n;\theta_1,\theta_2) = \frac{1}{(\theta_2-\theta_1)^n}\,\mathbf{1}
\{\theta_1 \le x_{(1)}\}\,\mathbf{1}\{x_{(n)} \le \theta_2\}.
$$
By the factorization theorem this factors as $g(T(x),\theta)\,h(x)$ with $T(x) =
 (x_{(1)}, x_{(n)})$, $g(T,\theta) = (\theta_2-\theta_1)^{-n}\mathbf{1}\{\theta_
1 \le x_{(1)}\}\mathbf{1}\{x_{(n)} \le \theta_2\}$, and $h(x)\equiv 1$. Hence $T
 = (X_{(1)}, X_{(n)})$ is sufficient.

**Minimality.** Use the Lehmann–Scheffé ratio criterion: a statistic $T$ is mini
mal sufficient iff $T(x)=T(y)$ is equivalent to $f(x;\theta)/f(y;\theta)$ being
independent of $\theta$ (for all $\theta$ where the ratio is well-defined).

For two samples $x$ and $y$,
$$
\frac{f(x;\theta_1,\theta_2)}{f(y;\theta_1,\theta_2)} = \frac{\mathbf{1}\{\theta
_1 \le x_{(1)},\; x_{(n)} \le \theta_2\}}{\mathbf{1}\{\theta_1 \le y_{(1)},\; y_
{(n)} \le \theta_2\}},
$$
which is a function of $(\theta_1,\theta_2)$ only through the two indicator func
tions. This ratio is constant (independent of $\theta_1,\theta_2$) iff the two i
ndicator functions are identical as functions of $(\theta_1,\theta_2)$, i.e., if
f
$$
\{(\theta_1,\theta_2): \theta_1 \le x_{(1)},\; \theta_2 \ge x_{(n)}\} = \{(\thet
a_1,\theta_2): \theta_1 \le y_{(1)},\; \theta_2 \ge y_{(n)}\}.
$$
These half-planes are equal precisely when $x_{(1)} = y_{(1)}$ and $x_{(n)} = y_
{(n)}$. Therefore the ratio is independent of $\theta$ iff $(x_{(1)}, x_{(n)}) =
 (y_{(1)}, y_{(n)})$.

By the ratio criterion, $T = (X_{(1)}, X_{(n)})$ is minimal sufficient.

**Conclusion.** A minimal sufficient statistic for a sample of size $n$ from $\t
ext{Uniform}[\theta_1,\theta_2]$ is the pair of extreme order statistics
$$
T(X_1,\dots,X_n) = \bigl(X_{(1)},\, X_{(n)}\bigr) = \bigl(\min_{1\le i\le n} X_i
,\; \max_{1\le i\le n} X_i\bigr).
$$
