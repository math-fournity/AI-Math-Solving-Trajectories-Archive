## Proof

Let $n = \lfloor t \rfloor$ and $x = t - n \in [0, 1)$. Then $t = n + x$, and th
e equilibrium equation becomes

$$n + x = 50\sin(x), \quad x \in [0,1), \quad n \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.$$

(The constraint $n \geq 0$ follows since $t = 50\sin(x) \geq 0$ for $x \in [0,1)
$, as $\sin(x) \geq 0$ on this interval.)

Define $f(x) = 50\sin(x) - x$ on $[0,1)$. We need to count integers $n \geq 0$ i
n the range of $f$, each giving exactly one solution $x$ (hence one $t = n+x$).

**Monotonicity.** $f'(x) = 50\cos(x) - 1$. For $x \in [0,1)$, $\cos(x) \in (\cos
 1, 1]$, so
$$f'(x) \geq 50\cos(1) - 1 \approx 50(0.5403) - 1 \approx 26.0 > 0.$$
Thus $f$ is strictly increasing on $[0,1)$, so each value in its range is attain
ed exactly once.

**Range.**
- $f(0) = 0$.
- $\lim_{x \to 1^-} f(x) = 50\sin(1) - 1 \approx 50(0.84147) - 1 \approx 41.074$
.

So the range of $f$ on $[0,1)$ is $[0, 41.074\ldots)$.

**Counting integers.** The non-negative integers in $[0, 41.074)$ are $0, 1, 2,
\ldots, 41$, giving **42** integers.

For each such $n$, strict monotonicity guarantees exactly one $x_n \in [0,1)$ wi
th $f(x_n) = n$, hence exactly one $t_n = n + x_n$.

- $n = 0$: $x = 0$, $t = 0$. ✓
- $n = 41$: since $f(1^-) \approx 41.074 > 41 = f(0) + \ldots$, there is a uniqu
e $x \in [0,1)$ with $f(x)=41$. ✓
- $n = 42$: $42 > 41.074$, no solution. ✗

**Consistency check.** For each solution, $\lfloor t_n \rfloor = \lfloor n + x_n
 \rfloor = n$ (since $x_n \in [0,1)$), confirming the decomposition is self-cons
istent.

The total number of distinct real values of $t$ is $\boxed{42}$.
