## Step 1: $n=4$ is impossible

**Claim:** Any four unit vectors summing to zero must contain an antipodal pair.

**Proof:** Let $v_1, v_2, v_3, v_4$ be unit vectors (complex numbers $e^{i\alpha
}, e^{i\beta}, e^{i\gamma}, e^{i\delta}$) with $v_1+v_2+v_3+v_4=0$. Then $v_1+v_
2 = -(v_3+v_4)$.

Writing $v_j + v_k = 2\cos\!\tfrac{\theta_j-\theta_k}{2}\, e^{i(\theta_j+\theta_
k)/2}$, the equality $v_1+v_2 = -(v_3+v_4)$ gives:

$$\cos\tfrac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\, e^{i(\alpha+\beta)/2} = \cos\tfrac{\gamma-\delta
}{2}\, e^{i((\gamma+\delta)/2+\pi)}.$$

Equating real-complex products yields two cases:

- **(A)** $\cos\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}=\cos\frac{\gamma-\delta}{2}$ and $\frac{\a
lpha+\beta}{2}=\frac{\gamma+\delta}{2}+\pi$. Then $\alpha+\beta=\gamma+\delta+2\
pi$ and $\alpha-\beta=\pm(\gamma-\delta)$, giving $\alpha=\gamma+\pi$ or $\alpha
=\delta+\pi$: an antipodal pair.

- **(B)** $\cos\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}=-\cos\frac{\gamma-\delta}{2}$ and $\frac{\
alpha+\beta}{2}=\frac{\gamma+\delta}{2}$. Then $\alpha+\beta=\gamma+\delta$ and
$\alpha-\beta=2\pi\pm(\gamma-\delta)$, giving $\alpha=\delta+\pi$ or $\alpha=\ga
mma+\pi$: an antipodal pair.

In every case an antipodal pair exists. So $n=4\notin S$. $\square$

## Step 3: All even $n\geq 6$ work

- Within each polygon, no antipodal pair exists (both have odd numbers of vertic
