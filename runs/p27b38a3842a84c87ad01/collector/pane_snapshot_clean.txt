## Solution

**Goal.** Evaluate $I=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1000}\frac{e^{-10x}\sin x}{x}\,dx$
to within $\pm 10^{-5}$.

**Step 1 — Reduce to the integral on $[0,\infty)$.**

For $x\ge 0$ we have $|\sin x|\le x$, so $\left|\frac{\sin x}{x}\right|\le 1$. H
ence for the tail
$$
\left|\int_{1000}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-10x}\sin x}{x}\,dx\right|
\le \int_{1000}^{\infty} e^{-10x}\,dx
= \frac{e^{-10000}}{10}.
$$
Since $e^{-10000}<10^{-4343}$, this tail is far smaller than $10^{-5}$. Thus
$$
I = \int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-10x}\sin x}{x}\,dx \quad\text{to within } \pm 10^
{-5}\ (\text{indeed to } \pm 10^{-4343}).
$$

**Step 2 — Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-10x}\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx$
 by series.**

Use the everywhere-convergent expansion $\displaystyle\frac{\sin x}{x}=\sum_{n=0
}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n x^{2n}}{(2n+1)!}$. The interchange of sum and integral is
 justified by absolute convergence:
$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2n+1)!}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-10x}x^{2n}\,dx
= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2n)!}{(2n+1)!\,10^{2n+1}}
= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2n+1)\,10^{2n+1}}<\infty.
$$
Therefore
$$
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-10x}\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx
= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n}{(2n+1)\,10^{2n+1}}
= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n\,(1/10)^{2n+1}}{2n+1}
= \arctan\!\left(\tfrac{1}{10}\right).
$$

**Step 3 — Compute $\arctan(0.1)$ to within $\pm 10^{-5}$.**

This is an alternating series with decreasing magnitudes, so the error after tru
ncating is bounded by the first omitted term:
$$
\arctan(0.1)=0.1-\frac{0.1^{3}}{3}+\frac{0.1^{5}}{5}-\frac{0.1^{7}}{7}+\frac{0.1
^{9}}{9}-\cdots
$$
The terms are
$$
0.1,\quad 3.3333\ldots\times10^{-4},\quad 2\times10^{-6},\quad 1.42857\ldots\tim
es10^{-8},\quad 1.111\ldots\times10^{-10},\ldots
$$
Keeping terms through $n=4$ gives an error below $10^{-11}/11<10^{-12}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\arctan(0.1)
&= 0.1 - 0.000333333333\ldots + 0.000002 \\
&\quad - 0.0000000142857\ldots + 0.0000000001111\ldots \\
&= 0.099668652491\ldots
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 4 — Combine the error bounds.**

- Truncation of the upper limit ($1000$ vs $\infty$): error $<10^{-4343}$.
- Truncation of the $\arctan$ series: error $<10^{-12}$.

Total error $\ll 10^{-5}$, so the required value is

$$
\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1000}\frac{e^{-10x}\sin x}{x}\,dx \;=\; 0.09966865
\ldots}
$$

which, rounded to five decimal places, is $\;0.09967\;$, comfortably within $\pm
 10^{-5}$ (the actual error is below $10^{-11}$).
