Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $T \ge PTP$ always holds wh
en $T$ is a positive operator and $P$ is a projection on a Hilbert space $H$.

**Answer: No, the inequality does not hold in general.**

**Proof by counterexample.**

Let $H = \mathbb{R}^2$ with the standard inner product. Define:

$$T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad P = \begin{pmatrix} 1
 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

**$T$ is positive.** The eigenvalues of $T$ are the roots of $\det(T - \lambda I
) = (1-\lambda)^2 - 1 = \lambda(\lambda - 2) = 0$, giving $\lambda = 0$ and $\la
mbda = 2$. Since both eigenvalues are non-negative and $T$ is symmetric (self-ad
joint), $T \ge 0$.

**$P$ is a projection.** We have $P^2 = P$ and $P = P^*$, so $P$ is an orthogona
l projection (hence a projection in any sense of the term).

**Compute $PTP$:**

$$PTP = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 &
 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1
 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin
{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

**Compute $T - PTP$:**

$$T - PTP = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0
 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

**Show $T - PTP$ is not positive.** Take $x = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatr
ix}$:

$$(T - PTP)x = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\
-1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

$$\langle (T - PTP)x,\, x \rangle = \left\langle \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 0 \end{pm
atrix}, \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} \right\rangle = (-1)(1) + (0)(-1)
= -1 < 0.$$

Since the quadratic form takes a negative value, $T - PTP$ is **not** a positive
 operator. Therefore $T \not\ge PTP$.

**Why the inequality fails (intuition).** Decompose $x = Px + (I-P)x = u + v$. T
hen:

$$\langle (T - PTP)x, x\rangle = \langle Tv, v\rangle + 2\,\operatorname{Re}\lan
gle Tv, u\rangle.$$

The first term is non-negative (since $T \ge 0$), but the cross term $2\,\operat
orname{Re}\langle Tv, u\rangle$ can be arbitrarily negative, dominating the firs

e of $P$ with its kernel, producing a large negative cross term. $\blacksquare$
