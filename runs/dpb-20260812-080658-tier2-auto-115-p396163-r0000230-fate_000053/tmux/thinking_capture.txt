Proof

Let $G$ be a cyclic group with $|G| \geq 3$. We show $G$ has at least two
distinct generators.

Recall that in a cyclic group $G = \langle g \rangle$, an element $g^k$ is a
generator of $G$ if and only if $\langle g^k \rangle = G$. We treat two cases.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Case 1: $G$ is infinite.

Then $G \cong (\mathbb{Z}, +)$, generated by $1$. The element $-1$ also
generates $\mathbb{Z}$, since every integer $m$ can be written as $m = (-1)(-
m)$. Since $|G| \geq 3$ and $G$ is infinite, this case applies, and $1 \neq -1$
are two distinct generators. In the original group, these correspond to $g$ and
$g^{-1}$, which are distinct (otherwise $g = g^{-1}$, i.e., $g^2 = e$, forcing
$|G| \leq 2$, contradiction).

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Case 2: $G$ is finite, $|G| = n \geq 3$.

Write $G = \langle g \rangle$ with $g$ of order $n$. The generators of $G$ are
exactly the elements $g^k$ with $\gcd(k, n) = 1$ and $1 \leq k \leq n$. The
number of generators is $\varphi(n)$ (Euler's totient function).

We exhibit two distinct values of $k$ coprime to $n$:

  • $k = 1$: $\gcd(1, n) = 1$ always. So $g^1 = g$ is a generator.
  • $k = n - 1$: $\gcd(n-1, n) = \gcd(n-1,, n - (n-1)) = \gcd(n-1, 1) = 1$. So
  $g^{n-1}$ is a generator.

Since $n \geq 3$, we have $n - 1 \geq 2 > 1$, so $k = 1$ and $k = n-1$ are
distinct. Hence $g$ and $g^{n-1}$ are two distinct generators of $G$.

To confirm they are distinct as group elements: if $g = g^{n-1}$, then $g^{n-2}
= e$, so the order of $g$ divides $n - 2$. But the order of $g$ is $n$, so $n
\mid (n-2)$, which forces $n \mid 2$, i.e., $n \leq 2$ — contradicting $n \geq
3$.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Conclusion

In both the infinite and finite cases, a cyclic group $G$ with $|G| \geq 3$
possesses at least two distinct generators. $\blacksquare$
