**Key structural observation:** Define the *level* of vertex $v_{i,j}$ as $j$. E
very edge connects a vertex at level $j$ to a vertex at level $i+j > j$. Therefo
re:

**Vertices at the same level are never adjacent** (an edge requires the target l
evel $i+j > j$, and equality would force $i=0$, impossible for positive integers
).

**Proof that the chromatic number is infinite:**

Suppose for contradiction that $G$ admits a proper coloring with $m$ colors (for
 some finite $m$). For each level $\ell \geq 1$, let $S_\ell \subseteq \{1, 2, \
ldots, m\}$ denote the set of colors that appear on vertices at level $\ell$.

**Adjacency constraint:** Vertex $v_{i,j}$ (at level $j$) is adjacent to *every*
 vertex at level $i+j$. Therefore, the color assigned to $v_{i,j}$ must differ f
rom the color of every vertex at level $i+j$, meaning:
$$c(v_{i,j}) \notin S_{i+j}.$$
Since $c(v_{i,j}) \in S_j$ (it is a color used at level $j$), we must have:
$$S_j \setminus S_{i+j} \neq \emptyset \quad \text{for all } i, j \geq 1.$$
Equivalently, $S_j \not\subseteq S_{i+j}$ for all $i \geq 1$. Since $i+j$ ranges
 over all integers greater than $j$ as $i$ varies over $\mathbb{Z}^+$:
$$S_j \not\subseteq S_\ell \quad \text{for all } \ell > j \geq 1.$$

**All levels must have distinct color sets:** If $S_a = S_b$ for some $a < b$, t
hen $S_a \subseteq S_b$, contradicting the constraint above. Therefore $S_1, S_2
, S_3, \ldots$ are pairwise distinct.

**Pigeonhole contradiction:** Each $S_\ell$ is a subset of $\{1, \ldots, m\}$, s
o there are at most $2^m$ possible values. But we need infinitely many distinct
sets $S_1, S_2, S_3, \ldots$ (one per level). This is impossible for any finite
$m$.

Therefore, no finite proper coloring of $G$ exists. The chromatic number of $G$
is **necessarily infinite**.

**Remark (the graph is not bipartite):** A 5-cycle exists: $v_{1,1} - v_{1,2} -
v_{1,3} - v_{1,4} - v_{2,2} - v_{1,1}$, confirming $\chi(G) \geq 3$. Each edge i
s verified: $v_{1,1}$ connects to level 2 (containing $v_{1,2}$ and $v_{2,2}$);
$v_{1,2}$ connects to level 3; $v_{1,3}$ connects to level 4; $v_{2,2}$ connects
 to level 4 (containing $v_{1,4}$). This is consistent with the chromatic number
