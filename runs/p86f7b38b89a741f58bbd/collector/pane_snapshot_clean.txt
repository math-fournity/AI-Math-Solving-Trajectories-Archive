## Solution

We analyze the linear operator $T: C[0,2] \to C[0,2]$ defined by
$$T(f)(x) = \int_0^2 \cos(x+y)\, f(y)\, dy.$$

**Step 1: Expand the kernel.** Using the addition formula,
$$\cos(x+y) = \cos x \cos y - \sin x \sin y.$$

Substituting into the integral:
$$T(f)(x) = \cos x \int_0^2 \cos y\, f(y)\, dy \;-\; \sin x \int_0^2 \sin y\, f(
y)\, dy.$$

**Step 2: Identify the range.** For any $f \in C[0,2]$, the two integrals
$$A(f) = \int_0^2 \cos y\, f(y)\, dy, \qquad B(f) = \int_0^2 \sin y\, f(y)\, dy$
$
are real numbers (constants depending on $f$). Hence
$$T(f) = A(f)\cos x - B(f)\sin x.$$

So every element of the range lies in $\operatorname{span}\{\cos x,\, \sin x\}$,
 giving
$$\dim \operatorname{Range}(T) \le 2.$$

**Step 3: Show both basis vectors are attained (dimension $\ge 2$).** We need $f
_1, f_2 \in C[0,2]$ with $T(f_1) = \cos x$ and $T(f_2) = \sin x$ (up to linear i
ndependence of the outputs).

Take $f_1(y) = \cos y$. Then
$$A(f_1) = \int_0^2 \cos^2 y\, dy = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^2(1+\cos 2y)\,dy = 1 + \tf
rac{1}{2}\sin 2 \approx 1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\sin 2,$$
$$B(f_1) = \int_0^2 \sin y\cos y\, dy = \tfrac{1}{2}\sin^2 y\Big|_0^2 = \tfrac{1
}{2}\sin^2 2.$$

Take $f_2(y) = \sin y$. Then
$$A(f_2) = \int_0^2 \cos y\sin y\, dy = \tfrac{1}{2}\sin^2 2,$$
$$B(f_2) = \int_0^2 \sin^2 y\, dy = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^2(1-\cos 2y)\,dy = 1 - \tf
rac{1}{2}\sin 2.$$

The Gram matrix of the coefficient functionals is
$$G = \begin{pmatrix} \int_0^2\cos^2 y\,dy & \int_0^2\cos y\sin y\,dy \\ \int_0^
2\cos y\sin y\,dy & \int_0^2\sin^2 y\,dy \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1+\tfra
c{1}{2}\sin 2 & \tfrac{1}{2}\sin^2 2 \\ \tfrac{1}{2}\sin^2 2 & 1-\tfrac{1}{2}\si
n 2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Its determinant is
$$\det G = \left(1+\tfrac{1}{2}\sin 2\right)\left(1-\tfrac{1}{2}\sin 2\right) -
\tfrac{1}{4}\sin^4 2 = 1 - \tfrac{1}{4}\sin^2 2 - \tfrac{1}{4}\sin^4 2.$$

Since $|\sin 2| < 1$ (as $2$ is not an odd multiple of $\pi/2$), we have $\tfrac
{1}{4}\sin^2 2 + \tfrac{1}{4}\sin^4 2 < \tfrac{1}{4} + \tfrac{1}{4} = \tfrac{1}{
2} < 1$, so $\det G > 0$.

Therefore $G$ is invertible, which means the map $f \mapsto (A(f), B(f))$ is sur
jective onto $\mathbb{R}^2$ when restricted to $\operatorname{span}\{\cos y, \si
n y\}$. In particular, there exist $f_1, f_2 \in C[0,2]$ producing $T(f_1) = \co
s x$ and $T(f_2) = \sin x$.

**Step 4: Linear independence of $\cos x$ and $\sin x$.** These are linearly ind
ependent functions on $[0,2]$ (e.g., evaluating at $x=0$ and $x=\pi/2$ gives an
invertible system).

**Conclusion.** The range of $T$ is exactly $\operatorname{span}\{\cos x, \sin x
\}$, a 2-dimensional subspace of $C[0,2]$.

$$\boxed{\dim \operatorname{Range}(T) = 2}$$
