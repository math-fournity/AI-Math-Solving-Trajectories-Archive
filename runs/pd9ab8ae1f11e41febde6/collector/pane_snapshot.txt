## Proof

We determine the number of zeros of $f(z) = z^4 + e^z$ in the open unit disc $|z
|<1$ via a homotopy (Rouché-type) argument.

**Step 1: Setup.** Define the one-parameter family
$$f_t(z) = z^4 + t\,e^z, \qquad t \in [0,1].$$
At $t=0$, $f_0(z)=z^4$ has a zero of multiplicity $4$ at the origin, so $N(0)=4$
 zeros (counted with multiplicity) lie in $|z|<1$.

**Step 2: No zeros on $|z|=1$ at $t=1$.** If $|z|=1$ and $f_1(z)=0$, then $z^4 =
 -e^z$, giving $|z^4|=|e^z|$, i.e. $1 = e^{\operatorname{Re} z}$, so $\operatorn
ame{Re} z = 0$, hence $z = \pm i$. But $f_1(i) = 1 + e^i \neq 0$ and $f_1(-i) =
1 + e^{-i} \neq 0$. So no zeros lie on the boundary at $t=1$; the count is well-
defined.

**Step 3: Locate all boundary crossings.** A zero crosses $|z|=1$ at some $t\in(
0,1]$ when $z = e^{i\theta}$ satisfies $z^4 + t\,e^z = 0$, i.e.
$$z^4 = -t\,e^z \quad\Longrightarrow\quad |z^4| = t\,|e^z| \quad\Longrightarrow\
quad 1 = t\,e^{\cos\theta},$$
so $t = e^{-\cos\theta}$. For $t \le 1$ we need $\cos\theta \ge 0$, i.e. $\theta
 \in [-\pi/2,\, \pi/2]$.

The phase condition $z^4 e^{-z} \in \mathbb{R}_{<0}$ requires
$$\arg\!\bigl(z^4 e^{-z}\bigr) = 4\theta - \sin\theta = (2k+1)\pi.$$
Set $\varphi(\theta) = 4\theta - \sin\theta$. Since $\varphi'(\theta) = 4 - \cos
\theta \ge 3 > 0$, $\varphi$ is strictly increasing. On $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$,
$$\varphi(-\pi/2) = -2\pi+1 \approx -5.28, \qquad \varphi(\pi/2) = 2\pi-1 \appro
x 5.28.$$
The odd multiples of $\pi$ in $(-2\pi+1,\; 2\pi-1)$ are exactly $\pm\pi$ (since
$3\pi > 2\pi-1$ and $-3\pi < -2\pi+1$). Hence there are exactly **two** solution
s:
- $\theta_0 \in (0,\pi/2)$ with $\varphi(\theta_0) = \pi$,
- $\theta_1 = -\theta_0 \in (-\pi/2,0)$ with $\varphi(\theta_1) = -\pi$ (by odd
symmetry of $\varphi$).

Both give the **same** crossing parameter $t^* = e^{-\cos\theta_0}$ (a conjugate
 pair crosses simultaneously). Since $\theta_0 \in (0,\pi/2)$, we have $\cos\the
ta_0 > 0$, so
$$t^* = e^{-\cos\theta_0} < 1.$$
Thus the unique crossing event occurs at $t^* \in (0,1)$, involving exactly two
zeros.

**Step 4: Crossing direction is outward.** Let $z(t)$ be a zero near $z^* = e^{i
\theta_0}$. Differentiating $z^4 + t\,e^z = 0$:
$$z' = \frac{-e^z}{4z^3 + t\,e^z}.$$
At the crossing, $t\,e^z = -z^4$, so $4z^3 + t e^z = z^3(4-z)$, giving $z' = -e^
z/(z^3(4-z))$. The radial velocity is
$$\frac{d|z|^2}{dt} = 2\operatorname{Re}(\bar z\, z') = 2\operatorname{Re}\!\lef
t(\frac{-e^z}{z^4(4-z)}\right) \quad (\text{since } \bar z = 1/z \text{ on } |z|
=1).$$
Using $e^z = -z^4/t$ at the crossing:
$$\frac{d|z|^2}{dt} = 2\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\frac{1}{t(4-z)}\right) = \frac{
2}{t}\cdot\frac{4 - \operatorname{Re} z}{|4-z|^2}.$$
On $|z|=1$, $\operatorname{Re} z \le 1 < 4$, so $4 - \operatorname{Re} z > 0$ an
d therefore
$$\frac{d|z|^2}{dt} > 0.$$
The zero moves **outward** through the unit circle. (The same holds for the conj
ugate zero at $\theta_1 = -\theta_0$.)

**Step 5: Conclusion.** Starting with $N(0) = 4$ zeros inside $|z|<1$, the only
crossing event in $t\in[0,1]$ occurs at $t^* \in (0,1)$ where exactly two zeros
exit outward. No further crossings occur before $t=1$, and no zeros lie on $|z|=
1$ at $t=1$. Hence
$$N(1) = 4 - 2 = 2.$$

The function $f(z) = z^4 + e^z$ has exactly $\boxed{2}$ zeros in the unit disc $
|z|<1$.
