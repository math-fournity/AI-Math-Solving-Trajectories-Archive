## Proof

Let $T: \ell^2 \to \ell^2$ be the diagonal operator $(Tx)_j = \alpha_j \xi_j$, w
here $(\alpha_j)$ is dense in $[0,1]$.

### Point spectrum $\sigma_p(T)$

$\lambda \in \sigma_p(T)$ iff there exists $x = (\xi_j) \in \ell^2$, $x \neq 0$,
 with $Tx = \lambda x$, i.e. $(\alpha_j - \lambda)\xi_j = 0$ for all $j$. Hence
$\xi_j \neq 0$ only when $\alpha_j = \lambda$. Such an $x$ is nonzero iff there
exists at least one index $j$ with $\alpha_j = \lambda$. Therefore

$$\sigma_p(T) = \{\alpha_j : j \in \mathbb{N}\}.$$

### Spectrum $\sigma(T)$

$T$ is a bounded normal operator (diagonal, self-adjoint since $\alpha_j \in [0,
1] \subset \mathbb{R}$), with $\|T\| = \sup_j |\alpha_j| \le 1$.

**Claim:** $\sigma(T) = \overline{\{\alpha_j\}} = [0,1]$.

*Proof that $\sigma(T) \supseteq \overline{\{\alpha_j\}}$.* For any $j$, $\alpha
_j \in \sigma(T)$ since $\alpha_j \in \sigma_p(T) \subseteq \sigma(T)$. The spec
trum is closed, so $\overline{\{\alpha_j\}} \subseteq \sigma(T)$. By density, $\
overline{\{\alpha_j\}} = [0,1]$.

*Proof that $\sigma(T) \subseteq \overline{\{\alpha_j\}}$.* Let $\lambda \notin
\overline{\{\alpha_j\}} = [0,1]$. Then $\delta := \inf_j |\alpha_j - \lambda| >
0$ (since the continuous function $t \mapsto |t - \lambda|$ attains its positive
 minimum on the compact set $[0,1]$, and $\lambda \notin [0,1]$). The operator $
T - \lambda I$ is diagonal with entries $\alpha_j - \lambda$, each of modulus $\
geq \delta$. Its inverse is the diagonal operator with entries $(\alpha_j - \lam
bda)^{-1}$, which is bounded with norm $\le 1/\delta$. Hence $T - \lambda I$ is
invertible, so $\lambda \notin \sigma(T)$.

Combining both inclusions:

$$\sigma(T) = [0,1].$$

### Summary

$$\boxed{\sigma_p(T) = \{\alpha_j : j \in \mathbb{N}\}, \qquad \sigma(T) = [0,1]
.}$$

The continuous spectrum is $\sigma_c(T) = [0,1] \setminus \{\alpha_j\}$ (those l
imit points not themselves eigenvalues), and the residual spectrum is empty sinc
e $T$ is self-adjoint.
