Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether for a point $z$ in a fundam
ental domain of $\Gamma(2)$, there exists $\epsilon > 0$ (depending on $z$) such
 that $\cosh(d(z, \gamma z)) > 1 + \epsilon$ for all $\gamma \neq \pm I$ in $\Ga
mma(2)$.

**Answer: Yes, such an $\epsilon$ exists for every $z$ in the upper half plane (
and in particular for $z$ in any fundamental domain of $\Gamma(2)$).**

## Proof

**Step 1: The hyperbolic distance formula.**

For $\gamma = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \in \mathrm{SL}_2(\ma
thbb{R})$ and $z \in \mathbb{H}$, we have $\gamma z = \frac{az+b}{cz+d}$ and $\o
peratorname{Im}(\gamma z) = \frac{\operatorname{Im}(z)}{|cz+d|^2}$. The standard
 formula for the hyperbolic distance gives:

$$\cosh(d(z, \gamma z)) = 1 + \frac{|z - \gamma z|^2}{2\,\operatorname{Im}(z)\,\
operatorname{Im}(\gamma z)}.$$

Computing $z - \gamma z = \frac{cz^2 + (d-a)z - b}{cz+d}$, we obtain:

$$\cosh(d(z, \gamma z)) = 1 + \frac{|cz^2 + (d-a)z - b|^2}{2\,(\operatorname{Im}
 z)^2}. \tag{$\ast$}$$

Thus the question reduces to whether $\inf_{\gamma \neq \pm I} |cz^2 + (d-a)z -
b|^2 > 0$.

**Step 2: $\Gamma(2)/\{\pm I\}$ is torsion-free.**

Recall $\Gamma(2) = \{\gamma \in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) : \gamma \equiv I \pm
od{2}\}$, so $a,d$ are odd and $b,c$ are even. The element $-I \in \Gamma(2)$ (s
ince $-1 \equiv 1 \pmod{2}$) and acts trivially on $\mathbb{H}$.

We show no other torsion exists. Any torsion element of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z
})$ has order $1, 2, 3, 4$, or $6$, with traces $2, -2, -1, 0, 1$ respectively.
For $\gamma \in \Gamma(2)$, the trace $a+d$ is even (both $a,d$ odd). This immed
iately rules out orders $3$ and $6$ (traces $-1$ and $1$, both odd). For order $
4$ (trace $0$): $d = -a$ and $ad - bc = -a^2 - bc = 1$, so $a^2 + bc = -1$. Sinc
e $a$ is odd, $a^2 \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$, and $b,c$ even gives $bc \equiv 0 \pmod{4
}$, so $a^2 + bc \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$, contradicting $a^2 + bc = -1 \equiv 3 \pmod
{4}$. The only remaining torsion is $\pm I$ (order $1$ and $2$).

Therefore $\Gamma(2)/\{\pm I\}$ acts **freely** on $\mathbb{H}$: for every $\gam
ma \neq \pm I$ and every $z \in \mathbb{H}$, $\gamma z \neq z$, hence $d(z, \gam
ma z) > 0$.

**Step 3: Proper discontinuity gives finiteness.**

$\Gamma(2)$ is a discrete subgroup of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{R})$ (it is a finit
e-index subgroup of the discrete group $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$). Discrete su
bgroups of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{R})$ act **properly discontinuously** on $\mat
hbb{H}$: for any compact set $K \subset \mathbb{H}$, the set $\{\gamma \in \Gamm
a(2) : \gamma K \cap K \neq \emptyset\}$ is finite.

Fix $z \in \mathbb{H}$ and let $R > 0$. The closed hyperbolic ball $\overline{B}
(z, R) = \{w \in \mathbb{H} : d(z,w) \leq R\}$ is compact. If $d(z, \gamma z) \l
eq R$, then $z \in \overline{B}(z,R)$ and $\gamma z \in \overline{B}(z,R)$, so $
\gamma(\overline{B}(z,R)) \cap \overline{B}(z,R) \neq \emptyset$. By proper disc
ontinuity:

$$\{\gamma \in \Gamma(2) : d(z, \gamma z) \leq R\} \text{ is finite.}$$

**Step 4: The infimum is a positive minimum.**

Choose any $R > 0$ (e.g., $R = d(z, T^2 z)$ where $T^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \
\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \in \Gamma(2)$, so $R > 0$ since $T^2 z = z + 2 \neq z$).
The set $S = \{\gamma \in \Gamma(2) \setminus \{\pm I\} : d(z, \gamma z) \leq R\
}$ is finite and non-empty (it contains $T^2$). Define:

$$\delta := \min_{\gamma \in S} d(z, \gamma z) > 0,$$

where the minimum exists because $S$ is finite, and $\delta > 0$ because each $d
(z, \gamma z) > 0$ by Step 2 (torsion-freeness).

For any $\gamma \neq \pm I$ with $d(z, \gamma z) > R$, we have $d(z, \gamma z) >
 R \geq \delta$. For $\gamma \in S$, $d(z, \gamma z) \geq \delta$ by definition.
 Therefore:

$$\inf_{\gamma \neq \pm I} d(z, \gamma z) = \delta > 0.$$

**Step 5: Constructing $\epsilon$.**

Since $\cosh$ is strictly increasing on $[0, \infty)$, for all $\gamma \neq \pm
I$:

$$\cosh(d(z, \gamma z)) \geq \cosh(\delta) = 1 + (\cosh(\delta) - 1).$$

Set $\epsilon := \frac{\cosh(\delta) - 1}{2} > 0$. Then for all $\gamma \neq \pm
 I$:

$$\cosh(d(z, \gamma z)) \geq 1 + 2\epsilon > 1 + \epsilon. \qquad \blacksquare$$

**Remark.** The argument uses only that $\Gamma(2)$ is discrete and that $\Gamma
(2)/\{\pm I\}$ is torsion-free, so the conclusion holds for **every** $z \in \ma
thbb{H}$, not just $z$ in a fundamental domain. The fundamental domain context i
s relevant in that $\epsilon$ depends on $z$: near a cusp (e.g., $\operatorname{
Im}(z) \to \infty$), the translation $T^2: z \mapsto z+2$ gives $\cosh(d(z, T^2
z)) = 1 + \frac{2}{(\operatorname{Im} z)^2} \to 1$, so $\epsilon \to 0$, but it
remains strictly positive for each fixed $z$.
