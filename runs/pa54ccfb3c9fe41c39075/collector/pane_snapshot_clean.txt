## Problem

Determine the condition for divergence of the multidimensional harmonic series
$$S \;=\; \sum_{\vec n \in \mathbb{N}^d} \frac{1}{\lvert \vec n\rvert^p},$$
where $\lvert \vec n\rvert = \sqrt{n_1^2+\cdots+n_d^2}$ is the Euclidean norm.

**Answer.** The series diverges if and only if $p \le d$.

---

## Proof

We assume $\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,3,\dots\}$ so that every term is finite (the case $\
mathbb{N}\ni 0$ differs only by excluding $\vec 0$, which does not affect conver
gence). The key tool is a lattice-point counting estimate for spherical shells.

### Lemma (shell counting)

For $R\ge 1$ let $N(R)=\bigl\lvert\{\vec n\in\mathbb{Z}^d : R\le \lvert\vec n\rv
ert < R+1\}\bigr\rvert$. There exist constants $c_d, C_d>0$, depending only on $
d$, such that
$$c_d\, R^{d-1}\;\le\; N(R)\;\le\; C_d\, R^{d-1}\qquad\text{for all }R\ge 1.$$

*Proof of Lemma.* Associate to each lattice point $\vec n$ the unit cube $Q_{\ve
c n}=\vec n+[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]^d$, which has volume $1$ and center $\vec n$. T
hese cubes tile $\mathbb{R}^d$ and are pairwise disjoint interiors.

**Upper bound.** If $\vec n$ lies in the annulus $R\le\lvert\vec n\rvert<R+1$, t
hen every $x\in Q_{\vec n}$ satisfies $\lvert x-\vec n\rvert\le \sqrt{d}/2$, hen
ce
$$R-\tfrac{\sqrt d}{2}\;\le\;\lvert x\rvert\;\le\;R+1+\tfrac{\sqrt d}{2}.$$
Thus $\bigcup_{\vec n\in A_R}Q_{\vec n}$ is contained in the thickened annulus $
\{x:R-\frac{\sqrt d}{2}\le\lvert x\rvert\le R+1+\frac{\sqrt d}{2}\}$, whose volu
me is
$$\omega_d\Bigl[(R+1+\tfrac{\sqrt d}{2})^d-(R-\tfrac{\sqrt d}{2})^d\Bigr]=O(R^{d
-1}),$$
where $\omega_d=\pi^{d/2}/\Gamma(d/2+1)$ is the volume of the unit ball. Since t
he cubes are disjoint with volume $1$, $N(R)\le C_d R^{d-1}$.

**Lower bound.** For $R\ge \sqrt d$, the inner annulus $\{x:R+\frac{\sqrt d}{2}\
le\lvert x\rvert\le R+1-\frac{\sqrt d}{2}\}$ is nonempty and has volume
$$\omega_d\Bigl[(R+1-\tfrac{\sqrt d}{2})^d-(R+\tfrac{\sqrt d}{2})^d\Bigr]\;\sim\
;\omega_d\, d\, R^{d-1}\quad(R\to\infty).$$
Every point of this inner annulus lies within distance $\sqrt d/2$ of some latti
ce point $\vec n$, and that $\vec n$ then satisfies $R\le\lvert\vec n\rvert\le R
+1$. Hence the cubes $Q_{\vec n}$ with $\vec n\in A_R$ cover the inner annulus,
giving $N(R)\ge c_d R^{d-1}$. $\square$

---

### Convergence when $p>d$

Partition $\mathbb{N}^d$ by integer shells $A_R=\{\vec n\in\mathbb{N}^d:R\le\lve
rt\vec n\rvert<R+1\}$, $R=1,2,\dots$ On $A_R$ we have $\lvert\vec n\rvert\ge R$,
 so using the upper bound of the Lemma:
$$S=\sum_{R=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\vec n\in A_R}\frac{1}{\lvert\vec n\rvert^p}\;\le\;
\sum_{R=1}^{\infty}\frac{N(R)}{R^p}\;\le\;C_d\sum_{R=1}^{\infty}R^{d-1-p}.$$
The series $\sum R^{d-1-p}$ converges iff the exponent $d-1-p<-1$, i.e. iff $p>d
$. Hence $S<\infty$ for $p>d$.

### Divergence when $p<d$

Consider the box $[1,N]^d\cap\mathbb{N}^d$, which contains $N^d$ points, each wi
th $\lvert\vec n\rvert\le\sqrt d\,N$. Therefore
$$S\;\ge\;\sum_{\vec n\in[1,N]^d}\frac{1}{\lvert\vec n\rvert^p}\;\ge\;\sum_{\vec
 n\in[1,N]^d}\frac{1}{(\sqrt d\,N)^p}=\frac{N^d}{d^{p/2}\,N^p}=\frac{N^{d-p}}{d^
{p/2}}.$$
When $p<d$, the right-hand side $N^{d-p}/d^{p/2}\to\infty$ as $N\to\infty$, so t
he partial sums are unbounded and $S=+\infty$.

### Divergence when $p=d$ (the boundary case)

The box argument above only gives a constant lower bound at $p=d$, so we use the
 shell lower bound. On $A_R$ we have $\lvert\vec n\rvert<R+1$, hence
$$S=\sum_{R=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\vec n\in A_R}\frac{1}{\lvert\vec n\rvert^d}\;\ge\;
\sum_{R=1}^{\infty}\frac{N(R)}{(R+1)^d}\;\ge\;c_d\sum_{R=1}^{\infty}\frac{R^{d-1
}}{(R+1)^d}.$$
For $R\ge 1$, $(R+1)^d\le (2R)^d=2^d R^d$, so
$$S\;\ge\;\frac{c_d}{2^d}\sum_{R=1}^{\infty}\frac{R^{d-1}}{R^d}=\frac{c_d}{2^d}\
sum_{R=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{R}=+\infty,$$
since the harmonic series diverges. Hence $S=+\infty$ for $p=d$.

### Conclusion

Combining the three cases:

$$\boxed{\sum_{\vec n\in\mathbb{N}^d}\frac{1}{\lvert\vec n\rvert^p}\text{ diverg
es }\iff\; p\le d.}$$

Equivalently, the series converges if and only if $p>d$ — the same critical expo
nent as the integral test $\int_1^\infty r^{d-1-p}\,dr$, which is natural since
the lattice-point density in $\mathbb{R}^d$ is $1$ and the surface area of the r
adius-$r$ sphere grows as $r^{d-1}$.
