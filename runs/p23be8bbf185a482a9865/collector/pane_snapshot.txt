Let $S = \{a \in K : a \text{ is purely inseparable over } F\}$. I claim $S$ is
a subfield of $K$.

**Proof.**

Recall that for an algebraic element $a \in K$ over $F$, $a$ is *purely insepara
ble* over $F$ iff its minimal polynomial over $F$ has exactly one root (namely $
a$ itself) in an algebraic closure. Equivalently:
- If $\operatorname{char} F = 0$: $a$ is purely inseparable over $F$ iff $a \in
F$ (since every irreducible polynomial is separable, the only way it has a singl
e root is to be linear).
- If $\operatorname{char} F = p > 0$: $a$ is purely inseparable over $F$ iff $a^
{p^{n}} \in F$ for some integer $n \geq 0$.

We verify $S$ is a subfield by checking closure under subtraction, multiplicatio
n, and inversion (and that $0,1 \in S$).

**Case $\operatorname{char} F = 0$.** By the characterization above, $S = F$, wh
ich is trivially a subfield of $K$.

**Case $\operatorname{char} F = p > 0$.** Let $a, b \in S$ with $b \neq 0$. Then
 there exist integers $n, m \geq 0$ with $a^{p^{n}} \in F$ and $b^{p^{m}} \in F$
. Set $N = \max(n, m)$. Since $F$ is a field, $a^{p^{N}} = (a^{p^{n}})^{p^{N-n}}
 \in F$ and likewise $b^{p^{N}} \in F$.

The Frobenius map $x \mapsto x^{p}$ is a ring homomorphism in characteristic $p$
 (the "Freshman's dream": $(x+y)^p = x^p + y^p$). Iterating, $x \mapsto x^{p^{N}
}$ is also a ring homomorphism. Therefore:

- $(a - b)^{p^{N}} = a^{p^{N}} - b^{p^{N}} \in F$, so $a - b \in S$.
- $(ab)^{p^{N}} = a^{p^{N}} b^{p^{N}} \in F$, so $ab \in S$.
- $(b^{-1})^{p^{N}} = (b^{p^{N}})^{-1} \in F$ (since $b^{p^{N}} \in F \setminus
\{0\}$), so $b^{-1} \in S$.

Also $0, 1 \in F \subseteq S$ (since $0^{p^{0}} = 0 \in F$ and $1^{p^{0}} = 1 \i
n F$).

Thus $S$ is closed under subtraction, multiplication, and inversion of nonzero e
lements, and contains $0$ and $1$. Hence $S$ is a subfield of $K$.

---

**Conclusion.** The set of all elements of $K$ that are purely inseparable over
$F$ is necessarily a subfield of $K$.
