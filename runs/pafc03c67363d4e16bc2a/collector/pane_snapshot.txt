## Proof

1+√5)/2 ≈ 1.61803 and ψ = (1-√5)/2. Since |ψ| < 1, for large n we have L_n ≈ φ^n
. The number of digits of L_n is

$$d(n) = \lfloor n \log_{10} \varphi \rfloor + 1, \qquad \log_{10}\varphi \appro
x 0.20899.$$

**Digit distribution heuristic.** Since $\log_{10}\varphi$ is irrational, by Wey
l's equidistribution theorem the fractional parts $\{n\log_{10}\varphi\}$ are eq
uidistributed in $[0,1)$. This implies the leading digit of $L_n$ follows **Benf
ord's law**: the leading digit equals $k$ with probability $\log_{10}(1+1/k)$. I
n particular, the leading digit is **not** 1 with probability

$$1 - \log_{10} 2 \approx 0.69897.$$

For the remaining $d-1$ digits, we use the standard heuristic (well-supported fo
r linear recurrence sequences) that they are approximately uniform and independe
nt over $\{0,1,\dots,9\}$, so each non-leading digit is not 1 with probability $
9/10$.

**Probability of no digit '1'.** For a $d$-digit Lucas number:

$$\Pr(L_n \text{ contains no } 1) \;\approx\; (1-\log_{10}2)\cdot\left(\tfrac{9}
{10}\right)^{d-1} \;\approx\; 0.69897 \cdot (0.9)^{d-1}.$$

**Counting by digit length.** Group the integers $n \in \{1,\dots,2016\}$ by the
 digit count $d(n)$. The number of $n$ with $d(n)=d$ is approximately $1/\log_{1
0}\varphi \approx 4.785$ per value of $d$ (alternating between 4 and 5). The exp

$$S = \sum_{n=1}^{2016} \Pr(L_n \text{ has no } 1) \approx 0.69897 \sum_{d=1}^{4
22} c_d\cdot(0.9)^{d-1},$$

where $c_d \in \{4,5\}$ with $\sum c_d = 2016$ and $c_d \approx 4.785$ on averag
