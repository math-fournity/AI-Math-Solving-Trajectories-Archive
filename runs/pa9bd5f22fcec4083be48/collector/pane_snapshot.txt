## Proof

**Example.** Let $p$ be a prime, and consider the field $K = \mathbb{F}_p(t)$, t
he field of rational functions in one variable over the finite field $\mathbb{F}
_p$. This is an infinite field of characteristic $p$.

**The Frobenius endomorphism.** The Frobenius map $\varphi: K \to K$ is defined
by $\varphi(x) = x^p$. It is a field homomorphism because in characteristic $p$
we have $(a+b)^p = a^p + b^p$ (by the binomial theorem, since $\binom{p}{k} \equ
iv 0 \pmod{p}$ for $0 < k < p$) and $(ab)^p = a^p b^p$.

**The image of Frobenius.** For any rational function $f(t)/g(t) \in \mathbb{F}_
p(t)$, we have
$$\varphi\!\left(\frac{f(t)}{g(t)}\right) = \frac{f(t)^p}{g(t)^p} = \frac{f(t^p)
}{g(t^p)},$$
where the last equality uses the fact that raising to the $p$-th power replaces
every $t$ by $t^p$ (since the coefficients lie in $\mathbb{F}_p$ and are fixed b
y Frobenius). Therefore
$$\operatorname{im}(\varphi) = \mathbb{F}_p(t^p).$$

**The image is a proper subfield.** We claim $t \notin \mathbb{F}_p(t^p)$, which
 shows $\varphi$ is not surjective.

Since $t$ is transcendental over $\mathbb{F}_p$, the element $t^p$ is also trans
cendental over $\mathbb{F}_p$, and $t$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{F}_p(t^p)$, sa
tisfying the polynomial
$$X^p - t^p \in \mathbb{F}_p(t^p)[X].$$
This polynomial is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_p(t^p)$: it equals $(X - t)^p$ o
ver a splitting field, so its only root is $t$ (with multiplicity $p$). If $t \i
n \mathbb{F}_p(t^p)$, then $X^p - t^p$ would have a linear factor $(X - t)$ over
 $\mathbb{F}_p(t^p)$, meaning $X^p - t^p = (X - t)^p$ splits completely in $\mat
hbb{F}_p(t^p)[X]$. But then $t \in \mathbb{F}_p(t^p)$ is what we are trying to p
rove, giving a circular argument.

More directly: $t$ has degree $p$ over $\mathbb{F}_p(t^p)$. To see irreducibilit
y of $X^p - t^p$, note that $t^p$ is not a $p$-th power in $\mathbb{F}_p(t^p)$:
the elements of $\mathbb{F}_p(t^p)$ are rational functions in $t^p$, and a $p$-t
h root would be a rational function in $t$, which would need to lie in $\mathbb{
F}_p(t^p)$. By Eisenstein's criterion applied to the ring $\mathbb{F}_p[t^p]$ wi
th the prime ideal $(t^p)$, the polynomial $X^p - t^p$ is irreducible over $\mat
hbb{F}_p(t^p)$ (the constant term $t^p$ is divisible by the prime $t^p$ but $t^{
p^2} \nmid t^p$ in the sense that $t^p$ is exactly divisible by the prime elemen
t $t^p$ once... let me state this more carefully).

Concretely: apply Eisenstein's criterion in the UFD $\mathbb{F}_p[t^p]$ with the
 prime element $\pi = t^p$. The polynomial $X^p - t^p \in \mathbb{F}_p[t^p][X]$
has all non-leading coefficients divisible by $\pi$, and the constant term $-t^p
$ is not divisible by $\pi^2 = t^{2p}$. Hence $X^p - t^p$ is irreducible over $\
operatorname{Frac}(\mathbb{F}_p[t^p]) = \mathbb{F}_p(t^p)$.

Since $X^p - t^p$ is irreducible of degree $p \geq 2$ over $\mathbb{F}_p(t^p)$,
the element $t$ does not lie in $\mathbb{F}_p(t^p)$.

**Conclusion.** The element $t \in K = \mathbb{F}_p(t)$ has no $p$-th root in $K
$, i.e., there is no $y \in K$ with $y^p = t$. Therefore the Frobenius endomorph
ism $\varphi: K \to K$, $x \mapsto x^p$, is not surjective. The field $\mathbb{F
}_p(t)$ is infinite (it contains the infinitely many elements $1, t, t^2, \ldots
$) and has characteristic $p$, so it satisfies all the requirements. $\blacksqua
