## Proof

**Setup.** Since $f$ is radial in $\mathbb{R}^3$, the Laplacian acting on $f(r)$
 is
$$\Delta f = f''(r) + \frac{2}{r}f'(r).$$
The equation $(-\Delta + g)f = 0$ becomes
$$f''(r) + \frac{2}{r}f'(r) = g(r)\,f(r). \tag{1}$$

**Key substitution.** Set $u(r) = r\,f(r)$. Then:
$$f = \frac{u}{r}, \quad f' = \frac{u'}{r} - \frac{u}{r^2}, \quad f'' = \frac{u'
'}{r} - \frac{2u'}{r^2} + \frac{2u}{r^3}.$$
Substituting into (1):
$$\frac{u''}{r} - \frac{2u'}{r^2} + \frac{2u}{r^3} + \frac{2}{r}\!\left(\frac{u'
}{r} - \frac{u}{r^2}\right) = g\,\frac{u}{r},$$
which simplifies to
$$\frac{u''}{r} = g(r)\,\frac{u(r)}{r},$$
i.e.,
$$u''(r) = g(r)\,u(r). \tag{2}$$

**Initial conditions for $u$.** Since $f(0) = 0$ and $f$ is continuous (being a
solution), $u(0) = \lim_{r\to 0} r\,f(r) = 0$. For the derivative:
$$u'(r) = f(r) + r\,f'(r),$$
so $u'(0) = f(0) + 0\cdot f'(0) = 0$.

**Uniqueness.** Equation (2) with initial data $u(0) = 0$, $u'(0) = 0$ has the i
ntegral form
$$u(r) = \int_0^r (r - s)\,g(s)\,u(s)\,ds. \tag{3}$$
Since $g$ is nonnegative with compact support, $g \in L^\infty_{\mathrm{loc}}(\m
athbb{R})$. Let $M = \|g\|_{L^\infty[0,R]}$ for any fixed $R > 0$. From (3), for
 $r \in [0, R]$:
$$|u(r)| \leq M \int_0^r (r-s)\,|u(s)|\,ds \leq M\,r \int_0^r |u(s)|\,ds.$$
By Grönwall's inequality, $u(r) = 0$ for all $r \in [0, R]$. Since $R$ was arbit
rary, $u \equiv 0$ on $[0, \infty)$.

**Conclusion.** Since $u \equiv 0$, we have $f(r) = u(r)/r = 0$ for all $r > 0$,
 and by continuity $f \equiv 0$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$. In particular, $f'(r) = 0$ fo

$$\lim_{r \to 0} r^2 \frac{df}{dr} = \lim_{r \to 0} r^2 \cdot 0 = 0.$$

**Yes**, it follows that $\lim_{r\to 0} r^2 \frac{df}{dr} = 0$. In fact, the con
