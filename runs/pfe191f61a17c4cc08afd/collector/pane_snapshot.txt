The statement is **true**. Here is the proof.

---

**Claim.** If $f \in L^2(0,1)$ and $\int_0^1 x^n f(x)\,dx = 0$ for every positiv
e integer $n \geq 1$, then $f = 0$ a.e. on $(0,1)$.

**Proof.**

Let $V = \overline{\operatorname{span}\{x^n : n \geq 1\}} \subseteq L^2(0,1)$, w
here the closure is taken in the $L^2$ norm. The hypothesis says precisely that
$f$ is orthogonal to every $x^n$ ($n \geq 1$), hence $f \in V^\perp$. We will sh
ow $V = L^2(0,1)$, which forces $V^\perp = \{0\}$ and thus $f = 0$ a.e.

It suffices to show that the constant function $\mathbf{1}(x) = 1$ belongs to $V
$, since then:
- $\mathbf{1} \in V$ implies $x^k = x^k \cdot \mathbf{1} \in \operatorname{span}
\{x^n : n \geq 1\} + \text{(already there)}$ trivially for $k \geq 1$, and $x^0
= \mathbf{1} \in V$,
- so $\{x^n : n \geq 0\} \subseteq V$,
- the algebra generated by $\{x^n : n \geq 0\}$ is all polynomials, which are de
nse in $L^2(0,1)$ by the Weierstrass approximation theorem (polynomials are dens
e in $C([0,1])$ in the uniform norm, hence in $L^2$),
- therefore $V = L^2(0,1)$.

It remains to prove $\mathbf{1} \in V$. For each $n \geq 1$, define
$$p_n(x) = 1 - (1 - x)^n.$$
Then $p_n(0) = 1 - 1 = 0$, so expanding,
$$p_n(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k+1} x^k,$$
which is a linear combination of $\{x, x^2, \ldots, x^n\}$; hence $p_n \in \oper
atorname{span}\{x^k : k \geq 1\} \subseteq V$.

Now $p_n(x) \to 1$ pointwise for every $x \in (0,1]$ (since $(1-x)^n \to 0$ for
$x \in (0,1]$), and $|p_n(x)| \leq 1$ for all $x \in [0,1]$. By the dominated co
nvergence theorem (with dominating function $g \equiv 1 \in L^2(0,1)$, using $|p
_n - 1|^2 \leq 4$ and pointwise convergence to $0$),
$$\|p_n - \mathbf{1}\|_{L^2(0,1)}^2 = \int_0^1 |p_n(x) - 1|^2\,dx = \int_0^1 (1-
x)^{2n}\,dx = \frac{1}{2n+1} \xrightarrow{n \to \infty} 0.$$

Thus $\mathbf{1} \in V$. As argued above, this gives $V = L^2(0,1)$, so $f \in V
^\perp = \{0\}$, i.e., $f = 0$ almost everywhere on $(0,1)$. $\blacksquare$
