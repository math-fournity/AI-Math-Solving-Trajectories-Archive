**Answer: Yes, $G \subseteq H$ follows.**

## Proof

**Setup and preliminaries.** Since $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n$ are Galois conjugat
es (roots of a single irreducible polynomial $p \in K[x]$), and $L = K(\alpha_1,
\dots,\alpha_n)$ is the splitting field of $p$, the extension $L/K$ is Galois. T
he roots $\alpha_i$ are distinct (the polynomial is separable since $L/K$ is Gal
ois). The action of $G = \operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ on $\{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n\
}$ is faithful (since $L$ is generated by the $\alpha_i$), giving an embedding $
G \hookrightarrow S_n$.

**Convention.** $S_n$ acts on $K[X_1,\dots,X_n]$ by $\sigma \cdot f(X_1,\dots,X_
n) = f(X_{\sigma(1)},\dots,X_{\sigma(n)})$. The embedding $G \hookrightarrow S_n
$ is defined by $\sigma(\alpha_i) = \alpha_{\sigma(i)}$ for $\sigma \in G$.

**Step 1: Reformulating the hypothesis.** For $\sigma \in G$ and $f \in R = K[X_
1,\dots,X_n]^H$, the hypothesis gives $f(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n) \in K$, so $\s
igma$ fixes it:

$$f(\alpha_{\sigma(1)},\dots,\alpha_{\sigma(n)}) = \sigma\bigl(f(\alpha_1,\dots,
\alpha_n)\bigr) = f(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n).$$

Setting $\mathbf{a} = (\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)$ and $\mathbf{a}_\sigma = (\alph
a_{\sigma(1)},\dots,\alpha_{\sigma(n)})$, this reads:

$$f(\mathbf{a}_\sigma) = f(\mathbf{a}) \quad \text{for all } f \in R,\; \sigma \
in G. \tag{$\star$}$$

**Step 2: Extending to $\bar{K}$-invariants.** Let $\bar{K}$ be an algebraic clo
sure of $K$ (with $L \subseteq \bar{K}$). We claim $\bar{K}[X_1,\dots,X_n]^H = \
bar{K} \otimes_K K[X_1,\dots,X_n]^H$.

Indeed, any $g \in \bar{K}[X]^H$ can be written as $g = \sum_i c_i m_i$ (monomia
l expansion, $c_i \in \bar{K}$). Grouping monomials into $H$-orbits, $H$-invaria
nce forces all coefficients within each orbit to be equal, so $g = \sum_{\text{o
rbits } O} c_O \bigl(\sum_{m \in O} m\bigr)$, where each $\sum_{m \in O} m \in K
[X]^H$. Thus $g \in \bar{K} \otimes_K K[X]^H$.

By ($\star$) and this decomposition, $g(\mathbf{a}_\sigma) = g(\mathbf{a})$ for
all $g \in \bar{K}[X]^H$ and $\sigma \in G$.

**Step 3: Orbit separation over $\bar{K}$.** We prove: for any finite group $H$
acting on $\bar{K}[X_1,\dots,X_n]$ by permuting variables, and any $P, Q \in \ba
r{K}^n$,

$$f(P) = f(Q)\ \forall\, f \in \bar{K}[X]^H \implies Q \in H \cdot P. \tag{$\dag
ger$}$$

*Proof of ($\dagger$):* Suppose $Q \notin H \cdot P$. Then the maximal ideals $\
mathfrak{m}_Q = (X_i - Q_i)$ and $\sigma(\mathfrak{m}_P) = (X_i - P_{\sigma(i)})
$ are distinct for all $\sigma \in H$. By **prime avoidance**, there exists $g \
in \mathfrak{m}_Q$ with $g \notin \sigma(\mathfrak{m}_P)$ for every $\sigma \in
H$.

Define the **norm** $N(g) = \prod_{\sigma \in H} \sigma(g) \in \bar{K}[X]^H$.

- Since $g \in \mathfrak{m}_Q$ and $\mathfrak{m}_Q$ is prime, $N(g) \in \mathfra
k{m}_Q$, so $N(g)(Q) = 0$.
- For each $\sigma$, $\sigma(g) \in \mathfrak{m}_P \iff g \in \sigma^{-1}(\mathf
rak{m}_P)$, which fails by construction. Since $\mathfrak{m}_P$ is prime, $N(g)
\notin \mathfrak{m}_P$, so $N(g)(P) \neq 0$.

Thus $N(g)(P) \neq N(g)(Q)$, contradicting $f(P) = f(Q)$ for all $f \in \bar{K}[
X]^H$. ∎

**Step 4: Conclusion.** Applying ($\dagger$) with $P = \mathbf{a}$ and $Q = \mat
hbf{a}_\sigma$ (using Step 2), we get $\mathbf{a}_\sigma \in H \cdot \mathbf{a}$
 for every $\sigma \in G$. So there exists $\tau \in H$ with $\alpha_{\sigma(i)}
 = \alpha_{\tau(i)}$ for all $i$. Since the $\alpha_i$ are distinct, $\sigma = \
tau \in H$.

Since $\sigma \in G$ was arbitrary, $G \subseteq H$. $\blacksquare$
