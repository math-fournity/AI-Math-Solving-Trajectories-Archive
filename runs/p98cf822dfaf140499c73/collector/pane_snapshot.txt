# Solution

## Setting up the problem

We have the inviscid Burgers' equation $u_t + (u^2/2)_x = 0$ with flux $f(u) = u
^2/2$, and the weak solution

$$u(x,t) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{x-2}{t+2} & x > \xi(t) \\[6pt] 0 & x < \xi(t) \e
nd{cases}$$

with the shock curve $x = \xi(t)$ satisfying $\xi(0) = 0$.

## Step 1: Verify the smooth part is a classical solution

For $u = (x-2)/(t+2)$ in the region $x > \xi(t)$:

$$u_t = -\frac{x-2}{(t+2)^2} = -\frac{u}{t+2}, \qquad u_x = \frac{1}{t+2}.$$

Then $u_t + u\,u_x = -u/(t+2) + u/(t+2) = 0$. ✓

In the region $x < \xi(t)$, $u \equiv 0$ trivially satisfies the PDE. ✓

## Step 2: Apply the Rankine–Hugoniot condition

Across the shock, the left and right states are

$$u_L = 0, \qquad u_R = \frac{\xi(t) - 2}{t+2}.$$

The Rankine–Hugoniot condition gives the shock speed:

$$\xi'(t) = \frac{f(u_R) - f(u_L)}{u_R - u_L} = \frac{u_R^2/2}{u_R} = \frac{u_R}
{2} = \frac{\xi(t) - 2}{2(t+2)}.$$

## Step 3: Solve the ODE for the shock curve

We have the linear ODE

$$\xi'(t) - \frac{1}{2(t+2)}\,\xi(t) = -\frac{1}{t+2}, \qquad \xi(0) = 0.$$

**Integrating factor:** $\mu(t) = \exp\!\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2}\ln(t+2)\Bigr) = (t+2
)^{-1/2}$.

Multiplying through:

$$\frac{d}{dt}\Bigl[\xi(t)\,(t+2)^{-1/2}\Bigr] = -(t+2)^{-3/2}.$$

Integrating:

$$\xi(t)\,(t+2)^{-1/2} = 2\,(t+2)^{-1/2} + C,$$

so $\xi(t) = 2 + C\sqrt{t+2}$. Applying $\xi(0) = 0$:

$$0 = 2 + C\sqrt{2} \implies C = -\sqrt{2}.$$

$$\boxed{\xi(t) = 2 - \sqrt{2(t+2)}.}$$

**Verification:** $\xi(0) = 2 - \sqrt{4} = 0$ ✓, and $\xi'(t) = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt
{2(t+2)}}$, while $\dfrac{\xi(t)-2}{2(t+2)} = \dfrac{-\sqrt{2(t+2)}}{2(t+2)} = -
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2(t+2)}}$ ✓.

## Step 4: Verify the Lax entropy condition

For a genuine shock we need $f'(u_L) > \xi'(t) > f'(u_R)$, i.e., $u_L > \xi'(t)
> u_R$.

- $u_L = 0$.
- $u_R = \dfrac{\xi(t)-2}{t+2} = -\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{t+2}} < 0$ for $t > 0$.
- $\xi'(t) = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2(t+2)}} < 0$.

**Left inequality** $u_L > \xi'(t)$: $0 > -1/\sqrt{2(t+2)}$ ✓.

**Right inequality** $\xi'(t) > u_R$: $-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{t+2}} > -\dfrac{
\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{t+2}}$, equivalently $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} < \sqrt{2}$, i.e., $1
< 2$ ✓.

The Lax entropy condition holds for all $t > 0$, confirming this is a valid entr
opy-satisfying shock.

## Step 5: Characteristics in the $xt$-plane

**Right region** ($x > \xi(t)$): Since $u = (x-2)/(t+2)$ is constant along chara
cteristics, and $dx/dt = u$, each characteristic is a straight line of slope $u
= c$ (constant) passing through the fixed point $(x,t) = (2,-2)$:

$$x = 2 + c\,(t+2), \qquad c \in \mathbb{R}.$$

These form a **fan of straight lines radiating from the point $(2, -2)$** (which
 lies below the $t = 0$ axis). Only the portion with $t > 0$ and $x > \xi(t)$ is
 in the physical domain.

**Left region** ($x < \xi(t)$): $u = 0$, so $dx/dt = 0$; characteristics are **v
ertical lines** $x = \text{const}$.

**Shock curve**: $x = 2 - \sqrt{2(t+2)}$, starting at the origin $(0,0)$, decrea
sing (moving leftward) and concave up ($\xi''(t) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}(t+2)^{-3/
2} > 0$), with $\xi(t) \to -\infty$ as $t \to \infty$.

The characteristics from the right (fan lines, slope $c$) impinge on the shock f
rom the right; the vertical characteristics ($u = 0$) impinge from the left. Bot
h families converge into the shock, consistent with the Lax condition.

## Conclusion

The shock curve is

$$\boxed{\xi(t) = 2 - \sqrt{2(t+2)}, \qquad t \geq 0,}$$

starting at the origin $\xi(0) = 0$, satisfying the Rankine–Hugoniot condition a
nd the Lax entropy condition. To the right of the shock, characteristics are str
aight lines $x = 2 + c(t+2)$ forming a fan from the point $(2,-2)$; to the left,
 characteristics are vertical lines $x = \text{const}$.
