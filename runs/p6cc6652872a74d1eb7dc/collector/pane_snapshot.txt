## Proof

We prove that for a weak solution $u \in H^1(B(x_0,R))$ of $\Delta u = 0$, each
weak derivative $u_{x_i}$ satisfies the mean value property over balls, hence
$$u_{x_i}(x_0) = \frac{1}{|B(x_0,R)|}\int_{B(x_0,R)} u_{x_i}(y)\,dy.$$

The argument proceeds in three steps.

### Step 1. Weak derivatives of harmonic functions are harmonic.

Since $u \in H^1(B(x_0,R))$ is a weak solution of $\Delta u = 0$, by definition
$u$ satisfies
$$\int_{B(x_0,R)} \nabla u \cdot \nabla \varphi\,dx = 0 \quad \text{for all } \v
arphi \in H_0^1(B(x_0,R)).$$

Fix $i \in \{1,\dots,n\}$. We claim $u_{x_i}$ is also a weak solution of $\Delta
 v = 0$ in $B(x_0,R)$. Take any $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(B(x_0,R))$. Using $\varp
hi_{x_i}$ as a test function in the weak formulation (valid since $\varphi_{x_i}
 \in C_c^\infty \subset H_0^1$):
$$0 = \int_{B(x_0,R)} \nabla u \cdot \nabla \varphi_{x_i}\,dx = \int_{B(x_0,R)}
\sum_{j=1}^n u_{x_j}\, \varphi_{x_i x_j}\,dx.$$

Integrating by parts in the $x_j$-variable (or equivalently, using the symmetry
of weak derivatives and the fact that $\varphi$ is compactly supported and smoot
h), we move the $x_j$-derivative from $\varphi$ onto $u_{x_i}$:
$$\int_{B(x_0,R)} \sum_{j=1}^n u_{x_j}\, \varphi_{x_i x_j}\,dx = -\int_{B(x_0,R)
} \sum_{j=1}^n (u_{x_j})_{x_i}\, \varphi_{x_j}\,dx = -\int_{B(x_0,R)} \sum_{j=1}
^n (u_{x_i})_{x_j}\, \varphi_{x_j}\,dx,$$
where we used the symmetry of second weak derivatives $u_{x_j x_i} = u_{x_i x_j}
$ (valid for $H^1$ weak solutions of elliptic equations; in fact $u \in H^1_{\te
xt{loc}}$ and $u_{x_i} \in H^1_{\text{loc}}$ by difference-quotient / Caccioppol
i estimates). Thus
$$\int_{B(x_0,R)} \nabla u_{x_i} \cdot \nabla \varphi\,dx = 0 \quad \text{for al
l } \varphi \in C_c^\infty(B(x_0,R)),$$
and by density for all $\varphi \in H_0^1(B(x_0,R))$. Hence $u_{x_i}$ is a weak
solution of $\Delta v = 0$ in $B(x_0,R)$.

### Step 2. Elliptic regularity: $u_{x_i}$ is smooth (hence classically harmonic

C^\infty(B(x_0,R))$ and $\Delta u_{x_i} = 0$ classically in $B(x_0,R)$.

### Step 3. The mean value property.

A classical fact: if $v$ is harmonic (classically $\Delta v = 0$) in a domain co
ntaining the closed ball $\overline{B(x_0,R)}$, then $v$ satisfies the mean valu
e property:
$$v(x_0) = \frac{1}{|B(x_0,R)|}\int_{B(x_0,R)} v(y)\,dy.$$

This follows from Green's identity / the representation formula, or equivalently

theorem on the vector field $F(y) = \nabla v(y)$ restricted appropriately, one o

$$\frac{d}{dr}\left[\frac{1}{|B(x_0,r)|}\int_{B(x_0,r)} v(y)\,dy\right] = \frac{

so the spherical mean is constant in $r$, and taking $r \to 0$ gives the value $
v(x_0)$.
