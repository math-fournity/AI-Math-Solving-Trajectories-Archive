**Yes.** We exhibit an explicit example: the compact Hausdorff topological group
 $G = (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\mathbb{R}}$ (an uncountable product of copies o
f the two-element group) is not a sequential space.

---

### Setup

Let $I$ be an uncountable set (e.g. $I = \mathbb{R}$) and let $G = \{0,1\}^{I}$
equipped with the product topology, where each factor $\{0,1\}$ carries the disc
rete topology and the group structure of $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.

- **$G$ is a topological group.** It is the product of the topological groups $\
mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$; coordinatewise addition is continuous, inversion (the id
entity here) is continuous.
- **$G$ is compact Hausdorff** (Tychonoff's theorem; product of compact Hausdorf
f spaces). Hence $G$ is locally compact Hausdorff.

It remains to show $G$ is **not sequential**.

### A sequentially closed, non-closed subset

For $x \in \{0,1\}^{I}$, let $\operatorname{supp}(x) = \{i \in I : x_i = 1\}$. D
efine

$$A = \{x \in \{0,1\}^{I} : \operatorname{supp}(x) \text{ is countable}\}.$$

**$A$ is not closed.** Let $\mathbf{1} = (1)_{i\in I}$ be the all-ones point. Th
en $\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{1}) = I$ is uncountable, so $\mathbf{1}\notin A$
. Yet $\mathbf{1}\in\overline{A}$: any basic open neighborhood of $\mathbf{1}$ f
ixes a *finite* set $F\subseteq I$ of coordinates to the value $1$. The point $x
$ with $x_i=1$ for $i\in F$ and $x_i=0$ otherwise lies in $A$ (finite, hence cou
ntable support) and lies in that neighborhood. So $\overline{A}\setminus A\neq\v
arnothing$.

**$A$ is sequentially closed.** Let $(x^{(n)})_{n\geq 1}$ be a sequence in $A$ c
onverging to some $y\in\{0,1\\}^{I}$ (convergence in the product topology). For
each coordinate $i$, the coordinate sequence $x^{(n)}_i$ converges to $y_i$ in t
he discrete space $\{0,1\}$, hence is eventually constant equal to $y_i$.

Now if $y_i = 1$, then $x^{(n)}_i = 1$ for all large $n$, so in particular $i\in
\operatorname{supp}(x^{(n)})$ for some $n$. Therefore

$$\operatorname{supp}(y) \;\subseteq\; \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\operatorname{supp}
(x^{(n)}).$$

Each $\operatorname{supp}(x^{(n)})$ is countable (since $x^{(n)}\in A$), so the
right-hand side is a countable union of countable sets, hence countable. Thus $\
operatorname{supp}(y)$ is countable, i.e. $y\in A$.

So every convergent sequence from $A$ has its limit in $A$: $A$ is sequentially
closed.

### Conclusion

$A\subseteq G$ is sequentially closed but not closed. In a sequential space ever
y sequentially closed set is closed, so $G$ is **not sequential**.

Therefore $G = (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\mathbb{R}}$ is a locally compact Hausd
orff topological group that is not a sequential space. $\blacksquare$
