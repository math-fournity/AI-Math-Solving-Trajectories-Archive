### Key Observations

**1. The Fourier transform $\hat{f}(w) = \frac{w}{1+w^4}$ is an odd function.**

Since $\hat{f}(-w) = \frac{-w}{1+(-w)^4} = \frac{-w}{1+w^4} = -\hat{f}(w)$, the
transform is odd in $w$.

**2. An odd Fourier transform implies $f$ is an odd function.**

Using the inverse transform $f(t) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \hat{f
}(w)\,e^{iwt}\,dw$ and substituting $w \mapsto -w$:

$$f(-t) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \hat{f}(w)\,e^{-iwt}\,dw = \frac
{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \hat{f}(-w)\,e^{iwt}\,dw = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{
-\infty}^{\infty}(-\hat{f}(w))\,e^{iwt}\,dw = -f(t).$$

So $f$ is odd.

**3. $\frac{\sin t}{t}$ is an even function.**

This is immediate since $\sin$ is odd and $t$ is odd, making the ratio even.

**4. The integrand $\frac{\sin t}{t}\,f(t)$ is odd (even × odd = odd).**

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin t}{t}\,f(t)\,dt = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\le

\underbrace{\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(t)\,e^{iut}\,dt\right)}_{\hat{f}(-u)}

With $\hat{f}(w)=\frac{w}{1+w^4}$, we get $\hat{f}(-u) = \frac{-u}{1+u^4}$, whic

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin t}{t}\,f(t)\,dt = \frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\f
