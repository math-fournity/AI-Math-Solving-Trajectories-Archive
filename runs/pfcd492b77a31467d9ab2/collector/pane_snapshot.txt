### Upper Bound: $r \leq n+1$

Suppose $\beta_1, \ldots, \beta_r \in \mathcal{L}$ satisfy $(\beta_i, \beta_j) <
 0$ for all $i \neq j$.

**Lemma.** Any $r-1$ of these vectors are linearly independent.

*Proof of Lemma.* Relabel so that we consider $\beta_1, \ldots, \beta_k$ with $k
 \leq r-1$. Suppose $\sum_{i=1}^k c_i \beta_i = 0$. Partition the index set into
 $P = \{i : c_i \geq 0\}$ and $N = \{i : c_i < 0\}$.

**Case 1: Both $P$ and $N$ are nonempty.** Set $v = \sum_{i \in P} c_i \beta_i =
 \sum_{j \in N} (-c_j)\beta_j$. Then
$$
(v,v) = \sum_{\substack{i \in P \\ j \in N}} c_i(-c_j)(\beta_i, \beta_j).
$$
Each summand has $c_i \geq 0$, $-c_j > 0$, and $(\beta_i,\beta_j) < 0$, so every
 term is $\leq 0$, and at least one is strictly $<0$ (whenever $c_i > 0$). Thus
$(v,v) < 0$, which is impossible. (If all $c_i = 0$ for $i \in P$, then $v=0$ an
d $\sum_{j\in N}(-c_j)\beta_j = 0$; taking inner product with any $\beta_m$, $m
\in P$, gives $\sum_{j\in N}(-c_j)(\beta_j,\beta_m) = 0$, a sum of strictly nega
tive terms — contradiction since $N \neq \emptyset$.) So this case cannot occur
with a nontrivial relation.

**Case 2: Only $P$ is nonempty** (all $c_i \geq 0$, not all zero). Since $k \leq
 r-1$, there exists $\beta_m$ with $m \notin \{1,\ldots,k\}$. Taking the inner p
roduct of $\sum c_i \beta_i = 0$ with $\beta_m$:
$$
0 = \sum_{i=1}^k c_i (\beta_i, \beta_m).
$$
Each term has $c_i \geq 0$ and $(\beta_i, \beta_m) < 0$, so the sum is $\leq 0$,
 and strictly $<0$ if any $c_i > 0$. Hence all $c_i = 0$.

**Case 3: Only $N$ is nonempty** — symmetric to Case 2.

In all cases, $c_1 = \cdots = c_k = 0$. $\square$

Since any $r-1$ of the vectors are linearly independent and $\mathcal{L}$ is $n$
-dimensional, we need $r - 1 \leq n$, i.e.,
$$
r \leq n+1.
