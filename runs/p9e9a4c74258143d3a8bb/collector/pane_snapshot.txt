## Theorem (`HadwigerNelsonAtMostSeven`): χ(ℝ²) ≤ 7

**Proof.** We construct an explicit 7-coloring of the plane such that no two poi
nts at Euclidean distance 1 receive the same color.

### Step 1 — Hexagonal tiling

Tile the plane with regular hexagons of side length $s$ (a constant chosen below
). Using axial coordinates, the center of hexagon $(i,j)\in\mathbb Z^2$ is $i\ma
thbf a + j\mathbf b$, where $|\mathbf a|=|\mathbf b|=s\sqrt3$ and $\mathbf a\cdo
t\mathbf b = \tfrac{3s^2}{2}$ (angle $60^\circ$). Adjacent hexagons have centers
 at distance $s\sqrt3$. We make each hexagon half-open by a consistent boundary
assignment so the tiling is a genuine partition; this does not affect the distan
ce argument since all inequalities below are strict.

### Step 2 — The 7-coloring

Assign to hexagon $(i,j)$ the color
$$c(i,j) = (i + 3j)\bmod 7 \in \{0,1,\dots,6\},$$
and color every point of that hexagon with color $c(i,j)$.

### Step 3 — Same-colored hexagons are far apart

Two distinct hexagons share a color iff $(i-i')+3(j-j')\equiv 0\pmod 7$. Put $(d
_1,d_2)=(i-i',\,j-j')$. The squared center-to-center distance is
$$|d_1\mathbf a+d_2\mathbf b|^2 = 3s^2\bigl(d_1^2+d_1d_2+d_2^2\bigr)=3s^2\,Q(d_1
,d_2).$$

We minimize $Q(d_1,d_2)=d_1^2+d_1d_2+d_2^2$ over all nonzero $(d_1,d_2)$ with $d
_1+3d_2\equiv0\pmod7$. Checking $d_2\in\{0,\pm1,\pm2,\pm3\}$:

| $d_2$ | $d_1\bmod 7$ | smallest $(d_1,d_2)$ | $Q$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $0$ | $0$ | $(7,0)$ | $49$ |
| $\pm1$ | $\mp3$ | $(\pm3,\mp1)$ | $7$ |
| $\pm2$ | $\mp6\equiv\pm1$ | $(\pm1,\pm2)$ | $7$ |
| $\pm3$ | $\mp9\equiv\mp2$ | $(\pm2,\mp3)$ | $7$ |

The minimum is $Q=7$, attained at $(\pm3,\mp1),(\pm1,\pm2),(\pm2,\mp3)$. Hence t
he **minimum center distance** between two distinct same-colored hexagons is
$$d_{\min}=s\sqrt3\cdot\sqrt7 = s\sqrt{21}.$$

### Step 4 — Bounding the gap

A regular hexagon of side $s$ has circumradius $s$, so every point of a hexagon
lies within distance $s$ of its center. By the triangle inequality, for any two
hexagons with centers at distance $d$,
$$\operatorname{gap} \;\ge\; d - 2s.$$
Therefore every pair of distinct same-colored hexagons has gap at least
$$s\sqrt{21}-2s = s(\sqrt{21}-2).$$

### Step 5 — Choosing $s$

We require:

* **(A)** Diameter $2s<1$, so two points at distance $1$ cannot lie in the *same
* hexagon: $\;s<\tfrac12$.
* **(B)** Gap $>1$ between same-colored hexagons: $\;s(\sqrt{21}-2)>1\;\Longleft
rightarrow\;s>\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{21}-2}=\dfrac{\sqrt{21}+2}{17}\approx 0.3872$.

Since $0.3872<0.5$, the interval $\bigl(\tfrac{\sqrt{21}+2}{17},\,\tfrac12\bigr)
$ is nonempty. Choose, e.g., $\boxed{s=0.45}$.

### Step 6 — Verification

With $s=0.45$:

* **Same hexagon:** diameter $=2s=0.9<1$, so no two points at distance $1$ share
 a hexagon. ✓
* **Different, same-colored hexagons:** gap $\ge s(\sqrt{21}-2)\approx 1.162>1$,
 so every cross-pair of points has distance $>1\neq 1$. ✓
* **Different, differently-colored hexagons:** different colors by construction.
 ✓

Thus no two points at Euclidean distance $1$ receive the same color. This exhibi
ts a proper 7-coloring of the unit-distance graph of the plane, proving
$$\chi(\mathbb R^2)\le 7.\qquad\blacksquare$$
