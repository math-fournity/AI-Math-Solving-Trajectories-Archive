## Proof

Let the angles of triangle $ABC$ be $\angle A = \alpha$, $\angle B = \beta$, $\a
ngle C = \gamma$, with $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = \pi$.

**Condition 1** gives $\gamma = 2\beta$, so $\alpha + 3\beta = \pi$, i.e., $\alp
ha = \pi - 3\beta$.

**Setting up the circumcircle.** Place the circumcircle as the unit circle. By t
he inscribed angle theorem, the arcs are:
- Arc $BC$ not containing $A$: $2\alpha$
- Arc $CA$ not containing $B$: $2\beta$
- Arc $AB$ not containing $C$: $2\gamma = 4\beta$

Place $B$ at angle $0$. Going counterclockwise: $C$ at angle $2\alpha$, $A$ at a
ngle $2\alpha + 2\beta$.

**Locating $D$ and $E$.** The internal angle bisector at $A$ meets the circumcir
cle at $D$, the midpoint of arc $BC$ not containing $A$, so $D$ is at angle $\al
pha$. The external angle bisector at $A$ meets the circumcircle at $E$, the midp
oint of arc $BC$ containing $A$, which is diametrically opposite $D$, at angle $
\alpha + \pi$.

Since $D$ and $E$ are antipodal, line $DE$ passes through the center (origin) wi
th direction $(\cos\alpha, \sin\alpha)$.

**Locating $F$.** Condition 2 states $BF = 2\cdot AF$, so $F$ divides segment $A
B$ internally in ratio $AF:FB = 1:2$, giving:
$$F = \tfrac{2}{3}A + \tfrac{1}{3}B = \left(\tfrac{2}{3}\cos(2\alpha+2\beta)+\tf
rac{1}{3},\;\tfrac{2}{3}\sin(2\alpha+2\beta)\right)$$

**Collinearity condition.** Since $F$ lies on line $DE$ (through the origin, dir
ection $(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)$):
$$F_x\sin\alpha - F_y\cos\alpha = 0$$

Substituting and multiplying by 3:
$$\bigl[2\cos(2\alpha+2\beta)+1\bigr]\sin\alpha - 2\sin(2\alpha+2\beta)\cos\alph
a = 0$$

The first two terms combine via the sine-difference identity:
$$2\bigl[\sin\alpha\cos(2\alpha+2\beta)-\cos\alpha\sin(2\alpha+2\beta)\bigr] = 2
\sin\bigl(\alpha-(2\alpha+2\beta)\bigr) = -2\sin(\alpha+2\beta)$$

So the equation becomes:
$$\sin\alpha = 2\sin(\alpha+2\beta)$$

**Substituting $\alpha = \pi - 3\beta$.** Then $\alpha + 2\beta = \pi - \beta$,
so $\sin(\alpha+2\beta)=\sin\beta$, and $\sin\alpha = \sin(\pi-3\beta)=\sin 3\be
ta$. The equation becomes:
$$\sin 3\beta = 2\sin\beta$$

Using $\sin 3\beta = 3\sin\beta - 4\sin^3\beta$:
$$3\sin\beta - 4\sin^3\beta = 2\sin\beta \implies \sin\beta(1-4\sin^2\beta)=0$$

Since $\beta > 0$, we get $\sin^2\beta = \tfrac{1}{4}$, so $\sin\beta = \tfrac{1
}{2}$, giving $\beta = \tfrac{\pi}{6}$ (the other solution $\beta=\tfrac{5\pi}{6
}$ yields $\alpha<0$, impossible).

Therefore:
$$\alpha = \pi - 3\cdot\frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{\pi}{2}$$

**Verification.** With $\alpha=\tfrac{\pi}{2}$, $\beta=\tfrac{\pi}{6}$, $\gamma=
\tfrac{\pi}{3}$: the angles sum to $\pi$ and $\gamma=2\beta$. ✓

Coordinates: $A=(-\tfrac{1}{2},-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2})$, $B=(1,0)$, $D=(0,1)$, $E=(0
,-1)$, so line $DE$ is the $y$-axis. Then $F=\tfrac{2}{3}A+\tfrac{1}{3}B=(0,-\tf
rac{\sqrt3}{3})$, which lies on the $y$-axis. ✓

Distances: $AF=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$, $BF=\tfrac{2}{\sqrt3}$, so $BF=2\cdot AF$. ✓

**The internal angle at Beacon $A$ is $\boxed{\dfrac{\pi}{2}}$ (90°).**
