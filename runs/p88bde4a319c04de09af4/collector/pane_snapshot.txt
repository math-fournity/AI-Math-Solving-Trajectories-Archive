## Proof

**Key Lemma.** A positive integer $N$ with $10^6 \le N < 10^7$ is the last seven
 decimal digits of a perfect seventh power (i.e., $N \equiv m^7 \pmod{10^7}$ for
 some integer $m$) whenever $N$ is odd and $\gcd(N,5)=1$.

*Proof of Lemma.* Since $10^7 = 2^7 \cdot 5^7$ with $\gcd(2^7,5^7)=1$, by CRT th
e map $x\mapsto x^7$ on $\mathbb{Z}/10^7\mathbb{Z}$ decomposes into the maps $x\
mapsto x^7$ on $\mathbb{Z}/2^7\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Z}/5^7\mathbb{Z}$. Thus $
N$ is a seventh power mod $10^7$ iff it is a seventh power mod $2^7$ and mod $5^
7$.

- **Mod $2^7=128$:** The group $(\mathbb{Z}/128\mathbb{Z})^*$ of odd residues ha
s order $\phi(128)=64=2^6$. Since $\gcd(7,64)=1$, the map $x\mapsto x^7$ is an a
utomorphism of this group, so **every odd residue mod 128 is a seventh power**.
(Even residues $x=2^k u$ give $x^7=2^{7k}u^7\equiv 0\pmod{128}$ for $k\ge1$, so
the only even seventh power mod 128 is 0.)

- **Mod $5^7=78125$:** The group $(\mathbb{Z}/78125\mathbb{Z})^*$ has order $\ph
i(78125)=62500=4\cdot 5^6$. Since $\gcd(7,62500)=1$ (as 7 is coprime to both 4 a
nd $5^6$), the map $x\mapsto x^7$ is an automorphism, so **every unit mod 78125
is a seventh power**.

Therefore, if $N$ is odd (seventh power mod 128) and $\gcd(N,5)=1$ (seventh powe
r mod 78125), then $N$ is a seventh power mod $10^7$. $\square$

**Alice's winning strategy.** Partition the digits $\{1,\dots,9\}$ into:
$$G = \{1,3,7,9\} \quad\text{(good: odd and not divisible by 5)}, \qquad B = \{2
,4,5,6,8\} \quad\text{(bad)}.$$

Alice moves at positions 1, 3, 5, 7; Bob at positions 2, 4, 6. Alice's strategy:

- **Moves 1, 3, 5:** Always pick a digit from $B=\{2,4,5,6,8\}$.
- **Move 7:** Pick any remaining digit from $G=\{1,3,7,9\}$.

**Feasibility of picking from $B$ in moves 1, 3, 5.** The set $B$ has 5 elements
. In each of Alice's first three moves, we verify a bad digit is available:

- *Move 1:* All 5 bad digits available. ✓
- *Move 3:* Alice used 1 bad digit; Bob used 1 digit (at most 1 from $B$). At le
ast $5-1-1=3$ bad digits remain. ✓
- *Move 5:* Alice used 2 bad digits; Bob used 2 digits (at most 2 from $B$). At
least $5-2-2=1$ bad digit remains. ✓

**Availability of a good digit at move 7.** After 6 moves, Alice has picked 3 ba
d digits and Bob has picked 3 digits (from whatever remains). The good set $G$ h
as 4 elements. Only Bob's picks can remove good digits (Alice never picks from $
G$ in moves 1–5), and Bob has at most 3 picks. So at most 3 of the 4 good digits
 are consumed, leaving **at least one** good digit for Alice's final move. ✓

**Conclusion.** By following this strategy, Alice guarantees $A_7 \in \{1,3,7,9\
}$, so the final number $N=\overline{A_1B_2A_3B_4A_5B_6A_7}$ is odd and not divi
sible by 5. By the Lemma, $N$ is the last seven decimal digits of a perfect seve
nth power. This holds regardless of Bob's play, so **Alice has a winning strateg
y**.
