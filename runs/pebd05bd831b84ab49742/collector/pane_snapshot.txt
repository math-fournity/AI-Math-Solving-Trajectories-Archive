**Claim.** The number of surjective (unital) ring homomorphisms $\mathbb{Z}[i] \
to \mathbb{F}_{11^2}$ is $\boxed{2}$.

### Proof

**Step 1: Reduction to choosing an image of $i$.**

Since $\mathbb{Z}[i] \cong \mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^2+1)$, a unital ring homomorphism $\
varphi: \mathbb{Z}[i] \to \mathbb{F}_{11^2}$ is completely determined by $a := \
varphi(i) \in \mathbb{F}_{11^2}$, subject to the constraint
$$a^2 = \varphi(i)^2 = \varphi(i^2) = \varphi(-1) = -1.$$
Conversely, every $a \in \mathbb{F}_{11^2}$ with $a^2 = -1$ defines a unital rin

1^2}$ for which the resulting map is surjective.

**Step 2: The characteristic is forced to be $11$.**

For any unital homomorphism $\varphi: \mathbb{Z}[i] \to \mathbb{F}_{11^2}$, the
restriction to $\mathbb{Z}$ sends $n \mapsto n\cdot 1$. Since $\mathbb{F}_{11^2}
$ has characteristic $11$, the kernel of $\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{F}_{11^2}$ is $
(11)$. Hence $11 \in \ker\varphi$, so $\ker\varphi$ is a prime ideal of $\mathbb
{Z}[i]$ lying over $(11) \subseteq \mathbb{Z}$.

**Step 3: $(11)$ is inert in $\mathbb{Z}[i]$, so $\ker\varphi = (11)$.**

The splitting of $(11)$ in $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ is governed by $x^2+1 \pmod{11}$. Sin
ce $11 \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$, $-1$ is **not** a quadratic residue mod $11$, so $x^2
+1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_{11}$. Therefore $(11)$ is inert: it remains

11)$ is $(11)$ itself, so
$$\ker\varphi = (11), \qquad \mathbb{Z}[i]/(11) \cong \mathbb{F}_{11}[x]/(x^2+1)
 \cong \mathbb{F}_{11^2}.$$

**Step 4: Every root gives a surjection.**

Each root $a$ of $x^2+1$ in $\mathbb{F}_{11^2}$ yields a homomorphism
$$\mathbb{Z}[i]/(11) \cong \mathbb{F}_{11}[x]/(x^2+1) \longrightarrow \mathbb{F}
_{11^2}, \qquad \bar{x} \mapsto a.$$
The domain is the field $\mathbb{F}_{11^2}$ (since $x^2+1$ is irreducible), and
the map is nonzero (it sends $1\mapsto 1$), hence injective. Both domain and cod
omain have exactly $11^2$ elements, so injectivity implies surjectivity. Thus **
every** root of $x^2+1$ in $\mathbb{F}_{11^2}$ produces a surjective homomorphis
m, and there are no other surjective homomorphisms (the kernel is forced to be $
(11)$).

**Step 5: Count the roots.**

The multiplicative group $\mathbb{F}_{11^2}^{\times}$ is cyclic of order $11^2 -
 1 = 120$. The equation $a^2 = -1$ has solutions iff $-1$ lies in the subgroup o

exactly $2$ elements of order $4$, equivalently exactly $2$ solutions to $a^2 =
-1$. Concretely, since $x^2+1$ is irreducible of degree $2$ over $\mathbb{F}_{11
}$, it splits completely in $\mathbb{F}_{11^2}$ into two distinct linear factors
, giving exactly $2$ roots.
