**Problem.** Let $C_1, C_2$ be circles with centers $O_1, O_2$, $|O_1O_2|=10$, r
adii $r_1=1, r_2=3$. Find the locus of all $M$ that are the midpoint of some seg
ment $XY$ with $X\in C_1, Y\in C_2$.

**Proof.**

Let $C=\tfrac{O_1+O_2}{2}$ be the midpoint of the two centers. Write points on t
he circles as
$$X=O_1+u,\quad |u|=r_1=1, \qquad Y=O_2+v,\quad |v|=r_2=3.$$
Then the midpoint is
$$M=\frac{X+Y}{2}=\frac{O_1+O_2}{2}+\frac{u+v}{2}=C+w,\qquad w:=\frac{u+v}{2}.$$
So the locus is $C+\mathcal{S}$, where $\mathcal{S}=\{\tfrac{u+v}{2}:|u|=1,\,|v|
=3\}$.

**Claim.** $\mathcal{S}$ is the closed annulus centered at the origin with inner
 radius $\tfrac{|r_1-r_2|}{2}=1$ and outer radius $\tfrac{r_1+r_2}{2}=2$.

*Proof of claim.* For any $u,v$ with $|u|=1,|v|=3$, the triangle inequality give
s
$$|r_1-r_2|\le|u+v|\le r_1+r_2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2\le|u+v|\le 4 \quad\Lo
ngrightarrow\quad 1\le|w|\le 2.$$
So $\mathcal{S}$ lies in the annulus $\{1\le|w|\le 2\}$.

Conversely, fix any $\rho\in[2,4]$ and any direction; we need $u,v$ with $|u|=1,
|v|=3$ and $|u+v|=\rho$ pointing in that direction. Let $\theta$ be the angle be
tween $u$ and $v$. By the law of cosines,
$$|u+v|^2 = 1^2+3^2+2\cdot1\cdot3\cos\theta = 10+6\cos\theta.$$
As $\theta$ ranges over $[0,\pi]$, $\cos\theta$ ranges over $[-1,1]$, so $|u+v|^
2$ ranges continuously over $[4,16]$, i.e. $|u+v|$ ranges over $[2,4]$. Hence ev
ery value $\rho\in[2,4]$ is attained, giving $|w|=\rho/2\in[1,2]$. Rotating the
pair $(u,v)$ together produces every direction. Thus every point of the annulus
$\{1\le|w|\le 2\}$ is attained.

Therefore $\mathcal{S}=\{w:1\le|w|\le 2\}$, and the locus of $M$ is

$$\boxed{\,\{M:1\le |M-C|\le 2\}\,}$$

the **closed annulus centered at the midpoint $C$ of the segment joining the two
 centers, with inner radius $1$ and outer radius $2$**.

*Sanity check on the boundary.* When $u,v$ point the same direction, $|u+v|=4$,
so $|w|=2$ (outer boundary). When they point oppositely, $|u+v|=2$, so $|w|=1$ (
inner boundary). Both extremes are achieved, confirming the boundary is included
. $\blacksquare$
