## Key Definitions and Lemmas

A sensor $S$ is **monitored** if either (a) some D-sensor is consecutive to $S$
on a diagonal (no sensor between), or (b) some O-sensor is consecutive to $S$ on
 a row/column. So $S$ is **isolated** iff:
- Every sensor consecutive to $S$ on either diagonal is **not** a D-sensor (i.e.
, is an O-sensor or nonexistent).
- Every sensor consecutive to $S$ on its row or column is **not** an O-sensor (i
.e., is a D-sensor or nonexistent).

**Lemma 1.** If two isolated sensors occupy orthogonally adjacent cells, both mu
st be D-sensors. If two isolated sensors occupy diagonally adjacent cells, both
must be O-sensors.

*Proof.* Orthogonally adjacent cells share a row or column with no cell (hence n
o sensor) between them, so they are consecutive on that line. If $S,T$ are both
isolated and consecutive on a row/column, then $T$ (consecutive to $S$) must not
 be an O-sensor, so $T$ is D; symmetrically $S$ is D. The diagonal case is analo
gous: consecutive on a diagonal forces both to be O. $\square$

## Upper Bound: At most 2 isolated sensors per 2×2 block

**Lemma 2.** In any 2×2 block of cells, at most 2 sensors can be isolated.

*Proof.* Label the block:
$$\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$$
The diagonal pairs are $\{a,d\}$ and $\{b,c\}$; the orthogonal pairs are $\{a,b\
},\{a,c\},\{b,d\},\{c,d\}$.

Suppose for contradiction that 3 of the 4 cells hold isolated sensors. Any 3-cel
l subset of a 2×2 block contains at least one orthogonal-adjacent pair and at le
ast one diagonal-adjacent pair that **share a common cell**. For instance, if th
e isolated cells are $\{a,b,c\}$: the pair $\{a,b\}$ is orthogonal (both D by Le
mma 1), the pair $\{b,c\}$ is diagonal (both O by Lemma 1), so $b$ must be both
D and O — contradiction. The other three subsets $\{a,b,d\}$, $\{a,c,d\}$, $\{b,
c,d\}$ are checked identically: each contains an orthogonal pair and a diagonal
pair sharing a cell, forcing that shared cell to be both D and O. $\square$

**Theorem (Upper Bound).** At most 50 isolated sensors exist.

*Proof.* Partition the $10\times 10$ grid into 25 disjoint 2×2 blocks. By Lemma
