Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether there exists an uncountable
 subset $X \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ such that $\mathbb{R} \neq \langle X \rangle$ (
where $\langle X \rangle$ denotes the subfield of $\mathbb{R}$ generated by $X$)
.

**Answer: Yes, such an $X$ exists.**

## Proof

**Key idea:** Take $X$ to be a transcendence basis of $\mathbb{R}$ over $\mathbb
{Q}$. This is uncountable, yet $\langle X \rangle = \mathbb{Q}(X)$ is a *purely
transcendental* extension of $\mathbb{Q}$, which cannot contain algebraic irrati
onals like $\sqrt{2}$.

---

**Step 1: A transcendence basis $T$ of $\mathbb{R}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is uncount
able.**

Let $T$ be a transcendence basis of $\mathbb{R}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ (a maximal al
gebraically independent set, which exists by Zorn's lemma). Then $\mathbb{R}$ is
 algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}(T)$. Since every element of $\mathbb{R}$ is algebrai
c (hence a root of a polynomial) over $\mathbb{Q}(T)$, we have:

$$|\mathbb{R}| \leq |\mathbb{Q}(T)| \cdot \aleph_0 = |\mathbb{Q}(T)|.$$

Now $|\mathbb{Q}(T)| = \max(|\mathbb{Q}|, |T|) = |T|$ (since elements of $\mathb
b{Q}(T)$ are rational functions in finitely many elements of $T$ with rational c
oefficients, and the number of finite subsets of $T$ is $|T|$ for infinite $T$).
 Therefore:

$$\mathfrak{c} = |\mathbb{R}| \leq |T|.$$

Since $T \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, we have $|T| \leq \mathfrak{c}$. Hence $|T| = \m
athfrak{c}$, which is uncountable.

---

**Step 2: $\mathbb{Q}$ is algebraically closed in $\mathbb{Q}(T)$.**

Since $T$ is algebraically independent over $\mathbb{Q}$, the extension $\mathbb
{Q}(T)/\mathbb{Q}$ is purely transcendental. A standard result in field theory s
tates:

> **Lemma:** If $K$ is a field and $\{t_i\}_{i \in I}$ is a family of algebraica
lly independent elements over $K$, then $K$ is algebraically closed in $K(t_i :
i \in I)$. That is, every element of $K(t_i : i \in I)$ that is algebraic over $
K$ already belongs to $K$.

*Proof of Lemma (by induction on the number of variables):* For a single variabl
e, suppose $\alpha \in K(t)$ is algebraic over $K$, where $t$ is transcendental
over $K$. Write $\alpha = f(t)/g(t)$ with $f, g \in K[t]$, $\gcd(f,g)=1$. If $\a
lpha$ satisfies $p(\alpha) = 0$ for some $p \in K[x]$, then $p(f(t)/g(t)) = 0$,
giving a polynomial relation in $t$. Clearing denominators yields $g(t)^n \cdot
p(f(t)/g(t)) = 0$, which is a polynomial in $t$ over $K$. Since $t$ is transcend
ental, this polynomial must be identically zero. One can show (by degree conside
rations or by noting $K[t]$ is a UFD and $K$ is integrally closed in $K(t)$) tha
t this forces $\deg f = \deg g$, i.e., $\alpha \in K$. The multivariable case fo
llows by induction: $K(t_1, \ldots, t_n) = K(t_1)(t_2, \ldots, t_n)$, and $K$ al
gebraically closed in $K(t_1)$, then $K(t_1)$ algebraically closed in $K(t_1,\ld
ots,t_n)$, so $K$ is algebraically closed in the full extension. For infinite $T
$, any element of $\mathbb{Q}(T)$ involves only finitely many variables, reducin
g to the finite case. $\square$

---

**Step 3: $\mathbb{Q}(T) \neq \mathbb{R}$.**

The element $\sqrt{2}$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$ (it is a root of $x^2 - 2$
) and $\sqrt{2} \notin \mathbb{Q}$. By the Lemma (Step 2), since $\mathbb{Q}$ is
 algebraically closed in $\mathbb{Q}(T)$, every element of $\mathbb{Q}(T)$ that
is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$ must lie in $\mathbb{Q}$. Therefore:

$$\sqrt{2} \notin \mathbb{Q}(T).$$

But $\sqrt{2} \in \mathbb{R}$. Hence $\mathbb{Q}(T) \subsetneq \mathbb{R}$, i.e.
,

$$\mathbb{R} \neq \langle T \rangle.$$

---

**Conclusion:** Setting $X = T$ (a transcendence basis of $\mathbb{R}$ over $\ma
thbb{Q}$), we have an uncountably infinite subset $X \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ with
$|X| = \mathfrak{c}$ such that $\mathbb{R} \neq \langle X \rangle$. This argumen
t works in ZFC independently of the Continuum Hypothesis. $\blacksquare$
