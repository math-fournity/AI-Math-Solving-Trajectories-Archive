Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{Z})$
 contains torsion-free abelian subgroups that are not finitely generated.

**Answer: No.** Every torsion-free abelian subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{Z}
)$ is finitely generated (indeed, free abelian of finite rank).

---

## Proof

Let $A \leq \mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{Z})$ be a torsion-free abelian subgroup. We sh
ow $A$ is finitely generated.

### Step 1: Construct a commutative order $S$ containing $A$

Let $S$ be the $\mathbb{Z}$-subalgebra of $M_n(\mathbb{Z})$ generated by $A$ (an
d the identity). Since $A$ is abelian, $S$ is a **commutative** ring. As an addi
tive subgroup of $M_n(\mathbb{Z})$, which is free abelian of rank $n^2$, the gro
up $S$ is itself free abelian of rank $\leq n^2$. In particular, $S$ is a **fini
tely generated $\mathbb{Z}$-module**.

Every element of $A$ lies in $S \cap \mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{Z})$. Moreover, if $s
 \in S^\times$ (a unit of the ring $S$), then $s \in S \subset M_n(\mathbb{Z})$
and $s^{-1} \in S \subset M_n(\mathbb{Z})$, so $s \in \mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{Z})$
. Thus $S^\times \subset \mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{Z})$, and we have
$$A \subset S^\times.$$

### Step 2: $S$ is an order in a finite-dimensional commutative $\mathbb{Q}$-alg
ebra

Set $R = \mathbb{Q} \otimes_\mathbb{Z} S$. This is a finite-dimensional commutat
ive $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra (with $\dim_\mathbb{Q} R \leq n^2$), and $S$ is an **or
der** in $R$: a subring that is a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}$-module spannin
g $R$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

Since $R$ is commutative and Artinian, it decomposes as $R \cong \prod_i R_i$ wh
ere each $R_i$ is a local commutative $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra with residue field $K
_i$ (a number field) and nilpotent Jacobson radical $J_i$. Let $J = \bigoplus_i
J_i$ be the Jacobson radical (nilradical) of $R$, so $R/J \cong \prod_i K_i$ is
semisimple.

### Step 3: The unit group $S^\times$ is finitely generated

We use the exact sequence
$$1 \to (1 + (J \cap S)) \to S^\times \to (S/(J \cap S))^\times \to 1.$$

**The quotient $(S/(J \cap S))^\times$ is finitely generated.** The ring $S/(J \
cap S)$ is an order in the semisimple $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra $R/J \cong \prod_i K_
i$. By the generalization of **Dirichlet's unit theorem** to orders in semisimpl
e algebras, the unit group of an order in a product of number fields is finitely
 generated (it is $\mu \times \mathbb{Z}^r$ where $\mu$ is finite and $r$ is det
ermined by the ranks of the unit groups of the $K_i$).

**The kernel $1 + (J \cap S)$ is finitely generated (and torsion-free).** Set $N
 = J \cap S$, a nilpotent ideal (since $J$ is nilpotent). The group $1 + N$ is *
*abelian** (because $S$ is commutative: $(1+x)(1+y) = 1 + x + y + xy = (1+y)(1+x
)$). It is **torsion-free**: if $(1+x)^k = 1$ for $k \neq 0$ and $x \in N$ nilpo
tent, then expanding $(1+x)^k = 1 + kx + \binom{k}{2}x^2 + \cdots$, the lowest-d
egree term $kx$ forces $x = 0$ (working over $\mathbb{Z} \subset \mathbb{Q}$, ch
aracteristic zero).

To see it is finitely generated, use the finite descending filtration
$$1 + N \supset 1 + N^2 \supset 1 + N^3 \supset \cdots \supset \{1\}.$$
For each $k$, the map $(1+x) \mapsto x \bmod N^{k+1}$ is a group homomorphism $(
1 + N^k) \to (N^k/N^{k+1}, +)$, since for $x, y \in N^k$ the cross term $xy \in
N^{2k} \subset N^{k+1}$ vanishes modulo $N^{k+1}$. Its kernel is $1 + N^{k+1}$,
giving
$$(1 + N^k)/(1 + N^{k+1}) \hookrightarrow N^k / N^{k+1}.$$
Each $N^k/N^{k+1}$ is a finitely generated abelian group (as $N$ is a finitely g
enerated ideal in the Noetherian ring $S$). By induction over the finite filtrat
ion, $1 + N$ is finitely generated.

**Conclusion of Step 3:** $S^\times$ is an extension of two finitely generated a
belian groups, hence is itself **finitely generated**.

### Step 4: $A$ is finitely generated

We have $A \subset S^\times$, where $S^\times$ is a finitely generated abelian g
roup. By the structure theorem, $S^\times \cong T \times \mathbb{Z}^r$ where $T$
 is finite (torsion) and $r \geq 0$. Since $A$ is **torsion-free**, $A$ embeds i
nto the free abelian part $\mathbb{Z}^r$. A subgroup of a free abelian group of
finite rank is free abelian of finite rank, hence **finitely generated**. $\blac
ksquare$

---

### Summary

The key mechanism is that any torsion-free abelian subgroup $A$ of $\mathrm{GL}(
n,\mathbb{Z})$ sits inside the unit group of a commutative order $S \subset M_n(
\mathbb{Z})$ of rank $\leq n^2$. The unit group of such an order is always finit
ely generated (by Dirichlet's unit theorem for the semisimple quotient, plus a f
inite filtration argument for the unipotent radical). Therefore $A$, being a tor
sion-free subgroup of a finitely generated abelian group, is itself finitely gen
erated.

**The answer is no:** $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{Z})$ does **not** contain torsion-f
ree abelian subgroups that are not finitely generated.
