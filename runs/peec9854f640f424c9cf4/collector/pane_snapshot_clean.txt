## Resolvent of the Neumann Laplacian on $(0,1)$

**Setup.** We have $L_N = -\frac{d^2}{dx^2}$ on $L^2(0,1)$ with $f'(0)=0$, $f'(1
)=0$. We seek the resolvent $(L_N - \lambda I)^{-1}$, i.e., the solution operato
r for
$$-f''(x) - \lambda f(x) = g(x), \quad f'(0)=0,\; f'(1)=0.$$

**Spectrum.** The eigenfunctions of $L_N$ are $\phi_0(x)=1$ (eigenvalue $0$) and
 $\phi_n(x)=\sqrt{2}\cos(n\pi x)$ (eigenvalue $n^2\pi^2$, $n\ge 1$). Thus $\sigm
a(L_N)=\{n^2\pi^2:n=0,1,2,\dots\}$. The resolvent exists for $\lambda\notin\sigm
a(L_N)$, equivalently $\sqrt{\lambda}\sin\sqrt{\lambda}\neq 0$.

**Green's function construction.** We seek $R_\lambda(x,t)$ satisfying
$$(L_N-\lambda I)\,R_\lambda(\cdot,t)=\delta(\cdot-t), \quad \text{i.e.}\quad -R
_{xx}-\lambda R=\delta(x-t),$$
with $R_x(0,t)=0$, $R_x(1,t)=0$. Equivalently $R_{xx}+\lambda R = -\delta(x-t)$.

Set $\alpha=\sqrt{\lambda}$ (complex in general). The homogeneous equation $u''+
\lambda u=0$ has solutions $\cos(\alpha x)$, $\sin(\alpha x)$.

- **Left solution** (satisfies $u'(0)=0$): $u_L(x)=\cos(\alpha x)$, since $u_L'(
0)=-\alpha\sin 0=0$.
- **Right solution** (satisfies $u'(1)=0$): $u_R(x)=\cos(\alpha(1-x))$, since $u
_R'(x)=\alpha\sin(\alpha(1-x))$ and $u_R'(1)=\alpha\sin 0=0$.

For $x<t$: $R=A\,u_L(x)=A\cos(\alpha x)$. For $x>t$: $R=B\,u_R(x)=B\cos(\alpha(1
-x))$.

**Continuity at $x=t$:**
$$A\cos(\alpha t)=B\cos(\alpha(1-t)). \tag{1}$$

**Jump condition** (from $R_{xx}+\lambda R=-\delta$, the derivative jumps by $-1
$):
$$R_x(t^+,t)-R_x(t^-,t)=-1.$$
Computing: $R_x(t^+)=B\alpha\sin(\alpha(1-t))$, $R_x(t^-)=-A\alpha\sin(\alpha t)
$, so
$$B\alpha\sin(\alpha(1-t))+A\alpha\sin(\alpha t)=-1. \tag{2}$$

From (1): $B = A\cos(\alpha t)/\cos(\alpha(1-t))$. Substituting into (2):
$$A\alpha\left[\frac{\cos(\alpha t)\sin(\alpha(1-t))}{\cos(\alpha(1-t))}+\sin(\a
lpha t)\right]=-1,$$
$$A\alpha\,\frac{\cos(\alpha t)\sin(\alpha(1-t))+\sin(\alpha t)\cos(\alpha(1-t))
}{\cos(\alpha(1-t))}=-1,$$
$$A\alpha\,\frac{\sin(\alpha)}{\cos(\alpha(1-t))}=-1,$$
$$A=\frac{-\cos(\alpha(1-t))}{\alpha\sin(\alpha)}, \qquad B=\frac{-\cos(\alpha t
)}{\alpha\sin(\alpha)}.$$

**Resolvent kernel.** Combining both regions:

$$\boxed{R_\lambda(x,t)=\frac{-\cos\!\big(\sqrt{\lambda}\,\min(x,t)\big)\,\cos\!
\big(\sqrt{\lambda}\,(1-\max(x,t))\big)}{\sqrt{\lambda}\,\sin\sqrt{\lambda}}}$$

valid for $\lambda\notin\{n^2\pi^2:n=0,1,2,\dots\}$ (i.e., $\sqrt{\lambda}\sin\s
qrt{\lambda}\neq 0$). Explicitly:

$$R_\lambda(x,t)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{-\cos(\sqrt{\lambda}\,x)\cos(\s
qrt{\lambda}\,(1-t))}{\sqrt{\lambda}\sin\sqrt{\lambda}}, & x\le t,\\[6pt]\displa
ystyle\frac{-\cos(\sqrt{\lambda}\,t)\cos(\sqrt{\lambda}\,(1-x))}{\sqrt{\lambda}\
sin\sqrt{\lambda}}, & x\ge t.\end{cases}$$

The resolvent acts as:
$$[(L_N-\lambda I)^{-1}g](x)=\int_0^1 R_\lambda(x,t)\,g(t)\,dt.$$

**Verification.**

1. **Boundary conditions.** For $x<t$ (near $0$): $R_x=-A\alpha\sin(\alpha x)$,
so $R_x(0,t)=-A\alpha\sin 0=0$. ✓ For $x>t$ (near $1$): $R_x=B\alpha\sin(\alpha(
1-x))$, so $R_x(1,t)=B\alpha\sin 0=0$. ✓

2. **Differential equation away from $x=t$.** Both $\cos(\alpha x)$ and $\cos(\a
lpha(1-x))$ satisfy $u''+\lambda u=0$, so $-R_{xx}-\lambda R=0$ for $x\neq t$. ✓

3. **Jump condition.** By construction, $R_x(t^+)-R_x(t^-)=-1$, giving $-R_{xx}-
\lambda R=\delta(x-t)$. ✓

4. **Consistency with spectrum.** The denominator $\sqrt{\lambda}\sin\sqrt{\lamb
da}=0$ precisely when $\sqrt{\lambda}=n\pi$ ($n=0,1,2,\dots$), i.e., $\lambda=n^
2\pi^2\in\sigma(L_N)$. The resolvent has simple poles at the eigenvalues, as exp
ected. ✓

5. **Spectral representation check.** The kernel equals the meromorphic expansio

$$R_\lambda(x,t)=\frac{1}{-\lambda}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2\cos(n\pi x)\cos(n
\pi t)}{n^2\pi^2-\lambda},$$
which is the standard spectral resolution $R_\lambda=\sum_n \frac{\phi_n\otimes\
phi_n}{\lambda_n-\lambda}$, confirming the closed form. ✓

6. **Negative $\lambda$ check.** For $\lambda=-\mu^2<0$: $\sqrt{\lambda}=i\mu$,

$$R_{-\mu^2}(x,t)=\frac{\cosh(\mu\min(x,t))\cosh(\mu(1-\max(x,t)))}{\mu\sinh\mu}
>0,$$
which is positive as expected for the resolvent of a nonnegative operator at a n
