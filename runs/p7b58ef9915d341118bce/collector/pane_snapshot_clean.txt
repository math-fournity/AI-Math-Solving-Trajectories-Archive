Answer: No. Under the hypotheses stated (smooth + effective action only), such a
point need not exist. The quotient $M/G$ can fail to be a smooth manifold near
every point.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Counterexample: Irrational linear flow on the torus

Let $G = (\mathbb{R}, +)$ and $M = \mathbb{T}^2 = \mathbb{R}^2/\mathbb{Z}^2$.
Fix an irrational number $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}$ and define
the action $$ t \cdot [x, y] ;=; [x + t,; y + \alpha t], \qquad t \in
\mathbb{R},; [x,y] \in \mathbb{T}^2. $$

The action is smooth. The map $(t, [x,y]) \mapsto [x+t, y+\alpha t]$ is a smooth
map $\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{T}^2 \to \mathbb{T}^2$ (it lifts to the linear
map $(t, x, y) \mapsto (x+t, y+\alpha t)$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$).

The action is effective. Suppose $t \cdot [x,y] = [x,y]$ for all $[x,y] \in
\mathbb{T}^2$. Then $(t, \alpha t) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$, i.e., $t \in \mathbb{Z}$
and $\alpha t \in \mathbb{Z}$. If $t \neq 0$, then $\alpha = (\alpha t)/t \in
\mathbb{Q}$, contradicting the irrationality of $\alpha$. Hence $t = 0$, proving
effectiveness. (In fact the action is free by the same argument applied to a
single point.)

Every orbit is dense. The orbit of $[x_0, y_0]$ is $$ \mathcal{O}_{[x_0,y_0]} =
\big{,[x_0 + t,; y_0 + \alpha t] : t \in \mathbb{R},\big}, $$ the image of the
line ${(x_0 + t,, y_0 + \alpha t) : t \in \mathbb{R}}$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ under
the quotient $\mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{T}^2$. Since $\alpha$ is irrational, this
line projects to a dense subgroup of $\mathbb{T}^2$ (a classical fact: the
subgroup ${(t, \alpha t) \bmod \mathbb{Z}^2 : t \in \mathbb{R}}$ is dense in $
\mathbb{T}^2$). Thus $\overline{\mathcal{O}_p} = \mathbb{T}^2$ for every $p$.

The quotient topology on $M/G$ is indiscrete. Give $M/G$ the quotient topology.
Let $U \subseteq M/G$ be a nonempty open set. Then $\pi^{-1}(U)$ is a nonempty
saturated open subset of $\mathbb{T}^2$ (a union of whole orbits). Since $U \neq
\emptyset$, $\pi^{-1}(U)$ contains at least one orbit $\mathcal{O}$, which is
dense. Being open, $\pi^{-1}(U) \supseteq \overline{\mathcal{O}} = \mathbb{T}
^2$. Hence $\pi^{-1}(U) = \mathbb{T}^2$, so $U = M/G$. The only open sets are $
\emptyset$ and $M/G$.

The quotient has more than one point. Two points $[x_1, y_1]$ and $[x_2, y_2]$
lie in the same orbit iff $(x_2 - x_1,, y_2 - y_1) \in {(t, \alpha t) : t \in
\mathbb{R}} + \mathbb{Z}^2$. The set of orbits is uncountable (e.g., the circle
${y = 0}$ meets each orbit in the countable set $\mathbb{Z} + \alpha\mathbb{Z}$,
so there are uncountably many distinct orbits).

No point has a smooth neighborhood. Any open neighborhood of any $\pi(p) \in M/
G$ is the entire space $M/G$, which carries the indiscrete topology on an
uncountable set. An indiscrete space with more than one point is not Hausdorff,
hence cannot be a topological manifold, and consequently admits no smooth
manifold structure. Therefore no open neighborhood of $\pi(p)$ is smooth, for
any $p \in M$.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Conclusion

The hypotheses "$G$ acts smoothly and effectively on $M$" are insufficient to
guarantee the existence of a point $p \in M$ such that an open neighborhood of $
\pi(p)$ in $M/G$ is smooth. The irrational linear flow of $\mathbb{R}$ on $
\mathbb{T}^2$ provides a smooth, free, and effective action whose quotient is an
indiscrete (hence non-Hausdorff, non-manifold) space at every point.

$\boxed{\text{No — such a point need not exist.}}$
