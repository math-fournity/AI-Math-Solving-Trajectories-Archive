## Proof

**Setup and notation.** Let $f$ be holomorphic on the horizontal strip
$$U = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : \alpha < \operatorname{Im}(z) < \beta\}, \qquad \alph
a < \beta,$$
and suppose $f$ has period $a \neq 0$, i.e. $f(z+a)=f(z)$ for all $z \in U$. (Th
e problem's notation reuses $a$ for both the period and the lower strip bound; I
 rename the strip bounds to $\alpha,\beta$ for clarity.) Since $U$ is a horizont
al strip, translation invariance $U + a = U$ forces $a \in \mathbb{R}\setminus\{
0\}$; replacing $a$ by $-a$ if necessary, we assume $a > 0$.

---

### Step 1. Reduction to an annulus via the exponential map

Define
$$\Phi : U \longrightarrow \mathbb{C}^*, \qquad \Phi(z) = e^{2\pi i z/a}.$$

Since $\Phi(z+a) = e^{2\pi i(z+a)/a} = e^{2\pi i z/a}\,e^{2\pi i} = \Phi(z)$, th
e map $\Phi$ identifies the quotient $U/\langle z\mapsto z+a\rangle$ with its im
age. Writing $z = x+iy$ with $y\in(\alpha,\beta)$:
$$|\Phi(z)| = e^{-2\pi y/a} \in \bigl(e^{-2\pi\beta/a},\; e^{-2\pi\alpha/a}\bigr
).$$
Conversely every $w$ with $|w|$ in this open interval has a preimage $z = \frac{
a}{2\pi i}\log w$ with $\operatorname{Im} z \in (\alpha,\beta)$. Hence $\Phi$ ma
ps $U$ **conformally onto the annulus**
$$\mathcal{A} = \{w \in \mathbb{C} : r_1 < |w| < r_2\}, \qquad r_1 := e^{-2\pi\b
eta/a},\quad r_2 := e^{-2\pi\alpha/a}, \quad 0 < r_1 < r_2.$$

Because $f(z+a)=f(z)$, the value $f(z)$ depends only on $\Phi(z)$, so there is a
 well-defined function $g$ on $\mathcal{A}$ with
$$f(z) = g(\Phi(z)), \qquad g(w) = f\!\Bigl(\tfrac{a}{2\pi i}\log w\Bigr).$$
The map $\Phi$ is a local biholomorphism, so $g$ is holomorphic on $\mathcal{A}$
.

---

### Step 2. Laurent expansion on the annulus $\Rightarrow$ Fourier series on the
 strip

Every holomorphic function on an annulus $\{r_1<|w|<r_2\}$ admits a Laurent expa
nsion converging absolutely and locally uniformly:
$$g(w) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n\, w^n, \qquad c_n = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint
_{|w|=\rho} \frac{g(\zeta)}{\zeta^{n+1}}\,d\zeta, \quad \rho\in(r_1,r_2).$$

Substituting $w = e^{2\pi i z/a}$:

$$\boxed{\;f(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n\, e^{2\pi i n z/a}, \qquad z\in
U.\;}$$

This is the **Fourier series** of $f$ on the strip.

---

### Step 3. Integral formula for the coefficients

Fix any $y\in(\alpha,\beta)$ and parametrize the circle $|w|=\rho = e^{-2\pi y/a
}$ by $w = e^{2\pi i(x+iy)/a}$, $x\in[0,a]$. Then $dw = \frac{2\pi i}{a}e^{2\pi
i z/a}\,dx$, and
$$c_n = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{a} \frac{f(x+iy)}{e^{2\pi i n(x+iy)/a}}\cdot\f
rac{1}{e^{2\pi i(x+iy)/a}}\cdot\frac{2\pi i}{a}\,e^{2\pi i(x+iy)/a}\,dx$$
$$= \frac{1}{a}\int_{0}^{a} f(x+iy)\, e^{-2\pi i n(x+iy)/a}\,dx.$$

Writing $f(x+iy) = \sum_k c_k e^{2\pi i k(x+iy)/a}$ and using orthogonality $\fr
ac1a\int_0^a e^{2\pi i(k-n)x/a}\,dx = \delta_{kn}$ confirms this. Crucially, **$
c_n$ is independent of $y$**: this follows from the Laurent coefficient being in
dependent of $\rho$, or directly by Cauchy's theorem applied to the rectangle wi
th horizontal sides at heights $y_1, y_2 \in (\alpha,\beta)$ (the vertical sides
 cancel by $a$-periodicity).

---

### Step 4. Convergence properties

From the Laurent theory on $\mathcal{A}$:

1. **Absolute convergence:** $\sum_{n}|c_n|\,|e^{2\pi i n z/a}| = \sum_n |c_n|\,
e^{-2\pi n\,\operatorname{Im}z/a}$ converges for every $z\in U$.

2. **Uniform convergence on compact subsets:** For any compact $K\subset U$, the
re exist $\alpha',\beta'$ with $\alpha<\alpha'<\beta'<\beta$ and $K\subset\{z:\a
lpha'\le\operatorname{Im}z\le\beta'\}$. The corresponding closed sub-annulus $\{
e^{-2\pi\beta'/a}\le|w|\le e^{-2\pi\alpha'/a}\}\subset\mathcal{A}$ lies inside t
he annulus of convergence, so the Laurent series converges uniformly there, henc
e $\sum_n c_n e^{2\pi i nz/a}$ converges uniformly on $K$.

3. **Termwise differentiation:** Because the convergence is locally uniform and
each term $c_n e^{2\pi i nz/a}$ is entire, the series may be differentiated term
 by term any number of times, and the differentiated series also converges local
ly uniformly. In particular $f'(z)=\sum_n \frac{2\pi i n}{a}\,c_n\,e^{2\piinz/a}
$, consistent with $f$ being holomorphic.

---

### Step 5. Growth/decay estimates on the coefficients

From the integral formula and $M(y):=\sup_{x\in[0,a]}|f(x+iy)|$:

$$|c_n| \;\le\; \frac{1}{a}\int_0^a |f(x+iy)|\,e^{2\pi n y/a}\,dx \;\le\; M(y)\,
e^{2\pi n y/a}, \qquad \forall\, y\in(\alpha,\beta). \tag{$\star$}$$

Taking the infimum over $y$:

- **For $n \ge 0$:** the factor $e^{2\pi ny/a}$ is minimized as $y\to\alpha^+$,
giving $|c_n| \le M_\alpha^+\, e^{2\pi n\alpha/a}$, where $M_\alpha^+ = \limsup_
{y\to\alpha^+} M(y)$. The **positive Fourier modes are controlled by the behavio
r near the lower boundary** of the strip.

- **For $n \le 0$:** the factor $e^{2\pi ny/a}$ is minimized as $y\to\beta^-$, g
iving $|c_n| \le M_\beta^-\, e^{2\pi n\beta/a}$. The **negative Fourier modes ar
e controlled by the behavior near the upper boundary**.

Equivalently, $(\star)$ says the function $y\mapsto |c_n|e^{-2\pi ny/a}$ is boun
ded by $M(y)$ on $(\alpha,\beta)$ for each fixed $n$.

**Converse direction (sufficiency).** If a doubly-infinite sequence $(c_n)$ sati
sfies, for some $\alpha<\beta$ and constants $A,B$,
$$|c_n| \le A\,e^{-2\pi n\alpha/a}\;(n\ge0), \qquad |c_n|\le B\,e^{-2\pi n\beta/
a}\;(n\le0),$$
then $\sum_n c_n e^{2\pi i nz/a}$ converges absolutely and locally uniformly on
$\{z:\alpha<\operatorname{Im}z<\beta\}$ and defines a holomorphic $a$-periodic f
unction there. This is exactly the Laurent-series criterion on the annulus.

---

### Step 6. Boundary behavior and uniqueness

- **Uniqueness:** The coefficients $c_n$ are uniquely determined by $f$ (they ar
e the Laurent coefficients of $g$). Equivalently, if all $c_n=0$ then $f\equiv0$
 (by the integral formula, or by the identity theorem on $\mathcal{A}$).

- **Extension to the boundary:** If $f$ extends holomorphically (and $a$-periodi
cally) to a wider strip $\{z:\tilde\alpha<\operatorname{Im}z<\tilde\beta\}$ with
 $\tilde\alpha<\alpha<\beta<\tilde\beta$, then the same Fourier series converges
 on the wider strip — the annulus of convergence of the Laurent series is at lea
st $\{e^{-2\pi\tilde\beta/a}<|w|<e^{-2\pi\tilde\alpha/a}\}$, which strictly cont
ains $\mathcal A$. Thus the **strip of convergence of the Fourier series is the
largest horizontal strip on which $f$ extends as an $a$-periodic holomorphic fun
ction**, and its width is governed by the inner/outer radii of convergence of th
e Laurent series of $g$.

- **Real-valued symmetry (optional):** If additionally $f$ is real-valued on the
 real axis (when $0\in(\alpha,\beta)$) and $a$-periodic, then $\overline{f(z)}=f
(\bar z)$, which gives $c_{-n}=\overline{c_n}$.

### Summary

**Theorem.** Let $f$ be holomorphic on the strip $U=\{z:\alpha<\operatorname{Im}
z<\beta\}$ with real period $a>0$. Then:

$$f(z)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}c_n\,e^{2\pi i n z/a}, \qquad c_n=\frac{1}{a}\in
t_0^a f(x+iy)\,e^{-2\pi i n(x+iy)/a}\,dx,$$

with the following properties:

| Property | Statement |
|---|---|
| **Convergence** | Absolute for each $z\in U$; uniform on every compact $K\subs
et U$ |
| **Coefficient independence** | $c_n$ does not depend on the choice of $y\in(\a
lpha,\beta)$ |
| **Termwise differentiation** | Valid any number of times, locally uniformly |
| **Growth bound** | $\|c_n\|\le M(y)\,e^{2\pi ny/a}$ for all $y\in(\alpha,\beta
)$ |
| **Positive modes ($n\ge0$)** | Controlled by behavior near the lower edge $\op
eratorname{Im}z=\alpha$ |
| **Negative modes ($n\le0$)** | Controlled by behavior near the upper edge $\op
eratorname{Im}z=\beta$ |
| **Uniqueness** | The coefficients $(c_n)$ are uniquely determined by $f$ |
| **Maximal strip** | The series converges on the largest strip to which $f$ ext
ends $a$-periodically and holomorphically |

The proof reduces, via $w=e^{2\pi iz/a}$, to the Laurent expansion of a holomorp
hic function on the annulus $\mathcal A=\{e^{-2\pi\beta/a}<|w|<e^{-2\pi\alpha/a}
\}$, and all listed properties are direct translations of standard Laurent-serie
s facts (absolute/local-uniform convergence, coefficient formula via contour int
egral, independence of the contour radius, Cauchy estimates, uniqueness, and max
imal annulus of convergence).
