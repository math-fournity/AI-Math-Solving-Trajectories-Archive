## Proof

We show that the only continuous solution of the homogeneous Volterra integral e
quation of the second kind

$$f(s) = \int_0^s K(s,t)\,f(t)\,dt, \qquad f\in C[0,1],\; K\in C([0,1]\times[0,1
]),$$

is $f \equiv 0$.

**Setup.** Define the operator $T$ on $C[0,1]$ by

$$(Tg)(s) = \int_0^s K(s,t)\,g(t)\,dt.$$

The equation is the fixed-point equation $f = Tf$. Iterating, $f = T^n f$ for ev
ery $n \ge 1$.

Let $\|g\|_\infty = \max_{0\le t\le 1}|g(t)|$ and set

$$M = \max_{(s,t)\in[0,1]\times[0,1]} |K(s,t)|,$$

which is finite since $K$ is continuous on a compact set.

**Key estimate.** We claim that for every $n\ge 1$ and every $g\in C[0,1]$,

$$|(T^n g)(s)| \le \frac{M^n\, s^n}{n!}\,\|g\|_\infty, \qquad 0\le s\le 1. \tag{
$\star$}$$

We prove $(\star)$ by induction on $n$.

*Base case $n=1$:*
$$|(Tg)(s)| \le \int_0^s |K(s,t)|\,|g(t)|\,dt \le M\,\|g\|_\infty \int_0^s dt =
M\,s\,\|g\|_\infty = \frac{M^1 s^1}{1!}\|g\|_\infty.$$

*Inductive step:* Assume $(\star)$ holds for $n$. Then

$$(T^{n+1}g)(s) = T(T^n g)(s) = \int_0^s K(s,t)\,(T^n g)(t)\,dt,$$

so using the induction hypothesis at the point $t\le s$,

$$|(T^{n+1}g)(s)| \le \int_0^s |K(s,t)|\cdot|(T^n g)(t)|\,dt \le \int_0^s M\cdot
 \frac{M^n t^n}{n!}\|g\|_\infty\,dt = \frac{M^{n+1}\|g\|_\infty}{n!}\cdot\frac{s
^{n+1}}{n+1} = \frac{M^{n+1}s^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\|g\|_\infty.$$

This establishes $(\star)$.

**Conclusion.** Since $f = T^n f$ for every $n$, applying $(\star)$ with $g = f$
 gives

$$|f(s)| = |(T^n f)(s)| \le \frac{M^n s^n}{n!}\,\|f\|_\infty \le \frac{M^n}{n!}\
,\|f\|_\infty, \qquad 0\le s\le 1.$$

Now $\dfrac{M^n}{n!}\to 0$ as $n\to\infty$ (the factorial dominates any exponent
ial). Therefore, taking $n\to\infty$,

$$|f(s)| \le 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(s) = 0 \quad\text{for every } s\in[0,
1].$$

Hence $f\equiv 0$ on $[0,1]$.

**Remark.** The argument does not require $K$ to be small or any contraction ass
umption on $T$ itself; the factor $1/n!$ arising from the Volterra (triangular)
structure of the kernel forces the iterates to vanish in the limit. This is the
standard Picard-iteration / Gronwall-type argument for homogeneous Volterra equa
tions of the second kind.
