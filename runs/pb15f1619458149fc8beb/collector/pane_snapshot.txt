# Characteristic Oscillations of a Spherical Resonator

## 1. Maxwell's Equations and the Helmholtz Equation

Inside a source-free spherical cavity of radius $a$ with perfectly conducting wa
lls, with time dependence $e^{-i\omega t}$, Maxwell's equations give:

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = i\omega\mu_0 \mathbf{H}, \qquad \nabla \times \math
bf{H} = -i\omega\epsilon_0 \mathbf{E}$$

Both fields satisfy the vector Helmholtz equation:

$$\nabla^2 \mathbf{E} + k^2 \mathbf{E} = 0, \qquad \nabla^2 \mathbf{H} + k^2 \ma
thbf{H} = 0, \qquad k = \omega/c$$

with $c = 1/\sqrt{\epsilon_0\mu_0}$, subject to the boundary conditions on the p

$$E_\theta\big|_{r=a} = 0, \quad E_\phi\big|_{r=a} = 0, \quad H_r\big|_{r=a} = 0

### 3.2 Boundary Condition and Eigenvalue Equation

The conducting-wall condition $E_\theta = E_\phi = 0$ at $r = a$ requires:

$$\frac{\partial^2(ru)}{\partial r\,\partial\theta}\bigg|_{r=a} = 0, \quad \frac
{\partial^2(ru)}{\partial r\,\partial\phi}\bigg|_{r=a} = 0$$

This means $\frac{\partial(ru)}{\partial r}\big|_{r=a}$ is independent of $\thet

$$\frac{\partial(ru)}{\partial r}\bigg|_{r=a} = 0 \qquad \text{(**)}$$

**Separation of variables.** With $u = R(r)\,Y(\theta,\phi)$, the Helmholtz equa
tion separates:

- Angular: $\nabla_\Omega^2 Y + n(n+1)Y = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; Y_n^{(m)}(\theta

- Radial: $R'' + \frac{2}{r}R' + \left(k^2 - \frac{n(n+1)}{r^2}\right)R = 0$, wi
th regular solution $R = \psi_n(kr)$ where $\psi_n(\rho) = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2\rh

So $u_{m,n}(r,\theta,\phi) = \psi_n(k_{m,n}r)\,Y_n^{(m)}(\theta,\phi)$.

**Applying the boundary condition (**)**. Set $\rho = kr$; then $ru = \frac{1}{k

$$\frac{d}{d\rho}\left[\sqrt{\frac{\pi\rho}{2}}\,J_{n+1/2}(\rho)\right]\bigg|_{\

$$\frac{d}{d\rho}\sqrt{\frac{\pi\rho}{2}}\,J_{n+1/2}(\rho) = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2\

$$\boxed{\frac{J_{n+1/2}(ka)}{J_{n-1/2}(ka)} = \frac{ka}{n}} \qquad (n = 1, 2, \

This is the **characteristic equation for electric-type oscillations**. Its root

## 5. Comparison with Acoustic Vibrations of the Sphere (Problem 25)

The characteristic acoustic vibrations of a sphere satisfy $\Delta p + k^2 p = 0
$ with either:

- **Dirichlet** ($p = 0$ at $r = a$, soft sphere): eigenvalue equation $J_{n+1/2
}(ka) = 0$ — **identical to magnetic-type EM oscillations**.
- **Neumann** ($\partial p/\partial r = 0$ at $r = a$, hard sphere): eigenvalue
equation $\frac{J_{n+1/2}(ka)}{J_{n-1/2}(ka)} = \frac{ka}{n+1}$ — differs from e
lectric-type by the shift $n \to n+1$.

The electric-type boundary condition $\frac{\partial(ru)}{\partial r} = 0$ is a
**modified Neumann condition** on the Riccati-Bessel function $\hat{j}_n(\rho) =
 \rho\,\psi_n(\rho)$, not on $u$ itself. This reflects the different physical or
igin: the EM boundary condition is on the tangential electric field (involving r
adial derivatives of $ru$), whereas the acoustic condition is directly on the pr
essure or its radial derivative.

**Key physical results:**

| Type | Lowest mode | Eigenvalue equation | $\omega_{\min}$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electric (TM), $n=0$ | $k_0 = \pi/(2a)$ | $J_{-1/2}(ka)=0 \Leftrightarrow \cos
(ka)=0$ | $\pi c/(2a)$ |
| Magnetic (TE), $n=0$ | $k_1 = \pi/a$ | $J_{1/2}(ka)=0 \Leftrightarrow \sin(ka)
=0$ | $\pi c/a$ |

The **lowest natural frequency of the spherical resonator** is the electric-type
 mode:

$$\omega_{\min} = \omega_{1,0} = \frac{\pi c}{2a}$$

which is exactly **half** the lowest magnetic-type frequency, reflecting the dif

root $ka = \pi/2$, while the magnetic-type condition $\sin(ka)=0$ requires $ka \
