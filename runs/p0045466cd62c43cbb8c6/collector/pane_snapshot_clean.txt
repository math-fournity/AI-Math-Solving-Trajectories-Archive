**Goal**: Determine $|[X, S^1]|$ when $X = S^1$, expressed in terms of $\pi_1(S^

### Step 1. The group structure on $[X, S^1]$

$S^1$ is a topological group under complex multiplication. This induces a pointw
ise multiplication on maps $f, g : X \to S^1$ by $(f \cdot g)(x) = f(x)\,g(x)$,
which descends to homotopy classes. The class $e$ of the constant map at $1$ is
the identity, and inverses come from pointwise complex conjugation. Hence $([X,
S^1], \cdot, e)$ is an abelian group.

### Step 2. Identify $[S^1, S^1]$ with $\pi_1(S^1)$

A based map $S^1 \to S^1$ represents an element of $\pi_1(S^1)$. Two based maps
are based-homotopic iff they represent the same element of $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \ma
thbb{Z}$.

For *unbased* homotopy classes (which is what $[X, S^1]$ denotes), we quotient b
y the action of $\pi_1(S^1)$ on itself by conjugation. Since $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \
mathbb{Z}$ is **abelian**, conjugation is trivial, so based and unbased homotopy
 classes coincide:

$$[S^1, S^1] \;\cong\; \pi_1(S^1) \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}.$$

Concretely, every map $f : S^1 \to S^1$ has a well-defined **degree** $n \in \ma
thbb{Z}$, and two maps are homotopic iff they have the same degree. The correspo
ndence $[f] \mapsto \deg(f)$ is the isomorphism above.

### Step 3. The group operation agrees with addition in $\mathbb{Z}$

Under the degree correspondence, pointwise multiplication of maps corresponds to
 addition of degrees:

$$\deg(f \cdot g) = \deg(f) + \deg(g),$$

since the induced map on $H_1$ satisfies $(f \cdot g)_* = f_* + g_*$. Thus the g
roup operation on $[S^1, S^1]$ is exactly addition in $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{
Z}$, with identity $e = 0$.

### Step 4. Count the elements

Since $[S^1, S^1] \cong \pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, and $\mathbb{Z}$ is counta
bly infinite,

$$\boxed{|[S^1, S^1]| \;=\; |\pi_1(S^1)| \;=\; |\mathbb{Z}| \;=\; \aleph_0.}$$

The set $[S^1, S^1]$ is in bijection with the fundamental group $\pi_1(S^1) \con
g \mathbb{Z}$, and therefore has **countably infinitely many** elements — one fo
r each integer, indexed by the degree/winding number of the map.
