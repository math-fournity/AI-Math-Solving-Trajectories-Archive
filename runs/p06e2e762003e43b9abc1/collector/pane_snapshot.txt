## Proof

**Claim:** Yes. For every $\tilde{x} \in M$ there exists $r_0(\tilde{x}) > 0$ su
ch that $|B_M(\tilde{x}, r)| \sim r^n$ as $r \to 0^+$ for $0 < r < r_0(\tilde{x}
)$.

The argument proceeds in three steps.

### Step 1: Torsion-free $\Gamma$ acts freely on $X$

Let $\gamma \in \Gamma$, $\gamma \neq e$, and suppose $\gamma$ fixes a point $x
\in X$. Then $\gamma$ belongs to the stabilizer $K_x = \{g \in G : g \cdot x = x
\}$, which is conjugate to $K$ and hence compact. The cyclic subgroup $\langle \
gamma \rangle$ is a discrete subgroup of the compact group $K_x$. A discrete sub
group of a compact group is finite (it has no accumulation points and is contain
ed in a compact set, so it must be finite). Therefore $\gamma$ has finite order,
 contradicting the torsion-free hypothesis.

Hence no non-identity element of $\Gamma$ fixes any point of $X$; the action of
$\Gamma$ on $X$ is **free**.

### Step 2: The quotient map $\pi: X \to M$ is a Riemannian covering and local i
sometry

Since $\Gamma$ is discrete in $G$ and acts on $X$ by isometries, the action is *
*properly discontinuous**: for any compact $C \subset X$, the set $\{\gamma \in
\Gamma : \gamma \cdot C \cap C \neq \emptyset\}$ is finite. Combined with freene
ss (Step 1), this means:

- $\pi: X \to M = \Gamma \backslash X$ is a covering map,
- Since $\Gamma$ acts by isometries of the $G$-invariant Riemannian metric on $X
$, the map $\pi$ is a **local isometry**: it pulls back the metric on $M$ to the
 metric on $X$.

In particular, $M$ is a smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension $n$ (not merely
an orbifold), since the action is free.

### Step 3: Local isometry preserves small-ball volume

Fix $\tilde{x} \in M$ and let $x \in X$ be any lift with $\pi(x) = \tilde{x}$. T
he **injectivity radius** at $\tilde{x}$ is:

$$\operatorname{inj}(\tilde{x}) = \frac{1}{2} \inf_{\gamma \in \Gamma \setminus
\{e\}} d_X(x,\, \gamma \cdot x).$$

This infimum is positive: by proper discontinuity, only finitely many $\gamma$ s
atisfy $d(x, \gamma \cdot x) \leq R$ for any fixed $R$, and by freeness each suc
h distance is strictly positive. Hence the infimum is a minimum over a finite se
t of positive values, so $\operatorname{inj}(\tilde{x}) > 0$.

Set $r_0(\tilde{x}) = \operatorname{inj}(\tilde{x}) > 0$. For every $0 < r < r_0
(\tilde{x})$, the restriction

$$\pi\big|_{B_X(x,\, r)} : B_X(x, r) \longrightarrow B_M(\tilde{x}, r)$$

is an **isometry** (it is a diffeomorphism onto its image by the covering proper
ty within the injectivity radius, and it preserves the metric). Therefore:

$$|B_M(\tilde{x}, r)| = |B_X(x, r)| \quad \text{for all } 0 < r < r_0(\tilde{x})
.$$

Since $X$ is an $n$-dimensional Riemannian manifold, the standard small-ball vol
ume asymptotic gives:

$$|B_X(x, r)| = \omega_n \, r^n \left(1 - \frac{\operatorname{Scal}(x)}{6(n+2)}\
, r^2 + O(r^4)\right) \quad \text{as } r \to 0^+,$$

where $\omega_n$ is the volume of the Euclidean unit ball in $\mathbb{R}^n$ and
$\operatorname{Scal}(x)$ is the scalar curvature. In particular:

$$\lim_{r \to 0^+} \frac{|B_X(x, r)|}{r^n} = \omega_n > 0.$$

Combining with the equality from the local isometry:

$$\lim_{r \to 0^+} \frac{|B_M(\tilde{x}, r)|}{r^n} = \omega_n > 0 \quad \text{fo
r every } \tilde{x} \in M.$$

### Conclusion

For every $\tilde{x} \in M$, there exists $r_0(\tilde{x}) > 0$ (namely the injec
tivity radius at $\tilde{x}$) such that for all $0 < r < r_0(\tilde{x})$:

$$|B_M(\tilde{x}, r)| \sim r^n.$$

The key ingredients are: (1) torsion-freeness of $\Gamma$ forces the action on $
X$ to be free (via the compactness of stabilizers and discreteness of $\Gamma$),
 (2) discreteness gives proper discontinuity, and (3) together these make $\pi:
X \to M$ a Riemannian local isometry, so small-ball volumes are identical on $X$
 and $M$ within the injectivity radius. $\blacksquare$
