## Solution

We need to evaluate $\lim_{n\to\infty} f(n)$, where
$$f(n) = \lim_{x\to 0}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(1+\sin\frac{x}{2^k}\right)\righ
t)^{1/x}.$$

### Step 1. Compute $f(n)$ for fixed $n$.

Set $P_n(x) = \prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(1+\sin\frac{x}{2^k}\right)$ and take logarith
ms:
$$\log f(n) = \lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\log P_n(x)}{x} = \lim_{x\to 0}\frac{1}{x}\sum_
{k=1}^{n}\log\!\left(1+\sin\frac{x}{2^k}\right).$$

For each fixed $k$, rewrite
$$\frac{1}{x}\log\!\left(1+\sin\frac{x}{2^k}\right) = \frac{\log(1+\sin(x/2^k))}
{\sin(x/2^k)}\cdot\frac{\sin(x/2^k)}{x/2^k}\cdot\frac{1}{2^k}.$$

As $x\to 0$, we have $\sin(x/2^k)\to 0$, so by the standard limits $\frac{\log(1
+t)}{t}\to 1$ and $\frac{\sin t}{t}\to 1$ (with $t = x/2^k$),
$$\frac{1}{x}\log\!\left(1+\sin\frac{x}{2^k}\right) \;\longrightarrow\; 1\cdot 1
\cdot\frac{1}{2^k} = \frac{1}{2^k}.$$

Since the sum is finite, we may interchange the limit with the sum:
$$\log f(n) = \sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2^k} = 1 - \frac{1}{2^n}.$$

Therefore
$$f(n) = e^{\,1 - 2^{-n}}.$$

### Step 2. Take $n\to\infty$.

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} f(n) = \lim_{n\to\infty} e^{\,1-2^{-n}} = e^{\,1-0} = e.$$

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} f(n) = e}.$$
