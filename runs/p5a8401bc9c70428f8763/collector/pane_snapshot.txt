Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether any two distinct points $x,
 y \in \mathbb{S}^1 - \{a, b\}$ can be connected by a continuous path in $D - (A
 \cup B)$.

**Answer: Yes, such a path always exists.**

## Proof

**Key observations:**

1. Since $A \cap \mathbb{S}^1 = \{a\}$, the entire arc $\mathbb{S}^1 - \{a\}$ li
es in $D - A$. This arc is connected (homeomorphic to an open interval), and con
tains both $x$ and $y$ (since $x, y \in \mathbb{S}^1 - \{a,b\} \subset \mathbb{S
}^1 - \{a\}$). Therefore, $x$ and $y$ lie in the **same connected component** of
 $D - A$.

2. By the identical argument with $B$: $\mathbb{S}^1 - \{b\} \subset D - B$ is c
onnected and contains $x, y$, so $x$ and $y$ lie in the **same connected compone
nt** of $D - B$.

**Applying the Phragmén–Brouwer Theorem:**

The closed disk $D$ is a compact, connected, locally connected Hausdorff space (
a locally connected continuum). The Phragmén–Brouwer theorem states:

> *Let $X$ be a locally connected continuum, and let $A, B$ be disjoint closed s
ubsets of $X$. If two points $x, y \in X - (A \cup B)$ lie in different connecte
d components of $X - (A \cup B)$, then $x, y$ lie in different connected compone
nts of $X - A$ **or** in different connected components of $X - B$.*

We apply the **contrapositive**: since $x, y$ are in the same component of $D -
A$ (observation 1) **and** in the same component of $D - B$ (observation 2), it
follows that $x, y$ are in the **same connected component** of $D - (A \cup B)$.

**From connectedness to path-connectedness:**

The set $D - (A \cup B)$ is open in $D$ (since $A \cup B$ is closed in $D$). We
verify it is **locally path-connected**:

- For any point $p$ in the interior of $D$: a small ball $B(p, r) \subset D - (A
 \cup B)$ is path-connected.
- For any point $p \in \mathbb{S}^1 - \{a, b\}$: since $p \notin A \cup B$ and $
A \cup B$ is closed, a small ball $B(p, r)$ is disjoint from $A \cup B$, and $B(
p, r) \cap D$ (a half-disk) is a path-connected neighborhood of $p$ in $D - (A \
cup B)$.

In a locally path-connected space, connected components coincide with path compo
nents. Since $x$ and $y$ are in the same connected component of $D - (A \cup B)$
, they are in the same **path component**.

**Conclusion:** There exists a continuous function $f: [0,1] \to D - (A \cup B)$
 with $f(0) = x$ and $f(1) = y$.
