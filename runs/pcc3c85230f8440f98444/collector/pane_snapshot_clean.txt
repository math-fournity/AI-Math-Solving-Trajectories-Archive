## Proof

**Construction.** Let $n = 1993$. For $j = 1, 2, \dots, n$, define the unit vect
or
$$v_j = \left(\cos\frac{j\pi}{n+1},\;\sin\frac{j\pi}{n+1}\right) \in \mathbb{R}^
2.$$
Since the directions $\frac{j\pi}{n+1}$ all lie in $(0,\pi)$ and are distinct, t
he vectors $v_1, \dots, v_n$ are pairwise distinct and **no two are antipodal**
(antipodality would require two directions to differ by $\pi$, impossible since
all directions lie in an open interval of length $\pi$).

Now define
$$A = \left\{\sum_{j=1}^{n} a_j\, v_j \;:\; (a_1, \dots, a_n) \in \{0,1\}^n\righ
t\} \subset \mathbb{R}^2.$$
Since $A$ is the image of the finite set $\{0,1\}^n$ (which has $2^n$ elements),
 $A$ is **finite**.

**Verification.** Let $X \in A$, so $X = \sum_{j=1}^n a_j v_j$ for some $(a_1,\d
ots,a_n) \in \{0,1\}^n$.

For each $j \in \{1,\dots,n\}$, let $a^{(j)}$ be the tuple obtained by flipping
the $j$-th coordinate:
$$a^{(j)}_j = 1 - a_j, \qquad a^{(j)}_i = a_i \;\;(i \neq j),$$
and set
$$Y_j = \sum_{i=1}^n a^{(j)}_i\, v_i \;\in A.$$

**Distance.** We compute
$$|X - Y_j| = \left|\sum_{i=1}^n (a_i - a^{(j)}_i)\, v_i\right| = \left|(a_j - (
1-a_j))\, v_j\right| = |(2a_j - 1)\, v_j| = |v_j| = 1,$$
since $|v_j| = 1$ and $|2a_j - 1| = 1$.

**Distinctness.** Suppose $Y_j = Y_k$ for some $j \neq k$. Then
$$Y_j - X = (2a_j - 1)\,v_j, \qquad Y_k - X = (2a_k - 1)\,v_k,$$
so $(2a_j-1)v_j = (2a_k-1)v_k$, i.e., $\pm v_j = \pm v_k$. This means either $v_
j = v_k$ or $v_j = -v_k$. The first is impossible since the $v_j$ have distinct
directions; the second is impossible since no two are antipodal. Hence $j = k$,
a contradiction.

Therefore $Y_1, Y_2, \dots, Y_{1993}$ are **1993 distinct points** in $A$, each
at distance exactly $1$ from $X$.

Since $X \in A$ was arbitrary, every point of $A$ has at least $1993$ points of
$A$ at distance $1$. This completes the proof. $\blacksquare$
