## Proof

**Answer: False**

**Counterexample.** Consider $\mathbb{Q}$ with the subspace topology inherited f
rom $\mathbb{R}$.

- $\mathbb{Q}$ is **second countable**: the collection $\{B \cap \mathbb{Q} : B
\text{ is an open interval with rational endpoints}\}$ is a countable basis.
- $\mathbb{Q}$ is **totally disconnected**: for any two distinct rationals $p <
q$, choose an irrational $r \in (p,q)$; then $(-\infty, r) \cap \mathbb{Q}$ and
$(r, \infty) \cap \mathbb{Q}$ form a separation of $\mathbb{Q}$, and similarly a
ny subset with two points is disconnected. Hence the only connected subsets of $
\mathbb{Q}$ are singletons.
- **Singletons are not open** in $\mathbb{Q}$: no singleton $\{q\}$ contains a n
onempty basic open set $B \cap \mathbb{Q}$ (every such set is infinite).

Therefore the **only connected open subset of $\mathbb{Q}$ is the empty set**.

Now take the open set $U = \mathbb{Q}$ (open in itself). If $U$ were a countable
 union of disjoint connected open sets, each such set would have to be empty (th
e only connected open set). A union of empty sets is empty, contradicting $U = \
mathbb{Q} \neq \emptyset$.

Thus $\mathbb{Q}$ is a second countable space containing an open subset that can
not be expressed as a countable union of disjoint connected open sets. The state
ment is **False**.
