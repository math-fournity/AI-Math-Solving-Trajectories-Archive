## Proof

**Coordinate setup.** Place $B=(0,0)$, $C=(\ell,0)$, $A=(p,q)$ with $q>0$. Let $
a=BC=\ell$, $b=CA$, $c=AB$, so
$$c^2=p^2+q^2, \qquad b^2=(\ell-p)^2+q^2=\ell^2-2\ell p+c^2.$$

**Computing J and K.** The $A$-excenter is $J=\frac{-\ell\cdot A+b\cdot B+c\cdot
 C}{-\ell+b+c}$. Setting $s=b+c-\ell$, this gives
$$J=\left(\frac{\ell(c-p)}{s},\;\frac{-\ell q}{s}\right), \qquad K=\left(\frac{\
ell(c-p)}{s},\;\frac{\ell q}{s}\right).$$
Write $k_x=\frac{\ell(c-p)}{s}$, $k_y=\frac{\ell q}{s}$, so $K=(k_x,k_y)$ and $J
=(k_x,-k_y)$.

**Computing E.** Since $E\in BJ$ and $BJ$ has direction $(c-p,-q)$, write $E=t(c
-p,-q)$. The condition $\angle EAB=90°$ gives $(E-A)\cdot(B-A)=0$:
$$t\,c(c-p)+c^2=0 \implies t=-\frac{c}{c-p},$$
so $E=\left(-c,\;\frac{cq}{c-p}\right)$.

**Computing F.** Since $F\in CJ$ and $CJ$ has direction $(\ell-p-b,-q)$, write $
F=(\ell,0)+t(\ell-p-b,-q)$. The condition $\angle CAF=90°$ gives $(F-A)\cdot(C-A
)=0$:
$$b^2+t\,b(b-\ell+p)=0 \implies t=-\frac{b}{b-\ell+p},$$
so $F=\left(\ell+b,\;\frac{bq}{b-\ell+p}\right)$.

**Key symmetry.** Set $u=F_x-k_x$, $u'=E_x-k_x$, $v=F_y-k_y$, $v'=E_y-k_y$. Then
:
$$u+u'=(\ell+b)+(-c)-2k_x=\frac{(\ell+b-c)s-2\ell(c-p)}{s}.$$
Expanding the numerator: $(\ell+b-c)(b+c-\ell)-2\ell(c-p)=2\ell c-\ell^2+b^2-c^2
-2\ell c+2\ell p=b^2-c^2-\ell^2+2\ell p=0$, since $b^2=\ell^2-2\ell p+c^2$. Ther
efore
$$\boxed{u'=-u.}$$

**Reducing the angle condition.** Since $K=(k_x,k_y)$ and $J=(k_x,-k_y)$:
$$\vec{KF}=(u,v),\quad \vec{KE}=(-u,v'),\quad \vec{JF}=(u,v+2k_y),\quad \vec{JE}
=(-u,v'+2k_y).$$

The condition $\angle FKE+\angle FJE=180°$ (with both angles in $(0°,180°)$) is
equivalent to $\sin(\angle FKE+\angle FJE)=0$, i.e.,
$$(\vec{KF}\times\vec{KE})(\vec{JF}\cdot\vec{JE})+(\vec{JF}\times\vec{JE})(\vec{
KF}\cdot\vec{KE})=0.$$

Computing with $u'=-u$:
$$\vec{KF}\times\vec{KE}=u(v+v'), \qquad \vec{KF}\cdot\vec{KE}=-u^2+vv',$$
$$\vec{JF}\times\vec{JE}=u(v+v'+4k_y), \qquad \vec{JF}\cdot\vec{JE}=-u^2+vv'+2k_
y(v+v')+4k_y^2.$$

Substituting and setting $P=v+v'$, $Q=-u^2+vv'$, the condition becomes:
$$P(Q+2k_yP+4k_y^2)+(P+4k_y)Q=0 \implies 2(P+2k_y)(Q+k_yP)=0.$$

Since $P+2k_y=F_y+E_y=\frac{bq}{b-\ell+p}+\frac{cq}{c-p}\neq 0$, we need $Q+k_yP
=0$, i.e.,
$$vv'+k_y(v+v')=u^2 \iff (v+k_y)(v'+k_y)=u^2+k_y^2 \iff F_y\cdot E_y=u^2+k_y^2.$
$

**Verifying the identity.** We must show:
$$\frac{bq}{b-\ell+p}\cdot\frac{cq}{c-p}=\left(\frac{c(b+c)-\ell p}{s}\right)^2+
\left(\frac{\ell q}{s}\right)^2.$$

Using $\ell p=\frac{\ell^2+c^2-b^2}{2}$ (from $b^2=\ell^2-2\ell p+c^2$):

*Right side numerator:* $c(b+c)-\ell p = \frac{2c(b+c)-\ell^2-c^2+b^2}{2}=\frac{
(b+c)^2-\ell^2}{2}=\frac{s(\ell+b+c)}{2}$.

*Factoring $q^2$:* By Heron's factorization,
$$q^2=c^2-p^2=\frac{4\ell^2c^2-(\ell^2+c^2-b^2)^2}{4\ell^2}=\frac{s(\ell+b+c)\bi
gl(\ell^2-(b-c)^2\bigr)}{4\ell^2}.$$

*Factoring the denominators:*
$$c-p=\frac{s(b+\ell-c)}{2\ell}, \qquad b-\ell+p=\frac{s(\ell+c-b)}{2\ell},$$
so $(b-\ell+p)(c-p)=\frac{s^2(\ell^2-(b-c)^2)}{4\ell^2}$.

**Left side:**
$$\frac{bcq^2}{(b-\ell+p)(c-p)}=\frac{bc\cdot s(\ell+b+c)(\ell^2-(b-c)^2)/(4\ell
^2)}{s^2(\ell^2-(b-c)^2)/(4\ell^2)}=\frac{bc(\ell+b+c)}{s}.$$

**Right side:**
$$\frac{1}{s^2}\left[\frac{s^2(\ell+b+c)^2}{4}+\ell^2 q^2\right]=\frac{1}{s^2}\l
eft[\frac{s^2(\ell+b+c)^2}{4}+\frac{s(\ell+b+c)(\ell^2-(b-c)^2)}{4}\right]$$
$$=\frac{\ell+b+c}{4s}\Bigl[s(\ell+b+c)+\ell^2-(b-c)^2\Bigr].$$

Since $s(\ell+b+c)=(b+c)^2-\ell^2$ and $\ell^2-(b-c)^2=\ell^2-b^2+2bc-c^2$, thei

$$\text{Right side}=\frac{\ell+b+c}{4s}\cdot 4bc=\frac{bc(\ell+b+c)}{s}.$$

Both sides equal $\dfrac{bc(\ell+b+c)}{s}$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

Since $F_y\cdot E_y=u^2+k_y^2$ holds, the reduction gives $\sin(\angle FKE+\angl
