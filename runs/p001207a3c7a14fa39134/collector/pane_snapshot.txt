## Solution

We claim $\Lambda_n = n-1$.

**Key identity.** For positive reals,
$$
\left(\sum_i a_i\right)\!\left(\sum_i \frac1{a_i}\right)-n^2 \;=\; \sum_{1\le i<
j\le n}\frac{(a_i-a_j)^2}{a_i a_j}.
$$
*Proof of identity.* $\bigl(\sum a_i\bigr)\bigl(\sum 1/a_i\bigr)=\sum_{i,j}a_i/a
_j = n+\sum_{i<j}(a_i/a_j+a_j/a_i)$, and $a_i/a_j+a_j/a_i-2=(a_i-a_j)^2/(a_i a_j
)$. ∎

So the desired inequality is equivalent to
$$
\sum_{i<j}\frac{(a_i-a_j)^2}{a_i a_j}\;\ge\;(n-1)\,\frac{(a_{n-1}-a_n)^2}{a_{n-1
}a_n}. \tag{$\star$}
$$

**A monotonicity lemma.** Fix $c>0$ and set $g(x)=\dfrac{(x-c)^2}{x}=x-2c+\dfrac
{c^2}{x}$. Then
$$g'(x)=1-\frac{c^2}{x^2}\ge 0 \quad\text{for } x\ge c,$$
with strict inequality when $x>c$. Hence $g$ is increasing on $[c,\infty)$.

**Proof of $(\star)$.** Since $a_1\ge\cdots\ge a_{n-1}\ge a_n>0$, every $a_i$ ($
i\le n-1$) lies in $[a_n,\infty)$. Applying the lemma with $c=a_n$ and using $a_
i\ge a_{n-1}$,
$$
\frac{(a_i-a_n)^2}{a_i}\;=\;g(a_i)\;\ge\;g(a_{n-1})\;=\;\frac{(a_{n-1}-a_n)^2}{a
_{n-1}},\qquad i=1,\dots,n-1.
$$
Dividing by $a_n>0$ and summing over $i=1,\dots,n-1$,
$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{(a_i-a_n)^2}{a_i a_n}\;\ge\;(n-1)\,\frac{(a_{n-1}-a_n)^2}{
a_{n-1}a_n}.
$$
The full pair-sum $\sum_{i<j}(a_i-a_j)^2/(a_i a_j)$ contains all the pairs $(i,n
)$ ($i=1,\dots,n-1$) plus additional non‑negative terms, so
$$
\sum_{i<j}\frac{(a_i-a_j)^2}{a_i a_j}\;\ge\;\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{(a_i-a_n)^2}{a
_i a_n}\;\ge\;(n-1)\,\frac{(a_{n-1}-a_n)^2}{a_{n-1}a_n}.
$$
This proves $(\star)$, hence $\Lambda_n\ge n-1$.

**Sharpness (equality is attained).** Take $a_1=\cdots=a_{n-1}=t>a_n=1$ for any
$t>1$. Then:
- Pairs among the first $n-1$ entries: all equal, contribute $0$.
- Pair $(n-1,n)$: contributes $(t-1)^2/t$.
- Pairs $(i,n)$ for $i\le n-2$: each contributes $(t-1)^2/t$, identical to the $
(n-1,n)$ term.

So the total is $(n-1)\cdot(t-1)^2/t$, and the gap term is $(t-1)^2/t$. The rati
o is exactly
$$
\frac{(n-1)(t-1)^2/t}{(t-1)^2/t}=n-1.
$$
Thus the inequality is tight for every $t>1$, giving $\Lambda_n\le n-1$.

(Equality condition: $g$ strictly increasing for $x>a_n$ forces $a_1=\cdots=a_{n
-1}$ whenever $a_{n-1}>a_n$, and then all cross-pairs $(i,n)$ equal the gap, con
firming the family above is the complete equality case.)

**Conclusion.** Combining both directions,
$$
\boxed{\Lambda_n = n-1.}
$$
