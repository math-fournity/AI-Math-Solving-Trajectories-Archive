## Proof

**Convention.** Let $\hat{g}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}} g(x)\,e^{-2\pi i x\xi}\,dx$
for $g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$ (the argument is identical under the convention $\h
at g(\xi)=\int g(x)e^{-ix\xi}dx$). The only properties we use are linearity and
the bound $|\hat g(\xi)|\le\|g\|_{L^{1}}$.

---

**Step 1. The series defining $f$ converges absolutely in $L^{1}$.**

Set $a_n=\dfrac{2^{n-1}}{3^{n}}=\dfrac{1}{3}\bigl(\tfrac{2}{3}\bigr)^{n-1}$. The
n

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n \;=\; \frac{1}{3}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(\frac{2}{3}\Bi
gr)^{n-1} \;=\; \frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\frac{2}{3}} \;=\; 1.
$$

Since $\|f_n\|_{L^{1}}\le 1$ for every $n$,

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n\,\|f_n\|_{L^{1}} \;\le\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n \;=\; 1
 \;<\;\infty.
$$

Hence the partial sums $S_N=\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_n f_n$ form a Cauchy sequence in the
 Banach space $L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$, so they converge to some $f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{
R})$ with

$$
\|f\|_{L^{1}} \;\le\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n\,\|f_n\|_{L^{1}} \;\le\; 1.
$$

In particular the pointwise series $\sum a_n f_n(x)$ converges to $f(x)$ for a.e
. $x$, and $\sum a_n|f_n|\in L^{1}$ with $\bigl\|\sum a_n|f_n|\bigr\|_{L^{1}}\le
 1$.

**Step 2. Interchange of sum and integral at $\xi=\xi_0$.**

Because $g(x)=e^{-2\pi i x\xi_0}$ has $|g(x)|=1$, the dominating function

$$
G(x):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n\,|f_n(x)|
$$

is integrable ($\|G\|_{L^{1}}\le 1$) and satisfies $\bigl|a_n f_n(x)e^{-2\pi i x
\xi_0}\bigr|\le a_n|f_n(x)|\le G(x)$. By the Dominated Convergence Theorem appli
ed to the partial sums,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hat f(\xi_0)
&= \int_{\mathbb{R}} f(x)\,e^{-2\pi i x\xi_0}\,dx \\
&= \int_{\mathbb{R}} \Bigl(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n f_n(x)\Bigr) e^{-2\pi i x\xi_
0}\,dx \\
&= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n \int_{\mathbb{R}} f_n(x)\,e^{-2\pi i x\xi_0}\,dx \\
&= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n\,\hat f_n(\xi_0).
\end{aligned}
$$

(Equivalently, this is continuity of the bounded linear operator $\mathcal F:L^{
1}\to C_0$, which sends the $L^{1}$-convergent series $\sum a_n f_n$ to the unif
ormly convergent series $\sum a_n\hat f_n$.)

---

**Step 3. Conclusion.**

By hypothesis $\hat f_n(\xi_0)=0$ for every $n$. Therefore

$$
\hat f(\xi_0) \;=\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n\cdot 0 \;=\; 0.
$$

---

**Answer.** Yes, $\hat f(\xi_0)=0$ holds. The $L^{1}$-absolute convergence of th
