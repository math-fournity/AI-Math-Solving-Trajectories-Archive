## Solution

**Theorem.** A commutative ring $R$ with identity satisfies the condition

> for every $a, b \in R$, either $a \in (b)$ or $b \in (a)$,

if and only if $R$ is a **chain ring**: a ring whose ideals are totally ordered
by inclusion.

---

### Proof

**($\Leftarrow$) Chain ring $\Rightarrow$ the condition.**

If all ideals of $R$ are totally ordered by inclusion, then in particular the pr
incipal ideals are. For any $a, b \in R$, either $(a) \subseteq (b)$ or $(b) \su
bseteq (a)$. The former gives $a \in (b)$; the latter gives $b \in (a)$. ∎

**($\Rightarrow$) The condition $\Rightarrow$ all ideals totally ordered.**

The hypothesis says: for all $a, b \in R$, either $(a) \subseteq (b)$ or $(b) \s
ubseteq (a)$; i.e., the principal ideals form a chain. We show **all** ideals fo
rm a chain.

Let $I, J$ be any two ideals of $R$. Suppose $I \not\subseteq J$; we prove $J \s
ubseteq I$.

Pick $a \in I \setminus J$. For any $b \in J$, the hypothesis gives either $(a)
\subseteq (b)$ or $(b) \subseteq (a)$.

- If $(a) \subseteq (b)$: since $b \in J$ and $J$ is an ideal, $(b) \subseteq J$
, so $a \in J$—contradicting $a \notin J$.
- Therefore $(b) \subseteq (a) \subseteq I$, so $b \in I$.

Since $b \in J$ was arbitrary, $J \subseteq I$. Thus any two ideals are comparab
le, and $R$ is a chain ring. ∎

### Structural characterization of chain rings

A commutative chain ring $R$ (with 1) has the following properties:

**(1) $R$ is local.** If $\mathfrak{m}_1, \mathfrak{m}_2$ are two maximal ideals
, they are comparable, say $\mathfrak{m}_1 \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_2$; by maximal
ity $\mathfrak{m}_1 = \mathfrak{m}_2$.

**(2) Every finitely generated ideal is principal.** Given $(a_1, \dots, a_n)$,
the principal ideals $(a_i)$ are pairwise comparable, so one—say $(a_1)$—contain
s all others. Then $(a_1, \dots, a_n) = (a_1)$.

**(3) The ideals form a chain** $\{0\} = I_0 \subseteq I_1 \subseteq \cdots$ ind
exed by a totally ordered set, with the unique maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m} = \bi
gcup_{a \text{ non-unit}} (a)$ being the set of all non-units.

---

### Classification in the Noetherian case

When $R$ is Noetherian, every ideal is finitely generated, hence principal (by p
roperty (2)), so $R$ is a **local principal ideal ring**. These are completely c
lassified:

- **Field**: $\mathfrak{m} = 0$.
- **DVR** (discrete valuation ring): $R$ is a domain, $\mathfrak{m} = (\pi)$, $\
bigcap_n \mathfrak{m}^n = 0$.
- **Artinian chain ring**: $\mathfrak{m} = (\pi)$, $\mathfrak{m}^n = 0$ for some
 minimal $n \geq 1$, and every ideal is $(\pi^k)$ for $0 \leq k \leq n$. Such a
ring is isomorphic to $V/(\pi^n)$ where $V$ is a DVR with uniformizer $\pi$.

In the non-Noetherian case, chain rings are general (possibly non-domain) **valu
ation rings**: local rings whose value group (or more generally, whose ideal lat
tice) is totally ordered. These include rank-$>1$ valuation rings and valuation
rings with zero divisors.

---

### Summary

The rings satisfying the given condition are precisely the **chain rings**—commu
tative rings with identity in which the ideals are totally ordered by inclusion.
 Equivalently, these are the local rings whose principal ideals form a chain (wh
ich forces *all* ideals to form a chain). In the Noetherian case, they are exact
ly the fields, DVRs, and quotients $V/(\pi^n)$ of DVRs.
