**Key concept — signatures.** For a scan design with $m$ scans $T_1, \ldots, T_m

$$\sigma(i) = \{j : i \in T_j\} \subseteq [m].$$

**Necessity of distinct non-empty signatures.**
- *Non-empty:* If sensor $i$ is never scanned, $f_i$ is completely unknown.
- *Distinct:* If $\sigma(i) = \sigma(i')$, then $i$ and $i'$ appear in exactly t
he same scans. In every scan containing one, both appear, and the unordered outp
ut never separates them. The assignments $(f_i = a, f_{i'} = b)$ and $(f_i = b,
f_{i'} = a)$ are indistinguishable. So distinct signatures are necessary.

**Sufficiency of distinct signatures.** Since all frequencies are distinct reals
, we can track each value across scans by its numerical value. For each value $v
$, compute its signature (the set of scans in which $v$ appears). Match $v$ to t
he unique sensor with that signature. This uniquely determines all assignments.
