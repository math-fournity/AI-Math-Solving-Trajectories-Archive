$$t=\frac{-bc}{ad-bc}.$$

*Intersection with $CD$:* Write $Y=(1-r)C+rD=-c(1-r)\hat{u}-dr\hat{v}$. Matching

$$t'=\frac{ad}{ad-bc}.$$

Therefore
$$|XY|=|t-t'|\cdot|TZ|=\frac{ad+bc}{ad-bc}\,|TZ|,$$
where we used $ad>bc$ (justified below).

**Circumdiameter of $\triangle ETZ$.** Since $\vec{ET}=-(d-b)\hat{v}$ and $\vec{
EZ}=(a-c)\hat{u}$,
$$\cos\angle TEZ=\frac{-(d-b)(a-c)\cos\theta}{(d-b)(a-c)}=-\cos\theta,$$
so $\angle TEZ=\pi-\theta$. By the extended law of sines, the circumdiameter equ

$$\frac{|TZ|}{\sin(\angle TEZ)}=\frac{|TZ|}{\sin\theta}.$$

**Goal:** show $\dfrac{ad+bc}{ad-bc}\le\dfrac{1}{\sin\theta}$, i.e., $\dfrac{ad}

$$\alpha_1+\delta_1=90°-\theta. \tag{$\star$}$$

By the law of sines in $\triangle CDE$ (angle $\theta$ at $E$): $\frac{d}{c}=\fr

$$\frac{R'}{R}=\frac{2}{\sin 2(\theta+p)}-\frac{2}{\sin 2p}=\frac{-4\sin\theta\c

$$R_{\min}=\frac{\tan(45°+\theta/2)}{\tan(45°-\theta/2)}=\tan^2\!\left(45°+\tfra

$$\frac{ad+bc}{ad-bc}=\frac{R+1}{R-1}=1+\frac{2}{R-1}\le 1+\frac{2}{\frac{2\sin\

$$|XY|=\frac{ad+bc}{ad-bc}\,|TZ|\le\frac{|TZ|}{\sin\theta}=\text{diameter of the
