Looking at this problem, I need to find the range of possible perimeters of an e
quilateral pentagon formed by cutting two corners off an equilateral triangle wi
th two straight-line cuts.

## Setup

Let the equilateral triangle have side length 1, with vertices:
- A = (0, 0), B = (1, 0), C = (1/2, √3/2)

By symmetry, removing any two corners is equivalent. WLOG remove corners A and B
, keeping C.

**Cut for A:** segment from P on AB to R on AC.
**Cut for B:** segment from Q on AB to S on BC.

The pentagon has vertices (in order): P, Q, S, C, R, with sides PQ, QS, SC, CR,
RP.

## Parameterization

Let P = (p, 0), Q = (p+ℓ, 0) on AB, so PQ = ℓ.
Let R = (r/2, r√3/2) on AC (AR = r), S = (1−s/2, s√3/2) on BC (BS = s).

Then SC = 1−s and CR = 1−r. Setting all sides equal to ℓ:
- PQ = ℓ gives q − p = ℓ ✓
- SC = ℓ gives s = 1−ℓ
- CR = ℓ gives r = 1−ℓ

So r = s = 1−ℓ. Now:
- R = ((1−ℓ)/2, (1−ℓ)√3/2)
- S = ((1+ℓ)/2, (1−ℓ)√3/2)

## Key equation: RP = QS = ℓ

Computing both distances (they turn out equal by bilateral symmetry about C's al
titude):

$$RP^2 = \left(\frac{1-\ell}{2} - p\right)^2 + \frac{3(1-\ell)^2}{4} = \ell^2$$

Setting $u = \frac{1-\ell}{2} - p$, we need:

$$u^2 = \ell^2 - \frac{3(1-\ell)^2}{4} = \frac{\ell^2 + 6\ell - 3}{4}$$

**Real solution requires:** $\ell^2 + 6\ell - 3 \geq 0$, giving $\ell \geq 2\sqr
t{3} - 3$ (taking the positive root ≈ 0.464).

## Constraints (convex, non-degenerate pentagon)

The pentagon is convex iff $p > 0$ and $p + \ell < 1$ (verified via cross-produc
t signs at all 5 vertices).

With $p = \frac{1-\ell}{2} \pm \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\ell^2 + 6\ell - 3}$ (the ± choi
ces are mirror images giving the same perimeter):

**Lower bound (closed):** At $\ell = 2\sqrt{3}-3$, the discriminant is zero, $p
= (1-\ell)/2 > 0$, and $p+\ell < 1$. Valid. ✓

**Upper bound (open):** $p > 0$ requires $(1-\ell)/2 > \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\ell^2+6
\ell-3}$, which simplifies to $\ell < 1/2$. At $\ell = 1/2$, $p = 0$ (cut passes
 through A — degenerate). Excluded. ✓

## Result

The side length range is $\ell \in [2\sqrt{3}-3,\; 1/2)$.

The perimeter is $5\ell$, so:
$$a = 5(2\sqrt{3}-3), \quad b = \frac{5}{2}$$

$$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{5(2\sqrt{3}-3)}{5/2} = 2(2\sqrt{3}-3) = \boxed{4\sqrt{3}-6
}$$
