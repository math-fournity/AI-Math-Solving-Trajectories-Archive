**Problem.** Determine whether the Jordan measure of any subset of the Cantor se
t is always zero.

**Answer: Yes.** Every subset of the Cantor set is Jordan measurable, and its Jo
rdan measure is zero.

---

**Proof.**

Let $C \subseteq [0,1]$ denote the middle-thirds Cantor set, and let $A \subsete
q C$ be an arbitrary subset. We work with the Jordan (outer/inner) content on $\
mathbb{R}$, using closed intervals as boxes; the argument is identical in higher
 dimensions.

**Step 1 — A finite cover of $C$ of arbitrarily small total length.**

Recall the standard construction of $C$. Set $C_0 = [0,1]$. Having defined $C_{n
-1}$ as a union of $2^{n-1}$ closed intervals each of length $3^{-(n-1)}$, obtai
n $C_n$ by deleting the open middle third of each component. Thus

$$C_n = \bigcup_{k=1}^{2^n} I_{n,k}, \qquad |I_{n,k}| = 3^{-n}, \qquad \sum_{k=1
}^{2^n} |I_{n,k}| = \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^n.$$

By construction $C = \bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty} C_n \subseteq C_n$ for every $n$, so
 the finite family $\{I_{n,k}\}_{k=1}^{2^n}$ is a finite cover of $C$ by interva
ls of total length $(2/3)^n$.

**Step 2 — The outer Jordan content of $C$ is zero.**

The outer Jordan content of a bounded set $S$ is

$$\overline{J}(S) = \inf\Bigl\{\sum_{j=1}^{m} |B_j| : B_1,\dots,B_m \text{ boxes
, } S \subseteq \bigcup_{j=1}^{m} B_j,\ m<\infty\Bigr\}.$$

Since $\{I_{n,k}\}_{k=1}^{2^n}$ is an admissible finite cover of $C$ with total
length $(2/3)^n$,

$$\overline{J}(C) \le \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^n \quad \text{for every } n \ge 0
.$$

Letting $n\to\infty$ gives $\overline{J}(C) = 0$.

**Step 3 — The outer Jordan content of $A$ is zero.**

Because $A \subseteq C \subseteq C_n$, the very same finite cover $\{I_{n,k}\}_{
k=1}^{2^n}$ covers $A$. Hence

$$\overline{J}(A) \le \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^n \quad \text{for every } n,$$

so $\overline{J}(A) = 0$.

**Step 4 — The inner Jordan content of $A$ is zero, and $A$ is Jordan measurable
.**

The inner Jordan content is

$$\underline{J}(A) = \sup\Bigl\{\sum_{j=1}^{m} |B_j| : B_1,\dots,B_m \text{ pair
wise-disjoint boxes with } \bigcup_{j=1}^{m} B_j \subseteq A,\ m<\infty\Bigr\}.$
$

Non-negativity of content gives $\underline{J}(A) \ge 0$, and monotonicity of in
ner content together with Step 3 gives $\underline{J}(A) \le \overline{J}(A) = 0
$. Therefore

$$\underline{J}(A) = \overline{J}(A) = 0.$$

By definition, $A$ is Jordan measurable and its Jordan measure equals $0$.

(Equivalently, one may note that $C$ has empty interior, hence so does $A\subset
eq C$; no interval of positive length fits inside $A$, so $\underline{J}(A)=0$ d
irectly.)

**Conclusion.** Since $A\subseteq C$ was arbitrary, *every* subset of the Cantor
 set is Jordan measurable and has Jordan measure zero. $\blacksquare$
