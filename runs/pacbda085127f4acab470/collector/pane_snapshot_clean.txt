## Proof

**Yes**, $\|T^n\|\to 0$ follows. In fact, the conclusion holds for *any* bounded
 linear operator with spectral radius $<1$; compactness is not needed.

The key tool is **Gelfand's spectral radius formula**: for every bounded linear
operator $T$ on a Banach space,

$$
\rho(T)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|T^n\|^{1/n}=\inf_{n\ge 1}\|T^n\|^{1/n}.
$$

Since $\rho(T)<1$, pick a number $r$ with $\rho(T)<r<1$. By the definition of th
e limit above, there exists $N\in\mathbb{N}$ such that for every $n\ge N$,

$$
\|T^n\|^{1/n}\le r,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \|T^n\|\le r^n.
$$

Because $0<r<1$, we have $r^n\to 0$ as $n\to\infty$. Hence

$$
0\le \|T^n\|\le r^n\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0,
$$

and by the squeeze theorem $\|T^n\|\to 0$.

**Remark on the role of compactness.** The hypothesis that $T$ is compact is sup
erfluous for this particular conclusion: Gelfand's formula is valid for all boun
ded operators, so $\rho(T)<1$ alone forces $\|T^n\|\to 0$. Compactness would be
relevant for finer structural statements (e.g. that every nonzero spectral value
 is an eigenvalue of finite multiplicity, or that the spectrum is at most counta
ble with $0$ as its only possible accumulation point), but it is not required he
re.
