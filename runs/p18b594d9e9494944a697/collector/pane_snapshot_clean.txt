Looking at this problem, I need to find the maximum area of a triangle where the
 incenter is the midpoint of the segment (length 4) joining the centroid and ort
hocenter.

## Setting Up the Key Constraint

Since the centroid $G$ and orthocenter $H$ both lie on the Euler line, their mid
point also lies on the Euler line. The incenter $I$ lies on the Euler line **if
and only if the triangle is isosceles** (a classical result). So the triangle mu
st be isosceles.

## Coordinate Setup

Place the isosceles triangle with base $BC$ horizontal, midpoint of $BC$ at the
origin:
$$B = (-s/2,\, 0),\quad C = (s/2,\, 0),\quad A = (0,\, h)$$

with equal sides $\ell = \sqrt{(s/2)^2 + h^2}$. Set $u = s/(2h)$, so $\ell = h\s
qrt{1+u^2}$.

The key centers (all on the axis of symmetry):
- **Centroid:** $G_y = h/3$
- **Orthocenter:** $H_y = s^2/(4h) = u^2 h$
- **Incenter:** $I_y = \dfrac{sh}{s + 2\ell} = \dfrac{uh}{u + \sqrt{1+u^2}}$

## Applying the Conditions

**Midpoint condition** $I = \frac{G+H}{2}$:
$$\frac{u}{u + \sqrt{1+u^2}} = \frac{1}{2}\!\left(\frac{1}{3} + u^2\right) = \fr
ac{1+3u^2}{6}$$

**Length condition** $|GH| = 4$:
$$h\left|\frac{1}{3} - u^2\right| = 4 \implies h = \frac{4}{|1/3 - u^2|}$$

## Solving the Midpoint Equation

Cross-multiplying and simplifying:
$$u(5 - 3u^2) = (1 + 3u^2)\sqrt{1+u^2}$$

Squaring both sides (with $v = u^2$) and expanding:
$$v(5-3v)^2 = (1+3v)^2(1+v)$$
$$25v - 30v^2 + 9v^3 = 1 + 7v + 15v^2 + 9v^3$$
$$45v^2 - 18v + 1 = 0$$

$$v = \frac{18 \pm \sqrt{324 - 180}}{90} = \frac{18 \pm 12}{90}$$

$$v = \frac{1}{3} \quad \text{or} \quad v = \frac{1}{15}$$

**Checking $v = 1/3$:** This gives $1/3 - u^2 = 0$, so $G_y = H_y$ (centroid coi
ncides with orthocenter), meaning $GH = 0 \neq 4$. This is the **equilateral** c
ase — degenerate for our problem.

**Checking $v = 1/15$:** Both sides of the pre-squared equation equal $\frac{24}
{5\sqrt{15}}$. ✓

## Computing the Area

With $u^2 = 1/15$:
$$h = \frac{4}{4/15} = 15, \quad s = 2uh = \frac{30}{\sqrt{15}} = 2\sqrt{15}$$

$$\ell = 15\sqrt{\frac{16}{15}} = 4\sqrt{15}$$

**Verification:**
- $G_y = 5$, $H_y = 1$, so $|GH| = 4$ ✓
- $I_y = \frac{2\sqrt{15}\cdot 15}{10\sqrt{15}} = 3 = \frac{5+1}{2}$ ✓ (midpoint
 confirmed)

**Area:**
$$K = \frac{1}{2}\, s \cdot h = \frac{1}{2}\cdot 2\sqrt{15}\cdot 15 = 15\sqrt{15
}$$

**Heron's formula check:** $s_{\text{semi}} = 5\sqrt{15}$, and
$$K = \sqrt{5\sqrt{15}\cdot 3\sqrt{15}\cdot \sqrt{15}\cdot \sqrt{15}} = \sqrt{5\
cdot 3\cdot 15\cdot 15} = \sqrt{3375} = 15\sqrt{15}\;\checkmark$$

## Conclusion

The isosceles constraint is forced (incenter on the Euler line $\iff$ isosceles)
, and the midpoint equation yields exactly one non-degenerate solution. The tria
ngle has sides $2\sqrt{15},\; 4\sqrt{15},\; 4\sqrt{15}$.

The maximum possible area is $\boxed{15\sqrt{15}}$.
