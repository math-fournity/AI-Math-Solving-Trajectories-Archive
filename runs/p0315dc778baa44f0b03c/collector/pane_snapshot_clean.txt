## Proof

The series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n$ diverges in the classical sense. The Riemann
zeta function provides its **zeta-regularized** value via analytic continuation,
 and the problem identifies this regularized value with $S = \zeta(-1)$. We comp
ute $\zeta(-1)$.

### Setup

The Riemann zeta function is defined for $\operatorname{Re}(s)>1$ by
$$\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-s}.$$
It admits a meromorphic continuation to all of $\mathbb{C}$, holomorphic except
for a simple pole at $s=1$. The continuation satisfies the **functional equation
**
$$\zeta(s)=2^{s}\,\pi^{s-1}\,\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{\pi s}{2}\Bigr)\,\Gamma(1-s)\,\z
eta(1-s),$$
valid for all $s\neq 1$ (with the pole of $\zeta(1-s)$ at $s=0$ cancelling the z
ero of $\sin$ there).

### Evaluating at $s=-1$

Substitute $s=-1$:

$$\zeta(-1)=2^{-1}\,\pi^{-2}\,\sin\!\Bigl(-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\Bigr)\,\Gamma(2)\,\zet
a(2).$$

Evaluate each factor:

| Factor | Value |
|---|---|
| $2^{-1}$ | $\tfrac{1}{2}$ |
| $\pi^{-2}$ | $\tfrac{1}{\pi^{2}}$ |
| $\sin(-\pi/2)$ | $-1$ |
| $\Gamma(2)=1!$ | $1$ |
| $\zeta(2)$ (Euler) | $\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{6}$ |

The value $\zeta(2)=\pi^{2}/6$ is Euler's classical result, obtained for instanc
e from the Fourier series of $x^{2}$ on $[-\pi,\pi]$ or from the product $\sin(\
pi x)/(\pi x)=\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-x^{2}/n^{2})$ by comparing the $x^{2}$-coefficie
nt.

### Combining

$$\zeta(-1)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\cdot(-1)\cdot 1\cdot\frac{\pi^{2}}
{6}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\cdot\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\cdot(-1)=\frac{1}{2}
\cdot\frac{1}{6}\cdot(-1)=-\frac{1}{12}.$$

### Consistency check (Bernoulli relation)

As an independent verification, the standard identity for non-negative integers
$n$,
$$\zeta(-n)=-\frac{B_{n+1}}{n+1},$$
with the Bernoulli number $B_{2}=1/6$, gives
$$\zeta(-1)=-\frac{B_{2}}{2}=-\frac{1/6}{2}=-\frac{1}{12},$$
agreeing with the functional-equation computation.

### Conclusion

By analytic continuation of the Riemann zeta function and its functional equatio
n, the zeta-regularized value of the divergent series is

$$\boxed{S=\zeta(-1)=-\frac{1}{12}}.$$
