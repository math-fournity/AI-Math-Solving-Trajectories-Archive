## Solution

**Claim:** $\operatorname{Tor}^A_1(A/I,\, A/J) \;\cong\; (I \cap J)\,/\,(I\!\cdo
t\!J)$.

### Proof

Take the standard free resolution of $A/I$ as an $A$-module:
$$
0 \longrightarrow I \xrightarrow{\;\iota\;} A \xrightarrow{\;\pi\;} A/I \longrig
htarrow 0,
$$
where $\iota$ is the inclusion and $\pi$ the quotient map. This is exact since $
A$ is free (hence projective) over itself, and $I = \ker\pi$.

Apply the right-exact functor $-\otimes_A A/J$. We obtain the chain complex
$$
0 \longrightarrow I\otimes_A A/J \xrightarrow{\;\iota\otimes 1\;} A\otimes_A A/J
 \xrightarrow{\;\pi\otimes 1\;} A/I\otimes_A A/J \longrightarrow 0,
$$
whose homology at the leftmost term is $\operatorname{Tor}^A_1(A/I,\, A/J)$. Con
cretely,
$$
\operatorname{Tor}^A_1(A/I,\, A/J) \;=\; \ker(\iota\otimes 1 : I\otimes_A A/J \t
o A\otimes_A A/J).
$$

**Identify the terms.** For any ideal $K\subseteq A$, the natural map $K\otimes_
A A/J \to K/(K\!\cdot\!J)$ sending $k\otimes \bar a \mapsto \overline{ka}$ is an
 isomorphism (it is the canonical isomorphism $K\otimes_A (A/J) \cong K/JK$, wit
h $JK = J\!\cdot\!K$). Likewise $A\otimes_A A/J \cong A/J$.

Under these identifications the map $\iota\otimes 1$ becomes
$$
\widetilde\iota : I/(I\!\cdot\!J) \longrightarrow A/J, \qquad \bar i \;=\; i + I
\!\cdot\!J \;\longmapsto\; i + J,
$$
i.e. it is induced by the inclusion $I\hookrightarrow A$ followed by reduction m
odulo $J$.

**Compute the kernel.** An element $i + I\!\cdot\!J \in I/(I\!\cdot\!J)$ lies in
 $\ker\widetilde\iota$ iff $i + J = 0$ in $A/J$, i.e. iff $i\in J$. Since we alr
eady have $i\in I$, this is the condition $i\in I\cap J$. Quotienting by $I\!\cd
ot\!J$ (which is contained in $I\cap J$), we get
$$
\ker\widetilde\iota \;=\; \{\,i + I\!\cdot\!J : i\in I\cap J\,\} \;=\; (I\cap J)
/(I\!\cdot\!J).
$$

Therefore
$$
\boxed{\;\operatorname{Tor}^A_1(A/I,\, A/J) \;\cong\; (I\cap J)\,/\,(I\!\cdot\!J
)\;}
$$

as $A$-modules (and in fact as $A/(I+J)$-modules, since both $I$ and $J$ annihil
ate it). $\blacksquare$
