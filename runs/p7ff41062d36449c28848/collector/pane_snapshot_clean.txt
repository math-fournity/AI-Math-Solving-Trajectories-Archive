Looking at this problem, I need to count ordered 4-tuples of subsets of $\{0,1,2
\}$ that generate the entire Boolean algebra $2^{\{0,1,2\}}$ under union and int
ersection (without complement).

## Setting Up the Framework

**The generated sublattice.** Given generators $S_1, S_2, S_3, S_4$, the sublatt
ice they generate under $\cup$ and $\cap$ consists of all elements of the form $
\bigcup_{I \in \mathcal{F}} \bigcap_{k \in I} S_k$ for $\mathcal{F} \subseteq 2^
{\{1,2,3,4\}}$, where $\bigcap_{\emptyset} = \{0,1,2\}$ and $\bigcup_{\emptyset}
 = \emptyset$.

**Key observation:** $\emptyset$ (take $\mathcal{F} = \emptyset$) and $\{0,1,2\}
$ (take $\mathcal{F} = \{\emptyset\}$) are *always* generated. So we only need e
very singleton $\{i\}$ to be generated.

**Singletons require intersections.** A singleton $\{i\}$ can only be a union of
 "intersection-blocks" $M_j = \bigcap_{k \in I} S_k$ if $\{i\}$ equals one of th
e $M_j$ (since the only subsets of $\{i\}$ are $\emptyset$ and $\{i\}$). So each
 $\{i\}$ must be expressible as an intersection of some subset of the generators
.

**Necessary and sufficient condition.** For each point $i$, $\{i\} = \bigcap_{k
\in I_i} S_k$ for some $I_i$ iff: for every $j \neq i$, there exists a generator
 $S_k$ with $i \in S_k$ and $j \notin S_k$.

## Translating to Types

Define the **type** of point $i$ as $t_i = (\mathbf{1}_{i \in S_1}, \mathbf{1}_{
i \in S_2}, \mathbf{1}_{i \in S_3}, \mathbf{1}_{i \in S_4}) \in \{0,1\}^4$.

The condition "for each $j \neq i$, some generator contains $i$ but not $j$" mea
ns $t_i \not\leq t_j$ (componentwise) for all $j \neq i$. By symmetry over all p
airs, this means **$t_0, t_1, t_2$ are pairwise incomparable** — they form an **
antichain** in $\{0,1\}^4$.

**Bijection:** Each ordered 4-tuple $(S_1,S_2,S_3,S_4)$ corresponds bijectively
to an ordered triple $(t_0, t_1, t_2) \in (\{0,1\}^4)^3$ (via $S_k = \{i : t_i^k
 = 1\}$). So we need to count ordered triples forming an antichain.

## Counting Antichain Triples in $\{0,1\}^4$

I count unordered antichains of size 3, then multiply by $3! = 6$.

Elements of $\{0,1\}^4$ have ranks $0,1,2,3,4$ with counts $1,4,6,4,1$. Rank-0 (
bottom) and rank-4 (top) are comparable to everything, so no antichain of size 3
 includes them. We use only ranks 1, 2, 3. Two same-rank elements are always inc
omparable; cross-rank incomparability requires the lower-rank element to *not* b
e a subset of the higher-rank one.

| Profile | Count | Reasoning |
|---------|-------|-----------|
| $(1,1,1)$ | $\binom{4}{3}=4$ | All rank-1 pairwise incomparable |
| $(2,2,2)$ | $\binom{6}{3}=20$ | All rank-2 pairwise incomparable |
| $(3,3,3)$ | $\binom{4}{3}=4$ | All rank-3 pairwise incomparable |
| $(1,1,2)$ | $6$ | Choose 2 rank-1 elements $\{a,b\}$ ($\binom{4}{2}=6$); rank-
2 must avoid both → 1 choice |
| $(1,2,2)$ | $12$ | Two rank-2 elements with $|e_1\cap e_2|=1$ give $|e_1\cup e
_2|=3$, leaving 1 valid rank-1 element; 12 such pairs |
| $(2,2,3)$ | $12$ | Rank-3 element $g$ with $[4]\setminus g = x$; both rank-2 e
lements must contain $x$: $4 \times \binom{3}{2} = 12$ |
| $(2,3,3)$ | $6$ | Two rank-3 elements $g_1,g_2$ with complements $x_1\neq x_2$
; rank-2 must be $\{x_1,x_2\}$: $\binom{4}{2}=6$ |
| $(1,1,3)$ | $0$ | Rank-3 avoiding 2 rank-1 elements needs a 3-subset of a 2-el
ement set: impossible |
| $(1,3,3)$ | $0$ | Forces $g_1=g_2$, not distinct |
| $(1,2,3)$ | $0$ | $f \notin e$ and $e \not\subseteq g$ with $f=[4]\setminus g$
 gives $x \notin e$ and $x \in e$: contradiction |

**Verification of (1,2,2) = 12:** Unordered pairs of 2-subsets with $|e_1 \cap e
_2| = 1$: choose common element (4 ways), choose the two distinct non-common ele
ments from remaining 3 and assign ($\binom{3}{2}\cdot 2!/2 = 3$), giving $4 \tim
es 3 = 12$. Each leaves exactly 1 valid rank-1 element. ✓

**Verification of (2,2,3) = 12:** Choose $x = [4]\setminus g$ (4 choices), then
choose 2 rank-2 elements containing $x$ from $\binom{3}{1}=3$ available: $\binom
{3}{2}=3$. Total $4\times 3=12$. ✓

**Total unordered antichains:** $4 + 20 + 4 + 6 + 12 + 12 + 6 = \mathbf{64}$.

**Ordered triples:** $64 \times 6 = \mathbf{384}$.

## Cross-Check via Inclusion-Exclusion

Total ordered triples: $16^3 = 4096$. Let $A_{ij}$ = triples where $t_i, t_j$ ar
e comparable.

- **Comparable ordered pairs** $C = \sum_{k=0}^{4}\binom{4}{k}(2^k+2^{4-k}-1) =
146$, so $|A_{ab}| = 146 \times 16 = 2336$.
- **$|A_{ab}\cap A_{ac}|$** $= \sum_{k=0}^{4}\binom{4}{k}(2^k+2^{4-k}-1)^2 = 256
+324+294+324+256 = 1454$.
- **Chain triples** (all pairwise comparable): $N_3 = \sum_k \binom{4}{k}(2^k-1)
(2^{4-k}-1) = 110$ (strict 3-chains); strict unordered comparable pairs $P = (14
6-16)/2 = 65$; chain triples $= 6\cdot110 + 6\cdot65 + 16 = 1066$.

**Inclusion-exclusion:** $|A_{ab}\cup A_{ac}\cup A_{bc}| = 3(2336) - 3(1454) + 1
066 = 7008 - 4362 + 1066 = 3712$.

**Antichain triples** $= 4096 - 3712 = \mathbf{384}$. ✓

**Validation on smaller cases:** For $\{0,1\}^2$ (max antichain size 2), the for
mula gives 0. ✓ For $\{0,1\}^3$, it gives 12 = $2 \times 6$ (the two antichains
$\{100,010,001\}$ and $\{110,101,011\}$, each with $3!$ orderings). ✓

## Answer

$$\boxed{384}$$
