The well-posed core of the problem is:

> Let $a_n=\int_0^1 x^n e^x\,dx$. Evaluate $\displaystyle L=\lim_{N\to\infty}\fr
ac{\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_n}{\ln N}$.

(Here the dummy index in the sum is $n$ and the outer limit variable is $N$; I u
se $N$ for the upper limit to avoid clash of notation.)

---

### Step 1. A sharp asymptotic for $a_n$

Write
$$
a_n=\int_0^1 x^n e^x\,dx = e\int_0^1 x^n e^{x-1}\,dx.
$$
The "main term" comes from replacing $e^{x-1}$ by $1$:
$$
\frac{e}{n+1}=e\int_0^1 x^n\,dx.
$$
We bound the error. For $x\in[0,1]$, using $e^u\ge 1+u$ with $u=x-1$ gives $e^{x
-1}\ge x$, hence
$$
0\le 1-e^{x-1}\le 1-x.
$$
Therefore
$$
\left|a_n-\frac{e}{n+1}\right|
= e\left|\int_0^1 x^n\bigl(e^{x-1}-1\bigr)\,dx\right|
\le e\int_0^1 x^n(1-x)\,dx
= e\cdot\frac{1}{(n+1)(n+2)}.
$$
So, for every $n\ge 1$,
$$
\boxed{\;a_n=\frac{e}{n+1}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{n^2}\right)\;},\qquad
\left|a_n-\frac{e}{n+1}\right|\le \frac{e}{(n+1)(n+2)}.
$$

### Step 2. Summation

Let $S_N=\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_n$. Using the estimate above,
$$
S_N = e\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n+1} + R_N,
\qquad
|R_N|\le \sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{e}{(n+1)(n+2)}.
$$
The error telescopes:
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{(n+1)(n+2)}
=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\frac{1}{n+1}-\frac{1}{n+2}\right)
=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{N+2}<\frac12.
$$
Hence $R_N=O(1)$ (bounded, in fact $|R_N|<e/2$).

For the main term, with $H_m=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac1j$ the harmonic numbers,
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n+1}=H_{N+1}-1.
$$
Thus
$$
S_N = e\,(H_{N+1}-1)+O(1).
$$

### Step 3. The limit

Using the classical asymptotic $H_{N+1}=\ln(N+1)+\gamma+o(1)=\ln N+\gamma+o(1)$,
$$
\frac{S_N}{\ln N}
= e\,\frac{H_{N+1}-1}{\ln N}+\frac{O(1)}{\ln N}
\;\longrightarrow\; e\cdot 1 + 0 = e.
$$

Therefore
$$
\boxed{\displaystyle \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_n}{\ln N}=e.}
$$
