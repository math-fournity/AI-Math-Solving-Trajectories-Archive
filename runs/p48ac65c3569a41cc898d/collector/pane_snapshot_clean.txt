## Proof

Let $L$ be a Lie algebra over a field $k$, and $U(L)$ its universal enveloping a
lgebra, equipped with the standard Hopf algebra structure in which every $x \in
L$ is **primitive**:
$$\Delta(x) = x \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes x, \qquad \varepsilon(x) = 0, \qquad \vare
psilon(1) = 1.$$

An element $g \in U(L)$ is **grouplike** if $\Delta(g) = g \otimes g$ (and $g \n
eq 0$, equivalently $\varepsilon(g)=1$). We show the only grouplike element is $
1$.

---

### Filtration and associated graded

Let $U_0 \subset U_1 \subset U_2 \subset \cdots$ be the canonical filtration of
$U(L)$, where $U_i$ is the $k$-span of monomials $x_1 \cdots x_m$ with $m \le i$
 (and $U_0 = k\cdot 1$). By the PBW theorem,
$$\operatorname{gr} U(L) \cong S(L),$$
the symmetric algebra. The coproduct respects the filtration:
$$\Delta(U_n) \subseteq \sum_{p+q=n} U_p \otimes U_q,$$
so on the associated graded it induces a coproduct on $S(L)$ in which every $v \
in L$ is primitive. Concretely, for a homogeneous element $a \in S^n(L)$,
$$\operatorname{gr}(\Delta)(a) \in \bigoplus_{p+q=n} S^p(L)\otimes S^q(L),$$
i.e. **every component of $\Delta(a)$ has total degree exactly $n$**.

---

### Degree argument

Write the image of $g$ in $\operatorname{gr} U(L) = S(L)$ as
$$\bar g = \bar g_0 + \bar g_1 + \cdots + \bar g_n, \qquad \bar g_i \in S^i(L),\
quad \bar g_n \neq 0,$$
where $n$ is the highest degree appearing. The equation $\Delta(g)=g\otimes g$ d
escends to the associated graded:
$$\operatorname{gr}(\Delta)(\bar g) = \bar g \otimes \bar g.$$

**Left-hand side.** Since $\operatorname{gr}(\Delta)(\bar g_i)$ has total degree
 $i$, the maximum total degree appearing on the LHS is $n$.

**Right-hand side.** The term $\bar g_n \otimes \bar g_n$ has total degree $2n$.

If $n \ge 1$, then $2n > n$, so the degree-$(n,n)$ component of the RHS, namely
$\bar g_n \otimes \bar g_n$, has no matching term on the LHS. Hence
$$\bar g_n \otimes \bar g_n = 0 \implies \bar g_n = 0,$$
contradicting the definition of $n$ as the top degree. Therefore **$n = 0$**, i.
e. $g \in U_0 = k\cdot 1$.

---

### Conclusion

So $g = c\cdot 1$ for some $c \in k$. The grouplike identity gives
$$\Delta(c\cdot 1) = c\,(1\otimes 1) = c^2\,(1\otimes 1) = (c\cdot 1)\otimes(c\c
dot 1),$$
hence $c = c^2$, so $c \in \{0, 1\}$. The counit condition $\varepsilon(g)=1$ (e
quivalently, the requirement that a grouplike element be nonzero) forces $c = 1$
.

Therefore the set of grouplike elements of $U(L)$ is
$$\boxed{\{1\}}.$$
