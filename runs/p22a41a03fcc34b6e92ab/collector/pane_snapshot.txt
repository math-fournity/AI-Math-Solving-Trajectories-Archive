## Frobenius Method Solution of Bessel's Equation

**Equation.** For integer order $n \ge 0$:
$$x^{2} f''(x) + x f'(x) + (x^{2}-n^{2})f(x) = 0.$$

Dividing by $x^{2}$ (the singular point is at $x=0$):
$$f'' + \tfrac{1}{x}f' + \Big(1-\tfrac{n^{2}}{x^{2}}\Big)f = 0.$$

Here $x=0$ is a **regular singular point** since $xp(x)=1$ and $x^{2}q(x)=x^{2}-
n^{2}$ are both analytic at $x=0$. The Frobenius method applies.

---

### Step 1. Frobenius ansatz

Assume
$$f(x) = x^{r}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} a_{k} x^{k} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} a_{k} x^{k+r
}, \qquad a_{0}\ne 0.$$

Compute derivatives:
$$f'(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} (k+r)\,a_{k}\,x^{k+r-1},$$
$$f''(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} (k+r)(k+r-1)\,a_{k}\,x^{k+r-2}.$$

Substitute into $x^{2}f'' + xf' + (x^{2}-n^{2})f = 0$:

- $x^{2}f'' = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} (k+r)(k+r-1)\,a_{k}\,x^{k+r}$,
- $xf' = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} (k+r)\,a_{k}\,x^{k+r}$,
- $x^{2}f = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} a_{k}\,x^{k+r+2} = \sum_{k=2}^{\infty} a_{k-2}\,
x^{k+r}$,
- $-n^{2}f = -\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} n^{2}\,a_{k}\,x^{k+r}$.

Combining the first, second, and fourth terms (all with power $x^{k+r}$):
$$\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big[(k+r)(k+r-1)+(k+r)-n^{2}\Big]a_{k}\,x^{k+r} + \sum_{k=
2}^{\infty} a_{k-2}\,x^{k+r} = 0.$$

Simplifying the bracket:
$$(k+r)(k+r-1)+(k+r) = (k+r)^{2},$$
so
$$\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big[(k+r)^{2}-n^{2}\Big]a_{k}\,x^{k+r} + \sum_{k=2}^{\inft
y} a_{k-2}\,x^{k+r} = 0.$$

---

### Step 2. Indicial equation (coefficient of $x^{r}$, i.e. $k=0$)

Setting $k=0$ with $a_{0}\ne 0$:
$$\big[r^{2}-n^{2}\big]a_{0} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; r^{2}-n^{2}=0.$$

**Indicial roots:**
$$\boxed{r = \pm\, n.}$$

The roots differ by the integer $2n$, so the two roots may lead to linearly depe
ndent or one divergent solution; we treat $r=+n$ first (giving $J_{n}$), and $r=
-n$ gives $J_{-n}$ which for integer $n$ is dependent on $J_{n}$.

---

### Step 3. Recurrence relation

**$k=1$ coefficient** (the $x^{k+r+2}$ sum starts at $k=2$, so only the first su
m contributes):
$$\big[(1+r)^{2}-n^{2}\big]a_{1} = 0.$$

With $r=n$: $[(n+1)^{2}-n^{2}]a_{1}=(2n+1)a_{1}=0$, so $a_{1}=0$.
With $r=-n$: $[(1-n)^{2}-n^{2}]a_{1}=(1-2n)a_{1}=0$, so for $n\ne \tfrac12$, $a_
{1}=0$.

**$k\ge 2$ coefficient:** equating the total coefficient of $x^{k+r}$ to zero:
$$\big[(k+r)^{2}-n^{2}\big]a_{k} + a_{k-2} = 0,$$

giving the **recurrence relation**
$$\boxed{\;a_{k} = -\,\frac{a_{k-2}}{(k+r)^{2}-n^{2}}, \qquad k\ge 2.\;}$$

---

### Step 4. Solution for $r = +n$ (the Bessel function $J_{n}$)

With $r=n$:
$$(k+n)^{2}-n^{2} = k(k+2n),$$
so
$$a_{k} = -\,\frac{a_{k-2}}{k(k+2n)}, \qquad k\ge 2.$$

Since $a_{1}=0$, the recurrence forces **all odd coefficients to vanish**: $a_{3
}=a_{5}=\cdots=0$.

For even indices, write $k=2m$ ($m\ge 1$):
$$a_{2m} = -\,\frac{a_{2m-2}}{2m(2m+2n)} = -\,\frac{a_{2m-2}}{4m(m+n)}.$$

Iterating:
$$a_{2m} = \frac{(-1)^{m}\,a_{0}}{4^{m}\,m!\,(m+n)(m-1+n)\cdots(1+n)} = \frac{(-
1)^{m}\,a_{0}}{4^{m}\,m!\,\dfrac{(m+n)!}{n!}} = \frac{(-1)^{m}\,a_{0}\,n!}{4^{m}
\,m!\,(m+n)!}.$$

Choosing the conventional normalization $a_{0} = \dfrac{1}{2^{n}\,n!}$:
$$a_{2m} = \frac{(-1)^{m}}{2^{n+2m}\,m!\,(m+n)!}.$$

Therefore
$$\boxed{\;J_{n}(x) = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!\,(m+n)!}\Big(\frac{
x}{2}\Big)^{2m+n}\;}$$

is the **Bessel function of the first kind of order $n$**. The ratio test gives
convergence for all finite $x$.

---

### Step 5. The second solution $r=-n$

With $r=-n$ the recurrence becomes
$$a_{k} = -\,\frac{a_{k-2}}{k(k-2n)}, \qquad k\ge 2.$$

For integer $n\ge 1$ the denominator **vanishes at $k=2n$**, so the recurrence b
reaks down — the second Frobenius solution is not a pure Frobenius series; a log
arithmic term appears, yielding the **Bessel function of the second kind** $Y_{n
}(x)$.

For $n=0$ the two indicial roots coincide ($r=0$ double root), again forcing a l
ogarithmic second solution $Y_{0}(x)$.

Formally, for non-integer order $\nu$ (replacing $n$ by $\nu\notin\mathbb{Z}$),
$r=-\nu$ gives the independent solution
$$J_{-\nu}(x) = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!\,(m-\nu)!}\Big(\frac{x}{2}
\Big)^{2m-\nu},$$
and $J_{\nu}, J_{-\nu}$ are linearly independent.
