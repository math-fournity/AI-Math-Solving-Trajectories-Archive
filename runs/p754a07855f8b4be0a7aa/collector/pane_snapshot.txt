Yes, $\varphi^*$ is injective. Here is the proof.

**Claim.** Let $X, Y$ be affine varieties over an algebraically closed field $k$
, and $\varphi: X \to Y$ a morphism. Then $\varphi$ is dominant if and only if $
\varphi^*: A(Y) \to A(X)$ is injective.

**Proof.**

Recall that for a morphism $\varphi: X \to Y$ of affine varieties, the pullback
$\varphi^*: A(Y) \to A(X)$ is defined by $\varphi^*(f) = f \circ \varphi$. We co
mpute its kernel:

$$\ker \varphi^* = \{\, f \in A(Y) : f \circ \varphi = 0 \text{ in } A(X) \,\}.$
$

The condition $f \circ \varphi = 0 \in A(X)$ means that $f$ vanishes at every po
int of $\varphi(X) \subseteq Y$. Since $f$ is a regular function (a polynomial f
unction on $Y$), its vanishing set $V(f) = \{y \in Y : f(y) = 0\}$ is Zariski-cl
osed in $Y$. Therefore $f$ vanishes on $\varphi(X)$ if and only if $f$ vanishes
on the Zariski closure $\overline{\varphi(X)}$. Hence

$$\ker \varphi^* = I\!\left(\overline{\varphi(X)}\right),$$

where $I(Z) = \{f \in A(Y) : f|_Z = 0\}$ denotes the ideal of a subset $Z \subse
teq Y$ inside the coordinate ring $A(Y)$.

Now we use the two directions:

- **Dominant $\Rightarrow$ injective.** If $\varphi$ is dominant, then $\overlin
e{\varphi(X)} = Y$, so $\ker \varphi^* = I(Y)$. But $I(Y) = 0$ inside $A(Y) = k[
Y]$, since $A(Y)$ is by definition the quotient $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]/I(Y)$, in whic
h the ideal of $Y$ itself has been modded out. Thus $\ker \varphi^* = 0$, and $\
varphi^*$ is injective.

- **Injective $\Rightarrow$ dominant** (for completeness). If $\varphi^*$ is inj
ective, then $I(\overline{\varphi(X)}) = \ker \varphi^* = 0$. Suppose for contra
diction that $\overline{\varphi(X)} \subsetneq Y$ is a proper closed subset. The
n $\overline{\varphi(X)} = V(J)$ for some nonzero ideal $J$ of $A(Y)$. By the Nu
llstellensatz (applied in the affine variety $Y$), any nonzero $g \in J$ satisfi
es $g \in I(\overline{\varphi(X)})$, contradicting $I(\overline{\varphi(X)}) = 0
$. Hence $\overline{\varphi(X)} = Y$, i.e., $\varphi$ is dominant.

Therefore, since $\varphi$ is given to be dominant, the induced map $\varphi^*:
A(Y) \to A(X)$ is **injective**. $\blacksquare$
