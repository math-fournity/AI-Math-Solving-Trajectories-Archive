## Proof

We construct three points with rational coordinates on the curve $\Gamma: y^2 =
x^3 + bx + b^2$ (with $b \in \mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\}$) and verify they form a n
on-degenerate triangle.

**Step 1: Two rational points on $\Gamma$.**

Setting $x = 0$ gives $y^2 = b^2$, hence $y = \pm b$. Since $b \in \mathbb{Q}$,
both
$$P_1 = (0,\, b), \qquad P_2 = (0,\, -b)$$
are rational points on $\Gamma$. They are distinct because $b \neq 0$.

**Step 2: A third rational point via the tangent at $P_1$.**

Differentiating $y^2 = x^3 + bx + b^2$ implicitly:
$$2y\,y' = 3x^2 + b, \qquad y' = \frac{3x^2+b}{2y}.$$
At $P_1 = (0,b)$ the slope is $y' = \dfrac{b}{2b} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so the tangen
t line is
$$y = \tfrac{1}{2}x + b.$$

Substituting into the curve equation:
$$\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}x + b\bigr)^2 = x^3 + bx + b^2,$$
$$\tfrac{1}{4}x^2 + bx + b^2 = x^3 + bx + b^2,$$
$$\tfrac{1}{4}x^2 = x^3,$$
$$x^2\bigl(\tfrac{1}{4} - x\bigr) = 0.$$

The double root $x = 0$ corresponds to the point of tangency $P_1$; the third in
tersection has
$$x = \tfrac{1}{4}, \qquad y = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\tfrac{1}{4} + b = \tfrac{1}{8}
+ b.$$

**Verification.** With $x = \tfrac{1}{4}$ and $y = b + \tfrac{1}{8}$:
$$\text{LHS} = \bigl(b + \tfrac{1}{8}\bigr)^2 = b^2 + \tfrac{b}{4} + \tfrac{1}{6
4},$$
$$\text{RHS} = \bigl(\tfrac{1}{4}\bigr)^3 + b\cdot\tfrac{1}{4} + b^2 = \tfrac{1}
{64} + \tfrac{b}{4} + b^2.$$
LHS $=$ RHS. ✓

Thus
$$P_3 = \Bigl(\tfrac{1}{4},\, b + \tfrac{1}{8}\Bigr)$$
is a rational point on $\Gamma$ (all coordinates are rational since $b \in \math
bb{Q}$).

**Step 3: The three points form a non-degenerate triangle.**

The points $P_1 = (0,b)$ and $P_2 = (0,-b)$ both lie on the vertical line $x = 0
$. The point $P_3 = (\tfrac{1}{4},\, b + \tfrac{1}{8})$ has $x$-coordinate $\tfr
ac{1}{4} \neq 0$, so it does **not** lie on the line $x = 0$. Hence $P_1, P_2, P
_3$ are non-collinear.

Moreover, $P_3 \neq P_1$ and $P_3 \neq P_2$ (their $x$-coordinates differ), and
$P_1 \neq P_2$ (since $b \neq 0$). Therefore the three points are pairwise disti
nct and non-collinear, forming a genuine triangle.

**Conclusion.** For every nonzero rational $b$, the triangle with vertices
$$\boxed{(0,\,b),\quad (0,\,-b),\quad \Bigl(\tfrac{1}{4},\, b + \tfrac{1}{8}\Big
r)}$$
is inscribed in $\Gamma$, and all its vertices have rational coordinates.
