# Proof

Let the vertices of the convex polygon be $v_1, v_2, \dots, v_n$ in cyclic order
. For a triangulation $T$, let $d_T(v_i)$ denote the **degree** of $v_i$: the nu
mber of triangles of $T$ incident to $v_i$. We control parities of $d_T(v_i)$.

A triangulation of an $n$-gon has $n-2$ triangles, so
$$\sum_{i=1}^n d_T(v_i) = 3(n-2) = 3n-6. \tag{$\star$}$$

## The key gadget: inserting three vertices

The following construction lets us pass from a triangulation of an $m$-gon to a
triangulation of an $(m+3)$-gon while **preserving the parity pattern** of all o
ld vertices and making the three new vertices odd.

**Construction.** Let $P$ be a convex $m$-gon with vertices $p_1,\dots,p_m$ and
a triangulation $T$. Pick a boundary edge, say $p_1p_2$. Form a new convex $(m{+
}3)$-gon $P'$ by replacing the edge $p_1p_2$ with the path $p_1, q_1, q_2, q_3,
p_2$ (place $q_1,q_2,q_3$ on the boundary arc so $P'$ stays convex). Draw the di
agonal $p_1p_2$; this splits $P'$ into

- the $m$-gon $\Pi_1 = (p_1,p_2,p_3,\dots,p_m)$, and
- the pentagon $\Pi_2 = (p_1,q_1,q_2,q_3,p_2)$.

Triangulate $\Pi_1$ using $T$. Triangulate $\Pi_2$ by the **fan from $q_2$** (di
agonals $q_2p_1$ and $q_2p_2$), producing triangles
$$(p_1,q_1,q_2),\quad (p_1,q_2,p_2),\quad (q_2,q_3,p_2).$$

This is a valid triangulation of $P'$: the two regions are convex, the diagonals
 lie in separate regions, and the total triangle count is $(m-2)+3 = (m+3)-2$. ✓

**Parity analysis of the gadget.** In the pentagon $\Pi_2$ the degrees are
$$d(p_1)=2,\quad d(q_1)=1,\quad d(q_2)=3,\quad d(q_3)=1,\quad d(p_2)=2.$$
In $\Pi_1$ the degrees are exactly those given by $T$. Hence in $P'$:
- $p_1$ and $p_2$ each gain $+2$ — **parity unchanged**;
- $q_1,q_2,q_3$ have degrees $1,3,1$ — **all odd**;
- every other $p_i$ ($i\ge 3$) is unchanged.

> **Lemma (parity preservation).** Applying the gadget to any triangulation pres
erves the parity of every old vertex and gives the three new vertices odd degree
. In particular: a triangulation with all vertices odd yields one with all verti
ces odd; a triangulation with exactly two even vertices yields one with exactly
two even vertices.

## Base cases

2),(2,1,3,1,2),(2,2,1,3,1),(1,2,2,1,3)$, each with **exactly two even**. ✓

## Part (a): $n = 3k$ — all vertices odd

We prove by induction on $k$ that a convex $3k$-gon admits a triangulation with
all degrees odd.

**Base $k=1$ ($n=3$):** established above.

**Inductive step $3k \to 3(k{+}1)$:** By hypothesis the $3k$-gon has a triangula

. The result is a triangulation of the $(3k{+}3)$-gon with **all degrees odd**.
✓

This covers $n = 3,6,9,\dots$, i.e. every $n=3k$. $\square$
