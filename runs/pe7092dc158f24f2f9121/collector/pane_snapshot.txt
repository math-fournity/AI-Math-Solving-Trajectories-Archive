**Proof.** We exhibit a non-commutative ring $A$ with $A \cong A^{\mathrm{op}}$.

Let $k$ be any field and let $A = M_2(k)$, the ring of $2\times 2$ matrices over
 $k$. This ring is non-commutative: for instance,
$$
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{
pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{
pmatrix}0&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
$$
so the two products differ.

We claim $A \cong A^{\mathrm{op}}$ as rings. Consider the transpose map
$$
T \colon M_2(k) \longrightarrow M_2(k)^{\mathrm{op}}, \qquad T(M) = M^{\mathsf T
}.
$$

- **Additivity:** $T(M+N) = (M+N)^{\mathsf T} = M^{\mathsf T}+N^{\mathsf T} = T(
M)+T(N)$.
- **Multiplicativity (in the opposite ring):** Recall that multiplication in $A^
{\mathrm{op}}$ is defined by $X * Y := YX$ (the product in $A$, reversed). Using
 $(MN)^{\mathsf T} = N^{\mathsf T}M^{\mathsf T}$,
$$
T(MN) = (MN)^{\mathsf T} = N^{\mathsf T}M^{\mathsf T} = T(M) * T(N).
$$
- **Unitality:** $T(I_2) = I_2^{\mathsf T} = I_2$, which is the unit of $A^{\mat
hrm{op}}$.
- **Bijectivity:** Transpose is its own inverse up to identification: $(M^{\math
sf T})^{\mathsf T} = M$, so $T$ is a bijection (involutive).

Hence $T$ is a ring isomorphism $A \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} A^{\mathrm{op}}$.

We have produced a non-commutative ring $A = M_2(k)$ with $A \cong A^{\mathrm{op
}}$. Therefore $A \cong A^{\mathrm{op}}$ does **not** force $A$ to be commutativ
e.
