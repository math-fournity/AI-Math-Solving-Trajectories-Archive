## Solution

**Answer:** The only positive integer $n > 3$ satisfying the conditions is $\box
ed{n = 4}$.

---

### Part 1: $n = 4$ always works

Let $A_1, A_2, A_3, A_4$ be any four points in general position (no three collin
ear). Define
$$T = S_{123} + S_{124} + S_{134} + S_{234}, \qquad r_i = \tfrac{T}{3} - S_{\wid
ehat{i}},$$
where $S_{\widehat{i}}$ denotes the area of the triangle not involving $A_i$ (e.
g. $r_1 = T/3 - S_{234}$). Then
$$r_i + r_j + r_k = T - (S_{\widehat{i}} + S_{\widehat{j}} + S_{\widehat{k}}) =
T - (T - S_{ijk}) = S_{ijk},$$
since the three "complement" areas sum to $T - S_{ijk}$. The $r_i$ are well-defi
ned real numbers (possibly negative, which is allowed), and $r_i + r_j + r_k = S
_{ijk} > 0$ automatically. Hence **$n = 4$ is achievable**.

---

### Part 2: $n \geq 5$ is impossible

#### Step A (Algebra): For $n = 5$, all $r_i$ are forced equal.

Suppose a solution exists for five points. Each 4-subset determines the same $r$
-values. Comparing $r_1$ computed from $\{1,2,3,4\}$ and from $\{1,2,3,5\}$:
$$\frac{S_{123}+S_{124}+S_{134}-S_{234}}{3} = \frac{S_{123}+S_{125}+S_{135}-S_{2
35}}{3}.$$
Substituting $S_{ijk} = r_i+r_j+r_k$, the left side equals $\tfrac{2r_1+r_4}{3}\
cdot 3$-adjusted… directly: LHS $= 2r_1 + r_4$, RHS $= 2r_1 + r_5$, giving $r_4
= r_5$.

Similarly, comparing $r_1$ from $\{1,2,3,4\}$ with $\{1,2,4,5\}$ gives $r_3 = r_
5$; comparing with $\{1,3,4,5\}$ gives $r_2 = r_5$; and comparing $r_2$ from $\{
1,2,3,4\}$ with $\{2,3,4,5\}$ gives $r_1 = r_5$. Therefore
$$r_1 = r_2 = r_3 = r_4 = r_5 =: r,$$
so **every** triangle area equals $3r$; in particular all $\binom{5}{3}=10$ tria
ngle areas are equal.

#### Step B (Geometry): Five points in general position cannot have all 10 trian
gle areas equal.

Suppose all 10 areas equal some $S > 0$. Consider any four of the points, say $A
_1,A_2,A_3,A_4$; all four triangle areas among them equal $S$.

**Case 1: One point lies inside the triangle of the other three.** The three sub
-triangles partition the outer triangle, so their areas sum to $S$. But each equ
als $S$, giving $3S = S$, hence $S = 0$ — contradiction.

**Case 2: The four points are in convex position.** Since $S_{123} = S_{124}$ (t
riangles sharing edge $A_1A_2$), points $A_3, A_4$ are at equal distance from li
ne $A_1A_2$ on the same side, so $A_3A_4 \parallel A_1A_2$. Likewise $S_{123} =
S_{134}$ (sharing edge $A_1A_3$) gives $A_2A_4 \parallel A_1A_3$. Hence $A_1A_2A
_3A_4$ is a **parallelogram**. Set coordinates (WLOG):
$$A_1 = (0,0),\quad A_2 = (1,0),\quad A_3 = (a,h),\quad A_4 = (a+1,h), \quad h \
neq 0,$$
so $S = h/2$ (taking $h > 0$).

Now $A_5$ must make every triangle involving it have area $h/2$. Apply the same
reasoning to $\{A_1, A_2, A_3, A_5\}$: either $A_5$ is interior to $\triangle A_
1A_2A_3$ (impossible: $3 \cdot h/2 = h/2$) or these four form a parallelogram. T
he fourth vertex of a parallelogram using three of $\{A_1, A_2, A_3\}$ is one of
:
$$A_5 = A_1+A_2-A_3 = (1-a,\,-h), \quad A_5 = A_1+A_3-A_2 = (a-1,\,h), \quad A_5
 = A_2+A_3-A_1 = (a+1,\,h) = A_4\ (\text{excluded}).$$

- If $A_5 = (a-1, h)$: $S_{145} = \tfrac12\bigl|\det[(a+1,\,h),\,(a-1,\,h)]\bigr
| = \tfrac12|2h| = h \neq h/2$. ✗
- If $A_5 = (1-a, -h)$: $S_{145} = \tfrac12\bigl|\det[(a+1,\,h),\,(1-a,\,-h)]\bi
gr| = \tfrac12|-2h| = h \neq h/2$. ✗

Both possibilities fail. Contradiction. Hence **no five points in general positi
on have all 10 triangle areas equal**, so $n = 5$ is impossible.

#### Step C: $n > 5$ is impossible.

If a configuration existed for $n > 5$, restricting to any 5-subset would yield
five points satisfying the same condition — contradicting Step B. Hence no $n \g
eq 5$ works.

---

### Conclusion

The condition is satisfiable for **$n = 4$** (Part 1) and for **no** $n \geq 5$
(Parts 2A–2C). The unique answer is $n = 4$.
