Looking at this problem, I need to evaluate:
$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \left(\frac{\sum_{k=1}^n k^k}{\left(\frac{n+1}{e}\right)^{
n+1}\cdot \sqrt{2\pi (n+1)}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}$$

## Step 1: Identify the denominator via Stirling's approximation

By Stirling's formula, $(n+1)! \sim \left(\frac{n+1}{e}\right)^{n+1}\sqrt{2\pi(n
+1)}$. So the denominator is asymptotically $(n+1)!$, and the limit becomes:
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{\sum_{k=1}^n k^k}{(n+1)!}\right)^{1/(n+1)}$$

## Step 2: Show the sum is dominated by its last term

I claim $\sum_{k=1}^n k^k \sim n^n$, i.e., $\frac{\sum_{k=1}^n k^k}{n^n} \to 1$.

The largest term in $\sum_{k=1}^{n-1} k^k$ is $(n-1)^{n-1}$. Its ratio to $n^n$
is:
$$\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^n} = \frac{1}{n}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{n-1} \sim \fr
ac{1}{en} \to 0$$

For any fixed $j \geq 1$, $(n-j)^{n-j}/n^n \to 0$ even faster. More rigorously,
for $k \leq n-1$:
$$\frac{k^k}{n^n} \leq \frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^n} \cdot \left(\frac{k}{n-1}\right)^
k$$
and summing over $k=1,\ldots,n-1$ gives a quantity $o(1)$. Therefore:
$$\sum_{k=1}^n k^k = n^n\left(1 + o(1)\right)$$

## Step 3: Evaluate the limit

Substituting:
$$\left(\frac{n^n(1+o(1))}{(n+1)!}\right)^{1/(n+1)} = \left(\frac{n^n}{(n+1)!}\r
ight)^{1/(n+1)} \cdot (1+o(1))^{1/(n+1)}$$

The second factor $\to 1$. For the first, take logarithms and use Stirling $\ln(
(n+1)!) = (n+1)\ln(n+1) - (n+1) + \frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi(n+1)) + o(1)$:

$$\frac{1}{n+1}\ln\frac{n^n}{(n+1)!} = \frac{n\ln n - (n+1)\ln(n+1) + (n+1) - \f
rac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi(n+1)) + o(1)}{n+1}$$

$$= \underbrace{\ln\frac{n}{n+1}}_{\to\, 0} + 1 - \underbrace{\frac{\ln n}{n+1}}
_{\to\, 0} - \underbrace{\frac{\ln(2\pi(n+1))}{2(n+1)}}_{\to\, 0} + o(1)$$

Every vanishing term comes from:
- $\ln\frac{n}{n+1} = \ln\left(1-\frac{1}{n+1}\right) \to 0$
- $\frac{\ln n}{n+1} \to 0$ and $\frac{\ln(2\pi(n+1))}{2(n+1)} \to 0$ since loga
rithms grow slower than any linear function.

Therefore:
$$\frac{1}{n+1}\ln\frac{n^n}{(n+1)!} \to 1$$

and the limit is $e^1 = e$.
