# Problem

In a remote mountain range, four weather stations—Alpha, Beta, Gamma, and Delta—form the vertices of a tetrahedral region. To ensure proper signal propagation, meteorologists define a "Reach Factor" for each station. 

The Reach Factor of a station is the perpendicular distance from that station to the flat triangular plane formed by the other three stations. Each triangular face of this tetrahedron also has its own internal "Altitudes," which are the three standard heights measured within that specific triangle (the perpendicular distance from each vertex of that face to its opposite side).

A station’s Reach Factor is classified as "Dominant" if its length is greater than or equal to each of the three Altitudes of the triangular face lying directly opposite it.

Based on the geometric constraints of the tetrahedral region, what is the maximum possible number of Dominant Reach Factors this network of stations can possess?
