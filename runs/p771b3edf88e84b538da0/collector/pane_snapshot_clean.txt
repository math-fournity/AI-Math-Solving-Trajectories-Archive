## Proof

**Setup.** Let $N = \binom{n}{2}$ denote the number of possible edges. By Burnsi
de's lemma:

$$g_n = \frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma \in S_n} 2^{N_\sigma}, \qquad u_{n,m} = \frac{1
}{n!}\sum_{\sigma \in S_n} a_\sigma(m)$$

where $N_\sigma$ is the number of orbits of $\sigma$ acting on $\binom{[n]}{2}$,
 and $a_\sigma(m)$ is the number of $m$-edge graphs fixed by $\sigma$ (the coeff
icient of $x^m$ in the orbit-counting polynomial $\prod_i(1+x^{s_i})$ for orbit
sizes $s_i$).

**Step 1: Counting orbits.** For the identity, $N_{\mathrm{id}} = N$ and $a_{\ma
thrm{id}}(m) = \binom{N}{m}$. For any $\sigma \neq \mathrm{id}$, the "savings" $
s(\sigma) := N - N_\sigma \geq 0$ measures how many pairs merge into nontrivial
orbits. We compute:

- **Transposition** $\sigma = (i\,j)$: pairs $\{i,k\},\{j,k\}$ for $k \neq i,j$
form $n-2$ orbits of size 2, so $s(\sigma) = n-2$.
- **$k$-cycle** ($k \geq 3$): within-cycle savings $\binom{k}{2} - \lfloor k/2\r
floor \geq 1$, plus cross-cycle savings with fixed points: total $s(\sigma) \geq
 2(n-k) + 2 \geq 2n-4$ for a 3-cycle.
- **General $\sigma$ moving $k$ points**: $s(\sigma) \geq \frac{k(n-k)}{2} - O(k
)$, which is $\geq n-2$ for all $\sigma \neq \mathrm{id}$, and $\geq kn/4$ for $
k \geq 3$.

**Step 2: $g_n \sim 2^N/n!$.** We bound the non-identity contribution:

$$\sum_{\sigma \neq \mathrm{id}} 2^{N_\sigma} = 2^N \sum_{\sigma \neq \mathrm{id
}} 2^{-s(\sigma)}.$$

The dominant terms are transpositions ($s = n-2$, count $\binom{n}{2}$): contrib
ution $\binom{n}{2}\cdot 2^{-(n-2)} = O(n^2 \cdot 2^{-n}) \to 0$. All other perm
utation types contribute $o(n^2 \cdot 2^{-n})$ (larger savings, polynomially man
y permutations). Thus:

$$g_n = \frac{2^N}{n!}\left(1 + O(n^2 \cdot 2^{-n})\right) \sim \frac{2^N}{n!}.
\tag{1}$$

**Step 3: $u_{n,m} \sim \binom{N}{m}/n!$ in the bulk.** Write $u_{n,m} = \frac{1
}{n!}\left[\binom{N}{m} + R(m)\right]$ where $R(m) = \sum_{\sigma \neq \mathrm{i
d}} a_\sigma(m)$.

For the total: $\sum_m R(m) = \sum_{\sigma \neq \mathrm{id}} 2^{N_\sigma} = O(n^
2 \cdot 2^{N-n})$.

For the maximum: $\max_m R(m) \leq \sum_{\sigma \neq \mathrm{id}} \max_m a_\sigm
a(m) \leq \sum_{\sigma \neq \mathrm{id}} 2^{N_\sigma} = O(n^2 \cdot 2^{N-n})$.

For $m$ in the bulk, say $|m - N/2| \leq C\sqrt{N\log N}$, we have $\binom{N}{m}
 \geq \binom{N}{N/2}/\mathrm{poly}(N) \sim 2^N/\mathrm{poly}(N)$. Since $R(m) \l
eq O(n^2 \cdot 2^{N-n})$ and $\binom{N}{m} \geq c \cdot 2^N/\sqrt{N}$, the ratio
 $R(m)/\binom{N}{m} = O(n^2 \cdot 2^{-n} \cdot \sqrt{N}) = O(n^3 \cdot 2^{-n}) \
to 0$. Therefore:

$$u_{n,m} = \frac{\binom{N}{m}}{n!}\left(1 + o(1)\right) \quad \text{uniformly f
or } |m - N/2| \leq C\sqrt{N\log N}. \tag{2}$$

**Step 4: $\sum_m u_{n,m}^2 \sim \frac{1}{(n!)^2}\binom{2N}{N}$.** We expand:

$$\sum_m u_{n,m}^2 = \frac{1}{(n!)^2}\left[\underbrace{\sum_m \binom{N}{m}^2}_{=
\,\binom{2N}{N}} + 2\underbrace{\sum_m \binom{N}{m}R(m)}_{\text{cross}} + \under
brace{\sum_m R(m)^2}_{\text{quad}}\right].$$

**Cross term:** $\sum_m \binom{N}{m}R(m) \leq \max_m R(m) \cdot \sum_m \binom{N}
{m} = O(n^2 \cdot 2^{N-n}) \cdot 2^N = O(n^2 \cdot 4^N \cdot 2^{-n})$.

Ratio to $\binom{2N}{N} \sim 4^N/\sqrt{\pi N}$: $O(n^2 \cdot 2^{-n} \cdot \sqrt{
N}) = O(n^3 \cdot 2^{-n}) \to 0$. ✓

**Quadratic term:** $\sum_m R(m)^2 \leq \max_m R(m) \cdot \sum_m R(m) = O(n^2 \c
dot 2^{N-n})^2 = O(n^4 \cdot 4^{N-n})$.

Ratio to $\binom{2N}{N}$: $O(n^4 \cdot 4^{-n} \cdot \sqrt{N}) = O(n^5 \cdot 4^{-
n}) \to 0$. ✓

Therefore:

$$\sum_m u_{n,m}^2 = \frac{\binom{2N}{N}}{(n!)^2}\left(1 + o(1)\right). \tag{3}$
$

**Step 5: Asymptotics of $T_n$.** Since $g_n \to \infty$:

$$T_n = \frac{\sum_m \binom{u_{n,m}}{2}}{\binom{g_n}{2}} = \frac{\sum_m u_{n,m}^
2 - g_n}{g_n^2 - g_n} = \frac{\sum_m u_{n,m}^2}{g_n^2}\cdot\frac{g_n}{g_n - 1} -
 \frac{1}{g_n - 1}.$$

As $g_n \to \infty$, the correction factors vanish, so $T_n \sim \frac{\sum_m u_
{n,m}^2}{g_n^2}$. Substituting (1) and (3):

$$T_n \sim \frac{\binom{2N}{N}/(n!)^2}{4^N/(n!)^2} = \frac{\binom{2N}{N}}{4^N}.$
$

By Stirling's approximation, $\binom{2N}{N} \sim \frac{4^N}{\sqrt{\pi N}}$, so:

$$T_n \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi N}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi \binom{n}{2}}} \sim \frac
{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}\, n} \to 0.$$

**Conclusion.** The limit $\lim_{n\to\infty} T_n$ exists and equals $0$. In part
icular, it is **not** nonzero — the limit is zero.

The intuitive reason: the edge-count distribution of a random non-isomorphic gra
ph converges to $\mathrm{Binomial}(N, 1/2)$ (since almost all graphs are asymmet
ric, the isomorphism-class weighting is asymptotically uniform over labeled grap
hs). The collision probability of a $\mathrm{Binomial}(N, 1/2)$ is $\sum_m \bino
m{N}{m}^2/4^N = \binom{2N}{N}/4^N \sim 1/\sqrt{\pi N} \to 0$.
