## Proof

### Setup and notation

For $SU(2)$, the irreducible complex representations are indexed by non-negative
 integers $n \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$. We denote by $\varphi_n$ the irreducible r
epresentation of highest weight $n$ (equivalently, of dimension $n+1$, realized
on homogeneous polynomials of degree $n$ in two variables, or as the symmetric p
ower $\mathrm{Sym}^n(\mathbb{C}^2)$). The trivial representation is $\varphi_0$.

We must decompose $\varphi_2 \otimes \varphi_3$ into irreducibles.

### The Clebsch–Gordan formula

We use the standard Clebsch–Gordan decomposition for $SU(2)$:

$$\varphi_m \otimes \varphi_n \;\cong\; \bigoplus_{k=0}^{\min(m,n)} \varphi_{m+n
-2k}.$$

Equivalently, the summands are $\varphi_{m+n},\; \varphi_{m+n-2},\; \varphi_{m+n
-4},\; \dots,\; \varphi_{|m-n|}$, stepping down by 2 each time.

**Derivation sketch (for completeness).** The highest-weight vector of $\varphi_
m \otimes \varphi_n$ has weight $m+n$, giving a copy of $\varphi_{m+n}$. Removin
g it, the next highest remaining weight is $m+n-2$ (coming from $(v_{m-1}\otimes
 w_n)$ and $(v_m \otimes w_{n-1})$, of which one linear combination is absorbed
into $\varphi_{m+n}$ and the orthogonal one generates a fresh highest-weight vec
tor of weight $m+n-2$), giving a copy of $\varphi_{m+n-2}$. Iterating, each step
 peels off one irreducible whose highest weight drops by 2, until we reach $|m-n
|$, at which point no weight remains. This is the classical Clebsch–Gordan recur
sion $\varphi_m\otimes\varphi_n \cong \varphi_{m+n}\oplus(\varphi_{m-1}\otimes\v
arphi_{n-1})$.

### Applying to $\varphi_2 \otimes \varphi_3$

Here $m=2$, $n=3$, so $m+n=5$ and $|m-n|=1$. The sum runs over $k=0,1,2$ (since
$\min(2,3)=2$):

$$\varphi_2 \otimes \varphi_3 \;\cong\; \varphi_{5-0}\;\oplus\;\varphi_{5-2}\;\o
plus\;\varphi_{5-4} \;=\; \varphi_5 \;\oplus\; \varphi_3 \;\oplus\; \varphi_1.$$

### Dimension check

- $\dim\varphi_2 = 3$, $\dim\varphi_3 = 4$, so $\dim(\varphi_2\otimes\varphi_3)
= 3\cdot 4 = 12$.
- Right-hand side: $\dim\varphi_5 + \dim\varphi_3 + \dim\varphi_1 = 6 + 4 + 2 =
12$. ✓

### Weight check

The weights of $\varphi_2$ are $\{-2,0,2\}$ and of $\varphi_3$ are $\{-3,-1,1,3\
}$. The multiset of weights of the tensor product is the sumset (with multiplici
ty):

| weight | $-5$ | $-3$ | $-1$ | $1$ | $3$ | $5$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mult. | 1 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 1 |

Now compute the weight multiplicities of $\varphi_5\oplus\varphi_3\oplus\varphi_
1$:
- $\varphi_5$: weights $-5,-3,-1,1,3,5$, each once.
- $\varphi_3$: weights $-3,-1,1,3$, each once.
- $\varphi_1$: weights $-1,1$, each once.

Summing: $-5\mapsto 1$, $-3\mapsto 2$, $-1\mapsto 3$, $1\mapsto 3$, $3\mapsto 2$
, $5\mapsto 1$. ✓ This matches the tensor-product weight multiset exactly, confi
rming the decomposition.

### Conclusion

$$\boxed{\;\varphi_2 \otimes \varphi_3 \;\cong\; \varphi_5 \;\oplus\; \varphi_3
\;\oplus\; \varphi_1\;}$$

The irreducible constituents of $\varphi_2\otimes\varphi_3$ are $\varphi_5$, $\v
arphi_3$, and $\varphi_1$, each appearing with multiplicity one.
