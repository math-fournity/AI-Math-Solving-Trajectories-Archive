 $a \wedge b = 0$, $a \vee b = 1$, $\neg a = b$, $\neg b = a$, with $0 < a < 1$

0, 1\}$ is a subalgebra of $M$ (closed under $\wedge, \vee, \neg$ and containing
 $0, 1$).

Set:

n $\{0, a, b, 1\}$),

- $C = \{p \in A : p_i = 0 \text{ for all but finitely many } i\} \cup \{p \in A
 : p_i = 1 \text{ for all but finitely many } i\}$.

**$B$ and $C$ are subalgebras of $A$.**

*$B$ is a subalgebra:* $0, 1 \in B$ (constant sequences). If $p, q \in B$, then
for all but finitely many $i$, both $p_i, q_i \in \{0,1\}$; since $\{0,1\}$ is c
losed under $\wedge, \vee, \neg$, the sequences $p \wedge q$, $p \vee q$, $\neg
p$ are also eventually in $\{0,1\}$, hence in $B$.

*$C$ is a subalgebra:* Let $C_0 = \{p : p_i = 0 \text{ eventually}\}$ and $C_1 =
 \{p : p_i = 1 \text{ eventually}\}$, so $C = C_0 \cup C_1$. The constants $0 \i
n C_0$, $1 \in C_1$. Check closure under operations for all combinations:
- $p, q \in C_0$: $p \wedge q, p \vee q \in C_0$; $\neg p \in C_1 \subseteq C$.
- $p, q \in C_1$: $p \wedge q, p \vee q \in C_1$; $\neg p \in C_0 \subseteq C$.
- $p \in C_0, q \in C_1$: $p \wedge q \in C_0$; $p \vee q \in C_1$; $\neg p \in
C_1$; $\neg q \in C_0$. All in $C$.

So $C$ is closed under all Boolean operations. ✓

**$C \subsetneq B$.** Clearly $C \subseteq B$ (if $p$ is eventually $0$ or event

\notin \{0, 1\}$, so $t_i \notin \{0, 1\}$ for *any* $i$, hence $t \notin B$. Th
us $B \subsetneq A$.

**Conclusion.** We have exhibited $M$ (the four-element Boolean algebra) such th
at
$$C \subsetneq B \subsetneq A,$$
so $A$, $B$, and $C$ are all distinct. $\square$
