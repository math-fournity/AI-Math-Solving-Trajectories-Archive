Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a torsion-free group $G$ wi
th a finite-index subgroup $H \cong \mathbb{Z}$ must itself be isomorphic to $\m
athbb{Z}$.

**Answer: Yes, $G \cong \mathbb{Z}$.**

## Proof

**Step 1: Reduce to a normal subgroup $N \cong \mathbb{Z}$.**

Let $[G:H] = n < \in$ with $H \cong \mathbb{Z}$. Let $N = \operatorname{Core}_G(
H) = \bigcap_{g \in G} gHg^{-1}$. Then $N \trianglelefteq G$, $N \leq H$, and $[
G:N] \leq n! < \infty$. Since $N \leq H \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and $N$ has finite ind
ex in $G$ (hence is infinite), we get $N \cong \mathbb{Z}$.

**Step 2: The conjugation action on $N$ is trivial.**

Conjugation gives a homomorphism $\varphi: G \to \operatorname{Aut}(N) \cong C_2
$ (since $N \cong \mathbb{Z}$, $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}) \cong \{\pm 1\}$)
. Let $K = \ker\varphi = C_G(N)$, so $[G:K] \leq 2$, $N \leq K$, $K \trianglelef
teq G$.

Suppose for contradiction that $[G:K] = 2$. Pick $g \in G \setminus K$; then $g$
 inverts $N$, i.e., $gng^{-1} = n^{-1}$ for a generator $n$ of $N$. Now $g^2 \in
 K$.

Apply the central case (Step 3 below) to $K$: since $N \leq Z(K)$ and $K/N$ is f
inite, Schur's theorem gives $K$ abelian; being torsion-free abelian and finitel
y generated with $N \cong \mathbb{Z}$ of finite index, $K \cong \mathbb{Z}$, say
 $K = \langle t \rangle$ with $N = \langle t^a \rangle$.

Since $g$ inverts $t^a$ and $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z})$ has only $\pm 1$, $
g$ inverts $t$: $gtg^{-1} = t^{-1}$. Now $g^2 \in K = \langle t \rangle$, so $g^
2 = t^s$ for some $s \in \mathbb{Z}$. Since $g^2$ commutes with both $g$ and eve
ry element of the abelian group $K$, we have $g^2 \in Z(G)$. Thus $gt^s g^{-1} =
 t^s$. But $g$ inverts $t$, so $gt^s g^{-1} = t^{-s}$. Hence $t^s = t^{-s}$, i.e
., $t^{2s} = 1$. Since $t$ has infinite order, $s = 0$, so $g^2 = 1$. But $G$ is
 torsion-free, forcing $g = 1$, contradicting $g \notin K$.

Therefore $[G:K] = 1$, i.e., $G = C_G(N)$, so $N \leq Z(G)$.

**Step 3: $G$ is abelian (Schur's theorem).**

Since $N \leq Z(G)$ and $G/N$ is finite, $G/Z(G)$ is a quotient of $G/N$, hence
finite. By **Schur's theorem**, $G/Z(G)$ finite implies $[G,G]$ is finite. But $
[G,G] \leq G$ is torsion-free (as a subgroup of the torsion-free group $G$), and
 a finite torsion-free group is trivial. So $[G,G] = 1$; $G$ is abelian.

**Step 4: $G \cong \mathbb{Z}$.**

$G$ is torsion-free abelian and finitely generated (since $N \cong \mathbb{Z}$ i
s finitely generated and $G/N$ is finite), so $G \cong \mathbb{Z}^r$ for some $r
 \geq 0$. Since $N \cong \mathbb{Z}$ has finite index in $G$, the ranks must mat
ch: $r = 1$. Hence $G \cong \mathbb{Z}$. $\blacksquare$
