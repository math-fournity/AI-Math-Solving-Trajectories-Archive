## Proof

We seek the smallest positive integer $x$ such that $10^x \equiv 1 \pmod{3^{2005
}}$, i.e., the multiplicative order of $10$ modulo $3^{2005}$.

**Step 1: Setup.** Since $10 = 1 + 9 = 1 + 3^2$, we have $10 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$,
 so $10$ lies in the subgroup of $(\mathbb{Z}/3^n\mathbb{Z})^\times$ consisting
of elements $\equiv 1 \pmod{3}$. This subgroup has order $3^{n-1}$ (the Sylow $3
$-subgroup of the cyclic group of order $2\cdot 3^{n-1}$). Hence $\operatorname{
ord}_{3^n}(10)$ divides $3^{n-1}$ and is a power of $3$ — no factor of $2$ is in
volved.

**Step 2: A lifting lemma.** Let $p$ be an odd prime and $a = 1 + p^s u$ with $p
 \nmid u$, $s \geq 1$. Then
$$v_p\!\left(a^{p^k} - 1\right) = s + k \quad \text{for all } k \geq 0.$$

*Proof by induction on $k$.* The base $k=0$ is $v_p(a-1)=s$. For the inductive s
tep, write $a^{p^k} = 1 + p^{s+k}\cdot w$ with $p \nmid w$. Then
$$a^{p^{k+1}} - 1 = (a^{p^k})^p - 1 = (a^{p^k}-1)\bigl((a^{p^k})^{p-1} + \cdots
+ a^{p^k} + 1\bigr).$$
The second factor equals $p + \binom{p}{2}p^{s+k}w + \cdots$; since $a^{p^k}\equ
iv 1\pmod p$, each of the $p$ terms $\equiv 1\pmod p$, so the factor $\equiv p \
pmod{p^2}$ (using $p$ odd, the cross terms are divisible by $p^2$). Thus $v_p$ o
f the second factor is exactly $1$, giving $v_p(a^{p^{k+1}}-1) = (s+k)+1$. $\squ
are$

**Step 3: Apply with $a=10$, $p=3$, $s=2$.** Here $10 - 1 = 9 = 3^2\cdot 1$, so
$s = v_3(10-1) = 2$ and $u=1$. The lemma gives
$$v_3\!\left(10^{3^k} - 1\right) = 2 + k.$$

**Step 4: Determine the order modulo $3^{2005}$.** Since the order is a power of
 $3$, say $3^m$, we need $10^{3^m}\equiv 1\pmod{3^{2005}}$, i.e., $v_3(10^{3^m}-
1)\geq 2005$. By Step 3 this is $2 + m \geq 2005$, i.e., $m \geq 2003$. The smal
lest such $m$ is $m = 2003$, and for $m=2003$ we have $v_3(10^{3^{2003}}-1)=2005
\geq 2005$ ✓, while for $m=2002$ we have $v_3(10^{3^{2002}}-1)=2004 < 2005$, so
$10^{3^{2002}}\not\equiv 1\pmod{3^{2005}}$.

**Verification of small cases.** For $n=3$: order $= 3^{3-2}=3$; indeed $10^3=10
00=37\cdot 27+1\equiv 1\pmod{27}$ and $10\not\equiv 1\pmod{27}$. ✓ For $n=2$: or
der $=3^0=1$; indeed $10\equiv 1\pmod 9$. ✓

**Conclusion.** The smallest value of $x$ is
$$\boxed{x = 3^{2003}}.$$
