# Problem

Five distinct pharmaceutical companies participated in a collaborative patent-sharing summit where every company engaged in exactly one private negotiation with every other company. During these negotiations, three outcomes were possible: Company A could secure a licensing advantage over Company B, Company B could secure a licensing advantage over Company A, or the two companies could reach a neutral "Equilibrium Agreement."

The ethics committee established a strict "Counter-Balance Rule" for the summit: For any two companies, $A$ and $B$, that reached an Equilibrium Agreement, there must exist two other distinct companies, $X$ and $Y$, such that:
1. Company $B$ secured an advantage over Company $X$, but lost an advantage to Company $Y$.
2. Company $A$ lost an advantage to Company $X$, but secured an advantage over Company $Y$.

Based on these regulatory constraints, what is the maximum possible number of negotiations that could have resulted in an Equilibrium Agreement?
