## Proof

Let $X = \mathbb{R}^{[0,1]}$ equipped with the product topology, and let
$$S = \{f \in X : |f(t)| \le 1 \text{ for all } t \in [0,1]\}.$$

**Claim:** $S$ is compact in $X$.

**Step 1: Identify $S$ as a product.**

By definition of the function space, a point $f \in \mathbb{R}^{[0,1]}$ is a fun
ction $f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}$, equivalently an element of $\prod_{t\in[0,1]}\math
bb{R}$ whose $t$-th coordinate is $f(t)$. The constraint $|f(t)|\le 1$ means $f(
t)\in[-1,1]$. Hence
$$S = \prod_{t\in[0,1]}[-1,1].$$

**Step 2: Each factor is compact.**

For every $t\in[0,1]$, the interval $[-1,1]\subset\mathbb{R}$ is closed and boun
ded, hence compact by the Heine–Borel theorem.

**Step 3: Apply Tychonoff's theorem.**

Tychonoff's theorem states that an arbitrary product of compact topological spac
es is compact in the product topology. Since $S = \prod_{t\in[0,1]}[-1,1]$ is a
product of compact spaces (indexed by the set $[0,1]$), $S$ is compact in the pr
oduct topology on $\prod_{t\in[0,1]}\mathbb{R} = X$.

**Conclusion.**

The set $S = \{f\in X : |f(t)|\le 1 \text{ for all } t\in[0,1]\}$ is compact in
the product topology on $X = \mathbb{R}^{[0,1]}$. $\blacksquare$
