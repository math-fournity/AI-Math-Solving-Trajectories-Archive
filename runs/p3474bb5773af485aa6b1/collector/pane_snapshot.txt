## Proof that $(X, d)$ is complete

**Setup.** $X = \prod_{i=1}^{\infty} \mathbb{R}$ is the set of all real-valued s
equences. For $f \neq g$, the set $\{i : f(i) \neq g(i)\}$ is a nonempty subset
of $\mathbb{N}$, so $k = \min\{i : f(i) \neq g(i)\}$ is well-defined, and $d(f,g
) = e^{-k}$. We set $d(f,f) = 0$. (One may verify this is an ultrametric: $d(f,h
) \leq \max(d(f,g), d(g,h))$, but we take the metric property as given.)

**Goal.** Show every Cauchy sequence in $(X,d)$ converges.

---

**Step 1 — Cauchy implies eventual coordinate-wise agreement.**

Let $(f_n)_{n=1}^{\infty}$ be Cauchy. Fix a positive integer $K$ and set $\varep
silon = e^{-K}$. By the Cauchy property, there exists $N_K$ such that for all $n
, m \geq N_K$,
$$d(f_n, f_m) < e^{-K}.$$
If $f_n \neq f_m$, then $d(f_n,f_m) = e^{-j}$ where $j = \min\{i : f_n(i)\neq f_
m(i)\}$, and $e^{-j} < e^{-K}$ forces $j > K$, so $f_n$ and $f_m$ agree on $\{1,
\dots,K\}$. If $f_n = f_m$ this holds trivially. Therefore:

> For every $K \in \mathbb{N}$, there exists $N_K$ such that $f_n$ and $f_m$ agr
ee on $\{1,\dots,K\}$ for all $n,m \geq N_K$.

**Step 2 — Construct the candidate limit.**

For each coordinate $i$, apply the above with $K = i$: there is $N_i$ such that
$f_n(i) = f_m(i)$ for all $n,m \geq N_i$. Hence the real sequence $(f_n(i))_{n}$
 is **eventually constant**. Define
$$f(i) := f_n(i) \quad \text{for any } n \geq N_i.$$
Since $f$ is a real-valued sequence, $f \in X$.

**Step 3 — Verify $f_n \to f$.**

Let $\varepsilon > 0$. Choose $K$ with $e^{-K} < \varepsilon$. For every $n \geq

i$ and $f(i)$ is the eventual value). Thus $f_n$ and $f$ agree on $\{1,\dots,K\}

exceeds $K$, giving
$$d(f_n, f) < e^{-K} < \varepsilon.$$
In either case $d(f_n, f) < \varepsilon$ for all $n \geq N_K$. Hence $f_n \to f$

he metric space $\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{R},\, d\right)$ is **complete
