## Solution

**Setup.** Starting from height 0, at each step we choose a segment type $m \in
\{30, 70, 110\}$ and jump to the smallest multiple of $m$ strictly greater than
the current height $h$:
$$f(h, m) = m\left(\left\lfloor \frac{h}{m}\right\rfloor + 1\right).$$

Let $d(h)$ = number of distinct sequences (ordered lists of segment-type choices
) reaching height $h$. We seek the smallest $h$ with $d(h) > 100$.

**Key observation.** A height $g$ is a predecessor of $h$ via segment type $m$ i
f and only if $m \mid h$ and $g \in [h - m,\; h - 1]$. (Because $h$ must be a mu
ltiple of $m$, and $g$ must be $\geq h - m$ so that $h$ is the *next* multiple o
f $m$ after $g$.)

So:
$$d(h) = \sum_{\substack{m \in \{30,70,110\} \\ m \mid h}} \;\sum_{\substack{g \
text{ reachable} \\ g \in [h-m,\, h-1]}} d(g).$$

**Reachable heights** (besides 0) are multiples of 30, 70, or 110. Up to 210 the
se are:
$$30, 60, 70, 90, 110, 120, 140, 150, 180, 210.$$

**Computing $d(h)$ for each reachable height in order:**

| $h$ | Divides $h$ | Predecessors (reachable $g$ in range) | $d(h)$ |
|-----|-------------|---------------------------------------|--------|
| 0 | — | (base case) | 1 |
| 30 | 30 | $g \in [0,29]$: $\{0\}$ → $1$ | 1 |
| 60 | 30 | $g \in [30,59]$: $\{30\}$ → $1$ | 1 |
| 70 | 70 | $g \in [0,69]$: $\{0,30,60\}$ → $1+1+1$ | 3 |
| 90 | 30 | $g \in [60,89]$: $\{60,70\}$ → $1+3$ | 4 |
| 110 | 110 | $g \in [0,109]$: $\{0,30,60,70,90\}$ → $1+1+1+3+4$ | 10 |
| 120 | 30 | $g \in [90,119]$: $\{90,110\}$ → $4+10$ | 14 |
| 140 | 70 | $g \in [70,139]$: $\{70,90,110,120\}$ → $3+4+10+14$ | 31 |
| 150 | 30 | $g \in [120,149]$: $\{120,140\}$ → $14+31$ | 45 |
| 180 | 30 | $g \in [150,179]$: $\{150\}$ → $45$ | 45 |
| 210 | 30, 70 | via 30: $g \in [180,209]$: $\{180\}$ → $45$; via 70: $g \in [14
0,209]$: $\{140,150,180\}$ → $31+45+45=121$ | **166** |

**Verification of the critical step ($h = 210$):**
- $210 = 7 \times 30 = 3 \times 70$, so both 30 and 110... no, $110 \nmid 210$.
- Via $m=30$: predecessors in $[180, 209]$ — only $180$ is reachable, contributi
ng $d(180) = 45$.
- Via $m=70$: predecessors in $[140, 209]$ — reachable: $140, 150, 180$, contrib
uting $31 + 45 + 45 = 121$.
- Note: from height 180, both segment type 30 and segment type 70 lead to 210 (s
ince $f(180,30) = 210$ and $f(180,70) = 210$). These are **distinct** segment ty
pes, so $d(180)$ is correctly counted in both contributions.
- $d(210) = 45 + 121 = 166 > 100$. ✓

**No earlier height exceeds 100.** The largest $d(h)$ before 210 is $d(150) = d(
180) = 45$, well below 100. No reachable heights exist between 180 and 210 (the
next multiple of 30 is 210, the next multiple of 70 is 210, and the next multipl
e of 110 is 220).

**Answer:** The lowest possible height is $\boxed{210}$ centimeters.
