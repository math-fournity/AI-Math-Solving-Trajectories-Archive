## Proof

**Claim:** $\bigcap_{f \in E'} \ker(f) = \{0\}$.

**The inclusion $\{0\} \subseteq \bigcap_{f \in E'} \ker(f)$.**

Every linear functional $f: E \to \mathbb{K}$ satisfies $f(0) = 0$, so $0 \in \k
er(f)$ for every $f \in E'$. Hence $\{0\} \subseteq \bigcap_{f \in E'} \ker(f)$.

**The inclusion $\bigcap_{f \in E'} \ker(f) \subseteq \{0\}$.**

We must show: if $x \in E$ with $x \neq 0$, then there exists $f \in E'$ (i.e.,
a continuous linear functional) such that $f(x) \neq 0$.

Let $x_0 \in E$, $x_0 \neq 0$. Consider the one-dimensional subspace $M = \mathb
b{K} x_0 = \{\lambda x_0 : \lambda \in \mathbb{K}\}$. Define $g: M \to \mathbb{K
}$ by
$$g(\lambda x_0) = \lambda \, N(x_0).$$
This is well-defined (since $x_0 \neq 0$, the representation $\lambda x_0$ is un
ique) and linear. It is continuous on $M$ because
$$|g(\lambda x_0)| = |\lambda|\, N(x_0) = N(\lambda x_0),$$
so $g$ is in fact an isometry on $M$ (in particular, bounded with $\|g\|_{M} = 1
$).

By the **Hahn–Banach theorem** (which applies to every normed vector space, incl
uding infinite-dimensional ones over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$), the function
al $g$ extends to a continuous linear functional $f: E \to \mathbb{K}$ with $\|f
\| = \|g\|_M = 1$.

Now evaluate at $x_0$:
$$f(x_0) = g(x_0) = N(x_0) \neq 0,$$
since $x_0 \neq 0$ implies $N(x_0) > 0$.

Therefore $x_0 \notin \ker(f)$, so $x_0 \notin \bigcap_{f \in E'} \ker(f)$.

**Conclusion.** We have shown both inclusions, hence
$$\bigcap_{f \in E'} \ker(f) = \{0\}. \qquad \blacksquare$$
