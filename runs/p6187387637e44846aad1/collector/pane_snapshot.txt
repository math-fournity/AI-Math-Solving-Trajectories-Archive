## Counterexample: The Cantor Function

**Construction.** Let $C\subset[0,1]$ be the middle-thirds Cantor set, obtained
by repeatedly removing the open middle third of each remaining interval. $C$ is
closed, uncountable, and has Lebesgue measure zero.

The **Cantor function** $f:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ is defined as follows. For $x\in[0,1]$
, write $x$ in ternary form $x=0.a_1a_2a_3\ldots$ choosing the representation th
at does not end in all $2$'s when $x$ is a ternary rational. Set
$$g(x)=0.b_1b_2b_3\ldots \quad(\text{binary}), \qquad b_i=\begin{cases}0 & a_i=0
\\ 1 & a_i=2\end{cases}$$
and when $x$ lies in a removed (middle-third) interval, $f$ is extended to be co
nstant there, equal to the common value at the two endpoints. Extend $f$ to all
of $\mathbb{R}$ by setting $f(x)=0$ for $x\le 0$ and $f(x)=1$ for $x\ge 1$.

**Properties.** It is standard that $f$ is continuous and non-decreasing on $\ma
thbb{R}$, constant on each component of $[0,1]\setminus C$ (an open set), and ma
ps $C$ onto $[0,1]$.

---

## $f$ is differentiable on $\mathbb{R}\setminus C$

The set $[0,1]\setminus C$ is a countable union of disjoint open intervals, on e
ach of which $f$ is constant. Hence $f'(x)=0$ at every $x\in[0,1]\setminus C$. O
utside $[0,1]$, $f$ is constant, so $f'=0$ there too. Thus $f$ is differentiable
 on $\mathbb{R}\setminus C$.

---

## $f$ is **not** differentiable at any point of $C$

Let $x\in C$. At stage $n$ of the Cantor construction, $x$ lies in a closed inte
rval $I_n$ of length $3^{-n}$, and the Cantor function restricted to $I_n\cap C$
 maps onto a dyadic interval $J_n$ of length $2^{-n}$ (this follows from the sel
f-similar construction: each surviving interval at stage $n$ carries a variation
 of $2^{-n}$ in $f$).

Concretely, the two endpoints $u_n,v_n$ of $I_n$ (which belong to $C$) satisfy
$$|f(v_n)-f(u_n)|=2^{-n}, \qquad |v_n-u_n|=3^{-n}.$$
Since $x\in I_n$, at least one of $u_n,v_n$ — call it $y_n$ — satisfies
$$|f(y_n)-f(x)|\ge \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 2^{-n}=2^{-(n+1)}, \qquad |y_n-x|\le 3^{-n}
.$$
Because $f$ is non-decreasing, the difference quotient has consistent sign, and
$$\frac{|f(y_n)-f(x)|}{|y_n-x|}\ge \frac{2^{-(n+1)}}{3^{-n}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\f
rac{3}{2}\right)^n \xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\infty.$$
Since $y_n\to x$ (as $|y_n-x|\le 3^{-n}\to 0$) and the difference quotients tend
 to $+\infty$, the derivative at $x$ does not exist as a finite real number. Hen
ce $f$ is not differentiable at $x$.

This holds for **every** $x\in C$.

---

## Conclusion

The set of non-differentiability of $f$ is exactly $S=C$, the Cantor set, which
is:
- **uncountable** (it is in bijection with $\{0,2\}^{\mathbb{N}}$ via ternary ex
pansions, hence with $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}\cong 2^{\mathbb{N}}$), and
- of **Lebesgue measure zero** (its total length is $\lim_{n\to\infty}(2/3)^n\cd
ot$ ... in fact $m(C)=\lim_{n}2^n\cdot 3^{-n}=\lim_n(2/3)^n=0$).

Yet $f$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$ and differentiable on $\mathbb{R}\setminus
 S$.

Therefore the statement

> "the set $S$ must be countable"

is **false**: there exists a continuous, a.e.-differentiable function whose non-
differentiability set is an uncountable set of measure zero.
