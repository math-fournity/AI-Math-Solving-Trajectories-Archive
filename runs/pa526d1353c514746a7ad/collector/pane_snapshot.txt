## Solution

We compute the Dirichlet series $F(s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} f(n)\,n^{-s}$ where
$f(n) = \sum_{d\mid n} \frac{\log d}{d}$.

**Step 1: Recognize the Dirichlet convolution.**

Define $g(n) = \frac{\log n}{n}$ and $\mathbf{1}(n) = 1$ (the constant function)
. Then

$$f(n) = \sum_{d\mid n} g(d)\,\mathbf{1}(n/d) = (g * \mathbf{1})(n),$$

so $f$ is the Dirichlet convolution of $g$ and $\mathbf{1}$.

**Step 2: Use the multiplicativity of Dirichlet series under convolution.**

A standard fact: if $f = g * h$, then the Dirichlet series factorize as

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} f(n)\,n^{-s} = \left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} g(n)\,n^{-s}\righ
t)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} h(n)\,n^{-s}\right).$$

Thus

$$F(s) = G(s)\cdot Z(s),$$

where $G(s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} g(n)\,n^{-s}$ and $Z(s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\mathbf{1}(n)\,n^{-s}$.

**Step 3: Identify each factor with the Riemann zeta function.**

The constant function gives the zeta function directly:

$$Z(s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^{-s} = \zeta(s).$$

For $G(s)$, substitute $g(n) = \frac{\log n}{n}$:

$$G(s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\log n}{n}\,n^{-s} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \fra
c{\log n}{n^{s+1}}.$$

Recall that $\zeta(s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^{-s}$, so termwise differentiation
 gives

$$\zeta'(s) = -\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (\log n)\,n^{-s}.$$

Evaluating at $s+1$:

$$\zeta'(s+1) = -\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (\log n)\,n^{-(s+1)} = -\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
 \frac{\log n}{n^{s+1}} = -G(s).$$

Therefore $G(s) = -\zeta'(s+1)$.

**Step 4: Combine.**

$$F(s) = G(s)\cdot Z(s) = -\zeta'(s+1)\cdot\zeta(s).$$

**Convergence note.** The series $\zeta(s)$ converges absolutely for $\Re(s)>1$,
 and $G(s) = -\zeta'(s+1)$ converges absolutely for $\Re(s+1)>1$, i.e. $\Re(s)>0
$. The product $F(s)$ is thus valid (as an absolutely convergent Dirichlet serie
s) in the region $\Re(s)>1$, where both factors converge absolutely, and extends
 meromorphically via the right-hand side.

$$\boxed{F(s) = -\,\zeta(s)\,\zeta'(s+1)}$$
