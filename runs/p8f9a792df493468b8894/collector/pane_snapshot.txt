**Claim.** Let $\{A_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ be a sequence of $d\times d$ positive se
midefinite (PSD) real symmetric matrices. If $\{A_n\}$ converges (in any matrix
norm) to a matrix $A$, then $A$ is positive semidefinite.

**Proof.**

Since the space $\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ is finite-dimensional, all norms are eq
uivalent, so convergence in any norm is equivalent to entrywise convergence. Hen
ce each entry $(A_n)_{ij}$ converges to $A_{ij}$, and in particular $A$ is the e
ntrywise limit of the $A_n$.

First, $A$ is symmetric: each $A_n$ is symmetric, so $(A_n)_{ij}=(A_n)_{ji}$ for
 all $n$. Passing to the limit, $A_{ij}=A_{ji}$, so $A=A^T$.

Now fix any vector $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$. For every $n$, since $A_n$ is PSD,
$$x^{T}A_n x \geq 0.$$

The scalar $x^{T}A_n x = \sum_{i,j} x_i x_j (A_n)_{ij}$ is a fixed linear combin
ation (with coefficients $x_i x_j$) of the entries of $A_n$. Since each entry $(
A_n)_{ij}\to A_{ij}$, by linearity of limits,
$$x^{T}A_n x \;\longrightarrow\; x^{T}A x.$$

Thus $\{x^{T}A_n x\}$ is a sequence of non-negative real numbers converging to $
x^{T}A x$. A convergent sequence of non-negative reals has a non-negative limit,
 so
$$x^{T}A x \geq 0.$$

Since $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$ was arbitrary and $A$ is symmetric, $A$ is positive sem
idefinite. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on the hypotheses.** The "bounded" assumption is redundant: in a finite
-dimensional normed space every convergent sequence is automatically bounded. Th
e essential content is simply that the set of PSD matrices is *closed* in $\math
bb{R}^{d\times d}$, being the intersection over all $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$ of the cl
osed sets $\{M : x^{T}Mx\geq 0\}$ (each the preimage of $[0,\infty)$ under the c
ontinuous map $M\mapsto x^{T}Mx$). An arbitrary intersection of closed sets is c
losed, so the limit of any convergent sequence of PSD matrices remains PSD.
