# Problem

A massive solar power grid is designed as a square grid of $2021 \times 2021$ individual sensor plots. To monitor the grid, high-intensity signal transmitters (acting like rooks) are positioned on specific plots.

The grid’s maintenance protocols dictate two strict operational constraints:
1. Every single sensor plot in the $2021 \times 2021$ grid must be within the transmission range of at least one transmitter. A transmitter covers all plots located in the same row and the same column as itself.
2. To prevent signal interference, no transmitter is allowed to be in the line-of-sight of more than $18$ other transmitters. (A transmitter sees any other transmitter located in its same row or column).

The grid supervisor needs to identify a universal safety margin for "dark zones"—square sub-grids that might not contain a transmitter. 

Find the smallest integer $k$ such that, regardless of how the transmitters are legally arranged, every $k \times k$ square sub-grid of plots is guaranteed to contain at least one transmitter.
