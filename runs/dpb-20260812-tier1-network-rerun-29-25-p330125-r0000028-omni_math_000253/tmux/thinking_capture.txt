Summary

The valid pairs $(m, n)$ are exactly:

  1. All $(m, n)$ with $m \geq 2$ and $n \geq 2$, and
  2. $(1, n)$ and $(n, 1)$ where $n \geq 3$ is odd.

The pairs that fail are:

  • $(1, 1)$: $P(Q(t)) = Q(P(t))$ always.
  • $(1, n)$ and $(n, 1)$ with $n$ even: $R$ always has odd degree, hence a real
  root.

Constructions proving the valid pairs:

  • $(m, n)$, $m, n \geq 2$, at least one even: $P(x) = x^m$, $Q(x) = x^n + c$
  (if $n$ even) or $P(x) = x^m + c$, $Q(x) = x^n$ (if $m$ even), with $c > 1$.
