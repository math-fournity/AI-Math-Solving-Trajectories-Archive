## Solution

Let \(X(t)\) be a standard Brownian motion with \(X(0)=0\), independent incremen
ts, and \(X(t)-X(s)\sim N(0,t-s)\) for \(t>s\).

**Key observation.** The vector \(\bigl(X(u),\, X(u+v),\, X(u+v+w)\bigr)\) is **
jointly Gaussian with mean zero**, since each component is a linear combination
of the independent Gaussian increments
\[
X(u),\quad X(u+v)-X(u),\quad X(u+v+w)-X(u+v).
\]

A fundamental property of any centered (mean-zero) multivariate Gaussian distrib
ution is that **all odd-order moments vanish**. This follows from the symmetry o
f the joint density under \((X_1,X_2,X_3)\mapsto (-X_1,-X_2,-X_3)\): the integra
nd of any odd-order moment changes sign while the density is unchanged, forcing
the integral to be \(0\).

Since \(E[X(u)X(u+v)X(u+v+w)]\) is a third-order (odd) moment of a centered join
tly Gaussian vector, it equals \(0\).

**Direct verification by expansion.** Set
\[
A=X(u),\quad B=X(u+v)-X(u),\quad C=X(u+v+w)-X(u+v),
\]
so \(A,B,C\) are independent with \(A\sim N(0,u),\, B\sim N(0,v),\, C\sim N(0,w)
\). Then
\[
X(u)X(u+v)X(u+v+w)=A(A+B)(A+B+C).
\]
Expanding:
\[
A(A+B)(A+B+C)=A^{3}+2A^{2}B+AB^{2}+A^{2}C+ABC.
\]
Taking expectations term by term, using independence and \(E[A]=E[B]=E[C]=0\):
- \(E[A^{3}]=0\) (odd moment of a centered Gaussian),
- \(E[2A^{2}B]=2E[A^{2}]\,E[B]=0\),
- \(E[AB^{2}]=E[A]\,E[B^{2}]=0\),
- \(E[A^{2}C]=E[A^{2}]\,E[C]=0\),
- \(E[ABC]=E[A]\,E[B]\,E[C]=0\).

Summing,
\[
\boxed{E\!\left[X(u)\,X(u+v)\,X(u+v+w)\right]=0.}
\]
