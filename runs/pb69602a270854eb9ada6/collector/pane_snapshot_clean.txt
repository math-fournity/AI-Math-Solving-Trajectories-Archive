## Solution

We find all isomorphisms of affine algebraic varieties $\mathbb{A}^1_k \xrightar
row{\sim} \mathbb{A}^1_k$.

**Coordinate ring correspondence.** The affine line $\mathbb{A}^1_k$ has coordin
ate ring $k[t]$. A morphism of affine varieties $\varphi: \mathbb{A}^1_k \to \ma
thbb{A}^1_k$ is equivalent (contravariantly) to a $k$-algebra homomorphism
$$\varphi^*: k[t] \to k[t].$$
Such a homomorphism is completely determined by the image of the generator $t$,
i.e. by a polynomial $f(t) \in k[t]$ with $\varphi^*(t) = f(t)$. Thus morphisms
$\mathbb{A}^1_k \to \mathbb{A}^1_k$ are in bijection with polynomials $f \in k[t
]$.

**Isomorphisms correspond to automorphisms.** The morphism $\varphi$ is an isomo
rphism of varieties if and only if $\varphi^*$ is an isomorphism (automorphism)
of $k$-algebras. Suppose $\varphi^*$ is an automorphism with inverse $\psi^*$, a
nd write $\psi^*(t) = g(t) \in k[t]$. The identity $\varphi^* \circ \psi^* = \ma
thrm{id}_{k[t]}$ applied to $t$ gives
$$f(g(t)) = t. \tag{$\ast$}$$

**Degree argument.** Taking degrees in $(\ast)$ and using $\deg(f \circ g) = \de
g f \cdot \deg g$ (valid over any field, since the leading coefficient of a comp
osition is the product of leading coefficients, which is nonzero when both facto
rs are nonconstant), we obtain
$$\deg f \cdot \deg g = \deg t = 1.$$
Since degrees are non-negative integers, this forces
$$\deg f = 1 \quad \text{and} \quad \deg g = 1.$$

Hence $f(t) = at + b$ for some $a, b \in k$ with $a \neq 0$.

**Converse.** Every polynomial $f(t) = at + b$ with $a \in k^\times$, $b \in k$
defines a $k$-algebra endomorphism $\varphi^*$ of $k[t]$ sending $t \mapsto at +
 b$. Its inverse sends $t \mapsto a^{-1}(t - b)$, which is again a polynomial in
 $k[t]$. Therefore $\varphi^*$ is a $k$-algebra automorphism, and correspondingl
y $\varphi$ is an isomorphism of varieties.

**Conclusion.** The isomorphisms $\mathbb{A}^1_k \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{A}^1
_k$ are exactly the affine linear maps
$$\boxed{\,t \;\longmapsto\; a\,t + b, \qquad a \in k^\times,\; b \in k\,}$$
i.e. the group $\operatorname{Aff}(1,k) = k^\times \ltimes k$ acting on the line
. No other polynomial (in particular no polynomial of degree $\geq 2$, and not t
he zero polynomial) can yield an isomorphism, since the degree constraint $\deg
f \cdot \deg g = 1$ admits only degree-one solutions.
