Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the covering condition (eve
ry vector is null for some form in the family) forces the existence of a trace-z
ero form.

**Key observation:** For a quadratic form $Q$ with associated symmetric matrix $
A$ (so $Q(v) = v^\top A v$), the quantity $\sum_{i=1}^n Q(e_i)$ over any orthono
rmal basis $\{e_i\}$ equals $\operatorname{tr}(A)$, which is basis-independent.
So the question reduces to: **must the family contain a form with trace zero?**

**Answer: No, not necessarily** (for $n \geq 2$). Here is a counterexample.

---

## Counterexample ($n \geq 2$)

**The family.** Parameterize by the unit sphere $S^{n-1}$. For each unit vector
$u \in S^{n-1}$, define the quadratic form
$$Q_u(v) = (u \cdot v)^2 = v^\top (u u^\top)\, v.$$
The map $u \mapsto u u^\top$ is continuous, so this is a continuous family of qu
adratic forms.

**Covering property.** For any nonzero $v \in \mathbb{R}^n$, since $n \geq 2$, t
here exists a unit vector $u \in S^{n-1}$ orthogonal to $v$ (extend $v/\|v\|$ to
 an orthonormal basis and take any other basis vector). Then
$$Q_u(v) = (u \cdot v)^2 = 0.$$
So every vector $v$ is null for some form in the family. ✓

**Trace is never zero.** The matrix associated to $Q_u$ is $A_u = u u^\top$, whi
ch has
$$\operatorname{tr}(A_u) = \operatorname{tr}(u u^\top) = u^\top u = \|u\|^2 = 1
> 0$$
for every $u \in S^{n-1}$. Therefore, for every form in the family and every ort
honormal basis $\{e_i\}$,
$$\sum_{i=1}^n Q_u(e_i) = \operatorname{tr}(A_u) = 1 \neq 0.$$

**Conclusion.** This continuous family satisfies the hypothesis (every vector is
 null for some form), yet no form in the family has $\sum_i Q(e_i) = 0$ for any
orthonormal basis. The conclusion does **not** follow in general.

## Remark on $n = 1$

For completeness: when $n = 1$, every quadratic form is $Q(x) = ax^2$. The condi
tion that some form vanishes on every nonzero $v$ forces $a = 0$ to appear in th
e family, giving trace $0$. So the answer is yes for $n = 1$, but **no for $n \g
eq 2$**, as shown above.
