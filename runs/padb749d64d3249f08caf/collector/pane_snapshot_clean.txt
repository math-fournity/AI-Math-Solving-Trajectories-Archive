## Solution

We evaluate
$$f(x) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty \frac{dk}{2\pi}\, e^{-ikx}\,\frac{\sin^2(k/2)}{(k
/2)^2}.$$

**Step 1: Identify the sinc function.**

Write $\frac{\sin^2(k/2)}{(k/2)^2} = \operatorname{sinc}^2(k/2)$, where $\operat
orname{sinc}(u) = \frac{\sin u}{u}$.

**Step 2: Fourier transform of $\operatorname{sinc}(k/2)$.**

Using the standard identity
$$\int_{-a}^{a} e^{ikt}\,dt = \frac{2\sin(ka)}{k},$$
with $a = \tfrac{1}{2}$ we get
$$\operatorname{sinc}(k/2) = \frac{\sin(k/2)}{k/2} = \int_{-1/2}^{1/2} e^{ikt}\,
dt.$$

With the convention $\widehat{g}(x) = \int \frac{dk}{2\pi}\,e^{-ikx}\,g(k)$, the
 Fourier transform of $\operatorname{sinc}(k/2)$ is
$$\widehat{\operatorname{sinc}(\cdot/2)}(x) = \int_{-1/2}^{1/2} dt \int \frac{dk
}{2\pi}\,e^{-ik(x-t)} = \int_{-1/2}^{1/2} \delta(x-t)\,dt = \mathbf{1}_{[-1/2,\,
1/2]}(x).$$

**Step 3: Apply the convolution theorem.**

With our convention, if $g(k) = a(k)\,b(k)$, then $\widehat{g}(x) = (\widehat{a}
 * \widehat{b})(x) = \int \widehat{a}(y)\,\widehat{b}(x-y)\,dy$. (Verified by su
bstituting the inverse $a(k)=\int e^{iky}\widehat{a}(y)\,dy$ and using $\int\fra
c{dk}{2\pi}e^{-ik(x-y-z)}=\delta(x-y-z)$.)

Therefore
$$f(x) = \bigl(\mathbf{1}_{[-1/2,1/2]} * \mathbf{1}_{[-1/2,1/2]}\bigr)(x) = \int
_{-\infty}^{\infty} \mathbf{1}_{[-1/2,1/2]}(y)\,\mathbf{1}_{[-1/2,1/2]}(x-y)\,dy
.$$

**Step 4: Evaluate the convolution (triangle function).**

The integrand is $1$ precisely when $y \in [-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]$ and $x - y \in
[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]$, i.e. $y \in [x-\tfrac12,\, x+\tfrac12]$. The measure of t
he overlap of $[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]$ and $[x-\tfrac12, x+\tfrac12]$ is:
- $1 - |x|$ when $|x| \le 1$,
- $0$ when $|x| > 1$.

Hence
$$f(x) = \max(1 - |x|,\, 0) = \begin{cases} 1 - |x|, & |x| \le 1,\\[4pt] 0, & |x
| > 1. \end{cases}$$

**Step 5: Verification.**

- **At $x=0$:** $f(0) = 1$. Directly, $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{sinc
}^2(k/2)\,dk = 2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{sinc}^2(u)\,du = 2\pi$, so
$f(0)=\frac{2\pi}{2\pi}=1$. ✓
- **At $x=\pm 1$:** $f(\pm 1) = 0$, consistent with the support of the convoluti
on of two width-$1$ box functions. ✓
- **Symmetry:** $f(x)=f(-x)$, matching the evenness of $\operatorname{sinc}^2(k/
2)$. ✓
- **Normalization:** $\int_{-1}^{1}(1-|x|)\,dx = 1 = \operatorname{sinc}^2(k/2)\
big|_{k=0} = g(0)$, consistent with $g(k)=\int e^{ikx}f(x)\,dx$. ✓

## Result

For $x \in [-1, 1]$,
$$\boxed{f(x) = 1 - |x|.}$$
