Looking at this problem, I need to determine the order of growth of the prime sh
ift function $s(n)$, which replaces each prime $p_i$ (the $i$-th prime) with the
 next prime $p_{i+1}$, keeping exponents fixed.

**Answer: The maximal order of growth is $n^{\log_2 3}$.**

## Proof

**Key observation: $s$ is completely multiplicative.** For any prime $p_i$ and e
xponent $e$, $s(p_i^e) = p_{i+1}^e = s(p_i)^e$. Since $s$ acts independently on
each prime power in the factorization, $s(n) = \prod_i s(p_i)^{e_i}$, so $s$ is
completely multiplicative.

### Upper Bound: $s(n) \leq n^{\log_2 3}$ for all $n$

Since $s$ is completely multiplicative, it suffices to show $s(p_i) \leq p_i^{\a
lpha}$ for every prime $p_i$, where $\alpha = \log_2 3$. Then:

$$s(n) = \prod_i s(p_i)^{e_i} \leq \prod_i p_i^{\alpha e_i} = \left(\prod_i p_i^
{e_i}\right)^{\alpha} = n^{\alpha}.$$

So we need: $p_{i+1} \leq p_i^{\log_2 3}$ for all $i \geq 1$.

**Case $i = 1$:** $p_2 = 3 = 2^{\log_2 3} = p_1^{\log_2 3}$. ✓ (equality)

**Case $i = 2$:** $p_3 = 5 \leq 3^{\log_2 3}$. Computing: $\ln(3^{\log_2 3}) = \
log_2 3 \cdot \ln 3 = \frac{(\ln 3)^2}{\ln 2} = \frac{(1.0986)^2}{0.6931} \appro
x 1.741$, so $3^{\log_2 3} \approx e^{1.741} \approx 5.704 > 5$. ✓

**Case $i \geq 3$ (so $p_i \geq 5$):** By **Bertrand's postulate**, $p_{i+1} < 2
p_i$. It suffices to show $2p_i \leq p_i^{\log_2 3}$, i.e., $p_i^{\log_2 3 - 1}
\geq 2$.

Since $\log_2 3 - 1 = \log_2(3/2) \approx 0.585 > 0$, we need $p_i^{\log_2(3/2)}
 \geq 2$, i.e., $\log_2(3/2) \cdot \log_2 p_i \geq 1$, i.e., $p_i \geq 2^{1/\log
_2(3/2)} = 2^{\log_{3/2} 2} \approx 2^{1.71} \approx 3.25$.

Since $p_i \geq 5 > 3.25$ for $i \geq 3$, the inequality holds. ✓

Therefore $s(n) \leq n^{\log_2 3}$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

### Lower Bound: The bound is tight

For $n = 2^k$:
$$s(2^k) = 3^k = (2^{\log_2 3})^k = (2^k)^{\log_2 3} = n^{\log_2 3}.$$

The upper bound is achieved **exactly**.

### Maximal order

For $x \geq 2$, taking $k = \lfloor \log_2 x \rfloor$ and $n = 2^k \leq x$:
$$\max_{n \leq x} s(n) \geq s(2^k) = 3^k = 3^{\lfloor \log_2 x \rfloor} \sim 3^{
\log_2 x} = x^{\log_2 3}.$$

Combined with the upper bound, $\max_{n \leq x} s(n) \sim x^{\log_2 3}$.

### Conclusion

The order of growth of the prime shift function is

$$\boxed{s(n) = n^{\log_2 3}}$$

in the sense that $s(n) \leq n^{\log_2 3}$ for all $n$, with equality when $n$ i
s a power of $2$. The constant $\log_2 3 \approx 1.585$ arises because the ratio
 $\log p_{i+1}/\log p_i$ is maximized at $i=1$ (i.e., at the prime $2$, where $s
(2)=3$), and this maximum governs the growth through complete multiplicativity.
