## Solution

**Answer: The implication is FALSE.**

### Setup and preliminary observations

Let $A$ be a normed algebra with submultiplicative norm $\|\cdot\|$ (i.e. $\|xy\
|\le \|x\|\,\|y\|$) and involution $*$, satisfying the hypothesis
$$\|a\|^{2}\le \|a^{*}a\|\qquad\text{for all }a\in A. \tag{H}$$

**The involution is isometric.** Applying (H) to $a$ and using submultiplicativi
ty:
$$\|a\|^{2}\le \|a^{*}a\|\le \|a^{*}\|\,\|a\|\;\Longrightarrow\;\|a\|\le \|a^{*}
\|.$$
Applying (H) to $a^{*}$ (note $(a^{*})^{*}=a$) and using submultiplicativity:
$$\|a^{*}\|^{2}\le \|(a^{*})^{*}a^{*}\|=\|aa^{*}\|\le \|a\|\,\|a^{*}\|\;\Longrig
htarrow\;\|a^{*}\|\le \|a\|.$$
Hence $\|a^{*}\|=\|a\|$ for all $a$, and combining with (H) yields the **C\*-ide
ntity**
$$\|a^{*}a\|=\|a\|^{2}\qquad\text{for all }a\in A. \tag{C*}$$

So the hypothesis forces $A$ to be a (possibly non-complete) C\*-algebra. The qu
estion reduces to: in a C\*-algebra, must $\|a^{*}\|\,\|a\|=\|a\|^{2}\le \|a^{2}
\|$ hold for every $a$?

### The counterexample

Take $A=M_{2}(\mathbb{C})$, the algebra of $2\times 2$ complex matrices, with:
- norm = operator (spectral) norm $\|M\|=\sigma_{\max}(M)$,
- involution $*$ = conjugate transpose.

This is a C\*-algebra, so (C\*) holds for every element; in particular the hypot
hesis (H) is satisfied (with equality).

Let
$$a=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\[2pt]0&0\end{pmatrix}.$$

Compute each piece:

- **$a^{*}$:**
$$a^{*}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\[2pt]1&0\end{pmatrix}.$$

- **$a^{*}a$:**
$$a^{*}a=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\[2pt]1&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\[2pt]0&0\e
nd{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\[2pt]0&1\end{pmatrix}.$$
Its eigenvalues are $\{0,1\}$, so $\|a^{*}a\|=1$. The singular values of $a$ are
 $\sqrt{\text{eig}(a^{*}a)}=\{0,1\}$, hence $\|a\|=1$ and $\|a\|^{2}=1=\|a^{*}a\
|$. ✓ (Hypothesis verified for this $a$.)

- **$a^{2}$:**
$$a^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\[2pt]0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\[2pt]0&0\en
d{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\[2pt]0&0\end{pmatrix}=0.$$
So $\|a^{2}\|=0$.

- **$\|a^{*}\|\,\|a\|$:** by isometry $\|a^{*}\|=\|a\|=1$, so $\|a^{*}\|\,\|a\|=
1$.

### Testing the proposed implication

The claimed inequality $\|a^{*}\|\,\|a\|\le \|a^{2}\|$ becomes
$$1\le 0,$$
which is **false**.

### Conclusion

The hypothesis $\|a\|^{2}\le\|a^{*}a\|$ (which, as shown, forces the involution
to be isometric and the C\*-identity to hold) does **not** imply $\|a^{*}\|\,\|a
\|\le\|a^{2}\|$. The matrix $a=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$ in $M_{2}(\
mathbb{C})$ with the operator norm and conjugate-transpose involution is an expl
icit counterexample: the hypothesis holds (it is a C\*-algebra), yet $\|a^{*}\|\
,\|a\|=1>\|a^{2}\|=0$.

The implication is **false**.
