Let $S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$ and $T_n = \sum_{i=1}^n Y_i$, where $X_i \sim \math
rm{Uniform}(0,1)$ i.i.d. and $Y_i \sim \mathrm{Exp}(1)$ i.i.d. (density $e^{-x}\
mathbf{1}_{\{x\ge 0\}}$).

**Step 1: Means.** We have
$$\mathbb{E}[X_i] = \tfrac{1}{2}, \qquad \mathbb{E}[Y_i] = \int_0^\infty x\,e^{-
x}\,dx = 1.$$

**Step 2: Apply the Strong Law of Large Numbers.** Since $\{X_i\}$ and $\{Y_i\}$
 are each i.i.d. with finite means, by the strong law of large numbers,
$$\frac{S_n}{n} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \frac{1}{2}, \qquad \frac{T_n}{n} \xri
ghtarrow{\text{a.s.}} 1.$$

Therefore, almost surely,
$$\frac{T_n - S_n}{n} = \frac{T_n}{n} - \frac{S_n}{n} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}}
1 - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2}.$$

**Step 3: Conclude the limit.** Since $\frac{T_n - S_n}{n} \to \frac{1}{2} > 0$
almost surely, there exists (a.s.) an $N$ such that for all $n \ge N$,
$$\frac{T_n - S_n}{n} > \frac{1}{4}, \qquad\text{i.e.,}\qquad T_n - S_n > \frac{
n}{4} > 0.$$

In particular, the event $\{S_n \ge T_n\} = \{T_n - S_n \le 0\}$ occurs only fin
itely often, almost surely. By the Borel–Cantelli–type consequence of a.s. conve
rgence,
$$\mathbf{1}_{\{S_n \ge T_n\}} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} 0.$$

Since indicators are bounded, dominated convergence gives
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^n X_i \ge \sum_{i=1}^n Y_i\right) = \lim
_{n\to\infty} P(S_n \ge T_n) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{1}_{\{
S_n \ge T_n\}}\right] = 0.$$

**Sanity check (CLT).** $\mathbb{E}[T_n - S_n] = \tfrac{n}{2}$, $\mathrm{Var}(T_
n - S_n) = n\!\left(1 + \tfrac{1}{12}\right) = \tfrac{13n}{12}$. By the CLT,
$$P(S_n \ge T_n) = P(T_n - S_n \le 0) \approx \Phi\!\left(\frac{-n/2}{\sqrt{13n/
12}}\right) = \Phi\!\left(-\sqrt{\tfrac{3n}{13}}\right) \to 0,$$
consistent with the rigorous SLLN argument.

$$\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^n X_i \ge \sum_{i=1}^n Y_i\right)
= 0.}$$
