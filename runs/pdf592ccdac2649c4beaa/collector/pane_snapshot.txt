## Proof

**Yes, such a subfield exists.**

### Construction

Let $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ denote the algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}$ in $\ma
thbb{C}$. Define

$$F = \bigcup_{\substack{K/\mathbb{Q} \text{ finite} \\ K \subset \overline{\mat
hbb{Q}},\; 5\,\nmid\, [K:\mathbb{Q}]}} K.$$

**$F$ is a field.** If $a \in K_1$ and $b \in K_2$ where $5 \nmid [K_i:\mathbb{Q
}]$, then $a+b, ab, a/b \in K_1 K_2$ (the compositum). Since

$$[K_1 K_2 : \mathbb{Q}] = \frac{[K_1:\mathbb{Q}]\,[K_2:\mathbb{Q}]}{[K_1 \cap K
_2:\mathbb{Q}]},$$

the degree $[K_1 K_2:\mathbb{Q}]$ divides $[K_1:\mathbb{Q}][K_2:\mathbb{Q}]$. Si
nce $5$ divides neither factor, $5 \nmid [K_1 K_2:\mathbb{Q}]$, so $K_1 K_2 \sub
set F$. Hence $F$ is closed under field operations and is a subfield of $\overli
ne{\mathbb{Q}} \subset \mathbb{C}$.

**Key property of $F$:** Every element of $F$ lies in some finite extension $K/\
mathbb{Q}$ with $5 \nmid [K:\mathbb{Q}]$.

### Verification that $F^{*5} \neq F^*$

The polynomial $x^5 - 2$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ by Eisenstein's criter
ion (at the prime $2$), so $[\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/5}):\mathbb{Q}] = 5$.

Since $2 \in \mathbb{Q} \subset F$, we have $2 \in F^*$. Suppose for contradicti
on that $2 \in F^{*5}$, i.e., there exists $b \in F$ with $b^5 = 2$. Then $b \in
 K$ for some finite extension $K/\mathbb{Q}$ with $5 \nmid [K:\mathbb{Q}]$. But
$b^5 = 2$ implies $\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/5}) \subset K$, so $5 = [\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/5}):
\mathbb{Q}]$ divides $[K:\mathbb{Q}]$—a contradiction.

Therefore $2 \notin F^{*5}$, so $F^{*5} \neq F^*$.

### Verification that $F^{*p} = F^*$ for every prime $p \neq 5$

Let $p \neq 5$ be prime and let $a \in F^*$. Then $a \in K$ for some finite exte
nsion $K/\mathbb{Q}$ with $5 \nmid [K:\mathbb{Q}]$.

Consider the polynomial $x^p - a \in K[x]$. Let $b$ be any root (in $\overline{\
mathbb{Q}}$), so $b^p = a$. The minimal polynomial of $b$ over $K$ divides $x^p
- a$, so its degree $[K(b):K]$ divides $p$. Since $p$ is prime:

- **Either** $[K(b):K] = 1$, meaning $b \in K \subset F$. Done.
- **Or** $[K(b):K] = p$, meaning $b \notin K$.

In the second case:

$$[K(b):\mathbb{Q}] = [K(b):K]\,[K:\mathbb{Q}] = p \cdot [K:\mathbb{Q}].$$

Since $p \neq 5$ and $5 \nmid [K:\mathbb{Q}]$, we conclude $5 \nmid [K(b):\mathb
b{Q}]$. Therefore $K(b) \subset F$, so $b \in F$.

In either case, $b \in F$ and $b^p = a$. Since $a \in F^*$ was arbitrary, $F^{*p
} = F^*$.

### Conclusion

The field $F$ is a subfield of $\mathbb{C}$ satisfying $F^{*p} = F^*$ for every
prime $p \neq 5$, while $F^{*5} \neq F^*$ (witnessed by $2 \in F^* \setminus F^{
*5}$). Such a field exists. $\blacksquare$
