Yes, such a vector field always exists.

**Proof.** Let $M$ be a smooth manifold, $m \in M$, and $v \in T_m M$. We constr
uct a global smooth vector field $F$ on $M$ with $F(m) = v$.

Choose a coordinate chart $(U, \varphi)$ centered at $m$ (so $\varphi(m) = 0 \in
 \mathbb{R}^n$, where $n = \dim M$). In this chart, the tangent vector $v$ can b
e written as
$$v = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a^i \left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right|_m$$
for some coefficients $a^i \in \mathbb{R}$.

On $U$, define the local vector field
$$\widetilde{F} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a^i \frac{\partial}{\partial x^i},$$
which is smooth on $U$ and satisfies $\widetilde{F}(m) = v$.

Now choose a smooth bump function $\rho: M \to \mathbb{R}$ with the properties:
- $\operatorname{supp}(\rho) \subseteq U$ (compact support inside $U$),
- $\rho \equiv 1$ on some neighborhood $V \subseteq U$ of $m$.

Such a function exists by the standard construction of bump functions on smooth
manifolds (using a smooth function on $\mathbb{R}^n$ that is $1$ near the origin
 and $0$ outside a compact set, transported to $M$ via the chart and extended by
 zero outside $U$).

Define the global vector field $F$ on $M$ by
$$F(p) = \begin{cases} \rho(p)\, \widetilde{F}(p), & p \in U, \\ 0, & p \in M \s
etminus U. \end{cases}$$

Since $\rho$ is compactly supported in $U$, the two definitions agree on the ove
rlap $U \cap (M \setminus \operatorname{supp}\rho)$ (where both give zero), so $
F$ is well-defined and smooth on all of $M$.

Finally, since $m \in V$ where $\rho \equiv 1$,
$$F(m) = \rho(m)\, \widetilde{F}(m) = 1 \cdot v = v.$$

Thus $F$ is a smooth vector field on $M$ with $F(m) = v$. $\blacksquare$
