## Proof

We show that $X$ is first countable.

Recall that a topological space is **first countable** if every point admits a c
ountable neighborhood basis.

**The space.** $X = \mathbb{Z} \cup \{\infty_1, \infty_2\}$. The points of $\mat
hbb{Z}$ are isolated (each $\{n\}$ is open, as $\mathbb{Z}$ carries the discrete
 topology in the compactification). A set $U\subseteq X$ is a neighborhood of $\
infty_i$ iff $\infty_i\in U$ and $X\setminus U$ is finite.

**Points of $\mathbb{Z}$ are first countable.** Each $n\in\mathbb{Z}$ is isolate
d, so $\bigl\{\{n\}\bigr\}$ is a (finite, hence countable) neighborhood basis at
 $n$.

**The points $\infty_i$ are first countable.** Fix $i\in\{1,2\}$. Consider the f
ull neighborhood system
$$\mathcal{N}_i = \bigl\{\, U\subseteq X : \infty_i\in U,\ X\setminus U \text{ f
inite}\,\bigr\}.$$
A set $U\in\mathcal{N}_i$ is completely determined by its finite complement $F =
 X\setminus U$, which is a finite subset of $X\setminus\{\infty_i\} = \mathbb{Z}
\cup\{\infty_{3-i}\}$. This latter set is countable, and the collection of all f
inite subsets of a countable set is countable (it is $\bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty} [\t
ext{countable set}]^k$, a countable union of countable sets). Hence $\mathcal{N}
_i$ is itself **countable**.

Since $\mathcal{N}_i$ is a neighborhood basis at $\infty_i$ (trivially: every ne
ighborhood of $\infty_i$ belongs to $\mathcal{N}_i$, so it contains a member of
$\mathcal{N}_i$ — namely itself), and $\mathcal{N}_i$ is countable, the point $\
infty_i$ admits a countable neighborhood basis.

**Conclusion.** Every point of $X$ — the isolated points of $\mathbb{Z}$ and the
 two points at infinity $\infty_1,\infty_2$ — admits a countable neighborhood ba
sis. Therefore $X$ is **first countable**.

The key observation is that $X$ is countable, so the family of cofinite subsets
of $X$ containing a fixed point is countable; the entire neighborhood system at
each $\infty_i$ is already countable, giving first countability for free. (Note
that $X$ is not first countable "by accident": the countability of $X$ forces th
e cofinite neighborhood filter at each $\infty_i$ to be countable, which is a st
rictly stronger conclusion than what holds for, say, an uncountable set with the
 cofinite topology, where the cofinite filter is uncountable and no countable lo
cal base exists.)
