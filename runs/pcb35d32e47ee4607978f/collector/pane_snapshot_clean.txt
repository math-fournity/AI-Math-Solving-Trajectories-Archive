## Solution

We compute the volume of the solid of revolution using the disk method. The volu
me is

$$V = \pi \int_0^{\infty} \left(e^{-x}\sin x\right)^2 dx = \pi \int_0^{\infty} e
^{-2x}\sin^2 x\, dx.$$

**Step 1: Reduce the integrand.** Using the half-angle identity $\sin^2 x = \fra
c{1-\cos 2x}{2}$,

$$\int_0^{\infty} e^{-2x}\sin^2 x\, dx = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\infty} e^{-2x}\, dx
 - \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\infty} e^{-2x}\cos 2x\, dx.$$

**Step 2: Evaluate the first integral.**

$$\int_0^{\infty} e^{-2x}\, dx = \left[-\frac{1}{2}e^{-2x}\right]_0^{\infty} = \
frac{1}{2}.$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the second integral.** We use the standard result (obtained v
ia two integrations by parts, or equivalently by taking the real part of $\int_0
^\infty e^{(-2+2i)x}dx$):

$$\int_0^{\infty} e^{-ax}\cos(bx)\, dx = \frac{a}{a^2+b^2}, \qquad a>0.$$

With $a=2$ and $b=2$:

$$\int_0^{\infty} e^{-2x}\cos 2x\, dx = \frac{2}{2^2+2^2} = \frac{2}{8} = \frac{
1}{4}.$$

**Step 4: Combine.**

$$\int_0^{\infty} e^{-2x}\sin^2 x\, dx = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{
2}\cdot\frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{8} = \frac{1}{8}.$$

**Step 5: Conclude.**

$$V = \pi \cdot \frac{1}{8} = \boxed{\dfrac{\pi}{8}}.$$
