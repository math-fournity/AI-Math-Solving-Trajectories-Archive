## Proof

**Setup.** Place the cube as $\{0,1\}^3$ with side length $1$. The available seg
ments are:
- **Edges** (Hamming distance 1, length $1$): 12 segments.
- **Face diagonals** (Hamming distance 2, length $\sqrt{2}$): 12 segments (2 per
 face × 6 faces).
- **Space diagonals** (Hamming distance 3, length $\sqrt{3}$): **not available**
.

The graph on 8 vertices where two vertices are adjacent iff their Hamming distan
ce is 1 or 2 is $K_8$ minus the 4 space-diagonal edges.

**Self-intersection constraint.** The only interior crossing point in the entire
 network is the **center of each face**, where the two diagonals of that face in
tersect. Edges and diagonals on different faces lie in different planes and can
only meet at shared vertices. Therefore, the "no point visited twice" constraint
 reduces to:
1. No vertex is revisited (simple path), and
2. **For each face, at most one of its two diagonals is used** (using both would
 pass through the face center twice).

**Parity structure.** Partition the 8 vertices by parity of coordinate sum:
- **Even:** $000, 011, 101, 110$
- **Odd:** $001, 010, 100, 111$

Face diagonals (Hamming distance 2) connect vertices of the **same parity**; edg
es (Hamming distance 1) connect vertices of **opposite parity**. Within each par
ity class, all 6 pairs are face diagonals, and remarkably, **each of the 6 diago
nals within a parity class lies on a distinct face** (one per face). The face co
nstraint then says: the set of faces used by even-parity diagonals and the set u
sed by odd-parity diagonals must be **disjoint**.

**Lower bound on edges.** A simple path visiting all 8 vertices has 7 segments.
Since diagonals preserve parity, to visit both 4 even and 4 odd vertices we need
 at least **1 edge** (parity switch). Thus at most $7 - 1 = 6$ diagonals, giving
 length $\leq 6\sqrt{2} + 1$.

**Construction achieving $6\sqrt{2} + 1$.** Consider the path:
$$000 \to 011 \to 101 \to 110 \to 111 \to 010 \to 100 \to 001$$

| Segment | Type | Face | Length |
|---------|------|------|--------|
| $000 \to 011$ | diagonal | $x=0$ | $\sqrt{2}$ |
| $011 \to 101$ | diagonal | $z=1$ | $\sqrt{2}$ |
| $101 \to 110$ | diagonal | $x=1$ | $\sqrt{2}$ |
| $110 \to 111$ | **edge** | — | $1$ |
| $111 \to 010$ | diagonal | $y=1$ | $\sqrt{2}$ |
| $010 \to 100$ | diagonal | $z=0$ | $\sqrt{2}$ |
| $100 \to 001$ | diagonal | $y=0$ | $\sqrt{2}$ |

**Verification:**
- All 8 vertices are distinct ✓
- Even-parity diagonals use faces $\{x=0, z=1, x=1\}$; odd-parity diagonals use
faces $\{y=1, z=0, y=0\}$ — **disjoint**, so no face center is visited twice ✓
- The single edge $110 \to 111$ (differ only in $z$) is a valid cube edge ✓
- No two diagonals share a face, so no interior crossing occurs ✓

**Optimality.** Any path with fewer than 8 vertices has at most 6 segments. Even
 if all 6 were diagonals (which is impossible — at most 4 vertices share one par
ity), the length would be $6\sqrt{2} \approx 8.485 < 6\sqrt{2} + 1 \approx 9.485
$. Since we need $\geq 1$ edge to switch parity (proven above), 6 diagonals is t
he maximum achievable, and we constructed a valid path achieving it.

$$\boxed{6\sqrt{2} + 1}$$
