**Proof.**

Let $z=x+iy$. The function $f$ is entire, hence holomorphic on all of $\mathbb{C
}$. For any point $z_0\in\mathbb{C}$ and any radius $r>0$, the mean value proper
ty of holomorphic functions gives

$$
f(z_0)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi} f\!\left(z_0+r e^{i\theta}\right)\,d\theta
.
$$

Taking absolute values and applying the triangle inequality,

$$
|f(z_0)|\le \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bigl|f\!\left(z_0+r e^{i\theta}\right)
\bigr|\,d\theta .\tag{1}
$$

Now average both sides of (1) over $r\in[0,R]$ with respect to the measure $r\,d
r$ (i.e. multiply by $r\,dr$, integrate from $0$ to $R$, and divide by $R^{2}/2$
):

$$
|f(z_0)|\le \frac{1}{\pi R^{2}}\int_{0}^{R}\!\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bigl|f\!\left(z_0+r
 e^{i\theta}\right)\bigr|\,r\,d\theta\,dr
=\frac{1}{\pi R^{2}}\iint_{D(z_0,R)}|f(w)|\,dA(w),
$$

where $D(z_0,R)=\{w:|w-z_0|\le R\}$ and $dA$ is Lebesgue area measure on $\mathb
b{R}^{2}$.

By hypothesis $|f|$ is integrable over $\mathbb{R}^{2}$; set

$$
M:=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f(w)|\,dA(w)<\infty .
$$

Since $|f|\ge 0$,

$$
\iint_{D(z_0,R)}|f(w)|\,dA(w)\le M,
$$

so

$$
|f(z_0)|\le \frac{M}{\pi R^{2}}\qquad\text{for every }R>0.
$$

Letting $R\to\infty$ yields $|f(z_0)|=0$, hence $f(z_0)=0$. The point $z_0\in\ma
thbb{C}$ was arbitrary, so $f\equiv 0$. $\blacksquare$
