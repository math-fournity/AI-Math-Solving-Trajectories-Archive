## Problem

Let $g$ be $2\pi$-periodic and real analytic. Find an upper bound for the best a
pproximation error
$$E_n(g) := \inf_{q \in \mathcal{T}_n} \|g - q\|_{\infty},$$
where $\mathcal{T}_n$ is the space of trigonometric polynomials of degree $\le n
$.

---

## Proof

**Claim.** There exist constants $C > 0$ and $\rho > 0$ (depending on $g$) such
that
$$\boxed{E_n(g) \;\le\; C\, e^{-\rho n}.}$$
That is, the best approximation error decays **exponentially** in $n$.

---

### Step 1. Analytic extension to a strip

Since $g$ is real analytic and $2\pi$-periodic on $\mathbb{R}$, it admits a holo
morphic extension to a horizontal strip
$$S_\rho := \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |\operatorname{Im} z| < \rho\}$$
for some $\rho > 0$, and this extension is $2\pi$-periodic in the real part. (Th
is is the standard characterization: a $2\pi$-periodic function is real analytic
 iff its Fourier series has radius of convergence $>1$ in the $z = e^{ix}$ varia
ble, equivalently iff it extends holomorphically to a strip of positive width.)

Fix any $0 < \rho' < \rho$ and set
$$M(\rho') := \sup_{|\operatorname{Im} z| \le \rho'} |g(z)| < \infty,$$
which is finite because $g$ is continuous on the compact set $\{z : 0 \le \opera
torname{Re} z \le 2\pi,\; |\operatorname{Im} z| \le \rho'\}$ (using $2\pi$-perio
dicity to reduce to a compact fundamental domain).

---

### Step 2. Exponential decay of Fourier coefficients

Let $\hat{g}(k) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} g(x)\,e^{-ikx}\,dx$ be the $k$-th
Fourier coefficient. For $k > 0$, shift the contour of integration up to $\opera
torname{Im} z = \rho'$ (justified by Cauchy's theorem and the $2\pi$-periodicity
, so no boundary terms at the vertical edges):
$$\hat{g}(k) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} g(x + i\rho')\,e^{-ik(x+i\rho')}\,dx
= \frac{e^{k\rho'}}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} g(x+i\rho')\,e^{-ikx}\,dx.$$
Taking absolute values:
$$|\hat{g}(k)| \;\le\; \frac{e^{k\rho'}}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}|g(x+i\rho')|\,dx \;\
le\; M(\rho')\,e^{k\rho'}.$$

Wait — this gives growth, not decay. The sign is wrong. Let me redo: with $e^{-i
kz}$ and $z = x + i\rho'$, we have $e^{-ik(x+i\rho')} = e^{-ikx}\cdot e^{k\rho'}
$, so $|\hat{g}(k)| \le M(\rho') e^{k\rho'}$. That's the bound for shifting *up*
. For $k > 0$ we should shift *down* to $\operatorname{Im} z = -\rho'$:

$$\hat{g}(k) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} g(x - i\rho')\,e^{-ik(x - i\rho')}\,d
x = \frac{e^{-k\rho'}}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} g(x - i\rho')\,e^{-ikx}\,dx,$$
so
$$|\hat{g}(k)| \;\le\; M(\rho')\,e^{-k\rho'}, \qquad k > 0.$$

Similarly, for $k < 0$, shift *up* to $\operatorname{Im} z = +\rho'$:
$$|\hat{g}(k)| \;\le\; M(\rho')\,e^{-|k|\rho'}, \qquad k < 0.$$

Combining:
$$|\hat{g}(k)| \;\le\; M(\rho')\,e^{-\rho'|k|}, \qquad \forall\, k \in \mathbb{Z
}\setminus\{0\}.$$

---

### Step 3. Bounding the best approximation by the Fourier tail

The $n$-th symmetric partial Fourier sum
$$S_n g(x) := \sum_{|k|\le n} \hat{g}(k)\,e^{ikx}$$
belongs to $\mathcal{T}_n$. Therefore
$$E_n(g) \;\le\; \|g - S_n g\|_{\infty} \;\le\; \sum_{|k|>n} |\hat{g}(k)| \;\le\
; 2\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty} M(\rho')\,e^{-\rho' k}.$$

Evaluating the geometric series:
$$\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty} e^{-\rho' k} = \frac{e^{-\rho'(n+1)}}{1 - e^{-\rho'}}.$$

Hence
$$E_n(g) \;\le\; \frac{2\,M(\rho')}{1 - e^{-\rho'}}\,e^{-\rho'(n+1)} \;=\; \frac
{2\,M(\rho')\,e^{-\rho'}}{1 - e^{-\rho'}}\,e^{-\rho' n} \;=\; \frac{2\,M(\rho')}
{e^{\rho'} - 1}\,e^{-\rho' n}.$$

---

### Step 4. Conclusion

Setting
$$C := \frac{2\,M(\rho')}{e^{\rho'} - 1} > 0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad \rho := \rho
' > 0,$$
we obtain the **exponential convergence bound**:

$$\boxed{E_n(g) \;=\; \inf_{q\in\mathcal{T}_n}\|g - q\|_\infty \;\le\; C\,e^{-\r
ho n}, \qquad n = 0,1,2,\dots}$$

where $\rho$ can be taken as any positive number strictly less than the half-wid
th of the maximal strip to which $g$ extends holomorphically, and $C = \frac{2\,
M(\rho)}{e^{\rho}-1}$ with $M(\rho) = \sup_{|\operatorname{Im}z|\le\rho}|g(z)|$.

**Interpretation.** Real analyticity of a $2\pi$-periodic function is *equivalen
t* to exponential decay of its best trigonometric approximation error. The rate
$\rho$ is governed by the width of the strip of analyticity: the wider the strip
, the faster the exponential convergence. This is the trigonometric analogue of
the classical result that real analytic functions on $[-1,1]$ are approximated b
y algebraic polynomials at an exponential rate (Bernstein's theorem).
