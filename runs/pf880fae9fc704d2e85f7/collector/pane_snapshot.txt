## Proof

Let $A, B, C$ denote the angles of triangle $ABC$, with $A + B + C = \pi$.

### Step 1: Circles tangent to both rays at a vertex

Consider vertex $A$ with rays $(AB$ and $(AC$ forming angle $A$. Any circle tang
ent to both rays has its center on the **internal** angle bisector of $\angle A$
. (A center on the external bisector yields perpendicular feet on the *extension
s* of the rays beyond $A$, not on the rays themselves.)

Place $A$ at the origin with the internal bisector along the positive $x$-axis.
A circle centered at distance $d$ from $A$ on this bisector, tangent to both ray
s, has radius
$$r = d\sin\frac{A}{2}.$$

### Step 2: Position of the circumcenter

The circumcenter $O$ is at distance $R$ from $A$. By the **alternate segment the
orem**, the tangent to $\mathcal{C}$ at $A$ makes angle $C$ with chord $AB$. Sin
ce $OA \perp$ (tangent at $A$), the angle between $OA$ and $AB$ is $\tfrac{\pi}{
2}-C$. The bisector makes angle $\tfrac{A}{2}$ with $AB$, so the angle $\theta$
between $OA$ and the bisector is
$$\theta = \tfrac{\pi}{2} - C - \tfrac{A}{2} = \tfrac{A}{2} + B - \tfrac{\pi}{2}
,$$
using $A+B+C=\pi$. Hence
$$\cos\theta = \sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{A}{2}+B\Bigr).$$

### Step 3: The two tangent circles

The distance from $O$ to the circle's center (at distance $d$ from $A$ on the bi
sector) is
$$OP = \sqrt{d^2 + R^2 - 2dR\cos\theta}.$$

**Internal tangency** to $\mathcal{C}$ ($OP = R - r$): Squaring $OP = R - d\sin\
frac{A}{2}$ and simplifying:
$$d^2\cos^2\frac{A}{2} = 2Rd\Bigl(\cos\theta - \sin\tfrac{A}{2}\Bigr).$$
Using
$$\cos\theta - \sin\tfrac{A}{2} = \sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{A}{2}+B\Bigr)-\sin\tfrac{A}
{2} = 2\cos\frac{A+B}{2}\sin\frac{B}{2} = 2\sin\frac{C}{2}\sin\frac{B}{2},$$
we obtain
$$r_{\mathrm{in}} = \frac{4R\sin\frac{A}{2}\sin\frac{B}{2}\sin\frac{C}{2}}{\cos^
2\frac{A}{2}}.$$

**External tangency** to $\mathcal{C}$ ($OP = R + r$): Similarly,
$$d^2\cos^2\frac{A}{2} = 2Rd\Bigl(\cos\theta + \sin\tfrac{A}{2}\Bigr),$$
and
$$\cos\theta + \sin\tfrac{A}{2} = \sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{A}{2}+B\Bigr)+\sin\tfrac{A}
{2} = 2\sin\frac{A+B}{2}\cos\frac{B}{2} = 2\cos\frac{C}{2}\cos\frac{B}{2},$$
giving
$$r_{\mathrm{out}} = \frac{4R\sin\frac{A}{2}\cos\frac{B}{2}\cos\frac{C}{2}}{\cos
^2\frac{A}{2}}.$$

These are the only two circles tangent to $\mathcal{C}$ and both rays $(AB$, $(A
C$.

### Step 4: The ratio

$$\alpha = \frac{r_{\mathrm{in}}}{r_{\mathrm{out}}} = \frac{\sin\frac{B}{2}\sin\
frac{C}{2}}{\cos\frac{B}{2}\cos\frac{C}{2}} = \tan\frac{B}{2}\tan\frac{C}{2}.$$

Note $\alpha < 1$ since $\frac{B}{2}+\frac{C}{2} = \frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{A}{2} < \
frac{\pi}{2}$, so $\cos\bigl(\frac{B}{2}+\frac{C}{2}\bigr)>0$, which gives $\cos
\frac{B}{2}\cos\frac{C}{2} > \sin\frac{B}{2}\sin\frac{C}{2}$.

By symmetry (cyclically permuting vertices):
$$\beta = \tan\frac{A}{2}\tan\frac{C}{2}, \qquad \gamma = \tan\frac{A}{2}\tan\fr
ac{B}{2}.$$

### Step 5: The key identity

Set $x = \tan\frac{A}{2},\; y = \tan\frac{B}{2},\; z = \tan\frac{C}{2}$. Since $
\frac{A}{2}+\frac{B}{2}+\frac{C}{2} = \frac{\pi}{2}$, the three-angle tangent ad
dition formula gives
$$\tan\!\Bigl(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{B}{2}+\frac{C}{2}\Bigr) = \frac{x+y+z-xyz}{1-xy-
yz-zx}.$$

The left-hand side is $\tan\frac{\pi}{2}$, which is undefined, so the denominato
r must vanish:
$$xy + yz + zx = 1.$$

Substituting back:
$$\tan\frac{A}{2}\tan\frac{B}{2} + \tan\frac{B}{2}\tan\frac{C}{2} + \tan\frac{C}
{2}\tan\frac{A}{2} = 1,$$

that is,
$$\boxed{\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 1.} \qquad \blacksquare$$
