### Setup

We evaluate
$$I=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x\cos(ax)}{(x^{2}+3)(x^{2}+s^{2})}\,dx,\qquad s
>0.$$

We use the Laplace-transform identity (valid for $b>0$)
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-bt}\sin(xt)\,dt=\frac{x}{b^{2}+x^{2}},\qquad\text{i.e.}\q
quad \frac{x}{x^{2}+b^{2}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-bt}\sin(xt)\,dt. \tag{1}$$

### Partial fractions

For $s^{2}\neq 3$,
$$\frac{x}{(x^{2}+3)(x^{2}+s^{2})}=\frac{1}{s^{2}-3}\left(\frac{x}{x^{2}+3}-\fra
c{x}{x^{2}+s^{2}}\right). \tag{2}$$

(The case $s^{2}=3$ follows by continuity, since the integrand becomes $\frac{x\
cos(ax)}{(x^{2}+3)^{2}}$, which is also odd — see the symmetry remark at the end
.)

### Apply the Laplace representation (1) to each term

Using (1) with $b=\sqrt{3}$ and $b=s$ in (2):
$$\frac{x}{(x^{2}+3)(x^{2}+s^{2})}=\frac{1}{s^{2}-3}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\bigl(e^{
-\sqrt{3}\,t}-e^{-st}\bigr)\sin(xt)\,dt. \tag{3}$$

Substitute into $I$ and interchange the order of integration (justified by absol
ute convergence of the $t$-integral for each fixed $x$, and the oscillatory natu
re of the $x$-integral interpreted as a principal value):
$$I=\frac{1}{s^{2}-3}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\bigl(e^{-\sqrt{3}\,t}-e^{-st}\bigr)\!\l
eft[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cos(ax)\sin(xt)\,dx\right]dt. \tag{4}$$

### Evaluate the inner integral

Use the product-to-sum identity
$$\cos(ax)\sin(xt)=\tfrac12\bigl[\sin\bigl((t+a)x\bigr)+\sin\bigl((t-a)x\bigr)\b
igr]. \tag{5}$$

For every real $k$, the principal-value integral satisfies
$$\operatorname{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\sin(kx)\,dx=0, \tag{6}$$
because $\sin(kx)$ is an **odd** function of $x$ and the domain $(-\infty,\infty
)$ is symmetric. (Equivalently, $\int_{-R}^{R}\sin(kx)\,dx=0$ for every $R$, so
the symmetric limit is $0$.)

Applying (5) and (6) with $k=t+a$ and $k=t-a$:
$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cos(ax)\sin(xt)\,dx=\tfrac12\bigl[0+0\bigr]=0\qquad\fo
rall\,t\ge 0. \tag{7}$$

### Conclude

Inserting (7) into (4):
$$I=\frac{1}{s^{2}-3}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\bigl(e^{-\sqrt{3}\,t}-e^{-st}\bigr)\cdo
t 0\,dt=0.$$

### Consistency check (symmetry)

The result is confirmed by a direct symmetry argument: $x$ is odd, $\cos(ax)$ is
 even, and $(x^{2}+3)(x^{2}+s^{2})$ is even, so the integrand
$$f(x)=\frac{x\cos(ax)}{(x^{2}+3)(x^{2}+s^{2})}$$
satisfies $f(-x)=-f(x)$, i.e. it is **odd**. Hence its integral over the symmetr
ic interval $(-\infty,\infty)$ vanishes. This covers the degenerate case $s^{2}=
3$ as well, where the integrand $\frac{x\cos(ax)}{(x^{2}+3)^{2}}$ is likewise od
d.

---

### Result

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x\cos(ax)}{(x^{2}+3)(x^{2}+s^
{2})}\,dx=0}$$

The Laplace-transform method reproduces the value dictated by parity: the inner
$x$-integral reduces to principal-value integrals of odd sine functions, each of
 which vanishes over the symmetric domain.
