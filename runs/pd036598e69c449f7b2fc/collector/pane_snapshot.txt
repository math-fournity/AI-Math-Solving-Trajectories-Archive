# Proof

We show that no such function exists.

**Key fact (Liouville).** The class of elementary functions is closed under diff
erentiation: if $g(x)$ is elementary, then $g'(x)$ is also elementary.

This follows because each elementary building block — polynomials, rational func
tions, $e^x$, $\ln x$, $\sin x$, $\cos x$, $\arcsin x$, etc., and algebraic func
tions — has an elementary derivative, and the sum, product, quotient, and chain
rules express the derivative of a combination as a combination of elementary fun
ctions. Hence the derivative of any elementary function is elementary.

Now suppose, for contradiction, that $f(x)$ is a function whose antiderivative i
s elementary but whose derivative is not elementary.

Since the antiderivative of $f$ is elementary, there exists an elementary functi
on $F(x)$ with
$$F'(x) = f(x).$$

Because $F$ is elementary and elementary functions are closed under differentiat
ion, $f = F'$ is elementary.

Applying closure under differentiation a second time, $f' = (F')' = F''$ is also
 elementary, since $F'$ is elementary.

Thus $f'$ is elementary — contradicting the assumption that $f'$ cannot be expre
ssed in terms of elementary functions.

The contradiction shows that the two required conditions are incompatible: eleme
ntary-antiderivative forces the derivative to be elementary as well.

**Conclusion.** No, there does not exist a function whose antiderivative is elem
entary but whose derivative is not elementary.
