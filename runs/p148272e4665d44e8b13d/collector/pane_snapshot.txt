## Reduction of Chevalley's Theorem to the Affine Case

**Chevalley's Theorem (statement).** Let $f : X \to Y$ be a morphism of finite p
resentation between schemes. Then the image $f(C)$ of any constructible subset $
C \subseteq X$ is a constructible subset of $Y$.

The proof proceeds by two reductions. We use throughout two standard facts:

- **(Locality of constructibility)** A subset $E$ of a scheme $S$ is constructib
le in $S$ if and only if $E \cap W$ is constructible in $W$ for every affine ope
n $W \subseteq S$ (equivalently, for the members of an affine open cover). In pa
rticular, a finite union of constructible sets is constructible.
- **(Stability of finite presentation)** Morphisms of finite presentation are st
able under base change and under composition. Open immersions are of finite pres
entation.

---

### Reduction 1: Reduce to the case $Y$ is affine

Cover $Y$ by affine open subschemes $V_i = \operatorname{Spec} B_i$. Constructib
ility of $f(C)$ in $Y$ is local on $Y$, so it suffices to show that $f(C) \cap V
_i$ is constructible in $V_i$ for each $i$.

Set $X_i := f^{-1}(V_i)$. The square

$$
\begin{CD}
X_i @>>> X \\
@V{f_i}VV @VV{f}V \\
V_i @>>> Y
\end{CD}
$$

is cartesian: $X_i \to V_i$ is the base change of $f$ by the open immersion $V_i
 \hookrightarrow Y$. Since open immersions are of finite presentation and finite
 presentation is stable under base change, the restricted morphism

$$f_i : X_i \longrightarrow V_i$$

is again of finite presentation. Moreover $C \cap X_i$ is constructible in $X_i$
 (constructibility is local on the source).

Now observe the key set-theoretic identity:

$$f(C) \cap V_i \;=\; f_i\!\bigl(C \cap X_i\bigr).$$

The inclusion "$\subseteq$" holds because any $y \in f(C) \cap V_i$ is $y = f(x)
$ for some $x \in C$, and $f(x) \in V_i$ forces $x \in f^{-1}(V_i) = X_i$, so $x
 \in C \cap X_i$ and $y = f_i(x)$. The reverse inclusion is immediate.

Therefore, **if the theorem holds for the affine target $V_i$**, then $f_i(C \ca
p X_i) = f(C) \cap V_i$ is constructible in $V_i$. Since this holds for every $i
$ in the affine open cover, locality of constructibility gives that $f(C)$ is co
nstructible in $Y$.

> **Conclusion of Reduction 1.** It suffices to prove the theorem when $Y = \ope
ratorname{Spec} B$ is affine.

---

### Reduction 2: With $Y$ affine, reduce to the case $X$ is affine

Now assume $Y = \operatorname{Spec} B$ is affine. Since $f : X \to Y$ is of fini
te presentation and $Y$ is affine (hence quasi-compact), the scheme $X$ is quasi
-compact. Consequently $X$ admits a **finite** affine open cover

$$X = \bigcup_{j=1}^{n} U_j, \qquad U_j = \operatorname{Spec} A_j.$$

For each $j$, the restriction

$$f\big|_{U_j} : U_j \longrightarrow Y$$

is the composition $U_j \xrightarrow{\;\iota_j\;} X \xrightarrow{\;f\;} Y$, wher
e $\iota_j$ is the open immersion. Both $\iota_j$ (open immersion) and $f$ (fini
te presentation) are of finite presentation, and finite presentation is stable u
nder composition, so $f|_{U_j} : U_j \to Y$ is of finite presentation.

Since constructibility is local on the source, $C \cap U_j$ is constructible in
$U_j$ for each $j$. Now decompose the image:

$$f(C) \;=\; f\!\Bigl(\bigcup_{j=1}^{n} (C \cap U_j)\Bigr) \;=\; \bigcup_{j=1}^{
n} f\big|_{U_j}\!\bigl(C \cap U_j\bigr).$$

**If the theorem holds for affine source and affine target**, then each $f|_{U_j
}(C \cap U_j)$ is constructible in $Y$. A finite union of constructible sets is
constructible, so $f(C)$ is constructible in $Y$.

> **Conclusion of Reduction 2.** With $Y$ affine, it suffices to prove the theor
em when $X = \operatorname{Spec} A$ is also affine.

---

### Summary

Combining the two reductions:

1. **Target reduction.** Constructibility of the image is local on $Y$; restrict
ing to an affine open $V_i \subseteq Y$ base-changes $f$ to $f_i : f^{-1}(V_i) \
to V_i$, preserving finite presentation, and $f(C) \cap V_i = f_i(C \cap f^{-1}(
V_i))$. So it suffices to take $Y = \operatorname{Spec} B$ affine.

2. **Source reduction.** With $Y$ affine, finite presentation forces $X$ quasi-c
ompact, yielding a **finite** affine open cover $\{U_j\}$. Each restriction $U_j
 \to Y$ remains of finite presentation (composition with an open immersion), $C
\cap U_j$ is constructible in $U_j$, and $f(C) = \bigcup_j f|_{U_j}(C \cap U_j)$
 is a finite union. So it suffices to take $X = \operatorname{Spec} A$ affine.

The three ingredients that make the reduction work are: (i) locality of construc
tibility on both source and target, (ii) stability of finite presentation under
base change and composition with open immersions, and (iii) quasi-compactness of
 $X$ over an affine base, which furnishes the *finite* affine cover needed so th
at the union of constructible images remains constructible.
