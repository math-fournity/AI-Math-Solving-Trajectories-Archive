## Proof

Let $a, b, c$ be the side lengths of triangle $ABC$ (opposite to $A, B, C$), $s
= \frac{a+b+c}{2}$ the semi-perimeter, $r$ the circumradius, and $P$ the area of
 $ABC$.

### Step 1: Compute the angles of triangle $A'B'C'$

The angle bisector from $A$ meets the circumcircle again at $A'$, which is the *
*midpoint of arc $BC$ not containing $A$** (since the bisector of inscribed angl
e $A$ bisects the subtended arc $BC$). Similarly for $B'$ and $C'$.

Going around the circle, the arc measures between consecutive points among $\{A,
 B, C, A', B', C'\}$ are:

$$A \xrightarrow{C} C' \xrightarrow{C} B \xrightarrow{A} A' \xrightarrow{A} C \x
rightarrow{B} B' \xrightarrow{B} A$$

The arcs of triangle $A'B'C'$ are:
- Arc $A'$ to $B'$ (through $C$): $A + B$
- Arc $B'$ to $C'$ (through $A$): $B + C$
- Arc $C'$ to $A'$ (through $B$): $C + A$

The angle at $A'$ subtends arc $B'C'$ not containing $A'$, which has measure $B+
C$. So the angle at $A'$ is $\frac{B+C}{2} = \frac{\pi - A}{2}$. Similarly, the
angles at $B'$ and $C'$ are $\frac{\pi-B}{2}$ and $\frac{\pi-C}{2}$.

### Step 2: Express $Q$ using the circumradius formula

Since $A'B'C'$ is inscribed in the circumcircle of radius $r$, its area is:

$$Q = 2r^2 \sin\frac{\pi-A}{2}\sin\frac{\pi-B}{2}\sin\frac{\pi-C}{2} = 2r^2 \cos
\frac{A}{2}\cos\frac{B}{2}\cos\frac{C}{2}.$$

### Step 3: Simplify $\cos\frac{A}{2}\cos\frac{B}{2}\cos\frac{C}{2}$

Using the half-angle formula $\cos\frac{A}{2} = \sqrt{\frac{s(s-a)}{bc}}$ (and c
yclically):

$$\cos\frac{A}{2}\cos\frac{B}{2}\cos\frac{C}{2} = \sqrt{\frac{s(s-a)}{bc}\cdot\f
rac{s(s-b)}{ca}\cdot\frac{s(s-c)}{ab}} = \frac{s\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}}{abc} =
\frac{sP}{abc},$$

where we used Heron's formula $P = \sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}$.

### Step 4: Obtain $Q = \frac{rs}{2}$

Since $P = \frac{abc}{4r}$ (the circumradius area formula), we have $abc = 4rP$.
 Substituting:

$$Q = 2r^2 \cdot \frac{sP}{abc} = 2r^2 \cdot \frac{sP}{4rP} = \frac{rs}{2}.$$

### Step 5: Reduce the inequality

The inequality $16Q^3 \geq 27r^4P$ becomes:

$$16\left(\frac{rs}{2}\right)^3 \geq 27r^4P \implies 2r^3s^3 \geq 27r^4P.$$

Dividing by $r^3 > 0$:

$$2s^3 \geq 27rP.$$

Since $rP = \frac{abc}{4P}\cdot P = \frac{abc}{4}$:

$$2s^3 \geq \frac{27abc}{4} \implies 8s^3 \geq 27abc \implies (a+b+c)^3 \geq 27a
bc.$$

### Step 6: Apply AM-GM

By the **AM-GM inequality** applied to the positive side lengths $a, b, c$:

$$\frac{a+b+c}{3} \geq \sqrt[3]{abc} \implies (a+b+c)^3 \geq 27abc.$$

This holds with equality if and only if $a = b = c$, i.e., triangle $ABC$ is equ
ilateral. $\blacksquare$
