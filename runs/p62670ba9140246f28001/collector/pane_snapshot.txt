# Solution

**Claim.** For every positive integer $n$, there exists a group $G$ with a finit
e-index subgroup $H$ such that every subgroup $N \le H$ with $N \trianglelefteq
G$ satisfies $[G:N] \ge n!$.

**Construction.** Take $G = S_n$ (the symmetric group on $\{1,\dots,n\}$) and $H
 = \operatorname{Stab}(n) \cong S_{n-1}$, the stabilizer of the point $n$. Then
$[G:H] = n$, so $H$ has finite index in $G$.

---

**Key observation.** If $N \le H$ and $N \trianglelefteq G$, then $N$ is a norma
l subgroup of $S_n$ that happens to be contained in $H$. We show that the only s
uch $N$ is the trivial subgroup $\{1\}$, which gives $[G:N] = |S_n| = n! \ge n!$
.

It suffices to show that every non-trivial normal subgroup of $S_n$ fails to be
contained in $H$.

---

**Case $n \ge 5$.** The normal subgroups of $S_n$ are exactly $\{1\},\; A_n,\; S
_n$.

- $S_n \not\le H$ since $H$ is a proper subgroup.
- $A_n \not\le H$: we have $|A_n| = n!/2$ and $|H| = (n-1)!$. For $n \ge 3$, $\;
n!/2 = \frac{n}{2}\cdot(n-1)! > (n-1)!$, so $|A_n| > |H|$, hence $A_n \not\le H$
.

Thus $N = \{1\}$ and $[G:N] = n! \ge n!$. ✓

---

**Case $n = 4$.** The normal subgroups of $S_4$ are $\{1\},\; V_4,\; A_4,\; S_4$
, where $V_4 = \{(),\, (12)(34),\, (13)(24),\, (14)(23)\}$ is the Klein four-gro
up.

- $S_4 \not\le H$ (proper subgroup).
- $A_4 \not\le H$: $|A_4| = 12 > 6 = |H|$.
- $V_4 \not\le H$: the element $(12)(34) \in V_4$ sends $4 \mapsto 3$, so it doe
s not fix $n = 4$; hence $(12)(34) \notin H = \operatorname{Stab}(4)$, and there
fore $V_4 \not\le H$.

Thus $N = \{1\}$ and $[G:N] = 24 = 4! \ge 4!$. ✓

---

**Case $n = 3$.** The normal subgroups of $S_3$ are $\{1\},\; A_3,\; S_3$.

- $S_3 \not\le H$ (proper).
- $A_3 \not\le H$: $|A_3| = 3 > 2 = |H|$.

Thus $N = \{1\}$ and $[G:N] = 6 = 3! \ge 3!$. ✓

---

**Case $n = 2$.** $G = S_2 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, $H = \{1\}$. The norma
l subgroups are $\{1\}$ and $S_2$; only $\{1\} \le H$. So $[G:N] = 2 = 2! \ge 2!
$. ✓

---

**Case $n = 1$.** $G = S_1 = \{1\}$, $H = \{1\}$. Then $[G:\{1\}] = 1 = 1! \ge 1
!$. ✓

---

**Conclusion.** For every positive integer $n$, the pair $(G, H) = (S_n,\, \oper
atorname{Stab}(n))$ satisfies the required property: $H$ has finite index in $G$
, and the only subgroup of $H$ that is normal in $G$ is the trivial subgroup, gi
ving index exactly $n! \ge n!$.

Therefore, **yes**, such a group exists for every $n$.
