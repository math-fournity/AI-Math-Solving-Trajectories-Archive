# Solution

**Claim:** The maximum value is $\boxed{225}$.

## Key Identity

For any $n$ points $P_1, \dots, P_n$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$:

$$\sum_{1 \le i < j \le n} |P_iP_j|^2 = n\sum_{i=1}^n |P_i|^2 - \left|\sum_{i=1}
^n P_i\right|^2.$$

*Proof of identity.* Compute
$$\sum_{i<j}|P_i-P_j|^2 = \tfrac12\sum_{i,j}|P_i-P_j|^2 = \tfrac12\sum_{i,j}\big
l(|P_i|^2+|P_j|^2-2\,P_i\cdot P_j\bigr).$$
The first two terms give $\tfrac12(2n\sum_i|P_i|^2)=n\sum_i|P_i|^2$, and the cro
ss term gives $-\tfrac12\cdot 2|\sum_i P_i|^2=-|\sum_i P_i|^2$. ∎

## Upper Bound

With $n=15$ and the constraint $|P_i|^2 \le 1$ for every $i$:

$$\sum_{i<j}|P_iP_j|^2 = 15\sum_{i=1}^{15}|P_i|^2 - \left|\sum_{i=1}^{15}P_i\rig
ht|^2 \le 15\cdot 15 - 0 = 225,$$

using $\sum_i|P_i|^2 \le 15$ (since each $|P_i|^2\le 1$) and $\left|\sum_i P_i\r
ight|^2 \ge 0$.

## Achievability

It remains to exhibit 15 points on the unit sphere with centroid at the origin (
so that $\sum|P_i|^2=15$ and $|\sum P_i|^2=0$).

**Construction.** Place three regular pentagons on the unit sphere:
- 5 vertices of a regular pentagon on the equator $z=0$,
- 5 vertices of a regular pentagon at latitude $+\theta$,
- 5 vertices of a regular pentagon at latitude $-\theta$,

for any $\theta\in(0,\pi/2)$. By the 5-fold rotational symmetry of each pentagon
, the $x$- and $y$-components of the sum within each pentagon vanish. The equato
rial pentagon contributes $0$ to the $z$-sum, while the pentagons at $+\theta$ a
nd $-\theta$ contribute equal and opposite $z$-amounts. Hence

$$\sum_{i=1}^{15} P_i = 0.$$

All 15 points lie on the unit sphere, so $\sum_i|P_i|^2 = 15$. Substituting into
 the identity:

$$\sum_{i<j}|P_iP_j|^2 = 15\cdot 15 - 0 = 225.$$

## Conclusion

The upper bound $225$ is attained by the construction above, so the maximum poss
ible value is

$$\boxed{225}.$$
