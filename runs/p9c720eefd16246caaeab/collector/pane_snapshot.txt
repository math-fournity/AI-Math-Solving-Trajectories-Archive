## Solution

**Answer: No.** There exist triples $(a,b,c)$ of positive irrationals with $a+b+
c=1$ that never become balanced under any number of iterations of $B$.

### Key observation

For $x\in(0,1)\setminus\{1/2\}$, the map satisfies $f(x)=\{2x\}$ (fractional par
t of $2x$), since $2x-1=\{2x\}$ when $x>1/2$ and $2x=\{2x\}$ when $x<1/2$. Irrat
ionals never equal $1/2$, and $\{2^n x\}$ is irrational whenever $x$ is, so no i
terate ever lands exactly on $1/2$. Hence after $n$ applications, each coordinat
e equals $\{2^n(\cdot)\}$.

Write each $x\in(0,1)$ in its (unique) binary expansion $x=0.d_1(x)d_2(x)d_3(x)\
ldots_2$ (unique because $x$ is irrational, hence non-dyadic). Then
$$\{2^n x\}<\tfrac12 \iff d_{n+1}(x)=0.$$
So the triple is **balanced at step $n$** (step $0$ = original) iff
$$d_{n+1}(a)=d_{n+1}(b)=d_{n+1}(c)=0.$$

### A lemma constraining "never balanced" triples

Let $s_n:=d_n(a)+d_n(b)+d_n(c)\in\{0,1,2,3\}$. Since $a+b+c=1$,
$$\sum_{n\ge1} s_n\,2^{-n}=a+b+c=1.$$
If the triple is **never** balanced, then for every $n\ge1$ at least one of the
three digits is $1$, i.e. $s_n\ge1$. But then
$$1=\sum_{n\ge1}s_n 2^{-n}\ge\sum_{n\ge1}2^{-n}=1,$$
with equality **only if** $s_n=1$ for every $n\ge1$. Therefore:

> **Lemma.** If a triple of positive irrationals summing to $1$ is never balance
d, then at every binary position exactly one of $a,b,c$ has digit $1$ and the ot
her two have digit $0$.

Conversely, any triple with "exactly one $1$ per position" sums to $1$ (since $\
sum 2^{-n}=1$) and is never balanced. So it suffices to construct three irration
als whose binary digit-$1$ positions partition $\mathbb{N}^+$, each part giving
a non-eventually-periodic digit sequence (so each number is irrational).

### Explicit counterexample

Partition $\mathbb{N}^+$ into three sets:
- $A=\{2^k:k\ge0\}=\{1,2,4,8,16,\ldots\}$,
- $B=\{3\cdot 2^k:k\ge0\}=\{3,6,12,24,48,\ldots\}$,
- $C=\mathbb{N}^+\setminus(A\cup B)$ (everything else: $5,7,9,10,11,13,\ldots$).

These are disjoint (no $3\cdot 2^k$ is a power of $2$) and cover $\mathbb{N}^+$.
 Define
$$a=\sum_{n\in A}2^{-n},\qquad b=\sum_{n\in B}2^{-n},\qquad c=\sum_{n\in C}2^{-n
}.$$

**Positivity and sum.** Each set is nonempty, so $a,b,c>0$; clearly $a,b,c<1$; a
nd since the three sets partition $\mathbb{N}^+$,
$$a+b+c=\sum_{n\ge1}2^{-n}=1.$$

**Irrationality.** A real number is rational iff its binary expansion is eventua
lly periodic. The indicator sequence of $A=\{2^k\}$ is not eventually periodic (
the gaps $2^k,2^{k+1}$ grow unboundedly), so $a$ is irrational. Likewise the ind
icator of $B=\{3\cdot 2^k\}$ is not eventually periodic, so $b$ is irrational. F
or $c$, its digit is $0$ exactly on $A\cup B$, a non-eventually-periodic set (it
 contains the powers of $2$ with unbounded gaps), so $c$'s digit sequence is not
 eventually periodic, hence $c$ is irrational.

**Never balanced.** At every binary position $n\ge1$, exactly one of $a,b,c$ has
 digit $1$. Hence there is no position at which all three digits are $0$, which
by the observation above means the triple is not balanced at step $0$, nor at an
y step $n\ge1$. Equivalently, at every step at least one coordinate is $\ge 1/2$
.

### Conclusion

The triple $(a,b,c)$ constructed above consists of positive irrationals, satisfi
es $a+b+c=1$, and never becomes balanced under any finite number of iterations o
f $B$. Therefore finitely many iterations of the balancing act do **not** always
 eventually produce a balanced triple.
