## Proof

Let the two distinct integer roots of $x^2+bx+a=0$ be $p,q$, and the two distinc
t integer roots of $x^2+cx+a=1$ (i.e. $x^2+cx+(a-1)=0$) be $r,s$. By Vieta's for
mulas:

$$a = pq, \qquad a-1 = rs,$$

so $pq - rs = 1$. The constraints require $p,q,r,s$ to be integers strictly less
 than $-1$, i.e. $\le -2$, with $p\ne q$ and $r\ne s$.

**Reduction to a number-theoretic condition.** Set $p=-m,\ q=-n,\ r=-u,\ s=-v$ w
ith $m,n,u,v \ge 2$ integers, $m\ne n$, $u\ne v$. Then

$$a = mn, \qquad a-1 = uv, \qquad mn - uv = 1.$$

Thus we need two consecutive integers $a$ and $a-1$, **each** expressible as a p
roduct of two **distinct** integers $\ge 2$. Since $m,n\ge 2$, we have $a=mn\ge
4$ and $a-1=uv\ge 4$, giving $a\ge 5$.

**Checking small values.** We test $a=5,6,\dots,15$:

| $a$ | $a=mn$ (distinct $m,n\ge2$)? | $a-1=uv$ (distinct $u,v\ge2$)? | Result |
|-----|------------------------------|--------------------------------|--------|
| 5 | $5$ prime → only $1\cdot5$ (invalid) | — | fail |
| 6 | $2\cdot3$ ✓ | $5$ prime → fail | fail |
| 7 | prime → fail | — | fail |
| 8 | $2\cdot4$ ✓ | $7$ prime → fail | fail |
| 9 | $3\cdot3$ (not distinct), $1\cdot9$ (invalid) | — | fail |
| 10 | $2\cdot5$ ✓ | $9$: $3\cdot3$ not distinct → fail | fail |
| 11 | prime → fail | — | fail |
| 12 | $2\cdot6$ or $3\cdot4$ ✓ | $11$ prime → fail | fail |
| 13 | prime → fail | — | fail |
| 14 | $2\cdot7$ ✓ | $13$ prime → fail | fail |
| 15 | $3\cdot5$ ✓ | $14=2\cdot7$ ✓ | **works** |

**Verification for $a=15$.** Choose $m=3,\ n=5$ so $p=-3,\ q=-5$:
$$x^2+bx+15=0,\quad p+q=-8=-b\Rightarrow b=8,\quad pq=15=a.$$
Roots $-3,-5$ are distinct integers, both $<-1$. ✓

Choose $u=2,\ v=7$ so $r=-2,\ s=-7$:
$$x^2+cx+14=0,\quad r+s=-9=-c\Rightarrow c=9,\quad rs=14=a-1.$$
Roots $-2,-7$ are distinct integers, both $<-1$. ✓

All four roots $-3,-5,-2,-7$ are strictly less than $-1$, and each pair is disti
nct. The discriminants are $b^2-4a=64-60=4>0$ and $c^2-4(a-1)=81-56=25>0$, confi
rming two distinct real (integer) roots.

**Minimality.** Every $a$ with $5\le a\le 14$ fails (shown in the table above),
and $a\le 4$ is impossible since $a=mn\ge4$ forces $m=n=2$ (not distinct) or req
uires a factor $<2$. Hence no value smaller than $15$ satisfies both constraints
.

$$\boxed{a = 15}$$
