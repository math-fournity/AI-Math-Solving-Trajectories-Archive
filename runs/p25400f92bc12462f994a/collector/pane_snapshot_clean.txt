## Problem

Determine the norm of the operator $A: L^{1}[1,2] \to L^{1}[0,1]$ defined by $(A
f)(x) = \int_{1}^{2} \frac{f(t)}{x+t}\,dt$.

## Proof

The operator norm is $\|A\| = \sup_{\|f\|_{1}\le 1} \|Af\|_{1}$.

**Upper bound.** For any $f \in L^1[1,2]$, using Fubini–Tonelli (the kernel $1/(
x+t)$ is positive and $|f|$ is integrable):

$$
\|Af\|_1 = \int_0^1 \left|\int_1^2 \frac{f(t)}{x+t}\,dt\right|dx \le \int_0^1\in
t_1^2 \frac{|f(t)|}{x+t}\,dt\,dx = \int_1^2 |f(t)|\left(\int_0^1 \frac{dx}{x+t}\
right)dt.
$$

The inner integral is

$$
\int_0^1 \frac{dx}{x+t} = \ln(x+t)\Big|_{0}^{1} = \ln\frac{t+1}{t}.
$$

The function $g(t) = \ln\frac{t+1}{t} = \ln(1+1/t)$ is strictly decreasing on $[
1,2]$ (since $1/t$ is decreasing and $\ln$ is increasing), so

$$
\sup_{t\in[1,2]} g(t) = g(1) = \ln 2.
$$

Therefore

$$
\|Af\|_1 \le \int_1^2 |f(t)|\,\ln 2\,dt = (\ln 2)\,\|f\|_1,
$$

giving $\|A\| \le \ln 2$.

**Lower bound (attaining the bound).** Define the approximate identity concentra
ting at $t=1$ (where $g$ attains its maximum):

$$
f_n(t) = n\,\mathbf{1}_{[1,\,1+1/n]}(t), \qquad n \ge 1.
$$

Then $\|f_n\|_1 = 1$, and

$$
(Af_n)(x) = \int_1^{1+1/n} \frac{n}{x+t}\,dt = n\ln\frac{x+1+1/n}{x+1} = n\ln\!\
left(1+\frac{1}{n(x+1)}\right).
$$

Hence

$$
\|Af_n\|_1 = \int_0^1 n\ln\!\left(1+\frac{1}{n(x+1)}\right)dx.
$$

Pointwise, as $n\to\infty$,

$$
n\ln\!\left(1+\frac{1}{n(x+1)}\right) \to \frac{1}{x+1}.
$$

To pass the limit inside the integral, we dominate the integrand. Using $\ln(1+u
)\le u$ for $u\ge 0$ with $u = \frac{1}{n(x+1)}$:

$$
n\ln\!\left(1+\frac{1}{n(x+1)}\right) \le n\cdot\frac{1}{n(x+1)} = \frac{1}{x+1}
 \le 1,
$$

which is integrable on $[0,1]$. By the dominated convergence theorem,

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|Af_n\|_1 = \int_0^1 \frac{dx}{x+1} = \ln(x+1)\Big|_0^1 = \ln
2.
$$

Since each $\|f_n\|_1 = 1$, we obtain $\|A\| \ge \ln 2$.

**Conclusion.** Combining both bounds,

$$
\boxed{\|A\| = \ln 2.}
$$
