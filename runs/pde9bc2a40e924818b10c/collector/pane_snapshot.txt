**Claim.** The largest cardinality of a Hausdorff separable space is $\boxed{2^{
\mathfrak{c}}}$, where $\mathfrak{c}=2^{\aleph_0}$.

---

### Upper bound: $|X|\le 2^{\mathfrak{c}}$

Let $X$ be Hausdorff and separable, and let $D\subseteq X$ be a countable dense
subset ($|D|=\aleph_0$). For each point $x\in X$ define its *neighborhood trace
on $D$*:
$$\mathcal{F}(x)=\{\,U\cap D : U\text{ is an open neighborhood of }x\,\}\subsete
q\mathcal{P}(D).$$

**$\mathcal{F}$ is injective.** Take $x\neq y$ in $X$. By the Hausdorff property
 there exist disjoint open sets $U\ni x$ and $V\ni y$. Then $U\cap D\in\mathcal{
F}(x)$. Suppose for contradiction that $U\cap D\in\mathcal{F}(y)$; then there is
 an open $W\ni y$ with $W\cap D=U\cap D$. Since $D$ is dense and $V$ is a nonemp
ty open set, $V\cap D\neq\varnothing$. But $V\subseteq W$ is false in general; r
ather, $V\cap D\subseteq W\cap D=U\cap D\subseteq U$, which forces $V\cap D\subs
eteq U\cap V=\varnothing$ — contradicting $V\cap D\neq\varnothing$. Hence $U\cap
 D\notin\mathcal{F}(y)$, so $\mathcal{F}(x)\neq\mathcal{F}(y)$.

Since $|\mathcal{P}(D)|=2^{\aleph_0}=\mathfrak{c}$, the number of distinct famil
ies $\mathcal{F}(x)$ is at most $2^{\mathfrak{c}}$. By injectivity,
$$|X|\le 2^{\mathfrak{c}}.$$

### Lower bound: the bound is attained

Consider the Stone–Čech compactification $\beta\mathbb{N}$ of the discrete space
 $\mathbb{N}$. It is a compact Hausdorff space, hence Hausdorff. The copy of $\m
athbb{N}$ embeds densely, so $\beta\mathbb{N}$ is separable (the countable set $
\mathbb{N}$ is dense).

Its cardinality is well known: points of $\beta\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{N}$ co
rrespond to free ultrafilters on $\mathbb{N}$, and the number of free ultrafilte
rs is $2^{\mathfrak{c}}$. Concretely, ultrafilters on $\mathbb{N}$ are elements
of $\{0,1\}^{\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})}$, giving at most $2^{\mathfrak{c}}$; and t
here are $2^{\mathfrak{c}}$ ultrafilters (e.g. by an independent-family construc
tion producing $2^{\mathfrak{c}}$ distinct ultrafilters). Thus
$$|\beta\mathbb{N}|=2^{\mathfrak{c}}.$$

So a Hausdorff separable space of cardinality exactly $2^{\mathfrak{c}}$ exists.

### Conclusion

The supremum is attained:
$$f(X)_{\max}=2^{\mathfrak{c}}.$$
