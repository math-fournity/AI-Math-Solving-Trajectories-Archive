## Proof

We determine the values of $s \in \mathbb{R}$ for which
$$S(s) = \sum_{k \in \mathbb{Z}^n} \langle k \rangle^{-s}, \qquad \langle k \ran
gle = (1+|k|^2)^{1/2},$$
converges. We claim **$S(s)$ converges if and only if $s > n$**.

---

### Upper bound (convergence for $s > n$)

Partition $\mathbb{R}^n$ into the unit cubes $Q_k = k + [-\tfrac12,\tfrac12)^n$
for $k \in \mathbb{Z}^n$. On $Q_k$, for $|x| \geq 1$ we have $|x| \leq |k| + \tf
rac{\sqrt n}{2}$, and crucially $\langle k \rangle \leq C\langle x \rangle$ for
a constant $C$ depending only on $n$ (since $|k| \leq |x| + \tfrac{\sqrt n}{2}$,
 so $1+|k|^2 \leq C'(1+|x|^2)$). Hence
$$\langle k \rangle^{-s} \leq C^{s}\int_{Q_k} \langle x \rangle^{-s}\,dx \qquad
(s>0,\ |k|\geq 1).$$
The finitely many terms with $|k|<1$ are harmless, so
$$S(s) \leq C_n + C^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \langle x \rangle^{-s}\,dx.$$
Using polar coordinates $x = r\omega$ with $r = |x|$, $\omega \in \mathbb{S}^{n-
1}$:
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \langle x \rangle^{-s}\,dx = |\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|\int_0^\inf
ty (1+r^2)^{-s/2} r^{n-1}\,dr.$$
As $r \to \infty$ the integrand is $\sim r^{n-1-s}$, and as $r \to 0$ it is $\si
m r^{n-1}$ (integrable). The integral at infinity converges iff $n-1-s < -1$, i.
e. **$s > n$**. Thus $S(s) < \infty$ for $s > n$.

### Lower bound (divergence for $s \leq n$)

For $|k| \geq 1$ we have $\langle k \rangle \leq \sqrt{2}\,|k|$, so $\langle k \
rangle^{-s} \geq 2^{-s/2}|k|^{-s}$ when $s \geq 0$. It suffices to show $\sum_{k
 \in \mathbb{Z}^n \setminus \{0\}} |k|^{-s} = +\infty$ for $s \leq n$.

Group lattice points by dyadic annuli $A_j = \{k : 2^j \leq |k| < 2^{j+1}\}$, $j
 \geq 0$. The volume of the annulus $\{2^j \leq |x| < 2^{j+1}\}$ is $\sim 2^{jn}
$, and each unit cube contains exactly one lattice point, so
$$|A_j| \geq c_n\, 2^{jn}$$
for some $c_n > 0$ (the annulus of inner radius $2^j - \tfrac{\sqrt n}{2}$ and o
uter radius $2^{j+1}+\tfrac{\sqrt n}{2}$ is contained in the union of cubes cent
ered at points of $A_j$, and its volume is $\sim 2^{jn}$). On $A_j$, $|k|^{-s} \
geq 2^{-(j+1)s}$. Therefore
$$\sum_{k \in A_j} |k|^{-s} \geq c_n\, 2^{jn}\cdot 2^{-(j+1)s} = c'_n\, 2^{j(n-s
)}.$$
- If $s < n$: $2^{j(n-s)} \to \infty$, so the summands of $\sum_j$ do not even t
end to $0$; the series diverges.
- If $s = n$: each dyadic block contributes $\geq c'_n > 0$, so $\sum_j = +\inft
y$.

Hence $S(s) = +\infty$ for $0 \leq s \leq n$.

For $s < 0$, $\langle k \rangle^{-s} = \langle k \rangle^{|s|} \geq 1$ for every
 $k$, so the terms do not tend to zero and the series diverges trivially.

### Conclusion

Combining both directions:
$$\boxed{\sum_{k \in \mathbb{Z}^n} \langle k \rangle^{-s} \text{ converges } \if
f s > n.}$$
