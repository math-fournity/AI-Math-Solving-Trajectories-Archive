## Proof

it sum $s$. For $1 \le s \le 9$, $m(s) = s$.

Suppose $N$ is expressible as a sum of 2002 positive integers each with digit su
m $s_1$, and as a sum of 2003 positive integers each with digit sum $s_2$.

**Modular constraint.** Since a number is congruent to its digit sum mod 9:
$$N \equiv 2002\,s_1 \equiv 4\,s_1 \pmod{9}, \qquad N \equiv 2003\,s_2 \equiv 5\
,s_2 \pmod{9}.$$
Therefore we need $4\,s_1 \equiv 5\,s_2 \pmod{9}$.

**Lower bound.** Each packet with digit sum $s$ is at least $m(s)$, so:
$$N \ge 2002\,m(s_1), \qquad N \ge 2003\,m(s_2).$$

For $N < 10010$, we need $2002\,m(s_1) < 10010$ and $2003\,m(s_2) < 10010$, i.e.
, $m(s_1) \le 4$ and $m(s_2) \le 4$, which forces $s_1 \le 4$ and $s_2 \le 4$.

**Exhaustive check of small pairs.** For all $s_1, s_2 \in \{1,2,3,4\}$, we veri
fy $4s_1 \not\equiv 5s_2 \pmod{9}$:

| $s_1$ | $4s_1 \bmod 9$ | $5s_2 \bmod 9$ for $s_2=1,2,3,4$ |
|-------|----------------|-----------------------------------|
| 1 | 4 | 5, 1, 6, 2 |
| 2 | 8 | 5, 1, 6, 2 |
| 3 | 3 | 5, 1, 6, 2 |
| 4 | 7 | 5, 1, 6, 2 |

The values $\{4, 8, 3, 7\}$ and $\{5, 1, 6, 2\}$ are disjoint, so **no pair with

**Construction achieving $N = 10010$.** Take $s_1 = 5$, $s_2 = 4$. Check: $4 \cd

- Packet count $= 222 + 1781 = 2003$. ✓
