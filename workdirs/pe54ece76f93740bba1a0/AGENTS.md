# Problem

A specialized architectural firm is designing a triangular solar panel, denoted as Panel $T$. This panel is shaped like an isosceles triangle. To maximize structural integrity, the head engineer determines that a single straight support beam must be installed. This beam must originate from one of the three corners (vertices) of the panel and terminate at the opposite edge, effectively partitioning the large panel into two smaller triangular sub-sections, $R$ and $S$.

The engineering specifications require that both resulting sub-sections, $R$ and $S$, must themselves be isosceles triangles. However, to ensure a unique distribution of stress, a strict "Non-Similarity Constraint" is applied: neither sub-section $R$ nor sub-section $S$ is permitted to be similar in shape to the original panel $T$.

Calculate all possible degree measures for the apex angle (the angle between the two equal sides) of the original panel $T$ that would allow such a support beam to be installed.
