## Proof

**Theorem.** The simultaneous conjugacy problem is undecidable for finitely pres
ented groups.

**Proof.** We reduce the (uniform) word problem to the simultaneous conjugacy pr
oblem.

**Background.** The word problem for finitely presented groups is undecidable: b
y the Novikov (1955) and Boone (1958) constructions, there exists a finitely pre
sented group $G = \langle S \mid R \rangle$ for which no algorithm can decide, g
iven a word $w$ in $S$, whether $[w] = 1$ in $G$.

**Reduction.** Given an instance of the word problem — a finitely presented grou
p $G = \langle S \mid R \rangle$ and a word $w$ in the alphabet $S \cup S^{-1}$
— we construct an instance of the simultaneous conjugacy problem over the **same
** group $G$ with $n = 1$:
$$v_1 = w, \qquad w_1 = \varepsilon \quad (\text{the empty word, representing th
e identity}).$$

We claim: there exists $g \in G$ with $g^{-1}[v_1]\,g = [w_1]$ if and only if $[
w] = 1$ in $G$.

**($\Rightarrow$)** Suppose there exists $g \in G$ such that $g^{-1}[w]\,g = [\v
arepsilon] = 1$. Then
$$[w] = g \cdot 1 \cdot g^{-1} = 1,$$
so $w$ represents the identity in $G$.

**($\Leftarrow$)** Suppose $[w] = 1$ in $G$. Take $g = 1$ (the identity element)
. Then
$$g^{-1}[v_1]\,g = 1^{-1}[w]\,1 = [w] = 1 = [\varepsilon] = [w_1]. \checkmark$$

**Correctness of the reduction.** The construction is computable — producing the
 pair $(v_1, w_1) = (w, \varepsilon)$ from the word-problem input $(G, w)$ requi
res no computation beyond copying. The equivalence established above shows that
a decision algorithm for the simultaneous conjugacy problem would yield a decisi
on algorithm for the word problem: on input $(G, w)$, query the simultaneous con
jugacy solver on $(G, (w), (\varepsilon))$ and return its answer.

Since the word problem is undecidable for finitely presented groups, the simulta
neous conjugacy problem must also be undecidable. $\square$

**Remark 1 (Semi-decidability).** The simultaneous conjugacy problem is recursiv
ely enumerable: enumerate all words $g$ in $S \cup S^{-1}$, and for each, check
whether $g^{-1}v_i g\, w_i^{-1} = 1$ for all $i$ — each such check is an instanc
e of the word problem, which is itself r.e. for finitely presented groups (one e
numerates all consequences of the relators $R$). If a conjugating element $g$ ex
ists, this procedure eventually finds it. Thus the problem is r.e. but not decid
able.

**Remark 2 (Strength for $n \geq 2$).** The reduction above uses $n = 1$, so und
ecidability already holds for the ordinary conjugacy problem. For $n \geq 2$, th
e simultaneous conjugacy problem is at least as hard (pad with trivial equations
 $g^{-1}\varepsilon\, g = \varepsilon$). Moreover, the simultaneous conjugacy pr
oblem for $n \geq 2$ is known to be undecidable even for certain groups with **d
ecidable** word problem and **decidable** (ordinary) conjugacy problem — it can
encode the subgroup membership problem or systems of equations over groups — but
 the basic undecidability already follows from the simple reduction above.

**Remark 3 (Decidable cases).** For specific classes of groups the problem is de
cidable: free groups (via automorphism/Whitehead algorithms), hyperbolic groups
(conjugacy problem is decidable, and simultaneous conjugacy can be resolved usin
g the structure of centralizers), and nilpotent groups with decidable word probl
em. The undecidability is a phenomenon of **general** finitely presented groups.
