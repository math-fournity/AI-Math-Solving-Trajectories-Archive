# Wiener Filter Derivation in the Frequency Domain

## Problem Setup

Let the degraded image be modeled in the spatial domain as

$$g(x,y) = h(x,y) * f(x,y) + n(x,y)$$

where $f$ is the original (unknown) image, $h$ is the degradation (blur) point-s
pread function, $n$ is additive noise, and $*$ denotes convolution. Taking the 2
-D Fourier transform gives the frequency-domain observation model

$$G(u,v) = H(u,v)\,F(u,v) + N(u,v),$$

where $G, H, F, N$ are the transforms of $g, h, f, n$ respectively.

We seek a **linear restoration filter** $W(u,v)$ producing an estimate

$$\hat{F}(u,v) = W(u,v)\,G(u,v)$$

that minimizes the mean square error

$$\mathcal{E}(u,v) = E\!\left[\,\bigl|F(u,v) - \hat{F}(u,v)\bigr|^{2}\,\right] =
 E\!\left[\,\bigl|F(u,v) - W(u,v)\,G(u,v)\bigr|^{2}\,\right].$$

## Standard Assumptions

1. The original image $f$ and the noise $n$ are **uncorrelated**, zero-mean rand
om processes:
   $$E[F(u,v)\,N^{*}(u,v)] = E[N(u,v)\,F^{*}(u,v)] = 0.$$
2. Define the **power spectrum of the original image** and the **noise power spe
ctrum**:
   $$S_{f}(u,v) = E\!\left[\,|F(u,v)|^{2}\,\right], \qquad S_{n}(u,v) = E\!\left
[\,|N(u,v)|^{2}\,\right].$$

## Applying the Orthogonality Principle

The error signal at frequency $(u,v)$ is

$$E_{e}(u,v) = F(u,v) - W(u,v)\,G(u,v).$$

By the **orthogonality principle** (the MSE is minimized when the error is ortho
gonal to the observation), the optimal $W$ must satisfy

$$E\!\left[\,E_{e}(u,v)\,G^{*}(u,v)\,\right] = 0.$$

Substituting the error:

$$E\!\left[\,F(u,v)\,G^{*}(u,v)\,\right] - W(u,v)\,E\!\left[\,G(u,v)\,G^{*}(u,v)
\,\right] = 0.$$

Solving for $W$:

$$\boxed{\;W(u,v) = \frac{E\!\left[F(u,v)\,G^{*}(u,v)\right]}{E\!\left[\,|G(u,v)
|^{2}\,\right]}\;}$$

## Computing the Two Correlations

**Numerator.** Using $G = HF + N$ and $G^{*} = H^{*}F^{*} + N^{*}$:

$$E[F\,G^{*}] = E\!\left[F\,(H^{*}F^{*} + N^{*})\right] = H^{*}\,E[|F|^{2}] + E[
F\,N^{*}] = H^{*}(u,v)\,S_{f}(u,v).$$

**Denominator.**

$$E[|G|^{2}] = E\!\left[(HF+N)(H^{*}F^{*}+N^{*})\right]$$
$$= |H|^{2}\,E[|F|^{2}] + H\,E[F\,N^{*}] + H^{*}\,E[N\,F^{*}] + E[|N|^{2}]$$
$$= |H(u,v)|^{2}\,S_{f}(u,v) + S_{n}(u,v),$$

where the cross-terms vanish by the uncorrelatedness assumption.

## The Wiener Filter

Substituting both results:

$$\boxed{\;W(u,v) = \frac{H^{*}(u,v)\,S_{f}(u,v)}{|H(u,v)|^{2}\,S_{f}(u,v) + S_{
n}(u,v)}\;}$$

Equivalently, dividing numerator and denominator by $S_{f}(u,v)$:

$$\boxed{\;W(u,v) = \frac{H^{*}(u,v)}{|H(u,v)|^{2} + \dfrac{S_{n}(u,v)}{S_{f}(u,
v)}}\;}$$

The restored image estimate is therefore

$$\hat{F}(u,v) = \frac{H^{*}(u,v)}{|H(u,v)|^{2} + S_{n}(u,v)/S_{f}(u,v)}\,G(u,v)
.$$

## Interpretation and Limiting Behavior

- **Inverse-filter limit.** When $S_{n}(u,v) \to 0$ (noise-free), the term $S_{n
}/S_{f} \to 0$ and
  $$W(u,v) \to \frac{H^{*}(u,v)}{|H(u,v)|^{2}} = \frac{1}{H(u,v)},$$
  recovering the pure inverse filter, which amplifies noise at frequencies where
 $|H|$ is small.

- **Noise-dominated limit.** When $S_{n}(u,v) \gg |H(u,v)|^{2}S_{f}(u,v)$, the f
ilter attenuates strongly:
  $$W(u,v) \to \frac{H^{*}(u,v)\,S_{f}(u,v)}{S_{n}(u,v)} \to 0,$$
  suppressing output at frequencies where the signal-to-noise ratio is poor.

- **The ratio $S_{n}(u,v)/S_{f}(u,v)$ acts as a frequency-dependent regularizati
on term** that balances deconvolution against noise amplification — the larger t
he noise power relative to the signal power at a given frequency, the more the f
ilter suppresses that frequency rather than blindly inverting $H$.

## Verification of Optimality

To confirm this is a true minimum (not merely a stationary point), note that the
 MSE as a function of $W$ is

$$\mathcal{E}(W) = E[|F|^{2}] - W^{*}\,E[F\,G^{*}] - W\,E[G\,F^{*}] + |W|^{2}\,E
[|G|^{2}],$$

which is a convex quadratic in the complex variable $W$ (via Wirtinger calculus
treating $W$ and $W^{*}$ as independent). The Hessian with respect to $W^{*}$ is
 $E[|G|^{2}] = |H|^{2}S_{f} + S_{n} > 0$, so the stationary point obtained from
the orthogonality condition is the **unique global minimum**.

## Summary

Given the degradation model $G = HF + N$ with signal–noise uncorrelatedness, the
 linear minimum-mean-square-error (Wiener) estimator in the frequency domain is

$$\hat{F}(u,v) = \frac{H^{*}(u,v)\,S_{f}(u,v)}{|H(u,v)|^{2}\,S_{f}(u,v) + S_{n}(
u,v)}\,G(u,v),$$

where $S_{f}(u,v)$ is the power spectrum of the original image and $S_{n}(u,v)$
is the noise power spectrum. This filter optimally trades off deconvolution (inv
erting $H$) against noise suppression (controlled by the ratio $S_{n}/S_{f}$), a
nd it reduces to the inverse filter in the noise-free limit.
