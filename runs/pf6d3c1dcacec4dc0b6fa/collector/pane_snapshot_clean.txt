## Solution

We seek all $z$ with $|z|<1$ satisfying $e^z = 1+2z$, i.e., the zeros of
$$f(z) = e^z - 1 - 2z = (e^z-1) - 2z$$
inside the unit disk.

**Step 1: Identify an obvious zero.** At $z=0$: $e^0 = 1 = 1+2\cdot 0$. So $z=0$
 is a zero. It is simple since $f'(z)=e^z-2$ and $f'(0)=1-2=-1\neq 0$.

**Step 2: Show it is the unique zero in $|z|<1$ via Rouché's theorem.** On the c
ircle $|z|=1$ we compare the two summands $g(z)=e^z-1$ and $h(z)=-2z$ of $f=g+h$
.

For $|z|=1$:
$$|g(z)| = |e^z-1| = \left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^n}{n!}\right| \leq \sum_{n
=1}^{\infty}\frac{|z|^n}{n!} = e^{|z|}-1 = e-1 \approx 1.718.$$
Meanwhile
$$|h(z)| = |{-2z}| = 2.$$
Since $e-1 < 2$, we have the strict inequality $|g(z)| < |h(z)|$ on $|z|=1$.

By Rouché's theorem, $f(z)=g(z)+h(z)$ has the same number of zeros (counting mul
tiplicity) inside $|z|<1$ as $h(z)=-2z$. The function $-2z$ has exactly one zero
 (a simple zero at $z=0$) inside the unit disk.

**Step 3: Conclude.** Since $f$ has exactly one zero in $|z|<1$ and $z=0$ is tha
t zero, the unique solution is:

$$\boxed{z = 0}$$
