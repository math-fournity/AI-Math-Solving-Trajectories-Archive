Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimal degree polynomial $P(x)$ wit
h coefficients in $\{0,1,\ldots,6\}$ such that some circular permutation of $\{1
,2,3,4,5,6\}$ makes every 3-consecutive sum divisible by 7.

## Setup

Let the circular arrangement be $a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_6$ (indices mod 6). Set $b_
i = P(a_i) \pmod{7}$. The condition is:
$$b_i + b_{i+1} + b_{i+2} \equiv 0 \pmod{7} \quad \text{for all } i.$$

This gives the recurrence $b_{i+2} = -b_i - b_{i+1}$, whose characteristic polyn
omial $x^2 + x + 1 = 0$ has roots that are primitive cube roots of unity. Hence
$b_{i+3} = b_i$, so the sequence has period 3: $(b_1, b_2, b_3, b_1, b_2, b_3)$
with $b_1 + b_2 + b_3 \equiv 0 \pmod{7}$.

**Consequence:** $P$ takes exactly 3 values on $\{1,\ldots,6\}$, each appearing
twice (when the values are distinct), with opposite elements $a_i$ and $a_{i+3}$
 sharing the same value.

## Ruling out degrees 0 and 1

**Degree 0 (nonzero constant $c$):** All values equal $c$, so $3c \equiv 0 \pmod
 7 \Rightarrow c = 0$. No nonzero constant works.

**Degree 1 ($P(x)=ax+b$, $a\neq 0$):** $P$ is a bijection on $\{1,\ldots,6\}$, g
iving 6 distinct values. But the period-3 condition forces $b_4=b_1$, a contradi
ction. Impossible.

## Degree 2 works

Consider $P(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$. Two elements $x \neq y$ share the same value if
f $a(x+y) + b = 0$, i.e., $x + y = -b/a =: s$.

For all 6 elements to pair up into 3 pairs with a common sum $s$, the involution
 $x \mapsto s - x$ must preserve $\{1,\ldots,6\}$. This requires $0 \notin \{s -
 x : x \in \{1,\ldots,6\}\}$, i.e., $s \neq x$ for any $x \in \{1,\ldots,6\}$, m
eaning $s = 0$. (If $s \neq 0$, then $x = s$ maps to $0 \notin$ the set, breakin
g the pairing.) So $b = 0$.

With $b = 0$: $P(x) = ax^2 + c$, and $P(x) = P(-x)$ pairs $(1,6), (2,5), (3,4)$.
 The three values are:
$$P(1)=P(6)=a+c, \quad P(2)=P(5)=4a+c, \quad P(3)=P(4)=2a+c.$$
Their sum: $(a+c)+(4a+c)+(2a+c) = 7a + 3c \equiv 3c \pmod 7$. For this to be $0$
: $c = 0$.

So $P(x) = ax^2$ with $a \neq 0$.

## Verification with $a = 1$

Take $P(x) = x^2$ and the circle $1, 2, 3, 6, 5, 4$:

| $x$ | 1 | 2 | 3 | 6 | 5 | 4 |
|-----|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $P(x) \bmod 7$ | 1 | 4 | 2 | 1 | 4 | 2 |

Every 3-consecutive sum: $1+4+2 = 7 \equiv 0 \pmod{7}$ (and all cyclic rotations
 give the same). ✓

## Conclusion

The minimal degree is $d = 2$. The polynomial $P(x) = x^2$ (with leading coeffic
ient $a = 1$) achieves this, and no polynomial of degree $< 2$ (other than the t
rivial zero polynomial) satisfies the condition.

$$\boxed{d = 2, \quad a = 1}$$
