## Setup

Let $z$ denote the depth of the probe's center below the water surface (positive
 downward). The probe has density $\rho_p = 100\;\text{kg/m}^3$, water density $

## Phase 1: Air trajectory (exit and reentry)

The probe is launched upward from $z=0$ with $v_0 = 23\;\text{m/s}$. Neglecting
air resistance, by energy conservation the probe returns to the water surface ($
z=0$) with velocity $v_0 = 23\;\text{m/s}$ downward.

**Key observation about partial submersion during exit/reentry through the surfa
ce ($0 < y < r$):** When the probe exits the water (moving up) and later reenter
s (moving down), it traverses the same partially-submerged region in opposite di
rections. Buoyancy always points upward, so the work done during exit (positive,
 force up × displacement up) exactly cancels the work done during reentry (negat
ive, force up × displacement down). These contributions vanish in the energy bud
get.

## Phase 2: Descent into water

Once the center passes below the surface ($z > 0$), buoyancy opposes gravity. Le
t $V_{\text{sub}}(z)$ be the submerged volume at depth $z$.

**For $0 \le z \le r$ (partial submersion):** The waterline cuts the sphere at h
eight $z$ above the center. The submerged cap has height $r + z$:
$$V_{\text{sub}}(z) = \frac{\pi (r+z)^2(2r - z)}{3}, \quad \phi(z) = \frac{V_{\t
ext{sub}}(z)}{V} = \frac{(r+z)^2(2r-z)}{4r^3}$$

**For $z \ge r$ (full submersion):** $\phi(z) = 1$.

## Energy conservation (from surface entry to maximum depth $d$)

At maximum depth, velocity is zero:
$$\frac{1}{2}\rho_p V v_0^2 + \rho_p V g\, d - \rho_w g \int_0^d V_{\text{sub}}(
z)\,dz = 0$$

Computing the integral (splitting at $z = r$, valid since $d \approx 3 \gg r$ fo
r small $r$):

$$\int_0^d V_{\text{sub}}(z)\,dz = V\left[\int_0^r \phi(z)\,dz + (d - r)\right]$
$

Substituting $u = z/r$:
$$\int_0^r \phi(z)\,dz = \frac{r}{4}\int_0^1 (1+u)^2(2-u)\,du = \frac{r}{4}\int_
0^1 (2 + 3u - u^3)\,du = \frac{r}{4}\cdot\frac{13}{4} = \frac{13r}{16}$$

So:
$$\int_0^d V_{\text{sub}}(z)\,dz = V\left[d - r + \frac{13r}{16}\right] = V\left
[d - \frac{3r}{16}\right]$$

Substituting back and dividing by $V$:

$$\frac{1}{2}\rho_p v_0^2 + \rho_p g\,d = \rho_w g\left(d - \frac{3r}{16}\right)
$$

$$(\rho_w - \rho_p)\,g\,d = \frac{1}{2}\rho_p v_0^2 + \frac{3\rho_w g\,r}{16}$$

$$d = \frac{\frac{1}{2}\rho_p v_0^2 + \frac{3\rho_w g\,r}{16}}{(\rho_w - \rho_p)
\,g}$$
