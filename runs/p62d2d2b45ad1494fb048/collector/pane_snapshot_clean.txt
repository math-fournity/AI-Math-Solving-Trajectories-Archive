**Claim:** $\mathbb{E}[T] = \dfrac{b}{a}$.

**Proof.**

Let $X_t := B_t - at$. Then $(X_t)$ is a Brownian motion with drift $-a$ startin
g at $X_0 = 0$, and
$$T = \inf\{t \ge 0 : X_t = -b\}.$$

**Step 1: $T < \infty$ almost surely.**

By the strong law of large numbers for Brownian motion, $B_t/t \to 0$ a.s. as $t
 \to \infty$. Hence
$$\frac{X_t}{t} = \frac{B_t}{t} - a \longrightarrow -a < 0 \quad \text{a.s.}$$
so $X_t \to -\infty$ a.s. Since $X_0 = 0 > -b$ and $X_t$ has continuous paths te
nding to $-\infty$, by the intermediate value theorem there exists some (finite)
 $t$ with $X_t = -b$. Thus $T < \infty$ a.s.

**Step 2: $\mathbb{E}[T] \le b/a < \infty$ (an upper bound via Fatou).**

For every $n$, the bounded stopping time $T \wedge n$ satisfies the optional sto
pping theorem applied to the martingale $B_t$:
$$\mathbb{E}[B_{T \wedge n}] = \mathbb{E}[B_0] = 0. \tag{1}$$

We establish a lower bound on $B_{T \wedge n}$. For $s < T$, the process has not
 yet hit the line, so $B_s > as - b \ge -b$ (using $a > 0, s \ge 0$). At $s = T$
, $B_T = aT - b > -b$ (since $T > 0$ because $B_0 = 0 \ne -b$). Therefore
$$B_{T \wedge n} \ge -b \quad \text{for all } n. \tag{2}$$

Since $B_{T \wedge n} \to B_T$ a.s. (as $T < \infty$ a.s.) and the family is bou
nded below by $-b$, Fatou's lemma and (1) give
$$\mathbb{E}[B_T] \le \liminf_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[B_{T \wedge n}] = 0.$$

Using $B_T = aT - b$:
$$\mathbb{E}[aT - b] \le 0 \implies a\,\mathbb{E}[T] \le b \implies \mathbb{E}[T
] \le \frac{b}{a} < \infty. \tag{3}$$

**Step 3: Apply Wald's identity to conclude $\mathbb{E}[T] = b/a$.**

Since $B_t$ is a continuous (local) martingale with $B_0 = 0$ and quadratic vari
ation $\langle B \rangle_t = t$, and we have shown $\mathbb{E}[T] = \mathbb{E}[\
langle B \rangle_T] < \infty$, **Wald's identity for continuous martingales** (s
ee e.g. Karatzas & Shreve, or Revuz & Yor) applies and yields
$$\mathbb{E}[B_T] = \mathbb{E}[B_0] = 0. \tag{4}$$

Substituting $B_T = aT - b$ into (4):
$$\mathbb{E}[aT - b] = 0 \implies a\,\mathbb{E}[T] - b = 0 \implies \mathbb{E}[T
] = \frac{b}{a}.$$

**Consistency checks.** As $a \to 0^+$, the hitting line becomes nearly horizont
al at $-b$, recovering the classical fact $\mathbb{E}[\inf\{t : B_t = -b\}] = \i
nfty$, and indeed $b/a \to \infty$. As $b \to 0^+$, $T \to 0$ and $b/a \to 0$. B
oth limits are consistent.

$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}[T] = \frac{b}{a}}$$
