## Solution

**Problem.** If the integer $k$ lies between 100 and 137, what is the probabilit
y that $k^3 - k$ is divisible by 72?

### Step 1: Factor and decompose

$$k^3 - k = k(k-1)(k+1),$$

the product of three consecutive integers. Since $72 = 8 \times 9$ and $\gcd(8,9
)=1$, we need both $8 \mid k(k-1)(k+1)$ and $9 \mid k(k-1)(k+1)$.

### Step 2: Divisibility by 8

Among $k-1, k, k+1$:
- **If $k$ is odd**, then $k-1$ and $k+1$ are consecutive even numbers; one of t
hem is divisible by 4, so their product (hence $k(k-1)(k+1)$) is divisible by 8.
 ✓
- **If $k$ is even**, then $k-1$ and $k+1$ are both odd, so the only source of f
actors of 2 is $k$ itself. We need $8 \mid k$, i.e., $k \equiv 0 \pmod{8}$.

So $8 \mid k(k-1)(k+1)$ iff $k \bmod 8 \in \{0, 1, 3, 5, 7\}$ (5 residues mod 8)
.

### Step 3: Divisibility by 9

Among three consecutive integers, exactly one is divisible by 3. For $9 \mid k(k
-1)(k+1)$, that one must be divisible by 9:
- $k \equiv 0 \pmod{3}$: need $9 \mid k$, i.e., $k \equiv 0 \pmod{9}$.
- $k \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$: need $9 \mid (k-1)$, i.e., $k \equiv 1 \pmod{9}$.
- $k \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$: need $9 \mid (k+1)$, i.e., $k \equiv 8 \pmod{9}$.

So $9 \mid k(k-1)(k+1)$ iff $k \bmod 9 \in \{0, 1, 8\}$ (3 residues mod 9).

### Step 4: Combine via CRT

By the Chinese Remainder Theorem (since $\gcd(8,9)=1$), the number of valid resi
dues mod 72 is $5 \times 3 = 15$. Enumerating, the valid residues mod 72 are:

$$\{0,\, 1,\, 8,\, 9,\, 17,\, 19,\, 27,\, 35,\, 37,\, 45,\, 53,\, 55,\, 63,\, 64
,\, 71\}.$$

### Step 5: Count in the given range

Interpreting "between 100 and 137" as $100 < k < 137$, we have $k \in \{101, 102
, \ldots, 136\}$, giving $136 - 101 + 1 = 36$ integers.

Since $100 = 72 + 28$ and $136 = 72 + 64$, every $k$ in this range satisfies $k
\equiv r \pmod{72}$ with $r \in \{29, 30, \ldots, 64\}$.

The valid residues (from Step 4) falling in $\{29, \ldots, 64\}$ are:

$$35,\; 37,\; 45,\; 53,\; 55,\; 63,\; 64 \quad \text{(7 residues)}.$$

These correspond to $k \in \{107,\, 109,\, 117,\, 125,\, 127,\, 135,\, 136\}$.

**Verification of a few:**
- $k = 107$: $107 \times 106 \times 108$; $106 \times 108 = 8 \times 53 \times 2
7$ (div by 8), $108 = 12 \times 9$ (div by 9). ✓
- $k = 136$: $136 \times 135 \times 137$; $136 = 8 \times 17$ (div by 8), $135 =
 15 \times 9$ (div by 9). ✓
- $k = 137$ (boundary, excluded): $137 \times 136 \times 138$; $8 \mid 136$ ✓ bu
t $137, 136, 138 \bmod 9 = 2, 1, 3$ — none divisible by 9. ✗ (correctly excluded
)

### Step 6: Probability

$$\text{Probability} = \frac{7}{36}.$$
