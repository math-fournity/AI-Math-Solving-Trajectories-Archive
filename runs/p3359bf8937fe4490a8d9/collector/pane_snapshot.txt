## Proof

We are given
$$f(z) = \sum_{n=m}^{\infty} a_n (z-w)^n, \qquad 0<|z-w|<r,\quad a_m\neq 0.$$

**Step 1: Factor out the leading term.** Set $t = z-w$. Then
$$f(z) = t^m \sum_{n=m}^{\infty} a_n t^{\,n-m} = t^m\, g(t),$$
where
$$g(t) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} a_{m+k}\, t^k = a_m + a_{m+1}t + a_{m+2}t^2 + \cdot
s.$$

**Step 2: $g$ is analytic and nonzero at $t=0$.** The series for $g$ converges f
or $|t|<r$, so $g$ is analytic at $0$, and
$$g(0) = a_m \neq 0.$$
By continuity, $g(t)\neq 0$ in some neighborhood of $0$. Hence $g'/g$ is analyti
c at $t=0$.

**Step 3: Compute $f'/f$.** Since $f = t^m g(t)$,
$$\frac{f'(z)}{f(z)} = \frac{d}{dz}\log f(z) = \frac{d}{dt}\bigl[m\log t + \log
g(t)\bigr] = \frac{m}{t} + \frac{g'(t)}{g(t)}.$$

**Step 4: Extract the residue.** The term $g'(t)/g(t)$ is analytic at $t=0$ (sin
ce $g(0)\neq 0$), so its Laurent expansion about $0$ contains no negative powers
 of $t$. The only contribution to the coefficient of $t^{-1}=(z-w)^{-1}$ comes f
rom $m/t$.

Therefore
$$\operatorname{Res}_{z=w}\frac{f'(z)}{f(z)} = m.$$
