## Proof

We analyze the functional
$$F(P) = \sum_{j \in J} \inf_{\varphi_j \in H^1(\Omega_j)} \int_{\Omega_j} \vare
psilon |\nabla \varphi_j|^2 + |\varphi_j - I|^2 \, dx$$
over the family $\mathcal{P}(D)$ of open partitions of $D$.

### Step 1: Monotonicity under refinement

Let $P = \{\Omega_j\}_{j \in J}$ and let $P'$ be a refinement of $P$, meaning ea
ch $\Omega_j$ is decomposed as $\Omega_j = \bigcup_{k \in K_j} \Omega_{j,k}'$ wi
th the $\Omega_{j,k}'$ open and pairwise disjoint. For any single $\Omega_j$ spl
it into two open pieces $\Omega_j^1, \Omega_j^2$:

**Claim:**
$$\inf_{\varphi \in H^1(\Omega_j)} \int_{\Omega_j} \varepsilon|\nabla\varphi|^2
+ |\varphi - I|^2\,dx \;\geq\; \inf_{\varphi_1 \in H^1(\Omega_j^1)} \int_{\Omega
_j^1}\!\cdots + \inf_{\varphi_2 \in H^2(\Omega_j^2)} \int_{\Omega_j^2}\!\cdots$$

*Proof of claim:* Any $\varphi \in H^1(\Omega_j)$ restricts to $\varphi|_{\Omega
_j^i} \in H^1(\Omega_j^i)$ for $i=1,2$, so the set over which the left infimum i
s taken is a subset of the product of the sets on the right (via restriction). H
ence the infimum over the joint space is at least the sum of the separate infima
. $\square$

By induction, $F(P') \leq F(P)$ for any refinement $P'$ of $P$.

### Step 2: The infimum is zero and is not attained

**Constructing a minimizing sequence.** Take any sequence of partitions $P_n = \
{\Omega_j^n\}$ where the maximal diameter $\operatorname{diam}(\Omega_j^n) \to 0
$ as $n \to \infty$ (e.g., a dyadic cube partition at level $n$). On each $\Omeg
a_j^n$, choose $\varphi_j^n$ to be the constant $c_j^n = \frac{1}{|\Omega_j^n|}\
int_{\Omega_j^n} I\,dx$ (the mean of $I$ on that region). Then $\nabla \varphi_j
^n = 0$, so:
$$F(P_n) \leq \sum_j \int_{\Omega_j^n} |c_j^n - I|^2\,dx = \int_D |I_n(x) - I(x)
|^2\,dx$$
where $I_n$ is the piecewise-constant approximation of $I$ (conditional expectat
ion onto the $\sigma$-algebra generated by the partition). By the martingale/Leb
esgue differentiation convergence theorem, $I_n \to I$ in $L^2(D)$ as the mesh s
ize $\to 0$. Therefore:
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} F(P_n) = 0.$$

Since $F(P) \geq 0$ for all $P$, we conclude $\inf_{P \in \mathcal{P}(D)} F(P) =
 0$.

**The infimum is not attained for general $I$.** Suppose $F(P^*) = 0$ for some o
pen partition $P^* = \{\Omega_j\}$. Then each summand must be zero, so for each
$j$:
$$\inf_{\varphi_j \in H^1(\Omega_j)} \int_{\Omega_j} \varepsilon|\nabla\varphi_j
|^2 + |\varphi_j - I|^2\,dx = 0.$$
Since the integrand is non-negative, this forces $\nabla \varphi_j = 0$ a.e. (so
 $\varphi_j$ is constant on each connected component of $\Omega_j$) and $\varphi
_j = I$ a.e. on $\Omega_j$. Hence $I$ must be **piecewise constant** on the part
ition $P^*$. For a general $I \in L^2(D)$ (e.g., $I$ continuous and non-constant
), no finite or countable open partition makes $I$ piecewise constant. The only
"partition" achieving $F=0$ would be the trivial partition into individual level
 sets $\{x : I(x) = c\}$, which are generally not open and the family is uncount
able—**not an element of $\mathcal{P}(D)$**.

Therefore, for generic $I$, **no minimizer exists** in $\mathcal{P}(D)$.

### Step 3: Why compactness fails (direct method breakdown)

The direct method of the calculus of variations requires:
1. **Lower semicontinuity** of $F$ in some topology, and
2. **Compactness**: every minimizing sequence has a convergent subsequence.

The monotonicity under refinement (Step 1) shows that minimizing sequences are d
riven toward **increasingly fine partitions** with $|J_n| \to \infty$ and interf
ace measure $\sum_j \mathcal{H}^{N-1}(\partial \Omega_j^n) \to \infty$. Such seq
uences have **no convergent subsequence** in any reasonable topology on $\mathca
l{P}(D)$: the number of regions and the total perimeter blow up, so the partitio
ns "escape to infinity" in the space of partitions. There is no topology in whic
h $\mathcal{P}(D)$ is compact that also makes $F$ lower semicontinuous, precisel
y because the refinement direction is unbounded.

### Step 4: The role of the additional penalization

An additional penalization term restores compactness by **blocking the degenerat
e refinement**. The standard choices are:

**(a) Penalizing the number of regions:** Add $\lambda |J|$ (with $\lambda > 0$)
:
$$F_\lambda(P) = \lambda |J| + \sum_{j \in J} \inf_{\varphi_j \in H^1(\Omega_j)}
 \int_{\Omega_j} \varepsilon|\nabla\varphi_j|^2 + |\varphi_j - I|^2\,dx.$$
Refining a partition increases $|J|$, so the penalty $\lambda|J|$ counterbalance
s the decrease in the data term. For a minimizing sequence, $|J_n|$ is bounded (
otherwise $F_\lambda \geq \lambda |J_n| \to \infty$), which provides compactness
: with bounded $|J|$, the partitions live in a finite-dimensional family (up to
relabeling), and compactness follows from the compactness of sets of finite peri
meter with bounded perimeter (under the $L^1$ topology on characteristic functio
ns).

**(b) Penalizing the total perimeter (Mumford–Shah type):** Add $\lambda \sum_j
\operatorname{Per}(\Omega_j)$:
$$F_\lambda(P) = \lambda \sum_j \mathcal{H}^{N-1}(\partial^* \Omega_j) + \sum_{j
 \in J} \inf_{\varphi_j \in H^1(\Omega_j)} \int_{\Omega_j} \varepsilon|\nabla\va
rphi_j|^2 + |\varphi_j - I|^2\,dx.$$
Refining increases total perimeter, so the penalty prevents infinite refinement.
 The compactness of sets with bounded perimeter (Ambrosio's compactness theorem
for $SBV$ / finite-perimeter sets) ensures that minimizing sequences have conver
gent subsequences, and lower semicontinuity of the perimeter (together with lowe
r semicontinuity of the Dirichlet-type energy) guarantees the limit is a minimiz
er.

### Conclusion

Without an additional penalization term, the functional $F(P)$ has infimum $0$ (
achieved in the limit by infinitely fine partitions), but this infimum is **not
attained** for generic $I \in L^2(D)$ because:

1. **Monotonicity under refinement** drives minimizing sequences toward partitio
ns with $|J| \to \infty$ and unbounded interface measure.
2. These sequences **lack compactness**—no subsequence converges in $\mathcal{P}
(D)$.
3. The only "partition" attaining $F = 0$ (the partition into level sets of $I$)
 is **not a valid open partition**.

An additional penalization term (on $|J|$ or on the total perimeter $\sum_j \mat
hcal{H}^{N-1}(\partial^* \Omega_j)$) **restores compactness** by bounding the co
mplexity of admissible partitions, ensuring that minimizing sequences have conve
rgent subsequences and that the infimum is attained. This is the essential mecha
nism behind the existence theory for the Mumford–Shah functional and related fre
e-discontinuity problems.
