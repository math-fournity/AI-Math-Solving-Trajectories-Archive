## Lower Bound: Zeros from the Gaps

*Proof.* If $f \equiv 1$ on $[d_i, d_{i+1}]$, then $f'' \equiv 0$ there (infinit

$$f'(m_i) = 0, \quad f''(m_i) \geq 0.$$

Now $f'$ vanishes at three points: $d_i$, $m_i$, $d_{i+1}$. By **Rolle's theorem

- There exists $z_i \in (d_i, m_i)$ with $f''(z_i) = 0$.
- There exists $w_i \in (m_i, d_{i+1})$ with $f''(w_i) = 0$.

These two zeros lie in disjoint subintervals, so they are distinct. Since the 35

## Lower Bound: Zeros from the Tails

**Claim:** $f''$ has at least **1** zero in $(-\infty, 1)$ and at least **1** ze

*Proof for $(-\infty, 1)$:* Suppose $f''$ has no zero in $(-\infty, 1)$. Since $
f''(1) \leq 0$ and $f''$ is continuous, $f''(x) < 0$ for all $x < 1$. Then $f'$
is strictly decreasing on $(-\infty, 1)$. Since $f'(1) = 0$, we get $f'(x) > 0$

Since $f'$ is decreasing and positive on $(-\infty, 1)$, for any fixed $x_0 < 1$

$$f(x) = f(x_0) - \int_x^{x_0} f'(t)\,dt \leq f(x_0) - f'(x_0)(x_0 - x) \xrighta
rrow{x \to -\infty} -\infty,$$
contradicting $f > 0$. So $f''$ must have at least one zero in $(-\infty, 1)$.

*Proof for $(2016, +\infty)$:* By the symmetric argument, if $f'' < 0$ on $(2016

**Total lower bound:** $70 + 1 + 1 = \mathbf{72}$.
