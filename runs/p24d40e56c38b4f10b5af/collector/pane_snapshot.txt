## Proof

We show that $f$ always has a unique fixed point.

**Existence.** Define the function $g : X \to \mathbb{R}$ by
$$g(x) = d\bigl(x, f(x)\bigr).$$
Since $d$ is continuous and $f$ is continuous (it is Lipschitz on every pair, in
 fact $d(f(x),f(y)) \le d(x,y)$ gives $1$-Lipschitz), $g$ is continuous as a com
position of continuous maps. As $X$ is compact, $g$ attains its minimum at some
point $p \in X$:
$$g(p) = \min_{x \in X} g(x).$$

Suppose for contradiction that $g(p) > 0$, i.e., $p \neq f(p)$. Applying the hyp
othesis to the distinct pair $(p, f(p))$,
$$d\bigl(f(p), f(f(p))\bigr) < d\bigl(p, f(p)\bigr) = g(p).$$
But the left-hand side is precisely $g(f(p))$. Thus
$$g(f(p)) < g(p),$$
contradicting the minimality of $g(p)$. Hence $g(p) = 0$, which means $d(p, f(p)
) = 0$, i.e., $f(p) = p$. So $p$ is a fixed point of $f$.

**Uniqueness.** Suppose $p, q \in X$ are both fixed points of $f$ with $p \neq q
$. Then
$$d(p, q) = d\bigl(f(p), f(q)\bigr) < d(p, q),$$
a contradiction. Hence $p = q$, and the fixed point is unique.

**Conclusion.** Every such $f$ on a compact metric space has exactly one fixed p
oint. $\blacksquare$
