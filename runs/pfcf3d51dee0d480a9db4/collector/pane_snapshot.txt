## Solution

We evaluate
$$I = \int_{|z|=3} \frac{z^{17}}{(z^2+2)^3(z^3+3)^4}\,dz$$
where the contour is the positively oriented circle $|z|=3$.

**Singularities.** The integrand $f(z)=\dfrac{z^{17}}{(z^2+2)^3(z^3+3)^4}$ has f
inite poles at:
- $z^2+2=0 \Rightarrow z=\pm i\sqrt{2}$, with $|z|=\sqrt{2}\approx 1.414<3$ (pol
es of order 3);
- $z^3+3=0 \Rightarrow z^3=-3$, giving three roots with $|z|=3^{1/3}\approx 1.44
2<3$ (poles of order 4).

All five finite poles lie strictly inside $|z|=3$, and none lie on the contour.
Computing residues at all five poles directly is cumbersome, so we use the **res
idue at infinity**.

**Residue at infinity.** With $w=1/z$,
$$\frac{1}{w^2}\,f\!\left(\frac{1}{w}\right)=\frac{1}{w^2}\cdot\frac{w^{-17}}{(w
^{-2}+2)^3(w^{-3}+3)^4}.$$
Since $(w^{-2}+2)^3 = w^{-6}(1+2w^2)^3$ and $(w^{-3}+3)^4 = w^{-12}(1+3w^3)^4$,
$$\frac{1}{w^2}\,f\!\left(\frac{1}{w}\right)=\frac{1}{w^2}\cdot\frac{w^{-17}}{w^
{-18}(1+2w^2)^3(1+3w^3)^4}=\frac{1}{w\,(1+2w^2)^3(1+3w^3)^4}.$$
This has a simple pole at $w=0$ (the factor $(1+2w^2)^3(1+3w^3)^4$ evaluates to
$1$ at $w=0$). Hence
$$\operatorname*{Res}_{w=0}\frac{1}{w^2}\,f\!\left(\frac{1}{w}\right)=\lim_{w\to
 0}\,w\cdot\frac{1}{w\,(1+2w^2)^3(1+3w^3)^4}=1.$$
Therefore
$$\operatorname*{Res}_{z=\infty}f(z)=-\operatorname*{Res}_{w=0}\frac{1}{w^2}\,f\
!\left(\frac{1}{w}\right)=-1.$$

**Consistency check.** The numerator has degree $17$ and the denominator has deg
ree $2\cdot3+3\cdot4=18$, so $f(z)\sim 1/z$ as $z\to\infty$; the coefficient of
$1/z$ in the Laurent expansion at infinity is $1$, confirming $\operatorname*{Re
s}_{z=\infty}f(z)=-1$.

**Applying the theorem.** Since every finite singularity of $f$ lies inside $|z|
=3$, the residue theorem (including the residue at infinity) gives
$$I=-2\pi i\,\operatorname*{Res}_{z=\infty}f(z)=-2\pi i\cdot(-1)=2\pi i.$$

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{|z|=3}\frac{z^{17}}{(z^2+2)^3(z^3+3)^4}\,dz=2\pi i.}
$$
