## Proof

**Yes, such an inequality is possible.** We show that one may take $a_\epsilon =
 \epsilon$.

**Setup.** Let $u \in H^1_0(\Omega)$. A standard characterization of $H^1_0(\Ome
ga)$ is that the zero extension $\tilde{u}$ of $u$ to $\mathbb{R}^n$ belongs to
$H^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$, with
$$\|\tilde{u}\|_{H^1(\mathbb{R}^n)} = \|u\|_{H^1(\Omega)}, \qquad \|\nabla \tild
e{u}\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)} = \|\nabla u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}.$$

Let $\rho \in C_c^\infty(B(0,1))$ be the standard mollifier with $\rho \geq 0$,
$\int \rho = 1$, and set $\rho_\epsilon(x) = \epsilon^{-n}\rho(x/\epsilon)$. Def
ine
$$u_\epsilon := \rho_\epsilon * \tilde{u} \quad \text{on } \mathbb{R}^n,$$
and restrict $u_\epsilon$ to $\Omega$.

**Key estimate.** For a.e. $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$:
$$u_\epsilon(x) - \tilde{u}(x) = \int_{B(0,\epsilon)} \rho_\epsilon(y)\bigl[\til
de{u}(x-y) - \tilde{u}(x)\bigr]\,dy.$$

Since $\tilde{u} \in H^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$, the fundamental theorem of calculus alo
ng lines gives (for a.e. $x, y$):
$$\tilde{u}(x-y) - \tilde{u}(x) = -\int_0^1 \nabla \tilde{u}(x - ty)\cdot y\,dt.
$$

Therefore, using $|y| \leq \epsilon$ on $\operatorname{supp}(\rho_\epsilon)$:
$$|u_\epsilon(x) - \tilde{u}(x)| \leq \epsilon \int_0^1 \int_{B(0,\epsilon)} \rh
o_\epsilon(y)\,|\nabla \tilde{u}(x-ty)|\,dy\,dt.$$

**Taking the $L^2$ norm.** By Minkowski's inequality (applied to the integral ov
er $t$ and then to the integral over $y$):
$$\|u_\epsilon - \tilde{u}\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)} \leq \epsilon \int_0^1 \left\|\
int_{B(0,\epsilon)} \rho_\epsilon(y)\,|\nabla \tilde{u}(\cdot - ty)|\,dy\right\|
_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)} dt.$$

For each fixed $t \in [0,1]$, Minkowski's integral inequality yields:
$$\left\|\int \rho_\epsilon(y)\,|\nabla \tilde{u}(\cdot - ty)|\,dy\right\|_{L^2}
 \leq \int \rho_\epsilon(y)\,\|\nabla \tilde{u}(\cdot - ty)\|_{L^2}\,dy = \|\nab
la \tilde{u}\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)},$$
since the $L^2$ norm is invariant under translations and $\int \rho_\epsilon\,dy
 = 1$.

**Combining:**
$$\|u_\epsilon - \tilde{u}\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)} \leq \epsilon \int_0^1 \|\nabla
 \tilde{u}\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)}\,dt = \epsilon\,\|\nabla \tilde{u}\|_{L^2(\math
bb{R}^n)} = \epsilon\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}.$$

**Restricting to $\Omega$:** Since $\tilde{u}|_\Omega = u$,
$$\|u_\epsilon - u\|_{L^2(\Omega)} \leq \|u_\epsilon - \tilde{u}\|_{L^2(\mathbb{
R}^n)} \leq \epsilon\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^2(\Omega)} \leq \epsilon\,\|u\|_{H^1(\Omeg
a)}.$$

**Conclusion.** The desired inequality holds with $a_\epsilon = \epsilon$, which
 satisfies $a_\epsilon \to 0$ as $\epsilon \to 0$.

**Remark on the role of $H^1_0$.** The hypothesis $u \in H^1_0(\Omega)$ (rather
than merely $H^1(\Omega)$) is essential. It guarantees that the zero extension $
\tilde{u}$ lies in $H^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$, so that $\nabla \tilde{u}$ is a well-def
ined $L^2$ function on all of $\mathbb{R}^n$ with no spurious boundary contribut
ion. For a general $u \in H^1(\Omega) \setminus H^1_0(\Omega)$, the zero extensi
on fails to be in $H^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$ (a jump discontinuity appears at $\partial
\Omega$), and mollification of the zero extension produces an $O(1)$ error near
the boundary that cannot be controlled by $\|u\|_{H^1(\Omega)}$ with any $a_\eps
ilon \to 0$.
