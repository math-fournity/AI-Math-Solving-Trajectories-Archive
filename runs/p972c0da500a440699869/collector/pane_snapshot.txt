## Answer

**No.** Two matrices with identical characteristic and minimal polynomials but d
ifferent ranks are **not** similar — and, moreover, such pairs genuinely exist (
the hypothesis is not vacuous).

### Proof

**Step 1. Rank is a similarity invariant.**

If $A$ and $B$ are similar, then $B = PAP^{-1}$ for some invertible $P$, so $B$
is a composition of invertible maps with $A$. Hence $\operatorname{rank}(B) = \o
peratorname{rank}(A)$. Therefore, **any** two matrices of different rank are aut
omatically not similar. This already answers the question negatively.

**Step 2. The hypothesis is not vacuous: a concrete pair exists.**

Work over any field (we use only the eigenvalue $0$, which is available in every
 field). Consider the $5\times 5$ nilpotent matrices in Jordan form

$$A = J_3(0)\oplus J_2(0), \qquad B = J_3(0)\oplus J_1(0)\oplus J_1(0),$$

where $J_k(0)$ denotes the $k\times k$ nilpotent Jordan block.

- **Characteristic polynomial.** Both are nilpotent of size $5$, so
  $$\chi_A(t)=\chi_B(t)=t^5.$$

- **Minimal polynomial.** The minimal polynomial of a direct sum of Jordan block
s for eigenvalue $0$ is $t^{\,\ell}$ where $\ell$ is the size of the largest blo
ck. Both $A$ and $B$ have largest block $J_3(0)$, so
  $$m_A(t)=m_B(t)=t^3.$$

- **Ranks.** A nilpotent Jordan block $J_k(0)$ has rank $k-1$ (it has $1$'s on t
he superdiagonal and zeros elsewhere, giving $k-1$ independent columns). Hence
  $$\operatorname{rank}(A)=\operatorname{rank}(J_3)+\operatorname{rank}(J_2)=2+1
=3,$$
  $$\operatorname{rank}(B)=\operatorname{rank}(J_3)+\operatorname{rank}(J_1)+\op
eratorname{rank}(J_1)=2+0+0=2.$$

Thus $\operatorname{rank}(A)=3\neq 2=\operatorname{rank}(B)$, while $\chi_A=\chi
_B$ and $m_A=m_B$.

**Step 3. Conclusion.**

Since $A$ and $B$ have different ranks, they cannot be similar (Step 1), even th
ough they share both their characteristic and their minimal polynomial (Step 2).
 The deeper reason is that the characteristic polynomial fixes the *total* size
of the Jordan blocks for each eigenvalue, and the minimal polynomial fixes only
the *largest* block size per eigenvalue — but the *number* of blocks (equivalent
ly, the geometric multiplicity of each eigenvalue, which determines the nullity
and hence the rank) is not pinned down by these two invariants alone. The two pa
rtitions $\{3,2\}$ and $\{3,1,1\}$ of $5$ both have largest part $3$, yet contai
n different numbers of parts, producing different ranks.

Therefore, two matrices with identical characteristic and minimal polynomials bu
t different ranks are **not similar**.
