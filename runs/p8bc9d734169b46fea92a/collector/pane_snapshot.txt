## Proof

**Setup.** Let $B = (B_t)_{t \geq 0}$ be a standard Brownian motion with $B_0 =
0$, and let
$$\sigma = \inf\{t \geq 0 : |B_t| = \sqrt{2}\}$$
be the first exit time of $B$ from the open interval $(-\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{2})$.

**Step 1. Value of $B_\sigma^2$.**

By continuity of Brownian motion, $|B_\sigma| = \sqrt{2}$, hence
$$B_\sigma^2 = 2 \quad \text{a.s.}$$

Therefore
$$\mathbb{E}[\sigma\, B_\sigma^2] = 2\,\mathbb{E}[\sigma]. \tag{1}$$

**Step 2. Compute $\mathbb{E}[\sigma]$ via the Optional Stopping Theorem.**

The process $M_t := B_t^2 - t$ is a martingale (Itô's formula gives $d(B_t^2) =
2B_t\,dB_t + dt$, so $d(B_t^2 - t) = 2B_t\,dB_t$).

For each $n \geq 1$, apply the Optional Stopping Theorem to the bounded stopping
 time $\sigma \wedge n$:
$$\mathbb{E}[M_{\sigma \wedge n}] = M_0 = 0,$$
i.e.,
$$\mathbb{E}[B_{\sigma \wedge n}^2] = \mathbb{E}[\sigma \wedge n]. \tag{2}$$

**Step 3. Take $n \to \infty$.**

- *Right-hand side:* $\sigma \wedge n \uparrow \sigma$, so by the Monotone Conve
rgence Theorem,
$$\mathbb{E}[\sigma \wedge n] \uparrow \mathbb{E}[\sigma].$$

- *Left-hand side:* For all $t < \sigma$ we have $|B_t| < \sqrt{2}$, hence $B_{\
sigma \wedge n}^2 \leq 2$ a.s. Moreover $B_{\sigma \wedge n}^2 \to B_\sigma^2 =
2$ a.s. By the Dominated Convergence Theorem,
$$\mathbb{E}[B_{\sigma \wedge n}^2] \to \mathbb{E}[B_\sigma^2] = 2.$$

Passing to the limit in (2):
$$\mathbb{E}[\sigma] = 2. \tag{3}$$

**Step 4. Combine.**

Substituting (3) into (1):
$$\mathbb{E}[\sigma\, B_\sigma^2] = 2 \cdot \mathbb{E}[\sigma] = 2 \cdot 2 = \bo
xed{4}.$$
