## Problem

Determine the Morley rank of the type $x = x$ in $ACF_0$.

## Proof

Work in a saturated model $K \models ACF_0$. The formula $x = x$ (in one free va
riable) defines the whole affine line $K = \mathbb{A}^1(K)$.

**Claim.** $\mathrm{MR}(x = x) = 1$.

We prove $\mathrm{MR}(K) = 1$ by establishing both inequalities.

### $\mathrm{MR}(K) \geq 1$

A set has Morley rank $\geq 1$ iff it contains infinitely many pairwise disjoint
 definable subsets of Morley rank $\geq 0$. For each $a \in K$, the singleton $\
{a\}$ is definable (cut out by $x = a$) and has $\mathrm{MR} = 0$. There are inf
initely many such singletons, and they are pairwise disjoint. Hence
$$\mathrm{MR}(K) \geq 1.$$

### $\mathrm{MR}(K) \leq 1$

We must show $K$ cannot be split into infinitely many pairwise disjoint definabl
e subsets each of Morley rank $\geq 1$.

**Key fact (ACF).** In one variable over an algebraically closed field, every de
finable subset of $K$ is *constructible* in the Zariski topology. The Zariski cl
osed subsets of $\mathbb{A}^1$ are: all of $\mathbb{A}^1$, and finite sets. Ther
efore every definable subset $D \subseteq K$ is either finite (hence $\mathrm{MR
}(D) = 0$) or cofinite (hence $\mathrm{MR}(D) = 1$, since a cofinite set is Zari
ski-dense in $\mathbb{A}^1$ and contains infinitely many singletons).

Now suppose, towards contradiction, that $K$ contains infinitely many pairwise d
isjoint definable subsets $\{D_i\}_{i \in I}$ with $\mathrm{MR}(D_i) \geq 1$ for
 all $i$. By the key fact, each $D_i$ must be cofinite (the only definable sets
of positive Morley rank in one variable are cofinite). But two cofinite subsets
of $K$ have finite complements, so their intersection is cofinite and in particu
lar nonempty. This contradicts pairwise disjointness.

Therefore no such infinite disjoint family exists, and
$$\mathrm{MR}(K) \leq 1.$$

### Conclusion

Combining both inequalities,
$$\boxed{\mathrm{MR}(x = x) = 1}$$

in $ACF_0$. (The Morley degree is $1$, since the only definable subset of $K$ of
 Morley rank $1$ is $K$ itself up to a finite set, and the generic type of the a
ffine line is unique.)
