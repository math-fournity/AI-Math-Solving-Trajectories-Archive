Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a $G$-invariant complement
$U'$ to $W$ exists, given that $G$ is finite, $V = W \oplus U$, $W$ is infinite-
dimensional and $G$-invariant, and $U$ is finite-dimensional and non-zero.

The answer is **yes**. The key insight is that Maschke's averaging argument only
 requires summing over the finite group $G$, so it works even when $V$ is infini
te-dimensional. Here is the proof.

---

**Proof.**

Let $\pi: V \to V$ be the linear projection onto $U$ along $W$, so $\pi^2 = \pi$
, $\operatorname{im} \pi = U$, and $\ker \pi = W$.

Define the **averaged** map:
$$\widetilde{\pi} = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g \in G} \rho(g)^{-1} \circ \pi \circ \rh
o(g).$$

This is a finite sum (since $G$ is finite), so $\widetilde{\pi}: V \to V$ is a w
ell-defined linear map, regardless of the dimension of $V$.

**Step 1: $\widetilde{\pi}$ is $G$-equivariant.**

For any $h \in G$:
$$\rho(h)^{-1}\, \widetilde{\pi}\, \rho(h) = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g \in G} \rho(h)
^{-1}\rho(g)^{-1}\,\pi\,\rho(g)\rho(h) = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g \in G} \rho(gh)^{-
1}\,\pi\,\rho(gh).$$

As $g$ ranges over $G$, so does $gh$ (left-translation is a bijection). Hence:
$$\rho(h)^{-1}\,\widetilde{\pi}\,\rho(h) = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g' \in G} \rho(g')
^{-1}\,\pi\,\rho(g') = \widetilde{\pi}.$$

Thus $\widetilde{\pi} \circ \rho(h) = \rho(h) \circ \widetilde{\pi}$ for all $h
\in G$.

**Step 2: $\widetilde{\pi}$ annihilates $W$.**

For $w \in W$: since $W$ is $G$-invariant, $\rho(g)w \in W = \ker \pi$, so $\pi(
\rho(g)w) = 0$ for every $g$. Therefore:
$$\widetilde{\pi}(w) = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g \in G} \rho(g)^{-1}\,\pi(\rho(g)w) =
 0.$$

So $W \subseteq \ker \widetilde{\pi}$.

**Step 3: $\widetilde{\pi}$ induces the identity on $V/W$.**

Let $\bar{v}$ denote the class of $v$ in $V/W$. For any $v \in V$ and $g \in G$,
 write $\rho(g)v = \pi(\rho(g)v) + w_g$ where $w_g \in W$. Then:
$$\rho(g)^{-1}\,\pi(\rho(g)v) = \rho(g)^{-1}(\rho(g)v - w_g) = v - \rho(g)^{-1}w
_g.$$

Since $W$ is $G$-invariant, $\rho(g)^{-1}w_g \in W$, so:
$$\overline{\rho(g)^{-1}\,\pi(\rho(g)v)} = \bar{v}.$$

Averaging over $g$:
$$\overline{\widetilde{\pi}(v)} = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g \in G} \bar{v} = \bar{v}.
$$

**Step 4: $\widetilde{\pi}$ is idempotent, and $\ker \widetilde{\pi} = W$.**

From Step 3, $\widetilde{\pi}(v) - v \in W$ for all $v \in V$. Since $\widetilde
{\pi}$ annihilates $W$ (Step 2):
$$\widetilde{\pi}\bigl(\widetilde{\pi}(v) - v\bigr) = 0 \implies \widetilde{\pi}
^2(v) = \widetilde{\pi}(v).$$

So $\widetilde{\pi}$ is a projection. Moreover, $\ker \widetilde{\pi} = W$: the
inclusion $W \subseteq \ker \widetilde{\pi}$ is Step 2, and the reverse inclusio
n holds because if $\widetilde{\pi}(v) = 0$, then $\bar{v} = \overline{\widetild
e{\pi}(v)} = 0$ by Step 3, so $v \in W$.

**Step 5: The image of $\widetilde{\pi}$ is the desired $U'$.**

Set $U' = \operatorname{im} \widetilde{\pi}$. Since $\widetilde{\pi}$ is a proje
ction with $\ker \widetilde{\pi} = W$:
$$V = \ker \widetilde{\pi} \oplus \operatorname{im} \widetilde{\pi} = W \oplus U
'.$$

Since $\widetilde{\pi}$ is $G$-equivariant (Step 1), for any $u' = \widetilde{\p
i}(v) \in U'$ and $h \in G$:
$$\rho(h)u' = \rho(h)\widetilde{\pi}(v) = \widetilde{\pi}(\rho(h)v) \in U'.$$

So $U'$ is $G$-invariant.

Finally, $U'$ is finite-dimensional (it is isomorphic to $V/W \cong U$, which is
 finite-dimensional) and non-zero (since $U \neq 0$). $\blacksquare$

---

**Remark.** The infinite-dimensionality of $W$ is a red herring. The averaging a
rgument requires only that $G$ be finite (so the sum is finite) and that $W$ be
$G$-invariant (so the quotient $V/W$ carries a $G$-action). The complement $U'$
is constructed as the image of the $G$-equivariant projection $\widetilde{\pi}$,
 and its dimension equals $\dim(V/W) = \dim U < \infty$.
