## Proof

We compute the convolution of $f$ with the Dirac comb $\mathrm{III}_p(x) = \sum_
{k=-\infty}^{\infty} \delta(x - kp)$.

By definition of convolution:

$$(f * \mathrm{III}_p)(x) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(t)\, \mathrm{III}_p(x - t)
\, dt.$$

Substitute the definition of the comb:

$$(f * \mathrm{III}_p)(x) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(t) \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\inft
y} \delta\big((x-t) - kp\big)\, dt.$$

Exchanging the sum and integral (justified in the distributional sense):

$$(f * \mathrm{III}_p)(x) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(
t)\, \delta\big(x - kp - t\big)\, dt.$$

By the sifting property of the Dirac delta, $\int f(t)\,\delta(x - kp - t)\,dt =
 f(x - kp)$, so

$$(f * \mathrm{III}_p)(x) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} f(x - kp).$$

**Effect:** Convolving $f$ with the Dirac comb $\mathrm{III}_p$ produces a **per
iodic replication** (periodic summation) of $f$ — an infinite superposition of c
opies of $f$ translated by every integer multiple of the spacing $p$. The result
 is a $p$-periodic function:

$$(f * \mathrm{III}_p)(x+p) = \sum_{k} f(x+p - kp) = \sum_{k} f(x - (k-1)p) = \s
um_{j} f(x - jp) = (f * \mathrm{III}_p)(x).$$

This is the spatial-domain counterpart of the Poisson summation formula: samplin
g in one domain corresponds to periodization in the other. In signal processing,
 convolving a signal with a Dirac comb creates a periodic extension of the signa
