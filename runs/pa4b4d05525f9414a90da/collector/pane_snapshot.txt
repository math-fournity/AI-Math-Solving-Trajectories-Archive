# Proof: The Smale-Birkhoff Homoclinic Theorem Implies Infinitely Many Distinct
Homoclinic Orbits

## Setup and Statement of the Theorem

Let $f: M \to M$ be a $C^1$ diffeomorphism of a smooth manifold $M$, and let $p$
 be a hyperbolic fixed point of $f$. Suppose there exists a transverse homoclini
c point $q \in W^s(p) \cap W^u(p)$, meaning the stable and unstable manifolds $W
^s(p)$ and $W^u(p)$ intersect transversally at $q$ (and $q \neq p$).

The **Smale–Birkhoff homoclinic theorem** states that under these hypotheses, th
ere exists a compact hyperbolic $f$-invariant Cantor set $\Lambda$ (a *horseshoe
*) containing $p$ and $q$, and a positive integer $N$, such that $f^N|_\Lambda$
is topologically conjugate to a subshift of finite type on a finite alphabet $\m
athcal{A}$ with $|\mathcal{A}| \geq 2$. That is, there exists a homeomorphism $h
: \Lambda \to \Sigma \subseteq \mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}$ such that

$$h \circ f^N|_\Lambda = \sigma \circ h,$$

where $\sigma: \Sigma \to \Sigma$ is the shift map $\sigma(\mathbf{s})_i = s_{i+
1}$.

## The Symbolic Coding of the Fixed Point

Under the conjugacy, the fixed point $p$ (which is fixed by $f$ and hence by $f^
N$) maps to a periodic point of $\sigma$. Without loss of generality (by replaci
ng $f$ with $f^N$ and passing to the appropriate subshift), we may assume $p$ co
rresponds to a fixed point of $\sigma$. Denote this fixed sequence by $\mathbf{s
}^* \in \Sigma$, so $\sigma(\mathbf{s}^*) = \mathbf{s}^*$.

Concretely, in the standard full shift on $\{0, 1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}$, the fixed poi
nt is $\mathbf{s}^* = \mathbf{0}^\infty = (\ldots, 0, 0, 0, \ldots)$.

## Homoclinic Orbits in Symbolic Dynamics

A point $x \in \Lambda$ is **homoclinic to $p$** (for $f^N$) if $f^{Nn}(x) \to p
$ as $n \to \pm\infty$. Under the conjugacy, this translates to: the sequence $\
mathbf{s} = h(x)$ satisfies $\sigma^n(\mathbf{s}) \to \mathbf{s}^*$ as $n \to \p
m\infty$.

In the symbolic space, $\sigma^n(\mathbf{s}) \to \mathbf{s}^*$ as $n \to +\infty
$ means that the forward tail of $\mathbf{s}$ eventually agrees with $\mathbf{s}
^*$: there exists $N_+$ such that $s_i = s^*_i$ for all $i \geq N_+$. Similarly,
 $\sigma^n(\mathbf{s}) \to \mathbf{s}^*$ as $n \to -\infty$ means there exists $
N_-$ such that $s_i = s^*_i$ for all $i \leq N_-$.

Thus, **homoclinic sequences** are exactly those that agree with $\mathbf{s}^*$
outside a finite window — they differ from $\mathbf{s}^*$ on only finitely many
coordinates.

## Constructing Infinitely Many Distinct Homoclinic Orbits

We now exhibit infinitely many homoclinic sequences that lie on **distinct $\sig
ma$-orbits**, which correspond to distinct $f^N$-orbits, and hence to distinct $
f$-orbits (since $f$-orbits refine $f^N$-orbits: two points on the same $f$-orbi
t are on the same $f^N$-orbit, so distinct $f^N$-orbits yield distinct $f$-orbit
s).

**Construction.** Work in the full shift on $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}$ with $\mathbf
{s}^* = \mathbf{0}^\infty$. For each integer $m \geq 1$, define the sequence $\m
athbf{s}^{(m)}$ by:

$$s^{(m)}_i = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } 0 \leq i \leq m-1, \\ 0 & \text{other
wise.} \end{cases}$$

That is, $\mathbf{s}^{(m)}$ is the sequence that is $0$ everywhere except for a
block of $m$ consecutive $1$'s at positions $0, 1, \ldots, m-1$.

**Each $\mathbf{s}^{(m)}$ is homoclinic to $\mathbf{0}^\infty$:** The sequence d
iffers from $\mathbf{0}^\infty$ only on the finite set $\{0, 1, \ldots, m-1\}$,
so $\sigma^n(\mathbf{s}^{(m)}) \to \mathbf{0}^\infty$ as $n \to \pm\infty$.

**The orbits are distinct:** Two sequences $\mathbf{s}^{(m)}$ and $\mathbf{s}^{(
m')}$ with $m \neq m'$ lie on different $\sigma$-orbits. To see this, note that
the **number of nonzero entries** is an invariant of the $\sigma$-orbit of any h
omoclinic sequence (shifting preserves the total count of $1$'s, since the seque
nce has only finitely many $1$'s). The sequence $\mathbf{s}^{(m)}$ has exactly $
m$ entries equal to $1$, while $\mathbf{s}^{(m')}$ has exactly $m'$ entries equa
l to $1$. Since $m \neq m'$, no shift of $\mathbf{s}^{(m)}$ equals $\mathbf{s}^{
(m')}$.

Therefore, $\{\mathbf{s}^{(m)} : m \geq 1\}$ represents infinitely many distinct
 $\sigma$-orbits, all homoclinic to $\mathbf{0}^\infty$.

## Lifting Back to the Dynamical System

Via the conjugacy $h^{-1}$, each $\mathbf{s}^{(m)}$ corresponds to a point $x_m
= h^{-1}(\mathbf{s}^{(m)}) \in \Lambda$. Since $\mathbf{s}^{(m)}$ is homoclinic
to $\mathbf{s}^*$ under $\sigma$, the point $x_m$ is homoclinic to $p$ under $f^
N$:

$$f^{Nn}(x_m) \to p \quad \text{as } n \to \pm\infty.$$

Since the $\sigma$-orbits of $\mathbf{s}^{(m)}$ are distinct, the $f^N$-orbits o
f $x_m$ are distinct. Since distinct $f^N$-orbits are necessarily distinct $f$-o
rbits (an $f$-orbit is a refinement of an $f^N$-orbit), the points $\{x_m : m \g
eq 1\}$ lie on **infinitely many distinct $f$-orbits**.

Each such $f$-orbit is homoclinic to $p$: since $f^{Nn}(x_m) \to p$ and $p$ is a
 fixed point of $f$, we have $f^k(x_m) \to p$ as $k \to \pm\infty$ (the iterates
 between multiples of $N$ remain bounded and converge to $p$ by the hyperbolicit
y of $\Lambda$ and the fact that $f$ maps $\Lambda$ to itself, preserving the Ca
ntor structure).

## Conclusion

Starting from a single transverse homoclinic point, the Smale–Birkhoff theorem p
roduces a horseshoe on which the dynamics is conjugate to a shift on at least tw
o symbols. The symbolic dynamics contains infinitely many distinct orbits homocl
inic to the fixed point (one for each finite "excursion" away from the fixed seq
uence). These lift to infinitely many distinct homoclinic orbits in the original
 system.

Therefore, **yes, the Smale–Birkhoff homoclinic theorem implies the existence of
 infinitely many distinct homoclinic orbits**. $\blacksquare$
