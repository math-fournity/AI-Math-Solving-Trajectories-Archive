Step 3: $f$ is surjective (for $X \neq \emptyset$).

As a local homeomorphism, $f$ is an open map, so $f(X)$ is open in $Y$. By Step
1, $f(X)$ is also closed (since $X$ is closed in itself). Since $Y$ is connected
and $f(X) \neq \emptyset$, we get $f(X) = Y$.

Step 4: The fiber cardinality $|f^{-1}(y)|$ is locally constant.

Fix $y_0 \in Y$ with $f^{-1}(y_0) = {x_1, \dots, x_n}$. Since $X$ is Hausdorff,
choose pairwise disjoint open neighborhoods $U_i$ of $x_i$. Shrink each $U_i$ so
that $f|{U_i}: U_i \to f(U_i)$ is a homeomorphism onto an open set. Set $V' =
\bigcap{i=1}^n f(U_i)$, an open neighborhood of $y_0$.

The set $X \setminus \bigcup_i U_i$ is closed, so $f(X \setminus \bigcup_i U_i)$
is closed in $Y$ (Step 1). Since $y_0 \notin f(X \setminus \bigcup_i U_i)$, the
set $$V = V' \setminus f!\left(X \setminus \bigcup_i U_i\right)$$ is an open
neighborhood of $y_0$.

For any $y \in V$:

  • $y \in V' \subseteq f(U_i)$ for each $i$, so each $U_i$ contains at least
  one preimage of $y$: $|f^{-1}(y)| \geq n$.
  • $y \notin f(X \setminus \bigcup_i U_i)$, so $f^{-1}(y) \subseteq \bigcup_i
  U_i$.
  • The $U_i$ are disjoint and $f|_{U_i}$ is injective, so $|f^{-1}(y) \cap U_i|
  \leq 1$, giving $|f^{-1}(y)| \leq n$.

Therefore $|f^{-1}(y)| = n$ for all $y \in V$, proving local constancy.

Step 5: $f$ is a covering map.

Since $Y$ is connected and $|f^{-1}(y)|$ is locally constant, it is globally
constant, say $n$. For each $y_0 \in Y$, construct $V, U_1, \dots, U_n$ as in
Step 4. Define $W_i = f^{-1}(V) \cap U_i = (f|_{U_i})^{-1}(V)$. Then:

  • Each $W_i$ is open and $f|_{W_i}: W_i \to V$ is a homeomorphism.
  • The $W_i$ are pairwise disjoint (since the $U_i$ are).
  • $f^{-1}(V) \subseteq \bigcup_i U_i$ (by construction of $V$), so $f^{-1}(V)
  = \bigsqcup_{i=1}^n W_i$.

Thus $V$ is evenly covered, and $f$ is a covering map. $\blacksquare$

Remark. The local path-connectedness of $Y$ is not actually needed for this
argument; the Hausdorff condition on $X$ (to separate fiber points), the
compactly generated Hausdorff condition on $Y$ (to ensure proper $\Rightarrow$
closed), and the connectedness of $Y$ (for global constancy of fiber size) are
the essential ingredients.
