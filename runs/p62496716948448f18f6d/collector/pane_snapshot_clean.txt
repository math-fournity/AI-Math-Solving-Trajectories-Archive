Proof

Let $f(x) \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ be irreducible of prime degree $p$, with exactly
two nonreal roots in $\mathbb{C}$. Let $K$ be the splitting field of $f$ over $
\mathbb{Q}$ and $G = \mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$. We view $G$ as a subgroup of
$S_p$ via its faithful action on the $p$ roots of $f$.

Step 1. $G$ acts transitively on the roots

Since $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, for any two roots $\alpha_i,
\alpha_j$ there is a $\mathbb{Q}$-embedding $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_i)
\hookrightarrow \mathbb{C}$ sending $\alpha_i \mapsto \alpha_j$. This embedding
extends to an automorphism of the splitting field $K$, so $G$ acts transitively
on the roots. In particular $p \mid |G|$, and by Cauchy's theorem $G$ contains
an element of order $p$, i.e. a $p$-cycle.

Step 2. $G$ contains a transposition

Since $f$ has rational (hence real) coefficients, complex conjugation $z \mapsto
\bar z$ permutes the roots of $f$, and therefore restricts to an automorphism $
\sigma \in G$ fixing $\mathbb{Q}$.

By hypothesis exactly two roots are nonreal. Nonreal roots of a real polynomial
occur in conjugate pairs, so the two nonreal roots are $\alpha$ and $\bar\alpha$
for some $\alpha$, and the remaining $p-2$ roots are real. Complex conjugation
fixes every real root and swaps $\alpha \leftrightarrow \bar\alpha$. Thus, as a
permutation of the roots, $\sigma$ is a single transposition. Hence $G$ contains
a transposition.

Step 3. A transitive subgroup of $S_p$ ($p$ prime) containing a transposition is
all of $S_p$

This is the key group-theoretic lemma.

Lemma. Let $p$ be prime and $G \leq S_p$ be transitive. If $G$ contains a
transposition, then $G = S_p$.

Proof of lemma. By transitivity and Cauchy's theorem ($p \mid |G|$), $G$
contains a $p$-cycle $\tau$. Conjugating within $S_p$ (which preserves the
hypothesis, since we conjugate the whole group), we may relabel so that $\tau =
(1;2;3;\cdots;p)$, acting on indices modulo $p$.

Let $(i;j) \in G$ be a transposition, and set $d \equiv j - i \pmod{p}$, $d
\not\equiv 0$. Conjugating $(i;j)$ by powers of $\tau$ shows that $G$ contains
every transposition $$ (a;; a+d), \qquad a \in \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}. $$ Since
$p$ is prime and $d \not\equiv 0 \pmod p$, we have $\gcd(d,p)=1$, so $d$
generates the additive group $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$. Consider the graph $
\Gamma$ on vertex set $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ with edges ${a,, a+d}$ for all
$a$. Because $d$ generates $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$, this graph is a single
cycle through all $p$ vertices, hence connected.

A standard fact: the transpositions corresponding to the edges of a connected
graph on $n$ vertices generate $S_n$. (Indeed, for any two vertices $u,v$, a
path $u=x_0,x_1,\dots,x_m=v$ gives $(u;v) = (x_0;x_1)
(x_1;x_2)\cdots(x_{m-1};x_m)\cdots(x_1;x_2)(x_0;x_1)$, so every transposition
lies in the group generated by edge-transpositions; and all transpositions
generate $S_n$.)

Therefore $G$ contains every transposition in $S_p$, so $G = S_p$. $\square$

2), and $p$ is prime. Hence $G = \mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) = S_p$.
