## Geodesics on the cone $z = x\rho$

### 1. Geometry and metric

Write the cone in cylindrical coordinates $(\rho,\theta,z)$ as
\[
z = x\,\rho,\qquad x=\text{const.}
\]
Let $\alpha$ be the half-angle of the cone (angle between its axis and a generat
or). Since $z/\rho = \cot\alpha$, we have $x=\cot\alpha$, hence
\[
\sin\alpha = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}\;=:\;\beta .
\]
The line element on the surface is
\[
ds^{2}=d\rho^{2}+\rho^{2}\,d\theta^{2}+dz^{2}
       =d\rho^{2}\bigl(1+\cot^{2}\alpha\bigr)+\rho^{2}\,d\theta^{2}
       =\frac{d\rho^{2}}{\beta^{2}}+\rho^{2}\,d\theta^{2}.
\]

### 2. First integral of the geodesic equation

Take $\theta$ as the parameter and put $\dot\rho=d\rho/d\theta$. The length func
tional is
\[
S=\int L\,d\theta,\qquad L=\sqrt{\rho^{2}+\frac{\dot\rho^{2}}{\beta^{2}}}.
\]
Since $L$ has no explicit $\theta$-dependence, the Beltrami identity gives a con
stant $c$:
\[
L-\dot\rho\,\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot\rho}=c
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\frac{\rho^{2}}{L}=c
\;\Longrightarrow\;
L=\frac{\rho^{2}}{c}.
\]
Squaring $L^{2}=\rho^{4}/c^{2}$ and using $L^{2}=\rho^{2}+\dot\rho^{2}/\beta^{2}
$,
\[
\boxed{\;\rho^{4}=c^{2}\!\left[\rho^{2}+\frac{\dot\rho^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\right]\;}
\]
which is the stated geodesic condition (with $\beta=\sin\alpha=1/\sqrt{1+x^{2}}$
 the particular cone-dependent value).

### 3. Form of $\rho(\theta)$

From the first integral,
\[
\dot\rho^{2}=\frac{\beta^{2}}{c^{2}}\,\rho^{2}\bigl(\rho^{2}-c^{2}\bigr)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{d\rho}{\rho\sqrt{\rho^{2}-c^{2}}}=\pm\frac{\beta\,d\theta}{c}.
\]
Using $\displaystyle\int\frac{d\rho}{\rho\sqrt{\rho^{2}-c^{2}}}=\frac{1}{c}\arcc
os\!\frac{c}{\rho}$,
\[
\arccos\!\frac{c}{\rho}=\pm\beta\theta+\delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{\;\rho(\theta)=\frac{c}{\cos(\beta\theta+\delta)}\;}.
\]
This is the equation of a straight line in the developed (unrolled) cone: the co
ne opens into a plane sector of angle $2\pi\beta$, with developed polar coordina
tes $(r,\phi)=(\rho/\beta,\,\beta\theta)$, and the relation above is exactly $r\
cos(\phi+\delta)=c/\beta$, a line at perpendicular distance $c/\beta$ from the a
pex.

### 4. Shortest path between $(R,-\theta_{0},xR)$ and $(R,\theta_{0},xR)$

Both endpoints lie at $\rho=R$, at heights $z=xR$, symmetric about $\theta=0$. B
y symmetry the minimizing geodesic has $\delta=0$, and the boundary condition $\
rho(\pm\theta_{0})=R$ gives
\[
R=\frac{c}{\cos(\beta\theta_{0})}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad c=R\cos(\beta\theta_{
0}).
\]
Hence the shortest path is
\[
\boxed{\;\rho(\theta)=\frac{R\cos(\beta\theta_{0})}{\cos(\beta\theta)},\qquad -\
theta_{0}\le\theta\le\theta_{0},\;}
\]
with $\beta=\sin\alpha=1/\sqrt{1+x^{2}}$.

Equivalently, in Cartesian coordinates on the cone,
\[
\boxed{\;\bigl(\rho\cos\theta,\;\rho\sin\theta,\;x\rho\bigr),\quad
\rho(\theta)=\frac{R\cos(\beta\theta_{0})}{\cos(\beta\theta)},\quad
-\theta_{0}\le\theta\le\theta_{0}.\;}
\]

**Validity.** The geodesic stays clear of the apex iff $\cos(\beta\theta_{0})>0$
, i.e. $\beta\theta_{0}<\pi/2$; the direct chord lies inside one copy of the dev
eloped sector when $2\beta\theta_{0}<2\pi\beta$, i.e. $\theta_{0}<\pi$. Under th
ese (generic) conditions the curve above is the unique shortest path.

**Length.** In the developed plane the two endpoints are at radius $r_{0}=R/\bet
a=R\sqrt{1+x^{2}}$ with angular separation $2\beta\theta_{0}$, so the chord leng
th is
\[
\boxed{\;\ell=2r_{0}\sin(\beta\theta_{0})
      =2R\sqrt{1+x^{2}}\;\sin\!\left(\frac{\theta_{0}}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}\right).\;
}
\]
