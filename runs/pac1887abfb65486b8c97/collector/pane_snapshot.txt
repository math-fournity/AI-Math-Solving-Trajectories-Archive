# Solution

The statement is **FALSE**. The "if" direction holds, but the "only if" directio
n fails.

## The "if" direction (TRUE): $X_n - X \to 0$ in distribution $\Rightarrow$ $X_n
 \to X$ in distribution

**Key fact:** Convergence in distribution to a *constant* implies convergence in
 probability.

*Proof of key fact.* If $Y_n \to c$ in distribution (constant $c$), then for eve
ry $\varepsilon > 0$,
$$P(|Y_n - c| > \varepsilon) = P(Y_n > c+\varepsilon) + P(Y_n < c-\varepsilon).$
$
Since the cdf of the constant $c$ is continuous at $c \pm \varepsilon$ (as $\var
epsilon > 0$), convergence in distribution gives $P(Y_n \le c+\varepsilon) \to 1
$ and $P(Y_n \le c-\varepsilon) \to 0$, so $P(|Y_n - c|>\varepsilon) \to 0$. ∎

Applying this: $X_n - X \to 0$ in distribution $\Rightarrow$ $X_n - X \to 0$ in
probability $\Rightarrow$ $X_n \to X$ in probability $\Rightarrow$ $X_n \to X$ i
n distribution (since convergence in probability implies convergence in distribu
tion). ✓

## The "only if" direction (FALSE): $X_n \to X$ in distribution $\nRightarrow$ $
X_n - X \to 0$ in distribution

**Counterexample.** Let $X \sim N(0,1)$. For each $n$, let $X_n \sim N(0,1)$ be
**independent** of $X$ (all defined on a common probability space, e.g., take $X
_n = Z_n$ where $\{Z_n\}$ are i.i.d. $N(0,1)$ and independent of $X$).

- **$X_n \to X$ in distribution:** Every $X_n$ has the same distribution as $X$
($N(0,1)$), so trivially $X_n \to X$ in distribution.
- **$X_n - X \not\to 0$ in distribution:** Since $X_n$ and $X$ are independent,
$X_n - X \sim N(0, 2)$ for every $n$. This is a fixed non-degenerate distributio
n, so it does **not** converge to the point mass $\delta_0$.

Thus $X_n \to X$ in distribution holds, yet $X_n - X \to 0$ in distribution fail
s.

## Conclusion

The biconditional is **false**. The "if" direction ($\Leftarrow$) is true (via t
he fact that convergence in distribution to a constant yields convergence in pro
bability), but the "only if" direction ($\Rightarrow$) is false, as demonstrated
 by the counterexample where $X_n$ and $X$ share the same marginal distribution
but are independent, making $X_n - X$ have a fixed non-degenerate distribution.
