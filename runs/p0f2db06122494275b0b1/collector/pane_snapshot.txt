## Solution

**Setup.** The square $S$ has vertices $(\pm1,\pm1)$, center at the origin, and
four sides $x=\pm1$, $y=\pm1$. A point $(x,y)\in S$ belongs to $R$ iff its dista
nce to the center is strictly less than its distance to **every** side:

$$\sqrt{x^2+y^2} < 1-x,\quad \sqrt{x^2+y^2}<1+x,\quad \sqrt{x^2+y^2}<1-y,\quad \
sqrt{x^2+y^2}<1+y.$$

**Symmetry.** The region and all constraints are symmetric under $x\mapsto -x$,
$y\mapsto -y$, and $x\leftrightarrow y$. So $\operatorname{Area}(R)=4\cdot\opera
torname{Area}(R\cap Q)$, where $Q$ is the first quadrant ($x\ge0,y\ge0$).

**Reducing the constraints in $Q$.** Squaring (all sides are nonnegative in $Q$)
:
- $\sqrt{x^2+y^2}<1+x \iff y^2<1+2x$, automatic for $y\le1,x\ge0$.
- $\sqrt{x^2+y^2}<1+y \iff x^2<1+2y$, automatic for $x\le1,y\ge0$.
- $\sqrt{x^2+y^2}<1-x \iff y^2<1-2x$, requiring $x<\tfrac12$.
- $\sqrt{x^2+y^2}<1-y \iff x^2<1-2y$, requiring $y<\tfrac12$.

So in $Q$, $R$ is exactly $\{x\ge0,\,y\ge0,\,y^2<1-2x,\,x^2<1-2y\}$, lying insid
e $[0,\tfrac12]\times[0,\tfrac12]$.

**Boundary curves.** The two binding parabolas are
$$\mathcal{P}_1:\ x=\tfrac{1-y^2}{2}\quad(\text{i.e. }y=\sqrt{1-2x}),\qquad \mat
hcal{P}_2:\ y=\tfrac{1-x^2}{2}.$$
They meet where $x=y$ (by symmetry): $x=\frac{1-x^2}{2}\Rightarrow x^2+2x-1=0\Ri
ghtarrow x=\sqrt2-1=:a$.

The parabola $\mathcal{P}_2$ passes through $(0,\tfrac12)$; $\mathcal{P}_1$ pass
es through $(\tfrac12,0)$. For $0\le x\le a$, $\mathcal{P}_2$ lies below $\mathc
al{P}_1$ (at $x=0$: $\tfrac12<1$), so the upper boundary is $y=\frac{1-x^2}{2}$
and the constraint $y^2<1-2x$ is automatic. For $a\le x\le\tfrac12$, $\mathcal{P
}_1$ lies below $\mathcal{P}_2$, so the upper boundary is $y=\sqrt{1-2x}$.

**Computing the first-quadrant area.** With $a=\sqrt2-1$, note $a^2=3-2\sqrt2$ a
nd $a^3=5\sqrt2-7$.

$$A_Q=\int_0^{a}\frac{1-x^2}{2}\,dx+\int_a^{1/2}\sqrt{1-2x}\,dx.$$

First integral:
$$\int_0^{a}\frac{1-x^2}{2}\,dx=\left[\frac{x}{2}-\frac{x^3}{6}\right]_0^a=\frac
{a}{2}-\frac{a^3}{6}=\frac{3a-a^3}{6}=\frac{3(\sqrt2-1)-(5\sqrt2-7)}{6}=\frac{4-
2\sqrt2}{6}=\frac{2-\sqrt2}{3}.$$

Second integral (substitution $u=1-2x$):
$$\int_a^{1/2}\sqrt{1-2x}\,dx=\left[-\tfrac13(1-2x)^{3/2}\right]_a^{1/2}=0+\tfra
c13(1-2a)^{3/2}=\tfrac13(a^2)^{3/2}=\frac{a^3}{3}=\frac{5\sqrt2-7}{3}.$$

Adding:
$$A_Q=\frac{2-\sqrt2}{3}+\frac{5\sqrt2-7}{3}=\frac{4\sqrt2-5}{3}.$$

**Total area.**
$$\operatorname{Area}(R)=4A_Q=\frac{16\sqrt2-20}{3}.$$
