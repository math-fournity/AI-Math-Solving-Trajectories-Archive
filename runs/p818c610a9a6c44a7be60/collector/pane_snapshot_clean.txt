## Solution

**The curve.** $\gamma(t) = e^{2it}$, $t \in [0,\pi]$, traces the unit circle $|
z|=1$ once counterclockwise (the argument $2t$ runs from $0$ to $2\pi$).

**Analyticity setup.** The integrand is $\frac{\log(z+e)}{z}$. For $|z|\le 1$, w
e have $|z+e|\ge e-1>0$, and $z+e$ lies in the disk of radius $1$ centered at $e
\approx 2.718$, entirely in the right half-plane. Hence $z+e$ never meets the br
anch cut of the principal logarithm (the non-positive real axis), so $\log(z+e)$
 is analytic on a neighborhood of the closed unit disk. The only singularity of
the integrand is the simple pole at $z=0$, which lies inside $\gamma$.

**Taylor expansion (no Cauchy integral formula used).** Since $\log(z+e)$ is ana
lytic for $|z|<e$, we expand about $0$:

$$\log(z+e)=\log e+\log\!\left(1+\tfrac{z}{e}\right)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{
(-1)^{n+1}}{n}\left(\frac{z}{e}\right)^{n},\qquad |z|<e.$$

This series converges uniformly on $|z|=1$ (since $1/e<1$), so we may integrate
term-by-term:

$$\frac{\log(z+e)}{z}=\frac{1}{z}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n\,e^{n}}
\,z^{n-1}.$$

**Term-by-term integration.** Parametrize $\gamma$ by $z=e^{i\theta}$, $\theta\i
n[0,2\pi]$, $dz=ie^{i\theta}\,d\theta$.

- For the principal term: $\displaystyle\int_\gamma \frac{dz}{z}=\int_0^{2\pi}\f
rac{ie^{i\theta}}{e^{i\theta}}\,d\theta=\int_0^{2\pi}i\,d\theta=2\pi i.$

- For $n\ge 1$: $z^{n-1}$ has the global antiderivative $\frac{z^{n}}{n}$ on $\m
athbb{C}\setminus\{0\}$ (indeed on all of $\mathbb{C}$), so
$$\int_\gamma z^{n-1}\,dz=\left[\frac{z^{n}}{n}\right]_{z=1}^{z=e^{2\pi i}=1}=0.
$$

(Equivalently, $\int_0^{2\pi} i\,e^{in\theta}\,d\theta=0$ for every integer $n\g
e 1$.)

**Combining.** Every series term integrates to $0$; only the $\frac{1}{z}$ term
survives:

$$\int_\gamma \frac{\log(z+e)}{z}\,dz = 2\pi i + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{
n+1}}{n\,e^{n}}\cdot 0 = 2\pi i.$$

**Result.**

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_\gamma \frac{\log(z+e)}{z}\,dz = 2\pi i.}$$
