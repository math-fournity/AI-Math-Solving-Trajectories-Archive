Looking at this problem, I need to find the minimum number of moves for a draw,
where both pieces land on A simultaneously, with no player having landed on A in
 any earlier move.

## Setup

Label the 8 fields as positions $0, 1, \ldots, 7$ (mod 8), where $0 = A$. Both p
layers start at position 0.

In each move, one player advances $+2$ and the other $+1$ (clockwise). Let $s_i
\in \{0,1\}$ indicate whether Player 1 gets $+2$ at move $i$ (so Player 2 gets $
+1$), or vice versa. Define partial sums $S_i = \sum_{j=1}^{i} s_j$.

After move $i$:
- Player 1 position: $P_1(i) = i + S_i \pmod{8}$
- Player 2 position: $P_2(i) = 2i - S_i \pmod{8}$

## Necessary condition on the number of moves

The total displacement is $D_1 + D_2 = 3n$. For **both** pieces to be on $A$ at
move $n$: $D_1 \equiv 0 \pmod{8}$ and $D_2 \equiv 0 \pmod{8}$, hence $3n \equiv
0 \pmod{8}$. Since $\gcd(3,8)=1$, we need $n \equiv 0 \pmod{8}$.

So $n \in \{8, 16, 24, \ldots\}$.

## $n = 8$ is impossible

For $n=8$: $D_1 = 8 + k$ where $k$ is the number of $+2$ moves Player 1 gets, $k
 \in \{0,\ldots,8\}$. The condition $D_1 \equiv 0 \pmod 8$ forces $k \in \{0, 8\
}$.

- **$k=0$**: Player 1 always $+1$, Player 2 always $+2$. Positions: $P_1 = 1,2,3
,4,\ldots$ and $P_2 = 2,4,6,0,\ldots$ At **move 4**, $P_2 = 8 \equiv 0$ while $P
_1 = 4 \neq 0$. Player 2 wins — game ends, not a draw.
- **$k=8$**: Player 1 always $+2$, Player 2 always $+1$. At **move 4**, $P_1 = 8
 \equiv 0$ while $P_2 = 4 \neq 0$. Player 1 wins — not a draw.

Both cases fail, so **$n=8$ is impossible**.

## $n = 16$ is achievable

For $n=16$: $D_1 = 16 + k$, need $k \equiv 0 \pmod{8}$, so $k \in \{0, 8, 16\}$.
 Take $k = 8$ (each player gets $+2$ exactly 8 times).

We need a binary sequence $s_1, \ldots, s_{16}$ with $S_{16} = 8$ such that for
