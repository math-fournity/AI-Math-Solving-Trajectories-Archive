**Answer: No.** $X$ need not be a martingale. Here is a counterexample (in discr
ete time, which embeds into the continuous-time framework by piecewise-constant
processes).

**Setup.** Let $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)$ carry a random variable $\xi$ wit
h $\mathbb P(\xi=1)=\mathbb P(\xi=-1)=\tfrac12$. Define the filtration
$$\mathcal F_0=\{\emptyset,\Omega\},\qquad \mathcal F_1=\sigma(\xi),\qquad \math
cal F_2=\sigma(\xi).$$
Define the adapted process
$$X_0=0,\qquad X_1=\xi,\qquad X_2=\xi+1.$$

**$X$ is not a martingale.** Each $X_n$ is integrable and $\mathcal F_n$-measura
ble. We have
$$\mathbb E[X_1\mid\mathcal F_0]=\mathbb E[\xi]=0=X_0,$$
but
$$\mathbb E[X_2\mid\mathcal F_1]=\mathbb E[\xi+1\mid\mathcal F_1]=\xi+1>X_1=\xi\
quad\text{a.s.}$$
So $X$ is a strict submartingale, hence not a martingale.

**Define a bounded predictable integrand.** Set
$$H_1=1\ (\mathcal F_0\text{-measurable}),\qquad H_2=0\ (\mathcal F_1\text{-meas
urable}).$$
Each $H_n$ is $\mathcal F_{n-1}$-measurable, hence predictable, and $|H_n|\le 1$
, so $H$ is bounded.

**The stochastic integral $(H\cdot X)$ is a martingale.** The discrete stochasti
c integral is
$$(H\cdot X)_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n}H_k\,(X_k-X_{k-1}).$$
Concretely:
$$(H\cdot X)_0=0,\qquad (H\cdot X)_1=1\cdot(\xi-0)=\xi,\qquad (H\cdot X)_2=\xi+0
\cdot 1=\xi.$$
Check the martingale property:
$$\mathbb E[(H\cdot X)_1\mid\mathcal F_0]=\mathbb E[\xi]=0=(H\cdot X)_0,$$
$$\mathbb E[(H\cdot X)_2\mid\mathcal F_1]=\mathbb E[\xi\mid\mathcal F_1]=\xi=(H\
cdot X)_1.$$
Thus $(H\cdot X)$ is a martingale.

**Conclusion.** We have exhibited a bounded predictable process $H$ (not even id
entically zero) such that $(H\cdot X)$ is a martingale, while $X$ itself is a st
rict submartingale. The martingale property of the stochastic integral only cons
trains $X$ on the "support" of $H$; the non-martingale behavior of $X$ can be hi
dden at times where $H$ vanishes. Hence $X$ is **not** necessarily a martingale.

(Trivially, $H\equiv 0$ also gives $(H\cdot X)\equiv 0$ a martingale for *any* $
X$; the example above shows the failure is not merely an artifact of the zero in
tegrand.)
