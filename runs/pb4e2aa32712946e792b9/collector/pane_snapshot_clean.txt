## Solution

**Step 1: Find $\angle BEC$ from $\triangle BEC$.**

Applying the Law of Cosines in $\triangle BEC$ with $BE=5$, $EC=7$, $BC=11$:

$$BC^2 = BE^2 + EC^2 - 2\cdot BE\cdot EC\cdot \cos\angle BEC$$

$$121 = 25 + 49 - 70\cos\angle BEC$$

$$\cos\angle BEC = \frac{74-121}{70} = -\frac{47}{70}$$

**Step 2: Relate $\angle AEB$ to $\angle BEC$.**

Since $A,E,C$ are collinear, $\angle AEB$ and $\angle BEC$ are supplementary:

$$\cos\angle AEB = -\cos\angle BEC = \frac{47}{70}$$

**Step 3: Solve for $AE$ in $\triangle ABE$.**

With $AB=5$, $BE=5$, and $\cos\angle AEB = \frac{47}{70}$, the Law of Cosines gi

$$AB^2 = AE^2 + BE^2 - 2\cdot AE\cdot BE\cdot \cos\angle AEB$$

$$25 = AE^2 + 25 - 2\cdot AE\cdot 5\cdot\frac{47}{70}$$

$$0 = AE^2 - \frac{47}{7}\,AE$$

$$AE\!\left(AE - \frac{47}{7}\right) = 0$$

Since $AE>0$, we obtain $AE = \dfrac{47}{7}$.

$$CD^2 = CE^2 + ED^2 - 2\cdot CE\cdot ED\cdot\cos\angle CED$$

$$ED\!\left(ED - \frac{47}{5}\right)=0 \implies ED = \frac{47}{5} > 0$$

This is a valid positive length, confirming the configuration is consistent.
