Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether the equality $\int_a^b |F'(
x)|\,dx = T_F(a,b)$ for a BV function $F$ forces $F$ to be absolutely continuous
.

**Answer: Yes, the equality implies $F$ is absolutely continuous.**

## Proof

**Setup and key decomposition.** Let $F$ be of bounded variation on $[a,b]$. Ass
ociated to $F$ is a finite signed Borel measure $\mu_F$ (the *variation measure*
 or *Lebesgue–Stieltjes measure*) satisfying $\mu_F((s,t]) = F(t) - F(s)$, and
$$T_F(a,b) = |\mu_F|([a,b]),$$
the total variation of the measure on $[a,b]$.

By the Lebesgue decomposition theorem, $\mu_F$ splits uniquely as
$$\mu_F = \mu_{\mathrm{ac}} + \mu_{\mathrm{sc}} + \mu_{\mathrm{j}},$$
where:
- $\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}$ is absolutely continuous w.r.t. Lebesgue measure: $\mu_{\m
athrm{ac}} = F'(x)\,dx$ (the density is the a.e. derivative),
- $\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}$ is singular continuous (singular w.r.t. Lebesgue measure,
no atoms),
- $\mu_{\mathrm{j}}$ is a pure point (atomic) measure concentrated on the (at mo
st countable) jump set of $F$.

These three components are mutually singular, so total variation is additive:
$$T_F(a,b) = |\mu_F|([a,b]) = |\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}|([a,b]) + |\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}|([
a,b]) + |\mu_{\mathrm{j}}|([a,b]). \tag{1}$$

Correspondingly $F = F_{\mathrm{ac}} + F_{\mathrm{sc}} + F_{\mathrm{j}}$, where
each part is the distribution function of the respective measure component.

**Computing the integral.** Since $F_{\mathrm{sc}}$ and $F_{\mathrm{j}}$ are sin
gular with respect to Lebesgue measure, their derivatives vanish a.e. (Lebesgue'
s theorem). Hence $F' = F_{\mathrm{ac}}'$ a.e., and
$$\int_a^b |F'(x)|\,dx = \int_a^b |F_{\mathrm{ac}}'(x)|\,dx = |\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}
|([a,b]). \tag{2}$$
The last equality uses that $F_{\mathrm{ac}}$ is absolutely continuous, so its v
ariation measure is exactly $|F_{\mathrm{ac}}'|dx$.

**Applying the hypothesis.** The given condition $\int_a^b |F'(x)|\,dx = T_F(a,b
)$, combined with (1) and (2), yields
$$|\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}|([a,b]) = |\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}|([a,b]) + |\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}|(
[a,b]) + |\mu_{\mathrm{j}}|([a,b]),$$
hence
$$|\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}|([a,b]) + |\mu_{\mathrm{j}}|([a,b]) = 0.$$

Since both terms are non-negative measures of $[a,b]$, each must be zero:
$$|\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}|([a,b]) = 0 \quad\text{and}\quad |\mu_{\mathrm{j}}|([a,b])
= 0.$$

Thus $\mu_{\mathrm{sc}} = 0$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{j}} = 0$ on $[a,b]$, so $\mu_F =
\mu_{\mathrm{ac}} = F'(x)\,dx$. This means $F$ is (up to an additive constant) a
n indefinite Lebesgue integral of an $L^1$ function, which is precisely the defi
nition of absolute continuity on $[a,b]$.

**Conclusion.** The equality $\int_a^b |F'(x)|\,dx = T_F(a,b)$ forces the singul
ar continuous and jump parts of $F$ to vanish, leaving $F$ absolutely continuous
. $\blacksquare$
