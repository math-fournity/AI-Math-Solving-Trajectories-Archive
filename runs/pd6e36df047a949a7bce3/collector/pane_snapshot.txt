## Proof

Let $a = T_1$. From the relation $T_n T_{n+1} = n$ for $n \geq 1$, we derive a r
ecurrence connecting terms two apart. Since $T_{n-1}T_n = n-1$ and $T_n T_{n+1}
= n$, dividing gives

$$\frac{T_{n+1}}{T_{n-1}} = \frac{n}{n-1}, \quad n \geq 2.$$

This decouples into two independent recurrences for odd- and even-indexed terms.

**Odd-indexed terms.** Starting from $T_1 = a$:

$$T_{2k+1} = a \prod_{j=1}^{k} \frac{2j}{2j-1} = a \cdot \frac{(2k)!!}{(2k-1)!!}
.$$

**Even-indexed terms.** From $T_1 T_2 = 1$ we get $T_2 = 1/a$, and then:

$$T_{2k} = \frac{1}{a}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{2j+1}{2j} = \frac{1}{a}\cdot\frac{(
2k-1)!!}{(2k-2)!!}.$$

**Key asymptotic (Wallis product).** The Wallis product states

$$\prod_{n=1}^{N}\frac{2n}{2n-1}\cdot\frac{2n}{2n+1} \;\longrightarrow\; \frac{\
pi}{2},$$

which rewrites as

$$\frac{\bigl((2N)!!\bigr)^2}{\bigl((2N-1)!!\bigr)^2\,(2N+1)} \;\longrightarrow\
; \frac{\pi}{2}.$$

Therefore

$$\frac{(2N)!!}{(2N-1)!!} \;\sim\; \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}\,(2N+1)} \;\sim\; \sqrt{\
pi\, N} \quad\text{as } N\to\infty. \tag{$\star$}$$

**Asymptotics of $T_{2k+1}$ and $T_{2k}$.** From $(\star)$:

- $T_{2k+1} = a\,\dfrac{(2k)!!}{(2k-1)!!} \;\sim\; a\sqrt{\pi\, k}.$

- For even terms, note $(2k)!! = 2k\cdot(2k-2)!!$, so $\dfrac{(2k-1)!!}{(2k-2)!!
} = \dfrac{2k}{(2k)!!/(2k-1)!!} \;\sim\; \dfrac{2k}{\sqrt{\pi k}} = \dfrac{2\sqr
t{k}}{\sqrt{\pi}}.$ Hence $T_{2k} \;\sim\; \dfrac{1}{a}\cdot\dfrac{2\sqrt{k}}{\s
qrt{\pi}}.$

**Applying the limit condition.** The hypothesis $\lim_{n\to\infty} T_n/T_{n+1}
= 1$ forces every subsequence of $\{T_n/T_{n+1}\}$ to converge to $1$. Consider
the subsequence with even $n = 2k$:

$$\frac{T_{2k}}{T_{2k+1}} \;\sim\; \frac{\dfrac{2\sqrt{k}}{a\sqrt{\pi}}}{a\sqrt{
\pi k}} = \frac{2}{a^2\,\pi}.$$

Setting this equal to $1$:

$$\frac{2}{a^2\,\pi} = 1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a^2\,\pi = 2.$$

**Consistency check (odd $n$).** For the subsequence with odd $n = 2k+1$, we nee
d $T_{2k+2}$. Since $(2k+1)!! = (2k+1)\cdot(2k-1)!!$ and $(2k)!! = 2k\cdot(2k-2)
!!$:

$$\frac{(2k+1)!!}{(2k)!!} = \frac{2k+1}{(2k)!!/(2k-1)!!} \;\sim\; \frac{2k}{\sqr
t{\pi k}} = \frac{2\sqrt{k}}{\sqrt{\pi}},$$

so $T_{2k+2} \sim \dfrac{2\sqrt{k}}{a\sqrt{\pi}}$, and

$$\frac{T_{2k+1}}{T_{2k+2}} \;\sim\; \frac{a\sqrt{\pi k}}{2\sqrt{k}/(a\sqrt{\pi}
)} = \frac{a^2\,\pi}{2}.$$

Setting this equal to $1$ gives $a^2\pi = 2$ — the same condition, confirming co
nsistency.

**Conclusion.** Both subsequences yield the identical constraint $a^2\pi = 2$. R
ecalling $a = T_1$:

$$\boxed{\pi\, T_1^2 = 2.} \qquad \blacksquare$$
