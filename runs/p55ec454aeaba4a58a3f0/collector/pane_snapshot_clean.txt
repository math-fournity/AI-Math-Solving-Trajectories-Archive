## Proof of Erdős 272

**Claim:** $\text{maxArithInterCard}(N) \sim \dfrac{N^2}{2}$ as $N \to \infty$.

That is, the answer function is $N \mapsto N^2/2$.

---

### Lower Bound Construction

For $N \geq 3$, define the family
$$\mathcal{F} = \bigl\{ A_{i,j} = \{1,\, i,\, j\} : 2 \leq i < j \leq N \bigr\}.
$$

This family contains $\binom{N-1}{2} = \frac{(N-1)(N-2)}{2}$ sets, each a subset
 of $\{1, \ldots, N\}$.

**Pairwise intersection property.** Take any two distinct sets $A_{i,j}, A_{i',j
'} \in \mathcal{F}$ with $(i,j) \neq (i',j')$. Since both contain the element $1
$:
$$A_{i,j} \cap A_{i',j'} = \{1\} \cup \bigl(\{i,j\} \cap \{i',j'\}\bigr).$$

- **Case 1:** $\{i,j\} \cap \{i',j'\} = \emptyset$. Then $A_{i,j} \cap A_{i',j'}
 = \{1\}$, which is an arithmetic progression of length $1$ (a singleton is triv
ially an AP).

- **Case 2:** $|\{i,j\} \cap \{i',j'\}| = 1$, say the common element is $x$. The
n $A_{i,j} \cap A_{i',j'} = \{1, x\}$, which is an arithmetic progression of len
gth $2$ (any two-element set $\{a, b\}$ is an AP with first term $a$, common dif
ference $b-a$).

In both cases the intersection is a **non-empty arithmetic progression**. (Case
$|\{i,j\} \cap \{i',j'\}| = 2$ would mean $(i,j) = (i',j')$, which is excluded s
ince the sets are distinct.)

Therefore $\mathcal{F}$ is a valid arithmetic intersection set, giving:
$$\text{maxArithInterCard}(N) \geq \binom{N-1}{2} = \frac{N^2}{2} - \frac{3N}{2}
 + 1. \tag{LB}$$

---

### Upper Bound (Szabó's Theorem)

Szabó proved (as stated in the variant `erdos_272.variants.szabo`, marked `resea
rch solved`):
$$\text{maxArithInterCard}(N) - \frac{N^2}{2} = O\!\left(N^{5/3} \log^3 N\right)
.$$

That is, there exists a constant $C > 0$ such that for all sufficiently large $N
$:
$$\text{maxArithInterCard}(N) \leq \frac{N^2}{2} + C \cdot N^{5/3}\log^3 N. \tag
{UB}$$

---

### Asymptotic Equivalence

Combining (LB) and (UB), for sufficiently large $N$:
$$\frac{N^2}{2} - \frac{3N}{2} + 1 \;\leq\; \text{maxArithInterCard}(N) \;\leq\;
 \frac{N^2}{2} + C \cdot N^{5/3}\log^3 N.$$

Dividing throughout by $N^2/2$:

$$\underbrace{1 - \frac{3}{N} + \frac{2}{N^2}}_{\to\, 1} \;\leq\; \frac{\text{ma
xArithInterCard}(N)}{N^2/2} \;\leq\; \underbrace{1 + 2C \cdot \frac{N^{5/3}\log^
3 N}{N^2}}_{= \; 1 + 2C \cdot N^{-1/3}\log^3 N \;\to\; 1}.$$

The upper bound ratio tends to $1$ because $N^{-1/3}\log^3 N \to 0$ as $N \to \i
nfty$ (polylogarithmic growth is dominated by any positive power of $N$). The lo
wer bound ratio clearly tends to $1$.

By the squeeze theorem:
$$\frac{\text{maxArithInterCard}(N)}{N^2/2} \longrightarrow 1 \quad \text{as } N
 \to \infty.$$

Therefore:
$$\boxed{\text{maxArithInterCard}(N) \sim \frac{N^2}{2}}$$

The answer function is $N \mapsto N^2/2$.

---

**Remark on the lower bound.** The construction uses 3-element sets $\{1, i, j\}
$ sharing a common "hub" element $1$. The key observation is that the intersecti
on of any two such sets is either a singleton $\{1\}$ (AP of length 1) or a pair
 $\{1, x\}$ (AP of length 2), both of which are non-empty arithmetic progression
s. This yields $\binom{N-1}{2} \sim N^2/2$ sets, matching the leading term of Sz
abó's upper bound. The residual gap of $O(N^{5/3}\log^3 N)$ between this constru
ction and the true maximum is the subject of Szabó's open conjecture (`szabo_str
ong`) that the error is $O(N)$.
