## Solution

We determine the values of $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ for which
$$f(x,y)=\frac{e^{-x}\sin x}{(1+xy)^{\alpha}}$$
is (Lebesgue) integrable over $(0,+\infty)\times(0,+\infty)$, i.e. $\iint |f|\,d
x\,dy<\infty$.

---

### Step 1. Reduce to a one-dimensional integral via Tonelli

Since $|f|\ge 0$, Tonelli's theorem gives
$$\iint |f|=\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-x}|\sin x|\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{(1+
xy)^{\alpha}}\right)dx.$$

**Inner integral.** Substituting $u=xy$ ($dy=du/x$):
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{(1+xy)^{\alpha}}=\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{
du}{(1+u)^{\alpha}}.$$
The integral $\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+u)^{-\alpha}du$ converges **iff $\alpha>1$**,
in which case it equals $\frac{1}{\alpha-1}$.

---

### Step 2. Case $\alpha\le 1$: not integrable

For $\alpha\le 1$, the inner integral $=+\infty$ for every $x$ with $|\sin x|>0$
, i.e. for a.e. $x>0$. Since $e^{-x}|\sin x|>0$ on a set of positive measure, To
nelli yields $\iint|f|=+\infty$. Hence $f\notin L^{1}$.

---

### Step 3. Case $\alpha>1$: integrable

For $\alpha>1$ the inner integral equals $\frac{1}{x(\alpha-1)}$, so
$$\iint|f|=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-x}|\sin x|}{x}\,dx.$$

We check convergence of the remaining integral:
- **Near $x=0$:** $|\sin x|/x\to 1$ and $e^{-x}\to 1$, so the integrand $\sim 1$
; integrable on $(0,1)$.
- **Near $x=\infty$:** $|\sin x|/x\le 1/x$ is not enough, but $e^{-x}|\sin x|/x\
le e^{-x}$, and $\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-x}dx<\infty$.

Thus $\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-x}|\sin x|}{x}\,dx<\infty$, and so $\iint|f|<\i
nfty$.

(As a consistency check, the iterated integral then evaluates to
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}f\,dy\,dx=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{\inf
ty}\frac{e^{-x}\sin x}{x}\,dx=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\cdot\arctan(1)=\frac{\pi}{4(\al
pha-1)},$$
using $\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-ax}\sin x}{x}dx=\arctan(1/a)$ at $a=1$.)

---

### Conclusion

$f$ is integrable over $(0,+\infty)\times(0,+\infty)$ if and only if
$$\boxed{\alpha>1.}$$
