We evaluate $\displaystyle \int_{\gamma} \frac{1}{z^2+i}\,dz$, where $\gamma$ is
 the circle $|z|=2$ traversed once (positively).

**Step 1: Locate the singularities.**

The integrand $f(z)=\frac{1}{z^2+i}$ has singularities where $z^2+i=0$, i.e. $z^
2=-i$. Writing $-i=e^{-i\pi/2}$, the two square roots are

$$z_1 = e^{-i\pi/4}, \qquad z_2 = e^{i3\pi/4}.$$

Both satisfy $|z_1|=|z_2|=1<2$, so **both poles lie inside $\gamma$**. They are
simple poles since $g(z)=z^2+i$ has $g'(z)=2z\neq 0$ at each root.

**Step 2: Compute the residues.**

For a simple pole of $1/g(z)$, the residue is $1/g'(z_0)$. Here $g'(z)=2z$, so

$$\operatorname{Res}(f,z_1)=\frac{1}{2z_1}, \qquad \operatorname{Res}(f,z_2)=\fr
ac{1}{2z_2}.$$

**Step 3: Sum the residues.**

Observe that $z_2 = e^{i3\pi/4} = e^{-i\pi/4}\cdot e^{i\pi} = -z_1$. Hence $z_2=
-z_1$, and

$$\operatorname{Res}(f,z_1)+\operatorname{Res}(f,z_2)=\frac{1}{2z_1}+\frac{1}{2z
_2}=\frac{1}{2z_1}+\frac{1}{-2z_1}=\frac{1}{2z_1}-\frac{1}{2z_1}=0.$$

**Step 4: Apply the residue theorem.**

$$\int_{\gamma} \frac{1}{z^2+i}\,dz = 2\pi i\bigl(\operatorname{Res}(f,z_1)+\ope
ratorname{Res}(f,z_2)\bigr)=2\pi i\cdot 0=0.$$

**Verification (partial fractions).** Since $z^2+i=(z-z_1)(z-z_2)$,

$$\frac{1}{z^2+i}=\frac{1}{z_1-z_2}\left(\frac{1}{z-z_1}-\frac{1}{z-z_2}\right).
$$

Both poles are enclosed by $\gamma$, so each term contributes $2\pi i$, and they
 cancel:

$$\int_{\gamma}\frac{1}{z^2+i}\,dz=\frac{2\pi i}{z_1-z_2}(1-1)=0.$$

This cancellation reflects the general fact that for $\int_{|z|=R}\frac{dz}{P(z)
}$ with $\deg P\ge 2$ and all roots inside the contour, the sum of residues is $
0$ (the residue at $\infty$ vanishes).

---

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \int_{\gamma} \frac{1}{z^2+i}\,dz = 0}$$
