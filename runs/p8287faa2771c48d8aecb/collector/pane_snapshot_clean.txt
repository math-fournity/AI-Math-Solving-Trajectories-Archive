## Solution

We evaluate the integral (in the principal-value sense, since the integrand has
a singularity at $u=0$):

$$I = \operatorname{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\cosh 4u}{\sinh 5u}\,du.$
$

**Convergence check.** As $u\to+\infty$, $\cosh 4u \sim \tfrac{1}{2}e^{4u}$ and
$\sinh 5u\sim\tfrac{1}{2}e^{5u}$, so the integrand $\sim e^{-u}\to 0$ exponentia
lly. As $u\to-\infty$, $\cosh 4u\sim\tfrac12 e^{-4u}$ and $\sinh 5u\sim -\tfrac1
2 e^{-5u}$, so the integrand $\sim -e^{u}\to 0$ exponentially. Near $u=0$, $\sin
h 5u\approx 5u$ and $\cosh 4u\approx 1$, so the integrand $\approx \frac{1}{5u}$
, a simple pole — the principal value exists.

**Substitution $u\mapsto -u$.** Define

$$I = \lim_{\substack{\varepsilon\to 0^+\\ R\to\infty}}\left(\int_{-R}^{-\vareps
ilon} \frac{\cosh 4u}{\sinh 5u}\,du + \int_{\varepsilon}^{R}\frac{\cosh 4u}{\sin
h 5u}\,du\right).$$

In the first integral, substitute $u=-v$, $du=-dv$ (limits $u=-R\Rightarrow v=R$
, $u=-\varepsilon\Rightarrow v=\varepsilon$):

$$\int_{-R}^{-\varepsilon}\frac{\cosh 4u}{\sinh 5u}\,du = \int_{R}^{\varepsilon}
\frac{\cosh(-4v)}{\sinh(-5v)}(-dv) = \int_{\varepsilon}^{R}\frac{\cosh 4v}{-\sin
h 5v}\,dv = -\int_{\varepsilon}^{R}\frac{\cosh 4v}{\sinh 5v}\,dv,$$

using $\cosh(-x)=\cosh x$ (even) and $\sinh(-x)=-\sinh x$ (odd). Therefore

$$I = -\int_{\varepsilon}^{R}\frac{\cosh 4u}{\sinh 5u}\,du + \int_{\varepsilon}^
{R}\frac{\cosh 4u}{\sinh 5u}\,du = 0.$$

The two pieces cancel for every $\varepsilon,R$, so the limit is $0$.

$$\boxed{\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cosh 4u}{\sinh 5u}\,du = 0.
}$$
