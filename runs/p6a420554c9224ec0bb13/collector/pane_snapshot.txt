## Solution

Let $V_1,\dots,V_n$ be i.i.d. $\mathrm{Uniform}[-2,2]$. Define
$$S_n=\sum_{i=1}^n V_i,\qquad Q_n=\sum_{i=1}^n V_i^2,\qquad Y=\frac{S_n}{\sqrt{Q
_n}}.$$

**Step 1: Moments of $V_i$.** For $V\sim\mathrm{Uniform}[-2,2]$,
$$\mu=\mathbb E[V]=0,\qquad \sigma^2=\operatorname{Var}(V)=\frac{(2-(-2))^2}{12}
=\frac{16}{12}=\frac{4}{3},\qquad \mathbb E[V^2]=\sigma^2+\mu^2=\frac{4}{3}.$$

**Step 2: Limit behavior of numerator and denominator.**

*Numerator.* By the CLT,
$$\frac{S_n}{\sqrt{n}\,\sigma}=\frac{S_n}{\sqrt{4n/3}}\;\xrightarrow{d}\; Z,\qqu
ad Z\sim N(0,1).$$
Equivalently $S_n = \sqrt{4n/3}\;Z + o_p(\sqrt n)$, i.e. $S_n/\sqrt n \xrightarr
ow{d} \sqrt{4/3}\,Z$.

*Denominator.* By the LLN,
$$\frac{Q_n}{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n V_i^2 \xrightarrow{p} \mathbb E[V^2]=\fr
ac{4}{3}.$$
Hence $Q_n = \frac{4n}{3}\bigl(1+o_p(1)\bigr)$ and
$$\sqrt{Q_n}=\sqrt{\frac{4n}{3}}\;\sqrt{1+o_p(1)}=\sqrt{\frac{4n}{3}}\;\bigl(1+o
_p(1)\bigr).$$

**Step 3: Limit distribution of $Y$.** Using Slutsky's theorem,
$$Y=\frac{S_n}{\sqrt{Q_n}}=\frac{\sqrt{4n/3}\;Z+o_p(\sqrt n)}{\sqrt{4n/3}\,(1+o_
p(1))}=\frac{Z+o_p(1)}{1+o_p(1)}\xrightarrow{d} Z\sim N(0,1).$$

So for large $n$, $Y$ is approximately standard normal.

**Step 4: The desired probability.** Since $0.5$ is a continuity point of $Z$,
$$\mathbb P(Y>0.5)\;\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\;\mathbb P(Z>0.5)=1-\Phi(0.5).$$

Using $\Phi(0.5)\approx 0.6915$,
$$\boxed{\mathbb P(Y>0.5)\;\approx\;1-\Phi(0.5)\;\approx\;0.3085.}$$

(For large $n$ the approximation error is $O(n^{-1/2})$, coming from the $o_p(1)
$ fluctuation of $Q_n/n$ around $4/3$.)
