**Example.** Let $p$ be a prime, and consider the field of rational functions $K
 = \mathbb{F}_p(t)$ over the finite field $\mathbb{F}_p$. This is an infinite fi
eld of characteristic $p$.

**Claim.** The Frobenius endomorphism $\varphi: K \to K$, $\varphi(x) = x^p$, is
 not surjective.

**Proof.** Since $K$ has characteristic $p$, the map $x \mapsto x^p$ is an endom
orphism (the Frobenius). Its image consists of all $p$-th powers of elements of
$K$.

Every element of $K = \mathbb{F}_p(t)$ can be written as $f(t)/g(t)$ with $f, g
\in \mathbb{F}_p[t]$, $g \neq 0$. Its $p$-th power is
$$\left(\frac{f(t)}{g(t)}\right)^p = \frac{f(t)^p}{g(t)^p}.$$

Because $\mathbb{F}_p$ is fixed by Frobenius (every $a \in \mathbb{F}_p$ satisfi
es $a^p = a$), and using the fact that $(t^n)^p = t^{pn}$, we get
$$f(t)^p = f(t^p), \qquad g(t)^p = g(t^p).$$

Hence every element in the image of Frobenius has the form $f(t^p)/g(t^p)$, i.e.
 it lies in the subfield $\mathbb{F}_p(t^p) \subseteq K$.

Now consider the element $t \in K$. Suppose for contradiction that $t = h^p$ for
 some $h \in K$. Write $h = f(t)/g(t)$ in lowest terms with $f, g \in \mathbb{F}
_p[t]$, $\gcd(f,g)=1$. Then
$$t = \frac{f(t)^p}{g(t)^p} = \frac{f(t^p)}{g(t^p)},$$
so $t \cdot g(t^p) = f(t^p)$.

The left-hand side $t \cdot g(t^p)$ has a nonzero term of degree $\equiv 1 \pmod
{p}$ (since $g \neq 0$, the leading term of $g(t^p)$ has degree divisible by $p$
, and multiplying by $t$ shifts it to degree $\equiv 1 \pmod p$). The right-hand
 side $f(t^p)$ has all terms of degree divisible by $p$. This is a contradiction
: a polynomial all of whose nonzero terms have degrees $\equiv 0 \pmod{p}$ canno
t equal a polynomial with a nonzero term of degree $\equiv 1 \pmod{p}$.

Therefore $t$ is not a $p$-th power in $K$, so $t \notin \operatorname{im}(\varp
hi)$, and the Frobenius endomorphism is not surjective. $\blacksquare$
