& 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, the Fibonacci matrix, so $M^n = \begin{pmatrix} F_{
n+1} & F_n \\ F_n & F_{n-1} \end{pmatrix}$ where $F_0=0, F_1=1, F_2=1, \ldots$ T

$$F_n = \frac{F_{n+1}\, a_n + F_n}{F_n\, a_n + F_{n-1}}.$$

**Translating the congruence.** The condition $a_0 \equiv F_n \pmod{p}$ means $a

$$a_n\,(a_0 F_n - F_{n+1}) \equiv F_n - a_0 F_{n-1} \pmod{p}. \quad (\ast)$$

## Criterion for Stability

Prime $p$ is **stable** iff there exists $a_0 \in \mathbb{F}_p$ avoiding $a_0 \e
quiv F_{n+1}/F_n \pmod{p}$ for all $n \geq 1$ with $F_n \not\equiv 0$.

The ratios $r_n = F_{n+1}/F_n$ satisfy $r_n = T(r_{n-1})$ with $r_1 = 1$, i.e.,
$r_n = T^n(\infty)$ in $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$. The orbit of $\infty$ under
 $T$ has size $\alpha(p)$, the **rank of apparition** (smallest $k > 0$ with $p
\mid F_k$), since $T^k(\infty) = \infty$ iff $F_k \equiv 0$.

The orbit $\{\infty, r_1, r_2, \ldots, r_{\alpha(p)-1}\}$ has $\alpha(p)$ elemen
ts, with $\alpha(p) - 1$ finite points. Since $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$ has $
p+1$ points, we have $\alpha(p) \leq p+1$.

**$p$ is NOT stable** $\iff$ the $\alpha(p)-1$ finite ratios cover all of $\math
bb{F}_p$ $\iff$ $\alpha(p) - 1 = p$ $\iff$ $\boxed{\alpha(p) = p+1}$.
