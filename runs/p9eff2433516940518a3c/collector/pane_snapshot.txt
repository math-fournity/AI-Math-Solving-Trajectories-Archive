## Proof

**Claim.** $Id \otimes \phi: B(H)\,\overline{\otimes}\, M \to B(H)\,\overline{\o
times}\, M$ is completely bounded, and
$$\|Id \otimes \phi\|_{cb} = \|\phi\|_{cb}.$$

We establish the two inequalities separately.

---

### Upper bound: $\|Id \otimes \phi\|_{cb} \leq \|\phi\|_{cb}$

We use the standard functoriality of the minimal (spatial) tensor product for co
mpletely bounded maps (see Effros–Ruan, *Operator Spaces*, or Pisier, *Introduct
ion to Operator Space Theory*): if $S: E_1 \to E_2$ and $T: F_1 \to F_2$ are com
pletely bounded maps between operator spaces, then $S \otimes T: E_1 \,\widehat{
\otimes}_{\min}\, F_1 \to E_2\,\widehat{\otimes}_{\min}\, F_2$ is completely bou
nded with
$$\|S \otimes T\|_{cb} \leq \|S\|_{cb}\,\|T\|_{cb}.$$

Applying this with $S = Id_{B(H)}$ and $T = \phi$, and noting that $\|Id_{B(H)}\
|_{cb} = 1$ (the identity on any operator space has cb-norm 1), we obtain
$$\|Id_{B(H)} \otimes \phi\|_{cb} \leq \|Id_{B(H)}\|_{cb}\,\|\phi\|_{cb} = \|\ph
i\|_{cb}.$$

---

### Lower bound: $\|Id \otimes \phi\|_{cb} \geq \|\phi\|_{cb}$

Recall that the cb-norm is computed via amplifications:
$$\|Id_{B(H)} \otimes \phi\|_{cb} = \sup_{n \geq 1} \big\|(Id_{B(H)} \otimes \ph
i)^{(n)}\big\|,$$
where $(Id_{B(H)} \otimes \phi)^{(n)}: M_n\big(B(H)\,\overline{\otimes}\, M\big)
 \to M_n\big(B(H)\,\overline{\otimes}\, M\big)$.

Using the natural completely isometric identification $M_n\big(B(H)\,\overline{\
otimes}\, M\big) \cong B(\mathbb{C}^n \otimes H)\,\overline{\otimes}\, M$, the $
n$-th amplification becomes
$$(Id_{B(H)} \otimes \phi)^{(n)} = Id_{B(\mathbb{C}^n \otimes H)} \otimes \,\phi
.$$

Set $k = \dim H \in \{1, 2, \dots\} \cup \{\infty\}$. Then $\dim(\mathbb{C}^n \o
times H) = nk$ (with $n \cdot \infty = \infty$).

We must show $\sup_n \|Id_{B(\mathbb{C}^n \otimes H)} \otimes \phi\| \geq \|\phi
\|_{cb} = \sup_{m \geq 1} \|Id_m \otimes \phi\|$.

**Case 1: $H$ is infinite-dimensional ($k = \infty$).** Take $n = 1$. Then $B(\m
athbb{C}^1 \otimes H) = B(H)$, which is infinite-dimensional. For each $m \geq 1
$, since $H$ is infinite-dimensional, we can find $m$ orthonormal vectors $e_1,
\dots, e_m \in H$ and the corresponding matrix units $e_{ij}$, giving a complete
 isometry $\iota: M_m \hookrightarrow B(H)$ as a unital $*$-subalgebra (corner).
 The map $Id_{B(H)} \otimes \phi$ restricts to $Id_m \otimes \phi$ on $M_m \,\ov
erline{\otimes}\, M \subseteq B(H)\,\overline{\otimes}\, M$ (via $\iota \otimes
Id_M$). Since restriction cannot increase the norm,
$$\|Id_{B(H)} \otimes \phi\| \geq \|Id_m \otimes \phi\| \quad \text{for all } m
\geq 1.$$
Taking the supremum over $m$:
$$\|Id_{B(H)} \otimes \phi\| \geq \|\phi\|_{cb}.$$
Since $\|Id_{B(H)} \otimes \phi\|_{cb} \geq \|Id_{B(H)} \otimes \phi\|$, we are
done.

**Case 2: $H$ is finite-dimensional ($k = \dim H < \infty$).** For any $m \geq 1
$, choose $n$ large enough that $nk \geq m$ (e.g., $n = \lceil m/k \rceil$). The
n $B(\mathbb{C}^n \otimes H) \cong M_{nk}$, and since $nk \geq m$, we have a uni
tal complete isometry $M_m \hookrightarrow M_{nk}$ (embedding as an upper-left c
orner). As in Case 1, $Id_{M_{nk}} \otimes \phi$ restricts to $Id_m \otimes \phi
$, so
$$\|Id_{M_{nk}} \otimes \phi\| \geq \|Id_m \otimes \phi\|.$$
Therefore
$$\sup_n \|Id_{B(\mathbb{C}^n \otimes H)} \otimes \phi\| = \sup_n \|Id_{M_{nk}}
\otimes \phi\| \geq \sup_{m} \|Id_m \otimes \phi\| = \|\phi\|_{cb}.$$

In both cases, $\|Id_{B(H)} \otimes \phi\|_{cb} \geq \|\phi\|_{cb}$.

### Conclusion

Combining the upper and lower bounds:
$$\boxed{\|Id \otimes \phi\|_{cb} = \|\phi\|_{cb}.}$$

In particular, $Id \otimes \phi$ is completely bounded (with the same cb-norm as
 $\phi$).

**Remark.** The result holds for any Hilbert space $H$ (finite or infinite dimen
sional). The upper bound relies on the functoriality and submultiplicativity of
the minimal tensor product for cb maps; the lower bound relies on the fact that
sufficiently large matrix algebras embed completely isometrically into $B(\mathb
b{C}^n \otimes H)$ for appropriate $n$, so that the amplifications of $Id \otime
s \phi$ capture all matrix amplifications of $\phi$.
