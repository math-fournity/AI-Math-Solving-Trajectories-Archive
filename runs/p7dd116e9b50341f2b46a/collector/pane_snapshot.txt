## Construction

Let $q \colon X \to X/R$ be the quotient map $q(x) = [x]_R$, and let $p \colon X
/R \to (X/R)/S$ be the quotient map $p([x]_R) = [[x]_R]_S$.

**Define** $T$ on $X$ by
$$x \mathrel{T} y \iff q(x) \mathrel{S} q(y), \quad \text{i.e.} \quad x \mathrel
{T} y \iff [x]_R \mathrel{S} [y]_R.$$
Equivalently, $T = (q \times q)^{-1}(S) \subseteq X \times X$ — the **pullback o
f $S$ along $q$**.

## $T$ is an equivalence relation

- **Reflexivity:** $[x]_R \mathrel{S} [x]_R$ since $S$ is reflexive, so $x \math
rel{T} x$.
- **Symmetry:** If $x \mathrel{T} y$, then $[x]_R \mathrel{S} [y]_R$, hence $[y]
_R \mathrel{S} [x]_R$ (symmetry of $S$), giving $y \mathrel{T} x$.
- **Transitivity:** If $x \mathrel{T} y$ and $y \mathrel{T} z$, then $[x]_R \mat
hrel{S} [y]_R$ and $[y]_R \mathrel{S} [z]_R$, so $[x]_R \mathrel{S} [z]_R$ (tran
sitivity of $S$), giving $x \mathrel{T} z$.

## $R$ refines $T$

If $x \mathrel{R} y$, then $q(x) = q(y) = [x]_R$, and reflexivity of $S$ gives $
[x]_R \mathrel{S} [y]_R$, so $x \mathrel{T} y$. Hence $R \subseteq T$, and every
 $T$-class is a union of $R$-classes. This guarantees the map below is well-defi
ned.

## The bijection $X/T \;\cong\; (X/R)/S$

Define
$$\varphi \colon X/T \longrightarrow (X/R)/S, \qquad \varphi\bigl([x]_T\bigr) =
p\bigl(q(x)\bigr) = \bigl[[x]_R\bigr]_S.$$

**Well-defined.** If $[x]_T = [y]_T$, then $x \mathrel{T} y$, so $[x]_R \mathrel
{S} [y]_R$, i.e. $q(x) \mathrel{S} q(y)$. Thus $p(q(x)) = p(q(y))$, and $\varphi
$ is independent of the representative.

**Injective.** If $\varphi([x]_T) = \varphi([y]_T)$, then $p(q(x)) = p(q(y))$, s
o $q(x) \mathrel{S} q(y)$, i.e. $[x]_R \mathrel{S} [y]_R$, which is exactly $x \
mathrel{T} y$. Hence $[x]_T = [y]_T$.

**Surjective.** Every element of $(X/R)/S$ is of the form $[[x]_R]_S$ for some $
[x]_R \in X/R$ (since $p$ is surjective), and $[x]_R = q(x)$ for some $x \in X$
(since $q$ is surjective). Thus every element equals $\varphi([x]_T)$ for some $
x$.

Therefore $\varphi$ is a bijection, and the equivalence classes of $(X/R)/S$ are
 in bijection with the equivalence classes of $X/T$.

## Summary

The desired relation is the **pullback of $S$ through the quotient map $q \colon
 X \to X/R$**:
$$\boxed{\;x \mathrel{T} y \iff [x]_R \mathrel{S} [y]_R.\;}$$
It is the coarsest equivalence relation on $X$ that makes $q$ a map of equivalen
ce relations $(X,T) \to (X/R, S)$, and the canonical factorization $X \xrightarr
ow{q} X/R \xrightarrow{p} (X/R)/S$ descends to the isomorphism $X/T \cong (X/R)/
S$.
