## Proof

Let $W$ be a standard Brownian motion. Then $W_1 \sim N(0,1)$, so write $X = W_1
$ with $X \sim N(0,1)$.

We need
$$\operatorname{Var}(W_1^3) = \mathbb{E}[W_1^6] - \big(\mathbb{E}[W_1^3]\big)^2
= \mathbb{E}[X^6] - \big(\mathbb{E}[X^3]\big)^2.$$

**Odd moment.** Since $X$ is symmetric about $0$ (its density $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\
pi}}e^{-x^2/2}$ is even), every odd moment vanishes:
$$\mathbb{E}[X^3] = 0.$$

**Sixth moment.** For $X \sim N(0,1)$, the even moments are given by
$$\mathbb{E}[X^{2k}] = (2k-1)!! = \frac{(2k)!}{2^k k!}.$$
With $k=3$:
$$\mathbb{E}[X^6] = 5!! = 5 \cdot 3 \cdot 1 = 15.$$

(Equivalently, $\frac{6!}{2^3 \cdot 3!} = \frac{720}{8 \cdot 6} = \frac{720}{48}
 = 15$.)

**Combine.**
$$\operatorname{Var}(W_1^3) = 15 - 0^2 = 15.$$

$$\boxed{15}$$
