### Setup

Both $\iota$ and $\iota'$ are $\mathbb{R}$-algebra isomorphisms
$$\iota,\, \iota' : B := D \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{R} \xrightarrow{\sim} \m

The notation $\iota(D)$ means the image of $D \otimes 1 \subset B$ under $\iota$

thbb{R})$, and similarly for $\iota'(D)$.

### Applying Skolem–Noether

Consider the composition
$$\varphi := \iota \circ (\iota')^{-1} : \mathrm{M}_2(\mathbb{R}) \xrightarrow{\
sim} \mathrm{M}_2(\mathbb{R}).$$
This is an $\mathbb{R}$-algebra automorphism of $\mathrm{M}_2(\mathbb{R})$.

**Skolem–Noether theorem** (automorphism form): *Let $A$ be a finite-dimensional
 central simple algebra over a field $K$. Then every $K$-algebra automorphism of
 $A$ is inner, i.e. of the form $\mathrm{Inn}_a : x \mapsto a x a^{-1}$ for some
 $a \in A^{\times}$.*

Here $A = \mathrm{M}_2(\mathbb{R})$ is a central simple algebra over $K = \mathb
b{R}$ (of dimension $4$), and $\varphi$ is an $\mathbb{R}$-algebra automorphism.
 By Skolem–Noether, there exists $a \in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{R}) = A^{\times}$
such that
$$\varphi(x) = a\, x\, a^{-1} \quad \text{for all } x \in \mathrm{M}_2(\mathbb{R
}).$$

Equivalently, for every $y \in B$,
$$\iota(y) = \varphi(\iota'(y)) = a\,\iota'(y)\,a^{-1}.$$

### Restricting to $D$

Embed $D$ into $B$ via $d \mapsto d \otimes 1$. Setting $y = d \otimes 1$ for $d
 \in D$ in the identity above:
$$\iota(d \otimes 1) = a\,\iota'(d \otimes 1)\,a^{-1} \quad \text{for all } d \i
n D.$$

By definition $\iota(D) = \{\iota(d \otimes 1) : d \in D\}$ and $\iota'(D) = \{\
iota'(d \otimes 1) : d \in D\}$, so:
$$\iota(D) = a\,\iota'(D)\,a^{-1}.$$

### Conclusion

The answer is **yes**. The key point is that $\iota$ and $\iota'$ are $\mathbb{R

alized by some $a \in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{R})$. Restricting the resulting conj
