**Problem.** Determine the natural numbers $n$ for which there exists a subset $
S\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ homeomorphic to the $n$-sphere $\mathbb{S}^n=\{x\in\mathbb
{R}^{n+1}:\|x\|=1\}$.

**Answer.** There are **no** natural numbers $n$ with this property.

---

**Proof.**

We show that for every $n\geq 1$ (and also for $n=0$, should one include it amon
g the naturals), no subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$ is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{S}^n$.

**Case $n\geq 1$.** Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists $S\subset\math
bb{R}^n$ and a homeomorphism $h:\mathbb{S}^n\to S$, where $S$ carries the subspa
ce topology inherited from $\mathbb{R}^n$.

*Step 1: $S$ is compact, hence closed in $\mathbb{R}^n$.*
$\mathbb{S}^n$ is a closed and bounded subset of $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$, hence compa
ct by the Heine–Borel theorem. The continuous image of a compact space is compac
t, so $S=h(\mathbb{S}^n)$ is compact. Since $\mathbb{R}^n$ is Hausdorff, every c
ompact subset is closed. Thus $S$ is closed in $\mathbb{R}^n$.

*Step 2: $S$ is open in $\mathbb{R}^n$ (by invariance of domain).*
$\mathbb{S}^n$ is an $n$-dimensional topological manifold (without boundary): ev
ery point $p\in\mathbb{S}^n$ has an open neighborhood $U_p\subset\mathbb{S}^n$ h
omeomorphic to an open ball $B\subset\mathbb{R}^n$.

Fix $p\in\mathbb{S}^n$ and let $q=h(p)\in S$. Let $\varphi:U_p\to B$ be a homeom
orphism onto an open ball $B\subset\mathbb{R}^n$. Consider the composition
$$f=h\circ\varphi^{-1}:B\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^n.$$
This map is continuous and injective (being the composition of the homeomorphism
 $\varphi^{-1}:B\to U_p$ with the injective map $h|_{U_p}:U_p\to\mathbb{R}^n$).
Its domain $B$ is an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$. By the **invariance of domai
n theorem**, $f(B)=h(U_p)$ is open in $\mathbb{R}^n$.

Now $q=h(p)\in h(U_p)\subseteq S$, and $h(U_p)$ is an open subset of $\mathbb{R}
^n$ contained in $S$. Since $p$ was arbitrary, every point of $S$ has an $\mathb
b{R}^n$-open neighborhood contained in $S$. Hence $S$ is open in $\mathbb{R}^n$.

*Step 3: Contradiction.*
$S$ is nonempty (it is the image of $\mathbb{S}^n$), and it is both open and clo
sed in $\mathbb{R}^n$. Since $\mathbb{R}^n$ is connected for $n\geq 1$, the only
 nonempty clopen subset is $\mathbb{R}^n$ itself. Therefore $S=\mathbb{R}^n$.

But $S$ is compact (Step 1) while $\mathbb{R}^n$ is not compact. This is a contr
adiction. Hence no such $S$ exists for $n\geq 1$.

**Case $n=0$ (if $0$ is counted as a natural number).** Here $\mathbb{R}^0=\{0\}
$ is a single point, so its only subsets are $\varnothing$ and $\{0\}$. Meanwhil
e $\mathbb{S}^0=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^1:\|x\|=1\}=\{-1,+1\}$ is a two-point discrete
space. Neither $\varnothing$ nor $\{0\}$ is homeomorphic to a two-point space. S
o $n=0$ also fails.

---

**Conclusion.** For every natural number $n$ (whether the convention starts at $
0$ or $1$), there is no subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$ homeomorphic to $\mathbb{S}^n$.
 The set of such $n$ is empty. $\blacksquare$
