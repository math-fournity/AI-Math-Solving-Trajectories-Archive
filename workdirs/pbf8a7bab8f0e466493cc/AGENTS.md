# Problem

In a bustling coastal city, a new architectural project involves designing a central plaza partitioned by $n=10$ rectangular glass pavilions. Each pavilion is oriented perfectly north-south and east-west. To ensure a sleek, staggered aesthetic, the architects mandate that no two pavillion walls (even those from different structures) can lie along the same line or be collinear.

These pavilions divide the plaza into several distinct paved zones. For any given zone $R$, let $v(R)$ represent the number of physical corners (vertices) found along its perimeter. A "landmark corner" is defined as any corner belonging to one of the original 10 glass pavilions.

The city council is interested in the total complexity of the zones that touch these landmarks. Let $S$ be the sum of $v(R)$ calculated across all zones $R$ that have at least one landmark corner on their boundary.

Calculate the maximum possible value of $S$.
