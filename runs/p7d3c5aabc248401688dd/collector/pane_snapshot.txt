**Key identity.** For all $x,y,z$:
$$x^3+y^3+z^3-3xyz=(x+y+z)\bigl(x^2+y^2+z^2-xy-yz-zx\bigr).$$

Set $a=y-x\ge 0$ and $b=z-y\ge 0$ (so $x\le y\le z$). Then
$$x^2+y^2+z^2-xy-yz-zx=\tfrac12\bigl(a^2+b^2+(a+b)^2\bigr)=a^2+ab+b^2,$$
and $s:=x+y+z=3x+2a+b$. The equation becomes
$$2^{3k}=s\cdot(a^2+ab+b^2),\qquad s=3x+2a+b,\quad a,b,x\ge 0.$$

---

**Lemma.** *If $a,b\ge 0$ and $a^2+ab+b^2$ is a power of $2$, then $a=0$ or $b=0
$.*

*Proof.* Suppose $a,b>0$. Write $d=\gcd(a,b)$, $a=dA$, $b=dB$, $\gcd(A,B)=1$, $A
,B\ge 1$. Then $a^2+ab+b^2=d^2(A^2+AB+B^2)$, so $d$ is a power of $2$ and $A^2+A
B+B^2$ is a power of $2$.

- If $A,B$ both odd: $A^2+AB+B^2\equiv 1+1+1\equiv 3\pmod 4$ (using $A^2\equiv B
^2\equiv 1$, $AB\equiv 1\pmod 4$ when $A\equiv B\equiv 1$, or check directly it
is odd $\ge 3$). In any case it is odd and $\ge 3$, so not a power of $2$.
- If one is even, one odd (wlog $A$ even, $B$ odd): $A^2+AB+B^2\equiv 0+0+1\equi
v 1\pmod 2$, odd. Since $A\ge 2, B\ge 1$, the value is $\ge 4+2+1=7>1$, so not a
 power of $2$.

Both cases give a contradiction. Hence at least one of $a,b$ is $0$. $\square$

So every solution has $a=0$ (i.e. $x=y$) or $b=0$ (i.e. $y=z$). These are disjoi
nt: $a=b=0$ forces $x=y=z$ and $a^2+ab+b^2=0\ne 2^{3k}$.

---

**Subcase A: $a=0$ ($x=y$).** Then $Q=b^2=2^v$ with $v=2m$, $b=2^m$, and $s=3x+b
=2^u$ with $u+2m=3k$. We need
$$3x=2^u-2^m\ge 0,\qquad 3\mid(2^u-2^m).$$
- $2^u\ge 2^m\iff u\ge m$.
- $3\mid(2^u-2^m)\iff (-1)^u\equiv(-1)^m\pmod 3\iff u\equiv m\pmod 2$.

Substituting $u=3k-2m$: the parity condition gives $m\equiv k\pmod 2$, and $u\ge
 m$ gives $m\le k$. Thus
$$m\in\{0,1,\dots,k\},\quad m\equiv k\pmod 2,$$
yielding $\lfloor k/2\rfloor+1$ solutions.

---

**Subcase B: $b=0$ ($y=z$).** Then $Q=a^2=2^v$ with $v=2m$, $a=2^m$, and $s=3x+2
a=3x+2^{m+1}=2^u$ with $u+2m=3k$. We need
$$3x=2^u-2^{m+1}\ge 0,\qquad 3\mid(2^u-2^{m+1}).$$
- $u\ge m+1$.
- $u\equiv m+1\pmod 2$.

With $u=3k-2m$: parity gives $m\equiv k-1\pmod 2$, and $u\ge m+1$ gives $m\le k-
1$. Thus
$$m\in\{0,1,\dots,k-1\},\quad m\equiv k-1\pmod 2.$$
For $k$ even this gives $k/2$ values; for $k$ odd this gives $(k+1)/2$ values.
