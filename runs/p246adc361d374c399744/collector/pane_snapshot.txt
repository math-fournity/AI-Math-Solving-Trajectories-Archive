The statement is **false**.

**Counterexample.** Let $L_1$ be the 2-dimensional non-abelian Lie algebra with
basis $\{x, y\}$ and bracket $[x, y] = y$.

**Center of $L_1$.** For $z = ax + by \in L_1$ to be central we need $[z, x] = 0
$ and $[z, y] = 0$. Computing:
$$[z, x] = [ax + by, x] = -b\,y, \qquad [z, y] = [ax + by, y] = a\,y.$$
Both vanish iff $a = b = 0$. Hence $Z(L_1) = \{0\}$.

**The quotient.** The 1-dimensional subspace $I = \langle y \rangle$ is an ideal
 of $L_1$, since $[x, y] = y \in I$ and $[y, y] = 0$. The quotient $L_2 = L_1 /
I$ is 1-dimensional, hence abelian, so $Z(L_2) = L_2 \neq \{0\}$.

Let $\phi: L_1 \to L_2 = L_1/I$ be the canonical projection, which is a Lie alge
bra epimorphism. Then
$$\phi(Z(L_1)) = \phi(\{0\}) = \{0\} \subsetneq L_2 = Z(L_2).$$

Thus $\phi(Z(L_1)) \neq Z(L_2)$, contradicting the claimed equality.

**Remark.** The inclusion $\phi(Z(L_1)) \subseteq Z(L_2)$ does hold for any epim
orphism: if $z \in Z(L_1)$ and $w \in L_2$, surjectivity gives $w = \phi(u)$ for
 some $u \in L_1$, and $[\phi(z), w] = \phi([z, u]) = \phi(0) = 0$. The reverse
inclusion is what fails — central elements of the quotient need not lift to cent
ral elements of $L_1$, as the example shows (every element of $L_2$ is central,
yet no nonzero element of $L_1$ is central).
