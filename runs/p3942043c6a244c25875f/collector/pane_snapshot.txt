## Setup

Let the gas occupy the half-space $x > 0$, bounded by a plane at $x = 0$ (statio
nary case) or $x = X(t)$ (moving case). The undisturbed gas has density $\rho_0$
, pressure $p_0$, and sound speed $c_0 = \sqrt{\gamma p_0 / \rho_0}$, where $\ga
mma$ is the adiabatic index.

**Definitions and linear acoustic relations:**

- **Velocity potential** $U$: particle velocity $\mathbf{v} = \nabla U$.
- **Displacement potential** $\Phi$: particle displacement $\boldsymbol{\xi} = \
nabla \Phi$, so $U = \partial \Phi / \partial t$.
- **Pressure perturbation** $p' = p - p_0$: from the linearized Euler equation $
\rho_0\, \partial \mathbf{v}/\partial t = -\nabla p'$, we get $p' = -\rho_0\, \p
artial U/\partial t$.
- **Density perturbation** $\rho' = \rho - \rho_0$: from the isentropic relation
 $p' = c_0^2\, \rho'$, so $\rho' = -\rho_0/c_0^2 \cdot \partial U/\partial t$.
- **Wave equation**: $\partial^2 U/\partial t^2 = c_0^2\, \nabla^2 U$.

The normal direction is $x$; we denote $\partial/\partial n = \partial/\partial
x$.

## Case (a): Stationary plane ($x = 0$)

**Kinematic condition.** The wall is rigid and fixed, so the gas cannot penetrat
e it. The normal component of the gas velocity must vanish at the wall:

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial U}{\partial n}\bigg|_{x=0} = 0}$$

**Displacement potential.** Since $U = \partial \Phi/\partial t$, the condition
$\partial U/\partial n = 0$ becomes $\partial/\partial t\,(\partial \Phi/\partia
l n) = 0$, so $\partial \Phi/\partial n = \text{const}$. Taking this constant as
 zero (no initial normal displacement):

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial n}\bigg|_{x=0} = 0}$$

**Pressure.** Differentiate $\partial U/\partial n = 0$ with respect to $t$:

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\!\left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial n}\right) = \fra
c{\partial}{\partial n}\!\left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial t}\right) = 0$$

Since $p' = -\rho_0\, \partial U/\partial t$, we have $\partial p'/\partial n =
-\rho_0\, \partial(\partial U/\partial t)/\partial n = 0$:

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial p}{\partial n}\bigg|_{x=0} = 0}$$

**Density.** Since $\rho' = p'/c_0^2$:

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial n}\bigg|_{x=0} = 0}$$

**Summary for (a):** All four quantities have **vanishing normal derivatives** a
t the fixed rigid wall. The primary condition is the kinematic one ($\partial U/
\partial n = 0$); the conditions on $\Phi$, $p$, and $\rho$ follow from the rela
tions $U = \partial\Phi/\partial t$, $p' = -\rho_0\,\partial U/\partial t$, and
$\rho' = p'/c_0^2$.

## Case (b): Plane moving at $\dot{X}(t) \approx c_0$ normal to itself

When the wall velocity is comparable to the sound speed, the perturbations are n
o longer small and **linear acoustics is insufficient**. We must use the **nonli
near theory of simple (Riemann) waves**.

### Kinematic boundary condition on $U$

The gas particles at the boundary must move with the wall. At the moving boundar
y $x = X(t)$, the gas velocity equals the wall velocity:

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial U}{\partial n}\bigg|_{x=X(t)} = \dot{X}(t)}$$

### Boundary condition on $\Phi$

Since $U = \partial \Phi/\partial t$ and $\partial U/\partial n = \dot{X}(t)$, i
ntegrating in time gives $\partial \Phi/\partial n = X(t) + \text{const}$. Takin
g the constant as zero (wall starts at $x=0$):

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial n}\bigg|_{x=X(t)} = X(t)}$$

This expresses that the normal displacement of the gas at the boundary equals th
e displacement of the wall itself.

### Nonlinear simple-wave relations for $p$ and $\rho$

For one-dimensional isentropic flow of a polytropic gas, the **Riemann invariant
s** are:

$$J_{\pm} = v \pm \frac{2c}{\gamma - 1}$$

where $c = \sqrt{\gamma p / \rho}$ is the local sound speed. A disturbance propa
gating into the undisturbed gas (where $v = 0$, $c = c_0$) is a **simple wave**
in which the invariant $J_-$ is constant throughout:

$$J_- = v - \frac{2c}{\gamma - 1} = -\frac{2c_0}{\gamma - 1}$$

This gives the fundamental simple-wave relation:

$$v = \frac{2(c - c_0)}{\gamma - 1}$$

At the moving boundary, $v = \dot{X}(t)$, so:

$$c\big|_{\text{boundary}} = c_0 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2}\,\dot{X}(t)$$

**Density.** Since $c^2 = \gamma p/\rho$ and $p/p_0 = (\rho/\rho_0)^\gamma$ (ise
ntropic), we have $c/c_0 = (\rho/\rho_0)^{(\gamma-1)/2}$. Therefore:

$$\left(\frac{\rho}{\rho_0}\right)^{(\gamma-1)/2} = 1 + \frac{(\gamma - 1)\,\dot
{X}(t)}{2\,c_0}$$

$$\boxed{\rho\big|_{x=X(t)} = \rho_0 \left[1 + \frac{(\gamma - 1)\,\dot{X}(t)}{2
\,c_0}\right]^{2/(\gamma-1)}}$$

**Pressure.** Since $p/p_0 = (\rho/\rho_0)^\gamma$:

$$\boxed{p\big|_{x=X(t)} = p_0 \left[1 + \frac{(\gamma - 1)\,\dot{X}(t)}{2\,c_0}
\right]^{2\gamma/(\gamma-1)}}$$

### Consistency check: linear limit

When $\dot{X} \ll c_0$, expanding to first order:

$$p' \approx p_0 \cdot \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma - 1} \cdot \frac{(\gamma-1)\,\dot{X
}}{2\,c_0} = \frac{\gamma\, p_0}{c_0}\,\dot{X} = \rho_0\, c_0\, \dot{X}$$

This recovers the familiar linear acoustic impedance relation $p' = \rho_0 c_0\,
 v$ for a one-directional wave, confirming consistency.

### Why nonlinearity is essential when $\dot{X} \approx c_0$

When $\dot{X} \sim c_0$, the density and pressure ratios become order-unity corr
ections. For example, with $\gamma = 1.4$ (diatomic gas) and $\dot{X} = c_0$:

$$\frac{\rho}{\rho_0} = \left(\frac{\gamma+1}{2}\right)^{2/(\gamma-1)} = (1.2)^5
 \approx 2.49, \qquad \frac{p}{p_0} = (1.2)^7 \approx 3.58$$

These are far from unity, so the linear approximation $\rho' \ll \rho_0$ fails c
ompletely, and the nonlinear Riemann-wave treatment is mandatory.

### Note on the validity range

The expression $c = c_0 + (\gamma-1)\dot{X}/2$ remains physical (i.e., $c > 0$)
as long as $\dot{X} > -2c_0/(\gamma-1)$. For $\dot{X} \approx \pm c_0$ this cond
ition is amply satisfied (the cavitation limit is $\dot{X} = -2c_0/(\gamma-1) \a
pprox -5c_0$ for $\gamma = 1.4$). However, if the wall moves **into** the gas at
 $\dot{X} \approx c_0$ for a sustained time, the simple wave steepens and a **sh
ock wave** forms at a finite distance from the wall. The boundary conditions abo
ve remain valid at the wall itself; the shock formation affects the solution in
the interior of the gas.
