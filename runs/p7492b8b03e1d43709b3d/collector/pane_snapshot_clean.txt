## Proof

Place the common center $O$ at the origin. Write the position vectors as
$$A = (\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha),\quad B = \sqrt2\,(\cos\beta,\sin\beta),\quad C =
\sqrt5\,(\cos\gamma,\sin\gamma).$$

The (signed) area of $\triangle ABC$ is
$$\Delta = \tfrac12\bigl|A\times B + B\times C + C\times A\bigr|,$$
where $\times$ denotes the planar cross product. Setting $u=\beta-\alpha$, $v=\g
amma-\beta$ (so $\alpha-\gamma=-(u+v)$),
$$\Delta = \tfrac12\bigl|\sqrt2\sin u + \sqrt{10}\sin v - \sqrt5\sin(u+v)\bigr|.
$$

Let $f(u,v)=\sqrt2\sin u+\sqrt{10}\sin v-\sqrt5\sin(u+v)$. Since $f$ is continuo
us on the compact torus, its extrema occur at critical points. Setting partial d
erivatives to zero:
$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}=\sqrt2\cos u-\sqrt5\cos(u+v)=0,\qquad \frac{\par
tial f}{\partial v}=\sqrt{10}\cos v-\sqrt5\cos(u+v)=0.$$

Denote $t=\cos(u+v)$. Then
$$\cos u = \sqrt{\tfrac52}\,t,\qquad \cos v = \frac{t}{\sqrt2}.$$

Using $\cos(u+v)=\cos u\cos v-\sin u\sin v=t$, we get $\sin u\sin v = \cos u\cos
 v - t = \tfrac{\sqrt5}{2}t^2 - t$. Squaring and using $\sin^2 u\sin^2 v=(1-\cos
^2 u)(1-\cos^2 v)$:
$$\Bigl(\tfrac{\sqrt5}{2}t^2-t\Bigr)^2 = \Bigl(1-\tfrac{5t^2}{2}\Bigr)\Bigl(1-\t
frac{t^2}{2}\Bigr).$$

Expanding and simplifying yields
$$\sqrt5\,t^3 - 4t^2 + 1 = 0 = \bigl(t+\tfrac1{\sqrt5}\bigr)\,\sqrt5\,\bigl(t^2-
\sqrt5\,t+1\bigr).$$

The quadratic gives $t=\frac{\sqrt5\pm1}{2}$; only $t=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}\in[-1,1
]$ is admissible (it yields $|f|\approx0.78$, area $\approx0.39$). The root $t=-
\frac1{\sqrt5}$ gives the extremum of interest.

**At $t=-1/\sqrt5$:** $\cos u = -\frac1{\sqrt2}$, $\cos v = -\frac1{\sqrt{10}}$,
 and $\sin u\sin v = \frac{3\sqrt5}{10}>0$, so $\sin u,\sin v$ share sign. Takin
g both positive: $\sin u=\frac1{\sqrt2}$, $\sin v=\frac3{\sqrt{10}}$, and $\sin(
u+v)=-\frac2{\sqrt5}$ (consistent with $\cos^2+\sin^2=1$). Then
$$f = \sqrt2\cdot\frac1{\sqrt2}+\sqrt{10}\cdot\frac3{\sqrt{10}}-\sqrt5\cdot\Bigl
(-\frac2{\sqrt5}\Bigr)=1+3+2=6.$$

The opposite sign choice gives $f=-6$, so $|f|=6$. This is the global maximum of
 $|f|$ (the other critical value is smaller, and the torus has no boundary).

Therefore
$$\Delta_{\max} = \tfrac12\cdot 6 = \boxed{3}.$$
