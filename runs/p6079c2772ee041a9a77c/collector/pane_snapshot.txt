## Proof

**Claim.** Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional real vector space and $Q: V \to \math
bb{R}$ a quadratic form. The group of units $\mathrm{Cl}^{\times}(V,Q)$ of the C
lifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(V,Q)$ is a Lie group.

We prove this as a consequence of a general fact about unit groups of finite-dim
ensional associative algebras.

**General Fact.** *Let $A$ be a finite-dimensional associative algebra over $\ma
thbb{R}$ with identity. Then the group of units $A^{\times} = \{a \in A : \exist
s\, b \in A,\; ab = ba = 1\}$ is a Lie group, with Lie algebra $(A, [\cdot,\cdot
])$ where $[x,y]=xy-yx$.*

*Proof of the General Fact.* We verify the three requirements: $A^{\times}$ is a
 smooth manifold, and the group operations are smooth.

**Step 1: $A^{\times}$ is an open subset of $A$, hence a smooth manifold.**

Consider the left-regular representation $L: A \to \mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(A)
\cong M_{N}(\mathbb{R})$ (where $N = \dim_{\mathbb{R}} A$) defined by $L(a)(b) =
 ab$. This is an $\mathbb{R}$-linear map, hence continuous. An element $a \in A$
 is a unit if and only if $L(a) \in \mathrm{End}(A)$ is invertible, i.e., $L(a)
\in \mathrm{GL}(A)$. Indeed:
- If $a$ is a unit with $a^{-1}$, then $L(a) \circ L(a^{-1}) = L(1) = \mathrm{id
}$, so $L(a)$ is invertible.
- Conversely, if $L(a)$ is invertible, let $b = L(a)^{-1}(1)$; then $ab = 1$, an
d one checks $ba = 1$ as well (since $L(a)(ba) = a(ba) = (ab)a = a$, and $L(a)(1
) = a$, so $ba = 1$ by injectivity of $L(a)$).

Thus $A^{\times} = L^{-1}(\mathrm{GL}(A))$. Since $\mathrm{GL}(A) = \{M \in \mat
hrm{End}(A) : \det M \neq 0\}$ is open in $\mathrm{End}(A)$ and $L$ is continuou
s, $A^{\times}$ is open in $A$. As an open subset of the finite-dimensional real
 vector space $A$, it inherits the structure of a smooth manifold of dimension $
\dim_{\mathbb{R}} A$.

**Step 2: Multiplication is smooth.**

The multiplication map $m: A^{\times} \times A^{\times} \to A^{\times}$, $(a,b)
\mapsto ab$, is the restriction of the bilinear (hence smooth) map $A \times A \
to A$. Since $A^{\times} \times A^{\times}$ is an open submanifold of $A \times
A$, the restriction $m$ is smooth.

**Step 3: Inversion is smooth.**

The inversion map $\iota: A^{\times} \to A^{\times}$, $a \mapsto a^{-1}$, factor
s as:
$$A^{\times} \xrightarrow{L} \mathrm{GL}(A) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{inv}} \mathrm{G
L}(A) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{ev}_1} A^{\times}$$
where:
- $L: A^{\times} \to \mathrm{GL}(A)$ is the restriction of the linear map $L$, h
ence smooth.
- $\mathrm{inv}: \mathrm{GL}(A) \to \mathrm{GL}(A)$ is matrix inversion, which i
s smooth (it is given by $M \mapsto M^{-1} = \mathrm{adj}(M)/\det(M)$, a rationa
l function in the entries of $M$).
- $\mathrm{ev}_1: \mathrm{GL}(A) \to A^{\times}$, $T \mapsto T(1)$, is the evalu
ation at $1 \in A$, which is linear (hence smooth), and lands in $A^{\times}$ be
cause $L(a)^{-1}(1) = a^{-1}$ is a unit.

The composition $\mathrm{ev}_1 \circ \mathrm{inv} \circ L$ sends $a \mapsto L(a)
 \mapsto L(a)^{-1} \mapsto L(a)^{-1}(1) = a^{-1}$, which is exactly $\iota$. Bei
ng a composition of smooth maps, $\iota$ is smooth.

**Step 4: The Lie algebra.** The Lie algebra of $A^{\times}$ is the tangent spac
e at the identity $1 \in A^{\times}$, which is $A$ itself. The bracket induced b
y the group commutator is the algebra commutator: for $x, y \in A$, the curve $\
gamma(t) = 1 + tx$ lies in $A^{\times}$ for small $t$ (since $A^{\times}$ is ope
n), and the group commutator $\gamma(t)\eta(s)\gamma(t)^{-1}\eta(s)^{-1}$ with $
\eta(s) = 1 + sy$ yields, upon taking $\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t\,\partial s}
\big|_{t=s=0}$, the element $xy - yx = [x,y]$. Hence the Lie algebra is $(A, [\c
dot,\cdot])$. $\square$

**Application to Clifford algebras.** The Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(V,Q)$ of
 a finite-dimensional real quadratic space $(V, Q)$ is a finite-dimensional asso
ciative $\mathbb{R}$-algebra with identity (of dimension $2^{\dim V}$). By the G
eneral Fact above, its group of units $\mathrm{Cl}^{\times}(V,Q)$ is a Lie group
, with underlying manifold the open subset of invertible elements in $\mathrm{Cl
}(V,Q)$, and Lie algebra $(\mathrm{Cl}(V,Q), [\cdot,\cdot])$.

**Remark.** Important subgroups of $\mathrm{Cl}^{\times}(V,Q)$ — such as the Cli
fford (Lipschitz) group $\Gamma(V,Q) = \{x \in \mathrm{Cl}^{\times}(V,Q) : \tild
e{x} V x^{-1} \subseteq V\}$ (where $\tilde{x} = \alpha(x)$ is the grade involut
ion) and the special Clifford group — are *closed* subgroups of $\mathrm{Cl}^{\t
imes}(V,Q)$, and are therefore Lie groups by the closed subgroup theorem (Cartan
's theorem). The pin and spin groups $\mathrm{Pin}(V,Q)$ and $\mathrm{Spin}(V,Q)
$, being further closed subgroups (defined by the additional constraint $x\tilde
{x} = \pm 1$), are likewise Lie groups — in fact compact Lie groups when $Q$ is
positive or negative definite. $\square$
