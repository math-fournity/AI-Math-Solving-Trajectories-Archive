Looking at this problem, I need to analyze the tensor product $kG \otimes_{kH} k
G \otimes_{kG} M$ and determine whether it's isomorphic to $M$.

**Answer: No, in general this is not isomorphic to $M$.** Below is the complete
proof.

---

## Proof

### Setup and module structures

- $kG$ is a $(kG, kG)$-bimodule. Via the inclusion $kH \hookrightarrow kG$, we v
iew $kG$ as a $(kG, kH)$-bimodule (first copy) and as a $(kH, kG)$-bimodule (sec
ond copy).
- $kG \otimes_{kH} kG$ is then a $(kG, kG)$-bimodule.
- $M$ is a left $kG$-module, i.e., a $(kG, k)$-bimodule.
- The full tensor product $kG \otimes_{kH} kG \otimes_{kG} M$ is a left $kG$-mod
ule.

### Step 1: Associativity reduces the problem

By the associativity of tensor products (applicable since all bimodule structure
s are compatible: $kG$ is $(kG,kH)$, the middle $kG$ is $(kH,kG)$, and $M$ is $(
kG,k)$):

$$(kG \otimes_{kH} kG) \otimes_{kG} M \;\cong\; kG \otimes_{kH} (kG \otimes_{kG}
 M).$$

### Step 2: $kG \otimes_{kG} M \cong M$ (this part IS true)

The multiplication map $\varphi: kG \otimes_{kG} M \to M$ defined by $g \otimes
m \mapsto g \cdot m$ is an isomorphism of left $kG$-modules, with inverse $\psi:
 m \mapsto e \otimes m$ (where $e$ is the identity of $G$).

**Check:** $\varphi(\psi(m)) = \varphi(e \otimes m) = e \cdot m = m$. ✓
$\psi(\varphi(g \otimes m)) = \psi(g \cdot m) = e \otimes (g \cdot m) = e \otime
s (g \cdot m)$. Since $g \cdot m = g \cdot m$ and in $kG \otimes_{kG} M$ we have
 $e \otimes (g \cdot m) = (eg) \otimes m = g \otimes m$. ✓

This is the standard fact $R \otimes_R M \cong M$ for any ring $R$.

### Step 3: Reduction to $kG \otimes_{kH} M$

Combining Steps 1 and 2:

$$kG \otimes_{kH} kG \otimes_{kG} M \;\cong\; kG \otimes_{kH} M.$$

This is the **induced module** $\mathrm{Ind}_H^G(\mathrm{Res}_H^G(M))$ — restric
t $M$ to $H$, then induce back to $G$.

### Step 4: $kG \otimes_{kH} kG \not\cong kG$ as a $(G,G)$-bimodule (in general)

**Dimension argument (finite groups):** When $G$ is finite:
$$\dim_k(kG \otimes_{kH} kG) = \frac{\dim_k(kG) \cdot \dim_k(kG)}{\dim_k(kH)} =
\frac{|G|^2}{|H|},$$
while $\dim_k(kG) = |G|$. These are equal if and only if $|G| = |H|$, i.e., $G =
 H$.

For any **proper** subgroup $H \subsetneq G$, we have $|G|^2/|H| > |G|$, so the
dimensions differ and no isomorphism of bimodules (or even vector spaces) can ex
ist.

**Explicit counterexample:** Let $G = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} = \{e, s\}$, $H = \
{e\}$, $k$ any field. Then $kG \otimes_{kH} kG = kG \otimes_k kG$ has dimension
$4$, while $kG$ has dimension $2$. Not isomorphic.

**Remark on the multiplication map:** There is a natural $(G,G)$-bimodule homomo
rphism $\mu: kG \otimes_{kH} kG \to kG$ given by $\mu(a \otimes b) = ab$ (multip
lication in $kG$). This map is **surjective** (since $\mu(x \otimes e) = x$ for
any $x \in G$), but it is **not injective** when $H \subsetneq G$. For instance,
 in the counterexample above, $e \otimes s - s \otimes e$ lies in the kernel: $\
mu(e \otimes s - s \otimes e) = s - s = 0$, but $e \otimes s \neq s \otimes e$ i
n $kG \otimes_k kG$ (they are linearly independent basis elements). So $\mu$ is
a surjective but non-injective bimodule map — confirming the modules are not iso
morphic.

### Step 5: $kG \otimes_{kH} M \not\cong M$ in general

**Explicit counterexample:** Let $G = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} = \{e, s\}$, $H = \
{e\}$, $k = \mathbb{C}$, and $M = k$ the **trivial** $G$-module (1-dimensional,
$s$ acts as $1$).

Then:
$$kG \otimes_{kH} M = kG \otimes_k k \cong kG \quad\text{as a left } kG\text{-mo
dule}.$$

This is the **regular representation** of $G$, which is 2-dimensional. Over $\ma
thbb{C}$, it decomposes as $\mathbf{1} \oplus \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}$ (trivial
 $\oplus$ sign representation). But $M = \mathbf{1}$ is 1-dimensional. Since $\d
im(kG \otimes_{kH} M) = 2 \neq 1 = \dim(M)$, they are not isomorphic.

**General dimension formula (finite groups, $[G:H] < \infty$):**
$$\dim_k(kG \otimes_{kH} M) = [G:H] \cdot \dim_k(M),$$
which equals $\dim_k(M)$ if and only if $[G:H] = 1$, i.e., $G = H$.

### Conclusion

1. **$kG \otimes_{kH} kG \simeq kG$ as a $(G,G)$-bimodule?** **No**, not in gene
ral. This fails whenever $H$ is a proper subgroup of $G$ (dimension mismatch $|G
|^2/|H| \neq |G|$). The natural multiplication map is surjective but not injecti
ve.

2. **$kG \otimes_{kG} M \simeq M$?** **Yes**, this is always true (standard isom
orphism $R \otimes_R M \cong M$).

3. **$kG \otimes_{kH} kG \otimes_{kG} M \simeq M$ as a $G$-module?** **No**, not
 in general. By associativity and Step 2, this module is isomorphic to $kG \otim
es_{kH} M = \mathrm{Ind}_H^G(\mathrm{Res}_H^G M)$, which has dimension $[G:H] \c
dot \dim_k(M) \neq \dim_k(M)$ when $H \subsetneq G$. The second sub-claim being
true does **not** rescue the situation, because the first sub-claim (the bimodul
e isomorphism) is false — one cannot replace $kG \otimes_{kH} kG$ with $kG$ insi
de the tensor product.

The module $kG \otimes_{kH} kG \otimes_{kG} M$ is isomorphic to $M$ **if and onl
y if** $G = H$ (in which case all tensor products collapse trivially).
