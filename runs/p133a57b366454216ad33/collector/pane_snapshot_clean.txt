## Proof

Since $f, f' \in L^2(\mathbb{R})$, the function $f$ belongs to the Sobolev space
 $H^1(\mathbb{R})$. A standard result gives that $f$ has an absolutely continuou
s representative (henceforth denoted $f$), with $f(x) \to 0$ as $|x|\to\infty$.

Define $h(x) = x\,|f(x)|^2$. We show $h(x)\to 0$ as $x\to+\infty$ and as $x\to-\
infty$.

**Step 1: $h \in L^1(\mathbb{R})$.**

Since $xf\in L^2$ and $f\in L^2$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} |h(x)|\,dx = \int_{\mathbb{R}} |x|\,|f(x)|^2\,dx = \int_{\ma
thbb{R}} |xf(x)|\,|f(x)|\,dx \le \|xf\|_{L^2}\,\|f\|_{L^2} < \infty.$$

**Step 2: $h$ is absolutely continuous and $h'\in L^1(\mathbb{R})$.**

Since $f$ is absolutely continuous, $|f|^2 = f\,\bar f$ is absolutely continuous
, and $h(x)=x\,|f(x)|^2$ (product of AC functions) is absolutely continuous, wit
h
$$h'(x) = |f(x)|^2 + 2x\,\operatorname{Re}\!\big(\overline{f(x)}\,f'(x)\big) \qu
ad \text{a.e.}$$

The first term satisfies $|f|^2\in L^1$ since $f\in L^2$. For the second term, C
auchy–Schwarz gives
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} |x|\,|\overline{f(x)}\,f'(x)|\,dx \le \|xf\|_{L^2}\,\|f'\|_{
L^2} < \infty,$$
so $x\,\overline{f}\,f'\in L^1$, hence $h'\in L^1(\mathbb{R})$.

**Step 3: $h(x)\to 0$ as $x\to\pm\infty$.**

Since $h$ is absolutely continuous with $h'\in L^1(\mathbb{R})$,
$$h(x) = h(0) + \int_0^x h'(t)\,dt.$$
Because $h'\in L^1$, the limits $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty} h(x) = h(0)+\in
t_0^{+\infty} h'(t)\,dt$ and $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to-\infty} h(x) = h(0)-\int_{
-\infty}^{0} h'(t)\,dt$ exist and are finite.

Since $h\in L^1(\mathbb{R})$ (Step 1) and $h$ has finite limits at $\pm\infty$,
those limits must equal $0$.

Therefore $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty} x\,|f(x)|^2 = 0$ and $\displaystyle\l
im_{x\to-\infty} x\,|f(x)|^2 = 0$, i.e.
$$\lim_{|x|\to\infty} x\,|f(x)|^2 = 0. \qquad\blacksquare$$
