## Proof

We are given the implicit equation

$$y(z) = z\,\phi(y(z)),$$

where $\phi$ is analytic at $0$, $\phi(0)\neq 0$, $\phi$ has non-negative Taylor
 coefficients, and there is a unique positive solution $\tau$ (within the disc o
f convergence of $\phi$) of

$$\phi(\tau) - \tau\,\phi'(\tau) = 0.$$

We determine the radius of convergence $\rho$ of $y(z)$.

### Step 1. The functional inverse

Rewrite the implicit equation as $y = z\,\phi(y)$, i.e. $z = y/\phi(y)$. Define

$$\psi(y) := \frac{y}{\phi(y)}.$$

Since $\phi(0)\neq 0$, $\psi$ is analytic at $y=0$ with $\psi(0)=0$, and $y(z)$
is the functional inverse of $\psi$ near $0$: $y(z) = \psi^{-1}(z)$.

### Step 2. Where the inverse fails to be analytic

The inverse function theorem for $\psi$ breaks down exactly where $\psi'(y)=0$.
Compute

$$\psi'(y) = \frac{\phi(y) - y\,\phi'(y)}{\phi(y)^2}.$$

The numerator vanishes precisely when $\phi(y)-y\phi'(y)=0$. By hypothesis the u
nique positive solution of this equation (within the disc of convergence of $\ph
i$) is $y=\tau$. Hence $\psi'(\tau)=0$, and $\tau$ is the first positive point w
here analyticity of the local inverse is obstructed.

### Step 3. Non-negative coefficients force the first singularity onto the posit
ive real axis

Because $\phi$ has non-negative coefficients, $y(z)$ (obtained by Lagrange inver
sion from $y=z\phi(y)$) also has non-negative coefficients. By Pringsheim's theo
rem, the dominant singularity of $y(z)$ lies on the positive real axis. The uniq
ue positive obstruction is at $y=\tau$, corresponding to

$$\rho = \psi(\tau) = \frac{\tau}{\phi(\tau)}.$$

### Step 4. Equivalent form via the characteristic equation

From $\phi(\tau)-\tau\phi'(\tau)=0$ we have $\phi(\tau)=\tau\phi'(\tau)$, so

$$\rho = \frac{\tau}{\phi(\tau)} = \frac{1}{\phi'(\tau)}.$$

### Conclusion

The radius of convergence of $y(z)$ is

$$\boxed{\rho = \frac{\tau}{\phi(\tau)} = \frac{1}{\phi'(\tau)}},$$

where $\tau$ is the unique positive solution of $\phi(\tau)-\tau\phi'(\tau)=0$ w
ithin the disc of convergence of $\phi$.
