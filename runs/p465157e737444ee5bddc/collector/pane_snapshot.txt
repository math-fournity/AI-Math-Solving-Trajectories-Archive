## Proof

**Claim:** Yes, $\det(A) > 0$.

**Step 1: Reduce to the symmetric part.**

Decompose $A = S + K$ where $S = \frac{A+A^T}{2}$ (symmetric) and $K = \frac{A-A
^T}{2}$ (skew-symmetric). For any real vector $x$,
$$x^T K x = (x^T K x)^T = x^T K^T x = -x^T K x,$$
so $x^T K x = 0$. Hence $x^T A x = x^T S x$, and the hypothesis says **$S$ is po
sitive definite**.

**Step 2: Every eigenvalue of $A$ has positive real part.**

Let $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$ be an eigenvalue of $A$ with eigenvector $v \in \ma
thbb{C}^n \setminus \{0\}$: $Av = \lambda v$. Taking conjugate transpose (using
$A$ real, so $A^* = A^T$):
$$v^* A^T = \bar\lambda\, v^*.$$
Then
$$v^*(A+A^T)v = (\lambda + \bar\lambda)\, v^* v = 2\,\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)\,\|v\|
^2.$$
But $A + A^T = 2S$, so the left side is $2\,v^* S v$. Since $S$ is real symmetri
c positive definite, write $S = B^T B$ (Cholesky, $B$ real invertible). Then
$$v^* S v = v^* B^T B v = (Bv)^*(Bv) = \|Bv\|^2 > 0$$
since $B$ invertible and $v \neq 0$. Therefore
$$\mathrm{Re}(\lambda) = \frac{v^* S v}{\|v\|^2} > 0.$$

**Step 3: Compute the sign of $\det(A)$.**

The eigenvalues of the real matrix $A$ are either real or come in conjugate pair
s $\lambda, \bar\lambda$.

- **Real eigenvalues:** By Step 2, $\mathrm{Re}(\lambda) > 0$; being real, $\lam
bda > 0$.
- **Complex conjugate pairs:** Each pair contributes $\lambda \cdot \bar\lambda
= |\lambda|^2 > 0$ to the product.

Since $\det(A) = \prod_i \lambda_i$ (with algebraic multiplicity), every factor
is positive: real eigenvalues are positive, and each conjugate pair contributes
a positive $|\lambda|^2$. Therefore

$$\det(A) > 0. \qquad \blacksquare$$
