**Proof.**

Let $A = \angle CAB$, $B = \angle ABC$, $C = \angle BCA$, so $A+B+C = 180°$. The
 triangle is acute, hence $A,B,C \in (0°,90°)$ and the circumcenter $O$ lies ins
ide the triangle. We are given
$$C \ge B + 30°. \tag{0}$$
Let $M$ be the midpoint of $BC$. Since $OB=OC=R$, the line $OM$ is the perpendic
ular bisector of $BC$; in particular $OM\perp BC$ and $M$ is the midpoint. The c
entral angle satisfies $\angle BOC = 2A$, and $OM$ bisects it (by symmetry of th
e isosceles triangle $OBC$), so
$$\angle MOC = A. \tag{1}$$

---

**Position of $P$ relative to $M$.** Since the triangle is acute, $P$ lies on se
gment $BC$. A direct computation gives
$$MP = BP - BM = c\cos B - \frac{a}{2} = \frac{c^{2}-b^{2}}{2a}.$$
From (0) we have $C>B$, hence $c>b$ (larger angle opposite larger side), so $MP>
0$; thus $P$ lies strictly between $M$ and $C$. Consequently the ray $OP$ lies b
etween rays $OM$ and $OC$, and using (1):
$$\angle COP = \angle MOC - \angle MOP = A - \angle MOP. \tag{2}$$

The desired inequality $\angle CAB + \angle COP < 90°$ is therefore equivalent (
via (2)) to
$$2A - \angle MOP < 90° \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \angle MOP > 2A - 90°. \ta
g{$\star$}$$

---

**A formula for $\angle MOP$.** In the right triangle $OMP$ (right at $M$),
$$\tan(\angle MOP) = \frac{MP}{OM}.$$
The distance from the circumcenter to chord $BC$ is $OM = R\cos A$. Using $a=2R\
sin A$, $b=2R\sin B$, $c=2R\sin C$ and the identity $\sin^{2}C-\sin^{2}B = \sin(
C+B)\sin(C-B) = \sin A\,\sin(C-B)$ (since $C+B=180°-A$):
$$c^{2}-b^{2}=4R^{2}(\sin^{2}C-\sin^{2}B)=4R^{2}\sin A\,\sin(C-B),$$
$$2a = 4R\sin A.$$
Hence
$$\tan(\angle MOP)=\frac{4R^{2}\sin A\,\sin(C-B)}{4R^{2}\sin A\,\cos A}=\frac{\s
in(C-B)}{\cos A}. \tag{$\dagger$}$$

---

**Verification of ($\star$).**

*Case 1: $A\le 45°$.* Then $2A-90°\le 0° < \angle MOP$ (the latter strict becaus
e $P\ne M$ as $c\ne b$), so ($\star$) holds.

*Case 2: $A>45°$.* Both $\angle MOP\in(0°,A)\subset(0°,90°)$ and $2A-90°\in(0°,9
0°)$ lie in the interval where $\tan$ is strictly increasing, so ($\star$) is eq
uivalent to
$$\tan(\angle MOP)>\tan(2A-90°).$$
Using ($\dagger$) and $\tan(2A-90°)=\dfrac{-\cos 2A}{\sin 2A}=\dfrac{-\cos 2A}{2
\sin A\cos A}$, and multiplying through by $\cos A>0$, this becomes
$$2\sin A\,\sin(C-B)+\cos 2A>0. \tag{$\ddagger$}$$

From (0) we have $C-B\ge 30°$; also $C-B<90°$ (since $C<90°$, $B>0°$). As $\sin$
 is increasing on $[0°,90°]$,
$$\sin(C-B)\ge \sin 30°=\tfrac12.$$
Therefore
$$2\sin A\,\sin(C-B)+\cos 2A \;\ge\; \sin A + \cos 2A \;=\; \sin A + 1 - 2\sin^{
2}A \;=\; (1-\sin A)(1+2\sin A).$$
Since $0<\sin A<1$ (because $A\in(0°,90°)$), the right-hand side is strictly pos
itive. Hence ($\ddagger$) holds, giving ($\star$).

---

In both cases ($\star$) holds, so by (2)
$$\angle CAB + \angle COP = 2A - \angle MOP < 90°.$$

$\blacksquare$
