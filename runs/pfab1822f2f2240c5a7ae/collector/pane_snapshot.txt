## Setup

Each cylinder has radius $r = \tfrac{1}{2}$ and is inscribed in the unit sphere
(its axis lies at distance $\tfrac{1}{2}$ from the origin, making the cylinder t
angent to the unit sphere). The two cylinders are tangent to each other at the o

**Coordinate choice.** Place the closest-to-origin point of Cylinder 1's axis at
 $\mathbf{p}_1 = (\tfrac{1}{2},0,0)$ with axis direction $\mathbf{d}_1 = (0,0,1)
$. For tangency at the origin, Cylinder 2's closest point must be $\mathbf{p}_2
= -\mathbf{p}_1 = (-\tfrac{1}{2},0,0)$, with axis direction $\mathbf{d}_2 = (0,\
cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)$ for some angle $\alpha$ (any direction perpendicular to $
\mathbf{p}_2$).

**Cylinder 1** (solid): $(x-\tfrac12)^2 + y^2 \le \tfrac14$, equivalently $x^2 +
 y^2 \le x$ (requires $x \ge 0$).

**Cylinder 2** (solid): using the rotation $u = y\sin\alpha - z\cos\alpha$, $v =
 y\cos\alpha + z\sin\alpha$ (which preserves $y^2+z^2 = u^2+v^2$), the equation
becomes $(x+\tfrac12)^2 + u^2 \le \tfrac14$, equivalently $x^2 + u^2 \le -x$ (re
quires $x \le 0$).

Since Cylinder 1 lives in $x \ge 0$ and Cylinder 2 in $x \le 0$, their interiors
 are **disjoint** (meeting only at the origin).

## Volume of Cylinder 1 inside the unit ball

$$V_1 = \iiint\limits_{\substack{x^2+y^2\le x\\ x^2+y^2+z^2\le 1}} dV = \iint\li
mits_{x^2+y^2\le x} 2\sqrt{1-x^2-y^2}\,dx\,dy$$

Switch to polar: $x = r\cos\theta$, $y = r\sin\theta$. The region $x^2+y^2 \le x
$ becomes $r \le \cos\theta$, $\theta \in [-\pi/2, \pi/2]$.

$$V_1 = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\int_0^{\cos\theta} 2r\sqrt{1-r^2}\,dr\,d\theta$$

## Volume of Cylinder 2 inside the unit ball

After the rotation $(y,z) \to (u,v)$, the integral for $V_2$ is **identical in f
orm** to $V_1$ (with $x \to -x$, $u$ playing the role of $y$). The unit ball is
invariant under this rotation. Therefore:

$$V_2 = V_1 = \frac{2(3\pi-4)}{9}$$

This holds for **any** angle $\alpha$, so the result is independent of the relat

## Volume of the bounded region

The two cylinders are disjoint (except at the origin), so the volume of their un

$$V_1 + V_2 = \frac{4(3\pi - 4)}{9}$$

The region **bounded by the two cylinders and the unit sphere** is the part of t
he unit ball lying **outside both cylinders**:

$$V = \frac{4\pi}{3} - \frac{4(3\pi-4)}{9} = \frac{12\pi - (12\pi - 16)}{9} = \f
