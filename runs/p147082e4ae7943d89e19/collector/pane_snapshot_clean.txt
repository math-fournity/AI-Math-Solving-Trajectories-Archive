## Proof

Let $X$ be a compact metric space and let $\mathcal{M}(X)$ denote the set of Bor
el probability measures on $X$, equipped with the topology of weak convergence (
i.e. the weak-$*$ topology inherited from $C(X)^*$). We prove that for every Bor
el set $A\subseteq X$, the evaluation map
$$\Phi_A:\mathcal{M}(X)\to[0,1],\qquad \Phi_A(P)=P(A)$$
is Borel measurable (where $\mathcal{M}(X)$ carries its Borel $\sigma$-algebra).
 Since $X$ is compact metric, the only natural $\sigma$-algebra on which probabi
lity measures are defined is the Borel $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{B}(X)$; "measu
rable set" is read as "Borel set."

### Preliminaries

Because $X$ is compact metrizable, $C(X)$ is separable, so the closed unit ball
of $C(X)^*$ with the weak-$*$ topology is compact metrizable (Banach–Alaoglu + s
eparability of the predual). $\mathcal{M}(X)$ is a weak-$*$ closed subset of thi
s ball, hence $\mathcal{M}(X)$ is itself compact metrizable. In particular it is
 a perfectly normal space, so upper/lower semicontinuous functions on it are Bor
el measurable.

### Step 1 — Closed sets belong to $\mathcal{D}$

Let
$$\mathcal{D}:=\bigl\{A\in\mathcal{B}(X):\Phi_A\text{ is Borel measurable}\bigr\
}.$$

Let $F\subseteq X$ be closed. By the **Portmanteau theorem**, weak convergence $
P_n\to P$ implies
$$\limsup_{n\to\infty}P_n(F)\le P(F).$$
Since $\mathcal{M}(X)$ is metrizable, sequential upper semicontinuity equals upp
er semicontinuity, so $P\mapsto P(F)$ is upper semicontinuous. An upper semicont
inuous function has $\{P:P(F)<a\}$ open for every $a\in\mathbb{R}$, hence is Bor
el measurable. Thus $F\in\mathcal{D}$.

### Step 2 — $\mathcal{D}$ is a $\lambda$-system (Dynkin system)

We verify the three defining properties.

1. **Whole space.** $\Phi_X(P)=P(X)=1$ is constant, hence measurable. So $X\in\m
athcal{D}$.

2. **Proper differences.** If $A,B\in\mathcal{D}$ with $A\subseteq B$, then
$$\Phi_{B\setminus A}(P)=P(B)-P(A)=\Phi_B(P)-\Phi_A(P),$$
a difference of two measurable $[0,1]$-valued functions, hence measurable. So $B
\setminus A\in\mathcal{D}$.

3. **Countable disjoint unions.** Let $(A_n)_{n\ge1}\subseteq\mathcal{D}$ be pai
rwise disjoint and set $A=\bigcup_n A_n$. By $\sigma$-additivity,
$$\Phi_A(P)=P(A)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P(A_n)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Phi_{A_n}(P).$$
The partial sums $S_N(P)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Phi_{A_n}(P)$ are measurable (finite sum
s of measurable functions), and $\Phi_A(P)=\lim_{N\to\infty}S_N(P)$ is a pointwi
se limit of measurable functions, hence measurable. So $A\in\mathcal{D}$.

Thus $\mathcal{D}$ is a $\lambda$-system.

### Step 3 — Apply the $\pi$-$\lambda$ theorem

The collection of closed subsets of $X$ is a **$\pi$-system**: a finite intersec
tion of closed sets is closed. It generates the Borel $\sigma$-algebra:
$$\sigma(\text{closed sets})=\mathcal{B}(X).$$

By Step 1, every closed set lies in $\mathcal{D}$. By Step 2, $\mathcal{D}$ is a
 $\lambda$-system. **Dynkin's $\pi$-$\lambda$ theorem** gives
$$\mathcal{B}(X)=\sigma(\text{closed sets})\subseteq\mathcal{D}.$$

The reverse inclusion $\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(X)$ holds by definition.
Hence $\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{B}(X)$.

### Conclusion

For every Borel (measurable) set $A\subseteq X$, the map
$$P\longmapsto P(A),\qquad \mathcal{M}(X)\to[0,1],$$
is Borel measurable. **The answer is yes.**
