## Proof

We evaluate
$$L = \lim_{n \to \infty} \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}\,n^{1/k}}\sin^{2}\!\
left(\frac{n \pi}{k}\right),$$
where we used $\sqrt[k]{n} = n^{1/k}$.

**Step 1: The series is well-defined for each $n$.**

Since $0 \le \sin^2(\cdot) \le 1$ and $n^{1/k} \ge 1$ for all $n \ge 1$, $k \ge
1$:
$$0 \le \frac{\sin^2(n\pi/k)}{k^2\, n^{1/k}} \le \frac{1}{k^2}.$$
Because $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} 1/k^2 = \pi^2/6 < \infty$, the series converges abs
olutely for every $n$.

**Step 2: Tail estimate (uniform in $n$).**

For any positive integer $M$ and any $n$:
$$\sum_{k=M+1}^{\infty} \frac{\sin^2(n\pi/k)}{k^2\, n^{1/k}} \le \sum_{k=M+1}^{\
infty} \frac{1}{k^2}.$$
The right-hand side is independent of $n$ and tends to $0$ as $M \to \infty$.

**Step 3: Head estimate (vanishes as $n \to \infty$ for fixed $M$).**

For any fixed $M$:
$$\sum_{k=1}^{M} \frac{\sin^2(n\pi/k)}{k^2\, n^{1/k}} \le \sum_{k=1}^{M} \frac{1
}{k^2\, n^{1/k}}.$$
For each fixed $k \in \{1, 2, \dots, M\}$, we have $n^{1/k} \to \infty$ as $n \t
o \infty$, so
$$\frac{1}{k^2\, n^{1/k}} \longrightarrow 0.$$
Since $M$ is fixed, the finite sum of terms each tending to $0$ also tends to $0
$:
$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \sum_{k=1}^{M} \frac{\sin^2(n\pi/k)}{k^2\, n^{1/k}} = 0.$$

**Step 4: Combine via a standard $\varepsilon$–$M$ argument.**

Let $\varepsilon > 0$. Choose $M$ large enough that $\sum_{k=M+1}^{\infty} 1/k^2
 < \varepsilon$. Then for all $n$:
$$0 \le S(n) = \sum_{k=1}^{M}(\cdots) + \sum_{k=M+1}^{\infty}(\cdots) \le \sum_{
k=1}^{M} \frac{1}{k^2\, n^{1/k}} + \varepsilon.$$
Taking $n \to \infty$ and using Step 3:
$$0 \le \limsup_{n \to \infty} S(n) \le 0 + \varepsilon.$$
Since $\varepsilon > 0$ is arbitrary, $\limsup_{n\to\infty} S(n) = 0$. Combined
with $S(n) \ge 0$, we conclude:
$$\lim_{n \to \infty} S(n) = 0.$$

**Intuition.** For each fixed $k$, the factor $n^{1/k} \to \infty$ drives the $k
$-th term to zero (even when $\sin^2(n\pi/k) \neq 0$, i.e., when $k \nmid n$). T
he remaining large-$k$ terms are controlled by the summability of $1/k^2$, unifo
rmly in $n$. The two effects together force the entire sum to zero.

$$\boxed{L = 0}$$
