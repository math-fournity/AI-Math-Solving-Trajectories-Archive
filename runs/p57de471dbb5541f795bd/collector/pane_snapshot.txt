## Proof

We show that the optimal constant is $C = 1$.

**Setup.** Write $z_1 = r_1 e^{i\theta_1}$, $z_2 = r_2 e^{i\theta_2}$ with $r_1,
 r_2 \ge 0$. Set $\varphi = 2(\theta_1 - \theta_2)$. Then
$$
|z_1^2 - z_2^2|^2 = \big|r_1^2 e^{2i\theta_1} - r_2^2 e^{2i\theta_2}\big|^2 = r_
1^4 + r_2^4 - 2 r_1^2 r_2^2 \cos\varphi.
$$
Hence
$$
\text{LHS} = 4 r_1^2 r_2^2 + r_1^4 + r_2^4 - 2 r_1^2 r_2^2 \cos\varphi = r_1^4 +
 r_2^4 + 2 r_1^2 r_2^2(2 - \cos\varphi).
$$

**Normalize.** Let $S = r_1^2 + r_2^2$. If $S = 0$ both sides are $0$, so assume
 $S > 0$. Set $t = r_1^2/S \in [0,1]$, so $r_2^2/S = 1-t$. Dividing by $S^2$,
$$
\frac{\text{LHS}}{S^2} = t^2 + (1-t)^2 + 2t(1-t)(2 - \cos\varphi).
$$
Expanding, $t^2 + (1-t)^2 = 2t^2 - 2t + 1$ and $2t(1-t)\cdot 2 = 4t(1-t) = 4t -
4t^2$, so
$$
\frac{\text{LHS}}{S^2} = (2t^2 - 2t + 1) + (4t - 4t^2) - 2t(1-t)\cos\varphi = 1
+ 2t(1-t) - 2t(1-t)\cos\varphi.
$$
Therefore
$$
\frac{\text{LHS}}{S^2} = 1 + 2t(1-t)(1 - \cos\varphi).
$$

**Bound from below.** Since $t \in [0,1]$, we have $t(1-t) \ge 0$, and $1 - \cos
\varphi \ge 0$ for all real $\varphi$. Hence
$$
\frac{\text{LHS}}{S^2} \ge 1.
$$
Equivalently,
$$
4|z_1|^2|z_2|^2 + |z_1^2 - z_2^2|^2 \ge (|z_1|^2 + |z_2|^2)^2.
$$

**Optimality.** The value $1$ is attained: take $\varphi = 0$ (e.g. $z_1, z_2$ b
oth real and positive), so $\cos\varphi = 1$ and the ratio equals exactly $1$ fo
r every $t$. Thus no constant $C > 1$ works.

**Conclusion.** Taking $C = 1 > 0$, the inequality holds for all complex $z_1, z
_2$, and this is the best possible constant.
