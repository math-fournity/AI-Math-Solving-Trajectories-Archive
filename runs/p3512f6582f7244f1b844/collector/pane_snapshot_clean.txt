## Proof

**Key observation.** For any polynomial $p(x) = \sum_n a_n x^n$, the operator $L
$ extracts exactly the even part:
$$Lp(x) = \sum_n a_{2n} x^{2n} = \frac{p(x) + p(-x)}{2} =: e(x).$$

**Upper bound: $\|L\| \leq 1$.**

By the triangle inequality, for every $t \in [-1,1]$:
$$|e(t)| = \left|\frac{p(t)+p(-t)}{2}\right| \leq \frac{|p(t)|+|p(-t)|}{2}.$$

Integrating over $[-1,1]$:
$$\|Lp\| = \|e\| = \int_{-1}^{1}|e(t)|\,dt \leq \int_{-1}^{1}\frac{|p(t)|+|p(-t)
|}{2}\,dt.$$

By the substitution $u = -t$ in the second term, $\int_{-1}^{1}|p(-t)|\,dt = \in
t_{-1}^{1}|p(u)|\,du = \|p\|$. Hence
$$\|Lp\| \leq \frac{1}{2}\bigl(\|p\| + \|p\|\bigr) = \|p\|.$$

This holds for every polynomial $p$, so $L$ is bounded and $\|L\| \leq 1$.

**Lower bound: $\|L\| \geq 1$.**

Take $p(x) = 1$. Then $Lp = 1$, and
$$\|Lp\| = \int_{-1}^{1}1\,dt = 2, \qquad \|p\| = \int_{-1}^{1}1\,dt = 2.$$

Thus $\|Lp\|/\|p\| = 1$, so $\|L\| \geq 1$.

**Conclusion.** Combining both bounds, $L$ is bounded and
$$\boxed{\|L\| = 1}.$$
