## Solution

**Answer-leak check:** The problem text contains only the problem statement, cha
racteristic function, and instructions. No solution, proof sketch, or answer is
embedded. Proceeding.

**Setup.** Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots$ be i.i.d. with characteristic function
$$\chi(t) = e^{-|t|^{1.9}}.$$
This is the characteristic function of a symmetric stable law with stability ind
ex $\alpha = 1.9 \in (0,2]$ (and scale $c=1$). In particular $X_k$ has a genuine
 stable distribution, so it is heavy-tailed and has infinite variance, but it is
 still a proper random variable.

**Characteristic function of the normalized sum.** Let $a_n = n^{-5/9}$ and cons
ider $a_n S_n = n^{-5/9}\sum_{k=1}^n X_k$. By independence and identical distrib
ution,
$$
\varphi_{a_n S_n}(t) = \mathbb{E}\,e^{it\,a_n S_n}
= \prod_{k=1}^n \mathbb{E}\,e^{i(t a_n) X_k}
= \bigl[\chi(t a_n)\bigr]^n.
$$

Substituting $\chi(u) = e^{-|u|^{1.9}}$ and $a_n = n^{-5/9}$:
$$
\varphi_{a_n S_n}(t)
= \exp\!\bigl(-n\,|t\,n^{-5/9}|^{1.9}\bigr)
= \exp\!\bigl(-n\cdot |t|^{1.9}\cdot n^{-(5/9)\cdot 1.9}\bigr).
$$

**Compute the exponent.** Since $1.9 = 19/10$,
$$
\frac{5}{9}\cdot 1.9 = \frac{5}{9}\cdot\frac{19}{10} = \frac{95}{90} = \frac{19}
{18}.
$$
Therefore
$$
n\cdot n^{-19/18} = n^{1 - 19/18} = n^{-1/18},
$$
and so
$$
\varphi_{a_n S_n}(t) = \exp\!\bigl(-n^{-1/18}\,|t|^{1.9}\bigr).
$$

**Take the limit.** As $n\to\infty$, $n^{-1/18}\to 0$, hence for every fixed $t\
in\mathbb{R}$,
$$
\varphi_{a_n S_n}(t) = \exp\!\bigl(-n^{-1/18}|t|^{1.9}\bigr) \;\longrightarrow\;
 e^{0} = 1.
$$

The constant function $1$ is the characteristic function of the Dirac measure at
 $0$, i.e. of the degenerate random variable identically equal to $0$.

**Why the normalization is too strong (sanity check).** For a symmetric $\alpha$
-stable law, the natural scaling that produces a non-degenerate limit is $n^{-1/
\alpha}$. Here $\alpha = 1.9$, so $1/\alpha = 10/19 \approx 0.5263$, whereas the
 problem uses $5/9 \approx 0.5556 > 10/19$. Equivalently,
$$
\frac{5}{9}\cdot\alpha = \frac{19}{18} > 1,
$$
so the factor $n^{1-(5/9)\alpha} = n^{-1/18}$ decays to $0$, and the normalized
sum collapses to $0$. Had the exponent been exactly $1/\alpha = 10/19$, we would
 have obtained the non-trivial stable limit with characteristic function $e^{-|t
|^{1.9}}$; the slightly larger exponent $5/9$ over-normalizes and forces degener
acy.

**Continuity of the limit.** The pointwise limit $1$ is continuous at $t=0$, so
by Lévy's continuity theorem it is the characteristic function of a probability
measure, namely $\delta_0$. Thus the convergence of characteristic functions gen
uinely implies weak convergence:
$$
n^{-5/9} S_n \;\xRightarrow{\;d\;}\; \delta_0.
$$

**Conclusion.** The weak limit of $n^{-5/9}S_n$ as $n\to\infty$ is the degenerat
e distribution concentrated at the origin.
