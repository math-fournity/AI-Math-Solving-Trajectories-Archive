Looking at this problem, I need to solve a minimum-time optimal control problem
for a space vehicle docking maneuver.

## Solution

### Problem Setup

The equation of motion is $\ddot{x} = U$ where $U = M\sin V$ and $|\sin V| \leq
1$, so the control constraint is $|U| \leq M$. We seek $U(t)$ minimizing $T = \i
nt_0^T 1\,dt$ subject to $x(0)=a$, $\dot{x}(0)=b$, $x(T)=0$, $\dot{x}(T)=0$.

### Applying Pontryagin's Maximum Principle

Introduce state variables $x_1 = x$, $x_2 = \dot{x}$. The state equations are:
$$\dot{x}_1 = x_2, \quad \dot{x}_2 = U, \quad |U| \leq M$$

The Hamiltonian (maximization convention) is:
$$H = p_1 x_2 + p_2 U - 1$$

**Costate equations:**
$$\dot{p}_1 = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial x_1} = 0 \implies p_1 = c_1 \text{ (co
nstant)}$$
$$\dot{p}_2 = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial x_2} = -p_1 = -c_1 \implies p_2(t) = c
_2 - c_1 t$$

So $p_2(t)$ is **linear in $t$** and changes sign at most once.

**Optimal control:** Maximizing $H$ over $U \in [-M, M]$ requires maximizing $p_
2 U$, giving:
$$U^*(t) = M\,\text{sgn}(p_2(t))$$

This is **bang-bang control**: $U = \pm M$ with **at most one switch** (since $p
_2$ is linear). The corresponding $V = \pm\pi/2$ (since $\sin V = \pm 1$).

**Transversality condition** (free terminal time, fixed terminal state): $H(T) =
 0$. Since $x_2(T) = 0$:
$$p_2(T)\,U(T) = 1 \implies |p_2(T)| = \frac{1}{M}$$
This is automatically satisfied by the boundary conditions.

### The Switching Curve

From any point on the switching curve, the origin is reached with constant $U =
\pm M$ (no further switch):

- **$U = -M$ branch** ($\dot{x} \geq 0$): Setting $\dot{x}(T)=0$ gives $T = \dot
{x}/M$; then $x(T)=0$ gives $x = -\frac{\dot{x}^2}{2M}$.
- **$U = +M$ branch** ($\dot{x} \leq 0$): Similarly $x = \frac{\dot{x}^2}{2M}$.

The complete switching curve is:
$$\Gamma:\quad x = -\frac{\dot{x}\,|\dot{x}|}{2M}$$

### Optimal Strategy

Define $\Phi = a + \frac{b|b|}{2M}$ (the signed distance of the initial state fr
om $\Gamma$).

- **$\Phi > 0$ (above $\Gamma$):** Apply $U = -M$ first, then switch to $U = +M$
.
- **$\Phi < 0$ (below $\Gamma$):** Apply $U = +M$ first, then switch to $U = -M$
.
- **$\Phi = 0$ (on $\Gamma$):** Single bang, no switch.

### Case 1: $\Phi > 0$ — $U = -M$ then $+M$

**Phase 1** ($0 \leq t \leq t_s$, $U = -M$):
$$\dot{x}(t) = b - Mt, \quad x(t) = a + bt - \tfrac{1}{2}Mt^2$$

**Phase 2** ($t_s \leq t \leq T$, $U = +M$): Integrating from $t_s$ with continu
ity.

**Terminal conditions** $x(T) = 0$, $\dot{x}(T) = 0$ yield:

From $\dot{x}(T) = 0$: $\;T = 2t_s - \frac{b}{M}$

From $x(T) = 0$ (substituting and simplifying): $\;Mt_s^2 - 2bt_s + \frac{b^2}{2
M} - a = 0$

Solving (taking the root with $t_s > 0$ and $T - t_s > 0$):

$$\boxed{t_s = \frac{b + \sqrt{\frac{b^2}{2} + Ma}}{M}, \qquad T = \frac{b + 2\s
qrt{\frac{b^2}{2} + Ma}}{M}}$$

The optimal thrust:
$$U^*(t) = \begin{cases} -M & 0 \leq t \leq t_s \\ +M & t_s < t \leq T \end{case
s}$$

### Case 2: $\Phi < 0$ — $U = +M$ then $-M$

By symmetric analysis (Phase 1 with $U=+M$, Phase 2 with $U=-M$):

From $\dot{x}(T) = 0$: $\;T = 2t_s + \frac{b}{M}$

From $x(T) = 0$: $\;Mt_s^2 + 2bt_s + a + \frac{b^2}{2M} = 0$

Solving:

$$\boxed{t_s = \frac{-b + \sqrt{\frac{b^2}{2} - Ma}}{M}, \qquad T = \frac{-b + 2
\sqrt{\frac{b^2}{2} - Ma}}{M}}$$

The optimal thrust:
$$U^*(t) = \begin{cases} +M & 0 \leq t \leq t_s \\ -M & t_s < t \leq T \end{case
s}$$

### On the Switching Curve: $\Phi = 0$, $b \neq 0$

No switch is needed. The single-bang control $U = -M\,\text{sgn}(b)$ brings the
vehicle to rest at the origin in:

$$T = \frac{|b|}{M}$$

(This is the limit of both cases as $\Phi \to 0$: the square root vanishes and $
t_s \to T$.)

### Verification

**Example** ($a > 0$, $b = 0$): $\Phi = a > 0$, so Case 1 applies.
$$t_s = \frac{\sqrt{Ma}}{M} = \sqrt{\frac{a}{M}}, \quad T = \frac{2\sqrt{Ma}}{M}
 = 2\sqrt{\frac{a}{M}}$$

Phase 1 ($U = -M$): At $t_s$, $x = a - \frac{M}{2}\cdot\frac{a}{M} = \frac{a}{2}
$, $\dot{x} = -\sqrt{aM}$.
Phase 2 ($U = +M$): At $T = 2t_s$, $\dot{x} = -\sqrt{aM} + M\sqrt{\frac{a}{M}} =
 0$ ✓, and $x = \frac{a}{2} - \sqrt{aM}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{a}{M}} + \frac{M}{2}\cdo
t\frac{a}{M} = \frac{a}{2} - a + \frac{a}{2} = 0$ ✓.

The vehicle accelerates toward the origin for half the time, then decelerates fo
r the other half — physically optimal.

### Sufficiency

The system $\dot{x}_1 = x_2$, $\dot{x}_2 = U$ is controllable, the control set $
[-M, M]$ is convex and compact, and the target is a single point. An optimal con
trol exists. The PMP necessary conditions uniquely determine the bang-bang struc
ture (at most one switch, since $p_2$ is linear), and the switching time and ter
minal time are uniquely determined by the four boundary conditions. Therefore th
e bang-bang solution is the unique optimal control.

### Summary

The optimal thrust is **bang-bang** with at most one switching:

$$U^*(t) = M\,\text{sgn}(p_2(t)), \quad p_2(t) = c_2 - c_1 t$$

The switching curve in the phase plane is $x = -\frac{\dot{x}|\dot{x}|}{2M}$. Th
e minimum time and switching time are given by the explicit formulas above, sele
cted by the sign of $\Phi = a + \frac{b|b|}{2M}$.
