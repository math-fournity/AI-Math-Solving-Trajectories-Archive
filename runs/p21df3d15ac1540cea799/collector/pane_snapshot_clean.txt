# Proof

**Claim:** The direct product of a free group of finite rank with a finite group
 is Hopfian.

**Notation.** Let $F_n$ be the free group of finite rank $n \ge 0$, and let $K$
be a finite group. We show $G = F_n \times K$ is Hopfian.

We use Mal'cev's classical theorem:

> **Theorem (Mal'cev).** Every finitely generated residually finite group is Hop
fian.

So it suffices to verify the two hypotheses for $G$.

---

**Step 1: $G$ is finitely generated.**

$F_n$ is generated by $n$ elements (a finite set, since $n$ is finite). $K$ is f
inite, hence finitely generated (e.g. by all of its elements). The direct produc
t of two finitely generated groups is finitely generated: if $F_n = \langle S \r
angle$ and $K = \langle T \rangle$, then $F_n \times K = \langle (S \times \{1\}
) \cup (\{1\} \times T) \rangle$. Hence $G$ is finitely generated.

---

**Step 2: $G$ is residually finite.**

Recall that a group is *residually finite* if for every nontrivial element there
 exists a homomorphism to a finite group that does not kill it.

- $F_n$ is residually finite. This is a standard fact: free groups are residuall
y finite (one proof maps a reduced word to a suitable symmetric group via the pe
rmutation action on cosets, or uses the residual finiteness of free products of
cyclic groups).

- $K$ is finite, hence trivially residually finite (the identity map $K \to K$ i
s a homomorphism to a finite group killing no nontrivial element).

- The direct product of two residually finite groups is residually finite. Indee
d, let $(g, h) \in F_n \times K$ with $(g, h) \ne (1, 1)$. Then either $g \ne 1$
 or $h \ne 1$.
  - If $g \ne 1$: since $F_n$ is residually finite, there is a finite group $Q$
and a homomorphism $\varphi: F_n \to Q$ with $\varphi(g) \ne 1$. Then the compos
ition
    $$F_n \times K \xrightarrow{\;\pi_1\;} F_n \xrightarrow{\;\varphi\;} Q$$
    is a homomorphism to a finite group sending $(g, h) \mapsto \varphi(g) \ne 1
$.
  - If $h \ne 1$: since $K$ is finite, the identity map $K \to K$ sends $h \maps
to h \ne 1$, and the composition
    $$F_n \times K \xrightarrow{\;\pi_2\;} K \hookrightarrow K$$
    is a homomorphism to a finite group sending $(g, h) \mapsto h \ne 1$.

  In either case the nontrivial element survives in some finite quotient. Hence
$G$ is residually finite.

---

**Step 3: Apply Mal'cev's theorem.**

Since $G = F_n \times K$ is both finitely generated (Step 1) and residually fini
te (Step 2), Mal'cev's theorem implies that $G$ is Hopfian: every surjective end
omorphism of $G$ is an automorphism.

---

**Conclusion.** Yes — the direct product of a free group of finite rank with a f
inite group is Hopfian, being a finitely generated residually finite group and t
herefore Hopfian by Mal'cev's theorem.
