## Proof

Let $E = B - A$. Since $A$ and $B$ are real symmetric and $B$ differs from $A$ o
nly in (symmetric pairs of) off‑diagonal entries, $E$ is real symmetric with $\|
E\|$ arbitrarily small (the entries are "sufficiently small"). Let $r = \operato
rname{rank}(A) = \operatorname{rank}(B)$.

Let the eigenvalues of $A$, counted with multiplicity and ordered, be
$$\lambda_1(A) \le \lambda_2(A) \le \cdots \le \lambda_n(A).$$
Write $p = \#\{i:\lambda_i(A)>0\}$, $q = \#\{i:\lambda_i(A)<0\}$, so $r = p+q$ a
nd $z = n-r$ eigenvalues are exactly $0$. Since $A$ has finitely many nonzero ei
genvalues, there is a spectral gap
$$\delta := \min\bigl(|\lambda_q(A)|,\ \lambda_{q+z+1}(A)\bigr) > 0,$$
i.e. $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_q \le -\delta$ and $\lambda_{q+z+1},\dots,\lambda_
n \ge \delta$.

By Weyl's inequality for Hermitian matrices,
$$|\lambda_i(B) - \lambda_i(A)| \le \|B-A\| = \|E\| \qquad \text{for all } i.$$
Choose the perturbation small enough that $\|E\| < \delta/2$ (this is permitted
by the hypothesis "sufficiently small"). Then:

- For $i \le q$: $\lambda_i(B) \le \lambda_i(A) + \|E\| \le -\delta + \delta/2 =
 -\delta/2 < 0$, so $B$ has **at least** $q$ negative eigenvalues.
- For $i \ge q+z+1$: $\lambda_i(B) \ge \lambda_i(A) - \|E\| \ge \delta - \delta/
2 = \delta/2 > 0$, so $B$ has **at least** $p = n-(q+z)$ positive eigenvalues.

Thus $B$ has at least $p$ positive and at least $q$ negative eigenvalues, hence
at least $p+q = r$ nonzero eigenvalues. But $\operatorname{rank}(B) = r$, so $B$
 has **exactly** $r$ nonzero eigenvalues. Therefore $B$ has exactly $p$ positive
 and exactly $q$ negative eigenvalues.

So the signature $(p,q)$ of $B$ equals the signature $(p,q)$ of $A$.

**Answer: Yes, $B$ has the same signature as $A$.**
