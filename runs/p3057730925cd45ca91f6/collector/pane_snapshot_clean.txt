## Proof

Let $G$ be a reductive group over $k$ ($\operatorname{char} k = 0$), $T \subset
B$ a maximal torus and Borel, $\Phi^+$ the positive roots determined by $B$, and
 $\rho = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha \in \Phi^+} \alpha$.

### 1. The fiber of $\omega_{G/B}$ at the base point

The flag variety $G/B$ is homogeneous, so it suffices to compute the $B$-charact
er of the fiber of the canonical bundle at the base point $eB$. We have

$$
T_{eB}(G/B) \;\cong\; \mathfrak g / \mathfrak b
\;\cong\; \bigoplus_{\alpha \in \Phi^+} \mathfrak g_{-\alpha},
$$

since $\mathfrak b = \mathfrak t \oplus \bigoplus_{\alpha>0} \mathfrak g_\alpha$
 and $\mathfrak g = \mathfrak t \oplus \bigoplus_{\alpha>0}(\mathfrak g_\alpha \
oplus \mathfrak g_{-\alpha})$. Under the adjoint action of $T$, each root space
$\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}$ has weight $-\alpha$. Hence

$$
\det T_{eB}(G/B) \;\text{ has } T\text{-weight } \sum_{\alpha>0}(-\alpha) = -2\r
ho.
$$

The cotangent space is the dual, so $\Omega^1_{G/B}\big|_{eB} \cong (\mathfrak g
/\mathfrak b)^* \cong \bigoplus_{\alpha>0} \mathfrak g_\alpha^*$, whose weights
are $\{+\alpha : \alpha \in \Phi^+\}$ (the dual of a weight $-\alpha$ space is a
 weight $+\alpha$ space). Therefore

$$
\omega_{G/B}\big|_{eB} \;=\; \det \Omega^1_{G/B}\big|_{eB}
\quad\text{has } T\text{-weight}\quad \sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^+}\alpha \;=\; 2\rho.
$$

Equivalently, as a homogeneous line bundle with the literal $B$-action on the fi
ber,

$$
\omega_{G/B} \;\cong\; G\times_B k_{2\rho}.
$$

### 2. The convention that produces the sign

The standard Borel–Weil notation $\mathcal L(\lambda)$ (Jantzen, Springer, …) is
 set up so that

$$
H^0(G/B,\mathcal L(\lambda)) \;\cong\; \nabla(\lambda) \quad(\text{highest weigh
t } \lambda),
$$

which forces the definition $\mathcal L(\lambda) := G\times_B k_{-\lambda}$, i.e
. the $B$-character on the *fiber* is $-\lambda$. (Indeed, sections of $G\times_
B k_\mu$ are $f:G\to k$ with $f(gb)=\mu(b)^{-1}f(g)$; setting $\mu=-\lambda$ giv
es $f(gb)=\lambda(b)f(g)$, the induced module $\operatorname{Ind}_B^G\lambda$ of
 highest weight $\lambda$.)

Comparing with §1, the fiber character of $\omega_{G/B}$ is $2\rho = -\lambda$,
so $\lambda = -2\rho$:

$$
\boxed{\;\omega_{G/B}\;\cong\;\mathcal L(-2\rho)\;}
$$

and the anticanonical bundle is $\omega_{G/B}^{-1}\cong\mathcal L(2\rho)$, which
 is ample — consistent with $G/B$ being a Fano variety of index $2\rho$.

### 3. Resolution of the $-2\rho$ vs $2\rho$ confusion

Both "$2\rho$" and "$-2\rho$" appear in the literature, and *both are correct in
 their respective conventions*:

- If one indexes the homogeneous line bundle by the **$B$-character on the fiber
** (writing $\mathcal M(\mu)=G\times_B k_\mu$), then $\omega_{G/B}=\mathcal M(2\
rho)$: the answer is **$2\rho$**.
- If one uses the **Borel–Weil weight** $\lambda$ (writing $\mathcal L(\lambda)$
 with $H^0(G/B,\mathcal L(\lambda))=\nabla(\lambda)$, the convention dominant in
 representation theory and algebraic geometry), then $\mathcal L(\lambda)=G\time
s_B k_{-\lambda}$ and $\omega_{G/B}=\mathcal L(-2\rho)$: the answer is **$-2\rho
$**.

The two are related by $\mu = -\lambda$; the apparent contradiction is purely a
sign convention. In the standard Borel–Weil convention — which is the one in whi
ch the question "is it $-2\rho$ or $2\rho$?" is normally posed — the canonical b
undle is indexed by

$$
\lambda = -2\rho.
$$
