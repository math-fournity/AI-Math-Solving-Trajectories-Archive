## Proof

Let $J = \operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ and let $A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d
\end{pmatrix} \in SL(2,\mathbb{R})$, so $ad - bc = 1$.

The condition $A^T J A = J$ gives
$$
\begin{pmatrix} a & c \\ b & d \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end
{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} a^2 - c^
2 & ab - cd \\ ab - cd & b^2 - d^2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 &
-1 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

This yields three equations:
1. $a^2 - c^2 = 1$,
2. $ab - cd = 0$,
3. $b^2 - d^2 = -1$,

together with $\det A = ad - bc = 1$.

From (1), $a^2 = 1 + c^2 \geq 1$, so $a \neq 0$. From (2), $b = \frac{cd}{a}$. S
ubstituting into (3):
$$
\frac{c^2 d^2}{a^2} - d^2 = -1 \implies d^2\!\left(\frac{c^2 - a^2}{a^2}\right)
= -1 \implies d^2 \cdot \frac{-1}{a^2} = -1 \implies d^2 = a^2,
$$
so $d = \pm a$.

- **If $d = a$**: then $b = c$, and $\det A = a^2 - c^2 = 1$ ✓ (consistent with
(1)).
- **If $d = -a$**: then $b = -c$, and $\det A = -a^2 + c^2 = -(a^2 - c^2) = -1$
✗ (violates $\det A = 1$).

Hence every element of $S$ has the form
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} a & c \\ c & a \end{pmatrix}, \qquad a^2 - c^2 = 1.
$$

Conversely, every such matrix satisfies $A^T J A = J$ and $\det A = a^2 - c^2 =
1$, so
$$
S = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a & c \\ c & a \end{pmatrix} : a^2 - c^2 = 1 \right\
}.
$$

The set $\{(a,c) \in \mathbb{R}^2 : a^2 - c^2 = 1\}$ is a hyperbola with **two b
ranches**:
- **Branch 1** ($a \geq 1$): parametrized by $a = \cosh t,\; c = \sinh t$, $t \i
n \mathbb{R}$,
- **Branch 2** ($a \leq -1$): parametrized by $a = -\cosh t,\; c = \sinh t$, $t
\in \mathbb{R}$.

The map $(a,c) \mapsto \begin{pmatrix} a & c \\ c & a \end{pmatrix}$ is a homeom
orphism from the hyperbola onto $S$ (with the induced topology from $SL(2,\mathb
b{R}) \subset \mathbb{R}^4$). Each branch is the continuous image of $\mathbb{R}
$, hence connected. The two branches are separated by the region $|a| < 1$, whic
h contains no points of $S$, so they are disjoint open-and-closed subsets of $S$
.

Therefore $S$ has **2 connected components** (one containing $I$, the other cont
aining $-I$).
