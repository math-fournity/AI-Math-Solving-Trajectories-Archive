## Solution

Let $M_t = \int_0^t |B_s|\,dB_s$. This is a well-defined Itô integral since $|B_
s|$ is adapted and square-integrable:
$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^1 |B_s|^2\,ds\right] = \int_0^1 \mathbb{E}[B_s^2]\,ds
 = \int_0^1 s\,ds = \tfrac{1}{2} < \infty.$$

**Applying Itô's formula to $M_t^2$.** Since $M_t$ is an Itô process with
$$dM_t = |B_t|\,dB_t, \qquad d\langle M\rangle_t = |B_t|^2\,dt = B_t^2\,dt,$$
Itô's formula for $f(x)=x^2$ (so $f'(x)=2x$, $f''(x)=2$) gives
$$d(M_t^2) = 2M_t\,dM_t + d\langle M\rangle_t = 2M_t|B_t|\,dB_t + B_t^2\,dt.$$

Integrating from $0$ to $1$ (with $M_0=0$):
$$M_1^2 = 2\int_0^1 M_s|B_s|\,dB_s + \int_0^1 B_s^2\,ds.$$

**Taking expectation.** The stochastic integral $\int_0^1 M_s|B_s|\,dB_s$ is a m
artingale (the integrand is adapted and square-integrable), so its expectation i
s $0$. Therefore
$$\mathbb{E}[M_1^2] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^1 B_s^2\,ds\right] = \int_0^1 \ma
thbb{E}[B_s^2]\,ds.$$

Since $B_s \sim \mathcal{N}(0,s)$, we have $\mathbb{E}[B_s^2]=s$, hence
$$\mathbb{E}[M_1^2] = \int_0^1 s\,ds = \frac{1}{2}.$$

$$\boxed{\mathbb{E}\!\left(\int_0^1 |B_s|\,dB_s\right)^{\!2} = \frac{1}{2}}$$
