# Problem

In a futuristic energy-harvesting facility, a central generator’s power stability is governed by a specific efficiency coefficient, $x$. For the system to reach a state of perfect equilibrium, the total energy flux must equal zero.

The flux is calculated through a combination of five distinct subsystems:
1. The primary fusion output is determined by eighty-one times the fifth power of the efficiency coefficient ($81x^5$).
2. The mechanical drag reduces the flux by eighteen times the square of the coefficient ($-18x^2$).
3. The thermal dissipation further subtracts ninety-eight times the coefficient ($-98x$).
4. Finally, a constant quantum leakage of fourteen times the square root of two units ($14\sqrt{2}$) must be subtracted from the total.

Engineers have determined that equilibrium is achieved when these four components sum exactly to zero. Find at least one real value for the efficiency coefficient $x$ that satisfies this equilibrium.
