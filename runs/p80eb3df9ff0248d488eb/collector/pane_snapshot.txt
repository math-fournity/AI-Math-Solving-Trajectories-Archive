## Proof

**Setup.** The regular dodecahedron has 20 vertices, 30 edges, and 12 pentagonal
 faces. We work over the field $\mathbb{F}_2$. Assign to each edge $e$ a variabl
e $x_e \in \mathbb{F}_2$ (1 = painted red, 0 = unpainted). The condition "each f
ace has an even number of painted edges" becomes a system of 12 linear equations
 over $\mathbb{F}_2$:

$$\sum_{e \in \partial f} x_e = 0 \pmod{2}, \quad \text{for each face } f,$$

where $\partial f$ is the set of 5 edges bounding face $f$. The number of valid
colorings equals $2^{30 - r}$, where $r$ is the rank (over $\mathbb{F}_2$) of th
e $12 \times 30$ face–edge incidence matrix $M$.

**Identifying the row space with the cycle space.** Each row of $M$ is the chara
cteristic vector of the boundary edges of a face — i.e., a face cycle in the dod
ecahedron graph $G$. The row space of $M$ is therefore the subspace of $\mathbb{
F}_2^{30}$ spanned by all 12 face cycles.

The dodecahedron graph $G$ is planar (by Steinitz's theorem, it is a 3-connected
 planar graph). For a connected planar graph with a 2-cell embedding on the sphe
re, the face boundary cycles generate the **entire cycle space** $\mathcal{C}(G)
$. The cycle space consists of all Eulerian subgraphs (edge sets in which every
vertex has even degree) and has dimension

$$\dim \mathcal{C}(G) = |E| - |V| + 1 = 30 - 20 + 1 = 11.$$

**The single relation.** The 12 face cycles satisfy exactly one linear relation
over $\mathbb{F}_2$: since every edge lies on exactly 2 faces, the sum (mod 2) o
f all 12 face-cycle vectors is the zero vector. This is the unique relation, con
sistent with $12 - 1 = 11 = \dim \mathcal{C}(G)$. Hence

$$\operatorname{rank}(M) = \dim \mathcal{C}(G) = 11.$$

**Counting solutions.** The valid colorings are exactly the vectors $x \in \math
bb{F}_2^{30}$ orthogonal to every row of $M$, i.e., orthogonal to the cycle spac
e. By the fundamental theorem of algebraic graph theory over $\mathbb{F}_2$, the
 orthogonal complement of the cycle space is the **cut space** $\mathcal{B}(G)$,
 which has dimension

$$\dim \mathcal{B}(G) = |V| - 1 = 20 - 1 = 19.$$

(Equivalently, $\dim \mathcal{B}(G) = |E| - \dim \mathcal{C}(G) = 30 - 11 = 19$.
)

**Verification of the orthogonal-complement claim.** Every cut $\delta(S)$ (edge
s between a vertex subset $S$ and its complement) crosses every cycle an even nu
mber of times, so every cut vector is orthogonal to every face cycle — cuts are
valid colorings. Conversely, any edge set orthogonal to all face cycles is ortho
gonal to the entire cycle space, hence lies in the cut space. So the valid color
ings are precisely the elements of the cut space.

**Conclusion.** The number of valid colorings is

$$|\mathcal{B}(G)| = 2^{19} = 524288.$$
