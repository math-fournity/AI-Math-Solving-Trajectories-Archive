# Hamilton's Ray Equations for Wave Propagation in an Inhomogeneous Medium

## Setup

Consider a wave field in an inhomogeneous medium described locally by a plane-wa
ve ansatz

$$
u(\mathbf{x},t) = A(\mathbf{x},t)\, e^{i S(\mathbf{x},t)/\epsilon},
$$

where $S(\mathbf{x},t)$ is the **eikonal** (phase function) and $\epsilon$ is a
small parameter (e.g., a short wavelength scale). We define the local wavevector
 and frequency as

$$
\mathbf{k}(\mathbf{x},t) = \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} S, \qquad \omega(\mathbf{x},t) =
-\partial_t S.
$$

These definitions guarantee the **consistency (integrability) conditions**

$$
\partial_t k_i = -\partial_{x_i} \omega, \qquad \nabla \times \mathbf{k} = \math
bf{0},
$$

which follow from the equality of mixed partial derivatives of $S$.

The medium is characterized by a **dispersion relation**

$$
\omega = \omega(\mathbf{k}, \mathbf{x}),
$$

which encodes all the local physics (e.g., for an isotropic medium with refracti
ve index $n(\mathbf{x})$, $\omega = c\,|\mathbf{k}|\,/n(\mathbf{x})$). Substitut
ing the definitions of $\mathbf{k}$ and $\omega$ into the dispersion relation yi
elds the **Hamilton–Jacobi equation**

$$
\partial_t S + \omega\!\left(\nabla S,\, \mathbf{x}\right) = 0.
$$

## Derivation of the Ray Equations

We treat $(\mathbf{k}, \mathbf{x})$ as canonically conjugate variables and $\ome
ga(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})$ as the **Hamiltonian**. The ray equations are obtaine
d by differentiating the dispersion relation and using the integrability conditi
ons.

### Evolution of $\mathbf{x}$

Differentiate the dispersion relation $\omega = \omega(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})$ w
ith respect to $k_j$ along a ray. Equivalently, consider the total rate of chang
e of $x_j$. From the Hamilton–Jacobi equation, the **group velocity** is

$$
\frac{d x_j}{dt} = \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial k_j}.
$$

**Justification.** The characteristics of the Hamilton–Jacobi PDE $\partial_t S
+ H(\nabla S, \mathbf{x})=0$ are, by standard Hamilton–Jacobi theory, given by $
\dot{x}_j = \partial H/\partial k_j$ with $H = \omega$. Physically, energy and w
ave packets travel at the **group velocity** $\mathbf{v}_g = \nabla_{\mathbf{k}}
\,\omega$.

### Evolution of $\mathbf{k}$

Take the partial derivative of the dispersion relation with respect to $x_j$:

$$
\frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x_j} = \sum_i \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial k_
i}\frac{\partial k_i}{\partial x_j} + \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x_j}\Big|_
{\mathbf{k}}.
$$

Using the integrability condition $\partial k_i/\partial x_j = \partial k_j/\par
tial x_i$ and $\partial_t k_j = -\partial \omega/\partial x_j$ (from $\partial_t
 \mathbf{k} = -\nabla \omega$ combined with the chain rule through the dispersio
n relation holding $\mathbf{k}$ fixed), we obtain

$$
\frac{d k_j}{dt} = -\frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x_j}.
$$

**Detailed step.** From $\partial_t k_j = -\partial_{x_j}\omega(\mathbf{x},t)$ a
nd the dispersion relation, holding $\mathbf{k}$ fixed:

$$
\partial_t k_j = -\frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x_j}\Big|_{\mathbf{k}}.
$$

Along a ray, $d k_j/dt = \partial_t k_j + (\dot{\mathbf{x}}\cdot\nabla) k_j$. Us
ing $\dot{x}_i = \partial\omega/\partial k_i$ and the irrotationality $\partial_
i k_j = \partial_j k_i$:

$$
(\dot{\mathbf{x}}\cdot\nabla)k_j = \sum_i \frac{\partial\omega}{\partial k_i}\fr
ac{\partial k_j}{\partial x_i} = \sum_i \frac{\partial\omega}{\partial k_i}\frac
{\partial k_i}{\partial x_j} = \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x_j}\Big|_{\mathb
f{k}} - \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x_j}\Big|_{\text{total}}.
$$

Since along the ray $\omega$ is evaluated at the local $(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})$
, the total $\partial\omega/\partial x_j$ at fixed $k$ cancels, leaving

$$
\frac{d k_j}{dt} = -\frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x_j}\Big|_{\mathbf{k}}.
$$

## Result: Hamilton's Ray Equations

Collecting both equations, the wavevector and position evolve according to the *
*canonical Hamiltonian system**

$$
\boxed{\;
\frac{d\mathbf{x}}{dt} = \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial \mathbf{k}}, \qquad
\frac{d\mathbf{k}}{dt} = -\frac{\partial \omega}{\partial \mathbf{x}}.
\;}
$$

In component form ($j = 1,2,3$):

$$
\frac{d x_j}{dt} = \frac{\partial \omega(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})}{\partial k_j},
\qquad
\frac{d k_j}{dt} = -\frac{\partial \omega(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})}{\partial x_j}.
$$

## Interpretation

- **Position equation**: The ray (energy packet) travels at the **group velocity
** $\mathbf{v}_g = \nabla_{\mathbf{k}}\omega$. In a homogeneous medium where $\o
mega$ is independent of $\mathbf{x}$, $\mathbf{k}$ is conserved and rays are str
aight lines.

- **Wavevector equation**: The wavevector changes in response to spatial inhomog
eneity. The term $-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\omega$ acts as a **force** bending the ra
y—this is the mechanism behind refraction. In an isotropic medium with $\omega =
 c|\mathbf{k}|/n(\mathbf{x})$, this reproduces the familiar Snell's law behavior
: rays bend toward regions of higher refractive index.

- **Hamiltonian structure**: $\omega(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})$ plays the role of t
he Hamiltonian, $\mathbf{k}$ the canonical momentum, and $\mathbf{x}$ the canoni
cal coordinate. The conservation law

$$
\frac{d\omega}{dt} = \frac{\partial\omega}{\partial\mathbf{k}}\cdot\dot{\mathbf{
k}} + \frac{\partial\omega}{\partial\mathbf{x}}\cdot\dot{\mathbf{x}} = \nabla_{\
mathbf{k}}\omega\cdot(-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\omega) + \nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\omega\cd
ot\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}\omega = 0
$$

shows that **frequency is conserved along a ray** (in a stationary medium), whic
h is the ray-theoretic statement of energy conservation.

Starting from the eikonal $S$ with $\mathbf{k}=\nabla S$ and $\omega=-\partial_t
 S$, the dispersion relation $\omega=\omega(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})$ becomes a Ha
milton–Jacobi equation. Its characteristics yield Hamilton's ray equations: posi
tion advances at the group velocity $\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}\omega$, while the wavev
