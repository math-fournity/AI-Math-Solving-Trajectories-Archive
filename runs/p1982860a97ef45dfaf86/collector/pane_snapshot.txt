### Goal

Find all $n \in \{1, \dots, 999\}$ with $\varphi(n) = n/3$, i.e. $\varphi(n)/n =
 1/3$.

### Characterizing the solutions

Using the product formula
$$\frac{\varphi(n)}{n} = \prod_{p \mid n} \left(1 - \frac{1}{p}\right) = \prod_{
p \mid n} \frac{p-1}{p},$$
the condition $\varphi(n)/n = 1/3$ is equivalent to
$$\prod_{p \mid n} \frac{p}{p-1} = 3,$$
where the product ranges over the **distinct** prime divisors of $n$.

Let $S$ be the set of distinct primes dividing $n$. We need $\prod_{p \in S} \fr
ac{p}{p-1} = 3$, i.e.
$$\prod_{p \in S} p \;=\; 3 \prod_{p \in S} (p-1). \tag{$\star$}$$

**Case 1: $2 \notin S$ (all prime divisors odd).** Then $\prod_{p\in S} p$ is od
d, while $\prod_{p\in S}(p-1)$ is divisible by $2^{|S|}$ (each $p-1$ is even). T
he right side of $(\star)$ is divisible by $2^{|S|}$, but the left side is odd.
Equality forces $|S|=0$, giving an empty product $=1 \neq 3$. **No solutions.**

**Case 2: $2 \in S$.** The factor from $p=2$ is $2/(2-1)=2$, so the remaining od
d primes must contribute $3/2$:
$$\prod_{\substack{p \in S \\ p \text{ odd}}} \frac{p}{p-1} = \frac{3}{2}.$$

- **Subcase 2a: $3 \in S$.** The single factor $3/(3-1) = 3/2$ already gives $3/
2$. Adding any further odd prime $q \geq 5$ multiplies by $q/(q-1) > 1$, oversho
oting $3/2$. So $S = \{2, 3\}$ exactly. ✓

- **Subcase 2b: $3 \notin S$, all odd primes $\geq 5$.** We need $\prod p/(p-1)
= 3/2$ with primes $p \geq 5$. Rewrite as $2\prod p = 3\prod(p-1)$. The left sid
e $2\prod p$ is $2 \times (\text{odd})$, so it is divisible by exactly $2^1$. Th
e right side $3\prod(p-1)$ is divisible by $2^{|S|-1}\cdot 3$ (each $p-1$ for od
d $p$ is even). Equality requires $2^{|S|-1} \mid 2$, so $|S|-1 \leq 1$, i.e. $|
S| \leq 2$.
  - $|S|=1$: one prime $p\geq 5$ with $p/(p-1)=3/2 \Rightarrow p=3$, contradicti
on.
  - $|S|=2$: primes $p,q \geq 5$ with $\frac{p}{p-1}\cdot\frac{q}{q-1}=\frac{3}{
2}$. Then $2pq = 3(p-1)(q-1) = 3(pq - p - q + 1)$, so $2pq = 3pq - 3p - 3q + 3$,
 giving $pq - 3p - 3q + 3 = 0$, i.e. $(p-3)(q-3) = 6$. With $p,q \geq 5$ (so $p-
3, q-3 \geq 2$): factor pairs of $6$ with both parts $\geq 2$: only $2 \times 3$
, giving $\{p,q\} = \{5, 6\}$ — but $6$ is not prime. **No solutions.**

**Conclusion:** The only $n$ with $\varphi(n) = n/3$ are $n = 2^a \cdot 3^b$ wit
h $a \geq 1,\; b \geq 1$.
