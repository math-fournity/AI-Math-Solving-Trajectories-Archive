Let $r(x)=\sqrt{f^2(x)+g^2(x)}$ and define the complex-valued function
$$h(x)=f(x)+i\,g(x)=r\,e^{i\theta},$$
where $\theta=\arg h$. The given system becomes
$$h' = f'+ig' = -\frac{f}{r}+i\!\left(1-\frac{g}{r}\right)=i-\frac{f+ig}{r}=i-\f
rac{h}{|h|}=i-e^{i\theta}.$$

Writing $h'=r'e^{i\theta}+ir\theta'e^{i\theta}$ and dividing by $e^{i\theta}$:
$$r'+ir\theta'=-1+ie^{-i\theta}=-1+\sin\theta+i\cos\theta.$$

Equating real and imaginary parts:
$$r'=-1+\sin\theta, \qquad r\theta'=\cos\theta. \tag{1}$$

**First integral.** Dividing the two equations,
$$\frac{dr}{d\theta}=\frac{r(-1+\sin\theta)}{\cos\theta}\implies \frac{dr}{r}=\l
eft(-\sec\theta+\tan\theta\right)d\theta.$$

Integrating,
$$\ln r=-\ln|\sec\theta+\tan\theta|-\ln|\cos\theta|+C.$$

Using $\sec\theta+\tan\theta=\frac{1+\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$,
$$\ln r=-\ln(1+\sin\theta)+\ln|\cos\theta|-\ln|\cos\theta|+C=-\ln(1+\sin\theta)+
C.$$

Hence
$$r(1+\sin\theta)=K.\tag{2}$$

At $x=0$: $f(0)=10,\ g(0)=0$, so $r(0)=10,\ \theta(0)=0$, giving $K=10\cdot 1=10
$. Therefore
$$r=\frac{10}{1+\sin\theta},\qquad g=r\sin\theta=\frac{10\sin\theta}{1+\sin\thet
a},\qquad f=r\cos\theta=\frac{10\cos\theta}{1+\sin\theta}.\tag{3}$$

**Limit.** The hypothesis $\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=0$ forces $\cos\theta\to 0$. Fr
om (1), $\theta'=\cos\theta/r>0$ near $\theta=0$, and at $x=0$, $\theta'=1/10>0$
, so $\theta$ increases from $0$; thus $\theta\to\pi/2$ (not $-\pi/2$). Conseque
ntly
$$\lim_{x\to\infty}g(x)=\frac{10\sin(\pi/2)}{1+\sin(\pi/2)}=\frac{10\cdot 1}{1+1
}=5.$$

**Consistency check.** As $\theta\to\pi/2$, set $\varepsilon=\pi/2-\theta\to 0^+
$. Then $\cos\theta\approx\varepsilon$, $1+\sin\theta\approx 2$, and from (1),
$$\theta'=\frac{\cos\theta\,(1+\sin\theta)}{10}\approx\frac{2\varepsilon}{10}=\f
rac{\varepsilon}{5},$$
so $d\varepsilon/dx\approx -\varepsilon/5$: $\varepsilon$ decays exponentially a
nd $\theta=\pi/2$ is reached only at $x=\infty$. Likewise $r'=-1+\sin\theta\to 0
$, so $r\to 5$ is consistent. All conditions are satisfied.

$$\boxed{\lim_{x\to\infty}g(x)=5}$$
