## Proof

**Notation.** For an ideal $I \subseteq A$, its *extension* is $I^e = f(I)B \sub
seteq B$. For an ideal $J \subseteq B$, its *contraction* is $J^c = f^{-1}(J) \s
ubseteq A$. An ideal $I$ of $A$ is *contracted* if $I = I^{ec}$, i.e., $I = f^{-
1}(f(I)B)$.

**Key Lemma.** A prime $\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Spec}(A)$ lies in the ima
ge of $f' : \operatorname{Spec}(B) \to \operatorname{Spec}(A)$ if and only if $\
mathfrak{p}$ is contracted, i.e., $\mathfrak{p} = \mathfrak{p}^{ec}$.

*Proof of Lemma.*

($\Rightarrow$) Suppose $\mathfrak{p} = \mathfrak{q}^c = f^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})$ f
or some $\mathfrak{q} \in \operatorname{Spec}(B)$. The inclusion $\mathfrak{p} \
subseteq \mathfrak{p}^{ec}$ always holds (if $a \in \mathfrak{p}$, then $f(a) \i
n f(\mathfrak{p}) \subseteq f(\mathfrak{p})B = \mathfrak{p}^e$, so $a \in f^{-1}
(\mathfrak{p}^e) = \mathfrak{p}^{ec}$). For the reverse, since $f(\mathfrak{p})
\subseteq f(f^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})) \subseteq \mathfrak{q}$, we have $\mathfrak{p}
^e = f(\mathfrak{p})B \subseteq \mathfrak{q}$, hence
$$\mathfrak{p}^{ec} = f^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}^e) \subseteq f^{-1}(\mathfrak{q}) = \m
athfrak{p}.$$
Thus $\mathfrak{p} = \mathfrak{p}^{ec}$.

($\Leftarrow$) Suppose $\mathfrak{p} = \mathfrak{p}^{ec}$. Set $S = f(A \setminu
s \mathfrak{p})$, a multiplicative subset of $B$. We claim $S \cap \mathfrak{p}^
e = \emptyset$. Indeed, if $f(a) \in \mathfrak{p}^e = f(\mathfrak{p})B$ for some
 $a \in A \setminus \mathfrak{p}$, then $a \in f^{-1}(f(\mathfrak{p})B) = \mathf
rak{p}^{ec} = \mathfrak{p}$, contradicting $a \notin \mathfrak{p}$.

Since $\mathfrak{p}^e$ is an ideal of $B$ disjoint from the multiplicative set $
S$, by the standard prime-avoidance/localization theorem (a consequence of Zorn'
s lemma), there exists a prime $\mathfrak{q} \in \operatorname{Spec}(B)$ with $\
mathfrak{p}^e \subseteq \mathfrak{q}$ and $\mathfrak{q} \cap S = \emptyset$.

We verify $f^{-1}(\mathfrak{q}) = \mathfrak{p}$:

- **$\mathfrak{p} \subseteq f^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})$:** If $a \in \mathfrak{p}$, th
en $f(a) \in f(\mathfrak{p}) \subseteq \mathfrak{p}^e \subseteq \mathfrak{q}$, s
o $a \in f^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})$.
- **$f^{-1}(\mathfrak{q}) \subseteq \mathfrak{p}$:** If $a \in f^{-1}(\mathfrak{
q})$ and $a \notin \mathfrak{p}$, then $f(a) \in S$ and $f(a) \in \mathfrak{q}$,
 contradicting $\mathfrak{q} \cap S = \emptyset$.

Hence $\mathfrak{p} = f^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})$, so $\mathfrak{p}$ is in the image o
f $f'$. $\square$

**Conclusion.** The image of $f'$ is precisely the set of contracted prime ideal
s of $A$. Therefore, if every prime ideal of $A$ is a contracted ideal, every pr
ime of $A$ belongs to the image of $f'$, i.e., $f'$ is surjective.

The answer is **yes**.
