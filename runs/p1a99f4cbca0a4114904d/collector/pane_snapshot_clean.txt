The answer is **No**. Here is the justification.

## Analysis

Recall that for any set $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$,
$$A^\circ \subseteq A \subseteq \overline{A}, \qquad \overline{A}\setminus A^\ci
rc = \partial A.$$
Both $A^\circ$ (open) and $\overline{A}$ (closed) are Borel, hence Lebesgue meas
urable, and so is $\overline{A}\setminus A^\circ$. Since $A^\circ \subseteq \ove
rline{A}$ with the union being disjoint,
$$m(\overline{A}) = m(A^\circ) + m(\overline{A}\setminus A^\circ). \tag{$\ast$}$
$

**When $m(A^\circ)<\infty$ (equivalently $m(\overline{A})<\infty$):** the hypoth
esis $m(A^\circ)=m(\overline{A})$ lets us subtract in ($\ast$), giving $m(\overl
ine{A}\setminus A^\circ)=0$. So the implication holds in the finite-measure case
.

**When both are $+\infty$:** the equation $\infty=\infty$ carries no information
, and $m(\overline{A}\setminus A^\circ)$ may be strictly positive. The following
 is a counterexample.

## Counterexample (in $\mathbb{R}$)

Let $C\subset[0,1]$ be the **Smith–Volterra–Cantor (fat Cantor) set**: closed, n
owhere dense, with empty interior and $m(C)=\tfrac12>0$. Define
$$A := \mathbb{R}\setminus C.$$

- Since $C$ is closed, $A$ is open, so $A^\circ = A = \mathbb{R}\setminus C$. He
nce $m(A^\circ)=+\infty$.
- Since $C$ has empty interior, $A=\mathbb{R}\setminus C$ is dense in $\mathbb{R
}$, so $\overline{A}=\mathbb{R}$. Hence $m(\overline{A})=+\infty$.

Thus $m(A^\circ)=m(\overline{A})=+\infty$, satisfying the hypothesis. Yet
$$\overline{A}\setminus A^\circ \;=\; \mathbb{R}\setminus(\mathbb{R}\setminus C)
 \;=\; C,$$
so
$$m\!\bigl(\overline{A}\setminus A^\circ\bigr)=m(C)=\tfrac12\neq 0.$$

## Conclusion

The implication is **false** in general. It is true when $m(\overline{A})<\infty
$ (by additivity ($\ast$) and subtraction), but it fails when $m(A^\circ)=m(\ove
rline{A})=+\infty$, as shown by $A=\mathbb{R}\setminus C$ with $C$ a fat Cantor
set of positive measure.
