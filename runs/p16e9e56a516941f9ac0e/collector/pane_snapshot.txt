## Answer: **True**

**Proof.**

Recall that two links $L$ and $J$ in $S^3$ are **equivalent** (ambient isotopic)
 if there exists a continuous family of homeomorphisms $h_t : S^3 \to S^3$, $t \
in [0,1]$, with $h_0 = \mathrm{id}$ and $h_1(L) = J$. (By the isotopy extension
theorem, this is equivalent to the existence of an orientation-preserving homeom
orphism of $S^3$ carrying $L$ onto $J$.)

**Step 1: $n = m$.**

Since $h_1$ is a homeomorphism of $S^3$, it maps connected components of $L$ bij
ectively onto connected components of $J$. Each component $L_i$ is a connected c
omponent of $L$ (a copy of $S^1$), so $h_1(L_i)$ is a connected component of $J$
, i.e. $h_1(L_i) = J_{\sigma(i)}$ for some map $\sigma : \{1,\dots,n\} \to \{1,\
dots,m\}$. Since $h_1$ is a bijection on $S^3$, distinct components map to disti
nct components, so $\sigma$ is injective; and every component of $J$ is hit (bei
ng the image of some component of $L$ under the surjection $h_1|_L$), so $\sigma
$ is surjective. Hence $\sigma$ is a bijection and $n = m$.

**Step 2: Each $L_i$ is equivalent to $J_{\sigma(i)}$ as a knot.**

The restriction $h_1|_{L_i} : L_i \to J_{\sigma(i)}$ is a homeomorphism between
the two embedded circles, and it extends to the ambient homeomorphism $h_1 : S^3
 \to S^3$. Moreover, $h_1$ is orientation-preserving (since $h_0 = \mathrm{id}$
is orientation-preserving and orientation type is preserved along the continuous
 family $h_t$). Therefore $L_i$ and $J_{\sigma(i)}$ are ambient isotopic as knot
s, i.e. equivalent as knots.

**Conclusion.**

The bijection $\sigma$ pairs each $L_i$ with a distinct $J_{\sigma(i)}$ such tha
t $L_i \sim J_{\sigma(i)}$ as knots, and $n = m$. The statement is **true**. $\b
lacksquare$
