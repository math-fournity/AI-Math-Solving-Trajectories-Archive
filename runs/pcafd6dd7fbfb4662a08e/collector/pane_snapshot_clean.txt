**Problem.** Find all continuous linear functionals $F\colon L_1(\mathbb{R},\mat
hbb{C})\to\mathbb{C}$ satisfying $F(f*g)=F(f)F(g)$.

(Here "continuous functional" is understood in the standard Banach-space sense:
a continuous *linear* functional. The multiplicativity condition alone does not
force linearity, so linearity is part of the hypothesis.)

---

## Proof

Let $F$ be a continuous linear functional on $L_1(\mathbb{R})$ with $F(f*g)=F(f)
F(g)$ for all $f,g\in L_1(\mathbb{R})$.

**The zero functional.** $F\equiv 0$ clearly satisfies the condition. Henceforth
 assume $F\not\equiv 0$.

---

### Step 1. Behavior under translations

For $a\in\mathbb{R}$ let $(\tau_a f)(x)=f(x-a)$ be the translate. Convolution is
 translation-invariant:
$$\tau_a(f*g)=f*\tau_a g=\tau_a f*g,\qquad \tau_a f * \tau_b g=\tau_{a+b}(f*g).$
$

Pick $f_0\in L_1$ with $F(f_0)\neq 0$ and define $h(a):=F(\tau_a f_0)$. Using th
e two identities above and multiplicativity,
$$h(a)\,h(b)=F(\tau_a f_0)\,F(\tau_b f_0)=F(\tau_a f_0 * \tau_b f_0)=F(\tau_{a+b
}(f_0*f_0)).$$
Since $\tau_{a+b}(f_0*f_0)=f_0 * \tau_{a+b}f_0$, the right-hand side equals
$$F(f_0)\,F(\tau_{a+b}f_0)=F(f_0)\,h(a+b).$$
Thus $h(a)h(b)=F(f_0)\,h(a+b)$. Setting $H(a):=h(a)/F(f_0)$ gives
$$H(a+b)=H(a)H(b),\qquad H(0)=1.$$

The map $a\mapsto \tau_a f_0$ is continuous $\mathbb{R}\to L_1$ (continuity of t
ranslation in $L_1$), and $F$ is continuous, so $H$ is continuous. The only cont
inuous homomorphisms $(\mathbb{R},+)\to(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\},\cdot)$ are the
 exponentials: there exists $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$ with
$$H(a)=e^{\lambda a},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad F(\tau_a f_0)=F(f_0)\,e^{\lambda a}
.$$

**The same eigen-relation holds for every $f$.** Indeed, using $\tau_a f * f_0 =
 f * \tau_a f_0$,
$$F(\tau_a f)\,F(f_0)=F(\tau_a f * f_0)=F(f * \tau_a f_0)=F(f)\,F(\tau_a f_0)=F(
f)\,F(f_0)\,e^{\lambda a}.$$
Since $F(f_0)\neq 0$,
$$\boxed{\;F(\tau_a f)=e^{\lambda a}\,F(f)\quad\text{for all }f\in L_1,\ a\in\ma
thbb{R}.\;}$$

---

### Step 2. Boundedness forces $\operatorname{Re}\lambda=0$

$F$ is bounded: $|F(f)|\le \|F\|\,\|f\|_1$. Applying this to $\tau_a f_0$ and us
ing the relation above,
$$|e^{\lambda a}|\,|F(f_0)|=|F(\tau_a f_0)|\le \|F\|\,\|\tau_a f_0\|_1=\|F\|\,\|
f_0\|_1.$$
Hence $|e^{\lambda a}|=e^{(\operatorname{Re}\lambda)a}$ is bounded for *all* $a\
in\mathbb{R}$, which is possible only when $\operatorname{Re}\lambda=0$. Write $
\lambda=i\xi$ with $\xi\in\mathbb{R}$, so $\varphi(x):=e^{i\xi x}$ is a unitary
character: $|\varphi|\equiv 1$, $\varphi(a+b)=\varphi(a)\varphi(b)$.

---

### Step 3. Identification of $F$

Define the bounded linear functional
$$G(f):=\int_{\mathbb{R}} f(x)\,\varphi(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}} f(x)\,e^{i\xi x
}\,dx.$$
$G$ is well-defined and bounded ($|G(f)|\le\|f\\|_1$) because $|\varphi|\equiv 1
$. A direct change of variables gives $G(\tau_a f)=\varphi(a)\,G(f)=e^{i\xi a}G(
f)$, the same translation eigen-relation satisfied by $F$.

We now show $F=G$. For any $f,\psi\in L_1$, the convolution admits the vector-va
lued (Bochner) representation
$$(f*\psi)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(a)\,f(x-a)\,da=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(a)\,(\t
au_a f)(x)\,da,$$
i.e. $f*\psi=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(a)\,\tau_a f\,da$ as an $L_1$-valued integral
 (convergence follows from continuity of $a\mapsto\tau_a f$ and $\psi\in L_1$).
Applying the continuous linear functional $F$ and using Step 1,
$$F(f*\psi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(a)\,F(\tau_a f)\,da=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(a)\,
e^{i\xi a}F(f)\,da=F(f)\,G(\psi).$$
On the other hand, multiplicativity of $F$ gives
$$F(f*\psi)=F(f)\,F(\psi).$$
Equating the two expressions: $F(f)\,F(\psi)=F(f)\,G(\psi)$ for all $f,\psi$. Ch
oosing any $f$ with $F(f)\neq 0$ yields
$$F(\psi)=G(\psi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(x)\,e^{i\xi x}\,dx\qquad\text{for all }\
psi\in L_1.$$

---

### Step 4. Verification

For $\xi\in\mathbb{R}$, the functional $F_\xi(f)=\int f(x)\,e^{i\xi x}\,dx$ is l
inear, continuous ($|F_\xi(f)|\le\|f\|_1$), and multiplicative:
$$F_\xi(f*g)=\iint f(t)g(x-t)e^{i\xi x}\,dt\,dx=\int f(t)e^{i\xi t}\!\int g(u)e^
{i\xi u}\,du\,dt=F_\xi(f)\,F_\xi(g).$$
The zero functional is the case $F\equiv 0$. These are exactly the functionals p
roduced above.

---

## Conclusion

The continuous linear functionals $F\colon L_1(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{
C}$ satisfying $F(f*g)=F(f)F(g)$ are precisely

$$\boxed{\,F\equiv 0\,}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\boxed{\,F(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}} f(x
)\,e^{i\xi x}\,dx\ \text{ for some }\xi\in\mathbb{R}\,}.$$

Equivalently (up to the sign convention for the Fourier transform), the nonzero
solutions are the Fourier transforms $\widehat{f}(\xi)$ evaluated at a fixed poi
nt $\xi\in\mathbb{R}$; the Gelfand spectrum of the convolution Banach algebra $L
_1(\mathbb{R})$ is $\mathbb{R}$.
