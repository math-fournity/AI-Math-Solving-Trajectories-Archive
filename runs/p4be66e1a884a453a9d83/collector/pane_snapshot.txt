## Proof

**Setup.** Let $S \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ be the standard simplex with vertices
$$v_0 = \mathbf{0},\quad v_1 = e_1,\quad \dots,\quad v_n = e_n,$$
so $\operatorname{Vol}(S) = \tfrac{1}{n!}$.

**Faces used.** An $n$-simplex has exactly $n+1$ facets (codimension-$1$ faces).
 The facet $F_i$ opposite to vertex $v_i$ is the convex hull of all vertices exc
ept $v_i$. Taking the barycenters of these $n+1$ facets yields exactly $n+1$ poi
nts — the right number to form a new simplex $T$. (This is the natural interpret
ation that produces an inscribed simplex; taking *all* faces would give $2^{n+1}
-2$ points, not a simplex.)

**Barycenters.** The Euclidean barycenter of facet $F_i$ is
$$b_i = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{j \neq i} v_j.$$

Computing explicitly:
- $b_0 = \tfrac{1}{n}(e_1 + \cdots + e_n) = \bigl(\tfrac{1}{n},\dots,\tfrac{1}{n
}\bigr)$,
- For $k = 1,\dots,n$: $b_k = \tfrac{1}{n}\bigl(e_1+\cdots+e_n - e_k\bigr) = b_0
 - \tfrac{1}{n}e_k$.

**Edge matrix of $T$.** Taking $b_0$ as the base vertex, the edge vectors are
$$b_k - b_0 = -\tfrac{1}{n}\,e_k, \qquad k = 1,\dots,n.$$
Thus the $n \times n$ matrix of edge vectors is $-\tfrac{1}{n} I_n$, with determ
inant $\bigl(-\tfrac{1}{n}\bigr)^n$.

**Volume of $T$.**
$$\operatorname{Vol}(T) = \frac{1}{n!}\left|\det\!\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{n}I_n\Bigr)\r
ight| = \frac{1}{n!}\cdot\frac{1}{n^n}.$$

**Ratio.**
$$\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(T)}{\operatorname{Vol}(S)} = \frac{\,\dfrac{1}{n!\,n^
n}\,}{\dfrac{1}{n!}} = \frac{1}{n^n}.$$

**Verification on small cases.**
- $n=1$: the two facets are the endpoints $\{0\},\{1\}$; their barycenters are $
0$ and $1$, so $T = S$ and the ratio is $1 = 1/1^1$. ✓
- $n=2$: the medial triangle with vertices $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12), (0,\tfrac12), (
\tfrac12,0)$ has area $\tfrac18$ vs. $\tfrac12$, giving ratio $\tfrac14 = 1/2^2$
. ✓
- $n=3$: face-centroid tetrahedron has volume $\tfrac{1}{162}$ vs. $\tfrac16$, g
iving ratio $\tfrac{1}{27} = 1/3^3$. ✓

All cases agree with the formula $\tfrac{1}{n^n}$.

**Conclusion.** The ratio of the volume of the inscribed simplex (formed by the
barycenters of the facets of $S$) to the volume of $S$ is exactly $\dfrac{1}{n^n
}$. The pattern holds.
