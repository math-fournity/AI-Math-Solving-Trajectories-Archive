## Proof

Let $c_n$ denote the $n$-th composite number (with $1$ excluded) and $p_n$ the $
n$-th prime. We evaluate

$$L=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{c_n}{n}-\frac{c_n}{p_n}-\frac{c_n}{n^2}\right).
$$

We establish three asymptotic facts.

### Fact 1: $\dfrac{c_n}{n}\to 1$.

For $x\ge 2$, the count of composites not exceeding $x$ (excluding $1$) is

$$\pi_c(x)=\lfloor x\rfloor-1-\pi(x),$$

since among $2,3,\dots,\lfloor x\rfloor$ there are $\lfloor x\rfloor-1$ integers
, of which $\pi(x)$ are prime and the rest composite. By definition $\pi_c(c_n)=
n$, so

$$n=c_n-1-\pi(c_n)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{c_n}{n}=1+\frac{1}{n}+\frac{\p
i(c_n)}{n}.\tag{1}$$

We bound $c_n$. Clearly $c_n\ge n$ (there are at most $n-1$ composites among $1,
\dots,n$). For an upper bound, using the Chebyshev estimate $\pi(x)\le Cx/\ln x$
 for $x\ge 2$ and some absolute constant $C$,

$$\pi_c(2n)=2n-1-\pi(2n)\ge 2n-1-\frac{2Cn}{\ln(2n)}.$$

For all sufficiently large $n$, $\frac{2Cn}{\ln(2n)}\le n-1$, so $\pi_c(2n)\ge n
$, which gives $c_n\le 2n$. Hence for large $n$,

$$\frac{\pi(c_n)}{n}\le \frac{\pi(2n)}{n}\le \frac{2C}{\ln(2n)}\xrightarrow[n\to
\infty]{}0.$$

Substituting into (1): $\dfrac{c_n}{n}\to 1$.

### Fact 2: $\dfrac{c_n}{p_n}\to 0$.

Using $c_n\le 2n$ (for large $n$) and the prime number theorem $p_n\sim n\ln n$,

$$0\le \frac{c_n}{p_n}\le \frac{2n}{p_n}\sim \frac{2}{\ln n}\xrightarrow[n\to\in
fty]{}0.$$

### Fact 3: $\dfrac{c_n}{n^2}\to 0$.

Using $c_n\le 2n$,

$$0\le \frac{c_n}{n^2}\le \frac{2}{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.$$

### Combining

By linearity of limits (each component limit exists),

$$L=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{c_n}{n}-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{c_n}{p_n}-\lim_{n\to\i
nfty}\frac{c_n}{n^2}=1-0-0=1.$$

$$\boxed{L=1}$$
