## Proof

Let $a_N$ denote the (unique) value of $a$ satisfying the equation for each $N$.
 Define
$$F(a,N) \;=\; a\int_0^1 \frac{dx}{1 - r(x,a)^N}, \qquad r(x,a) \;=\; \frac{p(x)
}{p(x)+a}.$$

We assume $p_0 > 0$ (so $p$ is bounded away from zero) and $A > 0$.

### Step 1. Pointwise limit of $F(a,N)$ for fixed $a > 0$

For any fixed $a > 0$ and $x \in [0,1]$:
$$0 < r(x,a) = \frac{p(x)}{p(x)+a} < 1,$$
so $r(x,a)^N \to 0$ as $N \to \infty$, hence $\frac{1}{1-r(x,a)^N} \to 1$. There
fore
$$F(a,N) \;\longrightarrow\; a \cdot \int_0^1 1\,dx \;=\; a \qquad (N\to\infty,\
; a>0\text{ fixed}).$$

### Step 2. Uniform convergence on compact subsets of $(0,\infty)$

Fix $\delta > 0$ and $M < \infty$. For all $a \in [\delta, M]$ and all $x \in [0
,1]$, using $p(x) \le 1$:
$$r(x,a) = \frac{p(x)}{p(x)+a} \le \frac{1}{1+\delta},$$
so $r(x,a)^N \le (1+\delta)^{-N}$, uniformly in $x$ and $a \in [\delta,M]$.

Now
$$F(a,N) - a \;=\; a\int_0^1 \frac{r(x,a)^N}{1 - r(x,a)^N}\,dx.$$

For $N$ large enough that $(1+\delta)^{-N} < \tfrac12$, we have $1 - r^N \ge \tf
rac12$, so
$$|F(a,N) - a| \;\le\; M \cdot 2(1+\delta)^{-N} \;\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}\; 0,$
$
**uniformly** for $a \in [\delta, M]$.

### Step 3. Existence and uniqueness of $a_N$

**Monotonicity.** Compute
$$\frac{\partial F}{\partial a}(a,N) = \int_0^1 \frac{dx}{1-r^N} \;+\; a\int_0^1
 \frac{N\,r^N}{(p(x)+a)\,(1-r^N)^2}\,dx \;>\; 0,$$
since both integrands are positive. So $F(\cdot, N)$ is strictly increasing in $
a$.

**Boundary behavior.**
- As $a \to 0^+$: using $r \approx 1 - a/p(x)$, we get $r^N \approx e^{-Na/p(x)}
$, and for $Na \to 0$, $\frac{1}{1-r^N} \approx \frac{p(x)}{Na}$, giving $F(a,N)
 \approx \frac{1}{N}\int_0^1 p(x)\,dx \to 0$.
- As $a \to \infty$: $r \to 0$, so $F(a,N) \to a \to \infty$.

By strict monotonicity and the intermediate value theorem, there exists a **uniq
ue** $a_N > 0$ with $F(a_N, N) = A$.

### Step 4. Convergence $a_N \to A$

Let $\epsilon \in (0, A)$ be arbitrary. Set $\delta = A - \epsilon > 0$ and $M =
 A + \epsilon$. By Step 2, for all sufficiently large $N$,
$$|F(a, N) - a| < \epsilon \quad \text{for all } a \in [A-\epsilon,\; A+\epsilon
].$$

Evaluating at the endpoints:
$$F(A-\epsilon, N) < (A-\epsilon) + \epsilon = A,$$
$$F(A+\epsilon, N) > (A+\epsilon) - \epsilon = A.$$

Since $F(\cdot, N)$ is continuous and strictly increasing, the unique solution $
a_N$ of $F(a_N, N) = A$ satisfies
$$A - \epsilon < a_N < A + \epsilon.$$

Since $\epsilon > 0$ was arbitrary,

$$\boxed{\lim_{N \to \infty} a_N = A.}$$
