### Setup

The equation of motion is

$$m\ddot x = -\frac{k}{x}, \qquad x(0)=L,\quad \dot x(0)=0.$$

With $k/m=1/2$ this becomes

$$\ddot x = -\frac{1}{2x}.$$

### First integral (energy)

Multiply by $\dot x$ and integrate. The potential is $V(x)=-\int F\,dx = k\ln x$
, so energy conservation gives

$$\tfrac12 m\dot x^{2}+k\ln x = k\ln L,$$

hence, using $2k/m=1$,

$$\dot x^{2} = \frac{2k}{m}\ln\frac{L}{x} = \ln\frac{L}{x}.$$

Since $x$ decreases, $\dot x=-\sqrt{\ln(L/x)}$, and therefore

$$dt = -\frac{dx}{\sqrt{\ln(L/x)}}.$$

### Reduction to a Laplace transform

The time to reach $O$ is

$$T = \int_{0}^{L}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{\ln(L/x)}}.$$

Substitute $u=\ln(L/x)$, i.e. $x=Le^{-u}$, $dx=-Le^{-u}du$; limits $x=L\Rightarr
ow u=0$, $x\to 0^{+}\Rightarrow u\to\infty$:

$$T = L\int_{0}^{\infty} u^{-1/2}\,e^{-u}\,du.$$

The integral $\int_{0}^{\infty} u^{-1/2}e^{-u}\,du$ is exactly the **Laplace tra
nsform** of $f(u)=u^{-1/2}$ evaluated at $s=1$:

$$\mathcal L\{u^{-1/2}\}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{-1/2}e^{-su}\,du=\frac{\Gamma(1/
2)}{s^{1/2}}.$$

This is the standard Laplace-transform/gamma-function identity, obtained by the
change of variable $v=su$:

$$\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{-1/2}e^{-su}\,du = s^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{-1/2}e^{-v}
\,dv = s^{-1/2}\,\Gamma(1/2).$$

### Gamma evaluation

Setting $s=1$,

$$T = L\,\Gamma(1/2).$$

Using the classical Gaussian integral,

$$\Gamma(1/2)=\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{-1/2}e^{-u}\,du = 2\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t^{2}}
\,dt = \sqrt{\pi}.$$

### Result

$$\boxed{\,T = L\sqrt{\pi}\,}$$

The particle reaches $O$ after a time $L\sqrt{\pi}$.
