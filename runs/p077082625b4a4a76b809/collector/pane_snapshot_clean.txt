Looking at this problem, I need to determine whether a non-constant holomorphic
map $f: U \to V$ exists with $f(y_0) = z_0$ and $f^{-1}(z_0) \cap U = \{y_0\}$,
where $U \subset Y$ and $V \subset \Omega$ are open neighborhoods.

The answer depends on the relative dimensions of $Y$ and $\Omega$ at the respect
ive points.

---

## Answer

Such a non-constant holomorphic mapping exists **if and only if** $\dim_{y_0} Y
\leq \dim_{z_0} \Omega$.

---

## Proof

Let $n = \dim_{y_0} Y$ and $m = \dim_{z_0} \Omega$. We treat both complex manifo
lds as equipped with local coordinates.

### Case 1: $n \leq m$ — Such a map exists (construction)

Choose local coordinates $\varphi: U_0 \to \mathbb{C}^n$ on $Y$ near $y_0$ with
$\varphi(y_0) = 0$, and $\psi: V_0 \to \mathbb{C}^m$ on $\Omega$ near $z_0$ with
 $\psi(z_0) = 0$. Define the linear embedding:
$$g: \mathbb{C}^n \to \mathbb{C}^m, \quad g(w_1, \dots, w_n) = (w_1, \dots, w_n,
 0, \dots, 0).$$
Then $g^{-1}(0) = \{0\}$. Set $f = \psi^{-1} \circ g \circ \varphi$, restricted
to a sufficiently small neighborhood $U \subset U_0$ of $y_0$ so that $f(U) \sub
set V_0$ (possible by continuity, since $f(y_0) = z_0 \in V_0$).

Then:
- $f$ is holomorphic (composition of holomorphic maps).
- $f$ is non-constant (since $g$ is non-constant and $\varphi, \psi$ are biholom
orphisms).
- $f(y_0) = \psi^{-1}(g(\varphi(y_0))) = \psi^{-1}(g(0)) = \psi^{-1}(0) = z_0$.
- $f(y) = z_0 \iff g(\varphi(y)) = 0 \iff \varphi(y) = 0 \iff y = y_0$.

So $f^{-1}(z_0) \cap U = \{y_0\}$. $\checkmark$

### Case 2: $n > m$ — No such map exists (impossibility)

Suppose for contradiction that a holomorphic $f: U \to V$ exists with $f(y_0) =
z_0$ and $f^{-1}(z_0) \cap U = \{y_0\}$.

In local coordinates near $y_0$ and $z_0$ (translating so that $y_0 = 0$ and $z_
0 = 0$), the map $f$ is represented by $m$ holomorphic functions $f_1, \dots, f_
m$ in $n$ variables on a domain $D \subset \mathbb{C}^n$ containing the origin.
The fiber is:
$$f^{-1}(z_0) = V(f_1, \dots, f_m) = \{w \in D : f_1(w) = \cdots = f_m(w) = 0\}.
$$

**Key tool — Analytic Krull's Principal Ideal Theorem (Hauptidealsatz):** If $h_
1, \dots, h_m$ are holomorphic functions on a domain in $\mathbb{C}^n$, then eve
ry irreducible component of the analytic set $V(h_1, \dots, h_m)$ has **codimens
ion at most $m$**, equivalently **dimension at least $n - m$**.

This is the analytic analogue of the algebraic Krull's theorem: each holomorphic
 equation can reduce the dimension by at most 1, so $m$ equations reduce it by a
t most $m$.

Applying this to $f_1, \dots, f_m$: every irreducible component of $f^{-1}(z_0)
= V(f_1, \dots, f_m)$ through the origin has dimension $\geq n - m$.

Since $n > m$, we have $n - m \geq 1$. Therefore every irreducible component of
$f^{-1}(z_0)$ through $y_0$ has dimension $\geq 1$. An analytic set of dimension
 $\geq 1$ through $y_0$ contains points other than $y_0$ in every neighborhood o
f $y_0$ (in particular, it is not the single point $\{y_0\}$, which has dimensio
n $0$).

This contradicts the assumption $f^{-1}(z_0) \cap U = \{y_0\}$. Therefore no suc
h holomorphic map exists when $n > m$.

**Remark on non-constancy:** Note that a constant map $f \equiv z_0$ gives $f^{-
1}(z_0) = U \neq \{y_0\}$, so the non-constancy condition is automatically neces
sary. The impossibility in Case 2 holds for *all* holomorphic maps, constant or
not.

---

## Conclusion

The existence of such a non-constant holomorphic mapping $f: U \to V$ with $f(y_
0) = z_0$ and $f^{-1}(z_0) \cap U = \{y_0\}$ is equivalent to the dimension ineq
uality $\dim_{y_0} Y \leq \dim_{z_0} \Omega$:

- **If $\dim_{y_0} Y \leq \dim_{z_0} \Omega$**: Yes — construct via local coordi
nates and a linear embedding.
- **If $\dim_{y_0} Y > \dim_{z_0} \Omega$**: No — the fiber dimension theorem (a
nalytic Krull's Hauptidealsatz) forces $f^{-1}(z_0)$ to have dimension $\geq \di
m Y - \dim \Omega \geq 1$, so it cannot be a single point.
