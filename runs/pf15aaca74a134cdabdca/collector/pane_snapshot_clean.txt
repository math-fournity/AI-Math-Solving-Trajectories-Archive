## Proof

**Claim.** If $X$ and $Y$ are homeomorphic topological spaces, then $d(X) = d(Y)
$.

Let $h \colon X \to Y$ be a homeomorphism. We use two standard facts about homeo
morphisms:

**Fact 1 (Closure preservation).** For every subset $S \subseteq X$,
$$h\!\left(\overline{S}^{\,X}\right) = \overline{h(S)}^{\,Y}.$$

*Proof of Fact 1.* A homeomorphism is a closed map, so $h(\overline{S}^{\,X})$ i
s closed in $Y$ and contains $h(S)$, giving $\overline{h(S)}^{\,Y} \subseteq h(\
overline{S}^{\,X})$. Conversely, $h^{-1}$ is continuous, so $h^{-1}\!\left(\over
line{h(S)}^{\,Y}\right)$ is closed in $X$ and contains $S$, hence contains $\ove
rline{S}^{\,X}$; applying $h$ gives $h(\overline{S}^{\,X}) \subseteq \overline{h
(S)}^{\,Y}$. $\square$

**Fact 2 (Cardinality preservation).** Since $h$ is a bijection, $|h(S)| = |S|$
for every $S \subseteq X$.

---

**Step 1: $d(Y) \le d(X)$.**

Let $A \subseteq X$ be a dense subset with $|A| = d(X)$, so $\overline{A}^{\,X}
= X$. Set $B := h(A) \subseteq Y$. By Fact 1,
$$\overline{B}^{\,Y} = \overline{h(A)}^{\,Y} = h\!\left(\overline{A}^{\,X}\right
) = h(X) = Y,$$
so $B$ is dense in $Y$. By Fact 2, $|B| = |A| = d(X)$. Since $d(Y)$ is the *mini
mum* cardinality of a dense subset of $Y$,
$$d(Y) \le |B| = d(X).$$

**Step 2: $d(X) \le d(Y)$.**

Apply Step 1 to the homeomorphism $h^{-1} \colon Y \to X$ (which is also a homeo
morphism). This gives
$$d(X) \le d(Y).$$

**Conclusion.** Combining the two inequalities,
$$d(X) = d(Y). \qquad \blacksquare$$

---

**Remark.** The density $d(\cdot)$ is therefore a *topological invariant*: it de
pends only on the homeomorphism type of a space, not on the particular represent
ative. The entire argument rests on the fact that a homeomorphism simultaneously
 (i) preserves the closure operator and (ii) preserves cardinalities of subsets,
 so it transports dense sets of minimum size bijectively between the two spaces.
