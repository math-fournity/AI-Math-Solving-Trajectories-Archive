## Proof

Let $S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n U_i$. The quantity of interest is
$$
p_n := \mathbb{P}\!\left(\frac{S_n}{n} \in \left[-\frac{\varepsilon}{n^{2/3}},\,
\frac{\varepsilon}{n^{2/3}}\right]\right) = \mathbb{P}\!\left(S_n \in \left[-\va
repsilon\, n^{1/3},\,\varepsilon\, n^{1/3}\right]\right).
$$

The $U_i$ are i.i.d. $\mathrm{Unif}[-1,1]$ with mean $0$ and variance $\sigma^2
= 1/3$.

**Local CLT (Gnedenko).** Since the distribution of $U$ is absolutely continuous
 with a bounded density and finite variance, the density $f_n$ of the standardiz
ed sum $S_n/(\sigma\sqrt n)$ converges *uniformly* to the standard normal densit
y $\varphi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^2/2}$. In particular, there is a consta
nt $C$ such that
$$
\sup_x f_n(x) \le C \qquad\text{for all } n\ge 2.
$$

Let $g_n$ be the density of $S_n$ and $h_n$ the density of $S_n/n$. By scaling,
$$
g_n(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt n}\,f_n\!\!\left(\frac{x}{\sigma\sqrt n}\right) \l
e \frac{C}{\sigma\sqrt n}, \qquad h_n(y) = n\,g_n(ny) \le \frac{Cn}{\sigma\sqrt
n} = \frac{C\sqrt n}{\sigma} = C\sqrt{3n}.
$$

**Upper bound.** For $n\ge 2$ (so that $S_n$ has a density),
$$
p_n = \int_{-\varepsilon/n^{2/3}}^{\varepsilon/n^{2/3}} h_n(y)\,dy \le \frac{2\v
arepsilon}{n^{2/3}}\cdot C\sqrt{3n} = 2C\varepsilon\sqrt 3\; n^{-1/6}.
$$
Since $n^{-1/6}\to 0$, we obtain $p_n \to 0$.

**Sharp asymptotic (optional).** The half-width of the interval, measured in uni
ts of the standard deviation $1/\sqrt{3n}$ of $S_n/n$, is
$$
\frac{\varepsilon/n^{2/3}}{1/\sqrt{3n}} = \varepsilon\sqrt 3\, n^{-1/6}\xrightar
row[n\to\infty]{}0.
$$
Since $f_n\to\varphi$ uniformly and $\varphi(0)=1/\sqrt{2\pi}$,
$$
p_n = \int_{-\varepsilon\sqrt 3\, n^{-1/6}}^{\varepsilon\sqrt 3\, n^{-1/6}} f_n(
x)\,dx = 2\varepsilon\sqrt 3\, n^{-1/6}\cdot\varphi(0) + o\!\left(n^{-1/6}\right
) = \frac{2\varepsilon\sqrt 3}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,n^{-1/6} + o\!\left(n^{-1/6}\right)
.
$$
This confirms $p_n \sim \frac{2\varepsilon\sqrt 3}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,n^{-1/6}\to 0$.

**Conclusion.** The interval $[-\varepsilon/n^{2/3},\,\varepsilon/n^{2/3}]$ shri
nks to $\{0\}$ faster than the standard deviation $1/\sqrt{3n}$ of $S_n/n$ shrin
ks (by the factor $n^{-1/6}$), so the probability of capture vanishes:

$$
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n U_i \in \left[
-\frac{\varepsilon}{n^{2/3}},\frac{\varepsilon}{n^{2/3}}\right]\right) = 0.}
$$
