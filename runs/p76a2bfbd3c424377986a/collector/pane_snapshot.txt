## Solution

**Setup and notation.** Label the hubs $1,\dots,n$. For each pair, $2$ units are
 split as $(2,0)$ or $(1,1)$. Let $B$ be the bottom-$m$ group and $T$ the rest.
For hub $i$ let $s_i$ = total score, and $s_i^B$ = score earned from matches aga
inst members of $B$. The condition is

$$s_i^B = \tfrac{1}{2}\,s_i \quad\text{for every hub } i.$$

Decompose each hub's score by opponent group:
- $i\in B$: $a_i$ = points from within-$B$ matches, $b_i$ = points from $B$–$T$
matches. So $s_i=a_i+b_i$ and $s_i^B=a_i$.
- $j\in T$: $c_j$ = points from $B$–$T$ matches, $d_j$ = points from within-$T$
matches. So $s_j=c_j+d_j$ and $s_j^B=c_j$.

**Necessary condition.** The half condition gives
- $i\in B$: $a_i = \tfrac12(a_i+b_i)\Rightarrow a_i=b_i$.
- $j\in T$: $c_j = \tfrac12(c_j+d_j)\Rightarrow c_j=d_j$.

Summing over each group:
$$\sum_{i\in B} a_i = m(m-1),\qquad \sum_{j\in T} d_j = (n-m)(n-m-1),$$
since within-group play has $\binom{m}{2}$ and $\binom{n-m}{2}$ matches, each wo
rth $2$ points. The $B$–$T$ matches contribute $2m(n-m)$ total points, split as
$\sum b_i+\sum c_j$. Using $a_i=b_i$ and $c_j=d_j$:

$$m(m-1)+(n-m)(n-m-1)=2m(n-m).$$

Expanding: $2m^2-2nm+n^2-n=2mn-2m^2$, i.e.

$$(n-2m)^2 = n.$$

So $n$ must be a perfect square, $n=k^2$, and $m=\dfrac{k^2\mp k}{2}=\dfrac{k(k\
mp1)}{2}$.

**The "bottom-$m$" constraint eliminates the larger root.** With $m=\frac{k(k+1)
}{2}$ (the "$+$" case), $|B|>|T|$ and the average scores satisfy
$$\bar s_B = \frac{2m(m-1)}{m}=2(m-1),\qquad \bar s_T=\frac{2(n-m)(n-m-1)}{n-m}=
2(n-m-1).$$
Here $m>n/2$, so $\bar s_B>\bar s_T$, forcing some $B$-member to outscore some $
T$-member. Then $B$ cannot be the bottom group. **Only the smaller root $m=\frac
{k(k-1)}{2}$ survives.**

**Explicit construction (sufficiency) for $m=\frac{k(k-1)}{2}$, $k\ge 2$.** Let
$n=k^2$, $|B|=m=\frac{k(k-1)}2$, $|T|=n-m=\frac{k(k+1)}2$.

- *Within $B$:* all matches drawn $\Rightarrow$ each $i\in B$ gets $a_i=m-1$.
- *Within $T$:* all matches drawn $\Rightarrow$ each $j\in T$ gets $d_j=n-m-1$.
- *Between $B$ and $T$:* take a **simple regular bipartite graph** where each $B
$-vertex has degree $r=m-1$ and each $T$-vertex has degree $s=3m-n+1$. A drawn c
ross-match gives the $B$-member $1$; a non-edge gives the $B$-member $0$ (and th
e $T$-member $2$).

This graph exists because $mr=(n-m)s$ (equivalent to $(n-2m)^2=n$, verified) and
 $r\le n-m$, $s\le m$, $s\ge 0$ — all checking out for $k\ge2$:
- $r=m-1\le n-m \iff k(k-1)\le k^2+1$ ✓,
- $s=3m-n+1\le m \iff 2m\le n-1 \iff k\ge1$ ✓,
- $s\ge0 \iff (k-1)(k-2)\ge0$ ✓.

Then $b_i=r=m-1=a_i$ and $c_j = 2m-s = 2m-(3m-n+1)=n-m-1=d_j$, so the half condi
tion holds for every hub. Scores are uniform:
$$s_i=2(m-1)=k^2-k-2\quad(i\in B),\qquad s_j=2(n-m-1)=k^2+k-2\quad(j\in T).$$
Since $k^2-k-2 < k^2+k-2$, the group $B$ is exactly the bottom $m$. ✓

**Enumerating pairs with $n<50$.** We need $k^2<50$, so $k\in\{2,3,4,5,6,7\}$ ($
k=1$ gives $m=0$, excluded):

| $k$ | $n=k^2$ | $m=\frac{k(k-1)}2$ | $n+m$ |
|-----|---------|---------------------|-------|
| 2 | 4 | 1 | 5 |
| 3 | 9 | 3 | 12 |
| 4 | 16 | 6 | 22 |
| 5 | 25 | 10 | 35 |
| 6 | 36 | 15 | 51 |
| 7 | 49 | 21 | 70 |

**Summing:**
$$5+12+22+35+51+70 = 195.$$

The set of valid pairs is $\{(4,1),(9,3),(16,6),(25,10),(36,15),(49,21)\}$, and
the required sum is

$$\boxed{195}$$
