# Asymptotic formula for $p_n$ via the saddle-point method

## 1. Setup

The partition generating function is
$$F(x)=\sum_{n\ge 0}p_n x^n=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-x^k},\qquad |x|<1.$$
By Cauchy's formula,
$$p_n=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint F(x)\,x^{-n-1}\,dx.$$
Set $x=e^{-t}$ with $\Re t>0$. Then $dx=-e^{-t}dt$ and $x^{-n-1}dx=-e^{nt}dt$, s
o (with the Bromwich-type contour $\Re t=c>0$)
$$p_n=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty} F(e^{-t})\,e^{nt}\,dt=\frac{1
}{2\pi i}\int e^{\phi(t)}\,dt,\qquad \phi(t):=\log F(e^{-t})+nt.$$

## 2. Expansion of $\log F(e^{-t})$ for small $t$

Write
$$\log F(e^{-t})=-\sum_{k\ge1}\log(1-e^{-kt})=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{1}{m(e^{mt}-1)}=
:g(t).$$
Using the Mellin transform of $\frac{1}{e^u-1}$ (or Euler–Maclaurin summation),
one obtains for $t\to0^+$ in $|\arg t|<\pi/2$:
$$\boxed{\;g(t)=\frac{\pi^2}{6t}+\frac12\log\!\left(\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)-\frac{
t}{24}+O(t^2).\;}$$
(The $\pi^2/(6t)$ comes from $\zeta(2)=\pi^2/6$; the logarithmic term is the res
idue of the double pole of $\Gamma(s)\zeta(s)\zeta(s+1)$ at $s=0$; $-t/24$ comes
 from $\zeta(-1)=-1/12$.)

Hence
$$F(e^{-t})=\frac{e^{\pi^2/(6t)-t/24}}{\sqrt{2\pi/t}}\bigl(1+O(t^2)\bigr).$$

## 3. Locating the saddle point

$$\phi'(t)=g'(t)+n=-\frac{\pi^2}{6t^2}-\frac{1}{2t}-\frac{1}{24}+n.$$
Setting $\phi'(t_0)=0$ and solving to leading order gives
$$n=\frac{\pi^2}{6t_0^2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t_0=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{6n}}\Bigl
(1+O(n^{-1/2})\Bigr).$$
We use $t_0=\pi/\sqrt{6n}$; the $O(n^{-1/2})$ correction only affects terms beyo
nd the precision we keep.

## 4. Value of $\phi$ at the saddle

Using $nt_0=\dfrac{\pi^2}{6t_0}$ (from the saddle equation, to leading order),
$$\frac{\pi^2}{6t_0}+nt_0=\frac{\pi^2}{3t_0}=\frac{\pi^2}{3}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{6n}
}{\pi}=\pi\sqrt{\frac{2n}{3}}.$$
Therefore
$$\phi(t_0)=\pi\sqrt{\frac{2n}{3}}+\frac12\log\!\left(\frac{t_0}{2\pi}\right)-\f
rac{t_0}{24}+O(n^{-1}).$$
With $t_0=\pi/\sqrt{6n}$,
$$\frac12\log\!\left(\frac{t_0}{2\pi}\right)=\frac12\log\!\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{
6n}}\right)=-\frac12\log 2-\frac14\log(6n)=-\frac14\log n-\frac14\log 24.$$
So
$$\phi(t_0)=\pi\sqrt{\frac{2n}{3}}-\frac14\log n-\frac14\log 24-\frac{\pi}{24\sq
rt{6n}}+O(n^{-1}).$$

## 5. The Gaussian integral

$$\phi''(t)=\frac{\pi^2}{3t^3}+\frac{1}{2t^2}+\cdots,\qquad \phi''(t_0)=\frac{\p
i^2}{3t_0^3}\bigl(1+O(n^{-1/2})\bigr)=\frac{2\sqrt6\,n^{3/2}}{\pi}\bigl(1+O(n^{-
1/2})\bigr).$$
The saddle-point (steepest-descent) evaluation gives
$$p_n\sim\frac{e^{\phi(t_0)}}{\sqrt{2\pi\,\phi''(t_0)}}.$$
Compute the denominator:
$$\sqrt{2\pi\,\phi''(t_0)}=\sqrt{2\pi\cdot\frac{2\sqrt6\,n^{3/2}}{\pi}}=\sqrt{4\
sqrt6\,n^{3/2}}=2\cdot6^{1/4}\,n^{3/4}.$$
Putting everything together,
$$p_n\sim\frac{\exp\!\left(\pi\sqrt{\frac{2n}{3}}-\frac14\log n-\frac14\log 24-\
frac{\pi}{24\sqrt{6n}}\right)}{2\cdot6^{1/4}\,n^{3/4}}.$$
Collecting powers: $n^{1/4}\cdot n^{3/4}=n$ in the denominator, and $24^{1/4}\cd
ot6^{1/4}=(144)^{1/4}=\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt3$, so the total constant denominator is $
2\cdot2\sqrt3\,n=4\sqrt3\,n$. Thus

$$\boxed{\;p_n\sim\frac{1}{4\sqrt3\,n}\exp\!\left(\pi\sqrt{\frac{2n}{3}}-\frac{\
pi}{24\sqrt{6n}}\right).\;}$$

Keeping only the dominant exponential (the $e^{-\pi/(24\sqrt{6n})}$ factor tends
 to $1$) yields the **Hardy–Ramanujan asymptotic formula**:

$$\boxed{\;p_n\sim\frac{1}{4n\sqrt3}\exp\!\left(\pi\sqrt{\frac{2n}{3}}\right)\qq
uad(n\to\infty).\;}$$

## 6. Sketch of the error estimate

The next corrections come from (i) the $O(n^{-1/2})$ shift in $t_0$, (ii) higher
 terms $O(t^2)$ in $g(t)$, and (iii) cubic/quartic terms in the Taylor expansion
 of $\phi$ about $t_0$. Each contributes a relative error of order $O(n^{-1/2})$
, so
$$p_n=\frac{1}{4\sqrt3\,n}\exp\!\left(\pi\sqrt{\frac{2n}{3}}-\frac{\pi}{24\sqrt{
6n}}\right)\Bigl(1+O(n^{-1/2})\Bigr).$$
(Refining the saddle-point method into the circle method, Rademacher obtained an
 exact convergent series for $p_n$ whose first term is precisely the formula abo
ve.)
