Looking at this problem, I need to find all composite $n$ such that some $a > 2$
 makes $a^d + 2^d \mid a^n - 2^n$ for every proper divisor $d$ of $n$.

**Key observation:** If $d \mid n$ and $2d \mid n$, then $a^d + 2^d \mid a^{2d}
- 2^{2d} \mid a^n - 2^n$ automatically (since $a^{2d}-2^{2d}=(a^d-2^d)(a^d+2^d)$
 and $x^k-y^k \mid x^m-y^m$ when $k\mid m$). So only divisors $d$ with $2d \nmid
 n$ are constraining.

Writing $n = 2^s m$ ($m$ odd), the divisors $d$ with $2d \nmid n$ are exactly $d
 = 2^s e$ where $e \mid m$.

**Powers of 2 work.** If $n = 2^s$ ($s \geq 2$), then $m = 1$ and there are no c
onstraining divisors — every proper divisor $d = 2^t$ ($t < s$) satisfies $2d \m
id n$. So any $a > 2$ works.

**No odd composite works.** For odd $n$, every proper divisor is constraining. F
rom $d=1$: $a+2 \mid a^n - 2^n \equiv -2^{n+1} \pmod{a+2}$, forcing $a = 2^k - 2
$ with $k \geq 3$, so $u := 2^{k-1}-1 \geq 3$ is odd. For any odd prime $p \mid
n$ (a proper divisor since $n$ composite), $a^p + 2^p \mid 2^{n+1}$, giving $u^p
 + 1 \mid 2^{n+1-p}$. Now $u^p+1 = (u+1)Q$ where $Q = u^{p-1}-u^{p-2}+\cdots+1$
is odd. Since $Q \geq u^{p-2}(u-1) + 1 \geq 7 > 1$, $u^p+1$ is not a power of 2
— contradiction.

**No even non-power-of-2 works.** With $n = 2^s m$, $s \geq 1$, $m \geq 3$ odd,
the divisor $d = 2^s$ is proper and constraining. Since $n/d = m$ is odd, $a^{2^
s}+2^{2^s} \mid 2^{n+1}$, so it must be a power of 2. If $a$ is odd, this sum is
 odd $> 1$ — impossible. If $a = 2c$ is even ($c \geq 2$), then $2^{2^s}(c^{2^s}
+1) \mid 2^{n+1}$, requiring $c^{2^s}+1$ to be a power of 2. For $c$ even this i
s odd $> 1$; for $c$ odd ($c \geq 3$), $c^{2^s} \equiv 1 \pmod{8}$ so $c^{2^s}+1
 \equiv 2 \pmod{8}$ has odd part $> 1$. All cases fail.

**Answer:** The composite natural numbers $n$ satisfying the condition are exact
ly $n = 2^k$ for $k \geq 2$, i.e., $\{4, 8, 16, 32, \ldots\}$.
