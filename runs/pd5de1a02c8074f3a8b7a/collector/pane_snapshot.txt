**Theorem.** A morphism $f\colon (X,E)\to (Y,F)$ in $\mathcal{C}_\Omega$ is a re
gular monomorphism if and only if $f$ is injective and
$$E(x,x')=F(f(x),f(x'))\quad\text{for all }x,x'\in X.$$

Equivalently, the regular monomorphisms are exactly the **embeddings of sub-$\Om
ega$-valued sets**: inclusions of a subset $S\subseteq Y$ equipped with the rest
ricted equality $F|_{S\times S}$.

**Proof.**

Recall the category $\mathcal{C}_\Omega$. An $\Omega$-valued set is a pair $(X,E
)$ with $E\colon X\times X\to\Omega$ satisfying:

1. **(Strictness)** $E(x,y)\le E(x,x)$,
2. **(Symmetry)** $E(x,y)=E(y,x)$,
3. **(Transitivity)** $E(x,y)\wedge E(y,z)\le E(x,z)$.

A morphism $f\colon (X,E)\to (Y,F)$ is a function $f\colon X\to Y$ with $E(x,x')
\le F(f(x),f(x'))$ for all $x,x'\in X$.

### Step 1 — Monomorphisms are exactly the injective functions

($\Leftarrow$) If $f$ is injective and $f\circ g=f\circ h$ for $g,h\colon (Z,G)\
to (X,E)$, then $f(g(z))=f(h(z))$ for all $z$, hence $g(z)=h(z)$ for all $z$, so
 $g=h$.

($\Rightarrow$) If $f$ is not injective, choose $x\neq x'$ with $f(x)=f(x')$. Th
e object $(\{*\},\bot)$ (singleton with equality $\bot$) is a valid $\Omega$-val
ued set. The maps $g,h\colon \{*\}\to X$ defined by $g(*)=x$, $h(*)=x'$ are morp
hisms $(\{*\},\bot)\to (X,E)$ since $\bot\le E(x,x)$ and $\bot\le E(x',x')$. The
n $f\circ g=f\circ h$ but $g\ne h$, so $f$ is not mono. $\square$

### Step 2 — Equalizers are subsets with the restricted equality

Given $g,h\colon (X,E)\to (Y,F)$, set
$$Z=\{x\in X : g(x)=h(x)\},\qquad E_Z = E|_{Z\times Z}.$$

The inclusion $i\colon (Z,E_Z)\hookrightarrow (X,E)$ is a morphism (since $E_Z(z
,z')=E(z,z')\le E(z,z')$) and satisfies $g\circ i=h\circ i$.

**Universal property.** Let $k\colon (W,G)\to (X,E)$ be a morphism with $g\circ
k=h\circ k$. Then $g(k(w))=h(k(w))$ for all $w$, so $k(w)\in Z$. Define $k'\colo
n W\to Z$ by $k'(w)=k(w)$. Then $i\circ k'=k$, and $k'$ is a morphism since
$$G(w,w')\le E(k(w),k(w'))=E_Z(k'(w),k'(w')).$$
Uniqueness of $k'$ is immediate from $i$ being injective. Hence $(Z,E_Z)\xrighta
rrow{i}(X,E)$ is the equalizer of $g$ and $h$.

In particular, every equalizer $i$ is **injective** and **fully faithful**: $E_Z
(z,z')=E(z,z')=E(i(z),i(z'))$. $\square$

### Step 3 — Every fully faithful injective morphism is an equalizer

Let $f\colon (X,E)\to (Y,F)$ be injective with $E(x,x')=F(f(x),f(x'))$ for all $
x,x'$. We construct a pair $g,h\colon (Y,F)\to (W,G)$ whose equalizer is $f$ (up
 to isomorphism).

**Construction of $(W,G)$.** Let $W$ be the pushout of $f$ with $f$ in $\mathbf{
Set}$:
$$W=(Y\sqcup Y)\big/\!\sim,\qquad (f(x),0)\sim(f(x),1)\ \text{for all }x\in X.$$

Concretely, the elements of $W$ are:
- $[y]$ for $y\in f(X)$ — a single equivalence class,
- $[y,0]$ and $[y,1]$ for $y\notin f(X)$ — two distinct classes.

Define $g,h\colon Y\to W$ by:
$$g(y)=\begin{cases}[y]&y\in f(X)\\ [y,0]&y\notin f(X)\end{cases},\qquad h(y)=\b
egin{cases}[y]&y\in f(X)\\ [y,1]&y\notin f(X)\end{cases}.$$

Then $g(y)=h(y)\iff y\in f(X)$.

Define $G\colon W\times W\to\Omega$ by:
- $G([y],[y'])=F(y,y')$ for $y,y'\in f(X)$,
- $G([y,i],[y',j])=F(y,y')$ if $i=j$, for $y,y'\notin f(X)$,
- $G([y,i],[y',j])=\bot$ if $i\ne j$, for $y,y'\notin f(X)$,
- $G([y],[y',i])=F(y,y')$ for $y\in f(X)$, $y'\notin f(X)$.

**$G$ is a valid $\Omega$-valued equality.** Strictness: $G(a,b)\le G(a,a)$ foll
ows from $F(y,y')\le F(y,y)$ (strictness of $F$) and $\bot\le\text{anything}$. S
ymmetry is immediate from symmetry of $F$. Transitivity: the only nontrivial cas
es are those involving a mix of indices $0$ and $1$; there, one factor is $\bot$
, so the meet is $\bot$, and $\bot\le G(a,c)$ always. For same-index or $f(X)$-o
nly cases, transitivity of $G$ reduces to transitivity of $F$.

**$g$ and $h$ are morphisms $(Y,F)\to (W,G)$.** We check $F(y,y')\le G(g(y),g(y'
))$ (and similarly for $h$) by cases:
- $y,y'\in f(X)$: $F(y,y')\le F(y,y')=G([y],[y'])$. ✓
- $y,y'\notin f(X)$: $F(y,y')\le F(y,y')=G([y,0],[y',0])$. ✓
- $y\in f(X),\,y'\notin f(X)$: $F(y,y')\le F(y,y')=G([y],[y',0])$. ✓

The verification for $h$ is identical (using index $1$ instead of $0$, and the s
ame cross-term value $F(y,y')$).

**The equalizer of $g,h$ is $(f(X),F|_{f(X)})\hookrightarrow (Y,F)$.** By Step 2
, the equalizer is $\{y\in Y:g(y)=h(y)\}=f(X)$ with equality $F|_{f(X)\times f(X
)}$.

Since $f$ is injective and $E(x,x')=F(f(x),f(x'))$, the map $f\colon (X,E)\to (f
(X),F|_{f(X)})$ is an isomorphism of $\Omega$-valued sets. Composing with the in
clusion, $f\colon (X,E)\to (Y,F)$ is the equalizer of $g$ and $h$ (up to isomorp
hism). $\square$

> **A morphism $f\colon (X,E)\to (Y,F)$ in $\mathcal{C}_\Omega$ is a regular mon
