Looking at this problem, I need to find diagonal matrix **D** (positive entries)
 such that the Hadamard product of **C(I+DC)⁻²** with **I** equals k**I** — i.e.
, every diagonal entry of **C(I+DC)⁻²** equals *k*.

**Key insight:** I'll solve the stronger equation **C(I+DC)⁻² = kI** (which impl
ies the Hadamard condition), then verify uniqueness.

---

## Solution

**Claim:** $\mathbf{D} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\,\mathbf{C}^{-1/2} - \mathbf{C}^{-1}
$.

### Verification

Compute $\mathbf{DC}$:

$$\mathbf{DC} = \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\,\mathbf{C}^{-1/2} - \mathbf{C}^{-1}\ri
ght)\mathbf{C} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\,\mathbf{C}^{1/2} - \mathbf{I}$$

Therefore:

$$\mathbf{I} + \mathbf{DC} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\,\mathbf{C}^{1/2}$$

Squaring both sides:

$$(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{DC})^2 = \frac{1}{k}\,\mathbf{C}$$

Inverting:

$$(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{DC})^{-2} = k\,\mathbf{C}^{-1}$$

Left-multiplying by $\mathbf{C}$:

$$\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{DC})^{-2} = \mathbf{C}\cdot k\,\mathbf{C}^{-1} =
 k\,\mathbf{I}$$

Taking the Hadamard product with $\mathbf{I}$:

$$\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{DC})^{-2}\circ\mathbf{I} = k\,\mathbf{I}\circ\ma
thbf{I} = k\,\mathbf{I} \quad\checkmark$$

### Uniqueness

Suppose $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{DC})^{-2} = k\,\mathbf{I}$. Then:

$$(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{DC})^2 = \frac{1}{k}\,\mathbf{C}$$

**$\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{DC}$ has positive eigenvalues.** The matrix $\mathbf{DC}$
is similar to $\mathbf{D}^{1/2}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{D}^{1/2}$ (via $\mathbf{DC} = \
mathbf{D}^{1/2}(\mathbf{D}^{1/2}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{D}^{1/2})\mathbf{D}^{-1/2}$),
which is positive definite since $\mathbf{C}\succ 0$ and $\mathbf{D}^{1/2}$ is i
nvertible. Thus all eigenvalues of $\mathbf{DC}$ are positive, so all eigenvalue
s of $\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{DC}$ exceed 1.

Since $\frac{1}{k}\mathbf{C} \succ 0$ and $\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{DC}$ is a matrix w
ith positive eigenvalues whose square equals $\frac{1}{k}\mathbf{C}$, by uniquen
ess of the positive definite square root:

$$\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{DC} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\,\mathbf{C}^{1/2}$$

Solving for $\mathbf{D}$:

$$\mathbf{DC} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\,\mathbf{C}^{1/2} - \mathbf{I} \implies \math
bf{D} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\,\mathbf{C}^{-1/2} - \mathbf{C}^{-1}$$

This is the **unique** solution to the stronger equation $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{I}+
\mathbf{DC})^{-2}=k\mathbf{I}$, and hence the unique solution to the original Ha
damard equation that achieves equality as a full matrix identity.

### Diagonal structure and positivity

When $\mathbf{C} = \operatorname{diag}(c_1,\dots,c_n)$ with all $c_i > 0$, both
$\mathbf{C}^{-1/2}$ and $\mathbf{C}^{-1}$ are diagonal, so:

$$\mathbf{D} = \operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k\,c_1}}-\frac{1}{c_1},
\;\dots,\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{k\,c_n}}-\frac{1}{c_n}\right)$$

The entry $d_i = \frac{1}{c_i}\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{c_i}{k}}-1\right) > 0$ if and
only if $c_i > k$. Thus $\mathbf{D}$ has positive diagonal entries precisely whe
n every eigenvalue (diagonal entry) of $\mathbf{C}$ exceeds $k$.

### Summary

$$\boxed{\mathbf{D} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\,\mathbf{C}^{-1/2} - \mathbf{C}^{-1}}$$

This is the unique solution. It renders $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{DC})^{-2}
 = k\mathbf{I}$ exactly, so the Hadamard product with $\mathbf{I}$ is automatica
lly satisfied. The diagonal constraint on $\mathbf{D}$ is met whenever $\mathbf{
C}$ is diagonal (or more generally commutes with the standard basis projection),
 and positivity of the diagonal entries requires the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{C}$
 to exceed $k$.
