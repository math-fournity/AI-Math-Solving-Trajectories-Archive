## Proof

Let $f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_n z^n$ be the Taylor expansion of $f$ about $0$,
valid on all of $\mathbb{C}$ since $f$ is entire.

**Step 1: $f(0)=0$.**
Setting $z=0$ in $|f(z)|\le \pi|z|$ gives $|f(0)|\le 0$, hence $a_0=f(0)=0$.

**Step 2: Apply Cauchy's inequality.**
For each $R>0$ let $M(R)=\max_{|z|=R}|f(z)|$. By hypothesis $M(R)\le \pi R$. Cau
chy's coefficient estimate gives, for every $n\ge 0$,
$$|a_n|\le \frac{M(R)}{R^n}\le \frac{\pi R}{R^n}=\frac{\pi}{R^{\,n-1}}.$$

**Step 3: Vanish coefficients with $n\ge 2$.**
For $n\ge 2$ the exponent $n-1\ge 1$ is positive, so letting $R\to\infty$,
$$|a_n|\le \frac{\pi}{R^{\,n-1}}\xrightarrow[R\to\infty]{}0.$$
Thus $a_n=0$ for every $n\ge 2$.

**Step 4: Identify $f$ and compute $f''$.**
Combining Steps 1 and 3, $f(z)=a_1 z$ with $|a_1|\le \pi$ (from the $n=1$ case o
f Cauchy's inequality, $|a_1|\le \pi R/R=\pi$). Therefore
$$f'(z)=a_1,\qquad f''(z)=0$$
for every $z\in\mathbb{C}$.

---

**Conclusion.** For every $z\in\mathbb{C}$,
$$\boxed{f''(z)=0.}$$
