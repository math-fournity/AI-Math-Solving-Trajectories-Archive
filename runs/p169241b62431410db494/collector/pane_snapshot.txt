## Proof

**Claim: Yes.** The Schwartz kernel theorem, combined with the uniform boundedne
ss principle (Banach–Steinhaus), implies that every separately continuous biline
ar functional $T:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^N)\times\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^M)\to\mat
hbb{C}$ is represented by a unique distribution $K\in\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^{N+
M})$, and the bound $|T(f,g)|\le C\,\|f\|_{D_1}\|g\|_{D_2}$ holds for suitable S
chwartz seminorms $\|\cdot\|_{D_1},\|\cdot\|_{D_2}$ and a constant $C$ independe
nt of $f,g$.

The proof proceeds in four steps.

---

### Step 1. From separate continuity to a weakly continuous linear map

Fix $g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^M)$. By separate continuity, $f\mapsto T(f,g)$ i
s a continuous linear functional on $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^N)$, i.e. an element
 of $\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^N)$. Define

$$A:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^M)\to\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^N),\qquad A(g)=T(\cdot,g
).$$

$A$ is linear. Moreover, for each fixed $f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^N)$, the map

$$g\longmapsto \langle A(g),f\rangle = T(f,g)$$

is continuous by separate continuity in the second argument. Hence $A$ is contin
uous when $\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^N)$ carries the weak-* topology $\sigma(\math
cal{S}',\mathcal{S})$.

---

### Step 2. Upgrading weak-* continuity to strong continuity (Banach–Steinhaus)

The strong topology $\beta(\mathcal{S}',\mathcal{S})$ on $\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R
}^N)$ is generated by seminorms

$$p_B(\ell)=\sup_{f\in B}|\langle\ell,f\rangle|,\qquad B\subset\mathcal{S}(\math
bb{R}^N)\text{ bounded}.$$

We must show: for every bounded $B\subset\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^N)$, the map $g\
mapsto p_B(A(g))$ is continuous on $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^M)$.

Consider the family of linear functionals on $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^M)$:

$$\mathcal{F}_B=\bigl\{\,g\mapsto\langle A(g),f\rangle : f\in B\,\bigr\}.$$

Each member of $\mathcal{F}_B$ is continuous (Step 1). For each fixed $g$, the s
et $\{A(g)(f):f\in B\}$ is bounded, because $A(g)\in\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^N)$
is a continuous linear functional and continuous functionals are bounded on boun
ded sets. Hence

$$\sup_{f\in B}|\langle A(g),f\rangle|<\infty\quad\text{for each }g.$$

Since $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^M)$ is a **Fréchet space** (hence **barreled**), t
he **Banach–Steinhaus theorem** (uniform boundedness principle) applies: the fam
ily $\mathcal{F}_B$ is **equicontinuous**. That is, there exists a continuous se
minorm $q_B$ on $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^M)$ such that

$$|\langle A(g),f\rangle|\le q_B(g)\quad\text{for all }f\in B,\;g\in\mathcal{S}(
\mathbb{R}^M).$$

Taking the supremum over $f\in B$:

$$p_B(A(g))\le q_B(g),$$

so $g\mapsto p_B(A(g))$ is continuous. Therefore $A:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^M)\to
\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^N)$ is **strongly continuous**.

---

### Step 3. Applying the Schwartz kernel theorem

The **Schwartz kernel theorem** states that there is a linear isomorphism

$$\mathcal{L}\bigl(\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^M),\,\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^N)\bigr)\
;\cong\;\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^{N+M}),$$

where $\mathcal{L}$ denotes strongly continuous linear maps. Concretely, every $
A\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^M),\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^N))$ correspon
ds to a unique $K\in\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^{N+M})$ such that

$$\langle A(g),f\rangle = K(f\otimes g),\qquad (f\otimes g)(x,y)=f(x)\,g(y).$$

By Step 2, $A$ is strongly continuous, so the kernel theorem yields a unique $K\
in\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^{N+M})$ with

$$T(f,g)=K(f\otimes g)\quad\text{for all }f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^N),\;g\in\m
athcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^M).$$

---

### Step 4. The product bound

Since $K\in\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^{N+M})$ is a continuous linear functional on
the Fréchet space $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N+M})$, there exists a continuous sem
inorm $\|\cdot\|_D$ on $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N+M})$ and a constant $C>0$ such
 that

$$|K(h)|\le C\,\|h\|_D\quad\text{for all }h\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N+M}).$$

The Schwartz topology on $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N+M})$ is generated by seminor
ms of the form

$$\|h\|_{\alpha,\beta}=\sup_{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+M}}\bigl|x^{\alpha_1}y^{\alph
a_2}\,\partial_x^{\beta_1}\partial_y^{\beta_2}h(x,y)\bigr|,$$

with $\alpha=(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)$, $\beta=(\beta_1,\beta_2)$ multi-indices. The
seminorm $\|\cdot\|_D$ is a finite maximum of such seminorms.

For a pure tensor $h=f\otimes g$, i.e. $h(x,y)=f(x)g(y)$, we have the **factoriz
ation**:

$$x^{\alpha_1}y^{\alpha_2}\,\partial_x^{\beta_1}\partial_y^{\beta_2}(f\otimes g)
(x,y)=\bigl(x^{\alpha_1}\partial_x^{\beta_1}f(x)\bigr)\bigl(y^{\alpha_2}\partial
_y^{\beta_2}g(y)\bigr),$$

so

$$\|f\otimes g\|_{\alpha,\beta}=\|f\|_{\alpha_1,\beta_1}^{(N)}\cdot\|g\|_{\alpha
_2,\beta_2}^{(M)},$$

where $\|f\|_{\alpha_1,\beta_1}^{(N)}=\sup_x|x^{\alpha_1}\partial^{\beta_1}f(x)|
$ is a Schwartz seminorm on $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^N)$ and similarly for $g$. T
aking the finite maximum over the seminorms defining $\|\cdot\|_D$, there exist
Schwartz seminorms $\|\cdot\|_{D_1}$ on $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^N)$ and $\|\cdot
\|_{D_2}$ on $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^M)$ such that

$$\|f\otimes g\|_D\le\|f\|_{D_1}\,\|g\|_{D_2}.$$

Combining with Step 3 and the continuity of $K$:

$$\boxed{|T(f,g)|=|K(f\otimes g)|\le C\,\|f\otimes g\|_D\le C\,\|f\|_{D_1}\,\|g\
|_{D_2}.}$$

The constant $C$ and the seminorms $\|\cdot\|_{D_1},\|\cdot\|_{D_2}$ depend only
 on $T$ (equivalently, on $K$), not on $f$ or $g$.

---

### Summary

The implication holds, and the argument has three essential ingredients:

1. **Separate continuity** $\Rightarrow$ weak-* continuity of the induced map $A
:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^M)\to\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^N)$.
2. **Banach–Steinhaus** (valid because $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^M)$ is Fréchet/ba
rreled) $\Rightarrow$ strong continuity of $A$.
3. **Schwartz kernel theorem** $\Rightarrow$ existence of $K\in\mathcal{S}'(\mat
hbb{R}^{N+M})$, whose continuity gives the product bound via factorization of Sc
hwartz seminorms on tensor products.

The answer is **yes**.
