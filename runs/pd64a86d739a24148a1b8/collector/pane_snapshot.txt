## Solution

The function is $\cot(z) = \dfrac{\cos z}{\sin z}$.

**Step 1: Identify the singularity type at $z=0$.**

We have $\sin(0) = 0$ and $\sin'(0) = \cos(0) = 1 \neq 0$, so $\sin z$ has a **s
imple zero** at $z = 0$. Meanwhile $\cos(0) = 1 \neq 0$. Therefore $\cot z = \co
s z / \sin z$ has a **simple pole** at $z = 0$.

**Step 2: Determine the form of the principal part.**

A simple pole means the Laurent expansion of $\cot z$ about $z = 0$ has exactly
one term with a negative power of $z$:

$$\cot z = \frac{a_{-1}}{z} + a_0 + a_1 z + a_2 z^2 + \cdots$$

The **principal part** (the collection of all negative-power terms) is therefore
 just $\dfrac{a_{-1}}{z}$, where $a_{-1} = \operatorname{Res}(\cot z,\, 0)$.

**Step 3: Compute the residue.**

$$a_{-1} = \lim_{z \to 0} z \cdot \cot z = \lim_{z \to 0} \frac{z \cos z}{\sin z
} = \lim_{z \to 0} \cos z \cdot \lim_{z \to 0} \frac{z}{\sin z} = 1 \cdot 1 = 1,
$$

using $\cos z \to 1$ and the standard limit $\dfrac{\sin z}{z} \to 1$, hence $\d
frac{z}{\sin z} \to 1$.

**Step 4: State the principal part.**

Since the only negative-power coefficient is $a_{-1} = 1$, the principal part of
 the Laurent series of $\cot z$ at $z = 0$ is

$$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{z}}.$$

*Remark.* The full Laurent series is $\cot z = \dfrac{1}{z} - \dfrac{z}{3} - \df
rac{z^{3}}{45} - \dfrac{2z^{5}}{945} - \cdots$, confirming that the sole negativ
e-power term is $1/z$.
