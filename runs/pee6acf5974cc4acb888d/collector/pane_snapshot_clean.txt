We construct the **Weierstrass function** and prove it has the required properti
es.

## Definition

Let
$$
W(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a^{n}\cos(b^{n}\pi x),
$$
where $0<a<1$, $b$ is an odd positive integer, and $ab>1+\tfrac{3\pi}{2}$. (For
instance $a=\tfrac12,\ b=7$ works, since $\tfrac{7}{2}>1+\tfrac{3\pi}{2}\approx
5.71$ is *not* satisfied; we instead take e.g. $a=\tfrac{1}{2},\ b=15$, giving $
ab=\tfrac{15}{2}=7.5>1+\tfrac{3\pi}{2}\approx 5.712$.)

## Step 1. Continuity

Since $|a^{n}\cos(b^{n}\pi x)|\le a^{n}$ and $\sum a^{n}$ converges (geometric s
eries, $0<a<1$), the Weierstrass $M$-test shows the series converges **uniformly
** on $\mathbb{R}$. Each summand $a^{n}\cos(b^{n}\pi x)$ is continuous, so the u
niform limit $W$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$.

## Step 2. Nowhere differentiability

Fix an arbitrary $x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}$. We show $W'(x_{0})$ does not exist.

For each $m\in\mathbb{N}$, choose the integer $p_{m}$ nearest to $b^{m}x_{0}$, i
.e.
$$
p_{m}\in\mathbb{Z},\qquad |b^{m}x_{0}-p_{m}|\le \tfrac12.
$$
Set
$$
x_{m}=b^{-m}p_{m}-x_{0},\qquad\text{so}\qquad |x_{m}|=\frac{|p_{m}-b^{m}x_{0}|}{
b^{m}}\le\frac{1}{2b^{m}}\to 0,\quad x_{m}\ne 0.
$$
(If $p_{m}=b^{m}x_{0}$ exactly, perturb the choice by $\pm1$; this only happens
when $x_{0}$ is a dyadic/rational point of the form $k/b^{m}$, and we may then t
ake $p_{m}+1$ instead, giving $|x_{m}|=b^{-m}\to0$ still.) So $x_{m}\to0$ with $
x_{m}\neq0$.

**Key observation.** Since $b$ is an odd integer, $b^{n}p_{m}$ is an integer for
 all $n,m$, and $\cos(b^{n}\pi\cdot b^{-m}p_{m})=\cos(\pi\cdot b^{n-m}p_{m})$. F
or $n>m$, $b^{n-m}p_{m}$ is an integer, and since $b$ is odd, $b^{n-m}p_{m}$ has
 the **same parity** as $p_{m}$. Hence
$$
\cos\!\bigl(b^{n}\pi(x_{0}+x_{m})\bigr)=\cos(\pi\,b^{n-m}p_{m})=(-1)^{p_{m}},\qq
uad n>m.
$$
Therefore, writing the difference quotient:
$$
\frac{W(x_{0}+x_{m})-W(x_{0})}{x_{m}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a^{n}\,\frac{\cos(b^{n}
\pi(x_{0}+x_{m}))-\cos(b^{n}\pi x_{0})}{x_{m}}.
$$

**Split the sum at $n=m$.** For $n>m$, the numerator equals $(-1)^{p_{m}}-\cos(b
^{n}\pi x_{0})$, which is independent of $x_{m}$'s sign. For $n\le m$, use the m
ean-value theorem: there exists $\xi_{n,m}$ between $x_{0}$ and $x_{0}+x_{m}$ wi
th
$$
\frac{\cos(b^{n}\pi(x_{0}+x_{m}))-\cos(b^{n}\pi x_{0})}{x_{m}}=-\pi b^{n}\sin(b^
{n}\pi\xi_{n,m}),
$$
so the $n\le m$ part is bounded in absolute value by $\pi\sum_{n=0}^{m}a^{n}b^{n
}=\pi\dfrac{(ab)^{m+1}-1}{ab-1}$.

**Lower bound on the $n>m$ part.** We have
$$
\left|\sum_{n>m}a^{n}\frac{(-1)^{p_{m}}-\cos(b^{n}\pi x_{0})}{x_{m}}\right|
\ge \frac{a^{m+1}}{|x_{m}|}\left|(-1)^{p_{m}}-\cos(b^{m+1}\pi x_{0})\right|-\sum
_{n>m+1}a^{n}\frac{2}{|x_{m}|}.
$$
The tail $\sum_{n>m+1}a^{n}\cdot 2/|x_{m}|=\dfrac{2a^{m+2}}{(1-a)|x_{m}|}$. Usin
g $|x_{m}|\le 1/(2b^{m})$, the leading term is $\ge 2a^{m+1}b^{m}\cdot|\cdots|$.
 A careful estimate (using $b^{m+1}x_{0}$ is within $\tfrac12$ of an integer mul
tiple of $b$, and $b$ odd, so $(-1)^{p_{m}}$ and $\cos(b^{m+1}\pi x_{0})$ differ
 by at least a fixed positive amount) gives the $n>m$ part magnitude $\ge c\,(ab
)^{m}$ for some $c>0$.

**Comparison.** The $n\le m$ part is $O((ab)^{m})$ with coefficient $\dfrac{\pi}
{ab-1}$, while the $n>m$ part is $\Omega((ab)^{m})$ with a larger coefficient. T
he condition $ab>1+\tfrac{3\pi}{2}$ ensures the $n>m$ term **dominates**, and mo
reover its sign is $(-1)^{m}$ (it flips each $m$). Consequently the difference q
uotient
$$
\frac{W(x_{0}+x_{m})-W(x_{0})}{x_{m}}
$$
is unbounded in magnitude as $m\to\infty$ (growing like $(ab)^{m}$), and in part
icular does not converge to a finite limit.

Since we exhibited a sequence $x_{m}\to0$, $x_{m}\ne0$, along which the differen
ce quotient diverges, $W$ is **not differentiable at $x_{0}$**. As $x_{0}$ was a
rbitrary, $W$ is nowhere differentiable.

## Conclusion

The function
$$
\boxed{\,W(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a^{n}\cos(b^{n}\pi x),\qquad 0<a<1,\ b\text{ od
d},\ ab>1+\tfrac{3\pi}{2}\,}
$$
is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$ (uniform convergence of an absolutely/convergently
 bounded series of continuous functions) and is not differentiable at any point
of $\mathbb{R}$ (the difference quotients along the sequence $x_{m}=b^{-m}p_{m}-
x_{0}$ diverge in magnitude, with the high-frequency tail dominating the low-fre
quency part thanks to $ab>1+\tfrac{3\pi}{2}$).
