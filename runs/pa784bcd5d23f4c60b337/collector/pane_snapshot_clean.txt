Looking at this problem, I need to find the largest class size where: (1) no 6 c
hildren are all mutual friends, and (2) for any non-friend pair within any 6 chi
ldren, some other child among those 6 is friends with both.

**Answer: 25**

## Proof

**Setup.** Model the class as a graph $G$ on $n$ vertices (children), with edges
 representing friendship. Let $H = \overline{G}$ be the complement graph (non-fr
iendship).

**Translating the conditions.**

- *Condition 1* (any 6 children contain a non-friend pair): $G$ has no 6-clique,
 so $\omega(G) \le 5$, equivalently $\alpha(H) \le 5$.

- *Condition 2* (for any non-friend pair $\{u,v\}$ among any 6, some of the othe
r 4 is friends with both): For every edge $\{u,v\}$ in $H$ and every 4-element s
et $T \subseteq V \setminus \{u,v\}$, at least one vertex in $T$ is a common nei
ghbor of $u,v$ in $G$. A vertex $w \neq u,v$ is a common neighbor of $u,v$ in $G
$ iff $w$ is non-adjacent to both $u$ and $v$ in $H$. So the condition requires:
 the set of vertices that are **not** common neighbors (i.e., adjacent to $u$ or
 $v$ in $H$, excluding $u,v$) has size at most 3. Equivalently:
$$|N_H(u) \cup N_H(v)| \le 5 \quad \text{for every edge } \{u,v\} \in H. \tag{$\
star$}$$

**Key observation: $\Delta(H) \le 4$.** For any vertex $v$ with a neighbor $u$,
condition $(\star)$ gives $|N_H(v)| \le |N_H(u) \cup N_H(v)| \le 5$, so $\deg_H(
v) \le 4$. (Isolated vertices have degree 0.) Thus every vertex has degree at mo
st 4.

**Upper bound: $n \le 25$.** Since $\Delta(H) \le 4$, a greedy algorithm constru
cts an independent set of size at least $\lceil n/5 \rceil$: repeatedly pick a v
ertex $v$, add it to the independent set, and remove $N[v]$ (at most 5 vertices)
. This yields $\alpha(H) \ge \lceil n/5 \rceil$. Combined with $\alpha(H) \le 5$
:
$$\left\lceil \frac{n}{5} \right\rceil \le 5 \implies n \le 25.$$

**Construction: $n = 25$ is achievable.** Partition 25 children into 5 groups of
 5. Define friendship: two children are friends **iff** they belong to different
 groups. Then $H$ is the disjoint union of 5 copies of $K_5$.

- *Check $\alpha(H) \le 5$:* $\alpha(H) = 5 \cdot \alpha(K_5) = 5 \cdot 1 = 5$.
✓

- *Check $(\star)$:* Every edge lies within some $K_5$. For an edge $\{u,v\}$ in
 $K_5$: $N_H(u) \cup N_H(v)$ is the entire $K_5$ (5 vertices). So $|N_H(u) \cup
N_H(v)| = 5 \le 5$. ✓

- *Check Condition 1 directly:* Any 6 children fall into 5 groups, so by pigeonh
ole at least 2 share a group — they are not friends. ✓

- *Check Condition 2 directly:* A non-friend pair $\{u,v\}$ lies in the same gro
up (size 5). Among any 4 other children, at most 3 can be from that same group (
since $u,v$ occupy 2 of the 5 slots). So at least one of the 4 is from a differe
nt group, hence friends with both $u$ and $v$. ✓

**Conclusion.** The upper bound $n \le 25$ and the matching construction prove t
he largest possible number of children is $\boxed{25}$.
