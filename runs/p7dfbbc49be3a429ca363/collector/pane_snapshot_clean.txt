## Proof

We prove the **Borsuk (Antipodal) Theorem**: if $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is
 a bounded, open, symmetric ($\Omega=-\Omega$) neighborhood of $0$, and $f\in C(
\bar\Omega,\mathbb{R}^n)$ is odd with $0\notin f(\partial\Omega)$, then $\mathrm
{d}(f,\Omega,0)$ is odd.

### Step 1 — Reduction to the smooth, regular case

Since $0\notin f(\partial\Omega)$, there is $\delta>0$ with $\operatorname{dist}
(0,f(\partial\Omega))>\delta$. By standard approximation, choose a smooth $h:\ba
r\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^n$ with $\|h-f\|_\infty<\delta$; then $0\notin h(\partial\O
mega)$ and $h$ is homotopic to $f$ through $tf+(1-t)h$ staying away from $0$ on
$\partial\Omega$, so $\mathrm{d}(h,\Omega,0)=\mathrm{d}(f,\Omega,0)$. Now set
$$g(x)=\tfrac12\bigl(h(x)-h(-x)\bigr).$$
Then $g$ is smooth, odd, and $\|g-f\|_\infty\le\|h-f\|_\infty<\delta$, so $0\not
in g(\partial\Omega)$ and $g$ is homotopic to $f$ (hence to $h$) avoiding $0$ on
 $\partial\Omega$. Thus $\mathrm{d}(f,\Omega,0)=\mathrm{d}(g,\Omega,0)$. Finally
, by Sard's theorem, perturb $g$ slightly (preserving oddness via the same avera
ging trick) so that $0$ is a **regular value** of $g$ and $0\notin g(\partial\Om
ega)$ still holds; the degree is unchanged. Hence we may assume $f$ is $C^1$, od
d, and $0$ is a regular value.

### Step 2 — Structure of $f^{-1}(0)\cap\Omega$

Since $f$ is odd, $f(0)=0$, so $0\in f^{-1}(0)\cap\Omega$. For any $x\neq 0$ wit
h $f(x)=0$, oddness gives $f(-x)=-f(x)=0$, and $-x\neq x$. Thus the **nonzero**
zeros come in antipodal pairs $\{x,-x\}$, while $0$ is a singleton. By regularit
y, $f^{-1}(0)\cap\Omega$ is finite, so
$$f^{-1}(0)\cap\Omega=\{0\}\cup\{x_1,-x_1,\dots,x_m,-x_m\}.$$

### Step 3 — Sign relation between $x$ and $-x$

Differentiating $f(-x)=-f(x)$ componentwise: $\frac{\partial f_j}{\partial x_i}(
-x)=\frac{\partial f_j}{\partial x_i}(x)$, i.e.
$$Df(-x)=Df(x).$$
Therefore $\det Df(-x)=\det Df(x)$, and $\operatorname{sign}\det Df(-x)=\operato
rname{sign}\det Df(x)$.

### Step 4 — Computing the degree

By the degree formula for a regular value,
$$\mathrm{d}(f,\Omega,0)=\sum_{x\in f^{-1}(0)\cap\Omega}\operatorname{sign}\det
Df(x).$$
Each antipodal pair $\{x_i,-x_i\}$ contributes
$$\operatorname{sign}\det Df(x_i)+\operatorname{sign}\det Df(-x_i)=2\,\operatorn
ame{sign}\det Df(x_i)\in\{+2,-2\},$$
and the origin contributes $\operatorname{sign}\det Df(0)\in\{+1,-1\}$ (nonzero
by regularity). Hence
$$\mathrm{d}(f,\Omega,0)=\operatorname{sign}\det Df(0)+2\sum_{i=1}^{m}\operatorn
ame{sign}\det Df(x_i),$$
which is an integer of the form $\pm 1 + 2k$ for some $k\in\mathbb{Z}$. This is
**odd**. $\blacksquare$
