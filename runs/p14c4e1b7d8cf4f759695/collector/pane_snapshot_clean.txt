## Proof

### Part 1: Computing $\aleph_2^{\aleph_0}$

I use the standard fact that for cardinals $2 \leq \kappa \leq 2^\lambda$ with $
\lambda$ infinite, $\kappa^\lambda = 2^\lambda$. The proof: $2^\lambda \leq \kap
pa^\lambda \leq (2^\lambda)^\lambda = 2^{\lambda \cdot \lambda} = 2^\lambda$.

Here $\lambda = \aleph_0$ and $\kappa = \aleph_2$. Since $2 \leq \aleph_2 \leq 2
^{\aleph_0} = \aleph_3$, the conditions are satisfied, giving:

$$\aleph_2^{\aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph_0} = \aleph_3.$$

### Part 2: Computing $\aleph_0^{\aleph_2}$

Now set $\lambda = \aleph_2$ and $\kappa = \aleph_0$. Since $2 \leq \aleph_0 \le

önig's theorem), the same fact gives:

$$\aleph_0^{\aleph_2} = 2^{\aleph_2}.$$

### Part 3: The sum

The expression becomes:

$$\aleph_2^{\aleph_0} + \aleph_0^{\aleph_2} = \aleph_3 + 2^{\aleph_2}.$$

By monotonicity of the continuum function, since $\aleph_2 \geq \aleph_0$:

$$2^{\aleph_2} \geq 2^{\aleph_0} = \aleph_3.$$

For infinite cardinal addition, $\alpha + \beta = \max(\alpha, \beta)$ when at l
east one is infinite. Since $2^{\aleph_2} \geq \aleph_3$:

$$\aleph_3 + 2^{\aleph_2} = \max(\aleph_3,\; 2^{\aleph_2}) = 2^{\aleph_2}.$$

- Monotonicity: $2^{\aleph_2} \geq 2^{\aleph_0} = \aleph_3$.

Thus $2^{\aleph_2}$ could consistently be $\aleph_3, \aleph_4, \aleph_5, \dots$
(any $\aleph_\alpha$ with $\operatorname{cf}(\aleph_\alpha) > \aleph_2$). The hy
