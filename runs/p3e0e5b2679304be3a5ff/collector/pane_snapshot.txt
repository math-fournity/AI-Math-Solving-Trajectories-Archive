## Solution

We evaluate
$$I = \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\arctan^2 x}{x(1+x^2)}\,dx.$$

**Step 1: Substitution.** Let $t = \arctan x$, so $x = \tan t$, $dx = \sec^2 t\,
dt$, and $1+x^2 = \sec^2 t$. As $x: 0\to\infty$, $t: 0\to \pi/2$. Then
$$I = \int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{t^2}{\tan t\,\sec^2 t}\,\sec^2 t\,dt = \int_0^{\pi/2}
 t^2 \cot t\,dt.$$

**Step 2: Integration by parts.** With $u=t^2$, $dv=\cot t\,dt$, so $v=\ln\sin t
$:
$$I = \bigl[t^2\ln\sin t\bigr]_0^{\pi/2} - \int_0^{\pi/2} 2t\,\ln\sin t\,dt.$$
The boundary term vanishes: at $t=\pi/2$, $\ln\sin(\pi/2)=0$; at $t\to 0^+$, $t^
2\ln\sin t \sim t^2\ln t \to 0$. Hence
$$I = -2\int_0^{\pi/2} t\,\ln\sin t\,dt.$$

**Step 3: Fourier expansion.** For $t\in(0,\pi)$,
$$\ln\sin t = -\ln 2 - \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\cos 2nt}{n}.$$
Thus
$$\int_0^{\pi/2} t\,\ln\sin t\,dt = -\ln 2\int_0^{\pi/2} t\,dt - \sum_{n=1}^{\in
fty}\frac{1}{n}\int_0^{\pi/2} t\cos 2nt\,dt.$$

We compute the two ingredients:
- $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2} t\,dt = \frac{\pi^2}{8}$.
- $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2} t\cos 2nt\,dt = \Bigl[\frac{t\sin 2nt}{2n}\Bigr]_
0^{\pi/2} - \int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\sin 2nt}{2n}\,dt = 0 + \frac{1}{4n^2}\bigl[\cos
 2nt\bigr]_0^{\pi/2} = \frac{(-1)^n-1}{4n^2}$,
  since $\sin n\pi = 0$ and $\cos n\pi = (-1)^n$.

Therefore
$$\int_0^{\pi/2} t\,\ln\sin t\,dt = -\frac{\pi^2}{8}\ln 2 - \frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=1
}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n-1}{n^3}.$$

**Step 4: The zeta sum.** Using $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n}{n
^3} = -\eta(3) = -\frac{3}{4}\zeta(3)$ (where $\eta(s)=(1-2^{1-s})\zeta(s)$), we
 get
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n-1}{n^3} = -\frac{3}{4}\zeta(3) - \zeta(3) = -\
frac{7}{4}\zeta(3).$$

Hence
$$\int_0^{\pi/2} t\,\ln\sin t\,dt = -\frac{\pi^2}{8}\ln 2 + \frac{7}{16}\zeta(3)
.$$

**Step 5: Conclusion.**
$$I = -2\left(-\frac{\pi^2}{8}\ln 2 + \frac{7}{16}\zeta(3)\right) = \frac{\pi^2}
{4}\ln 2 - \frac{7}{8}\zeta(3).$$

A quick sanity check: $\frac{\pi^2}{4}\ln 2 \approx 1.710$ and $\frac{7}{8}\zeta
(3)\approx 1.052$, giving $I\approx 0.658 > 0$, consistent with the integrand be
ing positive on $(0,\pi/2)$.

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\arctan^2 x}{x(1+x^2)}\,dx = \frac
{\pi^2}{4}\ln 2 - \frac{7}{8}\,\zeta(3).}$$
