## Solution

The transformation is the **Schwarz–Christoffel transformation**.

### Setup

Let the upper half plane be $\mathbb{H} = \{z : \operatorname{Im} z > 0\}$, with
 the real axis as its boundary. We seek a conformal map $w = f(z)$ taking $\math
bb{H}$ onto the interior of a polygon with vertices $w_1, w_2, \ldots, w_n$ (lis
ted in order along the polygon boundary) and corresponding interior angles $\alp
ha_1 \pi, \alpha_2 \pi, \ldots, \alpha_n \pi$, where $0 < \alpha_k < 2$ and

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} \alpha_k = n - 2.$$

### Derivation

Let $x_1 < x_2 < \cdots < x_n$ be the preimages on the real axis of the vertices
 $w_1, w_2, \ldots, w_n$, respectively (the case $x_n = \infty$ is also permitte
d and is handled by a limiting argument). Since $f$ is conformal and analytic in
 $\mathbb{H}$, its derivative $f'(z)$ is nonvanishing there.

Consider the argument of $f'(z)$ as $z$ travels along the real axis. Between con
secutive prevertices $x_k$ and $x_{k+1}$, the image traverses a straight side of
 the polygon, so $\arg f'(z)$ is constant on each interval $(x_k, x_{k+1})$. At
each prevertex $x_k$, the boundary direction changes by the exterior angle $\pi
- \alpha_k \pi = (1-\alpha_k)\pi$ (turning through the supplement of the interio
r angle). Therefore $\arg f'$ jumps by $(1-\alpha_k)\pi$ at $x_k$.

A function whose argument is constant on each interval and jumps by $(1-\alpha_k
)\pi$ at $x_k$ is, up to a real multiplicative constant,

$$\prod_{k=1}^{n}(z - x_k)^{\alpha_k - 1},$$

since each factor $(z-x_k)^{\alpha_k-1}$ contributes a jump of $(\alpha_k - 1)\p
i = -(\pi - \alpha_k\pi)$ in argument as $z$ crosses $x_k$, which matches the re
quired turning (with the sign convention giving the correct orientation). Hence

$$f'(z) = C\prod_{k=1}^{n}(z - x_k)^{\alpha_k - 1}$$

for some complex constant $C \neq 0$.

### The Schwarz–Christoffel Formula

Integrating,

$$\boxed{\;w = f(z) = C\int^{z}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(\zeta - x_k)^{\alpha_k - 1}\,d\ze
ta + C_{0}\;}$$

where:
- $x_1 < x_2 < \cdots < x_n$ are points on the real axis mapped to the vertices
$w_1, \ldots, w_n$,
- $\alpha_k \pi$ is the interior angle at $w_k$ (so $\alpha_k \in (0,2)$ and $\s
um \alpha_k = n-2$),
- $C$ and $C_0$ are complex constants: $C$ controls scaling and rotation, $C_0$
controls translation.

The powers $(\zeta - x_k)^{\alpha_k-1}$ are defined with the principal branch, a
nalytic in $\mathbb{H}$.

### Three normalization points

By Möbius automorphisms of $\mathbb{H}$ (three real degrees of freedom), three o
f the prevertices $x_k$ may be prescribed arbitrarily (commonly $x_1 = -1,\ x_2
= 0,\ x_3 = 1$, or $x_1 = 0,\ x_2 = 1,\ x_3 = \infty$). The remaining $n-3$ prev
ertices are the **accessory parameters**, determined implicitly by the vertex da
ta $\{w_k, \alpha_k\}$. Together with $C$ and $C_0$, this gives $n-3+4 = n+1$ re
al parameters matching the $n$ vertex positions (minus one for overall translati
on = $2n-2$ real) — the count is consistent once one accounts for the polygon's
rigid motions and the three normalization freedoms.

### Special case: one vertex at infinity

If $x_n = \infty$ is sent to $w_n$, the factor $(\zeta - x_n)^{\alpha_n-1}$ is d
ropped (absorbed into $C$ via the limit), giving the reduced form

$$w = C\int^{z}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(\zeta - x_k)^{\alpha_k - 1}\,d\zeta + C_0.$$

The angle condition becomes $\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\alpha_k = n - 2 - \alpha_n$, consi
stent with the limit.

### Verification of the angle condition

At each prevertex $x_k$, the local behavior of the integral gives $w - w_k \sim
(z - x_k)^{\alpha_k}$, so a half-neighborhood in $\mathbb{H}$ (angle $\pi$ at $x
_k$) maps to a sector of angle $\alpha_k \pi$ — precisely the interior angle at
$w_k$. The sum $\sum \alpha_k \pi = (n-2)\pi$ is the total turning of a simple c
losed polygon, confirming consistency.

This is the **Schwarz–Christoffel transformation**.
