## Proof

Let $W = H \wr K$ be the standard (regular) wreath product, with base group $B =
 \prod_{k \in K} H_k$ (each $H_k \cong H$, indexed by elements of $K$) and $W =
B \rtimes K$, where $K$ acts on $B$ by permuting coordinates via left multiplica
tion on itself:
$$g \cdot (h_k)_{k \in K} = (h_{g^{-1}k})_{k \in K}, \quad g \in K.$$

Since $H$ is abelian, $B$ is abelian. We identify $B$ with elements $(b, e)$ and
 $K$ with elements $(e, g)$ in $W$.

**Step 1: The $K$-component of a central element must be trivial.**

Let $(b, g) \in Z(W)$. Then $(b,g)$ commutes with every $(b', e) \in B$:
$$(b,g)(b',e) = (b\cdot(g\cdot b'),\, g), \qquad (b',e)(b,g) = (b'\cdot b,\, g).
$$
Equating: $b \cdot (g \cdot b') = b' \cdot b$ for all $b' \in B$. Since $B$ is a
belian, $b \cdot b' = b' \cdot b$, so this reduces to
$$g \cdot b' = b' \quad \text{for all } b' \in B.$$
The action of $g$ on $B$ permutes the $|K|$ coordinates via left multiplication
by $g$ on $K$. This permutation fixes every element of $B$ only if it is the tri
vial permutation. Taking $b'$ with a single non-identity coordinate shows the pe
rmutation must fix every index, hence $g \cdot k = k$ for all $k \in K$, i.e., $
g = e$. (The regular action is faithful.)

**Step 2: The $B$-component must be $K$-fixed.**

Now $(b, e)$ is central, so it commutes with every $(e, k) \in K$:
$$(b,e)(e,k) = (b,\, k), \qquad (e,k)(b,e) = (k\cdot b,\, k).$$
Equating: $b = k \cdot b$ for all $k \in K$, i.e., $b$ is fixed by the $K$-actio
n.

**Step 3: The $K$-fixed elements of $B$ are exactly the diagonal elements.**

Write $b = (h_k)_{k \in K}$. The condition $k \cdot b = b$ for all $k$ means $h_
{k^{-1}j} = h_j$ for all $j, k \in K$, i.e., all coordinates are equal: $h_k = h
$ for some fixed $h \in H$ and all $k \in K$.

**Step 4: Diagonal elements are central.**

Conversely, any diagonal element $d(h) = (h)_{k \in K}$:
- Commutes with all of $B$ since $B$ is abelian.
- Commutes with every $(e, k)$ since $k \cdot d(h) = d(h)$ (permuting equal coor
dinates leaves them unchanged).

Since $H$ is abelian, $Z(H) = H$, so every $h \in H$ gives a central diagonal el
ement.

**Conclusion.**

$$Z(H \wr K) = \{(h)_{k \in K} : h \in H\} \cong H,$$

the diagonal copy of $H$ embedded in the base group $B = H^{|K|}$.
