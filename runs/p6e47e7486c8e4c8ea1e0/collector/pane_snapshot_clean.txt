## Proof

**Setup.** The dodecahedron has 20 vertices, 30 edges, and 12 pentagonal faces.
We seek the number of subsets $S$ of edges such that every face contains an even
 number of edges from $S$.

**Linear algebra formulation over $\mathbb{F}_2$.** Let $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{
F}_2^{30}$ be the indicator vector of the selected edge set. For each face $f$,
the constraint "the number of selected edges in $f$ is even" is a linear equatio
n over $\mathbb{F}_2$:

$$\sum_{e \subset f} x_e = 0 \pmod{2}.$$

Stacking all 12 face equations gives a homogeneous system $M\mathbf{x} = \mathbf
{0}$, where $M$ is the $12 \times 30$ **face–edge incidence matrix** of the dode
cahedron. The number of valid configurations is

$$2^{30 - \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_2}(M)}.$$

**Duality reduces to the icosahedron.** The regular dodecahedron and the regular
 icosahedron are dual polyhedra. Under duality:

- each **face** of the dodecahedron ↔ a **vertex** of the icosahedron (12 ↔ 12),
- each **edge** of the dodecahedron ↔ an **edge** of the icosahedron (30 ↔ 30),
- a face is incident to an edge in the dodecahedron ↔ the corresponding vertex i
s incident to the corresponding edge in the icosahedron.

Therefore the face–edge incidence matrix of the dodecahedron **equals** the vert
ex–edge incidence matrix of the icosahedron.

**Rank of the vertex–edge incidence matrix.** Let $N$ be the $12 \times 30$ vert
ex–edge incidence matrix of the icosahedron (viewed as a connected graph with $V
 = 12$ vertices, $E = 30$ edges). Each column of $N$ has exactly two 1's (the tw
o endpoints of the edge).

The null space of $N$ (i.e., $N\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$) consists of edge subset
s where every vertex has even degree — precisely the **cycle space** of the grap
h. For a connected graph, the cycle space over $\mathbb{F}_2$ has dimension

$$E - V + 1 = 30 - 12 + 1 = 19.$$

Hence

$$\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_2}(N) = E - \dim(\ker N) = 30 - 19 = 11.$$

(Equivalently, the rank of the vertex–edge incidence matrix of any connected gra
ph on $V$ vertices is $V - 1 = 11$.)

**Counting.** The number of solutions to $M\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$ is

$$2^{30 - 11} = 2^{19} = 524288.$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{524288}$.
