# Convergence Rate of Newton's Method with Fixed Slope

## Setup

We consider the **simplified Newton method** (also called the chord method), whe
re the derivative is evaluated once at the initial point $x_0$ and reused:

$$x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_0)}, \qquad n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$$

We assume $f \in C^2$ in a neighborhood of a **simple** root $\alpha$ (i.e., $f(
\alpha)=0$, $f'(\alpha)\neq 0$), and that $x_0$ is chosen close enough to $\alph
a$ for convergence.

## Error Recurrence

Let $e_n = x_n - \alpha$. Taylor-expand $f$ and $f'$ about $\alpha$:

$$f(x_n) = f'(\alpha)\,e_n + \tfrac{1}{2}f''(\alpha)\,e_n^2 + O(e_n^3),$$

$$f'(x_0) = f'(\alpha) + f''(\alpha)\,e_0 + O(e_0^2) = f'(\alpha)\!\left(1 + \fr
ac{f''(\alpha)}{f'(\alpha)}\,e_0 + O(e_0^2)\right).$$

Form the quotient (using $\frac{1}{1+u}=1-u+O(u^2)$):

$$\frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_0)} = e_n\left(1 + \frac{f''(\alpha)}{2f'(\alpha)}\,e_n + O
(e_n^2)\right)\!\left(1 - \frac{f''(\alpha)}{f'(\alpha)}\,e_0 + O(e_0^2)\right).
$$

Multiplying out and keeping terms up to first order in $e_n$ and $e_0$:

$$\frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_0)} = e_n + \frac{f''(\alpha)}{2f'(\alpha)}\,e_n^2 - \frac{
f''(\alpha)}{f'(\alpha)}\,e_0\,e_n + \text{higher order}.$$

Substituting into $e_{n+1} = e_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_0)}$:

$$\boxed{e_{n+1} = \frac{f''(\alpha)}{f'(\alpha)}\,e_0\,e_n - \frac{f''(\alpha)}
{2f'(\alpha)}\,e_n^2 + O(e_n^3) + O(e_0^2\,e_n).}$$

## Asymptotic Behavior

Once the iterates are close to $\alpha$, $e_n$ becomes small and the **quadratic
 term** $e_n^2$ is negligible compared to the **linear term** $e_0\,e_n$ (recall
 $e_0$ is a fixed constant). The recurrence reduces to

$$e_{n+1} \approx C\,e_n, \qquad C := \frac{f''(\alpha)}{f'(\alpha)}\,e_0.$$

This is a **first-order (linear) recurrence**, giving geometric decay:

$$e_n \approx C^{\,n}\,e_0 = \left(\frac{f''(\alpha)}{f'(\alpha)}\,e_0\right)^{\
!n} e_0.$$

## Convergence Condition

The iteration converges locally provided

$$|C| = \left|\frac{f''(\alpha)}{f'(\alpha)}\right|\,|e_0| < 1,$$

i.e., the initial guess $x_0$ must be sufficiently close to $\alpha$ (the closer
, the smaller $|C|$ and the faster the linear convergence). Note that if $f''(\a
lpha)=0$ (e.g., $f$ is linear), then $C=0$ and the method converges in **one ste
p** — a degenerate special case.

## Conclusion

The simplified Newton method (fixed slope $f'(x_0)$) converges **linearly** (ord
er 1) to a simple root, with **asymptotic error constant**

$$\boxed{C = \left|\frac{f''(\alpha)}{f'(\alpha)}\right|\,|e_0|.}$$

This is to be contrasted with the standard Newton method (slope updated each ite
ration), which converges **quadratically** (order 2) with error constant $\left|
\frac{f''(\alpha)}{2f'(\alpha)}\right|$. The price of not updating the derivativ
e is the loss of one order of convergence: quadratic $\to$ linear, with the cons
tant depending on the quality of the initial guess through $e_0$.
