## Proof

**Prime factorization:** 1,000,000 = 10⁶ = 2⁶ × 5⁶.

### Step 1: Count ordered triples (a, b, c) with all components ≥ 1

Distributing 2⁶ among 3 positions: C(8,2) = 28. Distributing 5⁶ among 3 position
s: C(8,2) = 28.

Total ordered triples with a, b, c ≥ 1: 28 × 28 = **784**.

### Step 2: Apply inclusion-exclusion to enforce a, b, c > 1

Let Aᵢ = {triples where component i equals 1}.

- |Aᵢ| = number of ordered pairs (x, y) with xy = 10⁶ = d(10⁶) = (6+1)(6+1) = **
49**.
- |Aᵢ ∩ Aⱼ| = two components are 1, third is 10⁶: **1** way.
- |A₁ ∩ A₂ ∩ A₃| = all three are 1, product = 1 ≠ 10⁶: **0**.

By inclusion-exclusion, ordered triples with all > 1:
S = 784 − 3(49) + 3(1) − 0 = 784 − 147 + 3 = **640**.

### Step 3: Convert ordered count to unordered count

Define:
- **N₁** = unordered triples with all three distinct (a < b < c)
- **N₂** = unordered triples with exactly two equal {d, d, e}, d ≠ e
- **N₃** = unordered triples with all three equal (a = b = c)

Then: S = 6·N₁ + 3·N₂ + 1·N₃ = 640, and the answer is T = N₁ + N₂ + N₃.

### Step 4: Compute N₃ (all equal)

a³ = 10⁶ = 100³, so a = 100 > 1. **N₃ = 1**.

### Step 5: Compute N₂ (exactly two equal)

Triples of the form {d, d, e} with d²·e = 10⁶, d > 1, e > 1, d ≠ e.

We need d² | 10⁶. Writing d = 2ᵃ × 5ᵇ, the condition d² | 2⁶ × 5⁶ requires 2a ≤
6 and 2b ≤ 6, i.e., a ≤ 3, b ≤ 3.

This gives 4 × 4 = **16** candidate values of d:
{1, 2, 4, 8} × {1, 5, 25, 125} = {1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10, 20, 25, 40, 50, 100, 125, 2
00, 250, 500, 1000}.

Excluding:
- d = 1 (violates d > 1)
- d = 100 (gives e = 100, so d = e — this is the N₃ case)
- d = 1000 (gives e = 1, violates e > 1)

Remaining: **N₂ = 16 − 3 = 13**.

Verification of all 13 triples {d, d, e}:
| d | e = 10⁶/d² |
|---|---|
| 2 | 250000 |

All satisfy d > 1, e > 1, d ≠ e. ✓

### Step 6: Solve for N₁ and T

From S = 6·N₁ + 3·N₂ + N₃:
640 = 6·N₁ + 3(13) + 1 = 6·N₁ + 40
6·N₁ = 600
**N₁ = 100**

Total unordered triples:
T = N₁ + N₂ + N₃ = 100 + 13 + 1 = **114**
