## Proof

**Yes, such polynomials exist.** We construct one explicitly.

### Step 1: A quintic with Galois group $S_5$

Let $g(x) = x^5 - x - 1$. We show $g$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ with Galo
is group $S_5$.

**Irreducibility.** Reducing mod 3: $\bar{g}(x) = x^5 + 2x + 2 \in \mathbb{F}_3[
x]$. Checking all three elements of $\mathbb{F}_3$: $\bar{g}(0)=2$, $\bar{g}(1)=
2$, $\bar{g}(2)=2$, so no linear factors. The irreducible quadratics over $\math
bb{F}_3$ are $x^2+1$, $x^2+x+2$, $x^2+2x+2$; a direct remainder computation show
s none divides $\bar{g}$. Hence $\bar{g}$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_3$, so
 $g$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Discriminant.** Since $g(\alpha)=0$ implies $\alpha^5 = \alpha+1$, we get $\al
pha^4 = (\alpha+1)/\alpha$ (as $g(0)=-1\neq 0$, no root is zero). Then $g'(\alph
a) = 5\alpha^4 - 1 = (4\alpha+5)/\alpha$, so

$$\operatorname{Disc}(g) = \prod_{g(\alpha_i)=0} g'(\alpha_i) = \frac{\prod(4\al
pha_i+5)}{\prod \alpha_i}.$$

The product of roots is $\prod \alpha_i = (-1)^5(-1) = 1$. Also $\prod(4\alpha_i
+5) = 4^5\prod(\alpha_i+5/4) = 4^5\cdot(-1)^5 g(-5/4)$. Computing $g(-5/4) = -31
25/1024 + 5/4 - 1 = -2869/1024$, we get $\prod(4\alpha_i+5) = 1024\cdot 2869/102
4 = 2869$. Thus

$$\operatorname{Disc}(g) = 2869 = 19 \times 151.$$

Since $2\nmid 2869$ and $3\nmid 2869$, the Frobenius elements at 2 and 3 are wel
l-defined.

**Galois group computation.**

- **Mod 3:** $\bar{g}$ is irreducible, so the Frobenius at 3 has cycle type $(5)
$; $G$ contains a 5-cycle.
- **Mod 2:** $\bar{g}(x) = x^5+x+1 = (x^2+x+1)(x^3+x^2+1)$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$ (
both factors irreducible), so the Frobenius at 2 has cycle type $(2,3)$; $G$ con
tains an element of order 6.
- **Real roots:** By calculus, $g'(x)=5x^4-1$ vanishes at $x=\pm 5^{-1/4}$. The
local maximum $g(-5^{-1/4})\approx -0.465 < 0$, so $g$ has exactly **one** real
root and two pairs of complex conjugate roots. Complex conjugation acts as an el
ement of cycle type $(2,2)$.

The transitive subgroups of $S_5$ are $\mathbb{Z}_5$, $D_5$, $F_{20}$, $A_5$, an
d $S_5$. The element of type $(2,3)$ is an odd permutation (so $G\not\subseteq A
_5$) and has order 6 (so $G\neq F_{20}$, since $6\nmid 20$). The remaining possi
bilities not in $A_5$ are $F_{20}$ and $S_5$; we've ruled out $F_{20}$. Therefor
e $G = S_5$. $\checkmark$

### Step 2: Scaling to push all roots outside the unit circle

Let $r_1,\dots,r_5$ be the roots of $g$. Since $g(0)=-1\neq 0$, every $r_i\neq 0
$. Set $m = \min_i |r_i| > 0$ and choose an integer $c > 1/m$. Define

$$f(x) = c^5\, g(x/c) = x^5 - c^4 x - c^5.$$

The roots of $f$ are $c\,r_1,\dots,c\,r_5$, and $|c\,r_i| = c|r_i| \geq c\,m > 1
$ for every $i$. So **all roots of $f$ have absolute value greater than 1**.

### Step 3: $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$

Suppose $f(x) = h(x)\,k(x)$ with $h,k\in\mathbb{Q}[x]$ of positive degree. Then
$g(y) = f(cy)/c^5 = h(cy)\,k(cy)/c^5$. Since $c\in\mathbb{Q}$, both $h(cy)$ and
$k(cy)$ lie in $\mathbb{Q}[y]$ with positive degree, contradicting the irreducib
ility of $g$. Hence $f$ is irreducible. $\checkmark$

### Step 4: $f$ has Galois group $S_5$

The splitting field of $f$ is $\mathbb{Q}(c\,r_1,\dots,c\,r_5) = \mathbb{Q}(r_1,
\dots,r_5)$ (since $c\in\mathbb{Q}^\times$), which is exactly the splitting fiel
d of $g$. Therefore $\operatorname{Gal}(f) = \operatorname{Gal}(g) = S_5$. $\che
ckmark$

### Step 5: No root lies in a radical extension

$S_5$ is **not solvable** (its commutator subgroup is $A_5$, which is simple and
 non-abelian).

By the fundamental theorem of Galois theory connecting solvability by radicals t
o solvable Galois groups: an irreducible polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}$ (in charac
teristic 0) is solvable by radicals — meaning all its roots lie in some radical
extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ — if and only if its Galois group is solvable.

Concretely: if a root $\alpha$ of $f$ lay in a radical extension $K/\mathbb{Q}$,
 then the splitting field $L$ of $f$ (the Galois closure of $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)/
\mathbb{Q}$) would be contained in the Galois closure of $K/\mathbb{Q}$. The Gal
ois closure of a radical extension in characteristic 0 has solvable Galois group
, and solvability is preserved under passing to subgroups and quotients. This wo
uld force $\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q}) = S_5$ to be solvable — a contradict
ion.

Therefore **no root of $f$ lies in any radical extension of $\mathbb{Q}$**. $\ch
eckmark$

### Conclusion

The polynomial $f(x) = x^5 - c^4 x - c^5$ (for any sufficiently large integer $c
$) is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, all its roots have absolute value greater t
han 1, and none of its roots lie in a radical extension of $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Such polynomials exist.** $\blacksquare$
