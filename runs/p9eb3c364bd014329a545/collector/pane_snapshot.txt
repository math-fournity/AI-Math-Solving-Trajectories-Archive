# Solution

This is an estimation problem — the answer depends on real-world facts (the iden
tities and ages of HMMT November problem writers) that cannot be derived purely
from the problem text. The scoring rubric `max(0, 20−|X−Y|)` confirms this is de
signed as an estimation problem where partial credit is awarded for proximity.

## Reasoning for my estimate

**Number of problem writers.** HMMT November typically consists of three rounds:
- General Round (~10 problems)
- Theme Round (~10 problems)
- Guts Round (~36 problems, in sets of 4)

That is roughly 56 problems total. HMMT problem writers typically contribute 3–5
 problems each, so the number of distinct writers is likely in the range **11–19
**, with a central estimate around **13–14**.

**Age distribution.** HMMT is organized by Harvard and MIT students. The problem
 writing team is typically composed of:
- Undergraduates (ages ~19–22), the majority
- Graduate students (ages ~23–27), a smaller portion
- Occasionally one or two older advisors/former competitors (~28–35)

A weighted average age of roughly **21–22** is reasonable for the predominantly
undergraduate group.

**Estimate.** Taking approximately 14 writers with an average age of about 21.5:

$$X \approx 14 \times 21.5 \approx 301$$

Rounding to a clean estimate:

$$\boxed{X = 300}$$

## Caveat

This estimate is inherently uncertain — the actual value $Y$ depends on the spec
ific roster of writers for the contest in question, which I cannot determine fro
m the problem statement alone and am not permitted to look up. The true answer c
ould plausily fall anywhere in the range 200–400. My central estimate of **300**
 reflects a typical HMMT November problem-writing team of about 14 people with a
n average age around 21–22.
