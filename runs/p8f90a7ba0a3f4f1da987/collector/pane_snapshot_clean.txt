## Answer: **No.** The quotient does not always split.

We construct an explicit counterexample: a smooth complex algebraic group $G$ wi
th $G^\circ$ an abelian variety, $G/G^\circ\cong\mathbb{Z}/2$, such that no subg
roup $Q\cong\mathbb{Z}/2$ satisfies $G=G^\circ Q$.

---

### Construction

Let $A$ be an abelian variety over $\mathbb{C}$ of dimension $g\ge 1$ (e.g. an e
lliptic curve). Its $2$-torsion is
$$A[2](\mathbb{C})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2g},$$
so there exist nonzero $2$-torsion points. Pick $a_0\in A[2](\mathbb{C})$, $a_0\
neq 0$.

Let $F=\mathbb{Z}/2$ act on $A$ by inversion, i.e. the generator acts as $[-1]:A
\to A$, $a\mapsto -a$. Define $G$ as a variety by
$$G \;=\; A\;\sqcup\; g_0 A$$
(two disjoint copies of $A$, the second written with a formal symbol $g_0$). Def
ine the group law (in additive notation on $A$) by:

- On the first copy: the usual addition $+$ of $A$.
- **Conjugation:** $g_0\,a = (-a)\,g_0$ for $a\in A$, i.e. $\operatorname{Inn}_{
g_0}=[-1]$.
- **Coset multiplication:** $(g_0 a)(g_0 b)=a_0 - a + b$ for $a,b\in A$.

In particular $g_0^2 = a_0$ (take $a=b=0$).

### $G$ is an algebraic group

The multiplication map $m:G\times G\to G$ is a morphism of varieties in all four
 cases:

| restriction | formula | type |
|---|---|---|
| $A\times A\to A$ | $(a,b)\mapsto a+b$ | addition on $A$ ✓ |
| $A\times g_0A\to g_0A$ | $(a,\,g_0b)\mapsto g_0(b-a)$ | morphism $A\times A\to
 A$ ✓ |
| $g_0A\times A\to g_0A$ | $(g_0a,\,b)\mapsto g_0(a+b)$ | morphism $A\times A\to
 A$ ✓ |
| $g_0A\times g_0A\to A$ | $(g_0a,\,g_0b)\mapsto a_0-a+b$ | morphism $A\times A\
to A$ ✓ |

The inverse is $i|_A=[-1]$ and $i(g_0a)=g_0(a-a_0)$, both morphisms. Associativi
ty is the cocycle condition: the only nontrivial check is $(g_0\,g_0)\,g_0=g_0\,
(g_0\,g_0)$, which reduces to $[-1](a_0)=a_0$, i.e. $2a_0=0$, satisfied by hypot
hesis. Hence $G$ is a **smooth algebraic group over $\mathbb{C}$**.

### Component group

The first copy $A$ is connected and is the identity component:
$$G^\circ = A,\qquad G/G^\circ \cong \mathbb{Z}/2.$$

### The extension does not split

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a subgroup $Q\subseteq G$ with $Q\
simeq\mathbb{Z}/2$ and $G=A\cdot Q$. Then $Q=\{e,q\}$ with $q\in g_0A$ (the non-
identity coset) and $q^2=e$. Write $q=g_0 h$ for some $h\in A$. Using $h\,g_0 =
g_0(-h)$:
$$q^2=(g_0 h)(g_0 h)=g_0\,(h\,g_0)\,h=g_0\,g_0(-h)\,h = a_0 - h + h = a_0.$$
The crucial point is that the **norm map** $N:A\to A$, $N(h)=h+[-1](h)=h-h=0$, i
s identically zero, so every element of the non-identity coset squares to the *s
ame* value $a_0$.

Since $a_0\neq 0 = e$, we have $q^2=a_0\neq e$, contradicting $q^2=e$. Therefore
 **no such subgroup $Q$ exists**.

### Cohomological interpretation (optional)

The extensions of $\mathbb{Z}/2$ by $A$ (with the inversion action) are classifi
ed by
$$H^2\!\bigl(\mathbb{Z}/2,\,A(\mathbb{C})\bigr)=A[2](\mathbb{C})\,/\,N\!\bigl(A(
\mathbb{C})\bigr)=A[2](\mathbb{C})\,/\,0\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2g},$$
since $N=1+[-1]=[0]$ is the zero endomorphism. The split extension corresponds t
o the class $0$; every **nonzero** class $a_0\in A[2]$ gives a non-split extensi
on. Over an algebraically closed field this abstract group cohomology agrees wit
h fppf cohomology (both $A$ and $F$ are smooth, $F$ is constant), so these are g
enuine algebraic group extensions.

---

### Conclusion

The answer is **no**: there exist complex algebraic groups $G$ (even smooth ones
 with $G^\circ$ an abelian variety) for which $G/G^\circ$ does **not** split. Th
e obstruction is a nonzero class in $H^2\!\bigl(\pi_0(G),\,Z(G^\circ)\bigr)$; fo
r $G^\circ=A$ an abelian variety with $\pi_0(G)=\mathbb{Z}/2$ acting by inversio
n, this group is $A[2]\neq 0$, yielding explicit non-split extensions.
