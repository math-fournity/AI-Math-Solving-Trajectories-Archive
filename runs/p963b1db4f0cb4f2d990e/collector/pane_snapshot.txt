## Solution

Write the integral as
\[
F_v(z)=\int_C e^{\,z\psi(t)}\,f(t)\,dt,\qquad
\psi(t)=-i\sin t,\qquad f(t)=e^{ivt},
\]
with \(z\to+\infty\) and \(v\ge 0\) fixed, so that \(e^{ivt}\) is a slowly varyi
ng amplitude.

### 1. Saddle points

\[
\psi'(t)=-i\cos t=0\;\Longrightarrow\; t=\pm\frac{\pi}{2}.
\]
At these,
\[
\psi''(t)=i\sin t,\qquad
\psi\!\left(-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)=i,\quad \psi''\!\left(-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)=
-i,
\qquad
\psi\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)=-i,\quad \psi''\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)=i
 .
\]

### 2. Which saddle the contour crosses

With \(t=x+iy\),
\[
\Re\psi(t)=\cos x\,\sinh y .
\]
The endpoints of \(C\) are valleys of \(e^{z\psi}\):
\[
t=-\pi+i\infty:\ \cos(-\pi)\sinh(+\infty)=-\infty,\qquad
t=-i\infty:\ \cos 0\,\sinh(-\infty)=-\infty .
\]
The steepest-descent path through \(t_0=-\pi/2\) (where \(\Re\psi=0\)) has one b
ranch running up-left into the valley at \(x=-\pi\) and one branch running down-
right into the valley at \(x=0\), matching the prescribed endpoints. The saddle
\(+\pi/2\) lies on a path joining different valleys and is not crossed by \(C\).
 Since the integrand is entire, \(C\) may be deformed to this steepest-descent p
ath through \(t_0=-\pi/2\).

### 3. Local direction of steepest descent

Near \(t_0=-\pi/2\),
\[
z\psi(t)\approx z\psi(t_0)+\tfrac{z}{2}\psi''(t_0)(t-t_0)^2
=iz-\tfrac{i z}{2}(t-t_0)^2 .
\]
Set \(t-t_0=\rho e^{i\theta}\). Then \(-\tfrac{i z}{2}\rho^2 e^{2i\theta}\) is r
eal and negative when
\[
2\theta-\tfrac{\pi}{2}=\pm\pi\;\Longrightarrow\;\theta=\tfrac{3\pi}{4}\ \text{or
}\ -\tfrac{\pi}{4}.
\]
Branch A (\(\theta=3\pi/4\), toward \(-\pi+i\infty\)) and branch B (\(\theta=-\p
i/4\), toward \(-i\infty\)) give \(\exp(-z\rho^2/2)\).

### 4. Gaussian integration

Traversing \(C\) from \(-\pi+i\infty\) to \(-i\infty\) means entering along A (\
(\rho:\infty\to0\)) and leaving along B (\(\rho:0\to\infty\)):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_C e^{z\psi}f\,dt
&\sim f(t_0)e^{z\psi(t_0)}
\Bigl[\int_{\infty}^{0}e^{-z\rho^2/2}e^{i3\pi/4}\,d\rho
+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-z\rho^2/2}e^{-i\pi/4}\,d\rho\Bigr] \\[2mm]
&=e^{-i\pi v/2}e^{iz}\,
\bigl(e^{-i\pi/4}-e^{i3\pi/4}\bigr)\int_0^\infty e^{-z\rho^2/2}\,d\rho .
\end{aligned}
\]
Using
\[
e^{-i\pi/4}-e^{i3\pi/4}=2e^{-i\pi/4},\qquad
\int_0^\infty e^{-z\rho^2/2}\,d\rho=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2z}},
\]
we obtain
\[
F_v(z)\sim e^{-i\pi v/2}e^{iz}\cdot 2e^{-i\pi/4}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2z}}
=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{z}}\,
\exp\!\Bigl[i\Bigl(z-\frac{\pi v}{2}-\frac{\pi}{4}\Bigr)\Bigr].
\]

### Result

\[
\boxed{\,F_v(z)\sim \sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{z}}\,
\exp\!\left[\,i\!\left(z-\frac{\pi v}{2}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)\right]\,,\qquad z\
to+\infty,\ v\ge0\ \text{fixed}.\,}
\]

(Equivalently \(F_v(z)\sim \pi H_v^{(1)}(z)\), consistent with the Hankel asympt
otic
\(H_v^{(1)}(z)\sim\sqrt{2/(\pi z)}\,e^{i(z-\pi v/2-\pi/4)}\).)
