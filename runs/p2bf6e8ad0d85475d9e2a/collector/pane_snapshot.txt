**Claim.** If $\mathbb{E}[|Z|^q]<\infty$ for some $q>0$, then
$$\lim_{x\to\infty} x^q\,\mathbb{P}(|Z|>x)=0.$$

---

**Proof.**

**Step 1 (Layer-cake / Fubini identity).** For any non-negative random variable
$Y$ and $q>0$,
$$\mathbb{E}[Y^q]=\int_0^\infty q\,t^{q-1}\,\mathbb{P}(Y>t)\,dt.$$
This follows by writing $Y^q=\int_0^Y q\,t^{q-1}\,dt$ and applying Tonelli/Fubin
i:
$$\mathbb{E}[Y^q]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^\infty q\,t^{q-1}\,\mathbf{1}_{\{Y>t\
}}\,dt\right]=\int_0^\infty q\,t^{q-1}\,\mathbb{P}(Y>t)\,dt.$$
Setting $Y=|Z|$ and using $\mathbb{E}[|Z|^q]<\infty$,
$$\int_0^\infty q\,t^{q-1}\,\mathbb{P}(|Z|>t)\,dt=\mathbb{E}[|Z|^q]<\infty. \tag
{1}$$

**Step 2 (Tail of a convergent integral vanishes).** Since the integral in (1) i
s finite, its tail tends to zero:
$$\int_x^{\infty} q\,t^{q-1}\,\mathbb{P}(|Z|>t)\,dt\;\xrightarrow{x\to\infty}\;0
. \tag{2}$$

**Step 3 (Monotonicity lower bound).** The map $t\mapsto\mathbb{P}(|Z|>t)$ is no
n-increasing. Hence for every $x>0$,
$$\int_x^{2x} q\,t^{q-1}\,\mathbb{P}(|Z|>t)\,dt\;\ge\;\mathbb{P}(|Z|>2x)\int_x^{
2x}q\,t^{q-1}\,dt.$$
Computing the inner integral,
$$\int_x^{2x}q\,t^{q-1}\,dt=(2x)^q-x^q=x^q(2^q-1).$$
Therefore
$$\int_x^{2x} q\,t^{q-1}\,\mathbb{P}(|Z|>t)\,dt\;\ge\;x^q(2^q-1)\,\mathbb{P}(|Z|
>2x)=\frac{2^q-1}{2^q}\,(2x)^q\,\mathbb{P}(|Z|>2x). \tag{3}$$

**Step 4 (Combine).** The left-hand side of (3) is bounded above by the tail int
egral in (2):
$$0\;\le\;\frac{2^q-1}{2^q}\,(2x)^q\,\mathbb{P}(|Z|>2x)\;\le\;\int_x^{\infty} q\
,t^{q-1}\,\mathbb{P}(|Z|>t)\,dt\;\xrightarrow{x\to\infty}\;0.$$
Writing $y=2x$, this gives
$$0\;\le\;\frac{2^q-1}{2^q}\,y^q\,\mathbb{P}(|Z|>y)\;\le\;\int_{y/2}^{\infty} q\
,t^{q-1}\,\mathbb{P}(|Z|>t)\,dt\;\xrightarrow{y\to\infty}\;0.$$
By the squeeze theorem,
$$\lim_{y\to\infty} y^q\,\mathbb{P}(|Z|>y)=0.$$

Renaming $y$ back to $x$,

$$\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}x^q\,\mathbb{P}(|Z|>x)=0\qquad\text{whene
ver }\mathbb{E}[|Z|^q]<\infty.}$$

**Remark (converse direction).** The integration identity (1) also shows the tig
htness of the result: if $x^q\mathbb{P}(|Z|>x)\not\to 0$, say it stays $\ge c>0$
 along a subsequence, then by monotonicity $\mathbb{P}(|Z|>t)\ge c/(2x)^q$ for $
t\in[x,2x]$, forcing $\int_x^{2x}q\,t^{q-1}\mathbb{P}(|Z|>t)\,dt\ge c\,(2^q-1)/2
^q>0$ for arbitrarily large $x$, contradicting (2). So finiteness of $\mathbb{E}
[|Z|^q]$ is exactly the condition that drives the limit to $0$.
