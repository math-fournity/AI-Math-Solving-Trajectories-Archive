## Proof

The ellipse is $\frac{x^2}{4}+y^2=1$, with semi-major axis $2$ (along $x$) and s
emi-minor axis $1$ (along $y$).

**Setting up coordinates.** One vertex is $A=(0,1)$ (the top of the ellipse), an
d the altitude from $A$ lies on the $y$-axis. Since an altitude is perpendicular
 to the opposite side and bisects it, the opposite side $BC$ is perpendicular to
 the $y$-axis, i.e. horizontal, with its midpoint on the $y$-axis. By symmetry t
he other two vertices are
$$B=(a,b),\qquad C=(-a,b)$$
for some $a>0$.

**Ellipse constraint.** $B$ lies on the ellipse:
$$a^2+4b^2=4. \tag{1}$$

**Equilateral constraint.** The side $BC$ has length $2a$, and side $AB$ has len
gth $\sqrt{a^2+(b-1)^2}$. Equating:
$$4a^2 = a^2+(b-1)^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; 3a^2=(b-1)^2. \tag{2}$$

**Solving.** From (2), $a^2=\frac{(b-1)^2}{3}$. Substituting into (1):
$$\frac{(b-1)^2}{3}+4b^2=4 \;\Longrightarrow\; (b-1)^2+12b^2=12$$
$$b^2-2b+1+12b^2=12 \;\Longrightarrow\; 13b^2-2b-11=0.$$
Using the quadratic formula:
$$b=\frac{2\pm\sqrt{4+572}}{26}=\frac{2\pm 24}{26}.$$
This gives $b=1$ (which yields $a=0$, degenerate) or $b=-\frac{11}{13}$.

Taking $b=-\frac{11}{13}$:
$$a^2=\frac{(b-1)^2}{3}=\frac{\left(-\frac{11}{13}-1\right)^2}{3}=\frac{\left(-\
frac{24}{13}\right)^2}{3}=\frac{576}{169}\cdot\frac{1}{3}=\frac{192}{169}.$$

**Verification of the ellipse constraint:**
$$a^2+4b^2=\frac{192}{169}+4\cdot\frac{121}{169}=\frac{192+484}{169}=\frac{676}{
169}=4.\;\checkmark$$

**Side length.** Each side has length $2a$, so
$$s^2 = 4a^2 = 4\cdot\frac{192}{169}=\frac{768}{169}.$$

Since $768=2^8\cdot 3$ and $169=13^2$ are relatively prime, we have $m=768$, $n=
169$, and
$$m+n = 768+169 = \boxed{937}.$$
